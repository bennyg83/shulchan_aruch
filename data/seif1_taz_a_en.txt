(a) The Shulchan Aruch, “one who cooks on Shabbos,” rules in accordance with R. Yehuda and not R. Meir, because the unattributed (stam) text in Chullin follows R. Yehuda. However, Tosafos and those who side with them rule in accordance with R. Meir, and their proofs are very clear. As for what they wrote — that the stam in Chullin follows R. Yehuda — that is not a refutation: the stam in Terumos ch. 2 follows R. Meir, and the sugya at the end of Shabbos ch. 3 also follows that view, and Rav Amram Gaon likewise follows R. Meir. What the Ramban and the Ran rejected, namely that Rav “leans” toward R. Yehuda — we hold in accordance with R. Yochanan, who holds that the halacha follows [that view]. It is not so in our Bavli; on the contrary, R. Yochanan in chapter HaKotev “leans” — but there we hold like Resh Lakish; only their wording is difficult, and it must be that Rav according to his reasoning does not require these general principles of Eruvin ch. 17 seif 1. But we hold in accordance with R. Yochanan; nevertheless their words are not plausible — for if so, why did the Rif and Shulchan Peshuto rule like R. Shimon [when discussing] “one who leaves his house and goes,” since Rav holds like him — except as stated: where it is stated, it is stated; and even if you would force the matter, here is different because he did not rule halacha explicitly but only [raised a possibility] — still it does not appear reasonable to force the matter. Aside from this, this general principle does not apply here: even if one were to rule halacha like R. Yehuda, nevertheless here halacha follows R. Meir, because the dispute appears in a beraita with an unattributed mishnah, as stated in Yevamos ch. 4 102b, and as the Rif writes in chapter Kol HaKeilim — even though that [passage] in Chullin is also unattributed, that formulation refers specifically to Shabbos, analogous to Yom Kippur; and even where liability attaches… and we do not abandon the plain meaning of the mishnah. All the proofs that the Ramban cited from what is stated in chapter Tolim 100a regarding the wife of Ze’eiri… the mother of Abaye’s servant… these involve unintentional acts; likewise from the end of Yom Tov, chapter “He entered Rav Abba’s house”… he brought him… all these require the opposite conclusion, for R. Meir is strict in rabbinic matters and R. Yehuda is not strict, and all these cases are rabbinic — unless one can explain the Rif’s words, based on what we say in Yerushalmi chapter 3 of Orlah, and as Tosafos cite in Gitin 59a s.v. naflu: the reason of R. Meir… the reason of R. Yossi… R. Avahu said in the name of R. Yochanan: for all prohibitions that Scripture added, unintentional use is permitted and intentional use is forbidden… hence R. Yochanan holds like R. Yehuda and R. Yossi — and this matches what the Ramban says, that R. Yochanan argues on Rav; or perhaps according to their approach as stated above regarding the rule of R. Meir and R. Yehuda… R. Yochanan has another stam in Terumos seif 5: a se’ah of terumah… afterward… if unintentional, it is permitted… and now it also works out well: even though one stam follows R. Meir, since another stam follows R. Yehuda, we revert to the rule of R. Meir and R. Yehuda — Rav according to his approach and R. Yochanan according to his approach, as stated; likewise in Zevachim regarding a nefel, specifically when it fell by itself, as Tosafos [explain] Rav according to his approach, as stated; but according to R. Yochanan even if she miscarried… and the words of Shulchan Aruch there are incorrect, even if Tosafos would hold that way for halacha, as Beit Yosef writes. Also regarding what Razah challenged the Rif in Shabbos ch. 3 about what the Rif wrote — from which it would follow there is no [halacha of] R. Yehuda the sandal-maker — therefore halacha follows R. Yehuda, and the Ramban was forced to answer; the Rosh writes in chapter Meruba seif 5, and I find it difficult to understand his words, for these Amoraim argue about R. Yehuda the sandal-maker — how could he write there is none — it is not difficult, his words are quite clear from the sugya in Gitin 59a: the entire approach of the Rif to rule like R. Yehuda the sandal-maker against the rule of R. Meir and R. Yehuda is only because the conclusion of the Gemara there is that the Chachamim follow R. Shimon and hold like R. Yehuda the sandal-maker — hence rabbinically they hold like him, halacha like him. But according to one who holds melacha sheves is rabbinic, the conclusion there is that Chachamim exempt except… but for Shabbos slaughter Rabbinic prohibitions apply, and one must establish Rabbinic law like R. Yehuda the sandal-maker — yet for us, who hold melacha sheves is rabbinic, there is no need for Rabbinic law to follow R. Yehuda the sandal-maker; rather we revert to the rule of R. Meir and R. Yehuda… the dispute of R. Eliezer and Ravina depends on how one explains the mishnah, whether it applies and Chachamim exempt except… as stated; therefore halacha… Nevertheless the words of Tosafos are primary for halacha, and one can bring proof for them from what is stated in Yerushalmi ch. 3 of Yom Tov: the household of Rabban Gamliel would grind pepper in their mill; R. Abba bar Zavina… “Father ate at Rabban Gamliel’s,” and they brought before him an anigrin containing ground pepper; since its taste drew back his hands from it, he said to him: do not be concerned for them from Yom Tov — they are ground; they asked there: Rabban Gamliel at Rabbi Tzadok as unintentional may eat — only not to permit melacha through Rabban Gamliel — and according to this the proof is the opposite; except that in Milchamot ch. 3 he wrote that since it was not asked in our Bavli, [some might say] it does not apply — but one should not reject his explicit statements in Yerushalmi, and one can also answer as stated in ch. 3: cooking is different and stricter, but this [case]… likewise all his proofs are rejected as stated. Moreover, Yerushalmi Terumos ch. 2 explicitly states, asked before R. Yochanan: what do you say? He answered: I have only the mishnah of maaser and “one who cooks on Shabbos — unintentionally may eat, intentionally may not eat.” Hence halacha follows R. Meir that unintentionally one may eat… and even rabbinically he is not penalized, as R. Yochanan stated above; and what is stated there, R. Avahu in the name of R. Yossi “all prohibitions” applies both to Torah and rabbinic law — therefore Rabbi’s stam in Terumos there regarding maaser and cooking: maaser is rabbinic, likewise immersion of vessels… and cooking is from the Torah — and this stam follows R. Yochanan as stated. What the Geonim and Ran and Ramban wrote — that therefore one should rule like R. Yehuda because many are scrupulous — likewise the Rosh end of Chullin is not [convincing], for Tosafos already wrote in ch. 1 of Yom Tov… we are considered worthy students of Torah; proof from bishul akum; all the more so to be lenient in practical ruling like great teachers such as R. Meir; all the more so the sugya in ch. 21 of Shabbos follows him, and all the Amoraim there — and so is the primary halacha.
