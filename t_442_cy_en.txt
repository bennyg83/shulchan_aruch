And all similar things — namely red vinegar and Egyptian olives, all those listed in the mishnah at the beginning of Elu Overin; and also yayin sharaf made from sour grain called maltz, as written in the responsum of Magen Avraham siman 58 that it is complete chametz and worse than a mixture of chametz — see there: a matter in its taste; and so too Maharam in Tikatin in a printed responsum at the beginning of the Rif Krakow edition in Seder Nashim — see there; and there is no distinction between that made from sour grain or that made from beer leaven — and not as occurred to one wise person, as I will copy his question to me in his name. And thus: The question: I asked one matter and I ask His Excellency to answer me on an incident that came to me while I was in Ashkenaz. I was asked about Reuven who had yayin sharaf made from barley beer leaven; he forgot to sell before Pesach, and during the festival it was remembered that he did not sell it; and the yayin sharaf was in a gentile's house — the aforementioned Israelite had a room in the gentile's house — and I was asked the law of the yayin sharaf, which is a great loss worth about two hundred reichstaler. And behold, according to the quick view it is a simple law to forbid, for it is chametz on which Pesach passed and must be burned — from what we learned in the Gemara daf 42: and these transgress on Pesach: Babylonian kotach and Median beer, etc.; and Rashi explained these transgress in bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei; and so Riva; and the Rosh wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: specifically Median beer in which they put barley; but our beer made in Ashkenaz whose essence is from barley is complete chametz and has a kezayit in the measure of eating a peras; and so the Tur and all Acharonim; and the same in yayin sharaf made from grain — it is complete chametz prohibition with a kezayit in kedei achilat peras; and so ruled all Acharonim; and so is established that yayin sharaf is chametz and not as one who says it is mere sweat; and therefore in our case it is chametz on which Pesach passed and forbidden in benefit. But it appears to me to side some permission and save Jewish money; and it appears to divide between yayin sharaf made from grain and that made from leaven: since we establish like Rabbeinu Tam in Elu Overin — from the table, for they are not fit to eat; but in bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei there is no mixture of chametz; and in Tur siman 442 it states: all these to R' Eliezer are in a prohibition and have no karet; according to him forbidden to retain; and to the rabbis even a prohibition they have only a general prohibition; according to them permitted to retain; and so the Rif and Rosh; and so is established. And according to what is in the Gemara Bava Batra 96b: the rabbis taught: one date beer, one barley, one wine leaven — one blesses on them shehakol; others say leaven that has taste of wine — one blesses borei peri hagafen; and we establish the halachah is like the first tanna. Further there R' Nachman asked R' Chiya: leaven that has taste of wine — what? Rashi explained: he asks about wine blessing; and a baraita was not heard by him — if so it should have said halachah like others or not — end Rashi. And he answered him: who thought it is wine? It is mere kivui — end; and Tosafot wrote: who thought it is wine? It is mere kivui — from here R' Chananel ruled b'dieved if a gentile washed wine-leaven barrels it is permitted — it is not wine but mere kivui — end; and so Tosafos s.v. leaven of terumah: for all matters it is only mere kivui; only in consecrated they were stringent — see there; and so in Asheri in the name of R' Yonah — see there; if so, likewise regarding chametz one may be lenient since it is only mere kivui. And one should not say to be more stringent with leaven than grain because it is a davar ha'me'amed u'machmitz even in a thousand not nullified — this is not so if one puts leaven in the dough or honey to make a drink for chametz — that is forbidden, as in responsum of Maharam Lublin — to exclude here where they put nothing in the leaven except mere moisture that emerges from the leaven, which is really only mere sweat, as in Tosafos chapter HaMocher daf 62 s.v. one date beer — see there. And leaven that is only kivui is not better than the agent in leaf-serf whose essence is permitted as in first chapter of Orlah and as in Gemara Avodah Zarah 34 and 35; and certainly they do not have a kezayit in kedei achilat peras; and the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh in siman 442: everything not practiced except for mere kivui — permitted to retain until after Pesach; and even though we establish one blesses on wine leaven in pa'ah — as in Orach Chayyim — see there; in Beit Yosef he answered on the sugya in Bava Batra: behold it states there in bal yera'eh — it deals specifically with their wines that were strong — to exclude our wines; all the more beer leaven — see there. And further, since the incident was inadvertent and he forgot to sell, one should not penalize like R' Shimon who penalizes after Pesach, as in the Ran this reasoning regarding mixture of chametz; and further it appears to side permission since he nullified his chametz and hefker helps b'shogeg as in Avodat HaKodesh in the name of Maharam of Recanti; and although already challenged on this in responsum of Magen Avraham — see there — truth the law is so that this nullification does not help when he said all my chametz while his heart was not with him; even so in our case b'shogeg it appears it helps, as in Tur siman 431: from the Torah general nullification suffices; and as above, except the sages required removal from the house, etc.; and although the Rambam wrote it is d'oraisa, the Beit Yosef already challenged and we conclude it is d'rabbanan and permitted to retain on Pesach; and all the more the yayin sharaf was not in a Jewish house. And although we establish one who deposits chametz with a gentile — the Israelite transgresses bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei — here is different since he nullified his chametz and it is also only kivui; therefore it appeared from all these reasons to permit in benefit at least. And afterward one sage showed me responsum of Maharam Tikatin printed at the beginning of Seder Nashim to the Rif, and there it says to forbid whether from sour grain or beer leaven; but one may distinguish in our case: there the sage ruled l'chatchila to retain yayin sharaf in his house — unlike our case where it was b'shogeg and also not in a Jewish house; therefore it appears permitted; and the great rabbi R' Shmuel Av Beit Din of Frieda agreed with me because of some sides of permission I wrote and great loss, on a double condition that another of the teachers also agree — otherwise his words are void; thus instruct us your view on this — said the humble Meir son of AA Moharar Moshe called R' Meir R' Kaplans of Pozna. Answer: although my mind is not decisive, and especially in the strict law of chametz prohibition — and I, Yaakov, am a worm — how shall I open my mouth where great ones stand, vessels of precious speech who already ascended mountains and descended valleys; to Beit Pamiel she raised and agreed with him in reason and knowledge; but in order not to return a blank face I said to remove the veil of shame from my face, and God is our God for salvations, and give truth to Yaakov, and not come, heaven forbid, to error; and this came out first. Behold what your honor wrote as a side for permission: since we establish like Rabbeinu Tam that bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei do not apply to mixture of chametz, etc. — I do not know how this reasoning is simple to him, for behold in Shulchan Aruch siman 442 he ruled explicitly that one transgresses a prohibition on mixture of chametz even without a kezayit in kedei achilat peras, for he listed Babylonian kotach that has no kezayit in kedei achilat peras as explicitly in the Gemara: leave Babylonian kotach that has no kezayit; and if he roasted and ate (meaning they ate together without dipping) his mind is voided to everyone; and even though one may say nevertheless it deals with eating it in one sitting kedei achilat peras — therefore obligated to burn it; and what the Gemara says if he roasted and ate his mind is voided — this means his mind is voided and the tanna does not deal with this; but if it happened he ate so they are liable on it; and so in responsum Maharam of Trani siman 246 in the name of R' Yosef Beit Rav; and you will find this explanation also in Tosafot Nazir — see there; nevertheless the sealed language of Shulchan Aruch implies that in every matter one is liable on mixture of chametz even without kezayit in kedei achilat peras; and so agree all Acharonim (and when I looked at this law in rishonim and acharonim who extended greatly in these laws, I saw a standing and great wonder in the words of the Taz in the beginning of siman 442 wondering on the Beit Yosef who wrote that for the rabbis even though they do not transgress on mixture of chametz in the prohibition of bal yera'eh, nevertheless forbidden to retain from bal yimatzei; and the Taz wrote: and further wonder greatly on him why he establishes the mishnah of Elu Overin like R' Eliezer who holds there is a prohibition in this — why not establish the mishnah even like the rabbis — behold the mishnah does not teach lashes but transgresses; and Rashi: transgresses from the world; and it fits well like the rabbis that there is in it in any case a prohibition when they exist in the world even though there is no lashes — end Taz; and I do not know how this mishnah that teaches explicitly behold these are in a warning, proving there is a prohibition in its removal, and it does not come like the rabbis at all, was hidden from him; and on this the Gemara says there and there is not even a slight difficulty on the Beit Yosef). And further: even if we say in mixture of chametz we require kezayit in kedei achilat peras, nevertheless here in yayin sharaf it is not called mixture of chametz but chametz literally like the leaven themselves — sweat emerging from the beverage as beverage, as proven from many mishnayot in chapters 2 and 5 of Machshirin and chapter 25, and as explained in responsum of the Rosh general 20 siman 26 and in Ribash siman 295 — see there; and so explicitly in responsum Magen Avraham siman 58 that yayin sharaf made from chametz is chametz literally and not mixture of chametz — see there; and so in responsum Maharam Tikatin that it is considered prohibition in its essence and is a davar ha'me'amed and not mere sweat — see there. But what your honor raised to distinguish and say that specifically yayin sharaf from sour grain has concern of prohibition unlike that from leaven, because the leaven themselves are kivui as established in the Gemara Bava Batra — behold even though seemingly this division is correct, even though there is stringency for stam yayin and also for blessing, nevertheless one may distinguish and say for our case it was only kivui as the meaning of the Gemara and Tosafos there s.v. likewise he clarified from its measure, etc., and s.v. who thought — see there: I wonder from the outset of the law: from where did he come to compare beer leaven to wine leaven, which have no relation at all, as sense and intellect testify — certainly wine leaven: the wine itself is the clear thing and leaven comes only from waste of stones and dregs that settled below and have no taste except kivui; not so beer leaven that as soon as they receive chametz taste they are clear, and leaven comes from the mash and wheat and barley that were ground and settled below — they are the essence of chametz in taste and appearance and chametz most doughs in them, and they have only kivui of the beer, but they themselves are the essence of chametz and flour. And also what your honor tends to leniency because it was in a gentile's house — for according to many poskim one does not transgress bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei — behold from the wording of the question your honor wrote it implies the Jew had a rented room in the gentile's house, and if so it is as his own; and further that some poskim hold no transgression in bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei if in a gentile's house — that is if the gentile accepted responsibility; not so without accepting responsibility, and it is simple as explained in Beit Yosef end of siman 440 — see there; and your honor is silent whether he accepted responsibility. And because it was b'shogeg one may not be lenient at all, for chametz on which Pesach passed, whether b'shogeg or ones, is forbidden in benefit as explained in siman 448. And what your honor wrote on the law of nullification before witnesses — I did not wish to write from this, as I already explained this law in Torat HaShleimim printed in question 16; why should I repeat, for all parts of the answer are explained there — thus the words of the small one Yaakov.
