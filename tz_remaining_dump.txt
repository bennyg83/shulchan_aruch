[0] It is forbidden to engage in commerce with it. In the Beit Yosef, in the name of the Rashba, the reason is a decree lest he come to eat from them; excluded are a donkey and a camel, for a person raises them for labor. And I wondered, for in the chapter Kol Sha'ah (Pesachim daf 23) it is stated that it is a d'oraisa prohibition, for we learned: if tamei species came into his hands, it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew; and it explains the reason, for it is written “to you” — it shall be yours; and we ask: if so, even l'chatchila as well. And it answers: this case is different, for the verse says “they shall be,” meaning, they shall remain in their state; and Tosafos there wrote that it is by Torah law. And it appears that the Rashba wrote this decree: that the Torah forbade it for this reason, since we see a permission because of “to you,” and a prohibition because of “they shall be,” and thus the matter is given over to the Sages, that they choose what will be forbidden and what will be permitted. An example of the matter is the prohibition of melacha on Chol HaMoed in the second chapter of Chagigah. Therefore the Sages judged that in any matter that is food one must withhold it lest he come to eat it, but an item designated for labor is permitted. Afterwards I saw in Terumat HaDeshen siman 200, who wrote in the name of the Tosafos gilyon that this derivation is an asmachta. And this is written in the gilyon of Tosafos in the chapter Kol Sha'ah that I mentioned, where they wrote that the prohibition of commerce with these is by Torah law; on this the gilyon wrote that they did not mean a full d'oraisa, for in truth it is only d'rabbanan, and it is merely an asmachta. But it is difficult for me from what Tosafos wrote in the chapter Lulav HaGazul (Sukkah daf 39), s.v. “and let him give it,” etc., regarding Shevi'is, and this is their language: and it is possible that one who purchases from his fellow in order to profit and sell for a higher price, this too is commerce; and in the fourth chapter of Shevi'is we learned that one does not engage in commerce with fruits of Shevi'is, and not with firstborns, and not with terumos, and not with neveilos and treifos, etc.; you do not find that they are all equal regarding commerce except in such a case where the Torah explicitly permitted selling, as it is written: “or sell to a non-Jew.” End of their words. And if it is so that the prohibition of commerce with tamei items is only d'rabbanan, what do Tosafos prove from neveilos and treifos from the verse “or sell to a non-Jew,” for there it is certainly permitted by Torah law, and the Sages forbade it; and the Mishnah states that by rabbinic law they are equal. And it appears to answer that the Sages do not have power to forbid a matter that the Torah explicitly explained as permitted; for the Sages have power to be stringent only in a place where there is neither an explicit prohibition nor an explicit permission by Torah law. This is not so in a place where there is an explicit permission by Torah law. And this is included in their statement: I heard that a court would administer lashes and punishments not according to the law— and not to violate the words of Torah, but to make a fence for the Torah. For it is difficult: is it not obvious that no person has power to violate the words of Torah? And in Choshen Mishpat siman 2 we wrote about this. And according to the premise I mentioned, it is explained better: that here they meant that even to be stringent they have no power where they are overriding the Torah. Therefore Tosafos prove well: since we find a permission in the Torah, certainly the Sages did not forbid it at all for sale; rather, it must be that even by rabbinic law it is permitted, and that is where he did not purchase in order to profit.
[1] 
Or to lend on it. In Terumat HaDeshen siman 200 he inclined regarding the matter and concludes that it is a disgraceful thing to lend on pig hides; and according to what the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that one must be concerned lest he come to eat it, there is also a complete prohibition in lending. There: to feed it to his non-Jewish workers. In the Beit Yosef no reason for this is found, and on the contrary, he wrote in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud: one who purchased pigs for the needs of non-Jewish workers— when he sells them he sells them only for their value. And one must say that there it speaks regarding one who transgressed and purchased. And regarding the reason for the prohibition here, it is according to the Gemara in Avodah Zarah (daf 62): for it was taught: the donkey-drivers who were doing work with fruits of Shevi'is, their wage is Shevi'is. What does it mean “their wage is Shevi'is”? Shall we say that we give them from fruits of Shevi'is? It turns out this one is paying his debt from fruits of Shevi'is, and the Torah said “for eating” and not for commerce. It is clear from here that giving to one’s workers is called commerce; consequently, it is also forbidden to purchase for his workers, because he is paying his debt, which is like commerce. And even though Tosafos there wrote that the reason that it is paying his debt is because he profits with fruits of Shevi'is and we do not call it “for eating,” etc.— here he is not profiting with tamei items, for he purchases them with money; nevertheless, presumably they sell him these items cheaply in contrast to other tahor species; therefore we call it commerce. And it would seem that also when neveilos and treifos came into his house, another Jew should not buy them cheaply and sell them for a higher price to a non-Jew, as Tosafos wrote there in Avodah Zarah: Rabbeinu Yitzchak says that the commerce that is forbidden regarding fruits of Shevi'is is to purchase many at once and bring them from a cheap place to an expensive place, etc. And so they wrote further in the chapter Lulav HaGazul (Sukkah daf 39): and it is possible that one who purchases from his fellow in order to profit and sell for a higher price, that too is commerce; and in the fourth chapter of Shevi'is we learned: one does not engage in commerce with fruits of Shevi'is, etc., and not with neveilos and treifos, etc., and you do not find that they are all equal, etc.— end of their words, as I copied above. And another proof is from what the Tur wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 151 regarding selling an animal, and in Sefer HaTerumah he wrote: even according to this, one should permit only when it came into his hands, but to purchase in order to profit, no. End of his words. But upon precise analysis one finds a permission here: for by Torah law the sale of treifos remains even for us, as the Beit Yosef wrote regarding this, for it is an explicit verse: “or sell to a non-Jew.” And this sale has no difference whether he sells by himself or by another, for we rule at the beginning of the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh: a person’s agent is like himself. And the language of the verse also implies so, for it says “or sell” and it did not say “you shall sell” as it said first “to the convert you shall give it,” to teach that even if the sale is not by your hand but by another, it is permitted. And this is not comparable to Shevi'is and the sale of an animal that we mentioned, for there the buyer is not called the agent of the Jew, since the seller could sell it to the buyer so that he would eat it himself; why should we say that he is his agent to sell to a non-Jew? In contrast, here, regarding the sale of a treifah, where it is impossible to sell it to him except to a non-Jew, it follows that the seller who sells to the buyer is selling to him specifically this matter: that he will be in his place to sell to a non-Jew. And consider: if you do not say so, there will be a loss for one to whom a neveilah came into his hands, and he is not capable of selling to a non-Jew— shall he lose it and not sell it to another who can sell to a non-Jew? That is certainly not so. And there is no difference whether the agent profits or not, for every broker who buys and sells is called an agent. And for this reason, even one who purchases from his fellow Jew a treifah to feed it to his non-Jewish workers, there is no prohibition, for there is no prohibition here regarding this treifah, for he stands in the place of the seller and is his agent; and so is the simple practice, as I have seen. And even though my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote to forbid purchasing in order to profit thereby— what appears to me I have written.

And if tamei [species] came into a hunter’s hands. It is explained in the Beit Yosef that the halacha follows the Sages: that it is specifically a hunter whose livelihood is that that they permitted, but not another person.
[3] 
Provided that he did not intend for that. Here the gloss printed nearby— “and he must sell it immediately,” etc.— belongs, and it was printed there in error, for regarding a rabbinic prohibition it is permitted to engage in commerce in any manner that he wishes; and so it is in the Beit Yosef, in the name of Orchos Chayyim: this was written regarding the first clause. I was asked regarding the large goats in this district; it is the practice of craftsmen not to buy the hides except when they are killed by severing the throat, but not slaughtered, because they become somewhat damaged; therefore one must be in doubt whether a Jew may kill the goats in this manner for this. It appears to me that it is a complete permission. For if we will be concerned for bal tashchis, and like what I wrote at the end of the previous siman in the name of Tosafos that one should not give uncovered water even to a tamei animal— that is not so, for he has benefit from this and there is no bal tashchis, as written in the Smag, negative commandments siman 229, that whenever he has benefit that is greater than the destruction, there is no prohibition of bal tashchis. And so it is in Tosafos, Avodah Zarah (daf 11), s.v. “the main,” etc., that it is permitted to render the animal a treifah. And likewise we find in the chapter HaShochet (Chullin daf 28): one who slaughters and needs the blood may kill by severing the throat or by uprooting; and it is explained there regarding a wild animal, and he needs the blood to dye wool with it. This is exactly like our case. And likewise in the chapter Kisuy HaDam (daf 85), Rabbi permitted R' Chiyya to render the animal a treifah and to slaughter it and to remove its blood and dye wool with it. And if because one does not engage in commerce with neveilos and treifos— that also does not apply here, since in a permitted manner he makes it a neveilah; and it is afterwards as if a neveilah came into his hands in his house, for since he acts first in a permitted manner for his own benefit, afterwards there is no prohibition at all. For the Sages forbade only where he intended from the outset to act in a forbidden manner, such as to trap tamei species; which is not so here. And similarly, in the chapter HaYah Korei (Berachos daf 14), Rav said: one should not begin, and if he began he finishes; Abaye said: we begin, since they begin in the west; and since we began, we also finish. That is, since he began in a permitted manner, even though at the time of beginning he knows that he will finish— and here as well it is so. And I saw in a responsum newly printed, of the Rav R' Binyamin of blessed memory: he too permitted killing the goats by severing the throat, but in the gloss there it is written that that which the Gemara permitted killing by severing the throat or uprooting in order to dye wool is on condition that he afterwards throw the meat away, because one does not engage in commerce with neveilos, and that is a mistake in my view. For if so, it should have said in the Gemara that he should throw the meat to dogs. And furthermore, if so it should have forbidden the killing by severing the throat in the first place, lest he not throw the meat afterwards and he will eat it; for according to the Rashba, whom the Beit Yosef brings, this is the reason that it is forbidden to be involved with neveilos and treifos. Rather, it is simple: anything that he does first in a permitted manner, such as that he needs it for the blood or for the hide, the meat afterwards is as if a neveilah came into his hands, and it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew. But one who does not know the laws of shechitah and wants to sell the meat to a non-Jew, and wants to slaughter in a forbidden manner— that is certainly forbidden, for from the outset he makes it into a neveilah solely for selling the neveilah, and not for any permitted matter. And all of this appears to me clear and simple. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Orchos Chayyim that it is forbidden to smear one’s flesh with pig fat, for smearing is included in drinking; and it appears that we do not rule so, for Tosafos explicitly wrote in the last chapter of Yoma (daf 77) that it is permitted for a person to smear with cheilev, for we do not find ‘smearing is like drinking’ except regarding Yom Kippur, and prohibitions of benefit, since there is an asmachta; but cheilev, which is permitted for benefit, it is permitted. And so wrote in the chapter Bnos Kusim in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. And so wrote the Beit Yosef in siman 123 in the name of the Rashba in a responsum, that it is permitted to smear with pig fat even not in a place of danger. Except that in Orchos Chayyim, end of klal 39, I saw that he wrote that there is no permission for smearing with cheilev or with pig [fat] except where there is pain; but for a healthy person, for pleasure, no; and therefore one should not smear a baby with pig fat, for that is only for pleasure. But one may allow a non-Jew to smear them and to feed them, for we do not say that one must warn adults regarding minors except with respect to smearing them with one’s own hands. End of his words; and so is the primary ruling. And in the Levush he distinguished of his own reasoning to forbid smearing with pig fat but not with other cheilev, and he was not precise in the source of these matters, for there is no distinction between them at all. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that those who lease towns and villages from the minister— meaning, all the income of the minister, and included in this are the pigs in the courtyard— and during the period of the lease, a Jew raises the pigs and feeds them to his workers, they are not doing properly. End of his words. Nevertheless, it appears that one can find a permission for this, since their primary intention at the time of the lease is to lease other things, and this is secondary; there is no prohibition in it. For the Tur wrote at the beginning of this siman: or if he trapped tamei together with tahor— it implies that he intentionally trapped them together, and nevertheless it is permitted. That is because his intention is only on tahor alone, but it is impossible for the tahor to be trapped alone, for they are mixed and tamei are trapped with them. Therefore it is permitted even though he knows initially that it will be so; nevertheless we follow his intention in what he wants, and it is similar to that which is stated in the chapter HaSho'el: what is permitted he wants to acquire, what is forbidden he does not want to acquire. It is like what the Tur wrote in the laws of Pesach regarding a non-Jew who brings chametz to a Jew on the last festival day. Here too is similar. And that which is raised during the period of the lease is like “came into his hands,” and it is permitted to feed them to his non-Jewish workers; and they already practiced so in the lands of Russia and Volhynia, and many, and the Sages did not protest, because they are items belonging to the minister, and the Jew’s name is not upon them and he has no involvement with them; and the non-Jewish workers are the workers of the minister, and they eat from that which belongs to him— so it appears to me to find merit for them in this. It is forbidden to sell a neveilah under the presumption that it is kosher. In the Tur the reason is because of deception, which is forbidden even toward a non-Jew. And in what the Tur wrote: ‘and it appears that even according to those who explain,’ etc., one can ask: behold Tosafos wrote in the chapter Gid HaNasheh (daf 94), s.v. ‘treifah,’ etc., that there is no concern lest a Jew buy from him, for he was concerned that for deception of the non-Jew he said so. End of their words. So too here, why should it be forbidden before a Jew, for the Jew will not buy from the non-Jew, since now he holds that deception is permitted toward him? And why should we not say that those “explainers” also hold this reasoning, and we create a new dispute for no reason between this interpretation and the earlier interpretation regarding this reasoning? And even if you will say that the Tur has proof from the language “there are those who explain” who said “we do not decree lest he give it,” etc., which implies that they do not have that decree— if so, it is difficult upon the explainers themselves: why do they need this, and why do we not say that they were not concerned at all that a Jew would buy from him, for he was concerned to say it because of deception of the non-Jew, since it is permitted? This requires further study.

But if there were two safeks, etc., and both safeks became known, etc. — the Rav copied this from Hagahot Issur VeHeter at the end of the book, who wrote, and these are his words: and the same applies if the two safeks were complete double safek — that is, in one matter and in one substance — nevertheless it is not permitted thereby unless they come at one time; but if the first safek became known first, count that time as certain issur, and when the second safek comes, even without mixtures, there is only one safek, end of his words; and I did not understand these words at all — for since he said "unless they come at one time," this implies that sometimes it is possible they do not come at one time — and how can you find that it becomes known between the first safek and the second safek in double safek when they are in one substance and one matter, such as in the safek "under him she committed adultery" and safek oness mentioned above — for one cannot say the first safek became known before the second became known; and if you wish to say that at first they noticed the first safek and did not notice the second safek, and when they notice it the matter is revealed retroactively that it was never forbidden — behold no new matter was born for us, only knowledge was lacking — and in Rama's words there is somewhat room to force and explain that he says thus: whenever both safeks became known specifically together, this is a sign of double safek that is proper — for in double safek in two substances it is possible to become known at intervals. The general rule emerging in double safek is thus: whenever the first safek is forbidden because it is important and is not nullified in the majority — even though the essence of the issur is rabbinic, such as safek egg born on Yom Tov that afterward became mixed — double safek does not help in it, since the safeks are not in one matter; rather we require that both safeks be through mixtures; and "known at intervals" neither adds nor subtracts anything from double safek. And likewise if there are two safeks whether there is issur here entirely — then it helps in all cases; only there is a distinction in double safek through mixtures — that if it is certain issur and it is important, we require three mixtures, as I wrote in seif 8; but if safek issur became mixed, we require only two mixtures and there is double safek to be lenient:

[0] To write with the right hand. For we expound "and you shall bind" and "and you shall write" — just as binding is with the right, so writing is with the right — end of chapter HaKometz.

And what he wrote "and not with a feather." It is true that in Mordechai chapter 1 of Gittin it is written thus in Hagahah in the name of the Rosh; but I do not know the reason for the matter; and perhaps it is as we find in the order of gittin of Maharil Margoliot who wrote one should not write with an iron reed, for it is like engraving; and even though engraved letters are valid, nevertheless as much as possible to do without any engraving is preferable, etc.; and perhaps so too with a feather that is thin; and so the reason is written in Levush, and it is written that we do not practice thus.

And engraving — since it came to hand, let us say a word: for I heard people saying that printed books do not have sanctity like handwritten ones; and it appears to me there is no difference, for even if we say it is engraving, nevertheless it is like writing; for also regarding a woman's get, engraved letters are valid as written in EH siman 125, even though it is written "and he shall write" and not "and he shall engrave."

And further it appears to me clear proof from chapter Eglah Arufah (Sotah 48a) — those stones of the ephod and breastplate — one does not write them with ink, as it says "engraved like a signet" implies engraving; and one does not scratch them with a knife, as it says "in their fullness" they should be whole without lacking anything; rather one writes on them with ink and shows them shamir from outside on the face of the ink in the groove of the letter and they split open by themselves; and if it were so that printing were engraving, how do we know it was by shamir which is an uncommon thing — perhaps it was by printing with ink like our press, and it would work out well "engraved like a signet"; rather it is clear that printing is called actual writing and not engraving at all.

And again I saw in responsum She'elot to Mahar Benjamin z"l who also wrote that printing is sacred like writing, because engraving too is sacred, and he brought proof from the ephod; but according to my humble opinion we do not need this, for it is actual writing and there is no engraving here — what difference whether one presses the pen on the paper or the paper on the lead letters — either way it is writing. Nevertheless regarding a get certainly one should not do by press since it somewhat resembles engraving; but regarding sanctity of books, whoever is lenient will give account.

Rashal wrote in the last chapter of Gozel siman 37 on what is stated in the Gemara regarding uncovered beverages that were not strained, that it is forbidden to give them to his animal to drink, and Rashi explained the reason — because of danger lest he slaughter it afterward and eat it; and from this they practiced to forbid sheep in which grows a type of scab of nega that are accustomed to die in them; and in days of drought I saw that the community made a ban and forbade all sheep until the plague ceased — end of his words. And one should wonder: behold we said in siman 60 that even if the animal ate its own deadly poison it is kosher, and it implies there is no prohibition at all because of danger, except specifically in eating the deadly poison of a person; and it is possible there is in any case prohibition from the aspect that it was dangerous, as Beit Yosef wrote there in the name of Rashba that it is like dangerous and kosher, and that is according to what Tur wrote at the end of this siman and so in Shulchan Aruch — a person of refined spirit should not eat dangerous food; but it is difficult why he should learn this from what Rashi wrote that there is danger when it drank bad water, namely exposed water — there was danger also for a person because of snake venom; and Maharshal challenged from that which we say in siman 60 that if it drank bad water, namely exposed, it is permitted; and here forbidden according to Rashi mentioned above because of danger. And it appears to me to analyze further: there we say regarding the deadly poison of a person that it is forbidden because of danger — and why is it permitted in bad water, for behold there is deadly poison for a person? And it appears to me that there in those distinctions he lists, namely seizure of blood, etc. — in all of them we see harm because we see illness in it, and as Rashi wrote there that it became ill, and this is proven; for otherwise how would we know that this happened to it? Not so in exposed water — we do not see any illness after drinking, rather there are some hours when it is healthy after drinking as before drinking; and from this we say with certainty that surely a snake did not drink from that exposed water, for whenever there is deadly poison it would certainly not be healthy afterward; and only in that case they said it is permitted. But here in "he should not give it to drink" — the concern is lest he slaughter it immediately after drinking, and it is possible there is danger to life; and this fits Rashi's language that he said "lest he slaughter it afterward," that is after drinking; and the language "afterward" indicates proximity, and this matter is like a bolt and flies away; but Tosafot in the chapter Gozel explained the reason that one should not give an animal exposed water because of bal tashchit; and Maharshal wrote there: from here one who has a bad dog that harms and spoils food, even if it does not bite, may give it deadly poison and he does not violate tza'ar ba'alei chayim, for Tosafot forbade only because of bal tashchit, and here there is none; nevertheless I heard to protest not to put a needle in bread for them, for there is concern lest it go mad, and there is great danger to life — end of his words.

[0] Thirty days for a small animal, etc. Question: one received an iska from his fellow for a sum of money for a time, and after the payment time the recipient withheld the money with him and dealt with them and does not wish to give profit from what accrues after the payment time, because he says after that time the money was a deposit with him since he has no heter iska with him only until the time and afterward it would be ribbis what he gives him. Another question in Maharam Mintz: one accepted on himself three hundred gold coins principal in an iska and to give profit to the document issuer from that iska one thousand gold coins at payment time — principal and profit on Rosh Chodesh Nisan; and if payment is delayed even partially, the signatory accepted from now to deal with that sum withheld with him as that iska and to give profits according to the profit rate above; and afterward payment was delayed some time and the giver came saying he give him profits from the profits — meaning from Rosh Chodesh Nisan it became five hundred gold coins principal and he should give him profits also from the thousand gold coins if the law is with him or not. Answer to the first question: it seems clear the law is with the giver and he is obligated to give him profits also from the business after payment time; and the permission from the outset continues afterward as well until he informs after the time that he does not wish to receive further in the iska those monies; and proof for this from chapter Eizehu Neshekh (69a): the Sages taught — one who places an animal with his fellow until when is he obligated to tend the offspring — thirty days for a small animal and fifty for a large; from here onward he takes half his own and half of half of his fellow's. Rashi: if he wishes to tend them more than this time they assess them in money and what appreciates from here onward the recipient takes half his own share and in the second half which is his fellow's he takes half of the appreciation as he takes with a mother animal half the appreciation. Beit Yosef wrote in siman 177 in name of the Rashba's students: likewise one who takes money to deal for a time and profited and dealt, etc.; and the Rambam wrote chapter 8 of Laws of Partnership: he does not take half of his fellow's share unless he assessed the offspring and stipulated before three; and if he did not assess and stipulate before three — behold he waived and the offspring are divided equally. Tur in this siman did not write "assessed and stipulated" but wrote "and if he comes to divide he must inform his fellow," etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote they do not disagree and Tur cited one and the same applies to the other. Behold before us that in any event the recipient takes from the profit that accrues after the stated time — meaning thirty days or fifty for a large animal — and we do not say he is called iska recipient from his fellow only until that time and from here onward it shall be a deposit in his hand and he shall not take from profit after the time at all, for there is no iska here; perforce all the time the business is unspecified in his hand even though the time ended — nevertheless he withholds the business in his hand with the intent from the outset; except if he informed him — then a new iska is made and he acquires even in half his fellow's share. And Tosafot wrote there that even without any condition he takes half of his fellow's share, as Beit Yosef wrote there in name of the Rashba's students — because since from the outset as an iska he descended in it, meaning for half wages with intent that he tend profit he will always take in this matter — end of his words. Behold before you that the business from the outset was not completed with the time's completion but remained unspecified and even in profit; and further proof from chapter HaUmanin: Rava said — this one who accepted an iska from his fellow and lost and toiled and filled it and did not inform — he cannot say to him "rise from this loss with me." Meaning: you accepted half the loss upon yourself and for wages take a third according to the law of iska recipient from his fellow — and it results he diminishes from the principal; for he said to him therefore you toiled and filled it lest you be called one who ruins an iska. Rashi: you were ashamed — therefore you toiled to fill the principal since you did not inform initially; so too in our case, since from the outset he accepted the money as an iska unspecified he is ashamed to say that after the time he withholds the money with him and does not return them to their owners and their owners lose that they have expense in their homes and no profit and their principal is destroyed; and it is a fortiori — there with one who ruins an iska there is no negligence upon him for Heaven withheld him that he wished to profit willingly — nevertheless he is ashamed in this matter; all the more so here who withholds his fellow's money with him through negligence not according to law after the time — certainly he is ashamed in this and worries they will not give him to others in an iska; and certainly he dealt further for the iska's benefit as initially. And on the second question if there will be on the profit there principal after the time — it is explained in Yerushalmi that Nekudot Yosef brought in Perek Eizehu Neshekh in the plain sense: behold those two partners who did business together, etc.: money — as who divides it — since he does not need assessment, for what he left is as if he took — therefore he may divide by himself and take half his own and half of half his fellow's; and do not say for another partnership that requires assessment; and this is what Yerushalmi reads: a person who gave a hundred dinars to his fellow and he profited twenty dinars — he took for himself ten dinars and the other became principal on him; people do not do so — they give and at the end they divide; even though the law is so that if he comes to divide profit he divides — they do not practice so but carry and give and do not divide profit until the end — end of his words. Behold before you that there is profit on the principal; and this is at the iska time's completion as it implies from Beit Yosef's words that he brought in siman 177 in name of the Rashba's students this custom that the recipient may not take profit from profit; and it was mentioned there that he accepted the money for a time, etc. And behold in the case before us regarding that the giver shall take profit from profit — he mentioned explicitly in the document's wording that from today he accepts upon himself to deal with what will be withheld in his hand even part of the stated payment; and payment was already mentioned there both on principal and on profit; and on this he said that what will be withheld as iska in his hand — it appears in such a case there is no Yerushalmi custom; for the Yerushalmi spoke only of a recipient who has no explicit condition when he will divide and count profit as principal — not so with the giver, since he mentioned explicitly that there shall be considered a new iska in his hand since it is written explicitly even part of the payment.

The Tur brought here in the name of the Rambam: even though Pesach passed, etc. And he wrote on it: I do not understand, etc. And it appears according to my humble opinion to resolve the words of the Rambam with a broad explanation — that is, Rambam explains from that the vow said extra language — that is, until the festival — since after Pesach there is no prohibition, like the Tur's challenge: and why did he mention at all until the festival? And on this there are two answers. The first: he intends she should not benefit until Pesach lest she transgress afterward — and such is the law according to truth. And one may also say that what he said until the festival is a manner of retraction — that is, at first it entered his mind to forbid only until Pesach alone, and therefore he said konam she'at nehenit li until Pesach if you go to your father's house, and it was not in his mind to say anything afterward; and afterward he reconsidered and said until the festival — meaning everything shall be until the festival, both the benefit and the going. And we find this view of retraction also in siman 222. And therefore Rambam wrote that if she benefited and went before Pesach she is lashed, for in this there is certainly prohibition. But after Pesach it is nevertheless a doubt in prohibition, lest there is retraction here, and unspecified vows we follow to be stringent; but one is not lashed from doubt — so it appears correct in my humble opinion. And furthermore it appears to me the reason of the Rambam along with what Rambam wrote — Shulchan Aruch brought it siman 237 at the end of se'if 1: even though the others did not accept the oath, if they fulfilled his words — behold they are praiseworthy for not accustoming him to make a vain oath. And it appears to reconcile Baal HaItur's view correctly, for it appears it was difficult for him from what we say in chapter Kol Sha'ah (Pesachim daf 30) regarding an earthenware vessel — we see they are scraped and the Torah testified on an earthenware vessel that it does not leave its rim forever — Rashi: since they expel liquids from the outer rim they certainly absorbed and no longer expel, for the Torah testified on an earthenware vessel that once it absorbed it no longer expels, for in wood and metal vessels it is written "and he shall pour out and rinse in water," but in earthenware "it shall be broken."
