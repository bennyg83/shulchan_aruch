Magen Avraham, sub-paragraph 14 — It appears to me: if his wife is not with him. I find a slight proof from the sugya in Yoma beginning 30a—“this teaches there is a mitzvah to rinse.” Tosafot there give one answer: because it is forbidden to hold the penis and sparks may spray on his feet—even though in such a case he would have to be married, still it would be forbidden to hold the penis; hence in the bathroom, where it is forbidden with his wife, it is forbidden to hold the penis; the same applies with his niddah wife, etc. Perhaps one can say the Sages used a broad rule [lo plug]: everyone who urinates needs modesty because often one must rinse—that is, on ordinary days when a kohen is fit for service; in any case it must be so, for the Mishnah states this rule was in the Temple, etc., which implies the same for the rest of the year; and we find cases where his wife is with him and she is not a niddah—or [the stringency is] because of lo plug.
