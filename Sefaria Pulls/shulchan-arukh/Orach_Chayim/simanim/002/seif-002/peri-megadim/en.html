“To wear [it] properly.” Taz, in my view: our author had a deep subtlety, as follows. “Careful not to turn [inner outward]” is difficult — from the Torah’s wording one might turn inner to outer and look repulsive, hence the warning — but what exact care applies? It should have said “be careful with his robe to turn it to its proper side,” as Rabbi Yochanan was careful to turn his cloak (the Tur added “properly”). Therefore the Tur cited Rabbi Yochanan and, per Rashi, one “wears it reversed” and must remove several garments and wear properly (Rashi derives this from Rabbi Yosi Shabbat 118b: “They did not see my house beams — my cloak,” two points, as Perishah explains — not mere saintliness for a scholar alone but for everyone; a scholar has an extra stringency when wearing reversed that he removes it). Hence the Tur said “be careful to wear [it] properly,” not “to turn [it] properly,” lest one think the meaning is “remove when wearing reversed” like Rabbi Yochanan’s statement, or that “do not turn” means when undressing not to turn lest one forget and wear wrongly the next day, or other interpretations. By contrast, the Mechaber omitted here Rabbi Yochanan’s dict that should read “be careful with his robe to turn it to its proper side.” True, in Choshen Mishpat 182:21 the author cited the tenant who is careful to turn his cloak and did not finish per Rashi “wears it reversed”; still Rashi holds so — “see Orach Chayim” here and there — his words need study in what he wrote; only a distinction in outward appearance applies.
