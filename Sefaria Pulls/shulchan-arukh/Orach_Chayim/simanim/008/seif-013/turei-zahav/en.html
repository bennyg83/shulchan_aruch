“Walking” from home to synagogue is interruption — so wrote Beit Yosef by way of explanation on Rosh and Tur: one who regularly wraps in a large tallis in synagogue must bless because walking is interruption. One could ask from Tosafos “Who says” 49b who wrote: here netilas yadayim is immediately after blessing, and the measure of “immediate” may be inferred from closeness of leaning to slaughter — “all leanings there I call immediate to leaning except that at Nikanor gate,” etc., meaning from Nikanor gate to slaughterhouse is not “immediate,” and Middos implies it was 22 cubits — hence walking itself is not interruption, only the delay for that walk’s duration; for if walking itself were interruption, how derive from there the delay between washing and blessing — perhaps delay does nothing even longer than that? The case at Nikanor gate was interruption by the act of walking — unless every delay less than that duration, even while walking that distance, is not interruption — hence for tzitzis blessing we are not concerned with delay however long if he did not interrupt with speech — otherwise we should consider blessing immediately before every mitzvah act — certainly walking to synagogue is not interruption by walking alone — as Tosafos brought by Beit Yosef in this siman: walking is not interruption at all, only speech — so Beit Yosef below siman 104 citing Ri: if a snake is coiled on his heel he may walk to throw it off — we find nowhere that walking is called interruption. Therefore it appears to me the Tur and Rosh required blessing again on the large tallis in synagogue because when he blessed on the tallis katan at home he immediately put on tefillin afterward as Shulchan Aruch siman 25 s. 2, then in synagogue donned tallis gadol — presumably Rosh wrote similarly — thus the tefillin blessing interrupted between tallis katan and gadol, not the walk to synagogue — so it appears to me.
