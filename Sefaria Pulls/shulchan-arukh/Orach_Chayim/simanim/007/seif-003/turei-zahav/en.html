“One must bless twice,” etc. So too the Beit Yosef in the name of the great Rav Mahari Ashkenazi (Isserlein), in the name of Achron in the name of the Gaon — he derives it from our finding regarding prayer: if one erred and did not pray Shacharis one prays Mincha twice. At first glance this is difficult: prayer substitutes for a sacrifice and compensation (tashlum) applies; but Asher Yatzar is a blessing of thanks — one thanks once for many times, like one who ate, diverted attention, and ate again — one needs Birkas HaMazon only once, as in siman 178. Because of this difficulty Maharshal wrote that he asked Maharsha of Lublin and he said on this ruling “I did not hear” — meaning he did not agree — therefore he ruled not to decide like this clause; I greatly wondered how it is fit to disagree with a Gaon when Taz Choshen Mishpat 25 wrote one does not disagree with a Gaon — especially when the great Rav Mahari Ashkenazi brought it in the name of Achron and the Beit Yosef fixed it in the Shulchan Aruch. The above difficulty does not seem to me a difficulty: certainly Birkas HaMazon when one blesses once — either way it suffices: if the food already digested in his intestines i.e. he is hungry to eat again — surely the obligation of the first meal’s Birkas HaMazon has passed and there is no compensation for it — it is like Musaf: even if one erred and did not pray it one does not pray Mincha twice, as stated at the beginning of Tefillas HaShachar in Tosafos — because those verses of Musaf correspond to its offering and compensation does not apply — and similarly Birkas HaMazon is to thank for what he ate; if it already digested, for what will he thank and say “you ate and were satisfied” when he is already hungry again? Therefore one cannot say he should bless Birkas HaMazon twice unless it has not yet digested — but truly even then one should not act thus, for Birkas HaMazon has no fixed time until digestion — it depends on him: as long as he wishes to eat he may eat and he has no obligation of Birkas HaMazon yet if he wishes to eat again — therefore when he begins eating a second time it is as if he ate everything at once since the satiation of the first eating still stands — why bless Birkas HaMazon twice? Not so Asher Yatzar: as soon as he finished passing water once the obligation to say Asher Yatzar applies — it does not depend on him to say “I will pass water again” — therefore each relieving [hatzas mayim] counts as its own act and one cannot treat everything as a single occasion, since further relief is not entirely in his discretion — as stated explicitly in Rosh brought in Beit Yosef Yoreh De’ah siman 19: specifically Birkas HaMazon, which is praise to Hashem, one thanks once for two meals; but the act of covering is finishing one slaughter before beginning another — end quote. So too here the act ended after the first relieving and then there is an obligation of blessing; afterward he became obligated again since one can “compensate” also the first — therefore he must bless twice and say Asher Yatzar, and we do not say one Asher Yatzar counts for both here and there as with prayer — no single prayer counts both for itself and for compensation — so too Asher Yatzar: one blessing cannot count for both since there are two distinct obligations upon him — clear and correct like the Shulchan Aruch’s words.
