possible," etc. The Rabbi wrote this from his reasoning per his words in Darkei Moshe; and I wonder, since they stipulated from the outset that he must return and give it to him; and likewise where obligated to return by custom which is as explicit stipulation — if so this is not a sale and forbidden to take interest, as Rashba wrote in a responsum and the Rabbi brought below siman 174; and likewise explicit in Mordechai that it is permitted only because since he is not obligated to return collateral it is a sale, as he wrote that by default he intended for permission even without explicitly stating so his intent was complete removal, that if he wishes he need not return collateral at all but relies he will return and allow redemption and accept principal and interest, etc.; and likewise in Tosafot siman 303: in actual stipulation one cannot find remedy to act permissibly; but in assurance and trust one can act permissibly, etc.; and even though Darkei Moshe wrote Tosafot discusses only where he fixed interest as below — it is not so, for examining Tosafot shows no distinction between fixed and unfixed; and likewise Beit Yosef that complete trust in a complete condition is forbidden as stated explicitly in the Gemara regarding one who sold a house and said when I have money return it — forbidden until here; and explained below simanim 172 and 174. But in truth it seems the custom is simply to lend on non-Jewish collaterals and lenders return collaterals to the Jew — they do so willingly as if returned and sold by default, but not obligated; and even if obligated, because they relied on Rosh perek Kol Sha'ah in the name of Baal HaItur that a non-Jew's collateral in a Jew's hand with interest — permitted to pledge to another Jew at that interest even without presenting before the non-Jew, and we do not say the Jew takes interest, for he did not acquire except where he said from now — end quote; and Beit Yosef brought it; and from Rosh's words there it implies agreement with Baal HaItur; and likewise in Ramzim there; and therefore it discusses where he does not sell to him and the second Jew is obligated to return to the first to redeem — stipulated thus or custom; but if complete sale or pledging by default as one who said I sell to you — by default intends for permission — even if non-Jew said acquire from now likewise permitted; only truly the Jew's; permitted to sell to Jew and Jew returns and sells as below; or discusses only collateral in second Jew's hand and nevertheless permitted since non-Jew did not say acquire from now unless redeemed by set time — behold in non-Jew's possession; and since the second lending Jew's primary reliance is on collateral, the ribbit he takes is truly from non-Jewish collateral; and so Darkei Moshe seif 92: Rosh perek Kol Sha'ah discusses as explained — "and it seems Rosh follows his reasoning who wrote in responsum he need not sell but hands it over," etc.; therefore forbidden when saying from now; but Rabbeinu Tam who permitted because he sells — even in such case permitted — two sales — it seems to me — end quote. And now it is resolved what Rosh wrote perek Avodah Zarah that it is permitted only when the Jew sells collateral to the second Jew — unlike Beit Yosef and Bach who understood the rulings contradict; rather Avodah Zarah discusses collateral in the Jew's possession, such as if he said acquire from now or the Jew sees the non-Jew intends to remove it; and likewise Rosh in responsum permitted pledging collateral from the non-Jew in his hand to a Jew even if he does not sell but hands over on condition to take interest from the non-Jew — does not contradict what he wrote in his rulings, for there the reason since he said take interest from the non-Jew he automatically departs from collateral and therefore need not explicitly say he sold it — see there klal 108 siman 4 explained thus; and as Tur wrote Rosh's responsum does not contradict his rulings but is a novel law unlike Beit Yosef and Bach and other Acharonim. However all this when collateral is in Jew's possession — said acquire from now or sees intent to remove or custom after a year authority to sell — otherwise obviously permitted in every manner; and what most poskim and Mechaber wrote requiring the Jew explicitly say I sell it to you (unlike Mordechai) — also discusses if in Jew's possession (and see responsum Maharam Prague siman 328). And now we hold like most poskim, except our custom follows Baal HaItur and Rosh perek Kol Sha'ah whom none disagree with thus — it seems clear to me.
