Avodat HaTalmid; and see responsum Mahari"t siman 71: wine that the vineyard owner himself made through a Jew, and it is in the vineyard owner's possession — with two seals or key and seal; if he hires Jews to guard in purity and gave the wages of their guarding — there is room to be lenient, since he spends on wine purity he fears loss of his expenses; and behold it is like one who buys from other vineyards; and even though Rabeinu Tam permitted only when he acquired and toiled to bring it — nevertheless one may combine this to be lenient with two seals even for drinking; and see there regarding guards who deposited the key in the gentile's own hand, only they sealed the key with their seal — so that if some repair is needed in the wine the Jew would find peddlers who return, who would open the seal and stand until they repair and return and seal with their seal; and this is a bad and inferior custom; and especially in lands where apostates are common who already have seals; therefore if the key is not yet sealed with the first seal one may not permit; but if still sealed with the seal the first guard affixed — if the seal they affixed on the key is itself the seal on the wine — there is still concern this is not havla'ah ve-chatumah; for even a hundred sealings count as one, since all are one seal pattern. And nevertheless, if there is reason to fear the vineyard owner will sue the man who purifies wine, and there are court expenses — the young Torah scholar did not command him regarding guarding as required by law; and also there would be, Heaven forbid, desecration of Torah honor — one may be lenient when the first guard himself goes and recognizes his seal on the wine; and rely on one opinion in siman 118 that one seal suffices if he recognizes his seal — as above; and all this for temporary ruling due to the pressure mentioned; but in coming years, if the Jew wishes to send to purify his wine, he should know that at the time of making the wine he must sit and guard, and it does not suffice if they seal it or seal the room; and after the wine is made the wine should not remain in the vineyard owner's possession — rather he should rent him a cellar with another Jew; and the Jewish guard should deliver it to that Jew's possession with seal and key, or with two seals; and stipulate explicitly with the Jew owner of the cellar that he will not let the Jew vineyard owner go there and entice the seal without permission of the Jewish guard; and when he wishes to take it to other places to sell to Jews — the Jew should not leave it with the wine owner; only hire another Jew to take it; and likewise the Jew should stand that he hire that carrier and seal it as required with two seals — each seal in different letters — see there.
