(סימן קכ"ד ש"ך סק"י) אבל לפענ"ד דין זה ברור. לענ"ד נקט המחבר יש מי שאומר דבאמת נראה דעת הרמב"ן הובא בנ"י (פ' החולץ דף י"ח ע"ב) דס"ל דהא דאמרי' מעוברת שנתגיירה בנה א"צ טבילה היינו דמ"מ צדיך מילה. וא"כ קודם שמל אוסר במגעו. אבל לדעת הרא"ה שם וזהו שיטת תוספות ביבמות דא"צ מילה באמת הדין דהוי כישראל ע"ש. ובסימן רס"ח סתם המחבר דבנה א"צ טבילה ולא הזכיר כלל אם צריך מילה וגם האחרונים שם לא זכרו מזה כלום:
**** ENGLISH ****(Siman 124 Shach seif yud) But in my humble opinion this law is clear. In my humble opinion the author stated "there are those who say" — for it appears the view of Ramban brought in Nekudat HaKesef (perek HaChalitzah daf 18 side 2) holds that what we say regarding a pregnant woman who converted, her son needs no immersion — means nevertheless circumcision is required; and if so before he is circumcised he forbids through his touch. But according to Ra'ah there and this is Tosafot's approach in Yevamot — he needs no circumcision in actual law, being like an Israelite — see there. And in siman 268 the author stated simply that the son needs no immersion and did not mention at all whether circumcision is required; and the later authorities there also did not mention this at all.

(ט"ז סק"ד) מ"מ כאן נחית דרגא. מבואר דס"ל דהך דינא דעובד כוכבים שאינו עובד עבודת כוכבים דאסור בשתיה ומותר בהנאה הוא לכ"ע. דאף הרא"ש מודה בזה דגרע מגר תושב וכן משמע בערובין דהוא מלתא דלית ביה פלוגתא. ולכאורה החילוק דבגר תושב קיבל עליו ז' מצות. אולם לפ"ז יפלא דא"כ מאי פריך הש"ס והתניא איזה גר תושב ונדחק לאוקמי להחיותו ולא מוקי בפשטא לענין שיהא מגעו מותר בשתיה דהא מיניה מיירי הברייתא דקתני עלה מיחדין אצלו יין דהטעם דלא חיישינן למגעו משום דמגעו מותר בשתיה כמ"ש הרא"ש וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Taz s.k. 4) Nevertheless here one descends a level — it is explained that he holds that the law of a non-Jew who is not an actual idolater, that he is forbidden for drinking and permitted for benefit, is accepted by all, for even the Rosh agrees on this, that his status is inferior to a ger toshav; and so it implies in Eruvin that it is a matter without dispute. And apparently the distinction is that a ger toshav accepted the seven commandments. However according to this it would be surprising: why then does the Talmud challenge from "who is a ger toshav?" and forces a difficult explanation — it should explain plainly regarding the law of being permitted for drinking, for that is what the baraita deals with, which teaches regarding it that one may leave wine alone with him — the reason being that we are not concerned about his touch, since his touch is permitted for drinking, as the Rosh wrote. And requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****(Taz seif kaf 7) And it would be like touching all of it. At first glance from Rashba's and Ran's words brought in Taz (seif kaf 8) it appears only through the law of stream; and if so there is a difficulty on the author who ruled all forbidden in benefit; for according to the author's ruling (siman 126 seif 7) stream even through touch of a non-Jew is not like touching all of it but only as though the spurting mixed — and according to Rashba's approach; and accordingly stream for stam yeinam is permitted in benefit, for even when mixed it is permitted to sell except the value of forbidden benefit; and as Rashba explicitly wrote brought in Beit Yosef there; and if so here the law would be permitted in benefit. And perforce the author holds it is worse than stream, being like touching all of it actually. I again saw in Nekudat HaKesef (seif kaf 133) who wrote thus explicitly: what Ran wrote that it is like touching all of it means through the law of stream; and the reason Pri Megadim ruled that we establish in Pri Megadim letter aleph stream is not connection — see there; and if so there is a difficulty on Shulchan Aruch in what he wrote all forbidden in benefit — and likewise as stated.

**** ENGLISH ****(Taz seif kaf 12) And Shulchan Aruch did not mention here anything of this. Otherwise it would not be possible for the author, for he ruled in seif 9 like Rif and Rambam that measuring by hand is forbidden in benefit. And also on Rama one may say: regarding actual touch by hand, since there are two reasons to be stringent — first the Rosh's approach that saving wine is not called occupation; second that measuring by hand is forbidden in benefit — therefore they did not want to be lenient in such a case except in measuring by hand in seif 9 where there is certainly occupation, he relied on poskim who permit in benefit; and likewise resting his hand on a spigot in seif 23 he relied on the reasoning that rescue is occupation, that in such a case even for poskim who forbid measuring by hand one may say it is permitted since he did not actually touch, as Shach wrote there seif kaf 65. But regarding touching with his hand to rely on occupation of rescue — there are two weaknesses to be stringent, in my humble opinion.

(ב"ד) דהא הרמב"ם. הוא תמוה דלהרמב"ם דאוסר בכחו בהנאה באמת גם בעובדי כוכבים שלנו אסור. אבל מ"מ לדידן דקי"ל דכחו מותר בהנאה. וקי"ל בהא כהרמב"ם דנחית חד דרגא א"כ בעובדי כוכבים שלנו מותר בשתיה וכעין מ"ש הב"ח לענין מדדו הובא בט"ז (ס"ק ל"א) וכ"כ להדיא בש"ך (סימן קכ"ה סק"ב) וביותד נלע"ד דאף להרמב"ם יש להקל דהא במגעו בדבר אחר בכוונה דאסור בהנאה מדינא אעפ"כ בהעובד כוכבים דידן מותר דהוי מגעו ע"י ד"א שלא בכוונת מגע א"כ ה"נ בכחו הוי כחו שלא בכוונה דשרי:
**** ENGLISH ****(Letter dalet) For Rambam. It is astonishing, for according to Rambam who forbids through force in benefit — truly also regarding our non-Jews it is forbidden; but nevertheless for us who establish force is permitted in benefit; and we establish like Rambam we descend one level — if so regarding our non-Jews permitted for drinking; and similar to what Bach wrote regarding measuring, brought in Taz (seif kaf 31); and likewise explicitly in Shach (siman 125 seif kaf 2); and more in my humble opinion that even for Rambam one may be lenient, for in his touch in another thing with intention that is forbidden in benefit by law — nevertheless regarding our non-Jew it is permitted, being his touch through something else without intention of touch; if so here too in force it is force unintentionally that is permitted.
