**** ENGLISH ****
One must be careful, etc. In the Tur he concluded with this: and if he does not touch with his hand but with a knife it is permitted since he intends only to seal the vessel and not to touch wine ; being like unintentional touch through something else; and Beit Yosef challenged: behold here there is in any case intent of touch; and it is difficult, for according to Rabbenu Tam whom the Tur holds like ; unintentional touch through something else permitted even for drinking ; namely even if he intended touch but does not know it is wine permitted; likewise here where he intends to seal the vessel it is like not knowing there is wine there, for he has no business with wine; and do not challenge from the mishnah that said measuring with a reed ; sell, for there in any case his main business is with wine. And it appears to explain the Tur's words in what he first wrote "an Israelite should take the oakum," etc.; and Beit Yosef challenged: if so this is the latter part of drinking much wine; and further why should the non-Jew not give the oakum; and it appears: even though we find unintentional touch through something else permitted for drinking ; namely b'di'eved; but l'chatchila it is not fit to do this way ; therefore the Tur comes to inform us a stringency in the first part and in the latter part: in the first part where there is not much wine ; it informs us even so l'chatchila the Israelite should give oakum; except if there is much wine there is stringency that extra guarding is needed lest he touch wine ; unlike in the first part where extra warning is not needed on this; but nevertheless also in the latter part if he has extra guarding that he does not touch wine ; permitted even l'chatchila if the non-Jew does not give oakum but Israel does as in the first part; but b'di'eved permitted even in the latter part if the non-Jew gives oakum ; being unintentional touch through something else; except it is still difficult for me what he wrote regarding the mishnah sprinkling the hornet with a reed forbidden for drinking and not called his touch through something else ; behold there he has no need for wine at all except to sprinkle the hornet, and nevertheless since impossible except that he will touch wine forbidden; likewise here sealing the hole with oakum of flax ; since impossible except he will touch wine with the knife ; for the flax is soaked in wine as it seals the spurting; and one may say flax is not called wine in what is absorbed inside, for if so why need a knife ; let it suffice being something else through the flax; rather certainly flax is like wine since it is absorbed and soiled in wine; and Beit Yosef resolved the challenge from what is written here from measuring with a reed ; sell ; with two answers, namely in the first answer here one may be lenient since he only touches what drips ; and it is difficult what difference much wine little wine; and as he himself wrote in siman 126 in "there are those who say" regarding stream, etc. ; what difference pouring into much wine, etc.; and the second answer is obvious it does not help this difficulty from hornet, for also regarding hornet he intends to drive wine from the hornet and is very similar to sealing the hole to drive spurting water from there; and one must distinguish: hornet is different, for nevertheless he touches clear wine in a place where libation applies ; unlike here where he touches knife to oakum even though absorbed in wine nevertheless libation does not apply there at all. And further one may say since what goes outside goes to loss there is no concern in what goes out as Mordechai wrote perek R' Yishmael ; perforce also what is absorbed in flax is like spurting going to loss; nevertheless l'chatchila there is prohibition to leave the non-Jew at the spurting ; therefore he must tighten with knife specifically.

<b>אלא באומדנות המוכיחות. </b>דהיינו כעין מעשה דת"ה שהלכו עובדי כוכבים לפני הגיגית עם תירוש ולחשו זה עם זה ואח"כ טבל א' אצבעו בתירוש ומץ ממנו בפיו והלכו להם ופיהם מלא שחוק ורצו יהודים אחריהם ואמרו למה קלקלתם יין שלנו השיבו לא ידענו שיהא אסור בשביל זה והרגישו יהודים מתוך הלחש שכוונתם היה לנסך לקלקל והתיר שם דהוי כמו להכעיס דהכא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only where there are compelling indications.</b> This refers to a case like the incident in the Terumas HaDeshen, where non-Jews walked before a vat of grape juice and whispered to one another. Afterward, one of them dipped his finger into the grape juice and sucked it into his mouth, and they departed with their mouths full of laughter. Jews pursued them and asked, “Why did you ruin our wine?” They answered, “We did not know that it would become forbidden because of this.” The Jews understood from their whispering that their intention had been to pour it as a libation and ruin it. He permitted the wine there, for this is comparable to acting merely to provoke, as in the present case.
