<b>שאינו מזכיר עבודת כוכבים כו'. </b>בטור כתב דמיקרי גדול שיודע בטיב עבודת כוכבים ומשמשיה דהיינו כל שזוכר ומזכיר בפיו עבודת כוכבים ומשמשיה והוא ע"פ התוספתא הביאה תה"ה רק ששם לא הוזכר תיבת בפיו ופי' בפרישה דזוכר קאי על המחשבה ול"נ דבפיו קאי אתרוייהו והוא ע"פ שמצינו לשון כזה בפ"ק דכתובות כל המנבל את פיו ומוציא דבר נבלה מפיו אפי' אם נגזר עליו כו' נראה פירושו דבא למעט שלפעמים מדבר ניבול פה בלא כיוון להרע ע"כ הזכיר בכפל לשון שמוציא דבר נבלה מפיו בכוונה וברצון וה"נ הוי כיון שאפשר שזוכר כן שם עבודת כוכבים בלי כוונה לרעה ע"כ אמר שמזכיר להורות שבכוונה ורצון מזכיר זה ולשון מזכיר פירושו שנותן לב להזכיר וע"כ לא נקט בש"ע אלא ומזכיר לחוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Who does not mention idolatry, etc.</b> The Tur wrote that one is considered an adult when he understands the nature of idolatry and its attendants, meaning anyone who remembers and mentions verbally idolatry and its attendants. This is based on the Tosefta cited by the Terumas HaDeshen, except that there the word “verbally” is not mentioned. The Prishah explained that “remembers” refers to thought. It appears to me, however, that “verbally” refers to both expressions. This accords with the wording found in the first chapter of Kesubos: “Whoever sullies his mouth and utters a shameful matter from his mouth, even if a decree was issued against him,” etc. Its meaning appears to be that it excludes one who at times speaks obscenely without intending evil. Therefore, it uses a doubled expression, that he utters a shameful matter from his mouth intentionally and willingly. Here too, since it is possible that he remembers the name of idolatry without an evil intention, it therefore says that he “mentions” it, to indicate that he mentions it intentionally and willingly. The expression “mentions” means that he directs his attention to mention it. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch states only “and mentions.”

And there are those who are lenient even in touch of a ger toshav. This is the Rosh who wrote that even though regarding a purchased slave from idolaters who circumcised and did not immerse he makes stam yeinam, as appears in seif 3 ; there it is different since his heart is not for Heaven but he becomes Jewish from fear of his master; and in the Tur this distinction is explained according to Rabbenu Tam who rules regarding a slave who circumcised and immersed that we still require twelve months ; he wrote on this: and specifically when he does not convert willingly; but if he converted willingly immediately after he circumcised and immersed, etc.; and he does not want to distinguish regarding the slave himself whether he converts willingly or against his will, for all willingly is also against his will since his heart is not whole in the matter; and likewise explicitly in Tosafot perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 57) that you do not circumcise a slave against his will except nevertheless fear of his master is upon him; and it appears a word is missing in the Tur and it must read: and specifically a slave who does not convert, etc.; and such is the text in Mordechai ; the essence of the distinction is between slave and other convert; but regarding the slave itself there is no distinction at all here; and if he converts explicitly against his will it does not help at all, as we establish siman 267; and Rashal wrote that Karaites have the law of a ger toshav and it is permitted to drink with them wine of Israel.

therefore whoever is not an idolater there is no prohibition of benefit here; and according to "there are those who are lenient" that Rama brought there ; nevertheless here we descend a level and at minimum permitted in benefit; and in the Tur he wrote in the name of Rashba that one should be lenient regarding Ishmaelites since they do not worship avodah zarah; and Derishah challenged from what Beit Yosef wrote above in this siman in the name of Rashba in this language ; and he expanded to resolve ; and there is no difficulty at all, for there it means whether according to R' Meir if he accepted not to worship avodah zarah or according to the Sages if he accepted seven commandments ; and what difficulty at all, for Rav Yehuda deals where the entire nation does not worship avodah zarah, and if so the baraita also holds that when one accepts not to worship avodah zarah and the entire nation is so ; R' Meir agrees that suffices; but if the nation worships avodah zarah ; R' Meir and the rabbis disagree; and it is difficult to resolve.

A vessel that has spigots, etc. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba's responsum: the making of this vessel is called in the Gemara kani shekiz and I did not know what it is; nevertheless what is in its mouth is forbidden and all the wine is drawn after it ; why should it be less than stream connection, etc. ; end; and in Mishmeret HaBayit daf 134 Rashba also wrote on this that the form of kani shekiz was not clarified for us and one cannot permit on it through stream; and in any case it is unlike Rashi's explanation; and at the end he wrote that kani shekiz was in a manner where there was no stream connection; and Ran wrote in the name of Raavad to resolve that we do not say stream connection except when everything came out through that opening ; which is not so here, for it comes out through the other hole where the non-Jew drinks; and in my humble opinion to resolve: all as long as he does not stir there is no prohibition, and all the time he drinks he does not stir but draws to himself; only when he stops and the wine slips from his mouth he stirs below ; therefore there is no prohibition as long as the non-Jew did not stop; and in that vessel wine comes by itself into the reeds not through sucking ; for if through sucking it would be the law of a spigot that I mentioned in siman 124 seif 24 in the name of Maharil; and for practical halachah it appears since this was not clarified for Rashba and he wrote that Raavad also was challenged on this ; we have no leniency to learn from this vessel, and one should not drink with the non-Jew in any vessel.

<b>And they brought him up from there dead.</b> The wording implies that he died before being brought up. However, if he was alive when brought up and then died immediately afterward, we are concerned that at that moment he offered praise to his deity, as stated in the Gemara, that it is regarded by him like their festival day. Rashi explains: “He goes and gives thanks for having been saved, and presumably he poured it as a libation while being brought up.” End quote. It appears that the Gemara needed this reasoning and did not simply say that he presumably poured it as a libation like other non-Jews, since at that point he was no longer under duress. Rather, it is discussing even a case in which at that moment he was dying and in mortal danger; nevertheless, he gives thanks and praise that he was saved from immediate death. According to this, what the Tur wrote is clear: “Therefore, a minor who does not understand the nature of idolatry and its attendants, or an adult who fell into a pit and came up dead, the wine is permitted for benefit.” The Beit Yosef explained that the Tur repeated the matters, first stating the rule generally and then giving a detail for each case. Regarding the minor, he gave the detail that he does not understand the nature of idolatry; regarding one who fell into a pit, the Beit Yosef did not know what new detail was given and said that it was mentioned incidentally together with the others. According to what I wrote, however, it is clear. The Tur teaches that it is specifically when he came up dead, meaning that he was dead at the time he was brought up, and not otherwise. For had he died afterward, the Tur should have said, “he came up and died.” A parallel appears in the variant readings in the chapter Eilu Tereifos regarding worms, whether the text reads “it separated dead” or “it separated and died,” as the Beit Yosef cites above in siman 84. This is what the Tur teaches, that only when he was dead at the time he was brought up is the wine permitted for benefit. This appears to me correct. So too it appears from the words of the lengthy Terumas HaDeshen cited by the Beit Yosef, who wrote that the same applies to any other compulsion that does not involve imminent death, which is considered compulsion. The Gemara teaches that one should not say that since he fell into a pit and was close to death, he is panicked, and even if he came up alive he would not turn his attention to pour the wine as a libation. It therefore teaches that once he was saved, he turns his attention and gives thanks, etc.
