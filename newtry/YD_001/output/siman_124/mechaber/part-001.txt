The touch of a non-Jew in wine by means of another item, unintentionally — such as if he was descending from a palm tree and had a lulav in his hand, and he touched the wine with the top of the lulav unintentionally; or if he was walking and touched it with the corner of his garment unintentionally; and likewise if he intended to touch it by means of another item but does not know that it is wine — it is permitted even for drinking. {Rama: And the same law applies if he inserted a spigot into a barrel or removed it unintentionally, as was explained. And in our times, since the nations are not idol-worshippers, any touch of theirs is called “unintentional” (Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael; Hagahot Ashiri; and Mahariv in the laws, siman 15). Therefore, if he touched wine by means of another item, even though he knows it is wine and intended to touch it, it is permitted even for drinking, for it is called “touch by means of another item, unintentionally.” And the same law applies if he touched even with his hand without intent of touching, or he did not know it was wine — it is permitted; and one should not publicize the matter to an am ha’aretz. Wine that was placed in a public domain, and non-Jews came and stabbed there with a knife to remove wine: what came out outside is forbidden, and what is inside is permitted; since the non-Jew intended only to puncture the barrel, and did not intend to touch the wine — even if he touched the wine, it is touch of a non-Jew by means of another item unintentionally, and is permitted (a responsum of R. Shmuel in Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael).}
