אסור לאכול וכו' הרמב"ם כתב דהוי איסור תורה רק אין לוקין משום דהוי לאו שבכללות ורש"י חולין דף קכ"א כתב דהוא מדרבנן ואסמכתא בעלמא מקרא וצריך טעם מנ"ל לרש"י זה דבגמ' משמע דהוא דאורייתא ואפשר ס"ל לרש"י כיון דאמרינן שם חולין דחותכו מבית שחיטה תיכף קודם צאת נפשה וממתין לה עד שתצא נפשה וקשה איך התירו חכמים לחתוך תיכף דלמא משתלי ואכל קודם שמתה הבהמה וקעבד איסורא דאורייתא א"ו דרבנן היא ולא חשו חכמים לכך כולי האי:
**** ENGLISH ****
Forbidden to eat, etc. — Rambam wrote it is Torah prohibition; only one is not lashed because it is a negative commandment included in a generalization. Rashi, Chullin daf 121, wrote it is rabbinic and the verse is mere support. A reason is needed: whence for Rashi? In the Gemara it implies d'oraisa; and possibly Rashi holds: since we say there, Chullin: one cuts it from the slaughter-house immediately before its soul departs and waits until its soul departs — it is difficult how the Sages permitted cutting immediately, lest he forget and eat before the animal died and commit a d'oraisa prohibition; or it is rabbinic and the Sages were not concerned so much.

Until, etc. — we say in the Gemara, Chullin 121: Israel who slaughtered a tamei animal for a non-Jew — he slaughtered it and it convulses; flesh and limb separating from it are eiver min hachai and forbidden to a son of Noah even after its soul departs. One who wishes to heal his body may eat from an animal before its soul departs — he cuts an olive's bulk of flesh from the slaughter-house and waits until its soul departs; one non-Jew and one Israel are permitted in it. And Rashi: in tamei, where there is no shechitah — all this is before its soul departs, eiver min hachai; if you ask regarding tahor, where there is shechitah — permitted even while convulsing; and since permitted for Israel, also for a non-Jew permitted. And on daf 33: R' Avahu said: we invite Israel for intestines, for they are subject to shechitah and shechitah permits; we do not invite a non-Jew, for they are not subject to shechitah — and it is eiver min hachai. R' Pinchas said: I would sit and seek: is there anything permitted for Israel and forbidden for a non-Jew? And I would not say, for he stated the reason: it teaches not like R' Eliezer bar Yose: one non-Jew and one Israel permitted. From this Rashba ruled: tahor convulsing — even for a non-Jew permitted; and he wondered on Rambam, who ruled in Hilchot Melachim that convulsing is forbidden to a son of Noah even in tahor animal — and it requires study. And what can be said: Rambam holds: since it teaches not like R' Eliezer bar Yose, and does not say a refutation to R' Avahu, who is against the baraita — and in Tosafot, s.v. echad nochri, they wrote explicitly: R' Levi does not hold this principle — is there anything like R' Avahu? And presumably R' Levi would not dispute a baraita unless he has support from another baraita; and one must say the Gemara knew there is a baraita like him — only the Gemara's intent is that this of R' Avahu is not the subject, and the baraita teaches not like him. And behold one must know in what these baraitot dispute, and which is better in their division. It appears Tosafot wrote "and not the reason he said" — meaning "is there anything," etc.; and Tosafot challenged: Israel is subject to shechitah and a non-Jew is not subject to shechitah, as we say regarding yefat to'ar — see there. And it appears that which we say "is there anything permitted for Israel," etc. — can be explained in two ways: either Israel was sanctified with extra commandments — how is it possible they had permission and a non-Jew prohibition — with this Tosafot's explanation fits well: they are subject to shechitah, etc. However there is another way: the essence warning we do not find explicitly for a son of Noah — only hinted and accepted in tradition that the warning stated for Israel applies to him; with this it is properly difficult: in Israel itself permitted, and the inclusive warning severity — how do you forbid for a son of Noah? And if you resolve: one warning for two explanations — permitted for this and forbidden for that — with this Tosafot's explanation "not subject to shechitah" does not help, for after all one cannot divide one warning into two explanations. And they said in Sanhedrin, chapter 7: seven commandments were commanded to Adam haRishon, as it says: and He commanded — you may surely eat, and not eiver min hachai; and Rambam in Hilchot Melachim ruled six commandments were commanded to Adam, and Noah added eiver min hachai, as written: only flesh with its soul, etc.; and they wrote explicitly: Rambam holds that statement of Adam haRishon — flesh was not permitted to him; he disputes that eiver min hachai exists in reality; and Tosafot's words are strained for me. And this is certainly that tanna who derives eating eiver min hachai from "you may surely eat" — there prohibition of eiver min hachai is not explained, only a mere hint; the hint in it is prohibition of eating eiver min hachai, whose prohibition is stated in the Torah for Israel; if so, this baraita that disputes R' Avahu and holds even tahor convulsing is permitted to a son of Noah — that is, it holds like this baraita: "you may surely eat" — this is eiver min hachai commanded to Adam; and its explanation is on the details of the prohibition stated for Israel; if so, it is properly difficult "is there anything," etc. — how do you divide one prohibition into two explanations: for Israel convulsing is not included, and for a son of Noah it is included; and Tosafot's words "not subject to shechitah" do not apply, for we do not judge the reason of the matter — only that in any case one verse cannot bear divided explanations. However R' Avahu holds: in truth Adam was not commanded — only Noah; and there the prohibition is stated explicitly: its blood in its soul, etc.; and there are no alternating explanations — there are two verses; and the second challenge "is there anything," etc. — for Israel cautioned in commandments — Tosafot's explanation may be said: not subject to shechitah; and Rambam, who ruled in Hilchot Melachim Noah was commanded and not Adam — if so, he properly ruled like R' Avahu that convulsing is forbidden for a non-Jew. And in my chiddushim I said in Tosafot's explanation it is difficult for Rambam with this baraita "you may surely eat" — this is eiver min hachai: how does he hold? Behold flesh was not permitted to Adam haRishon; and to say they dispute — strained, for it implies it is the subject. And it appears it depends on the dispute at the end of Zevachim regarding sacrifices sons of Noah offered — whether they were olah or shelamim; if shelamim, they needed to eat flesh — heaven forbid coming to notar and they ate flesh, and perhaps even flesh dangling, which for Rambam is absolute prohibition — they would also eat; it teaches: no, this is not eiver min hachai; but according to the view they were olot, there is no eating flesh in reality; therefore Rambam holds this baraita — he holds Adam was commanded on eiver min hachai, holds they offered shelamim, and eating is possible; but Rambam ruled in Hilchot Avodah Zarah they were olot — no need Adam was commanded; therefore he wrote Noah was commanded. And if we say Adam haRishon was commanded and they were shelamim — necessarily it is proven he was not commanded on convulsing — who says? And no need "not subject to shechitah," for this entire commandment is in kodashim, for chullin did not exist then in reality; and the essence of his commandment is in shechitah — and therefore convulsing was not forbidden; and if so, since they were not yet commanded then, prohibition goes otherwise — unlike Rambam, who ruled they were olot; and in truth Adam was not commanded, etc.; and it is fine to say like Tosafot's challenge: not subject to shechitah, etc.; therefore Rambam ruled like R' Avahu — examine well. And behold one may analyze for Rambam: according to the view there is no Torah shechitah for a bird — convulsing is forbidden, for its shechitah does not permit; if so, even though the Sages enacted shechitah afterward — did they say this to be lenient? We did not hear there is one who forbids convulsing bird for Israel; and one must say as Tosafot wrote in Yevamot: similar matter — Chazal have power to uproot Torah law even in active violation; and when they enacted shechitah, they permitted convulsing. And printed in Kushi'ot in the name of the gaon Mahar R' Shmuel: that which the Gemara challenges in chapter 2 of Pesachim: we taught one may not give eiver min hachai to a non-Jew and a cup of wine to a nazir because of "before the blind," etc. — let it derive: wherever a negative commandment is stated, prohibition of eating and prohibition of benefit are included — what is the challenge? Perhaps it deals with convulsing, permitted for Israel to eat even to benefit from it, and for a non-Jew forbidden. And in truth this challenge is difficult not only for Rambam but for all — for we conclude in Chullin daf 102: in tamei, eiver min hachai does not apply to Israel, but for a non-Jew forbidden; if so, perhaps it deals with tamei eiver min hachai, permitted in benefit even though written "you shall not eat," as explained in the Gemara there. Therefore it appears we should understand why both are needed: cup of wine for a nazir and eiver min hachai for a non-Jew — one would suffice to teach not to cause another to stumble. And one must say: if it taught nazir — one might say it applies only to Israel; but a non-Jew, who is not in the category of responsibility — what practical difference? And if it taught eiver min hachai — one might say it applies only to what he too was commanded in this, relevant to "before the blind"; but for a nazir, who may drink wine — only for a nazir forbidden — one might say we have no case here; it teaches forbidden. And from this it is proven: with the aforementioned manners, if so, there is still difficulty — why teach eiver min hachai alone? For also in this there is no prohibition on him; therefore it deals with eiver min hachai, which is also in the category of prohibition; and it properly challenges — examine well; and see above what I wrote siman 62. And in any case we have practical difference between Rashba and Rambam to sell flesh from the slaughter-house before it dies: for Rambam forbidden; for Rashba permitted. And the presumption of most poskim is like Rashba, that a non-Jew may eat them before its soul departs — or not? At first glance there is to forbid, for all their permissibility is because for Israel it is permitted; with this it is forbidden until it dies. And if the prohibition for Israel is not from this name eiver min hachai but another prohibition — you shall not eat upon the blood — as Tosafot wrote there daf 33, s.v. echad, etc. — it implies nevertheless it is not in the category of "is there anything," etc., since in any case for Israel it is forbidden even from the other name — nevertheless one may rely here on Rashi, who holds for Israel it is Torah-permitted, only rabbinically they were stringent; if so, "is there anything," etc. applies well. But to sell intestines to a non-Jew — Rashba's view: in tamei absolute prohibition, for it is eiver min hachai; in tahor permitted — "is there anything," etc.; and accordingly, if shechitah did not occur and it became neveilah in his hand — forbidden to sell intestines to a non-Jew. But Peri Chadash challenged: this is in the Gemara entirely according to R' Levi, who holds: he slaughtered the trachea and the lung was perforated — kosher; but for us, who do not hold like R' Levi — this law does not exist. And he was very lenient in Torah prohibition, for Ra'osh wrote: therefore we hold he slaughtered the trachea and the lung was perforated — treifah; for R' Levi said contradictory matters, and who knows which was last? If so, here where we come to permit, we say the last one; and it is always eiver min hachai and forever concerned to be stringent. And Ramban in Milchamot Hashem wrote: therefore the lung was perforated — forbidden, for it is not clear to us the lung was perforated from the trachea alone — perhaps from two simanim, trachea and esophagus; if so, when he slaughtered two simanim — according to all it is like one who places in a basket; and it is eiver min hachai. And that which they said to R' Avahu: for R' Levi one does not invite, etc. — one should understand: what did R' Levi argue? According to all, after slaughtering two simanim — according to all like flesh in a basket they are. It is true — but we do not find one who says so explicitly — perhaps they are like other organs that depend on the animal's death; but for R' Levi we hear he said so; if so, with two simanim we found no disputant of R' Yehuda; and from here to innovate a new dispute — with this R' Avahu and R' Pinchas' pilpul is resolved, seemingly not according to the halakhah, for we do not hold like R' Levi. According to what I wrote, it is resolved: with two simanim all hold like R' Levi; therefore one should be stringent; and especially here it does not apply that we require two sides of a river, for such eiver min hachai is not common — non-Jews do not slaughter trachea and esophagus at all; and when it dies it is permitted; therefore if found thus in Israel that the animal became neveilah — protest without selling intestines to a non-Jew. And at the end of the siman the gaon author of Taz brought a responsum from his brother gaon, may his memory be blessed — both as one answer: that which we hold, broken spinal column and majority of flesh with it — neveilah; and that the spinal cord too was severed with it; otherwise kosher. And all their proof from Tosafot there daf 32b, s.v. velichashov, etc.: they challenged why the Gemara did not challenge and count Ze'eira, broken spinal column, etc.; and answer: one may say it is not neveilah unless the cord was severed — and it taught there cord severed alone — forbidden; end; from this they ruled we require the cord too; therefore Rav Peri Chadash agreed. And the gaon Chacham Tzvi in his responsum daf 29 tends to be stringent, although he admits from Tosafot's words it is clear we require the cord severed too — nevertheless, since Rambam and all poskim were silent, silence implies that even without the cord it is neveilah; therefore they were concerned to be stringent; and so from other latter authorities. And in truth, if it were proof from other poskim to forbid without the cord, it would not be so necessary to say Tosafot wrote the Gemara did not wish to challenge and count Ze'eira because Ze'eira's intent can be rejected with the cord — and it taught there explicitly; unlike the conclusion that Ze'eira's matter is not difficult — one may say simply neveilah without the cord. But there is no necessity to remove Tosafot's words from their implication — their words indicate as halakhah that without the cord it is kosher. And behold in Nekudot HaKesef it is written daf 72: Avraham haChasid — spinal column cut and majority of flesh with it — neveilah. R' Chananel said: we too taught: melikah with a knife imparts to garments, etc.; if you say treifah — let the knife avail to purify from neveilah, etc.; and it is difficult: if one thinks Ze'eira's matter without the cord — what support does R' Chananel bring from melikah with a knife? There in melikah it is not what breaks the bone — also the cord is cut, as we say there: the mitzvah of melikah cuts the spinal column — meaning the spinal cord and spinal column; if so, it is obvious it is neveilah and the knife cannot purify it; but Ze'eira teaches: spinal column cut with majority of flesh, and even cord whole — nevertheless neveilah; if so, what support is there (and in Chacham Tzvi's responsum he was lengthy in Gemara words and I did not understand his intent) — this is clear proof for these gaonim. And so it is proven from Rashba's words I heard, who challenged Ze'eira, who said broken spinal column, etc. — neveilah; and Shmuel, who said it imparts in a tent — this of our mishnah in Ohalot: specifically "their heads were severed" impart though convulsing; and he answered: they interpret "their heads were severed" — since spinal column and majority of flesh broke, even though simanim remain — see there. And it is difficult: how does "their heads were severed" apply, since the spinal cord with the membrane and skin — the main connection of head to body — remains? And why did Rashba mention simanim? It must be Ze'eira forbade only when the cord was severed. And this is clear proof — Tosafot decided Ze'eira deals with the cord, from Rashba's challenge of "their heads were severed." And I have to learn from Rambam's words, who wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchot Tum'at Met, law 15: the dead does not impart until its soul departs; broken spinal column and majority of flesh, or called like a fish, or its head was severed, etc. — behold it imparts though still convulsing. And Kesef Mishneh explained there: its head was severed completely without separation — see there. And one should understand: if spinal column and majority of flesh alone impart, even though it did not touch the simanim — all the more "its head severed" imparts; and so in chapter 2 of Hilchot Avot HaTumah he wrote it does not impart until it dies or its head is severed; and afterward wrote: broken spinal column and majority of flesh with it — behold it is neveilah. And one should understand: if so, "its head severed" is redundant; therefore one must say: intent of "head severed" — but the spinal cord is still whole; and with this the head is connected to the body — unlike broken spinal column, where the cord within is also attached and severed; if so, there are two types here: cord intact, and cord severed — and this is clear that "its head severed" — Rambam deals with this, and not the head only — simanim; for Rambam wrote regarding slaughtering simanim, etc., they do not impart; and regarding sheratzim in chapter 4 of Hilchot Avot HaTumah he wrote: even when attached in the skin — he did not write this regarding an animal; and it is clear he intends what I wrote; and see Rashba, who wrote there is a difference between sheratzim and an animal — therefore the matters are compelled as I wrote; and the aforementioned gaonim's words are correct.
