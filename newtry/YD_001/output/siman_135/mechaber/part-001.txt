**** ENGLISH ****
In which vessels must one be concerned due to libation-touch? It contains 16 se’ifim. Vessels that are not used for long-term storage — whether they are of leather, wood, glass, stone, or metal — if they are not pitched, one rinses/shakes them thoroughly with water and they are permitted, whether their first use was in the hand of a non-Jew or in the hand of a Jew. If they are pitched and new: if it is their way to place wine in them at the time of pitching — and all the more so if they are old, that aged in the hand of a non-Jew with use of wine — he peels off the pitch and rinses/shakes them and they are permitted. If he did not want to peel, he fills them and empties them for 3 days. And the same law applies to our vessels that a non-Jew touched while the wine was tofe’ach upon them. {Rama: There are those who say that regarding our vessels, when liquid was tofe’ach upon them and a non-Jew touched them — if they are of wood, stone, or metal, and one rinsed/shook them, even if they are pitched — and he went ahead and put wine into them, one should not forbid after the fact (Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, Baal HaTerumah, and Rosh), and in a case of great loss one may be lenient.}

Wineskins are not considered “used for long-term storage,” for they are made only to transport wine from the winepress to the barrel.
