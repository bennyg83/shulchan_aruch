**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And used.</b> Perishah writes that this applies only if the non-Jew used them, but mere touch while they were moist does not forbid. Taz disagrees and rules that touch also forbids. Accordingly, one who sells wine but leaves lees in the empty barrel should not allow the non-Jew to remove them. The barrel still contains enough moisture to moisten another object, and some clear wine remains among the lees; the non-Jew's touch would forbid a barrel used for long-term wine storage, requiring hagalah or filling and pouring. The Jew should remove the lees himself. Nekudot HaKesef agrees with Perishah here, because an unpitched vessel was not subjected to so severe a decree merely through touch.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A surface layer.</b> Shach understands this where it is unknown whether wine remained in the vessel for a full day. If it is known that yayin nesech remained for a full day, immersion is like cooking and the entire vessel becomes forbidden. It would then require full hagalah according to its material, and earthenware would require breaking. He nevertheless leaves room to consider that because the absorption occurred cold, filling and pouring might still help for wine. For other prohibitions absorbed through prolonged cold soaking, filling and pouring does not help; this leniency was stated specifically for wine, whose taste water weakens. Filling with wine itself accomplishes nothing. If there is sixty against the vessel's surface layer and wine was placed in it a second time, the second wine is permitted after the first wine already absorbed and nullified the vessel's taste.

<b>רותחים. </b> כ' הט"ז נראה דוקא מכלי ראשון ובמזופף לא מהני שום דבר רק קליפת הזפת ולהכשירו אחר כך אם א"א שיכניסנו לכלי ראשון ואי לא עביד הכי אפילו דיעבד אסור ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Boiling.</b> Taz writes that the water must come from a primary vessel. In a pitched vessel nothing helps until the pitch is removed and the vessel is then kashered. If it cannot be inserted into a primary vessel and this is not done, even after the fact the contents are forbidden.
