**And his words are astonishing etc.** And I say that on the contrary his words are astonishing — for what he brought from the Shulchan Aruch siman 138 is astonishing, for the Shulchan Aruch follows his view, as he wrote in the Beit Yosef there; but the Perishah brings the Beit Yosef's words there and argues on him. And the Tur's language there they strained — why did he not also teach regarding iruy as he taught regarding niguv see there. And what he wrote further, that the Tur HaYashar wrote etc. — to resolve this, in Tur HaYashar there he explicitly deals with a stone winepress that needs only niguv see there; and the Perishah also agrees that his entire intent is to say that they did not decree so stringently except where only niguv is needed, but where milui v'iruy is needed they did not decree on a non-Jew's touch alone. And what he wrote further, that even when not used for long-term storage etc. — to resolve this, there the reason is because the pitch absorbs into them. However, it is difficult regarding the Perishah's words in siman 131, for even in a wooden vessel that is not used for long-term storage and is pitched, the Tur brings the poskim's view that iruy is required in our vessels that a non-Jew touched, and he wrote that the Rosh's conclusion is forbidden l'chatchila and b'dieved if one used them it is kosher — if so, how is it possible that winepress vessels below siman 138, pitched, that a non-Jew touched in our vessels, do not require iruy? It stands on earth and a convert in heaven! Nevertheless the Perishah's words here regarding vessels used for long-term storage that are not pitched may be true; and perhaps the Mechaber also agrees to this, and the reason here is since it is not pitched they did not decree on touch so stringently as to require iruy — investigate.

<b>(שם בש"ע סעיף י"ב בהג"ה) שיש בהן שליש כו'. </b>לשון הטור והרמ"ה כתב דמסתברא מיא דמלחא היכא דתקף מלחייהו דומיא דציר ומורייס שפיר דמי ואפילו לדבריו אין להתיר אלא בריבוי מלח כי היכי דמפרש גבי שבת היכי דמי מי מלח עזים תלתא מלח כו' עכ"ל ונ"ל שט"ס הוא וצ"ל ב' תלתא מלח וכן הוא בש"ס שבת דף ק"ח ע"ב תרי תלתא מלח ותלתא מיא. כן הוא בכל הפוסקים ובטור ומחבר גופיה באורח חיים סי' שכ"א סעיף ב' ואם כן דברי הרב שכתב שליש מלח וב' שלישית מים צ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
**(There in Shulchan Aruch seif 12 in the hagahah) that has a third etc.** The Tur's language and the Ramah wrote that it is reasonable that strong saltwater, where its salt is strong, is similar to tzir and morayis — it is fine — and even according to his words one should not permit except with abundant salt, as explained regarding Shabbat: what is strong saltwater? Three parts salt etc. end of his words. And it seems to me it is a scribal error, and it should read two-thirds salt and one-third water; and so it is in the Gemara Shabbat daf 108 side 2: two-thirds salt and one-third water. So too in all the poskim, and in the Tur and the Mechaber himself in Orach Chayyim siman 321 seif 2; and if so, the rabbi's words that wrote one-third salt and two-thirds water require investigation.
