<b>כלים שאין כו'. </b>בסימן זה לא נכללו כלי הגת שהם חמירי טפי אע"פ שאין מכניסין יין שם לקיום דטרידי ביין ודינם מבואר בסי' קל"ח כ"כ ב"י והוא מוזכר בכל הפוסקים דכלי גת חמירי משאר כלים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Vessels that are not, etc.</b> The vessels of the winepress were not included in this siman. They are more stringent, even though wine is not placed in them for long-term storage, because they are constantly occupied with wine. Their law is explained in siman 138. So wrote the Beit Yosef, and all the poskim mention that winepress vessels are more stringent than other vessels.

<b>היו מזופתין. </b>שהזפת מבליע היין לא שהאיסור משום היין שהם נותנים בשעת הזפיתה שאותו היין הוה כמשליך לאיבוד אלא שאח"כ נותנים בהם יין ואינו ניכר כ"כ ב"י בשם התוספות וכתב הטור בשם ר"י דוקא בימיהם שהיו רגילין ליתן יין בזפת שאז אינו ניכר אם נתן בהם יין אחר הזפת אבל האידנא שאין רגילין ליתן בהם יין בשעת זפיתה כל זמן שהם חדשים מראיתן צהוב עד שיתנו בהם יין לפיכך יכול לקנותן מהם כל זמן שהם צהובין עכ"ל. ועיקר האיסור בימיהם הוא כשנתן יין בשעת זפיתה משום שאחר כך לא יהא ניכר באם נתן בו יין וכלי שהוא מזופף מבליע בכלי טפי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If they were pitched.</b> The reason is that the pitch causes the wine to become absorbed. The prohibition is not because of the wine that they place in them at the time of pitching, for that wine is considered thrown away. Rather, they subsequently place wine in them, and this is not readily discernible. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos. The Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak that this applies specifically in their times, when they commonly put wine into the pitch, so that it was not discernible whether other wine was placed in them after the pitching. Nowadays, however, when it is not customary to put wine into them at the time of pitching, as long as they are new their appearance remains yellow until wine is put into them. Therefore, one may purchase them from non-Jews as long as they are yellow, end quote. The primary prohibition in their times was when wine was put into them at the time of pitching, because afterward it would not be discernible whether wine had been placed in the vessel. A pitched vessel is comparable to a vessel coated with clay.

<b>כלי חרס כו'. </b>משמע סתם כלי חרס שלנו ולא כמו שמביא הטור דעת הרא"ש דכתב שהוא מין אדמה כו' ולפי זה צריך ליזהר בהנהו כדים של חרס שפיהם קצר מלמעלה ומיוחדים ליין על הרוב שאין לשתות בהם יין של היתר אם הם של עובד כוכבים דתחלה נשתמש בו עובד כוכבים ביין ולא מועיל הדחה אבל באותן שממלאין בהן גם מים הוי ספק דרבנן שמא נשתמש בו תחלה במים ומותרים בהדחה. כן נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Earthenware vessels, etc.</b> This implies ordinary earthenware vessels of ours, and not merely the type of earth described by the Rosh, whose view is cited by the Tur. Accordingly, one must be careful with those earthenware jugs whose openings are narrow at the top and which are generally designated for wine. One may not drink permitted wine from them when they belong to a non-Jew, because the non-Jew first used them for wine, and rinsing does not suffice. With vessels that are also commonly filled with water, however, there is a doubt in a rabbinic matter, for perhaps their first use was with water, and they are permitted through rinsing. So it appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Leather flasks, etc.</b> The Tur writes: Some say that they are considered vessels into which wine is placed for long-term storage, etc. The explanation of his words appears to be that people sometimes keep wine in a leather flask for several days. Therefore, some maintain that it is considered a vessel used for long-term storage. The Tur disagrees and holds that a vessel is considered designated for long-term storage only when it was made for that purpose from the outset. By contrast, when it was not made for that purpose and wine is kept in it only incidentally, the Sages did not decree regarding temporary use by a non-Jew. On this basis, the Tur cites the responsum of the Rosh concerning leather flasks used to transport wine from the winepress to the barrel. The Rosh permitted them through rinsing and was not concerned that perhaps wine had occasionally remained in them for several days. He proves this from the daily practice regarding earthenware vessels, for which rinsing suffices. Meaning, it happens every day that a non-Jew drinks wine in a Jew's house from the Jew's earthenware vessels, and they rinse them afterward. We do not say that because wine is sometimes kept in them for several days, they are considered vessels used for long-term storage and rinsing would not suffice. Rather, as I explained, the Sages did not decree in such a case. This resolves the Beit Yosef's difficulty with the Tur. I am astonished, however, that the Rosh permitted through rinsing the leather flasks used to carry wine from the winepress to the barrel, for they are winepress utensils like the strainer and funnel in siman 138, and they require wiping, as written there. It therefore appears that the Rosh intended only that they do not require filling and filling and pouring like vessels used for long-term storage. For this he brings proof from ordinary earthenware vessels used throughout the year, which require only rinsing. The same applies here, but wiping is nevertheless required in addition to rinsing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Those waters do not count for him.</b> These are the words of the Rosh, and the Tur concludes that he requires 3 additional times apart from these. This implies that they do not help even if he wishes to combine other water with them; the period during which this water remained does not count. The Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Rashba, who wrote: Moreover, even if on the first day he filled it for 6 hours, and on the second day he filled it for 6 hours with fresh water, and so on in separate intervals until the full measure of 3 days was completed, it is permitted, end quote. The Beit Yosef reconciles the Rosh with the Rashba by explaining that the Rosh intended only to exclude counting it as an entire day when even 1 hour was lacking. This appears to me to be the primary explanation, for the Rosh brings proof from a mikveh lacking even a minute quantity. Had his intent been that the partial period cannot be combined at all, he would have no proof from a mikveh. This is the very proof that the Rashba also cited for the ruling that a period lacking even 1 hour is insufficient. The wording of the Tur, however, disagrees with the Rashba. The Beit Yosef wrote that we rule like the Rashba, but in the Shulchan Aruch he did not mention the Rashba's ruling, which implies that he rules stringently like the Tur. In my humble opinion, if after the fact one performed the kashering in accordance with the Rashba and subsequently used the vessel for permitted wine, we do not prohibit it. In truth, I do not know the reason for the Tur's stringency. If we say that the interruption during the filling and pouring process of 1 day causes the moisture that had begun to emerge to become reabsorbed, so that the vessel must remain moist for 24 consecutive hours, then for the same reason we should require all 3 days to be consecutive. The Gemara does not explicitly permit separated days, and from where do we derive leniency? Rather, this reasoning is plainly unsound. Whatever was expelled, even a little, combines with what will be expelled afterward. The same is true on the first day. There is no distinction between separating one day from another and separating the hours within a single day. Since we permit separated days by logical reasoning, why should we not also permit separated hours? Perhaps the Tur's reason is maris ayin, because an observer might think that this counts as a full day. Therefore, after the fact it certainly combines. If, however, during a single day one empties the water and immediately fills it with other water, this is plainly even better and is permitted even initially, provided that he intends the total period to be slightly more than 24 hours, to compensate for the brief interval between emptying the first water and adding the second. Perhaps even this is unnecessary, for the Beit Yosef already cited the the Rosh's responsum that even if one cannot be exact to the very moment, the Torah was not given to ministering angels. It is possible, however, that there he was compelled because exact precision is impossible, whereas here he deliberately creates a short delay between one water and the next. Therefore, he should leave it afterward slightly longer than 24 hours, and there is then no concern at all. So it appears to me.
