Accordingly it seems here too, although he used the language 'the non-Jew used it' — touch has the same law in all cases; for regarding a winepress below siman 138 we forbid a non-Jew's touch even though in the Gemara they only said 'a non-Jew pitched it' — for it is one matter; and by plain reasoning we say so, as the Beit Yosef wrote there — and so too here; and we should not invent distinctions and laws from our hearts, especially to be lenient in my humble opinion; and the Perishah author was not sufficiently precise here; and Maharach also argues on him in this.

**And some permit even through iruy etc.** This is the Tur's language; and Rabbeinu Tam permitted pouring boiling water into it and rolling it until it passes over all of it; and Avraham Abulafia wrote: even according to his words one should not permit thus except when it is not pitched; but when pitched, such weak hag'alah does not help; and it implies from his words that good hag'alah helps without peeling the pitch; and Rabbeinu Yonah required hag'ael each stave separately — that he put the vessel into the pot when it is boiling and pour on each stave etc. end of his words. What he wrote regarding Rabbeinu Tam's view to pour boiling water — at first glance it implies even boiling water of a kli sheini, since he used plain 'boiling water'; and in Rabbeinu Yonah's words he said to put the vessel into the pot etc. — but this is not so, for explicitly in the Rosh's responsum kelal 19 siman 11: and Rabbeinu Tam permitted the barrel that one take a pot full of boiling water and pour it into the barrel etc.; and even though this is not a kli rishon, since the water does not reach all of it except through rolling — nevertheless, since the absorption was through cold, he was lenient etc. — behold even for Rabbeinu Tam one must pour specifically from a kli rishon; and although through rolling the barrel it helps even from one stave to many staves, nevertheless the main heat must enter the barrel specifically from a kli rishon. And that which Rabbeinu Yonah afterward wrote to put the vessel into the pot — this did not come to exclude Rabbeinu Tam's words on this, but to inform us of a leniency of Rabbeinu Tam: since Rabbeinu Yonah is stringent to pour on each stave separately and rolling from stave to stave does not help — if so, pouring would not help even from that vessel itself once boiling ceased after removing it from the fire — we learn that it is still called a kli rishon; and so too is explicitly explained in Tosafos' words that the Beit Yosef brought. And what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh that for pitched vessels such weak hag'alah does not help — do not err to say specifically in this case that was through rolling; but if one pours on each stave alone it helps even through iruy from a kli rishon without peeling pitch — and this is the good hag'alah he mentioned afterward, meaning pouring over the entire barrel not through rolling that helps even without peeling — this is not so, for it is explained in the Rosh's responsum this siman, and these are his words: and what Rabbeinu Tam was lenient to pour etc. — this is specifically in a wooden barrel that is not pitched; but pitched absorbs more, as stated in the Gemara 'if they were pitched they are forbidden.' And after I wrote this, it seems to me from your words that you asked how they are koshered after peeling the pitch — this is a simple matter, that it suffices to pour boiling water into it from a large vessel, and he need not fill water three days; and what you said they cannot because the pitch does not stick well after water — for this I knew no remedy end of his words. Behold before you that he required peeling the pitch specifically; and although he knew no remedy and the vessel would spoil, he did not say to pour water from a large vessel anywhere on the barrel in a way that would not require rolling — but it is clear that whatever is pitched has no remedy at all through any pouring; it needs only to be put into a kli rishon on the fire — and this is Avraham Abulafia. And the good hag'alah the Tur mentions is plain hag'alah — that everywhere one puts the item being koshered into the pot where it is possible to put it there; and there one need not remove the pitch; and so it seems from the Beit Yosef's explanation who wrote good hag'alah — meaning from a kli rishon helps without peeling pitch — and this is plain end of his words. And do not err in what the Beit Yosef wrote 'from a kli rishon' — meaning through iruy from a kli rishon; on this he would not write that it is plain, for almost most poskim hold iruy does not help at all but one must put it into the pot; and as the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, they are permitted through hag'alah in a kli rishon — rather the Beit Yosef intended that the hag'alah comes from a kli rishon into which it was put; and this language the Rashba wrote in Tur HaYashar HaKatzar that the Beit Yosef brought siman 138 in the passage beginning 'the Rashba wrote that winepress vessels etc.' — and these are his words: and some of the great teachers taught that winepress vessels are koshered only from a kli rishon etc.; and there it refers to putting it into a kli rishon — end. I wrote all this because I saw in Perishah that he wrote that according to Rabbeinu Tam one need not pour from a kli rishon into the barrel — and I already proved this is not so; and he also wrote that the good hag'alah the Tur mentions means pouring on each stave without rolling — and this is permitted even when pitched; the Tur's language in the name of the Rosh misled me, who wrote such weak hag'alah — he holds specifically such is the case; but in truth in the Rosh's responsum kelal 19 siman 8 he did not write the word 'such' but plainly 'weak hag'alah does not help'; and we already proved that for pitched nothing helps except peeling the pitch; and to kosher it afterward, if impossible to put it in a kli rishon, and if one does not do so, even b'dieved it is forbidden — and with this understand the Rama's words who wrote to permit through iruy that one rolls etc. in the details of the laws we mentioned with heavenly assistance.
