**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They must hear, etc.</b> Semag writes that this follows from Nedarim 72a, where the betrothed man had not heard but the father heard, and the baraita says, “on that day,” etc. His words require study, and Lechem Mishneh already rejected them; see there. At first glance, however, Nedarim 68a implies this from, “or he heard and annulled and then died,” etc. This suggests that on the following day the father cannot annul, since had the betrothed man remained alive he could no longer annul, and the same should apply after his death. Ran rejects this proof there, s.v. ta shema, etc. Rosh likewise reads in his rulings: “or he heard, remained silent, and died on that day,” etc., and writes that the father annuls on the day he hears and need not, etc. Rambam also rules this way in chapter 11, law 16: “or the betrothed man also heard and annulled, or remained silent and died on that day.” Yet this does not prove the contrary, since there the betrothed man died and his authority reverted to the father, as Lechem Mishneh writes. Rather, Rambam derived the rule from the verses, as he states: “If her father restrained her on the day he heard,” and, “If she shall indeed be married to a man,” etc., “on the day he heard.” From this you learn that after the father annulled and the betrothed man heard, the latter annuls on the day the father heard; the same applies if the betrothed man heard and annulled, etc. Rambam understood the plain meaning of these verses to require that both hear on the same day. He did not explain “on the day he heard” in the ordinary sense because of the Sifrei, which teaches from “If her father restrained her on the day he heard” that, just as a husband’s hearing followed by silence on the day of the vow confirms it, so too a father’s; it derives this from “These are the statutes,” which compares father and husband. Likewise, the Sifrei derives the husband’s rule from “If on the day her husband hears he restrains her,” rather than from the earlier verse, “and her husband hears it on the day he hears.” This is why Rambam cited these verses here.

The primary question concerns annulment by the husband, though the same applies to annulment by the father, as Rosh and Ran write there. Yet one who examines Rabbeinu Yerucham will see that he explicitly rules like the Sages, except that the condition must depend on a matter of self-affliction or a matter between husband and wife, etc. This is unlike one who says that the vow should take effect after thirty days, for there it takes effect automatically. Ran likewise writes in chapter 1, s.v. veRabbi Yosi, etc., where he raises a difficulty and answers it, etc. Ran adds another possibility, namely, etc., based on the statement on 89b, “Konam that I will not,” etc. He writes similarly on 90a, s.v. mihu, etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, does not make this distinction, because he follows Tur and Rosh in their explanation there, s.v. konam, that this is a matter between husband and wife. Rabbeinu Yerucham states this explicitly, as does Shulchan Aruch below in seif 69: “and matters,” etc.

<b>Specifically if she said, etc.</b> Rosh writes this in his rulings there and in Ketubot, based on the Gemara’s statement: “Rather, Rav Huna said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, where she says,” etc. Rambam writes that specifically regarding consecration she must say, “Let them become consecrated,” etc., but with a konam this is unnecessary. Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer 81:2, rules likewise. Rambam’s reason is that once the Gemara concludes, “Konamot are different,” etc., the first answer, requiring that she say, etc., is no longer necessary. Although one cannot consecrate to the Temple treasury something not yet in existence, as Rambam writes in the laws of acquisition, one may consecrate an object with inherent sanctity, as shown at the beginning of chapter 5 of Temurah, 24b, concerning an animal about to give birth, etc., and 25a, where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that offspring, etc. Even so, he agrees in the first clause, though an embryo is not yet fully in existence, as stated in Kiddushin 62b and Bava Batra 142b. This also appears from Yevamot 93a and Kiddushin 63a: “It was stated concerning it,” etc., implying that this remains the conclusion. Tosafot in Kiddushin there, s.v. veyadayim, however, write, “the same applies,” etc., in accordance with Rambam. Rambam’s ruling nevertheless requires study, for if she merely says, “Let them become consecrated,” without using a konam, the Gemara’s difficulty remains: her hands are pledged to her husband.
