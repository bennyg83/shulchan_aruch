(There Taz s.k. 40) "Very astounding" etc. "he was not precise" etc. — he was not precise, and the rabbi was very precise: for if so Rambam should also have said "he told her 'annulled for you tomorrow' is confirmation," as he wrote at the beginning; rather he certainly infers from that which the Talmud says "do we say 'for tomorrow' — he cannot annul" etc. — why does it ask "for tomorrow he cannot annul"? Behold, if he confirmed, even immediately he cannot annul her. And it is forced to say that it means: since he said "annulled for you tomorrow," even immediately he cannot annul her; for if so it should have said "since he said 'for tomorrow' he cannot annul." And further, from that it also says afterward "for tomorrow" as if it exists — it implies it asks about tomorrow; and if it exists, since it is confirmation, even immediately he cannot annul her. Rather certainly the explanation is: certainly if he wants to annul her immediately after he said "annulled for you tomorrow," he can annul; for although "annulled for you tomorrow" is not annulment, it is also not confirmation — rather the question is whether he can annul her for tomorrow: do we say he cannot annul her because he confirmed her vow — meaning he did not annul her today, and if so he confirmed, for he said "annulled for you tomorrow"; or perhaps when he said "annulled for you tomorrow" he meant from today; and if so, behold he annulled her from yesterday — and this is what it says afterward "and if you find to say: since he confirmed today, for tomorrow it is as if it exists," and it does not say "since he confirmed today he can no longer annul her." This is clearly Rambam's view, and so is implied more to one who examines Rambam at the end of Laws of Vows — and his view is correct:

<b>(שם בש"ע סעיף נ"ד) ואם הפר לה שלו בטל כו'. </b>הוא מדברי הרמב"ם ותמה בתוספות י"ט דפ' ד' דנזיר דהא תנן במתניתין מפר את שלה ושלו קיים ומפרש בש"ס שם דהיינו כשאומ' הריני נזיר ומה תאמרי את. ונראה דהרמב"ם היה גורס במתניתין מפר את שלה ושלו בטל וכן הוא בירושלמי במתני' ומשמע התם בירושלמי דטעמא הוא שנעש' כמי שתלה נדרו בנדרה עיין שם ולפי זה בש"ס נמי היה הרמב"ם גורס בטל במקו' קיים ופסק כאביי ומפרש דאביי משני ברייתא כגון דקאמר לה הריני נזיר ואת דקא תלי נדרו בנדרה ולא דנעשה כמי שתלה נדרו בנדרה אלא דתלי ממש נדרו בנדרה ומתני' כגון דקאמר הריני נזיר ואת מאי דלא תלה נדרו בנדרה ממש אלא דנעשה כמו שתלה נדרו בנדרה כדקאמר בירושלמי דהיינו כאלו היה אומר הריני נזיר אם תהיה את נזירה ומה שתעשי בנזירות שלך יהיה בנזירות שלי ואת מאי הילכך מפר את שלה ושלו בטל. ולכך כ' הרמב"ם והמחבר כאן שזה כמי שתלה נדרו בנדרה ולא כתבו שתלה נדרו בנדרה וכדקאמר בש"ס דקא תלי נדרו בנדרה אלא כוונתם להירושלמי בהא דלא כתבו ברישא שתלה נדרו בנדרה משום שפשוט הוא כן נראה לי לדעת הרמב"ם. ותמיהני על הכסף משנ' שמביא גירסת הש"ס שלפנינו וכתב שהרמב"ם פסק כאביי והא דבר שאי אפשר ליישב דעת הרמב"ם על פי הגרסא שלפנינו אלא ודאי כמו שכתבתי. ומיהו מדברי התוספו' מבואר להדיא שהיו גורסים כגירסת הספרים שלפנינו וכן מובא ברש"י ולכן דברי המחבר צרי' עיון לדינא:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There in Shulchan Aruch seif 54) "And if he annulled hers, his is void" etc. — this is from Rambam's words, and Tosafot Yevamot chapter 4 of Nazir wondered: behold we learned in the mishnah "he annuls hers and his stands," and the Talmud there explains this is when he says "I am a nazir — what do you say?" It appears Rambam had the mishnah text "he annuls hers and his is void," and so it is in the Yerushalmi mishnah; and it is implied there in the Yerushalmi that the reason is he becomes like one who tied his vow to her vow — see there. Accordingly in the Talmud too Rambam had "void" instead of "stands," and ruled like Abbaye, who explains Abbaye answers from a baraita such as when he says to her "I am a nazir and you" — that he ties his vow to her vow, and not that he becomes like one who tied his vow to her vow, but rather he literally ties his vow to her vow; and the mishnah is such as when he says "I am a nazir and you" — not that he literally tied his vow to her vow, but rather he becomes as if he tied his vow to her vow, as stated in the Yerushalmi — meaning as if he said "I am a nazir if you are a nazir, and what you do in your naziriteship shall be in my naziriteship" — and you, what? Therefore "he annuls hers and his is void." Therefore Rambam and the Mechaber wrote here that this is like one who tied his vow to her vow, and they did not write "he tied his vow to her vow" as the Talmud says "he ties his vow to her vow," but rather their intent is the Yerushalmi in this — that they did not write initially "he tied his vow to her vow" because it is obvious — so it appears to me according to Rambam. And I wonder at Kesef Mishnah who brings our Talmud's version and writes that Rambam ruled like Abbaye — behold it is impossible to reconcile Rambam's view according to our version except certainly as I wrote. However from Tosafot's words it is explicit they had the version of our books, and so is brought in Rashi; therefore the Mechaber's words require study for practical law:

(There Taz note 59) challenged Perishah and Bach — no difficulty from "may my hands be sanctified for their work" — Beit Yosef himself explained regarding "may my hands be sanctified for their work on your mouth" — gemara resolves so on "konam I do for you" — brought in Shach note 86; Rambam challenge also no difficulty — even initially not inclusive only as Perishah explained — initially when said in hekdesh language, in conclusion even without that language as Ran and Beit Yosef from his words; Ran's wonder on Rambam left in wonder — appears Rambam correct as I explain; Beit Yosef's answer for conclusion can forbid not his on another — impossible: gemara says "you said konamot" per Rava who only removes subjugation; also Rava holds one cannot forbid another's not-his — Nedarim 47: Ramy bar Chama, konam fruit on so-and-so on substitutes — do we say regarding his own since one can forbid another's fruit on himself, can forbid not-his on himself; regarding another since one cannot forbid another's fruit on another, cannot forbid not-his on another; or because substitutes are like adults and must resolve loan/creditor there — Rava rejected perhaps ab initio forbidden because substitutes like adults, only if done it is done — no reason to permit except cannot forbid not-his on another; if say can forbid on another not-his, then even b'dieved forbidden; Rambam brought this doubt end ch. 5 Nedarim — ruled safek, if transgressed and benefited he benefited; if holds can forbid not-his on another no safek, plainly forbidden even if not like adults; why "if transgressed benefited"?
