<b>שיאמר בתחילת העיבוד. </b>זהו כמסקנת הרא"ש שהביא תחילה שנסתפק רבינו ברוך בזה אי בעינן אמירה דוקא או סגי במחשבה דבפיגול כתיב לא יחשב ובעי דיבור ותרומה ניטלת בלא דיבור ובשליחות יד דכתיב על כל דבר פשע והחושב לשלוח יד בפקדון חייב עכ"ל ויש לתמוה הא לבית הילל אינו חייב בשליחות יד עד שיעשה בו מעשה כדאיתא סוף פרק המפקיד ותו הקשה בדרישה דאפי' לבית שמאי דמחייבי התם בחושב היינו באומר כדפירש רש"י ותוס' וזה נוכל ליישב דרבינו לא ס"ל כרש"י ותוס' בזה ונראה לי דהצעת דבריו הוא דבפיגול כתיב לא יחשב ומצינו בו שהוא בעי דבור נמצא יש לנו ספק בכאן מאיזה דין נלמוד וכן גבי שליחות יד דכתיב על כל דבר פשע ומצינו בו ג"כ לשון מחשבה במשנה שאמרה החושב לשלוח יד בפקדון חייב לב"ש והתם סמך התנא עצמו על הפסוק אבל בשאר מקומות נשאר הספק במקומו ומ"ש בתחילת עיבוד נראה דלא בא למעט בסוף העיבוד דאדרבה מצינו בכ"מ שאין המעשה נקרא אלא על שם הגומר ואם כן כי מסייע בגמר ודאי מהני אלא בא למעט שא"צ לסייע מתחילה ועד סוף אלא די אפילו בתחילה לחוד אלא די"ל כיון דמתחילה עשה העובד כוכבים לבדו הרי נעשית מקצתה שלא לשמה משא"כ כשעושה ישראל תחילה סיוע איכא למימר דאחר זה גריר כל מעשה העובד כוכבים עד גמר כולה דקי"ל כדעת רא"ש דכי עשה עובד כוכבים אדעתיה דישראל מותר אלא דמדברי הטור כאן בשם רב נטרונאי כתוב וז"ל אבל אם יש להם יכולת להחזיר הדק לידי מתוח ולהעביר עליהם סיד לשמה הרי יצאתם ידי עיבוד לשמה עכ"ל אם כן כ"ש כשמסייע בתחלה בשעה שיש עליה סיד הראשון דמהני וכן משמע מסמ"ג שמביא ב"י באו"ח סימן ל"ב וצריך שיתן העורות בעיבוד לשמן או יסייע לעובד כוכבים בשעת עבוד לשמן עכ"ל. ומ"מ נ"ל שלא נקרא סיוע בעיבוד אלא במעשה העיבוד עצמו אבל לא במה שמסייע להשחיר את העורות של תפילין כי זה לאו מעיבוד הוא וכל הנוהג קולא בזה הוא מחטיא רבים:
**** ENGLISH ****That he should say at the beginning of the processing. This is like the Rosh's conclusion that he brought first — Rabbeinu Baruch was uncertain whether we require explicit declaration or thought suffices; for regarding pigul it is written "it shall not be reckoned" and requires speech, whereas terumah is taken without speech; and regarding misappropriation as it is written "for any matter of trespass" — one who thinks to misappropriate a deposit is liable — end quote. And it is surprising — for according to Beit Hillel he is not liable for misappropriation until he acts, as stated at the end of chapter HaMafkid; and further he challenged in Derishah that even for Beit Shammai who obligate there for thought — that is when he speaks, as Rashi and Tosafot explained; and we can resolve that Rabbeinu does not hold like Rashi and Tosafot here, and it appears to me his suggestion is: regarding pigul it is written "it shall not be reckoned" and we find it requires speech — thus we have doubt here from which law to learn; and likewise regarding misappropriation as written "for any matter of trespass" and we find there too language of thought in the Mishnah that said one who thinks to misappropriate a deposit is liable according to Beit Shammai — and there the Tanna himself relied on the verse; but in other places the doubt remains. And what he wrote "at the beginning of processing" appears he did not come to exclude at the end of processing — on the contrary we find everywhere the act is called only by the name of the finisher; if so, when he assists at the end it certainly helps; rather he came to exclude that one need not assist from beginning to end — rather it suffices even at the beginning alone. But one could say: since at the beginning the gentile alone did the processing, part of it was done not for its sake; unlike when a Jew assists first — one could say afterward he draws all the gentile's processing until the entire finish, for we rule like the Rosh that when a gentile acts with the Jew's intent it is permitted. But from the Tur's words here in the name of Rav Netronai it is written: but if they have ability to return the hide to stretched form and pass lime over them for its sake — behold you have fulfilled processing for its sake — end quote; if so, all the more when one assists at the beginning at the time there is the first lime it helps; and so it implies from Semag that Beit Yosef brings in Orach Chayyim siman 32 — one must place the hides in processing for their sake, or assist the gentile at the time of processing for their sake — end quote. Nevertheless it appears to me that assistance in processing is called only in the act of processing itself, but not in what assists to blacken the hides of tefillin, for that is not from processing; and all who practice leniency in this mislead the many.

**** ENGLISH ****To write with the right hand. For we expound "and you shall bind" and "and you shall write" — just as binding is with the right, so writing is with the right — end of chapter HaKometz.

And what he wrote "and not with a feather." It is true that in Mordechai chapter 1 of Gittin it is written thus in Hagahah in the name of the Rosh; but I do not know the reason for the matter; and perhaps it is as we find in the order of gittin of Maharil Margoliot who wrote one should not write with an iron reed, for it is like engraving; and even though engraved letters are valid, nevertheless as much as possible to do without any engraving is preferable, etc.; and perhaps so too with a feather that is thin; and so the reason is written in Levush, and it is written that we do not practice thus.

And engraving — since it came to hand, let us say a word: for I heard people saying that printed books do not have sanctity like handwritten ones; and it appears to me there is no difference, for even if we say it is engraving, nevertheless it is like writing; for also regarding a woman's get, engraved letters are valid as written in EH siman 125, even though it is written "and he shall write" and not "and he shall engrave."

And further it appears to me clear proof from chapter Eglah Arufah (Sotah 48a) — those stones of the ephod and breastplate — one does not write them with ink, as it says "engraved like a signet" implies engraving; and one does not scratch them with a knife, as it says "in their fullness" they should be whole without lacking anything; rather one writes on them with ink and shows them shamir from outside on the face of the ink in the groove of the letter and they split open by themselves; and if it were so that printing were engraving, how do we know it was by shamir which is an uncommon thing — perhaps it was by printing with ink like our press, and it would work out well "engraved like a signet"; rather it is clear that printing is called actual writing and not engraving at all.

And again I saw in responsum She'elot to Mahar Benjamin z"l who also wrote that printing is sacred like writing, because engraving too is sacred, and he brought proof from the ephod; but according to my humble opinion we do not need this, for it is actual writing and there is no engraving here — what difference whether one presses the pen on the paper or the paper on the lead letters — either way it is writing. Nevertheless regarding a get certainly one should not do by press since it somewhat resembles engraving; but regarding sanctity of books, whoever is lenient will give account.
