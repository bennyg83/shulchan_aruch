<b>שעל היתר. </b>פי' קיבה עשויה כמין קשת וחלב שעליה מבחוץ נקרא דאקשתא וחלב שבפנים לעיגול נקרא דאייתרא כלומר חלב במקום יתר שעל קשת ואע"ג דקי"ל בסימן ס"ד בחלב שעל היתר אסור וקי"ל חלב טמא אינו סותם היינו טעמא דסותם לפי שאמרינן בגמרא שבני א"י אוכלים אותו שעל היתר אלא שבני בבל נהגו לאיסור לכל הפחות מהני גם לבני בבל שמתיר בסתימת הנקב אבל שעל הקשת אסור באכילה לכ"ע ע"כ אינו סותם:
**** ENGLISH ****
On the yeter. Explanation: the kivah is shaped like a kind of arch; chelev on it from outside is called dakashta, and chelev inside the circle is called yeter — meaning chelev in the extra place on the arch. And even though we rule in siman 64 on chelev on the yeter that it is forbidden, and we rule that tame chelev does not seal — the reason it seals here is because we say in the Gemara that the people of Eretz Yisrael eat that on the yeter, only that the people of Bavel practiced to forbid it; nevertheless it helps also for the people of Bavel who permit through sealing the puncture. But that on the arch is forbidden to eat for everyone — therefore it does not seal.

<b>כרס שניקבה כו' ואין לו דבר שיסתום אותו כו'. </b>זה לשון רמב"ם וקשה דהא מצינו שפיר סתימה ביה דהיינו אם ניקב במקום שהטחול דבוק בו והטחול סותמו כמ"ש רמב"ם גופיה בההוא פירקא וז"ל וכן כל נקב שהבשר או החלב המותר באכילה סותם אותו ה"ז מותר וא"כ גם כאן הרי בשר של הטחול סותם שפיר את הנקב וי"ל דכיון שיש קרום על הטחול שאסור משום חלב גמור במקום הדבוק כרס כמ"ש סי' ס"ד ס"י קרום שעל דד הטחול חייבים עליו כו' והיינו במקום שדבוק לכרס וכ"כ רש"ל בפרק ג"ה סי' נ"ז וז"ל שעל הדד הוא מחובר שם לכרס ולחלב גמור עכ"ל וע"כ אין סתימה לכרס שניקב דחלב טמא אין סותם ואפי' במקום הדק של טחול יש עכ"פ איסור חלב ואינו סותם כלל ועי' מה שכתבתי סי' מ"ג ס"א עוד מזה וכן נראה פשוט כיון שכת' הרמב"ם סתם אין לך דבר שיסתום משמע בכל מקום וגדולה מזה איתא בהג"ה ש"ד דטחול שניקבה אין הכרס סותם אותו:
**** ENGLISH ****
Kereis that was punctured, etc., and it has nothing to seal it, etc. This is Rambam's language, and it is difficult — for we find proper sealing in it, namely if it was punctured in the place where the spleen is attached to it and the spleen seals it, as Rambam himself writes in that chapter: "And likewise any puncture that flesh or chelev permitted in eating seals — it is permitted." If so, here too the flesh of the spleen properly seals the puncture. And one may say: since there is a membrane on the spleen that is forbidden as full chelev in the place attached to the kereis, as written siman 64 seif 10 — "membrane on the teat of the spleen one is liable for it," etc., and this is where it is attached to the kereis; and so Rashba wrote in chapter 3 siman 57: "that on the teat is attached there to the kereis and to full chelev," end of his words — therefore there is no sealing for kereis punctured, for tame chelev does not seal; and even in the thin place of the spleen there is in any case a prohibition of chelev and it does not seal at all. And see what I wrote siman 43 seif 1 further on this; and so it appears simple, since Rambam wrote anonymously "you have nothing to seal" — it implies in every place. And greater than this: Hagahot Shaarei Dura — spleen punctured, the kereis does not seal it.

**** ENGLISH ****
Most of the thickness of the flesh. This is Rambam's view; and his language is that which the Gemara says regarding the outer kereis "in its majority" — meaning in the majority of its thickness; and even if not mafpolash it is treifah. Rabbenu Yerucham wondered on him: if so we would not know whether it was punctured mafpolash — how is its law, since the Gemara does not deal with this. Beit Yosef wrote: and to me it appears that because we taught anonymously, etc. — explanation of his words: it is simple that if torn with a knife it is forbidden even in any amount in mafpolash from concern lest the kereis was punctured; and since the tanna did not distinguish between tear by knife or by illness — we must say that also in illness it was in something; and why did the tanna say we require a majority — but certainly the tanna deals with not mafpolash, and there is truly no distinction between illness and knife — in both we require a majority; in mafpolash treifah in both in any amount. And what Beit Yosef wrote "their measures are equal" means whether by illness or by knife they are equal — in mafpolash in any amount, and in not mafpolash in its majority. This is explanation of his words; but they are very astonishing — for certainly there is a distinction between knife and illness in mafpolash, only that the knife prohibition does not apply specifically here; for in every place facing the cavity of the body there is concern in puncture by knife or thorn lest it touched inner organs and punctured them — why should the tanna distinguish here in flesh covering the kereis between knife and illness, when the knife stringency does not depend specifically here. And further: in esophagus there is a distinction between thorn and illness as stated siman 33 in Shulchan Aruch from Tosafot and Ra'ash — and so we rule; and why not distinguish here too. And the tanna need not mention this distinction — for knife stringency is not from itself but from concern lest the kereis was punctured, and the tanna does not deal with that but with treifah from itself. And more difficult for his view: why did the tanna mention treifah that is not mafpolash which is in a majority, and not treifah that is mafpolash which is in any amount — there is a great innovation that it is forbidden even from illness. But it is certain there is no prohibition here even in mafpolash in less than the measure of three pits — namely if by illness; but by thorn or knife we are concerned in every place facing the cavity of the body as written siman 91. Also Rashba challenged Rambam as Beit Yosef brought, and he holds that specifically mafpolash we require in the measures mentioned here; and Rashba brought for halacha like them. Nevertheless one may not be lenient since they established in Shulchan Aruch for halacha. Nevertheless it is clear that also regarding mafpolash its law is thus — that we forbid only at a tefach, as written here regarding thickness, since others explained so regarding mafpolash as above.

<b>הקרע הזה בארכו כו'. </b>בטור כתו' בלשון זה או נקרע בו קרע באורך טפח או ניטל טריפה ואח"כ כתב נקדר הבשר בעיגול או באורך אם אין בחסרון אלא כסלע כו' וכ' ב"י בשם מהר"י חביב וזה לשונו לא ידעתי כוונתו כי אם הוא באורך אין זה קדירה ושיעורו טפח אבל אם הוא בעיגול אז כשתשים חוט סביב הקדירה יהיה בחוט אורך טפח עכ"ל פי' דהסלע הוא שליש טפח וכל שיש ברחבו טפח יש בהיקפו שלשה ובכסף משנה כת' ע"ז ולי נראה פשוט שכשאמרו שיעורו טפח הוא בנקרע ולא חסר ונקדר היינו שחסר ולפיכך שיעורו כסלע עכ"ל ולא תיקן כלום דעיקר קושיית מהר"י חביב היתה מכח מ"ש הטור תחלה אם נקרע או ניטל כו' וניטל היינו קדירה ומכ"ש לדברי הרמב"ם כפי מה שנזכיר למטה דקשה ורש"ל תירץ דברי הטור על כוונת דברי הרמב"ם שהעתיק כאן בש"ע דהיינו שלא נקרע ולא ניטל במפולש אלא בעובי הבשר ואח"כ כת' נקדר דהיינו מפולש ואין משמעות לשון הטור כן ונלע"ד דתחלה בא הטור להורות דכאן אינו כטחול דמצינו בו ניקב בסומכיה טריפה ואפ"ה ניטל כולו כשר ע"כ כת' כאן הן אם נקרע הרוב ממנו הן אם ניטל כל אותו הכרס טריפה דאין כאן מעלה לניטל יותר מבנקרע וע"כ כת' או ניטל אחר שכתב אם נקרע כטפח לגלות דבניטל כולו קאמר וא"ל למה כתב דין זה דלא דמי לטחול דוקא כאן ולא בשאר אברים י"ל לפי שנתן כלל בסי' נ' שבכ"מ שאמרו אם ניקב במשהו טריפה ה"ה אם ניטל חוץ מטחול א"כ ה"ה כאן שאין נקיבתו במשהו יהיה דינו כטחול לענין ניטל קמ"ל דליתא וכיון שגילה לנו דניטל אינו מועיל כאן בא לתת בו שיעור אח"כ דאם נקדר וניטל הבשר משם הן בעיגול הן באורך כיון שיש חסרון שיערו כסלע כנ"ל ומ"ש בטור או ניטל בו תיבת בו הוא ט"ס ובס"א אינה ויותר נראה שהיה כתוב כולי דהיינו בסיגנון זה כו' והמעתיק טעה וכ' במקום כו' תיבת <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="2"></i>בו אך בדברי הש"ע כאן שהם דברי הרמב"ם לא מתייש' בכל מה שזכרנו שהרי כתב תחלה או שניטל וניטל היינו קדירה ומה זה שכ' בסעיף ד' או באורך הא כבר כ' דינו וצ"ל דתני והדר מפרש דמתחלה כתב דרך כלל שיש טריפו' בנקרע ובניטל ואח"כ מפרש תחלה דין שיעור הקרע עד סוף הסעיף ובסעיף ד' מפרש דין ניטל דהיינו נקדר בעיגול או באורך:
**** ENGLISH ****
This tear in its length, etc. Tur wrote in this language: or a tear was made in it of the length of a tefach, or it was removed — treifah; and afterward he wrote: the flesh was bored in a circle or in length if the deficiency is only like a sela, etc. Beit Yosef in name of Mahari Chaviv — and this is his language: I did not know his intent, for if in length it is not boring and its measure is a tefach; but if in a circle, when you place a thread around the bore it will be a tefach in the thread — end of his words. Explanation: the sela is a third of a tefach, and whatever has a width of a tefach has a circumference of three; and in Kesef Mishneh he wrote on this; and to me it appears simple that when they said its measure is a tefach — it is in a tear and not deficient; and bored means it is deficient — therefore its measure is like a sela, end of his words. And he did not fix anything — for the essence of Mahari Chaviv's difficulty was from what Tur wrote first "if torn or removed," etc.; and removed means bored, and all the more according to Rambam as we will mention below. Rashba and Rashba answered Tur's words according to Rambam's intent that he copied here in Shulchan Aruch — namely that it was not torn or removed in mafpolash but in the thickness of the flesh; and afterward he wrote bored — meaning mafpolash. And Tur's language does not imply so. And it appears to me: first Tur came to teach that here it is not like the spleen where we find punctured in its support — treifah, and even if all of it was removed — kosher; therefore he wrote here: whether the majority was torn from it or all that kereis was removed — treifah, for there is no advantage to removal more than tear; and therefore he wrote "or removed" after writing "if torn" a tefach — to reveal that in removal he speaks of all of it. And one may say: why did he write this law — for it is not like the spleen specifically here and not in other limbs. One may say: because he gave a general rule in siman 50 that wherever they said "if punctured in any amount — treifah," the same if removed except the spleen — if so here too that its puncture is not in any amount, its law for removal would be like the spleen; he teaches that removal does not help here. And since he revealed that removal does not help here, he came to give it a measure afterward: if bored and flesh was removed from there, whether in a circle or in length — since there is deficiency, its measure is like a sela as above. And what Tur wrote "or removed in it" — the word "in it" is a scribal error and in some texts it is not; and more likely it was written "all of it," i.e., in this formulation, etc., and the copyist erred and wrote "in it" instead of "all of it." But in Shulchan Aruch's words here, which are Rambam's words, it does not reconcile with all we mentioned — for he wrote first "or removed," and removed means bored; and why in seif 4 did he write "or in length" — he already wrote its law. And it must be: he taught and then explained — first he wrote generally that there is treifah in tear and in removal; afterward he explains first the law of the measure of the tear until the end of the seif; and in seif 4 he explains the law of removal — meaning bored in a circle or in length.

Mafpolash from side to side. To understand the dispute that Rama brings — we will copy their dispute: the rabbis taught: a needle found in the thickness of beit hakosot from one side — kosher; from two sides — treifah. Rashi: specifically in beit hakosot we require a mafpolash puncture; but in the meses even from one side where the puncture is not seen outside — this is not sealing, because the wall is very thin. Tosafot challenged him: we learned "the meses and beit hakosot punctured outward — treifah" — we learn that in the meses too we require mafpolash. R' Yitzchak says: this "outward" is not entirely outward but toward the outside — excluding punctured one into the other. And R' Yitzchak further says: we find to say that in the meses even from one side it is treifah, for we are concerned lest it punctured outward and returned and healed, as regarding esophagus siman 33. And that which we learned specifically punctured outward is treifah — when we know it did not heal, such as when it ate a needle before us and they slaughtered it immediately, end of his words. In name of Rabbenu Tam they wrote: in the meses too we require mafpolash like all treifot. And that which it specifies beit hakosot teaches that from one side it is kosher even though one whole skin was punctured and we are not concerned lest it healed — but regarding esophagus it is different, for it moves and contracts and sometimes the needle returns inside the esophagus; but in the meses if it healed it would not return inside. Tur and other poskim agreed with Rabbenu Tam, and so ruled here in Shulchan Aruch; and the "some forbid" of Rama is Rashi, and Terumat HaDeshen and Orach Chaim haArukh agreed; therefore Rama ruled we follow the lenient view in great loss.
