כלומר אפילו אם יש בה הרבה ספיקות בפעם אחת אסרינן ואין כן בשאר טריפות דכלל גדול בס"ס דמותר ואע"ג דאנו מקילים גם בדרוסה בס"ס כמבואר סימן נ"ז סעיף י"ג היינו כשיש ספק אם היה שם הדורס או לא אבל אם היה שם בודאי לא מהני שאר ספיקות ועוד נראה לתרץ ע"פ דברי התוספות בפא"ט דף מ"ג שהקשו אמאי לא נימא לענין ספק דרוסה נשחטה הותרה ותירצו דדרוסה שכיחי טפי לאיסור מלהיתר ולזה כוון גם כאן בספק דרוסה להחמיר. בטור כתוב **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Eight types of treifot, etc.</b> Tur wrote in the name of Rambam: even though all of them are halacha leMoshe miSinai, you have none explicit except derusah; therefore they were stringent in it, and any doubt one will doubt in derusah — forbidden; but in the other seven types of treifot there is doubt that is permitted. And Beit Yosef wondered: he found no doubt of treifah that would be permitted — end. And it appears to me that double doubt (s'feik s'feika) applies: in other treifot there is permission in s'feik s'feika, but in derusah we find prohibition even in s'feik s'feika — that is, where they are silent and trembling (me'ot vehen mekrakrin) we forbid in siman 57, seif 9, because of doubt of derusah, even though there is s'feik s'feika there — perhaps they did not touch them yet, but only scream from fear; and if you say they touched them — perhaps the predator did not inject venom; and if you say it injected venom — perhaps it struck them below the hollow of the body where venom does not kill; and even so we forbid. This is what he said: any doubt one will doubt — meaning even if there are many doubts at once we forbid; and it is not so in other treifot, for the general rule is that in s'feik s'feika it is permitted. And even though we are lenient also in derusah in s'feik s'feika, as explained in siman 57, seif 13 — that is when there is doubt whether the predator was there or not; but if it was definitely there, other doubts do not avail. And it further appears to answer according to Tosafot in Pesachim (daf 43), who challenged: why do we not say regarding doubt of derusah — if it was slaughtered, it is permitted; and they answered: derusah is more common to forbid than to permit; and to this he also intended here regarding doubt of derusah — to be stringent. In Tur it is written: and it is impossible to say regarding treifah in general, etc., for it is written: to a dog you shall cast it — it is difficult: why not derive from what we find in the Gemara: whence do we know treifah is not a living animal? As it is written: this is the animal, etc. — a living animal eat, and what is not a living animal do not eat; learn from this that the matter depends on being alive; and likewise the reverse difficulty: why does the Gemara not derive from the verse "to a dog"? And further difficulty in what Tur wrote: just as regarding neveilah, etc. — behold regarding neveilah there is no derivation on this except from reasoning (svara), as he said above: what is neveilah to me, etc.; if so, let us say here too from this reasoning; and what is the superiority of this reasoning of neveilah over this reasoning of treifah? And Kesef Mishneh wrote: there is some good reasoning in this regarding neveilah — whence for us? In truth to say: just as regarding neveilah, etc. — and it appears to me: that which he said above "what is neveilah to me" — he does not rely on reasoning here; rather, since the verse said neveilah, whose meaning is dead, behold the name of dead includes whether it died from itself or from others; it is found as though written explicitly that there is no distinction in this regarding neveilah; and perforce one cannot explain treifah as already dead, but perforce that it is still alive, only that we still have to say that in treifah there is a distinction, since the term treifah means by a lion, and its meaning is even any treifah whatsoever; if so, you have difficulty in what we find in the verse "this is the animal," which is simple to all that from there we derive the matter depends on being alive; and they contradict each other; and because of this difficulty I was compelled to say there is a distinction between treifot of a lion, which is even in any amount, and treifot from itself, which depends specifically on being alive; perforce Tur wrote: even so, regarding treifah Scripture was strict not to explain it regarding any amount, since it is written regarding it "to a dog," etc. — automatically here too one cannot explain regarding any amount; and we explain it that it depends on being alive; and on this he said: just as regarding neveilah, etc. — explanation: since here too it depends on being alive, automatically its law is like neveilah, which is explicit in the verse that there is no distinction. And if you say: why do I need two verses — "to a dog" and also "this is the animal"? One may say: if not for "this is the animal," one might have said that what is written "to a dog" — that it is fit for a dog — does not depend on being alive, but on danger, which is also not fit for a person, only for a dog; and if not for the verse "to a dog," I would have been compelled to distinguish in treifah between from itself and from others, from the difficulty that treifah implies any amount; but now that "to a dog" is written, the explanation of "any amount" is hidden, and we say well that also from itself the law is like from a lion, since you have no difficulty that compels this; we say rather it is more reasonable to liken it to neveilah. It is found Tur did not need to bring "this is the animal," which is simple; and in the Gemara he did not need to bring the verse "to a dog," for it was simple to them that the verse's meaning deals with what is fit for a dog, and not regarding striking in any amount; and all is settled, with God's help.
