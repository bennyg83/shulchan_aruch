**** ENGLISH ****
Court does not compel, etc. In chapter Kol HaBasar it is stated thus: that man who did not honor his father; they brought him before Rav Chisda and bound him to flog him, as stated regarding a positive commandment that a person does not wish to fulfill; they strike him until his soul departs; and Rav Mari happened there and said leave him; for it is taught: every positive commandment whose reward is beside it; lower court is not warned regarding it. And one may challenge from Yerushalmi that the Ramban brought in responsum siman 88 in his name; Beit Yosef brought in Choshen Mishpat siman 97 regarding return of collateral; that if he did not wish to fulfill even though court is not warned regarding it; since its reward is beside it; nevertheless if court shut their eyes to this there is no punishment upon them; but certainly if they wish to compel; permission is in their hand; and likewise ruled the Rama in the gloss. And it is difficult from what Rav Mari said here to Rav Chisda "leave him"; for if he wishes he properly compels him. And in Mordechai chapter Na'arah sheNitpatetah he brings this Yerushalmi and wrote on it: and according to this there is no difficulty from what we say in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Kiddushin "would that all my traditions were clear to me like this that they compel the son to sustain the father"; and in chapter Kol HaBasar he said leave him; end quote; behold the difficulty was only these two matters that contradict each other; namely Yerushalmi said they compel the son to sustain the father and it is a positive commandment whose reward is beside it; and in chapter Kol HaBasar he said leave him. And he answered on this that the matter of compelling the son is from the will of the court; but on what we challenged he did not answer at all; for what did Rav Mari bring from what is taught to disagree with Rav Chisda; and in Tosafos chapter 1 of Bava Batra page 8 they wrote in name of Ritzba and likewise Mordechai there also brought the Yerushalmi above and wrote that they do not compel great compulsion regarding striking him until his soul departs like other positive commandments; but they do some compulsion; and therefore he said leave him; meaning since you already did for him immediately with compulsion on the pillar it appears this is how we rule; even though the Beit Yosef brought below siman 248 that we do not rule like Ritzba but like those who disagree with him; namely regarding charity they do even great compulsion for the reason he brings there; but in this matter where there is to do some compulsion like other positive commandments whose reward is beside it; there is no disagreement with Yerushalmi; and so is the essence; and therefore also here regarding honor of father and mother; if court wishes they do some compulsion until he fulfills; and even though the Rama did not write anything from this; it is not worth making distinctions in an explicit clear matter as above. And in Sema in Choshen Mishpat siman 107 he wrote that we do not rule like the Yerushalmi above that if they wish they compel; except regarding return of collateral that they compel him since the collateral is in possession of the borrower; unlike regarding honor it is not correct to distinguish so at all since there is no proof here except from that the Rama omitted to write here as he wrote in Choshen Mishpat; the Rama himself has no place to distinguish in this if he intended it; but rather also here the law is so that there is no disagreement with Yerushalmi.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Not in his presence. </b>So the Rambam writes explicitly. This is self-evident — for in his presence it is forbidden to call someone by his father's name even if it is not a distinctive name, lest his father think he is calling him, and similarly anyone standing there might think so. There is no distinction whether the name is distinctive or not. Although the Tur's transcription of the Rambam does not mention "not in his presence," this is of no concern, for he relied on the obvious. The Derishah elaborated here to dispute the Rama's gloss of "not in his presence," but he was not precise at all, as will be explained in siman 242 with God's help.

**** ENGLISH ****
Rather he should say "do for the sake of," etc. In the Tur he concluded: but if he knows they will not pay his desire for his father's sake; he should ask for his own sake and not for his father's sake which is only disgrace for him since they will not do for him; end quote. And one may question: the anonymous text at the beginning implies we require specifically that he know clearly to ask for his father's sake; and at the end implies the opposite from his precision; and one may say the beginning is mitzvah and the end prohibition; and in anonymous text permission is in his hand as he wishes; this is not so for even in doubt of mitzvah. And furthermore one may challenge; for his honor is included in his request for his father's sake; for all that they do for him they will also do when he mentions honor of his father; and likewise Beit Yosef in name of Rabbeinu Yerucham in name of RaMaH that even if they do not divide honor in that place for his father; he should attribute to the father that they do for the son even though he attributed to his father; end quote. And it is difficult on RaMaH; why did they say in the Gemara "if he is heard for his father's sake he should ask for his father's sake," etc.; and it appears that certainly so; in all manners he should ask for his father's sake unless in that place there is hatred toward his father; and automatically when he mentions him hatred will arouse quarrels; and also for his own sake they will not do it; then he should ask for his own sake. It turns out the precisions in the Tur do not contradict; this is what is meant: if he knows they will do for his father's sake; namely in this request that he asks for his father's sake that he not spoil in what he mentions him; then he should ask for his father's sake and attribute honor to him; and such is the intent of the Gemara as I wrote "heard in the words of his father"; but if he knows he will spoil because of their hatred toward him; then he should not mention him at all; so it appears correct to me.
