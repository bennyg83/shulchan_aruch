**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he said: Konam, etc.</b> As stated in Shevuot 29a concerning one who says, etc. Such a declaration is not a vow at all, as stated in Nedarim 15a: 'Do we leave him,' etc.; see Rosh there, s.v. mi shavkinan, etc. This resolves Tosafot's difficulty in Shevuot there, s.v. be'omer. The statement in Nedarim 24b, 'the produce shall be forbidden,' etc., is specifically because it was an exaggeration; otherwise it would be forbidden, as the Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh states, that when he insists upon the literal truth of his words, the vow is binding. See above, siman 239, seif 1. This is certainly so according to the version, 'exaggerated oaths are forbidden.' The Rosh there, citing Rabbeinu Yitzchak, answers that there he did not say 'all.' The Gemara in Shevuot could likewise have answered: 'an oath of falsehood,' etc., namely, 'an oath that I will not eat all the produce'; rather, it taught this formulation to exclude the Gemara's question there and in Nedarim: 'perhaps this person,' etc. This is why the ruling here specifies, 'If he said: Konam, all,' and similarly, etc. Likewise, the statement in Nedarim 28a, 'one who says: the produce shall be forbidden,' etc., also refers to a case where he did not say 'all.' The words 'all produce' in the continuation there should be omitted, as should the word 'all' in Gittin 35a. Tosafot there, s.v. venoderet, also write that 'all' is not part of the text. Their view, however, is that he nevertheless transgresses immediately, as with a vain oath. <small>See below, beginning of siman 215.</small>

<b>And if an unavoidable circumstance occurred, etc.</b> As stated in Gittin 30a: 'something like a vessel containing three kav of dinars,' etc., according to the opinion that unavoidable circumstances are legally recognized. According to Rashi there, however, this applies specifically where he lacks the money. The Rivash likewise writes elsewhere, as cited by the Beit Yosef, that financial compulsion is not deemed an unavoidable circumstance. The Mordechai in chapter 3 of Shevuot and the Gemara likewise indicate this. Even if he says, 'Had I known this, I would not have vowed,' he certainly cannot release his own vow, as stated at the end of Nedarim, 81b: 'he shall not profane,' etc. Moreover, even a sage cannot release it on this basis, because it is a newly arisen circumstance. <b>ואם יכול כו' ואם כשהגיע כו' לכ"ע כו'. </b>זה ע"פ שיטתו שכתב בסי' רכ"ה סמ"א שחילק בדברי הרשב"א שם בין אם מחויב כו' וס"ל כי"ח שם ולכן פסק כאן מי שנשבע כו' ואם יכול כו' ובדין השני כתב ולכ"ע כו' דמיירי שהיה חייב לו בלא"ה אלא שאם פטרו המלוה היה פטור מלשלם אף מכח השבועה כמ"ש בסי' רכ"ח סל"ט אלא שא"י להרויח לו זמן ובאונס אפי' פטרו אינו כלום כמו תלאו ויהיב וכמ"ש בתשובת הרמב"ן הביאו ב"י בס"ס רכ"ח דף רי"א ב' כתוב בתשובה להרמב"ן סי' רמ"ו מי שנשבע לחבירו לפרעו לזמן פלוני והכריח האדון למלוה להאריך לו זמן כו' והיינו שנתחייב לו בכבר ושם הביא ב' תשו' הרשב"א שפטור ושם מיירי שלא היה חייב לו וכתב שם ע"ג ומ"ש אפי' אם פטרו היינו האריכו כנ"ל דהארכת זמן לא מהני א"נ אפי' פטרו ממש אם אנסו המלך וכנ"ל בתשובת הרמב"ן וז"ש כאן ואם כו' או כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he is able, etc.; and if, when the time arrived, etc., according to all opinions, etc.</b> This follows his approach in siman 225, seif 41, where he distinguishes within the Rashba's rulings between a case in which the person was already obligated, etc. He accepts the second opinion there. Accordingly, he rules here: 'one who swore,' etc., 'and if he is able,' etc. In the second ruling he writes, 'according to all opinions,' because it concerns one who already owed the money independently of the oath. If the creditor released him, he would also be exempt from the oath, as stated in siman 228, seif 39, but the creditor cannot merely extend the time. Where the extension was coerced, even an express release is ineffective, like a coerced gift. This accords with the responsum of the Ramban cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 228, folio 211b, responsum 246: one who swore to repay his fellow by a fixed time, and the ruler compelled the creditor to extend the deadline, etc. There the debt already existed. The Beit Yosef also cites two responsa of the Rashba ruling that he is exempt, but those concern a case where no prior debt existed. What he writes there, that even if the creditor released him, means that he extended the deadline, as above, since an extension alone is ineffective. Alternatively, it means an actual release where the king coerced him, as in the Ramban's responsum. This is the meaning of the wording here, 'and if,' etc., 'or,' etc.
