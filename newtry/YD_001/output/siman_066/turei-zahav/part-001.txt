<b>אבל חכמים אסרוהו. </b>והוא לחד תירוצא בתוס' פ' אלו טרפות שב"י מביא כאן והיינו שבכל מקום אסור מדרבנן אבל לתירוץ השני יש חילוק דדם שעל הקשר אסור מן התורה דחשיב מין בשר כיון שהאפרוח נוצר ממנו אבל במקום אחר אין בו איסור אפי' מדרבנן ומשמע מטור וש"ע דתפסו חומרא של שני התירוצים. ובב"י כתוב אבל הרמב"ם כתב ביצת עוף טהור כו' עכ"ל משמע דס"ל דפליג הרמב"ם על מה שכ' הטור דחייבים עליו אם הוא מרוקם האפרוח דהיינו מן התורה וקשה דהא הטור עצמו מביא דברי הרמב"ם אלו בסי' פ"ו וכן בש"ע מביא בשני המקומות כמו בטור ונראה לי דמה שכתב כאן דחייבין עליו היינו על הדם כמו שכתב ב"י בשם רשב"א דהוה דם בשר אבל בסי' פ"ו ברמב"ם מיירי שאין שם דם רק ריקום בשר וזהו מדרבנן. והביא רש"ל שיש להחמיר ולומר דדם ביצים דאורייתא ונפקא מיניה לענין ספק עד כאן ומ"מ יש להחמיר גם כתירוץ הראשון ולאסור מדרבנן הדם בכ"מ אבל בת"ח כלל ס"ב כתב בשם הגהת שערי דורא וסמ"ק ומהרא"י דספיקא דרבנן הוה ולקולא וכן כתב או"ה כלל מ"ב בשם אגודה אלא שכתב דדוקא בתערובת סמכינן על זה וכן עיקר וכמו שפסק גם רמ"א בסעיף ד' ועיין מה שכתבתי שם:

But the Sages forbade it. And this is according to one resolution in Tosafot Perek Elu Treifot that Beit Yosef brings here — meaning that in every place it is forbidden d'rabbanan; but according to the second resolution there is a distinction, that blood on the knot is forbidden from the Torah, for it is considered a type of flesh since the embryo is formed from it; but in another place there is no prohibition, even d'rabbanan. And it is implied from Tur and Shulchan Aruch that they adopted the stringency of both resolutions. And in Beit Yosef it is written: but Rambam wrote regarding a tahor fowl's egg, etc., end of his words — it appears that he holds that Rambam disagrees with what Tur wrote, that one is liable if it is marukem ha'afroach — meaning from the Torah. And it is difficult, for Tur himself brings Rambam's words in siman 86; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch he brings in both places like Tur. And it appears to me that what he wrote here 'one is liable' refers to the blood, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba that it is blood-flesh; but in siman 86 regarding Rambam he deals when there is no blood there, only formation of flesh — and that is d'rabbanan. And Maharshal brought that one should be stringent and say blood of eggs is d'oraisa, and a practical difference regarding doubt — until here. And nevertheless one should be stringent also like the first resolution and forbid the blood in every place d'rabbanan; but in Torat Chatat general rule 62 he wrote in the name of Hagahot Shaarei Dura and Semak and Maharai that it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 42 in the name of Agudah — except that he wrote that specifically in a mixture we rely on this; and such is the essential ruling, as Rama also ruled in seif 4 — see what I wrote there:

But if it is found in the chalmon, etc. Their dispute depends on the sugya in this matter. According to Rosh and Tur, it is stated in the Gemara: if blood is found upon it, one discards the blood and eats the rest. R' Yirmiyah said: and this is when it is found on its knot (Rashi: it is the rooster's seed tied at the head of the blunt end of the egg, and from there embryo formation begins; and since it is still there and has not spread through the rest of the egg, therefore one eats the rest; but if found on the chalmon — the entire egg is forbidden, as below). Dosetai taught: they taught only when found on its chelevon; but if found on the chalmon — the entire egg is forbidden, for 'he cast the flaw into the whole of it' — meaning the defect spread through the whole egg; and this regarding the chalmon that forbids the whole — means on the knot of the chalmon, even if it did not spread outside; and the reason that specifically on the knot of the chelevon and it did not spread outside one eats the rest, but if found on the knot of the chalmon we say that it was already on the knot of the chelevon, from where formation begins and spread outside and came to the knot of the chalmon which faces it; but if found on the chalmon not on the knot and it is not recognizable that it spread from the knot — the egg is permitted. And R' Yirmiyah and Dosetai do not argue: R' Yirmiyah teaches that in the chelevon itself there is a distinction — if the blood is on the knot the blood is forbidden and the egg permitted; if it spread outside, the egg too is forbidden; but not on the knot — whether in chelevon or chalmon the egg is permitted. And Dosetai teaches that on the knot of the chalmon it is as if it spread from the knot of the chelevon outside, and the entire egg is forbidden. This is Rashi's view, and it is one view that Rama brings here. But Rif and Rambam explain that Dosetai argues on R' Yirmiyah and holds that in the chalmon there is no distinction between knot or other place, and it is forbidden everywhere; and in the chelevon everywhere the blood is forbidden and the egg permitted — and this is Shulchan Aruch's view in this seif. And Rosh and Tur ruled to be stringent like both views — meaning in the chalmon the egg is forbidden everywhere, and in the chelevon too it is forbidden if the blood spread from the knot outward, and there is no permission for the egg except in chelevon not on the knot, or on its knot and it did not spread. And Rama wrote that our custom is to forbid the egg in every manner; and the reason is explained in Torat Chatat — that we are not expert which place is called the knot. But there is reason to forbid for another reason — there is a view of Ra'ah that the Gemara's version is reversed, that chelevon is worse; and he wrote that we know the embryo is formed from the chelevon and not from the chalmon — for if you break the egg while the embryo is still inside you will not find anything from the chelevon from which it was formed, and the chalmon remains whole; if so, Ra'ah would forbid everywhere — if found on chelevon, forbidden by his view; if found on chalmon, forbidden by Rif and Rambam's view. And Maharshal wrote that such is the halacha like Ra'ah's view:

That he discarded the chalmon, etc. Beit Yosef wrote that the concern of this law is perhaps the blood was in the place that forbids and came here through beating, and we go stringently — blood of eggs is from the Torah; and according to this, whoever holds blood of eggs is d'rabbanan — we go leniently, for perhaps it was not in the place that forbids. And Torat Chatat general rule 42 wrote that his words are incorrect — for even according to those who say d'rabbanan it is forbidden here, since blood was found on the chalmon we are stringent like Rif, that the chalmon is forbidden by law; only that if there is doubt whether on chalmon or chelevon — in that case Beit Yosef's words are correct, end of his words. Maharshal wrote: one should analyze — if they were beaten, how can he take the chalmon upon which the blood was found, for it is already beaten and mixed; certainly it deals after he began to beat them, he saw blood on the chalmon while it was still whole. And he wrote further that this is according to Rosh like Rashi; but according to Rif, who forbids without uprooting reason except because of the chalmon itself being forbidden. And I say further that if they were not beaten and blood was found on the chalmon not on the knot — he should take the egg with the chalmon and discard it and the rest is permitted, since according to Rashi and Ra'ah it is permitted and their words are essential — only that we are stringent like Rif; therefore we are not so concerned to be stringent in mixtures that a little of it mixed with other eggs; but when he already began to beat, even a little — forbidden, for it is a Torah doubt and one should be stringent, end of his words. Since in any case one in two is nullified. Meaning from the Torah one in two is nullified even wet in wet; only the Sages required sixty, as below siman 98; therefore here, when there is doubt whether it was in the place that forbids it, it is a rabbinic doubt and we go leniently. But if there was no mixture except one in one — this is not a rabbinic doubt, since from the Torah it is not nullified — if the blood were in the place that forbids, it would be a Torah prohibition; if so, it would be a Torah doubt and stringently; therefore Rama mentions one in two, etc. And one may say that even one in two is not nullified — below siman 111 — only that there it deals with an important matter where the Sages decreed it should not be nullified even in sixty; therefore it has no permission except in two mixtures — not so with a beaten egg, whose importance is diminished; therefore we go leniently in a rabbinic doubt. And even according to Maharshal, who ruled like Ra'ah that everywhere is forbidden as I mentioned in his name shortly — nevertheless a doubt whether it was in the place that forbids is relevant here; if we say the essential view is Rif's version, automatically in chelevon not on the knot there is permission; if we say Ra'ah's version, automatically on the chalmon there is permission; only that from the side of stringency, since we cannot decide, we forbid everywhere; but in truth according to the truth there is doubt whether there is prohibition here. And Maharshal himself ruled to be lenient in mixtures, as I mentioned in his name in this seif — that since according to Rashi and Ra'ah it is permitted and their words are essential, only that we are stringent like Rif, etc. — sixty is not required, and as below siman 98 regarding spilled gravy it is called a rabbinic doubt. Yet we still need to explain Rama's words here — for according to his words it is difficult why Rosh wrote in the responsum that Beit Yosef and Tur bring — since the thin chelevon mixes together and everything is forbidden — for there too it deals when it is not clear to us whether it came from chelevon or chalmon, as explained in the responsum Beit Yosef brings, that it is unknown whether it came from chelevon or chalmon, etc.; and if so, he should have permitted because of Rama's reason. And in truth this rationale of one in two being nullified does not help here to say that therefore it is a rabbinic doubt — since there is doubt whether it is in the place that forbids from the Torah, for whoever says blood of eggs is d'oraisa — it is as if it were certainly in the place that forbids, as we find in siman 110 seif 9: wherever the doubt is in its substance, it does not help afterward to say a double doubt through mixtures; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 27 din 10 — if the first mixture was only one in one and afterward a second mixture — all is forbidden, since in the first mixture it was not nullified from the Torah but was a Torah doubt; it does not help afterward for the second mixture to say one in two is nullified, as if definite prohibition fell there, since it is from the Torah if so. If so, here too this blood would be a Torah doubt and be as if it were certainly in the place that forbids, and afterward one in two being nullified does not help, for sixty from the rabbinic level is still required; and it is not comparable to spilled gravy in siman 98 seif 2 where we go leniently — for there there is certainly a Torah prohibition, only that it has remedy through nullification in the majority which helps from the Torah, only that d'rabbanan sixty is required; here we come to be lenient from the side of doubt of Torah prohibition — in such a case we say it is as definite prohibition; and this is a principle in many places — see what I wrote siman 107 seif 1 on Torat Chatat. And that Rama permitted here because one in two is nullified — is because he goes according to his view that Torat Chatat general rule 62 wrote: specifically when it did not become mixed we say stringently that perhaps it was in the place that forbids, for blood of eggs is d'oraisa; but if it became mixed we rely on that resolution of Tosafot that blood of eggs in every place is only d'rabbanan, since many later authorities hold thus — if so, here he ruled properly in mixtures to be lenient, since there is another leniency that one in two is nullified, and there is no Torah doubt of prohibition here, for we may say there is no Torah prohibition at all — unlike the egg alone, where we are stringent like one who says blood of eggs is d'oraisa; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 42 — therefore Rosh's responsum that forbids the mixture is not difficult at all, for he holds blood of eggs is d'oraisa, and therefore one in two being nullified does not help, as I mentioned. And Maharshal holds that even in mixtures we are stringent from the stringency of blood of eggs being d'oraisa, as I copied in his name above; and for practical halacha one should rule like Rama, as Issur VeHeter also wrote thus, that such is the practice — especially since many later authorities are lenient in every such doubt; and therefore I ruled in a case that came before me: a woman made dough kneaded with eggs, and after kneading a drop of blood was found in the shell of one of them, and there were many eggs from that mixture — and I permitted everything for Rama's reason that I mentioned, that it is as if there is only a rabbinic doubt, since even if it were in the place that forbids it, it would be nullified in the majority from the Torah (and likewise) here in doubt we go leniently. And they said to me that it became mixed in the dough and it is a type not in its type requiring sixty from the Torah, as below siman 98 — this is not so, for it also became mixed in kosher eggs, and we say: remove what is not its type as if it were not there, and its type is greater and nullifies it, as below siman 98 — therefore I permitted the aforementioned mixture, end of his words:
