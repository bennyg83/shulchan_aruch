**** ENGLISH ****
If a speck of blood is found upon it, one discards the blood and eats the rest — and this is when it is found in the chelevon. But if it is found in the chalmon, the entire egg is forbidden. {Rama: And some say that if it is found upon the knot of the chelevon and also outside it — that is, that it spread from the knot — the entire egg is forbidden (Rosh, Rashba, Rashi, Tosafot, and Or Zarua; and the Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that one should be stringent like both views). And some are even more stringent: whatever is found upon the knot of the chelevon, even if it did not spread at all, is forbidden (Or Zarua, Long Handbook beginning general rule 42, and Maharai of Speyer). And from this spread the custom in these lands to forbid any egg in which there is blood, and they do not distinguish between chalmon and chelevon. And in a place where they are accustomed to be lenient — to discard the blood and permit the egg in a place where it is permitted — one must take the measure of a peel along with the blood when discarding it (Long Handbook there in the name of Semag).}

**** ENGLISH ****
Eggs that were beaten in a bowl and blood is found on the chalmon of one of them — even though one discarded the chalmon upon which the blood was found, they are forbidden, because since they are beaten in a bowl the chelevon, which is thin, mixes together and everything is forbidden. But eggs that are broken together in a bowl and were not beaten, and blood is found in the chelevon of one of them — one takes the kosher chalmonim each one by itself and they are permitted; and one leaves that one in which there was blood in the bowl with the chelevon of the rest and discards them. {Rama: And all this is when we know that the blood was in the place that forbids it. But if there is a doubt whether it was in the place that forbids it or not, one should permit the mixture (his own reasoning in explaining the responsum of the Rosh), since in any case one in two is nullified b'dieved. But if the egg alone is forbidden — and likewise if an egg was cooked or roasted and we do not know whether it is in the forbidden place (all from Issur VeHeter; and see Torat Chatat general rule 62 din 3).}
