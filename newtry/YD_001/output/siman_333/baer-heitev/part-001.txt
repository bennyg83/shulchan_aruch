<b>For their shearing.</b> Meaning that he purchased the fleece while it was still attached. Even if he did not acquire the bodies of the sheep and purchased only the fleece, he is obligated, even though the fleece grew while owned by the gentile and the sheep themselves remain the gentile's. <b>פירש. </b> כתב הש"ך דהיינו אם אחר גיזה מכרה המוכר חייב דהא משעת גזיזה חל עליו ליתן ולמה מכרה. והלוקח פטור דלאו גז צאנך הוא. ואם בעודו מחובר מכרה לו ופי' לו הכל אני מוכר לך שניהם פטורים המוכר פטור דבעודו מחובר לא חל עליו ראשית הגז ובשעת גזיזה לאו גז צאנך הוא דגיזה לאו שלו והלוקח ג"כ פטור דהצאן אינו שלו ומ"מ לכתחלה אסור לעשות כן למכור הצמר במחובר ולהפקיע ממונו מיד מצוה עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He specified.</b> Shach writes that if the seller sold the fleece after shearing, the seller is obligated, because the obligation to give reishit hagez already took effect at the moment of shearing. Why did he then sell it? The purchaser is exempt because it is not "the fleece of your sheep." If the seller sold the wool while still attached and expressly said, "I sell you everything," both are exempt. The seller is exempt because while the wool was attached the obligation had not yet taken effect, and when it was sheared it was no longer his fleece. The purchaser is likewise exempt because the sheep themselves are not his. Nevertheless, initially it is forbidden to arrange matters this way by selling the wool while attached in order to remove one's property from the mitzvah.
