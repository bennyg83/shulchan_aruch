And Beit Yosef brought in the name of Mordechai in this language: if the non-Jew said to Reuven, place collateral in my place, and the Jew took his collateral and said to Shimon, this collateral belongs to the non-Jew, or said to him, I transfer it to you in his place, and Shimon agreed — it is permitted for him to receive interest from the Jew who pledged, who is Shimon's agent to lend to the non-Jew; and likewise when he transfers collateral to him. And he extended to explain Mordechai in another matter and disagrees with Beit Yosef and Bach — see there (and Nachalat Yaakov wrote that for R. Meir, since he admitted the collateral belongs to the non-Jew, his admission is like a hundred witnesses and the non-Jew acquired through his admission, and it is comparable to below seif 13 — see there).
