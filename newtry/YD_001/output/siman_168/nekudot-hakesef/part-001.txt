<b>(שם) ולא כדמשמע לכאורה כו'. </b>ולפי עניות דעתי נראה כדמשמע לכאורה וגם בתשו' רשב"א מיירי כן אלא דמכל מקום אין כאן איסור רבית דמיירי שאין הישראל הב' יכול לתבוע את הראשון כגון שהעובד כוכבי' הקנה החוב לישראל הב' בדיניהם ולא בדינינו ומכל מקום אין העובד כוכבים יכול לחזור מישראל הראשון ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Ibid.) And not as it would appear at first glance, etc.</b> In my humble opinion, the matter is indeed as it appears at first glance, and the responsum of Rashba also addresses such a case. Nevertheless, there is no prohibition of interest here, because the case is one in which the second Jew cannot bring a claim against the first, such as where the non-Jew transferred the debt to the second Jew according to their law but not according to our law. Even so, the non-Jew cannot retract from the first Jew. Examine this carefully.

<b>(בט"ז ס"ק י"ד) בחנם </b>תמה על הבית יוסף ובית חדש ודבריו בפירוש דברי המרדכי דחוקים כנראה לכל מעיין ולא קשה מידי דכיון שהודה הודאתו כק' עדים וזכי ליה העובד כוכבים בהודאתו ודמי לדלקמן סעיף י"ג דאפילו עדים מעידים שהוא של ישראל לא מהני כיון שהודה תחלה שאינו של ישראל ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Taz seif katan 14) Gratuitously.</b> He expressed surprise at Beit Yosef and Bach, but his interpretation of Mordechai is strained, as is evident to every careful reader. There is no difficulty at all, because once he admitted it, his admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses, and the non-Jew acquired it through that admission. This is comparable to the ruling below in seif 13, where even witnesses who testify that the item belongs to a Jew are ineffective once he had first admitted that it did not belong to a Jew. Examine this carefully.

<b>(בט"ז ס"ק כ') השיג </b>על הב"ח והעט"ז שלא כדת דמה שכתב הטור בשם הרא"ש יוסיף לו משכון מלתא באפי נפשיה היא כמה שכתב ב"י אבל לא קאי אריש' דכשאחריו' המשכון על המלוה וראייה שהרי דין זה בתשו' הרא"ש כלל ק"ח סי' ז' ושם לא הוזכר שהאחריות הוא על המלוה ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Taz seif katan 20) He objected.</b> His objection to Bach and Atzei Levonah was improper. What the Tur wrote in the name of Rosh, that the borrower should add collateral, is an independent point, as Beit Yosef wrote. It does not refer back to the opening case in which responsibility for the collateral rests upon the lender. The proof is that this ruling appears in the responsa of Rosh, klal 108, siman 7, and there it is not stated that responsibility rests upon the lender. See there.

<b>(בט"ז ס"ק כ"ד)) ותמהתי מאד על דברים אלו כו'. </b>דבריו תמוהים ודברי העט"ז ברורים דכיון דהוי כמוכר לו משכון אם כן כשחוזר ולוקח ממנו ברבית הוי כחוזר ולוקחו ממנו בסך ההוא והמותר מעות שהיו ביד הישראל הראשון היו בידו בחנם והראיה שהביא מטרשא דרב פפא אינה ענין לכאן כלל דהתם מוכר להן השכר ביותר ממה שהוא שוה עכשיו והוי רבית אבל הכא כיון שמכר לו המשכון נמצא עכשיו דמי משכונו הוא נוטל דהוי כחוזר ולוקחו ממנו וזה ברור וכן המנהג פשוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Taz seif katan 24) I was greatly astonished by these words, etc.</b> His own words are astonishing, while the words of Atzei Levonah are clear. Since the arrangement is regarded as though he sold him the collateral, when he later takes it back from him with interest, it is as though he repurchases it from him for that amount. The excess money that had remained with the first Jew had therefore been in his possession without charge. The proof he brought from the deferred sale of Rav Pappa is entirely irrelevant. There, he sells them the liquor for more than its present value, and that constitutes interest. Here, however, since he sold him the collateral, he is now receiving the value of his collateral, for it is as though he repurchases it from him. This is clear, and such is the established practice.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Taz seif katan 37) Rather, Rama decided, etc.</b> This is incorrect, for Rama wrote this in Darkei Moshe in the name of Beit Yosef, as I wrote in Shach, seif katan 73. See there. He was likewise imprecise in writing that the ruling appears to be proven from the passage in the chapter HaRibbit. There, the document itself records interest, whereas here the claimant says, 'I took it permissibly.' His further statement that Rama derived the ruling from the case in Choshen Mishpat is also incorrect. There, the claimant does not swear and collect. Rather, he pays and afterward takes an oath administered after payment. Moreover, there he claims that he took nothing at all, unlike here, where he claims that he took it permissibly. It may therefore be said that a person would not abandon a permitted course and consume a forbidden item, as I wrote in Shach. See there.
