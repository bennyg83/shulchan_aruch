After them. Baer Heitev and what he wrote — if one forgot the yud of Eloheinu, etc. — Taz wrote that likewise there is no remedy to scrape the nun and vav and write yud-nun-vav, for here too he sanctified the name of Elokai, etc.; rather the correct way is to remove the sheet entirely; and if it is not easy to remove the sheet and write another, one should scrape the entire Name — and though it is forbidden to scrape mentions, nevertheless scribes already practice this — see there. And what he wrote "scribes already scrape" — see Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 81 who protests those who scrape — see there at length; and see previous note from this. And what he wrote there is no remedy to scrape nun-vav — see responsum Maharam of Lublin siman 112 who raised one should do so — erase nun-vav and write yud-nun-vav; and see responsum Shemen Rokeach part 1 siman 47 that the essential view is like Maharshal, and had Taz seen Maharam Lublin's words he would not have written thus — see there; and see responsum Avodat HaGershuni siman 95 and She'eilat Yaavetz part 1 siman 152 [and see Chatom Sofer siman 263 regarding a sefer Torah found in the word Elokeinu where the sofer extended the bottom of the nun and wrote vav beside it so the upper part of the nun is separate from the vav and the bottom is adjacent — invalid per Magen Avraham OC siman 32 note 33; and the questioner's view not to erase the vav explicitly since it is attached to the Name but to write vav first inside the nun as Magen Avraham wrote like "penei artzi" — thus automatically the attachment of that vav is nullified and one may then erase it explicitly even though the vav was written inside the nun — it is only causing erasure, supported by responsum Avodat HaGershuni siman 98; he answered Avodat HaGershuni there plainly that attachment after the Name is only d'rabbanan — therefore he entered this leniency; in my opinion both are incorrect — attachment after the Name is d'oraisa (see there siman 260 where he himself agreed to those who hold only d'rabbanan); and to write a letter whereby another letter becomes invalid through complete erasure is not gerama but worse — if one erases the vav first by hand there is only prohibition of erasure, but when writing a second vav one nullifies the first erasure — the mitzvah is done through an actual transgression. He raised in our case: if it clearly appears as writing split in two — certainly permitted to erase the vav explicitly and rewrite inside the nun, for we erase because it appears as two words — then it is not attached to the Name; but if it does not clearly appear as two words — Beit Yosef is in doubt. Magen Avraham there that Levush wrote to be stringent — is a mistake, not found in Levush. Thus impossible to erase the vav — perhaps it is valid; also forbidden to scrape the Name though a mistakenly written Name scribes scrape when the Name causes disqualification — but here it was written in holiness and perhaps the vav is valid — its erasure is doubtful d'oraisa prohibition — I know no remedy; nevertheless so as not to bury an entire sheet, one may permit scraping the vav and burying it and writing another vav inside the nun — see there; and see Penei Yehoshua Choshen Mishpat siman 5 regarding one who needed to write Elokay and forgot the vav — permitted to hang it between lines — see there; and Shemen Rokeach regarding a sefer Torah missing yud in Elohim — best to erase mem and write yud-mem, and Taz agrees — see there. [Similarly Chatom Sofer siman 274 in a sefer Torah in the verse "et haElohim hithalech Noach" where the sofer omitted hei of Elohim and wrote Elayim — plainly one should erase yud-mem and write hei-yud-mem in their place and they require sanctification as initially — see there.] See Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 72 regarding a sofer who wanted to write "vayomer Hashem Elokim el haNachash" and erred and did not write Elokim — doubtful whether he wrote the word El for sanctity of Elokim or not — raised to scrape the word El and attach parchment outside, only not to write the Name on the patch — wrote that though Taz was stringent not to scrape only when not easy to remove the sheet, here it is close to certain he intended "el haNachash" literally, therefore permitted to scrape even when easy to remove the sheet so as not to come to burying Names written in holiness because of a distant doubt — see there; and responsum R' Akiva Eiger siman 70 on similar case in Bereishit "vayomer Yaakov el Yosef el Shaddai" written only "vayomer Yaakov el Shaddai" and two words "el Yosef" missing — cannot fix by erasing earlier words and writing "vayomer Yaakov el Yosef" — who says the sofer forgot "el Yosef," perhaps he wrote "vayomer Yaakov el" and forgot "Yosef el" — then the word El was not written in holiness; passing the quill for its sake does not help; one should fix by hanging between lines between El and Shaddai the two words "Yosef el" and sanctify that El — though one may question whether the sofer sanctified El and now the first El would be called El — like erasure; this reasoning is proven in Levush this siman though not mandatory (see note 10 in the name of Beit Yosef); even for Levush one could say it is only d'rabbanan resembling erasure — thus here doubtful whether sanctified — doubtful d'rabbanan; therefore no concern that by hanging they will think the first El is mundane and come to erase it (see Avnei Nezer siman 143 note 3) — since only doubt and even if erased perhaps no prohibition — we are not so concerned — see there. Therefore also in Chinuch Beit Yehudah's case one may do this regulation — hang the word Elohim before "el haNachash" — this is preferable. See Pri Tevuah siman 73 regarding a sheet where the sofer should have written two Names Havayah and Elohim and wrote Havayah instead of Elohim and Elohim instead of Havayah — since not easy to remove the sheet, the lenient one relies on Taz and scrapes the Names (only not write Names on the patch, as note 1); see above siman 275 end note 4 in the name of responsum Shivat Tzion. See Penei Aryeh siman 45 regarding Elohim Elohekha written instead of Eloheikha — permitted to erase kaf and write closed mem in its place — attached letters have no sanctity of their own except through the Name that sanctifies them — not sanctified unless written knowingly with intent they be sanctified through the Name when attached; if written without intent or mistakenly — no sanctity, permitted to erase (see responsum Brit Avraham part 1 siman 12); therefore this kaf written in error — even if he thought he was writing mem and wrote kaf intending kaf to attach to the Name — also as if written without intent, permitted to erase; he concluded one who is stringent and brings an entire sheet with Names to burial must bring proof [see previous note this is Bnei Yonah's view, not Chatom Sofer — per his words forbidden to erase kaf explicitly, only permitted to peel and bury and write mem in its place — see there]; and see there s.v. vehu hein hacha that if this error were at the head of the sheet where he began to write Elohim and wrote Eloheikha — not evident to all that he erred, perhaps he intended another parashah beginning Eloheikha though he himself knows not so — one could say devarim shebalev — therefore the kaf was sanctified — see there siman 46 that erasing attachment after the Name is only d'rabbanan per Taz and Mishna Berurah and Maharam Lublin, not like Levush — see there.
