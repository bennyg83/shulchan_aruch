**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An agent.</b> Shach cites several leading poskim who rule that a father may redeem his firstborn son through an agent. Likewise, beit din may redeem him if the father failed to do so, or if the father died before the redemption. Taz writes that as long as the father is alive he may appoint an agent to redeem the child using the father's money. For this reason the paternal grandfather is like the father, and another person may also give his own money in place of the father and redeem the firstborn as the father's agent. But if the father died, the child should not be redeemed until he grows up. Maharil writes likewise. Nekudot HaKesef disputes Taz at length and concludes that if the father died, beit din may redeem the child. See there.

<b>בשבת. </b> משום דהוי כמקח וממכר ומזה הטעם גם ביו"ט אין פודין אותו ונראה דפודין נמי בלילה מיהו נוהגין לפדות ביום ואם חל יום ל"א בתענית צבור יש לראות אם הגיע הבן לכ"ט י"ב תשצ"ג קודם יום התענית שהוא יום ל' יש לפדותו ביום ל' אחר כ"ט י"ב תשצ"ג <small>(ואפשר לומר דמשום סעודה לחוד לא מקדמינן לפדות ביום ל')</small> ואם לאו אז יש לפדותו בליל ל"א של יום התענית אבל אין לפדותו ביום התענית ולעשות סעודה בליל שאחר התענית דליכא פרסומא כה"ג מיהו שמעתי שנוהגין לפדות ביום התענית ולעשות הסעודה בליל שאחר התענית. עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On Shabbos.</b> The redemption is not performed on Shabbos because it resembles buying and selling. For the same reason it is not performed on Yom Tov. It appears that it may be performed at night, although the custom is to redeem during the day. If day 31 falls on a public fast, one must determine whether the child has completed twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts before the fast day, which is his thirtieth day. If so, he may be redeemed on day 30 after that precise time. Perhaps we do not advance the redemption to day 30 merely for the sake of the meal. If he has not yet reached that point, he should be redeemed on the night of day 31, the night before the fast. He should not be redeemed during the fast day with the meal postponed until after the fast, because there is insufficient publicity in that arrangement. Nevertheless, I have heard that the custom is to redeem during the fast day and hold the meal that night.

<b>ילדו. </b> כ' הט"ז דזה קאי אסעיף כ"ט בב' נשים של ב' אנשים ושניהם לא בכרו וקשה למה כתב כאן בב' זכרים וב' נקבות דאז אין כאן בכור ודאי דכל חד אומר נקבה יצאה קודם לו לימא רבותא אפי' בשני זכרים ונקבה אחת דאז הוי א' בכור ודאי ואפ"ה פטורים שניהם דכל חד ידחה על חבירו לומר דהוא הבכור ובאמת כתב הטור גם בזה דפטורים ונראה דהמחבר אזיל לטעמיה שכתב בסכ"ו דהבנים חייבים במת אביהם תוך ל' יום ולא מצי כל חד לדחות על חבירו ה"נ כן הוא אלא דקשה הא ברייתא ערוכה היא כאן דאף בכה"ג פטור וכו' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They gave birth.</b> Taz writes that this refers back to seif 29, concerning two women belonging to two different men, neither of whom had previously given birth. It is difficult why the Mechaber here discusses two males and two females, where there is no definite firstborn because each father can say that the female was born first. He could have stated a greater novelty: even if there were two males and one female, where one of the males is certainly a firstborn, both fathers are still exempt because each can push the claim onto the other and say that the other child is the firstborn. Tur in fact rules that they are exempt in that case too. It appears that the Mechaber follows his own view in seif 26, where he says the sons are obligated when their father dies within thirty days and each cannot deflect the obligation onto the other. The same would apply here. But this remains difficult because there is an explicit baraita stating that even in such a case they are exempt. See there.
