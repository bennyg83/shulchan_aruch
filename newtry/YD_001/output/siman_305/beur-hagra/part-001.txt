**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc. </b>One needs to know that the measure of the perutot and all the coins has not changed from the days of Moshe, as is written in the first chapter of Kiddushin (12a): Rav Yosef thought a perutah, etc. Only the sela, which one can increase as above, but not decrease, and its measure is as above, 24 ma'in. And the Geonim weighed the perutah and found that it weighs half a barleycorn, as the Rif and Rambam and all the poskim wrote. It emerges that a ma'ah is 16 barleycorns, and a dinar 96, and a sela 384. And the later authorities weighed and found, in Kaiser weight, 5 lot and a third. And even though the Taz wrote "and a quint, which is a quarter," he was not precise in a small matter, and see there. And the Geonim wrote that all this is refined, purified silver. Now, in earlier times their coin was refined silver and large, and because of this silver was very cheap, until a zekuk, which is 16 lot, which is called in our language a mark, was two gold coins, and at most two gold coins and a half. Therefore our ketubah, which the later authorities enacted as two hundred zekukim, is 400 gold coins, and in another place 500 gold coins. Afterwards they began to adulterate the coins and put copper in them, and silver became expensive, until in the days of the Maharil a zekuk was five gold coins, as he wrote there in siman 80; and after him, in the days of Mahariv, a zekuk was six gold coins, as is written in his responsa in siman 14. And so is the calculation of the Rav, that a zekuk is six gold coins. It emerges that 5 lot and a third, which is a third of a zekuk, is 2 gold coins; and so he wrote in Nachalat Shivah. But now that the coins have been ruined and adulterated very, very much — more than our fathers imagined — the Taz and Nachalat Shivah wrote that all this is Kaiser weight, that 11 lot and a quarter of Kaiser is our zekuk. It emerges that each of their lot is one and a half lot less a twelfth, so that 5 lot and a third is 7 lot and a half and a twelfth. And all this is if our lot equals that which was in the days of the Taz, and it is possible that this too has diminished. And the rule: know that a Kaiser lot weighs 360 barleycorns. Nevertheless the ketubah did not change: even though they write zekukim, nevertheless in all such cases it is according to the custom, as the Rif wrote (at the beginning of chapter 7 of Yevamot), and they remained with the original custom, that they were obligated only for 400 or 500 gold coins. And so Mahariv wrote there according to their custom. And the measure of the ketubah according to this calculation is, for a virgin, 2 gold coins and a half, and for a widow a gold coin and a quarter. And what the Rav wrote in Even HaEzer siman 66, for a virgin 10 gold coins and for a widow 5 gold coins, that is according to Rabbeinu Tam, who wrote that a maneh is a zekuk, and the Maharil wrote according to this in his days that a zekuk is 5 gold coins, as he wrote there, and this is "and there are those, etc." that he wrote there, and so the later authorities wrote. But according to the main law it is only 2 gold coins and a half in the days of Mahariv and the Rav. And now, if we were to come to measure by the silver of the coin itself and the copper that is in it, even though it is worth something, one should not count it at all, as is written in Nachalat Shivah, for it is not sufficient for extracting the silver from it and its expense. And so it is in the Rambam and all the poskim: the calculation is only according to the silver that is in it; the calculation would be large, except that nevertheless one should calculate according to the rate at which they take refined silver and redeem with coins not in the category of silver but in the category of equivalent-to-silver. Therefore the pidyon is not equal in two places even with one coin, only according to the rate of silver in that coin there (and see what I wrote in Even HaEzer siman 66 seif 6).
(Likut) See Torat Kohanim, parashat Bechukotai, end of chapter 11 [and in Bekhorot 50a]: "twenty gerah shall be the shekel" — from where that if he wished to increase he may increase? The verse says "shall be." And from where that if he wished to decrease he may not decrease? The verse says "it is" (etc.).

<b>או שני זכרים כו'. </b>גמ' שם מ"ט א' תנא ר"ה כו'. והנה שיטת רש"י דקאי אמ"ש בסכ"ט ב' נשים של שני אנשים שלא בכרו וזש"ש ותנא דידן כו' אבל הרא"ש כתב ומציין זו"נ אין כאן לכהן כלום תני ר"ה שני זכרים ונקיבה אין כאן לכהן כלום וכ"כ הרמב"ם והראב"ד אלא שדעת הראב"ד דקאי אסיפא לבד אמ"ש בסל"א כאן וכמש"ש ותנא דידן כיון דבשני כו' והרמב"ם כתב דקאי ארישא לבד אשתי נשים של איש א' א' בכרה וא' לא בכרה דסכ"ח וגירסתו היתה ותנא דידן כיון דבאיש אחד כו' אבל הטור מפרש דקאי אשניהם דאין סברא לחלק ביניהם ולכן כתב כאן ובסכ"ח אבל מש"ש ותנא דידן כו' קשה לדבריו ונמשך אחרי דברי הרא"ש שלא הזכיר מש"ש ותנא כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or two males etc. </b> Gemara there 49a: a Tanna [taught in the name of] Rav Huna etc. And behold, Rashi's view is that it refers to what was written in seif 29: two women of two men who have not given birth, and that is what it said "and our Tanna" etc. But the Rosh wrote, and cites: a male and a female — there is nothing here for the kohen; Rav Huna taught: two males and a female — there is nothing here for the kohen. And so wrote the Rambam and the Raavad; except that the Raavad's opinion is that it refers to the seifa alone, to what was written here in seif 31, and as written there: "and our Tanna, since with two" etc. And the Rambam wrote that it refers to the reisha alone: two women of one man, one who has given birth and one who has not given birth, of seif 28, and his girsa was: "and our Tanna, since with one man" etc. But the Tur explains that it refers to both of them, for there is no reason to distinguish between them, and therefore he wrote here and in seif 28. But what is written there "and our Tanna" etc. is difficult according to his words, and he followed the Rosh's words, who did not mention what is written there "and our Tanna" etc.
