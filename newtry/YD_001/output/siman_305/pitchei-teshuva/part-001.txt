**** ENGLISH ****
<b>We give to a kohen. </b> See what I wrote above siman 61 se'if katan 1 regarding giving against his will, whether it is called giving or not. [And see in the teshuvah of Chatam Sofer siman 252, who was asked: when the day of his son's pidyon has arrived and there is no adult kohen here, only a minor, or one whom the adult kohen made an agent to receive the five sela'im for him, whether he can redeem through a kohen's agent or through a minor kohen. And he answered: our eyes have already seen what Pri Chadash wrote in siman 61, that one fulfills giving matanot kehunah by acquiring for the kohen through his agent with the kohen's knowledge and will, and his proof from seif 14 there is clear; except that he wrote according to the Sifrei that without the kohen's knowledge he would not acquire through an agent, and the reason is simple, for the kohen who receives matanot kehunah also performs a mitzvah in his receiving, and a mitzvah performed by himself is greater than through his agent; therefore sometimes it is not agreeable to him; but with the kohen's will the agent can acquire for him and the owners fulfill giving. And the same applies to a minor kohen, as is explained at the end of the chapter Lulav HaGazul. And even if we say a minor has no kinyan from the Torah, nevertheless regarding matanot kehunah it is not written "and he shall give into his hand" that a hand is needed to acquire etc. But since the Geonim enacted that the kohen should say "what do you prefer more" etc., and all their words are tradition and Torah, and it implies from this that the kohen himself also has power in the act of the pidyon, and for this he cannot make a Yisrael an agent, and likewise a minor has no power to take the bechor out of his obligation accordingly; therefore it is good to bring a kohen from another place; see there.]

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A gift given on condition that it be returned is legally a gift.</b> See Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah, part 1, simanim 55 to 57. He writes that this applies specifically where the very same object is to be returned, because during the interim it genuinely belongs to the recipient, who is even exempt from unavoidable loss. If the condition is to return different money, however, it is not a gift but a full loan. Therefore, if one gives a kohen five sela on condition that the kohen return five different sela, the son is not redeemed. See his lengthy discussion.

<b>Exempt from the pidyon. </b>See Be'er Heitev of the Rav Maharit of blessed memory, what he wrote that the Shach left this law as requiring further study. And one who looks in the Shach will see that it is not so, but rather he left as requiring further study whether also regarding his wife who is married or an arusah the law is thus; see there. And see in Sha'ar HaMelech chapter 15 of Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah, end of halacha 17, after he brought the opinion of many of the Rishonim that one who says "this is my son, a mamzer" is believed even when she contradicts him, and he wrote: and I am astonished at the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 305 se'if katan 23, that the matter is in doubt for him whether this law of Rabbi Yehudah who said a person is believed to say "this is my son, a mamzer" is even when she contradicts him, and he proved so from the words of the Rav Terumat HaDeshen, and in the end he left the matter as requiring further study; and how did the company of prophets that we wrote escape him, who hold that even when she contradicts him he is believed, except according to the opinion of Tosafot Rid; and nevertheless it appears that in his case he is exempt from pidyon, since according to the opinion of Tosafot Rid it is true, and also according to the opinion of Bahag and Rabbeinu Tam that Maran the Beit Yosef wrote in Even HaEzer siman 4, that they hold that specifically when there is recognition of a bechor he is believed to make him a mamzer, but otherwise he is not believed; also according to the opinion of Riaz that he wrote in Shiltei Giborim on the Mishnah of "this is my son, a mamzer," he holds that specifically when he says "this is my son and he is a mamzer" he is believed, but when he says "he is not my son" he is not believed; and if so he can say "I hold like those rabbis," and the one who would take from his fellow must bring proof; so it appears to me as the halacha, end of his words:

<b>Its head was dead.</b> Dagul MeRevavah explains that this means the fetus retracted and afterward its siblings emerged, such as with twins. If the entire fetus was born, however, it certainly exempts the one born afterward, because a dead eight-month fetus is no less than a formed fetal sac, which exempts the subsequent birth.
