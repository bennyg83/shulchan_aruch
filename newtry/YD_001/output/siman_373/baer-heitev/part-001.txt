**** ENGLISH ****
<b>She becomes impure.</b> Meaning she is not obligated to become impure for him, but she is permitted to do so. Shach. Tur and Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer siman 55, write likewise. Rashi in Yevamot explains that "she does not become impure" is not literal, since daughters of Aharon were never warned against corpse impurity. The phrase appears only because the Mishnah also says she does not observe aninut. In a second explanation Rashi says it refers to a festival, when Israelites too are commanded to purify themselves and avoid impurity. According to that second explanation, a woman would be prohibited from becoming impure for him on a festival because she too is obligated to purify herself for the festival. Baer Heitev notes that it is surprising the early and later poskim omitted this detail. He answers that the general obligation to purify oneself for a festival fundamentally applied in Temple times, when people needed purity in order to eat sacred foods. Nowadays everyone is impure from corpse impurity, there is no ashes of the red heifer, and the Sages also imposed impurity on lands outside Eretz Yisrael. Thus there is no meaningful distinction between the festival and the rest of the year regarding corpse impurity. Some authorities who say that even nowadays a person must purify himself for the festival refer to bodily impurities and similar matters, and also to spiritual purification. Accordingly, the poskim adopted Rashi's first explanation: "she does not become impure" is not literal. It means only that she is not obligated to involve herself in the burial. But for one's seven close relatives, a kohen, and certainly an Israelite or a woman, may and where applicable must attend to them both on festivals and during the year.

<b>הגולל. </b> כתב הש"ך בסי' שע"ה כתב הטור שיש פלוגתא בין רש"י ור"ת מהו הגולל והב"ח כ' דנקטינן כרש"י שפירש דהוא כסוי הארון וכתב הרמב"ן דלרש"י כשנותנין אותו בבית בארון וסותמין הכסוי במסמרים ע"ד ליתן הארון בקבר הוי סתימה והוא תמה עליו דהיאך אפשר לומר שאם סתמו ארונו בבית שלא יטמא בנו עליו כו' ולי נראה דמה שפירש"י בכל מקום גולל היינו כסוי הארון אין ר"ל אפילו סתמו בבית אלא ר"ל משיסתם כסוי הארון בקבר תדע דבפ' שואל אמרי' כל שאומרים בפני המת יודע עד שיסתם הגולל דכתיב וישוב העפר אל הארץ כשהיה ופירש"י גופו קרי עפר ומששב אל הארץ כאשר היה מיד והרוח תשוב שאין בו עוד רוח ע"כ ולפ"ז אפילו נסתם הארון במסמרים בבית גם לרש"י בנו מיטמא עליו עד שיסתם בקבר עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The golel.</b> Shach writes that in siman 375 Tur records the dispute between Rashi and Rabbenu Tam regarding the meaning of golel. Bach rules like Rashi, who explains it as the coffin cover. Ramban writes that according to Rashi, once a corpse is placed in a coffin in the house and the lid is nailed shut with intent to place the coffin in the grave, that closing counts as the final closure. Ramban questions this: how can it be that once the coffin is sealed at home, a son may no longer become impure for his father? Shach explains that Rashi never meant closing the coffin in the house. Wherever Rashi explains golel as the coffin cover, he means when the coffin is closed in the grave. Proof appears in Shabbos, where the Gemara says that everything said in the presence of the deceased is known to him until the golel is closed, citing, "the dust returns to the earth as it was." Rashi explains that the body is called dust and that when it returns to the earth as it was, the spirit departs. Accordingly, even if the coffin is nailed shut in the house, according to Rashi the son may still become impure for him until it is closed in the grave.
