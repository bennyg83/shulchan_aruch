Therefore we estimate it by the majority of the width of the cavity of the kaneh. Thus Rashba: and it appears that nevertheless if the measure of the indentation is not more than half the round width of the opening of the kaneh — kosher; and as we say regarding a bird: one folds it and places it on the opening of the kaneh — if it covers the majority of the kaneh — treifah; and if not — kosher; hence in a bird whose life-force is small we go by the majority of the opening of the kaneh — all the more in an animal whose life-force is great — end of his words. And regarding what the Rosh challenged — "and according to what will be explained shortly," etc. — to my humble opinion certainly the measure of an issur is more than the majority of the circumference of the animal's gargeret; and this is sensible to the sense of sight — for it is in any case larger than a bird's gargeret, as Rashi, Rosh, Ran, and other poskim wrote regarding a bird that there is not in its gargeret like an issur — see there; and in the majority of the circumference of the gargeret it is only one strap from punctures like this (and in an issur there are many such straps — except where punctures have no deficiency we never combine to a majority except when standing around the circumference like this) — in a matter that if you join them together the majority of the gargeret would be severed; but when standing along the length of the kaneh like this — even if the strap is so long as the majority of the circumference of the gargeret — they do not combine to a majority, for they are not connected together; certainly no less than split in seif 7 — that even if only something remained above and below — kosher (and see Kesef Mishneh chapter 3 of Hilchot Shechitah law 23 "if punctured punctures," etc.). But in punctures that have deficiency — combination applies only when standing along the length or in the middle of the gargeret; for when standing on the circumference of the gargeret it is obvious they combine to a majority — certainly no less than punctures without deficiency; and we derive nothing from "if they have deficiency" except that certainly what combines in punctures with deficiency to an issur is when standing along the length or in the middle of the gargeret in a matter that does not encircle the majority of the kaneh; and therefore to my humble opinion — even though a majority is less than an issur, as stated, and as implied somewhat from Rashi (daf 45a) "hence since," etc., until "and regarding punctures requires an issur to estimate them" — see there — nevertheless without deficiency it is treifah since they stand on the circumference of the gargeret — which is literally as though severed if you join them; but with deficiency — necessarily we deal when along the length of the kaneh, as explained; and combination of a majority does not apply, for they do not stand on the circumference; and therefore when treifah in punctures with deficiency — necessarily the reason is not severance but as though deficient; and therefore they combine to the measure of deficiency — meaning an issur, which is more than the majority of the cavity of the kaneh; and as I wrote it is proven from Rabbeinu Yerucham's words: some wrote that if it is a large bird and in its kaneh there is like an issur — we estimate it as an issur like in an animal; and some say a bird whose life-force is not so great as an animal — we do not estimate it as an issur even if in its width there is like an issur, but we estimate by a majority; and the first view appears primary — end; and Beit Yosef brings it; and since he wrote a bird's life-force is not so great, etc. — implies the measure of an issur is more than if it covers a large bird; and all the more it is more than the majority of its gargeret's circumference; and nevertheless he wrote the first view is primary — meaning in punctures with deficiency that combine to an issur in a large bird; and in punctures without deficiency he admits that even in a large bird they combine to a majority; if so, punctures without deficiency would be more stringent than punctures with deficiency — but certainly punctures without deficiency apply only when standing on the circumference of the gargeret, and punctures with deficiency only in the middle of the gargeret or along its length, as explained; and so it appears from other poskim' words, as explained. And in Hagahot Rama he wrote: and now that we are not expert in an issur how much it is — we estimate by the width of the cavity of the kaneh; and also in an animal we estimate thus — that if the punctures between them cover the cavity of the opening of the kaneh — treifah; and likewise piercing one long puncture — we estimate the deficiency of the strap also at this measure — end; and Rama omitted the hagahah of the Rosh; and perhaps he intended what I wrote. And what Bach wrote in seif 8 on this — I did not descend to the end of his mind in all his words — see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
["If punctured around its circumference," etc.] Meaning specifically punctured around the circumference exactly like this; but punctures without deficiency along the length of the kaneh do not combine to the majority of the circumference — for it would only be as though split; therefore punctures without deficiency never disqualify except when standing on the circumference of the gargeret in its majority — so too Rosh: punctures without deficiency combine to a majority if standing on the circumference of the gargeret — end; but punctures with deficiency apply only when standing in the middle or along the length of the gargeret — for on the circumference, even without deficiency, they disqualify, similar to severance; and we derive nothing from "if they have deficiency." And therefore the Mechaber's wording is ambiguous: he opened "if punctured around its circumference — if they have no deficiency," etc., and "if they have deficiency," etc. — implying with deficiency also refers to around the circumference; and Tur's wording is more ambiguous: he wrote "and if punctured around its circumference thin punctures adjacent to one another like sieve punctures — if they have deficiency they combine to an issur — that if there is an issur in the punctures with the intact portion between them — treifah; and if they have no deficiency — the measure is a majority" — until here his language; and therefore it must be said thus: "and if punctured around its circumference" — then the law is thus: if there is an issur in the punctures, etc. — treifah even though they do not encircle the majority but some punctures stand on the circumference and some along the length — for it is possible when there are many punctures the size of an issur that they encircle a little of the kaneh; and if they have no deficiency — then it is not treifah on a slight circumference except we require encircling the kaneh in its majority; and so interpret the Mechaber's words — and understand.
