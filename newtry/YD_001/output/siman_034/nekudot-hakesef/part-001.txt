<b>(סימן ל"ד סעיף ב' בהג"ה) שיעור איסר. </b>ברמב"ם בפי' המשנה בפ"ב דחולין דאיסר הוא ארבעה גרגרי שעורים ועוביו כראוי וצורתו עגולה ובפ"ק דקידושין דף י' סלע הוא ד' דינרין ודינר הוא כ"ד איסרין נמצא שהאיסר הוא אחד מצ"ו חלקי' מסלע וסלע הוא שליש טפח דהיינו שלשה גרעיני תמרים וכן הוא בסימן מ"ח ובשפתי כהן סי' ל' ס"ק ח' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 34 seif 2 in hagahah) Measure of an issur. In Rambam's explanation of the mishnah in chapter 2 of Chullin — an issur is four grains of barley and its thickness as fit and its form round; and in chapter 1 of Kiddushin daf 10 — a sela is four dinarim and a dinar is twenty-four issurim; hence an issur is one ninety-sixth of a sela; and a sela is one-third tefach — meaning three date-pits; and so in siman 48 and Siftei Kohen siman 30 s.k. 8 — see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
(Shach s.k. 4) The Gaon z"l expanded to challenge my elder Maharam Isserles on what Rama wrote in this siman: "and according to what will be explained shortly," etc. — and he challenged him in s.k. 4; and Taz also expanded and challenged him; and both intended one matter; and to my humble opinion their words are wondrous in my eyes — for from the Rosh's and Rabbeinu Yerucham's words it is proven explicitly that the majority of the cavity of the opening of the kaneh is more than what is asked on a bird: if we estimate as an issur according to animal proportion — then there would be a great stringency for a bird, since it is a very small measure; therefore we estimate in a bird by the majority of the width of the cavity of the opening of the kaneh — thus Beit Yosef; and the Rosh explains his words: wherever we estimate in an animal by a majority — likewise in a bird we estimate by a majority; and wherever we estimate in an animal as an issur — he need not ask on a bird what is obvious that we estimate according to its smallness; but regarding knafah puncture where we estimate the intact portion with the punctures as an issur when they are close like sieve punctures destined to be deficient — that is what he asks on a bird: for when estimating the measure of deficiency not proportionate to the animal's issur — its measure of deficiency is very small; do we be stringent at such a small measure to combine the intact portion with the deficiency? — and he answers: no — only another measure the Sages gave: "one folds it and places it on the opening of the kaneh" — end; and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote these matters with some expansion of explanation: if the kaneh was punctured in a bird knafah-style — punctures with deficiency — in an animal its measure is like an issur; and if you estimate thus in a bird you will not find in it an issur, for the entire bird's kaneh is not like an issur; and if you say everything according to large and small — its measure of deficiency would be very small; and if you say estimate the issur according to large and small and consider in this the intact portion between puncture and puncture with the deficiency of the punctures — there would be a great stringency; rather we estimate in a bird: one cuts below the punctures and at their sides until one can fold the strap with the punctures and place it on the opening of the kaneh — if it covers between the punctures and the intact portion between puncture and puncture the majority of the cavity of the kaneh — treifah — until here Beit Yosef's language; and the Gaon z"l brings it in small seif 6 — behold explicitly as his words. And what the Gaon proved from Rabbeinu Yerucham's words: "and the first appears primary," etc. — is according to his method in what he wrote initially that an issur is more than the majority of the cavity of the opening of the kaneh; but according to what I wrote there is no such thing — the majority is more; yet this may be challenged on Rabbeinu Yerucham's words: "and some explain a bird whose life-force is not so great as an animal — we do not estimate it as an issur even if in its width there is an issur; on the contrary, because its life-force is small — it should be estimated as an issur since it is a small measure" — one may say thus: according to the first explanation — in a large bird we estimate like in an animal; he meant: we estimate by proportion; and it teaches us: do not say since in a bird there is nothing to estimate as an issur because it is very small — likewise in a large bird; it teaches us we estimate by proportion; and some hold: in a bird whose life-force is not so great as an animal — we do not estimate as an issur but by the width of the cavity of a small bird's kaneh; and we do not distinguish large bird and small — examine. And this difficulty I challenged in my youth to the Gaon z"l; and this was his written reply yesterday: I studied what my son Moshe challenged — about three hours at night; and I saw that what my son-in-law answered (that even in an animal the issur is larger than a majority, but in a bird the majority is larger than its issur) — this is not so (from Rashba who learns in an animal we estimate by majority stringently from bird; and Beit Yosef brings it; and according to my son-in-law — whence do we know bird is different? — examine); but I wished to say Rashba and the Mechaber disagree with the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham in small seif 7; but now I arose at dawn about two hours and saw he challenged nothing; and it is clear to me like the sun at noon that truth is with me — therefore you too be precise and aim at truth — end. And behold I spoke with the Gaon mouth to mouth — thus is the explanation of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham: always an issur is more than the majority of the cavity; and "very small," etc., he meant: since we have no known measure in a small bird how much its deficiency will be, but we estimate according to the proportion of the animal's measure — and in a very small bird impossible to estimate by proportion to a large animal so that the measure in the small bird would be its narrow measure; and perforce we need be stringent to a lesser measure proportionate to very small; and this is a great stringency; and I challenged him: where did the Rosh write "and wherever we estimate in animal as an issur," etc. — "we estimate according to its smallness" — see there — implies we estimate; and according to the Gaon's words — where do we estimate, since impossible to narrow as mentioned above? And what he answered me: it is not so regarding deficiency in one place — one cannot estimate it, only we are stringent even more than its measure even in very small, since punctured in one place; unlike knafah puncture — there are two stringencies: one that impossible to estimate so much; and must be the puncture less than an issur; and we are stringent that the intact portion between punctures must combine — if so it is two stringencies and a great stringency: what cannot be estimated and also must count the intact portion between punctures — until here his words; and I challenged his words: one, from the Rosh's words it does not imply thus; and further, the Talmud sages could estimate; and further difficult is the Rosh's challenge on Rif's explanation in a bird: what? — it refers to deficiency — what is the question on a bird? — how do we estimate more than other issurim such as deficiency of skull for Beit Shammai as a full drill and for Beit Hillel as a sela — see there; and according to the Gaon we are different here because impossible to estimate — but certainly they could estimate; and further difficult: according to the Gaon's answer — because impossible — what did the Talmud sages give us the measure of the majority of the cavity of the opening — a measure less than an issur — so we would know to estimate? If so they should have given us a measure more than the majority of the cavity of the kaneh and less than an issur proportionately; and so we find they dispute in siman 48 whether like a sela or more than a sela; and similarly the Gaon challenged in siman 30 s.k. 20 in Siftei Kohen — see there (and also according to what I wrote the measure of an issur implies it is very small); and still requires examination.
