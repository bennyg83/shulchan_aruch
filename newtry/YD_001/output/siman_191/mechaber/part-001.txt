**** ENGLISH ****
The law of a woman who found blood in her urination; it contains one se’if. A woman who urinated water and blood came out with her urine — whether she urinated while standing or whether she urinated while sitting — behold she is tehorah. And even if she felt her body and shuddered, she is not concerned, for this feeling is the feeling of her urination, for her urine is not from the cheder, and this blood is the blood of a wound in the rectum or in the kidney. {Rama: And some say that one should not permit her except when she urinated while sitting; but while standing: if it flows into a vessel and blood was found there, she is tehorah; but if it drips on the rim of the vessel and blood was found there, she is tamei, since, because the makor is narrow, they return to the makor and bring blood (Tur in the name of Rosh). And some say that even while sitting one should not permit except when it flows and the blood was found inside the vessel, but on the rim of the vessel she is tamei; and while standing, in every case she is tamei (Mordechai, Hilchos Niddah; and Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos and Sha”d and Be’er Sheva, siman 190; and so it implies from a responsum of Ran and from Maharam in responsa, siman 630; and Agudah; and the gloss of Sha”d in the name of Maharat). And so is the practice. And this is specifically when the blood was found in the vessel into which she urinates alone, for it is known that it is from her; but if it was found in a vessel into which a man and a woman both urinate, she is tehorah in every case (Tur and poskim from the Talmud). And all this is when blood was found incidentally; but a woman who is accustomed to seeing blood in her urine and feels pain at the time she urinates, such as the illness they call “arin vind,” it appears that one may permit in every case, for there are clear indications that she has a wound that pains her when she urinates, and from it the blood comes out; and even if she found blood after urinating, when she wipes herself, she is tehorah, for since she feels pain, and she does not find blood except after urinating, certainly it is the blood of a wound (in the gloss of Sha”d in the name of Mahari”l; and it is in the responsum of Mahari”l, siman 203, who wrote so in the name of Maharash; and Beit Yosef in the name of Agur in the name of Maharash). However, some are stringent not to permit except for a woman who has a veset, to require bedikah (the view of Mahari”l there) — meaning, before she urinates she should check herself well in the holes and cracks, and if she does not find blood, she should insert clean cotton inside on the makor, and urinate, and wipe herself well from the urine, and remove the cotton; if it is clean, it is a great proof that the blood is not from the makor (there, and in Mahari”v). And so is the practice. And if she checked herself 3 times in this manner and found the cotton clean, afterward she is permitted without checking not at the time of her veset, for it is a chazakah that it is the blood of a wound, since she finds it only after she urinated. And all this is specifically when she feels pain with her urine; but if she does not feel pain and she checks herself after urinating and finds blood — if she did not find blood in her urine, she is certainly tamei (so it implies there, and in Hagahos Maimoniyos and in Mordechai there). But if she found blood within the urine, and also on the ed with which she checked herself, some say that she is tamei, for they permitted only blood that was found within urine (Mordechai, Hilchos Niddah); and some say that she is tehorah, for we attribute that blood was still remaining from the essence of the urine (Beit Yosef from the implication of Rosh and Ran) (and in my humble opinion nothing is implied from Rosh). And one should be stringent; however, she does not need to check after this, and even if she was accustomed to see [blood] — if she checked herself 3 times and found [herself] tehorah, she no longer needs bedikah (there in Mordechai). And if she is not accustomed to see except at intervals, she establishes for herself a veset if it is in a fixed manner, whether a veset shavah or a veset of skipping (there). And if afterward, when she checks herself, she does not find blood, but rather only small grains like sand and red gravel, and this is found also in her urine, and at the time of her veset, or at other times, she sees actual blood like other women, and she does not find that sand except after urinating, she is tehorah, for it is not blood but sand, whose way is to be formed in the kidneys (Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Ran).}
