<b>Small granules.</b> My father-in-law, of blessed memory, writes: it appears certain that if she does not see actual blood after urination, then even if these granules themselves are actual blood, and when rubbed between the fingers dissolve and become blood, we do not take them into account. They are no more stringent than actual blood that exits together with urine, which we attribute to a wound in the kidneys. All the more so these granules.<br><b>Question.</b> A woman has a veset in the ordinary manner of women. At times other than her veset, examination shows her clean from any flow of blood, but she finds tiny red granules as fine as the point of a needle. Some of those granules are absorbed into the linen cloth and some are not absorbed and can be picked from the linen with a needle. When found, they are not sharp like sand. During her veset, once the main flow of blood ceases in the ordinary manner, she finds fluid resembling water in which meat was soaked, containing many more of these granules than at other times. She says that on many occasions she felt pain in the area of her kidneys, although for about nine weeks she has not felt that particular pain. She still constantly feels pain in her abdomen above that place, at times very severe, and it feels to her as though something is moving back and forth there. She sometimes finds these granules while feeling the pain and sometimes afterward. This has been her condition for a long time. What is her law?<br><b>Answer.</b> There are two grounds for permission here. One is the nature of the granules themselves, which commonly arise from a kidney condition, as Beit Yosef cites in siman 191 from Responsa Ran, even where the woman feels no pain. The second is the presence of a wound or condition indicated by actual pain, which can permit even genuine blood. We will first consider the granules themselves. Beit Yosef cites Ran's responsum as permitting them, but then raises a difficulty from Mordechai in the second chapter of Shevuot concerning a woman who finds granules in her urine and is ruled impure. Beit Yosef gives two answers. First, permission applies only to actual sand-like matter, while Mordechai speaks of something merely resembling sand. Second, Mordechai's prohibition concerns a case where she afterward saw actual blood. According to the first answer, we would have no basis to permit the present granules because they are not truly sand, and it would seem proper to be stringent. In truth, however, Darkei Moshe rejects that first answer, and in my opinion correctly, because it has no real foundation. Mordechai's second explanation explicitly shows that his entire discussion concerns whether the visible blood she later found creates prohibition. That itself implies that the earlier granules did not prohibit her. This would already follow even on Beit Yosef's assumption that the two sources conflict and require reconciliation. But in truth I am greatly surprised at Beit Yosef, because there is no contradiction at all. Mordechai is not discussing whether the granules themselves prohibit or permit. Even actual blood can be permitted when found in urine. Mordechai merely teaches that one should not say that because blood found in the urine itself is permitted, actual blood found afterward is also permitted. That is not so. The later visible blood is not covered by the urine-based leniency. Ran, by contrast, speaks about the granules themselves and says they are not legally considered blood at all. He therefore permits even according to R. Meir, who prohibits actual blood found in urine. One might still argue from logic that the granules in the present case perhaps differ in some respect from Ran's granules and we should not automatically infer permission. Even so, there remains the separate ground of the woman's pain. She has experienced pain internally in the kidney area, so it is reasonable to say these granules come from there. She has an ordinary veset like other women, while this phenomenon is unusual, and we may attribute it to a kidney wound outside the time of her veset, as ruled in Piskei Mahari, siman 47. Although for several weeks she has not felt that exact pain, once a wound or condition exists we may continue to attribute the discharge to it even when the pain temporarily subsides, because many conditions cease hurting at times without having fully healed. Even if one wished to be stringent on that basis, here she presently experiences continuing severe pain somewhat above that area. We can certainly attribute the granules to this condition. The fact that the sensation sometimes subsides briefly and returns, while the granules may appear at different moments, does not matter. It is all one condition, and the granules are an excretion resulting from the internal ailment, analogous to Ran's responsum concerning granules found in urine. One should not object from Rama's citation of two opinions where blood is found during urination and afterward, where he says one should be stringent. That case involves no pain and relies only upon the urinary circumstance. Where there is pain, Rama himself earlier permits even when blood is found after urination. Nor is the requirement of examination there a contradiction. Hagahot Sha'arei Dura explicitly writes that if a woman never sees except when the lower abdomen hurts, she is pure because a woman is believed to say she has such a wound. Beit Yosef at the end of this siman cites Agur that if she sometimes sees without pain she is forbidden. None of this is difficult. Those rulings concern a woman whose pain exists only while urinating, while at other times she has no pain at all. She therefore has no basis for leniency outside the painful urination. In our case, however, the woman has a continuing internal pain and it is clear the condition persists at all times. Even when she does not feel it for a short interval, it has not healed because the pain repeatedly returns. Everything can therefore be attributed to the same condition. This would permit even actual blood. All the more so here, where she finds only granules, granules are known to arise from the kidneys, and this woman has repeatedly experienced strong kidney pain. We can reasonably identify them with the granules discussed by Ran. She has an ordinary veset like other women, and immediately after her normal menstrual blood ends these granules appear in a different form. This demonstrates that they are a separate phenomenon.
