**** ENGLISH ****
But only. Shach wrote that from the rav's words it appears that for this view even if he did not learn most of his wisdom from him, as long as he did not grow up to be close to his rav he is not called his student; but in my humble opinion this is not so; rather if most of his wisdom is not from him he need not show him any honor except for tearing keriah only. What the rav proved; that otherwise one cannot have many rav muvhakim; is nothing; one may say he learned most of Scripture from one, Mishnah from one, Gemara from one, midrash and aggadot from one, Kabbalah from one, and similarly in other Torah wisdoms. So too appears the Mechaber's view; below seif 30 he wrote that one who is not rav muvhak, i.e. most of his wisdom is not from him, is not obligated in these matters; here he wrote even though he took leave from one rav it does not suffice etc., but certainly as he explained. Greater still Mahariik wrote: even if he taught him and also ordained him, if ultimately the ordained one became wise he may disagree with him and rule halachah lema'aseh before him; for it is a simple matter that Resh Lakish was a complete student of R' Yochanan and R' Yochanan was his rav muvhak and all his wisdom was from him and he ordained him, yet he disagreed with him; end of his words.
