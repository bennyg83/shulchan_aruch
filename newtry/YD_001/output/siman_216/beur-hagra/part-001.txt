<b>אמר קונם כו'. </b>הוא בעיא דירושלמי הנ"ל והרא"ש פי' שהם שני בעיות ופשיט הירושלמי לשניה דבלא אמר אלא זה בלבד נפל מותר אבל בעיא הראשונה לא נפשטה ותליא בפלוגתא דס"פ השואל ופ' בית כור וקי"ל שם דספקא הוא וכאן הוא לחומרא כמש"ש נ"ג א' ספק כו' וז"ש בין כו' אבל הר"נ מפרש דהכל בעיא אחת היא דפשיטא ליה דזה מותר בנפל אלא דמיבעיא ליה בביתך זה ונ"מ לנפל ופשיט דמשום זה ופירושו צ"ע דהיאך פשיט ליה ועב"י שישבו בדוחק וזהו וי"א הב' וכתב ב"י דמדברי הרמב"ם הביאו בש"ע ס"ה שכתב ככרי זה כו' משמע דפשיט הבעיא דמשום ביתך וזהו וי"א הראשון וכגירסתו בגמ' ככרי זה א"כ בגמ' שלנו איפשיט דמשום ביתך אבל לח"מ כתב דאין ראיה מדברי הרמב"ם שי"ל דוקא במדיר אמרינן כן וכמו שחילק הר"נ מ"ב ב' <small>(ומ"ז א') </small>ד"ה בעי כו' ועס"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He said, “Konam,” etc.</b> This is the question of the Yerushalmi cited above. Rosh explains that there are two separate questions. The Yerushalmi resolves the second, that if he said only “this,” then if the house fell the prohibition ends. The first question, however, remains unresolved and depends upon the dispute at the end of the chapter HaShoel and in the chapter Beit Kor. There we rule that it is a doubt, and here the doubt is treated stringently, as stated on 53a, “a doubt,” etc. This is the meaning of “whether,” etc. Ran, however, explains that it is all one question. It was obvious to the Yerushalmi that if he said only “this,” the prohibition ends when the house falls. Its question concerned the expression “your house, this one,” and the practical difference was whether the prohibition continues after it falls. The Yerushalmi resolves that the decisive wording is “this one.” His explanation requires study, for how was this resolution established? See Beit Yosef, who resolves the difficulty only with strain. This is the second “some say.” Beit Yosef further writes that Rambam's wording, cited in Shulchan Aruch, seif 5, “this loaf of mine,” etc., implies that he resolves the question in favor of the word “your,” and this is the first “some say.” According to Rambam's version of the Gemara, which reads “this loaf of mine,” our Gemara resolves the question in favor of the word “your.” Lechem Mishneh, however, writes that there is no proof from Rambam, for perhaps this applies specifically when one person prohibits his property to another, as Ran distinguishes on 42b <small>and 47a</small>, s.v. “He asked,” etc. See seif 7.
