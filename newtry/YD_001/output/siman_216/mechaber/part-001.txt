**** ENGLISH ****
The distinction between one who says “these fruits” and one who did not say “these,” and the law of one who vows regarding cooked food (and how we follow vows according to Torah language); it contains twelve se’ifim. “Konam, these fruits are upon me” — he is forbidden in their substitutes and in their growths; needless to say regarding the liquids that come from them. And likewise he is forbidden in the growths of their growths. But substitutes of their substitutes are permitted. And if it is a matter whose seed is consumed, the growths of its growths are permitted. And the same law applies if he did not say “these,” but they were before him and he said, “Konam, they are upon me.” {Rama: And likewise if he said, “The fruits of so-and-so,” or “of such-and-such a place — they are konam upon me,” it is as if he said “these fruits” (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran).}
