<b>בחילופיהן. </b>היינו שהחליפן בדבר אחר אסור אותו הדבר אחר וכתב הרא"ש פרק הנודר מן הירק דוקא להמחליף אסורים אבל לאחר מותרים וכן אם החליפם אחר מותרים להנודר וכ"כ סמ"ג בשם ר"י והר"ן אוסר בכל ענין דמשוי ליה כהקדש (ומיהו היכא דלא אמר אלו כתב הר"ן דמותר בהחליפן אחר וע"ש) והתוס' פרק השותפין (נדרים דף מ"ז) כתבו כהרא"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
In their substitutes. — meaning he exchanged them for something else, that something else is forbidden; and Rosh wrote in chapter “the one who vows from vegetables” — specifically to the one who exchanged they are forbidden, but to another they are permitted; and likewise if another exchanged them, they are permitted to the one who vowed — so Semag in the name of R' Yosef; and Ran forbids in every case, treating them like hekdesh (but where he did not say “these,” Ran wrote the substitute is permitted — see there); and Tosafot in chapter HaShutafin (Nedarim page 47) wrote like Rosh.

**** ENGLISH ****And likewise forbidden in growths of their growths. In a matter whose seed is not consumed — and Bach wrote that Rambam, Rosh, and Tur did not forbid growths of growths except when he says konam what I eat or taste from them, as below se'if 2 — and not here. And such we have established — end quote. And nothing is implied from their words; for if because they wrote thus in the latter part — behold as Ran explained the Mishnah, the latter part also refers to the first part — so their words may be explained. And so it appears explicitly in Kesef Mishneh who explains thus the words of the Rambam, and so Derishah explained explicitly the words of the Tur. And so is the language of the Mishnah in Rosh: "One who says to his wife konam the work of your hands upon me, konam they are" — whether per konam or per "upon" — forbidden in their substitutes and in their growths; but in a matter whose seed is not consumed, growths of growths are forbidden. And even though in our Mishnah the language is not so — it is possible it is a scribal omission in Asheri. Nevertheless the proof is clear. And also Tosafot (page 55) wrote: therefore growths of growths are forbidden in a matter whose seed is not consumed; but in a matter whose seed is consumed, the growths are forbidden and growths of growths are permitted — end quote. And therefore when he says konam these fruits, it deals with this, since they wrote in the latter part "the growths are forbidden" — therefore one should not be lenient against the view of the Ran and the author, since there is no dispute in the matter. And also in Tosafot it appears thus:
