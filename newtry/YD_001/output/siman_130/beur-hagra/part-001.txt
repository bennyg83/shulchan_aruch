**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Likut) How, etc.</b> Rashi explains the phrase 'a cover over the mouth of the barrel, plastered and sealed' to mean that the cover is plastered and attached to the barrel; 'plastered and sealed' is a single concept. Similarly, 'the sealing of its mouth below,' etc., refers to a knot, and 'bound and sealed' is one concept and one seal. The barrel's own stopper, attached to the barrel with clay, is the second seal. Likewise, a basket secured tightly is one seal and the stopper is the second. Tightening is needed there because it is not attached to the barrel with clay, but the barrel is inserted within it. With a wineskin, the knot of the wineskin is one seal and the knot of the sack is the second. Likewise, when its mouth is bent over, two knots are required. Rambam, however, explains that the cover over the mouth of the barrel is itself the stopper. 'Plastered and sealed' is, as Rashi explains, one matter, attached with clay to the barrel like ordinary stoppers, and this is one seal. Since a cover is not normally used as a stopper, that deviation counts as two seals. A basket is likewise used as the stopper and is plastered and sealed, but since a basket is commonly used as a stopper it must also be tightened; the unusual tightening provides the second seal. With a wineskin placed in a sack, the sack is not tied, only the wineskin, but the unusual placement in a sack with its mouth downward counts as a second seal. Similarly, where its mouth is bent over, etc., it is not tied below but above. Rambam lists the second case of each pair because it is the greater novelty, while the first case of each pair is included in his general statement, 'and similarly every unusual arrangement,' together with plastering or tying, etc. Tosafot 69b, s.v. 'What is the reason?' explains instead that plastering is one seal and sealing is a second seal. (End of Likut.)

Rabbi Meir and the Sages here therefore dispute the same issue as Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages there. According to Tosafot, this case concerns a key or a seal, and here too the halachah follows the first Tanna, who accords with Rabbi Eliezer. See Ran and siman 131 in the gloss. (End of Likut.)
