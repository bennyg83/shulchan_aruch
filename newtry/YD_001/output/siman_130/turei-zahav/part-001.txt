**** ENGLISH ****And we are not concerned lest he draw wine from between the boards. **** ENGLISH ****
And likewise the custom, etc. And my father-in-law z"l wrote: nevertheless nowadays the custom is not to permit at all sending from city to city without a trusted Israelite who guards day and night — for it is common that gentiles draw wine with a small drill or by a knife; however if all the barrels are covered with hoops called "riffins" and the bottoms in boards, there is room to permit — as Tosafot wrote in chapter Ein Masikin folio 31, etc. — end. And it appears to me: we need not be concerned here where everything is covered with hoops lest he draw wine from between the hoops — for the main prohibition is because at time of sealing he touches it, as I wrote nearby; and when he touches wine he draws between the hoops he is somewhat distant from the wine in the barrel and is like touching what exits; there is no prohibition on what is in the barrel except from the force of stream connection — as written in siman 124 seif 23; therefore we have permit — for regarding barrels we do not say stream connection, as written beginning of siman 126. Not so if he draws wine with a drill or knife from the barrel's own board or between the boards — for certainly he touches at sealing-time the wine in the barrel itself. Also in Maharil I saw he distinguishes between a thin hole and a somewhat wider hole — at the time the gentile seals the hole we fear his finger flesh entered inside and forbids; so it appears correct to distinguish. And the law of gentiles who drink through a hollow reed I wrote in siman 124 seif 23 — that in a case of great loss one may be lenient.

And I saw in Masat Binyamin they asked about wine conveyed without Israel's guarding and the barrel was sealed with a vine tendril and around it plastered with clay and among the tendrils a thick tree branch was inserted to strengthen the sealing as is the way of new-wine carriers — and he permitted the wine because this is one seal — as Rama wrote siman 129 that I brought above; except he distinguished there if the wine ferments as new wine does and rises upward and spoils the barrel's plastering — and his words are astonishing, for then all new wine ferments and there is no way to do thus with tendrils except when it is still fermenting; and in essence of law there is no distinction between fermenting and not fermenting, for certainly also without fermenting the wine goes upward through the wagon's motion; and therefore it appears in no case is there permit in conveying wine because of plastering alone without another sign — only when resting; then plastering helps — so it appears to me.

rather we certainly require a strange tie — as written nearby "two strange ties, etc."; therefore they said in the Gemara: if he tied and sealed — meaning he made a recognition in the matter of the tying — automatically it was one seal; and its mouth downward was seal two; or what he folded the skin's mouth inward — with this are also the Tur's words we mentioned explained: the tie of the skin was seal one because it is strange — he made it in the manner of sealing; if the skin's mouth was downward and the sack tie above — meaning an ordinary tie that is not strange — two seals; and as we mentioned, so it appears to me to explain the Tur's words also here; settled what Beit Yosef inferred on the Tur here; and according to this putting in the sack was no seal — only the tie and inverting the skin's mouth or folding its mouth inward was two seals; and it appears the reason is: whenever the skin is in a sack and its mouth upward, immediately when he unties the ordinary sack tie the skin's mouth is exposed as if without sack — therefore when the Tur wrote "but he folded the skin's mouth inward" — even though its mouth is upward in the sack it is two seals — likewise if it is without sack at all; and as Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch wrote here from Rambam's language who did not mention putting in a sack at all and did not write "even though its mouth is upward" but since at the beginning he said if it is in a sack upward it is not a seal — here he said it is permitted here even though there is no seal because of putting in the sack; and Maharach ruled because of the Tur's language that putting in a sack is required at least; and it appears to me since from Rambam it is clear we do not require a sack and Shulchan Aruch brings it — we have no reason to dispute his ruling, since in the Tur it is not proven a sack is required; and that Shulchan Aruch seif 1 wrote "put the skin in a sack, etc." — there it deals with wood as explained there, and one should concern lest he draw wine through its boards; and nevertheless Rama concludes there one is accustomed to be lenient; but if the skin is of leather and its stitches are not visible from outside — it appears clear we do not require putting in a sack, only two seals as I explained; and the tie was also a seal — namely it is somewhat strange from other ties as we proved above.
