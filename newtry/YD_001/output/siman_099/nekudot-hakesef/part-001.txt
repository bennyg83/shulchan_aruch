(Hagahah in Shach s.k. 15) And if it were so it should have been resolved that there it deals with without knowledge, etc. And it appears to me to distinguish — granted this law he wrote the reason is we consider as if the issur fell now or a piece of meat fell into milk and now there is sixty in sixty without knowledge; but in milk the taste of milk was absorbed in meat and if so the milk is as if it is a piece of meat whose taste is in milk and milk in milk is min b'mino — therefore it is not nullified — investigate. Also what he wrote "and that which we learned in chapter 5 of Terumot," etc. — also here it appears to explain that which we learned in chapter 5 of Terumot — this is what it says: if b'shogeg, meaning without knowledge of the mixture, and it mixed b'shogeg permitted; and if b'mezid, even if the mixture is not known, only that it mixed b'mezid for another reason, nevertheless forbidden, for we decree b'mezid lest he mix even when known; but b'shogeg we do not decree shogeg against known, since b'shogeg with knowledge is only a general stringency and we do not decree; and with this the Gemara's difficulty on Rama is resolved.
