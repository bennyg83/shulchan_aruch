<b>עצמות. </b> כ' הט"ז וז"ל לפי שאינם בני אכילה אינם בכלל האיסור ומ"מ בולעים הם ע"י הבישול ע"כ מצטרפים לבטל האיסור ומו"ח ז"ל הקשה על רמ"א ממ"ש בת"ח דאפי' החרס שבכלי צריך ס' נגדו אם כן גם כאן נימא דהעצמות נ"נ ואני אומר משה אמת ותורתו אמת דעצם זה דומה ממש לכלי מתכות דלא אמרינן ביה חנ"נ משום דיש לו היתר בהגעלה ה"נ בעצם דמבואר בא"ח סי' תנ"א דכלי עצם ניתרים בהגעלה ע"כ לא שייך כאן חנ"נ עכ"ל והר"ן פ' ג"ה כ' דהעצמות הרכות מצטרפים לאיסור מפני מוח שבהן ומחמת רכותן שיצא לחות אבל עצמות יבשים או קליפות של ביצים וכה"ג אף אותן של איסור מצטרפין לבטל האיסור וכן דעת האו"ה וב"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
"Bones." Taz wrote and these are his words: since they are not edible they are not included in the prohibition; nevertheless they absorb through cooking, therefore they combine to nullify the prohibition. Mochiach wrote challenging Rama from what Turei Chayyim wrote — that even the earthenware in a vessel requires sixty against it; if so, here too we might say the bones become nevelah. I say Moshe is truth and his Torah is truth — this bone is truly similar to a metal vessel, for we do not say chein nafsho on it because it has a remedy through hagalah; so too with bone, as explained in Orach Chayyim siman 451 that bone vessels are permitted through hagalah, therefore chein nafsho does not apply here, end of his words. And Ran in chapter 5 of Hullin wrote that soft bones combine with the issur because of the marrow within them and because of their softness moisture emerges; but dry bones or egg shells and the like — even those of issur combine to nullify the issur; and so is the view of Issur Veheter and Bach:

**** ENGLISH ****
"For his sake." For if you permitted one for whom it was nullified on his behalf, we are concerned lest he come to say to a non-Jew or a servant to nullify it for him — therefore we penalize him; but if it was not his and he also did not intend to nullify for himself, it is permitted for him, for his bad deed does not help when the vessel is ben yomo. And Rashal wrote: specifically when this one knew it was nullified for him it is pleasing to him even if he did not command him to nullify it; but if he did not know from him, it is permitted for him, for behold he is like a shogeg; and where it is forbidden for him it is forbidden also for the members of his household. And Taz wrote: if he erred in law and thought it permitted to nullify the issur, he is called a shogeg; and proof from Shabbat chapter Kol Gadol in the Mishnah — if he forgot there is a melachah prohibition here, even though he knows today is Shabbat, he is called a shogeg; so too here; and so it is explicit in Tosafot in Bekhorot daf 23 s.v. savar, etc.:

"Nevelah." Shach wrote: at first glance it is difficult — what relevance does this have to chein nafsho?
