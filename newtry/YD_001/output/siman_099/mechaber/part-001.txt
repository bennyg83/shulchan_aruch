**** ENGLISH ****
The law of bones — whether they combine to nullify a forbidden item, and not to nullify a forbidden item l'chatchila. In it are 7 seifim: A piece of nevelah that has meat and bones that fell into a pot of permitted food — the bones of the forbidden item combine with the permitted food to nullify the forbidden item; and needless to say that bones of permitted food combine with permitted food. But the marrow in forbidden bones combines with the forbidden item, and the body of the pot itself does not combine neither with the forbidden item nor with the permitted food: {Rama: And some are stringent not to combine forbidden bones with permitted food to nullify (a hagahah in Sh"D in the name of Or Zarua); and in a place of loss one may rely on those who are lenient and permit, for such is the essential ruling:}

**** ENGLISH ****
If a small amount of forbidden food was absorbed into a permitted vessel — if it is the way of that vessel to be used abundantly with permitted food, it is permitted to use it l'chatchila, since the forbidden amount is small and it cannot come to impart taste; therefore a slight forbidden item that was absorbed in a pot or inside jugs and the like is permitted to use l'chatchila with the same kind, and one need not kasher the vessel; and this is when it is not ben yomo (Tur). And if it is ben yomo, it is forbidden l'chatchila to cook in it even the same kind, until one kasher it (there in the name of Semak). And if one cooked in it b'dieved, it is permitted (Mordechai and Ran and Rashba and Tur and Beit Yosef). And some forbid even b'dieved (Hagahot Or Zarua and Semag); and the first view is primary.
