**** ENGLISH ****For this is the essence. Bach wrote: it is astonishing — one should rule that bones of prohibition combine with prohibition, since they adhere to meat they first received prohibition taste and became nevelah; and he holds (above siman 98 seif 5) that even in something without taste of its own we say chein nafsho. There is no astonishment here, for presumably the Rav did not write above siman 98 seif 5 to be stringent in a new earthenware vessel that became nevelah even though it has no taste of its own — only because it has no remedy through hag'alah and the prohibition cannot be separated, as written there; if so a bone vessel that has remedy through hag'alah, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 451, certainly is like a metal vessel, for the Rav holds we do not say chein nafsho unless it is old, when what is absorbed in it became nevelah.

**** ENGLISH ****But a piece that absorbed prohibition, etc., for we say chein nafsho. At first glance this is difficult — what connection does this have to chein nafsho? For even according to those who do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions, nevertheless a piece that absorbed prohibition and permitted food was added afterward — the piece itself remains in its prohibition, as Rashba and Mechaber wrote below siman 106, that the prohibition in it is not entirely emitted; rather the Rav comes to teach us that a piece that absorbed prohibition and permitted food was added afterward — even the addition is forbidden because there is not sixty against the entire piece; but if he increased b'shogeg sixty against all that became nevelah, even in basar b'chalav wet in wet all is permitted; but in a piece that absorbed prohibition, even if he increased sixty against the entire piece, the piece itself remains forbidden, as below siman 106 — see there. Nevertheless what is written here in Shulchan Aruch — Rashba's responsum siman 495 — is true, for so he wrote in dry mixture; see Rashba there that we do not say chein nafsho only in a piece absorbed from prohibition, but in wet in wet we establish that all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted; and through this Tur's words are resolved, that he wrote one need not investigate if water was added after the prohibition was put in — for in basar b'chalav chein nafsho, and one would have to explain that in basar b'chalav one must investigate; only the reason is in wet in wet we do not say chein nafsho — end of his words. But one cannot say thus, for in Bavli chullin (108b) regarding what they challenge there — why is milk permitted, it is nevelah milk, etc. — it is proven explicitly that even wet in wet chein nafsho applies in basar b'chalav; and so Rashba wrote in Taharat HaBayit 98 explicitly, and so it appears explicitly from Rosh's words end of chullin regarding hag'alah of a metal vessel and end of Avodah Zarah regarding yayin nesech that fell into a pit — we do not distinguish thus regarding chein nafsho — see there; and even though there are poskim who distinguish thus, as explained above siman 92 seif 4 — that is in other prohibitions, but in basar b'chalav one cannot distinguish thus; and furthermore Rashba and Tur presumably do not hold thus, as written; and what is written in the name of Rashba's responsum is not so, for otherwise he would contradict his own words in Taharat HaBayit and also be against Bavli; but one who examines Rashba's responsum there will see he wrote only regarding other prohibitions where we do not say chein nafsho, distinguishing between wet and piece — in wet, if afterward added up to sixty all is permitted, and in a piece it remains forbidden because the prohibition is not entirely emitted, as explained below siman 110; and this matter is not relevant here (and I settled Tur's words in my book another way — see there); if so the Rav's words require study.
