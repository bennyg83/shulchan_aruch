<b>עצמות האיסור מצטרפין. </b>לפי שאינם בני אכילה אינם בכלל האיסור ומ"מ בולעים הם על ידי הבישול ע"כ מצטרפין לבטל האיסור כי האיסור מתפשט גם לתוכן ויש בהם ג"כ לחלוחית לבלבל האיסור שבולעים מן הבשר האסור ומו"ח ז"ל הקשה על רמ"א דכתב בת"ח הבאתיו בסי' צ"ח סעיף ה' דאפילו החרס שבכלי צריך ס' נגדו א"כ גם כאן נימא דהעצמות נ"נ כיון שדבוקים הם לבשר האיסור ומאי שנא מחתיכת בשר שהלב דבוק בו ובכבד לעיל שאותה חתיכה נ"נ וכן הקשה בפרישה ואני אומר משה אמת ותורתו אמת דעצם זה דומה ממש לכלי דאמרינן ביה בסימן צ"ח סעיף ה' דלא אמרינן ביה חתיכה ענ"נ אם הוא מתכות לדברי מרדכי כיון דיש לו היתר בהגעלה הכי נמי האי עצם דאפשר להפריד הנבלע בו עכשיו על ידי בישול עם בשר שעליו בהגעלה דהא קי"ל בא"ח סימן תנ"א דכלי עצם ניתרים בהגעלה וע"כ לא שייך כאן חתיכה עצמה נ"נ והוא מצטרף עם ההיתר:
**** ENGLISH ****Forbidden bones combine. Since they are not edible they are not in the category of prohibition; nevertheless they absorb through cooking — therefore they combine to nullify the prohibition, for the prohibition spreads into them and they also have moisture to blend the prohibition absorbed from the forbidden meat. Maharach challenged Rama from what Turei Chayim wrote that I brought in siman 98 seif 5, that even pottery in a vessel needs sixty against it — if so, here too one should say the bones are nevelah, since they cling to the forbidden meat; and what differs from a piece of meat to which the heart clings and liver above, that that piece is nevelah? So too he challenged in Perishah. I say: Moses is true and his Torah is true — this bone is truly like a vessel, for we do not say chein nafsho in it if it is metal according to Mordechai, since it has a remedy through hag'alah; likewise this bone, for it is possible to separate what was absorbed in it now through cooking with meat upon it through hag'alah, for we establish in Orach Chaim siman 451 that bone vessels are permitted through hag'alah — therefore chein nafsho does not apply here, and it combines with the permitted food.

<b>וגוף הקדירה כו'. </b>פי' אותה הקדירה שמבשל בה עכשיו איסור והיתר אבל פשוט שקדירה שכבר נאסרה ואח"כ מבשל בה היתר גוף הקדירה ודאי מצטרף לאיסור וע"ז כתב הטור לעיל סי' צ"ח דלהחמיר מצטרף הקדירה והטעם שאין גוף הקדירה מצטרף כלל דבגמ' איכא תרי לישני במאי שמצטרף לבטל חד אמר בקדירה עצמה בחרסית הקדירה וחד אמר במאי דבלעה הקדרה ולא בחרסית ממילא לישנא בתרא מחמיר כן משמע מפרש"י וכ' ע"ז וכיון דאיפלגי לישני ואיסורא דאורייתא הוא אזלינן לחומרא ולא משערינן בקדירה ועיקר הדבר כמות שהוא לפנינו משערינן ליה ולא משערינן במאי דבלעה קדירה כו' עכ"ל משמע דקי"ל דלא כתרווייהו לישני אבל הרשב"א מביא מקצת מפרשים דס"ל דמ"ש בקדירה היינו חומרא כלומר דלא משערינן רק במה שבתוך הקדירה דהיינו קופה ורוטב אבל לא בבלוע בקדירה ולישנא בתרא מיקל דאפילו בבלוע משערינן וקי"ל כלישנא בתרא והיינו כדעת רמב"ם שהביא הש"ע בסעיף ד' שמשערינן באומד יפה בבלועה וזה ס"ל גם לרשב"א להכריע כן במין במינו וא"כ מ"ש הש"ע וגוף הקדירה לא מצטרף היינו החרס לחוד וכלישנא בתרא אלא דאלישנא בתרא לא סמכינן אלא במינו אבל בטור אצ"ל כך אלא אפשר דלגמרי לא מצטרף הקדירה אפי' הבלוע אלא דאח"כ מביא דהרשב"א חולק ומכריע בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****And the body of the pot, etc. Explanation: that same pot in which one is now cooking prohibition and permitted food; but plainly, a pot that was already forbidden and afterward one cooks permitted food in it — the body of the pot certainly combines with the prohibition; and on this Tur wrote above siman 98 to be stringent that the pot combines. The reason the body of the pot does not combine at all is that in the Gemara there are two versions regarding what combines to nullify: one says in the pot itself, in the pottery of the pot; and one says in what the pot absorbed and not in the pottery — automatically the latter version is stringent, as it appears from Rashi and as I wrote on this; and since they disputed the versions and it is a Torah prohibition we go to stringency and do not estimate the pot — and the essence of the matter we estimate as it comes before us and do not estimate what the pot absorbed, etc. — end of his words; it appears we establish not like both versions; but Rashba brings some commentators who hold that what he wrote "in the pot" is a stringency, meaning we estimate only what is inside the pot, i.e. the contents and gravy, but not what was absorbed in the pot; and the latter version is lenient, that even in what was absorbed we estimate; and we establish like the latter version, and that is like Rambam whom Shulchan Aruch brought in seif 4 that we estimate with a good estimate in what was absorbed; and this is also Rashba's view to decide thus in min b'mino; if so what Shulchan Aruch wrote "and the body of the pot does not combine" — that is the pottery alone and like the latter version; except that in the latter version we rely only in its kind; but in Tur one must say thus — or perhaps the pot does not combine at all, even what was absorbed; except that afterward he brings that Rashba disagrees and decides on this.

<b>אע"פ שהיה בהיתר יותר כו'. </b>הרשב"א בחידושיו והר"ן כתבו דאם ראינו האיסור בשעת נפילתו ולא נתמעט בשיעור אחד עם ההיתר משערין ההיתר שנבלע בקדרה ומצטרפין אותו למה שבתוך הקדירה ומשערינן האיסור כשעת נפילתו להיתר. וכ' רש"ל פג"ה סי' ל"ד להחמיר אפי' בראינו כי אין לעמוד על שורש הדבר ולא ידע כמה בלעה הקדירה אלא לעולם משערין כמו שבא לפנינו עכ"ל ולפ"ז אין להורות היתר במ"ש הש"ע בסעי' זה ומשערין באומד יפה כו':
**** ENGLISH ****Even though there was more permitted food, etc. Rashba in his novellae and Ran wrote that if we saw the prohibition at the time it fell and it did not diminish by one measure together with the permitted food, we estimate the permitted food that was absorbed in the pot and combine it with what is inside the pot, and we estimate the prohibition as at the time it fell against the permitted food. And Rashal Pischei HaGolah siman 34 wrote to be stringent even when we saw, for one cannot stand on the root of the matter and does not know how much the pot absorbed — rather one always estimates as it comes before us — end of his words; and accordingly one may not rule permissively in what Shulchan Aruch wrote in this seif "and we estimate with a good estimate," etc.

<b>וה"מ מין בשאינו מינו. </b>שבזה צריך ששים מן התורה כיון שנ"ט ע"כ אין לשער הבלוע וא"ל אמאי אנו מתירין ביש עכשיו ששים לפנינו שמא בעת נפילת האיסור היה האיסור גדול מאוד ונצטמק ממנו הרבה ממה שנצטמק ההיתר דאחזוקי איסורא לא מחזקינן כיון שעכשיו אין ריעותא ויש ס' ובזה מיושב מה שהאריך בדרישה להקשות במה שאנו מצרפין העצמות אל ההיתר והם אינם מתמעטים בבישול והאיסור אפשר שהיה יותר ונתמעט דמסתמא אמרינן כאשר נמצא כן היה כדי שלא לאחזוקי ריעותא לומר שהיה שינוי בזה הקדירה תחלה ממה שיש עכשיו ומ"ש הטור שגם האיסור היה יותר כו' היינו כיון שאתה רוצה להקל בקדרה זחת ולחשוב גם הבלוע כיון שידוע שהיה בתחלה יותר א"כ נימא לך שגם באיסור היה יותר בקדרה זו כיון שאינו כקדרה דעלמא ובחנם טרח הרב בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****And this is only not its kind in its kind. For in this one needs sixty from the Torah, since it gives taste — therefore one cannot estimate what was absorbed; and some say: why do we permit now when there is sixty before us? Perhaps at the time the prohibition fell the prohibition was very large and much contracted from what the permitted food contracted — we do not establish prohibition presumptively since now there is no presumption and there is sixty; and through this is resolved what he extended in Derishah to challenge why we combine the bones with the permitted food though they do not diminish in cooking, and the prohibition may have been greater and diminished — for we presume as it is found so it was, in order not to establish presumption to say there was change in this pot initially from what there is now; and what Tur wrote that the prohibition too was greater, etc. — that is, since you wish to be lenient in this pot and consider also what was absorbed, since it is known there was more initially — if so we tell you that in the prohibition too there was more in this pot, since it is not like an ordinary pot — and in vain did the Rav toil in this.

**** ENGLISH ****One does not nullify a prohibition l'chatchila, etc. In siman 84 seif 13 it is explained that if his intent is only for another matter, such as to fix honey, it is permitted; and in siman 137 seif 2 I wrote this is specifically when it cannot be done otherwise. And what he wrote here "he transgressed and it was nullified" or "he increased," etc. — it appears this is repetition, for nullification is increasing; and it appears his meaning is: he transgressed and nullified — he threw a small amount of prohibition into abundant permitted food where there is sixty against it, or it fell by itself when there was not sixty in the permitted food but he increased afterward. And what he wrote "if b'shogeg permitted" — one must say his meaning is: he transgressed b'mezid except that he thought this matter is permitted; and this supports what I wrote shortly that one who errs in law is called b'shogeg.

and therefore Semag said one must investigate afterward whether increase was made there; and on this Tur challenged: for what need to investigate? Granted if we penalized b'shogeg as b'mezid there would be reason to investigate if he says he increased b'shogeg — we tell him that even so it is forbidden, for we penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; but in truth it is not so, rather b'shogeg is permitted for the reason we mentioned above; and on this he said we establish all prohibitions, etc.; and he said afterward "lest he intended to increase b'mezid" — meaning: that which we say all prohibitions that one increased b'shogeg are permitted — that is when we know it was done b'shogeg, such as done by another, or it is clear to us he did not know of the fall of the prohibition and increased — then permitted and we do not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; but here where it is not known to us — therefore even if he says it was done b'shogeg it is forbidden, lest he intended b'mezid and lies and says he is b'shogeg; and therefore we need to investigate whether any increase was made in order to forbid even if he says he was b'shogeg — this is not so, for if so you hold him a liar at the time of investigation — what does investigation help? Even if he says it did not increase he would not be believed if we suspect he lies. Therefore there is no need for investigation at all, even in basar b'chalav and a neighbor's, in other prohibitions; except that in other prohibitions we need not say it deals with increase immediately after the fall, for according to Tur's view we do not say the piece itself becomes nevelah — if so increase b'shogeg afterward also helps to remedy the prohibition; and in this Semag requires investigation of a sage lest he made the remedy of increase afterward; and on this Tur disagrees, for b'shogeg is permitted, as I mentioned regarding basar b'chalav if he increased immediately — found resolved the great wonder: how did Tur write b'shogeg permitted — behold in basar b'chalav we say the piece itself becomes nevelah, and Tur should have said but in basar b'chalav one needs investigation of a sage; and further resolved what he challenged in what Tur wrote "and we need not be concerned lest he intended to increase b'mezid," etc. — why do I need this, since he already brought we establish for permission from the Gemara — why challenge Semag here more than the Gemara; and according to what I wrote all works with heaven's help, and it is an explanation like a bolt and flower.

**** ENGLISH ****If a small amount of prohibition was absorbed, etc. Rashba in Taharat HaBayit daf 126 learned this law — and these are his words: from that which is stated in chapter Ein Ma'amidin: gentile vessels — gentile puts wine in them, Israel puts water in them, meaning and drinks them; and they asked there: what about putting beer in it? And we conclude it is permitted, for the text there is: R' Yitzchak son of Rav Yehuda permitted Rav Chiya to put beer in it and he went and put wine in it, and nevertheless he was not concerned to forbid beer because of wine — he said it is merely incidental; and the reason for this matter is that wine absorbed in a vessel is a small matter, for whatever is used in cold, its absorption is only small; and since so, and this vessel is used with permitted food abundantly, it is impossible to come to noten taam — therefore even l'chatchila permitted; and therefore a slight prohibition absorbed in the pot or in jugs and the like is permitted to use them l'chatchila even b'ben yomo, since it is impossible ever to come to noten taam; but if absorbed in a vessel whose way is to use it sometimes in a small amount, such as a bowl and the like — forbidden to use it even abundantly, a decree lest one use it in a small amount and come to noten taam — end of his words. One may challenge this: whence does he learn thus from this? Perhaps it is different here, for permitted to put other liquids there because they spoil wine, as Rashi explained in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (daf 33) that beer nullifies the taste of wine; and Tosafot wrote there in the name of R' Yitzchak that blood and all liquids are permitted like beer; and it appears one may say Rashba holds liquids do not spoil wine and disagrees with Rashi and Tosafot; and so wrote in Sefer HaPerishah — and this is not correct, for Rashba himself in Taharat HaBayit daf 155 wrote — and these are his words: all vessels, if one comes to use them with other liquids before they were kashered — permitted, provided he rinses first the wine residue on the mouth of the vessels, as taught: jugs, etc. — end of his words; and here the permission is not because wine is a small matter and the vessel's use is abundantly specifically, for all vessels equal to permitted implies even small ones; rather certainly because they spoil wine was the permission — if so the challenge returns to its place: whence does he say the reason is because what was absorbed is small? And Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit challenged Rashba on this: if so, wine too let Israel use it; and some say: wine in wine forbids in whatever amount, for we say there in the Gemara that jugs are not forbidden in benefit, and whatever is not forbidden in benefit does not forbid in a minute amount. And this is not a challenge on Rashba, for already proven from his words in Taharat HaBayit daf 149 that even where not forbidden in benefit it forbids in a minute amount. And Ran in chapter Ein Ma'amidin brought these words of Rashba, for so wrote in the name of R' Yonah and Ra'avad — and these are his words: however we hear that water of filling and pouring are permitted for drinking; and Rashba challenged: if so we find nullifying prohibition l'chatchila, for they put water or beer in this vessel in order to nullify the taste of wine — and the water and vessels are permitted. And R' Yitzchak resolved for him that absorption of wine is only slight, and since impossible to come to noten taam they nullify it l'chatchila; and from here proof for what some rabbis of France permitted a wooden vessel used with yayin nesech that needs pouring of mere shell, like any slight prohibition that suffices with shell; and so Ra'avad wrote that from this we hear nullifying slight prohibition l'chatchila, even in other prohibitions such as milk and blood that fell into a pot — and specifically when the prohibition that fell there is slight that impossible to come to noten taam, or fell in a vessel in which one does not cook except extensive cooking, etc. And I do not see their law has proof from here, for kashering of filling and pouring is not because they emit the vessel from wine, for we do not find anywhere that cold emits — rather certainly water does not kasher except because water spoils wine absorbed in the vessel and nullifies taste and does not mix it with permitted food, but it is as if burning it in its place; and further, even if you say they extract it and mix it with permitted food — nullifying prohibition l'chatchila is not forbidden except for one who intended to nullify it in order to benefit from it, etc.; but intending to kasher the vessel and not benefiting from the prohibition — permitted; and therefore no proof from here that absorption of wine should be only slight that shell suffices — end of his words. And behold from this a challenge arose for me on Tur who wrote in siman 122 on Rashba's words, and it does not appear to permit by kal v'chomer, for even a pot that is not ben yomo which is permitted is forbidden because of a decree lest ben yomo — all the more so in that vessel itself, even though its way is not to use it in a small amount, we should decree lest one use it in a small amount — end of his words; since he disagrees with Rashba, automatically it appears to him like Ran that there is no slight prohibition alone — automatically shell does not suffice for gentile vessel; and why did he bring in siman 135 Rashba's words that shell suffices — behold these matters contradict each other: if shell suffices stands, automatically it is only slight prohibition; if stands that he disagrees on what he permitted slight prohibition in a vessel used abundantly. And it appears for practical halachah to resolve everything correctly, also what Beit Yosef challenged on Tur in siman 122 — his challenge is no challenge at all on Rashba, for perforce permitted only because not common in a small amount — and what relevance has this to ben yomo pot, etc., common to cook in this and that — end of his words; and certainly proper to resolve Tur's words from Beit Yosef's challenge: how did our master Tur compare them to each other, and the distinction is before us at the beginning of investigation. Therefore it appears to me Rashba's method that we have two challenges: one, why did they permit putting other liquids in gentile jugs — in this one may resolve because they are noten taam for spoilage; except there is another challenge: after all one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila, and in that you mix prohibition in permitted food and spoil it you nullify it to prohibition and this matter is forbidden; and on this he resolved that here there was only slight prohibition and impossible to come to noten taam — therefore they nullify it; and since we came to this resolution it appears to Rashba and his teachers that we need not the resolution we mentioned in what spoils, but even what does not spoil, such as milk and blood, permitted in this since impossible to come to noten taam since it is a large vessel; and we do not need resolution of spoilage except in a small vessel where we decree lest one use permitted food in small amount — in this the spoilage resolution helps; and therefore Rashba wrote all vessels that gentile put wine in are permitted with other liquids, meaning even small ones, because they spoil; and there it is not relevant to decree lest one use in small amount and nullify prohibition l'chatchila, for there is no decree from this prohibition that one does not nullify — rather the decree is lest one come to prohibition of noten taam; and in this the spoilage resolution helps; and therefore Rashba wrote here "and the reason for this matter is that wine absorbed in a vessel is a small matter" — and concluded with this; and therefore permitted even l'chatchila — his intent here: regarding taste of permitted food we could have said because they spoil, except that if so it would in any case be forbidden l'chatchila since one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila; therefore I explain the reason is only slight prohibition — therefore it helps even l'chatchila in this since it is a large vessel; and on this Tur challenged in this way: certainly a challenge that one does not nullify — you spoke well, since the vessel is large in this no decree applies, for in any case it spoils; except you come to resolve also the first challenge regarding permission, and practical difference for other prohibitions such as milk and blood — this reasoning helps; and this is not so, for since you have no proof from the Gemara on this — the Gemara deals with something that spoils — whence do you say in what does not spoil that large vessel helps? Perhaps there too we decree because of small permitted food, as we decree in a pot that is not ben yomo; and even though one may distinguish between these decrees regarding common use as Beit Yosef wrote — nevertheless difficult whence do we distinguish; granted if proven from the Gemara I would be forced — what is not so now, for certainly we say as we decree in not ben yomo a distant decree, kal v'chomer for such a distant decree — and whence do we distinguish between them; found according to this Tur also holds Rashba's reasoning that large vessel helps regarding that one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila since it is only slight — and this is in something that spoils; but in something that does not spoil it does not help, since in any case we must decree; therefore well wrote in siman 135 that shell helps since it is only slight and shell in any case we require lest one use in small amount, for there it deals with wine in wine that does not spoil; and since Tur and Ran disagree with Rashba and they are later, proper to be stringent like them that large vessel does not help except where it spoils — in this Tur and Ran agree; also Ramban holds like Ran as I bring in Taharat HaBayit daf 145 that shell does not permit except that Taz wrote on him "and in their words we listen to be lenient" — and this does not apply here regarding Torah prohibition such as milk and blood where there is no spoilage — for in this Rashba and his teachers permitted, and for Ramban and Ran and Tur forbidden, and we listen to them since it is of Torah; and in truth Tur's reasoning we mentioned is very correct, for from the Gemara there is no proof to permit even in large vessel in prohibition that does not spoil — for in the Gemara large vessel helps only regarding spoilage, and lest the challenge "behold one does not nullify l'chatchila." And so we establish stringently that large vessel does not help where there is no spoilage, like Ramban and Ran and Tur and Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit, and so Maharach and Levush.
