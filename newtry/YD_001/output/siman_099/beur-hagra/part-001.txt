**** ENGLISH ****
"A piece, etc." Yerushalmi chapter 5 of Terumot — terumah waving does not combine with terumah to forbid on non-sacred produce, but non-sacred waving combines with non-sacred produce to raise the terumah; and in chapter 1 of Orlah and chapter 6 of Nazir, R' Avahu in the name of R' Yochanan — all issurim are estimated as if they were in a shadow and absorbed: what is the case? Here one says noten ta'am one in a hundred, and here one says noten ta'am one in sixty. According to the one who says in sixty, you do not make the foreleg one part in a hundred for the ram; according to the one who says in a hundred, you make the foreleg one part in a hundred for the ram; according to R' Yehudah in a hundred, you remove the bones from the foreleg; and according to the one who says in sixty, you do not remove the bones from the foreleg. And just as you remove the bones from the foreleg, so you remove them from the ram — you cannot, for it teaches: terumah waving does not combine with terumah to forbid on non-sacred produce, but non-sacred waving combines with non-sacred produce to raise the terumah. Rav Beivai asked: does terumah waving combine with non-sacred produce to raise the terumah? From what R' Huna said — issur shells combine with heter — this says terumah waving combines with non-sacred produce to raise the terumah. And in chapter 8 of Terumot, egg in sixty — R' Chanina: the vegetable and the shell combine; R' Zeira: and its issur from within it — meaning we estimate only the food in the egg. R' Huna said issur shells raise the heter; and the Yerushalmi above disagrees with that which there says according to the one who says in sixty, foreleg bones combine with issur, and all the more so they do not combine with heter; and the sugya in chapter 5 of Hullin 98b disagrees further — according to the one who says in a hundred, even heter shells do not combine with heter, as it says meat with meat, etc. And it appears the Yerushalmi "it appears to me" and "just as you find," etc. — rather one may say it is not specifically so, and since it mentioned meat in the foreleg it also mentioned meat in the ram; in any event we learn from all the sugyot that heter bones combine with heter, and all the more so according to the one who says in sixty, as written in the Gemara meat and bones with meat, etc.; but according to the one who says in sixty, whether in the Gemara sugya or the Yerushalmi sugya, it appears issur bones combine with issur and all the more so they do not combine with heter; and it appears it disagrees with that of chapter 8 of Terumot, as appears to me. And Ran wrote this case is different — bones in the foreleg combine with issur because of the marrow within them and because of their softness moisture emerges; but dry bones are like shells in chapter 8 of Terumot, which are no better than gid — according to the one who says there is not noten ta'am in gid, it does not forbid; and as written in chapter 11 of Terumot, consecrated bones at the time, etc. R' Avahu in the name of R' Yochanan at the heads of the wings and the cartilage — this is the Mishnah; and so in the Gemara of Pesachim chapter 3 — if we say there is no marrow in them, etc.; and in this they disagree — according to the one who says in a hundred, even those that have marrow do not combine with issur, and we hold like the one who says in sixty; and this is what the Yerushalmi "it appears to me" and "just as you," etc. — you cannot, for it teaches "there is not," and it appears that baraita refutes according to the one who says in sixty — but it is not so, for even according to the one who says in sixty he agrees regarding waving and shells; rather according to the one who says in a hundred, even those that have marrow are like waving; and this is what it says "there is not," etc.; and this is what Shulchan Aruch wrote "but the marrow," etc.; and Shach too regarding thin bones, as appears to me from the Yerushalmi "it appears to me" at the heads of the wings, etc.:
