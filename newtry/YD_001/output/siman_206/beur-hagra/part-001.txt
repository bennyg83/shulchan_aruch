<b>וי"א כו'. </b>כ"ה גירסת הרא"ש וע"ש ד' ב' ד"ה שאני אוכל כו'. והר"נ כתב במתני' ד"ה שאני שתי הגירסות וכתב בגמ' ד' ב' ד"ה א"ה דלס"ד דשאני אוכל לבד הוי יד לכאורה גירסת הרמב"ם ניחא דלגירסא שאני אוכל דאכילנא קאמר אבל תירץ דא"כ מאי שאני הל"ל אוכל לך ויותר ניחא גירסא זו דלגירסא שאיני מנ"ל דיד לנדר דלמא לשבועה דא"א לומר דאמר קונם דא"כ נדר גמור הוא ודחק עצמו כגון דאמר ומ"מ הואיל ל"ק שאני לא הוי נדר גמור דלשון שבועה הוא. ומ"מ ודאי שאני יותר ניחא ולשיטת הרא"ש דוקא דאמר שאני ולשון הרב תמוה במ"ש דאפילו ואף לשיטת הר"נ הל"ל דכ"ש כו'. ועמ"ש בס"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> This is the Rosh's version; see there, 4b, s.v. 'that I eat,' etc. The Ran records both versions in his commentary to the Mishnah, s.v. 'that I drink,' and writes in the Gemara, 4b, s.v. 'if so,' that according to the initial assumption that the words 'that I eat' alone constitute an incomplete expression of a vow, the Rambam's version is apparently preferable, because according to the reading 'that I eat,' he is saying, 'that which I eat.' He answers, however, that if so, why did he say 'that I'? He should have said, 'I will eat from you.' This version is nevertheless more satisfactory, because according to the reading 'that I do not,' how do we know that it is an incomplete expression of a vow? Perhaps it is an incomplete expression of an oath. One cannot say that he said 'konam,' for then it would be a complete vow. The Ran therefore presses the interpretation that, for example, he said another expression as well, but since he did not say 'that I,' it is not a complete vow because it is phrased in the language of an oath. Nevertheless, the reading 'that I' is certainly more satisfactory. According to the Rosh's approach, this applies specifically when he said 'that I.' The wording of the Rama is puzzling where he writes 'even'; and even according to the Ran's approach, he should have written 'all the more so,' etc. See what I wrote in seif 5.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he said, etc.</b> This is the view of the Rambam and the Ran in the name of the second version, following the plain meaning of the Gemara: in any event, the expression 'I am vowed from you,' etc., implies, etc. This also resolves the baraita cited above, for one may say that according to the first tanna, 'that I eat' together with 'I am vowed from you' is effective, while 'I am vowed from you' alone also prohibits speaking with him, etc. In any event, the matter is resolved according to the Ran's explanation of that view, namely, that he said 'konam,' as above. Others say, see Taz, seif katan 1, and Shach, seif katan 3, that unless he concluded, 'that I eat,' or, 'that I do not eat,' his words have no effect. This is the Rosh's view in both his commentary and his halachic work. He derives this from the Gemara's wording, 'Shall we say that Shmuel holds,' which implies that according to the opinion that such expressions are effective, he is prohibited from eating with the other person but permitted to speak with him. This shows that the wording indicates eating more strongly than speaking.

<b>אמר מנודה כו'. </b>ר"ל כת"ק וכמ"ש הרא"ש והר"נ דאין הלכה כר"ע הדא דאין הלכה כר"ע מחביריו ועוד דאמר שם לית דחש כו' וכ"ז בשסיים שאיני אוכל דקאי ארישא כמ"ש הרא"ש והר"נ אבל בלא סיים ס"ל דלעולם אסור דהטעם דמותר במנודה ומשמיתנא משום דלשון שמתא הוא כמש"ש ורבנן סברי כו' והוא אמר שאני אוכל משא"כ בל"ק שאני אוכל דהוי שמתא ונדינא לשון הנאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he said, 'I am excommunicated,' etc.</b> Meaning, in accordance with the first tanna, as the Rosh and the Ran write, that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Akiva, both because the halachah does not follow Rabbi Akiva against his colleagues and because the Gemara says there, 'There is no one who is concerned,' etc. All this applies when he concluded, 'that I do not eat,' which refers back to the opening clause, as the Rosh and the Ran explain. But if he did not so conclude, this opinion holds that he is always prohibited. The reason 'I am excommunicated' and 'I place myself under a ban' are permitted is that they are expressions of excommunication, as the Gemara explains, 'and the Rabbis hold,' etc., and he added, 'that I eat.' By contrast, if he did not say, 'that I eat,' the expression denotes excommunication, while 'I separate myself' denotes abstention from benefit.

<b>ויש מי כו'. </b>הר"נ בשם רמב"ן ב' ב' ממ"ש בפ"ב ובפ"ג דשבועות איזהו איסר כו' הרי עלי שלא אוכל כו' ובשבועות כ"ב גירסתם באומר אכילה משתיהן עלי שבועה וגירסת ספרים שלנו אינו כן אלא בנדרים כ"ד ב' <small>(ובשבועות כ"ט א') </small>אמרינן באומר יאסרו כו' וסברא הראשונה ס"ל דל"ד נקט ובירושלמי פליגי בהו דר' יודן ור' מונא סברי אין נדר בלשון שבועה ואין שבועה בלשון נדר ור' יוסי פליג עלייהו וקי"ל כרבים. שם. וז"ש הרא"ש דשאיני אוכל לך לאו כלום הוא והרמב"ם ס"ל דנדר הוא ואזלי לשיטתייהו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is an authority who, etc.</b> The Ran, citing the Ramban on 2b, derives this from what is stated in chapters 2 and 3 of Shevuot: 'What is an issar,' etc., and, 'It is upon me that I will not eat,' etc. In Shevuot 22, their version reads, 'One who says, eating from both of them is upon me as an oath.' The version in our texts is not so. Rather, in Nedarim 24b, and in Shevuot 29a, the Gemara says that he stated, 'They shall be forbidden,' etc. The first opinion maintains that the Gemara's use of vow terminology is not precise. The Yerushalmi records a dispute: Rabbi Yudan and Rabbi Mona maintain that a vow cannot be expressed in the language of an oath, and an oath cannot be expressed in the language of a vow, while Rabbi Yosi disagrees with them. We rule in accordance with the majority. This is why the Rosh writes that the expression, 'that I do not eat from you,' has no effect, while the Rambam holds that it is a vow, each following his own approach.
