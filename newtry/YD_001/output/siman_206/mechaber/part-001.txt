**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of yados of vows; it contains 5 se’ifim. Yados (meaning: extensions — when one began to vow and did not complete the statement, and nevertheless he is forbidden as if he vowed the entire statement, like a person who holds a vessel by its handle and through that uses the vessel as if he held it itself) of vows are like vows — provided that they indicate somewhat regarding the vow. How so? One who says to his fellow, “I am forbidden to you that I will not eat with you,” or “I am separated from you that I will not eat with you,” or “I am distant from you that I will not eat with you” — he is forbidden to eat with him. (And some say that even if he said, “I will eat with you,” in these formulations, he is forbidden to eat with him) (Ran in the name of “there are those who read,” and see se’if katan 2). But if he says, “I will not eat with you,” alone, without “I am forbidden to you,” it is not a yad. And if he said, “I am forbidden to you,” or “I am separated from you,” or “I am distant from you,” and did not complete his words, “that I will not eat with you”: with “I am forbidden to you,” he is forbidden to speak with him; with “I am separated from you,” he is forbidden to do business with him; with “I am distant from you,” he is forbidden to stand within 4 amos of him.

**** ENGLISH ****
If he said to his fellow, “I am forbidden to you from food or benefit,” both are forbidden to eat or benefit one from the other. But if he said, “I am forbidden from you from food or benefit,” he is forbidden regarding his fellow, and his fellow is permitted regarding him.

**** ENGLISH ****
If he said, “I am excommunicated to you,” or “I am under a ban of excommunication from you,” and he completed his words, “that I will eat with you” — it is not a vow, and he is permitted in everything. And if he did not complete his words, he is forbidden to stand within 4 amos of him. But if he said, “I am under judgment from you,” and completed his words, “that I will not eat with you,” he is forbidden to eat with him. If he did not complete his words, “that I will not eat with you,” he is forbidden to benefit from him. {Rama: And it was already explained that there are those who disagree, and one holds that anything in which he did not say, “that I will not eat with you,” or “that I will eat with you,” is nothing.}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who vows, “That I will not eat,” or “That I will eat with you” — it is not even a yad, for this formulation is the language of an oath and not the language of a vow — unless he vowed to perform a mitzvah. Nevertheless, since nowadays it is common in people’s mouths to vow with this language, one should not be lenient, and release is required so that they not act lightly regarding vows. And there is one who says that a vow that one expressed in the language of an oath, and an oath in the language of a vow, is effective by the law of yados. Nevertheless, regarding nezirut that one expressed in the language of an oath — according to all, it is forbidden.
