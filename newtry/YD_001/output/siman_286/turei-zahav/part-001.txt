Cattle stable. <b>טוב להיות נראית. </b>נראה לי דאין לעשות שתהיה נראה בלי כיסוי כלל דנמשך מזה עבירה דבכל פעם שממשמשין בה בכניסה ויציאה מוחק מעט משם שדי עד שנמחק לגמרי אחר זמן רב כמו שאנו רואין ברוב בתים אלא יכניס שם חתיכת זכוכית ממילא יהי' השם נראה ובזה בטלה ג"כ טענת המקילים בעשיית מזוזה מחמת שמצוי שם טינופת סביבות המקום ההוא לפעמים או שנשים רוחצות שם דרך עראי דבזה הוה מכוסה אע"ג שנראה וראיה מדאיתא בא"ח סי' ע"ו צואה בעששית מותר לקרות ק"ש כנגדה אע"פ שרואה אותה דרך דופנותיה משום דבכיסוי תליא רחמנא והא מתכסיא וה"ה נמי כאן שהוא להיפך כיון דמתכסיא השם אין כאן איסור דלאו בראיה תליא מילתא וק"ו במה שהיא למעלה מי' טפחים דהוה כרשות אחרת כמו שכ' הסמ"ג הביאו ב"י ולא היה צריך אפי' לכיסוי זכוכית אלא דזהו צד היותר טוב ובחדר שאיש ואשתו שם יכסה המזוזה בשעוה כמ"ש ב"י בשם סמ"ג בשם ריב"א לענין אם טינופת מצוי שם וכן יש לעשות בחדר הנ"ל כי בזה לא די כיסוי זכוכית כיון דיש שם תשמיש וכתיב לא יראה בך ערות דבר כמו דאמרינן ערוה בעששית אסור לקרות ק"ש כנגדה כן נ"ל ועיין מ"ש בסימן רפ"ז סעיף ב':
**** ENGLISH ****Good that it be visible. It seems to me one should not make it visible without any cover at all, for from this follows transgression — each time they handle it on entry and exit a little of the name Sh-dai is erased until completely erased after much time, as we see in most houses; rather insert there a piece of glass, and thereby the name will be visible; and thereby is voided also the claim of those lenient in making a mezuzah because filth is found around that place sometimes or women bathe there casually — for with this it is covered even though visible; and proof from that which is in Orach Chayim siman 76: excrement in a lantern — permitted to read Shema facing it even though he sees it through its sides, because the Merciful One depends on cover and behold it is covered; and the same here conversely — since the name is covered there is no prohibition, for the matter does not depend on seeing; and a fortiori in what is above ten handbreadths, which is like another domain, as Semag wrote that Beit Yosef brought; and one would not need even glass cover — but this is the better side; and in a room where husband and wife have relations — cover the mezuzah with wax as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag in the name of Riv'a regarding if filth is found there; and so one should do in the aforementioned room, for here glass cover is not sufficient since there is marital relations there; and it is written "no nakedness of anything shall be seen in you" — as we say nakedness in a lantern forbidden to read Shema facing it — so it seems to me; and see what is written in siman 287 seif 2.

**** ENGLISH ****
Exempt. “Your house” — just as a house is designated for dwelling, so too whatever is designated for dwelling — to exclude these that are not for dwelling. And as for what is written “gatehouse” — it is a small house at the courtyard gate where the watchman sits. And as for “some say a gatehouse, etc.” — it is the view of Rabbi Yitzchak in the Tur, who found two baraitot contradicting each other: in Yoma we learned — in those: exedra, gatehouse, and porch — they are exempt; and in Hakomeitz it is taught that they are obligated. Rabbi Yitzchak resolved this: Hakomeitz speaks of rabbinically. If so, those “some say” also apply to porch and exedra; and Tur and Shulchan Aruch mentioned only one of them. But according to this, also in a garden there would be rabbinic obligation, and even though a garden is not mentioned in the Talmud alongside these — nevertheless Tur holds they are as one since they have no dwelling and are not comparable to a house, yet the rabbis obligated them.

הפתחים של בית שמשייר בבנין הכותל מקום הפתח ומגיע עד הפצים והיאך הבינו ברש"י מה שכתב מחותכין בו היאך תתפרש לשון חיתוך בזה והאמת הברור לפע"ד דשני הפתחים הם בפצים עצמו מחותכים בו דהיינו שבמקצוע מערבית צפונית עומד פץ אחד עב ורחב ונחתך בו חתך גדול כדי שיעור פתח מצד צפון לתוך חלל הבית ושם נכנסין ויוצאין מצפון לבית ומבית לצפון ועוד נחתך שם שיעור פתח מצד מערב לחלל הבית ושם הוי כניסה ויציאה מבית למערב וממערב לבית ועשה המזוזה בחלל הפתח המערבי דהיינו באותו צד שהוא ימין הנכנס ממערב לבית שהרי באותו פצים עצמו יש חלק ימין ושמאל בפתח העשוי בו נמצא הכל ניחא דהכי קאמר שהמזוזה של פתח המערבי פשיטא שהיא כדין חיוב אלא פי' למה נקרא חד פתח אחורי הדלת בגמרא ועיקר הכוונה של בעל התלמוד להורות לנו סמיכות הפתחים באיזה דרך שנוכל להבינו שב' המקומות הם מקום אחד והיינו על דרך שכתבתי ובזה ניחא הכל שהמזוזה היה ממש בימין לשניהם ובמקומה הראוי מה שא"כ למה שהבינו החכמים הנ"ל דהמזוזה בפצים והפתחים הם בלאו הפצימים היאך תצרף המזוזה באין עובי טפח הא אין המזוזה לצד חלל הפתח של אחוריה ובמה שכתבתי הכל ניחא בסייעתא דשמיא ואולי מן השמים מקום הניחו לי בזה לפרש כן:
**** ENGLISH ****Doorway behind the door. In Tur Rashi is explained thus: such as in the corner of the house there are fixed pegs and two (so in Rashi in the Gemara, preferable to Tur's version that reads "to two") doorways cut in it — one to the north and one to the west; and he fixed the mezuzah inside in the hollow of the northern doorway; and when the door is open it is behind the door — if in the thickness of the pegs there is a handbreadth thickness of separation between doorway and doorway, another mezuzah is needed for the western doorway, since the mezuzah is not in its hollow; and if not — no other mezuzah is needed, for behold it is as placed in its own hollow — end quote. And Mordechai challenged Rashi: from where does he have this? And Ran explained: such as at a doorway of a room that needs a mezuzah, and there are pegs there that are right for this and that; and since there is not a handbreadth between them, one mezuzah suffices — end quote; meaning Mordechai challenged Rashi's explanation: when one enters the western doorway the mezuzah would be to his left, for the pegs would be to the left of one entering there — how does that mezuzah suffice? And therefore one must say that since he fixed it in the northern doorway and there at that doorway the mezuzah is to the right of one entering, it helps also for the western doorway. On this Mordechai challenged "from where does he have this?" — therefore Ran explained there is a room where they enter that room via the western doorway, and for that purpose that doorway was made; and it turns out the mezuzah is to the right of one entering from his house to that room — only via the western doorway; and in this way Maharach Or Zaru'a was strong and extended in his explanation; and in Derishah and Perishah he also extended in explaining this matter of Rashi; and the permission is because since at the essence of its fixing it was to the right, it suffices also for the second doorway that is to the left. And I am astonished at the early and late authorities for an hour: first, why did they not explain Rashi's words literally and all is well — those sages understood there are doorways in west and north as other doorways of a house that leaves in building the wall space of the doorway and reaches to the pegs — how did they understand in Rashi what he wrote "cut in it" — how can the language of cutting be explained here? The clear truth according to my humble opinion: the two doorways are in the pegs themselves cut in it — meaning in the northwest corner stands one thick wide peg, and a large cut is made in it for doorway measure from the north side into the house hollow, and there they enter and exit from north to house and house to north; and another cut for doorway measure from the west side to the house hollow, and there is entry and exit from house to west and west to house; and he made the mezuzah in the hollow of the western doorway — meaning on that side that is right for one entering from west to house, for in that peg itself there is right and left part in the doorway made in it — all is well that he says the mezuzah of the western doorway is plainly as required; only in the northern doorway there is a distinction: if in that peg part separating west from north there is a handbreadth measure, another mezuzah is needed for the northern; but if there is no handbreadth separation, we consider that separation as if it does not exist and the northern too is fit as if also in the western hollow, and one right side for both suffices; and even though for the northern the mezuzah would be far from a handbreadth near the outside — nevertheless it is valid as written siman 289, and all the more here that at least toward the north it was as fitting. And this is also clear in Rashi's language in the Gemara that Beit Yosef brings — only there he mentions southern and western doorway, and it is also in this way, except here the mezuzah must be in the southern doorway in the right part of one entering, and the western doorway is fit with that mezuzah too if there is no handbreadth in the separation, for behold it is right for both. And what Rashi wrote "and when the door is open it is behind the door" — this is not relevant to any reason of exemption or obligation; rather he explains why one doorway is called behind the door in the Gemara; and the Talmud's main intent is to teach us the adjacency of doorways in what way we can understand that the two places are one place — and this is as I wrote; and thereby all is well that the mezuzah was literally to the right of both and in its proper place; unlike what those sages understood that the mezuzah is in the pegs and the doorways are without the pegs — how would the mezuzah join without handbreadth thickness, behold the mezuzah is not at the side of the hollow of the doorway behind it? In what I wrote all is well with Heaven's help; and perhaps from Heaven a place was left for me to explain thus.

The pillar does not divide it, etc. It is explained in the Tur: that is, if they are connected in such a way that they are not separated from each other — if this opens, the other also opens; but where there is one large gate and next to it a small gate in the manner of the wealthy who do not want the large gate to be open, but always use the small one and only occasionally the large one — in that case two mezuzot are required.
