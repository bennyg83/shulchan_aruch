(סעיף י"ג) <b>ד"א</b> על ד"א פטור. דהיינו דמצד עצמותו פטור ממזוזה אבל בחדר שלפנים מחדר (שקורין קעמערכי) שיוצאים מחדר הגדול לחדר הקעמערכי הפתח ההוא חייב במזוזה מצד הגדול שיוצאים ממנו דרך פתח זו לחדר קטן ויהא נידון חדר קטן כאויר דמ"מ חייב כאלו יוצא מפתח זו לשוק כנלע"ד בעזה"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 13) <b>Four cubits by four cubits is exempt.</b> Meaning that in itself such a small room is exempt from mezuzah. But if there is a small inner room opening from a larger room, the doorway leading from the large room into the small one is obligated by virtue of the large room, since one exits the large room through that doorway into the small room. The small room is treated like open air, just as an exit from a house into the street still requires a mezuzah. This appears correct to me, with Hashem's help.
**** END BLOCK ****

**** ENGLISH ****
(Shach seif katan 25) And he should go back and affix it without a berachah. One must study why he should affix it without a berachah, for even according to that view that from the beginning it was also obligated in mezuzah, nevertheless it appears that since he removed it, if he goes back and affixes it he needs to bless — unless we say that the Shach's intent is according to the view of the poskim in Orach Chaim (siman 5), that one who removed a tallit with the intent to go back and put it on immediately, whether he needs to bless or not — see there. And that is necessarily because since his intent is to go back and put it on, it is as if he did not remove it at all. If so, according to that view it is possible that the same law applies with a mezuzah: if he removed it with the intent to go back and affix it immediately, it is as if he did not remove it, and he does not need to bless.
