<b>A cattle stable.</b> Although it is a place of filth, its foulness is nevertheless not excessive. The Derishah writes that the general rule is as follows: where there is a concern of sanctity, such as a synagogue, or where there is a place of filth, an actual dwelling is required, one in which people live constantly. Where neither concern applies, however, such as a hayloft and the like, each such structure is considered a dwelling even if people do not actually live there, since they regularly enter and leave it.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Likewise, courtyards or towns, etc.</b> Where some non-Jews reside, even if most residents are Jewish, the gates are exempt because of danger. For this reason mezuzot are not affixed to street gates, such as in the holy communities of Krakow and Prague, where gates were erected in the Jewish streets and closed at night, yet mezuzot were never affixed to them. The reason is the danger of the ruler's accusations, which were always liable to be directed against the Jewish people. There is also another reason not to affix mezuzot there: they would certainly be removed and disgraced, thereby causing sacred writings to be treated contemptuously. From this we learn that when a Jew leases a concession from a nobleman to sell spirits, beer, or mead, and the nobleman's servants enter the premises every day and are accustomed to taking mezuzot and treating them contemptuously, mezuzot should not be affixed to those entrances unless the entrance is protected from the non-Jews so that they cannot remove it. So writes the Bach.

<b>פתח שאחורי הדלת כו'. </b>פירש רש"י כגון שיש במקצוע הבית פצים קבוע לשני פתחים מחותכי' א' לצפון ואחד למערב וקבעו המזוזה בפצים בחלל הפתח הצפוני וכשהדלת פתוח הוי אחורי הדלת אם יש בעובי הפצים עובי הבדלת טפח בין פתח לפתח צריך מזוזה אחרת לפתח מערבי הואיל ואין המזוזה בחללה ואם לאו א"צ מזוזה אחרת שהרי היא כמונחת בחלל עצמו ע"כ ועב"ח וירא' מכאן אותן פתחים שהם חלוקים ועמוד באמצע די להם במזוזה א' אפילו אם יש בעובי העמוד טפח דלא מחייב בשתים כשיש בעובי העמוד טפח אלא כשאחד בצפון וא' במערב אבל כששני הפתחים ברוח א' אפי' אם יש בעובי הפצים טפח חשוב הכל כפתח א' אפילו לפי' השני שפי' רש"י כמו שדרך לעשות פתח אחד קטן אצל שער הגדול שאין פותחין תמיד השער הגדול ופותחים הקטן ואם יש פצים רוחב טפח ביניהם צריך מזוזה אחרת אפילו לפי זה נראה שפתח החלוק אין צריך דשאני התם דפתח הקטן נבדל משער הגדול לגמרי שהרי נפתח ונסגר כל אחד בלא חבירו אבל הכא שאין נבדלין זה מזה דכשזה נפתח גם זה נפתח גם נסגרים ביחד ואין העמוד נעשה אלא לנוי ודאי כחד חשיבי עד כאן לשון טור ועיין ב"ח ודרישה מה שכתבו בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A doorway behind the door, etc.</b> Rashi explains that there may be a fixed doorpost in the corner of a house serving two openings, one facing north and one west. If the mezuzah was affixed to that post within the northern doorway, then when the door is open the mezuzah is behind the door. If the thickness of the post creates a separation of one handbreadth between the two openings, another mezuzah is required for the western opening, since the first mezuzah is not within its doorway. If not, no second mezuzah is required, because the first is considered to be within the western doorway as well. See Bach. It appears from this that where two openings are divided by a pillar in the middle, one mezuzah suffices even if the pillar is one handbreadth thick. Two mezuzot are required because of a handbreadth-thick divider only when one doorway faces north and the other west. If both openings face the same direction, they are considered one doorway even when the divider is one handbreadth thick. This is true even according to Rashi's second explanation, concerning a small doorway beside a large gate, where the large gate is not usually opened and the small doorway is used instead. There, if a divider one handbreadth wide separates them, another mezuzah is required. A divided doorway is different, however, because in Rashi's case the small doorway is entirely independent of the large gate, and each opens and closes without the other. Here the two sections are not independent; when one opens, the other opens, and they close together. The central pillar is merely decorative, so the two sections are certainly regarded as one doorway. This is the language of the Tur. See Bach and Derishah regarding their discussion of this matter.

<b>It is obligated in mezuzah.</b> In the Diaspora, after thirty days; in Eretz Yisrael, immediately. This teaches that we do not say that because the house belongs to a non-Jew it is entirely exempt from mezuzah. All this applies only where there is no danger in affixing a mezuzah in the non-Jew's house. Where danger exists, it is exempt, as explained above in seif 8.
