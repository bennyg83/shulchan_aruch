**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With sourdough.</b> Since the sourdough is not nullified, it renders the entire dough tevel. Therefore, if one separates challah from elsewhere, it is sufficient to separate according to the measure of the sourdough alone. But if he has no other flour and must separate from this dough itself, he is not separating from something independently obligated in challah, but only from dough that became obligated because it absorbed the taste of the sourdough. The entire dough is therefore treated as obligated, and he must separate according to the measure of the whole dough, following the form of the actual separation. Tur writes that if he wishes, he may bring a quarter-kav of his own flour, mix it with this dough, and separate challah from it. Maharshal writes that he need not actually mix the flour into the dough; he may place it next to the dough if he wishes. Taz. Shach writes that the same law applies if ordinary dough from which challah had not been separated became mixed into dough whose challah had already been separated. In these laws it is also sufficient for the doughs to be adjacent even if they do not touch and are not joined in one vessel, as explained below in siman 325.
