**** ENGLISH ****
The measure obligating challah, species that combine, and the law of exempt bread that became mixed with obligated. In it are 14 seifim. One is obligated in challah only from five species of grain; and one is obligated only with five quarters of a measure that holds forty-three and one-fifth eggs and a fifth of an egg; and one fills it with flour, and that flour is the measure of challah. And when one fills the measure with flour, it should be leveled and not heaped; and how they estimate to know it holds forty-three and one-fifth eggs was explained in Tur Orach Chaim (and see in Orach Chaim siman 456). And the weight of five quarters of flour is 520 Egyptian drachma of wheat flour from Egypt. {Rama: A vessel holding ten fingerbreadths by ten fingerbreadths in height of three fingerbreadths and a ninth of a finger approximately is the measure of challah; and likewise a measure that has seven fingerbreadths less two ninths of a finger [by seven less two ninths of a finger] in height seven fingerbreadths less two ninths of a finger is the omer; and all these fingerbreadths are the width of the thumb of the hand (Rambam, chapter 6 of Bikkurim).}

**** ENGLISH ****
The five species of grain combine—if the dough has only five quarters from all five of them, they combine. In what case is this said? When they were mixed as flour; but if each was kneaded alone and does not have the measure, and he attached it to another species, they do not combine except in this order: wheat combines only with spelt; spelt combines with each one; barley combines with all except wheat; oats combine with barley and spelt and not with oats and wheat; oats combine with barley and spelt and not with wheat and oats. And for the Rambam, spelt, oats, and oats combine. And if each species was kneaded alone and has the measure and he wishes to separate from this species upon that—they separate only from its own species. How so? They do not separate wheat upon any of them, nor any of them upon wheat; spelt and oats separate from one upon the other, and likewise oats and barley separate from one upon the other. {Rama: If he mixed a kav of dough with another kav of dough not of its kind and afterward divided them and added to each a quarter-kav of its kind—it is obligated in challah, since they did not combine when mixed; but if he mixed a kav of flour with a kav of flour of another kind intending to divide them [and divided them] and added to each a quarter-kav of its kind—it is exempt (Tur).}
