**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And according to Rambam.</b> Rambam holds that the first Tanna does not disagree and therefore rules like R. Yochanan ben Nuri. Kesef Mishneh explains the Yerushalmi according to him. There is strong proof for Rambam from the Bavli, which says oats and rye are barley species, implying they are one category. The Yerushalmi is difficult according to Rash and Rosh, because it says rye combines with wheat while the Mishnah says otherwise, and in any event the Bavli appears to disagree. The only practical dispute between Rambam and Rosh is whether rye and oats alone combine with one another. See Bedek HaBayit, though its discussion is difficult.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He mixed.</b> This is the Yerushalmi cited by Rash at the beginning of chapter 4. Although Rash later raises a difficulty and Raavad agrees with that concern, Rosh ultimately follows the straightforward first explanation: once there was already a moment of obligation, the later mixture does not alter the obligation. Rambam likewise holds this way. See siman 326, seif 4. Nevertheless, the halachah does not follow that particular Tanna but our Mishnah, under which species that do not combine are treated separately both for leniency and stringency. Rosh. <b>(Likkut) He mixed.</b> Rash there concludes by asking why, when one added to something already obligated, the addition should not combine to become obligated. He answers that the case must be where challah was first separated and only afterward more was added. Rosh can likewise be read this way.
