(Siman 39, seif 2 in the hagahah) And some are stringent if the lung was lost, etc. One may ask: if the enactment of the Sages was to require examination also regarding milk one already has — since if sircha is found, milk milked within three days is forbidden retroactively, as Rama wrote in siman 81 seif 2 in the hagahah — were they required to examine the lung for this? And what is the law if it was lost without examination — is the milk forbidden or not? Answer: It appears to me that this is the doubt in the Gemara, first chapter of Chullin (daf 17): limbs of neveilah that Israelites brought into the Land — what is the law? Do we say from now on it is forbidden for them to make neveilah, but what is in their hands from what was already made neveilah when they were in the desert while it was permitted — is it permitted, or not? And Rashi and the Rosh there explain that there is practical consequence in this. And it appears to me there is practical consequence regarding a rabbinic enactment such as this, that they enacted it is forbidden to eat meat without lung examination — was the enactment only on the meat itself, but if he has milk already milked while permitted he may eat it; or does the enactment apply also to what is already in his hands that was while permitted? Since this was not resolved, we hold: a safek d'oraisa lechumra and d'rabbanan lekula. And I found this explicitly in Yoreh Deah, Laws of Vows siman 218 seif 2 in the hagahah, and in Taz and Shach — see there. And we hold lung examination is d'rabbanan and safek lekula, as the Rashba wrote in a responsum and as the hagahah brings in this siman seif 17. One should not challenge: why did the Gemara not resolve this safek from that which is taught in Torat Kohanim, parashat Tazria, brought in the Yalkut there on the verse "When a woman conceives and bears" — to exclude one who gave birth before the [giving of] speech; brought in Tosafot Nazir (daf 54a) and Niddah (daf 70b) — see there. One may say: there the Merciful One made it dependent on birth, and it already passed while permitted before speech; but regarding neveilah the question is whether it depends on making neveilah while it was permitted, or perhaps it depends on the time of eating and it is now forbidden — examine well. But it is difficult for me: let it be resolved from the braita in Torat Kohanim brought in Horayot (daf 10): "A man, when there is in the skin of his flesh" — from speech onward; and Rashi explained: those lesions that occurred before speech do not impart impurity after speech. And likewise it is taught in Torat Kohanim, parashat Metzora, regarding a zav: "A man, a man when he becomes a zav" — from speech onward. And since Scripture was needed for lesions and zav to purify what was before speech — learn that generally, without a verse, even what was before was forbidden. One may answer per what Noda B'Yehuda wrote in responsa Mahadura Tinyana, siman 64: it is not asked except regarding limbs of neveilah, for when they were commanded they were not forbidden immediately, only after time; not so regarding something forbidden immediately — certainly even what was in their hands was forbidden; therefore the question does not arise for all prohibitions forbidden at the giving of the Torah whether what was in their hands was forbidden — end. According to this one may say it cannot be resolved from lesions and zav to limbs of neveilah that they are forbidden, since there the command was to impart impurity immediately, not so this command regarding limbs of neveilah. However, regarding the question, it appears there is room to forbid from the mishnah in the last chapter of Nega'im: "One who buys garments from a non-Jew must examine them first." And Rosh explains: this garment is fit for nega'im. And in responsum of Bach siman 130, acharonim bring it, s.k. 5, wrote regarding intentionally destroying the lung: it is forbidden on account of milk he already has. And it appears to me that regarding lung lost without examination — even for those who are stringent to forbid the meat — regarding milk one need not be stringent:

(There seif 16) The examiner said it was not in order and another contradicts him — they establish the animal in the presumption of permissibility. One must say this deals when the lung was lost and it is impossible to clarify; but if it were possible to clarify, we are concerned for the words of the one who forbids and clarification is required. And as Tosafos wrote, chapter HaOmer (daf 65b), s.v. nitme'u (and on daf 66), s.v. shalach. And likewise the hagahah, siman 127 seif 3. And the same law applies regarding a boil on the lower part, as the hagahah wrote above siman 37 seif 4. And see Mahari Weil, klal 20 s.k. 19. And see Shach siman 83 s.k. 11: where one could say "from whence did it come?" — here there is a general rule that clarification is not required; however, according to one view it is worse. And Ritva in Eruvin (daf 59) wrote: even in d'rabbanan, where it is possible to establish the matter, we do not rely on establishing lekula unless there is great effort — we judge as though it cannot be established. I have proof that in a safek of law clarification is not required: we hold that in d'rabbanan we act and afterward raise objections. And where there is a safek, clarification is not required; and likewise we rely on majority — only on presumption we do not rely. And so in pesakim of Mahari and in She'erit Menachem, Laws of Mikvaot. With this one may explain what was challenged on the Rambam, chapter 12 of Laws of Bikkurim — see there. And he wrote in She'erit Menachem it was safek 1 in d'rabbanan. However, he wrote in Beit Avraham: if it is about to become clear on its own, such as an animal that limps — it is forbidden to eat its milk even where there is safek that perhaps the limb did not fall off, and you will say it fell off, perhaps the tendons were not consumed. However, in responsa Ginat Shoshanim he wrote to permit. And likewise in responsa Tur HaEven he permits even when certainly the tendons were consumed, for it is a balanced safek and the animal itself has presumption of permissibility, for it was not born in this safek; and also in Beit Avraham itself he retracted below. However, what Beit Avraham brought as proof from the Rosh, chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah — it slipped his attention, for he already rejected this in Mahari Weil. Also in She'erit Menachem he concludes that clarification is never required. And see Noda B'Yehuda who distinguishes regarding safek where there was presumption of prohibition: if the presumption was always, such as presumption of an animal in its lifetime that never had presumption of permissibility — clarification is required. But a prohibition that had presumption of permissibility — safek helps to remove from presumption of prohibition and establish presumption of permissibility as originally, without clarification — such as presumption of a limb, and as Hagahot Maimoniyot regarding three sefeikot. (Shulchan Aruch in the hagahah) However, if non-Jewish slaughterers are accustomed to do so, there is concern even b'dieved. It appears that if no sircha leak is found, it is no worse than what the Rambam wrote and Shulchan Aruch brought above seif 3: a non-Jew or Israelite came and removed the lung before examination while it exists — they inflate it. And according to acharonim who declare kosher there without great financial loss — likewise here. However, when he left it intentionally it is as intentional nullification; it appears from Shach's words it is forbidden for all. Not like Peri Megadim who wrote that for others it is permitted, as in every intentional nullification. And so in Hagurat Shmuel, brought in Lechem Shlomo. And it is not so: for Rivash wrote in responsum siman 498 regarding nullifying lung examination — it is as though nullified for the entire community, for whoever wishes to buy; even though it was not done for a specific person but stam for whoever wishes to buy — and behold it is as though done for them specifically. And proof from that which is in the second chapter of Eruvin regarding techumin: if he came to sell also for Israelites — likewise as though it came for them specifically.
