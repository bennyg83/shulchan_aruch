Rashi and his supporters explained the reason that not in order is treifah: because there is no sircha without a hole. According to his explanation, the Tosafos conclude that the reason that in order is kosher is because when it is sircha'd in a place that is not positioned to separate, its neighbor closes it and protects it more, because the membrane goes and strengthens — end of his words. And the meaning is that the sircha closes its neighbor. And the Beit Yosef copied: the uma closed its neighbor. And the Tosafos already rejected this there; and so it is explained in the Rosh, as he explained; and so understood Maharam of Tiqqun in the hagahah on the Alfasi; and the Rashba and the Ran wrote according to Rashi that the uma closed its neighbor. In the name of the Raavad they wrote: in order, the sircha comes on account of the pressure of its reclining and not on account of a hole. The Rashba and the Ran themselves hold that there is sircha without a hole, and not in order is treifah because it is destined to separate and is considered like a hole. And in truth Maharshal ruled thus. But it appears to be the Rama's view: for perforce we, who are stringent regarding a lost lung, it is certainly d'rabbanan — for examination of the lung is d'rabbanan, as all the poskim wrote. And since he did not act according to the Sages' enactment, they forbade even b'dieved; for if not so, he would not fulfill the Sages' enactment. But when one says it was examined properly and was kosher, and one says it was treifah — in either case it was examined and he did the enactment of the Sages; only this one says kosher and this one says treifah. If so, it is one against one, and we establish the animal in the presumption of permissibility — for certainly all agree it was slaughtered and stands in full presumption of permissibility, and every doubt after slaughter we lean leniently. Only that the Sages required examining the lung because it is liable to become treifah; and from this they forbade even b'dieved when it was not examined. But by law it is full permission — as I demonstrated in my book with many clear proofs and demonstrations; and as written in s.k. 8. And the Bach himself likewise at the beginning of this siman. If so, when the lung is before us and one says in order and one says not in order, and the like — certainly it stands in the presumption of kosher (and in any case there is no difficulty, for the majority of poskim and the Mechaber hold that a lost lung is kosher, and their reasoning is primary in many places in the Talmud, as in my book; and the Rav in the hagahah and the Bach relied on them in a case of great loss. Therefore, even though they are stringent where there is not great loss, nevertheless give it to them — do not add upon it; and this is specifically when it was truly lost, unlike here). And Rabbenu Shimshon and the Mechaber, who wrote "and it is like an animal whose lung was lost," etc. — this is what he meant: just as there it is permitted by law from the reason "once slaughtered, it was permitted," so too here. And this is clear:
