**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Some say, etc., and some prohibit.</b> Their reasoning is based on the statement there, 'Any divination that is not,' etc., which implies that in circumstances like theirs it is divination and is prohibited. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'Like Eliezer,' etc., and s.v. 'And like Yonatan,' etc. Regarding the statement, 'A house, a child, and a woman,' etc., the Rambam writes that it is permitted because the person did not direct his conduct by it and did not refrain from acting. Rather, he made a sign for himself from something that had already occurred. This differs from Rashi's explanation. Semag and the other authorities challenged the Rambam, since Rabbi Yochanan tested matters through a child and refrained from descending to Babylonia because of it. The same appears at the beginning of the chapter 'One Who Was Seized,' and from the account that Rav tested matters by a ferry, etc. Rather, the statement, 'A house, a child,' etc., means that one may rely on them, as Rashi explains. The statement, 'Any divination,' etc., was not said concerning the prohibition itself, but concerning whether one may rely on it or be apprehensive because of it. Only where one initially established it as a sign, as Yonatan and Eliezer did, may he rely on it and be apprehensive. If he did not initially establish it as a sign and it occurred on its own, he need not be concerned. Concerning this they said, 'A house, a child, and a woman, although,' etc. Meaning, although he did not initially establish them as a sign, they may nevertheless constitute a sign that warrants concern, provided the pattern has become established, etc. This differs from Yonatan's divination, which was significant even the first time. See Radak to I Samuel 14. This is also Raavad's view, and there is consequently no need for Tosafot's strained explanation cited above.

<b>And although, etc.</b> This is the Rambam's view, as he also writes in his Commentary to the Mishnah, chapter four of Avodah Zarah. All later authorities disagreed with him, since many incantations are recorded in the Gemara. He was drawn after philosophy, and therefore wrote that sorcery, Divine Names, incantations, demons, and amulets are all false. They have already struck his position upon its crown, for many actual incidents are found in the Gemara. Regarding Divine Names and sorcery, she uttered a formula and bound the boat, and they responded, etc. (Shabbat 81b; Chullin 105b). There are also the incidents at the end of chapter four of Sanhedrin and in the Yerushalmi there involving Rabbi Eliezer, Rabbi Yehoshua, and Ben Beteira; Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Oshaya, who created a third-grown calf; Rabbi Yehoshua, who uttered a formula and suspended him between heaven and earth (Bekhorot 8b); and Avishai ben Tzeruyah (Sanhedrin 95a), among many similar accounts. The Sages also said, at the end of chapter four of Sanhedrin and in Chullin 7b, 'Why are they called keshafim,' etc. The Torah itself testifies, 'and they became serpents.' See the Zohar there. Amulets are likewise mentioned in many places, and the incantations are too numerous to count. Philosophy misled him, through its persuasive influence, to interpret the entire Gemara allegorically and uproot its statements from their plain meaning. Heaven forbid that I believe either the philosophers or their multitude. Rather, all these matters are according to their plain meaning, though they also possess an inner dimension, not the so-called inner meaning of the philosophers, which is itself external, but the inner meaning taught by the masters of truth.

<b>מעשה כו'. </b>ממש"ש בלטיהם אלו מעשה שדים בלהטיהם אלו מעשה כשפים כו' אלמא דבכלל כשפים הוא. הרמ"ה. והרא"ש הוכיח משם להיפך מדאביי פי' שם הי ניהו מעשה שדים והי ניהו כו' משמע דמעשה שדים מותר דאל"כ מאי נ"מ ואינו מוכרח שכן פי' בפ' ע"פ (קי"א ב') דבי פרחא כו' למנ"מ לקמיע ה"נ כן ואדרבה מדקאמר וע"י ספר יצירה מותר לכתחלה ול"ק רבותא יותר מעשה שדים ודברי הרא"ש דחוקין אבל מההיא דפ' חלק (סנהדרין ק"א א') אין שואלין בשדים בשבת רי"א כו' ואף ר"י לא אמר כו' משמע להדיא שמותר אלא שהרא"ש דחאה ואמר שמא דוקא לישאל על הגניבה אבל לעשות מעשה אסור וז"ש ויש כו' ועבא"ח סי' ש"ז סי"ח: <br><b>(ליקוט) מעשה כו' ויש כו'. </b>וכמש"ש (ק"א א') שרי שמן כו' אלא שמכזבין אבל גירסת הרי"ף (ברי"ף שלפנינו ליתא אלא ברא"ש) מפני שמכזבין ור"ל ולא אתי לאמשוכי ולהימנו בהן ומשמע דשאר מעשה שדים אסור וצ"ע הא דאמר שם ת"ר (גי' הרי"ף ורא"ש שם) אין כו' ואף ר"י לא אמר כו' ואפשר דג"כ בשרי שמן כו' ודוחק וצ"ע (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An act, etc.</b> This follows the statement there: 'Through their secret arts,' these are acts of demons; 'through their sorceries,' these are acts of witchcraft, etc. Evidently, an act involving demons is included within witchcraft. This is Ramah's view. Rosh proves the opposite from Abaye's explanation there, 'Which are acts of demons and which are,' etc. This implies that acts involving demons are permitted, for otherwise what practical difference would there be? This proof is not conclusive, since the Gemara similarly explains in Pesachim 111b, 'of the house of Paracha,' etc., and asks what practical difference follows, answering that it concerns an amulet. The same may apply here. On the contrary, since the Gemara says that an act performed through Sefer Yetzirah is permitted initially, it should have stated the greater novelty that an act involving demons is permitted. Rosh's words are therefore strained. However, the passage in chapter Chelek (Sanhedrin 101a), 'One may not consult demons on Shabbat; Rabbi Yose says,' etc., and, 'Even Rabbi Yose said only,' etc., clearly implies that consulting them is permitted during the week. Rosh rejects this proof by suggesting that perhaps only asking about a theft is permitted, while performing an act is prohibited. This is the meaning of, 'and there are,' etc. See Orach Chaim, siman 307, seif 18.<br><b>(Likkut) An act, etc., and there are, etc.</b> As stated there (101a), consulting the princes of oil, etc., is permitted, except that they give false answers. Rif's version, though it is absent from our text of Rif and appears only in Rosh, reads, 'because they give false answers.' Meaning, one will not be drawn after them and come to trust them. This implies that other acts involving demons are prohibited. This requires study, for the baraita there states, according to Rif's and Rosh's version, 'One may not,' etc., and, 'Even Rabbi Yose said only,' etc. Perhaps this too refers specifically to the princes of oil, etc., but that is strained and requires study. End of Likkut.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And through adjuration, etc.</b> This resembles use of Sefer Yetzirah, which is permitted. As stated regarding Ashmedai (Gittin 68a), 'Perhaps your master's Name is upon you,' meaning that he had adjured him. Many adjurations are recorded, including the incidents involving Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Oshaya (Shabbat 81b; Chullin 105b), Rabbi Yehoshua (Yerushalmi, end of chapter four of Sanhedrin), and Rabbi Chanina bar Papi (Kiddushin 39b), as well as many similar incidents in the Gemara and Midrashim in which the Sages used them. See Ramban's responsum, cited by Beit Yosef.

Apart from the fact that Shach's explanation is strained, Keritot 3b explicitly records Rava's statement, 'One who burns incense to a demon in order to bind it.'
