**** ENGLISH ****
The law of sharp food that was cut with a meat knife. In it are 5 seifim: A radish or beet that one cut with a meat knife that is ben yomo or that is not well scraped is forbidden to eat with milk until one removes from the place of the cut an amount for removal of place that is like the thickness of a finger, or one tastes it and there is no meat taste in it — then it is permitted with rinsing. And some say the same law applies when it is not ben yomo and it is well scraped. And if one did not remove the amount for removal of place and also did not taste them, and cooked them in milk, one needs sixty against what the knife touched in them. And the same law applies if one cut them with a non-Jew's knife: {Rama: And if one cut them finely, one must estimate sixty against the entire radish (Beit Yosef in the name of Semak). And some say if one cut a radish with a forbidden knife, all of it is forbidden (Rashba and Ran, and so implied in Arukh general rule 35); and likewise if one cut with a meat knife, all of it is forbidden with milk — so is the custom l'chatchila, but b'dieved one forbids only the amount for removal (Beit Yosef and Or Zarua). And all this is if one cut the radish itself; but if one cut the greens on the radish, there is no concern. And if there is doubt whether it was cut with a forbidden knife, we follow leniency (there); therefore they buy radishes that are cut toward their tails, for we rely on presumption and half-and-half (there in the hagahah and Maharai in Hagahat Sh"D). And in a place where only cut ones are found, the custom is to buy and permit through removal of place.}
