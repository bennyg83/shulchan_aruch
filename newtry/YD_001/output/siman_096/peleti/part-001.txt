צנון וכו' הר"ב הפר"ח השיג דלא שמענו דמאן דסבר נצלו מותר דיהי' חורפא דצנון אלים מצלי כי הסה"ת דאוסר נצלו אבל מאן דמתיר לא שמענו ובאמת רש"י דס"ל נתבשלו מותר מותר משמע דנצלו אסור דרש"י פי' בזבחי' דלכך אסר רבה ב"א תנור משום דאין מוציא בלעו ואי ס"ל לרש"י נצלה מותר ובתנור אסור משום טיחה ע"גא"כ מי דחקו לרש"י לומר דרבה ב"א פליג וס"ל דנ"ט בר נ"ט אסור דלמא מותר ומשום טיפה א"ו דס"ל לרש"י בנצלו אסור וטיחה שע"ג לא ס"ל לרש"י ואם כי היא לדחות זה דס"ל לרש"י על הטיחה מהכ"ת לא יועיל היסק מ"מ נכרים הדברים דרש"י ס"ל לחלק ולכן דין זה צל"ע ובמקום צידד עוד דבר א' יש להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
Radish, etc. — Rama: Peri Chadash challenged that we have not heard that one who holds roasted is permitted holds radish sharpness is stronger than roasting, for Semak forbids roasted; but one who permits — we have not heard. In truth Rashi, who holds cooked is permitted, implies roasted is forbidden, for Rashi explains in Zevachim that therefore Rabbah bar Avuha forbade an oven because it does not emit absorption; if Rashi held roasted permitted and oven forbidden because of plastering on top — who pushed Rashi to say Rabbah bar Avuha disagrees and holds noten taam bar noten taam is forbidden? Perhaps permitted; and because of a drop I say Rashi holds roasted forbidden and plastering on top Rashi does not hold. Even if to reject that Rashi holds regarding plastering from the verse, heating would not help; nevertheless it is recognizable that Rashi holds to distinguish; therefore this law requires study; and in its place I sided with another matter to be lenient.

And some say the same when not ben yomo, etc. — one should not challenge this view: what is the difficulty from purging? Because it is nat bar lichtamil — perhaps the Torah forbade lest one use a sharp thing that spoils for improvement even when not ben yomo; and similarly Rashba challenged in responsa 397; see there. According to what the poskim wrote, therefore gentile vessels we establish as not ben yomo because there is a safek safeka doubt it was not used today; if you say it was used, perhaps with permitted matter — no difficulty, for here too safek safeka perhaps used permitted matter; if you say forbidden, lest one not use sharp thing with pressure; and more one may say as Rashba wrote we follow majority use, and since majority is not with sharp thing hag'alah is not needed; but R' Meir is concerned for minority — the difficulty remains; and from where for R' Meir is nat bar lichtamil forbidden from gentile purging? We are concerned for minority lest one cook sharp thing — certainly according to Tosafot at the beginning of Chullin 12b R' Meir rabbinically is concerned for minority — investigate; but according to what appears from Rashba there he holds thus for Torah law — one may say as a ben yomo pot spoils somewhat, so a sharp thing spoils somewhat; and for R' Meir there is no difference between spoiling somewhat and spoiling for improvement, as explained in the Gemara; and as spoiling somewhat is forbidden, so spoiling for improvement is forbidden; and Rabbanan — in truth the Mechaber taught thus only she is she that the Torah did not forbid ben yomo pot or sharp thing; and with this one may resolve Tosafot's difficulty how they purged large vessels — the main reason was because of sharp thing, and this is the Gemara's intent ben yoma leid — except because of sharp thing that makes it ben yomo; if so regarding water nevelah does not apply, for it is nat bar lichtamil and not a sharp thing; and perhaps therefore Tosafot were precise and said the Torah forbade only ben yomo, for we already stood on it twice; and accordingly it is well that otherwise we would say because of sharp thing; and Tosafot's language ben yomo specifically — they hold for Rabbanan we follow majority, and they challenged well — investigate.
