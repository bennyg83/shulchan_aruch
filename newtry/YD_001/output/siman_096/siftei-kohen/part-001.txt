**** ENGLISH ****
Or that one tastes it, etc. That tasting is effective is explained in the Gemara and in Ashiri, Tur, and Rashba; however I proved in my book from the Gemara that l'chatchila it is forbidden to eat it with kutach through tasting, and I wrote there that so is implied by the Rambam, Semag, Semak, Mordechai, and other authors who did not mention the heter of tasting; and so too Maharshal in his book chapter 25 siman 61, and the Rav in Turei Chayim introduction to general rule 61 — unlike what is implied from the words of Rosh, Tur, Rashba, and Mechaber that even l'chatchila it is permitted. However, certainly if one tasted and there is no taste and one cooked it b'dieved in milk it is permitted, since certainly there is no taste in this pot, as stated explicitly in the Gemara and poskim, and it is plain; and so is implied in Maharshal and Turei Chayim there — that is, the Rav did not emend here at all what Mechaber said "or one tastes it," as he emended at the beginning of simanim 92 and 98, because nevertheless regarding b'dieved tasting is effective: specifically for tasting by a non-Jew we are not accustomed to rely even b'dieved, unlike tasting by a Jew, as written below siman 98 s.k. 5. (And that he did not emend that l'chatchila one may not rely on tasting is because it is included in what he wrote that if one cut with a meat knife all of it is forbidden with milk, etc., for the language "all of it is forbidden" implies there is no remedy.) This is unlike HaEtz Yosef, who omitted entirely that tasting is effective — it appears he holds that even b'dieved we do not rely on tasting by a Jew, as written below siman 98 — but this is not so; see what is written there.

**** ENGLISH ****
One must estimate sixty against the entire radish. For each piece and piece is forbidden the amount for removal of place; however this is specifically when the radish is smaller than the knife or the knife is not before us so we can estimate with it. But when the knife is smaller than the radish and it is before us, it is plain one does not need sixty but only against the knife. However, with a non-Jew's knife or a forbidden knife, for us who hold chein nafsho in all prohibitions, each piece and piece is nevelah the amount for removal of place; therefore when one cuts it finely one must estimate sixty against the entire radish even if it is larger than the knife — investigate. However, certainly where they cooked a whole radish that was cut with a non-Jew's or forbidden knife, since it does not forbid more than the amount for removal of place, if there is sixty in the dish against the removal of place it is permitted; and so is implied from the words of the Rav in Turei Chayim there seif 2 and here in the hagahah, and it is plain in his view. And in Pri Megadim he wrote that if one cut with a non-Jew's knife it is nevelah and needs sixty against the entire radish even if one did not cut finely — end of his words; this is astounding, for since it is only nevelah the removal of place, sixty against the removal of place should suffice. Perhaps he meant what Maharam of Lublin wrote in responsa siman 92: even though initially it is only nevelah the removal of place, immediately when placed in the dish the entire radish becomes nevelah, for that removal of place is absorbed prohibition if there is not sixty in the radish itself against the removal of place — end of his words. But this too is not so, for since the removal of place is not forbidden in itself and it is not a distinct visible prohibition and it is only forbidden the amount for removal of place, absorbed prohibition does not apply — investigate. And certainly some disagree with all absorbed prohibition in simanim 72 and 92 and several places; and I brought in siman 22 s.k. 10 in the name of some poskim who say chein nafsho only when the piece stands by itself but not in half a piece and the like; and even for poskim who do not hold thus, they agree absorbed prohibition does not apply in such a case; and so too Bach in siman 22 seif 6: if the fowl was salted with its entrails it needs removal of place, and if cooked afterward one needs sixty in the dish against the removal of place and does not require sixty in the fowl itself against the removal of place — or as he explained; and this is the view of the Rav and it is clear.
