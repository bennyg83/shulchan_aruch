**** ENGLISH ****As an idle worker, etc. In the Gemara it states: the tanna taught "as an idle worker" — what is "as an idle worker"? Abaye said: as an idle worker of that work from which he refrained. Rashi explained: meaning if he first did heavy work and profits greatly and this is light work, they estimate how much he would forgo from his wages to do this work — which is more than what they give to do it, for a person does not forgo great profit except a little even though it comes to him with more trouble. Tosafot challenged Rashi and explained: therefore it appears to explain "as an idle worker" meaning as one who sits completely idle, etc.; however in chapter Elu Metziot regarding returning a lost object where it teaches "he takes his wages as an idle worker" — it appears like the Kuntres's explanation from that they challenge there "behold he is not idle" because it implies to them as one who sits completely idle — end of their words. It appears from Tosafot's words that they have this version in the Gemara "behold he is not idle"; and likewise the Rosh regarding returning a lost object like Rashi's explanation — meaning such as work for which one takes four zuz and to leave it and sit idle he would take one zuz, and to toil in returning a lost object he would take two zuz and he reaches one zuz in wages for returning the lost object — end of his words. Tosafot challenged this: if there like Rashi he takes wages for returning a lost object, and in HaMekabel we say he was an unpaid guardian of a lost object who has no benefit — one may say there is no concern if he takes wages since he is engaged in other work and loses, etc. — end of their words. The implication of Tosafot's words that they wrote "as one who sits completely idle" is as Beit Yosef wrote: they estimate how much a person wishes to take in order to be idle from his work completely — that now whether he gives him now light work or heavy work his measure is equal, meaning they assess his usual work how much this one would wish to take and sit completely idle. And Tur wrote: and not all his wages but they look at a person who is idle and has no work — how much he would wish to take and engage in this business; and even if he had work for which they give him good wages and he refrains from it — we do not say how much this one would wish to take and refrain from his work and engage in this business that he would take only much; rather they estimate an idle person as I explained — end of his words. And behold Maharshal in Sefer Chokhmat Shlomo agreed to Tur's explanation — he challenged according to Bach's explanation: what is different in truth between the law of ribbis and the law of returning a lost object, and why did it not challenge there as well "behold he is not idle" since from the language "as an idle worker" it challenges — see there in chapter Eizehu Neshekh. And it appears there is no difficulty at all — for this challenge "behold he is not idle" is not the plain Gemara challenging it, but Abaye himself challenges it and answers "as an idle worker of that work," etc., in the manner Tosafot are accustomed to explain everywhere there is a difficulty and afterward an amora is mentioned who is also the questioner; and as Tosafot wrote in chapter Elu Metziot in what is written "how many did Rabbi Yitzchak establish four cubits," etc. And if so here on Abaye himself there is what to challenge: why was he not challenged in chapter Eizehu Neshekh regarding giving the dealer's wages where they also said "as an idle worker" — one may say he holds in dust of ribbis they were lenient as the Rosh wrote in this chapter; and likewise he himself according to Tur's explanation here for his leniency — meaning they assess an idle person how much he takes from this work; and in returning a lost object they also assess according to the first work like Rashi's explanation — and I wondered: for behold the Rosh wrote explicitly in chapter Eizehu Neshekh "but here in dust of ribbis the Sages were lenient," etc. And in Derishah he challenged Tur's explanation: since he pays him for the work of returning a lost object even though he was already engaged in work — as if he were idle — why did Tosafot challenge specifically on Rashi's explanation, behold according to them too he takes his wages and there is no difficulty; for in truth according to Tosafot per Tur's explanation he takes nothing for returning a lost object if he is an idle person — only if he was already engaged in work it is proper he not lose because of the returning and therefore they give him wages for refraining from his work in this manner: they assess how much accrues to him in wages for the returning work and likewise give him for the loss he has from his prior work. But according to Rashi who takes one zuz more as I copied in the Rosh's name — certainly it is difficult "behold he takes wages for returning a lost object"; and on this they answered there is no concern since in any case he has a loss and must take — there is no concern if he also takes for the returning; and Mahari Weil also elaborated to challenge and answer between the explanations; and in my humble opinion both Bach's explanation and Tur's explanation are true — for certainly the implication of Tosafot is like Bach's explanation as I wrote above; and likewise it appears in Tosafot of the Rosh that Beit Yosef brought; and as I will explain shortly — and it is that according to Bach's explanation we say how much one worker wishes to take in order to be idle from his work completely — it works out for a master of work: we know the measure of labor wages for this; but what shall we do with a completely idle person who accepts an iska — how shall we estimate labor wages? Therefore in this we assess according to what an idle person takes to do this business — for if less than this we do not know his measure; perforce we assess according to what an idle person takes from this business, which is less than what a plain person takes; and even for Rabbi Meir who is lenient in the Gemara and holds his wages whether much or little — he is lenient only with a master of work that he not calculate according to his work when he already engaged; but nevertheless he is not lenient from what an idle person takes, meaning even though the way of workers in that city is to take four gold coins — only that an idle person from all work takes less than that — that lesser sum the giver must give the recipient; and in this Bach agrees with Tur — except if he is a master of work and his work was completely refrained, Tur is more stringent to give him that loss. And what Bach challenged on Tur in what he wrote that they assess an idle person how much he would take in this business — and if so everything is according to the weight of the business the recipient accepts; and why do we say in the Gemara "it is necessary" — for if the latter clause were not taught, meaning when he gives him money to buy produce for profit together, we might say it does not suffice as an idle worker since the trouble is great; and if according to Tur's words how could we say so — behold he pays him that trouble that an idle person wishes more in order to do heavy work than light — there is no difficulty: for we might say since there is great trouble in this we do not assess his wages as if he were idle but we assess with a plain person how much he would take in this business. But in what Tosafot challenge Rashi one cannot answer so — for according to Rashi there is no leniency at all in the world; rather the mishnah follows Rabbi Shimon and teaches us the stringency that he must specifically pay all his loss.
