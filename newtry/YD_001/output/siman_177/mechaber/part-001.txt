**** ENGLISH ****
That one may not accept “tzon barzel” from a Jew, and many laws of business dealings that are forbidden because of interest; it contains 40 se’ifim. We do not accept “tzon barzel” from a Jew — meaning, that he accepts from him 100 sheep, and the shearings and the offspring and the milk are [split] in the middle as a third or a quarter until a year or until 2 years, as they stipulated between them; and if the sheep die, the recipient pays their value — this is forbidden, for the owner of the sheep is close to profit and far from loss. Therefore, if the owner of the sheep accepted upon himself that if they became more expensive or cheaper, or were torn [by predators], they remain in his domain, this is permitted — and so too, anything similar; and the same law applies to one who accepts merchandise in such a manner (Tur). {Rama: And all this is only when the recipient has profit; but if he has no profit at all in what he accepts, there is no loan here at all, and even if he accepted upon himself all responsibility it is permitted, for it is permitted for an unpaid custodian to accept upon himself to be like a borrower (Beit Yosef and Ba’al HaTerumos in the name of the sages of Lunel, and the Ran in a responsum).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who gives money to his fellow as an iska — meaning, that half the responsibility is on the giver and half on the recipient — it is forbidden, for every iska is half loan and half deposit, and it turns out that he toils with the deposit portion because of the loan portion; therefore he must give him wages for his toil — for each and every day of the days of the partnership — like an idle laborer for that labor from which he is idle. And if he had another business, any amount to engage in with this one’s money, he need not give him wages for every day; rather, even if he gave him a dinar for all the days of the partnership, it suffices; and if they diminished or increased, it is equally for the middle. And likewise, if he said to him, “All the profit will be yours; [give] its third or its tenth as your wages,” since he has another business, this is permitted; and if they lost, he will lose half. And if the one engaging was his sharecropper, and he had another business, he need not give him any other wages at all, for the sharecropper is subjugated to the owner of the field. {Rama: And the entire responsibility of the deposit-half must be on the giver, even responsibility for accidents (Tur and poskim in the name of R' Yitzchak), and not like those who are lenient and permit with partial responsibility (Rivan).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who gives money to his fellow, without specification, to engage in with them, and he did not set with him at the beginning of the business anything as wages for his toil; and when they came to an accounting, and he did not want to give him wages for each and every day of the days of the partnership — and if he had another business, he did not want to give him a dinar for all the days of the partnership — the wages of the one engaging, regarding the half that is a deposit in his hand, is a third of the profit of the deposit, which is a sixth of the profit of all the money. Therefore, if they profited, the one engaging takes 2 thirds of the profit: half the profit of half the money, which is a loan, and a sixth of the profit as wages for engaging with the deposit; it turns out that all is 2 thirds of the profit. And the owner of the money takes a third of the profit. And if they diminished, the one engaging loses a third of the loss, for he is liable for half the loss because half the money is a loan, and he has a sixth as his wages in that half that is a deposit; it turns out that there remains upon him a third of the loss, and the owner of the money loses two thirds of the loss.
