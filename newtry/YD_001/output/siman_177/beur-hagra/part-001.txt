<b>דהיינו כו'. </b>רמב"ם וכפירש"י שם אבל תוס' פי' שנותן לו דבר קצוץ וכ"ה בתוספתא וירושלמי שם והביאו תוס' שם ע"ש בד"ה אין כו' וערש"י שם ד"ה אין כו' ואע"ג כו' והר"ן כתב דכאן קמ"ל אע"ג דנותן לו שכרו כפועל לא מהני ובזה מתורץ קושיא שניה של תוס' שם ועוד דפריך גמ' כו'. וז"ש כאן אין מקבלין כלל ובס"ב כ' הנותן כו' שמותר ע"י שכר עמלו. ואין נ"מ בין פירש"י ותוס' לדינא אלא בפי' דמתני' דלרש"י הוא אבק רבית ולר"ת הוא רבית קצוצה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Meaning, etc.</b> Rambam and according to Rashi there; but Tosafot explain that he gives him a fixed amount; and likewise in Tosefta and Yerushalmi there, and Tosafot brought it there, see there s.v. ein, etc.; and see Rashi there s.v. ein, etc., and even though, etc.; and Ran wrote that here it teaches us that even though he gives him his wages as an idle worker it does not help; and with this the second difficulty of Tosafot there is resolved; and further the Gemara challenges, etc. And this is what is written: here one does not accept at all; and in seif 2 it writes "the giver," etc., that it is permitted through his labor wages. And there is no practical difference between Rashi's explanation and Tosafot's for the law, only in explaining the mishnah, for Rashi it is dust of ribbis and for Rabbeinu Tam it is fixed ribbis.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And all this not, etc.</b> As written in Yerushalmi: one who carries a bundle from place to place, he said to him "give and I will give you as you give in such-and-such place", if with the giver's liability it is forbidden and the taker permitted; but porters who receive from homeowners set produce for them in the expensive place at the cheap rate, Rabbi Huna said he becomes his agent; they challenged Rabbi Huna: an agent who was compelled is exempt from accidents, not always does he stipulate to be like a borrower. And meaning: not so with one who carries a bundle, since he also does it for his benefit, not so with porters; and there is no need for what is written in the Gemara "it is satisfactory to them that he reveals," etc. (and as written above siman 173 s.k. 34); and there in the mishnah: one does not accept iron flock, etc.; what is iron flock, etc.; Rabbi Yirmiyah asked there: you say a paid guardian is liable for accidents, forbidden; and here you say a paid guardian is liable for accidents, permitted. Meaning: here in iron flock he said since he accepts liability upon it, forbidden; and there with porters you said even though he accepts liability upon it, permitted. And the language of the Yerushalmi is reversed, as is known in many places, and as the Rosh wrote at the end of chapter HaDayanim; and they answered: not always does he stipulate to be like a borrower, therefore there it is permitted since they have no benefit at all, not so here where he takes wages and does it for his benefit, forbidden when he accepts liability upon it. And this is what is written: and even if he accepted, etc. (Likkut) And all this not, etc. As written page 1: the Sages taught, etc., close to loss and far from profit, a pious person; and meaning that he takes no wages at all; and see Rashi there s.v. close to profit, etc. (end). (Likkut) And all this, etc. And likewise Rashi end 72b s.v. give me, etc.; and Tosafot 73a s.v. the porters. But it appears, etc., and the porters would profit, etc. (end).

<b>The giver, etc.</b> This entire seif is the language of the Rambam and so is correct. And behold what is written "and if he had a business," etc., and likewise "if," etc., "since," etc., is as written seif 9: Rav said permitted a third, etc.; rather Shmuel said he fixes, etc.; and as the first authority there, for Rav said, etc., and he holds Shmuel agrees in this manner, for it was stated in this context; and he explains what is written "permitted a third," etc., meaning from what is permitted on the principal a third shall be yours, meaning a third of the wages is yours permitted, and he need not give him as an idle worker; and Shmuel disagrees on this lest he have no wages at all; and likewise he disagrees with Rava's words there "half in profit," etc., for Rava speaks according to the halachah that the halachah is like Rav, and as the Rif wrote that we rule like Rav in prohibitions; and "permitted a third" he explains not specifically so but less as well; and he explains that all this is when he did not fix for him from the outset but when they come to divide he gives him, and as written in seif 3 "in what case," etc.; and likewise holds the Rambam as written below; and likewise Shmuel's words that Rav agrees with him that all the more so in such a case it is permitted; and also even if he did not fix from the outset, since he has another business; and likewise one could challenge Shmuel according to his opinion, except he could say Shmuel holds like Rabbi Yehudah even if he did not dip, etc.; but to the conclusion it is even according to Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Shimon; and he explains what is written Rabbi Eliezer of Gerona, etc., he said to him until now money, etc., not like Rashi's explanation because now it is not an iska, for the Rambam wrote explicitly chapter 6 that in such a case it is also an iska. <b>(ליקוט) ובעל המעות כו'. </b>ממ"ש בפ' בתרא דב"ב (קס"ז ב' במתני') ואין כותבין שטר אריסות כו' אע"ג דבמלוה א"צ רק דעת הלוה כמש"ש אלא משום דא"י לחזור בו ועבח"מ סי' ש"כ ס"ב בהג"ה ובתוספתא דמ"ק אין כותבין שטר אריסות וקבלנות במועד ר"י מתיר שמא יקדמנו אחר ועבא"ח סי' תקמ"ה וכ"ש התחלת מלאכה אף בלא כתיבה ועח"מ סי' י"ג ס"ב בהג"ה (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Likkut) And the owner of the money, etc.</b> This follows from the Mishnah in the final chapter of Bava Batra, 167b: one may not write a sharecropping contract, etc. Although with a loan only the borrower's consent is required, as stated there, here both parties' consent is needed because the worker cannot retract. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 320 seif 2 in the gloss. The Tosefta of Moed Katan states that one may not write a sharecropping or contracting agreement during Chol HaMoed; Rabbi Yehudah permits it lest another person precede him. See Orach Chaim, siman 545. All the more so, beginning the work itself requires consent even without a written document. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 13 seif 2 in the gloss. (End.)
