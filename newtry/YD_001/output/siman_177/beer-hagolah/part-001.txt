לשון הרמב"ם בפ"ו מהלכות שלוחין ושותפי' דין ב' מברייתא שם וכדמפרש אביי: <br>(°) פי' רש"י שאם היה נפח או נגר כמה אדם רוצה ליטול ליבטל ממלאכה כבדה כזו ולעשות מלאכה קלה וכן משמע מדברי הרי"ף והתוס' פירשו כפועל בטל היינו כיושב ובטל לגמרי ואין לו שום מלאכה כמה היה רוצה ליקח להתעסק בזה העסק וכ"כ הרא"ש שם. וכתב עוד דהכא באבק רבית הקילו חכמים ובדבר מועט שנותן לו לא מחזי כרבית וכ"כ הטור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The wording of the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchot Sheluchin VeShutafin, halachah 2, from the baraita there, as Abaye explains it.</b> Rashi explains that if he was a bellows-worker or carpenter, one estimates how much a person would accept to desist from such heavy labor and engage in lighter work. This is likewise implied by the Rif. Tosafot explains "an idle worker" as one who is entirely idle and has no other work, estimating how much he would accept to engage in this venture. The Rosh writes likewise. He further writes that here the Sages were lenient regarding rabbinic interest, and a small payment does not appear as interest. The Tur likewise writes this.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>This too is stated there.</b> He writes that we do not say that because, according to the claimant, he is wicked, he cannot be believed under oath. The same is written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 82, seif 10. In my humble opinion, this does not disagree with what was written above at the end of siman 169. There, he claims that he took the money permissibly and specifies the basis of permission, namely that he took it through a non-Jew, and he is believed by analogy to the law of a prozbul. As for Rama's words, "and some disagree," I did not merit to see his source in Darkei Moshe.
