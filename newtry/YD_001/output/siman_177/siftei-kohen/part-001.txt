**** ENGLISH ****
As an idle laborer etc. Not his full wages, rather we assess: a person who is idle and has no work at all — how much would he want to accept to engage in this business; even if he had a job with good pay that he must leave — we do not assess how much this person would want to accept to leave his work and engage in this business (which he would not do for a little) — rather we assess by an idle person, as Tur explained; and so is the view of some poskim. But Beit Yosef explains we assess how much a single laborer wants to take to leave his work entirely, and whether given light or heavy work — the amount is the same; and so is the view of some poskim; and see Derishab and Bach and Maadanei Melech who elaborated on this. And Rashi and some poskim explain: if he was originally doing heavy work and earning a lot, and this is light — we assess how much he would reduce from his wages to do this work. And students of Rashba and some poskim explain: we assess by his first work as he earns during idle time — such as he is a tailor and his practice is to sew a garment for a sela at the festival season when there is much work, and during other days he sews it for less than a sela since he cannot find that wage — and that is what he takes now as his wage; and see above siman 312 and in Derishab there who elaborated.

**** ENGLISH ****And it must be, etc. It implies that an iska too (to which inflation and deflation apply, and it stands in its original form) is included — and this is according to Rabbi Yitzchak in the Tur and the poskim; and all the more so when he gives him money for half the profit, for in this even those who are lenient agree, as found in Maharik shoresh 119 that Beit Yosef brings and D.M.; and in Shulchan Aruch it was copied on "and it must be," etc., Maharik shoresh 119; and on "there are those who are lenient," Beit Yosef according to Rabbi Yitzchak — it seems to me the copyist erred, for he understood the Rav comes to say he must accept all the liability of half the deposit and it does not suffice with liability for half of half the deposit — not like Beit Yosef who wrote according to Rabbi Yitzchak that it is permitted if liability for accidents and deflation is on both equally. And the matter is simple that Beit Yosef also agrees he must accept all liability of half the deposit; and what he wrote that liability for accidents should be on both equally means the entire business. And this is simple. And what the Rav wrote that he must accept all liability, etc., comes to exclude the opinion of Rivavan in the Tur and poskim who hold it suffices when the giver accepts liability for deflation even though liability for accidents is on the recipient — meaning "there are those who are lenient" who permit with partial liability, namely liability for deflation alone; and this is clear.

But the Rambam chapter 7 of Shelichin law 7 wrote: he buys whatever type he wishes and does not buy clothing or wood, and likewise anything similar — end of his words; it implies he holds the reason is that clothing and wood are not good merchandise; and accordingly, where it is good merchandise he may buy.
