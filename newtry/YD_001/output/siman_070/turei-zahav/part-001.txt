**** ENGLISH ****
And we are not concerned that the second salt absorbs, etc. — this is according to the view of the Rosh that the Tur wrote in his name, that salting's way is not to absorb but to emit; and according to the poskim who hold that salt also absorbs, as I wrote above siman 69 seif 2 in the name of Or Zarua regarding the reason for first rinsing, and likewise Shaarei Dura chapter 3 and Or Zarua end of chapter 6 — they forbid explicitly in second salting because the second salt will absorb the moisture on it into itself. It appears to me there is another reason here: since the meat is still busy emitting brine when the second salt comes upon it, therefore the second salt (does not) emit the brine and does not absorb what already emitted, because when the salt comes there is emission of brine. And if so, why is it forbidden below regarding blood in the eye that fell on meat at the time of salting — even though at that time it is busy emitting? There the prohibition is not because the salt absorbs it, but because the meat itself is boiling from the salting, like roasting upon which blood fell. Not so here, where the prohibition is only from the second salt — and in this there is no prohibition. But if it was salted without first rinsing there is prohibition from the salt, because when the salt came upon it there was no emission — therefore we say the salt absorbs it. And accordingly here too, if it already remained for brine emission — that is, after twenty-four hours — there is no permission except according to the Rosh; and it appears that after brine emission — that is, after twenty-four hours of its salting — one should forbid if it was salted again, for below siman 80 we hold that if it fell into brine after this measure it is forbidden because the brine will absorb in it; and here too it will absorb in it:

And likewise Rashal: there is a fortiori if the fish was not salted and the meat salted — the fish is forbidden. And Rama in Darkei Moshe and Tosafot Chullin disagrees and holds: since the fish is unsalted it absorbs only blood, and we say since it is still emitting its own blood it will also emit blood absorbed from elsewhere, as stated regarding meat never salted placed with meat already salted, end of his words. And Maharach wrote: one may explain Beit Yosef's words that the fish was already salted and afterward called unsalted; and it is written in Perishah on Rama — requires further study, for at first glance one may distinguish: here the fish are permitted upon them, so how are they permitted when they received forbidden blood; and in seif 6 he ruled to forbid because fish blood is only brine and one cannot say since it emits its blood, etc., end of his words. And in my humble opinion likewise according to those who forbid, for since brine in fish is a slight thing and to emit it in a short time — how can we rely on their brine emission to say that since it emits brine it will emit absorbed blood, lest the time of brine emission finish before absorbed blood emits, and there is a Torah doubt here; not so with meat and meat where there is much brine to emit for a long time — therefore there is much time to emit also blood absorbed from elsewhere. And according to the explanation of R' Yitzchak of Orleans in Tosafot Perek 25 that Beit Yosef brought at the beginning of siman 70, who explained regarding meat and meat that all the time one is open to emit before the other begins absorbing, its emission does not close afterward — one may also permit fish through their salting. But we do not hold that answer as explained there. And further there is reason to forbid, for the Rosh wrote in Perek 25 explaining Rashbam: regarding meat and meat they did not trouble them excessively, since blood is rabbinic, etc., therefore they were lenient there; this does not apply here, for it is not a rabbinic enactment to salt fish with meat — therefore one may not be lenient that one return and salt and emit, as appears to me. And further proof from what Teshuvot HaRash wrote: perhaps fish, since their skin is soft, absorb much and blood coming to them from elsewhere clings in them more and salt has no power to extract it, end of his words. And further proof from what Or Zarua general rule 5 wrote: unsalted fish placed with brine of meat are forbidden — no worse than if they were salted with them, end of his words. Behold explicitly as he wrote to forbid; and I am astonished at Rama that he did not notice this:

And if it touched within this time, etc. — it is not forbidden because it is still busy emitting its brine, it also emits what it absorbed from a piece not salted. And Rama wrote in Tosafot Chullin: this is specifically when the salted meat was not rinsed after salting; but if it was rinsed after salting we do not say one returns to salt and brine in it emits through brine in its body, for after it was salted once and rinsed the emission holes were sealed and will not open for brine in it, end of his words. And this reasoning appears thus in Ran's words in the name of R' Yona in Perek 25 regarding fish and poultry salted, etc., who equated meat that salted and left its blood and was rinsed to meat that salted and emitted all blood and brine — see there: it implies after blood left, rinsing is done and holes close regarding brine in it, like if it stood without rinsing after salting a full day when brine in it also finished. And see what is written siman 69 s.k. 15; and Tur wrote in the name of Rashba: there are those who permit also in this through salting, etc., end of his words. And in Teshuvot HaRash he wrote that view in the name of his teacher the Rav, and mentioned there even if rinsed after salting; and one must say that Rav is not Rabbenu Yona, for Ran wrote in the name of Rabbenu Yona that rinsed forbids; and this Rav holds rinsing does nothing because emission holes closed. Also the Tur appears to hold thus that rinsing does nothing, for he brought the Rav of Rashba's view and did not cite an authority that even if rinsed there is remedy through salting — rather certainly this rinsing neither elevates nor lowers. And according to what is written in seif 1 that as long as it emits brine it does not absorb, one need not return to salt here; rather there is another answer in Tosafot that it does absorb but the permission is that it return and re-emit, as I mentioned in this siman. And regarding if it has not yet remained for the time of salting and still has blood — this distinction does not apply, for even if rinsed, second salting helps; otherwise how do we find remedy for one who rinsed before it remained for the time of salting — and if so it would be forbidden; but certainly the poskim did not trouble us to teach this law except plainly that rinsing does not seal except brine holes but not blood; and everywhere Rama writes "return and salt again" because there is still brine in it and not blood — specifically without rinsing yet after the first salting. However it is astonishing what Rama wrote nearby afterward: if it remained for the time of salting and fell into brine within twelve hours one returns to rinse and salt — there the main permission is from its brine that it still has; and if one rinses the brine holes close — what will salting help afterward? And likewise difficult on what Or Zarua chapter 5 din 7 wrote. It appears Or Zarua and Rama here do not hold that rinsing closes emission holes, like the Rashba's teacher I mentioned in seif 6 and like the Tur appears there. And here Rama [plainly] is like that view the Tur brought that there are those who permit also through salting; and although Rama wrote there that even b'dieved the custom forbids — that is after all brine emission; not so here within twelve hours. And in Tosafot Chullin Rama wrote the opposite of here: there he wrote if rinsed no remedy helps if meat first stood for salting time — behold stringency; and if not rinsed not forbidden at all, for we say even though it absorbs from the forbidding thing, nevertheless as it absorbs so it releases through brine still in it. And here he reversed and wrote rinsing does not seal at all and specifically rinsing and second salting are required. In any case it must deal here when the meat in brine did not remain for the measure of pickling, for otherwise there is no remedy for it is like pickled, as siman 105 and Rashal extended in these laws Perek 25 siman 77; his conclusion: remedy of returning to salt helps only if it has not yet remained for the time of salting and still has blood — then we say through salt it emits its blood and also what it absorbed from brine that fell in; but if it remained for the time of salting and only brine remains, not second salting if it fell in brine. And that poskim mention "since it emits its brine" — meaning its blood; and likewise if meat not rinsed and salted fell in brine, second salting also does not help since there is blood in the eye on meat not rinsed that was absorbed in meat at salting and does not leave afterward. And see what I wrote at the end of the siman if not rinsed and not salted fell in brine; but if meat that remained for the time of salting fell among pieces resting in salting — there is permission from as it absorbs so it releases, for as it absorbs from salted pieces so it releases through also emitting its brine; and although for falling in brine this does not help because there is no as it absorbs so it releases through its brine — for in brine it is like cooked in it and therefore as it absorbs so it releases does not apply; nevertheless if meat did not remain for the time of salting and fell in brine and remained there for the measure of pickling, it still has remedy through returning to salt; and although we say pickled like cooked — that is if it fell after it remained for the time of salting and remained for the measure of pickling — all forbidden and a peel does not suffice; but not without remaining for the time of saluting, its remedy is through returning to salt since it did not remain in brine a full day. And that we say after the time of salting there is permission for meat mixed among salted pieces from as it absorbs so it releases — when it still has its brine; but if it already emitted all brine, such as after a full day after salting or rinsed immediately after salting time — emission holes for brine closed — then forbidden when falling among salted pieces and has no remedy, end of his words; and his words appear according to the poskim except what he permitted meat that did not remain for salting time and fell in brine and remained there for pickling measure to return and salt — poskim's implication is not so, for we hold siman 105 that pickling measure is like cooked for everything and second salting does not help, as Rama wrote; and nearby we explain further.
