**** ENGLISH ****
In what manner one must slaughter the veins. And in it are 2 seifim. In a bird one must slaughter the veins (meaning: threads from which blood emerges—and Yonatan in a foreign tongue) or puncture them at the time when it is convulsing, while the blood is still warm so that it will emerge and not congeal inside; and if he did not do so, he may not roast it whole; and if he roasted it whole, he shall discard the veins and cut around them the measure of removal, which is the thickness of a finger: {Rama: And if he salted it thus, afterward one removes the threads and it is permitted (and see s.k. 6); and some are stringent to peel around the threads (Agur in the name of Mahari Weil).} And if he roasted it whole, he scrapes and picks out the threads; and the rest, if there is in everything in the pot enough to nullify the blood in all the threads—60 parts—it is permitted: {Rama: And if he removed the head from it, it is not called whole (Mordechai, Hagahot Ashiri, Agur, and responsum of Maharil siman 58 there); and therefore they practiced removing it from whole birds; and see below siman 76 whether the veins were not slaughtered.}

**** ENGLISH ****
An animal—one need not puncture the veins at the time of shechitah, because it is not customary to roast it whole; but if he wants to roast it whole, he must puncture its veins at the time of shechitah; and if he did not puncture them, it is forbidden to roast it or cook it whole: {Rama: And if he roasted or cooked it whole, its law is like a bird (Beit Yosef according to several poskim); and an unspecified bird—its veins were slaughtered; and an unspecified animal—its veins were not slaughtered; and all this when blood emerged at the time of shechitah; but if blood did not emerge, we are not concerned for slaughter of the veins, for the blood was not aroused to emerge (Ran, chapter HaShochet, in the name of Rashba—and he is in Mishmeret HaBayit daf 20).}
