<b>שאני נהנה לך לך כו'. </b>בטור כתוב ולך בוא"ו וכתב בית יוסף דלאו דוקא הוא דקי"ל כר"ש דאמר עד שיאמר לשון נדר לכל אחד אבל הרמב"ם פסק כרבנן דבווי"ן תליא מילתא דהיינו לזה ולזה אבל לזה לזה הוה כל אחד נדר בפני עצמו ובסעיף ד' משמע דקי"ל כרמב"ם כיון שפסק שם במה שאמר בלא התרה הוה נדר אחר ואף על גב דלא אמר פעם אחר לשון נדר אכל דבר ועיין מ"ש שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That I will not benefit from you, from you, etc. </b>In the Tur it reads, “and from you,” with a vav. The Beit Yosef wrote that this is not precise, for we rule like R’ Shimon, who says that the vow is not divided unless he states an expression of a vow for each individual. The Rambam, however, rules like the Sages, who hold that the matter depends upon the conjunctions. Thus, “from this one and from this one” is one general vow, whereas “from this one, from this one,” without conjunctions, constitutes a separate vow for each. In seif 4 it appears that we rule like the Rambam, since it rules there that what he said, “without release,” constitutes another vow even though he did not repeat an expression of a vow regarding each matter. See what I write there.

**** ENGLISH ****

The latter opinion holds that even regret is effective for this purpose, since the sage uproots the vow retroactively.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And his fellow released part of it for him. </b>This means that the fellow says, “I regard myself as though I have received part of the payment,” which is equivalent to receiving partial payment. Nevertheless, the remainder is not released. Regarding an obligation owed to another person, we do not say that when part is released the entire obligation is released, because he already became obligated to pay the entire amount. The creditor does not forfeit the remainder merely because he waived part. See what I wrote in siman 228, seif 49, from which it is implied that one who swore to pay his fellow does not fulfill his oath until he pays everything. In siman 232, seif 8, it will be explained that we say, “When part is released, the entire vow is released,” only when the release changes the substance of his original statement. The same applies in every place where we use this principle. So wrote the Beit Yosef there in the name of the Ramban and the Ran. One should therefore not be lenient against them, especially since no authority explicitly disagrees. <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>The words of my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, who ruled leniently here, are astonishing. The Darkei Moshe also ruled as I have written.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Until he seeks release from it, etc. </b>The Tur writes at the end of siman 238: “An oath that I will not eat this loaf, an oath that I will not eat this loaf. The second oath does not take effect. If he seeks release from the first, the second takes effect,” end quote. Since it states that the second does not take effect, it implies that he cannot release the second before releasing the first, for the second has not yet taken effect. This resembles what is written in siman 228, seif 17, that a vow may not be released until it takes effect. So too it is implied by the Rosh in the second chapter of Nedarim, 18a: “Thus, so long as he has not sought release from the first, the second does not exist,” end quote. Since it does not exist, how can it be released? The Rambam’s words at the end of chapter 6 of Hilchos Shevuos, however, do not imply this. He writes: “An oath that I will not eat today, an oath that I will not eat today, an oath that I will not eat today. If he seeks release from the first, he is liable because of the second. If he then seeks release from the second, he is liable because of the third. If he seeks release only from the third, he remains liable because of the first and second. If he seeks release from the second, he remains liable because of the first. Why, then, did they say that an oath does not take effect upon an oath? It means that if he did not seek release and ate, he is liable only once,” end quote. This implies that he can release the second or third even before releasing the first. We must therefore say that this differs from siman 228, seif 17. There, the oath does not yet exist at all. Here, however, the second and third oaths exist, but have not yet found a place to take effect. Mahari L. Chaviv proposed this explanation of the Rambam in responsum 139. Based on it, he proposed an additional leniency. Just as siman 228, seif 46, states that several vows of one person may be released through a single release, so too one who swore regarding the same matter many times should require only one release for all of them. This is unlike the Shulchan Aruch there, which requires a separate release for each oath because the second does not take effect before release of the first. In my humble opinion, the Shulchan Aruch ruled correctly there. First, according to the Rosh and Tur cited above, the second oath does not take effect at all. They therefore certainly disagree with the Rambam and hold that it cannot be released before the first is released. This is also evident from the fact that they did not distinguish between an oath that has not taken effect in this case and the case in siman 228. They should have taught such a distinction, for it would be a major novelty. Evidently, they do not accept the Rambam’s view. Furthermore, even according to the Rambam, who permits release of the second before the first, it does not appear that he accepts Mahari L. Chaviv’s further leniency of releasing them all at one time. There is a sound reason for this distinction. Releasing the second before the first contains nothing that contradicts the release, for only one oath is being released. When, however, he releases the first together with the second, the moment the sage says, “It is permitted,” regarding the first, he thereby gives force to the second so that it now takes effect. How can the sage’s statement continue and release the second as well, when the two effects contradict one another? When this one falls, the other rises. One release cannot therefore apply to both. Reason itself requires this conclusion. Anyone who wishes to dispute it must bring clear proof, and the Rambam’s words provide no proof. Even the first leniency, to release the second before the first, should not be relied upon in a Torah matter, since the Rosh and Tur imply otherwise, as the Shulchan Aruch ruled in siman 228. There is further proof from the Rosh’s responsum at the end of klal 12. One who stated at the time of his vow that no sage would be able to release that vow has thereby made another vow. It is like a vow within a vow. They must first release the vow that no one may release it, etc. Thus, the Rosh compares one who vowed that he would not seek release, the case mentioned here in seif 9, to the law in siman 228, seif 46, of a vow within a vow, meaning that he vowed twice regarding one matter, as discussed in the second chapter of Nedarim, 17a. The Rosh writes that they must first release the vow presently in force. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote on the basis of Mahari L. Chaviv’s words that the Beit Yosef had erred. The matter is clear as I have written. Mahari L. Chaviv himself wrote in that responsum that he had seen another authority rule against him, though he disagreed.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Regarding what he said, “without release.” </b>This statement is considered a separate oath. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba and the Rosh. This is difficult. In seif 1, according to the first opinion, the Shulchan Aruch ruled that there is no separate vow until he repeats the word “konam.” That follows R’ Shimon in the Gemara, as mentioned there. If so, here too one release should suffice, since the phrase “without release” is part of the original oath. The Beit Yosef likewise wrote at the end of siman 228, page 273a, in the name of a responsum of the Ran, that requiring a separate release when one swore regarding one matter and said that he would not seek release applies according to R’ Yehuda. According to R’ Shimon, he must say a second time, “I swear that I will not seek release,” etc. We must say that the first opinion in seif 1 follows R’ Shimon only regarding the rule that when part of a vow is released, the entire vow is released. So long as he did not repeat the word “oath,” it remains one general statement. Here, however, when he says, “without release,” it is as though he swore twice regarding the same matter, for he doubled the language concerning one matter. Two releases are therefore required. So it appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They first release the later cherem, etc. </b>It appears that the later cherem itself requires two releases. First, they release what he said, “and no release shall be made for the release.” Afterward, they release what he said, that they should not make a release. Then they release the original cherem itself. Proof may be brought from what the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran in siman 228, which the Shulchan Aruch cites here in seif 5, concerning one who stated at the time of his vow that if the vow were released, a prohibition against eating fruit would take effect upon him, and the same would recur every time and forever. The Ran wrote that every number of repetitions he mentioned constitutes a separate oath. He wrote: “We released him from the prohibitions many times, according to our estimate of how many times he had said, ‘and so each time,’ etc. From then onward there was no concern even if he later admitted that he had said it one thousand times, since at the time he sought release he did not think that the fruit prohibition would continue as a result,” end quote. It is further clear that although he said, “and so forever,” which has no limit, it is nevertheless counted as only one additional repetition.

<b>אין תנאו כלום. </b>פי' דהוה אמינא כיון דהתנה תנאי זה לא יועיל לו שום שאלה זה לא אמרינן דמוכח בגמרא שאין לך שום נדר בעולם שלא יועיל לו שאלה כן כתב בית יוסף בשם ריב"ש ומלשון דאם כן מתירין תחילה שכתב כאן בסיפא משמע דכל שלא אמר שלא ישאל עליו אלא סתם שלא יהיה ניתר לעולם אין צריך רק היתר א' דהיינו מה שמתיר עיקר השבועה ואין צריך היתר על מ"ש שלא יהא ניתר לעולם <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="3"></i>ותמיה לי מה בין זה לאומר בלא התרה דסעיף ד' והנלע"ד דריב"ש בעל סעיף זה ס"ל גם באמירה בלא התרה א"צ רק היתר אחד מטעם שלא הזכיר נדר בפעם אחרת וכר"ש שזכרתי בסעיף ד' על פי הר"ן או מטעם דכל שאינו זוכר שום דבר על עצמו שהוא לא יהיה הנשאל אין זה כמו שבועה אחרת או אפשר דכשחומר בלא התרה הוה כאלו הזכיר את עצמו שהוא לא יהיה המתיר מה שאין כן באמירה שלא יהיה ניתר לעולם ולענין מעשה נ"ל דאף כאן יעשה ב' היתרים אחד על מה שאמר שלא יהיה ניתר לעולם וא' על עיקר השבועה דאין הפרש בין זה לבלא התרה דסעיף ד' כן נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>His condition is ineffective. </b>Meaning, one might have thought that since he stipulated this condition, no request for release could ever help him. We do not say so, for it is proven in the Gemara that there is no vow in the world that cannot be released through a request. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rivash. From the wording in the latter clause, “for otherwise they first release,” it appears that when he did not say that he would not seek release, but merely said that the vow should never be released, he requires only one release, namely, release of the underlying oath. He does not require a separate release for having said that it should never be released. <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="3"></i>I am puzzled by the distinction between this case and the phrase, “without release,” in seif 4. It appears to me that the Rivash, whose opinion is cited in this seif, also holds that saying, “without release,” requires only one release, either because he did not repeat an expression of a vow, following R’ Shimon as I explained in seif 4 from the Ran, or because whenever he does not place a separate obligation upon himself not to seek release, it is not considered a second oath. Alternatively, the phrase “without release” may be considered as though he mentioned himself and stated that he would not be the one to seek release, unlike the phrase that it should never be released. As practical halacha, however, it appears that even here two releases should be performed, one for what he said, that it should never be released, and one for the underlying oath. There is no difference between this and saying, “without release,” in seif 4. So it appears to me.
