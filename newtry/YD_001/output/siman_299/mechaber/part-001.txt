**** ENGLISH ****
In which species kilayim applies. In it are 2 seifim. Wool of lambs and wool of camels and the like—if one mixed them together and spun threads from them: if half was from lambs, the whole is considered wool and is shaatnez with linen. And if the majority was from camels, it is permitted to mix it with linen, because the appearance of the whole is the appearance of camel wool, and we are not concerned for the strands of sheep wool mixed in them, because they are not wool threads. Therefore hides of sheep from which garments are made—it is permitted to sew them with linen threads, and we are not concerned for strands of wool even though they are wrapped in the linen thread with which it is sewn, for they are annulled in their minority. And likewise hemp and linen that were mixed—if the majority is hemp, it is permitted to weave the threads from them with wool threads; and if they were half and half, it is forbidden. Therefore, one whose wool became mixed with linen brings another species and mixes it with them and nullifies one of them; and nullification does not apply except before threads are made. But after threads are made, kilayim has no measure—even one thread of wool in a large linen garment, or of linen in wool, is forbidden. {Rama: And even if the thread was lost and we do not know its place, nullification does not apply and it is forbidden (Rashba siman 259, and so Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot and the Rosh and Semag). A garment whose warp is silk and woof is linen—it is forbidden to sew it with wool even though the majority is silk (Tur); therefore it is forbidden to make tallitot from garments called fustian, because the woof is linen and it is shaatnez with the tzitzit (own reasoning).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who makes a garment entirely of camel wool or hares or hemp, and wove in it a thread of wool on this side and a thread of linen on that side—this is forbidden because of shaatnez. {Rama: And some say it is permitted in such a case unless he wove a thread of wool next to the thread of linen; but if they do not touch one another, it is permitted (Beit Yosef in the name of the Rosh and R' Shimshon). And see below siman 300, for they are accustomed to be lenient.}
