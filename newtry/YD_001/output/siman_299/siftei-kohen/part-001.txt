**** ENGLISH ****Nullification does not apply to it, etc. And we do not say it is nullified in majority since its place is not recognizable — for nullification in majority applies only to issur mixed in heter; but heter mixed in heter, and through their mixture it became forbidden — nullification does not apply, for here there is only one piece of issur (meaning now everything became as one piece of issur) — Rosh and the poskim, and Beit Yosef brings them; and Taz wrote: some say for meat in milk we hold it is nullified, for there the taste of the meat or milk spreads and mixes throughout and there is no special place for the issur, and it is as if lost from the world; but here where the issur has existence in its place and did not spread — only we do not recognize it — nullification does not apply — so it appears to me — end quote. And it is difficult on his words — for if so, he should have answered briefly that for this reason it is not nullified here, since the issur has existence in its place; but certainly because we hold generally that even when issur has existence in its place, such as dry mixed in dry — it is nullified; and if so, the question returns to its place, for meat in milk is nullified, and even dry in dry; except the reason is clear and explained in the words of Rosh and poskim — that it is not comparable to meat in milk, for there the issur is only because of giving taste; and therefore when taste is nullified the issur is nullified. And Taz omitted these words of Rosh and poskim; and therefore in dry too there is no issur of kilayim, for it is said to be a Scriptural decree to forbid without taste — rather the taste of the issur is that one may eat the issur; and therefore whatever is nullified in majority is permitted; and likewise in meat in milk too — understand well.
