<b>דיני בשולי עובדי כוכבים. ובו ט"ז סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="1"></i> דבר שאינו נאכל כמו <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="1"></i>שהוא חי <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="1"></i>וגם <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="2"></i><i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="1"></i>עולה על שלחן מלכים <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="2"></i>ללפת בו את הפת <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="3"></i>או לפרפרת שבישלו עובד כוכבים <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="4"></i>אפילו בכלי ישראל <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="5"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="2"></i>ובבית ישראל אסור משום בישולי עובד כוכבים:
**** ENGLISH ****
Laws of bishul akum. It contains 16 seifim: A food that is not eaten in its raw state, and also is fit to be served on a king's table to dip bread in it or as relish — if a non-Jew cooked it, even in a Jewish vessel and in a Jewish home, it is forbidden because of bishul akum.

**** ENGLISH ****
If one mixed a food eaten in its raw state with a food not eaten in its raw state and a non-Jew cooked them — if the main component is from something that has [the prohibition of] bishul akum, it is forbidden; if not, it is permitted. {Rama: And it is permitted to eat roasted peas of a non-Jew, and likewise legumes called arabshin roasted, for they are not served on a king's table; and so they practiced leniency with them, except in a place where they practice greasing the pan with milk — then they practice prohibition with them (Rambam chapter 17 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, Mahara of Prague, and glosses of the Arukh). But without this it is permitted, and one need not be concerned for the intestines of non-Jews, for usually they are not same-day [slaughter]. And any fruit eaten raw — even if a non-Jew cooked it and it dissolved and became a dish in their hands — is permitted; and therefore they eat compote that non-Jews make (private opinion from the implication of Rambam, Ran, and Tosafot, and it is straightforward).}

**** ENGLISH ****
