[וע' בת' חתם סופר סי' ס"ד וס"ס ש"ח מבואר שאין דעתו כן אלא דגם בשאר איסוריה לא נקרא מסל"ת רק באופן המועיל בעדות אשה ע"ש עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or if a gentile speaks incidentally.</b> See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 37. He writes that the same applies when one incidental gentile reports what another incidental gentile said. But if the statement is not genuinely incidental, it does not help. He rejects a sage who wished to be lenient by combining a less-than-fully-incidental statement with the fact that the cow was producing milk. That sage relied on Terumat HaDeshen, siman 271, where there was uncertainty whether the gentile spoke to enhance his sale or innocently, and Terumat HaDeshen combined it with the milking factor. Chavot Yair says one should not extend this. The milking factor may be joined only to a genuinely incidental statement that was certainly not intended to improve a sale, as Rama's plain wording indicates. Regarding whether the gentile himself must open the conversation first, as required for testimony concerning a woman's husband in Even HaEzer siman 17, he concludes that for other prohibitions, even Torah prohibitions, this additional condition is not required. He brings proof from reliance on a gentile's tasting. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 64 and the end of siman 308, disagrees and holds that even in other prohibitions a statement is considered genuinely incidental only in the same form that would qualify in testimony regarding a woman's husband.
