(סימן ק"ב בש"ע סעיף א' בהגה"ה) <b>מיהו אם לבנו בה מאכל או נתנו בקדרה וכו'</b>. המ"א בא"ח סי' תקי"ג סק"ז כתב וז"ל משמע דאם אינו ניכר במראה אלא בטעם בטיל אע"פ שבא לתקן הקדר' כגון מים ומלח בעיס' כו' וכ"מ בי"ד סי' ק"ב בש"ע דדוקא שמלאו בתרנגול' דניכר במראה דאז אינו בטל ע"כ וצ"ע דכמו דאיתא בש"ע לפנינו אדרבה משמע היפוך דבריו ואפשר שגירסא אחרת היה לפניו בש"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 102 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1 in the hagahah) However, if one whitened it in food or placed it in a pot, etc. Magen Avraham in Orach Chayim siman 513 s.k. 7 wrote: it appears that if it is not recognizable in appearance but only in taste it is nullified, even though it came to fix the pot, like water and salt in dough, etc.; and so is in Yoreh Deah siman 102 in Shulchan Aruch — specifically when one filled it with chicken, which is recognizable in appearance — then it is not nullified — end of his words; and it requires investigation, for as Shulchan Aruch states before us, on the contrary his words appear reversed; and it is possible another version of Shulchan Aruch was before him.

**** ENGLISH ****
(There seif 2) There are those who say that they only said something has a permitted aspect when the permitting matter will certainly come, etc. — therefore an egg of doubtful tereifah. Shach s.k. 4 wrote: I do not know why he wrote "there are those who say," etc. — and similarly is proved in the Gemara at the beginning of Beitzah which challenges, etc. — see there; and one who examines Rashi's words there will see this is not a challenge at all, for Rashi explained doubtful tereifah egg does not mean the doubt comes from the chicken having a tereifah doubt, but that there is an egg here and it is unknown whether from a tereifah or kosher one — and tereifah doubt as such is not something that has a permitted aspect [see Peri Megadim in Shaar HaTzedek s.k. 7 what he wrote explaining Shach's words]; and one can say according to what Rambam wrote that chametz on Pesach is something that has a permitted aspect — even though next year it will return to prohibition, nevertheless it is called something that has a permitted aspect since there is permission for its prohibition; and it is called something that has a permitted aspect — "until you eat it in prohibition eat it in permission." And behold we do not hold that a certainly tereifah egg is muktzeh because it is fit for a dog; nevertheless a doubtful tereifah egg appears to be muktzeh, since for him it is not fit because of prohibition, and for a dog too it is not fit, since what is fit for him he will not give to a dog — for if he keeps the chicken twenty-one days it is possible she will lay and become fit for him; therefore the Gemara's challenge is difficult; unless tereifah doubt is something that does not have a permitted aspect. The baraita that cites "and if mixed" would not deal only with doubt, but the baraita deals with all cases whether certain or doubtful; and even though it appears to R' Yehudah as a stretch that "if mixed" refers to certainty — namely certainty alone — but both certain and doubtful together this is not a stretch; if so there is no Gemara challenge — if it deals with doubtful tereifah, "if mixed" would also refer to certain tereifah; if so why is certain tereifah not nullified, for it is something that does not have a permitted aspect? Therefore the Gemara's challenge is from certainty; and one can say: behold Tzemach Tzedek challenges how can a tereifah egg be something that has a permitted aspect? Either way: if they return and lay, there is no prohibition at all; if they do not return and lay, there is no permitted aspect and it should nullify like other forbidden items. Granted regarding muktzeh it works well — even though tomorrow it will be permitted, nevertheless now it is called forbidden; unlike tereifah doubt — if it will be permitted also now there is no prohibition here at all; and it appears certain tereifah doubt in general is not called something that has a permitted aspect at all, like Tzemach Tzedek's challenge; and why Rashba and Tur needed to innovate that a doubtful tereifah egg is not something that has a permitted aspect because the permitting matter will not certainly come — one can say it deals with a doubtful tereifah egg mixed on Yom Tov or Shabbat, forbidden to move on account of muktzeh; and as stated, he holds something that will return to prohibition after time, even though it has permission in the interim, is not something that has a permitted aspect; if so, certainly on the first and second Shabbat within twenty-one days it is not something that has a permitted aspect, since for the coming Shabbat it will return to prohibition; but on the last Shabbat within twenty-one days one might say it is something that has a permitted aspect — for if they return and lay it will be permitted for the coming Shabbat — Rashba teaches: since the permitting matter will not certainly come — for perhaps they will not return and lay and the egg will remain in doubt until after twelve months and afterward too forbidden to move — therefore it is not something that has a permitted aspect at all; if so, according to this, Rashba's challenge is resolved — the baraita deals with doubtful tereifah in general; even if it deals with doubtful tereifah mixed on Shabbat or Yom Tov — for the baraita deals with every Shabbat — if so on the first and second Shabbat within twenty-one days why is it not nullified, for it is not something that has a permitted aspect at all? And the Gemara challenges well: behold it is something that does not have a permitted aspect — investigate well.
