להיתר בנקפל ממקומו בשלחן ערוך ובודאי נתחרט ממ"ש בזה בכ"מ ובב"י כנלע"ד ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
If the junction of sinews was removed, etc. This law is explained in Ra'avad's hasagot on chapter 8 of shechitah on what Rambam wrote; and we already explained that an animal whose legs were cut and likewise a bird in the place of junction of sinews, etc.; therefore if the sinews alone were cut, etc., Ra'avad wrote on this: this craft did not succeed for him properly here, to consider the sinews as removed; what difference between cut and severed; and he holds that "junction of sinews was removed" means they were cut; therefore he counts them among removals and did not analyze well — rather if he wished to count it among removals he should say that if they were not cut but uprooted from the knee and folded from the bone upward — treifah; therefore he said junction of sinews was removed and did not say they were cut — understand this, end of his words. And Kesef Mishneh wrote on this: meaning he challenges why he should have included severed junction of sinews in the laws of severed, and says the Rav holds that when we taught "junction of sinews was removed" we mean cut, for from the language "removed" it implies they forbade from the reason of removal; and he challenged that he did not analyze well — for if he wished to count it among removals he should say that removal means not cut but uprooted from the knee and folded from the bone upward — treifah; and it appears to him that even if uprooted from the knee and folded from the bone upward, if they still draw from the body — kosher for all, and he said treifah only according to the Rav who holds removed means cut; better he should explain that removed means uprooted from the knee, etc., end of his words. And in my humble opinion the Rav did not fully discharge his duty of investigation here, for according to his words how could Ra'avad himself explain the mishnah that said junction of sinews was removed; and further, what is this that Ra'avad wrote "therefore he said removed" — implying he comes to prove from the mishnah and not Rambam's words as Kesef Mishneh understood; and further, according to his explanation the main challenge of Ra'avad is only on the arrangement of Rambam's words — what practical difference in this. And it appears to me the explanation of the hasagah is thus: Ra'avad found it difficult that the mishnah used language of removal for junction of sinews, and in the Gemara they used language of severed, for they said: if the thick one was severed, etc.; rather the removal here has two laws — law of removal of halacha leMoshe miSinai and law of severed of halacha leMoshe miSinai; removal is when entirely removed or removed from its place of attachment — treifah, like liver removed from its hanging place; and there is also stringency of severed if part was cut, like severed spinal cord — treifah; and this is proven from the mishnah's wording itself: animal whose legs were cut, etc., and likewise junction of sinews removed — behold the wording "and likewise" implies it deals with cut as in the beginning; if so why conclude with removed — it should say and if junction of sinews were cut; rather to reveal that the law of removal applies also to junction of sinews; and if because entirely removed — obvious, no novelty; rather because removed from its place the mishnah needed to teach; and therefore the Gemara said the law of severed applies also to junction of sinews — this is Ra'avad's approach; and accordingly we explain his hasagot: he saw Rambam placed junction of sinews among laws of removed liver and upper jaw and used language of cut in all his laws; and on this he first challenged: why not write junction of sinews in laws of severed since his explanation of removal of junction of sinews is actual cut — if so it is actual severed; and he wrote on this it challenges only Rambam and not the Talmud — for since Rambam holds the explanation of removal in junction of sinews is actual cut, if so it has only the law of cut which is severed, and therefore he included it among other removals for this law of cutting; but he did not analyze well — for if he wished to write it among removals in the mishnah's language he should explain another law on which it is called removal, namely uprooted from its place alone — treifah like other removals; therefore the tanna preserved "junction of sinews was removed" and did not say cut as in the beginning, as we said above; and Rambam should have done so too. The general conclusion: for Ra'avad himself it is obvious treifah when folded from its place even if not uprooted on the bone itself; only he challenged Rambam that since he wished to arrange and write like the mishnah he should explain the mishnah's intent as we said; therefore he wrote if he wished to count it among removals, etc.; and it appears clear that Rambam too holds thus for the law that folded from its place is also treifah, for he gave it the law of removed liver and upper jaw, and it is clear there that when removed from their places — treifah, and in one blow he struck them; only he abbreviated where he should have expanded; and Beit Yosef did well not to decide his view to permit when folded from its place in Shulchan Aruch, and certainly he regretted what he wrote in Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef in this — so it appears clearly to me.

If the majority of each was cut, etc. To clarify this law we explain Rosh's words, which many find difficult; I will explain as appears to me with God's help: Rosh wrote — Rif brought only the latter version of Rav, and even though by Torah law one should be stringent like the first version, it is impossible that we rule like Mar bar Rav Ashi who permits by majority of building or majority of counting; and so one must explain the latter version of Mar bar Rav Ashi: what is "its majority" — majority of each strand among those that render treifah; if the thick one was severed and the thin ones remain and the majority of them was severed — treifah; and likewise if the thin one was severed and the majority of the thick remains — treifah; and unlike Shmuel who said even if only a strand of sarbala remains it is kosher; but one should not explain Rav requires majority of each, for then it would not be like Mar bar Rav Ashi unless we say majority of each is weaker than majority of building or counting — and this is not reasonable at all, for plainly for Mar bar Rav Ashi if the majority of the thick remains or majority of the two thin ones — kosher, for its majority is like all of it, and all the more majority of each; end of Rosh. And explanation is needed in what he wrote "but one should not explain Rav requires majority of each" — this means the majority of each was severed as he first said when majority of one was severed; if so there is no explanation for what he said afterward "and all the more majority of each." And I saw many explain that when Rosh wrote Rav requires majority of each, he means he does not permit unless majority of each remains — and this plainly is not correct, for in the Gemara when he said it before Shmuel, Shmuel said: since there were three, behold there is a third of each — implying it refers to what Rav said, majority of each. And one may challenge: if so there is no difference between the latter and first versions, for in the first too we require three majorities; and as appears clearly to me, Rosh's words are as their plain meaning: he must explain majority of each on those that render treifah, not mentioning at all that which is not treifah, such as thick and thin — we say the thick one was entirely severed and there is no concern since the thin ones remain; Rav comes to explain if the majority of those two thin ones that treifah depends on was severed — treifah; but he did not intend in this statement of the thick one to say that from it too there is majority, rather that thick one was entirely severed and he does not speak of it; and on this he said afterward one should not explain as plainly that he deals also from the thick one and on all three said if their majority was severed — treifah; this is certainly not kosher since three thirds remain, namely from the large one a third and from the two thin ones two thirds — this is actually kosher, and we learn from Mar bar Rav Ashi who permits by majority of building or counting; and likewise there is this permission if not that you say specifically when both thin ones entirely remain, then what was severed from the thick does not harm, unlike here where the minority of the two thin ones remains — and this is not reasonable, for certainly there is permission when two thin ones remain even if their minority was severed and their majority remains; if so you have four thirds that remain — automatically you may permit here too, for three thirds remain and the third that remains from the large one is considered like two thirds from the thin ones, for in all views the one thick counts as two thin; if so you also have four thirds; and there is greater virtue here since in any case there is remnant from all three strands, unlike when the thick was entirely severed and majority of two thin ones remains; therefore he wrote all the more in severed majority of each and three thirds remain as we wrote — only according to the first explanation it refers to treifah; and one may say why did he not rule like the first version that majority of one refers also to treifah — meaning if the thick was severed and majority of one of the thin ones — treifah automatically follows Mar bar Rav Ashi; this is not certain, for in any case the majority of two thin ones remains, namely a third from one and one whole since you have four thirds as explained — Rosh's view; and accordingly Tur's words: and it is impossible — Rosh wrote even if majority of each did not remain, etc., meaning only minority remained — kosher, forbidden only if the entire large one was severed and majority of each of the small ones; it implies but if only minority of the large and majority of the small — kosher since four thirds remain; and what he said afterward but if the large was severed, etc., did not count the case where all three majorities were severed and three minorities remain — this is all the more as we said; and so I saw Maharshal chapter HaBehemah HaMekasheh rule like Rosh and Tur to permit when three minorities remain; and certainly he intended what we said. Only I also saw there he wrote one should doubt and say even without majority of each severed, majority of the thick and majority of one thin and one thin whole — perhaps treifah, for we say the majority of those severed is like all; and in severed two and one of them the thick, none permit — therefore be stringent also in this, end of his words; and he did not reach our proof that the first version too is placed on treifah; rather certainly on severed entire thick and majority of one thin and one whole — kosher since four thirds remain; and all we said above that the thick counts as two applies if the two thin were severed and the thick remains. With this Bach's challenge is resolved: from Rosh's words it implies he explains Shmuel's "even if only a strand of sarbala remains" even if entire junction was severed and one strand remains — difficult; and it appears to me to challenge further: since for Rosh the latter version refers to treifah, automatically the thick was entirely severed — how does Shmuel say since there were three; it appears everything is fine — Rav's students also refer to treifah requiring remnant from both or from the thick alone like sarbala, and sarbala means at least a third, not something minimal as Bach wanted; therefore he said properly: since initially there were three and one was entirely severed and majority remained in the two, two thirds from the small ones suffice; and this supports Rav's students who also said remnant from each like sarbala strand which is third measure — everything is reconciled with clear mind.

The width of a finger.
