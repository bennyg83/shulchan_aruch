One who relies on the many early and late authorities who are lenient, as the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch conclude, does not lose — a wolf has no derisah at all in a large animal.

<b>בצפרנו לבית החלל. </b>בזה חמיר מכלב דאין לו דריסה כלל אבל בנץ יש לו דריסה אלא שאינו שורף אלא דוקא כשהוא מטיל ארס בפנים וכתב ב"י בשם חדושי רשב"א ונ"מ דאילו בקוץ אע"ג דניקב לחלל אית ליה בדיקה אבל בנקיבת נץ כיון שניקב לחלל והאדים הבשר אין לו בדיקה ומיהו למ"ד דקוץ נמי לית ליה בדיקה אפשר לחלק בהפך דאילו בקוץ לית ליה בדיקה כלל דנקב כל שהוא אינו ניכר אבל בנץ בודקין אותו אם ניקב לחללו והאדים הבשר מבפנים טרפה ואם לאו כשרה עכ"ל וכתב מו"ח ז"ל וצריך לפרש לפ"ז דס"ל דצפורן נץ אינו דק וחד כמו קוץ או מחט דאל"כ אמאי כשרה בלא האדים הבשר מבפנים תיפוק ליה דטרפה משום קוץ שניקב לחללו דאינו ניכר בנקב כל שהוא עכ"ל ולעד"נ דא"כ לא היה הרשב"א צריך לעשות שום נפקא מינה כיון דאין לצפורן נץ דמיון לקוץ אלא נ"ל דה"ק הרשב"א דכל שלא האדים הרי הוכחה לפנינו שלא נגע באברים דאי נגע ודאי היה אודם באותו מקום דכל שהוא נוגע הוא מטיל ארס ומאדים וא"כ אין לנו חשש שמא עשה נקב בקוץ נמצא שבכל מקום ששייך דרוסה בנץ שם הוא קיל לענין חשש נקב טפי מקוץ לפי מ"ד דקוץ אין לו בדיקה לנקב:
**** ENGLISH ****
Into the cavity with its claw. In this it is stricter than a dog that has no derisah at all; but a hawk has derisah only that it does not burn except when injecting venom inside. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba's novellae: there is a difference — for a thorn, even if it perforated into the cavity, it has examination; but for a hawk's perforation, once it perforated into the cavity and the flesh reddened, it has no examination. However, for the view that a thorn also has no examination, one may distinguish the opposite: a thorn has no examination at all, for a minute hole is not recognizable, but for a hawk one examines if it perforated into its cavity and reddened inside — treifah, and if not, kosher, end of his words. Maharshal wrote one must explain that therefore he holds a hawk's claw is not thin and sharp like a thorn or needle — otherwise why is it kosher without reddening of flesh inside; it could be treifah from a thorn that perforated into the cavity, not recognizable with a minute hole, end of his words. In my humble opinion, if so the Rashba need not make any practical difference, since a hawk's claw is not comparable to a thorn; rather the Rashba means: wherever it did not redden, we have proof it did not touch the organs, for if it touched it would certainly redden in that place, for whatever it touches it injects venom and reddens; therefore we have no concern perhaps it made a thorn-hole — wherever derisah of a hawk applies, it is more lenient regarding concern of a minute hole than a thorn, for the view that a thorn has no examination for a hole.

**** ENGLISH ****
That nowadays they practice, etc. This deals when it is silent and they cry out — otherwise even for those with derisah there is no concern, as in seif 9 and the end of this seif: "and all that did not strike it — they are not concerned," which implies if it struck they are concerned, even when both are silent, as in seif 11: where we see it leaped in the manner of predators — forbidden even when both are silent, even if domesticated; likewise here when it struck before us in the manner of predators. Tur and Rashba wrote in this language: "and all this when we did not see it pursue and strike," end of his words — implying concern only with two grounds for concern: we saw it pursue and also struck; but if we saw one of them, they are not concerned. In the Shulchan Aruch here, since it only wrote "did not strike," it implies from the Ran in the Beit Yosef that for a strike alone they are concerned. If the Shulchan Aruch means Rashba and Tur require that we did not see neither pursuit nor strike, but for one of them they are concerned — the Shulchan Aruch should have written both and informed that if we saw pursuit alone or strike alone they are concerned; also it is strained to say Rashba and Ran disagree. It appears all striking is somewhat through pursuit, for one who strikes another wants first to save himself as much as he can, and the struck one pursues somewhat — it is obvious that what is not the manner of striking but standing together and placing a hand is mere play. Maharshal also wrote it is not considered striking unless from anger, such as pursuing; but he wrote when both are silent that daily cats strike chickens and we are not concerned and attribute it to play, and concludes nevertheless one should be concerned whenever it struck, even without pursuit, and likewise entering a chicken coop while silent and they cry out without knowing if it struck them, etc., end of his words. In my humble opinion there is no concern without our seeing it struck, even when it is silent and they cry out — what does it matter that there is fear on the chickens, since our cats are domesticated; Rashba and Ran explicitly permitted this by law, and one should not be stringent except when we saw striking through pursuit; with this it is forbidden even when both are silent, like seif 11; and even then there is permission to sell to a gentile, for there are two sefeikos; one who is stringent to kill first and then sell to a gentile — well done, as explained.

And so appears primary, for Terumat HaDeshen HaAruch also mentioned where they graze in open space, not as in Sefer Masat Binyamin to be lenient in this.

And why not say also here: since it is in a wide place it certainly fled and the claw came from the wall, since this is learned from safek derusah — how can derusah law be stricter than itself — how can a geir stand on the ground and the convert in the heavens? In truth it appears clear that also the Rashba intended this, and his novelty is that the law of a claw on the back has the stringency of "it is silent and they cry" — i.e., safek derusah — and we do not attribute to the wall; for leniency of Rav from safek derusah reason does not help us, and its law is exactly like safek derusah. And so is explicit in Terumat HaDeshen HaAruch daf 64: he first wrote safek derusah with difference between narrow and wide, then this law of claw on back — since we concern for safek derusah we concern for this too; it is simple they are tied, and the claw law is not stricter. The Beit Yosef wrote proof for his words from the Rashba writing claw law after writing "it is silent and they cry" — I do not recognize any hint of proof; explicitly the Gemara says claw law was safek derusah. If so we have no reason to say it is stricter than other safek derusah; Rif and Rosh mentioned claw law due to safek derusah and gave no extra stringency. The Beit Yosef's words here are very puzzling. The essence: in a wide place, even the one with the claw on its back is permitted — the claw came from elsewhere; whatever we do not concern for due to safek derusah has Rav's law that we do not concern for that claw. And what is written: if three derusah are found, be stringent and forbid all even in a wide place, for the flock was established as derusah — it appears to me this is a gross error of that author who thought this like a dish where three worms were found forbidden as siman 84 seif 9; this is not comparable, for there we say the nature of this vegetable is to grow worms, not so here where each animal has a good presumption alone — what harm is one's prohibition to another?
