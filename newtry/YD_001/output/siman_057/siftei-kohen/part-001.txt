<b>בבהמה גסה. </b>הב"ח כתב כיון דרש"י וסה"ת וסמ"ק וסמ"ג והגהמי"י וחכמי הצרפתים אוסרים בבהמה גסה אין לנו להקל בזה עכ"ל וכ"נ דעת ראב"ן סי' רנ"ב וכ"כ האו"ה ריש כלל נ"ז הלכך נראה דהיכא דאפשר למכרו לעובד כוכבים בלא הפסד (ודאי דמותר למכרו לעובד כוכבים וכמ"ש הב"ח) יש למכרו לעובד כוכבים ואסור לשחטו ועיין בס"ק נ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
In a large animal. The Bach wrote that since Rashi, Semak, Semag, Hagahot Maimoniot, and the French sages forbid in a large animal, we have no basis to be lenient in this; end of his words. And likewise is the view of Raavan siman 252, and likewise Orach Chaim at the beginning of general rule 57; therefore it appears that where it is possible to sell it to a gentile without loss (and certainly it is permitted to sell it to a gentile, as the Bach wrote), one should sell it to a gentile and it is forbidden to slaughter it; and see s.k. 51.

<b>כגון בעגלים. </b>ומהרש"ל פא"ט סי' ע' ובב"ח אוסרים בעגלים ויש להחמיר במקום שאין הפסד מרובה כיון דבלא"ה יש פוסקים אסרי בזאב אף בגסה אבל ודאי מותר למכרו כיון דהרבה פוסקים מתירין בעגלים וכמ"ש התרומת הדשן והרב בהג"ה והאחרונים בס"ס זה ודלא כהב"ח דאוסר למכרו לעובד כוכבים ונראה דהיכא דליכ' הפסד מרובה במכירתו לעובד כוכבים אע"פ שצריך לזלזל קצת בדמי המכירה חייב למכרו לעובד כוכבים כמ"ש התרומת הדשן סי' ר"ד והרב בהג"ה לקמן סי' קכ"ו סעיף ב' לענין יין נסך:
**** ENGLISH ****
Such as in calves. And Maharshal Perek Elu Treifot siman 70 and the Bach forbid in calves, and one should be stringent where there is no great loss, since in any case there are poskim who forbid a wolf even in a large animal; but certainly it is permitted to sell it, since many poskim permit in calves, as Terumat HaDeshen and the Rav in the gloss and the acharonim in this s.s. wrote; and not as the Bach who forbids selling it to a gentile. And it appears that where there is no great loss in selling it to a gentile, even though one must somewhat reduce the sale price, one is obligated to sell it to a gentile, as Terumat HaDeshen siman 204 and the Rav in the gloss below siman 126 seif 2 wrote regarding yayin nesech.

**** ENGLISH ****
From a wolf and upward. It is clear in Terumat HaDeshen there in the name of the Or Zaru'a that bears and leopards are above a wolf, and likewise in Orach Chaim at the beginning of general rule 57, and likewise in the Rashba's novellae daf 52 at the beginning of side b, and these are his words: but regarding those above a wolf, such as a bear and leopard, it implies they have no derisah in an ox and other large animals, end of his words (and this is according to his view that anything above a wolf has the law of a wolf; but the Or Zaru'a and his supporters hold its law is like a lion). And I did not fully resolve the end of the view of Rabbeinu Yerucham netivot, end of section 17, who wrote: a bear and leopard — the commentators (it is the view of the Rosh) wrote they have no derisah at all, and it appears from the words of the Rashba that they have the law of a cat and mongoose, and this is the essence, end of his words — for the Rashba wrote in Terumat HaDeshen (and the Tur and Beit Yosef brought it) that whatever is above a wolf has the law of a wolf, and in his novellae he wrote that a bear and leopard above a wolf are nevertheless so; in Ashiri he wrote that a leopard is below a wolf, and it appears from his words there that he agrees a bear is above a wolf, and likewise Raavan siman 252 that a bear has the law of a lion; therefore one should be stringent with a bear and leopard like the Or Zaru'a that they have derisah in a large animal; however to sell it to a gentile is certainly permitted, as below in this s.s.; but in a small animal it is forbidden to sell it to a gentile, for it is no less than a wolf, and likewise is the view of Maharshal Perek Elu Treifot siman 70, not as appears from the Taz in this s.s.

<b>ויש לחוש לחומרא. </b>כתב בס' ל"ח דף קס"ה ע"ב דמ"ש הרב ויש לחוש לחומרא ר"ל דלפעמים יש יותר חומרא אי חשבינן ליה כנקיבת קוץ כמ"ש הרשב"א בדריסת הנץ עד שתינקב לחללו (דמאן דפוסל משום נקיבת קוץ אין בדיקה לנקב משהו דקוץ אבל למאן דפוסל משום דריסה אם ניקב לחלל והאדים מבפנים טריפה ואם לאו כשרה) ומיהו לדידן לא נ"מ מידי דאין אנו סומכין על הבדיקה כלל (ר"ל אפילו בדרוסה וכדלקמן סעיף י"ח) עכ"ל וקשה דע"כ מאן דפסיל משום דרוסה ס"ל דבדריסת הנץ ליכא משום נקיבת קוץ דאל"כ נהי דמשום דריסה ליכא כשלא האדים מ"מ משום נקיבת קוץ איכא אלא ודאי ס"ל דצפורן נץ אינו דק וחד כמו קוץ או מחט כמ"ש הב"ח ודינו כקנה דיש לו בדיקה וכדלעיל סי' נ"א ס"ב אבל דריסת דוב ונמר וכה"ג נחזי אנן אי צפרנם חד דינו כקוץ ולית ליה בדיקה לכ"ע ואי אין צפרנם חד הא לא דמיא אלא לקנה ודו"ק עוד דכיון דלדידן אין נפקותא בזה היאך אפשר שכוון הרב לזה אבל אם באנו לדייק לשון זה נ"ל דה"פ כיון דיש מחמירים יש לחוש לחומרא שלא לאכלו אבל למכרו לעובד כוכבים מותר א"נ ה"ק יש לחוש לחומרא אבל לא לקולא כגון לענין כסוי הדם דקי"ל לעיל סי' כ"ח סעי' י"ז השוחט את הטרפה פטור מכסוי וכגון לענין מתנות דקי"ל לקמן סי' ס"א השוחט את הטרפה פטור ממתנות ואע"ג דאפי' בספק טרפה פטור ממתנות דמצי אמר לכהן זיל אייתי ראיה דלאו טרפה היא ואתן לך וכמ"ש שם בס"ק ג' מ"מ נ"מ דאי תפס כהן אין מוציאין מידו וע"ש וזה ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
And one should be concerned for stringency. He wrote in Lev Chaim daf 165 side b that what the Rav wrote "and one should be concerned for stringency" means that sometimes there is greater stringency if we consider it like perforation of a thorn, as the Rashba wrote regarding derisah of a hawk until it perforates into the cavity (for one who disqualifies due to perforation of a thorn — there is no examination for a minute thorn-hole; but for one who disqualifies due to derisah, if it perforated into the cavity and the flesh reddened inside, it is treifah, and if not, it is kosher); but for us there is no practical difference at all, since we do not rely on examination at all (meaning even for derusah), end of his words. And it is difficult — for one who disqualifies due to derusah must hold that in derisah of a hawk there is no concern of perforation of a thorn, for otherwise, even though for derisah there is no concern when it did not redden, nevertheless for perforation of a thorn there would be concern; rather certainly he holds that a hawk's claw is not thin and sharp like a thorn or needle, as the Bach wrote, and its law is like a windpipe that has examination, as above siman 91 seif 2; but derisah of a bear and leopard and the like — we see whether their claws are sharp: if so, its law is like a thorn and it has no examination according to all; and if their claws are not sharp, it is not comparable except to a windpipe; and examine further: since for us there is no practical difference in this, how could the Rav have intended this? But if we come to analyze this wording, it appears to me that perhaps since there are those who are stringent, one should be concerned for stringency not to eat it, but to sell it to a gentile is permitted; or alternatively, this is what he means: one should be concerned for stringency but not for leniency — such as regarding covering the blood, for we hold above siman 28 seif 17 that one who slaughters a treifah is exempt from covering; and likewise regarding gifts, for we hold below siman 61 that one who slaughters a treifah is exempt from gifts; and even though for a safek treifah one is exempt from gifts, since the kohen can say "bring proof it is not treifah and I will give you," as written there in s.k. 3 — nevertheless there is a difference, for if the kohen seized it we do not remove it from his hand; see there. And this is clear.

And likewise even, etc. In Terumat HaDeshen he wrote so regarding the incident of a bear that leaped on an ox; he sided with several reasons to permit eating and concludes to forbid eating. And these are his words: and it appears one may not permit the ox for eating in such a case at all; but to sell it to a gentile appears permitted in such a case, even though in Perek Kol Sha'ah in that incident of a cow that miscarried in a pit Rava forbade selling to a gentile lest it return and be sold to a Jew — here we do not decree, for there are two sefeikos: perhaps it will not be sold to a Jew, and if you say it will be sold to a Jew, perhaps we hold like the Or Zaru'a who ruled that a bear has no derisah in a large animal. And likewise explicitly in the Mordechai in the name of the Or Zaru'a Perek Kol Sha'ah regarding wheat that fell in water and did not crack — Mar Ukva and Shmuel disagree, and some poskim hold like Mar Ukva and some like Shmuel; and the Or Zaru'a wrote it is permitted to sell to a gentile, lest he not sell them to a Jew, and if you say he sells them, perhaps the halachah is like Shmuel who permits entirely. And it appears the Or Zaru'a's reason is: since there is a dispute in the matter, whether it is entirely permitted or forbidden — and prohibition of selling to a gentile, even a simple prohibition for all, is only a rabbinic decree lest he sell to a Jew; and for rabbinic matters we follow the lenient view, even though the essence of the dispute is Torah law — nevertheless for selling to a gentile the dispute is of scribes, and we follow the lenient view. And do not say what the Mordechai wrote Perek HaShochet that one who slaughters a bird and cuts a little until a little blood comes out and lifts his knife should beware not to complete his slaughter, lest he slaughtered a little of the esophagus and sell the bird to a gentile and they eat it before him — but not because perhaps the gentile will return and sell it to a Jew; and there too there are two sefeikos: perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew, and if you say he will sell it to a Jew, perhaps he did not cut the esophagus at all and the bird was never treifah — for those two sefeikos they decreed on a Torah safek, or even on something whose prohibition from the root is only rabbinic, such as pat Yisrael that a gentile baked not for sale — a decree lest he sell it to a Jew; but in a matter where there is a dispute of amoraim or great ones, we always say regarding selling to a gentile that since it is rabbinic we follow the lenient view, for safek of dispute is not comparable to safek of the act itself; and it is simple. And there is doubt in many places in the Gemara on this matter, end of his words (and I omitted the Hagahot Derishah seif 48 — these words of Terumat HaDeshen; therefore it entered with difficulty; see there). And it appears to me that since Terumat HaDeshen himself wrote at the end of his words "and there is doubt," and also his proof is not compelling in my humble opinion — it is possible to say the Or Zaru'a holds the halachah is like Shmuel but they are stringent like Mar Ukva, and this is specifically for eating; and also in all Mordechai books we have, this matter is not found at all; therefore one should not be lenient in this except for derusah, since in any case in derusah there are several sides to permit: one, that it has permission through examination by law; and further, Rashi's view that wherever the place of trampling is not recognizable from outside it is entirely kosher, as in s.k. 36; and likewise in that incident of Terumat HaDeshen there are in any case sides to permit, as written in Terumat HaDeshen that the bear is domesticated. But for other treifot, in a dispute of great ones where there are no sides otherwise to permit, one may not permit selling to a gentile. And now it is settled why Terumat HaDeshen challenged from that Mordechai Perek HaShochet and did not challenge from what is in the Gemara Perek Elu Treifot (daf 53 side b) and all poskim who forbid selling safek derusah to a gentile — only definite was simple to him where there is only a safek, for such a safek is not a proper safek and is not permitted except in such a case of a bear where there are sides to permit; therefore he challenged from that of "one who slaughters a bird" specifically, that they render treifah from the stringency of the law but we are stringent from concern of perforation of the esophagus, as above s.s. 23; and what he brought proof from wheat that did not crack — there too there is a side of permission, as he explained that the Or Zaru'a holds like Shmuel, or even if sold to a Jew, perhaps he will sell little by little and eat them before Pesach; and it does not appear to be the Rav's view in the gloss and other acharonim who did not write this law except here regarding derusah specifically.
