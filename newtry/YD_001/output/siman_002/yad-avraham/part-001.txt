**(siman 2 seif 4) Believed to say an expert slaughtered for me.** Tur and Mechaber and gloss did not mention saying “expert so-and-so” because even without this he is believed—for he is mumar for appetite who does not leave permitted and eats forbidden, and certainly bought from expert. (See Beit Yosef and responsa Rashba who brought proof we say he does not leave permitted even where there is effort of going, from Chamtzan of transgressors permitted immediately because they exchange; he wrote all the more believed to say regarding another's that it is kosher.) But without this, even “expert so-and-so” does not help for Torah issur as Beit Yosef end siman 119 and gloss there seif 19. No contradiction to what is written here: there speaks of suspected one not concerned with that mitzvah at all even without appetite, as gloss there in that siman seif 2—examine; but here speaks of one who transgresses for appetite—not called suspected and does not leave permitted, etc. All this appears plain view of Taz, Beit Yosef, and gloss here and siman 119—they do not contradict each other. Taz and Shach conclude one must also say “expert so-and-so” as there in that siman—for there is also reason of fear. This does not contradict what gloss there wrote: for Torah issur it does not help—meaning regarding suspected one; not so here. However in Rashba responsum siman 287 it implies one need not “expert so-and-so”—not from fear he permits, but whoever is not troubled like knife examination is believed; see Tiferet leMoshe there: specifically for d'rabbanan suspected one is not believed.

**(seif 5 in gloss) And one who is not concerned with shechitah, etc.**—his law is like mumar to provoke—end. It appears this is specifically when already established he transgressed three times; and even so it is a question for Rav Ashi in Temurah (27) whether we say one does not leave permitted and does forbidden once established three times in issur; but if not established three times in issur, plainly there he said he does not leave, etc., if he did not transgress to provoke even though not for appetite as that case in Temurah—and if so one may permit his shechitah. And although in Rif manuscript it is written implying from the sender that wherever he leaves permitted, etc., he is mumar to provoke—as Peri Megadim chapter 2 Avodah Zarah. One can say there regarding reviving him specifically; and even from first view since he renounced, one cannot revive him. Nevertheless he still has more credibility than mumar to provoke and will not leave permitted in future. It appears Ran too who wrote eating nevelot not for appetite does not help without standing over—means after three times specifically, but he holds even three times helps with standing over. In Rif manuscript he challenged from sender; per what is stated one can say matter of reviving is not credibility. **I saw** in responsa Panim Me'irot vol. 2 siman 41: one who ate meat and afterward it became known it was treifah—he need not bring offering. He brought proof from Yoreh Deah siman 185 regarding sun not at her time she need not offering, considered ones. One can reject: there reason as Noda BiYehudah wrote in Mateh Efrayim Choshen Mishpat siman 96: woman need not examine herself so his heart not pounding and separate from marital relations; but here he should have waited. Also Choshen Mishpat siman 19 Maharik: mishnah Pesachim chapter 6 they slaughtered and found treifah in hiding—exempt; no proof—for there reason error in mitzvah matter and follows R. Meir who exempts even though did not do mitzvah, as Tosafot there (73); even Rambam there exempts from ones because could not recognize—unlike here if he had checked could have known before eating, liable for not checking and not waiting. Only proof from Tosafot Beitzah (25) is correct proof: implies only what he ate before skinning and cutting is considered unintentional, not what he ate after. Must distinguish from Tosafot Bava Kamma (26) regarding chelev: even if never recognized it was chelev he is liable—must say there it was recognizable to someone; not so here. **(seif 10) If the unfit began with half the kaneh and the fit finished—it is kosher.** So Hagahot Ashrei beginning Chullin; concluded we hold it is not for shechitah but only at end—implies per view that shechitah applies from beginning to end it would be invalid. One might say it challenges view that shechitah from beginning to end—for here is explicit baraita brought in Shas chapter HaOr veHaRotav (121) that if non-Jew began with something not making treifah and Israel came and finished—it is kosher. Rather learn: all agree kosher, for non-Jew shechitah is only ape act and no worse than if half kaneh damaged. See Rashi Chullin (20). Further clear required proof: R. Yochanan holds shechitah from beginning to end chapter 2 Chullin (29), and he himself holds chapter 1 Chullin (19b) non-Jew began half kaneh and Israel finished—kosher—examine. This is irrefutable proof. Rambam too although ruled chapter 1 Hilchos Shechitah halachah 18 shechitah from beginning to end, nevertheless ruled this baraita of HaOr veHaRotav chapter 4 Hilchos Shechitah. This is clear. Words of Hagahot Ashrei above cannot be settled unless we emend: “but in something making treifah invalid”; although we hold it is not for shechitah, etc.—these are Rava's words chapter 2 Chullin (29); so Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen and Beit Yosef here—examine.
