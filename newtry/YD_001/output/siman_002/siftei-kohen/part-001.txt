**** ENGLISH ****
And even if he does not worship idols, etc. — It appears to me that his shechitah is nevelah d'oraisa according to all, as stated explicitly in the Tosefta and as implied by Tosafot and Rosh below in seif kaf-vav, and as is also proven explicitly from the Rambam chapter 4 Hilchos Shechitah as printed with Kessef Mishneh: “A non-Jew who slaughtered—his shechitah is nevelah and one is lashed from the Torah for eating it, as it says ‘And you shall call and eat of his altar’—since the Torah warned lest you eat of his altar, you learn his sacrifice is forbidden.” And he fenced a great fence in a matter where even a Kuti who does not worship idols—his shechitah is nevelah—end of his words. It implies explicitly that only Kuti shechitah who does not worship idols is d'rabbanan as in chapter 1 of Chullin: the Sages decreed on Kutim even when they are not idol worshippers because they once found among them an image of a dove they worshipped; but non-Jew shechitah even if he does not worship idols is forbidden d'oraisa. In Beit Yosef and Kessef Mishneh it is written that the Rambam holds a literal non-Jew who does not worship idols—his shechitah is not forbidden except d'rabbanan, and Bach and Levush followed him (daf 334). In truth they wrote thus per the Rambam books printed in Venice year 531, and this version was copied in all editions; but in my books it was proven in many proofs that the version is mistaken and the true version is the one mentioned, and so it is found in old Rambam manuscripts. It also appears to me from Shas (Chullin 5a) regarding “from you and not all of you”—to exclude the mumar—that even an Israelite mumar to the entire Torah except idol worship and Shabbat—his shechitah is forbidden from the Torah, how much more a non-Jew who does not worship idols; and so is implied from Rambam chapter 2 Hilchos She'ar Avot HaTumah as explained there in the edition with Kessef Mishneh, and Kessef Mishneh there followed his view here and therefore entered with difficulty to settle the Tosefta for the Rambam. I have been brief—examine. And the law of a non-Jew’s thought toward idol worship—see siman 4 and what is written there.

**** ENGLISH ****
An Israelite examines, etc. — The reason is that he does not leave the permitted and eat the forbidden; therefore when he examines the knife and gives it to him it is permitted even l'chatchila, for since there is a fine knife in his hand he will certainly slaughter with it and presumably will slaughter well and not delay and not press. Beit Yosef ruled that when he examined the knife before shechitah he need not examine it after shechitah even l'chatchila; after him followed the Taz and so Maharshal siman 6. And even per their words—the reason we say is that presumably he examined and found it fine; but if we know for certain he did not examine after shechitah, he must examine l'chatchila, for he is not better than a fit Israelite who must examine l'chatchila also after shechitah as below siman 18. But R. Yerucham wrote in the name of the Geonim and agreed, and so appears Ran’s view, that one must examine the knife after shechitah also l'chatchila, for we suspect perhaps on the hide it was damaged—for whatever can be checked we check, as above siman 1 seif 2 in the gloss. So too Bach. But b'dieved it is obvious it is kosher even if he did not examine after shechitah, for it is no worse than a lost knife after shechitah which is kosher as below siman 18—for since they examined before shechitah we establish him on his presumption.

Without examining for him, etc. — Below siman 18 it is written that even a fit Israelite when slaughtering must examine the knife at the beginning before shechitah—and this is Rashba’s words in Terumat HaDeshen; he wrote the reason: we suspect he may forget to examine after shechitah. That which the Taz teaches here regarding an Israelite mumar—because we would rely on his examination that he will examine—we teach that we suspect lest he find it damaged and nevertheless slaughter with it, for he will not trouble to fix it. So Rashba and Bach; except Bach afterward added further to resolve that with a fit Israelite the reason is not “lest he forget to examine” but “lest it be damaged and he bless a blessing in vain.” Therefore per this view, if he already heard the shechitah blessing from another and went out, he need not examine—even so in the name of chapter 1 Chullin (17b) regarding “whence do we know knife examination from the Torah,” etc.—it implies the reason is not concern for blessing in vain—examine.

This is not clear, as stated. One must say what Rabbenu Yerucham wrote—“one who slaughtered and brought treifah”—means the matter is not clear that he brought treifah but there is concern he brought treifah, e.g. one witness testified—and this is strained. Uncertain.

**** ENGLISH ****
Or the fit began and the unfit finished. — It implies that if the fit slaughtered the majority and afterward the unfit slaughtered—it is kosher; and so Shas chapter 1 Chullin (19) and Rashi there—examine. Derishah siman 23 seif 8 challenged: Rashi disqualifies when delay was in the minority at the end; he resolved: here it is different since the other finishes the shechitah. Bach understood in last pamphlet in this siman and siman 23 that his challenge is: why do we not disqualify here because of delay? He answered on him: here with non-Jew one can say it is different for there is no delay; but with Israelite, partnership disqualifies. And one might say: perhaps here we speak where there was no delay of the measure of delay (and one can also say there is no delay at all); rather his challenge is: just as delay disqualifies in minority at the end, so unfit slaughter should disqualify in minority at the end—examine, for so his words imply explicitly. But if so, with delay there is no distinction between him and another as Shas and all poskim imply. It is also possible: where the non-Jew slaughtered the minority at the end after the measure of delay it is disqualified; nevertheless it appears to me to resolve: here it is different, for the non-Jew disqualifies only when he does an act of treifah as in Shas and all poskim; therefore after the majority was slaughtered it is no longer rendered treifah. But with delay we derive nothing from an act of treifah—rather since shechitah applies in minority at the end and he delayed and returned and slaughtered, it is considered delay.
