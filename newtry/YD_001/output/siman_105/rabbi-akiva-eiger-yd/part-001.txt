(seif 9) And if it is known that it did not touch, etc. — one in two is nullified. At first glance one should investigate: even if it is possible it touched all of them, nevertheless to permit each piece by itself from one doubt on top of a doubt — perhaps it did not touch this one; and if you say it touched, perhaps it touched only this one and is nullified in the others, and all is permitted; but in truth this is not so to what Rashba wrote in responsum (siman 472) that was asked regarding one piece of two pieces — why should he be liable for a provisional guilt-offering; the issur should be nullified in majority heter; and he answered and said: since they are not recognizable, perhaps the large one was issur, and when Yisrael ate the first, perhaps it was issur, whether he ate the large or the small, etc.; rather they said only when the issur is not recognizable and there is certainly majority heter; and in dry matter you do not find with fewer than three pieces — one of issur mixed in two of heter — end of his words; behold it is forbidden even the small one, even though at first glance the small one should be permitted by majority, for even if it is issur, nevertheless it should be nullified in majority — meaning they did not say nullified in majority except when there is certainly majority heter; but when the heter is not certainly majority, for perhaps the large one is the issur, it is not called majority at all, and consequently even if the small one is issur it is not nullified; and if so, here too there is no reason to say: if you say it touched, perhaps it touched only this one and is nullified in majority, for even if before us it is clear that it touched only this one, the others are not called majority heter, for it is possible it touched them too and there is no majority heter; but still difficult: even if it touched all of them, there is one doubt on top of a doubt, for necessarily it is impossible that all pieces are forbidden, for in all of them there is sixty — and see in Peri Chadash that it deals with a case where there is measure to forbid two pieces; if there is no measure except to forbid one piece, automatically since certainly only one piece is forbidden, it is nullified in majority, as Shach's challenge — see there; therefore one of those pieces is permitted; and if so, we may say that to eat one by one is permitted from one doubt on top of a doubt — perhaps it spread in all equally and there is sixty and all is permitted; and if you say it did not spread equally and in one much entered and in one little, perhaps this is the piece into which little entered — and see; and in the principal law of Shulchan Aruch above, Peri Chadash agreed with Or Zarua that there is no nullification here, for if one encounters what touched he will feel the taste and issur is recognizable; and likewise Peri Chadash above (siman 102) regarding a vessel that became mixed — see there; and it appears to me that the view of Ra'ah too, as Ran wrote in his name (perek 3), regarding fatty roasting in milk — it forbids throughout and sixty does not help; and he wrote there and said: milk that exudes is never nullified in roasting, for in a pot it is nullified because gravy conveys issur equally; but in roasting, which exudes by itself, it is possible that much of it gathers in one place — end of his words; and at first glance it is astonishing how he wrote that it is never nullified in roasting, for you find that if there are two times sixty, certainly permitted, for milk cannot forbid except up to the part of noten taam, and the part of the kid that was forbidden is nullified in the other that is sixty-one olives, for we do not know the place that was forbidden; if so, he holds like Or Zarua's view above and not nullified — and requires study:
