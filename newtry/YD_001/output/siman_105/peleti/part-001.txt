בולע אא"כ האור מהלך תחתיו ואם כן מבואר דע"י כבישה אינו אוסר ועיין מה שכתבתי לקמן בזה:

Issur that fell, etc. Taz wondered on Orach Chayyim, who wrote that a vessel forbidden through kavush may not have permitted matter soaked in it from me'eit l'eit — for when is it kavush? At the end of me'eit l'eit, and already noten ta'am li'fegam; and likewise Shach above siman 69; and Peri Chadash wrote that completion of kavush is at the end of me'eit l'eit, but noten ta'am li'fegam is not until after me'eit l'eit finishes; and Magen Avraham in Orach Chayyim siman 447 preceded him — see there. But all this does not suffice, for if so it must be that the issur emerges and the permitted enters the vessel without resting a single moment — for if one waits, still impairment and kavush come together, and this is impossible to narrow so it does not delay even one moment; therefore a remedy for this question has not yet arisen. And what appeared to me — that it is far to perceive by sense a cold thing that was kavush twenty-four hours, that all twenty-four hours until the last moment it should do no action at all to absorb or emit anything, and in the last moment of twenty-four hours it should do its action to absorb and emit — this thing reason does not compel, only it emits slowly and absorbs slowly, only taste is not sensed until completion of twenty-four hours, then the action of emission and absorption of complete taste finishes; and if so, there is no longer noten ta'am li'fegam, for most taste already emitted before and taste was also absorbed before, and it awakens to absorb so it should not be impaired, since it absorbed in it anew — see there and requires further study. And after I wrote this I found likewise in responsum Chut HaMeshulash daf 100, who also wrote this reasoning — see there and in the omissions; and the matter is close to natural reality. However I do not know from where Orach Chayyim learned this, that when one says this from himself without proof from the early ones, may their light shine. And in truth one may understand in Tosafot Chullin daf 8 side 2 s.v. one who slaughters, and likewise in Avodah Zarah, and likewise in Rosh, who all inferred from gentile oil that R' Yehuda the Prince permitted — for he holds noten ta'am li'fegam is permitted — from which plain gentile vessels are not ben yomo — see there. And it is difficult: from where do we know — for the oil R' Yehuda the Prince permitted was not shelek, for below daf 38 side 2 R' Shimon and R' Shimon bar Rabbi dispute whether shelek oil is forbidden — from which the oil R' Yehuda the Prince illuminated was plain oil without shelek; and if so, from where did it absorb — and therefore it must have been kavush in it from me'eit l'eit; and the above question is difficult for him: if gentile vessels are ben yomo, nevertheless before kavush finished, time of impairment already passed, like Taz's question — and in this Peri Chadash's answer certainly does not suffice, from where we prove gentile vessels are not ben yomo — for so much we are not concerned everything happened in one moment, and this is almost not in reality, and all the more not common. However from the Gemara and Rosh it is not so much proof, for it appears they brought the view of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam that night spoils — only to be stringent they said me'eit l'eit, as Tosafot wrote, and the stringent practice. And likewise it appears from Rosh's words, who wrote: and all the strained explanations — if we heed our teachers; but if we say me'eit l'eit, one need not all the strained explanations — and that is to be lenient he did not rely; but to be stringent he certainly holds one should not distinguish from Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam's words; and according to their view, if one removed the issur from the vessel in the middle of the night and two hours afterward they soaked permitted matter and it fell until the second night before daybreak — behold it fell from me'eit l'eit and is kemevushal, and impairment did not occur, for the entire night did not pass in completeness — and this is certain that the entire night is required, as written above siman 103 at length; and six hours from this night and six hours from the second night do not combine to carry the night to impairment — and if so, certainly forbidden, since their intent is not to be lenient against Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, and especially Tosafot concluded like Rabbeinu Tam, only they wrote the stringent practice. However I did not see in Orach Chayyim that Taz brought so much necessity from what he wrote in general rule 2 law 8, that a vessel forbidden in kavush is permitted l'chatchila to soak in it — for one does not soak the second meat me'eit l'eit — there is no proof to say if they soaked, b'dieved it would be forbidden; one may say he speaks l'chatchila, that noten ta'am li'fegam is forbidden l'chatchila, and this even l'chatchila is permitted, since there is no way to soak it so much time, and he was not stringent even initially. And what he wrote in general rule for one who holds permitted soaked in an issur vessel ben yomo is forbidden, and soaking less than me'eit l'eit is not permitted — if so, Beit Yosef's question: how do you find it — one may say thus: his entire intent is to say the vessel forbids what fell into it from me'eit l'eit; and we say even in a kavush vessel, even though the outer side is not surrounded by water as above in salting laws; and also according to Mahariv's view, that in a copper vessel we require fire passing underneath — he teaches that kavush helps; and it exists in reality if they soaked milk in a vessel me'eit l'eit, and after milk already soaked in it twelve hours they also placed meat in the water, and at the end of twelve hours the milk was taken but the meat remained twenty-four hours — and the soaking water is not forbidden until they forbid the meat — it deals after soaking milk me'eit l'eit one added more water — and examine. And so very much; and if so, because of kavush of meat with milk it did not become forbidden, for they were not kavush together me'eit l'eit; but because of the vessel it became forbidden, kavush in it me'eit l'eit, and all of it is not noten ta'am li'fegam, for from the time of removing milk from the vessel there were only twelve hours and it was kavush in it from me'eit l'eit — see there; and Orach Chayyim did not come here to teach us noten ta'am li'fegam and nosen ta'am for Shabbos — that is known — only his entire intent is to teach us that kavush helps in a vessel as in foods — examine. And what Orach Chayyim wrote to say metal vessels are not forbidden in kavush — Taz wondered from the Tur to distinguish — it appears he holds like Mahariv, who rules like the Yerushalmi that copper does not absorb unless fire passes underneath; and if so, it is explained that through kavush it does not forbid; and see what I wrote below on this.

וספק כבוש אסור וכו' דהוא ד"ת מדאמרינן חידוש הוא דאי תרו לי' וכו' ומזה למדו דין כבוש מע"ל ש"מ דהוא דאורייתא והכה"ג הביא בשם ד"א אשר אינו בידי לעיין בו כי הוא חוכך דמנלן דהוא דאורייתא ולא הבנתי דמה יאמר And doubtful kavush forbidden, etc. — for it is a matter of Torah from what we say: it is a novelty that if you feed him, etc.; and from this they learned the law of kavush from me'eit l'eit — from which it is d'oraisa; and Knesset HaGedolah brought in the name of an authority that is not in my hands to examine, for it is locked away — from where do we know it is d'oraisa; and I did not understand what he would say in the proof mentioned; and perhaps he would say in kavush in vinegar and brine that is only enough to place on fire — and this is possible that it is not d'oraisa. However Rambam wrote this law of kavush kemevushal plainly and did not mention at all in his book at the end of chapter 15 of the laws of forbidden foods that a tame fish kavush with a pure fish, etc., requires two hundred — this taught us the law of kavush; from this it appears he holds kavush kemevushal like Rashi in vinegar and brine — for if he held also in water and me'eit l'eit he would have written; and likewise in chapter 7 of Shevi'it he explains on that mishnah of new sumac kavush in old oil that sumac taste is absorbed more in oil — and specifically old sumac, but not new — from which in general we do not say regarding oil kavush, only sumac is different. And likewise the baraita: carobs in wine forbid, for it gives taste in it — implying other things kavush in wine do not forbid; and likewise in the laws of terumah chapter 15 halachah 9 he ruled explicitly that all terumah vegetables kavush with non-sacred vegetables are permitted except with garlic, onions, and lettuce that are sharp, as Kesef Mishneh wrote — from which in general we do not say kavush kemevushal. However in chapter 22 of the laws of Shabbos halachah 10 he wrote: one who pickles on Shabbos is forbidden, for it is like cooking — and it implies there from the Torah; therefore there is room to say Rambam distinguishes between pickling and pickling, between brine and water, against those poskim who hold in every case kavush from the Torah — that mishnah of terumot is difficult, for it teaches: all that are kavush with one another are permitted except lettuce — why do they absorb from terumah, since kavush kemevushal; and what it says sharp versus not sharp. Again I saw Tosefot Yom Tov on the mishnah challenged thus on Shmuel, who said salted is like roasted, etc., and also wrote Rambam copied "all that are kavush" — and in truth requires further study as I wrote, that he holds like Rashi and distinguishes between kavush; but against those who disagree with Rashi — it is difficult and strained to say he holds like the Yerushalmi that teaches kavush is like shelek, and he holds shelek is weaker than cooking, even though according to our Gemara shelek is more than cooked, as Tosefot Yom Tov also wrote accordingly — and one must say he holds since terumah is d'rabbanan and terumah of vegetables is also d'rabbanan, the Sages were lenient and did not forbid it except sharp things like onion and lettuce, etc. However on Mordechai it is difficult, for he challenged Rashi who does not say kavush except in brine and vinegar, from sumac of Shevi'it kavush in oil, etc. — for if so Rabbah and R' Yochanan in chapter 25 daf 111 side 2 should be difficult, that kavush is not kemevushal — that mishnah as Tosefot Yom Tov wrote in truth; and one must say as Rambam wrote, that sumac absorbs immediately from oil; and see Tosafot there s.v. salted, etc., who also distinguish for R' Yochanan in fish whose scales softened — and likewise in sumac; and if so, even for Rashi requires further study that in a place where they softened, all agree we say kavush everywhere, even in water and oil — and strained to reconcile — requires further study. Summary: kavush in vinegar and brine one may lean to be lenient in its doubt, since a complete mishnah: all that are kavush, etc., are permitted.

Or basar b'chalav — Shach in s.k. 15 elaborated whether the explanation is meat that absorbed milk and that meat was roasted with something else, or meat that was roasted with milk — and he agreed to be stringent. And behold in what he wrote in the question from the Gemara without stirring and covering — behold it did not emit; and it is difficult: behold it absorbed from milk and became issur in its own right; and he properly answered on this Beit Yosef's answer, that it does not emit in the permitted piece, only as much as a peel or removal, but every piece of the cutting board should be forbidden. And Shach wrote this is correct. And I did not understand: behold that piece that fell on a drop of milk — for Rashi it rests in the gravy but not all of it; and if so, the piece beneath it is entirely in the gravy; and for Rashi it is entirely outside the gravy, but the piece beneath it is half in gravy; and for R' Yehuda it is as if all is in the gravy — if so, since that first piece is like issur from itself and absorbs in the second piece, behold there is gravy that carries absorption inside and in the entire piece; and until here we say removal — meaning in roasting; but this piece resting in gravy — it is obvious: if it receives absorption and taste that carries, it is as if it fell inside the gravy; and likewise for Ran, who holds in a drop of milk the taste is because of steam of the pot — this too in steam of the pot spreads through the entire piece; and therefore Ran wrote explicitly, and likewise Magen Avraham, that the entire piece is forbidden; and Beit Yosef's words were not hidden from them, that roasting forbids only as much as removal — for here there is no roasting — and it is clear. And there is no answer here, only as Ran and Rashba wrote; and nevertheless after he concluded chadichah na'aseh nevelah — in truth even without stirring and covering at all, all is forbidden for R' Yehuda; and this is strained, for the Gemara's language implies that which it says "apparently he holds it can be squeezed out — forbidden" refers to that establishment where he did not stir at the beginning, only at the end; but he did not return to the first establishment, that it deals without stirring at all — why should the Gemara bring this establishment at all, that he did not stir at the beginning, only at the end — do we not know that if it is not chadichah na'aseh nevelah, it is not applicable to forbid all because of its kind, and is milk a kind of meat — it should have said immediately: behold it did not absorb and did not emit — apparently he holds it can be squeezed out — forbidden; and everything is resolved — and why did the Gemara need this establishment, that it deals without stirring at the beginning, only at the end — and the matter is very strained; and also according to the law, that we say R' Yehuda forbids even with stirring at the beginning, etc., and the rabbis permit even if he did not stir at the beginning, only at the end — if so, it appears R' Yehuda in every case permits, if R' Yehuda also deals as Ran wrote without stirring at all — and all are equal for issur; if so, the sages who disagree in every case and said it is not forbidden until it gives taste in the gravy, etc. — their words have two explanations, and they fit his understanding: in the case where he did not stir at all — if so, what of it — in the entire pot there is sixty — nevertheless that piece remains in issur, for according to the rabbis it is absorbed issur and does not leave that piece at all, and other pieces do not help to permit it, and issur is not squeezed from it at all; and if so, one must say "until it gives taste," etc., means to forbid another — but that piece is forbidden; whereas if he stirred and covered at the end — for the sages even that piece is permitted, for it was squeezed and permitted; and if so, how can one say two explanations in the sages' words — but according to the disputants it is resolved, for it does not deal at all with one who did not stir at all — that is not in their dispute; and for all opinions it does not forbid the pot — it only deals with covering at the beginning or at the end; and therefore for the sages in every case even the piece is permitted — see there. And therefore Maggid Mishneh wrote well: in every case, even in basar b'chalav, it is not called issur from itself primarily, for the language is strained for him. And also according to Shach and his supporters, who call it from itself — I truly did not understand what difference there is between chadichah na'aseh nevelah in basar b'chalav and other issurim in general regarding a piece fit to be served to guests — there is properly a difference, for there one is lashed for eating meat, and behold here is the body of issur itself, unlike permitted absorbed from issur, for one is not lashed — if so, there is no permitted body of issur here; but regarding this, that it should forbid another piece — in any case, if we say that piece is chadichah na'aseh nevelah, even if Elijah came and said the issur was entirely squeezed out — nevertheless forbidden — if so, it is obvious that piece forbids, and "what became forbidden cannot forbid" does not apply — what of it that issur does not go — the body of the piece goes, as Ran wrote explicitly; if so, in all issurim likewise, as Ran wrote; and if we say that when it is clear to us it was squeezed, it returns to be permitted — if so, since issur can spread, what of it that permitted spreads — behold it was squeezed — if so, in basar b'chalav too we should say thus, and say a difference that in basar b'chalav, even when it is clear the milk was squeezed, nevertheless the meat is forbidden, unlike other issurim — when it is clear the issur was squeezed, it returns to be permitted — if so, see there. But this is strained, for we do not find in any posek (except in responsum of Maharil that I wrote above in his name siman 94) to distinguish between basar b'chalav and other issurim for the view that in all issurim chadichah na'aseh nevelah — if so, there is no proof from Tur and Shulchan Aruch whether they hold that in basar b'chalav they forbid — meaning it is not proven to them to say this view that what became forbidden cannot forbid; and Tosafot's question is not difficult — if so, the peel should forbid again as much as a peel, for he holds like R' Eliezer, that we do not say in other issurim chadichah na'aseh nevelah, and in basar b'chalav a peel does not apply, as Ran wrote, and also its peel is other issurim and not chadichah na'aseh nevelah for R' Eliezer — if so, see there; but according to what is established for us chadichah na'aseh nevelah in all issurim, and difficult from salting and a peel returns and forbids as much as a peel — therefore one must say what became forbidden cannot forbid, etc. — if so, in basar b'chalav too one may say thus, that from the cutting board one should distinguish. And it is possible nevertheless to be stringent, for we hold like Rabbeinu Tam to be stringent, but not to be lenient, lest the halachah is not like Rabbeinu Tam, only like R' Eliezer — if so, this view that what became forbidden cannot forbid goes; and one may say that since it became nevelah, it forbids like Ran's view (and even though it implies above siman 22 like Ra'avan that we do not say on half a piece chadichah na'aseh nevelah — if so, there is no proof from here that a peel returns and forbids as much as a peel — nevertheless from salting it is difficult, as the explicit authors wrote) — except this is strained, for otherwise that which absorbs, etc., is difficult for them, as above — as I wrote. And behold at first glance I would say these words are necessary in Tosafot in Pesachim daf 75 "it was cut," etc., who proved specifically from what is roasted — for we say regarding five that became mixed, etc., that a kohen comes while eating breast and thigh — how can he do so — behold breast and thigh forbidden to non-priests — impossible to roast it with the Pesach and one must cut it, and it is not roasted — except b'dieved one need not roasted — see there. And the matter needs study: behold Pesach is roasted on fire, and in roasting it is clear in the Gemara that it forbids only as much as a peel or removal — if so, what is the question to take a lean kid that does not spread and roast, and breast and thigh with as much as removal give to the kohen numbered with them, and the remainder permitted to non-priests without hesitation. And therefore I would say at first glance he holds like Rabbeinu Efraim — if so, one may say what became forbidden through as much as removal can forbid up to as much as removal; and so always, if we say chadichah na'aseh nevelah — only we do not say chadichah na'aseh nevelah except in basar b'chalav, as above — with this it does not apply, as I wrote. And behold Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen wrote: therefore only in basar b'chalav chadichah na'aseh nevelah — one who eats half a kezayit of milk and half a kezayit of meat is lashed — unlike other issurim, where permitted does not combine with issur, only in kodshim; and likewise Mordechai — if so, it implies in kodshim we hold permitted combines with issur, and even R' Eliezer concedes chadichah na'aseh nevelah. And with this the question is resolved properly, that removal forbidden from breast and thigh returns and forbids as much as removal, and so always, until the entire piece is forbidden; and if so, this is Riva's view, that it should forbid the entire Pesach for non-priests — removal forbidden from breast and thigh returns and forbids, for permitted combines with issur, and so always — thus it arose in his mind at first glance. However from that which we taught in Zevachim daf 97: a wafer that touched a wafer — remove its place; and the Gemara there: until it absorbs in its thickness — see there; Rashi wrote: permitted combines with issur — and we say it forbids only the place it absorbed — difficult: behold every piece and the wafer should be forbidden, as above; and strained to say this comes according to the view that we do not say chadichah na'aseh nevelah — and likewise in kodshim; and Riva's question: why does the Gemara later challenge, why does he need a remedy to bring a kohen, etc. — what is the question, perhaps he holds like the halachah of chadichah na'aseh nevelah — this is strained, and one must say even for Rabbeinu Efraim the main reason in basar b'chalav is not because permitted combines with issur, only since this alone would be permitted, as Tosafot wrote — and this does not apply in kodshim — see there; and if so, Riva's wonder stands in its place — requires further study. However from everyone's words who wrote that therefore a peel does not return to forbid as much as a peel, that chadichah na'aseh nevelah does not occur, or absorbed issur does not emerge — I did not understand the mishnah: if it touched the earthenware of the oven — scrape its place — for it deals with a hot oven, as Tosafot wrote there s.v. we taught, etc. — if so, it roasts in its heat the peel of the Pesach, as explained there in the Gemara; and if so, behold full issur and full nevelah — why should it not forbid a second time as much as a peel, for behold it is full nevelah; and Tosafot there answered: therefore a peel suffices and not removal, for it is a little gravy — but the above question is not resolved, that a peel should return and forbid as much as a peel, and there would be requiring twice as much as a peel — strained to say that which teaches "one shall scrape" means besides the peel of contact — this is strained — requires further study.
