<b>איסור שנשרה עם היתר כו'. </b>באו"ה כלל ב' כ' דאם נשתהה הבשר בשרייתו מע"ל ודאי אף הכלי אסור בדיעבד וצריך ס' נגד כולו אם הוא של עץ או חרס דקי"ל כבוש כמבושל אפי' בכלים ולא דמי לחבית יי"נ דשרי בסמ"ק לשום בו שכר לכתחלה דביין נסך הקילו אמנם בשל מתכות אין מצריכין ס' נגדו בדיעבד כי אם שנאסר ע"י רותחין אך לכתחלה מותר לשרות בו פעם אחרת דהא אין שורין הבשר הב' מע"ל עכ"ל וכ"כ עוד בכלל ל' וז"ל דמאחר דאמרי' כבוש כמבושל אף בכלי אמרינן שבמעת לעת בלע הכלי מידי דהוה אחבית דיין נסך דצריך הגעלה וכן בהיתר ששרוי בכלי של איסור ב"י וכן איסור ששהה בכלי של היתר מע"ל שאסור הכל עכ"ל. ולא אוכל להבין למה יאסור ההיתר בכלי של איסור ב"י דהרי האיסור מחמת כבוש הוא אחר שיעור כבישה שהוא מע"ל דאז נעשה מבושל והרי באותה שעה נעשה אינו בן יומו ואין לו לכלי כח לאסור ההיתר ששרוי בתוכו ואינו דומה לחבית יין נסך שצריך הגעלה אע"פ שיש לו זמן רב דהיינו כל י"ב חודש מפני שטעמו לשבח כשנותן בו יין (ושכר) אבל באמת שאר משקים מותר ליתן לתוכו כדאיתא בסי' קל"ז והוא תלמוד ערוך בפ' א"מ ומזה תמה אני ג"כ מאד על מ"ש לעיל והא דשרי בסמ"ק לתת בו שכר לכתחלה דמה לו לכתוב כן בשם סמ"ק דבהדיא איתא כן בפ' א"מ דר"י שרי ליה לר' חייא למירמי ביה שיכרא והטעם שהם פגומין עם היין כמ"ש בסי' צ"ט ס"ז ויותר אני תמה מה קשה לו מנתינת שכר דהתם הוא פוגם היין וראיתי בד"מ סי' ס"ט וז"ל מדכתב או"ה בכלי ששרו בו בשר מע"ל דנאסר הכלי ומותר לחזור ולשרות בו בשר דהא אין שורין הבשר השני מע"ל משמע דאי שורין הבשר מע"ל נאסר הבשר מזה יש ללמוד דכבוש חשבי' כמבושל ממש ואם שרו דבר איסור כגון יין נסך וכיוצא בו בכלי מע"ל אסור לתת בו דבר היתר חם ואפי' בצונן יש איסור אם הוא מע"ל עכ"ל ותמהתי על זה דהא אמרי' בגמרא שזכרתי דמותר לתת בו שאר משקין מפני שפגומין ואם דבר היתר דקאמר קאי על היין קשה דמה צריך ללמוד מאו"ה זה הדין הא משנה ערוכה היא דקנקנים של עובדי כוכבים אסורים ועל עיקר הדין שכתבו או"ה דצריך ששים נגד כולו באם נאסר ע"י כבישה מע"ל קשה לי דהא כ' הרשב"א בתה"ה הבאתיו בסימן צ"ט סעיף ז' דהחבית שנאסרה ע"י בלע יין עובד כוכבים בצונן אינו אלא מועט וע"כ סגי ליה בקליפת הכלי להכשיר וכ"כ הטור סימן קל"ה בשם הרשב"א ומשם יליף הרשב"א לדינים אחרים כגון חלב ודם לענין איסור מועט בכלי גדול כמו שנזכר שה ש"מ דלא שייך לומר בזה ביי"נ הקילו כסברת או"ה ונמצא דעכ"פ אין לומר כבוש כמבושל ממש דא"כ לא היה סגי בקליפת הכלי להכשירו וזה דוקא בכלי של עובד כוכבים שהיה בו יין והישראל רוצה ליתן שם יין כשר דאין כאן פגם אף אם יש זמן רב משא"כ בשאר איסורים דאחר מע"ל אין הכלי האסור אוסר אפי' ע"י בישול דנותן טעם לפגם ולמה יאסר הכלי כאן ע"י כבישת איסור את ההיתר אח"כ דבשעה שיש לו לאסור דהיינו אחר מע"ל כבר הוא נ"ט לפגם ולפי הנראה דאין לאסור בדיעבד מה שנשרה או נתבשל בכלי ששרה בו איסור מע"ל אלא לכתחלה כדין) נותן טעם לפגם גם מ"ש או"ה לחלק בין כלי עץ וחרס למתכות איני מכיר חילוק זה דלא מצינו בשום מקום דמתכות יהיה לו קולא יותר אדרבה מצינו בסי' קכ"א בטור דאין חילוק בין מתכות לעץ ואבן אלא העיקר בזה דבכל הכלים אין לאסור אותם בדיעבד ע"י שנשרה בם איסור מע"ל כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
Issur that fell with permitted, etc. In Orach Chayyim general rule 2 he wrote: if the meat remained in its soaking me'eit l'eit — certainly the vessel too is forbidden b'dieved and requires sixty against all of it if it is of wood or earthenware, for we hold kavush kemevushal even in vessels, and it is not similar to a libation-wine barrel that Semak permits to put beer in it l'chatchila, for with libation wine they were lenient; however of metal they do not require sixty against it b'dieved, only if it became forbidden through boiling; but l'chatchila it is permitted to soak in it again, for one does not soak the second meat me'eit l'eit — end of his words; and likewise further in general rule 30, and these are his words: since we say kavush kemevushal even in vessels, we say that in me'eit l'eit the vessel absorbed as with a libation-wine barrel that requires hag'alah; and likewise permitted soaked in a vessel of issur ben yomo; and likewise issur that remained in a vessel of permitted me'eit l'eit — all is forbidden — end of his words. And I cannot understand why permitted should be forbidden in a vessel of issur ben yomo — behold the issur is because of kavush after the measure of pickling, which is me'eit l'eit, when it becomes cooked; and behold at that hour it becomes not ben yomo, and the vessel has no power to forbid permitted soaked in it; and it is not similar to a libation-wine barrel that requires hag'alah even though it has much time — that is all twelve months, because its taste is for enhancement when putting wine (and beer) in it; but in truth other beverages are permitted to put in it, as is found in siman 137, and it is Talmud in the chapter "How does one redeem" — and from this I too wonder greatly at what he wrote above; and that which Semak permits to put beer in it l'chatchila — why did he need to write thus in the name of Semak, for plainly it is so in the chapter "How does one redeem," that R' Yehuda permitted R' Chiyya to put beer in it; and the reason is that they impair with wine, as written in siman 99 seif 7. And I wonder more: what did he challenge from putting beer — there it impairs the wine; and I saw in Darchei Moshe siman 69, and these are his words: since Orach Chayyim wrote regarding a vessel in which meat was soaked me'eit l'eit that the vessel became forbidden and it is permitted to return and soak meat in it, for one does not soak the second meat me'eit l'eit — it implies that if one soaks meat me'eit l'eit, the meat becomes forbidden from this — one should learn that kavush is considered literally cooked; and if one soaked an issur such as libation wine and the like in a vessel me'eit l'eit, it is forbidden to put permitted matter hot in it, and even cold there is issur if it is me'eit l'eit — end of his words; and I wondered at this, for behold we say in the Gemara I mentioned that it is permitted to put other beverages in it because they impair; and if permitted matter that he mentions refers to wine — difficult: why need to learn this law from Orach Chayyim — behold the mishnah is arranged: gentiles' flasks are forbidden; and on the essence of the law Orach Chayyim wrote, that sixty is required against all of it if it became forbidden through kavush me'eit l'eit — it is difficult for me, for Rashba wrote in Terumat HaDeshen that I brought in siman 99 seif 7, that the barrel that became forbidden through absorbing gentile wine when cold is only slight — and therefore a peel of the vessel suffices to declare fit; and likewise Tur siman 135 in the name of Rashba; and from there Rashba infers for other laws such as milk and blood regarding slight issur in a large vessel, as mentioned — from which it is not applicable to say regarding libation wine they were lenient, as Orach Chayyim's reasoning; and it turns out that in any case one should not say kavush kemevushal literally, for then a peel of the vessel would not suffice to declare it fit; and this is specifically in a gentile vessel that had wine and an Israelite wants to put kosher wine there — there is no impairment, even if there is much time — unlike other issurim, that after me'eit l'eit the forbidden vessel does not forbid even through cooking, for noten ta'am li'fegam; and why should the vessel be forbidden here through pickling issur into permitted afterward — at the hour it has power to forbid, meaning after me'eit l'eit, it is already noten ta'am li'fegam; and according to what appears there is no prohibition b'dieved of what fell or was cooked in a vessel in which issur was soaked me'eit l'eit, only l'chatchila as the law of) noten ta'am li'fegam; also what Orach Chayyim wrote to distinguish between wood and earthenware vessels and metal — I do not know this distinction, for we do not find anywhere that metal has more leniency; on the contrary, we find in siman 121 in Tur that there is no distinction between metal and wood and stone — only the essence is that in all vessels one should not forbid them b'dieved through issur falling in them me'eit l'eit, as appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****

Meaning: we see it was kavush, only there is a doubt whether it was kavush a full day; and it appears he compares this to a doubt whether there was sixty against the issur that was spilled in siman 98 — except requires further study, for it is different there, for the issur is clear, only the spilling is doubtful — unlike here; and there is clear proof to permit from siman 201 seif 65 regarding a mikveh that was not presumed that its waters diminish — for even though sometimes they diminish, one need not return and immerse by law; and if so, a fortiori here: what there, where there is certainly impure — nevertheless we go to leniency because of the mikveh's presumption — a fortiori here, where there is only a good presumption to establish on presumption of kashrus, that initially we do not presume a defect from a doubt.

And it is fitting to be concerned to be cautious, etc. And Rashal in the chapter Kol HaBasar siman 71 wrote, and these are his words: to rule the law — although a kli sheni does not cook, even if yad soledes bo in it, and also does not emit and absorb at once, such as forbidden matter already forbidden and permitted matter that came in a kli sheni — they are not forbidden one from another; but forbidden matter that fell into a kli sheni of permitted, or permitted into issur — it properly absorbs and forbids through all of it, since the yad soledes bo in it like in a kli rishon, and all the more in iruy — end of his words. And so it appears to me to explain Tur's words, in what he challenged Rashba here — for I saw various explanations and my mind was not settled on what Tur wrote "heat into heat," for who mentions his name, except in this explanation of Tur, who inferred from Rashba's wording that he wrote "but any heat by fire emits," even if the yad does not soledet bo in it — for he included in kli sheni all its heat in one category to be stringent; if so, as the more severe law in it — namely hot into hot — so should the lighter law be — namely the yad does not soledet bo in it — and in all of them it should be forbidden through all of it; and with this he wrote there is no proof from heat of the slaughterhouse, for there it forbids only as much as a peel — rather necessarily that which Rashba said, that all laws of kli sheni are equal — meaning he holds also in hot into hot, which is more severe, a peel suffices; and on this he directed and said that also when the yad does not soledet bo in it, there is this issur; and on this he said he saw some authors write in kli sheni that the law is thus; and with this Tur's wonder on Beit Yosef and on this is resolved; and Tur said: in truth this is not in kli sheni, even cold into hot, only that the yad soledes bo in it — forbidden in all of them, and all the more hot into hot; and its law is regarding emitting from what is inside to absorb into another, like a kli rishon — even though regarding cooking there is a distinction between kli rishon and kli sheni, as written in Orach Chayyim siman 318 — nevertheless regarding absorbing and emitting there is no distinction; if so, there is a distinction in kli sheni, and Rashba should not have included all his laws in one rule, as appears to me — explanation of his words; and from this emerges that even in a kli sheni in which the yad soledes bo, it forbids all of it, as written in the name of Rashal, and likewise Beit Yosef — Tur's words in conclusion, as I wrote; and if it is hot so the hand is burned in it — then it forbids like cooked — meaning even in a kli sheni; and according to this, all the stringencies mentioned in siman 94 regarding ben yomo apply also in kli sheni, and not as Rama wrote there at the beginning of the siman — see there Orach Chayyim: if the spoon is ben yomo, meaning it was used within me'eit l'eit in a kli rishon. And for practical law one should rule stringently like Rashal, especially since Tur also holds thus — only in a place of great loss and an important matter one may rely on Beit Yosef in siman 68 and Rama in seif 3 here to be lenient b'dieved in kli sheni that it does not emit and absorb at all, even if the yad soledes bo in it; and if the yad does not soledet bo in it, one needs only rinsing, as appears to me; and see further what I wrote in siman 94 seif 14 in the name of Rashal, that in a thick item such as a hot piece of meat, its law is literally like a kli rishon — and what I wrote on it there.

**** ENGLISH ****
And some dispute and say that in salting there is no distinction, etc. The reason is that in salting there is no power in what is bland to nullify the power of salting; and likewise salting has no power to make the bland salty, for such is the way of salt — it cannot be nullified except through rinsing — likewise Semak; and in Tur it is written in this plain meaning, and these are his words: it appears he holds that heat through salting forbids all of it, etc.; and Beit Yosef challenged: behold Rashba himself, in what deals specifically with fatty substance, etc. — and every examiner must search with all his power to justify our rabbi Tur's words from this wondrous difficulty, that he copied the beginning of the words and not the end; and I saw emendations on this that do not please me at all; and what appears to my mind in this: Tur inferred from Rashba's words, who distinguished between issur salted above or below — from which the reason of the matter that all is forbidden in fatty issur is because the permitted itself became entirely hot, and through this it receives taste of issur that is fatty; but Tur does not explain thus, rather even though the permitted is cold — nevertheless the fatty substance that comes from salted issur goes and spreads in the permitted, even though it is cold, because spreading itself is hot; and the difference between these two reasons: if according to Rashba's reason, that it is hot — it implies that in one piece that absorbs d'oraisa issur through salting, the entire piece becomes forbidden, since salting of fatty issur makes the permitted piece hot — likewise in lean in one piece, for they did not require fatty substance except from piece to piece, which does not absorb in lean; but in one piece it certainly emits what it absorbed in all its parts, since it is hot; and on this he says: it appears "heat through salting" — for at first glance it is superfluous language, this "heat" — he should have said that through salting it forbids all of it; rather he intended what I wrote, that it implies from Rashba's words that the heat of salting causes the entire piece to boil and automatically the entire piece is forbidden, even in lean; and Rosh could not have written thus, only in lean there is issur only as much as a peel, even in one piece; and therefore Tur afterward wrote properly; and also what he distinguished, etc. — meaning: and further, why did he distinguish between above and below — from another side thus appears to my mind in this explanation; and even if you find somewhere in Rashba's words that it does not seem to him as our rabbi Tur explains his words here — nevertheless our rabbi is saved from the great difficulty we mentioned, that he did not examine Rashba, only the beginning of his words and not the end, which contradict one another.

See what I wrote in siman 98 seif 4 on the law of acting according to its taste, and what Tur wrote here at the end of the siman: one must estimate as if all the blood were salted — meaning that sixty does not help as long as the taste is sensed; and on this they said that even in a thousand it is not nullified, because of nosen ta'am — meaning any amount that gives taste, as opposed to initial intent; and likewise Rama wrote at the end of siman 98.
