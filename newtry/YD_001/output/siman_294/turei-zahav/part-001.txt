**** ENGLISH ****And outside the land — permitted. In chapter 1 of Kiddushin (39a): R' Asi said R' Yochanan said: orlah outside the land — halacha leMoshe miSinai; R' Zeira said to him: but we taught: doubt orlah outside the land — go down and gather; and if certain — forbidden outside the land, how are we lenient in a doubt? He was astonished an hour; He said to him: say thus it was stated — doubt permitted, certain forbidden. We learn from this: if not that it was stated plainly to Moses that doubt is permitted — we would be stringent in doubt; for what difference between halacha leMoshe miSinai and explicit verse? And why was Beit Yosef astonished on Tur who wrote: in the land of Israel it is from Torah, outside halacha leMoshe miSinai; therefore in the land doubt is forbidden, outside doubt permitted — implies because it is only halacha leMoshe miSinai doubt is permitted — and it is not so, for certainly other halacha leMoshe miSinai — doubt is forbidden; only here it was stated thus explicitly as we mentioned; and it seems to me Tur's words are correct and one must say thus: it was difficult for him from R' Yochanan's words who said plainly orlah outside the land halacha leMoshe miSinai — implies like other halacha leMoshe miSinai; and he should have explained his words — behold in God's statement to Moses He needed to state thus explicitly lest one err; and why was R' Yochanan not careful of this? And further: we say everywhere an amora must explain his words; and he said to him: he relied on) the braita that the questioner brings — a braita is not revealed to everyone; and as we find in truth R' Asi did not know the braita — for he was astonished an hour — therefore it seems in truth this was not stated plainly in halacha leMoshe miSinai that its doubt is permitted; rather one must say thus: if not, there is great difficulty why the Holy One needed to say in the land of Israel in the verse prohibition of orlah and outside halacha leMoshe miSinai; and why a verse in the land of Israel; and so challenged in first chapter of Hullin the verse "and you shall slaughter" — for what did it come, since slaughter laws are halacha leMoshe miSinai; all the more here where there is change in learning this prohibition — in the land from verse, outside from halacha leMoshe miSinai; rather certainly He revealed to us there is a legal distinction — namely outside lenient in doubt. And what the Gemara says "say thus it was stated" — this means: say that for this reason it was stated halacha leMoshe miSinai and differs from the land of Israel — to reveal that doubt is permitted; and this explanation seems required in the Gemara also from the language "say" which is ostensibly superfluous; and per our words it is well; and if so Tur wrote well: since we see change in the warning of this prohibition — in the land written in verse, outside halacha leMoshe miSinai — therefore there is distinction between them, as we wrote. And this matter seems fitting and accepted in Gemara and Tur with the Rock's help.

**** ENGLISH ****
Because they had not yet come into the world. — I do not know the source for this and it puzzles me: why in seif 12 do we require that they stipulated specifically at the start of the partnership — why would it not help also afterward, so long as the fruits had not yet grown, as here? And if you say here is different because he had already received payment before the prohibition came into the world — if so he should not say simply "it is permitted to sell" but only if he received payment beforehand. And if you say the reason for the leniency here is that the sale is not a sale since it had not yet come into the world, and therefore it is as though he received the money for nothing from the non-Jew — that is no argument, for then according to Rabbeinu Tam who holds that money does not acquire a non-Jew’s goods except through meshikhah, as written below siman 320, it would be permitted to sell orlah fruits to a non-Jew for money — and that is certainly not so. This ruling requires further study.

<b>ואחד המרכיב כו'. </b>בפ"ק דר"ה הקשה הר"ן דהא אמרינן בספרי דמרכיב אין בו משום ערלה ותירץ דהתם שהרכיב נטיעה ילדה באילן זקן שבטל לגביה ואפי' ילדה בילדה נמי לכי מטו ג' לילדה ראשונה שריא גם שניה אבל הכא מיירי שהרכיב באילן הנטוע לסייג ולקורות דפטור מערלה ע"כ חייבת הנטיעה המורכבת בערלה א"נ באילן סרק הרכיבה אע"ג דעביד איסורא מ"מ יש בה דין ערלה ע"כ וב"י הביאו בסמוך ורש"י כ' בפרק משוח מלחמה על מה דאמרינן ילדה בזקנה בטילה לגבה ואין בה דין ערלה דהא דתנן המרכיב חייב בערלה היינו ילדה בילדה שאף אותו ענף חייב בערלה ואע"ג שקצצו מזקנה נעשה עכשיו ילדה שזו היא נטיעתה וא"כ גם דברי הטור וש"ע כאן מתפרשים כן שהרכיב ענף היתר בנטיעה ילדה שאף הענף נתחייב בערלה והיינו כל ימי משך האיסור של הנרכבת וכמ"ש הר"ן:
**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise one who grafts, etc. — In chapter 1 of Rosh HaShanah the Ran raised a difficulty: we say in Sifrei that one who grafts is not subject to orlah? He resolved that there it refers to grafting a young shoot onto an old tree where it is nullified by it; and even young onto young — when the first young one reaches three years it is permitted, and so too the second. But here we are dealing with grafting onto a tree planted as a fence or for beams which is exempt from orlah — therefore the grafted shoot is subject to orlah; or grafting onto a sterile tree — even though he commits a transgression, it nevertheless has the law of orlah — end quote. Beit Yosef brought this nearby. Rashi wrote in the chapter of the anointed for war: regarding what we say “young in old is nullified by it and has no law of orlah” — that which the Mishnah teaches “one who grafts is subject to orlah” refers to young in young, where that branch too is subject to orlah; and even though it was cut from an old tree, it now becomes young, for this is its planting. Therefore the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here are interpreted likewise — that he grafted a permitted branch onto a young shoot, and that branch too became subject to orlah, for all the days of the prohibition of the grafted one, as the Ran wrote.
