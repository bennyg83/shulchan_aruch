The laws of orlah—where it applies and for whom. In it are 28 seifim. One who plants a food tree counts three years from the time of its planting, and all fruit that will be on it within three years are forbidden in benefit forever—whether the essence of the fruit, the pits, or the shells, such as walnut shells and pomegranate rinds, and their sap and dyes, and grape pits and the beer made from them, and unripe fruit (pagim), and dates that do not cook—and grapes that were scalded and do not finish in their cooking—all are liable for orlah and exempt from revai. **** ENGLISH ****
These three years are not counted day by day; rather one follows the years of the world, which begin from Tishrei. And if it was a leap year, it is a leap year for orlah and for revai. And sometimes there are only two years and a day, and sometimes more than three years. How so? If he planted before the sixteenth of Av and there still remained forty-four days until Rosh Chodesh Tishrei—once Rosh Chodesh Tishrei arrives, a year is counted for it and it counts two more years. And if he planted on the sixteenth, from the sixteenth onward he counts three complete years from the coming Rosh Chodesh Tishrei; and after Rosh Chodesh Tishrei of the fourth year, all fruit that chanata in it before the fifteenth of Shevat have the status of orlah as well, even though they complete later. And what chanata from the fifteenth of Shevat of the fourth year until the fifteenth of Shevat of the fifth year are called revai; and after the fifteenth of Shevat of the fifth year they are fully permitted.
