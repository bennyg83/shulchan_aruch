**** ENGLISH ****
That which we say, etc. Rambam explained there that if it was planted from the 15th of Shevat onward until 44 days before Rosh Hashanah, we count until the 15th of Shevat of the fourth year; and from the 15th of Av until Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden until Rosh Hashanah of the fourth year; and from after Rosh Hashanah until the 15th of Shevat we count three years day for day. Raavad wrote there that all this is only the 14 days of absorption, but Rambam's opinion is that we count three years from the time of planting specifically; for year calculation we count the days of absorption, unlike Kiryat Sefer there; and so in the Tosefta cited and Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Orlah "from when" — from the time of planting. Ran and Rosh wrote this is a dispute in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Rosh Hashanah (and chapter 2 of Sheviit), and the conclusion in Yerushalmi is forever forbidden until 15 Shevat — and so is his opinion. Yeish Omrim disagree, but Raavad wrote from Yerushalmi proof for Rambam's words, and this is its language: Yerushalmi — R' Abba bar Mamal before R' Zeira: it appears correct when planted 30 days before Rosh Hashanah, but when planted less than 30 days before Rosh Hashanah — you see a complete year did not count for him. Say thus? He said to him: yes, even if planted less than 30 days before Rosh Hashanah it shall be forbidden. What is the reason? R' Mani: since it stands within its tree's year, it completes its year. So is Ran and Rosh's text. Raavad's text: a complete year we count for him. Say thus? And it is more settled and there is no practical difference. Ran explained R' Zeira disagreed and said yes, even if planted less than 30 days before Rosh Hashanah it shall be forbidden; and said what is the reason — meaning nevertheless; and R' Mani — not due to stringency but the reason of the matter: since the tree grows on majority water until 15 Shevat from rainwater that passed, and it is as if it ripened before Tishrei; and since so there is no dispute between 30 days and less. But Raavad's Yerushalmi text: he said to him yes, even if planted 30 days before Rosh Hashanah it shall be forbidden — explanation: since we are strict about three complete years, why is it forbidden only until 15 Shevat — let three complete years be forbidden; and R' Mani answers: since etc., meaning it completes its year until 15 Shevat and that suffices, since Rosh Hashanah is for trees; unlike when less, since there is weighing of a complete year; and he wrote further proof from what is written in Rosh Hashanah there "fruits of this planting," etc., implies specifically such a case; and further from "sometimes in the fourth year" implies only sometimes — they rejected his proofs, see there and there; and Rashi there s.v. ufeiros, etc. — therefore they were helped, etc., and it is like the first reasoning; Ran wrote that also for the latter reasoning there is a difference in what he wrote that Tishrei is Rosh Hashanah for a tree, for otherwise if planted 44 days before 15 Shevat a year would count for him. (Likut) That which we say, etc. And so in the Yerushalmi cited (in Likut in note 48) that says but they said fruits of planting, etc., implies specifically such a case (end of comment).
