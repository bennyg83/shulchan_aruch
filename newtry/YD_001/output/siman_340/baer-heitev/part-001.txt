**** ENGLISH ****
<b>His garments.</b> Maharshal writes that for a father or mother one rends on the left side, and for other relatives on the right. Bach writes that this is reasonable: for a father or mother one must rend until the heart is exposed, and the heart is on the left, so the rending should be on the left. This is the custom in the communities. Nevertheless, this distinction is only customary. By strict law, it appears that the same side used for rending over a father or mother is used for other relatives as well, as implied by the Gemara, the poskim, and Tur and Mechaber below in seif 22. Perhaps there, once one has already rent on one side, the custom is not to require a new tear on the other side, and adding to the existing tear is sufficient. Shach.

<b>Custom.</b> Responsa Masat Binyamin writes that throughout the kingdom of Poland the custom is to rend on Chol HaMoed for all relatives, whereas in Germany the custom is to rend only for a father or mother, as Rama writes in Orach Chayyim 544:6. Bach writes that although the practice is not to rend on Chol HaMoed for other relatives, that applies where it will still be possible to rend after the festival. If he hears a recent report during the festival and after the festival it will already become a distant report, so that he will no longer rend afterward, there is no established custom to be lenient. The law should be followed and he should rend during Chol HaMoed. Bach also cites Maharshal that where one hears a distant report, he should not rend during the festival even for a father or mother. Since the mourning obligation has already ended, the rending is not severe enough to override the festival, and he should rend after the festival.
