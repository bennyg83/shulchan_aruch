<b>Except.</b> Tur, following Rif's reading that both a man and a woman are subject to the rule. According to that version, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says a woman likewise rends all her garments, unlike the first Tanna in the Yerushalmi and Evel Rabbati, who requires her to rend only the upper garment for modesty. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel provides a practical arrangement by which she can rend and preserve modesty. Since the Bavli cites only his position, this indicates the halachah follows him. Bach's claim that Rif's text contains no disagreement is incorrect. Nor is the principle that in rabbinic matters the halachah follows the lenient opinion enough to decide for an individual against a majority. Beit Yosef suggests that Tur may understand Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel as explaining rather than disputing the first Tanna, a pattern found elsewhere. This may explain Tur's similar rulings later. His further suggestion that Tur follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel simply because his reasoning is persuasive is less convincing where the Talmud did not explicitly rule like the individual. Mordechai rules like the first Tanna. See below, seif katan 42.
