**** ENGLISH ****And some say. In Shulchan Aruch it is written thus — it seems from Tur — but it is not so; rather the opposite seems from Tur — except it is Rambam's view brought by Rosh and Hagahot Maimoni in the name of "some say" who brought proof from what we say in the Talmud: when Rav Safra's soul departed the rabbis did not tear for him — they say we do not learn from him; Abaye said to him: does it teach "a rabbi died"? — "a sage died" it teaches; and further: every day we heard him in our mouths in the study hall. Rosh wrote: and it seems to me the opposite of his words — and this is the explanation: and further, even if we taught "a rabbi died" — every day we heard him in our mouths — and behold he is our rabbi — end quote; and as Beit Yosef; and it seems to me Rosh's words are explained in their reason and from it one does not budge; but to me Rambam's words seem clear in their reason — for per Rosh's explanation it is difficult: do the rabbis not know that every day we heard him in our mouths? Rather they held this one is not called a rabbi; and Abaye says he is a rabbi; and the essence is missing from the text — and so it should read; and further, since we heard him in our mouths he is our rabbi; and further, whence does Abaye know this — perhaps the rabbis held hearing him in our mouths is not a rabbi; and further, why need this; and further, Rav Safra in any case was a sage — rather this is the explanation: does it teach "a rabbi died"? — "a sage died" it teaches — that is, we know from his teachings that he innovated; and further: every day we heard him in our mouths — meaning still all day his teachings are in our mouths — and if so we know from his teachings that he innovated — and this is clear.

**** ENGLISH ****And even one who hears from witnesses, etc. That is, such as he heard from witnesses that so-and-so blessed God — even though the witness does not utter the blasphemy from his mouth he is obligated the hearer from the witness's mouth to tear; and he need not hear from two witnesses — rather it suffices from one of the witnesses; and likewise when he is not a witness when he testified in court — but he heard the blasphemy itself — everyone who hears from the one who heard the blasphemy itself — even though this one did not utter the blasphemy from his mouth but said so-and-so blasphemed God — the hearer is obligated to tear; but one who hears from one person "so-and-so blasphemed God" and he himself did not hear the blasphemy itself but heard it from one of the witnesses — this last one is not obligated to tear.
