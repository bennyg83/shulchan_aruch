**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And certainly a green appearance, etc. </b>And certainly a white appearance that does not incline at all toward redness. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that he found in writings in the name of Mahariv Margolis that a woman from whom a thick, white substance emerged approximately two or three hours after she bathed in a bathhouse is tamei, because blood commonly becomes white as a result of bathing, etc. These words are astonishing, for no allusion to this is found in the Talmud or the poskim, nor is Mar bar Rav Ashi signed upon them to establish that these words indeed came from Mahariv Margolis. In the Gemara we say regarding a black appearance that it is red, except that it deteriorated. Were this reasoning correct, it should have stated an even greater novelty, that even a white appearance is sometimes found to have deteriorated. Likewise, it is explained below in this seif that a white, thick substance is permitted, and no distinction is made whether she had previously bathed in hot water. Rather, this is an excessive stringency. One who wishes to be stringent should be stringent for himself, but he should not issue such a ruling to others at all. So it appears in my humble opinion. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, also introduced another stringency, that when a green or white appearance is brought before the halachic authority while it is still moist, he should not rule concerning it until it dries, because sometimes, after it dries, redness will be found at its edges. End quote. His words are astonishing. If so, what is the meaning of what they said in siman 186 regarding a woman who has no fixed veset, that she should examine herself before relations? What benefit is there in the examination, since according to his words she must wait until it dries, and while it remains moist she knows nothing? Furthermore, this is difficult in light of an explicit Talmudic passage at the end of the chapter Kol HaYad, which cites a Baraisa that states expressly: A sage should not say, 'Had it been moist, it would certainly have been tamei.' Rather, we say that a judge has only what his eyes see. If so, the same applies in the reverse direction: he has only what his eyes see while it is moist. From where do we derive a distinction based merely on reasoning? Furthermore, it is stated explicitly there that Rebbi saw blood at night and ruled it tamei; by day he ruled it tahor. He waited one hour, then again ruled it tamei, and said, 'Woe is me, perhaps I erred.' The Gemara asks: 'Perhaps I erred?' He certainly erred. Rashi explains that the Gemara initially understood that by day Rebbi ruled it tahor, and afterward he did not see it again, but rather reconsidered on his own and ruled it tamei. He reasoned that perhaps it had been tamei the previous night, but now that it had dried its appearance had changed, and had it remained moist as it initially was, it would have appeared tamei. He therefore retracted his ruling of tahor. Concerning this he afterward said, 'Perhaps I erred,' meaning in having retracted and ruled it tamei, since its present appearance was tahor. The Gemara therefore asks that he certainly erred, for it was taught that a sage should not say, etc. We thus see that, according to this explanation, Rebbi viewed the blood at night while it was moist, and he was not concerned that although it then appeared tahor, it might appear tamei the following day after drying. This reasoning remains valid even according to the conclusion there. Rather, it is obvious that we are not concerned for this, because here too a judge has only what his eyes see. One cannot say that Rebbi viewed it while moist only for purposes of stringency, so that if it appeared tamei he would rule her tamei, but not conversely for purposes of leniency. This is untenable, for the statement, 'Rebbi saw blood at night,' implies that he viewed it in order to rule upon it according to law, whether to declare it tahor or tamei. Furthermore, Rashi explains there that Rebbi acted according to his own reasoning, as it is stated that Rebbi examined by candlelight. Were it conceivable that he viewed it only in order to be stringent, why did Rashi connect this with the fact that he examined by candlelight, which is an excellent examination even for purposes of leniency, whereas here it would have been only for stringency? Rather, it is clear that he viewed it even for purposes of leniency, if it appeared lenient, and he was not concerned that it was still moist. There is also proof from siman 52 above, where the Tur wrote regarding an animal that fell into a fire that if the appearance of its organs changed after they were boiled, the animal is a tereifah. Nevertheless, it is permitted to eat them roasted, and we are not concerned that perhaps they would have changed had they been boiled. Rashi explains the reason in the Gemara: we do not presume a defect where no defect is visibly evident. The same applies here, and all the more so, for while it is moist there is no defect at all. It therefore appears that no one is concerned for this stringency of refusing to inspect it while it is moist.

<b>אפילו ראתה דם. </b>זהו קאי אסיפא דנעקר מקור שלה כו' והוא מדברי הטור שכתב ולא היתה פוסקת לראות כל זמן שאותן חתיכות היו בבית החיצון ופי' בית יוסף תחילה דלא פסקה לראות דם ממש ואפ"ה טהורה דתלינן הדם בחתיכה זאת והוקשה לו על זה דאע"ג דלעיל תלינן במכה ה"מ במכה שבצדדין אבל הכא שהמכה היתה במקור עצמו אפשר דטמא ועל כן חזר ופירש דהטור לא מיירי מדם אלא חתיכות קטנות ראתה והקשה בד"מ על הבית יוסף מה שהחמי' במה שהיתה המכה במקור עצמו והלא אמרי' פרק התינוקת נאמנת אשה לומר מכה יש לי במקור שממנה יוצא דם כו' ע"כ פי' כדבריו הראשונים מ"ה כל מה שהיא רואה אפילו דם גמור אחר שמרגשת שנפל המקור בבית החיצון היא תולה הדם באותן החתיכות וכך הם דבריו כאן שכתב ומחמת מכה היא וק"ל דא"כ למה אמרו בגמ' ובפוסקים הטעם לפי שאין דרכה לראות בכך מאי שנא מההיא דסי' קפ"ז סעיף ה' דהקילו במכה בלאו האי טעמא ונ"ל אע"פ שלענין דין יפה כתב רמ"א מכל מקום אין טעם שלו מתיישב דהכא התירו אפי' בדם נדה ממש שבא מאותה חתיכה דהיינו שהדם נדה שיש באותה חתיכה של המקור הוליכה אותה חתיכה עמה הדם נדות לבית החיצון אינה אוסרת דלא אסרה תורה אלא כשהמקור נשאר במקומו והדם יוצא ממנו מה שאין כן כאן שהמקור עצמו מוליך הדם עמו ואין זה דרך ראייה שאסרה תורה וכך מבואר מדברי הרא"ש שמביא בית יוסף וז"ל והא דאמרינן שאין זה דם נדה פי' אין דרך דם נדות לבוא בזה הענין כו' הרי ממש כמו שזכרתי ואין כאן שייכות לדם מכה כלל ולפי זה אפילו הוא בשעת ווסתה מותר זה הדין כיון שראייתה משונה וכן משמע מלשון רמ"א בסעיף ה' שכתב שם דוקא שלא בשעת וסתה כו' וכאן לא כתב כלום אלא שמו"ח ז"ל כתב דדין זה אינו מותר אלא בין ווסת לווסת ועוד חלק על רמ"א והחמיר שצריך שתבדוק עצמה בשעה שנופל המקור לבית החיצון והיא טהורה ואחר כך תראה דם כו' ואיני רואה שום חילוק בין שעת נפילה לאחר נפילה בזה ותו דמה שיעור יש ליתן בזה אימתי מקרי בשעת נפילה או אחריה והנראה לע"ד כתבתי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if she saw blood. </b>This refers to the latter clause, where her source became detached, etc. It is taken from the words of the Tur, who wrote that she did not cease seeing as long as those pieces remained in the outer chamber. The Beit Yosef initially explained that she did not cease seeing actual blood, and even so she is tahor, because we attribute the blood to this piece. He found this difficult, because although above we attribute blood to a wound, that applies to a wound located at the sides, whereas here the wound was in the source itself, and perhaps she should be tamei. He therefore reinterpreted the Tur as not referring to blood, but rather that she saw small pieces. The Darkei Moshe challenged the Beit Yosef's stringency concerning a wound located in the source itself, for we say in the chapter HaTinokes that a woman is believed to say, 'I have a wound in the source from which blood emerges,' etc. He therefore explained according to the Beit Yosef's first interpretation. For this reason, whatever she sees, even actual blood, after she senses that the source has fallen into the outer chamber, she attributes the blood to those pieces. This is also the meaning of his words here, where he wrote that it is due to a wound. This is difficult to me. If so, why did the Gemara and the poskim state the reason that it is not her usual manner to see in this fashion? How is this different from the case in siman 187, seif 5, where they were lenient regarding a wound even without this reason? It appears to me that although the Rama wrote correctly regarding the practical law, his reason is not satisfactory. Here they permitted even actual menstrual blood that came from that piece. Namely, the menstrual blood contained in that detached piece of the source was carried together with the piece into the outer chamber. It does not render her forbidden, because the Torah prohibited only when the source remains in its place and the blood emerges from it. Here, however, the source itself carries the blood together with it, and this is not the manner of seeing prohibited by the Torah. This is also clear from the words of the Rosh cited by the Beit Yosef: 'That which we say that this is not menstrual blood means that menstrual blood does not ordinarily emerge in this manner,' etc. This is precisely as I have explained, and there is no relevance here to the blood of a wound. According to this, this ruling is permitted even at the time of her veset, since her seeing is abnormal. This is likewise implied by the Rama's wording in seif 5, where he wrote there, 'specifically not at the time of her veset,' etc., whereas here he wrote no such qualification. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, however, wrote that this ruling applies only between one veset and the next. He further disagreed with the Rama and ruled stringently that she must examine herself at the time the source falls into the outer chamber and establish that she is tahor, and only afterward may she see blood, etc. I see no distinction in this matter between the time of the falling and afterward. Furthermore, what measure can be assigned to determine when it is considered the time of falling and when it is considered afterward? I have written what appears correct in my humble opinion.

<b>שום לחלוח דם כו'. </b>ולא שייך כאן אין דרכה של אשה לראות בכך דכאן ודאי אמרינן כיון דיש דם מוכח דגם הנהו הם דם אע"פ שלא נימוחו ועוד דאפשר דדרכה של אשה לראות לפעמים כך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Any moisture of blood, etc. </b>The principle that it is not a woman's usual manner to see in this fashion does not apply here. Here we certainly say that since blood is present, this proves that those pieces too are blood, even though they did not dissolve. Furthermore, it is possible that a woman does sometimes see in this manner.
