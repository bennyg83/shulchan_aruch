**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of the appearances of blood; it contains 6 se’ifim. Every red appearance, whether it is very faint or deep, is tamei; and likewise every black appearance. And there is nothing tahor except a white appearance; and likewise a green appearance, even like the appearance of wax or gold, and all the more so the green like leek-green or like grasses (Mordechai). (And likewise the appearance that they call in the vernacular “bluo” is included in green.) And even if it has the consistency of blood and it is very thick — and even if she felt that her makor opened and she checked immediately and found these appearances — she is tehorah. (These are my words, not like Terumas HaDeshen, siman 246, who stammers about this.) {Rama: And so is the main ruling (Beit Yosef and Tur), unlike those who are stringent to deem her tamei if it has the consistency and it is thick (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 246). (And if she felt that her makor opened and she checked immediately and found nothing, see above siman 190.)}

**** ENGLISH ****
A woman is believed to say, “Such as this I saw, and I lost it”; and if it is a white appearance or a green one, it is tahor. But if she brought blood before us and we deemed it tamei, or even if we were in doubt whether it is tamei or tahor, and she says, “Such-and-such a chacham was metaher for me something like this,” we do not rely on her.

**** ENGLISH ****
Any blood that comes out of a woman, whether moist or dry, renders her tamei. Moreover, even if a formed creature came out of her — like shells, or like hairs, or like tiny red gnats — she is tamei, provided that they dissolve within 24 hours by soaking them in lukewarm water, and the water remains lukewarm the entire 24 hours that they are in it; and the measure of the warmth is like water that was drawn in the summer from the river or from the spring and stood in a house whose heat of the house warms it; and like the warming of these, so is the measure of “lukewarm” in the days of winter. (And unspecified “lukewarm” is not hotter than the warmth of saliva (Beit Yosef in the name of Ritva, perek HaMapeles).) But if they are so hard that they do not dissolve within 24 hours, she is tehorah, even if they dissolve through crushing, that one crushes them with his fingernails. (And if one crushes them with his fingernails and they did not dissolve, she is tehorah, and there is no need to check by soaking.) (The Maggid Mishneh, perek 5; and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.)
