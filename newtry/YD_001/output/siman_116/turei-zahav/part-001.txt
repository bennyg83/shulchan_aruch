"And now that there are no snakes, etc." And there is no issue here of a matter forbidden by vote, etc., for it was forbidden only because of snakes; and see above siman 115 seif 2 in the name of Semak: In Tur — liquid dripping drop by drop has no concern of exposure; Beit Yosef wrote this refers to the lower vessel but in the upper vessel there is concern of exposure; and one may ask — in Gozel (daf 115) it appears from Tosafot's words beginning "When, etc." that the reason it is permitted drop by drop is that the snake assumes a person is there and does not come — if so one should permit the upper vessel too; and one may answer Beit Yosef wrote so according to Rambam's reason for drop by drop — the snake fears the bubbling of the liquid, etc.; according to this one must forbid the upper vessel, and requires study why Beit Yosef wrote this as plain when in truth it is disputed. And further there is room to investigate in Beit Yosef in what he wrote on Ran — perhaps he explains that the cat stated in the Gemara is not literal, etc.; it is astonishing — for other impure animals die from venom as Tosafot wrote in last chapter of Gozel (Bava Kamma daf 115) — if so there is a violation of bal tashchit as Tosafot wrote there, proven from the challenge regarding his cat too; if so how does Ran compare other impure animals to cat? One must say Ran explains thus: in what the Tanna said one may not give water to another's animal — which is superfluous — he intended to teach there is a distinction: regarding one's own there is prohibition only lest he eat it — meaning fit for eating; but regarding impure animal one need not beware of this — are we dealing with fools who kill their animals? as Tosafot wrote there in Bava Kamma; and if in a case where he needs to kill it, plainly permitted and no bal tashchit, as we will write further in this siman; but another's impure animal — prohibition even if unfit for eating because of damage to his property; and we challenge and the baraita teaches but he gives to his animal; and we answer it deals with impure — meaning necessarily where he needs to kill it as stated; if so the challenge is obvious and we answer regarding cat that venom does not kill it; on this he asks either way why his differs from another's — there is certainly damage since he forbade another's; if so there is no permission regarding his because of bal tashchit but because he needs this — obvious challenge and we answer it weakened and recovered — in this there is prohibition regarding others because of damage when he wishes to sell it in its weakened state; but regarding his own it is permitted even if he does not need this but only to test water — no bal tashchit since it recovered; thus Ran properly wrote regarding his own there is no prohibition at all in impure animal — if venom kills it he would not do so himself, and if he needs it certainly permitted, and if it weakens and recovers likewise no prohibition, and if it does not recover it is like actual killing that a person does not do unless he needs; but regarding another's it is forbidden in every manner — not only where it kills but even weakened that recovered — forbidden; and all is resolved: In Derishah he brings here what can be asked from what is written in Orach Chayim siman 472 that one does not recite kiddush on wine that was exposed even if strained in a strainer — implying but at least permitted for drinking, namely like R' Nechemyah who permits when strained in a strainer; and here Tur ruled straining does not help like the Sages; and it appears he teaches not to say that since he wishes to perform a mitzvah with it he should not be concerned for danger since straining helps for R' Nechemyah even for drinking, as stated in chapter Areivin — cup of blessing combines for good and not for bad regarding pairs — he teaches that nevertheless one does not recite kiddush on it.

<b>מיהו בדיעבד אינו אסור. </b>היינו בריחא לחוד אבל אם נתבשלו יחד אסורין אף דיעבד וכתב או"ה כלל כ"ג וז"ל וכ' המרדכי וא"ז פרק א"מ דבר שיש בו משום סכנה דינו כאיסור לבטלו בס' ויותר חמור דאפילו ביבש במינו צריך ששים עכ"ל וכ"ש חתיכת בשר שנפלה ליורה שיש בו דגים או איפכא שיש שם סכנה שצריך ס' נגדו אבל משום טעם בליעת כלי ליכא למיחש כו' עכ"ל <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>והקשה בד"מ דהא איתא בטור דחיישינן לגילוי אפילו במאה סאה מים וא"א שלא יהא ס' אפילו שתה נחש ממנו עכ"ל וכן כתוב במהרי"ל שלפוחית מדג שנפלה לקדירת בשר ונתבשל שם ואסר מהר"י סג"ל הכל משום סכנה עכ"ל משמע דלא מהני ס' דחמירא סכנתא מאיסורא וכתב רש"ל פרק ג"ה סי' ט"ו דאפילו לכתחלה אין להחמיר בתנור גדול לענין זה אע"פ דלענין איסור בשר בחלב מחמרינן לכתחלה וע"כ נ"ל פשוט דאותן הלחמים שאופין בתנור אחד עם בשר אין סכנה כלל לאכלם עם דגים אע"פ שהתנור היה סתום:
**** ENGLISH ****
"However b'dieved it is not forbidden." That is reicha alone; but if they were cooked together they are forbidden even b'dieved. Issur VeHeter wrote general rule 23 — and these are his words: Mordechai and Or Zarua, chapter Eizehu Mekoman — a matter involving danger has the law of prohibition to nullify in sixty, and is more severe — even dry in its kind requires sixty — end; and all the more a piece of meat that fell into a pot containing fish or the reverse — there is danger requiring sixty against it; but regarding taste of vessel absorption there is no concern, etc. — end. And Darkei Moshe challenged — for it is stated in Tur that we are concerned for exposure even in one hundred se'ah of water and it is impossible there should not be sixty even if a snake drank from it — end; and so in Maharil — a small fish from a fish fell into a meat pot and was cooked there and Maharai"l forbade everything because of danger — end; implying sixty does not help, for danger is stricter than prohibition; and Maharshal wrote chapter 35 siman 15 that even l'chatchila one need not be stringent in a large oven regarding this even though regarding meat and milk we are stringent l'chatchila; and accordingly it appears plain that those loaves baked in one oven with meat involve no danger at all to eat with fish even if the oven was closed.

<b>רק כשמבשלם יחד. </b>וה"ה אם נצלו כא' ממש זה עם זה כן איתא בגמ' בפ' כיצד צולין להדיא דאמר מר בר רב אשי אסרה אפילו במילחא ופי' רש"ל פ' ג"ה סי' ט"ו אליבא דהרמב"ם דשם מיירי שצלו דג עם בשר ממש ע"כ אסרו משום סכנת נפשות אבל לא כפרש"י דמיירי בתנור א' דבזה אין סכנה אבל נלע"ד כיון דלרש"י קאי על צליית בשר ודג בתנור יש להחמיר אפילו בריח ותו דהא כתב ב"י סי' צ"ה דרמב"ם מפרש ג"כ ההיא דכיצד צולין בתנור אחד וא"כ אסור למר בר רב אשי אפילו משום ריח משום סכנה כמו לענין בשר בחלב ומ"מ אין זה דומה ללחמים שאופין בתנור אחד עם בשר שזכרתי בסמוך שאין שם רק ריח בשר בלחם ובטל בו ואין אח"כ חשש לאכול עם דג. וכתב באו"ה כלל ל"ט דיש לאסור אם נצלו יחד בלתי מחבת תחת כלי אחד או אף אם בתנור סמוכין זו לזו דברוב פעמים יוצא מזו לזו השמנונית עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
"Only when cooking them together." And the same applies if roasted literally one with the other, as stated in the Gemara chapter Keitzad Tzolin explicitly — Mar bar Rav Ashi forbade even in salt; and Maharshal explained chapter 35 siman 15 according to Rambam that it deals with fish roasted with actual meat — therefore forbidden because of danger to life; but not like Rashi who deals with one oven where there is no danger; but in my humble opinion, since according to Rashi it refers to roasting meat and fish in an oven one should be stringent even for smell; and furthermore Beit Yosef wrote siman 95 that Rambam also explains the case in Keitzad Tzolin in one oven — if so forbidden to Mar bar Rav Ashi even for smell because of danger like meat and milk; nevertheless this is not comparable to loaves baked in one oven with meat mentioned nearby where there is only smell of meat in bread nullified therein and no subsequent concern to eat with fish. Issur VeHeter general rule 39 wrote one must forbid if roasted together without a pan under one vessel or even if in an oven adjacent to one another, for in most cases grease passes from one to the other — end.

**** ENGLISH ****
"At the time of the tekufah." It is written in Darkei Moshe in the name of Avudraham: I found written one must beware of drinking water at the time of the tekufah because of danger lest he be harmed and swell; and the reason is a drop of blood falls between tekufah and tekufah; but Ibn Ezra responded this is mere superstition, etc., and there is no danger at all; and some Geonim said there is no divination in Jacob — rather the early ones said these things to frighten people that they should fear Hashem and repent so Hashem would save them from the four seasons of the year — end.

<b>ולא בסופו. </b>כיון דכבר איתחזיק הדבר ח"ו ועוד איתא בגמרא פרק הכונס דבר בעיר אל יהלך אדם באמצע הדרכים מפני שמלאך המות יש לו רשות מסגי להדיא שלום בעיר אל יהלך בצדי הדרכים מפני שאין לו רשות והוא מחבא את עצמו וכתב רש"ל ונראה דסיפא מיירי דווקא בלילה שביום לא שכיחי מזיקים אבל רישא לענין דבר אין חילוק בין יום ללילה:
**** ENGLISH ****
"And not at its end." Since the matter has already become established, God forbid; and further it is stated in the Gemara chapter HaKones — when plague is in the city a person should not walk in the middle of the roads because the angel of death has permission and goes openly; when there is peace in the city he should not walk at the sides of the roads because he has no permission and hides himself; and Maharshal wrote: it appears the latter clause deals specifically with night, for by day harmful spirits are not common; but the first clause regarding plague applies with no distinction between day and night.

"Included in Do not make yourselves abominable." In Tur it is written in the name of Rashba: for this they would administer lashes of rebellion; and one may ask why he is not liable for full lashes — it appears because it is a general prohibition including many matters, and every general prohibition one is not lashed for, as stated in chapter Four Deaths: I found in one old collection — these are days of great danger for letting blood, a tradition from Rabbi Yehudah he-Chasid, may his memory be a blessing — Rosh Chodesh, Iyar, Elul, Tevet when the eighth day of one of them falls on Monday or Wednesday; and likewise one who slaughters a goose on that day or eats from it is endangered, God forbid; and in another collection I found likewise, only in place of the first day of Tevet it is written the fifteenth of Tevet when it falls on the first day — Rosh Chodesh Tevet that falls on Shabbat, the first day of Rosh Chodesh. Further we find the Sages forbade because of danger not to cut down a fruit-bearing tree, as stated in chapter HaGozel (daf 91) — Rav said this palm laden with fruit may not be cut; Rav Chanina said no righteous person dies except one who cut a fig tree before its time; and we say there Rav Chisda saw a palm standing among vines and told his attendant go uproot it — meaning they harm vine nourishment and vines are more important; and Rosh wrote there: and if he needed its place it is permitted — end) — from this I permitted one who had land with trees to cut the trees even though they bore fruit to build a dwelling house on them; and I wondered that Tur did not bring this law of cutting the tree anywhere except Semag siman 229 brought it: Maharshal wrote in last chapter of Gozel siman 37 on what is stated in the Gemara regarding exposed liquids that were not strained, that it is forbidden to give them to one's animal to drink; Rashi explained the reason — because of danger lest he slaughter it afterward and eat it; and from this they practiced to forbid sheep among whom grew boils of plague accustomed to die from them; and in my youth I saw the community made a ban and forbade all sheep until the plague ceased — end; and one may wonder — we stated in siman 60 that even if the animal ate its own poison it is kosher, implying no prohibition at all because of danger except specifically eating a person's poison, for perhaps there is prohibition since it was dangerous; and Beit Yosef there in the name of Rashba that it is like treifah and kosher, per what Tur wrote at end of this siman and in Shulchan Aruch that a careful person should not eat treifah; but it is asked why he learned this from Rashi that there is danger when drinking bad water — exposed water where there was danger also for a person because of snake venom; and Maharam Chaviv, z"l, challenged from what we say in siman 60 that if it drank bad water — exposed — it is permitted, and here forbidden per Rashi above because of danger; and it appears to me to analyze further — there we speak of a person's poison forbidden because of danger — why permitted in bad water when there is poison for a person? It appears there in those distinctions listed — blood congestion, etc. — in all we see harm because we see illness in it as Rashi wrote there that it became ill, proven — for otherwise how would we know this happened to it? Unlike exposed water we see no illness after drinking, only some hours it is healthy after drinking as before — from this we say certainly no snake drank from that exposed water, for wherever there is poison it would certainly not be healthy afterward — only there we said permitted; but here where he may not give it to drink the concern is lest he slaughter it immediately after drinking and perhaps there is danger to life — this fits Rashi's language "lest he slaughter it afterward" — he means after drinking, and the language "afterward" indicates immediacy; but this is forced. However Tosafot in chapter HaGozel explained the reason one may not give exposed water to an animal is bal tashchit; and why even impure animal one may not give; and Maharshal wrote there: hence one who has a bad dog that harms and spoils food even if it does not bite may give it poison and does not violate tza'ar ba'alei chayim, for Tosafot forbade only because of bal tashchit and here there is none; nevertheless I heard to protest against putting a needle in bread for them lest it go mad and there is great danger to life — end.
