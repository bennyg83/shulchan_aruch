אסור והכל ניחא: <br><b>בדרישה </b>מביא כאן מה שיש להקשות ממ"ש בא"ח סימן רע"ב שאין מקדשין על יין שנתגלה אפילו סננו במסננת דמשמע אבל מותר עכ"פ בשתייה והיינו כרבי נחמיה דמתיר בסננו במסננת וכאן פסק הטור דלא מהני מסננת כחכמים ונראה דקמ"ל דלא תימא דכיון שהוא רוצה לעשות בו מצוה לא יחוש לסכנה כיון דעכ"פ מהני לר' נחמיה מסננת אפילו לשתייה וכדאיתא בפרק ערבי פסחים כוס של ברכה מצטרף לטובה ולא לרעה לענין זוגות קמ"ל דאפ"ה אין מקדשין עליו:
**** ENGLISH ****And now that there are no snakes, etc. And there is no issue here of a matter forbidden by count, etc., for it was forbidden only because of snakes; and see above in siman 115 seif 2 in the name of Semak: Tur — a beverage that drips drop by drop has no issue of exposure; and Beit Yosef wrote that this is in the lower vessel, but in the upper vessel there is issue of exposure. And one may challenge: behold in Gozel (daf 115) it is implied in Tosafot's words beginning "When," etc., that the reason it is permitted drop by drop is that the snake thinks there is a person there and does not come — if so one should permit the upper one too; and one may say that Beit Yosef wrote thus according to what Rambam wrote regarding the reason of drop by drop — the snake fears the bubbling of the beverage, etc.; according to this one must forbid the upper one, and it requires investigation why Beit Yosef wrote this as plain, when in truth it is a dispute. And there is further room for investigation in Beit Yosef in what he wrote on Ran — perhaps he explains that the cat we say in the Gemara is not precise, etc.; it is puzzling, for behold other impure animals die from venom as Tosafot wrote in the last chapter of Gozel in Bava Kamma (daf 115); if so there is a violation of bal tashchit as Tosafot wrote there, and this is proven from that we challenge on the cat itself; and if so how does Ran compare other impure animals to the cat? And one must say that Ran explains thus: in that which the tanna said one should not give to drink to another's animal — which is superfluous — he intended to teach us that there is a distinction in the matter, namely regarding one's own there is prohibition only lest he eat it, and that is when fit for eating; but regarding an impure animal one need not be careful about this — are we dealing with fools that he would kill his animals? as Tosafot wrote there in Bava Kamma; and if in a case where he needs to kill it, plainly it is permitted and there is no bal tashchit here; and as we will write further in this siman. But regarding another's impure animal there is prohibition even when not fit for eating, from the aspect of damaging his property; and we challenge: but it is taught — rather he gives to his animal; and we say it deals with an impure one, and that is necessarily where he needs to kill it as written; if so the challenge is obvious, and we answer: regarding the cat — venom does not kill it; and on this we challenge: whichever way you want, what difference between his and another's, for necessarily there is damage since it is forbidden for another's; if so there is no permission in his because of bal tashchit but because he needs this — the challenge is obvious, and we answer that it weakens and then becomes healthy: in this there is prohibition regarding others from the aspect of damage, for when he wishes to sell it at the time of its weakness; but regarding his own it is permitted even when he does not need this but only to test the water, for there is no bal tashchit since it becomes healthy again. It turns out that Ran wrote well that regarding his own there is no prohibition at all with an impure animal: if venom kills it he would not do so himself; and if he needs it, certainly permitted; and if it weakens and becomes healthy, likewise no prohibition; and if it does not become healthy again, it is like killing it literally, for a person does not do this unless he needs it; but regarding another's it is forbidden in every case — not only where it kills, but even when weak and becomes healthy it is forbidden; and all is well. In Derishah he brings here what there is to challenge from what is written in Orach Chaim siman 472 that one does not recite kiddush on wine that was exposed even if strained in a strainer — it implies but it is permitted at least for drinking, and that is like Rabbi Nehemiah who permits when strained in a strainer; and here Tur ruled that a strainer does not help, like the Sages; and it appears he teaches us not to say that since one wishes to perform a mitzvah with it he should not be concerned for danger, since in any case a strainer helps for Rabbi Nehemiah even for drinking, as stated in the chapter of the eve of Pesach — a cup of blessing combines for good and not for bad regarding pairs; he teaches us that nevertheless one does not recite kiddush on it.

<b>מיהו בדיעבד אינו אסור. </b>היינו בריחא לחוד אבל אם נתבשלו יחד אסורין אף דיעבד וכתב או"ה כלל כ"ג וז"ל וכ' המרדכי וא"ז פרק א"מ דבר שיש בו משום סכנה דינו כאיסור לבטלו בס' ויותר חמור דאפילו ביבש במינו צריך ששים עכ"ל וכ"ש חתיכת בשר שנפלה ליורה שיש בו דגים או איפכא שיש שם סכנה שצריך ס' נגדו אבל משום טעם בליעת כלי ליכא למיחש כו' עכ"ל <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>והקשה בד"מ דהא איתא בטור דחיישינן לגילוי אפילו במאה סאה מים וא"א שלא יהא ס' אפילו שתה נחש ממנו עכ"ל וכן כתוב במהרי"ל שלפוחית מדג שנפלה לקדירת בשר ונתבשל שם ואסר מהר"י סג"ל הכל משום סכנה עכ"ל משמע דלא מהני ס' דחמירא סכנתא מאיסורא וכתב רש"ל פרק ג"ה סי' ט"ו דאפילו לכתחלה אין להחמיר בתנור גדול לענין זה אע"פ דלענין איסור בשר בחלב מחמרינן לכתחלה וע"כ נ"ל פשוט דאותן הלחמים שאופין בתנור אחד עם בשר אין סכנה כלל לאכלם עם דגים אע"פ שהתנור היה סתום:
**** ENGLISH ****However b'dieved it is not forbidden. That is regarding odor alone; but if they were cooked together they are forbidden even b'dieved. And Issur VeHeter wrote klal 23, and these are his words: Mordechai wrote, and Ibn Ezra in chapter 1 of forbidden foods — a matter involving danger has the law of a prohibition to nullify it in sixty, and is more stringent, for even dry in its kind requires sixty — end of his words; and all the more so a piece of meat that fell into a pot that has fish or vice versa — there is danger and requires sixty against it; but regarding taste of vessel absorption there is no concern, etc. — end of his words. And Darkei Moshe challenged: behold it is stated in Tur that we are concerned for exposure even in one hundred se'ah of water, and it is impossible that there not be sixty even if a snake drank from it — end of his words; and so it is written in Maharil — a flask of fish fell into a pot of meat and was cooked there, and Maharil forbade everything because of danger — end of his words; it implies sixty does not help, for danger is more stringent than prohibition; and Rashal chapter 35 siman 15 wrote that even lechatchilah one need not be stringent in a large oven regarding this, even though regarding the prohibition of meat and milk we are stringent lechatchilah; and therefore it appears plain to me that those breads they bake in one oven with meat — there is no danger at all to eat them with fish even if the oven was sealed.

<b>רק כשמבשלם יחד. </b>וה"ה אם נצלו כא' ממש זה עם זה כן איתא בגמ' בפ' כיצד צולין להדיא דאמר מר בר רב אשי אסרה אפילו במילחא ופי' רש"ל פ' ג"ה סי' ט"ו אליבא דהרמב"ם דשם מיירי שצלו דג עם בשר ממש ע"כ אסרו משום סכנת נפשות אבל לא כפרש"י דמיירי בתנור א' דבזה אין סכנה אבל נלע"ד כיון דלרש"י קאי על צליית בשר ודג בתנור יש להחמיר אפילו בריח ותו דהא כתב ב"י סי' צ"ה דרמב"ם מפרש ג"כ ההיא דכיצד צולין בתנור אחד וא"כ אסור למר בר רב אשי אפילו משום ריח משום סכנה כמו לענין בשר בחלב ומ"מ אין זה דומה ללחמים שאופין בתנור אחד עם בשר שזכרתי בסמוך שאין שם רק ריח בשר בלחם ובטל בו ואין אח"כ חשש לאכול עם דג. וכתב באו"ה כלל ל"ט דיש לאסור אם נצלו יחד בלתי מחבת תחת כלי אחד או אף אם בתנור סמוכין זו לזו דברוב פעמים יוצא מזו לזו השמנונית עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****Only when cooking them together. And the same applies if they roasted as one literally one with the other; so it is stated in the Gemara in the chapter How One Roasts explicitly — Mar bar Rav Ashi forbade even in salt; and Rashal chapter 35 siman 15 explained according to Rambam that there it deals with fish roasted with meat literally, therefore they forbade because of danger to life; but not like Rashi who deals with one oven — in that there is no danger. But in my humble opinion, since for Rashi it applies to roasting meat and fish in an oven, one should be stringent even regarding odor; and further Beit Yosef wrote siman 95 that Rambam also explains that which How One Roasts deals with one oven; and if so Mar bar Rav Ashi is forbidden even because of odor because of danger like meat and milk; nevertheless this is not comparable to breads baked in one oven with meat that I mentioned nearby, where there is only meat odor in bread and it is nullified, and there is no afterward concern to eat with fish. And Issur VeHeter wrote klal 39 that one must forbid if they roasted together without a pan under one vessel, or even if in an oven they are adjacent to one another, for in most cases grease passes from one to the other — end of his words.

<b>בשעת התקופה. </b>כתוב בד"מ בשם אבודרהם מצאתי כתוב שיש ליזהר מלשתות מים בשעת התקופה משום סכנה שלא יתנזק ויתנפח והטעם כי טיפת דם נופלת בין תקופה לתקופה אבל החכם אבן עזרא השיב על זה כי ניחוש בעלמא הוא כו' ואין בו סכנה כלל ויש מהגאונים אמרו כי לא נחש ביעקב אלא הקדמונים אמרו אלה הדברים להפחיד בני אדם שייראו מאת השם יתברך וישובו כדי שיצילם השם יתברך מארבע תקופות השנה עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****At the time of the tekufah. It is written in Darkei Moshe in the name of Abudraham: I found written that one must be careful not to drink water at the time of the tekufah because of danger lest one be harmed and swell; and the reason is that a drop of blood falls between tekufah and tekufah; but the sage Ibn Ezra answered on this that it is mere superstition, etc., and there is no danger at all; and some of the Geonim said there is no divination in Jacob — rather the ancients said these things to frighten people that they should fear God, blessed be He, and repent so that God, blessed be He, would save them from the four seasons of the year — end of his words.

<b>ולא בסופו. </b>כיון דכבר איתחזיק הדבר ח"ו ועוד איתא בגמרא פרק הכונס דבר בעיר אל יהלך אדם באמצע הדרכים מפני שמלאך המות יש לו רשות מסגי להדיא שלום בעיר אל יהלך בצדי הדרכים מפני שאין לו רשות והוא מחבא את עצמו וכתב רש"ל ונראה דסיפא מיירי דווקא בלילה שביום לא שכיחי מזיקים אבל רישא לענין דבר אין חילוק בין יום ללילה:
**** ENGLISH ****And not at its end. Since the matter has already become established, God forbid; and further it is stated in the Gemara chapter HaKones: when plague is in the city, a person should not walk in the middle of the roads because the angel of death has permission and walks openly; when peace is in the city, he should not walk at the sides of the roads because he has no permission and hides himself. And Rashal wrote: it appears the latter clause deals specifically with night, for by day harmful forces are not common; but the former clause regarding plague — there is no distinction between day and night.

דהא יש סם המות לאדם ונ"ל דשם באותן חילוקים דקא חשיב דהיינו אחוזת הדם כו' בכולהו ראינו הריעותא משום שאנו רואין בה חולי וכמ"ש רש"י שם שחלתה והוא מוכח דאל"כ מנא ידעינן שאירע לה כך משא"כ במים מגולים אין אנו רואים שום חולי אחר השתייה אלא יש איזה שעות שהיא בריאה אחר השתייה כמו לפני השתייה ומזה אנו אומרים בודאי לא שתה נחש מאותן מים מגולים דכל שיש סם המות ודאי לא היתה בריאה אח"כ ובזה דוקא אמרו מותרת אבל כאן דלא ישקנה הוא החשש דשמא ישחטנה אחר השתייה תכף ואפשר שיש בה סכנת נפשות וזה מכוון בלשון רש"י שאמר שמא ישחטנה [אחרי כן] דהוא יתור לשון אלא דנתכוין למה שכתבתי שישחטנה אח"כ כלומר אחר השתייה ולשון כן הוא מורה על הסמיכות ודבר זה הוא כפתור ופרח אבל התוספות בפרק הגוזל פירשו הטעם דלא ישקה לבהמה מים מגולים משום בל תשחית ומ"ה אפילו בהמה טמאה לא ישקה וכתב רש"ל שם מכאן מי שיש לו כלב רע שמזיקו ומפסיד המאכל אפילו אינו נושך שיכול ליתן לו סם המות ואינו עובר משום צער בעלי חיים דהא לא אסרו התוספות אלא משום בל תשחית והכא ליכא ומ"מ שמעתי למחות שלא יתן להם מחט בתוך הלחם דאיכא למיחש שמא ישתגע ואיכא סכנות נפשות טובא עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****Included in "Do not abhor." Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that for this they would administer lashes of rebellion; and one may say: why should he not be liable for full lashes? It appears because it is a negative commandment stated in general terms, for many matters are included in it, and every negative commandment stated in general terms one is not flogged for, as stated in the chapter Four Deaths. I found in an old collection — these are days of great danger to let blood for kabbalah according to Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid: alef-alef-tav, that is Rosh Chodesh Nisan, Elul, Tevet; when the eighth day of one of these falls on Monday or Wednesday; and likewise one who slaughters a goose on that day or eats from it is in danger, God forbid. And in another collection I found likewise, only that in place of the first day of Tevet it is written the fifteenth of Tevet when it falls on Sunday, that is Rosh Chodesh Tevet that falls on Shabbat, and that is the first day of Rosh Chodesh. We further find that the Sages forbade because of danger not to cut down a fruit-bearing tree, as stated in the chapter Gozel (daf 91) — Rav said: this palm tree that bears a kav is forbidden to cut; and Rav Chanina said: Shibchas bar did not die except that he cut a fig tree not in its time; and we say there that Rav Chisda saw a palm standing among vines — he said to his attendant: go uproot them, meaning they cause harm to the nourishment of the earth for the vines, and they are more important; and Rosh wrote there: and likewise if he needed its place it is permitted — end of his words) and from this I permitted someone who had land with trees to cut the trees even though they have fruit in order to build a dwelling house on it; and I wondered that Tur did not bring this law anywhere regarding cutting the aforementioned tree, only Semag siman 229 brought it. Rashal wrote in the last chapter of Gozel siman 37 on what is stated in the Gemara regarding uncovered beverages that were not strained, that it is forbidden to give them to his animal to drink, and Rashi explained the reason — because of danger lest he slaughter it afterward and eat it; and from this they practiced to forbid sheep in which grows a type of scab of nega that are accustomed to die in them; and in days of drought I saw that the community made a ban and forbade all sheep until the plague ceased — end of his words. And one should wonder: behold we said in siman 60 that even if the animal ate its own deadly poison it is kosher, and it implies there is no prohibition at all because of danger, except specifically in eating the deadly poison of a person; and it is possible there is in any case prohibition from the aspect that it was dangerous, as Beit Yosef wrote there in the name of Rashba that it is like dangerous and kosher, and that is according to what Tur wrote at the end of this siman and so in Shulchan Aruch — a person of refined spirit should not eat dangerous food; but it is difficult why he should learn this from what Rashi wrote that there is danger when it drank bad water, namely exposed water — there was danger also for a person because of snake venom; and Maharshal challenged from that which we say in siman 60 that if it drank bad water, namely exposed, it is permitted; and here forbidden according to Rashi mentioned above because of danger. And it appears to me to analyze further: there we say regarding the deadly poison of a person that it is forbidden because of danger — and why is it permitted in bad water, for behold there is deadly poison for a person? And it appears to me that there in those distinctions he lists, namely seizure of blood, etc. — in all of them we see harm because we see illness in it, and as Rashi wrote there that it became ill, and this is proven; for otherwise how would we know that this happened to it? Not so in exposed water — we do not see any illness after drinking, rather there are some hours when it is healthy after drinking as before drinking; and from this we say with certainty that surely a snake did not drink from that exposed water, for whenever there is deadly poison it would certainly not be healthy afterward; and only in that case they said it is permitted. But here in "he should not give it to drink" — the concern is lest he slaughter it immediately after drinking, and it is possible there is danger to life; and this fits Rashi's language that he said "lest he slaughter it afterward," that is after drinking; and the language "afterward" indicates proximity, and this matter is like a bolt and flies away; but Tosafot in the chapter Gozel explained the reason that one should not give an animal exposed water because of bal tashchit; and Maharshal wrote there: from here one who has a bad dog that harms and spoils food, even if it does not bite, may give it deadly poison and he does not violate tza'ar ba'alei chayim, for Tosafot forbade only because of bal tashchit, and here there is none; nevertheless I heard to protest not to put a needle in bread for them, for there is concern lest it go mad, and there is great danger to life — end of his words.
