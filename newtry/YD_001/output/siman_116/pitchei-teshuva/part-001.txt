<b>בשר ודג. </b>עיין בתשובת שבות יעקב ח"ב סי' ק"ד שנשאל על שומן אווז שנתערב תוך תבשיל של דגים אם יש חשש סכנה בדבר או לא דאולי אין סכנה רק בבשר בהמה משא"כ עופות שנבראו מהרקק וברגליהם יש להם קשקשת כדגים והשיב דאין חילוק והכי מוכח מדברי הפוסקים דלעיל סי' ט' הובא בבה"ט שם סק"ד ע"ש. ועמ"ש לעיל סי' פ"ז ס"ט:
**** ENGLISH ****
"Meat and fish." See in responsum of Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 104, where he was asked about goose fat that became mixed in a fish dish — whether there is concern of danger or not, for perhaps danger applies only to animal meat, unlike fowl created from mucus that have scales on their legs like fish — and he answered there is no distinction, and this is proven from the words of the poskim above in siman 9 brought in Ba'er Heitev there s.k. 4 — see there; and see what is written above siman 87 seif 9.

"B'dieved." Avodat HaTaharah regarding a matter involving danger — whether it is nullified in sixty; and see in responsum of Chavot Yair siman 64 that he ruled to be lenient that it is nullified; and so wrote in responsum of Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 104 that one should not be stringent at all — see there; and likewise in responsum of Chut HaShani siman 67 that specifically snake venom is not nullified as the Tur wrote that we are concerned for exposure even in one hundred se'ah of water, but a matter that is not venom even though it involves danger is nullified in sixty — see there. And what the Taz brought as proof from Maharil that a small fish fell from a fish into a meat pot and Maharil forbade everything because of danger — see there — already the author of Shach in Nekudot HaKesef and in responsum of Chut HaShani there explained that there was not sixty, therefore he forbade everything — see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that nevertheless the Taz properly proved that if you say where there is sixty it is permitted, therefore Maharil forbade because there was not sixty — it would be asked why he did not command to add until sixty so the taste of fish would be nullified; for although in prohibitions it is forbidden to add because of nullifying prohibition l'chatchila as stated in siman 99 seif 6, nevertheless here certainly nullifying prohibition does not apply, for this is not a prohibition but only so long as the taste of fish is sensed in the meat pot there is danger, and when they add and no taste of fish is sensed in the meat pot there is no longer danger (and see chapter 8 of Terumot mishnah 4 and 5 and investigate and it can be reconciled) [and see in Magen Avraham siman 455 s.k. 15 and in responsum of Binyan Olam ch. Orach Chayim siman 27]. Also squeezing out applies only if absorbed in a piece and not in a wet matter. Or that even where there is sixty it is forbidden; nevertheless for practical law the main view is as the responsa above that it is nullified [also in responsum of Chavot Shalom siman 101 he wrote this is the main view; and there he was asked about fish broth poured into a fat vessel and after it cooled they removed the congealed fat — what is the law of the broth and fish; he answered that in prohibitions in such a case we estimate sixty against the whole and removing the congealed prohibition does not help, Yoreh De'ah siman 98 s.k. 16 — if so apparently all the more here regarding danger that regarding doubt danger is stricter than prohibition for all. However, since we see in Rambam that Rav Geviha in medical and natural matters omitted the case of fish roasted with meat, presumably according to his great wisdom he knew and understood that natures changed in this matter; and although we do not rely on this to act and eat them at will, for perhaps it is like a matter forbidden by vote, nevertheless we gain that we emerged from stringency of danger, for now in any case there is no danger. And perhaps even in their time the danger was only in a species of fish and not in species found among us at all, and there is only a light rabbinic prohibition; and even in a Torah prohibition Raavad holds it suffices to estimate a good measure, etc.; and furthermore perhaps through pouring that is only absorbing and emitting they did not decree even in their time; and behold we practice to be lenient to eat meat in a fish vessel even in the same meal through mere rinsing — evidently we establish there is no danger in mere mixing of tastes. Nevertheless regarding congealed fat one should investigate somewhat — perhaps if one breaks a piece of hard fat one will find some of the broth moisture between the layers and then one must certainly forbid; but if it cannot be determined it appears one need not be so stringent — end of his words there]. And see in Sefer Tiferet Moshe that he wrote in the name of his grandfather the gaon Mahar"r Yaakov, z"l, that if a fish fell into a pot with meat or the reverse he would forbid the vessel without hagalah, for although one cooks fish in a meat vessel or the reverse — that is because the noten ta'am coming from the pot lacks power to create venom, but when meat and fish were cooked together in the pot, behold venom was cooked within it and absorbed into the pot and it is forbidden — see there. [And see in Sefer Chacham Tzvi that he wrote if a deadly fish called spiny fell into food or drink, Beit Lechem Yehudah wrote sixty does not help because the reason is venom; and see in Magen Avraham siman 316 s.k. 23 that he wrote one in a thousand that is dangerous in food — perhaps one may rely and permit at least in sixty; and I also found in Sefer HaBrit that they have no venom at all — see there.]

"Leading to danger." Avodat HaTaharah of Maharai"t — what the Sages forbade not to cut down a fruit tree, etc. [and in Ba'er Heitev below s.k. 8] — and see in responsum of Bashemim Rosh siman 334 that he wrote that when he needs its place it appears permitted, for Rambam said the essence of its prohibition is specifically through destruction, and perhaps if he needs its place it is not called destruction and one should not be stringent through a gentile at all — end of his words there; and see in She'eilat Yaavetz vol. 1 siman 76 what he wrote on this. [And at the end of his words he wrote it is plain that if one uproots a tree with its roots and with the nourishing earth so it can live from it and plants it elsewhere there is no prohibition at all, for plainly this is not cutting and it was not forbidden in any manner, for behold it is as planted in its place, as we establish regarding orlah in a tree uprooted with its rock if it can live it is exempt from orlah — all the more here — see there; and see in responsum of Chavot Shalom siman 102 that he wrote on him: I would not act according to him leniently — that not for need at all I would not permit even to uproot and plant elsewhere; but for stringency such as for need of its place, nevertheless where it suffices, and perhaps uprooting with the root to plant elsewhere — it is forbidden to cut, for so it appears from precision of the language of the Gemara in Bava Kamma — see there.] And see in responsum of Tzemach Tzedek siman 41 that he wrote if the tree harms vines one may uproot it — see there [and in responsum of Chavot Shalom there he elaborated on cutting a fruit tree and concludes: anything not clear that the profit of cutting exceeds the fruit — "a fruit-bearing tree may not be cut" — and there is danger even in doubt; but if there is profit even only for need of place to build houses in our time and in our country it is certainly preferable to a palm tree that it is permitted; but if one can uproot with its roots and a clod of earth with them so it can live and can plant them elsewhere — it is forbidden to cut them — see there]. And what Ba'er Heitev wrote further "he should not wash, etc." — see in responsum in Orach Chayim siman 4 s.k. 7 that he wrote so it appears in Sedei Chemed; but in our country they are not concerned for exposure at all even to rinse the mouth, and all the more for washing hands — see there. And see in responsum of Adnei Paz siman 25 and siman 34 that he wrote one may call two sons by one name whether one died or both live; and there in the omissions he wrote that nevertheless one should be concerned because of ayin hara, and if one died one should not call the second by that name because of ruach ra'ah — see there. And it appears plain that also with a gentile who is not his brother ruach ra'ah applies; and perhaps from this reason "the name of the wicked shall rot" — we do not mention their names because of ruach ra'ah. However, in Yoma daf 38 it does not quite appear so — see there. And see in the will of R' Yehudah he-Chasid many matters to beware of because of danger. And what he wrote there that one should not marry a woman whose name is like his mother's or whose name is like his father-in-law's — see in responsum of Chochmat Adam general rule 123 law 13 that it applies specifically when they are tripled, such as a man named Reuven and his son-in-law named Reuven, and if this man takes another son-in-law named Reuven — then he will not succeed; and likewise a woman named Rivkah, etc.; and so explicitly in Sefer Chasidim siman 477, and the world errs in this — end of his words; and see in responsum of Noda biYehudah second edition part Even HaEzer siman 79 that he wrote all his will was only for his seed after him and not for other people, and even for his seed there is concern only in the essential name given at birth or at circumcision but not in a name changed during illness — see there further that he protested against most of the world who give their daughters to gentiles without concern (see below siman 217 s.k. 76 in the name of Chavot Yair) and this is against the words of the Sages and one should heed the will of R' Yehudah he-Chasid [and see on this in Pit'hei Teshuvah to Even HaEzer siman 2 s.k. 7 and siman 50 s.k. 14].
