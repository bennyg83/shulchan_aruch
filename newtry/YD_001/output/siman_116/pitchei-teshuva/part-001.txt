**** ENGLISH ****B'dieved. See Baer Heitev regarding whether a matter involving danger is nullified in sixty; and see in responsum Chavot Yair siman 64 that he ruled to be lenient that it is nullified; and so wrote in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 104 that one should not be stringent at all — see there; and so in responsum Chut HaShani siman 67 — specifically snake venom is not nullified as Tur wrote that we are concerned for exposure even in forty se'ah of water; but a matter that is not venom, even though it involves danger, is nullified in sixty — see there. And that which Turei Zahav brought proof from Maharil — a flask of fish fell into a pot of meat and he forbade everything, Maharil, because of danger — see there; they already challenged him in Shach in Nekudat HaKesef and in responsum Chut HaShani there — one may say there were not sixty, therefore he forbade everything — see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that nevertheless Turei Zahav proved well: for if you say that where there are sixty it is permitted, and therefore Maharil forbade because there were not sixty — it would be asked why he did not command to add until sixty so the fish taste would be nullified; for even though in prohibitions it is forbidden to add because of nullifying a prohibition lechatchilah as stated in siman 99 seif 6, nevertheless here certainly nullifying a prohibition does not apply, for this is not a prohibition — only as long as they perceive fish taste in the pot of meat there is danger; and when they add and fish taste is no longer perceived in the pot of meat, there is no longer danger (and see chapter 8 of Terumot mishnah 4 and 5, and understand well; and there is to resolve) [and see in Magen Avraham siman 495 s.k. 15 and in responsum Binyan Olam part 1 siman 27]. Also squeezing applies only if absorbed in a piece, not in something moist. Or that even where there are sixty it is forbidden; nevertheless the law follows those responsa that it is nullified [also in responsum Chut HaShani siman 101 he wrote that this is the main ruling, and there he was asked about fish broth that was poured into a vessel of fat, and after it cooled they removed the congealed fat — what is the law of the broth and fish; and he answered: behold in prohibitions in such a case we require estimating sixty against the whole, and it does not help that they removed the prohibition that congealed — Shach siman 98 s.k. 16; and if so, all the more so here regarding danger, for regarding safek danger is more stringent than prohibition for all — investigate. In truth, since we see from Rambam that Rav Geviriya in remedies and natural matters omitted this matter of fish roasted with meat, presumably according to the depth of his wisdom he knew and understood that natures changed in this matter; and although we should not rely on this to act and eat them one after the other, for perhaps it is like a matter forbidden by count, nevertheless we have gained that we emerge from the stringency of danger, for now at least there is no danger. And it is possible that even in their time the danger was only in a certain type of fish and not in types found among us at all, and there is only a light rabbinic prohibition; for although a matter forbidden by count from the Torah is forbidden as a fence and guard of a prohibition, in such a case it is very light. And even in a Torah prohibition Raavad holds it suffices to estimate well, etc.; and further it is possible that through pouring, which is only swallowing and emitting, they did not decree on this even in their time; and behold we practice to be lenient to eat meat in a fish vessel, Beit Yosef, even at the same meal through mere rinsing — evidently we hold there is no danger in mere mingling of tastes. Nevertheless regarding the congealed fat one should investigate somewhat: perhaps if one breaks a piece of congealed fat he will find some of the broth water between the layers, and then it is certainly forbidden; but if one cannot determine, it appears one need not be so stringent — end of his words there. And see in Sefer Tiferet Lemoshe that he wrote in the name of his grandfather the gaon Rabbi Yaakov, of blessed memory: when a fish fell into a pot with meat or vice versa, he would forbid the vessel without hagalah, for although they cook fish in a meat vessel or vice versa, that is because the forbidden taste emerging from the pot does not create venom; but when meat and fish were cooked together in the pot, venom was cooked in it and absorbed without sixty in the pot and it is forbidden — see there. [And see in Sefer Chacham Tzvi that he wrote: if a deadly creature called shafin fell into food or beverage — Beit Lechem Yehudah wrote that sixty does not help, for the reason is venom; and see in Magen Avraham siman 216 s.k. 23 that he wrote that one in a thousand that is dangerous in food — perhaps one may rely and permit at least in sixty; and I also found in Sefer HaBrit that they have no venom at all — see there.]

[And at the end of his words he wrote that plainly if one uproots a tree with its roots and with the earth of its nourishment so it can live from it and plants it elsewhere, there is no prohibition here at all, for plainly this is not cutting and it was not forbidden in any manner, for behold it is as planted in its place, and as we hold regarding orlah in a tree uprooted and the rock with it — if it can live it is exempt from orlah, all the more so here — see there; and see in responsum Chut HaShani siman 102 that he wrote on this: I will not act according to him to be lenient that not for need at all I would not permit even to uproot and replant elsewhere; but to be stringent, such as for need of its place, nevertheless where it suffices; and it is possible with uprooting with the root to plant elsewhere it is forbidden to cut, for so it is implied from the precise language of the Gemara in Bava Kamma — see there.] And see in responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 41 that he wrote: if the tree harms the vines, he may uproot it — see there [and in responsum Chut HaShani there he elaborated on cutting down a fruit tree and concluded: whatever is not clear that the gain of cutting exceeds the fruit — the tree complains and it is forbidden to cut it, and there is danger even in safek; but if there is gain, even only where needed to build houses in our time and in our country — certainly preferable to a palm tree it is permitted; but if one can uproot with its roots and a clod of earth with them so it can live and one can plant them elsewhere, it is forbidden to cut them — see there]. And what Baer Heitev wrote further: one should not wash, etc. — see in responsum Orach Chaim siman 4 s.k. 7 that he wrote that so it appears in Sdei Chemed; but in our country they are not concerned for exposure at all, even for rinsing the mouth, and all the more for washing hands — see there. And see in responsum Adnei Paz siman 25 and siman 34 that he wrote one may call two sons by one name whether one died or both are alive; and there in the omissions he wrote that nevertheless one should be concerned for ayin hara; and if one died one should not call the second by that name because of bad fortune — see there. And it appears plain that also with a gentile who is not his brother bad fortune applies; and perhaps from this reason "the names of the wicked shall rot" — we do not mention their names because of bad fortune. However in Yoma daf 38 it is not quite implied thus — see there. And see in the will of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid how many matters one must be careful about because of danger. And what he wrote there: one should not marry a woman whose name is like his mother's or whose name is like his father-in-law's — see in responsum Chachmat Adam klal 123 din 13 that he wrote this is specifically when they are tripled, such as a man named Reuven and his son-in-law named Reuven, and if he takes another son-in-law named Reuven — then he will not succeed; and likewise a woman named Rivkah, etc.; and so explicitly in Shulchan HaTahor siman 472; and the world errs in this — end of his words; and see in responsum Noda BiYehudah second edition part Even HaEzer siman 79 that he wrote all his will was only for his seed after him and not for other people; and even for his seed there is concern only for the essential name given at birth or at circumcision, but not for a name changed at illness — and see there further that he protested against most of the world who give their daughters to a gentile without concern (see below siman 217 s.k. 76 in the name of Chavot Yair), and this is against the words of the Sages; and to the will of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid they are concerned [and see on this in Pithei Teshuva to Even HaEzer siman 2 s.k. 7 and siman 50 s.k. 14].
