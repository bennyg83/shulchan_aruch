**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"Sitting in a sukkah is upon me." </b>One should not raise the difficulty that sitting in a sukkah is an intangible thing. Tosafos answered that it is only considered intangible when he does not mention the object — since he is not speaking of something concrete. But when he mentions the prohibited object, it is a tangible thing. Accordingly, above in siman 234 seif 1, when she says "may my hands be consecrated to their Maker" — it is not that she literally said this, but rather she is considered as if she said it, since she mentioned her hands. So the Ran wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Upon me through an oath. </b>The Maharshal wrote: From here it is implied that the terminology of an oath is effective when expressed in the terminology of a vow, and it would have been effective had an oath taken effect regarding a mitzvah, end quote. His intent is that although at the end of siman 206 there is a dispute regarding this, and the Tur wrote there in the name of the Rosh that a vow expressed in the terminology of an oath, such as, “I vow that I will not eat,” is ineffective, nevertheless here an oath expressed in the terminology of a vow is effective. So too is ruled below in seif 11.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he is prohibited from meat for forty-two days. </b>This is the view of the Tur. The Beis Yosef challenged him: what is different from one who says "I shall not eat these nine," etc. in siman 238 seif 12 — where if he ate one more he violated the oath? Therefore he concluded here as well: "This does not appear correct to me," etc. <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="3"></i>In my humble opinion, the Tur's words are well-founded. For in truth there are two possible interpretations of the second oath: it is possible that regarding those twenty days he already swore about, he swore again about them, now including them together with two additional days. And it is possible that he is speaking of entirely different days. Therefore we say that he intended both in all cases — that is to say, he will be prohibited for that many days total. Similarly above in every case — if he says "ten I shall not eat" — since we hold in siman 208 that unspecified vows are ruled stringently, therefore he is obligated above for ten, and here for forty-two days. However, it appears obvious that if he explicitly states what his intention was, there is no dispute — all agree we follow his explanation. Regarding the halachah, one should be stringent like the view of the Tur and he is prohibited for forty-two days.
