**** ENGLISH ****
One who swears about a matter that has no substance, or to nullify a mitzvah. It contains 17 se'ifim:

The law of an oath is like the law of a vow in every matter—whether regarding that he must utter it with his lips; whether regarding that he intended to say wheat bread and said barley bread or bread without specification; whether we follow the language of people; whether regarding establishing it and annulling it; whether regarding a condition. And some say that just as vows of urging, empty vows, vows under compulsion, and vows in error are permitted, the same law applies regarding an oath—provided that when he swore an empty oath he does not stand by his words to say he certainly saw on that road something like the Egyptians, for if so it is forbidden. And some say that empty oaths are forbidden in every case. And from the words of the Rambam it appears that oaths of urging are forbidden. And also in other oaths it appears from his words that even though he is exempt from a korban, nevertheless there is a prohibition.

{Rama: A Jew who stole from a gentile and they obligated him to swear in the presence of other Jews, and they know he will swear falsely—they may compel him to compromise with the gentile and not swear falsely, even if he would be under compulsion regarding the oath, since there is desecration of the Name in his oath. (Responsum of Maharam and Hagahot Ashiri beginning of chapter Shevuat HaEidut.) And if he was under compulsion and there is no desecration of the Name in the matter, he annuls the oath in his heart, since he is compelled in the oath, as above siman 232. (Opinion.)}

Since vows in error are permitted—if he swore that he will not eat this loaf if so-and-so said this thing, and it was found that he said it, he is permitted the loaf.
