**** ENGLISH ****
Field mouse, etc. Shach wondered: whence for Shulchan Aruch to say field mouse certainly imparts beneficial taste in beer and vinegar — in the Gemara we only say that since it is served on kings' tables it is not repulsive; but what does that have to do with noten ta'am lifgam? One can say the Beit Yosef wondered why the Gemara brought this pilpul: whether field mouse is repulsive or served on kings' tables — for the law there is no difference, for a mouse itself is forbidden, and for emission too there is no difference, for even city mouse if it fell where it imparts beneficial taste it is forbidden, and in the field likewise one eats taste whether it imparts beneficial taste or spoils; therefore we are uncertain whether it imparts beneficial taste or spoils in itself — if in substance it spoils, one can say its emission's taste also spoils; this is the safek in the Gemara whether it imparts beneficial taste; if like its substance so its emission, then field mouse that in substance is praised, its taste too has no safek that it is highly praised; therefore the Gemara brought it and his words are correct.

And regarding whether it is beriah: know Rambam and Rashba disagree on the law of separated limbs of sheratzim that impart tumah, the eight sheratzim in the Torah; Rambam chapter 2 of Forbidden Foods: after their death, one who eats a limb from them nevertheless its measure is like a lentil like eating from sheretz; there is no distinction between in their quantity or limbs; and so seems Tosafot Avodah Zarah regarding bee legs; therefore a limb from them is not called beriah, for it needs measure like sheretz; one cannot say "do not eat" between large and small.
