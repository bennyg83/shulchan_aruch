**** ENGLISH ****
The law of a mouse found in wine or beer. In it are 3 seifim: A field mouse imparts beneficial taste, for it is served on kings' tables; but a city mouse — we are uncertain whether it imparts beneficial taste in beer and vinegar or whether it spoils; and therefore if it fell into beer or vinegar while cold and they removed it whole, if it did not remain inside twenty-four hours it is permitted; but if it was boiling, or even cold and remained inside twenty-four hours — whether they removed it whole, whether it was cut into fine pieces and one can strain it in a sieve so nothing remains, whether it dissolved completely and became entirely liquid and no substance remained — it is permitted by having sixty in the heter against the mouse; and if it was cut into fine pieces and cannot be strained, such as when the beer or vinegar was mixed into thick food — all is forbidden and there is no nullification, for we fear one may encounter the substance of the issur and not notice: {Rama: and specifically for sheretz we are concerned if something remains that cannot be removed; but for other issurim there is no concern (so it appears in Arukh general rule 32).}

If it fell into wine, oil, or other liquids — it certainly spoils and one does not need sixty to nullify its emission: {Rama: and accordingly if it fell into fat the law is the same; and some are stringent in fat, even if hard before us: if they pour fat daily into the pot, there is room to suspect the mouse was there when they heated boiling fat on it — all is forbidden, even if there is sixty in the fat against the whole mouse; and if they did not heat on it, or heated and it is known the mouse was not there when they heated and the fat came before us hard (responsum of Rosh, Hagahot Maimonides chapter 15, Hagahot Mordechai on Chullin, Hagahat Sh"D, and Arukh) — and likewise if the mouse is found on it, removal of place suffices and we do not establish issur lest the fat was soft when it fell there, for pickling is like cooking and permitted from double safek: safek it fell when hard; and if you say when soft, perhaps it hardened before pickling. And where the fat is repulsive to eat, it is forbidden to light it in synagogue because of "bring it to your fire" (Ran in the name of a Gaon, Rashba Pesachim, R' Yerucham, and Or Zarua in the name of Mordechai Pesachim, and the author in Orach Chaim siman 124 seif 12). And where there is great loss one may rely on the lenient words.}

**** ENGLISH ****
Repulsive things at which a person's soul is disgusted, such as ants, flies, and mosquitoes that every person separates from because of their repulsiveness — even if they were mixed into a dish and their bodies were crushed into it, if the heter is greater they are permitted; and nevertheless, wherever one can check and pass through a sieve, one checks and strains (however in vinegar and beer there is concern like with a mouse) (Beit Yosef according to Rashba).
