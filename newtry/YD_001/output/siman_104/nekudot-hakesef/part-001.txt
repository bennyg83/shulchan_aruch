**** ENGLISH ****
(Shulchan Aruch siman 104, Taz s.k. 1) He expanded to challenge Rama and his supporters; all his words are surprising. At the root of his foundation he wondered: in the laws of beriah we said in siman 100 regarding an ant that if part was crushed the importance is nullified, etc. — great wonder at his wonder, for Rama and his supporters' reason is that a mouse is a sheretz and its tumah is like a lentil and one is liable for eating it like a lentil; this does not apply to an ant, for it is simple in Mishnah and Gemara and poskim that only the eight sheratzim, including the mouse, have measure like a lentil, but not other sheratzim; and in any case there is no question from ant to mouse according to what is written below that even like a lentil from a mouse is nullified dry in dry; and what he wrote is also written in Or Zarua general rule 25 law 2 — like a lentil of the eight sheratzim, and a crushed sheretz, etc., even though one is liable on them, they are nullified — he was not precise, for Or Zarua deals explicitly with dry in dry, and in that certainly Rama agrees it is nullified dry in dry; and Rama's intent is not that like a lentil from sheretz is not nullified dry in dry because of beriah, but here the law is wet in wet since it mixed in liquid; but if so why forbidden — it should be nullified with sixty against it; rather certainly this case is different since it is like beriah, for one is liable d'oraisa on like a lentil, therefore the rabbis were more stringent and we fear lest one encounter the substance of like a lentil and come to d'oraisa liability as where they decreed on beriah that is not nullified; but in other issurim, since one is liable only on a kezayit, they did not decree; and so is explicit in Or Zarua general rule 32: if it crumbled, throw away the mouse and the taste is nullified in sixty like any taste of beriah; but if it crumbled and was cut in a way one cannot clarify and remove the issur, it is forbidden here lest a piece like a lentil remained and it is beriah; specifically in liquid; but if lost until unrecognizable, even a kezayit of the eight sheratzim is well nullified like other issurim in sixty after it is not whole, as stated above general rule 25 — end of his words; clearly they distinguish: only in liquid they fear lest a piece like a lentil remained and it is beriah; but dry in dry it is well nullified in sixty; and this is why poskim did not write the law of fearing lest one encounter issur substance except here with a mouse; and Rashi, Ra'avan, Mordechai, and Agudah all wrote here the reason: since sheretz's measure is like a lentil, etc., intending like Rama; and so is clear in Shach s.k. 3; and so Riaz in Shiltot Avodah Zarah daf 82 end 1: the mouse rotted and crumbled in liquids — these are forbidden even in more than sixty lest like a lentil from the sheretz enter the liquids and sheretz's measure is like a lentil — end; understand specifically for mouse we fear this, but not other issurim; and as Shach wrote there — clear proof from Rashba himself regarding a gnat that fell into a pot of soup: if a limb was cut from it, it is nullified; and so is proven in Shulchan Aruch siman 100 seif 3: a pot of soup into which a beriah fell and was lost — all is forbidden; implying specifically a whole beriah; if part was cut, it is permitted and we do not fear encountering issur substance — we certainly do not fear this except for mouse, one of the eight sheratzim, as I wrote. What he wrote is also proven from Tosafot who challenged: how do we eat bee honey when bee legs are mixed in the honey, etc. — nothing is proven, for Tosafot there challenge that our eyes see bee legs mixed in honey and we eat honey with bee legs; and furthermore bee legs are whole limbs, meaning like donkey bones; and what we say limbs have no measure, even less than a kezayit from a corpse and less than a lentil from sheretz — is for tumah and when there is flesh on them; otherwise they are pure; and for eating it is fine — end; with this his challenge from their words that they concluded "what we say limbs have no measure," etc., is rejected. And what he wrote is also proven from their words there that they concluded: and what we say limbs have no measure, even less than a kezayit from a corpse and less than a lentil from sheretz, etc. — learn that it deals with less than a lentil — he was not precise, for specifically for limbs there is no measure, and even less than a lentil — otherwise a lentil is required; and all this is clear.
