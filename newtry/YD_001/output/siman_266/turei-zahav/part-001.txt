And it appears to me this is the Rosh and Rif's reason — that certainly according to the Gemara's conclusion we circumcise in all aspects even without completion; only since if he were definitely eight-year-old we would not circumcise without completion since certainly he is not fit for circumcision and it does not help to say cutting flesh as the Tur wrote — "since he is like a stone why cut flesh not for need"; if so the primary permission is specifically in doubt; and for us doubt does not help, for perhaps what is doubt for us was certainty for Talmudic sages, as Tosafot wrote this reasoning in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah (Shabbos 135) s.v. ben shemonah, etc. — we are not expert and all are called doubt, etc.; even though they wrote this for permission regarding definite eight-year-old which for us does not exist — namely with completion as their words imply; but without completion it would be stricter from the reasoning we are not expert, as the Tur wrote siman 58 — "but if before us only that we are not expert in his hair — even rabbinically we do not rely to be lenient" — end quote; and likewise here — many times born bein hashemashot and we are not expert whether it was day or night, and perhaps in Talmudic sages' days they knew with certainty it was day or the opposite — so appears to me the Rif and Rosh's reason that they did not permit circumcising on Shabbat in doubtful eight-day-old unless completion, etc.; and this is Shulchan Aruch's opinion; and I do not know why Rama ruled leniently on Shabbat against Rif and Rosh's opinion; again I saw that Rama in Darkei Moshe wrote that Rif and Rosh also hold we circumcise from doubt and did not need to write it; and in Derishah he brought them — and it is a very remote way from several matters and no reasoning that it should be obvious to them; and in the Gemara they needed to say thus in the name of Rav Ada bar Ahavah; also in Derishah he disputed him. And so the meaning of Tosafot in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah that I mentioned — we require no deficiency at all in his nails and hair even in doubt — see there; and so Nekudot HaKesef in chapter HaErel — even with completion of his signs we would have to be stringent, only in this we rely on doubt; and so Rambam and Ritva — until here; and certainly it is not proper to be lenient at all against these to circumcise on Shabbat if his signs were not completed, as it appears to me; and so Maharshal ruled.
