**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To put it away in an enclosed courtyard. </b>The Beit Yosef permits this in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. Maharil, however, records that Mahari Segal instructed that immediately after the circumcision all the instruments should be removed from the mohel's hands, for they become muktzeh, and that he should not return to take the knife from the sand where he placed the foreskin while occupied with periah. The Maharshal also permits carrying it afterward, as the Rama rules here, because it was fit for use at twilight and there is no muktzeh for only part of Shabbos. Although I am not worthy to decide between these great authorities, <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>it appears clear to me that carrying it is forbidden. The Gemara in Beitzah teaches that a pestle, though permitted for cutting meat on Yom Tov, may not be carried after its permitted use has ended. The same applies to a roasting spit after the meat was roasted. A circumcision knife is comparable: once the mitzvah has been completed, it returns to its muktzeh status. The principle that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos applies where an item was fit at twilight, later became unfit, and then became fit again. Here it was fit at twilight only for the mitzvah itself, not for later carrying. Rabbeinu Yerucham's permission should therefore be understood narrowly: the knife may be placed safely in the same room so that it will not be lost, since otherwise a mohel might refrain from performing the circumcision. It may not be carried elsewhere. The same reasoning applies to a shofar after the blasts have been completed. Even before the circumcision, the knife may not be carried unnecessarily, but only for the needs of the mitzvah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Through roofs and courtyards. </b>This refers to utensils that were resting in those areas when Shabbos began. All roofs and courtyards are regarded as one domain for such utensils, even when they belong to different owners. The prohibition applies only to utensils that were inside a house when Shabbos began. Such utensils may not be carried from the house into the courtyard unless the houses joined in an eruv, and even after such an eruv they may not be carried from that courtyard to another courtyard that did not join with it.

<b>One who was born in the seventh month. </b>The Tur writes that he must be complete. The Beit Yosef understands this to mean complete in his limbs, while the completion of his hair and nails is not essential. I find this difficult, since no requirement of limb development is found elsewhere regarding circumcision. The Gemara identifies an eight-month infant by incomplete hair and nails, and the halacha follows that view. It therefore appears that even a definite seventh-month infant must show completion of these signs. The Tur's concise phrase, “complete,” means merely that no clear defect is visible; regarding an eight-month infant he requires clear completion of the hair and nails. The Tur later rules leniently even in a doubtful seventh-month case, based on the reasoning that if the infant is viable the circumcision is proper, and if not, cutting the flesh is not a prohibited wound. The Rif and Rosh, however, appear to require completed signs in a doubtful case before permitting circumcision on Shabbos. Their concern is that what is doubtful to us may have been ascertainable to the sages, and our lack of expertise should not create a leniency. Tosafos, the Nimukei Yosef, the Rambam, and the Ritva also indicate that the signs must be complete. One should therefore not circumcise such an infant on Shabbos unless the hair and nails are fully developed. This is also the conclusion of my revered father-in-law.

<b>אין מלין כו'. </b>שולד כותית היא כמותה וכתב בית יוסף בבדק הבית דישראלית מומרת שהולידה ממומר מלין בשבת דאע"פ שחטאו ישראלי' הם והא דנקט מומר שנשא ישראלית אתי לאפוקי כותית ולא משמע כן בטור שהרי כתבתי בסעיף י"ב משמו שאין מחזיקין שיצא לתרבות רעה כיון שאמו ישראלית ובזה שאמו מומרת ודאי מחזיקין אותו בכך ואין למולו בשבת ואע"ג דנותנין אותו לימול שמא אחר כן יחזירוהו לדרך שלהם כיון שהם נשארו כך ותו דאפשר שהם מומרים לכל התורה חוץ ממצות מילה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He may not be circumcised, etc. </b>The child of a non-Jewish woman follows her status. The Beit Yosef writes that the son of an apostate Jewish woman and an apostate Jewish man may be circumcised on Shabbos because, despite their sin, they remain Jews. This does not appear consistent with the Tur. The Tur's permission was based on the mother being observant enough that we do not presume the child will be raised outside Judaism. Where both parents remain apostates, there is substantial reason to presume that they will raise the child in their way, even if they presently permit circumcision. They may also be apostates regarding the entire Torah except for circumcision. Such a child should therefore not be circumcised on Shabbos.
