**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But one does not desecrate Shabbos for him in other matters.</b> The Darkei Moshe writes: It appears to me that the Rif certainly agrees with the Rosh and the Tur that one circumcises him in any event. When he writes that one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, he means that preservation of life does not override Shabbos for him, since his hair and nails were not fully formed. Nevertheless, one circumcises him because either way there is no prohibited labor: if he is a seven month child, he is viable, and if he is an eight month child, cutting him is merely like cutting flesh, as stated in the Tur. This reasoning does not apply to other forms of Shabbos desecration. The Gemara establishes the case regarding preparations for circumcision according to Rabbi Eliezer because the discussion there concerns circumcision, while the poskim derived the law of other desecrations from it for practical ruling. They may not have stated explicitly that he is circumcised either way because they relied on the phrase, one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, which implies only that other desecrations are prohibited, while circumcision itself is performed because either way it involves no Shabbos desecration, as the Tur states. The Semag likewise writes explicitly that one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, meaning that one does not remove rubble from upon him, even though one circumcises him. See also the Derishah and Bach.

<b>From a Jewish woman, etc.</b> The Bedek HaBayit writes: It appears to me that even if he is married to an apostate Jewish woman, a son born to them is circumcised on Shabbos, since they remain obligated in all the commandments. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>One circumcises him, etc.</b> The Bedek HaBayit cites the Rashbatz that a mamzer is circumcised on Shabbos, and Maharil writes likewise. The Bedek HaBayit further writes in the name of the Rambam that Karaites should be treated respectfully so long as they do not speak perversely against the rabbis or mock the words of our holy Sages of the Mishnah and Gemara. It is proper to greet them, even in their homes, and to circumcise their sons even on Shabbos when they observe our circumcision procedure, including milah and periah, since worthy descendants may emerge from them and return in repentance. Rav Hai Gaon also ruled this way. Rav Betzalel Ashkenazi, responsum 3, cites the Rambam and Rav Hai Gaon, and writes that the Karaites living among us in his time possessed none of the qualities mentioned by the Gaon. They continually deteriorated, did not properly circumcise their sons because they did not perform periah, and one who circumcises without periah is regarded as though he did not circumcise. They did not use our mohalim, did not admit Torah scholars into their homes, fled from them as from a snake, and did not pray according to our liturgy, including the Amidah. Since they differed from us in all these matters, it was forbidden to circumcise their sons on Shabbos. Because they did not accept periah, performing it for them was inappropriate, while circumcising without periah would desecrate Shabbos without fulfilling a mitzvah.

**** ENGLISH ****
The Bedek HaBayit cites a responsum of the Rashbatz concerning the teaching that one may not set sail fewer than three days before Shabbos. The Baal HaMaor explains that the reason is that the voyage will inevitably lead to Shabbos desecration, so it appears that the traveler stipulated in advance that Shabbos would be desecrated. Since the three days before Shabbos are considered connected to Shabbos, the Rashbatz derives that one may not circumcise a convert on Thursday, lest the third day after circumcision occur on Shabbos and require Shabbos desecration. Likewise, if a sick child recovered on Thursday, one should wait until Friday to circumcise him. These words do not appear correct. The Gemara, Shabbos 19a, states regarding setting sail that the prohibition applies only to an optional journey, while travel for a mitzvah is permitted. All the poskim agree, as explained in Orach Chaim, siman 248, and there is no greater mitzvah than circumcision. Furthermore, the Rashbatz necessarily maintains that only the third day after circumcision overrides Shabbos, and not an earlier day, which is why he permits circumcision on Friday. But according to the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchot Shabbos, citing some commentators, the Ramban, and the Rashba, the second day certainly also overrides Shabbos. The Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah, the Bartenura, and Rashi in the chapter Rabbi Akiva write likewise. According to that view, a circumcision not performed at its proper time that falls on Thursday would have to be postponed until after Shabbos, which is impossible. The Mishnah teaches that a child may be circumcised on the eighth through the twelfth day, but neither earlier nor later. If he was born at twilight and two festival days of Rosh Hashanah precede Shabbos, he is circumcised on the twelfth day. Yet if the two festival days fell on Tuesday and Wednesday, the twelfth day would be Thursday. According to the Rashbatz, since this circumcision is not at its proper time and does not itself override Shabbos, it should be postponed from Thursday until Sunday, making the fifteenth day. The Mishnah clearly excludes that possibility. Therefore, even a delayed circumcision may be performed on Thursday. Even those poskim who maintain that only the third day after circumcision overrides Shabbos may agree that a delayed circumcision is permitted on Thursday, as I have demonstrated from the view of most poskim. This appears to be the principal ruling.
