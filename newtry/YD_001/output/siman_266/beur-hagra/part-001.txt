<b>ומותר לטלטל כו'. </b>כמ"ש בפ"ג די"ט <small>(כ"ו ב') </small>אין מוקצה כו' אבל מהרי"ל כתב דמיד אחר המילה יזרוק האזמל מידו וד"מ כתב שנראה לו דברי רי"ו ואשתמיטתהו דברי תוספתא וירושלמי מפורש כדברי מהרי"ל שבתוספתא ספ"ב דסוכה רי"א י"ט הראשון של חג שחל להיות בשבת כיון שיצא י"ח בו אסור לטלטלו ובירושלמי ספ"ג שם הניחו בארץ אסור לטלטלו ולא שייך כאן אין מוקצה דבאמת מוקצה הוא אלא שמותר משום צורך מצוה וכן השיגו בט"ז ע"ש וגם מ"ש מהרי"ל שצריך לזרוק מידו הדין עמו ואע"ג דגבי מלאכתו לאיסור כשמטלטלו לצורך מקומו מותר להצניעו כמ"ש בא"ח סי' ש"ח ס"ג כאן אסור וראיה ממ"ש מספ"ב דשבת שמעתי שאם בא כו' מקום כו' <small>(ועמ"א סי' רס"ו ס"ק י"ט ובדברי רבינו שם) . </small>דדוקא בכלי שמלאכתו לאיסור שאינו מוקצה שמותר לטלטל לצורך גופו ומקומו רק מחמה לצל אסור דהוי שלא לצורך דאפי' ר"ש דל"ל מוקצה כלל מודה בזה כמ"ש תוס' ספ"ב דשבת שם דאינו מחמת מוקצה וכמו כלי שמלאכתו להיתר שלא לצורך כלל בזה מותר כיון שמטלטל לצורך משא"כ במה שאסור לטלטל אף לצורך מקומו וברייתא דשם אוקמוה כר' נחמיה ובזה מתורץ קושית תוס' שם ל"ה ב' ד"ה והתניא תימא כו' וליתא דאילו היה מותר לצורך גופו ומקומו היה מותר ג"כ להצניעו כנ"ל וז"ש בברייתא לפי שאין מטלטלין כו' ר"ל לגמרי כמ"ש תוס' שם ולפי' תוס' הוא מיותר אפי' היה מותר לטלטל כדברי ר"ש מ"מ אסור להצניע. ודלא כט"ז ומ"ש סופן משום תחלתן אינו ענין לכאן דלא אמרו אלא בשמחת י"ט ובירושלמי הנ"ל הניחו בארץ כו' ובי"ט מתירין להחזיר התריסין וליתן לפני הדורסן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is permitted to move, etc.</b> As stated in chapter 3 of Yom Tov <small>(26b)</small>, “There is no muktzeh,” etc. Maharil, however, wrote that immediately after the circumcision he should cast the circumcision knife from his hand. Darkei Moshe wrote that Rabbeinu Yerucham's view appeared correct to him, but the explicit words of the Tosefta and Yerushalmi, which agree with Maharil, escaped his notice. The Tosefta at the end of chapter 2 of Sukkah states: Rabbi Yehudah says that when the first day of Sukkos falls on Shabbos, once one has fulfilled his obligation with the lulav, it is forbidden to move it. Likewise, the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 there states: Once he placed it on the ground, it is forbidden to move it. The rule that there is no muktzeh is inapplicable here, for it is indeed muktzeh, and it was permitted only for the sake of the mitzvah. The Taz likewise challenged this ruling; see there. Maharil is also correct in writing that one must cast it from his hand. Although regarding a utensil whose primary function is prohibited, when one moves it because he needs its place he may carry it to a protected location, as stated in Orach Chaim, siman 308, se'if 3, here this is forbidden. Proof may be brought from the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos: “I heard that if one came,” etc., “to a place,” etc. <small>See Magen Avraham, siman 266, s.k. 19, and the words of the Gra there.</small> This applies specifically to a utensil whose primary function is prohibited, which is not inherently muktzeh and may be moved for use of the utensil itself or its place. It is forbidden only to move it from the sun to the shade, because that movement serves no permitted need. Even Rabbi Shimon, who generally rejects muktzeh, agrees to this, as Tosafos writes at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, because the prohibition is not due to muktzeh. It is comparable to moving a utensil whose primary function is permitted when there is no need at all. Here, however, carrying it onward is permitted because the initial movement was for a permitted need. This differs from an object that may not be moved even because one needs its place. The baraisa there was established according to Rabbi Nechemiah. This resolves Tosafos' question there, 35b, s.v. Vehatanya, “This is difficult,” etc. Their question does not stand, because if it were permitted to move the object for use of the object itself or its place, it would likewise be permitted to carry it to a protected location, as explained above. This is why the baraisa states, “because they may not move,” etc., meaning that they may not move it at all, as Tosafos writes there. According to Tosafos' explanation, that phrase is superfluous, for even if movement were permitted according to Rabbi Shimon, carrying it to a protected location would still be forbidden. This is unlike the Taz. His reliance on the principle that “their end was permitted because of their beginning” is irrelevant here, since that principle was stated only concerning the joy of Yom Tov. The Yerushalmi cited above explicitly states: “Once he placed it on the ground,” etc. On Yom Tov, however, the Sages permitted returning shutters and spreading hides before those who tread upon them.

<b>מלו כו'. </b>ר"ל דמש"ש אם לא שחק כו' הכל קודם המילה שדוחין המילה אם לא אפשר ללעוס בשיניו אף שיהיה אח"כ סכנה מ"מ אין מלין אותו שיבא לידי כך וכן באין לו חמין לרחוץ אותו אחר המילה ובזה מתורץ קושית תוס' שם ד"ה לועס כו' אבל אחר המילה נתפזרו מותר כדרכו הכל וכמו שמותר למצוץ וכמש"ש פשיטא מדקא מחללי כו' ועתוס' דעירובין ס"ז ב' ד"ה והא לא כו' וכ"כ הרי"ף ורמב"ם ע"ש ושם: <br><b>(ליקוט) מלו כו'. </b>דעת הרמב"ן שאפי' נתפזרו קודם מילה מלין כדרכו שאין דוחין מפניה המילה אף שאח"כ יצטרך לחלל בדאורייתא כיון שסכנה אין חוששין וההוא ינוקא דאשתפוך חמימי היינו חמימי דקודם המילה אבל הרז"ה והרשב"א חולקין עליו וכמש"ש והביא הרשב"א ממ"ש בריש י"ט לא ישחוט אא"כ יש לו דקר נעוץ ומודים שאם שחט כו' דה"נ ישחוט ואח"כ ידחה הכיסוי ממילא כו' וכן פי' הגאונים בההוא ינוקא חמימי דאחר המילה וכתב ומתני' דשרי לועס בשיניו כורך על אצבעו כו' בדלא אתיידע אלא לאחר המילה ואפ"ה בשינוי וכ"ד תוס' שם ד"ה לועס כו' אבל אח"כ מסיק דאפי' קודם המילה ע"ש ובר"נ אבל ל"ג דוקא קודם מילה ושבות ע"י שינוי התירו אבל לאחר המילה מותר כדרכו וכן דייק לשון הרמב"ם והטוש"ע כאן מלו כו' עושין כו' ר"ל כדרכו וכן ממ"ש הרמב"ם וטוש"ע בס"ו כל המכשירים כו' אלמא קודם מילה דלאח"כ אינה משום מילה אלא משום סכנה והתירו בשינוי כמו שהתירו שבות ע"י עובד כוכבים (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They circumcised, etc.</b> That which the Gemara states, “If he did not grind,” etc., refers entirely to the period before the circumcision. The circumcision is postponed if it is impossible to chew the medicine with one's teeth, even though afterward the child might be endangered. Nevertheless, one may not circumcise him and thereby bring him into that danger. The same applies when there is no hot water with which to wash him after the circumcision. This resolves Tosafos' question there, s.v. Lo'es. After the circumcision, however, if the medicinal ingredients became scattered, all necessary acts may be performed in the ordinary manner, just as suction is permitted. This is clear from the Gemara's statement, “It is obvious, since they desecrate,” etc. See Tosafos to Eruvin 67b, s.v. Veha, and this is also the ruling of the Rif and Rambam; see there. <br><b>(Likkut) They circumcised, etc.</b> Ramban maintains that even if the medicinal ingredients became scattered before the circumcision, one performs the circumcision in the ordinary manner. The circumcision is not postponed on that account, even though afterward it will become necessary to violate a Torah prohibition, because once there is danger we do not take this into consideration. According to him, the incident of the infant whose hot water spilled refers to hot water needed before the circumcision. The Baal HaMaor and Rashba disagree, as stated there. Rashba adduces proof from the beginning of Yom Tov, where the Mishnah states that one may not slaughter unless he has earth already prepared, yet all agree that if he slaughtered, etc. Thus here too, he would slaughter first, after which the obligation of covering the blood would arise automatically, etc. The Geonim likewise explain that the infant's spilled hot water was needed after the circumcision. Rashba writes that the Mishnah permitting one to chew with his teeth or wrap the remedy around his finger, etc., refers to a case in which the need became known only after the circumcision, and even then it is done with an unusual method. Tosafos there, s.v. Lo'es, also takes this view, though afterward Tosafos concludes that the same applies even before the circumcision; see there and in the Ran. According to the Gra's text, however, this applies specifically before the circumcision, when only a rabbinic prohibition performed with an unusual method was permitted. After the circumcision, one may act in the ordinary manner. This is also indicated by the language of the Rambam and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here: “They circumcised,” etc., “they perform,” etc., meaning in the ordinary manner. It is further indicated by the Rambam and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in se'if 6, who write, “All preparatory acts,” etc. This shows that the preparatory acts under discussion are those needed before the circumcision, because acts needed afterward are no longer permitted because of the mitzvah of circumcision, but because of danger. A rabbinic prohibition performed with an unusual method was permitted just as a rabbinic prohibition performed through a gentile was permitted. <small>(End of Likkut.)</small>

<b>And if he was born, etc.</b> His view accords with Rabbeinu Yonah, who writes concerning the dispute of Rabbah and Rav Yosef there that we rule stringently. The Rif, however, writes that this is not merely a stringency, but that the halachah follows Rabbah. See the Rosh there. There is no practical difference between them. Rabbeinu Yonah's words there are difficult, for he writes that there is a practical difference regarding an individual who accepted a fast upon himself, since it is permitted during bein hashmashos; according to him, it is not permitted until the bein hashmashos of Rav Yosef, which is the stringent view. This is incorrect. If we follow the stringent view, it should not be permitted until the bein hashmashos of Rabbi Yose, which begins after the bein hashmashos of Rabbi Yehudah has ended, as stated there: “The halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah regarding,” etc. Moreover, the commentators did not explain the reference to bein hashmashos as applying to the end of a fast, but to its beginning, and specifically to communal fasts, such as the final ten fasts and similar cases. <small>The beginning of a day differs from the end of a day. See what the Gra wrote in his Likkutim to Orach Chaim, at the beginning of siman 562, and in the printed Likkut in siman 261, s.k. 11.</small> This is also indicated in chapter 4 of Pesachim, 54b: “Shall we say that he maintains,” etc.; “but did we not learn,” etc. It is possible that Rabbeinu Yonah interprets the passage as applying to both the beginning and end of the fast. He was pressed to this interpretation by the statement in chapter 1 of Taanis <small>(12a)</small>: “Any fast during which the sun did not set,” etc. The commentators, however, explain that this refers to the completion of sunset, meaning the emergence of the stars. Nevertheless, the plain language implies sunset itself, and it must therefore refer to the second sunset. <small>This follows Rabbeinu Tam and the other authorities. According to the Gra in his Likkutim, however, bein hashmashos begins at the start of sunset, as explained above in siman 262, s.k. 9, and in Orach Chaim, siman 261.</small> Yet this is the beginning of bein hashmashos, as it is likewise regarding Chanukah. According to this, however, that passage proves that the halachah follows Rabbah, because according to Rav Yosef the relevant time would be after sunset. It appears to me that the authorities require the emergence of the stars regarding both fasting and Chanukah because we rule stringently according to Rabbi Yose. The baraisa concerning Chanukah that says “from the time the sun sets” follows Rabbi Yehudah, as does Rav Chisda's statement regarding fasting. We, however, follow Rabbi Yochanan, who ruled stringently according to Rabbi Yose. <small>Although we are lenient regarding a fast, because a doubt concerning a self-imposed fast is permitted, we do not combine two leniencies by also ruling according to Rabbi Yehudah.</small>
