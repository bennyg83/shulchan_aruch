["<b>ושל שאר מינים. </b>כלו' של חיות או של בהמות טמאות אין בהם איסור חלב אלא איסור של בהמה טמאה. וכתב הב\"ח דנ\"מ שאלו היה בכלל חלב היה מותר לעשות בו סחורה מדכתיב יעשה לכל מלאכה אבל כיון שחלבם כבשרם דאסור משום בהמה טמאה אסור לעשות בהם סחורה עכ\"ל ולפי מ\"ש בסימן קי\"ז ס\"ק ד' דבחלב בהמה טמאה אסור לעשות סחורה משום דטמא הוא לא נ\"מ מידי ודוק ונ\"ל דנ\"מ במי שהוא חשוד לדבר אחד שאינו חשוד לדברים אחרים כדלקמן סימן קי\"ט ודוק:"]
**** ENGLISH ****
And of other species — meaning of undomesticated animals or of impure animals: there is no prohibition of chelev in them, only prohibition of impure animal. And Bach wrote there is a practical difference: if these were in the category of chelev, it would be permitted to do business with them, from the verse "it may be used for any work"; but since their chelev is like their flesh, forbidden because of impure animal, it is forbidden to do business with them, end of his words; and according to what is written siman 117 s.k. 4, that with chelev of an impure animal it is forbidden to do business because it is impure — there is no practical difference at all; examine; and it appears to me there is a practical difference regarding one suspected of one matter who is not suspected of other matters, as below siman 119; examine:

**** ENGLISH ****
And some say, etc. — and in my book I brought proofs for these "some say," see there; all the more so its gid is forbidden (and see above siman 65 seif 7); and so Bach; and not like the Levush, who brought only the first reason; however, what Bach brought as proof from Rabbenu Meir in Sefer Yereim siman 147, who wrote that Rabbenu Meir holds that according to R. Meir, who said chelev of a stillborn fetus is forbidden, it is only a rabbinic stringency, not like the view of the Rambam (and the Mechaber), who hold one is liable for karet, end of his words — I did not reach the end of his view, for if so Rabbenu Meir would be against the Gemara, Chullin 92b, where it is proven explicitly that chelev of a stillborn fetus is forbidden d'oraisa according to R. Meir, and so it is stated in several places in the Gemara; rather, in his haste he did not examine, for Rabbenu Meir there explicitly refers to chelev of the gid, regarding which we say in the Gemara that for R. Meir it is d'rabbanan, and the Rambam ruled chapter 8 of Forbidden Foods and the Tur below siman 68, and all the poskim — and this is clear:
