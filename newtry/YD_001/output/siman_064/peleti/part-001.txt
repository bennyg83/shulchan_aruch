And some say that if its months were completed, etc. — its chelev is forbidden, etc. — this is the view of the Rambam, who holds to distinguish: even though we rule like R. Yehuda that a nine-month fetus is permitted through slaughter of its mother, nevertheless its chelev is forbidden; and all the poskim wondered; and in Chullin 92b we say that R. Meir and R. Yehuda dispute chelev of a stillborn fetus, and we say the dispute is regarding a live nine-month fetus, and R. Meir and R. Yehuda follow their views — behold they depend on each other; and since the offspring is permitted through slaughter, the chelev too is permitted; and the later authorities elaborated on this. And I brought proof for the Rambam's words from that which we return on 74b: R. Yehuda and Resh Lakish dispute, according to those who permit its chelev, whether its blood is permitted; for Resh Lakish — even its blood is permitted like its chelev; and R. Yehuda challenges from "one tears it and removes its blood" — R. Zeira: to say there is no karet punishment; and this is Resh Lakish's intent when he said permitted — meaning there is no karet punishment; and according to this, to whom are they speaking — to R. Yehuda? It should only be residue blood, for R. Yehuda holds residue blood is karet; and it is difficult: to whom are they speaking — not to R. Yehuda, but to us who rule chelev is permitted like R. Yehuda; and on this Resh Lakish said: for us there is no karet in blood, for we do not rule like R. Yehuda that residue blood is karet, only a mere prohibition; and what of it that R. Yehuda holds karet — because we rule like R. Yehuda in that chelev is permitted, are we responsible to rule every matter of R. Yehuda in the Gemara? Therefore the Rambam was compelled in his view that for practical law we do not rule like R. Yehuda, and its chelev is truly forbidden; and that R. Yehuda and Resh Lakish dispute whether its blood is like its chelev — they do not dispute for the law, for for the law both are forbidden; they only dispute and debate according to R. Yehuda, who holds its chelev is permitted — how does he hold regarding blood, whether permitted or forbidden; and according to this one tears and removes its blood in an eight-month fetus, where there is no dispute at all, as Rashi wrote; and the Gemara says Resh Lakish holds permitted — meaning to exempt from karet; and this is all for R. Yehuda, for whom we forbid everything; and the Gemara challenges well to R. Yehuda that residue blood is karet — and it comes out well, and the Rambam's words are compelled. And in resolving the body of the challenge and the matter in the Gemara as above, it appears the Rambam holds in that which the Gemara challenges at the beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh, and holds gid hanasheh applies to a stillborn fetus, and challenges: how can this be, etc., from that which the end teaches "and it applies to a stillborn fetus" — implying the first part does not deal with a stillborn fetus; thus he says: this matter is a dispute of R. Meir and the Sages; and Tosafot challenged: their words deal with after the mother was slaughtered, whether its slaughter permits; and here we deal with "applies to a stillborn fetus" before the mother was slaughtered; and see in Tosafot what they wrote, for it is a great challenge (and do not say as I wrote siman 62 that where there is permission for its prohibition, prohibition in one act does not apply — if so the Gemara should be difficult, since there is possibility of permission for its prohibition through slaughter of the mother; then gid prohibition and consecrated prohibition in one act do not apply — if so, for R. Meir too who holds it applies to a stillborn fetus it is difficult, for in any case he concedes when slaughtering the mother while it is five or six months, slaughter of its mother permits, and nevertheless for R. Meir there is not prohibition in one act). And to resolve it, it appears that which R. Eliezer says disputes regarding a live nine-month fetus, and R. Meir follows his view — as the simple meaning: what does it teach us? If slaughter of its mother does not permit, from the verse chelev would be permitted — behold it is like other animals; rather, thus is the explanation: that which R. Yehuda holds that even a nine-month fetus, slaughter of its mother permits — because it is difficult: "from every animal you may eat" to permit its chelev — behold months complete it; and all the time its months are not completed, there is no chelev prohibition at all; and if it has completed months, there is no longer slaughter of the mother that permits (and to say a nine-month fetus that died — this is not relevant, for perhaps that which we say for R. Therefore I elaborated somewhat to establish religion on its peg and uphold ancient custom in its place.

And it happened in Prague that a nail was inserted at the head of the intestine, only chelev adhering in the intestine sealed it, and the matter came out kosher that it is permitted to eat and pure and seals; and the report came to the ears of the three Maharar David Oppenheim z"l, Av Beit Din of Prague, and he ruled to forbid, and they cried out on him in the beit midrash; and they said Rama erred in the source of the law, Heaven forbid that it be permitted to eat; and he composed responsa and sent, and part to Yaakov, and most did not heed him, as you will see in responsa Knesset Yechezkel and Shulchan Melech, Laws of Unintentional Sin, except the Rav Baal Shev Yaakov in his responsa held his words that l'chatchila one should forbid, for Rama erred in a printing error in Kol Bo and Rama did not notice the error — and in truth the evil was not only disputing these Gaonim, but also casting aspersion on our ancestors who practiced thus from Rama's time until today eating full chelev and a karet transgression — ancestors eating chelev flesh, Heaven forbid — is this the measure of all our deceased rabbis who toiled to establish ancestral custom without doubt, and this toil turned into bad reasoning saying ancestors sinned and always ate full chelev — would that he had been silent and it would have been wisdom to him.
