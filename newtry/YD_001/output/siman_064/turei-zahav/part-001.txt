**** ENGLISH ****
And of other species, etc. — in Tur is written the law of impure animal and stillborn: their flesh is forbidden and there is no prohibition of chelev in them; and there is a practical difference regarding lashes; and Tur had no need to write them, as Beit Yosef wrote; and Maharshal z"l answered that there is a practical difference regarding that it is forbidden to do business with chelev of impure animal and stillborn — for if they were in the category of chelev, it would be permitted to do business with them, as written "it may be used for any work," etc. And this is difficult for impure animal: even if they have because of chelev prohibition, it does not help to be lenient but to be stringent, for it is an added prohibition, as with chelev of pure animal nevelah, which is an added prohibition, as Beit Yosef wrote — except that the Torah's decree is that it is permitted for work, as written "and chelev of nevelah," etc.; but with impure animal one certainly cannot learn from nevelah, for nevelah is of a pure kind, unlike impure animal; but in my humble opinion there is a practical difference regarding min b'mino, for we rule min b'eino mino is stricter than min b'mino, siman 96; and Beit Yosef brings there in the name of Maharshal that chelev and flesh are two species — if so, if impure chelev mixed with kosher flesh, one might say it is min b'eino mino; therefore it teaches us there is no name of chelev here and it is min b'mino; and also on this answer one may challenge: nevertheless it has the name chelev, only it does not forbid — if so, it would be min b'eino mino with flesh; therefore it appears the practical difference is for one who vows benefit from anything forbidden because of chelev, that we know in what it is forbidden; and by this method one may generally answer Tur's words in several places, such as siman 66 regarding blood of impure animal and evar min hachai; and by this method it is in Tosafot chapter HaSefinah (Bava Batra daf 84) regarding what she said "this bundle is red" — practical difference for one who vows from red; and therefore it is fine that Tur did not write regarding gid that it does not apply to impure animals, as stated in the Mishna, for for us there is no practical difference from this, for one who vows benefit from gid is not relevant — for according to Rambam, even without a vow gid hanasheh is forbidden in benefit — this appears very correct to me:

**** ENGLISH ****
Tosevet, membrane. In the Gemara it is taught: "all chelev that covers the innards" — just as chelev covering the innards is membrane and peeled, so all membrane and peeled; Rashi: a thin membrane is on it and the membrane is peeled from above the chelev that is not so adherent; and he includes chelev on the abomasum, and all the more so on the intestines; R. Akiva says: just as chelev covering the innards is tosevet, membrane, and peeled, so all, etc.; Rashi: tosevet is a word — when it is a word spread on the innards, and likewise on the intestines at their head; but chelev of the abomasum is not tosevet but pieces by pieces and adherent; and on the intestines from the head onward — that is from the hadra dekanta when they separate it, chelev remains with them and it is not tosevet but adherent to them. And in Tur is written tosevet, membrane, and peeled — meaning spread upon the membrane such that when the membrane is peeled it peels with it; and he gave another sign that flesh should not cover it — and this is also explicit in the Gemara. And some challenge Tur: why did he explain what they said, membrane and peeled, on the membrane under the chelev — behold Rashi explained on the membrane above the chelev; and it appears there is no difficulty, for Rashi explained so in R. Yishmael's words, but in R. Akiva's words it is not so; for if you say R. Akiva's membrane and peeled is also like R. Yishmael, only he adds tosevet — R. Akiva should have said tosevet and membrane, etc.; rather, it must be one matter: tosevet sits entirely on the membrane beneath it — and this is Rashi's intent when he wrote spread on the innards, namely that the membrane with the chelev on it is spread on the innards; and Maharshal also wrote Tur's words thus, that R. Akiva, not like R. Yishmael, for another reason — and he elaborated on this unnecessarily:
