**** ENGLISH ****
But, etc. In Torah HaBayit; and nevertheless even the Sages did not require filling and iruy in earthenware except at the beginning of its use in a gentile's hand, etc.; but at the beginning of its use in a Jew's hand, wiping-dry suffices ; and this is what Machatz wrote, etc. "of idolaters" ; for otherwise why does it teach "of idolaters"; and so said Raavad z"l ; end; and nevertheless specifically when they are not pitched, for in pitched ones there is no distinction as above. (Extract) But, etc. requires wiping-dry ; this is Raavad's view; but Ran disagrees there and wrote: from what the mishnah teaches anonymously "forbidden," it implies even if the beginning of its use is by Israel; and that which the braita teaches "of idolatry" is to include that nevertheless R. permits; and all this is for the Rif's version; but for Tosafot's version who wrote Shulchan Aruch ; end ; the mishnah [deals with] at the beginning of use by Israel; and it requires study (end). (Extract) But if it is earthenware it requires wiping-dry ; it requires study, for the mishnah [deals with] at the beginning of its use by Israel, as Tosafot wrote and as above; and even when it is not pitched it is forbidden, as stated "even though he peeled," etc.; and the same applies when it is not pitched as above; and even though Tosafot wrote "and one can say the mishnah," etc. ; for a temporary matter or many times; nevertheless from the Tur's language, who distinguishes between its beginning by a gentile or by Israel, it implies he explains Tosafot's answer in such a case; and even though in Torah HaBayit he wrote thus ; that was according to the version that does not have the first version, and Tosafot's answer is not needed; but for us it is difficult. And as the Rosh wrote that wiping-dry does not help for earthenware, as above; and it is not comparable to a cover that they distinguished in chapter 2 (one version 2) between the beginning of their use in a Jew's hand, etc. ; for there they are not constantly occupied with wine abundantly as in a winepress, as Ran wrote; therefore even wiping-dry is not required; but here in all cases iruy is required (end).
