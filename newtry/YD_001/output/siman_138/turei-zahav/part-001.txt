<b>גת של אבן וכו'. </b>אע"ג דאין מכניסין לקיום בגת מ"מ הואיל ומשהין בו יין כל ימי הגיתות ותדיר בו יין דומה לכלי שמכניסין בו לקיום בזה דאסור אפילו לפי שעה אבל מ"מ לא לגמרי כמכניסו לקיום להצריכו עירוי ג' ימים כ"כ רשב"א וגם שאר פוסקים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A stone winepress, etc. </b>Even though wine is not placed in a winepress for long-term storage, nevertheless, since wine remains in it throughout the entire winepressing season and wine is constantly present in it, it resembles a vessel into which wine is placed for long-term storage in this respect, that it is forbidden even for temporary use. Nevertheless, it is not entirely like a vessel used for long-term storage so as to require pouring water into it for three days. So wrote the Rashba, and so too the other poskim.

<b>אינה ניתרת בקליפה וניגוב. </b>בטור כתוב צריכה קליפה וניגוב והוכיח ב"י שהוא ט"ס דהא בשל עץ אמרו במתניתין אליבא דחכמים דיקלוף והיינו שאחר כך ינגב כמ"ש הרא"ש והטור על זה ואח"כ אמר במתניתין לרבנן ואם של חרס אע"פ שקלף הזפת אסורה משמע דלא מהני קליפה וניגוב דאל"כ היינו של עץ ויפה הוכיח כן אלא ליישב דעת הטור אם אינו ט"ס נ"ל דהטור ס"ל דבאמת עץ וחרס שוין הם לענין אם לא דרך דדינו בקליפה וניגוב ומה שהקשה ב"י היינו רישא דעץ י"ל דעל מתניתין לא קשה מידי דלא בעי לומר אלא דבחרס גם רבי מודה לחכמים דצריך קליפה וניגוב אבל לרבנן אין חילוק באמת ביניהם ובטור נמי לא קשה מידי דכוונתו דבלא דרך אין חילוק בין עץ לחרס אבל בדרך יש חילוק דהיינו שהטור זכר ברישא בדרך דצריך עירוי דוקא וקמשמע לן כאן בחרס דלא מהני בדרך קליפה וניגוב בעלמא וע"כ לא הזכירו אלא יקלוף לחוד שהיא מעכבת אפילו דיעבד מה שאין כן הנגיבה אבל בשל חרס נקיט לשון איסור אחר הקליפה דהכא הנגיבה מעכבת כמו הקליפה ועל כן נקט הרא"ש הביאו ב"י בעץ ואח"כ ינגוב ויש הוכחה לפי' זה מדאיתא בגמ' במחץ ומשפך של חרס רבי אומר ינגב וחכמים אוסרים משמע. דהניגוב לחוד אוסרים אבל אי איכא קליפה גם כן מותר דע"כ הך וחכמים אוסרים לאו בכל גווני אמור דהא בעירוי עכ"פ מותר אלא ע"כ דלא אסרי רק מה שמתיר רבי ואם כן אף אנו נאמר דבקליפה מותר ולא אסר רק נגיבה לחוד אבל לא קליפה וניגוב דאם לא כן היה ליה לומר וחכמים אוסרים אף בקליפה כנ"ל לדעת הטור ואע"ג דבדברי הרא"ש לא משמע כן מכל מקום הטור שפיר הכי ס"ל ובהגהת ב"י לא ניחא ליה לשון הטור דכיון שכתב אינה ניתרת כו' משמע דבא לכתוב חומרא והיאך מסיים אלא בעירוי בלא קליפה הך בלא קליפה היא קולא אלא כך היה לו לכתוב אלא בעירוי אבל קליפה לא צריך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is not rendered permitted through peeling and wiping. </b>The Tur states that it requires peeling and wiping. The Beit Yosef proved that this is a scribal error, for regarding wood the Mishnah states, according to the Sages, that one peels it, meaning that he afterwards wipes it, as the Rosh and the Tur wrote concerning this. The Mishnah then states, according to the Rabbis, that if it is earthenware, even though one peeled off the pitch, it remains forbidden. This implies that peeling and wiping do not help, for otherwise it would have the same law as wood. He proved this well. However, to reconcile the view of the Tur if it is not a scribal error, it appears to me that the Tur holds that wood and earthenware are in fact equal when wine was not trodden in them, and their law is peeling and wiping. Regarding the Beit Yosef's difficulty from the first clause concerning wood, one can say that there is no difficulty from the Mishnah, for it merely means that with regard to earthenware, even Rabbi concedes to the Sages that peeling and wiping are required, whereas according to the Rabbis there is in truth no distinction between wood and earthenware. There is likewise no difficulty in the Tur, for his intent is that when wine was not trodden in them there is no distinction between wood and earthenware, but when wine was trodden in them there is a distinction. The Tur stated at the outset that when wine was trodden in it, pouring water into it is specifically required, and he teaches here regarding earthenware that ordinary peeling and wiping do not help when wine was trodden in it. Therefore, regarding wood, the Mishnah mentioned only peeling, because peeling is indispensable even b'dieved, unlike wiping. Regarding earthenware, however, it used an expression of prohibition after peeling, because here wiping is indispensable just as peeling is. Therefore the Rosh, cited by the Beit Yosef, states regarding wood, 'and afterwards he shall wipe it.' There is proof for this explanation from the Gemara's statement concerning an earthenware ladle and funnel, where Rabbi says that one should wipe them, and the Sages forbid them. This implies that the Sages forbid wiping alone, but if peeling is also performed, it is permitted. Necessarily, the words 'and the Sages forbid' were not said in every circumstance, for through pouring water into them they are certainly permitted. Rather, they forbid only what Rabbi permits. Accordingly, we can likewise say that peeling renders them permitted, and the Sages forbid only wiping alone, but not peeling together with wiping. Otherwise, the Gemara should have said, 'and the Sages forbid even through peeling.' This is how the Tur's view may be understood. Although the words of the Rosh do not imply this, the Tur can properly hold this way. The Beit Yosef's proposed emendation does not fit the wording of the Tur, for since the Tur wrote, 'It is not rendered permitted, etc.,' this implies that he is coming to state a stringency. How, then, could he conclude, 'rather, through pouring water into it without peeling'? The words 'without peeling' express a leniency. Instead, he should have written only, 'rather, through pouring water into it,' and peeling is not required.

<b>קליפה ומילוי ועירוי או הגעלה. </b>בדרישה הקשה דכאן משמע דהגעלה עדיפא מעירוי ובסימן קל"ה בסופו כתב הטור בכלי חרס בשם הרמב"ן דאם מכניסו לקיום דלא סגי בהגעלה רק בעירוי ותירץ לחלק בזה עיין עליו שדחק עצמו הרבה ונראה לע"ד דמעיקרא לא קשה מידי ודאי במקום ששייך הגעלה אז היא עדיפא מעירוי אלא דבכלי חרס לעיל ס"ל לרמב"ן דלא שייך הגעלה כלל מטעם דהתורה העידה עליו שאינו יוצא מידי דפיו לעולם והיינו דרך בישול דכתיב בכלי חרס אשר תבושל בו ישבר ואפ"ה אמרו חכמים דעירוי מהני שם אמרינן דהגעלה אינה שולטת בכל הכלי) ועירוי שולט בו אבל בשאר כלים שטבעם שההגעלה שולטת בכולו אז ודאי היא עדיפא מעירוי שהגעלה שולטת בו ברותחין ועירוי שולט בצונן ממילא לדידן גם בכלי חרס הוי כן דקיימא לן דמהני הגעלה לכלי חרס בסימן קל"ה סעיף ט"ו באיסור זה הוה חרס כשאר כלים והגעלה עדיפא בהו ג"כ כך נ"ל פשוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Peeling, filling and pouring, or hag'alah. </b>The Derishah asks that here it appears that hag'alah is more effective than pouring water into the vessel, whereas at the end of siman 135 the Tur wrote, concerning an earthenware vessel, in the name of the Ramban, that if wine is placed in it for long-term storage, hag'alah does not suffice and only pouring water into it is effective. He answered by drawing a distinction, see there, but he greatly strained himself. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty at all. Certainly, wherever hag'alah is applicable, it is more effective than pouring water into the vessel. However, concerning an earthenware vessel above, the Ramban holds that hag'alah is entirely inapplicable, because the Torah testified that what it absorbed never fully leaves it. This refers to absorption through cooking, as it is written, 'An earthenware vessel in which it was cooked shall be broken.' Nevertheless, the Sages said that pouring water into it is effective there. We say that hag'alah does not penetrate the entire vessel, whereas pouring water into it does. With other vessels, however, whose nature is such that hag'alah penetrates the entire vessel, hag'alah is certainly more effective than pouring water into it, for hag'alah acts through boiling water, while pouring acts through cold water. According to our ruling, the same applies even to earthenware vessels, for we rule in siman 135, seif 15, that hag'alah is effective for earthenware vessels with respect to this prohibition. Thus, earthenware has the same law as other vessels in this regard, and hag'alah is likewise more effective for it. This appears straightforward to me.

<b>One should distinguish, that if its initial, etc. </b>The words of the Tur here require explanation so that they may be reconciled. We shall set forth what is necessary for this. The Mishnah states that a stone winepress is wiped, and a wooden one is peeled, according to the Sages, while an earthenware one remains forbidden even if it was peeled. This means that pouring water into it is required, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote from the beginning of this siman. These are all the details stated there. Afterwards, the Gemara states that Rabbi Abbahu cites a Baraisa concerning the 'adashim,' meaning the winepress, that rinsing suffices. Rashi's view, which the Tur cites, is that this Baraisa contradicts the Mishnah. Since Rabbi Abbahu cites it, it is authoritative, and we rule in accordance with it that rinsing suffices. Rabbeinu Tam disagrees with Rashi and holds that we rule according to the Mishnah. The Rosh writes according to his view that the 'adashim' of the Baraisa does not mean the winepress itself, but something else. Alternatively, even if we explain it as the winepress itself, there is no difficulty, for the Mishnah discusses a vessel whose initial use was in the hands of a non-Jew, whereas the Baraisa discusses one belonging to a Jew. The Rashba in Toras HaBayis and the Ran likewise cite the Ra'avad as distinguishing, regarding earthenware vessels, between one whose initial use involved prohibition and one whose initial use involved permitted wine. They have another proof for this, from the Baraisa in the Gemara concerning the ladle, meaning a vessel with which liquid is drawn, and the funnel 'of a non-Jew.' This wording implies that specifically where their initial use involved prohibition, pouring water into them is required, but where their initial use involved permitted wine, wiping suffices, as Rabbi states. Regarding the contradiction between the Mishnah and the Baraisa, the Ra'avad gives the first resolution of the Rosh. Therefore, the Beit Yosef wrote correctly that the Ra'avad distinguishes according to initial use only with regard to earthenware, whereas the law of wood remains as stated in the Mishnah in all circumstances. This is unlike my father-in-law, of blessed memory, who wrote concerning the Beit Yosef, 'May his Master forgive him,' for the Beit Yosef is a man whose Master assisted him. The Tur cites the words of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, and afterwards writes in the name of the Ra'avad as follows: 'The Ra'avad likewise wrote that even an earthenware winepress and its utensils, if their initial use was by a Jew, require only wiping; and a stone winepress requires only rinsing.' End quote. At first glance, this is astonishing. If the Ra'avad's proof for this distinction concerning initial use is the contradiction between the Mishnah and the Baraisa, and he distinguishes between them as I stated in the name of the Rosh's second resolution, why did he rule concerning stone that rinsing is required, in accordance with the Baraisa, while mentioning nothing concerning wood? Since he stated that the law of stone changes here from his ruling at the beginning of the siman, where wiping is required, he should have stated that rinsing likewise suffices for wood when its initial use involved permitted wine. Or, if its law is wiping, he should have stated that. Since earthenware requires wiping and stone requires rinsing, to which should wood be compared? If, on the other hand, the Ra'avad's proof is from the Baraisa concerning the ladle and funnel that we cited according to the Rashba, rather than from the contradiction between the Mishnah and the Baraisa, from where does he know that rinsing suffices for stone, contrary to the Mishnah? Perhaps the distinction exists only with regard to earthenware, as we found above that at the beginning it absorbs more, but with other vessels there is no such distinction. We must therefore say that, according to the Tur's understanding of the Ra'avad, both proofs apply. From the Baraisa concerning the earthenware ladle and funnel, which specifies that they belong to a non-Jew, he learns that when their initial use involved permitted wine, wiping suffices, as Rabbi states. He is additionally troubled by the contradiction between the Mishnah and the Baraisa, and he likewise distinguishes between a vessel whose initial use involved permitted wine and one whose initial use did not, with the Baraisa discussing a vessel whose initial use involved permitted wine. However, he is uncertain what type of vessel the Baraisa concerning 'adashim' discusses. One possibility is that there is no distinction between wood and stone, and both require rinsing, as stated in that Baraisa. Were it not for the Baraisa concerning the ladle and funnel, which implies that when their initial use was not by a non-Jew they still require wiping, and that the Sages concede to Rabbi in this case, I would say that even earthenware requires only rinsing. But stone and wood are certainly alike according to the Baraisa concerning 'adashim,' and both require rinsing. Alternatively, we may interpret the Baraisa concerning 'adashim' only with regard to what is more reasonable, namely, stone alone. In its case they were lenient to require only rinsing when its initial use involved permitted wine. With wood, however, rinsing does not suffice, but its law is lowered by one level. In the Mishnah, where its initial use involved prohibition, it requires peeling, while in the Baraisa it requires wiping, like earthenware. Once earthenware has become saturated from absorbing permitted wine, its law is like wood, as stated above. Therefore, the Tur mentioned only stone, for it is clear that rinsing suffices for it, whereas concerning wood there is reasoning in both directions. Since Rashi holds that rinsing suffices for every winepress, we should not increase the dispute between the authorities. We therefore say that rinsing likewise suffices for wood when its initial use involved permitted wine, even according to those who disagree with Rashi. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch ruled at the end of the siman that rinsing suffices for wood and stone when their initial use was in the hands of a Jew. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, disagreed with the Shulchan Aruch and held that wood requires wiping in this case. According to what I have written, the words of the Shulchan Aruch are correct.
