And Rashba in Torah HaBayit and Ran bring them also in the name of Raavad to distinguish in earthenware vessels between the beginning of use in issur or in heter — namely that they have another proof for this; and it is from what is in the Gemara, braita: the ladle — meaning a vessel with which they draw — and the funnel of a gentile specifically; it implies specifically at the beginning of its use in issur it requires iruy, but for heter wiping-dry suffices, as R. said; and on the difficulty of mishnah against braita he wrote one resolution — that of the Rosh — end; Beit Yosef properly wrote that he does not distinguish except in earthenware, but in wood its law remains like the mishnah in all cases; and not like Machatz z"l who wrote on it that its master permitted it — he is a man whose master is his helper; and the Tur brought the words of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam and afterwards wrote in the name of Raavad, and thus: and likewise Raavad [wrote] that even earthenware and its vessels — if the beginning of its use is by Israel, wiping-dry suffices for it; and stone suffices through rubbing — end; and at first glance it is astonishing on him, for if Raavad's proof for this distinction regarding the beginning of its use, etc., is from the contradiction of mishnah against braita and he distinguishes between them as I mentioned in the name of the Rosh for the second resolution — why did he rule regarding stone that it requires rubbing — meaning rinsing — as in the braita, and regarding wood he did not mention anything? For since he mentioned stone whose law changed here from what he ruled at the beginning of the siman that it requires wiping-dry, he should have mentioned that also for wood rinsing suffices here since its beginning is in heter; and if its law is wiping-dry, he should have mentioned thus, since we have before us in earthenware that wiping-dry is required and in stone rinsing — how does wood compare? And if Raavad's proof is from the braita of ladle and funnel that we mentioned according to Rashba, and not from the contradiction of mishnah against braita — whence does he have that stone suffices through rubbing, not like the mishnah? Perhaps there is no distinction except in earthenware, as we find above that at its beginning it absorbs more; but in other vessels there is no distinction in this; and it must be that the Tur according to Raavad has both — from the braita of ladle and funnel, for earthenware; and he mentioned among them "of idolaters" — from this we learn that at its beginning in heter wiping-dry suffices, as R. said; and further it is difficult for him also mishnah against braita, and he also distinguishes in them between its beginning in heter or not — the braita deals with its beginning in heter; except he is uncertain what the braita of lentils deals with — one can say in truth whether wood or stone, both [require] rinsing as in the braita of lentils; and if not for the braita of ladle and funnel that implies at its beginning not by a gentile it requires wiping-dry — in this the Rabbis concede to R. — I would say that also earthenware would suffice through rinsing; but in stone and wood certainly according to the braita of lentils it is one matter and both [require] rinsing; or one can say we do not explain the braita of lentils except for the more reasonable matter — namely stone specifically, for in it they were lenient with rinsing at the beginning of its use in heter; but in wood rinsing does not suffice, only wiping-dry — also one level: in the mishnah whose beginning is in issur its law is peeling, and here in the braita its law is wiping-dry like earthenware — since after earthenware is satiated from absorbing in heter its law is like wood as above; and therefore the Tur mentioned only stone, which is a clear matter that rinsing suffices; but in wood there is reasoning for here and for there; and since for Rashi in every winepress rinsing suffices, we do not split their dispute and we say in wood rinsing also suffices at its beginning in heter, even for those who disagree with him; and perforce Shulchan Aruch ruled at the end of the siman that wood and stone suffice through rinsing at the beginning of their use in a Jew's hand; and Machatz z"l disagreed with Shulchan Aruch that for wood wiping-dry is required in this; and according to what Shulchan Aruch wrote, his words are correct.
