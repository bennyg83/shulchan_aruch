**** ENGLISH ****
The law of one who forbids something upon himself with a Torah prohibition; it contains 2 se’ifim. One who says, “These fruits are upon me,” or “Such-and-such type is upon me,” or “What I eat with so-and-so is upon me,” like pig meat, or like idolatry, or like nevelah and treifah and the like; and likewise one who says to his wife, “You are upon me like my mother,” or “like my sister,” or “like orlah,” or “like kil’ei hakerem” — these are permitted, and there is no vow here. But if the speaker was an am ha’aretz, he needs to ask a chacham, and they show him that his wife is forbidden and that those fruits are forbidden; and they are stringent with him, for regret alone is not enough for him — rather, they must open for him a petach from another place, and release his vow so that they not act lightly regarding vows. {Rama: Some say that in our time everyone judges them like the law of an am ha’aretz, since most are not Torah scholars (responsum of Rashba). Nevertheless, some say that even with an am ha’aretz one does not need to ask — only when he forbids his wife upon himself, but not with other vows (Tur; and see se’if katan 4).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who says that his wine shall be libation wine and his bread Samaritan bread — he is not forbidden by this. But if he said it in the manner of a penalty — such as when he said, “If I do such-and-such, or if I transgress such-and-such, my bread shall be Samaritan bread and my wine libation wine,” and he transgressed — there are those who forbid and there are those who permit. {Rama: Nevertheless, with an am ha’aretz one may release it. And all this is with an individual who accepted upon himself; but the many, at the time of the Sanhedrin, could subdue rebels and transgressors [by decreeing] that their wine shall be libation wine and their bread Samaritan bread, and whatever they decree in this [manner] would stand (responsum of Rashba). See above siman 229 regarding one who said, “If I do such-and-such, I will not be Jewish.”}
