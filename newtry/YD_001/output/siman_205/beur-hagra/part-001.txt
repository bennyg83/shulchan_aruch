<b>ומחמירים עליו כו'. </b>מתני' שם פותחין כו' וכפי' הרא"ש אבל במתני' שם כ' א' פותחין כו' ופי' הר"נ דאף בחרטה סגי דלא כר"מ וכ"כ הרא"ש בפסקיו וע"ל סי' ר"ח ס"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And we rule stringently concerning him, etc. </b>The Mishnah there states, "We suggest an opening," etc., in accordance with Rosh's explanation. In the Mishnah there on 20a, however, it states, "We suggest an opening," etc., and Ran explains that even regret alone suffices, contrary to Rabbi Meir's opinion. Rosh likewise wrote this in his rulings. See above, siman 208, se'if 2.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>ומיהו כו'. </b>מדקתני בסיפא פותחין ול"ק ברישא. הרא"ש וכ"ה להדיא בגמ' שם וכ"כ הר"נ וכן תמה בכ"מ על הרמב"ם ואף שבלח"מ דחק עצמו דבריו מסותרין מעצמן אלא דגירסת הרמב"ם בגמ' רבינא כו' בעי שאלה והא קתני סיפא פותחין לו פתח ממקום אחר אלא מחוורתא כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, etc. </b>This follows because the latter clause teaches, "We suggest an opening," yet the Talmud does not raise this as a question on the first clause. Thus writes Rosh, and this is also explicit in the Talmud there. Ran wrote likewise, and Kesef Mishneh similarly questioned Rambam. Although Lechem Mishneh strained to explain the matter, his words contradict themselves. Rather, Rambam's version of the Talmud reads: "Ravina, etc., requires release. But does the latter clause not teach, 'We suggest an opening for him from another source'? Rather, it is clear," etc.

<b>האומר כו'. </b>כמ"ש בס"א וע"ל סי' ר"ח ס"א ושם ואם רוב כו' וה"ה עכשיו להיפך. ש"ך וכמ"ש במתני' י"ח ב' רי"א כו' בגליל מותרת כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who says, etc. </b>As stated in se'if 1. See above, siman 208, se'if 1, and the statement there, "If the majority," etc. The same applies now in the reverse situation. Thus writes Siftei Kohen. This accords with the Mishnah on 18b: Rabbi Yehudah says, etc., "In the Galilee it is permitted," etc.
