**** ENGLISH ****
To partner with the idolater. See in the Mishneh Lamelech chapter 4 of Hilchos Bechoros, who was asked about one who had an animal giving birth for the first time and wanted to exempt it from bechorah by selling to an idolater a part of it for a perutah, and he appointed a shaliach and gave him a perutah in order that he sell to an idolater, and the shaliach went and we do not know if he performed his agency, and in the meantime the animal gave birth and the shaliach is not there to ask him. And he elaborated greatly on the law of the chazakah that a shaliach performs his agency, and he concluded that in our case it is impossible to be lenient for several reasons: we do not say the chazakah of a shaliach etc. to be lenient except in a matter that if the shaliach does not do it immediately, it is possible that the sender will come to a transgression, like that of techumin and that of chadash; but here, so long as she has not given birth there is nothing here, and the shaliach is lazy to say tomorrow I will perform my agency. And further, here it depends on the will of others, for perhaps he will not want to buy. And further, the view of the great Rishonim is that in a Torah matter we do not say this chazakah to be lenient; therefore in our case this bechor has the sanctity of a bechor until a blemish falls in it. Later it was told to him that the owners inflicted a blemish l'chatchila and want to eat it on this blemish, and he wrote that they accomplished nothing, and it is forbidden to slaughter the bechor on this blemish, even though that which we hold that one who inflicts a blemish in a bechor shall not slaughter upon it is only a penalty d'rabbanan, as above siman 313, and in d'rabbanan we say this chazakah even to be lenient — nevertheless one should be stringent because of the other two reasons. And further, that which we distinguish here between a Torah prohibition and that of the Scribes is specifically a matter that has no root from the Torah. And there is a further supporting factor to be stringent, since the view of Rabbeinu Tam is that money does not acquire from an idolater; therefore in our case it has the sanctity of a bechor and he cannot slaughter it until another blemish falls in it; however he is not obligated to give it to a Kohen, but waits until another blemish is born in it and eats it in its blemish. See there at great length. And see in the responsa Chavos Yair end of siman 40, who wrote that there are those who hesitate to say that even if a Yisrael made a partnership with an idolater and sold him a share in the mother or in the fetus, if afterwards this Yisrael sold to another Yisrael, the buyer also needs to go back and make the aforementioned remedy, to sell a second time to that idolater or to another idolater; and this matter is a fiction, for the first sold to the second only what belongs to him in them, and the idolater's share remains as at first, and he is a partner with the second, and we are witnesses that it is pleasing to the second thus; see there.

And in siman 311 there regarding one woman in the land of Hungary who had a firstling-bearing goat and sold it to a non-Jewess for half a gold piece and told her to take it to her house, and the non-Jewess did not want to lead it through the street because of the evil eye and told the woman to leave it in her house, and meanwhile it bore a male: whether one should be concerned for the sanctity of a bechor. And he replied that one should not be lenient at all; even though according to Rashi money acquires for a non-Jew, and according to the Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah he acquires either with money or with meshichah, and according to the other poskim too whatever acquires in their laws avails to uproot the sanctity of a bechor, as Mishnah Berurah wrote, brought in Magen Avraham siman 448, and unlike responsa Maharam Mintz; and the law is with Mishnah Berurah; nevertheless it appears to me that that is when he gave all the money; but in our case where she gave only half a gold piece for a goat worth much — needless to say in their laws it does not acquire, as was also testified in responsa Even HaEzer (see there further in siman 318 on this); but even according to the poskim that a non-Jew acquires with money, and if he gave a perutah he acquired — that is when he fixed with him fitting prices, and if afterwards he gives him a perutah as a kinyan not as a deposit he acquired everything and the rest of the money is a debt upon him; but to give half a gold piece for a goat worth several gold pieces is only a mere asmachta, and neither of them intends to convey or to acquire; and a proof from what Tosafot wrote in Avodah Zarah 71 end of s.v. "Rav Ashi" (brought in Be'er Heitev se'if katan 3), that there it is explained that were it not that we are witnesses that the Jew resolved and was lenient in order to be exempt from the bechorah, there would be voiding of the sale; and if so it is fine when he sells only one limb cheaply, for even if he loses the limb and the non-Jew takes it for himself for a small amount, the Jew profits much more in the offspring that is exempted from the bechorah; which is not so where she sold the entire goat, etc. (see in Orach Chayyim siman 448 seif 3, that there it is stated "or that he sell it to him a complete sale for a small amount," and see Chok Yaakov there, and one can resolve). And further I am in doubt: since she said she should take it to her house, she did not resolve to convey except with meshichah, and since she did not pull it she did not acquire — see in Choshen Mishpat siman 200 seif 17; and in truth one may discuss this and say: since the non-Jewess said that the Jewish woman should keep the goat in her house, it is as if she asked her to lend her a place for standing the goat, and she acquired the goat incidental to the loan of the place, as the Tur wrote that this is the preferred way; but this is not correct, for with what did she acquire the room without chazakah and without money and a document, and she also did not mention "incidental" in her kinyan; and even though the poskim wrote regarding one who conveys to her a share in the fetus that they resolve that the intent was in a manner that avails, that he conveys the cow for its fetus — already Tzemach Tzedek wrote that specifically there Maharai said so, and not in our manners — see there. Therefore it appears to me in our case that it is sanctified with bechorah at least from doubt — end of his words; see there. However in the book Turei Even on tractate Rosh Hashana daf 13a he proved simply that hekker does not avail — see there; and in responsa Chatam Sofer siman 316–317 he wrote that the aforementioned geonim missed an explicit Tosefta, chapter 2 of Bechorot: an animal of the wilderness and hekdesh and an animal of a convert who died and has no heirs is exempt from bechorah; and why this enactment was not mentioned, and also it is not mentioned anywhere that we decree because of scheming as with chametz — that is because hekker of chametz is not similar to ordinary hekker, for hekker is only similar to shemittah, namely that he take the animal out of his house or open the doors of the pen and say whoever wishes to take may come and take, and it will be complete hekker proper that any person's hand can control it; but chametz does not need such hekker, since it is forbidden in benefit and is not in his domain, and the Merciful One made it as if in his domain — it suffices even if it is within his house, only that in his heart he truly nullifies it that it is not his; and in this scheming is applicable, for who will descend to the depths of his heart; therefore the Sages forbade even one who properly declares ownerless; but regarding a bechor the Sages of blessed memory were careful and taught in the aforementioned Tosefta "an ox of the wilderness," etc., and the same law applies to one who declares his animal ownerless and takes it outside — and that is an ox of the wilderness; and in this there is no room to decree because of scheming; but against this there is in this no remedy to declare his animals ownerless and strangers plunder his portion — see there.
And there in responsa Chatam Sofer siman 317 he elaborated on the case of one man who sold his firstling-bearing cow to a non-Jew minor nine years old with money and situmta: whether a kinyan of a non-Jew minor avails to uproot from the sanctity of bechorah. And he concluded that certainly in all laws practiced among Noachides there is no place for the measures of a Jew at the time of majority, except regarding valuations and vows that were included from "ish ish" — that is specifically when he brought two hairs in his time; and at the end of tractate Nazir it excludes a remarkable child near manhood; but for all their laws and kinyanim no measures were given them, and once they come to the category of mind such that if explained to him he understands — behold he is an adult; and therefore in our case if the minor did not reach the divisions of Reuven this sale is nothing; but if he is already a person of understanding then he is considered an adult and his purchase is a purchase from the Torah; and if there were here money and meshichah or conveyance of a place it would avail; but since there was here only a money kinyan and situmta — although I explained in siman 314 that situmta avails — nevertheless here I am in doubt: who will tell us that the merchants agreed to convey through situmta (and a handshake) of such a young lad with whom it is not the way to deal in commerce, and also in their laws he is called unmundig; and he concludes that nevertheless if one great authority agrees with me I will be counted with him to rely on the poskim that money alone acquires, and especially perhaps situmta acquires here too, and he should give a perutah to a non-Jew minor (who is not confused with an adult Jew) to inflict on it a blemish upon which we slaughter nowadays, and slaughter it with its blemish — see there.

<b>He should transfer to him.</b> Levushei Serad explains that this means by kinyan agav, not through acquisition by courtyard, as will be explained below. He reports a case where a Jew owned a cow kept in a village at a gentile's house. The gentile was in town when the Jew sold him the cow and received money. The cow later gave birth to a male. Levushei Serad ruled that it was a doubtful firstborn, like any case of money alone, despite the apparent acquisition through the gentile's courtyard. A gentile, who has no law of agency, is not automatically acquired property through his courtyard. Certainly this is so where he was not standing beside his courtyard. Even if the sale had occurred at his village house while he stood there, Levushei Serad argues it would not help, following Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 243, seif katan 10: a woman's courtyard is derived from the law of her hand, but an ordinary male's courtyard does not operate as a hand in that sense, and a gentile therefore never acquires through courtyard. This differs from Responsa Avodat Gershuni, siman 45, which implies that standing beside it would help. According to Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 316, cited above, even without standing beside it acquisition might work where it is literally his own courtyard, so the matter requires study. Levushei Serad adds that if the Jew told the gentile, "Go, pull it and acquire," then even if the gentile was in town the acquisition likely works, because meshichah can occur without the seller present, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 197. Since the cow was in the gentile's house and he fed and watered it, it is nearly impossible that he did not move it. Where it remains uncertain whether he actually performed meshichah and the gentile says that he did, the matter requires further consideration.

<b>That he receive money.</b> See Baer Heitev, seif katan 7. The statements that even after the fact one kinyan alone may be insufficient, and that the same applies when purchasing a cow from a gentile so that if the Jew performs meshichah or gives money, are from Shach. Taz, seif katan 8, however, cites Responsa Rama, siman 87, that requiring both money and meshichah applies only initially. After the fact, we follow Rabbenu Tam that meshichah, not money, acquires from a gentile. Therefore, if the Jew did not pull the cow, it plainly remains in the gentile's ownership. See Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, siman 79, Chinuch Beit Yehudah simanim 84, 87, and 88, and Nachalat Shivah siman 31, who agree with Rama that after the fact Rabbenu Tam's view is followed. Many earlier and later authorities disagree, as Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 62, explains. See also Panim Meirot, part 2, siman 52, Meir Netivim siman 39, and Be'er Yaakov at length. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 37, discusses one who owned a pregnant cow of uncertain firstborn status and sold it to the gentile shepherd for money alone. The shepherd afterward led it out to the field to graze with the rest of the animals in the ordinary way, without specific intent to make a kinyan, and it later gave birth. Rabbi Akiva Eiger rules there is no concern for firstborn sanctity. First, meshichah took place; although without conscious intent, where another party intentionally transfers ownership the recipient can acquire even without independent intent. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 313, for an extensive discussion. Second, there is a double doubt: perhaps the cow had previously given birth, and perhaps the halachah follows the view that a gentile acquires with money. The second doubt alone would not suffice against the chazakah that it had not given birth, unless it was a milking cow whose majority status weakens the chazakah, but it can serve as an additional factor. He adds other supporting considerations. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Bechorot chapter 4, writes that if one sold to a gentile for money alone and afterward the owners inflicted a blemish upon the newborn, perhaps one may permit slaughter on that blemish because the rule barring benefit from a deliberately inflicted blemish is only rabbinic. There is therefore a rabbinic doubt whether the gentile's money acquisition already removed firstborn sanctity. He later rejects the leniency because the doubt first arose while the animal was under a possible Torah prohibition and only afterward developed into a rabbinic issue; in such a case we do not say that a rabbinic doubt is lenient. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 64. Responsa Maharam Ziskind, siman 7, cites the gaon Maharashak regarding a Jew who bought a sheep from a gentile outside the city, paid money based merely on seeing it, did not pull or lift it, and left it at the gentile's house. The Jew later sold that same sheep to another gentile, also for money alone without meshichah, and it gave birth while still at the gentile's house. He ruled there is not even a doubtful firstborn. Either money does not acquire from a gentile, in which case the Jew never acquired the sheep at all, or money does acquire as Rashi holds, in which case the second gentile likewise acquired it from the Jew. We do not perversely rule stringently in opposite directions within the same animal. He adds that even if the Jew never resold it, where he had not paid the full purchase price there may be room to say the gentile retained a legal interest and the animal is exempt. Responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah, siman 81, discusses a firstborn-producing animal sold to a gentile by the owner's wife for a perutah. He is uncertain whether her sale is valid, since a wife ordinarily has no authority to sell her husband's property, unless we say it is self-evidently beneficial and the husband desires it. If she regularly conducts the household's business transactions, there is plainly no concern. Pri Megadim, Orach Chayyim at the end of siman 434, writes that although one may acquire merit for a person in his absence, ordinarily some instruction is required, but an expression of the owner's intent can suffice. See Even HaEzer at the end of siman 86 and Beit Shmuel there regarding when a wife's sale of movables is valid. Panim Meirot, part 2, end of siman 52, however, writes straightforwardly that where the owner's mother sold his pregnant cow to a gentile without his knowledge, the sale is valid because one may act beneficially for another in his absence. He similarly writes that if the owner is away and someone else sells his chametz, the seller is treated as the owner's agent because the act is beneficial.

<b>הואיל והעובד כוכבים. </b>עבה"ט בשם ש"ך מ"ש מאי אית לך למימר דשלב"ל הוא כו' וע' בדגמ"ר לעיל ס"ו שכ' דמהרא"י גופיה לא סמך ע"ז רק בצירוף קולות אחרות ע"ש וכ"כ הוא ז"ל בתשובת נו"ב תניינא חי"ד סימן קצ"א שאין לסמוך על דברי רמ"א אלו מאחר שמהרא"י עצמו לא סמך ע"ז ומה"ט כתב בענין בכור שבעל הבהמה אמר לעובד כוכבים קודם שילדה אם תלד בהמה זו זכר יהיה להעובד כוכבים והעובד כוכבים הוא מגדל הבהמה לטובת ישראל בלי שום הנאת גיזה או ולדות ואח"כ ילדה זכר והעובד כוכבים רוצה להחזיק לעצמו כדברי בעליו וכתב דיש בו קדושת בכור מאחר שהיה אז דבר שלב"ל וגם יש כאן ריעותא אחרת במה שאמר אם תלד זכר ויש בו ג"כ חשש אסמכתא ע"ש. וע"ש בסימן קצ"ב שכתב דאם הוא בענין שאין בו חשש אסמכתא כגון שלא התנה אם יהיה זכר והוכר עוברה אז יש לצרף דעת הרמב"ם שזה מיחשב בא לעולם ואעפ"כ למעשה הייתי מתיישב אך כיון שכבר הורה מורה א' שאינו בכור וסמך עצמו על דברי רמ"א וש"ך קשה לסתור אך לא ישחטנה כמו שהיא תמה רק יצוה לעובד כוכבים לחתוך אוזן ואח"כ יכול היהודי לשחטו ואם גזזו כבר ומכר הגיזה המעות מותרים [ועיין בת' ח"ס סי' שי"ב ג"כ בנדון זה וע' עוד בתשו' חמדת שלמה סי' ל"ג מ"ש בזה] ע"ש היטב באריכות ועיין בשו"ת תשובה מאהבה ח"א סימן קע"ו וקע"ז באריכות:
**** ENGLISH ****
Since the idolater. See Ba'er Heitev in the name of the Shach, what he wrote: what have you to say, that it is something that has not come into the world etc. And see in Dagul Mervavah above seif 6, who wrote that Maharai himself did not rely on this except in combination with other leniencies; see there. And so he of blessed memory wrote in the responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah siman 191, that one should not rely on these words of the Rama, since Maharai himself did not rely on this. And for this reason he wrote regarding a bechor, that the owner of the animal said to an idolater before she gave birth: if this animal gives birth to a male it shall belong to the idolater, and the idolater is raising the animal for the benefit of the Yisrael without any benefit of shearing or offspring, and afterwards she gave birth to a male and the idolater wants to keep it for himself according to the words of its owners; and he wrote that it has the sanctity of a bechor, since it was then a matter that had not come into the world, and there is also another defect here in that he said if she gives birth to a male, and there is also a concern of asmachta; see there. And see there in siman 192, who wrote that if it is in a manner that there is no concern of asmachta, for example he did not stipulate if it will be a male and her fetus was recognized, then one should combine the view of the Rambam that this is considered as having come into the world; and even so in practice I would deliberate; but since already one teacher ruled that it is not a bechor and relied on the words of the Rama and the Shach, it is difficult to contradict; however he should not slaughter it as it is unblemished, but he should command an idolater to cut an ear, and afterwards the Jew can slaughter it; and if they already sheared it and sold the shearings, the money is permitted [and see in the responsa of the Chasam Sofer siman 312 also on this matter, and see further in the responsa Chemdat Shlomo siman 33 what he wrote on this]; see there well at length; and see in responsa Teshuvah Me'Ahava volume 1 siman 176 and 177 at length.
