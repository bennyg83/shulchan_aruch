<b>מאלף. </b> בב"י כתב בשם תוספות דבכורות דסגי אפי' באזנה אכן בתוס' פרק בתרא דעבודת כוכבים כתבו שיש להחמיר להקנות לעובד כוכבים דבר שעושה אותה טריפה כגון הריאה והראש אמנם א"צ שיתן העובד כוכבים מעות שוה הראש או הריאה אלא שיקנה בפרוטה אע"פ ששוה יותר כפלים אפ"ה אין לחוש משום ביטול מקח דאנן סהדי שהישראל גמר ומקני לעובד כוכבים כדי להפטר והקשה בפרישה מאי שנא מסי' ס"א גבי מתנות כהונה שצריך שיהיה לעובד כוכבים חלק בכולה ואם אין שותף עמו אלא בראש אינו פטור רק מן הלחיים וכו' וא"כ קשה לדעת התוס' דבעינן דוקא דבר חשוב כמו הראש ובו סגי ונראה דלק"מ דדוקא הכא גבי בכור דכתיב פטר רחם בישראל ילפינן מינה שיהיה הכל בבני ישראל ואם ניטל דבר חשוב ממנו בטל הכל משא"כ לענין מתנות דאין לימוד מקרא על זה אלא דכל שיש לעובד כוכבים חלק באותו חלק פטור ממתנות ותו לא דאין א' תלוי בחבירו כלל עכ"ל הט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From a thousand.</b> Beit Yosef cites Tosafot in Bechorot that even transferring a share in the ear is sufficient. However, Tosafot at the end of Avodah Zarah write that one should be stringent and transfer to the gentile a part whose loss would render the animal a tereifah, such as the lung or the head. The gentile need not pay the full value of the head or lung. He may acquire it for a perutah even if it is worth twice as much, and there is no concern of an invalid sale due to the price disparity, because it is evident that the Jew fully intends to transfer ownership in order to exempt himself from the firstborn obligation. Perishah asks why this differs from siman 61 concerning priestly gifts, where the gentile must own a share in the relevant part of the animal, and if his share is only in the head the animal is exempt only from the cheeks and not the other gifts. According to Tosafot, by contrast, here a significant part such as the head suffices for the entire firstborn exemption. Taz answers that there is no difficulty. Regarding a firstborn, the verse teaches that the opening of the womb must be entirely "in Israel," so if a significant part is removed from Jewish ownership, the whole status is lost. Regarding priestly gifts, there is no such verse. The gentile's ownership exempts only the particular portion in which he has a share, and each gift stands independently.

Then. The Shach wrote: because there is a dispute among the poskim whether meshichah acquires and not money, or money and not meshichah; therefore to fulfill the doubt one must make a kinyan of meshichah and money. It implies that even after the fact, if he did only one kinyan, meshichah or money, he is not exempt from bechorah. And the Bach wrote that even if he did both, meshichah and money, except that the idolater did not give the money immediately at the time of the kinyan, but promised him to give him such-and-such money and he trusted him — even though the idolater afterwards gave him what he promised him, this money does not acquire; and needless to say if the Yisrael owed the idolater and the idolater said to him, your cow is acquired to me with the money that you owe me, and the Yisrael transferred it to him, it is obvious that this is not a kinyan of money. Therefore. And it implies that the same applies when he bought a cow from an idolater, that according to Rabbeinu Tam specifically meshichah acquires, and according to Rashi money; and if so if the Yisrael did meshichah or gave money and she gave birth, he is obligated in bechorah out of doubt. And so it appears in the responsa of the Rav siman 87. And he further wrote there that if he bought a cow from idolaters with money and it was stolen, and the idolater agreed to pay the cow to the Yisrael, and afterwards the thief was identified and the cow gave birth, he is exempt from bechorah, since the idolater agreed to pay, the cow was acquired to him wherever it is. End of his words. End of his words.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>הואיל והיה כאן משיכה ומעות. </b> דכיון דברשות העובד כוכבים הוי בשעת התנאי וקנה לו רשותו במקום משיכה ומעות נמי נתן במה שקבל עליו להאכילה וליטפל בהן מאי אית לך למימר דבר שלא בא לעולם הוא יש להביא ראיה על זה מפרק המפקיד דאמרינן נעשה כאומר לו קנה בהמה לולדותיה כו' עכ"ל מהרא"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Since there were both meshichah and money.</b> Since the animal was in the gentile's domain when the condition was made, his domain acquired it in place of meshichah. Money was also effectively given through his acceptance of the obligation to feed and care for the animals. If one objects that the offspring did not yet exist, proof can be brought from the chapter HaMafkid, where we say it is regarded as though he said, "Acquire the animal with respect to its offspring." Terumat HaDeshen.
