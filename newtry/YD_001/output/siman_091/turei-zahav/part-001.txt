<b>כל מידי דבעי הדחה כו'. </b>בטור כתב בזה ש"מ כל מידי כו' יש להקשות מאי ש"מ דקאמר הא היא היא דבהדיא אמרינן במתני' ובלבד שלא יהיו נוגעים זה בזה וי"ל דה"א דוקא בשר וגבינה עצמם אבל בכלי שלהם א"צ ליזהר קמ"ל דכל מידי דבעי הדחה ואפילו בכלי ויליף לה מדאמר אביי נהי דקליפה לא בעי הדחה מיהו בעי מדתלי טעמא בהדחה ממילא כל מידי דבעי' הדחה שוין הם. ויש להקשות עוד הא כתב ב"י בסי' ב' דבכ"מ דבדיעבד מותר לא חיישינן מתחלה שמא ישכח והוא מוכח בכמה דוכתי וכמו שנעתיק בסעיף ד' וא"כ אמאי חיישינן כאן דילמא אכיל בלא הדחה כיון דאי אכיל בלא הדחה אין איסור דאפילו במקום שצריך קליפה יש דעות בסי' זה אי שרי בדיעבד בלא קליפה. וי"ל דבכ"מ דאין איסור ברור לפנינו אלא ספק אם יש כאן איסור וצריך דרישה אחר זה בזה אמרי' כיון דדיעבד שרי מותר לסמוך לכתחלה על הדרישה שיעשה אחר זה משא"כ כאן שיש איסור ברור במה שישים בשר כשר בכלי של איסור אלא שיש לו תקנה בהדחה בזה לא סמכינן על הדחה שאח"כ דשמא ישכח:
**** ENGLISH ****
Everything that requires rinsing, etc. Tur wrote in this regard "everything," etc. — one may challenge: what does "everything" mean, for explicitly in the Mishnah we say "provided they do not touch each other"; and one may answer that specifically meat and cheese themselves, but regarding their vessels one need not be careful — it teaches that everything requiring rinsing, even in vessels, are equal; and one may challenge further: Beit Yosef wrote in siman 2 that wherever b'dieved is permitted we are not concerned initially lest one forget, as will be cited in seif 4; if so why are we concerned here lest one eat without rinsing, since if one eats without rinsing there is no prohibition, for even where peeling is required some hold it is permitted b'dieved without peeling? And one may answer that where there is no clear prohibition before us but only doubt requiring investigation afterward, since b'dieved it is permitted one may rely l'chatchila on the investigation afterward; unlike here where there is clear prohibition in placing kosher meat in a forbidden vessel, only that it has remedy through rinsing afterward — we do not rely on rinsing afterward lest one forget.

and so implied at the end of seif 5 in hagahah regarding gravy falling on a vessel — hag'alah and earthenware breaking are required, and for wooden vessels peeling suffices — thus b'dieved we require nullification of what was forbidden the measure of peeling; and further proof from what Rosh brought, Beit Yosef beginning siman 96 — a slice of cress cut with a forbidden knife is all forbidden though it only forbids the measure of peeling, because the slices are small and one cannot know where it was cut, therefore all was forbidden — end of his words, from which we learn that where peeling is impossible, all is forbidden; and further proof from Orach Chaim siman 95 seif 3 — if one poured from a kli rishon of meat onto a milk vessel it forbids all of it, etc. — end of his words; and although pouring does not forbid more than the measure of peeling, nevertheless it forbids all where peeling is impossible; and further proof from Rama siman 98 seif 4 — but if through hot salting, etc., one estimates only the measure of peeling, etc. — end of his words; and further proof from Orach Chaim general rule 36 din 6 — if placed in a boiling pot, 60 is required against its peel, etc.; and I saw in hagahah at the end of Orach Chaim in the name of Maharai that practice is like Riva to require sixty, and so is practical law.

And some say even if they are cold. This is Rosh's view, who explained so the incident of the dove that fell into milk in the Gemara; and Maharai wrote in Hagahot Shach, etc. — in Hagahot Ashiri from Avodah Zarah he wrote that live meat has no cracks; and so is our practice that before salting the meat we soak it and there is much blood mixed with water and we are not precise whether there are cracks in the meat that would forbid from blood and water together; and sometimes it soaks a long time and one may distinguish between blood and other prohibitions; and Maharshal chapter 25 siman 69 wrote on this — what appeared to him from Maharai's words regarding roast that even if roast and milk were not salted at all one should forbid, for he did not bring proof to permit live meat except from soaking meat, etc. — it does not appear to me, for cold does not absorb from cold even if seasoned, and the Sages only forbade in the dove that fell into brine that was slightly salted — then forbidden if it has cracks; but if not salted at all, neither meat nor brine — no prohibition at all in cold, even roast; but I fear to disagree with Semak, Semag, Hagahot Maimoniyot, etc., who wrote plainly that the law is so even if neither meat nor milk was salted, etc. — I fear to disagree with them l'chatchila and to permit what they forbid — end of his words; it implies that in live meat it is obviously permitted if neither was salted; and in roast, if one was salted even if eaten because of its salt, it is forbidden even per Maharshal, for at least one was slightly salted; and Rama who wrote that with great loss one may be lenient per the first view lenient regarding eaten because of its salt — all that is not hot roast; and above in seif 5 he wrote we are not expert and should forbid even salting roast without great loss; and it appears to me we do not combine two leniencies for great loss; however in hot roast without seasoning and without cracks, peeling suffices — and so appears to me; however all this when hot roast fell into milk, but if milk or other prohibition fell on hot roast — its law in siman 92 and 105.
