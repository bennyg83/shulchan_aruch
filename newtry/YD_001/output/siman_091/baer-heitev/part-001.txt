<b>דילמא. </b> כתב הט"ז ויש להקשות הא כתב ב"י בסי' ב' דבכל מקום דבדיעבד מותר לא חיישינן מתחלה שמא ישכח והוא מוכח בכמה דוכתי וא"כ אמאי חיישינן כאן דילמא אכיל בלא הדחה וי"ל דבמקום דאין איסור ברור לפנינו אלא ספק אם יש כאן איסור וצריך דרישה אחר זה התם אמרינן כיון דבדיעבד שרי מותר לסמוך לכתחלה על הדרישה שיעשה אח"כ משא"כ כאן שיש איסור ברור שימלח בשר בכלי של איסור אלא שיש לו תקנה אח"כ בהדחה חיישינן שפיר שמא ישכח להדיחו וכ' מהרש"ל שלא יפה הם עושים ההולכי דרכים שאוכלים הערינג"ש מלוחים על כלי העובד כוכבים שהרי צריך הדחה וכ"ש שלא יפה עושים הקונים מיני קשואים מלוחים שקורין אוגרק"ש עם מי המלח ואוכלים בקערה שלהם שהרי אפילו הקשואים עצמן צריכים הדחה אם הושמו בקערה שלהם וא"כ המי מלח דלא שייך בהו הדחה נאסרו כולם ובת"ח כתב בשעת הדחק כגון שנתאכסן בבית עובד כוכבים כדיעבד דמי עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
Lest one eat. Taz wrote — and one may challenge: Beit Yosef wrote in siman 2 that wherever b'dieved is permitted we are not concerned initially lest one forget, and this is proven in many places; if so why are we concerned here lest one eat without rinsing? And one may answer that where there is no clear prohibition before us but only a doubt whether there is a prohibition requiring investigation afterward — there we say since b'dieved it is permitted, one may rely l'chatchila on the investigation he will do afterward; unlike here where there is a clear prohibition that one will salt meat in a forbidden vessel, only that it has a remedy afterward through rinsing — we are properly concerned lest one forget to rinse; and Maharshal wrote that travelers who eat salted herring on gentile vessels do not act well, for rinsing is required; and all the more those who buy salted cucumber types called ogrekes with brine and eat in their bowl, for even the cucumbers require rinsing if placed in their bowl; if so the brine, to which rinsing does not apply, is all forbidden; and Terumat HaChacham wrote in time of pressure, such as lodging in a gentile house, it is considered b'dieved — end of his words.

להדיא לאכול צונן אפי' לח אבל באמת נ"ל דמ"ש הא"ח כלי של עובד כוכבים שהודחו יפה מיירי דידעינן בודאי כגון שהדיח ישראל עכשיו ואין ר"ל דדוקא הדחה בעינן דהא הפוסקים שלמד מהם דין זה ס"ל דבכל מקום הקנוח יפה בלא הדחה וכן אם לא השתמשו בו איסור תחלה רק בצונן מותר אח"כ בלא הדחה אפי' בדבר לח עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
And specifically. Behold Shach disagrees with this hagahah and wrote that Rama's words in Darkei Moshe too require study — he learned this law from what Orach Chaim wrote that gentile vessels that were rinsed well are permitted to place spices or onion and other sharp things that do not extract a hard dry thing in dry — end of his words; and from Ba'al HaItur's words it does not appear so, only spices and the like — what is not customary to rinse is forbidden to place in a forbidden vessel; and from this he distinguishes that a forbidden vessel used hot is forbidden to use with permitted cold use, and so Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 121; however if forbidden cold use was used, permitted cold use is permitted even l'chatchila, and so we find with chametz vessels that one may use matzah — it also deals in such a case as Orach Chaim siman 451; or one may distinguish whether the vessel was washed well initially or not — if washed well initially it is certainly permitted; and thus Ba'er Hetev's view that although the vessel is forbidden because of absorption of prohibition, nevertheless since prohibition was not absorbed through hot use only through pickling and salting and the like, permitted cold use is permitted; but his words are astonishing at the outset in distinguishing whether prohibition was absorbed through hot or not — this is not so, for it is explained in siman 121 to the contrary that even if absorbed through hot, permitted cold use is permitted; and likewise regarding chametz — what difference whether absorbed through cold or hot if one now uses cold; and in the Gemara they distinguish between hot use and cold use only for using it now hot, but for cold use wiping the vessel alone suffices; and likewise siman 69 regarding salting in an unperforated vessel that one may eat afterward cold — he brought there one view that even if a boiling vessel was used the law is so; also what Darkei Moshe distinguishes between moist permitted and dry is not so, for Rashba explicitly permits eating cold even moist; but in truth it appears to me that what Orach Chaim wrote — gentile vessels that were rinsed well — deals where we know certainly, such as that a Jew rinsed now, and it does not mean that rinsing specifically is required, for the poskim who learned this law hold that thorough wiping everywhere is like rinsing; and Ba'al HaItur deals without wiping; or rather the main point is that earthenware is different, forbidden to use even cold, and Taz follow their reasons that even in earthenware permitted cold use is permitted; and what Orach Chaim wrote that it does not extract a hard dry thing in dry — is to exclude spices and other sharp moist things; but here, that it does not extract a hard thing, even moist; however all this is occasional use, but to use them regularly even cold is forbidden — a decree lest one use them hot, as Rama wrote in hagahah siman 121 s.k. 5 — end of his words; and Taz emends Rama's words and it should read: but a completely dry thing, or if the vessel did not absorb only through cold use, it is permitted to place moist food without rinsing at all; and the explanation of his words is that he refers to what Shulchan Aruch wrote regarding a forbidden cold bowl, namely that hot use was used; and on this he said that with a dry thing it is permitted to use without rinsing, and likewise if forbidden use was not used initially only cold, afterward permitted without rinsing even for moist food — end of his words.

**** ENGLISH ****
And some say. Behold Shach challenged this hagahah and his view is that the Mechaber was not precise at all in comparing meat and cheese to blood prohibition and followed Terumat HaDeshen siman 159 — there the case was salting blood prohibition in geese where one was found treifah, etc., and he permitted with great loss and a mitzvah meal; but that case is not comparable here, for there it appeared to permit even without great loss because regarding blood prohibition we say that after waiting the measure of salting the strength of the salt already ceased; but since from other poskim's words regarding Rashi's incident it implies that even regarding blood we do not say the strength of the salt ceased after waiting the measure of salting — if so do not add — not to permit except with great loss and mitzvah meal; unlike here regarding meat and cheese where the salt expelled nothing — it is obvious that even after the measure of salting it is considered hot; and he wrote that he proposed this to the Gaon our master and teacher and other great teachers and they agreed to his words.
