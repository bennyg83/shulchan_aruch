**** ENGLISH ****
The law of hatarat nedarim (release of vows) and the difference between petach and charatah, and how one is called al daat rabim (with the consent of the many). And it contains 51 seifim.
One who vowed and regrets — there is a remedy through charatah, and even if he vowed by the God of Israel. How shall he do it? He shall go to an expert sage who is expert and wise; and if there is no single expert, he shall go to three ordinary men who are expert for themselves and wise, and who also know how to open for him a petach and release him. And nowadays there is no expert who is fit to release by himself alone.

**** ENGLISH ****
In what matters is this said — that they release while standing when they release through charatah without petach; but one who does not regret from the outset and needs to find for him a petach needs to settle the matter and needs great examination, for what "he does not regret from the outset" means is because he had benefit in the vow — such as if his friends wanted to dissuade him, such as that he would eat with them or marry off his son or daughter according to their desire and the like, and through the vow he escaped from them and he did not wish that he had not vowed. **** ENGLISH ****
One who regretted the essence of the vow from the outset does not need a petach; rather the sage asks him "Do you want this vow?" and he says "I do not want it; I regret that I vowed" and [the sage] releases him. However, he must regret the essence of the vow — that he wished he had never vowed; but if he says "now I regret it" and until now he desired what he vowed, that is not charatah, for he needs to uproot the vow from its root. And the one who vowed should be careful not to say that he regrets from the outset unless it is clear to him that he wished he had never vowed; for if not, the release is not a release and he remains in the prohibition of his vow all his days. And if he does not regret, one must seek for him a petach, to say to him "If you had known this you would not have vowed," and thus the vow is uprooted from its root. And how is the petach? For example, one who vowed about one matter and they urge him greatly to ask about it — they say to him "If you had known they would urge you so much and you would not be able to answer them, you would not have vowed," and he says yes. {Rama: (And some say they return and ask him if he regrets) (Mordechai chapter Shevuos shtayim)} And even a petach that is from the body of the vow is a petach — such as if one forbade so-and-so from his property if he does something, and he transgressed and did it — they open for him "If you had known you would transgress your words you would not have vowed," and he says yes. And there are those who require both petach and charatah. {Rama: And they are accustomed to be stringent and to make from charatah a petach — that after he says he regrets from the outset they say to him "If you had known you would regret, would you have vowed?" and he says no, and then they release him.}
