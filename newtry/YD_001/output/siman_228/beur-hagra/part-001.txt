And this distinction is similar to what is written in Yerushalmi, which Rosh brought in the first chapter of Shabbat and the first chapter of Ta'anit — between a vow and fasting, an unspecified day versus this day — end quote. And Maharam Padua wrote in siman 72 that the distinction between the first clause and the last is that the last said his vow and set its time at once; and he wrote there — and even though Rosh in kelal 8 seif 66 wrote that even if they did not claim within the time, the oath stands in its place because the time was made as a spur and the essence of the oath is on payment, and such is the language of the document; and it appears to one who swore to eat this loaf today etc. — that there he is not obligated to eat that loaf and the essence of the oath is on the time; but here he is obligated to pay and their main intent is on payment and the time is a spur etc. — see there. And Maharam Padua wrote that Rosh does not dispute, for only there was the document language thus. And Rivash wrote that since the time passed he is exempt, like one who swore on a loaf etc. — likewise Rashba; and even though Rosh wrote the opposite, his words are not plausible, and even according to his words one can be lenient here, for payment of a debt is different etc. And in another responsum he wrote to distinguish as written in the gloss here — since etc., unlike his case; and therefore the Rav also wrote to distinguish between the first clause and the last — and his words are puzzling here, for certainly one cannot distinguish except as Maharam Padua wrote, or as Mahari wrote to distinguish between saying with lamed or with bet — and such is the language; and further, for he wrote "and some say" etc. — which is Rosh's view; and likewise Taz questioned.
