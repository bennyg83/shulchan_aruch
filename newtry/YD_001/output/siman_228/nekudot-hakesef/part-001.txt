<b>(שם ס"ק כ"ז) אלא תלמיד טועה כתבן. </b>ואני אומר דברי הפרישה ברורים דהדבר פשוט דהכא מיירי אפילו לא קיים חבירו את הנדר רק שנדר לדע' חבירו כלומר בשביל רצון חבירו אע"פ שחבירו שותק והטעם כיון שנודר בשביל רצון חבירו א"כ הוי נדר גמור שאין תלוי בדעת חבירו רק שעושה הנדר בשביל רצון חבירו וכן משמע מדברי הפוסקים וזהו דעת הפרישה להדיא וזה ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(There, seif katan 27.) Rather, an erring student wrote them.</b> I say that the words of the Perishah are clear. It is obvious that here the discussion concerns even a case in which the other person did not affirm the vow, but the one who vowed did so with the other person's wishes in mind, meaning for the sake of fulfilling the other person's desire, even though the other person remained silent. The reason is that since he vowed in order to fulfill the other person's desire, it is a complete vow that does not depend upon the other person's consent. He merely made the vow for the sake of the other person's desire. This is also implied by the words of the poskim, and this is explicitly the view of the Perishah. This is clear.

<b>(שם ס"ק מ"ה) ולא משמע כן כו'. </b>ולי נראה דדעת הר"ן דאם נדר סתם ע"ד חבירו אין חבירו יכול להתירו דאין בלשון ע"ד חבירו שחבירו יתירו אבל אם אמר שכוונתו היה כן שחבירו יבטלו הוה כאילו התנה כן בפירוש דבנדרים הולכין אחר כוונת הנוד' וגם דעת הריב"ש כן ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(There, seif katan 45.) This does not appear to be so, etc.</b> It seems to me that the Ran's view is that if one made an unspecified vow based upon another person's wishes, that other person cannot annul it, because the expression "based upon another person's wishes" does not imply that the other person may annul it. However, if he said that his intention was that the other person should be able to nullify it, it is as though he expressly stipulated this, because with vows we follow the intention of the person who made the vow. This is also the view of the Rivash. Examine this carefully.

Nevertheless, one may say that when a person says "on Rosh Chodesh," he intends the second day of Rosh Chodesh, because the two days are considered like one extended day, since both are Rosh Chodesh.
