**** ENGLISH ****

It implies that when the non-Jew does it for his own benefit it is permitted regarding a negative prohibition even if it belongs to an Israelite — and so in Hagahot Asheri chapter HaPoalim: regarding something the non-Jew is not warned such as muzzling — permitted to tell a non-Jew "muzzle my cow and thresh with it your threshing" but to thresh his grain — not — end quote; and so Rabbeinu Maggid end Hilchot Sekhirut: no prohibition of telling a non-Jew except regarding Israelite's work, but regarding non-Jew's work permitted — similar to Shabbat, for even on Shabbat an Israelite may tell a non-Jew to do the non-Jew's own work (see Orach Chayim siman 307). But Rashi chapter HaPoalim 90 end 1a and Tur CM 338 wrote even to thresh a non-Jew's grain — unresolved and not decided, and stringently; so too Rosh there; so too conclusion of Tosafot and Agudah there in name of R' Yitzchak; Rosh wrote there: likewise forbidden on Shabbat to tell a non-Jew "your sustenance is not upon you — take this meat and cook it for your need." However they wrote there: even though permitted to tell a non-Jew "eat this nevelah" — there if the Israelite did by hand what the non-Jew does by speech, i.e. placing nevelah in his mouth — it would be permitted, for an Israelite may place nevelah in his mouth; but regarding muzzling forbidden to do what the non-Jew does by speech — end quote (R' Yerucham wrote in Netiv 7 beginning ch. 6: forbidden to tell a non-Jew "your sustenance is not upon you — take this nevelah meat and cook it for your need" but permitted to tell him "eat this nevelah" — and so simple in Tosafot — end quote; he did not mention Shabbat and it is an error, for obviously even though nevelah there is no cooking prohibition). It appears from their words that likewise regarding a non-Jew's cow it is forbidden since the Israelite would be forbidden to do so; however Noda B'Yehuda wrote in chapter HaPoalim: an Israelite who tells a non-Jew "muzzle the mouth of my cow and thresh with it your threshing" or "muzzle your cow and thresh with it my threshing" — forbidden because of telling a non-Jew, for he did prohibited work regarding an Israelite's matter. One can say per what is taught in Mechilta: regarding non-Jew's work there is no prohibition of telling a non-Jew, for there it is different since he does not do regarding an Israelite's matter — end quote; and one can align their words thus too. Nevertheless it is explained from Tosafot, Agudah, Rosh, and Noda B'Yehuda that even if the non-Jew did for his own benefit it is forbidden regarding an Israelite's matter — unlike Hagahot Mordechai who wrote "the rav" and so implied from Rambam chapter 17 Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah and Mechaber Even HaEzer siman 5: forbidden to tell a non-Jew to castrate our animal etc. — implies even without benefit to him it is forbidden since it is an Israelite's animal.
