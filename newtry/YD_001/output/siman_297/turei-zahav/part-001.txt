**** ENGLISH ****
And it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to sow for him etc. — it appears he speaks of an Israelite, and so Tur wrote per Rambam — and it is difficult: regarding kilay hakerem in chutz la'aretz he ruled at end of siman 296 that specifically a minor non-Jew is permitted, not an adult, even though it is rabbinic; here regarding kilay zera, which is d'oraisa in Eretz Yisrael, he permitted in general to any non-Jew. Further difficulty from sirus — he ruled in Even haEzer siman 5 seif 14 forbidden through a non-Jew, and so Rambam chapter 16 of forbidden relations. This is because we find in chapter HaPoalim (Bava Metzia daf 90) that it must not be publicized regarding amirah to a non-Jew on a lav — why permit here through a non-Jew? Further, in chapter 1 of Kilayim Rambam wrote explicitly that an Israelite may not let a non-Jew graft his tree. Perhaps Rambam holds: whatever is forbidden even in chutz la'aretz is a bodily obligation — therefore forbidden even amirah to a non-Jew; not so kilay zera, whose prohibition is only in Eretz Yisrael — not a bodily obligation, and amirah to a non-Jew is permitted. It appears here he means: permitted to tell a non-Jew to sow for the non-Jew's own need, not for an Israelite's need; so I found in Kesef Mishnah chapter 1 of Kilayim in the name of R' Yitzchak Kurkus — with this the Tur's difficulty is resolved too: what difference if he tells him to sow in an Israelite's field — for it is forbidden to uphold afterward — tell him it is for the non-Jew's need, which is obviously permitted. One may say the main reason forbidden to sow for a non-Jew is because a non-Jew has no acquisition in Eretz Yisrael; if so, even amirah to a non-Jew in his own field would be forbidden, since the land is Eretz Yisrael — it would be as telling a non-Jew to sow kilayim in an Israelite's field; this teaches it is permitted.

**** ENGLISH ****
Or drives them as one. This is the Rambam's language — and apparently it implies that for driving alone one is liable even if they have no work together or carrying anything; but it is not so, as explained in Tosafot and Rosh in chapter Bameh Beheimah, brought by Beit Yosef in this siman on what they said in the Gemara "a person is permitted with all of them" etc. — they challenged: a person too should be forbidden; the difficulty — what relevance has kilayim since he does not pull the wagon? For Shabbat is discussed — and they answered forbidden to drive even if he pulls nothing, for otherwise forbidden to move a disqualified consecrated ox from its place, considered as two species in the last chapter of Makot. One can say: only when he does not bind on weekday he speaks, and it deals with pulling something; or one can say pulling is considered from what the ropes carry — and so is proven in Yerushalmi that because of carrying the ropes there is liability — end quote. If so, here too where Rambam wrote "or drives them as one" — meaning they do some work or carry the rope they carry together. Also in Mishnah commentary chapter 8 of Kilayim Rambam wrote: what they said "one who drives kilayim is liable" — means there are wagons pulled by them; but Rambam holds even if the kilayim are not tied together it is forbidden when doing work together; Rosh holds specifically when tied together; Tur concluded in name of Avi HaEzri — no aspect of prohibition in driving until doing work with both together and by tying — end quote. However I saw Maharach z"l wondered on Tur who says without work it is permitted even when tied together — this is not so, for when tied together even without doing work with them, merely driving them together — certainly forbidden per mishnah chapter 8 Kilayim "one who drives receives forty" etc.; he expanded to explain Tur's words that then he speaks of tying; all this is not as explained from Tosafot, Rosh, and Rambam as I wrote. Since the prohibition is because of carrying the rope as we mentioned, it seems one should not tie two kinds of birds on one rope so they do not flee — for when they shake themselves as birds move from place to place even when bound, they then carry the thread they are tied with; also two kinds of birds have kilayim as stated end of Parah, and people do not know to be careful of this.
