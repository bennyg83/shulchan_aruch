**** ENGLISH ****
Its milk is forbidden to drink. — This too is an unresolved question in the Talmud (daf 69a): what is the law of sucking its milk—is milk in general not like eiver min hachai (as in siman 81 s.k. 12 and siman 87 s.k. 9) and this too is permitted, or perhaps there it has a remedy for its prohibition through shechitah—teiku; and so Rambam: behold this is forbidden to drink from doubt. The Bach challenged: why did the Talmud not ask similarly regarding ben pakuah that came on a regular animal and engendered, that the offspring has no remedy because of shehiyah, as above siman 13 s.k. 12; and he answered: there it is plain it is permitted, for it is not treifah but only that shechitah does not permit it, and it is like what the Rashba wrote; and the Rabeinu Yerucham (siman 24 s.t. 15) regarding a dislocated animal wrote: its milk is permitted because a dislocated animal is not treifah but only shechitah is not effective in it; but here it is forbidden because of treifah, and milk of treifah is forbidden—end of his words. But this explanation of the sugya does not seem right to me; and further, the dislocated animal there is according to Maharshal that a dislocated animal is not treifah while alive, unlike above where the offspring is treifah because one of its simanim is as slaughtered—consider for yourself: if he slaughtered one siman and afterward left it, is its milk permitted, lest one say he will finish the shechitah afterward—and this case is similar; and further, with dislocated animal it had a time of fitness, unlike here where from the day it was born it has no remedy. And this distinction is explained in Perishah. And that the Talmud asks thus specifically regarding issur yotzei—for the prohibition is because of eiver min hachai, and milk in general is also like eiver min hachai—and so the Rashba in his chiddushim: thus the question arises—this limb has no prohibition in itself like treifah at all, but because it has no simanim to permit it through shechitah, and behold the whole body is like flesh placed in a basket, and the limb that came out alone remained alive—what difference is there between this milk and other milk from a live animal; or perhaps it is diminished—for there its life stands to be corrected through shechitah, but this fetus's life has no eternal remedy through shechitah—end of his words; and so the Ran; but above, where the whole animal is forbidden because one siman is as slaughtered, plainly it is forbidden.
