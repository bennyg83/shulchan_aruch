(שם ש"ך סק"ג) <b>ר"ן</b> בשם התוס' בכה"ג שכתב המחבר סוכה שאני יושב פשיטא דהוי דבר שיש בו ממש דהא אוסר על עצמו הסוכה שיושב בה אלא דהר"ן הוסיף אף באומר ישיבת סוכה עלי מקרי יש בו ממש כיון שמזכיר החפץ וא"כ אדרבה מדלא כתב המחבר גם כה"ג משמע דלא ס"ל כהר"ן וכן משמע בדעת המחבר (סרי"ג) אלא דהרמ"א בהג"ה שם הביא דעת הר"ן בשם י"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, Siftei Kohen, subsection 3) <b>Ran.</b> In the name of Tosafot: In the formulation written by the Mechaber, "The sukkah in which I sit," it is obvious that the vow concerns a tangible object, for he prohibits to himself the sukkah in which he sits. Ran adds that even if one says, "Sitting in a sukkah is prohibited to me," the vow is considered to concern a tangible object because he mentions the object. If so, the contrary inference may be drawn: since the Mechaber did not also record such a case, it appears that he does not accept Ran's opinion. This also appears to be the Mechaber's view in siman 213. Rema, however, cites Ran's opinion there in the gloss in the name of some authorities.

(סי' רט"ו סעיף ב') <b>אלו</b> שמת על לבך והא שאינו מותר בלא התרה כלל כמו לקמן (סרל"ב ס"ו) דהתם שוגג גמור הוא על אביו אבל הבא הו"ל למידע שא"א לשנה בלא שבתות וי"ט הרא"ש בפירוש והר"ן כתב שם דלקמן הוי טעות בעיקר הנדר שלא נתכוין לאביו לעולם אבל הכא באוסר על עצמו שנה יודע שיעברו עליו שבתות וי"ט וחל הנדר אלא דפותחים לו אלו היה יודע דאסור להתענות ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 215, se'if 2) <b>These matters that you took to heart.</b> Why is he not permitted without any release, as he is in the case below, siman 232, se'if 6? There, his error concerning his father was complete. Here, however, he should have known that a year cannot pass without Shabbatot and Festivals. Rosh states this explicitly. Ran writes there that the later case involves an error concerning the essence of the vow, since he never intended it to apply to his father. Here, by contrast, when one forbids something to himself for a year, he knows that Shabbatot and Festivals will occur during that period, and the vow therefore takes effect. The authorities merely suggest an opening for release by asking whether he would have made the vow had he known that fasting was prohibited. See there.

(שם סעיף ה') <b>וחזר</b> ואמר הריני נזיר ושתה. קשה לי הא הוי כולל מגו דנאסר בחרצנים וזגים וצ"ע אח"ז רב מצאתי בעזה"י בת' זכרון יוסף (סס"י כ"ו) שעמד בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, se'if 5) <b>And he then</b> said, "I am hereby a nazir," and drank wine. This is difficult for me, for the second prohibition should take effect as a more inclusive prohibition, since he thereby also becomes forbidden to eat grape seeds and grape skins. The matter requires examination. After a long time, with the help of Heaven, I found that Responsa Zichron Yosef, at the end of siman 26, raises this question.

(שם סעי' ו') <b>וי"א</b> שאם נדר מדברים האסורים ודעת הר"ן לחלק דעל דבר האסור בעצמותו כגון נבילה וכדומה אין נדר חל דאין איסור חל על איסור דמעיקרא היה האיסור על החפץ אבל בנשבע שלא יאכל ואח"כ אמח קונם האכילה עלי חייל דמעיקרא לא היה האיסור על החפץ אלא על גופו דלא יאכלנו וכן באומר אכילה דיוה"כ עלי קונם עי"ש והכי משמע דעת הרשב"א בת' (סי' תרט"ו) אבל הב"י הביא בשם תשו' רשב"א דבכל ענין לא חייל כדעת א' דכאן:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, se'if 6) <b>Some say</b> that if one made a vow concerning forbidden matters, etc. Ran distinguishes as follows: if the object is inherently forbidden, such as an animal carcass and the like, the vow does not take effect because one prohibition does not take effect upon another, since the original prohibition already rested upon the object. If, however, one first swore not to eat and then said, "Eating this is prohibited to me as a konam," the vow takes effect. The original prohibition did not rest upon the object but upon his person, requiring him not to eat it. The same applies if one says, "Eating on Yom Kippur is prohibited to me as a konam." See there. This also appears to be Rashba's opinion in his responsa, siman 615. Beit Yosef, however, cites a responsum of Rashba stating that the vow does not take effect under any circumstances, in accordance with the first opinion recorded here.
