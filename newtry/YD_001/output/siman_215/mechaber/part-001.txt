**** ENGLISH ****
Vows take effect on a matter of mitzvah; it contains six se’ifim. Vows take effect on a matter of mitzvah. How so? If he said, “Konam sukkah that I sit,” “lulav that I take” — he is forbidden to sit in a sukkah and to take a lulav. {Rama: And there are those who say that they would flog him for having vowed to nullify mitzvot, and they release his vow and he fulfills the mitzvah (Mordechai, beginning of Shevuos Shetayim, in the name of Rabbenu Chananel). And there is one who says that there are no lashes for a vain vow, only regarding a vain oath (Mordechai found in the name of Raavyah, and Ran, and Raash in Nedarim; and Orach Chayim, siman 236); and so is the essential ruling.}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who accepts upon himself in the language of a vow to fast on Shabbat and Yom Tov — the vow takes effect. And likewise one who vows to fast on Sunday or Monday all his days, and Yom Tov or the eve of Yom Kippur fell on them — he is obligated to fast; needless to say regarding Rosh Chodesh. **** ENGLISH ****
That which vows take effect on a matter of mitzvah — specifically regarding positive mitzvot, whether there is in it a standalone positive commandment, or whether there is a positive commandment in its fulfillment and a negative commandment in its nullification. And there is no difference in this between a prohibition that comes by itself and a prohibition that comes through his own action. But regarding a general negative commandment, it does not take effect, whether in its nullification or in its fulfillment. How so? Behold, one who said, “Eating nevelah is upon me” — the vow does not take effect upon him. And the same law applies to a prohibition that comes through his own action — for example, he said an oath that he would not eat this loaf, and returned and said, “This loaf is upon me” — he is liable only once, namely because of “You shall not profane” regarding an oath. And likewise if he said an oath that he would not drink, and returned and said, “Behold, I am a nazir,” and he drank — he is liable only once, namely for “You shall not profane” regarding an oath, but not for nezirut, unless he is questioned regarding the oath, for then nezirut takes effect upon him. And a vow takes effect upon one who swore that he would sleep or that he would drink, even though in its nullification there is a negative commandment.

And there is one who says that if he vowed regarding forbidden matters, such as nevelot and treifot, the vow takes effect, and if he eats from them he transgresses “You shall not profane.” But if he associated with them another matter, he is not bound, for his intention is only on the core prohibition — for it is as associating with a forbidden matter. But regarding a matter forbidden d’rabbanan, and he forbade it upon himself in the language of a vow and associated with it another matter — he is bound by the vow and it is forbidden. {Rama: One who vows or swears not to eat matzah on the night of Pesach — rich matzah is not included. But if he vowed not to eat matzah all year, it is possible that rich matzah is included (Rivash, brought by Beit Yosef, end of siman 236).}
