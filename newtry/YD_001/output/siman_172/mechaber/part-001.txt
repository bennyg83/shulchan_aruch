**** ENGLISH ****
One who mortgages a house or field on condition that he will eat the fruits; it contains 6 se’ifim. One who lends to his fellow and he pawned to him a house or field on condition that he will eat its fruits all the days of the mortgage: if it is like a mashkanta d’Sura — meaning, he mortgages it to him for known years, and he writes, “At the completion of these years, the land will go out without money” — even if he greatly extends the time such that in each year it amounts only to a small matter, for example, he lent him a maneh and stipulated with him that after 10 years this land will return to its owner for free, and the rent of that land is worth 1000 dinarim each year — it is permitted, for this is only like one who rented [it] for less. And likewise, if the owner of the land stipulated with him that whenever the borrower wants to remove him he will bring him money, and he will calculate for him 10 [dinarim] each year and remove him from the field or the house, and he will return to him the remaining money and withdraw — it is permitted, since since the lender has no power to collect anything from his debt and then return the land to the borrower, it is only rental. {Rama: And some say that even without mashkanta d’Sura there is a permission to lend. How so? If he lent for a fixed time of such-and-such years, if the borrower can remove him within the time and he deducts for him each and every year, even a small matter, this is called a mashkanta b’nichayasa and it is permitted; and if he does not deduct for him anything, it is forbidden and is avak ribbis. But if the borrower cannot remove him within the time, even without nichayasa it is permitted (Tur in the name of Rashi and Rosh). And some say there is no difference between a place where they remove and a place where they do not remove; rather, in both, with nichayasa it is permitted and without nichayasa it is forbidden (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba), and one should follow the custom in this matter (a responsum of Rashba and Sefer HaTerumos). And in these lands they practice the permission of mashkanta b’nichayasa even if he can remove (Rashba and Nimukei Yosef and Maggid Mishneh wrote this custom). And there is no difference in this between a field and a house (Tur in the name of R' Yitzchak and Rabbeinu Tam and Rabbeinu Yerucham; and see Bach, who also holds that Rosh holds so), or other movable property (Mordechai; and Beit Yosef at the end of this siman in the name of a responsum of Rashba; and see in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 73 in the gloss), for with anything, b’nichayasa is permitted. And some permit lending on seforim or seats of a beis haknesses and to sit on them even without nichayasa, for it is for the needs of a mitzvah, and it is permitted to borrow with interest for the needs of a mitzvah (a responsum of Maharil, siman 37, and the Agudah), and it is good to be stringent to do b’nichayasa. And all this is only when responsibility for the collateral is on the lender; but when the borrower writes to him responsibility on his other assets and the lender cannot incur any loss, it is forbidden (Tur). And in every place that the mashkanta is avak ribbis, if the borrower comes to deduct for the lender the fruits he ate, we do not deduct for him, since the mashkanta is in the lender’s domain and he borrowed on it initially; if we would deduct for him from his debt, it would be as if we extract the avak ribbis from him in beis din (Tur). However, if he said to him, “I do not want you to eat my fruits as interest,” and he ate them afterward, then we deduct for him the fruits that he ate afterward, even in a place where they do not remove (Beit Yosef in the name of Ra’avad; and in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 305, in the name of Ramban).}

**** ENGLISH ****
A non-Jew who pawned his courtyard to a Jew, and the non-Jew went back and sold it to another Jew: the one to whom it was pawned is not obligated to pay rent to the Jew from the time the Jew bought it; rather, he lives in the courtyard without rent until the non-Jew returns to him the money that he has on this courtyard, for it is in the authority of the one to whom it was pawned, by their law, until he gives him his money and withdraws.
