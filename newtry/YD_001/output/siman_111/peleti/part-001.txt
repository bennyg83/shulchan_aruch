Even if the heter in the pot is not greater — the Shach wrote in the name of Nemukei Yosef that he wrote, and these are his words: it is possible that regarding stringent issurei mashahu we do not say "I say," even for d'rabbanan issurim with less than an increase; but with an increase we say "I say," even for Torah issur. The Peri Chadash challenged: since issur mashahu is only d'rabbanan, it is proper to suspend to be lenient like other d'rabbanan issurim, for even though they were stringent about mashahu here they were not stringent. His words are correct in general; but if we analyze what issurei mashahu exist among d'rabbanan issurim, one almost finds only hard chametz which is d'rabbanan — nevertheless many authors forbid it in mashahu; and likewise tevel that is d'rabbanan, dough from which challah was not taken in the present era; and in these one may truly doubt, for that which we say we suspend without increase is because we rely on a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient — whereas tevel and chametz, the root of their issur is davar sheyesh lo matirin as is known; and in Beitzah, even though the issur is d'rabbanan, we say the doubt is stringent — and if so, perhaps also in these, where the doubt is stringent, we do not say we suspend without increase; requires study, investigate.

And the same law, etc. — the Shach's view is specifically with two pieces, one issur and one heter, recognizable and standing by themselves, this issur and this heter; one fell and both are lost until it is unknown whether issur or heter fell. But if the pieces mixed before falling, issur spread through all of them one with one, issur was established, and whichever fell is as if issur fell. The Shach himself strained in the Perishah's intent and the Peri Chadash challenged that what he wrote — even when recognizable we call it established forbidden — whereas we concluded above that a d'rabbanan doubt is lenient. I wonder: is there not a complete mishnah in Terumot, chapter 7, mishnah 5 — two baskets, one terumah and one hullin, etc., and it is not known which is terumah and which hullin; if one ate from one he is exempt, etc.; one of them fell into hullin — it does not cause dimu'a, and the second is treated as terumah and liable for challah, and Rabbi Yosi exempts; a second fell elsewhere — it does not cause dimu'a; both fell in one place — it causes dimu'a, etc., end. And it is difficult: these se'ah are unknown which is terumah and which hullin — thus they were mixed and forbidden was established as the Shach wrote — so why do we not say "I say" regarding them? And if so, why when it fell into hullin does it not cause dimu'a — do we not say "I say" that it fell into hullin? Requires study. Yet one may understand: if so, even when they were mixed, when one fell into one of them we suspend to be lenient with "I say," etc.; and for d'rabbanan issur we do not require greatness, for for d'oraisa we require increase — and if so it is difficult what we teach: an idolatrous ring fell into ten thousand and from ten thousand into three — forbidden; why? Those thousands have one of issur and resemble two baskets of issur and one of heter mixed one with another, and when one fell elsewhere to three and the like — do we not say heter fell into heter? And if there is a majority of heter as we teach for three, even for d'oraisa we say so — and why mention that a ring is an important item, for the Shach and Peri Chadash raised that even for an important item we say "I say"? Requires study, apparently. Unless we say that they are never nullified even in thousands, even with "I say" we do not say regarding them — and if so, these are the things said in Nemukei Yosef that for issurei mashahu we do not say "I say."

Also in this we suspend, etc. — the Shach and Peri Chadash stood that the comparison from those cases mentioned is not equal: in the first case there are two fallings of heter and issur; even if issur is greater than heter it helps. It appears to me that the Tur and Mechaber do not hold so; and although the Rashba said this, it is the reading he had from the Tosefta that caused him, for it teaches: two spice-measures, one hullin and one terumah, and he put from one of them and it is not known from which — I say from hullin he put, since it teaches stam "and he put" and did not specify inside the pot — we learn there is no insistence to put in whichever place he wishes, even a small vessel among small ones; and thus issur is greater and nevertheless we say "I say," and from this the Rashba learns his law. **** ENGLISH ****
If issur is greater, etc. — this is the Rashba's view, that he holds like one version in Yevamot; but the Raah challenged and holds that even if issur is greater. Apparently the Raah has a decisive proof from Pesachim chapter 1: two baskets, one chametz and one matzah, and before them two houses, one checked and one not; a mouse came, took, and entered the houses — we say this is two baskets: chametz entered the unchecked, matzah entered the checked. It is difficult: is this not analogous to saying heter fell into heter — that issur does not exceed heter? If so, even issur into heter is half-and-half and we are lenient, unlike here with chametz: if chametz came into a checked house, behold all is issur and there is no heter against it — why do we permit it? See there in Pesachim; Tosafot also wrote: if it deals without nullification and it is d'oraisa it cannot be according to Rabbi Yochanan who requires increase — and if so this too is like it; requires study, apparently. Perhaps one may say: requiring that issur not be greater than heter is because then presumption of heter leaves the heter, for issur is greater upon it and it has presumption of issur; and this does not apply to houses, for a checked house certainly has presumption of heter. And further: therefore we require that issur not be greater than heter — when they are equal it is half-and-half, and one who eats from the pot into which the basket fell has two doubts: doubt that nothing fell from the issur at all, and if it fell, lest what he eats is the heter (see Tosafot Pesachim that all deals with dry in dry, see there, and see what I wrote above siman 109 at length). Unlike if a majority of issur fell — why say lest what he eats is heter? Behold issur is the majority and whatever separates from the majority is presumed separated, and he certainly eats issur — therefore it is forbidden, for on this "I say" they did not rely; without falling it is forbidden for he certainly eats what fell since it is the majority and from the majority we presume separation — therefore they were stringent, unlike regarding houses where this does not apply and a majority is not required; investigate.

If the heter was greater than the issur, etc. — this law requires study, especially regarding the Rashba, for we say in the Gemara two baskets, one hullin, etc. — we say hullin into hullin fell; and the Gemara challenges: according to the one who says an olive in the measure of eating a peras is d'oraisa, how do we say "I say"? And it answers: terumah that is d'rabbanan. Tosafot challenged in Chullin: it deals with its species and by Torah law it is nullified in the majority; and Rabbi Yochanan establishes it in Yevamot — that baraita when issur is greater upon heter; why need not its species without the measure of eating a peras — behold the taste is like the essence of d'oraisa; and necessarily in species not in its species; and Tosafot answer that thus the Gemara challenges: if you hold an olive in the measure of eating a peras is d'oraisa in not its species, even in its species the Sages should decree and forbid because of not its species, etc., end. And accordingly we who hold taste is d'oraisa must answer Tosafot's challenge as Tosafot say: if an olive in the measure of eating a peras is d'oraisa, even in its species one should decree, and on this the Gemara answers terumah that is d'rabbanan; for d'oraisa we decree. And if so, how does Shulchan Aruch explain for d'oraisa issur when there is a majority we say "I say" — for in not its species an olive in the measure of eating a peras is d'oraisa, even in its species one should decree; requires study. One may strain and say Shulchan Aruch chose another answer to Tosafot's challenge — "according to your reasoning," etc. But what shall we say of the Rashba who himself brought Tosafot's challenge and how can we say "I say" in d'oraisa doubt with a majority? Requires study. The great Rabbi my colleague Rabbi Shalom R"B resolved: when we need Tosafot's answer, it is if we hold taste is d'oraisa — then it is difficult to say in not its species that taste is d'oraisa, and it must deal with its species — and it is difficult, behold it is nullified in the majority, and one must say the majority does not help because of a decree because of not its species, etc.; but taste is not d'oraisa, and one may say it deals with not its species — then the challenge is well: an olive in the measure of eating a peras is d'oraisa, how are they nullified by the majority? Tosafot's answer is not needed. Behold this dispute of the rabbis whether taste is d'oraisa or not — the Rashba wrote: since it is Torah issur its law is to be stringent — meaning where there is no Torah issur we do not go to be stringent; if so, accordingly, when there is sixty, if taste is not d'oraisa there is no challenge, and necessarily from Tosafot's place it is proven one must decree regarding an olive in the measure of eating a peras, species because of not its species; but if taste is not d'oraisa there is no challenge, and if we are responsible for Tosafot's answer that one must decree, etc. — accordingly the view that one must decree is only a d'rabbanan decree, and for d'rabbanan we follow the lenient and say taste is not d'oraisa and we do not decree at all — and this is the Mechaber's intent; it requires further study, end of the words of Peh HaChochma Chen. It appears to me one should understand why in species in not its species it is not nullified in the majority and by Torah law it is nullified in the majority, and we do not decree in its species because of not its species — and necessarily one must say that here one cannot err, for everyone knows that by Torah law it is nullified in the majority, for there is its majority of heter and one of its species; but in not its species nullification in the majority does not apply since they are not one kind — and this is plain and necessary. Yet accordingly it is difficult how it can be thought to forbid in the measure of eating a peras in its species as a decree because of not its species — in not its species there is no nullification in the majority and we properly forbid an olive in the measure of eating a peras; but species in its species where there is a majority we do not say an olive in the measure of eating a peras, and in the majority itself it is not fit to say because of not its species as written, for regarding majority one cannot compare this to that or err — and apparently there is no place for Tosafot's answer, only for worldly issurim where truly by Torah law it is nullified in the majority and permitted to eat, and in not its species it is forbidden — so how can we decree an olive in the measure of eating a peras in its species because of not its species — this majority is forbidden and this permitted? But for Tosafot's answer in terumah, that even by Torah law it is not nullified in the majority and requires a hundred, and nevertheless for an olive in the measure of eating a peras they are lenient and say "I say" — one may fear even in not its species, err and be lenient; and one may say: this case is different, its species that is permitted in its majority and the difference is recognizable — for also in its species in its majority it is forbidden and requires a hundred, only because of "I say" they are lenient, fearing lest in not its species too one will be lenient; and the entire Tosafot answer speaks only of terumah, but for worldly issurim Tosafot's answer does not apply and there is no place to decree — and this is Shulchan Aruch's law; it requires further study, investigate well.
