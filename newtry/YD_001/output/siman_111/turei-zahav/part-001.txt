"Also in this we suspend to be lenient." The language implies that here there was more reason to be stringent, hence it had to say "also in this"; and in truth it is all the more so, as the Beit Yosef wrote that this case is all the more so — from the Rashba in Torat HaBayit HaArukh, because we did not see anything fall into heter; and in the Perishah he thought this all the more so that the Beit Yosef wrote is his own words and therefore challenged him and said it is not all the more so, and he entered strained matters and was not precise. It appears to me to reconcile one who further analyzed what he wrote afterward: but if issur is greater than heter we do not suspend to be lenient — "all the more so" — he challenged: what is his intent with the language "all the more so" that he used, and why did he not say we do not suspend to be lenient as he wrote afterward for Torah issur? It appears to me that in the Gemara, chapter ha'Areil (Yevamot daf 82), it challenges Rabbi Yochanan from a mikveh and answers: no, we do not take the majority; and if you wish, say "I say" there, for one may say "I say" — meaning, lest it fell into terumah and there is no issur at all; and everywhere we hold like the latter language. And here, in the latter language, one may explain two meanings: one — since one may say "I say," therefore there is room to be lenient even if only a minority of hullin remained, nevertheless it is permitted — and this is the Raah's view in Bedek HaBayit. It is also possible to say: in a mikveh a majority must remain, and here half suffices; and so Mishmeret HaBayit. Therefore the Rashba said here: if only a minority of heter remained — although one may explain thus, nevertheless we do not follow such leniency "all the more so," since the leniency is by mere suspension — for here there is leniency in half, unlike a mikveh. And one may say that which he wrote before, "also in this we suspend," intended: it should not be that in this case of only one falling and a pot of issur present, one might say in every case there is room to be lenient and the leniency is clear in this — and there is a difference from what we wrote afterward regarding not suspending so much to be lenient; therefore he said here "also in this we suspend to be lenient," meaning there is no clear leniency here, only by mere suspension; therefore also here we do not suspend so much to be lenient. And it appears one should challenge what is written in this siman that if there is doubt whether d'oraisa issur fell, it is permitted at a d'rabbanan measure — and this contradicts the rule in siman 66, seif 4, that here we do not rule leniently but it is like certain d'oraisa; it requires further study, for here the heter is by force of "I say," and we explicitly say in chapter ha'Areil that I cited nearby that every doubt in which one can say "I say" that no issur fell here is more lenient.

**** ENGLISH ****
"But if issur is greater, etc." One should not challenge: since one knows the measure of heter in the pot and the issur — if so, let us see whether the heter in the pot increased from what was certainly there, issur fell; one may say this knows clearly there was more issur, such as he heard from another — but he does not know how much heter there was itself, whether it was as much as the heter now or less, until with the issur that fell there it became so much. And in Derishah he explains this regarding two dry pieces — that is, he knows the measure of issur and not the measure of heter; and now he finds two pieces, one large and one small, and he knows at least the small one is not issur, and it is unknown whether the large is issur — automatically there is a majority of heter over issur, or the large is also heter and issur fell into the issur pot.

**** ENGLISH ****
"Except after he mixes them." Derishah uPerishah wrote, and these are his words: however, in Issur VeHeter, general rule 26, Maharish responded that we do not rule so, even for one person; and even for one who would permit, one may not eat except after they mix together, for then the taste of issur is already nullified, end of his words. It appears to me astonishing at what he wrote that one who permits does not permit except after mixing — this is not so, for the Rashba permits, and he brings in Torat HaBayit HaArukh, daf 114, Yerushalmi: Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Chiya in the name of Rabbi Shimon — before him two baskets, in this fifty se'ah and in that fifty se'ah; a se'ah of terumah fell into one and it is not known into which — if he wished to separate from this he separates, from that he separates, half from this and half from that he separates; and therefore if in one there were a hundred and in one fifty, I say they fell into the hundred — and from there he raises and not from the fifty, end, meaning for giving the kohen's portion one gives only from where terumah was nullified. And if you say there is no heter except after mixing — how is it fit to say he separates half from this and half from that? Behold they are already mixed and all became one; otherwise all is forbidden. And further: if there is no heter except after mixing, why did he use the language that both combine — combination is not applicable here since they are already one through mixing; therefore it appears plain that even if they were not mixed, since by Torah law it is permitted through nullification in the majority but d'rabbanan requires sixty, and since there is doubt into which it fell and both entered doubt — both combine to nullify and each is permitted by itself, since they will become mixed we view them as already mixed. And what Issur VeHeter wrote: even for one who permits — he did not intend the Rashba's view who permits here, rather: one should not rule thus for halachah to be lenient, and even one who wishes to permit for halachah should at least not permit except after mixing; but forever the Rashba's view is permitted even without mixing — that after mixing it is permitted even for two people, for one may say that with two people it is nevertheless forbidden l'chatchila to mix because of nullifying issur l'chatchila, as above siman 99, seif 5; and for one person it is permitted l'chatchila to mix — for if you say permitted l'chatchila to mix because we view it as already mixed, we too say that in truth they are permitted even without mixing, since we view them as already mixed. Rather necessarily in truth it is so, only he was stringent here to mix first — and this is also the Rama's intent here. I wrote all this because Maharach wrote that the permission here is only for those who hold chein nafsho applies only to meat and milk, as written in siman 92 and 99; but for us, who establish chein nafsho for all issurim, there is no permission — for what fell into one of them became nevelah — this does not appear at all, for since both entered doubt it is as if they were already in one vessel and afterward fell there, as we proved shortly — and this is a plain, clear matter: for even according to those who say chein nafsho for other issurim, nevertheless one does not nullify issur l'chatchila — and if so, since here we left that rule and say we nullify l'chatchila, it itself also helps for everyone regarding not calling it nevelah — so it appears clear and plain to the understanding.
