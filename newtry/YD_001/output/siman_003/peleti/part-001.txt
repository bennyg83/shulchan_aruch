Does not require intention: we say in Gemara Chullin 32a Rava said: he slaughtered a cow and another animal was slaughtered with it—per R. Natan {Rashi explained: he renders chullin shechitah kosher without intention}; cow invalid, animal kosher—for without intention he renders kosher; but the cow invalid even though also without intention, and they are not disqualified because of melachah, nevertheless they are disqualified because of “and he slaughter it” and not its companion—end quote. Rambam ruled chapter 4 of Parah halachah 18: if another was slaughtered with it, the cow too is kosher—for he did not intend melachah. Maggid Mishneh wondered—he is against the arranged Gemara above, and especially since Rambam ruled in Hilchot Shechitah like R. Natan that chullin does not require intention; Maggid Mishneh wrote perhaps Rambam had another girsa here—tzarich iyun. And what I suggested to resolve: Rambam holds that which we say regarding slaughtering another without intention—nevertheless invalid because of “it and not its companion,” even though he did not intend to slaughter its companion at all; automatically it was slaughtered—for otherwise why need this verse “it and not its companion” with intention—in any case invalid because of melachah and hesech hada'at like below. But one may say in such a case: certainly he did not move one head of two animals as one; only he went or brought a knife to slaughter two animals—cow and chullin—as one; if so by way of parable: if he brought a knife along the length of the chullin neck and afterward the cow before the knife reached to slaughter the cow's neck, already at least majority of the chullin neck was slaughtered per manner and order of shechitah—if so when he began to slaughter the cow and already majority of chullin was slaughtered, melachah went for him; not comparable to below where cut at time of shechitah; if so because of melachah kosher, and Scripture teaches nevertheless invalid because of “it and not its companion.” But always one may say it deals with intention—must understand in both ways: if you say in cow slaughter chullin slaughter already finished and no longer disqualified because of melachah, only because of “it,” etc.—also because of “it,” etc., it should not be disqualified, for slaughter already finished and this is not at once. And if we say nevertheless it is “it,” etc., melachah too applies. But one may distinguish: certainly if majority of chullin slaughtered—even if minority slaughtered afterward, melachah already finished, for majority slaughtered—life and treifah and kashrut depend on majority; but regarding name of shechitah: so too called slaughtered—even though already slaughtered majority, nevertheless called shechitah; and so Rashi explicitly regarding the question if he waited and hichlid in minority of simanim—well said: even though already slaughtered majority from chullin, nevertheless since before he finished slaughter he began to slaughter the cow—as one shechitah it is considered and disqualifies because of “it,” etc.; not comparable to one who slaughtered one in morning and second in evening—one shechitah it is. But this is if shechitah exists from beginning to end; but if shechitah exists only at the end and beginning of shechitah is not called shechitah—and if he slaughtered kodshim outside he does not disqualify; and if he stripped on the cow before slaughtering majority—does not impart impurity to garments, as explained there Chullin 29—see there. If so if majority from chullin slaughtered before he began slaughtering the cow—will not escape, for before he slaughtered majority from the cow, chullin slaughter already entirely finished; in this certainly “it” does not apply—like one in morning and one in evening; if so Rava must hold Zevachim that shechitah exists only at the end; if so unnecessary the verse “it”—comes regarding those who slaughtered chullin and cow in one bringing one after another; if so before he was lenient to slaughter the cow, majority of chullin already slaughtered; if so before majority of cow slaughtered, chullin slaughter already finished and “it,” etc., does not apply—this morning and this evening, for we hold shechitah exists only at the end. And as written—perforce “it” comes to exclude chullin slaughtered without intention, and melachah does not apply; nevertheless Torah excluded “it.” But we do not hold like Rava, only like R. Yochanan who holds shechitah exists from beginning to end, as Rambam wrote chapter 4 Hilchos Shechitah; if so well said as I wrote there is difference between “it,” etc., and melachah, and no need to strain to say verse “and he slaughter” deals with intention to exclude chullin without intention—and therefore Rambam renders kosher; think.

Human force and we do not require da'at gavra—for even minors their shechitah is kosher; we mentioned this matter, for I did not understand apparently Tosafot's words who challenged Chullin 12 s.v. ma'an tana that implies even with others standing over them they lack da'at; and in Gittin we say when others stand over them they have da'at—and they expanded and I did not know from where we learn this—perhaps the Gemara truly did not infer from “others standing over them,” only from the reisha that teaches they do not slaughter lest they ruin—and deals without others standing over; and difficulty: “lest they ruin”—he ruins and stands, and there is no shechitah at all, for there is no intention at all; and for Rabbanan it requires intention, and without this there is no shechitah; and even if we say per Rashi who infers they do not slaughter l'chatchila even with others standing over—one may say also besides minors it deals with standing over, and nevertheless l'chatchila invalid—for this is not continuation and connection of mishnah that teaches “and if they slaughtered their slaughter is kosher” except minors—teaches clearly even b'dieved invalid; perforce deals without standing over; and implies we are concerned only lest they ruin, not in shechitah itself; strained to say one may say mishnah teaches for all—even per Rabbanan and per R. Natan; therefore teaches “lest they ruin” because of R. Natan—for whom shechitah itself is kosher, nevertheless because “lest they ruin” invalid; not so Rabbanan—in truth invalid because there is no intention for shechitah; therefore Gemara infers from siyafa it is R. Natan and not Rabbanan—for Rabbanan standing over does not help render kosher, for there is no intention; thereby Tosafot infer well. But this is strained—from where for Tosafot to say so and challenge—did not explain as plain that main inference is from reisha. Perhaps one may say that which standing over helps for intention—because we say with his da'at he does and slaughters; if so only if at beginning when he began to slaughter the adult stood and afterward left—kosher because of intention that with his da'at he slaughtered; but for “lest they ruin” requires standing from beginning to end; if so one may say that which we teach minors “lest they ruin”—stood at beginning of slaughter and left; but “all who slaughtered” and others see from beginning to end—kosher; so much the more; tzarich iyun.
