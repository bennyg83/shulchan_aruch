**** ENGLISH ****
If he forbade her not to derive benefit from him, it does not take effect, for behold he is obligated to her. And if he said to her, "Go—your handiwork is for your sustenance," and she provides from them for large matters but does not provide for small matters—if her entire family is accustomed to those small matters, it does not take effect, for behold he is obligated to her regarding them as well. And if not all her family is accustomed to them but only the wealthy, and her father is wealthy and accustomed to them, and her husband is not wealthy and not accustomed to them—the vow takes effect, for behold he is not obligated to her for them. Nevertheless, since he forbade her benefit from him and she returned to her father's house, he is now obligated to her for them; therefore, for up to thirty days he provides her support for those small matters through a supporter who says, "Whoever feeds does not lose," and he feeds her and collects afterward from the husband. It makes no difference whether he forbade her for thirty days or more, or forbade her without specification—after thirty days he divorces her and gives the ketubah.

**** ENGLISH ****
If he forbade her not to adorn herself, or not to eat one of the kinds—even if it is bad and she never tasted it—and attached it to marital relations, having said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if you adorn yourself or eat such-and-such a kind;" or if he said, "Konam, the benefit of your marital relations is forbidden to me if you go to your father's house" more than once a month if he lives with her in the city, or more than twice a year if he lives in another city—he upholds it for seven days and then divorces her and gives the ketubah.

And if she vowed, having said, "Konam is such-and-such a kind upon me," or "Konam that I not adorn myself with such-and-such adornment," or she said, "Your marital relations shall be forbidden to me if I eat such-and-such a kind or adorn myself with such-and-such adornment," or "if I go to my father's house," and he heard and did not annul for her—he divorces her immediately and gives the ketubah. And according to the Rambam, the vow is hers and he upholds it for her: if he wishes to remain and she does not eat of that kind, she sits; and if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," he divorces her and gives the ketubah.

The same law applies if she vowed not to go to her father's house, having said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I go to my father's house," and the husband heard and did not annul. And similarly if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I go to a house of mourning or to a house of feasting," and he heard and did not annul for her. The same law applies if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I do any work," and he heard and did not annul for her. The same law applies if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I borrow anything from a neighbor or if I lend a utensil," and he heard and did not annul.
