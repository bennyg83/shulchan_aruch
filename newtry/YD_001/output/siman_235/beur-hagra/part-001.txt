<b>ותלאו כו' או כו'. </b>ע"ש הסוגיא ע"א א' ב' תנן המדיר כו' עד הוא דסני לי וע' תוס' שם ד"ה בשלמא וד"ה שנדרה כו' והר"נ ביאר יותר דקושית הגמ' תנן כו' דע"כ א"א לומר שהדירה סתם שלא תטעום כו' דלאו כל כמיניה כמש"ש נ"ט א' וש"מ אלא ע"כ שהדירה מנכסיו אם תטעום כו' וא"כ תטעום ותמתין ל' יום נהי דבטעימת פירות א"א כלל להמתין אבל מליהנות לו תמתין ל' יום כמו ברישא וכן איתא בירושלמי המדיר את אשתו שלא תטעום ויש אדם מדיר את אשתו באומר לה אם תאכלי מחפץ פלוני תהא אסורה מנכסי ותאכל מאותו חפץ ותהא אסורה מנכסיו בשנדרה אשתו ושמע בעלה ולא הפר וכתי' גמ' שלנו ותוס' ד"ה בשלמא פי' דתלנהו בתשמיש וקפריך תאכל ותמתין ז' ימים ואין נ"מ בזה אלא דתוס' מפרשים כעין סוגין דמפרש לקמן אלא דתלנהו כו' והר"נ מפרש כסוגין דירושלמי ומשני גמ' הב"ע כגון שנדרה כו' ולא תלנהו כלל דא"א להמתין כלל כנ"ל וא"ת מה תרויח בגט וי"ל דאפ"ה י"ל לא בעינא גברא דמקיים לי נדרי ותוס' ד"ה שנדרה פי' דנדרה כ"ז שהיא תחתיו ומיהו במסקנא אמרינן אלא הב"ע כגון דתלנהו כו' אבל הכא דנדרה כו' אפי' תלנהו בתשמיש או ליהנות מנכסיו אפ"ה יוציא מיד ואף שזה התי' הוא לר' יוסי דוקא ואליבא דר"ה ועתוס' שם ה"ג רי"א כו' ואנן לא קי"ל כן כמ"ש בסי' רל"ד סנ"ט מ"מ צריכין להאי שינוייא אף לרבנן דהא סיפא ע"מ שתאמרי לפלוני כו' לאו עינוי נפש ולא מדברים שבינו לבינה וצ"ל ע"כ כה"ג והטור כתב דכן כל המתני' דשם שלא תלך לבית אביה ולבית האבל והמשתה כולם לאו ע"ג ולאו דשבל"ב וא"י להפר ועבא"ע שם סד"ה. נמצא הכלל דבהדיר הוא כגון דתלנהו בתשמיש תמתין ז' ימים אבל בנדרה איהי ושתק הוא או קיים בין דתלנהי בתשמיש או לא יוציא מיד דקי"ל כר"י ור"י דהוא נתן כו' וכסתם מתני' דידן וע"פ זה נתבאר כל הסימן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he made it contingent, etc., or, etc.</b> See the discussion there on 71a to 71b, from “We learned: one who imposes a vow,” etc., until “it is he who hates me.” See Tosafot there, s.v. “Granted,” and s.v. “that she vowed,” etc. The Ran explains the Gemara's question more fully. When it asks, “We learned,” etc., it cannot mean that he simply prohibited her from tasting, etc., because he has no power to do so, as stated there on 59a and elsewhere. Rather, he must have forbidden his property to her if she tastes, etc. If so, she should taste and then wait thirty days. Although with respect to tasting fruit she cannot wait at all, with respect to deriving benefit from him she can wait thirty days, as in the first clause. This is also stated in the Yerushalmi: “One who imposes a vow upon his wife that she not taste,” but can a person impose a vow upon his wife? Rather, he says to her, “If you eat from a certain item, my property shall be forbidden to you.” She may eat from that item and thereby become forbidden to benefit from his property. The case is where his wife vowed and her husband heard and did not annul it. This accords with the answer of our Gemara. Tosafot, s.v. “Granted,” explain that he made the prohibition contingent upon marital relations. The Gemara asks that she should eat and then wait seven days. There is no practical difference between these explanations, except that Tosafot interpret the case in the manner of our Gemara's later explanation, namely that he made it contingent, etc., while the Ran interprets it in accordance with the Yerushalmi. The Gemara answers that the case is where she herself vowed, etc., without making it contingent at all, so that she cannot wait at all, as explained above. One may ask what she gains through divorce. The answer is that even so she may say, “I do not want a husband who upholds my vows.” Tosafot, s.v. “that she vowed,” explain that she vowed for as long as she remains married to him. Yet in the conclusion the Gemara states: “Rather, the case is where he made it contingent,” etc. However, here, where she vowed, etc., even if she made the vow contingent upon marital relations or upon benefiting from his property, he must nevertheless divorce her immediately. Although this answer was stated specifically according to Rabbi Yose and following Rav Huna's interpretation, see Tosafot there, s.v. “This is the correct text: Rabbi Yose says,” etc., and we do not rule that way, as stated in siman 234, seif 59, we still require this answer even according to the Sages. The final clause, concerning a condition that she tell a certain person, etc., involves neither personal affliction nor matters between husband and wife, so he cannot annul it. It must therefore be understood in this manner. The Tur writes that the same applies to the entire Mishnah there, including the cases where she may not go to her father's house, to a house of mourning, or to a wedding feast. None of these vows involves personal affliction or matters between husband and wife, and he cannot annul them. See Even HaEzer there, seif 4. The governing rule is therefore as follows: when he imposed the vow by making it contingent upon marital relations, she waits seven days. When she herself vowed and he remained silent or upheld the vow, whether or not she made it contingent upon marital relations, he must divorce her immediately. We rule like Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, that he is deemed to have caused the separation, and in accordance with the anonymous Mishnah here. On this basis the entire siman is explained.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The same applies, etc.</b> Rambam wrote this only regarding a vow that she not taste one of all kinds of fruit. He writes: “If one imposes a vow,” etc., “they allow him up to thirty days,” etc.; “if she vowed,” etc., “if he wishes,” etc. By “one who imposes a vow,” he means that the husband forbade his property to her, as stated in the Yerushalmi. He omitted the Gemara's concluding explanation that the prohibition was made contingent, etc., because that explanation follows Rav Huna according to Rabbi Yose, as explained above. In all the later clauses, however, he merely writes “one who imposes a vow,” using the wording of the Mishnah, as is his practice. The Maggid Mishneh explains that here too “one who imposes a vow” means that he forbade his property to her, as above, and she therefore waits thirty days. Rambam relied upon what he wrote in the first clause concerning a vow that she not taste. Likewise, in all these cases he did not write the law where she herself vowed. The Mechaber derived it from the preceding discussion.
