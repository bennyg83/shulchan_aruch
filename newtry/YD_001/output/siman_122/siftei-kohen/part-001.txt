["And we are not concerned b'dieved. It does not mean that the food that was used in it is permitted b'dieved — that is obvious; and furthermore he has not yet mentioned anything of the food — rather it means that since they already placed it in the hand of a non-Jew and took it from him, we are not concerned b'dieved and it is permitted to use it even l'chatchila, as Ra'ah wrote and he brings it in Turei Chayim general rule 59 law 4; and so too in the simanim of Turei Chayim there — to exclude that l'chatchila it is forbidden to give it to a non-Jew with intent that he leave it afterward from time to time; and even though Ra'ah spoke only when the Jew left it from time to time it is permitted to use l'chatchila, it appears to the Rav that all the more so whenever we can say it stood in the hand of a non-Jew it is permitted, for it is not comparable to a utensil of a non-Jew; and so it appears in the simanim of Chavot Yair there; and even though in Turei Chayim there it appears that for Ra'ah, if one used it the same day without hag'alah the food is permitted — that is according to the Rav's view; but Issur VeHeter who holds the food is forbidden, and like the plain meaning of Mordechai's words, one may say that both exist: even though the food is forbidden — that is because one used it the same day — but whatever stood from time to time, even l'chatchila, is permitted; and for this the Rav wrote in hagahah here at the beginning in stam that we are not concerned, and that is when it stood from time to time that it is permitted to use l'chatchila; and afterward he wrote the view of those who forbid the food; and even though in Turei Chayim there it appears that for Issur VeHeter who forbids the food, he also forbids using the utensil even if it stood from time to time — nevertheless for the halachah it appears to the Rav to distinguish as he wrote; and all this according to the Rav; but Bach in this siman and in siman 137 disagrees and wrote: where it stood in the hand of a non-Jew half a day and he returned it the same day — it requires hag'alah, and if it is earthenware it requires breaking, and standing from time to time does not help; and if one used it the same day the food is forbidden — and this is like the view of Issur VeHeter who explained thus Mordechai's words in the name of Rabbeinu Tam; and where it stood in the hand of a non-Jew for an hour — standing helps to use l'chatchila, and in this the Orach Chayim in the name of Ra'ah deals; and if one used it without standing the food is permitted — and that is what Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam; but for an hour one need not be concerned — end of his words; however what Bach challenged below siman 137 from what Tur and Rashi and the poskim wrote, that one who sends a wine utensil in the hand of a non-Jew even one hour requires kashering, etc., and he strained greatly, and the conclusion of his words is that Rabbeinu Tam in Mordechai does not hold like Tur and his companions; and for the halachah there too it is permitted to leave them from time to time and to use them l'chatchila; and where it stood in the hand of a non-Jew a day or two days it is forbidden to use them l'chatchila in any case, like utensils of a non-Jew themselves — and not like R' Shimshon of Bunberg in Mordechai who is alone against Tur and Rashi and the poskim who require kashering for a wine utensil; and for Rabbeinu Tam and Ra'ah in a cauldron whose use is hot he agrees that kashering is required — and not like Beit Yosef wrote in this siman 122 that Rabbeinu Tam and R' Shimshon of Bunberg agree on this matter — end of his words; and I did not descend to the end of his intent, for there with a wine utensil standing from time to time is not relevant, as we hold below siman 137 that a wine utensil is not subject to distinguishing between ben yomo and not ben yomo (and so too Ran at the end of Avodah Zarah); and as Ra'ash proved in responsum general rule 19 seif 96 that as long as twelve months have not passed upon them there is in them ta'am for improvement — therefore kashering is required for a wine utensil for Rashi and Tur and their companions, for Rabbeinu Tam's reason does not apply to them, that even if one used them it would be spoiled after from time to time; but in a cauldron everyone agrees to R' Shimshon of Bunberg that it is permitted to use them l'chatchila immediately, for the reason Maharshal wrote there: establish the cauldron on its presumption (and for this reason some say for utensils of gentiles that are forbidden to use l'chatchila — for a utensil of a Jew we say establish it on its presumption); and say it did not use issur, and furthermore even if it used it would be spoiled that is not ben yomo, for stam utensils of a non-Jew we establish them thus, as Rabbeinu Tam's words, and it would be a doubt of rabbinic issur and to be lenient — end of his words; and from now there is no dispute among the poskim at all, and this is clear; and so in Mordechai Perek Elu Metziot he brought Rabbeinu Tam's words and R' Shimshon of Bunberg's words in stam one after the other; and all this is with utensils whose manner is to use them hot; but utensils whose manner is to use them cold — certainly rinsing suffices — investigate:"]
