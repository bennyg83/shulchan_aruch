and even though we are not concerned for the words of the non-Jew neither to forbid nor to permit — that is regarding that thing that was already in a presumption of kashrut; but here, since the presumption that stam utensils of gentiles are absorbed with issur has already been impaired, only through safek sefeika it came to permit — therefore we rely on his word; behold certainly there is safek sefeika here: a doubt lest he is lying in what he says that he used it today, for stam a non-Jew is presumed to lie, as is found in chapter Chazakat HaBatim regarding one who was forced for his donkey — from what is written "whose mouth speaks vanity," etc.; and furthermore what he wrote that here the presumption of kashrut of the utensil was impaired — why do you see that we follow the presumption of the utensil, go after the presumption of the dish that cooks in it that has a presumption of kashrut — and this is exactly like the sugya in the first chapter of Chullin: establish the knife on its presumption and say it did not spoil; and what he wrote that we do not rely on the words of the non-Jew — specifically when he intends to improve his merchandise — it is true that at the end of Yevamot it is found thus: a non-Jew who brings fruit to the market and said these fruits are of orlah, etc. — he is not believed neither to forbid nor to permit, only he intended to improve his merchandise; there the Gemara needed this, lest you say since he harms himself and says they are of orlah — certainly he spoke truth — to this he said on the contrary, he intends to improve his merchandise; but otherwise certainly the non-Jew is not believed at all, even to forbid, even where improving merchandise is not relevant — he would say thus, for even a Jew is not believed to forbid his fellow's property as long as it is not in his hand — and so it is found in Mordechai perek R. But for an hour one need not be concerned. And all this when one wishes to use, etc. This is from Mordechai's words in perek Ein Ma'amidin, and these are his words: stam utensils of gentiles are not ben yomo; and one may say it is a d'oraisa safek; one may say it is safek sefeika — a doubt whether they used it today, and if you say they used — lest with something that spoils they used; and according to this, when they bring knives to sharpen in the craftsman's house and return them the same day — there is not this reason of safek sefeika, because the same day he returned them, and there is no room to say a doubt lest he did not use at all, for certainly he is accustomed to use since no Jew stands over him — therefore it requires hag'alah; and so Rabbeinu Tam — one must go for sharpening and he was stringent with them more than utensils of a non-Jew themselves; and specifically when he left them half a day in the house of the non-Jew or more, but for an hour — no; however those knives that gentile servants carry in a Jew's house here and there — those one may permit for the reason of safek sefeika like utensils of a non-Jew, and it is proper to be stringent; and they asked Rav R' Shimshon of Bunberg only about a Jew who baked in the house of the non-Jew and forgot the cauldron that they heated hot water in the house of the non-Jew until the next day — and he answered: even though we learned, etc. — all the more with the non-Jew one may say he used them; however one may say establish the cauldron on its presumption and say it did not use issur, and even if it used it would be spoiled that is not ben yomo, and stam utensils of gentiles we establish them thus, as Rabbeinu Tam's words — end of his words; and Issur VeHeter general rule 33 wrote: Mordechai's words here where he was stringent at first with knives more than a non-Jew's cooking — presumably he speaks b'dieved, that if one cut boiling meat with that knife it is as if it is a non-Jew's ben yomo utensil — for otherwise what difference is there, behold even a utensil of a non-Jew requires hag'alah l'chatchila, and if one forgot an earthenware bowl at home — break it — end of his words; it appears that even where there is safek sefeika in a Jew's utensil that he left in a non-Jew's house it is forbidden even b'dieved; and so appears Mordechai's intent from that he said "however those knives," etc. — one may permit because of safek sefeika — it appears that above safek sefeika does not help for Rabbeinu Tam's stringency; and it is difficult: why did Rabbeinu Tam actually be stringent where there is safek sefeika, for in the entire Torah safek sefeika helps; and in Turei Chayim general rule 59 he wrote that Rabbeinu Tam was stringent l'chatchila here more than l'chatchila with a non-Jew's utensil, but b'dieved permitted — and it is not clear at all, since both are forbidden l'chatchila, what stringency in one more than its fellow; and furthermore we already proved from Mordechai that in Rabbeinu Tam's stringency safek sefeika does not help at all even b'dieved — he did not permit b'dieved except with gentile servants in a Jew's house specifically; and it appears to me: all safek sefeika that is permitted b'dieved is after safek sefeika came to be; but this Jew who intentionally leaves his knife in a non-Jew's house and relies that there will be safek sefeika here — one should be stringent; and accordingly if one forgot his knife in a non-Jew's house it is permitted where there is safek sefeika; and accordingly Mordechai's words afterward in the name of R' Shimshon of Bunberg to permit the cauldron for the reason of safek sefeika is resolved; and at first glance it contradicts what he wrote first in the name of Rabbeinu Tam to be stringent even with safek sefeika; and Beit Yosef at the end of this siman wrote that R' Shimshon permits since he returned them the next day — there is safek sefeika, and above he returned them the same day and there is only one doubt — and it is impossible to say this, since we already proved that even with safek sefeika Rabbeinu Tam is stringent; and Maharach wrote that R' Shimshon disagrees with the earlier case — and it does not appear to me thus, for Mordechai's language does not imply dispute, rather according to what I wrote it works out: Rabbeinu Tam was stringent only when placing intentionally, but when forgetting it is like a utensil of a non-Jew and permitted with safek sefeika; and R' Shimshon deals with forgetting and not like Issur VeHeter who is stringent even when forgetting; but nevertheless one may say that even R' Shimshon did not permit the cauldron except if one cooked in it b'dieved afterward — until here it appears to me that even where there is safek sefeika l'chatchila kashering is required since it is possible here; but with earthenware that requires breaking — here it would be l'chatchila permitted, for it is b'dieved; and we find such a distinction in perek Chomer BaKodesh (daf 22) that he distinguishes between earthenware and all other utensils; and even though it is according to Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel are lenient in all utensils — nevertheless Beit Shammai's reasoning is true where one should be stringent, such as here; and we say not to be stringent entirely; and further proof from what Tur wrote siman 142: a new oven that they lit with wood of issur — permitted l'chatchila to bake in it, etc., for it is considered b'dieved since it must be dismantled — end of his words; and so too here — so it appears to me for practical halachah; and all the above is where there is safek sefeika; but where there is no safek sefeika, such as that the non-Jew returned them the same day — forbidden by law, for there is only one doubt, as Mordechai wrote: there is no room to say lest he did not use, and if you say he used — lest with something that spoils, for every stam utensil that is used — and this is a regular doubt that is not considered a doubt at all, as Teshuvot HaRosh wrote siman 171 regarding wormy wheat that every regular doubt is not considered a doubt at all; and even though in siman 84 seif 9 I wrote that even a regular doubt is considered safek sefeika there — nevertheless here it is more regular, since Mordechai did not want to count it as safek sefeika; from that he does not distinguish how long they stood in the craftsman's house and when he returned them — it implies in all cases he speaks that one leaves it and it is permitted, and he disagrees with Mordechai above; and it appears we hold like Mordechai: first, Mordechai is a famous later decisor and in most of his matters we practice accordingly; and furthermore Ra'ah's words are puzzling, for behold with a utensil of a non-Jew it is also permitted b'dieved specifically, and we do not say leave them because of a decree lest one use it today — here too likewise; and in Turei Chayim general rule 59 he wrote that Ra'ah deals where he did not return them the same day — there is safek sefeika — then standing helps; and it appears that nevertheless Ra'ah's words are not for halachah, for if it deals with a matter where Rabbeinu Tam's stringency applies who is stringent even with safek sefeika with a Jew's utensil that he left with a non-Jew — certainly Ra'ah's words are void against Rabbeinu Tam's words; on the contrary, from there is proof to forbid, for there since the issur is only because of a decree it is like safek sefeika here automatically — where there is only one doubt it is forbidden even b'dieved; and in Turei Chayim there he rules that it appears to him for the halachah that b'dieved it is permitted, for there is no clarity that a non-Jew used it, even though he is accustomed to use — and this is not reasoning at all, as I brought proof above from Teshuvot HaRosh that it is not considered a doubt at all; and one cannot distinguish from his own reasoning to be lenient in what Mordechai explicitly forbids; also what Rama wrote here that there is only one doubt lest he did not use it, etc. — he should not have written thus, for this is not a doubt at all as Mordechai wrote, rather he should have written lest he used with something that spoils — this doubt Mordechai mentioned there and Tur in this siman regarding utensils of a non-Jew — end of his words; it appears there is no leniency b'dieved even for great need, not like Rama wrote nearby and in case of need one may be lenient b'dieved — rather even b'dieved forbidden: General rule: where there is safek sefeika, such as that they did not return it the same day — if one placed intentionally one should forbid the utensil and it requires hag'alah, and earthenware requires breaking; and if one forgot, there is room to permit the utensil, only if it is a utensil that can be kashered one kasheres it, and earthenware is permitted; and so with a wine utensil in siman 136; but where there is no safek sefeika, such as that they returned it the same day — one should forbid even b'dieved by law; and that is: if it is an eating utensil, the issur is only that it should stand with the non-Jew part of the day, a large measure that according to estimation of the mind he used it for eating issur;
