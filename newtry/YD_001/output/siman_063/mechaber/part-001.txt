**** ENGLISH ****
The law of meat that is found and whose identity is unknown. In it are 2 seifim. Meat that is found tossed in the market — one follows the majority, for everything that separates from the majority is presumed to have separated from the majority. If most of the sellers were idolaters — forbidden; if most of the sellers were Israelites — permitted. And likewise meat that is found in the hand of an idolater and it is not known from whom he took it: if the meat sellers were Israelites — permitted. This is Torah law. And the Sages already forbade all meat that is found, whether in the market or in the hand of an idolater, even though all the sellers and all the slaughterers are Israelites, except for one who bought meat and left it in his house and it disappeared from sight — forbidden, unless he had a siman upon it, or he had teviyat ayin upon it and he recognizes it with certainty that it is this one, or it was tied and sealed.

**** ENGLISH ****
If one hung a vessel full of pieces of meat and the vessel broke and the pieces fell to the ground, and one came and found pieces and has neither siman nor teviyat ayin upon them — behold this is forbidden, for one may say that the meat which was in the vessel was dragged off by a wild animal or creeping thing, and this is other meat. And if he hung it on a nail and the like, such that it is impossible for a creeping thing to take and replace — permitted. And there are those who permit meat that disappeared from sight if he found it in the place where he left it. {Rama: And the custom is to be lenient like the latter view, even if it was in the hand of an idolater, in a place where all the sellers are Israelites who sell kosher meat. And see below siman 118.}
