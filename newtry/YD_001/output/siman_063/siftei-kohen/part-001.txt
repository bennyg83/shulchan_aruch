**** ENGLISH ****
And the Sages already forbade all meat, etc. — It implies that whether found or in the hand of an idolater they forbade it because of meat that disappeared from sight; and so is implied from the words of the Rav HaMagid. And accordingly, even if it is known with certainty that the idolater took kosher meat from an Israelite — nevertheless it is forbidden because of meat that disappeared from sight — not as it appears at first glance from Taz, who in such a case permits, as he wrote: 'But the Sages already forbade, etc., even if they saw the idolater take it from an Israelite, lest he sold him nevelah or treifah, and there are those who permit if he saw,' etc. — it implies: but if it is known with certainty that he sold him kosher meat — permitted; and this is not so, but rather as I explained. And further, one may ask: if there is concern that the Israelite sold him treifot, how is it possible that 'there are those who permit if he saw'? What difference does it make that the sellers are Israelites — in the end the Israelites also sell treifot? Rather, certainly we require that the Israelites sell kosher meat and not treifot at all, as will be explained in s.k. 10. And it appears that Taz also holds that we require specifically that the Israelite sell kosher meat — only that for stringency he says: even though he certainly sells kosher meat, we say perhaps it happened that he sold him treifot, and 'there are those who permit if he saw' since they always sell kosher meat; and nevertheless he did not need this reason, but rather the main reason is as the Rav HaMagid wrote — because of meat that disappeared from sight. And also where most sellers sell kosher meat with certainty — one does not need that he saw, for we follow the majority; and this is proven from that which it is settled that meat found in the hand of an idolater is permitted specifically when he did not see the idolater take it — as written in s.k. 3:

And he means: a city where most slaughterers are idolaters — and so wrote Rashba in his novellae in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, and ruled thus. And it is explained in Rashba that the same applies if pieces of meat are found on the road — and likewise if most passersby on the road are Israelites — permitted, and we are not concerned for a raven as long as we do not see that a raven took it; and all the more so if he found meat in the market in a place where most slaughterers are Israelites — permitted. All this is the view of the aforementioned poskim (and so wrote Rokeach siman 97, that one who finds an animal and the like in a place where most are Israelites — permitted, and we are not concerned for meat that disappeared from sight; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 22 din 7: where he left meat in the street in a place where most idolaters are found, and he went and returned and found it in the place where he left it — permitted, end of his words). And it appears that such is also the Rosh's view in the name of Rashbam in all this — for behold he comes to settle why Rashba ruled like the plain meaning of the mishnah, that it deals with meat found in the market where most are Israelites; and further that he wrote (to settle that incident in Perek Elu Metziot that deals with a majority of Israelites). But where ravens took and returned it — we are concerned perhaps they exchanged; it implies: presumably when we did not see that it was taken we are not concerned in a majority of Israelites. And further that he wrote that these incidents in the Gemara that required simanim or teviyat ayin — one may say they were in a city that is mostly idolaters; and since it was lost there is greater concern — and these incidents imply that he did not find it in the place where he left it, as the aforementioned poskim wrote. And also from the Rosh's words, who wrote 'and since it was lost,' etc. — it implies thus; and so Bach, that the Rosh holds those incidents were where he did not find it in the place where he left it — and nevertheless they forbade only in a majority of idolaters. And further, presumably that which the Rosh wrote 'and since it was lost there is greater concern' comes to say that it was lost and he found it not in the place where he left it — for otherwise why say it at all; and 'and since it was lost,' etc. — it should have said only that it deals with a majority of idolaters; rather, certainly he holds that in a majority of idolaters alone they did not forbid except where there is also the circumstance that it was lost. And what the Rosh wrote at the beginning of his words, that that which we are not concerned for meat that disappeared from sight is where he found the meat in the place where he left it — that we are not concerned perhaps ravens exchanged it and brought it to that very place — he means: we are not concerned at all for meat that disappeared from sight, even in a majority of idolaters. He also forced to explain that Rashbam did not need to say according to his view that those incidents were mostly idolaters, etc. — all these are forced and far-fetched.) The Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that even if he left ten and found nine — they are forbidden, for we are concerned perhaps those were taken and these are others; and in Taharat HaKodesh they learned from chametz. And the Rambam wrote in Taharat HaKodesh at the end of general rule 32 that the custom is not like Rashba when he left ten and found nine, but rather they permit in every case; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 22 din 10 wrote that it is permitted when he left ten and found nine, for we say a cat or mouse dragged it; and even a complete half-rib, lest a mouse stole it, end of his words. However, one may say that the Rav in Taharat HaKodesh did not say that they are accustomed to be lenient except regarding pieces that are not tied — for such is the custom to leave several pieces together without being tied; and in such a case perhaps he holds like Tosafot's and the Tur's view in Orach Chaim siman 439, that regarding chametz too it is forbidden only when tied together; and the Mechaber brought it in Orach Chaim there.
