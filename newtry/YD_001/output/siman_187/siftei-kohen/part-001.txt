<b>מיד בכדי כו'. </b>כלומר דאם ראתה מיד בשיעור זה אף על גב די"ל דאחר תשמיש ראתה מ"מ כיון דהיתה חייבת אשם תלוי מקרי רואה מחמת תשמיש אבל אחר השיעור הזה טהורה אע"ג די"ל קודם לכן ראתה יצאה מכלל רואה מחמת תשמיש כן משמע בפוסקים אבל אם בדקה מיד אחר תשמיש ולא מצאה כלום ואח"כ ראתה אפילו תוך שיעור זה לא מיקרי רואה מחמת תשמיש וכל זה פשוט וכן ביאר הגאון אמ"ו ז"ל בתשובה בארוכה עיין שם ואני מוסיף דכן משמע להדיא מדברי הראב"ד בס' בעלי הנפש ומביאו ב"י וז"ל הרואה דם מחמת תשמיש כו' אסורה לשמש והוא שבדקה עצמה כדי שתושיט ידה לתחת הכר כלומר בכדי שיהא בעלה באשם תלוי אבל אחר כך הרי הוא כמעת לעת שבנדה שאינה מטמאה בועלה ואינה מוחזקת ברואה מחמת תשמיש כו' עכ"ל מבואר להדיא דאע"ג דלא בדקה עדיין כלל מ"מ אחר שיעור דכדי שתושיט ידה אינה מוחזקת ברואה מחמת תשמיש ועוד דהא קאמר הרי הוא כמעל"ע שבנדה כו' והתם מיירי שלא בדקה עדיין דאם בדקה אינה מטמאה למפרע מעל"ע אלא מפקידה לפקידה ולפי זה לדידן דקי"ל דכל סמוך לתשמיש מקרי רואה מחמת תשמיש וכמ"ש הרב היינו דוקא בידוע בודאי שראתה סמוך לתשמיש אבל אם במופלג אחר התשמיש בדקה עצמה ומצאה דם אע"ג די"ל דראתה בשעת תשמיש מותרת וכ"ש באשה שראתה ג"פ בבוקר בליל טבילתה כשקמה ממטתה מצאה על כתונת שלה ריבוי דם שהיא מותרת. שוב פסק הגאון אמ"ו ז"ל בתשובתו שם דאף אם תשמש אשה זו עוד פעם אחרת או ב' ותמצא מיד בשעת תשמיש מותרת ולא אמרינן אגלאי מילתא למפרע שהיה ג"כ בשעת תשמיש אלא ימים הראשונים יפלו ומכאן והלאה חושבנא וכל זה ברור לא הוצרכתי לכתבו אלא להוציא מלב הקורא בדברי הרב שחלק על תשובת הגאון אמ"ו ז"ל בענין זה והדבר פשוט דכל היכא דלא מיקרי רואה מחמת תשמיש אפילו ראתה כמה וכמה פעמים מותרת וכן הוא בדברי האחרונים וכ"כ בתשובת מהר"מ מלובלין סימן כ"ב דאין חילוק בין שמשה ג"פ או מ' ונ' פעמים ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Immediately, within the time required, etc.</b> That is, if she saw blood immediately within this interval, although one could say that she saw it only after relations, nevertheless, since her husband would have been liable to a provisional guilt-offering, she is considered one who sees blood because of relations. After this interval, however, she is pure. Although one could say that she had seen earlier, she is no longer included among those who see blood because of relations. This is implied by the authorities. But if she examined herself immediately after relations and found nothing, and afterward saw blood, even within this interval, she is not considered one who sees blood because of relations. All this is straightforward. My revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, likewise explained it at length in a responsum; see there. I add that this is also explicitly implied by the words of the Raavad in Baalei HaNefesh, cited by Beis Yosef: “A woman who sees blood because of relations, etc., is forbidden to have relations, provided that she examined herself within the time needed to extend her hand beneath the cushion,” that is, within the interval in which her husband would be liable to a provisional guilt-offering. “Afterward, however, it is like the twenty-four-hour period of a menstruant, when she does not render the man who had relations with her impure and she is not established as one who sees blood because of relations,” end quote. This explicitly shows that although she had not yet examined herself at all, once the interval needed to extend her hand had elapsed, she is not established as one who sees blood because of relations. Furthermore, he says that it is like the twenty-four-hour period of a menstruant, and there the case is one in which she had not yet examined herself, for if she had examined herself she would not convey impurity retroactively for twenty-four hours, but only from one examination to the next. Accordingly, for us, who rule that whenever the blood is found close to relations she is considered one who sees blood because of relations, as the Rama wrote, this applies only when it is definitely known that she saw blood close to relations. But if she examined herself a substantial time after relations and found blood, although it is possible that she saw during relations, she is permitted. All the more so, a woman who on three occasions, in the morning after the night of her immersion, arose from bed and found a large quantity of blood on her garment, is permitted. My revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, further ruled in that responsum that even if this woman has relations once or twice more and then finds blood immediately at the time of relations, she remains permitted with respect to the earlier occasions. We do not say that it has now been revealed retroactively that the earlier blood also appeared during relations. Rather, the earlier days fall away, and the reckoning begins from this point onward. All this is clear. I wrote it only to dispel the reader's possible impression that the Rama disagreed with the responsum of my revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, on this matter. It is obvious that wherever she is not considered one who sees blood because of relations, she remains permitted even if this occurred many times. This is also the view of the later authorities. Maharam of Lublin likewise wrote in responsum 22 that there is no difference whether she had relations three times or forty or fifty times; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
Near her veset etc. Perishah seif 11 wrote that we do not deal with the wicked; and per Rabbenu Tam who is stringent above siman 184 seif 10 to separate from her near her veset even for one who goes on a journey — one finds a case here where nearness is permitted, such as if her seeing is at the start of day, then the whole night before is permitted (as above siman 184 seif 2) — end of his words; and it is difficult for me: for the reason the whole night before is permitted is because since her veset is in the day, we are not concerned she will see at night as she is not accustomed to see at night; if so, since we are lenient for that reason, all the more so we should not attribute to leniency that she will not see due to relations, since she is not accustomed to see then; rather here it deals with where she already went ahead and had relations; or alternatively Rabbenu Tam explains "and if she has a veset" like Rashi or like the Mordechai; or alternatively here it deals with a minor or one who has not yet developed signs, where he need not separate from her as long as she has not established it three times, as above siman 184 seif 3; and here he says that if it was clarified that she had relations near her veset — meaning she saw after relations once or twice more at that same time such that she was found to have established a veset three times — she attributes to her veset; and requires careful study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And all this, etc.</b> See my work, where I proved that the Mordechai, and Maharam's responsa, Prague edition, siman 655, maintain as follows: if a woman has a fixed time at which she sees blood, then outside that time she attributes the blood to her fixed cycle even if she has no wound at all. We say that this blood is pure blood from the sides, produced by the pressure of relations, since the time of her cycle has not yet arrived. I also proved there that Terumas HaDeshen understood the Mordechai this way in his rulings, siman 47. He inferred from the Mordechai by an a fortiori argument that, since the Mordechai maintains that by virtue of the established presumption we say that the menstrual flow comes only at its proper time, and this enables us to attribute the blood to a pure source where we would not otherwise do so, all the more so he maintains that where there is a wound, even if it does not discharge blood, she is pure. In such a case it is possible that even the authorities who disagree with the Mordechai concede, since there is an additional ground for leniency, namely, that she has a wound. The Rama's words are based on this understanding. The Bach understood the plain meaning of the Mordechai differently, namely, that the phrase “she attributes it to her wound” is connected to “she attributes it to her fixed cycle.” He raised many questions and offered many resolutions, but I already refuted all his words at length there; see there. Rather, it is clear that the Mordechai is citing the baraisa, “she attributes it to her wound,” etc. This is also evident from Beis Yosef, Darkei Moshe, the responsa of Maharam of Padua, simanim 9 and 10, and the other later authorities. It is straightforward. Accordingly, it appears that this leniency applies only with respect to remaining with her husband, because in many places the Sages were concerned not to remove a woman from her husband and were lenient due to the risk that she remain unable to remarry. Therefore, we do not establish her as one who sees blood because of relations. Nevertheless, she must count seven clean days, for in this respect one may not rely on the Mordechai. Rashi appears to disagree with him, and the Ramban, Rashba, Rambam, Tur, and all the other authorities interpret “if she has a fixed cycle” to mean that if she had relations close to her cycle, she attributes the blood to her cycle. This implies that if she did not have relations close to her cycle, even if she has a fixed cycle we do not attribute the blood to a pure source. Maharam of Padua likewise wrote in responsum 10: “Regarding your honor's uncertainty whether one may be lenient in accordance with the Mordechai's interpretation of ‘if she has a fixed cycle, she attributes it to her cycle,’ this is a great leniency. Who is strong enough to be lenient against all the other authorities?” end quote. The Rama too was lenient only not to establish her as one who sees blood because of relations, and even this only when she has a wound, although it does not discharge blood. The Bach's words imply that he permits her in every respect, but in my humble opinion the correct ruling is as I have written.

<b>Likewise, if she does not, etc.</b> The Rama derived this ruling in Darkei Moshe from the words of Shaarei Dura, siman 6: “A woman who has a wound in that place and does not know whether the wound discharges blood, if she senses that the blood comes from the womb, she is impure and cannot attribute it to the wound. If she knows that it came from the wound, she is pure, for she attributes it to the wound. But at the time of her fixed cycle, even if she knows that it came from the wound, she is impure, for otherwise a woman with a wound in that place would never become impure. However, all stains found on her garments are pure, because they are attributed to the wound, even if she does not know whether the wound discharges blood, since we rule leniently regarding stains,” end quote. Darkei Moshe wrote that this is implied by his words. He then wrote that this is likewise implied by Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 11 of the Laws of Forbidden Relations, in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, and by the Mordechai cited above, whose words Beis Yosef brought in this siman under the heading beginning “However, Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote,” end quote. In my humble opinion, the Rama understood Shaarei Dura this way because he wrote, “if she senses that the blood comes from the womb, she is impure,” which appears to mean that she is impure only when she knows that it came from the womb, that is, from the source. Otherwise she is pure, even if she does not know that the wound discharges blood. He also apparently understood that the unqualified wording includes both a woman with a fixed cycle and one without a fixed cycle. On this basis he wrote that the same is implied by Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Mordechai cited above, whose words accord with Shaarei Dura. In truth, however, nothing of the kind is implied. Shaarei Dura said, “if she senses that the blood comes from the womb,” only to contrast it with the concluding clause that all her stains are attributed to the wound. Proof of this is that Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote in the name of Sefer HaTerumah: “A woman who has a wound in that place and does not know whether her wound discharges blood, if she examines herself in that place and finds blood, or if she senses that the blood is falling from the womb,” etc., “nevertheless, all her stains are attributed leniently,” end quote. The same wording appears in Sefer HaTerumah, siman 92. Thus, Hagahos Maimoniyos and Sefer HaTerumah explicitly prove the opposite: even if she does not sense the blood falling from the womb, but examined herself in that place and found blood, she is impure if she does not know that her wound discharges blood. This is clear, and it is also the plain implication of the authorities, that even in an unspecified case she must know that her wound discharges blood. Furthermore, the Rama's ruling is difficult for me, for what double doubt exists here? One can immediately say that perhaps it is menstrual blood. A case of this kind is not considered a double doubt at all, as I wrote in the laws of double doubt, rules 11 and 13; see there. I later found that Maharam of Lublin, at the end of responsum 111, wrote regarding the Rama's gloss that his distinctions require examination. Perhaps he intended the objections I have written.

Yochanan there who wrote: at first glance it appears from his saying in the third birth, in this matter without change — implying that in the third birth as well, etc. — implying all births were exactly equal, and the questioner did not come to ask until after the third birth, and in the third birth necessarily they were consecutive — for otherwise it is obvious she is permitted to everyone even without birth, for we specifically require consecutive, as in the responsum there and as above seif 1; and there was no need for the respondent to find a reason for the childbirth permission; if so it implies the first three times as well were consecutive — see there and investigate; and Bach explained the Rabbi's view that she saw after the birth three times but they were not consecutive, etc., and wrote that so implies from R. Yochanan's responsum — but the opposite is true, as I explained; and apart from this, there is no logic to say so, for since she is permitted after the last birth even if they were consecutive — what does it matter whether the first relations were consecutive or not; and all this appears to me clear.
