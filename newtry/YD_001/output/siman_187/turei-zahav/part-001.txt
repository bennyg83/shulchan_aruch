<b>If she engaged in relations close to her expected period, etc. </b>The Beit Yosef cited Rashi: If there is a fixed pattern for this impairment, meaning that she does not always see blood because of relations but only at intervals, she attributes it to her expected period and may engage in relations without examination between one expected period and the next. He further cited the Mordechai as follows: A woman who has a fixed period at which she would see menstrual blood may attribute the blood to her expected period, for she can say that the blood she was seeing was pure, since her expected period had not yet arrived. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even though she does not know, etc. </b><i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="2"></i>I have not fully understood the Rama's intention in this ruling, in light of the later poskim whom he generally follows. The Gemara states in the chapter Tinokes that a woman is believed to say, “I have a wound in the source from which blood emerges.” This is the ruling written in the Shulchan Aruch in seif 6. The Beit Yosef cites a dispute regarding its meaning. The Rashba explains that even if she does not actually sense blood flowing from the wound, for her eyes are not positioned between her wound and she cannot know this without an examination by means of a tube, the statement means as follows: She herself senses that she has a wound, and she is believed to say, “I have a wound there in which there is blood.” She may attribute the blood to her wound, for we say that the blood descends from it. The Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos, however, hold that she must know with certainty that she has a wound that emits blood. She is then permitted specifically when it is not the time of her expected period.

In Piskei Mahara'i, responsum 46, cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, he permits a woman who examines herself in the folds and crevices and finds blood only in one place at the side. This is the ruling copied into the Shulchan Aruch here in seif 7. It applies where she has a fixed period, in which case she has this permission whenever her expected period has not arrived. Under other circumstances, however, he did not permit her even when it was not the time of her expected period, since according to the Mordechai she must know that her wound certainly emits blood, and this woman did not know that with certainty.

In the glosses to Shaarei Dura, the responsum of Maharil is cited regarding a woman who would see blood after urinating and experienced pain while urinating. The Rama cites it in siman 191. Maharil wrote that she may be permitted by means of an examination in which she inserts clean cotton inside. The Rama copied this in siman 191. In his conclusion Maharil wrote: “In our case she does not have a clearly established wound. Nevertheless, between one period and the next it appears that one may rely upon the permissions stated above, for in that responsum the authority is lenient outside the time of her period even though she does not sense that the blood comes from a wound. We attribute it to blood from a wound according to the view that the laws of expected periods are rabbinic.” End quote. Thus, he was lenient only by means of the permissions stated above, namely, the examination with clean cotton that we mentioned. It appears that he did not wish to be lenient without this, unlike the responsum that permits her even though she does not sense the blood coming from a wound, because that responsum holds that the laws of expected periods are rabbinic, whereas Maharil himself was concerned for the view that they are according to the Torah, as is explained there in the preceding gloss; see there.

Accordingly, since we rule that the laws of expected periods are rabbinic, as explained in siman 184 and in several other places, it would follow that here too we should be lenient without any examination with clean cotton, even when she does not sense that blood emerges from her wound. This is apparently why the Rama rules here leniently where she has a fixed period and it is not the time of that period. Yet this is not in accordance with the view of Mahara'i cited above, who was concerned for the Mordechai's ruling.

This presents two difficulties. First, the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 7, “If whenever she examines herself,” etc. This is the ruling of Mahara'i cited above, and it implies that unless it is clear to her that the blood is found in one specific place at the side and nowhere else, we rule stringently whenever she does not know with certainty that her wound emits blood, in accordance with Mahara'i. Mahara'i was discussing a woman with a fixed period, as the Beit Yosef cites at the end of this siman. The Rama should therefore have commented there that we do not rule so according to his own position, but that in every case where she has a wound and it is not the time of her period she is pure.

Second, since the Rama saw that Mahara'i and the Beit Yosef rule stringently when she does not know that her wound emits blood, and Mahariv likewise ruled in responsum 25 in accordance with Mahara'i and Maharil that she specifically requires an examination, from where did the Rama derive grounds to rule leniently against them? A further difficulty is that in Darkei Moshe he cites their words. He first copies Mahara'i's ruling and then writes: “So too wrote the glosses to Shaarei Dura in the name of Maharil, that when she has a fixed period we attribute the blood to a wound outside the time of her period even if she does not know that the wound emits blood.” End quote. This directly contradicts the text of the glosses to Shaarei Dura that we cited, which specifically requires the permissions mentioned above, namely, examination with clean cotton.

The Rama evidently wrote this because according to our ruling the laws of expected periods are rabbinic, as explained above. Yet if so, he should not have presented Maharil's words as agreeing with those of Mahara'i, according to his understanding, for Mahara'i clearly permits only where it is established that the blood is found specifically at one side and not elsewhere.

I am further greatly astonished, for in siman 191 the Rama himself rules in accordance with Mahara'i and Maharil stringently regarding a woman who sees blood with her urine, and writes that the accepted practice is to require her to examine herself with clean cotton. He does not permit her without this. Here, however, he is lenient outside the time of her period in every case, even though she does not know that her wound emits blood. One cannot answer that the present law, where the woman has a wound, is more lenient than the case where she finds blood with her urine. This is not so, for Mahariv writes explicitly in responsum 25 regarding blood found with urine: “It appears to me that this case is more favorable than that of a woman who has a wound in that place, since the fact that she experiences pain while urinating demonstrates that the blood came with the urine,” etc. Thus, the present case is more stringent. The Rama's rulings therefore contradict one another. In the more stringent case he permits her outside the time of her period without any examination, while in siman 191, in the more lenient case of urine, he writes that the accepted practice is to permit her only by means of an examination.

My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that one should rule in accordance with this gloss, even though it is contrary to Mahara'i, since the Rashba is lenient under all circumstances. I am astonished, however, what are we to do with the rulings of Mahara'i, the glosses to Shaarei Dura, Mahariv, the Beit Yosef in seif 7 here, and the Rama himself in siman 191? In view of all these authorities, whose teachings we constantly follow, it is proper to rule stringently and not permit a woman who has a wound but does not know that it emits blood, except specifically in the manner written by the Shulchan Aruch in seif 7.

It further appears to me that if she finds blood at the time of the pain, the circumstances demonstrate that it came from the wound, and she is therefore permitted. Even if, after the pain ceases, she finds blood, it appears that this is the residue of the blood that was present during the pain, as the Rama wrote in siman 191. See what I wrote there in a responsum concerning this question. It also appears to me that if she has the type of wound from which other women commonly see blood, she may attribute the blood to that wound even though she herself does not know that it emits blood. So it appears to me.

According to Shmuel, this means as long as saliva remains present in the mouth, meaning as long as she continues to see because of relations. The Rambam likewise rules in accordance with Shmuel in chapter 5 of Hilchos Issurei Biah.

If she has reached the age at which she is expected to see, the allowance extends for four nights. If she had already seen menstrual blood while in her father's home before marriage, it extends only through the first act of marital relations, and no longer. After that she no longer has the halachic status of a virgin for this purpose. Why, then, is the Beit Yosef lenient regarding all virgins?

The Beit Yosef further brings proof from the teaching in the chapter Tinokes that she is permitted until the wound heals. He writes that although according to our practice all of these women are permitted only for the first act of marital relations, nevertheless, regarding the question whether to establish her as one who sees blood because of relations, we presume that the blood is hymenal blood, since most women do not see blood because of relations. He concludes, however: “Yet I am afraid to permit her unless there are indications demonstrating that it is hymenal blood.” It appears that his expression of fear is based upon the difficulty I mentioned, and he therefore requires indications demonstrating that it is hymenal blood, namely, that she experiences at least some pain during relations.

Maharam of Padua likewise wrote that if she experiences no pain at all during relations, she is like all other women. The Rama follows his ruling here. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that one should not accept this stringency, since it has no proof. The Gemara says that the allowance continues until the wound heals, which according to Shmuel means as long as she sees because of relations.

We have already explained, however, that this is no proof regarding a woman who has reached the age at which she is expected to see menstrual blood. All the more so, if she had already seen blood in her father's home before marriage, her allowance even under the law of the Mishnah lasts only for the first act of marital relations. Thereafter her hymen is considered spent. From where, then, do we know that she should still be treated as a virgin when there are no indications supporting this? The Rambam too, in chapter 5 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, writes that where a woman sees blood because of relations we attribute it to her virginity specifically in the case where her law is that the allowance continues until the wound heals.
