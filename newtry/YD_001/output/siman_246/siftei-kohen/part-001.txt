**** ENGLISH ****
And in time of pressure, etc. — and so in Semag positive commandment 12, concluding "and this thing is forbidden to say before a gentile"; however in Hagahot Maimoni he does not bring this conclusion, and afterward the Rav continues. It appears the reason we learn in Menachot (99b): R. Yochanan said in the name of R. Shimon ben Yochai: even if a person read only Shema morning and evening, he fulfills "it shall not depart"; and this thing is forbidden to say before a gentile; and Rava said it is a mitzvah to say it before a gentile. Rashi explained: he holds that because of Shema one receives such great reward — if he were occupied all day, how much more so his reward is great, and he accustoms his sons to Torah. Others explain: those rabbis could exempt themselves with Shema and sit all day and study — from this, great reward; end. If so, since Rava said it is a mitzvah to say it, we hold like him; and all the more so according to the version of Yalkut at the beginning of Yehoshua, where we learn: Abaye cursed one who says it before a gentile, and Rava said it is a mitzvah — if so we hold like Rava against Abaye; also in Midrash Tehillim it is anonymous in the name of R. Yehoshua ben Levi: even if one read only Shema morning and evening he fulfills, and "in his Torah he meditates day and night," and Yalkut brings this at the beginning of Tehillim; see there.
