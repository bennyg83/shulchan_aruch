"A blended matter, etc. — whether flour is called wet in wet or dry in dry — Tosafot disagreed; and in chapter Orlah they wrote two versions that contradict each other: for one answer it is wet in wet, and for one resolution it is dry in dry; and since I need to learn from this I will copy the matter briefly: we say in chapter Meshilin on the mishnah — a woman who borrowed from her friend water and salt according to their manner of drinking, and the Gemara challenges: and let water and salt be nullified regarding dough — some say: behold, a kav of wheat became mixed in ten kavim of his friend's wheat — the latter eats and your brethren rejoice; came up: what he wrote wheat in barley — for wheat in wheat is also not nullified according to R' Yehudah who holds it is not nullified, etc. end of his words. Tosafot challenged in chapter Orlah daf 82 s.v. R' Yehudah: behold dry in dry even in its kind is nullified according to R' Yehudah; and Tosafot answered: it deals with flour in flour — for if not so, behold wheat in barley would be recognizable, and how do we say it is nullified; and further answered flour in flour: since it is cold it is dry in dry and nullified — only there in Beitzah it deals with boiling, etc., and the taste is wet in wet, end of his words; and from this comes the above dispute.

And in truth I say in my humble opinion: the second answer of Tosafot appears reasonable, for they raised for us a difficulty of Maharshal and a question of Maharsha — Maharsha challenged: behold Tosafot themselves distinguish between money that is not nullified, for its owner is upon it, and issur that is nullified — if so, what does the Gemara say wheat in barley — for it is not in its kind and is nullified — from where do we know? This applies to issur; but money that its owner is upon it, even not in its kind, is not nullified, see there, he strained to resolve it. And Maharshal challenged plainly in what it says wheat in barley — for it is not in its kind and is nullified — if so it still challenges properly from water and salt that it should be nullified regarding dough, for it is not in its kind; however for the second resolution it appears to me requires further study, for Tosefta proved it does not deal with flour — for if so, even wheat in barley would be called species in its species — only actual wheat and barley and boiling that absorb one from another; and if so the barley is recognizable and returns, as Tosefta wrote; only the entire matter is on the taste that the wheat received from barley — and if so the Gemara properly says it is nullified and Maharsha's question does not apply that its owner is upon it, for we do not judge on the body of the barley but on its taste, and taste is mere and its owner is upon it does not apply at all; and were it not that the Torah multiplied in a server that taste is not like essence — if so from the Torah to say regarding money its owner is upon it; but wheat in wheat that actual wheat became mixed — behold they are not recognizable and also received taste as well — in this it applies properly that its owner is upon it, for his friend's exists visible; and see also Maharshal is resolved, for even though wheat in barley are nullified — that is, its owner does not apply to mere taste and the body of wheat returns as he wrote; but in water and salt that become mixed in dough and it is impossible through removal and return — it is obvious its owner is upon it; and if so the second resolution appears reasonable.

And behold the Ram of Lublin sensed in Tosefta that they wrote it deals with boiling, and he explained as I wrote that it does not deal with flour in flour; and indeed he removed wheat and questioned him why wheat in barley is nullified — behold there is here taste and not in its kind, taste forbids; and he wrote in this language: not in its kind — taste is nullified according to R' Yehudah, etc. end of his words; and Maran's discussion — I do not know that not in its kind does not forbid taste according to R' Yehudah from the Torah — that R' Yehudah would not think noten ta'am is from the Torah; and perhaps he holds like R' Nachman that we do not say noten ta'am except where the body of the issur dissolved in heter — that is, milk in cracked wheat; but if the issur was drawn and only its taste remained — that is its taste and not its substance and is not forbidden from the Torah; or one may say in tevel they were lenient as Tosefta wrote in several places; or one may say R' Yehudah holds even d'rabbanan it is permitted; and there is proof for this from the mishnah of terumah chapter ten — R' Yehudah: all that are cooked together are permitted except meat; and until here it deals with not in its kind — species in its kind — behold according to R' Yehudah it is not nullified; and we learn in not in its kind he holds like R' Nachman that its taste does not forbid even d'rabbanan, only in meat whose taste is strong.

And accordingly for the first resolution in Tosefta that they resolved it deals with flour in flour and is called wet in wet — the difficulty in Bamah Ma'alah: a kav of wheat in ten kavim of barley — why nullified? behold not in its kind wet in wet requires sixty; and here behold there is a mixture of issur and forbids to all; and to say ten kavim is not specifically only more is strained — the Gemara should explain from where that it does not deal with abbreviation of its language to say immediately from where — rav sixty or seventy; and therefore one must say certainly regarding species in its kind that is not nullified it deals specifically with wet in wet; and the reason Tosafot wrote: regarding something that mixes into all the heter and does not stand by itself — in this even flour mixes well and does not stand by itself — what difference if the flour is hot or cold; but regarding noten ta'am in not in its kind that wet in wet noten ta'am — that is wet in wet hot and the like that mixes into heter entirely; but flour in flour since it is cold it does not give taste in heter and does not mix so much as to make heter issur — for each kort of flour stands by itself and is not comparable to wine and oil as Rambam wrote; and therefore noten ta'am does not apply to it. And therefore in Beitzah regarding that species in its kind that is not nullified according to R' Yehudah — even flour in flour is called wet in wet and is not nullified; but not in its kind that issur of mixtures is from the Torah of taste as essence — regarding this it is not called wet in wet to forbid when cold. And if so Tosafot's words are well explained: it deals with flour in flour; and regarding species in its species it is flour in flour wet in wet and not nullified according to R' Yehudah; but wheat in barley is nullified, for it is not in its kind; and because of noten ta'am it does not forbid, for regarding noten ta'am flour in flour is called dry, for it is cold and there is no noten ta'am in it.

And if so it appears for halachah: species in its species — flour became mixed in flour — one should be lenient in a place of loss, since the view of many poskim is that it is called dry in dry; and even according to Tosafot that it is called wet in wet — according to what I wrote it is only regarding R' Yehudah's matter that species in its species is not nullified, but not regarding noten ta'am; and species in its species even if it is wet in wet — behold this too from the Torah in the majority is nullified, only because of decree they forbade in this one may be lenient; for if it is not in its kind as well, it is possible there is no noten ta'am — and therefore there is no concern for stringency; and it is fitting to rely on Rambam, and in particular this of flour in flour being called wet in wet as written above that R' Yehudah holds species in its species is not nullified — that is wet in wet as I wrote; and this itself has no absolute basis, for the entire necessity is that R' Yehudah holds terumah in one in a hundred in its kind, and likewise in expensive nuts he holds according to R' Yehudah it is not nullified; and Tosafot challenged: behold without this species in its species is not nullified; and therefore Tosefta was compelled to distinguish between wet in wet and dry in dry; and besides that Tosafot further resolved this of theirs and what the Rabbis expounded without this — one may resolve easily, for we challenge in Chullin daf 92 side 2 on that which R' Yehudah said: impure fish broth in two hundred; and it challenges: and did not R' Yehudah say species in its species is not nullified; and it answers: broth is different, it is merely secretion; and Tosafot explained s.v. it is different: separated and forbidden only d'rabbanan; and so too Rashba in his novellae: broth is d'rabbanan that forbids; and in the language of their words R' Yehudah did not say species in its species is not nullified. And in that sugya of a litra of dried figs — Zevachim chapter HaTaruvot it appears R' Yehudah holds terumah is d'rabbanan; and in particular in fruits for all d'rabbanan — if so because of species in its species behold in d'rabbanan it is nullified; and likewise kilayei kerem for one who holds terumah is d'rabbanan that sanctity of the land is nullified — even kilayei kerem is d'rabbanan as Tosafot wrote in Zevachim beginning of chapter Kol HaZevachim and Yevamot daf 81 see there; if so this of pomegranate in Dan is established entirely in terumah and kilayei kerem and it is d'rabbanan and species in its species is nullified; and therefore they needed to say that because of their importance it is not nullified — requires further study and investigation on Tosafot that they did not mention from this anything. True that one must understand in that sugya of Beitzah cited that we say wheat in wheat as well — though according to R' Yehudah it is not nullified — behold there is tevel of boundaries that we establish is only d'rabbanan as Tosafot wrote; and if so in d'rabbanan behold species in its species is nullified according to R' Yehudah — requires investigation apparently. And what can be resolved in this is briefly: we do not say two contradictory rulings — for behold here is davar sheyesh lo matirin that is not nullified; and if it were d'rabbanan behold regarding something that has a permitted aspect we judge it like Torah as we say in first chapter of Beitzah regarding safek of davar sheyesh lo matirin forbidden like Torah — only we need to say tevel is different, for they were very lenient in it; and behold further there is room to arouse: behold davar sheyesh lo matirin is not nullified — behold it has to him davar sheyesh lo matirin; and in davar sheyesh lo matirin as well even in a thousand it is not nullified — and one must say also in this since it is tevel they were very lenient in it more than other davar sheyesh lo matirin that it should be nullified; and if so behold here are two rulings in tevel and all the more we do not say — investigate; and therefore the correct practical halachah is as he wrote:"

"One in two is nullified; and if two became mixed in three — Shach and Peri Chadash expanded that likewise it is nullified, and all the more so wet in wet. And I brought proof from Yerushalmi first chapter of Challah — R' Yehudah wrapped matzah and maror and ate; and it challenged: behold Hillel wrapped matzah and maror and ate them; and he sought to answer: here in time that the Temple exists, and here in time the Temple does not exist; and he returned and answered: even if R' Yehudah were in Temple times he would take only matzah and maror and not Pesach, for both of them nullify one another, end of his words Yerushalmi; and in our Gemara in Pesachim the Gemara says in this time a person should not eat matzah and maror together, for matzah is d'oraisa and maror d'rabbanan and they nullify one another — and behold this apparently contradicts Yerushalmi that said one does not nullify one, and R' Yehudah acted thus in practice; but it appears requires further study: certainly one does not nullify one except if so he chews a k'zayit of matzah and a k'zayit of maror — he must intend and restrict in his eating a bit from a k'zayit of matzah so that he not eat more maror and chew it, for if so it is maror majority and nullifies matzah; and likewise the reverse; therefore he must restrict that the chewing from matzah and maror be not less and not more — and this is difficult to analyze on him; and therefore they said do not let a person eat them together, for it does not suffice that one dominate its fellow and nullify it — and this is for ordinary people; but R' Yehudah who was a master of strength and the five of them were precise in his eating that he not chew and eat extra even any amount more matzah than maror, and he always intended them together so that nullification not occur and he fulfill the mitzvah of wrapping as Torah — and this is clear.

Rama wrote in Turei Chayyim general rule 39 seif 2 in name of Hagahot Or Zarua: that which we require in a dry matter one in two — that is in Torah issur; but in d'rabbanan, even if only one in one became mixed — permitted for two people, or Israelite and gentile, to eat them, end of his words; and the Rav wrote in Turei Chayyim: and Rosh in first chapter of Beitzah disagrees on this, end of his words. And all the commentators wondered, for we do not find in Rosh in that chapter any contradiction or that he mentioned a general rule. And it appears we say in first chapter of Beitzah: wood that fell on Yom Tov into the oven — he adds wood to them and nullifies them, etc.; and the Gemara challenges: behold he nullifies issur l'chatchila; and it answers: this applies in d'oraisa, but in d'rabbanan they nullify; and according to Rav Ashi who said: anything that has a permitted aspect, even in d'rabbanan, is not nullified from the Torah — this applies when it exists for prohibition in reality; but here it diminishes greatly the prohibition, end of his words. And Rashba's view is that this version 'in d'rabbanan they nullify issur' applies from the fact that he only said it in that version that diminishes greatly, and it comes only to answer that question of davar sheyesh lo matirin that is like Torah prohibition; but the body of the version applies in d'rabbanan they nullify. But Rosh wrote: specifically regarding wood that diminishes greatly the prohibition they nullify d'rabbanan issur; but in general, even d'rabbanan issur they do not nullify. And one must understand from where Rosh got this reasoning, for it does not appear so in the Gemara; and one must say he had difficulty with Tosafot there s.v. etc. who challenged from that of a litra of dried figs — round upon rounds he adds to it and nullifies them; and Tosafot answered to distinguish between whether its essence is from the Torah or not — and this strained Rosh; and therefore he explained that they nullify only when it diminishes greatly, etc.; and so too Maharsha that Rosh resolves Tosafot's question. And behold according to Hagahot Or Zarua there is no difficulty at all: granted d'rabbanan issur that became mixed one in one — if so, for two people it is permitted; if so there is here no issur whatsoever — what of it that it is forbidden for one person, behold for two it is permitted, and there is here no complete issur until we say upon it 'one does not nullify issur'; but regarding terumah his law of Or Zarua does not apply, for behold terumah is forbidden until it ascends in one in a hundred — if so there is issur upon it and one does not nullify issur, and there is no difficulty at all; and Rosh need not say it does not apply in all d'rabbanan issurim — and perforce one must say Rosh did not accept the reasoning of Or Zarua, and his words are very compelling — investigate."
