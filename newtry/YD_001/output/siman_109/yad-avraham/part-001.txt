(סימן ק"ט בהג"ה סעיף א') <b>ויש מחמירין להשליך א' כו'.</b> וראיתי להפר"ח בא"ח סי' תמ"ז שהקשה ע"ד האומרים כן מדתנן במסכת ערלה הערלה וכה"כ עולין בא' ומאתים וא"צ להרים וה"ה ביבש כשנתבטל ברוב ואף באיסור הנאה וכתב על רש"י מה שאינו נכון שפירש בפ"ק דביצה גבי חבילי תלתן של כה"כ יעלו בא' ומאתים היינו שיעלה א' מהן וידלק והשאר מותרין. ונראה שנעלם ממנו מ"ש המרדכי בזה בפ"ק דביצה גבי ביטול ברוב דצריך לזרוק א' מהן ולא דמי לתרומה דהכל מעורב ביחד ואין יכול לאכול האיסור לבדו בלא תערובות חולין ולכך א"צ להסירה כ"א מפני גזל השבט ע"כ. ובזה א"ש נמי דלא תקשי על פרש"י גבי תלתן מההיא מתני' דערלה דא"צ להרים דאיירי בדברים שא"י לאכול האיסור לבדו בלא תערובות היתר משא"כ בחתיכות וחבילי תלתן:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 109 in the Hagahah seif 1) "And some are stringent to throw one out," etc. I saw Peri Chadash in Orach Chayyim siman 447, who challenged those who say so — from what we learned in tractate Orlah: orlah and likewise ascends in one in two hundred and one need not separate; and the same applies in dry when nullified in the majority, even in issur hana'ah; and he wrote on Rashi what is incorrect — that he explained in first chapter of Beitzah regarding bundles of clover of kohanim that they ascend in one in two hundred, meaning one of them ascends and the rest are permitted. It appears he was unaware of what Mordechai wrote on this in first chapter of Beitzah regarding nullification in the majority — that one must throw one of them out; and it is not similar to terumah, where everything is mixed together and one cannot eat the issur by itself without a mixture of chullin, and therefore one need not remove it except because of robbing the tribe, end. And with this one resolves that there is no difficulty on Rashi's explanation regarding clover from that mishnah of Orlah that one need not separate — for it deals with things where one cannot eat the issur by itself without a mixture of heter, unlike pieces and bundles of clover:

(There) "And there is no distinction in all this whether the issur is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan." Beit Yosef concludes — Shach, and perhaps the Hagahah refers to what he wrote that we require one in two, see there. And behold proof that the source of the Hagahah is from Beit Yosef, and there it is stated explicitly at the end of the siman, see there — he challenged what ShaDa wrote, that in d'rabbanan issur one in one suffices; only that there is no distinction in this — see Maharsha chapter 3 of Pesachim; and it appears to me that so too Rambam, chapter 13 of Laws of Terumot halachah 11 [and so too chapter 6 of Laws of Bikkurim din 12, and see there in Kesef Mishneh my intent in his view, with God's help] regarding terumat chutz la'aretz, which is d'rabbanan — one in one suffices, and he relied on the Yerushalmi that Tosafot brought in Menachot (67a), and Ra'avad in hasagot disagrees and holds a majority is required; and so it is proved in my view in the Gemara Pesachim (daf 98) where we say there "do not treat lightly the measures of d'rabbanan," and Rashi explained: even a matter whose issur is d'rabbanan — do not treat its measure lightly; and so the poskim and Taz siman 98 seif 6; and it appears he refers to all measures, whether the measure of sixty in wet or the measure of majority in dry — do not treat lightly; and Rambam goes according to his reasoning, that also regarding sixty in wet he treats lightly d'rabbanan issurim and holds it is issur min haminyan; but Rashba already challenged him, and so Taz siman 98 — in all d'rabbanan issurim that require sixty in wet, the issur is not min haminyan, except for udder siman 90 and siman 98 seif 8; if so, the same applies regarding nullification of dry in the majority — we also do not treat lightly; investigate. It appears to me that if one in one became mixed and afterward one piece was crumbled into two — forbidden, lest the issur was crumbled; and even if afterward a heter piece was added — for if it had not been crumbled it would be permitted, as the Hagahah wrote siman 92 seif 5, that we do not say "piece after piece" in dry, and if heter was added it is permitted; but here it is forbidden, lest issur was crumbled, and there is no majority of heter; and as he wrote siman 101 seif 6, that one may not rely to be lenient — here too is similar, see there, investigate. And it appears to me further that if a piece worthy of honor became mixed with two heter pieces — we hold siman 101 that it is not nullified — and afterward one of them was cut into two — forbidden in any case: if it is heter, behold the issur is still whole and is not nullified; and even if you say the issur was cut, one may not permit, for there is no majority of heter pieces to nullify it, since one issur piece increased — we hold it returns and is aroused even in dry, siman 99 seif 6 in the Hagahah; and even if it increased from within, as proved in chapter 5 of Terumot and Beit Yosef in the siman cited regarding a se'ah of terumah that fell into less than a hundred of chullin — they ground and added, etc., see there, investigate. And what he wrote siman 101, that all that are cut are permitted — meaning that a majority of heter pieces still remained, as proved there that he permitted only b'makom tzorech, see there; unlike when there is no majority, there is no b'makom tzorech: Further, it appears to me that dry in dry that became mixed one in two, and one fell from the mixture into two others of the second mixture — permitted for one person to eat all of them, and one need not throw out. For nevertheless there is here sixty to one — perhaps that which fell is heter, and there is no issur at all; for what difference to me if here there is certainly issur but one does not know if one eats the issur that fell, or if one certainly eats what fell but one does not know if it is issur; and see in Asheri chapter 3 siman 38:
