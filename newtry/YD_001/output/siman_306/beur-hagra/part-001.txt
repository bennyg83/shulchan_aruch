**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Likkut) And it is a mitzvah to consecrate it.</b> This is Rambam's view. Tur, in the name of Rosh, disagrees, and Bach writes that the matter requires study. Rambam's reason is the sugya in Arachin 29a, where he rules like the Sages of the Mishnah. Kesef Mishneh questions this because although Ulla follows the Sages, Rav disagrees and is Ulla's teacher, and Rav Yehudah also acted in practice according to Rav. But the sugya there concerning R. Yishmael and the sanctity taking effect on sacred items aligns differently. Rosh follows Kesef Mishneh's objection and rules like R. Yishmael. Rambam, however, rules like the Sages regarding a firstborn, and therefore also like the Sages in Arachin. Proof that the halachah follows the Sages regarding firstborn comes from the discussions in the first chapters of Nedarim 13a and Nazir, which follow their position. I later found the same in Lechem Mishneh.

<b>ונותנין כו'. </b>ר"ל אע"ג דאמרינן בחולין קל"א קל"ב דמתנות אפי' לכהנת ואמר שם רב כהנא אכיל בשביל אשתו ובספ"ג דפסחים א"ר כהנא אי לאו דנסיבי כו' אלמא לא היה כהן ובפ"ק דקדושין <small>(ח' א') </small>רב כהנא שקיל כו' ותי' שם תוס' בתי' השני דבשביל אשתו היה לוקח כמו במתנות וכ"כ בפסחים שם אבל בחולין שם כתבו כתי' הראשון דתרי רב כהנא והוכיח הרא"ש דבפדיון הבן לכהנת לא דגמ' בחולין שם פריך איתביה רבא לעולא כו' ומשני אהרן כו' ובפדיון כתיב בפ' במדבר ויתן את כסף הפדוים לאהרן ובניו ואיתקש בכורי אדם ובהמה להדדי הרא"ש סוף בכורות ומ"ש ואיתקש כו' א"י היכן דבגמ' שם ד' א' אמרינן דלא איתקש אלא בכורי אדם ובהמה טמאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And they give.</b> Meaning that although Chullin 131b and 132a say that the priestly gifts may be given even to a daughter of a kohen, and Rav Kahana ate gifts on account of his wife, while Pesachim says that Rav Kahana was not himself a kohen, nevertheless Rosh proves that pidyon haben may not be given to a daughter of a kohen. Tosafot in Kiddushin 8a give one answer that Rav Kahana received gifts because of his wife, as with other priestly gifts. Tosafot in Pesachim similarly explain. In Chullin, however, they prefer the answer that there were two different sages named Rav Kahana. Rosh proves that a daughter of a kohen cannot receive pidyon haben from the Gemara's exchange between Rava and Ulla, which derives the law from the designation to Aharon and his sons. Regarding pidyon haben the verse says that the redemption money is given to Aharon and his sons. Rosh further writes that firstborn humans and firstborn animals are compared, though I do not know where this comparison is established, since the Gemara says the comparison is only between firstborn humans and firstborn non-kosher animals.
