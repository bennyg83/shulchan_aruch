שמחמירים דבכה"ג אפשר דאין הכהן צריך לקבלו. כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****But in a place, etc., such as one who bought a cow, etc. — this is not precise; Darkei Moshe and Piskei Mahariw siman 327 (should be 227) wrote: even a doubtful firstborn requires acceptance, specifically from an Israelite; but a poor kohen who has a firstborn and gives it to a rich kohen need not accept it; but if the Israelite was negligent not selling it to a gentile before birth, it appears he need not accept it, etc.; and Maharai wrote in his rulings siman 368: what Maharam wrote that the kohen must accept in a doubtful firstborn — specifically when the doubt arose on its own; but one who buys a milking cow from a gentile need not accept it — end quote Darkei Moshe; and therefore the Rav mixed matters here; but in truth I do not know what negligence applies to buying a milking cow if he could not sell the firstborn; and even if negligent, why mention doubtful firstborn — even a definite firstborn need not be accepted as implied in Mahariw's responsum siman 327; and what Maharai wrote in his rulings siman 368 regarding buying a milking cow from a gentile that he need not accept — this is for the reason he wrote: Maharam does not deal with doubt depending on dispute of amoraim but with doubt arising on its own as we find several doubts in tractate Bekhorot; but in such a case where there are several doubts depending on amoraim' dispute (meaning whether milking exempts from firstborn status, as below siman 316 s.k. 3) we do not go so far to be stringent because of karet prohibition to satisfy all — end quote; and I do not know how to reconcile the Rav's words unless he erred in his words and specifically regarding doubt arising on its own; but regarding doubt depending on amoraim' dispute such as buying a milking cow from a gentile or Israelite negligence who could sell the firstborn to a gentile before birth, etc. — and Taz copied the Rav's words without emendation or noticing; nevertheless requires study in the law regarding what Maharai divided between doubt from itself and doubt depending on amoraim' dispute, from Halevi's responsum he brought there, which is Maharil siman 204 — it does not appear so, for regarding buying a milking cow from a gentile he wrote the kohen must accept it and learned from doubtful firstborn of Maharam, even though he brought there poskim's reasoning whether milking exempts; and possibly since Maharai saw Maharil's responsum and nevertheless did not rule like him, we follow the latter; and it also appears Maharai there did not lighten for this reason alone — it is strained to divide between doubt from itself and doubt depending on amoraim' dispute — unless we are stringent that Maharai holds we compel the kohen in doubtful firstborn and he informs us that in doubt depending on amoraim' dispute we do not compel him, for he can say "I hold like the permissive ones" — this is strained, for we should say Maharai disputes Maharil above who wrote we do not compel even in doubtful firstborn; moreover it implies in amoraim' dispute he says he need not accept at all even if we do not compel him and there is no prohibition in not accepting; rather it appears because there in Maharai's case there were several doubts in amoraim' dispute — sold to gentile for money which some poskim hold is a sale, as below siman 320 seif 6; and it was milking which some exempt from firstborn — therefore he was lenient; but in one doubt of amoraim' dispute alone one should not be lenient unless the law appears essential as the lenient ones, but we are stringent; in such a case possibly the kohen need not accept it — so it appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****One may keep it all twelve months — from its birth if its months ended, or until its year is completed from the eighth day if its months did not end; so wrote Taz from Beit Yosef's words (and likewise in Perishah); but in truth from Rambam and Semag asin 211 (which is the Mechaber's wording) it does not appear so, for they wrote at the beginning: if born blemished one counts from the day it was born, etc., for it is fit for eating on the day of its birth — implying specifically born blemished; and since they were silent here and did not divide, it implies since not born blemished one always counts from the eighth day of its birth, for though known its months ended, nevertheless it was not fit for eating on the day of its birth for it was unblemished; and likewise Rashi and Tosafot: why does the Gemara in Rosh Hashanah chapter 1 page 7 say "if unblemished (count from eighth day), if blemished (count from birth)" — blemished, who can eat it since its months are known?
