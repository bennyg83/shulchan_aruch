<b>אסור מיום ליום כו'. </b>משמע משעה שנדר אסור מיום ליום כגון אם נדר בחצי יום ט"ו לחדש תמוז אסור עד חצי יום ט"ו לחדש אב אע"פ שאינו אלא כ"ט ימים שתמוז הוא חסר מ"מ הוא חדש שלם מיום ליום וכן פי' הרא"ש אם עמד בח' לחדש ואמר חדש עלי אסור עד ח' לחדש הבא אבל הרמב"ם פ"ט מה"נ והסמ"ג לאוין רמ"ב ור' ירוחם ני"ד ח"ו כתבו אם אמר חדש א' אסור משעה שנדר עד תשלום ל' יום גמורים מעל"ע ונראה דהם מיירי בסתם חדש שהוא שלם וכמ"ש סי' קפ"ט ס"ק ל' ולכך צריך ל' יום אבל בחדש חסר מודים דסגי בכ"ט יום תדע שהרי במשנה מבואר אם אמר חדש א' אסור מיום ליום א"כ משמע דלעולם סגי במיום ליום אלא ודאי כדפי':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He is forbidden from day to day, etc.</b> This means from the hour of the vow until the corresponding hour on the same date of the next month. Thus, if he vowed at midday on the fifteenth of Tammuz, he is forbidden until midday on the fifteenth of Av, although this is only twenty-nine days because Tammuz is deficient. It is nevertheless a complete month from day to day. The Rosh explains similarly. The Rambam, Semag, and Rabbeinu Yerucham write that if he said 'one month,' he is forbidden for thirty full twenty-four-hour periods. It appears that they discuss an ordinary full month, but they agree that a deficient month requires only twenty-nine days, as the Mishnah's phrase 'from day to day' indicates.

<b>עד סוף אדר שני. </b>בין ידע שהיא מעוברת או לא לכ"ע אסור עד סוף אדר שני דב' אדר חד ירחא חשיבי. וכתב הב"ח דאם אמר עד חצי אדר דינו כאומר עד אדר דדוקא באומר עד סוף אדר משמעו עד סוף כל מה שנקרא אדר דהיינו עד סוף אדר שני עכ"ל. ונראה לפ"ז דה"ה באומר עד כ' או כ"א אדר דינו כאומר עד אדר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Until the end of the second Adar.</b> Whether or not he knew that the year was intercalated, all agree that he is forbidden until the end of the second Adar, because the two months of Adar are regarded as one month for this purpose. Bach writes that if he said 'until the middle of Adar,' it has the same law as saying 'until Adar.' Only the phrase 'until the end of Adar' implies the end of everything called Adar, meaning the end of the second Adar. Accordingly, the same applies if he said until the twentieth or twenty-first of Adar.

**** ENGLISH ****
17 in Marcheshvan, which is the first rain; and all this is Rambam's view; but Rosh and Tur ruled like R' Yehuda that the first rain is 7 Marcheshvan and the second on 17; and if he said "until the rain" he is forbidden until 7 Marcheshvan and then permitted whether rains fell or not; and if he said "until the rains" or "until there are rains", forbidden until the second rain falls (and so R' Yerucham, part 4, chapter 6, though there is a printing error in his wording, see there); and the Mechaber and Rav omitted their view because Beit Yosef wondered how they ruled like R' Yehuda against R' Yosi, but in truth I wonder at his wonder, for it is clear in the Talmud in Nedarim (daf 63 side a), what is the difference for asking, vows, and fasting; and for asking we hold it is on 7 Marcheshvan, as Taz wrote in Orach Chaim beginning siman 117, and it is agreed by all poskim; and likewise Rashi first chapter of Taanit (daf 6 end side a): it appears to R' Hagah that R' Chasda said halacha is like R' Yehuda who said first on 7 Marcheshvan and then they begin to ask, as below, end of his words; and likewise proven there in the Talmud that R' Chasda said halacha like Rabban Gamliel who ask on 7 Marcheshvan, and so the halacha is fixed in the Talmud (there daf 10); and on the contrary one should wonder on Rambam who in chapter 2 of Hilchot Tefillah ruled we ask on 7 Marcheshvan, meaning like R' Yehuda, and here ruled the first rain is 17 Marcheshvan; and likewise on the Mechaber, though it can be resolved with difficulty, requires study. And it appears to me for this reason they omitted, because in practice we follow Rambam and Mechaber for vows and fasting, and for asking we follow Rosh and Tur, see below end of this note.
