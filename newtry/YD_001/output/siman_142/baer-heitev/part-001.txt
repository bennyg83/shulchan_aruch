<b>משלהבתה. </b> כתב הט"ז פי' כשאין שם אלא השלהבת לחוד אבל אם השלהבת קשורה בגחלת פשיטא שאסור לחמם משלהבת טפי מגחלת גרידא והיתר דשלהבת הוא לפי. שאין בה ממש ונראה פשוט דבגחלת אפילו עוממות ואינם לוחשות אסור כיון שיש שם ממש ול"ש הנאה רבה או זוטא דהא כתיב ולא ידבק בידך מאומה וא"כ אפילו דיעבד אסור והב"י כתב דדוקא לכתחלה אסור וכתב עליו הב"ח שכן הוא פשוט ולענ"ד לא דקו בזה כלל עכ"ל וכ"כ הש"ך בס"ק י' דאף בדיעבד אסור אם נהנה מגחלתה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From its flame.</b> Taz explains that this refers to flame alone. If the flame remains attached to a coal, heating oneself from it is certainly forbidden, even more than from the coal itself. Flame is permitted only because it has no substance. Even dim, non-glowing coals are forbidden because they have substance, and there is no distinction between great or slight benefit, for the verse says, 'Nothing of the banned property shall cling to your hand.' Taz therefore rules that even after the fact benefit from the coals is forbidden. Shach agrees.

The case involves destructive cutting, such as pieces intended as a gift.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The garment.</b> Sending only the benefit of the shuttle to the Dead Sea does not suffice; the value of the whole garment must be discarded. This implies that before mixture, discarding the value of benefit does not help, and the same applies to bread in seif 4. Levush's contrary ruling concerning bread baked with chametz coals is incorrect. Shach nevertheless proves from the Yerushalmi and other authorities that one may sell the garment or bread to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibition, provided the non-Jew cuts it before the Jew so it cannot be resold to another Jew. Discarding the benefit does not help before mixture because idolatry has no redemption. Sale excluding its value is permitted because the Jew derives no benefit from the idolatry and no longer possesses the forbidden object.

Although joint causation is ordinarily forbidden initially, here the oven has no remedy except demolition, so it is treated as after the fact. One may not initially place the bread inside and then heat it. Shach.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Permitted.</b> Shach notes that the Mechaber appears to apply this even to a new oven and to cooking upon coals. He disagrees. Regarding orlah, whose coals and ashes are permitted, Rambam's ruling may apply. Regarding idolatrous wood, whose coals and ashes are forbidden, cooking in a new oven is entirely caused by prohibition. Shach therefore concludes against the Mechaber that coals of idolatry forbid even after the fact. Taz agrees, and even in an old oven, after all fire is removed, food cooked or baked in the residual heat is forbidden. Shach also questions the Mechaber's differing ruling in Orach Chaim concerning chametz coals.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For benefit.</b> Bowls and cups are later used without any permitted contributing factor. A pot likewise is ordinarily filled with water and meat before being placed upon the fire, so it is initially used without permitted causation. If it is certain that the pot was not used until placed upon permitted wood, the food is permitted, though initial use remains forbidden. Shach questions why this differs from a new oven whose bread is permitted after reheating. Taz answers that the case concerns small cups used for drinking; cooking pots have the law of a new oven and should be allowed to cool.

<b>שנהנה. </b> וכן להריח בריח של עבודת כוכבים אסור והאי בנוי פי' בעבודת כוכבים עצמן שנעבדו אסור להסתכל בהן לראות נויין אבל צורות שנעשו לנוי ולא לעבוד מבואר בתוס' והרא"ש דמותר עכ"ל הש"ך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he benefits.</b> It is likewise forbidden to smell a fragrance belonging to idolatry. 'Its beauty' means the idol itself that was worshipped; one may not gaze at it to enjoy its appearance. Forms made only for decoration and not worship, however, are permitted, as Tosafot and the Rosh explain. Shach.

<b>מתכוין. </b> כתב הש"ך בשם הפוסקים דאפי' אפשר לו לילך למקום אחר מותר כשאינו מתכוין ומיירי בענין שיכול לאטום אזניו ולעצום עיניו ולסתום נחיריו שלא יהנה מן הקול והמראה והריח מותר כשאינו מתכוין להנאתם דלא הוי פסיק רישיה הא לא"ה אסור אבל כשמתכוין אפילו אם אפשר לו לילך למקום אחר אסור עד כאן לשונו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Intends.</b> Shach writes in the name of the authorities that even if another route is available, it is permitted where he does not intend benefit, provided he could block his ears, close his eyes, or stop his nostrils so as not inevitably to benefit from the sound, sight, or smell. If benefit is inevitable, it is forbidden. If he intends benefit, it is forbidden even when no alternative route exists.
