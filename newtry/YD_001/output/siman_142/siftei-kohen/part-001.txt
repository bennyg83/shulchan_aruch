However it appears proven from the Jerusalem Talmud and several decisors one can sell garment and bread to a non-Jew except prohibition value when the non-Jew cuts garment and bread before the Jew in manner impossible to sell again to another Jew like yayin nesech above siman 134; sell to non-Jew except yayin nesech value; and that casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea is forbidden is because as long as not mixed and there is prohibition of object of object of idolatryatryatry in garment or bread; no redemption for object of object of idolatryatry; but when sold to non-Jew except prohibition value; no benefit from object of object of idolatryatry and prohibition not in his hand; permitted. And we hold above siman 134 seif katan 11 that certainly yayin nesech mixed in wine does not help selling to non-Jew except yayin nesech value even though certainly Israel will not again buy wine from non-Jew; there different for actual prohibition; but bread and garment actual prohibition not recognizable; and although we say "there is wood benefit in bread"; meaning to say it is more stringent than this-and-that-causes, for prohibition benefit is recognizable; but not that it should be actual mixture; rather forbidden to sell garment and bread wrim in manner he can sell afterwards to Israel; as explained in Orach Chaim siman 467 regarding grain sunk in river forbidden to sell to non-Jew before Pesach much together from benefit; and see end of siman 23, siman 57 seif katan 21, siman 84 seif 5, end of siman 86, siman 112 seif 11, and siman 134 seif 11.

Eliezer who says zeh v'zeh gorem forbidden; and that which teaches "allowed to cool" is Rabbis who say zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; meaning therefore although new oven was forbidden, when allowed to cool and heated with permitted wood it will be zeh v'zeh gorem. Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran wrote: although this-and-that-causes forbidden ab initio, as proven chapter "all images"; and from benefit [old] allowed to cool although this-and-that-causes, because ab initio nevertheless forbidden here since it has no remedy except demolition it is considered after the fact; but in any case whether new or old allowed to cool ab initio, since there is only minor loss of wood value; and in cooking on coals Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan dispute; one says only dim but glowing forbidden; and one says even glowing permitted (and proven there even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted they dispute; and so proven in Rif and Rosh; see there); and that Rabbi said bread forbidden is when flame opposite it; as long as bread was in oven the flame burned opposite it and he benefited from prohibition while prohibition still existed in kind; and the Ran wrote the Rif's opinion is even in glowing permitted; and the Rosh forbids in glowing; end Gemara and decisors with slight explanation of mine; and behold the Tur wrote: nevertheless if he baked in it (meaning in old not cooled) bread permitted (zeh v'zeh gorem permitted); and although zeh v'zeh gorem permitted, if flame opposite it bread forbidden; and Ramah wrote even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted, here regarding object of object of idolatryatry in bread baked with forbidden wood forbidden; and so appears from R. Avraham Rosh's words whom the Mishnah brought for halacha; end; and Beit Yosef wrote: I do not know Ramah's reason here, for garden fertilized with object of object of idolatryatry manure we permit in chapter "all images" as permitted (and as below end of seif 11); also wonder on our Rabbi; if because he brought the Mishnah for halacha he was so precise; also on the Rif he could write so; and in truth it appears between Rif and Rosh no necessity to say they brought it only because it concludes "it became mixed" etc. to teach where he baked in new not cooled (and it appears we can also say where he baked in old with flame opposite, as above) forbidden for benefit; and they relied on what he wrote before regarding garden fertilized with object of object of idolatryatry manure; halacha like the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; end.
