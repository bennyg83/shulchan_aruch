One whose dead lies before him on Shabbat or Yom Tov, and the laws of the onen. In it are 6 seifim. One to whom a dead relative died whom he is obligated to mourn for before burial—he eats in another house; if he has no other house he eats in a friend's house; if he has no friend's house they make him a partition and he eats; and even a sheet partition suffices if he fastens the edges of the sheet so it is not carried by wind; and if he has nothing to make a partition he turns his face and eats; and in any case, even if he is in another city, he does not recline and eat; he does not eat meat; he does not drink wine; he does not recite ha-motzi nor birkat ha-mazon; they do not bless over him nor invite over him; even if he eats with others who bless he does not answer amen after them; and he is exempt from all mitzvot stated in the Torah, even if he need not engage in the dead's needs because others engage for him; and some say even if he wishes to be stringent to bless or answer amen after those who bless, he is not permitted (and see in Orach Chayim siman 71); and on Shabbat and Yom Tov he eats meat and drinks wine (if he wishes) (Rabbenu Yonah, chapter Mi she-Meto) and blesses and is obligated in all mitzvot except use of the bed which is forbidden for him (and permitted to go to synagogue on Shabbat; and on weekday there is no prohibition, only since he may not pray what will he do there) (Kol Bo); and if he must darken for the boundary to engage in the dead's needs, onenut applies to forbid him in all from when he begins to go to darken for the boundary; and if he wishes to bury him on the first Yom Tov through gentiles, forbidden in all; and how much more on the second Yom Tov when he himself can bury him—all laws of onenut apply. (Some say a student for his prominent rabbi is forbidden meat and wine all the time the dead lies before them) (Hagahot Ashiri, chapter Aveilut).
