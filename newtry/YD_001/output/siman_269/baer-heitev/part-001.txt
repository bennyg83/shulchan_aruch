<b>מאמו. </b> כתב הש"ך וכ"ש מאביו דגר שנתגייר כקטן שנולד דמי אלא שחכמים גזרו וכ' והיינו בשאר האם אבל בשאר האב לא גזרו דאין אבות לעובדי כוכבים עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From his mother. </b>Siftei Kohen writes: All the more so from his father, for a convert who converted is considered like a newborn child; the prohibition exists only because of a rabbinic decree. This applies to maternal relatives. The Sages did not decree concerning paternal relatives, for legal paternity is not recognized among non-Jews. These are his words.

<b>מותרת. </b> שאינה אשת אחיו שלא היה לו קידושין בה כיון שנשאה בגיותו וכתב הלבוש דבשאר האם נ"ל שאפילו נשא יוציא כיון שנשא באיסור דיהדות חמיר מפי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>She is permitted. </b>She is not considered his brother's wife, for his brother's marriage to her did not take effect according to Jewish law when he married her as a non-Jew. Levush writes that in the case of a maternal relative, it appears to him that even if he married her, he must divorce her, since the marriage was contracted in violation of a prohibition and Jewish law is more stringent.
