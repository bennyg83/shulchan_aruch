**** ENGLISH ****
That one may not engage in commerce with an item of prohibition, and it contains 1 seif: Anything that is forbidden by Torah law, even though it is permitted for benefit, if it is an item designated for food, it is forbidden to engage in commerce with it (this is the wording of the Mechaber) (or to lend on it) (Terumat HaDeshen siman 200) (and even to purchase it in order to feed it to his non-Jewish workers is forbidden) (so it is implied from the Beit Yosef, from the Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 8 of Ma'achalos Assuros), except for cheilev, for it is stated regarding it, “it may be used for any labor”; and if tamei animals, birds, or fish came into a hunter’s hands (and likewise one in whose house neveilah or treifah came into his hands) (Tur), it is permitted to sell them, provided that he did not intend for that. {Rama: And he must sell it immediately and not wait until it becomes fat with him (Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chayyim). Likewise, it is permitted to collect tamei items in payment of a debt from a non-Jew, for this is like rescuing them from their hand (Rashba in a responsum). And it is forbidden to sell a non-Jew a neveilah under the presumption that it is kosher (Tur) (and see Choshen Mishpat siman 228).} And anything whose prohibition is only by rabbinic law, it is permitted to engage in commerce with it.
