**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Likkut) What is... they came.</b> The Yerushalmi in Nazir chapter 6 defines a met mitzvah as one who cries out and the townspeople do not come. If townspeople do come, one withdraws once there are enough bearers and replacements, unless his assistance is still needed. If the deceased is known to him, or if attendance is fitting to his honor, he may remain. A nasi is regarded as one for whom such honor is fitting. The Yerushalmi then asks whether a kohen may become impure for the honor of the nasi even where he is not technically a met mitzvah. When R. Yehudah Nesia died, R. Yannai announced, "There is no kehunah today." Likewise, when his descendant died, R. Chiyya bar Abba caused R. Zeira to become impure. This explains the Bavli's statement in Ketubot 103b that on the day Rabbi died, kehunah was suspended. The Yerushalmi explains that they treated him like a met mitzvah. It further asks whether a student may become impure for his teacher and leaves the matter unresolved, so he should not. Torat HaAdam. Regarding a nasi, Raavad permits and Ramban remains uncertain. Evel Rabbati chapter 4 gives parallel rules. <br><b>(Likkut) What is a met mitzvah... they came.</b> Evel Rabbati chapter 4, halachot 29 to 30, gives the corresponding rules: if he has sufficient attendants, and so forth.
