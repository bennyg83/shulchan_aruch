(ס"ו בהג"ה) <b>אין</b> מוחין בידו. היינו בדבר הרשות חליצת מנעלים וכדומה אבל לא לבטל מד"ת או למנוע עונה מאשתו בליל טבילה כנה"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 6, gloss) <b>We do not protest against him.</b> This means regarding optional mourning practices, such as removing shoes and similar matters. But he may not thereby neglect Torah study or withhold marital relations from his wife on the night of her immersion. Knesset HaGedolah.

(סעיף ח' בהגה"ה) <b>שניהם</b> מתאבלים עליו. י"ל דוקא ששניהם קיימים דהוי כבאו לשאול בב"א אבל אם מת א' מהם אין השני חייב להתאבל כ"כ הגאון פורת יוסף בספרו תיבת גומא בסוף החקירה הששית:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 8, gloss) <b>Both mourn for him.</b> It can be said that this applies specifically while both possible fathers are alive, because it resembles two litigants who come to ask simultaneously. If one of them has died, the other need not mourn. So writes the Gaon Porat Yosef in Teivat Gome, at the end of the sixth inquiry.

**** ENGLISH ****
(Shach seif katan 9) <b>And this is also how Hagahot Derishah explains it, and this is primary.</b> See Responsa Mekor Baruch, siman 31. His words seem far from convincing to me. Thank God, I merited seeing Or Zarua in manuscript, where it is explicit that if one twin dies within thirty days, no mourning is observed for him. We do not say that because both originated from one drop that divided into two, and the second survived beyond thirty days and is viable, the first must also have been viable. That is not conclusive, because perhaps part of the drop was absorbed immediately and part only two days later. No source is cited there for this rule. It appears to me that the source is Nazir 13a, where the matter remains unresolved. See Tosafot there, s.v. hipilah.
