<b>רובן של כ"ב. </b>כתב הר"ן הא דכשר בנשתברו מיעוט צלעות אע"ג דקי"ל בקוץ עד שינקוב לחלל חוששין שמא ניקב אחד מהאברים הפנימים בנשתברו מיעוט צלעות בין שהשבר כלפי פנים או כלפי חוץ כשרה ולא חיישינן שמא ניקבו הדקין או כרס או הריאה וטעמא דדוקא גבי קוץ חיישינן להכי כיון שניקב בכח מבחוץ ונכנס מדוחק אבל בראשי הצלעות דליכא למימר הכי לא חיישינן למידי הלכך בין שהשבר נוטה לחוץ בין לחלל הבהמה בין שעורה ובשרה קיים בין שאינו קיים כשרה עכ"ל ודומה לזה כתבתי סי' נ"ג סעיף א' בסופו דלא כדעת הלבוש שמכח קושיא כזו אוסר בנשבר צלע אחד בעוף וכן כתב מו"ח ז"ל בסימן זה ואנו אין לנו איסור כלל בזה וכמשמעות דברי הטור ובעל הש"ע שכתבו להיתר בנשברו מיעוט צלעות אפילו לדידן:
**** ENGLISH ****
A majority of the twenty-two. Ran wrote: that which is kosher when a minority of ribs broke — even though we rule regarding a thorn that until it perforates into the cavity we are concerned perhaps it perforated one of the internal organs — when a minority of ribs broke, whether the break faces inward or outward — kosher, and we are not concerned perhaps the intestines or stomach or lung were perforated; and the reason is that specifically regarding a thorn we are concerned for this, since it perforated with force from outside and entered with difficulty; but at the heads of the ribs one cannot say thus — we are not concerned for anything; therefore, whether the break inclines outward or into the animal's cavity, whether its skin and flesh remain or not — kosher, end of his words; and similar to this I wrote siman 53 seif 1 at its end, not like the view of the Levush who from such a difficulty forbids one broken rib in a bird; and so wrote Maharshal in this siman — and we have no prohibition at all in this, as is the meaning of Tur and Shulchan Aruch who wrote to permit when a minority of ribs broke, even for us:

**** ENGLISH ****
And specifically if uprooted, etc. This is Terumat HaDeshen siman 177's view, and his reason: this treifah of deficient and excess ribs and vertebrae disqualifies only when it had a vertebra and was uprooted — we compare it to what we learned for Beit Hillel who hold regarding tumat met in a tent that we rule the spine defiles in a tent, and all that lacks one vertebra from the spine does not defile in a tent; and Shmuel said: and likewise for treifah — the animal becomes treifah through lacking one vertebra; and there regarding tumah it deals when it had a vertebra and was lacking — one cannot say at all that if a person was created with only seventeen vertebrae his spine would not defile in a tent if nothing was removed from him; rather it certainly deals only when there was a number of vertebrae and they were lacking; and on this Shmuel said "and likewise for treifah"; and the same for uprooting — it deals only with uprooting from its creation, but not with deficient from the beginning of its creation; and so is proven from Rambam who holds: wherever they said "removed" — treifah; if created missing — kosher, etc.; and Darkei Moshe challenged on this: for according to Rashba, wherever removed is treifah, even if created missing — treifah, as written in siman 50; if so, it is difficult on Beit Yosef who ruled above like Rashba and here wrote like Terumat HaDeshen to permit when created missing — his words contradict each other and he did not notice; and afterward Darkei Moshe wrote: for Rashba there is another side of permission in excess — namely, he holds that "all excess is considered as removed" means only that excess alone from its place of connection, as Beit Yosef wrote siman 41; and according to this view it is kosher, for no vertebra was lacking; and even though we do not rule like that explanation but rule like "removed" also applies to what ought to be there — nevertheless it is kosher if more than eleven ribs and eighteen vertebrae are found, for I wrote above we rule like Rambam that deficient is not like removed (and excess like removed means deficient — requires further study); and even for Beit Yosef who is stringent like Rashba that deficient is like removed — nevertheless for Rashba it is permitted for another reason I mentioned; if so, for all it is permitted in every case, end of his words; and Beit Yosef's words are still not settled, for he himself does not hold like Rashba in the explanation of "all excess is considered as removed," as is proven from his ruling above siman 43 that two livers disqualify even on the thick side — how can he rule here leniently like Rambam regarding the dispute of deficient when he himself ruled like Rashba regarding deficient. And Maharshal elaborated here on settling this ruling — and in my humble opinion there is no beginning of difficulty here; Terumat HaDeshen's main reason is from what he wrote initially that from the reasoning it appears to him from that of tumah that here the concern is only when it had and was lacking; only that afterward he brought proof from Rambam's words that deficient is kosher — one who examines the responsum at its end will see he could answer the proof from Rambam, only that he strengthened himself from the reasoning he mentioned initially; it is found that Beit Yosef ruled well to permit here deficient and excess in ribs in creation per Terumat HaDeshen's reasoning; and also the Talmud's language indicates thus, for it said the language "uprooted" — implying specifically that they were and were uprooted; and Terumat HaDeshen wrote there that experts told him they often found an extra rib on each side and no one objected to prohibition, only some people refrain from eating the extra rib because it is close to kidney fat, end of his words; and Maharshal wrote: I found in Issur VeHeter where extra ribs were found such as thirteen, fourteen, fifteen on each side, and all similar to each other — all kosher, only the upper one they leave on the back side — so taught the Rav R' Shachna from Lublin, end of his words:
