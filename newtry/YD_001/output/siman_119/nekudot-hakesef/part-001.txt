**** ENGLISH ****
(there s.k. 12) It appears from this that even if the prohibition is safek tereifah, etc. — meaning even safek tereifah which is forbidden only in eating; likewise explicit in his words in notes 13–14; not clear a contradiction that the seller will say it is not kosher and the buyer must bring proof; Rashi deals with a firstborn forbidden in benefit — likewise d'rabbanan forbidden in benefit requires returning money, for benefit-prohibition has no law of sale at all; d'rabbanan prohibition and safek prohibition share one law; further it appears there regarding firstborn it is like not checking the lung which is a rabbinic enactment — likewise showing a firstborn is their enactment as below siman 309; on the contrary showing firstborn is stricter for we find in mishnah and Gemara one must show a sage; if so it is like something forbidden in benefit d'rabbanan; what Rashi wrote safek prohibition means therefore they penalized him because of safek prohibition they touched it — meaning the Sages enacted thus out of doubt; likewise lung checking they enacted out of doubt; know that Rashi's intent is as I explained and this is clear. I again found as my words in Semak Choshen Mishpat siman 234: only for certain tereifah; but for safek tereifah he need not return money if eaten; and a slaughterer who sold a cow and it became known tereifah — he returns even if already eaten — meaning certainly tereifah; and firstborn differs for an animal is presumed without defect; also firstborn not checked — even if afterward known it had a defect — still forbidden in benefit d'rabbanan as mishnah chapter Ad Kamah and Tur Yoreh Deah siman 310; since it is benefit-prohibition the seller does not deduct from payment and the reason is sensible: even if intact the seller would have to discard and not benefit — therefore no law of sale; as above; and clear from his words even if the seller knew before sale it is safek tereifah he need not return if eaten; if he did not know, even certain tereifah they do not penalize as Shach s.k. 25.
