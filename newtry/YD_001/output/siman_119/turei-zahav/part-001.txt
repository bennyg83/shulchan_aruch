**** ENGLISH ****
Transgressing a sin for appetite. The honor of the Rav is in its place and his words are astounding in this gloss, for in Rashba that Beit Yosef brought it is written that one who transgresses one of the transgressions is believed in other prohibitions, and all the more if he does so for appetite, etc.; if so why did the Rav write this gloss — if he is presumed to transgress other prohibitions, even not for appetite he is believed for this prohibition; and if presumed regarding this prohibition, even for appetite certainly he is not believed. What Beit Yosef wrote below in this siman in the name of Rashba that a habitual sinner for appetite is believed even for that matter, like Rava in the first chapter of Chullin, end of his words — meaning there is leniency when permitted and forbidden are before him he does not abandon the permitted and eat forbidden; but otherwise forbidden, as Tur siman 2 wrote that without knife checking forbidden to eat from his slaughter; here too how may they eat with him lest there was no permitted before him and he ate forbidden — how may others eat with him; if so this is not called suspected as the Rav said, and certainly this gloss has no explanation. What is harder: we never find that suspected is worse than transgressing for appetite — if you say there he is suspected meaning he instructs so intentionally even when permitted is before him he abandons it and eats forbidden, then he is like a full gentile, as Rama wrote in siman 2 regarding one who eats nevelah not for appetite; how can Rashbag in chapter Ad Kamah rule that one suspected regarding something may judge and testify. Also proof from what Terumat HaDeshen wrote: if you suspect him for this you suspect him as a full gentile intending to mislead, end of his words; and if you say suspected means he is not concerned for prohibition at all, in truth he is like a full gentile as we said; plainly he is not worse than transgressing for appetite — if so what does it mean he is not called suspected. Darkei Moshe in Hagahot Asheri: one suspected of a prohibition forbidden to eat with him — even permitted things, lest he feed forbidden too, end of his words.
