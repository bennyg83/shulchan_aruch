**** ENGLISH ****
A law of wine that was poured as a libation and became mixed; it contains 13 se’ifim. Wine that was poured as a libation to idolatry and became mixed with permitted wine forbids [the mixture] in benefit with any amount. In what case is this said? When the permitted wine was poured onto a drop of yayin nesech. But if he poured yayin nesech from a small tzinzror (meaning, a small jug) into a pit of wine — even if he poured all day long — the first [drop] is batel and then the next is batel. If he poured from a barrel — whether he poured from the permitted into the forbidden or from the forbidden into the permitted — everything is forbidden, because the column descending from the mouth of the barrel is large.

Vinegar of wine of a non-Jew that fell into hot grisin spoils [the dish] and is permitted; but into cold [grisin] it improves [the taste] and is forbidden, even if he afterwards boiled them and they returned to be a spoiled taste. {Rama: Spices that absorbed yayin nesech and were placed into a cooked dish: if there are 60 against the absorbed wine in them, everything is permitted, even though the spices are made for taste, since they are not forbidden on their own they are batel. **** ENGLISH ****
All beverages of a non-Jew, whose way is to mix into them wine or vinegar, are forbidden. {Rama: And see above siman 114. And all this is regarding beverages in which the wine and vinegar improve them, or in which it does not spoil; but if it spoils — such as dye to which they put vinegar, or salt that non-Jews cook and put wine or blood into it to whiten it (Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin), or ink of a non-Jew (Rabbeinu Tam) — it is permitted, for all this is a spoiled taste, and the same applies to anything similar. And some are stringent regarding the ink of a non-Jew, because sometimes he puts his quill into his mouth and the ink is upon it (Terumat HaDeshen siman 129; Kol Bo in the name of R' Peretz). And see above siman 123, that the practice is to permit something into which they put vinegar, since it is a spoiled taste; and it appears that the same applies to ink — even if there was certainly yayin nesech in it, it is only a spoiled taste and it is permitted; so it appears to me.}
