**** ENGLISH ****When the permitted wine was poured, etc. And even if much permitted wine fell at once on the drop of yayin nesech until there is 60 — nevertheless it is forbidden; and likewise Ran, whose language is: and the reason of the matter is that that thing which stands in its place is more important than what comes upon it; and therefore when the prohibition stands in its place, even any amount forbids the wine that comes upon it, and even a large barrel at once, due to the stringency of yayin nesech; but when the permitted stands in its place and the prohibition comes upon it, the permitted nullifies the prohibition unless it fell at once to the measure of natan ta'am — and so from Rambam's words — end quote; and so is primary in the Gemara; and further, many poskim hold that even forbidden into permitted forbids in any amount; however in great loss and such a case one may rely on Ramban and Rosh and Tur who hold that in permitted into forbidden too, if it fell at once and there is 60 it is permitted; only when a little fell we say the first is batel, and even if permitted was afterwards added and increased until 60 it does not return and revive, even though in other prohibitions and likewise wine in water it would return and revive (see above s"k 12) — in yayin nesech min b'mino they were stringent — see there (and in Eliyahu Rabba he mixed matters — he opened with the Mechaber's words which are Rambam's words and concluded with the Tur's words); however all this is certainly yayin nesech, but stam yeinam in our time the practice is that in every case it is batel in 60, as will be explained.

**** ENGLISH ****
From the barrel, etc. Ran wrote: I did not know in what regard the distinction between a small flask and a barrel — how did he assign measures to this? And it seems to me the resolution: since it is customary to pour wine into a pit by means of a large barrel, and since the end of the wine in the barrel will descend through this vessel and it is its way — one cannot say the first is null, for whatever stands to fall and mix is considered already mixed; unlike a small flask — since it is not customary to pour into a pit with a vessel like it, we do not view it as fallen and mixed except what has already descended; therefore we say in every vessel like it the first is null — end.

and likewise it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch and Tosafot and Rosh perek 3 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, unlike what appears from Derishah siman 92 who forbids afterwards also in any amount as if the prohibition itself fell, which forbids in any amount there, and we do not say it did not become neveilah in any amount except regarding whether the prohibition in any amount would not forbid in mixtures of 2 even if it fell there b'ein — but it is not so, rather as explained) — and anything forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — until here his language; evidently we say chatichah na'aseh neveilah even when cold, whatever is not forbidden in any amount; and here in water in wine it is forbidden to the measure of natan ta'am; and it appears the Rosh follows his reasoning that he holds we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah only in meat in milk, as above siman 92; and that he needed here to resolve that it was forbidden in any amount is as Perishah wrote siman 11, that regarding yayin nesech which is stringent and forbids in any amount — if not for this reason that since it was forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah, it would be chatichah na'aseh neveilah; but water in wine does not forbid except to the measure of natan ta'am for prohibition — there is no chatichah na'aseh neveilah, for it is similar to other prohibitions; or also for clarity of the matter the Rosh wrote so to resolve even for one who learns chatichah na'aseh neveilah in all prohibitions.
