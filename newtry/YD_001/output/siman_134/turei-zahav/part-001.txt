**** ENGLISH ****Water that became mixed, etc. Rama challenged that his words contradict one another, etc.; and in my humble opinion it appears the Shulchan Aruch's words are correct, and even if he had not written them they would still be fit to write — for the essence of the difficulty is that he first wrote "in natan ta'am," and on this he said "but if it fell," etc., which implies he comes to be lenient even in natan ta'am; and further one can challenge: apparently from what he wrote "if the prohibition fell into the permitted, the first is batel" — meaning even if afterwards more than the measure of natan ta'am became mixed — specifically, for if with less than natan ta'am why did he write that specifically he should pour from a small tzinzror, etc. — behold a large tzinzror is forbidden even with less than natan ta'am; and behold in all prohibitions it is specifically in natan ta'am, and here too it should be in the measure of natan ta'am, since it is not its kind; and even though in Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Tam he brought that when prohibition fell into permitted we do not say kama kama batel when the stream is large, even when there is enough permitted to nullify the stream — and this Derishah also brought; but from this there is no proof, for Rabbeinu Tam there deals with yayin nesech that fell into wine, which is min b'mino and forbids in any amount, unlike when not its kind which is in natan ta'am; rather certainly "the first is batel" means if there is even more than natan ta'am; and if so it contradicts what he wrote afterwards "and if it increased," etc.; and Maharach explained that "and if it increased" means he pours from a large vessel whose stream is large — and this is not in the language's meaning at all; and further, if the stream from a small tzinzror does not forbid even with more than natan ta'am in the permitted, why should a large stream forbid specifically in natan ta'am — for if there is prohibition due to natan ta'am, why would it be permitted with a small stream, behold natan ta'am is before us? It should come from wherever it comes — nevertheless the taste is equal in this and that; and it seems to me everything is correct: the first se'if is entirely Rambam's language perek 16 of Mishneh Torah, and likewise this se'if until "little by little" is also Rambam's language, and Shulchan Aruch added "and if it increased," etc.; and it is necessary that he was challenged in Rambam's words — why in seif 1 he wrote "even if he poured all day, the first is batel," and here he did not mention "all day" but certainly there is a distinction in the matter: for even though in min b'mino, forbidden into permitted, it is batel even if the prohibition is a large sum — this is because the taste has not changed and therefore the nullification of the first helps; but when not its kind, how do we permit when there is natan ta'am in it? And as Tosafot wrote in perek HaSocher (daf 73): and know that so it is — for if not, when a person mixes milk in a pot of permitted little by little intermittently until it increased at the end that there is natan ta'am in the pot — is it sensible that it should be permitted due to kama kama batel? Rather certainly we do not say kama kama batel, since there is of the prohibition at the end in natan ta'am — end quote; and likewise Rambam holds; and this is in min b'mino: on one side there is stringency more than when not its kind, and on one side there is leniency more than when not its kind — namely in min b'mino in permitted into forbidden it is very stringent, forbidden even in any amount and much permitted; but when not its kind it is forbidden only in noten ta'am; and in forbidden into permitted in its kind there is great leniency — namely with a small stream it does not forbid even if he poured all day, since the taste does not change and the prohibition is only from the decree of Scripture; therefore since the first is batel there is no decree of Scripture here; but when not its kind, where the prohibition is due to natan ta'am — if it increased until there is enough for natan ta'am in the permitted, certainly it is forbidden even though it came into it little by little; and nevertheless with a large stream, min b'mino and not its kind are equal when the prohibition fell into permitted — namely as it forbids in its kind even with less than natan ta'am, so too when not its kind; and even though in all Torah prohibitions we say the prohibition is not in less than natan ta'am when not its kind, nevertheless here where the prohibition is due to idolatry it is more stringent and forbids even when not its kind even with less than natan ta'am, since the stream is large — for just as a large stream has stringency in its kind, joining permitted to forbidden and forbidden to permitted, and forbids in both in any amount and nullification does not help, and even where nullification applies — so too a large stream causes in the prohibition of idolatry that even when not its kind it forbids with less than natan ta'am in the permitted; and the continuation of this se'if's words are correct in this way: "water became mixed," etc. — here there is leniency that it forbids only in natan ta'am; in what case is this said — there is leniency here in permitted into forbidden; but from forbidden into permitted in one matter there is stringency here like in min b'mino — namely the first is batel — specifically with a small tzinzror we say so, as above; and he used "the first is batel" to inform us specifically a small tzinzror, as the Rosh wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that Beit Yosef brought regarding a barrel that is like a tzinzror for Rabbeinu Tam — its permitted is up to natan ta'am, and nevertheless he said the first is batel; but with a large stream it is not batel, as above, and forbids even with less than natan ta'am; therefore he did not need to mention it here in explanation, except that here in when not its kind there is one stringency — that whatever increased and reached natan ta'am in the permitted is forbidden here even with a small stream, and the first is batel does not help for this, unlike in its kind above where the first is batel helps even if the prohibition increased very much; it turns out according to this that Shulchan Aruch's conclusion is also like Rama — only that in when not its kind he was stringent with a large stream even with less than natan ta'am in the permitted, since it is idolatry; and the prohibition of idolatry has two stringencies more than other prohibitions: one — when idolatry prohibition is below and permitted fell, even very much does not nullify it until permitted of a different kind comes upon it and nullifies its taste; and the second — even where it could be nullified, namely permitted below — nevertheless if the stream is large it is not batel; and this is even in when not its kind where nullification applies in natan ta'am in the permitted — nevertheless a large stream prevents nullification; and I call upon Shulchan Aruch regarding this "the secret of Hashem is for those who fear Him."
