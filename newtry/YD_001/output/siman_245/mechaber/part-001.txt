<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="29"></i>If one resident of a courtyard or alley wishes to become a teacher of children, <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="13"></i>his neighbors cannot prevent him. <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="30"></i>Likewise, if another teacher opens a school beside an existing teacher so that other children will come to him, or even so that the children presently studying with the first teacher will come to him, the first teacher cannot prevent him, as it is written, "Hashem desired, for the sake of His righteousness, to magnify Torah and make it glorious." <small>Rama: <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="34"></i>If a rav resides in a city and teaches Torah publicly, <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="14"></i>another scholar <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="11"></i>may come and teach there as well, <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="15"></i><i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="35"></i>even if <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="11"></i>this somewhat diminishes <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="12"></i>the livelihood of the first. For example, even if the community accepted the first as their rav and gives him a stipend, the second may nevertheless reside there and exercise rabbinic authority in all respects like the first, provided he is sufficiently learned and qualified. (Mahari Weil, siman 151, and Terumat HaDeshen, Pesakim siman 158) <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="16"></i>However, if a visiting scholar comes to the city, he may not deprive the resident rav of the fees from weddings and kiddushin, since those fees form part of the resident rav's stipend. He may conduct the wedding if the fee is given to the established rav. He may also adjudicate a dispute between two local litigants who voluntarily come before him, since perhaps the local rav is not acceptable to them as an impartial judge. <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="36"></i>But he may not issue local rulings of issur veheter or deliver public sermons in a manner that exercises authority within another rav's jurisdiction. (Maharik, shoresh 170, and Mahardach, responsum 22) <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="37"></i>One who has become established as rav of a city, even if he established himself in some position of authority, may not be removed from his status merely because another scholar greater than he later arrives. (Rivash, siman 271) <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="38"></i>His son and son's son retain precedence over outsiders forever, provided they adequately fill their ancestors' place in fear of Heaven and possess some scholarship. (Rambam, Hilchot Melachim chapter 1) Where the custom is to appoint a rav for a fixed term, or to choose whomever the community wishes, they may do so. (Kol Bo) <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="39"></i>But once the community has accepted someone upon themselves, <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="40"></i>and certainly if this was done <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="13"></i>with the approval of the governing authority, no great scholar anywhere may assert authority over him and remove him.
