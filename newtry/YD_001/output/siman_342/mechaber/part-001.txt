**** ENGLISH ****
One who prepared wedding needs and a relative died for whom he must mourn. In it is one seif. One who prepared all wedding needs—he baked his bread and slaughtered his feast and mixed his wine—and the groom's father died while he is in a place where he cannot sell and if the wedding is delayed he will lose what he prepared; or the bride's mother died and there are women's cosmetics and adornments that do not keep (since they have no one to trouble for them another time) (as the Gemara implies)—they bring the dead into a room and the groom and bride to the chuppah and he consummates the mitzvah act and separates; and since the chuppah has fallen on him it is for him like a festival and he observes seven days of feasting and afterward observes seven days of mourning; and all seven days of feasting he observes private matters and is forbidden in use of the bed; therefore all seven days of feasting and seven days of mourning he sleeps among the men and she among the women. {Rama: Some say she is forbidden to be alone with him by day as at night and they need not have two guards—only he among men or she among women (Tur in the name of Rosh); and if they do not sleep in one room they need no guard at all (as Rashba wrote in a bride who menstruated, etc.); and some say at night two guards are needed and by day permitted to be alone with her (Tur in the name of Raavad); and the custom is to take a minor with the groom and a minor with the bride and they are not alone without a minor or minor girl.} And regarding thirty he counts only from the seven days of mourning onward; and all seven days of the chuppah he is permitted ironing and haircutting; and if he has no loss, such as in a place where he can sell what he prepared—or even if he has loss and the groom's mother died or the bride's father or one of their other relatives and someone remains to prepare for them another time—they did not permit delaying mourning but bury him immediately and observe seven days of mourning and afterward bring groom and bride to the chuppah immediately and observe seven days of feasting.
