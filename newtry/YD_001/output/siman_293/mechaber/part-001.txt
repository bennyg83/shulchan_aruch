**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of chadash and when it applies. In it are 5 seifim. It is forbidden to eat chadash from produce of the five species until the omer is offered, which is on the sixteenth of Nisan, as it says, "And bread, parched grain, and groats you shall not eat until this very day." And nowadays, when there is no omer, it is forbidden all day on the sixteenth; and in the Diaspora, where they observe two days, it is forbidden all day on the seventeenth until the beginning of the night of the eighteenth.

**** ENGLISH ****
Produce that did not take root before the sixteenth of Nisan is forbidden until the omer that will come. {Rama: Nevertheless, all ordinary produce is permitted after Pesach by force of a double doubt: perhaps it is from a year that passed, and even if you say from this year, perhaps it took root before the omer (Tur in the name of the Rosh). And in types of produce that are certainly sown after Pesach, such as in some lands where they sow barley and oats after Pesach—then one should be stringent after the harvest if the doors of the province are not locked and most produce comes from elsewhere where they sow before Pesach (Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 11 of Ma'achalot Assurot and Tosafot first chapter of Kiddushin). And likewise in a time when rains continue after Pesach and in all that Galil where they sow after Pesach the aforementioned matters—one should be stringent and be concerned from the ordinary presumption. But one should not rule for others in a place where most of their drinking and eating is from these types, for it is better they be inadvertent than intentional (so it appears in Tur in the name of the Rosh and Terumat HaDeshen siman 188).} (°)
