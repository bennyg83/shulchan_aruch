**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For wages.</b> Taz writes that this does not contradict seif 16, where a mourner hired by others may not work during mourning. There the case involves full labor, which is forbidden to a mourner in every form. Here the work concerns household needs, which the woman herself is permitted to perform, as stated in the preceding seif, and therefore a maidservant may do it as well. Nevertheless, the maidservant may not perform work unrelated to household needs merely for profit, and certainly may not leave the house, because she too has the law of a mourner. Taz reports that someone ruled that a synagogue attendant who became a mourner could continue his service by going to the synagogue and attending to communal needs. He says this was incorrect and based on misunderstanding the reason for the maidservant's leniency. Maharshal, citing Agudah, writes that a mourner may let blood. Kol Bo writes that a mourner who performs forbidden labor is placed under nidui.
