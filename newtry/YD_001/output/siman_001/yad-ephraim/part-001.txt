**Unless he took kabbalah.** See Ba'er Heitev; see Chavat Ya'ir siman 178—in a village where the slaughterer fell ill near Yom Tov and one person learned knows laws of shechitah and examination, only he never slaughtered or examined—he may not slaughter except for the joy of Yom Tov: permit to slaughter birds with the sick slaughterer, even though the joy of the festival does not come out in birds—nevertheless the custom of important people who sustain themselves with birds. (In my humble opinion it appears this is not so except for complete joy; but joy of Yom Tov certainly exists, as in Beitzah regarding stocking dovecotes—they come to diminish joy of Yom Tov; see Tosafot Ketubot 5 who wrote that in four chapters they were accustomed to increase in animals and not in birds—see there.) Likewise permitted to slaughter an animal, only if some safek arises that requires removal, and the sick one cannot attend to the whole treifah; see Lechem HaPanim who copied this. What he wrote regarding kaparah—see Chavat Ya'ir who wrote he should accept some fast for kaparah and some sum for charity for publicity that it was not permitted except in an hour of pressure. See there Chavat Ya'ir brought in Lechem HaPanim: regarding one who must give meat to idolaters—one may perform nehirah through an idolater, for there is no concern of mistake here, unlike if he slaughtered without kabbalah—see there.

<b>עד שידע בו.</b> עבה"ט וע' בשו"ת שבות יעקב שהשיג על הל"ק שכתב כיון דע"א נאמן באיסורין אפי' קרוב מכתובות דף כ"ב דהא דהאב נאמן להאכילו בתרומה היינו משום שבידו להאכילו. ודעת הרמב"ם כשידוע שהוא אביו נאמן. ועיין באה"ע סי' ג' מ"מ כתב דבשחיטה כיון דרוב מצויין כו' רק תקנות אחרונים ליטול קבלה וגם הוא מלתא דעל"ג אם הוא בקי או לא אינו משקר וקצת ראיה ממתני' דסנהדרין דף ה' כו' מ"מ יש לו ליטול קבלה מאחר שאינו קרובו ע"ש. ומ"ש בבה"ט בשם חו"י שם לא מיירי מדין זה אלא מדין התרת בכור ע"ש. ובפנים מאירות ח"ב כ' דאם הוא לפנינו ובדקוהו שיודע הל' שחיטה פשיטא דמהימן שהוא מוחזק ג"כ ואין זה אלא גילוי מלתא. וגדולה מזו נראה דאפילו בדקוהו ואינו יודע מהני הקבלה להכשיר כמו בנטל קבלה מאחר ואמרינן דהשתא הוא דאיתרע והיכא שאינו לפנינו וידענו שהיה לו קבלה מאביו פשיטא דיש לסמוך על הרוב מצויין כו'. וכבר יצא להיתר מגדולי הדור כמ"ש בלחם הפנים כו' ע"ש. ועיין בתשובה שבסוף ספר בל"י שהביא השבו"י וכתב שאין זה מלתא דעל"ג דכל שנטל קבלה אין בודקין אחריו. גם השיב על ראייתו מסנהד' ולכן מסיק בנמצא שאין לו חוש הרגשה לא מהני קבלה מאביו דודאי לא בדק שפיר וצריך להגעיל הכלים וכ"ח עכ"פ ישהו מעל"ע אך אם הב"ד נותנים לו קבלה יכול להיות האב א' מהן ע"ש. ומהפמ"א שהבאתי מבואר דמהני הקבלה שלא להטריף למפרע. ונ"ל להכריע שאם השוחט מומחה לרבים בהרגשה ומוחזק בכשרות טובא מהני הקבל' משא"כ בסתם יש להחמיר והכל לפי ראות עיני המורה:
**** ENGLISH ****
**Until he knows him.** Ba'er Heitev; see responsa Shevut Yaakov who challenged Lechem HaPanim: since an idolater is believed regarding issurim even a relative—from Ketubot 22—for the father is trusted to feed him terumah because it is in his power to feed him. Rambam: when it is known he is his father, he is trusted. See Even HaEzer siman 3; nevertheless he wrote regarding slaughter: since "most found at slaughter," etc.—only later enactments to take kabbalah; also a matter of tel appearance—whether he is expert or not he does not lie; somewhat a proof from Mishnah Sanhedrin 5; nevertheless he should take kabbalah from another since he is not his relative—see there. Ba'er Heitev in the name of Chavat Ya'ir—not this law but the law of permitting a bechor—see there. Peninei Me'orot part 2 wrote: if he is before us and they examined that he knows laws of shechitah—it is obvious he is trusted, also established in presumption; this is only revelation of the matter. Greater than this—it appears even if they examined him and he does not know, kabbalah helps to declare kosher, like one who took kabbalah from another and we say "now he became defective"; where he is not before us and we know he had kabbalah from his father—it is obvious to rely on "most found," etc. Already permission went out from gedolei hador as Lechem HaPanim—see there. See responsa at the end of Sefer Beli—Shevut Yaakov brought and wrote it is not a matter of tel appearance: all who take kabbalah are not examined afterward. He also answered his proof from Sanhedrin; therefore he concludes: when found that he has no sense of feeling—kabbalah from his father does not help, for certainly he did not examine well; must kasher utensils; in any case delay from Torah; but if Beit Din give him kabbalah, the father may be one of them—see there. From Peri Megadim that I brought it is clear kabbalah helps not to declare treif retroactively. It appears to decide: if the slaughterer is expert for the public in feeling and well established in kashrut—kabbalah helps greatly; unlike unspecified—be stringent; all according to the eyes of the instructor.

**"Now he became defective."** See Ba'er Heitev; see Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 91: when found that he does not know laws of shechitah and took kabbalah—one may lighten per Peri Megadim; if he did not take—forbid everything retroactively, and even in safek forbid; and so concludes responsa Beit Yaakov siman 14, etc. Regarding one who pokes who is not expert in poking—it appears to forbid everything retroactively, for they are stringent in poking—see siman 65; see in Beli that his view is there is no distinction between shechitah and poking; see Beit Shmuel and see there siman 52 who wrote regarding a slaughterer who came to anger and slaughtered in anger, then grasped the simanim and went on his way; the second slaughterer showed him the esophagus was not slaughtered; when the first returned and they asked him why he made the animal nevelah, he answered he did not know—from this he believed it was slaughtered properly, and when he grasped the simanim he thought it was the windpipe of the neck and not the esophagus—he ruled a simple matter to dismiss him, since there is no excuse for his words. If he would give an excuse that he knew it was nevelah but did not wish to attribute the deficiency to himself, and his intent was to declare the animal treif through examination of the lung as slaughterers' way—it is not fit to dismiss him. But one who gives no excuse at all—this is complete negligence. And one may say: since he came to anger he came to mistake—this too is somewhat negligence, for "all who are angry," etc.; and in particular as is heard he is not established in kashrut per the versions and bad language—one should be concerned; if enmity is before us until he returns in complete repentance; also he must pay what he made nevelah since they give him wages as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 66 (see below siman 2 seif 12 in gloss printed there in the name of Even HaEzer who brought this responsa briefly, and there is a printing error: it wrote "and when he did not answer" and it should read "and when he came he answered"). (And think regarding what he wrote: if he gives an excuse that he knew but wished to declare treif through examination—if so he is suspected of money, since by law he must pay; and even though for this his slaughter is not invalid as written below seif 4 regarding stolen kids—nevertheless there is no excuse here, for he makes himself wicked with this claim. And it is possible he holds amirah lebarzel in this—that one may say he truly intended to pay, only secretly or to evade until he has money; and in particular since slaughterers were accustomed to do so from the outset with this intent they accepted upon themselves not to need to shame himself that they establish him unable to train his hands to slaughter properly—and still think per what we hold below siman 27: when it became nevelah in slaughter, forbidden to sell lung and intestines—and if so when the slaughterer conceals from the owner that it became nevelah in slaughter and tells him it became treifah, he will sell intestines to idolaters and transgress lifnei iver—and if so behold the slaughterer causes Israel to transgress lifnei iver, and there is no excuse? And they place this matter on the custom slaughterers do thus, as Shevut Yaakov wrote that such is slaughterers' way. One should wonder why sages did not protest—for hereby he causes Israel lifnei iver. It appears therefore they turned a blind eye—for one must be concerned lest because he will be obligated to pay or from embarrassment he will not inform at all that it became nevelah in slaughter; therefore they left this stumbling block under their hands—for better a small issur by them than a great one. **Some say his slaughter is forbidden.** See Peri Chadash; Turei Zahav siman 2 holds one should not forbid slaughter when they did not forbid explicitly; Tiferet Tzvi siman 1: slaughterer who swore not to slaughter without a receipt from the leaseholder of the community and transgressed and slaughtered—do not forbid his slaughter as Peri Chadash wrote; Turei Zahav siman 2 agreed, since the community did not decree slaughter invalid, only regarding the oath alone—for this he is not suspected on slaughter, and one should not suspect him for benefit, for behold he slaughters for others and is kosher to slaughter from now on; also his law is not like one who transgresses an oath, since he excuses himself that he thought there is no objection of the leaseholder when he informs him immediately; and in particular since in truth the butcher had a receipt, only the slaughterer sinned that he was not commanded to see it and take from him—and his intent was to slaughter without receipt—there is no difference, and it is like a transgression without lashes; nevertheless fit for punishment and fine and to dismiss him for some time from descending before the ark, and fine him in money according to his ability, and accept repentance in Beit Din with fasting as fit—but do not disqualify him from slaughter, since by law he is not disqualified and dependents rely on him; what the community does not wish to trust him for the future—the community may forbid what he slaughters without receipt as nevelah, and for this he is not suspected to feed nevelot, and one should not dismiss him, for it appears not in rebellion and over ten—see there. He also wrote there siman 20: slaughterer who swore before Beit Din not to drink wine, and if he transgresses he will not slaughter again—and he transgressed and drank and slaughtered afterward; some permitted the slaughterer when informed he drank only on Motza'ei Yom Kippur and it is considered Yom Tov—and he expressed in his oath Shabbat and Yom Tov; one may say as in Orach Chaim siman 579 that Chanukah and Purim are not Yom Tov; nevertheless if the one who vowed says he intended them—he is believed; likewise Motza'ei Yom Kippur—in derashot of Maharil it is called Yom Tov language—see Orach Chaim in Beit Yosef siman 624; Rama also wrote it is somewhat Yom Tov. But per what Taz wrote he drank also on other days; and according to Rambam he is like apostate requiring knife examination; most poskim disagreed; but what he said he did not forbid himself drinking as all the land, only not to become drunk—not believed, for if so why mention Shabbatot and Yom Tov—and then he wishes to become drunk—it is abomination to be drunk even on Shabbatot and Yom Tov "who gives wine to eyes," etc.; his thought is recognizable that he forbade himself drinking even less than revi'it per Taz—for on wine even less than revi'it one blesses after-blessing, for such is way of drinking; and in vows follow language of Beit Din. But one who slaughtered after Beit Din forbade his slaughter—it is as nevelah and he did not turn to this and slaughtered—he is as one who fed nevelot and is suspected to feed nevelot and not trusted on slaughter; and even though only rabbinic issur as Taz siman 217—nevertheless certainly in slaughter there are issurim and he is suspected on them—who will take such a slaughterer—see there. See Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 5: slaughterer whom community forbade to eat from his slaughter—if written in document forbidden like slaughter of idolater—even if examining knife and giving to him, forbidden to eat from his slaughter; but if written simply his slaughter is forbidden—one may lighten through examining knife and giving to him, as law of apostate slaughter—see there; and see there regarding community decree in cherem not to eat from his slaughter: if the matter is doubtful in the enactment—we go to lighten, for cherem from words of Kabbalah is derabbanan and its safek is lenient (see there he retracted from Noda BiYehudah part 1 Even HaEzer siman 77); therefore if majority of city agreed, and in particular scholars, to declare slaughterers kosher—they have power to declare him kosher if he did not feed treifot to Israel—see there. See Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 58: what is close—that some rabbis enacted in their communities to proclaim all meat brought from a province as if eating treifah and forbid even utensils cooked in that meat—this is not correct, for one should fear enmity; fit for all Israel to be as one man regarding eating and drinking and not separate and make factions; and great urging needed regarding examination of knife and feeling, and to appoint for this kosher known people as siman 18; and so fit for every rabbi and leader in his city and province to supervise. So I practiced in Serbian lands under my jurisdiction—I examined all slaughterers and examiners in laws of Shulchan Aruch and examination and feeling and sharpening knives; urged them very greatly; all struck hands in actual deed to be zealous in examining knives, even if in their settlement one expert slaughterer or teacher to show his knife before him once a week, also not to declare any animal kosher that has a defect until he joins one expert slaughterer or teacher. Also to investigate when buying meat whether that slaughterer is expert and God-fearing and another will eat. What will he do if the slaughterer transgresses in rebellion—the collar hangs on that slaughterer's neck and all Israel are clean and no harm will befall, etc. But to forbid generally meat of people of the province and villages and even utensils—this is certainly great enmity, etc. Also from my fathers and teachers the Geonim I did not hear such—see there. It appears this is in general; but if known in villages and towns around there is concern because they are not expert or light-minded—a blessing on the teacher who comes to fence in his city to raise a stumbling block from the people's way; all this deals only if coming to forbid from concern for zeal in slaughter and examination; but not included is because of meat tax called taks in many places—enmity does not apply and it is straightforward. He wrote in Noda BiYehudah siman 1: enactment by city rabbi with community that proclaimed in synagogue two slaughterers may not slaughter alone without companion; proclaimed explicitly what one slaughters alone is treifah—forbidden to them from kabbalah they accepted; but permitted to people of another city; and even people of that city there is remedy even retroactively; and even though community cherem does not help except for the future as siman 228—nevertheless when asked at the opening and regret uproots retroactively; and even if this is called community opinion as Rama there—nevertheless for a mitzvah matter with their consent all may permit as explained there; and since there is dispute in the city—this is for a mitzvah matter and great is peace—see there. He also wrote there: slaughterers who struck hands to the rabbi not to declare any animal kosher until he places a seal on kosher sign—for butchers are suspected in his eyes; they transgressed the enactment; though fit for punishment to dismiss them from here onward if they do not repent—do not forbid retroactively, for the enactment is not in the matter of slaughter; and even if we say intent is on examination—if so they are suspected on examination—nevertheless if lung was lost, permitted in place of loss of minority, and here even with loss of minority permitted since only examined—they do not believe the slaughterer—from safek it is permitted. Further: in examination, since he only testifies the lung is kosher—even suspected for that matter he is believed regarding others—see there. See She'elot Ya'avetz part 2 siman 196: one who slaughtered without permission and kabbalah from rabbi Av Beit Din of the province; proclaimed in synagogue with rabbi's knowledge and community that his slaughter is forbidden, utensils cooked in that meat forbidden—he did not supervise and slaughtered afterward all he slaughtered after proclamation—meat and utensils forbidden until Beit Din and community return and permit him to slaughter; before that if appointed shatz must remove him—see there (in my responsa Beit Efrayim part 2 in manuscript I wrote regarding a butcher clarified several times sold treifot to city people; also possible he sold milk where fat—for failing in issurim all city people may not lighten to permit all utensils through waiting from Torah, even though in responsa siman 2 I was lenient; nevertheless here majority have forbidden utensils accustomed to cook daily and became forbidden through hot anger which is like cooking issur—only permit through waiting from Torah—this is like making safek derabbanan l'chatchila; see my responsa Beit Efrayim printed Orach Chaim siman 6 and I brought from Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 91; I wrote if owner has many utensils where there is great loss—permit at least those not accustomed to use with heat, only Shabbat—for those utensils transgressed waiting from Torah at birth of safek; there I wrote more and abbreviated here).
