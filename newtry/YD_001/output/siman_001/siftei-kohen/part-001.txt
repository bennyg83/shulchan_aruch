<b>מותר ליתן לו לכתחלה לשחוט. </b>פי' על סמך שיבדקנו אחר השחיטה אם הוא מומחה ועי' בב"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
And slaves and any person—even if we do not recognize him, etc. — The Tur wrote “freed slaves,” etc.; and it appears to me to explain his view: he holds that ordinary slaves have all evil traits more than the empty and reckless, as we say in Shas in many places, and they lack presumption of reliability; therefore the Mishnah that Tosafot and Rosh brought—that slaves slaughter l'chatchila—means freed slaves. For otherwise, even if circumcised and immersed, we do not trust him regarding shechitah unless they know him to be reliable; but when they know him, he is among the majority found at shechitah who are experts and established. So too Rashba in a responsum—and Beit Yosef siman 62 brings that the Rambam’s view (chapter 3 Hilchos Shechitah, and so Semak) is that slaves are among the majority present who are experts. So appears Darchei Moshe seif 10; hence the Mechaber, who omitted the word “freed,” follows his reasoning as he wrote in Beit Yosef: why raise specifically freed slaves—even unfreed ones too, etc. (and so Terumat HaDeshen, short version 6b, that women and slaves who are not freed slaughter l'chatchila like Gedolei Yisrael). Therefore the Mechaber speaks when they recognize those who are reliable, for ordinary slaves are not reliable (except it does not sound to him that the Tur speaks only when they are unknown to us). And the Mechaber did not mention this because he holds that with other people too we require a slaughterer who is trustworthy, per Rambam and Rosh as Beit Yosef brings, as he ruled in Simanei HaChadashim and below end siman 65; therefore he did not need to mention it, for it is obvious that slaves are not superior to other people (see responsa R' Shlomo Kohen part 2 siman 175, who also wrote somewhat on this). The Bach challenged the Mechaber that from Rambam and Semak it appears that with slaves specifically we require that they know they are experts; he also challenged Darchei Moshe—and it appears to me as stated above.

<b>על דבר זה כו'. </b>כלומר אילו בא לפני דין זה הייתי מסתפק בו ושואל:
**** ENGLISH ****
But if he did not take kabbalah, etc. — Darchei Moshe challenged: why is this different from an animal found treifah, whose cheeses made from it are kosher if we can say it is now that it became treifah, because most animals are kosher and we say “establish the animal on its presumption” (as below siman 81)? He answered that one can distinguish: most animals being kosher is a majority for all animals in the world, therefore it is properly called a majority; but here it is only “most found at shechitah are experts,” whereas most people are not experts—therefore this presumption is not called a presumption due to majority. Further, perhaps it is not comparable: there one can say it did not leave life except now, near shechitah it became treifah—unlike a slaughterer. And it appears to me it is not comparable: there one can say from birth it was kosher and now it became treifah; but here, from birth he surely knew nothing—so we would have to say he learned and forgot, which we do not say. Further, since now he does not know, it is evident he never knew, for one does not forget shechitah laws one must be fluent in; but if he received kabbalah, it must be that he forgot—and perhaps this too is included in the Mechaber’s words; with this it is reconciled that we do not challenge from a mikveh below siman 201 seif 71 that all purifications done on impure grounds are retroactive. Nevertheless a question remains: here there is presumption and majority that most found at shechitah are experts, unlike there where there is no majority—or here there is doubt; and one can distinguish in several ways—needs study. Per what I wrote: one who was inspecting and afterward a treifah was found from his hand through negligence—not lack of knowledge—the utensils are kosher by law, and we establish him on his presumption that until now he was reliable; so I wrote in siman 119 seif 30 in the name of the Bach—see there; and Maharshal copied this in chapter 3 seif 16, responsum of Maharam Katz. Regarding utensils his townspeople kashered—to establish him as suspected retroactively—they did not intend well, for we do not establish him as suspected retroactively, etc.; however, if they examined him and he is not learned, certainly all his shechitah should be forbidden retroactively—end of his words. So Beit Yosef brought in Bedek HaBayit siman 119: Avraham Chaviv wrote in the name of Tosefta that even one who apostatized is not suspected retroactively—if he slaughtered and removed forbidden fat and afterward apostatized that day, everything is kosher—end of his words. See below siman 119.

In Darchei Moshe he answered that a deaf person who speaks but does not hear—even if expert—does not slaughter l'chatchila; b'dieved, even when before us, it is as when not before us, for he cannot be examined since he does not hear—and his shechitah is valid even if not expert; therefore we derive nothing there from “expert.” A question arises, for one can examine him through writing, as they examine for gittin and gifts, as Tur wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 235 and Orach Chayyim siman 121.
