ל"א רוב מצויים דמנ"ל להש"ס דלמא ס"ל דל"א רוב מומחים ולא רוב מוחזקים ואסור לשחוט רק בידוע שהוא מומחה ומוחזק בשלמא בלא דברי הראב"ן י"ל דפליגי רק על חד ואמרי דס"ל דל"א רוב מומחים או דל"א רוב מוחזקים אבל לומד דפליגי בתרתי לענין רובא דמומחים ורובא דמוחזקים מה"ת ומש"ה לא הומ"ל דס"ל להאמוראי דבעי' מומחה ומוחזק גם י"ל כיון דבאמת דהי' לה בסוגיא דהתם דפליגי רק במצאן באשפה א"כ כלהו ס"ל דמצאן שחוטים מותרים דאמרי' רוב מומחי' ומוחזקים מש"ה מוקמי' טעמא דאמוראי בהכי דסלקא לפי ההלכה אבל במצינן למרדף י"ל דלא סמכי' לא על רוב מומחים ולא על רובא דמוחזקים. וא"כ מנ"ל להש"ס דאינך אמוראי פליגי לקולא עם רבינא דלמא בהיפוך דפליגי לחומרא דלא ישחוט אא"כ בידוע שהוא מומחה ומוחזק וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Shach s.k. 5.) **Thus wrote Bach**, etc. It appears Ra'avad emerged thus in order to resolve Tosafot's question on Ravina from the baraita of a found chicken; therefore he wrote that Ravina concedes when they are not before us and we cannot pursue after him—it is permitted. Nevertheless, per this it is difficult on that which we say "all" like Ravina, one version, etc.—from where does the Gemara learn that they hold leniently regarding "most found at slaughter are experts" and established? Perhaps conversely they hold only that most found are experts and not established; if so, what does "all" come to include? For Tosafot it fits, since for the first tanna who is lenient regarding "his kids were found"—perforce they hold two matters in the baraita, only Ravina holds like the other tanna who forbids. If so, one may say the tanna who forbids does not hold one majority; but from Torah to say regarding experts and established to forbid, even though it is a majority, and he disagrees with the first tanna in two matters—therefore we say the other Amoraim hold leniently like the first tanna. But for Ra'avad it is difficult—perhaps all hold that l'chatchila and b'dieved, when we can pursue after him, one must know he is expert and established. One may say: **And it appears** further that he comes to answer the above question of Tosafot; therefore he distinguishes: there they are not before us and it is impossible to clarify—unlike here, where we can pursue after him. Even for Ravina, b'dieved when we cannot pursue after him—it is permitted. If so, why do we not give him to slaughter on the basis of examination afterward? If Ravina disagrees with the other Amoraim—for Ravina holds that even if he forgets afterward it is permitted—nevertheless we do not give on that basis. The other Amoraim hold we give on that basis; if so, the Gemara should have said "all" like Ravina, one version—that they hold it is permitted to give on that basis. One must strain that it depends on this: for Ravina the majority is not so strong, and it is permitted only in true b'dieved without being able to pursue after him; therefore the law is that we do not give him l'chatchila on the basis of examination afterward. But the other Amoraim hold "most found" and the majority is strong, and there is no need to pursue after him; therefore it is fit to give l'chatchila on the basis of examination afterward. **However**, per this it is difficult on that which we say "all" like Ravina, one version—"most found"; from where does the Gemara learn—perhaps they hold only most found are experts and not most established, and it is forbidden to slaughter only when it is known he is expert and established? Without the words of Ra'avad, one may say they disagree only on one matter, and they say only most found are experts, or only most found are established. But to say they disagree on both matters—regarding majority of experts and majority of established—from Torah, and therefore it could not be said the Amoraim require expert and established. Also one may say: since in truth it was for them in that sugya that they disagree only regarding "found in the garbage heap," therefore all hold found slaughtered animals are permitted—for we say majority of experts and established; therefore they establish the Amoraim's reason accordingly, that it accords with the halachah. But when we can pursue after him, one may say they do not rely on majority of experts nor on majority of established. If so, from where does the Gemara learn the other Amoraim disagree leniently with Ravina—perhaps conversely they disagree stringently, that he may not slaughter unless it is known he is expert and established—and think.

**** ENGLISH ****
(There.) **Thus it is**, etc. One should examine from where this is—for we say: where it is possible to clarify, we clarify; but regarding examination of the lung, where there is no presumption of issur, one may say there is no need to clarify—see Magen Avraham siman 337 s.k. 2. Also here there are two majorities—majority of animals are kosher and majority of examiners are experts—no need to clarify. Per the words of Magen Avraham, it appears difficult that we must check the esophagus l'chatchila—for there is presumption of issur where it is possible to clarify, we clarify; and behold, regarding "most found at slaughter are experts," Rosh wrote the minority is an uncommon minority; nevertheless, when they are before us, examination is required—if so, the esophagus too should be checked. One must say Chazal required this only if there is a safek whether he performed the act of slaughter properly—for they are concerned perhaps he is not expert and delayed or pressed. But where he certainly performed the act of slaughter properly, only the concern is from elsewhere—that perhaps there was a hole in the esophagus, whereby it becomes invalid in the slaughter; since the animal is in presumption of wholeness and in presumption that if they slaughter it according to all laws of slaughter it will be permitted—in this there is no need to clarify. What must be said per the intent of Shach siman 25 s.k. 2: without checking, regarding dislocated—it is kosher, for a live animal is not in presumption that the simanim were uprooted, and it is not comparable to one who did not see that most were slaughtered—for there a live animal stands in presumption it is not slaughtered—see there. And this is even though regarding dislocated the slaughter does not help and it is also "not slaughtered"; nevertheless this is only if the dislocation is before us and there is safek whether it slipped before slaughter or after. But all cases where we do not see a defect are not called presumption of issur—for for this reason it was in presumption it is not dislocated, and that if they slaughter it according to laws of slaughter it will be permitted. It is different without seeing whether the simanim are mostly slaughtered—for there the safek is perhaps he did not perform the act of slaughter; nevertheless, regarding dislocated they need to check l'chatchila, since it is common, like examination of the lung. But regarding a hole in the esophagus—no need to clarify.

(בא"ד) <b>דהא בגיטין וכו'.</b> אין זה ראיה כ"כ די"ל דכמו דלר"ח הכא עדיף ממקוה דהוי ס"ס ה"נ י"ל אף לדידן דאסרי' בלא שיבא בה עצמות מ"מ לא דמי לגמרי למקוה דאלו התם דנין לודאי דאף ברה"ר טמא וכדאיתא להדיא בריש נדה אבל הכא כיון דאיכא ס"ס אלא דלא מהני ס"ס באתחזק איסורא מ"מ ודאי לא הוי וע' ש"ך (סימן ק"י בדיני ס"ס אות כ"ח) דבג' ספיקות מהני במקום חזקה דהספק הראשון הוי כודאי ושוב מהני ס"ס א"כ בס"ס הוי כמו ספק א' בגופו של איסור ואסור רק מספק ולא הוי ודאי. ואפשר לומר עוד דאסור דק מדרבנן דס"ס הוי כמו רוב ומדאורייתא מהני גם במקום חזקה אלא דמדרבנן אסור דהוי כמו סמוך מיעוטא לחזקה א"כ י"ל דמש"ה במקוה הטהרות אסורים כיון דאנו דנין לודאי טמא ואף לר"ש דס"ל גבי מקוה דהוי רק ספק הא לדידיה ממילא דק ברה"י טמא מש"ה לא מהני חזקה דטהרות כמו בכל ספק טומאה ברה"י דלא מהני חזקה אבל הכא דהוי רק ספק י"ל דהכלים מותרים משום חזקת היתר אולם מסברא נראה דלא שייך בכלי חזקת היתר דחרסי הכלי עדיין מותרים ואנו דנין על הבליעה משחיטה זו שבא לתוכם ועל הבליעה לא היה מעולם חזקת היתר ואך הכלים אם הם אין ב"י ובפרט אם לא נודע הריעותא עד אחר שנעשו הכלים אב"י יש לצדד להקל ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
(In the response.) **For in Gittin**, etc. This is not such a proof—for one may say: just as for R' Chanina here it is preferable to mikveh, which is safek, likewise one may say even for us, who forbid without bones having entered; nevertheless it is not fully comparable to mikveh—for there we judge it certainly impure even in the public domain, as explicit at the beginning of Niddah. But here, since there is only safek—and safek does not help against presumption of issur—nevertheless it is certainly not vadai. See Shach siman 110, laws of safek safeka, letter 28: in three doubts it helps where there is presumption—the first doubt is like vadai, and again safek safeka helps; if so, in safek safeka it is like one doubt in the body of the issur, forbidden only from doubt and not vadai. It is also possible to say further: the thin stringency is derabbanan—for safek is like majority, and min haTorah it helps even where there is presumption; only derabbanan it is forbidden, as minority against presumption. If so, one may say: therefore in mikveh the purities are forbidden, since we judge it certainly impure; and even for R' Shimon, who holds regarding mikveh it is only safek—for him automatically the thin stringency of public domain impure applies; therefore presumption of purity does not help, like in all safek tumah in private domain presumption does not help. But here, where it is only safek, one may say the vessels are permitted because of presumption of heter. However, from reasoning it appears presumption of heter does not apply to a vessel—for the earthenware of the vessel is still permitted, and we judge regarding the absorption from this slaughter that entered them; regarding the absorption there was never presumption of heter. Only regarding vessels that are not earthenware—and in particular if the defect was not known until after the vessels were made as forbidden vessels—there is room to lean lenient—and think.

(In the response.) **Or here it is safek safeka.** Meaning: even though Taz contradicted this—for we hold like R' Chanina, who forbids even safek safeka—nevertheless one may say the reason we do not call it safek safeka is that typically the knife goes through the whole animal. And likewise explicitly in Nekudat HaKesef. However, in my humble opinion this is hidden—for according to Tosafot's answer "the knife became defective, the animal did not become defective," it is safek; if so, there is no distinction between a defect in the knife or in the animal, only the distinction is from safek safeka. If so, if we hold we do not call it safek safeka—for us, who hold like R' Chanina regarding "he broke bones in it," the Gemara's question from tevel remains—for there it dealt with the same species, like "he broke bones in it," because it is safek with presumption of issur, half and half, like minority against presumption. Therefore we can be lenient regarding "he broke bones in it"—presumption of issur is removed because of safek safeka, and we rely on what is found in the spine. But regarding tevel, where there is no safek safeka, presumption of issur stands in its force. Perhaps one may say: although according to Tosafot's answer in explaining the sugya it is proven that we also establish it as safek, nevertheless here they did not use that answer; rather the intent of the sugya in its plain meaning is that there is a distinction between a defect in a matter that has presumption, or a defect elsewhere. As appears clearly—for according to that answer, where there is no distinction except from safek safeka, automatically also regarding "he broke bones in it" the permission is only from safek safeka; if so, where the knife is small, only as much as fills the neck—there is no safek safeka, and it should be forbidden—and we do not find this distinction in the law, although we do not hold like that answer of Tosafot. Nevertheless, on their question—that even a defective knife should be forbidden, like a mikveh that was measured—they distinguish: here it is safek safeka, and we truly hold it is not safek safeka, and we forbid truly without "he broke bones in it," like a mikveh that was measured. But regarding "he broke bones in it" they hold to be lenient without safek safeka, unlike a mikveh that was measured—for since "he broke bones in it," tevel too is not comparable, since the animal did not become defective. Still difficult: if we say for R' Chanina it is a great safek safeka, and the change of "the knife became defective" is not from the reason of safek safeka, but he distinguishes between a defect in a place of presumption or not—the Gemara itself should be difficult: why does the Gemara not answer plainly to distinguish from the law of tevel, that here it is safek safeka, except we say that truly also for R' Chanina it is not a great safek safeka, and the distinction between knife and measured mikveh is truly the first answer of Tosafot—since there is no lack in it, or because the spine certainly became defective; and we hold like R' Huna without "he broke bones in it." Nevertheless, only where there is no safek safeka; but where there is safek safeka, they are lenient.

(Seif 3.) **And even if he said "it is clear to me."** Rashba proved this from that they do not establish the Amoraim's Mishnah thus: "all slaughter"—unspecified people; but when it is known he is not expert, he may not slaughter; and if he slaughtered and said "it is clear to me," his slaughter is kosher—"it is clear to me" does not help—see there. Even though Rashba holds like the Geonim, and that which they discuss from Ravina is because l'chatchila it is permitted to give on the basis of examination afterward—and this is not implied in the language "all slaughter," as Rosh wrote. If so, we should not establish "all slaughter" as unspecified—for it is not permitted except on the basis of examination. Nevertheless, it appears to me that certainly for Ravina, who establishes "all slaughter" as established even if they are not experts—for regarding experts they give without the basis of examination, whether all are experts or a minority of a minority—for whatever reason, since for Ravina it is permitted l'chatchila entirely, automatically for the other Amoraim who are more lenient it is likewise. What we rule—that they give only on the basis of examination—is because for Ravina, since Ravina holds that it must be known he is expert, one may say even the other lenient Amoraim hold that at least the basis of examination is required—not to make a distant dispute.
