(סימן א' בט"ז בפתיחה) <b>וזבחת כאשר צויתיך מלמד שנצטוה משה כו'.</b> הרמב"ם בס' היד ריש הלכות שחיטה מנה שחיטה למצות עשה. וכ"ה בתוס' ביצה (דף כה) ובחולין (דף לז) דוזבחת הוא עשה. והראב"ד בהשגותיו כתב שאין לו טעם והכ"מ ביאר טעמו של הראב"ד משום שאין זה מצוה מחוייבת שאם ירצה שלא לאכול בשר או שלא לשחוט בעצמו רשאי אלא הוא לאו הבא מכלל עשה שתאכל שחוטה ולא אבר מ"ה ונבילה. ולדעת הרמב"ם תירץ הכ"מ דמ"מ הוא עשה למי שירצה לאכול בשר שישחוט או בעצמו או שאחר ישחוט לו. ותמוה שסותר הכ"מ דברי עצמו שכ' פי"א מהל' ברכו' דנטילת ידים ושחיטה הוו דבר הרשות דאפשר לו שלא לאכול. ולי נראה דטעמו של הרמב"ם והתוס' דחשבו שחיטה למ"ע. הוא מטעם שכ' הרא"ש בריש כתובות דמש"ה מברך על השחיטה וצונו אף שאינו מחוייב לשחוט ולאכול. דהא אפקי' קרא בלשון צווי דכתיב וזבחת ואכלת. וכן משמע דקדוק ל' הרמב"ם שם הלכה ד'. ועל כל הדברים האלה צוונו בתורה ואמר וזבחת כאשר צויתיך. משמע דמשום שאמר בלשון צווי קאמר דהוי מ"ע. וכ"ה גם לפ"ד התוס' שם.<br><b>ועיין</b> בפמ"ג בפתיחה דעשה שאינו זבוח הוי בכל שהוא. ולא משמע הכי בתוס' חולין (דף לג).
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 1, Taz in introduction.) **"And you shall slaughter as I commanded you"—teaching that Moses was commanded**, etc. Rambam in Yad, beginning of Hilchot Shechitah, counts shechitah as a positive mitzvah; and likewise Tosafot Beitzah 25 and Chullin 37—that "and you shall slaughter" is a positive commandment. Raavad in his hasagot wrote it has no reason; Maggid Mishneh explained Raavad's reason: it is not an obligatory mitzvah—for if he wishes not to eat meat or not to slaughter himself he is permitted; rather it is a lav haba michlal aseh that you eat slaughtered, not a limb from a live animal and nevelah. According to Rambam, Maggid Mishneh answered: nevertheless it is a positive commandment for one who wishes to eat meat—that he slaughter, whether himself or another slaughter for him. It is wondrous that Maggid Mishneh contradicts his own words, for he wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchot Berachot that netilat yadayim and shechitah are optional matters, for it is possible for him not to eat. It appears to me that the reason of Rambam and Tosafot who counted shechitah as a positive mitzvah is the reason Rosh wrote at the beginning of Ketubot: therefore one blesses on slaughter and "He commanded us" even though he is not obligated to slaughter and eat—for they expounded the verse in language of command, as it is written "and you shall slaughter and eat"; and likewise the precision of Rambam's language there, law 4: "on all these matters we were commanded in the Torah, and He said 'and you shall slaughter as I commanded you'"—implies that because He said it in language of command it is a positive mitzvah; and likewise according to Tosafot there. See Peri Megadim in the introduction: a positive commandment that is not slaughtered applies in any amount; this does not appear so in Tosafot Chullin 33.

(In Shach s.k. 27.) **That a minor is commanded regarding shechitah**, etc.—meaning he is called bar zevicha, and it is forbidden to feed him by hand, and we are commanded not to give him nevelot; as Peri Megadim wrote and likewise in responsa of Maharil—and this is if we say forbidden to feed him by hand from Torah; because of derabbanan he is not called bar zevicha, just as he is not called bar keshira regarding tefillin because chinuch is derabbanan. One may bring proof from what Tosafot wrote in Avodah Zarah 28—that he is called bar brit because we are commanded to circumcise him; otherwise it should be difficult: perhaps a minor is among them, as Tosafot wrote regarding apostate for orlah. And likewise one may say deaf-mute and shotah are ben brit—for otherwise let us say behold there is among them. What Peri Megadim wrote here that they are warned and sons of mitzvot is against the sugya at the beginning of Mi sheHechshich; and Rambam chapter 9 of testimony and beginning of Yevamot that Beit Din is not commanded to separate them; and likewise Magen Avraham siman 489—and it implies there they are worse than minors with da'at. Know that from Tosafot Avodah Zarah it is proven that even an apostate lehachis is ben brit; and likewise in Ahavat Chesed Even HaEzer siman 109, not like Magen Avraham there; and likewise from the sugya in chapter 2 of Kiddushin; and likewise in responsa Ginat Veradim siman 23; and from this is proof for what Taz wrote at the beginning of siman 2 that he is bar zevicha since he is commanded even though he does not perform. What Magen Avraham proved from what Tosafot wrote in Sanhedrin—one may say there he speaks in the language of ben brit. See Beit Shmuel siman 123 who wrote apostate applies to Torah of gittin; and in siman 141 s.k. 47—not so.

**** ENGLISH ****
(In Shach s.k. 29.) **That blessing does not impede b'dieved**, etc. In Ba'er Heitev at the beginning of Chullin another reason appears: regarding all blessings one fulfills b'dieved if he did not make it heard to the ear, only he blessed in his heart—and likewise here he fulfills even though he does not hear—see there. It appears there is a practical difference between the two reasons according to Geonim and Ra'avyah, as Mordekhai wrote in chapter 25: if he acted intentionally and did not bless, one should fine him; if so, per this view, if a deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear slaughtered intentionally—it is forbidden according to us; but according to Rosh's reason it is kosher even according to us; and so is the view of Taz who wrote "and if he slaughtered, his slaughter is kosher"—this language is for everyone, as we say in the Gemara beginning of Chullin 15 regarding one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Kippur—and think. See Shulchan Aruch siman 6 who proved from Tur's words regarding one who became mute: even where he is not fit to bless, even hirhur helps to hear from another—and we say "one who hears is as one who answers"; and likewise regarding naked— forbidden, sheomea keoneh; and think—if so a mute should perform chalitzah and hear the reading from another, for it is keoneh and speech.
