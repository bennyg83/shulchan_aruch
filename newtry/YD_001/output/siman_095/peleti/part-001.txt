When they were cooked — Mechaber and Rama brought the view of those who say whether they came up specifically or even when they were cooked; and I found support for those who say specifically they came up, for in the Gemara at the end of Avodah Zarah we say every day purging is made from one to the other, and the Gemara challenges: sin-offering and peace offerings — this is eaten in one day and that in two days, etc. And it answers: sin-offering slaughtered today and again they cook in it peace offerings slaughtered yesterday that completed their times together, and again they cook peace offerings slaughtered today when the time ended, etc. And one must understand what the Gemara sought here in cooking two types of peace offerings — yesterday's and today's — and it did not say immediately after cooking the sin-offering to cook today's slaughtered peace offerings; and why do I need cooking yesterday's peace offerings? However according to what the Tosafot supplement for one who says when cooked it is forbidden — one must say there regarding a sacred vessel we establish it on a Torah basis to nullify species in its species in the majority, and this is certainly to rely on the majority — there is some hesitation until they said regarding something that has a permissible aspect — therefore it is not nullified: why should you rely on the majority — wait until it is permitted; if so, in sin-offering against peace offerings slaughtered on the same day there are two prohibitions here — one prohibition of non-priests for peace offerings, and second prohibition of leftover from tomorrow's absorption of sin-offering leftover; if so when tomorrow we eat peace offerings there is in it absorption of two prohibitions — prohibition of non-priests and prohibition of leftover — and in both one must rely on nullification in the majority; and as much as we can minimize that one should not eat prohibition through relying on the majority we do, as in that which was stated above; therefore he cooks first yesterday's peace offerings that must be eaten on that day — if so there is no absorption of prohibition except from prohibition to non-priests, and on this he relies on nullification in the majority; and when afterward he cooks today's slaughtered peace offerings there is in them absorption only from leftover, for absorption of prohibition to non-priests from the sin-offering was already nullified in the first peace offerings and it went away since prohibition returned to being permitted; and it is also third noten taam bar noten taam and is not considered, and there is only prohibition of leftover and he relies on the majority; if so, never does one eat a piece of kodshim that has absorption of two prohibitions, unlike if immediately he cooked peace offerings whose time is until tomorrow — there would be in it absorption of two prohibitions together and to rely on both through nullification in the majority — all this they did not want to be lenient, and this is clear. And from this it is proven that when cooked it is forbidden, for if permitted there is no nullification of leftover at all, for it is noten taam bar noten taam of heter as Tosafot wrote; and certainly according to Rambam that rinsing was immediately before it became leftover — if so the Gemara that challenges heter that comes to prohibition means even though now it is permitted, nevertheless ultimately it becomes prohibition; if so according to this view even noten taam bar noten taam of heter is not called, as regarding chametz on Pesach according to many authors — and this is permitted; but according to the conclusion that it is heter that when its prohibition time arrives it no longer exists in substance — even this is noten taam bar noten taam of heter, and it is simple; but apart from Rambam and his supporters the matters are clear as stated. And behold according to those who say when roasted it is also permitted — and that which we say an oven that he smeared with its tail requires heating because of grease on top — at first glance one may challenge: why does the Gemara challenge, and now that Rava was answered, etc., why pots on Pesach, and likewise in the Temple break — and what is the difficulty, perhaps in any case it does not emerge through kedei kelipah even through libun, and here on Pesach and in the Temple where it is prohibition of prohibition it is forbidden, but there in the oven the absorbed matter in any case does not forbid, for it is noten taam bar noten taam — only what is in substance on top of the oven; and this is certainly that it burns in the burning of the oven and its libun; and one must say the challenge is to Rav who holds noten taam bar noten taam is forbidden — if so necessarily libun of the oven extracts the absorption too, and he challenged well why break. And with this one may resolve Avi Ezra that Rosh brought in chapter 2 of Pesachim that in an oven and pot they do not make like their bonfire; and Rosh challenged: behold the Gemara is arranged that it helps only that they do not make like their bonfire in Jerusalem — and according to what I said it works out that he holds when we need to strain between heating it from inside and from outside, all is for Rav who holds noten taam bar noten taam is forbidden; but according to what we hold noten taam bar noten taam is permitted, we say forever that renewed kashering for kedei kelipah does not help, and heating the oven is only to burn the plaster on top, and it is simple, requires study. And on the contrary there is some proof in the Gemara: it left the refutation of this matter of Rava that the oven is forbidden, and afterward Rava challenged and answered one distinction between metals and earthenware; and it is difficult — if so how did Rava remove the refutation; and according to what I said it works out, for we hold noten taam bar noten taam is permitted — if so the prohibition is because of plaster on top, and in this there is no difference between metals and earthenware, requires study.

It appears to me that if they placed, etc. — see Shach what Peri Chadash wrote, and he is correct; and in truth it was not hidden from Shach's words the view of Orach Chaim HaArukh that water for washing that they place ash in it — hagalah does not help. However nevertheless he challenged well: just as for R' Eliezer one must say they purged twice, so too for R' Tam one may say they first purged in water with ash to spoil the absorbed matter, and afterward purged in hot water — for the absorbed matter already became spoiled and the water will not become nat bar lichtmile. But certainly if we say what Tosafot wrote in the challenge, and we say the Torah forbade only a pot of that day and did not challenge without qualification whether the Torah forbade — we learn it is forbidden in any case — their intent one may say: what is the challenge from hagalah — behold noten taam lefegem in general is permitted, only in purgings by law of scriptural decree to forbid; if so how would it become nevelah — therefore Tosafot wrote this explanation is not because of scriptural decree, for according to one who holds noten taam lefegem is permitted they truly did not purge except ben yomo, and if so they challenged well; if so there is no place for Shach's challenge who challenged Shach's explanation that they purged in water with ash, and challenged according to one who holds nat bar lichtmile is forbidden how they purged — one need not say that according to one who forbids he holds like one who holds it can be squeezed out and permitted, for in the Gemara in Avodah Zarah for Rav we are uncertain whether nat bar lichtmile is forbidden and the halachah is permitted, or Rav holds nat bar lichtmile is permitted.
