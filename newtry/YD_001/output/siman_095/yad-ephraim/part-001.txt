<b>ואם עירה מכ"ר כו'.</b> כ"ש שהוא תמוה דהא עירוי אינו אוסר יותר מכ"ק ובמקום שא"א לקלפו שרי והיינו אפילו היה שם שומן ומכ"ש בזה שהטעם שבמים אינו רק נ"ט בר נ"ט והביא שם דברי האו"ה שער ל"ד ודברי הת"ח כלל נ"ז ונ"ח והביא הגהת ש"ד כלל נ"ז מעשה היה במגנציא כו' דמשמע כהרמ"א ודחה זה דמאן לימא לן דבעירוי מיירי כו' והאריך וכתב לבסוף שהב"י ות"ח לא הביאו הגהת ש"ד זו והעלה הלכה למעשה דבמקום הפסד קצת אין לאסור אם עירו מכלי של חלב ב"י או איפכא מים שהיס"ב אפילו אם הכלי שעירו עליו מלוכלך עכ"ד ונראה שכן הוא ג"כ דעת הש"ך ובחידושי הבאתי טעם הט"ז דחושבין קלוח העירוי כאלו הוא בכלי שמערין ממנו והמנחת יעקב הקשה ע"ז דהא באיסור לא אמרי' ניצוק חיבור לענין דיעבד כמ"ש בכלל כ"ג וכתבתי דבכלי מתכות דאפשר בהגעלה הוי כלכתחילה כדמשמע בש"ך סימן ק"ג ושם כתבתי דאפשר דמאן דס"ל נצלו מותר מודה בהא דדוקא המאכל דאין לו תקנה הוי דיעבד משא"כ הכלי ובתשובת משאת בנימין סי' כ' כתב דאין טעם לחלק בין מאכל לכלי וכן בפ"מ סי' זה ס"ק ה' כתב ג"כ ולא ידענא מאין הרגלים שהמאכל מותר והכלי אסור הלא כל עצמו של איסור זה כמ"ש הט"ז כו' ולפי מ"ש ניחא וכן מבואר בש"ך סי' צ"ד ס"ק ל"ד ובארתו דברי הש"ך שם שכתב שאין להתיר הכלי מטעם המרדכי דשמנונית פוגם בדבש כו' ואמנם כתבתי שם דלפ"ז היה מקום להתיר בכ"ח שאין לו תקנה בהגעלה ובש"ך סי' צ"ד ס"ק ט"ו משמע דאפילו בכ"ח בעינן ג' נ"ט ע"ש ובסי' ק"ג בנה"ך וקצרתי כאן ונלע"ד שבכלי חרס חשוב כגון פירצלא"י יש להתיר אחר שהיית מעל"ע כיון שיש הפסד קצת וכמאכל דמי אבל בכלי דאפשר בהגעלה יגעיל ומה בכך ועי' במ"י כלל ל"ג לענין כלי שני:

And if one pours from a kli rishon, etc. — all the more so this is astonishing, for pouring does not forbid more than kedei kelipah, and in a place where it cannot be peeled it is permitted; and this is even if there was fat, and all the more so here where the taste in the water is only noten taam bar noten taam; and he brought there Issur VeHeter chapter 34 and Turei Chayim general rules 57 and 58 and Hagahot ShaDa general rule 57 — an incident occurred in Mainz, etc., that it appears like Rama, and he rejected this — who will tell us that in pouring he deals with, etc., and he expanded and wrote at the end that Beit Yosef and Turei Chayim did not bring this Hagahot ShaDa, and he raised practical halachah that in a place of slight loss one need not forbid if they poured from a ben yomo dairy vessel or the reverse with water to which yad soledes bo applied, even if the vessel onto which they poured was soiled — so too; and it appears that so is also Shach's view; and in my innovations I brought Taz's reason that they consider the splash of pouring as if it were in the vessel from which he pours; and Minchat Yaakov challenged this, for in prohibition we do not say connected flow for b'dieved, as he wrote in general rule 23; and I wrote that in metal vessels where hagalah is possible it is like l'chatchila, as it appears in Shach siman 103; and there I wrote that perhaps one who holds roasted is permitted agrees in this that specifically the food that has no remedy is b'dieved, unlike the vessel; and in responsum of Masat Binyamin siman 20 he wrote there is no reason to distinguish between food and vessel, and so in practical halachah this siman s.k. 5 he also wrote, and we do not know from where the custom that food is permitted and vessel forbidden — behold the entire essence of this prohibition is as Taz wrote, etc.; and according to what he wrote it works out, and so explained in Shach siman 94 s.k. 34 and he explained Shach's words there who wrote one may not permit the vessel for the reason of Mordechai that grease spoils in honey, etc.; and however I wrote there that accordingly there was room to permit in every case that has no remedy through hagalah; and in Shach siman 94 s.k. 15 it appears that even in every case we require three noten taam, see there; and in siman 103 in Nekudot HaKesef I shortened here; and it appears to me for practical halachah that in earthenware considered like porcelain there is room to permit after it was within twenty-four hours since there is slight loss and it is like food; but in a vessel where hagalah is possible — one purges and what of it; and see in Maharam in Lublin general rule 33 regarding kli sheni.

But if one pours boiling water. He wrote in responsum of Pri Megadim manuscript that they asked: if they poured boiling water from a dairy vessel onto hot meat — what is its law; and he wrote that even though in the law above that pouring onto a meat vessel — one may say the water was not forbidden since pouring forbids only kedei kelipah and it cannot be peeled — nevertheless in this case it would be forbidden since the meat is hot, if so the lower prevails and the water becomes forbidden and returns and is absorbed in the meat; but one may challenge: who will tell us that the vessel from which he pours imparts taste at the time of pouring to the water poured outside the vessel — one may say the taste applies that the poskim wrote regarding vessels that were rinsed in a dairy cauldron, etc., that both tastes emerge at once to the water, etc.; and if we come to forbid in pouring one must say the vessel imparts taste in the water poured at the time of pouring, and then taste emerges from the meat too and the tastes mix and the water becomes forbidden — and where do we have this from; and he brought proof from Maggid Mishneh in name of Maharam that Beit Yosef brought end of seif 8 in poultry slaughtered by nikur in a dairy bowl, etc., and explains that what he wrote: but if they poured the water onto the poultry — meaning they poured onto it from a dairy kli rishon, and proves from this that tastes emerge at once, for otherwise it would be noten taam bar noten taam; and he holds that forbidden water requires sixty against the peel; and if so even though here we do not establish so and it is reasonable to us that the water is not forbidden because of pouring since peel does not apply in water — nevertheless in pouring onto hot meat the lower prevails and imparts taste throughout — one may say it is forbidden until there is sixty against the water poured onto the meat, for we estimate throughout we do not know how much it absorbed — so too; and this is a novel matter to say in pouring that the taste emerging with the water strikes the taste emitted from the meat and they forbid one another and the water becomes forbidden; for in pouring from a meat vessel onto a dairy vessel we do not say so, and what he wrote that there the water is not forbidden since emission from the vessel through pouring forbids only kedei kelipah and peel does not apply in water — behold the crux in the poskim who permit did not come for this reason, as explained in Issur VeHeter chapter 34 who wrote that taste of a dairy cauldron is not considered taste b'dieved — it is no worse than if they placed in pewter, etc., and explained that because the water is noten taam bar noten taam they are permitted even with meat and he was not concerned for meeting of tastes regarding pouring; and so explained in Shach and other poskim; and also what he explains Maharam's words in Mordechai — meaning they poured from a dairy kli rishon — is explained from Shulchan Aruch siman 68 and those who came after who explain as its plain meaning that the poultry was placed first in a dairy pan and they poured water from a kli rishon onto the poultry into the pan; and the Gaon, and these are his words, also explains Shulchan Aruch's words on this basis that they poured from a dairy kli rishon, and he carried on in two views that Shulchan Aruch wrote on this; and see what he wrote in my Yad Ephraim on Orach Chaim siman 467 in Shulchan Aruch's words; and I found in Sefer Tiferet Lemoshe who also separated as the Gaon wrote — however in my view it is a great forced explanation, and I toiled and found the source of Maggid Mishneh's words taken from the small Tashbetz that Maharam's student composed, and these are his words: there in siman 34 it is stated — poultry slaughtered by nikur in a bowl (dairy) is permitted even if it is ben yomo, for it is a kli sheni and does not cook; and he wrote a gloss on this (and it is Maharaf's gloss as explained in Kol Bo in copying laws from Tashbetz), investigate: when they poured the water in the vessel first and afterward placed the poultry in it; but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first and afterward poured water onto the poultry — if the water is boiling they forbid the poultry the measure of a peel, end of his words; and also what he wrote there in Hagahot Maimoniyot: and one may not return the poultry, etc., is also from Tashbetz there; and also what he wrote there an incident with udder and meat, etc., is also from Tashbetz there siman 141; and according to the plain meaning it appears that what he wrote: but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first — means that vessel, namely the dairy bowl he spoke of at first; and what he wrote: if the water is boiling they forbid the poultry — meaning they emit from the vessel and absorb in the poultry kedei kelipah, and then the second noten taam is from the vessel to the poultry without intermediary of water, since the poultry is on the edge of the bowl literally, and it is a second noten taam in prohibition; and according to the Gaon's words one must explain what he wrote: but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first — does not refer to the dairy bowl above but to a vessel in general, and afterward poured water — meaning water from a dairy vessel; and accordingly the language boiling water forbids — means the water itself that becomes forbidden because of meeting tastes of pouring water and emission of poultry together; and proven is his law in the Gaon that meeting of tastes also applies in pouring; but in my view it is forced to explain thus in Tashbetz's gloss, see there who wrote that even according to his explanation one may say Maharam holds when cooked it is forbidden, therefore they forbid pouring water well — unlike for us who establish when cooked it is permitted; and he again wrote to prove that even roasted Maharam holds permitted as explained in responsum of Maharam Bava Batra siman 213 in honey cooked in a pan, etc., who concludes there and further he would permit because noten taam bar noten taam; and even though in Mordechai he wrote the responsum in name of R' Yehudah in a responsum — he only mentioned they asked according to R' Yehudah, and perhaps that one and his student who arranged the responsum wrote this, and perhaps in Mordechai there is a scribal error from R' Yehudah to R' Yitzchak; and one must say according to R' Yehudah; and if so, since Rabeinu Yehudah permits roasted, all the more when cooked; and until here what forbids in that case of poultry slaughtered by nikur is because of meeting of tastes — until here his holy words; and in my innovations Mordechai's words and ben yomo and Rashal and Minchat Kohen in that incident of honey, etc., and there I wrote that this responsum of Maharam that they asked according to R' Yehudah refers to R' Yehudah as the written questions there siman 214, 215, 216 see there — Rabbi, and he is R' Yehudah, as explained in ShaDa siman 482 that R' Yehudah answered that Rabbenu Tam was particular, etc.; and if so, as it appears the Mordechai did not have before him the conclusion of the responsum that even for improvement he would permit because of noten taam bar noten taam; and from this proof for Beit Yosef in R' Yehudah's view; and I expanded on this; and regarding practical halachah it appears to me in that law that pouring from a clean dairy vessel onto hot meat — one who is lenient in a place of need has not lost; but without that one should not be lenient, since even in pouring onto a ben yomo meat vessel some are stringent; and in truth there is contradiction in Issur VeHeter chapter 34 from seif 15 to seif 23, and the Gaon in responsum above stood on this and wrote to resolve what he wrote in seif 23 that the vessel in which they rinse becomes forbidden even b'dieved, etc., by way of if you say — even if we hold like R' Yehudah who requires sixty against the peel and the vessel became forbidden, see there; and one should investigate much on this, and here I must shorten.

Be'er Heitev; and see responsum of Chavot Tzvi siman 101 whose view is to rule practical halachah like Shulchan Aruch, and therefore he ruled also regarding lye to permit, see there who wrote on Shach's challenge from Tosafot who challenged in the large cauldrons how they acted when they could purge in water with ash — he answered this explanation is from the law of hagalah it comes and applies, and not laws of spoilage — that winnowing shovel fit for winnowing, etc., emerges, see there. And it appears Shach also agrees that the verse speaks of the law of hagalah, only he is difficult on Tosafot who challenged from the large cauldrons — on this one may say they too were included in the law of hagalah to emit the prohibition inside; only they were concerned the water would return and become forbidden — for this they would fix through ash; and nevertheless the essence of emission of prohibition was helped by this hagalah in all the vessels, see there that hagalah for them that they cooked in them something that spoils well — one may say the verse speaks of the fix of hagalah that helps for most vessels which is a better fix for them than placing in vessels spoiled taste that would spoil the dish the Israelite would cook afterward. And also in large cauldrons one may say the intent is not that it remains so, but that afterward they return and purge in water alone so spoiled taste will emit — as Maharam in Lublin wrote and in Korban Netanel to answer Shach — that which we establish Orach Chaim siman 451 that one may not purge in water with ash, see there;
