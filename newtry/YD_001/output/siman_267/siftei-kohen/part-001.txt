**** ENGLISH ****

According to them, regarding a convert whose slaves preceded him and immersed before him, if they immersed before their master converted, they acquire freedom even without specifying their intent. Semag, negative commandment 117, writes likewise, as does Rashba in the responsum cited by Beit Yosef at the end of page 311.

<b>This fine is not collected, etc.</b> Rosh questions what connection this has to fines that are not collected in Babylonia. Those are Torah-imposed fines that require ordained expert judges, who were not present in Babylonia. But if the Sages instituted a fine as a safeguard for the Torah, why should it not be collected in Babylonia? The Tur cites this question.

<b>אם מסרו כו'. </b>לדעת רש"י והטור ה"ה אם לא הכניסו לרשות העובד כוכבים ולוה עליו על תנאי לכשיבא אותו זמן שימשכנו ליקח העבד ועבר הזמן ולא נתן לו המעות אע"פ שלא משכנו בו והוא עדיין ברשותו קונסין אותו ויצא לחירות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he delivered him, etc.</b> According to Rashi and the Tur, the same applies if the master did not place the slave in the non-Jew's possession but borrowed money against him on condition that, when the due date arrived, the creditor could seize the slave. If the time passed and the master did not pay, he is penalized and the slave goes free, even if the creditor did not actually seize him and he remains in the master's possession.
