(סימן ס"ט סעיף א') <b>אם אחר שהדיח חתך כל נתח לב' או לג' צריך להיות ולהדיחם. ואם לא עשה כן הוי כלא הודח.</b> הטעם במרדכי דאגב דוחקא דסכינא מפליט דם בעין. ולטעמי' אזיל שהוא סובר דטעם הדחה קמייתא משום דם בעין שעל פניו שהמלח נתמלא ממנו ואינו מוציא דם. ולפ"ז נראה דדי בהדחה היטב בלא שרייה שהרי טעם השרייה הוא כדי שיתרכך הבשר והרי כבר נתרכך. וא"צ אלא להעביר הדם בעין אותו מקום שחתך. והשתא אתי שפיר דלא תקשי אהדדי מ"ש הג"ה בס"ס זה סעיף כ' דמותר לחתוך הבשר בתוך שיעור מליחה אם רוצה להדיחו שם ולחזור ולמלחו שם ע"ש ודו"ק. ועיין מש"ש. וכ"מ בס' לחם הפנים ובל"י. ואם נחתך רק מצד א' מסתמא יש ס' כמש"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 69 seif 1) If after rinsing he cut each piece into two or three, he must rinse them; and if he did not do so it is as if he did not rinse. The reason in Mordechai: through knife pressure it expels visible blood. According to my reasoning he holds the reason for initial rinsing is visible blood on the surface, that salt becomes full from it and does not expel blood; therefore it seems sufficient with thorough rinsing without soaking, for the meat is already softened, and one need only remove the visible blood from the place he cut. Now it is clear there is no contradiction with what the hagahah wrote at the end of this siman seif 20, that one may cut during the salting period if he wishes to rinse there and return to salt there — see there and examine. And so too in Lechem HaPanim and Bedek HaBayit. And if he cut only from one side, presumably there is sixty as I wrote.

(סעיף ב) <b>אם מלח בלא הדחה.</b> חחיכת בשר תפל שלא הודחה שנוגעת לחתיכה מלוחה ה"ל דין נמלח בלא הדחה אותה חתיכה טפילה שהמלח מבליע בתוכה הדם בעין שעליה. אבל בשר תפל שלא הודח שנפל לציר אין הציר מבליע הדם בעין שעל פניה ושרי. זה יש ללמוד ממ"ש הש"ך סי' ע' ס"ק כ"ט ע"ש. ולא דמי לחתיכה תפילה שנפלה בין חתיכות מלוחות בכלי שאינו מנוקב דמותרת. שאני התם שאין הטפל פולט ע"י נגיעתו למלוח ואין הדם שבה פירש ממקום למקום. עמ"ש בס"ד בסי' ע'. מיהו במ"י כלל ו' ס"ק ד' משמע שפ' כאו"ה דשרי גם בנתערבה בלא הדחה בחתיכות מלוחות. ועכ"פ כשאינה נוגעת אלא מצד א' שרי כמש"ל: <b>כתב</b> בד"מ אם נכבש הבשר במים מע"ל קודם הדחה אחרונה ה"ל כמבושל בלא הדחה אחרונה דצריך ס' נגד דם ומלח שעליו כמ"ש ס"ט. ופשוט הוא. ודין הצלי עיין לקמן סימן ע"ו דלכתחילה צריך הדחה ובדיעבד שרי בכל ענין. ובמ"י כלל ה' ס"ק ה' ביאר דלא שרי רק היכא דלא נמלח רק מעט ולא שהה שיעור מליחה ע"ש: <b>ושומן</b> אווז שלא הודח הדחה ראשונה התיר הבל"י ס"ק נ"ב בהפס"מ. ואם שהה ג' ימים בלא הדחה משמע שיש להתיר אף בלא הפס"מ ודלא כמשמעות המ"י כלל ד' ס"ק ה' ע"ש. ובסימן ע"ה מתיר בשומן בלא הדחה ראשונה לצלי ואם לא שהה שיעור מליחה או לא נמלח רק מעט אפילו לבשל. ולפי מ"ש בסימן זה היינו שלא נתבשל עדיין וליכא הפסד בצלייה דאי איכא הפס"מ אפילו לבשל שרי וכ"ש אם כבר נתבשל דאין לאסור. והפמ"ג מיקל בשומן שלא הודח בראשונה ומחמיר בשהה ג"י. ועי' בנודע ביהודה סימן כ"ד דאוסר ג"כ בשומן אווז ששהה ג"י בלא הדחה. ומיהו בלא הדחה אחרונה אוסר באו"ה בשם רוקח כלל י"א ד"ב אף בשומן אווז:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 2) If he salted without rinsing. Plain unsalted meat touching a salted piece has the law of unsalted meat salted without rinsing, for salt absorbs into it the visible blood on it; but plain unsalted meat that fell into brine — brine does not absorb the visible blood on its surface and it is permitted. One may learn this from what Shach wrote siman 70 s.k. 29 — see there. And it is not comparable to a subsidiary piece that fell among salted pieces in an unperforated vessel, which is permitted, for there the subsidiary does not expel through contact with the salted piece and the blood in it did not spread from place to place. See in our words there siman 70. However, in Mei Yisrael general rule 6 s.k. 4 it implies like Ohr HaChaim that one permits even if it mixed without rinsing among salted pieces; and in any case, when it touches only from one side it is permitted as I wrote. Darkei Moshe wrote: if the meat was soaked in water beforehand, it is like meat cooked without final rinsing and requires sixty against the blood and salt on it as siman 69. And this is obvious. And the law of roasting — see below siman 76, that l'chatchila one needs rinsing and b'dieved it is permitted in every case. And in Mei Yisrael general rule 5 s.k. 5 he explained that it is permitted only where it was not salted much and did not remain the salting period — see there. And goose fat without first rinsing — Bedek HaBayit s.k. 52 permitted it in great loss. And if it remained three days without rinsing, it seems one should permit even without great loss, unlike what seems from Mei Yisrael general rule 4 s.k. 5 — see there. And in siman 75 he permits goose fat without first rinsing for roasting; and if it did not remain the salting period or was salted only a little, even for cooking. And according to what I wrote in this siman, namely that it was not yet cooked and there is no loss in roasting it, if there is great loss even for cooking it is permitted, and all the more if it was already cooked that there is no prohibition. And Pri Megadim is lenient regarding goose fat without first rinsing and stringent if it remained three days. And see Noda BiYehudah siman 24, who forbids goose fat as well if it remained three days without rinsing. However, without final rinsing, Ohr HaChaim in Rokeach general rule 11 forbids even goose fat.

(There Shach s.k. 40) Or one finds a case where they were rinsed by rainwater. Kereti challenged this text that Shach brought, that it deals where they were rinsed in rainwater that are not mekarev. But their rinsing pleases him and post-intent watering is mekarev. And one may say Shach holds like Tosafot Bava Batra (daf 97) that rainwater is not called watering by default until one intends them for watering and they do not help what pleases him. And one may say it deals where the owners were wicked and there is no intent without action. However, the view of Tosafot and Chiddushei HaRamban is that they do not require specifically the owners' intent, for another's intent is also mekarev, unlike the Rambam.

(Seif 10) A gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. Shach wrote in the name of Pe'er HaAdam that regarding his slave and maidservant fear applies; it seems that if he is not his slave, fear does not apply since he is not his slave. And so too Pe'er HaAdam wrote explicitly. And it is not clear, for it is evident plainly in the Gemara in the first chapter of Chullin regarding inspection of the knife for Kutim, and in Avodah Zarah regarding "the guard need not sit and watch" but even though he goes out and comes in it is permitted — that even for all gentiles who are not his slaves, fear applies when going out and coming in. And so it seems from the language of Hagahot Maimoniot in Hilchot Maachalot Assurot chapter 6 halacha 10. And in the laws of Tefillin it is brought in Orach Chaim siman 32 seif 10. And so in Mordechai, chapter Kol HaBasar siman 1266. And so I found explicitly in Shach siman 118, and he rejected the words of Ohr HaChaim who explained that it speaks only of his slave. And may God set me on the path of truth, for before me came sixty copies of Lechem HaPanim and I saw that he copied Shach's words and added at the end the word "forever"; and he meant that for his slave and maidservant that he acquired, fear applies forever even without going out and coming in; but Shach never intended at all to say that for one who is not his slave fear does not apply when going out and coming in. And this is clear. And what Turei Zahav wrote — a gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. — it seems at first glance that if he is not serving, behold it is forbidden in any case because of bishul akum. But for his slave and maidservant he is not forbidden according to one view that the Mechaber brought siman 113 seif 4. However, it is strained to explain thus the words of Tur, for he did not distinguish between his slave and maidservant and other gentiles. Also the words of the Mechaber are somewhat strained, for he brought two views in this siman 113. And one must say that therefore he wrote "a gentile serving in a Jewish home": if he is not serving, presumably he does not know the custom of Israel and fear does not apply, as the hagahah wrote. And what there is to infer — behold in any case it is forbidden because of bishul akum — it seems to me it deals where it was not yet cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, and then stirring by a Jew helps for all; unlike if it was cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, where stirring by a Jew does not help again according to Rashba that Tur brings there. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 9 — see there. And this is that they inferred Turei Zahav and Shach and wrote "and there the meat is in the pot," meaning it was not yet cooked at all, even like ben drusai, only that he placed the meat in the pot and it is forbidden because it was cooked without final rinsing. For even in kli sheni the hagahah in seif 9 forbids. And even boiling did not cook it in this like ben drusai, as is clear in Ashiri, chapter Kirah siman 11 — see there. And requires further study in siman 17. And it seems to me that in this the words of Beit Yosef are correct, who wrote that here it is only a d'rabbanan matter of blood that was salted, and Darkei Moshe wondered why he needed to say it is blood that was salted, for behold it is cooked before us — see there. And according to what I wrote, one must say it deals here where it was not yet cooked like ben drusai, for otherwise it would be forbidden because of bishul akum. And since it was not cooked like ben drusai, this is not blood in cooking, for blood in cooking is not such unless it was cooked like ben drusai.
