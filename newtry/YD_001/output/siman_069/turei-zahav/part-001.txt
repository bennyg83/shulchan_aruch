<b>צריך להדיח הבשר. </b>בב"י מביא ב' טעמים להדחה ראשונה. האחד משום דם בעין שהוא על הבשר שאין המלח מפליט אלא דם הבלוע שהוא לח אבל לא מה שנתיבש על פניו ואם לא ידיחו אותו תחילה יש לחוש שמא לאחר שיניח מלפלוט דם וציר יהא ניתך ויבלענו. טעם ב' משו' שיתרכך הבשר ויצא דמו ע"י מליחה שאם יהא נקרש על פניו לא יהא כח במלח להוציא דמו עכ"ל ונראה דבהא פליגי היש אוסרים ויש מתירין לענין מלח בלא הדחה תחלה כי היש מתירין שהוא הרא"ש ס"ל הטעם השני ומש"ה מדמה לה לבשר שנמלח שנתנוהו על בשר שלא נמלח שיש לו תקנה במליחה ויפלוט דם שבלע עם דמו דהא גם כאן יש לו דם אחר שיחזור וידיחנו ויתרכך הבשר ואם יחזור וימלחנו יהיה לו דם בלוע לפלוט ואגב זה יפליט גם מה שבלע ממה שהוא עליו בעין. והיש אוסרין ס"ל טעם הראשון ממילא אחר המליחה הראשונה יצא כבר כל דמו ואין חשש רק שיבלע מדם בעין שעליו ובזה לא שייך לומר שיצא על ידי מליחה אגב דמו דהא בשעת מליחה אין לו דם של עצמו והבית יוסף הביא בשם הג"ה מיימון בשם סמ"ק דהמלח מבליע דם בעין שעל הבשר לתוך הבשר וכן כ' באו"ה כלל ד' וכן בש"ד שער ג'.
**** ENGLISH ****
One must rinse the meat. In Beit Yosef he brings two reasons for the first rinsing. One is because of visible blood on the meat, for salt expels only absorbed blood that is moist, but not what dried on the surface; and if he does not rinse it first, one should be concerned that after he leaves it to expel blood and gravy it will melt and be absorbed. The second reason is so the flesh softens and its blood exits through melicha — for if it hardens on the surface, the salt will not have power to expel its blood, end of his words; and it appears on this those who forbid and those who permit regarding salting without rinsing first disagree: those who permit — the Rosh — hold the second reason, and therefore he compares it to meat salted placed on meat that was not salted, that has remedy in melicha and will expel blood it absorbed along with its blood, for here too it has other blood that he can return and rinse and soften the flesh, and if he returns and salts again it will have absorbed blood to expel, and through that it will also expel what it absorbed from what is on it visibly. Those who forbid hold the first reason — after the first salting all its blood already exited and the concern is only that it absorb visible blood on it; in this one cannot say it will exit through melicha along with its blood, for at the time of salting it has no blood of its own. Beit Yosef brought in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Semak that salt absorbs visible blood on the meat into the meat; and so in Orchot Chaim general rule 4 and in Shach Devarim chapter 3:

**** ENGLISH ****
And if the butcher rinsed it, etc. At first glance this teaches that one need not soak in water specifically, but pouring water on it in the butchers' manner suffices; but in truth we hold it does not suffice, as below — therefore it appears to me we are careful in this lest we say from Rav Huna in the Gemara 'he salts and rinses' and he did not mention first rinsing, and that is because it was already rinsed in the slaughterhouse, as Beit Yosef wrote; and why did Rav Huna actually rely on this? Only that certainly their custom was that butchers rinsed, and therefore he did not need to mention it, although the baraita mentioned it — nevertheless R' Huna relied on the custom; and if so, one might say what Tur mentioned that rinsing is required is after rinsing that is from custom, and we need two rinsings at first — this teaches not; and although the tanna in the Gemara said simply 'he rinses' and was not careful about this, the tanna need not explain his words:

**** ENGLISH ****
Within twelve hours. For then the meat expels gravy and thereby does not absorb from the other side; but afterward one should be concerned it will re-absorb what the un-salted side expelled from the side not yet expelled; and although in siman 70 Rama wrote in great loss one may say it expels gravy up to a full day — nevertheless here great loss does not apply, since there is permission in roasting, as his view above; and Maharach wrote: this is specifically when he did not rinse the meat after salting one side; but if he rinsed, salting the other side no longer helps, lest it absorb what the already-salted side absorbed from the other side, and as it absorbs so it expels does not apply, since it has no gravy, for the expulsion holes closed, as I wrote siman 70 — end of his words; and I wrote in siman 70 that the law that rinsing closes the holes of gravy expulsion is not agreed upon, and here all agree one should not forbid because of this, since there is permission in any case in a place of loss. And I wondered at Rama in siman 75 seif 1 regarding chalchulah that has fat, forbidden if cooked without melicha even b'dieved if he salted only the inner side, and nevertheless whatever was not cooked has permission to return and salt the outer side even after twelve hours — the reason: it is not so presumed in blood; and here there is more reason that the unsalted side is not presumed in blood, since b'dieved it is permitted in a place of loss — and why did he rule here specifically within twelve hours? And Maharai, who distinguished here regarding twelve hours, holds in all respects forbidden here b'dieved — therefore it appears for practical halacha, since here there is leniency in any case b'dieved, one may permit even after twelve hours, like chalchulah:

Rashal wrote chapter 25 siman 98 on meat that remained three days without melicha and afterward they salted it in a perforated vessel for the measure of melicha and afterward rinsed and salted again and placed it in an unperforated vessel and did not remain the measure of pickling, and they found gravy there — at first glance one should be concerned perhaps the first melicha had no power to expel all blood inside, since it hardened, but always expels little by little, therefore forbidden; and he answered it is not so, rather in any case permitted: if it did not exit in the first melicha, it will not exit in the second; and if it exited in the first melicha, everything exited. And one may distinguish why they said regarding something requiring libun and hag'alah it is forbidden to cut hot in it according to Rosh, because through hag'alah it expels little by little — there there is more expulsion, the hotter the more — therefore one should be concerned perhaps through first hag'alah that weakened its power it does not exit completely and additional heat is added after cutting hot in it — unlike melicha, etc. — therefore Rashal's explanation appears: in this concern that blood dried in the meat and is not aroused by melicha's heat to exit, we say properly in any case: if it was aroused to exit in the first melicha, as Rivash's view Beit Yosef brings, automatically one need not melicha a second time at all, and permitted in second melicha in unperforated vessel; and if it did not exit in first melicha and the drying remains in place, second melicha will not forbid it and permitted for roasting — unlike hag'alah, where there is no drying reason, only hag'alah lacks power to expel completely since libun is needed — nevertheless hag'alah's power moves what was absorbed somewhat, and when second boiling comes it exits outside — so Rashal's reason, and it is correct; and it appears if they salted only in unperforated vessel and did not first salt in perforated vessel, forbidden, lest some hardened blood exit through melicha.
