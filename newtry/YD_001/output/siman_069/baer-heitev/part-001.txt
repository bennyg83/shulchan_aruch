**** ENGLISH ****
To rinse. Shach wrote in the name of Ran: the reason is so the meat softens and its blood exits through melicha; and Mordechai wrote the reason that the first rinsing is to remove the filth on the piece, for otherwise the salt becomes full from it and the salt no longer expels the blood in the piece — and for these two reasons it helps if he returns and rinses and salts it again even after time; but Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Semak: the reason is because salt absorbs and does not expel visible blood, and it is not applicable to say m'sharek sharek nor kevul'o kach polto; therefore we also do not say iydei d'tarid lefalot lo bala; and Ra'ah wrote: because of visible blood that is on the meat, for salt does not expel except absorbed blood that is moist, but not what dried on its surface — and if he does not rinse it first there is concern that perhaps after he leaves it to expel blood and gravy it will melt and absorb it. And the two views in seif 2, if he salted and did not rinse first — they disagree in these reasons; see Taz (and see likewise the ramifications in these reasons); and see above end of seif 1:
