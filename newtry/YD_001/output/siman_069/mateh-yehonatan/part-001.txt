(סימן סט סעיף א') <b>לשון המחבר ואם אחר שהדיח חתך כל נתח לשנים כו'</b>. דין זה הוא מהמרדכי וראייתו מהא דאי' בפ"ק דחולין דאגב דוחקא דסכינא בולע אף למ"ד דבית השחיטה צונן ה"ה נמי אגב דוחקא דסכינא פליט ומקשה עליו הלא הוא עצמו פסק לעיל דאף למ"ד בה"ש צונן היינו שאין בה"ש רותח לגמרי אבל מ"מ הוא רותח קצת ואגב דוחקא בלוע משא"כ הכא דהוי צונן גמור משום אגב דוחקא דסכינא ל"א דמפליט וי"ל דהכא כיון שא"צ להפליט רק מבשר שהוא רך שאני וי"ל דדוחקא דסכינא לחוד נמי פולט וראייתו כך הוא כי היכי דמהני ברותח קצת דוחקא דסכינא פליט בלוע לגמרי ה"ה הכא דאין כ"כ נבלע בתוכה מהני דוחקא דסכינא להפליט אפילו בצונן לגמרי:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 69 seif 1) The Mechaber's wording "and if after he rinsed he cut each piece into two," etc. This law is from the Mordechai, and his proof from that stated in chapter 1 of Chullin that through the pressure of the knife it absorbs — even according to the one who says the place of slaughter is cold, likewise here through the pressure of the knife it expels. He challenges: did he not himself rule above that even according to the one who says the place of slaughter is cold — i.e. the place of slaughter is not completely hot but nevertheless is somewhat hot and through pressure it absorbs — not so here that it is completely cold; because of the pressure of the knife he says it does not expel. One may say that here, since it need only expel from meat that is soft, it is different. One may say that the pressure of the knife alone also expels, and his proof is thus: as in hot somewhat the pressure of the knife expels completely what was absorbed, likewise here that it is not absorbed so much in it, the pressure of the knife suffices to expel even when completely cold.

**** ENGLISH ****
(seif 9 in hagahah) Even if placed only in a kli sheni, etc. Since there is salt and tzir, etc., and even though we hold melicha requires cooking like ox meat, etc., salt does not hasten to cook, etc., and one may challenge the Rosh in chapter Kirah brought in Taz s.k. 23 who wrote that if old salt, perhaps forbidden, etc., and even if salted anew, although the meat does not cook, nevertheless the salt and moisture and blood in it cooked, etc. — how does he say that salting in it cooked? Melicha requires cooking like ox meat! One must distinguish: where he says cooking required, that is salt in its strength and in the eye; but if it dissolved somewhat on the meat and its power ceased, it cooks properly and also hastens to cook through the power of salt and tzir even in kli sheni. What Taz wrote "his words are astonishing," etc. — not necessarily compelled; one may say the Rosh deals with Shabbat, and what he concludes that it returns and absorbs, etc., is to say the meat is forbidden because of cooking on Shabbat — if it had not absorbed the moisture of blood cooked on Shabbat the meat would be permitted, for the meat does not cook at all in kli rishon; if so no prohibition was made in this meat at all, therefore he says the moisture of blood that cooked is absorbed and the meat is forbidden. What Rama wrote in Teshuvot HaRosh, etc., Rabbenu Yerucham permits unsalted meat — one who examines Rabbenu Yerucham Netiv 15 part 5 sees explicitly he deals when there is no water, dry stuck to vessel walls; but if there is water, one may say he agrees forbidden as the Rosh said.
