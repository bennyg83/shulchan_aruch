<b>שמלא קרח. </b>עבה"ט מ"ש בשם רש"ל בשר שנקרש כו' ועיין בספר לוית חן פרשה אחרי שכתב דבשר שנמלח ושהה שיעור מליחה והודח ואח"כ נמצא באמצעיתו קרח לא מהני לבשר זה שיחזור וימלחנו אחר שנפשר כיון דהבשר סביביו היה נפשר בשעת מליחה ופלט כל דמו שוב לא יפלוט המליחה לדם שבפנים כמו בחלחולת בסימן ע"ה ואין תקנה לבשר זה רק לצלותו עכ"ד ע"ש ועיין בתשובת הר הכרמל חלק יו"ד סי' י"ב שדחה טעם זה אלא דמטעם אחר יש לאסור דאותו חלק בשר שכבר שהה במלחו כראוי ויצא דמו והודח ונסתמו נקבי הפליטה כשמולחין שנית יחזור ויבלע מבשר זה שבאמצע ושוב העלה דגם משום זה אין לאסור ע"ש ועיין בתשובת גבעת שאול סי' ט"ז שדעתו בדין זה דאם לא הודח עדיין יש תקנה להתיר ע"י שידיחנו קודם רק להעביר הליכלוך של ציר ואז ודאי דמהני מליחה שנית אפילו שלא במקום הפסד אך אם כבר הודח היטב אין תקנה רק לצלותו ואם כבר נמלח שנית אחר שהודח כדין הדחה גמורה יש לאסור אף לצלי ע"ש היטב ועיין בדגמ"ר מ"ש בזה. ומ"ש הבה"ט עוד בשם מ"י בשר ששרה במים ונקרש כו' עיין בזה בשו"ת דבר שמואל סימן שע"ג שדעתו לאסור אף אם לא נשרה בקרח רק מעל"ע ועיין בשו"ת קרית חנה סימן כ"ט שהעלה כדעת המ"י דקרח לא חשיב כמים ולכן אם לא שהה בקרח רק מעל"ע בודאי שרי ואף אם שהה כן ג"י ג"כ אין ברור לאסור ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
Full of ice. Be'er Heitev — what he wrote in the name of Maharshal, meat that hardened, etc. And see in Levit Chen on the Torah, parashat Acharei Mot, who wrote regarding meat that was salted and remained the measure of melicha and was rinsed and afterward ice was found in its middle — it does not help for this meat to return and salt it again after thawing, since the meat around it thawed at the time of melicha and expelled all its blood, melicha will no longer expel the blood inside like in the brain in siman 75, and there is no remedy for this meat except to roast it — end of his words, see there. And see in responsum Har HaKarmel, Yoreh De'ah part, siman 12, who rejected this reason but forbids for another reason — that part of the meat that already remained in its salt properly and its blood exited and was rinsed and the holes of expulsion closed — when they salt again it returns and absorbs from this inner meat — and again he raised that also for this reason one need not forbid — see there. And see in responsum Givat Shaul siman 16 that his view in this law is that if not yet rinsed there is remedy to permit by rinsing beforehand only to remove the moisture of tzir, and then certainly melicha a second time helps even not in a place of great loss; but if already rinsed well, no remedy except roasting; and if already salted again after rinsed as proper complete rinsing, one must forbid even for roasting — see there well. And see in Dagul Meryavah what he wrote on this. And what Be'er Heitev further wrote in the name of Mateh Yehonatan, meat that soaked in water and hardened, etc. — see on this in responsum Devar Shmuel siman 573 that his view is to forbid even if not soaked in ice, only from above; and see in responsum Kiryat Chana siman 29 who raised like Mateh Yehonatan that ice is not considered like water, therefore if not remained in ice, only from above, certainly permitted; and even if remained so three days, also not clear to forbid — see there.

Thus Semak: if an idolater serves in an Israelite's house and placed meat in a pot and we do not know if they rinsed it — not believed in masiach lefi tumo according to Peri To'ar; and all the more so if they know Jewish customs; and if there is a lad or maiden who knows the matter of rinsing, or an Israelite going in and out — permitted because he is afraid, etc. — end of his words. And behold, if he knows their Jewish customs, called presumptive grounds to act like the custom since he serves in an Israelite's house; if so, three distinctions in the matter: if masiach lefi tumo — even if he does not know their customs and there are no presumptive grounds, believed because of masiach lefi tumo; if he knows their customs — presumptive grounds and believed even if not masiach lefi tumo; and possible that presumptive grounds with testimony of an idolater even if not masiach lefi tumo is preferable to masiach lefi tumo where there are no presumptive grounds; therefore Semak wrote the wording "and all the more so." And where there is a minor of understanding going in and out and the idolater knows their customs — permitted and not required even that the idolater say at all that he rinsed; and without his statement we say presumably from fear he certainly rinsed; unlike if no Israelite going in and out — even if he knows their customs, nevertheless not permitted presumptively, only if he says explicitly that he rinsed, even if not masiach lefi tumo. And with this Semak's wording is explained well — end of his words, see there.
