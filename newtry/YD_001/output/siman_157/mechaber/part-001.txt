Regarding for which transgressions one must be killed and not transgress; it contains 3 se’ifim. All transgressions in the Torah, except for idolatry, gilui arayos, and shefichas damim: if they say to a person that he should transgress them or be killed — if it is in private, he should transgress and not be killed. And if he wants to be stringent upon himself and be killed, he is permitted, if the non-Jew intends to make him deviate from the religion. {Rama: And if he can save himself with all that he has, he must give everything and not transgress a negative commandment (Ran, perek Lulav HaGazul; and Rashba and Raavad; and Rivash, siman 387). And in a place where they said, “Anyone who has the ability to protest and does not protest is seized for that sin,” nevertheless, regarding a matter where there is concern of danger, one need not spend his money for this (Mahari Weil, siman 156).} And if it is in public — that is, in the presence of 10 Jews — he is obligated to be killed and not transgress, if the non-Jew intends to make him deviate from the religion (even regarding “the strap of a shoe”) (Beit Yosef). But if he does not intend [that] other than for his own benefit, he should transgress and not be killed. And if it is a time of decree (upon the Jews alone) (Beit Yosef in the name of Nimukei Yosef), even regarding “the strap of a shoe” (meaning, the strap of the shoe), he must be killed and not transgress. {Rama: And this is only when they want to make him deviate regarding a negative commandment; but if they decreed a decree not to fulfill a positive commandment, he need not fulfill it and be killed (Ran, perek Bameh Tomnin, and Nimukei Yosef, perek Sorer UMoreh). However, if the time requires this, and he wants to be killed and fulfill it, the permission is in his hand (Maharik, shoresh 88, in the name of the Ran).} And regarding idolatry, gilui arayos, and shefichas damim — even in private and not at a time of decree, and even if the non-Jew intends only for his own benefit — he must be killed and not transgress. {Rama: And this is specifically when they tell him to do an act; for example, when they tell a man to uncover nakedness or be killed. But if they coerce a woman to come upon her, or they want to throw him upon an infant to kill it, or he is already erect and they want to coerce him to [commit] gilui arayos — he need not be killed (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos and the Ran, perek Kol Sha’ah). And every prohibition of idolatry, gilui arayos, and shefichas damim, even though there is no death penalty in it, only a simple prohibition, he must be killed and not transgress; but for the prohibition of “lifnei iver lo titen michshol,” he should transgress and not be killed (Ran, perek Kol Sha’ah and perek Ben Sorer UMoreh). And a non-Jew who has relations with a Jewish woman is not included in gilui arayos (Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban and the above poskim).} Non-Jews who said to Jews, “Give us one of you and we will kill him,” they may not give them one of them (Mishnah, perek 8 of Terumos; and Rambam, perek 5 of Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah), unless they designated him and said, “Give us so-and-so” (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi and the Ran). And some say that even in such a case one may not hand him over unless he is liable for death, like Sheva ben Bichri (Rambam, the aforementioned perek). And likewise, women to whom non-Jews said, “Give us one of you and we will defile her,” they should all be defiled and not hand over one Jewish soul. Any place where it says “he must be killed and not transgress,” if he transgressed and was not killed — even though he desecrated the Name — nevertheless, he is called coerced and exempt; and this is specifically when he could not flee, but if he can flee and does not do so, behold he is like a dog returning to his vomit, and is called one who transgresses intentionally (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam, perek 5 of Yesodei HaTorah).

It is forbidden for a person to say that he is a non-Jew in order that they not kill him; but if it is in order that they not recognize that he is a Jew, he may change his clothing at a time of decree, since he is not saying that he is a non-Jew. {Rama: And he may even wear kilayim (Nimukei Yosef, perek HaGozel Basra).
