<b>דיני הרכבת האילן. ובו ז' סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="1"></i>כלאי האילנות הרי הם <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="1"></i>בכלל מה שנאמר שדך לא תזרע כלאים כיצד המרכיב אילן באילן כגון שהרכיב יחור של תפוח באתרוג או אתרוג בתפוח ה"ז לוקה מן התורה בכל מקום בין בארץ <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="2"></i>בין בח"ל <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="2"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="3"></i>וכן המרכיב ירק באילן או אילן בירק לוקה <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="3"></i>בכל מקום:
**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of grafting trees. In it are 7 seifim. Kilayim of trees are included in what was said, "Your field you shall not sow with kilayim." How so? One who grafts a tree onto a tree—for example, grafted a shoot of apple onto citron or citron onto apple—receives lashes d'oraisa everywhere, both in Eretz Yisrael and in the Diaspora. And likewise one who grafts a vegetable onto a tree or a tree onto a vegetable receives lashes everywhere.

**** ENGLISH ****
There is a prohibition of grafting even under the ground—for example, a vine shoot that was layered under the ground: it is forbidden to sow vegetables upon it unless there is three tefachim of earth on top of it; and even if the layering was inside a gourd that dried out, or inside a pipe of earthenware, because it takes root in it and it becomes grafting of vegetable onto tree. And if there was less than this on top, it is forbidden to sow on top of it; but it is permitted to sow at its side. But layering in rock is difficult: even if there is no earth on it but three fingerbreadths, it is permitted to bring seed upon it. When does this apply? When the main vine is not visible; but if it is visible, one must distance six tefachim in every direction and then sow, as one distances from every individual vine that was not layered.

**** ENGLISH ****
There are among trees some similar to one another in leaves or in fruit, and nevertheless since they are two species, these are kilayim with one another. How so? Apple with quince (meaning: a forest apple species); peach (meaning: persica in the vernacular) with oleaster (meaning: he grafted olives on pomegranate, which are fruit similar to olive, and oleaster comes out from them) with pomegranate—even though they are similar to one another, they are kilayim with one another. But pears (in the vernacular) with crustumenian pears (a species of pears), and quince with medlars (sorbele in the vernacular) are not kilayim with one another. {Rama: And likewise it is permitted to graft one species even black on white and thin on thick, even though it has an accompanying name (Kol Bo and Bach in the name of Orach Chaim). And likewise wood on wood is permitted even if not its species (so it appears from the Tur). Nevertheless, since most of the world does not recognize most species, it is good to be careful with all of them (Beit Yosef in the name of Semak).}
