**** ENGLISH ****
If a person is trusted to forbid the wine of his fellow. In it are 4 seifim. One who says to his fellow, “Your wine was poured as a libation”: if it is in his hand, he is believed. And even if it is not in his hand now, for it was already returned to the owner — if the first time he found it he said to him, “Your wine was poured as a libation,” he is believed, even if the owner contradicts him. But if it is not in his hand, and he did not say so to the owner the first time he saw it, and afterwards he said to him — he is not believed if the owner contradicts him within “toch k’dei dibbur” (Mordechai, perek HaOmer), or he says, “I do not know.” But if he was silent, silence is like admission. {Rama: We teach him l'chatchila that he should say, “I do not believe you,” and then according to everyone it is permitted, for the owners are believed regarding what is theirs (Tur). And if the owners do not contradict him, even though another contradicts him, it does not help (Hagahot Maimoniot, chapter 11), since the owners are there and are silent; but if the owners are not there, it helps (Arukh). And some wrote that one witness in the face of contradiction is nothing (Rashba siman 545). And if the owners say, “I do not believe you,” even though in his heart he believes him as two witnesses, one should not be stringent (Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai); and this is specifically regarding yayin nesech, for one need not be so exacting; but regarding other issurim it does not help if he said “I do not believe you” while he believes in his heart (Arukh, general rule 13).} And this is when he says to him “Your wine was poured as a libation” in your presence, or he said to him, “You know that there are indications for the matter.” But if he was silent and afterwards contradicted him and said, “What I was silent about at first was not because I admitted, but to settle and think in my heart, to remember whether it is true” — he is believed. {Rama: And if he did not tell him the first time he found it, and later says it and gives an excuse why he did not say it at first, one should be concerned for his words (Hagahot Mordechai, perek HaOmer). And anyone who is trusted regarding something and witnesses contradict him: even though he has a migo that he would have been believed to forbid it in a situation where witnesses were not contradicting him, nevertheless since he contradicts the witnesses it is a migo in the presence of witnesses, and he is not believed even regarding issur. Nevertheless, for himself it is forbidden because he has made it for himself a “chatichah d’issura” (so it appears from Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rashba). A butcher who made a sign on an animal in the manner that is done on treifah animals, and says about it that it is treifah, and later says that it is kosher, and he did not say so initially except so that it would remain for him to take meat from it — there is one who says that since he gives an excuse for his words, he is believed; and see above siman 1 regarding the law that one witness is trusted regarding issurim. Two brothers are only like one witness regarding issurim (a responsum of Rashba siman 544). And all those disqualified for testimony are fit regarding issurim, unless he is suspected regarding those matters (Ran). One who says to two partners, “Your wine was poured as a libation,” and one is silent and the other contradicts: for the one who is silent it is forbidden, and for the other and for the whole world it is permitted (Hagahot Alfasi).}

One witness is trusted regarding issurim to permit, but not to be stringent. {Rama: However, there are those who say that regarding a matter that can be clarified — such as if one says to him, “Come and I will show you a non-Jew pouring a libation to your wine” — one must be concerned for his words (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot). And any matter that is not established as heter and not established as issur, one witness is trusted regarding it, even to forbid it (Ashiri, perek HaNizakin, and Mordechai, perek HaIshah Rabbah). But any place that a matter is established as issur, such as tevel or a piece of meat that is not menukkar, the witness is not trusted to permit it unless it is in his power to fix it. And if there are here 2 pieces, one of issur and one of heter, the witness is trusted to say “this is heter and this is issur” (Ran). And a person is trusted regarding what is his, even where issur is established. And see above siman 119, the law of one who is suspected regarding a matter, if he testifies about it. And see below siman 185, one who says, “So-and-so, the chacham, permitted this for me,” and the chacham denies it. And see in Even HaEzer siman 152 whether we say regarding testimony “the mouth that forbade is the mouth that permitted.” And a woman is trusted regarding a matter of issur to say, “I fixed it,” and this is specifically regarding definite issur, such as nikur of meat and the like; but regarding a doubt, perhaps there is no issur here — such as when she must clarify tamei fish from tahor — a woman is not trusted; and likewise regarding an issur that has sides to be lenient, a woman is not trusted, for a woman’s mind is light to be lenient. A minor does not have the law of a witness to be trusted regarding issurim; nevertheless, regarding a sharp and knowledgeable minor, where there are indications to his words, one should be stringent if he testifies about an issur. And if he testifies about a d’rabbanan issur to be lenient, and issur was not established — such as bedikat chametz — he is trusted, for the Sages trusted him for d’rabbanan; but if issur was established, he is not trusted at all.}
