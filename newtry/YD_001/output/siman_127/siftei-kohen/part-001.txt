<b>אם הוא בידו. </b>ל' הטור כגון שנתנו בידו לשומרו ור"ל דל"ת דבידו היינו בידו לנסכו אלא דהיינו שנתנו בידו לשומרו והוי כבעליו עליו ולא מטעם מיגו וכ"פ הרא"ש פ' הנזקין בחילוק י"א והמרדכי ר"פ האשה רבה אבל הב"י לא הביא דברי הרא"ש ומרדכי אלו ואדרבה הביא בסתם דברי רש"י ור"ת והרשב"א שפירשו כל שבידו לטמא ואולי לזה השמיט כאן ל' הטור וכן בעט"ז פי' הטעם משום שישנו בידו לחסרו וכן הוכיח הרשב"א בחידושיו פרק הנזקין ריש דף ע"ט בראיות וכן משמע בתו' בקדושין פרק האומר (דף ס"ה ע"ב) דמהימן משום מגו ועפ"ז יש לדון במי שאומר לחבירו על יין מבושל שלו שנתנסך קודם הבישול אינו נאמן) אפי' הוא בידו כיון דלית ליה מגו:
**** ENGLISH ****
If it is in his hand. The language of the Tur: such as when it was given into his hand to guard it; and he means that “in his hand” does not mean in his hand to libate it, but rather that it was given into his hand to guard it, and it is as if he were its owner over it; and not because of a migu; and so ruled the Rosh in the chapter Nezikin in distinction 11, and Mordechai at the beginning of the chapter HaIsha Rabba; but Beit Yosef did not bring the words of the Rosh and Mordechai, and on the contrary brought simply the words of Rashi, Rabbenu Tam, and the Rashba, who explained: anyone within whose hand it is to defile it; and perhaps for this reason he omitted the language of the Tur here; and likewise in Pri Chadash he explained the reason because it is within his power to diminish it; and the Rashba proved it in his chiddushim in the chapter Nezikin at the beginning of daf 79 with proofs; and it is implied in Tosafot in Kiddushin in the chapter HaOmer (daf 65 side 2) that he is believed because of a migu; and according to this one may consider: one who says to his fellow regarding his boiled wine that it was libated before the boiling is not believed) even if it is in his hand, since he has no migu.

<b>אם בפעם כו' אפילו הבעל דין מכחישו. </b>באמת זאת היא דעת התוספות והרא"ש ומרדכי ושאר הרבה פוסקים אבל קשה דהא כללא הוא לא יקום עד אחד באיש וגו' דעד א' אינו קם רק לשבועה ונהי דקי"ל עד א' נאמן באיסורים היינו היכא דאין הבע"ד מכחישו או במקום דאיכא מגו אבל במקום דליכא לא הא ולא הא לא מיסתבר כלל דעד א' יהא נאמן להכחיש הבע"ד ולהפסיד ממונו ואף בדאיכא מגו ק"ק דהא כתבו הרא"ש והט"ו בח"מ סימן ק"ח סי"ד דאינו נאמן (וכה"ג הקשו התוס' בעשרה יוחסין והרא"ש פ"ק דמציעא ושאר פוסקים אמ"ש רש"י דהיכא דאין מקחו בידו נאמן לומר לזה מכרתי אי נקט זוזי מחד דאמאי יהא נאמן טפי מעובד כוכבים דעלמא עכ"ד אע"ג דהיה בידו מתחלה) אלא דיש לחלק קצת ע"ש והשטה המחוורת בש"ס היא שטת הראב"ד והרשב"א דמפרשים הך דאמרינן בהנזקין דאי אשכחי' וא"ל מיד נאמן בשותק ומחלקין בין פועל דגרע מעד דעלמא ע"ש וכ"כ הר"ן בהנזקין והיא שטת האחרונים שהביא הר"ן פרק האומר וזהו דעת הרמב"ם סוף הלכות פסולי המוקדשין וסוף ה' מטמאי משכב ומושב דדוקא בשותק נאמן ע"ש ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****If for the first time, etc., even if the owner of the wine contradicts him. In truth this is the view of Tosafot, the Rosh, Mordechai, and many other poskim; but it is difficult, for the principle is "one witness shall not rise against a man," and a single witness stands only for an oath; and although we hold one witness is believed in prohibitions, that is where the owner of the wine does not contradict him, or where there is a migu; but where there is neither this nor that, it is not reasonable at all that one witness should be believed to contradict the owner of the wine and cause him monetary loss; and even where there is a migu it is difficult, for the Rosh and Tur in Choshen Mishpat siman 108 seif 14 wrote that he is not believed (and similarly Tosafot challenged in Yuchasin, the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, and other poskim, as Rashi explained: where the purchase is not in his hand he is believed to say "I sold to this one" if he took payment from one — why should he be more believed than an ordinary gentile? — end of his words, even though it was in his hand initially); but one may distinguish somewhat — see there; and the correct approach in the Gemara is the view of the Raavad and Rashba, who explain the case in Nezikin that if we find him and he says immediately he is believed in silence, and they distinguish between a worker, who is inferior to an ordinary witness — see there; and likewise the Ran in Nezikin; and it is the view of the Acharonim that the Ran brought in the chapter HaOmer; and this is the view of the Rambam at the end of Hilchot Pesulei HaMukdashin and at the end of Hilchot Metamei Mishkav uMoshav, that specifically in silence he is believed — see there, and examine carefully.

<b>אבל אם שתק כו'. </b>פי' אפי' אחר שלא היה בידו מעולם ולא א"ל בפעם הא' לעולם כיון דשתק כהודאה דמיא וז"ל הטור אבל אחר שאינו בידו או שלא אמ"ל השומר לבעליו בפעם הא' וא"ל אח"כ אינו נאמן ודוקא שהבעלים מכחישין אותו או אומר אינו יודע אבל אם שותק שתיקה כהודאה דמיא עכ"ל וכתב הב"ח ונראה לי מלשון הטור מדכתב אבל אחר שאינו בידו או שלא אמר השומר כו' ולא כתב או שלא אמר בפעם הא' אלמא משמע דבאחר שאינו שומר שלא היה בידו אין חילוק בין בפעם הא' לפעם הב' לעולם אם שותק כהודאה דמיא וכ"כ ב"י ע"ש הרשב"א בחידושיו כו' עכ"ל ותימא גדולה דהא גבי הכחשה הוא דכתב הטור או שלא אמר השומר כו' אבל בסיפא גבי שתיקה לא חילק ועוד זו אלא אדרבה סברת התוס' והרא"ש היא להדיא הפוכה מסברת הרשב"א וס"ל דשומר עדיף מעד דעלמא כיון שהיה פעם אחד בידו והכי משמע נמי להדיא ממ"ש הרא"ש ואדרבה נמי קשה דאע"ג דמהימן ליה כו' ע"ש והכי מוכח נמי להדיא במרדכי פ' האומר גבי מ"ש שם וכתב ראבי"ה רבא נמי מודה באין בידו ושותק כדמשמע הכא כו' דגבי שומר שהיה בידו נמי אמרינן דשתיקה הוי הודאה אפי' בפעם ב' וע"ק דכיון דאמרינן שתיקה כהודאה דמיא מה חילוק בין שומר דהא דקאמר אביי שותק נאמן לאו משום דשתיקה כהודאה היא ואצ"ל בפניך וכה"ג אלא אפי' שותק מפני שאינו יודע בירור הדבר נאמן וכמ"ש בס"ק ט"ו בשמו ולכך מחלק בין שומר לאחר דשומר לא מהימן כולי האי דחזקת מיזהר זהיר כיון דעליה רמיא מילתא כו' אבל להטור דס"ל שתיקה כהודאה דמיא ודאי דאין חילוק אלא ודאי כך הוי פשט הטור אבל אחר שאינו בידו כלומר שלא היה בידו מעולם אפילו אמר ליה מיד אינו נאמן כל שהבעל מכחישו או אפי' שומר שהיה בידו אם לא א"ל בפעם הא' אינו נאמן כשמכחישו או אומר איני יודע אבל אם שותק אין חילוק בין אחר לשומר בין א"ל מיד או לא לעולם שתיקה כהודאה דמיא וכך הם דברי המחבר וכן מבואר בתוס' והרא"ש וסייעתם ונראה דהוי שותק בענין דודאי שתיקתו הודאה כגון דא"ל בפניך וכה"ג גם הרשב"א מודה דאין חילוק בין שומר לאחר בין א"ל מיד או לא לעולם נאמן וכל זה ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****But if he was silent, etc. Meaning: even after it was never in his hand and he never told him the first time, since he was silent it is like admission; and such is the language of the Tur: but afterward, when it is not in his hand, or the guardian did not tell the owner the first time and afterward told him, he is not believed — and specifically when the owners contradict him or say they do not know; but if they are silent, silence is like admission — end of his words; and Bach wrote: it appears to me from the Tur's language, since he wrote "but afterward when it is not in his hand, or the guardian did not say," and did not write "or did not say the first time," it implies that afterward, when he is no longer the guardian and it was not in his hand, there is no distinction between the first time and the second time forever — if he is silent, silence is like admission; and likewise Beit Yosef there, Rashba in his chiddushim, etc. — end of his words; and one may raise a great astonishment, for regarding contradiction the Tur wrote "or the guardian did not say," but in the latter part regarding silence he did not distinguish; and further, behold he follows the view of Tosafot, the Rosh, and their supporters, and they challenged from that which Abaye said that a silent witness is believed even though it was never in his hand, against that which is in Nezikin regarding a guardian that if it is not in his hand he is not believed; and they resolved with difficulty; and if so, there is no beginning to this question — rather, on the contrary, the view of Tosafot and the Rosh is explicitly the opposite of Rashba's view, and they hold that a guardian is superior to an ordinary witness since it was once in his hand; and such appears explicitly from the Rosh's words; and on the contrary it is also difficult, for even though he is believed to him, etc. — see there; and such is proven explicitly in Mordechai in the chapter HaOmer regarding what is written there, and Rabbenu Yehuda wrote: Rava too agrees when it is not in his hand and he is silent, as appears here, etc., that regarding a guardian who was in his hand we also say silence is admission even the second time; and one may challenge: since we say silence is like admission, what difference between a guardian or an ordinary witness — for whoever was told "it was libated in your presence" or "you knew circumstantial evidence" should have answered him, and since he was silent he admitted; if so there is no distinction; and thus Darkei Moshe challenged Rashba, and he brings it Derishah; except Darkei Moshe did not challenge Rashba at all, for he follows his reasoning that that which Abaye says a silent witness is believed is not because silence is admission, and all the more "in your presence," and similarly; rather, even if he is silent because he does not know the matter clearly, he is believed, as written in s.k. 15 in his name; and therefore he distinguishes between a guardian and others, for a guardian is not so believed because he is presumed to be very careful since the matter rests upon him, etc.; but according to the Tur, who holds silence is like admission, certainly there is no distinction; but when it was never in his hand, even if he told him immediately, he is not believed as long as the owner contradicts him; or a guardian who was in his hand, if he did not tell him the first time, he is not believed when they contradict him or say they do not know; but if they are silent, there is no distinction between others and a guardian, whether he told him immediately or not forever — silence is like admission; and such are the words of the Mechaber, and so it is explained in Tosafot, the Rosh, and their supporters; and it appears this is silence in a matter where certainly his silence is admission, such as where he was told "in your presence," and similarly; Rashba too agrees there is no distinction between a guardian and others, whether he told him immediately or not — he is believed; and all this is clear.

but one may be astonished at this law, for Abaye who says in the Gemara "one witness told him and the other is silent — he is believed" learned it from what we taught in Keritot in the first chapter: they told him, he told one witness "you ate forbidden fat," and the other says "I did not eat" — he is exempt; the reason is that he said "behold he was silent" — he is believed; and at the beginning of that mishnah we taught: one witness says he ate and one witness says he did not eat — he brings a conditional guilt offering; and it must be when he is silent (due to admission, such as where they told him "you should have known," as written in the Sifrei — see there); for if they contradict him it is obvious; and all the more from the end that he is exempt — that even so the contradiction of the witness who says "he did not eat" to exempt him from a definite sacrifice proves that even though he was silent we do not say he admitted to this one who says "he ate"; nevertheless, since one witness contradicts him, one may say he relies on the contradictor, or he knows circumstantial evidence for this one and for that one; and even though where he is silent because he does not know the matter clearly he is not believed — that is different there, for the witness's testimony is not valid at all, for we require that the owner of the wine admit somewhat to him; or chelev is different, for there even if his heart tells him he brings a conditional guilt offering, as written in s.k. 13 in the name of the Yerushalmi — see there; nevertheless it is proven that even though the owners are there and silent, the contradiction of the witness helps; and it is strained to say that that which he says "it does not help" is only so the wine should be permitted with certainty, but it does not emerge from doubt; but it is not so, for it is in Semak siman 223 and Or Zarua there and Darkei Moshe in the name of the hagahot that the contradiction of the witness who says "it was not libated" does not help even to permit it to himself; and if it is a doubt, why should it not be permitted to the witness himself? And behold even regarding a married woman we hold that if one witness says her husband died and one witness says he did not die, if she married the one who says he died, even though each time he comes upon her he stands under a conditional guilt offering, she does not leave — as the Rambam wrote in chapter 12 of Hilchot Gerushin din 19, and as the Hagid proved there with clear proof from the Yerushalmi and other proofs as well; and even for the Ramban and Rosh in their responsum who disagree there — that is because since she is in the presumption of a married woman, we establish man against man and establish the woman in the presumption of a married woman, as is proven there; not so here; and one must say for Semak and Or Zarua that nevertheless there is an issur l'chatchila, and according to this view b'dieved one should not be stringent on the witness himself.

**** ENGLISH ****It helps. **** ENGLISH ****A minor does not have the status of a witness, etc. In the responsum of the Rivash siman 245 it appears he deals with when the owner of the wine is not silent and admitting, but only that he does not know; but when he is silent due to admission it appears he is believed; and the Rivash follows the view of Rashba and his supporters that I brought above in s.k. 15, that one witness is believed even if the owner of the wine says "I do not know"; and according to this, the rabbi, who holds one witness is not believed when the owner of the wine says "I do not know" — comes to inform us that a minor has no status of witness at all; and there is a practical difference where he says "you knew circumstantial evidence," or where another contradicts him and the owners are not present; or in a matter that can be clarified; or where he says "come and I will show you a gentile libating your wine"; or in a matter where neither issur nor heter was established; or with two pieces, one of issur and one of heter, and all similar cases, where the witness is believed without the silence of the owner of the wine that is like admission — a minor is not believed; however where it is in his hand, a minor is believed even to remove a matter from its presumption to permit or to forbid — such appears in the responsum of the Rivash there; and such is its language: "That which we say in tractate Sukkah — a minor who knows to guard his hands, they feed him by means of pure foods — that is with pure foods in his hand; but regarding what is not in his hand, a minor is not believed to remove a matter from its presumption to heter or to issur" — examine; and see in Orach Chaim at the end of siman 457.
