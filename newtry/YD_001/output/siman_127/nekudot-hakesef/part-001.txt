דגים טמאים עם טהורים אם מאמינו שמעון לראובן אסור לקנות אבל אם אמר שמעון איני מאמינך מותר לקנות וכו' מיהו התוספות וכו' ע"ש וא"כ כך הצעת הג"ה זו ואם אין בעלים מכחישין אותו אע"פ שמכחישו אחר לא מהני כי היכא דגבי עד אחד אמר אכל חלב וע"א אומר לא אכל שמביא אשם תלוי משום דלבו נוקפו ה"נ אי מהימן ליה אסורים לבע"ד ונאמנים לאסור שלהם (וכיון שכך אסורה ג"כ אפילו לאחרים משום דהיין נאסר במה שמהימן ליה בע"ד) אבל **** ENGLISH ****(There in Shach s.k. 13) It is difficult — what does it help, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot follows his view, who wrote in s.k. 14: when the owner of the wine is not before us, even without the contradicting witness, the witness who says "it was libated" is not believed — see there; and it appears to me the Rav holds that when the witness says not in the presence of the litigant that the wine was libated, and at the time it was libated the litigant himself was there — even though he testifies not in the presence of the owner of the wine, the witness is believed; for he holds that since he said it was libated in the presence of the owner of the wine, had the owner of the wine been here he might have admitted to the witness's words (and all the more so according to Rashba and his supporters, that certainly "in your presence" — this law is true); and even though he ruled in s.k. 16 that he must say "in your presence" — this is like "before you," since the owner of the wine is not before us; and what he brought as proof from Or Zarua in s.k. 13 — it appears to me on the contrary, from Or Zarua it implies he is believed even not in his presence, since he wrote "any person can contradict him," and if his testimony is nothing, behold it is as if he did not testify at all — why should he contradict him; and also the language "contradiction" applies to a clear matter where he contradicts him that he speaks falsely; but if he does not contradict him, we infer it helps; and with this Hagahot Maimoniyot's challenge in s.k. 14 is also resolved; and further it is greatly astonishing — see there. But after examining well, I also settled what he challenged in s.k. 11 and s.k. 13; it appears to me to explain the gloss on its basis; and take this principle in hand: the Rav holds that if the witness is a person he believes — meaning he believes him — it is forbidden to him; and likewise in Turei Zahav general rule 32 law 6: if Reuven came and told Shimon before him that he saw impure fish with pure ones — if Shimon believes Reuven, it is forbidden to buy; but if Shimon said "I do not believe you," it is permitted to buy, etc.; however Tosafot, etc. — see there; and if so, this is the proposal of this gloss: and if the owners do not contradict him, even though another contradicts him, it does not help — for just as regarding one witness who said "he ate forbidden fat" and one witness says "he did not eat," who brings a provisional guilt-offering because his heart pounds — likewise if he believes him, they are forbidden to the owner of the wine and are believed to forbid theirs (and since so, it is forbidden also to others, because the wine was forbidden in what he believes regarding the owner of the wine); but if the owners are not present, it helps; for if no person contradicts him, if we believe him it is forbidden as Turei Zahav wrote; and here since another contradicts him and the owner of the wine is not present, it helps; and some wrote that one witness in contradiction is nothing, since we believe the first witness, the contradiction of the second does not help even if the owners are not present; and he emerges from what Rashba wrote: one witness in contradiction is nothing, as stated in Kiddushin, etc.; since the owner of the wine contradicts him, this one cannot forbid his own — the reason implies he cannot forbid his own, and specifically the litigant contradicts him; unlike when a second witness contradicts, for the first is believed as above; and what he wrote "and even though there is an answer," etc. — this is what Rashba wrote: and greater than this R. Tam said, etc., "I do not believe your words," etc., "and even though there is an answer to his words" (meaning in what R. Tam holds that when he says "I do not believe you" his testimony is nothing — Rashba does not hold so; one witness in contradiction is nothing; but when another contradicts him not in his presence — meaning when the owner of the wine contradicts the one witness, it is nothing; if we believe the first, it is forbidden; but when the owners are present and silent, they admit — Rashba holds it is forbidden); and what he wrote in the name of Ran, that even if the owners are silent it is nothing — and in my humble opinion it appears there is no proof from Ran, for there in Kiddushin it is different, for even if the woman admits she was betrothed between them it is nothing according to R. Pappa until there are two witnesses there; therefore with one witness his contradiction is no testimony; unlike in other prohibitions where the owner is believed regarding his own; and likewise what he wrote "and this view is not correct," etc. — one may say his intent regarding Kiddushin is different — so it appears clear to me in my humble opinion — investigate well:
