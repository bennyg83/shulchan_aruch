לבנים חזקת כשרות אלא מצד אביהם מכל מקום הואיל ולדבריו עובד כוכבים הוא אינו נאמן לפסול בניו הכי נמי בחמאה זאת הואיל ולדבריו אינו אלא כאחר ולא כשומר חמאה זאת אינו נאמן הואיל ובעלים מכחישים אותו עכ"ל ותמה אני אם יצאה זה מפה קדוש דהא בהדיא פרש"י פרק החולץ (יבמות דף מ"ו) וז"ל ואין עדות לעובד כוכבים הואיל ובניך מוחזקין בכשרו' אין אתה נאמן לפוסלן עכ"ל. ש"מ שבלאו אבוהון היה להם חזקת כשרות ע"כ נ"ל דכאן בחמאה הוי ליה כנמצא ביד מי שאינו נאמן לסמוך עליו. אחי הגאון מוהר"ר יצחק הלוי ז"ל השיב בתשובה על חמאה שנשלחה על ידי עובד כוכבים בלא חותם שמותר בדיעבד ולא חיישינן שמא החליפה בשלו:
**** ENGLISH ****Ploni the sage permitted for me. In Darkei Moshe he wrote: such is its language — an incident came before us regarding Reuven, who fixed butter for Shimon, and Shimon sold much butter in the presumption of kosherness and said Reuven fixed it and gave it all to him in the presumption of kosherness; and Reuven said he did not give it all to him in the presumption of kosherness, but only part of it, and the rest he said explicitly is forbidden; and Shimon contradicts him — and we were lenient in the matter, since gentile butter has many who permit it, and in some places they practice heter with it; but regarding other issurim it appears to me that it is forbidden, for it is similar to "Ploni fixed my vessel for me" — if he said "I did not fix it," it is forbidden; and one should not distinguish specifically where forbidden matter was established, for if so he should have listed fifteen laws and not fourteen; rather one should not distinguish in this — end of the words of Darkei Moshe. And in Derishah he copied the language of Darkei Moshe, who arranged fourteen laws according to Mordechai and Ashiri, and he also copied this incident as though it is from Mordechai and Ashiri; but in truth it is the words of Darkei Moshe. And in my humble opinion it appears one should not be lenient in this, for we say in seif 1 regarding a matter that was already in his hand — he can no longer contradict if he said so the first time; and behold this is the first time; and all the more here, for this certainly should be called "he said," since he attributes the fix to Reuven and Reuven contradicts him; and as the Rosh wrote in the chapter Nezikin: here it is not relevant that the Torah believed one witness like two, since he admits himself that Ploni fixed it and he contradicts him; if so, here there is only a mixture of gentile butter in Jewish butter; and even though in siman 115 seif 1 the Rama wrote that a mixture of butter is permitted — that is specifically with a majority of heter, as we proved there; and here we do not clearly deal with a majority of heter — for if so, even if Shimon had admitted to Reuven he could have permitted it here; and nevertheless it appears we consider Shimon here as negligent, who mixed issur in heter and lies in his words; therefore one should be stringent to forbid all his butter, for this is worse than nullifying issur l'chatchila, mentioned in siman 99 seif 5; such appears in my humble opinion. Afterward the responsum of the Rama was printed, and he wrote there in siman 66 the law of butter, and he sensed this difficulty from the words of the Rosh in the chapter Nezikin; and he wrote on this that it is different there, for the vessel is in the presumption of tevel issur; but where forbidden matter was not established, he is believed — and this is very astonishing, since there is no credibility for this one, since that Ploni contradicts him — what helps here "forbidden matter was not established"? Ultimately the credibility of this owner of the butter goes away, and it is like butter of one who has no credibility; and further, behold the Rosh mentioned there other laws and distinguished among them between whether forbidden matter was established or not — and in this he mentioned nothing; and further the Rama wrote there proof from the chapter HaChalutza: "I converted privately between myself and myself," etc., until "he said to him: according to your words you are a gentile and you are not believed to disqualify your sons" — behold before us; even though the sons have no presumption of kosherness except from their father — nevertheless, since according to his words he is a gentile, he is not believed to disqualify his sons; likewise here with this butter: since according to his words he is only like another and not like the guardian of this butter, he is not believed, since the owners contradict him — end of his words. And I am astonished whether this emerged from the holy mouth, for Rashi explicitly explained in the chapter HaChalutza (Yevamot daf 46): "and testimony of a gentile — since your sons are presumed kosher, you are not believed to disqualify them" — end of his words; learn from this that without their father they would have had presumption of kosherness — examine; therefore it appears to me that here with the butter it is like something found in the hand of one on whom one may not rely. My brother the gaon Mahar"r Yitzchak Levi z"l answered in a responsum on butter that was sent by a gentile without a seal, that it is permitted b'dieved, and we are not concerned that perhaps he exchanged it for his own.
