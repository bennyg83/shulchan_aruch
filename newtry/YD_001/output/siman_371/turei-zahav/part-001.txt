<b>Unless it can stand without support.</b> Tosafot in Bava Batra 19 give two reasons. First, anything that requires external support is merely a temporary closure. Second, the barrier may not depend for support upon an object susceptible to tumah. Even if the barrier itself is not susceptible, if it is held in place by something that is, it is treated as though the barrier itself depended upon a tumah-receiving object. R. Shimshon and Rambam rule similarly in Ohalot. It is obvious, however, that if one fills a window with enough stones or wood that the window walls themselves hold the material in place, that is a valid barrier and is not called external support.

<br><b>An incident occurred</b> where tumah was present in a house adjacent to a synagogue and could pass through a window of the women's section. One halachic authority instructed that the window be closed with a small curtain that hung there permanently on iron hooks for privacy. I said he erred. Ohalot teaches that a sheet or mat interposes against tumah only when it is made and positioned as an actual tent. If merely stretched flat without sloping sides or walls, it does not interpose. Here the curtain merely covers the window and is neither a wall nor an ohel. Furthermore, the curtain is held by iron rings. Rambam rules that if a door is kept shut by a key or by a person supporting it, the house is pure only if the door can stand closed by itself. If not, the supporting person or utensil is the effective barrier, and people and utensils transmit tumah but do not interpose. Tosafot likewise require the barrier to stand by means of something not susceptible to tumah. The iron rings here function like the key. Even if certain rings are not independently susceptible to tumah when detached from their normal use, when joined to the curtain they clearly function as utensils.

There is also reason to say that even a support not susceptible to tumah may fail. Kelim teaches that certain metal door components made for attachment to the ground are not susceptible to tumah. Ohalot nevertheless says that a door supported by a key fails unless the door can stand on its own. Tosafot offer two explanations: either any barrier requiring support is temporary and invalid, or specifically a tumah-receiving support invalidates it. They then cite a commentary holding that even a key not susceptible to tumah invalidates because the barrier is not independently established. Mordechai follows the first explanation and requires the material in the window to stand without any other support. Rama rules this way below. Therefore, a door or window cannot be relied upon as a tumah barrier if it stands only by iron hinges or other iron fittings, even if those fittings are deemed attached to the ground and not susceptible to tumah. According to this, the locking arrangements mentioned later work only where the closure is independently established and does not rely on such supports. One should not rely upon Derishah's leniency of locking the house in order to protect a kohen. The same concern applies to an iron lattice in a window. Since it is iron and not formed as an ohel, it does not create the necessary interposition. Therefore, one should not permit a kohen to remain inside merely by closing the door or window in such a fashion.

<b>הבית טהור והעליה טמאה. </b>לפי שהטומאה עולה כנגדה מאחר שהיא מונחת תחת הארובה והוה כאלו אין שם אהל עליה ואין לה כח לירד ולהחזיר הטומאה לבית מאחר שאין בה פותח טפח כ"פ הרא"ש בהלכות קטנות ואין להקשות יהיה פתח הבית ההוא טמא משום שסוף הטומאה לצאת דרך אותה פתח כמו דאמרינן אחר כך בסי' זה דכאן שאין שם רק כזית מן המת ועליו אין אהל הוה כמונח ברשות הרבים ואינו מטמא כלל בבית כנ"ל פשוט ובחנם האריך על זה בדרישה.) ומזה למדנו שאם יש מת בבית בחדר שאין עליו אהל אין לכהן להיות בכל הבית הן מטעם שהטומאה חוזרת ויורדת הן מטעם שסוף הטומאה לצאת דרך הפתח דלא הקיל הרא"ש כאן אלא אם אין פתח רק פחות מטפח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The house is pure and the upper story is impure.</b> Since the tumah lies directly beneath the opening, it rises upward through that opening as though no roof were present above it. Because the opening is less than one tefach, it has no power to bring the tumah back downward into the house. Rosh explains this in Hilchot Tumah. One need not ask why the doorway of the house is not impure because the tumah will eventually leave through it. Here there is only a kezayit of the deceased and there is no ohel over it, so it is considered like tumah lying in the public domain and does not spread through the house. From this we learn that if a deceased person is in a room without a roofed enclosure, a kohen nevertheless may not remain elsewhere in the house if tumah can spread downward or is destined to leave through the doorway. Rosh's leniency here exists only because the opening involved is less than a tefach.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The house is impure and they are pure.</b> Each room contains only half a kezayit, which is below the minimum measure. The house is nevertheless impure because the two halves are destined to leave through it, and we treat them as though both were in the house. The rooms remain pure because their doors are closed. <b>We do not apply lavud to be stringent.</b> The wording might imply that lavud is applied to be lenient. That cannot be correct, because then tumah could almost never pass through an opening of less than three tefachim, since it would always be treated as sealed. Mahari Weil was asked whether lavud applies regarding tumat met to two adjacent structures separated by less than three tefachim and answered that it does not. Tosafot in Eruvin say simply that lavud is not applied in the laws of tumah, without limiting that rule to stringency. Sukkah likewise implies the same. Therefore, Rama's statement should be understood as saying that lavud is not applied here at all, even where doing so would create a stringency.
