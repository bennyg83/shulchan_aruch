Alternatively, it teaches us that if the rest of the openings of the house were open, then the open houses would also be tamei and everything within them would be tamei, for tumah exits beneath the projections since the end of tumah is to exit, and afterward exits from beneath the projections and enters their open houses.

**** ENGLISH ****"Projecting roofs," etc. — Derishah wrote: it all depends on the roof over the opening of the house containing the dead, and from there tumah comes from the roof before the opening to the roof of the house; and even though the houses are not adjacent to one another but there is a partition between them from the side, even though there is no small roof on it — if the beam resting on it is a tefach wide, when tumah comes from the opening to the roof and the roof encompasses the entire house, tumah is beneath the roof from under that partition, and that partition brings tumah beneath the roof of the adjacent house and it enters within through openings or holes. But when there is no roof over the opening of the house containing the dead, even if it has a roof from the side that overhangs the houses adjacent to it on every side, tumah does not come there. What people are accustomed to say — if it is surrounded by a high wall, that wall makes no difference except regarding the side before the opening; or if there is no high wall before the opening of the house containing the dead and there is a wall in the adjacent house on the side before the opening, so that tumah not be brought from the roof of the dead to beneath the roof before the opening of the adjacent house, even though from the side there is no blocking wall and the roofs overhang one upon the other — we have no concern with this. In my view, there must be a roof of an ohel of a tefach from beginning to end; but whenever there is an interruption by a high wall, even if it is several tefachim thick, since there is no ohel of a tefach, tumah again has no place to go — and many great authorities agreed, and so is the widespread custom.

In my view, the reason of those who are lenient is specifically regarding a dead person placed in a house in the manner the Tur discusses, and likewise in what Mordechai discusses — this applies to a dead person placed on a ship, which is like one placed in a house, which is his fixed place and seizes four amot; but not one placed on a bier. What he wrote "they stand it" — it seems all the more so when they carry it on the bier it does not seize.

<b>ומותר לכהן לעמוד כו'. </b>ואין חילוק בין בית פתוח או סתום לענין זה והב"ח כתב דיש להחמיר דאף בבית סתום אם פרץ פצימתו ומת בתוכו תופס ד' אמות סביביו וצריך להרחיק ארבע אמות מהבית ע"כ וכ"כ הרוקח סימן שט"ו מת בבית סתום אסור לכהן ליקרב בתוך ד' אמות:
**** ENGLISH ****"And a kohen is permitted to stand," etc. — and there is no distinction between an open house or sealed house for this matter. The Bach wrote that one should be stringent: even in a sealed house, if its wall was breached and a dead person is within it, it seizes four amot around it, and one must distance four amot from the house; so too Rokeach siman 315: a dead person in a sealed house — a kohen may not approach within four amot.
