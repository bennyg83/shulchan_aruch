<b>חייב ליתן לכהן.</b> ואין מברכין על נתינה זו כ"כ הפר"ח בשם תשובת הרשב"א והרמב"ן בהשגות לספר המצות וכ"כ בכה"ג והשיג על אא"ז דמש"א שכתב שמברך להפריש מתנות וע"ש שהביא מדבר אברהם בשם תשובת ראב"י אב"ד וכ"כ בתמים דעים סי' קע"ט ובמח"ב הביא מהרוקח סימן שס"ו שדעתו כהדמש"א ומ"מ כתב דיחידאה הוא ועיין בפרי תואר שכתב בשם תשובת הרשב"א סי' של"ח שאין הכהן מברך שהחיינו:
**** ENGLISH ****
Obligated to give to the kohen. One does not recite a blessing on this giving, as Rashba and Ramban in Hagahot on Sefer HaMitzvot and Knesset HaGedolah — he challenged Avraham Azulai who wrote one blesses when separating gifts; see there what he brought from Avraham in responsum Ra'avyah; so too Temim De'im 179; and Peri To'ar in Rashba responsa 338 that the kohen does not recite shehecheyanu.

<b>הלחיים.</b> הוא לחי התחתון הימיני והשמאלי ודלא כתרגום יונתן בן עוזיאל שהוא הלחי התחתון והעליון וליתא ובשו"ת הל' קטנות ח"ב סי' ק"ע הניח הדבר בספק וכתב שלא ראה מעשה במקומו (בירושלים) כי הכל פודין במה שנותנין פרוטות לכהן וכתב במח"ב שבזמנו בארץ ישראל מקיימין המצוה שבמתנות עצמם ונותנים הלחי התחתון והלשון:
**** ENGLISH ****
The cheeks. It is the lower right and left cheek — not as Targum Yonatan ben Uziel (upper and lower) — rejected; in responsum Halakhot Ketanot part 8 siman 170 he left it in doubt and wrote he never saw the practice in Jerusalem where all redeem with coins to the kohen; Machaneh Ephraim wrote in his time in Eretz Yisrael they fulfill the mitzvah with the gifts themselves — lower cheek and tongue.

But the main point per my humble opinion in the essential law regarding terumah and gifts: it is permitted to give to a minor and one fulfills giving thereby; for in terumah when the stack was smoothed and terumah obligation fell upon it, kohanim have a share in it, and kohanim are like partners in that stack, as Tosafot wrote in Sukkah 35 regarding an etrog of tevel that is like partners' etrog; and that which he stated "another's intent acquires" is not precise acquisition — in truth the owner's acquisition is not needed, for automatically he acquires; only he means another's intent acquires works even where acquisition is needed, acquiring through such a courtyard even if not beside it, for his thought makes it a guarded place for the acquirer — and all the more here where acquisition is not needed, only entry into possession suffices through the homeowner's thought removing from his possession and bringing to their possession — it counts as their possession even if they are not beside it; and by giving to him it becomes clear this is his share the Torah granted; but if the minor has a father and we say he needs no acquisition for himself only through "his hand is like his father's hand," or even an adult supported at his father's table — for acquisition his hand is like his father's — if so, initially the Torah did not grant this one to take a share in this tithe, since the right is to the poor and this one's hand is like his father's and his father is wealthy — he does not acquire for himself and his father acquires from him; therefore necessarily from another's intent acquiring — namely the homeowner — and his intent is to give to the minor himself who is poor and his father acquires from him — one may say the owner's intent does not help here, for it is not his, he has only benefit of use to give to a poor person not a rich one; therefore even if he stipulated on condition his father has no right in it — it does not help, since at the time the poor acquired through the Merciful One's statement this one was not included in the acquisition, only other poor people; therefore regarding ma'aser ani of money, since the poor acquired immediately when he separated — it is not in his power to take their portion and give to this one who is not poor at that hour — and the homeowner's will and intent do not help at all.
