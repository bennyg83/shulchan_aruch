"And he lifted it and the wine went out etc." Beit Yosef in siman 124 brought these words of Rambam and wrote that they are from what they said in perek R. Yishmael — non-Jew adna, Yisrael akova — forbidden, for when wine comes it comes from the ko'ach of the non-Jew; and it appears to Rambam that specifically when the non-Jew lifts the vat and pours the wine, but if he does not lift it but only tilts it on its side and pours the wine into another vessel it is permitted; and likewise it appears from Ran who wrote that he takes the barrel in his hand etc.; and afterward he brought the words of Rosh who also wrote — evidently the essence of libation is in lifting and pouring etc. I wonder — for above in siman 123 Tur and Rama wrote that if a non-Jew treads wine, even though he did not touch with his feet, wine that comes from his ko'ach is forbidden; and this is an explicit gemara in perek R. Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60) — in his ko'ach all disagree that it is forbidden, and there he did not lift anything at all. And further — what he wrote in siman 124 seif 14, that if the tap does not pass through all the walls the wine that goes out is forbidden because of the ko'ach of the non-Jew — and there is no lifting; and Darkei Moshe wrote on this challenge, and one may say that perhaps there it is different, for it is his manner thus to remove wine from the barrel — until here; and this is nothing — for if the manner of libation is only in lifting specifically, what do we care for the manner of removing wine thus? And in any event there is no manner of libation in this; and even if this answer is true, the first challenge is not resolved, for there wine is not relevant at all until after it comes from his ko'ach. Maharach wrote that in removing the tap the reason is not because of his ko'ach but we consider it as if he touched the wine through something else — and he was not precise, for Tur and Shulchan Aruch explicitly wrote that it is his ko'ach if it does not pass through all the walls; and in truth every contact through something else is called ko'ach, as Beit Yosef wrote explicitly in siman 124. And further — we find explicitly in Tosafot perek R. Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60): one should be stringent with people when they tilt the vat that has a little wine to one side, that a non-Jew should not tilt it etc., because of the ko'ach of the non-Jew; and likewise in Hagahot Asheri; and if there is a little wine in the barrel that must be lifted from one side — forbidden etc.; and likewise in Mordechai; and likewise the import of Tur's words here, that he wrote the law of the ko'ach of a non-Jew who pours from vessel to vessel and did not mention that lifting is required. And further proof from what Mordechai wrote in the name of HaRif, which Rama brought nearby, that if the non-Jew shakes the hand of the Jew it is forbidden where it would not have flowed without this, even though he did not lift anything; and what Beit Yosef brought as proof from that Rambam mentions here "he lifted" — it appears because of the end he mentions it, that he concluded there "he lifted and did not agitate and did not touch — permitted"; and therefore he also mentions at the beginning "he lifted" — that even though he lifted, agitation is required; and therefore one must say thus: if lifting specifically forbids, why did he mention at the end that if he did not lift, even though nitzok is permitted — and this is a greater novelty than what he wrote "he lifted" at the end and did not agitate and did not touch — permitted, that is without pouring; and this is proof for the explanation of Rambam's words that I mentioned, that there is no answer to them and one cannot challenge from what Rambam wrote chapter 12 of Avodah Zarah: a non-Jew who touched wine and did not intend for this wine is permitted in benefit — how so? For example he fell on a wineskin of wine, or he reached his hand to a barrel intending it was oil and it was wine and the wine came from his ko'ach without intention — since he did not touch the wine it is permitted for drinking — how so? For example he lifted a barrel of wine and poured to another vessel and he thought it was beer — behold this is permitted, end of his words — we learn that we require lifting for a stringency, for in a leniency he said thus — even if he lifted and poured it is permitted in this. And what Beit Yosef brought from Ran — plainly there is no proof from there that Ran intended only to reveal that the non-Jew did not intend — the non-Jew drew from the vessel, as Beit Yosef himself wrote; and that which he mentions "the non-Jew took the barrel in his hand and pours" is not specifically thus but he mentions the usual manner, that the manner is to take in his hands when he pours; and what he brought as proof from Rosh who mentioned lifting and pouring in haste — he did not examine this well, for Rosh challenged Rabbeinu Tam who says the pouring of the non-Jew forbids only because of his ko'ach — and that is for drinking specifically — why should it not be full libation since there is lifting and pouring as one who libates fully? And on this he resolved that not in his presence it is not the manner of libation — until here there is no issur except because of his ko'ach; and in truth in his ko'ach we do not require even lifting, for the issur of his ko'ach is because of "go, go we say to the nazir" etc., as Rashi explained in perek R. Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60); and if so, what is this that Beit Yosef wrote that from Rosh's words it appears there is no manner of libation without lifting — do we forbid because of libation? We forbid it only for drinking and it is because of his ko'ach and we do not care about libation — until here it appears Beit Yosef did not write these words except as pilpul and not to establish the halachah thus; and therefore he did not establish thus in Shulchan Aruch, and Rama who emended here at the end of the seif "or that he did not lift the vessel" did not trouble to examine fully but relied on what Beit Yosef wrote; and one should not be lenient at all here in the ko'ach of a non-Jew even without lifting, for nevertheless there is ko'ach of a non-Jew here — thus it appears to me plainly:

"And some say we do not forbid etc." Beit Yosef likewise in the name of Rashi, and he challenged from what Tosafot and Rosh wrote — the incident of Rashi with a non-Jew who poured into the cup etc. — we learn that the pitcher is forbidden because of nitzok to the ko'ach of the non-Jew; and it appears to me that one who examines Asheri plainly has no difficulty, for he did not write this in the name of an incident but wrote: Rashbam wrote in the name of Rashi that one may learn from here that even according to those who say nitzok is connection, nitzok bar nitzok is not connection etc.; and if so, if the non-Jew emptied wine from a pitcher to a cup and the wine in the cup is forbidden because of nitzok etc., and if afterward a Jew drew from the barrel to the pitcher, what is in the barrel is permitted, for it is nitzok bar nitzok, end of his words — behold all these matters follow those who say nitzok is connection, and that is the view of Raavad and his companions who hold nitzok is connection in all places even in the ko'ach of a non-Jew; and that of Rav Sheshet who permits nitzok to the ko'ach of a non-Jew is a mere rejection, as Beit Yosef wrote in his name — automatically there is nitzok to the ko'ach of a non-Jew, nevertheless what is in the barrel is permitted for all, for it is bar nitzok; and this is the main novelty to permit bar nitzok even for one who holds there is nitzok in the ko'ach of a non-Jew; and likewise in Tosafot the intent is thus — they first wrote "even for one who says nitzok is connection," and near this they wrote "an incident came before Rashi" etc., when the wine in the cup was forbidden — meaning, for that view it is forbidden, but in any event what is in the barrel is permitted as I mentioned; but Rashi himself holds that even nitzok is permitted in this:

"And even if the non-Jew was able by himself to pour etc." Explanation: and the Jew is not able by himself to pour — thus Rashba in Teshuvot HaRashba; and on this Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that even a non-Jew assists the Jew — why is the non-Jew made primary etc. Explanation: even though the non-Jew has greater force, nevertheless now that the Jew also bears in his force, who will tell us that in that force needed for this carrying the non-Jew's share is greater than the Jew's, since in this carrying both have force? Ran too ruled to be lenient when the non-Jew is able and the Jew is not — and all this is specifically b'dieved, but l'chatchila it is forbidden:

"Forbidden even in benefit."
