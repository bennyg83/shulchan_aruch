(סימן קכ"ה סעיף א') נאסר בשתיה. לכאורה נראה ראיה לשיטה זו מההיא עובדא דינוקא דמתיר לדרוך בציירנא לידיה. והא מ"מ הוי כחו מחמת הדריכה כדאמרינן במעצרתא ואסור הכל מטעם ניצוק בכחו. אע"כ דכחו אינו אסור רק בשתיה ולהרשב"א דס"ל דכח א"ב וס"ל ג"כ דאמרינן בכחו ניצוק ג"כ ניחא דהא ס"ל דכל ניצוק אין בו איסור הנאה דהוי רק כמעורב דמותר חוץ מדמי יי"נ שבו אך להראב"ד דס"ל דניצוק א"ב קשה לכאורה וצ"ל דכיון דהישראל דורך עמו הוי כח ישראל וכח עובד כוכבים מעורבים אולם לא ידענא בכה"ג שדורכים על גוף הענבים כדאמרינן והא נגע ברגל אם נקרא כח ישראל וכח עובד כוכבים מעורבים דהא מ"מ באותו מקום שדורך העובד כוכבים מאותם הענבים זב היין מכחו לבד ולכאורה לא מקרי מעורב רק ששניהם עירו ביחד או שהטעינו הקורה על הענבים ביחד וצ"ע לדינא והנה לדעת הרמב"ם דכחו אסור בהנאה כמ"ש הה"מ תמוה לי מאד מדברי הרמב"ם והוא לשון המחבר לעיל (סי' קכ"ג) עובד כוכבים שדרך וכו' אסור בשתיה וצע"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 125 seif 1) Forbidden for drinking. It appears there is proof for this view from that incident of the child who permits treading in the press at his hand; and nevertheless it is his ko'ach because of the treading, as we say in Me'itzarta, and everything is forbidden because of nitzok in his ko'ach; one must say that his ko'ach is forbidden only for drinking; and for Rashba who holds that ko'ach is stam yeinam and also holds that we say nitzok in his ko'ach — it works out well, for he holds that all nitzok has no issur of benefit, being only as mixed, permitted except for the value of stam yeinam in it; but for Raavad who holds that nitzok is stam yeinam it is difficult at first glance, and one must say: since the Jew treads with him it is ko'ach of a Jew and ko'ach of a non-Jew mixed; however I do not know in such a case where they tread on the body of the grapes, as we say — and he touched with his foot — whether it is called ko'ach of a Jew and ko'ach of a non-Jew mixed, for nevertheless in that place where the non-Jew treads from those grapes wine drips from his ko'ach alone, and at first glance it is not called mixed except that both of them poured together, or that they loaded the beam onto the grapes together — requires study for the halachah; and behold according to Rambam that his ko'ach is forbidden in benefit, as he wrote in Mishneh Torah — I am very puzzled by the words of Rambam, and it is the language of the Mechaber above (siman 123): non-Jew who treads etc. forbidden for drinking — and requires great study:

**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 6) "And if a Jew assists." In the source of the law in Teshuvot HaRashba and Teshuvot HaRosh it appears to me for practical halachah that it is explained that this is when there is no wine in the winepress, but it is forbidden from concern for wine on the grapes in the small tubs that substitutes for pure wine — in this, if a Jew assists, it is permitted, since even in pure wine by law it is permitted through ko'ach of non-Jew and Jew mixed in it; but in wine in a pit that is forbidden according to Ramban lest he touch with his hand the wine in the pit — in this there is no advantage in the Jew assisting — see there; and if so the Mechaber who mentions a winepress in which there is wine — it appears he holds like Ramban, and on this he says "and if a Jew assists" — requires study:
