**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of mourning on Shabbos. It contains 2 seifim: Shabbos does not interrupt mourning and counts toward the count of seven, for behold some laws of mourning apply on it, namely private matters, which are marital relations and bathing; but public matters are not permitted, namely to remove his wrapping (and specifically when he is wrapped in Ishmaelite wrappings as explained above siman 386; but a bit of wrapping that they practice in some places all thirty — he need not remove on Shabbos, since he has shoes on his feet) (Mahariv siman 5), and to wear his shoes, and to set upright the bed from its inversion, and not to wear a torn garment but to change it, and if he has nothing to change into he returns the tear to behind him; and Torah study is a private matter; but to return to the portion — since a person is obligated to complete his portions with the congregation, it is like reading Shema and is permitted; and if they called the mourner to ascend to the Torah, he must ascend, for if he refrained it would be a public matter; and Rabbeinu Tam — they would call him every third time, and mourning occurred to him and the chazzan did not call him, and he ascended by himself and said: since he was accustomed to be called third every Shabbos, one who sees that he does not ascend says that because of mourning he refrained — and it is a public matter. (And likewise if the kohen is a mourner and there is no other kohen in the synagogue — it is permitted to call him, but for another matter it is forbidden) (Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 3, Laws of Mourning).

**** ENGLISH ****
If an animal dragged him or bandits killed him, and on Shabbos he despaired of seeking — since private matters apply on Shabbos, it counts for him as one day: {Rama: If the thirtieth day of mourning fell on Shabbos and the twenty-ninth on the eve of Shabbos, it is permitted for him to bathe on the eve of Shabbos even in places where they practice not to bathe all thirty, for since by law it is permitted after seven but they practiced to be stringent all thirty — in this manner it is permitted because of the honor of Shabbos (Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Maharam chapter 10 and chapter 6, Laws of Mourning); and the same applies to anything similar, such as to return to his place on Friday night and to wear Shabbos garments, for these too are only a mere custom (so it appears from the words of Maharash), as explained above siman 389 and 390; and even though one could distinguish between bathing that is forbidden on Shabbos and these matters that he can do on Shabbos — nevertheless it does not appear to me to distinguish in this.}
