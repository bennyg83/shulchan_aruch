<b>א) [סעיף א'] יין ובשר וכו'.  </b>בגמ' ע"ז לט. איתא גם תכלת דצריך ב' חותמות. ובש"ע השמיעו לפי שאין תכלת מצוי בזמן הזה. כדאי' במדרש תנחומא ס"פ שלח. וכ"כ מנחת יעקב (כלל לב או' כב). וידוע שבדור שלפנינו המציא הגאון ר' גרשון חנוך מראדזין החלזון שצובעים ממנו התכלת. ונחלקו עליו גדולי דורו. ומהם הגאון מקוענא בשו"ת ישועות מלכי (חאו"ח סי' ב). ורמז כן בקצרה בערוך השלחן או"ח (סי' ט אות יב). וע' בשו"ת בשמים ראש (סי' רמד). וע"ע בס' שלחן מלכים (דר"י ע"א). ואכמ"ל.

A) [Seif 1] Wine and meat, etc.—in the Gemara, Avodah Zarah 39, tekhelet also requires two seals; in Shulchan Aruch it was omitted because tekhelet is not found in our time, as in Midrash Tanchuma, parashat Shelach; and so Minchat Yaakov (klal 32, letter 22). It is known that in the generation before ours the Gaon R' Gershon Henoch of Radzin produced the chilazon from which tekhelet is dyed, and the great ones of his generation disputed him, including the Gaon of Koenigsberg in Yeshuot Malki (Orach Chaim, siman 2), and he hinted briefly in Arukh HaShulchan Orach Chaim (siman 9, letter 12); see responsum Basheilim Rosh (siman 244); and see in Sefer Shulchan Melachim (Darkei Yosef 1a); and understand.

B) And meat—even with majority of Israelite slaughterers we require seal within seal for meat, since the Mechaber ruled (siman 63) that meat lost from sight is forbidden—Shach s.k. 1, Peri Chadash s.k. 1, Lechem HaPanim s.k. 1, Erekh Shai. However, at first glance it is not so from the Rashba (siman 466) who wrote—and know in a city of Israelite slaughterers and majority of sellers selling slaughtered meat and also nevelah meat found there in a non-Jew's hand is permitted; nevertheless it seems one does not send l'chatchila meat via a non-Jew without a seal, etc., even though b'dieved one may permit when found in a non-Jew's hand, end of his words—brought in Bedek HaBayit; and so noted in Sefer Chadarim Da'ah. One may say the Rashba follows his view disputing the Rambam and permitting when found in a non-Jew's hand like our Gemara (Chullin 95)—unlike the Mechaber who ruled like the Rambam that even then forbidden, therefore we require seal within seal; but in Knesset Yehudah he wrote to reject the Peri Chadash—for since from the Torah we follow the majority and the prohibition is only d'rabbanan, one seal suffices; therefore he established the Mechaber's words only where there is no majority of Israelite slaughterers—see there; and so Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 2; and so in Minchat Chinuch (klal 32, s.k. 52); and so from the Perishah, letter 11—see there; and see in Shach below s.k. 25.

**** ENGLISH ****
E) Via a non-Jew—and it seems this means specifically an idol-worshipping gentile; but Ishmaelites who are not idol worshippers—permitted to send via them or deposit with them wine with one seal, for the Mechaber rules like the Rif, Rambam, and Rashba that Torah prohibitions need seal within seal and rabbinic ones one seal; the Rashba wrote: even though stam yeinam is only d'rabbanan, they were stringent like Torah because of affection of libation—they trouble and forge one seal, end of his words; therefore with Ishmaelites, where affection of libation does not apply, one seal suffices. Even if one says perhaps he bought from another idol-worshipping gentile who libated—in Ishmaelite wine too one could say so, yet we are not concerned to forbid benefit, as below (siman 124, seif 6); see Kenesset HaGadol (there, Hagahat Beit Yosef letter 19); see Avodah Zarah (64) and below (siman 128); therefore the same here; and so from most Acharonim brought below s.k. 26.

E) And greater: we find the Taz, s.k. 4, wrote that in our time there is no affection of libation and wine suffices with one seal—see there; and Minchat Yaakov (klal 32, letter 32) agreed; and in his responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1 (siman 63); and the conditions of those who support them: Or Zarua (chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, siman 160) and Rokeach (siman 496) wrote that in our time, when gentiles do not libate to idolatry, one need not seal within seal, as we say regarding vinegar, etc.—see there; and so the Meiri (Avodah Zarah 31), regarding one who deposited his wine, in the name of some later rabbis who permitted wine even for drinking via one seal, because in these days libation is not common to them as in those times, end of his words (the publishing rabbi, letter 3, did not mention the above); see Arukh HaShulchan s.k. 13 that one should rule like the Taz; and it is true all this does not suffice for the Mechaber's view that we accepted his rulings that wine needs seal within seal (and it seems per his reason he forbids stam yeinam in our time even for benefit in siman 123—unlike dissenters); and so explicitly in responsum Chakrei Lev, part 1, Yoreh Deah (siman 199): from the poskim who ruled even in our time two seals for wine; and again brought the Rashba in Torat HaBayit (141a): even though gentiles today are not idol worshippers, nevertheless since some among them libate, we require seal within seal; Beit Yosef brought it (siman 128) and concluded even b'dieved forbidden; and so the Rama's hagahah there; he concluded: nevertheless to permit in great loss one may rely on this great authority—see there; and see responsum Yismach Lev, Gagin (Yoreh Deah, siman 8); in Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 4 he wrote explicitly unlike the Taz; and so in Sefer Erekh Shai—see there; nevertheless with Ishmaelites it seems there is less concern; and I do not deny I saw in Or Sameach (1 Kings 5:32) he explained the Rambam there to forbid drinking when he transgressed and deposited with an Ishmaelite with one seal—see there; but in my opinion one may rely and be lenient on all the above; however l'chatchila one should heed Or Sameach to require two seals even with Ishmaelites; see Lechem Mishneh (1 Kings 5:32); see responsum Tashbetz, part 3 (siman 89); responsum Bnei Binyamin (siman 6); Sefer Ginzei Chaim (maarechet 8, end of letter 91)—and understand.

G) But cooked wine—in Beit Yosef below (siman 123) he brought the Rashba in Torat HaBayit: what is cooked? Whatever was boiled is cooked, and so received from the Geonim; some say it is not called cooked until its measure diminishes through cooking; it seems impossible to boil without diminishing measure, and the Geonim's reception decides, end of his words; and so the Ran; also the Rosh agreed with the Ra'avad in the name of the Geonim that if boiled it is certainly cooked; and so in Sefer HaTerumah; and so the Tur ruled there: from when is it called cooked—from when it boiled on the fire; the Shach wrote there s.k. 7 that this means its measure diminishes through boiling, end of his words; and so ruled Or Zarua (chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, siman 215); responsum Tashbetz, part 1 (siman 29); Orchot Chaim Yoreh Deah (page 247); and more; the Geonim's words in Sha'arei Teshuvah (siman 258)—see there. However, Kenesset HaGadol (siman 123, Hagahat Beit Yosef letter 14) wrote in the name of the Riaz: the measure of cooked wine is whatever is not drunk as wine [and see Kenesset HaGadol there letter 50 regarding vinegar]. However, Shulchan Gavoah (there s.k. 7) wrote the Riaz's view is nullified against all these rabbis who hold whatever was boiled on the fire, however much, is considered cooked; brought to law in Sefer Einei David (chapter 6, page 3) and Zikhronenu LeChaim, part 2 (folio 31b)—see there. We find the Rambam (chapter 6 of Issurei Mizbeach, law 9): cooked on fire until its taste changed through cooking (unfit for libation); therefore it is proven that even for stam yeinam prohibition we require cooking until taste changes; as written (chapter 1 of Mishneh Torah, law 9)—see there; and so proved in Mishor Ba'ahalei Teshuvah (siman 123, s.k. 5) and R' Menuchah—see there; and so the Meiri (Avodah Zarah 29:) wrote: the end of the matter in this topic—in my opinion whatever is cooked until its taste changed from what it was, etc., its charm is nullified and there is no affection of libation on it, end of his words; the publishing rabbi noted the Rambam's words there—see there; and so for law in Sefer Kikar Le'aden (folio 162a) in the name of Maharam of Lunzano: what is written 'cooked wine has no stam yeinam' is not ordinary cooking but extensive cooking until taste changed in cooking—brought in Sefer Ikrei HaDat (siman 13, letter 13); and Maharach Palaggi in Ruach Chaim (siman 123, s.k. 2) explained the Riaz's view—that is also until taste changed from wine taste—see there; see Sefer Vayomer Yitzchak (folio 114b); we found accordingly companions to the Riaz, unlike Shulchan Gavoah that his view is nullified against all poskim and he did not mention the Rambam's view; and so the Meiri and R' Menuchah—as said; and so ruled Ben Ish Chai, part 2 (parashat Balak, letter 7); see Sefer Beit Aharon Krispin (folio 44b); responsum Shevitat Yom Tov (Yoreh Deah, siman 1-4); see Kisei Eliyahu (siman 123, s.k. 2)—and understand. For law, in my opinion l'chatchila one should heed the Rambam and his camp and require seal within seal as long as wine has not reached the measure that taste changed; but b'dieved one should not be stringent, since many poskim hold boiling alone suffices; and so is the Mechaber's view we accepted; therefore if there was only one seal one may be lenient.
