<b>That was presumed wormy.</b> Ba'er Hetev in the name of the Maharshal. And this is in the chapter Elu Treifot siman 100, and the Taz brought it s.k. 17; and there he concludes with it — and it appears specifically regarding something in which infestation is common, but he did not inspect as required we say so; but regarding something in which infestation is not common, of which we say it fell from elsewhere, we are not concerned more than these that were found — end of his words. His intent is clear: regarding something in which infestation is not common, even if three were found it is permitted — even that cooked dish; and so understood the Magen Avraham in Orach Chayyim siman 467 s.k. 12, and he rejected for this reason the words of the Kol Bo there in the name of the Raavad, if three grains were found in a cooked dish. And see in Chak Yosef there s.k. 25, who wrote that the Maharshal's words are not compelling and this distinction is not found in any posek — see there. And see in the responsum of Makom Shmuel siman 64 and siman 66 ot 25, who wrote that the Maharshal's intent is not as they understood — that even that cooked dish is permitted even if three were found; rather he means regarding forbidding that entire species because it was presumed wormy — for this he distinguishes that regarding something that comes from elsewhere we do not forbid that entire species; but certainly this cooked dish in which the worms were found we forbid with three, even if it came from elsewhere — see there.

And what the Ba'er Hetev wrote in the name of the Taz regarding an incident, etc. — see in the Peri Megadim, who wrote that if only one woman found it in what she cooked, we say "here it was found and here it was," etc.; but if three people or more bought and mealworms were found in three, one must forbid also in the rest of the cooked dishes, etc. And what the Taz wrote that those who bought afterward are forbidden does not mean they bought after the woman bought — for that is certainly permitted even if found afterward also with the seller; for what is in the woman's hand we say it formed there by force of "here it was found, here it was," and with the seller we say perhaps they formed afterward, etc.; rather he means they bought after the mealworms were found with the seller — end of his words.

And behold, what is written "we say here it was found," etc. — see in the responsum of Chavot Yair siman 109, who wrote there that in this we do not say "here it was found, here it was," because specifically regarding something that comes from elsewhere we say so. And see in the responsum of Tiferet Tzvi, Yoreh De'ah siman 6, who wrote that certainly the correct view is with the Chavot Yair in this, as is proven from a woman who is rendered impure retroactively even though she goes from place to place — see there.

The Chavot Yair further wrote there that ordinary vessels designated for flour have dry flour stuck to them; therefore they can rely on it, and even if the woman said it was clean the other women can tell her "we do not believe you" — see there.

And what the Ba'er Hetev further wrote, and the Peri Chadash disagrees, etc. — see in the responsum of Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 49, who also disagrees with the Taz and forbids even what was bought before the mealworm was known; and he wrote that thus ruled the sages of Poznan (and according to the Peri Megadim, one who rules to permit b'di'eved and Simchat Yom Tov — one may not protest) — and see there in siman 57 in the middle of the responsum, who wrote there that specifically if it was only a short hour, for it is far from reason to say that in a short hour they formed; but in our case of four days from erev Shabbat until Monday they interrupt — all agree to the Taz that it is possible to say they formed in the interim and at the time of baking they were not yet there. (And it appears that what he cited "four days they interrupt" is because the incident was so; but otherwise even in half an erev Shabbat or less we rely that they formed afterward — and so too in the responsum of Chavot Yair siman 29, that all agree to the Taz when there is a time interval of six or seven hours between them, all the more a full night — see there.)

And he further wrote in the responsum of Chavot Yair there: regarding something where several cooked dishes were bought from one person — dried fruits — and three women who bought inspected and each found only one, or do we combine all three together and it is presumed wormy? It appears that since it can be said they grew in the buyers' houses, perforce if no more are found after they inspect well in sunlight we do not establish a prohibition; but after three were found, since they were already established as forbidden with the buyer, further inspection and washing will not help, for the senses testify that cleaning is impossible; and especially dried fruits that have cracks and the worms are small, so that eyesight tires to look at them only with great effort — and they have no remedy except to sell them little by little to a non-Jew (and according to what is written above s.k. 3 it is forbidden to do so); nevertheless a scrupulous person should be stringent for himself after three were found, and it is possible it was already so with the seller — see there.
