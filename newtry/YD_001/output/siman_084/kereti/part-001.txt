And also sometimes the nature of the worm is not to move from the fruit at all, and it adheres to it with utmost adhesion and will not swarm because of cold water — and it itself is forbidden even if it did not go outside, for it is attached; but after twelve months, if the worm's nature is not to move from the fruit, it is obvious it is permitted, for there is no separation; and if its nature is to move and we are concerned it will separate into the cooked dish — if so, even with cold water it will separate; and with pouring hot water we do not rely when there are many worms, only if through cold water they diminished — and if so it is a double doubt: perhaps nothing remained after cold water, and even if you say something remained, perhaps it died in the hot water; but this does not enter the doubt of "perhaps there was none at all," for certainly there are such species.

**** ENGLISH ****
A woman after whose inspection [a sheretz was found] — and Maharshal's view is not to rely in these inspections on women; and Rama wrote that practice is to rely on them; but truly now one should rely more on women's inspection than on men, who are hasty and businessmen do not supervise so much, and common sense attests. And truly to eat raw vegetables such as salad and the like — behold here is a Torah doubt, for nullification does not apply, for one can check two and three times until one finds the worm; and if so it is a Torah doubt, and Heaven forbid to be lenient; and it is hard for me to say whether to trust women or men hasty in their work. But regarding cooked vegetables one may rely, for if they checked at least their inspection suffices to remove certainty that there is swarming there — only a doubt; and if so it is a double doubt: whether any remained after inspection or not; and even if you say some remained, perhaps it dissolved. And also after cooking one cannot recognize the worm or permit it; and from the Torah it is nullified, only d'rabbanan a definite item is not nullified — and if so one may be lenient regarding a d'rabbanan matter. And such is my practice from the day I reached understanding — not to eat raw vegetables from salads and the like relying on the aforementioned inspection; and so is fitting for every Torah scholar, for the stumbling block is great.
