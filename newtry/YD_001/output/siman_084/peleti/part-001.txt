אבל אסור לשאוב בכלי וכו' דע כי הרב ב"י כתב וז"ל והיכי שעירה משקין שבו השרצים בכלי לכלי אחר נראה דכלי שני דין כלי ראשון ישלו וכשפירשו על דופני שבפנים כיון שלא פירשו שם אלא עם משקה היינו רבותי' ואף שמדברי הרא"ש גבי בורות נראה שאם שאב מים בכלי ופי' לדופני הכלי אסורין אפשר דהתם שאני דמעיקרא היה בבור ועכשיו בכלי אבל הכא דמעיקרא כלי ועכשיו כלי שייך שפיר למימר היינו רביתי' עכ"ל ונמשכו הש"ך והפר"ח אחריו ולכך פירשו הא דכתב רמ"א דאסור לשאוב בכלי פירושו מבור אבל מכלי לכלי מותר והפר"ח תמה על רמ"א שסתם דבריו ע"ש: ואני לא הבנתי הקושיא דלא ידענו הבדל בין בור לכלי ובין כלי לכלי רק זה יש הבדל בין עירה מכלי לכלי ובין שואב מתוך כלי קטן לגדול דודאי בדופני כלי בפני' מקום שיש המשקין מיקרי היינו רביתי' אף שלפעמים רוחש התולע למעלם מן משקין מ"מ כך רביתא וטבע מלמעלה למטה וזהו רביתא אבל אם פי' לדופני הכלי מבחוץ במקום שאין משקין כלל בזו לא שייך היינו רביתא ואסור ולכך כשמער' מכלי לכלי א"כ הרחש דבתוך הכלי ירחיש על הדופן למעלה ממשקין זהו רביתא ולרחוש לחוץ לדופן השני כולי האי לא חיישינן אבל השואב א"כ דופן החיצון בא לתוך המשקין ונדבק ונרחשו עליו הרוחשין וכשמוציא כלי לחוץ הרי הרחש על הדופן הכלי מבחוץ בלי משקין ולכך חיישינן שירחיש מבחוץ לפנים אבל לא מבפני' לחוץ כי אין דרכו להתרחק מן משקין וא"ש וזהו ברור ולכך סתם הרמ"א דאסור לשאוב אפי' מכלי לכלי דלערות מותר אבל לשאוב אסור וזהו ברור ונכון לענ"ד וכן משמע קצת בט"ז ע"ש ס"ק ה' ע"ש:

But it is forbidden to draw with a vessel, etc. Know that the Beit Yosef wrote as follows: How when one pours liquid that has sheratzim in it from one vessel to another — it appears that a second vessel has the law of a first vessel. And when they separated onto the inner walls, since they did not separate there except together with the liquid — meaning their place of growth. And although from the words of the Rosh regarding cisterns it appears that if one drew water in a vessel and they separated onto the walls of the vessel they are forbidden — it is possible that it is different there, for originally they were in a cistern and now they are in a vessel; but here, since originally they were in a vessel and now they are in a vessel, it is properly said that this is their place of growth — end of his words. And the Shach and Peri Chadash followed him; therefore they explained what the Rama wrote — that it is forbidden to draw with a vessel — that its meaning is from a cistern, but from vessel to vessel it is permitted. And the Peri Chadash wondered at the Rama for being terse in his words — see there.

And I did not understand the difficulty — for we know no difference between a cistern and a vessel, or between vessel and vessel. There is only this difference between pouring from vessel to vessel and drawing from a small vessel into a large one. For certainly on the inner walls of a vessel in a place where there is liquid it is called their place of growth — even though sometimes the worm crawls above the liquid, nevertheless this is its place of growth, and its nature is from above to below; and this is place of growth. But if it separated to the outer walls of the vessel in a place where there is no liquid at all — in this case the term "place of growth" does not apply, and it is forbidden.

Therefore when one pours from vessel to vessel — the crawling creature inside the vessel will crawl on the wall above the liquid; this is place of growth. And for it to crawl outward onto the second wall — we are not concerned about that at all. But when one draws — the outer wall comes into the liquid, sheratzim stick to it and crawl upon it; and when one removes the vessel outside, behold the crawling creature is on the outer wall of the vessel without liquid — therefore we are concerned lest it crawl from outside to inside, but not from inside to outside, for it is not its way to move away from liquid. And this is clear.

Therefore the Rama was terse that it is forbidden to draw even from vessel to vessel — to pour is permitted, but to draw is forbidden; and this is clear and correct in my opinion, and so it is implied somewhat in the Taz — see there — s.k. 5 — see there.

That are not flowing — some forbid and some permit. The dispute is explained in the Gemara: since it is written "in water, in water" — one tanna holds this is not kelal u'prat but ribuy u'miyut, and therefore it included grooves and spikes even though they are not flowing, for prohibition; and one tanna holds like the tanna of the house of Rav: wherever you find two generalizations that are adjacent, remove the specification between them and interpret them as kelal u'prat. And even in this — what does "water" specify? Flowing water — all flowing water; but those that are not flowing are permitted. And in this the poskim disagreed whether the halachah follows the tanna of the house of Rav or the two tannaim of Rabbi Yishmael; and the Rambam ruled like the tanna of the house of Rabbi Yishmael stringently, to forbid even those that are not flowing.

And the Peri Chadash challenged: behold in Bechoros in the chapter "Yesh bekhor" we say in the mishnah that one does not redeem with land and slaves and not with documents, and Rabbi disagrees that everything may be redeemed except documents; and we establish the dispute — Rabbi holds like the tanna of the house of Rabbi Yishmael, that two adjacent generalizations are interpreted as ribuy u'miyut; Rabbi holds everything, and what excludes documents; and the tanna of the mishnah holds like the tanna of the house of Rav — remove the specification between them and exclude land, slaves, and documents — see there. And accordingly, the Rambam and all poskim who ruled that one does not redeem with land and slaves — infer that they hold we interpret them as kelal u'prat and not ribuy u'miyut; if so, likewise regarding grooves and spikes we should also require flowing water, for from "in water, in water" we derive the above regarding bekhor — and a worthy difficulty applies to him.

And one could answer regarding this that perhaps the Rambam did not decide the matter of halachah — with whom do we rule — whether in such a case we expound kelal u'prat or ribuy u'miyut; and therefore he ruled in all places stringently: regarding pidyon haBen he ruled stringently that one does not redeem with land — perhaps we expound kelal u'prat; and regarding permitting water-sheratzim we go stringently — perhaps we expound ribuy u'miyut — even grooves that are not flowing are forbidden; and we go stringently in every direction.

And in my novellae I said that it already appears from Tosafos in the first chapter of Shevuos, fol. 4b, s.v. yatzu shetarot — that which is implied here that selling applies to documents, since it was necessary to specifically exclude documents in the verse — perforce it appears that Tosafos hold that if by Torah law selling does not apply to documents, how is pidyon possible? For the ordinary case is that one gave the Kohen a document that he has against another for redemption money; and if Torah acquisition does not apply, it is obvious there is no redemption in it. (And see my novellae in Choshen Mishpat siman 60, where I wrote approaches that there are documents in which Torah sale can apply; but nevertheless the plain meaning implies ordinary documents, and if there is no Torah sale in them the verse would not need to exclude — and as Tosafos wrote.)

And behold Tosafos in Bava Basra 56b, s.v. katni, etc., wrote: and if you ask — why is Torah acquisition not effected also for the lien of land written in it, for land is acquired by money, document, and possession, etc.; and one can answer — just as one cannot dedicate it, as proven in the chapter "Kol sha'ah," since it is not in his possession, so too one cannot effect Torah acquisition of them, etc. — see there. And accordingly it works out for the one who says a person does not sell or dedicate something not in his possession; but for Rabbeinu Tam and his colleagues who hold a person dedicates and sells something that has not yet come into existence — and all the more something not in his possession — it is obvious he can sell documents of Torah law. And we say in Yevamos 93a that Rabbi is like R' Meir, that a person sells something that will come — if so, for Rabbi Torah sale of documents is possible, like Tosafos' difficulty; and properly the verse was needed to exclude documents. And similarly for the tanna of the mishnah one must say he expounded kelal u'prat and we do not say he holds document sale is not d'oraita and the verse would not need to exclude documents — for an anonymous mishnah is R' Meir, and R' Meir holds a person can acquire something that will come; therefore we say they disagree about kelal u'prat and ribuy u'miyut. But for us, who hold a person does not sell something that will come and document sale is not d'oraita — the verse was not needed to exclude documents, and it comes to exclude land and slaves that are equated to land; and therefore even though we rule to expound ribuy u'miyut, nevertheless one does not redeem with land — and this requires investigation; see and investigate.

And it is true that according to what Tosafos wrote in Bava Metzia 4b and 100b, R' Meir expounds ribuy u'miyut; if so, one cannot say the mishnah "one does not redeem with land" is R' Meir, as explained in the Gemara that the tanna of the mishnah expounds kelal u'prat — see in Assifat Zekeinim Bava Metzia 100 — and I will see that Tosafos' view is that R' Meir expounds kelal u'prat and not ribuy u'miyut; and one can say the Rambam and his colleagues hold like my view — and this requires investigation.

And some forbid even if it died inside the fruit. The Shach's view is to permit, and likewise the Peri Chadash inclines; and it appears clearly that even those who forbid do so only d'rabbanan — for how can sheretz haShoretz al haAretz apply after death, when while alive it did not swarm at all? And it is clear that it is forbidden d'rabbanan, lest it be confused with one that separated while alive; and if so, in a rabbinic matter we listen to be lenient.

And know that in the Gemara it also asks: if it separated into the air, what is the law? And Rashi explained: it separated from detached fruit into the air, and before it reached the ground he caught it in his mouth and swallowed it, etc. And one must understand — why was this needed? It is difficult to exist, and he did not explain according to the simple meaning that part of the worm separated through a hole while part was still inside the fruit and it was alive — whether we judge it like one that separated onto the ground and it is forbidden, or not, since it did not separate onto the ground at all, even partially, but only into the air. And it appears that Rashi holds that in this case we certainly require all of it, and a partial separation does not suffice, for such is the way of worms — and this is place of growth. And this is not separation, but only partial separation onto the ground is doubtful for us; but not into the air. Therefore Rashi needed to explain that it separated entirely, and before it fell he caught it in his mouth and ate it. And with this the words of the Rashba are correct, who permits through a hole, for it is not the worm's way to return. And so too the Peri Chadash, except that he did not bring proof for his words; and according to what I wrote the matter is compelled from Rashi — see and investigate.

Worms found in flour, etc. — the Peri Chadash's view is to permit if the flour rests inside a vessel — meaning place of growth, as with liquids; and his words are incomprehensible. And properly he says in the hagahah of ShaDa that one should not compare: granted, regarding sheratzim that have no scales and fins, which are forbidden only because the Torah permitted "all that is in the water" to eat them — and we say specifically "that is in the water," but if they separated from water they are forbidden; for this we say: since this is place of growth to separate to the walls of the vessel and return — it is called "in the water," for they are inside water; and if they separated, this is not called separation from water, and the permission of "all that is in the water" applies to them. Nevertheless one must inspect them, etc. And the Shach explained that inspection of the surface is not required, but inspection of the exterior is required — for if it separated outward, behold the worm is forbidden; therefore inspection is required. And from this I wonder at the words of the Taz, who wrote s.k. 12 that flour and dried raisins do not require inspection after mealworms, since the prohibition is only after separation — it is a double doubt; for behold this is exterior inspection, for mealworms crawl on the surface and inspection is required to remove them, and this is impossible; therefore they forbid everything, and especially in hot days when mealworms are common. And behold this is more of a common minority than lung inspection that requires inspection; and also after adhesions inspection is required, even though it is a double doubt — perhaps there is no adhesion, and if there is, perhaps it is kosher — and why do we not require inspection here? And especially regarding mealworms, the concern "lest they separated" does not apply at all, for their entire nature is to crawl, move, and stir, and they are always judged as separated and standing. Therefore in hot seasons inspection is especially required; but in cold days they are not common and inspection is not required.

And behold, this too is in the case of a woman who made raisin pudding, and afterward a mealworm was found in the stomach of the seller and in the hands of the other buyers who bought raisins; and the Taz permitted — perhaps they formed afterward, and it is a double doubt: perhaps there were none from the outset, and even if you say there were, perhaps they dissolved — end of his words.

And in truth this is what I wrote: if in hot seasons when this crawling is common in them — then certainly one must be stringent, for we say it certainly was there, and inspection is required. And now that we find in the Magen Avraham from the Torah we say they formed afterward; unlike in days that are not so hot until crawling is very common — it appears correct like the view of the Taz.

And what the Peri Chadash objected to him — that in this short time mealworms will form, the seller's conversation — I do not know; and will he say that this crawling and these fruits were forever? Perforce he must admit they were born in time; and if so, one moment before that time it was not there, and afterward it was — and if so, perhaps that was the moment the crawling creature was born and formed, and previously it was not. And according to the Peri Chadash it must be that it was forever, and that is impossible.

But in my opinion a double doubt is not needed — for why do we not say "render the fruits in their presumption, and now it is that they spoiled"? And is animal tereifah preferable? And this is the sheretz found in an alley that Tosafos brought in Chullin — we say "now it is found" and it was not there retroactively; and even for those who disagree — meaning that there the sheretz was found; but here in these fruits they were not found, only we are concerned from the Torah. For Chullin we render everything permitted retroactively and we do not render everything forbidden retroactively; and even for those who disagree one can say there is a distinction between tumah and issur — in any event it is not inferior to animal tereifah. And from the Torah to be stringent also in the second doubt that the Taz gave — perhaps it dissolved — the Peri Chadash wrote that even this is not applicable; and if it dissolved, behold its quantity still exists. And the Peri Chadash also did not speak correctly regarding the rabbi mentioned above — that small mealworms, when heat dominates them, are like nothing and like nonexistence; they dissolve in substance. Therefore even in this, since it is not recognizable — if so, by Torah law it is nullified; only d'rabbanan a beriah is not nullified — it is a rabbinic doubt for leniency. And here we do not establish a prohibition, for the raisins themselves have a presumption of permissibility, and it is like something spilled in a rabbinic issur that we rely on leniency — even though a prohibition was established, because the cooked dish established permissibility; and the same here. And especially most authors hold that regarding an established rabbinic prohibition we also say a rabbinic doubt for leniency. Therefore the correct view for the law is as I wrote.

For there are those who permit them in every case — this is what is written in the name of the Ravyah, that separation is forbidden only in something that grows from the ground, like fruits and the like; but in meat, fish, and cheese there is no concern at all for sheretz, even if it separated. And the rabbi, the Peri Chadash, objected at length.

And in truth, in the essence of the matter he is correct with him that many of the authors disagree and did not distinguish at all between fruits and meat; but he overstepped in speaking great words against the Magen Avraham and Or Zarua as if they erred in the words of the Ravyah — for the entire aim of the Ravyah is to distinguish in this: if they separated from inside the meat to the surface of the meat — in fruit it is forbidden, for there the worm's way is to cling to the pit; unlike in meat and cheese, whose way is to separate from inside onto its surface.
