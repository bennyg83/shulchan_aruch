אבל אסור לשאוב בכלי וכו' דע כי הרב ב"י כתב וז"ל והיכי שעירה משקין שבו השרצים בכלי לכלי אחר נראה דכלי שני דין כלי ראשון ישלו וכשפירשו על דופני שבפנים כיון שלא פירשו שם אלא עם משקה היינו רבותי' ואף שמדברי הרא"ש גבי בורות נראה שאם שאב מים בכלי ופי' לדופני הכלי אסורין אפשר דהתם שאני דמעיקרא היה בבור ועכשיו בכלי אבל הכא דמעיקרא כלי ועכשיו כלי שייך שפיר למימר היינו רביתי' עכ"ל ונמשכו הש"ך והפר"ח אחריו ולכך פירשו הא דכתב רמ"א דאסור לשאוב בכלי פירושו מבור אבל מכלי לכלי מותר והפר"ח תמה על רמ"א שסתם דבריו ע"ש: ואני לא הבנתי הקושיא דלא ידענו הבדל בין בור לכלי ובין כלי לכלי רק זה יש הבדל בין עירה מכלי לכלי ובין שואב מתוך כלי קטן לגדול דודאי בדופני כלי בפני' מקום שיש המשקין מיקרי היינו רביתי' אף שלפעמים רוחש התולע למעלם מן משקין מ"מ כך רביתא וטבע מלמעלה למטה וזהו רביתא אבל אם פי' לדופני הכלי מבחוץ במקום שאין משקין כלל בזו לא שייך היינו רביתא ואסור ולכך כשמער' מכלי לכלי א"כ הרחש דבתוך הכלי ירחיש על הדופן למעלה ממשקין זהו רביתא ולרחוש לחוץ לדופן השני כולי האי לא חיישינן אבל השואב א"כ דופן החיצון בא לתוך המשקין ונדבק ונרחשו עליו הרוחשין וכשמוציא כלי לחוץ הרי הרחש על הדופן הכלי מבחוץ בלי משקין ולכך חיישינן שירחיש מבחוץ לפנים אבל לא מבפני' לחוץ כי אין דרכו להתרחק מן משקין וא"ש וזהו ברור ולכך סתם הרמ"א דאסור לשאוב אפי' מכלי לכלי דלערות מותר אבל לשאוב אסור וזהו ברור ונכון לענ"ד וכן משמע קצת בט"ז ע"ש ס"ק ה' ע"ש:

But it is forbidden to draw with a vessel, etc.—know that the Rav Beit Yosef wrote as follows: and how did he pour liquids that have sheratzim in them from vessel to another vessel—it appears the second vessel has the law of the first vessel; and when they separated onto the interior walls from inside, since they did not separate there except with the liquid, it is "their majority," end quote. And even though from the words of the Rash regarding pits it appears that if one drew water in a vessel and they separated onto the interior walls of the vessel they are forbidden—perhaps there it is different, for initially it was in a pit and now in a vessel; but here, where initially it was a vessel and now a vessel, it is fitting to say "their majority," end quote. And the Shach and Pri Chadash followed him; and therefore they explained that which the Rama wrote—that it is forbidden to draw with a vessel—its meaning is from a pit; but from vessel to vessel it is permitted. And Pri Chadash wondered at the Rama that he ruled anonymously—see there. I did not understand the difficulty—for do we not know the difference between pit and vessel and between vessel and vessel? Only this: there is a difference between pouring from vessel to vessel and drawing from within a small vessel to a large one—for certainly on the interior walls of a vessel, in a place where there is liquid, it is called "their majority," even though sometimes the worm stirs above the liquid; nevertheless, such is "their majority," and nature is from above to below—and this is "their majority." But if they separated onto the exterior walls of the vessel, in a place where there is no liquid at all—in this "their majority" does not apply, and it is forbidden. Therefore, when pouring from vessel to vessel—if so, the stirring inside the vessel will stir on the wall above the liquid—this is "their majority"; and to stir outward to the other wall—we are not concerned at all. But when drawing—if so, the exterior wall comes into the liquid and sticks, and the stirrers stirred on it; and when he takes the vessel outside, behold the stirring is on the exterior wall of the vessel without liquid—and therefore we are concerned lest it stir from outside to inside; but not from inside to outside, for it is not its way to distance from liquid—and this is clear. Therefore the Rama ruled anonymously that it is forbidden to draw—even from vessel to vessel pouring is permitted, but drawing is forbidden—and this is clear and correct in my humble opinion; and so it appears somewhat in the Taz, see there, s.k. 5—see there.

And they do not flow—some forbid and some permit; the dispute is explained in the gemara, since it is written "in water, in water": one tanna holds this is not klal u'frat but ribuy u'miut; therefore it included charitzin and neitzin even though they do not flow, for prohibition. And one tanna holds like the tanna of the house of Rav: wherever you find two adjacent klalot, cast the prat between them and judge them as klal u'frat. And even so, what prat—flowing water; all flowing water; but those that do not flow are permitted. In this the poskim disagreed whether the halachah is like the tanna of the house of Rav or like the two tannaim of Rabbi Yishmael; and the Rambam ruled like the tanna of the house of Rabbi Yishmael, stringently to forbid even those that do not flow. And Pri Chadash challenged: behold in Bechorot, perek Yesh Bekhor, we say in the mishnah that one does not redeem with land and slaves and not with documents; and Rabbi disagrees that everything is redeemed except documents—and we establish the dispute: Rabbi holds like the tanna of the house of R' Yosi that two adjacent klalot are judged by ribuy u'miut; Rabbi—all; and what did he exclude—documents; and the tanna of the mishnah holds like the tanna of the house of Rav—cast prat between them and excludes land, slaves, and documents—see there. If so, the Rambam and all poskim who ruled one does not redeem with land and slaves—it implies they hold we judge by klal u'frat and not ribuy u'miut; if so, also regarding charitzin and neitzin we should also require flowing, for from "in water, in water" we derive the above regarding bechor—and the difficulty is fitting for him. And one can answer in this: either the Rambam does not decide for us the halachah like whom—in such a case do we expound klal u'frat or ribuy u'miut—and therefore he ruled everywhere stringently regarding pidyon haben he ruled stringently that one does not redeem with land, for perhaps we expound klal u'frat; and regarding permitting water sheretz we go stringently, perhaps we expound ribuy u'miut—even charitzin that do not flow are forbidden; and we go on every side stringently. And in my chiddushim I said: it already appears from Tosafot, perek 1 of Shevuot 4b, d"h yatz'u shetarot—that which appears here that sale applies to documents, since it was necessary to exclude documents explicitly in Ma'achalot Asurot—behold they hold like Tosafot that if not from the Torah sale does not apply to documents, how is it possible regarding pidyon that the default is that he gave the kohen a document that has another as counterparty in redemption money—and if Torah acquisition does not apply, obviously there is no redemption in it (and see my chiddushim to Choshen Mishpat siman 60, where I wrote ways that there are documents where Torah sale is possible; but nevertheless the plain meaning of documents implies ordinary documents, and if Torah sale is not in them, the verse was not needed to exclude—and as Tosafot wrote). And behold Tosafot in Bava Batra 56b, d"h ketani, etc., wrote: and if you ask why he does not acquire from the Torah also the lien of land written in it—for land is acquired by money, document, and chazakah, etc.—and one can answer: just as he cannot dedicate it as proved in perek 26, since it is not in his possession, so he cannot transfer Torah acquisition, etc.—see there. If so, it works out for those who hold a person does not sell or dedicate what is not in his possession; but for R' Tam and his supporters who hold a person dedicates and sells something that has not yet come into the world—and all the more so what is not in his possession—obviously he can sell Torah documents; and we say in Yevamot 93a that Rabbi is like R' Meir, that a person acquires what is not in his possession; if so, for Rabbi Torah sale of documents is possible—as Tosafot's difficulty—and properly the verse was needed to exclude documents; and likewise for the tanna of the mishnah it was necessary to say he expounded klal u'frat and we do not say he holds sale of documents is not from the Torah and the verse was not needed to exclude documents—for the anonymous mishnah is R' Meir, and R' Meir holds a person acquires what is not in his possession; therefore we say they disagree in klal u'frat and ribuy u'miut. But for us, who hold a person does not sell what is not in his possession, and sale of documents is not Torah law and the verse was not needed to exclude documents—it comes to exclude land and slaves that are equated to land; and therefore even though we rule to expound ribuy u'miut, nevertheless one does not redeem with land—and examine and understand.

And some forbid even if it died inside the fruit—and the Shach's view is to permit; and so inclines the view of Pri Chadash; and so it appears clear—even those who forbid forbid only rabbinically, for how does "crawling on the ground" apply after death, and while alive it did not crawl at all; and it is clear it is forbidden rabbinically lest it be confused with separating while alive. Nevertheless one must inspect them, etc.—and the Shach explained: internal inspection is not required, but external inspection is required—for if it separated outward behold the worm is forbidden; and therefore inspection is required. From this I wondered at the words of the Taz, who wrote s.k. 12, that flour and raisins dried do not require inspection after milvein, since the prohibition is only after separating—there is safeik safeika: for this is external inspection, for the milvein stir on top and inspection is required to remove them—and this is impossible; therefore everything is forbidden, and particularly in hot days milvein are common; and behold this is more common than lung inspection that requires inspection; and also after adhesions inspection is required, even though there is safeik safeika: perhaps there is no adhesion, and if there is perhaps it is kosher—why should we not require inspection here? And particularly in milvein it does not apply at all "lest they separated"—for their entire matter is to stir and move and be moved; and they are always judged as separated and standing; and therefore in hot times inspection is most necessary; but in cold days they are not common and inspection is not required. And behold, likewise in the case of a woman who made Paladin Marziyani, and afterwards worms were found at the seller's and in the hands of other buyers who bought milvein—and the Taz permitted: one can say they formed afterwards, and there is safeik safeika: safeik they were not there initially; and if you say they were, perhaps they dissolved, end quote. And in truth it is as I wrote: if in hot times this stirring is common in them—certainly one should be stringent, for we say certainly they were there and inspection is required; and now that we find in Ma'achalot Asurot from the Torah let us say they formed afterwards—unlike in days that are not so hot until it is very common—it appears correct as the view of the Taz. And what Pri Chadash attacked on him—that in a short time the milvein would form—I do not know; and will he say this stirring was forever, and these fruits—he will concede they formed in time; and if so, one moment before the time it was not there and afterwards it was—and if so, perhaps this is the moment the stirring was born and came into being, and before it was not; and according to Pri Chadash it must be it was forever, which is impossible. But in my opinion safeik safeika is not needed—for why should we not say establish fruits in their presumption, and now it deteriorated? And is this better than treifah of an animal—and this is the sheretz found in a alley that Tosafot raised in Chullin: we say now it is found and it was not retroactively; and even for those who disagree—we were that there the sheretz is found; but here in these fruits they are not found—only we are concerned from the Torah; let us hold retroactively for Chullin everything is permitted, and we do not hold everything retroactively; and even for those who disagree one can distinguish between tumah and issur—in any case it is not worse than treifah of an animal. And from the Torah to be stringent also in the second safeik that the Taz gave—perhaps it dissolved—and Pri Chadash wrote: in bees it does not apply, and if it melted behold its quantity still exists; and also he did not speak correctly regarding the aforementioned Rav: small milvein, controlled by the heat of fire, are a vessel of themselves and like nothing and as if they have no substance; therefore even in this, since it is not recognizable—and if so from the Torah it is nullified—only rabbinically clear is not nullified—there is safeik derabbanan lekula. And here we did not establish its prohibition—raisins themselves have presumption of heter, and it is like something spilled in rabbinic prohibition where we depend leniently—even though prohibition was established because of the stew, heter was established; and likewise here; and particularly most authors hold regarding establishing prohibition we also say safeik derabbanan lekula; and therefore the correct ruling is as I wrote.
