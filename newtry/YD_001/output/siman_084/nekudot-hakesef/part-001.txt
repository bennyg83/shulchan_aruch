**** ENGLISH ****
(There Taz s.k. 17) The Gaon and the author of Taz both intended the same thing in what they wrote — that it is permitted to sell it little by little, etc., as is written in Orach Chayyim at the beginning of siman 466, etc., regarding grain, etc. They are astonishing — for some say the rule applies there, for it is different there since their reason is that even if the non-Jew sells to an Israelite the Israelite will eat it before Pesach, see there; and there the reason is on account of double doubt — perhaps the non-Jew will eat it, and if you say he will sell to an Israelite, perhaps the Israelite will eat before Pesach — see there in Shulchan Aruch. Unlike here, where we are concerned that perhaps the non-Jew will sell to an Israelite and there is no double doubt; and similarly regarding bread of non-Jews in siman 112 seif 11 — it is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew and we do not say to sell to a non-Jew little by little; and similarly regarding yayin nesech in siman 134 seif 1 — he permitted only through grinding and baking bread, etc., and did not permit selling little by little; and also the remedy that Rava stated in Avodah Zarah 65b and in Pesachim 42a — Rava also stated 'kava kava' — and why did she not say the remedy 'kava kava' also regarding yayin nesech? Rather, we certainly are concerned that perhaps the non-Jew will sell to an Israelite and there is no double doubt; and if so, here too we are concerned that perhaps he will sell to a non-Jew; and one could say that here too there is double doubt — one, perhaps they did not separate; and if you say they separated, perhaps the non-Jew will eat them — meaning that if so, why is it forbidden to sell all of it together to a non-Jew? Is there not double doubt — perhaps they did not separate, and if you say they separated, perhaps the non-Jew will eat? Rather, it certainly is not called double doubt, as the Gaon wrote in s.k. 18; and so one could say — and perhaps one will say: granted, all of it is forbidden, for presumably he will sell to an Israelite; but kava kava presumably he will not sell to an Israelite; and also the doubt that perhaps they did not separate combines to permit selling kava kava; but from what the Gaon wrote in s.k. 18 — that here is different since it is forbidden for everyone — this refutes that answer; requires further study and requires further analysis:
