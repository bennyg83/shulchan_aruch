<b>וי"א דכל דבר שבמנין כו'. </b>היינו כרבי יוחנן בגמרא אליבא דרבי מאיר דסבירא ליה את שדרכו למנות שנינו פירוש שלעולם אין מוכרין אלא במנין מה שאין כן ביצה שפעמים מוכרים סל מלא ביצים זהו בטל ולא כריש לקיש דסבירא ליה כל שדרכו למנות שנינו וביצה אסורה אינה בטילה וזהו דעת הטור שהביא דעה זו וכן סמ"ג בשם ריצב"א אבל בש"ד סי' מ"ב וכן בהג"ה שם בשם רוב הגאונים פסקו כר"ל וכ"פ רש"ל בא"ו שלו והש"ע שפסק כאן בתחילת הסימן דדוקא דבר חשוב כו' אינו בטל ודבר שבמנין בטל היינו כר' עקיבא בגמרא דפליג עם ר"מ ותימה עליו שבסימן פ"ז סעיף ג' פסק כמו ר"ל דכאן דביצה אינה בטילה והאידנא הוה ביצה דבר שמוכרין תמיד במנין ואפילו רבי יוחנן מודה וכן כתב רש"ל ולענין הלכה כיון דמילתא דרבנן היא כדאי הם המתירים לסמוך עליהם במקום הפסד וכמו שכתב רמ"א דלא מקרי דבר שבמנין רק אם לעולם אין מוכרין אלא במנין כר' יוחנן וכתב רש"ל דכרכשתא אף על פי שהאידנא אין מוכרין אותה אלא במנין בטילה כיון דאין אוכלין אותה אלא על ידי מילוי והוא מדברי המרדכי בפרק שני דביצה:
**** ENGLISH ****
Some say that every davar sheb'minyan, etc. — that is like R. Yochanan in the Gemara according to R. Meir, who holds "we taught: that which is customarily counted" — meaning they always sell only by count, unlike an egg, which sometimes they sell a basket full of eggs; that is nullified — unlike R. Levi, who holds "we taught: everything customarily counted," and a forbidden egg is not nullified; that is Tur's view, which he brought, and so Semag in the name of Ritzba; but in Shulchan Aruch siman 442 and in the hagahah there in the name of most geonim they ruled like R. Levi, and so Maharshal in his Or Zarua, and Shulchan Aruch who ruled here at the beginning of the siman that specifically an important item, etc., is not nullified, and davar sheb'minyan is nullified — that is like R. Akiva in the Gemara, who argues with R. Meir. It is surprising on him that in siman 87 seif 3 he ruled like R. Levi, for here an egg is not nullified — and nowadays an egg is something always sold by count, and even R. Yochanan would concede; so too Maharshal wrote. And regarding the halachah, since it is a rabbinic matter, it is fitting to rely on those who permit in a case of loss — as Rama wrote, that it is not called davar sheb'minyan unless they always sell only by count, like R. Yochanan; and Maharshal wrote that karkashta, even though nowadays they sell it only by count, is nullified, since they eat it only by filling — and that is from Mordechai in the second chapter of Beitzah.

<b>ודוקא בעלי חיים קטנים. </b>חילוק זה הכריחו מדכתב הטור בסימן ק"א דכבש שלם אינו בטל והך קטנים דכאן צריך לומר כמו צפרים קטנים ולא תרנגולת דהא בתרנגולת כתב לעיל דאינה בטלה ורש"ל פרק ג"ה סימן י"א תירץ בזה דלעיל מיירי בכבש שהופשט והוא שלם עדיין אבל כאן מיירי שלא הופשט ממילא מחוסר מעשה גדול ודומה למה שפסק לעיל תרנגולת בנוצתה דבטילה וכל שכן כאן דכבש בצמרה הוה מחוסר מעשה גדול טפי. ולי נראה דחשיבות של בעל חי הוא חשיבות בפני עצמו דהיינו אם הוא דבר כחוש ואינו ראוי לאכילה ממילא אין לו חשיבות מצד חתיכה ראויה להתכבד כשנשחט רק כ"ז שהוא חי יש לו חשיבות מצד שהוא בעל חי ועל זה אמר הטור דכאן שהוא חשוב מצד בעל חי בטל חשיבותו כשנשחט אבל אם יש לו חשיבות מצד ראויה להתכבד זה כתבו כבר בסי' ק"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
Specifically small animals. This distinction was compelled from what Tur wrote in siman 101, that a whole sheep is not nullified — and these "small" here must mean small birds, not a chicken, for above he wrote that a chicken is not nullified. Maharshal chapter 35 siman 11 resolved this: above deals with a lamb that was flayed and is still whole; but here deals with one that was not flayed — automatically it lacks great preparation, and is similar to what he ruled above regarding a chicken in its feathers, that it is nullified — and all the more so here, that a lamb in its wool lacks great preparation even more. To me it appears that the importance of a living animal is importance in itself — that is, if it is a weak thing not fit for eating, automatically it has no importance from the side of a piece worthy of honor when slaughtered; only while it is alive does it have importance because it is a living creature. On this Tur said that here, where it is important as a living creature, its importance is nullified when slaughtered; but if it has importance as piece worthy of honor — that they already wrote in siman 101.

Most stores, etc. — There is wonder here on Shulchan Aruch, for Rashba did not write thus, that mixtures help, except when one bought from butcher shops where there is treifah meat mixed among the permitted pieces — that two kinds of kevua are before us: one is Torah-level kevua, namely nine permitted stores and one treifah, regarding which we say from the Torah "every fixed item is half-and-half," etc., since the issur is recognizable in its place, and in this there is no distinction between piece worthy of honor or not; and rabbinic kevua is in one butcher shop where treifah was found, mixed among the permitted pieces, and he does not know from which he bought — for from the Torah all meat in the butcher shop would be permitted, nullified in the majority; only the Sages decreed that if there is a piece worthy of honor there, it should not be nullified, and they gave this butcher shop the law of kevua, as they called it in the Gemara in Zevachim (73a) regarding a mixture of living creatures that are important, that it had kevua; and so Tosafos wrote in Pesachim (9), in the passage beginning "this is nine stores"; and so in Semak at the end of siman 214 they also called it rabbinic kevua. Rashba wrote regarding that rabbinic kevua that if afterward one bought from this fixed set and it became mixed with others, it is permitted because of double safek; but in Torah-level kevua that we mentioned, certainly afterward mixtures do not help, as Rashba himself wrote — Tur brought at the end of this siman that the first safek must be permitted from the Torah specifically, and the issur is only d'rabbanan; and likewise the law of a non-Jew's cup that became mixed, which Rashba brings proof from — it would be thus, that from the Torah even in an important vessel it is nullified, and the issur is only d'rabbanan, as they wrote in Zevachim (73a), in the passage beginning "rather Rava said," and so it is explained in Terumat HaDeshen HaArukh — see there — that they wrote this law on this law that I mentioned; and so Shulchan Aruch should have written: from butcher shops where treifah became mixed and one bought from there and does not know from which of them he bought, and it became mixed with others, etc.; and so is the view of Issur VeHeter klal 25 din 12, that specifically regarding rabbinic issur — such as nine stores — and one of them separated not before us, it is permitted from the Torah, since it separated not before us, only the Sages forbade it because of meat hidden from the eye, as Shulchan Aruch wrote here at the end of seif 3 — only then does what became mixed afterward help, and it is permitted even if piece worthy of honor; and one should not challenge: behold we establish rabbinic safek to leniency — why do we need two safeks? Issur VeHeter already resolved there: since here there is Torah issur that was permitted through nullification in the majority and the Sages forbade it and considered the minority as if it were half, since it is piece worthy of honor — therefore two safeks are needed to permit. It follows that Rashba and Issur VeHeter are entirely equal — Rashba deals with rabbinic kevua, and even if one bought before us from there and afterward it became mixed with others, it is permitted, since even at the place of fixedness there is only rabbinic issur; but in Torah-level kevua like the case of nine stores, double safek does not help except after it separated not before us, whose issur is rabbinic. And behold I saw the author of Hagahot Issur VeHeter at the end of the book, who wrote on this that Issur VeHeter disagrees with Rashba, that here he does not distinguish whether the safek arose at the time of fixedness or not, and he was not precise at all — and although I am not worthy to disagree, nevertheless the clear truth is that the authors of Shulchan Aruch, the Rav Beit Yosef and afterward Rama, who brought here Issur VeHeter's dispute on this law in the name of "some forbid," etc., did not investigate this sufficiently — for in truth all agree in this law, as I wrote; so it appears to plain understanding. Maharach too explained Rashba's words as I wrote, not like Shulchan Aruch — and see what I will write in this siman seif 8 on Shulchan Aruch's words here; further, we do not establish like this seif except that we require three mixtures specifically.

<b>אבל ליקח מכאן ואילך אסור. </b>פירוש אפי' אם פירש שלא בפנינו שבזה מותר בקבוע דאורייתא בתשע חניות מ"מ כאן אסור גזירה שמא יקח מן הקבוע עצמה משא"כ בקבוע דאורייתא שהאיסור ניכר במקומו לאו ברשיעי עסקינן שיקח במזיד מן האיסור הברור ומטעם זה נמי מותר כל שלקח קודם שנולד הספק בקבוע דרבנן אפי' פירש בפנינו דהא עדיין כולו היתר ולא גזרינן בלקח בשעת היתר אטו שיקח אחר האיסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
But to buy from here onward is forbidden. Explanation: even if it separated not before us — whereby it would be permitted in Torah-level kevua of nine stores — nevertheless here it is forbidden as a decree lest one buy from the fixed item itself; unlike Torah-level kevua, where the issur is recognizable in its place, we are not dealing with wicked people who would buy intentionally from the clear issur. For this reason too, whatever one bought before the safek arose in rabbinic kevua is permitted, even if it separated before us — for behold it is still entirely permitted, and we do not decree on one who bought at a time of permissibility lest he buy after the issur.

There is room to permit by double safek, etc. — I saw in this a dispute of Baalei Karnayim — that is Issur VeHeter, and on him the author of Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim klal 43 seif 7, and on him in responsum Masat Binyamin siman 38 — and in all their words I wonder, as I will explain: Issur VeHeter klal 26 din 2 wrote: one who slaughters with a checked knife and after shechitah the knife is found damaged and afterward became mixed among other animals — one may not permit by double safek, perhaps it is not this one, and if you find to say that it is this one, perhaps it was slaughtered properly — for we conclude in the Gemara: an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur — therefore there is only one safek, perhaps it is not this one — for if you say it is this one, perhaps it was slaughtered properly — for whatever is safek we forbid it because of presumption it was certainly issur and not safek — and therefore one may not permit by double safek except where there is no presumption of issur — when you say "if you say" you cannot establish it on presumption of issur, like this open door that I found at the beginning of Kesubos — "if you say" "under him" there is no establishing presumption of issur beforehand, etc., end of his words. And he wrote on this in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim that this is not correct — for Rashba wrote in responsum siman 401 that double safek helps even where there is presumption of issur; and so it appears from the laws of safek derusah that became mixed — for if not for the reason that the first safek was in its substance, there would be double safek — and we would permit even though it had presumption of issur of limb from a living creature, end of his words. And so too here in Shulchan Aruch — and Maharar Binyamin mentioned disagrees with him on Rama, and wrote that there is no proof from safek derusah — for there presumption of issur of limb from a living creature helps only regarding safek that occurred because of shechitah, but not regarding issur that does not depend on shechitah — and he elaborated to explain this matter. And according to him safek derusah where the safek was born from life will be permitted — for it is like the case where a wolf took, etc. — for there is no presumption of issur, and we say in it "it was slaughtered, it is permitted" — and Tosafot's words in chapter 4 of Achin (Yevamos daf 30) escaped him, who wrote, and these are his words: and in the first chapter of Chullin we say: an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur, etc. was slaughtered, it is permitted etc. it appears specifically for a defect born after shechitah it was in presumption of heter, such as a wolf came and took its innards, etc. — but if the safek was born from life, such as safek derusah — not because at the hour the safek was born it was still in presumption of issur end of his words — behold before you that presumption of issur of limb from a living creature helps for safek derusah — and if so Rama properly brings proof from safek derusah regarding double safek, that we see in it that it helps if it is possible to say so; but we do not care in this what was at first in presumption of issur of the living limb — even though it helped to forbid in one safek — rather I see that regarding the halachah all were correct, as I will explain — it is stated in responsum of Rashba siman 401, and these are his words: I see wonder in the matters you asked regarding double safek where issur was presumed, like Kesubos — how is issur presumed? For there is only safek of one corpse that was lost — and if you call it "issur was presumed," every mixed item that we presumed in issur because of its mixture — behold your question is explained from a ring of gentiles, etc., end of his words — and he brings on this many proofs; and afterward wrote: and do not answer me from Rav Huna in the first chapter of Chullin — he checked the knife and slaughtered, and it was found spoiled — behold this is forbidden, lest it was spoiled in the skin; and an incident occurred with thirteen animals and they forbade even the first — learn from this that double safek is forbidden, for he follows his own view that an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur, etc.; and know further that double safek is preferable to majority, and it is possible its permission is stronger than majority — for in lineage one is disqualified by one majority and qualified by double safek; and just as we follow majority to permit in all Torah prohibitions, even where presumption of issur was established, such as a piece of issur that became mixed in two of heter — the same applies to double safek that it is permitted, even where presumption of issur was established, end of his words. And it appears difficult what he wrote in that case of Rav Huna that he mentioned first — that we follow presumption of issur and double safek does not help — and what is different from that which he wrote afterward that we follow double safek even where presumption of issur was established; and if you investigate you will find that Rashba distinguishes in that presumption of issur of Rav Huna — that an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur until you know it was slaughtered properly; and end — since the doubt afterward is in shechitah itself whether it was done properly, and to remove it from presumption that it was not slaughtered, double safek does not help — since in that issur itself from which it had not certainly departed; but if the presumption of issur is because of double safek — that is, it had presumption of limb from a living animal and was slaughtered properly, only there is doubt because of double safek, such as it became mixed first and afterward became mixed in other mixtures — certainly double safek helps; and so all other issur, such as a piece of chelev that became mixed in two mixtures — for Rashba explicitly compared double safek to majority in this way; and it follows from our words that Rama did not challenge Issur VeHeter at all — for Issur VeHeter forbade only in safek shechitah, and that which Rashba mentioned, that case of Rav Huna — there certainly double safek does not help for the reason I mentioned; and also Maharar Binyamin — it appears to me — disagreed with Rama as we mentioned, and said double safek does not help where presumption of issur was established; he ruled well on his question that deals also with safek shechitah — only it appears he wants to rule thus in every double safek where presumption of issur was established, even in a safek not regarding shechitah — for he wrote against Rama that he has no proof from Rashba's words siman 401 — for there in siman 401 he does not speak of presumption of issur, as Rashba wrote explicitly in responsum that where there was full presumption, double safek does not help; rather it deals with presumption of safek issur, such as safek challal in Kesubos; and according to the questioner's view they called presumption of issur as explained there, and on this he wrote to permit; but with full presumption of issur, like this case of Rav Huna, double safek does not help — until here is his language; and may he rest in honor — for he did not investigate Rashba's words well, because he did not see the end of the matter — for Rashba wrote explicitly at the end "and know further," etc., as I mentioned; it is clear that even with full presumption of issur it helps; and from the outset he wrote plainly that that presumption of issur that the questioner called presumption of issur — that is, from the side of mixtures — obviously it helps; and he concluded that even with full presumption of issur, such as a piece of chelev, double safek helps in it like majority, and we do not say it does not help except in that case of Rav Huna where the doubt is in shechitah, as I mentioned; and the Rav Maharar Binyamin understood in Rashba that every presumption of issur is like that of Rav Huna — and it is not so. What emerges from our words for the halachah: even in every issur certainly double safek helps to permit if it does not contradict the presumption itself — like safek in shechitah, that if double safek helps in it, it would contradict the presumption that it was not slaughtered but limb from a living animal — in this we say it does not help; and similar to this is a woman who is in presumption of married woman and afterward safeks arose regarding her husband's death or divorce — even if there were several double safeks they would not help, since you come to remove her from the category of married woman that she was first, and you come to contradict it directly; but in other presumption of issur where safeks for permission arose from another side, such as from mixtures — certainly double safeks help to remove from issur, for from the outset there is nothing here that contradicts one another; and therefore in safek derusah, if there was full double safek, it well helps to nullify presumption of issur of limb from a living animal that the innards call thus — it appears to me clear and correct in this:
