**** ENGLISH ****
(there in Shulchan Aruch seif 4 in the gloss) "And there are those who say," etc. It appears difficult against the Mechaber, for in Orach Chayyim siman 439 seif 1 the Mechaber brings the words of the Tur there, and these are his words: However, if the mouse is small such that the mouse could eat it, we rely to be lenient that it ate it and one need not search; and the reason is because there it is on account of a double safek — see there — and there they deal with even nine groups of matzah and one of chametz; even so it is called a double safek even though the one prohibition is by Torah law; and in particular that we are stringent with chametz more than other prohibitions — even so it is a double safek and one need not search; and there is not to say that searching for chametz is rabbinic, for this law is from the words of Tosafos in the first chapter of Pesachim daf 9 side 2, and there it is proven that they deal with a case where they did not nullify; and here it appears even if it is explained before us that it is rabbinic, as the Gaon and these are his words wrote in s.k. 26 — even so it is not called a double safek, and it is difficult what he wrote; and one can say that that case is different there, since through a double safek there is no longer any prohibition left — lest the mouse ate — which is not so here, for in any event the prohibition is still in existence, and it is similar to what the Mechaber wrote in the gloss below seif 8 — see there, investigate. But nevertheless against Bach one should be astonished that he challenged the Mechaber, as the Gaon and these are his words wrote in s.k. 22 from seif 9, since the first safek is in its body — so too; and it is difficult — did not the Mechaber write in Orach Chayyim in that siman that it is called a double safek as mentioned above, and these are the words of the Tur in that siman in name of Rashba; and according to Bach's words it is difficult there — where is it called a double safek, since the first safek is in its body — but certainly until here one must say that kavu'a chidush is different, and if so Bach has no difficulty at all against the Mechaber, for the Mechaber follows the words of Rashba in Orach Chayyim in that siman; and there is not to say that the reason in Orach Chayyim is because there is no longer any prohibition here as written above — this is not so, for according to the Mechaber's words the distinction is only whether the prohibition is in its body — investigate and requires further study:
