(סימן ק"י בש"ע סעיף ג') <b>ט' חנויות כו' ה"ז אסור שכל קבוע כמחצה על מחצה דמי.</b> כתב הש"ך שיש עוד קבוע דאורייתא כגון שיש בחנות א' טריפה ואינו יודע באיזה חנות ולקח מא' מהן ויודע מאיזה הוי קבוע דאורייתא. והוא נלמד מהד"מ שפי' כן דברי הטור בדברי הרשב"א. אבל לפ"ז יהיה דברי הרשב"א דכאן סותרים לדבריו שבחידושיו פ' התקבל גם דברי הטור דכאן יהיו סותרים לדבריו שבאה"ע סי' ל"ה כפי מ"ש המ"ל פ"ט מהל' אישות דסובר דכה"ג לא מיקרי קבוע וכמ"ש התוספות בנזיר (דף י"ב) ד"ה אסור. ובפ"ק דמציעא (דף ו') וז"ל וקבוע לא שייך אלא בדבר שאיסור והיתר נמכרין וידועין ולא ידע מאיזה לקח כגון ט' חנויות. ומ"ש בס' ח"ד מפי' רש"י בגיטין (נ"ד ב') א"מ די"ל דלאו מטעם קבוע הוא אלא משום שהאיסור מופרש ואינו ראוי להתערב וכמ"ש הרמב"ם בפ"ט דתרומות ותוי"ט שם ובר"ש פרק ג' דשביעית. וכ"כ המ"י כלל ע"ג בשם ס' כריתות דלא מיקרי קבוע רק אם האיסור ניכר וידוע במקומו. וכ"מ להדיא בס' אלי' רבה בא"ח סי' תרצ"ח ובשו"ת מוצל מאש סי' נ' ויש לה"ר מפ"ג דברכות גבי מדלגין היינו ע"ג ארונות ש"מ כו' דרוב יש בהם פ"ט ומיעוט אין בהם פ"ט והתם הרי הארונות קבועין במקומן ולא אמרינן כל קבוע כמחצה על מחצה אלא ש"מ דבעינן שיהא האיסור ניכר וידוע ומיהו בס' ב"א כתב בשם הרוקח דהתם היינו משום שיש ספק אם יש שם ארון שאין בו פותח טפח ומספק לא אמר קבוע. ועי' בעבודת כוכבים (נ"ג ב') דמשמע דאילנות של אשירה לא נתבטלו ברוב ואף אותן שאינן נכרין ומיהו ע"ז שאני שאין לה בטול לעול' אף בשאינו קבוע: <b>בס'</b> שעה"מ הל' מגילה כתב בשם ס' לשון למודים דלא אמר קבוע כמחצה על מחצה אלא היכא דשקיל או שפירש לפנינו מן הקבוע אבל היכא דלא יצא מהקבוע כגון עיירות דבאפי נפשייהו קיימי אזלינן בתר רובא כמ"ש הר"ן שם לענין קריאת מגילה. אבל מדברי ס' כריתות משמע דכה"ג חשוב קבוע כגון כשיש שדה דודאי אסורה בחרישה וזריעה ונולד ספק בשאר שדות והקשה עליו מפ' כל הצלמים דאם יש מקום אחד שודאי עובדים ובשאר מקומות ספק לא הוו נאסרים:
**** ENGLISH ****
(SA siman 110 seif 3) Nine stores, etc. — this is forbidden, for every fixed item is treated as half-and-half. Shach wrote that there is another Torah-level fixed case: when one store has a treifah and it is not known which store, and one bought from one of them and knows from which — that is a Torah-level fixed case. This is learned from Darkei Moshe, who explained Tur's words in Rashba accordingly. But then Rashba's words here would contradict his chiddushim in chapter HaTekabel; likewise Tur's words here would contradict his words in EH siman 35, per Maggid Mishneh ch. 9 of Laws of Marriage, who holds that such a case is not called fixed — and as Tosafos in Nazir (12b) s.v. assur, and in the first chapter of Bava Metzia (6a): "Fixed applies only where forbidden and permitted are sold and known, and he did not know from which he bought — such as nine stores." Chochmat Adam cites Rashi in Gittin (54b), that one might say it is not because of fixed status but because the issur is distinct and not fit to mix — as Rambam ch. 9 of Terumos, Turei Zahav there, and Rosh ch. 3 of Shevi'is. So too Magen Yeshuah klal 73 in the name of Kerisos: it is not called fixed unless the issur is recognizable and known in its place. This is explicit in Eliyah Rabbah OC 698 and in responsa Motzal Me'esh siman 50. There is a question from Berachos regarding medalgim on aronot of a tefach — most have a tefach opening and a minority do not; there the aronot are fixed in place, yet we do not say every fixed item is half-and-half — rather we require the issur to be recognizable and known. However Beit Avraham cites Rokeach that there it is because there is safek whether an aron without a tefach opening exists, and safek is not called fixed. See Avodah Zarah (53b), which implies asherah trees were not nullified in the majority even when unrecognized; but idolatry is different, for it is never nullified even when not fixed. Shaarei Teshuvah on Megillah cites Leshon Limudim: we do not say fixed is half-and-half except where one took or separated before us from the fixed set; but where it did not leave the fixed category — such as towns that exist on their own — we follow the majority, as Ran there regarding reading the Megillah. But from Kerisos it appears such a case is considered fixed — e.g., when one field is certainly forbidden for plowing and sowing and safek arose in other fields; he challenged from chapter Kol HaTzlamim that if one place certainly worships and in other places there is safek, they would not all be forbidden.

(There seif 3) But meat found in the market, etc. — and even if found among the stores it is considered separated. So too Tosafos in the first chapter of Kesubos — specifically where one store is recognizable; but where it is known there is a treifah in one store and it is not known which, if it separated it is forbidden according to Rosh who forbids separated automatically, lest one take from the fixed item since the issur is not known. It appears that if there is a Torah-level safek in one store and it is not known which, and it separated automatically, all permit — as proven in the Gemara in Nazir regarding one who tells his agent, "Go and betroth a wife for me," and he died — he is forbidden to all women; and it challenged: we find in the market and he betrothed — let us say everything that separates from the majority separates. Know that Magen Yeshuah permitted in great loss if it separated automatically regarding important items. And regarding whether we follow leniency in a double safek — Ramban wrote, cited in Chiddushei HaRan on Megillah chapter 1, that it is like two doubts; but Taz wrote we follow stringency in its safek, and R. Matityahu Ber forbids in his Beit She'arim. There is a question from Megillah: "You were a child" — one does not bring proof from a child; and if double safek is like two doubts, behold in rabbinic matters an adult is believed to testify what he saw as a child. But from Kesef Mishneh chapter 6 of Laws of Forbidden Foods regarding tumah there Rambam is lenient and there it is double safek. See Tosafos Sanhedrin (12). And at first glance there is a question according to Rambam who holds we follow leniency from the Torah from Pesachim (3b) and Chullin (91) regarding "they were identified and afterward became mixed" — for if they were forbidden from the Torah, burning would not be required; nevertheless we follow stringency on every side; and so too it is proven that one in one was not established as issur — for otherwise it would be forbidden from the Torah, and why require burning? One can say: just as it was established for eating, it was also established for burning. And what he wrote in Atzei Levona as proof for Rambam from Tosafos' difficulty in Shabbos (130) — why was a verse needed to forbid safek circumcision on Shabbos? One can say Shabbos is different, for there is a Torah prohibition — requires study. Further there s.k. 14 he left in doubt Rashi's explanation in Zevachim that he challenges R. Eliezer and R. Nissim from the mishnah of a stoned ox that became mixed — they all die; why not say the issur died and the rest are offered? What is the difficulty? After all, one must offer two at once — and how can one person slaughter two sacrifices at once? We say "slaughter it" — that one person should not slaughter two sacrifices. One can say: since it is impossible otherwise that they not die and consecrated animals not be lost, it is considered bedi'eved.

(שם בט"ז ס"ק ה') <b>וכי תימא דבמקולין דשכיחי רבים גזרינן טפי והא איפכא תנן בפ' חבית (דף קמ"ז) אבל עשרה בני אדם כו' ופרש"י כיון דמרובין הן מדכרי אהדדי.</b> ועי' בנקודת הכסף שהשיג עליו דשאני התם דהעשר' בנ"א הם במעמד אחד ע"כ אמרינן דמדכרי אהדדי כו' אבל הכא יש לחוש שיבואו ליקח בזה אח"ז ונ"ל דחילוק זה הוא מוכרח מהא דריש עירובין (דף ג') גבי מקצת קורה בתוך עשרים ומקצת למעלה מעשרים כו' דאמר רבה במבוי פסול ומפרש רבא מפרזנקא טעמא דמבוי לרבים הוא סמכי אהדדי ולא מדכרי ותקשי מהא דתנן בפרק חבית (שם) דאדרבה דברבים מדכרי אהדדי. אמנם לפי מה שחילק בנקוה"כ בין אם הרבים במעמד אחד אם לא א"ש נמי הך דעירובין דהתם נמי לפעמים אין רבים במבוי ואתי יחיד להשתמש באותה שעה וליכא מדכר ומ"מ אין מהתם קושיא על הט"ז דחילוק זה אינו רק להך לישנא אבל ללישנא קמא שם דרבה אמר רבים מדכרי בכל גווני אמרי' רבים מדכרי אהדדי. וכן עיקר כמ"ש בחידושי לעירובין (דף י"ח) ויש משם סעד לדעת הט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There in Taz s.k. 5) "You might say that in butcher shops where many frequent we decree more stringently — but we learned the opposite in chapter Chavis (147a): but ten people, etc." Rashi: since they are numerous they remind one another. See Nekudot HaKesef, who challenged that there is different — there the ten people are in one gathering, therefore we say they remind one another; but here we fear they will come to buy afterward. It appears this distinction is compelled from Eruvin (3a) regarding part of a beam within twenty and part above twenty, etc.: Rabah said an alleyway is invalid; Rava of Parzinka explained — an alleyway belongs to the public, they lean on one another and do not remind. This challenges the mishnah in Chavis that with many they remind one another. However, per Nekudot HaKesef's distinction whether the many are in one gathering or not — one can also answer from Eruvin: there too sometimes there are not many in the alleyway and an individual comes to use it at that hour and there is no reminder; nevertheless there is no difficulty on Taz from there. This distinction applies only to the latter wording; but in the first wording there Rabah said "the public reminds" — in every case we say the public reminds one another. So too is the main point in his chiddushim on Eruvin (18a), and there is support for Taz's view.

(שם בש"ך ס"ק נ"ט) <b>ספק איסור דרבנן בדשיל"מ אסור.</b> ודע שכתב הפר"ח בהל' יו"ט והפמ"ג בא"ח דהיינו דוקא בספק מעשה כמו ספק נולד ביו"ט דגבי ביצה וס' מוכן אבל בספק דפלוגתא דרבוותא וספיקא דדינא אזלינן לקולא בדרבנן אף בדבר שיל"מ וכ"מ בש"א סי' צ"ו ויש להוכיח כן מסוגיא דמנחות (דף ס"ח ע"ב). ובזה א"ש סוגיא דשלהי שבת דא"ר יוחנן הלכה כר"ש דלית ליה מוקצה ופריך והא"ר יוחנן הלכה כסתם משנה ותנן כו' אין משקין ושוחטין את המדבריות ומשני ר' יוחנן סתמא אחרינא אשכח ומסיק דב"ה כר"ש ס"ל ופרש"י ב"ה חשוב כסתמא. וקשה דאכתי מאי אולמא לר' יוחנן ב"ה מסתמא. אבל לפי מה שחילק הפר"ח בין ספיקא דעובדא לספיקא דדינא או פלוגתא דרבוותא דאזלי' לקולא א"ש הך דשבת דבאיסור מוקצה דרבנן לא בעינן לאולמא סתמא דב"ה דלית להו מוקצה דאף בשוין קיי"ל דהלכה כדברי המיקל בדברי סופרים וכדפסק ר' יוחנן גופיה בפ"ק דעבודת כוכבים (דף ז'). וכן מוכח דעת הרשב"א והר"ן בנדרים (דף מ"ז ע"ב) שכתבו דמסתברא דנקטינן בעיא דרמי ב"ח לקולא ואם אמר קונם פירות אלו על פלוני מותר בחילופיהן דכיון דגבי איסור הנאה בעלמא לא הוו חליפין כגדולין אלא מדרבנן ה"ל ספיקא דרבנן ולקולא עכ"ד. וקשה הא נדרים ה"ל דשיל"מ בשאלה כדאיתא התם (דף נ"ט) וגבי דבר שיל"מ אחמור בספיקו. כדאמר רב אשי בריש ביצה (דף ד') גבי ביצה שנולדה ביו"ט דספיקה אסורה משום דה"ל דבר שיל"מ וכל דבר שיל"מ אף בדרבנן לא בטל ופרש"י דכי היכי דאחמור בביטולה ה"נ אחמור מה"ט בספיקא אלא מוכח מזה דס"ל להרשב"א והר"ן דדוקא בספיקא דעובדא כי ההיא דספק נולדה ביו"ט או בחול הוא דקאמר רב אשי דאחמור נמי בספיקא משא"כ בספיקא דדינא בפלוגתא דרבוותא או באיבעיא דלא איפשטא כההיא דנדרים אזלינן לקולא בדרבנן אף בדשיל"מ וכתבתי עוד בזה בס"ד בחי' לחולין:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There in Shach s.k. 59) A rabbinic safek issur in something that has a permitted aspect is forbidden. Know that Peri Chadash in Laws of Yom Tov and Peri Megadim in OC wrote that this applies specifically to a factual safek — such as safek whether it was born on Yom Tov regarding an egg and prepared food — but in safek of a dispute among authorities or safek of the halachah we follow leniency in rabbinic matters even regarding something that has a permitted aspect. So too in She'eilat Shalom siman 96; one can prove this from the sugya in Menachos (68b). With this one can answer the sugya at the end of Shabbos: R. Yochanan ruled like R. Shimon who has no muktzeh; it challenged — but R. Yochanan ruled like an anonymous mishnah, "We do not give water or slaughter desert animals"; R. Yochanan answered he found another anonymous source; Rashi: Beit Hillel is considered like an anonymous mishnah. The difficulty remains: what relevance does Beit Hillel's anonymous mishnah have for R. Yochanan? But per Peri Chadash's distinction between factual safek and safek of halachah or dispute of authorities where we are lenient — one can answer this Shabbos case: regarding muktzeh, a rabbinic issur, we need not infer from Beit Hillel's anonymous mishnah that they have no muktzeh, for in any case we hold the halachah follows the lenient view in rabbinic matters — as R. Yochanan himself ruled in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (7). So too Rashba and Ran in Nedarim (47b) wrote it is reasonable that we pose a question that leans to leniency: if one said "These fruits are konam upon so-and-so," substitutes are permitted — since regarding issur hanaah itself substitutes are only rabbinic for adults, it is a rabbinic safek and we are lenient. The difficulty: nedarim has something that has a permitted aspect in the question (59b), and regarding something that has a permitted aspect we are stringent in its safek — as Rav Ashi said at the beginning of Beitzah (4a) regarding an egg born on Yom Tov, its safek is forbidden because it is something that has a permitted aspect, and anything that has a permitted aspect is not nullified even d'rabbanan; Rashi: just as we are stringent in its nullification, so too we are stringent in its safek. Rather it is proven that Rashba and Ran hold specifically regarding factual safek — as in egg born on Yom Tov or on a weekday — that Rav Ashi said we are also stringent in safek; but in safek of halachah in a dispute of authorities or in an unresolved question like nedarim, we are lenient d'rabbanan even regarding something that has a permitted aspect. (Laws of double safek in Shach letter 2) He wrote in the name of Rashba's responsum that regarding a rabbinic issur where something has a permitted aspect, we follow stringency when there is presumption of issur. Eben Ha'ozer chapter 2 of Shabbos disagrees, for we do not establish like R. Yehudah in safek tevel, as Rambam wrote — the anonymous mishnayos are not like him. So too Maggid Mishneh chapter 4 of Bechoros, and Ran in Sukkah (46b); see Avnei Yashpe and Chochmat Adam. However, the anonymous ruling in Taharos refers to safek tumah where there was no presumption of issur, not to tevel, as Tosefta wrote. Rather our author's reason is that we do not establish R. Yehudah against his colleagues, as in Magen Yeshuah. What he wrote in Beit Avraham — that this depends on two resolutions Tosafos wrote in Pesachim (9) — wondered why the later authorities did not mention them; they already preceded this in Shaarei Teshuvah on mikvaos klal 7 and in Tzitz Eliezer on Berachos; and his words there are impossible according to Rambam, as in Magen Avraham. See Seder Taharah. What Avnei Yashpe wrote as proof for issur from the fact that we are stringent in safek muchan — there it is something that has a permitted aspect. What Beit Avraham wrote as proof from Eruvin (32) — I omitted Maggid Mishneh's words in Laws of Bechoros; see there. What Chochmat Adam wrote as a question to forbid from safek impure hands d'rabbanan — Peri Megadim likewise, but it is not proof, for there even though impure issur was not established, the reason is they made it like Torah law to render a person impure, to burn terumah and kodshim upon him they also made it like Torah law to render safek impure. Rather requires study what Taz wrote siman 201 s.k. 86, explaining there the Mechaber's words that he forbids like R. Yose. That is also Rambam's language in Laws of Mikvaos. What Kereti Ufleiti wrote to bring proof to permit from Menachos (68b) regarding sixty days of waving — presumption of issur was pushed aside in Avnei Yashpe and Chochmat Adam, for there it is preferable to other safek because we are expert in the fixed month. There is a question on their words from what Rambam wrote chapter 10 of Laws of Forbidden Foods that sixty is forbidden from double safek — and if this were like all sixty it would be forbidden from the Torah since presumption of issur was established in sixty, a Torah issur. Regarding double safek from one place — he wrote in responsa Tur HaEven siman 41 that this is like there regarding ones regarding seduction of a minor — automatically it is ones; and if it were not ones presumably she would not be a minor; but if in one safek there is a dispute and in one safek all permit, there is not one place that is like one permitting more than his colleague. Levushei Serad wrote that the view of poskim not brought in Shulchan Aruch are not combined even for double safek — and similarly I saw Or HaChaim wrote in laws of seizure that one cannot say "I am certain like the view not brought in Shulchan Aruch." Mari argued on this — can the views of great poskim be nullified based on Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe's ruling? The Gaon already wrote thus in Noda B'Yehuda EH siman 47.

Question and responsum for siman 110. Question: Seven animals were slaughtered in a village a parsa from our city; a man took one tripe called tzaptzucha and found a needle in it — head protruding externally, most embedded diagonally through the cavity; no drop of blood, only redness near the needle externally without disease or pus; cutting the red spot showed no pus or blood; rust test negative, middle black. Before finding the needle all seven were cut into large pieces together, salted in three or four places, and rinsed; afterward the needle was found from one of the seven, unknown which — great loss and time of distress. Answer: The mixtures appear permitted as dry-in-dry (siman 109), since before cooking there is no prohibition from salting or piece worthy of honor. Initially only safek treifah — all issur because the tripe was rinsed before presentation; if not rinsed it would plainly be permitted per Shach siman 48 s.k. 14 and 30 (needle through-and-through without blood drop = after shechitah). Redness alone does not disqualify (Peri Megadim, Nishmas Adam). Rust in needle middle — follow Maharshal; even Bach disagrees, rely on Maharshal in great loss. Rinsing creates safek treifah (Rashba responsum, Beit Yosef, Avodat HaGedolah). Needle commonness does not block double safek (Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav). Double safek applies: perhaps R. Tam's view (Taz siman 110) or Rashba on large piece not piece worthy of honor (siman 101). Shach rules like R. Tam; acharonim lenient on piece not worthy of honor. Slaughterhouse decree (Shach seif 5) does not apply to individual at home. Peri Megadim permitted safek treifah in store mixture by double safek — all the more here. Dry-in-dry nullified; rabbinic issur — Torah spared Israel's property (Chullin 49). Signed Avraham A.A. R. Yehuda Leib.
