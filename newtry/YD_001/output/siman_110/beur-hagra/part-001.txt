Important thing, etc. All of this is the language of the Rambam, and it appears to mean specifically in species in its kind, not as he wrote above in siman 101, seif 6, in the hagahah. And even though one can push back, because one does not find a dry mixture of a species not in its kind that is not discernible, nevertheless it is superfluous language — only here does he say specifically.

And behold, ostensibly there is proof for the words of the aforementioned hagahah from what is written at the beginning of Yevamot (54), and in chapter 8 of Zevachim — the Tanna of the case of a liter of dried figs, etc.; and behold, for R' Yehudah without blilah, species in its kind is not nullified. And likewise what he says there at the end, "she rolled it in a circle," etc., s.v. ya'alu. And likewise in the chapter Nezikin (54b), "they fell and split," etc. — the words of R' Yehudah; only here it deals with not in its kind. But already Tosafot explained in Zevachim 73, s.v. Rabbi Yehudah — R' Yitzchak asks that species in its kind is not nullified except wet with wet. And with this it is also resolved the chapter Notel (141b) — R' Yitzchak says even we raise, etc. And behold, here it is species in its kind, that not in its kind is nullified in sixty, as written in chapter 2 of Orlah to be lenient and to be stringent — species not in its kind, etc. And likewise in Yerushalmi chapter 6 of Nedarim: "and anything that has no permitted form, such as terumah," etc., and not in its kind nullified in sixty. And that passage in chapter 5 of Yevamot (38b) that says wheat and wheat are not nullified according to R' Yitzchak. And likewise that which is said in Menachot 23 regarding the mishnah "two meal offerings," etc. — it is according to R' Yitzchak in flour that is wet with wet. And so too the Tosafot there (22), s.v. veR' Yitzchak, etc. — see there; and there, and in Yevamot chapter 1, 1, s.v. R' Yehudah, etc. Nevertheless, the words of Tosafot are not necessarily that he holds like the Rambam, but because one does not find a mixture of not in its kind in dry, as noted above.

(Collected notes) "Important thing," etc., and likewise beriah, etc. See Rambam chapter 16 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, who wrote "important thing," etc., seven things, etc.; and likewise a piece of nevelah, etc. — for behold it is a piece worthy of honor; and the same applies to a piece of basar b'chalav or of hullin that was slaughtered in the Azarah, etc.; and likewise gid hanasheh, etc.; and likewise every beriah — therefore the stoned ox, the heifer whose neck was broken, the bird of the metzora, and the firstborn donkey, etc. But the rest of the things — even though their way is to count them — behold, these are elevated in their measure, and that is the mishnah in chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah (74a):

[And what he wrote nevelah and gid hanasheh in chapter gid hanasheh, and not like the sugya of Avodah Zarah that says there to exclude nevelah, as Tosafot wrote]

And that which is not counted in the mishnah of Orlah except those seven things — Kesef Mishneh wrote that only what grows from the ground is counted there, similar to bundles of clover over which they disagree there. And the Rambam at the end of Avodah Zarah wrote that only Orlah and kilayei hakerem are counted there, as it says "what is fit for Orlah is Orlah; what is fit for kilayei hakerem is kilayei hakerem," etc. Even though in the commentary it appears the mishnah there is R' Meir and his view is better — that specifically also prohibitions of benefit apply; but some say that even according to the Rabbanan it is derived from what is asked in Avodah Zarah there: "and let it teach nut shells," etc.
