שהוא וכו' ועיין ש"ך ואחרונים דימכור הכל לא"י חוץ מדמי איסור שבו וכתב הרשב"א הביאו הטור דאין צריך לומר לא"י הריני מוכר לך חוץ מדמי י"ינ שבו אלא רשאי למוכרו סתם עכ"ל ונראה דצ"ל דא"י אינו יודע מהשער דמוזיל גבי' דאי יודע אם כן הא הא"י מחזיק לו טובה וזו הנאה גמורה והרי נהנה מאיסורי הנאה ומ"ש דנותן לא"י במתנה או דמוזיל גבי' אמת דמשום חשש דלא תחנם לא תתן להם מתנת חנם אין לחוש דאין כוונתו לכך אף דרשב"א נתן טעם דאסור לשחרר עבדו משום מתנת חנם אף על פי שמתכווין שיהיה לו יו"ד להתפלל מכל מקום שאר מחברים לא פירשו כרשב"א לכן נראה ברור דצריך לומר דא"י אינו יודע מהשער כלל ומאמין לישראל כל מה שהוא אומר אם רב אם מעט אם כן אף דמבליע דמי האיסור מכל מקום אין הקונה מרגיש בזה ואין כאן החזקה טובה ודוק: והנה במ"ש הש"ך דהרבה חולקים אהראב"ד וסוברים דלא מהני השלכת דמים לים המלח רק בע"ז שתופסת דמיה ולענ"ד גם דעת הראב"ד כך דהרמב"ם שם הלכות מ"א פרק ט"ז הלכה וי"ו כתב ב"ח חשובי' ולא בטלים כגון שור הנסקל וכו' ופטר חמור שנתערב באלף חמורים כולם אסורים וכתב הראב"ד פטר חמור קיימ"ל כר"א ורשב"ג דיוליך הנאה לים המלח. ויש לדייק למה העמיד הראב"ד דבריו אפטר חמור ולא על כל ב"ח המוזכרים בפתיחת דברי רמב"ם ב"ח וכו' כגון שור הנסקל וכו' אלא דסבירא ליה להראב"ד כל היכי דדמיו נתפסים באיסור שייך בי' יוליך הנאה לים המלח דאיסור קביר בי' אבל כל היכי דדמיו מותרים לא מהני הליכתו לים. ואולם הרמב"ם פסק בהלכות בכורות דאם מכר פטר חמור דמיו אסורין בהנאה וע"ש בראב"ד דתמה וכי פטר חמור תופס דמיו. וע"ש בכ"מ דכתב דצ"ל דהשגת הראב"ד אינו על הרמב"ם רק על הגמרא דגמ' ערוכה היא אם שיהי' הדין אמת שדמי פטר חמור אסור ואיסור פ"ח עליו והוא גמרא ערוכה ולכך ס"ל להראב"ד דמועיל הליכתו לים והעמיד השגתו על פ"ח אבל באינך מודה כיון דאין כאן תפיסת דמים ודוק:

"He is, etc." — See Shach and the acharonim that one may sell everything to a non-Jew except the monetary value of the issur in it; and Rashba wrote that the Tur brought that one need not tell the non-Jew, "I sell to you except for the value of libation wine in it," but one may sell it outright, end of his words; and it appears one must say that the non-Jew does not know the market price at which the Jew reduces the purchase price — for if he knows, then the non-Jew holds a benefit for him and this is full benefit, and behold he benefits from prohibitions of benefit; and what he wrote that one gives to a non-Jew as a gift or reduces the purchase price for him — it is true that because of concern for "do not show them favor, do not give them a free gift" one need not be concerned, for his intent is not for that; even though Rashba gave a reason that it is forbidden to free one's slave because of a free gift, even though he intends that he have a synagogue to pray — nevertheless other authors did not explain like Rashba; therefore it appears clear that one must say the non-Jew does not know the market price at all and relies on the Jew for whatever he says, whether much or little; if so, even though he absorbs the monetary value of the issur, nevertheless the buyer does not perceive this and there is no presumption of benefit here; investigate. And behold in what Shach wrote that many disagree with Raavad and hold that casting money into the Dead Sea does not help except for avodah zarah that seizes its monetary value — and in my view Raavad's view is likewise, for Rambam there in Laws of Forbidden Foods chapter 16 halachah 16 wrote important items and they are not nullified, such as a stoned ox, etc.; and a firstborn donkey that became mixed in a thousand donkeys — all are forbidden; and Raavad wrote regarding firstborn donkey: we maintain like R' Eliezer and Rashbag that one takes the benefit to the Dead Sea. One must investigate why Raavad established his words on firstborn donkey and not on all important items mentioned at the opening of Rambam's words — important items, etc., such as stoned ox, etc.; rather it appears to Raavad that wherever its monetary value is seized in issur, one may take the benefit to the Dead Sea, for the issur is weighty in it; but wherever its monetary value is permitted, his taking it to the sea does not help. However Rambam ruled in Laws of Firstborn that if one sold a firstborn donkey its monetary value is forbidden in benefit — see there in Raavad that he wondered: does a firstborn donkey seize its monetary value? See there in Maggid Mishneh that wrote one must say Raavad's critique is not on Rambam but on the Gemara — the Gemara is arranged: if the law is true that monetary value of firstborn donkey is forbidden and issur of firstborn donkey is upon it, and it is an arranged Gemara — therefore Raavad holds his taking it to the sea helps and he established his critique on firstborn donkey; but in other cases he concedes, since there is no seizure of monetary value here; investigate.

"And sealed barrels." — This is a great matter, even regarding libation wine that forbids in any amount; and so too Rama below in siman 134 seif 2 in the hagahah; and this is unlike Rashba's words in Terumat HaDeshen daf 107, that wrote: this difference between wet and dry applies only to something that is davar sheb'minyan; but in orlah and terumah, just as in wet it forbids more than sixty, so in dry it forbids more — for what is wet and what is dry does not depend on davar sheb'minyan at all; therefore in orlah and the like, wet is not forbidden in any amount — even in dry they are nullified unless it is an important item that is not nullified; but chametz on Pesach and the like, which forbids in wet in any amount — in dry too it forbids in any amount, and one does not need an important item; if so, regarding libation wine one does not need important sealed barrels, for in wet it forbids in any amount; and so too in chametz: in wet in any amount, even in dry we do not say one in two is nullified. Pri Chadash challenged in Orach Chayyim: it appears from Rama's words that stam the first view in Shulchan Aruch siman 447 that chametz in dry in dry we do not say one in two is nullified — whereas here regarding libation wine he concludes unlike Rashba that one specifically needs a sealed important barrel, and if not they are nullified. It appears the root of these matters stems from the Gemara in Avodah Zarah daf 74: these are forbidden and forbid in any amount — libation wine, avodah zarah, and hides of stillborn calves, etc.; and the Gemara challenged: what did the tanna count? If davar sheb'minyan — let him teach pieces of nevelah; if prohibitions of benefit — let him teach chametz on Pesach; and it answered: the tanna has two — prohibitions of benefit and davar sheb'minyan. Rosh wrote: no — this disagrees with R' Chaim Levi that it is not nullified; rather this tanna does not count anything that does not have both; and in the end Rosh proved that chametz on Pesach in dry — if it forbade in any amount, we would say the tanna has two — prohibitions of benefit and davar sheb'minyan — even though chametz forbids in benefit, since there is no davar sheb'minyan it is nullified in the majority, etc., end of his words. One must understand his words — for he already explained: even though we say it requires two — davar sheb'minyan and prohibitions of benefit — davar sheb'minyan alone forbids; only the tanna does not count except what has both; if so, conversely we could say chametz forbids because it has prohibitions of benefit — only the tanna does not count except something that has both; but for the law, chametz that has prohibitions of benefit forbids; and one must say: granted regarding davar sheb'minyan one can say as I wrote that therefore he does not count it except in combination with prohibitions of benefit — and the difficulty itself is difficult: why does the tanna teach only what has both, and why does he not teach even something that has both apart from that? Besides that one can say there is no room to err, since it relies on the mishnah in Chullin that a piece of nevelah is not nullified because it is a piece worthy of honor — it itself teaches that one should not say that where an important item is not nullified, that is eating prohibition, but prohibition of benefit is nullified — as some authors hold; it teaches in the mishnah two — important item and prohibitions of benefit — that it is not nullified even the prohibition of benefit in it; but here there is no need, for he counts only two; but in truth prohibitions of benefit alone are not nullified — this itself is difficult: why does he not count prohibitions of benefit alone, which we do not find in any mishnah; and from this we learn that prohibitions of benefit are not nullified even regarding prohibition of benefit they are not nullified — one must say he does not hold it. And for Rashba who holds chametz forbids even without an important item, the above difficulty stands in its place: why did the tanna not teach it? One must say plainly: the tanna teaches what in wet is nullified — it teaches us, since an important item is not nullified, as he wrote; but where in wet it is not nullified in any amount, it is obvious that in dry too it is not nullified — for what is wet from dry; and as Rashba wrote, therefore he does not teach chametz, since in wet too it forbids in any amount; and all those that he teaches are not forbidden in wet and are forbidden in dry — unlike chametz. But it is difficult: behold libation wine in wet in any amount — if so, in dry too in open barrels it is not nullified, and behold he taught it (and see in Terumat HaDeshen to Rashba regarding what he wrote where in wet in any amount, in dry in any amount — and he wrote a practical difference for chametz and did not write a practical difference for libation wine); therefore it appears regarding libation wine what he wrote wet and what he wrote dry does not apply, that there is reason to distinguish between wet and dry — for Tosafot wrote several times that which they said specifically liquid in its kind is not nullified is because they relied on R' Yochanan who holds species in its species is not nullified; accordingly for R' Yochanan it must be specifically wet; and see what Tosafot wrote that even rabbanan who hold they ascend do not require wet in wet; if so, wherever they established in its kind it is not nullified they relied on R' Yochanan or on rabbanan regarding ascending; if so, wet is specifically required, and Rashba's claim applies only to Rashba speaking for himself regarding chametz — for the reason is not because of R' Yochanan, for even not its kind forbids, and the reason is because of the stringency of chametz; and he challenged well what he wrote wet in dry; if so, regarding libation wine one needs sealed barrels, but for chametz one does not need an important item — and requires study; and there is no contradiction from chametz to libation wine; investigate.

"The last ones, etc." — This is all to resolve that there would not be contradiction in Tur; but Shulchan Aruch who did not make this distinction — if so it is difficult: for here he forbids in the explanation intentionally and in siman 16 regarding oto v'et beno that became mixed he ruled "let us suppress them since they move"; and with this Maharshal and Pri Chadash resolved: since oto v'et beno is davar sheyesh lo matirin — lest he take from kevua does not apply, for tomorrow everything is permitted and from the Torah one does so for one day — what tomorrow is entirely permitted; and one should examine what is written in Orach Chayyim siman 497 regarding eggs that a gentile brings to sell on Yom Tov — even though majority of eggs are from Yom Tov nevertheless forbidden, for we do not follow davar sheyesh lo matirin after majority — so Ran; and it is difficult: nevertheless gentiles who bring to sell in the market from their house and in their house majority of eggs laid on Yom Tov — let us say whatever separates the majority separates; and if it is kevua behold it is not recognizable; and one must say we decree lest he take from kevua; and it is difficult: behold for davar sheyesh lo matirin we are not concerned he will take from kevua — if so this contradicts his reasoning; and one can force to say: regarding an egg in any case there is presumption now that it laid — therefore we are not lenient to rely on separation, etc. since presumption contradicts. And there is proof for Maharshal's reasoning from this that we say in Beitzah: one set aside two and found three — forbidden; and the Gemara says "if you wish": if they are others they are forbidden, and if not behold there is one, etc.; and one can say: why did the Gemara count at all this concern "if they are others" — for this depends on the dispute of Rabbi and rabbanan: he left nine and found ten — R' Eliezer says: stacked coin, scattered coin, all of them; and rabbanan say: hullin — and behold there is clear issur that one not ready became mixed in it (and see Ran and Yesh Seder who wrote in the mishnah only this reason of mixed in); and one must say: if there is only this one mixed in it one issur — it is difficult how it teaches forbidden to "let us suppress them since they move" and whatever separates the majority separates; therefore the Gemara says another reason: they are all others. Only it is difficult: how is it possible to suppress them, etc. — behold there is a decree lest he take from kevua; and until here one must say regarding davar sheyesh lo matirin we do not decree thus — and the matter is forced.

**** ENGLISH ****
"The egg permitted, etc." — Shach cited in the name of Taz that an egg is separated and we do not decree lest he take from kevua — for from an egg regarding a chicken we do not decree, end of his words; and it is unnecessary, for Taz wrote this according to the poskim's approach that even separated of itself we decree lest he take from kevua; but according to the Mechaber's and Rama's ruling who did not emend regarding separated of itself — we are not concerned he will take from kevua in any case; regarding an egg there is no concern, for behold it is separated of itself.
