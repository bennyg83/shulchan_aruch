"And one of them ate, etc." — Terumat HaDeshen's view is that if one ate it b'shogeg, it is not applicable to decree shogeg on account of meizid, for how will he eat issur. And Shach and Taz challenged that we decree lest he feed it to a non-Jew and to a dog — and this is not so great a difficulty, for the explanation of shogeg on account of meizid is that he will do the matter b'meizid and say "I am shogeg" — throw into the sea b'meizid and say "I am shogeg"; if so, here regarding eating how can we say he will eat intentionally and say shogeg — are we suspected that he will eat issur b'meizid? And if he feeds a non-Jew b'meizid and says "I am shogeg" — this is truly permitted, for it is nevelah; but regarding the food that he eats b'shogeg there is no concern that he will be meizid and say shogeg — end of his words.

But this that the mishnah teaches "one who cooks on Shabbos" — dispute of R' Meir and R' Yehudah whether we decree shogeg on account of meizid — I did not understand according to Terumat HaDeshen's words; behold he does not come to cook on Shabbos b'meizid; and just as we are not concerned he will eat issur, how is it possible to be concerned he will do a primary labor on Shabbos to cook by hand.

And the entire sugya we heard in Gittin revolves around this. And one must say that certainly if the normal law is to be concerned and to penalize shogeg on account of meizid in every manner — we are concerned even that he will perform a transgression by hand, whether light or severe; and the Gemara's sugya follows correctly.

But we rule regarding cooking issur that we do not decree shogeg on account of meizid — only Beit Yosef wrote regarding falling to the sea we are more concerned and decree shogeg on account of meizid; and on this is Terumat HaDeshen's view: since the normal law is not to decree at all, only we are stringent where we are stringent when there is concern that nevertheless he will keep the Torah's statute; but here if he transgresses b'meizid he will eat issur so much — we do not act to decree shogeg on account of meizid; and nevertheless there is reason to be concerned for the words of the acharonim to forbid, for this is a mere stringency — requires study in any way.

**** ENGLISH ****
"One of them, etc." — It appears that if one cooked in a vessel and it was eaten and the vessels still exist, it is forbidden — for behold we come to judge the nature of the vessels, whether they require hagalah in a rinsing vessel and earthenware breaking that the issur was cooked in it; if so, behold we judge upon it and the matter returned to safek and everything is included in the safek — and this is obvious.

And if it is difficult — this that the Gemara challenged in chapter HaTaarovot on Rav who said: a ring of avodah zarah that became mixed in a hundred rings and one fell to the sea — all are permitted; and the Gemara challenged: all sacrifices that became mixed in a sin offering of those to die and a stoned ox — all die; why should all die, etc.; and the difficulty is: what is the difficulty? Even regarding that animal that died we need to judge the nature of the hide and the ash whether it is forbidden in benefit; and since we need to judge, everything is included in safek — if so there is no difficulty, for behold it became mixed a forbidden benefit in kodshim, and also kodshim that died is like a stoned ox and all are forbidden in benefit like all kodshim that died, and you need not judge upon it at all.
