(סימן ק"י בש"ע סעיף א') <b>דבר חשוב אוסר במינו בכ"ש כו'</b>. והנה הרב בעל פרי תואר ר"ל הא דד"ח ל"ב היינו בדבר שאסור בהנאה אבל במידי דאסור רק באכילה אפילו ד"ח בטיל ודבריו אין להבין כלל דהא מצינו בעלי חיים ל"ב אפילו דבר שאינו אסור רק באכילה דבדרוסה שנתערב פסק ר"ת דמותר ע"י שיכבשינהו דניידי ואמרי' כל דפריש כו' משמע בלא"ה בעלי חיים ל"ב אע"פ שאין אסור בהנאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 110 in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) <b>An important item forbids in its kind in any amount, etc.</b> Behold, the Rav, author of Peri Toar, explained that the matter of "not nullified in sixty" applies to something forbidden for benefit; but regarding something forbidden only for eating, even an important item is nullified — and his words are entirely incomprehensible, for we find that live animals are never nullified even for something forbidden only for eating: in a gored animal that became mixed, Rabbeinu Tam ruled that it is permitted through suppressing them, since they move, and we say "whatever separates, etc." — it appears that otherwise live animals are never nullified even though there is no prohibition of benefit.

(שם סעיף א') <b>אבל שאר דברים אע"פ שדרכן לימנות כו'</b>. ז"ל הרמב"ם ומקשה עליו מהא דפרק הערל דף פ"ב מי שה"ל חבילי תלתן של כלאי כרם ידלקו נתערבו באחרות ואחרות באחרות כולן ידלקו דר"מ וחכ"א יעלו בא' ומאתים שהיה ר"מ אומר כל שדרכו למנות שנינו א"כ כיון דר"י ור"ל פליגי בדבר שבמנין אי את שדרכו למנות או כל שדרכו למנות שנינו ש"מ ד"ד שבמנין ל"ב א"כ איך פירש הרמב"ם והמחבר נגד ר"י ור"ל וי"ל (הא דפליג) דהנה הא דכתב הרמב"ם כל דבר שהוא חשוב אצל בני אדם במקום מהמקומות כאגוזי פרך ורמוני בדן בא"י כו' א"כ י"ל דפליגי ר"ל ור"י אליביה דרבנן דס"ל ד"ח ל"ב א"כ ס"ל לרבנן דדבר שבמנין הוא בטיל והיינו כיון שאין המנין תלוי בחשיבותיהן כלל רק מחמת הקנין שהמנין מודיע מקח אבל אם הוא דבר שנמכר באומד ובתוך אותו מין יש מין א' שנמכר במנין ד"מ אגוזי פרך דשאר אגוזים נמכרים באומד ואגוזי פרך במנין א"כ ודאי מחמת שהם חשובין יותר משאר אגוזים נמכרים במנין וגם לרבנן ל"ב ולא מחמת דבר שבמנין אלא מחמת שהוא דבר חשוב לפ"ז י"ל דר"י ור"ל פליגי אליבא דרבנן דר"י ס"ל דוקא אם הוא את שדרכו למנות הוי ד"ח אבל אם אותו מין דהיינו אגוזי פרך נמכרים לפעמים באומד לא הוי דבר חשוב ור"ל ס"ל אפילו כה"ג נמי הוי דבר חשוב ולק"מ על המחבר והרמב"ם וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, seif 1) <b>But other things, even though their way is to count them, etc.</b> These are the words of the Rambam; and he challenges him from that which is in the chapter HaAreil, daf 82: one who had bundles of fenugreek of kilayim of the vineyard — they must be burned; if they became mixed with others and others with others, all must be burned — R' Meir; and the Sages say one in two hundred suffices; for R' Meir used to say: anything whose way is to be counted we learned — if so, since R' Yehuda and R' Levi disagree regarding davar sheb'minyan, whether "you count its way" or "anything whose way is to be counted" we learned — infer that davar sheb'minyan is not nullified in sixty; if so, how did the Rambam and Mechaber explain against R' Yehuda and R' Levi? And one may say (this is the dispute): behold, that which the Rambam wrote — any item that is important to people in some places, such as peeled nuts and Badan pomegranates in Eretz Yisrael, etc. — if so, one may say that R' Levi and R' Yehuda dispute according to the view of the rabbis, who hold that an important item is not nullified in sixty; if so, the rabbis hold that davar sheb'minyan is nullified — and that is since counting does not depend on their importance at all, but only because of the purchase, that counting informs the sale; but if it is something sold by estimate and within that species there is one type sold by counting — for example peeled nuts, since other nuts are sold by estimate and peeled nuts by counting — if so, certainly because they are more important than other nuts they are sold by counting, and even for the rabbis it is not nullified — not because of davar sheb'minyan but because it is an important item. Accordingly, one may say that R' Yehuda and R' Levi dispute according to the rabbis: R' Yehuda holds specifically that if it is "you count its way" it is an important item; but if that species — namely peeled nuts — is sometimes sold by estimate, it is not an important item; and R' Levi holds that even in such a case too it is an important item. It remains difficult against the Mechaber and Rambam — investigate.

(שם סעיף ב') <b>ודוקא בעלי חיים קטנים כו'</b>. עש"ך ס"ק י"ג וי"ל בהי' ק"א ס"ק כ"ו והיה נראה לאדמ"ו דש"ה כיון דמעיקרא הוי ב"ח ול"ב א"כ יהיה אח"כ ראוי להתכבד אף שאין ראוי עכשיו להתכבד דמחוסר מעשה אפ"ה ל"ב. וי"ל ראיה לסברא זו מהא דפ' התערובת דף ע"ג דפריך ונכבשינהו דניידי ושמא כל דפריש מרובא פריש ומשני גזירה שמא יבואו עשרה כהנים בב"א ויקרבו א"ל האי מדרבנן אלא מעתה מגופא אסור' אלא שמא יבואו עשרה כהנים בב"א ויקחו כו'. והנה לכאורה מאי קאמר בתחלה שמא יבואו וכו' הא דעודו בחיים ליכא חשש כלל לפי מה דס"ד השתא וליכא חשש אלא אחר מיתה ואחר מיתה אמאי לא יהיה בטיל אע"כ צ"ל כיון שכבר היה ב"ח ואחר הפשט וניתוח יהי' חה"ל ל"ב וכמ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, seif 2) <b>And specifically small live animals, etc.</b> In the Shach, s.k. 13; and one may say in the Beit Yosef, siman 101, s.k. 26 — and it would appear according to Shach's view that since from the outset it is a live animal and is never nullified, if so afterward it will be worthy of honor even though it is not now worthy of honor because it lacks action, nevertheless it is never nullified. And one may say a proof for this view from that which is in chapter HaTaarovot, daf 73 — he challenged: "and we suppress them, since they move, lest 'whatever separates, the majority separates'" — and he answered: a decree lest ten priests come at once and offer. One said to him: this is d'rabbanan; "if so, from the body itself it should be forbidden" — except: lest ten priests come at once and take, etc. Behold, at first glance what does he mean at the outset, "lest they come," etc. — while it is still alive there is no concern at all according to what we now hold, and there is concern only after death; and after death, why should it not be nullified? Until here one must say: since it was already a live animal and after flaying and butchering it becomes a piece worthy of honor, never nullified — as I wrote.

(שם סעיף ג' בהגה"ה) <b>והא דאמרינן כו'</b>. לכאורה הוא סותר מ"ש בא"ח סי' תל"ט סס"א בט' ציבורין של מצה וא' חמץ ובא עכבר ונטלה צריך לחזור ולבדוק דכל קבוע כו' ואם פירש העכבר ממקום קביעתה ובא עכבר ונטלה משם א"צ לחזור ולבדוק דכל דפריש מרובא וכו' ומשמע שאם נטל העכבר ממקום הקביעות אף שלא נטל בפנינו אפ"ה אמרי' דמקרי קבוע ככל קביעות כו' והכא גבי עובד כוכבים אמרי' בנמצא בידו הוי פירש אע"ג דהעובד כוכבים נטלה ממקום קביעתה וי"ל בשלמא התם שאני דמיד שנטל עכבר חל עליו חובת ביעור ואף שלא נטל בפנינו מ"מ השתא כשנודע לו הספק א"כ עיקר השאלה על שעת נטילתה ואז היתה קבוע לכך היה צריך שיטול העכבר אחר שפירש אבל הכא בעובד כוכבים כשלקחו מן המקולין לקחו לעצמו א"כ על אותו שעה לא חל על ישראל שום ספק דהא העובד כוכבים לקח לעצמו ולא נולד ספיקא לישראל אלא השתא כשרוצה ללקחו מן העובד כוכבים אז חל ספק ואז הוי פירש ואמרינן כל דפריש מרובא פריש:
**** ENGLISH ****
(There, seif 3 in the hagahah) <b>And that which we say, etc.</b> At first glance this contradicts what he wrote in Orach Chayyim siman 339 s.v. "nine bundles of matzah and one chametz" — a mouse came and took one, he must return and check, for every fixed case, etc.; and if the mouse separated from the place where it was fixed and a mouse came and took it from there — he need not return and check, for "whatever separates, the majority separates," etc.; and it is implied that if the mouse took from the place of fixing, even though it did not take it before us, nevertheless we say it is called fixed like every fixing, etc. But here regarding a non-Jew we say: if it is found in his hand — it is considered separated, even though the non-Jew took it from the place where it was fixed. And one may say: granted, there it is different — for as soon as the mouse took it, the obligation of removal applies to him; and even though it did not take it before us, nevertheless now when the safek becomes known to him — if so, the main question is about the time of its taking, and then it was fixed; therefore the mouse would have to take it after it separated. But here regarding a non-Jew — when they took from the slaughterhouses they took for themselves; if so, at that time no safek applied to a Jew at all, for behold the non-Jew took for himself and no safek arose for the Jew; rather only now, when one wants to buy from the non-Jew, then the safek applies, and then it is separated, and we say "whatever separates from the majority separates."

(There, in Shulchan Aruch s.k. 7) <b>And specifically when eating the remainder two by two.</b> The Rambam explained that which says "R' Eliezer only permitted with two" — that is, it refers to falling, meaning two must fall; and afterward even if one remains, it is permitted. And he challenges him: according to his view he should not challenge from that which the Sages say, etc.; according to my view one may well say that if one remains it is permitted, and R' Eliezer only spoke regarding the fallen ones. And it appears to me that from here one may say: why did the Gemara not challenge from R' Eliezer himself, who said in explanation "if he offered the first one, all are permitted"? And one must say that the Gemara was in doubt about the explanation of Bava Batra — whether it refers to falling or to eating — and it did not wish to challenge from R' Eliezer himself; one may say that for falling one suffices, but when eating from the remainder one must eat two by two; therefore the Gemara challenges from both sides. And the Sages say: even if they offered all of them and not one remained among them — that one is forbidden; it is implied that according to R' Eliezer if one remained, it is permitted to offer; and this is difficult for R' Eliezer. And if so, if we say that which says "R' Eliezer only permitted with two" refers to falling — the beginning of the mishnah is difficult, that which says in explanation "if he offered the first one, all are permitted" — that is falling; and the Gemara answers: what is "one"? One pair — that is, for falling there must be one pair; but regarding eating one may eat each one independently. And one should not challenge — from where does the Rambam know to explain thus? One may say the Rambam proved that necessarily "one pair" refers to falling — for behold Tosafos challenge: how do we derive from R' Eliezer that if one fell into the sea it is permitted? Behold R' Eliezer derives from a verse that is written "no blemish shall be accepted in it" — it is implied specifically when standing independently; but through mixtures it is permitted; and how do we derive from him other forbidden things — perhaps it is a gezeirah ha-katuv in a korban? And one may say: necessarily the verse refers regarding one in one — for if one in two, otherwise by Torah law it is nullified in the majority; if so we derive well, as we see that the Torah permitted if a blemished animal became mixed — even one relative to one is permitted for offering; if so, the same applies to other forbidden things where one became mixed one in two, for by Torah law it is nullified in the majority — only the Sages decreed; but where it fell properly we say the forbidden item fell — until here from this the Rambam proved that necessarily "one pair" refers to falling; for Tosafos' difficulty is difficult for him; and one must say as he wrote, and it is difficult for him: how do we say that one must eat them two by two? If so, how do we find that one may offer a blemished animal? For behold if one became mixed with one, even offering two at once does not help — for behold the entire reason is that one must eat both at once because we say "since this one was permitted, this one too"; but here there is no need for this, for both are forbidden — or it refers to falling; and we find well that if two became mixed with two and two fell into the sea, we say these two fell — investigate. See Shach, who brought that for something whose falling is recognizable one needs only one; and at first glance there is proof for his words from that which he challenges R' Nachman, who said "a ring," etc. — behold R' Eliezer only permitted with two; and he answered: I too say two; and why did he not also challenge R' Levi, who permits with barrels where one of them fell? Or that in this all agree that one needs only one because its falling is recognizable; but it is difficult from the Gemara's explanation — why two? And derive one from its fellow — behold they are not equal, for with barrels one needs only one, and with rings two must fall — requires study.
