(סימן ק"י בש"ע סעיף א') <b>דבר חשוב אוסר במינו בכ"ש כו'</b>. והנה הרב בעל פרי תואר ר"ל הא דד"ח ל"ב היינו בדבר שאסור בהנאה אבל במידי דאסור רק באכילה אפילו ד"ח בטיל ודבריו אין להבין כלל דהא מצינו בעלי חיים ל"ב אפילו דבר שאינו אסור רק באכילה דבדרוסה שנתערב פסק ר"ת דמותר ע"י שיכבשינהו דניידי ואמרי' כל דפריש כו' משמע בלא"ה בעלי חיים ל"ב אע"פ שאין אסור בהנאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 110 in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) <b>An important item forbids in its kind in any amount, etc.</b> Behold, the Rav, author of Peri Toar, explained that the matter of "not nullified in sixty" applies to something forbidden for benefit; but regarding something forbidden only for eating, even an important item is nullified — and his words are entirely incomprehensible, for we find that live animals are never nullified even for something forbidden only for eating: in a gored animal that became mixed, Rabbeinu Tam ruled that it is permitted through suppressing them, since they move, and we say "whatever separates, etc." — it appears that otherwise live animals are never nullified even though there is no prohibition of benefit.

**** ENGLISH ****
(There, seif 1) <b>But other things, even though their way is to count them, etc.</b> These are the words of the Rambam; and he challenges him from that which is in the chapter HaAreil, daf 82: one who had bundles of fenugreek of kilayim of the vineyard — they must be burned; if they became mixed with others and others with others, all must be burned — R' Meir; and the Sages say one in two hundred suffices; for R' Meir used to say: anything whose way is to be counted we learned — if so, since R' Yehuda and R' Levi disagree regarding davar sheb'minyan, whether "you count its way" or "anything whose way is to be counted" we learned — infer that davar sheb'minyan is not nullified in sixty; if so, how did the Rambam and Mechaber explain against R' Yehuda and R' Levi? And one may say (this is the dispute): behold, that which the Rambam wrote — any item that is important to people in some places, such as peeled nuts and Badan pomegranates in Eretz Yisrael, etc. — if so, one may say that R' Levi and R' Yehuda dispute according to the view of the rabbis, who hold that an important item is not nullified in sixty; if so, the rabbis hold that davar sheb'minyan is nullified — and that is since counting does not depend on their importance at all, but only because of the purchase, that counting informs the sale; but if it is something sold by estimate and within that species there is one type sold by counting — for example peeled nuts, since other nuts are sold by estimate and peeled nuts by counting — if so, certainly because they are more important than other nuts they are sold by counting, and even for the rabbis it is not nullified — not because of davar sheb'minyan but because it is an important item. Accordingly, one may say that R' Yehuda and R' Levi dispute according to the rabbis: R' Yehuda holds specifically that if it is "you count its way" it is an important item; but if that species — namely peeled nuts — is sometimes sold by estimate, it is not an important item; and R' Levi holds that even in such a case too it is an important item. It remains difficult against the Mechaber and Rambam — investigate.

(There, seif 2) <b>And specifically small live animals, etc.</b> In the Shach, s.k. 13; and one may say in the Beit Yosef, siman 101, s.k. 26 — and it would appear according to Shach's view that since from the outset it is a live animal and is never nullified, if so afterward it will be worthy of honor even though it is not now worthy of honor because it lacks action, nevertheless it is never nullified. One said to him: this is d'rabbanan; "if so, from the body itself it should be forbidden" — except: lest ten priests come at once and take, etc. Behold, at first glance what does he mean at the outset, "lest they come," etc. — while it is still alive there is no concern at all according to what we now hold, and there is concern only after death; and after death, why should it not be nullified? Until here one must say: since it was already a live animal and after flaying and butchering it becomes a piece worthy of honor, never nullified — as I wrote.
