<b>עד אחר הטבילה. </b>טעמייהו דכיון דבטבילת גר אין שייך שיברך קודם הטבילה דעדיין עובד כוכבים הוא לא חילקו חכמים בין הטבילות:

Until after the immersion. Their reason is that since for a convert's immersion one cannot recite the blessing before the immersion — as he is still a non-Jew — the sages did not distinguish between the immersions.

זה כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****She covers herself, etc. In Derishah he elaborated on this law and wrote that our women who bless on immersion while still naked in the water and do not muddy the water — they do not act properly, even though they embrace their arms on their body; for according to what Maharach wrote the reason they must be murky is so the heart should not see their nakedness — arm embrace should help to separate between heart and nakedness; but regarding a woman there is no concern of heart seeing nakedness since her hair is very low, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Acharon in Orach Chaim siman 74 — and these are his words: it appears women can bless and pray when dressed in their robe even without separation below the chest because their hair is very low — end of quote. It implies that for women the prohibition is because they stand naked without covering nakedness, and it is not proper conduct because of honor of the Shechinah; and if so what does arm embrace help — if because heart sees nakedness, behold no separation is needed for a woman; and perforce because of honor of the Shechinah that she should not stand naked — what does this separation between heart and nakedness help? This is the conclusion of his words there. And in my opinion, women who do so with arm embrace do well even initially; and even without arm embrace they have upon what to rely — for what he wrote that there is prohibition in standing naked because of honor of the Shechinah is astonishing — why seek reasons because of honor of the Shechinah? Behold it is an explicit Torah prohibition because of "and your nakedness shall not be seen," as is written regarding separating challah in Yoreh De'ah siman 328 — a man is forbidden to cut challah and say the blessing because of revelation of nakedness; and in all places they forbade praying opposite a fellow's nakedness, and likewise opposite his own nakedness if revealed even though his heart is covered — and this is simple even for schoolchildren. Rather it appears simple and proven that if a person stands inside water, even if clear, there is no revelation of nakedness concern, not like Derishah who holds all clear water is as standing naked; and explicit proof from end of Mi sheMeto — we learned "he should cover himself in water and recite Shema" — we challenge: but his heart sees the nakedness — we answer in murky water they taught, for like resting on ground; and if you say clear water is because of revelation of nakedness, what difference that the challenger mentions heart — he should challenge "but he transgresses 'and your nakedness shall not be seen'" meaning revelation of nakedness, even if heart covered; rather it is simple that whenever his hair is covered in water, even if clear, nevertheless there is no revelation upon them, even though when intending to see in water opposite his nakedness he can see it — nevertheless since without intending to see that place through water there is no revelation concern — similar to fish in water seeing the sun in Nedarim (page 30) that I cited in siman 217 seif 36. And likewise Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim siman 206 in the name of Rashba — and these are his words: "but behold his heart sees the nakedness" — meaning his heart with the nakedness inside the water; but for his eyes seeing nakedness there is no concern, for since his eyes are outside the water and he looks outside he does not see the nakedness — end of quote. Behold even for heart seeing there is no concern unless it is with the nakedness inside the water, as the Mishnah implies "he should cover himself in water"; but if the heart is outside the water there is no concern. And likewise proven from Rabbenu Tam that Beit Yosef brought in Orach Chaim siman 74 — when he washed in clear hot water and wished to drink, he covered with a garment from below his heart so his heart should not see the nakedness — end of quote. And if clear water while standing naked, what did covering his heart fix? And Derishah wrote Rabbenu Tam sat inside a vessel — therefore no revelation concern; this is not correct at all, for even sitting in a vessel with revealed nakedness is certainly complete prohibition whenever there is no water, for he himself sees his nakedness; proof from what we say chapter 1 of Sukkah end page 10 — Shmuel: one who sleeps in a bed naked and it is ten handbreadths high — he puts his head outside the bed and recites Shema, for he holds heart seeing nakedness is permitted — so Tosafot; it is found for us forbidden, and it is exactly like a vessel Rabbenu Tam washed in. Rather it is clear there is no relevance of "and your nakedness shall not be seen" except when his hair is revealed not in water — then forbidden whenever he recites opposite it unless he turns his face as explained in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim siman 75. But if he is inside water there is no revelation concern and it is like turning the face, only there is prohibition because of heart seeing nakedness — similar to one who covered himself in clothing and did not separate between his heart and nakedness; and if so a woman immersing and standing in water after immersion, certainly the separation she makes with her hands between her heart and nakedness helps, as written in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim siman 74 — complete permission. And initially it would be better her heart not enter the water so heart and nakedness should not be inside water together, as I mentioned in the name of Rashba; but greater modesty that she be entirely covered to her neck in water as Shulchan Aruch wrote in Hilchot Niddah, and afterward embrace with hands to separate since Shulchan Aruch already ruled in Orach Chaim siman 74 seif 36 it is a separation. And those standing in water at time of blessing and do not separate with hands between heart and nakedness — they also have upon whom to rely, namely Acharon that Beit Yosef brought as I remembered above, that regarding a woman heart seeing her nakedness does not apply at all since it is very low; and therefore she can bless if she has covering garment even without separation between heart and nakedness under the garment; and we establish the Gemara statement that at time of separating challah her face should be pressed to ground — because of revelation of nakedness when naked — simple matter even though for a woman there is no heart seeing nakedness even when standing; nevertheless there is revelation of nakedness when standing, as in chapter 1 of Sotah (page 8) — a woman is stoned while covered in front and behind according to Rabbi Yehudah, because all of her is nakedness, and Rashi explained her place of shame is visible front and back; therefore in Gemara end of Mi sheMeto regarding challah she must be sitting, and even sitting is insufficient because of her buttocks that are revealed — therefore they required face pressed to ground meaning stuck very to ground so even her buttocks are covered. And if so, a woman standing with covering garment covering her nakedness and buttocks — even without separation between heart and nakedness — no prohibition according to this opinion of Acharon that Beit Yosef brought; and Maharach holds regarding standing there is no revelation prohibition according to this Acharon, and face need not be pressed to ground, only to cover buttocks; therefore he challenged what seemed in the Gemara there — from Mi sheMeto regarding buttocks there is no nakedness concern, why must she be sitting — behold standing too there is no prohibition since heart does not see nakedness; and therefore he rejected Acharon's opinion from the law. He was not precise — certainly revelation of nakedness prohibition exists even when standing, as we mentioned; rather Acharon informs us that whenever nakedness and buttocks are covered by garment, even without separation between heart and nakedness, it is permitted like sitting with face pressed to ground, since her hair is very low there is no heart seeing nakedness prohibition. And it appears clear that also the Rosh holds like Acharon, for the Tur wrote this siman from him — and these are his words: and likewise my master the Rosh wrote — when she removes her garment while standing in her robe she blesses, removes the robe and immerses — end of quote. Behold he was concerned only for revelation of nakedness alone; but when wearing a robe it suffices even without separation between heart and nakedness, for such is the way of walking in a robe — he did not require a belt to separate; rather clearly he holds like Acharon above that for a woman there is no heart seeing nakedness concern. And with this the Tur's language is resolved, that he elaborated the Rosh's words and did not write briefly "and likewise my master the Rosh" as is his way, but clear matter — since above from him he wrote Raavad's words that she bless before immersion, and if she did not bless before immersion she must muddy the water — perforce because of heart seeing nakedness, for he does not hold like Acharon above; and the Tur wrote the Rosh holds regarding blessing before immersion like Raavad not like Rabbi Yochanan, and Beit Hagmig who wrote to bless after immersion; but regarding what Raavad wrote she must muddy water — the Rosh does not hold so, rather it suffices while robe is on her she blesses and we are not concerned for heart seeing nakedness. And if so, since Acharon and the Rosh hold that for a woman heart seeing nakedness does not apply, and Shulchan Aruch already ruled in Orach Chaim siman 74 — who can object to any woman who relies on this; and whenever she is in water, even if clear, automatically her nakedness and buttocks are covered as if garment were on her front and back, and there is no revelation concern as we proved above — we do not care about heart seeing nakedness; but nevertheless better to do arm embrace to separate between heart and nakedness while in water — she fulfilled her obligation according to the law. And even though Rama wrote here they practice to cover with garment or robe — possibly in his days they practiced so, but now they do not practice as Derishah testified; rather he wrote they do not act properly; and we too heard women are not careful even with arm embrace separating between heart and nakedness — and it appears this too is according to the law, only initially one should do arm embrace as we said — so it seems to me.
