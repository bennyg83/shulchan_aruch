<b>אפילו בבקעה כו'. </b>אף על פי שאין אדם מקפיד עליו דקי"ל אפי' ויתור אסור במודר הנאה וכתב הב"ח נראה דאם אינו מקצר דרכו ליכא הנאה ושרי דלא גרע מפורע חובו דבסמוך עכ"ל ול"נ דל"ד דבפורע חובו לא מתהני כלום ולא הוי אלא גרמא אבל הכא דמתהני בעצמו בדריסת הרגל אסור וכן משמע ממה שכתבו רש"י והרא"ש והר"ן לקמן גבי אמר הנאה המביאה לידי מאכל דאסור לעבור דרך שדהו לקצר דרכו ללכת לבית המשתה ולא כתבו כן ברישא דמתני' אלמא דוקא התם לא מיתסר אלא כשמקצר דרכו משום דאל"כ ליכא הנאה המביאה לידי מאכל כשמקצר דרכו לאכול מהרה אבל הכא דאסור בכל הנאה א"כ בדריסת הרגל אסור בכל ענין ואע"ג דרש"י פי' גם במתני' דריסת הרגל לעבור בחצרו ולקצר דרכו נראה דלאו דוקא קאמר דהא לקמן סוף סעיף ד' מוכח דדריסת הרגל אסור בכל ענין והוא מהש"ס ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even in a valley, etc.</b> Although people are not particular about such passage, we hold that even a minor concession is forbidden to one who is prohibited from deriving benefit. The Bach writes that if he does not shorten his route, there is no benefit and it is permitted, no worse than paying his debt as discussed below. This does not appear correct to me. When another pays his debt, he derives no direct benefit and it is only an indirect cause, whereas here he himself benefits from walking through the property, so it is forbidden. This is also implied by Rashi, the Rosh, and the Ran below regarding one who says, “Benefit that leads to food is forbidden to me.” They write that he may not pass through the other's field to shorten his route to a feast, but they do not say this in the opening clause of the Mishnah. Evidently, only there is it forbidden specifically when he shortens his route, because otherwise there is no benefit leading to food. By shortening the route he reaches the food sooner. Here, however, all benefit is forbidden, so walking through the property is forbidden in every manner. Although Rashi also explains “walking through the property” in the Mishnah as passing through his courtyard to shorten his route, this is not precise, for the end of seif 4 below proves from the Gemara that walking through the property is forbidden in every manner.

<b>קרדום כו'. </b>ואיתא בירושלמי ר"פ אין בין המודר דמותר להשאילו כוסות וקערות ותמחויים שאינן מהנין את המאכל אבל מכנסין האוכל לפסי' (כלומר לאלפסין) וקדרות אסור לטחון ולדרוך לקצור צריכא (כלומר מבעיא) ולבצור צריכא ומביאו ב"י וצ"ל דקורדם שמבקעין בו גרע טפי לפי שהוא מהנה האוכל ומתקנו שא"א לבשל בלא עצים א"כ הקורדם שמבקעים בו עצים מתקן המאכל אבל כוסות וקערות ותמחויין אינם מהנין המאכל שכבר המאכל נתקן כהלכתו אלא שנותנו לקערות וכוסות ותמחויים והלכך אלפסים וקדרות אסורים לפי שהמאכל מתבשל בהן והן מתקנים המאכל תו איתא התם בירושלמי רבי בון ברבי חייא מעתה אסור להשאיל לו מעות שהוא כנותן לו מעות ליקח בו ככר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An axe, etc.</b> The Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter Ein Bein HaMudar states that one may lend him cups, bowls, and serving dishes, because they do not improve the food, but vessels into which the food is placed for cooking, namely pans and pots, are forbidden. Whether grinding, pressing, harvesting, and gathering grapes are forbidden remains a question. The Beit Yosef cites this. It must be explained that an axe used to split wood is worse, because it improves and prepares the food, since cooking is impossible without wood. Thus, an axe used to split wood assists in preparing the food. Cups, bowls, and serving dishes do not improve the food, since the food has already been properly prepared and is merely placed in them. Pans and pots are therefore forbidden because the food is cooked in them and they prepare it. The Yerushalmi further states: Rabbi Bun son of Rabbi Chiya said, according to this reasoning it should be forbidden to lend him money, for that is like giving him money with which to buy a loaf.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He may, etc.</b> This applies only when done without the debtor's knowledge, as the Ran writes and the Beit Yosef cites in siman 217, and as is clear from the authorities in this siman. The Bach rules in accordance with Rabbenu Chananel, Rabbenu Tam, the Rosh, and the Tur that one may not pay his debt when it is evidenced by a document or secured by collateral, since it is a definite debt. Chanan's ruling was stated only regarding support of one's wife and children, and similar oral debts. This also appears to be the view of the Sema in Choshen Mishpat, beginning of siman 128, seif katan 3. The Bach further writes that if the creditor agreed not to compel payment, but to allow the debtor to pay whenever he wished, another may pay the debt for one prohibited from benefit, even where there is a promissory note or collateral. It appears that collateral is comparable to a document only when the payer may satisfy the debt and retain the collateral for himself, rather than return it to the debtor. Although the creditor agreed not to compel payment, he is not obligated to return the collateral without payment, unless he expressly agreed to do so. If the payer is the one who prohibited the debtor from benefit, he must return the collateral to the debtor without charge. See below, siman 223, seif katan 5.

<b>להעיד כו'. </b>דמצוה שלא לכבוש עדותו באם לא יגיד כו' ואין נודרין לבטל לאוין כדלקמן סימן רל"ט עכ"ל עט"ז וקשה דא"כ מאי איריא עדות כל חייבי לאוין נמי ועוד דמשמע הכא יכול ואין איסור בדבר ואי איתא ה"ל למימר חייב אלא נראה דהכא שאני כיון שנדר הנאה ממנו דנדרים חלים על דבר מצוה כדלעיל סימן רט"ו ואפי' אם אינה חלה או בשבועה הכא כיון דנדר הנאה ממנו ה"ל איסור כולל דחיילא כדלקמן סי' רל"ט ס"ד א"נ מיירי שעכשיו רוצה להיות עד לראות המעשה כדי להעיד וה"א דאינו רשאי משום שמהנהו קמ"ל דליכא בזה משום הנאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To testify, etc.</b> The Ateret Zekenim writes that it is a mitzvah not to withhold testimony, as Scripture states, “If he does not testify,” and one cannot vow to nullify a negative commandment, as stated below in siman 239. This is difficult. If so, why mention testimony specifically, for the same should apply to every negative commandment. Furthermore, the wording here implies that he may testify and that no prohibition is involved. According to that explanation, it should have said that he is obligated. Rather, this case is different. Since he vowed against deriving benefit from the other, and vows take effect even regarding mitzvah matters, as stated above in siman 215, the prohibition applies. Even where a vow would not take effect, or where this was an oath, the prohibition against deriving benefit is a comprehensive prohibition and therefore takes effect, as stated below in siman 239, seif 4. Alternatively, the case concerns one who now wishes to observe the event in order to testify later. One might have thought that he may not do so because he thereby benefits the other; the ruling teaches that this is not considered benefit.

<b>ולא ימכור כו'. </b>דגזרינן אטו לא יקנה וכן לא ישאילנו ולא ילונו [כו'] גזירה אטו להשאיל וללות. ש"ס. ופרישה כתב ולא ימכור ולא יקנה ממנו ר"ל זבינא מיצעי ע"ד שנתבאר בר"ס רכ"ז עכ"ל וז"א דדוקא התם דאסר מכל ישראל לא מיתסר משום גזירה אלא בזבינא מיצעא אבל הכא דאסור משום גזירה בכל ענין אסור וכ"כ הב"ח וכן משמע להדיא מהרמב"ם פ"ז מה"נ ומביאו הטור לקמן סימן רכ"ז וכ"כ הר"ן להדיא ריש דף מ"ג ע"ש גם בעט"ז לא כוון יפה שכתב ולא ימכור לו שמא יוזיל לו ויהנהו וכן לא יקח ממנו שמא יקח ביוקר ויהנהו עכ"ל והא ליתא דאפילו אינו מהנהו במכירה אסור משום גזרה וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he may not sell, etc.</b> This is prohibited as a decree lest he purchase from him. Likewise, he may not lend an object or money to him, as a decree lest he borrow an object or money from him. Gemara. The Perishah writes that “he may neither sell to him nor buy from him” means only an average sale, as explained at the beginning of siman 227. This is incorrect. There, where he prohibited himself from all Jews, the restriction is not based on a decree and therefore applies only to an average sale. Here, however, the prohibition is a decree and applies in every type of sale. The Bach writes likewise. This is also explicit in Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchot Nedarim, cited by the Tur below in siman 227, and in the Ran at the beginning of page 43; see there. The Ateret Zekenim also did not explain correctly when he wrote that he may not sell to him lest he discount the price and benefit him, and may not buy from him lest he pay too much and benefit him. Even where the sale itself provides no benefit, it remains forbidden because of the decree.

<b>תפיסת יד. </b>דמקבל (בעיסקא) [בטסקא]. ש"ס. ופירש"י והרא"ש דהיינו שהשוכר קבלו לתת ממנו מס בכל שנה ושנה והר"ן פירש שאינו נוטל חלק ידות בריוח המרחץ אלא נותן לו דבר קצוב בכל שנה. והרמב"ם והסמ"ג מפרשים דהיינו שאין למשכיר אלא דמי השכירות בלבד ולא נשאר לו בגוף המרחץ שום שיור וכדאיתא בב"י:

The Ran explains that the owner does not take a percentage of the bathhouse's profits, but receives a fixed amount each year. Rambam and the Semag explain that the landlord retains only the rental payment and no ownership interest in the bathhouse itself, as stated in the Beit Yosef.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if he explicitly prohibited it, etc.</b> The Perishah writes that even the first opinion and the Rosh in the following seif concede here, because the owner does not prohibit the bathhouse to the tenant, but only to an unrelated Shimon. This is incorrect. The first opinion and the Rosh below explain the Gemara's ruling here, that if the owner retains no interest it is permitted, as referring to a konam. Tosafot at the beginning of chapter HaShutafin and the Rosh also imply that there is no distinction. The Ran, on the contrary, holds the reverse. The owner can prohibit the bathhouse to the tenant, and likewise can prohibit it to everyone, including the tenant, because a konam removes a lien and thereby wholly terminates the tenant's right. But he cannot prohibit it to another person, because the tenant's lien remains. When that other person benefits, he benefits from the tenant, not from the one who imposed the prohibition. The Rivash concludes likewise in responsum 355 and writes that this view appears correct.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With the language of hekdesh.</b> This implies that regarding hekdesh everyone agrees that the owner can sanctify it. The Beit Yosef likewise cites no dissenting view. Rabbenu Yerucham, however, writes in netiv 1, part 1, in the name of the Rashba and Rabbenu Yonah that whether the property is rented or mortgaged, the owner cannot sanctify it even if its value exceeds the debt. This requires careful consideration. The limitation applies only where he rented this specific house; if he rented an unspecified house, he may sanctify one house and provide another. The Ran in chapter HaShutafin and the Rivash in responsum 355 write similarly, and the Rivash states that the later authorities agreed. The Yerushalmi in chapter Makom SheNahagu supports this. It states that if one rented a house to another and then sanctified it, the tenant continues living there and pays the rent to hekdesh. This applies when he had not prepaid the rent. If he prepaid, he continues living there without further payment. Tosafot and the Rosh understood the Yerushalmi to distinguish between prepaid and unpaid rent regarding the owner's power to sanctify the house. The Rosh therefore questioned why this should depend on prepayment, since rental of land is acquired by a document and possession, with money merely completing the transaction. Their interpretation is difficult. If the house became hekdesh when rent was not prepaid, how could the tenant continue living there, as the Gemara in Arakhin asks regarding misuse of hekdesh? Also, why does the Yerushalmi say that when rent was prepaid he lives there without payment? Perhaps they had another version of the Yerushalmi, as Tosafot in Arakhin cite a different formulation. Nevertheless, even their version can be explained as I have stated. The Semag cites the version described above and also distinguishes between prepaid and unpaid rent regarding whether the sanctification takes effect, but this is incorrect. Rather, both clauses of the Yerushalmi concern a house already acquired by the tenant. Its purpose is to reject the assumption that even when rent was prepaid the tenant must pay rent to hekdesh because hekdesh removes liens. That is not so, because the owner cannot sanctify the house so as to remove the tenant's lien. When rent was not prepaid, the tenant pays the rent to hekdesh, because although the owner cannot sanctify the house to remove the tenant's lien, he can sanctify the rental payments owed to him. When rent was prepaid, the tenant lives there without further payment, because the rent was already paid and the owner cannot undo the transaction. This is explicit in Rivash, responsum 355. He writes that if the owner rented this specific house, the tenant does not pay rent to hekdesh and does not violate misuse by living there, because the sanctification takes effect only after the rental term. Monetary hekdesh does not remove the tenant's lien, even if rent was not prepaid, since the tenant has a lien on that house. If rent was prepaid, he lives there without payment; if it was not prepaid, he pays the rent to hekdesh, because when the body of the house was sanctified, its rental income was included. Tosafot in Arakhin and the Rivash, citing the Tosefta, likewise write that the landlord cannot sanctify the tenant's present use. The Gemara in Arakhin can be understood as referring to an unspecified house, as Rabbenu Yerucham, the Ran, and the Rivash write. It may also refer to a case where the landlord retains a proprietary interest. The Gemara inferred from the phrase “and he sanctified it” that the sanctification took effect, and therefore asked how the tenant could live there. The Rama writes below in siman 228, seif 7, regarding collateral, that some say the owner cannot sanctify even the surplus value. This is based on Rabbenu Yerucham cited above. It is therefore surprising that here he rules without qualification that the owner can sanctify the property. This requires study.

<b>מותר לתקוע בו כו'. </b>משמע אפילו הוא עצמו ותימה דבא"ח סימן תקפ"ו ס"ה כתב המודר הנאה משופר אדם אחר תוקע בו וזה יוצא משמע אבל הוא עצמו לא וכמו שכתב בבית יוסף בשם הכל בו וצ"ע. ולענין הלכה הביא בב "ח בא"ח שם מחלוקת הפוסקים בזה ופסק דבשעת הדחק יכול לתקוע אפי' בעצמו וע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He may sound it, etc.</b> This implies that even the prohibited person himself may sound the shofar. This is surprising, because Orach Chaim, siman 586, seif 5, states that when one is prohibited from benefit from a shofar, another person sounds it and he fulfills his obligation, implying that he may not sound it himself, as the Beit Yosef writes in the name of Kol Bo. This requires study. For practical law, the Bach in Orach Chaim there cites a dispute among the authorities and rules that in pressing circumstances he may sound it himself. See there.

<b>יצא. </b>דמצות לאו ליהנות ניתנו אבל ביום ראשון לא יצא דבעינן ולקחתם לכם משלכם ולא משל אסורי הנאה וכן כתב הרב בא"ח סי' תרמ"ט ס"ב ע"ש. דין המודרים בהנאה אם מצטרפין לזימון נתבאר בא"ח ס"ס קצ"ו עיין שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He has fulfilled his obligation.</b> Because mitzvot were not given for physical benefit. On the first day, however, he has not fulfilled his obligation, because Scripture requires, “You shall take for yourselves,” meaning from your own property and not from property forbidden for benefit. The Rama writes likewise in Orach Chaim, siman 649, seif 2. The law concerning persons prohibited from benefiting from one another joining for zimun is explained in Orach Chaim, end of siman 196; see there.
