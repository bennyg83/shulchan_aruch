**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Ibid., Shach seif katan 45, I wrote) Perhaps they had a different text of the Yerushalmi, etc.</b> After my book was printed, I found, thank God, that Rosh in the chapter HaShoel cites the wording of the Yerushalmi as I wrote. From what he writes there in the name of his teacher, Maharam, who brought proof from the Yerushalmi, it is explicit that Maharam interpreted the Yerushalmi as I did. See Sema, siman 312, seif katan 2, and Maadanei Melech to the chapter HaShoel, where you will find proof for my words. Examine this carefully. <b>I further found</b> that Maharshal, in the chapter Shor Shenagach Et HaParah, siman 33, ruled unlike Ran on the basis of a garment afflicted with tzaraat and a sandal afflicted with tzaraat. He likewise left the Yerushalmi unresolved and rejected the Tosefta from the halachah. His words are astonishing for several reasons. First, this is not stated in the Gemara in the chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah, but only by Rashi, and Tosafot in Arakhin likewise wrote it only in Rashi's name. Furthermore, it appears clear to me that Rashi wrote this only regarding idolatry. This is also evident from Rashi there, since he did not write it earlier regarding afflictions. Alternatively, garments are different, because Scripture states, “It is malignant,” from which the Sifra derives: place a curse upon it so that one may not benefit from it. This does not necessarily apply to houses, for even after it is demolished it is still called a house, as stated in the Gemara there. Examine this carefully. Although Korban Aharon at the end of parashat Tazria writes that the Sifra's reasoning is that the word “malignant” is not merely a description of the tzaraat, for then Scripture should have written “a cursed tzaraat,” but instead says “malignant,” teaching that it proceeds from the curse of the garment and renders it forbidden for benefit, this still does not compel the same conclusion regarding a house afflicted with tzaraat, even though the phrase “malignant tzaraat” is written there as well. It may be that the Sifra derives the law from the fact that the phrase “malignant tzaraat” appears twice regarding an afflicted garment, which is why the Sifra does not expound it this way regarding an afflicted house. Rambam rules at the beginning of chapter 15 of Hilchot Tumat Tzaraat that both an afflicted garment and an afflicted house are forbidden for benefit because Scripture says “malignant tzaraat.” This means that he ruled in accordance with the conclusion of the Yerushalmi in the final chapter of Orlah, halachah 3, which derives from “malignant tzaraat” that an afflicted house is forbidden for benefit. It is not as Kesef Mishneh appears to understand, that Rambam independently compared an afflicted house to the afflicted garment mentioned in the Sifra. See there. Nevertheless, it remains possible that the Sifra speaks specifically about a garment. Even if it also refers to an afflicted house, the baraita in Arakhin may follow the other opinion in that Yerushalmi, which permits benefit from an afflicted house, an opinion Rambam cites in his commentary to the Torah at the end of parashat Tazria. See there. <b>Also,</b> Maharshal's rejection of Ran's proof is strained. He says that when the kohen demolished the house he must provide another one because he transgressed, even though Rashi explains it that way. If so, the same should apply to a house that was definitively declared afflicted. Furthermore, does committing a transgression make the house his property and place it in his possession with respect to monetary liability? Examine this carefully. Apart from this, I already wrote in Siftei Kohen that even if the passage in Arakhin refers to this particular house, there is no difficulty, because it may concern a case in which the landlord retained some right of use in it. My heart tells me that Maharshal did not know that Rashba, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rivash, and other later authorities agreed with Ran. He apparently thought Ran stood alone, and therefore cited only Ran's words. Had he known that all these great authorities held this way, and certainly that the Tosefta and Yerushalmi support their view, he would have retracted. I later found in Teshuvot Maimoniyot to Sefer Mishpatim, siman 27, a responsum of Rabbeinu Shimshon ben Avraham stating that the passage in Arakhin must be understood as referring to an unspecified house. See there. This is explicit support for my words.

<b>(שם ס"ק מ"ב) הקשה על אוהל מועד </b>ולק"מ דכיון דבעבודת כוכבים שרי אע"ג דכתיב ולא ידבק (בך)[בידך] מאומה מן החרם כיון דקלקולו יותר מתקונו אם כן כ"ש בנדרים ומ"ש וראיה מפורשת כו' כיון דאמרינן דדוקא גבי עבודת כוכבים גלי רחמנא ולא במקום אחר כו'. לא דק דאין כוונת התוספות לומר דדוקא גבי עבודת כוכבים דגלי רחמנא אמרינן הכי דאם כן בכל דוכתי דילפינן במה מצינו היכא מצינן למילף נימא היכא דגלי גלי ובכל דוכתי בש"ס פרכינן ולילף מיניה וצריכין לשנויי דלא מצינן למילף מיני' ובכה"ג אמרינן דוקא מדגלי רחמנא התם דוקא הוא וזו מדה בתורה אלא כוונת התוספות דדוקא לענין עבודת כוכבים לא חשיב נהנה כיון שקלקולו יותר מתיקונו ואם כן ס"ס לא נהנה כלומר דהקלקול גורם ולא חשיב נהנה אבל גבי שבת נהי דהקלקול גורם דלא נהנה ס"ס עשה מלאכה וודאי דהעושה מלאכה אפילו אינו נהנה חייב אבל שיהא נחשב מקלקל ולא הוי מלאכה זה לא אמרינן זהו כוונת התוס' גם מה שאמר דהא גם בנדרים אנו הולכים אחר כוונת הנודר כו' אינו כלום דמה בכך סוף סוף אין כאן הנאה. גם מ"ש מויתור דאסור במודר הנאה אינו כלום דהתם סוף סוף יש כאן הנאה אבל הכא כיון דקלקולו יותר מתיקונו אין כאן הנאה גם מה שהביא מהראב"ד אינה ראיה וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Ibid., seif katan 42) He challenged Ohel Moed.</b> There is no difficulty. Since it is permitted regarding idolatry, even though Scripture states, “Nothing from the condemned property shall cling to your hand,” because its damage exceeds its benefit, then certainly the same applies to vows. His statement that there is an explicit proof because we say that only regarding idolatry did the Torah reveal this rule and not elsewhere is imprecise. Tosafot did not mean that the rule applies only to idolatry because the Torah revealed it there. Were that so, whenever the Gemara derives one law from another through a common characteristic, one could always object that the rule applies only where Scripture revealed it. Yet throughout the Gemara we ask why another law should not be derived from that source, and an answer is required explaining why the derivation cannot be made. Only in such a case do we say that the Torah revealed the rule specifically there, and this is one of the Torah's methods of interpretation. Rather, Tosafot meant that regarding idolatry he is not considered to have benefited, because its damage exceeds its benefit. In the final analysis he did not benefit, since the damaging result is what caused the action, and it is not considered benefit. Regarding Shabbat, however, even though the damaging result means that he derived no benefit, he nevertheless performed a melachah. One who performs a melachah is certainly liable even if he receives no benefit. We do not say that it is therefore classified as destructive and ceases to be a melachah. This is Tosafot's intent. His further statement that regarding vows we also follow the intention of the person making the vow is of no consequence, because in the final analysis there is no benefit here. His proof from the rule that even a trivial concession is forbidden to one who is prohibited from benefit is likewise irrelevant. There, there is ultimately some benefit, whereas here, since the damage exceeds the benefit, there is no benefit. The proof he brought from Raavad is also no proof. This is straightforward.
