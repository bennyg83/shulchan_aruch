**** ENGLISH ****(There Shach note 45 I wrote) and perhaps they had a different version in the Yerushalmi etc. And after printing my books I found Talmud in Rosh perek HaSho'el who brought the Yerushalmi language as I wrote; and from what he wrote there in the name of his teacher Maharam, who brought proof from the Yerushalmi, it is proven explicitly that Maharam explained the Yerushalmi as I wrote. And see Semag siman 312 note 2 and in Sefer Ma'adanei Melekh perek HaSho'el you will find proof for my words — examine carefully. And further I found in Maharshal perek Shor she-Nagach et haParah siman 33 that he ruled not like Ran from biased garment and biased sandal, and he also left the Yerushalmi in wonder, and also rejected the Tosefta from halacha. And his words are astonishing for several reasons: one, that it is not mentioned thus in the Gemara perek Mitzvat Chalitzah except in Rashi, and also Tosafot in Arakhin did not write thus except in Rashi's name. And further it appears clear to me that also Rashi did not write thus except regarding idolatry, and likewise is proven in Rashi there that he did not write thus before this regarding negaim; and one may say garments are different, for the verse revealed to us "it is a curse — give him a curse that he not benefit from them," as stated in Torat Kohanim — which is not so regarding houses, for even though one demolishes it is called a house as stated in the Gemara there — examine carefully. (And even though in Sefer Korban Aharon end of perek Tazria he wrote the reason of Torat Kohanim is that the word "from a curse" is not an adjective for tzara'at, for otherwise it should write "tzara'at curse" and it says "from a curse" — we derive it "going out from the curse of the garment" and it forbids it in benefit — so far his language; according to this it implies the same regarding a biased house, for it is also written there tzara'at from a curse. Nevertheless it is not necessary, for one may also say the reason of Torat Kohanim is because it is written regarding biased garment twice "tzara'at from a curse," and therefore Torat Kohanim does not derive thus regarding biased house. And even though Rambam ruled in beginning of perek 15 of Hilchot Tumah that biased garment and biased house are forbidden in benefit as it says "tzara'at from a curse" etc. — meaning he ruled like the conclusion of the Yerushalmi last perek of Orlah halacha 3, where they derive there from "tzara'at from a curse" that biased house is forbidden in benefit, and not as appears from Kesef Mishneh there that Rambam compared on his own biased house to biased garment mentioned in Torat Kohanim — see there. But nevertheless it is possible Torat Kohanim deals specifically with garment, and even if it also deals with biased house, nevertheless one may say the baraita in Arakhin holds like the other opinion in the Yerushalmi there that biased house is permitted in benefit, and Rambam brings it in commentary on the Torah end of perek Tazria — see there.) Also what Maharshal wrote to reject Ran's proof — meaning the reason they refuted "the priest is obligated to provide him another since he transgressed" even though Rashi explained thus — is strained, for if so mochlat is also; and further that because he transgressed a prohibition it should be his and in his possession regarding monetary obligation — examine carefully. And apart from this law I already wrote in Siftei Kohen that even if it deals in Arakhin with this house it is not difficult at all, for one may say it deals where the landlord has possession of it; and my heart tells me Maharshal did not know that Rashba, Rabbenu Yonah, Rivash, and other later authorities agree with Ran's words — he only thought Ran was alone in the matter, and therefore he did not bring in his book except Ran's words alone; and had he known all these great ones hold thus, and all the more so Tosefta and Yerushalmi rule like their words, he would have retracted. (I again found in responsum Meimoni to Sefer Mishpatim siman 27, responsum Rabbeinu Shimshon ben Avraham, who wrote that that case in Arakhin must be established regarding an unspecified house etc. — see there; behold explicitly like my words.)

**** ENGLISH ****
(there note 42) challenged Ohel Moed. Per my understanding, since regarding avodah zarah it is permitted even though it is written "and nothing of the ban shall cleave to your hand" — because its spoil exceeds its repair — all the more so regarding nedarim. And what he wrote that explicit proof etc., since we say that only regarding avodah zarah did the Merciful One reveal this and not elsewhere etc. — he was not precise, for Tosafot's intent is not to say that only regarding avodah zarah where the Merciful One revealed do we say thus, for then wherever we derive by "from where do we learn" we could not learn; rather Tosafot's intent is that only regarding avodah zarah is it not considered benefit since its spoil exceeds its repair, and if so we would not consider it benefit — meaning the spoil causes it and it is not considered benefit; but regarding Shabbat, although spoil causes it that there is no benefit, we would say he did melacha and certainly one who does melacha is liable even if he does not benefit; but that he be considered a spoiler and not doing melacha — we do not say this is Tosafot's intent. Also what he said that in nedarim too we follow the noder's intent etc. is nothing — after all there is no benefit here. Also what he brought from Raavad that forbidden in maddir benefit is nothing — there in the end there is benefit, but here since spoil exceeds repair there is no benefit. Also what he brought from Raavad is not proof — examine.
