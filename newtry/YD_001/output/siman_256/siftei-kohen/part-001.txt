**** ENGLISH ****For whatever they want — even for optional matters (and similarly Mahari"k end of shoresh 128 in the name of many poskim, and he agreed), and specifically a fixed matter — if they lack they will collect again another time. But if a case occurred that they needed to collect for the need of the poor, e.g. they needed to collect for clothing or many poor came and they collected for their sake, they may not change for the need of another matter, not even for the need of other poor — the Rosh and Tur. And so it seems the view of Mahari"v siman 26; see responsum of Mahar"a Mizrachi siman 9 and responsum of Maharam Alashkar siman 10; and it seems for redemption of captives even in such a case they may change as above siman 252.

Beit Yosef: it seems the Rosh's view is that the Yerushalmi of Shekalim when they come to change for a matter that is the need of the poor specifically — but for optional matters they may not change, neither the administrators nor the community — end quote. Darkei Moshe wrote on this: but the Tur wrote that even for the need of other poor it is forbidden — end quote. The difficulty is that the Rosh explicitly wrote and we learn in Yerushalmi "the poor may give to the poor", etc., and "they do not protest the hands of the administrators" — meaning if they see there is need in the matter and they come to change, they do not protest their hands — end quote. And that of "the poor may give to the poor" the Rosh explicitly establishes there in the case that occurred that they needed to collect for the need of the poor, etc. Rather certainly Yerushalmi speaks when the administrators see there is need in the matter — and that is what the Rosh wrote: if they see there is need in the matter, etc. — meaning even though they cannot change in a case that occurred by chance, etc., even for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless when the administrators see there is need in the matter they may change for a mitzvah matter. On this Beit Yosef wrote: it seems his view is that the Yerushalmi of Shekalim when they come to change for a matter that is the need of the poor specifically — therefore the administrators may change when they see there is need in the matter; but for optional matters not — and this is clear.
