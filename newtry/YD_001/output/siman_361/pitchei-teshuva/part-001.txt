<b>If there are burial societies in the city.</b> Magen Avraham, siman 153, seif katan 41, writes that people who have established a practice of burying the dead cannot prevent others from participating, because everyone wishes to merit performing the mitzvah. Chatam Sofer, siman 329, explains that this refers to people who wish to join the established burial society and are being excluded. The society cannot prevent others from participating in the mitzvah. But if outsiders refuse to join the society and merely wish to displace it from its established role, they cannot thereby deprive the society of its mitzvah. Chatam Sofer further writes that if the burial society offers to perform the burial without charge, its prior claim is stronger. If it demands the customary fee while others will do the work free or more cheaply, it cannot compel outsiders to pay simply in order to preserve its income. A society may regulate its own members with communal agreement and the approval of the local Torah authority, but it cannot impose an arbitrary tax upon people outside the society and refuse burial unless they pay. If the local custom to charge outsiders was originally enacted by the entire community with proper authority and on a fixed, equal scale, it can stand; an arbitrary charge imposed at will cannot. In siman 330 he recounts a case where an outsider died in Pressburg and the burial society collected eight hundred gold coins from the heirs for a prestigious grave site. The society explained that an ordinary grave was available for a small fee, but the heirs insisted on a distinguished location near prominent righteous people. Chatam Sofer ruled that if this was truly the arrangement, the custom was valid. A person should be buried in a grave that legally belongs to him, and where the heirs request an unusually honorable plot the society may charge its value. He nevertheless questioned by what authority they could bury an unknown outsider among distinguished righteous people merely because of money. He also defended the custom of a separate area for travelers from Ramban on Chayei Sarah. Since the money had already been collected under an established arrangement, he would not order its return. See also his siman 331 and Kavod Chachamim, page 57.
