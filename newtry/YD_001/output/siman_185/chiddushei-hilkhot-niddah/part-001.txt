**** ENGLISH ****Until she tells him she immersed — since she certainly saw; but in doubt whether she saw, explained above we count her veset and he had relations — it is double doubt. Mahari Brandeis challenged: we do not rely on double doubt where inquiry is possible. Requires study: here even double doubt is not needed since veset is rabbinic and rabbinic doubt is lenient; only needs the other doubt that chazal made like certainly saw; therefore needs doubt of immersion — as written, doubt of immersion removes doubt of seeing immediately; through this chazal did not fix veset law; therefore no proof from here that Torah law requires inquiry — only leniency from double doubt; cannot compare rabbinic prohibition to Torah (Abraham Kapp).

**** ENGLISH ****She is not believed. Tur wondered why an excuse does not help as when she says "I am tamei" — and Rashba explained she did not do an act. One can analyze: granted an excuse that initially she did not think she was niddah but only thought they would not cast an eye on her — on this she is properly believed as Rashba wrote such an act she did not do; but in an excuse Mordechai and Sefer HaTerumah wrote "I think I am niddah and now it was revealed the blood came from a wound" — why should this not help even in wearing garments? Behold then she thought she was truly niddah and therefore wore these garments; and now it was revealed she erred — and in such an excuse Ramban agrees, for Ramban's inference from mishnah that she leaves without ketubah when he had relations with her as niddah — we establish she was presumed niddah among neighbors — from this he proved excuse does not help; otherwise how would she leave? Perhaps there is an excuse. Per the above still difficult — behold she has such an excuse; must say he holds when she does an act she settles greatly and examines well to know if menstrual blood or the like before doing the act — therefore she is not believed in any manner. Later authorities challenged from above siman 1 regarding butcher who does an act on sheep head, etc. — they entered forced explanations; truly one could say therefore excuse does not help although this woman stands in presumption of purity and when she gives an excuse the matters are recognizable as truth — for daughters of Israel are not suspected of relations with niddah, Torah believed her; nevertheless she made herself a single entity by doing an act to testify on her niddah — this is no more than if he said it was treifah and witnesses testify it was kosher — he is not believed regarding others and regarding herself she is a single entity and forbidden — as explained above s.s. 1 (Abraham Kapp).

And likewise here — no excuse regarding herself to remove her from category of single entity; and see Rambam ch. 18 Laws of Forbidden Relations halachah 9 who rules likewise when she says "I am tamei" — although not believed regarding husband, nevertheless she is single entity and forbidden after husband's death to a kohen; and Raavad challenged why excuse does not help — must say for Rambam also since she was so brazen to say "I am tamei" she is single entity and excuse does not help. Per this the essence of prohibition is regarding herself — when feeding a man forbidden food, regarding others excuse helps for we certainly rely on excuse; only we say she made herself single entity; therefore above regarding butcher it does not apply and Shulchan Aruch did not descend on whether butcher himself may eat that sheep — only whether meat is permitted to others; in this certainly single entity does not apply for what concern is it to us; and we rely on excuse supported by presumption of animal standing in presumption of permissibility — therefore permitted. Although author was silent above following Rashbatz and possibly he does not hold Ramban's distinction — and so appears in his responsum he cited law she is believed with excuse and did not mention Ramban's law that with garments excuse does not help; nevertheless for law this distinction seems correct — others are permitted for essence of prohibition is making herself single entity. Taz s.k. 2 brought explanation in name of Maharal of Prague that presumed niddah is different because neighbors know from this she was presumed niddah and do not know from excuse — shall she take a drum to announce excuse to all? Therefore not believed. He challenged this and likewise Mahari Brandeis — for we say in Ketubot regarding woman men jumped on who were not proper and she said "I cannot" and returned and said "I am available" — she is believed with excuse; and in such excuse neighbors did not know — therefore Mahari Brandeis rejected these words; and finally he himself wrote in name of Levush the reason she gives bad name among neighbors — since thereby she causes bad name among neighbors she would not do so to rely on excuse; and unless she is truly niddah she would not wear; therefore we presume niddah — and with this Taz's challenge falls away. Beit Meir in Even HaEzer siman 115 s.k. 6 wrote they take her out without ketubah because of stumbling block not bad name — requires study: excuse helps when there is bad name — therefore certainly she is niddah and therefore caused stumbling and she leaves without ketubah — this is the above regarding Maharal of Prague that neighbors will not hear excuse and presume husband niddah. Indeed Maharal's words are fitting for one who said them — entire reason is neighbors saw her presumed niddah and husband has relations with her and they did not know excuse — behold woman and husband in their eyes like niddah relations; this name does not apply — for even if she says to herself and establishes for all presumption of "I cannot" and afterward marries another — even if they did not know excuse she would not be considered in their eyes transgressing — perhaps husband died or divorced and no defamation of lips at all — not so here. Therefore if she already passed time she could immerse — perhaps excuse helps she was not tamei and permitted without immersion — no concern among people they would think perhaps she immersed; but although Maharal's reasoning is straight, for law it does not stand — besides in Even HaEzer siman 115 seif 6 implies somewhat not like his words; can reject saying it deals when she said so to husband as in mishnah "I am tamei to you"; see Bach's responsum who also wrote it deals when she said to husband; also siman 178 seif 10 explains one who raised voice on wife she committed adultery and said it was clarified true and afterward gives excuse — he is believed although he raised voice in Hebrew camp — who will tell each one the excuse? Nevertheless believed — this teaches not like Maharal and can reject (Abraham Kapp).

Indeed per Ramban's words in his Laws of Niddah ch. 2 halachah 10 — woman counts for herself as said etc. and believed to say "I immersed and am pure" — presumed niddah among neighbors such as wearing garments designated for her niddah days — she is not believed, etc. — appears he explains she is certainly tamei only said "I am pure because I immersed" and still wears garments designated for niddah days — not believed; with this plain she is in presumption of tamei only Torah believed her; and since she did such act — with all excuses in world at end she diminishes her credibility for we see she did act to contradict — what did you see to rely on this? — returned matter as if she never spoke and behold presumption of tamei. But in Rashba in Tosefta implies even if she said "I was not tamei at all" and presumed niddah among neighbors — not believed; nevertheless can say it deals when veset time already passed — then she became tamei without statement likewise presumption of tamei for way comes in its time; and even per view veset is rabbinic nevertheless needs examination and statement "I am pure" — then she who established herself wearing garments for niddah although she says excuse does not help so much since she did act and there is room for doubt — on whom do we rely? We say behold she as if said nothing whether for tumah or taharah and behold presumption of tamei; not so when veset time not yet arrived and behold presumption of purity — perhaps even wearing garments excuse helps — if we say resolve this with that behold presumption of purity; and from where for Rashba? For all his proof from "he had relations with her as niddah" we establish presumed niddah among neighbors — from this he learns excuse does not help; possibly deals when veset already passed therefore we establish presumption of tamei; but before this we did not hear — from where to innovate this law that Tur challenged and argues on entire law — do not add (a) on it.
