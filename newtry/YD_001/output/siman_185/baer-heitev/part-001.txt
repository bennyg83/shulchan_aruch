**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Impure.</b> A later explanation does not help, unlike the next seif. There, she merely said, "I am impure," and one can say that she said so out of embarrassment. Here, however, she performed an act by putting on the garments of a niddah, and she is therefore not believed by a later explanation. Rashba. R. Leib of Prague explained that when a woman established herself publicly as a niddah, not everyone knows her later explanation, so on what did she rely when she presented herself that way before everyone? By contrast, if she said only to her husband, "I am impure," she may have relied on the fact that she would later tell him the reason. Taz derives that even with a verbal statement there is a distinction: if she said before many people that she was impure, a later explanation does not help. This is why the poskim formulate the case as, "She said to her husband, I am impure," meaning specifically to her husband alone. A case occurred with a woman who had miscarried three times in the eighth month and feared that ayin hara was the cause. She wished to conceal her pregnancy from the members of her household and neighbors, and her husband agreed that at the beginning of the pregnancy she should say, "I am impure," in order to hide it. Rama was asked whether her husband could be with her privately and permitted it. See Taz and Responsa Rama, siman 2. A woman who accuses her husband of having relations with her while she was a niddah, while he denies it entirely, is not believed. Mahariv, siman 202. If one witness testifies that she was impure, or said to her, "Are you not impure?" and she answered yes, a later explanation does not help. Knesset HaGedolah in the name of his teacher.
