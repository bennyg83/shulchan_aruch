**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even, and so forth.</b> From Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and the other poskim it appears that the concern is only writing an entire word outside the column, not two letters of a longer word. Rashi, Beit Yosef, and Rosh say explicitly regarding a two-letter word that happened to fall at the end of the line: one should not throw it between the columns, meaning one should not write that entire word outside the ruled margin. Although one may write two letters outside the column from a five-letter word, an entire word was not permitted. This clearly implies that two letters from a larger word are permitted.

<b>אם יכול לכתוב חציה כו'. </b>משמע דאפי' יותר מג' אותיות יכול לכתוב חוץ לדף כיון שאינה רוב התיבה וכ"פ הב"ח אבל לפעד"נ עיקר להחמיר כהרא"ש וטור דדוקא תיבה של ד' אותיות יכול לכתוב חציה חוץ לדף אבל ג' אותיות חוץ לדף אע"ג שרוב התיבה תוך הדף לא יעשה כן וכן דעת הסמ"ק והגהת מיימוני שכתבו גבי תפילין שלא יכתוב שלשה אותיות חוץ לשיטה ולמדו כן מספר תורה ומביאם ב"י בא"ח סי' ל"ב וכ"כ הטור והמחבר שם סעיף ל"ג ותימה שנראה שדברי המחבר סותרים זא"ז וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he can write half of it, and so forth.</b> This implies that even more than three letters may be written outside the column provided they are not the majority of the word. Bach rules this way. In my opinion, however, the primary ruling is to be stringent like Rosh and Tur: only with a four-letter word may half be written outside the column. Three letters outside the column should not be written even when most of the word remains inside. Semak and Hagahot Maimoniyot say regarding tefillin that one should not write three letters outside the line, deriving this from Sefer Torah. Beit Yosef cites them in Orach Chayyim siman 32, and Tur and Mechaber rule this way there in seif 33. It is surprising that Mechaber appears to contradict himself. This requires study.
