It is explicit in Shevuot 41a, as cited in Choshen Mishpat siman 87, seif 9. The reason lashes are administered is because he delays while a shamta remains upon him, as stated in Yevamot 52a and Kiddushin. Those passages can imply even if he does not ask to have the nidui lifted, and likewise Choshen Mishpat siman 98, seif 5. Although ninety days are mentioned there, that nidui is prospective, compelling him to come to beit din and pay or set a time, so every moment he remains in violation. This differs from a nidui imposed for a past refusal, as in Shevuot, where it is not imposed for nonpayment but for refusing to swear. The incidents of R. Eliezer and Akavya imply release specifically when the person seeks it, as does the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 3 of Moed Katan. That Yerushalmi seems to require thirty days before any release, but we do not rule so, as stated in Moed Katan 16a according to Rif.
