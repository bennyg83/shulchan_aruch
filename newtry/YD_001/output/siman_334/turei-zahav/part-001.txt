This matter is very puzzling to me: how shall we be concerned for the prohibition that this one is doing, and bring him to bad culture, Heaven forfend, of which all who come to her will not return? And behold, the source of this law is in the Piskei Maharai siman 138, and this is his language: And a proof from what I found in a compilation that proved from that which is said in the Agudah, perek HaNosei, that Rabbeinu HaKadosh said at the time of his passing by way of prophecy regarding Rav Acha bar Yoshiyah, that they placed shamta on those who piled fruit in a kiln and they desecrated Shabbat and apostatized because they had been placed in shamta; and it is difficult, what does the Talmud teach us with this matter, and Rabbeinu HaKadosh himself, for what halacha did he prophesy this thing, except to teach us that even if it appears to the chachamim that because they place nidui on someone, on that account he will go out to bad culture, they should not refrain because of this from doing according to halacha, end of his words. And what he wrote in the name of perek HaNosei is a scribal error; rather this prophecy of Rebbi is in perek Asarah Yuchasin (Kiddushin daf 72). And it appears to my humble opinion that from this there is no proof at all, for there it implies that Rabbeinu HaKadosh came to warn the hearers that they should know there are pesulim in Bavel, as he said there much: that there is a place where all are mamzerim, and there is a place where there are two brothers who exchange their wives with one another; and regarding this he also said that there is a place in Bavel that they have turned away today from after the Omnipresent, blessed is He, for Rav Acha placed shamta on them etc., and therefore one should distance from them. And if you say: behold, that which is well-known needs no proof, and it is not similar to the other places that he warned that there are mamzerim, who are not known, which is not so in this case that he converted, and certainly even without this they would separate from them — to this I would say to you that all the more so there is no difficulty, for Rebbi's intent was to prove that prophecy spoke through him in the other things that he said, even though they were not clear in the eyes of the hearers, and they will bring proof from this matter, that they will see that he knows in Eretz Yisrael what is done today in Bavel regarding those whom Rav Acha placed in shamta, and this is impossible except by way of prophecy; from this they will know that also in the other things the spirit of Hashem spoke through him, as above, the intent of this prophecy is clear. <b>To be more stringent with him and place him under cherem, and so forth.</b> Beit Yosef asks why Tur presents this as discretionary, since he had already written that after two successive thirty-day periods of nidui, one places the person under cherem. Some answer that here the person is requesting release. Since he has not repented, beit din may not only continue nidui but even impose cherem despite his request. That does not fit Tur's wording. It appears instead that Tur first taught the possible escalating structure of nidui, including distancing for up to sixty days and then cherem. He then clarifies that these details are not mandatory in every case. Beit din has discretion regarding the duration and whether to impose cherem at all. This also explains Shulchan Aruch's later repetition of restrictions already mentioned under nidui: the novelty is that beit din has authority to impose them, not that they are invariably required.

<b>That which we say, and so forth.</b> This is Raavad's view, cited by Rosh and Tur. The baraita says there is no nidui of less than thirty days. Raavad asks from Abaye, who distinguishes monetary cases from disrespectful conduct: in monetary disputes, once the litigant appeases the other party, beit din may release him immediately, while for disrespect he requires thirty days. Rif rules not like Abaye, but that even for disrespectful conduct, if the offended party is appeased, release may be immediate. If so, what does the baraita mean? Raavad gives two answers. First, the initial nidui is imposed for thirty days, but if he repents it may be released immediately. Second, even if he did not appease the offended person, after thirty days beit din may release him because he has endured the penalty, provided he comes before beit din and asks for release. Tur and Shulchan Aruch adopt both leniencies: appeasement can permit release even within thirty days, and after thirty days release can occur even without appeasement if he petitions beit din. Rambam disputes the second leniency regarding a sage who was insulted and requires the sage's appeasement. Beit Yosef also cites a pamphlet claiming Rosh disputes the first leniency and follows Abaye, requiring thirty days for disrespectful conduct. That cannot be correct. Rosh explicitly rules like Rif in responsa and cites Raavad without dissent. Rif, Rambam, Raavad, and Rosh all agree that genuine appeasement can end the nidui immediately.

<br><b>Beit Yosef</b> sought to support the pamphlet from the Yerushalmi concerning a person under nidui whose release occurs during a festival. The Yerushalmi asks: if he was released before the festival, why did he not cut his hair then? If he was not released beforehand, why should he cut during the festival, since he could have appeased the offended party before the festival? It answers that his thirty days ended during the festival, because nidui is not less than thirty days. Beit Yosef inferred that Rosh, by citing this Yerushalmi, must hold a fixed thirty-day minimum even where appeasement occurs. This cannot be sustained. Tur himself follows Rif and Raavad here, yet in Orach Chayyim siman 531 he cites the same rule about the thirtieth day falling during the festival. Rosh too cites both discussions in the same chapter. Beit Yosef proposed that release within thirty days merely becomes effective only after day thirty, but that also conflicts with the explicit cases used by Rif and Rosh where no thirty-day wait occurred.

<br><b>It appears to me</b> that the baraita has two applications. Where the nidui is tied to a fixed period, because the offended sage cannot be appeased, is absent, or beit din deliberately imposed a timed penalty, the simple rule applies: nidui is not less than thirty days. But where the nidui depends on appeasement, the original declaration may state thirty days, yet once genuine appeasement occurs the person may be released immediately. The Yerushalmi's discussion concerns the first type, a nidui that could not be ended by appeasement before the festival. Therefore, when the thirty days expire during the festival, he is genuinely considered prevented from cutting his hair beforehand. In the second type, where he could have appeased the offended party before the festival but failed to do so, he is not considered prevented and may not cut during the festival. This also explains Tur's wording in Orach Chayyim. Thus all the sources agree: where release depends on appeasement, effective appeasement permits immediate release. Rama's statement here that repentance within thirty days does not help follows no accepted opinion.

<b>המנדה למי שאינו חייב נידוי. </b>כתו' בהגהת מיימוני דוקא שיודע שאינו חייב נידוי כי הא דר"ל במועד קטן אבל מורה שנידה שסבור שזה חייב נידוי וטעה אין לנדות המורה וכתב רש"ל י"א דוקא אם אותו שנידה שלא כדין הוא ת"ח אבל לא בעם הארץ והמנדה ת"ח ומההיא דר"ל אין ראיה דשמא אותו שנידה ר"ל היה גדול ממנו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who places under nidui someone who did not deserve nidui.</b> Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that this applies specifically where he knows the person does not deserve nidui, as in the case of Reish Lakish in Moed Katan. But a halachic authority who imposed nidui because he mistakenly believed it was warranted is not himself placed under nidui. Maharshal cites an opinion that this applies only where the person improperly placed under nidui was a talmid chacham, not an ordinary ignoramus. The case of Reish Lakish proves nothing against that, because perhaps the person whom Reish Lakish placed under nidui was greater than he.

<b>ולכן נהגו להקל. </b>גדולה מזאת כתוב בדרישה בשם מהרמ"ץ דמהר"מ כתב דבזמן הזה שהנדויין מקולקלין אין מנדין כל מי שראוי לנדות אף בעבירות גדולות דאפילו בשורת הדין לא החמירו חכמים פן ימנענו מלעשות תשובה כל שכן שבדורינו שנבלים מביישים את האדם על ככה ולפעמים אין האדם מושל ברוחו ויבא לידי שפיכות דמים כו' ע"כ לשונו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Therefore the custom is to be lenient.</b> Derishah cites an even broader ruling in the name of Maharam: nowadays, when the institution of nidui has become degraded, we do not place under nidui everyone who technically deserves it, even for serious transgressions. Even under ideal circumstances Chazal did not always insist on the harshest penalty, lest it prevent repentance. All the more so in our generation, where coarse people may publicly shame the person because of the nidui and he may lose self-control to the point of bloodshed. Therefore restraint is often appropriate.
