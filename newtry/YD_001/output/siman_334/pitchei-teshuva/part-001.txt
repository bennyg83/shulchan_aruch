[וכן העלה בתשו' ח"ס סי' שכ"ב וכתב שם אמנם יש להסתפק היכא דאיכא למיחש שימיר דתו ויקח גם בניו הקטנים עמו מהו לחוש לקטנים שלא חטאו. ודעתו דיש לחלק דמי שיש בו דעות כוזבות דקרוב לודאי שילמוד גם לבניו אחריו ואז יהיו גם הבנים בכלל כל באיה לא ישובון אין לחוש על הבנים ומה"ט נ"ל דהכת הארורה המאמינים בש"צ ימ"ש אם ח"ו לא יהפך לבבם לטוב רודפין אותם עם בניהם ובנותיהם כו' אבל סרבן ועבריין בעלמא מאן לימא לן שלא נחוש על בניו כו' והאריך בזה ומסיק עדיין הדבר צריך עיון ומתון ע"ש]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if there is concern, and so forth.</b> See Baer Heitev and Responsa Tashbetz, part 2, siman 47, who agrees with Nekudot HaKesef. Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, Yoreh Deah siman 48; Chavot Yair, siman 141; She'ilat Yaavetz, part 1, end of siman 79; and Radbaz, new responsa siman 187, likewise. Radbaz concludes that the leader of the generation must act with patience in such matters, because people differ and transgressions differ, and all actions must be for the sake of Heaven. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 322, reaches a similar conclusion but raises a further doubt where there is concern that the sinner will apostatize and take his minor children with him. Should concern for innocent children alter the response? He distinguishes between a person holding corrupt theological beliefs, where it is nearly certain he will teach the same beliefs to his children and they too will be drawn irreversibly into them, and an ordinary stubborn transgressor, where perhaps concern for the children should matter. He therefore writes that regarding the accursed sect of believers in Shabbetai Tzvi, if they do not repent they may be pursued together with their children, but regarding an ordinary rebellious sinner the issue remains one requiring further study and caution.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They are permitted.</b> See Responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah, siman 77, citing his grandfather regarding a person placed under cherem whose son had been born before the ban but whose brit milah had been delayed due to illness. During the delay the father was excommunicated, and beit din decreed that his son not be circumcised. One sage argued that only if the son had not yet been born before the ban could they impose such a decree, because the obligation had not yet taken effect. Once born, they could not. Similarly, with a burial decree, distinction should be made whether the relative died before or after the nidui. The grandfather disagreed: regarding burial such a distinction may exist, but regarding milah there is no distinction. The printed grandson adds that perhaps the ruling was limited to that case because Heaven itself had already delayed the milah through illness, so once it was deferred it remained deferred; one should not infer that beit din may deliberately postpone a healthy infant's eighth-day brit.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But not regarding other Sages.</b> See Baer Heitev in Shach's name, who disagrees. One can ask: if one placed under nidui by his rav is under nidui to all Sages, why does the Mishnah specifically say he is under nidui to the student? It should have said to all Sages. Responsa Radbaz, new responsa siman 378, likewise concludes that one under nidui to his rav is under nidui to all Sages. He explains that the Mishnah's reference to the student teaches that this applies even where the student later became wiser and greater than his rav. From this it follows that the converse rule, that one under nidui to a student is not under nidui to the rav, likewise applies even where the student is greater than the rav. The matter requires study.
