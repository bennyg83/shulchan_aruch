He should not slaughter—see Kereti. And behold I did not understand the Rashba's words who gave in Tosafot HaRosh the short reason: lest he delay or press or make chaladah—what connection does candlelight or darkness have to delay and pressing? In the long Tosafot HaRosh chaladah alone is mentioned here, which fits well: that he inserts under the wool and feathers and does not see; but delay—does he not know in darkness whether he slaughtered a thing or paused briefly? Similarly if he carried back and forth or slaughtered with pressing. Therefore, were it not that I fear raising my head against our last Gaonic authorities on this, I would say that nevertheless it is difficult for me that the Rashba disputes Rashi—from where did Rashi's words appear, and what did he see to dispute even Rashi? And this is not the Rashba's way; therefore it appears the Rashba came only to explain Rashi's words that are difficult to me: what is this concern lest perhaps he did not slaughter the majority—he can go and see by candlelight whether it was slaughtered or not? Rather the Rashba explains: certainly all the time until slaughter is finished and the majority is slaughtered he is very careful not to do a disqualifying matter—delay, pressing, chaladah. But once he slaughtered the majority, he is no longer so careful about these laws; and even though delay in the minority portion there is an opinion that forbids, nevertheless by Torah law it is valid and he is no longer obligated to be careful so much. If so, one who slaughters at night will certainly not move until he slaughtered all the simanim—only he will think the majority was already slaughtered and will delay or press or make chaladah, not paying attention anymore to these matters of slaughter, only for convenience; and in truth the majority was not yet slaughtered—and these are disqualifying matters. And we are also concerned for delay and pressing. And Rashi's and the Rashba's words are one—examine. And in any case we derive from this that even though the simanim were cut, according to Rashi it is forbidden.

One who slaughters on Shabbat—know that in this that the mishnah teaches "one who slaughters on Shabbat, his slaughter is valid," most commentators stood on it: if he was a mumar to desecrate Shabbatot, how can his slaughter be validated? The Rambam in commentary on the mishnah says it deals with shogeg, for on Shabbat immediately when he began to slaughter he desecrates Shabbat through his connective tissue (chibbur) and becomes a mumar to desecrate Shabbat, and when he completes slaughter he is already a mumar to invalidate his slaughter—therefore he establishes it in shogeg. And so Rashba wrote in his novellae: one can say shogeg, as we establish our mishnah for R' Yehuda that it deals with shogeg. And so in Tosafot HaRosh on the mishnah one can say shogeg. And one need not all the Tosafot texts I will write below—and all wondered, for Tosafot sensed this, for in the Gemara it asks and establishes it in intentional per R' Meir, etc., and the Gemara deflects similar to Yom Kippur—what is Yom Kippur? Intentional also here; and the Gemara asks: can you establish it in shogeg, and it is R' Yehuda?—and even though he is liable to death it teaches, etc.—behold the Gemara's view is to say the mishnah deals with intentional, and the language "even though he is liable to death" supports him. And it is difficult—for it is impossible to establish intentional, for then his slaughter is nevelah. On this Tosafot based their proof, and I extended in my novellae. And what is written in the very short text is Tosafot on daf 14 s.v. v'nasvin: they challenged—it is obvious forbidden to eat that day, for salting is required and salting on Shabbat is forbidden; and the text: if he transgressed and salted, or also to eat it raw and salting is not needed, as Tosafot extended. And one must understand according to what Rashi wrote s.v. v'nasvin, etc., that we derive from what is taught "Yom Kippur" to teach Shabbat similar to Yom Kippur: just as on Yom Kippur forbidden to eat on its day, so too this forbidden to eat on its day. And one can derive: what does it teach us that because of Shabbat slaughter it is forbidden?—for otherwise it is forbidden because he salted and it is like cooking on Shabbat (and especially for Ra'avad who establishes it in an animal standing to bear young—because of mukhan, and it is R' Yehuda—and it is difficult: was the famous baraita of R' Eliezer hidden from Ra'avad, that R' Yehuda forbids cooking on Shabbat in shogeg and all the more so intentional? If so, even standing for eating is forbidden, and mukhan is unnecessary; it must be said he holds they decreed only on cooking, but not on slaughtering, since it lacks action until it comes to eating—from this caution. And such reasoning in many places; and see there daf 15 s.v. mori leih, etc. However the above is difficult—for it is impossible to eat without salting, and in this it is similar to cooking that it no longer lacks action and is fit to eat. It must be said—as the Ran already wrote—that this salting of meat is not a Torah melacha but a rabbinic melacha; and we say in the chapter HaNizakin: when R' Yehuda decreed on Torah matters he did not decree on rabbinic matters (there). If so, it is not forbidden if done in shogeg. But it works out for R' Yehuda—the Gemara asks and establishes R' Meir, etc.—and it is difficult how it is possible according to R' Meir: why especially slaughter?—for otherwise forbidden because of salting; and if rabbinic—we established in HaNizakin that R' Meir is more stringent in scribes' words than Torah and decreed shogeg on account of intentional. It must be said: in truth the second text of Tosafot that it is eaten raw without salting at all. And behold in Orach Chaim siman 318 it is explained in Magen Avraham s.k. 58 and Taz s.k. 56 that when we say "fit for raw" and permitted to move it on Shabbat—that is a duck and similar poultry meat that is soft; but not other meat that is hard—see Tosafot. If so, it must deal with slaughtering poultry that is fit for raw. And behold the Rambam in the mishnah gave a reason that therefore he is a mumar—and agrees to Tosafot that through this he becomes a mumar to desecrate Shabbat and is not drawn as mumar to invalidate slaughter; only he gave a reason: immediately when he began to wound he became a mumar to desecrate Shabbat, and at completion of slaughter he was a mumar from the outset. According to this, with poultry here in reality even if intentional—for half the trachea was defective and almost when he slaughtered his slaughter was finished, then the wounding was done—and behold wounding and slaughter come as one, then he became a mumar and it is permitted and simple. If so, all the above is not difficult—R' Abba who holds R' Yehuda's view is preparation (hachanah). And this is with an animal that generally stands to bear young; but with poultry we certainly conclude at the beginning of Beitzah that a hen stands for eating and to lay eggs (there)—and so he said explicitly; if so, the above is unnecessary, and one cannot say "raw"—only that he transgressed in shogeg and salted; if so, for R' Yehuda it works well—it is difficult, for he is a mumar to desecrate Shabbat; and on this the Rambam's and Rashba's text works well for R' Yehuda that it deals with shogeg and there is no warning at all. And for R' Meir that we establish intentional for R' Meir—we already proved one need not say he transgressed and salted as above, and it must be raw; if so, it deals with poultry—we find intentional, as written regarding half defective trachea. And warning and slaughter come as one. And for R' Yehuda who does not establish with poultry—one can say shogeg, and the Rambam's and Rashba's words ascend as one. But if difficult—this is difficult what we say in Bava Kamma daf 71 and Ketubot chapter Elu Na'arot: a thief who slaughters on Shabbat, etc., pays fourfold and fivefold, and the Sages exempt—and the Gemara establishes it as slaughter through another so there should not be certainty for Rav; and the Gemara asks: why do the Rabbis exempt? and answers: it is R' Shimon who holds slaughter that is not fit is not called slaughter, and he holds like R' Yochanan the sandalmaker who holds an act of Shabbat in shogeg—others may eat; in intentional all forbid, as we derive from Scripture (there). And it is difficult—if so, it must deal with intentional; for in shogeg, as written, fit for others and slaughter is fit. And in intentional he is a mumar—and not only that it is obvious it is slaughter that is not fit and R' Yochanan the sandalmaker is unnecessary; also since he is a mumar he is not fit for slaughter at all and there is no slaughter here, only stabbing like a gentile slaughterer, and there is no liability here as all compilers and Tosafot regarding Orach Chaim that a mumar is not in the category of slaughter—and see Torat HaBayit in Mishmeret HaBayit daf 80b—and it requires study. And it is strained to say: since in shogeg he is not a mumar, one can say an Israelite slaughtered simanim in shogeg and did not slaughter the majority, and when he reached the majority another Israelite slaughtered them and it was slaughtered intentionally—if so, when did he become a mumar at completion of slaughter?—and his warning and slaughter come as one as above; nevertheless since he completed slaughter intentionally, he is one who slaughters Shabbat intentionally—and if so, for R' Meir forbidden, and so R' Yochanan the sandalmaker—and all the above is settled. And on this the Rambam and Rashba intended: since you find in shogeg valid, therefore everything is not difficult, for one can say as above; and also one can say he slaughtered them in shogeg but before he finished the majority they warned him "you desecrate Shabbat" and he did not pay attention and completed his slaughter—and his slaughter and warning come as one as above; therefore the Rambam and Rashba wrote it deals with slaughter that was in shogeg—and it requires study. And further it appears—for we say in Eruvin daf 69: an Israelite who is a mumar to desecrate Shabbat in public does not give and annul domain, because they said an Israelite gives and annuls domain and gentiles only when they rent—and this is considered like a gentile; and there we say explicitly that for R' Meir a mumar to desecrate Shabbat in public annuls domain as an Israelite is considered and not like a gentile—see there Eruvin daf 69 s.v. hotzi. According to this, R' Meir holds he can annul domain and is not like a gentile; if so, his slaughter too is not like a gentile and he is fit for slaughter—see Bedek HaBayit daf 19 (there). If so, it is not difficult what we say "intentional"—that is R' Meir, and for R' Meir he is not a mumar that his slaughter be like a gentile's. (And Tosafot do not hold this and challenged in Chullin—they went to their view and do not hold in Eruvin like Rashi.) And they properly establish intentional; and so in Bava Kamma R' Meir obligates—and for R' Meir there is slaughter as written. And that they do not establish R' Shimon—R' Shimon holds he is a mumar like R' Yehuda, and all the more so R' Yochanan the sandalmaker—this is not a difficulty, for it implies similar to idol worship that there is slaughter only because it is not fit—and examine, for this is correct. And behold Tosafot and especially Rashba wrote that here it deals in private and he is not a mumar; and one must understand the Rambam who establishes shogeg—and so Tosafot in one text that in that matter he is not invalidated—and not the text as plain that it deals in private. And it appears the Rambam holds in public he does not need ten men, only by doing in public he reveals his intent to cast off the yoke—and for this they are exact like a mumar; and see Mordechai beginning of first chapter of Chullin—you will find these matters explicitly regarding one who casts off the yoke; therefore in intentional with warning as the mishnah teaches—even though liable to death, meaning with warning, and he permitted himself to death—there is no greater publicity than this. In truth I do not understand in my humble opinion Tosafot's words who challenged establishing the mishnah as R' Meir—and R' Meir holds a mumar for one matter is a mumar for the entire Torah; and I did not know what the difficulty is—for do we say he is not fit for slaughter at all?—behold he is in the category of gentile and left the presumption of Israel, suspected for the entire Torah; and so they said there in Bekhorot daf 30b: one suspected for one matter is suspected for the entire Torah—it is a suspicion matter, not that he left Torah of Israel that he is not fit for slaughter; if so, what is difficult to him from one who slaughters Shabbat?—are we coming to him from Torah of reliability? The mishnah itself came to inform us—and it deals with one standing on one side seeing that he slaughters according to law and halachah, and there is nothing more to be concerned—requires study. And Tosafot answer: either the first time he is not considered a mumar, or in that slaughter when he became a mumar that slaughter was not performed—and this view the Rambam also holds, only he wrote in one cutting in the simanim; and similarly if he slaughtered anything he desecrates Shabbat and becomes a mumar, and when he finishes he is already a mumar. And it is unnecessary—for even slaughtering anything he is ruining the connective tissue that he did not fix to remove a limb from the living—as already explained one can say since his intent is to finish slaughter he is ruining in order to fix, like tearing in order to sew and liable. And I wonder at the later authorities who wrote he is not invalidated that time—and in Shulchan Aruch Even HaEzer siman 29 seif 5 in Rama's hagahah: if he wrote a get intentionally on Shabbat, behold it is void—behold he decided he is not fit to write; and this is not like the later authorities here—and Beit Shmuel sought to reconcile it (there s.k. 8) that it is not the first time that every two letters writing desecrates Shabbat and he is a mumar—and it is difficult, for also in slaughtering this reasoning applies that every cut and cut he desecrates Shabbat as the Rambam wrote in commentary on the mishnah itself; and it is strained to say regarding slaughter if his intent is not on completion he is only ruining, only his intent is on completion—it is melacha as one; unlike a get where every two letters count as melacha each time—see Bach siman 7. But all this is strained—and who forces us to these strained things and to dispute the Rambam and be lenient? Therefore it is clear it is forbidden like Rama's hagahah in Even HaEzer.
