מוכח דלאינש דעלמא אף באין בו כדי חלוקה לא שרי אלא לצורך אחד מן השותפים כו' ודאי דכן הוא ומ"מ ודאי דמוכח נמי דביש בו כדי חלוקה אפילו לצורך אחד מהשותפין אסור דהא קאמר בהדיא בש"ס ומביאה הרא"ש בשאין בו כדי חלוקה אבל בשיש בו כדי חלוקה אפי' ר"א מודה דאסור אלמא דאפי' בנכנס לצורך אחד מהשותפין דבכה"ג דוקא הוא דשרי באין בו כדי חלוקה אבל ביש בו כדי חלוקה אסור ודוחק גדול לומר דבש"ס לא קאי אלא ארישא דמתניתין בשותפים שנדרו ולא אסיפא דאחד מן השוק. עוד כתב דמהרמב"ם והסמ"ג משמע דבאחד מן השוק אין חילוק בין יש בו כדי חלוקה או לא אלא בין נכנס לצורך השותפים או לא. וליתא שהרי לא הוזכר בדבריהם שום דבר מנכנס לצורך השותפים או לא אלא סתמו דבריהם כלשון המשנה וכמו שמפרשים המשנה כך יש לפרש דבריהם. עוד כתב הב"ח אכן מדברי רבינו ירוחם ני"ד ח"ה משמע דגם לגבי א' מהשוק לא התיר אלא כשאין בו כדי חלוקה אבל ביש בו כדי חלוקה אסור כו' ואין שומעין למשמעות שאינו מפורש בדברי רבינו ירוחם שסותר דברי הרא"ש והטור והמפרשים ותו דבאין בו כדי חלוקה משמע דמתיר רבינו ירוחם אפי' נכנס לצורך עצמו והרי אף ב"י גופיה כ' דאין בו כדי חלוקה לא אחרים בינתים וז"ל והא דאמרינן היה אחד מן השוק מודר הנאה מאחד מהן מותר ליכנס לחצר דוקא כשנכנס לצורך חבירו אבל לצורך עצמו אסור עכ"ל הרי מפורש בהדיא ברבינו ירוחם דלצורך עצמו אסור וגם מפורש בהדיא בדבריו דבשיש בו כדי חלוקה אפילו לצורך חבירו אסור דהא מחלק ברישא בין יש בו כדי חלוקה או לא ואם איתא דלצורך חבירו מותר אין חילוק. ואם כן דברי הטור ממש כדברי רבינו ירוחם ומ"ש הב"ח כהרא"ש וטור נקטינן כו' כבר כתבתי למעלה דאדרבה דברי הרא"ש מוכיחי' כרבינו ירוחם ובעל כרחך צריך לומר כמו שכתב ב"י דטעות סופר הוא בטור והאי אם לא לצורך השותפין צריך לומר בסיפא באין בו כדי חלוקה ועוד נ"ל שאין צריך להגיה רק אות א'. וכן צריך להיות וכ"ש אם אחד מהן הדיר לאחד מן השוק מנכסיו שאסור ליכנס בו לצרכו אם לא לצורך אחד מן השותפים בחצר אם אין בו כדי חלוקה שלא חל הנדר ומותרים כו' ואם כן דבריו ממש כדברי ר' ירוחם. וכן מוכח בטור ח"מ סימן ק"פ וז"ל שותפין שנדרו הנאה זה מזה ויש להן חצר בשותפות אם יש בו כדי חלוקה אסורין ליכנס בו ואם אין בו כדי חלוקה מותרין ליכנס בו הדיר אחד מהן א' מהשוק אסור ליכנס בו לצרכו עכ"ל ודוחק גדול דהאי הדיר א' מהשוק כו' קאי ארישא. כל זה כתבתי כבר בספרי והעתקתיו לכאן לפי שמתוך מה שכתבתי יש להקשות גם על הטורי זהב. גם מ"ש הטורי זהב לפרש בדברי הרא"ש ודאי ליתא דלדבריו מ"ש הרא"ש ומשום ברירה אין להתיר משותפין בשיש בהן דין חלוקה דלא שרי ראב"י כו' אין לו הבנה כלל והכי הוה ליה למימר ומשום ברירה אין להתיר ליכנס בחצר השותפין ביש בה כדי חלוקה דלא שרי ראב"י להשותפין משום ברירה כו' ועוד קשה דהא בכל ספרי הרא"ש איתא משום ברירה אין להתיר משותפין כו' במ"ם ואיך העתיק הוא בשותפין בבי"ת. ועוד קשה לדבריו למה ליה להרא"ש לומר כלל האי מילתא ומשום ברירה אין להתיר כו' דהיאך סלקא דעתך להתיר משום ברירה ביש בו כדי חלוקה דהא אפילו בשותפין גופייהו ביש בו כדי חלוקה ליכא ברירה. ועוד קשה מ"מ מנא ליה להרא"ש להקשות דילמא כפשוטו דהא דשרי ראב"י במתניתין היינו דוקא באין בו כדי חלוקה וכדעת הרשב"א אלא הדבר ברור דכך הם המשך דברי הרא"ש דמתחלה כ' בשם ר"י דאם הדיר אחד מן השוק אסור ליכנס לחצר והשתא קשי' ליה אמאי קאמר ר"י דאסור ומשמע דקאי אמתניתין בחצר השותפין שהדיר אחד מהן לאחד מהשוק באין בו כדי חלוקה דהא מתניתין באין בו כדי חלוקה מיירי וכדקאמר בש"ס דבשיש בה כדי חלוקה אפילו ראב"י מודה וא"כ באין בו כדי חלוקה בלאו הכי לישתרי משום ברירה וקאמר דמשום ברירה אין להתיר באין בו כדי חלוקה משותפין ביש בו כדי חלוקה כו' כלומר דילפינן משותפים כשיש בה כ"ח דלא שרי ראב"י משום ברירה כיון שיש תקנה לחלוק ולא יכנוס לחצר חבירו בחנם אלמא דהיכא דאפשר בתקנתא לא שרי ר"א וכ"ש הכא באינש דעלמא באין בה כ"ח דאפילו תקנה לא צריך דלמא יכנס בחצר והא דשרי ר"א במתניתין באין בו כדי חלוקה באינש דעלמא צריך לומר דמיירי לצורך אחד מבני החצר זהו ברור כשמש פשט דברי הרא"ש. ומ"ש וכן הוא ברמזים כו' אדרבה ברמזים משמע כדפרישית דאל"כ הוה ליה לאשמועינן דבא' מן יחלוק ולא יכנס וכ"ש אם היה אחד מן השוק כו' שלא יכנס אפי' באין בו כדי חלוקה דהא א"צ לתקנתא דחלוקה ולמה יכנוס בחנם אם לא לצורך אחד מבני החצר. וגם קשה לדבריו דהא בטור ח"מ סימן ק"פ משמע להדיא דאפילו אין בו כדי חלוקה לא שרי לצרכו וכמ"ש לעיל אלא ודאי דברי הב"י ברורים וגם דעת הטור כן וכמ"ש למעלה:
**** ENGLISH ****(Siman 226 in Taz s.k. 1) He elaborated to challenge the Beit Yosef and concludes: and it is a great wonder on the Beit Yosef that he did not see after the words of the Rosh, etc. And he continues somewhat after the words of his father-in-law the Bach z"l; and it appears to me their words are not correct, and on the contrary the Beit Yosef saw these words of the Rosh and from there is proof for the Beit Yosef's words; and at first I came to refute the Bach's words and afterward his words; and what the Bach wrote "and I heard and do not understand" — since the Beit Yosef holds that when there is not enough for division, one of the market is permitted to enter for the need of one of the partners, as written in name of the Rashba — if so why should it be deficient when there is enough for division that he not enter for the need of one of the partners, etc. — his words are puzzling, for per his words what does he challenge on what the Beit Yosef brought in name of the Rashba — challenge the Gemara itself that rules halachah like Rabbi Eliezer that one of the market is permitted to enter, and so all the poskim; rather certainly there the reason is since there is not enough for division, he said it is permitted from the reason of berirah; but when there is enough for division, since there is a remedy in division it is forbidden — if so the Beit Yosef challenges well: and is one of the market better than the partner himself, that the partner himself is forbidden when there is a side of division — all the more one of the market who is lesser, since he has no share in the courtyard, he is not better than a person who comes on his account; and likewise in the Derishah that all the more he is not better than the partner (and so it appears from Tosafot and the explanation of the Rosh and Rambam in the first Mishnah of partners in what he wrote that the end "one of the market" to Rabbi Eliezer was needed, for even though he has no share in the courtyard it is permitted; and so it appears from the words of the Tur itself in what he wrote "and all the more if one of them forbade one of the market," etc. — evidently one of the market is lesser than the partner himself). If so, since the partner himself is forbidden when there is enough for division, all the more one of the market. Furthermore the Bach wrote that from the words of the Rosh it is proven that for an ordinary person even when there is not enough for division it is not permitted except for the need of one of the partners, etc. — certainly so, and nevertheless certainly it is also proven that when there is enough for division, even for the need of one of the partners it is forbidden, for he says explicitly in the Gemara and the Rosh brings it when there is not enough for division, but when there is enough for division even Rabbi Eliezer agrees it is forbidden — evidently that even entering for the need of one of the partners, in such a case specifically it is permitted when there is not enough for division, but when there is enough for division it is forbidden; and it is a great stretch to say in the Gemara it refers only to the first part of the Mishnah regarding partners who vowed and not the end regarding one of the market. Furthermore he wrote that from the Rambam and Semag it appears regarding one of the market there is no distinction whether there is enough for division or not, only between entering for the need of the partners or not; and it is not so, for behold nothing was mentioned in their words about entering for the need of the partners or not — they only obscured their words like the Mishnah, and as the Mishnah is explained so should their words be explained. Furthermore the Bach wrote: indeed from the words of Rabbi Yerucham may his light shine, part 5, it appears that also regarding one of the market he did not permit except when there is not enough for division, but when there is enough for division it is forbidden, etc. — we do not listen to an implication that is not explicit in the words of Rabbi Yerucham that contradicts the words of the Rosh and Tur and the commentators; and furthermore in "there is not enough for division" it appears Rabbi Yerucham permits even entering for his own need — behold even the Beit Yosef itself wrote that when there is not enough for division it is not permitted except when entering for the need of the partners; therefore Rabbi Yerucham's view is not so, rather like the Rosh and Tur we rule practically, and not like Shach — end quote. And it is a great wonder that he looked at the beginning of Rabbi Yerucham's words where he wrote: partners who vowed benefit from each other are permitted to enter the courtyard, and the same law if one forbade his fellow, and likewise if one of the market was mudar benefit from one of them — permitted to enter the courtyard, and specifically in a courtyard that has no law of division; but in a courtyard that has a law of division everything is forbidden — end quote; thus he wrote "indeed from Rabbi Yerucham's words it appears," etc., and we do not listen to an implication not explicit in Rabbi Yerucham's words, etc., and furthermore in "there is not enough for division" it appears Rabbi Yerucham permits even entering for his own need, etc. — had he looked at the end of Rabbi Yerucham's words he would not have written so; for he wrote afterward after interrupting other laws: and that which we say "one of the market was mudar benefit from one of them — permitted to enter the courtyard" — specifically when he enters for his fellow's need, but for his own need it is forbidden — end quote; behold it is explicit in Rabbi Yerucham that for his own need it is forbidden; and also explicit in his words that when there is enough for division, even for his fellow's need it is forbidden, for he distinguishes at the beginning between there being enough for division or not; and if for his fellow's need it were permitted there would be no distinction. And if so the words of the Tur are exactly like Rabbi Yerucham's words; and what the Bach wrote "like the Rosh and Tur we rule," etc. — I already wrote above that on the contrary the words of the Rosh prove like Rabbi Yerucham, and perforce one must say as the Beit Yosef wrote that it is a scribal error in the Tur and this "if not for the need of the partners" must be said in the end when there is not enough for division; and furthermore it appears to me one need not emend except one letter; and so it must be "and all the more if one of them forbade one of the market from his property" that it is forbidden to enter for his need if not for the need of one of the partners in a courtyard when there is not enough for division, for the vow did not take effect and they are permitted, etc.; and if so his words are exactly Rabbi Yerucham's words. And so it is proven in Tur Choshen Mishpat siman 180: partners who vowed benefit from each other and have a courtyard in partnership — if there is enough for division they are forbidden to enter it, and if there is not enough for division they are permitted to enter it; one of them forbade one of the market — forbidden to enter for his need — end quote; and it is a great stretch that this "one forbade one of the market," etc., refers to the first part. All this I already wrote in my books and copied here, for from what I wrote one can also challenge the Turei Zahav. Also what Turei Zahav wrote to explain the words of the Rosh — certainly it is not so, for per his words what the Rosh wrote "and because of berirah one should not permit partners when there is a law of division, for Rabbi Eliezer does not permit," etc. — he has no understanding at all; and thus he should have said: and because of berirah one should not permit entering the courtyard of partners when there is enough for division, for Rabbi Eliezer does not permit the partners because of berirah, etc.; and furthermore difficult for his words, for behold in all books of the Rosh it says "because of berirah one should not permit partners," etc., with a mem, and how did he copy it "partners" with a bet. And furthermore difficult for his words why the Rosh needed to say this general matter "and because of berirah one should not permit," etc. — how could it enter your mind to permit because of berirah when there is enough for division, for even among the partners themselves when there is enough for division there is no berirah. And furthermore difficult: nevertheless from where does the Rosh challenge — perhaps as the simple meaning, that Rabbi Eliezer permits in the Mishnah specifically when there is not enough for division, like the view of the Rashba; rather the matter is clear that such is the continuation of the Rosh's words: at first he wrote in name of Rabbi Yochanan that if one of the market forbade — forbidden to enter the courtyard; and now he challenges why Rabbi Yochanan says forbidden, and it implies he refers to the Mishnah of the partners' courtyard where one forbade one of the market when there is not enough for division, for the Mishnah deals when there is not enough for division, as the Gemara says that when there is enough for division even Rabbi Eliezer agrees; and if so when there is not enough for division in any case it would be permitted because of berirah, and he says that because of berirah one should not permit partners when there is enough for division, etc. — meaning we learn from partners when there is enough for division that Rabbi Eliezer does not permit because of berirah since there is a remedy in division and he should not enter his fellow's courtyard for free; evidently where remedy is possible they do not permit Rabbi Eliezer; and all the more here for an ordinary person when there is not enough for division; and that Rabbi Eliezer permits in the Mishnah for an ordinary person when there is not enough for division must be said he deals entering for the need of one of the members of the courtyard — this is clear as the sun, the simple meaning of the Rosh's words. And what he wrote "and so it is in the hints," etc. — on the contrary in the hints it appears as I explained, for otherwise he should have informed us that for one of the market it is permitted when there is not enough for division even for his own need; and thus it should have been written "and all the more if one of the market was mudar from them that he should not enter except for the need of one of the members of the courtyard when there is enough for division"; rather certainly he says: but when there is enough for division — no; for since division is possible he should divide and not enter; and all the more if one of the market, etc., that he should not enter even when there is not enough for division, for he needs no remedy of division — why should he enter for free if not for the need of one of the members of the courtyard. And also difficult for his words, for behold in Tur Choshen Mishpat siman 180 it explicitly appears that even when there is not enough for division it is not permitted for his need, as I wrote above; rather certainly the Beit Yosef's words are clear and also the Tur's view is so, as I wrote above.

<b>(סימן רכ"ו בש"ך ס"ק י"ג) ודבריו תמוהין כו'. </b>עתה ראיתי שדבריו נכונים דעל כרחך מיירי הכא שהדיר את חבירו דהיינו שאמר יאסר חצרי עליך דאי באסר עצמו מחלק חבירו ודאי אפי' כשאמר חצר זה כופין אותו כדי שיהיה כולו של חבירו ולא יכנוס בו כלל וכן משמע באשר"י דמיירי בשהדיר את חבירו ע"ש גם לשון חצרי שכתבו הרא"ש וטור משמע כן וא"כ דכשמיירי שהדיר את חבירו אסור למכור להשותף כמ"ש הריב"ש ואע"ג דהריב"ש קאי אהדיר עצמו מנכסי חבירו ה"ה בהדיר חבירו מנכסיו דחד טעמא אית להו וכן הר"ן מדמי להו להדיא להדדי ומביאו בית יוסף בסי' זה:
**** ENGLISH ****(Siman 226 in Shach s.k. 13) And his words are puzzling, etc. Now I have seen his words are correct, for perforce here it deals with one who forbade his fellow — meaning he said "my courtyard shall be forbidden upon you"; for if he forbade himself from his fellow's share, certainly even when he said "this courtyard" they compel him so it will all belong to his fellow and he will not enter it at all; and so it appears in Ashri that it deals with one who forbade his fellow — see there; also the language "my courtyard" that the Rosh and Tur wrote implies so; and if so when it deals with one who forbade his fellow, it is forbidden to sell to the partner as Rivash wrote; and although Rivash refers to one who forbade himself from his fellow's property — the same applies when he forbade his fellow from his property, for they have one reason; and likewise the Ran explicitly compares them reciprocally and the Beit Yosef brings it in this siman.
