**** ENGLISH ****
Partners who forbade benefit from one another; it contains three se'ifim. Two who are partners in a courtyard and vowed benefit one from the other — if it has a law of division, the vow takes effect and they are forbidden to enter it until they divide and each enters his own. And all the more so if one of them forbade one from the market from his property, that he is forbidden to enter it (in my humble opinion, and so is implied by R' Yerucham). And if it does not have a law of division, the vow does not take effect and they are permitted to enter it, whether they forbade one another or one of them forbade one from the market from his property; and specifically when that one from the market who is mudar enters for the partner's need — whether he needs the partner or the partner needs him — but to enter it not for the partner's need is forbidden. {Rama: And the same law applies to two who had together a place in the synagogue, and one said to his fellow, "my share shall be for you hekdesh" — since he said "for you hekdesh," certainly his intent was to forbid him upon him; but if he said "shall be hekdesh" and did not say "for you," he did not intend except that it be hekdesh for the poor, and his fellow is permitted to benefit and sit on it (Beit Yosef, siman 221, in the name of responsum of Rashba). And the same law applies in every similar case. And likewise if they were partners in a movable item — if it has a law of division they are forbidden in it, and if it does not have a law of division they are permitted in it (Taz, siman 180).}

When both forbade one another — they are forbidden to place in it an oven, stoves, a mill, and chickens.
