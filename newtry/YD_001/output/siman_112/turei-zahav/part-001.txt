דוחק שנאמר לאו דוקא נקט ראבי"ה שאוכל אצלו ישראל שאינו נזהר כן נראה לע"ד ברור:

One who is not careful, etc. In Mordechai, chapter Ein Ma'amidin, he wrote in the name of Ravyah, and these are his words: a Jew who eats pat akum and has clean bread in hand, and the baal habayit is careful about pat akum — since this mitzvah is incumbent upon him to cut, he cuts from the finer; proof from the Yerushalmi: clean bread is impure and cemetery bread is pure — on whichever he wishes he cuts; and since he was permitted to cut, he is permitted for the entire meal — end of his words. He wrote on this in Torat HaBayit, siman 32: Ravyah did not specifically mean only if an Israelite eating pat akum was reclining at the table, for without him too it is permitted; rather he expressed it thus because who would bring pat akum to the table when the baal habayit is careful about it — end of his words. At first glance the main explanation seems to be that since he brings proof from the Yerushalmi that rules regarding clean bread, etc., that the choice is in his hand — automatically in Ravyah's law too the reason is that since pat akum is available before the baal habayit, out of honor for the mitzvah he is permitted to cut from it. And even though we find in Mordechai, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, in the name of the minister of Kutzi, that one careful about gentile bread should remove the clean gentile bread from the table and cut from Israel's bread — nevertheless we find in Agudah, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, that there is a dispute in this, and some say even one who is careful blesses on whichever he wishes; and if so, Ravyah may hold like this view. In Issur VeHeter, section 44, he brought Ravyah's words in this language: one careful about pat akum who sits at a table with one who is not careful and has cleaner pat akum — since he is permitted to cut from it, he is permitted for the entire meal, for there is no honor of Heaven that he should cut from it and then separate — end of his words. It appears that what he wrote regarding one who must be with him, etc., and he wrote there in the margin on this that his explanation is that the cutter is not careful about pat akum, and what he wrote that he is permitted to cut means only to fulfill the cutting; but if the cutter is careful and the diners are not careful, it is certainly better for him to remove the clean bread from the table until after cutting — end of his words. And similarly Maharach explained that the Mordechai deals with a baal habayit who has one person in his house as a hired worker and the baal habayit eats with that lodger, and the mitzvah of cutting is upon the host and the baal habayit follows him — and therefore he is permitted in pat akum with him. The main novelty: the baal habayit, who is primary, becomes secondary to the host; and he wrote: if the baal habayit makes the meal, he certainly must cut from Israel's bread since he does not wish to eat pat akum, and he need not even remove it from the table; and he disagreed with Shulchan Aruch on this. Had they seen Ravyah's language as copied in Agudah they would not have written thus, for it states there, chapter Ein Ma'amidin, in this language in the name of Ravyah: one careful about pat akum who has at his table an Israelite who is not careful and has fine bread on the table; and we say in the Yerushalmi: clean bread, etc.; and since he is permitted to cut from it, he is permitted to eat it for the entire meal, etc. — end of his words. Behold before you that the one careful is the host and he is primary, only that he has a guest who is not careful, as the language "sits with him" teaches that the one sitting is secondary at his table; and if so, the baal habayit must cut — therefore he blesses on the finer. But the main explanation is like Maharai's explanation: regarding the permission of the cutting itself he brings the Yerushalmi, and he holds it applies even to one who is careful; nevertheless there is a difficulty on Shulchan Aruch, for according to this ruling even one careful may cut on pat akum, not like the minister of Kutzi who commanded to remove; and above in Orach Chaim, siman 168, he ruled like the minister of Kutzi that one removes what is not Israel's — end of his words. It appears he explains Ravyah's words because the guest who is not careful will certainly eat pat akum since it is clean, and the law requires cutting from the bread one will eat, as Maharai wrote in Torat HaBayit, siman 32, that one should not cut first except from what one will eat; and if so, the baal habayit who must cut and cuts and brings out the guest cuts from what the guest will eat; and since he was permitted to cut for his sake, he was permitted for the entire meal for his sake, even though afterward each may eat his own bread — nevertheless, since at the time of cutting they need one bread, it was also permitted afterward; and so that the guest himself should not be obligated to cut on pat Yisrael, for this he brings from the Yerushalmi that one not careful cuts on whatever he wishes; and the same applies to this guest for whom the baal habayit cuts on his behalf. And so is the main halachah according to the ruling of Shulchan Aruch here and in Orach Chaim, siman 168 — but not for one careful who eats alone, as arose in Maharai's view, for in his explanation there is strain in saying Ravyah did not specifically mean an Israelite eating with him who is not careful; so it appears to my clear mind.

And we are not concerned about bread, etc. And one should not forbid because of gentile milk, for impure milk does not stand; and if we say there is a slight admixture of milk in it, nevertheless it is nullified in its minority since it is mixed in, and it is not comparable to butter in siman 115 which is not mixed in — end of Rashal's words; and in Derishah and Maharach, of blessed memory, they brought it; and to me, the younger, it seems astonishing how he said milk admixture is nullified in its minority, for we say at the beginning of chapter Elu Overin: Babylonian kutach dulls the heart because of milk admixture in it — and if it were nullified it would not dull; and furthermore it is stated explicitly in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (Avodah Zarah, daf 35) that one who curdled kemakha — which is kutach — the milk is forbidden; and so in Mordechai it is written explicitly in chapter Ein Ma'amidin in the name of Rabbenu Tam that milk that a gentile milked — for eating and for kutach I forbid — end of his words; and so in Issur VeHeter, general rule 44, law 5.
