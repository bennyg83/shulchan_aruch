**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Treifah.</b> And Shach wrote: and I did not know why Bach was lenient to permit long thin grass lying along the esophagus without reason and proof. He wrote: an incident came to him where one slaughtered fowl and at the time of shechitah cut his finger and declared treifah because he panicked from the cut on his finger and raised the knife somewhat — and it is shehiyah; and several geonim agreed with him that it is treifah. And in responsum of Or Zarua, siman 86, he distinguishes: if he cut his finger not at the time of cutting the siman, such as after he passed the entire knife in holacha or hava'ah outside the neck, and then cut his finger, and afterward returned and did holacha or hava'ah with the knife and finished the shechitah — certainly it is shehiyah and invalid. But if he cut the simanim and his finger at once — there is no shehiyah here, and it is not comparable to gomi found within the simanim, for there the gomi is soft and through the knife's sharpness he pauses longer to cut it than when cutting the siman. And regarding kashering utensils or transferring the slaughterer — lean to leniency. And if the slaughterer is in doubt in what manner the cut on his finger was — be lenient — see there. And if he cut the finger of the one holding the simanim — it is kosher, for the slaughterer is not startled. See responsum Chinuch, Beit Yosef, question 24 — end. And I say: not only gomi, but even a hair found either in the esophagus or trachea is forbidden — see there. He wrote to Kenesset HaGedolah: if the esophagus is full of bran feed and our eyes see the knife full of bran and going down with spurting of blood — forbid by law — see there. If in doubt whether the gomi was cut with the shechitah or not — do not declare treifah, since it is mere stringency that they practice to forbid shehiyah in any amount; one may be stringent in certain cutting but not in doubt. And so I ruled practical halakhah several times — Kenesset HaGedolah, this siman.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Esophagus.</b> And Shach wrote in the name of Darkei Moshe: even though we know that through plucking feathers the esophagus is not perforated, nevertheless we decree lest the skin tear from the neck; according to this there is no distinction between plucking at the time of shechitah or before — always, if blood came out from them, declare treifah because of the decree; and it is great stringency in my eyes. Also Maharshal validated this and wrote it is stringency without reason; therefore it appears as Bach wrote, that in great loss one should be lenient. And Taz wrote the reason for this stringency: lest one come to permit also a bar goose whose blood spurts, as in siman 33; therefore one forbids only specifically when blood came out, for since blood came out we are concerned perhaps a very fine hole was perforated and touched the esophagus, and it is not visible to us, or the blood blocked it; for otherwise from where did this blood come, since in the skin there is no blood (and I omitted the sugya in Yoma, daf 48, that there is blood in the skin — responsum Shai, question 28). Therefore one may not be lenient in a cut, even part of the skin, when blood is seen; and in plucking feathers, when blood is seen one should be stringent specifically if a little blood came out — end. But if only redness is seen, not blood — do not be stringent at all. And he wrote he proposed this before Maharam Yafeh and he agreed with his words.

<b>The cut.</b> And Taz wrote: it appears simple — if on the fowl's skin there is dry scratch on its outer side, and he slaughtered elsewhere and saw on the inner side that there is no defect on the skin — do not be stringent according to all views. And if the upper membrane on the simanim peeled even somewhat — treifah; and examination does not help, since we are not expert in examining the esophagus. However, in mere swelling around the simanim, when there is no other defect — we are not concerned for perforation of the esophagus; rather we attribute it to illness — see Kenesset HaGedolah.
