Melting of the liver, etc. In this he followed Rokeach whom Beit Yosef brought, who learned it from chapter "One who seized him"; and the honor of Rama rests in its place, but he was not precise in this ruling; and I will set before you the Gemara's words that are stated there: for blood that comes from the mouth we examine it with a wheat stalk whether it absorbs; if the blood sticks to the stalk — lung disease comes and it has a remedy; if not — liver disease comes and it has no remedy. We challenge: but we learned the opposite — liver removed and nothing remained of it; lung punctured or lacking — treifah. We answer: since it comes from the mouth, say perhaps it melted and was removed; and Rashi explains: since blood of the liver, which hangs below the lung, rises upward and exits via the mouth — say perhaps it melted and was removed entirely and nothing remained of it, and it fell to the lung via the reed of the liver connected to the reed of the lung — end; it appears clearly the main ruin that the animal or person dies is that the entire liver melted, not part of it; for if there is a flaw also in part of it — the difficulty returns to its place, for a lung that melted and spoiled inside is kosher as long as the simfonot did not melt; if so, liver would be stricter than lung when lacking in part — but we learned the opposite as the questioner cited. Rather, simple: whoever did not lack the two olive-volumes is kosher even if the liver melted. And Rokeach wrote: reed of liver like liver in removal — all of it and nothing remained like an olive — treifah; liver that melted — treifah, as we say in "One who seized him": blood from the mouth — if from liver disease it has no remedy; liver that dried or is hard as stone — treifah — end. And in this Beit Yosef understood Rokeach forbids in melting even if the two olive-volumes remained intact, and he disagreed; and indeed Rokeach did not come to forbid except in melting entirely even the two olive-volumes; and a great novelty he teaches us: if melted — it is as removed; and so we find in Beit Yosef siman 50 in name of Rashba: any limb whose puncture or lack disqualifies — when melted also disqualifies — end; and so Mordechai whom Beit Yosef brought siman 50: Rabbenu Tam said to Ra'am mouth to mouth that wherever punctured forbids — likewise if the flesh around it is bad and rotted; and it itself teaches also Rokeach: even in liver that is all blood — nevertheless if it melted and became blood it forbids if the two olive-volumes did not remain; this is proven clearly from his words, for behold adjacent to this he wrote: liver that dried or is hard as stone — treifah, meaning even the two olive-volumes; and so Rama wrote here: if not so — kosher, as written in this seif; and why not explain the first clause thus too, namely what he wrote "liver that melted" — what difference to me liver that melted or dried? Rather it is clear and simple that Rokeach who forbids in melting means also in melting entirely; and this matter is a great wonder on Rama: in dried he required dryness in all of it, and likewise hard as stone, and in melting he forbids even if the two olive-volumes remained; and Rokeach wrote them adjacent one to another in one language; rather it is simple he refers to all of it in melting too; and Moharach came to strengthen Rama's ruling and said Rokeach disagrees with Rashi and explains another meaning: namely since it exits via the mouth certainly there is rot there and "throw the rot in all of it"; and it is difficult: if so, that which is stated Piskei Avodah Zarah (daf 56): Rav Yosef son of Rav Yehudah asked Rav Yehudah: liver became yellow opposite the intestines — what is the law? He said: treifah. We challenge: let it be only removed — kosher. Rava said: since liver became yellow opposite the intestines, it is known the intestines fell into fire and constricted; and if we say "throw rot in all of it" and in the end it will rot — he would not challenge at all; and also Rava would not need to make treifah on account of the intestines — let it be forbidden because we throw rot in all of the liver. Rather, certainly we do not say in liver "throw rot in all of it," aside from that it is already hidden in our words; for if so it would be stricter than lung — why not say also in lung we throw rot in all of it? Perforce we have no basis to be stringent in a matter Rashi explicitly permits; and to learn from Rokeach's words what he did not intend at all. And again I saw he wrote as our words in Masat Binyamin newly printed: we decide practical law and so we judge a long time practically every day that one does not forbid rotted and became blood if the two olive-volumes remained whole; and one should not be stringent at all in this except if it is known the animal fell from a height of ten handbreadths and crushing and loss of form in the liver is found — forbid, as written siman 58 seif 3.

In the flesh, etc. In Tur it is written we are concerned lest it entered via the esophagus to the thin parts, etc.; if it entered via the simfon, etc. — wondrous: for it implies from this that if we did not have this proof we would not forbid from doubt but would attribute that it entered via the reed; and afterward he wrote: but with a thin one there is no distinction and treifah in every case, etc. — it implies from doubt alone we forbid; and it appears we certainly forbid on account of doubt alone, since most swallowings are via the esophagus; but in the first clause he should have said we do not attribute that it entered via the esophagus since it has a pouch even though its point downward punctures well — nevertheless how can the pouch enter via there, which is a small puncture? Rather there was room to attribute (in the pouch) [in the reed]; perforce he said you must be stringent even though it is not reasonable except what entered via the reed — how did it puncture the flesh of the liver after exiting from the simfon? Thus the two sides are balanced; nevertheless treifah from doubt. And the explanation of the matters regarding kofa outward is thus: if found kofa outward — namely toward the cavity of the body and its point toward the simfon — then perforce it did not come via the simfon to the flesh but from outside, namely via the esophagus to the thin parts and from thin parts to liver; the law is: its point inside flesh of liver toward the simfon; but if kofa toward the simfon — there is proof it came specifically from the simfon; for if from outside it would not have been possible to enter with the pouch inside the liver; and we need not be concerned lest it came from outside from another side of the liver and there its point was in the flesh of the liver; for certainly it is reasonable that here it was found here, since it is possible to say it came here via simfon — it is more reasonable than to say it came from another side from outside to here; and Beit Yosef and Rama already wrote: since if placed straight in the liver — treifah, for then it is impossible it came via the simfon. And Rashba in the long Tosafot daf 36 wrote another explanation according to Rif and Rambam, and these are his words: and it appears they explain kofa outward opposite Rashi's words: and inward means opposite the cavity of the animal; and outward means opposite outside the cavity — namely opposite the head; and this principle is transmitted to us from the sages: whatever is found in lung or in one of the hanging organs — we say it took the simfon and came; and here this is what it says: if kofa inward — kosher, for it took the simfon and came, and we are not concerned it turned here and there and punctured; but when kofa outward and its sharp head enters inward — we are concerned lest it turned here and there via its descent and punctured the reed of the liver, whose puncture is slight, since it passed there in a narrow place; not so in lung, for until the lung the place is wide and close — we are not concerned at all, even kofa outward, etc.; but wondrous in liver too: if kofa inward and lodged in liver — why kosher? For if via simfon it came — how was it lodged? And I can say: whatever is inside — the power of attraction in the organs pulls and pushes — end; and it appears in Bahag that Tur brings siman 40 he explains here also per this explanation of Rashba; and why was he not concerned above lest it entered via esophagus? For we say more that it entered via simfon.

<b>מצד הכבד. </b>כ"ה במרדכי ומו"ח ז"ל תמה דכאן משמע שיש חילוק דאם ניקב שלא מצד הכבד כשר ושאר פוסקים לא חלקו בזה וכן בת"ה סי' קס"ג הביא דברי המרדכי אלו ולא הזכיר מצד הכבד גם באו"ה משמע שאין חילוק ע"ש כלל נ"א סימן ל"ד ושער נ"ד ע"כ כתב דהאי מצד הוא ט"ס שהיה כתוב במרדכי חצר הכבד והמעתיק טעה וכתב מצד במקום חצר ול"נ דהא דכתב מצד הכבד לא אתי למעוטי שיהיה כשר בחצר הכבד באיזה מקום שבשטח חצר הכבד דודאי בכל מקום טרפה אלא כוונתו שבחצר הכבד יש בו חלק שהוא פרוש על דוכן הבהמה ואח"כ מתחיל לירד בחלל הבהמה דרך הפסקה על הכבד באויר החלל ועל זה אמר כל שיש בו נקב בכל שטח שבתוך אויר החלל אבל באותו חלק שהוא פרוס על הדופן אין נקב פוסל ודבר זה ברור בטעמו דהא כתב ב"י בשם הגהת אשיר"י דטעם הטרפות בחצר הכבד הוא אינו מצד עצמו אלא שסוף שתינטל הכבד ומ"ה אין סירכא פוסלת בו דאדרבה כל שנסתם מהני מ"ה גם אם יש נקב מפולש באותו חלק שדבוק לדופן כ"ש שאין איסור דהא הדופן מכסה הנקב אע"פ שאינו נכנס לתוכו ודבוק בו מ"מ אין הרוח נכנס לשם דרך אותו נקב דהדופן מכסה את הנקב ע"כ כשרה בזה נמצא שכל הפוסקים יפה עשו שלא חילקו בכך שהם לא דברו אלא מחלק שהוא באויר הבהמה והמרדכי רצה להורות לנו שלא נטעה לומר דאף חלק שאצל הדופן נקרא חצר הכבד כנ"ל ברור ונכון:
**** ENGLISH ****
From the side of the liver. So in Mordechai; and Moharach was astonished: here it implies there is a distinction that if punctured not from the side of the liver — kosher, and other poskim did not distinguish; and so in Tosafot siman 163 he brought Mordechai's words and did not mention "from the side of the liver"; also in Orach Chaim it implies there is no distinction — see his name; general rule 51 siman 34; therefore he wrote this "from the side" is a scribal error — it was written in Mordechai "courtyard of the liver" and the copyist erred and wrote "from the side" in place of "courtyard"; and it appears to me that which he wrote "from the side of the liver" does not come to exclude that it would be kosher in courtyard of the liver in any place on the surface of courtyard of the liver — for certainly everywhere treifah; rather his intent is: in courtyard of the liver there is a part spread on the platform of the animal, and afterward it begins to descend in the cavity of the animal via the break on the liver in the air of the cavity; and on this he said: whatever has a puncture in all the area inside the air of the cavity; but in that part spread on the wall — a puncture does not disqualify; and this is clear in his reason, for Beit Yosef wrote in name of Hagahot Ashiri: the reason for treifot in courtyard of the liver is not from itself but that in the end the liver will be removed; therefore adhesion does not disqualify — on the contrary, whatever was sealed helps; therefore also if there is a puncture open through in that part stuck to the wall — all the more there is no prohibition, for the wall covers the puncture even though it does not enter inside and is stuck to it — nevertheless air does not enter there via that puncture, for the wall covers the puncture; perforce kosher. Thus we find all poskim did well not to distinguish, for they spoke only of the part that is in the air of the animal; and Mordechai wanted to teach us not to err and say that even the part by the wall is called courtyard of the liver — as explained, clear and correct.

<b>אבל אם נסרכה כו'. </b>דכיון דאין הנקב פוסל מצד עצמו כמ"ש המרדכי פא"ט בדין נפנה לאור מהני ליה סתימה שע"י הסירכא וע"כ אפי' חלב טמא שאינו סותם בעלמא סותם בהאי נקב של חצר הכבד וכן מ"כ. ומלבד זה אין סירכא אוסרת בחצר הכבד דלא אמרינן אין סירכא בלא נקב אלא בריאה ששואבת כל מיני משקה כ"כ באו"ה כלל ס"ב ולא כתרומת הדשן. כתב בד"מ ומצאתי למהר"י מולין אי ניקב חצר הכבד במקום דמשמשא ידא דטבחא כשר ואי נמצא סביבו חוטים לבנים כמו חלב אז הוא נקב ישן וטרפה עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
But if it adhered, etc. For since the puncture does not disqualify from itself, as Mordechai wrote Piskei Avodah Zarah, it turned to light — sealing via the adhesion helps; and perforce even impure milk that generally does not seal — here in the puncture of courtyard of the liver it seals; and so he wrote. And aside from this, adhesion does not forbid in courtyard of the liver, for we do not say there is no adhesion without a puncture except in lung that draws all kinds of liquids; so Orach Chaim general rule 62; and not like Terumat HaDeshen. Darkei Moshe wrote: and I found to Mahari Mullin: if courtyard of liver was punctured in place where butcher's hand rests — kosher; and if white threads like milk are found around it — then it is an old puncture and treifah — end.
