<b>אפי' אינו כו'. </b>כריה"ג דאיסי ב"י ע"כ ס"ל כוותיה כיון דדריש שיבה כל שיבה ע"כ זקן לאו לזקן ממש אלא כריה"ג וקי"ל כאב"י הרא"ש והר"נ וכ"פ הרמב"ם דלא כרי"ף [ועוד ראיה ברורה בכתובות ס"ב ב' קם קמיה עוברא כוותיה]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if he is not, etc.</b> This accords with Rabbi Yose HaGelili, for Issi ben Yehuda necessarily agrees with him. Since Issi expounds “old age” to include every elderly person, the term “elder” cannot refer only to an aged person, but must also bear the meaning assigned to it by Rabbi Yose HaGelili. We rule in accordance with Issi ben Yehuda, as do the Rosh and the Ran, and the Rambam likewise rules this way, unlike the Rif. [There is also clear proof in Ketubot 62b from the incident in which one rose before an elderly non-Jew.]

<b>ואפי' אינו רבו. </b>ממש"ש ל"ג ב' חדא דאתון חכימי כו' הלא"ה שפיר קמו ובשבת ל"א ב' ניקו כו' וכן כמה עובדי ומזה הוכיח הר"נ ג"כ דקי"ל כריה"ג דאל"כ כיון דקי"ל כאיסי ב"י א"כ לא נ"מ בחכמה כלל ולמה אמרו בכ"מ לקום מפני חכם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if he is not his teacher.</b> This follows from the statement there on 33b, “First, because you are sages,” etc. Otherwise, their rising would have been proper regardless. Likewise, in Shabbat 31b, “Shall we rise before him,” etc., and in several other incidents. From this the Ran also proved that we rule in accordance with Rabbi Yose HaGelili. Otherwise, since we rule in accordance with Issi ben Yehuda, wisdom would have no practical relevance at all, and why would the Sages repeatedly require one to rise before a Torah scholar?

<b>ואם אין שום כו'. </b>דל"ל שיהיה שוה לשגיהם מופלגים דא"כ אמאי קאמר והוא דמיפלג כו' הל"ל כה"ג אלא דודאי הולכין בכ"מ אחר אחד מהן ולא ידעינן מנו וכתב הרשב"ם דמסתבר אחר הזקן ועבה"ג משמו וכ"ז כתכ הרא"ש ג"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if neither, etc.</b> One cannot explain that the person is equally related to both of them and that both are distinguished. If so, why does the Gemara say, “provided that he is distinguished,” etc.? It should have stated that precise case. Rather, it is certain that in every instance priority follows one of the two, but we do not know which one. Rashbam writes that it is reasonable to follow the elderly person. See Baer HaGolah, who cites this in his name. The Rosh likewise writes all of this.
