**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But it is permitted, etc.; and there are, etc.</b> Nimukei Yosef at the end of Makkot writes: 'Some are uncertain whether a Jew is prohibited from rounding the hair of a non-Jew according to Rav Huna, just as he is prohibited regarding a minor. The Rosh wrote that although Rav Huna forbids a man to round the hair of a minor, that case is different because the minor will eventually become subject to the commandment, whereas it is permitted to round the hair of a non-Jew. According to this, it is likewise permitted to round the hair of a woman.' Tosafot in Nazir, at the end of the cited passage, rejected the Rosh's reason and gave another reason to permit doing so for a non-Jew, while remaining uncertain regarding a woman. Tosafot in Shevuot, at the end of the cited passage, implies that it is forbidden to do so for a woman, for they wrote that Shmuel agrees with Rav Adda, etc.

<b>ורבו כו'. </b>שרש"י פי' במכות שם שנים תחת האוזן בחודו של לחי הבולט לחוץ ושנים לסוף הלחי וא' בשבולת הזקן וע"ש במתני' בד"ה שתים ובגמ' כ"א א' בד"ה בי פרקי דדיקנא אבל בשבועות פי' שהלחי רחב לצד הצדעים וכ"ד רחב יש בו ב' קצוות והן ד' פיאות וא' בשבילת הזקן ור"ח פירש מקום חיבור הלחי לצדעים ב' פיאות כנ"ל וב' בגבולי השפה וא' בשבולת הזקן ולד"ה החמישית בשבולת הזקן אלא שהרא"ש כתב שם אחר כל הפירושים ובפ"י דנגעים תנן איזהו הזקן מפרק של לחי עד פיקה של גרגרת ומשמע מדבריו שר"ל שלפ"ז החמישית הוא בראש הגרגרת וכ"כ הרמזים ובטור זה לפי' ר"ח ודבריו תמוהים וכן תמה הב"י עליו ועוד דבהדיא תניא בת"כ פאת זקנם סוף הזקן שבולת הזקן וכ"ד כל הפוסקים שתחת הגרון אין שום פאה וז"ל סמ"ק טוב שלא יעלה תער על הזקן ואפילו תחת הסנטר אוסר ר"ת בתער שפעמים שמושך אליו העור של פאה ר"ל של שבולת הזקן וה"ר יונה כתב מטעם אחר משום העברת שער דאסור בכל הגוף בתער כמ"ש בר"ס קפ"ב וז"ש בהג"ה כאן ואפילו כו' ומ"מ דברי הר"י לא נראה דמ"ש מכל מקומות הזקן שלא במקום הפאות דמותר אף בתער ולא אסרו אלא בגוף שאינו גדל כ"כ ואינו עושה אלא ליפותו ואסור משום לא ילבש כו': <br><b>(ליקוט) ורבו בהם כו'. </b>והעיקר כפירש"י וכ"ה בא"ר (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there are many opinions concerning them, etc.</b> Rashi explains in Makkot that two corners are beneath the ear, at the protruding edge of the jaw; two are at the end of the jaw; and one is at the tuft of the beard. See his commentary to the Mishnah there, s.v. 'two,' and to the Gemara 21a, s.v. 'the joints of the beard.' In Shevuot, however, Rashi explains that the jaw is broad toward the temples, and every broad surface has two extremities. These make four corners, with the fifth at the tuft of the beard. Rabbenu Chananel explains that the two points where the jaw joins the temples are two corners, the two edges of the lip are two more, and the fifth is at the tuft of the beard. According to all these explanations, the fifth corner is at the tuft of the beard. The Rosh, however, after citing all the explanations, wrote that Mishnah Negaim, chapter 10, teaches: 'What is considered the beard? From the joint of the jaw until the prominence of the throat.' His words imply that, according to this, the fifth corner is at the upper part of the throat. The Remazim states this as well, and the Tur attributes it to Rabbenu Chananel. His words are astonishing, as Beit Yosef also objects, especially since Torat Kohanim explicitly teaches: 'The corner of their beard means the end of the beard, the tuft of the beard.' All the authorities agree that there is no corner beneath the throat. Semak writes: 'It is proper that a razor not pass over the beard. Rabbenu Tam forbids even beneath the chin with a razor, because at times one draws toward it the skin of the corner,' meaning the skin of the beard's tuft. Rabbenu Yonah gave another reason, namely that removing body hair with a razor is forbidden throughout the body, as stated at the beginning of siman 182. This is the meaning of the gloss here, 'and even,' etc. Nevertheless, Rabbenu Yonah's reasoning does not appear correct, for why should the portions of the beard outside the five corners differ from one another? Those areas are permitted even with a razor. The prohibition concerning the body applies only where hair does not grow so heavily and a person removes it merely for beautification, which is forbidden because of 'A man's garment shall not be upon a woman,' etc. <br><b>(Likkut) And there are many opinions concerning them, etc.</b> The principal view is Rashi's explanation, and this is also stated in Eliyah Rabbah. This concludes the passage.
