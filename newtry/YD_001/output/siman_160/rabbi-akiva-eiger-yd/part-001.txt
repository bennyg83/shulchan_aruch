(סימן ק"ס סעיף א') וכמה לאוין נאמרו בו. המלוה עובר בו' לאוין. את כספך ל"ת בנשך. ובמרבית ל"ת. לא תהיה לו כנושה. אל תקח מאתו נשך. ולא תשימון. לפני עור. והלוה עובר בב' ל"ת לפ"ע והה"מ כתב דבנוסחא שלפנינו בסוגיא הלוה עובר משום ל"ת ולאחיך ל"ת. ובמ"ל כ' ומוהר"ש בתשו' (סי' קס"ב) מסתפק אם הלוה עובר משעת הלואה או משעת נתינה דוקא עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 160, seif 1) <b>And how many negative commandments were stated concerning it.</b> The lender transgresses six negative commandments: "You shall not give him your money with interest"; "nor your food for increase"; "You shall not be to him as a creditor"; "Do not take from him interest or increase"; "You shall not impose interest"; and lifnei iver. The borrower transgresses two negative commandments, lifnei iver and, according to the version before us in the sugya cited by the Hagahot Maimoniyot, "Do not impose interest" and "Do not lend your brother with interest." The Mishneh LaMelech writes that Maharshal, in responsum siman 162, is uncertain whether the borrower transgresses from the time of the loan or only from the time the interest is actually given. See there.

(בהג"ה) אבל ברבית דרבנן א"ע. ומה"ט בנשבע ליתן הרבית חלה השבועה וצריך ליתן כ"כ הרשב"א בת' (סי' תתקל"ח) הובא בב"י ס"ס זה אמנם בת' הר"ן דאף בא"ר הדין כמו כל נשבע לעבור על דרבנן אף דהשבועה חלה מ"מ אין מתירים לעבור על דרבנן בקום ועשה ע"ש. וע' ת' ת"ח למהרח"ש (כלל ג' סי"ז) דאף להרשב"א דהלוה מחוייב ליתן אבל המלוה אינו רשאי ליטול הרבית ומונעים אותו הב"ד שלא ליטול. והיכי דהוי ס' אם הוא רבית דרבנן או לא בזה להרשב"א כיון דאף בודאי א"ר מחוייב הלוה ליתן אלא דהמלוה אסור לקבלו ממילא בספק א"ר מותר למלוה לקבלו דהוי ספק דרבנן. ואף להר"ן י"ל כיון דאף בוודאי א"ר השבועה חלה אלא דאינו רשאי לקיים שבועתו לעבור על דרבנן בקום ועשה ממילא בספק א"ר כיון דרשאי ליתן מטעם ספק דרבנן ממילא מחוייב ליתן מכח השבועה ואף להסוברים דהיכא דיש ספק בחיוב השבועה וספק ממונא א"צ לשלם מכה ספק השבועה ע' בש"ך חוה"מ (סע"ב סקכ"ג) מ"מ הכא אין ס' בחיוב השבועה דהא השבועה חלה בודאי אף בא"ר ממש וכיון דספק א"ר מותר ליתן אלא דיכול לומר ק"ל דהוי א"ר ופטור מליתן אבל בנשבע חייב מכח השבועה וע' בת' מבי"ט (ח"א סי' קנ"א) וברשד"ם (חיו"ד סי' ע"ב). וברש"ך (ח"ג סל"ו) וברלב"ח (סל"ח וק"ג) ובמהרי"ט (חיו"ד ס"מ) ובת' שני המאורות הגדולים (ס"ג) וע' ת' הראנ"ח (ח"ב סכ"ב):
**** ENGLISH ****
(In the gloss) <b>But regarding rabbinic interest, he does not transgress.</b> For this reason, if one swore to pay the interest, the oath takes effect and he must pay it. The Rashba writes this in responsum siman 938, cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. The Ran, however, writes that even regarding rabbinic interest the law is like that of anyone who swears to violate a rabbinic prohibition. Although the oath takes effect, he is nevertheless not permitted to violate a rabbinic prohibition through an affirmative act. See there.

See also Responsa Torat Chaim of Maharchash, kelal 3, siman 17, that even according to the Rashba, although the borrower is obligated to pay, the lender may not accept the interest, and beit din prevents him from taking it. Where there is uncertainty whether the matter constitutes rabbinic interest, according to the Rashba, since even definite rabbinic interest must be paid by the borrower and only the lender is forbidden to receive it, in a case of doubt the lender may accept it, because the doubt concerns a rabbinic prohibition.

Even according to the Ran, one may say that since the oath certainly takes effect even regarding definite rabbinic interest, and he is prevented from fulfilling it only because he may not actively violate a rabbinic prohibition, where the status is doubtful he may pay because of the rule governing doubt in a rabbinic matter. He is consequently obligated to pay by force of the oath.

Even according to those who hold that where the obligation created by an oath is itself doubtful, and the matter is one of monetary doubt, he need not pay because of the doubtful oath, see Shach to Choshen Mishpat 72:23, here there is no doubt concerning the oath's validity. The oath certainly takes effect even regarding actual rabbinic interest. Since in a doubtful case he may pay, although he could otherwise claim kim li that it is rabbinic interest and thereby exempt himself, once he swore he is obligated by the oath. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 151; Rashdam, Yoreh De'ah, siman 72; Rashach, part 3, siman 36; Ralbach, simanim 38 and 103; Maharit, Yoreh De'ah, siman 40; Responsa Shnei HaMeorot HaGedolim, siman 3; and Responsa Raanach, part 2, siman 22.

(סעיף ד' בהג"ה) רק דרך מכר מותר. בב"י בשם תלמידי הרשב"א הנ"מ בהלואה אבל הכא דדרך מכר הוא כל שלא הזכיר לו שכר מעותיו שרי דאזולי קמוזיל גבי' עכ"ל. נ"ל דהיינו כעין עובדא דהש"ס דלקח ממנו בדרך פסיקה ובשעת פרעון הפירות נתן לו פירות יותר אבל אם נתחייב לו ממון אין חילוק בין אם נתחייב לו בדרך הלואה או מכה מכר כל שפורע לו יותר אסור ומלישנא דהש"ך (סק"ד) לא משמע כן ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 4, in the gloss) <b>It is permitted only by way of sale.</b> The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the students of the Rashba that this distinction applies to a loan, but here, since the transaction is by way of sale, as long as he did not state that the addition was compensation for the use of his money, it is permitted, because he merely sold to him at a reduced price.

It appears to me that this refers to a case like the incident in the Gemara, where he purchased from him by way of advance pricing, and when the produce was delivered he gave him additional produce. If, however, one already owes money, there is no distinction between a debt arising from a loan and one arising from a sale. Whenever he pays more than he owes, it is forbidden. The language of the Shach, seif katan 4, does not appear to indicate this. Examine carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 6) <b>He would send him a gift and explicitly state that it was so that he would lend to him.</b> In my humble opinion, this applies specifically where he sends it as a gift and asks the recipient to lend him money because of it, in which case it is merely a form of inducement. If, however, he stipulated that the gift was given on condition that the recipient lend to him, then if the recipient does not lend, he must return it. At the time of the loan it is therefore considered as though the borrower then gives him the gift.

This resembles one who says, "The debt that you owe me is forgiven on condition that you lend to me." That is equivalent to saying, "If you lend to me, the debt is forgiven," which constitutes fixed interest, as the Beit Yosef writes. This appears correct to me.

(ש"ך סק"י) וכ"כ בה"ת ומביאו ב"י. באמת דעת בעה"ת משמע דאף באינו מתכוין אסור דהא כתב אם רגיל לשלוח לו דורון שרי והוא שלא יתכוין לכך הרי דבאינו רגיל אפי' בלא מתכוין אסור וכ"כ המ"ל. ואולם זהו רק למאי דס"ל לבעה"ת כהרמב"ם דאפי' בסתם אסור וכמ"ש הב"י (ולענין זה פליגי דלהרמב"ם אינו אסור אלא במתכוין לתירוצו של הט"ז (סק"ג) וכדבריו כתבו ג"כ הפרישה והלח"מ ובמ"ל. ולהבע"ת מוכח דאפי' בלא מתכוין אסור ולמ"ש הט"ז (סקי"ב) בדעת המחבר י"ל דלא פליגי) אבל לשיטת הרא"ש והטור דסתם הרמ"א כוותייהו להלכה דאינו אסור אלא במפרש אין נ"מ מכל זה דבודאי אפי' במתכוין מותר אלא במתנה מרובה כיון דמוכחא מלתא הוי כפירש. ודברי הש"ך כאן דמשמע דכ"כ להלכה דבמתכוין אסור צ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Shach, seif katan 10) <b>And the Baal HaTerumot likewise writes this, as cited by the Beit Yosef.</b> In truth, the Baal HaTerumot appears to hold that it is forbidden even without intent. He writes that if one was accustomed to sending the lender gifts, it is permitted, provided that he does not intend the gift because of the loan. This implies that if he was not accustomed to doing so, it is forbidden even without such intent. The Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes this.

This is true only because the Baal HaTerumot follows the Rambam, who holds that even an unspecified gift is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef writes. They disagree only on the following point. According to the Rambam, it is forbidden only where he intends it because of the loan, in accordance with the Taz's explanation in seif katan 3, which is also adopted by the Perishah, Lechem Mishneh, and Mishneh LaMelech. According to the Baal HaTerumot, however, it is evident that even without intent it is forbidden. According to the Taz's explanation in seif katan 12 of the Mechaber's view, it may be that they do not disagree.

According to the Rosh and the Tur, however, whose view the Rama adopts as normative, an unspecified gift is forbidden only when the borrower expressly states its connection to the loan. All of this is therefore irrelevant. Even if he intends the gift because of the loan, it is permitted, unless it is an unusually large gift, in which case the circumstances themselves demonstrate its purpose and it is treated as though he expressly stated it. The Shach's words here, which imply that he rules as a matter of halachah that intent alone renders it forbidden, require study.

**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 9, in the gloss) <b>He receives compensation for the loan.</b> If he acted in this manner and then lent to him again, expressly stating that this second loan was made as payment of the interest that had been fixed in exchange for his agreement to lend again, one may say that this is like any other case of fixed interest.

The borrower must then pay him according to an appraisal of the value of having the money available for that period. This payment must come from the profit earned with the lender's money. The Torah prohibited only a loan made willingly by the lender. Here, however, he had no intention to lend at all, except as a form of payment of the interest. The transaction therefore does not have the status of a loan, and it is comparable to one who uses money in his possession without permission, from whom interest may be accepted, as the Mishneh LaMelech writes. This requires practical study.

(ט"ז ססק"ח) ואי לאו אדם חשוב הוא אין איסור. ע' בר"ן (פ"ק דקדושין) שכתב הא דללוי קונים בכליו של מקנה דבההיא הנאה דמקבל מיניה אף אם אינו אדם חשוב היינו דמ"מ הוי הנאת פחות מש"פ. ובחליפין דקונין בכליו אע"פ שאינו ש"פ מהני אף באינו אדם חשוב וכן הא דלרב אין קונין בכליו ש"מ אף באדם חשוב היינו משום דנותן ע"מ להחזיר הוא לא מחשב קבלתו הנאה עיי"ש א"כ י"ל דבהא לא פליג רב על לוי ואף באינו חשוב מקרי עכ"פ הקבלה הנאה כ"ש. אלא דחליפין גרע כיון דע"מ להחזיר הוא ליכא הנאה כלל וא"כ הכא לענין רבית דלדעת הרבה פוסקים אף בפחות מש"פ אסור ועכ"פ מדרבנן י"ל דאף באדם שאינו חשוב אסור ודו"ק ועי' ש"ע א"ח (סי' רכ"ג ובמג"א סק"ח):
**** ENGLISH ****
(Taz, end of seif katan 8) <b>But if he is not an important person, there is no prohibition.</b> See the Ran in the first chapter of Kiddushin. He explains that according to Levi, acquisition by exchange is performed with the vessel of the transferor because of the benefit the transferor receives from the recipient, even when the recipient is not an important person. In that case, however, the benefit is worth less than a perutah. Since acquisition by exchange is effective with a vessel even when the vessel is worth less than a perutah, it is effective even where the recipient is not important.

The Ran further explains that according to Rav, acquisition by exchange is not performed with the transferor's vessel, even when the recipient is an important person, because the transferor gives it on condition that it be returned, and receipt of something given on condition of return is not considered a benefit. See there.

Accordingly, one may say that Rav does not disagree with Levi concerning the basic principle. Even receipt from a person who is not important is still considered some benefit. Acquisition by exchange is different, because the object is given on condition that it be returned and there is therefore no benefit at all. Here, regarding interest, many authorities hold that even a benefit worth less than a perutah is forbidden, at least rabbinically. It may therefore be forbidden even where the recipient is not an important person. Examine carefully, and see Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 223, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 8.

**** ENGLISH ****
(Shulchan Aruch, seif 18) <b>With the money of orphans.</b> This applies even to those who are themselves orphans of orphans, as the Mishneh LaMelech writes in the name of Maharit, responsa part 1, siman 144.

If their father lent money with rabbinic interest and then died, the orphans may not collect even the interest that accrued after their father's death. This is more stringent than the case of one who borrowed from orphans without any stipulation concerning interest.

If orphans themselves lent money with rabbinic interest and did not collect until they reached adulthood, they may collect the interest that accrued while they were minors, but not what accrued after they became adults. This remains true even if, while they were minors, they capitalized the entire amount as principal at the time of the loan. The Mishneh LaMelech concludes this.

Maharikash, however, writes in siman 161 that where a promissory note containing rabbinic interest came before orphans, they may collect what accrued after their father's death. See there. He further writes that if the borrower undertook to pay the lender's successors, the orphans may recover rabbinic interest through beit din as though it had been given to their father as rabbinic interest after his death.

The Gidulei Terumah writes that the same law applies where the owner became mentally incompetent, for an administrator is appointed to supervise his property. The Mishneh LaMelech writes that the same applies to a deaf-mute.

(ש"ע סעיף כ') והאכיל היתומים. משמע דוקא האכיל אבל אם עדיין בעין מוציאים מהם ובת' הריב"ש הובא בב"י מבואר דבקטנים אף שעדיין בעין אין מוציאים מהם כיון דהחזרה הוא רק משום מצוה ולא שעבוד נכסים וכדלקמן [סקס"א ס"ה] ויתומים קטנים לאו בני מעבד מצוה נינהו ע"ש ומ"מ משמע שם דדוקא כשנתן להיתומים קטנים אבל כשעדיין הרבית ביד האפוטרופוס מוציאים מהם וכ"כ המ"ל. ולכאורה יש לדון הא קיי"ל דזכין לקטן וא"כ נימא דמיד שקבל האפוטרופוס מיד הלוה זכה בשביל היתומים וכבא לידם דמי ואפשר כיון דזכיי' טטעם שליחות אתרבאי והוי בכלל אין שליח לד"ע וא"י לזכות להם גם בלא"ה י"ל כיון דהוא עבירה להיתומים לקבל הרבית אין זה זכות לפניהם שיזכה עבורם להכשילם באיסור רבית ולשון הש"ע פטורים מלשלם אפי' לכשיגדילו לא ידעתי אמאי לא נקט הדין באפוטרופוס דגדולים דג"כ פטורים כדאית' במקור הדין בהרשב"א וע' בת' כרם לשלמה (ס"ד):
**** ENGLISH ****
(Shulchan Aruch, seif 20) <b>And he fed the orphans.</b> This implies specifically that the interest had already been consumed for their maintenance. If it still exists, it is recovered from them.

In the Rivash's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef, however, it is clear that regarding minor orphans, even if the money still exists, it is not recovered from them, because the obligation to return interest is only a mitzvah and not a lien upon property, as stated below in siman 161, seif 5. Minor orphans are not obligated in the performance of mitzvot. See there.

Nevertheless, it appears from that responsum that this applies specifically after the money was given to the minor orphans. If the interest remains in the administrator's possession, it is recovered from him. The Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes this.

At first glance, there is room for analysis. We maintain that one may acquire property on behalf of a minor. It should therefore be said that as soon as the administrator received the money from the borrower, he acquired it on behalf of the orphans, and it is considered as though it came into their possession.

Perhaps, since acquisition on another's behalf is derived from the law of agency, this falls within the rule that there is no agency for the commission of a transgression, and he therefore cannot acquire it for them. Independently, one may also say that since it is forbidden for the orphans to receive the interest, acquisition of it is not considered beneficial to them, because it causes them to violate the prohibition of interest.

The wording of the Shulchan Aruch, that they are exempt from payment even after reaching adulthood, is difficult to me. Why did he not state the same law regarding an administrator acting for adult orphans, who are likewise exempt, as appears in the source of the ruling in the Rashba? See Responsa Kerem Shlomo, siman 4.
