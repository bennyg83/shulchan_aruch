<b>ל"ש האם. </b>בגמרא אמרי' אותו ואת בנו אין לי אלא אותו ואת בנו בנו ואותו מנין ת"ל לא תשחטו הרי כאן שנים פרש"י הזהיר את שניהם בין שחט האם בין שחט הבת ולא משכחת ב' עושין עבירה אלא בשלשה בהמות הא כיצד בת ואם ובת דאי אם וב' בנים ל"ל קרא פשיטא מה לי אחד מה לי שנים דכי היכי דמחייב האי מיחייב האי ואי בפרה ובתה ובת בתה פשיטא תרווייהו אותו ואת בנו נינהו אלא ע"כ באחד שוחט פרה והב' שוחט אמה והג' בתה וקמ"ל דאבנו ואותו נמי חייב דהיינו השני:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whether the mother.</b> In the Gemara they say: oto v'et beno—I have only it and its son; whence do I include its son and it? It says "you shall not slaughter"—behold two. Rashi: he warned both, whether he slaughtered the mother or the daughter; and you do not find two performing one transgression except with three animals. How so? Daughter, mother, and daughter—for if mother and two sons, why do I need a verse—it is obvious; whether one or two, as this one is liable so that one is liable; and if cow and its daughter and daughter's daughter—it is obvious both are oto v'et beno; rather necessarily one slaughters the cow and the two slaughter mother and the third daughter—and it teaches us that for its son and it one is also liable—that is the second.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only the right in his hand.</b> Ostensibly one could learn from this: if one swore not to slaughter his animal but only keep it for plowing and sold it—even the buyer cannot slaughter, for the seller had no right to slaughter and can sell only the right in his hand; and this is not so at all—for then it would be difficult from here on one who swore not to sell something and transgressed and sold, for we say in Choshen Mishpat siman 208 it is a sale and not as Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 230; and I wrote from this also below siman 228 seif 35. Rather it appears they did not say here he cannot sell only his right, but not to annul another's right in what he already acquired; but if there is no annulling another's right, the buyer has no obligation to fulfill the seller's oath—for as long as it was in his possession the oath applied, not when it came to another; and even though a person can forbid his own thing upon another—even when it leaves his possession, as Tur Yoreh Deah siman 217—there it is different, for the prohibition applies to the object and remains on it forever, unlike an oath that applies to the person who swore, as explained in Laws of Vows—therefore there is no obligation except while in his possession; but on the object nothing applies, as explained.

<b>After he sold them, etc.</b> Implies if he said so at time of sale he is believed to forbid; and so from what Rosh wrote in chapter Oto V'et Beno: I saw written in name of Rav Achai: if a non-Jew merchant sells animals one must be concerned lest he sold the mother today; and it is not reasonable it is common, and we do not establish prohibition so long as the seller did not inform, etc.; if there is concern from doubt, etc.—end; implies if he informed at time of sale one must forbid slaughtering that day the animal bought from him; and it is plain if there is reason he does so to enhance his sale, his words are nothing, like that in last chapter of Yevamot (daf 122): non-Jew who sells produce and said they are orlah—his words are nothing; and see siman 122 seif 6 more on law of idolatry in this.
