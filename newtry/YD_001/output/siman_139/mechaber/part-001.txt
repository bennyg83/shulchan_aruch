**** ENGLISH ****
A law of elilim, their nullification, and their appurtenances and vessels; it contains 15 se’ifim. An elil is forbidden in benefit — it itself, and its appurtenances, and its ornaments, and its offerings — whether it belongs to a non-Jew or to a Jew. Except that the elil of a non-Jew is forbidden immediately, while that of a Jew is not forbidden until it is worshipped. And its appurtenances and its ornaments — whether of a non-Jew or of a Jew — are not forbidden until one uses them; and its offering is forbidden once they brought it before it. And something from which one made an offering becomes forbidden. (If a Jew set up a brick and a non-Jew bowed to it, it becomes forbidden) (Hagahot Ashiri, perek R. Yishmael; Rambam, Hilkhot Avodat Kochavim 8:3; and Shas, perek Kol HaTzelamim (Avodah Zarah, daf 43a) end of amud a, and perek Rabbi Yishmael (Avodah Zarah, daf 53b).)

**** ENGLISH ****
What is an ornament, and what is an offering? An ornament is, for example, that one lights candles before it, or spreads before it garments and fine vessels as ornamentation. And an offering is anything of which the like is offered upon an altar — such as all kinds of foods, for example meat, fats, fine flour, water, and salt: if he placed it before it as an offering, it becomes forbidden immediately. But something of which they do not offer in the interior does not become forbidden unless he did with it something like slaughtering or something like a breakable sprinkling, and it is the manner of worshipping it with that thing — even though it is not the manner of worshipping it in this way. How so? An elil that they worship by clacking before it with a stick: if he broke a stick before it, it becomes forbidden, because breaking the stick resembles slaughtering. But if they do not worship it with a stick at all, and he broke a stick before it, he is not liable and it is not forbidden. And if he worshipped it by clacking his stick and this is the manner of its worship, he is liable and it is not forbidden. And likewise with anything that one worships in the manner of its worship, whether it is a manner of honor or a manner of disgrace, and it is not like an “interior” offering — he is liable and it is not forbidden. But if he did not worship it with a stick in the manner of its worship, but rather threw it before it, he is not liable and it is not forbidden.
