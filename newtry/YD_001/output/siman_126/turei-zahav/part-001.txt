"And even if he can sell it to a non-Jew." The reason there in Terumat HaDeshen siman 204: even though he can sell it to a non-Jew cheaply and this is not called great loss, as is proven there from terumah mixed with chullin — nevertheless here we consider this great loss wherever he must sell cheaply; for regarding yayin nesech we are not so precise in this time as with other issurim; and furthermore, if you consider it minor loss and sell it to a non-Jew, leniency will follow — for many great ones hold it forbidden to sell to a non-Jew wine forbidden through contact of a non-Jew, until here; and the second reason does not apply for us, for we hold above siman 123 that stam yeinam is permitted in benefit in this time, and all the more so regarding nitzok, which is only a stringency; and furthermore his words here are surprising — for it is plain that there is greater issur when a Jew drinks it than when he sells it to a non-Jew, even according to those who forbid; if so, it is better to make a minor issur than a major issur; nevertheless the first reason of Terumat HaDeshen permits us — from the leniencies of wine in this time they changed here, and we consider this great loss — what must be sold cheaply to gentiles; and it appears there that even if it is somewhat cheap in selling to a non-Jew, it is called great loss regarding this wine:

"Is called minor loss." It is written in Levush that he should pour it out and not sell it to a non-Jew — since in great loss we permit for this reason, that is: since there are many views that forbid selling contact of a non-Jew to a non-Jew, it is like contradictory views, end of his words; and he was not precise at all in this — for until here the author of Terumat HaDeshen did not say this reasoning except for himself, who did not have a clear halachah of permission to sell contact of a non-Jew to a non-Jew, until here he wrote that there is no stringency to sell it to a non-Jew, for this too has issur; but for us, who hold that even wine of a non-Jew itself is permitted in benefit — why should it be less if a non-Jew touched our wine through nitzok connection, that it should be forbidden in benefit? And let it be matters of rabbinic law like a mere nothing — until here it is plain that even in minor loss he can sell it to a non-Jew; and not only that, but it appears to me that even the great ones who forbid stam yeinam in benefit agree here that he can sell it to a non-Jew, since there is no issur here at all — for in great loss we rely on Rabbeinu Tam, who holds nitzok is not connection at all — so it appears to me clearly:

"One who pours wine etc." Tur wrote: some say that nitzok applies even to stam wine, etc. Beit Yosef raised many challenges on Tur; and it appears for understanding that this is the explanation of Tur's words — some say that nitzok applies even to stam yeinam, everything is forbidden in benefit, for he holds that the stringency in the lower one itself applies to the upper one, and we do not consider them as mixed to nullify in sixty, for in truth they are not mixed — rather we say the severity of the lower is upon the upper literally, as if it itself is stam yeinam because of itself. And Rashba disagrees with this in truth, as is explained in his language that Beit Yosef brings, who wrote that it is only by the law of connection to the lower one, as if it became mixed into it, and it is found well nullified in sixty — for so Rashba holds regarding stam yeinam, that it is nullified in sixty, or he carries benefit to the Dead Sea, as Tur wrote nearby; and likewise Rashba in his explanation in Terumat HaDeshen daf 151 side 1, who disagrees with these "some say" that Tur brought, and this is the view of Raavad; and on daf 152 he further wrote: and nevertheless, according to all, nitzok is not like contact of a non-Jew but like mixtures of wine; and after Tur brought the view of "some say" who are very stringent, he brought the view of Rashba who is very lenient — for he holds that they said nitzok is connection only regarding pure yayin nesech, meaning clear wine visible — whether certainly yeinam or stam — in this connection applies, each according to its law; certainly yeinam, everything is forbidden; and in stam yeinam he carries benefit, etc.; and likewise in Teshuvot HaRashba daf 151: but regarding stam yeinam forbidden in benefit, yayin nesech is connection they said, and yayin nesech is called only what is forbidden in benefit, end of his words. But regarding one who pours into a utensil that was not rinsed, where there is no pure wine but only absorption alone — Rashba is lenient, for here there is no connection at all and the upper is permitted even for drinking; and Tur disagrees with both of them regarding pure yayin nesech, meaning certainly yeinam — the upper has no remedy; even though in Rashba's language too he mentioned the language of pure yayin nesech, and we explained in it that it includes stam yeinam too — this is not surprising, for each learns from his context; from the end we hear the beginning — in Rashba it is written at the end: but one who pours into a utensil of a non-Jew — automatically we learn that the beginning deals even with stam yeinam; but here he afterward wrote in explanation: and if to stam yeinam — automatically the beginning deals with certainly yeinam; it is found that Tur disagrees with "some say," and also Rashba; but in what he wrote that in pouring into a utensil that was not rinsed there is no connection at all — in this Tur disagrees with him, for it is connection in all types of connection; and on what Tur wrote: if to stam yeinam he carries benefit — Beit Yosef challenged: what benefit does he have, for nothing was mixed there? I wondered why he did not challenge thus on what Tur wrote afterward in the name of Rashba: if there is in the water of the barrel sixty, etc.; and likewise he ruled in Shulchan Aruch in the next seif; and why did Beit Yosef not challenge: how is nullification in sixty applicable regarding a winepress against the stream, for the stream is not mixed there? Rather it is plain that the Sages made nitzok connection in yayin nesech like heter absorbed in issur when it is hot — for we hold that even when the issur is in its completeness we require sixty against all of it; so too the Sages made a stringency here and require sixty to nullify the stream as if it is literally there; and likewise regarding if there is not sixty there, we view as if part of the heter above became issur literally and he must carry benefit, etc.; also Rama in Darkei Moshe brought proof for Tur's view from the words of Mordechai perek HaSocher, that we require sixty against yayin nesech in a utensil from which a non-Jew pours — we learn that it is as if it is mixed; therefore Rama well ruled here: and if it is stam yeinam it is nullified in sixty; and in what I wrote all the challenges that Beit Yosef raised are resolved without any forced explanation — what some strained in this; also the language of Rashba that Beit Yosef brought is well explained according to this path:
