**** ENGLISH ****
When they arrange the marriage, etc. Bach wrote: the explanation is they postponed the marriage generally and did not agree at that time on which date the marriage would take place, but said when they would gather together after they have settled and made peace they would set the time for marriage — in this we are concerned perhaps she did not take it to heart to be careful in her bedikot, since she does not know the set time for marriage she is not concerned about those first seven clean days; but if the postponement from the first set time and the setting of the second time were all at the same session and they were occupied with that matter — there is no doubt she need not return to count seven clean days anew; and if she examined herself at that time and found herself pure — she is pure; and unlike Taz who was stringent here from his own reasoning without proof. However if through the quarrel the assembly broke up and at that session the bride agreed to marry another man and she entered the chuppah immediately that same day — the matter is obvious that this is a genuinely new proposal, and even if she examined herself and found herself pure she must count seven clean days again, for there is new desire for another man — end quote; and so Taz. And also in Maadanei Melech he copied Taz's latter words but not the former.

**** ENGLISH ****Although she checked, etc. And in responsum Mas'at Binyamin siman 42 he elaborated to disagree and holds she need not return to sit 7 clean days except when she did not check herself; and concludes at end — nevertheless one should be stringent per Mahari"k and Rav; and nevertheless regarding a bride who wore whites and counted 7 clean days with intent for marriage set for Rosh Chodesh Nisan; when Rosh Chodesh Nisan arrived he did not come, and the next day a messenger came from the groom that an ones befell him on the road and immediately he will come in a day or two; they made chuppah 2 or 3 days after Rosh Chodesh Nisan — in this certainly she does not need other 7 clean days even if she did not check herself in the days between, if 7 days did not pass without bedikah; and even Rav and Mahari"k agree, for since marriage was not postponed except due to some ones that delayed the matter, certainly she did not despair of the first 7 clean days and relied that groom will come after day or two, and relied on herself and gave her mind she would be clean and not come to concern of desire — end quote. And in truth one need not be stringent in these laws, for anyway many poskim hold 7 clean days not required; and also di'eved one bedikah within 7 suffices for all; and also the entire essence of this law is from their words.
