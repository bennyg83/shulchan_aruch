**** ENGLISH ****That day, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Aviassaf: how much is onah — a day or night in the days of Nisan and Tishrei, and half a day and half a night in the days of Tammuz and Tevet, end of his words. And Beit Yosef rejected his words since the poskim did not mention it, and also the language of the Talmud implies it is particular only about day or night, not about hours, etc. And Maadanei Melekh on the first chapter of Niddah and Bach challenged him, that Aviassaf's words are correct and explained in the Talmud chapter Tinoket (Niddah 65b), end of his words; and so I found in Raavan siman 318 like Aviassaf. And that the poskim did not mention it is no proof — for it deals with an unspecified day and unspecified night; and alternatively in the season of Tammuz and Tevet one should calculate seasonal hours, and similarly we find in many places in Talmud and poskim, such as regarding keriat shema in Orach Chaim siman 58, and regarding chametz on Pesach in Orach Chaim siman 443 seif 1 in the gloss, and regarding calculation of seasons — and similarly Turei Zahav wrote: a person separates from his wife near her veset twenty-four hours; if she is accustomed to see by day he separates from her that entire day and the night before, and likewise the reverse, end of his words. And see Raavan siman 318 and in the Talmud chapter HaIshah end of 63a — and investigate. In my humble opinion the opinion of Raavan is certainly: if she has a fixed veset by day, such as she has a veset to see always at its beginning, or always in its middle, or always at its end — she need only separate that day and not before; and thus the Talmud and poskim deal. But if she is accustomed to see by day and has no fixed hour, only sometimes at the beginning, sometimes in the middle, and sometimes at the end — then the entire day is her veset and it is as if her veset were the entire day; certainly a woman whose veset is the entire day must separate the entire night before, and thus Or Zarua deals. And with this Beit Yosef's challenge that Or Zarua is against the Talmud is well settled; and in my humble opinion to extract from the Talmud as written — investigate. Also Bach wrote that it implies one who fears the word of God practices like Or Zarua; and also in Hagahat Shach siman 7 he brought Or Zarua's words in conclusion; and likewise in Or Zarua of Maharil in the name of Maharil.
