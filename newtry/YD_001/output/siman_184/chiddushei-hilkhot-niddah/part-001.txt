**** ENGLISH ****
He came upon her, etc. — Shach in seif kaf 1 holds that if she has no veset, presumably her veset is every thirty days, and within thirty is called "not at the time of her veset" and one may be with her without bedikah. This has no basis: Shulchan Aruch wrote that a woman with a fixed veset — because "one who has no veset" below in siman 186 follows R. Chananel and Rambam that even her husband needs bedikah. If so, what does "intermediate onah" help? Everywhere in Shas and poskim, one without veset needs bedikah — how can there be intermediate onah? It is strained to say uprooting intermediate onah after three times without seeing; and Shach equates intermediate onah to veset. The author correctly omitted intermediate onah here, unlike elsewhere. (Avraham Karelitz.)

**** ENGLISH ****
One onah, etc. — see Shach seif kaf 7: Rosh in Niddah often holds veset derabbanan, but in Yevamot agrees with R. Chananel that one going on the road should not visit his wife near her veset except hugging and kissing, not tashmish, since veset is deoraita. Beit Yosef finds these contradictory. One can say Rosh there holds veset is not deoraita — then why permit derabbanan issur from "ufkadta"? Or the verse applies when not at veset time; near veset there is sin concern but not deoraita issur when traveling. (Avraham Karelitz.)

**** ENGLISH ****
Time of her veset — thirty days like veset: mishnah "all women presumed tahor for husbands if he finds her within days of their onah" — Rashi: intermediate onah. If no veset no bedikah needed; if bedikah needed, mishnah means "has veset." "Within days of onah" means before fixed veset — permitted without bedikah; after veset time — presumed tamei until checking. Intermediate onah din doesn't exist in Shas; Rama omitted it. (Avraham Karelitz.)
