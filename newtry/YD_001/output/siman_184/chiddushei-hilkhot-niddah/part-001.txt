בא עלי' וכו' דעת הש"ך בס"ק א' דאם אין לה וסת מסתמא וסתה כל ל' יום ובתוך ל' מיקרי שלא בשעת וסת' ומותר לבוא עליה בלי בדיקה ודבר זה אין לו שחר דהא דכתב הש"ע אשה שיש לה וסת קבוע משום דבאין לה וסת לקמן בסי' קפ"ו פוסק כדברי ר"ח ורמב"ם דאף לבעלה בעי בדיקה וא"כ מה אהני דיש לה עונה בינונית וא"כ הא דתנן בכל הש"ס ופוסקים דאין לה וסת בעי בדיקה היכי מצינו דהא יש כאן עונה בינונית ודוחק לומר דמיירי דעקרוה לעונה בינונית שעברו שלשה זמנים ולא ראתה בעונה בינונית חדא דזה דוחק דכל הך משא ומתן באין לה וסת יהי' בכה"ג דוקא באופן רחוק ועוד להש"ך דמשוה עונה בינונית לוסת א"כ בממ"נ אם עקרוה לעונה בינונית לג' ראי' אחרים שווים הרי קבעה וסת לראי' ההוא ואין לה וסת איך קרית לה ואם עקרוה לג' ראי' בלתי שוין עדיין עונה בינונית במקומו כמו וסת הקבוע כמבואר לקמן בסי' קפ"ט אלא שאני מוסף דלא מצינו בשום דוכת' עקירה לעונה בינונית כל זמן שלא קבעה וסת דאמרינן אשה זו הואיל ואין לה קביעות לראי' אנו אמרינן דרך נשים לה כרוב נשים מעונה לעונה ולכן ברור דכי אמרינן עונה בינונית היינו להחמיר דינו כדין וסת קבוע אבל לא להקל דאין עונה קובע זמן כמו הוסת וכמ"ש הר"ן דבוסת דיו שעתה ובעונה בינונית לא אמרינן כן ולכך כאן להקל ולהתיר באין לה וסת תוך ל' יום בלי בדיקה ליתא ולכן דקדק המחבר ולא כתב כאן דעונה בינונית דינה כוסת קבוע כמ"ש בכמה דוכתי ונכון (עבק"א):
**** ENGLISH ****
He had relations with her, etc. The Shach's opinion in seif kaf alef: if she has no veset, presumably her veset is every thirty days, and within thirty is called not at her veset time and it is permitted to have relations with her without bedikah — and this has no basis. For that which Shulchan Arukh wrote "a woman who has a fixed veset" — because in a woman without veset later in siman 186 he rules like Rabbenu Chananel and Rambam that even her husband needs bedikah — if so, what help is it that she has intermediate onah? And if so, that which we learned throughout Talmud and poskim that a woman without veset needs bedikah — how do we find this? For behold there is intermediate onah here. And it is strained to say it deals with one who uprooted intermediate onah after three times passed and she did not see at intermediate onah — one, this is strained, for all this negotiation in a woman without veset would be specifically in such a remote case; and further for the Shach who equates intermediate onah to veset — if so perforce, if she uprooted intermediate onah for three other equal sightings, behold she established veset for that sighting and has no veset — how do you call her without veset? And if she uprooted for three unequal sightings, intermediate onah is still in its place like fixed veset as explained later siman 189. But I add that we do not find anywhere uprooting of intermediate onah as long as she did not establish veset — for we say: this woman, since she has no fixity of sighting, we say the way of women for her is like most women from onah to onah; and therefore it is clear that when we say intermediate onah, we mean to make its law like fixed veset law, but not to be lenient — for onah does not fix time like veset; and as Ran wrote: for veset its time suffices, but for intermediate onah we do not say so. And therefore here to be lenient and permit in a woman without veset within thirty days without bedikah — there is none. And therefore the Mechaber was precise and did not write here that intermediate onah has the law of fixed veset as he wrote in several places — and it is correct (Avraham Karelitz).

**** ENGLISH ****
One onah, etc. See Shach seif kaf heh who wrote: from derabbanan, for we rule veset derabbanan; and so the Rosh in tractate Niddah in many places. But in Yevamot the Rosh agreed to Rabbenu Chananel that one going on a journey should not be intimate with his wife if she is near her veset — only embrace and kiss, not relations — for veset is deoraita; and the matters appear contradictory, as Beit Yosef wrote — investigate. And it is possible to say the Rosh's words there are perforce: if you hold veset is not deoraita, from where do we permit a rabbinic prohibition? And the verse "you shall visit your nest" deals with not at veset time; and from where do we permit rabbinic prohibition because of departure on a journey? And if you say we expound thus: "and you shall visit your nest and you shall not sin" implies there is concern of sin, but in this case of going on a journey you shall not sin — and it must deal with near her veset where besides going on a journey there is sin — if so, all the more veset is deoraita, since about this the verse revolves. And if so it is simple: why did he actually permit transgressing Torah prohibition because of the woman's longing for her husband? This matter is far from reason that Torah prohibition would be permitted for this; and the Rosh's words are perforce and strained — and it requires investigation (Avraham Karelitz).

That day, etc. See Shach seif kaf zayin who brought Aviassaf and Raavan's opinion that the measure of separation of onah is twelve hours; and Bach and so Levush supported him from the onah of virgin blood that is explained as twelve hours. And in truth the Gemara sugya in Niddah 63 is clear not like Raavan and Aviassaf — for we say: if she was accustomed to see by day, she is permitted the night before, for the entire night is hers; and in the Gemara the presumption was that separation of onah is another onah, and it answers: no, that onah. And Shach seif kaf zayin explained the Gemara deals with one who has veset to see always at its beginning or always in its middle, etc. — she need only separate that day and not before; and Or Zarua deals if there is no fixity that day, sometimes at beginning, etc. — investigate. And these matters have no basis — apply yourself: a woman whose veset is to see immediately at sunrise — she would be permitted the night before; and if sometimes she delays seeing from sunrise onward, the entire night would be forbidden according to my view; and still nothing returns to Aviassaf's approach who holds onah is twelve hours — and behold Shach admits if she fixed to see at the beginning of the day she is permitted the night before — where then are twelve hours? And Taz in seif kaf bet struggled to distinguish between this onah of virgin blood and onah of separation — and one need not strain, for the distinction is clear: there it is the measure that blood flows from the wound of virginity compression, and what does blood care to flow at changed times day and night — behold twelve hours from the wound blood flows; and shall we say when the day is long in winter fifteen hours it flows fifteen hours from the wound, and in summer when day is short nine hours it flows nine hours — this is impossible; therefore we always say twelve hours. But niddah veset is already known to depend on the course of sun and moon until they established monthly veset for the moon's course, saying this day of the month causes sighting; and likewise weekly veset that this day of the week causes sighting; and likewise sometimes in a woman the sun above the horizon causes sighting and sometimes the reverse when sun is below the earth — according to the woman's constitution and qualities the occurrence changes, sometimes absence of sun and sometimes presence of sun fixes and causes veset — and this is simple. And if so, what difference if day and night are long or short — in the end the sun shines or sets and sighting from the woman depends on this, not on duration long or short — and this is simple and correct.

And I made support for the above distinction in what we say in the Gemara Shabbat chapter Rabbi Meir: according to Rabbi Akiva who said a woman emits seed on day five of onah — they separate in the morning, and behold they are tevulei yom; and it answers: the Torah was given to tevulei yom. And it asks: was it given or fit — and one must understand: what difference if given or fit? And also what it answered "fit" is only prophetic words — from where did he know to say sixty myriads of women of Israel, not one emitted seed and immersed? This requires kabbalah halakhah leMoshe miSinai that was then at Sinai; and for what purpose is this transmitted where there is no difference? However the matter is clear: there is doubt whether we say onah is twelve hours like Aviassaf's approach and it does not depend if day is long or short, or everything depends on day and there is no difference long or short day — and accordingly if onah depends on hours, when Israel separated on day four, behold here is day four and night twenty-four hours; day five and night likewise twenty-four hours — behold four onot; and on day six twelve hours — if so on day of Sivan which is a long day, before the sun set all five onot ended, and even though they still emitted on the long day to immerse before sunset — behold they are tevulei yom. But if we say it depends on days — day six would be so long like the day the sun stood for Joshua — nevertheless if she emits with sunset she is impure, and if so they have immersion only at night and are tevulei yom — it is not difficult, for if so why does the Gemara challenge "behold tevulei yom"? And it answers: the Torah was given to tevulei yom — from where do we know this approach who holds twelve hours is onah? One can say: otherwise it is difficult from where does a woman emit seed on day five of onah — four onot suffice; and that they separated on day four in the morning — because if they had relations on day four until eve, behold it is night five and day five two onot, night six and day six two onot — behold four onot; and if they emit on day six at sunset, behold tevulei yom — how will they receive the Torah? Therefore Moses had to advance one onah for them; and since he advanced, he gave them in the morning — for in the morning it rose, etc.; and on this it answers well: the Torah was given to tevulei yom. And if so there was no need to nullify peru urevu from Israel every day four; and therefore we require five onot. But now that we rule five onot are required and on four they separate — it works well for them; and on this it answered: fit to give it — this is truly so, for twelve hours it depends and not days; and if so then the day was long and they immersed before evening and were tevulei yom — and one need not prophetic words that no woman was careful about emission, only we hold as above — and this is clear in my humble opinion.

Nevertheless we must resolve Aviassaf and Raavan's words that the Gemara's words should not challenge them; and it appears they certainly hold according to Rabbi Yosi who holds for her veset time: if she saw at midday she is permitted to have relations until midday until the hour she sees — and Rabbi Yehuda should wonder: will he not expound this warning that they separate from their wives near their veset? And if so Rabbi Yosi's warning to Israel to separate, etc., is like tannaim — for Rabbi Yosi we do not expound — and this is very strained. And one need not establish this warning on veset time itself besides that the Gemara language does not imply so; and also one need not a verse — "and you shall be careful" is obvious at veset time one must separate without warning at all, lest at time of relations she see — how can one think a) at onah and at that time have relations? One must say there are two matters of warnings: veset is at the time fixed for seeing — this is called veset, and this is certainly forbidden to have relations for one who holds veset deoraita from Torah and for one who holds derabbanan from the rabbis; but this has no relevance to "and you shall be careful," for this is a full obligation and prohibition even for one going on a journey and similar. But afterward near it — this is called near veset; and this is included in "and you shall be careful" — that they should separate near veset specifically, for that time when she sees is called her veset; however the earlier time is called near her veset, and this is included in general carefulness; and this one the Torah permitted for one going on a journey and similar. And if so, the mishnah never mentioned this warning near her veset, for it is only a general stringency and fence — the mishnah sages did not speak of it, only Talmud sages afterward were stringent in this warning to say even near her veset it is forbidden to have relations; and therefore we do not find separation near veset in the mishnah. Rabbi Yosi and Rabbi Yehuda divided in the mishnah the law of separating veset itself, for this is included in prohibition either of Torah or derabbanan; and therefore Rabbi Yehuda permits one who sees by day on the night before, for this is not included in veset; and Rabbi Yosi permits even by day until the boundary fixed in hours of her day of seeing. But near veset is not dealt with in the mishnah — and with this it is settled that the Torah permitted having relations near veset for one going on a journey: for one who holds her veset deoraita, how did Torah permit — Tosafot and Rosh's challenge; but according to what is written it works well: this is not included in veset deoraita, and the Torah did not permit for one going on a journey except the adjacent onah; but Talmud sages were stringent to separate from onah near veset, and it is included in rabbinic warning — and this is specifically to separate near veset, for on the day itself it is called veset but before it is called near her veset. And accordingly it is justified what the Gemara challenges on that which Rava said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda, and we learned, etc., and Rava said: how much onah — what, not another onah? It is difficult: one, how can one think to say another onah, and why did Rava understand thus and challenge from him further questions; two, according to this if onah is another onah, then Rava is not like anyone — not Rabbi Yehuda who holds only that onah as the Gemara's challenge, and all the more he is not like Rabbi Yosi — and if so he should have challenged Rava is not like anyone; three, why specifically that he said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda. But according to Aviassaf it works well: we say a person must separate from his wife near veset — how much onah? The Gemara understood this "near" is the measure of onah; and the Gemara knew daily practice would prove; and so halakhah is taught: one separates from a woman only twelve hours measure of onah from time of sighting — if she saw midday, he separates from her half the night before that aligns to twelve hours; and if so, granted halakhah like Rabbi Yosi that veset is called at the hour she sees midday — he says well that near twelve hours from it one must separate, and this agrees with daily practice. But if halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — according to his words even one seeing at midday, the entire day is considered veset; and if you fix him to separate onah beforehand, he is also forbidden the entire night — and this is against daily practice; and the Gemara's challenge works well. And the Gemara answers: not that onah — the intention of "how much onah" is not on near veset alone that this would be onah, but the intention is one must separate so that between all from time of separation to time of sighting there are twelve hours that is measure of onah; and thus Rava said onah. And if so the matter agrees with Rabbi Yehuda and daily practice. And one can say: on this the Gemara challenges "why two" — certainly this explanation; and one need not Rava's explanation of measure of onah to know halakhah like Rabbi Yosi; and the Gemara answers: I would think like Rabbi Yosi except for that veset time, but that which it challenged requires the reverse — let us say halakhah like Rabbi Yosi and he should not say onah — this is difficult at first glance for Aviassaf's approach, for from this we do not know he separates further outside veset — and according to Rabbi Yehuda we only heard the entire day is included in prohibition. However according to the above one can also say: a reason is needed why the Sages made him separate twelve hours beforehand — what difference twelve hours, why not less or more? For granted for us everything depends on sunset and sunrise as above; but for his approach it is difficult. And one must say: the entire essence of the stringency of separating before her veset is: if you say only according to the stringency mentioned in the mishnah for Rabbi Yehuda — if she sees at dawn she is permitted to have relations the night before — the woman will not notice the difference between sunset and sunrise and will think before time of sighting she is permitted to have relations like Rabbi Yosi; and even if she sees near sunset at day's end she will have relations beforehand and this is forbidden, for the entire day is included in prohibition; and therefore the Sages made separation of twelve hours — and if so it is always impossible to come to prohibition of having relations at her veset time. And one can say: even though Rava did not say "how much onah," only he teaches halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — this Rabbi Yosi statement alone suffices: separate from one's wife near veset — we would know measure twelve hours, for the entire essence of separation is because of the above error; and therefore the measure is twelve hours, for otherwise what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment? And more than twelve hours is unnecessary — for what purpose shall one say so? Granted if halakhah like Rabbi Yosi it works well that separation near is that entire day or night, and what for Rabbi Yehuda is forbidden by law for Rabbi Yosi is because of reason of separation near veset; and Rava needed to inform us it is not so, only measure of onah which is twelve hours. But now that he said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — separation reason is because of the above error — if so it is obvious it is twelve hours. And if so Rava's word onah is unnecessary — and one can say; and therefore he says: I would think for her husband halakhah like Rabbi Yosi — and one can say; investigate well this explanation for Aviassaf's approach in my humble opinion as a key that flies — and it has proof from the above Gemara challenges. And we wonder at the Shach for his words: what does the Gemara challenge "why two" — behold from Rabbi Yehuda's statement we do not hear that if she has no fixed hour that day, only earlier and later, she must separate also the night before; and therefore Rava needed to inform us she separates onah twelve hours beforehand — and the Gemara's challenge does not apply — it requires investigation. And according to what is written everything is settled with a good conclusion. And know that the author of Magen Avraham erred who wrote Raavan had a different version in the Gemara — namely Raavan copied "how much said Rava onah, and how much onah said Rabbi Yochanan day or night" — and this "how much onah Rabbi Yochanan" is not in our Gemara; and from this he judged Raavan had a different version — and this is not so, for it is not correct that Rabbi Yochanan explains Rava's matter; rather this Rabbi Yochanan statement was stated in the Gemara regarding virgin blood, and it is explained in Gemara Niddah 65 as is known. And since Raavan's opinion is that the onah mentioned here is one measure with the onah mentioned there, he copied in the manner of a posek copying halakhah Rabbi Yochanan's statement there to here for halakhah ruling — not that Rabbi Yochanan's statement is in the Gemara here; and this is common among poskim and simple; Heaven forbid there should be a new version not mentioned by any posek, Rashi, Tosafot — and simple. Nevertheless, although we toiled to resolve Aviassaf's words, the main thing is like Rosh and Rif and anonymously Shulchan Arukh, for so is the opinion of most poskim; and especially since we rule veset derabbanan, and in rabbinic matter to be lenient we listen — and so is the main thing.

שתבדוק הש"ך בס"ק כ"ג הביא דעת הב"ח המחמיר כמאן דצריך בדיקה תיכף כשיעור וסת. ודעת ההיא ס"ל דהא דא"ר זירא דכ"ע וסת דאורייתא אין הכונה לגמרי דאורייתא רק החמירו בו כשל תורה ואליבא יתורץ הא דבעי ר' יוחנן בפ"ק דנדה הי' מעוברת והגיע שעת וסתה ולא בדקה תבעי למ"ד וסת תורה לאו שלא אליבא דהלכתא קמבעי רק ס"ל בהא לענין בדיקה קבעוה כשל תורה וצ"ל דוקא בהא לחוד ס"ל דקבעהו כשל תורה דהא בזה דבעלה מחשב ימי וסתה ובא עליה דנזכר בסעיף י"א תלינן בגמ' ג"כ אי וסת תורה או דרבנן ובהא כלל פוסקים דמותר וצ"ל רק לענין זה קבעוה כשל תורה וטעמו של דבר הואיל ותקנת חכמים היא לבדוק בהגיע וסתה ולחוש לו אם אתה אומר דא"צ בדיקה סמוך לו ממש אתה מבטל תקנת חכמים ומה הועילו חכמים בתקנתן ואחר ישוב כל זה מעיין ישפוט כמה רב מדוחק יש בזה ולא ידעתי מה ראה הב"ח להחמיר כזה דסוף כל סוף כ"ע מודים שהוא דרבנן ואיך נחמור במילתא דרבנן במקום דהרי"ף והרמב"ם אינן מצריכין בדיקה כלל והתוס' ורש"י והרא"ש ורשב"א הכל לא סוברין כדעת הרא"ם מרדכי הלא בטל דעתן נגד הנך רבוותא ולכן אין להחמיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
That she check. Shach seif kaf gimmel brought Bach's opinion who is stringent like one who requires bedikah immediately at veset measure. And that opinion holds: that which Rabbi Zeira said everyone veset deoraita — the intent is not entirely deoraita, only they were stringent with it like Torah; and according to him the challenge of Rabbi Yochanan in first chapter of Niddah: pregnant woman and her veset time arrived and she did not check — it asks for one who holds veset Torah, not according to halakhah it asks, only he holds here regarding bedikah they fixed it like Torah; and one must say specifically here alone he holds they fixed it like Torah — for here her husband counts her veset days and has relations with her mentioned in seif 11 — we depend in Gemara too whether veset Torah or derabbanan; and in this generally poskim permit; and one must say only for this matter they fixed it like Torah. And the reason: since it is Sages' enactment to check when veset arrives and be concerned for it — if you say no bedikah is needed right at it, you nullify Sages' enactment; and what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment? And afterward one returns to investigate how much rabbi is strained in this; and I do not know what Bach saw to be so stringent — after all everyone admits it is derabbanan; and how shall we be stringent in rabbinic matter where Rif and Rambam do not require bedikah at all, and Tosafot, Rashi, Rosh, Rashba — all do not hold like Ra'em Mordekhai? Behold their opinion is nullified against these great ones — and therefore one should not be stringent.

ותטבול דעת זו הוא דעת נ"י דפי' כן בגמ' (א) על לא צרכה אלא סמוך לוסתה דאין פירושו סמוך לוסת נידתה רק סמוך לוסת טבילתה ולפ"ז נראה דאם מזדמן דנמשך זמן טבילתה כ"כ עד סמוך לוסת נידתה אע"פ שבעלה רוצה לצאת לדרך א"צ להמתין על טבילתה דבממ"נ אם אתה תפרש כפי' הנ"י אין כאן היתר ליוצא לדרך לשמש עמה סמוך לוסת נידתה ואם יתפרש כפי רוב פוסקים אין כאן חיוב וענין כלל להמתין על טבילתה ואם יוצא לדרך והיא נדה לשיטה זו ל"ש האשה משתוקקת וכו' ולא התירה חכמז"ל כלל להמתין ולבעול סמוך לוסת' ופשוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
And she immerses. This opinion is Noda Biyhudah's who explained thus in the Gemara on "she needs only near her veset" — it does not mean near niddah veset, only near immersion veset. And accordingly it appears: if it happens her immersion time extends so much until near niddah veset — even though her husband wants to go on a journey, she need not wait for her immersion; for perforce: if you explain according to Noda Biyhudah there is no permission for one going on journey to have relations with her near niddah veset; and if it is explained according to most poskim there is no obligation and general matter to wait for her immersion. And if he goes on journey and she is niddah according to this approach — the woman's longing, etc., and the Sages did not permit at all to wait and have relations near her veset — and it is simple.

שעת וסתה דין זה דיום ל' כיש לה וסת נחשב למדוה מהך דאמרינן על משנה (ב) כל הנשים בחזקת טהרה לבעליהן והוא שבא ומצאה תוך ימי עונתהן ופירש כפירש"י על עונה בינונית שהוא ל' יום אך תינח למ"ד דאין לה וסת לבעלה לא בעי בדיקה אבל למ"ד בעי בדיקה לבעלה וא"כ ע"כ המשנה (ג) מתפרש ביש לה וסת דהא באין לה אינה בחזקת טהרה ובעי בדיקה וא"כ א"א לומר בתוך ימי עונתה היינו עונה בינונית דהא ביש לה וסת אין נ"מ בעונה בינונית כמ"ש בכמה דוכתי ועכצ"ל דהפי' כמ"ש הר"ן והוא שבא בתוך ימי עונתה היינו קודם וסת' הקבוע ולכך מותרת בלי בדיקה כדין אשה שיש לה וסת אבל אם כבר עברה זמן וסתה הרי היא בחזקת טמאה עד שתבדוק וצריך שאלתה פיה וא"כ הך דינא דעונה בינונית לית לי' מקום בש"ס ויתד לתלות בו וא"כ לפי מה דהעלה המחבר לקמן סי' קפ"ו דלהרמב"ם והרא"ש צריכה בדיקה תמיד באין לה וסת א"כ הך דינא דעונה בינונית ליתא ולמה סתם כאן דינא דעונה בילונית וצ"ל דגם דעת המחבר כאומרין דאין לה וסת א"צ בדיק' מדינא רק כמ"ש הרא"ש שלא מלאו לבו להקל נגד שיטת ר"ח א"כ לחומרא חוששין לי' אבל לא להקל כי לדינא קי"ל דהגמ' איירי באין לה וסת וע"ז אמרינן בתוך ימי עונתה לחוש לעונה בינונית כפירש"י ולא סמכינן אר"ח להקל כי אם להחמיר שומעין לחוש להנ"ל ומיני' דלשיטת הרמב"ם וסייעתו דס"ל לדינא כר"ח אין כאן סמך לעונה בינונית כלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
Her veset time. This law of day thirty is like she has veset — considered from that which we say on mishnah: all women are in presumption of purity for their husbands when he comes and finds within their onah days; and he explained like Rashi on intermediate onah which is thirty days. But it works for one who holds a woman without veset her husband does not need bedikah; but for one who holds her husband needs bedikah — if so the mishnah is explained regarding she has veset, for without veset she is not in presumption of purity and needs bedikah; and if so one cannot say "within their onah days" meaning intermediate onah, for with veset intermediate onah has no relevance as written in several places — and it ends: the explanation is as Ran wrote: when he comes within her onah days — meaning before her fixed veset; and therefore she is permitted without bedikah as law of woman who has veset. But if her veset time already passed, behold she is in presumption of impurity until she checks and her mouth must be asked. And if so this intermediate onah law has no place in Talmud and no peg to hang on. And if so according to what the Mechaber brought later siman 186: for Rambam and Rosh she always needs bedikah in woman without veset — if so this intermediate onah law does not exist; and why did he rule anonymously here intermediate onah law? And one must say also the Mechaber's opinion is as they say: without veset she does not need bedikah by law, only as Rosh wrote his heart was not full to be lenient against Rabbenu Chananel's approach — if so stringently we are concerned for her, but not to be lenient; for by law we rule the Gemara deals with woman without veset; and on this we say within her onah days to be concerned for intermediate onah as Rashi explained — and we do not rely on Rabbenu Chananel to be lenient, only to be stringent we listen to be concerned for the above; and from this according to Rambam and his supporters who hold by law like Rabbenu Chananel, there is no support for intermediate onah at all.
