**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If, etc.</b> Certainly, throughout the actual time of her fixed period she is forbidden even according to Rabbi Yose, as stated in the Mishnah there. Rabbi Yehudah comes only to impose an additional stringency. Nevertheless, she is not forbidden for the entire day. This follows from the statement there: 'If she was accustomed,' etc., 'at sunrise,' etc.; Rabbi Yehudah says, 'The entire day is hers.' The phrase 'at sunrise' means immediately at sunrise, and it is impossible that the sun should not rise during the span of sunrise itself. Moreover, the Mishnah teaches the rule without qualification and does not distinguish between a brief flow and a prolonged one. See the two passages there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> The Tur writes this. His basis is the statement there cited by the Rama: 'If,' etc., 'and she did not examine,' etc., which implies that the fixed period passed and she did not examine. The statement there, 'This proves,' etc., 'and not,' etc., means after the time of the fixed period. The further statement, 'The reason is that there is,' etc., 'this proves,' etc., but where the indication comes from her own body, does not establish a Torah prohibition. Thus, even according to the view that fixed periods are rabbinic, this ruling applies specifically when the period passed and she did not examine herself.

<b>And some say, etc.</b> This is the view of the Tosafot cited above, as indicated by the statement there: 'The Sages say: She must examine herself.' The first opinion understands 'she must examine herself' as referring to the actual time of the fixed period. The passage concerning the dispute of Rav and Shmuel, which concludes that she later examined herself, was stated according to Shmuel. Their basis is the statement on 15a of Rabbah bar bar Chanah: 'Even if the time arrived,' etc. This implies that the rule applies even immediately after the fixed period, provided that the onah has passed, unlike Tosafot there, s.v. 'Even.' Rabbi Yochanan, who says, 'She calculates the days of her fixed period,' etc., maintains that fixed periods are a Torah law, and we do not rule in accordance with him. See the Ran in Shevuot.
