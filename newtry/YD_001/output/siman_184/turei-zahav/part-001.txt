<b>בא עליה שלא בשעת וסתה. </b>בטור כתוב דבא עליה כמו שירצה ור"ל בין שהיא ערה בין ישנה פי' שאינה ישנה לגמרי דא"כ אסור לשמש עמה כדאיתא בנדרים אלא שאינה ערה כ"כ שתוכל להשיב אם היא טהורה אי לאו כן כתבו התוספות בנדה דף י"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He had relations with her not at the time of her veset. </b>The Tur states that he may have relations with her as he wishes. This means whether she is awake or asleep. It refers to a case in which she is not completely asleep, for otherwise it is forbidden to have relations with her, as stated in Nedarim. Rather, she is not sufficiently awake to answer whether she is pure or not. So wrote Tosafos in Niddah 12.

היום או אותה הלילה שרגילה לראות הן לפניו הן לאחריו והוה שיעור שוה בכולן ולפני אותה הלילה או היום או לאחריו מותר ותמיהני על הב"י שלא הקשה כן על סברת אביאסף בזה ונראה דלאביאסף עצמו אפשר דאין הכי נמי היו חושבין תמיד לאיסור ששה שעות שלפני השעה שרגילה לראות וששה לאחריו נמצא דברגילה לראות בתחלת היום ה"נ דמותרת בסוף היום אחר ששה שעות אבל אנו אין לנו לומר כן דהלכה פסוקה בידינו שאסורה כל היום אפילו אם הוא יום ארוך. וזה ברור ופשוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One onah. </b>This is the explicit conclusion at the end of the chapter HaIshah. The Beit Yosef cited the Or Zarua, who ruled that if she is accustomed to see during the day, he must separate from her throughout that entire day and the preceding night. The Beit Yosef wrote concerning this that one need not be concerned for it, but should follow what the Tur and the poskim wrote, that the prohibition applies only during the day itself, as the Shulchan Aruch writes here. For the Gemara explicitly rules that the halacha follows R' Yehuda, who holds this way. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, justified this ruling of the Or Zarua by saying that although the halacha follows R' Yehuda, it is nevertheless proper to be stringent in this matter. This is astonishing, for he would be stringent regarding something that the Gemara explicitly permits, especially since vesatos are rabbinic. <br><b>Furthermore, </b>the Beit Yosef cited the Hagahos Maimoniyos, who wrote in the name of Aviassaf that this onah is a day or a night during Nisan and Tishrei, when the day and night are equal; but during Tammuz and Teves, one requires half a day and half a night. He concluded, "This is astonishing"; end quote. The Beit Yosef wrote concerning this: "It appears that his reason is that he understood the measure of an onah to be 12 hours, from the 24 hours comprising the day and night. Nevertheless, it appears that one need not be concerned for this, since the poskim did not mention it. Moreover, the language of the Gemara implies that it is particular only about the day or the night, and the chapter HaIshah mentioned hours only because of R' Yose's dispute there"; end quote. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote concerning this that the author of the Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Beit Yosef overlooked the explicit statement at the beginning of the chapter Tinokes that regarding a niddah as well, an onah is specifically 12 hours. That is, a day or a night during Nisan and Tishrei, and half a day and half a night during Teves and Tammuz; and this is how we rule; end quote. It is difficult for me, for if the matter depends on hours, the prohibition of the onah would automatically apply during the period in which she is accustomed to see, whether during the day or at night. One could then say 12 hours only if she saw during Nisan or Tishrei in the middle of the day or in the middle of the night. But if she sees at the end of the day during Nisan or Tishrei, how would one calculate the 12 hours? If they are calculated from the hour at which she is accustomed to see and onward for 12 hours, she would also be forbidden during the following night, yet permitted before that hour on the very same day. This certainly is not so, for she is forbidden throughout the entire day according to R' Yehuda in the Gemara and according to the poskim. If they are calculated backward for 12 hours, what would one do when she sees at the beginning of the day? According to this, she would be permitted after that hour on the very same day, and Heaven forbid that one say so. Likewise, if you would say that one calculates six hours backward and six hours forward, she would not be forbidden throughout the entire day when she sees at the beginning of the day. Rather, it must be that the matter depends on the day and the night. The Sages established the measure of the prohibition here as either a day or a night, whatever its length may be, whether short or long, both before the hour at which she is accustomed to see and afterward. They applied a uniform measure and called this the measure of an onah, because in most places we reckon the night as 12 hours, and likewise the day. This has no connection to the passage at the beginning of the chapter Tinokes, Niddah 65, which depends only on hours. There the discussion concerns a young girl who saw blood in her father's house and later married, who is permitted to have relations after the first intercourse because we attribute the blood to her virginity, for a day or a night during Nisan and Tishrei, and for half a night and half a day during Teves and Tammuz, according to one answer there. In that case it is impossible to make the matter depend on the actual night, as it does here, because there the permission is based on a fixed period during which the blood after the first intercourse is attributed to virgin blood, and it does not depend on day or night. Thus, if he first had relations with her at the end of the night, she would otherwise receive only a brief period, while one with whom he first had relations at the beginning of the night would be permitted for a long period afterward. Therefore, they said there that the matter depends on 12 hours, and every such virgin is permitted for 12 hours after her first intercourse. This is unlike the present case of seeing niddah blood, which is linked to a time, either the night or the day, according to her veset. The Sages established the measure of the prohibition as requiring him to separate from her throughout that entire day or that entire night during which she is accustomed to see, both before and after the accustomed hour. The measure is therefore equal for all women, and she is permitted before that night or day and after it. I am astonished that the Beit Yosef did not raise this difficulty against Aviassaf's position. It appears possible that Aviassaf himself indeed held that one always calculates six hours before the hour at which she is accustomed to see and six hours afterward. Accordingly, if she is accustomed to see at the beginning of the day, she would indeed be permitted at the end of the day after six hours. We, however, cannot say this, for we have a settled halacha that she is forbidden throughout the entire day, even when it is a long day. This is clear and straightforward.

<b>ולא משאר קריבות. </b>בב"י כתב דאפילו חיבוק ונישוק מותר ולא חיישינן לביאה כיון שביאה איסור דרבנן דלא כת"ה שכתב לאיסור בחיבוק ונישוק וכתב מו"ח ז"ל המחמיר בזה תע"ב ולי נראה שאסור מן הדין לדעת הרא"ש וראיה מסי' שמ"ב כתב הרא"ש לענין אבילות שיש להחמיר בחיבוק ונישוק שהוא מביא לידי חשק והוא הדין נמי הכא וכ"ש הוא דיש לו חשק טפי כשהולך לדרך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But not from other forms of closeness. </b>The Beit Yosef wrote that even embracing and kissing are permitted, and we are not concerned that this may lead to relations, since relations at that time are forbidden only rabbinically. This is unlike the Terumas HaDeshen, who wrote that embracing and kissing are forbidden. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that one who is stringent in this matter is deserving of blessing. It appears to me, however, that according to the Rosh this is forbidden as a matter of law. The proof is from siman 342, where the Rosh wrote regarding mourning that one must be stringent concerning embracing and kissing, because they lead to desire. The same applies here, and all the more so, since his desire is greater when he is departing on a journey.

<b>ולא הביאה סימנים. </b>זהו פי' על מה שאמר אבל בקטנה ור"ל תרי גווני קטנה יש האחת שלא הגיעה לימי הנעורים ועוד יש סימן לקטנות במה שלא הביאה סימנים ואז אף על פי שהגיעו ימי הנעורי' קטנה היא אבל לא הגיעה לנעורים והביאה סימנים עדיין קטנה היא דשומא בעלמא הם ועיין סימן קפ"ט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And she did not manifest the signs. </b>This explains the phrase, "But regarding a minor." That is, there are two forms of minority. One is that she has not reached the years of adolescence. There is also an indication of minority when she has not manifested the physical signs; even if she has reached the years of adolescence, she is still a minor. Conversely, if she has not reached adolescence but has manifested the signs, she is still a minor, because those signs are regarded as a mere mole. See siman 189.

<b>אלא עונה הראשונה כו'. </b>דגם כאן אזלינן בתר התחלת הווסת אלא שמן הראוי היה לאסור כאן כל השלשה ימים כמו בסעיף ה' ביום כשיעור הנמשך בו וכן הוא דעת הראב"ד באמת בטור בשניהם אלא דהבעל הש"ע הסיע כאן להקל אחר שעברה עונה ראשונה וטעמו דגבי דבר מועט שנמשך ביום חשוב מענין הוסת עצמו משא"כ בכאן שא"א שתהיה הכל ראיה אחת אלא תוספת דמים וכיון שנסתלק עיקר הראיה נסתלק ג"כ התוספת כן מתרצים רבים דברי הש"ע אלא דצ"ע מנ"ל לחלק בכך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only the first onah, etc. </b>Here too, we follow the beginning of the veset. Strictly speaking, however, it would have been proper to forbid all three days, just as in seif 5 she is forbidden during the portion of the day for as long as the flow continued. Indeed, this is the Ra'avad's opinion, as cited by the Tur, in both cases. The author of the Shulchan Aruch, however, ruled leniently here after the first onah passed. His reason is that when the flow continues only briefly into the day, that continuation is regarded as part of the veset itself. This is not so here, where it is impossible for the entire three-day flow to be one continuous sighting; rather, it is additional blood. Once the principal sighting has ceased, the additional blood is also deemed to have ceased. Many explain the words of the Shulchan Aruch this way, but it requires further study from where he derived this distinction.

<b>אינה חוששת לו. </b>כ"כ ב"י בשם הרשב"א דאפילו לכתחלה אינה חוששת והוה זו כמו מעוברת דסעיף ז' אלא שהטור כתב בזו שחוששת לוסת אלא ששם עבר היום כו' וכמו שאמר רמ"א אח"כ בשם י"א והקשה ב"י על הטור דהא בגמרא יליף רבי יוחנן דין מעוברת מדין היתה במחבא דאינה צריכה בדיקה והיאך נחלוק ביניהם והאריך בפלפול זה. ומו"ח ז"ל כ' דהיה להטור והרא"ש גירסא אחרת בגמרא ולע"ד נראה הדברים כפשוטן דגבי סילוק דמים משום פחד איכא למיחש שמא באותה שעה שתזקק לבעלה יצא הפחד מלבה וכיון שהוא שעת ווסתה אפשר שאז תטמא כי אורח בזמנו בא ולא אמרו בה שחרדה מסלקת הדמים אלא אם היתה כל שעת הוסת בחרדה ולא הפסיקה ממנה וזה דבר ברור אם היתה בפחד בתחלת שעת הוסת ויצאה מן הפחד בתוך אותו זמן הוסת דאז היא כשאר נשי' דחזר טבעה למקומה הראשון ע"כ לא תשמש עד אחר שעבר כל שעת הוסת משא"כ במעוברת שכבר היא מסולקת דמים ואין חשש לאסור אותה אפילו בשעת הוסת ונמצא שאין דמיון מעוברת להיתה במחבא אלא אחר גמר החרדה דהיינו אחר שיעור הוסת שהוא סילוק דמים בודאי והיינו כל אחת לפי דרכה בזו אחר עבור החרדה ובזו אפילו תיכף וע"כ פשט בגמרא במעוברת לכתחילה מהיתה במחבא כל זמן הוסת שאחר כך אינה צריכה בדיקה וזה נכון וברור ותמיהני על המפרשים שלא הרגישו בדבר זה כי סברא נכונה היא וראוי לקבוע כן הלכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>She need not be concerned for it. </b>So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba, that even l'chatchila she need not be concerned. This case is therefore like that of a pregnant woman in seif 7. The Tur, however, wrote here that she must be concerned for her veset, except that if the day passed, etc., as the Rama subsequently states in the name of some authorities. The Beit Yosef challenged the Tur, for in the Gemara R' Yochanan derives the law of a pregnant woman from the law of a woman who was hiding, who does not require an examination. How, then, can we distinguish between them? He discussed this at length. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the Tur and the Rosh had a different textual version in the Gemara. In my humble opinion, however, the matter is straightforward. When blood withdraws because of fear, there is reason to be concerned that at the time she joins her husband, the fear may leave her heart. Since it is the time of her veset, she may then become impure, because the accustomed time arrives at its proper time. The Sages said that fright causes the blood to withdraw only when she remained frightened throughout the entire time of the veset and the fright did not cease. This is clear: if she was afraid at the beginning of the time of her veset, but the fear left her during that period, she is then like other women, for her natural state has returned to its former condition. Therefore, she may not have relations until the entire period of the veset has passed. This is unlike a pregnant woman, whose blood has already withdrawn and whom there is no reason to forbid even during the time of the veset. Thus, the similarity between a pregnant woman and one who was hiding applies only after the fright is complete, meaning after the period of the veset has passed, when the withdrawal of blood is certain. Each case follows its own circumstances: in the frightened woman, only after the fright has passed; in the pregnant woman, even immediately. Therefore, the Gemara properly derives that a pregnant woman is permitted l'chatchila from the case of one who was hiding, who, after the entire time of the veset has passed, does not require an examination. This is correct and clear. I am astonished that the commentators did not note this point, for it is a sound rationale and it is proper to establish the halacha accordingly.

<b>If she has a veset, etc. </b>However, if she has a veset that she has not yet established three times, and it is shorter than the average interval, such as when she saw from the 25th day to the 25th day or the like, then even if she did not examine herself, she is pure without an examination, since she did not feel a discharge of blood; end quote from the Tur at the end of this siman. The Perishah raised a difficulty, for siman 186 states that a woman who has no veset is forbidden to have relations without an examination. He therefore discussed many resolutions at length. It appears to me that there is no difficulty at all. Certainly, a woman who has no veset, but has already become accustomed to many sightings with no equal interval at all from one to the next, retains a presumption that she may have seen until she examines herself. Here, however, we are discussing a woman who has a veset like other women, but on one occasion changed and saw from the 25th day to the 25th day. This is because she has not established through three sightings that she sees at that time; it is sufficient that she be concerned l'chatchila. This is straightforward.
