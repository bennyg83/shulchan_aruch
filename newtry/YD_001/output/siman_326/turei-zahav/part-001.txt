<b>וכל זה שאין שיעור בכל אחת. </b>פירוש אז אין מצטרפות אפילו על ידי נגיעה או נשיכה אבל אם יש שיעור מפריש אחד על חבירו כו' פירוש ע"י נגיעה ונשיכה ואע"ג דבסוף סי' קכ"ה פסק כיון ששתיהן לפניו מפריש מזו על זו ואין צריכין נשיכה ואפילו צירוף סל שאני הכא דהוי דבר שמקפיד עליו וכן מבואר בת"ה סי' ק"ץ וראיה ברורה מן תלמוד ערוך בשתי עיסות אחת טהורה ואחת טמאה דהוי דבר המקפיד צריך דוקא חוט בצק לחברם ועל כן כתב דהא דמבואר בי"ד דבדבר המקפיד אין מצטרף אפי' על ידי נשיכה היינו אם אין בכל אחד כשיעור חלה אין בידינו להחשיבה כעיסה אחת לחייבה בחלה ורמ"א שכ' כאן מפריש מאחד על חבירו ולא זכר נשיכה היינו דסמך על מ"ש בתחלת הסי' שמיירי בנשכו ומ"מ גרם מכשול לפני בעל הלבוש <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>ובמחלה מכבודו שגג מאד בזה שכ' בד"א שצריך נשיכה כשאין בכל אחד שיעור חלה ורוצה לצרפם אבל אם יש בכל אחד שיעור חלה אע"פ שאחד פת כרכום ובשני אין בו מפריש מאחד על חבירו אם הם של אדם אחד אפילו בלא נשיכה אלא שהם לפניו דהא ממין אחד הם עכ"ל טעה בתרתי דכ' באין שיעור מהני צירוף על ידי נשיכה וביש שיעור לא צריך נשיכה וחשיב שהך יש שיעור הוא כאותו של סוף הסי' שלפני זה ולא היא דכאן מיירי בדבר המקפיד ועל כן בדבר שאין בו שיעור לא מהני נשיכה לצרפה לשיעור חלה וביש שיעור מהני נשיכה שיפרוש מאחד על חבירו ובלי ספק לא עיין בעל הלבוש במקור הדין וזה גרם לו שכתב כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All this is where neither contains the required amount.</b> Meaning that where neither dough contains the challah measure, they do not combine if the owner is particular that they remain distinct, even through contact or adhesion. But where each dough independently contains the challah measure, one may separate from one for the other through contact and adhesion. Although at the end of siman 325 we ruled that where both are before him one may separate from one for the other without adhesion or even basket combination, here is different because he is particular that they remain distinct. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 190, makes this clear from the case of one pure dough and one impure dough, where because the owner is particular they require a strip of dough joining them. Therefore, the statement elsewhere that where one is particular, even adhesion does not combine, concerns two sub-minimum doughs, because we cannot treat them as one dough to create the obligation. But where each already contains the measure, adhesion is sufficient to permit separation from one for the other. Rama here relied on the opening of the siman, which speaks of doughs that touched or adhered. Levush erred badly by saying that adhesion is needed only when each lacks the challah measure, while if each contains the measure no contact is needed provided both are before him. He confused this case with the prior siman and failed to notice that here the owner is particular. In this case, if neither has the measure, adhesion does not create one obligated dough; if each has the measure, adhesion does help for separation. There is no doubt he did not examine the source carefully.

<b>One who makes dough in order to divide it while still dough.</b> Meaning he intends to divide it into portions each smaller than the challah measure. But if he does not intend to divide it until after baking, it is obligated. Beit Yosef writes this, unlike Semag. <br><b>It is written</b> in Responsa R. Binyamin, siman 1, which discusses a custom in some places where a Jewish baker prepares one large dough and women come to buy from him, each taking the challah measure and separating challah from her own purchased portion. He defends the practice because the law depends on intent. Just as intending at the outset to divide the dough into sub-minimum portions exempts it, so where the baker's intent from the beginning is that each regular customer acquire her own challah-sized portion, it is as though each portion had been kneaded separately for her and each must separate challah. My father-in-law rejects this and says he received from his teachers that the practice is absolutely prohibited. If this is a received tradition, we accept it. But by logic I believe the custom can be defended on another basis. Gittin teaches regarding one who lends money to a kohen and then separates terumah on his behalf against the debt that the arrangement works where the lender regularly gives his terumah to that kohen. Since all other kohanim have abandoned expectation and the gift is certain to go to him, it is treated as though it already reached him. The same applies to a baker with regular women customers who purchase their portions every Erev Shabbos. Since the baker intends to sell each regular customer her portion and the transaction is understood on that basis, it is as though he transfers ownership of their shares already at the stage when the dough becomes obligated, before full rolling. Two partners who jointly make a dough with each person's portion independently containing the challah measure cannot have one partner separate for the other's portion against the other's will. Similarly here, the baker's presumed intent is to structure the dough in the legally effective manner for his regular customers. Each customer's share is treated as hers at the time the dough becomes obligated, and she later separates challah with a blessing from her own share. This defense applies only where the women are regular customers and the baker knows from the outset that they will purchase in this manner. With a gentile baker, the concept does not apply because he transfers ownership only after division. At a wedding, where many women take challah-sized pieces from one dough, each separates challah, and they then recombine the pieces into one loaf, this is plainly prohibited, as the responsum itself states.

<b>לא מהני החלוקה אח"כ. </b>נ"ל דאף שיחלוק אח"כ אין צריכא ליטול כי אם חלה אחת כיון דבתר מעיקרא אזלינן כשהיה עיסה אחת אע"פ שיש אחר החלוקה שני שיעור חלות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The later division does not help.</b> It appears that even after dividing the dough, only one challah separation is required. Since we follow the original status when it was one dough, subsequent division does not create separate obligations even if each resulting part itself contains the challah measure.
