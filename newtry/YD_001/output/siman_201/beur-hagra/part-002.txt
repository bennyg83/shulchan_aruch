<b>ויש מקילין כו'. </b>ער"ש י' ד' תניא בתוספתא כו' שפשף או שטבל בחמין טהור וכנ"ל סי' קצ"ה סמ"ה ועתוס' דנדה ס"ו ב' ד"ה אם. ומיהו לפי' ר"ח אין ראיה שפי' כו'. וסברא ראשונה דחקו ופי' ההיא דתוספתא הנ"ל בחמי טבריה והוא דוחק [ולא משמע כן דהא מתחיל שם היו רגליו כו' במקוה כו'] וראייתם מפ"ק דתענית (י"ג א') עתוס' שם ד"ה טבילה כו' אלמא דטעמא משום שאובין הוא וכ"ה בגמ' שם שאובין נינהו אלא שפי' כשאובין נינהו והוא דחוק מאד ואע"ג דמשכחת טבילה בחמין כה"ג מ"מ הוא בדרך רחוקה ולומר דתנא דקאמר כל חייבי טבילות טובלין כו' קמ"ל כה"ג וגם היה רחוק להם בכה"ג אבל כה"ג שכתבו תוס' הוא קל מאד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there are those who are lenient.</b> See Rash, chapter 10, Mishnah 4. The Tosefta states that if one rubbed or immersed in hot water, he is pure, as above in siman 195. See Tosafot, Niddah 66b. According to Rabbenu Chananel's explanation, however, there is no proof. The first opinion was forced to explain that Tosefta as referring to the hot springs of Tiberias, which is difficult and does not fit the context, since the passage begins with feet standing in a mikveh. Their proof is from the first chapter of Taanit 13a. See Tosafot there, who infer that the issue is drawn water. The Gemara likewise says, "Are they not drawn water?" Yet to interpret the passage as merely resembling drawn water is very forced. Although immersion in hot water can theoretically occur in that manner, it is an unusual case, and it is difficult to say that the Tanna's general statement, "all those obligated in immersion immerse," comes merely to teach such a remote circumstance. The case described by Tosafot, by contrast, is much more common.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And afterward... but.</b> This follows the first chapter of Shabbos 14a, concerning one who immersed his head and similar cases. Initially the concern was that people would say, "These waters purify, not those." This does not apply merely to sweating. Others have already disputed this proof, because the decree there was only that such a person becomes rabbinically impure for terumah. His immersion itself remains effective even for terumah, such that if several days pass he does not require a new immersion and sunset. Certainly he remains pure for his wife, since marital relations are ordinary chullin, as stated in chapter 2 of Chullin 31a concerning intention, which is among the additional stringencies Chazal imposed for terumah. See chapter 2 of Chagigah.
