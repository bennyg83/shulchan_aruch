**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of a mikveh and its waters; it contains 75 se’ifim. A woman does not ascend from her tumah by washing in a bathhouse; and even if all the waters of the world rose upon her, she is still in her tumah, and [one is] liable for her to kareis, until she immerses her entire body at one time in the waters of a mikveh or spring that has 40 se’ah. And its measure is: one amah by one amah, by a height of 3 amos, squared, with an amah that is 6 tefachim and a half-finger. And if it is wider and not as high, it is kosher if she can cover her entire body in it at one time. And it must add up in calculation to 44,118 fingers in the thumb-measure and another half-finger (these are my words in Sefer Ba’al HaBayis). And the trench in which the waters are must be larger than this measure, so that when the immersing person enters and the waters rise, 40 se’ah will remain there (Tosafos, perek Arvei Pesachim).

Spring-water purifies even when flowing (meaning: “flowing” — they are drawn and go and are not gathered). Rainwater does not purify except in a gathered pool (meaning: a deep place in which the waters gather, called an “ashboren”) (but through flowing, they are invalid by Torah law (Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari”k, shoresh 115, and Terumas HaDeshen siman 224), if they are alone without a spring). If the flowing waters are from a spring and mix with dripping waters that are rainwater, everything is like a spring for every matter. But if the drippings exceeded the flowing, and likewise if rainwater exceeded the river-water, they do not purify when flowing, only in a gathered pool. Therefore, one needs to place around a “mappatz” (meaning: like a mat) and the like in that mixed river, until the waters gather, and immerse in them. {Rama: And so it is proper to rule and be stringent (Mahari”k and Terumas HaDeshen there; and other later authorities wrote that one should be stringent like the method of Rambam and Rosh and those who support them). And so they practiced in most places where there is no mikveh, and one should not protest those who practice leniency, for they have upon whom to rely (Mahari”k and Terumas HaDeshen there; and Mahari”v siman 70; and Be’er Sheva siman 154). Nevertheless, one should be careful not to immerse in a river that is formed entirely by rainwater, and when there is no rain it stops entirely — even though other rivers pour into it during the rains and it is formed through them — nevertheless, since it stops entirely when there is no rain, it is forbidden to immerse in it while it is flowing until the waters in it gather (there in Mahari”k). But a river that does not stop, even though during the rains it widens and spreads over all its banks, it is permitted to immerse in it in every place (Beit Yosef according to Mordechai and Ashiri and Tosafos), according to the reasoning of the lenient ones and according to the practice.}

A vessel is considered significant to disqualify the water within it due to drawing only if it is fit to receive [water] before one sets it in place, and it becomes filled intentionally; then it disqualifies — whether it is a large vessel that holds 40 se’ah in liquid (which are 2 kor in dry), or whether it is a very small vessel, even vessels of dung, vessels of stone, or vessels of earth.

**** ENGLISH ****
If one placed a board under the pipe near the mikveh so that the waters fall from it into the mikveh: if it has 4 rims, so that it is fit to receive water, it disqualifies; and if not, it does not disqualify. And this is when the waters would have been fit to come to the mikveh without it; but if the waters would not have been fit to come to the mikveh without it, even if he stood it on its side or turned it over on its mouth, it disqualifies.

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="83"></i>צינור שאין לו ד' שפות אינו חשוב כלי וראוי להביא על ידו מים למקוה <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="77"></i>ואם חקק בו גומא א' קטנה קודם שקבעו <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="44"></i>אם היא של <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="55"></i>עץ אפילו אין הגומא מחזקת אלא כל שהוא נעשה כולה על ידה כלי וכל המים שעוברין עליו חשובין שאובים ואם הוא של חרש <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="84"></i>אין החקיקה פוסלתו אלא אם כן היא מחזקת רביעית ואם נפלו צרורות או עפר בגומא <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="85"></i>אינו חשוב סתימה לבטלו מתורת קבלה אלא אם כן יהיו מהודקים לתוכה. <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="78"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="86"></i>סילון שהוא צר מכאן ומכאן <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="79"></i>ורחב באמצע אינו חשוב קבלה לפסול בו המקוה: <small>הגה ולכן מותר לעשות מקוואות ע"י <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="23"></i>צנורות וסלונות של עץ שמביאין מים מן הנהר או שאר מעיין אל המקוה (מרדכי דקדושין בשם הר"ר שמריה ור"ן ה"נ ותשובת רא"ש וכן הוא דעת הטור ורמב"ם) או צינורות של הגגות (מהרי"ק שורש נ"ד) ולא חיישינן שמא נעשה בהן גומא מכח לחלוחית ומקבלים <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="68"></i>דלא מיקרו קבלה אף אם היה בהן גומא <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="24"></i>הואיל ולא עשאו אדם בכונה (שם טעמא דהר"ש) <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="80"></i>ואם המים באים אל הצינור על ידי כלים הקבועים בגלגל והם נקובים בדרך שלא מקרי כלי מותר לטבול בהן אם יש מ' סאה במקוה (מרדכי הלכות נדה בשם רוקח) אבל אם אין בה מ' סאה אין לטבול שם <i data-commentator="Pithei Teshuva" data-order="25"></i>דלא מקרי <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="56"></i>חבור לנהר ע"י זה ועי' בא"ח סי' קנ"ט (ב"י ס"ס זה ולא כרוקח):</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
A pipe that does not have 4 rims is not considered a vessel, and it is fit to bring by it water to the mikveh. If one carved into it one small hollow before setting it in place: if it is of wood, even if the hollow holds only the slightest amount, all of it becomes, through it, a vessel, and all the water that passes over it is considered drawn. If it is of earthenware, the carving disqualifies it only if it holds a revi’is. If pebbles or dirt fell into the hollow, it is not considered a sealing to nullify it from the law of receiving unless they are pressed tightly into it. A gutter that is narrow on both ends and wide in the middle is not considered “receiving” to disqualify the mikveh with it. {Rama: Therefore, it is permitted to make mikva’os through wooden pipes and wooden gutters that bring water from the river or another spring to the mikveh (Mordechai in Kiddushin in the name of R’ Shemaryah; and Ran; and a responsum of Rosh; and so is the opinion of Tur and Rambam), or roof pipes (Mahari”k, shoresh 54). We are not concerned that perhaps a hollow was formed in them due to dampness and they receive [water], for they are not called “receiving” even if there was a hollow in them, since a person did not make it intentionally (there, the reason of Rashi). And if the waters come to the pipe by means of vessels fixed in a wheel, and they are perforated in a way that is not called a vessel, it is permitted to immerse in them if there are 40 se’ah in the mikveh (Mordechai, Hilchos Niddah, in the name of Rokeach). But if there are not 40 se’ah in it, one does not immerse there, for this is not called a connection to the river through this (and see Orach Chayyim siman 159) (Beit Yosef at the end of this siman; and not like Rokeach).}

**** ENGLISH ****
This that they said that anything not made to receive water does not disqualify the mikveh — they said only when the waters fell from within it into the mikveh on their own; but if a person put them into the mikveh, it disqualifies, for anything done through a person — even if he sprinkled with his hands or with his feet, and even if he passed through water and they were splashed by his feet on their own into the mikveh — they disqualified it.
