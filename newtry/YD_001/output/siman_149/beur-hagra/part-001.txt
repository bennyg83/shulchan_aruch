<b>יריד כו' והעובד כוכבים כו'. </b>בפי' ה"ר יונה והרא"ש שם ס"ט וה"ד יונה ז"ל כתב לא אסור כו' וכן פר"י בתוס' שם ד"ה עטלוזא כו' וכ"כ הראב"ד בהשגות ושכן הוא בתוספתא בהדיא: <br><b>(ליקוט) שעושין בה' יריד. </b>עמש"ש וכ"כ ברא"ש סי"א מדקאמר בגמ' דלא חשו לדמי עבודת כוכבים ומ"ש משאר יום אידן דלא אסור אלא משום אזיל ומודה או לפני עור כו' אלא יריד לעבודת כוכבים כו' וכ"ה בירושלמי דגרסינן שם עיר שיש בה עבודת כוכבים תוכה אסור משום דיש בה צלם א' תוכה אסור ר"ל אמר ביריד שנו כו' ע"ש (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
Fair, etc., and the gentile, etc. In the explanation of R. Yona and the Rosh there 69; and R. Yona wrote: it is not forbidden, etc.; and so R. Yitzchak in Tosafot there on "Atalutza, etc."; and so the Raavad in hasagot; and so it is explicitly in the Tosefta. (Collected) That they make on the fifth day a fair. As we said; and so in the Rosh 11 ; from what the Gemara says they were not concerned about idolatry money; and what is different from other festival days that are forbidden only because of "he goes and acknowledges" or lifnei iver, etc. ; rather a fair for idolatry, etc.; and so in the Jerusalem Talmud, for we read there: a city that has idolatry within it ; within it is forbidden because it has an image; within it is forbidden ; meaning: it was taught regarding a fair ; see there (end).

**** ENGLISH ****
(Collected) Permitted, etc. And so to borrow and lend; and as we said; and the Rif and poskim omitted all the give-and-take there in the question of Atalutza, etc., because we hold like the first version; and further because Rava and Rabba bar Ulla each add on the previous to be lenient ; Rava says because of idolatry money it is unnecessary, for idolatry money is permitted; and so that which we say there 33a "rather gentiles, etc." ; to establish even Abaye; but we hold like Rava and the Ran; and Rabba bar Ulla says that Rava's statement is obvious, for if the gentile were dragging with it he would not stand on his festival day outside it ; unlike Rashi who explains everything stringently; and see there in the Ran at length (end). (Collected) Permitted to go outside, etc. The language of the Rambam ; and it is wondrous, for the mishnah deals with borrowing and lending, and afterwards it says "what about going there" ; except the Rambam explains the latter regarding passing through there, as he explains onwards "one who goes, etc."; and so Rashi there ; end of passage; and so in the Gemara there the Sages were not concerned, etc.; and so the Raavad challenged from the Tosefta ; see there in hasagot (end).
