<b>דיני יריד מעובדי כוכבים. ובו ה' סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="1"></i>עיר שעושים בה <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="1"></i>יריד ביום חגם והעובדי כוכבים מתקבצים מכמה מקומות והולכים שם לכבוד עבודת כוכבים <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="2"></i>מותר לילך חוצה לה <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="1"></i>ואסור <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="1"></i>ליכנס בתוכה היתה חוצה לה מותר ליכנס בתוכה ההולך ממקום למקום <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="2"></i>אסור לעבור בה <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="1"></i>אם הדרך מיוחד לה <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="2"></i><i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="3"></i><small>(דהיינו <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="3"></i>שאין עוברים מאותה עיר לעיר אחרת):</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
A law of the fair of non-Jews; it contains 5 se’ifim. A city in which they hold a fair on the day of their festival, and non-Jews gather from several places and go there in honor of idolatry: it is permitted to walk outside of it, but it is forbidden to enter into it. If he was outside of it, it is permitted to enter into it. One who goes from place to place is forbidden to pass through it if the road is designated for it (meaning, that one does not pass from that city to another city).

**** ENGLISH ****
In what case is this said? Regarding a guest; but a resident of the city is permitted, and if he goes with a caravan, it is permitted. {Rama: A courtyard of idolatry: there are those who say that its law is like a city of idolatry (Tur in the name of Rashba). And at a time when non-Jews do not gather there for their “terufos,” it is permitted to enter there (Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh). And some say that in every case it is forbidden if there is no road passing through it to another place; but when there is a road passing through it to another place, according to everyone it is permitted, and the common practice is to go by way of that courtyard to another place (Rashba). Nevertheless, it is a trait of piety to distance oneself from going there if he has another route as short as it (the words of the Rema, and so it implies in Sefer Chasidim); and see above siman 142.}

**** ENGLISH ****
One may go to the fair of non-Jews and buy from them animals, slaves and maidservants, houses, fields, and vineyards, and he may write and file [the documents] in their courts. In what case is this said? When he buys from a homeowner who does not pay customs; but one who buys there from a merchant is forbidden, because he pays customs (and even if they waive the customs for him, it is forbidden) (Tur). And the customs goes to idolatry. If he transgressed and bought an animal, he cuts off its hooves from the knee and below. If he bought garments and vessels, they should rot. If he bought money or metal vessels, he should take them to the Dead Sea. If he bought a slave, we do not raise him up and we do not lower him.

<i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="13"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="6"></i>אם המכס הוא לכהנים ואוכלים ושותים אותו ואין קונים ממנו לא תקרובת אלילים ולא נוייה מותר. <small>הגה <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="4"></i><i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="8"></i>וסתם יריד בזמן הזה מותר דסתמא לאו לעבודת כוכבים הוא. <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="14"></i>במקום שדרך עובדי כוכבים המוכרים לומר <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="5"></i><i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="10"></i>ועוד פשוט לאלוה מותר לקנות ממנו דאפשר שיתננו <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="9"></i>לעניי עובדי כוכבים (טור בשם ב"ה) אבל אם מפרש בהדיא לעבודת כוכבים שלו <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="11"></i>או <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="10"></i>שידוע שהוא לעבודת כוכבים או שאומר לקדש פלוני אסור לקנות ממנו (שם וכן פשוט בתוס' ובהגמי"י):</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
If the customs goes to the priests and they eat and drink it, and they do not buy with it either offerings of elilim or ornaments, it is permitted. {Rama: And a typical fair in our times is permitted, for presumably it is not for idolatry. And in a place where it is the practice of the non-Jews selling to say, “and even more plainly, [give] to the god,” it is permitted to buy from him, for it is possible that he will give it to the poor among the non-Jews (Tur in the name of Baal HaItur). But if he explicitly explains that it is for his idolatry, or it is known that it is for idolatry, or he says “to sanctify so-and-so,” it is forbidden to buy from him (ibid., and so is simple in Tosafos and Hagahot Maimoni).}
