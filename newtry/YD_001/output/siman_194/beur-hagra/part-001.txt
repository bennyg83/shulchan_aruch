**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nowadays, etc.</b> See Tosafot, 36a, s.v. 'a week,' etc., and the other authorities, based on what is stated there, 66a, 'why specifically,' etc. It is impossible for the womb to open without blood, as stated below. Although labor blood is pure when a fully formed child is born, they were concerned that she might see even a small amount of blood during the easing of labor, and therefore treated every woman who gives birth as one who gave birth while in a state of zivah.<br><b>(Addendum) Nowadays, etc.</b> The Mordechai, siman 737, quotes a responsum of the Geonim: You asked about a woman who gave birth through difficult labor and not during an easing of labor, and who saw blood, whether she requires seven clean days. This matter was disputed by the Geonim. Mar Rav Yehudah Gaon ruled that she does not require seven clean days, in accordance with the Gemara in Niddah concerning the daughters of the Cutheans, and his disciples ruled likewise. Mar Rav Tzemach Gaon and all the members of the academy disagreed with him, saying that nowadays we are not expert in distinguishing between easing and difficult labor, and the matter involves a possible liability of karet. Furthermore, Rav Zeira said that the daughters of Israel accepted stringency upon themselves. Since Rav Zeira stated this without qualification, it may be said that women who give birth also accepted this stringency. Rav Yehudah bar Yakar likewise ruled that the statement was unqualified and therefore includes a woman who gives birth. The Rif also applies Rav Zeira's enactment even during the days of blood of purity. See there. (End of addendum.)
