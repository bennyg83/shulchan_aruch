**** ENGLISH ****And wine that congealed, etc. And it is written in Hagahot Ashiri from Or Zarua chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah that wine that congealed has no status of the touch of non-Jews, etc.; and likewise if a non-Jew touched it after it dissolved, there is status of the touch of non-Jews — end of his words; and Darkhei Moshe brings it. **** ENGLISH ****First and second time forbidden. For the grape-pulp absorbs from the water and wine comes out from them; therefore there is always status of the touch of non-Jews until it is inferior that people do not drink it — end of his words; Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. And Maharshal wrote in his hagahot to the Tur: explanation — always, even if he did not find its full measure, only a tiny amount; but nevertheless it deals specifically with the first and second time — examine carefully — end of his words. And in Perishah seif kaf 27 he challenged him, for the wording "always" does not imply this; also nearby the Tur wrote (and the author in seif 11) in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah: grape-skins of non-Jews are always forbidden, and he explains there that in every case it deals, even if soaked many times — end of his words. And to me it is like Maharshal's words, for otherwise he should not have said "first and second time forbidden," since it is always forbidden. And further, behold in Rabbeinu Yonah's words that the Rosh brought, the wording "always" is not mentioned; and from what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote regarding grape-skins, on the contrary from there is proof, for there he explained and wrote "always forbidden, even if soaked many times"; and here he did not explain except on the contrary he wrote "first and second time forbidden" — it implies first and second time specifically. And this appears to be the author's view, who wrote "first and second time forbidden" and omitted the word "always"; and so it appears explicitly in Turei Zahav: first and second time specifically forbidden and no more. Nevertheless it appears this is specifically when he found its full measure; and Maharshal deals with such a case. But if he found more than its full measure, even after the first and second time it is forbidden, for behold there is wine in it. And it is astonishing, for in Derishah itself seif kaf 23 he wrote that after one and two times Rabbeinu Yonah holds like the Raavad, who holds that there is no status of the touch of a non-Jew unless he put three measures of water and found four; if so, perforce what he wrote regarding grape-skins "always forbidden" means specifically there, for there he explicitly explains thus, which is not so here; and as I explained.

And further, that he wrote nearby "a non-Jew who treaded the wine and did not touch it," etc. — "this is forbidden for drinking" — it implies even though he treaded with his feet it is not forbidden in benefit. And one should not reject that all these deal when he did not touch the grapes in the winepress, such as when there are boards on the grapes and he treads on them; for if so he should not have closed the matter — end of his words. And it is astonishing, for here he copied the Rambam's wording, and below siman 124 seif 11 he ruled that libation by foot is called libation to forbid in benefit; and it is possible that specifically from the Rambam he deduced, because he should not have closed the matter; but the author here explained below that libation by foot is called libation; if so, perforce here it deals when he treads on boards — this is the author's view in my opinion. But in truth it appears to me there is no proof from the Rambam's words, for behold the Rosh and Tur siman 124 hold that libation by foot is called libation, and nevertheless hold that if he treaded with his feet it is permitted in benefit because he is occupied with treading; also what he brought in Kesef Mishnah there and in Beit Yosef below siman 124 — Rashba's responsum, who wrote: from the Rambam's wording in chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods, "How is touch that the non-Jew forbids in it — that he touched the wine itself, whether by his hand or by other limbs accustomed to pour libations" — it implies he holds touch by foot does not forbid in benefit — end of his words; this is not conclusive, for it is possible he holds like the Rosh's and Tur's view and their support, that the way to pour libation is by foot (I again found in Be'er HaGolah that he retracted and wrote that perhaps the Rambam also holds libation by foot is called libation). And see below siman 124 seif kaf 18 and seif kaf 53; and see there seif kaf 71: I wrote that wherever it is forbidden by law for drinking, for us nowadays it is permitted even for drinking in a place of loss, since non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters.
