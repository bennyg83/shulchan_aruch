Stepping. The Shach wrote that treading is forbidden only in a winepress that is corked and full; but his touch in it does not forbid, and in [a press that is] covered it forbids only for drinking. The Shach challenged himself: here it implies that foot-libation is not called libation to forbid in benefit, for he wrote that perhaps he touched with his hand and libated — and if so, derive it from the fact that he touches it with his foot when treading. And below siman 124 seif 11 he ruled that foot-libation is called libation to forbid in benefit; perforce it must be that here we deal with one who treads on boards, or that here it is permitted in benefit because he is occupied with treading. The Taz distinguishes: here it is different because a Jew treads with him. And it is explained in siman 125 regarding a supporting Jew — even by hand there is a leniency. The Shach concludes that wherever it is forbidden by law for drinking, for us nowadays it is permitted even for drinking in a case of loss, since non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters. The Taz wrote that for halachah one should rule that in every vessel that has a spigot — even a large spigot — one should be careful about a non-Jew treading there, since there is some drawing that slightly moves the spigot and clear wine comes out, and this is common as I have heard; but in a vessel that has no spigot at all there is no prohibition even with a non-Jew treading. And so I practiced in the region of Mehrin in Galilee when I was there at harvest time — it was never heard that they turn the grapes to one side in the vat to the brim to make clear wine in the vat; therefore there is no decree lest [drawing follow] drawing. Mahariik wrote that nevertheless there is no prohibition of a non-Jew touching grapes in a vat even though it is forbidden to tread according to Rabbenu Tam; and it appears this is only in a closed press, but in an open press it is obviously forbidden by touch even in grapes. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag that whenever there is a basket in the press and clear wine enters there, it is called drawing and is forbidden even if a non-Jew touched the grapes — so far his words.
