**** ENGLISH ****
[And since he intended to draw it off. See the responsum of Chasam Sofer siman 117 regarding kosher wine made among the winepresses in countries where gentiles are common: a person went innocently and drew wine into his jug from a vat full of trodden grapes to take to the press; he made a wine-filter with his fingers between them so his hand would be like a strainer to strain pits from the wine flowing into the jug—and nevertheless some pits and skins remained in the clear wine in the jug. The winemakers paid no attention to this act and made the wine through a gentile as usual; that vat became mixed with several vats and they were put into the press. The wine is now mixed—whether there is concern of gentile contact in our wine. He elaborates: at first glance it is plainly forbidden per the Shulchan Aruch, and "since he intended to draw it off" implies everything depends on the drawer's intent; if he intended to draw off, even if he did not succeed properly, nevertheless through his intent the entire press becomes fit for libation. So too explicitly in Sheltei HaGibborim 4th year—that if he intended, it is considered drawing even if some skins and pits rose. However, since the loss to the merchants/winemakers was great, they permitted the wine without hesitation through combining several leniencies that apply here (see end).]

See in Derishah, who wrote for one explanation that "full" is not precise, because even according to Rashi drawing is not called on the winepress until he turns aside with his hands the grape-skins and pulp to one side. And see in responsum Zikaron Yosef siman 6, who wrote that he relied on this, and did not protest against the people of his country who practiced that non-Jews brought grapes in baskets and poured them on the closed winepress that has a slope, and the non-Jews touched here and there with their hands even though the winepress is not full and wine is drawn on the slope toward the hole — because according to Derishah's words there was no prohibition here, and for them it is like a time of pressure. And he further wrote: nevertheless I saw their wickedness, that after the first treading they swept with a broom all the grapes and grape-skins scattered in the winepress onto the press-board; and they again arranged, etc.; and since he turned aside the grape-skins to the side of the press-board, behold clear wine was gathered on the side of its slope and this is drawing on the winepress; and nevertheless they were not careful about the touch of non-Jews in grapes to put them on the press-board, and therefore the wine was forbidden. And nevertheless there is reason to side with permitting, because even according to the commentators who follow Rashi's view, that through drawing a little wine the status of wine applies to the whole winepress — this is specifically on wine mixed with grapes and grape-skins on them, not on the clusters and grape-skins and pulp and wine stored in them, for those have no status of wine at all; and that everything was forbidden was because there is much wine in the winepress and we are concerned lest there are not sixty in grape-skins and pulp and wine stored in them against what was forbidden of the clear wine (it appears also cold-in-cold applies, and then rinsing suffices; one may say that presumably there are leaves, and the Rosh according to his view who holds in Chullin 112 regarding "if there are leaves in it, all of it is forbidden" — even in a living creature — and Shach brought this above siman 91 seif kaf 21; but there is a difficulty, for behold also Ran's view is so, that everything was forbidden, as explained in Beit Yosef, and he wrote that also Rashba's view inclines thus; and they do not hold so there — and one should investigate this) — and this is specifically in the first treading; but if there are not certainly sixty, nevertheless since stam wine is from their words, one may say it is similar to what spilled in the second treading, where only a little wine remained that certainly has sixty against it; and even in siman 98, since no person in the world can know. There are further branches to permit — see there, where he expanded on this.
