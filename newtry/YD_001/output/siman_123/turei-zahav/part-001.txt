<b>משום גזרת י"נ כו'. </b>פי' משום בנותיהם אסרוהו בשתייה ומשום גזירת י"נ אסרוהו אף בהנאה ולפי טעם זה יש היתר עכשיו כדמסיק דהיינו כיון שאין י"נ גמור שכיח בינינו בטל הטעם והותרה הגזירה והכי ס"ל לרשב"ם שמביא הטור וכעין זה אמרינן גבי גילוי לעיל סימן קט"ז אבל לפי מ"ש הטור ברישא הטעם כיון שי"נ גמור אסור בהנאה עשו סתם יינם כאלו ודאי נתנסך ולא משום גזירה ממילא גם עכשיו אין היתר הנאה דדבר שנאסר במנין צריך מנין אחר להתירו דאין שייך לומר בזה בטל הטעם ע"כ שפיר כתב הטור אבל רשב"ם כו' דתמ' ב"י על ל' אבל ולפי מ"ש ניחא כן כתב ד"מ ולי נראה דבמ"ש הטור ומחמירו לאסור כו' כוון בזה דאף שאין שייך בזה גזירה כלל דמשום בנותיהן פשיטא לא גזרו בזה ואפילו משום י"נ הוא גזירה רחוקה ביין שלנו נמצא דמצד גזירה דרבנן אין כאן איסור אלא מצד החומרא ואם כן אף לדידן אסור מצד החומרא אבל רשב"ם כו' דס"ל דמצד גזירה הוא האיסור וא"כ השתא דלא שייך גזירה מותר וכתב ב"י דעכשיו שהעובד כוכבי' מקריבו קמיה עבודת כוכבים ולא שפכיה כדרך ניסוך פנים לא מיקרי רק תקרובת עבודת כוכבים ולא נסך ומיהו הנהו יונים דלא שתו חמרא עד שהכומר בא וזורק עליהם מים שלהם עם היין היה נראה דהוי ניסוך אלא י"ל דזה לא הוה רק תערובות מים אסורים וה"ל מים ביין דבטל כדאיתא לקמן ע"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
Because of the decree of yayin nesech, etc. Explanation: because of their daughters they forbade it for drinking, and because of the decree of yayin nesech they forbade it also in benefit; and according to this reason there is permission now, as he concludes, namely since complete yayin nesech is not common among us, the reason is nullified and the decree is permitted; and so holds Rashbam, whom the Tur brings; and similarly we say regarding uncovered wine above siman 116. But according to what the Tur wrote at the beginning, the reason is: since complete yayin nesech is forbidden in benefit, they made stam yeinam as though it was certainly poured as a libation, and not because of a decree; therefore also now there is no permission in benefit, for a matter forbidden by a count requires another count to permit it, one cannot say here "the reason is nullified"; therefore the Tur properly wrote "but Rashbam," etc. And Beit Yosef wondered at the word "but"; and according to what I wrote it works well, as Darkhei Moshe wrote; and to me it appears that in what the Tur wrote "and he is stringent to forbid," etc., he intended with this that even though no decree applies here at all, for because of their daughters it is obvious they did not decree on this, and even because of yayin nesech it is a distant decree on our wine, it is found that regarding a rabbinic decree there is no prohibition here, only from the side of the stringency; and if so, for us also it is forbidden from the side of the stringency. But Rashbam, etc., holds that from the side of decree is the prohibition; and if so, now that no decree applies, it is permitted. And Beit Yosef wrote: now that non-Jews offer it before avodah zarah and do not pour it in the manner of libation before the face, it is called only an offering to avodah zarah and not a libation; nevertheless those Greeks who did not drink wine until the priest came and threw their water on them with the wine, it would appear this is libation; but one may say this was only a mixture of forbidden water, and it is water in wine that is nullified, as is found below, end of his words.

<b>אינו אסור בהנאה. </b>דעובדי כוכבים בזמן הזה אין רגילין לנסך לעבודת כוכבים והויין לזה כתינוק עובד כוכבי' שעושה יי"נ רק לשתייה זהו דעת היש מקילין שמביא רמ"א בסמוך אלא שקצת אחרונים החמירו שאין היתר הנאה בסתם יינם רק לענין הפסד ובטור כתב וגם אם נגע עובד כוכבים כו' לכאורה קשה לשון וגם דמאי רבותיה ונ"ל דס"ד דכאן אין לו הפסד שיוכל לכוף את העובד כוכבים שישלם לו הזיקו ע"כ לא הקילו למכור אותו לאחרים קמ"ל דמותר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is not forbidden for benefit. </b>For the non-Jews of the present time are not accustomed to pour wine as a libation to idolatry, and in this regard they are like a non-Jewish child, who renders wine forbidden only for drinking. This is the view of the lenient authorities cited by the Rama below. However, some later authorities were stringent that stam yeinam is permitted for benefit only in a case of financial loss. The Tur wrote: “And even if a non-Jew touched,” etc. At first glance, the word “even” is difficult, for what additional teaching is there? It appears to me that one might have thought that here he suffers no loss, since he can compel the non-Jew to pay for the damage he caused, and therefore they did not permit him to sell it to others. He therefore teaches us that it is permitted.

<b>יין מבושל. </b>באשר"י כתוב וב"י מביאו תמוה הוא כיון שגזרו משום בנותיהן וכי משום שהרתיחו לא שייך הך גזירה ואי משום דלאו בר ניסוך הוא והלא יין מזוג נמי לאו בר ניסוך הוא ואפשר כיון שהמבושל אינו מצוי כל כך מילתא דלא שכיחא לא גזרו ביה עכ"ל וקשה מ"ש ואי משום דלאו בר ניסוך הוא מה מהני זה כיון דחיישינן משום בנותיהן ונ"ל דה"ק דאף משום בנותיהן לא גזרו ביין אלא במקום שיהיה אסור אף בהנאה דומיא די"נ ואין שייך לגזור איסור הנאה אלא בראוי לניסוך אבל באין ראוי לניסוך שרי לגמרי דזה עדיף טפי משנגזור בשתיה לחוד שיבא מכשול להתיר גם י"נ בהנאה וא"כ א"ש הא דשרי במבושל כיון דאין לגזור בו לגמרי על זה פריך מיין מזוג דאע"ג דאין ראוי לניסוך אסרוהו לגמרי הכי נמי ביין מבושל ותירץ משום דמבושל לא שכיח על כן לא גזרו ביה כלל וקשה למה לא כתב הטור כן אלא כתב משום דאין ראוי וכו' ונ"ל דהטור לא רצה לכתוב טעם לא מצוי דגם הרא"ש לא כתבו אלא דרך אפשר ע"כ כתב טעם זה שהוא אליבא דהראב"ד דס"ל אף במזוג ראוי לנסך כמ"ש ב"י בתמד לפי שהוא משובח לשתיה (לא גזרו אלא במקום שאפשר) נמצא דלדידיה שפיר אמרינן סברא זו שכתבתי דלא גזרו אלא במקום שאפשר לגזור אפילו בהנאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
Cooked wine. In Hagahot Ashiri it is written, and Beit Yosef brings it: it is astonishing, since they decreed because of their daughters, how does the fact that it was boiled apply to this decree? And if because it is not fit for libation, behold mixed wine also is not fit for libation; and it is possible that since cooked wine is not so common, an uncommon matter, they did not decree on it, end of his words. And there is a difficulty in what he said: and if because it is not fit for libation, what does this help, since we are concerned because of their daughters? And it appears this is what he said: even because of their daughters they did not decree on wine except where it would be forbidden also in benefit, similar to yayin nesech; and one does not decree prohibition of benefit except on what is fit for libation; but where it is not fit for libation it is entirely permitted, this is better than when they decreed only for drinking alone, lest a stumbling block come to permit also yayin nesech in benefit. If so, there is no reason that it is permitted in cooked wine, since they do not decree on it entirely; on this he challenges from mixed wine, that even though it is not fit for libation they forbade it entirely; so too in cooked wine. And he answers: because cooked is not common, therefore they did not decree on it at all. And there is a difficulty: why did the Tur not write thus, but wrote "because it is not fit," etc.? And it appears the Tur did not want to write the reason "not common," for also the Rosh did not write it except as a possibility, therefore he wrote this reason, which is according to the Raavad, who holds that even in mixed wine it is fit for libation, as Beit Yosef wrote regarding temed, because it is improved for drinking (they decreed only where it is possible); it is found that according to him we properly say this reasoning that I wrote, that they decreed only where it is possible to decree even in benefit.

<b>אין בו משום יי"נ. </b>כתב ב"י דמשמע מדברי הפוסקים דאפילו בשתיה מותר והוקשה לו ממ"ש רש"י מותרת בהנאה גבי אלונתית משמע דבשתייה אסור ודחק ליישב פרש"י להסכימו גם כן להיתר שתייה והקשה עליו בדרישה ממ"ש רש"י בפרק א"מ סוף דף כ"ט דאם בישלו ישראל מותר בהנאה ושם לא שייך תירוץ ב"י) וכ' מו"ח ז"ל דשם חיישינן שמא עירב בו עובד כוכבים יין כיון שהוא בביתו ובאמת יפה כתב בעל הדרישה דאי משום חשש עירוב יין היה אסור אף בהנאה ומשנה מפורשת היא שם המורייס אסור בהנאה ופירש רש"י משום תערובת יין וגבי חומץ שם במתניתין כתב נמי רש"י דאילו חומץ ישראל שלקחו עובד כוכבי' לא מתסר בהנאה משמע דבשתיה אסור אלא פשוט דלרש"י כל מה שבא מחמת יין אע"פ שאח"כ הלך טעם היין מחמת שנעשה חומץ או איזה תערובות לא מהני לשתייה אם נגע בו עובד כוכבים אבל אנו קיי"ל כרוב הפוסקי' דמותר אפילו בשתייה:
**** ENGLISH ****
It does not have the status of yayin nesech. Beit Yosef wrote that it appears from the words of the poskim that even for drinking it is permitted; and he was challenged from what Rashi wrote "permitted in benefit" regarding aluntit, it appears that for drinking it is forbidden; and he strained to reconcile Rashi's explanation also to agree to permission of drinking; and Chokhmat Shlomo challenged him in Derishah from what Rashi wrote in chapter Elu Metziot end of daf 29: if a Jew cooked it, it is permitted in benefit, and there the explanation of Beit Yosef does not apply. And Chokhmat Shlomo wrote: there we are concerned lest the non-Jew mixed wine into it since it is in his house; and in truth Be'er Heitev properly wrote that if because of concern for mixing wine it would be forbidden also in benefit; and the explicit mishnah there is: morayis is forbidden in benefit, and Rashi explained because of a mixture of wine; and regarding vinegar there in the mishnah Rashi also wrote: if vinegar of a Jew that a non-Jew took, it is not forbidden in benefit, it implies for drinking it is forbidden. Rather plainly for Rashi everything that comes because of wine, even though afterward the taste of wine went away because it became vinegar or some mixtures, does not help for drinking if a non-Jew touched it; but we establish like most poskim that it is permitted even for drinking.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The first and second steeping are forbidden even, etc. </b>The Tur writes: “It always remains subject to the law of a non-Jew’s contact until it becomes so inferior that people do not ordinarily drink it.” End quote. Maharshal explains that “always” means even when only a very small amount of wine was found, but nevertheless this refers specifically to the first and second steepings. End quote. It appears to me, however, that this is what he means: It always remains subject to the law of a non-Jew’s contact, even if its quality as a beverage is poor, until it becomes so extremely inferior that people do not drink it at all. The Prishah explains that this applies even if it was steeped many times, but this is imprecise.

A winepress that is closed, etc. This is from the Tur's words; and to understand the matters we will quote the Rosh's words in perek R' Yishmael, Rav Huna said: I only said this when he did not return the press-boards, etc.; even though Rav Huna said that once it begins to be drawn it becomes wine, and if so even without returning the press-boards the winepress in which one treads is forbidden, Rav Huna here spoke to the matter of the first mishnah and informed us that even though the winepress has no status of wine to forbid by the touch of a non-Jew, nevertheless it is forbidden in mixtures; and there is a difference for the latter mishnah in a closed and full winepress. The explanation of his words appears in the first answer: he thought that if the winepress is open, even though grape-skins go from it with the wine, the beginning of drawing is called drawing since afterward in that dripping it goes to the basket and there wine alone is drawn, he considered also what is in the winepress as this; and so is the implication of Rashi's words in the Gemara (daf 55): "likewise in a closed winepress," etc., "and the wine descended and it is drawn immediately", the word "immediately" indicates this that I mentioned. It is found that they decreed winepress lest winepress; and this is reasonable to decree, as it appears to me.

לדרוך לר"ת דגם בדריכה מבואר בתוס' דהקדמונים לא חשו להך חומרא דר"ת עיין שם. ונראה דה"ה בגת סתומה אבל בגת פתוחה פשיטא שאסור במגע אפילו בענבים וכתב ב"י בשם סמ"ג דכל שיש סל בגת ונכנס שם יין צלול נקרא המשכה ואסור אפי' אם יגע עובד כוכבים בענבים:
**** ENGLISH ****
One may not tread with the non-Jew in the winepress. These are Rambam's words, chapter 11 of Forbidden Foods; and it deals with a winepress that is not closed and there is drawing there; and on this the reason he wrote "lest he touch," etc., applies. But in a closed and full winepress, where drawing is not relevant, it does not forbid even if he touched with his hands; but the Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that even in a closed winepress one may not tread with the non-Jew, since in any drawing whatsoever it becomes yayin nesech, we decree lest [one come to act] after drawing; and when treading in a vat where drawing is not relevant, etc., the reason is: in a vat there is no slope in its floor, and therefore even though it is lacking there is no drawing in the empty place because of moving grapes from the force of treading; and all that is squeezed by treading is stopped among the grapes since there is no slope, and there is no drawing here. But if there is a small spigot there from which clear wine comes out, certainly there is drawing there; but not if it is large, for then grapes also come out there; and likewise if he turns all the grapes to one place in the vat and wine from the side, this is properly drawing. But Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Tam was stringent also in a vat in the treading of non-Jews; and so it appears in Hagahot Ashiri, from the reason that in any case since there is relevance of drawing in some manner we are concerned for it lest there be drawing. But in a vat that has no hole with a spigot at all, it is certainly permitted with the non-Jew's treading, for there is no relevance of drawing at all; one should not say lest he turn aside the grapes until the edges of the vessels and wine will be drawn there clear, for what purpose would he do so? We do not concern ourselves lest this come about, and it is an uncommon matter; and likewise it is not common that he takes out a little clear wine, as Beit Yosef wrote, all this is not common. And further, Beit Yosef wrote that according to Terumat HaDeshen he does not forbid what he takes out a little clear wine, only what is in the cup is forbidden; and even though we establish stringently, as he wrote in seif 18, nevertheless for a doubt we are not stringent on this. And nevertheless there is a slight inconsistency in Rama's words, who wrote "or in a vat", namely even while there is still a spigot inside it; and this is better than a closed winepress, namely from the reason of decree lest after drawing, as we mentioned. And these words of the author are in an open winepress specifically, since he concludes "lest he touch," etc., as we proved. And regarding practical halachah one should rule as we mentioned: in every vessel that has a spigot, even if the spigot is large, one should be careful about the non-Jew's treading there; and even though Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Tosafot to permit with a large spigot like the mouth of a barrel, nevertheless from Mordechai's words it appears to be stringent also in this, as Rama wrote here, namely since there is some drawing there, the spigot moves slightly and clear wine comes out, and this is a common occurrence as I have heard. But in a vessel that has no spigot at all, there is no prohibition there even with the non-Jew's treading, as it appears to me; and so I practiced in the region of Mehrin in Galilee when I was there at harvest time: they did not at all turn the grapes to one side in the vat to the edges to make clear wine in the vat; therefore there is no decree lest after drawing. And Maharik wrote that nevertheless there is no prohibition of the touch of a non-Jew in grapes in a vat, even though treading is forbidden according to Rabbeinu Tam, for also in treading it is explained in Tosafot that the ancients did not heed this stringency of Rabbeinu Tam, see there. And it appears the same is in a closed winepress; but in an open winepress it is obvious that touch is forbidden even in grapes. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag that whenever there is a basket in the winepress and clear wine enters there, it is called drawing and is forbidden even if the non-Jew touched the grapes.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden for drinking. </b>This is because only the force of the non-Jew is involved, <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="5"></i>for he did not touch the wine even with his foot. These are the words of the Rambam. I am astonished by what the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 124 in the name of the Rashba, that according to the Rambam, contact with the foot does not forbid the wine for benefit, although it does forbid it for drinking. Why, then, did the Rambam write here that he did not touch it? Even had he touched it with his foot, it would have been forbidden only for drinking. One can answer that the Rambam teaches a greater novelty: even when he did not touch it at all, it is nevertheless forbidden for drinking. Indeed, the same law would apply had he touched it with his foot. Here the discussion concerns fully developed wine, part of which has already been drawn away. If none of it was drawn away, there is no prohibition except due to Rabbeinu Tam’s stringency discussed above. The words of the Tur are to the same effect, for he writes: “Even if he did not touch it, but merely trod the grapes, the wine in the pit is forbidden.” End quote. This means that if he did not touch the wine at all, even with his foot, all agree that it is permitted for benefit but forbidden for drinking. If he touched it with his foot while treading, he is considered preoccupied with his work, as explained in siman 124, and the law is likewise the same. If he touched it with his foot other than while treading, there is a dispute in siman 124: the Rashba permits benefit, while the Rosh forbids even benefit. The Drishah raises a difficulty in siman 124 from that ruling against what the Tur writes here, but there is no difficulty. <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="6"></i>What is difficult, however, is this: If the Tur means here that even without contact, the wine in the pit is forbidden because of the non-Jew’s force, since the non-Jew is preoccupied with his work this should be considered force exerted without intent, which is permitted even for drinking, as written at the end of siman 125. See siman 124, seif 19.
