(ט"ז סק"ג) דאע"ג דאין ראוי לניסוך אסרוהו לגמרי. אינו מובן לי דמנ"ל להרא"ש ז"ל דלמא דרך עובד כוכבים לנסך יין מזוג וכעין שהקשה הרא"ש על הרמב"ם בנתן בו מעט שאור. ואף לדעת רבינו יונה דגם לענין מגע עובד כוכבים בתמד תליא ברמא תלתא ואתי ד' היינו דהיכא דלא הוי יין לענין בפה"ג לא נכנס כלל תוך גזירת חז"ל דלא גזרו רק על היין. ובפרט כיון דעיקר הגזירה היה משום בנותיהן. וזה לא גזרו רק על היין אבל מ"מ י"ל דדרך עובד כוכבים לנסך יין מזוג. ובעיקר קושית הרא"ש לא זכיתי להבין דמנ"ל להרא"ש דמגע עובדי כוכבים היה משום בנותיהן. הא בפשוטו במגע ל"ש בנותיהן רק כיון דאסרו סתם יינם בהנאה דהוי כאלו נתנסך מש"ה החמירו ג"כ במגען מחששא דניסוך ואדרבה הלא הרא"ש לשיטתו דס"ל דמגע גר תושב מותר בשתייה ויינו אסור בשתיה. ע"כ דמגע ליכא משום בנותיהן והכי משמע ג"כ מרברי הרא"ש שכ' על דברי רשב"ם בעובדי כוכבים שלנו וטעמים הללו מספיקים ביין שלנו שנגע בו עובד כוכבים אבל סתם יינן שאסרו משום בנותיהן וכו' הרי דבמגע ל"ש בנותיהן ובפרט למה שצדד הרא"ש אח"כ די"ל דמתחלה לא אסרו סתם יינם אלא בשתייה אלא דאח"כ שהיו רגילין לנסך אסרו בהנאה בודאי י"ל דבכללא דגזירה א' לא היה בכלל איסור במגען. רק אח"כ כשהורגלו לנסך אסרו גם מגען וא"כ ביין מבושל שלנו דמיירי רק מנגיעת עובד כוכבים לכאורה אין מקום כ"כ לקושית הרא"ש וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****(Turei Zahav seif kaf 3) That even though it is not fit for libation they forbade it entirely. I do not understand: whence did the Rosh know that perhaps the non-Jew's way is to pour a libation of mixed wine — similar to what the Rosh challenged against the Rambam regarding putting a little leaven into it. And even according to Rabbeinu Yonah, who also regarding the touch of a non-Jew in temed depends on "he poured three and four came" — namely where it is not wine for purposes of birkat ha-gomel it does not enter at all within the decree of the Sages, for they decreed only on wine. And especially since the main decree was because of their daughters; and they did not decree on this except on wine. Nevertheless one may say that the non-Jew's way is to pour a libation of mixed wine. And mainly I did not succeed in understanding the Rosh's difficulty: whence did the Rosh know that the touch of non-Jews was because of their daughters? Behold plainly regarding touch their daughters are not mentioned — only since they forbade stam yeinam in benefit, which is as though it was poured as a libation, therefore they were also stringent regarding their touch from concern for libation. And on the contrary, the Rosh according to his view, who holds that the touch of a ger toshav is permitted for drinking and his wine is forbidden for drinking — therefore regarding touch their daughters are not relevant; and so it also appears from the Rosh's words, who wrote on Rashbam's words regarding our non-Jews: "these reasons suffice for our wine that a non-Jew touched; but their stam wine that they forbade because of their daughters," etc. — behold that regarding touch their daughters are not mentioned; and especially why the Rosh afterward suggested that one may say: initially they did not forbid stam yeinam except for drinking, but afterward when they were accustomed to pour libations they forbade it in benefit — one may say that within one general decree there was no prohibition in their touch; only afterward when they were accustomed to pour libations they also forbade their touch. If so, regarding our cooked wine, which deals only with the touch of a non-Jew, there is not so much place for the Rosh's difficulty. And investigate.

**** ENGLISH ****(Shach seif kaf 18) Rather he means more than three and a half. I am astonished, for we say on this in the sugya: Rava, according to his reasoning — "all wine that does not carry," etc. — behold here it depends only on the measure of mixing; and so it appears explicitly from Ran's words brought in Beit Yosef, who first wrote that some compare mixed wine to actual temed, which requires a quarter-log of wine; and he contradicts this, for there it is mere foam, but in mixed wine it depends on taste; and some hold it is nullified in six; and likewise explicitly in Rashba. And behold explicitly: if one compares mixed wine to temed, six are not required; for in temed specifically a quarter-log of wine is required, and so explicitly in Rashbam. And what Shach brought as proof from Rashba regarding "stopped in a wheel," etc. — there is no proof, for there it explicitly deals with grape-skins of non-Jews, and likewise grape-skins of non-Jews here, which are already forbidden and are worse. And know that Rashba wrote in that case of "stopped": according to those who forbid in mixed wine up to sixty, here also sixty are required; and behold in temed it is explicit that he poured three and three and a half came — it is permitted. Therefore here it is worse since it is already forbidden. And see in Rashba and it is clear *):

(ש"ך ס"ק מ"ג) אבל באמת נלפע"ד. מהטור אין ראיה דכיון דס' מדדו ביד מותר בהנאה. ה"נ ברגל אבל להרמב"ם דס' מדדו ביד א"ב וכמו דמסיק הב"י א"כ לכאורה אף בדרך ברגל ליתסר בהנאה. אולם כיון דהרמב"ם לא הזכיר דינא דרגל ע"כ אי נימא דס' שמיה ניסוך ע"כ דכללו בלישנא בין בידו בין בשאר אברים דדרכו לנסך ורגל בכלל וכמ"ש הש"ך עצמו א"כ משמע דדמי ליד ולא מהני טרדא:
**** ENGLISH ****(Shach seif kaf 43) But in truth it appears to me. From the Tur there is no proof, for since measuring by hand is permitted in benefit, so too by foot; but according to the Rambam, measuring by hand is rabbinically [forbidden], and as Beit Yosef concludes — if so, it appears that even by treading with the foot it should be forbidden in benefit. However, since the Rambam did not mention the law of the foot, if so we must say that measuring is called libation; therefore he included it in the wording whether by his hand or by other limbs that are accustomed to pour libations, and the foot is included — and as Shach himself wrote; if so, it appears it is like the hand and being occupied does not help.

**** ENGLISH ****(In Be'er Heitev) Because he is occupied with treading. This is according to his view (siman 114 seif kaf 50) that he disagrees with Beit Yosef, and his view is like the Rambam that measuring by hand is permitted in benefit. And I have a difficulty: if the Rambam holds that measuring by hand is permitted in benefit, and a fortiori this regarding what the Rambam omitted — measuring by hand — for he relied on the case of oil, as Shach wrote there; if so, the Rambam must hold that unintentional wine and unintentional touch are equivalent; if so, it is impossible to say that treading is forbidden because of his force, for behold his force unintentionally regarding wine is permitted for drinking, as is explicit in Noda B'Yehuda. And investigate.
