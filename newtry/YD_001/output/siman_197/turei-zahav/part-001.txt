**** ENGLISH ****
And she is permitted to immerse on Friday night. In Tur he brought the view of Rashi Chananel, who holds that tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah; and he brings proof from the Yerushalmi, which says: a woman who has a veset for her husband — her husband calculates the days of her veset and comes upon her; she said that it is forbidden for a woman to stand in her tumah end quote. And Me'iri wrote that there is no proof from this, for one may say that therefore it is forbidden for her to stand in her tumah, lest her husband come upon her. And it appears to me to answer that when we say "her husband calculates the days of her veset and comes upon her" — they did not say this specifically in a case where it is clear to the woman when her husband will come; rather, in every manner, even if she thought he would not come at that time. And if so, it is proven that there is a prohibition for a woman to stand in her tumah — not because of her husband, but because of tevilah at its proper time being a mitzvah. And upon this the husband relies and comes upon her — she certainly acted according to law. And per this it would be permitted to immerse on Shabbat; but Rashi Chananel holds that the halachah is that tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah from the immersion itself, but from the side of being fruitful and multiplying; and therefore Rama ruled specifically when her husband is in the city, as will be written shortly.

After childbirth, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen siman 255 they asked about a yoledet who immersed after eighty days for a female, or within that time — and the case was that she could have immersed seven or eight days earlier from the side of cleanliness, but of her own will she waits, because she does not yet have much strength — whether she may immerse on Shabbat, even though it is possible for her to immerse beforehand, or not. And he answered that Mordechai wrote that they do not permit immersing on Shabbat except because of tevilah at its proper time being a mitzvah; and this case was not at her proper time, since it was possible for her beforehand. And afterward he wrote: if it was not possible for her beforehand, for example because of oness — one may say that even according to Rashi, who ruled that tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, nevertheless it is permitted to immerse because of the mitzvah of fulfilling onah; and even when it is not the time of onah it is considered a mitzvah, for a man gladdens his wife in relations, as stated in chapter Elu Devarim in Pesachim, etc. And he concluded there: women practice to be careful not to immerse on Shabbat unless her husband is in the city and it was not possible for her beforehand, for in such a case it was an immersion of mitzvah because of onah. And even though in chapter 2 of Beitzah we say that every tamei person immerses on Shabbat because it appears like miykar — nowadays is different, for the practice is not to heat oneself on Shabbat in any water; if so, it does not appear like miykar end quote — his words did not permit except when she had no obligation to immerse before Shabbat, for example because of oness, and her husband was with her on Friday evening; and he does not reduce this except when he is not in the city at the time of her immersion. But it is simple that this does not help if he did not come until Friday evening and she had an opportunity to immerse beforehand, for it does not help her that her husband was not in the city on a weekday — nevertheless she should have immersed on a weekday. And in Agur and Mahari"v they brought Darkei Moshe, and Me'iri was more stringent — that even if she was forced and could not immerse before Shabbat, and her husband comes on Friday evening, she should not immerse on Shabbat; and their proof is from Mordechai mentioned above. And Beit Yosef disagrees with them and concludes to be lenient, even when it was possible beforehand, because of the reason that it appears like miykar; and the words of Terumas HaDeshen above he did not bring at all. And Darkei Moshe wrote: since they practiced to immerse on Shabbat, this implies that we do not hold like Mordechai — if so, it should be permitted even if she could have immersed beforehand; nevertheless it appears to me to reconcile the custom and to say that whoever could not immerse beforehand — even by a stringency that women themselves practiced — is called tevilah at its proper time, and she immerses on Shabbat; but in a place where it was possible, no. And nevertheless I do not find to be stringent in this in a place where they did not practice to be stringent end quote. And so he ruled here in Shulchan Aruch that the matter depends on custom. And his words are astonishing, since according to Mordechai, and Agur in the name of the great ones, and Mahari"v, and Mahari"l — they forbid immersing on Shabbat entirely; and Terumas HaDeshen permits only when it was not possible at first, but when it was possible it is forbidden, even if her husband comes on Friday evening. Why did Rama be lenient here in a place where there is no custom, if her husband comes on Friday evening, even if it was possible — and similarly after childbirth — and he is against all these great ones? Also Me'iri disagrees with Rama on this and is stringent in every place; only that he wrote that according to the words of Terumas HaDeshen above it is implied that specifically when her husband is in the city and it was possible beforehand she is forbidden on Shabbat; but when her husband is not in the city and he comes on Friday evening, even though it was possible beforehand, she is permitted on Shabbat — because women do not immerse without their husband being in the city. And this is not with precision; certainly Terumas HaDeshen did not intend to permit this, but his intent is that we require her husband to be in the city at the time of her immersion; and there is no distinction whether he came on Friday evening or earlier — nevertheless we require that it be in a situation where it was not possible at first to immerse. With this we should rule according to the view of Terumas HaDeshen, whose words are intermediate between those who are completely stringent and those who are completely lenient — namely, that whoever can immerse before Shabbat without any oness and did not immerse is forbidden to immerse on Shabbat, even if her husband came on Friday evening; and whoever could not because of some oness, like that case of Terumas HaDeshen that we mentioned, should be permitted to immerse on Shabbat in every place.

<b>אין מרחיקין החפיפה מן הטבילה. </b>פירוש שאנו חוששין לפרש"י שמביא הטור סימן קצ"ט שצריכה לחוף ביום קודם טבילתה ולא בלילה וכאן אי אפשר כיון שהוא שבת א"כ צריכה לחוף בערב שבת ויהיה הרחקה בין החפיפה לטבילה אע"פ שפסק לקמן דבדיעבד סגי שתחוף היטב בלילה היינו בחל טבילתה דוקא במ"ש מה שאין כן כאן שפשעה מה שהמתינה עד מוצאי שבת:
**** ENGLISH ****
One does not distance the chafifah from the immersion. Explanation: for we are concerned to interpret Rashi that the Tur brings in siman 199 — that she needs to comb on the day before her immersion, and not at night. And here it is impossible, since it is Shabbat — if so, she needs to comb on Friday evening, and there will be a distance between the chafifah and the immersion; even though he ruled below bediavad it suffices that she combs well at night — namely, when her immersion falls specifically on Motzaei Shabbat — unlike here, where she sinned in what she delayed until Motzaei Shabbat.

<b>אסורה לטבול ביום כו'. </b>פי' דדין תורה הוא שנדה שטובלת ביום ז' שלה אע"פ שלא היו נקיות טובלת דוקא בלילה דבדידה תליא דוקא בימים אבל זבה שסופרת ז' נקיים בספירה אמרינן מקצת היום ככולו כיון שספרה מקצת יום השביעי בנקיות טובלת אפילו ביום וילפינן לה מקרא דואחר תטהר אחר מעשה תטהר אלא שחכמים אסרו לטבול ביום שמא תשמש בעוד יום ואח"כ בו ביום תראה ותסתור למפרע נמצא שבא עליה באיסור על כן כתב הטור הנשים שלנו אע"פ שהם ספק זבות כלומר שהם סופרות ז"נ וא"כ לעולם הוה לכל הפחות ח' ימים עם יום הראיה והיה לה היתר לטבול ביום כיון שכבר כלו ז' ימים אפ"ה לא תטבול ביום ובזה מבואר דברי הש"ע בכאן והטעם משום סרך בתה פי' שבתה תראה שטבלה אמה ביום ח' ותסבור שביום ז' טבלה ותעשה כן גם היא:
**** ENGLISH ****
She is forbidden to immerse by day, etc. Explanation: the law of the Torah is that a niddah who immerses on her seventh day — even if there were no clean days — immerses specifically at night; regarding her, it depends specifically on days. But a zavah who counts seven clean days in her counting — we say "part of the day is like the whole day," since she counted part of the seventh day in cleanliness, she immerses even by day; and we derive this from the verse, "and afterward she shall be pure" — after the act she shall be pure. Only the Sages forbade immersing by day, lest she have relations while it is still day, and afterward on that same day see and annul retroactively — and it be found that he came upon her in prohibition. Therefore the Tur wrote: our women, even though they are doubt zavot — meaning that they count seven clean days — and if so there would always be at least eight days with the day of sight, and she would have permission to immerse by day, since the seven days have already been completed — nevertheless she should not immerse by day. And with this the words of Shulchan Aruch here are explained; and the reason is because of sarach batah — meaning, her daughter will see that her mother immersed on day eight, and will think that on day seven she immersed, and she too will do so.
