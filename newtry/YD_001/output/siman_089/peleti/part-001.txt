אם יש בשר בין שיני' בטור משמע לרמב"ם דנתן טעם להמתנתו' שעות משום בשר בין שינים משמע אח"כ חשיב לי' כמעוכל והקש' הפר"ח הא בגמ' שאל בשר בין שינים מהו והשיב לי' בשר בין שניהם וכו' ע"ש ונראה דמזה ראיי' וסמך לו' שעות דהא שיעור בין סעוד' לסעוד' ו' שעות והטעם והוא דבתוך כך אין תואב לאכול אבל כשיעברו ו' שעות תואב לאכול כמבואר בא"ח שיעור עיכול וזהו שאלת הגמ' בין שיני' מהו עד כמה הזמן להמתין שיהי' כמעוכל והשיב לי' מקרא דכתיב הבשר עודנו בין שניהם וכו' ובתהלים נאמר לא זרו עוד מתאותם עוד אכלם בפיהם ש"מ דעדיין לא נתעכלה וכ"כ במדרש טרם נתעכל' האכיל' והוא כמבואר בא"ח שאין תאב לאכול הרי דהך שיעור דבש' בין שיני' הוא שיעור טרם עיכול האכיל' דאל"כ איך קאמר הקר' סתם שהבשר בין שניהם דמשמע זמן מאוחר הא הי' תיכף קודם עיכול וע"כ צ"ל דזה וזה אחת היא דכל זמן העיכול קרוי בשר ואח"כ לא וא"ש וברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
If meat remains between his teeth: Tur implies that according to Rambam, the reason for waiting six hours is because of meat remaining between the teeth. After that interval, it is considered digested. Pri Chadash asks that the Gemara itself asks what the law is regarding meat between the teeth and answers from the verse, "while the meat was still between their teeth." In my view, this very passage provides support for the six-hour interval. The ordinary interval between one meal and the next is six hours, and the reason is that during that time a person is not yet hungry to eat again, whereas after six hours he again desires food, as explained in Orach Chayyim regarding the measure of digestion. This was the Gemara's question: how long does meat between the teeth retain its status before it is considered digested? The answer is from the verse, "while the meat was still between their teeth." Tehillim likewise says, "They had not yet turned from their craving; their food was still in their mouths." This shows that the food had not yet been digested, and Midrash likewise says it was before the food had been digested. Thus the period during which meat between the teeth is still considered meat is the same period before digestion is complete. Once digestion is complete, it no longer has that status. This is clear.

**** ENGLISH ****
After eating meat one hour — Peri Chadash's view to give reason for this custom from Zohar that says do not eat one time — this is not so, for language of one time means one period and is not measured in hours of the world but a time — and from afternoon until evening is also called one time; but it appears this is whether to wait one hour or six hours they disagree about digestion — see Orach Chaim siman 184 Magen Avraham s.k. 9: beginning of digestion is about an hour and a fifth and end of digestion six hours; the lenient counted this as measure of beginning of digestion for Birkat HaMazon so no longer need Birkat HaMazon — if so also for this it is already digested and gone and therefore permitted to eat — and almost their reasons are sensible; the stringent follow end of digestion which is six hours as Magen Avraham wrote there — therefore six hours waiting is required — and this is clear. And here we learn we require full six hours and not like Peri Chadash to say in short winter days one eats at noon and can eat cheese at evening — for that is between meal and meal, for per my words we require measure of digestion.
