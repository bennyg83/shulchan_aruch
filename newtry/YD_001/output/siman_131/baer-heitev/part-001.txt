**** ENGLISH ****The matter. Even with gentile wine in the possession of a gentile who lives there, and the Jew does not live there, and the whole city is gentile — because havla'ah ve-chatumah is like a guard; and it implies that key and seal count as havla'ah ve-chatumah, which is like a guard; and Bach challenged the Rav, that he should have written that for us it is permitted even for drinking; and this is not a valid challenge — for in many places the Rav wrote thus, and that is according to the law of the Gemara; and there is also practical difference for us: where there is no loss, it is forbidden in benefit — as above siman 124 — nevertheless here it is permitted; and further it seems that even nowadays the Rav did not wish to be lenient for drinking here — since if it were known that he touched, it would be forbidden in benefit by Gemara law; nevertheless in great loss one may perhaps permit for drinking. And know: in this entire siman, and likewise in every siman 129 and 130, we are concerned lest a gentile touched it — that is only where there is reason to fear lest he touched to drink, or to exchange, or for some other benefit; or that he touched through inadvertence; but otherwise we do not fear that he touched to libate, and it is permitted for drinking in a place of loss — for gentiles nowadays are not idolaters — as above siman 128 seif 4 in Hagah; and the Rav did not emend thus because he relied on himself above — end Shach. And Taz wrote that one should not be lenient in this at all for drinking — only regarding buying it initially to profit from it, it is permitted.
