**** ENGLISH ****
A law of kosher wine that was produced under the authority of a non-Jew; it contains 2 se’ifim. If a Jew produced the wine of a non-Jew in purity in order that it be permitted for Jews — whether he sold it to a Jew and the Jew did not yet pay him money, or whether he did not sell it to him but rather purifies it in order to sell it through him to Jews — if the Jew placed it in the courtyard of that same non-Jew himself, and the Jew lives in the courtyard, the wine is permitted even without a seal, even if the non-Jew also lives there. And if the Jew does not live in that courtyard: if a Jew lives in that city and the house where the wine is is open to the reshus harabim, it is permitted, if the Jew has a key and a seal (and after the fact, even with 1 seal, as was explained above in siman 130). But if the house is not open to the reshus harabim — even though a Jew lives in the city — or if it is in a city that is entirely non-Jews, even if the house is open to the reshus harabim, it is forbidden, even with a seal within a seal, until a Jew sits and guards; and one who goes out and comes in is like one who sits and guards. In what case is this said? When the wine is in the house of the non-Jew who owns it; but if he placed it in the house of another non-Jew, it is permitted with a key and a seal, provided that the other non-Jew is not subordinate under the hand of the non-Jew who owns the wine, for if he is subordinate to him in such a manner that if he comes to touch the wine he is not able to object to him, it is as if it is in the authority of the owner of the wine. And if Jewish peddlers are accustomed to come to the city, and it does not have a wall, doors, and a bolt in such a manner that they can come there at any time they wish, it is considered like Jews live there. And if the house is open to a dump, or there is in the courtyard opposite the entrance of the house a window open to the reshus harabim, or a Jew has a palm tree opposite the entrance of the house, it is considered like it is open to the reshus harabim (and it has already been explained at the end of siman 130 that an open window is effective only by day, and not at night) (Ra’avad, and Hagahot Ashiri in the name of Maharai’ch, at the end of perek R. Yishmael, and Tosafos there). And if the non-Jew wrote to the Jew, “I have received,” then if, when the Jew comes to remove it, he does not prevent him, it is considered like his wine and it is permitted with a key and a seal (and after the fact, even with 1 seal), even in a city that is entirely non-Jews. But if, when the Jew comes to remove it, he does not allow him until he gives him his money, its law is as if he did not write, “I have received.” {Rama: And there is one who says that with a seal within a seal it is permitted in every case (Tur in the name of Baal HaTerumos), and one may rely on him to permit it in benefit but not for drinking (Ran in a responsum).}

Non-Jews who buy wine from a Jew and seal it with a seal within a seal, and transport it for several days on a ship by themselves, it is permitted. {Rama: If a Jew and a non-Jew purified the wine of 1 non-Jew in partnership, and the key is in the hand of the Jew: if the wine has 1 additional seal upon it, it is a seal within a seal, as was explained above in siman 130, and we are not concerned for the partnership of the non-Jew (a responsum of Rashba; and see Beit Yosef at the end of siman 130).}
