**** ENGLISH ****[And he did not pay him. See Bach in the name of Shach; and see responsum Chavot Yair siman 126: a Jew who purified gentile wine and again bargained with him according to the market price on market day, and in between gave him money for that wine; and because it is the custom of the government there that from every barrel of kosher wine he must give him tax, and he wished to evade that tax — therefore he did not seal the barrels or the cellar, so the official would not notice when he comes to the cellar; and the rabbi who was asked ruled: it is obvious that giving partial money is like acquisition — as Shach wrote — and one seal suffices; and since one seal suffices, a key also suffices — as Taz agreed siman 118, and most Acharonim, unlike Shach. And also obvious: even though this acquisition was before the bargain, nevertheless since he bargained at the exit of the market it was a complete bargain — as proven from chametz of Zultspach in Bava Metzia 73; and further that in their law it was complete acquisition; except here there is room to doubt — since he has no document on his money, and the gentile can deny him; and further, since the Jew trembles before the gentile lest he inform on him before the official that he was caught on his wine as a thief — he too will say he purified wine and evaded the tax. And he z"l answered him: that which he left, that giving partial money is like acquisition — still lacks a condition in the matter — Shach analyzed, etc.; but if he paid partial in a matter where the Jew has a share in the wine; and he wanted in this that he gave him partial — it does not help if the gentile can remove him with money; rather if he must give him wine according to the measure of his money — the Jew has partnership in the wine. And that which he said the market bargain was complete acquisition from chametz of Zultspach — his words are astonishing; there it does not deal with acquisition law, etc.; but that which he said that in their law the law is so — certainly we follow their law specifically, and not Jewish law at all — neither to be stringent nor lenient; for here the reason is not acquisition law as in sale of chametz and firstborn and permitting an animal to rest — here everything depends on trembling; and since the city judge will judge him if he transgresses their law — he trembles; what difference to me whether the Jew acquired by law or not. And that which he said the Jew has no document on his money — it seems to me this does not harm — nevertheless he trembles to be brazen to deny; and we do not require a document in such a case; and proof from Ran's responsum in Beit Yosef this siman. And that which he said the gentile does not tremble because the Jew fears him to inform on him — it is a great reasoning to forbid; and this is like a mute gentile — above end of siman 128; but if the Jew can excuse himself before the judge and say he never purified this wine, only bought it for commerce — this gentile already trembles; and therefore in the case asked, if in their law the gentile is obligated to give the Jew wine according to his money and he is caught as a thief; and if the Jew can excuse himself before the official that he did not evade the tax — the gentile trembles — one may permit the wine in great loss specifically; and if one condition is lacking one may not permit even in great loss; and one who is stringent — good for him — at this time]:
