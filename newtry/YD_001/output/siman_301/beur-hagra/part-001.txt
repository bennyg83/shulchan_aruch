**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he has etc.</b> In the Yerushalmi there: this that you say is when he intends it as a garment etc., and the Rash and the Rosh brought it there and there, and the Rosh explained there and there in this manner.
<b>(Likkut) If he has etc.</b> Tur. And it is difficult to reconcile, for if he intends, it is anyway forbidden, like hand-towels etc., and taking a hot egg with a cloth, and many similar cases, even if it is not made in the form of a garment; and if he does not intend, it is always permitted, as they said: sellers of clothing etc. And this is the language of the Rosh there: Yerushalmi — when he intends for wearing, but if he does not intend, no. There are places whose towels have a head-covering and he wears it as a kind of garment etc. And this is an explanation of the Yerushalmi: how can one intend with a towel as a garment? And this requires great further study. But the shielding of the hair upon his garments is not considered a benefit, for only the shielding of his body from sun and rains is considered a benefit. And the words of the Shach at the end of subparagraph 5 are also not correct, and likewise the words of the Taz subparagraph 5; and he challenged the Prisha incorrectly. And the Shach's difficulty from taking out dung — one can say that anything that is sometimes done for benefit is always forbidden, as they said regarding hand-towels and books and a sponge, and as he wrote in seif 9 in the gloss. And even the first Tanna only disagrees because it is at rare times. And that is what he wrote: he shall not put the saddle-cloth etc., even etc., implying that the beginning is not because of taking out dung but for benefit; and he said: even though underneath it is permitted even for benefit, on top it is forbidden; and not only that, but even to take out etc. (end of his words).
