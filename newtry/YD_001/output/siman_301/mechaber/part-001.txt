**** ENGLISH ****
Whether it is permitted to spread kilayim beneath oneself, and the law of a pack-saddle and shrouds. In it are 15 seifim. It is permitted by Torah to sit on bedding of kilayim, as it says, "it shall not ascend upon you"—but you may spread it beneath you. {Rama: And likewise it is permitted to raise them upon oneself not in the manner of warming, such as to spread a tent of kilayim over oneself (Tur); and likewise to spread a garment of kilayim over a vat in which one bathes in order to retain the heat (Mordechai, first chapter of Beitzah, and Hagahos Ashiri there)—for it is forbidden only in the manner of wearing or raising them upon oneself in the manner of warming.} And by rabbinic enactment, even ten mattresses one atop the other, and the lower one among them is kilayim—it is forbidden to sit on the upper one, lest a thread wrap upon his flesh. In what case is this said? With soft items such as sheets and garments; but pillows and cushions that are hard, and there is no concern lest a thread wrap upon him—it is permitted to sleep on them, provided his flesh does not touch them. In what case is this said? When they are empty and placed on a wooden or stone bench; but if they are full, or even if empty when they are placed on a bed or on straw—it is forbidden, because it bends beneath him and wraps upon his flesh. {Rama: And some say all this applies to kilayim d'oraisa; but with kilayim d'rabbanan that are hard, it is permitted to sit on them in every case, since when they are hard it is only d'rabbanan—they did not decree on hard items (Rashba siman 962). Nevertheless, l'chatchila one should not make items of kilayim (Kol Bo).}
