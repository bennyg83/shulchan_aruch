**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Up to one hundred... and not.</b> This is like terumah. The Yerushalmi in chapter 6 of Nedarim states the general rule in the name of R. Shimon citing R. Yehoshua: anything that will later become permitted has one law, while something with no later permission, such as terumah, has another. Ran cites this briefly on Nedarim 52a. The Mishnah in chapter 2 of Orlah distinguishes stringently between mixtures of the same type and leniently with different types. Chullin likewise discusses a pot in which terumah was cooked. <b>(Likkut) And when mixed with a different type, up to sixty.</b> This is stated in the Yerushalmi on Nedarim and cited by Ran there. The Bavli in Chullin 97a and elsewhere likewise uses the taste standard for a pot in which terumah was cooked. On 99a Abaye's discussion indicates sixty, and the alternative answer there follows the opinion that ordinary prohibitions require one hundred. Torat HaBayit. See Tosafot there.
