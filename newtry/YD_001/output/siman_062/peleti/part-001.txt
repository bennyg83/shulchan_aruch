אבר מן החי וכו' דעת הרמב"ם בתולש מן אבר שאין בו עצם אפי' מקצתו חייב וכתב הפר"ח דראיי' שלו מהא דאמרינן דחלב הוי כאמ"ה וחידוש הוא דשרי תורה וכו'. ואין זו ראיי' דלכ"ע אסור מידי דהוי חצי שיעור רק דראב"ד ס"ל דאין בו מלקות וחלב דס"ד לאיסור לא למלקות רק לאיסורא בעלמא כמו חצי שיעור וגם בלא"ה למ"ד טעם כעיקר דאורייתא ולוקין עליו א"כ אם אבר מהחי שלם שנתן טעם בקדירה וכי אין לוקין עליו ולא אמרינן לו יהי' דחתך מן אבר אינו לוקה ולמה על טעמו ילקה וע"כ כיון דכ"ע דאורייתא ה"ל כאלו אוכל כל האבר שלם כברייתא וא"כ אף החלב ס"ד לאסור משום הטמאין לאסור צירן ורוטבן ודו"ק. ועיין מש"ל סימן מ"ג ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
Eivar min hachai, etc. — the Rambam's view: one who plucks from a limb that has no bone, even part of it, is liable; and Peri Chadash wrote that his proof is from that which we say chelev is like eivar min hachai, and the novelty is that the Torah permitted it, etc. And this is not proof, for according to all it is forbidden — it is a case of half a measure; only Raavad holds there are no lashes, and chelev that one thought was for prohibition not for lashes — only for prohibition in general, like half a measure; and also according to the one who says taste is like essence d'oraisa, one receives lashes for it — if so, if a whole limb from a living animal gave taste in a pot, is there no lashes for it? And we do not say to him: you should have cut from the limb, he does not receive lashes — why for its taste would he receive lashes? And necessarily, since according to all it is d'oraisa, it is as if he ate the whole limb whole, as in the baraita; and if so, chelev too one thought is forbidden because of impure animals to forbid their broth and sauce; and requires further study. And see what I wrote above siman 43, see there.

And fowl — and the Rambam at the end of Laws of Kings wrote that fowl appears that a child of Noah is not executed for it; it teaches that he is not warned from the Torah, for its warning is its death regarding a child of Noah; and see responsa Emunat Shmuel that he also wrote on this; and behold Raavad challenged him; and there is a practical difference for practical law whether it is permitted to give a limb from a living animal from fowl to a child of Noah — whether there is lifnei iver do not place a stumbling block, or not. And in this too is reconciled that which Rashi wrote daf 103 that eivar min hachai came and applied to treifah because of miggo that it applies to a child of Noah — and he did not write because of miggo that it applies to limbs that do not have an olive-bulk of flesh, only in combination of sinews and bones it applies to all limbs, even very large ones, for Rashi holds that anonymously it teaches even in very large rams where there is no limb in which there would not be an olive-bulk of flesh. And in this is reconciled that which Tosafot wrote for Rava who said: he plucked a limb and rendered it treifah — from this we learn he holds the prohibition does not stand for limbs; if so, that which we say he plucked chelev from treifah, he is liable for three — how do you find it, for how can eivar min hachai apply to treifah; and why need inclusive prohibition, for Rava does not hold otherwise, for if so, why does he require specifically that it became treifah in it; and according to what is written it is well: this inclusive prohibition that applies to a child of Noah — Rava does not hold; but this inclusive prohibition he holds from miggo that it applies to a limb that does not have an olive-bulk of flesh, it applies also to all limbs; and Rava does not establish it that it deals in one animal, only in two, and there is no inclusive prohibition; but that which we say he plucked from treifah etc. — it deals in one animal, and he has inclusive prohibition, as written; and requires further study.

Forbidden — if the animal is treifah, if eivar min hachai applies to treifah (and practical difference as I wrote above regarding apostate and the like) — we say in Chullin daf 33: one who ate eivar min hachai and from treifah — R' Yehudah said he is liable for two; and Resh Lakish said he is liable only for one; and Rava established it like his view against Abbaye, who deals with one who plucked a limb and rendered it treifah in it; and the animal — the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and if so, treifah prohibition and limb prohibition come together; and Resh Lakish holds the prohibition stands for limbs, and treifah prohibition did not come and apply to eivar min hachai; and afterward the Gemara concluded in the name of R' Yochanan: he plucked chelev from treifah and it lived and he ate it — he receives three lashes; and the Gemara was divided on the reason of the matter — whether because the prohibition stands for limbs and all three applied as one; and it deals with that it became treifah immediately at birth; or the prohibition does not stand for limbs, and eivar min hachai prohibition applied to treifah and chelev; and see there another establishment. And behold the Rambam in Forbidden Foods chapter 5 halachah 8 wrote: one who plucked a limb from a living animal and rendered it treifah in removing it and ate it — he is liable for two because of eivar min hachai and because of treifah, for two prohibitions came as one; from this it is implied that the Rambam holds the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and eivar min hachai is not considered an inclusive prohibition, since it applies to a child of Noah; for otherwise how did they apply as one — behold eivar min hachai applied immediately at birth; and also if eivar min hachai were an inclusive prohibition, what of it that it was treifah from before — behold it would be an inclusive prohibition; and also at first glance it is astonishing, for the Rambam himself holds inclusive prohibition; and it is difficult like Tosafot's difficulty on R' Yochanan; and also afterward the Rambam wrote: one who plucks chelev and ate it receives two lashes because of chelev and because of eivar min hachai (and this is difficult, since the prohibition does not stand for limbs, chelev prohibition preceded from exiting to the air of the world — how can eivar min hachai prohibitions apply, behold I have no inclusive prohibition either); he plucked chelev from treifah and ate it — he receives three lashes; necessarily; and this is more difficult, since the prohibition does not stand for limbs, how can eivar min hachai prohibition apply to chelev and treifah, for behold I have no inclusive prohibition either, and treifah in it was not mentioned. And already Lechem Mishneh and authors stood on it; and they reconciled strainedly, as Peri Chadash wrote; and Peri Chadash also explained strainedly, and understanding is difficult. And what appears to me: it is certainly difficult how Rava can say to R' Yehudah if not eivar min hachai and treifah prohibitions come as one — he is not liable for limb and for treifah; and afterward R' Yochanan stated simply that he is liable for limb and treifah; therefore it appears that this is the Rambam's view: for there to be inclusive prohibition because it applies to a child of Noah — we do not say as I wrote above that a prohibition stated regarding children of Noah is not a prohibition for which Israel is liable; and he holds that which we say the animal while alive stands for limbs — and Rashi explained: to cut it into pieces; and one must explain how it stands to cut it — for if one cuts it, the pieces are forbidden because of eivar min hachai; and from the Torah you shall destroy the animal — and it must be said that thus are the matters: were it not that the Torah forbade because of eivar min hachai, the animal would stand to be cut as he wishes, only the Torah forbade because of eivar min hachai and it is impossible to cut it; therefore on every piece and piece eivar min hachai applies lest he cut it and cut from it; and the Rambam holds this applies when other pieces and limbs in the body stand to be cut, were it not for the Merciful One's prohibition of eivar min hachai; but a limb that renders treifah in it in the body — such as thigh and the like — if so, how can it be thought to cut — who is crazy and foolish who would cut a limb and render treifah thereby the whole animal. And this is certainly not for cutting and limbs remain; and this is what Rava innovated: certainly in general the prohibition stands for limbs and treifah prohibition did not come and apply; but in one who cuts a limb that renders treifah in it — this is certainly the prohibition does not stand for limbs, and treifah prohibition applied, for it is prohibition in a single body; and this is what R' Yehudah holds in such a case the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and if so, now that we were informed of a limb that renders treifah in it, such as thigh and the like — the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and if so, an animal that became treifah — all such limbs, treifah prohibition applied to them, for they did not stand for limbs; and at the time of treifah it applied to them, for then he can pluck them; for otherwise the animal is treifah; and if so, they come together in Rava's matter, and it applied to them; and if so, even on other limbs where there is no treifah — it depends; and even though eivar min hachai preceded in other limbs that stand for limbs — nevertheless treifah prohibition applied from miggo that it applies to limbs where treifah depends, like thigh, the like, etc. — it applies to all limbs of the animal inclusively; and it is an inclusive prohibition; if so, Rava who innovated this for us in limbs that became treifah in it — it is prohibition in a single body, and it applied from this; we learn the other of R' Yochanan that treifah applies to eivar min hachai in every death and in every manner it became treifah, from the reasoning of miggo, etc.; and one was stated from its associate — and examine, for it is correct and deep; and these are the words of the Rambam who summarized in one statement: he plucked a limb and rendered treifah — for it is prohibition in a single body; and he taught us this in that limb and the like the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and inclusive prohibition was innovated for us for all limbs; therefore he afterward wrote that he is liable for three, for treifah applied to the limb; and examine, for it is correct. And behold one should infer: why does treifah prohibition not apply to eivar min hachai — and we do not say from miggo that it applies to chelev in an animal, for chelev of treifah is forbidden, it should apply also to the animal itself, and it would be inclusive prohibition. And it is possible that therefore the Rambam holds chelev of treifah has no prohibition of negative commandment, only prohibition in general; and if so, miggo that it applies to chelev is not relevant, for it applied only to prohibition in general; and we require that prohibition of treifah negative commandment apply to eivar min hachai — and this does not exist. If one says in found treifah chelev found in the animal's innards — this is not nullified by the rule of treifah, only chelev that was formed afterward; and this is not because of the reason of treifah, only it is a species of animal and forbidden matter, and resembles broth and its sauce; but in eivar min hachai their broth and sauce are forbidden; but this is strained, for in eivar min hachai we do not forbid chelev since there is permission for eivar min hachai; and if so, there should still be miggo that it applies to chelev, for what is not relevant in eivar min hachai — since there is permission for its prohibition; and it is clear as Rosh wrote: in any case Taz wrote above in Laws of slaughter siman 24: that which we say in the Gemara chelev is permitted in eivar min hachai, for there is permission for its prohibition — and Taz explained: this means eivar min hachai prohibition is light in itself, since there is permission for its prohibition; it is not implied from this sugya; for if so, it would be appropriate for treifah prohibition to apply to eivar min hachai prohibition in a severe prohibition. However, this is difficult according to what Tosafot wrote in Yevamot chapter 2: one who comes upon his brother's wife and his wife's sister — they thought for R' Shimon he is liable because of his brother's wife, for it is not prohibition in a single body, for his sister's prohibition has permission for its prohibition when his wife dies, unlike his brother's wife where there is no permission, see there in Tosafot; and if so, that which the Gemara said why need the verse that eivar min hachai applies in impurity — let it be derived that its prohibition applies to a child of Noah; and the Gemara answers for R' Yehudah: so too it was needed only for R' Eliezer; and they further challenged: even for R' Eliezer according to the one who holds the prohibition stands for limbs — behold it is prohibition in a single body, see there; and it is difficult: perhaps R' Eliezer holds like R' Shimon, and eivar min hachai has permission for its prohibition in slaughter, unlike impurity; and if so, it is not prohibition in a single body, and impurity prohibition preceded; and requires further study.
