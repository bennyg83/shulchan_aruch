**** ENGLISH ****
A limb that separated from a living animal is forbidden; and flesh that separated from a living animal is forbidden because of "flesh in the field you shall not eat" (treifah). Even if it separated from the animal and is still inside it — such as if a piece was cut from the spleen or kidneys and the cut piece remained inside — it is forbidden.

**** ENGLISH ****
A limb or flesh that is dangling — if it cannot return and live, even if it did not separate from the animal until after slaughter — it is forbidden. {Rama: And see above siman 55: the cauda equina standing in a place that does not render the animal treifah is permitted to eat, and we do not say the extra portion is considered as removed and is like a dangling limb (Or Zarua, HaArukh). A limb that slipped from its place in a place that does not render the animal treifah is permitted to eat it even if skin and flesh do not cover most of it (Rashba siman 69). But the custom is to be stringent also in this (Or Zarua, HaArukh).}

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="6"></i>ביצי זכר שנתלשו ועדיין הם מעורים בכיסם אינו אסור מן התורה שהרי יש בו מקצת חיות ולפיכך אינו מסריח ואעפ"כ אסור לאכלו <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="4"></i>ממנהג שנהגו ישראל שלא לאכלו מפני שדומה לאבר מן החי:
**** ENGLISH ****
Male eggs that were plucked and are still wrapped in their sac are not forbidden from the Torah, for there is in them a measure of life-force; therefore they do not rot; nevertheless it is forbidden to eat them — from the custom that Israel practiced not to eat them because they resemble a limb from a living animal.
