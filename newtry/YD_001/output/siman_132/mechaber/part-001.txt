**** ENGLISH ****
One may not benefit from yayin nesech; it contains 7 se’ifim. A non-Jew who touched our wine — even though it is forbidden in benefit — it is permitted to take its value from that non-Jew who forbade it; or that non-Jew may sell it and give its value to the Jew. And if a Jew sold it to another non-Jew: if he gave him the money before the Jew gave him the wine, the money is forbidden in benefit; but if the non-Jew took the wine first and afterwards gave the money to the Jew, there is one who says that even though to the owner of the wine who sold it the money is forbidden in benefit, to others it is permitted. {Rama: And all this speaks only of non-Jews who pour libations, but in our times all of their touch does not forbid except for drinking (Rashi in the name of the Geonim), as was explained above in siman 123.}

A Jew who sold his wine to a non-Jew: if he set the price before the non-Jew drew it, its value is permitted, for once he set the price his mind relied [on the sale]; and once he drew it, he acquired it, and it does not become yayin nesech until he touches it — it turns out that at the time of the sale it was permitted. But if the non-Jew drew the wine before the price was set, its value is forbidden, for his mind did not rely [on the sale] even though he drew it (and since it came into the non-Jew’s possession, the Jew is not careful with it, and one must be concerned that he touched it), and it turns out that when he touched it his mind had still not relied on taking the money, and the wine became forbidden through his touch — and this is like one who sells stam yeinam. In what case is this said? When the Jew measured it into his own vessel (or into the non-Jew’s vessel that has no “delay of wine” in it) (Tur). And even l'chatchila, it is forbidden only when there was a bit of the wine at the mouth of the non-Jew’s vessel, for the wine becomes forbidden immediately; but if there was “delay of wine” at the bottom of the vessel, and the non-Jew’s vessel was standing in the courtyard of the non-Jew, or he was holding it in his hand, or it was standing in the domain of the seller and the seller said to him, “Let your vessel acquire for you,” it is permitted, for immediately when it came into the airspace of the vessel he acquired it, and the wine does not become forbidden until it touched the “delay of wine” at the bottom of the non-Jew’s vessel. And likewise, if the non-Jew measured it and first lifted it in order to acquire it, everything is permitted, for behold he acquired it first before he measured; but if he did not lift it and measured it, its law is like other wine that comes through the power of a non-Jew, as was explained above in siman 125. And all this speaks only in their days, when non-Jews poured libations and their touch forbade in benefit; but in our times, when non-Jews do not pour libations and their touch forbids only for drinking, in every case it is permitted in benefit, and the custom is simple to sell wine to a non-Jew, and they are not particular about all these laws (all in Tur).}

Jewish workers who did work for a non-Jew, and he sent them a barrel of wine as their wages: if it was before they received it as their wages, it is permitted that they say to him, “Instead of sending us this barrel, let it be yours and give us money in exchange for it” (Ran).
