<b>But if, etc.</b> There the Gemara says: No, it is necessary where he measures, etc. Although the Gemara later asks: In the end, etc., and answers that the case is where, etc., the Tur and Rama write accordingly. One can explain that this further answer was needed only to address the Gemara's question, 'In the end, when it reaches,' etc., which would prove that a poured stream is a connection. Since we rule that a poured stream is a connection, that additional answer is unnecessary, and the first answer, 'where he measures,' etc., suffices. This is why the Mechaber writes, 'or into the vessel of,' etc., even though the Gemara says that in such a case it is permitted even in the non-Jew's vessel, for it says: If the non-Jew is holding it, etc. According to the foregoing this is resolved: all that was said according to the view that a poured stream is not a connection, but according to the view that it is a connection, even if it is the Jew's vessel, once the non-Jew shakes it the wine in the vessel becomes forbidden, and the same applies to the non-Jew's vessel. The Tur and Rama, however, write according to the Gemara's conclusion. Beit Yosef writes that they understand that a poured stream involving ordinary gentile wine is not forbidden for benefit. See siman 126 in the Tur and Beit Yosef, and see the Shach. The phrase 'that contains residual wine' means even at the bottom, namely enough moisture to wet another surface. Without that amount it is not forbidden for benefit, as stated in the Mishnah in chapter 2, 29b, regarding non-Jewish wineskins, etc., whose proceeds are permitted.

His objection is not difficult, because this ruling follows Ran's explanation of the Mishnah, 'He removed the funnel,' etc., that 'forbidden' means forbidden for benefit. If it meant only forbidden for drinking, even without residual wine it would be forbidden because of the non-Jew's vessels. According to this, since we follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, we do not follow that Mishnah. The Rif's citation of it is therefore surprising. Rather, even Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who permits selling the entire mixture, permits only when the seller explicitly excludes the value of the prohibition. If he sells it without specification, it is forbidden even though he derives no benefit from the prohibited substance. The Tur, who does not require an explicit exclusion and writes simply that after the fact it is permitted, holds like Tosafot there, s.v. 'If there is,' etc., despite what is stated later. This accords with siman 137, seif 1, 'if it is,' etc., so the prohibition there is only for drinking. Mordechai writes likewise, and Ran cites this view in the name of others. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 137. Orach Chaim, siman 447, seif 10, likewise says that it is not sold at a higher price, etc., proving that even an unspecified sale is permitted. <b>(Likut) Likewise, if he measured it, etc.</b> Rashba explains in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and Ramban that the non-Jew measured it for himself and had drawn it before measuring. Although he lifted it while measuring, he did not acquire it, because that lifting was not done for acquisition.
