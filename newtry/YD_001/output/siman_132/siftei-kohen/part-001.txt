**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is one who says, etc.</b> The Mechaber presents this in the name of “one who says” because Rabbeinu Yerucham cites an opinion that permits only when the non-Jew sold the wine and afterward paid the Jew, so that when he paid the Jew he retained nothing of the wine. The Beis Yosef cites this, and his words there clearly imply that even for others the money is forbidden so long as the non-Jew has not sold the wine. This is the opinion of Rabbi Elchanan cited by the Rosh at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah. The Derishah understood that the Mechaber wrote this as “one who says” because he held that according to the Tur, even the owner of the wine may benefit when he first takes back the wine. This is certainly not the view of either the Tur or the Mechaber. As to the practical ruling, although the Beis Yosef overlooked the words of the Rosh at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah, from which the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham derived their statements, and the Rosh there rules like this opinion, nevertheless the Ramban and the Ran at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah prohibit it. The Ran writes that this also appears to be Rashi's view, and Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise did not decide the matter. The Mordechai there, however, follows the Rosh, and this is also the conclusion of Tosafos there in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak, as cited by the Beis Yosef below at the end of siman 144. Therefore, where there is a substantial loss it is permitted, but otherwise it is forbidden. Although this has no practical application to ordinary non-Jewish wine, since we rule that nowadays it is permitted for benefit, it does apply to the sale of idolatry and its accessories, as the poskim state explicitly that the same law applies to idolatry. This is clear. See also the end of siman 144.

<b>For as soon as it entered the airspace, etc.</b> The Beis Yosef asks that all this is said in the Gemara only according to the opinion that nitzok is not considered a connection. According to our ruling above in siman 126 that nitzok is a connection, however, even if the non-Jew holds the vessel in his hand or it stands in his courtyard, the wine should always be forbidden, since before entering the vessel's airspace it already became forbidden through nitzok. He answers that even the opinion which holds that nitzok is a connection concedes that when one pours into ordinary non-Jewish wine, nitzok prohibits only drinking, not benefit. The Darkei Moshe cites this. The Bach objects that this interpretation is incorrect according to the Tur and the Rashba, as he explained at length in siman 126. Yet he himself requires that very correction. It is clear that the Rashba in Toras HaBayis, folio 151, third column, holds that regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, nitzok is considered a connection only to prohibit drinking, not benefit. The Beis Yosef cites this at the end of siman 126, and the Bach himself writes so there. The Rashba himself also explicitly explains our case as the Beis Yosef does. Nevertheless, it is difficult to explain the Tur this way. The Bach answers that the Tur follows the conclusion in siman 126 that where there is substantial loss, nitzok may be permitted, and therefore here he permits the wine even for drinking after the fact, since prohibiting all the wine measured for the non-Jew even for benefit would presumably cause substantial loss. This is very puzzling. There is no loss at all, since after the fact it may be sold to the non-Jew. Just as he originally intended to sell it to the non-Jew, he may sell it now. How, then, could one permit it for drinking? Although we said above in siman 126, se'if 2, that even where it can be sold to a non-Jew it is considered a substantial loss, that is because there he must sell it somewhat cheaply, as is explicit there. Here, however, there is no loss at all, since he can sell it now just as he originally intended, particularly since the price had been fixed, for otherwise the non-Jew's vessels or domain would not acquire it. It is also astonishing that the Bach describes this as a substantial loss because all the wine measured would be forbidden for benefit, for it is certainly permitted for benefit, as the Tur and the Rama write. Moreover, our case concerns a method by which it is permitted to sell the wine initially. It appears to me that the Tur's reasoning is as follows. Where an actual mixture is permitted after the fact, logic dictates that nitzok should be permitted even initially. Similarly, the Rosh and the Tur write above at the beginning of siman 125 that if a non-Jew pours wine from a vessel, although the wine that leaves the vessel is forbidden because of his force, the wine remaining inside is not forbidden through nitzok, since the wine outside is prohibited only because of his force and not because of actual contact. All the more so here, where the mixture is entirely permitted after the fact. Even one who prohibits nitzok there may do so only because the wine that left remains forbidden after the fact, unlike here. When the Gemara says that according to the opinion that nitzok is a connection the wine is forbidden, it is discussing the view that even after the fact wine mixed with wine is forbidden in any amount, and may not be sold to a non-Jew except after deducting the value of the forbidden wine. We, however, rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that after the fact the mixture is permitted. Therefore, nitzok should not prohibit it here. These words are clear. Nevertheless, the Rama remains difficult. He rules like the Tur and Rashba that when the non-Jew holds the vessel in his hand, it is permitted even initially, yet he also records without qualification the Rambam and Mechaber's ruling that when the non-Jew holds it in the air, it is forbidden. One must say that the Rama explains the Mechaber's prohibition where the non-Jew holds and shakes the vessel as follows: before the wine reaches the vessel, the non-Jew, through his shaking, causes it to touch the vessel's rim, and the entire flow is immediately forbidden. But if he holds the vessel gently in the air so that the wine is poured into its center, only the first drop at the bottom becomes forbidden, and the rest is not prohibited through nitzok. This may be because nitzok involving ordinary non-Jewish wine does not prohibit benefit, as the Rashba and Beis Yosef hold, or because, since an actual mixture is permitted after the fact, nitzok does not prohibit it. This explanation is strained. Alternatively, the Rama explains that the Rambam and Mechaber's phrase “When does this apply” refers back to the immediately preceding case in which the price had not been fixed. They mean that the wine is forbidden only because the non-Jew might touch it, implying that if the Jew guarded it from touch it is permitted, as explained above in s.k. 8 regarding wine measured into a Jew's vessel. But when it is poured into a non-Jew's vessel, or into a Jew's vessel held and shaken by the non-Jew, it is forbidden immediately, even before actual touch, and the Jew must first take the money. In such a case, the non-Jew does not acquire the wine merely when it enters the airspace through lifting, because neither lifting nor any meshichah is effective until the price has first been fixed, as stated by the Tur and the poskim and explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 200, se'if 7. According to this, the Beis Yosef's objection in Bedek HaBayis against the Rambam, cited above in s.k. 18, is resolved. In any event, one may also say that the Rambam follows the Raavad, cited by the Rashba in Toras HaBayis and by the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 126, who holds that nitzok involving ordinary non-Jewish wine prohibits all of it even for benefit.

<b>But, etc.</b> The Tur concludes: “Some say that if his share has already been recorded against the Jew, it is forbidden regardless of the wording he uses. <b>הרי זה מותר. </b>מדברי הב"ח נראה דהיכא דאינו ידוע שמכרו לצרכו אף ביי"נ איכא למיחש שמא מכרו לחזור ולקנות בו יי"נ כדלקמן ס"ס קמ"ד גבי עבודת כוכבים אבל הדרישה בסימן קמ"ד חילק בין עבודת כוכבים ליי"נ דביי"נ לא חיישינן מן הסתם שלקחו לחזור ולקנות בו יי"נ ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>This is permitted.</b> The Bach appears to hold that when it is not known that the non-Jew sold it for his own needs, even with ordinary non-Jewish wine one must be concerned that he sold it in order to purchase more ordinary non-Jewish wine, as explained below at the end of siman 144 regarding idolatry. The Derishah in siman 144, however, distinguishes between idolatry and ordinary non-Jewish wine. Regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, we do not ordinarily suspect that he took the money in order to purchase more of it. See there.

<b>וי"א כו'. </b>והראב"ד חילק כשבידו לנגשו ומרצונו ממתין לו אסור שהרי על ידו מתקיים ע"א אבל כשאין בידו לנגשו וע"כ ממתין לו מותר עכ"ל טור ודברי הראב"ד הביאו הרשב"א והר"ן ורבינו ירוחם במסקנא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> The Raavad distinguishes as follows. If the creditor has the power to press the non-Jew for payment but willingly grants him additional time, it is forbidden, since the idolatrous object continues to exist through him. But if he lacks the power to press him and therefore must grant additional time, it is permitted. Tur. The Rashba, Ran, and Rabbeinu Yerucham cite the Raavad's words as their conclusion.
