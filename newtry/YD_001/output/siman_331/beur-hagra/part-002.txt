**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that he will take them.</b> Meaning even in such a case. This is implied by the incident of Mari bar Isak's sharecropper and the case of the steward there. Although Bava Metzia according to Abaye could be read as requiring explicit agency, Rambam understands that the conclusion rejects that limitation. Ameimar's exchange with the baraita and the Kiddushin case involving the steward show that a mitzvah context is different because a person ordinarily wants the mitzvah performed. Tosafot strain to preserve an agency requirement, but the straightforward Gemara does not support it. <b>(Likkut) One who separates.</b> Rambam's textual reading is "and do you think so?" Tosafot cite another reading. The point is that if explicit agency were required in every case, why would the Gemara need the test of whether the owner says, "Take from the better produce"? Rather, the agency answer is only Rava's resolution; the simpler explanation is that a mitzvah is different. Kiddushin 52b likewise involves no prior appointment, unlike Tosafot's forced explanation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He separated.</b> Kiddushin there and Shevuot 26b. See Tosafot, Gittin 31a, who indicate that the Sages do not dispute thought alone for terumah, and similarly Bechorot 59 and Menachot 54. <b>(Likkut) He separated.</b> Yom Tov 13 states that just as terumah gedolah can be taken by estimation, it can be taken through thought. The Sages dispute Abba Elazar ben Gimel only regarding terumat ma'aser and estimation, not thought, because the verse itself says "and it shall be reckoned." In the first chapter of Terumot, the mute may not separate because he cannot bless, showing speech is not inherently required. Kiddushin and Shevuot likewise treat terumah and consecrated property as Scriptural exceptions where thought has legal effect. Tosafot in Gittin therefore write that even for terumat ma'aser the Sages dispute only estimation, not thought. Rashi explains "with thought" as fixing one's eyes on one side and designating it mentally. The same idea appears in Chullin concerning R. Meir possibly having designated tithes mentally. Although one might distinguish demai, the passage there speaks of definite produce, as shown by the statement that chaverim were not suspected. Nevertheless, separating terumot and ma'asrot on Shabbos and Yom Tov is forbidden, even demai, as stated in Yom Tov, Shabbos, and Demai. R. Yehudah's permission in the Shabbos passage using mental designation applies only to meduma, not tevel, and R. Shimon ben Elazar disputes even there. The first Tanna apparently prohibits even mental designation in meduma. If, however, one designated the place and name before Shabbos, actual physical separation may be completed on Shabbos as taught in Demai. The same applies to challah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he was... and if.</b> This follows our reading in the latter clause that R. Yehudah says he may finish. Rambam has the same reading in his commentary and in chapter 4 of Yom Tov. Kesef Mishneh's explanation is difficult, and Tosafot Yom Tov's even more so. The Gemara's question there from the baraita nevertheless requires explanation, as does the Yerushalmi in Terumot and Ma'asrot. The basic law is difficult because Shabbos itself fixes produce, as stated in seif 111. Rash raises this. Tosafot answer that the case concerns a cluster whose processing had not been completed. This implies both R. Eliezer and R. Yehudah hold that Shabbos and a courtyard can establish liability even before completion of processing, which would not accord with our ruling. Rash answers based on the fact that he began eating permissibly. The Yerushalmi, however, records R. Natan as rejecting that reason and saying R. Eliezer means he should wait until Motzaei Shabbos or leave the courtyard and then finish. According to Rambam one must read the Yerushalmi with difficulty: not that beginning permissibly itself allows finishing on Shabbos, but that it allows finishing afterward. The Yom Tov sugya similarly assumes at first that "he may finish" means even on Shabbos and in the courtyard, which forces the case to concern incomplete processing. It then answers that the baraita is as stated. Another approach is that R. Natan follows Reish Lakish, who holds Shabbos fixes produce only for that Shabbos unless it was specifically designated for Shabbos. This fits the Yom Tov sugya and Rashi. Even then, eating on Shabbos itself is prohibited, as Rashi explains from "and you shall call Shabbos a delight." It remains possible that we do not rule like R. Natan, because R. Yochanan's formulation elsewhere treats Shabbos more broadly. <b>(Likkut)</b> The Bavli appears to follow Reish Lakish, as do Rashi and Tosafot. The phrase "when the day became sanctified upon him, Hillel forbids for that Shabbos" likewise means only for Shabbos itself, as in the Mishnah concerning one eating from a cluster.
