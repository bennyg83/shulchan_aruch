Let a non-Jew taste it. Derishah wrote: from that it is forbidden to an Israelite to taste — from this one learns that sometimes they buy meat from the butcher and it is unknown whether it is salted or not, it is forbidden to taste with the tongue — until here is his language; and this is very astonishing, for in siman 42 regarding marah he wrote explicitly that one tastes with the tongue, and therefore it appears that in tasting with the tongue alone there is no concern of issur in a place of safek, and therefore he mentioned explicitly regarding marah that one taste with the tongue, as Beit Yosef wrote there in name of Mordechai, and likewise permitted if it is clean of blood there; and this case is different here, for one needed to taste through actual eating in order to sense if there is taste there, and therefore a non-Jew must taste it; and regarding Taanis in Orach Chaim siman 567 it is explained plainly that there is no issur in tasting with the tongue alone, for it was mentioned there only regarding tasting to take into the mouth, and in this there is a difference between Taanis and other issurim. And this 'let a non-Jew taste it' implies even not a professional baker, as Beit Yosef inferred from Raavad's language who wrote there non-Jew and did not mention baker; and if so the essence of the heter is from mesiach lefi tumo as explained in Beit Yosef in name of Rashba, and in Bedek HaBayit the Re'ah challenged on this that stam yeinam is not believed except for testimony of a woman which is only revealing a matter in general, etc., and this challenge was also raised in Terumat HaDeshen siman 79 on Rashba and he resolved that Rashba holds taste is like substance is not d'oraisa and in rabbinic matters stam yeinam helps; and indeed Beit Yosef wrote in name of Terumat HaDeshen HaAruch that he agrees with Rabbeinu Tam who holds d'oraisa — perhaps he does not agree with them except regarding the essence of the law, but regarding stam yeinam he well relies on Rashi and Rambam who hold taste is like substance is rabbinic; and perhaps to this Beit Yosef intended when he wrote in this siman that on this we rely on Rambam and Rashba that a non-Jew stam yeinam helps even if he is not a baker; but indeed we find such a dispute also in Piskei Maharai siman 130 regarding bechor that he wrote that for Rosh stam yeinam helps when it does not improve his purchase, but he challenged him that it only helps for testimony of a woman as stated in Hagozel, and it appears to me that Rashba and Rosh hold that what we say in Hagozel that stam yeinam is not believed in matters of d'oraisa is specifically in something that requires testimony, unlike issur and heter where it helps well, for in this we do not require complete testimony but if we have proof it suffices — know that a gentile is believed in issurin to permit, and if so likewise stam yeinam, for from this we have a presumption that there is no issur there and it suffices; and proof that a baker helps even without stam yeinam because he does not impair his presumption, and in something that requires testimony certainly such a thing does not help, but perforce in issurin one needs only proof — so it appears to me to resolve Rashba's and Rosh's view; but regarding halachah Tur and Shulchan Aruch already ruled in siman 316 regarding bechor that stam yeinam does not help, and if so it does not help in any place where there is d'oraisa issur: in Tur it is written and likewise Rosh wrote that one needs, etc.; he who did not write anonymously and likewise Rosh explained Beit Yosef meaning there is no resolution in his words whether one needs a baker specifically — investigate, for Rosh wrote baker non-Jew implying we require specifically a professional, for otherwise why write this, for Rosh's way is not to write except what emerges for law; therefore it appears the opposite, that certainly Rosh holds like those who say before this that one needs a professional specifically; except there is one novelty in Rosh's words, for it implies if the non-Jew knows we rely on him in another matter not regarding issur and heter it is called lefi tumo, which is not so in other lefi tumo such as testimony of a woman — this does not help; therefore he wrote in name of Rosh that this too helps here:

And if it became mixed in its species, etc. Rashba derives this from what we say in the Gemara for R Yehuda who holds min b'mino is not nullified — if it became mixed in its species and not its species, remove its species as if it is not, and what is not its species increases upon it and nullifies it; so too here for us, since the opposite — min b'mino is lighter from the Torah than a mixture in not its species — perforce if it became mixed in its species and not its species, remove what is not its species as if it is not, and its species increases upon it and nullifies it; and if it had not been spilled and the mixture were still before us, rabbinic issur would remain, for rabbinically a majority does not suffice; but now that it was spilled and we can say there was sixty in its species against the issur, we go leniently — perforce we need it known and clear to us that there was a majority of its heter species against the issur so there not be concern of d'oraisa issur; and the language 'we rely to say its species was majority,' etc., that Tur wrote — Beit Yosef already resolved this, see there; and Maharshal in Orach Chaim siman 46 wrote on this Rashba as disagreeing with him, and these are his words: 'It appears to me this is not analogous at all — for there regarding min b'mino in the Gemara there is no issur from imparting taste, but the Scriptural decree for R Yehuda is that it is not nullified in its species; therefore when there is also not its species with it we say remove its species so the taste will be nullified in not its species and automatically all will be permitted; but here what forbids not its species through noten taam from the Torah? If so, where the sauce was spilled, even if its species is also with it, in the end it still stands in safek, and safek d'oraisa is stringent' — end. In my view Rashba's words are correct and clear, for what we say in the Gemara 'remove its species,' etc., is such as slaughtered meat and nevelah meat and sauce, and there is sixty in the sauce against the nevelah meat alone — we could have said that nevertheless slaughtered meat should be forbidden since it is its species with the issur and in its species nullification of sixty does not help; nevertheless we do not say so, but since in slaughtered meat there is no issur except from the mixture, we now say remove its heter species as if it is not and the mixture will not harm it since the issur was nullified in sixty in the sauce — the issur is as if it does not exist and there is not even absorption of any amount; if so, the same applies for us — its species is nullified in majority from the Torah and not its species in sixty for all, and slaughtered meat mixed with nevelah and sauce and there is not sixty to nullify the nevelah — there is heter here to nullify the issur in slaughtered meat since it is majority against it and in its species majority helps from the Torah; except the sauce should be forbidden since there is not sixty against the issur — it imparts taste there; therefore we say we consider the sauce as if it did not mix with the nevelah since the nevelah was already nullified through abundance of its species and is as if it does not exist, and for heter the issur is considered entirely gone — so it is from the Torah, except the Rabbis were stringent to require sixty even in its species; perforce here where there is safek whether there was sixty in not its species against the issur and it is impossible to stand on it since it was spilled — permitted, for we leave it to Torah law that its species nullifies in majority and does not need sixty since it is possible there was sixty in not its species — and all this is correct without doubt; see what he wrote in siman 109 s.v. 1 from this.) It is plain that here it deals only with one-time mixture in its species and not its species; but if it first mixed with not its species and afterward its species was added there, certainly chanan already applied before its species came — so it appears to me. If it is known, etc. Before we explain these matters we explain that there is a difference in interpreting Tur's words between Beit Yosef and Rama. Tur wrote, and these are his words: If it is known how much the issur is — such as a new spoon in which one shook a k'zayit of milk, and afterward on its day one shook a pot of meat in it — one needs only sixty against the k'zayit absorbed; but an old spoon in which one shook many times — we do not know how much it absorbed and we estimate against all of it — end Tur's language. Turei Chayim siman 183 interpreted Tur's words that he holds there is no vessel chanan at all; and what Tur wrote 'but an old spoon,' etc., means that today one shook many times with meat and stuck it into milk within twenty-four hours, requiring sixty against all of it — Tur means: if new, sixty against the issur suffices for all; if old, all need against all of it — except if there is already issur in the spoon because there was heter and it absorbed a k'zayit issur and stuck it into heter — on this he relied on what will be written in siman 106 that there is a dispute; so Turei Chayim interpreted Tur's words. Beit Yosef interpreted differently, and this is the essence of his interpretation even though not his exact language — for Tur holds like Rashba's first view that Beit Yosef brings, unlike Ramban — namely that also in absorbed in a vessel we say chanan; Tur means specifically if the spoon is new and absorbed a k'zayit of milk, not necessarily new literally, but likewise an old spoon already used before within twenty-four hours with much meat and today absorbed a k'zayit of milk — still called new, for whatever is not ben yomo is as if new since its taste does not forbid — therefore noten taam does not apply, for at milk absorption it was heter — perforce if stuck afterward into meat, one needs only sixty against the milk absorption. But if an old spoon — on one day used much with meat and afterward absorbed a k'zayit of milk — since at absorption there was basar b'chalav issur, we consider all absorbed in it as issur because chanan; if returned and stuck into heter meat, since we do not know initial meat absorption — perforce we estimate against the whole spoon since all absorption became issur through chanan. And some say from Tur siman 107 removing a fly we need only sixty against the fly, not the spoon — specifically here in basar b'chalav we say chanan, unlike other issurim where Tur holds like R' Efrayim in siman 92; accordingly Shulchan Aruch wrote if the spoon is new or not ben yomo — an old meat spoon that today absorbed a k'zayit of milk — chanan does not apply as above since no issur here; Rama wrote per Shulchan Aruch that we do not say chanan regarding a vessel even if it absorbed issur — perforce one cannot say the vessel body becomes chanan; meaning not only new or old spoon etc. since still heter chanan does not apply, but even nevelah meat k'zayit — we do not say the new vessel becomes entirely nevelah — according to all; but in the latter part, old spoon ben yomo — meat spoon used today much with meat and absorbed a k'zayit of milk — there is absorbed issur, same day stuck into meat — all absorbed became nevelah; Rama concludes whatever the pot absorbed, even meat taste before milk — chanan requires estimating against the whole pot. 'There are those who say' Shulchan Aruch brings is Ran in name of Ramban — even meat spoon used today, absorbed k'zayit milk same day, stuck into meat — need only sixty against milk, not chanan in absorbed — all the more so vessel body.
