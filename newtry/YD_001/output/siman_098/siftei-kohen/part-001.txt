<b>איסור שנתערב כו' כגון חלב כו'. </b>משמע דהיינו חלב בציר"ה דהיינו תרבא וכן משמע בטור וא"כ דעת הט"ו דטעם חלב ובשר אינו שוה וכן משמע מדברי שאר פוסקים אבל ראב"ן כ' בתשובה בסי' ב' ובסוף עבודת כוכבים דחלב ובשר טעמם שוה וצ"ל דמיירי בבשר שמן ועיין בס"ק ו':
**** ENGLISH ****
Prohibited matter that became mixed, etc., such as milk, etc. It appears this means milk in broth — that is, fat (tarda) — and so too it appears in Tur; if so, the view of Turei Zahav is that the taste of milk and meat is not the same, and so too it appears from the words of other poskim. But Raavan wrote in a responsum siman 2 and at the end of Avodah Zarah that milk and meat have the same taste, and one must say it deals with fatty meat; see s.k. 6.

**** ENGLISH ****
Let a non-Jew taste it, etc. He must not know that we rely on him. In Beit Yosef he ruled that regarding a non-Jew who is not a kapila (meaning: a baker, a craftsman) we rely on him only in mashe'as l'fi tumo, and regarding a kapila we rely even if he is not mashe'as l'fi tumo, for he does not lie lest he lose his craft — and it has been explained to you that here it deals with a non-Jew who is not a kapila, and therefore he must not know that we rely on him. Nevertheless it is difficult to me: since he ruled in seif 2 that min she'eino mino is forbidden d'oraisa whenever it gives taste, how can a non-Jew be believed in mashe'as l'fi tumo? Behold, the Talmud is arranged in the last chapter of HaGozel (Bava Kama 114) that a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo is not believed in Torah prohibition except for a woman's testimony alone, and they ruled thus in Hagahot Maimoniot, Rif, and Rosh there, and Semag at the end of the laws of divorce (end of positive commandments 50). And even though from the words of Rambam and other poskim it also appears that we rely on a non-Jew who is not a kapila in mashe'as l'fi tumo, nevertheless it is possible they hold like Rashi and his supporters that min she'eino mino is nullified from the Torah in the majority — but on Mechaber it is difficult, and likewise on Rashba who also ruled that min she'eino mino is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty, and ruled that a non-Jew who is not a kapila is believed in mashe'as l'fi tumo, as brought in Beit Yosef. Again I found in Terumat HaDeshen siman 79 that he sensed something of this and wrote: a kapila is different, for his craft is in this and he does not impair his presumption — and so it is in Tur Yoreh De'ah, that the conclusion of Rosh is that we require both kapila and mashe'as l'fi tumo, unlike Rashba who holds that each one alone suffices. And if you ask, challenge Rashba why mashe'as l'fi tumo helps — one may say he holds like Rashi, etc. And it is astounding, for Rashba ruled explicitly in Taharat HaBayit 102a like Rabbenu Tam — see there — and it is forced to say that since he wrote "nevertheless regarding ruling the halachah we rely on Rabbenu Tam and Rabbenu Yitzchak and Baal HaHalachot and Raavad, who are many and said to be stringent in Torah matters" — if so he did not rule like them except because of stringency, and the lenient view helps regarding a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo. And furthermore, in the short Taharat HaBayit there he is entirely like Rabbenu Tam and did not mention the lenient view (and my son-in-law the exalted Aluf Mahar Gershon challenged likewise on Taharat HaBayit, for Rosh and his supporters require kapila — if so the reason is not because he does not impair presumption, for he is mashe'as l'fi tumo; rather the reason is that otherwise whence do we know he can distinguish taste — and so it appears in Beit Yosef, and so too in Taharat HaBayit 106b; if so the challenge returns to its place, for Rosh ruled like Rabbenu Tam and ruled that we rely on mashe'as l'fi tumo. [And furthermore] the reason must be because of mashe'as l'fi tumo alone — for if because he does not impair his craft, kapila alone should suffice — investigate). And furthermore, Mechaber's words are still not settled, for he ruled plainly in this work; and Maharshal in Pischei HaGolah siman 33 ruled that even nowadays we rely on a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo even if not a kapila; and in siman 46 there he brought what Rashba wrote "and they said to be stringent in Torah matters," and wrote on this: according to what I explained it is without stringency, for thus is the clear tradition that it is d'oraisa, etc. — see there. And some wished to resolve that what we say in HaGozel that a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo is not believed in other matters — that is where issur was established, unlike here where we do not know if there is any taste at all — and this is forced. And furthermore, there we challenge from that they permitted a captive on a minor mashe'as l'fi tumo, and answer: for a captive they were lenient — and now what is the challenge? For in a captive issur was not established. And even though Rivash siman 433 wrote on the law of a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo on gentile wine vessels of yayin nesech that passed twelve months that he is not believed — for even if we say regarding min she'eino mino that we rely on a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo even if not a kapila, nevertheless here is different, for issur was established, for certainly they used the vessel with prohibited wine, and you come to extract from his testimony a matter of certain prohibition — if so one must say Rivash holds like Rashi and his supporters; and therefore in rabbinic prohibition (for gentile vessels are forbidden only d'rabbanan, as below in the laws of wine vessels) one may distinguish thus, and as must be distinguished thus in the Rav's view as written at the end of siman 137 — see there; but in Torah prohibition certainly one cannot distinguish thus, and as I wrote. Therefore it appears to me to distinguish that specifically where there is no way to establish the matter, a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo is not believed in Torah prohibition; but in min she'eino mino, since there is a way to establish the matter by having a kapila taste it, we rely on him — and this distinction is proven in Rivash there — see there; and see siman 16 seif 11, siman 69 seif 10, siman 122 seif 1, end of siman 137, and what is written there.

And we are not accustomed nowadays to rely on a non-Jew, etc. Since he did not say "and we are not accustomed nowadays to rely on tasting," but said "and we are not accustomed nowadays to rely on a non-Jew," it appears specifically on a non-Jew we do not rely because he is a non-Jew; but regarding tasting by an Israelite we rely in min she'eino mino of heter — such as terumah that fell into hullin that a priest tastes for her, as stated in the Talmud and poskim; and even though terumah does not apply in our time, nevertheless there is relevance regarding one who says "Konam, meat and wine that I shall taste," and it fell into a dish forbidden to him b'noten taam — we rely on tasting by an Israelite, as below siman 217 s.k. 26. If a species became mixed in its species. The reason Rabbenu Tam wrote, and all poskim who stand in his method that taam ka'ikar is forbidden d'oraisa in min she'eino mino: since the prohibition gives taste in the heter, the heter becomes entirely prohibition and all of it is forbidden d'oraisa. But min b'mino, since it does not give taste in it, is nullified from the Torah in the majority, as written "follow the majority" — except that the Sages required sixty; and therefore when it is known that its majority was heter and the doubt was only whether there is sixty there or not, it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently; unlike when it was not known that its majority was heter, which is a Torah doubt and forbidden. But min she'eino mino, since it is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty, once it gives taste — even if it is known that its majority was heter and it is not known that there was sixty — it is a Torah doubt and forbidden. In view of this I wonder at what the Rav wrote: "and regarding min b'mino we follow the name," etc. — his view is that if it is equal in name even though not equal in taste, it is min b'mino and forbidden d'rabbanan; and if it is not equal in name even though equal in taste, it is min she'eino mino and forbidden from the Torah; and so too from the words of Maharshal in Avodat HaKodesh and in his book Pischei HaGolah end of siman 32. And behold it is explained in all poskim that therefore min she'eino mino is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty since it gives taste; and therefore min b'mino, which does not give taste, is nullified from the Torah in the majority as we follow the majority everywhere. If so the opposite is true — we all follow taste: if it is not equal in taste even though equal in name, nevertheless since it gives taste in heter and the heter becomes entirely prohibition, it is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty; and therefore in a spill we follow stringency. And if it is equal in taste even though not equal in name, nevertheless since it does not give taste it is nullified from the Torah as we follow the majority everywhere, as written "follow the majority" — and therefore in a spill we follow leniency. And even though we establish like Rava in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah 66 regarding new wine that we follow name and not taste regarding min b'mino — that is specifically regarding yayin nesech and tevel that forbid in a minute amount in min b'mino, and likewise regarding terumah and orlah whose measure is above sixty in its species; and therefore if it is equal in name even though not equal in taste it is min b'mino and forbidden in a minute amount or in 101 or 201; and if it is not equal in name even though equal in taste it is forbidden b'noten taam; and likewise according to R' Yehuda who said in all prohibitions min b'mino in a minute amount — and therefore in such a case there is no longer [reason] because of taam ka'ikar or "follow the majority." And so it is explicitly in Tosafot Pesachim 97a s.v. Amar Rava, etc., and in Ashiri, Raavan, and Ran at the end of Avodah Zarah, and all poskim — that this [sugya] of Rava deals with yayin nesech and tevel and their kind; if so one may say specifically in those, but not for us in other prohibitions, and regarding a spill (and these words reached several great ones of the generation and they agreed with my words, also Mahar Yehoshua said they are clear matters). And this is also proven in the words of Maharai in Taharat HaBayit that I brought in s.k. 2 — for otherwise it would not need to be said that Rashba holds like Rashi — see there and investigate. And therefore in all those cases there is no longer [reason] because of taam ka'ikar or "follow the majority"; but regarding other prohibitions that are forbidden in sixty, and regarding a spill whether d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — certainly everything depends on taste, as we said; also Bach and Etz Yosef followed the Rav's words. And there in that klal he wrote explicitly that we establish like Rabbenu Tam that min b'mino [requires] sixty d'rabbanan and not in its species sixty d'oraisa, and there is relevance if it spilled — end of his words. Rather the reason that not in its species, even in a dry thing that does not give taste, is stricter, because it is called a thing that has a remedy, for one can somewhat recognize the prohibition and remove it from there; and nevertheless it is well nullified in sixty, for since it is also minute and one does not find it, it is well called a thing that has no remedy. But in truth his reason is forced, and the reason is clear and explained in the words of Rosh in Pesachim and Ran there, and in Rashba's novellae there 105 at the top, and in Tur s.k. 9 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah and Rokeach siman 479, and in Maharil's responsum siman 194 and Ri"d, that min she'eino mino in dry [mixture] requires sixty for we are concerned lest they cook them and it will give taste and it is Torah prohibition; and even if the mixture was known before cooking, nevertheless since it gives taste afterward it is forbidden from the Torah, as stated there (and from these poskim it is also proven that we follow taste — investigate). And so too the Rav's view below siman 109 and all other Acharonim, unlike min b'mino; and therefore since the matter depends on taste, also in dry we follow taste — and all this is clear in my humble opinion.

But if it gave taste in the pot, etc., and therefore, etc. Meaning: that all prohibitions in the world are not ordinarily made for taste and are nullified in sixty, since they do not give taste in more than sixty except if its taste is sensed — then it is obvious it is forbidden, as Rosh, Rashba, and Ran wrote and Beit Yosef brings in this siman, and so too other poskim, and it is plain. And therefore since if its taste is sensed it is not nullified even in more than sixty, therefore salt and spices, etc., are not nullified even in a thousand, since ordinarily they give taste even in a thousand once they are made to give taste — for a thing made for taste is not nullified also because ordinarily it gives taste even in a thousand, as proven from the words of Sefer HaTerumah that Tur and Beit Yosef brought end of siman 105. And accordingly, where it is known clearly that it does not give taste — such as if a grain of salt or spice fell into a large cauldron — it is permitted; and so too it appears explicitly in Turei Chayim klal 85 law 20; and so I found (in Tosafot Avodah Zarah 66a) end of s.v. tavlin explicitly: "and that taste is not nullified — one need not say it is not nullified at all, but it is not nullified like other prohibitions, but it is certainly well nullified, for we know certainly that the taste was nullified, such as in a large cauldron" — end of his words; and so too in poskim.

<b>כל אחד מצטרף כו' לבטל חבירו כו'. </b>דסברא הוא דכל איסור בטל כשאינו יכול ליתן טעם בהיתר וטעם דם וחלב אינה שוה הרי נתבטל טעם החלב בס' וטעם הדם בס' דמה לי איסור המבטל איסור ומה לי היתר המבטל איסור רק שיתבטל טעם האיסור ולא יהא לו עוד כח ליתן טעם בהיתר עכ"ל הרא"ש:

<b>וכן כ"ט כו'. </b>בטור כתוב וכן שלשים ובש"ע הישנים מבלי הגהות כתוב וכן כ"ט זיתים וכן הוא בתשו' הרא"ש והוא יותר נכון דה"ל רבותא יותר ודוק ומ"מ צ"ע דהאי דינא לא שייך לפי מאי דקי"ל חנ"נ בכל האיסורים דכיון דנפל כזית דם לקדרה אחת ולא היה בו ס' נ"נ וכן הקדרה הב' מיהו רישא איכא לפרושי בנפלו הב' זיתים בבת אחת דכל אחד מבטל טעם חבירו אבל הכא דמיירי בב' קדרות קשה דלא ה"ל להרב לסתום הדברים ועוד שכתב וכ"ש בשני זיתים א' של גבינה כו' משמע דברישא מודה להמחבר והא ליתא למאי דקי"ל חנ"נ בכל האיסורים דפשיטא דהיינו אפי' בשוגג כדמוכח בכמה דוכתי וכדלקמן סי' צ"ט ס"ה בהדיא וי"ל דסתם הדברים לענין לח בלח דלא אמרי' חנ"נ בהפסד מרובה כדלעיל בסי' צ"ב ס"ד וכ"כ בהג"ה בסי' צ"ט ס"ה ועי"ל דהרב אזיל לטעמיה שפסק בת"ח הבאתיו לקמן סי' צ"ט ס"ק ט"ו דאם ניתוסף על האיסור בשוגג קודם שנודע התערובות לא אמרי' חנ"נ וא"צ אלא ס' נגד האיסור אם כן מיירי הכא שנתערבו ב' הקדרות יחד קודם שנודע התערובות אבל למאי דהוכחתי שם דאפי' לא נודע התערובות אמרי' חנ"נ א"כ לא שייך הא דינא בנפל לב' קדרות בפחות מס' אם לא בלח בלח בהפסד מרובה אבל מ"מ נ"מ לענין דכל א' מבטל טעם חבירו כשנפלו שניהם בבת אחת לנ"ט א"נ אם נפל כזית גבינה לכ"ט זיתים היתר ובקדרה אחרת נפל כזית בשר לשלשים זיתים של היתר ונתערבו אלו ב' התערובות בשוגג דמותר דלא שייך לומר חנ"נ כיון דעדיין כולו היתר וכדלעיל סי' צ"ד ס"ו וכמה דוכתי א"נ נ"מ אם נפל כזית דם לתוך ס' זיתים של היתר ואחר כך נפל כזית ועוד של חלב לאותן ס"א זיתים דא"צ ס' נגד ב' זיתים אלו של איסור אלא גם הכזית דם שנפל בראשונה מצטרף לבטל הכזית ועוד של חלב ואילו לא היינו מצטרפים הכזית של דם לא היה ס' לבטל הכזית ועוד והשתא דכל אחד מבטל טעם חברו גם הוא מצטרף לבטל מיהו כל זה דוקא בשני מיני איסורין שאין טעמם שוה אבל במין אחד לעולם חוזר וניעור וכדלקמן סימן צ"ט ס"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise twenty-nine, etc. In Tur it is written "and likewise thirty," and in old Shulchan Aruch without hagahot it is written "and likewise twenty-nine olives," and so too in Rosh's responsum — and this is more correct, for it is a greater novelty — investigate. And nevertheless it is uncertain, for this law is not relevant according to what we establish chein nafsho in all prohibitions, for since an olive's bulk of blood fell into one pot and there was not sixty it became nevelah, and so too the second pot; however in the first part one can explain when the two olive's bulks fell at once, that each nullifies the taste of the other; but here, dealing with two pots, it is difficult that the Rav should conceal the matters. And furthermore that he wrote "and all the more so with two olive's bulks, one of cheese," etc. — it appears in the first part he agrees to Mechaber; but this is not so according to what we establish chein nafsho in all prohibitions, for it is obvious this is even b'shogeg, as proven in several places and as below siman 99 seif 5 explicitly. And one may say the matters are stam regarding moist in moist, for we do not say chein nafsho with great loss, as above siman 92 seif 4, and so too in hagahah siman 99 seif 5. And one may explain the Rav follows his view that he ruled in Turei Chayim I brought below siman 99 s.k. 15: if more was added to the prohibition b'shogeg before the mixture was known, we do not say chein nafsho and one needs sixty against the prohibition — if so here it deals when two pots became mixed together before the mixture was known; but according to what I proved there that even if the mixture was not known we say chein nafsho — if so this law is not relevant when it fell into two pots in less than sixty, unless moist in moist with great loss. But nevertheless there is relevance regarding that each nullifies the taste of the other when both fell at once for noten taam; or alternatively if an olive's bulk of cheese fell into twenty-nine olive's bulks of heter and in another pot an olive's bulk of meat fell into thirty olive's bulks of heter and these two mixtures became mixed b'shogeg — it is permitted, for chein nafsho is not relevant since all is still heter, as above siman 94 seif 6 and several places. Or alternatively there is relevance if an olive's bulk of blood fell into sixty olive's bulks of heter and afterward an olive's bulk and more of milk fell into those sixty-one olive's bulks — one does not need sixty against these two olive's bulks of prohibition, but also the olive's bulk of blood that fell first combines to nullify the olive's bulk and more of milk; and if they did not combine, the olive's bulk of blood would not have had sixty to nullify the olive's bulk and more. And now that each nullifies the taste of the other, it also combines to nullify; however all this is specifically with two types of prohibitions whose tastes are not equal, but in one species it always returns and is sensed, as below siman 99 seif 6.
