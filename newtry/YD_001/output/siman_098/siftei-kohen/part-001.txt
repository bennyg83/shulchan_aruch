["<b>Let a non-Jew taste it for us, etc.</b> He must not know that we rely on him. Beit Yosef ruled that for a non-Jew who is not a kafila (meaning a baker, an expert) we rely on him only in mesiach l'fi tumo, and on a kafila we rely even if he is not mesiach l'fi tumo, for he will not lie lest he lose his craft — and it has been explained to you that here we deal with a non-Jew who is not a kafila, and therefore he must not know that we rely on him. Nevertheless it is difficult for me: since he ruled in s.k. 2 that min she'eino mino is forbidden d'oraisa whenever it gives taste — how can a non-Jew be trusted in mesiach l'fi tumo? Is not the entire Talmud arranged in the last chapter of HaGozel (Bava Kama 114) that a non-Jew is not trusted mesiach l'fi tumo regarding Torah prohibition except for a woman's testimony alone — and they ruled so in HaGaon, Rif, and Rosh there, and Semag at the end of Laws of Divorce (end of Asin 50)? And although from Rambam's words and other poskim it also appears that we rely on a non-Jew who is not a kafila in mesiach l'fi tumo, nevertheless it is possible they hold like Rashi and his supporters that min she'eino mino is nullified from the Torah in the majority — but on Mechaber it is difficult, and likewise on Rashba who also ruled that min she'eino mino is forbidden from the Torah until sixty, and ruled that a non-Jew who is not a kafila is trusted in mesiach l'fi tumo, as brought in Beit Yosef. Again I found in Terumat HaDeshen siman 79 that he sensed this somewhat and wrote: 'A kafila is different, for his craft is in this and it does not impair his presumption' — and so it is in Tur Yoreh De'ah, for Rosh's conclusion requires both kafila and mesiach l'fi tumo, not like Rashba who holds each alone suffices; and if you ask, Rashba should be challenged why mesiach l'fi tumo alone helps — one can say he holds like Rashi, etc. And it is astonishing, for Rashba ruled explicitly in Teshuvot HaRashba daf 102a like Rabbeinu Tam — see there — and it is strained to say that since he wrote 'nevertheless' regarding ruling halachah we rely on Rabbeinu Tam, R' Yehuda, Ba'al HaHalachot, and Ra'avad z"l who are many and said to be stringent in Torah matters — end — therefore he did not rule like them except out of stringency, and the lenient view helps regarding non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo. And furthermore in Terumat HaDeshen the short version there is completely like Rabbeinu Tam's view and did not mention the lenient view (and my father-in-law the eminent aluf HaRav R' Gershon n"r also challenged Terumat HaDeshen, for Rosh and his supporters require kafila — perforce the reason is not because it does not impair presumption, for he is mesiach l'fi tumo; rather the reason is that otherwise whence do we know he can distinguish taste — and so appears in Beit Yosef, and so in Teshuvot HaRashba daf 106b; and if so the difficulty returns to its place, for Rosh ruled like Rabbeinu Tam and ruled that we rely on mesiach l'fi tumo [and furthermore] perforce the reason is for mesiach l'fi tumo alone, for if because it does not impair his craft, kafila alone should suffice — investigate). And furthermore Mechaber's words are still not settled, for he stated plainly in this work the rulings — and so Maharshal Yoreh De'ah siman 33 ruled that even now we rely on non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo even if not kafila; and in siman 46 there he brought Rashba's words 'they said to be stringent in Torah matters' and wrote on it: per my explanation it is without stringency, for thus is the clear sugya that it is d'oraisa, etc. — see there. And some wished to resolve that what we say in HaGozel that non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo is not trusted regarding other matters — that is where issur was established, unlike here where we do not know if there is any taste at all — strained. And furthermore there we challenge from that they permitted a captive on a minor mesiach l'fi tumo and answer that regarding a captive they were lenient — now what is the challenge, for regarding a captive issur was not established. And although Rivash siman 233 wrote regarding the law of non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo on gentile wine-libation vessels that passed twelve months — he is not trusted, for even if we say regarding min she'eino mino we rely on non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo even if not kafila, nevertheless here is different where issur was established, for certainly they used the vessel with forbidden wine, and you come to extract from his testimony something certainly forbidden — end — perforce Rivash holds like Rashi and his supporters; therefore regarding d'rabbanan issur (for gentile vessels are forbidden only d'rabbanan, as below in laws of wine vessels) one may distinguish thus, and as one must distinguish thus per the Rav as stated end siman 137 — see there; but regarding d'oraisa issur certainly one cannot distinguish thus, as I wrote. Therefore it appears to me to distinguish that specifically where there is nothing to establish the matter, non-Jew mesiach l'fi tumo is not trusted regarding Torah prohibition; but in min she'eino mino, since there is something to establish the matter by having a kafila taste it, we rely on him. And this distinction is proven in Rivash there — see there; and see siman 16 seif 11, siman 69 s.y., siman 122 s.k. 1, end of siman 137, and what is written there:"]

["<b>If a species mixed in its species.</b> The reason: Rabbeinu Tam wrote, and all poskim who follow his approach, that we hold ta'am k'ikar — forbidden d'oraisa in min she'eino mino, for since the issur gives taste in the heter, the heter becomes entirely issur and is entirely forbidden d'oraisa. Therefore since it is known that its majority was heter and the doubt was only whether there are sixty or not — it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently. Unlike when it is not known that its majority was heter — it is a Torah doubt and forbidden. But min she'eino mino, since forbidden from the Torah until sixty once it is noten taam — even if known that its majority was heter and not known that there were sixty — it is a Torah doubt that is forbidden.
