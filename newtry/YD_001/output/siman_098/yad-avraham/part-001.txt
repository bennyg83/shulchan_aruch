**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 98 seif 1) An issur mixed into heter that is not its species — such as milk mixed with meat, "let a non-Jew taste it for us," etc. So too the Tur; so too the Beit Yosef in the name of Matza Katuv; and so too the Mordechai at the end of Chullin and in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah in the name of Rashi, that tallow in meat are two kinds. This is certainly proven from what Rashi wrote in the chapter Kol HaBasar (109), that the halachah follows R' Yehuda, who said min b'mino b'mashahu. And if a piece of meat was cooked in the milk attached to it, if there is not sixty in that piece alone against the milk, it becomes nevelah and forbids all the pieces b'mashahu, because they are its kind — as is proven from Hadich: if there had been sixty against the milk it would have been nullified; hence tallow in meat is she'eino mino. This is also proven from what the Mordechai wrote in chapter 3 regarding milk and meat that were salted together: according to Raavyah we do not estimate salting in sixty, but only kedei kelipah, for milk does not waft from piece to piece in salting. Nevertheless it implies that in the piece itself, where the milk is attached, it does waft, and we require sixty — as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 105, that this is Raavyah's view. Hence it is she'eino mino, for Raavyah holds like Rashi that min b'mino is not nullified, as the Mordechai wrote and as the Derishah brings here and the Beit Yosef in siman 73. It also truly appears to be so, for whether according to the one who says in Avodah Zarah (66) that for min b'mino we follow ta'am, or according to the one who says we follow shem, {Rama: and the Rama ruled so in the hagahah to seif 2,} we must count them as two kinds, for they are not equal in ta'am or in shem. However, in Chiddushei HaRan he wrote in the name of the Raavad that milk is called meat, and he brought proof from the end of chapter 3 that one is liable for it because of basar min hachai. But one may say that there it deals with eiver min hachai and not basar min hachai, as he himself wrote in that chapter. Nevertheless, it appears that this was Rashi's view, for in Pesachim (30) he explained that the halachah follows Rava, that min b'mino is b'mashahu; therefore if milk fell into a pot it forbids b'mashahu. This implies that he holds tallow in meat is min b'mino. And so too in the chapter Keitzad (108a) regarding fat with meat — unlike what he wrote in Kol HaBasar (109), and in Shiltei HaGiborim chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, where he expanded on this — see there — he did not notice that Rashi contradicts himself. The Maharshal emended Rashi in Pesachim, and was followed in Sefer Ateret Aharon, and they did not notice Rashi in Chullin (108). But one may say that he holds like the Raavad; if so, according to those who follow shem it is mino, and in Keitzad he deals with those who follow ta'am. This is comparable to what Rashi wrote in Pesachim regarding fat that is called milk, and likewise meat fat that is called milk. On this basis one may resolve what the Mahari Katz challenged in Machatzit HaShekel on Zevachim (110), that milk and meat are min she'eino mino — how can we hold here in any case that it is she'eino mino? According to what was said above, one may say that there it is according to those who follow shem, whereas the poskim here, regarding bittul, dealt with the matter and hold that we follow ta'am. But the Raavan wrote the opposite: they are equal in shem, but not in ta'am. According to what was said above, one may say regarding what Chiddushei HaRan challenged in Chullin (96b) about Shmuel, who permits a roasted thigh with the gid, from Rabbah, who forbids a kid roasted in its milk: it implies there that if there had been sixty it would be permitted even according to Shmuel, who holds min b'mino b'mashahu. And according to Rabbah he wrote that he holds it is nullified; but for Shmuel it is difficult, for milk and meat are min she'eino mino. According to what was said above, one may say that min she'eino mino is according to those who follow shem, whereas Shmuel and Rabbah possibly hold that we follow ta'am; and so too Tosafot in Avodah Zarah (56b). The mishna: one who vows from meat is permitted in gravy and kifah — the Rosh explained fine particles of meat and fat that are not meat. One may say that vows are different, for there we follow the language of the one who vowed, and in his language they are not called meat. But accordingly there is a question on the words of the Mahari Katz that the Beit Yosef brought. In any event, one may also resolve what the Shach challenged there regarding those who hold that milk and meat are min b'mino: how was the fat of the gid nullified in sixty, according to Rashi that min b'mino is b'mashahu? He left it as tzarich iyun. According to what was said above, one may say that there is a nafka mina: it is certainly not their intent to say that Torah milk, such as the milk of a korban and the like, or thin fat, should be called a species of meat, as is proven from the poskim mentioned above. Rather, they mean only that milk attached to a piece of meat, and likewise the fat of the ribs and the meat, are called a species of meat — they are b'sar shuman. This is proven explicitly in the Gemara in Keitzad (108), in what they answered: "Here we are dealing with thick gravy." Rashi and the Ran explained: fine particles of meat and fat — all of it is min b'saro. Behold, it is explicitly clear that the fat of meat is mino with the meat, for meat fat is so called. If so, likewise the milk of meat is mino with the meat. If so, the fat of the gid that is attached to the gid is called the fat of the gid, and it is mino with the gid; whereas the gid and meat are two kinds, as we say: one who vows from the gid is permitted in meat — Yoreh De'ah siman 217. See in Machatzit HaShekel, which the Beit Yosef brought, that he also brought proof for she'eino mino from vows — see there. This appears clear to me. Accordingly, do not challenge them from what the Gemara says in several places that milk and meat are estimated in sixty, such as in chapter 3 (98a), and as Sefer Agudat Esev wrote: there it deals with korban milk and the like, which is not attached to meat. But milk that is attached by its nature to meat is called b'sar shuman according to them. Know that all this is according to the Rama's ruling nearby, that we follow shem; therefore, since it is called b'sar shuman, it is mino with the meat. But according to those who follow ta'am, certainly all milk does not have a taste equal to meat — although the Raavan in siman 20 wrote that milk and meat are not equal in shem but are equal in ta'am. Therefore the Mechaber in Shulchan Aruch, where he deals with tasting the issur well, wrote "such as milk mixed with meat" — all agree; investigate. These words of the Mechaber are the statement of Rava in Chullin (97): min she'eino mino is by ta'am; min b'mino cannot be established on taste in sixty. He must mean she'eino mino, whose tastes are not equal, for one can establish the matter upon it; whereas min b'mino means that their tastes are equal, so one cannot establish the matter upon it, and we estimate in sixty. This is not the same as what Rava said in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah (66), that we follow shem; there he deals with issurim where we do not follow ta'am or sixty, but rather forbid b'mashahu in mino — as the Shach wrote. In this way Rava's words do not contradict each other; and so too the Peri Chadash. Nor is it difficult what Tosafot wrote in chapter 21 and in chapter 3, and R' Efrayim: since we follow shem, one would not expound min b'mino; the truth is that in such a case, where we estimate in sixty, we do not follow shem. Know that the Minchat Yitzchak in general rule 14 left this as tzarich iyun. According to what was said above, regarding what the Tur wrote in siman 123 — that challah mixed into other loaves, i.e. wheat into barley, is nullified in sixty: challah and terumah are from those whose measure is above sixty; if so, we follow shem, and both are called loaves, as we say regarding chametz, that it is called min b'mino even for wheat in barley. One may say that he holds as Issur VeHeter wrote in general rule 23 in the name of the Rashba: a d'rabbanan issur that has no root in the Torah, such as shehiyah, gerama, and challah — and likewise challat chutz la'aretz, if mixed, is nullified in rov; and it too is a beriyah — as the Chavot Yair wrote accordingly. And the Tur deals with challat chutz la'aretz; therefore even a whole loaf is nullified. That he wrote sixty is because he deals with she'eino mino; and likewise in mino, in 101, according to his view even challat chutz la'aretz requires 101 in mino. There is no distinction regarding a davar chashuv, since it has no root from the Torah.

(סעיף ט') <b>קדירה שיש בה נ"ט זיתים כו'.</b> נלע"ד דדין זה דאיסורין שאין טעמם שוה מבטלין זא"ז. לא שייך אלא בשני מיני איסורין של שני שמות. כמו חלב ודם שכתבו הרא"ש והט"ו. אבל באיסור של שם א' אף שאין טעמם שוה ואין נרגש בקדירה כגון חלב ובשר של נבילה או טמאה (עי' רמב"ם פ"ב מהל' מ"א ופ"ד ופ"ז. ועיין ט"ז ר"ס ס"ד). מ"מ א"א שיהא א' מבטל לחבירו. כיון דהכל איסור א' הוא. צריך ס' לבטלו ואי אין ס' אף שאין הטעם נרגש ה"ל כמין במינו דאף שאין טעמו נרגש בעינן ס' ודוק. וכן טריפה ונבילה מצטרף למלקות כמ"ש הרמב"ם בהל' מ"א מפני שטריפה תחלת נבילה היא וכן מוכח בפ"ב דערלה מ"ב שהוא מקור דין זה דקתני התם הערלה מעלה את הכלאים כו' והערלה את הערלה. וכתב הרע"ב דהיינו שאין איסורן משם א' כגון ערלה ונטע רבעי. דאי איסורן משם א' א"א שיהא מבטל קצתו לקצתו עכ"ל. וכן מוכח מהא דקתני התם מ"ו ז' דשנים או שלשה שמות ממין א' או ב' מינים משם א' אסור ומצטרפין זה עם זה וכיון דמצטרפין לאסור ולמלקות א"א שיהא מבטלין זא"ז. וכדמוכח להדיא בתוספות דזבחים (דף ע"ח) ד"ה והפיגול לשנויא בתרא ודוק היטב. ועי' תוס' כריתות (דף י"א) וצ"ע בתוס' שבת (פ"ט ב') ומיהו לדעת הראב"ד פי"ד מהל' מ"א הלכה ו' אין מצטרף למלקות כשחלוק בטעמו וא"כ אפשר שמבטל זא"ז. ועי' בר"ש שלא פי' כהרע"ב אלא דערלה מעלה ערלה כשהיתה לו ידיעה והמפרש כתב שם דב' מינין שאין שוין בטעם ובשר מבטלין זא"ז אף שהן שם איסור א': <b>ונ"ל</b> דזהו כוונת הטור שמביא בס"ס זה הא דאמר ר"ל בזבחים (דף ע"ח) הפגול והנותר והטמא שבללן זה בזה ואכלן פטור. וז"ל הטור וכן ג' מיני איסורים של יבש כו' ותמה הב"י דלמה ליה להטור לכתוב מה שאין נפקותא האידנא. [ומ"ש בשו"ת עבודת הגרשוני סי' ל' דגירסת הטור וב"י הוא ר' יוחנן ולכן פסק כוותיה נגד ר"א. ק"ל דהא מסיים ש"מ תלת וא' מהן דה"ס ל"ש התראה ואלו ר"י ס"ל שמה התראה בריש שבועות] ונ"ל דהטור הביא זה כדי ללמוד מזה דבעינן שיהא נתערב בהיתר ב' מיני איסורים משני שמות דאז מבטלין זה את זה ועי' מ"ש בס"ד בסי' צ"ט סעיף ו' כוונת הטור עוד בזה. ונלע"ד מן ההכרח לומר כן מהא דאמרינן בש"ס פג"ה (דף צ"ז) גיד בס' ואין גיד מן המנין. וכתב הרא"ש דס"ל יש בגידין בנותן טעם. והשתא קשה דלמה לא יהיה הגיד מן המנין שהרי צריך לשער ס' נגד הגיד ונגד שמנו שהרי קאי על מתניתין (דף צ"ו) דירך שנתבשל בה גיד הנשה דמיירי ששמנו ג"כ נתבשל כדמוכח להדיא בתוס' ר"פ ג"ה (דף פ"ט) ד"ה אם יש כו' ע"ש. והרשב"א בתה"א וש"פ וא"כ אמאי לא יהיה הגיד סיוע לבטל שמנו ושמנו יבטל טעם הגיד. שהרי אין טעם גיד שוה לטעם שמנו. אלא מוכרח דה"ט שהשומן איסורו משום הגיד וא"א שיהא מבטל זא"ז. וכ"מ בתב"ש סי' ל"ג שכ"כ בשם הב"ח. וכ"כ הפמ"ג בפתיחה לה"ת. ומיהו כתב דז"א רק מדרבנן אבל מה"ת שרי כל שאין טעמו נרגש. אבל באו"ה כלל כ"ד משמע דאף מדרבנן שרי ולא כתב הרמב"ם דמצטרף אלא עצמותו אבל בטעמי' לא שייך צרוף וכל שאין בו טעם מותר. וכ"כ בתשובת עבודת הגרשוני סי' ל'. ועי' בס' ח"ד סי' צ"ב ס"ק ט"ו שכתב דמשמע באו"ה דמצטרף לאיסור וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 9) A pot that has noten taam olives, etc. In my humble opinion, this law that issurim whose tastes are not equal nullify one another applies only to two kinds of issurim of two names, such as milk and blood, as the Rosh and the Taz wrote. But regarding one issur of one name — even though their tastes are not equal and the taste is not sensed in the pot, such as milk and meat of nevelah or tamei meat — see the Rambam in chapters 2, 4, and 7, and the Taz in siman 64 — nevertheless it is impossible for one to nullify the other, since all of it is one issur; it requires sixty to nullify it. And if there is no sixty, even though the taste is not sensed, it is like min b'mino: even though its taste is not sensed, we require sixty; investigate. Likewise tereifah and nevelah combine for lashes, as the Rambam wrote in Hilchot Ma'achalot Assurot, because tereifah is the beginning of nevelah. This is also proven in chapter 2 of Orlah (42), which is the source of this law, for the mishna there teaches: orlah raises kilayim, etc., and orlah with orlah. The Raavad wrote that this means that their issur is not from one name, such as orlah and neta revai; but if their issur is from one name, it is impossible for part of it to nullify part — end of his words. This is also proven from what the mishna teaches there (46:7): two or three names from one species, or two kinds from one name, are forbidden and combine with one another; and since they combine to forbid and for lashes, it is impossible for them to nullify one another. This is proven explicitly in Tosafot in Zevachim (78), s.v. vehapigul, in the later version; investigate well. See Tosafot in Keritut (11); and tzarich iyun in Tosafot in Shabbat (89b). However, according to the Raavad in chapter 4, halachah 16 of Hilchot Ma'achalot Assurot, they do not combine for lashes when they are divided in taste; if so, it is possible that they nullify one another. See the Rosh, who did not explain like the Raavad, but rather that orlah raises orlah when there was knowledge; and the commentator wrote there that two kinds that are not equal in taste, issurim nullify one another even though they are one issur in name. It appears to me that this is the intent of the Tur, who brings at the end of this siman what R' Levi said in Zevachim (78): pigul, notar, and tamei that one blended with one another and ate are exempt. The Tur wrote: "And likewise three kinds of dry issurim," etc. The Beit Yosef wondered why the Tur needed to write what has no practical relevance nowadays. [And what was written in the responsa Avodat HaGereshuni siman 30, that the text of the Tur and Beit Yosef is R' Yochanan, and therefore he ruled like him against R' Elazar — investigate, for we conclude three items, and one of them lacks the language of a lash warning; and R' Yochanan holds that a warning of doubt is a warning, at the beginning of Shevuot.] It appears to me that the Tur brought this in order to teach from it that we require that two kinds of issurim of two names be mixed into heter; then they nullify one another. See what I wrote below in siman 99 seif 6 regarding the Tur's further intent in this. It appears to me necessarily to say so from what we say in the Gemara in chapter 3 of Chullin (97): the gid in sixty, and the gid is not counted. The Rosh holds that there is noten taam in gidin. Now it is difficult: why should the gid not be counted, since we need to estimate sixty against the gid and against its fat? For it deals with the mishna regarding a thigh in which the gid of the sciatic nerve was cooked, where its fat was also cooked, as is proven explicitly in Tosafot in chapter 3 (89), s.v. im yesh — see there. The Rashba in his responsa and at the beginning of the siman: if so, why should the gid not assist to nullify its fat, and its fat nullify the taste of the gid? For the taste of the gid is not equal to the taste of its fat. Rather, it is necessary to say that the fat is forbidden because of the gid, and it is impossible for them to nullify one another. And so too in Teshuvot Beit Shmuel siman 33, as the Bach wrote; and so too the Peri Megadim in his introduction to Torah Temimah. However, he wrote that this nullification applies only d'rabbanan, but from the Torah it is permitted as long as the taste is not sensed. But in Issur VeHeter general rule 24 it appears that even d'rabbanan it is permitted; and the Rambam wrote that they combine only regarding the bones themselves, but regarding tastes there is no combination, and anything that has no taste is permitted. And so too in the responsa Avodat HaGereshuni siman 30. See in Chochmat Adam siman 92, s.k. 15, where he wrote that it appears from Issur VeHeter that they combine to forbid; tzarich iyun.
