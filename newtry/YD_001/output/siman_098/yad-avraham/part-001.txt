(Siman 98 seif 1) Issur mixed into heter min she'eino mino — such as milk mixed into meat, have a non-Jew taste it etc. So too Tur, and so Beit Yosef in name of Matza Kotzo. So too Mordechai end of Chullin and Avodah Zarah ch. 2 in name of Rashi — broth in meat are two species. And this is certainly proven from Rashi Kol HaBasar 109 — halachah follows R Yehuda who said min b'mino b'mashahu; if a meat piece cooked in milk attached to it, if there is not sixty in it alone against the milk, it becomes nevelah and forbids all pieces b'mashahu because they are its species. Proven in reverse — if there were sixty against the milk it would be nullified; apparently broth in meat is min she'eino mino. And so proven from Mordechai PG"H regarding milk and meat salted together — Raaviyah does not estimate salting at sixty but only a peel, for milk does not spread from piece to piece in salting; nevertheless in the piece itself where milk is attached it spreads and we require sixty, as Beit Yosef siman 105 wrote this is Raaviyah's view — apparently it is min she'eino mino, for Raaviyah holds like Rashi that min b'mino is not nullified, as Mordechai wrote and Derishah uPerishah brought in this siman and Beit Yosef siman 73.

And it truly appears to say so, for whether one holds in Avodah Zarah 66 that for min b'mino we follow taste, or there we follow name — hagahah seif 2 ruled thus: we must count them as two species, for they are not equal in taste or name. However Chavot Yair wrote in name of Raavad that milk is called meat and brought proof from end of Chullin that one is liable for it as meat of five — but one can say there it deals with limb of five not meat of five as he wrote himself at end. Nevertheless it appears this was Rashi's view, for in Pesachim 30 he explained halachah follows Rava min b'mino b'mashahu — therefore if milk fell into a pot it forbids b'mashahu; apparently he holds broth in meat is min b'mino. And so Chullin 108a regarding fat with meat — unlike what he wrote Kol HaBasar 109 and Shiltei HaGiborim Avodah Zarah ch. 2 expanded on this see there, and did not notice Rashi contradicts himself. Maharshal emended Rashi Pesachim and Avodat HaGershuni followed him and they did not notice Rashi Chullin 108. But one can say he holds like Raavad — if so, for one who follows name it is its species, and in Chullin 108 he deals with one who follows taste. And compare to Rashi Pesachim regarding fat called milk — likewise fatty meat is called milk.

And accordingly one can say what Magen Avraham challenged in Zevachim 110 — milk and meat are min b'eino mino, yet how can one hold here in any event it is min she'eino mino? And per his words it works out — there is for one who follows name, and poskim here regarding nullification dealt with this and hold we follow taste. But Raavan wrote the opposite — in name they are not equal, only in taste.

And per his words it works out what Chiddushei HaRashash challenged PG"H 96b — Shmuel permits a thigh roasted with gid hanasheh from R Hiyya who forbids a kid roasted in its milk — apparently there if there were sixty it would be permitted even for Shmuel, yet he holds min b'mino b'mashahu. Per R Hiyya he wrote he holds it is nullified, but for Shmuel it is difficult for milk and meat are min b'eino mino. Per his words it works out — being min b'eino mino is for one who follows name; Shmuel and R Hiyya perhaps hold we follow taste. And so Tosafos Avodah Zarah 56b.

And what we learned — one who vows from meat is permitted in gravy and kipah — Rosh explained fine particles of meat and fat which is not meat; one can say nedarim are different for we follow the language of people and in people's language they did not call it meat. But accordingly Magen Avraham's words which Beit Yosef brought are difficult.

In any event one can also resolve what Shitah Mekubetzet challenged there per those who hold milk and meat are min b'mino — how was gid hanasheh fat nullified at sixty per Rashi min b'mino b'mashahu? He left it undecided. Per his words one can say there is what to divide — they certainly did not intend to say Torah milk such as korban milk and similar fine fats should be called meat species, as proven from the poskim above. Rather they mean milk attached to a meat piece and similarly rib fat and meat are called meat species — they are fatty meat. And this is proven explicitly in Gemara Chullin 108 in what they answer here — in what do we deal? In thick gravy. Rashi and Ran explained fine particles of meat and fat — all is meat species — end of his words. Behold explicitly proven meat fat is its species with meat, for fatty meat is so called. And likewise meat milk is its species with meat. And gid fat attached to gid is called gid fat and is its species with gid; gid and meat are two species, as we say one who vows from gid is permitted in meat — YD siman 217. See Magen Avraham Beit Yosef brought who also brought proof for min she'eino mino from nedarim see there. This appears clear to me.

And accordingly do not challenge them from what can be proven in Gemara in many places that milk and meat are estimated at sixty, such as PG"H 98a and as Agudas Azov wrote — there it deals with korban milk and similar not attached to meat; but milk attached in its product to meat is called fatty meat according to them. Know that all this is per hagahah's ruling nearby that we follow name; therefore since fatty meat is so called it is its species with meat. But for one who follows taste — certainly all milk taste is not equal to meat (but Raavan siman 20 wrote milk and meat are not equal in name and equal in taste). Therefore the Mechaber Shulchan Aruch dealing with tasting the issur well wrote such as milk mixed into meat — and it comes out for all opinions — investigate. The author's words here are Rava's statement in Chullin 97 — min she'eino mino by taste; min b'mino where taste cannot be established is at sixty. Perforce he means min she'eino mino whose tastes are not equal, for it can be established; and min b'mino means their tastes are equal where taste cannot be established — we estimate at sixty. This is not what Rava said in Avodah Zarah ch. 2 (66) that we follow name — there he deals with prohibitions where we do not follow taste or sixty but forbid in any amount min b'mino. As Shach wrote, and with this Rava's words do not contradict each other. And so Peri Chadash; and there is no difficulty in what Tosafos wrote in Pesachim and Chullin, and R' Efrayim — since we follow name, he would not have called it min b'mino in truth, for in such a case where we estimate at sixty we do not follow name.

Know that Magen Avraham general rule 84 left undecided — per this, what Tur wrote siman 323 regarding challah mixed in other loaves — wheat in barley — nullifies at sixty, for challah and terumah are from those whose measure is above sixty, and if so we follow name and both are called loaves as we say regarding chametz called min b'mino even wheat in barley. One can say he holds as Issur VeHeter general rule 23 in name of Rashba that rabbinic issur with no root in Torah — such as basar shehora, gid hanasheh, challah, and likewise Eretz Yisrael challah — if mixed is nullified in majority — and so Chavot Yair end. And Tur deals with challah of Eretz Yisrael and therefore even an entire loaf is nullified; and that he wrote sixty is because he deals with min she'eino mino, and likewise in min b'mino at 1001 per his view that even challah of Eretz Yisrael requires 1001 min b'mino, and there is no difference regarding an important thing since it has no Torah root:

(There) If it is min b'mino. Such as milk mixed with fat of Tur. And even though hagahah seif 2 wrote that for min b'mino we follow name — nevertheless milk and fat are one name, for fat is also called milk and 'fat' is only rabbinic language, such as two pieces one of milk and one of fat where perforce it calls it fat next to milk. So too Rashi Pesachim (daf 45) regarding fat on the lung wall which is a tube of fat connected to the heart — included in heart fat called milk, as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Matza Kotzo; and so Rashi and Tosafos Bechoros (daf 10) see there; and in Ma'adanei Yom Tov likewise from his own reasoning and he did not mention Rashi Pesachim I mentioned. (seif 9) A pot containing fifty-nine olives etc. It appears to me for practical halachah that this rule — issurim whose tastes are not equal nullify one another — applies only to two types of issur from two names, such as milk and blood as Rosh and Taz wrote. But regarding issur of one name, even if their tastes are not equal and are not detectable in the pot — such as milk and meat of nevelah or tamei (see Rambam ch. 2, 4, and 7 Ma'achalos Assuros; see Taz end s.k. 64) — nevertheless it is impossible for one to nullify the other, since all are one issur; sixty is required to nullify it, and if there is not sixty, even if taste is not detectable, it is like min b'mino — even if taste is not detectable we require sixty — investigate carefully.

Raavad wrote this means their issur is not from one name, such as orlah and neta revai; but if their issur is from one name it is impossible for part to nullify part — end of his words. And so proven from there mishnah 6:7 — two or three names from one species or two species from one name are forbidden and combine with each other; and since they combine for prohibition and lashes it is impossible they nullify one another. And as proven explicitly in Tosafos Zevachim 78 s.v. vehapigul lenunya batra — investigate well. See Tosafos Kerisos 11 and undecided in Tosafos Shabbos 9b. However per Raavad ch. 14 Ma'achalos Assuros halachah 6 they do not combine for lashes when divided in taste — if so perhaps they nullify one another. See Rashi who did not explain like Raavad but orlah raises orlah when he had knowledge; and the commentator wrote there two species not equal in taste and meat nullify one another even though they are one issur name.

And it appears to me this is Tur's intent in bringing at end of this s.k. what R Levi said in Zevachim 78 — pigul, notar, and tamei blended together and eaten — exempt. And these are Tur's words — and likewise three types of dry issurim etc. Beit Yosef wondered why Tur wrote what has no practical application today. [And what Avodat HaGershuni responsum 30 wrote — Tur and Beit Yosef's text is R Yochanan and therefore ruled like him against R Eliezer — investigate, for he concludes three and one of them is lacking the language of warning, while R Yochanan holds the name of warning at the beginning of Shevuos.] And it appears Tur brought this to teach that we require mixed into heter two types of issur from two names — then they nullify one another; see what he wrote in seif 4 siman 99 seif 6 on Tur's intent further in this.

And it appears to me for practical halachah it is necessary to say thus from what we say in the Gemara Chullin 97 — gid at sixty and gid is not counted. Rosh wrote he holds gid has noten ta'am. Now it is difficult — why should gid not be counted, for one must estimate sixty against the gid and against its fat, since it refers to the mishnah 96 — a spoon in which gid hanasheh was cooked, dealing with its fat also cooked as proven explicitly in Tosafos beginning Chullin 89 s.v. im yesh — see there. Rashba in Teshuvos and Shitah — if so why should gid not assist to nullify its fat and its fat nullify gid taste, since gid taste is not equal to fat taste? Rather it is forced that fat's issur is because of the gid and they cannot nullify one another. And so in Taz siman 33 as Bach wrote. And so Pri Megadim in Petichah to Halachos Ta'aruvos. However he wrote this nullification is only rabbinic, but from Torah it is permitted whenever taste is not detectable. But Issur VeHeter general rule 24 implies even rabbinically permitted; and Rambam wrote combination only of bones, but tastes do not combine and whatever has no taste is permitted.
