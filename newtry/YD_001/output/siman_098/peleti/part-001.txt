"Milk in meat" — see Shach that milk and meat are not equal in taste; and so too Peri Chadash that even regarding min b'mino it is called meat and they are equal in name, nevertheless regarding sixty/taste they differ. In our sugya gid hanesheh 96a it appears in Gemara and Tosafos the reverse: milk and meat are not equal in name, but fatty meat taste and milk are equal — Tosafos in chapter gid hanesheh 96a s.v. she'ani challenged on R' Hillel's ruling that a kid roasted in its milk is forbidden even from the tip of its ear, and established the case is different because milk spreads; they challenged: gid fat also spreads — it is proven they hold even in taste they are equal, for otherwise what is the question, and is it certain it spreads? One can say it spreads; therefore according to Shulchan Aruch below 105, even though there is sixty, nevertheless removal is required lest it not spread throughout; see Chullin 100 where Tosafos wrote some doubt on milk spreading — see there; and only from safek we forbid, for it is not inferior to nishpakh, and this is Torah prohibition stringently; but gid fat is rabbinic and only holy Israel practiced prohibition — if so, even if spilled, nevertheless permitted, and a light prohibition safek is lenient; and one must say they hold milk in meat is min b'mino; and if so when we doubt, behold min b'mino even Torah safek is lenient as below in nishpakh; and certainly it spreads and the question resolves well. But apparently there is contradiction — if so why does Gemara challenge from Rav Huna who forbids kid roasted in milk vs R' Yehuda who says peel and eat — lest this spreading milk is not certain as written; and R' Yehuda in his view holds min b'mino is batel — it is rabbinic safek that milk in meat is min b'mino and permitted; but Rav Huna, student of Rav, holds like Rav, like the Shabbos sugya — since min b'mino is not batel, it is Torah safek and forbidden. That question is not difficult — lest there was sixty and R' Hillel forbids who holds min b'mino not batel — Gemara knows an unstressed kid is not sixty against its milk. But the aforementioned is difficult — must be milk and meat unequal in name (this is called milk and this is meat) but their taste in fatty meat is equal as Shach wrote fatty meat; if so Tosafos' words are correct and Gemara's question is fine — R' Hillel holds Torah prohibition that for min b'mino not batel we follow name — why then does R' Hillel forbid min b'mino not batel since called not its kind; but above Tosafos hold for taste it is min b'mino — if we doubt whether it spreads it is permitted since it depends on taste and taste is min b'mino — investigate. With this one can resolve — Gemara begins challenging on Shmuel's matter from R' Hillel who forbids roasted kid, and after answering milk spreads challenges R' Yehuda who permitted in such case — and calls out why challenge Shmuel since R' Yehuda agrees with him and has almost no continuation in Gemara; and as I wrote it is fine Gemara did not want to challenge R' Yehuda lest milk and meat are one species even in name per Raavad, Rashba, and Ran — difficult — as I wrote R' Yehuda who holds min b'mino permitted was lenient and R' Hillel about min b'mino not batel was stringent; but now that they answered milk spreads and gid fat is lean as Tosafos wrote, Gemara holds since in any event it is milk only that it does not spread so much to spread throughout all noten taam completely, nevertheless something spreads and this will not escape — therefore question on Shmuel who holds min b'mino in any amount — one must say milk and fat regarding name are called not its kind — if so R' Hillel challenges R' Yehuda — investigate. However per this reasoning it is difficult what Tosafos challenged that gid fat spreads — explain gid fat is rabbinic and need not all this; for us one must say in truth what we say spreads does not spread in all noten taam only in any amount — only Tosafos' question that a thing forbidden in the measure of removal returns and forbids, and so all in their place, and no need for Tosafos' explanation that the forbidden etc., since any amount of issur goes with it and milk spreads that any amount goes with it and therefore forbidden; but gid for Shmuel is no question since Shmuel does not hold piece chein nafsho and it is not applicable that the peel returns and forbids — if so no question and requires study; I wrote above siman 92; one must say in Chullin 97b Ravina supports from that statement — they taught only when cooked etc.; if so question on Ravina who certainly holds chein nafsho — one must say it does not exist and Tosafos' words are necessary that all agree gid fat does not spread — investigate.

Regarding min b'mino we follow the name, etc. Shach extensively disagreed with Rama on matters of taste, and even the proof he brought from Issur VeHeter — who wrote that since we estimate that even min b'mino wet is in sixty there is no difference which is called its species or not its species — learn there is no difference regarding nishpakh — is not conclusive proof, for Maharshal also wrote thus although he holds like Rama, and he does not rest on this; nevertheless Shach's words are reasonable. And at first glance it also appears like Rama from Rosh and Rashba who wrote in min she'eino mino and nishpakh per Rashi's view that ta'am k'ikar is not d'oraisa — permitted; and difficult — one can taste the remainder through kafila, and where there is doubt to clarify, rabbinic safek does not apply as is known; and even for us who do not rely on kafila — that is to be stringent, but Heaven forbid to be lenient. And per Rama it fits that it deals when equal in taste and differ in name — and if so it is min she'eino mino and impossible to taste. However with all this it is strained to say this is d'oraisa forbidden, for in full min she'eino mino if they taste and there is no taste it is permitted — and 'there is no taste' clearly has one reason; and perhaps one can say d'rabbanan like Torah until for those who hold ta'am k'ikar d'oraisa even this they made like Torah that rabbinic safek does not help leniently — and this is strained. But from Tosafot and Rosh who explained on that which purging is done daily, etc. — it is not noten taam bar noten taam, for in Temple vessels the Torah established min b'mino nullified in majority — difficult: forever when he cooked ox meat, does he return to cook ox meat specifically and not lamb and ram and similar from the altar — this thing is impossible; and if so behold it is in oil not its species. In truth Issur VeHeter distinguishes whole offerings from pieces, but Derishah uPerishah already challenged (and so appears somewhat in responsum Maharil siman 214); and if so difficult; and Rosh explains that which R' Elazar said pigul and leftover that were mixed, etc. — he thought it deals with min she'eino mino — wrote sacrifice and meal-offering and did not explain plainly ox and ram; or regarding noten taam we do not follow name, and regarding nishpakh wrote Rosh and Rashba — see what I wrote in Kereti. Salt and spices — behold Issur VeHeter's view in general rule 25: if spice fell into its species we do not say to estimate as if it fell into not its species, only permitted in sixty like other prohibitions; proof from 'brine in two hundred.' And if it fell into not its species — behold salt works for taste and is not nullified even in a thousand. One must understand whence this — for all agree taste must be noticeable; and perhaps the Sages established that brine's taste is not more noticeable than two hundred — and one must say they hold: if so why does the Gemara challenge R' Yehuda that min b'mino is not nullified — perhaps what R' Yochanan said 'in two hundred' is in min she'eino mino; or this the Gemara knew its measure is greater — perforce in its species it deals — and why need we say perhaps the Gemara knew in not its species its measure is less — certainly they were not more stringent in its species than not its species, for the whole concern is lest one come to taste in not its species — end. Rather the Rav author of Magen Avraham in Turei Chayim general rule 85 raised two objections: if so how did they say regarding brine in two hundred it is min b'mino and does not give taste and they decreed lest not its species — behold they hold we do not decree; contradiction is from him and in him. And furthermore he challenged how he wrote in not its species noten taam more than 101 — behold Issur VeHeter wrote chapter 23 law 6 that brine in a pot forbids only until sixty — see there; and it appears for resolution the first difficulty — and did he not notice what Issur VeHeter wrote in chapter 23 law 6 explicitly that brine of impure fish with pure fish is nullified in sixty — difficult, R' Yochanan holds in two hundred and R' Yehuda in 101 and sixty — what need here. And also wrote that brine even of nevelah mixed in a pot in sixty — for even R' Yochanan said only in its species; and at first glance difficult — behold R' Yochanan agrees regarding brine that was secretion that it is nullified — and if so all stringency is because not its species — how can he be lenient in not its species; but the matter is explained in Mordechai chapter 3 — since it is its species in min b'mino and generally R' Yochanan holds it is not nullified at all, only since brine is d'rabbanan they were lenient in it and not so much lenient — nevertheless required two hundred for recognition that min b'mino is not nullified — requires study; therefore for us who do not hold like R' Yochanan, its species is also like other prohibitions and sixty suffices like not its species — end; for brine in 101 is not because not its species only for recognition that min b'mino is not nullified — and what he ruled sixty suffices is for us who do not hold like R' Yochanan that min b'mino is not nullified; but for R' Yochanan truly needs more and not because decree of not its species only to recognize that min b'mino is not nullified. In the second difficulty he also did not notice there are three matters — Issur VeHeter holds spices that are not nullified are d'rabbanan as above, and when the Sages decreed, everything in its species — as Rambam wrote in explanation of the Mishnah that all they decreed, everything in its species; only he holds whatever is usual to put into the dish is its species, as Tosafot wrote regarding davar she'yesh lo matirin — see there. And if so spices put into the pot that are usual to put therein for taste and are not nullified even in a thousand, and spices not usual to put therein are not included for their taste and their law is in sixty — if so he infers well regarding spices permitted and we do not decree lest a pot usual to put spices therein — even in a thousand not nullified — end. And this is all Issur VeHeter's words in several places that permits spices in a pot in sixty in something not usual to put therein — meaning he holds required min b'mino is in what is usual — called eye in its species; and the matters are clear as he wrote and there is no questioning at all. Henceforth what the talmid chacham resolved regarding what we say 'nullified, diluted, and seasoned' min b'mino — meaning as he wrote fit to put into the pot — true law as Issur VeHeter wrote as stated. But difficult to say so in the Mishnah and the strain is clear. More appears he holds as stated above regarding brine that they were stringent — so one can say regarding terumah they were stringent to forbid in min b'mino; and so he infers the Mishnah's language regarding orlah that teaches 'all who leaven, spice, and dilute' in terumah, orlah, and kilayei kerem — and does not teach plainly 'all who leaven' in prohibitions — why chose these three — except those the Sages were stringent in their measures, they were stringent to forbid even in its species lest not its species; but in other prohibitions the Sages were not stringent; but nevertheless for law the truth is as Rama wrote. But accordingly since all R' Yochanan's reason is because min b'mino makes recognition — Rambam who ruled end chapter 15 Laws of Forbidden Foods that impure fish pickled in brine with pure fish its measure is two hundred — end — behold he ruled for us who do not hold like R' Yochanan — and if so one must say they decreed lest its species — and the matter is proven not like Issur VeHeter.
