"That we rely on them." Shach wrote: in Beit Yosef he ruled regarding a Samaritan who is not an expert taster — meaning a baker who is an artisan — we rely on him in mesicha l'fi tummo specifically, and on an expert taster we rely even if he is not mesicha l'fi tummo, for he will not lie lest he lose his craft; and it has been explained to you that here it deals with a non-Jew who is not an expert taster, and therefore he must not know that they rely on him; but they challenged in Taz and Shach several difficulties on the Mechaber, for we rule that a non-Jew is not believed in mesicha l'fi tummo except for a woman's testimony alone, and how much more so since the Mechaber himself wrote in seif 2 that not its species is forbidden d'oraisa — if so, how is a non-Jew believed? And Shach resolved that one must distinguish — specifically where the matter cannot be established, a non-Jew is not believed in mesicha l'fi tummo regarding a Torah prohibition; but in not its species, since the matter can be established by having him taste for an expert taster, we rely on him; and Taz resolved that these poskim hold that a non-Jew is not believed in mesicha l'fi tummo in a Torah matter only where full testimony is required, whereas in issur v'heter full testimony is not required and proof suffices — and mesicha l'fi tummo works well; and this is proof that an expert taster helps even if not mesicha l'fi tummo because he does not impair his presumption, whereas where testimony is required such a thing certainly does not help — as appears to me to reconcile the poskim; but for halachah we rule in siman 316, and regarding bechor mesicha l'fi tummo does not help, and if so in other places too it does not help regarding a Torah prohibition — end of his words; and see siman 16 seif 1, and siman 69 seif 10, and siman 122 seif 1, and end of seif 137:
