**** ENGLISH ****
That one may not lend a se’ah for a se’ah; it contains 5 se’ifim. It is forbidden to lend a se’ah for a se’ah, even if he did not set a time for repayment; and likewise, anything [lent in kind], except for a silver coin that goes out in circulation (then), for perhaps they will become more expensive, and it turns out that he gives him more than he lent to him — unless he makes it into money-value, such that if they become more expensive he will give him that money, and if he did not make it into money-value and they became more expensive, he gives him the money that they were worth at the time of the loan; and if they became cheaper, he gives him the se’ah that he lent to him. {Rama: There is one who wrote that nowadays a gold coin has the law of silver, and one may borrow gold for gold, and so they are accustomed to be lenient, and one should not protest them, for they have upon whom to rely (Piskei Mahari, siman 54). There is one who says that it is permitted to borrow a loaf of bread for a loaf of bread, like a silver coin, for since it is a small matter people are not particular with one another about it (the Tur and many poskim, and see Beit Yosef), and so they are accustomed to be lenient.}

**** ENGLISH ****
If he has a small amount of that type, even if the key is not in his hand, he may borrow upon it many se’ah-measures, to repay se’ah for se’ah. But if he has nothing of that type, the lender should give him a little of that type, or lend it to him, and afterward he may lend him many se’ah-measures (the author’s words, not like the students of Rashba). {Rama: If the borrower had a small amount of that type as a deposit in the hand of another, this is as if it were in his hand; but if others owe him, it is not called “he has” (Beit Yosef; and so it is implied in the Gemara, perek Eizehu Neshech). And likewise, if he has it in another place and the lender has no access there (Hagahos Mordechai). A borrower who said that he has a small amount of this type — the lender may lend to him, and he does not need proof for his words (there, in the name of Rabbeinu Tam). That which it is permitted to lend to him when he has of that type is when it is unspecified; but if the lender stipulates that if wheat becomes more expensive he will pay him wheat, and if it becomes cheaper he will give him money, the value of them at today’s rate — it is forbidden, for it is close to profit and far from loss (Rivash, siman 18).}

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="10"></i>מלוה אדם את אריסיו <small>(פי' אריס הנוטע או זורע שדה חבירו ויש לו חציו או שליש בפירות) </small>סאה בסאה לזרע בין קודם שירד האריס לשדה בין אחר שירד במה דברים אמורים במקום שנהגו <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="6"></i>שיתן האריס הזרע <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="11"></i>אבל במקום שדרך בעל הקרקע ליתן הזרע אם <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="9"></i>כבר ירד האריס לשדה אסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
A person may lend his sharecropper (meaning: a sharecropper who plants or sows his fellow’s field and has half or a third in the produce) a se’ah for a se’ah for seed, whether before the sharecropper descended to the field or after he descended. To what does this apply? In a place where the practice is that the sharecropper provides the seed; but in a place where it is the practice for the landowner to provide the seed, if the sharecropper already descended to the field, it is forbidden.

**** ENGLISH ****
He lent him 2 se’ah-measures of wheat, and made a document for him for them: if the lender does not clarify that the market rate was set at the time of the loan, or that the borrower had a small amount of that type, the borrower swears that the market rate was not set and that he had nothing of that type, and he pays only money that they were worth at the time of the loan.
