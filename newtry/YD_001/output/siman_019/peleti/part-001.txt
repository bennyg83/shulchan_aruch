In any case it is an interruption; and he was aided by the words of Tosafot explicitly who wrote so. And it appears to say that certainly Tosafot and the Rambam gave the measure of interruption as twenty-two amot walking; and proof from that which we say in Zevachim regarding that which we learned: an asham metzora does not have immediate semichah to shechita—and the Gemara challenged: according to the one who says partial entry is not called entry, let him put his hand in and perform semichah; and it answers: distance it to the north; and they stood to calculate—and he walks twenty-two amot: ten amot the priests' feet and ten amot Israel's feet; and they all challenged—the Magen Avraham and Chavat Yair—but rather what of "partial entry is called entry"? He cannot put his hand in adjacent at the side of the opening and bring in the asham and slaughter it immediately—for according to all the courtyard is called north; and they left it requiring further study. And the Agudah certainly holds that in two rooms in any case it is called interruption; only in one room it is not interruption except twenty-two amot. If so the Gemara's words are correct: if partial entry is called entry he cannot put his hand in at all, only perform semichah outside and bring in the animal—since it is in two rooms it is interruption; but if partial entry is not called entry and he can put his hand in, then everything is in another room—he holds it is not interruption; and the Gemara answers: distance it to the north, etc.—and it is twenty-two amot and interruption. And if it is still difficult regarding semichah—to perform semichah outside and slaughter inside, and his intention was on inside—then still there is no room to room and no interruption; one may say that since he cannot enter there, what of it that his intention is there—since he cannot enter and partitions separate, it is obvious it is from room to room. In any case we learn from this that all the Rambam's words mean there is no distance of twenty-two amot, but if there is a distance of twenty-two amot it is interruption.
