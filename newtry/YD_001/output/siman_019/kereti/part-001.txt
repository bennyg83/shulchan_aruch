**** ENGLISH ****
And when it is found kosher—specifically in such a case he blesses afterward; but if he slaughtered and did not bless, he may not bless after shechita; such is the Rambam's view, and the Shach agreed with him, and the Peri Chadash—his view is like the Hagahah of the Rosh, who holds regarding berachot on mitzvot that if he did not bless before doing it he may bless afterward; and his words do not appear correct; and see Peleti. And behold the Peri Chadash's view: after completing the mitzvah, within twenty-four hours according to all he can bless, for it is like one who blesses at the time of doing the mitzvah; and see Peleti that I wrote his words do not appear correct, and he may not bless; and the rule is: safek in berachot is lenient because of concern for "lo tisa" (bearing God's name in vain), see there; and they wrote that now we are accustomed where there is a defect to slaughter first a bird or similar and bless and intend to discharge also this one in which a defect developed—and then there is no concern; and it is simple. And on Yom Tov, where one should not increase slaughter unnecessarily, one should act as the Rambam wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
At a distance of four amot—I did not understand the need for four amot; immediately outside the kitchen he can bless; and see Peleti: only in kitchens, if it is a special place for slaughtering and there is filth, he may distance four amot and slaughter; but if the whole room is full of filth and he sells in it, he blesses outside the room—he need not distance four amot at all, only he can slaughter immediately; see Peleti. And that he can slaughter outside—this means there is no gap from there until the place of slaughter of twenty-two amot; and even if it is in two rooms, nevertheless it is not called an interruption, since his intention when he blesses is to slaughter inside; but if there is a distance of twenty-two amot it is an interruption and it is forbidden to slaughter relying on this blessing. So I raised in Peleti.
