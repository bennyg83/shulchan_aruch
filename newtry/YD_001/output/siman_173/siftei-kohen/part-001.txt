**** ENGLISH ****
On condition that he give him good wine in Nisan. We do not consider as ribbit what the seller accepts responsibility for in exchange for the advance of money — Tur; meaning responsibility for souring — that the wine will be good; but if the seller also accepts responsibility for price fluctuation — it is forbidden; and likewise proved in Beit Yosef. The Derishab reversed the matter and elaborated, but I did not reach the bottom of his view in all his words. Also what he wrote that it is no worse than fixing produce-credit below siman 175 where the seller can fix even with responsibility for price fluctuation on the seller etc. — that is not correct; for below siman 175 only fixing at the current rate or the lower rate is permitted; but when the seller accepts responsibility for price fluctuation — it is certainly forbidden (and likewise Bach in the final treatise that it is forbidden); also the proof he brought from Nimukei Yosef is nothing — for certainly both the questioner and the respondent require that the buyer accept responsibility for price fluctuation; only the questioner posited that with a known barrel even if the buyer accepts responsibility for the lower price — it is forbidden to say "if it turned sour in your possession," and because of responsibility for the lower price it is considered like responsibility for souring — see there and examine carefully.

<b>בניסן. </b>שבירור היין הוא עד ניסן ומעיקרא קלקולו בתוכו אלא שאינו ניכר אבל בתר הכי אפשר דמעיקרא חמרא והשתא חלא ואסור ויש פוסקים עד טבת ונראה דהכל לפי המקום והזמן שהוא עת בירור היין והתוס' כתבו דטבת לאו דוקא אלא ה"ה כל ימות השנה וכב"י כיון דמידי דרבנן הוא נראה דסמכינן על דברי התוספות ומביאו ד"מ ומ"מ יש להחמיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
In Nisan. For the clarification of wine is until Nisan, and from the outset its spoilage is within it, only it is not apparent; but after that it is possible that originally it was wine and now it is vinegar — and it is forbidden; and some poskim say until Tevet; and it appears that everything depends on the place and time, which is the time of wine clarification; and Tosafot wrote that Tevet is not specifically but the same applies to all days of the year; and per Beit Yosef, since it is rabbinic, it seems we rely on Tosafot's words; and Darkei Moshe brings it; nevertheless it is proper to be stringent.

<b>חבית כו'. </b>כל הסעיף הוא ל' הרמב"ם והב"י והאחרונים נדחקו בישוב דבריו. הפרישה כתב דבדין הא' מיירי שהמקבל מחזיק המעות עד הקיץ ומרויח בו ושם הלואה עליו לכך בעינן שהמוכר יקבל אחריות ובדין הב' מיירי שאין דמי הלואה בידו כלל כו' והב"ח כתב דברישא אינו מקבל אחריות אלא עד הזמן אבל אחר שהגיע הזמן הרי היא ברשות הלוקח לכל דבר ויכול להחזירם לבעליו דג"ז בכלל אחריות דתקלה הוא ובדין הב' איירי דלא קבעו שום זמן כלל אלא לעולם אם לא ימכרנה כמו שירצה. יחזירנה לו עיין שם וכל זה איננו שוה לי ולבי אומר לי שטעות נפל בספרי הרמב"ם והאי שאם לא מצא למכרה ולהרויח בה כו' צריך להיות אחר סוף דין השני מותר אע"פ שאם אבדה או נגנבה או החמיצה תהיה ברשות הלוקח שאם לא מצא למכרה ולהרויח בה ה"ל להחזיר' לבעלים אבל ברישא לא שרי אלא מטעם דאבידה או שבירה היא באחריות (הלוקח) [המוכר] אבל לא היה יכול להחזירה למוכר כשלא היה מוכרו כמו שירצה. כן נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
A barrel etc. The entire seif is Rambam’s language; and Beit Yosef and the Acharonim struggled to reconcile his words. The Derishab wrote that in the first law he speaks of a case where the recipient holds the money until summer and profits from it and it bears the name of a loan on him — therefore we require the seller to accept responsibility; and in the second law he speaks of a case where there is no money of a loan in his hand at all etc. Bach wrote that in the first clause he accepts responsibility only until the appointed time, but after the time arrives it is in the buyer’s possession for all purposes and he can return them to their owner — for that too is included in accident-responsibility; and in the second law he speaks of a case where they set no time at all, rather always if he does not sell it as he wishes he should return it — see there. And all this does not seem right to me, and my heart tells me that a scribal error fell in the books of Rambam: and this phrase "if he did not find to sell it and profit from it etc." should appear after the end of the second law — "it is permitted even though if it were lost, stolen, or turned sour it would be in the buyer’s possession, since if he did not find to sell it and profit from it he must return it to its owners"; but in the first clause it is permitted only because loss or breakage is in the seller’s responsibility, but he could not return it to the seller when he did not sell it as he wished — so it seems to me.
