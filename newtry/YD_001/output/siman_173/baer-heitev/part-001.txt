**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the higher price.</b> Baal HaTerumot writes that if the seller did not say, 'If you pay now the price is this, but later it is more,' and merely fixed delivery in Iyar at the Iyar price, the transaction is permitted whether the future price rises or falls, even if it usually rises by then. With land and other items lacking a fixed market price or subject to onaah, an ordinary sale is permitted unless the seller expressly sets one price for immediate payment and another for deferred payment. If done improperly, the prohibition is only avak ribbit because it arose through a sale. Shach.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The correct view.</b> Maharshal, Levush, Bach, and Taz disagree with Rama. This is unlike a seah-for-seah loan, because the recipient receives more than the present value, and permission exists only as a sale. Therefore he must own the full amount of the currency at home. Taz condemns the custom of creating a false sale by having the borrower admit that he owns silver he does not actually possess; a false admission cannot remove a Torah prohibition. Later authorities defend the established Polish heter only when structured according to Maharam's enactment as a genuine investment arrangement.
