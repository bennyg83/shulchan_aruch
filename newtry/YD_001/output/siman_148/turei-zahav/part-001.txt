<b>להשאיל. </b>שאלה שייך בדבר החוזר בעין כגון חפץ והלואה שייכא במעות דלהוצאה ניתנה. וכל הנזכר כאן הוא דהעובד כוכבים אזיל ומודה לאליל שלו שהישראל נצרך לו ובגמרא (דף ז') איבעיא להו משום הרווחה פרש"י דרווח ואזיל ומודה לעבודת כוכבים ועבר ישראל משום לא ישמע על פיך או דילמא משום לפני עור לא וגו' ופרש"י דמזבין ליה בהמה ומקריבה לעבודת כוכבים שהעובד כוכבים נצטוה גם כן על אליל ולא יהיה נענש על ידך למאי נפקא מינה דאית ליה בהמה לעובד כוכבים אי משום הרווחה קא מרווח ליה ואי משום לפני עור הא אית ליה בלאו הכי והנה לפרש"י והרמב"ם שהש"ע העתיק דבריו כאן שיש איסור במכירה וקנייה עם העובד כוכבים תמוה מאד הא איכא בלאו הכי נ"מ דאי משום הרווחה אסור אפילו במידי דלא חזי להקרבה ואי משום לפני עור ליכא איסור אלא בחזי להקרבה וכבר הקשו התוס' בראש הפרק בשם ר"ת ומ"ה פר"ת דבמכירה וקנייה אין שייך אזיל ומודה לאליל כמ"ש בטור בשמו וצ"ל לרש"י ורמב"ם דאין הכי נמי דיכול לעשות נפקותא זאת אלא דקמ"ל רבותא דבבהמה שהיא תקרובת יש גם כן נפקותא דהיינו דאית ליה לדידיה ועיקר הבעיא היא אם יש כאן חשש שמודה לעבודת כוכבים במכירה וקנייה ונמצא שיש איסור בכל דבר משום הרווחה או אין חשש במכירה וקנייה שיודה והיינו כסברת ר"ת ולא נפשטה הבעיא וקיימא לן לחומרא דבמכירה וקנייה שייך גם כן מודה לאליל ועובר על לא ישמע על פיך. ומו"ח ז"ל הקשה למה פרש"י דבמודה לאליל עובר על לא ישמע ואמאי לא אסור משום לפני עור ותירץ דבמודה ליכא לפני עור דה"ל כמו גיפוף ונישוק דאין העובד כוכבים מוזהר עליו כיון שגם הישראל אינו חייב מיתה עליו <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>ותמהתי כאן דודאי ישראל חייב מיתה במודה לעבודת כוכבים כדאי' פרק ארבע מיתות (סנהדרין דף ס') מתניתין באומר אלי אתה כו' ובהדיא אמרינן כל המודה בעבודת כוכבים ככופר בכל התורה וקושיא מעיקרא אין כאן דהא מבואר שם בגמרא פ"ק דעבודת כוכבים (דף ו') דאין שייך לפני עור אלא במקום שא"א לעובד כוכבים לעשות זולת הישראל כמו במושיט כוס יין לנזיר דקאי בתרי עברי דנהרא שלא היה אפשר לנזיר ליקח היין אבל אם היה אפשר לו רק שהישראל מסייע ומקרב לו הדבר אין כאן לפני עור וכמ"ש המרדכי ריש עבודת כוכבים הביאו רמ"א סימן קנ"א לענין למכור להם השייכים לעבודתם אם יוכלו לקנות במקום אחר ואפילו להיש מחמירין שמביא שם היינו מדרבנן ולא מן התורה אבל משום לא ישמע יש כאן שמ"מ על ידו נשמע אע"פ שבלאו הכי היה ג"כ נשמע דמ"מ עכשיו נשמע על פיך:
**** ENGLISH ****To lend. A question applies to something that returns in kind, such as an object; and a loan applies to money, which is given for expenditure. And all that is mentioned here is that the gentile goes and acknowledges to his idol that Israel needed him; and in the Gemara (7a) they asked: because of profit — Rashi explained: he profits and goes and acknowledges idolatry; and Israel transgresses "he shall not listen at your mouth" — or perhaps because of lifnei iver, etc.; and Rashi explained: he sells him an animal and he offers it to idolatry — the gentile was also commanded regarding idolatry and he will not be punished through you. What difference does it make — he has an animal for the gentile: if because of profit he profits for him; and if because of lifnei iver — behold he has it anyway. And behold according to Rashi and the Rambam whom Shulchan Aruch cited here — that there is prohibition in selling and buying with a gentile — it is very wondrous, for there is a difference anyway: if because of profit it is forbidden even in something not fit for offering; and if because of lifnei iver there is prohibition only in what is fit for offering; and Tosafot already challenged at the beginning of the chapter in the name of R. Tam, and Mahari Abuhav explained R. Tam that in selling and buying the concept of "he goes and acknowledges to an idol" does not apply, as the Tur wrote in his name; and it must be according to Rashi and Rambam that this too can make such profit — rather it teaches a stringency that regarding an animal that is an offering there is also profit, namely that he has it for himself; and the essential question is whether there is concern he acknowledges idolatry in selling and buying — and it is found there is prohibition in everything because of profit; or there is no concern in selling and buying that he will acknowledge — and this is R. Tam's view; and the question was not resolved and we hold stringently that in selling and buying the concept of acknowledging an idol also applies, and one transgresses "he shall not listen at your mouth." And Mahari Chalav challenged: why did Rashi explain that in acknowledging an idol one transgresses "he shall not listen" — why is it not forbidden because of lifnei iver; and he resolved that in acknowledging there is no lifnei iver, for it is like bowing and kissing — the gentile is not warned about it since Israel too is not liable to death for it. And I wondered here: certainly Israel is liable to death for acknowledging idolatry, as stated in chapter Four Deaths (Sanhedrin 60a) — the mishnah: one who says "to you," etc.; and explicitly we say: whoever acknowledges idolatry is like one who denies the entire Torah. And the initial difficulty is not here, for it is explained there in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (6a) that lifnei iver applies only where the gentile cannot do it except through Israel, like handing a cup of wine to a nazir when he is on opposite sides of the river and the nazir could not take the wine; but if it were possible for him and Israel only assists and brings it near — there is no lifnei iver; and as the Mordechai wrote at the beginning of Avodah Zarah, whom the Rama brought siman 151 regarding selling them things connected to their worship if they can buy elsewhere; and even to be stringent — what he brings there is rabbinic and not from the Torah; but because of "he shall not listen" there is nevertheless concern — for through him it is heard even though it would also be heard without him — nevertheless now it is heard at your mouth.

**** ENGLISH ****And he should throw it in his face into a pit. In the Gemara it is thus in the incident where a heretic sent a dinar to R. Yehuda the Prince on his festival day and said: how shall I act — shall I give it to him? He goes and acknowledges that Israel needs him; shall I not give it to him? It is enmity. R. Yehoshua ben Levi said: take it and throw it in his face into a pit indirectly, so he does not understand you threw it intentionally; and since he sees it is lost he will not rejoice. And in the Rambam it is written: and if he fears enmity he takes it in his face and does not benefit from it; and it appears this "in his face" refers to afterwards — meaning he shows in his face that he does not benefit from it, namely he shows him an angry face at the time of accepting the gift so it appears from it he does not enjoy this; and he accepts it out of fear; and as stated in seif 9 that he should tell him in a soft tone, etc.; and this is a general remedy everywhere — for what is mentioned in the Gemara that he should throw it into a pit applies only when standing beside the pit; but if he goes to the pit the gentile will certainly sense his intent. And so one should explain the Tur's words that wrote "he accepts it and does not benefit from it" — meaning the gentile should not have benefit from what Israel accepts; whatever can be done in his face like this — that he is not happy — and now the Tur's language is settled: "he shall not benefit from it" — for if it referred to Israel he should have said "he shall not benefit from it"; rather certainly it refers to the gentile, that he should not benefit from the gift Israel accepts; and with this the Beit Yosef's wonder on the Rambam and Tur is resolved — why they did not mention as in the Gemara. But it appears that if it is impossible to act before the gentile so he loses it — it is permitted to accept and benefit from it; and proof from what the Rosh wrote here: "evidently if there were no evasion so there not be enmity he would take even though he goes and acknowledges"; and so we say in chapter One Who Is Saved that Rav Yosef permitted midwives with beer because of enmity; and so to raise a gentile from a pit, etc. — end. And behold he compares to those where there too is concern he goes and acknowledges idolatry when he comes out of the pit, as stated siman 124 "her husband is alive," etc.; and nevertheless the beer is permitted; and one can say: from where did the Rosh learn to explain thus — perhaps if he cannot evade he should not accept at all; one can say he deduced from that it says "take and throw it indirectly" and did not say "if you can throw it in his face take, and if not do not take" — rather certainly he takes in any case, only if possible afterwards to throw it in his face he should do so; and so at the end of this siman in the Tur who wrote permission for us to borrow and lend with them because of enmity — and we do not say it should be forbidden to benefit from it; rather as he explained — so it appears clear to me, and not as he understood the Tur's words that in every case benefit afterwards is forbidden:

**** ENGLISH ****Therefore it is good to precede him. Apparently this is difficult from what we find in R. Yehoshua ben Levi: no one preceded another in peace, not even a gentile in the market — what "even"? The essential law is so — one must precede so he need not repeat; and one can say there it deals with a gentile he never knew, as the language "in the market" hints; and it is not reasonable he would ask of his welfare and need to repeat; and even here he opens with his welfare because he feared lest the gentile happen to give him peace first; and here it deals with one he knows — and therefore it is by law. Again I saw in Semak siman 133: "and it is forbidden to repeat peace to them — and specifically peace, which is the name of the Holy One, blessed be He, or a blessing in divine language; but another blessing there is no concern" — end. And this Beit Yosef did not bring; and therefore it is settled: that which they praised him for preceding peace to a gentile in the market — even in another language of blessing; and this is correct:
