**** ENGLISH ****
Some say he forbids, etc. — For in his share he certainly did not intend to pain—therefore we say presumably also in friend's share he did not intend to pain. And you might ask: here Taz wrote two views on this, and in Choshen Mishpat siman 385 they wrote anonymously regarding one who libated friend's wine, if he had partnership he must pay—and Semag there distinguished between wine and animal; and one might say this is against Shas Chullin (41)—see there (I again found in Derishah here who rejected this answer). Also what Bach answered there—that Taz holds like Ran regarding eating we say a person forbids what is not his—wonder to me, for Ran wrote only that slaughtering for idol worship is forbidden in eating—for there is no room to say regarding issur of eating a person does not forbid what is not his, for he did not forbid it for her but did not permit it, and it is as if it did not slaughter but died by itself—end quote; and this does not apply to libating wine. Also their plain words here imply entirely permitted; and so in Darkei Moshe not like Ran—and as proven in Shas (40) not like Ran—for otherwise there would be no difficulty from that baraita of slaughtering on Shabbat, Rav Amram and R. Yitzchak—see there; and Ra'ah in Beit HaBechirah (11) like Ran, and Rashba already answered him at length in Mishmeret HaBayit on this sugya; and so appears from all poskim entirely permitted. Rather it appears that although for issur there is dispute among amoraim, the injured party can say perhaps we hold like one who forbids in benefit—what do you say we hold like the one who renders kosher? Take your wine, for I do not want to enter doubt; sell it yourself since you caused me damage, and take another in its place; think.
