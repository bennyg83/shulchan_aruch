**** ENGLISH ****
Therefore one asks the Israelite hunter, etc. Mechaber's view is like Rambam chapter 3 of Forbidden Foods — specifically with an Israelite who is not established in kashrut we rely on him, but with a non-Jew we never rely on him; therefore he wrote that one does not buy eggs from a non-Jew unless he recognized them and has recognizable familiarity with them; and as Rambam wrote there, meaning specifically in such a case we buy from him, but when he says from such-and-such fowl and it is pure we do not rely on him. But Ramban, Ran, and Tur disagree and hold that with a non-Jew too we rely on him as with an Israelite who is not established in kashrut; and so too in Sifrei — this is the view of many poskim from what they wrote regarding fish innards in seif 2 of Avodah Zarah; and so I proved there from the Gemara in Pesachim that this is primary. I also proved there from the Gemara in chapter Kol HaBasar (Chullin daf 116) — see there; and this is Rama's view. And what he did not emend at all from what Mechaber wrote "therefore one does not buy," etc., is because he relied on what he wrote before — or non-Jew — meaning that with a non-Jew too we rely; and automatically it appears that Mechaber did not write this except according to his view; and Taz mixed up the matter by first writing "or non-Jew" and afterward writing "therefore one does not buy," etc. And see in siman 83 s.k. 24 and 25.

<b>באלף כולן אסורות. </b>תימה דזה אינו אלא לסברת סה"ת דס"ל דדבר שדרכו לימכר פעמים באומד ופעמים במנין כמו ביצה לא בטיל אבל לפי מה שפסק המחבר לקמן ר"ס ק"י כהרמב"ם וסייעתו דאפילו דברים שדרכן למנות לעולם בטלים ואף להר"ב בהגהה דפסק שם דדבר שדרכו למנות תמיד לא בטיל מ"מ ביצים שלפעמים מוכרין סל מלא ביצים בטיל וכדאמר בש"ס ריש ביצה בהדיא וכמו שיתבאר שם (וכ"כ בת"ח סוף כלל ס"ג בהדיא) א"כ הכא אמאי באלף אסורות ה"ל ליבטל ברובא דיבש ביבש חד בתרי בטל וכדלקמן סי' ק"ט ואף שהטור כתב ג"כ דבאלף אסורות היינו טעמא משום דדעת הטור דדבר שפעמים נמכר במנין ופעמים באומד כמו ביצה לא בטיל כמו שהביא בשם בעל העיטור בר"ס ק"י ואף שהביא דעת הרמב"ם שם באחרונה מ"מ אפשר דס"ל דעת בעל העיטור עיקר להחמיר וכ"כ בת"ח כלל מ"ב סוף ד"א שדעת הטור כדעת בעל העיטור א"נ דהטור אזיל לטעמיה דס"ל כהתוספות והרא"ש דביצה חשוב בריה דלא בעינן בריית נשמה אבל המחבר שפסק בסי' ק' דדוקא ביצה שיש בה אפרוח הוי בריה כיון שיש בו נשמה אבל ביצה דעלמא לא כיון שלא היה בו חיות א"כ אמאי נתערבה באלף כולן אסורות וצ"ע ולענין דינא ע"ל סי' ק"י ס"ק ט':
**** ENGLISH ****
In a thousand all are forbidden. It is astonishing — this is only according to Beit HaTaharah, who holds that an item sold sometimes by estimate and sometimes by count, like an egg, is not nullified; but according to what Mechaber ruled below siman 110 as Rambam and his supporters — that even items always sold by count are nullified; and even Rama in his hagahah ruled there that an item always sold by count is not nullified — nevertheless eggs that are sometimes sold in a basket full of eggs are nullified, as stated in the Gemara at the beginning of Beitzah explicitly, and as will be explained there (and so too in a sage at the end of general rule 63 explicitly). If so, here why in a thousand are all forbidden? It should nullify in a majority — dry in dry, one in two is nullified, as below siman 109; and even though Tur also wrote that in a thousand they are forbidden, the reason is Tur's view that an item sometimes sold by count and sometimes by estimate, like an egg, is not nullified, as Beit HaEzer brought in name of Baal HaItur in siman 110; and even though he brought Rambam's view there last, nevertheless it is possible he holds Baal HaItur's view is primary to be stringent; and so too in a sage at the end of seif 41 of general rule 42 that Tur's view is like Baal HaItur's view. Or Tur follows Tosafot and Rosh that an egg is considered a complete creature and we do not require formation of a soul; but Mechaber ruled in siman 110 that specifically an egg that has a chick in it is a complete creature since it has a soul, but an ordinary egg is not, since there was no life in it — if so, why if mixed in a thousand are all forbidden? Requires study; and for practical law see above siman 110 s.k. 9.

But in truth, according to what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 66, Rambam (here) disagrees with what Tur wrote — if it is known the chick is formed one is liable d'oraisa — if so Mechaber's words in Shulchan Aruch cannot be reconciled; but one may say that Tur himself brought Rambam's words in general, and on Bach's challenge: for Tur wrote in siman 66 in general one is liable from the Torah, and Rosh in a responsum (Beit Yosef brings this in siman 66) holds that formed blood is forbidden d'oraisa; and what Tur wrote afterward that R' Yehudah permits blood even outside the knot does not contradict what he wrote first in general that one is liable d'oraisa — for certainly when found outside the knot it is entirely permitted, and when found on the knot, if it is formed blood, it is forbidden from the Torah; and moreover from what they wrote, Rosh and Tur afterward in siman 66 "and good to be stringent," etc. — see there, it appears explicitly that he holds formed blood is d'oraisa; only Perishah's words appear clear in their reason, and I found support and proof for his words from what Maharach in Pesachim wrote — that Ra'avyah received from Ra'avan: if blood is in the yolk in a place where the start of the chick is not recognizable, then only it is forbidden because of the law of blood; but if full of blood and the place where the chick begins is recognizable, it is permitted, for near the formation it is like mere dust — end of his words. And even though Beit Yosef wrote in siman 66 on this, and it appears these words are rejected and it is not the view of poskim — end of his words — one may say they disagree only on permitting entirely, but from the Torah it is nevertheless permitted; and Mechaber's words may also be reconciled thus, and he retracted from what he wrote in siman 66 that Rambam disagrees with Tur; and so it appears somewhat in his book Bedek HaBayit in this siman.

And some say, etc. Tur's view appears that even where they are not careful about bread of gentiles, it is permitted to sell him a tereifah egg; and so it appears to me that this is Tosafot in chapter Eizehu Mekoman (daf 64 side 1 s.v. signs), Rashba, Mordechai there, and Ran there explicitly; and so appears Semag; and so from other poskim.
