**** ENGLISH ****
Liver ground. Machatzit HaShekel wrote: in Shulchan Aruch that mentioned tearing — so is in Bahag and Pesikta Zutarta and Ra'avan; and Machatzit HaShekel brought many rishonim and acharonim who did not mention tearing; and implies tearing is to include even if not bitter outside — if torn and taste helps because we say bitterness absorbed; and so I saw Jerusalem rabbis taste liver without tearing and if bitter taste sensed — permitted; and they have basis to rely. But in Taharat HaShulchan wrote: error to taste without tearing, for liver bitter outside is no proof it absorbed on contact, etc.; and Machatzit HaShekel wrote: since tearing mentioned in Bahag and Shulchan Aruch — concern to tear and taste; and b'dieved external tasting suffices; and see there: even if pouch not found — also kosher via tasting; and see there: wrote per main text in Bahag: if trace that pouch and gall moisture were and removed — also permit via tearing and tasting liver; and not like Ahel Mo'ed; and l'chatchila do inflation as Peri Chadash; if did not inflate — all who saw it whole suffices; and all the more if lost without inflation. If tasted its place and bitter found and tasted again and not found — we attribute wiped what liver absorbed from gall and kosher — so Peri Chadash in name of Maharikash; meaning tasted at gall place and bitter found there even though tasted liver afterward and no bitter taste in liver — we attribute wiped what liver absorbed; and this is "tasted its place and again tasted"; and not as copyists seem on matter; and it is straightforward: if tasted that very place that was bitter at first and now not bitter — we attribute wiped in first tasting; not like Mishbetzot who gave taste that liver absorbed bitterness. And this is not Peri Chadash's intent. Therefore even if afterward they taste liver again and no bitterness — permit; but per Taharat HaShulchan appears not sufficient with slight bitterness — see there. And appears to be stringent without Peri Megadim in such case — rishonim gave no such limit. If gall as required and tasted liver and bitterness found — Peri Megadim in name of Pri Te'arah: kosher and straightforward. If separated intestines from liver and threw them and afterward no gall in liver — kosher; attribute remained with intestines as above in name of Shevut Yaakov; and so Mohar"i of Prague in responsum manuscript. And in truth so explained Peri Chadash s.k. 4. If no bitter taste and tasted intestines and bitter — permit as Sefer Shevet Yehudah to Mohar"i — see there; and essence not like disputant. If no bitter taste in liver or intestines and roasted Ba'al Mechaber and bitter taste found — declare treifah. In responsum Orach Chaim they brought in Bnei Chayya: liver somewhat crushed and cut and no gall in tasting liver; tasted gizzard — bitter like wormwood — permit. Liver of calf that had gall as required but very white; tore gall and clear sweet water inside — Mishbetzot in this siman declared treifah from doubt. Machatzit HaShekel wrote he saw in letter of gaon Moharav Baruch Katz Rapaport av beit din Frankfurt incident came to him and he declared treifah; brought from Maharshal who permits in variations not mentioned in gemara; and nevertheless in missing rose he declares treifah — none on it; and similarly when sweet — no gall on it; and all this for those who declare treifah in missing rose; but one who permits there — same here; end. (Words are closed: in my opinion this is not general rule for one who permits missing rose; understanding is easy and I shortened.) If sweet gall and tasted liver and bitter — permit and attribute pouch drew water like bubble as Moharikash in incident: pouch found without bitter water at gall — kosher, not gall but blister (brought Ba'er Heitev; and what brought in name of Ribash is corrupted — must read Rikanti); and similarly its counterpart; and see Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 56. If liver lost before tasting — many acharonim wrote minority of minority taste bitter in liver; therefore not even permit from double doubt that Kenesset HaGedolah and Machatzit HaShekel wrote: in their regions birds found without gall — mostly find bitter taste in liver; and still half-half; and in such places permit in lost case from double doubt. If they ate liver and did not taste bitter and remembered and asked for gall and not found — declare treifah as Dvar Moshe in name of Mohar"i David; and see Kereti: search at bird spleen and near it small gall found; and in vain many chickens declared treifah — see there. And see Machatzit HaShekel: his view that even Maharshal admits women are trusted to check via tasting liver to say it is bitter, since no exertion at all; and not like responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 86 (and what written there that Rama too admits — not so: Rama in Taharat HaKodesh general rule 46 disputes Maharshal on checking legumes — see there) who wrote: even household saw no gall found and went — woman working says she tasted bitter in liver — believe her. And so Zichron Yosef; and so appears from Dvar Shmuel siman 313. Gaon Mohar"m son of R' Betzalel in Sefer Vikuach Mayim Chayim wrote: matter placed on women and they themselves know we rely on them — then certainly check well and not suspected, etc.; and see Machatzit HaShekel who upheld as poskim wrote: when wanting to taste must wipe blood from liver; and not like Be'er Yaakov and Zichron Yosef. And see my books Beit Avraham siman 36; and see there in name of Beit David: if did not taste bitter in liver — treifah — eggs it laid before slaughter are permitted, for we say now gall was removed; but eggs found inside bird even fully developed like those sold in market — forbidden. And responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef forbids all eggs born; and even if mixed in others — all forbidden; and Beit Lechem Yehudah wrote in mixture to permit; and Machatzit HaShekel wrote: only when no trace of pouch and gall at all; but if trace visible or empty pouch found in such case — even if did not taste bitter in liver — permit eggs already born; and again brought Zichron Yosef's words that seemed and debated; and at end upheld his view agrees with Beit David. And we would know gall was and afterward entirely removed — doubt when entirely removed; possibly removed recently — then eggs laid after three days may be permitted from double doubt.
