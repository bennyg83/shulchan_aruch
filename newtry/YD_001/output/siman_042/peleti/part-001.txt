**** ENGLISH ****
And taste it, etc. This law the Rav Ba'al HaG wrote and after him most poskim followed; and the Rav Ba'al Peleti argued: what of it that it fell inside the liver — in any case gall-bladder skin hanging is a punctured treifah and the like; what do we judge here that we do not have before us? And he asked a great question. In truth I and my colleagues and my associate in Prague wondered, for we did not see among our fathers and instructing rabbis who stood on this; and therefore we ruled that it was a tradition in the hands of Bahag that gall-bladder membrane is thin membrane adhering to the liver; and when all moisture and everything inside went into the liver, it too adheres to the liver; and the liver by breath and power of the soul draws the membrane until it adheres to the liver and becomes one membrane with liver membrane; therefore if you search the membrane you will not find it, for it adheres to the liver; and so we would learn in many when we reached this law with students; and so is implied in Ra'avan siman 214. And it appears more to me than what was difficult to me than what the rabbis challenged Rabbenu Yehudah from that which "he pours his gall upon the ground" and Job still lived — what difficulty? And is it for this that the gall bladder was punctured? Cannot gall moisture spread within the liver duct as we say in the gemara and below siman 59? And from there the duct was punctured and kosher and gall spread through the whole body and everywhere in Job's cry; but for this the gall bladder was not punctured — see Peri Chadash s.k. 12 in name of Ahel Mo'ed: if the pouch was emptied it is kosher, for we say via duct and not via puncture it was emptied. And further difficulty to Ra'avyah and his supporters who permit removed and missing gall bladder — only punctured is treifah — difficult per one who says daf 25a; and impossible punctured alone and cut alone, for cutting is not like punctured except in removal — what did they challenge Rabbenu Yehudah from Job "he pours upon the ground"? If so evidently Satan did not puncture the gall bladder, for his soul was guarded — only the gall was cut; and it is obvious his bitterness was poured; and nevertheless he was not declared treifah from this; and strained that "upon the ground" is precise — if inside the body how does "ground" apply, for it is in his midst; rather he took the gall outside his body — "they pour upon the ground" — and this is removal of gall; and examine — this is proof for treifah of gall removal. And therefore it must be that they established treifah of gall is on account of bitter gall moisture inside the membrane; and the membrane is only a guard for that moisture and treifah depends on it; but membrane itself neither raises nor lowers — only guards lest bitterness spread in the body; therefore punctured membrane is treifah, for bitterness will spread and exit; therefore even if bitterness spread via duct and membrane exists — nevertheless treifah; and all the more if membrane was cut — at least bitterness spread. If so when Job poured his gall upon the ground it is obvious it was treifah; and do not answer from this that Bahag ruled: if gall is inside liver we do not look further at gall-bladder membrane, for it neither raises nor lowers treifah in the body; and in truth in Sefer Ahel Mo'ed one may say "was emptied" is not precise that nothing remained, only that it was lacking — and this is language of "was emptied"; and it teaches kosher, for we are not concerned for puncture — requires further study. But it appears more as Peleti stated; but not for his reason that Bahag was in doubt in law if gall removed or missing is kosher since gemara did not mention treifot or treifah — only punctured is treifah as Mechabrim wrote; and Bahag holds: if we doubt whether it had gall — behold safek treifah; and "establish the animal on its presumption of kashrut" — presumption of kashrut, for if it never had gall at all it has no presumption of kashrut; and on this we judge kosher or treifah; and how does presumption of kashrut apply? Even per R' Shimson who holds we follow majority — here it turned out it has no gall and majority does not apply; but when we know it had gall — then it has presumption of kashrut and we establish on presumption; and if so — do not answer; certainly in any case it is difficult to understand: because we sense a little bitter taste we judge all gall comes to liver and daily gall throws a drop; but because gall was not entirely lacking until no remnant remains — in truth animal and bird are before us and there is no gall — there is doubt whether removed from its measure kosher or not; and we establish on presumption of permissibility; impossible perhaps it was only lacking and never had presumption of permissibility; therefore if they tasted bitter taste in liver this is conclusive proof there was gall here and afterward removed; then since treifah is doubt we establish on presumption of permissibility and kashrut; not so if no bitter taste found — no presumption of kashrut and doubt is forbidden stringently; and this is clear and correct; and one whose mind is toward truth will concede.

If he tastes. And even though safek issur — forbidden to taste — Shach wrote s.k. 4: here they were lenient, for mostly they have gall and will sense bitter taste; and this is strained, for on the contrary it is uncommon; and I — fifty years I instruct righteousness to God's congregation — did not find literally twice in missing liver that bitter taste was sensed in liver; and so testified the gaon elder instructor Mahar"g in Gershon's work siman 52: liver lost before tasting — wrote they did not find so in liver; and so the gaon Ba'al Sha'arei Yeshurun; therefore requires further study to permit lost liver; and on Bahag's law we are distressed; especially no proof to permit from Bahag, for there it says only to taste — but not said via Israel; and possibly via gentile taster who does not impair his craft and is trusted for law; but in truth per what I wrote above — if so it is like somewhat siman 36: doubt whether will sense bitter taste or not; and if you say he will sense — perhaps gall was removed and kosher. And it is not similar to meat in doubt of salting that forbidden matter is established — unsalted — therefore Maharshal forbade; not so here forbidden matter is not established and it is rabbinic matter.

Like date pits without sharp point — permitted per Rambam and his supporters: regarding olive we are concerned because it has sharp point punctured at its base; not so date; if so even if not as large as date, since no sharp point — kosher. מפני שהוגלד פי המכה מזה משמע קצת דלא מועיל נפיחה במרה כמו בריאה אבל נראה דרש"י ס"ל דמועיל ובזה מיושב קושי' מהרש"א ד' מ"ט ברש"י זיתא מבזע בזע דס"ל לרש"י מאן דמכשיר בריאה מכשי' בהא נמי ותמה מהרש"א מאן מכריחו הא בכבד קיימינן הכא ולפמ"ש ניחא דרש"י ס"ל כל הבדל בין ריאה לכבד דזהו אי' לי' בדיקה משא"כ כבדא ולכך בחתוכה דריאה דל"ל בדיקה ג"כ ס"ל לתלק בין קופא לגאו ולבר וכמ"ש רש"י דף מ"ח ע"ב ד"ה הלכת' ועיין רשב"א וא"כ המרה דאית לי' בדיקה דמי לריאה ולא לכבד ויפה דן רש"י דשניהם שוים בדינא וגם מ"ש מהרש"א כיון דהך דמרה אליבא דמטריף אתמר ומ"מ מכשיר בשל תמרה א"כ ה"ה יכול להכשיר בקופא לבר וכו' ולמה כתב רש"י בד"ה הנ"ל דבכל גוונא טריף דלפמ"ש ניחא דגבי מרה יש לה בדיקה א"כ תרתי לטיבות' בדיקה ואלימתא מכשיר מה שאין כן כבד דליכא רק חד ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
Because mouth of wound scabbed over — from this implies somewhat inflation does not help in gall bladder like lung; but it appears Rashi holds it helps; and thereby Maharsha's difficulty is settled daf 49 in Rashi "olive splits" — Rashi holds one who permits lung permits here too; and Maharsha wondered who forces him — we are in liver here; and per my words it is settled: Rashi holds all difference between lung and liver — this is examination for him, not liver; therefore in cut lung where no examination he also holds distinguish pouch inside outside and bar; and as Rashi daf 48b entry "the law"; and see Rashba; and if so gall bladder that has examination resembles lung not liver; and Rashi ruled well both equal in law; and also what Maharsha wrote: since this gall bladder is stated for one who forbids — nevertheless he permits date; if so he could permit pouch outside, etc.; and why did Rashi write there entry "in every case treifah" — per my words it is settled: regarding gall bladder there is examination; if so two leniencies — examination and adhesion — permit what is not so in liver where only one; and examine.
