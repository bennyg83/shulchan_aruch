**** ENGLISH ****
The law of a place where there is wine and a non-Jew was alone there. In it are 5 seifim. A non-Jew who was alone with the wine — even in our domain, even for a short time — is forbidden in benefit. But one who is known to us not to worship avodah zarah: it is permitted to leave wine alone with him in our domain for a short time, such as the time it takes to walk the distance of a mil, or more, even in a city that is entirely non-Jews, and even if he informed him that he is leaving; but we do not entrust wine to him in his home. And even in our homes it is forbidden if it is for a long time; and if one transgressed and deposited it, it is forbidden for drinking. (Non-Jews outside of Eretz Yisrael, even though they do not worship avodah zarah, nevertheless we do not leave wine alone with them — Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.)

**** ENGLISH ****
A non-Jew who was found in a house in which there is wine: if he has a loan secured on that wine — such as if he made it an apotiki (or he mortgaged it to him — Tur) — it is forbidden, even if the wine was sealed with one seal. But if he has no loan secured on the wine, even though he has a loan against the Jew who owns the wine and the time of the loan has arrived, it is permitted — even if he was standing at the side of the wine, right near it within the reach of his hands, and even if the wine has no seal at all — because since he knows the Jew would lose his wine, he is afraid to touch (Rosh). And even if foam, which commonly rises on the mouth of the wine, was found in his hand, it is permitted, for we assume he took it from the sides of the barrel and not from its mouth. {Rama: But things that are found only on the mouth of the barrel are forbidden (Tur). And likewise if one found that the non-Jew was holding the spigot in his hand, the wine is forbidden, and we do not say that the spigot fell by itself and the non-Jew lifted it (Mordechai, perek HaSocher, in the name of Raavyah), for we always attribute to what is usual.}

**** ENGLISH ****
A house that belongs to a Jew, and there is wine of a non-Jew in it, and the non-Jew entered and locked the door behind him with a lock from inside, such that if the Jew comes to enter he cannot enter without the non-Jew’s knowledge — the wine is forbidden (Rosh). But if there were holes in the door such that the Jew can look and see from there all the barrels, they are permitted for drinking, even if they are open. And if not, that which he can see through there is permitted and the rest is forbidden in benefit, even sealed barrels, if he remained long enough that he could open and reseal and wipe (meaning: reseal and make another cover; and “wipe” means to wipe and dry), even if the Jew lived in that house.

**** ENGLISH ****
A house that contains only the wine of a Jew, and a non-Jew was found in it: if the door is not locked, it is permitted for drinking. But if it is locked with a key from inside: if he is not “caught as a thief” regarding entry, it is forbidden in benefit; and if he is “caught as a thief” regarding entry, it is permitted for drinking. {Rama: And there are those who say that in our times, when non-Jews do not pour libations, even if he is not “caught as a thief” it is permitted, unless there is reason to be concerned that they touched it in order to drink from it or for another benefit (so it is in Hagahot Ashiri, perek HaSocher; and see seif katan 4). However, if the mouth of the barrel is wide, or it is in a jug, such that there is concern perhaps he touched it in the manner of “mis’ashek”, we are concerned (Mordechai, perek HaSocher; and Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot; and in Semag).}

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="11"></i>היו בשוק חביות יין ונמצא עובד כוכבים ביניהם אם הוא עובד כוכבים חלש שאימת שופטי העיר עליו ואם ימצאוהו נוגע נתפס כגנב מותר בשתייה ואם לאו אסור בהנאה. <small>(ועיין לקמן סי' קכ"ט עוד מדינים אלו):</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
If there were barrels of wine in the market and a non-Jew was found among them: if he is a weak non-Jew such that the fear of the city judges is upon him, and if they would find him touching he would be “caught as a thief,” it is permitted for drinking; and if not, it is forbidden in benefit. (And see below siman 129 for more of these laws.)
