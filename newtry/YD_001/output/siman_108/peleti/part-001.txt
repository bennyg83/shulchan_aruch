"One may not roast, etc." — this is the view of Rashi and the Rif: even though we establish that Levi holds reicha is insignificant, nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden; and Tosafot challenged: if so, why does the Gemara challenge Levi from a baraita that teaches "One may not roast two Pesach offerings together" — behold, we learned "One may not roast," and Levi agrees l'chatchila that it is forbidden. And the Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen sought to distinguish between non-sacred and consecrated animals, that with consecrated animals one need not be stringent l'chatchila — and the strain is evident. And in truth, for the Rif it requires further study: the Rif ruled that therefore we do not establish like Rav, because certainly that reicha is nullified and is only a trace — only Rav, according to his reasons, holds that min b'mino is not nullified, and we do not establish thus; and accordingly one may say: certainly Levi, who said reicha is insignificant — if it is insignificant, even l'chatchila it is permitted; only we in this hold like Rav, that it is significant but nullified — if so, l'chatchila certainly even a trace is lacking; and see there: the Gemara's question applies to Levi, and according to him, since it is insignificant, even l'chatchila it is permitted; but we establish in this like Rav, regarding a trace of significance and l'chatchila it is forbidden. However, for other poskim who do not hold thus like the Rif, that it constitutes a trace — the question is in Avodah Zarah. And it appears that certainly the Gemara that challenges Levi from the baraita that teaches "because of admixture" also knew this reasoning that there is concern for exchange of bodies — only he holds by strain that the baraita's intent is on exchange of bodies, that it should have said "because of exchange" and not "admixture"; therefore he holds that necessarily the intent is "because of admixture of flavors"; only the body itself is difficult: why does it teach "admixtures," which implies flavors and not exchange of bodies — but this is not difficult, since for both they are forbidden, it is convenient for him to cite even without exchange forbidden, and there is no difference between them; but this is if because of admixture of flavors b'dieved it is forbidden — if so, one may say it is convenient for him to cite that reason; but if because of flavors it is not forbidden at all b'dieved, and because of bodies it prevents b'dieved — it is obvious that it is difficult, that it should have said "because of admixture of bodies" and not "because of flavors," for the latter is only l'chatchila and the former even b'dieved; and the Gemara challenges properly — investigate:

"Twelve issaronim" — see Kereti, which I wrote: meaning that it should be fit to adhere to all walls of the oven, above and below and on the sides, with dough to bake; and therefore they are not counted, and they did not give a measure for the height of the oven, for according to its height and narrowness the air will decrease and increase — and this is included in the twelve issaronim — see there. "If the issur is a sharp item, etc." — this law Issur VeHeter learned from that of terumah cumin, for we say even taste of cumin in bread is permitted; and Rava brings proof that reicha is not significant, and Abaye rejects: it is different there, for mikalei kli issura; and from this Issur VeHeter inferred that were it not for mikalei kli issura it would forbid, since its reicha is strong and sharp; and Peri Chadash challenged him — all this is for Abaye, but we who establish like Rava — we do not say this mikalei kli — see there.  And Peri Chadash shortened in his challenge: and so it is — why do we need one [reason] — from where do we have to innovate a distinction between sharp items; and that Abaye needed to answer mikalei kli — because Abaye holds in every matter reicha is significant, but for us who establish it is not significant — one need not mikalei kli, and without it permitted even though cumin is as difficult as a sharp item; and moreover it reverses: according to Issur VeHeter's view — if so, even if reicha is not significant in a sharp item like cumin and the like — it is significant; if so what does Rava say "from where do I say" bread baked with cumin — and it is difficult, let him say to himself: behold here all agree reicha is significant, and one must say mikalei kli — and if so, even for Abaye one may say thus — and why does he challenge Abaye; and it is possible to say Maharsha challenged Tosafot, who hold Rava holds like Rav reicha is significant — only bat ta'anah is permitted because it damages — if so what does Rava bring proof from bread with cumin, let him say to himself for he holds reicha is significant — and therefore one must say mikalei kli — if so also for Abaye requires further study.
