**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that he not come to be lenient, etc.</b> And in Rivash he wrote that also the korkeshot that they take from the kosher alone — it is forbidden to take them for this reason; and Darkei Moshe brings it; and even though in Choshen Mishpat siman 306, seif 4, Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that butchers who take korkeshot from the kosher are called proper wages, and if they became neveilah they must pay — and these are Tur's words there: "and even though they do not take anything from treifot, since if it had not become treifah he would have taken — behold this is his wages; and also the korkeshot that they give him from the kosher — they themselves are his wages also for the treifot" — there it deals where the custom is thus, even though it is not correct, as he challenged; also Rambam on the custom that was in his time, that they took wages only from the kosher alone, as in Rivash; or Rabbenu Yerucham holds that when he takes korkeshot it is permitted for him to take from the kosher alone, for we cannot say he will come to be lenient in order to take korkeshot — for we do not suspect him that he will eat treifot, and if so he will have to sell them and fears lest they perceive that he does not eat them and sells them; but when he takes other wages it is forbidden; and perhaps from this the custom spread in some places that slaughterers take korkeshot from the kosher and not from treifot, even though it is a bad custom; and so in Hagahot Maimoni, chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah: "and it is difficult — how do the treasurers take from the butchers? For there is suspicion that because of the butchers he permits" — end of his words; and he is Avodah Zarah; and see Maharshal, chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah, seif 8 — he gave a settlement for this custom; and still it requires study in the matter.

<b>His actions prove against him, etc.</b> For certainly when they gave it to Shimon they did not give it to him to check halfway; and when he saw that he did not check as required, he took it from his hand and went to slaughter — his actions prove against him, etc. — end of Rashba; and see Maharam of Lublin's responsum siman 76.
