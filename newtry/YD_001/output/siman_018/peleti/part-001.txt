Measure of flaw — any amount; and catching a fingernail was not mentioned; and Shach wrote in the name of Maharshal: even if it does not catch the fingernail, nevertheless treifah — only ordinarily we are not concerned for such a small flaw; and he attacked Bach who declares fit a small flaw. And Peri Chadash lengthened on this; and with all the length I found no rest; and the essence of the difficulty: all foundation of Rashba and Tur — Rambam did not mention that it must catch a fingernail; and from this they exempted a flaw of any amount from treifah; and he in chapter 1 of Laws of Beit HaBechirah wrote: altar flaw — measure of catching fingernail — and that invalidates a shechitah knife; and if so, apparently Rambam's words contradict; and what appeared at first — we would say that catching fingernail suffices when slaughtering only trachea in fowl, since a flaw has no power due to small size to catch and inhibit the fingernail — also trachea is not inhibited, for no harm happens to trachea from smallness of flaw, and permitted to slaughter with it; but to slaughter esophagus, which is soft — a small flaw of any amount cannot catch the fingernail and passes over it but inhibits and uproots the esophagus; therefore even any amount inhibits; therefore in Laws of Shechitah Rambam wrote plainly: slaughtered with trachea knife, and also esophagus — even flaw of any amount renders treifah; and he did not mention catching fingernail at all, unlike Laws of Beit HaBechirah, where Rambam's intent is to give a sign to the altar: a flaw on it — catching fingernail — he said like a shechitah knife; and this is one who comes to slaughter trachea — and it is strained as I said. But truth guides his way: certainly hard to say the Gemara considers a measure and challenges and answers; and let it say: flaw of knife — what difficulty? Perhaps he holds flaw of knife is on fingernail, and flaw of any amount flaws, as Rambam wrote flaws. Rather clear to my humble opinion, as expert slaughterers agreed with me: there is no flaw in the world, truly the smallest of the small, that will not be felt by fingernail; and if it does not catch, even felt on fingernail — in truth it is not a flaw, only dependent on the checker's feeling; and according to sharpness and intensity of his feeling — some people even a small flaw do not feel; some feel even a hair's-breadth; some feel nothing at all — all according to man's feeling and intensity of his examination; and this is clear and known to all; therefore as tradition and kabbalah came from the Sages, a flaw was catching fingernail, for they spoke regarding a whole man, upright in feeling, without defect; and Torah did not speak of weak feeling and small examination — then no flaw would fail to be felt on fingernail; and this is the proper measure for him — catching fingernail. However, the Sages, for intensity of their holy separation, saw for their souls: perhaps they do not have correct feeling on their parents as fitting for a slaughterer, and do not feel every flaw; therefore they clarified other ways to investigate what does not need so much feeling — in water and sun, etc.; and these are Rambam's words and his supporters, who wrote flaw of any amount — they did not wish to say catching fingernail, lest the slaughterer not be whole in feeling as required and feel a flaw on avshara but not on fingernail, for he does not feel fingernail inhibits him, and he permits not as required; therefore they said plainly: whatever feels on flaw — treifah. But in truth, if the knife came to the hand of one whole in feeling and he testifies he does not feel on fingernail — in truth it is kosher, for the essence of flaw for us by tradition of Maharam is catching fingernail; therefore in Laws of Beit HaBechirah, coming to give a sign according to the general rule and tradition — and sons of understanding, Beit and altar, plainly they are whole in feeling, and immediately God graced them with understanding: flaw — he gave a sign according to Torah and tradition that everything depends on catching fingernail; and plain — examine. And so one may align Rosh's words — at any rate from our words: even a small flaw of smallnesses is forbidden by Torah, even if not felt on fingernail, because the lack is from the checker — if he had felt on the knife that flaw, certainly it would slightly catch fingernail, but his feeling is coarse and he does not feel fineness — and this is clear; and with this all dispute of poskim on this is settled.

One who wishes, etc. — see Shach, seif kaf 18, who lengthened: not like Bach; rather the essence as Rama wrote — and these are Terumat HaDeshen's words. And in truth in Rashba's words there is contradiction: he wrote that requiring checking is only good advice so as not to lose the slaughtered; and afterward he returned and was precise on the wording "requires" — not so, it does not imply good advice but obligation; and therefore he wrote he is obligated — lest he transgress bal teshachet — see there in Terumat HaDeshen and his chiddushim. To bring himself in; and if he slaughtered for others and did not check and treifah was found — Taz's view: obligate the slaughterer to pay; Taz in seif kaf 8 disagrees; his proof from the hide that came before Rav, and he exempted — perhaps the law is like one who declares fit; and the same here — perhaps on joint-bone it was flawed; and see that he lengthened. And if so, in our case by law of Gemara requiring checking between each and each, and all slaughterers know there is truly obligation as above to check between one and fellow without putting all animals in presumption of doubt of prohibition, and he was not careful — plainly he was negligent; and your friend will answer: Tosafot there in Bava Kamma deals with other rings and Rav had a doubt and nevertheless exempted — implies on doubt one is exempt; strained to say like Nehar Shalom: the butcher knew Rav permits, the butcher knew — and in truth Nehar Shalom goes to his method that Rav, Shach in Choshen Mishpat 306 seif kaf 10, as Terumat HaDeshen: every stringency spread and known among slaughterers, and he did not do, and thereby it became treifah — he is liable as a damager. And Shach's words: in their days shehiyah in last minority was not spread as certain prohibition — strained, for Rama should have emended according to his view that it does not apply in this time. And in my humble opinion, on this Tur in Choshen Mishpat wrote: and so if knife found flawed and he checked first, since we can say on joint-bone it was flawed — we do not take money from doubt — end; implies: if impossible to attribute such as he held the simanim in hand and slaughtered — clear he did not hit joint-bone — why liable even if flawed in hide? And see Tosafot there, s.v. sakin: more common knife flawed on joint-bone last than first; and even though they wrote for R' Hoshaya — nevertheless reasoning did not move, and one does not take money — examine.

Since it went out in permissibility — Bach challenged from seif 15, who ruled in doubt if fell to hard ground — forbidden; and Taz and Shach both answer: here, since it went out in permissibility, it is different; and Shach explains more: here the knife is before us, we establish presumption of prohibition; but in seif 13, where it was lost and permitted, you cannot forbid it afterward. Therefore more correct: in seif 13, where knife was lost, there is a good spin and many ways how flaw happened to knife and several doubts — therefore we attribute to be lenient; but in seif 15 we have no doubt the knife is in presumption of flawed, only to say perhaps it fell on its point to earth — and no more than one doubt — we do not permit; not so seif 13, where there is a good doubt the knife was not in presumption of prohibition as above.

**** ENGLISH ****
The sage may forgive — see Shach, who wrote Rambam in chapter 1 of Laws of Shechitah gave reason therefore they excommunicate: because he will rely on himself another time and slaughter with flawed knife — see there. And if so, what helps forgiving where there is concern of prohibition? And in truth Rambam's own words are hard to align: in Gemara they say showing knife to Torah scholar is because of sage's honor, and Rambam gave reason of concern of prohibition. Strained to say: at first he must show to sage because of concern of prohibition as Rambam wrote — otherwise what did the Sages see to divide honor to Torah scholar here more than other things; but if he was established and showed knife to Torah scholar and it was found good time after time — again he is established lest stumbling block go out from under his hand. And nevertheless requires seeing Torah scholar because of sage's honor; and on this the Sages intended sage's honor; and in this way he may forgive — all strained. Therefore it appears to me Rambam holds what Gemara says: if he did not show knife to Torah scholar they excommunicate him; and if the reason is contempt of Torah scholar's honor — what does it teach? We also know one who treats Torah scholar's honor lightly is liable to excommunication. And perforce intent of excommunication is from concern of prohibition; and Rambam holds like what they say regarding one who rules halachah before his rabbi: the matter does not help to rule halachah; and even if Torah scholar forgives it does not help, as Tosafot wrote: for he rules not according to law, even in setting knife and not showing to sage — the matter does not help to set knife on his parents and he will not feel flaw — this truly needs more understanding than ruling; therefore they excommunicate him: since he is unrestrained he will stumble with knife that has flaw — the matter does not help that he set knife properly — and it is strained; and Rambam's words and Gemara as one.
