<b>א) [סעיף א'] השוחט בסכין בדוקה ונמצאת פגומה אפי' מן הצד.  </b>ואין בכלל זה רושמי הסכין שעושין האומנין בשעת עשיית הסכין כיון שהוא רחוק הרבה מן החוד. כ"כ רש"ל. ט"ז סק"א. וכ"כ לעיל סי' ט' או' ו' בשם כמה פו' ועי"ש עוד מאו' ז' ואילך דינים השייכים לענין זה יעו"ש.

**** ENGLISH ****
(There) The measure of a flaw is any amount, etc. In Gemara (Chullin 17b) many amoraim are cited who were stringent in checking — to check in sun or in water; when one passes its point on water, if flaw in knife they feel it in water — and that is even any amount; and Maharshal concludes in Yeshuot Yaakov, chapter 1 of Chullin siman 38: these amoraim who checked in sun and water — a stringency they added to check after all doubts; and we conclude the law: with these checkings of avshara and atufra it suffices; but certainly if a flaw of any amount is found in sun and water — it is treifah, even b'dieved — see there; Bach, Taz, seif kaf 2; and so Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 5: custom to check on fingernail and flesh; but certainly if a small flaw visible in sun and water is found, even if not felt on fingernail and flesh — invalid, Rambam's words — see there; and so Beli Yadi, letter 3; and such is Taz's view, siman 89, letter 13; Zivchei Rishon, siman 10, in Tamidim, letter 2.

**** ENGLISH ****
(There) The measure of a flaw is any amount, etc. And so Shach, seif kaf 4: the custom is plain to invalidate even a flaw of any amount, not like Bach — see there; and so Peri Chadash, letter 5; Peri Toar, letter 3; Lechem HaPanim, letter 5; Chochmat Adam, general rule 3, letter 11; and this is according to law of Talmud and Shulchan Aruch and several more poskim; but our custom is to forbid even any sensation, even though it does not inhibit a hair's-breadth and is not visible to the sun; and also forbid not to slaughter if before shechitah one sees like a flaw of any amount by sun vision, even though it does not inhibit a hair's-breadth and is not felt on fingernail; and in my opinion correct to do so — see l'chatchila by sun vision on knife point, and be precise that he not find even a bit of whiteness, for even though this is not a flaw, nevertheless through shechitah lightly it will flaw there; therefore do not slaughter with knife until there be no sensation at all and its sharpness black to the eye's vision from head to end without white point; whoever practices thus — good for him; Komtzo, letter 3. However, this is certainly plain: Heaven forbid to rely on vision checking alone without fingernail checking — for aside from transgressing Sages' words who require fingernail specifically, they said expert slaughterers: there are flaws not visible in vision and felt in fingernail checking; and one who relies on knife checking by eye vision alone is among those who feed neveilah; Darkei Torah, letter 20; and as appears from poskim' words that vision helps only to be stringent, not to be lenient, Heaven forbid.

**** ENGLISH ****
And Peri Toar wrote, letter 3: already known and plain that an expert checker feels on fingernail less than a hair's-breadth; and those who feel more — in any case of checking let them feel more; a checker until where his hand reaches. And so Peleti, letter 1: clear to my humble opinion, as expert slaughterers agreed with me: there is no flaw in the world, truly smallest of small, not felt by fingernail, etc.; only everything depends on checker's feeling and sharpness and intensity of his feeling — some do not feel small flaw, some feel even hair's-breadth, some feel nothing — all according to man's feeling and intensity of examination; and this is clear and known to all — end; and so Chochmat Adam, general rule 3, letter 11: one whole in mind feels on fingernail even flaw like hair's-breadth; therefore the finest thin flaw — all is called measure of catching fingernail — see there. Moral of all said: regarding knife checking one needs sharpness, expertise, and intensity of feeling; everything depends on settling mind and fear of Heaven, as written below seif 17 — see there.

<b>טוב) [סעיף ג'] צריך לבדוק הסכין קודם שחיטה.  </b>ואין מברכין על בדיקת הסכין כמו על השחיטה דכל מצוה שאין עשייתה גמר מלאכתה פי' גמר המצוה אין מברכין עליה וראיה מסוכה שאין מברכין על עשייתה ובדיקת הסכין אין עשייתה גמר מלאכה שהרי צריך אח"כ שחיטה. מהרי"ו בהגהות ה"ש. כנה"ג בהגה"ט או' ט' וגם שמא ימלך ולא ישחוט והוי ברכה לבטלה. לה"פ או' ו' זב"ר במוספים או' א' ועוד מפני שאין מברכין על כל מצוה שאינה עיקר מצוה כמ"ש בדברינו לאו"ח סי' ח' או' ב' והכא עיקר המצוה הוא השחיטה.

<b>חי) שם. צריך לבדוק הסכין קודם שחיטה.  </b>ויש בזה ג' טעמים. חדא שמא ישכח לבדקו אח"כ ונמצא אוכל נבילה. ב' משום חשש ברכה לברכה. ג' משום דאסור לנבל בהמה בחנם דקעבר על בל תשחית. שמ"ח או' א' ואפי' אם שמע הברכה מאחר דליכא חשש ברכה לבטלה וגם נבלה שוה ככשרה דליכא משום בל תשחית וסכין מוצנע דאז א"נ משתלי ולא יבדוק אח"כ דליכא איסורא אפ"ה יש ליזהר שלא לשחוט בלי בדיקה תחלה דאפי' הסכין מוצנע מ"מ עלול הוא ליפגם בקל מאליו ואין לסמוך על שמירתו גם אפי' שהנבלה שוה ככשרה אסור לגרום טרפות משום צער ב"ח ומה גם ע"ד הסוד דיש לחוש לתקן ניצוצי הקדושה או המגולגלים כידוע. מחב"ר או' ב' דלא כשפ"ד או' ה' זב"ש או' ד' זב"צ או' ט' מנ"י או' א'.
**** ENGLISH ****
(There) One must check the knife before shechitah. And in this there are three reasons. One: lest he forget to check afterward and behold he eats neveilah. Two: because of concern of blessing in vain. Three: because forbidden to render animal neveilah for nothing, for he transgresses bal teshachet; Shemirat Shabbat, letter 1; and even if he heard the blessing from another — no concern of blessing in vain; and also neveilah equals kosher — no bal teshachet; and hidden knife — then perhaps he will rely and not check afterward — no prohibition; nevertheless one should be careful not to slaughter without checking first, for even hidden knife may easily flaw by itself, and one may not rely on guarding it; also even though neveilah equals kosher — forbidden to cause treifot because of pain to living creatures; and all the more according to the secret one should be concerned to repair sparks of holiness or those dwelling, as known; Mechaber's letter, letter 2, not like Shapirdatz, letter 5; Zivchei Shlomo, letter 4; Zivchei Tzedek, letter 9; Minchat Yitzchak, letter 1.
