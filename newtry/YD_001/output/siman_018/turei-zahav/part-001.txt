<b>סכין שתבדק בהולכה כו'. </b>הך הולכה והבאה אינם כמו הולכה והבאה שלנו שאנו מחזיקין הסכין ביד אחד וחודו למעלה ומוליכין האצבע עליו דלפי זה לא יובנו הדברים שבסעיף זה אלא מיירי שמוליך חוד הסכין על אצבעו וכן בהובאה ובדרך זה בודק הסכין וכן מבואר בסעיף ט' שהיה בדיקתם בדרך זה וא"כ הוה הולכה דבדיקה והולכה דשחיטה חד ענינא. כתב הטור ורי"ף פוסק אפילו באמצע כו' אם העוקץ כלפי הראש כו' וטרח ב"י בפירוש הדברים ונראה שהם כפשוטן דכל פגימה אוגרת יש לה שני עוקצין האחד כלפי הראש של סכין דהיינו עוקץ העליון והשניה כלפי הקתא דהיינו העוקץ התחתון וע"ז אמר שאם נשחז העוקץ של צד הקתא ואין כאן אלא עוקץ העליון אפילו הוא באמצע הסכין כשר בהוליך ולא הביא וכן להיפך וכן פירש מו"ח ז"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A knife that one checks on holacha, etc.</b> This holacha and hava'ah are not like our holacha and bringing back, where we hold the knife in one hand and its point upward and pass the finger on it — for according to that the matters in this seif would not be understood; rather it deals where he passes the point of the knife on his finger, and so on hava'ah; and in this way one checks the knife, and so it is explained in seif 9 that their checking was in this way; and if so, holacha of checking and holacha of shechitah are one matter. Tur and Rif rule: even in the middle, etc., if the point faces the head, etc.; and Beit Yosef toiled in explaining the matters, and it seems they are as their plain sense: every catching flaw has two points — one toward the head of the knife, namely the upper point, and the second toward the handle, namely the lower point; and on this he said that if the point of the handle side was honed and there is only the upper point, even if it is in the middle of the knife, it is valid on holacha and did not bring back, and so the reverse; and so explained Mahari Chagiz, may his memory be blessed.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All are masuchseches.</b> Tur brought this in the name of Baal HaItur: it deals with catching; and Beit Yosef challenged: if so, even one is invalid; and many answered what they answered; and it seems to me a correct answer: after he analyzed the wording — for they said in the baraita, and Tur cited: "a knife that has many flaws" — judge it like a saw; and why did they not say "invalid"? Rather it appears they taught another law: that we find above siman 6, with a knife whose one side is like a saw, etc., that if he slaughtered on the beautiful side it is valid b'dieved; and the same is a long knife that has a flaw and a measure of shechitah remains, etc., as Rama wrote there — it teaches us: even though if there were only one catching there would be permissibility b'dieved if he slaughtered not opposite the catching, nevertheless if there are two or more there is no permissibility, but it is like a saw — meaning as if the whole is flawed and there is no beautiful part at all; so it is correct according to Baal HaItur; and there is a practical difference for this explanation even for us in this law.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one who wishes to bring himself, etc.</b> Levush wrote: one who slaughters for others and afterward a flaw is found — he is liable to pay all the loss, for he is a damager with his hands — end of his words. And it is not clear to me, for it is in Bava Kamma (daf 99): that hide which came before Rav — it explains one who slaughtered his fellow's animal in a matter where the cause was for the rabbis and for R' Yosi it is valid, and Rav ruled treifah and exempted the slaughterer from paying because of doubt of theft — perhaps he holds like R' Yosi that it is valid; and so Tosafot, that Rav had a doubt in this; if so, why not say also there that the slaughterer was negligent by bringing himself into doubt against the rabbis, and certainly they would declare treifah for him? Rather, even so, since there is reason to rule like R' Yosi, one does not take money from his hand; all the more so here, where he thought there would be no treifot at all — certainly one does not take from him, for perhaps on the second joint-bone it was flawed; and against what is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 307, that a butcher is liable when it became neveilah for certain — but if the knife was found flawed and he checked it first, since we can say on the joint-bone it was flawed, we do not take money from doubt — end of his words; from this we learn: if he did not check, he is liable — this case is different, for it deals with the first checking that is required for everyone, and there is no doubt that he brought himself in first; therefore certainly he was negligent by not doing as required, for which he is liable; not so between shechitah and shechitah, which is only ordinary diligence, as Beit Yosef in the name of Baal HaMaor and Rashba, and so is Maharai's view here, that Rama wrote in his name that he may bring himself into doubt; certainly it is better than that hide of Rav that we mentioned; and furthermore, how is gerama relevant here? For Terumat HaDeshen siman 215 wrote that gerama is specifically where there are words of damage, or damage was done immediately; and therefore he exempted one who incited a dog against his fellow, because the damage was done after the incitement — all the more so here, where there is no treifah except from doubt — end of his words; one does not take money from this at all. And even though Terumat HaDeshen siman 186 wrote that for all stringencies that we practice, even though by law it is permitted, such as a slight shehiyah — nevertheless, since it is publicized as forbidden, the slaughterer is liable in wages to pay — it is not similar to this, for there the damage is certain, since you know it will become treifah by this; but here the damage is not certain at the time of the act, as above.

<b>הואיל ויצא בהיתר כו'. </b>בזה מתורץ מה שיש להקשות מסעיף ט"ו שכתב אבל מספק אין תולין כו' וכאן אנו מקילין בספק וכן מסעיף י"ו דפסק דוקא בדק הסכין אחר שחיטה אמרינן אימור שיבר עצמות וכאן אמרינן ליה אפילו לא בדקו אחר שחיטה. ובאמת הכל ניחא דוודאי בכל התורה אנו הולכין אחר החזקה אא"כ הדבר לפנינו לבדקו אז לא סמכינן אחזקה כמ"ש המרדכי ריש חולין בשם ר"י הלוי לענין רוב מצויין אצל שחיטה מומחין הן דבאם הוא לפנינו אין סומכין על הרוב וכן הוא כאן בסכין דכל שהוא לפנינו חייבים אנו לבודקו וכ"כ הר"ן להדיא כדברים האלה בפ"ק דחולין וע"כ כל שלא בדקו אזלא ליה חזקת סכין שהיה יפה בשעת השחיטה שבדקו קודם מ"מ חייב עכשיו לבדקו שנית מ"ה בההיא דסעיף ט"ו וסעיף י"ו שלא נאבד הסכין ממילא חייב לבדקו וכל שלא בדקו אין לו חזקה טובה ולא היה לו שעת הכושר שום פעם עדיין אילו בא לשאול קודם שנולד ספק זה של עכשיו שנמצא פגום לא היינו מורים לו היתר ואין כאן שייך זמן להיתר ע"כ יש להחמיר מספק כאלו אין כאן חזקה משא"כ כאן שנאבד הסכין והיה לו אז חזקה טובה ואילו בא אותו פעם לשאול היינו מורים לו היתר נמצא שהיה זמן להיתר ע"כ אמרינן דאף בנמצא אח"כ והוא פגום כיון שאפשר שנפגם אחר שחיטה וכבר היה לו היתר אילו בא לשאול אין הספק אוסרו אח"כ נמצא הכל על נכון. וכן רש"ל פסק להנהו דינים כמו שנזכרו בש"ע ומו"ח ז"ל הוקשה לו מה אכפת לן ביצא בהיתר דאם אסור מעיקרא בדין מה אכפת לן ביצא בהיתר אלא ודאי הטעם להיתר כאן כיון שנתוסף ספק להקל אחר השחיטה שנאבד הסכין ממילא גם בסעיף ט"ו יש להקל כל שראינו שנפל אפילו לא ראינו שנפל על חודו וע"כ כתב שמאן דשרי בהא דסעיף י"ג שרי אפילו בספיקא דסעיף ט"ו וכתב ששגגה היא לפני השליטים להתיר כאן ולאסור בספיקא דסעיף ט"ו ולי נראה שדברים נכונים הם כי בחזקה תליא מילתא וזו היא שאמרנו הואיל ויצא בהיתר דהיינו שנולד הספק אחר שהיה לסכין חזקה טובה והוראה להיתר ודברי השליטים הם ברורים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Since it went out in permissibility, etc.</b> With this is resolved what is difficult from seif 15, which wrote "but from doubt we do not attribute," etc., and here we are lenient in doubt; and so from seif 16, which ruled specifically when he checked the knife after shechitah they say perhaps he broke bones, and here they say to him even if he did not check after shechitah. And indeed all is settled: certainly in the whole Torah we follow presumption unless the matter is before us to check — then we do not rely on presumption, as Mordechai, beginning of Chullin, in the name of R' Yehudah HaLevi, regarding majority present at shechitah are experts — if it is before us we do not rely on the majority; and so here with the knife: whatever is before us we are obligated to check; and so Ran explicitly with these matters, chapter 1 of Chullin; and perforce, whoever did not check — the presumption of the knife that it was good at the time of shechitah, since he checked before, nevertheless he is now obligated to check again; therefore in that of seif 15 and seif 16, where the knife was not lost — perforce he must check, and whoever did not check has no good presumption and never had a time of fitness; if he had come to ask before this doubt arose that it is now found flawed, we would not have ruled permissibility, and there is no time for permissibility here — one should be stringent from doubt as if there is no presumption; not so here, where the knife was lost and then it had a good presumption, and if he had come to ask that time we would have ruled permissibility — it turns out there was time for permissibility; therefore we say that even when found afterward and it is flawed, since it is possible it was flawed after shechitah and it already had permissibility if he had come to ask, the doubt does not forbid afterward; it turns out everything is correct. And so Maharshal ruled these laws as mentioned in Shulchan Aruch; and Mahari Chagiz was challenged: what do we care about "since it went out in permissibility" — if it was forbidden from the outset by law, what do we care that it went out in permissibility? Rather certainly the reason for permissibility here is because an additional doubt was added to be lenient after shechitah, since the knife was lost — perforce also in seif 15 one should be lenient wherever we saw it fell, even if we did not see it fell on its point; and perforce he wrote that one who permits in that of seif 13 permits even in the doubt of seif 15; and he wrote that it is an error before the rulers to permit here and forbid in the doubt of seif 15; to me their words seem correct, for the matter depends on presumption — and this is what we said: "since it went out in permissibility," meaning the doubt arose after the knife had a good presumption and ruling for permissibility; and the rulers' words are clear.
