(סימן י"ח סעיף י"ב) <b>צריך לחזור ולבדוק אחר השחיטה.</b> קשה לי הא הוי ס"ס ספק שאינו פגום ואת"ל פגום שמא בעצם המפרקת נפגמה והרי הרמ"א פסק (בסי' ק"י ס"ט) דבס"ס אפילו היכא דאפשר לברר א"צ לברר ואף להחולקים דצריך לברר מ"מ נלע"ד דזהו רק אם אחר הבירור נעמוד על הבירור דאסור ודאי או לא אבל היכא דרק ספק א' יתברר אלא דאזי לא יהיה ס"ס ויהיה אסור מספק י"ל דלכ"ע א"צ לברר כיון דהשתא איכא ס"ס וגם אחר הבירור לא יהיה ודאי איסור ובזה מיושב קושי' הש"ך (סימן ק"י בדיני ס"ס אות ס"ו) מההיא דרואה דם מחמת תשמיש דהתם ע"י בירור שפופרת יבורר דממקור הוא אבל עדיין לא הוי ודאי איסור דשמא לאו כל אצבעות שוות א"כ הכא בסכין דאף אם ימצא פגימה עדיין יהא ספק שמא בעצם המפרקת נפגמה יהי' הדין דא"צ לברר וצ"ל דמ"מ באתחזק איסור דאינו זבוח צריך לברר אף בס"ס ובכה"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Siman 18 seif 12) <b>He must return and check after shechitah.</b> It is difficult for me: behold there is a double doubt that it is not flawed; and even if you say flawed, perhaps on joint-bone it was flawed; and Rama ruled (siman 110, seif 9) that in a double doubt, even where it is possible to clarify, one need not clarify; and even for those who require clarifying — it appears to me this is only if after clarifying we will stand on clarification that it is certainly forbidden or not; but where only one doubt will be clarified, so that afterward there will not be a double doubt but forbidden from one doubt — one may say for all opinions one need not clarify, since now there is a double doubt, and also after clarifying there will not be certain prohibition; with this the difficulty of Shach (siman 110, laws of double doubt, letter 14) is settled — from that of seeing blood due to intercourse: there through clarifying with a tube it will be clarified it is from a source, but still not certain prohibition, for perhaps not all fingers are equal; if so, here with the knife: even if a flaw is found, there will still be doubt perhaps on joint-bone it was flawed — the law is one need not clarify; and it must be that nevertheless in presumption of prohibition, that it is not slaughtered, one must clarify even in a double doubt — and in such a case.

(There) <b>And if he touched the joint-bone.</b> See Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen and Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, that we hold like this establishment that we establish Rav Kahana like R' Chiya, and to validate the second — for in Torah law we follow the stringent; and this establishment is for stringency of R' Chiya, who makes animal shechitah like breaking bones — say he touched joint-bone and knife was flawed; and if he slaughtered and knife was lost — all are forbidden from the second onward; and R' Hoshaya for leniency and R' Chiya for stringency; and even though we do not attribute joint-bone in one animal to be lenient — we are concerned for R' Chiya's view if he slaughtered two or three animals; and Rashba concludes in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger: one who is stringent — blessing upon him; and I did not merit to understand this method — how is it proven from this wording that animal shechitah makes the knife not checked? We only find: if after the last a flaw is found, all from second onward are forbidden; and he does not hold like the reasoning that we say above: from when they attribute, perhaps on the second it was flawed — we mean the reasoning is actually difficult, since R' Chiya's reason is bone certainly flaws, hide doubtfully flaws; but regarding in which bone it was flawed, first or second — there is no such distinction; and perforce we must push that the reasoning of this wording: he is more careful at first than at last, and more reasonable to attribute on last than on first; and as Tosafot wrote; and if so, plainly one may say: for this establishment of Rav Kahana like R' Chiya and to validate the second — he holds like the books, that plainly we attribute on last more than on first; therefore all are forbidden; but when lost — whence do we have that R' Chiya is more stringent and judges it as unchecked knife? And even though this method is proven from the Talmud's challenge — if so, sage's checking is required; and this is only if we judge the knife is included in "not checked" and forbidden to slaughter by law without checking; nevertheless it itself is difficult: whence for the Talmud to challenge how proven from this wording that he makes it unchecked? And possible to settle with strain. But more wondrous to me in what he wrote: here R' Hoshaya for leniency and R' Chiya for stringency — where do we find R' Hoshaya lenient here? From that we do not attribute flaw on joint-bone there is no proof, for R' Hoshaya is stringent even when he broke bones, for certainly he makes it unchecked; and what they challenge: if so, sage's checking is required — R' Hoshaya's wording is fine, but this is what they say: for R' Hoshaya's first wording, to invalidate the first, one may say the knife did not become unchecked; but for this wording of R' Chiya, to validate the second — meaning through shechitah it became unchecked; and in truth automatically all the more for R' Hoshaya it is so, for we do not find R' Hoshaya more lenient than R' Chiya — sage's checking required for all, even according to R' Hoshaya; and apparently this is the plain and very spacious way; and I rejected; and see Rashba there: Ramban's view — for R' Hoshaya also one who slaughters with checked knife and it was lost — forbidden, for animal while alive in presumption of prohibition until known; and knife checking inhibits; and difficult for me: if so, difficulty on R' Hoshaya from baraita their kids and chickens were lost — what helps that majority present are experts? Behold before us there is no checking after shechitah, for perhaps truly knife flawed after shechitah; for regarding checking simanim that inhibits, one may say since he is expert he checks simanim, and when he sees not slaughtered, majority finishes immediately shechitah, as rishonim on apostate; but for knife checking — not so; and we must push R' Hoshaya holds they argue first tanna and R' Yehuda: truly R' Yehuda forbids for this reason, perhaps knife found flawed; and as Tosafot beginning chapter, for Ravina, who holds like R' Yehuda; and he holds R' Yehuda's reason: not majority present; and not as rejected, they argue in found in ash heap; and still requires study.

(סעיף ט"ו) <b>ואחר השחיטה שיבר בו עצמות.</b> לפי מ"ש תוס' בחד תירוצא דמה דמשנינן עלה דר"ח אההיא דטבל ועלה סכין אתרע בהמה לא אתרע דאין מקום לחלק אלא דהכי אמרי' דאף אם הסכין אתרע עדיין בהמה לא אתרע דשמא שחט שלא במקום הפגימה והיינו ע"כ בסכין ארוך כמלא ב' צווארים א"כ ממילא לדידן בשיבר בו עצמות ג"כ הכי דהא אף בשיבר בו עצמות קשה מטבל ועלה כיון דנתעסק באותו המין הוי כשיבר עצמות וצריך גם בזה לשינויא דהש"ס סכין אתרע וא"כ בסכין קטן דל"ש כן אסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 15) <b>And after shechitah he broke bones in it.</b> According to what Tosafot wrote in one answer: what we answer on it that R' Chiya on that of dipped and ascended — knife deteriorated, animal not deteriorated — there is no place to distinguish, but rather we say thus: even if knife deteriorated, still animal not deteriorated, for perhaps he slaughtered not at the place of the flaw; and we were so in a long knife like two necks full; if so, automatically for us too when he broke bones in it likewise, for even when he broke bones it is hard like dipped and ascended, since he busied with that species it is like breaking bones; and the Talmud's wording must be changed: knife deteriorated; and if so, with a small knife that is not so — forbidden.
