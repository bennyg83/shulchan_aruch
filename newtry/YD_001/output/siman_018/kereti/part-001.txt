**** ENGLISH ****
So that he not come to be lenient — acharonim wondered at the custom that slaughterers take wages in korkeshot from kosher and not from treifot; ancient custom was in Tur's time, as appears from him, Choshen Mishpat siman 306; and in Avodah Zarah's time before Tur there was a directive on slaughterers — see gloss, chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah; and acharonim toiled to settle the custom. And it seems to me from this the custom spread: certainly one individual slaughters an animal, and if it becomes treifah he will not slaughter more — then there is concern he will be lenient to receive korkeshot; if it becomes treifah he loses; but the custom was established in walled cities that must support the city in meat so meat not lack for every meat-asker; and if there will be treifot in this animal he will hurry to slaughter another to prepare meat for God's people; and what benefit to the slaughterer if he declares this animal treifah — he slaughters another until there is kosher and takes korkeshot from it; and if the first becomes kosher he will not slaughter more — what profit and what loss if he declares fit or invalid? And if one can depict that sometimes the hour presses and they will not multiply slaughter and he may lose korkeshot for this — they did not fix at all, and it is plain.
