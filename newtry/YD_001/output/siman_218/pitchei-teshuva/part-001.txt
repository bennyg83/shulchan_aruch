**** ENGLISH ****Because of them. Baer Heitev of the Rav Maharit, and see in the responsum of Chavat Yair siman 15 regarding one who traveled to distant places and became very weary and vowed that when he comes to his house he will be forbidden to walk a parsah-distance. He returned home and to sustain his life it is necessary from time to time to go to villages around him, some of which are somewhat distant beyond a parsah. And behold this question divides into several aspects. First: whether we follow intention, for he vowed only because of great weariness day after day on a long and distant road frequently without pause — unlike when he goes only once or twice a week. Second: whether even walking two or three parsaot in one day is permitted if he rested in between in a manner that he does not walk a parsah at one time. Third: if he goes less than a parsah to a village and returns home the same day — whether it is permitted, lest going and returning combine to forbid. Fourth: lest even in less than a parsah it is also forbidden because we hold in all prohibitions that even half a measure is forbidden. And the Rav elaborated greatly on this and concluded that there is no substance in the first reason to permit him without release — for it is not comparable at all to all those mentioned in Yoreh De'ah siman 218; on the contrary, we see that he exceeded his measure to forbid even a parsah. And regarding whether he rested in between — there is a distinction: if he stands or sits a little it does not help; but if he establishes a meal in the manner of fixed meals of travelers, the meal interrupts and he is permitted to walk from there onward again up to a parsah. And going and returning do not combine at all, even if he returns immediately after his walking and did not rest at all. And all the more so there is no need to forbid in less than a parsah because of half a measure — see there. And see in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 140 regarding one who had some illness on Rosh HaShanah so he could not go to the synagogue, and his children pressed him that they blow for him and he eat — and he did not want; and because of their pressing he vowed not to eat until after the time of leaving the synagogue. And when they finished their prayer and wanted to leave, a scholar came and preached words of reproof and they delayed leaving — and he wrote that at first glance one could say in vows we follow the language of people and his intention was after the time of prayer; but one may compare to what is written in Rosh HaShanah page 30 — "they stirred to stand," etc. — if so, in our case the people wanted to leave and did not leave — there is also room to be concerned in such a manner. And since a doubt in vows arises to be stringent, and if they want to release him they release him according to the law of vows that are for the need of Yom Tov — end quote. And see in the responsum in Orach Chaim siman 584 se'if kaf gimmel who questioned this and wrote his view that if there is no one to release him there is room to be lenient in this, especially for a person who has illness and it is difficult for him to fast until the time of their actual leaving — see there. And see in the responsum of Me'ir Netivim siman 72 regarding one who swore not to teach his son — Poilish wrote regarding the matter that there is no release for his oath. And behold he produced for him a scheme and taught his son the names of the letters by heart in a manner that the order of them is fluent in the son's mouth well, and the son understands on his own the letters as they are printed until he understands all the writing — if he transgressed his oath. And he wrote that if there were not here a scheme in deception, only he came to ask if it is permitted to teach their names by heart — we would instruct him permission; but since there is here a scheme that he intended to teach him by heart so that he would understand afterward the writing — this one transgressed his oath — see there his proof:
