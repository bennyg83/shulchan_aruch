or if a court wanted to forbid something — whether what he has of that species in an Israelite's hand is forbidden — end; and since it remained in his pouch we are stringent biblically; yet we can say on the contrary from there is proof to permit here, since this remained in his pouch and every rabbinic doubt is lenient, and here too is rabbinic, for there is no prohibition except from the community's enactment; and so I saw in Teshuvot HaRashba siman 281 who brought in the name of one of the great ones who ruled regarding a community enactment not to sell meat above the price they set — and one had slaughtered meat to sell dear since he had it before the enactment — he brought proof from this question and wrote in the name of Baal Teshuvot HaRashba that since it was merely an enactment and not an oath; but whenever they made a ban it is biblical and we are stringent; and he wrote afterward: although I have some hesitation on that ruling of Semag who wrote one who swears not to enter the community enactment is considered a vain oath — apparently community enactment is also biblical — he concludes that in vows that are only rabbinic, such as on something without substance, etc., it is a rabbinic doubt for leniency; therefore according to this there is no place for the "all the more" Rama wrote here regarding community enactment — for first Shulchan Aruch wrote regarding one who swore and an oath is biblical — how does he derive from this regarding enactment that is not from Torah; and although Teshuvot HaRashba wrote he has some hesitation on enactment, nevertheless it is clear there his intent is not to dispute and be stringent on enactment and nullify the practical ruling of that great one; and indeed I already wrote in siman 228 seif 33 to resolve Teshuvot HaRashba's hesitation well — specifically where it is to fence a matter of sin it is biblical, unlike where it is to save money or other reasons not related to sin. I again saw in Rashal first chapter of Chullin siman 36 who brought this Rosh explanation on the question we mentioned — Rosh's first answer that it emerges regarding one who forbids himself, etc. — follows that it is a rabbinic doubt, for whenever a person forbids himself in vow language saying he will not eat a certain thing without attaching a forbidden matter it is only rabbinic; and he disputes Rosh on this, for in vows one follows people's language and according to people's language it is forbidden to eat from this species when the time arrives; therefore the second answer of Rosh applies regarding when a court wanted to forbid, etc. — rabbinic doubt for leniency; nevertheless specifically when a court wanted to forbid, but if they decreed with ban and curse not to eat this thing it is biblical, for a court has power to ban and excommunicate even beyond the law if their intent is to fence; if so it is a biblical doubt and stringent; and according to this what was done in days when sages of Russia gathered the majority of the people not to drink ordinary Israelite wine unless notable witnesses testify to its kashrut and they limited time for their decree — all that already remained in their hands is also forbidden, for it is a biblical doubt — end Rashal; behold in enactment without ban he clearly permits in doubt; and since in Rama's case it was rabbinic doubt we are lenient and there is no prohibition unless the enactment was with community ban; and even here there is a practical difference if that remaining thing became mixed in some mixture where there is doubt — it is a double doubt since the prohibition itself is not clear but only from what remained in his pouch in the Gemara; and when a doubtful prohibition became mixed — when is it double doubt — was explained in siman 110 above end of seif 9 that two mixtures are required; unlike if the source were Rashbatz's words and the prohibition were certain — it would not be called double doubt except with three mixtures as written in siman 110 there; and truth will teach his way that this is only doubtful prohibition as Rosh explicitly explained — so appears clear to me.
