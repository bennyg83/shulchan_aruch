**** ENGLISH ****
How we follow the intention of the one who vows; it contains six se’ifim. Regarding anyone who vows or swears, we look at the matters on account of which he swore or vowed, and we learn from them to what he intended, and we follow the subject matter and not the literal meaning of the speech. How so? If he was carrying a load of wool or flax, sweated, and its odor was foul, and he swore or vowed that wool or flax would never come upon him — behold, he is permitted to wear wool or flax garments and to cover himself with them; he is forbidden only to drape them over his back. If he was wearing wool garments, suffered distress in wearing them, and swore or vowed that wool would never come upon him — he is forbidden to wear wool, but permitted to carry a load on it, and permitted to cover himself with wool shorn from the living, for he intended only regarding wool garments. And likewise all similar cases. {Rama: And all this is specifically when one vows between himself and himself; but when one vows to his fellow, wherever the language of the vow includes, we follow it, unless there is clear presumptive evidence (so it appears in Rivash and in Ramban).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One against whom a false accusation was made because he sold wine to Ishmaelites, and on account of this he swore that he would not make wine to sell — and he had wine already made beforehand — it is forbidden to sell it. {Rama: For even though he said “that I not make,” we follow his intention (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbatz). And all the more so a community that enacted an ordinance like this — they are forbidden to sell even what they had beforehand (the view of the Rav from the implication of Rashbatz; and not like Terumat HaDeshen, siman 481).}

**** ENGLISH ****
If he said to his fellow, “Lend me your cow,” and he said to him, “It is not available,” and he said, “Konam that I not plow with it forever” — if it was his custom to plow his field himself, he is forbidden to plow with that cow; but others are permitted to plow for him with it. And if it was not his custom to plow himself, but sharecroppers plow for him — he and everyone are forbidden.
