**** ENGLISH ****
Regarding idolaters — to save them from death; it contains 2 se’ifim. Idolaters from the 7 nations, with whom there is no war between us and them, and shepherds of small animals from among Jews in Eretz Yisrael at a time when most of the fields belonged to Jews, and those similar to them: one does not cause their death, and it is forbidden to save them if they are inclined to die — for example, if one saw one of them who fell into the sea, he does not raise him up, even if he will give him payment. Therefore, one may not heal them even for payment, unless where there is concern of enmity, {Rama: for then, even for free it is permitted, if he cannot evade [treating him] even for free (Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban); and likewise, it was permitted to try a remedy on a Canaanite slave, if it will be effective (Tosafos and Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin; and in Semag).} To what does this apply? To a Jew who is a ba’al aveiros, who persists in his wickedness and repeats it constantly — for example, shepherds of small animals, who became lawless with robbery and go in their folly. But a Jew who is a ba’al aveiros who does not persist in his wickedness constantly, but rather commits transgressions for his own benefit — for example, he eats neveilos out of appetite — it is a mitzvah to save him, and it is forbidden to stand over his blood.

**** ENGLISH ****
The apikorsim — that is, those who serve idolatry — or one who commits transgressions in order to provoke: even if he ate neveilah or wore shatnez in order to provoke, behold he is a kofer. And the apikorsim — that is, those who deny the Torah and the prophecy of Israel — they would conduct themselves at the time of the Beis HaMikdash to kill them: if one has the power in his hand to kill them with the sword in public, he would kill; and if not, he would come with pretexts until he causes his killing. How so? If he saw one of them who fell into a pit, and there was a ladder in the pit, he comes first and removes it, and he would say, “Behold, I am occupied with bringing down my son from the roof, and I will return it to you,” and similar things. {Rama: And see in Choshen Mishpat siman 425: meshumadim who apostatize themselves to idolatry, and defile themselves among idolaters to serve idolatry like them — behold they are like meshumadim “to provoke,” and they would lower them and not raise them (Tosafos, perek Ein Ma’amidin). Nevertheless, if he comes to repent, they did not act stringently with him, for it is difficult to separate from them, and we are concerned that he will not return to his deviancy (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 158).}
