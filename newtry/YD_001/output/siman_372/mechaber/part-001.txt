**** ENGLISH ****
Permission of impurity in a place of mitzvah, and the law of gentile graves. In it are 2 seifim. Even though a kohen is forbidden to enter a beit haperas (meaning: a field in which a grave was lost and a grave was plowed in it, its measure one hundred amot) or lands of the nations — if he must go there to marry a woman or learn Torah and has no other way, he may pass through there even if he could learn elsewhere; and likewise one passing through may follow to console him; and likewise he may become impure with their impurity to judge with gentiles and argue with them, because it is like saving from their hand; and so all similar cases: {Rama: A kohen lying naked in an ohel with the dead and he did not know — they should not tell him except call him generally to leave so he dress first; but if they already told him, it is forbidden to wait until he dresses — specifically in an ohel of the dead, which is d'oraisa impurity; but in beit haperas or lands of the nations, which is d'rabbanan impurity, he dresses first, for human dignity is great (Terumat HaDeshen siman 285), as explained above siman 303 regarding kilayim. A kohen sleeping and a dead person with him in an ohel — they must wake him and tell him to leave (responsum of Maharil siman 69).}
