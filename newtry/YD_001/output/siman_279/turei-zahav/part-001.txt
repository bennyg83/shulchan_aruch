Yehuda — because of blemish of the first, for one man reads in two Torah scrolls — meaning he said because of a blessing not needed; Rashi explained — if he brings another Torah scroll the reader would need bless before and after it; therefore he reads orally, for with one blessing he exits. And although here there is no blemish of the first — for behold it is blemished and lacking — nevertheless because of R. Eliezer one must be concerned because of unnecessary blessing; they dispute only — one said one thing and one said one thing and they do not dispute. And that which we rule ab initio one reads orally missing verses — this applies if he has not yet read three verses, or he is fourth on Rosh Chodesh or on Chanukah who must finish the parashah that one does not add to them, or the lack is adjacent to the parashah two verses — for one does not leave in a parashah less than three verses, for it is like the High Priest who is obligated to read everything; but if he already read three verses and far from the parashah so he can finish and bless — he blesses "Who gave" and they bring another Torah scroll — end quote. And Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim siman 143 wrote on Mordechai's words — we do not practice so, but as written here; and this is his rabbi R. Yosef Bei Rav's view — see there Rambam's words; and I am not worthy to decide, but it appears what Mordechai challenged from unnecessary blessing if they take out another Torah scroll is not difficult at all — for first he was precise that it is very astonishing why another blessing is needed on another Torah scroll regarding the High Priest, and what he blessed initially does not suffice also for the second; behold in other places we do not say so, such as one who blesses on fruits and ate and afterward they brought him more of that kind — need not return and bless, as written in Orach Chaim siman 206. And further he brings there in Beit Yosef in name of Yerushalmi — truly water one blesses on and those waters before him went and others come while he drinks — therefore he intended from the start; and the reason is explained there in Orach Chaim — therefore he intended initially, unlike lupin that fell from his hand and takes another must return and bless, for his intent was not on it initially, as stated there in Orach Chaim in name of Yerushalmi to distinguish. And likewise one who slaughters who blesses on what they will bring him to slaughter — although not all were before him when he begins to slaughter — need not return and bless, as written in siman 19. And if so, here too let us say regarding Torah scroll he intended initially also for the second; and if so he would read also parashat VeAsor LaChodesh in another Torah scroll — and it appears clear Torah scroll of the High Priest is different, for two mitzvot are before him initially and he knows initially he must do two mitzvot, and one is not exempt in its fellow — example: where there is no Levi in synagogue, the kohen reads also in Levi's place and returns and blesses, although at time of blessing he blessed initially he knew he would read a second time in Levi's place — nevertheless since this is another mitzvah he cannot exempt it initially with one blessing. So too here — since at time of blessing the High Priest blessed initially he knew he would need afterward take out another Torah scroll for another reading — therefore he must bless again; automatically this is not like water — although there too he knew initially other water would come, nevertheless drinking twice or more is not considered obligatory, unlike the High Priest who certainly had to read two readings in two Torah scrolls — if so two mitzvot before him with obligation on both, and not like slaughter we mentioned — for there he has no necessity initially to slaughter more, therefore he can include all in one blessing; and like eating one need not bless on each olive by itself because authority is in his hand to eat what he wishes, and all that arose in his mind to eat he exempts with one blessing — and so you find many times. Therefore Gemara says the High Priest reads parashat VeAsor LaChodesh orally — no other blessing needed except on what he reads in another Torah scroll; but on oral reading they did not enact a blessing. And after this is proven, there is no question at all in finding invalidity in the Torah scroll that he must bless again — for here there are not two obligations, rather the second comes after the first was disqualified; and with this we say at time of blessing he intended if this Torah scroll be invalid the blessing extends to the second — unlike lupin that fell from his hand inadvertently, he did not think initially he would eat another eating from the remainder, as Rama wrote in Orach Chaim siman cited — end quote; and certainly need not bless again on Torah scroll they take out again, provided he did not read three verses — for if he read three verses all agree he blesses final blessing after reading the invalid one, as Rama wrote in Orach Chaim siman 143; but certainly he has not yet read three verses — behold he has not yet fulfilled any part of reading except through the second, and there is no other mitzvah but correction completing the lack of the first so he fulfills what he blessed on the invalid one — therefore need not return and bless, and it is like all worldly matters we mentioned nearby. And if so Shulchan Aruch's way here per his rabbi is correct, and Mordechai's proof he cited is not difficult. And further Mordechai wrote proof for his words from what is written in Megillah chapter 2 — "if all of it" versus "if part of it" — meaning if all is effaced or torn one does not fulfill with what is read orally; if part of it — one fulfills with what is read orally; and from this he learned for Torah scroll lacking, etc., one reads orally — and this is astonishing, who compares megillah to Torah scroll — for in Torah scroll even if partially torn it is invalid. And I do not know any superiority in Mordechai's way over R. Yosef Bei Rav and Shulchan Aruch after him; on the contrary it is a fortiori — if you can say at time of his blessing he intended also if invalid he reads orally, a fortiori you can say he reads in another Torah scroll since all is one mitzvah. However if it is difficult — behold it is difficult from what Gemara challenges in Yoma regarding High Priest who reads VeAsor orally — why not roll and read; and they answer because of honor of congregation; Rashi explained they should wait — and similarly; and again he challenges let us bring another Torah scroll and read; Rashi explained he would roll to VeAsor, etc.; and they answer because of blemish of, etc., as I brought above. And if so it is difficult for R. Yosef Bei Rav and Shulchan Aruch — here too let us be concerned for honor of congregation and not take out another, for when we take out another he must roll in it — for what helps there the other is because they already prepared for this and rolled it to its place, unlike here in case of error there is more trouble of rolling; regarding High Priest here would be doubly taking out another Torah scroll and rolling it. And if you say now we are not concerned for this honor of congregation — this is not so, for Beit Yosef concludes in Orach Chaim siman 144 there is no distinction between times in this. Therefore one must answer — here necessarily must take out another afterward when this one finishes his reading and another stands in his place — if so why not take out another immediately and the first too reads in a valid scroll; therefore it appears to my humble opinion correct that certainly even though one must not change from what Shulchan Aruch wrote and established halachah here and in Orach Chaim — nevertheless if error is found in the one reading last with no reader after him, then he practices like Mordechai; but not for reasons we mentioned above, rather for honor of congregation which is Gemara — and even though afterward he reads for haftarah, nevertheless honor of congregation does not apply here, for otherwise we interrupt somewhat in saying Kaddish; and if error found in haftarah reading too — one does not take out another but says orally per Mordechai; and although in Orach Chaim siman 144 Beit Yosef wrote in name of Orchot Chaim — on a day they take out two Torah scrolls where there is no other Torah scroll, better to defer honor of congregation and roll the Torah scroll and read in the other place, and we do not say he should read it orally — there it is different, for certainly one cannot say he makes blessing on second reading for entire parashah he reads orally, unlike here where he makes blessing on Torah reading but afterward error is found — why not read orally like the High Priest and not trouble the congregation.
