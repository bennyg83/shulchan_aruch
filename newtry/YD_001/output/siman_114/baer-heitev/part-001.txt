<b>קולא. </b> כ' רש"ל נ"ל דוקא במקום שמקילין קאמר אבל האידנא שנעשה בקצת מקומות בעו"ה היתר גמור מותר לשתות שם שכר מעובד כוכבים רק שלא ישתה בביתו עכ"ל וביאר הט"ז דבריו דדוקא במקום שיודעים שיש איסור ביין של עובדי כוכבים רק שנוהגין קולא בו שם עשו חכמים היכרא שלא ישתה שם שכר אטו חמרא שירגישו שלא יקילו עוד אבל במקומות שנוהגים היתר גמור והם סבורים שאין כאן איסור כלל נמצא שלא ידעו ממה להפריש כי היתר גמור הוא בעיניהם ע"כ אין שייך בזה גזירה ומותר לשתות שם שכר בין מישראל בין מעובד כוכבים עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****Leniency. Rashal wrote: it appears to me he speaks only of a place where they are lenient; but nowadays that in some places, with permission, there is complete permission, it is permitted to drink beer of a gentile there, only that he should not drink in his house — end of his words. And Turei Zahav explained his words that specifically in a place where they know there is prohibition in gentile wine but they practice leniency there, the Sages made a marker that he should not drink beer there lest it be treated like wine, so they will perceive not to be lenient further. But in places where they practice complete permission and they think there is no prohibition at all, it is found they do not know from what to separate, for complete permission is before their eyes; therefore no decree applies here, and it is permitted to drink beer there, whether from a Jew or from a gentile — end of his words.

**** ENGLISH ****That they mixed. Turei Zahav wrote, and it deals with when one does not know which gentile does not mix, for then on each and every one I say this one did not mix, and even if the majority's practice is to mix, the one who does not mix saves all of them; but if it is known which gentile does not mix, he does not save the others whose practice of the majority is to mix. [Also if there is one house that certainly mixes wine into it, even if there is a house that certainly does not mix, it does not help him, similar to two paths, etc.; rather we follow the majority — see there.]

And it is possible that in stam yeinam the Sages were lenient and we do not decree lest he take from the fixed source — end of his words. (And Nachalat Binyamin wrote that when explicitly automatic, or a gentile took it not in our presence, we do not decree even in Torah prohibitions — see there.)

<b>הרשב"א. </b> כתב הש"ך דוקא הרשב"א שהיה יודע בודאי שבכל הארץ ההיא היו מזלפין עליו יין אבל אנן דלא ידעינן בודאי א"כ אמרינן דא"א שלא יהא א' שאינו מזלף ומציל על כולן ועיין עוד שם שמביא כמה ראיות להתיר אך מה שכתב אף אם היה בו חוטי בשר בטלין ברוב השיג הוא בעצמו ע"ז בספרו נה"כ דהא מין בשאינו מינו הוא ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****Rashba. Shach wrote: specifically Rashba, who knew certainly that in all that land they sprinkled wine on it; but we who do not know certainly — if so we say it is impossible that there not be one who does not sprinkle, and he saves all of them. And see further there that he brings many proofs to permit; but what he wrote, even if there were threads of meat in it nullified in majority, he challenged this himself regarding this in his book Nachalat Binyamin, for behold it is a different type in a different type — see there.
