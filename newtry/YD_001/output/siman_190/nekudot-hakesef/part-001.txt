and this is the view of our French rabbis and Ramban whom Beit Yosef brings, and Rashba's words, etc. What he distinguished between chametz and paths from here is not clear as I will explain; also what he challenged Derishah who wrote specifically in rabbinic matters we say thus — he was not precise, for even in Torah matters we say so in Pesachim regarding two paths, and in ch. 2 of Ketubot regarding permitting a woman, etc. He was not precise — how could one say in Torah prohibition thus; we do not permit except from doubt leniently, and Torah doubt is stringent; rather the entire sugya in Pesachim proves specifically in rabbinic matters we say thus — and so is clear from Rashi there who explained on "there is one group of chametz," etc., two paths, etc. — Rashi: if each asked alone tehor; Beit Din cannot declare tamei as we hold doubt of impurity in reshut harabim is tehor, etc.; and regarding houses too if each asked alone we tell each you need not check since bedikat chametz is rabbinic we attribute leniently — end quote; (There s.k. 41) He challenged Rama's responsum; his proofs can be rejected; nevertheless his ruling is reasonable — examine. Rav Yerocham Netiv 15 part 2 wrote plainly we are never concerned above the makor, etc., until if the stain is found on garment and on her flesh and she did not pass through the butchers' market from the belt down she is tamei, from the belt up tehorah — end quote. What he wrote at end "from the belt up" perforce deals with one who turned aside as initially, and even so tehorah — and per Rashba's view, for otherwise even on her flesh tehorah as implied from the beginning of his words and as proven in the Gemara beginning of the chapter "one who sees a stain" which asks: if from the belt up on flesh why tamei — and we learned "she saw blood on her flesh not opposite the makor — tehorah" — namely without passing through butchers' market as the mishnah deals "she saw blood," etc. And further the question was not yet resolved what answers "like where she passed through butchers' market"; and if so what does it ask — why not answer always from belt up and when she did not pass through butchers' market and on flesh tamei and on garment tehorah. And the mishnah deals with passing; but certainly above the belt when she did not turn aside, even without passing through butchers' market, even on flesh tehorah — and so all poskim; certainly deals when she turned aside — in my opinion.
