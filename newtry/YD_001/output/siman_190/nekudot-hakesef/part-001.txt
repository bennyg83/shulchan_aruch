(Taz s.k. 17) He challenged the Bach; and in my humble opinion the Bach's words are correct, as I will explain. What the Bach wrote — and this is the implication in Tosafot — does not refer only to when they come to ask at once and both are impure; rather one who examines the Bach will see explicitly that it applies even when they engaged one after another and both are impure. The Bach's language: two women who engaged, etc. — it appears he comes to teach us even where they did not engage with the bird together but one after another; and if the first had been asked, we would purify her since she engaged; nevertheless, since they came to ask together, both are impure — and this is Tosafot's implication; therefore his language is as I explained. What he wrote: since they came to ask — each one — is because he holds that generally we rule thus, that when they come to ask one after another we attribute leniently, as he ruled above siman 111 seif 5. What he brought as proof from Rashba in Torat HaBayit 178b is no proof at all, for Rashba follows his own reasoning — he forbids also above siman 111; but according to what we hold there to be lenient one after another, as Shach wrote there in Siftei Kohen small seif 19, if so the same law applies here; and this is the view of our French rabbis and Ramban whom he cited in Torat HaBayit and Beit Yosef 205a. Rashba's language: some of my teachers, etc. — and if you say: what is different about an unchecked cloth from the mishnah of three women who wore one garment and blood was found on it — all are impure; our French rabbis answered that there too by right all should be pure, but since they depend on this garment it is like two paths — if they come to ask at once both are impure, one after another both are pure. These matters were not clarified for me; if so, asking about a garment applies even to a gentile who did not see, and if one of three women saw a stain after asking, the others are purified since one of them does not come to ask. Ramban's words are close to Tosafot's, etc. What Rashba challenged from a gentile who did not see — I did not reach the end of his intent; there, since she did not see, how can she be attributed? Also what he challenged — that one of them does not come to ask — one can say it is not so; according to Tosafot and supporters, in such a case we purify each one, since they came one after another; and that the Gemara did not distinguish is because it only came to teach the fundamental law that regarding a stain all are impure. Possibly for this reason Raah in Bedek HaBayit challenged Rashba. What he distinguished between chametz and paths versus here is not clear as I will explain. Also what he challenged Derishah — that we say this only regarding derabbanan matters — is not precise, for even regarding deoraita we say so in Pesachim regarding two paths, etc., and in chapter 2 of Ketubot regarding permitting a woman, etc. He is not precise — how can one say thus regarding deoraita prohibition? We only permit due to safek lekula, and safek deoraita lechumra; rather the entire sugya in Pesachim proves we say this only derabbanan, as Rashi explained there on the case of chametz before two checked houses. Regarding paths too — if each asks separately we tell each he need not check, since bedikat chametz is derabbanan we attribute leniently. Evidently only derabbanan matters do we attribute leniently due to safek derabbanan lekula; and regarding paths whose safek is in public domain is pure — but not regarding deoraita prohibition. What we attribute leniently in Ketubot chapter 2 is only regarding a captive woman where they were lenient — as stated in the Gemara. Accordingly here too we attribute leniently: just as with chametz we attribute leniently because bedikat chametz is derabbanan — meaning biblically nullification suffices and the rabbis required checking — if a stain is found on her body she is pure until she feels blood from her flesh, and the rabbis decreed on the stain where there is nothing to attribute. Therefore where they came to ask one after another they are pure.

(There s.k. 41) He challenged Rama's response — and one can reject all his proofs; however his ruling is reasonable; investigate. Rabeinu Yerucham, Netiv 15 part 2, wrote plainly: we never concern ourselves above the genital area, etc., until if the stain is on her garment and on her flesh and she did not pass through the butchers' market — from the belt and below she is impure, from the belt and above pure — until here his language. What he wrote at the end 'from the belt and above' — perforce it deals with when she was startled, as discussed initially; and even so she is pure, as Rashba's view — for otherwise even a stain on her flesh would be pure, as implied from the beginning of his words and as proven in the Gemara in the chapter HaRo'eh, which asks: if from the belt and above on her flesh, why impure? We learned: she saw blood on her flesh not opposite the genital area — pure; and this is without passing through the butchers' market, as the mishnah deals with 'she saw blood,' etc. Further, the Gemara had not yet concluded what it answers — such as that she passed through the butchers' market; and further, if so, why does it ask and not answer that always from the belt and above, when she did not pass through the butchers' market? Therefore on her flesh impure and on the garment pure. The mishnah deals when she passed; but certainly above the belt when not startled, even if she did not pass through the butchers' market and even on her flesh — pure; and so all poskim hold; but certainly it deals when she was startled — in my humble opinion.
