**** ENGLISH ****
(Shulchan Aruch seif 25) Two women who both handled one bird that has no blood but the size of a sela, and a sela-sized amount was found on each — both are impure. The Taz sought to distinguish between stains and chametz: there, regarding safek nichnas in one house we are lenient — but this is not Rambam's view, who is stringent in one house; and therefore Rambam's reason for leniency in two houses is that even regarding two paths in a private domain, where all agree both are impure if they come to ask one after another, it is different there with two paths because on one side there is safek tumah in a private domain which is biblically impure — therefore one must impurify also in two paths and two houses. But regarding chametz, one side is safek nichnas in one house which is only rabbinic because of safek bedikat chametz derabbanan — therefore they are not stringent in two houses. And likewise in all matters where they were stringent in its safek in one [house] derabbanan, they are lenient in two one after another. And further one could say Rambam's reason for leniency in two houses is as Hagahot HaTaz wrote in chapter 2 of Hilchot Chametz halachah 12 — certainly one of them is checked; and therefore regarding a captive woman too the rule is that we are lenient in one hiding place even though otherwise we are stringent in safek; and if so, Hagahot HaTaz's words to be stringent regarding stains — since without attributing the safek it is forbidden and no worse than chametz and captive woman — do not hold; rather as Derishah wrote, it is possible both are impure, and therefore it must be so according to Rambam who is lenient in chametz even in one house; and if you say stains equal chametz and as Nekudot HaKesef wrote, we should not impurify both even in one house — rather stains are weaker and not like Nekudot HaKesef; see Sefer Toldot Adam.
