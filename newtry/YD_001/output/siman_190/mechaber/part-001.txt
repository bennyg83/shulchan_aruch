**** ENGLISH ****
The laws of ketamim and the bedikah of a woman; it contains 54 se’ifim. By Torah law, a woman does not become tamei and is not forbidden to her husband until she feels that blood came out from her flesh. And the chachamim decreed regarding a ketem that is found on her body or on her garments, that she is tamei and forbidden to her husband even though she did not feel, and even if she checked herself and found [herself] tehorah. And she needs a hefsek taharah — that she should check herself and find [herself] tehorah, and afterward she should count 7 nekiyim, excluding the day of the finding (as though she certainly saw, as will be explained below in siman 196). And if she felt that her makor opened to emit blood, and she checked afterward and found nothing, there is one who says that she is tamei.

He lent it to a minor who never saw, and this minor wore it after she had intercourse, before the wound healed; or he lent it to a young woman who did not see and she wore it within the first 4 nights of her intercourse; or he lent it to one sitting on dam tohar — he attributes to them, even in this time (Raavad; and Tur in the name of some say; and the same law applies; and Rabbeinu Yerucham according to Rambam; and Ra’ah in Sefer Ba’al HaNefesh). **** ENGLISH ****
He lent it to one who has a ketem — whether she was already sitting on the ketem before she borrowed, or whether she saw a ketem on another garment after she borrowed this one — we do not attribute one to the other, neither this way nor that way, and both need to be concerned. And all the more so if he lent it to a tehorah, and she again wore it, that both need to be concerned (Tur; and so they rule plainly in the Talmud and the poskim many times).
