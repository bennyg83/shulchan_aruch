<b>שיש בהם סכנה וכו'. </b>משמע דעת המחבר דאם אין בו סכנה בכל ענין מותר וכדפי' רש"י ורי"ף ורמב"ם אבל הרא"ש וטור פסקו דבדבר שאין בו חולי ואין בו סכנה כמו הקזה וכה"ג לא יתרפא ממנו דכיון דאין ברפואה זו אומנות אם ימיתנו לא יתלו הדבר בחסרון ידיעתו אלא יאמרו בלאו הכי הגיע זמנו למות או ששונאו היה והמיתו ולא מרע נפשיה לגבי אחרים וכן דעת התו' בשם ר"ת וכן הר"ן וגם ר' ירוחם והאגודה הביא דברי ר"ת במסקנא:
**** ENGLISH ****
That there is danger in them, etc. It appears the Mechaber's view is that if there is no danger — in every matter it is permitted; and so explained Rashi, Rif, and Rambam; but the Rosh and Tur ruled that in a matter without illness and without danger, such as bloodletting and the like, one may not be healed from him — since there is no expertise in this medicine, if he kills him they will not attribute it to lack of knowledge but will say his time to die had come anyway, or that he was his enemy and killed him — so he does not harm himself relative to others; and so is the view of Tosafot in the name of Rabbenu Tam, and so the Ran; and also R. Yerucham and the Agudah brought R. Tam's words in the conclusion.

<b>אבל כו' בשכר בכל ענין מותר. </b>ומהרש"ל כ' ע"ז חידוש הוא דש"ס סתמא קאמר וגם גדולה השנאה כו' דלא חייש לשכרו היכא דליכא למיחש למירע חזקתו וכן דעת רוב המחברים ועיקר עכ"ל ואחריו נמשך הב"ח וכתב דמדכתבו הרא"ש והר"ן ושאר מחברים בחולה שאין בו סכנה דמתרפאים ממנו שאולי ימות יתלו הדבר בחסרון ידיעתו ויקפח פרנסתו אלמא דמיירי בשכר ואפ"ה בחולי שיש בו סכנה או בריבדא דכוסילתא אין מתרפאים ממנו עכ"ל ולא עמדתי על סוף דעתם דמ"ש דש"ס סתמא קאמר אדרבה בש"ס (דף כ"ו ע"א) קאמר מאי רפוי ממון ומאי רפוי נפשות אילימא רפוי ממון בשכר נפשות בחנם ופרש"י דלא קטיל דחייש לפסידא דאגריה אלמא דס"ל לש"ס דבשכר שרי ונהי דש"ס מסיק רפוי ממון בהמתו נפשות גופו היינו משום דפריך ליתני מתרפאים מהם בשכר כלומר דלישנא דרפוי ממון ונפשות לא משמע הכי מכל מקום הדין אמת דבשכר שרי דאל"כ ה"ל לאקשויי מאי שנא בחנם ומאי שנא בשכר ומ"ש וגם גדולה השנאה כו' לקשי אש"ס דבעי לפלוגי בהכי ולא אמרינן גדולה השנאה ועוד תימה מנין לו לומר גדולה השנאה וכי בעובד כוכבים שהוא שונא לו עסקינן והלא בסתם עובד כוכבים קא מיירי אלא מפני שהם חשודים על ש"ד אסור וכל שבשכר לא מפסיד אגריה ומ"ש דמדכתבו המחברים יקפח פרנסתו אלמא דבשכר מיירי ליתא דה"פ שיתלו הדבר בחסרון ידיעתו ויאמרו שהוא אינו בקי ויקפח פרנסתו לגבי אחרים שלא יתרפאו ממנו מחמת חסרון ידיעתו תדע דהא כתבו בריבדא דכוסילתא וכה"ג דלא יתלו הדבר בחסרון ידיעתו אלא יאמרו ששונאו היה ואי מקפח פרנסתו היינו שכרו שנוטל מחולה זה א"כ כשיאמרו ששונאו היה אין ספיקא שמקפח פרנסתו אלא ודאי כדפי' ואדרבה משמע מדבריהם איפכא דמיד שרי משום טעמא דאינו מקפח פרנסתו לגבי אחרים כ"ש היכא דמפסיד שכרו לגבי האי דשרי וכל זה ברור ונמצא דברי הרב עולים כהוגן:
**** ENGLISH ****But, etc., for wages in every matter it is permitted. Maharshal wrote: this is a novelty in Avodah Zarah — the Gemara states plainly, and also "great is hatred," etc. — that he is not concerned for his wages where there is no concern to ruin his reputation; so too is the view of most authors — essence; and after him Beit Chadash followed and wrote: since the Rosh, Ran, and other authors wrote regarding a sick person without danger that one may be healed from him — perhaps he will die — they will attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge and diminish his livelihood; behold it deals with wages, and nevertheless in illness where there is danger or in tzamarurin one may not be healed from him — end. I did not stand on the end of their minds in what they wrote that the Gemara states plainly — on the contrary in the Gemara (26a) it says: what is healing money and what is healing lives? If we say healing money for wages, healing lives for free — Rashi: he does not kill because he is concerned for loss of his wages; behold the Gemara holds that for wages it is permitted; and although the Gemara concludes healing money is his animal, healing lives is his body — because it challenges "let it teach one may be healed from them for wages," meaning the language of healing money and lives does not imply so; nevertheless the law is true that for wages it is permitted — otherwise it should have asked what is different for free and what is different for wages. And what is written "and also great is hatred," etc. — is difficult on the Gemara that it should have divided thus and we do not say "great is hatred"; and more — from where does he say "great is hatred," and are we dealing with a gentile who hates him? Behold it deals with a plain gentile — rather because they are suspected of sorcery it is forbidden; and whoever for wages does not lose his wages — and what they wrote that the authors "will diminish his livelihood" — behold it deals with wages — it does not exist; for then they will attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge and say he is not expert and diminish his livelihood relative to others who will not be healed from him because of lack of his knowledge — know that they wrote regarding tzamarurin and similar where they will not attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge but will say he was his enemy; and if he diminishes his livelihood — that is his wage he takes from this sick person — if so when they say he was his enemy there is no doubt he diminishes his livelihood but certainly as explained; and on the contrary from their words it implies the opposite — immediately it is permitted because of the reason he does not diminish his livelihood relative to others, all the more where he loses his wage relative to this one it is permitted. And all this is clear — and the Rav's words stand properly.
**** END BLOCK ****

And the Bach in a later pamphlet elaborated and wrote: even if the gentile does not say explicitly, but we learn so from the implication of his words — there are two ways: (1) when those waters or trees are more available and the gentile says “take from these” — it is forbidden even if he did not say explicitly “take from those of the idol”; (2) when those are not available and he said “take from those of the idol” — also forbidden; and there is no permit except when they are not more available and he also did not say “take from these” — unlike the Shulchan Aruch and unlike the Derushah — end. In my humble opinion the Derushah's view is also so, and so appears the Mechaber who seems to agree.

**** ENGLISH ****Permitted. Darkei Moshe wrote: in my humble opinion those who permit — it appears also for yayin nesech it is permitted to be healed in place of danger where there is nothing to decree to draw after him — such as when he said to him "bring me plain wine"; and so appears in responsum the Rashba (brought by Beit Yosef siman 123) that he compares them to each other — end. And R. Yehudah Loew: even if he brought him wine that was certainly libated for idolatry it is permitted even to drink it in place of danger, since he said to him plainly "bring wine"; and it appears stam yeinam is permitted for everyone in place of danger even for drinking, and all the more in our time where it is obvious it is permitted for drinking in place of danger as it appears explicitly from the Rav's words below nearby; and all the more other prohibitions that are forbidden only in eating are permitted in eating in place of danger — so in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, and Darkei Moshe brings it; so in Orach Chaim klal 59 law 35 in the name of Maharam.
