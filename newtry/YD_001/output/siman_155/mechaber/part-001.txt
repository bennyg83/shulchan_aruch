**** ENGLISH ****
Whether it is permitted to receive medical treatment from a non-Jew; it contains 3 se’ifim. Any injury or illness that involves danger — for which one desecrates Shabbos — one may not receive treatment from a non-Jew who is not an expert for the public (and all who perform bloodletting are considered experts for bloodletting) (Tur and Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos and Rosh, and in Hagahot Maimoni, perek 9, and R' Yerucham, and Agudah, and Semag), for we are concerned about murder. And even if it is a safek whether he will live or die, one does not receive treatment from him; but if he is certainly going to die, one receives treatment from him, for we are not concerned here for chayei sha’ah. And if he said, “This drug is effective,” or “it is harmful,” one may rely on him — provided that he does not take that drug from him. (And some say that all this is forbidden only when the non-Jew treats for free, but if he treats for payment, in every case it is permitted, for he fears the loss of his fee) (Hagahot Ashiri, perek Ein Ma’amidin, from Or Zarua) (and the practice is to be lenient). But if he comes to heal him with an incantation, it is permitted, provided that he does not know that he mentions the name of elilim; but if he knows that he mentions the name of elilim, it is forbidden even if he knows that he will certainly die. And if he is an apikores, even an unspecified incantation is forbidden, for certainly he mentions the name of elilim. (And it is likewise forbidden to learn an incantation from him) (Hagahot Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin):

**** ENGLISH ****
A non-Jew who came to heal a Jew and said to him, “Take from these waters,” or “from such-and-such a tree,” that are of elilim — it is forbidden. But if he said to him, “Take from these waters,” or “from this tree,” and he did not mention to him the name of elilim — even though those waters and that tree are found only of elilim — it is permitted, since he did not attribute the healing to the fact that they are elilim; and some forbid even this, even when he said to him, “Bring leaves,” without specification, and he brought him from the trees of an asheirah.

**** ENGLISH ****
Regarding other prohibitions: one may receive medical treatment in a case of danger even in the manner of their benefit; but when there is no danger, in the manner of their benefit it is forbidden, while not in the manner of their benefit it is permitted — except for kil’ei ha-kerem and basar b’chalav, which are forbidden even not in the manner of their benefit, and are permitted only in a case of danger. {Rama: And see above siman 123: there are those who say that all prohibitions of benefit that are by rabbinic law are permitted for medical treatment even for a sick person who is not in danger (Ran, perek Kol Sha’ah, in the name of “there are those who say,” and Rivash siman 45). And even yayin nesech in our times is permitted for medical treatment, and to make from it a bathhouse, even though it is in the manner of their benefit — provided that he does not eat or drink the prohibition, since there is no danger. And all the more so, it is permitted to sprinkle wine upon a fire, for even for a healthy person it is permitted, for smell is not a matter (Arukh, rule 39). It is permitted to burn a sheretz or other forbidden matter and eat it for medical treatment, even for a sick person who is not in danger, except with wood of idolatry (Arukh, rule 32). And any sick person whom we feed prohibition needs the medical treatment to be known, or by the word of an expert (ibid., rule 59). And we do not permit any prohibited matter to a sick person if he can do the medical treatment with something permitted as with the prohibited matter, even though he needs to wait a bit before he finds the permitted [substance], since there is no danger in the matter (Beit Yosef and a responsum of Rashba, end of siman 134).}
