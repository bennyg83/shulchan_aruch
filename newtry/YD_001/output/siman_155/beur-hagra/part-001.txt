Not like Mordechai chapter 2 of Pesachim who wrote that also in kilaei hakerem forbidden because he holds ashes are permitted because it is not in the usual manner of benefit — therefore he also wrote that all prohibitions not in the usual manner of benefit are permitted ab initio (and see above siman 142 s.k. 28 what is written there, and it appears the Mordechai learned from this) — but Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 12b and the Rosh in Pesachim there wrote there is prohibition, and so the Ramban learns from there that in rabbinic prohibitions even in the usual manner of benefit it is permitted for healing — for Torah prohibitions not in the usual manner of benefit there is nevertheless prohibition; so too the Ran and Rivash. And in Tosafot in Avodah Zarah there they were uncertain regarding idolatry whether one is lashed not in the usual manner of benefit — behold it does not depend on ashes; however even if you say for idolatry one is only lashed in the usual manner of benefit, nevertheless idolatry for healing is forbidden here as the Ran and Rivash siman 255 write — in idolatry even rabbinic prohibition is forbidden for healing even in danger, as written in the chapter "Ben sorer u'moreh" they forbade there in danger even with an unmarried woman [therefore he wrote except, etc., and did not also write all buried things whose ashes are forbidden because not in the usual manner of benefit is permitted for healing] — and in other prohibitions both are permitted in one matter: ashes are permitted, and moreover because not in the usual manner of benefit; and according to the first reason even for a healthy person it is permitted, and so it implies permitted ab initio; and one might say specifically for benefit and not for eating — what is different that both are from the Torah (end).
(Likkut) Permitted to burn a sheretz or, etc. Not precise — likewise with meat and milk that is forbidden, which is among buried things whose ashes are forbidden as written at the end of Temurah; granted other buried things nevertheless one is not lashed for them not in the usual manner of benefit and it is permitted for healing as written in chapter 2 of Pesachim and above in Shulchan Aruch — not so with meat and milk as above in Shulchan Aruch.
Except for idolatry wood. Not because one is lashed for it not in its usual manner of benefit — in Tosafot in Avodah Zarah they were uncertain, and see above siman 142 seif 10 dispute on this — rather because of the seed of idolatry prohibition, even something rabbinic is forbidden (end).
