Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Hilchos Shevuos: If one swore that he would eat and then ate foods unfit for consumption, drank liquids unfit for drinking, or ate neveilos, treifos, and the like, he is exempt from an oath of expression, for he has fulfilled his undertaking to eat, since these items are considered significant by him. It appears that all of this likewise applies to a konam when the vow was made dependent upon whether he eats or does not eat.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But it is forbidden even in any amount.</b> The Tur writes this, but I do not know its source. Moreover, below in seif 15, regarding one who swore, “I will eat it,” and part of it was burned, we say that he need not eat what remains. This implies that there is not even a prohibition if he does not eat the remainder. The words of Bach in seif 8 appear to compare this to the Torah prohibition against half a measure. According to this, one could say that specifically where he swore, “I will not eat it,” even half a measure is forbidden, whereas where he swore, “I will eat it,” he undertook only to eat the entire loaf. Yet this requires study, for what is the basis for such a distinction? This is certainly unlike the law of half a measure. There, since he forbade the item upon himself without qualification, we say that although lashes apply only to a kezayis, even half a measure is forbidden by Torah law. Here, however, he swore only that he would not eat the whole loaf. This resembles one who explicitly swore not to eat a kezayis of bread, who is certainly not forbidden to eat less than that measure. This also appears from the wording of Rashi and Ran, who write that “I will not eat it” means the whole loaf, but that he did not forbid part of it upon himself. All the more so according to Bach above in seif 4, who understands the Tur to maintain that even an oath not to eat less than a kezayis of neveilah does not take effect because half a measure is already forbidden. If an oath not to eat the loaf also created a prohibition upon half a measure, why would a second oath, “I will not eat this loaf,” take effect afterward? This requires study.

<b>אין השניה חלה. </b>והרי אט' וי' דעלמא נדר ואם כן א"א לו לאכול י' שלא יהיה בהן ט' שנדר תחלה עליהם ובכל י' שאכל י"ל שעל ט' מאלו נשבע בראשונה ממילא יש כאן ב' שבועות על ט' ואין שבועה חלה על שבועה משא"כ כשאומר ט' אלו ושבועה שלא יאכל י' דעלמא דהנהו י' דעלמא א"א שיהיה בהן הני ט' שנשבע עליהם תחלה שהרי מאלו ט' נשבע נמצא דאם היה אוכל ט' אלו וי' דעלמא פשיטא שהיה חייב ב' הלכך אם אוכל ט' אלו וא' דעלמא האי א' יש לו לצרף לט' דעלמא שאם היה אוכל עוד ט' דעלמא הוי חייב עליה בשביל שבועה ומגו דחיילה אי' דעלמא חיילה נמי אט' אלו שיצטרף האי א' לט' אלו בענין שיעבור על ב' שבועות א' על י' וגם יש לו כבר שבועה על ט' אלו נמצא שעובר על ב' שבועות והוי דומיא דנשבע שלא לאכול תאנים וחזר ונשבע שלא לאכול תאנים וענבים דחייב ב' על תאנים ואפשר דאם אכל ט' אלו וי' דעלמא דחייב ג' שבועות דומיא דתאנים וענבים דאם אכל ג"כ הענבים פשיטא שחייב ג' ב' אתאנים וא' אענבים. פרישה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The second oath does not take effect.</b> He vowed concerning nine and ten unspecified loaves. It is therefore impossible for him to eat ten loaves without those ten including nine upon which he had already sworn. Regarding any ten that he eats, we may say that nine of them were included in his first oath. Consequently, there are two oaths upon those nine, and an oath does not take effect upon an existing oath. This differs from where he said, “These nine,” and then swore not to eat ten unspecified loaves. Those ten unspecified loaves need not include the specific nine upon which he first swore. Thus, if he ate these nine and ten other loaves, he would certainly be liable twice. Therefore, if he eats these nine and one other loaf, that additional loaf can combine with nine unspecified loaves, for had he eaten another nine unspecified loaves he would be liable by reason of the later oath. Since the later oath takes effect upon that one unspecified loaf, it also takes effect upon these nine so that the one loaf combines with them, and he thereby violates two oaths, one concerning the ten and the earlier oath concerning these nine. This resembles one who swore not to eat figs and then swore not to eat figs and grapes, who is liable twice for the figs. It is possible that if he ate these nine and ten unspecified loaves, he would incur liability for three oaths, like the case of figs and grapes, where if he also ate the grapes he would certainly incur three liabilities, two for the figs and one for the grapes. Derishah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And this applies only, etc.</b> Ran writes: It is difficult. How can we say that if one swore, “I will not eat nine and ten,” he is liable only once, because since the first oath forbids nine, it certainly forbids ten; whereas if he swore, “I will not eat ten and nine,” he is liable twice, because the first oath forbade only ten and the second oath also forbade nine? The reverse should be true. If he said, “I will not eat nine and ten,” he should be liable twice, because the first oath did not prohibit the tenth loaf, whereas the second oath now prohibits it. We stated above in seif 10 that one who says, “I will not eat this loaf,” swears regarding each act of eating from it. Conversely, if he said, “I will not eat ten and nine,” he should be liable only once, because through the first oath he became forbidden regarding every act of eating from those loaves. One may answer that here he did not vow concerning designated loaves, but swore generally concerning a number of loaves. Therefore, when he says, “An oath that I will not eat nine and ten,” the oath concerning nine already forbids ten, for if he did not eat nine he certainly did not eat ten. He is therefore liable only once. But when he says, “I will not eat ten and nine,” the oath concerning ten did not forbid nine, since it forbids ten while permitting nine, and he is therefore liable twice. If, however, he vowed concerning designated loaves and said, “An oath that I will not eat nine and ten,” he is liable only once, for through the first oath he vowed concerning each act of eating from them. End quote. See Derishah.

<b>מקצתה כו'. </b>לשון הרא"ש סוף פ"ג דשבועות אמר רבא שבועה שלא אוכל ככר זה כיון שאכל ממנה כזית חייב שלא אוכלנה אינו חייב עד שיאכל את כולה הלכך כיון שאכל ממנה כזית חייב דמקצתה נמי במשמעות השבועה ומיהו כולה נמי בכלל השבועה הוא ואכל אכילה ואכילה דידיה נשבע דה"ק שבועה שלא אוכל שום אכילה ממנה וכן הדין באומר שבועה שאוכל ככר זה מקצתה נמי במשמע וחייב לאכול את כולה ואם נשרף מקצתה חייב לאכול את השאר כדי לקיים שבועתו עד כאן לשונו וכ"כ הר"ן ובזה א"ש דל"ק מ"ש התוס' ספ"ג דשבועות דף כ"ט ע"ב תימה דשניהם שבועות בטוי הם דשבועה שאוכל היינו כזית כמו שבועה שלא אוכל דלעיל (סעיף י') ושלא אוכלנה היינו שלא אוכלנה כולה ושמא יש לחלק בין אוכל ולא אוכל עכ"ל דודאי אין לחלק בין אוכל לשלא אוכל אלא דאוכל משמע נמי כולה וזה דעת הט"ו בסעיף י' וט"ז ודלא כהגה' דרישה שהכניס דברי התוס' בדברי הטור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Part of it, etc.</b> Rosh writes at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuos: Rava said that if one swore, “I will not eat this loaf,” once he ate a kezayis from it he is liable; but if he said, “I will not eat it,” he is not liable until he eats all of it. Therefore, where he ate a kezayis he is liable because part of it is also included in the meaning of the oath. Nevertheless, the entire loaf is also included in the oath, and he swore regarding each act of eating from it, for his statement means, “An oath that I will not eat any act of eating from it.” The same law applies if one says, “An oath that I will eat this loaf.” Part of it is also included, yet he is obligated to eat the whole loaf. If part of it was burned, he must eat the remainder in order to fulfill his oath. End quote. Ran writes likewise. This resolves the difficulty raised by Tosafos at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuos 29b: Both are oaths of expression. “An oath that I will eat” means a kezayis, just as “an oath that I will not eat” above in seif 10; whereas “I will not eat it” means that he will not eat all of it. Perhaps one should distinguish between “I will eat” and “I will not eat.” End quote. In truth, there is no distinction between “I will eat” and “I will not eat.” Rather, “I will eat” also includes the entire loaf. This is the view of the Tur and Mechaber in seifim 10 and 16, unlike the gloss of the Derishah, which inserted the words of Tosafos into the Tur.

<b>יש להסתפק כו'. </b>מתשובת הרשב"א שהביא בית יוסף בקצרה והוא בתשובת הרשב"א סימן [ת]ש"ב ומדמי שם להדיא דין זה לשבועה שלא אוכלנה שבועה שלא אוכלנה ונשאל על הראשונה אלא דנסתפק בזה גופה אי השבועה הב' חלה אח"כ או לא כיון דנראה מדברי הרי"ף שדחה להך דרבה משבועה שלא אוכל שבועה שלא אוכל ונשאל על הראשונה דחלה השניה וכן הוא בתשובת מהר"ל ן' חביב סימן קל"ז ואם כן תימה על המחבר דלקמן סעיף כ"א וכן לעיל סימן רכ"ט סי"ג פסק כהך דרבה וכאן כתב דיש להסתפק בדבר ואפשר דעת המחבר לחלק דשאני הכא כיון דהשבועה הב' היא נגד השבועה הא' וצ"ע. וע"ק דבח"מ סימן ע"ג ס"ה העתיק תשובת הרא"ש בנשבע שבועה שניה לפרוע לבטל הראשונה אם ימצא פתח לשבועה הראשונה ויתירוה לו ימכור ויקיים שבועה שניה עכ"ל משמע דאחר כך חלה שבועה ב' למפרע וכ"כ מהר"ל ן' חביב שם וי"ל בזה דה"ק יקיים שבועה ב' מטעם שמחויב לפרוע לא מטעם שבועתו וצריך עיון. ומ"מ לענין דינא נראה דאם התיר הראשונה יש להתיר השבועה שניה גם כן וכ"פ מהר"ל ן' חביב שם וזהו דלא כהעט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is room for doubt, etc.</b> This is based on a responsum of the Rashba cited briefly by Beis Yosef, found in Rashba, responsum 402. There he explicitly compares this law to one who said, “An oath that I will not eat it,” and again, “An oath that I will not eat it,” and then had the first oath released. He was uncertain whether the second oath then takes effect, because the Rif appears to reject Rava’s ruling from the case of one who said, “An oath that I will not eat,” repeated the oath, and then had the first released, where the second takes effect. This is also discussed in Responsa Mahari ibn Chaviv, siman 137. If so, it is difficult that the Mechaber below in seif 21, and above in siman 229, seif 13, rules in accordance with Rava, while here he writes that the matter is doubtful. Perhaps the Mechaber distinguishes this case because the second oath directly contradicts the first. This requires study. There is a further difficulty. In Choshen Mishpat, siman 73, seif 5, the Mechaber cites the responsum of Rosh regarding one who took a second oath to pay, thereby contradicting the first oath. If he finds grounds for releasing the first oath and it is released, he shall sell and fulfill the second oath. End quote. This implies that the second oath then takes effect retroactively. Mahari ibn Chaviv writes likewise. One may answer that he means only that the person must fulfill the second undertaking because he is independently obligated to pay, not because of the oath itself. This requires study. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law, it appears that if the first oath was released, the second oath should also be released. Mahari ibn Chaviv rules likewise. This is unlike the Levush.

<b>יש כאן עונש כו'. </b>דלא אמרינן אין שבועה חלה על שבועה אלא בשבועת ביטוי דלהבא שחוזר ואוסר עליו את האיסור וה"ל נשבע לקיים את המצוה אבל בשבועה שלעבר משנשבע שבועה הראשונה יצתה מפיו לשקר והלכה לה והשניה שבועה אחרת היא לעצמה וחייב עליה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is punishment here, etc.</b> The principle that an oath does not take effect upon an existing oath applies only to an oath of expression concerning the future, where one again forbids upon himself what is already forbidden and is therefore considered to have sworn to fulfill a mitzvah. But regarding an oath about the past, once the first oath was uttered falsely, it has left his mouth and is finished. The second oath is an independent oath, and he is liable for it.
