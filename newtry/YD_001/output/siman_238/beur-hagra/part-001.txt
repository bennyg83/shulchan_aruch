<b>שבועה כו'. </b>רמב"ם ור"נ [וכ"כ הרא"ש שם סס"ה] וכמו בכל איסורין דאסור מן התורה לר"י ביומא ע"ד א' ואף שכאן איסור הבא ע"י עצמו וי"ל דלא נתכוין לפחות מכן וכמ"ש מחלוקת בסתם אבל במפרש כו' מ"מ כיון שביומא שם אמרינן משום דחזי לאצטרופי הה"נ דלא כמ"ש הר"נ בחידושיו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An oath, etc.</b> This is the view of the Rambam and Ran, and the Rosh likewise writes so there at the end of siman 5. The rule is like all other prohibitions, where even less than the prescribed measure is prohibited according to the Torah under Rabbi Yochanan's view in Yoma 74a. Although here the prohibition was created by his own oath, and one might argue that he did not intend to prohibit less than the stated measure, as indicated by the dispute concerning an unspecified oath but not an explicit one, nevertheless, since the Gemara in Yoma explains the prohibition by the possibility that the amounts may combine, the same reasoning applies here, unlike the Ran's statement in his novellae.

<b>שבועה שלא כו' או כו'. </b>רי"ף ורמב"ם אבל לשיטת רש"י דוקא בסתם כמש"ש רישא בסתם כו' מפרש כו' אלא כדרבא כו' אבל הר"נ כתב דבכל הספרים הגירסא משכחת לה כדרבא ול"ג אלא ועוד דגרסינן בדרבא שבועה שאוכל ואכל עפר פטור ופי' הר"נ דה"ק כיון דמשכחת להן באוכל סתם סגי וחייב בלאו אפי' במפרש וכמ"ש בתוספתא הביאו תוס' שם ד"ה אלא שבועה שאוכל ואכל אוכלין אסורין כגון פיגול נותר וטמא פטור וכ"ת אמאי פטור הא אמרינן ברישא דמתני' שבועה שלא אוכל ואכל אוכלין שאינן כו' דאין נבלות בכלל ל"ד דנשבע שלא אוכל אין כוונתו אלא לדברים ראויין דשאינן ראויין א"צ לישבע עליהן אבל שאוכל כוונתו על כ"ד שיאכל יצא בזה כדרבא דאמר כו' ואע"ג דאם נשבע שלא יאכל פטור באכל עפר כמ"ש ברישא ואכל אוכלין שאינן ראויין דגם עפר אינו ראוי וכמ"ש רבא שם בעצמו שבועה שלא אוכל כו' והוא שיטת הרי"ף והגאונים וכ"מ שם ב' וא"ר למאן דא"ל כו' ושם ואם איתא משכחת כו' ועתוס' שם ד"ה שלא כו' שדחקו עצמן בזה וכן שם א' סד"ה אלא. ומיהו י"ל כו' וער"נ <small>(בחידושיו) </small>שתירץ בע"א אבל לשיטת הגאונים הכל ניחא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An oath that I will not, etc., or, etc.</b> This is the view of the Rif and Rambam. According to Rashi, however, the rule applies only where the oath was unspecified, as the Gemara says, "the first clause is unspecified, etc.; if he specified, etc.; rather, according to Rava, etc." The Ran writes that all reliable texts read, "It can be found according to Rava," without the word "rather." They also read in Rava's statement: "One who swore that he would eat and ate earth is exempt." The Ran explains that since the liability can apply where he said simply, "I will eat," that suffices to establish the prohibition, and he is liable even if he explicitly referred to forbidden foods. This is also stated in the Tosefta cited by Tosafos there, s.v. Ela: "One who swore that he would eat and then ate forbidden foods, such as piggul, nosar, or tamei meat, is exempt." One might ask why he is exempt, since the opening clause of the Mishnah says that one who swore not to eat and then ate inedible items is exempt, implying that neveilos are not included. The cases are not comparable. When one swears not to eat, he intends only edible things, for there is no need to swear against inedible things. When he swears that he will eat, however, his intention is satisfied by anything he eats; earth is excluded only by Rava's special ruling. Although one who swears not to eat is exempt if he eats earth, as stated in the opening clause, because earth is also inedible, this remains consistent with Rava's own ruling there. This is the approach of the Rif and the Geonim, and it is also implied there, 29b, in the statements, "according to the one who says, etc.," and "if so, the case can be found, etc." See Tosafos there, s.v. Shelo, and there, 29a, at the end of s.v. Ela, who were forced into strained explanations. The Ran in his novellae answers differently, but according to the Geonim everything is clear.

<b>ומ"מ כו'. </b>שם ואב"א שלא אוכלנה כו' ופי' הרמב"ן דאי לא שייר כזית כבר עבר על שבועתו ולפיכך אינו נשאל וכ"מ בירושלמי דאמרינן שם גבי פלוגתא דר"ע ורבנן בריש פרקין באכל כ"ש. גרסינן שם מה מפקינן מביניהון שבועה שלא אוכל כבר זו ואכלה חסר כ"ש ע"ד דר"ע פטור ע"ד דרבנן חייב ור"ל שאמר שלא אוכלנה. הר"נ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, etc.</b> The Gemara there says, in an alternative answer, that the case is where he said, "I will not eat it, etc." The Ramban explains that if he did not leave an olive's volume, he has already violated his oath and therefore can no longer petition for its annulment. This also appears from the Yerushalmi concerning the dispute between Rabbi Akiva and the Sages at the beginning of the chapter regarding one who ate any amount. It states there: "What practical difference is there between them? One who swore, 'I will not eat this loaf,' and ate all but the smallest amount. According to Rabbi Akiva he is exempt; according to the Sages he is liable." This means that he said, "I will not eat it." Ran.
