**** ENGLISH ****For behold he is not obligated, etc. — so in Beit Yosef in the name of Ran; and he wrote in these words: from the force of the first oath he is not obligated to betroth her except when that so-and-so requests from him; and even after so-and-so's request he is not obligated to betroth her until after Passover; and it turns out the second oath takes effect upon him after Passover; and also before Passover, all the time that so-and-so does not request from him, it also takes effect when he is sworn to betroth her, etc. — only I do not delve into this because it appears the first oath was for his fellow's benefit, after he said "all the time so-and-so requests from me"; and also it appears that oath was on condition of matchmaking between him and the woman, and not entirely in his power to annul others' rights; therefore he will be asked about the second, etc. — end quote.

And Rama who wrote "for the woman's benefit or through matchmaking conditions" is not precise — both are one matter; not so Ran who wrote for his fellow's benefit who urged him to betroth this woman — and also the woman's benefit, since done through matchmaking that reveals her mind that she is pleased with this. And I wonder at Ran's view — why tie it to being done through matchmaking? For even without matchmaking she has a right nevertheless, that he is obligated to betroth her if she wants. And one can say: without matchmaking she did not rely that perhaps he will release his oath through a sage; but through matchmaking he binds himself with a penalty if he transgresses — for he compared the woman's right to that so-and-so's right who requested from him to betroth her; and upon that so-and-so there was no penalty, only the oath alone is considered a right that his will was done; therefore it seems to my humble view that for clarity Ran mentioned done through matchmaking conditions, explicitly clear she is pleased; but even without this she is also pleased that she has the choice. And even though in the eyes of the world this match is not fitting for her, nevertheless it is possible it is good in her eyes — for a woman in any case is pleased, for it is good to sit with two legs, as stated in several places — so it seems to me.

**** ENGLISH ****He need not eat the permitted. For on eating it all he swore, and he does not fulfill by eating part of it — so Rosh, Tur, and Ran. And from this it seems to learn: one who swore to his fellow to pay him a maneh on a certain day, and duress occurred that he did not have the full maneh — neither money nor worth money — on that day he set for him — exempt from the oath since he cannot fulfill as he swore. Further one can learn from this: one who swore to pay a maneh on a certain day, and if he transgresses the oath he gives a fine of ten gold pieces — and he paid half of what he owes — he gives the fine entirely, since the oath was on the full amount specifically. And if he said in this language: "oath that I will pay you a maneh on a certain day, and if I do not pay you the maneh I will give you the fine of ten gold pieces" — and he gave him part — he gives the fine according to the value of what he transgressed.
