And they turned yellow like grass, etc. Shach wrote: from Tur's words it appears that in every heart, gizzard, and liver, appearance of gogel does not disqualify — only green; and so Ran in Ra'ah's name; but Rashba in Torat HaBayit — his view is to disqualify except in liver, and even there there is doubt if he means every liver or only fatty liver; and Pri Chadash wrote from Rambam's commentary on the mishnah it implies to be stringent. And I wonder, for I did not find in Rambam's words a place of prohibition, for he wrote in those organs whose way is to be green and like grass, and they judged from this that green means gogel like appearance of egg; and I say it is not so, for in change of appearance in lung Rambam wrote grass like kashuta, which is the growing herb; these five types are treifah, and grass like grass or green like tekhelet is kosher — behold the Gemara reads: only green — kosher means like grass; and Tosefta has a version thus; and Tosefta holds grass and grass are one; but Rambam explains on kashuta — grass, treifah; and grass — kosher; this teaches he holds to distinguish between grass and grass; therefore Rambam wrote these appearances whose way is green or like grass, and did not write like grass — this teaches he intended these two appearances, grass and grass, both green: grass means grass, and like grass means kashuta, both green and not gogel. In truth kashuta is a spice species called neglich, for Yerushalmi translates shechelet as kashuta, and Targum tophera is neglich; they tend to blackness, therefore called kashuta — language of Kush, black; and this is clear in Rambam's words; therefore there is no proof here to declare treifah; nevertheless one declares treifah when it is known it fell into fire; but if not known it fell, even Rashba did not say so, and one does not declare treifah except in green appearance similar to field weeds. And they are in Rashba's novellae chapter 1, on the passage "and R' Mani," etc.; he concludes at the end on that which lung was not counted among organs that fell: one could also say it is impossible to mix and change lung with them, because green includes two colors — one green like grass and herbs, and second like morika; disqualifying green in heart, gizzard, and liver is grass; whereas like morika — fatty liver's way is thus; and from mixing and teaching heart and gizzard, disqualifying green in heart and gizzard is like disqualifying green in liver, which is grass; but in lung, green like grass does not disqualify from R' Nasan; disqualifying green in lung is like morika, and it is even without fire-fall treifah, even if clarified it did not fall into fire, end of his words. One must understand his words: initially he brought that green stated in heart, gizzard, and liver all deals with green like grass similar to liver; if so, no difficulty why the mishnah did not teach lung — it deals with green like grass, and regarding lung kosher from R' Nasan; and now we must say like morika in any case disqualifies because of change of appearance. And conversely, since he concludes lung falling into fire does not apply at all — green like grass is kosher from R' Nasan, and like morika is disqualified in any case from change of appearance — why did Rashba need this, that green of heart, etc., is specifically grass similar to liver? It requires further study. And the liver, etc. Taz in s.k. 3 brought Mordechai, who wrote: even though the liver was perforated — kosher — nevertheless, since the liver turned yellow we are concerned also for heart and intestines even though not yet evident, and furthermore one should be concerned perhaps ultimately all will be removed; from here is support for Geonim who declare treifah in perforation of liver's courtyard, for we are concerned ultimately the entire liver will be removed, etc., end of his words. And Beit Yosef wondered: if so, why did they say liver turned yellow opposite intestinal children; and Taz also wondered. In truth it appears simple Mordechai wondered why they asked liver turned yellow opposite intestinal children and did not ask opposite bile or opposite any corner at place of life-source — where treifah is in truth; or he knew the Gemara: in all liver treifah, as we say greens stated in heart and liver — and it implies no distinction, only the Gemara should have asked liver turned yellow opposite intestinal children — meaning the inner side of liver opposite intestinal children, and intestinal children were not deficient at all, as Rashba elaborated; if so, the doubt falls: if it were a fire blow ruling, how is it possible the side opposite intestines, high side, outer side, and animal cavity where fire rules most — and soft intestinal children quickly absorb fire — would show no mark, while on the liver's inner side there is yellowing not from fire but from another accident or smoke that entered — that is the Gemara's question: liver turned yellow specifically opposite intestinal children; but certainly the baraita knew liver that turned yellow is treifah in those cases, etc.; and it is simple to him: nevertheless, since we see a defect here, we say the essence of fire does not rule in liver but in intestinal children, and smoke entered the liver and treifah; and all the more if there is scald in liver leaning toward the body where fire rules — it is obvious it is common and treifah, etc.:

In truth, if for law there is an answer: as is clear in Gemara and poskim and Shulchan Aruch seif 3, if they did not change and it happened they were scalded and changed — treifah, because through scalding the defect was revealed; and this is only in falling into fire, as Shulchan Aruch explains — see Shach s.k. 12; but in mere change of appearance, change of appearance in lung was never devised to be concerned for at all; if so, one could say here is a great practical difference: if it happened that change is found only after scalding — for Rashba, who holds falling into fire applies even to animal lung, the defect was revealed and treifah; unlike Rosh, for fire does not rule at all in animal — what will you say, there is change of appearance? This is not so, for lung was inspected and there is no change, and after scalding we do not pay attention at all. And I add: if found after scalding — treifah; the same if found when lung is not inflated and cannot inflate, etc. — treifah, that it is only scalding and not inflation; unlike from change of appearance we do not pay attention, for our law in all lung appearances is after inflation, and we do not establish defect so much, and we do not see defect of change of appearance after inflation; if so, great practical difference, and let it be clear from all he argued. And I think Rashba did not decide this for practical law to say even defect of falling before us we hang on R' Nasan, as I wrote above — only he spoke to reject those who claim falling law does not apply to lung from that they said heart, gizzard, intestines, and did not say lung; and Rashba answered on this; and certainly lung color is neither red nor green until Ran — red and green are change of appearance in lung; if so, in falling into fire, reddened or green — treifah, for there is fire-scald; if so, the Gemara said on the mishnah: if greens — disqualified; if reds — kosher — they did not say greens disqualified except in heart, gizzard, and liver; but reds kosher; if so, how consider lung among them — behold lung: even reds disqualified; and the mishnah teaches reds kosher; therefore lung is not included; and if it challenges the mishnah — why not count lung? One could also say the mishnah counts what whose way is to be red and changed to green; but in lung, which is declared treifah in every change of appearance, it does not count what reddened to green; and how consider what is declared treifah in every color and appearance; therefore it must be said there are those who hold nevertheless in lung reds are kosher from R' Nasan — only greens disqualified, namely morika; and on this Rashba properly argued either-or to your words: impossible to consider in grass immediately you are kosher, and in morika behold it is change of appearance, etc. And that is all for their words; but in truth Rashba also holds since there is scald, falling from R' Nasan does not apply; only Rav Beit Yosef did not grasp this, who emended emptying in Tur's words in place of reddening; and at first glance unnecessarily, for I already clarified even reddening disqualifies lung in fire-fall; but it seems to Rav Beit Yosef that nevertheless kosher from R' Nasan, and as Rashba wrote; therefore he emended turned yellow, meaning morika, etc. Summary for law: no practical difference in green like grass — kosher for all; and the same in reddening; but green like morika — it appears to be stringent; and so Pri Chadash — correct:
