And the liver — the change does not forbid in it except opposite the intestines. So wrote Rashba and Tur; and in the Gemara it is asked of Rav Yosef, son of R' Yehudah bar Levi, from R' Yehudah bar Levi: the liver turned yellow opposite the intestinal children — what is the law? He said to him: treifah. And it should not be except removed. Rava said: since it turned yellow opposite the intestines, it is known that it fell into fire and its intestines contracted; and Rashba holds that the force of the fire that ruled in the liver there already prevailed even in the organ beneath it; therefore we say it is known that the intestines beneath the liver also contracted, only they are not yet visible, for it is not their nature to be revealed as quickly as the liver; and it is not specifically opposite the intestines, but the same applies in other places — there is prohibition in yellowing, namely at the place of bile; for bile that was perforated is treifah; therefore here, since the heat of the fire ruled and makes the place of the liver as if perforated there, it also makes the bile as if perforated there; and if the yellowing is at the place of its life-source, where there is no prohibition from another organ that would disqualify through perforation, but the prohibition is from the liver that is as if that place of life-source was removed — therefore there is no treifah in this unless the yellowing is over the entire place of its life-source; but in part of it — namely, that something remained whole like an olive — there is no prohibition, for it is no worse than other perforation or removal, etc.; in all the liver except these places there is no prohibition in liver that turned yellow. But Mordechai wrote: liver that turned yellow opposite intestines — treifah, and the measure of its greenness is in any amount; and even though the liver was perforated — kosher — nevertheless, since the liver turned yellow we are concerned also for heart and intestines even though it is not yet evident in them; and furthermore one should be concerned that perhaps ultimately all of it will be removed; and from here there is support for four Geonim who declare treifah in perforation of the liver's courtyard, for we are concerned that ultimately the entire liver will be removed, since we declare treifah in liver yellowing at the measure of perforation of any amount that they forbade its beginning because of its end, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wondered: if so, why did they say specifically opposite the intestines? And he answered that perhaps we hold that ultimately all of it will not be removed, only specifically in yellowing opposite the intestines; and this matter is a great strain — as if Mordechai was told this thing, that the reason is because ultimately all of it will be removed; and from this one must enter a strain that they know specifically opposite the intestines; and he also brings support for the Geonim, and there is no hint or allusion in the Talmud, for they forbade only because of intestinal children alone. And it appears to me that Mordechai holds there are two reasons for treifah here: one, that since we see a defect in the yellowing, certainly other organs near it were also scalded, even though not yet visible — nevertheless it is possible that their nature is not revealed as quickly as this scalded place; and the second reason, that even in that organ itself that was scalded in part, and we see that the remaining part was not scalded — since in this organ itself whose nature is to change and be revealed, and nevertheless it was revealed only in part — this teaches that in another place in that organ it was not yet scalded; nevertheless we are concerned that perhaps afterward it will be scalded, for this blow will cause it; and he learned this from Rava's words, who said it is known that it fell into fire — for why did he need to say this; he should have said briefly it is known the intestines contracted, for the questioners also asked about liver that fell into fire, that it is known; otherwise yellowing does not disqualify at all, as Mordechai himself wrote there; rather Rava means there are two prohibitions: one, it is known it fell into fire and forbidden because this will harm it even in a place where the blow has not yet ruled; and another reason, that the intestines already contracted since they are near it, only it is not their nature to be revealed as quickly as the liver. It turns out that for the first reason there is prohibition also from the liver, for we forbid it because of its end; and Mordechai's words are explained from what is stated in the Gemara according to this; and according to this there is prohibition in liver yellowing in every place from the first reason in Rava's words; and for practical law we have only the ruling of Shulchan Aruch, that we do not say at all that ultimately all of it will be removed, but we say only Mordechai's last reason alone, and that is Rashba's and Tur's view above, for the Gemara's language proves as their words, from the challenge "and it should not be except removed" — this teaches that from the liver there is no prohibition; and so Ran; and therefore there is prohibition only at the place of bile and opposite intestines even in any amount, and opposite the place of its life-source specifically in all of it, as Beit Yosef emended Tur's words "life-source," etc.; and from this is proof for what is written in siman 41 seif 2, that liver that melted not at the place of life-source and place of bile — kosher, and we do not say cast the spoon in all of it and ultimately all of it will be scalded; for even in falling into fire here, which is explicit treifah in the Talmud, we conclude the halacha here that there is no prohibition because ultimately all of it will be scalded — a fortiori there, which is doubtful treifah; and even according to Mordechai who forbids here in liver yellowing in every place because ultimately all of it will be scalded — one does not compare treifot to each other, for there is a great difference between a blow that comes through fire and other illnesses, for we did not find in the Gemara concern in them, as it appears to me:

<b>ואי ליתא קמן כו'. </b>זה דברי הר"ן וכ' ב"י ראייתו מדאמרי' בגמ' אין ריאה לעוף ליחמר וההוא ודאי בסתמא קאמר דאילו היכא דנחמרה בודאי הא קמן שלא הגינו צלעות עליה [אלא] אם נפלה לאור דא"צ בדיקה ודוקא ריאה שצלעותיה מגינות עליה הא שאר בני מעים דעוף דליכא מה דמגין עלייהו צריכה בדיקה עכ"ל ולפ"ז למאי דקי"ל אפי' אם ראינו שהוריקה הריאה כשירה כמ"ש הטור בשם הרא"ש וכן מסקנת ב"י כמו שהעתקתי לעיל סעיף א' א"כ הא דאמרו אין ריאה לעוף הוא אפי' אם ראינו שהוריקה ואין ראייתו של הר"ן ראייה וא"נ לא קי"ל כותיה בהך דאסר בלא בדיקה ולא היה לו להש"ע להביא כאן דעת הר"ן כיון שבב"י ובש"ע ס"א פסק כהרא"ש שזכרתי אלא דאפשר דאף שאין ראייה של הר"ן ראייה מ"מ מסברא יש לפסוק כיון שיש כאן ריעותא דנפלה לאור אמרינן ודאי יש שינוי עד שיתברר שאין שינוי וכפי מה שאפרש בסמוך א"צ ראיה וע"כ דברי הר"ן הם הלכה ורש"ל חלק על דברי הר"ן אלו ומביא ראיה מדתנן במתני' נפלה לאור ונחמרו בני מעיה ולא קתני נפלה לאור לחוד כמו שתני דרסה בכותל כו' אלא ודאי שאין איסור בנפלה לחוד אלא בנחמרו מעיה בודאי דוקא אבל בנפלה לאור לחוד אין כאן ריעותא עדיין <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="3"></i>ולי נראה דבריו תמוהים בזה דהא יש חד פי' להך ונחמרו דהיינו לשון חמימות כמ"ש סעיף א' בשם המרדכי וא"כ מנא ידעינן דנתחממו המעיים כל שאין שם שינוי מראה ע"כ לומ' דבשיעור תליא מלתא דהיינו שנשתהה באור כדי שיעור חימום א"כ אין ראייתו ראיה כלל דה"ק מתני' נפלה לאור ונחמרו מעיה דהיינו ששהתה שם כשיעור הזה אבל ודאי אין אנו רואים שום סי' חמירה אלא מסתמא שנפלה לאור ושהתה שם דזה ודאי אין סברא שתיכף שתפול לאור תאסר אפי' אם מיד סלקוהו אבל אם שהתה כשיעור שתתחמם ודאי הוה ריעותא לפנינו וצריך בדיקה ובדבר זה סגי לקיים דברי הר"ן בלי ראייה אחרת:
**** ENGLISH ****
And if it is not before us, etc. These are Ran's words; and Beit Yosef wrote his proof from what we say in the Gemara: there is no lung for a bird to contract; and that one certainly speaks in an unspecified case; for where it certainly contracted — behold it is before us that ribs did not protect it; but if it fell into fire — inspection is not needed, and specifically lung whose ribs protect it; but other intestinal children of a bird, for nothing protects them — need inspection, end of his words. And accordingly, according to what we hold, even if we saw that the lung turned yellow — kosher, as Tur wrote in the name of Rosh, and so is Beit Yosef's conclusion as I copied above in seif 1; if so, that which they said there is no lung for a bird is even if we saw that it turned yellow, and Ran's proof is not proof; or we do not hold like him in forbidding without inspection, and Shulchan Aruch should not have brought Ran's view here, since in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch seif 1 he ruled like Rosh as I mentioned; only it is possible that even though Ran's proof is not proof, nevertheless it is reasonable to rule thus, since there is a defect here that it fell into fire — we say there is certainly change until it is clarified there is no change, as I will explain shortly — proof is not needed, and therefore Ran's words are halacha; and Maharshal disagreed with these words of Ran and brings proof from what we taught in the mishnah: fell into fire and intestinal children contracted — and it did not teach fell into fire alone, as it taught "trampled in a wall," etc.; rather certainly that there is no prohibition in falling alone but specifically when intestinal children certainly contracted; but in falling into fire alone there is not yet a defect before us. And it seems to me his words are wondrous in this, for there is one explanation of "and they contracted," namely the language of heat, as written in seif 1 in the name of Mordechai; if so, from where do we know the intestines were heated when there is no change of appearance? Therefore it must be said the matter depends on measure — namely, that it remained in fire long enough for the measure of heating; if so, his proof is not proof at all, for the mishnah means: fell into fire and intestinal children contracted — meaning it remained there for that measure; but certainly we do not see any sign of contraction, only presumably it fell into fire and remained there; and this certainly has no reason that as soon as it falls into fire it should be forbidden, even if they immediately removed it; but if it remained for the measure that it would heat — certainly there is a defect before us and inspection is needed; and in this matter it suffices to uphold Ran's words without another proof:
