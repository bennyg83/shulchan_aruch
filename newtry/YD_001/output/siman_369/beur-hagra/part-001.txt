<b>(Likkut) Some say.</b> Meaning specifically metal utensils. This is Rabbenu Tam's position: the rule that utensils touching a corpse become like the corpse applies only to metal utensils. Tosafot Nazir 54b, Rash at the beginning of Ohalot, Rashi, and Raavad follow this. Ri, Rambam, Ramban, and other poskim disagree and apply it to all utensils and garments except earthenware vessels, which never become an av hatumah. Their proof is from the opening Mishnah of Ohalot, which speaks generally of utensils, and from Sifrei. According to Rabbenu Tam, a nazir shaves for impurity from a sword that touched a corpse, and a kohen is likewise prohibited from becoming impure through it, as the Tosefta says that any impurity for which a nazir shaves is prohibited to a kohen. On his view, even entering a tent containing such a metal utensil is forbidden because it conveys impurity through a tent. Most poskim disagree: the nazir does not shave for such impurity and a kohen is not warned against it in the same way. The Tosefta in Ohalot distinguishes between terumah and kodashim, where secondary corpse impurity matters, and the laws of nazir and Mikdash, where only direct corpse impurity creates the full liability. Rambam rules accordingly in Hilchot Nezirut and Hilchot Tumat Met.
