**** ENGLISH ****
And esophagus, etc. — Thus wrote Rambam chapter 3 of Laws of Shechitah halachah 19: if the spinal column was broken and the majority of flesh with it, or it was torn from its back like a fish, or the majority of the trachea was severed, or the esophagus was punctured in any amount in the place fit for shechitah — behold it is neveilah while alive — thus is his language. And it is implied from his words that puncture of the esophagus is neveilah while alive even before slaughter; and so is implied the view of the Mechaber. However, it requires study: in chapter HaShochet (Chullin daf 32b) the gemara asks on this that Rava answered in the mishnah "these treifos are forbidden" it teaches "and some of them are neveilot" (meaning puncture of esophagus and majority severance of windpipe are neveilah) and "some are treifos" — let it also count one who made it a gistera neveilah; and it answers: when it teaches neveilah it means not that it imparts tumah while alive, for it does not teach imparting tumah while alive — thus puncture of esophagus and majority severance of windpipe are not neveilah while alive, but if slaughtered, even though slaughtered in the place of shechitah, it is invalidated by the shechitah and we call them [neveilah in that sense]. And even though it seems there that Rava's answer does not stand in the conclusion, nevertheless it seems there that this law — the default of the Talmud that puncture of esophagus and majority severance of windpipe are not neveilah while alive — is correct; and so is implied in Rashi there; and so in Semag, who wrote puncture of esophagus is treifah — thus it deals while alive, for it is only treifah alone. Even though Rashba in Teshuvot HaRishonim daf 27b and 3 holds that majority severance of windpipe is not even treifah while alive according to Rava — his words are difficult. **** ENGLISH ****
And all this does not apply, etc. — even if only the inner was punctured, etc. — Explanation of what he wrote nearby: if one of them was punctured — kosher, implying whichever was punctured, inner or outer — applies to this; and he says this is specifically when punctured through disease; but if there is concern it was punctured by thorn or needle, even if only the inner was punctured alone — treifah, for we are concerned perhaps the outer was also punctured; and esophagus has no examination from outside. But when only the outer was punctured we find it well kosher through examination from inside, as below seif 7. And that Shach wrote we are concerned perhaps punctured and esophagus has no examination from outside implies that from inside there is examination; and so is proven from Tosafot and Rosh from whom Shulchan Aruch brought this law — see there; and so in Darkei Moshe; and so wrote Taz and Bach. Bach understood what Shach wrote "even if only the inner was punctured — treifah" to mean certainly not that only the outer was punctured is treifah; therefore he entered with difficulty to reconcile Shach's view here; and the matter is simple as I wrote — no need to elaborate. Do not ask: if so, in this siman and at end of seif 8 and in siman 34 end of seif 9 Shach wrote if done through thorn or needle, for us who are not expert in examination of esophagus — treifah in every case and examination from inside does not help — say specifically when punctured by needle or thorn in an unknown place we are not expert even from inside, and that is the case at end of seif 23 and end of seif 8 and in siman 34 seif 9; but if a thorn or needle was inserted in esophagus from outside in a known place, examination of esophagus from the inside helps, for it is no worse than beit ha-kosot punctured from inside with a needle — kosher, as below siman 48; and where a needle is found in the body cavity — treifah, for we are concerned perhaps one of the organs puncturable in any amount was punctured and they have no examination, as below siman 51. Rather certainly, since the puncture in beit ha-kosot is in a known place, it has examination; and so esophagus. Now resolve what Mechaber wrote in seif 7 that regarding puncture too esophagus has no examination from outside but from inside; and in siman 51 wrote if needle found in body cavity — treifah, concerned perhaps punctured one of places puncturable in any amount and they have no examination — there we do not know at all where it was punctured and a puncture in any amount is not discernible; but here at least we know opposite the dirty place it was punctured and one can examine opposite the dirty place. And Shach's view: even in such a case there is no examination for puncture in any amount; but when thorn or needle inserted in esophagus in known place — kosher also for Shach, as in s.k. 7 — except this is called "not known" since one must examine along entire length and width of dirty place:
