But if it stood me'eit l'eit. Tur wrote in name of Baal HaItur: if one cooked (meat) [milk] in a pot of (milk) [meat] ben yomo [and there is sixty in it] one may cook afterward whatever species he wishes — and he wondered at this, see there. And it appears to reconcile Baal HaItur's view correctly, for it appears it was difficult for him from what we say in chapter Kol Sha'ah (Pesachim daf 30) regarding an earthenware vessel — we see they are scraped and the Torah testified on an earthenware vessel that it does not leave its rim forever — Rashi: since they expel liquids from the outer rim they certainly absorbed and no longer expel, for the Torah testified on an earthenware vessel that once it absorbed it no longer expels, for in wood and metal vessels it is written "and he shall pour out and rinse in water," but in earthenware "it shall be broken." And one might challenge: on the contrary let us say from what the Merciful One revealed regarding kodashim where we say "it shall be broken" — learn from this that for non-sacred matters it would be lenient compared to this, as Maharam wrote in Mordechai that Beit Yosef brought siman 93 regarding that an earthenware vessel does not expel into a vessel — from what we find in Scripture regarding kodashim, learn from this that in non-sacred matters it is not so; and if so let us say likewise that in non-sacred matters it is lenient compared to this; and Tosafot there challenged another difficulty — perhaps it is a Scriptural decree and one may not learn from it; and they resolved from what we find that for a copper vessel rinsing and pouring help even in cooking and absorption — if so, what Scripture was strict about in earthenware for cooking without absorption is because it made it like cooking with absorption, end of their words. This helps to resolve Tosafot's difficulty that it was not a decree; but in any case our difficulty remains — perhaps non-sacred matters are different from kodashim as in the case above; and it appears that in Baal HaItur's view he held from this difficulty that in earthenware for non-sacred matters likewise from the Torah it is permitted with hag'alah, but only regarding kodashim did the Torah say it does not leave its rim; and the Rabbis also said so in non-sacred matters; and now it is settled that Baal HaItur held here that the taste of meat was already expelled through hag'alah that came through cooking milk, but rabbinically there is prohibition from "does not leave its rim"; and likewise if one comes to cook meat in it afterward — let it stand me'eit l'eit and its taste is spoiled — if one is concerned here for rabbinic prohibition it will lead to loss, for it is impossible to use it further; and we see in the decree of spoiled taste they were not concerned except l'chatchila but b'dieved it is permitted; and here it is b'dieved since it is impossible to use it — therefore they abandoned here Torah law and there is no rabbinic decree at all; and one might challenge: in an earthenware vessel of prohibition too let us say thus and let it stand me'eit l'eit — no difficulty, for l'chatchila it is obvious one may not hag'alah it, and this is the essence of the rabbinic decree; and even if one hag'alahs without intent, nevertheless it does not help since in any case there was already prohibition on this vessel — hag'alah does not help for it; unlike here where we say through this hag'alah of milk the prohibition comes and from this repair this defect was made — the Rabbis did not decree, as explained — Baal HaItur's view is correct; and also in siman 121 Tur brings Baal HaItur's words to be lenient in an earthenware vessel that is not ben yomo since it is only rabbinic.

And it is a stringency without reason. Maharshal chapter Kol HaBasar siman 47 wrote that the essence of the stringency is specifically in those covers that are short at the top and have a hollow, and it is impossible to wipe them well of the boiling that rises there — for wherever there is prohibition in substance we do not say in it noten ta'am li'fegam; but in a wide cover where the hand can control to wipe well — permitted whenever it is not ben yomo, end of his words. And it appears to me to practice thus: if there is in the cooked dish sixty against the short place on the cover then there is no concern of prohibition on the cover more than on the pot; also Rema in Levush wrote there is no place at all for stringency of a cover; and Maharshal z"l wrote a reason for stringency of a cover — since boiling always rises there it sticks greatly and it is impossible to wipe it well — and this is astonishing, for the pot sticks there from the substance of the food — why should the cover be worse than it; rather there is no place for this stringency more than Rashba was stringent, and whoever offers reasons to be stringent in this what is not found in Talmud and poskim — the proof is upon him: Tur wrote in name of Rashba that if one cooked meat or milk and afterward cooked vegetables — it is hag'alah, unlike if one cooked forbidden matter — cooking vegetables is not hag'alah; and his proof is from what we say at the end of tractate Avodah Zarah the spit and grate are whitened in fire, and likewise regarding kodashim we learned the spit and grate are hag'alah in boiling water; and the Gemara answers it is different there because every day becomes purging for its fellow, and he cooked in it peace-offerings of the present or a chatat and afterward peace-offerings — it is hag'alah; and Rashba learns from this why should what one cooks now be hag'alah — it is only ordinary cooking; rather since heter absorbed, even though one cooks heter afterward it weakens its taste and is permitted; but if forbidden matter absorbed, even though its taste is weak, nevertheless there is taste of prohibition in it and it does not help until one hag'alahs it properly — this is his law and proof; and Beit Yosef wrote Tosafot and Rosh challenged how every day becomes purging for its fellow — behold it diminishes through eating and it becomes noten ta'am bar noten ta'am; and they resolved from basar b'chalav that from the Torah it is batel b'rov and in a Temple vessel they establish it on a Torah basis; and therefore Rashba's proof is nothing; and Shach in Netivot HaKesef challenged Beit Yosef and wrote that Tosafot's, Rosh's, and supporters' words do not contradict Rashba — they came only to settle how it is permitted to cook peace-offerings a second time, for it diminishes through eating; and on this they wrote they establish it on a Torah basis and it is batel b'rov, since in the second time there is still no prohibition and it has not yet become notar; and likewise in a chatat that was cooked afterward with peace-offerings for the kohen's portion — they are batel b'rov; but certainly if hag'alah does not help — challenge how one cooks kodashim a third time, since it already became notar; certainly when the vessel became forbidden one cannot say they establish it on a Torah basis and it is batel in the majority, and one cannot say every day becomes purging since it already became forbidden; however it is reasonable to say per Rashba that if heter absorbed hag'alah helps — this, see there where he elaborated; and likewise wrote the responsum of the Gaon R' Gershom in Sefer Avodat HaGershuni see there. And what he elaborated on the body of the matter — if it already became notar one may not rely on bitul b'rov — rather one must understand: Rashba himself compared whether hag'alah helps or not to noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of heter and forbidden matter — for in heter noten ta'am bar noten ta'am is permitted for we are not concerned with weak taste, but in forbidden matter it is forbidden for weak taste also forbids, as Beit Yosef wrote;
