**** ENGLISH ****
Prohibition of benefit from a grave, stone, and structure. In it are 7 seifim. A built grave is forbidden in benefit, but eternal land of a grave is not forbidden: {Rama: And some say that earth taken from the grave and returned upon it — being detached and later attached — is forbidden in benefit (Tur in the name of R' Yeshaya); and some forbid further sitting on the stone placed on the grave as a monument (also in his name, and so Hagahot Ashiri in the name of Or Zarua parashat Elu Megalchin); and some permit (Tur in the name of Rosh); vessels they dig and bury with are permitted in benefit and one should not use them except with the trustee's knowledge like other charity (responsum of Rashba siman 97).} And that a built grave is forbidden forever — specifically when he built it for the sake of the dead and placed him in it, even on condition to move him; but if he built it for himself and did not place him in it — permitted; and likewise if they placed him on condition to move him and they did not prepare it initially — permitted after they cleared it; but if they placed him on condition to remain in it forever — forbidden even after they cleared it, even if he did not build it for himself; and if they did not build it for himself and placed him inside and he added a course (meaning: a row, and it is a row of the wall's masonry) for himself — all of it is forbidden even after clearing, even if they buried there on condition to move; and if he recognizes the course he added for himself, he removes it and it alone is forbidden and the rest of the grave is permitted.

**** ENGLISH ****
A met mitzvah that a person found in a field and buried there — even without the field-owner's knowledge — it is forbidden to move him, for met mitzvah acquires its place; and whoever finds him must bury him where he found him; and if he found him on the path that must be cleared because of the many lest they shelter over him — if he found him between an unplowed field and a plowed-but-unseeded field, he moves him to the unplowed field; between plowed-unseeded and seeded — to plowed-unseeded; between seeded and orchard — to seeded; between orchard and vineyard — to orchard; and if both are equal, he moves him to whichever is closer; and if both are equally close, he moves him to whichever he wishes. In what case? When he found him outside the techum; but if he found him within the techum, they bring him to the cemetery and he is not called met mitzvah unless he found his head and majority.
