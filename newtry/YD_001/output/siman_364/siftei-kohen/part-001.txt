**** ENGLISH ****Or one who fell and died — Maharil wrote: if blood came from him, they do not purify him, for the blood will leave him; but they bury him in his garments; Bach wrote: and it seems this is specifically when he died in his garments; but if he fell and did not die immediately but after several days when he already removed his garments, and no blood comes from him because it already stopped and he died on his bed from that fall — then certainly they purify him and make shrouds for him like other people who die on their beds. In this matter there is a distinction between falling from a roof and one killed: for one killed by gentiles — even though when they filled him blood already stopped — they bury him as found, to incite anger and avenge; this does not apply to falling from a roof. Likewise a woman who died in childbirth and blood comes from her — they do not purify her but bury her in her garments; but if she did not die until after several days and weeks and blood already stopped — they treat her like other dead: purify and make shrouds. I later found written: I heard the reason regarding killed — blood already left him and is absorbed in his garments; we fear perhaps the fourth blood through which the soul departs is absorbed in them — therefore bury him as found and do not remove even his shoes, lest the blood that left with the soul's departure is in the shoes; therefore the custom for killed is to dig and shovel with a hoe and spade in the ground where the killed is found, and all his blood that left near him, and bury the dust in which his blood was absorbed with the killed, lest in that blood the soul-blood departed. For this reason too: one who fell from a roof and died, or a house fell on him and died and wounds and bruises formed on his body expelling blood — likewise the reason for a birthing woman: most do not die except because wounds formed in her inner organs when she bends to give birth, and thereby much blood left absorbed in the shirt on her body and other garments on her, and the blood that left with the soul's departure was absorbed in them; unlike when there are no wounds and no blood left at all — then they do not bury her in her garments but in shrouds like other dead, and purify them. Accordingly: one who died on the road in great cold and snow — it seems they remove his garments, purify, and dress him in shrouds, since there are no wounds and no blood left him. Likewise one drowned in water in his garments — they remove his garments, purify, and dress him in shrouds; as written at end of Maharil; end quote. And I have seen in many communities the custom to purify every birthing woman, dress her in shrouds, and put her garments above the shrouds and above them the suddin.
