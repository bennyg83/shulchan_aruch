(סימן ט"ו סעיף ב') <b>ואם אין ידוע שכלו לו חדשיו אסור משום ספק נפל עד תחלת ליל שמיני.</b> בס' ברכי יוסף כ' גדי שנשחט ונחלקו ב"א אם היה בן ח' מותר מכח ס"ס. ול"ד לנידון דהרשב"א גבי עובד כוכבים מסל"ת. ונראה דהיינו משום שכתב הט"ז דהתם הוי דשיל"מ והיינו כשעדיין לא נשחט משא"כ כשנשחט לא הוי דשיל"מ ומהני ס"ס. וא"כ ה"ה בספק אי היה בן ט' יש להתיר מה"ט. ובס' ת"צ מתיר מטעם אחר. אך לפמ"ש בשע"ה דל"מ ס"ס היכא דחשו חכמים למיעוט א"כ ל"מ אף שנשחט:

(Siman 15 seif 2) <b>And if it is not known that its months are completed—it is forbidden because of doubt of stillborn until beginning of the eighth night.</b> In Birkhei Yosef he wrote: a kid slaughtered and two witnesses disputed whether it was eight days old—permitted through double doubt. And one may say for our case that Rashba regarding gentile mesisach l'fi tumo—and it appears this is because Taz wrote there it is davar sheyesh lo matirin, and this is when not yet slaughtered; unlike when slaughtered it is not davar sheyesh lo matirin and double doubt helps. And if so, similarly in doubt whether it was nine months one may permit from Torah. And in book Tzitz Eliezer he permits for another reason. But according to Pri Megadim in Sha'ar HaTefillah that there is no double doubt where sages concerned for minority—if so there is no double doubt even after slaughter.

(Seif 3) <b>We do not rely on a gentile regarding small kids purchased from him who says they are eight days old.</b> And even mesisach l'fi tumo he is not believed in Torah prohibition—Shach; and Pri Megadim wrote it appears from his words that all who say it is not known whether months completed is a mistaken presumption that if rabbinic, gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is believed as written in siman 98 and 137. But it is impossible to say so as proved in Shulchan Aruch siman 256 and 366, and so in book Beit Avraham. And they did not see Knesset HaGedolah already sensed this—for it is proved there majority of offspring are not stillborn, only rabbinically we concern for minority; and where there is majority we rely on gentile mesisach l'fi tumo as stated in Orach Chaim siman 513 regarding gentile selling eggs on Yom Tov 1. And so in responsa Tiferet Tzvi; and Pri Megadim wrote possibly this presumption is called that until now it was pregnant—and so in book Tiferet Tzvi. But this is certainly not so—for then in Orach Chaim there regarding eggs sold on Yom Tov we would also say thus; and explicitly Ran wrote an egg does not become presumption of prohibition—from which presumption of pregnant does not help; and now she gave birth—and this is because we say establish the offspring in presumption of permission it had in its mother's womb. And similarly Tosafot beginning of Bechoros wrote. But one may say according to what Noda B'Yehuda wrote part Even HaEzer siman 19 regarding stillborn of animal there is presumption of prohibition because it was in presumption it is not slaughtered, and we combine minority of stillborns to presumption it is not slaughtered—for if stillborn shechitah does not help; in present minority all say combine minority to presumption. And therefore one may say we do not rely here on gentile mesisach l'fi tumo—for there is presumption of prohibition. And this is not as Tiferet Moshe wrote simply that here there is no presumption of prohibition—for also on day one it is permitted if known months completed, and while alive until day eight the matter is revealed retroactively it was never stillborn and was before eight like a piece of doubt milk—end of his words. And according to Noda B'Yehuda's view as above.
