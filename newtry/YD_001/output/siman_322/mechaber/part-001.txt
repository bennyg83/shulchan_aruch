**** ENGLISH ****
The difference between challah of Eretz Yisrael and challah outside the land. It contains 5 seifim. It is a positive mitzvah to separate terumah from the dough and give it to the kohen, as it is said, the first of your doughs you shall raise up terumah. This "first" has no measure from the Torah; even if he separated an olive's bulk he exempted the dough. One who makes his entire dough challah has done nothing until he leaves some. By rabbinic law they separate one twenty-fourth from the dough. A baker who makes to sell in the market separates one forty-eighth. If the dough became impure unintentionally or under duress — even a homeowner separates one forty-eighth.

**** ENGLISH ****
There are three laws for challah in three lands. All the land that the exiles from Babylon took possession of until Acre — they separate one challah of the proper measure and it is eaten by kohanim. The rest of Eretz Yisrael that the exiles from Egypt took possession of and not the exiles from Babylon — from Acre until the Emek — they separate two challot: the first one forty-eighth and it is burned; the second has no measure and they give it to the kohen to eat. All the land from the Emek and outward — whether in Syria or other lands — they separate two challot: the first has no measure and is burned; the second one forty-eighth and is eaten, and it is permitted to those impure, even zavim and zavot. In this time, when there is no pure dough because of impurity of the dead — they separate one challah in all Eretz Yisrael, one forty-eighth, and burn it because it is impure. From Acre until the Emek they separate a second for the kohen to eat, and it has no measure as it was formerly.

Challah outside the land — even though it is impure, since its main obligation is rabbinic — is not forbidden in eating except to a kohen from whom impurity issues from his body: those who had seminal emission, zavim, zavot, niddot, and yoledot. But other impure persons through contact with impurity — even impure persons are permitted to eat it. Therefore, whether in Syria or outside the land — if he wishes to separate one challah he separates one forty-eighth and it is eaten by a minor who has not yet seen keri, or a minor girl who has not yet seen niddah; he need not separate a second. So too if there was a kohen gadol who immersed from his seminal emission or his ziva — even though he did not wait for sunset (and some say he must wait for sunset) (Ran, chapter Elu Overin) — behold, he is permitted to eat the first challah and need not separate a second outside the land: {Rama: Some say that since challah is not eaten in this time in Eretz Yisrael, in other places too they need separate only one challah and burn it (Tur in the name of R' Yitzchak and other poskim). So is the widespread custom in all these countries — they separate only one challah without measure and burn it, as they did when separating two challot, when the challah of light had no measure; nevertheless the custom is to take an olive's bulk (Maharil). One who eats challah — even outside the land — blesses first on the species he wishes to eat, then blesses Who has sanctified us with the holiness of Aaron and commanded us to eat terumah (Kol Bo, and it is from the words of Rambam at the end of Hilchot Terumot). Some say when burning challah they make a separate fire for it, for an Israelite is forbidden to benefit from it (Maharik, and so in Tosafot, chapter Kol She'ah).
