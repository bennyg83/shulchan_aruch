**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It has no fixed measure.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah siman 201, who develops an important distinction in the mitzvah of separating challah. There are two aspects: removing the prohibition of tevel from the dough, and giving the challah to a kohen. These are distinct. To exempt the dough, the Torah imposes no minimum amount, just as one grain can exempt an entire pile. But to fulfill the mitzvah of giving, there must be an amount significant enough to constitute a gift, because the verse says, "you shall give." The familiar fraction, such as one twenty-fourth or one forty-eighth, therefore has both a Torah and rabbinic dimension. By way of example, if one twenty-fourth is the relevant amount, the Torah aspect is that one twenty-fourth of an issaron constitutes a significant gift. Once that amount is given, there is no Torah distinction whether the dough was one issaron or one hundred se'ah. The rabbinic aspect is to require one twenty-fourth of the actual dough. Thus, if the dough consists of twenty-four issaron, one full issaron must be given. See his lengthy discussion.
