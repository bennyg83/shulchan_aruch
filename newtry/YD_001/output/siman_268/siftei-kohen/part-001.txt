**** ENGLISH ****
<b>However, after the fact, if he did not circumcise or immerse, etc.</b> Regarding acceptance of the mitzvos, if he did not accept them before three judges, this invalidates the conversion even after the fact, as Tosafos and the Rosh wrote, because acceptance of the mitzvos is comparable to the beginning of a judicial proceeding, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 5. Immersion and circumcision, however, are comparable to the completion of a judicial proceeding, which may be concluded at night, as explained there. This requires some study, for the Rama wrote there in seif 2 that some say that if judges transgressed and adjudicated at night, their judgment is valid. According to this, even acceptance of the mitzvos should certainly suffice after the fact at night, and the Rama should not have ruled without qualification. Perhaps only regarding monetary law did the Rama take the dissenting view into account, because property declared ownerless by beis din is ownerless. The Bach likewise wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 5, that although regarding chalitzah one should rule stringently that even after the fact chalitzah performed at night is invalid, nevertheless the dissenting opinion is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon in monetary law. End quote. The same applies here, for conversion is comparable to chalitzah.
