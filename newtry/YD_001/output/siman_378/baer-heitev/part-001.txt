**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To eat.</b> This implies that if the mourner wishes to fast that day, he may do so, and Bach writes likewise. Hagahot Ashri, citing Or Zarua, says that one should not allow a mourner to fast on the first day, but should feed him and give him drink. Even on the fast before Rosh Hashanah, Rashbam did not allow a mourner to fast on the day of burial. Beit Yosef and the poskim cite this. Nevertheless, even according to our ruling that he may fast if he wishes, if he prefers not to fast on the days before Rosh Hashanah or similar voluntary fasts, he may refrain. If the death occurred at midday after he had already accepted the fast, he waits until evening after prayer but need not complete the fast fully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On Shabbos.</b> Shach writes that this requires study, because at the end of siman 399 Beit Yosef rules that the mourner's meal is provided on Shabbos, and writes that this is the simple custom throughout the world, with no one objecting. Perhaps the present statement follows the practice in some places, especially among Sephardim, to provide meals throughout all seven days because the mourner is preoccupied with grief and does not prepare food, and also so as not to embarrass one who cannot support himself during the seven days when he does not work. Rabbenu Yerucham writes similarly. According to that custom, the mourner's meal is also provided on Shabbos because mourners rely upon it and otherwise may have nothing to eat. But according to the custom to provide only the first meal, with bread and eggs, we have never heard of doing so on Shabbos. <b>..</b>
