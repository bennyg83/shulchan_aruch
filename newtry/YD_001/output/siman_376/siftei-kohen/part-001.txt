**** ENGLISH ****Within seven for their mourning, etc. — written in Minhagim: this is specifically on Shabbat within the seven; but on weekdays he does not go to synagogue; but if the seven-year-old is a minor not bar mitzvah and goes to synagogue all seven to say Kaddish, or one who had his father's death and if within those seven a yarzeit occurs he says orphan Kaddish once; end quote. And it seems one whose mourning was nullified by a festival — e.g. one hour before the festival — the decree of seven or thirty was nullified for him; nevertheless he has the law of seven and thirty even on weekdays as on Shabbat — for by law he goes to synagogue. Maharshal responsum siman 73: Minhagim' words contradict each other — initially wrote the day a father died, and a yarzeit for a resident pushes aside a thirty-day mourner, a fortiori a twelve-month mourner — the resident gets the entire fast day, i.e. evening, morning, and mincha prayer and the Kaddishim of that day before the Amud — implies he holds yarzeit is preferable; again wrote a twelve-month mourner and yarzeit guest gets one Kaddish but a thirty-day guest shares all Kaddishim and prayers — implies thirty-day is preferable; he resolved: initially it deals where there are many yarzeit — if they do not say at all its time passed and the sacrifice is void; per this what Minhagim wrote — if the seven-year-old is a minor or a yarzeit occurred within those seven he says orphan Kaddish once — this is specifically yarzeit because time passed and sacrifice void; but for thirty-day need not give even one Kaddish; end quote — does not seem so initially where there are many yarzeit; and from what he wrote that thirty pushes aside a twelve-month mourner — apparently in the twelve-month case it deals with thirty, and in twelve-month certainly even one yarzeit; and if so what does a fortiori mean — perhaps where there is only one yarzeit it does not push even twelve-month; and that pushing thirty is because there are many yarzeit — rather it seems the words as their plain meaning: there is only one yarzeit and nevertheless it pushes the thirty-day mourner — Minhagim hold yarzeit preferred over thirty-day; so Taz below; no difficulty with what is written at the end that a yarzeit guest gets only one Kaddish and thirty-day shares with a resident twelve-month mourner — one can say certainly regarding guest against resident thirty is preferred because the resident can say by law need not give you anything at all — where both are thirty-day or yarzeit or twelve-month mourners the guest gets nothing; but since you are thirty and I twelve-month you share with me; but yarzeit whose time is fixed and known to him a full year ahead and you came to my boundary — one Kaddish suffices; but for thirty-day cannot say so; per our practice that thirty-day is preferred — if a thirty-day resident occurred within seven of a minor, the minor also gets one Kaddish — and so is the custom.

"Within thirty he has one Kaddish" implies if there are three Kaddishim, e.g. on Shabbat — the thirty-day mourner says two and the yarzeit one; so Maharil end of Laws of Prayer and Minhagim: the day a father or mother died, yarzeit for a resident pushes aside thirty-day, a fortiori twelve-month — the resident gets that entire fast day, i.e. evening, morning, and mincha prayer and the Kaddishim of that day before the Amud; end quote.
