<b>מנהג המנחמים ודין מת שאין לו מנחמים. ובו ד' סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="1"></i>אין המנחמים רשאים לפתוח עד שיפתח האבל תחלה <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="2"></i>והאבל מיסב בראש <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="3"></i>וכיון שנענע האבל בראשו בענין שנראה שפוטר את המנחמים אינם רשאים לישב אצלו: <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="1"></i><small>(אין אבל או חולה חייבים לעמוד אפילו מפני נשיא): (פ' א"מ)</small>:
**** ENGLISH ****
The practice of comforters and the law of a deceased who has no comforters. And it contains 4 seifim. The comforters may not begin speaking until the mourner begins first; and the mourner sits at the head. Once the mourner nods his head in a manner that appears he dismisses the comforters, they may not sit with him: {Rama: (A mourner or sick person is not obligated to stand even before a nasi:) (chapter 1 of Moed Katan).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who comes to honor his fellow and to rise before him tells him, Sit — except if he is a mourner or sick person, for it implies: sit in your mourning, sit in your illness: {Rama: One should not say, I have not been repaid according to my deeds, or similar things, lest he open his mouth to the Satan (so it appears in the chapter Mi SheMeto and in Hagahot Alfasi there); and one should not say to a mourner, What do you have to do, for it is impossible to change — for this is like blasphemy, as it implies that if it were possible to change he would do so; rather he should accept upon himself the decree of Heaven with love (Nimmukei Yosef, chapter Shor SheNagach, as it appears from the Gemara).}

**** ENGLISH ****
A deceased who has no mourners to be comforted — ten fit men come and sit in his place all seven days of mourning, and the rest of the people gather upon them; and if there were not ten fixed there each day, each day ten from the rest of the people gather and sit in his place: {Rama: And I have not seen people practice thus; and it is written in Maharil that the custom is to pray with ten all seven days in the place where the person died; and this is regarding a person who left no known relatives to mourn him; but if he has somewhere that they mourn him, it is not needed (and so it is fit to practice).}

Now the custom is that after the sealing of the grave with earth (or after the mourner turns his face from the deceased) they remove shoe and sandal and distance themselves slightly from the cemetery and say Kaddish that is destined to renew the world; and afterward they pluck earth and pluck grass and throw behind their backs and wash their hands in water: {Rama: Some say they sit three times because the spirits accompany him and as long as they sit they flee from him (Maharil in responsum 23 in the name of some say); and in these lands they did not practice sitting, only three times after washing hands, and each time they say, And may the pleasantness, etc., He sits in secret, etc.; and when the deceased is buried on a festival they may sit thus three times as on a weekday (so in Hagahot Minhagim); and the same if buried close to Shabbat they do so on Shabbat; and the custom is to be strict that a person not enter another house before he washes and sits three times — and the custom of our fathers is Torah (there in Maharil); and it is found in midrashim to say Kaddish for a father (Kol Bo and Rivash in the name of Tanchuma and Sifri and Bachyei in the name of tractate Kallah and Beit Yosef in the name of the Zohar and Avraham Zacuto in the name of Tana deVei Eliyahu Rabbah); therefore the custom is to say for father and mother the final Kaddish for twelve months; and so the custom is to read haftarah from the Prophets and to pray the evening prayer on Motza'ei Shabbatot, for that is the time souls return to Gehinnom; and when the son prays and sanctifies in public he redeems his father and mother from Gehinnom (Kol Bo in the name of Hagahot); and the custom is to say Kaddish for the mother even though the father still lives — it is not in his power to protest that his son not say Kaddish for his mother (the reasoning of Beit Yosef according to the custom); it is a mitzvah to fast on the day one's father or mother died (Rivash and Kol Bo). and whoever knows how to pray the entire prayer prays; and if there are other mourners, the custom is that within seven for their mourning they take precedence and he has no Kaddish at all; within thirty he has one Kaddish; after thirty all the Kaddishim of that day are his; and they count seven and thirty from the day of burial even if the mourner did not hear immediately (in Maharil); and if he is a non-Jew, for this Kaddish matter he is like one of the townspeople — they follow the custom (Agur in the name of Maharil); and there is no place for this Kaddish except for father and mother alone, not for other relatives (Bach); and if there is no mourner in the synagogue for his father and mother, he says that Kaddish, Who has no father and mother, on behalf of all the dead of Israel (there); and there are places that practiced that other relatives say Kaddish for their relatives when there are no mourners for their father and mother; and there are places that even when there are mourners for father and mother other relatives say Kaddish, but they make a compromise between them that they do not say as many Kaddishim as mourners for father and mother (Mahariq root 44); and in all this they follow the custom, provided it be a fixed custom in the city; and mourners say Kaddish even on Shabbat and festivals (Bach in the name of R' Yehudah of Korbeil); but they did not practice praying on Shabbat and festivals (so in responsum of Maharil), even though there is no prohibition in the matter; but on weekdays whoever knows how to pray should pray, and it helps more than orphan's Kaddish that was instituted only for minors; and whoever does not know how to pray the entire prayer should pray Lamenatzeiach and Uva leTzion, etc. (Bach); and the custom is not to say Kaddish and prayer for more than eleven months, so they not make their father and mother wicked, for the judgment of a wicked person is twelve months; and if there were mourners here and afterward other mourners came, the second have the Kaddishim and prayers all thirty days from the day of burial even if they did not hear. Some say that an informer killed by gentiles — his sons say Kaddish for him (Radach, Beit Yosef, and in Bach) (and see above siman 340).}
