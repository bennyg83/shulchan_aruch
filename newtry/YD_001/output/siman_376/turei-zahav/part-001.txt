**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Which implies that if it were possible to change matters, and so forth.</b> Bava Kamma recounts that Ulla would not go to comfort R. Shmuel bar Yehudah because the Babylonian consolation phrase, "What can you do?" sounded as though if something could be done, one would alter Hashem's decree. He called this a form of blasphemous speech. Maharshal rejects this from David's statement after his child's death, "Can I bring him back again?" which sounds similarly as though he would do so if possible. I am surprised at this objection. David was explaining why he ceased fasting. Before the child died, every possible prayer and fast was appropriate in the hope that Hashem would spare him. After death, such fasting could no longer change the decree. He adds, "I am going to him, but he will not return to me," meaning even future techiyat hameitim will not occur during David's lifetime. The problematic condolence formula is different. It tells the mourner not to grieve because nothing can be done, implying that if human action could reverse the decree, he would prefer his own will to Hashem's. Proper consolation should direct the person toward acceptance of Hashem's will, not merely toward helplessness. This distinction is clear.

<b>The custom is to pray with ten people throughout all seven days.</b> Orach Chayyim siman 131 states that Tachanun is not recited in a mourner's house throughout shivah. Rokeach writes that after the conclusion of prayer Tachanun is recited elsewhere, and that Hallel is not recited in the mourner's house on Rosh Chodesh because aninut-like sorrow is present there and the verse "the dead do not praise Hashem" would constitute loeg larash. My father-in-law writes that during the Yamim Noraim, if the mourner is a particularly fitting prayer leader and no equal is available, he takes precedence to lead even within thirty days for father or mother. This is also found in a gloss, unlike those who are strict.

He says this contains a kabbalistic secret. Levush records that he himself followed this practice.

<b>שתוך ז' לאבלם. </b>במנהגים כתוב שבן ל' נדחה מבן יארציי"ט לגמרי אם הוא תושב כל אותו יום ורמ"א כ' כאן שאין ליא"צ אלא קדיש א' תוך ל' ובספר משאת בנימין הקשה דברי המנהגים אהדדי דמתחלה כתבו בן ל' ויא"צ דוחה היא"צ לבן ל' כמו לי"ב חודש אלמא דיא"צ עדיף מבן ל' ושוב כ' דבן י"ב תושב ויארציי"ט אורח או בן שלשים אורח מסלק היב"ח תושב להיארציי"ט אורח בקדיש אחד ותפלה אחת ולבן שלשים אורח מחלק עמו כל הקדישי' כו' ש"מ דבן שלשים עדיף מיארציי"ט וחילק שם דהיכא דאיכא רווחא כגון בשבת דאיכא שלשה קדישים ואחד בן שלשים ואחד יארציי"ט אז בן שלשים עדיף דהוא אומר שני קדישים והיארציי"ט קדיש אחד אבל היכא דאיכא דוחק בקדישים ואינו מגיע לכל אחד קדיש אחד אז נדחה הבן שלשים מפני היארציי"ט והטעם שאם לא יאמר היארציי"ט אותו יום קדיש לא יוכל לומר למחר כי עבר היום בטל הקרבן מה שאין כן בבן שלשים שיוכל לומר למחר וחילוק נכון הוא דכל שאפשר לתת קדיש לשניהם אז יש מעלה לבן שלשים על היארצייט משא"כ אם יהיה היארציי"ט נדחה לגמרי זהו אינו מסתבר וכתב עוד דלענין שבעה אין חילוק בין גדול לקטן תושב לאורח <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>נראה לי דמי שמת בשבת או בי"ט וא"א לקברו בו ביום דעל כל פנים יאמר קדיש תיכף אחר המיתה דאין אמירת קדיש תלוי באבילות כלל אבל בחול אין לעשות כן דהא כשהוא אונן פטור מן התפלה כן נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Who are within the seven days of mourning.</b> The minhagim state that a person within thirty days is completely displaced by one observing a yahrzeit if the yahrzeit mourner is a resident for that entire day. Rama here says a yahrzeit receives only one Kaddish against one within thirty days. Masat Binyamin notes an apparent contradiction in the customs: in one place yahrzeit outranks thirty-day mourning, while elsewhere a thirty-day visitor can share all Kaddishim and even displace a twelve-month resident more than a yahrzeit visitor does. He distinguishes between abundance and scarcity of Kaddish opportunities. Where there are enough, such as three Kaddishim on Shabbos with one mourner in sheloshim and one yahrzeit, the thirty-day mourner receives two and the yahrzeit one. But where there are too few for each to receive one, the yahrzeit takes priority, because if he misses that day the opportunity is gone, while the sheloshim mourner can say Kaddish the next day. This is a sound distinction. Regarding shivah there is no difference between adult and minor, resident and visitor. In my opinion, if someone dies on Shabbos or Yom Tov and cannot be buried that day, the son should nevertheless begin saying Kaddish immediately after death, because Kaddish does not depend on the formal start of mourning. On a weekday, however, while he is an onen and exempt from prayer, he should not do so.
