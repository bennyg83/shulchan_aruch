**** ENGLISH ****The patch. Bach wrote: Mahari"k permitted what they practice — patching with cloth over holes in a sheet before beginning writing a Torah scroll, and afterward writing on the patch — unlike some sages who brought proofs to forbid; and he rejected their proofs. And to me the essence is like the forbidders; but since ancients practiced reading in such a Torah scroll before great ones of the world and they did not protest — therefore no great one should protest and teach prohibition to others; but one who is stringent for himself in his Torah scroll — behold this is praiseworthy and will be rewarded. However it appears if he wrote part of the letter on the sheet and part on the patch — this is invalid per all, for when the patch is separated here will be found half a letter and there half a letter. And not only that — even if the whole letter was written on the sheet but part of the ink from one side of that letter also passes onto the patch — encircling the hide on the patch does not help for the letter written on the sheet; rather the letter on the sheet must be encircled with hide on the sheet itself without patch; and likewise the letter on the patch must be encircled on the patch itself without the sheet — end quote. And Taz disputes this and wrote: since attached parchment helps for what is written on it, as though written on the sheet — why should it not also help to write half on this and half on that?
