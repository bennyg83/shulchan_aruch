<b>תרומה. </b>כמ"ש תרימו תרומה ובלשון המקרא חלה נקרא העיסה כמ"ש חלות בלולות והרבה כיוצא אבל בלשון המשנה נקרא חלה בכל מקום וז"ש או כו' ומ"מ נכון לברך בלשון מקרא כמ"ש ברפ"ו דברכות רי"א ב"מ דשאים ול"ק ירקות בלשון מתני':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Terumah.</b> As the verse says, "you shall separate terumah." In Scriptural language, challah can mean the dough or loaf itself, as in "cakes mixed with oil" and many similar examples. In Mishnahic language, however, the separated gift is everywhere called challah. This is why one may say either formulation. Nevertheless, it is preferable to formulate the blessing in Scriptural language, as stated at the beginning of chapter 6 of Berachot, where R. Yehudah says "the various grasses" rather than the Mishnahic term "vegetables.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Provided that they are.</b> Rambam and Tur imply this even according to our ruling that the buttocks are not considered ervah, as stated in Berachot concerning turning one's face away. The Yerushalmi here likewise says that this statement refers to the buttocks, as cited by Rash and Rosh. It appears that Rambam did not have the reading "shall we say this supports," but rather simply "this supports." Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak's explanation is an independent point: even if her face is pressed down, the buttocks remain visible.
