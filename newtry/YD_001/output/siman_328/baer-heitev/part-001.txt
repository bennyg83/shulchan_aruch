**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A maidservant.</b> Bach disputes Rama. Terumat HaDeshen did not permit a maidservant initially to separate challah merely because the mistress regularly gives her permission, unless there is concern that the dough will spoil. In such a case even a woman who is not a maidservant becomes an agent without explicit appointment. It follows that where she is not a maidservant and there is no concern of spoilage, she may not separate without the owner's knowledge even if she kneaded the dough. Nevertheless, Bach writes that after the fact, if the kneader separated challah without permission and the owner is pleased that she did so, the separation is valid and challah need not be taken again. But if a person who was not the kneader separated it without permission, even after the fact it is not challah and it must be separated again. Shach. Taz discusses the matter at length and concludes that on Erev Shabbos, when women customarily insist on separating challah themselves, no maidservant should separate without the mistress's instruction unless there is concern that the dough will spoil. Baer Heitev adds that even if the dough will spoil on Erev Shabbos, the maidservant still has no authority to separate challah. On other weekdays, however, the maidservant or another household member may separate if the mistress regularly allows them to do so and is not particular about it.
