**** ENGLISH ****
It is not a vow. Tur wrote here: certainly it is not a vow; his intent is that in the mishnah it states: one who wants to vow his fellow that he eat with him should say, "All vows I am destined to vow shall be null"; and the Gemara challenges: since his fellow heard, he certainly will not listen and will not go eat; and answers: defective text, and thus it teaches: one who wants his fellow to eat with him and he refuses and he forbids him — these are deziruzin vows; and one who wants his vows not to stand all year should stand on Rosh Hashanah and say all vows I am destined to vow shall be null — meaning the tanna states two matters; it appears from the questioner's words that it is obvious the vow does not take effect since he first said to nullify it; and although in seif 2 we say if he remembers the stipulation at the time of the vow his vow stands — Ran answers, brought by Beit Yosef, that it is different here because he stipulated at the time of the vow, and since he says explicitly he vows on his condition, therefore the vow is null; but here, where stipulation and vow do not come together, one may say he uproots it; and it appears not necessarily that he must say explicitly at the time of the vow that he vows on his condition, for Tur did not mention this — rather since he leans the vow on the stipulation it is as if he said explicitly; and our master Tur considers this law simple since everything is done at once; and they only wrote because they need to teach the end specifically if he articulated with his lips; and one may say he states the stipulation in a matter his fellow does not hear — nevertheless we do not establish the mishnah thus, for the mishnah needs to teach a remedy what he should do lest his fellow think he forbade him, and this is not a remedy, for perhaps his fellow will also hear; and in any case it is difficult, for it is impossible to say Tur and Shulchan Aruch deal here with the case of the mishnah we mentioned — one who wants to forbid him to eat — for in that case it would be nedarim deziruzin as we conclude in the Gemara, and likewise in siman 232 seif 3; and if so, even without prior stipulation there is no vow, as we establish the mishnah — unless he wants to make a full vow and cancel it first; if so, why need stipulation and vow — be silent entirely, since in one act he does both? And it appears we deal with one who wants to vow a full vow before his fellow to mislead him, and therefore it is not nedarim deziruzin, as Rama wrote in siman 232 that one may not extend deziruzin to another place beyond what the Sages said; and this one wants to cancel that vow must cancel explicitly; and the Gemara's challenge: if he remembered the condition he uprooted it — why not answer he does so to mislead his fellow? One may say there it refers to a general stipulation on what he will vow all year, and necessarily two matters are taught, and there is no misleading; unlike here where he does so by chance like one who happens to need a vow before him — so it appears to reconcile Tur's view. And written in Perishah: seemingly the matter does not depend on whether he stipulates now at the time of the vow or before, only if he stipulates and says explicitly "tomorrow or after a time I will vow not to eat meat, and behold I nullify it now" — behold it is nullified, for he stipulated explicitly on this vow; unlike when he stipulated generally and said "if I vow," etc. And I did not know a place for these words of Perishah, for certainly in this case the distinction applies — if he remembers the condition, etc., as written shortly: if he remembers the condition and vows, behold he uproots his condition; but here we deal as Ran wrote above — he stipulated at the time of the vow; and likewise Darkei Moshe — here specifically at the time of the vow.

וצריך עיון:
**** ENGLISH ****Unless he remembers it within kedei dibbur of the vow. Tur wrote thus in Rambam's name and concluded on him that Rosh did not distinguish thus. But in truth there is room for investigation — for in seif 3 he wrote "and he remembered what he conditioned," and there it helps even if he remembered after much time, as Ran wrote "and he remembered afterward"; and why is remembrance needed here within kedei dibbur of the vow. Let us quote Rambam's and Raavad's language and their intention will be explained — it is not so. Rambam's language in chapter 2 of Nedarim: "If he does not remember the condition until after he vowed — even though he accepted the condition in his heart and fulfilled the condition, behold the vow is void even though he did not now utter the retraction with his lips, for he already preceded the retraction to the vow and uttered it with his lips beforehand. And some direct to be stringent and say — and it is that he remember the condition after he vowed within kedei dibbur," end quote. Raavad wrote: "Well directed this — and not in remembering the condition as he holds, but in accepting and fulfilling it — that if he waited kedei dibbur after he remembered it and did not fulfill it, his vow is already void and its condition, for he must say in his heart 'on condition I rely' and he should have said 'and it is that he accept the condition within kedei dibbur after he remembers it,'" end quote. Raavad's intention is clear — we do not care at all about within kedei dibbur of the vow, only that he remember the condition even after much time, he needs within kedei dibbur of that remembrance to set upon his heart to fulfill the condition; and even Rambam intended this in this view that is stringent and says "and it is that he remember the condition after he vowed within kedei dibbur" — his intention to say he remember to fulfill the condition after the vow within kedei dibbur — meaning within kedei dibbur of his remembrance; and since he concluded "within kedei dibbur" it does not refer to the vow that follows, but I mention remembrance of the condition he first remembered. And since the language is ambiguous accordingly — for he did not remember anything of fulfilling the condition in his heart — Raavad wrote he should have said in this language that he accept the condition within kedei dibbur after he remembers it. In truth Rambam's language too should be corrected — remembrance means fulfillment of the condition, as said before that he accepted the condition in his heart; and if Rambam's view were as Tur quotes in his name that within kedei dibbur of the vow remembrance of the condition is needed — Raavad would not write he agrees with him, for Raavad does not need this as we mentioned; rather an entirely different view; but perforce Raavad holds Rambam also holds thus, only his language is not precise.

General principle: if we explain Rambam's words as Tur understood, he has no connection with all the poskim before us — even Tur himself wrote Rosh did not distinguish; and we see no posek wrote thus; and Raavad wrote explicitly this view — within kedei dibbur of remembrance the matter depends; and likewise in Darchei Moshe in Raavad's name — we do not care at all about kedei dibbur of the vow; and if so Shulchan Aruch should not have established halachah requiring within kedei dibbur of the vow, since he himself wrote in Beit Yosef Raavad's words on Rambam "well directed," etc.; and according to Raavad's words he wrote here in Shulchan Aruch "and he shall say in his heart he relies on the condition." Accordingly within kedei dibbur of the vow is not needed; and one may say Shulchan Aruch rules like both views — and we require also fulfillment of the condition within kedei dibbur; and what he wrote "and he shall say in his heart," etc. — also refers to within kedei dibbur of the vow that he first remembered; for if so we truly do not require fulfillment within kedei dibbur of the vow but within kedei dibbur of remembrance — and requires investigation.
