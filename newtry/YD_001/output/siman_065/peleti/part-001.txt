And it is customary to be stringent — thus testified Maharalbach from a great city of sages and scribes, who confessed regarding the milk of a fetus to permit and regarding the sinew to forbid; and there is proof from the Gemara in Keritut daf 101b, which says: blood of grasshoppers and fish, all of which is permitted; and the Gemara says: what is entirely permitted — living animal milk is permitted, and behold a wild animal is permitted; and if so, behold a bird in which there is no sinew and its blood is forbidden only because it does not require shechitah. And it is difficult: a fetus that was slaughtered — if it proves that it does not require shechitah and its blood is nevertheless forbidden, as we establish — its blood is forbidden; and perforce one must say regarding a fetus that gid hanasheh is forbidden and it is not entirely permitted, for there is gid hanasheh, unlike fish; nevertheless one may reject: "all of which is permitted" — not so a fetus; and behold the sugya in the Gemara, beginning of chapter 25, implies one law for it; and one may also challenge: that which we say in Pesachim daf 23 — R' Yishmael holds the sinew is permitted for benefit from an a fortiori from milk — what is milk that is punishable by karet yet permitted for benefit, all the more so the sinew for which there is no karet; and the Gemara challenges: what is milk that was permitted from its entirety with a wild animal, see there; and why does it not challenge: what is milk that was permitted in a fetus and the mother's slaughter permits it, not so the sinew which is not permitted in a fetus and the mother's slaughter does not permit it at all. Therefore the reason of the matter appears that the sinew is not permitted because the Ran gave a reason why the blood of a fetus is not permitted — it says here "every animal you may eat," and explain: blood is not included in eating and drinking; it is considered, and Scripture says "you may eat," end of his words; and this is somewhat strained throughout the Torah — we mean drinking is eating. And behold, if there is no taste in sinews and it is wood, and the Torah forbade it, it is not included in eating; and so Tosafot in Pesachim daf 22 in his comment on Rabbi Shimon, etc. — since there is no taste in sinews, eating is not relevant to it at all; therefore we do not establish the sinew on an a fortiori; and this is correct and clear. However, this is if there is no taste in sinews; but according to the one who holds there is taste in them, this reasoning is not truly applicable — "every animal you may eat" includes the sinew as well; and behold the sugya at the beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh is all according to the one who holds there is taste; and if so, it is obvious that the mother's slaughter permits it, and it has one law with milk — as stated; and also the sugya in Pesachim that we say: if when nevelah was permitted its milk and sinew were permitted — perforce all this is according to the one who holds there is taste in sinews, see there; and if so, the sinew is equal to milk regarding permission through the mother's slaughter, and there is nothing to challenge at all from a fetus; and requires further study:
