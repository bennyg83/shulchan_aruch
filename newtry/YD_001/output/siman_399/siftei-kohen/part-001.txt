**** ENGLISH ****"We do not say that Shmini Atzeret cancels" — and sheva neki'im count only one day when they buried on a festival; so Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Beit Yosef brings this. Specifically when they buried before Sukkot does sheva neki'im also count toward seven — below seif 11; not so here. So too Bach in the name of Tosafot and Mordechai, and he ruled thus — not like Maharshal in responsum siman 5, who wrote that it cancels — that it does not cancel it for counting seven; for the law of sheva neki'im counts even if they buried on a festival. However, in responsum of Mas'at Binyamin siman 71 he ruled like Maharshal; see there — and so seems Derishah's view.

**** ENGLISH ****"And on the eve of Pesach," etc. — the Rav extracted this from responsum of Mahari Mintz siman 4; it requires investigation, for on the contrary it seems there that according to all it is equal for every eve of Yom Tov regarding laundering and bathing — that it was not permitted except close to nightfall, as he concludes there; if so, he may cut hair before noon. Another view: for bathing, perhaps from nightfall, etc.; see there. Only that it is stated there that on the eve of Pesach one may shave before noon, since others are forbidden to shave after noon — for they do not do work on the eve of Pesach after noon because it was the time of slaughtering the Pesach, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 548; see there. I saw in Beit Hadash that he copied the Rav's words and omitted that on the eve of Pesach everything is permitted — and this requires investigation.

<b>וכן נוהגין. </b>וכתוב בת"ה סימן רפ"ז דאפילו מת לו מת בע"ש ואותו שבת הוא ערב הרגל מותר לרחוץ בע"ש לפי מנהגינו ואין להקשות דהיאך כ' הרב וכן נוהגין דהא לעיל ר"ס שפ"א כתב הרב דאנן נהגינן איסור רחיצה כל ל' וכן לעיל ר"ס שפ"ט כתב דנהגינן איסור כביסה כל ל' י"ל דאינו אלא חומרא ומדינא שרי וכדמשמע מדברי הרב שם והכא משום כבוד הרגל לא נהגו להחמיר וכ"כ העט"ז ותדע דהא בת"ה שם כתוב בשם מהר"מ אפילו לא נהג אבילות אלא שעה א' לפני הרגל מותר ברחיצה בחמין סמוך לערב והרי מהר"מ ס"ל דאסרי' רחיצה כל שלשים וכמ"ש הגהמ"י על שמו ומביא ב"י סוף סי' ת' אלא ודאי כדאמרן ואע"ג דבא"ח סימן תקמ"ח ס"ח וס"י כתב הרב דלדידן דנוהגים איסור רחיצה כל ל' אסור לרחוץ דהא הרגל לא בטל ממנו רק גזרת ז' וה"ה לענין כיבוס במקום דנוהגין איסור כיבוס כל ל' עכ"ל היינו משום דכך הוא ראוי להורות אי הוי איסור רחיצה וכיבוס כל ל' מן הדין אבל כאן כתב דמ"מ המנהג אינו כן וכ"כ הב"ח אלא דצריך ליישב לאיזה צורך כ' הרב שם כן כיון דס"ס המנהג אינו כן ונ"ל דס"ל דהא דהמנהג אינו כן היינו משום כבוד הרגל והלכך היכא דליכא משום כבוד הרגל אסור ונ"מ דלאחר הרגל אסור רחיצה וכביסה עד ל' דהרגל לא בטל ממנו רק גזרת ז' כנ"ל ודוק:
**** ENGLISH ****"And so is the custom" — and it is written in Teshuvot Maharil siman 287: even if his dead died on erev Shabbat and that Shabbat is the eve of the festival, it is permitted to bathe on erev Shabbat according to our custom; and one should not ask: how did the Rav write "and so is the custom," for above siman 381 the Rav wrote that we observe the prohibition of bathing all thirty, and likewise above siman 389 he wrote that we observe the prohibition of laundering all thirty? One may answer that it is only a stringency and by law it is permitted, as implied from the Rav's words there; and here because of honor of the festival they did not observe stringency — so too Taz. Know that in Teshuvot Maharil there it is written in the name of Maharam: even if he observed mourning only one hour before the festival, it is permitted to bathe in hot water close to evening — yet Maharam holds that bathing is forbidden all thirty, as Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in his name and Beit Yosef brings at the end of siman 10. Rather, certainly as we said; and even though in Orach Chaim siman 548 seif 8 and seif 10 the Rav wrote that for us, who observe prohibition of bathing all thirty, it is forbidden to bathe — for the festival canceled from him only the decree of seven, and the same for laundering where they observe prohibition of laundering all thirty — end quote; meaning that thus it is proper to teach if bathing and laundering all thirty were forbidden by law; but here he wrote that nevertheless the custom is not so — so too Bach. But one must reconcile for what purpose the Rav wrote thus there, since presumably the custom is not so; and it seems to me he holds that the custom not being so is because of honor of the festival — therefore where there is no concern for honor of the festival it is forbidden. Practical difference: after the festival, bathing and laundering are forbidden until thirty, for the festival canceled from him only the decree of seven, as stated; investigate.
