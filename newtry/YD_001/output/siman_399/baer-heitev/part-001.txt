**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To wear.</b> Responsa Masat Binyamin discusses a groom who married on Erev Yom Tov and experienced a bereavement during the festival. Ordinarily, if a groom becomes a mourner during the seven wedding days, he does not begin counting the thirty-day mourning period until after the seven wedding days, because wedding days are stronger than a festival: a groom is permitted pressed garments and haircutting, while a festival itself restricts these activities. Masat Binyamin argues that where the wedding days themselves fall during the festival, the groom is in any event forbidden pressed garments and haircutting because of the festival, and therefore those days should count toward the thirty days. Taz strongly rejects this. The festival counts only where the person actually bears a mourning prohibition that overlaps with the festival prohibition, so he can say the restriction he observes also reflects mourning. Here the groom has no mourning obligation at all during his wedding days; his restrictions arise only from the festival. Those days therefore cannot count toward the thirty-day period. Taz concludes that the festival days do not count for him, just as with any other groom. Shach writes that Mechaber's rule that festival days count toward the thirty applies even when one first hears of the death during the festival, not only when he knew before the festival, unlike the wording in Beit Yosef. Perhaps Beit Yosef did not mean to limit the law, or the text should read "during the festival.

<b>Shavuot.</b> Shach writes that Shemini Atzeret counts only as one day when burial occurred during the festival. Only if burial took place before Sukkot does Shemini Atzeret count as seven days. This is unlike Maharshal, who wrote that although it does not cancel mourning, it always counts as seven toward the thirty even when burial occurred during the festival. Responsa Masat Binyamin and Derishah agree with Maharshal. Taz, however, argues at length against Maharshal and Masat Binyamin.

<b>נוהגין. </b> וכתב בת"ה דאפילו מת לו מת בע"ש ואותו שבת הוא ערב הרגל מותר לרחוץ בע"ש לפי מנהגינו. וא"ל היאך כתב הרב וכן נוהגין הא לעיל ר"ס שפ"א כ' דאנן נהגינן איסור רחיצה כל ל' וכן בר"ס שפ"ט כתב דנהגינן איסור כביסה כל ל' י"ל דאינו אלא חומרא ומדינא שרי והכא משום כבוד הרגל לא נהגו להחמיר וכ"כ הלבוש ואע"ג דבא"ח סי' תקמ"ח ס"ח וס"י כתב הרב דלדידן דנוהגים איסור רחיצה כל ל' אסור לרחוץ דהא הרגל לא בטל ממנו רק גזירת ז' וה"ה לענין כבוס במקום דנוהגין איסור כבוס כל ל' ע"כ היינו משום דכך ראוי להורות אי הוי איסור רחיצה וכבוס כל ל' מן הדין אבל כאן כתב דמ"מ המנהג אינו כן וכ"כ הב"ח אלא דצריך ליישב לאיזה צורך כתב הרב שם כן כיון דס"ס המנהג אינו כך. ונ"ל דס"ל דהא דמנהג אינו כן היינו משום כבוד הרגל והלכך היכא דליכא משום כבוד הרגל אסור ונ"מ דלאחר הרגל אסור רחיצה וכביסה עד ל' דהרגל לא ביטל ממנו רק גזרת ז' ודוק עכ"ל הש"ך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>We practice.</b> Terumat HaDeshen writes that even if a relative dies on Erev Shabbos and that Shabbos is immediately before the festival, according to our custom he may bathe on Erev Shabbos. One might ask how Rama can say "this is the custom" when above, at the beginning of siman 381, he writes that our custom is to prohibit bathing for thirty days, and at the beginning of siman 389 that we prohibit laundering for thirty days. The answer is that those are stringencies rather than strict law, and for the honor of the festival the custom was not to maintain those extra stringencies. Levush and Bach write likewise. In Orach Chayyim siman 548, however, Rama writes that according to our custom of prohibiting bathing throughout thirty days, one may not bathe merely because the festival canceled only the seven-day decree, and the same applies to laundering in places where it is prohibited for thirty days. This describes the strict ruling if the thirty-day bathing and laundering prohibitions were treated as full law. Here, however, Rama records the actual custom, which is more lenient for the honor of the festival. Shach explains that after the festival, where there is no festival honor involved, bathing and laundering remain prohibited until thirty days because the festival canceled only the seven-day decree.

<b>כ"ב. </b> כתב הט"ז קשה ליחשב ש"ע כרגל בפני עצמו גם לענין ביטול שלשים כיון דנתבטל השבעה קודם החג והחג נחשב לשבעה הרי י"ד כמו דאמרי' בי"כ בס"י. תירץ הרא"ש דשאני הכא שלא נהג דין ל' כלל ואע"ג שנהג גיהוץ ותספורת מ"מ הרי בלאו אבילות נמי אסור באלו מחמת הרגל וצ"ע לתרץ זה אליבא דהרמב"ן דס"ל דיש באבלות איסור נוסף על איסור הרגל א"כ קשה למה לא יבטל ש"ע גזרת ל' כיון שכבר נהג בדין ל' בתוספת האיסור. ונראה דמ"מ יש שינוי משאר ל' בחול דהתם יש היכר בעיקר דין גיהוץ ותספורת משא"כ כאן שאין היכר אלא בתוספת וזה אין הוכחה כ"כ לכך די בזה שנאמר הרגל עולה למנין הל' אבל לא להיות כנהג מקצת שלשים ממש לענין שש"ע יבטל לגמרי שאר הל' עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Twenty-two.</b> Taz asks why Shemini Atzeret is not counted as a separate festival also for canceling the thirty-day period. Since the seven-day mourning was canceled before Sukkot and the festival itself counts as seven days, there are already fourteen days, just as regarding Yom Kippur above in seif 10. Rosh answers that here the mourner did not actually observe any laws of the thirty days. Although he refrained from pressed garments and haircutting, those activities are prohibited during the festival even apart from mourning. Taz asks how this works according to Ramban, who holds that mourning adds an additional prohibition on top of the festival restriction. If so, why should Shemini Atzeret not cancel the remainder of the thirty days, since he already observed some distinct element of the thirty-day mourning? Taz answers that there is still a difference from an ordinary weekday thirty-day period. On weekdays, the restriction on pressed garments and haircutting itself visibly marks mourning. During the festival, there is no such visible marker because those activities are already prohibited; only the additional layer of prohibition comes from mourning, and that is not sufficiently recognizable. It is therefore enough to say that the festival counts toward the thirty, but not that the partial observance cancels the remainder entirely.
