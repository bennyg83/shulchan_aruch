(Siman 87, seif 1) Three times: once for the prohibition of eating, once for the prohibition of benefit, and once for the prohibition of cooking. Thus we say in the Talmud in chapter Kol HaBasar, and the Rambam in the beginning of chapter 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot wrote as follows: basar b'chalav is forbidden to cook and forbidden to eat from the Torah and forbidden in benefit, etc., and one who cooks from both of them an olive-size as one would receive lashes, etc., and likewise one who eats an olive-size from both of them, etc., would receive lashes — end of his words. And it is astonishing that he did not write that one who derives benefit from basar b'chalav would receive lashes, since it is the negative commandment of "you shall not cook," the third one that is written. And also in the enumeration of the commandments he did not count this negative commandment. And it is more difficult for me regarding the words of the Rav the Maggid, who wrote on what the Rambam wrote in chapter 8 of Ma'achalot Asurot halacha 16, and these are his words: every food that is forbidden in benefit — if one derived benefit and did not eat, such as if he sold it or gave it to a gentile or to dogs, he does not receive lashes — end of his words. And the Migdal Oz wrote on this that the reason is that in chapter Kol Sha'ah they say: all prohibitions in the Torah one does not receive lashes for them except in the manner of their benefit. And every thing fit for eating — its manner of benefit is only through eating as proper — end of his words. And this reasoning is also found in Rashi in Eruvin (daf 30) — see there. And it is difficult: if so, regarding basar b'chalav and kilayei hakerem, where they say there in chapter Kol Sha'ah that one would receive lashes for them even not in the manner of their benefit, and likewise the Rambam in chapter 14 of Ma'achalot Asurot — if so one should be liable to lashes for their benefit. And the Rambam stated anonymously "every food," etc. Therefore it appears to me that the Rambam, according to his reason, holds that anything that comes from a verse amplification and a midrash one does not receive lashes for it, as the Migdal Oz wrote in several places. And therefore in every food forbidden in benefit one does not receive lashes for its benefit, for its benefit is not written explicitly but from the amplification of "you shall not eat," etc. — one prohibition of eating and one prohibition of benefit are implied, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 8 of Ma'achalot Asurot. It turns out the principal verse came for the prohibition of eating. And therefore one does not receive lashes for its benefit, as the Migdal Oz wrote in chapter 3 of Ma'achalot Asurot regarding eggs of impure fowl — see there. And regarding basar b'chalav we heard the prohibition of its benefit: if it is not fit for cooking and eating, make it fit for benefit, and therefore he is not lashed. And that which is so even though in the Yerushalmi it states that one is lashed, as written in Sefer She'eilot uTeshuvot — one can say that in our Talmud in Chullin daf 115 it does not imply so, as written in Magen Avraham. And this resolves what Tosafot wrote that one who derives benefit should be in karet. And perhaps they follow their reason, for Rashi on Avodah Zarah wrote that one is lashed when it is not fit for the context — investigate. And with this what the author of Peri Megadim in his book Shoshanet HaAmakim general rule 9 wrote is rejected — that for one who forbids benefit as well one should be lashed for benefit, because since there is no noten taam, its benefit is manner of benefit. And again, after I wrote, I saw that the Rav author of Lechem Mishneh likewise wrote in the reason of the Rambam that he did not write that one is lashed for benefit in basar b'chalav.

And one can ask: from the Rambam it is proven that even not in the manner of benefit one would be lashed — only he is not lashed, but the prohibition exists. And likewise in Hagahot Maimoniyot explicitly there in chapter 14 of Ma'achalot Asurot, and thus it states explicitly in Tosafot in Niddah (daf 32) s.v. vekhasheman, etc. And in Sefer Sha'ar HaMelekh he brought proof for this from the mishnah "one may not kindle with it an oven and stoves," even though it is chametz not in the manner of benefit. (However, one can say this is different because it is not fit for eating, and since he did not designate it for manner of benefit, kindling is also like manner of benefit — and this reasoning is in Tzelach.) And these in chapter Kol Sha'ah (daf 26) regarding "the Sanctuary is different," for that which is made for its interior proves that even if it is rabbinic there is none; for if it were worth even a rabbinic prohibition it would be a prohibition for Rava there when he intends even though it is impossible, as Tosafot wrote there nearby. And one can say according to what is written in Sefer She'eilot uTeshuvot that it deals with benefit of the shadow of the Sanctuary, and a shadow has no substance of sacrilege from the Torah because there is no substance in it. And if so it is two rabbinic prohibitions. Or it is one rabbinic prohibition and it works out well that there it was a need of mitzvah in order to expound. And similarly they resolved Tosafot there regarding the matter of lowering the artisans.

But it is still difficult, for the Rambam holds there is a Torah prohibition in kodshim, and certainly one fulfills the positive commandment of eating kodshim raw, as proven in Pesachim (daf 70) and in Menachot end of chapter 1, and nevertheless it appears in Zevachim that the mitzvah of eating overrides a negative commandment if not for positive and negative, etc., and we do not say it is possible to fulfill both that they eat raw. And although there is a place for two rabbinic prohibitions, as found in Eruvin (daf 103b), nevertheless we learn that there is a Torah prohibition not in the manner of benefit. And this is not like what is written in Sefer She'eilot uTeshuvot that the positive commandment does not emerge from a negative commandment not in the manner of benefit. And with this the matter in Zevachim is resolved. And the refutation is: behold there certainly one emerges through raw. And perforce, as stated, there is a Torah prohibition not in the manner of benefit. And also what he brought proof from Tosafot in Kiddushin (daf 38) — and also the author of Peri Megadim in Shoshanet HaAmakim — the refutation is: behold it should have emerged with matzah smooth — except as stated. And I elaborated on this in Magen Avraham. And in Sefer Machaneh Levi, son of the author of Hafla'ah, he brought proof from Yerushalmi in Shevuot (daf 24), and already likewise in Sefer She'eilot uTeshuvot. But it is still difficult, for still it is possible to fulfill both — namely that he eat half an olive-size not in the manner of benefit. According to what is written in the aforementioned book, that even if we say there is a Torah prohibition not in the manner of benefit, that is in the requisite measure, but in less than the requisite measure certainly there is none, for it is not fit to combine. And he further wrote there that similarly in mixtures more than the eating of a Persian in thirty-eight parts for combination — and it is not forbidden from the Torah except regarding milk there is a verse so that it is forbidden even without thirty-eight for combination. And this accords with the Gemara in Chullin (daf 98a). But this is not necessary, for one can say that even though they do not combine for lashes, for prohibition there is combination. And regarding matzah it is possible that even one eats one-eighteenth matzah — that is because he is not permitted to eat raw; for since he emerges with this one-eighteenth, behold he also accepted upon himself the prohibition of raw. And similarly wrote Machaneh Levi in Hilchot Shevuot.

(There) And he derived the prohibition of eating in the language of cooking, etc. And what he wrote — the prohibition of benefit in the language of cooking — according to the Rambam's explanation that one is not lashed when it is not fit for the context — it works out well. And according to Tosafot's explanation one can say it teaches us that there is cooking after cooking in basar b'chalav, as written in siman 94. And know that Kerem Shlomo wrote in siman 92 that in cooking one is liable immediately — only in eating: if they ate before it was cooked, like food of ben drusai, he is exempt, as in the Tosefta that it is not in the manner of benefit, for he holds that in basar b'chalav too, not in the manner of benefit, one is exempt. And he relied on what is stated in Tosafot in Chullin: how much must he eat to be liable — enough that it would be eaten on account of cooking — and he did not ask how much must he cook; learn from this that in cooking one is liable immediately. And it escaped me the Tosefta of lashes that is stated there: how much must it be cooked. Nevertheless, according to his view, the difficulty of Machaneh Levi in Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah is resolved — he left the Rambam's words as requiring study, who wrote there in chapter 5 halacha 8: likewise in basar b'chalav they are forbidden even not in the manner of benefit — it implies the prohibition exists but lashes do not; if so, perforce the Rambam explains what Abaye said in chapter Kol Sha'ah (daf 24): all agree in such a case that one would receive lashes for them, for not in the manner of benefit refers to eating, for in benefit there are no lashes. And since it deals with eating, it should have said: all agree in basar b'chalav that one would receive lashes for eating them not in the manner of benefit — end of his words. And according to Kerem Shlomo's words it works out well — the view of our great rabbi follows his approach, for he omitted in chapter 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot that baraita in chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 108b) regarding cooking in basar b'chalav: in what cooking did they speak — in cooking that others eat it on account of its cooking. And he stated anonymously and did not give a measure for cooking in basar b'chalav — it implies he is liable even in cooking that is not fit for eating. And Kerem Shlomo explained his reason: he explains that the tanna of that baraita in chapter 25 who requires cooking fit for eating holds that also in basar b'chalav there is no Torah prohibition even in eating not in the manner of benefit. But according to what we rule, that in basar b'chalav one is liable for eating even not in the manner of benefit, one is also liable for eating and cooking that is not fit for eating. And according to this Abaye could not say: all agree in basar b'chalav that one would receive lashes for eating it not in the manner of benefit — for this tanna of chapter Kol HaBasar who requires a measure for cooking in basar b'chalav so that it be fit for eating — perforce he does not hold thus, as stated.

And according to this it works out well what Machazit HaShekel challenged on this, that he sought to derive in chapter Ein Ma'amidin that taste is like substance in all prohibitions from basar b'chalav, and did not challenge: what of basar b'chalav, for one receives lashes for them not in the manner of benefit — for it follows the view that does not hold thus. And what Machazit HaShekel further wrote regarding Pesach too — only through eating, from what is written "you shall not eat from it raw" — there is no understanding to his words, for since it is raw it is not the manner of eating, and nevertheless the Merciful One made it liable and called it eating; and likewise for cooked and the others written in that verse one is liable not in the manner of benefit. And what is written in Yad Malkei that he did not find "all agree in basar b'chalav" — because there is one who derives in Chullin (daf 115) from the verse "you shall not eat," etc. — the proofreader already noted this in Machaneh Levi in Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah and rejected this.

And wild animal meat resembles domestic animal meat, as stated in poskim siman 89 seif 2 regarding that which is stated in the Talmud (daf 104) that it teaches: a reward — fowl and cheese are eaten in apikomon, and they wrote that it specifically mentions fowl and not wild animal, whose meat resembles domestic animal meat.
