**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is not comparable to two shirts, and so forth.</b> At first glance these words are surprising, because the opposite seems true. With two shirts, one cannot fully remove the sleeve of the lower shirt before first removing the upper sleeve. With leggings, however, if they are broad enough, one can insert his hand underneath, raise the linen legging, and lower the wool one, so that they separate without undoing any stitching while both remain on his body. Levush already challenged Rama on this basis from the case of two shirts, where the lower cannot be removed without the upper. Levush therefore writes that no special caution is necessary and relies on Beit Yosef, who says the other poskim follow R. Yose and permit every case. In truth, however, Rama's words are correct. Whenever the lower garment cannot be removed without first removing the upper one, there is a prohibition. The Yerushalmi cited by Beit Yosef says that R. Chila concedes that one who wears woolen leggings over linen leggings is prohibited because one cannot remove the lower pair without removing the upper pair. Beit Yosef explains that the garments are narrow and tight so the upper cannot be removed without the lower, and therefore they are considered attached. This explanation is difficult. If that were the point, the Yerushalmi should have said it is forbidden when the leggings are tight; instead it states the prohibition generally and then gives the reason that one cannot remove the lower without the upper. Rather, the explanation is as Rama understood it: the lower cannot be removed without first removing the upper. The essential point is that whenever removal of the lower garment requires even beginning to remove the upper one, there is a prohibition. Ordinary leggings are fitted closely to the leg, with only a little extra room. The leggings Rama discusses have at their lower end a foot covering like woolen stockings. Consequently, the lower pair cannot be displaced while the upper pair remains in place. Two shirts differ. One can insert a hand into the sleeve of the lower shirt and pull that sleeve upward close to the body. He can then draw the body of the lower shirt backward and over his head. Only the part of the upper garment covering the same area must be shifted somewhat, and he can uncover a portion of the upper shirt in front so that the lower shirt is no longer covered there. But if leggings are made without any foot covering and one can insert a hand below and slide the lower pair upward until they are no longer covered by the upper pair, they too are permitted. This appears to me the proper explanation of Rama's ruling and why one must be careful with two pairs of leggings in the configuration described.
