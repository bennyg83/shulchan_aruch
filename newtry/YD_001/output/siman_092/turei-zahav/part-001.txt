<b>כזית בשר כו'. </b>ויש כאן ב' איסורין האחד מצד טעם בשר שמפליט להחלב תכף אחר הנפילה לשם ולזה מהני ששים בחלב לכ"ע ויש איסור אחר דהיינו החלב שבולע הבשר לתוכו חוזר ואוסר שאר החלב שביורה ובזה לא מהני ס' לרש"י דס"ל מין במינו במשהו ואיסור זה אינו שייך אלא אחר שתנוח היורה אז חוזר הבשר ומפליט החלב שבלע ולא קודם לזה אבל אנן קיימא לן כר"ת דאפי' מין במינו בטל בס' ע"כ גם לאחר שתנוח היורה מ"מ מהני ס' רק שהרמב"ם שהוא בעל דיעה זו ס"ל דאף בפחות מס' יש היתר ע"י טעימת קפילא ומש"ה אם לא קדם וסילקה צריך ס' לפי שאז בא האיסור מצד החלב שנאסרה בתוך הבשר ובא לשאר החלב וא"א להרגיש הטעם ואנו לא קי"ל כן אלא בכ"מ בעינן ס' נגד הבשר ואז שרי וכזית דנקט כאן לאו דוקא אלא ה"ה פחות מכזית רק לענין מלקות יש חילוק:
**** ENGLISH ****
A kezayit of meat, etc. There are here two prohibitions: one from the side of meat taste that the meat discharges to the milk immediately after the falling there — and therefore sixty in the milk helps according to all; and there is another prohibition, namely the milk that the meat absorbs into itself returns and forbids the rest of the milk in the cauldron — and in this sixty does not help according to Rashi who holds min b'mino in mashahu; and this prohibition applies only after the cauldron rests, then the meat returns and discharges the milk it absorbed, and not before; but we hold like Rabbenu Tam that even min b'mino is nullified at sixty — therefore also after the cauldron rests nevertheless sixty helps; only Rambam, who is the holder of this view, holds that even with less than sixty there is permission through tasting of kafeilah — and therefore if he did not precede and remove it, sixty is required because then the prohibition comes from the milk that became forbidden inside the meat and entered the rest of the milk, and the taste cannot be sensed; and we do not hold thus, but in every case we require sixty against the meat and then it is permitted; and the kezayit mentioned here is not precise, but the same applies to less than a kezayit — only regarding lashes there is a difference.

<b>לתוך קדירה של בשר. </b>זה מיירי שנפלה הטיפה על החתיכה אחת חוץ לרוטב דאי בתוך הרוטב ודאי כל מה שבקדירה מצטרף לבטל הטיפה וכתב הטור מחלוקת רש"י ור"י בזה דלר"י דוקא שהחתיכה כולה חוץ לרוטב כגון שחתיכה אחת קצתה חוץ לרוטב וזו מונחת עליה וע"כ נאסרה החתיכה לבדה אבל אם מקצתה ברוטב הרתיחה מערבת כל הקדירה והכל מצטרף לבטל החלב ולרש"י אפי' מקצתה ברוטב אין הכל מצטרף לבטל החלב ויש קולא לרש"י מצד אחד דהיינו אם מקצתה ברוטב וזו החתיכה נאסרת אין אוסרת השאר כ"כ בד"מ ולא נראה לע"ד דאע"פ שאין ברוטב דלמטה מסייע לבטל טיפת חלב שנפלה לפי שאין הרתיחה מגיע לשם מ"מ אותה חתיכה עצמה נאסרת חלק התחתון מחלק העליון וחוזרת ואוסרת למטה כל הרוטב ומה שבתוכו עד ששים נגד כל החתיכה דכל החתיכה נעשית נבילה כיון דמקצת החתיכה מונחת תוך הרוטב הרוטב מוליך לשאר ומדברי הש"ע האלו שהם דברי רמב"ם<i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>נ"ל דס"ל כרש"י מדכ' באם נאסרה החתיכה אוסרת השאר ע"כ נ"ל דהכי קי"ל אע"פ שרש"ל בא"ו שלו פסק כר"י מ"מ בספרו בפרק כ"ה סי' מ"ט החמיר כרש"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
Into a pot of meat. This deals when the drop fell on one piece outside the gravy — for if inside the gravy certainly everything in the pot joins to nullify the drop; and Tur wrote the dispute of Rashi and R' Yehuda: R' Yehuda holds only when the whole piece is outside the gravy, such as one piece has part outside the gravy and this rests on it — therefore that piece alone is forbidden; but if part is in the gravy, boiling mixes the whole pot and everything joins to nullify the milk; and Rashi holds even if part is in the gravy not everything joins to nullify the milk; and there is a leniency for Rashi on one side — namely if part is in the gravy and this piece is forbidden it does not forbid the rest, as Darkei Moshe wrote; and it does not appear to my humble opinion that although the gravy below does not help nullify the drop of milk that fell since boiling does not reach there, nevertheless that piece itself is forbidden — the lower part from the upper part — and returns and forbids all the gravy and what is in it until sixty against the whole piece, for the whole piece becomes nevelah since part of the piece rests in the gravy the gravy conveys to the rest; and from these words of Shulchan Aruch which are Rambam's words it appears to me he holds like Rashi since he wrote if the piece was forbidden it forbids the rest — therefore it appears to me this is how we hold, although Maharshal in his Orach Chaim ruled like R' Yehuda, nevertheless in his book ch. 25 siman 49 he was stringent like Rashi.

<b>נאסרה אותה חתיכה. </b>בת"ח כלל כ"ב כתב דדברי הטור אנו סותרים מ"ש בסי' ק"ה סעיף ד' דאם נפל איסור על החתיכה שבקדירה דאינו אוסר רק כדי נטילה ורש"ל תירץ דהכא מיירי באיסור צלול ושם מיירי באיסור עב שאינו מתפשט כ"כ. ולפי מה שכתבתי בסימן פ"ז ס"י דלדעת הרא"ש והטור הוה בחלב איסור שמן ומפעפע גם כאן ל"ק מידי דהא מבואר בסי' ק"ה החילוק בין כחוש לשמן וכן מצאתי אח"כ בד"מ סי' ק"ה שתירץ כן וכ' שזה דלא כרשב"א שמביא ב"י שם דחלב אינו מפעפע אבל בסי' ק"ה סעיף ז' מוכח שם דחלב לא מפעפע וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
That piece was forbidden. Terumat HaChacham general rule 22 wrote that Tur's words here contradict what he wrote in siman 105 seif 4 that if issur fell on the piece in the pot it only forbids kedei netilah; and Maharshal resolved that here it deals with a clear issur and there with a thick issur that does not spread as much. And per what I wrote in siman 87 seif 10 that according to Rosh and Tur milk is a fat issur and steams here too — there is no difficulty, for it is explained in siman 105 the distinction between weak and fat issur; and so I found afterward in Darkei Moshe siman 105 that he resolved thus, and wrote this is not like Rashba that Beit Yosef brings there that milk does not steam — but in siman 105 seif 7 it is proven there that milk does not steam — requires study.

<b>אבל אם ניער מתחלה ועד סוף. </b>לאו דוקא אלא בניער מיד סגי כמ"ש הטור בהדיא והקשה מהר"י ן' חביב דלר"י א"צ לניעור אלא שתהא מקצתה ברוטב <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="2"></i>ונ"ל דאה"נ דסגי אלא כיון שהקדירה מליאה וא"א להכניס היטב החתיכה שמונחת בחוץ על החתיכה אחרת שמקצתה ברוטב כמ"ש לעיל לר"י ע"כ צריך שינער הקדירה ויהיו עליונים למטה וכבר נתבאר דקי"ל כרש"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
But if he stirred from beginning to end. Not precise — rather if he stirred immediately it suffices, as Tur wrote explicitly; and Mahari ibn Chabib challenged that for R' Yehuda one need not stir but only that part be in the gravy; and it appears to me that here too it suffices, only since the pot is full and one cannot well insert the piece that rests outside on another piece that has part in the gravy, as I wrote above for R' Yehuda — therefore one must stir the pot so the upper become lower; and it is already explained that we hold like Rashi.

<b>וכן אם לא ניער כלל. </b>דברים אלו צ"ע<i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="3"></i>דהרי הם דעת הראב"ד בב"י וז"ל אם לא ניער כלל אין שאר חתיכות נאסרות אע"פ שאם לא ניער יוצא רוטב והוי נבילה מאות' חתיכ' לשא' חתיכו' מ"מ לא אסרי דבעי' יוצא החלב לשאר החתיכו' עכ"ל וא"כ קשה למה כ' רמ"א שצריך ס' בקדירה נגד החלב דהא אין החלב יוצא לשאר החתיכות כלל ואי יוצא הרי אוסר ויצטרך ס' בקדירה נגד כל החתיכה האסורה וצ"ע. ובאו"ה כלל כ"ח כתב ג"כ להתיר כשיש בכל הקדרה ס' נגד הטיפה שנפלה על חתיכה שמקצתה חוץ לרוטב משום דס"ל כר"י אלא שכתב שאז גם החתיכה חוזרת להיתר דלא כרמ"א כאן וצ"ל דרמ"א מחמיר מסברא דנפשיה לאסור אותה חתיכה אף לדעת ר"י וכן משמע מדבריו בסעיף ד' דפוסק כר"י במקצתה חוץ לרוטב ולקולא ותימא דהיה לו לרמ"א לכתוב זה כאן בשם י"א דהא הרמב"ם שהוא דעה ראשונה ס"ל כרש"י שצריך ס' נגד כל החתיכה כמו שכתבתי בסמוך ולשון רמ"א משמע שלא בא לחלוק אלא להוסיף ותו קשה דבסעיף ד' מסיים רמ"א דבבשר וחלב יש להחמיר וכאן פסק להקל ומיירי בבשר בחלב:
**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise if he did not stir at all. These matters require study — behold they are Raavad's view in Beit Yosef, and these are his words: if he did not stir at all the other pieces are not forbidden, although if he did not stir gravy goes out and becomes nevelah from that piece to another piece — nevertheless they are not forbidden, for the milk must go out to the other pieces — end of his words; and if so it is hard why Rama wrote that sixty is required in the pot against the milk, for the milk does not go out to the other pieces at all; and if it does go out, behold it forbids and one would need sixty in the pot against the whole forbidden piece — requires study. And in Orach Chaim general rule 28 he also wrote to permit when there is sixty in the whole pot against the drop that fell on a piece that has part outside the gravy, because he holds like R' Yehuda — only he wrote that then the piece also returns to permission, unlike Rama here; and one must say Rama is stringent from his own reasoning to forbid that piece even per R' Yehuda, and so it appears from his words in seif 4 that he rules like R' Yehuda where part is outside the gravy, for leniency; and one might say Rama should have written this here in language of "some say," for Rambam who is the first view holds like Rashi that sixty is required against the whole piece, as I wrote below; and Rama's language implies he did not come to dispute but to add; and further it is hard that in seif 4 Rama concludes in basar b'chalav one should be stringent and here he ruled leniently, and it deals with basar b'chalav.

Until near the gravy, etc. Per Beit Yosef's explanation it should be that the more it steams the more permission — for there is much moisture in that place of the drop's falling and its spread — requires much study why he said so much that there is not sixty against the drop, rather he should have said perhaps it only steams a little and there is not sixty in that place; and further such language implies he wants to enlarge the measure and afterward said there is not sixty there, implying he comes to diminish it. And one must say that all views Tur brings do not hold that moisture inside the vessel can nullify the drop, for who knows how much moisture is there, as we say in siman 98 that we estimate in the whole pot for we do not know how much discharges from it — so too here; and although Beit Yosef brings in name of Maharam in Mordechai: since it does not spread in all its walls so that one could say all moisture in the walls helps nullify — for ease of expression he wrote thus that even if we knew the measure of moisture there to nullify, nevertheless it would not help, for moisture does not steam at all; and therefore in this plain explanation the more it spreads the more it forbids that place and is not nullified at all; and the continuation of Tur's words is thus: at first he wrote there is issur in the drop in all its surroundings, and afterward explains how far the issur reaches — Maharam wrote if there is sixty times sixty it helps, from which two stringencies and one leniency follow: one stringency that even if it fell opposite the food we do not say the food nullifies it; a second stringency that the pot remains forbidden, for this matter is like a nevelah piece that fell into a pot and was nullified and you recognize it — certainly it remains in its issur; so too in this place where issur spread it becomes actual nevelah forever. And one leniency that one may pour the gravy immediately from the pot while hot since there is sixty in the food against the whole issur. And Sefer HaMitzvot disputes and holds the reverse: namely he holds there is doubt whether the drop steams, or we say it does not move from its place at all — not as Beit Yosef wrote that there is doubt how far steaming reaches, whether in all or in part, for the doubt in the Gemara ch. Dam Chatat is not in this way but if steaming goes from the chatat dish outside its place in the rest of the vessel and forbids it — so too here; and therefore he wrote here if it fell opposite the food it is permitted either way: if it steams in all of it — meaning the drop steams from its place to another place, and he used language "in the whole vessel" following Talmudic language — but in truth he did not intend here except if it has steaming at all outside its place, behold it is nullified through the food, for since it has steaming it enters inward into the food and the food nullifies it; and if it does not steam at all but stands in its place, there is no issur at all since it does not spread from its place — it does not enter inward; and therefore Tur did not need to bring this distinction, for it is simple; and Beit Yosef's words are not difficult, and one enters to resolve as explained in his words; and behold per this the pot is also permitted: if it steams inward it is as if it fell into the food itself, and then the pot is also permitted, for we consider the pot wall as part of the food itself; and if it does not steam, the food cooked in it afterward should not be forbidden — why is the first different; and afterward he said if it fell opposite the empty place, a large part of the pot is forbidden, lest it steam a large part, in any case not until the gravy, for otherwise it would be like falling opposite the food that we mentioned, since here too there is connection of the drop with the food; and you might still say there is permission for food that has sixty against that part of the pot that was forbidden — therefore he said perhaps a very large part spread so there is not sixty in the food against that part — therefore forbidden to pour through there, for in this part of the pot the issur remains forever and nullification does not help; and one may still challenge why he needed to say until near the gravy — this closeness is unnecessary, he should have said it steams outside the gravy, for the main intent is that it not connect to the gravy; one may say also because he wants to rule there is no remedy except to leave it until it cools, and no permission to pour from the other side where issur did not spread, or pierce from the bottom of the vessel as other poskim mention — therefore he said there is concern lest steaming stopped near the gravy and if the vessel moves from its place it will reach the forbidden place — therefore leave until it cools specifically; and what he said so much that there is not sixty against the drop — he does not speak of the drop alone but with its spread — and this is clear.

**** ENGLISH ****
Like two pots, etc. From this reason it appears to me to permit if they baked in a milk panada oven and afterward in that place while boiling they placed a pot with meat inside, for as long as there is no milk in appearance in that place in the oven it is like a milk pot and two pots do not forbid each other.

But milk of yours, etc. Meaning from its mouth in the place where the wick burns — that side is considered through the fire itself; so Hagahot Sh"D in name of Maharai — automatically if it dripped from another side of the vessel where the wick is inside, it is not called boiling to forbid what drips on it; and greater than this he wrote there in the hagahah in name of Maharam: if a hot drop of milk fell on a knife or other cold vessel, one needs only scraping where the drop fell, since the drop ceased at the time of falling and did not come in a splash; and specifically when it falls on wood, but if it fell on iron stab ten times in the ground and enough — end of his words; and he distinguishes there between this law of Maharam, which is hot from boiling by fire, and the law of "milk of yours" above, which is from the fire itself — so is the explanation of this matter. Written in Maharil Laws of Pesach: one who purifies by iruy who pours boiling water on the vessel to purify it should beware to pour the boiling water specifically from the vessel in which it was cooked, which is called kli rishon, and not draw from the vessel containing the boiling water with another vessel and pour from it to purify, because the drawing vessel with it is kli sheini and the boiling water was nullified in cooking before he purifies with it; however if he leaves the drawing vessel within the vessel in which they cook the boiling water, and while it is there raises boiling water — it is properly called also kli rishon — end of his words. From these words it implies that if one draws from kli rishon and removes boiling water, it is afterward called kli sheini and has the law of iruy; and I wondered greatly at this, for behold while still in kli rishon the vessel one inserts in it is also kli rishon, for we purify vessels in kli rishon and discharge from them what is absorbed; and so we hold regarding inserting a forbidden spoon into a pot that forbids the spoon even if he removes the spoon from it — and how can one say here that when he removes the drawing vessel from kli rishon it becomes kli sheini because kli rishon name came upon it; who gives it another name afterward — behold kli rishon is its name even if removed from the fire, so too what was removed from boiling water inside kli rishon. And further, if he waited there a bit, Maharil concedes the name kli rishon remains — and if so who measures how long the waiting should be, as Tur wrote in Orach Chaim siman 452: Rif wrote one leaves vessels in the yorah until they discharge — and Rabbenu Avraham Azriel wrote who can measure, etc. — so too we may say here who can measure the waiting in this. And further proof from ch. Kirah: R' Shmuel bar Avdimi said: I entered after Rabbi to the bathhouse on Shabbat, etc., and he told him take with a kli sheini so the heat lessens; and do not err and say he means take with kli sheini from the bath which is kli rishon and we learn the drawing vessel is kli sheini — this is not so, for then he should have said simply take a vessel and give; and further it is clarified that what he said kli sheini refers to take — meaning at the time of taking it should be kli sheini; and certainly one cannot say thus, for at the time of taking from boiling water it is kli rishon since it is inside it; and also he told him it should be kli sheini after it leaves the water, for he said take kli sheini — learn he spoke at the time of taking and not about removing afterward; and further when he removed it, who put kli sheini name on it — can every kli rishon after it cools become kli sheini? And further Tosafot wrote there the main distinction between kli rishon and kli sheini is that kli sheini cools because its walls are not hot — therefore it goes on cooling; and this certainly has walls boiling, for they were heated in kli rishon's boiling water — only a simple matter: Rabbi said take from the water with some object and put it in a kli sheini — meaning pour it into a kli sheini like every kli sheini in the Talmud; and know that if there were novelty here that kli rishon becomes kli sheini, he would have stated this novelty where we learn from it that kli sheini does not cook well; also Tur in Orach Chaim siman 318 when he brought this law of take kli sheini, etc., wrote only if it was hot in kli sheini one may put into it, etc., and did not write that one draws from kli rishon and nevertheless puts into it — rather plainly this is not so; and explicit proof further from what Agudah brought in Orach Chaim general rule 60: a fly found in a bowl is permitted, for it is a double doubt: perhaps it fell in kli sheini, and even if you say kli rishon, perhaps when they removed the fly — namely in the spoon — there was sixty in the spoon — end of his words; and likewise regarding removing a forbidden egg in a spoon, and so Shach and Tur regarding removing a fly in a spoon siman 107 — behold they required sixty in the gravy in the spoon against the fly, meaning the spoon is considered kli rishon even after removing from the pot, for all the time it was in the pot there is no issur from the fly because there was sixty there, only after removing it in the spoon it forbids; and on this they said perhaps there was sixty in the spoon — learn the spoon still has kli rishon name even after removing; and so proven from Rama's ruling in siman 107: but if a little of the food was with the issur in the spoon and returned to the pot, sixty is needed also against the little food, for it became nevelah in the spoon — learn the spoon is kli rishon in what it draws from the pot and forbids the gravy in it until sixty. And I again saw in Terumat HaDeshen siman 185 regarding absorption inside the spoon becoming nevelah: one may say after removing the spoon from the boiling gravy, all absorption inside it is considered like kli sheini and neither discharged nor absorbed — all the more per the view that absorbed issur does not forbid absorbed heter; it appears to reconcile this with what I wrote: there he removes an empty spoon from the pot and there is only what is absorbed in it — in this we certainly say there is not so much heat in an empty vessel to discharge and absorb in what is absorbed in the absorption; and even in actual kli rishon removed from fire and emptied it appears to say thus — not so when there is still boiling gravy or food inside, certainly the spoon has kli rishon name like the pot; otherwise certainly there is no reason Terumat HaDeshen would dispute Shach, Tur, and Agudah I cited — rather certainly as I wrote. And I again saw in Tosafot ch. Am MiMazikin daf 33 s.v. kinisa: R' Yehuda would say it is a mitzvah min hamuvchar to purify each board alone and throw on it from that vessel itself that is boiling on the fire and that suffices, although boiling ceased when moving it from the fire; and some place a bowl inside a pan on the fire until boiling rises in the bowl and from that bowl they purify — and in this R' Yehuda is uncertain whether this helps as kli rishon or not, and left the matter to be stringent and forbade purifying with them, considering them kli sheini; and for melichah of chicken too it is forbidden, considering it kli rishon — end of his words. Behold Tosafot were uncertain here and raised to stringency; except Rosh there wrote in name of R' Yehuda that it is like kli rishon since he removed it from the yorah boiling; and so Tur in Yoreh De'ah siman 131 in name of R' Yehuda; and one must say they had a different text in Tosafot; and from Rama's words per Hagahot Maimoniyot in Orach Chaim siman 451 it implies he ruled stringently that it is not kli rishon, for he wrote there: and pour on them boiling water from kli rishon, etc. — therefore it appears to be stringent like Tosafot's words.
