(סימן צ"ב סעיף ב' בהג"ה) <b>טיפת חלב שנפלה על חתיכה שבקדרה שהיא חוץ לרוטב כו'.</b> אם לא ניער כלל אינו אסור רק החתיכה לבד. כ"כ הב"י בשם הראב"ד והכי מוכח להדיא בתוס' והרא"ש פג"ה (דף ק') גבי הא דאמר חתיכה של נבילה ושל דג טמא אינה אוסרת עד שתתן טעם ברוטב כו'. וכתבו דמיירי שחתיכת היתר מונחת כולה חוץ לרוטב ועליה מונחת חתיכת נבילה ונאסרה רק אותה חתיכה. ואח"כ כשמנער הקדירה נאסרה כולה. עכ"ד. מוכח להדיא דבלא ניער לא היתה נאסרה רק אותה חתיכה. ובד"מ מסופק אם זה דוקא לר"י דמיירי כשהחתיכה כולה חוץ לרוטב אבל לרש"י דמיירי שהחתיכה שנפלה עליה הטיפה מקצתה ברוטב נאסרו שאר חתיכות אף בלא ניעור כלל. ודקדק מדברי הטור סי' זה דקדק קלוש דה"ה לרש"י בלא ניער כלל לא נאסרו שאר חתיכות שבקדירה. והט"ז כתב בפשיטות דלרש"י דמיירי שמקצתה ברוטב נאסרו שאר החתיכות אף בלי ניער. ונעלם מהם דברי הרשב"א בתה"ק והט"ו סי' ק"ה ס"ד ומבואר מדבריהם להדיא דה"ה לרש"י שכתבו וז"ל אם נפל איסור על חתיכה שבקדירה שהיא חוץ לרוטב ולא ניער הקדירה אינו אוסר אלא כדי נטילה. אבל אם היא ברוטב לרש"י כולה ולר"י אפי' מקצתה. הרוטב מפעפע הטעם ונכנס בכולו עכ"ל. הרי מבואר דאלו לרש"י מקצתה אין הרוטב מפעפע בלי ניעור והיינו אפי' אם היא ברוטב דהיינו אפי' מאותה נטילה הנאסר רק שפניה העליונים מגולים וכמ"ש הב"י ודו"ק היטב. ועיין ברשב"א המובא ביתה יוסף סי' ס"ו. והכי נלע"ד מוכח בפרש"י פ' כה"ב (דף ק"ח סוף ע"ב) שפירש אהא דאמר בש"ס שם אי לא ניער כלל ולא כיסה כלל מיבלע בלע מיפלט לא פליט. פרש"י החלב נבלע בחתיכה אבל אין נפלט ממנה לאסור שאר החתיכות בלא ניעור. שהחלב בגגה של חתיכה הוה עכ"ל. משמע דמיירי שהחתיכה תוך הרוטב רק שגגה דהיינו פניה העליונים מגולים ששם נפלה הטיפה ושם היא עומדת ואינה מתפשטת ממנה לחוץ בלא ניעור. וכ"כ הג"ה מיי' ומביאו הב"י וז"ל מתוך פרש"י משמע כו' וכמו שפירש הב"י דבריו. עיי"ש. ונ"ל שמדברי רש"י אלו הוציא כן. דה"ה לענין הצטרפות שאר החתיכות לבטל הטיפה שאין מצטרפין אפי' היא ברוטב אם פניה העליונים מגולים. דכל שכן הוא דאם אינו אוסר שאר החתיכות כ"ש שאין מצטרפין לבטל. ועיין ש"ך ס"ק ו' וס"ק י"ג:

(Siman 92 seif 2 in the hagahah) A drop of milk that fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy, etc. If he did not stir at all, only that piece is forbidden — so Beit Yosef in name of Raavad, and so is proven explicitly in Tosafot and Rosh in ch. Gid HaNasheh (daf 100) regarding that which he said: a piece of nevelah and of unclean fish do not forbid until they impart taste in the gravy, etc. — and they wrote it deals when the permitted piece is placed entirely outside the gravy and on it is placed the nevelah piece, and only that piece became forbidden; and afterward when he stirred the pot, the whole became forbidden — end of his words. It is proven explicitly that without stirring only that piece was forbidden. And in Darkei Moshe he is uncertain whether this is specifically for R' Yehuda, which deals when the piece is entirely outside the gravy; but for Rashi, which deals when the piece on which the drop fell is partly in the gravy — other pieces are forbidden even without stirring at all. And he inferred from Tur's words in this siman a weak inference that the same applies to Rashi — without stirring at all other pieces in the pot are not forbidden. And Taz wrote explicitly that for Rashi, which deals when part is in the gravy, other pieces are forbidden even without stirring. And it was hidden from them Rashba's words in Torat HaKahanim and Taz siman 105 seif 4, and it is explained from their words explicitly that the same applies to Rashi who wrote, and these are his words: if issur fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy and he did not stir the pot, it only forbids kedei netilah; but if it is in the gravy — for Rashi entirely, and for R' Yehuda even part of it — the gravy steams the taste and it enters throughout — end of his words. Behold it is explained that for Rashi, if part of it — the gravy does not steam without stirring; and this is even if it is in the gravy, meaning even from that netilah that was forbidden — only its upper face is exposed, as Beit Yosef wrote — investigate well. And see Rashba brought in Beit Yosef siman 66. And so it appears to me proven in Rashi's explanation in ch. Kol HaBasar (daf 108 end of side 2) who explained on that which is said in the Gemara there: if he did not stir at all and did not cover at all — absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge. Rashi: the milk is absorbed in the piece but is not discharged from it to forbid other pieces without stirring — for the milk is on the roof of the piece — end of his words. It implies it deals when the piece is inside the gravy, only that its roof, meaning its upper face is exposed, where the drop fell and where it stands, and it does not spread from it outward without stirring. And so Hagahot Maimoniyot, brought by Beit Yosef, and these are his words: from Rashi's explanation it appears, etc., and as Beit Yosef explained his words — see there. And it appears to me that from these words of Rashi he derived thus: the same applies regarding combination of other pieces to nullify the drop — they do not combine even if it is in the gravy if its upper face is exposed; for all the more so if it does not forbid other pieces, it does not combine to nullify. And see Shach s.k. 6 and s.k. 13.

(There in Shulchan Aruch) And likewise if it fell into the gravy, etc. This is Rambam's language. And Tur wondered at him: why need to stir when it fell into the gravy? And it appears to me Rambam deals with thick gravy, as we establish the mishnah in the Gemara in ch. Kol HaBasar (daf 108) for Rav who holds min b'mino is not nullified; and that the braita does not challenge is because he did not hear it, as Tosafot wrote there — and this answer is primary, as written in chiddushim in Sha'ar HaTevunah. And it appears this is R' Yehuda's reason in the Gemara there (daf 109) who holds that even if he stirred immediately, other pieces do not combine to nullify the drop — for he is concerned lest he did not stir well, and that piece on which the drop fell became nevelah and forbids all the pieces because they are its species; for R' Yehuda holds min b'mino is not nullified, and they did not explain why he is concerned lest he did not stir well. And in my humble opinion necessarily R' Yehuda deals with thick gravy, as we establish for Rav there — for if thin gravy, it would be not its species relative to meat, and we say it removed its species, etc., and min in not its species is great upon it and nullifies it — and why does R' Yehuda say it forbids all the pieces because they are its species? If so, since he deals with thick gravy, therefore he is concerned lest he did not stir well well, since it is thick. If so, similar to this the Sages of R' Yehuda also deal with this, and hold stirring helps, for they are not concerned lest he did not stir well; however stirring certainly is required since the gravy is thick — investigate. And it appears to me clear and correct in Sha'ar HaTevunah. And see Beit Yosef who challenged Rambam who wrote "he stirred the pot" — behold we do not nullify issur l'chatchila. And one may say he holds this is not nullification, for he only mixes; and this reasoning Tosafot wrote in ch. 4 of Terumot mishnah 8, and Peri Chadash brought in Orach Chaim siman 453. And greater still Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman Shelah wrote: if his intent is not to eat the issur but only to save the heter through its nullification, it is not called nullifying issur; and if so here too his intent is not to eat the drop of milk but only that the whole meat not become forbidden — and such a case I found in responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg siman 190 — see there. And I saw Peri Megadim here and in Orach Chaim challenged Peri Chadash who brought proof from the mishnah there, which deals only for R' Yehuda and we do not hold like him, as Rambam wrote; and so he challenged in responsum of R' Akiva Eiger siman 77 and did not mention Peri Megadim preceded him. And further he challenged: if so that grinding is permitted since his intent is only to fix — why are basar b'chalav not nullified — slaughter them in order to eat them and they will be nullified by themselves? And so with pomegranates — one may wound them for fixing food — what difference between grinding wheat and wounding pomegranates? And one may say grinding is forbidden since everything mixed together — it is as intending to nullify. And in the end one may say wheat is different, that he grinds everything together — if so there is also permission here. Not so with slaughter of basar b'chalav and wounding pomegranates, where the way is to do one by one, and on each one there is doubt lest it is the issur — his intent is not to fix. Rather he challenged one difficulty: what the Yerushalmi implies, that we only permit to permit regarding meduma because the priests will eventually grind them — and why permit to grind wheat that has shoots? Requires study. And one must say: the reason is still permission in any case. And accordingly there is no contradiction to our master's words here, that he mixes the whole pot together before it imparts taste — it is literally like grinding wheat — requires study. And further I have to say the Yerushalmi that implies forbidding grinding wheat in other issurim is because he considers it intent to nullify, since it is pikuach nefesh. And this is only for the view that nullifying issur is from the Torah forbidden; but per what we hold it is only d'rabbanan — he is not considered as having intent due to pikuach nefesh, for we permit in d'rabbanan.

(סעיף ד' בהג"ה) <b>דאמרי' בכל האיסורין חתיכה עצמה נעשית נבילה.</b> מבואר בר"ן ס"פ כל הבשר דאינו אלא מדרבנן משום גזירה דבב"ח ע"ש וכ"כ האחרונים. ונ"ל דכזית בכדי אכילת פרס דלר"ת היה לוקה על כל כזית מהתערובות דנהפך כל ההיתר להיות איסור. כמ"ש הרא"ש פג"ה והטי"ד סי' צ"ח. א"כ ע"כ חתיכה נ"נ מדאורייתא. ומוכח להדיא בתוס' והרא"ש והר"ן פג"ה (דף ק') והמרדכי שם סי' תר"פ דאפילו לר' אפרים שפסקו הט"ו דלא אמרי' חנ"נ בשאר איסורין רק בב"ח. מ"מ בכזית בכדי א"פ מודה. שהרי כתבו דמש"ה בב"ח ס"ל לר"א חנ"נ. וצריך לשער נגד כל החתיכה שנפל עליה הטיפה ולא סגי בביטול טיפה לחוד. לפי שבב"ח נעשה כל התערובות איסור דלוקה על חצי כזית בשר וחצי כזית חלב כדאי' בש"ס ע"ש מוכח להדיא דה"ה נמי בכל שאר האיסורין היכא דנעשה כל התערובות איסור ולוקה על כל כזית ממנו דהיינו כדי א"פ אמרי' שפיר חנ"נ וצריך לשער ששים נגד כל התערובת לכ"ע וזה ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
(Seif 4 in the hagahah) That we say in all issurim chatichah na'asah nevelah. It is explained in Ran end of ch. Kol HaBasar that it is only rabbinic due to the gezeirah of basar b'chalav — see there — and so the Acharonim. And it appears to me that a kezayit within the time to eat a peras — per Rabbenu Tam one would be lashed for every kezayit of the mixture, for all the permitted became issur. As Rosh wrote ch. 3 and Taz siman 98. If so, necessarily chatichah na'asah nevelah is d'oraisa. And it is proven explicitly in Tosafot and Rosh and Ran ch. 3 (daf 100) and Mordechai there siman 448 — that even for R' Efraim, whom Taz ruled we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim except basar b'chalav — nevertheless for a kezayit within the time to eat a peras he concedes. For they wrote that therefore in basar b'chalav R' Eliezer holds chatichah na'asah nevelah. And one must estimate against the whole piece on which the drop fell, and it does not suffice to nullify the drop alone. For in basar b'chalav the whole mixture becomes issur, for one is lashed on half a kezayit of meat and half a kezayit of milk as stated in the Gemara — see there — it is proven explicitly that the same applies in all other issurim where the whole mixture becomes issur and one is lashed on every kezayit from it — namely within the time to eat a peras — we properly say chatichah na'asah nevelah and need to estimate sixty against the whole mixture for all views. And this is clear.
