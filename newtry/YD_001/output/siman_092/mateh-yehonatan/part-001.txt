(Siman 92 seif 1) A kezayit of meat, etc. From that piece is forbidden — that which the Mechaber ruled without specification that that piece is forbidden implies even if kafeilah tastes it and says there is no milk taste, nevertheless it is forbidden — and why? And one may answer: we are concerned lest at first there was milk taste in it and that piece was already forbidden, and afterward it discharged from it so that now there is no taste at all — nevertheless, since that piece was already forbidden, it does not return to permission, for this deals when he preceded and removed before it discharges. If so, necessarily there was no taste in it, for if so where did the taste in it go — it does not discharge anything before it rests from its boiling. And one cannot say that it does not discharge full taste but nevertheless discharges weak taste. If so, one may answer: perhaps at first there was full taste and now full taste discharged from it and weak taste remained in it, and therefore its taste is not sensed. If so, it is difficult per our words regarding that which in ch. 25 daf 108 side 1 Rav — a kezayit of meat fell into a cauldron of milk, etc., and challenges: it is nevelah milk, and answers when he preceded and removed — absorbs but does not discharge. And per our words it is still difficult: since there was milk inside the meat, if so even if it only discharges weak taste, why does it not forbid the milk in the cauldron? And one must say: since there is only weak taste, again there is no importance of its species here and it is only like not its species and nullified in sixty. And one may answer: nevertheless it is still difficult regarding that which he wrote that if he removed before it rests from boiling we rely on kafeilah's tasting, and it is impossible that weak taste discharges even before it rests from boiling — why is sixty not required; let us be concerned lest weak taste discharged from it, and on weak taste kafeilah does not sense through tasting. And further, both are milk and one cannot recognize the taste. And one must say: certainly only a slight weak taste discharges from it. And that piece is forbidden is because we are concerned lest that piece received milk taste at first — namely congealed such that when a slight taste is removed from it again its taste is not sensed. If so, we are concerned here lest this slight weak thing discharged from it, and for this reason its taste is not sensed — and if so that piece is properly forbidden. Although Rashi explains on the aforementioned daf side 1 that Rav's statement "since noten ta'am in the piece chatichah na'asah nevelah" refers to the mishnah, and the mishnah deals when part of the piece was in the gravy and part outside the gravy — even though he did not stir and cover at the end, nevertheless we forbid the whole piece, and this is because even without stirring and covering, since part is in the gravy, afterward without stirring and covering it discharges from it. And that they do not combine immediately with the gravy is from this piece, since it is not entirely in the gravy but absorbed in that whole piece before it came to the gravy — but afterward it discharged from it to the gravy and forbids the other pieces. And it is clear from the plain meaning that a drop of milk that fell — if there is noten ta'am — forbids even without stirring at all — in the latter part it teaches stirring and covering, implying in the first part it forbids without stirring and covering; and so it appears Taz explains there — and it appears that if there is not in it, permitted even that piece alone, and why be concerned lest there was taste in that piece and now discharged outside as above. Granted if we explain as Shach wrote that if part in gravy and part outside it does not discharge at all to the gravy except through stirring and covering — if so necessarily the mishnah of the milk drop deals that he stirred and covered at the end — if so one may permit the piece's taste before stirring and there was no taste in it. Not so per Taz's explanation of Rashi that the mishnah deals without stirring and covering, and there is no place to forbid the piece's taste — if so it is difficult, etc. And one must say Rashi follows his view that we do not rely on kafeilah except where there is sixty. If so that which implies in the mishnah "if there is noten ta'am" permitted — it follows there is not sixty in that piece either. If so we say: certainly if kafeilah tasted and said there is no taste, since we already hold there is sixty in it, stam there was never any taste at all and it is permitted even that piece, and we do not say in it that there was taste and afterward discharged. And if so for us who hold by law we rely on kafeilah even when there is no sixty — we do not need Rashi's explanation, for otherwise it would be difficult: if there is no taste in it, why should that piece be permitted? If so one should wonder somewhat at Maharshal and his supporters who ruled like Rashi, since they hold kafeilah's tasting without sixty — it was not for them to rule like him, for otherwise the difficulty as stated applies.

(שם) <b>אבל אם לא הספיק כו' אסור אא"כ יש בו ס'</b>. י"ל לפ"ז על הא דתניא בחולין צ"ד קדירה שבשל בה בשר לא יבשל בה חלב ואם בשל בנ"ט ואמרינן מאן טעים קפילא טעים לי' ולפמ"ש המחבר אמאי סמכינן אקפילא נימא דנבלע חלב בקדירה ונ"נ וחזר ופליט ליה ואוסר השאר חלב והוי מב"מ עם שאר חלב ויצטרך ס' כי הכא וי"ל דהנה הר"נ מפרש הא דפריך על הא דאמר רב כזית בשר שנפל לתוך יורה של חלב כו' חלב נבילה הוא ומק' הר"ן אמאי נ"נ הא כיון דהאי חלב שנבלע עדיין מחובר הוא להאי חלב שהוא חוצה לה שהוא ס' נגד טעם בשר ונתבטל טעם בשר שקבל האי חלב שנבלע בו ותי' כיון דנבלע תוך הבשר מיד הוא נפרד משאר חלב ועומד בפ"ע ושפיר קבלה טעם בשר ונ"נ והנה לפ"מ דקיי"ל דאם נבלע איסור תוך מאכל דהאיסור א"א לסוחטו שאין האיסור יכול להפליט ממנו ובנבלע האיסור בכלי אמרי' דמהני הגעלה ושפיר יכול האיסור לפרוד ממנו והיינו ע"כ הטעם דבנבלע במאכל האיסור נדבק בו ונסרך ביותר ונבלע ממש בגוף עד שאין יכול לתת ריוח בין הדבקים לעולם משא"כ בנבלע בכלי שאין נדבק ונסרך כ"כ ולכן בהגעלה שפיר יכול לפרוד ממנו לפ"ז י"ל הא דאמרי' דאם נבלע דבר לתוך דבר אחר שנחשיב כנפרד מן הדבר שחוצה לה היינו אם נבלע לתוך המאכל כדלעיל והוי הכל בגוף א' ושפיר אמרי' דהוי כמפריד ונ"נ משא"כ הנדבק בכלי שאין נדבק כ"כ עם הכלי אמרי' שעדיין הוא מחובר במאכל שתוך הכלי ולא אמרינן כיון שנבלע שיהיה נ"נ לפ"ז לק"מ [וכ"כ הפמ"ג בר"ס צ"ד בש"ד האי חילוקא בשם המרדכי] דבשלמא הכא דמיירי שנבלע תוך הכלי אמרי' שנ"נ ושפיר צריך ס' משא"כ התם גבי קדירה שבישל בה כו' דכיון שנבלע בכלי לא אמרינן שיהיה כנפרד מן בשר ויהיה נ"נ ולא צריך ס' כלל לבטלו אך עדיין ק' הא דכ' המחבר לעיל ס"ס פ"ד דאם העמיד בעור הקיבה כשרה אם יש בה נ"ט אסורה משמע אין בה נ"ט מותר אפי' בדליכא ס' נימא דחלב הנבלע בעור של קיבה נ"נ וחזר ופליט ויהיה צריך ס' לבטל דהתם נמי נבלע במאכל וצ"ל כיון דאין נאסר רק ע"י כבוש ומן התורה אינו נאסר כלל בב"ח אלא ע"י בישול ס"ל להמחבר דל"א חענ"נ משום דס"ל כרבינו אפרים דל"א חנ"נ רק בבו"ח והיינו מטעם דכיון דהאי לחודא שרי וכו' רק ע"י שנתחברו יחד נעשה אסור הוי שפיר כ"א כגוף נבילה וכ"ז אם נאסר ע"י בישול דהוי כגוף איסור משא"כ ע"י כיבוש ומליחה אף שאסרו לאכול מ"מ ל"א חענ"נ שנחשב הכל כגוף א' רק נחשב כשאר איסורים. [הג"ה וכ"כ בחידושי הגרע"א ז"ל שס בס"ס פ"ז דלהט"ז בסי' צ' סק"ד דהמחבר ס"ל דבו"ח דרבנן לא אמרינן חנ"נ ניחא]:

(There) But if he did not manage [in time], etc. — forbidden unless there is sixty in it. One may say accordingly on that which is taught in Chullin 94: a pot in which he cooked meat — he may not cook milk in it; and if he cooked and there is noten ta'am, and we say: who tastes — kafeilah tastes for him. And per what the Mechaber wrote, why do we rely on kafeilah? Let us say milk was absorbed in the pot and noten ta'am and returned and discharged it and forbids the rest of the milk, and it is min b'mino with the rest of the milk and would require sixty as here. And one may say: behold Ran explains that which challenges on that which Rav said: a kezayit of meat fell into a cauldron of milk, etc. — it is nevelah milk; and Ran asks: why noten ta'am? For since this milk that was absorbed is still connected to this milk that is outside it, which is sixty against the taste of meat — the taste of meat that this absorbed milk received was nullified. And one may say: since it was absorbed inside the meat it is immediately separated from the rest of the milk and stands alone, and properly received meat taste and noten ta'am. And behold per what we hold: if issur was absorbed in food, the issur cannot be squeezed out, for the issur cannot discharge from it; but if issur was absorbed in a vessel we say hag'alah helps and the issur can properly separate from it — and this is the reason: in absorption in food the issur clings to it and adheres more and is absorbed literally in the substance until it cannot give space between the adhesions forever; not so in absorption in a vessel, which does not cling and adhere so much with the vessel, and therefore in hag'alah it can properly separate from it. Accordingly one may say that which we say: if something was absorbed into something else it is considered as separated from what is outside it — this is if absorbed into the food as above, and it is all one body, and we properly say it is as separating and noten ta'am; not so what clings in a vessel, which does not cling so much with the vessel — we say it is still connected to the food inside the vessel, and we do not say that since it was absorbed it is noten ta'am. Accordingly it works out [and so too Peri Megadim at the beginning of siman 94 in Sha'ar HaTevunah this distinction in name of Mordechai]: for here, which deals with absorption inside the vessel, we say noten ta'am and properly requires sixty; not so there regarding a pot in which he cooked, etc. — for since it was absorbed in the vessel we do not say it should be as separated from meat and be noten ta'am and need no sixty at all to nullify it. But still difficult is that which the Mechaber wrote above at the end of seif 4: if he placed [milk] in the hide of the stomach — if there is noten ta'am in it, it is forbidden; it implies if there is no noten ta'am it is permitted even when there is no sixty. Let us say the milk absorbed in the stomach hide is noten ta'am and returned and discharged and would need sixty to nullify — there too it was absorbed in food. And one must say: since it is only forbidden through pickling, and from the Torah it is not forbidden at all in basar b'chalav except through cooking — the Mechaber holds we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, because he holds like Rabbenu Efraim that we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah except in basar b'chalav — and this is because since this alone is permitted, etc., only by their joining together it became forbidden — it is properly as one body of nevelah; and all this if forbidden through cooking, which is as a body of issur; not so through pickling and salting — even though they forbade eating, nevertheless we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah that the whole is considered one body, only it is considered like other issurim. [Hagahah: and so too in the Gra's chiddushim at the end of seif 7, that for Taz in siman 90 s.k. 4 that the Mechaber holds in basar b'chalav that is d'rabbanan we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — it works out.]

(Siman 92 in the hagahah) And likewise if he did not stir, etc. Shach s.k. 6 wants to explain Rama's words that he is in doubt whether we hold like R' Yehuda or like Rashi — see there — and this is that he follows his view that below s.k. 3 he holds like Rashi: if part is in the gravy and part outside the gravy it does not spread at all without stirring and covering. But as appears from Taz's words s.k. 5 who explains Rashi's words that if part, etc., is forbidden — this is because it absorbed before the absorption came to the other pieces and gravy; but nevertheless, since part is in the gravy, afterward it discharges forbidden taste into it and forbids even without stirring and covering — see there. And behold at first glance it is proven like Shach, for behold Tosafot in ch. 7 daf 93 side 2 s.v. im yesh bah noten ta'am explained: when he stirred the pot it carries the drop throughout the pot and all inside assists to nullify the drop; and likewise when he covered the pot, steam rises from the sides and carries the drop throughout the pot — it implies they mean even the piece on which the drop fell had part in the gravy: if he did not stir and cover, the gravy and pieces do not assist to nullify it. And for R' Yehuda it appears that when the piece on which issur fell has part in the gravy, stirring and covering are not required — and there it deals when it is entirely outside the gravy, for then issur does not spread except through stirring and covering, etc. — see there. And it is implied explicitly from their words that for Rashi, Rabbi's statement "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring and without covering" regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah deals when part is in the gravy and part outside the gravy — and on this the Gemara challenges: let us say without stirring and covering at all — absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge — behold explicitly for Rashi the piece does not forbid what is in the pot at all if he did not stir, as Shach's view. However, from the Gemara in Chullin daf 100 it appears at first glance the opposite, as Taz's view, from that which we say there: since it imparted taste in the piece it becomes chatichah na'asah nevelah; and Tosafot there s.v. chatichah na'asah nevelah explained: they were cooked first before the other pieces were placed in the pot and there was not sixty in the issur, and the nevelah imparted taste in it, etc. — and it is difficult per the explanation, for if so the gravy that was at that time was also forbidden first through the absorbed taste, and shortly after it implies they nullify, etc. — see there. Behold per Shach's view in Rashi's words there is room to say: perhaps it deals in first cooking when part was in the gravy and part outside the gravy — this does not discharge at all to the gravy and the gravy was not forbidden; or that it is not like Shach, for even in such a case it discharges — and Tosafot's challenge is well, also from Rashi himself in the sugya of the milk drop daf 108 it appears not like Shach, for Rashi wrote there s.v. ach rav, etc.: that which the mishnah teaches "if there is noten ta'am in it," etc. — in that piece and it forbids the whole piece, etc. — and if so Rav refers to the mishnah, and the plain meaning of the mishnah implies the first part deals without stirring and covering at all, and nevertheless it forbids the rest of the pot — and if so it is proven like Taz's words that if part is in the gravy, nevertheless it discharges and forbids other pieces; and one must say certainly the truth is with Taz. And that which Tosafot wrote in ch. 7 that it appears they mean even what fell on it had part in the gravy, etc. — which per force appears they explain according to Rashi — for Rabbi's "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" deals when part is in the gravy; if so per Taz's view the Gemara should not challenge at all from absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge, etc. One may say: certainly Tosafot explain that Rashi also establishes Rabbi's "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" when it is entirely outside the gravy; and that which Tosafot wrote "and it appears to me that even the piece on which it fell had part in the gravy" — this is because Rashi holds this law that the piece itself is forbidden and other pieces do not combine to nullify the issur — and if so there is no place to challenge why Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" with stirring, etc., and he did not say "the words of the Sages appear correct" when part is in the gravy — because in truth in such a case they hold like R' Yehuda who say chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids also the other pieces; not so per R' Yehuda's view that in such a case other pieces combine. If so per Taz's view there is a difficulty: why should it have said "the words of the Sages" when part is in the gravy and not challenge per Rashi's view that Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" without stirring at first but only at the end — it should have been established that Rabbi's words are where he did not stir and cover at all and it deals when part is in the gravy, etc. [Hagahah: and Peleti noted here also Taz's words and wrote requires study to resolve with difficulty — end of hagahah.] And one may answer: behold certainly the dispute of R' Yehuda and the Sages is in every case: for R' Yehuda, even if he stirred from beginning to end, it is forbidden and the Sages disagree that in every matter it is not forbidden, whether issur fell on a piece with part in the gravy or entirely outside the gravy it is permitted; and R' Yehuda disagrees with them and forbids everything they permitted. And although Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring" regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah — it implies in all cases they disagree — nevertheless they hold like R' Yehuda without stirring, and even entirely outside the gravy; and on this the Gemara challenges well: if he did not stir at all — absorbs absorbs, etc. And it was necessary to answer that it means he did not stir at the beginning but only at the end — for in every case this forbids the rest of the pot; and per this Taz's view stands, and Shach's challenge on Rama returns to its place where he wrote that piece is forbidden and also requires sixty against the drop — if so he is concerned for Rashi's view; if so one should require sixty against the whole piece, for it discharges even without stirring at all; and one must say Rama deals when it is entirely outside the gravy; and that which requires sixty against the drop is because it is not clear to him whether the steam of the pot carries throughout — one might say the steam of the pot carries taste throughout the pot, and if so that piece is forbidden lest perhaps it does not carry, and one also needs sixty in the pot against the drop lest perhaps it carries throughout. And per this one must say that which Tosafot wrote in ch. 7 daf 100 s.v. chatichah na'asah nevelah — "but one may say it deals such as when there is a permitted piece entirely outside the gravy and a nevelah piece placed on it outside the gravy, and the permitted piece became nevelah because it absorbs nevelah taste in which there is no sixty to nullify the taste; nevertheless when he stirred the pot it forbids all pieces because it is its species" — end of his words; one must say Tosafot's view is that it deals after he removed the issur he then added other pieces; for otherwise if it deals that even before removing the nevelah the pieces were in the pot — if so it should be difficult why need it impart taste in the pieces, for even without imparting taste the whole pot is forbidden, lest perhaps the steam of the pot carries throughout, and if so it is a slight amount of nevelah itself forbidding the other pieces — for granted one may say if placed entirely outside the gravy it certainly does not carry throughout, only that piece — if so it must impart taste in the piece, for otherwise it is two slight amounts and we do not say two slight amounts; not so now that we say the steam of the pot carries throughout — if so this slight amount of nevelah itself forbids the other pieces, and one need not say two slight amounts. Or it deals that it was cooked first without other pieces, only in first cooking it was placed entirely outside the gravy — which is not so for Rashi who does not establish such a case — the challenge is well, for the gravy was forbidden; and one may answer: if so per their view too why was the gravy not forbidden, lest perhaps the steam of the pot carries throughout? One may say it deals that there was sixty in the gravy against the nevelah itself — if so either way the gravy is permitted: if the steam of the pot carries throughout, when nevelah fell there it carried throughout the pot and the taste was nullified in the gravy; and if it does not carry throughout, it did not discharge its taste at all to the gravy. Not so that piece forbidding other pieces, lest perhaps it does not carry throughout. Not so per Rashi's view — the challenge is well either way how does it deal: if placed entirely in the gravy — necessarily there is no sixty in the gravy, for if there were sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, this piece too would not be forbidden at all — one reason: we say it removed its species, etc.; and further, even if it did not remove, etc., nevertheless since this piece is only forbidden due to a slight amount, it should not forbid the other pieces, for it is two slight amounts. And if it deals that it was placed with part in the gravy — and properly this piece placed on it is forbidden even though there is sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, for in such a case one cannot say it removed its species and stands in its prohibition — per Taz's view who holds in his words that since part is in the gravy it discharges even without stirring and covering — if so even though there is sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, nevertheless against all the pieces there is not sixty, and if so the gravy is forbidden from the piece since there is no sixty against it — and Tosafot challenge Rashi well — investigate. And behold the author of Pri Toar wrote one may say he holds like Rashi that that piece is forbidden is only what is above the gravy where the power of the gravy does not reach and part is forbidden; but what is inside the gravy is not forbidden, for as soon as absorption comes there the gravy combines to nullify it since part is in the gravy — what is above the gravy does not harm what is inside — see there. And from Tosafot the opposite is implied, for they wrote ch. 7 daf 90 side 2 s.v. im yesh noten ta'am: per Rashi's view "if there is noten ta'am in it" specifically — for he holds that if part is in the gravy, etc., that piece alone is forbidden and the gravy does not combine. But for Rashi it must be that "if there is noten ta'am in it" is not precise — and it is impossible as Peri Toar wrote, for also for Rashi it must be not precise, for it is implied in the mishnah: since there is in the gid enough to impart taste in that thigh, behold this thigh is forbidden — meaning the whole thigh; and per Peri Toar's words that the part placed inside the gravy combines with the gravy to nullify — if so for Rashi too it must be not precise when it said "if there is in it" — rather certainly for Rashi the whole piece is forbidden — investigate.

(There in the hagahah) And some say we state chatichah na'asah nevelah in all issurim. And behold Ran wrote: and so most authors — that even per Rabbenu Tam's view that in other issurim we say chatichah na'asah nevelah, it is only d'rabbanan because of the taste of basar b'chalav. And it is difficult accordingly: that which Tosafot challenged in ch. 25 daf 100 s.v. keshekadim vesalku, etc., per Rabbenu Tam's explanation — how did they hag'alah the large yorot, since the water is noten ta'am, etc. — see there. And per what the commentators said, well they hag'alahed it from the Torah, and there is no chatichah na'asah nevelah at all in other issurim. And some want to resolve that certainly Tosafot also hold it is only d'rabbanan, and that which they challenged from the large yorah — this is since we find explicit permission in the Torah, the Sages would not forbid what the Torah explicitly permitted to the seeing eye — investigate. And in my humble opinion it appears thus: behold Tosafot challenged Rabbenu Tam's view from the large yorah — this is specifically per Rabbenu Tam who holds that only taste is d'oraisa up to sixty; if so, well one may say chatichah na'asah nevelah is forbidden from the Torah in all issurim; not so per Rabbenu Tam the Kohen who on the view of noten ta'am one is not lashed unless there is a kezayit in the time to eat a peras — if so, certainly for him chatichah na'asah nevelah is d'rabbanan; and we hold like R' Chaim Kohen who on taste one is not lashed — if so, certainly chatichah na'asah nevelah is d'rabbanan. [And as he wrote in his book Kereti uPeleti.] And one may say: Tosafot's challenge is even per R' Chaim Kohen — for even though he holds that for more than the time to eat a peras one is not lashed, nevertheless all this is in min b'mino; not so min b'mino according to Rav who holds min b'mino with any amount — nevertheless we certainly say chatichah na'asah nevelah according to all — and if so Tosafot challenged well: if so in hag'alah of the law too there is concern lest he used it on a day of wine offered to idolatry, which is min b'mino with hag'alah, and we say each time chatichah na'asah nevelah. And it is fine per R' Eliezer's view who holds we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim — if so, well they could hag'alah the water of issur absorbed in vessels: a quarter-log of water and the vessel holds thirty zayitim — if so, if they hag'alah it the first time and the water removed the issur and it was absorbed afterward in the vessel — if so it only absorbed a thirtieth of a zayit of issur; for even though what is absorbed now is also a zayit like what was first absorbed, nevertheless since it fell into hag'alah water and returns and absorbs from hag'alah water, it is absorption for the view that parts of the permitted water and one part of issur — if so when hag'alahed a second time there is no noten ta'am at all in the second hag'alah water, only the second hag'alah water is forbidden in any amount, which is the second hag'alah water that is also boiling; and if the vessel holds less than that, such that even in the second hag'alah there is noten ta'am in the water — hag'alah three times, and each time according to the size of what was absorbed and the smallness of the vessel's capacity — increase hag'ala until what was absorbed remains without noten ta'am. Not so Rabbenu Tam who holds chatichah na'asah nevelah also in other issurim — if so when the water was first forbidden up to noten ta'am the water became nevelah; if so even if they hag'alahed a hundred times it does not help, for each time we say in it chatichah na'asah nevelah, and each time what is absorbed absorbs a kezayit of issur as before, since there is no sixty at all in hag'alah water against what was absorbed from it, and the water becomes like issur of nevelah — if so Tosafot challenged well according to Rav who holds min b'mino in any amount — investigate well. [Hagahah of the great Rav, etc., R' Shlomo Kohen, may his light shine, first chapter Vilna. The Gaon's words here are astonishing in my humble opinion, for the matter is plain that min b'mino according to Rav, even though it is from the Torah — for we derive from "and he shall take" the blood of the bull and the blood of the goat — nevertheless lashes certainly do not apply as long as there is no kezayit in the time to eat a peras, for it is no greater than min in not its species; for us, for R' Chaim Kohen there are no lashes on taste, and since min in not its species for us chatichah na'asah nevelah is only d'rabbanan, as written above in name of Peleti — per Rabbenu Tam's view it is plain that the same applies to min b'mino according to Rav; if so necessarily Tosafot's challenge is only to Rabbenu Tam, as Peleti concludes. And incidentally, since we deal with the law of chatichah na'asah nevelah, I saw fit to write on what Peri Megadim was uncertain in Mishbetzot Zahav siman 100 s.k. 1 regarding things that forbid above sixty, such as chametz and spice, etc. — whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in them; and he wrote that from Rabbenu Tam's words in Tur siman 92 it is explained explicitly that we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in a thing forbidden in any amount. However from Ran in ch. 25 who challenged there from that which we taught in the first chapter of Temurah that the diluted mixture does not return to dilute except by calculation, etc., and he needed to resolve that in dry in dry they did not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim because it is not similar to basar b'chalav — see there; in Ran it appears that in wet in wet, even above sixty, we say chatichah na'asah nevelah — so too Peri Megadim there. However in my humble opinion it appears one may say Ran follows his view that he holds chametz and spice and the like forbid from the Torah above sixty, as Peri Megadim wrote in siman 98 in Sha'ar HaTevunah s.k. 29 in his name, and so too there the Gra in the Yoreh De'ah margin — if so it is not less than other issurim that forbid up to sixty, regarding which they decreed chatichah na'asah nevelah because of the taste of basar b'chalav, as Ran wrote. But per what Shach wrote in siman 98 s.k. 29 in name of Issur VeHeter explicitly that it is only d'rabbanan — certainly one may say we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in them; not to mention per Rambam and Mechaber in siman 90 regarding basar b'chalav itself where it is only d'rabbanan, such as milk of a slaughtered animal and the like, that we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, as Taz wrote there per their view — how much more so in other d'rabbanan issurim or in a d'rabbanan mixture; but even for us who do not hold thus, nevertheless above sixty one may certainly say we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, since the main reason we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim is because of the taste of meat and milk, and in basar b'chalav we do not find that it forbids above sixty from the Torah — and see Shach in this siman s.k. 15 what he wondered about Afiei Revavai. And in my humble opinion it appears Afiei Revavai follows Maharshal's view that Taz brought in s.k. 15: in other issurim themselves, that we say in wet in wet we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — specifically when cold mixed with cold and there is no sixty; but hot in hot through cooking, where the issur gives wet taste in the heter — also in other issurim we say chatichah na'asah nevelah — see there. And per this, that which Rama wrote in the hagahah in basar b'chalav one may not rely on this — also cold in cold deals with it; if so, Afiei Revavai wrote well that it is only a d'rabbanan stringency, for from the Gemara that we say "it is nevelah milk" there is no proof at all, for there it is hot in hot and through taste that the piece imparted taste in milk — in such a case Afiei Revavai concedes it is forbidden from the Torah in basar b'chalav; but cold in cold one may well say that also in basar b'chalav it is only from stringency as above — to resolve Afiei Revavai's words. And so one must explain Darkei Moshe's words in siman 99 letter 2 in that which he wrote there to resolve Tur's words per Rashba's view in his responsum that he distinguishes and wrote: specifically if cold mixed with cold or dry with dry — then we say that if he increased upon them unintentionally they are permitted, etc. And it appears Tur wrote stam all issurim, etc., and it implies he deals even with basar b'chalav, as Darkei Moshe himself concludes there; and in basar b'chalav it is explained explicitly in the Gemara that even cold in cold chatichah na'asah nevelah is from the Torah, for we challenge "it is nevelah milk" — if so necessarily it deals with cold and cold. However in truth Tur's language there implies he deals with a hot dish into which issur fell — and then Darkei Moshe's explanation there cannot stand at all, and still requires study. End of hagahah of the great Rav, etc., R' Shlomo Kohen, may his light shine, first chapter Vilna.]

(Seif 5) A drop of milk that fell on the pot, etc. This law has its source in Semag, and these are his words: a drop that fell on the wall of the vessel — we are in doubt in ch. Dam Chatat whether it steams through the whole vessel or not, etc. And see Shach s.k. 18 who brought his wording. And behold in Semag's doubt one may say two explanations, and these are the Gemara's words in ch. Dam Chatat daf 93: they asked — partial cooking in a vessel requires rinsing and washing in the whole vessel or not? He said: it does not require; it is reasonable: as with a garment — as a garment requires laundering only where there is blood, so too a vessel requires rinsing and washing only where there was cooking. He said: are they comparable? Blood does not steam; cooking steams. And further it teaches: rinsing and washing are more stringent than sprinkling — partial cooking in a vessel requires rinsing and washing in the whole vessel. He said: if it teaches, it teaches, etc. — perhaps I would include even terumah; the verse says "it" — to exclude terumah. And it challenges: terumah does not require rinsing and washing, yet it teaches: a pot in which he cooked terumah — he may not cook non-sacred food in it; and if he cooked and there is noten ta'am — Abaye said: it was not needed, for the Master said partial cooking in a vessel requires rinsing and washing in the whole vessel. And Rashba explains: certainly if he cooked in part of the vessel it steams through the whole vessel, only it does not steam above the place where he cooked — only weak taste — and this was the stringency of chatat that even above requires rinsing to remove weak taste. If so one may say this law of Semag: certainly it cannot be that it steams through the whole vessel, only it is doubtful to him whether the explanation is like Rashba — if so, even nevelah in the empty place combines the dish to nullify all that steams; if the explanation is as plain meaning — that "it" comes to exclude from terumah, meaning it does not steam at all, only in chatat it is a gezeirat hakatuv that it requires rinsing and washing, and one might say the same for terumah — therefore the Merciful One wrote "it" — to exclude terumah. Accordingly, if we say like Rashba's explanation, he need not wait only until it cools, for it is already nullified; but if it cannot be that it does not steam at all — if so the drop stands in its place and it is forbidden to pour on that side, or he must wait until it cools. And that which Semag wrote: but when it fell in the empty place, perhaps it steams to the wall of the pot near the food — so too that there is no sixty against the drop and that part becomes nevelah, etc. — this is where the wall of the vessel is thick, such that if he pours the dish by way of the wall of the vessel where the drop fell there will not be sixty in the dish — then it forbids the dish. And all Semag's doubt is whether it steams along the length or width of the vessel; or one may say this is Semag's doubt — and so is the meaning of all Acharonim in his view who explain Semag's words thus: this is certain — partial cooking steams above the place of cooking; only it is doubtful to him whether it steams through the whole vessel or only part of the vessel. And if it steams through the whole vessel — then the absorption reaches the dish too and is nullified in sixty; but if it cannot be that it does not steam through the whole vessel — then one must be concerned lest it steams on the wall of the vessel along length and width until there is no sixty against the drop and chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids the dish afterward when he pours from it; therefore he must wait until it cools. And accordingly, even if the wall of the vessel is very thin, nevertheless it is forbidden to pour from it — and so Shach s.k. 23 who wrote, and these are his words: and even though it appears it is not comparable to a spoon, etc., nevertheless since you come to forbid the wall of the pot because of the drop — necessarily one must say lest cooking does not steam through the whole vessel, etc. — end of his words. It appears from his words that he explains the second explanation: for if the first explanation, even if the dish steams, in any case it is not forbidden, for it only steams weak taste; or he holds the second explanation that it steams full taste.

(There) And splashing from a boiling pot, etc. It is like kli sheni — meaning he does not even need kelipah; and even though we hold hot meat that fell into cold milk requires kelipah — when it is absorbed it absorbs even though the stream broke — one may distinguish between thick and thin. However one may say: it appears from the mishnah of dripping from their gravy onto the earthenware and it returned to it — remove its place — even though it is a thin thing, and even so it is forbidden because of the earthenware, and even though the stream broke. But it works out, for that which Rama wrote: if the stream broke with a thin thing, it only forbids even in kedei kelipah — this is for us who hold the lower prevails; if so, since the lower is cold it cools the upper, and therefore if the stream broke it does not absorb at all, even in kedei kelipah — how much more so for the view that the upper prevails: even when the stream broke it also absorbs, for there is nothing here to cool it. Accordingly it works out for the view that the lower prevails — in any case the Gemara there establishes it with hot earthenware; and for the view that the upper prevails — it establishes it with cold earthenware — still it works out for us that even when the stream broke it also cooks. However from Maharsha's words there it appears that even for the view that the lower prevails, even a thin thing and the stream broke also absorbs in it — as he challenged: that which Tosafot wrote there regarding dripping from its gravy onto the batter — we do not have the text "remove its place," for we hold the lower prevails, and his intent was to say: when it is absorbed it absorbs; or he holds even when the stream broke with a thin thing, when it is absorbed it absorbs — and not like Rama.
