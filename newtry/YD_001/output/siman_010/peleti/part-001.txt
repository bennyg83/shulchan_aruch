Accordingly one can ask: why does the Gemara ask and derive from grease—perhaps Rav only says peel because Rav holds noten taam lifgam is forbidden and they were stringent like Beit Shammai; but for us, who hold noten taam lifgam is permitted, only l'chatchila forbidden—they are not so stringent with heat in beit hashchitah; and if it says regarding avodah zarah: how does Rav hold, whether noten taam lifgam is permitted or forbidden—perhaps his doubt, and for Rava Rav Nachman is clear that Rav holds noten taam lifgam is forbidden—and so Rashi must say in Pesachim daf 46; therefore there is no proof from "peel"; therefore the Rosh explained the Gemara's difficulty "and derive" refers to the dangerously ill animal case—and this is what Rava says, and Rava is uncertain how Rav holds regarding noten taam lifgam—see there. If so, even noten taam lifgam is forbidden, and the Gemara asks and derives well, etc.; and the Rosh's proof and the Rambam's words are correct.

Repairs—and the Shach's view: even the Rosh concedes at least knife-wage benefit must be given; and Peri Chadash challenged him: the Rosh's view regarding bread baked in an oven—all is forbidden—is not understood in his words, for the Rosh distinguishes bread benefit from knife benefit; and the Shach holds the distinction is we require only knife-wage benefit to slaughter—if so, what proof from bread? And if Tosafot held like the Mordechai, the rule should require peeling and sticking should not help—they should have said briefly: the Gemara comes according to the conclusion that hag'alah is required and sticking does not help—what difference if beit hashchitah is boiling or cold, finally sticking does not help by law, and shall the Gemara be against halachah? Rather Tosafot clearly hold: since we hold halachah like Ravina in cold because beit hashchitah is cold, as Rashi wrote explicitly—only sticking is needed, for beit hashchitah does not absorb or expel the knife's absorption; if so, even with that avodah zarah knife, sticking suffices; therefore Tosafot wrote a brick is needed to reconcile even for one who holds beit hashchitah is boiling—namely R' Elazar's colleague, who holds hot; and this is also resolved according to him, and for his view sticking does not help. And if so, it is proved Tosafot do not hold like the Mordechai, only according to what we hold like Ravina, sticking suffices, as the Rashba's view. And Rashi wrote, s.v. vehalachta: even in cold regarding a permitted knife he ruled halachah for the permitted side, that it is not forbidden from this absorption, and it is hard to absorb except through boiling; but regarding a forbidden gentile knife, in the earlier dispute he did not rule, because the knife and meat are soft to absorb, end of his words. And so the Rosh wrote there; and thus according to the plain meaning: Rav forbids because the meat is soft and absorbs forbidden grease; but in Ravina's matter, where the knife absorbs treifah through heat in beit hashchitah, and likewise in second shechitah beit hashchitah expels from the hard knife—we do not say so. And so the Rashba wrote: and if so, with a gentile knife that was stuck or wiped well, there is no concern of grease on the surface, only from absorption in the knife—heat in beit hashchitah has no power to expel and re-absorb; sticking helps and hag'alah is not required. These are exactly Rashba's words, following Rashi. Indeed in the Rosh's words one can wonder: the above Rashi holds like the Rashba regarding a gentile knife with grease smeared on its face, therefore peeling is required, for beit hashchitah absorbs forbidden grease into soft meat. But the Rosh, who wrote a gentile knife speaks where known ben yomo—otherwise it would be noten taam lifgam and permitted—we learn he does not hold grease on the surface; if so, there is no absorption and expulsion in beit hashchitah, and here the knife is hard—and strained to divide between absorption and expulsion, for the rule is: as it absorbs, so it expels; and why should the Rosh hold: even though grease is smeared, nevertheless if the knife is not ben yomo, even smeared grease is spoiled, as the Rambam and his support hold—for the Tur siman 128 wrote without any dispute that visible matter is not spoiled; and if his father disagreed, how plain against his view? And it is clear this is the Rosh's view: we are not concerned that fat is smeared on the face, as appears from his words; if so, the difficulty is in place. And truly we must be precise in Rashi: if Rashi's intent is only to distinguish Rav from Ravina, as appears from the Rashba—there soft matter and in Ravina hard knife—why did Rashi always mention forbidden and permitted language, saying regarding a permitted knife he ruled halachah for the permitted side, etc., but regarding a forbidden gentile knife in the earlier matter he did not rule because knife and meat are soft to absorb—what did Rashi seek here? Forbidden or permitted—all depends on soft and hard; and if permitted is also soft, peel. And it appears certainly besides soft/hard distinction, there is a broad distinction between Rav and Ravina: certainly through heat in beit hashchitah the knife absorbs or expels a slight thing, for knife pressure or pressure is not great; therefore with a knife that is inherently permitted, only that he slaughtered treifah with it and it absorbed slightly through heat in beit hashchitah, and afterward when slaughtering another animal, heat in beit hashchitah expels from this second slight matter—it is a small of a small and we are not concerned. But with a forbidden gentile knife with much absorption—even if beit hashchitah expels only slightly, there is still a small amount and peeling is required. Therefore also with a knife with which he cut boiling, it is forbidden and requires peeling—for although the knife absorbed only slightly, this slight matter is expelled explicitly to boiling and forbids. And this explanation you will find the faith of these words almost in the Rashba's and Razah's novellae. Therefore Rashi may have intended: why he precisely distinguished forbidden from permitted—look and you will see as words only seemingly difficult; therefore it appears certainly the Gemara's plain gentile knife speaks of ben yomo, as Tosafot and Rosh wrote, or of not ben yomo only not wiped and grease smeared on it. In every case Rav speaks, and for that case where it is not ben yomo and we judge because of smeared grease—there is no distinction between forbidden and permitted, for smearing itself is a slight visible matter, as all poskim wrote; if so, if beit hashchitah's heat does not expel and absorb only slightly, it is still a small of a small and still difficult to permit—therefore we distinguish soft from hard: with soft, beit hashchitah absorbs more—therefore with a gentile treifah knife. This is in that case; but in another case, not Rav's smearing matter, only ben yomo knife—there is no soft/hard distinction, for heat in beit hashchitah is needed to expel the knife's absorption for Rav. And one must say the second answer: although it is hard and beit hashchitah absorbs only slightly—what of it, the knife was forbidden beforehand and its absorption is much, unlike a treifah knife that absorbed only slightly; therefore heat in beit hashchitah does not expel from hard either except slightly—and it is a small of a small, and we are not so stringent—and think. And it is precisely analyzed in Rashi's language—and if the Rosh wrote: with a knife that slaughtered, forbidden to cut boiling without hag'alah, even though he ruled there is no heat in beit hashchitah controlling the hard knife—for according to the one who says beit hashchitah is boiling, even for Ravina, and since there is some absorption, the boiling cut afterward fully absorbs and forbids (and what he wrote below for the one who says cold, etc., I will write in the next s.k.)—if so, it is clarified for us: with a ben yomo knife, even clean of smeared fat, nevertheless heat in beit hashchitah expels slightly into the slaughtered animal, even though the knife is hard, and peeling is required, not sticking—and this is the Mordechai's view above; nevertheless one may be lenient, since in any case the Rif and Rambam rule not like Rav and Rashba, and Tosafot permit sticking—one may rely.
