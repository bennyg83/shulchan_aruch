**** ENGLISH ****
That a Jew cannot be a guarantor for a Jew who borrowed from a non-Jew with interest; it contains 2 se’ifim. A Jew who borrowed money from a non-Jew with interest — it is forbidden for another Jew to be his guarantor, since in their law they first claim from the guarantor; it turns out that the guarantor claims from the Jew with interest, which the guarantor is obligated for to the non-Jew. Therefore, if the non-Jew accepted upon himself that he will not claim from the guarantor first, it is permitted. {Rama: And nowadays, presumably it is as if he accepted upon himself so, for this is the standard form of guarantorship (Tur); and this is specifically in a place where the custom is so, that they claim from the borrower first.} And some say that it is forbidden only with a “arev sheluf d’utz” (meaning: a guarantor who absolves the borrower from the lender’s claim, like “sheluf d’utz,” which is a type of grass that they dry and wash with it the hands to remove filth) — that is, where the lender has no claim against the borrower at all, for he can push him off to the guarantor. {Rama: And if he transgressed and became a guarantor for him, he must pay all loss that comes to him through him; but he need not give the interest that he pays for him, for they do not say to give interest (Tur). And if he already paid him the interest, they do not extract it from his hand, for it is no worse than avak ribbis (the author’s reasoning).}

**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise, a non-Jew who borrowed from a Jew with interest — it is forbidden for a Jew to be his guarantor. {Rama: Unless he stipulates with the non-Jew that as long as he has from what to pay, he will not push him off to the guarantor; then it is permitted to receive the principal and the interest from the Jew if the non-Jew will not pay (Tur). And if the Jew is guarantor for him for the principal and not for the interest, rather he has to take the interest from the non-Jew; or the opposite — that he is guarantor for him for the interest and not for the principal — it is permitted. And specifically where the non-Jew takes the money directly from the lender; but if a Jew is guarantor and takes it from his hand, even though he does so for the non-Jew, it is forbidden (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 301). And specifically without collateral; but if he gives collateral of the non-Jew for the principal, he can be guarantor for him for the interest, for the essence of the loan is on the collateral; even though he took them from the Jew, the lender, it is as if he lent into the hand of the non-Jew (the author’s reasoning). And if a Jew had collateral from a non-Jew, or a guarantor who is a non-Jew, and a Jew came and said to the lender that he should return to the non-Jew his collateral, or that he should exempt the non-Jew’s guarantor, and he becomes his guarantor: he must pay him all the principal and the interest that accrued on it until that day, but not the interest that will accrue from now and onward (Mordechai, perek Eizehu Neshech, in the name of Ravyah). Unless the collateral was worth more, for then he must pay him interest until the value of the collateral, or return his collateral (Beit Yosef and Beis Ze’ev, siman 364). And if the non-Jew combined the principal with the interest, then even what will accrue afterward is as principal and he must pay him everything (Mordechai; and according to Terumas HaDeshen, even Rosh holds so; and Maharik, shoresh 136) — even if the collateral was not worth [that], or even if he had no collateral at all, rather he exempted a non-Jew’s guarantor that he had.}
