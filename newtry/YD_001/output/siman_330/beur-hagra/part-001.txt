<b>ושל ישראל כו'. </b>הרא"ש בפסקיו למד מרישא עיסת עובד כוכבים כו' הנ"ל כמו שאין גלגול ישראל מחייב כו'. ואמרינן בספ"ב דפאה שדה שקצרוה עובדי כוכבים כו' ויליף לה בספרי ובקצרכם פרט לשקצרוה עובדי כוכבים והביאו הר"ש שם ואמרינן בירושלמי שם והביאו הר"ש שם מתני' כשקצרוה לעצמן אבל אם קצרוה לישראל חייבת ותני כן אין כו' וכ"ש כאן דלא אימעוט אלא מעריסותיכם ולא של עובדי כוכבים כמ"ש במנחות ס"ז. בתשו' כלל ב' ס"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And that of an Israelite.</b> Rosh derives this from the opening clause concerning gentile dough: just as a Jew's kneading does not obligate dough belonging to a gentile in that case, so too ownership remains decisive. At the end of chapter 2 of Peah the Mishnah states that a field harvested by gentiles is exempt, derived by Sifrei from "when you reap," excluding what gentiles reaped. The Yerushalmi there, cited by Rash, limits this to gentiles who harvested for themselves. If they harvested for a Jew, it is obligated. All the more so here, where the exclusion is only from "your kneadings," meaning not those of gentiles. See Menachot 67 and Rosh, responsa klal 2, siman 2.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if there is... and he can.</b> Tur, based on Rosh, writes that the exemption of a partnership with a gentile applies where the Jewish share alone lacks the minimum challah measure. Rosh adds that one can separate challah from the mixture itself because there is blending in a liquid-like mixture and challah has no fixed Torah amount. This discussion is difficult. Chullin does not mention a gentile partnership in challah there, but terumah. Sefer HaTerumah and the other poskim ask from terumah, where Rosh's answer cannot work because even the smallest amount creates an obligation. In truth that passage follows R. Ilai; according to the Sages the Gemara itself explains a gentile partnership differently. The case of royal tax in Pesachim 6a remains difficult, and one must say that even where the Jewish share alone lacks the minimum measure the obligation remains because the government's claim is uncertain, as Sefer HaTerumah writes. Practically there is no difference on that point. Rosh's further statement that one can separate from within the mixture is also difficult, because we rule that there is no automatic blending for such legal allocation, as stated in Rosh Hashanah 13b. Rashi's phrase there about liquid matter led him astray; the relevant distinction there concerns ordinary physical mixing, not legal identification of ownership. I agree only where the grain or flour was originally acquired jointly, because each particle is jointly owned and challah has no minimum amount, as in siman 331, seif 11. But if separately owned flours were later mixed, there is no remedy except to remove enough that some portion must certainly come from the Jewish share, as Beit Yosef cites from an anonymous written source and as is explicit in Zevachim regarding a mixture containing a sufficient quantity. If the grain was originally purchased jointly, Rashi in Chullin likewise supports the distinction.

<b>(ליקוט) ודוקא שניכר כו'. </b>ירושלמי שם איזהו עיסת כלבים רשב"ל אומר כל שעירב בה מורסן מתני' אמרה כן בזמן שהרועים אוכלים ממנה פעמים שאין הרועים אוכלים ממנה ר' יוחנן אמר כל שעשאה כעבין ותני כן עשאן כעבין חייבת עשאה למודים פטורה ר' בא בשם שמואל ר' אמי בשם ר' חייא רבא אפי' עשאה קלוסקין והא תנינן אם אין הרועים אוכלים ממנה תפתר שעשאה משעה ראשונה שלא יאכלו הרועים ממנה ופסק הרא"ש ס"ב והטור בשם הרמב"ן כר' יוחנן אבל הרמב"ם לא כתב רק לשון מתני' ופסק כשמואל ור' חייא רבא וס"ל דהן פליגי אר' יוחנן וכן נראה דאמר קלוסקין דלא כב"י. ור"ש פסק כר"ל. ועבא"ח סי' תנ"ד ס"ב שכתב הרמב"ם שאין זו כו' וכשיטתו כר' בא שעשאה מתחלה כו' ומ"מ גם לר' בא י"ל טעם המפרשים וכמ"ש בהג"ה שם וטעמו של הר"ש שפסק כר"ל דסוגיא דגמ' דידן בפ"ב די"ט כוותיה דאמרינן שם כ"א א' מתיב ר' חנא כו' שאני עיסת כו' אלמא אע"ג שנעשית לשם כלבים הואיל ויכול לאוכלה נאפית בי"ט וצ"ל בסיפא דוקא שאין יכול לאוכלה וכל הני אמוראי דירושלמי הנ"ל ס"ל דלא אמרינן הואיל אבל אנן קי"ל דאמרינן הואיל וע"כ צ"ל כר"ל (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Likkut) And specifically when it is recognizable.</b> The Yerushalmi asks what constitutes dough made for dogs. Reish Lakish says when bran is mixed into it. R. Yochanan says when it is formed in the manner made for dogs, and a baraita distinguishes ordinary cakes from special dog-food forms. R. Ba in the name of Shmuel and R. Ami in the name of R. Chiyya Rabbah say that even if made as fine rolls, it is exempt where from the outset it was prepared so shepherds would not eat from it. Rosh and Tur, in the name of Ramban, rule like R. Yochanan. Rambam, however, records only the Mishnah's language and appears to rule like Shmuel and R. Chiyya Rabbah. This also accords with Rambam's ruling in Orach Chayyim siman 454. Rash rules like Reish Lakish because the Bavli in chapter 2 of Yom Tov follows his approach: if the dough can be eaten by a person, even though made for dogs, it may be baked on Yom Tov. Thus, according to the Bavli, the decisive issue is whether it remains human food, and this supports Reish Lakish.
