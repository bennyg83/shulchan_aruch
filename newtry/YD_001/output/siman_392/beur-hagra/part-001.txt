**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say that also.</b> Rambam appears to understand the Gemara's objection and answer as referring to a meal as well. Mourning of Tisha B'Av is more stringent, which explains why business activity is prohibited there. Rashi's explanation leaves that point difficult, and Tosafot struggle with it. Rambam further infers from the wording "all thirty days for marriage" the broader rule, like Tosafot's question. <b>(Likkut)</b> Evel Rabbati chapter 7 teaches thirty days for betrothal and thirty days for marriage, though there it limits both to the festive meal. See Ramban's chiddushim to Yevamot and Torat HaAdam.

<b>לקדש מיד כו' וליכנס כו' אא"כ כו'. </b>עבתוס' שם ושם ורא"ש שם: <br><b>(ליקוט) אא"כ כו'. </b>כדעת ר"ת ואף שהתוס' ביבמות שם וכתובות שם הקשו על ר"ת תירץ הרא"ש במ"ק שם דמ"ש בא"ר אחר ל' מילתא פסיקתא נקט כו' ע"ש: שאז מותר לבא כו'. שבדידיה אין הכניסה אלא משום ביאה אבל דעת ר"י שבזה אפי' הכניסה אסור עד שלשים ושכן משמע בא"ר: אחר שבעה. דוקא מהא דכתובות ד' א' דע"כ שם בבחור וכמ"ש תוס' שם (ע"כ):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To betroth immediately... and to marry... unless.</b> See Tosafot there and Rosh. <b>(Likkut) Unless.</b> This follows Rabbenu Tam. Although Tosafot in Yevamot and Ketubot challenge him, Rosh in Moed Katan answers that Evel Rabbati's phrase "after thirty days" states the ordinary fixed rule. <b>Then it is permitted to have relations.</b> In that case the entry into marriage is only for the sake of marital relations. According to Ri, even entry into marriage itself remains forbidden until thirty days. Evel Rabbati appears to support Ri. <b>After seven days.</b> This follows Ketubot 4a, which necessarily concerns an unmarried man, as Tosafot explain.
