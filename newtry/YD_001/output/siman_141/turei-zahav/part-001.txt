<b>כל הצלמים כו'. </b>במתניתין פליגי רבי מאיר וחכמים דר"מ ס"ל כיון שאותה צורה נעבדה במקום אחד גזרינן שאר המקומות בשבילו וחכמים לא גזרו אא"כ יש בידו סייף כו' כמו שכתוב כאן בש"ע ומ"מ משמע דאף אם יש בידו סייף לא מהני לאיסור אלא בצורה שידוע לנו שיש עובדין לצורה כזו דומיא דאיסורו של ר"מ משום גזירה וא"כ אין חילוק בין אם הצורה ההיא של אדם או שאר צורה ובטור כתוב לקמן בסימן זה צורת אדם עומדת כו' ובידה מקל כו' וכתב ב"י כאן שמשמע מדבריו דוקא צורת אדם אבל לא שאר צורות נראה כוונתו דבצורת אדם הטעם שידוע שיש עובדים לו משא"כ שאר צורות אבל אם היה לנו בירור שיש עובדים גם לשאר צורות הוה גם כן דינא הכי משום הכי כתב הרמב"ם והעתיקו הש"ע כאן והיה ביד הצורה כו' ולא זכר צורת אדם מטעם שאמרנו ונראה דמ"ש בגמ' אמר רב יהודה אמר שמואל באנדרטי של מלכים שם הוא ג"כ מורה ע"ז שידוע שיש עובדים אותה כמ"ש רש"י. ומו"ח ז"ל כתב דליתא למה שכ' הש"ע סתם צורה וקשה דאטו דידיה היא הא דרמב"ם הוא בפרק ז' מהלכות עבודת כוכבים והכוונה למ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All images, etc. </b>In the Mishnah, Rabbi Meir and the Sages disagree. Rabbi Meir holds that since that form was worshipped in one place, we issue a decree concerning all other places on its account. The Sages did not issue such a decree unless it has a sword in its hand, etc., as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. Nevertheless, it appears that even if it has a sword in its hand, this renders it forbidden only when it is a form that we know is worshipped in such a manner, similar to Rabbi Meir's prohibition, which is because of a decree. If so, there is no distinction whether that form is a human form or another form. The Tur writes below in this siman, “A standing human form, etc., with a staff in its hand, etc.” The Beit Yosef writes here that the Tur's words imply specifically a human form, but not other forms. It appears that his intent is that regarding a human form, the reason is that it is known that people worship it, unlike other forms. However, if it were established for us that they also worship other forms, the same law would apply to them. Therefore, the Rambam wrote, and the Shulchan Aruch copied here, “and the form had in its hand, etc.,” without mentioning a human form, for the reason we have stated. It appears that what the Gemara states, “Rav Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: This concerns a royal statue,” likewise indicates that it is known to be worshipped, as Rashi wrote. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that what the Shulchan Aruch stated without qualification concerning a form is incorrect. This is difficult, for is this the Shulchan Aruch's own ruling? It is the ruling of the Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilchos Avodah Zarah, and its intent is as we have explained.

<b>דסתמא לשם עבודת כוכבים נעשו. </b>בטור כתוב בזה כיון דספק עבודת כוכביה אסורה דסתמא כו' ויש לתמוה כיון דמסתמא הוה כן למה לו הטעה דספיקא לחומרא. ועוד קשה במה שאמר אח"כ בכרכין דודאי לנוי נעשו למה יהיה ודאי דהא נמי באפשר הוא שנעשה לשם עבודת כוכבים ונראה דאף על פי דספק עבודת כוכבים לחומרא מכל מקום כל שיש סברא להיתר ולאיסור ראוי להקל דהא קי"ל בכל דוכתי דלא מחזקינן איסורא מספק ואין מחמרינן מספק אלא במקום דאתחזק איסורא כבר ע"כ כיון שיש סברא דלנוי עשוי אין מחזקינן להאי סברא לודאי כיון שיש לה סיוע מכח לא מחזקינן איסורא אבל בכפרים דאין דרך לעשותה לנוי א"כ האי סברא דלעבודת כוכבים נעשה הוא בשוה עם צד ההיתר דלשניהם יש ריעותא דלאיסור יש סתירה דאין מחזקינן איסור ולהיתר יש סתירה דאין דרך לעשות לנוי הוה שניהם שוין ומספיקא אזלינן לחומרא בעובד כוכבים ומו"ח ז"ל כתב דכוונת הטור דלא להוי ספק ספיקא ספק אם נעשה עבודת כוכבים את"ל נעשה אימור בטלוה וקשה דאין ביטול בזה אפילו גררה לא הוה ביטול כל זמן שלא פחתה בצורתה וכאן מיירי משלמים דאי בשבורים הכי נמי באמת הוה ביטול כמ"ש אח"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For they are ordinarily made for the sake of idol worship. </b>The Tur writes concerning this, “Since a doubt concerning idol worship is forbidden, for ordinarily, etc.” This is surprising. Since it is ordinarily so, why does he need the reason that a doubt is ruled stringently? It is also difficult that he afterwards says that in cities they were certainly made for decoration. Why is that certain? It is also possible that they were made for idol worship. It appears that although a doubt concerning idol worship is ruled stringently, nevertheless, wherever there is a rationale for permitting and a rationale for forbidding, it is proper to be lenient. We rule everywhere that we do not establish a prohibition on the basis of a doubt, and we are stringent because of a doubt only where the prohibited status was already established. Therefore, since there is a rationale that it was made for decoration, we do not establish the rationale for prohibition as certain, because the permissive rationale is supported by the principle that we do not establish a prohibition. In villages, however, where it is not customary to make such a form for decoration, the rationale that it was made for idol worship is equal to the permissive possibility. Each side has a weakness. The prohibited possibility is weakened because we do not establish a prohibition, while the permissive possibility is weakened because it is not customary to make it for decoration. The two possibilities are therefore equal, and because of the doubt we rule stringently regarding a non-Jew. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the Tur's intent is to prevent this from being a compound doubt, perhaps it was not made for idol worship, and even if it was made for idol worship, perhaps they nullified it. This is difficult, because there is no nullification here. Even if one dragged it, that would not constitute nullification so long as its form was not diminished. Here we are discussing whole images, for if they were broken, that would indeed constitute nullification, as stated afterwards.

<b>עומד על בסיסו. </b>פי' שיש לה בית מושב רחב שאז אינם נראים כשברים אלא כך נעשו מתחילה ולא היה שם רק אבר זה לבד ואין כאן ביטול משמע כאן דכשעבדוה בשעה שהיתה שלימה ונשבר ממנה הוי ביטול וקשה דהא בסוף סימן קמ"ו פסק הטור דעבודת כוכבים שנשתברה מאיליה אסורה וא"כ ניחוש כאן שמא מאליה נשבר זה ממנה ואמאי מותר למוצאה באין עומד על בסיסו הא ספיקא דאורייתא ונראה דיש לומר כיון ששם כתב הטור דאין האיסור אלא בהדיוט יכול להחזירה וכאן שנשתבר ונאבד הרי אין הדיוט יכול להחזירה אח"כ ראיתי ברש"י סוף פרק כל הצלמים שהקשה כן ותירץ דנשתברה מאליה לא שכיחא ואזלינן בתר רובא דעובד כוכבים שברה והטור כתב בשם הרמב"ם דאסור אפילו באין עומד על בסיסו והקשה הטור עליו דהא אוקמיה בגמרא אליביה דשמואל דוקא בעומד על בסיסו ותירץ ב"י דרמב"ם ס"ל דשמואל סבר גם בעבודת כוכבים שנשתברה מאליה דמותר ומ"ה הוצרך לכך אבל לדידן דאסור אסור בשברי אליל דודאי נעבד ולא מהני הביטול ויפה תירץ שכ"כ התוס' ג"כ שם דשמואל כר"ל ס"ל דמותר בנשבר מאליו. ומו"ח ז"ל חלק עליו ותירץ דרמב"ם מחלק בין צלמים לאליל דבצלמים בעינן דוקא בסיסו אבל באליל אסור בלאו הכי וק"ל דהא בהדיא מתיר שמואל אפילו שברי עבודת כוכבים ורמב"ם אסרו שכתב כיון שידוע שזו הצורה נעבדה הרי היא באיסורה עד שיודע שהעובד כוכבים ביטלה וב"י מביאו אלא ע"כ דפסק דלא כשמואל וכמ"ש ב"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Standing on its base. </b>This means that it has a broad pedestal. In that case, the pieces do not appear to be fragments, but rather to have been fashioned this way originally, with only this limb present, and therefore there was no nullification. It is implied here that when it was worshipped while whole and a part broke off, this constitutes nullification. This is difficult, for at the end of siman 146 the Tur rules that an idol that broke by itself remains forbidden. If so, we should be concerned here that this piece may have broken off by itself. Why is it permitted when it is found not standing on its base? This is a doubt concerning a Torah prohibition. It appears that one can answer that there the Tur writes that it remains forbidden only where an ordinary craftsman could restore it. Here, however, where it broke and the piece was lost, an ordinary craftsman cannot restore it. I later saw that Rashi, at the end of the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, asks this question and answers that an idol breaking by itself is uncommon, and we follow the majority assumption that a non-Jew broke it. The Tur writes in the name of the Rambam that it is forbidden even when it is not standing on its base. The Tur challenges him, for the Gemara established Shmuel's ruling specifically where it stands on its base. The Beit Yosef answers that the Rambam holds that Shmuel also maintains that an idol that broke by itself is permitted, and therefore Shmuel required the condition that it stands on its base. According to us, however, who rule that an idol that broke by itself remains forbidden, fragments of an idol are forbidden, for it was certainly worshipped and the breaking does not effect nullification. He answered well, for Tosafos likewise write there that Shmuel follows Reish Lakish, who permits an idol that broke by itself. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, disagreed and answered that the Rambam distinguishes between images and an actual idol. Regarding images, it must stand on its base, but regarding an idol it is forbidden even without this. This is difficult, for Shmuel explicitly permits even fragments of an idol, whereas the Rambam forbids them, writing that since it is known that this form was worshipped, it remains forbidden until it is known that the non-Jew nullified it. The Beit Yosef cites this. Necessarily, the Rambam rules unlike Shmuel, as the Beit Yosef wrote.

<b>וצורת דרקון מותר לעשותה. </b>כן הוא תלמוד ערוך פ' כל הצלמים (עבודה זרה דף מ"ב) בסופו ואי בעושה צורת דרקון מי אסר והא כתיב לא תעשון אתי אלהי כסף ואלהי זהב הני אין דרקון לא ותמהני על הב"י שכתב מקור דין זה לפי שאינו מכלל דברים הנזכרים בגמרא לאיסור ורש"ל נתן טעם למה לא נכלל דרקון בכלל לא תעשון כדמות שמשי שבמרום לפי שהדרקון שבמרום פורח באויר ואינו בשמים. ומו"ח ז"ל הקשה מדברי המרדכי בשם רבינו אליקים הביאו ב"י בסוף סי' זה במאי דאמרינן כל הפרצופים מותר חוץ מפרצוף אדם דהאי כל לאו דוקא דהא צורת חמה ולבנה ודרקון הם בכלל האיסור הואיל וחשיבי ציירי ופלחי עכ"ל משמע דגם דרקון אסור לעשות משום לא תעשון אתי כמו חמ' ולבנ' (וקשה) [ולא קשיא] דשם קאי אלהשהותם דהא בהדיא מסיים הואיל וחשיבי ציירי ופלחי וזה שייך על עובד כוכבים שעשאה לזה וישראל מוצאה כדאיתא פרק כל הצלמים (עבודה זרה דף מ"ב) ואע"ג דרבינו אליקים מיירי שם לאביי דמוקי לה בעושה מבואר כוונתו שם דה"ק אפילו לאביי דמיקל ומוקי לה אפילו בעושה וממילא להשהותה מותר לגמרי מ"מ האי כל לאו דוקא כו' כן צריך לומר שם למעיין בדבריו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is permitted to make the form of a dragon. </b>This is an explicit Gemara at the end of the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42b: “If one makes the form of a dragon, who forbade it? Is it not written, ‘You shall not make with Me gods of silver and gods of gold’? These are forbidden, but a dragon is not.” I am astonished at the Beit Yosef, who wrote that the source of this law is that a dragon is not included among the forms mentioned in the Gemara as forbidden. The Maharshal gave a reason why a dragon is not included in “You shall not make with Me,” as a likeness of My servants who serve before Me in the heavens, because the heavenly dragon flies in the air and is not in the heavens. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, raised a difficulty from the words of the Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Elyakim, which the Beit Yosef cites at the end of this siman. Concerning the statement, “All faces are permitted except the human face,” he writes that “all” is not precise, for the forms of the sun, moon, and dragon are likewise included in the prohibition, since they are significant forms that people fashion and worship. End quote. This implies that making a dragon is likewise forbidden because of “You shall not make with Me,” just like the sun and moon. There is no difficulty, for there he refers to retaining them. He explicitly concludes, “since they are significant forms that people fashion and worship.” This applies where a non-Jew made it and a Jew finds it, as stated in the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42b. Although Rabbeinu Elyakim there discusses Abaye, who establishes the teaching as referring to making the form, his intent is as follows: Even according to Abaye, who is lenient and establishes it even as referring to making, so that retaining it is certainly entirely permitted, nevertheless, the word “all” is not precise, etc. This is how one must explain his words upon examining them.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Such as the four faces. </b>One might ask: He later states that even a human form by itself is forbidden. The Ran answers that here there is a greater novelty, because he joined the other forms to the human form. One might have thought that it is permitted because it is not a human form. The same applies to ministering angels, because they have wings. Therefore, he teaches us that it is forbidden. This is difficult, for if so, were one to make a human form together with one or two of the other four faces, it should likewise be permitted, because there is neither a human form alone nor the form of all four faces. It does not appear reasonable to permit such a novelty, for the poskim certainly would not have remained silent without informing us that it is permitted. Therefore, it appears that there is a practical difference here. There is a prohibition derived from “with Me,” referring to the heavenly servants, the four faces together, and another prohibition derived from “Me,” referring to the human form alone. Were there only the exposition of “Me,” I would have said that after diminishing the human form it is permitted to retain the object, because the prohibition was only due to the human form alone. Therefore, the exposition of “with Me” teaches that there is also a prohibition because of the four faces. Consequently, all of them were made in a prohibited manner, and correcting only the human form does not help, since a prohibition remains even apart from the human form alone. He must diminish all of the forms. This appears correct. I later saw in the Semak, siman 160, as follows: “The text asks: Let the prohibition follow from the human form itself, for even by itself it is forbidden. It answers: This is needed only where the human form was already made and he completes the four faces by adding the other forms, which are permitted to make by themselves.” End quote. One cannot answer that a human form is forbidden only when it is a complete form with all of its limbs, as the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 7, whereas the face alone is permitted, while the four faces are forbidden even when only their faces are depicted. For if so, why does the Gemara ask, “Accordingly, let the human face alone be permitted”? In truth, the form of a human face alone, without the entire body, is permitted. Necessarily, all four faces are depicted in their complete bodily form as they appear in the Divine Chariot.

<b>It is permitted to make them. </b>This implies that all the forbidden forms mentioned above may be made when they are recessed. However, the Tur subsequently cites the Ramban, who writes that every form whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah because of “You shall not make with Me,” namely, the four faces of the Divine Chariot, the likeness of the angels in the upper abode, the likeness of the sun, moon, stars, and constellations in the lower abode, and the human form because it is stated, “You shall not make Me,” meaning in the likeness in which I show Myself to the prophets, is forbidden whether recessed or protruding. Other forms are likewise forbidden to make, but only rabbinically, and regarding those forms there is a distinction between recessed and protruding. Consequently, regarding those forms whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah, although retaining them is permitted by the Torah and prohibited only rabbinically because of suspicion, there is likewise a distinction between recessed and protruding. The Ran cites this opinion in the name of the early authorities, writes that it is the primary view, and proves it from the Gemara. If so, it is astonishing that the Shulchan Aruch did not cite the Ramban's opinion. As a matter of practical halacha, one certainly should not be lenient at all against the Ramban, for the Tur concludes in accordance with him, and so does the Ran. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that according to the Ramban, objects whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah and whose retention is forbidden rabbinically may not be retained even when recessed. However, the Ran's words that I cited, which express the Ramban's view, state explicitly that they may be retained when recessed. As for the implication of the Ramban's words that there is likewise a rabbinic prohibition to make other forms, the Ran explains that this refers to forms that non-Jews were accustomed to worship. If there is no custom to worship them, it is permitted to make them, as the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 6.

<b>And the form of the sun, etc. </b>This is because these celestial bodies do not protrude in the firmament. So wrote Tosafos. In the Gemara and the Tur, the constellations are also mentioned together with the stars. If so, it is astonishing that the Shulchan Aruch later writes in seif 6 that it is permitted to make forms of animals. Are not the kid, lamb, and ox among the constellations, and likewise the lion? The Beit Yosef explicitly writes at the end of the siman, in his difficulty concerning the Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Elyakim, that a lion is among the permitted forms. Furthermore, if all forms of the constellations are forbidden, it would be forbidden to make the form of a fish, which is the constellation of Adar. Yet we find in the Gemara, in the chapter HaShole'ach, that Rav drew a fish. Were this forbidden, it would be forbidden even when recessed, as the Tur writes in the name of the Ramban concerning every form forbidden because of “You shall not make with Me.” It appears possible that the forms representing the constellations have distinctive designs, similar to what the Rama writes concerning the forms of the sun and moon in seif 3. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch properly writes that forms of animals are permitted, implying all of them. However, this is difficult, for why did the Gemara not answer concerning Rabban Gamliel, for whom they made forms of the moon, that they did not draw the image of a king seated in a wagon mentioned by the Rama? Necessarily, with regard to making them, every depiction is forbidden, even a plain depiction of the sun. The distinctive image of a king is relevant only to retaining a form that one found. If so, the forms of the constellations, such as a lion and an ox, should likewise be forbidden even in their ordinary form. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, indeed wrote that it is forbidden to make the form of a lion or an ox because they are constellations. This is difficult because of what we cited, that Rav drew a fish. It appears that a drawing is more lenient even than a recessed image, because a drawing has no substance at all. Nevertheless, it appears that those who draw the forms of the constellations in machzorim in the prayer for rain do not act properly, since they intend to make the forms of the constellations.
