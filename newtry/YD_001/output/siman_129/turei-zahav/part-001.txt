**** ENGLISH ****One who leaves a gentile, etc. In the Gemara he makes a distinction: if it taught "one who passes his wine" one would say specifically in this it is permitted, for the gentile thinks perhaps he will come and see me; but in a wagon or ship, if he sails he does as he wishes — meaning one would say we should concern for this; it teaches us we do not concern for this. And if it taught wagon and ship alone, one would say because he thinks perhaps he will come another way and stand by the wall and see me; but in a shop he closes the door and does as he wishes — it teaches us we do not concern for this. And all those above siman 128 — if he closed the door it is forbidden — that is when we know clearly he closed the door in a matter the Jew cannot enter there without the gentile's knowledge; but when we do not know — permitted; and even though there is a lock inside he could lock if he wished; nevertheless we learn: if here the gentile clearly closed the door it is forbidden, since he has connection in the house and has where to rely — standing there with permission, for Israel left it there; and likewise in the permit of wagon and ship that we say the reason of the permit is he thinks he will come another way and stand by the wall and see — evidently specifically when there is another way; but if there is only one way, since he sailed his eyes are from him it is forbidden, for he cannot come unless he sees him; and likewise if he clearly sailed on a ship or wagon in a place impossible to see — certainly forbidden; but when uncertain we do not concern for this. All this from Rashba in Tosafot HaRosh; and this is what he concludes here — and that it was not known, etc.

**** ENGLISH ****In another matter — when there is a roundabout way.
