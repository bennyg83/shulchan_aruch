The Ran wrote: that which we require a winding path applies specifically when the gentile holds the barrel in his hand or on his shoulder; but when he left it and Israelites go in and out, we do not require a winding path, for at every moment he fears lest an Israelite now come and see him approaching the barrel — end. And Bach wondered at the Ran, for where there is no winding path, even in his own store one should be concerned that he keeps the entrance open and sees from a distance whether an Israelite is coming or not, and does not fear; and such is the view of the Tur, and thus we hold — end. But he wondered in vain, for as Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe wrote — even the Ran agrees with this, and he only excludes the case where the gentile cannot see whether the Israelite is coming — see there; and nevertheless when holding the barrel in his hand we require a winding path, for otherwise it is impossible that he not see him when nearby and immediately withdraw his hand; but to move away from the barrel one cannot do so immediately — examine.
