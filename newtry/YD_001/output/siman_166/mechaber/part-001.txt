**** ENGLISH ****
One who lends to his fellow should not do labor with his slave, and should not live in his courtyard; it contains 3 se’ifim. One who lends to his fellow should not do labor with his slave, even if he is idle; and he should not live in his courtyard for free, and he should not rent from him for less — even in a courtyard that is not meant to be rented (meaning: it is not intended to be rented out), and [even if] it is a person who does not rent out. And if he already lived in it: since the courtyard is not meant to be rented, he need not give him [payment], even in order to fulfill his obligation before Heaven — even if it is a person who does rent out. And according to Rambam, even if it is a person who does not rent out, it is avak ribbis, and he must return [it] if he comes to fulfill his obligation before Heaven. (And in a courtyard that is meant to be rented it is avak ribbis according to all) (Tur); and see further in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 72.

**** ENGLISH ****
If he said to him, “Lend to me, and live in my courtyard”: if it is meant to be rented, it is fixed interest; and if it is not meant to be rented, it is avak ribbis. {Rama: And this is specifically when it is unspecified; but if he said to him, “Live in my courtyard in payment for the loan,” even if it is not meant to be rented it is like fixed interest, since he said to him “in payment for your money,” it is as if he rented it to him now (Beit Yosef in the name of the students of Rashba).} And if he lent to him, and after some time he demanded his debt, and he said to him, “Lend [it longer], [and] live in my courtyard”: some say that it is only avak ribbis, and some say it is fixed interest — provided that he says so at the time that he grants him time. {Rama: And the same law applies to any interest that comes to a person where he did not stipulate initially: there is this dispute whether it is avak ribbis or fixed.}

**** ENGLISH ****
In a case where it is avak ribbis, some say that even if he has not yet repaid, and the borrower demands that he deduct from his debt the measure of the rent, we do not deduct for him; and some say that we do deduct for him. {Rama: And the latter reasoning is the primary one (it is the view of Rosh and the Tur and R' Ephraim and Rashba). One who received money as a loan from someone, and also learns with his son, and stipulated with him that he will give him the expenses for this: even though he would have given him the expenses without the loan because he learned with his son, even so it is forbidden, for it is like “lend to me and live in my courtyard,” which is forbidden even when it is not meant to be rented (in Mordechai, perek Eizehu Neshech, in the name of a responsum of Maharam; and Maharik, shoresh 119), and the same applies to any similar case. However, if he gives the money by way of a gift, and if the borrower wishes he can keep it for himself and not pay him, it is permitted, even though he afterward pays him. One who does not need to borrow money except for the benefit of the lender — he says to him, “Build this ruin for yourself, and everything that you spend on this is upon me to pay you; and as long as I do not pay you, live in it for free” — it is permitted, since there is no loan here, only that he did [this] for the benefit of the lender (Beit Yosef in the name of Ba’al HaTerumos).}
