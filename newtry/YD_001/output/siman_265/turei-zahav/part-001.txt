**** ENGLISH ****Between cutting the foreskin, etc. And in Tur it is written Rashbam innovated to practice blessing to bring him in before circumcision specifically — for "to bring him in" implies the future; and further one must bless on all mitzvot preceding their performance; and Rabbeinu Tam wrote one need not change the custom, and his reason Rosh wrote: specifically if the performer of the mitzvah blesses then he must precede its performance — unlike here where it is done through another; and Rav Shalom too holds like Rabbeinu Tam, only he was stringent and said there is prohibition to bless until after circumcision since it is done through another; and Beit Yosef wrote Rabbeinu Tam too holds like Rav Shalom's stringency, for Tosafot and Rosh wrote in chapter Rabbi Eliezer of circumcision Rabbeinu Tam restored the custom to bless after circumcision, etc.; and to me on the contrary from there is proof — for they wrote initially they practiced blessing after circumcision, only Rashbam came and changed the custom, and Rabbeinu Tam was particular and said one should not change the custom, and on this all his proofs go to justify the custom — implies if not a change of custom he would not be particular; and Rosh's conclusion as written here: since he makes the blessing before uncovering it is also preceding its performance — he circumcised and did not uncover as if he did not circumcise; and it appears thus he fulfills both, since it is possible thus; but where this is impossible certainly one should precede its performance as much as possible, as I wrote in siman 1 seif 7; and so his language implies — he wrote this is called preceding its performance — implies even when done through another it is fitting to precede its performance as much as possible; and it seems to me the father who circumcises his son should act like Rashbam's view — bless first the two blessings on circumcision and to bring him in, and afterward cut — for between cutting and uncovering he cannot, since he is occupied in the middle of performing the mitzvah — how can he pause then in a blessing to bring him in; and further there is pain for the infant if they pause between cutting and uncovering and the cut fills with blood and with difficulty the uncovering is found afterward — end quote; therefore it appears in this certainly one should not act between cutting and uncovering — what can you say, that he should say to bring him in after uncovering? Behold even for Rabbeinu Tam and Rav Shalom there is prohibition in this, since he himself performs the mitzvah and certainly must precede its performance — end quote he should say both before cutting; and so I practiced myself when I circumcised my son may he live, and this is according to all; and afterward I found Maharil also ruled thus.
