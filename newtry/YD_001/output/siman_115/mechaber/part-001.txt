**** ENGLISH ****
The law of milk that a non-Jew milked and no Jew sees it, and cheese and butter; it contains 3 se'ifim. Milk that a non-Jew milked and no Jew sees it is forbidden lest he mixed impure milk into it. If he milked in his house and a Jew sits outside — if it is known that he has no impure animal in his flock, it is permitted even if the Jew cannot see him at the time he milks. If he had an impure animal in his flock and the Jew sits outside and the non-Jew milks for the Jew's need — even if he cannot see him while sitting, if he can see him when he stands, it is permitted, for he will fear lest he stand and be seen; and this is when he knows that impure milk is forbidden to a Jew. {Rama: And initially the Jew must be present at the beginning of the milking and see in the vessel that there is no impure matter in the vessel they milk into (Issur VeHeter and Tur klal 45; and they practice to be stringent not to milk in a vessel that a non-Jew is accustomed to milk into, lest remnants of a non-Jew's milk remain (there); however b'dieved one need not be concerned for all this (Shach and s.k. 8). And maidservants who milk animals in a Jew's house or in their pen — wherever a non-Jew's house does not interrupt and one need not be concerned for impure matter, it is permitted even initially to leave them to milk even though no Jew is there at all, for since it is in a Jew's house or in his neighborhood one need not be concerned for impure matter (Shach siman 82 in the name of Rashi and Maharai; but if a non-Jew's house interrupts, a Jew must be there as though milking in a non-Jew's house (Issur VeHeter there). And even a minor boy or girl suffices, for the non-Jew is in awe of them (same there). And if a non-Jew milked some animals and no Jew was there and afterward a Jew came to the others — nowadays when impure milk is not found at all, it is permitted (hagahot Shach). However, if the Jew did not come there until after all were milked, it is like milk that a non-Jew milked that is forbidden even though there is no impure matter in the flock (Beit Yosef in the name of the Rif and in the name of Semak). Milk of a non-Jew forbids vessels in which it was cooked like any other prohibition even though it is only a doubt lest impure matter was mixed into it; and likewise their cheeses (responsum of Rashba siman 143 and Tur). But not their butter — even in a place where they practiced prohibition with it, it does not forbid vessels in which it was cooked nor its mixtures, for if it was mixed with permitted matter everything is permitted (Tur), as above regarding bread of a non-Jew; and see above siman 112 (responsum of Maharil 38 and Issur VeHeter there). Milk of gentiles that became forbidden does not help if they afterward make cheeses or butter from it, but it remains in its prohibition and everything made from it is forbidden (Mordechai Pesachim and Agudah there and responsum of Maharam siman 216 and Issur VeHeter and Rashba and Beit Yosef; and it appears to me that so too Rambam, and not as the Maggid).}

They forbade cheeses of gentiles because they curdle them with rennet of their slaughter that is nevelah; and even if they curdled them with herbs, it is forbidden. {Rama: And such is the custom and one should not breach the fence (Beit Yosef) unless in a place where they practiced leniency from olden times. And if a Jew sees the making of the cheeses and the milking, it is permitted (Agur chapter "Sho'el"). And such is the widespread custom in all these lands; and if he saw the making of the cheeses and did not see the milking, there is room to permit b'dieved, for one need not be concerned lest impure matter was mixed into it, since he made cheeses from the milk, for impure matter does not stand and certainly the non-Jew did not mix into it since his intent is to make cheeses (hagahot Ashiri Pesachim and Tur). Nevertheless it is forbidden to eat the milk thus (there).}

One does not protest against the people of the place who practice leniency with butter of a non-Jew; and if the majority of the people of the place practice prohibition, one should not change. And in a place where there is no custom — if he cooked it until the milk residues went away, it is permitted. {Rama: It is also permitted to cook it initially so that the milk residues go away (Beit Yosef). And one who goes from a place where they practiced leniency to a place where they practiced prohibition there — it is forbidden to eat it there (Tur).
