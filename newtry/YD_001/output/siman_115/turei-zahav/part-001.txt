"Even if the Jew cannot, etc." But nevertheless we require that he sit outside beside his flock; and so it is proved in the Gemara, for a baraita states — a Jew sits beside the flock of a gentile and the gentile milks for him and brings it to him; how so? If there is no impure matter in the flock it is obvious; and if there is impure matter in the flock, why? Always that there is impure matter, and it deals with "he sees him," etc.; and Mordechai wrote: if it is clear to us that there is no impure animal in the flock, one may take from that gentile even if a Jew does not see; and this is an error — for if so the Gemara should have asked: if there is no impure matter in the flock, why do I need a Jew sitting in the flock and seeing? Rather he explains: if there is not, etc. — it is obvious that sitting beside the flock suffices, end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote that such is the view of R' Yitzchak of Corbeil — that a Jew must be there at the beginning of the milking lest he mix or the gentile put impure milk in a vessel before the Jew comes, even if there is no impure matter in the flock; it appears that sight is required even when there is no impure matter, and sitting alone does not suffice, since Mordechai mentions language of sight; and it is difficult — what is the challenge: if there is impure matter, why? "There is room to permit b'dieved because there is no concern, etc." This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam that some poskim wrote in his name permit when it is known that the gentile milks for cheese, but not when he milks for eating or kutach; and Issur VeHeter ruled like him; and I did not reach the end of the Rama's view, for Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch already ruled like Rambam that even if they curdled it certainly with herbs it is forbidden because they did not distinguish — likewise forbidden in this since he was not at the milking; and further, one will come to permit also eating the milk — for in siman 110 Rashba forbade even a piece that is not fit because of a fit piece, for it is close to error, even in something important, even though all is d'rabbanan; all the more so here — who will discern to say the milk is forbidden since the cheese made from it is permitted? And further — in milk there is concern of mixture of impure milk, which is a d'oraisa prohibition; and the Rama himself wrote nearby that one should not breach the fence even in a place of permission; and in Levush I saw that he wrote on this — that which he forbade nearby even in a place where they make in heter certainly — that is, other reasons combine there; and I did not know the reason to add to this — since it is known that it was made in complete heter, the prohibition is only because they did not distinguish — likewise one should forbid because they did not distinguish; what difference to me whether he was there or not, since it is known that it was made in heter, and nevertheless we forbid it; and there is no proof from Issur VeHeter who did not bring Rambam's words at all; necessarily it appears to my humble opinion that one should not be lenient in this either, and one should not permit unless a Jew was at the milking according to Rambam; and further — Beit Yosef wrote that even now there is prohibition according to those who hold because of exposure according to Rabbeinu Tam, as I copied nearby in name of Semak:

"Butter of a gentile, etc." In butter they were lenient in a place where there is no concern of prohibition — that is, of impure milk; it is obvious that one need not be concerned that it does not curdle; and if you say that he first mixed impure milk in order not to make butter but only to eat milk — nevertheless, since they cook it, the impure milk residues that are in it go away, and what did not go and remained there is nullified in its minority; and even though regarding cheeses we forbid nearby even in a place of permission — butter is different, for it was never within the decree in the mishnah; necessarily one cannot say here "the rabbis did not distinguish." And in Issur VeHeter end of siman 59 — for places that forbid, because they are not careful to mix prohibition into it and bowls that are not well scraped; and as the Rama wrote — permitted to cook l'chatchila according to Beit Yosef's words, for this is not nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, for the intent is only to consume the prohibition; and if a little remains by chance it is nullified in the majority — I wondered, for here we well nullify a prohibition l'chatchila since it is a d'rabbanan prohibition, as Maggid Mishneh wrote chapter 15 of Forbidden Foods; and these matters are according to Rambam as explained in Beit Yosef; and Rambam holds that in every d'rabbanan prohibition one nullifies l'chatchila whenever it is not a Torah prohibition proper, as Kesef Mishneh wrote chapter 15 of Forbidden Foods; and necessarily Rambam wrote this specifically b'dieved — and that is, even though this prohibition is d'rabbanan, nevertheless it is not proper to take from the gentile in order to nullify the prohibition; and as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Raavad; therefore Rambam wrote that if one took the butter — language of b'dieved:
