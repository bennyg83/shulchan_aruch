And in a place, etc. Language of the Rambam: it appears to me that if one took the butter from the gentile and cooked it until the milk residues went away, this is permitted, for you cannot say they became mixed and were cooked — they are nullified in their minority; but butter that gentiles cooked is forbidden on account of absorption from gentile vessels, as is written; and the Raavad challenged him for nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila in a majority; and in several places it is written: because there are many doubts — perhaps it did not become mixed; and if you say it became mixed, perhaps none remained; and if you say some remained, perhaps it will be melted on the fire. Rather they decreed to distance from their prohibitions more than Torah prohibitions; and this is what is meant by because they curdle in skin, etc., even though it is not by majority; and there is no difficulty from all this to the Rambam, for there is no doubt at all in cheese even for Rabbi Chanina, for which reason he said it is impossible without, etc.; and Shmuel who said because they curdle, etc., wrote there because a curdling agent even in a thousand is not nullified; and he further challenged him that if so cheese is more so in cooking; and he himself already resolved this: cheese is forbidden everywhere on account of a decree; and so milk; and as is written seif 62 according to Tosafot that if he milked with intent to make cheese the cheeses are permitted if a Jew made them; and nevertheless the milk itself is forbidden; and so are the Rambam's words; and because of this, etc., that all milk, etc., and cheese, etc.; and in Tosefta chapter 5 it implies like the Rambam's words: it is taught there one does not buy cheese of Beit Hinayki except from the expert; and burnt cheese is bought from anywhere — meaning even from a suspected Jew; and the same applies to butter even from a gentile, for although cheese is forbidden from a gentile on account of the decree they do not distinguish as above, not so with butter. And Beit Yosef decided: in a place where there is no custom one should rely on the Rambam's words.
