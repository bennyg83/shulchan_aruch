**** ENGLISH ****It negated. Taz wrote that even for us, who are stringent even when seeing a drop of blood like mustard, she needs seven clean days — what is the practical difference? The Mechaber wrote this law of negation; and it appears there is practical difference also now: per what we hold, a woman who has a veset need not check at all, as written beginning siman 187, for she is in presumption of purity; not so regarding seven clean days — we hold here that lechatchila she needs checking, for she is in presumption of impurity until completion of the seven clean days; therefore he informs us here that presumption of impurity is upon her until seven consecutive days are completed; lest you say that when the seven clean days end she returns to presumption of purity and need not check even lechatchila — he informs us that even if she sees at the very end of the seven she negates everything; if so, her original presumption is still upon her and she needs checking lechatchila also at that time.

**** ENGLISH ****Stain. Meaning, a pure woman who finds a stain — her law is like an actual sight also for this; but a woman who wore whites and found a stain, and even an actual sight within the days of clean days — she need not wait six onot but counts anew seven clean days from the day she can. Taz. And Shach wrote that it appears regarding a stain one may be lenient where she did not have relations, to count from the morrow of finding the stain, for this case of not having relations itself is a distant decree; therefore one should be stringent only regarding actual sight but not regarding rabbinic stains, for we follow a lenient approach in many places (and see responsum Sha'y part 2 question 77, who ruled regarding a woman who immersed and her husband was not in the city on the night of her immersion and she found a stain — he rules for that day and counts from the morrow, even though the custom is to be stringent; nevertheless in such a case, where she did not have relations and her husband is not in the city, lean toward leniency in stains; and so responsum Pnei Yehoshua siman 12 end quote); and possibly for this the Rav did not write this; and some wrote now etc. after the law of stain only after the law of sight; but nevertheless what Bach wishes, to be lenient even in actual sight when the husband is not in the city — his words do not appear correct, and so is the settled custom in these lands to be stringent even when the husband is not in the city; and greater than this, the custom that even a bride does not count seven clean days until day five of her sight, even though the groom has not yet come to the city; and even though per my view there is no reason to be stringent at all, nor is it mentioned in any early or late posek — nevertheless let it be only among permitted matters where others practiced prohibition; and nevertheless in a time of pressure one may permit a bride that immediately when she stops in purity she counts seven clean days — for thus is better than the custom that they marry while she is niddah, which Rambam ruled she should not marry at all until pure; and even though we are not meticulous so, as written in Even HaEzer siman 61 — nevertheless certainly where possible in purity is better; and Taz wrote that it appears to be lenient regarding a widow who declared niddah near her chuppah, and even after the chuppah before she secluded with her groom she does not wait at all, for in this certainly there is no reason to decree lest she had relations as if she had relations, since she has not yet entered complete relations with him — why decree such? end quote.
