**** ENGLISH ****
What is a shevuat bitui and a shevuat shav. It contains 6 se'ifim:

"A soul when it swears"—to express with the lips, meaning that one swore, whether regarding a past matter, such as if he swore that he ate and he did not eat, or that he did not eat and he ate; whether regarding a future matter, such as that he will eat and he does not eat, or that he will not eat and he eats; and similarly any matter, even if there is in it neither harm nor benefit, such as "I threw into the sea and I did not throw," or "I will throw and I will not throw;" and even regarding a matter that has no substance, such as "I slept and I did not sleep," or "I will sleep and I will not sleep;" and even regarding a matter that he cannot do except through others, such as "I gave to so-and-so" or "I did not give," or "I will give to so-and-so" or "I will not give;" and even if he swore about others, such as "So-and-so threw a stone into the sea" or "did not throw"—all these are shevuot bitui, and if he lied in his oath he is liable for a korban.

There are matters regarding which, even if he lied in his oath, he is not liable for a korban—such as if he swore to fulfill a mitzvah and did not fulfill it; or if he swore to harm another, such as to strike him or to curse him.

(But if he swore to strike himself, even though he is not permitted, the oath takes effect.) (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran chapter 3 of Shevuot.)

And if he swore that his fellow will do a certain matter, such as that so-and-so will throw a stone into the sea or will not throw, and the like—he is not liable for a korban. But there is a transgression in every matter in which he lies in his oath. And also, one may not swear that his fellow will do any matter that is not in his power that he do it in any case, and his fellow is not obligated to do it, unless he accepts his oath—that is, that he says "amen," or a word whose meaning is like the meaning of amen, such as if he said "yes," or "I am obligated by this oath," or "I accept this oath upon myself," and similarly all comparable cases. And even though the others did not accept the oath upon themselves, if they fulfilled his words, behold, they are praiseworthy.

{Rama: And all the more so the one who swore himself must fulfill his oath if possible, even though initially it was a shevuat shav, such as if he swore to marry a certain woman and perhaps she will not consent and there is a shevuat shav—nevertheless, when she consents he must fulfill his oath. (Maharik shoresh 22.) And some say that in such a case it is not called shevuat shav but only shevuat bitui—for one who swore to give something to his fellow and he does not wish to accept it, he is exempt from his oath; and the same law if she does not wish to marry him; therefore it is only shevuat bitui. (So it is implied from the responsum of Rivash siman 387, which Beit Yosef brought.)}

{Rama: And even if this one who passed the date claims he was under compulsion, he is not believed that his counterpart is still bound by his oath. And it appears that if two swore together and did not swear to one another, and one transgressed, the second is obligated to fulfill his oath—this applies specifically when they are not dependent on one another; but if they are dependent on one another and one cannot fulfill his oath without his fellow, such as if they swore to go together to Eretz Yisrael and one transgresses and it is difficult for the second to go alone—he is also exempt, as explained above siman 228 seif 44.
