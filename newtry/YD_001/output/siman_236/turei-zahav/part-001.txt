<b>שבועות ביטוי. </b>פי' שאלו נכללים בכלל נפש כי תשבע לבטא בשפתים וגו' והיינו כל דבר שבידו לעשותו דכתיב ביה להרע או להיטיב מה הטבה רשות אף הרעה רשות דהיינו לעצמו כגון שלא אוכל משא"כ דבר שביד אחרים שכ' אחר כך וכן הרעה לאחרים אינה בפסוק של ביטוי אלא נכללים בשבועת שוא ואין שם קרבן אלא עבירה היא בידו:

This excludes something dependent upon another person, discussed later. Likewise, doing harm to another is not included in the verse of an oath of expression, but within a vain oath. There is no offering for it, though he has committed a transgression.

<b>מכל מקום כשהיא רוצה כו'. </b>ואם אינה רוצה הוא חייב משום שבועת שוא שבשעה שנשבע שישאנה נשבע לשוא שהרי אין בידו לכופה לכך ונראה דמודה דעה זאת באתן מנה לפלוני שאין שם שבועת שוא שהרי יש בידו הנתינה עכ"פ ואם לא ירצה חבירו לקבלו הרי הוא לא נשבע על הקבלה משא"כ בשבועה שישאנה שאי אפשר לו כלל לקיים הנשואין כל שהיא מעכבת והי"א ס"ל דגם שבועה שאשאנה לא הוה לשוא דהוא דומיא לאתן רק שחייב לתבעה שתנשא לו ואם לא תרצה לא נתבטלה שבועתו כלל ואין עליו עונש כי לא נתחייב אלא להנאת המפציר בו שיתן לו מנה או שישא את האשה הזאת ואם הם אינם רוצים נקי הוא כן הוא תוכן דברי ריב"ש סימן שפ"ז שהוא בעל היש אומרים כאן ואם אמר שבועה שלא אשא שום אשה זולת פלונית ואחר כך לא רצתה לישאנו לכאורה יכול לישא אחרת בלא התרה שהרי מצוה לישא אשה והוא נשבע לבטל את המצוה דלא חיילא אבל מדברי הר"ן שהבאתי בסימן רל"ז סעיף ו' למדתי שצריך התרה דחיילה השבועה מחמת כולל דהא כל שהיא תרצה להנשא חל עליו איסור אחרת משום הכי גם עכשיו שהיא אינה רוצה מכל מקום השבועה חלה וצריך התרה כן נראה לי ואע"ג דבלאו הכי יש כולל כיון שחל על נשים שלא יוכל לקיים מצות פריה ורביה כמ"ש רמ"א סי' רל"ט סעיף ח' מ"מ נפקא מיניה אם נשבע בפירוש מכל אשה שהיא בת בנים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, when she wishes, etc. </b>If she does not wish to marry him, he is liable for a vain oath according to this opinion, because when he swore to marry her he swore to something not within his power, as he cannot compel her. It appears that this opinion agrees regarding one who says, “I will give one hundred zuz to so-and-so,” that it is not a vain oath. He can perform the act of giving. If the other person refuses to accept, he did not swear concerning acceptance. Marriage differs, because he cannot perform it at all while she refuses. The alternative opinion holds that even “I will marry her” is not vain, similar to “I will give.” He is obligated to ask her to marry him, and if she refuses his oath is not nullified and he bears no punishment. He obligated himself only for the benefit of the person urging him to give the money or marry the woman. If they refuse, he is free of blame. This is the substance of the Rivash, siman 387, who is the authority behind the alternative opinion. If one swore, “I will not marry any woman except so-and-so,” and she later refused to marry him, at first glance he may marry another without release, because there is a mitzvah to marry and an oath to nullify a mitzvah does not take effect. However, from the Ran cited in siman 237, seif 6, I learned that release is required. The oath takes effect through inclusion, because whenever she is willing to marry him, the prohibition against every other woman applies. Therefore, even now that she refuses, the oath remains in effect and requires release. So it appears to me. Although the oath already includes women through whom he cannot fulfill procreation, as the Rama writes in siman 239, seif 8, there is a practical difference if he explicitly swore concerning all women capable of bearing children.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He must be careful regarding his oath. </b>So wrote the Rambam. The Tur objects: I do not understand, for a person cannot prohibit his own property to another except through a konam. The Rambam may be reconciled according to the Ramban at the end of siman 206, who holds that a vow expressed in oath language and an oath expressed in vow language are effective. The other person must therefore be careful. The Rambam concludes that if the other person enters, he is the transgressor, while the one who swore is considered under duress.

<b>שהוא גוזמא קצת כו'. </b>אין לדברים אלו הבנה דכל שאינו אמת גמור היאך ישבע להחזיק אותה) ובלבוש כתב בזה לפי שדרך העולם להחזיק דבריהם בקצת גוזמא גם זה אינו מכוון דמכל מקום היאך ישבע על זה כיון שאינו אמת ומקור דין זה הוא ממרדכי פרק שבועות שתים והכי איתא התם בירושלמי אמר חזקיה הדין דמשתבע על תרין שהן תרין לוקה משום שבועת שוא והקשה רשב"ם דמתני' אמרה דוקא כשנשבע על דבר שאינו אמת אבל על דבר אמת אין כאן שוא ותירץ דהיכא דהוה קצת גוזמא להחזיק דבריו אין זה שבועת שוא אבל נשבע על תרין דאינון תרין שמאמת מה שא"צ הרי זה שבועת שוא עכ"ל הרי שלא בא להתיר רק מי שנשבע על דבר שהוא אמת רק בעיני בני אדם הוא קצת גוזמא ואין מאמינים לו אין בזה שבועת שוא שהשבועה היא צריכה לו וכ"ש אם הוא יותר גוזמא שיכול לישבע שהוא אמת דאין שבועה זאת בחנם אא"כ נשבע על מה שהוא פשוט בעיני הכל שהוא אמת שהשבועה היא בחנם ואם נתכוין גם רמ"א לזה לא הוצרך לכתבו דהא כבר כתב המחבר בסעיף ד' על דבר שהוא ידוע לכל ונכללו בו דברי המרדכי ותו דלא היה לו לרמ"א לכתוב זאת בסעיף זה דמיירי מדברים שהם שקרים בעיני העולם ועל כן אין ללמוד שום קולא מן הג"ה זאת להשבע על דעתו שהוא קורא שם על איזה דבר שלא כדרך העולם ובהדיא אסיקנא ברי"ף פרק שני דשבועות דלא אזלינן בתר דעתיה דהוא מכנה שם זה דכיון דכ"ע לא קרי ליה גמלא בטלה דעתו אצל כל אדם והוי ליה שבועת שוא ע"כ אלמא לא מהני מה שחושב בדעתו אלא כשכל עולם או רוב קורין אותו השם לאותו דבר שקורא הוא אותו אבל אם מקצת קורין אותו כך בטלה דעתו ג"כ דדוקא לענין ממון אמרינן ריש פרק המניח דרובא קרי לכדא כדא כו' דלא אזלינן בממון בתר הרוב אבל לא בשבועה החמורה כן נ"ל פשוט והרוצה ללמוד קולא מהג"ה זאת אין לו שורש ועיקר וכמדומה לי שרמ"א לא עיין בדברי המרדכי עצמו רק בסימני המרדכי על כן כתב כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That it is somewhat exaggerated, etc. </b>These words are incomprehensible. If the statement is not completely true, how may he swear in order to strengthen it? The Levush writes that since people ordinarily strengthen their statements with slight exaggeration, this too is allowed. That is also imprecise, for how may one swear to something untrue? The source is the Mordechai in the chapter “Two types of oaths.” The Yerushalmi states that one who swears that two are two receives lashes for a vain oath. The Rashbam asks that the Mishnah speaks specifically of an oath concerning something untrue, whereas an oath concerning truth is not vain. He answers that where a statement seems somewhat exaggerated and he swears to strengthen it, it is not a vain oath. But if he swears that two are two, affirming what requires no affirmation, it is vain. Thus, the Mordechai intended to permit only an oath concerning something actually true, but which appears exaggerated to people and is not believed. The oath is needed to establish the truth. All the more so, if the matter appears highly exaggerated but is true, he may swear, for the oath is not purposeless. Only when he swears to something plainly known by everyone does the oath become vain. If this was the Rama’s intent, there was no need to write it, for the Mechaber already ruled in seif 4 concerning something known to everyone, which includes the Mordechai’s rule. Nor should the Rama have written it in this seif, which concerns matters regarded by the world as false. Therefore, no leniency may be learned from this gloss to swear based upon a private terminology that differs from ordinary speech. The Rif explicitly concludes in the second chapter of Shevuos that we do not follow a person’s private understanding when he calls an object by an unusual name. Since everyone does not call it a camel, his view is nullified to the common usage, and it is a vain oath. His private intention helps only when everyone, or at least the majority, calls the object by that name. If only a minority does, his usage is likewise nullified. Only in monetary law, at the beginning of Bava Kamma, do we say that although the majority calls one vessel by one name, we do not follow the majority because monetary law does not follow a majority. A severe oath is different. This appears obvious. Anyone who derives leniency from this gloss has no foundation. I suspect the Rama saw only the Mordechai’s abbreviated index and not the Mordechai’s full words.

<b>The second person is exempt from his oath. </b>The Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham and the Sar of Shantz. <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="2"></i>Rabbeinu Yeruham brought proof from the first chapter of Sotah. It appears that the proof is from the end of that chapter, where Moshe said to Yosef: The oath Hashem swore that He would redeem us has arrived, and the oath you imposed upon Israel has arrived. If you reveal yourself, well and good. If not, we are released from your oath. Why did Moshe first mention Hashem’s oath to redeem them? It would have sufficed to say that the oath Yosef imposed had arrived. The reason is that Moshe wished to conclude that if Yosef did not reveal himself, they would be permanently free of the oath. Without the introductory statement, they might still have been required to return on another occasion for his bones. Yosef had said, “You shall bring my bones up from here with you.” Perhaps “with you” meant only that if possible they should take them along, but otherwise they should return later. However, Yosef had also said, “Hashem will surely remember you, and you shall bring up,” which appears superfluous unless the oath was fundamentally dependent upon that redemption. Moshe therefore began by saying that the time of redemption had arrived. If Yosef did not reveal himself then, they were fully released. From this we learn a general principle. When an oath contains a condition upon which the essence of the matter depends, the condition must be fulfilled. If it fails, even through duress, the oath does not remain. For example, if one contracts with another to supply specified merchandise for a fair, when that is the time it can be sold and not afterward, and the supplier is prevented from delivering it at that time but can deliver later, the buyer is certainly not obligated to accept it later. Although duress exempts the supplier from a penalty he undertook if he failed to deliver on time, for he never obligated himself under such circumstances, the buyer likewise committed only if the goods arrived at the crucial time. If the condition is not essential and duress prevents timely fulfillment, the underlying agreement is not void. We find a similar distinction regarding a sale for a particular time where the seller presses for payment and it is not paid, in Choshen Mishpat, siman 190, seif 16. Seif 15 explains this applies when it is known that he sold only because he needed the money. If not, the sale is not void. So too in many cases, only a condition essential to the act causes the act to be void when unfulfilled. If the act does not fundamentally depend upon it, the act remains. Therefore, when a man and woman swear to marry and fix a date in the oath, and duress prevents one from marrying then, the principal oath to marry is not void, because the match does not fundamentally depend upon that date. Further proof is siman 228, seif 41, in the Rosh’s name, that even after the date passes the oath remains. From the Rashba and the responsum of the Ramban cited there, it is clear according to all opinions that the marriage oath is not void merely because the date passed. If one party intentionally delayed and violated the oath to marry by the date, the other is entirely released, even if the first later wishes to marry, because each party’s oath depended upon the other’s fulfillment. If one intentionally violates even a minor element, the corresponding oath is void. When both were prepared but duress prevented one, however, the oath remains and they must marry after the duress passes. If the duress is of a kind that clearly was not included in the oath, he should seek release from a sage. This is clear. Therefore Mahariv, siman 136, cited by the Rama nearby, writes that if the date passed because of duress, the oath certainly was not void. He adds that when one failed to appear by the appointed date and it is not established that he was prevented, it is as though he stood before us and said he did not wish to marry at that time. Nevertheless, the other party should seek release. Although Mahariv writes that this release is merely an added precaution, it appears to be strict law. At the beginning of Kesubos the Sages enacted rules because of modest and immodest women, lest the husband have been prevented from appearing while she assumes he was not. Here too, perhaps he was prevented, so release is required. I write this because my revered father-in-law challenged Mahariv: The person accepted the cherem or handshake oath only on condition that the other appear at the agreed place and time. Since he did not appear, even through duress, the other bears no penalty and may marry someone else. He therefore preferred the Sar of Shantz and said release is only an extra precaution. These words are astonishing, as explained. The Sar of Shantz likewise did not permit a case of duress. He wrote “the one who obstructed,” implying intentional obstruction. Regarding duress, all agree that the other party is not released. The Rama’s ruling is primary and is applied daily. Mahariv also writes that we often see parties withdraw from a match and pay the penalty without release, and no one objects that the ancient communal cherem remains. Nevertheless, the customary engagement document says that the penalty does not exempt from the cherem, implying that they presently accept a new cherem in addition to the ancient one. It therefore appears that this new cherem requires release by a sage. So it appears to me.
