**** ENGLISH ****In any case, when she wants, etc. And if she does not want, he is obligated on account of a false oath — for at the time he swore he would marry her, he swore falsely, for it is not in his power to compel her. And it seems he concedes this view regarding "I will give a maneh to so-and-so" — there is no false oath, for giving is in his power in any case; and if his fellow does not want to accept, behold he did not swear on the acceptance. Not so an oath that he will marry her — for it is impossible for him at all to fulfill the marriage as long as she withholds. And the first opinion holds: also an oath "I will marry her" is not false — it is similar to "I will give"; only he is obligated to claim that she marry him, and if she does not want, his oath is not voided at all, and there is no punishment upon him, for he only obligated himself for the benefit of the one who urged him — that he give him a maneh or marry this woman; and if they do not want, he is clean. Such is the substance of Rivash siman 387, who is the holder of the "some say" here.

And if he said "oath that I will not marry any woman except so-and-so," and afterward she did not want to marry him — ostensibly he can marry another without release, for there is a mitzvah to marry a woman, and he swore to nullify the mitzvah — it does not take effect. But from Ran's words that I brought in siman 237 seif 6, I learned that release is needed, for the oath takes effect on account of inclusion — for whoever she wants to marry, prohibition of another applies to him; therefore also now that she does not want, nevertheless the oath took effect and release is needed. So it seems to me.

And even though otherwise there is inclusion, since it applies to women he cannot fulfill the mitzvah of procreation, as Rama wrote siman 239 seif 8 — nevertheless a difference emerges if he swore explicitly on every woman who is childbearing.

**** ENGLISH ****That it is somewhat exaggeration, etc. These words have no understanding — how can one swear to uphold it? And in Levush he wrote on this: because the way of the world is to uphold their words with some exaggeration — also this is not intended, for in any case how can he swear on this since it is not true? And the source of this law is from Mordechai, chapter Shevuot Shetayim; and so it states there in Yerushalmi: Chizkiyah said, the law that one who swears on two that are two is lashed for a false oath. And Rashbam challenged: the mishnah said specifically when he swore on a matter that is not true; but on a true matter there is no false oath here. And he answered: where there is some exaggeration to uphold his words, this is not a false oath; but one who swears on two that are two from truth that is not needed — behold this is a false oath — end quote. Behold he did not come to permit except one who swore on a matter that is true, only in people's eyes it is somewhat exaggeration and they do not believe him — in this there is no false oath, for the oath is needed for him. And all the more so if it is more exaggeration that he can swear it is true — this oath is not in vain, unless he swore on what is plain in everyone's eyes that it is true — then the oath is in vain.

And if Rama also intended this, he did not need to write it, for Mechaber already wrote in seif 4 on a matter known to all, and Mordechai's words are included. And further, Rama should not have written this in this seif, for it speaks of matters that are lies in the world's eyes, and therefore one cannot learn any leniency from this gloss to swear according to his mind that he calls a name on some matter not in the world's way. And explicitly we conclude in Rif, second chapter of Shevuot: we do not follow his mind — he calls this name; for since everyone does not call it a camel, his mind is voided before every person, and it is a false oath — end quote. Behold what he thinks in his mind does not help, except when the whole world or the majority call that name for that thing he calls. But if some call it thus, his mind is also voided — for only regarding monetary matters do we say in the beginning of chapter HaMenich that the majority call each as it is, etc., that we do not follow the majority in monetary matters; but not in a grave oath — so it seems to me plainly.

And one who wants to learn a leniency from this gloss has no root or essence; and it seems to me Rama did not examine Mordechai's words themselves, only the simanim of Mordechai — therefore he wrote thus.

The second is exempt from his oath. Beit Yosef likewise in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham and Sar Shalom; and he wrote Rabbenu Yerucham brought proof from chapter 1 of Sotah; and it appears the proof is from what we say at the end of chapter 1 of Sotah — Moses said to Joseph: the time has arrived of the oath the Holy One swore that I will redeem you; and the time has arrived of the oath you made Israel — if you show yourself, fine; if not, behold we are clear from your oath, etc.; and it appears from this proof to introduce a general rule in this law — I wondered why Moses extended to say the time arrived of the oath God swore to redeem us; it would suffice to say the time arrived of the oath you made us; rather it is clear he preceded this because he wishes to conclude: if not, we are clear from your oath forever; and if not for this introduction, Joseph would have had to go there another time after his bones — for although Joseph said at the time of the oath "and you shall bring up my bones from here with you," who says "with you" specifically? Perhaps he meant: if it is possible to take you, take you; if not, come another time; but since Joseph said "pakod yifkod Elohim etc. and you shall bring up" — which is seemingly superfluous language — certainly the essence of the oath depends on this visitation; therefore Moses preceded that the time of visitation arrived that they be redeemed — therefore we are entirely clear if you do not show yourself; and from this we learn for every similar case that if there is a condition in the oath and it depends on him, the condition must exist; and if it does not come about, he is exempt from the oath entirely; unlike one who swears he will not come to such-and-such place unless his fellow comes with him simultaneously — when he knows his fellow comes he is not exempt unless the condition is not an evil thing impossible at the time of the act; and one who swears he will not take possession of such-and-such field unless his fellow comes with him simultaneously — when he knows his fellow comes, he is not exempt unless the condition is not such an evil thing that he cannot be killed at the time of this act; and one is not killed at the time of the act unless he comes simultaneously — if the condition is not such an evil thing, he is not exempt; and from all this one may learn regarding one who swears regarding a matter that depends on his fellow's coming simultaneously — when he knows he comes, he is not exempt unless the condition is not impossible at the time of the act; and the essence of the oath depends on the condition's fulfillment — end quote.
