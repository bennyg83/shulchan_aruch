And the proof he brings from Moshe has no relevance here, for there Yosef did not swear at all; rather Moshe said they should be clean from the matter that they would be under duress, since he does not show himself. Also the difficulty he raised — why did Moshe speak so long — is not difficult at all; for therefore he said "the oath has arrived" — if the oath had not arrived, they would not be clean from his oath in what he does not show himself, for the time of deposit had not arrived. And Yosef adjured "pakod yifkod," and "you shall bring up my bones" — and they would transgress Yosef's oath, for they should have waited until God would remember them and take his bones, and then he would show himself.

Also in what he distinguishes between a condition upon which the essence depends, and a time condition and the like, and he challenged Beit Chadash — all his words are wondrous in this; and I already proved in my work in Choshen Mishpat siman 21 from Yerushalmi Kiddushin chapter HaOmer and from Ran there, that even with a time condition and such a case he is exempt — for it states there: "on condition I will betroth you on such-and-such day," and the day did not come and he did not betroth her, etc. — it shall be nothing; and rape occurred, and we rule like Rabbi Yochanan that rape is as if he did not act and she is not betrothed — see there. If so, it is obvious: since kiddushin of forbidden relations d'oraita are voided thus, all the more so an oath of marriage and monetary conditions and the like. And also there is no logic at all to distinguish thus — what of it that the essence of the match depends on this? Ultimately, since he tied the condition to this and the condition was not fulfilled, his fellow is not obligated — he only obligated himself on this condition, whoever it may be. And Bach's words are correct in this for practical law, for the reason I wrote, and not as he challenged. But what Bach challenged on Rabbenu in Hagahot Shulchan Aruch is not a challenge for another reason, as written below.

And what he wrote "and so we find regarding sale," etc., as in Choshen Mishpat siman 190, etc. — not comparable at all, for there it does not specify that he sells on this condition, which is not so here where it is specified thus. And what he wrote "and there is further proof from what is written in siman 228," etc., and as I brought there the words of Rashba and responsum of Ramban — also not comparable at all, for certainly he himself who swore is obligated to do so even though he already transgressed the oath; but his fellow opposite him was only obligated specifically on this condition, etc. And "these words of wonder," etc. — I already proved that on the contrary, his words are wondrous and Bach's words are correct and necessary, as written.

And "so we rule every day" is nothing — for in our conditions it is not written a condition that the match was made on condition the marriage be at a set time, and if it does not come at the set time the marriage is voided; certainly if they conditioned thus or it were written thus, the conditions would be voided even if there were duress. But in our conditions, initially the match was made in general, and afterward they condition and specify the time of marriage, and the like; and so written in the conditions — automatically, since initially the match was made in general and afterward duress occurred in other conditions, the conditions are not voided thereby, and his fellow must uphold the match; and thereby Maharam's words in Hagahot Shulchan Aruch are settled, and not as Bach challenged him — for the Rav refers to a man and woman who were matched to each other and accepted a cherem to marry at a fixed time, etc. — implies initially they were matched in general, and afterward accepted cherem to marry at a fixed time. But Mahariv's words are strained to explain thus, for it is written there that she gave a handclap to marry the matched one on the specified written condition, signed, etc.; and perhaps meaning she did not say so, only this condition is written thus — and sweet. And it is possible that Taz also speaks in such a matter, that it was not specified initially that he does so on this condition; but his words' plain meaning does not indicate thus. Also in any case his proofs are nothing, as I wrote; only for practical law the essential view seems as I wrote — and understand.
