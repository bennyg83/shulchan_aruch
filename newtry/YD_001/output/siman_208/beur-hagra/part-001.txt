<b>וא"צ שאלה כו'. </b>מדקתני בסיפא ואם נשאלו כו' ול"ק כאן אלא דוקא שם משום דמשמעותו להחמיר טפי מלהקל כמ"ש הר"נ שם ועתוס' ד"ה סתם כו' ולדעת הרמב"ם דאף במתפיס בדבר האסור להדיא צריך שאלה לע"ה כמש"ל סי' ר"ה א"צ למתני כאן דבלא"ה צריך שאלה ודברי ש"ע צ"ע ועש"ך שדחק בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he does not require release, etc.</b> This is inferred from the latter clause, which states, 'and if they sought release,' etc. The Mishnah does not state this here, but only there, because that expression is more readily understood stringently than leniently, as the Ran writes there. See Tosafot, s.v. 'an unspecified expression,' etc. According to the Rambam, who holds that even one who explicitly associates his vow with something forbidden requires release when he is an unlearned person, as explained above in siman 205, there is no need to teach this law here, since release would be required in any event. The words of the Shulchan Aruch therefore require study. See the Shach, who presses to resolve this.

<b>ואם רוב כו'. </b>דאז הוי כמו נדר בחרם כו' כיון דמשמעותו יותר לאסור כנ"ל וכמ"ש במתני' ס"ב ב' הכל לפי כו' ושם מ"ט א' מר כי כו' ושם נ"ב ב' נ"ג א' ועש"ך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if the majority, etc.</b> In that case it is like one who vows by a cherem, etc., because the expression is more readily understood as prohibitive, as explained above. This accords with the Mishnah on 62b, 'everything follows,' etc.; and see there, 49a, 'one Sage according to,' etc.; and there, 52b and 53a. See the Shach.

<b>ומיהו כו' ואם כו'. </b>כפי' הרא"ש שם דחכמים פליגי אתרווייהו דלא עונשין ולא מחמירין וכ"כ תוס' ד"ה וחכ"א כו' ע"ש והביאו ראיה מירושלמי וצ"ע הא דלעיל סי' ד"ה ס"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, etc.; and if, etc.</b> This follows the Rosh's explanation there that the Sages disagree with both positions, meaning that they neither punish him nor rule stringently. Tosafot likewise write this, s.v. 'and the Sages say,' etc.; see there. They bring proof from the Yerushalmi. This requires study in light of the ruling above in siman 205, seif 1.

<b>בד"א כו'. </b>כר' יוסי דהלכה כוותיה נגד ר"י כמ"ש כפ"ד דעירובין <small>(מ"ו מ"ז) </small>וכ"פ הרמב"ם ואע"ג דיש לחלק דדוקא בנזירות דסתם נזירות ל' יום משא"כ בסתם נדרים דל"ל קיצותא כמש"ש ד' ב' ושם ט"ו א' ושם כ"ח א' ואפשר דמודה ר"י מ"מ בתוספתא ספ"א דנדרים תני פלוגתייהו בנדרים. תוס' ד"ה מי וכ"כ ש"פ דלא כריב"ש אבל הטור פסק כר' יהודה ונ"ל דדעתו לפסוק כר' יהודה לגמרי כמ"ש תוס' שם דאע"ג דלכאורה נראה דהלכה כר' יוסי מ"מ כיון דבנזיר ל"ב א' הביא דר' יהודה סתם וקרי ליה רבנן הלכתא כוותיה וכ"פ בסי' של"ד סכ"ח ואע"ג דסתם נידוי ל' יום כמו בנזירות כמ"ש בפ"ג דמ"ק <small>(ט"ז א'):</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When does this apply, etc.</b> This follows Rabbi Yosi, because the halachah follows him against Rabbi Yehudah, as explained in the fourth chapter of Eruvin, 46b and 47a. The Rambam rules likewise. Although one could distinguish that this applies specifically to naziriteship, because an unspecified term of naziriteship is thirty days, unlike unspecified vows, which have no fixed term, as stated there on 4b, 15a, and 28a, and it is therefore possible that Rabbi Yehudah would agree regarding vows, nevertheless the Tosefta at the end of the first chapter of Nedarim records their dispute regarding vows as well. See Tosafot, s.v. 'who,' and so rule the other authorities, unlike the Rivash. The Tur, however, rules in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah. It appears to me that he intends to rule like Rabbi Yehudah entirely, as Tosafot write there. Although at first glance it would appear that the halachah follows Rabbi Yosi, nevertheless, since Nazir 32a cites Rabbi Yehudah's view anonymously, refers to it as the view of the Sages, and states that the halachah follows it, the Tur rules accordingly. He likewise rules this way in siman 334, seif 28, even though an unspecified term of excommunication is thirty days, just as an unspecified term of naziriteship is thirty days, as stated in the third chapter of Moed Katan, 16a.

<b>מי שעבר כו'. </b>כמ"ש בנדר בחרם בס"ב כיון שהוא מדרבנן אבל בסי' של"ד שם לא פסק כן כנ"ל משום דל"ד למתני' דמתני' אינו אלא חומרא בעלמא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who transgressed, etc.</b> This is like the ruling regarding a vow by a cherem in seif 2, because the prohibition is rabbinic. In siman 334, however, he did not rule this way, as explained above, because that case is not comparable to the Mishnah. The Mishnah's ruling is merely an added stringency.
