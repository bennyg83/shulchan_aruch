**** ENGLISH ****
Both. And Shach wrote: what the Mechaber wrote — if the knife has the measure of three necks, kosher — means whether he placed the knife at the beginning on the width of both heads and the knife length extended a full neck beyond both heads and drew to the other side until nothing remained protruding on that side, or he began at the knife edge and drew until a full neck extended beyond the second head — then certainly on both heads he passed two full necks, kosher; but plainly if he placed the knife on both heads' width and drew to the side that protruded beyond both heads a full neck — this is as if the knife has only two necks; and what the Mechaber wrote — if not, concern to forbid both — because since we see one darsahed, we say the second too darsahed, for in one act he passed over both; no difference even if he began at the edge and half the knife passed over the second — both forbidden, not like Bach who distinguished beginning at edge versus placing on both heads in unacceptable matters; and it also appears: if he began at the edge and passed to the other side so the entire knife passed over both — both kosher according to all, simple — not like Bach.

Thus. And Shach wrote: it appears to him the Rav's view is specifically where there is no great loss one should be stringent; but in great loss one may be lenient {Rama: (and Peri Chadash wrote: even in small loss the lenient did not lose; but Beit Yisrael wrote slaughterers now declare treifah even in great loss; and so by experience. Further: one should not slaughter a bird, even a large bird, with a knife used for animals, for easily he will come to darsah. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote to warn slaughterers not to slaughter seated, for sitting leads to darsah. Peri Chadash wrote: b'dieved, whatever he knows he did not darsah is permitted, and so Beit Yisrael. Once someone slaughtered many birds for a wedding resting a finger — Vilna sages permitted b'dieved due to great loss and mitzvah need. One who does not know the laws of shechitah must stand over him from start to finish, even in the last minority, lest he darsah — Kenesset HaGedolah)}. And Taz wrote one should be lenient in all doubts — even if one sees a cut in the spinal column, no need to check majority; all the more if the spinal column is not present — rely to be lenient; stringent only if they already saw true majority of spinal column was slaughtered, not in another matter; and this majority of spinal column — the essence depends on the cord life depends on.

**** ENGLISH ****
And from the flesh. And Shach wrote: but if the flesh tore with the simanim, even if all uprooted — he holds kosher. Maharim Tiktin wrote: according to the Mechaber, even for the view of one who invalidates what he mentioned above in seifim 10 and 12 — ikkur is not like darsah and haladah; what was slaughtered properly and shechitah fitness completed — the majority — if he uproots the remaining minority after shechitah, kosher, because it is not the manner of shechitah; and according to the gloss, who was stringent above in hagramah though not the manner of shechitah, he was stringent here too for ikkur. However, if it slipped before shechitah, we establish according to law; and this is what Rama wrote "but while alive," etc.; and from Rama's words it appears even if the majority dangled while alive, milk and eggs are kosher; but most poskim hold if the majority dangled it is actual treifah even while alive — Shach. Also what Rama wrote practical difference for milk — Peri Chadash, siman 14, s.k. 11, disagrees and forbids milk and eggs.

<b>לשנות. </b> וכ"פ רש"ל ובתשובת נ"ש סימן ס"ט ועיין תשובת חב"י שאלה כ"ה כ"ו כ"ז כ"ח. מיהו בנמצא על הארץ נסתפק בת' ש"י סי' ס"ז ובמ"ב סימן ק' הכשיר בנמצא על הארץ. וב"ח החמיר אף בנחתך הטבעת מצד א' וע"ש בט"ז שהאריך בזה ופר"ח הביא הפוסקים הנ"ל ומסיק ואנו מכשירים בכל גוונא כדעת המחבר. והע"י הביא ג"כ האחרונים הנ"ל ומסיק כשהוא מחותך מצד א' וצד השני מהטבעת מונחת על חוד הסכין וב' עוקצין מהטבעת על שני צדי הסכין שהוא כשר דרואין בחוש שלא נעקרו קודם שחיטה דהיאך נשחט הטבעת מצד א' אלא וודאי שהסכין עקר צד השני מחמת שלא היה הסכין חד כ"כ שיוכל לשחוט הצד השני ע"כ נעקר הטבעת לאחר רוב שחיטת הקנה. ואף כשנופלת החצי טבעת על הארץ או כשהיתה מונחת על הסכין שאין כאן בית מיחוש כלל וכשר אבל נמצא שלם בין על הסכין בין על הארץ הכל אוסרין גם כשהחתיכה מהטבעת תלויה בגרגרת ג"כ כשר וע"ש בש"י סימן מ"ו. וכתב בש"ך ואף אם נמצא על הארץ ג"כ טריפה ודלא כשו"ת משאת בנימין וכתב בט"ז פעם אחת בא לידי בא' ששחט אווז ומצא אחר השחיטה על חוד הסכין טבעת אחת דהיינו שהיתה חתוכה ושני צדדי החיתוך על שני צדדי הסכין והכשרתיו דאין סברא להחזיק ריעותא אלא כשרואין דלא נחתך הטבעת אלא בא כולו שלם על זה אנו אומרים שמא נדחף בלא שחיטה עיין בשו"ת שבות יעקב שאלה מ"ו דהסכים לפסק הט"ז ודלא כש"ך ועיין תשובת מהר"ם לובלין סימן כ"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
One should not deviate. And so Rashal; and in responsum of Nishmat Shabbat, siman 69; and see responsum of Chavot Bair, questions 25–28. However, if found on the ground — doubt in She'erit Yosef, siman 47, and in Mishnah Berurah, siman 100, he validated if found on the ground; and Bach was stringent even if the ring was cut on one side — see there; Taz was lengthy; and Peri Chadash brought the poskim and concludes we validate in every case like the Mechaber; and Pri Megadim also brought the latter authorities and concludes: when cut on one side and the other side of the ring rests on the knife tip with both points of the ring on both knife sides — kosher, for we see clearly they were not uprooted before shechitah — how could the ring be cut on one side unless the knife uprooted the other side because it was not sharp enough to slaughter the second side, therefore the ring was uprooted after majority of trachea slaughter; and even when half the ring falls on the ground or was on the knife, there is no pain concern at all and kosher; but if found whole on knife or ground — all forbid; also when the cut of the ring hangs on the crop — also kosher — see there in She'erit Yosef, siman 46; and Shach wrote: even if found on ground, treifah — not like Ma'aseh Binyamin; and Taz wrote: once a goose was slaughtered and after shechitah found on the knife tip one ring — cut on both sides — I validated, for no basis to hold presumption except when we see the ring was not cut but came whole — on this we say perhaps pushed without slaughter; see Shevut Yaakov, question 46, who agreed with Taz — not Shach; and see responsum of Maharam Lublin, siman 24.
