**** ENGLISH ****
<b>How does she establish it, etc.</b> See Tosafot, 64a, s.v. It was stated, etc., and Tosafot to Bava Kamma 37b, s.v. The day, etc., and likewise in several places. <br><b>(Supplement) How, etc.</b> From the Mishnah there, 63b, which states, “If she was accustomed,” etc., and “she changed three times,” etc., it is evident that she must repeat the pattern three times according to the intervals. One cannot answer that a woman who was already accustomed is different, for if so, when the Gemara there, 64a, challenges from “If she was accustomed,” etc., why did it cite this? It is necessarily different because she was already accustomed, as is clear from the Mishnah. Furthermore, from the subsequent teaching, “A woman does not establish,” etc., it is evident that there is no distinction between the establishment of the veset of this woman, who was previously accustomed, and that of another woman who was not previously accustomed. Thus writes Torat HaBayit. See Tosafot to Bava Kamma 37b, s.v. The day, etc. Although Shmuel said there, 63b, “They stated,” etc., that follows Rabbi, as Tosafot there explain, s.v. For days, etc., and as stated there in the Gemara. When the Gemara says there, “These are the words of Rabban Gamliel,” etc., it means that this Tanna holds accordingly, for Rav and Shmuel dispute there, 64a, and one says, “until she repeats it three times,” etc. The anonymous Mishnah there likewise follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and the halachah is expressly decided in the chapter HaBa al Yevimto, 64b, in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding vesetot. Thus writes Torat HaBayit. See Tosafot 63b, s.v. This, etc., and 11a, s.v. She jumped, etc. (End of supplement.)

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>She jumped and saw, etc.</b> This follows the latter version, which he understands as disputing the first version. All the poskim, however, write that it does not dispute the first version, for the latter version concerns a case in which she did not jump at all on Shabbos. The words found in our text of the latter version, “and on Shabbos she jumped but did not see,” are a scribal error copied from the wording of the Rambam. They are not found in the other textual versions. Rather, the Rambam explains that the latter version also concerns a case like that of the first version. The Rosh, Rashba, and the other poskim write that these two versions do not disagree. This is also the unqualified ruling below in seif 2.

The Rashba writes, however, that the other poskim rule like Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, and that even for a fixed veset she is not concerned during this period, as stated there. All the more so is this true for a non-fixed veset, since vesetot are rabbinic. Furthermore, the Gemara there says, “When Ravin came,” etc. The passage cited by the Raavad is no proof, because when a woman is presumed removed from bleeding, we may attribute a sighting to any minor cause even though she is not required to be concerned for that date at all. Nevertheless, the Rashba concludes that although this appears to be the primary ruling, one should take the Raavad's words into account.
