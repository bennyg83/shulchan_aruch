<b>הנשבע אם צריך לישבע בשם או בכינוי. ובו י"ב סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-order="1"></i>האומר אני נשבע שאעשה דבר פלוני או שלא אעשנו <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="1"></i>הרי זה <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="1"></i>שבועה אע"פ שלא הזכיר לא שם ולא כינוי: <small>הגה <i data-commentator="Beur HaGra" data-order="2"></i>ואין חלוק אם הוציא השבועה בלשון הקודש <i data-commentator="Siftei Kohen" data-order="2"></i>או בכל לשון שיאמרנה (תשובת הרשב"א סימן תתמ"ב ובמרדכי פ' שבועות שתים וברמב"ם פי"ב מהלכות שבועות)</small>:
**** ENGLISH ****
Whether one who swears must swear by the Name or by a substitute. It contains 12 se'ifim:

One who says, "I swear that I will do such-and-such," or "that I will not do it"—this is an oath, even though he mentioned neither the Name nor a substitute.

{Rama: And there is no distinction whether he uttered the oath in the holy tongue or in any language in which he says it. (Responsum of Rashba siman 442, Mordechai chapter Shevuot Shetayim, and Rambam chapter 12 of Hilchot Shevuot.)}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who says twice regarding one matter, "I will do it" or "I will not do it"—it is an oath, provided he intends it as an oath.

When is this stated? When he said them one after the other without interruption. But if they request it of him and he said, "I will not do it," and they requested again and he said, "I will not do it"—it is not an oath.

**** ENGLISH ****
One who swore by heaven and earth, sun, and the like—even though his intention is only to Him who created them, it is not an oath. The same applies to one who swore by a prophet of the prophets.

(Or by another writing from the holy writings.) (Rambam.)

Or by the table of the Temple or by one of the holy vessels. Nevertheless, they intimidate them and show them that this is an oath so that they not treat oaths lightly, and they annul them for them.

{Rama: And all this is when he did not take it in his hand. (Opinion of Beit Yosef.) And see above siman 212. The same applies if he did not grasp them in his hand but they were placed before him and he swore by what is written in them.}

A substitute for an oath is like an oath. How? "Shevuata" or "shevuqa" that I will not eat this loaf; or "momi" or "omi." But if he said "be-omta" or "be-omi" with a bet—it is nothing. {Rama: Those who are accustomed to say in the holy tongue "emunah, I will do" or "I will not do"—even though they are like vows of urging, nevertheless require annulment. (Hagahot Maimoni chapter 4 of Nedarim in the name of Maharam.)}
