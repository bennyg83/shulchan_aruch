<b>בכלי ראשון תוך מעת לעת. </b>בסימן צ"ה סעיף ג' יתבאר <i data-commentator="Nekudot HaKesef" data-label="♯" data-order="1"></i>דגם ע"י ערוי מכלי ראשון על ב' כלים אחד של חלב ואחד של בשר והם מלוכלכים יש איסור כמו בכלי ראשון ממש ומ"ה אם עירה נמי מכלי ראשון על כלי שהוא מלוכלך מבשר נחשב כאילו השתמש באותו כלי בכלי ראשון. וכתב רש"ל פכ"ה סי' ע"א דמסתבר דכלי ראשון כל זמן שאין היד סולדת בו אין בו כח להבליע כלל אלא דמסתפינא להקל בכלי ראשון שעומד אצל האש כל שהוא חם ואפילו אין היד סולדת בו ראוי לאסור דעשו הרחקה לכלי ראשון כדאיתא בירושלמי עכ"ל אבל בת"ח כלל נ"ז פוסק כב"י ואו"ה דאפילו בכלי ראשון שעל האש אינו אוסר אם אין היד סולדת בו וכן עיקר ועיין סי' ק"ה סעיף ב' מה שכתבתי על דברי רמ"א בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
In a kli rishon within twenty-four hours. In siman 95 seif 3 it will be explained that also by pouring kli rishon onto two vessels, one of milk and one of meat, and they are soiled, there is issur like kli rishon itself; therefore if one also poured kli rishon onto a vessel soiled from meat it is considered as if he used that vessel in kli rishon. And Rashal Piskei HaGahot siman 71 wrote: it seems that kli rishon — as long as yad soledes bo is not in it — has no power to absorb at all; but I am inclined to be stringent with kli rishon standing by the fire: as long as it is hot, even if yad soledes bo is not in it, it is fit to forbid, for they made distance for kli rishon as in the Yerushalmi — end; but Taz rules like Beit Yosef and Orach Chaim that even kli rishon on the fire does not forbid if yad soledes bo is not in it, and so is primary; and see siman 105 seif 2 what I wrote on Rama's words in this.

<b>בקדירה שני פעמים. </b>דהוה כאילו תחב שם ב' כפות של איסור ואם נודע בנתיים נתבטל הראשון ונעשה היתר גמור ובת"ה סי' קפ"ג נתן טעם להצריך שני פעמים ס' דחיישינן שמא לא פלט הכף תחילה כל הבלוע בתוכו מן האיסור ונשאר בתוכו חלק ס' ונכנס בתוכו מן ההיתר שבקדירה נ"ט חלקים ונאסר הבלוע בתוך הכף שנעשה הכל נבילה וצריך עוד ששים נגד זה שנתחדש בכף מתחיבה [ראשונה שפולט תוך הקדירה בתחיבה] שנייה ואח"כ דחה זה דאין היתר בלוע נאסר מאיסור בלוע ועוד האריך למצוא טעם להתיר בפעם א' ס' ורש"ל פ' גיד הנשה סי' מ' החמיר להצריך ב' פעמים ס' ואין לנו אלא המנהג שכתב רמ"א להקל הן בבשר בחלב הן בשאר איסורים:
**** ENGLISH ****
Into the pot twice. For it is as if two forbidden spoons were inserted; and if known in the interim the first was nullified and became fully permitted. In Terumat HaDeshen siman 283 he gave a reason to require two times of sixty: we are concerned lest the spoon did not first emit all the forbidden absorbed matter and part of sixty remained and permitted matter in the pot entered in parts and the absorbed matter in the spoon became forbidden and the whole became nevelah, and another sixty is needed against what renewed in the spoon from first insertion [that emits within the pot on second insertion]. And he rejected this, for forbidden absorbed does not forbid permitted absorbed; and he further sought a reason to permit one time of sixty. And Rashal perek Gid HaNasheh siman 40 was stringent to require two times of sixty — we have only the practice Rama wrote to be lenient, both in basar b'chalav and other issurim.

**** ENGLISH ****
Fruits or cold. Taz wrote in general rule 85 and Sha'arei Dura that even hot water to rinse the head or to do things not needed for eating and drinking is permitted, and so is the practice — unlike Mordechai perek Ein Ma'amidin that forbids using a moist thing; and he further wrote: per this practice one may be lenient also if a pot was forbidden from basar b'chalav and after twenty-four hours they cooked food in it — one need not throw the pot's value into the river as Maharai ruled — until here; and Rashal perek Kol HaBasar siman 46 disagrees with Rama, for the practice is not to warm but only to rinse the head in general, not water fit for any food and drink; and there is proof that rinsing the head is permitted even l'chatchila, etc.; therefore one must throw the pot's value into the river — until Rashal's words. And all this in a forbidden pot; but forbidden food — Issur VeHeter HaAroch and Rashal and Taz wrote one must throw it specifically in the latrine, not before a dog even if not his.

**** ENGLISH ****
It does not forbid except the amount of a peel. Tur likewise in the name of R' Peretz; and from Tur's arrangement here it seems there is a textual error, and it should read: if it is ben yomo — if not, for presumably it is not ben yomo, since default vessels are not ben yomo — permitted, but a peel is required; and so in Hagahot Semak, and he wrote the reason is grease on the knife — default knives have grease on them. And it is difficult at first glance: in the first part, if there is sixty against the knife's place, why is a peel needed here if there is sixty against that grease on the knife — and what does a peel help if it carries taste throughout like the first part? Me'orei Or siman 105 wrote: if there is no sixty against the drop, all is forbidden even though the issur is slight; except it is clear that certainly there is sixty against the issur, or se'ah terumah in a hundred; but the difference is: in the first part, where the issur is from the knife's emission, one had to say it forbids up to sixty; but in the second part, permitted from the knife, only from grease on it, and it is a slight matter and there is sixty in the measure of a peel against it — therefore he did not need to mention sixty here since in any case a peel is required; and even in the first part where there is sixty against the whole knife, nevertheless a peel is required at the place the issur fell — slight in roasting where the issur is more attached than elsewhere; therefore that place must be peeled, and from this Rashba learned for every roasting where slight issur fell — sixty and also a peel; and so in Mordechai end perek Gid HaNasheh on Pesach that a peel is required even more than sixty; and so Ran perek Kol HaBasar; and so ruled Shulchan Aruch siman 105 seif 5. For this intent Tur wrote in the name of R' Peretz that a peel is required — meaning also in the first part where there is sixty against the knife's place; and he did not divide into two sections, but taught us: when it is not known to be ben yomo we go to leniency and sixty is not needed against the knife's place, but against the grease in actual form it is clear there is sixty and he did not need to mention it, and he concluded "but a peel is required" — both of them; and Shulchan Aruch that brought in the second part "it forbids only a peel" intended this too. I further saw in Hagahot Ashiri perek Gid HaNasheh on roasting requiring removal: he wrote there likewise what we said in perek Keitzad that grease on the knife is a slight matter, as in "salting is different, a little," etc. — end. There too he speaks after there is sixty; only concern from the place of falling in roasting here a peel suffices and full removal of the place is not required — so appears to me plainly; if there is no sixty against grease on the knife, all is forbidden, and one who is lenient is only among the astonished.
