**** ENGLISH ****
That one may not dress like non-Jews; it contains 3 se’ifim. We do not walk in the statutes of non-Jews (and we do not resemble them) (Tur in the name of Rambam). And one should not wear a garment that is designated for them, and one should not grow the lock of his head like the lock of their head, and one should not shave from the sides and leave hair in the middle, and one should not shave the hair opposite his face from ear to ear and leave the forelock, and one should not build places like the structure of the palaces of idolatry, so that many will enter them as they do. {Rama: Rather, he should be distinct from them in his garments and in his other deeds (there). And all this is forbidden only regarding something that non-Jews practice for the sake of licentiousness — for example, they practice to wear red garments, which is the garment of ministers, and similar, from garments of licentiousness — or regarding something that they practice as a custom and as a statute with no reason in the matter, for there is concern in it for “darchei ha’Emori,” and that it has a trace of idolatry from their ancestors. But something that they practice for a benefit — for example, their practice is that anyone who is an expert doctor has a special garment by which it is recognizable that he is a professional doctor — it is permitted to wear; and likewise, that which they do because of honor or another reason is permitted (Maharik, shoresh 88). Therefore they said that we burn [objects] for kings, and there is no concern of “darchei ha’Emori” in it (Ran, first chapter of Avodah Zarah).}
