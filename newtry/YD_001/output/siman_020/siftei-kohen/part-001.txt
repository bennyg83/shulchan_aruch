<b>And if he slaughtered from the sides, etc.</b> Thus wrote Beit Yosef; and it appears from the words of Tosafot that if he did not return the simanim it is invalid—for thus they wrote: "one who slaughters from the sides—we find to say specifically b'dieved; but l'chatchila, even if he returned the simanim, we decree lest he not return"; and Ra'osh wrote: "provided he knows he cut the simanim before he cut the spinal column, for the simanim are soft and pushed aside by the knife"; and it appears from his words that even if he returned he must know; and even if he did not return, as long as he knows—it is kosher—end; and he brings it in Darkei Moshe; and what he wrote that it appears from Tosafot that if he did not return it is invalid—in my humble opinion it is so, for if without return it were kosher, the decree "lest he not return" would not apply, since even without return itself it is kosher b'dieved and mishap does not come from this; and we do not find anywhere a decree like this; and so agreed in Sefer Torat Chaim; and so in Sefer Zivchei Tzedek on the end of Tosafot and Mordechai; and I cannot descend to the end of the view of Maharash Idelstein in his chiddushim and Bach who challenged Beit Yosef on this; and that which they challenged—behold the mishnah teaches at the end "one who slaughters from the nape—his slaughter is invalid," and the Gemara establishes it specifically without return; if so, what difference is there between the beginning and the end? It is not a difficulty—for at the end his slaughter is always invalid since he slaughters from the nape, which is not so in the beginning, which deals specifically with knowing—as Ra'osh and Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote: kosher even without return; only Tosafot say that even if he returned, l'chatchila we decree "lest he not return" from the default case, for then it is invalid—as Ra'osh and his supporters wrote; and so wrote Agudah chapter 1 of Chullin siman 25 explicitly etc.: "one who slaughters from the sides—his slaughter is kosher specifically b'dieved, and he should return the simanim"—end; behold without return even b'dieved it is invalid; and this is specifically from the default case. However, what Beit Yosef wrote that it appears from Ra'osh's words that even if he returned he must know—in my humble opinion it is not so at all, for it is not reasonable to invalidate since he returned the simanim and certainly cut them before the spinal column; and behold Rashi and Ran chapter 1 of Chullin validate even without return—even if he did not know—because certainly from the default case he slaughtered them before the spinal column; and so appears the view of Rashba in teshuvah ha'arokhah daf 26a; if so, why should we say Ra'osh argues—a distant dispute like this? Rather it appears that therefore Ra'osh explained "provided he knows"—because the Gemara establishes the mishnah without return; but he agrees that with return it is kosher in the default case, as Agudah holds (and as is proven from Tosafot who wrote "lest he not return" since they did not say "lest the return itself," and understand); and so it appears from the words of Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote "and if he returned the simanim behind the nape—it is kosher," and they did not condition knowing as they conditioned in the beginning; rather certainly as I said—and understand well, for all this in my humble opinion is clear: that now Tosafot, Ra'osh, his supporters, and Agudah stand on one view; only it is difficult on the Mechaber in what appears from his words that knowing alone does not help—this is against the Gemara that establishes the mishnah without return; and in any case knowing helps (and according to what is written in Sefer Torat Chaim it is possible the beginning of the mishnah deals specifically with return—but his words are very forced—see there and understand). And one must say that what the Mechaber wrote "and know" is a vav of division, meaning: and that is, he returned or he knew; and what he wrote afterward "and the same applies to one who slaughters from the nape" refers only to return; and the words of Pri Chadash cannot be reconciled thus—see there; and see in siman 24 seif 5 and what is written there.
