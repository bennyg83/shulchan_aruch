<b>שבסוף הקנה כו'. </b>ז"ל כל בו בשם הרב הברצלו"ני ענין שיפוי כובע הוא שע"פ הטבעת הגדולה יש שם בשר ופי הבשר עשוי חטים ועל אותן חטים כיסוי שדומה לעלה של הדס ועליה עצם אחד מטבעת הגדולה ומכסה סביב זה הבשר כמו כובע ובאמצע זה העצם יש שיפוי אחד בגוף העצם ונקרא שיפוי כובע ואמרו רבנן משיפוי כובע ולמטה כשירה שהוא כמו אצבע למעלה מטבעת הגדולה עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That is at the end of the trachea, etc.</b> Thus wrote Kol Bo in the name of Rav Bar Tzeloni: the matter of hat slope is that opposite the large ring there is flesh there, and the mouth of the flesh is made like wheat grains, and upon those grains there is a covering similar to a hadas leaf, and upon it one bone from the large ring surrounds this flesh like a hat; and in the middle of this bone there is one slope in the body of the bone and it is called hat slope; and the sages said: from hat slope downward is kosher, for it is about a finger above the large ring—end of his words.

<b>בלי שתאנוס עצמה. </b>בגמרא איתא אמר רבא ובלבד שלא תיאנס פרש"י למשוך הסימנים חוץ נחזה בידי אדם בעי רבי חנינא אנסה עצמה מהו פרש"י לפשוט מאד צוארה בשעת שחיטה תיקו ולחומרא והתוס' פירשו בעיא זאת אנסה עצמה שפשטה ראשה יותר מדאי ליטול ירק בבור עכ"ל נראה כוונתם דלא ניחא להו לפרש פרש"י דאבעיא על שעת השחיטה דאם הוא טפי ממה שרגיל להתפשט בשעה שהיא רועה ודאי פסולה כיון דכבר קי"ל השיעור בכדי שהיא רועה ע"כ פירשו דמספקא ליה בפי' דכל שהיא רועה דדרך הבהמה לפשוט ראשה ביותר ליטול ירק מן הבור ואח"כ אוכלת אחר שחוזרת קצת מן הפישוט הגדולה מי נימא דגם זה בכלל השיעור שאמרו כשהיא רועה או נימא דוקא כשהיא רועה כפשוטו ואין פישוט ליטול הירק בכלל זה כנ"ל נכון ובזה מתורץ למה לא הביא הטור שאין חילוק בין אונס שע"י אדם לאנסה מעצמה בשחיטה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Without forcing itself.</b> In the Gemara it is stated: Rava said, provided that it does not force itself—Rashi explained: to pull the simanim away; we require human hands; Rabbi Chanina asked: if it forced itself—what? Rashi explained: that it stretched its neck excessively at the time of shechitah; teiku—and for stringency. And Tosafot explained this ask: it forced itself—that it stretched its head more than necessary to take a vegetable from a pit—end of their words. It appears their intent is that they are not pleased to explain Rashi's explanation about the time of shechitah, for if it is more than what is normal to stretch when it grazes, it is certainly invalid since we already established the measure is when it grazes; therefore they explained that the doubt is in the explanation of "whenever it grazes"—for it is an animal's manner to stretch its head more to take a vegetable from a pit and afterward eat after it returns somewhat from the great stretch—shall we say this too is included in the measure they said "when it grazes," or shall we say only when it grazes in its simple sense and stretching to take the vegetable is not included in this—as explained, it is correct; and with this it is resolved why Tur did not bring that there is no distinction between forcing by a person and forcing by itself in shechitah.

<b>And know that he cut them, etc.</b> From his words it appears there are two things: returning the simanim and knowing the cutting in the simanim before the spinal column; and he learned thus from Ra'osh who wrote on the mishnah "one who slaughters from the sides—his shechitah is kosher, provided he knows he cut the simanim before he cut the spinal column because the simanim are soft and pushed aside by the knife"—end of his words. It implies that even if he returned, he must know he cut the simanim before the spinal column; so too Beit Yosef; and therefore he wrote here that two are required. And I wonder—for then Ra'osh should have mentioned the law of returning and written upon it that knowledge too is required; rather it is clear that the return itself is the knowledge, for by returning he certainly knows he cut the simanim before the spinal column; and so it appears to me from Tosafot who wrote "the mishnah mentions one who slaughters from the sides is kosher specifically b'dieved; even if he returned, we decree lest he not return"—accordingly, if it were that returning does not suffice, why decree lest he not return—let it be invalid even with return, lest he not have cut the simanim before the spinal column; rather plainly that cutting the simanim first is since he returned, and with this he knew that matter; and so Agudah: returning alone is kosher. And for us there is no nafka mina from what we said, for the main concern here is lest he cut the spinal column before the simanim, and we hold it is forbidden even if he cut the spinal column after the simanim, as written in siman 24; and it turns out the kosher status is only without cutting the spinal column; nevertheless kosher even without return, for we have no concern—as explained. Rather Maharshal introduced one stringency: any time he cut flesh of the neck before the simanim there is to forbid, and this is from concern for hesech—lest when he began to slaughter in the flesh he touched the esophagus slightly and returned and cut in the flesh—and he derived this from Rashba in teshuvah haketzarah etc. "from the sides his slaughter is kosher because the simanim are slaughtered before he cut the spinal column and the flesh upon them"—end; from "and the flesh upon them" it implies if he cut in the flesh first there is concern for perforation of the esophagus; and in my humble opinion there is no place for this stringency, for then we should be concerned from doubt in every slaughterer lest in the middle of slaughtering the simanim he cut in the flesh and returned to the simanim, or lest when he began to cut in the skin he touched the siman and returned and cut in the skin. And furthermore I found explicitly in Rashba Mishmeret HaBayit daf 26a at the end etc.: what he cut in the flesh does not affect; when he reached the simanim and slaughtered two-thirds, according to all kosher—end; behold before you Rashba clearly that we are not concerned for cutting in flesh at the time of slaughter; and that which he mentioned in the brief teshuvah "and the flesh upon them" is for another reason—for he holds there is no concern lest he cut the entire spinal column before cutting the simanim, automatically it would first be forbidden, since there is flesh on the spinal column certainly the cutting of all the simanim will finish before the cutting of the flesh and entire spinal column finishes; and so explained in Tur HaTeshuvot: "the reason of the matter is that even though he slaughters from the side and cuts the spinal column with slaughtering the simanim, slaughter of the simanim precedes cutting the entire spinal column; and even treifah is not until he slaughters the simanim"—end; this is explained as I said, that he means: not only is concern for nevelah obvious there is none—for that is what Ze'ira said "the spinal column broke and majority of flesh with it is nevelah," for certainly he will not afterward need to come to cutting majority of flesh; rather even concern for treifah—that is, without majority of flesh—there is none here, since in any case he must cut a little in flesh before cutting spinal column and simanim, and simanim are slaughtered before a cut is made in flesh and spinal column. But he does not deal with prohibition of hesech—for certainly there is no measure of hesech here; and even for us who are stringent in all hesechot this is only if he raised the knife even slightly—in this we decree "he raised and paused a little" because of "pausing greatly"; but when he does not raise at all, why should we be more stringent for us than the sages of the Talmud—this is not subject to decree and proof for speech, for behold in slaughtering in two or three places in one circuit it is kosher even for us, and we should not say to be stringent for us because of pausing a little, which is routine every day—accordingly there is stringency only when he raises slightly; and see what I wrote in siman 21 seif 4. This is Rashba's intent; and see what I wrote further on this in siman 27. But from hesech they were not strict even if within the slaughter he cuts the spinal column, and how much more so flesh of the neck—there is no measure; and with this for us too it is permitted, for specifically if he raised the knife even slightly we are stringent because of "do not distinguish," but not here—as written in siman 23 seif 2 on this, see there; and what is written in the next seif—and it is from the words of Kol Bo—that simanim should present themselves before flesh of the neck: his main concern is lest he come to cut the spinal column, as he concluded there explicitly "and to cut the spinal column, etc."; and therefore they were also strict about flesh of the neck that is on the spinal column because of distancing from the unseemly; and on the contrary, from there is proof—since he found no reason for prohibition except because of the spinal column and not because of hesech in slaughter even without cutting the spinal column, but certainly there is no concern at all as we said.
