And "that is at the end of the trachea" etc.—and the view of Mahari"l and Mahari"v and the rest of the latter geonim is to be stringent not to slaughter above the large ring; and Beit Chadash wondered: behold the Gemara is arranged until hat slope is kosher; and Shach wrote they relied on what is said in Bava Kamma daf 99 that even though it would be kosher according to Rav, nevertheless they were stringent because of avodah zarah; and this too is so. And it is strained—for there it was within the large ring and discusses the dispute of the rabbis and R. Yehuda ben Beteira; but here above, since the view of R. Yehuda ben Antigonos is that it is kosher—from where do we derive to be stringent? And it appears there is a difficulty if the matters are as they sound simply: did the sages disagree with R. Yehuda ben Antigonos likewise, or that from the ring upward is a place fit for shechitah—and why did they call it hagramah, meaning deror? And Rashi wrote: anything not in the place of shechitah is called hagramah—behold according to R. Yehuda ben Antigonos alone it is a place of shechitah, so why call it hagramah? Therefore he holds that all agree that d'rabbanan it is forbidden to slaughter above the place of the large ring; only if he was derore half outside the ring and half inside—according to R. Yehuda ben Beteira it is treifah because majority in the ring is required; according to R. Yehuda ben Antigonos it is kosher, as with half trachea defective and the place of shechitah is d'oraisa kosher; therefore they called it hagramah for it is outside the place of shechitah—and understand well. It appears at first glance according to what Rashi said in the sugya "one who slaughters within the ring" etc., but in the rest of the rings his slaughter is not kosher in their majority at all except between ring and ring—and Rashi held this on column 2 s.v. lashon shlishi etc., for they wrote; and the first wording appears to me, and there is no obstacle to switch the halakhah, for there is no halakhah in all of them except like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos; and one must understand: if so, why did Rashi trouble to clarify which wording is correct and sharp in the chapter of the Gemara—behold all are mere pilpul; and for practical halakhah it does not stand as Rashi himself said. And especially Tosafot challenged Rashi's explanation that he holds one may not slaughter in the rest of the rings, and explained another explanation; and so in Piskei Tosafot—not like Rashi—that it is permitted to slaughter even within another ring; and all this is unnecessary, for since we establish halakhah like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, everything falls clearly as Rashi himself said. Therefore it appears: in any case one must understand how R. Yehuda ben Antigonos's testimony that until hat slope is called a place of shechitah—that the rest of the rings that do not surround the entire cavity, as Rashi explained, are fit for shechitah; and if this depends on the large ring being a place of shechitah, nevertheless to slaughter in the ring below it is forbidden because of what Rashi said that they do not surround the entire trachea; if so, one can say that even though R. Yehuda ben Antigonos's testimony is true that the place of shechitah is from hat slope, it is also possible that the rest of the rings are not a place of shechitah. But in truth this is not a difficulty, for from the large ring until hat slope the wheat grains and the like—all do not surround the entire cavity of the trachea, only the majority, and the remainder adhere in the skin and the skin surrounds; and accordingly, since R. Yehuda ben Antigonos said from the large ring until hat slope is fit for shechitah, we learn we do not require that it surround the entire cavity of the spinal column; and how much more so the rest of the rings that are below the large ring, which are called a place of shechitah—and they depend on each other. And accordingly we can understand what we say in the Gemara: R. Zeira ate hagramah of Rav and Shmuel, and the Gemara challenged: he has no stringency of "the place he left" etc.; and the Gemara answered: from Babylonia to Eretz Yisrael it does not apply; or R. Zeira does not intend to return. And one must understand: what is the difficulty—R. Zeira holds as explained there, like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, that from hat slope is a place of shechitah even though it does not surround its entire cavity; and how much more so hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of the rings, as said; and one must say: in Babylonia the prohibition of hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of the rings spread, how much more so above the ring, as said; and therefore it challenged "he has no stringency of the place he left" from there, for even though halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, in Babylonia they practiced prohibition; and it answered he does not intend to return. And accordingly we derive: in Babylonia we do not practice like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, and even hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of their rings they practiced to invalidate; and see below chapter Eizehu Mekoman regarding chelev on the maw that they challenged etc., that Rashi wrote: even in a place where the people of Eretz Yisrael and Babylonia disagree, we follow the residents of Babylonia—see there; and accordingly for us there is no R. Yehuda ben Beteira, and even hagramah of Rav and Shmuel is forbidden; and therefore Rashi troubled to clarify what is hagramah of Rav and Shmuel, and clarified the first wording: mainly in the rest of the rings it is forbidden; and he wrote there is no obstacle since halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, only we practice like the people of Babylonia to forbid in any case; from Torah law it is permitted, only by force of custom—and there is no obstacle at all in a mere custom—and understand. Therefore Tosafot challenged Rashi and raised that even for us they forbid like the people of Babylonia—this is the reconciliation; and so Piskei Tosafot—not like Rashi—for us there is a nafka mina that we practice like the people of Babylonia. One must say: just as in melikah certainly he must know clearly that he did not perform melikah on the simanim first but on the spinal column—for that is the essence of the mitzvah of melikah—if so, learn from it in shechitah: since the mitzvah of shechitah is in the simanim, he must know he slaughtered simanim first; so too one must say for this view. And the Mechaber holds Ra'osh and Tosafot disagree: Tosafot hold knowledge is not needed, only return suffices; and Ra'osh holds there is no nafka mina in return, only knowledge suffices; and the Mechaber is stringent like both—that return and knowledge are required—to satisfy both views; and this is also for the nape kosher, since there is return and knowledge—and understand.
