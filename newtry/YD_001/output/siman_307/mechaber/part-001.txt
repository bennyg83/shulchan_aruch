If he slaughtered it and it was found to be a treifah, its hide with its flesh is forbidden in benefit and requires burial: (and therefore it is good to sell it to a non-Jew before slaughter) (Hagahot Mordechai chapter HaIshah, and so Beit Yosef in the name of an Ashkenazic responsum, which is in the responsum of Maharil):

**** ENGLISH ****
The kohen must keep it and raise it forever until a blemish falls upon it; and he may sell it even to an Israelite even though it is unblemished, and the purchaser observes upon it the sanctity of bechor. This is when the purchaser buys it for his need; but it is forbidden to make merchandise of it—that is, to buy it in order to profit. And if he bought it for his need and did not need it, he may return and sell it:
