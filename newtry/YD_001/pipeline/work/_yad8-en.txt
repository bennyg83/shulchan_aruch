And one who gives them to her Israelite husband. Peri Chadash wrote that if the woman revealed her intent that it is not pleasing to her that they be given to her husband but only to her — he did not fulfill the mitzvah of giving from what was given to her husband; but Peri To'ar wrote that even if she cries out that she does not want them given to her husband and they were given — he fulfilled the mitzvah of giving; the reason: since she acquires upon finding at the time she acquired, her husband acquired them and behold he takes them in exchange for what he is obligated to do for her — end of his words. But it appears the husband must give them to her and then he again takes them from her through the power of acquisition he has over her, for what a woman acquires her husband acquires; but if it did not reach her hand at all, there is no acquisition for the woman at all and he did not fulfill the mitzvah of giving. And if the woman is from kohen acquaintances who acquire immediately when slaughtered and the bechor takes double in them, as explained in Bava Batra 123 — possibly it is not so simple to say "I do not want" — before she said "I do not want" she acquired and the husband acquired; and although the owners can return and give to another even among kohen acquaintances, nevertheless as long as they did not return, it is as if it reached their hands — see below seif 14; and it requires study. And he further wrote in Peri To'ar that it is reasonable if he gave them to the kohen's father-in-law he fulfilled the mitzvah of giving — see there; and possibly it deals with kohen acquaintances; and it requires study. And see Shach Choshen Mishpat 86 s.k. 3 who explains per Rambam what the Gemara says anonymous tanna does not hold like R' Natan — namely in all matters of charity, as in the Tosefta — see there; and in Chullin 24 pe'ah and ma'aser ani do not pay the debt — and Rambam ruled in chapter 6 of Hilchot Matanot Aniyim, and likewise Mordechai in Bava Batra regarding one who borrowed and they gave him money for charity — he is exempt from paying per this Tosefta — see there; and in Chullin 134 it is clear the Gemara compares priestly gifts to gifts to the poor regarding doubt — see there; however for what was said afterward that Ulla said "standing crop against standing crop" one may say there is a distinction between priestly gifts and gifts to the poor; and I wrote on this in a responsum and shortened here. And see Peri Chadash that the husband may sell or give to others without his wife's knowledge, for women are not particular — see there; and it appears if it already came to her hand or his hand with her consent, her objection no longer matters at all.

And Peri Chadash wrote what is written in Sifrei and Pesikta to give to the kohen and not to his agent — this is in the default case lest it is not pleasing to him that they be given into the agent's hand; but if knowingly he sent an agent to take them — it is fine, for a person's agent is like himself. And in Machaneh Ephraim laws of acquisition siman 26 he wrote that it appears from Peri Chadash that even if it did not reach the kohen's hand he fulfilled when giving to his agent; and he challenged him, for certainly whatever did not come to his hand he did not fulfill the mitzvah of giving when he is not kohen acquaintances; and so it appears from Peri To'ar; and he further wrote one may say the verse was particular that the kohen himself go to the homeowner to take his gifts — not that he send his agent — see there; and it requires study. And per my humble opinion one may say it is similar to pe'ah where the agent does not acquire for him, since he is not kohen acquaintances — it is another's debt; and see responsa Beit Efrayim that I brought from Yerushalmi Gittin that a person does not make an agent to receive a gift that is not his; and likewise I brought in Ketzot HaChoshen; and if so, if it was lost in the agent's hand and did not reach his hand he did not fulfill. And Bekorotai wrote that in Sifrei it implies for himself and not for his agent; and that which a husband acquires on behalf of his wife — a husband's hand is like his wife's hand and preferable to an agent, as we find in several matters, unlike Peri Chadash — end of his words. And in truth Peri Chadash did not bring any proof from a husband, only from reasoning he wrote that if knowingly he sent his agent it is like himself; and what he wrote acquisition from a kohen who assigns to another — although there it is kohen acquaintances, nevertheless it is fine all the more where the agent brings and delivers to his hand. And Mishna Berurah wrote that a husband of a kohenet who wishes to be stringent on himself to separate gifts and give to another kohen is permitted, and this is not within "whoever is exempt from a matter and performs it is called a simpleton," for in truth the obligation rests on him since he is an Israelite who is obligated — only it is as if he separates and gives to his wife, as Peri Chadash wrote siman 33; and regarding redeeming a firstborn donkey, a husband of a kohenet is obligated to give, as written below siman 321 in name of responsa Rashba; and here he is exempt, for it is as if given to his wife; therefore if he wishes to give to another he is permitted. And see there that he elaborated on "whoever is exempt from a matter," etc. — see there.
