But in basar b'chalav the Rav Shach challenged that it implies we are stringent in basar b'chalav beyond the law — and it is not so, for by law it is forbidden; if so there is no difficulty in the Gemara \"why is milk permitted — it is nevelah milk,\" and this difficulty is explained in Rashba and Ran. And I have difficulty: even Raavad and Mordechai, holders of this reasoning, hold that in basar b'chalav there is no difference between wet and dry — whence do we learn to innovate in other issurim a difference; and although to resolve Tosafot's difficulty from \"a pitcher of water fell at the end\" in tractate Avodah Zarah — nevertheless the matter itself is hard: what did the Sages see to divide equal things, and what distinguishes this from that. And in truth it is hard for me that they ruled explicitly against the view of Tosafot and Rabbenu Tam and Rashbam, who all hold in the matter of the piece that two sixties are required — for even gravy that is wet in wet is not nullified. And Tosafot challenged Rashi from this: since taste entered the piece, behold the gravy became chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids all. And Tosafot challenged from this: a kid — the Torah forbade and not milk — nevertheless chatichah na'asah nevelah from the issur. And likewise the challenge from vessels from hag'alah also fits this reasoning — it appears like R' Eliezer: they hag'alah twice; so for Rabbenu Tam three times — for in the first time the water became forbidden and returned and hag'alah a second time and discharged the water, and then it did not become chatichah na'asah nevelah for it is wet in wet; and if so the vessel also did not absorb except proportionally, and the third hag'alah helps; and there is no difficulty that in wet in wet chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply — therefore it appeared that all Mordechai's reasoning is as Beit Yosef wrote precisely: a piece that received recognizable issur taste — the piece; but wet in wet, a blended thing, we do not say this — this is for the view that min b'mino is nullified; but for the view that min b'mino is not nullified — and this is what the Torah revealed regarding blood of the bull and blood of the goat; and although bull blood is more, nevertheless goat blood is upon it — behold on the contrary wet in wet is not nullified; nevertheless the first name is upon it; what is dry in dry we do not say; and if so on the contrary the first name is upon it in its place — and from here to be lenient in wet in wet since the verse revealed explicitly in wet in wet that it is not nullified. And it shall be the first name upon it and it shall be called by the name goat blood, although bull blood is more — therefore Raavad and Mordechai said their words for us who hold min b'mino in wet in wet is nullified, for the first name is uprooted; and they properly wrote: if gravy of issur mixed with permitted, chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply, for its name is not upon it, for it is blended and its name uprooted; but the whole sugya in the Gemara is for the view that min b'mino is not nullified, and all Tosafot follow this. And likewise this of yayin nesech — they already wrote in Zevachim ch. HaTa'aruvot and likewise in ch. HaKometz that yayin nesech forbids in its species in mashahu, for they ruled thus like R' Yehuda who holds min b'mino is not nullified; and they properly challenged that it should be chatichah na'asah nevelah even in wet in wet, for they stand in importance by Torah law — and no difficulty remains except the challenge from hag'alah of vessels; but in any case requires study what is Tosafot's difficulty from the large yorah — for the parable of the yorah was that it absorbed a kezayit of issur and hag'alah water was fifty log, and behold all became nevelah and the pot returned and absorbed from hag'alah water all nevelah — and what does hag'alah help a second time, it will again become nevelah — end of the challenge; and one may argue that this log of water it absorbed is truly forbidden and full nevelah; but what do you judge in it if it is min b'mino with the second hag'alah water — behold min b'mino is nullified in majority; and if you consider it not its species since it absorbed issur and became forbidden — it is not its species and is called by issur name — it is hard, for it did not absorb only one fiftieth of a kezayit of milk, only each log became nevelah; but nevertheless there is no milk taste, only one fiftieth part; and if so when he returned and put in a third time hag'alah water — if kafeilah tastes this second hag'alah water he will certainly not find issur taste, for there was only one fiftieth part and it is nullified in fifty full log the third — and in not its species we follow all by taste whether much or little, and here no taste is sensed; and one must say all Tosafot's difficulty is for Rav who said there chatichah na'asah nevelah and holds min b'mino in mashahu — and if so these first waters absorbed that became nevelah are not nullified at all in hag'alah water; and Tosafot's difficulty fits well: everything is for the view that min b'mino is not nullified — one cannot distinguish at all between wet and dry — and they properly challenged. And in truth my heart pounds that all Tosafot's difficulty should be entirely Rabbenu Tam's and Rashbam's view; but because Tosafot brought below that waters forbidden from issur are not called issur relative to other waters, only issur name upon them — and if so even for Rav water is called species in not its species and nullified as we said — and therefore one may say that when Tosafot hold gravy forbidden from building is relative to gravy species in not its species — namely for Rava who follows after the name and upon this issur name it is forbidden from its importance, not so for Abaye who follows after taste and certainly their tastes are equal — and if so it is min b'mino; and if so even per Tosafot's view Abaye should be challenged how they hag'alah in the yorah; therefore Tosafot did not explain that per their view it stands — also the challenge stands for Abaye; or one may say Tosafot's difficulty applies to everything — hag'alah is as Tosafot wrote in Pesachim even for a wine vessel for a nazir; and if so in vessels they used gentile water forbidden in benefit — how did they hag'alah for Rav who holds min b'mino is not nullified. Granted for R' Eliezer they hag'alah three times — and although the first time it is not nullified, nevertheless what was absorbed from the first time was not nevelah, and they hag'alah a second time; and if they again became forbidden, nevertheless they were only forbidden from one fiftieth part in a kezayit and it is mashahu — and if so when they returned and hag'alah a third time it cannot forbid, for then it is species in not its species as Rosh wrote; but for Rabbenu Tam forever they absorbed a full kezayit of complete issur and forever it will not be nullified — and in this one may understand Tosafot's words that they preceded in the challenge and said the Torah only forbade a pot beit yomo — for apparently it is superfluous, for either way for the view that all vessels they hag'alah who hold noten ta'am li'fegam forbidden — obviously the difficulty is in place how they hag'alah large yorot; and did they have no remedy and the answer that they left until tomorrow does not apply; but per what I wrote it is satisfactory, for even noten ta'am li'fegam forbidden nevertheless its taste is not equal to its species and it is like species in not its species; and likewise from Tosafot in Avodah Zarah daf 66 — if so it stands for Tosafot that nevertheless min b'mino is not called — therefore Tosafot wrote that they praised hag'alah in Beit Yosef and properly challenged — investigate. And see Mishmeret HaBayit daf 123 that wrote these matters — one should be concerned about gentile water from the holy mouth of Ramban.

And behold from Tosafot Chullin daf 110 s.v. this for a pot that they wrote: and for what I explained that the mishnah deals with water in a pot with meat — if so the first part of the braita that teaches also the kchal tears it, etc., is also with meat, and it is not similar to heart that tears it, etc., that does not deal with meat — for if with meat the meat becomes nevelah and returns and forbids the blood, etc. — end of their words. And it is hard: why specifically with meat — per Rabbenu Tam's explanation even per Rashi's, for in the Gemara we establish it for the pot; and per Rabbenu Tam it is without meat only in water — nevertheless the water becomes nevelah and forbids the heart; and what is special about with meat — see Maharsha who explains explicitly that this challenge is only if it deals with meat; but afterward Tosafot wrote the heart is not the subject and the challenge is even if cooked without meat — and in any case this challenge is specifically with meat; and this matter is not understood at first glance — therefore one must say they hold wet in wet does not become nevelah; and if so blood in water is wet in wet and does not become nevelah; but with meat it becomes nevelah for it is wet in dry — and they properly challenged. And from this it is explained that they hold the distinction between wet and dry — and at first glance they contradict their words on the earlier daf as I wrote; but per what I wrote it is satisfactory; and there is no difficulty whence for Tosafot this distinction between wet and wet — for from this difficulty of the pitcher of water, etc., it does not imply; and it appears the difficulty of Ran from two cups, one terumah and one hullin that they mixed — we say it rose — and why should the water mixed in the terumah cup not become chatichah na'asah nevelah, for it fell only mashahu, for it is only one third part — so Tosafot explicitly in Menachot daf 82 side b that it was only mixture two parts water — see there Tosafot who elaborated; and likewise in Zevachim ch. HaTa'aruvot; and per the above it is satisfactory that here min b'mino is not nullified as Tosafot brought there — if so we properly say wet in wet there is no chatichah na'asah nevelah — investigate.

And behold Peri Chadash s.k. 17 holds in rabbinic issur we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, and brought proof from Rambam regarding chicken meat in milk we do not say thus — see there. And Tosafot regarding kchal implies they hold chatichah na'asah nevelah; and likewise regarding wine that fell into a pit they say chatichah na'asah nevelah even though it is rabbinic; and likewise regarding fish and fowl they salted — they inferred chatichah na'asah nevelah; and likewise fish-sellers chatichah na'asah nevelah even though salting and cooking are rabbinic; and one may say as Kereti wrote: they hold in all issurim chatichah na'asah nevelah from one matter in basar b'chalav — even rabbinic issur we say thus; but Rambam holds like R' Eliezer — specifically in basar b'chalav we say thus, specifically Torah issur that is similar to basar b'chalav, not rabbinic — investigate.

And behold Taz s.k. 16 elaborated from Tur's words that wrote for Rabbenu Tam's view that in other issurim too it becomes nevelah — \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids — specifically in noten ta'am when there was not sixty in permitted to nullify the issur absorbed; but if forbidden in mashahu, even if forbidden due to its species that forbids in mashahu, the whole does not become nevelah; and Taz wrote the source Chullin daf 100: once taste entered a piece, chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids all pieces that are its species; and they challenged why need noten ta'am — even mashahu also; and Rabbenu Tam answered: although it forbids in mashahu, nevertheless chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply; and from this Taz elaborated to permit chametz on Pesach forbidden in mashahu that it does not return and forbid other pieces in mashahu. And the Rav Shach in Nekudat HaKesef elaborated to challenge and agreed with him, Magen Avraham, etc.; and the main reason: what was forbidden in mashahu does not forbid the one that comes regarding species in its species, since it is only mashahu it is like not its species and does not forbid; but chametz on Pesach forbids even in not its species — again there is no difference between mashahu and noten ta'am — what difference; and he brought proof from Ran that implies like Shach's words; and the truth they came in Ran — but in this proof that Tosafot's intent was not like Shach's intent, for Ran on this challenge \"why need noten ta'am in the piece — even if it received mashahu it should forbid another in min b'mino,\" etc., wrote: and the authors strained themselves in its resolution; and it appears to me it is another power of mixture, etc.; and if Rabbenu Tam and R' Eliezer's answer were as Shach thinks, what did Ran innovate until he wrote the predecessors strained themselves in this and it appears to him to answer thus — and per Shach's words these are exactly the early authorities' words and there is no innovation here, only it is clear this did not occur to the early authorities.

However what did he need to bring from Ran — in Rosh it appears more clearly in his words, and these are his words for R' Eliezer's views: since taste did not enter the piece, behold it is as if it does not exist and is not considered min b'mino; but when it received taste it is called min b'mino, etc.; and likewise he wrote afterward: specifically in noten ta'am of issur then it is called min b'mino to forbid other pieces; but if forbidden in mashahu, since one need only nullify the first issur, it is not considered min b'mino in any amount — end of his words; and this teaches like Shach's view.

However this is certainly for R' Eliezer's view that the second piece itself does not forbid, for it did not become nevelah — only what is absorbed inside it; and in this one may well say if the absorption is slight it is like not its species and nullified — and this is what Rosh inferred: since one need only nullify the first issur, it is not considered min b'mino in any amount — behold for Rabbenu Tam who must nullify the second issur that became chatichah na'asah nevelah, it is considered min b'mino; and so is reasonable for Rabbenu Tam who holds chatichah na'asah nevelah in min b'mino — not that one should ask why does it not become nevelah, behold it itself forbids; and whence — or that what is absorbed is not its species as Shach thinks — nevertheless the body of the second piece itself forbids, it is species and species; and if you say what was forbidden cannot forbid — this does not apply at all, for in truth it goes with it — whatever taste there is, since in any case it goes with it as poskim wrote in many places regarding fish salted with meat and the like.

Therefore it is clear there is no reasoning for Rabbenu Tam to say it is not its species; and Shach built Rabbenu Tam's words onto Rabbenu Ephraim's words — and it is not so, for in R' Eliezer's matter who holds the body of the piece does not forbid, only the absorption — in this one may say like Shach's reasoning that the absorption is not its species; but for Rabbenu Tam that the body of the second piece forbids, there is no place for all the above.

And at first glance there was an opening to say according to Rabbenu Tam there is another reasoning: that chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim we learn from basar b'chalav — and if so we have only as said in basar b'chalav: what the Torah said is specifically in noten ta'am and the manner of cooking the Torah forbade — even whatever is in noten ta'am chatichah na'asah nevelah; but if not in noten ta'am, although forbidden from its species generally, it is not similar to basar b'chalav — we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah upon it; and see Ran who wrote many reasonings like this.

But still difficult: Tosafot for Rabbenu Tam from that a pitcher of water fell at the end, etc., and answered that initially a slight thing fell and did not become nevelah — and they challenged: nevertheless \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids; and if so that wine is forbidden — how does a pitcher of water help, etc. And difficult either way: if Tosafot hold these are not dependent on each other — even if no chatichah na'asah nevelah, nevertheless \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids per Rashba's view — if so Rabbenu Ephraim should also be challenged what does a pitcher of water help at the end, for \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids in any case; and if Tosafot hold they are dependent — if so since we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim, we also do not say \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids — if so for Rabbenu Tam it stands, for he holds in issur forbidden in mashahu we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, and even \"possible to squeeze out\" we do not say; therefore one must say that for Rabbenu Tam we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in mashahu — the reason is not that the issur name does not apply since we do not learn from basar b'chalav, only as I wrote that regarding forbidding others it is as if it does not exist and the forbidden one, etc.; and this is to forbid others; but regarding itself it is equal whether forbidden through noten ta'am or mashahu — nevertheless it is forbidden by Torah; and if so like receiving noten ta'am, \"possible to squeeze out\" forbids — so too receiving mashahu regarding that piece itself there is no difference at all; and if nothing discharges from there onward, obviously forbidden.

And at first glance I would bring proof that even for R' Eliezer this reasoning of \"not its species\" is not precise, only \"as if it does not exist\" is similar — for Tosafot explain \"why need noten ta'am\" — even without noten ta'am also; and answer that we say its species rose as if it is not, etc.; and Tosafot explain such as nevelah was two zayitim and shrank on another zayit — and if so one might not estimate except at sixty in gravy — therefore they said since taste entered the piece it received two zayitim — end. And still difficult: why did they give the parable of taste entering a piece — a piece of sixty and put in it taste of a zayit — even if they put only half a zayit, since in min b'mino it is not nullified, that half zayit of gravy forbids until there is sixty against it; and if it is not its species — what of it, behold the piece itself is also not its species relative to the gravy; and still difficult: why \"since it gave taste,\" etc. — rather learn that it is entirely lost; and without taste entering it is as if it does not exist — and at first glance this is compelling.

However one may reject: the other pieces without gravy would nullify the absorption that is species in not its species — but this is strained, for behold it returns and is stirred with the nevelah kezayit placed there that was a zayit and a half — why should it be not its species — requires study.

But go and learn what Rashba wrote in Mishmeret HaBayit daf 97: if issur did not give taste in the piece — although for the view that min b'mino is not nullified the piece is forbidden — nevertheless if the piece entered the gravy, sixty is not required even to nullify that absorption, for since it is mashahu it is as if it does not exist — behold he wrote explicitly that sixty is not required against the absorption; why — behold it absorbed issur, and what of it that it is not its species — and because it is not its species it should not require nullification; and Rashba did not mention at all that other pieces entered with it into the gravy until we say they nullify it — only the piece alone entered and from here it should be permitted; and perforce one must say as he wrote himself that mashahu is as if it does not exist — and so Rashba wrote in his novellae; and so it appears at length in Torat HaBayit; and so Ritva wrote in his novellae to Chullin: it appears more correct that Rav holds nothing forbids in mashahu except when it is visible; but when mixed between issur and heter it is not forbidden; and so Orach Chaim HaAroch general rule 24 din 3 wrote: a thing forbidden in mashahu from issur forbidden in mashahu — chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply — behold he ruled without distinguishing between chametz and other issurim. And if so Taz spoke well, and so Chokhmat Adam — and it is clear he did not intend the remote path Shach spoke in Nekudat HaKesef on erev Pesach after chatzot and Rambam's views. And therefore I wonder at Magen Avraham who wrote on Taz that he exceeded measure to permit what also the Rav Chokhmat Adam, who was among the elder rabbis and student of Maharal of Prague, wrote to permit, and so Orach Chaim explicitly general rule 24 din 3 — end of his words; and the world practices on issur — it appears from the statement \"chametz\" they were concerned for Rosh's view as I wrote; one may question Ran's view and Ramban's and Ra'ah in Beit HaBayit that noten ta'am is not precise — even mashahu forbids; and for this branch there is issur, for it emerges from the mouths of sages of the generation; but to lean to permit is clear it is permitted.

And there is some proof for Ran and Shach's words from that we learn: taste and not substance, etc. — for if it is a novelty, even without noten ta'am also; and Rava said the manner of cooking, etc. — learn that if it is a novelty one cannot say in it noten ta'am also, even mashahu also; and if so it is hard: we say for one tanna hag'alah of gentiles is a novelty as explained in the Gemara in Pesachim — if so even without noten ta'am also; and if so gentile vessels cooked in them should be forbidden even without noten ta'am also, for in any case it is a novelty that even a detrimental thing is forbidden — so too even noten ta'am also; and one must say: if so how do you find hag'alah of gentiles — behold taste is absorbed in water and returns and absorbs; and if they hag'alah in a large vessel that has sixty in the water against the gentile absorption, it forbids even without noten ta'am also — how can one hag'alah; only the difficulty is: behold the absorbed thing more than noten ta'am does not forbid its discharge again — and therefore one may say they hag'alah twice; and what of it that first it was absorbed in it — nevertheless afterward hag'alah helps since also the water does not forbid its discharge; and it helps that the first water was only forbidden in mashahu — and therefore one must say like Shach's view that whatever is forbidden in mashahu, whether its species or not its species, even a thing forbidden in mashahu forbids its discharge; if so per the view that hag'alah of gentiles is in mashahu — how do you find it, for forever the water forbids without end, for there is no difference between its species and not its species — requires study, for one may say these are different aspects — requires study.
