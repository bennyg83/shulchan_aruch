(Siman 92 in the hagahah) And likewise if he did not stir, etc. Shach s.k. 6 wants to explain Rama's words that he is in doubt whether we hold like R' Yehuda or like Rashi — see there — and this is that he follows his view that below s.k. 3 he holds like Rashi: if part is in the gravy and part outside the gravy it does not spread at all without stirring and covering. But as appears from Taz's words s.k. 5 who explains Rashi's words that if part, etc., is forbidden — this is because it absorbed before the absorption came to the other pieces and gravy; but nevertheless, since part is in the gravy, afterward it discharges forbidden taste into it and forbids even without stirring and covering — see there. And behold at first glance it is proven like Shach, for behold Tosafot in ch. 7 daf 93 side 2 s.v. im yesh bah noten ta'am explained: when he stirred the pot it carries the drop throughout the pot and all inside assists to nullify the drop; and likewise when he covered the pot, steam rises from the sides and carries the drop throughout the pot — it implies they mean even the piece on which the drop fell had part in the gravy: if he did not stir and cover, the gravy and pieces do not assist to nullify it. And for R' Yehuda it appears that when the piece on which issur fell has part in the gravy, stirring and covering are not required — and there it deals when it is entirely outside the gravy, for then issur does not spread except through stirring and covering, etc. — see there. And it is implied explicitly from their words that for Rashi, Rabbi's statement "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring and without covering" regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah deals when part is in the gravy and part outside the gravy — and on this the Gemara challenges: let us say without stirring and covering at all — absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge — behold explicitly for Rashi the piece does not forbid what is in the pot at all if he did not stir, as Shach's view. However, from the Gemara in Chullin daf 100 it appears at first glance the opposite, as Taz's view, from that which we say there: since it imparted taste in the piece it becomes chatichah na'asah nevelah; and Tosafot there s.v. chatichah na'asah nevelah explained: they were cooked first before the other pieces were placed in the pot and there was not sixty in the issur, and the nevelah imparted taste in it, etc. — and it is difficult per the explanation, for if so the gravy that was at that time was also forbidden first through the absorbed taste, and shortly after it implies they nullify, etc. — see there. Behold per Shach's view in Rashi's words there is room to say: perhaps it deals in first cooking when part was in the gravy and part outside the gravy — this does not discharge at all to the gravy and the gravy was not forbidden; or that it is not like Shach, for even in such a case it discharges — and Tosafot's challenge is well, also from Rashi himself in the sugya of the milk drop daf 108 it appears not like Shach, for Rashi wrote there s.v. ach rav, etc.: that which the mishnah teaches "if there is noten ta'am in it," etc. — in that piece and it forbids the whole piece, etc. — and if so Rav refers to the mishnah, and the plain meaning of the mishnah implies the first part deals without stirring and covering at all, and nevertheless it forbids the rest of the pot — and if so it is proven like Taz's words that if part is in the gravy, nevertheless it discharges and forbids other pieces; and one must say certainly the truth is with Taz. And that which Tosafot wrote in ch. 7 that it appears they mean even what fell on it had part in the gravy, etc. — which per force appears they explain according to Rashi — for Rabbi's "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" deals when part is in the gravy; if so per Taz's view the Gemara should not challenge at all from absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge, etc. One may say: certainly Tosafot explain that Rashi also establishes Rabbi's "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" when it is entirely outside the gravy; and that which Tosafot wrote "and it appears to me that even the piece on which it fell had part in the gravy" — this is because Rashi holds this law that the piece itself is forbidden and other pieces do not combine to nullify the issur — and if so there is no place to challenge why Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" with stirring, etc., and he did not say "the words of the Sages appear correct" when part is in the gravy — because in truth in such a case they hold like R' Yehuda who say chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids also the other pieces; not so per R' Yehuda's view that in such a case other pieces combine. If so per Taz's view there is a difficulty: why should it have said "the words of the Sages" when part is in the gravy and not challenge per Rashi's view that Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct" without stirring at first but only at the end — it should have been established that Rabbi's words are where he did not stir and cover at all and it deals when part is in the gravy, etc. [Hagahah: and Peleti noted here also Taz's words and wrote requires study to resolve with difficulty — end of hagahah.] And one may answer: behold certainly the dispute of R' Yehuda and the Sages is in every case: for R' Yehuda, even if he stirred from beginning to end, it is forbidden and the Sages disagree that in every matter it is not forbidden, whether issur fell on a piece with part in the gravy or entirely outside the gravy it is permitted; and R' Yehuda disagrees with them and forbids everything they permitted. And although Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring" regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah — it implies in all cases they disagree — nevertheless they hold like R' Yehuda without stirring, and even entirely outside the gravy; and on this the Gemara challenges well: if he did not stir at all — absorbs absorbs, etc. And it was necessary to answer that it means he did not stir at the beginning but only at the end — for in every case this forbids the rest of the pot; and per this Taz's view stands, and Shach's challenge on Rama returns to its place where he wrote that piece is forbidden and also requires sixty against the drop — if so he is concerned for Rashi's view; if so one should require sixty against the whole piece, for it discharges even without stirring at all; and one must say Rama deals when it is entirely outside the gravy; and that which requires sixty against the drop is because it is not clear to him whether the steam of the pot carries throughout — one might say the steam of the pot carries taste throughout the pot, and if so that piece is forbidden lest perhaps it does not carry, and one also needs sixty in the pot against the drop lest perhaps it carries throughout. And per this one must say that which Tosafot wrote in ch. 7 daf 100 s.v. chatichah na'asah nevelah — "but one may say it deals such as when there is a permitted piece entirely outside the gravy and a nevelah piece placed on it outside the gravy, and the permitted piece became nevelah because it absorbs nevelah taste in which there is no sixty to nullify the taste; nevertheless when he stirred the pot it forbids all pieces because it is its species" — end of his words; one must say Tosafot's view is that it deals after he removed the issur he then added other pieces; for otherwise if it deals that even before removing the nevelah the pieces were in the pot — if so it should be difficult why need it impart taste in the pieces, for even without imparting taste the whole pot is forbidden, lest perhaps the steam of the pot carries throughout, and if so it is a slight amount of nevelah itself forbidding the other pieces — for granted one may say if placed entirely outside the gravy it certainly does not carry throughout, only that piece — if so it must impart taste in the piece, for otherwise it is two slight amounts and we do not say two slight amounts; not so now that we say the steam of the pot carries throughout — if so this slight amount of nevelah itself forbids the other pieces, and one need not say two slight amounts. Or it deals that it was cooked first without other pieces, only in first cooking it was placed entirely outside the gravy — which is not so for Rashi who does not establish such a case — the challenge is well, for the gravy was forbidden; and one may answer: if so per their view too why was the gravy not forbidden, lest perhaps the steam of the pot carries throughout? One may say it deals that there was sixty in the gravy against the nevelah itself — if so either way the gravy is permitted: if the steam of the pot carries throughout, when nevelah fell there it carried throughout the pot and the taste was nullified in the gravy; and if it does not carry throughout, it did not discharge its taste at all to the gravy. Not so that piece forbidding other pieces, lest perhaps it does not carry throughout. Not so per Rashi's view — the challenge is well either way how does it deal: if placed entirely in the gravy — necessarily there is no sixty in the gravy, for if there were sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, this piece too would not be forbidden at all — one reason: we say it removed its species, etc.; and further, even if it did not remove, etc., nevertheless since this piece is only forbidden due to a slight amount, it should not forbid the other pieces, for it is two slight amounts. And if it deals that it was placed with part in the gravy — and properly this piece placed on it is forbidden even though there is sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, for in such a case one cannot say it removed its species and stands in its prohibition — per Taz's view who holds in his words that since part is in the gravy it discharges even without stirring and covering — if so even though there is sixty in the gravy against the nevelah, nevertheless against all the pieces there is not sixty, and if so the gravy is forbidden from the piece since there is no sixty against it — and Tosafot challenge Rashi well — investigate. And behold the author of Pri Toar wrote one may say he holds like Rashi that that piece is forbidden is only what is above the gravy where the power of the gravy does not reach and part is forbidden; but what is inside the gravy is not forbidden, for as soon as absorption comes there the gravy combines to nullify it since part is in the gravy — what is above the gravy does not harm what is inside — see there. And from Tosafot the opposite is implied, for they wrote ch. 7 daf 90 side 2 s.v. im yesh noten ta'am: per Rashi's view "if there is noten ta'am in it" specifically — for he holds that if part is in the gravy, etc., that piece alone is forbidden and the gravy does not combine. But for Rashi it must be that "if there is noten ta'am in it" is not precise — and it is impossible as Peri Toar wrote, for also for Rashi it must be not precise, for it is implied in the mishnah: since there is in the gid enough to impart taste in that thigh, behold this thigh is forbidden — meaning the whole thigh; and per Peri Toar's words that the part placed inside the gravy combines with the gravy to nullify — if so for Rashi too it must be not precise when it said "if there is in it" — rather certainly for Rashi the whole piece is forbidden — investigate.
