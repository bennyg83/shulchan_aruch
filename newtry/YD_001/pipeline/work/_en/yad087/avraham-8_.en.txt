(There) Some say it is forbidden to stoke fire under a gentile's pot, etc., lest one come to cooking basar b'chalav. And in the Gemara chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah (daf 12) regarding "you did not go out to battle in your days," and I saw Israel and gentiles who placed two pots on one stove and the Sages were not concerned for them — it does not imply thus, for behold when an Israelite stokes his pot he also stokes under the gentile's pot, and nevertheless the Sages were not concerned for them, because one does not come to cooking basar b'chalav on account of several reasons. One: that an anonymous vessel is not ben yomo, as Taz wrote siman 122 seif 6. And we do not find that they decreed that what is not ben yomo is forbidden l'chatchila lest it be ben yomo, except regarding eating and not regarding cooking absorbed in it. And further, consider for yourself: if you say it is cooking — if so, how is it permitted to kasher a vessel absorbed with basar b'chalav within twenty-four hours? Behold it was cooked in basar b'chalav absorbed in it. Rather, learn from this that one need not be concerned for this. And likewise Shach — see there.
