(There) The vessel in which they make water for head-washing is forbidden to use, for they make it from ash, etc., and it is routine that basar b'chalav mixes there. And that which it is not forbidden for this reason for head-washing — for washing one's head is not considered benefit, since one does not benefit from the substance of the prohibition — Shach and Acharonim. And one must say that here the intent of the hagahah is that meat and milk are still visible and ash has not yet been made, and one does not benefit from the substance of the prohibition. But if ash had been made, it would be forbidden for head-washing, as explained explicitly at the end of Temurah: all buried ones — their ash is forbidden and considered there as basar b'chalav. And Raavad explained: their ash is forbidden for laundering garments — see there. And if so, likewise to wash one's head it is forbidden. And likewise the Rambam in the beginning of chapter 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot explicitly that ash of basar b'chalav is forbidden. And it escaped Beit Yosef siman 294. And the author of Magen Avraham at his end wrote that it was not mentioned in any posek that ash of basar b'chalav is forbidden — and behold the Rambam wrote it explicitly as stated. And likewise in Sefer HaChinuch parashat Mishpatim.
