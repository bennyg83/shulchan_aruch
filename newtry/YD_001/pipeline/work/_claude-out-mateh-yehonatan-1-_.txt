(Siman 87, seif 3.) "And the flesh of a non-domesticated animal and poultry etc. is permitted in cooking and in benefit etc." This ruling was written by the Rambam, and it must be explained from the passage in perek Kol HaBasar, daf 113, regarding [the teaching] that the word "kid" serves as an exclusion for non-domesticated animals and impure poultry. And the Talmud questions this according to Shmuel: but we require the word "kid" to include the carcass! And it answers: Shmuel holds that ein issur chal al issur, etc. If so, according to us, who hold that ein issur chal al issur, we necessarily require the word "kid" to include both the prohibition of cooking with milk and the carcass, as the Talmud's question implies. And if so, from where do we derive that "kid" comes to exclude non-domesticated animals, poultry, and non-kosher animals?

And do not say that the Rambam holds that it is more reasonable to establish the word "kid" as excluding non-domesticated animals and poultry rather than as including the carcass — unlike Shmuel — for if so, what compelled the Talmud there on daf 116, where it holds that Rabbi Akiva maintains issur chal al issur, to say that it is more reasonable to establish the verse as excluding non-domesticated animals and poultry rather than as including the carcass? Rather, perforce: were it not that Rabbi Akiva holds issur chal al issur, we would establish the verse as including the carcass.

And if so, a difficulty arises, for it states there: it was stated — regarding one who cooks forbidden fat in milk — Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi: one says he is liable to lashes and one says he is not liable. Regarding eating, everyone agrees it is forbidden. The dispute is regarding cooking. The one who says he is liable holds that it is one single prohibition. And the one who says he is not liable holds: since the Torah expressed it in the language of "cooking," this comes to teach that since one is not liable to lashes for eating it, one is also not liable to lashes for cooking it.

If so, according to the one who says one is liable for cooking because it is one single prohibition, we do not need a verse for cooking to include the carcass. And if the word "kid" comes to include the carcass in order to forbid eating it — that is not consistent with the semantic implication of the inclusive force of the word "kid." And as for what is said — that from the superfluous element in the verse "you shall not cook" we derive the prohibition regarding eating — nevertheless, certainly the inclusion written in the verse does not apply to eating alone, for eating is not stated explicitly in the verse. Therefore, certainly "kid" comes to exclude non-domesticated animals and poultry.

And as for the fact that the Talmud earlier required the explanation that Rabbi Akiva holds issur chal al issur — that is according to the one who says: since one is not liable to lashes for eating it, one is also not liable to lashes for cooking it. In that case, we indeed need the word "kid" to include the carcass, that is, to render one liable for cooking it. Therefore they were compelled to conclude that he holds issur chal al issur. Thus there is no difficulty with the Rambam, who holds like the one who says one is liable for cooking; therefore "kid" comes to exclude non-domesticated animals and poultry. Consider this carefully.
