Immediately after Shabbat. Specifically for a gentile doing [the work] we require “kedei she’asu,” lest we suspect one might tell him to do it — but a Jew would not listen even to Rashi’s reason that one not benefit from Shabbat labor — siman 319 — still here it is permitted because in an uncommon matter they did not decree [Tosafot Chullin 15a], unlike Bach: if it was Motza”ei Yom Tov it is forbidden because Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov [Lekutei Mahari”v siman 2]. Even Ha’ezer siman 102: if that substance mixed with others it is nullified and is not “nisraf labechirut” like terumah — see there — and it requires analysis: the case is not like terumah proof — terumah is not “nisraf” for anyone: for a kohen even today permitted; for Yisrael forever forbidden — but here once the name of something that is “nisraf” for others applies to him, for him too it is not nullified as Bikkurim chapter 3: if bikkurim mixed — forbidden even to zarim even though for them it would be “she’ela” since for kohanim it is “nisraf” — zarim forbidden — so Ra’avad and Rambam in Mishnah commentary and in his work chapter 4 Bikkurim; however Rosh wrote it is not “nisraf” since forbidden to zarim — still one should be stringent; what Yoreh De’ah siman 102 says perhaps speaks when mixed after Shabbat — then truly like terumah — this is main; and even if Rambam commentary implies specifically mixed to kohen forbidden to Yisrael — in Mishneh Torah it does not imply so — and his commentary words can be read thus. It appears to me that one for whom it was cooked has the same law as himself — Yoreh De’ah siman 99:5 — same reason because of penalty; however from Rabbeinu Yerucham there it implies only there we suspect telling gentile to nullify — but here in any case must wait kedei she’asu when gentile cooked — and for Jew we do not suspect “adam choteh,” Rema 103:1.
