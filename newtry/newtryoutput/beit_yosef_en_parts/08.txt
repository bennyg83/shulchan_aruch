Just as it is forbidden to cook with fire, so it is forbidden to cook with a “offspring of fire” — e.g. placing an egg beside a pot, etc. Mishnah in Keirah (Shabbat 38a): One may not place an egg beside a kettle so it will roll [and cook]; nor crack it on hot cloths; Rabbi Yosei permits. One may not bury it in road dust on weekdays so it will roast — Rashi: beside the kettle — a copper kettle — “so it will roll” — roast somewhat until rolled; “nor crack on cloths” — not crack it on a cloth heated on Shabbat in the sun so it cooks from the cloth’s heat; “on weekdays in road dust” — heated by sun.

In the Gemara they asked: what about rolling? Rav Yosef said: rolling — liable for a sin-offering. Why? We learned “nor crack on cloths,” and we say (39b) there is a Mishnah: one places a stew in a pit… and warms cold [liquid] in sun — let us say Rabbi Yosei and not the rabbis? Rav Nachman: in sun all agree it is forbidden [as indirect fire]; they disagree only regarding “offspring of sun”: some decree offspring of sun because of offspring of fire; some do not decree.

Rashi: “offspring of fire” — if this cloth was heated originally by fire it is forbidden — that is cooking; “offspring of sun” — cloth heated by sun. The Rif ruled like the first Tanna; so too the Rambam in chapter 9.
