Regarding food that was cooked (the matter of “she’uya”): Rabbi Meir holds that if it was done unintentionally (shegagah), it is permitted even on that same day; Rabbi Yehudah holds that if unintentional, it is permitted only after Shabbat; Rabbi Yochanan Hasandlar holds that if unintentional, it is forbidden to him forever — [these positions appear] in the first chapter of Chullin (15a), in “HaNizakin” (Gittin 53a), in “Eilu Na’aros” (Ketubot 34a), and in “Merubah” (Bava Kamma 71a). The Rif cited this in “Keirah.” In the first chapter of Chullin it says that when a rabbi teaches his students he teaches like Rabbi Meir, but when he delivers the public discourse he teaches like Rabbi Yehudah, because there are many ammei ha’aretz [who might misunderstand leniency]. The Rif ruled like Rabbi Yehudah, and so did the Rambam in chapter 6. The Ran explained the reason: presumably the rabbi does not teach his students like Rabbi Meir except because he holds that between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah the halacha was only stated tentatively (“metin”), not as a fixed ruling — but we hold that the halacha was stated definitively, so we adopt Rabbi Yehudah. Moreover, Rav himself, when he taught the discourse, taught like Rabbi Yehudah on account of ammei ha’aretz; we too have many ammei ha’aretz among us. Rav Hamaggid in chapter 26 [of Hilchot Shabbat in Tur] wrote that the Geonim agreed, and the Ramban decided likewise — therefore one inclines toward the words of the Rosh in the first chapter of Chullin. But Tosafot wrote there that he teaches them like Rabbi Meir, implying that such is the halacha; and Rava taught in “Keirah” like Rabbi Meir; Semag and Sefer HaTerumah likewise — except that Semag wrote that since when he teaches his chapter he teaches like Rabbi Yehudah because of ammei ha’aretz, therefore we too should teach like Rabbi Yehudah. For practical halacha: since the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh agree on one view, and the Geonim and Ramban also hold thus, we rule accordingly.
