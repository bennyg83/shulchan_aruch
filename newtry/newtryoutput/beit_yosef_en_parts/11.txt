That which we learned: “All that came in hot water erev Shabbat one soaks in hot water on Shabbat; all that did not come in hot water one rinses in hot water on Shabbat, except old salted fish and kulyas ha’ispanim — rinsing them completes their labor.” Rashi: “All that came in hot water” — meaning was cooked — one soaks in hot water so it dissolves; “all that did not come” — like dry meat eaten with difficulty — one rinses — we do not call that its cooking — “but one does not soak”; except old salted fish — fish salted a year ago — and kulyas ha’ispanim — fish eaten because of its saltiness by rinsing in hot water — even rinsing completes their processing and is cooking — end quote.

In the Gemara: if one rinsed old salted fish and kulyas in hot water — liable for sin-offering; from Rashi’s words it implies only if cooked before Shabbat may one soak in hot water on Shabbat — he did not clarify whether we need fully cooked or ben drusa’i suffices. Tosafot wrote in Keirah (39a): “All that came in hot water” means fully cooked as explained in chapter Chavit like Rabbi Abba’s chicken; so Rabbeinu Yerucham in Halachot Gedolot; so Hagahot chadashot chapter 9. But Rambam chapter 22: something cooked before Shabbat or soaked in hot water erev Shabbat — even if now cold — permitted to soak in hot water on Shabbat — from his words it seems even not ben drusa’i — since it soaked in boiling water it counts as “cooked” for not being considered cooking on Shabbat when soaking on Shabbat — and Mishnah’s wording “came in hot water” troubled him — not “was cooked” — therefore Rashi in Keirah explained “all that came in hot water” — any salted thing that came in hot water erev Shabbat — one may soak again on Shabbat — no issue of “repair” since partially repaired already — end quote — meaning “came” applies to salted and unsalted — for unsalted “came” means cooking; for salted item “came” alone without “was cooked.” Rambam did not want to force Mishnah thus and wrote plainly “cooked or soaked,” without distinguishing salted.

Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote: Mishnah “one soaks in hot water on Shabbat” — even in first vessel permitted — so Ran Keirah — I wrote shortly below; so Tosafot there (39a). Mishnah “all that did not come in hot water one rinses” — Tosafot Keirah — according to Rashba’s explanation “pouring is like second vessel” fits here: one does not soak in first vessel but rinses from first vessel by pouring; but according to Rabbenu Tam pouring is like first vessel — then “rinses” means from second vessel — if so why “rinses” only — even soaking should be allowed… Orcho Zeira: even second vessel one does not soak — since water is hot it looks like cooking — or Rabbeinu Yerucham: one soaks because discussing second vessel — Mishnah says “rinses” to teach even rinsing completes labor for old salted fish and kulyas — end quote.

Kulyas ha’ispanim — Rashi Keirah: fish called tunina. Sefer HaPardes wrote old salted fish and kulyas are both tarit — not so: old salted is heavily salted meat — precise wording “old” — cannot eat at all without rinsing in hot water; tarit I say permitted to rinse even in cold water on Shabbat like any amount — like rinsing cups and bowls permitted on Shabbat — they used to say forbidden to eat tarit (tunina) without erev Shabbat rinsing — tarit rinsing is not completion of labor such that rinsing on Shabbat forbidden — eaten because of salt — anything eaten because of salt its rinsing is not completion — “tarit tarufa sailors eat raw from saltiness” — anything eaten from saltiness rinsing is not completion — kulyas ha’ispanim heavily salted fish impossible to eat from salt without hot rinsing — end quote — his words are tangled — if so even hot water should be permitted to rinse tunina — why permit only cold?
