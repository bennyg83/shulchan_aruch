“One who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat is permitted to a healthy person,” etc. (first chapter of Chullin, ibid.): Rabbi Yitzchak bar Ada reported in the name of Rav: “One who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat is forbidden to a healthy person; one who cooks for a sick person on Shabbat is permitted to a healthy person.” Why? This [slaughter] is fit for a cup, that [cooking] is not fit for a cup. Rav Papa said: Sometimes the slaughterer’s [meat] is permitted — e.g. when there was already a sick person before Shabbat; sometimes cooking is forbidden — e.g. when he cut for him a squash. Rav Dimi of Neharde’a reported the halacha: One who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat is permitted to a healthy person in the innards, because one cannot obtain a kezayit of meat without slaughter — when he slaughters, he slaughters with the sick person in mind. One who cooks for a sick person on Shabbat is forbidden to a healthy person, by decree lest one increase for him.

Rashi explained: “One who slaughters for a sick person” who fell ill on Shabbat — forbidden for a healthy person to eat from that animal that day; for although there is no Shabbat prohibition because saving a life overrides Shabbat, there is still muktzeh: at twilight it was muktzeh and was not fit to be slaughtered on Shabbat, for he was not yet sick. “One who cooks meat slaughtered yesterday and today cooks it for one who fell ill today” — permitted to a healthy person: that meat was fit at twilight for this healthy person to consume raw; therefore there is no muktzeh, and there is no Shabbat prohibition because he cooked for the sick person. This slaughter [case] was not fit for drinking at twilight — there is muktzeh even without a Shabbat prohibition. Sometimes slaughter for a sick person is permitted to a healthy person to drink — e.g. when there was a sick person before Shabbat: no muktzeh and no Shabbat issue. “One who cooks” for one who fell ill today is forbidden to a healthy person — e.g. he cut a squash from the attached vine and cooked it: there is muktzeh because it was attached at twilight.

The halacha: “One who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat…” — for a sick person from before Shabbat: he slaughters with the sick person in mind, and we need not decree lest one increase for a healthy person, for because of that olive-sized portion for the sick person he must slaughter the whole animal. The Rif in Keirah and the first chapter of Chullin recorded Rav Dimi’s halacha that one who slaughters for a sick person is permitted in the innards, etc., but omitted Rabbi Yitzchak bar Ada’s statement that one who slaughters is forbidden to a healthy person. The Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Chullin that the reason is that it is not the halacha — the discussion concerns one who fell ill that day and forbids because of muktzeh; but we rule like Rabbi Shimon, who does not hold muktzeh from prohibition except for a lamp lit on Shabbat that one extinguishes with one’s hands, and dried figs and raisins and the like, and muktzeh from financial loss — as the Rif ruled in the first chapter of Shabbat; and the Rambam in chapter 2 wrote plainly that one who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat may eat raw meat from it… The Ra’avad commented on this: provided there was a sick person from before Shabbat. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote: for us who rule like Rabbi Shimon, muktzeh on Shabbat applies only to muktzeh from prohibition that one actively rejected with one’s hands; if one did not reject with one’s hands it is permitted — even if the animal was muktzeh from the prohibition of slaughter, he did not actively reject it. But the Ran wrote: one who slaughters for a sick person is permitted in the innards only if the sick person fell ill before Shabbat; if he fell ill on Shabbat it is forbidden to a healthy person because what was slaughtered on Shabbat was muktzeh from twilight — and this is clear from this sugya in the first chapter of Chullin. The Rif stated the matter generally without full explanation — end quote.

It appears to me that the Ran holds Rav Dimi does not disagree with Rabbi Yitzchak bar Ada or Rav Papa except regarding what they permit — cooking for one who fell ill today from meat slaughtered yesterday — for him [Rav Dimi] even so it is forbidden by decree lest one increase. But regarding slaughter, all agree: if ill from before Shabbat — permitted; if fell ill that day — forbidden because of muktzeh — and so Rashi explained. According to the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham it seems Rav Dimi disagrees even regarding slaughter and says in every case it is permitted, even if he fell ill that day. If you prefer to say that even for them Rav Dimi disagrees only about cooking but regarding slaughter even Rav Dimi permits only when ill from before Shabbat — as Rashi — one could say he holds that because the Stam [anonymous voice] says “one who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat is permitted to a healthy person,” the Rif cited it so one should hear that even when he fell ill that day it is permitted because we rule like Rabbi Shimon that muktzeh applies only when actively rejected. One may question the Ran: how did he not notice what the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham said — since we rule like Rabbi Shimon that muktzeh applies only to a lamp lit that Shabbat or dried figs and raisins (Shabbat 45a; Rashi: two rejections — set aside and unfit), how can there be muktzeh in this animal not actively rejected? One could answer: it is compared to attached produce: even without active rejection it is forbidden — Beitzah 24a, a gentile who brought a gift: if of that species there is [produce still] attached, it is forbidden; Rashi there — because of muktzeh; even Rabbi Shimon — because anything not gathered erev Yom Tov one mentally abandons, like figs and raisins. So too here, since it was not slaughtered erev Shabbat one abandoned it. According to his view who wrote there on Rashi’s words: “The Razah wrote he did not need all this, for it is clear that Rabbi Shimon admits all muktzeh from prohibition without intent on it, like cup, bowl, lamp — that is his reason for bechor: if he transgressed and inspected it is not inspected because he holds one does not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov — it is muktzeh from its sanctity and prohibition” — his words seem primary though the Ramban disagreed — end quote — it is clear that without Rashi’s particular reason one still correctly says there is muktzeh in an animal slaughtered for one who fell ill that day.

The Rashba wrote in the first chapter of Chullin that some say even Rabbi Shimon who does not hold muktzeh from prohibition admits slaughter is forbidden — even Mar brei d’Rav Yosef who distinguishes Rabbi Shimon regarding animals that died (Beitzah 27a) admits here… Rabbeinu wrote that Rabbi Shimon certainly: since muktzeh from prohibition applies only when actively rejected, like cup, bowl, lamp — two weaknesses — slaughter on Shabbat is certainly permitted to eat from that day.

One might ask the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Rashba’s teacher: what distinguishes an animal from attached produce? Just as by gentile’s gift we say since he could have gathered erev and did not, he abandoned it and it is muktzeh — so too an animal slaughtered for a sick person who fell ill that day should be forbidden to a healthy person from muktzeh. I would answer that for them the reason of gentile’s gift is not muktzeh but decree lest one climb and detach — Tosafot Beitzah 3a first answer — and even if due to muktzeh, that follows Rabbi Yehudah; we rule like him for Yom Tov per Rif/Rambam, but on Shabbat we are lenient like Rabbi Shimon — not muktzeh except lest climb and detach. But the Rosh wrote regarding gentile’s gift that even Rabbi Shimon: muktzeh since not gathered yesterday — one might say the Rosh holds only attached [produce] has “not gathered yesterday,” because anyone can pick by hand; an animal needs expert slaughter with a checked knife and effort — perhaps he intended to slaughter but lacked expert or knife or time — non-prevention from slaughter does not prove he permanently designated it [as abandoned].

One could answer that we hold like those who say (Shabbat 45a; Beitzah 27a) Rabbi Shimon distinguishes even dead animals — permitted to move them — so they are not muktzeh; if slaughtered permissibly for a sick person even who fell ill that day — permitted… One might say that whatever is not muktzeh for carrying is not muktzeh for eating — though this does not clearly follow from what the Rosh wrote in Beitzah “Ein Muktzeh LeChatzi Shabbat.”
