That which our teacher wrote — that it is permitted to eat it raw and without salting — Tosafot and the Rosh in the first chapter of Chullin (14a) [say so] regarding “the colleagues wished to say Rabbi Yehudah,” etc.; so too the Ran there regarding “one who slaughters for a sick person on Shabbat.” It is written in Orach Chaim [another work]: One who cuts produce not fully cooked for a sick person on Shabbat — forbidden to a healthy person because of muktzeh, even if ill from before Shabbat, for there is no “preparation” in attached [produce] in such a case because it continues to grow — so too wrote the Ra’ah.

That which we say — cooking for a sick person on Shabbat is forbidden lest one increase — the Rashba explained: our teacher’s explanation is a decree lest after one sets the pot on the fire one add another piece for oneself; therefore we decree lest one do so and come to a Torah prohibition of stoning. But one should not explain “lest he increase pieces in the pot before placing it on the fire,” for even if he does so there is only a rabbinic prohibition, not a Torah one.

In siman 331 I will write the Ran’s words in chapter 2 of Beitzah about one who increases on Shabbat beyond the needed measure — whether forbidden from the Torah or rabbinically.

I already wrote at the beginning of this siman that all agree that one who cooks on Shabbat for a sick person — it is permitted for a healthy person immediately after Shabbat and we do not require “kedei she’asu.” The Agur in the name of Rashba’s responsum: one who cooks on Shabbat for a sick person where there is danger — forbidden to [another] sick person afterward when there is no danger, since it can be done through a gentile. I found that responsum in siman 714; the reason given is lest sometimes no gentile or slave can be found and this person will increase for himself. Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of Gittin (8b) that perhaps only for milah one may increase, as there (Eruvin 68a) “comfort him through his mother”; but for other needs even through a gentile it is forbidden to increase — so too from the Ran’s words, which I will write shortly. The Ran wrote in “Ein Ma’amidin”: when Ravin came he said we heal [by violating Shabbat] for everything except idolatry, forbidden relations, and bloodshed — and a gentile who cooked for a sick person on Shabbat: permitted to a healthy person after Shabbat — there is no issue of Bishulei Akum at all, for marriage-like attachment does not apply here — and he wrote that this reasoning is not a proof.
