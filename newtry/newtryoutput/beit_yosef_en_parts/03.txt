That which our teacher wrote — that one need not wait “kedei she’asu” (the time it would take to prepare that food) — the Rambam likewise wrote in chapter 6, and Rabbeinu Yonah held the same. The reason is that we require “kedei she’asu” only for something not ordinarily prepared by a Jew by hand, lest because it seems trivial to him he will do the same another time; but for something a Jew normally does with his hands there is no such concern — if you permit it immediately he would openly cook [which we do not permit]. So too the Rosh and Ran, against Rashi, the Ge’onim, and Ramban, who also require “kedei she’asu.” The Rashba wrote in the first chapter of Chullin. However, all agree that one who cooks for a sick person on Shabbat, or who cut for him a squash [from the attached vine] and cooked it — it is permitted for a healthy person immediately after Shabbat; we do not require “kedei she’asu” except when Shabbat was desecrated, whether unintentionally, intentionally, or otherwise; but here it was done permissibly: for a healthy person it is forbidden only because of muktzeh or a rabbinic decree lest one increase for him — therefore we do not require “kedei she’asu.” So wrote the Ran; and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote in chapter 2 that the commentators agreed.
