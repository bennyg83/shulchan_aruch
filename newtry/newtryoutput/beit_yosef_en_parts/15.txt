A copper kettle removed from the fire… Mishnah Keirah (41a): A kettle one emptied — one may not put cold water in to heat it; but one puts into it or into a cup to thin [make drinkable]. Gemara: what does it say? Abaye: if kettle emptied and hot water remains — one may not put in a little water to heat it; one puts abundant water to thin it. Kettle from which one poured water — one may not put any water because it strengthens [the metal] — and Rabbi Yehudah’s position — davar she’ein mitkaven forbidden — Rashi: emptied from fire for oneself — “strengthening” means when metal vessel is hot and cold is added it strengthens the vessel — that completes smiths’ labor.

Afterward we say: Rav — only measure to thin but measure to strengthen forbidden; Shmuel — even measure to strengthen permitted — Rashi: “only emptied” — permitted to put cold; “only measure to thin” — not to strengthen — like not completely filled; Shmuel — even measure to strengthen — fully filled. Rav understands “emptied” he poured out hot water — Shmuel — even measure to strengthen — they filled completely. We establish Shmuel like Rabbi Shimon who permits unintentional [melacha]; since we rule like Rabbi Shimon the halacha follows Shmuel — in every case permitted to put abundant water to thin.

Ran wrote: even though Rabbi Shimon admits pesik reisha — here not pesik reisha because when smiths strengthen they first overheat the vessel — here water inside prevents such heating… Rabbeinu Yerucham chapter 12 similarly wrote from Rambam’s words: anything one does not intend to strengthen — should not call pesik reisha — when intending one performs melacha; when not intending no melacha — one does not want to make a vessel from it.
