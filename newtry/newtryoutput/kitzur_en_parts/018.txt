Introduction to the section: There is no Torah prohibition of cooking except through the heat of fire. This includes both cooking directly with fire, such as roasting or cooking on a stove, and cooking on something that was heated by fire and remains hot, called toldot ha-or, such as sand on which a bonfire had burned. By contrast, the Torah did not forbid cooking in the sun. This section explains that cooking directly in the sun is permitted even initially, but the Sages forbade cooking with something heated by the sun, called toldot hachamah, such as cooking on sand that became very hot from the sun, because it resembles cooking with toldot ha-or. Cooking in the sun itself is permitted even rabbinically, but cooking with toldot hachamah is rabbinically forbidden. Cooking with electricity, such as in an electric oven or on electric burners, has the status of fire and is forbidden by Torah law.
