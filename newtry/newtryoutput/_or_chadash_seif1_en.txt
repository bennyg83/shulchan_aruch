Cooking and “ma’aseh Shabbat” (Shabbat labor-product): according to which Tanna is the halakhah determined? Beit Yosef in seif 1, s.v. uleinyan hamitbashel, records a dispute. It should be noted that Tur Choshen Mishpat 378:18 writes that the Rosh, regarding [the rules of] paying “four and five,” rules like R. Yochanan the sandal-maker; Bach there explains that when the Rosh discusses ma’aseh Shabbat he rules leniently like Rabbi Yehudah, whereas regarding monetary law he rules like R. Yochanan the sandal-maker—that is, because he is uncertain how the halakhah should go: in monetary matters he rules not to extract payment [from the defendant], while in prohibitions he rules leniently since we hold ma’aseh Shabbat is rabbinic. And in Tur 373:1 it is explained from Beit Yosef s.v. katav Baal haItur that the rabbis of Cologne and R. Yoel hold the halakhah follows Rabbi Yehudah; so too Ra’avan (father-in-law of R. Yoel) in his laws of slaughter in path hashir shura 26 and in siman 211 he holds like Rabbi Yehudah; Meiri on Shabbat 38a s.v. hamvashel likewise; while Shevulei haLeket shibbolet 122 writes that the halakhah follows Rabbi Meir.

When ma’aseh Shabbat renders food forbidden for the entire Shabbat, must one wait after Shabbat ends for kedei sheyaasu? Beit Yosef in seif 1 s.v. umah shekatav Ribenu ein cites a dispute; note Shevulei haLeket shibbolet 86 cites R. Simcha of Speyer that one must wait; so too Semag negative commandment 95 in the laws of the baker; Ra’avan in slaughter path hashir shura 27; and Ra’ah in Bedek haBayit 1:4:18 tends that way.

Ma’aseh Shabbat performed by a Jew in a forbidden manner when the same outcome could have been achieved permissibly: Rash in Demai 7:4 s.v. shnei login—in what he wrote “and if the stipulation of ‘I am prepared [to pay],’ etc., is permitted,” he writes that ma’aseh Shabbat is forbidden only when one cannot produce that same result by a permitted route—and Tur and Beit Yosef in 325:10 cite a parallel dispute regarding a gentile who performs melakhah for a Jew on Shabbat; see what I will write there.

Rabbinic ma’aseh Shabbat: Gra in his commentary seif katan 2 brings proofs to permit [benefit] in cases of shegagah even according to Shulchan Arukh who rules like Rabbi Yehudah; but apparently rabbinic ma’aseh Shabbat depends on an unresolved Gemara: according to Rashi on Beitzah 17a s.v. issura deShabbat, and as implied there regarding ma’aser, rabbinic ma’aseh Shabbat in shegagah is certainly permitted and in mezid it is uncertain; others hold mezid is certainly forbidden.

One who slaughters for an ill person who fell ill on Shabbat—may a healthy person eat [the meat], or is it muktzeh? Beit Yosef in seif 2 cites a dispute; note Rashba in Torat haBayit haArukh 1:4:18 writes it appears to him like those who permit; it is somewhat implied from Semag in the latter part of negative commandment 95, who writes anonymously that one who slaughters for an ill person—a healthy person may eat, and he made no distinction whether [the patient] fell ill today or earlier; Ra’avyah end of siman 197 likewise writes anonymously that it is permitted; Ra’ah in Bedek haBayit there 18 forbids.

One who cuts fruit for an ill person—when the patient recovers, is the fruit muktzeh? Beit Yosef in seif 2 cites Ra’ah that it is muktzeh; however see what I will write on this in 328:3.

“Cooking” through solar derivatives is forbidden: Shulchan Arukh and Beit Yosef in seif 3 cite this; note Ra’avyah siman 197 ot 32 and Meiri Shabbat 38a s.v. amar haMeiri hamishnah.

A stew that contains gravy, already cooked erev Shabbat and cooled—may it be reheated? Beit Yosef at the end of seifim 4–15 cites a dispute; note Ra’avyah siman 197 ot 28 writes anonymously that something fully cooked erev Shabbat may be reheated, without distinguishing whether it contains gravy.

Is there “cooking” for a moist cooked food that is still boiling hot yet not in a keli rishon? Shulchan Arukh in seif 4 writes that for moist cooked food there is a prohibition of “cooking” only if it cooled, but if it is still boiling there is not—hence he holds there is no “cooking” while boiling even if not in a keli rishon; note Ran on Shabbat 47b cites Rabbeinu Yona who holds that anything that cannot “cook” in place—for example something in a keli sheni—even if yad soledet bo, it is forbidden to put it into a keli rishon because cooking applies.

Is there cooking after roasting or baking? Beit Yosef in seif 5 cites a dispute; note Shevulei haLeket shibbolet 86 cites Yere’im that there is cooking [after roasting], and does not disagree.

For those who hold there is cooking after baking, is it forbidden to put bread into a keli sheni lest it finish “cooking”? Tur and Beit Yosef in seif 5 cite a dispute; note Shevulei haLeket there cites Yere’im who forbids and does not disagree.

According to Yere’im’s approach that there is cooking after baking, is there also baking after baking? Tur at end of seif 5 cites a dispute; note Ra’avyah siman 197 ot 31 writes he heard from Rabbeinu Eliezer of Metz that there is no baking after baking.

Where do we find in the Gemara that keli sheni does not cook and one may place food into it? We find so for oil on Shabbat 36a and for spices on Shabbat 38b in the Mishnah.

Is there cooking in keli shlishi for soft foods that cook quickly? Mishnah Berurah on seif 4 seif katan 39 elaborates how tea may be prepared on Shabbat; since he did not suggest the simple counsel of pouring into a third cup, it proves he holds that if the third or fourth cup is boiling hot it still constitutes cooking; yet some today wrote that “foods that cook quickly” were restricted only regarding keli sheni but in keli shlishi it is always permitted—that is incorrect: keli sheni does not cook, yet we say this rule does not apply to soft foods; therefore for soft foods we do not follow the kelim generalization but the temperature; hence whenever third, fourth, even hundredth vessel is at a heat level that would be forbidden in keli sheni, it is likewise forbidden in “hundredth vessel”—there is no reason to distinguish; only regarding keli rishon vs keli sheni could one say that even at a heat that would cook in keli rishon we say keli sheni does not cook—because for prohibitions we follow halakhic categories, not physical change in the object, and usually keli sheni does not cook so even boiling it is treated as non-cooking—but this could be said only for sheni vs rishon since we say anonymously Shabbat 36a keli sheni does not cook; for other vessels there is no distinction when heat level is the same—this needs no proof; Yere’im explicitly siman 104 ot 44 that keli shlishi cooks soft foods.

From Tur’s words in seif 5 it is clear Yere’im holds even for keli sheni vs rishon everything depends on heat level: one does not permit “cooking” in keli sheni at a temperature that would be forbidden in keli rishon; from Tur’s wording it appears he disagrees and permits “cooking” in keli sheni even at a temperature that would be forbidden in keli rishon.

How hot is yad soledet? Rashba in Torat haBayit haArukh 7:1:3 cites Eitur; among other things Eitur wrote that lukewarm water is slightly hotter than yad soledet such that an infant’s belly would be scalded; it is explained there that lukewarm water is water drawn in summer from a cold spring that stood a short time indoors until warmed slightly by summer heat—thus yad soledet is cooler than that, i.e., nearly cold.

Does salt “cook” in keli rishon and sheni? Shulchan Arukh, Beit Yosef, and Rema in seif 9 cite a dispute; note Semag negative commandment 95 baker laws writes one may put in keli rishon and one who is stringent receives blessing; Ra’avan siman 437 writes anonymously one may put in keli rishon; Maorot Shabbat 38b s.v. umelach write salt does not cook in keli rishon; so too R. Chananel Shabbat 38b s.v. melach; Ra’avyah siman 203 s.v. ha'ilpas. Beit Yosef cites that “salt does not cook in keli rishon” means specifically once removed from the fire but on the fire it certainly cooks; note Rashi Shabbat 38b s.v. shebekelei and Maharam Chalavah Pesachim 30a s.v. birotechim.

How long does keli rishon retain keli rishon status before becoming keli sheni? Beit Yosef in seif 9 cites Hagahot Mordekhai that only immediately after removal from the fire it is keli rishon but after brief delay it is keli sheni; Beit Yosef disagrees and writes it remains keli rishon as long as yad soledet bo; note Hagahot Mordekhai’s source is Ra’avyah siman 202 ot 11; Rashi Shabbat 38b s.v. lo yiten and Meiri Shabbat 38b s.v. amar haMeiri write keli rishon cooks as long as boiling—implying even shortly after removal from fire—while Tosafot Shabbat 36a s.v. veShima write keli rishon cooks as long as yad soledet bo.

May one pour from keli rishon onto something not cooked? Beit Yosef in seif 10 cites whether iruy constitutes cooking; likewise in siman 318 & Ta"n—see what I will write there.

If one inserted a vessel into keli rishon and lifted it with [liquid] from keli rishon, is that vessel considered keli rishon or sheni? See what I will write in Yoreh Deah end siman 92.

Pouring from keli rishon into mostly cold liquid: Tur in seif 12 and Beit Yosef seifim 11–12 cite that one may pour keli rishon into cold—note even if generally iruy cooks, Ran Shabbat 47b s.v. tanya writes iruy does not cook when pouring liquid into liquid where they mix; Ra’avyah siman 202 ot 11 writes when pouring into liquid it does not cook because cold water in the vessel overwhelmingly cools the pour.

Putting hot into cold in ambati (bathing vessels): Beit Yosef seifim 11–12 cites dispute whether R. Shimon ben Menasya follows the latter clause and halakhah follows him—then in ambati [mixing] hot and cold either way is forbidden—or we are lenient like Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua who was not strict; note Eshkol hilchot ba’al keri s.v. venish’al Ribenu (4.) writes Rabbeinu Hai held we are lenient like Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua; so ruled R. Chananel Shabbat 38b s.v. pisqa aval and Ra’avyah siman 202 ot 10, and so ruled the Geonim.

That which we say one may not put cold into hot in ambati—does it refer to ambati as keli sheni? Beit Yosef there cites dispute whether it means keli rishon or sheni; note Ra’avyah siman 202 ot 10 s.v. venireh writes it refers only to ambati resting on the fire, but if removed from fire it is permitted—even though still keli rishon.

What is the reason one may not put cold water into ambati? Beit Yosef cites dispute tied to whether ambati is keli rishon or sheni; Ra’avyah siman 202 ot 10 holds it discusses ambati on the fire and forbids because it resembles hatmana (insulation), and also since it is a place suited for cooking it is considered cooking rabbinically; since he did not simply write “because it cooks,” one must say it refers when pouring much cold water that cools the hot water, and when intent is to remove ambati from fire immediately—otherwise the fire would cook the water.

May one rest a vessel containing cold water inside keli rishon? Tur seif 13 forbids even if there is an intervening vessel; note Ran Shabbat 58b s.v. umide’amrin only inclined to forbid without decisive proof. Semag negative commandment 95 writes that forbidding placing into keli rishon to thaw is a decree lest one cook—his words do not clarify whether water is in the vessel or not; still his words imply even where no actual cooking it is forbidden by decree.

Is there a cooking prohibition on Shabbat for pickled items that are boiling-hot pickled like cooked? See Beit Yosef and Shulchan Arukh 321:3 and what I will write there.

A solid item that melted—forbidden after melting because of nolad? Beit Yosef seif 16 cites dispute; note Shevulei haLeket shibbolet 85 forbids crushing snow and hail by hand but if melted by itself it is permitted; Shevulei haLeket there cites his brother R. Binyamin for practice: only something that has never melted is forbidden such as snow/hail falling from sky; something that was liquid and froze—like water drawn at night found frozen in morning—may be crushed; by his reasoning there is no prohibition in anpanda that Tur cites since not done with hands and was already melted before.

Beit Yosef [discusses] Ran who wrote Baal haTerumah forbids melted fat as nolad; Ramban challenged from fruits not designated for squeezing where squeezing is permitted and the squeezed liquid is permitted; Ran rejected there that fat resembles fruits designated for squeezing—but one must say in that rejection Ran only answered Ramban’s proof permitting using hands; regarding nolad prohibition Ramban’s proof stands: Beitzah explains nolad is akin to muktzeh but stricter since it did not exist and was more “set aside”; all the more fruit juices not designated as beverages are not nolad even without usual extraction—how much more congealed fat normally clear, resembling designated fruits, should not count as nolad but as already present.

One must analyze Baal haTerumah: if prohibition is nolad why into cup is permitted—perhaps only if bitul of rabbinic prohibition lechatchilah is permitted; perhaps since when placed in cup it was still permitted one does not treat nullifying the forbidden substance directly; perhaps even drops already melted are not forbidden since one does not intend them; still one must analyze how beverage is permitted after snow melts: Shulchan Arukh YD 101:6 cites min in different min requires sixty—even snow in wine cup likely lacks sixty—one could say first melt nullifies; yet into water same min might be forbidden; Shevulei haLeket shibbolet 90 cites Sefer Terumah regarding wine from grapes on Shabbat forbidden while wine from erev Shabbat permitted—the issuing wine nullifies since first flow nullifies and no din davar she’ein lo matirin since not visible when immediately mixed; here too works; Beit Yosef suggests variant reading that Baal haTerumah’s prohibition is molid—then into cup is permitted since once nullified it is as if nothing new was generated.

Tur regarding warming anpanda: Tur seif 16 forbids heating a solid that melts because heat restores liquidity—nolad and forbidden; Sefer HaMitzvot permits since not done with hands—but difficult: even without molidei repair, where does nolad muktzeh go? Where does Sefer HaMitzvot answer Tur who forbids as nolad? One must say Tur means since it becomes “new” it is forbidden as molidei—but even though “new,” it is not nolad muktzeh prohibition since not completely “born.”

Beit Yosef regarding olive oil: Beit Yosef end seif 16 cites Mordekhi in name of Rabbeinu Yechiel miParis that plain olive oil is solid—he relied on this for a Gemara proof—but from where did he know this?

Placing cold food atop a hot pot on Shabbat is explained in Shulchan Arukh and Beit Yosef siman 258.
