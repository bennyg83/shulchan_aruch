Salted meat — Ashkenazi Responsa wrote: it implies ox meat does not cook in kli rishon and one may put raw meat in kli rishon removed from the fire; but if old salt possibly forbidden — it cooks quickly as said above; even if salted anew — granted meat itself does not cook, moisture and blood and salt inside cook and absorb — forbidden — end quote. From this it implies salt causes quick cooking — salt softens meat when it sits long in salt as salting meat after shechita from blood — that is when it sat there; salting anew does not yet soften meat but adds another taste for Shabbat prohibition because salt and moisture cook — so even without salt at all there is Shabbat prohibition because of cooking moisture; therefore the Tur here mentioned only moisture and not salt; and the Rosh who mentioned salt included another prohibition — not only Shabbat cooking but even on chol because of blood — that is Rema’s intent when he wrote “it appears to me even without salting,” etc., only he wrote “salted” meaning the Rosh wrote “salted” because otherwise — and it appears Shulchan Aruch could hold likewise — moisture obviously cooks; unless there is no moisture, e.g. it is dry — still since there is salt on it but dried on it — it cooks — therefore Rema did not write “there is a dispute” — and it appears to me — “and it appears to me,” etc. — there is no dispute since Tur explains moisture alone cooks — end quote; further what he wrote from blood prohibition…
