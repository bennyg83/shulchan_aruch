It became null in sixty parts of the food — and it is not “nisraf labechirut” after Shabbat, for even in a thousand it is not nullified, because here it was nullified before the prohibition arose: salt does not cook except like beef (as above), and one does not forbid because “for taste” it is done and not nullified as with leaven and spices — since presumably it was also salted from erev Shabbat but not fully salted, this is “this and that cause” and it is permitted, like terumah leaven and chullin leaven that fell into dough and together caused fermentation — we hold “zuzei” both cause and it is permitted — Beit Yosef in the name of Shach in the name of Rabbi Shimon. It appears to me very astonishing: we do not find “zuzei” unless at the time the forbidden factor acts the permitted factor also acts, like the leaven we mentioned — both ferment at once — and so all “zuzei” in Yoreh De’ah siman 142; here it first cooked with permitted salt and already did its work but did not suffice for taste, and then forbidden salt gives good taste — this is not “zuzei” but the forbidden causes good taste, and the difference is obvious: it is like a dish that has spices but they are insufficient and one adds forbidden spices that improve the dish — could that be permitted? There is no greater eating of issur than this. And “zuzei” applies only if they mixed before taste entered, i.e. before they came to the pot — then they give taste together; not in our case, which is like adding spices first on Shabbat in kli rishon — here too one clearly tastes in the mouth what the forbidden adds to the permitted. It appears to me to distinguish; one who would attack this distinction — the proof is on him. It seems one should not permit eating this on Shabbat but wait until after Shabbat — as I wrote.
