If they then cooked it, etc. — the Tur wrote in the name of Ra’avan of Metz: although there is no cooking after cooking for a dry thing, still there is cooking after roasting; his proof is from chapter Kol Shekadar 41a regarding korban pesach: if they roasted and then cooked — liable — whose view is it? Rabbi Yehudah, who said we fulfill with the rakik soaking in water on Pesach but not if boiled, for it is not called bread — even if already baked in the oven, since they cooked again it loses the name bread — so too there is cooking after baking for Shabbat. Rabbi Avraham disputed him: in chapter Kol Mah they bring Rabbi Yehudah for blessing — cooked vegetables get shehakol and not ha’adamah as initially like matzah for Rabbi Yehudah — and they conclude: Rabbi Yehudah said only that we need matzah taste and there is none — Beit Yosef praised Rabbi Avraham that his question is strong, for we hold its blessing is ha’adamah — so the dismissal that we need matzah taste follows halacha; so Tosafot there in chapter Kol She’ochel — one can dismiss because of matzah taste — Pesach is not like blessing. I am not worthy to decide between great mountains; still in my humble opinion one can explain Ra’avan’s words along these lines: he holds like Rivan and Rabbi Yehudah above in siman 1 regarding “metzamek vera lo” — there is no cooking after fully cooked. We already explained the reason: for “metzamek veyafeh lo” there is cooking because he benefits from Shabbat labor in a new taste — not so “metzamek vera lo” — so we say here this reasoning too: even for what you want to dismiss and explain Rabbi Yehudah’s view because we need matzah taste — that helps dismiss the Pesach case of roasted then cooked, for there the Written Law requires roasting and the matter does not depend on taste — we can say the name roasting was not lost; but matzah needs matzah taste — “bread of affliction” — so we can dismiss the Pesach case per Tosafot; but here for Shabbat this dismissal does not work: even if you say because we need matzah taste we follow the dish’s taste, and since taste changed by cooking we say he intended that on Shabbat — it is no better than “metzamek veyafeh lo,” where there is cooking even after cooking, all the more so after baking — we wrote per Tosafot who hold the dismissal applies also to Pesach because we need matzah taste — so one must say that for roasting one cannot say “we need roasting taste,” so one must say matzah teaches only where we need matzah taste from “bread of afflictive”; but it appears to me Ra’avan holds this dismissal does not apply to Pesach at all — even on Pesach one could say we need roasting taste — therefore one is liable for cooking after roasting; but for blessing the dismissal in chapter Kol Mah works — certainly vegetable taste before cooking remains afterward but improved — why should blessing be less than initially, ha’adamah — therefore the Gemara in chapter Kol Shekadar did not dismiss any view with “we need matzah taste” — so here for cooking on Shabbat obviously this dismissal does not exist — “melacha machshevet asrah Torah,” as many places; a fortiori rabbinically forbidden because of taste change — what Rema wrote here “forbidden” — so it appears correct in my humble opinion.
