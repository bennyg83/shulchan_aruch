(ז) <b>Therefore a widow who remained, etc.</b> Maharil wrote in responsum siman 46: a widow who did not swear on her ketubah, although she spent much from her ketubah, whether for taxes or for marrying off sons, we do not deduct it from her ketubah, for everything she spent was from the profit of her ketubah, unless the heirs want her to swear; then she takes from what their father left, and the surplus that existed at their father's death belongs to the heirs. This is the dissenting view. The first view is Mahari Weil, siman 176, who wrote: since the woman sat in the property for eighteen years, presumably she already spent for her sustenance and support more than the value of the property her husband left, so whatever is in her hand is from profit, and a woman does not collect her ketubah from appreciation after her husband's death. These are two views, and neither has proof, only his own reasoning. It appears, with Heaven's help, that Maharil's words are primary. First, we find in siman 101 that a widow who was silent twenty-five years and did not claim her ketubah has waived it, implying that for less than twenty-five years she may claim her ketubah. If Mahari Weil were correct, why would this be needed? In those twenty-five years she certainly spent more than the value of the property left by her husband. One cannot say that there it speaks where he was wealthy and left much money, for it is not plausible to establish it specifically where he left so large a sum that it would preserve the measure of her ketubah for so many years. Rather, the principal remains permanently, and the expenses are from profit, not from the principal. Another proof is from Bava Metzia 105, where if one partner says, "give me my half of the profit," Rashi explains that profit is subject to the principal, lest they lose in future business and the profit be needed to fill the principal. We learn that anything missing from the principal and later replenished by profit is not treated as profit until the deficiency of the principal is filled. So too here, whatever was diminished by the woman's expenses from the principal is replenished by the profit, and she properly takes the ketubah, like Maharil's view.
