**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>If she said: see.</b> This is Rashbam's view. According to Raavad, Mordechai, and Agudah, whom I cited nearby, even when she is sustained the improvement belongs to her, all the more here where she is not sustained. So too according to Rashba in the responsum cited by Beit Yosef; it implies that he speaks both of land and of movables, as is explicit in Raavad. This is also proven from Rashba's responsum: at the beginning he wrote that regarding movables the improvement is hers, because once she said "see," her law is like that of a robber. Later he did not give the reason of robbery. One must say that he first wrote that reason according to the poskim who hold that improvement is included in her handiwork when she is sustained; regarding movables, where robbery applies, if she says "see," her law is like a robber. But he himself holds that improvement is not included in her handiwork even when she is sustained, all the more when she is not sustained. Therefore even with land, where robbery does not apply, all the improvement is hers. The reason she takes all the improvement, more than another person who enters his fellow's field and improves it, is that she is considered as though she has a share in the assets, since she has a ketubah to collect. The "some say" cited here is the view of Tosafot and the Rosh, who hold that although she has a ketubah to collect, as long as beit din has not collected it for her she has no share, and her law is like another person entering his fellow's field. However, regarding movables one can say that there they agree, for the reason Rashba wrote.
