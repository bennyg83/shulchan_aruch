<b>ממקום למקום</b>. הנה בש"ס מבואר אליבא דרבא דקי"ל כוותי' הא דצ"ל בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם משום עדים אינן מצוים לקיים וכשאומר בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם נתקיים הגט ובמדינה א' מצוים לקיים ואצ"ל בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם ורמב"ם פוסק ממקום למקום צ"ל בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם אפילו במדינה א' ובמקום א' אצ"ל ב"נ ס"ל לפי המסקנא דתירץ שאני בני מחוזא דניידי יליף ממקום למקום דומה לבני מחוזא אבל בחד מקום אצ"ל ב"נ וכ"כ המגיד פ"ז ותוס' למדו מזה אפילו בחד מקום דינו כבני מחוזא:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>From place to place.</b> In the Talmud it is explained according to Rava, whom we rule like, that the reason one must say "it was written before me and signed before me" is because witnesses are not available to validate it. When he says "it was written before me and signed before me," the get is validated. In one country, witnesses are available to validate it, and one does not need to say "it was written before me and signed before me." The Rambam rules that from place to place one must say "it was written before me and signed before me" even in one country, while in one place one does not need to say "befanai nichtav." He holds that according to the conclusion, where the Gemara answers that the people of Mechoza are different because they travel, we learn that place-to-place is similar to the people of Mechoza, but within one place one does not need to say "befanai nichtav." So wrote the Maggid, chapter 7. Tosafot learned from this that even in one place its law is like the people of Mechoza.

<b>הרי זה אומר בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם</b>. הטעם כמ"ש בסמוך ואז נתקיים הגט על פיו אף על גב בעלמא קי"ל קיום צריך ב' עדים כאן משום תקנות עגונות הקילו חז"ל הואיל וקיום שטרות דרבנן הוא דקי"ל עדים החתומים כמה שנחקרו עדותן בב"ד דמי כמ"ש בש"ס אז מ"ש בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם אפי' אם אתא הבעל ומערער לא משגיחין בו ולהני פוסקים דס"ל כשטוען מזויף צריך קיום מדאורייתא ובסי' הקודם כתבתי דכן ס"ל להמחבר לפ"ז לכאורה קשה כשאתא הבעל ומערער שהגט מזויף למה מהני קיום של ע"א כיון שצריך קיום מדאורייתא ואפשר אם אתא הבעל מיד ומערער לא מהני העדות של ע"א אלא כשאינו מערער מיד בעת שאומר בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם ואז הוי כאלו נתקיים ע"פ ב' עדות תו אפילו אם מערער לא משגיחין ביה כיון דכבר נתקיים, ולהרמב"ם דס"ל כל עדות בשטר מדאוריית' אין עדות קשה ליישב סוגי' זו ועיין תשו' מהרשד"ם סי' א' ב' וכשנתקיים הגט ע"פ השליח גובה הכתוב' מן הלקוחות דנלמד מספר כתובה כשתנשא לאחר תטלי וכו' ול"ד למ"ש בסימן קמ"א סעיף נ"ג דאינה גובה מלקוחות דשם אין היתר גמור דהא יש לחוש שמא יבוא עוד ויערער לכן אינה גובה מלקוחות אבל כאן מותרת לגמרי ואין מועיל ערעור הבעל לכן גובה מן הלקוחות מיהו תוספ' כתובה כתב הר"ן והמגיד פ"ז בשם הרמב"ן דאינה גובה דאין מדרש כתובה אלא לעיקר כתובה ולא לתוספ' וכיון ממדרש כתובה אין לה לית לה מדינא דקי"ל אין קיום שטרות אלא בתרי עדים לענין ממון, ולכאורה נראה גם מן הבעל אינה גובה ת"כ דהא לענין ממון לא מהני קיום ע"א ומדרש כתובה לא אמרינן לת"כ מכ"ש כשמביא גט בא"י דאינה גובה ת"כ מבעלה מחמת ספר כתובה גובה מב"ח ות"כ לא מהני מדרש כתובה מיהו יש לומר לענין זה מהני הרשאה דהוי כאלו נתקיים הגט בשני עדים ותגבה ת"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>He says: it was written before me and signed before me.</b> The reason is as written nearby, and then the get is validated through him. Although generally we hold that validation requires two witnesses, here, because of the enactment for agunot, Chazal were lenient, since validation of documents is rabbinic, for we hold that witnesses signed on a document are considered as though their testimony was investigated in beit din, as written in the Talmud. Therefore, when he says "it was written before me and signed before me," even if the husband comes and challenges it, we do not pay attention to him.

According to those decisors who hold that when he claims it is forged, validation is required biblically, and in the previous siman I wrote that this is the view of the author, according to this it is seemingly difficult: when the husband comes and challenges that the get is forged, why does validation by one witness help, since validation is required biblically? Perhaps if the husband comes immediately and challenges, the testimony of one witness is not effective. It is effective only when he does not challenge immediately at the time the agent says "it was written before me and signed before me," and then it is as though it was validated by two testimonies. Afterward, even if he challenges, we do not pay attention to him, since it was already validated. According to the Rambam, who holds that all testimony in a shtar is not biblical testimony, this sugya is difficult to explain. See Responsa Maharshdam, siman 1 and 2.

When the get is validated through the agent, she collects the ketubah from purchasers, for this is learned from the ketubah text: when you marry another, you will take, etc. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 141, seif 53, that she does not collect from purchasers, because there the permission is not complete, for there is concern that he may yet come and challenge. Therefore she does not collect from purchasers. But here she is completely permitted, and the husband’s challenge is not effective. Therefore she collects from purchasers.

However, regarding the additional ketubah, the Ran and Maggid, chapter 7, wrote in the name of the Ramban that she does not collect, for the derivation from the ketubah applies only to the main ketubah and not to the addition. Since she does not have it by the ketubah derivation, she does not have it by law, for we hold that validation of documents requires two witnesses regarding monetary matters. At first glance, it appears that even from the husband she does not collect the additional ketubah, since regarding money, validation by one witness is not effective, and we do not apply the ketubah derivation to the additional ketubah. All the more so when one brings a get in Eretz Yisrael, where she does not collect the additional ketubah from her husband by virtue of the ketubah text: she collects from liened property, but the ketubah derivation is not effective for the addition. However, one can say that for this matter authorization is effective, for it is as if the get was validated by two witnesses, and she may collect the additional ketubah.

<b>וכן שליח שהביא גט מא"י וכו'</b>. אבל נשים השונאים זא"ז יש פוסלין להביא גט בא"י אפילו אם אומרות בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם כמ"ש בסימן קמ"א משום בנשי חיישינן טפי דלאו דיני גמירי והן סברי דבעל יכול לערער אפי' כשאומרת בפני נכתב וב"נ כיון בעלמא בא"י יכול לערער אפשר דטעה בזה ומעידים מחמת שנאה וסברוהו דבעל יכול לערער אבל בח"ל דא"י לערער לא אמרינן דסברא דבעל יכול לערער:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ג) <b>And likewise an agent who brought a get from Eretz Yisrael, etc.</b> But women who hate one another, some invalidate them from bringing a get in Eretz Yisrael, even if they say "it was written before me and signed before me," as written in siman 141. Regarding women we are more concerned, because they are not learned in the laws, and they think that the husband can challenge even when she says "it was written before me and signed before me." Since generally in Eretz Yisrael he can challenge, perhaps she errs in this and testifies because of hatred, thinking that the husband can challenge. But outside Eretz Yisrael, where he cannot challenge, we do not say that she thinks the husband can challenge.

<b>הרי זה פסול</b>. כ"כ הרמב"ם ולפי הכלל שכתב בפ"י אם נשאת לא תצא אף על גב לרמב"ם כל עדות בשטר אינו אלא מדרבנן מ"מ כל שאין ערעור עליו אינו פסול אלא מדרבנן ועיין בכ"מ, אבל אם הבעל מערער וא"י לקיים ס"ל דלא נעשה כתיקון חז"ל שתקנו אם מערער הבעל צריך קיום מ"ה הגט בטל ועיין במגיד פ"ז אבל לדעת תוס' והרא"ש והטור אם לא אמר בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם תצא וכ"פ בש"ג והיינו כשא"י ליטול ממנה וליתן ולומר ב"נ כמ"ש בסעיף ז':
**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) <b>This is invalid.</b> So wrote the Rambam. According to the rule he wrote in chapter 10, if she married, she need not leave. Although according to the Rambam all testimony in a shtar is only rabbinic, nevertheless whenever there is no challenge against it, it is invalid only rabbinically. See Kesef Mishneh. But if the husband challenges and it cannot be validated, he holds that it was not done according to the enactment of Chazal, who enacted that if the husband challenges, validation is required. Therefore the get is void. See Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7. But according to Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur, if he did not say "it was written before me and signed before me," she must leave. So ruled Shiltei Gibborim, namely where he cannot take it from her, give it again, and say "befanai nichtav," as written in seif 7.

<b>אינה מגורשת</b>. כך כתב הרמב"ם לפ"ז הגט בטל ואם נשאת תצא והולד ממזר ודאי והרא"ש כתב בשם הרמב"ם ספק ממזר הוא וכ"כ בטור ולכאורה תמוה הא רפ"ז מבואר דממזר ודאי הוא וב"ח כתב מ"ש הרא"ש ספק ממזר היינו היכא דהבעל מערער אחר שנאבד הגט אבל אם מערער כשהגט עדיין בעין וא"י לקיים הוי ממזר ודאי ואנ"ל דאיך כתב הרא"ש בשם הרמב"ם מה שהוא אינו כותב ובודאי ט"ס בהרא"ש ומ"ש הטור ספק ממזר הוא משום דס"ל כהני פוסקים שהביא המגיד שם דס"ל ספק ממזר הוא כי שמא יתקיים עוד ומ"ש באבד הגט ספק מגורשת לאו לענין ממזר כ"כ דהא כתב בערעור הבעל ספק ממזר ש"מ באבד הגט לאו ספק ממזר אלא ספק מגורשת לענין קידושין של אחר וכ"כ בדרישה וכאן נמי מוכח דס"ל להרא"ש והטור אם טוען מזויף צריך קיום מדאורייתא דאל"כ אינו ממזר ולא ספק ממזר דהא העדים החתומי' הם כאלו נחקרה עדותן בב"ד ואינו אלא פסול דרבנן אלא ודאי ס"ל דצריך קיום מדאורייתא וכבר הוכחתי כן בסי' הקוד' מן הפוסקים שהביא המגיד ועיין תשו' מהרי"ק שורש ע"ז ובש"ך בח"ה סי' מ"ו וא"י איך לא הזכירו מכל הני פוסקים וש"ך דלא הזכיר מה שמבואר בסי' זה ובסי' הקודם, ועיין תשו' מהרשד"מ סי' א' ב':
**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) <b>She is not divorced.</b> So wrote the Rambam. According to this, the get is void, and if she married, she must leave, and the child is a definite mamzer. The Rosh wrote in the name of the Rambam that he is a safek mamzer, and so wrote the Tur. At first glance this is astonishing, for at the beginning of chapter 7 it is clear that he is a definite mamzer. The Bach wrote that what the Rosh wrote, "safek mamzer," is where the husband challenged after the get was lost. But if he challenges while the get still exists and it cannot be validated, he is a definite mamzer. This does not appear to me, for how could the Rosh write in the name of the Rambam something that he does not write? Certainly there is a scribal error in the Rosh. What the Tur wrote, that he is a safek mamzer, is because he holds like those decisors brought by the Maggid there, who hold that he is a safek mamzer, for perhaps it will yet be validated.

What is written that when the get was lost she is doubtfully divorced does not mean regarding mamzer, for he wrote that when the husband challenges, the child is a safek mamzer. Learn from this that when the get was lost, it is not a safek mamzer, but rather she is doubtfully divorced regarding kiddushin from another. So wrote the Derishah. Here too it is proven that the Rosh and Tur hold that if he claims forgery, validation is required biblically, for otherwise he is not a mamzer and not a safek mamzer, since the signed witnesses are as though their testimony was investigated in beit din, and it is only rabbinically invalid. Rather, certainly they hold that validation is required biblically. I already proved this in the previous siman from the decisors brought by the Maggid. See Responsa Maharik, shoresh 77, and the Shach in Choshen Mishpat, siman 46. I do not know how they did not mention all these decisors, and the Shach did not mention what is explained in this siman and the previous siman. See Responsa Maharshdam, siman 1 and 2.

<b>כשם שא"צ לקיים הגט וכו'</b>. כ"כ הרא"ש ריש גיטין ושאר פוסקים לכאורה נראה לדייק כמה דיוקי' מזה א' דוקא ש"ה נאמן משום דהוא נאמן לומר ב"נ נתנו חז"ל ליה נאמנות ג"כ לומר דהוא שלוחו אבל ש"ק אינו נאמן גם משמע דוקא בח"ל דאומר ב"נ וב"נ נאמן ולא בא"י ולא כהרמ"ה ובסעיף י' בהג"ה לא משמע כן גם משמע אפילו בעיר א' נאמן ואפילו אם הבעל מכחישו ובסמוך גם בסי' קמ"א לא משמע כן וכתב בט"ז אשה שנתגרשה צריכה כתב מב"ד שנתגרשה ואין הרב נאמן להעיד ע"ז יחידי ול"ד לקיום שטרות דכאן מוציא אותה מחזקתה לכן צריכה היא מעשה ב"ד ואז יכולה להראות בכל מקום אשר תבוא דהיא מגורשת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Just as one does not need to validate the get, etc.</b> So wrote the Rosh at the beginning of Gittin and the other decisors. At first glance, several inferences can be made from this. First, specifically an agent for delivery is believed, because he is believed to say "befanai nichtav," and Chazal also gave him credibility to say that he is the agent. But an agent for receiving is not believed. It also implies that specifically outside Eretz Yisrael, where he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," he is believed, but not in Eretz Yisrael, unlike the Ramah. In seif 10 in the gloss it does not imply this. It also implies that even in one city he is believed, and even if the husband contradicts him. Nearby and also in siman 141 it does not imply this.

The Taz wrote: a woman who was divorced needs a writing from beit din that she was divorced, and the Rav is not believed to testify about this alone. It is not comparable to validation of documents, for here he removes her from her presumed status. Therefore she needs an act of beit din, and then she can show in any place she comes that she is divorced.

(ב) <b>Although he can write.</b> Although regarding testimony of her husband’s death, testimony by writing is effective, as written in siman 17, there they were lenient because of the enactment for agunot. But regarding the death of her husband, where there is no assistance, it is not effective. However, regarding testimony by writing, the issue is not credibility but that we should not distinguish between one testimony and another. Therefore, although they were lenient regarding testimony that her husband died for the above reason, nevertheless they were not lenient by get. So wrote the Beit Yosef.

One should not ask, according to the Rambam, why when one became mute, if he writes to divorce his wife, we follow his writing. There it is not testimony, but only revealing his intent. According to what the Beit Yosef wrote, it appears that even if he can speak and he writes "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," it is not effective, so as not to distinguish between one testimony and another.

See Responsa Rivash, siman 413, who validates authentication through writing. So too it is written in the gloss of Shulchan Aruch, Choshen Mishpat, siman 46, seif 7. According to this, writing "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" is also valid, since it is validation. What the Rivash wrote in siman 140, that it is not effective through writing, is specifically with a mute person who cannot speak; there writing is not effective. This is unlike where he can speak. So it is also precise there in the responsum. Although we hold regarding other testimony that one cannot testify in writing even if he can speak, that is specifically real testimony, where they need to say it with their mouths and writing is not effective at all, even if he can speak. But regarding validation they were lenient to testify through writing specifically when he can speak. In this way, what he ruled in the gloss there in seif 7 and seif 36 are both correct, unlike the Shach there.

In Responsa Rivash, siman 240, he answered differently: in testimony of death they were always more lenient. As for the fact that women who hate one another testify regarding a get but not regarding death, that is because with a get she says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," and the husband can no longer challenge, so there is no suspicion against her, not because of leniency.

(ג) <b>And she must say it, etc.</b> Although if she said plainly, "I was divorced," she is believed when the get is in her hand, as written nearby, nevertheless when she says that she is an agent, she must say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" like any other agent, and she is not believed through migo. (ב) <b>A husband who brings the get himself, etc.</b> This law was written by Orchot Chaim and brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 141. At first glance this law is astonishing, for in the baraita it says that a husband who brought a get does not need to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," as written at the beginning of Gittin, page 5. In Orchot Chaim he wrote this language: so that the husband can say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," since the husband himself brings it, as we say at the beginning of Gittin, the husband himself, etc., because because of our sins we are not bnei Torah like the earlier ones, etc.

It appears to me that Orchot Chaim holds like the Ra'avad, who holds the halacha follows Rabbah, who said that the reason the agent must say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" is because they are not expert in lishmah, and because witnesses are not available to validate it. Then one does not need "befanai nichtav." Likewise, the husband specifically after they learned does not need to say it. If so, now, because of our sins, we are not bnei Torah like the earlier ones, and therefore even the husband must say "befanai nichtav," for it is like before they learned.

But according to us, who hold like Rava, who said the reason is validation, the husband does not need to say "befanai nichtav," since he himself brought the get and his challenge is not effective. He is also not suspected of deliberately ruining her, as written in Tosafot. The Rama added on his own that he must say "befanai nichtav" so as not to confuse it with another agent, since we are not bnei Torah. He holds that the reason that we are not bnei Torah means that because of this it is proper to decree lest it be confused with another agent. This does not appear to me, for if so, regarding two who bring a get we should also be concerned lest it be confused with another agent. The Talmud says that after they learned, nevertheless one person and a woman must say "befanai nichtav" lest the matter return to its ruin, and so as not to distinguish between agencies. Nevertheless, when the husband brings the get, or two bring it, we do not say "do not distinguish between agencies," and they do not need to say "befanai nichtav." We do not decree because of another agent.

(ד) <b>And some say even if he did not appoint, etc.</b> So wrote Tosafot, the Rosh, the Ran, and the Rashba. <b>ויש מי שמצריך</b>. היינו סברות הרמ"ה בין אם שניהם מעידים שמסר לידו ובין אם א' מעיד צריכים עדים אלו להיות בעת מסירת הגט לידה ומכל שכן כששניהם שלוחים כי צריכים להיות בעת המסירה דהא צריכים לקיים שליחותם ועיין בב"ה והטעם דצריכים המעידים להיות בעת המסירה כי היכא דלא הוי חצי דבר כיון השליח לא יאמר ב"נ והעיקר הגט ע"י עדות אלו האומרים שמסר הגט לידו צריכים להיות שם, ובסעיף י"ב פסק בפשיטות כדעת הרמ"ה וכאן כתב ויש מי שמצריך וראיתי בבדק הבית שהקשה כאן על הרמ"ה למה הוי חצי דבר הרי עידי שליחות לחוד ועידי מסירה לחוד מ"ה כתב כאן ויש מי שמצריך והא דלא הקשה כן לעיל בסומא אפשר שאני סומא שאינו ראוי כלל לשליחות בלא עדים מודה הב"י דהוי חצי דבר אבל שליח כשר ושני מעיד בפניו מסר לידו כי היכא דאצ"ל ב"נ ס"ל דהוי דבר שלם, ומה שאחד העיד בפניו מסר הגט ליד השליח היינו שאומר שעשאו לשליח בפני שנים ומסר בפניו הגט כמ"ש ר"ס קמ"א דאין מצרפין השליח:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) <b>And there is one who requires.</b> This is the reasoning of the Ramah: whether both testify that he delivered it to him, or whether one testifies, these witnesses must be present at the time of delivery of the get to her. All the more so when both are agents, they must be present at the time of delivery, for they must fulfill their agency. See Bedek HaBayit. The reason the witnesses must be present at the delivery is so that it not be half a matter. Since the agent will not say "befanai nichtav," and the essence of the get is through these witnesses who say that he delivered the get to him, they must be there.

In seif 12 he ruled plainly like the Ramah, while here he wrote "there is one who requires." I saw in Bedek HaBayit that he asked here against the Ramah: why is this half a matter? The witnesses of agency are separate and the witnesses of delivery are separate. Therefore he wrote here "there is one who requires." The fact that he did not ask this above regarding a blind person may be because a blind person is not fit for agency at all without witnesses, so the Beit Yosef agrees that it is half a matter. But with a valid agent, where two testify that it was delivered to him in their presence so that he does not need to say "befanai nichtav," he holds that it is a whole matter. What it means that one testifies that the get was delivered to the agent in his presence is that he says the husband appointed him as an agent before two and delivered the get before him, as written at the beginning of siman 141, that we do not join the agent.
