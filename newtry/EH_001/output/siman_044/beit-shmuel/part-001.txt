<b>והיא מותרת לבעלה</b>. אף על גב אם גירש החרש ונשאת  לפקח אז אפילו לחרש אסורה לבעלה כמ"ש בסי' י' מ"מ כאן לא גירש לא חיישינן שמא יאמרו גירש כיון אפילו אם היה מגורש ליכא א"ד דהא קדושי חרש רק מדרבנן ואם בא פקח ונשא אותה אפילו חרש אסורה לבעלה כשם שאסורה לבועל כך אסורה לבעל ובספר ב"ה כתב כיון דליכ' א"ד לא קנסינן אותו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And she is permitted to her husband.</b> Although if the deaf-mute divorced her and she married a competent man, then even with respect to the deaf-mute she is forbidden to her husband, as written in siman 10, nevertheless here he did not divorce her, and we are not concerned that people may say he divorced her. Even if she had been divorced, there is no issue of a married woman, for the kiddushin of a deaf-mute is only rabbinic. If a competent man came and married her, she is forbidden even to the deaf-mute husband; just as she is forbidden to the adulterer, so she is forbidden to the husband. In Sefer Beit Hillel he wrote that since there is no issue of a married woman, we do not penalize him.

<b>הרי זו מקודשת קדושין גמורים</b>. עיין מ"ש ר"ס ט"ו ומכ"ש המקדש אשה ויש לו אשה אחרת אף על גב דעובר על חר"ג ה"ז מקודש' ד"מ וצרת יבמ' בכלל איסורי לאוין ועשין היא ועיין סי' קס"ב וצרת ערו' היא אשת אח שלא במקום מצוה ומחייבי כריתות היא ואין תופסים בהן קדושין וצרת יבמ' שאני ומ"ש חוץ יבמה וכו' הטעם הוא משום דכתב לא תהיה אשת המת החוצ' ואנן מסופקי' אי ללאו אתא וקדושין תופסי' בה או לא תהי' היינו דאין בה הויה וקדושין אין תופסין בה ש"ס:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>She is fully betrothed.</b> See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 15. All the more so, one who betroths a woman while he has another wife: although he violates Cherem deRabbeinu Gershom, she is nevertheless betrothed. This is from Darkei Moshe. A yevamah's rival wife is included among prohibitions involving negative and positive commandments; see siman 162. The rival wife of an ervah, however, is a brother's wife not in the context of a mitzvah and is among those liable for karet, and kiddushin does not take effect with them. A yevamah's rival wife is different. What is written, "except a yevamah," etc., is because Scripture says, "the wife of the deceased shall not be outside." We are uncertain whether this comes for a negative commandment, so that kiddushin takes effect with her, or whether "shall not be" means that there is no havayah with her and kiddushin does not take effect. This is from the Talmud.

<b>ואפילו זרעו</b>. משמע דקאי על מ"ש דקדושיו קדושין גמורים הם וכן מסתבר כיון דמומרת דינה כישראלית אף על פי שחטאה ישראלית היא א"כ  הולד ממנה הוא ישראל ודינו כמומר אפי' אם היא פרוצה ביותר מ"מ הוי כעכו"ם הבא על הישראלית והולד כמות' וכן כתוב בח"מ וכן גר שחזר לסורו וקידש קדושין גמורין הן כמ"ש בי"ד סי' רס"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And even his seed.</b> It appears that this refers to what was written, that his kiddushin is full kiddushin. This is also reasonable, since an apostate woman has the status of a Jewish woman; although she sinned, she remains Jewish. If so, the child from her is Jewish and has the status of an apostate. Even if she is extremely licentious, nevertheless this is like a gentile who has intercourse with a Jewish woman, where the child follows her. So it is written in Chelkat Mechokek. Likewise, a convert who returned to his former ways and betrothed a woman, the kiddushin is full kiddushin, as written in Yoreh De’ah siman 268.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A gentile.</b> This means a Kuti who betrothed. Although we say in the first chapter of Chullin that they made them like full gentiles, they made them like full gentiles only stringently and not leniently, as written in Beit Yosef at the end of seif 4. Although at the end of the first chapter of Yevamot Shmuel says that a gentile or Kuti who betroths nowadays, we are not concerned that perhaps he is from the ten tribes, even in places where they are common, because they made them like complete gentiles even leniently, one must say that the statement in the first chapter of Chullin was said regarding other prohibitions, where perhaps we do not say this leniently, and if he betrothed we are concerned for kiddushin. But in Yevamot, Shmuel stated his law regarding kiddushin; this teaches that they made them like complete gentiles even leniently.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or the first divorces.</b> In the Tur it says, one divorces, implying that even if the second divorces her, she is permitted to the first, and we are not concerned that people may say the first divorced her and now is remarrying his divorcee, because everyone knows that his kiddushin was not full kiddushin. What is written here in Shulchan Aruch, that the first divorces, was written according to what he wrote in Bedek HaBayit, that we are concerned lest people say so; see there. If two brothers betrothed her, one before emancipation and one after emancipation, and they died, she is permitted to the third brother. It is not relevant to say here that she is the wife of two dead men, because either way she was betrothed only to one. Gemara.
