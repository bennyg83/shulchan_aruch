<b>והוא מותרת. </b> עב"ש סק"ב שכ' ואם בא פקח ונשא אותה או אפילו חרש אסורה לבעלה כשם שאסורה לבועל כך אסורה לבעל ובספר ב"ה כתב כיון דליכא א"ד לא קנסינן אותה עכ"ל. ועיין בס' בית מאיר שכ' אולי קיצור ל' יש כאן כי בס' בה"י לא קאמר זה על הדין דנשא אותה פקח דמשמע שנשאת מדע' וא"כ מה ענין זה לקנס אבל דבריו אמורים על אשת חרש שהלך בעלה למדה"י ואמרו לה מת בעלך ונשאת בטעות ואח"כ בא כו' אמנם לע"ד נעלם מתרווייהו דברי הרמב"ם פ"י מה"ג הלכה ה' שכ' וכן הדין באשה שבאו עדים שמת בעלה ונשאת ואח"כ בא בין שהיה פקח בין שהיה חרש שאין קידושין גמורים תצא משניהם וצ"ג מזה ומזה ואסורה על שניהם עולמית והוא מירושלמי עכ"ד. וע"ש עוד בענין אם זינתה אשת חרש והביא שם דברי המל"מ פי"א מה"א בשם הרב מוהר"ר יחיאל באסאן שהביא ראיות דאשת חרש שזינתה מותרת לבעלה וכנראה דעתו להשוותה ליבמה לשוק (לקמן סימן קנ"ט ס"ב וג') שאם זינתה מותרת ואם נשאת לאחר בין שוגג בין מזיד אסורה. והוא ז"ל פקפק על זה ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And she is permitted.</b> See Beis Shmuel, s.k. 2, who wrote that if a mentally competent man came and married her, or even a deaf-mute, she is forbidden to her husband, for just as she is forbidden to the paramour, so is she forbidden to the husband. In Be'er Heitev he wrote that since there is no Torah prohibition of adultery here, we do not penalize her. End quote. See Beis Meir, who wrote that perhaps the language here is abbreviated, for Be'er Heitev did not state this regarding the case in which a mentally competent man married her, which implies that she married knowingly, and if so, what does this have to do with a penalty? Rather, his words concern the wife of a deaf-mute whose husband traveled overseas, where they told her that her husband had died, she married in error, and afterward he returned. However, in my humble opinion, both of them overlooked the words of the Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Gerushin, halacha 5, who wrote: The same law applies to a woman concerning whom witnesses came and testified that her husband had died, and she married, and afterward he returned. Whether the second husband was mentally competent or a deaf-mute whose kiddushin is not complete, she must leave both of them, requires a get from each, and is permanently forbidden to both. This is derived from the Yerushalmi. End of his words. See there further regarding a deaf-mute's wife who committed adultery. He cites the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 11 of Hilchos Ishus, in the name of Rabbi Yechiel Bassan, who brought proofs that the wife of a deaf-mute who committed adultery remains permitted to her husband. His view apparently equates her with a yevamah who had relations with an outsider, as discussed below in siman 159, se'ifim 2 and 3, who remains permitted if she merely committed adultery, but is forbidden if she married another, whether unintentionally or intentionally. Beis Meir questioned this; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From those forbidden by negative commandments.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 50. The questioner wished to distinguish and say that only kiddushin through money or a document takes effect with women forbidden by a negative commandment, since the act of kiddushin alone contains no transgression. If, however, he betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse, the kiddushin should not take effect, according to our ruling like Rava that wherever the Torah says, 'Do not do this,' if one did it, his act is ineffective. Noda BiYehudah replied that this is mistaken. If it were so, why did the Gemara at the end of the passage in Temurah labor to find a practical difference between Abaye and Rava? It could have identified the case of one who betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse. Moreover, according to your position, one who betroths a woman through intercourse on Yom Kippur would not effect kiddushin. Likewise, according to the opinion that intercourse with a virgin on Shabbos is forbidden because it causes a wound, one who betroths a virgin through intercourse on Shabbos would not effect kiddushin. All of this is astonishing. There is no distinction whatsoever between kiddushin through money, a document, or intercourse. The principle that if one transgressed, his act is ineffective does not apply here, for one who slaughters on Shabbos or Yom Kippur has performed a valid shechitah, and we do not say that his act is ineffective. The reason is as the Shach wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 208, that this principle applies only to an act that cannot be performed permissibly in any manner. Accordingly, even if he betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse, the essential kiddushin could have been performed permissibly through money or a document. He merely performed it in a prohibited manner, and therefore the kiddushin remains valid. Apart from this, one may further say, based on what Noda BiYehudah himself wrote in siman 129 concerning this principle, that with respect to kiddushin, Scripture revealed that the act is effective even when performed contrary to the Torah's warning, as where a Kohen Gadol betrothed a widow. The same applies to all prohibitions. See there. This will be cited below, siman 134, s.k. 6. One may further say based on Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 174, cited in Pitchei Teshuvah to Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, s.k. 1, that we say an act is ineffective only where nullifying the act would nullify the transgression. See there. Here, even were we to say that the act is ineffective and the kiddushin is void, the transgression of intercourse with a woman forbidden by a negative commandment would remain. This matter depends upon the dispute between the Rambam and the Ra'avad in chapter 15 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, whether lashes are incurred for intercourse with women forbidden by negative commandments without kiddushin. See there carefully. Responsa Maharit should also be examined, for it appears that he too raised the possibility that one who betroths a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse does not effect kiddushin, but rejected it because the prohibition would not thereby be remedied. I do not presently have the work before me. In the gloss there, the son of the author brought another proof from Kesubos, folio 74. Although kiddushin through intercourse cannot be performed through an agent, it may nevertheless be made conditional because the different modes by which marriage comes into being are compared to one another. Accordingly, regarding kiddushin with women forbidden by negative commandments, since kiddushin takes effect through money or a document, it likewise takes effect through intercourse, because the forms of kiddushin are compared to one another and may not be distinguished in any respect. See there. See also Shaar HaMelech, chapter 6 of Hilchos Ishus, in Kuntres Chuppas Chasanim, se'if 9, who likewise wrote as obvious that our ruling that kiddushin takes effect with women forbidden by negative commandments applies even where he betrothed her through intercourse, notwithstanding that it was prohibited intercourse. He brought several proofs for this. He wrote there that this is not contradicted by the Ran in the first chapter of Kiddushin, cited in Beis Shmuel, siman 55, s.k. 3, and siman 61, s.k. 2, who wrote that prohibited intercourse should not acquire. The Ran stated this only regarding the husband's rights to inherit from her, to her found objects, and to the work of her hands, so that a sinner should not profit. See there. See further Tzelos HaBayis, siman 4, regarding this matter.

<b>ישראל עוזב דת שקידש. </b> עי' תשובת נו"ב תניינא סי' קס"ב אודות מקרה בלתי טהור שאירע בקהילה אחת במדינת ישמעאל שבא שם נער א' בחזקת שהוא יהודי פנוי ודיבר שם על לב אלמנה אחת שתנשא לו ואחר שישב שם ג' שבועות נשאת לו בחופה וקידושין ואחר הנישואין לקח את הכל מידה וברח לו ועתה נודע הדבר שהוא עוזב דת זה כמה שנים והאשה העלובה תהיה מוכרחת להשאר עגונה כל ימיה. והנה רב אחד רצה לצדד היתר קצת לבטל הקידושין מתחילתן והוא ז"ל השיב לו ישתקע הדבר וח"ו להתיר האשה לא מבעיא בנ"ד שבא לפנינו מתנהג ביהדות ג' שבועות וקידש אשה בחזקת יהודי דכ"ע מודו דחיישינן שמא הרהר תשו' ומה שחזר אח"כ לסורו אין ראיה שהיה למפרע בשעת קידושין כך אלא אפילו בעוזב דת שהוא עוזב דת לפנינו לית דחש להי"א שהביא הב"י בשם בה"ע והלכה רווחת עוזב דת שקידש קידושיו קדושין וכ"כ הכנה"ג דאפילו החולק ביבום (בסי' קנ"ז ס"ד) מודה שקידושיו קדושין. ומ"ש הרב השואל דאפשר לו דבכה"ג שהיא לא ידעה שהוא עוזב דת אדעתא דהכי לא קידשה נפשה כדפריך בפ' הגוזל ק"ו כו' זה אינו דאפילו באיש דלא שייך ביה דבכל דהו ניחא ליה ובאיסור שאין לו היתר מכל מקום המוסכם מכל הפוסקים דח"ל ולא הכיר בה חיילי הקדושין (עמ"ש לעיל סי' ל"ט סק"ד) מכ"ש באשה דבכל דהו ניחא לה ובאיסור שיש היתר לאיסורו וסמא בידו בכל רגע לשוב בתשובה פשיטא דלא אמרינן שיוחשב קדושי טעות וסיומא דפיסקא שאשה זו אשת איש היא וכל זמן שלא תשיג גט ממנו תשב עגונה ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An apostate Jew who betrothed.</b> See Noda BiYehudah Tinyana siman 162, concerning a troubling case in an Islamic country: a young man came to a community presumed to be an unmarried Jew, persuaded a widow to marry him, stayed three weeks, married her with chuppah and kiddushin, then took all she had and fled. It was then learned that he had been an apostate for years, leaving the woman in danger of permanent agunah. A rabbi suggested annulling the kiddushin from the outset. Noda BiYehudah responded forcefully that this must not be accepted. In that case, the man behaved as a Jew for three weeks and betrothed her as a Jew, so all would agree we must consider that he may have contemplated repentance; his later return to apostasy does not prove his state retroactively at the time of kiddushin.

Even when the apostate is presently before us as an apostate, no one takes account of the dissenting view cited by Beit Yosef in the name of Ba'al HaItur. The settled halakhah is that when an apostate Jew betroths, his kiddushin is valid, and Kenesset HaGedolah writes that even one who disagrees regarding yibbum concedes that his kiddushin is kiddushin. Nor can one argue that because she did not know he was an apostate, she did not betroth herself with that in mind. Even by a man, where the maxim that he is content with any wife does not apply, and even where the prohibition has no permitted remedy, all authorities agree that kiddushin to a woman forbidden by a lav whom he did not recognize nevertheless takes effect. All the more so for a woman, who is presumed content with any husband, and where the prohibition can be remedied because he can repent at any moment, the marriage is not a mistaken kiddushin. The conclusion is that she is a married woman and remains an agunah until she obtains a get from him.
