**** ENGLISH ****
She is permitted to her deaf-mute husband, and we are not concerned that people will say: the deaf-mute divorced her, this man married her, and afterward the deaf-mute took back his divorcee. Since a deaf-mute has no kiddushin or divorce by Torah law, there is no decree lest people err; for even if the truth were so, there would be no Torah prohibition, and we do not add decrees. So wrote Chelkat Mechokek. It appears that all the more so, if the deaf-mute divorces her, she is permitted to the competent man who betrothed her. The Tur wrote that nevertheless the deaf-mute woman cannot perform mi'un, for if she could perform mi'un no one would marry her, since there would be no time limit for mi'un, unlike a minor girl, where there is a limit: once she matures he will appease her until she grows up.

שחציה שפחה זה הוא דעת הרא"ש כמ"ש הטור וחולק על הרמב"ם כמבואר שם ולעד"נ שבחנם כתב רבינו הטור בזה מחלוקת דז"ל הרמב"ם המקדש חציה שפחה וחצי' בת חורין אינה מקודשת קדושין גמורין עד שתשתחרר וכיון שנשתחררה ה"ז ספק קידושין לשניהם עכ"ל ויש מקום עיון מאד בפי' דברי הרמב"ם אלו דלמה הוצרך לדמותה לקטנה ותו כיון דנגמרו הקידושין הראשונים למה תהיה ספק לשניהם והביא ב"י בשם הר"ן והמ"מ והמחוור שבכולן דברי הב"י אלא שנוסף קצת בביאורו דאיתא בגמרא אתמר חציה שפחה וחציה ב"ח שנתקדש לראובן ונשתחררה וחזרה ונתקדשה לשמעון רב יוסף בר חמא א"ר נחמן פקעו קדושי ראשון וקשה דלמה הוצרכו לחלק באם קדש אחר והיה להם לחלק באותו ראשון עצמו דלמ"ד פקעי צריך הוא לקדשה שנית ולמ"ד גמרו א"צ לקדש שנית ע"כ צריכי לומר הכל מודים דהוא א"צ לקדשה שנית דהקידושין הראשונים הם קידושין אלא שהית' אסורה לו מכחש היתה חציה שפחה וע"י השחרור נסתלק האיסור ממילא מותר' לו וע"כ א"צ קדושין אחרים אפי' למ"ד פקעי דילפינן זה מקדושי קטנה שאינה קדושין מן התור' וא"כ כשנתגדל' (תהיה) צריכה קידושין אחרים אלא ודאי כיון שלא אפסק' אחר נגמר' הקידושין הראשונים ה"נ כאן כי פליגי באיפסק' אחר דלמ"ד גמרו אין באותו אחר כלום דאדרב' נתחזק הראשונים ואין לזר אתו ולמ"ד פסקו ממילא דבתר' הוה קידושי ודאי ונסתפק הרמב"ם הלכה כדברי מי וכ"כ התוס' שם שרב חסדא נסתפק כן ע"כ פסק הרמב"ם לחומרא דאם בא אחר וקידשה צריכה גט משניהם (מספק אי) קיי"ל כמ"ד פסקו מראשון (או) דלמא קי"ל כמ"ד גמרו אבל אם לא קדשה אחר אלא הראשון יכניסנה א"צ לקדושין אחרים וא"כ דברי הרמב"ם מבוארים היטב כפשוטן וא"צ תיקון כלל ומ"ש אין הקדושין קדושין גמורין לא נתכוין לומר דהוה ספק קידושין כמו שהבין רבינו הטור בדבריו אלא פשוט דאין כאן ספק דא"כ היה לו לומר ספק כמ"ש אח"כ ספק מקודשת לשניהם אלא ודאי דמקודשת ודאי וצריכ' גט כמו דס"ל להרא"ש אלא דקאמר דלא הווין גמורים דהא אסור' לו מחמת חלק שפתח ע"כ נגמרו הקידושין אחר השחרור ואז מותרת לו מכל צד וא"צ לקדושין אחרים וזה לכ"ע אלא דאם בא אחר וקדש' אחר השחרור יש פלוגתא ואזלינן לחומרא זה נ"ל נכון מאד אף שלפ"ז יהיה קשה על הטור למה כתב מחלוקת בין הרא"ש להרמב"ם מ"מ ניחא לן לפרש שיהיה דברי הרמב"ם מתוקנים שפיר ואין מחלוקת כלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
That she is half-maidservant: this is the view of the Rosh, as the Tur wrote, and he disagrees with the Rambam as explained there. In my humble opinion, however, Rabbeinu the Tur wrote this as a dispute unnecessarily. The Rambam's language is: one who betroths a woman who is half-maidservant and half-free is not fully betrothed until she is freed; once she is freed, she is doubtfully betrothed to both men. End quote. There is much to examine in explaining these words of the Rambam. Why did he need to compare her to a minor? And further, if the first kiddushin were completed, why should she be doubtful to both men? The Beit Yosef cited the Ran and the Maggid Mishneh, and the clearest of all is the Beit Yosef's explanation, though a little should be added to it. In the Gemara it says: a woman who is half-maidservant and half-free was betrothed to Reuven, then freed, and then betrothed again to Shimon; Rav Yosef bar Chama said in the name of Rav Nachman that the first kiddushin are uprooted. This is difficult: why did they need to distinguish where another man betrothed her? They should have distinguished with the first man himself: according to the opinion that the first kiddushin are uprooted, he must betroth her again; and according to the opinion that they are completed, he need not betroth her again. Necessarily, all agree that the first man need not betroth her again, because the first kiddushin are kiddushin, except that she had been forbidden to him because she was half-maidservant. Through emancipation that prohibition is removed, and she is automatically permitted to him; therefore she needs no other kiddushin, even according to the one who says the first kiddushin are uprooted. We learn this from kiddushin of a minor, which are not kiddushin by Torah law, and yet when she matures she does not need other kiddushin. Rather, since no other man interrupted, the first kiddushin are completed. So too here: they dispute only where another man interrupted. According to the one who says the first kiddushin are completed, the other man's act is nothing; on the contrary, the first kiddushin are strengthened, and an outsider cannot come. According to the one who says they are uprooted, the later kiddushin are certainly kiddushin. The Rambam was uncertain whom the halakhah follows, and Tosafot there also wrote that Rav Chisda was uncertain about this. Therefore the Rambam ruled stringently: if another man came and betrothed her, she needs a get from both, because of doubt whether we rule like the view that the first kiddushin are uprooted or like the view that they are completed. But if no other man betrothed her, the first man may bring her into marriage and she needs no other kiddushin. Thus the Rambam's words are very well explained in their plain sense and need no correction at all. When he wrote that the kiddushin are not complete kiddushin, he did not mean that they are doubtful kiddushin, as Rabbeinu the Tur understood; rather, there is no doubt here. Had that been his intent he should have said "doubtful," as he later wrote that she is doubtfully betrothed to both. Rather, she is certainly betrothed and requires a get, as the Rosh holds; only he says that they are not complete, because she is forbidden to him on account of the servile half. Therefore the kiddushin are completed after emancipation, and then she is permitted to him from every side and needs no other kiddushin. This is so according to everyone. Only if another man came and betrothed her after emancipation is there a dispute, and we rule stringently. This appears very correct to me. Although according to this it will be difficult why the Tur wrote that there is a dispute between the Rosh and the Rambam, nevertheless it is preferable to explain the Rambam's words properly, so that there is no dispute at all.
