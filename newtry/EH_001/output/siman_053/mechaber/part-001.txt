**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Law of one who stipulates for his son or daughter if they acquired; law if she fell before the yavam; law if the daughter died. It contains 3 seifim.</b>

A father who stipulated via his daughter — the daughter did not acquire that gift until her husband enters her; and likewise the son did not acquire until he enters, for every stipulator stipulates only on condition of entering. Therefore one who stipulates money to his son-in-law (or to his son) and died before he entered and she fell before his brother for yibbum — the father can say to the yavam: to your brother I wished to give, but to you I do not wish to give; and even if the first was an am ha'aretz and the second a scholar, and even though the daughter wants him.

One who stipulates money to his son-in-law at betrothal time and gave them to him before the chuppah and the daughter died and she has a son — he returns the money to the father, for every stipulator's intent is on condition of entering.

And there is one who says this applies only when stipulating to the son-in-law; but one who gave to his daughter unconditionally that she marry with it and did not mention that she marry so-and-so — she acquired that gift; and once she was betrothed, even though the kiddushin were voided when the fiancé died — the father cannot retract.

{Rama: One who vowed money to marry off an orphan and she died — see Yoreh Deah siman 253. One who stipulates money to his son-in-law or to his daughter and he entered and afterward the daughter died and he still did not give — some say the husband acquired them and he must give him all he stipulated (Rashi and Rambam and Ran and Mordechai in name of R' Yehudah HaLevi). And Rabbenu Tam explained the husband did not acquire them and from doubt we do not extract money from the father (Terumat HaDeshen who ruled thus and Maharik root 91 and Hagahot Maharam). All as long as they did not come once to the groom's hand (Mordechai chapter Na'arah). Even if they came to a third party's hand — from the power of both (there in Terumat HaDeshen). And some say if they came to a third party's hand he acquired them (Mordechai). And likewise if he set up guarantors for him (Hagahot Mordechai). But without this — even if the father made a document on him and liened them on him as a loan and he has children from her — the husband did not acquire them (Terumat HaDeshen siman 221) and we do not extract from the father. But if the groom seized — we do not extract from him for he can say I hold like the first view (Mordechai). And all this when they did not come once to the groom's hand in the daughter's lifetime; but if they came to his hand once even though he returned them to the father's hand — he acquired all. And there is no distinction in all this between stipulating to his daughter or to his son and died (so appears from the enactment language and so Hagahot Mordechai in name of Maharam). Rabbenu Tam and the sages of France further enacted that even if the father already gave the dowry — if the woman died or the man died within the first year without viable offspring — everything returns to the father or to the heirs of the deceased. And some say even in the second year half the dowry returns (all in Mordechai). And so is the widespread custom in these lands that practice according to the enactment of the communities of Ashkenaz; and specifically what is in existence returns; but what he spent or was lost — he is exempt provided he did not squander after her death except for her burial needs (likewise there). Some say if the groom wishes to sell her property within two years the heirs can protest for this reason (Hagahot Alfasi chapter A Woman's Found Article). And see further regarding these laws below siman 118.}
