**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Law of matters acquired by speech if given to write. It contains 1 seif.</b>

A man and woman between whom there was matchmaking, and he said to her how much you bring in to me such-and-such, and she said to him and how much do you give me or write for me such-and-such; and likewise a father who stipulated via his son and daughter how much you give to your son such-and-such and how much you give to your daughter such-and-such.

{Rama: And specifically regarding such matters that are in the father's power to fulfill; but if he stipulates regarding his son that he do some matter such as to follow her to another place — it is not acquired by speech, for this does not depend on the father but only on the son (Mordechai chapter The One Who Marries and chapter One Who Died).}

They stood and betrothed — those matters were acquired even though there was no kinyan between them; and these are the matters acquired by speech: that the matters they stipulated be in their possession, for a person does not transfer what has not come to the world, and these matters were not given to be written; therefore even if they were written they are not like a document with which to collect from liened property.

When does this apply? When the father stipulated for his daughter — whether minor or adult, or for his son — in first marriage.

{Rama: And some say we require that they stood and betrothed immediately and did not interrupt with other matters; but if they interrupted with other matters — no (Tur). And likewise if they betrothed and afterward stipulated — it does not help (Tosafot chapter Na'arah). (And some say we also require that they stipulated thus together; but if they did so without condition, each speaking on his own — it is not acquired by speech) (Rivash siman 245).}

But a brother who stipulated for his sister, or a woman who stipulated for her daughter, and likewise other relatives, and likewise a father who stipulated for his son or daughter in second marriage — they did not acquire those matters until they acquire from the stipulator himself who is giving such-and-such.

{Rama: Matters acquired by speech, or if the father stipulated and they acquired from his hand — are like any other debt; and even if the father died before the groom married, he collects from the heirs, and even from liened property like any debt (Rivash siman 129). And some say we do not deduct from the daughter's inheritance since she acquired them in the father's lifetime. But if he said I will give such-and-such, or a groom who stipulated to make for her a ketubah of such-and-such and at the time of marriage they retract — see Choshen Mishpat siman 245 whether he must fulfill from the side of kinyan; but from the side of the fine they stipulated upon themselves he certainly must fulfill; therefore the custom is to make new conditions before marriage — so it seems to me. And all matters explained here that are not acquired by speech — refers specifically to a large gift to a wealthy person; but one who stipulates to a poor person must fulfill because it is like a vow (Beit Yosef in name of R' Meir, and so in Yerushalmi). And likewise for a wealthy person a small gift — there is concern of breach of faith if he retracts (Beit Yosef in name of Rashbash) (and see Choshen Mishpat siman 249 in laws of gift). The groom must pay the scribe's fee for the conditions if only one document was written (Nimmukei Yosef chapter Gittin Pashut); but if two documents were written each pays his own. All that they stipulate must give before he enters; but he cannot say enter and afterward I will give. But if he wishes to deposit the money the authority is in his hand (Hagahot Alfasi and see Choshen Mishpat siman 73 seif 8). One who swore to give dowry to his son-in-law and he instigates quarrels with the daughter (Maharik root 81).}
