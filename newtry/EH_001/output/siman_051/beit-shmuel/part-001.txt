**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> See Choshen HaMishpat siman 286, where he ruled that it is deducted from her, because there it speaks where he pledged to her but she did not acquire it by speech. Even though he accepted a kinyan generally, we say the kinyan was not to grant a right to the son or daughter, but only to impose a penalty payable to the other side. But if she acquired it by speech, or he made a kinyan explicitly to give it to her, she acquired it and it is not deducted. These distinctions flow from responsum Mahari Weil, as written in Darkei Moshe. That is, in responsum Mahari Weil siman 105 he ruled that if the father pledged a sum to give and died, his sons need not fulfill it, as written in the previous siman. In siman 104 he ruled that if he pledged a dowry to his son, the son does not take that portion first, but takes it from his inheritance share, as written there in Choshen HaMishpat. In siman 142 he ruled that if there was a kinyan, it is not deducted from his inheritance share, as written here. Therefore one must say that what he wrote in responsum 104, that it is deducted from his inheritance, speaks where there was no kinyan to grant a right to his son, but only a general kinyan; then it does not operate to grant a right to his son, and therefore it is deducted from his inheritance. This is what is written in the above Choshen HaMishpat siman. Responsum 142 speaks where there was an explicit kinyan to grant a right to his son or daughter; then they acquire it and it is not deducted from his inheritance share. This is what is written here. Responsum 105 appears to speak where the inheritance share of the son or daughter does not amount to the sum their father pledged, and there was no explicit kinyan to grant it to them; therefore the heirs need not fulfill it. Siman 104 and siman 142 speak where that much did come to his inheritance share, but the heirs want to deduct that sum from their inheritance share. In that, there is a distinction whether there was a kinyan to grant them a right or only a general kinyan. In this way all three responsa of Mahari Weil are properly reconciled.

<b>משום דהוי כנדר</b>. הסמ"ע בסימן קכ"ה כתב שיכול להתיר הנדר כל זמן שהוא בידו כמו בנודר לצדק' כמ"ש בי"ד סימן רכ"ח, כתב בתשו' מהרי"ל מי שכתב להלביש בתו לפי כבודו צריך לקיים כי הזוג עניים הם בשעת השידוכים ואפילו אם אבי החתן ג"כ התחייב את עצמו להלביש את בנו מ"מ לא נתלה זה בזה האב שעשה שידוך עם בנו ואח"כ רוצה שיסלק הצד השני את הנדוני' והוא דוחה לאב ואומר לאו בעל דברים דידי את כי המעות מגיע להחתן ולהחתן אומר שהוא לא היה בשעת התנאים וא"י כלום רק שמע מאביו השיב ר"ת דיכתוב החתן הרשאה לאביו ואז חייב לסלק לאביו דאז אביו שלוחו של החתן וא"י הצד השני לדחות את אבי החתן תשו' מהר"מ ב"ב סימן קפ"ז וכלבו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because it is like a vow.</b> The Sema in siman 125 wrote that he can release the vow as long as it is in his hand, as with one who vows to charity, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 228. Responsum Maharil wrote that one who wrote that he would clothe his daughter according to his honor must fulfill it, for the couple were poor at the time of the shidduchim; and even if the groom's father also obligated himself to clothe his son, nevertheless the two obligations are not dependent on one another. Regarding a father who made a match for his son and afterward wanted the other side to pay the dowry, and that side pushed the father away saying, you are not my legal counterpart, because the money is owed to the groom, while to the groom he says, I was not present at the tenaim and know nothing, I only heard from my father: Rabbeinu Tam answered that the groom should write authorization to his father, and then the other side is obligated to pay his father, for then the father is the groom's agent and the other side cannot push away the groom's father. Responsum Maharam Bar Baruch siman 187, and Kol Bo.
