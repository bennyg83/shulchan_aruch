**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And even from encumbered property.</b> This wording implies that even without a written document the same rule applies, which is surprising, for the Gemara makes clear that collection is only from unencumbered property and that the obligation is not given to be written. In Darkhei Moshe the language is: matters acquired by verbal commitment, or where the father accepted a formal kinyan and the groom died, are collected in full as ruled; and if they made a kinyan from him or there was a written document about it, collection may be made even from encumbered property. Certainly there is a scribal error here, and the text should read as in Darkhei Moshe.

Still, this is difficult. The Rivash must be following Rashi's view mentioned nearby, yet the Shulchan Aruch has already ruled like Rambam that a document does not help to collect from encumbered property, and there Rema wrote nothing. Here Rema rules simply that one may collect, without presenting it as a dissenting view. It seems that this case concerns a full debt document, and in such a case everyone agrees. I indeed saw in Darkhei Moshe on Rambam's words that if the parties agreed to make a true legal document, it certainly works.
