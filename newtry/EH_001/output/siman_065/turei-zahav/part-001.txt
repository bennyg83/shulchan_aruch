**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>It is a mitzvah to gladden groom and bride; the law of a corpse and a bride.</b><br />
And to dance before her, meaning that the "saying" mentioned is not identical with dancing. But in Rashi, chapter 2 of Ketubot, it is clear that dancing means the praise that is said. Since this matter came up, I will mention what I have explained, with God's help, in the plain sense of the Gemara. Beit Shammai say: praise the bride as she is, meaning according to her beauty and importance. Beit Hillel say: every bride is beautiful and gracious; Rashi explains that a thread of grace is drawn over her. Beit Shammai said to Beit Hillel: if she was lame or blind, do we say "a beautiful and gracious bride"? The Torah says, "Distance yourself from falsehood." Beit Hillel answered: according to you, one who bought a bad purchase in the marketplace, should one praise it in his eyes or degrade it? Say that one praises it in his eyes. From here the Sages said that a person's disposition should always be mixed pleasantly with people. There are several difficulties: why does the text repeat "bride"; it should have said simply "as she is." Further, Tosafot write that if she has a defect they should be silent and not praise her, but there is no greater embarrassment than that: for pretty brides they dance, and for unattractive ones they do not. The Sages were very concerned not to embarrass people in such matters, as on the fifteenth of Av the daughters of Israel would go out in borrowed white garments so as not to shame one who lacked. Also the phrase "how does one dance before the bride" implies that one dances before all brides. It appears that Beit Shammai held one should say a phrase that fits each bride according to her true quality, whereas Beit Hillel held the fixed formula "beautiful and gracious bride" is not false, because the groom views her favorably and she is graceful in his eyes. The proof from a bad purchase is not a proof from ordinary flattery, but from the rule of social peace: once the purchase is made, praise helps the owner and no deception results. So too here, the bride is to be praised in a way that increases affection and honor.

להכנסת כלה לחופה כתב בדרישה דפירש"י בגמ' להכנסת הכלה ללות מבית אביה לבית חופתה מזה נראה הגון ללות הכל' בזמנינו כשמביאין אותה תחת החופה כמי שנוהגים לעשו' לחתן ואדרבא היא עיקר שהרי לא נזכר אלא הכנס' כלה. והמרדכי פ"ק דכתובו' כתב דזימנין מקדש בשע' ברכת הנשואין ואז הכנסת כלה קודמ' לקידושין כי הכנס' היא שמסרה בשחרית להחתן קודם הברכה נמצא מה שהולכין בזמנינו כל הקהל עם החתן בשחרית לכסות ראש הכלה היא בזמנינו הכנס' כלה לחופה ע"כ בדרישה והוא תמו' האיך כ"כ דהא כתב רמ"א בסי' נ"ד דהמנהג עכשיו דמה שמוליכים אותה תחת המפ' שעל הכלונסות הו' חופה ותו דהרבה פעמים שביום החופה בשעת שמסלקין הנדוניא אם אין החתן משוה הולך משם ולא דאינו עליו חיוב שישאנ' מחמת כסוי ההינומא ותו דבהרבה מקומות מכסין הכלה בשחרית בלא חתן וא"כ האיך הוא חופה בלא חתן ע"כ נראה דלדידן לא היה כסוי ההינומא חופ' כלל אלא הכנה לחופה שתהיה אח"כ אז הולכ' אנשים לזה ואח"כ בשעת הולכ' לחופה שלנו שהוא הגמר אין הולכים אלא ללות החתן אבל לא ללות הכל' ששם כל הנשים הולכים עמה ואין להתערב שם עמהם וראיתי בקצ' מקומות שהרבה אנשים חשובי העיר הולכים באמת לקראת הכלה וכשמתקרבים אליה חוזרים לחוריהם להחופה וזה נקרא כג"כ לוי' ונכון הוא:

(ב) <b>To bring the bride into the chuppah.</b> Derishah wrote that Rashi explains in the Gemara that bringing the bride means accompanying her from her father's house to the house of her chuppah. From this it appears proper in our time to accompany the bride when she is brought under the chuppah, as people customarily do for the groom; indeed she is primary, for only "bringing the bride" is mentioned. Mordekhai in the first chapter of Ketubot wrote that sometimes kiddushin is done at the time of the wedding blessings, and then bringing the bride precedes kiddushin, because bringing her in means that she was handed over to the groom in the morning before the blessing. Derishah therefore says that what our communities do, going with the groom in the morning to cover the bride's head, is now the bringing of the bride into chuppah. This is surprising. Rama writes in siman 54 that the present custom is that what brings her under the canopy on poles is the chuppah. Moreover, many times on the day of the chuppah, when the dowry is being settled, if the groom is not satisfied he leaves, and there is no obligation upon him to marry her merely because of the covering of the veil. In many places they cover the bride in the morning without the groom; how can that be chuppah without the groom? Therefore it appears that for us the covering of the veil is not chuppah at all, but only preparation for the later chuppah. Men go for that, but afterward, at the time of the actual chuppah, the custom is to accompany the groom and not the bride, because the women accompany her and men should not mix with them. I have seen in some places that many important men of the city do in fact go to meet the bride, and this is proper where it can be done with modesty.
