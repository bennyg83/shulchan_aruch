**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A woman who brought a loan to her husband and forgave it. It contains 5 seifim.</b>

A woman who brought a promissory note of debt to her husband and afterward forgave it — it is not forgiven, because his hand is like her hand.

{Rama: Therefore she is likewise not believed to say it was paid (Nimmukei Yosef chapter HaKotev). And the same applies — she cannot forgive a verbal loan (Beit Yosef in name of R' Yitzchak).}

**** ENGLISH ****
A husband in his wife's property is like a buyer. Therefore one who said to a woman "my property is yours and after you to so-and-so" and she married — he is like a buyer and "after you" has nothing in place of the husband. For we have established: "my property is yours and after you to so-and-so" and the first sold — the second has only what the first left. When does this apply? When he said so to an unmarried woman. But one who said to a married woman "my property is yours and after you to so-and-so" and she died — "after you" acquires and not the husband. Therefore one who said to a married woman "my property is yours and after you to so-and-so" and she sold and died — the property stands in the buyer's hand.

**** ENGLISH ****
If she had a verbal loan upon her and afterward married — it is not collected from the husband, for a verbal loan is not collected from buyers.

{Rama: However, if the money the loan was for is still extant he is obligated to pay (R' Yitzchak and Mordechai). And if the lender came before she married and wishes to protest her marriage until she pays him and that he not lose his — the law is with him even though the time of payment has not yet arrived (Beit Yosef in name of Nimmukei Yosef).}

But if she had a loan with a document — he collects from property she brought to her husband.

A community that made regulations among themselves with fines and a woman transgressed the regulation — her husband is obligated to pay (Beit Yosef siman 70 in name of Terumat HaDeshen siman 282).

One who sent gifts to a betrothed woman and she went and married another, and that one claims his gifts — their law is like a loan with a document.

{Rama: If she took more than her ketubah and married — the husband must return the excess (Mahari"k shoresh 18). And likewise if she became liable for tax and married — it is like sending gifts, for all these have an outcry and there is no way to make a document for them, therefore it is like a loan with a document (Maharam Padua siman 55). But damages and purchases and other matters — he is not obligated to pay (Nimmukei Yosef chapter Yesh Nochalin). A woman who betrothed her daughter to a man and became liable for dowry and afterward married herself to the man betrothed to her daughter — he extracts everything she became liable for from her husband from property she brought him, for it is like a loan with a document; even though at the time she made the betrothal arrangement for her daughter she had not yet sworn on her ketubah — nevertheless since she swore at the end, the matter is revealed retroactively that what she did initially was lawful (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Rashba). Some say that a loan with a document collects from the husband from property she brought him — this is when she brought him land or movable property and wrote for her husband the debt of the movables by way of the land; but if she did not write for him by way of land and did not bring him land, only movables — even a loan with a document she does not collect from her husband, for a creditor does not seize from encumbered movables (responsum of Ramban siman 56). And this is sound reasoning; nevertheless it appears to them that regarding a garment that is not hers, such as if she took more than her ketubah — he must return in every case (and see Choshen Mishpat end of siman 96) (explained above).}
