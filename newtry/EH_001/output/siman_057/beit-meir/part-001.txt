Since she came, etc. So wrote Beit Shmuel — and he does not annul her vows. So too Rashi and Ran.

In truth it is explained in the Ran and Rosh — there is no resolution; for he cited anonymously in the second version regarding her inheritance; but for terumah she does not eat until she enters the chuppah.

And in his hints he wrote: handed over, etc. — not for terumah until, etc.; but for all other matters she left her father's domain.

And since he did not write for other matters she is in the husband's authority — it implies as the Ran explained: the tanna of R' Yehudah — for annulment she left the father's domain, but to the husband's authority she did not come.

If so, the laws are not equal — for inheritance she enters the husband's authority; and from this Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 254 deduced he holds like Tosafot.

And in my humble view there is no resolution — one can say a definitive matter: it takes hold in brief that for this all other matters are equal — she leaves her father's domain.

And regarding the husband's authority one can say they differ — as Yoreh Deah wrote there.

But Beit Yosef challenged the Ran in Nedarim folio 89: it is stated this principle — handing of the father to the husband's agents — the husband does not annul previous vows.

For the Ran wrote: since he handed her to them, the father no longer has authority over her — this handing is in marriage, where the husband annuls vows from marriage onward; but vows she already vowed — the husband does not annul previous ones.

And in partnership the father also does not — for through this handing she left her father's domain and he no longer has right in her. See end.

And Beit Yosef deduced: since he wrote this handing is in marriage — meaning in complete marriage where the husband alone annuls — if so the Ran explains the principle comes to teach that if he handed her over, the husband cannot annul what she vowed during betrothal before the handing.

And the reason: even though her vows are now — he alone annuls — nevertheless he does not annul previous ones, even in partnership with the father; and even if he forgot this at the time of the vow — nevertheless she already left the father's domain regarding his power; and regarding this additional power to the husband it is called previous.

And so he explained for the Rambam.

If so, the Ran contradicts his own view — for in chapter Na'arah he also questioned: behold this principle is stated in this language: whoever leaves to her own authority for one hour — he cannot annul.

And in the aforementioned manner — at what hour did she leave to her own authority before the handing? She was in the authority of both, and from there she came fully to the husband's authority.

Therefore it seemed to me per forced explanation to strain the Ran's explanation: what he wrote that this handing is like marriage — meaning for this matter she left her father's domain; but not fully, rather in her own authority.

And he further wrote that the husband annuls vows from marriage onward — meaning actual marriage; but vows she already vowed — not meaning this; but what she vowed after handing and marriage — not even after complete marriage does the husband annul previous ones (and this is the law of Taz) — and this too is called previous.

And he wrote: to annul at least in partnership — for this he wrote: and in partnership the father also does not, etc.

Therefore it properly comes out she left for one hour to her own authority — meaning the hour of handing.

And we are informed that one might have thought the hour of handing should not be considered her own authority since she is betrothed — it teaches: since for vows she was in her own authority, it is called previous.

But in truth even per this explanation there is a question: this principle seems stated in comparison to one who vowed and he divorced her and returned her — she leaves to her own authority between the vow and marriage for two hours.

And in the aforementioned, her leaving to her own authority is at the time of handing before the vow.

Therefore the Rosh explained the principle comes to teach handing, etc.: since she left her father's domain — and if she vowed and he divorced and returned her, she has no annulment; for from her father's domain she left, and the husband does not annul previous vows.

And it is compelled from his explanation that from the time of handing, had he not divorced her, he could annul.

But this is from when she left through a get and returned — he does not annul previous vows.

And when Tosafot's view in Ketubot — uncertain on the Tur Yoreh Deah who explained not like his father; and Taz's words do not add much, and without this they are strained.

And per this explanation there is also a question: what is the novelty of this principle? Is it conceivable that divorce from the time of handing would be better than divorce from marriage?

Therefore one must strain that the novelty is that the father does not annul after divorce — and to exclude from what is doubtful for the tanna of the house of R' Yehudah in chapter Na'arah.

And know that the Rambam who wrote chapter 11 of Laws of Vows: the husband annuls from entering the chuppah — implies like Rashi's view, since he combines one language with Laws of Terumot; and his explanation in the Ran — possibly also to include in his language what he concluded that the husband does not annul previous vows — meaning therefore he does not annul what she vowed from when she was handed over, even after she enters the chuppah; meaning this vow has no annulment ever. Uncertain.
