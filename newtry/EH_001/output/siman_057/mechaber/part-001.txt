**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If she had a courtyard on the way and entered with him. It contains 1 seif.</b>

The husband precedes every person in inheriting his wife. From when does he acquire her inheritance? From when she leaves her father's domain — even though she has not yet entered the chuppah, since she has come under her husband's authority, he inherits her.

How so? A woman who was betrothed and her father handed her to her husband or to the husband's agents, or the father's agents handed her to the husband or to his agents, and she died on the way before entering the chuppah — even though the dowry is still in her father's house, her husband inherits her.

(And see above end of seif 53 and below siman 118; and some dispute if the dowry did not reach his hand) (Tur in name of Rabbeinu Tam).

And likewise if the father or the father's agents went with the husband and he entered with her on the way into a courtyard and was secluded with her there for the purpose of marriage and she died — her husband inherits her.

But if the father is still with the husband to bring her to the husband's house, or the father's agents went with the husband's agents or with the husband — even if the husband entered with her into a courtyard to lodge as travelers lodge in an inn — since the father or his agents are with her and he has not yet been secluded with her for the purpose of marriage, if she dies her father inherits her, even though her dowry is in the husband's house.

And likewise if she was an adult, or an orphan, or a widow, and she went by herself from her father's house to her husband's house and neither her husband nor his agents are with her, and she dies on the way — the husband does not inherit her.

{Rama: And some say that in his courtyard, or in one belonging to both of them, seclusion alone is for the purpose of marriage (Ran chapter Na'arah); but in her courtyard, or one belonging to neither of them, seclusion alone is for lodging (Maggid Mishneh in name of Ramban and Rashba).}
