**** ENGLISH ****
Not to accept deposits from women, slaves, and minors. It contains 2 seifim.

One does not accept a deposit from a married woman; and if he accepted it he returns it to the woman. (And if she said they belong to so-and-so she is believed (Tur). If the woman went overseas and the husband came and claimed from the depositor and says his wife stole from him — he does not give them to him until the woman comes or dies — so it seems to me.) And if she said at her death that they belong to so-and-so — if she is believed by him he does as she said, and if not he gives to her heirs. (And likewise if she died and said nothing (Tur).) And some say that if she conducts business within the house she is not believed to say they belong to so-and-so.

{Rama: However, as long as she is alive he returns to the woman (Tur). However, if he returned to the husband the depositor is exempt even if she does not conduct business within the house (Mordechai chapter Chazakat HaBatim).}

**** ENGLISH ****
A husband who borrowed from the woman and afterward divorced her — she has no claim against him.

{Rama: And all the more so if he did not divorce her (logical opinion). And some say specifically if the money was hidden when he borrowed from her; but if the money was not hidden and he borrowed from her he must pay her even after he divorced her (Tur). And likewise if she seized them by force — since the money was not hidden she is believed to say they are mine or belong to so-and-so (the Rav's view). However, if she conducts business within the house or the husband trusts her regarding his own property she is not believed (Tur) (and see above siman 85 seif 12). If he borrowed from her with a document — she says the money was not hidden and he says it was hidden — she must bring proof or he is exempt from paying (Nimmukei Yosef chapter Chazakat HaBatim). And see Choshen Mishpat siman 62 regarding a woman on whose name documents are issued — what is their law. A woman who sold or pledged movable property — if the buyer or lender does not know they belong to the husband he need not return them to him even if the husband wishes to return their value and claims she stole them from him (Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 22 Laws of Marriage). And some say that if she conducts business within the house the husband is believed and must return to him without payment (R' Yitzchak siman 123). And it seems to me specifically when it is not her custom to pledge or sell without her husband's permission; but if her custom is such — what she did is done — so it seems to me (and see above siman 90).}
