**** ENGLISH ****
And some say, etc. This is the view of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rambam. The early authorities found difficulty from the fact that the Rambam rules like Rav Nachman, that in the case of acquire, you and the donkey, he acquires half; see Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel. This is the language of the Ran: it appears to me that they hold that even according to us, who rule that he acquires half, here not one of them is betrothed. For regarding one who transfers ownership, it is possible that his intent is that whoever can acquire should acquire, since there is no reason that because this one has no legal hand to acquire, one who does have a legal hand should not acquire. But this man who betroths acquires those women with the basket, and it was given to them only so that he should acquire all of them; for that is the way of a purchaser, to buy bad and good together because of the good, and the sellers make compromise among themselves. Just as one who buys one sale can say, I want my whole purchase and not half of it for half its price, so should the law be where one buyer purchases from two sellers. End quote. In my humble opinion, one should derive from his words that there is a distinction between one who gives a gift and one who transfers ownership through a sale. For example, one who transfers by way of sale items acquired through a kinyan sudar together with items not acquired that way, such as coins, by one kinyan sudar: according to his words, the whole sale is void once it is void regarding the coins, for the matter depends on the purchaser's intent. This is unlike a gift, as above, where certainly the recipient intends to acquire whatever he can. Regarding the transferor there is no reason to say that since he cannot acquire the whole gift, he transfers nothing to him; certainly he acquires whatever he can acquire. According to the Ramban and Ritva, who distinguish between including them in one word and in two words, there is no distinction between gift and sale, but only this: if he made a kinyan sudar to transfer land and coins, then he acquires only the land; but if he placed coins and movable items in one place and said, everything in this place I transfer to you by kinyan sudar, then since he included both together, he acquires nothing. With this the Mordekhai in Perek Mi Shemet becomes clear to me. His language is: there was an incident where one transferred by kinyan sudar land, movable items, and coins all at once, and the sages of the generation disputed the matter. Some say it is like acquire, you and the donkey, and just as he did not acquire the coins, so too he acquired nothing. Others say he acquired everything, since by migo that the kinyan takes effect on the other items, it also takes effect on the coins. R. Shimshon wrote, although he acquired the land and movable items, he did not acquire the coins; this must be the correct reading. For if not, when we say in the first chapter of Kiddushin that property without responsibility is acquired together with property that has responsibility, and we say that agav is required, why do I need agav? Let them be acquired through migo. And one should not say it is like acquire, you and the donkey, for a donkey is not subject to acquisition, but coins are acquired by meshikhah, etc. See Shiltei Gibborim, who wonders: but explicitly, etc. The Sema in siman 203 wrote that the first opinion must hold unlike Rav Nachman, based on the plain sugya in Kiddushin; therefore the Rama in siman 209 ruled simply like the view of R. Shimshon, which accords with the halachah, since we rule like Rav Nachman.
