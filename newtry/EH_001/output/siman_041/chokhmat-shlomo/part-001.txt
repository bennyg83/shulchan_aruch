<small>סעיף ב'</small> <b>המקדש וכו'</b> נ"ב עי' בב"ש ס"ק ב' מפורש הטעם דקי"ל כל שאינו בזאח"ז אפילו בב"א אינו והנה קשיא לי בש"ס זבחים משנה ג' ב' שאחזו בסכין ושחטו פטורין אחזו באבר והעלוהו חייבין וקשה לי לדברי ר' יוסי שמבואר שם בסיפא דס"ל דהעלה וחזר והעלה פטור ואינו חייב אלא אחת אף דהיה לו ידיעה בנתיים ובע"כ הטעם דבעינן שיהיה כולו שלם ולא חסר וא"כ למה יתחייבו שנים שהעלו הרי אינו בזאח"ז דאם העלה וחזר והעלה פטור בין הוא בין אחר כיון דהטעם הוא מכח דאינו שלם א"כ מה לי הוא מה לי אחר יהיה פטור א"כ אף בב"א יהיו פטורין דכל שאינו בזאח"ז אפילו בבת אחת אינו והנה על המשנה לק"מ די"ל דהך רישא נמי דברי ר"ש הוא דמחייב בהעלה וחזר והעלה והוי בזאח"ז ולר"י י"ל באמת שנים שהעלו פטורין אך על הרמב"ם קשה דהוא פסק כר"י ופסק בשנים שהעלוהו חייבין וקי"ל דכל שאינו בזאח"ז אפי' בב"א אינו ולו יהא דהרמב"ם פסק כר' יוחנן דבכמה אברים חייב על כל אחד עכ"פ באבר אחד פסק כר' יוסי דהעלה וחזר והעלה פטור עי' שם בכ"מ פ' י"ט מה"ל מעה"ק (הל' י"א) ולמה פסק שם בב' שאחזו באבר אחד והעלוהו חייבין הרי באבר אחד אינו בזאח"ז וא"כ אפי' בב"א אינו וצ"ע ודוק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif 2</small> <b>One who betroths, etc.</b> NB: See Beit Shmuel, s.k. 2, where the reason is explained: we hold that anything that cannot take effect one after the other cannot take effect even simultaneously. I have a difficulty from the Talmud in Zevachim, Mishnah 3: if two held a knife and slaughtered they are exempt, but if they held a limb and brought it up they are liable. This is difficult according to Rabbi Yosi, for it is explained there in the latter clause that he holds that if one brought it up and then brought it up again, he is exempt and liable only once, even though he had knowledge in between. Necessarily, the reason is that we require the item to be whole and not lacking. If so, why should two who brought it up be liable? Since this cannot be done one after the other, for if he brought it up and then brought it up again he is exempt, whether it was he or another, because the reason is that it is not whole. If so, even simultaneously they should be exempt, for anything that cannot take effect one after another cannot take effect even simultaneously.

There is no difficulty from the Mishnah itself, for one may say the first clause is also Rabbi Shimon’s view, who holds one liable when one brings it up and then brings it up again, and therefore it can take effect one after another. According to Rabbi Yosi, it may be that two who brought it up are truly exempt. But the Rambam is difficult, for he rules like Rabbi Yosi and also rules that two who brought it up are liable, while we hold that anything that cannot take effect one after another cannot take effect even simultaneously. Even if the Rambam rules like Rabbi Yochanan that with several limbs one is liable for each one, nevertheless with one limb he rules like Rabbi Yosi that if one brought it up and brought it up again he is exempt. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 19 of Hilchot Ma'aseh HaKorbanot, halacha 11. Why then did he rule that if two held one limb and brought it up, they are liable? With one limb it cannot take effect one after another, and therefore even simultaneously it should not take effect. This requires study.

<small>Seif 3</small> <b>And some say that even the foreign women are not betrothed, etc.</b> NB: See my responsum to the holy community of Ackerman, in my fifth edition, siman 252, where I wrote that this is specifically when they were not engaged in that matter. But if they were engaged in that matter, even the Rambam agrees that the foreign women are betrothed. This is only according to the Maggid Mishneh’s reason in the Rambam’s view, not according to the Ran’s reason. See there, with the help of Heaven.

The Maggid Mishneh wrote to resolve the Rambam’s words from the rule that when one says, "Acquire, you and a donkey," the person acquires half, because one statement is different. But this is difficult from the first chapter of Gittin, 8b, where the Gemara discusses "all my property is acquired by you," etc. It is proven there according to Rava, and we rule like him, that even in one statement we split the statement. The Maggid Mishneh’s reasoning appears to follow Abaye, but Rava holds that even in one statement we split the statement. One cannot answer that there it is only for rabbinic matters such as "it was written before me," because if so, why does the Gemara say "according to whom, according to Rabbi Shimon"? It could have answered that it follows Rabbi Meir for biblical matters, while Rava speaks only in rabbinic matters. Necessarily, there is no such distinction, and this is not like the Maggid Mishneh.

Regarding Tosafot there, s.v. "Abaye retracted," who write that he did not retract because of Rava’s question, in my humble opinion he certainly retracted because of Rava’s question. Since one statement is not split, there are two possibilities: either since it takes effect for this, it takes effect for that; or the reverse, since it does not take effect for this, it does not take effect for that. At first Abaye thought that migo can remove money from its possession, and therefore since he acquired himself, he acquired the property. Rava holds that migo is not effective to extract money, and therefore the property is not acquired. Once Rava introduced that migo cannot extract from possession, the reverse follows: since it cannot take effect for the property, it does not take effect for the other either, because the statement is one. Thus Abaye properly retracted because of Rava’s question. I later saw in Beit Meir, siman 41, that he hints to this sugya and to the view of the Maggid Mishneh, and I have explained the matter more fully, with the help of Heaven.
