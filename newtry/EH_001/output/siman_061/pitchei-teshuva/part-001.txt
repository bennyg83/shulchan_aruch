<b>"His betrothed to chuppah." — Whether kiddushin must precede chuppah; Mishneh LaMelech, Ramban, Mahariv, Sha'ar HaMelech.</b>
His betrothed to chuppah. See the Mishneh LaMelech (ch. 10 of Hilkhot Ishut, law 2), who raises the question whether for chuppah to acquire, the kiddushin must precede the chuppah — but if the chuppah preceded the kiddushin it does not effect marriage-status — or whether whenever there are both chuppah and kiddushin this avails. He proved from the Ramban's novellae (ch. 1 of Kiddushin, folio 10) that it does avail. He disputed the responsum of Mahariv [Beit Shmuel siman 63 (correctly: 64, subsection 6)], who ruled it is entirely null. The annotator there wrote that Tosafot (Ketubot folio 57, s.v. "Rav") and the Rashba's novellae there and Rabbi Yeruham (Nesiv 22, Part 2) share the view of Mahariv — see there. (In the responsa of the Radbaz, Part 1, siman 372, I see that he too is uncertain about this and writes: "Were I not hesitant I would say that order does not render it invalid — that if one betroths a woman, brings her to chuppah, and says the seven blessings, and afterward performs kiddushin, she is his wife in all respects, even though it is not proper to do so ab initio. This matter still requires study" — end of his words. See the Chelkat Mechokek on siman 55, subsection 9.)

See in the Sha'ar HaMelech there, in the treatise "Chuppat Chathanim," seif 8, where he writes regarding the view of the annotator cited above that there is no proof at all from Tosafot, the Rashba, and Mahariv — for all their words are to exclude chuppah alone without kiddushin, in disagreement with Rav Huna; but it is always the case that where there is chuppah and kiddushin, even not in order, it avails — following the Ramban's view. So too appears from Tosafot in the chapter "HaIshah Rabbah" (folio 89b) and from the Rashba's novellae (folio 55). He subsequently wrote that it seems there is no contradiction from all these to Mahariv's words, since Tosafot, the Ramban, and the Rashba wrote that even out of order it avails only where she entered the chuppah without kiddushin and afterward kiddushin was performed through intimacy — for even though she entered the chuppah before the kiddushin, at the moment of betrothal through intimacy she is at that very moment also delivered into the chuppah, and chuppah and kiddushin come simultaneously. For whether according to those who hold the main form of chuppah is bringing her to his house, or according to the Rambam who holds it requires bringing to his house plus seclusion — in any case, at the moment he secluded with her and betrothed her through intimacy while she is in his house, she is delivered into the chuppah; thus the chuppah extends until the moment of kiddushin, and they come simultaneously.

But in Mahariv's case — a widow who married on Erev Shabbat, where the custom is to seclude her on Erev Shabbat before the chuppah and kiddushin so as to permit intimacy on Shabbat — it follows that at the moment of kiddushin there is no chuppah at all, since a widow's chuppah consists only of seclusion; at the moment of seclusion she was not yet betrothed; that seclusion is therefore as nothing; and at the moment of public kiddushin there is no seclusion — obviously this avails nothing at all, and therefore if he is intimate with her on Shabbat it is like performing an act of acquisition on Shabbat. He elaborated further and concluded: in all cases the rule is that chuppah which preceded the kiddushin only avails where at the moment of the kiddushin the chuppah is still present; but in Mahariv's case, where at the moment of kiddushin there is no chuppah at all, apparently for all opinions this does not acquire, and he cannot be intimate with her on Shabbat — see there.

See also the Mekane in the treatise, siman 26, where he too writes that Rabbi Yeruham's view and Mahariv's view are primary, and rejects the Mishneh LaMelech's proof from the Ramban — see there.

(א) "Even though she was not yet intimate" etc. — In the margin of the Shulchan Aruch of Reb Akiva Eiger of blessed memory, he notes: "The Raanach (Part 1, siman 67) wrote that this is a doubtful legal matter, since for those decisors who hold that the 'if you say' formulation is not equivalent to a resolution (see Beit Shmuel siman 137, subsection 6), it is an unresolved question. I am puzzled: even though he cited there the view of the Responsa Maimoniyot in the name of the Ritzba that it is an unresolved question, nevertheless the Rosh wrote that it was resolved from what Rav Yosef taught — see there" — end of his words.

<b>הרי היא כאשתו. </b> עיין בס' שיטה מקובצת כתובות דף ז' ע"ב בענין מציאות החופה אם הוא מדאורייתא או מדרבנן ועיין בשעה"מ שם בקונ' חופת חתנים ס"ה ובהגהת טעם המלך שם מ"ש בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"Behold she is as his wife." — Whether chuppah is Torah or rabbinic.</b>
Behold she is as his wife. See the Shitta Mekubetzet (Ketubot folio 7b) regarding whether the institution of chuppah is of Torah origin or rabbinic. See also the Sha'ar HaMelech there in the treatise "Chuppat Chathanim," seif 5, and the Hagahot Ta'am HaMelech there regarding what was written on this matter.
