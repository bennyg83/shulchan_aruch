<b>אם קידש כו'. </b> דסגי בברכות ראשונות כמ"ש בפ' המדיר קדשה בטעות או בפחות מש"פ כו' ואם בעלו קנו משמע שא"צ הקטן לא כניסה לחופה אחרת ולא ברכה וכן בפ"ד מיתות המשיא בניו כו' סמוך לפירקן כו' ואע"ג דקדושי קטן אינו כלום ואפ"ה שרי למיבעל וכן בפ' חרש חש"ו שנשאו נשים כו' אין לה עליהם כלום רצו לקיימן י"ל כתובה ואין להן קדושין אפילו מדרבנן כמש"ו ועבהגמ"ר דקדושין סי' תתפ"ד שהאריך בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(ז) "If he betrothed etc." — Sources for not requiring new blessings.</b>
If he betrothed etc. The first blessings suffice, as stated in the chapter "HaMadir" [of Ketubot]: "If he betrothed her in error or for less than a perutah's worth" etc., "and if he was intimate with her, they have acquired" — indicating that neither re-entry into a new chuppah nor new blessings are required. Similarly in the chapter "Four Methods of Execution": "One who marries off his sons" etc. "close to their coming of age" etc. — and even though a minor's kiddushin amounts to nothing, intimacy is nonetheless permitted. Similarly in the chapter of the deaf-mute: "A deaf-mute, an imbecile, and a minor who married women" etc., "she has no claim against them" — "if they wished to keep them, she has a ketubah, and they have no kiddushin even rabbinicly," as I will write; see also the Haggahot Maimoniyot on Kiddushin, siman 884, which elaborated on this.

<b>כשר כו'. </b> רמב"ם וכשיטתו בס"א וראיה ממ"ש ב' א' לפיכך כו' או שפירסה נדה כו' אלמא א"י להנשא ובפ' בתרא דנדה ס"ו א' איעכב ז' יומי כו' ועוד לפי שיטתו שפירש דחופה היינו יחוד ונדה אסורה להתייחד עמו כל שלא בעל כמש"ש ד' והר"ן דריש כתובות כ' אבל לפי החולקים כנ"ל וכן חופה אינו יחוד לפ"ד כמש"ל סי' נ"ד אין אנו מקפידים ע"ז ומ"מ כשר הדבר כו' כמש"ש בש"ע כאן ולכן איעכב רבינא כו' וערא"ש בפ"ה ס"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(א) "Valid etc." — Proof from Ketubot and Niddah for the Rambam's view; the dissenting view.</b>
Valid etc. The Rambam, following his own method in seif 1. There is proof from what is written in two places [in Ketubot]: "Therefore" etc., or "she became a niddah" etc. — evidently she cannot marry. And in the last chapter of Niddah (66a): "He waited seven days" etc. Furthermore, according to his method, he explained that chuppah means seclusion, and a niddah is forbidden in seclusion with him unless he has already been intimate, as I wrote there, seif 4. The Ran at the beginning of Ketubot wrote: but according to those who disagree — as stated above — and since chuppah does not mean seclusion according to his view, as I wrote above in siman 54 — we do not object to this. Nevertheless it is proper etc., as written in the Shulchan Aruch here. Therefore Ravina waited etc. See also the Rosh, ch. 5, seif 6.
