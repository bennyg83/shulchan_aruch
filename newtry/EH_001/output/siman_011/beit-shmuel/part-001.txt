<b>אם היה לה קינוי וסתירה</b>. כ"כ הרמב"ם פ"ב ה"ס והטור בסימן קס"ח ומבואר שם דאסורה לבעלה אחר קינוי וסתירה משמע דכופין לבעלה להוציאה אלא בזמן הבית היה תקנה לדבר להשקות אותה מים המרים כדי לברר אם היא טהורה או טמאה, ובזה"ז דאין תקנה לדבר היא אסורה לבעלה והא דתניא פ"ק דכתובות דאסר' בקינוי וסתירה וע"א היינו כשבא ע"א תו אין משקין אותה אבל היא אסורה בזה"ז בלא ע"א אלא רק ע"י קינוי וסתירה ורי"ו דף קצ"ב כ' בקינוי וסתירה וע"א כופין אותו לגרש אותה ואם יש קינוי וסתירה ולית' ע"א אין כופין אותו לגרש אותה וא"י מנ"ל חילוק זה ולא משמע כן משאר פוסקים:
**** ENGLISH ****
If she had warning and seclusion. So too Rambam ch. 2 of Hilchot Sotah and Tur siman 178; it is explained there she is forbidden to her husband after warning and seclusion — implies we compel her husband to remove her; except in Temple times there was an enactment to give her bitter waters to clarify if she is pure or impure, and nowadays without that enactment she is forbidden to her husband. That which is taught ch. 1 of Ketubot that she is forbidden with warning and seclusion and one witness — means when one witness additionally came they do not give her to drink, but she is forbidden nowadays without one witness, only through warning and seclusion alone. And Rivan 192 writes: with warning and seclusion and one witness we compel him to divorce her; and if there is warning and seclusion without one witness we do not compel him to divorce her — and I do not know from where this distinction; and it does not seem so from other poskim.

<b>הרי היא אסורה</b>. ומבואר ברמב"ם אפילו העיכוב הוא ממנו דהוא לא היה רוצה להשקותה מ"מ היא אסורה לו והטעם הוא משום אחר קינוי וסתירה היא אסורה לבעל ולבועל ואין לה תקנה אלא ע"י בדיקת המים ובע"כ אינה שותה אף על גב דהוא מעכב שלא כדין מ"מ א"י להשקותה בלא דעתו ורצונו א"כ ממיל' היא אסורה כיון דא"י להתברר אם היא טהורה, ואפשר הב"ד יכולים לכופו שישקה אותו כדי לברר הספק מ"מ אם לא השקה אותה אסורה עליו ולנחשד ובחנם תמה הח"מ על הרמב"ם בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
Behold she is forbidden. It is explained in Rambam: even if the delay is from him — he did not wish to give her to drink — nevertheless she is forbidden to him; the reason is because after warning and seclusion she is forbidden to husband and paramour and has no remedy except through testing the waters, and therefore she does not drink even though he improperly delays; nevertheless one may not give her to drink without his knowledge and will — if so automatically she is forbidden since she cannot be clarified if she is pure; and possibly beit din can compel him to give her to drink to clarify the doubt; nevertheless if he did not give her to drink she is forbidden to him and to the suspect — Chelkat Mechokek wondered at Rambam about this.

**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise if she became forbidden, etc. These words of this hagah are seemingly wondrous — what did it innovate beyond the Mechaber's words; one may strain and resolve; but it seems this is the meaning: if her husband is a kohen and a Yisrael came upon her by force — rape of a Yisrael is permitted; and if her husband was a Yisrael she was permitted to husband and paramour as Tosafot write in ch. Bameh Madlikin that a Torah-observant wife was permitted to David since he came upon her by force; nevertheless if her husband is a kohen she is forbidden to her husband, and simple: where forbidden to paramour forbidden also to husband and likewise the reverse; therefore if forbidden to her kohen husband because of this forced relations she is also forbidden to the paramour even though her husband/paramour is a Yisrael. And part 4 is in doubt about this law; and from what is written it seems from this hagah she is forbidden to her husband and paramour even though paramour is Yisrael — and all this is per Tosafot's view above; and Bach also brought Tosafot's words: if a Yisrael came upon her by force she is permitted to husband and paramour — we learn from sugya ch. 1 of Ketubot where it states: why was Bathsheba not forbidden to David — because it was force — learn: if by force she is permitted even to paramour even though he came willingly; and there is another answer: because of Torah writ of bill of divorce he had written to his wife — per this one may say force is forbidden to paramour since he came upon her willingly; and must say Tosafot hold the first answer is primary. But in Yerushalmi it states, and in Tosafot Sotah beginning, as they brought — tannaitic source there: he was inadvertent and she intentional — forbidden to him since she leaves because of him he is intentional and she inadvertent — permitted to her husband; and if he divorced, forbidden to this one — learn: to paramour forbidden forever whether he came upon her inadvertently or intentionally and she is permitted to her husband; nevertheless forbidden to him since with him she was intentional; if so if Yisrael came upon her by force forbidden to paramour and permitted to her husband — and must say he holds the last answer in the Talmud is primary: Torah-observant wife was permitted to David because of bill of divorce he would write. And there is further proof from Tur siman 20: one by force and one willingly — force exempt and this one willingly liable; and Rama explained: difference for us — this one willingly forbidden and this one by force permitted — learn: if he came upon her by force forbidden to him since with him it was willingly; however for what he wrote there one may reject this proof. And if she was secluded with one man she is permitted to him and her husband, for we hold we do not forbid for seclusion; nevertheless we warn him — if he truly came upon her forbidden to him even though there are no witnesses — so we hear from responsum of Rosh and Mechaber Beit Yosef brought; and we learn from the sugya above ch. 1 of Ketubot where it says: why did they not forbid her to David; and Tosafot explain: even though there were no witnesses at the time David came upon her, nevertheless David knew he came upon her — why did they not forbid her to him.

Ugly matter. In this sugya all posekim including later ones already elaborated in their books — see responsum Maharshal siman 33, responsum Shiyurei Yisrael siman 77, responsum Maharam of Lublin siman 80, responsum Maharashdam siman 31; the principle per Hagahot Maimoniyot, Ri, and Rabbenu Tam: we remove from husband only with witnesses of defilement and then remove even if he had children from her; and if we do not know whether he came by force we do not say he came by force — so Beit Yosef; from the suspected man we remove when there is non-decisive rumor and witnesses of ugly matter and there were no children with her; appears Rif and Rambam also hold this view — see more nearby; Rashba also ruled from husband we remove only with witnesses of defilement — so responsum Maharam; per law we do not remove from husband except with witnesses of defilement — so responsum Ben Lev part 3 siman 6; see responsum Mahari of Lvov siman 183 — wrote in these laws one does not follow the stringent since there is a side to leniency to compel a get therefore follow majority views;
