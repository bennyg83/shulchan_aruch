וכן העד האחד המעיד מל' זה הי' משמע דוקא ע"א המעיד הא במסיח לפ"ת דלא נתכוין להעיד מותר לישאנה אמנם מדכ' הרשב"א יבמות כ"ה ד"ה גזלן דד"ת וק' דא"כ כי אקשינן מינה למתני' לימא מתני' במל"ת ולא תי' דא"כ אמאי לא ישא משמע מיני' דאף מל"ת אסור. וצ"ע קצת דהא אלו הי' שייך בעדות כזה לחשוד במכוין אמאי גזלן דד"ת כשר בו:
**** ENGLISH ****
And likewise the single witness who testified — from this language it implies specifically the testifying witness; but one who speaks incidentally without intent to testify is permitted to marry her. However from what the Rashba wrote Yevamot 25 s.v. robber it appears that even incidentally it is forbidden; and it requires slight examination — for if in such testimony one could suspect intent, why is a robber who is Torah-prohibited fit.

**** ENGLISH ****
{Rama: And some say even with two — Beit Shmuel s.k. 4; and likewise with three, etc. Taz. And per Perishah it is proven thus from the sugya of married woman that there is a difference between three and two — what does it challenge the reason of court, and what is written, etc. Perhaps what it is strict about court is because of "he may marry her" which implies even for a scrupulous person; or that there is no difference; and like the proofs of Taz that there too it refers to judging the case which also discusses court as explained in Tosafot Chullin; nevertheless it teaches "but they said," etc. But there is great difficulty: truly "he may marry her" which implies even for a scrupulous person one need not be concerned. However Taz wrote Tosafot imply to him that since they omitted here Rambam and Rosh and Tur — it appears to him that this "but" refers only to purchase where there is suspicion that both together signed improperly for their benefit; but regarding testimony for a woman even for a scrupulous person it is permitted, for a woman is not fit for two; and one can say they brought this out truly from "he may marry her"; and it requires examination.}
