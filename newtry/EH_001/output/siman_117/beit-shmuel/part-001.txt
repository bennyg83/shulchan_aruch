**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>But not the added ketubah.</b> The Mechaber ruled like Rambam, who holds that she does not receive the added ketubah because she is comparable to a woman with a defect, who does not receive the added ketubah; and there is no greater defect than this. The reason an aylonit receives the added ketubah is that he could examine whether she is an aylonit, and she is at least fit to live with him; but this woman is forbidden to him and is not fit to live with him, and he cannot examine her. So wrote the Maggid Mishneh. See siman 172, where it is explained that Rambam holds an aylonit is where she has all the signs of an aylonit. Although some signs cannot be examined, nevertheless he could have examined her, and if he had seen some signs he should not have married her. The general rule according to the Maggid Mishneh's approach is: a woman with a defect, even though he could examine her, nevertheless since she knows she has a defect she should have informed him; therefore she has neither ketubah nor added ketubah. When blood is found at intercourse, even though she did not know, he also could not examine her, and therefore she has neither ketubah nor added ketubah. This is similar to what is written in the Talmud and in Choshen Mishpat siman 333: whenever both the householder and the worker know, or both do not know, the loss falls on the worker, and he cannot extract from the householder unless the householder knew and the worker did not. So too here, she does not have a ketubah unless the husband could examine her and did not, while she did not know. Therefore with an aylonit she has a ketubah and the added ketubah, because she does not know whether she is an aylonit, for she does not know whether he had difficulty during intercourse. Although she knows some signs, it is not incumbent upon her to reveal to him that she is an aylonit, since perhaps she is not an aylonit and he can examine some signs; then he loses and is liable. In cases of those prohibited by negative commandments and other prohibitions, he could have investigated, and she thought perhaps she would appease him; therefore she has the added ketubah. According to this, the Maggid Mishneh does not disagree with the Ran's words that I cited in the preceding siman. However, according to Ramban and Itur, if blood is found she has the added ketubah, and they compare it to an aylonit because she does not know. This is also implied by the Tur. See Maggid Mishneh chapter 25, who also wrote in the name of Ramban and Itur that they hold an aylonit does not know that she is an aylonit, as I wrote in the preceding siman; Chelkat Mechokek discusses this at length unnecessarily. See Yoreh Deah siman 187 for these laws, where we hold that whenever blood is found close to intercourse, she is forbidden to her husband. At first glance it appears to me that even regarding money, her law is as though she saw during intercourse, and she has neither ketubah nor added ketubah. Although it is uncertain, perhaps she did not see during intercourse, nevertheless in any event she is forbidden to him because of the doubt; if so, she cannot extract the ketubah from him. This may be compared to a case of doubtful treifah, where the seller must return the money even if the seller did not know, as written in Rivash siman 499, because people separate from it due to the doubt, and there is no greater defect than this. See Yoreh Deah siman 119 and Choshen Mishpat siman 232. See Responsa Rashakh I, siman 45, who did not write so; this requires study.

(ג) <b>He must divorce her and may never take her back.</b> Even if she is later healed, there is concern that he will cast aspersions and say, "Had I known she would be healed, I would not have divorced her." Although he is forbidden to live with her, he can say, "I would not have divorced her; I would have waited," as written in the Ran. See siman 7 regarding these laws. In Maggid Mishneh and Ran there is a dispute even where he divorces her without specifying, and did not say it was because she is an aylonit, whether we are concerned for such aspersions. The Ran wrote that when it says he must divorce her, this does not mean that beit din forces him to do so; rather, since he cannot have marital relations with her, it is incumbent upon him to divorce her. It appears that what he wrote, that beit din does not force, means that they do not force the get itself. But they do force them not to live together, for she is forbidden to him. According to this, the gloss innovates in the name of Responsa Maharam that if he wishes to remain with this woman, he need not divorce her.

(ד) <b>And from the first act of intercourse.</b> So wrote Rambam in chapter 25. This refers to a widow. But if she is a virgin, we attribute it to blood of virginity. If she saw three times with every act of intercourse and intercourse was not painful for her, then we do not attribute it to blood of virginity, since it was without pain. There is room to doubt: perhaps what she saw in the first acts of intercourse also was not blood of virginity, but rather she always saw at the time of intercourse; or perhaps what she saw at first was blood of virginity, and now his field became ruined and she saw because of intercourse. If so, she has a ketubah. In Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 10, he wrote that Rambam does not speak of a virgin. However, there is not much proof from there, for what he wrote that Rambam does not speak of a virgin is because with a virgin, even if she saw three times after marriage, she is permitted to her husband.

(ז) <b>He need not divorce her.</b> Although below, siman 154, it is ruled that if she stayed with him ten years and did not give birth, and he says, "I will not have relations with her, but I will live with her before witnesses," we do not listen to him. Chelkat Mechokek answered that here, since she is certainly not fit for marital relations, he will marry another woman. One can also say that there it speaks according to the law of the Shas, where we compel him to marry another, and then there is concern lest he not marry another woman. Here it speaks regarding the concern that he might come upon her, and for that it helps if he lodges with her in the neighborhood. Responsa Maharam wrote that if he wants to marry another, he must divorce this one, so that people not say, "This is his wife and this is his prostitute." Chelkat Mechokek challenged him from what is written in siman 154, that where she stayed with him ten years he may marry another, and we are not concerned that people will say, "This is his wife and this is his prostitute."

(א) <b>The husband must bring proof.</b> We say "here the defects were found, here they were"; therefore the husband must bring proof that his acquisition was mistaken. If she is a bogeret, even if she is in her father's house she is considered as in the husband's domain, and the husband must bring proof. In all these cases it makes no difference where she is physically standing: before marriage she is in her father's domain even if she is in the husband's house; after marriage, or if she is a bogeret, even if she is in her father's house she is not in her father's domain, and the husband must bring proof. See the Rosh. If the husband brings proof that these defects existed in her father's house, we do not say "here they were found and here they were before erusin," because she has two chazakot: bodily chazakah that she was born without defects, and the chazakah that a person does not drink from a cup unless he examined it. Against this stands the chazakah that a person is not appeased by defects. Where there are competing chazakot, the burden remains on the husband. The note continues by comparing the sugya's answers of Rava and Rav Ashi, the question whether the ketubah belonged to the father or to her when the doubt arose, and Rama's rule in Yoreh Deah that a doubt is judged from the time it arose. The conclusion is that for the principal ketubah the husband must prove the defects preceded erusin; for the additional ketubah, bodily chazakah may not help in the same way, because the husband's consent to add was made without knowledge of the defects.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) <b>But because of other defects, etc.</b> He must provide the other obligations of food and clothing, but regarding conjugal relations we do not compel him to have children from a woman he hates. Even though he knew of her defects and waived them, nevertheless now she is repulsive to him and he cannot accept her. We also do not compel him to return the dowry to her, since she is the one delaying and does not wish to accept the Get; see there, shoresh 107. It appears that this speaks of defects that arose in her, or that he knew about. But if she had defects and he did not know, one may say that we compel her, since she acted improperly, as the Rosh wrote in his responsa, klal 35, that if he acted improperly and betrothed through deception, we compel him; likewise, we compel her.
