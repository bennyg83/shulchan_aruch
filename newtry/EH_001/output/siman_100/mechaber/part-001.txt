<b>From what the ketubah is collected and all other ketubah laws. It contains 16 seifim.</b>

By Talmudic law, the ketubah — i.e., principal ketubah and tosefet — are collected only from land. By enactment of the Geonim they are also collected from movables; and the same for all its conditions, except ketubat bnei d'krayin. Therefore if the heirs collected money owed to their father — she collects from them.

(However, the heirs have discretion to pay her with land (Hagahot Alfasi ch. HaKotev).) And specifically melog movables; but if the husband sold or gave them as a healthy gift — she does not collect from them.

{Rama: And some say specifically when he gave to others; but if he gave to his heirs as a healthy gift — a woman collects her ketubah from them (Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 16 and Maharai siman 86 and Mahariu siman 156), for all that he gave to his heirs is only like inheritance and the woman collects her ketubah from there (Mordechai ch. Na'arah in name of Maharam). One who commanded to give from his property to another after his death so-and-so — even though he commanded thus while healthy — a woman collects her ketubah from there, for it is only like a gift of a healthy person from whom she collects her ketubah (and see Choshen Mishpat simanim 251, 252, and 257).}

And if the heirs sold them after his death — she collects from them. And now they are accustomed to write in all ketubot that all his property, land and movables, movables bound to land — she acquired and acquired — nevertheless they did not practice collecting from movables he sold or gave as healthy gift, nor even from movables the heirs sold, because of market regulation.

(And see in Choshen Mishpat siman 113; and see above siman 118 whether a woman must bury her husband before collecting her ketubah.)
