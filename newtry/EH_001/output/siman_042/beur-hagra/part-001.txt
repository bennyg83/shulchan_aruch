**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But, etc.; and some say, etc.</b> They said that if one was coerced and sold, the sale stands; they did not say that if he was coerced and acquired, it stands. But the Rashba and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that if he was coerced to acquire, it is all the more so; and this is also the Ra'avad's view, except that he wrote that he must say "I want." Yet he already wrote that whenever one performs the act with his own hands, he need not say "I want," and the Maggid Mishneh wrote so here regarding the Ra'avad. The Baal Ha'itur's reason is as stated there: specifically coerced sale, where he received money, as there. See Tosafot there, s.v. "if we say," and s.v. "he betrothed," and the Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat 205 in his name.

The Rav wrote at the end of section 205, but it escaped them that the Rambam, Ra'avad, Rashba, Maggid Mishneh, Semag, and other decisors disagree; and this is the main view. It is specifically in a sale where he gives and receives no benefit at all. But where he takes money, even if the benefit is less than the loss, since a person does not wish to sell land, nevertheless Amemar says in Kiddushin that she is betrothed even though she receives only a perutah. Maharik wrote similarly, even where the benefit is very small. All the more so one acquires the field, for we say that he jumped and sold. According to the Semag, the passage "they hung him and he betrothed" refers to the man, and all the more to a sale. The Hagahot Maimoniyot wondered at him and explained like Rashbam and Tosafot that it refers to the woman, from "he acted improperly, etc." Still, as stated at the beginning of chapter 14 of Yevamot and chapter 5 of Gittin, the principle differs between him and her.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If they found written, etc.</b> As stated, testimony must be "from their mouths" and not from their writing. He holds like the Rambam that anything not validated is nothing, though many disagree, as stated in the second chapter of Ketubot: the Sages were lenient regarding validation of documents because it is rabbinic. The Rashba, however, wrote that even if it is validated it is nothing, lest they signed without her knowledge, as in Sanhedrin 29b, the case of the deed, etc., where they asked the scribes, etc. In such a case one does not know in the present time; it is not a document and is testimony from writing. See Choshen Mishpat 39:11 and 18.

The glosses of Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbi Simchah that even a validated document written properly is nothing, from the Tosefta in the second chapter of Ketubot: the power of witnesses is greater than the power of a document, for if witnesses say that so-and-so died or was killed, his wife may remarry, but if they found written in a document that so-and-so died or was killed, his wife may not remarry. The power of a document is greater than witnesses in that one who lends by document collects from encumbered property, whereas by witnesses he collects only from free property. Thus even a full document is nothing regarding a married woman, as the end regarding loans shows. The reason is that such a document was not given to be written in the case of a woman. The Yerushalmi at the end of Yevamot, cited by the Rosh, also discusses finding it in a document; even the lenient view there is because they were lenient in that matter. His words are correct, for the Yerushalmi can be read as speaking of a full document, and the baraita's reason is that it was not given to be written and is therefore testimony from writing.

<b>But, etc.</b> Since by Torah law they are valid; see above, section 28, paragraph 21, and Kiddushin 24b, "since it is a rabbinic exposition, etc.," all the more for a rabbinic matter. This is the Rambam's view, for he holds that even maternal relatives create betrothal rabbinically, as stated in Choshen Mishpat 33:2 in the gloss. Rav Hai Gaon, Baal Halakhot Gedolot, many Geonim, and the She'iltot brought the above proof. The Rif disagreed and wrote that the proof is nothing, for if so, all relatives should be the same, since all are from rabbinic exposition, while the plain verse "fathers shall not be put to death for children" concerns the sins of children. There is also an explicit verse in Chronicles indicating that no bill of divorce is required, as stated, "whoever betroths does so according to rabbinic intent."

What is said there, "a rabbinic exposition," the Rashba explained in responsum 1188, and Tosafot in Gittin 42b wrote similarly. He writes that the concern is lest one err, find him in the marketplace, and say, "you are my slave." Nevertheless, his view follows the Geonim, and he wrote that we do not say "whoever betroths does so according to rabbinic intent" except where it was stated explicitly; otherwise many cases in the Talmud would have used that principle, and they did not.
