<b>Against her will she is not betrothed.</b> Although we hold that one who sells under coercion, his sale is a sale, and she is as though she sold herself to her husband, the Gemara states that since the husband acted improperly, the Sages uprooted the kiddushin, for we hold that anyone who betroths does so according to the will of the Sages. If they coerced him to betroth, the Maggid Mishneh wrote that we do not say this is comparable to coercing her, because a woman cannot leave him and release herself from him, whereas a man can divorce her against her will; therefore she is betrothed. At first glance one may say that nowadays, after the enactments of Rabbeinu Gershom, that one may not divorce against her will, the woman's law should equal the man's. Perhaps in such a case, where he betrothed through coercion, Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact, and he can divorce her against her will. So too wrote the Taz. It is proven from the Maggid Mishneh that he holds that if one acquires under coercion, he acquires; for otherwise, from where would one have thought that if they coerced him to betroth, his kiddushin are kiddushin? After all, he acquires against his will. Although he can divorce her against her will, how does he acquire against his will? Rather, one must say that he holds that one acquires under coercion, as with a seller. Still, one might have thought to compare his coercion to her coercion; for this the Maggid Mishneh gave his reason, that they are not comparable because he can divorce her against her will. So too it is necessarily proven from the Semag that one acquires under coercion, for he holds that the sugya speaks where they coerced him, and his text is, she acted improperly; this shows that coerced acquisition is effective. The some-say view he brought is what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rif and the Ittur. So too it appears in Choshen HaMishpat at the end of siman 205 in his name, that they hold coerced acquisition is ineffective. There the Rama ruled like him. This is surprising, for here it is clear according to these poskim that one acquires under coercion, and Chelkat Mechokek also asked this. The Raavad wrote that here it speaks where they coerced him and he said, I want. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that he need not say, I want. See Sema at the beginning of siman 205, and see siman 134. If he betrothed her while he was half-asleep and half-awake, his acts are valid; Rashbatz.

<b>והא' מהם קרוב</b>. והא דלא נפסל השני בצרופו כאשר מבואר בח"ה סי' ל"ז דשניהם נפסלים אפילו אם יבוא השני להעיד מחדש לא מהני וכ"כ הסמ"ע סי' ל"ז ובסי' מ"ה אלא דוק' כשחתומים העדים אז מהני כשהכשר מעיד מחדש אבל בע"פ לא מהני וכן מוכח בש"ס פ"ק דמכות בסוגי' מה יעשו שני אחים וכו' וצ"ל דמיירי כאן בגווני דלא נתבטל העדות ושם פליגי הרי"ף והרא"ש אימת נתבטל העדות וכ"כ בח"מ גם י"ל דלא נתכוין לשם עדות ומ"מ מה שמעידים היום מהני אף על גב דלא  נתכוונו לשם עדות, שוב ראיתי לשיטות הרא"ש א"צ לזה אלא אף על גב דנתכוונו לשם עדות והעידו  כבר ס"ל דנתבטלו העדות היינו לענין דהב"ד א"י לפסוק על פיהם אבל כאן בעת הקדושין עדיין לא נתבטלו עדותן א"כ הקדושין היו קידושין כשירים א"כ אף על גב דהב"ד א"י  לפסוק ע"פ עדותן מ"מ המקדש קידש לפני כשירים, וכן אם קידש רק לפני שנים וא' מהם קרוב והעידו כבר לפני הב"ד מ"מ בעת הקידושין היה הקדושין לפני ע"א ובספר ב"ה כתב היכ' דשניהם מודים מהני עדות של הכשר ולית' דהא ילפינן מקר' דעד קרוב מבטל העדות והוי כאלו היה מקדש לפני עדות פסולי' ולא מהני הודאה שלהם אלא נ"ל כמ"ש ואם העד קרוב לראובן ורחוק לבתו המתקדשת אם אבי' מקבל הקדושין פסול לעדות כי האב הוא עיקר בעל הדבר אף על גב לענין ממון צוואה יש מכשירים בכה"ג כמ"ש בח"ה סי' ל"ג ועיין תשו'  רש"ך ח"א סי' ב':
**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) <b>And one of them is related.</b> The reason the second witness is not invalidated by joining with him, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 37, where both are invalidated and even if the second later comes to testify anew it does not help, and so Sema wrote in siman 37 and siman 45, is that specifically when the witnesses are signed, it helps for the valid witness to testify again; but with oral testimony it does not help. This is also proven in the Talmud, the beginning of Makkot, in the sugya of what should two brothers do, etc. One must say that here it speaks in a manner where the testimony was not nullified, and there Rif and the Rosh disagree when testimony is nullified; so wrote Chelkat Mechokek. One may also say that he did not intend to testify, and nevertheless what they testify today helps, even though they did not originally intend to testify. I later saw that according to the Rosh's approach this is unnecessary: even if they intended to testify and had already testified, he holds that the testimony is nullified only in the sense that the beit din cannot rule on their testimony; but here, at the time of kiddushin, their testimony had not yet been nullified. If so, the kiddushin were valid kiddushin, and although the beit din cannot rule on the basis of their testimony, nevertheless the man performed kiddushin before valid witnesses. Likewise, if he betrothed only before two people and one of them was related, and they already testified before the beit din, nevertheless at the time of kiddushin the kiddushin was before one witness. In Sefer Bayit Chadash he wrote that where both admit, the testimony of the valid witness helps. This is not so, for we derive from Scripture that a related witness nullifies the testimony, and it is as though he betrothed before invalid witnesses; their admission does not help. Rather, it appears to me as I wrote. If the witness is related to Reuven but unrelated to his daughter who is being betrothed, if her father accepts the kiddushin, he is invalid as a witness because the father is the principal party. Although regarding monetary wills some validate in such a case, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 33, see Responsa Rashakh I, siman 2.

<b>We compel him.</b> In the Rambam the text reads: we compel her. Kesef Mishneh wrote that the Tur's reading is primary, because compulsion does not apply to her; if she wishes to marry, she must take a get. In Kuntres Acharon, the Bach explains that we compel her not to marry without a get. According to the Tur's reading, that we compel him, the reason is that the witnesses' testimony is as though he said, I betrothed you, and she says, you did not betroth me, in which case we compel him, as written below. However, if he wishes to betroth her anew, one may say that we do not compel him, even though when she says, you did not betroth me, one may say that we compel him even if he wishes to betroth her; that case is different, because she does not want him. Here, however, he certainly betrothed her, so we do not compel him to divorce if he wishes to betroth her; rather, this speaks where he withholds and does not wish to betroth her, and therefore we compel him.
