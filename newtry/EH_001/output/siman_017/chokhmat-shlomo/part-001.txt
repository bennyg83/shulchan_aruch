There in seif 1. See Beit Shmuel brought in the name of Maharival that Elijah's wife or R' Yehudah bar Liva'i is permitted, for she is not called a married woman but an angel's wife; and in my humble opinion this does not appear so — for if so there is difficulty on the plain sugya in chapter 4 of Eruvin that there are techumim above ten handbreadths from the baraita "I am a nazir on the day Ben David comes," etc.; and afterward it challenges there on one Shabbat to permit, and what proof from Elijah — behold among the dead he is free and is exempt from all mitzvot; also what did Rabbah challenge in chapter HaMekabel in Bava Metzia — "my master is not a kohen," behold he is an angel and is not obligated in fulfillment of Torah — perforce since he did not die he is judged as a person as if alive and is considered a person and not an angel, and automatically his wife is forbidden; and this is clear proof in my humble opinion. Seif 2 — The woman who said to her husband in his presence "you divorced me," etc. I was asked: why when she says "you divorced me" is she believed based on the presumption that a woman does not act brazenly, etc. — behold there are against it two presumptions: presumption of Torah prohibition and presumption of money for those who hold she collects her ketubah thereby; if so it is one against two. And even though the presumption that a woman does not act brazenly is a presumption that comes from reasoning and is preferable to other presumptions — nevertheless there is no reasoning to say it is more preferable than two presumptions. The questioner asked and I answered him: the difficulty here is only for one opinion; for regarding death she certainly collects her ketubah, and Tosafot challenged regarding one who had relations with her on Yom Kippur — behold he stands for a sin-offering, for two against two is rabbinic safek and from Torah we establish on presumption; and they answered: she examines and marries, etc. Difficulty: behold here too are two presumptions — also presumption of money — and still he stands for a sin-offering; strained to say she examines for two presumptions. Further difficulty: regarding falling in endless waters all wrote she leaves Torah prohibition for majority indicate death, and there is an opinion she is completely permitted — why does she take her ketubah thereby, and we do not follow money after majority? But in truth there is no beginning to this difficulty — for prohibitions and monetary matters are separate matters and do not combine; similar to what we hold in Choshen Mishpat siman 87 — where the essence of the claim is because of prohibition, even though there is practical difference for money, nevertheless he is exempt from oath; behold we follow the essence of the claim and do not regard what follows automatically. So too here one may say: behold when claiming ketubah she is not believed; only when not claiming she is believed to say "you divorced me," and the essence of the claim is because of prohibition — one should not judge what will follow afterward regarding money (the Maharach said a word of truth in his mouth, and so wrote the author of Pela'ah in his novellae end of chapter HaIshah ShenitArmelah and the Rosh). And see Yevamot 93b — R' Shimon asked regarding one witness with a yevamah; and in Tosafot there s.v. one witness with a yevamah until Rava said one witness is believed with a yevamah from a kal vechomer; and Rashi explained there and Tosafot wondered on him; and in my humble opinion one may say the Gemara certainly knew the reason one witness is believed is because of dayika uminsaba — only it needed to ask whether prohibitions and monetary matters are one matter and combine, or are separate matters and do not combine; therefore if here it is one matter — behold there is presumption of Torah prohibition also presumption of money — therefore one witness alone without dayika cannot reject two presumptions; perforce the reason is because of almoni and one presumption is rejected by avdei legluyei and one by dayika uminsaba; automatically with a yevamah where there is no presumption of money — obligation of ketubah applies either way when her husband died — therefore the reason of almoni alone suffices; or perhaps prohibition and money do not combine and there is only one presumption alone and dayika suffices; but almoni does not help to reject the presumption — therefore with a yevamah where dayika is inapplicable she is forbidden; and thus Tosafot's difficulty s.v. one witness with a yevamah is resolved; therefore Rava holds simply that prohibitions and money combine and there are two presumptions; and although we also need dayika — nevertheless we need almoni and it is proven the reason of almoni helps to overturn one presumption; therefore with a yevamah one reason of almoni alone suffices; therefore he says kal vechomer for prohibition of karet he permitted; and Rashi is settled — examine and understand, for I shortened. And behold what I wrote that with a yevamah there is no presumption of money — this is even though if she performs yibum she has no ketubah immediately; nevertheless as long as she did not perform yibum the yavam did not acquire his dead brother's property — if so he and the yavam are equal in them and he has no greater presumption than she to establish money on her presumption; on the contrary she has a clear debt and he has only safek if he wishes to perform yibum — therefore the property is not in his presumption more than hers. Examine and examine. And behold what I wrote above that prohibition and money do not combine — do not reject this from what we say at the end of Yevamot that R' Akiva holds witnesses for women do not require examination because there is ketubah to collect — it is similar to what the questioner above wanted to reject; but in truth it is not similar — for there from Torah even for money we require examination, only the rabbis enacted we do not require examination in such a case; therefore if it can be said ab initio the rabbis did not divide in their enactment — it depends on this: if there is also practical difference for money — since there is practical difference also for money it is in the category of such a case and one can say the rabbis did not divide in their enactment; but if there is no practical difference for money at all — it is inapplicable to say they did not divide, for it is not in the category of the enactment. But regarding Torah certainly prohibition and money do not combine — so is correct and plain; examine.

Seif 3 — A woman whose husband went to a distant land and they testified he died, etc. Behold in Yevamot (93b) it is asked regarding testimony of death whether it is because of almoni or because of dayika; and I wondered: why is it not resolved from there — behold the view of Rav Acha in chapter 1 of Sanhedrin that from Torah even one is fit to judge; how is one witness believed to extract money? I already challenged thus in my composition Turei Zahav on Yoreh De'ah 100 laws of bekhor in responsum 130 and wrote the reason is because of almoni; if so it is proven that for money one witness is believed by almoni; behold a matter of forbidden relations is derived matter from matter from money; if so here too he is believed by almoni. This is a question; one may say the Gemara's questions refer to that opinion in Sanhedrin that from Torah requires three; with this it is a question. Rambam ruled that the question was resolved; and it is true per what I wrote in several responsa already that not every matter of almoni is equal — there is almoni that helps from Torah as in ashtamud'inanhu, and there is not almoni immediately such as woman's testimony — here it does not help from Torah; yet it is not settled that in chapter 4 of Yevamot R' Eliezer and Ravina dispute regarding ashtamud'inanhu; and if two are required why does it not say it is proven R' Eliezer holds one suffices, for he holds one judges from Torah by almoni; and it is proven in any case where almoni immediately helps with one — how can one think here two are required; strained to say rabbinically two are required, for regarding woman's testimony the rabbis were lenient and from Torah we say they were more stringent here; behold how many leniencies they were lenient regarding woman's testimony. Further difficulty for Rivash who holds one does not help in ashtamud'inanhu except if they testify before the act and not at the time of the act — and from Torah one suffices; strained to say Rivash follows the opinion that requires three from Torah — uncertain in all this. And behold Tosafot there s.v. one witness with a yevamah — even according to the opinion that because of almoni one also needs the reason of dayika; only it is asked whether a small dayika suffices or not — see there; and apparently it lacks reason: if because of almoni he is believed from Torah — why need the reason of dayika? But the intent is per what we learn in Bekhorot (36b) there — since he has a claimant he trembles; it is proven explicitly there that one must distinguish regarding almoni between there is a claimant or not; therefore here too if dayika is applicable almoni helps, but if dayika is inapplicable almoni does not help. And behold one may say Rambam's words that he wrote the reason one witness is believed is because of almoni — all wondered on him, for in the Gemara it is an unresolved question; per the above it appears Rambam derived this from the sugya of Bekhorot where there are two versions for one version almoni helps even without a claimant, and for one version almoni does not help except where there is a claimant; and so too should be the Gemara's question in Yevamot whether the reason one witness is believed is because of almoni even without dayika like one version in Bekhorot that even without a claimant almoni helps, or only because of dayika — because there is a claimant almoni helps but without a claimant almoni does not help; and the Gemara's question is whether all like the first version in Bekhorot or the second; and if so since the Gemara concludes in Bekhorot the halakhah is like the first version that almoni helps without a claimant — we hold also one witness for a woman that almoni helps even without dayika; and from this Rambam derived thus. Seif 4 — All are believed to testify for her, etc. Behold in my composition edition 4 siman 21 I brought a safek in the name of another sage — perhaps Chazal did not believe one witness for a husband's death except if he testifies on actual death and not on other details of the matter; and I proved there there is no distinction in the matter; however it appears this is specifically if there is no testimony of witnesses or women and disqualified persons against the presumption of Torah prohibition; or one may say on the contrary it is preferable if they do not testify on actual presumption of Torah prohibition; and in any case it is not worse; therefore since a woman dayika uminsaba examines for presumption of Torah prohibition — Chazal believed them. But if they testify against the actual presumption — such as one came before us and established his name Yosef son of Shimon and married a woman, and afterward wandered and left his wife an agunah, and his wife heard from guests and youths that he changed his name and his name was Reuven Ba'al Milchamah [as in the incident that came before me responsum to Premishla] — and afterward came one witness that Reuven Ba'al Milchamah died — in this certainly she is forbidden, since there is no clear testimony before us that he changed his name, only she says so and she too did not hear from witnesses in court; if so since he was established before us by another name — this testimony that he changed his name is against the presumption; therefore it is like two presumptions of Torah prohibition and presumption that his name was established as Yosef son of Shimon; if so for one presumption the reasoning of dayika has power to harm the presumption; but for two presumptions one presumption that a woman dayika does not have power to diminish both; therefore we say the presumption of dayika is removed for presumption of married woman — still remains presumption that his name was established thus and so; and they are believed only with fit witnesses. Seif 58 — A woman who was betrothed and erred, etc. Behold from Rama's language that wrote "she must leave from this one and that one" — it implies she requires a get from the second; and in my opinion this law is very uncertain. Behold the source of this law is from chapter HaIshah Rabba — they decreed lest they say the first divorced and the second married; and in truth it is explicit there that it discusses her husband went to a distant land and they came and told her your husband died, etc.; per this one may say specifically in such a case — since she married based on one witness or two witnesses and from presumption they do not come to court to testify and she married based on complete permission of court — here there is concern that even if he comes and they say the first divorced and the second married — they will say the court certainly dayika and would not permit her except because they know the first divorced; but in her error thinking she was not betrothed — since it was not done by court nor by witnesses nor was the matter publicized that she married with permission from the outset — they will err to say the first divorced and the second married; and whence to be stringent here in a rabbinic matter — since the get from the second is only rabbinic; and proof for this directly from what is stated above in this siman seif 1 — "a married woman is among the forbidden relations and kiddushin do not take effect in her," etc.; and behold since afterward he mentioned rabbinic get in the end and in the beginning did not mention thus — it is proven kiddushin do not take effect even rabbinically; perforce it discusses she was betrothed not in her husband's presence — for if in his presence behold he wrote afterward she is betrothed to the second for a woman does not act brazenly, etc.; perforce it discusses not in her husband's presence; and so there is proof from siman 46 seif 5 — "the kiddushin of the second are nothing" proves she does not need a get at all even rabbinically; and why are we not concerned lest the first divorced, etc. — perforce in such a case we are not concerned for this. However these proofs — behold it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch above seif 57 that specifically when she married she needs a get from the second and not when only betrothed; nevertheless although we have no proof — since the Gemara and poskim specify specifically in such a case when her husband went to a distant land and they told her your husband died — and it does not teach when her husband is here and she went and married another — that she needs a get from the second; perforce in such a case she does not need a get from the second; and whence to be stringent in such a case. And even Rama who is stringent — it appears this is specifically in her error since in any case she married with permission and the matter was publicized — there is concern they will say the first divorced since she married with permission; but if she married another wantonly — it appears certainly she does not need a get from the second; and even though she excuses herself now that they told her at first her husband died — she is not believed to compel him to give her a get to be forbidden to her relatives; for what Chazal believed her to say her husband died is because of presumption dayika uminsaba; but since she already married and it is known her husband is alive — it appears she did not dayika; if so on the contrary the presumption of dayika supports the opposite that they did not tell her he died since she married wantonly; therefore she has no power to compel him to give a get — only she is forbidden for she made herself a piece of prohibition; per his words she needs a get from the second rabbinically and he is not obligated to give her a get — only we request from him as the explicit law in siman 48. And behold proof for my words from Yevamot 72 regarding a woman who did not wait after her husband three hours — and we establish it in erroneous kiddushin; and see Rashi there she does not need a get; nevertheless since it is the way of marriage the rabbis did not decree; difficulty: why not establish she married the first husband literally and they told her your husband died and she married another within three hours, or erred and thought she was not betrothed and married another — in this she needs a get from this one and that one; therefore they did not decree since it is rabbinic marriage and requires a get; nevertheless he is not profaned from the second — one may say she became pregnant from first intercourse and is not yet a zona; and he is not profaned, for he is profaned only from those disqualified from kehunah; as stated explicitly in Shulchan Aruch siman 7 seif 14 that one who has relations with forbidden relations in first intercourse is not profaned except from mamzer; therefore he does not defile them. And we say that wherever she does not need a get at all — they properly decreed only where she needs rabbinic get — then they did not decree; for either way from Torah even in promiscuity he is kohen and the rabbis decreed; and rabbinically behold it is marriage — why not answer thus; perforce she does not need a get from the second only when he went to a distant land and not when the husband is here; and when he went to a distant land it is not common that the time would be so close from his departure until the second marriage that there would not be three hours from the day of his departure until the second marriage; and there would be witnesses in the middle who told her he died; therefore it does not establish such a case; and still uncertain in resolving the above difficulty; however for the law it appears as I wrote; and even in this I do not decide — perhaps God will merit me another time to explain more on this; examine well.
