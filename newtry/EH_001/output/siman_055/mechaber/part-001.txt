**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Law of a betrothed woman regarding sustenance, burial, inheritance, and whether she has a ketubah. It contains 7 seifim.</b>

A betrothed woman is forbidden to her husband by rabbinic law all the time she is in her father's house; and one who has relations with his betrothed in his father-in-law's house — they strike him with stripes of rebellion.

{Rama: And even in seclusion they are forbidden; therefore a betrothed couple who are in one house — they recite seven blessings lest they be secluded (Mordechai first chapter of Ketubot). And some say they should not dwell together lest they become disgusted with each other (Kol Bo). And even in matchmaking without betrothal there is concern (Chiddushei Agudah first chapter of Ketubot).}

Even if he betrothed her through intercourse — it is forbidden for him to have a second act of intercourse in her father's house until he brings her into his house and is secluded with her and sets her apart for him; and this seclusion is called entering the chuppah, and it is called marriage everywhere. One who has relations with his betrothed for the sake of marriage after he betrothed her — from when he secluded with her he acquired her and she became a married woman and behold she is as his wife for all matters; and he must bless the blessing of bridegrooms in the groom's house before marriage.

{Rama: And some say chuppah is not seclusion; rather whenever the groom brought her to his house for marriage (so Ran in his commentary beginning of Ketubot). And some say chuppah is when they spread a cloth over her head at the time of blessing (Beit Yosef brought it). And some say a virgin's chuppah is from when she went out in the wedding canopy, and a widow from when they were secluded (Tosafot first chapter of Yoma). And the widespread custom now is to call chuppah the place where they bring a spread canopy on poles and bring the groom and bride under it in public; and he betroths her there and they bless there the blessing of betrothal and marriage; and afterward they bring them to their house and eat together in a modest place — and this is the chuppah practiced now. See below siman 62 seif 9 and siman 60.}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who betroths a woman and wrote her a ketubah and did not enter her to the chuppah — she is still betrothed and not married, for the ketubah does not effect marriage; and if he died or divorced her she collects the principal of the ketubah from free property and does not collect additions at all since he did not enter. But if he betrothed a woman and did not write her a ketubah and died or divorced her while she was betrothed — she has nothing, not even the principal, for they did not institute for her principal ketubah until she marries or until he writes.

{Rama: And some say a betrothed woman has a ketubah — Rosh and Ran and Tur (but the custom follows the first view).}
