<b>למעשה. </b> הראב"ד והטור והרשב"א והר"ן הרמ"ך ס"ל דאפי' באמירה צריך להקדים התנאי ולא כהרמב"ם שכתב הקדים המעשה היינו שעושה המעשה ועיין בב"י וכתב הפרישה והב"ח הביאו בק"א. אם נתן הדינר בידה ולא אמר הרי את מקודשת לי ואח"כ אמר אם תתני לי מנה הרי את מקודשת לי בדינר זה ואם לא תתני וכו' ה"ז תנאי גמור וצריך לקיימו אבל אם אמר בעת הנתינה הרי את מקודשת ואח"כ אפי' תכ"ד אמר על תנאי כך וכך לא הוי תנאי עכ"ל. וה"ה בעלמא מי שנותן מתנה לא' ואחר הנתינה אפילו תכ"ד אומר ע"מ כך וכך לא מהני התנאי כיון שקודם לזה זכה בסתם ט"ז וכל התנאים שמתנה עם השליח אפילו לא כפל התנאי והמעשה קודם לתנאי קיים שלא תתרצה את עצמו אלא בתנאי זה ב"י בשם הר"ן. וכנה"ג הביא בשם ספר חוקות הדיינים דאפילו בתנאי הנעשה ע"י שליח צריך שיהא קודם למעשה ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
Before the act. Ra'avad, Tur, Rashba, and Ran — RaMAh holds that even in speech one must place the condition first, unlike Rambam who wrote the act came first — meaning he performs the act. See Beit Yosef; Perishah wrote and Bach brought in siman 101. If he gave the dinar into her hand and did not say "behold you are betrothed to me," and afterward said "if you give me a maneh behold you are betrothed to me with this dinar" — etc. — this is a complete condition and must be fulfilled; but if at the time of giving he said "behold you are betrothed" and afterward even within the time of an utterance said on condition thus-and-so — it is not a condition. And likewise generally: one who gives a gift to another and after giving even within the time of an utterance says on condition thus-and-so — the condition does not help since beforehand he acquired it plainly (Taz). And all conditions one stipulates with an agent — even if he did not double the condition and the act preceded the condition — stands that she does not reconcile herself except with this condition (Beit Yosef in name of Ran). And Knesset HaGedolah brought in name of Sefer Chukot HaDayanim that even in a condition done through an agent the condition must precede the act — see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
Or not. This law divides into four aspects: (1) if both say the condition was fulfilled; (2) if both say it was not fulfilled; (3) if they contradict each other; (4) if one says fulfilled and the other says he does not know. If the condition is active fulfillment: if both say fulfilled they are believed — lest they waive to each other; if both say not fulfilled — as kiddushin are void they are also believed; if he says fulfilled and she says she does not know — safek; if she contradicts him with certainty that it was not fulfilled — for all kiddushin are void. One who says the condition was fulfilled is as saying I betrothed you and she is forbidden to his relatives. If he said it was not fulfilled — even if she says with certainty it was fulfilled she is not believed without proof; nevertheless she is forbidden to his relatives. If the condition is passive: if both say fulfilled and did not transgress — likewise if he said fulfilled and did not transgress and she says she does not know — he is believed; if she contradicts him saying it was not fulfilled — it appears from Ramban too she cannot contradict without witnesses — we are not concerned he transgressed in practice; if both say not fulfilled but transgressed in practice — they are also not believed — we are not concerned they transgressed the condition in practice. See Beit Shmuel and examine; see Be'er Heitev.
