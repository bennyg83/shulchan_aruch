המעשה ממש להתנאי ולהטור החילוק בלשון התנאי ושיט' הטור ברורה בפי' תנאי קודם למעש דהיינו בתנאי עצמו יש חילוק דצ"ל באם אומר אם תתן לי מנה דזהו תנאי ואח"כ אמר ה"א מ"ל דזהו מעשה לאפוקי להיפך ה"א מ"ל אם תתן לי מנה אבל בנתינה עצמה לא תלי מידי כ"ז נ"ל ברור ונכון במשא ובמתן זה בס"ד ומשמעות רוב הפוסקים שמביא ב"י לענין הלכה כהטור:
**** ENGLISH ****
Condition before the act. Rambam wrote: if he said to her "Behold you are betrothed to me with this," gave into her hand, and completed and said "If you give me a maneh behold you are betrothed to me, and if you do not give me you shall not be betrothed" — behold the condition is void because the act preceded, even though everything was within an utterance. Thus he explains that the act the mishnah mentions — what he actually does, such as the act of kiddushin — requires the condition precede giving kiddushin; and in condition language there is no distinction. Tur challenged this — this is obvious: if he gave and afterward stipulated it is a different law entirely, and even without sons of Gad condition language; and for kiddushin we do not say within an utterance is like speech. Beit Yosef answered: it teaches that even though kiddushin speech is the condition — he says "behold you are betrothed with this on condition" — even if he gave kiddushin before mentioning the condition, nevertheless since they are different matters without pause, his opening words are adjacent to the end and we say kiddushin did not take effect except on that condition; were it not that we derive only from sons of Gad condition that the condition must precede the act. Below it is explained this answer does not hold. Perishah wrote: Rambam holds we require two things — giving also requires speech at time of giving; if he said "behold you are betrothed" when he gave the dinar into her hand the condition does not help; but if he gave the dinar and did not say "behold you are betrothed" she is betrothed — this is called condition before act since he gave before saying anything, as a deposit not as kiddushin; if afterward he said "if you give me thus behold you are betrothed" — this is a condition requiring fulfillment. Why does Tur challenge "obvious" — if Rambam holds even after giving in her hand and afterward saying "if you give behold you are betrothed" it is not a condition, what is Tur's challenge? Perhaps the mishnah teaches even in such case it is not a condition. Maharshal also wrote this is obvious: if he was silent first, gave, and afterward said the condition — it is a complete condition; he strained to explain Tur's challenge with difficulty. I wonder how those Geonim were not precise — with a deposit in her hand it does not help if he said "behold you are betrothed" unless she said yes; here she said nothing — not mentioned in Rambam; if so how did they prove from Rambam's language mentioning speech first "behold you are betrothed" — there is necessity besides condition before act, otherwise there is no kiddushin at all since it was after giving money and she did not say yes; if so there is no proof from Rambam's words — we cannot say from reasoning that even if she says yes after silence, giving, and stipulating it helps to fulfill the condition since kiddushin did not take effect until now; nevertheless there is no actual giving but only to retain what is already in her hand and it helps for kiddushin law as if giving now; but it does not help that we say condition preceded act — her yes only retains the original giving and the act preceded the condition, like saying first "behold you are betrothed," giving, and then she need not say yes — Rambam cited one of these. What Tur wrote on Rambam is unclear — "obvious" if he gave in her hand without condition she is betrothed without condition. It appears: one who gives a gift and says "I give you this gift," gives it, and afterward says some condition in the gift — even within an utterance the condition is nothing since he already acquired plainly. Why need we learn sons of Gad condition here since without it there is no condition? For Rambam, without sons of Gad condition the condition is void and the gift stands — Tur and Rambam differ only in reasoning: per first view — within an utterance is like speech; per Tur without it does not help since he already gave plainly. Proof for Tur from Bava Metzia 10: one who lifts a found object for his fellow and said "I acquired it first" — they say in the end "why do I need" — Rashi: even if he did not say first explicitly, presumably he says first; how can he say I acquire it now when it is not in his hand? If within an utterance helps even after giving there would be no question — we would say he wishes to acquire after giving within an utterance; he must say "the condition — I acquired first." Also this is obvious — it does not help even within an utterance since already in his possession; likewise here. This is irrefutable proof per my humble opinion; see siman 47 and 48. With what we noted, Beit Yosef's words to answer Tur's challenges from "since they are different matters without pause" fall away — it is not so since kiddushin were delivered before stating the condition the condition no longer helps even within an utterance, as I brought clear proof from that Gemara; and one can say for sons of Gad condition there is distinction between Rambam and Tur — Rambam has no distinction in condition language, only whether the act literally preceded the condition; Tur's distinction is in condition language itself — in the condition itself there is distinction: he must say "if you give me a maneh" which is the condition and afterward "behold you are betrothed" which is the act; not the reverse "behold you are betrothed if you give a maneh"; but in the giving itself nothing depends. This seems clear and correct in give and take, and most poskim Beit Yosef cites for halakhah follow Tur.

**** ENGLISH ****
Food and clothing etc. — this is not stipulating against Torah since it is monetary and given for waiver; unlike onah which is bodily pain not subject to waiver. Seif 4 on condition she have relations with one forbidden to her — Tur wrote on condition she have relations with my father etc.; Perishah wrote the reason is prohibition of incest; if so likewise with any other forbidden by incest; Tur cited father because in Gemara chapter HaMegaresh regarding get (it says there "to my father") and there prohibition is specifically to father but to another she is permitted after the get; therefore here too he cited father. Truth is as Perishah — in chapter HaMegaresh regarding one who gives a get and retains — we need to ask there besides husband's sister what if sister dies and he sees her — we do not resolve; perhaps per this reasoning death is required; if so how say plainly here it is possible through perhaps he dies — Perishah's words are essential.

Beit Yosef challenged siman 39: Tur wrote if he betrothed and had relations plainly etc. and did not mention entry there; Derishah added challenge — if entry helps all the more relations for entry is the bringing in; if without entry relations are promiscuous relations and we stand on reason a person does not make his relations promiscuous — from this Beit Yosef greatly strained to reconcile. Per my opinion no challenge: in Beit Shmuel in name of Rif it is clear — where "brought her in" means after time; where "and had relations" means immediately.
