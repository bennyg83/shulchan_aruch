<b>אסורה לכהן</b>. ואם היא נשואה לכהן תצא ממנו אף על גב בזנתה ואומרת לכשר נבעלתי ונשאת לכהן דקי"ל דלא תצא אפילו ברוב הפסולים וכאן אפי' אם אמרה טהורה אני אינה נאמנת שאני זנות דאמרינן אשה בודקת ומזנה אף על גב דאינו  אלא ספק שמא זנתה ובעלמא קי"ל אין אשה נאסרת מבעלה אלא בקינוי וסתירה או ע"פ שני עדים בין אשת כהן ובין אשת ישראל כאן מפני מעלת יוחסי' כהונה אסורה כ"כ במהרי"ק שורש ק"ס ועיין סקי"א ואפשר אם שבויה נשאת לישראל ונמצ' דהי' בתולה מותרת אחר מיתתו לכהן וצ"ע ובט"ז אוסר וכתב דיש לחוש שמא הערה בה השבאי:
**** ENGLISH ****
Forbidden to a kohen. If she is married to a kohen she must leave him even though she prostituted and says to a kosher man I was lain with and married a kohen — we hold she does not leave even among mostly disqualified men; here even if she says I am pure she is not trusted as a zona, for we say a woman checks and prostitutes. Even though it is only safek perhaps she prostituted and generally we hold a woman is not forbidden from her husband except through kinuy and setirah or two witnesses — whether kohen's wife or Israelite's wife — here because of elevation of kehunah lineage she is forbidden — so Maharik shoresh 165; see s.k. 51. It is possible if a captive married an Israelite and was found a virgin — permitted after his death to a kohen — uncertain; and Taz forbids lest we suspect the captor had initial contact with her.

**** ENGLISH ****
And even women. Even per the Rosh who holds a Kuti is not trusted because we do not find in the Talmud that he is fit for captive testimony and cannot learn from his being trusted to testify her husband died — one may say she proves she is trusted to say her husband died and not trusted here — as written in responsum Rosh. But women we teach alongside that they are trusted for a captive; and her mother is trusted — only her mother-in-law presumably disqualified as she is disqualified to testify her husband died — teaches not like there where her intent is to slander her husband; not so here where without her testimony she is forbidden to her husband — therefore no distinction between mother-in-law and other women; automatically she is included in permission of other women; not like Chelkat Mechokek who left uncertain what distinguishes gentile testimony from mother-in-law testimony — no difficulty; general rule: we have only what the Sages said; and on the Sages one cannot challenge what distinguishes gentile testimony from mother-in-law testimony — for otherwise difficulty on Rosh who wrote gentile testimony we do not find in Talmud fit for captive though slave minor and women trusted — we have only what Sages said.
