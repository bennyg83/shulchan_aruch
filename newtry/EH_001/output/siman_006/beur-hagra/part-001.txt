But one who was lain with, etc.; or, etc. — Yevamot 70:1 and 68:1: nine years, etc.; Egyptian and Edomite hold thus, as Ketubot 30 — no need; and as stated in the Gemara: whence? R' Yochanan, R' Yosei, R' Akiva: a daughter of a kohen, once she was lain with an unfit person, disqualifies her, etc.; we find kohenet, etc.; we find for terumah, for kehunah — whence, etc.? He does not exclude from the general rule except one who returns his divorcee; the general rule is: for every Torah prohibition she is forbidden to kehunah — whether a Torah prohibition or equal to all — only this is zona and this is chalalah, except niddah, unmarried women, and an animal. So too according to the eleven: only that they are not called zona per their view — but the law agrees. Practical difference: according to Tosafot and the book at the end, regarding all these — the child is not blemished; Chelkat Mechokek: since she became a zona, the child is blemished, even according to R' Yehoshua, as in the second version — unless it was stated: likewise, since her husband made her a zona. If so, the same for chalal according to us — if she was a zona. But according to the Rambam, even from forbidden relations the child is not a chalal, as stated in siman 5 seif 14; and see there — all the sugyot do not prove his words. Further difficulty: from what is stated there, 60: "these" — to exclude niddah, because it does not make her a zona; if so, for all forbidden relations it is likewise, even though he made her a zona. It seems he relied on Yerushalmi chapter 4, halakhah 13: R' Chiya in the name of R' Yochanan — one who returns his divorcee after she married is disqualified from kehunah; without this, she is not disqualified from kehunah, only disqualified from eating terumah. R' Chiya in the name of R' Yochanan: one who returns his divorcee after she married — her daughter is fit for kehunah. Why? "She is an abomination" — she is an abomination, but her children are not abominations. Hence they are not dependent on each other. Therefore the general rule: one who returns his divorcee is also included among all forbidden relations who disqualify, per the Yerushalmi above. But it is not forced: there it is different, for the Torah limited "she is an abomination" — even she herself is disqualified — disputing our Gemara, as below: what not to a widow, etc.; 69:1; and say, etc.; returns his divorcee; so too Rashi 76b, s.v. "she," etc.; Rashi on yevamah leshuk: fit for kehunah also for this reason — he is not a stranger to her initially; chalalim are disqualified only when he is a stranger to her initially, unlike forbidden relations where we do not require a stranger initially. See Rashi and Tosafot 68b, s.v. "gentile," etc. The general rule: from all posekim it appears that yevamah leshuk and one who returns his divorcee are not disqualified for kehunah — unlike the Rambam. Other forbidden relations disqualify; zona is not called zona; forbidden relations make her a zona and their children are disqualified. After examination, the Rambam's words in siman 7 seif 14 that the child is not a chalal are correct — for we say in Kiddushin chapter 4 explicitly: chalalah is only from forbidden relations for kehunah; "which chalalah" — born from disqualifications for kehunah — implies even from forbidden relations, no; only his words need correction. Certainly a daughter is blemished from forbidden relations for kehunah, as Yevamot; and so all forbidden relations, except the two — yevamah and divorcee — from the kal va-chomer from widow, as forbidden relations; gentile and slave, since forbidden relations disqualified her, as stated; Maharal 1:1: likewise, since her husband disqualified her, even though she does not become a chalalah but only disqualifies her daughter, as Rashi there, s.v. "her son," etc.; 44:1, s.v. "the child," etc.: only she is not a chalalah; blemished. Practical difference: a male is not a chalal; his wife is fit; his daughters are fit. This is what the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch mean: the child is not a chalal. The Gemara says the child is blemished — no difficulty between chalal and blemished; we do not derive by kal va-chomer that he should be a chalal — the Torah limited: no chalal, etc. Agrippas, who wrote there "king" — is different, for he must be fit on every side, choicest of your brothers. If we say what is written in Kiddushin: "there is no chalalah except from forbidden relations for kehunah" — meaning those written alongside her: divorcee, zona, chalalah — practical difference: dispute of the later books that zona is not from forbidden relations — children are not chalalim; Kiddushin: therefore a kohen who lies with his sister — zona, etc. — only his sister; but the plain Gemara does not seem so, for it says "except from forbidden relations for kehunah" — implying all forbidden relations for kehunah.
