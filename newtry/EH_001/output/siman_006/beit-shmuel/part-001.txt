**** ENGLISH ****

**** ENGLISH ****
And she was divorced. It seems from Rashi: even if a mere rumor went out that she was betrothed and afterward a rumor went out that she was divorced, she is permitted to everyone—for it is rumor and rumor, and the latter rumor contradicts the former; and so the Rambam chapter 5 law 20. The Raavad wrote: only when a rumor went out about both together; otherwise we do not concern ourselves for the latter rumor and she is forbidden to everyone on account of the mere rumor that she was betrothed to so-and-so that went out at first. It seems that is the Tur's view; therefore he wrote that the rumor went out that she was betrothed and divorced immediately—not like the Beit Yosef who wrote in Badei HaBayit what the Tur wrote "immediately" to include many. Also the Bach challenges him. But if a rumor of divorce went out after a rumor of betrothal, we pay no heed to the rumor that afterward went out that she was divorced, and she is permitted to this one about whom the rumor went out that he betrothed her—even if he is a kohen and she is forbidden after his death to another kohen. And if two rumors went out at once, she is forbidden to marry this one if he is a kohen, for to him it is a rumor before marriage; and so in the Derishah that she is forbidden to him.

**** ENGLISH ****
Or he divorced her. So the Tur; and it seems the meaning is: if an excuse emerged in one of these—whether in betrothal or divorce—there is no longer concern for the prohibition of kehunah; for if an excuse emerged in betrothal we say there is no betrothal from the outset, so there is no divorce; and so in the Prisha. However, it is difficult: whence does the Tur learn to be lenient here, since a rumor of divorce upholds the rumor of betrothal? And the mishnah teaches "provided there is no excuse at all"—one may say: that is regarding betrothal alone; if there is an excuse we are not concerned; and likewise regarding betrothal and divorce on condition we are not concerned. But from the Tur and Mechaber it seems regarding betrothal and divorce he nullifies the excuse of betrothal and divorce—and it is difficult whence he learns this. And what Choshen Mishpat challenged from what is written in seif 1 "if she was divorced, even on account of rumor she is forbidden to a kohen"—one may say: there the reason is lest someone heard of the get and did not hear of the betrothal, as written in responsum Rashba §350—unlike here where the rumors went out at once. Also one may say: there we saw the get, and here there is only rumor.

To resolve the sugya where it is clear there that one is lashed for her—see Bach what he wrote; his words are very forced. It seems he wrote properly: all agree she becomes a zona, only he is not lashed. To resolve the sugya one may say: there in that sugya he learned the prohibition of kehunah from terumah by kal va-chomer, and they challenged "do we warn from logical inference?" and answered it is mere verbal revelation and one is lashed; and in this matter Abaye and Rava dispute in chapter Four Deaths and chapter HaNisrafim—to Abaye one is lashed for verbal revelation and to Rava one is not lashed; and the halakhah follows Rava against Abaye everywhere except yaaleh veyavo, kadesh, gamar; therefore these poskim hold he is not lashed. In Makkot 17 it states: even according to the one who says punishments from logical inference—nevertheless we do not warn from logical inference; hence for Rava even for verbal revelation we do not warn. The sugya Yevamot there must be according to Abaye who holds for verbal revelation one is punished because it is as though written explicitly; hence one need not distinguish between punishment and warning. At first glance it is astonishing regarding the Rambam who holds he is lashed.

And there is one who says. That is the Tur's view; and the Beit Yosef wrote the Gemara girsa "it is not necessary" when she goes to them—hence if we do not know whether she goes, it helps. The Rambam girsa "perhaps he goes"—and his words are very astonishing in my eyes, both in the Rambam's explanation and the Tur's explanation. Also the girsa "it is not necessary" is an error and already erased by Maharshal in Kuntres HaSfeikos. Rather the Rambam and Tur girsa "perhaps they go." However, according to the resolution they elevated in lineages: we require by law two majorities without decree. The Rambam holds: if the lying was in the city and there is concern perhaps she went to him—then there are no longer two majorities; therefore we require the lying to be on the open road. The Tur: even though he too holds for the conclusion we require two majorities, nevertheless in Eretz Yisrael they are not so stringent unless it is known she went to him; and if it is known who the layer is from the city or from the caravan, then two majorities do not help—for it is known who he is; only when it is unknown who he is do two majorities help—so Rashba; and he does not disagree with the Tur except that even according to the Tur nevertheless where we require two majorities they do not help when it is known who he is—not like the Beit Yosef's understanding that Rashba disagrees with the Tur. It seems from all poskim: when it was in this place and she conceived and in this place there are two majorities, we are not concerned perhaps she committed harlotry in another place where there are not two majorities; rather we say here found here was the harlotry—for we rule regarding prohibition we do not establish presumption from place to place, as written beginning of Niddah—the dispute of Chizkiyah and R. Yochanan regarding tumah; and so ruled in Orchot Chaim and brought in Hagahot Rav Rama: when a milking vessel is found among meat vessels we do not establish presumption from place to place and we are not concerned perhaps it was rinsed among the vessels—even though we do not derive prohibition from tumah, nevertheless in such a case where there is an opinion we derive prohibition from tumah. See Yoreh De'ah siman 105. Even though here it is a traveling matter—for she always goes—nevertheless we are not concerned perhaps she committed harlotry elsewhere. It seems in this dispute one should be concerned according to the Rambam even when she says "to a fit man I was lain with" we require two majorities; and when she does not speak clearly then even bedi'eved we require two majorities; however if there are two majorities she is permitted even lekhatchilah—for most poskim hold thus and for the Maggid one may say the Rambam too holds thus; and when she speaks clearly and married, even with a majority of unfit she need not leave.
