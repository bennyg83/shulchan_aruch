Seif 1: A kohen is forbidden by Torah from divorcee, etc. NB I saw to mention here one matter — though we need not for halachah, nevertheless we too must fulfill Chazal's "know what to answer" — therefore I said to raise here in the book: certainly there is no distinction whether she was divorced now or was already divorced and married and became widowed — even though now she is a widow, still forbidden to kohen since she was forbidden once; what happened afterward does not help. The matter is plain to everyone; but to refute erring ones a clear proof from Talmud Yevamot 72b: from what Shmuel challenges from mishnah "she did not wait after her husband three months" etc. — "how do you find this?" etc. — "rather not through prostitution"; and what does he challenge? Say he divorced her and another betrothed her within three months and she became widowed from erusin and married this kohen within three months and bore — then not chalalah and you find both are mourners — therefore must prove even if widowed in between forbidden to kohen. Further proof from what Chazal expounded verse "and a woman divorced from her husband" — even if divorced only "from her husband" such as "you are not permitted to any man" — also forbidden to R' Eliezer who expounds it; why not expound per "from her husband" now implying if divorced from husband now forbidden but if widow from husband now though previously divorced permitted — must prove this is not so. See also Yevamot 56b mishnah widow to High Priest, divorcee and chalutzah to common kohen etc. became widow or divorced from nissuin may not eat, from erusin may eat; see Rashi there — if widow after nissuin permitted to kohen why need teach widow from erusin may eat terumah — even to kohen permitted since now widowed; perforce either because erusin now did not make chalalah or because first divorce now widowed — must prove not so; silence mouth of liars. Can reject: say nissuin that permitted uproot divorce but nissuin of prohibition do not uproot divorce; but first proof strong and I have more proofs but no leisure; raised only per "know what to answer." See Yevamot 20a mishnah forbidden mitzvah and forbidden sanctity performs chalitzah not yibbum — explains forbidden sanctity such as widow to High Priest etc.; why not yibbum — behold now widowed; see Rashi there — and explicit after death also forbidden; and logic to distinguish forbidden nissuin from permitted nissuin not reasonable — let matter rest — examine.
