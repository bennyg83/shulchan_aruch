<b>חומרא בעלמא פסולה לכהונה כו' </b> בב"י סימן ק"ן (צ"ל י"ג) לקמן מביא זה בשם תשובת רשב"א סימן תק"ן וכתב וז"ל וכ"ש זו שיצא עליה קול והצרכת אותה גט יאמרו קמו רבנן בקידושין וקידושין מעליא הוי כו' ואע"ג דלרווחא דמלתא הוציאה בגט נמצאת אתה מצריכו כרוז ואפי' דנכריז איכא מאן דשמע בקידושין ולא שמע בהכרזה וכההיא דר"פ החולץ עכ"ל משמע מזה דאפי' דיעבד אם נשא כהן יוציא דהא בר"פ אשה שהלך אמרינן ג"כ לחשש זה דאיכא דשמע בחליצה ולא שמע בהכרזה כו' ואמרינן שם דמ"ה לא תנשא לעולם ואין לך דיעבד גדול מזה שהרי אין לה תקנה וכמ"ש הר"ן מביאו (ב"י סי' ו') צ"ל קנ"ו לענין אחר וכן משמע לשון רמ"א דנקט פסולה לכהונה משמע אפי' דיעבד אלא שמו"ח ז"ל כתב היכא שנתגרשה מחמת קול בעלמא ואין הגט אלא מחמת חומרא דאם עבר ונשא אין מוציאין ממנו ואין שום ראיה לקולא זו ואין להקל בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>As a mere stringency she is disqualified for kehunah, etc.</b> Beit Yosef in siman 150, which should read siman 13 below, brings this in the name of Responsa Rashba, siman 550, and writes as follows: all the more so this woman, about whom a rumor emerged and whom you required to receive a get, for people will say the Sages upheld the kiddushin and that it was proper kiddushin, etc. Even though he gave her a get only as a precaution, you would require him to make a proclamation, and even if we proclaim it, there may be one who heard about the kiddushin but did not hear the proclamation, like that case at the beginning of chapter HaCholetz. End quote. It follows from this that even after the fact, if a kohen married her he must divorce her. For at the beginning of chapter HaIshah SheHalach we also say this concern, that there may be one who heard of the chalitzah and did not hear the proclamation, etc., and there we say that because of this she may never marry; there is no greater after-the-fact case than this, for she has no remedy. So wrote Ran, brought by Beit Yosef, siman 6; regarding another matter it should read siman 156. So too the language of Rama implies, for he wrote that she is disqualified for kehunah, which implies even after the fact. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that where she was divorced only because of a mere rumor and the get was only because of stringency, if he transgressed and married her, we do not remove her from him. There is no proof for this leniency, and one should not be lenient in this.

And if after he gave her a get, etc. — when miyun cancels the get: he explains that he revealed upon her that she is a minor and the get is void; and the Gemara says specifically if the first one who divorced her took her back and she exercised miyun — this miyun helps to cancel the get; but if she married another after the get and he died — she is permitted to the first and there is no [issue of] returning his divorcee. But if after the get she married another and exercised miyun against him — this miyun does not help to cancel the get and she remains a divorcee and forbidden to return to the first, as the law of returning his divorcee. The reason: specifically his miyun helps, but not another's miyun; for if you say another's miyun helps — we are concerned that the first sold her with hints and winks, lest he hint to her husband while she is still under the second that she should exercise miyun and return to him, since he loves her though he divorced her against his will; not so with his miyun, for he sees she hates him and will not come to hint that she return to him after marrying another. And the Rambam wrote that there is also this distinction regarding kehunah: if she exercised miyun against the first — the get is void and she is permitted to a kohen; and another's miyun does not help and she remains a divorcee and forbidden to a kohen. The Tur challenged him: granted regarding returning to the first there is reason for the distinction as we mentioned — lest the first come to entice her to leave the second, therefore another's miyun does not help; but regarding permission to a kohen there is no distinction between another's miyun and his miyun. And Beit Yosef wrote the Tur's words are reasonable; nevertheless one can say they did not give their words to measures to say for this matter another's miyun cancels the get and for this matter it does not. Here in Shulchan Aruch he brought the Tur's view in the name of "some say" and did not bring the Rambam's view at all, even though the Rambam's words are dear to him everywhere; and though I am not fit to decide between two great mountains — in my humble opinion the essential view is like the Rambam, for from the Talmud she is forbidden to a kohen on account of a decree lest they see this woman is a divorcee from our forbidding her to the first husband on account of returning his divorcee; and now she married a kohen they will say a divorcee is permitted to a kohen. And clear proof for this from what we say in the beginning of chapter HaIsha: a doubt whether she needs chalitzah — she shall neither perform chalitzah nor marry another; and we say there the reason is lest it afterward become clear through witnesses that the chalitzah was in vain and she would be permitted to a kohen, and the public will err to say a chalutzah is permitted to a kohen; and even if they made a proclamation that the chalitzah was in vain — nevertheless lest some people not hear the proclamation and err to return the chalutzah to a kohen. Behold the matter is a fortiori: there, where according to truth it became clear through witnesses that she chalitzah in vain, they are concerned to permit her to a kohen — all the more so here where in truth the divorce is effective, for we forbid her to the first and they think it is a complete divorce even though she is a minor — divorce helps for her, as they were truly concerned when she was divorced in her minority from the first that they are disqualified from kehunah even though all see she is a minor; nevertheless they were concerned for kehunah. And behold now after miyun from the other they will also say thus — that divorce was not uprooted from her. Proof: from our forbidding her to the other husband; and if she marries a kohen they will say a divorcee is permitted to a kohen, for they do not know to distinguish the reason between them; therefore permission to a kohen is only with the first one's miyun after the get — then she may return to the first and they will know; but where divorce was uprooted from her there — in my humble opinion.

And if they do not call [giving a get "writing"], etc. — for thus they say in the Gemara: Rav sent to Shmuel — a rumor went out that so-and-so the kohen wrote a get for his wife and she sits under him — what is the law? He sent to him: she must leave; and the matter needs investigation. Rashi explained: she must leave on account of the rumor, but the matter needs investigation before we remove her — what is stronger: that we do not nullify a rumor, or not? But the Nehardeans in Shmuel's locale were, and in Nehardea they do not nullify a rumor except if they call giving "writing," etc. And the Rambam wrote here stam "she must leave" and did not distinguish whether they call, etc. Beit Yosef challenged this and wondered at Maggid Mishneh for not noticing; and Maharshal answered as he answered and brought in the name of a sage that the Rambam did not explain Shmuel's words who said "she must leave," etc. per Rashi's explanation — for if so it should say the opposite: "the matter needs investigation and she must leave," for investigation is needed before removal per Rashi. Or the explanation is like in chapter HaTekabel (Gittin 66a) where Shmuel said "she must leave and the matter needs study" — meaning she must leave out of doubt because study is needed whether it is valid or not; so too here he says she must leave out of doubt because investigation is needed whether they call giving, etc.; and it is impossible to stand on this. And likewise per what seemed to him that the challenge is whether investigation is needed whether the custom is to nullify the rumor or not — also here he would say "she must leave" since it is impossible to stand on its clarity what the custom is. And he challenged: but the Nehardeans in Shmuel's locale — there it is clear they nullify; therefore they are correct — end quote. In my humble opinion they are not correct; for what he brought proof from the language "she must leave and the matter needs study" — he overlooked that this language appears three times — namely in chapter HaMegaresh (Gittin 89a): they sent from the house of Rav to Shmuel — teach us, our teacher: a rumor went out about her from the first and one came and betrothed her — what is the law? He sent to them: she must leave; and they established the matter on its basis — meaning they should investigate after the rumor, etc.; and afterward they said they did not find the matter on its basis — what is the law? Rav Huna said: the first divorces and the second marries, etc. Behold before you this "she must leave" does not mean she must leave in any case out of doubt, for they conclude there that if they did not find the matter's basis she is permitted to the second; therefore "she must leave" only means first the matter must be clarified whether the second must divorce or she is permitted to him. And if so this "she must leave" is not "she must leave in any case" but possibly she must leave; and likewise here exactly as I say and as Rashi explained, and exactly like the case in chapter HaTekabel where he said "she must leave and the matter needs study" — explained there explicitly that he means his opinion that she will leave, but before she leaves study is needed whether she leaves or not; and Rashi explained explicitly that the matter is not clear in my opinion and I must learn from the great ones — end quote. And further: since he said "needs investigation" — implies possibly through investigation; and further: what he explained per the challenger's view — since there is doubt in the custom whether they nullify — she must leave out of doubt; and this is certainly not so, for certainly we follow leniency in doubt of rumor, for rumor is only rabbinic stringency and prohibition exists only where they do not nullify clearly. And to answer the Rambam's view — in my humble opinion this word "investigation" (how) (should read thus) means: in chapter HaMenich there is also distinction between calling [giving] a barrel or jugs regarding whether it depends on the majority of the locale's people or specifically all of them; and here it was not clarified whether we follow the locale's majority — therefore he did not bring this investigation at all and we follow stringency that investigation does not help at all (emendation notes: Beit Shmuel s.k. 8 on "rumor so-and-so kohen" — this "kohen" is superfluous — end quote). In my humble opinion: possibly if the rumor went out on an Israelite we are concerned afterward for a kohen, lest they say the Israelite also gave the get and afterward married her; and like the Rambam who does not distinguish between calling and writing for giving — if so they will say a kohen married a divorcee; but for a kohen there is no concern for a second kohen, since she was certainly under the first he did not give her the get after the writing, for the rumor went out only on the writing and they do not call writing "giving"; and also source of this law is from Gemara end of chapter HaZorek, and there it deals explicitly from here; and possibly for an Israelite the law is not so — even where they do not call writing "giving" she is forbidden afterward to a kohen on account of what we wrote, for with a kohen there are two factors for stringency: behold she was under the first and also they do not call writing "giving"; but with an Israelite there is only one factor — they do not call writing "giving" — we are concerned like the Rambam who does not distinguish between calling, etc. And if we wanted to say it is superfluous and erroneous — we should rather say the Rambam erred in writing "kohen"; and there was never any challenge the posekim raised on him why he does not distinguish between calling and writing for giving — or not; for it is possible to establish that it deals with an Israelite and there is no distinction mentioned — as I wrote end of words.
