**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he inherits her offspring.</b> But by virtue of his wife's loan he does not acquire, even though we hold that a shtar chatzi zachar is a debt, because it is a loan from before the marriage. See Chelkat Mechokek, siman 285, where it is explained that a shtar chatzi zachar is a complete debt; therefore the father cannot stipulate anything after he has already obligated himself in this debt. Even though he can stipulate and arrange among his heirs whatever he wishes, nevertheless with regard to a shtar chatzi zachar he cannot stipulate anything. See Responsa Maharshal, Responsa Rama, and Responsa Maharam of Lublin. If he wrote a shtar chatzi zachar for one daughter but did not write one for the other daughters, and he left no son but only daughters, the daughters divide equally, and this daughter does not take precedence for the amount of the shtar chatzi zachar, because we say his intent in giving her a shtar chatzi zachar was only against male sons, not against daughters. If that daughter died and left offspring, and afterward that offspring died, her husband inherits nothing, for there is no shtar chatzi zachar here, only inheritance; and we hold that a son does not inherit his mother in order to transmit to his brothers from the father. So too here, the offspring does not inherit his mother in order to transmit to his father. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 96. <b>He inherits that land.</b> This implies that he does not inherit the produce, because it is considered ra'ui, since the legator could sell it. See Chelkat Mechokek, siman 256, unlike Chelkat Mechokek here.
