**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) <b>The document.</b> Specifically regarding his wife, he does not take what is only due to come later; but his offspring he inherits, even though it had not yet come into the offspring's hand. Nevertheless, the document belonged to his offspring, and he inherits the document. However, it is difficult: how is this different from a dowry, where we hold that even if he gave a debt document and his daughter died, he need not pay, because of the assessment that his intent was that his daughter benefit, as written in siman 53? Why do we not say such an assessment here as well? Chelkat Mechokek wrote that therefore it is good to write: "If my daughter dies without surviving offspring after my death," and then the offspring must be alive after the father's death. If he wrote a half-male-share document for one daughter and did not write for the other daughters, and he left no son, only daughters, the daughters divide equally, and this daughter does not take the amount of the half-male-share document first, for we say that his intent in giving this one a half-male-share document was only where there would be sons.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ו) <b>From her.</b> He does not inherit the produce, because it is considered property due to come later, for the one who bequeaths could sell it. Beit Shmuel. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that he inherits even the produce, since he was in possession of the body of the land; see there. Reuven married off his daughter while his father Yaakov was alive and gave his son-in-law a half-male-share document according to custom. Afterward Yaakov died, and the son-in-law also claims a half-male-share from Yaakov's inheritance, saying that it is considered due to come later with respect to his father-in-law, and he wrote to me both what is in possession and what is due. The law is with him. Responsa Maharshal, siman 49. See Responsa Rama, siman 3, and Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 14. Reuven gave a gift to his daughter from now, so long as he would not have a son from a certain wife. His daughter died during her husband's lifetime, and afterward Reuven or his wife died without a son. This is called in possession, and the husband inherits.
