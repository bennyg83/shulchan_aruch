{Rama: Some say a claim of open opening, etc.} See Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 6 who challenged why Rama did not emend here that nowadays he is not believed to say "open opening I found," since there is Rabbenu Gershom's ban against forced divorce, as Rama wrote below siman 178 that he is not believed to say he believes the witness lest his eye turned to another; later rabbis there wrote likewise one saying he saw she committed adultery is not believed, as Mordechai in Kiddushin, etc. Do not say he is believed on open opening because there is a presumption a person does not trouble for a feast and lose — first, that presumption helps only for rabbinic ketubah not to remove a woman from her husband; further Rashi explained if he hated her he would divorce her, and after Rabbenu Gershom's enactment this presumption is hidden. He wrote to answer: "his eye turned to another" applies only where per his words she loses ketubah, such as saying he saw willing adultery — then she has no ketubah; but where he claims open opening, it could be she was raped under him and then she has ketubah and he must divorce and pay — we are not concerned, etc. Or perhaps Rama relied on what he wrote below and concludes whoever finds a place to answer Rama's words otherwise is a great sage and reward from Heaven. See there. I do not understand the first answer that "his eye turned" applies only where she loses ketubah — for below siman 178 seif 9 Rama on Shulchan Aruch there "did not warn her and one witness came, etc., he divorces and gives ketubah" — Rama wrote nowadays not believed, etc., or believes the witness lest his eye, etc. — clearly even where he must give ketubah we say his eye turned. And what he wrote such as saying he saw willing adultery then she has no ketubah — also not understood, for truly the law even saying he saw his wife commit adultery he must give ketubah as Shulchan Aruch siman 115 seif 7, only he can impose oath (and some dispute Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel there); since we suspect he lies because his eye turned, certainly she swears and he must pay ketubah. Therefore this answer fails and the second is necessary — Rama relied on below, and thus a novel law that nowadays he is not believed to claim open opening to forbid her to him. Even admitting this law, one may say specifically for a kohen's wife; but for an Israelite's wife where the father accepted betrothal under three years, even nowadays he is believed per Rama — one may say in the next seif if she justifies herself saying "I was raped" she is believed; thus we find he is not believed "his eye turned" only when saying he saw adultery or believes or believes the witness — where per his words she is certainly forbidden to him we suspect he lies wanting forced divorce; not here where even per his words she is not certainly forbidden, lest she claim "I was raped" and he cannot force divorce — we do not say his eye turned lest he lie on doubt for benefit. Nevertheless on the main difficulty it seems only per later rabbis he brought that even saying he saw adultery he is not believed — but for Rama himself, he in siman 178 seif 9 gloss did not mention the husband saying he saw adultery, only believing or believing the witness; explained in Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 11 (brought siman 178 there) that Rama's view when saying she committed adultery all agree she is forbidden; what Hagahot Maimoniyot in Rosh and R' Yehuda's name that one saying his wife committed adultery she is not forbidden — does not mean he saw with his eyes but knows through some excuse; also responsum Mahari ben Lev part 3 siman 226 explains Hagahot Maimoniyot thus. See there. And one claim of open opening — they said in the Gemara it is like two witnesses, like saying he saw with his eyes she committed adultery — Rama also agrees he is believed, therefore Rama did not emend here at all; uncertain in all this.

**** ENGLISH ****
{Rama: Some say this that she becomes forbidden.} Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel. See Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 21: you challenged that siman 178 seif 3 gloss implies she is not believed to say "I was raped" only because of miggo, but siman 68 seif 7 gloss Rama wrote some say if she said "I was raped" she is believed and there is no miggo. Know the lenient view's reason is establishing her on presumption of fitness; but here seclusion lost presumption of fitness since she did transgress. I found Perishah on Kuntres Acharon Kesubos siman 30 also wrote thus, and I say this is necessary from Hagahot Maimoniyot brought Beit Shmuel s.k. 15: even for a kohen's wife if she said raped before betrothal she is permitted — seemingly wondrous, for if she speaks of before betrothal why say "I was raped" — while still unmarried no difference between rape and consent; or the main reason is presumption of fitness, and when they admit intercourse with consent she lost her presumption and is not believed at all. Therefore I wonder at Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 10 who wrote likewise for a kohen's wife saying raped before betrothal, etc., and so the Maggid — per the lenient view specifically when she says "I was raped" he brings Hagahot Maimoniyot. Nevertheless for practical law Chelkat Mechokeh's words can be upheld, etc. See there and siman 14 where Gaon Or Chadash also challenged this contradiction; his grandfather Gaon Pnei Aharon part 3 (there s.k. following) noticed this and answered no contradiction as said above. See also responsum Brit Avraham siman 78 letter 2, siman 82 letter 2, siman 84.

"I was raped" — she is believed. Even HaEzer ch. 1 Kesubos siman 18; Maggid Mishneh ch. 18 Laws of Forbidden Relations; Beit Yosef here; Beit Shmuel s.k. 15; Perishah on Kuntres Acharon siman 23. See Maggid Mishneh there halakhah 10 who brought Yerushalmi explicitly like the forbidders even saying plainly, not like Rabbeinu Yonah and supporters. Yerushalmi also brought responsum Maharam Alashkar siman 94 supporting forbidders. See there at length also end of siman 77. Nevertheless Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 14 on a case where her husband went far away about a year and a half, she gave birth and claims raped by a wicked disqualified man while her husband abandoned her in a distant house with only a small daughter — is she permitted to her Israelite husband. He elaborated on Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh and brought Yerushalmi and wrote no proof from Yerushalmi, for Yerushalmi per its view that rape has voice overriding even certainty and even plain claim does not help; but our Talmud disputes Yerushalmi, for explicitly an Israelite's wife is permitted — certainty maybe not under him maybe rape — if certainty permits without plain claim, permitted even on one doubt (so too responsum Brit Avraham siman 78 letter 3). He further argued Rambam also holds thus, not like Maggid Mishneh, and elaborated concluding if her husband believes her and his heart does not rebuke him as she is presumed modest before him, he may rely on Rabbeinu Yonah and she is permitted. At the end he wrote Knesset Yechezkel brought in Maharam Alashkar's name that "I was raped" — she is believed means saying plainly "I was raped" without naming the adulterer, or even naming him if not present; but if present and contradicts her even against the husband as plain certainty, since the adulterer contradicts with plainness, considered plain against the husband too. He wrote to doubt this, deducing from Gemara R' Chiya: one saying open opening is believed to forbid her — why conclude specifically against himself as plain and plain, but not believed to forbid priests if he died before divorcing her, for regarding other kohanim it is plain and maybe. Thus not like Maharam Alashkar; in any case we need not send after the adulterer since not present we accept the woman's words. He concludes nevertheless for stringency do not rely on my view alone unless two great authorities of the generation agree. See there siman 15: what place Maharam Alashkar has is when that person contradicts saying not by rape but willingly (as the questioning Gaon thought — he answered far to say so, for then Knesset Yechezkel would not omit this). See Maharam Alashkar siman 94 — the case where that person completely contradicted saying never came upon her by rape or consent; per what is explained there Maharam Alashkar did not write this view definitively only "we can say," and he forbade the woman there for other reasons. But one completely contradicting he never had intercourse with her — she is permitted since the adulterer does not contradict her permission whose main permission is rape — we split her statement, etc. Further if the person contradicted before she was permitted, perhaps Maharam Alashkar's words apply; but if already permitted, the adulterer's contradiction does not help even if he says willing intercourse, like captives once permitted, etc. Maharam Alashkar's words are not so clear. See there siman 17 further on this; siman 16: regarding this woman claiming rape, I already informed you, what more shall I add; if the husband's heart does not rebuke and he trusts her righteousness saying she surely does not lie — this is as saying plain to me; good also to investigate neighbors if she is always presumed modest and no licentiousness heard — he may permit her. See siman 16: I am very doubtful regarding a woman saying "I was raped" — Rabbeinu Yonah permits and Rambam forbids as explained Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 18; I am doubtful even per Rambam she is not believed, but perhaps one witness testifying she was raped is believed to permit her to her husband because one witness is believed regarding prohibitions — specifically regarding the woman's credibility Rambam was stringent; even in Sotah where husband warned and she secluded and one witness testifies she was not defiled — in this contradiction where he peered through a hole and saw she did not commit adultery — certainly not believed to permit; proof from Ulla in Sotah 31 — captive differs since there are grounds for matter he warned and secluded. See there siman 16, 18, 19. The main Noda B'Yehuda words that plainly her heart is between her teeth and husband overseas and she says raped equals open opening law — per Rabbeinu Yonah and supporters permitted when presumption of fitness not lost — see responsum Knesset Yechezkel siman 78: his view is not that any woman whose husband was overseas and she bore illegitimate child saying raped is believed per Rosh and supporters; I saw no posek stated this explicitly; perhaps they relied on open opening where Israelite's wife father accepted betrothal under three years. Truly one may say "I was raped" for two reasons: rape has voice; and from embarrassment she says raped only regarding open opening where father accepted betrothal under three years with miggo she could say seduced as minor without voice or embarrassment. Further distinguish open opening claim — prohibition is not established except per her husband and she immediately says raped — therefore believed, unlike illegitimate child where prohibition was established before she said raped. I remember as a youth in Brisk a doctor came from Italy several years; his wife came pregnant with illegitimate child claiming raped on the road — they did not listen and forbade her to him, and I think he was Israelite, etc. Perishah on Kuntres Acharon Kesubos siman 31 disputes him, writing from these words there is room to permit a woman whose husband went overseas, tarried more than twelve months, she conceived and claims raped on the road — she is believed and permitted to her Israelite husband since there is plain and presumption of righteousness better than majority of consent as Tosafot taught majority consent is not a full majority; since I saw Knesset Yechezkel's case in Brisk where sages of the generation forbade her, I had to write it is not a general rule; and even though we do not answer lions, one may say in that case even per her claim of rape she transgressed by secluding with a warrior in an inn forbidden even with many kosher men until a guard sits — thus where she transgressed seclusion improperly presumption is greatly impaired and no rape claim; not so where she claims rape in a matter that can be established afterward — seems we should not forbid her to her Israelite husband; I say this only to learn, and more Rama (brought above seif 4 s.k. 14) would permit if she claims privately her husband came by name, even for forbidders. Also responsum A.A.Z. part 8 ch. 3 brings also illegitimate pregnant saying raped equals open opening questioned there about a woman whose husband is in another country, she conceived and bore illegitimate child; beit din investigated and she answered raped in sleep on the road from road weariness sleep seized her and she did not sense a man had intercourse; afterward her husband came asking to permit dwelling with her as he believes her rape claim and testifies he saw nothing unseemly since the wedding — he answered at length clarifying two doubts: whether sleep-rape is rape (see above siman 11 Ba'er Hetev s.k. 18); and whether a woman is believed saying raped where she has no miggo. Regarding first doubt — seems if intercourse in sleep she is not forbidden to husband, for we learn ch. 2 Keritot all forbidden relations whether awake or asleep — asleep is exempt; Tur siman 20 and Rama there in Darkhei Moshe and glosses Shulchan Aruch: therefore Tur wrote, etc., relevance nowadays in which intercourse a woman becomes forbidden — thus if she is asleep she is not forbidden. Though one may say Tur is not universal as Beit Shmuel there regarding intercourse with dead limb, and perhaps here too specifically Torah rape but sleep is considered inadvertent not rape; main proof from Darkhei Moshe from Nekudot Yosef is not necessary — Tosafot in Yerushalmi name and Beit Shmuel siman 11 s.k. 3: he is intentional and she inadvertent — she is permitted to husband; so too Talmud Sotah 25, plainly in sleep one may say considered rape, etc. Regarding second doubt if believed saying raped in sleep since no miggo — all know pregnant and husband distant — like witnesses she had intercourse — seems also believed per Rosh ch. 1 Kesubos Tur siman 68, etc.; even Mordechai ch. 2 Kesubos and Shulchan Aruch siman 178 gloss from Mordechai — those Geonim Mordechai brought hold like forbidders rejecting Rabbeinu Yonah; but ultimately Mordechai wrote to uphold Rabbeinu Yonah and permitted regarding open opening even on one doubt even without miggo — therefore wonder at Rama (see previous s.k.) and he elaborated concluding in the questioned case not only beit din need not remove her but even on the husband no obligation to remove since she says plainly raped in sleep by another and husband believes her — husband cleared of sin and may maintain her if sages agree.
