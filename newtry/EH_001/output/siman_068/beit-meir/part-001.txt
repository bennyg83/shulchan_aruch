And anyone who is secluded, etc. Beit Shmuel wrote: if she claims, "I was a virgin," etc., it requires study whether she is believed through a migo that he had relations with her, or whether this is like a migo used to extract money. I do not understand the doubt, for even without this she has a migo, as he brings in s.k. 22 in the name of the Rosh and Ran. Both of them, and likewise Tosafot on Ketubot 9, s.v. "lo de-ka ta'an," wrote that if we do not say a migo to extract, the sugya is also settled well. From all of them we learn that this is considered extracting, and we do not say that because the document is in her hand it is not called a migo to extract, as the Shach maintained in Choshen Mishpat siman 82, in Darkei Moshe letter 15, in his question. If so, how can this be made dependent on the dispute whether we say a migo to extract? Perhaps Beit Shmuel was uncertain whether we should say that she does not willingly claim that he had relations with her during erusin; but his wording does not imply that doubt. Their words also prove against the Hagahot Mordekhai in Bava Metzia cited by the Shach there, who wrote that where there is a chazakah together with the migo, we do say a migo to extract. For if so, they have answered nothing, unless one presses to say that here, as against him as well, there is the chazakah that a person does not trouble himself for a wedding feast and lose it, and therefore her chazakah of virginity does not help her. Yet even without this, the basis for that position requires study: from the sugya of "from after you betrothed me" it appears only as the Rash explains, that where there is a definite claim against an uncertain claim we say a migo to extract, or a definite claim and uncertain claim joined with a chazakah. But a migo to extract together with a chazakah where both claims are definite is not mentioned and is not implied by the sugya, which says "or alternatively"; see there. In my humble opinion, in Beit Shmuel's case, even according to the opinion that says a migo can extract money, she is not believed, because of the reason Tosafot wrote there: it is a migo against a chazakah. They needed to add that perhaps the chazakah here is stronger only so that one should not resolve the general question from here. But since the question is not resolved, even though in Choshen Mishpat siman 78 we rule leniently for the defendant that he is believed, here the husband is the defendant, for we hold that a document is not considered as already collected. All agree that we do not say a migo against the chazakah that a man does not trouble himself for a wedding feast and lose it. See seif 9. What the Shach pressed himself to distinguish in the cited passage, that in the case of sitrai all agree that with a strong document we say a migo to extract because the money did not come into his hand through the ketubah, is forced; for what difference should that make? Rather, it appears to me that in the case of sitrai the document assists him and shows his claim to be true, for if this payment had been made on this document, what is your document doing in my hand? By contrast, in a claim of mistaken sale, the document came into his hand by mistake; what could he have done about that? In such a case the holder of the document has no advantage. See below the words of Ramban and Ran. Some of these points are included in the Shach's words, but in my view he wrote at too much length.
