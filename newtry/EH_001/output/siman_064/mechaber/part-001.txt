**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Time of marriage of a virgin and her rejoicing, and a widow, and if he does not wish to make a feast. It contains 6 seifim.</b>

One who marries a virgin must rejoice with her seven days — he should not do work and should not trade in the market but eats and drinks and rejoices with her — whether he is a bachelor or a widower. And a groom is forbidden in doing work and forbidden to go out alone in the market (Ran first chapter of Kesubos and Pirkei R' Eliezer).

**** ENGLISH ****
Some say one should not marry women neither on erev Shabbat nor on Sunday — a decree lest he come to desecrate Shabbat in preparing the meal; and some permit and so is the widespread custom to marry women on erev Shabbat — provided he toils at the wedding feast three days before the marriage. And in a place where beit din sits only on Monday and Thursday — a virgin marries on Wednesday so that if he has a claim of virginity he rises early to beit din. And the custom of sages is that one who marries a non-virgin marries her on Thursday so that he rejoices with her three days — Thursday, erev Shabbat, and Shabbat — and goes out to his work on Sunday.

{Rama: And the custom is not to marry women except at the beginning of the month while the moon is full (Ran end of chapter Four Deaths; and see Yoreh Deah siman 179).}
