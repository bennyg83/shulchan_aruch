**** ENGLISH ****
<b>How long do they give a betrothed woman. It contains 4 seifim.</b>

One who betroths a woman and several years pass and she demands marriage — if she is a na'arah, they give her twelve months from the day of the demand to support herself and prepare what she needs, and afterward she marries.

If she demanded after she matured, they give her twelve months from the day of maturity. And likewise if he betrothed her on the day of maturity, they give her twelve months from the day of betrothal, which is the day of maturity.

If he betrothed her after she matured, if twelve months passed in her maturity and afterward she was betrothed — they do not give her more than thirty days from the day of the demand.

And likewise one who betroths a non-virgin — they give her thirty days from the day of the demand.

**** ENGLISH ****
Just as they give time to a woman when the husband demands that she support herself, so they give time to a man to support himself when the woman demands him. And how much time do they give him? As they give her — if twelve months, twelve months; and if thirty days, thirty days.

**** ENGLISH ****
When the time they gave the man arrives and he did not marry her, he is obligated in her sustenance even though he did not enter the chuppah.

If the delay was due to duress — such as he or she fell ill, or she became a niddah, or the time arrived on Shabbat when he cannot enter — he is not obligated to sustain her.

{Rama: And specifically when the illness occurred during the allotted time; but if the time arrived and he did not marry her, and afterward she fell ill — he is obligated in her sustenance even though now it is duress (Mordechai beginning of Ketubot and chapter The One Who Performs Chalitzah).}

**** ENGLISH ****
One who betroths his minor daughter and the husband demands marriage — whether she or her father can delay that she not marry until she grows and becomes a na'arah.

If he wishes to enter her, he may enter; but it is not proper to do so.

{Rama: And if she and her husband agree to enter and the father died — the relatives cannot protest, even if she is sickly and he is rushing to enter in order to inherit her (Mordechai chapter The Man Who Betroths).}
