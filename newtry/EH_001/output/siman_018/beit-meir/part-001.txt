**** ENGLISH ****
Kiddushin take effect for chiyvei lavin and aseh. In the Tur it states: therefore one of those betrothed — whether a widow to a High Priest, or a divorcee and chalutzah to a common kohen, etc. — needs a get; and even one who betroths a yevamah leshuk likewise, as will be clarified with God's help. Behold he specified widow to High Priest and divorcee to common kohen to say: even though the negative command "they shall not take" is stated — Rava holds in Temurah: whatever the Torah said "you shall not do" — if one did it, it does not help. The Gemara per Rambam: kiddushin take effect, since it is written "he shall not take" and "he shall not profane" — he makes profaned ones and not mamzerim; learn kiddushin take effect in them. In yevamah leshuk we rule like Shmuel, who said in our poverty she needs a get. In Yevamot 49b: Abaye — a guardian yevamah is doubtful to him whether like Rav or like Shmuel. Rashi explains: if like Rav, who said whence that kiddushin do not take effect in a yevamah — as it is written "she shall not be the wife of the dead to a strange man" — she shall not be in her existence to a stranger; if like Shmuel, who said in our poverty she needs a get — it is doubtful; therefore it comes as Rav said in one version: it comes to establish the negative command, and kiddushin take effect in her, since he derived his negative command in the language of existence. Tosafot wrote "and it does not appear so," and proved that according to all there is a negative command in a yevamah. And Or Zarua: according to all there is a negative command in her, and this is what is meant later "and Shmuel is doubtful to him whether it comes for the negative command" — and the way of the verse (meaning it states the prohibition of relations) in clean language, or to teach us that he is lashed for kiddushin (meaning for kiddushin without relations, or that it comes even for kiddushin that did not take effect). In my humble opinion, to resolve Rashi's explanation: on 92b in Shmuel's words "whether it comes for the negative command" — Rashi explains: behold she is like other chiyvei lavin and kiddushin took effect in her, and every viewer will wonder why here it needs to elaborate the versions. One version: to establish the negative command, and kiddushin took effect, since he derived his negative command in the language of existence. However in Kiddushin 67b it states: let us say both cases are with a brother's wife — this lekhatchilah, that b'dieved; and Chiddushei HaRashba wrote: but we hold like Rava, who said if one did it it does not help. And they answered in Tosafot: since the verse revealed in "he shall not take" regarding a High Priest that we learn from it "he shall not take" regarding a father's wife that if one did it, it helps — and there the sugya learns for all chiyvei karet that kiddushin do not take effect. If so, for Rava certainly the dispute of Rav and Shmuel is as its plain meaning: for Rav certainly it comes for the negative command, and since he derived in the language of existence to say that even a lav kiddushin take effect, as Tosafot wrote; and even though he learns it in all chiyvei lavin from the lav of a High Priest that kiddushin take effect — this is only in the language of "he shall not take," which is similar to a High Priest; but this lav in the language of "she shall not be" remains in Rava's category that if one did it, it does not help. Nevertheless he is lashed even for kiddushin alone, since he transgressed the statement of the Merciful One. And Shmuel is doubtful whether it comes for the negative command; and Rashi explains: behold she is like other chiyvei lavin — meaning we derive from a High Priest that kiddushin take effect, even though the lav is in preventing kiddushin in the language of "he shall not take," and the same here; or literally like Rav, unlike here for Abaye Rashi's explanation is compelled: necessarily Rav, who said kiddushin do not take effect, does not hold it comes for the negative command — for if for the negative command, behold the prevention is in the language of existence, and whatever the Torah said "you shall not do" — necessarily if one did it, it helps, as with a High Priest and a widow; if not, why is he lashed? Or it does not come at all for the negative command, but only to rule that kiddushin do not take effect, and there is a positive commandment prohibition in her, as Rashi glosses Yevamot 55b. And in Shmuel it is compelled to explain: whether it comes for this and not for the negative command — and this is like Rav said in one version: to establish the negative command, and kiddushin take effect in her, since he derived his negative command in the language of existence — it is compelled that kiddushin take effect in her; for if not, why is he lashed, as Abaye? And automatically all Tosafot's difficulties are resolved; for all sugyot are explained per Rava: according to him one may say it comes for the negative command, and nevertheless kiddushin do not take effect. And in 55b the text: according to the one who says positive commandment — this is Abaye per Rav.
