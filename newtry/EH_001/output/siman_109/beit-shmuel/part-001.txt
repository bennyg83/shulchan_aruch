**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say that if he gave her as a gift.</b> See siman 99, where it is explained that an item he gives her for adornment is appraised against her ketubah, even though he gave it to her as a gift. One must say that here it speaks where he explicitly gave her more than the ketubah. One may also say that what is written here, that bracelets are not included, is relevant after his death, but during his lifetime he is not obligated to leave them for her adornment. Taz challenged this law from what is stated in the Gemara and in Choshen Mishpat siman 220: one who sells a wagon sells the mules if they were tied to it. According to this, here too, if the bracelets are attached to her garments, she acquired them, whether by sale or by gift. It appears to me that from there there is proof to the contrary, for it states there that one who sells a donkey has not sold the saddlebag or riding pad, and one who sells a maidservant has not sold the bracelets or rings, even if they are upon her. One must say that a wagon and mules are different, because the wagon is nothing without the mules; but things that do not belong to one another, such as bracelets, are not acquired even if attached. However, according to Ri"v this requires study: do all the items listed there become acquired if he gave the maidservant or donkey as a gift? At first glance, whether by sale or by gift, the recipient does not acquire them; likewise, if he said "her garments," he did not acquire the bracelets. The reason for the distinction here between sale and gift is explained by Ri"v: in a sale, the buyer is not embarrassed to stipulate, and therefore should have stipulated, so he does not acquire them; but one receiving a gift is embarrassed to ask that everything be given to him. If she does not wish to marry him until he writes this for her, then it is like a sale. According to this, the distinction here between giving at death and giving at marriage does not depend on the fact that it is given at death; rather, what he gives at marriage is like a sale, for she sells herself to him, whereas what he gives not at the time of marriage is a gift.
