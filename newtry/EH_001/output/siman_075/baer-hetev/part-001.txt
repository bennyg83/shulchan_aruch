**** ENGLISH ****
(ז) <b>One, etc.</b> This is Rabbenu Tam's approach, and it is the opposite of all the poskim. He holds that the Mishnah speaks of coercing the man, when she can compel him to follow her. According to his approach it is this way: if one is from Judea and the other from the Galilee, then wherever they were married, they are obligated to live there; even if it was his place, he cannot compel her to uproot from there and go to her province. Likewise, if they are from one province, and his place is a city and her place is a large town, or the reverse, we always follow the place of marriage. If both are from Judea and the marriage was in another province, she can compel him to go to their province, even from a large town to a city. Beit Shmuel wrote that it implies that if he is from Judea and she is from the Galilee, and the marriage was in the Galilee, he cannot compel her. Likewise, if one is from the Galilee and one is from Judea, and the marriage was in Judea, he cannot compel her to go to his province. See there. Beit Shmuel disagrees with Rabbenu Tam. He wrote that according to the disputing view, the husband cannot compel her to leave the place of marriage unless both are from one province. See Darkei Moshe and Beit Shmuel.
