It is known what Choshen Mishpat wrote; but if at time of kiddushin etc. even if afterward known there was already despair — it is like despair without knowledge. What relevance does despair without knowledge have here? This applies only to lost objects: if it came to his hand before owners knew it was lost, even though when he learned it was lost they presumably despair — nevertheless considered despair without knowledge since it came to his domain before the loser despaired, as explained siman 262 Semag s.k. 9; there too implies even in lost objects if found thing though presumably at finding did not despair and later witnesses that at finding already despaired — he acquires. And Yeshuot Shlomo what is said on this does not refer at all to without knowledge of the taker but as Semag explains without knowledge of the loser; the essence depends on whether it came to his hand permissibly after despair or forbidden after despair. Therefore in my view Rambam's precision is only in this: if there was despair after kiddushin — change of domain and afterward despair; but if known afterward there was despair before kiddushin — what difference her knowing? He left Rambam's and Shulchan Aruch words in doubt. This is Tosafot R. Yehuda's language there: R. Shimon's view is not concerned — seems same for theft, for one view plain despair of owners; for braitot teach robbery and theft together implying equal law; in Sefer Ayelet wrote on them and I did not reach their intent. In my view perhaps they intended: since braitot teach robbery and theft and explain how regarding hers specifically — implies in others robbery and theft law equal — not betrothed plainly; since in robbery even stringently we do not concern R. Shimon — likewise plain theft not betrothed even stringently. This too may be Rambam's intent though he rules chapter 6 of Hagahot plain theft is despair — perhaps holds for kiddushin because whoever betroths etc. they annulled kiddushin so as not to come to doubts of plain cases and established law on its basis: with clear despair certainly betrothed and plainly not betrothed. Thus can rescue Tur Choshen Mishpat 368 who understood his father's view that even plain Israel robbery is despair; they challenged from Rava who said R. Shimon not concerned even Israel. Per above can say second kiddushin; but for law Ayelet well concludes need concern all stringencies; see responsum Mahari Segal brother of Taz 7; see Semag 368 s.k. 3 greatly questioned Rambam; especially Yeshuot Shmuel chapter 10 siman 77 explains Rambam words that only customs officer even Israel plain not despair; what mentions or customs officer gentile is inclusion; but certainly per him plain customs officer i.e. robber plainly not despair except bandit i.e. thief Israel plain despair; siman 76 wrote law of mishnah from which flows Rambam's law siman 369 per halakha we rule Rabbanan — customs officer only when known despaired; one might not notice Rambam's contradictions establishing mishnah literally even plainly; nevertheless ruled in his laws both gentile and Israel; Semag's explanation: though plain gentile customs officer not despair — for money because thinks tomorrow will claim him on money; but body of object once in another's hand owners despair; besides explanation strained and remote — if so customs officer law of 369 does not deal when victim stands sees donkey given to second — not in mishnah at all and reasoning itself also not; also gemara does not challenge Rava who said even known also mishnah dispute — perhaps Rambam's reason in reisha since from outset plain if robbery in customs officer's hand only through plain he was established in robbery — plain despair comes and removes plain robbery; perhaps also from doubt; unlike sippa definite robbery needs definite despair; R.
