**** ENGLISH ****
(ג) <b>But from marriage, etc.</b> The reason given is that there is no decree here. I did not understand this: how does "the kiddushin return" apply after marriage? With erusin one can find such a case, where he or she dies and she retracts; but with marriage this does not apply. If he dies, she takes her ketubah and no more; if she dies, everything belongs to the husband, and no return is relevant. I later saw in Responsa Maharil, siman 23, that this refers to Rabbenu Tam's enactments where she died within two years. The glossator's words appear to mean that Taz's difficulty is only with Rama's wording in the name of Maharil, that "the kiddushin return"; such language applies only to an arusah, not to a married woman. With a married woman, the language of return is not appropriate, for everything is his; only if he dies or divorces her does he give her the ketubah and no more, and if she dies everything is his. The expression "return" applies only where she dies within a year or two. This answers Beit Shmuel's difficulty on Chelkat Mechokek, who said that Maharil holds kiddushin were not given to be sunk permanently, only that they are not returned lest people say, etc.; this does not apply after marriage. If so, Maharil speaks about ordinary marriages, whether he or she died or she was divorced, and not specifically where she died within a year or two. According to Taz's precision that the language of return does not apply where either spouse dies after a long time, it must be that Maharil speaks within a year or two, when the language of return applies. But as a matter of law there is no practical difference: Taz too holds that kiddushin were not given to be sunk permanently, and for marriage her ketubah follows Maharam of Lublin, as seen from Taz's statement that she takes her ketubah and no more.

(א) <b>When they wish to make guarantees, etc.</b> This implies that there is no distinction between matchmaking and other matters: even with matchmaking one must be careful that it not be an asmakhta. This is Rambam's view. The "some say" cited by Rama is the view of Tosafot and the Rosh, that because of the embarrassment that the one retracting from matchmaking appeases, there is no asmakhta. So too the Tur wrote here in the name of Ri. Immediately afterward, however, he wrote that one who gives collateral for the penalty has done nothing: "If each gives collateral for the penalty he obligated himself to pay," etc., because in such a case one does not acquire through collateral so long as he has not yet become obligated at all. But for a matter in which he is not yet obligated, no lien takes effect through collateral; rather, he must transfer it through a formal kinyan, not as an asmakhta, in an important beit din, so that he becomes legally obligated if he retracts, and then he gives the object as collateral and the lien takes effect on it, etc. If so, his words contradict one another, and they are adjacent. So Beit Yosef asked. He further asked on what the Rosh wrote in responsum, that there are other grounds to void the whole matter because of asmakhta, although the Rosh himself ruled that there is no asmakhta in matchmaking. In truth, the latter question is not difficult: when the Rosh wrote "other grounds," his intent was that according to other poskim there is asmakhta even in matchmaking, and therefore he called them other grounds, bringing them only for additional support. Nearby I will write another proper answer to this, with Hashem's help. But the first question is certainly difficult. One cannot say that the end of the Tur's words refers to other laws and not matchmaking, as Perishah wrote at length; if so, he should not have written it here but in Choshen Mishpat, and indeed it is written there in Choshen Mishpat siman 207. Also, he wrote "the collateral for the penalty" with the definite article, meaning the penalty he had just mentioned; otherwise he should have written "a penalty" without the definite article.
