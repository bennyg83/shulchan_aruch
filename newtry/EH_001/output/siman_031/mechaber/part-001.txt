That one may not betroth with less than the value of a perutah, and if appraisal is required. It contains 9 seifim.

One may not betroth with less than the value of a perutah; however if he betrothed her with an unspecified object he need not appraise it first — if it is worth a perutah she is betrothed. And even if he said to her "Be betrothed to me with this object that is worth fifty zuz" and they did not appraise it first, if it is afterward found to be worth fifty zuz she is betrothed.

Some say that if he betrothed her with something people are not expert in appraising and sometimes err greatly — such as precious stones and pearls and the like — and he said to her "Be betrothed to me with this stone that is worth fifty zuz" — it requires appraisal, for she cannot rely on it. {Rama: And if he betrothed her with a ring that has a stone, or with an ordinary ring and it turns out to be copper — even though it is not customary to betroth thus we are concerned for betrothal (Beit Yosef citing responsum of Rosh bar Tzemach and Mordechai first chapter of Kiddushin). And even if witnesses under the wedding canopy said it was gold and it turns out copper — even though for law it seems she is not betrothed — nevertheless for practical application one should rule stringently (responsum of Maimonides at end of Laws of Women). And nevertheless under the canopy they customarily ask witnesses whether the ring is worth a perutah so the bride knows she is betrothed only with something worth a perutah (Orach Chaim). They also customarily cover the faces of modest brides and they are not particular about what they are betrothed with (explained in responsum of Rashba).}
