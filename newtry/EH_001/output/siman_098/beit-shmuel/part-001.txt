**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) <b>He exempted his wife at the time of his death.</b> So wrote Mordechai, and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rav Natronai: Abba Shaul said "what can I do," etc., only where he wrote her exemptions at the time of kiddushin; then the condition helps only for himself. But if he exempted her at the time of his death, it helps, for then it is as though he gave her as a gift all the bundles he gave her or what she took herself. Therefore, even if he did not say "from me and from my heirs," the heirs cannot administer an oath to her. Mordechai there concludes and writes that Sha'arei HaRif disagrees with this and holds that if he did not write exemptions on the ketubah, he accomplished nothing to extract the ketubah. Bach understood that Rif holds that exemptions given at the time of death are worse, but this does not appear to me. Rather, Rif holds that he must specify that she is exempt from the oath of the ketubah.

(א) <b>Even if she or the heirs impaired it.</b> This is not comparable to an apotropos. Although he gave her exemptions, nevertheless if she became an apotropos after his death she must swear, for he exempted her only from his own money, and after his death the money left his domain. <b>Even if he said, "from these assets."</b> This is Abba Shaul's statement: whether he wrote "without oath" or "clean of oath," whether "without vow" or "clean of vow," whether "from my assets" or "from these assets," neither he nor his heirs may impose an oath upon her. But what can I do, for Chazal said that one who comes to collect from orphans' assets may collect only with an oath. There are three approaches. According to Rif, Rambam, and several poskim, exemption from his heirs helps when he says explicitly that she is exempt from his heirs. Abba Shaul's statement "what can I do" refers to where he wrote "clean of vow from these assets" but did not explicitly say "from me and from my heirs"; we then say his intent was to exempt her from himself but not from his heirs, since the rule that one who comes to collect from orphans requires an oath is an enactment of Chazal, and presumably he did not exempt her from that oath. Although according to several amoraim a general exemption helps, we rule like Abba Shaul. According to Rabbenu Chananel, as Ritva wrote in his name, if he explicitly exempted her from himself and his heirs, it helps even for the orphans' oath; only when he exempts generally do we say he exempted her only from an apotropos oath and an oath regarding impairment of the document, but not from the orphans' oath. Then she must swear also regarding apotropos and impairment by way of gilgul. This is the meaning of Abba Shaul's "what can I do, for Chazal said one who collects from orphans requires an oath": the exemptions do not help, since they can roll other oaths onto her. According to this, it is clear that he holds even explicit exemptions can be reached by gilgul by the orphans. At first glance this is difficult, for the Mishnah explains that they impose oaths about the future but not the past, implying that even by gilgul they cannot impose those oaths. Perhaps Abba Shaul disagrees with the Mishnah, and the halakhah follows Abba Shaul, as Rav Nachman said in the Gemara. What Tosafot wrote in the name of Rabbenu Chananel, that the halakhah follows R. Shimon that she requires an oath, can be understood where he did not explicitly exempt her from the orphans. However, from the Rosh it is clear that according to Rabbenu Chananel and Rabbenu Tam they do not impose the apotropos and impairment oaths by gilgul, for the Rosh holds that what he explicitly exempted cannot be imposed by gilgul. Necessarily, those exemptions do not help for these oaths, and she must swear not because of gilgul but because the oath itself is required. According to this, Rabbenu Chananel follows a third approach: even explicit exemption from orphans does not help. This also proves that the Rosh holds an apotropos oath can be imposed even without gilgul, for R. Shimon speaks about the Mishnah where he gave her exemptions, and says that when she claims the ketubah they impose an oath upon her. The Rosh wrote that they impose an oath upon her regarding everything that was in her hand, namely the apotropos oath. It cannot be by gilgul, because he exempted her and one cannot roll onto her something from which she was exempted. Necessarily he holds that the rule that one who comes to collect from orphans must swear includes both an oath that her husband did not transfer hidden funds to her and that she did not take them herself; about both Abba Shaul said "what can I do." Thus even exemptions do not help, and these two oaths are equal. It follows that if he died suddenly, so there is no concern that he transferred hidden funds, she nevertheless requires an oath lest she took funds herself; this too is the orphans' oath, and if she died without swearing, she cannot transmit that oath to her heirs. Yet according to this it is difficult why the Tur wrote that if he designated land or movables for her, she collects without an oath because we no longer suspect hidden payment; there remains concern that she took it herself. Nor can one answer with the reason of Maggid Mishneh that once he designated property for her she is no longer claiming her ketubah and they impose an oath only when she claims, for then why give the reason that we do not suspect hidden payment? It would suffice to say that she is not claiming the ketubah. The third approach is the view of the Rosh and several poskim, that exemptions do not help even explicitly against the orphans.
