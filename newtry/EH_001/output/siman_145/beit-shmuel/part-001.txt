<b>אינו גט</b>. וא"ל למה לא אמרינן התנאי בטל והגט חל מיד דהא לא כפל התנאי אם כן המעשה קיים והתנאי בטל ויש לומר אם מתי הוי כאומר לאחר מיתה והוי כאלו קובע זמן כמ"ש בסימן אח"ז דאין צריך ת"כ והא דהתקין שמואל באם מתי תנאי כפול היינו כמ"ש המגיד כשכפל התנאי אז ניכר דאין כוונתו לגרש אחר המיתה אלא מגרש מיד ואינו אלא תנאי אם ימות משא"כ באומר סתם אם מתי אמרי' דכוונתו לגרש לאחר מותו והוי קובע זמן ואין צריך ת"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>It is not a get.</b> Do not ask why we do not say that the condition is void and the get takes effect immediately, since he did not double the condition; if so, the act stands and the condition is void. One may say that "if I die" is like saying "after death," and it is as though he fixes a time, as written in the following siman, where no double condition is needed. Shmuel's enactment of a doubled condition for "if I die" is as Maggid Mishneh wrote: when he doubles the condition, it is recognizable that his intent is not to divorce after death, but rather he divorces immediately and it is only conditional on whether he dies. This is unlike one who says plainly "if I die," where we say that his intent is to divorce after his death, and it is fixing a time, so no doubled condition is needed.

**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>From today if I die.</b> Because the phrase if I die implies two meanings: it can imply from now, and it can imply after death. Therefore, if he said from today, it is like one who said from now; if he did not say from today, it is like one who said after death. In siman 148 the dispute is explained: whether his intention is that the Get take effect immediately, in which case even if the Get no longer exists at the time of death she is divorced, because the divorce takes effect immediately. This is the opinion of Rif and Rambam, chapter 9 law 19. But according to the Rosh, the Tur, and the Maggid Mishneh, for Rabbi Yose it is uncertain whether his intention is to delay the Get as much as possible, meaning that the condition begins now but the Get takes effect one hour before his death; the Get must therefore exist at that time, and if it does not exist the Maggid Mishneh ruled there that she must leave. Darkei Moshe in siman 267 briefly cited the view of Mordekhai and wrote regarding it: see siman 143, for many poskim disagree and hold that anyone who says from now, even if the Get was lost or burned, the Get is valid. At first glance, it appears that one should not compare them. Certainly, a healthy man who divorces with a condition from now begins the divorce immediately according to all opinions. But with a shekhiv mera, they hold that his intention is to delay the Get as much as possible. Regarding seclusion with her: according to Rif's approach, if he secludes himself before witnesses there is concern for kiddushin, lest he die and the Get take effect retroactively immediately, and perhaps he betrothed her at that time; without witnesses there is a prohibition of seclusion. But a stale Get is not relevant here either way, for the Get takes effect immediately when he dies, and if he does not die she is his wife. According to the Rosh's approach, there is concern for a stale Get, because the Get has not yet taken effect. I already wrote in the preceding siman that we are not concerned here that perhaps both of them will agree to nullify the Get, since the condition is fulfilled against their will and is not in their control; it is not appropriate to say that it is as though it was not fulfilled. See Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9 law 9.

<b>מחולי זה</b>. משמע לכאורה אם אמר סתם אם מתי אין צריך אומדנא וכ"כ הרמב"ן ורי"ו וכתבו אפילו אם מת מחולי אחר מגורשת כיון שלא הבריא לגמרי כאשר היה קודם שחלה אף על פי שהלך בלא משענת הוי גט ואם הבריא מחולי לגמרי כמו שהיה קודם שחלה בטל הגט אף על גב שחלה אחר כך ומת, ובסעיף אח"ז הביא דברי הרשב"א והר"ן ש"מ שאמר אם מתי מחולי זה קאמר לכן אם הבריא וחלה אח"כ ומת אינו גט נראה דל"פ על הרמב"ן אלא אם הבריא כמו שהיה קודם שחלה אז הגט בטל אף על גב שחלה אחר כך ומת ואמרי' מ"ש אם מתי היינו מחולי זה קאמר אבל אם לא הבריא כמו שהיה קודם שחלה אף על גב דהלך בשוק בלא משענת וחלה מחולי אחר מגורשת אא"כ שהתנה בפירוש אם מתי מחולי זה ואז אפילו לא הבריא כמו שהיה קודם שחלה בעינן אומדנא אם מת מחולי הראשון אבל מדברי המגיד ספ"ח ה"ז מבואר דהרי"ף והרמב"ם לא ס"ל כן אלא אפילו אם אמר סתם אם מתי צריך אומדנא אם מת מחולי הראשון וסוגיא קאי אפי' אם אמר סתם אם מתי, ולכאורה קשה לפ"ז על מ"ש המחבר בסעיף ט' עצה טובה וכו' אכתי החשש במקומו עומד דהא ס"ל אפי' באומר סתם אם מתי בעינן אומדנא אם מת מאותו חולי:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>From this illness.</b> At first glance it implies that if he said simply, "if I die," no assessment is needed. So wrote Ramban and Ri"v, and they wrote that even if he died from another illness she is divorced, since he did not fully recover to the state he was in before he became ill; even if he walked without a cane, it is a get. But if he fully recovered from the illness as he was before becoming ill, the get is void, even if he later became ill and died. In the following seif the Mechaber brought the words of Rashba and the Ran, which imply that one who says "if I die" means "from this illness"; therefore, if he recovered and afterward became ill and died, it is not a get. It appears that they do not disagree with Ramban except where he recovered as he was before illness; then the get is void even though he later became ill and died, because we say that "if I die" meant from this illness. But if he did not recover as he was before illness, even though he walked in the marketplace without a cane and then became ill from another illness, she is divorced, unless he explicitly stipulated "if I die from this illness." In that case, even if he did not recover as he was before illness, we require assessment whether he died from the first illness. But from Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 8, halakhah 7, it is clear that Rif and Rambam do not hold so; rather, even if he said simply "if I die," we require assessment whether he died from the first illness, and the sugya applies even where he said simply "if I die." At first glance this makes difficult what the Mechaber wrote in seif 9 as good counsel, etc., for the concern still remains, since he holds that even when one says simply "if I die," assessment is required whether he died from that illness.
