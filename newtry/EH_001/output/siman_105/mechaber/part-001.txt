**** ENGLISH ****
A woman is allowed to sell or give to others her marriage contract whether it is all of it or just part of it. The buyer or the receiver take her place that if she should become a widow or a divorcee they get to receive her marriage contract. If she sold it to her husband, the sale is a sale and there cannot be a delay unless he would write her another one that would be in the spirit of the marriage contract.

**** ENGLISH ****
The woman who sells her ketubbah to others and her husband dies in her lifetime, if she died before she swore regarding her ketubbah, the one who bought it and the one who accepted it [do not receive] anything. Regarding what are we speaking? When she is widowed and she died, [in the case] when she was not able to pay [off the debt] from [being upon] the orphans except by oath. But if she was divorced and she died, the purchasers swear an oath that they have not left an order, and they take. And specifically when she died, but if she is alive, she needs to swear that it will be paid, and if she does not want to swear, the purchasers will lose [their money]. If she said that she will be paid after she sells [her ketubbah], she is believed, because if she had wanted to forgive [the debt]. But if she said that it will be paid before the sale, she is not believed.

**** ENGLISH ****
One who sells a debt document to his fellow, and then he goes back and forgives the debt, it is forgiven. And even the inheritor may forgive. <i data-commentator="Chelkat Mechokek" data-order="19"></i><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="נ" data-order="14"></i>מי שמכר בחיי אביו כתובת אמו שאם ימות אביו ואח"כ תמות אמו ויירש הוא את כתובתה שיהי' הלוקח עומד במקומו לגבותה והתנה שאם תערער אמו על המכר שלא יסלקנו מערעור ומתה ולא ערערה <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ס" data-order="15"></i>אינו יכול לומר אני במקום אמי וכמו שהיא יכולה לערער על המקח גם אני מערער ואבטלנו שערעור של עצמו קבל עליו ודאי:
**** ENGLISH ****
If, in the life of his father, a man sold the ketubah of his mother, so that in the circumstance that his father died first, and then his mother died, and he would consequentially inherit his mother's ketubah, and the purchaser would stand in his place to collect the ketubah from the estate, and he made a condition that if his mother complained about the sale, that [the purchaser] would not remove her from her complaint; and she subsequently died without having complained about the sale; the son may not say, "I stand in the place of my mother, and just like she could have complained about the transaction, I can also complain about the transaction and hereby abrogate it," for he has definitely relinquished his own right to complain.
