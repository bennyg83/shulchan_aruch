<b>One may not sell a Sefer Torah.</b> Beit Shmuel wrote that, nevertheless, what the Rama wrote at the end of siman 63 in the gloss in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim, etc., does not prove the matter in my humble opinion. One may say that he chose a reason that applies even to an elderly woman or to a woman who already has children. Rambam's view, however, is clearly unlike that of Tosafos. See Baer Heitev, s.k. 27. This also appears to me from the unqualified language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. According to Tosafos, I have difficulty with the sugya at the end of the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto. Why does the Gemara ask, "If she is not commanded, what claim does she have to a ketubah?" Perhaps she nevertheless has a ketubah because she is commanded regarding "He did not create it for emptiness, He formed it to be inhabited." Likewise, what is gained by telling her, "Go, you are not commanded"? She may answer, "I wish to fulfill the mitzvah of inhabiting the world." Although one can answer with difficulty, as Tosafos answered concerning a half-maidservant, that we are concerned that even after he divorces her she will not marry because she is not commanded in procreation, for which we compel him, it remains difficult. From where did the Gemara know to ask its question? It is forced to say that it relied upon what is implied by the case of the half-maidservant. Furthermore, even when she presents her claim, do we know that she will marry? Perhaps she is lying. Rather, depriving her of her ketubah is different. Yet why should her claim based upon the mitzvah of inhabiting the world differ from her claim, "I need a staff for my hand and someone to bury me"? Magen Avraham's proof from the fact that the verse concerning inhabiting the world is cited regarding the sale of a Sefer Torah, implying that this is on account of a woman, may be rejected. The verse is brought to explain why this positive commandment differs from others, to the extent that one sells a Sefer Torah for it. It teaches that this is a great mitzvah. This also appears to be the view of Tosafos in their answer at the beginning of Bava Batra, and likewise in Tosafos, Gittin 41b.

<b>But before thirteen he should not marry, for it is like promiscuous relations.</b> Beit Yosef explained: because the Sages did not enact marriage for a minor; therefore Beit Shmuel brings the view of Tosafot and Rosh who wrote that it is not promiscuous relations since through kiddushin she is bound to him. This language; "since through kiddushin she is bound to him"; is said only according to Rosh chapter 2 of Kiddushin; but Tosafot in the chapter HaNisrafin and the chapter HaIshah Rabbah wrote in this language "nevertheless Rabbi Yose says there is no prohibition, for it is not intercourse of promiscuous relations." And one could say their reason: they follow Ramban in siman 26 in Beit Shmuel seif 2, that for a designated woman there is no prohibition at all even without kiddushin; and marriage of a minor is no worse than a designated woman. (And according to the flow of the argument there is proof that at least for a concubine there is no Torah prohibition, from what they enacted marriage for a minor orphan girl; and even according to Maggid Mishneh that any relationship without kiddushin has a positive prohibition of "when he takes," etc.; according to his words even for a designated woman, i.e. concubine, this prohibition applies; how can they enact for him to transgress a positive commandment, even a positive commandment? We conclude in Yevamot chapter 9 specifically for "sit and do not act"; but for according to the Maggid Mishneh one must explain that marriage of a minor is considered a matter in which the Sages have power to uproot even a Torah matter, as Tosafot conclude there in the passage beginning "since," etc.; see their words. And one opinion of Tosafot in the passage beginning "they immerse.") And one could also say the intent of Tosafot is as Beit Shmuel understood: "since nevertheless," etc. However, in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto 62b they explained their intent is not according to this, for they wrote "nevertheless for this reason they enacted marriage for him, that his intercourse not be promiscuous relations"; it appears from their language that specifically since through kiddushin and marriage she is bound to him, because of the mitzvah to marry near his maturity they needed to enact this rabbinic marriage so that his intercourse not be promiscuous relations. And necessarily they hold there is prohibition at least for concubine; and it also appears from their words that before near his maturity, i.e. the year before majority, in this there is no mitzvah and there is also prohibition because of "to add the satiated to the thirsty"; and from the Torah they would enact marriage for him; if so, besides the prohibition of "lest she become pregnant," promiscuous relations also applies. And a practical difference: one who marries a minor non-Jewish girle; because of "to add the satiated to the thirsty" there is no concern since she too is saturated; but because of promiscuous relations there is concern. And even though we establish that a minor who eats nevelah; bet din is not commanded to separate him; nevertheless regarding those who endeavor, the prohibition applies not to marry him off. And behold, according to Tosafot in the chapter HaNisrafin and the chapter HaIshah Rabbah one could say the default follows their explanation in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto, and not as Beit Shmuel's wording implies; but in Rosh one cannot explain it this way; "for this reason they enacted marriage for a minor that his intercourse not be promiscuous relations"; for if so, how does he derive from this marriage of a minor during his father's lifetime? Even though the Torah did not give the Sages power over marriage for the matter of refusal, nevertheless he is not obligated to separate them and it is not promiscuous relations.
