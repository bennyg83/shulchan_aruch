**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He is obligated, etc.</b> This is taught in the Mishnah in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto, folio 61: a regular kohen, etc., as explained there in the Gemara. The Mishnah there also states, "he may not refrain, etc." As the Gemara there says, "I have only, etc.," and it states, "in order that, etc.," meaning that his intention should be for the sake of children. For at the end of Taanis our Rabbis taught: "The beautiful among them, etc., provided that you adorn me, etc." This means with ornaments, for a woman's beauty depends upon her ornaments, as stated in the first chapter of Megillah: "provided that she come naked," because a woman is for adornment. This is also what is stated in Kesubos 59b: R. Chiyya taught, "A woman is only, etc.," and R. Chiyya further taught, etc. These are the three matters stated here. The Tanna of the Mishnah, however, stated only, "Set your eyes upon the family," because a woman is for children. "Charm is false" refers to ornaments, for charm results from something external, as stated regarding Esther, who was greenish, etc., yet it says, "Esther found favor, etc." "And beauty is vain, etc."

<b>When does this apply, etc.</b> There it states: "a son for a son, etc.; according to everyone, two, etc." Since it says "a son for a daughter," it implies that this applies specifically when the grandchildren descend from both a male and a female. The plain sense of the passage implies that it applies even when both grandchildren are males, and according to Rava even when both are females. See Tosafos there, s.v. VeKol Shekein. One might press the language and explain: all the more so a son born to a daughter where the elderly man's son remains alive, and similarly a daughter born to a son where his daughter remains alive. The essential reading is that of the Rambam: Rava said to him, "We require 'He created it to be inhabited,'" and the text does not read, "but there is, etc." Rava disputes Abaye's statement, "all the more so a son for a daughter," because the Mishnah's reason is that a male and female should exist in the world. That is the meaning of inhabitation according to the Tanna of the Mishnah. The correct reading of the Rambam in Abaye is: "But a son for a daughter or a daughter for a son does not suffice." Rava said to him, "We require inhabitation, and there is, etc."

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he and they converted.</b> Otherwise, what does the Gemara mean when it says, "and both statements were necessary, etc." A non-Jew inherits his father according to Torah law. See Rashi and Tosafos there, who were forced to explain the discussion regarding the firstborn right. According to the Rambam, however, it is clear. The Gemara means as follows: Reish Lakish said that he has not fulfilled procreation in the case of a convert who converted, etc., meaning where the children also converted, for after conversion they are not legally his children. His own conversion is not the reason, since while he was a non-Jew, etc., as Tosafos writes there, s.v. R. Yochanan, and even Reish Lakish agrees to that. He disagrees because of the children's conversion, which is as though the children died, since a convert is regarded as a newborn child. Concerning this the Gemara says that both teachings were necessary, for originally, etc., meaning before the children converted, even after the father converted. This differs from inheritance, because a non-Jew does not inherit a convert, as stated there in Kiddushin. This is why the text says, "they converted," in the plural. The passage in Bechoros does not prove the Rambam's view. Likewise, in Choshen Mishpat, siman 277, seif 9, it does not state that the children converted. The Gemara there and in Yevamos explains why both teachings are necessary, yet the children's conversion is not mentioned at all, only the father's conversion. Even if one says that the children did not convert, it is certainly true concerning them that a convert is regarded as a newborn child, as stated there. The Gemara states plainly that in such a case he certainly has no paternal lineage, and this is obvious without proof. This is the view of all the poskim, unlike the Rambam.
