<b>אשה. </b> וחייב לבעול בכל עונה אפילו שהרשת את בעלה שימנע עונתה חייב לבעול בכל עונה עד שיהא לו בנים הרמב"ם פט"ו ה"א. וכן איתא לקמן סימן ע"ו ס"ו: ומשמע אחר שיהיה לו בנים מהני רשות שלה אפי' אם יוכל עוד להוליד. וצ"ע מנ"ל להרמב"ם חילוק זה דמהני מחילה שלה אחר הקיום פ"ו דהא אפי' אם קיים פו"ר מ"מ חייב לקיים לערב אל תנח ידך ב"ש ע"ש. הראנ"ח ח"ב סי' מ"ג נסתפק אי מהני דבורא בעלמא ולא מצי הדרי בה או לא ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A woman.</b> He is obligated to fulfill the marital duty (onah) every period — even if she has granted her husband permission to forgo it, he is obligated every period until he has children (Rambam ch. 15, halacha 1), and likewise below siman 76:6. It follows that after he has children her permission is effective even if he could still produce more. But this requires examination: from where does the Rambam derive this distinction that her waiver is effective after fulfillment of peru u-revu, given that even if he has fulfilled it he is still obligated in "in the evening do not withhold your hand" — see Beit Shmuel there. Ra'anach part 2 siman 43 was uncertain whether a verbal waiver alone is effective with no ability to retract — see there.

<b>תורה. </b> בכלל זה איתא הספקת תלמידים. ובכלל לישא אשה איתא להשיא יתומים בדמיו כמ"ש בא"ח סימן קנ"ג. ול"ד ס"ת אלא ה"ה שאר ספרים נמי אסור למכור אלא לדברים אלו כמ"ש ביו"ד סי' ר"ע וכ"כ בה"י: וכתב הח"מ דוקא משום מצות פו"ר מוכרים ס"ת אבל להשיא יתומה אין מוכרין כי היא אינה מצווה על פו"ר אלא דוקא להשיא יתום מוכרים משום פו"ר. והב"ש כתב עליו מיהו למ"ש לקמן ס"מ מוכרין ס"ת לקיים לערב אל תנח ידך י"ל להשיא יתומה נמי מוכרים ס"ת לקיים לשבת יצרה דאשה נמי מצווה על שבת. ומיהו למ"ש ס"ס י"ג בהג"ה אשה אל תשב בלא בעל משום חשד. והוא מהרמב"ם פט"ו מה"א דין י"ו. משמע דאינה מצווה על שבת. ועיין תשובת רד"ך שכתב דהאשה מצווה על שבת. ולכאורה קשה דאם י"ל דמוכרים ס"ס. בשביל להשיא יתומה כדי לקיים שבת יצרה א"כ ה"נ מוכרים ס"ת בשביל שלא יעמוד הוא בלא אשה אף בשכבר קיים פו"ר כסברת י"א שבסעי' ח' א"כ לא מוכח תוס' מידי בחגיגה דף ב' ע"ב האי עשה אלים טפי כדאמרי' במגילה מוכר אדם ס"ת וכו' ומייתי לא תהו בראה כ"א לשבת וגו' וכן בגיטין דף מ"א ע"ב דילמא להכי אתיא הגמר' הקרא דלא תהו וגו' להורות אף בשכבר קיים פ"ו וה"ה בשביל יתומה דלא שייך פו"ר מוכרים בשביל שבת יצרה. ועיין במ"א בא"ח סימן קנ"ג ס"ק ט' ע"ש. ולאחר עיון לאו קושיא הוא. דתוס' שכתבו האי עשה אלים טפי וכו' סברי באמת כדיעה א' שבסעי' ח' וכן הוא בהדיא ביבמות דף ס"א ע"ב תוס' בד"ה נפקא מינה וכו'. והב"ש דכתב דמוכרים ס"ת בשביל להשיא יתומה כדי לקיים שבת יצרה. כ"כ לפי י"א שבסעי' ח' עיין ודו"ק. ולענין אם יש חילוק בין ס"ת של יחיד או של רבים עיין בי"ד סימן ר"ע ובא"ח סימן קנ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Torah.</b> Included in this is providing for students. And included in marrying a woman is marrying off orphans at his expense, as stated in OC siman 153. Nor is it only a Sefer Torah — the same applies to other books, which may also not be sold except for these purposes, as stated in YD siman 270, and so too Beit Yosef. And Choshen Mishpat wrote that only for the mitzvah of peru u-revu may a Sefer Torah be sold; but to marry off an orphan girl, one may not sell — since she is not commanded in peru u-revu; only to marry off a male orphan may one sell on account of peru u-revu. And Beit Shmuel argued: but according to what is written below seif 40 — one sells a Sefer Torah to fulfill "in the evening do not withhold your hand" — similarly one could say to marry off an orphan girl too, selling a Sefer Torah to fulfill shevet yitzrah, since a woman too is commanded in shevet. However, from what is written at the end of siman 13 in the Gloss — "a woman should not remain without a husband on account of suspicion" — which comes from Rambam ch. 15 halacha 16 — it implies that she is not commanded in shevet. See the responsum of Radbaz who wrote that the woman is commanded in shevet. At first glance it is difficult: if one can say we sell a Sefer Torah to marry off an orphan girl in order to fulfill shevet yitzrah, then likewise we sell a Sefer Torah so that he himself should not remain without a wife, even after he has already fulfilled peru u-revu, as held by the "some say" in seif 8 — in that case Tosafot in Chagigah 2b, "this positive commandment is stronger," as said in Megillah "a man may sell a Sefer Torah, etc.," citing "He did not create it to be desolate, but formed it to be inhabited, etc." — proves nothing; and likewise in Gittin 41b, perhaps the Gemara brings the verse "He did not create it to be desolate, etc." to teach that even after fulfilling peru u-revu, and similarly for an orphan girl where peru u-revu is not relevant, one still sells for shevet yitzrah. After careful analysis, the question is not a difficulty. For the Tosafot who wrote "this positive commandment is stronger, etc." truly hold the first opinion in seif 8, as is explicit in Yevamot 61b, Tosafot in the passage "nafka mina, etc." And Beit Shmuel who wrote we sell a Sefer Torah to marry off an orphan girl to fulfill shevet yitzrah — wrote so according to the "some say" in seif 8. See and think carefully. And regarding whether there is a distinction between a privately-owned Sefer Torah and a communally-owned one, see YD siman 270 and OC siman 153.

**** ENGLISH ****

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And a female.</b> It implies [one needs] two males — and certainly two females are insufficient. But Tosafot in Yevamot 62b hold that with two males coming from a son and a daughter, one fulfills peru u-revu. And Shitah Gedushah added that even with two daughters coming from a son and a daughter one fulfills it. If he had two males and they left a male and a female — or if he had two males and one of them had a daughter — he has not fulfilled peru u-revu, since he himself must first father a male and a female. If the son is alive and the daughter died leaving a daughter, he has fulfilled it; but if the son is alive and has not yet fathered children and the daughter died leaving a son, he has not fulfilled it. There is a question whether a woman who became pregnant in a bathhouse [without intercourse] — does the father fulfill peru u-revu, and is the child considered his son for all purposes (Choshen Mishpat)? And Beit Shmuel brought proof that he is his son for all purposes.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he converted.</b> In the responsum of Mahari Segal siman 223 he ruled that even if his sons did not convert with him he fulfills peru u-revu. And so it appears from the Tosefta Yevamot 62a in the passage beginning "Rabbi Yose says: he fulfills peru u-revu," etc. And Kenesset HaGedolah ruled that gentile children do not count toward fulfillment of peru u-revu — see there. And see what Beit Yosef wrote. And see what the Tosafot in Chagigah 2b wrote in the passage beginning "He did not create it to be desolate," etc., where they conclude "and furthermore, peru u-revu is written regarding all the children of Noah, even regarding Canaan" — see there. And this is against the sugya in chapter four of [Arba Meitot] where they say peru u-revu was stated for Israel and not for the children of Noah — see there. And so too Maharsha, who left it as undecided. And in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 134 he was asked about this and answered: the Tosafot in Chagigah wrote this as an answer for the one who holds that the children of Noah are commanded even regarding castration, according to Rabbi Chidka who said so in chapter four of Arba Meitot. And do not wonder at this, for Semag also ruled thus, and so too Tur HaShulchan siman 299, whose words are cited in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 5. And we must say: although it is straightforward in the Gemara that the mitzvah of peru u-revu was not stated for the children of Noah — meaning they are not compelled to fulfill the mitzvah of peru u-revu, and the choice is theirs not to engage in it at all — nevertheless, to castrate oneself in a manner that makes it impossible for them to engage in peru u-revu, this certainly remains forbidden according to Rabbi Chidka. And if so, regarding this half-slave half-freedman who is like literal castration — unable to marry a maidservant or a free woman — the Tosafot properly wrote that in this matter even the children of Noah are commanded — end quote. And I heard to answer based on Tosafot Yevamot 62a in the passage "sons of peru u-revu, etc.": "And if you ask — from chapter four of Arba Meitot it seems that gentiles are not commanded in peru u-revu" — one can answer that here he does not speak of those obligated in peru u-revu, but rather that his sons are called by his name — see there. Accordingly, one can explain what Tosafot wrote in Chagigah that peru u-revu "is written regarding all the children of Noah" — meaning: his sons are called by his name, and from this that they are relevant to peru u-revu even though they are not obligated. Thus I heard — but I did not understand it, for the Tosafot conclude "even regarding Canaan," and there they say that everyone agrees regarding a slave who has no lineage — see and think well.

<b>חשדא. </b> כ"כ הרמב"ם פט"ו מה"א. ותימא דבפכ"א מהא"ב כתב ורשות לאשה שלא תנשא לעולם או תנשא לסריס ע"ש. ויש ליישב דמ"ש הרמב"ם בה"א דלא תעמוד בלא איש הוא מצד עצה טובה דלא תחשד. ושם בהלכות א"ב ע"פ הדין דמצד הדין הרשות לאשה שלא תנשא לעולם דאפי' איסור דרבנן ליכא גבה דידה לא משום שבת יצרה ולא משום הרהור משא"כ גבי דידיה וק"ל. כנה"ג ובהרד"ך בית י"ז חדר יו"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Suspicion.</b> So too Rambam ch. 15 halacha 1. It is a wonder, for in ch. 21 of Hilkhot Ishut he writes "a woman has the right never to marry, or to marry a saris" — see there. And one can reconcile: what Rambam wrote in halacha 1, that she should not remain without a husband, is from the perspective of good counsel — so that she should not be suspected. Whereas there in Hilkhot Ishut — according to strict law — a woman has the right never to marry, for there is not even a rabbinic prohibition upon her, neither because of shevet yitzrah nor because of improper thoughts — unlike for a man. And so too Kenesset HaGedolah, and the Radbaz in Beit 17 Cheder 10.
