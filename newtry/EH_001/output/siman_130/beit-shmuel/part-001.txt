**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>Two witnesses must sign.</b> The Gemara explains that Rabban Gamliel enacted that witnesses sign. According to R. Elazar, who says delivery witnesses effect the divorce and signing witnesses are unnecessary, Rabban Gamliel enacted a good counsel that witnesses sign, because sometimes the delivery witnesses die or go overseas. According to R. Meir, who says signing witnesses effect the divorce and they must sign by law, Rabban Gamliel enacted that they specify their names, for at first they did not specify them; he enacted this so that the signatures could be validated easily. Although for us the get is torn immediately and these reasons do not apply, nevertheless ab initio one must write the get so that it is valid even according to R. Meir. So wrote Terumat HaDeshen, siman 232. Still, it remains difficult for us: since we tear the get immediately after delivery, why must they specify their names? Even according to R. Meir, they specified their names only because Rabban Gamliel enacted this so the signatures could be validated, and now the get is torn. If so, even if they do not specify their names it should be valid even according to R. Meir. This is the reason of Rashba and Ri"v, who wrote that they need not specify their names. But Rambam, the Tur, and the Mechaber wrote that they should specify their names, and this is difficult as above. For Rambam one can say that he wrote the law as it applied in the Gemara, when they did not tear the get. One can also say that he holds that according to R. Meir they must specify their names by law, while Rabban Gamliel enacted that they specify both their names and their fathers' names so that validation be easy; today, in truth, he holds that they need not specify their fathers' names. But the Mechaber is difficult, for in Seder HaGet he wrote that the get is torn, while here he wrote that one must write his name and his father's name. See Tosafot at the beginning of Gittin and the Rosh in Perek HaSholeach, who wrote that Rabban Gamliel's enactment that they sign does not mean they must sign, but is good counsel. Although Tosafot wrote this according to the first answer, which establishes the sugya according to R. Elazar, who says delivery witnesses effect the divorce, nevertheless even according to R. Meir the law is so. Rashbatz wrote that by strict law the witnesses' signatures need not be in Ashurit script; if a sharp child can read it, it is valid even where a non-sharp child cannot.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some disagree and validate.</b> This is the Rambam’s view. It is difficult why the Mechaber ruled in Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, that if they distanced the signatures two lines it is invalid, and did not distinguish whether delivery witnesses are before us. It is even more difficult on the Rambam, for he holds that in gittin signing witnesses alone suffice, yet he rules here that if there are delivery witnesses it is valid, while in Hilchot Malveh he invalidates. Perhaps documents are different because they must stand for many days. If a witness erred and signed Yosef ben Shimon, while his father’s name was Reuven, and then signed again Yosef ben Reuven, Beit Yosef cites Tashbetz that it is invalid, because the signature Yosef ben Shimon is not a real person here and it is like forgery within the document. This should not be compared to Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, where invalid witnesses signed merely to fill space, because with a get we do not say they signed only to fill space, as written in siman 120 in the name of Tosafot. The Taz compares the case here to that law, but this does not appear correct.

<b>פסול</b>. ולשיטות הרמב"ם די בע"ח לבד ואם ליכא ע"מ יש חילוק בדבר אם מוכח דהעדי' החתומי' לא קאי על גט זה אז הגט בטל אבל אם אינו מוכח כגון בשאלת שלום וכן אם שייר מקצת הגט וכתב בדף השני הוי ספק גט ואפילו לא כתב וא"ו של ושאלו מ"מ י"ל דחתימתו קאי על הגט נמי ואינו אלא ספק ומ"ש בסמוך בראש הגט וכן כולם פסול ל"ד אלא בכה"ג בטל דהא מוכח דלא חתמו על גט זה לפ"ז מ"ש בסעיף ו' אם יש כאן ע"מ כשר ואפילו אם חתמו בראש הגט ומוכח דלא חתמו על הגט מ"מ כשר צ"ל היינו בדיעבד שניתן הגט אבל לכתחלה לא דהא קי"ל דבעינן ע"מ וע"ח, נשמע מזה גט שלא היה ע"ח כלל וניתן בע"מ אפילו לא נשאת כשר דאם לא כן לא שייך לומר כשר וכן כתוב בפרישה סימן קל"ג וכן משמע בהג"ה סעי' ח' אלא בד"מ ס"ס זה כתב בשם הג"א אם כתב הסופר ועד חתם אפי' אם נמסר לפני עדים וניתן הגט לא תנשא ועיין ס"ס מ"ש, לפ"ז נראה אם אין מוכח בגט מי הוא המגרש כגון דיש כאן שני יב"ש וע"מ מעידי' מי הוא המגרש כשר הגט אפילו לא נשאת דהא ר"מ ס"ל ע"ח כרתי הוא ס"ל דבעינן שיהא מוכח בתוך הגט מי הוא המגרש ושני דיני' אלו תלי' זה בזה ולדידן אם ניתן הגט בלא ע"ח כשר וכן הוא כשאין מוכח בתוכו ולא כתשובת ש"י סי' ל"ו דפסק דלא תנשא בו אלא אם נשאת לא תצא כמשמעות הג"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Invalid.</b> According to the Rambam’s approach, signing witnesses alone suffice. If there are no delivery witnesses, there is a distinction: if it is proven that the signed witnesses do not refer to this get, the get is void; but if it is not proven, such as where there is an added greeting, or where he left part of the get and wrote it on the second page, it is a doubtful get. Even if he did not write the vav of "vesha'alu," one can still say the signatures may refer to the get, and it is only doubtful. What is written nearby that if they signed at the top of the get and similar cases are invalid is not precise; in such a case it is void, because it is proven they did not sign on this get. According to this, what is written in seif 6 that if there are delivery witnesses it is valid, even when they signed at the top and it is proven they did not sign on the get, must mean bedieved after the get was given; lechatchilah it should not be done, because we require both delivery and signing witnesses. We learn from this that a get with no signing witnesses at all, given before delivery witnesses, is valid even if she has not remarried. Otherwise the term "valid" would not apply. So wrote the Perishah in siman 133, and so it implies in the gloss to seif 8. Darkei Moshe at the end of this siman, however, cites Hagahot Ashri that if the scribe wrote and one witness signed, even if it was delivered before witnesses and given, she should not marry. See the end of the siman. It follows that if the get itself does not prove who is divorcing, such as where there are two Yosef ben Shimon, but the delivery witnesses testify who the divorcing man is, the get is valid even if she has not remarried. Just as according to Rabbi Meir, who holds signing witnesses effect the divorce, the identity must be evident within the get, these two laws depend on each other. For us, if the get was given without signing witnesses it is valid; likewise where the identity is not evident within it. This is unlike Responsa Shevut Yaakov, who ruled she should not marry with it, though if she married she need not leave.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he attached two hides, etc.</b> The same applies if he sewed them. Darkei Moshe. This is invalid even if there are delivery witnesses, for even though it is not two gittin, it is invalid because Scripture says "a book," one book and not two books. Although "book" comes to teach the narration of matters, we also learn one book and not two books. So wrote Levush. This requires study: why should attachment not help? In Menachot 34, Rabbi Yehudah expounds that tefillin must be one sign outside and one inside, and therefore if tefillin were written on two parchments they are invalid; but if he attached them, they are valid. Rabbi Yosi validates two parchments because he does not expound this, but we still learn that attachment helps even where the Torah requires one sign. Tosafot and Agudah write that if tefillin or mezuzot were written on two parchments and sewn together, they are valid, even if sewn after writing, and all the more if sewn or attached before writing. Tosafot in Sotah invalidated only where sewn or attached after writing, but if sewn before writing it is valid, and so hold the Rambam and Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 288. One cannot prove from an attached shofar, which is invalid because the Torah said one shofar, that attachment never helps, for Tosafot in Sotah write that shofar is different because the addition is recognizable. See Orach Chaim, siman 32, Bach. This requires study.

<b>And he likewise said, etc.</b> Meaning that he wrote, "So-and-so divorced so-and-so," and wrote the entire get formula for each one. The Derishah writes that he need not write the entire formula for each; rather, for each he writes until "to you," and at the end finishes the whole formula. Then it is not two women leaving with one get, because he wrote for each one the essential get, and the rest of the formula is only additional wording. It appears to me that this law depends on what constitutes the toref of the get. Everything that is part of the toref must be written for each one. I wrote above that "you are hereby permitted," etc., is also the toref of the get; if so, he must write the whole get for each one. If he wrote one formula for all of them, the get is void, because biblically it is two women leaving with one get. So wrote the Tur in the name of Ramah. The Rambam’s words can be explained this way, and this is the Mechaber’s language. So explained Rashach. The Bach’s explanation is not necessary.

<b>והוא שיכירו ל' הגט</b>. הטור פסק אפי' אין מכירין הלשון אלא הקורא' מתרג' לפניה' מהני וכ"כ המגיד אלא הב"י מפרש דברי הרמב"ם שיכירו לשון הגט ואז כשיבינו לשון הגט אינו אלא גילוי מילתא בעלמא משא"כ כשאינם מכירים לשון הגט וראיה מירושלמי דתניא הדייני' חותמי' אף על פי שאין יודעי' לקרות והעדי'  אין חותמי' אא"כ דיודעי' לקרות וס"ל דאיירי הירושלמי כשקוראי' בפניה' דאל"כ איך יחתמו הדייני' אם אין קוראי' בפניה' אלא ע"כ דאיירי בקוראי' בפניה' ומ"מ צריכי' לידע לקרות ויש לחדות דירושלמי איירי בדייני קיום אז א"צ הדייני' לקרות השטר מה שכתוב בו כי אין מעידים על גוף השטר רק מקיימי חתימות העדי' בזה איירי הירושלמי וקאמר העדי' אין חותמים אלא אם כן דיודעי' לקרות והיינו כשאין קוראי' בפניהם והדיינים חותמים אף על פי שא"י לקרות כיון דאין צריך לידע מה שכתוב בשטר ומ"ש הרמב"ם פ"ד  והוא שיודעין לשון הכת' כמ"ש בסעי' י"ד כתבתי בסמוך דשם איירי כשקוראי' בעצמם אבל אם קוראי' בפניה' י"ל אפילו א"י הלשון אלא הקורא מתרגם בפניה' נמי מהני, ועיין בש"ך ר"ס מ"ה שם פוסק בדיעבד מהני אפילו לא ידעו לשון הגט אם קוראי' בפניהם ומתרגם להם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Provided they recognize the language of the get.</b> The Tur rules that even if they do not recognize the language, but the reader translates before them, it helps; so wrote the Maggid Mishneh. Beit Yosef, however, explains the Rambam as requiring that they recognize the language of the get, and then, once they understand the language, the reading is only revealing the matter. But if they do not recognize the language, it does not help. He proves this from the Yerushalmi, which teaches that judges may sign even though they cannot read, while witnesses may not sign unless they can read. He assumes the Yerushalmi speaks where others read before them; otherwise how could judges sign? Nevertheless, witnesses must know how to read. One can reject this, because the Yerushalmi speaks of judges validating signatures, who need not read the shtar because they testify only to the signatures. It says witnesses may sign only if they know how to read, meaning where no one reads before them. The Rambam’s statement that they must know the language of the writing, as in seif 14, was explained above as referring to where they read by themselves. But if others read before them, one can say that even if they do not know the language and the reader translates for them, it helps. See the Shach at the beginning of siman 45, who rules that bedieved it helps even if they did not know the language of the get, if it was read and translated before them.

<b>רושמי' להם</b>. דוקא בגט מקילין בזה ורושמי' להם אבל לא בשאר שטרו' כמ"ש ברמב"ם פכ"ד ה' מלוה ובפ"א הלכות גירושין כתב הרמב"ם דמקילין בגט משום תקנות עגונות הואיל חתימת העדי' בגט מדבריה' משמע דוקא כשנמסר לפני ע"מ אז מקילין ולשיטות הפוסקי' דבעינן ע"מ וע"ח אף על פי דנמסר בע"מ מ"מ אם  רשמו בדבר דהוי כתב הוי כאלו לא חתמו כלל ואין מגרשין לכתחלה אבל בדיעבד בכל מה שנרשם כשר לא יהא דלא חתמו כלל דקי"ל אם ניתן לה הגט כשר כמ"ש לעיל אלא מ"ש הטור בשם הרמ"ה אם רושמי' במיא דאברא אפי' בדיעבד פסול לכאורה קשה דהא לדעת הטור צריך למסור לפני ע"מ ואם לא היה ע"מ הגט בטל כמ"ש בסי' קל"ג אם כן ע"כ איירי כאן שהיה ע"מ קשה למה בדיעבד פסול למה גרע טפי מגט שאין ע"ח כלל עליו ואפשר דגרע טפי משום דגזרי' חתימה אטו כתיבה כמ"ש בתוספות ריש גיטין עיין בת"ה סימן רל"ד, ועיין סימן קנ"ה שם מבואר במה שכותבי' הגט ואם רושמי' בו החתימות פסול דהוי כאלו לא חתמו כלל, וברמב"ם פכ"ד משמע דפי' רושמי' היינו מקרעי' בהקלף החתימות ועדים ממלאים דיו אלא קשה הא ס"ל להרמב"ם הקורע על העור הוי כתב כמ"ש בסימן קכ"ה א"כ הוי כתב ע"ג כת' וצ"ל דס"ל דמקרעי' מקצת חתימה היינו מכל אות מקצת או שמרחיבי' הקריעה והם חותמי' בו כמ"ש בתוס', ומכאן ראיה קצת לדברי הת"ה בחק תוכות אם לא נגמר האות כשר ודיו ע"ג דיו לרמב"ם אם כותב הגט כך לא הוי גט כלל ואינו פוסל לכהונה כמ"ש בפ"ג ויתבאר בסי' אח"ז והרמ"ה ס"ל דאינו גט ופוסל לכהונה לכן כתב אם רשם להם במיא דאברא או בעפצא אינו גט ופוסל לכהונה משום דהוי כדיו ע"ג דיו ולא כהב"י דמדמה לדיו ע"ג סקרא לכן הקשה על הרמ"ה מה שהקשה וליתא אלא ס"ל להרמ"ה דהוי כדיו ע"ג דיו וכן לענין שבת קי"ל דיו ע"ג דיו פטור אבל אסור ולרשב"א שהביא בסי' קל"א הוי ספק גט ונ"מ מזה להפוסקי' דס"ל ע"ח בלא ע"מ כשר אם ליכא ע"מ וע"ח חתמו בכה"ג הגט בטל אם כותב דיו ע"ג דיו אבל לדידן דבעינן ע"מ לעולם הגט אינו בטל אם נמסר לפני עדים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They mark for them.</b> Specifically with a get they were lenient and mark for them, but not with other documents, as written by the Rambam in chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh. In chapter 1 of Hilchot Gerushin the Rambam writes that they were lenient with a get because of agunot, since signing witnesses on a get are rabbinic. This implies specifically where it was delivered before delivery witnesses. According to decisors who require both delivery and signing witnesses, even if it was delivered before delivery witnesses, if they marked with something considered writing, it is as though they did not sign at all, and one should not divorce with it lechatchilah. Bedieved, any marking is valid, for it is no worse than having no signatures at all, where if the get was given it is valid. The Tur cites Ramah that if they mark with lead-water, it is invalid even bedieved. At first glance this is difficult, because according to the Tur delivery witnesses are required and without them the get is void; if so, the case must be where there were delivery witnesses, and why is it worse bedieved than a get with no signing witnesses? Perhaps it is worse because we decree signing because of writing, as Tosafot write at the beginning of Gittin. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 234. In siman 155 it is explained regarding the writing of the get that if the signatures are marked, it is invalid as though they did not sign.

The Rambam in chapter 24 implies that "marking" means cutting the signatures into the parchment and the witnesses fill them with ink. This is difficult, because the Rambam holds that engraving on skin is writing, as written in siman 125, so it is writing over writing. One must say they cut only part of the signature, part of each letter, or widen the cut and the witnesses sign in it, as Tosafot write. From here there is some proof to Terumat HaDeshen’s view regarding chak tochot: if the letter was not completed, it is valid. Ink over ink according to the Rambam, if one writes a get that way, is not a get at all and does not disqualify for kehunah. Ramah holds it is not a get but disqualifies for kehunah; therefore if he marked for them with lead-water or gall-water, it is not a get and disqualifies for kehunah, because it is like ink over ink, not like the Beit Yosef who compared it to ink over red paint. According to the Rashba cited in siman 131, it is a doubtful get. The practical difference is for decisors who hold signing witnesses without delivery witnesses are valid: if there are no delivery witnesses and the signing witnesses signed in this manner, the get is void if it is ink over ink. But according to us, who require delivery witnesses, the get is never void if it was delivered before witnesses.

<b>שלא לחתום הסופר לעד</b>. בש"ס מבואר הטעם דחיישינן שמא יאמר הבעל לשנים אמרו לסופר ליכתוב ולפלוני ופלוני ויחתמו ומחמת כסופא דסופר חיישי ומחתמי חד מהנך סהדי וסופר בהדייהו או שיאמר לשנים שיאמרו לסופר ליכתוב ואתם חתמו ומחמת כיסופא יאמרו לסופר שיחתום הוא אף על גב דקי"ל אומר אמרו פסול  א"כ לא יכתוב הסופר אם לא שמע מהבעל מ"מ אינו פסול אלא מחמת ספק חיישינן כאן וכאן לחומרא כמ"ש באריכות בפוסקי' נשמע אומר אמרו וחתם סופר ועד אלו שני דיני' סותרי' זה את זה ואנן חיישינן בזה ובזה לחומרא, עולה מזה מי שנתן שני גיטין א' ע"י אומר אמרו וא' ע"י חתם סופר ועד ממ"נ חד מהם כשר ה"ה אם אמר לג' שיעשו כרצונם או שיכתבו ויחתמו הגט או שיאמרו לאחרי' שיחתמו ויכתבו ומת א' מאלו ג' יש תקנה הנ"ל וכשר ממ"נ מיהו בש"ס מבואר אם אמר לשנים אמרו לסופר ויכתוב ופ' ופ' ויחתמו איכא חשש אחר שמא תשכיר עדי' ויאמרו כן בשם הבעל לפ"ז בכה"ג לא שייך ממ"נ הנ"ל ואם יתן גט א' חתם סופר ועד וא' ע"י אומר אמרו כהנ"ל י"ל שמא שניהם פסולים היינו אומר אמרו לסופר ולעדי' פסול משום שמא תשכיר העדי' וחתם סופר ועד פסול משו' אומר אמרו לסופר ויחתום ואתם חתמו דאז לא שייך שתשכיר כשר ולא שייך ממ"נ הנ"ל אלא אם גירש ע"י חתם הסופר והעד וא' ע"י אמרו לסופר ויכתוב ואתם חתמו, והיכא דאמר לג' שיכתבו ויחתמו ומת א' מהם י"ל במקו' עיגון ודחק כשר שלא יחתמו כלל ויתנו לה לפני עדים דהא לשיטות הרי"ף והרמב"ם כשר אף לדידן כתבתי ר"ס אם ניתן הגט בלא ע"ח כשר כן הוא במקו' דחק ועיגון וכן משמע ר"ס ק"מ מ"ש הטור בשם העיטור וע"ש בב"ח, ואם שני עדים חתמו וגם הסופר לדעת הרא"ש כשר בדיעבד, ולהר"ן ס"פ התקבל אפילו לכתחלה כשר והמרדכי כמ"ש כתב דפסול וכ"כ פ' המגרש והב"י כ' היכא דא"א בגט אחר כשר, והריב"ש בתשובה סי' ר"ה פסק בחתם סופר וע"א אם נשאת לא תצא, ואם הובא גט ממקו' דמכשירי' חתום סופר ועד למקום דפוסלי' עיין תשובת רשב"א סי' אלף ק"מ, ואם העדי' אמרו לסופר שיחתו' את שמם עיין בהג"ה פ"ב, והסופר יכול להיות א' מעידי מסירה פרישה סימן קמ"א ס"ק ל"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That the scribe should not sign as a witness.</b> The Talmud explains the reason: we are concerned that perhaps the husband will tell two people, "Tell the scribe to write, and so-and-so and so-and-so shall sign," and because of the scribe’s embarrassment they will have one of those witnesses and the scribe sign together; or he will tell two people to tell the scribe to write and "you sign," and because of embarrassment they will tell the scribe to sign too. Although we hold that "tell others" is invalid, so the scribe should not write unless he heard from the husband, nevertheless that invalidity is because of doubt, and we are stringent in both directions. It follows that "tell others" and "scribe plus one witness signed" are two contradictory concerns, and we are stringent for both.

If someone gave two gittin, one through "tell others" and one signed by the scribe and one witness, one of them is valid whichever way you look at it. Likewise, if he told three people to do as they wished, either write and sign the get themselves or tell others to write and sign, and one of the three died, there is the above remedy, and it is valid whichever way you look at it. However, the Talmud explains that if he said to two, "Tell the scribe to write and so-and-so and so-and-so to sign," there is another concern, that she may hire witnesses who will say this in the husband’s name. According to that, the above whichever-way remedy does not apply in such a case, for perhaps both gittin are invalid. If he said to three to write and sign and one died, in a case of igun and pressure one can say it is valid for them not to sign at all and give it before witnesses, because according to the Rif and Rambam it is valid, and even according to us I wrote at the beginning of the siman that if the get was given without signing witnesses it is valid in a pressing case of igun. If two witnesses signed and also the scribe, according to the Rosh it is valid bedieved; according to the Ran it is valid even lechatchilah, while the Mordechai writes it is invalid. Beit Yosef writes that where no other get is possible, it is valid. Rivash rules that with a scribe and one witness, if she remarried she need not leave. If a get was brought from a place that validates scribe-plus-one-witness to a place that invalidates, see Responsa Rashba. If the witnesses told the scribe to sign their names, see the gloss. The scribe may be one of the delivery witnesses. Perishah, siman 141.

<b>אפ"ה פסול</b>. כ' הרא"ש והר"ן הלכתא כת"ק דגט פסול דגזרינן אטו שמות שאינן מובהקי' ואתי לסמוך עלייהו, והא דלא כתב הטעם דהוי כאלו אין עדים חתומי' על גט זה דהא החתומי' הם עכו"ם וקי"ל דצריך להיות ע"ח גם ע"מ משום מהאי טעמא אם כבר ניתן הגט כשר כמ"ש לעיל אבל מהאי טעמא דגזרינן אטו שמות שאינו מובהקי' והוי כמזויף בתוכו ופסול אפילו אם כבר ניתן ולר"ח ולראב"ד הלכה כר"ש בשמו' מובהקי' הגט כשר מיהו דוקא בידוע דהיה ע"מ כי בחתימות הללו אין אומרי' בודאי היה שם ע"מ ונ"מ מפלוגת' זו הפירוש של הסוגיא שם אם בא לפנינו גט והעדי' החתומי' עליו שמותם כשמות עכו"ם כשר לשיטות הראב"ד איירי שמות מובהקי' הם אמרינן אלו החתומי' הם כותים ומ"מ הגט כשר והיינו בע"מ, לשיטות הגאוני' כי אמרי' אלו חתומים עכו"ם הם, ולשיטות הרא"ש אמרינן החתומי' הם ישראלי' כיון דשמותם כשמות עכו"ם לא היה חותמי' אותם אלא ודאי ישראלי' הם לפי זה נראה אף על גב המחבר  יפה פסק בדין זה בידוע שהם כותי' לחומרא בשמות מובהקי' דהגט פסול מ"מ בסיפא אם גט הבא לפנינו ושמות העדי' כשמות העכו"ם יש לחוש לשיטת הראב"ד שמא עכו"ם הם, ואם ניתן בע"מ כשר ממ"נ דהא לראב"ד מבואר בש"ס דכשר ולשיטות הרא"ש דס"ל אפילו בשמות מובהקי' פסול מ"מ בכה"ג אמרינן ישראלי' הם וכשר, מיהו נ"מ דלא אמרינן בודאי היה שם ע"מ לשיטות הגאוני' והמחבר כתב תנשא בו דודאי ישראלי' הם משמע אפילו אם א"י אם היה ע"מ מכשיר היינו כשיטות הרא"ש אבל לדעת הראב"ד פסול בכה"ג דליכא ע"ח דהא עכו"ם הם וחיישינן שמא לא היה ע"מ גם הרשב"א והר"ן נראה דס"ל בפי' הסוגיא כראב"ד ונ"ל להחמיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless it is invalid.</b> The Rosh and Ran write that the halacha follows the first Tanna, that the get is invalid, because we decree lest one rely on non-distinct gentile names. They do not give the reason that it is as though no witnesses are signed on this get, since the signatures are gentiles and we hold both signing and delivery witnesses are needed, because for that reason alone, if the get was already given, it would be valid as written above. Rather, because of the decree regarding non-distinct names it is like forgery within the get and invalid even if already given. According to Rabbenu Chananel and the Ra'avad, the halacha follows Rabbi Shimon that with distinct gentile names the get is valid, but this is only when it is known there were delivery witnesses, because from these signatures we do not presume there were certainly delivery witnesses. A practical difference in the explanations is a get that comes before us with the witnesses’ names like gentile names. According to the Ra'avad, distinct names mean we say the signers are gentiles and the get is nevertheless valid with delivery witnesses. According to the Geonim, we also say they are gentiles. According to the Rosh, we say the signers are Jews, because one would not have gentiles sign with such names; they must be Jews. Therefore, although the Mechaber ruled stringently where it is known they are gentiles that even distinct names invalidate, nevertheless at the end, where a get comes before us and the witnesses’ names are like gentile names, one should be concerned for the Ra'avad’s view that perhaps they are gentiles. If it was given before delivery witnesses, it is valid whichever way you look at it. However, we do not say there were certainly delivery witnesses according to the Geonim. The Mechaber wrote she may marry with it because they are certainly Jews, implying even if we do not know whether there were delivery witnesses. That follows the Rosh. According to the Ra'avad it is invalid in such a case, because there are no signing witnesses and we are concerned there may not have been delivery witnesses. The Rashba and Ran also appear to explain the sugya like the Ra'avad; therefore it appears one should be stringent.

<b>אינו גט</b>. המחבר פסק כרמב"ם דפסק כרב כ' סופר וע"א חתו' גרע טפי מכת"י ומשמע דאין חילוק בין אם הסופר הוא סופר מובהק ובין אם אינו סופר מובהק והיינו לשיטות הרמב"ם דס"ל ע"ח בלא ע"מ מהני איירי בדליכא **** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is not a get.</b> The Mechaber rules like the Rambam, who rules like Rav, that scribe’s writing with one witness signed is worse than the husband’s handwriting. It implies no distinction whether the scribe is expert or not. According to the Rambam, who holds signing witnesses without delivery witnesses help, the case is where there are no delivery witnesses and no two signing witnesses, only the scribe’s writing and one witness signed; it is not a get at all and does not disqualify for kehunah. However, Rivash at the end of siman 413 writes in the name of the Itur and Rabbenu Chananel that even according to Rav, an expert scribe is no worse than the husband’s handwriting, with practical effect for monetary law as in Choshen Mishpat, siman 46. For us, who hold delivery witnesses are required, this law has little practical effect: if there are no delivery witnesses, even two signed witnesses would leave the get void; if there are delivery witnesses, then even without signing witnesses, once the get was given it is valid. But if there are delivery witnesses and the husband wrote the get in his own handwriting, it may be like signing witnesses and valid lechatchilah. Likewise, if a get signed before us comes and we do not know whether there were delivery witnesses, we presume there were delivery witnesses. If a get has only the husband’s handwriting and she remarried with it, she need not leave, because bedieved we presume delivery witnesses were present. But if the scribe wrote the get and one witness signed, she must leave; this is what the Rosh means by the practical difference for us regarding the validity of signing witnesses according to Rabbi Elazar as according to Rabbi Meir.

If there were delivery witnesses and only one signing witness, it is as though no witness signed, and it is not valid to give lechatchilah, but if already given it is valid. If it is the writing of an expert scribe, the Itur holds it is like the husband’s handwriting, and for us the practical difference is that she need not leave; according to the Rambam, even bedieved it is not like signing witnesses. The Tur’s words can also be resolved by saying that all the amoraim at the end of Perek HaMitkabel hold that scribe-plus-one-witness is invalid, as the Rosh wrote in Perek HaMegarish in the name of Ri. Therefore they must hold like Shmuel, who establishes the Mishnah as the scribe wrote but did not sign, not like Rav and Rabbi Yirmiyah, who explain it as the scribe signed. Even according to Rav Ashi, who excludes "tell others," one need not say the Mishnah speaks of the scribe signing; he may hold that the scribe’s writing is also valid. Therefore the ruling of "she need not leave" can apply even with a non-expert scribe.
