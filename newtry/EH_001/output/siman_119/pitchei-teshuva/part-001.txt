<b>ואם הודיע' כו'. </b> עי' בס' גט פשוט סק"ג מ"ש ליישב דלא תיקשי מהא דאמר בסוף פ"ב דנדרים וברותי מכם הפושעים והמורדים בי אמר ר"ל אלו בני ט' מדות כו' ע"ש באורך. ועי' במל"מ פכ"א מהא"ב סוף דין כ"ח שכ' דמ"ש הרב ג"פ דבני גרושת הלב לא מקרי אלא כשאינו מודיעה אבל אם מודיעה שרי (וכן מבורר מדברי הפרישה שהביא הב"ש כאן סק"א ולעיל סי' כ"ה סק"ט) דברי תימה הם שהרי טעמא דאיסורא כ' הרא"ש ספ"ב דנדרים כפי דמהרהר באחרת א"כ אף אם הודיעה שייך האי טעמא אך נ"ל דע"כ לא אמרינן כשגמר בלבו לגרשה דאסיר לבא עליה אלא כשרוצה לקחת אשה אחרת דאז שייך טעמא דמהרהר באחרת אבל כשאינו רוצה לקחת אשה אחרת שרי וא"כ רב ור"נ שכבר היה להם אשה בעירם וכשהלכו לעיר אחרת רצו לקחת אשה לימים שיתעכבו שם אף שגמרו בלבם להוציא לאחר זה הזמן מ"מ לא היו מהרהרין בנשותיהן ואף מרן ז"ל שכ' סתם ואם הידיעה מותר בכה"ג קא מיירי עכ"ל. וע"ש עוד דמה דמשמע מדברי הרב ג"פ דס"ל דאיסורא דבני גרושת הלב לא הוי אלא אם תתעבר. אבל אם אינה מתעברת לא (גם הב"ש סק"א כתב וז"ל גם י"ל בני תשע מדות היינו הבנים פגומים כו') ל"נ היינו דוקא למאן דסבר דקרא דוברותי מכם המורדים והפושעים קאי אבנים אבל לסברת הראב"ד שהביא הב"י א"ח סי' ר"מ דקרא קאי לבועלים בא מט' מדות שהם מורדים ופושעים אף שלא תתעבר אסור ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he informed her, etc.</b> See Get Pashut, s.k. 3, who resolves why this is not difficult from the statement at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim, "and I will purge from you the rebels and sinners against Me," which Reish Lakish explains as referring to children born from nine improper traits. See there at length. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 21 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, end of law 28, who writes that what Get Pashut says, that children of a woman divorced in the heart are so called only when he does not inform her, but if he informs her it is permitted, is astonishing. The Rosh at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim writes that the reason for the prohibition is that he is thinking about another woman; if so, even if he informs her, the reason applies. Mishneh LaMelech therefore explains that when one has decided in his heart to divorce her, it is forbidden to have relations with her only where he wants to take another woman, for then the reason of thinking about another applies. But where he does not want to take another woman, it is permitted. Thus Rav and Rav Nachman, who already had wives in their own cities and when they traveled wanted to take a woman for the days they would be delayed there, even though they had decided to divorce after that time, were not thinking about their wives while with another. The Mechaber, who wrote generally that if he informed her it is permitted, speaks of such a case. He further writes that what appears from Get Pashut, that the prohibition of children of a woman divorced in the heart applies only if she becomes pregnant, is not correct according to the Ra'avad’s view brought by Beit Yosef, Orach Chaim, siman 240, that the verse refers to those who have relations with one of the nine traits; according to that, even if she does not become pregnant it is forbidden.

<b>A matter of ervah.</b> See Baer Heitev. Regarding what he wrote in the name of Rashdam, that this is specifically with a married woman, etc., see Mishneh LaMelekh, chapter 10 of Hilkhot Gerushin law 21, who wrote that according to Rashdam, with an arusah, even if he did not find another more beautiful than her, he may divorce her. <b>יכול לגרשה בלא דעתה. </b> עיין בתשו' ברית אברהם סי' ק"ט שכתב דמדברי הרא"ש (פ"ב דגיטין סי' ה') בענין גט מאוחר יש ללמוד דין מחודש דהבעל אינו יכול לגרש בע"כ דהאשה על תנאי כגון מהיום אם מתי תוך ב' שנים וכה"ג דהא כשמגרש על תנאי הדין דלא תתיחד עמו (כדלקמן סי' קמ"ח ס"ב וא"כ מפסדת עונתה והיא אינה מתרצת לכך רק או שיגרשה לגמרי או שתשב עמו ואין יכול לכוף את אשתו לגרש ע"ת שתהא אגיד ביה מכ"ש ממ"ש הרא"ש שם דא"י לגרשה בגט מאוחר בע"כ להראב"ד ע"ש באורך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He may divorce her without her consent.</b> See Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 109, who wrote that from the words of the Rosh, Gittin chapter 2 siman 5, regarding a postdated Get, one can derive a new law: a husband cannot divorce a woman against her will conditionally, such as, from today if I die within two years, and the like. For when he divorces conditionally, the law is that he may not seclude himself with her, as below in siman 148 seif 2, and if so she loses her conjugal rights. She does not consent to that; rather, he must either divorce her completely or remain with her. He cannot force his wife to accept a conditional divorce while she remains bound to him, all the more so from what the Rosh wrote there, that according to Raavad he cannot divorce her against her will with a postdated Get. See there at length.

<b>הכתובה </b> עבה"ט מ"ש ופר"ח הקשה מש"ס דיבמות כו' גם מפ' הניזקין כו' וכבר קדמוהו בראיית אלו בתשובת ח"ג סי' רנ"ג והובא ג"כ בתשו' ברית אברהם סי' צ"א אות ה' ע"ש עוד. ומ"ש הבה"ט ואני אומר דאין למידין מן ההגדות וכ"כ התוי"ט כו' לאו קושיא היא על הפר"ח כי הפר"ח בעצמו בס' מים חיים השיג על התוי"ט שם וכתב דהך כללא דאין למידין ממדרשות הוא רק לענין פסק הלכה כדברי מי היכא דלא מפרש בש"ס ההלכתא אבל דינא דלא איתפרש בש"ס מצינו כמה פעמים דלמדין ממדרשות והובא דבריו בגליון המשניות פ"ה דברכות בתוס' רע"ק אית ל"ו. גם בס' באר וניקב דחה דברי הבה"ט בזה מטעם דהא דאין למידין היינו אם סותר לדברי התלמיד וגם יש לחלק דאם הוקבעה בש"ס דילן שפיר למידין ע"ש. ועיין בס' נו"ב תניינא חיו"ד ס' קס"א מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The ketubah.</b> See Ba'er Heitev, who wrote that Pri Chadash challenged this from the Talmud in Yevamot, etc., and also from Perek HaNizakin, etc. He was already preceded in bringing these proofs by Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 253, and this is also brought in Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 91, letter 5; see there further. As for what Ba'er Heitev wrote, "I say that we do not derive law from aggadot," and that Tosefot Yom Tov wrote similarly, etc., this is not a difficulty against Pri Chadash. Pri Chadash himself, in his work Mayim Chayim, objected to the Tosefot Yom Tov there and wrote that this principle, that one does not derive law from midrashim, applies only regarding deciding the halacha according to one view where the Talmud does not explicitly state the halacha. But with a law not explained in the Talmud, we find many times that we do derive from midrashim. His words are brought in the margin of the Mishnayot, Berachot, chapter 5, in the additions of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, letter 36. Sefer Ba'er VeNekev also rejected the words of Ba'er Heitev in this matter, because the rule that we do not derive applies when it contradicts the words of the Talmud. One can also distinguish that if the matter was established in our Talmud, we certainly derive from it. See there. See also Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh Deah, siman 161, on this matter.

<b>He placed a ban not to divorce.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 75, regarding one who divorced his wife against her will through an agent: soldiers seized the woman in the street, the agent threw the get into her lap, and she cried out with no one to save her, did not consent, and threw the get away. He ruled that since there is a grave transgression of violating the cherem of Rabbenu Gershom by divorcing against her will, there is no agency for a transgression, and the agency act is entirely void. She was not divorced through the husband’s agency, there is no divorce, she may not marry, and the husband remains her husband and is obligated in all marital obligations. He is also forbidden to marry another woman because of the cherem against marrying two wives. Many sages challenged him, saying that "there is no agency for a transgression" means only that the sender is not liable, but the act itself is not void. Noda BiYehudah defended his ruling at length, from siman 75 through siman 82.

The note then discusses whether this rule that the agency itself is void is found in Mishneh LaMelech. It points out that Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 3 of Hilchot Geneivah, law 6, implies that even where the Torah included agency for slaughter and sale, if he made an agent to slaughter on Shabbat the sender is exempt from four- and fivefold payment, because the agency for the slaughter is not effective. This supports the position that the agency is void. Maharit and Machaneh Ephraim also were uncertain whether in cases of transgressive agency the act itself is void.

See Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, simanim 111, 112, and 135, and what I wrote below, siman 141, seif 36, s.k. 38. Noda BiYehudah also proposed a new law based on the Rosh in Gittin 11 regarding one who seizes for a creditor: even though generally a person’s agent is like himself, he cannot appoint an agent in a way that harms others. Therefore, even before Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, and under Talmudic law where a man could divorce against his wife’s will, he cannot divorce her against her will through an agent when the get is a detriment to her. Several scholars objected from explicit passages about agency for divorce against her will. He answered that he agrees agency for delivery can exist against her will where the get is not a detriment, such as an arusah who has not yet acquired benefits from the husband, or a woman already forbidden to kehunah, or a woman who committed zenut and thereby lost her ketubah. But where the get is a detriment, he cannot appoint an agent to harm her.

<b>Not to divorce.</b> See Beit Shmuel below, s.k. 12, who wrote that if Rabbenu Gershom’s cherem is comparable to an enactment of Chazal, then nowadays even if he transgressed and divorced, she is not divorced. But from the Rama’s gloss here, which says that if he transgressed and divorced her against her will nowadays and she remarried, the man is no longer called a transgressor, it is explicit not like this; she may marry another through this get. This is also clear from the Beit Shmuel himself above, simanim 77 and 79, where he compares the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom to the prohibition of divorcing a first wife without cause; if one transgressed and divorced a first wife, she is divorced, and so too here.

Beit Meir also writes that the Rama is not like the Beit Shmuel. He cites Maharshal, whose responsum implies that nevertheless the divorce did not help him to permit him to marry another. Beit Meir notes that the Rama wrote only that after she remarries he is no longer called a transgressor, which implies that before she remarries he may still remain under the ban. In the responsa at the end of Beit Meir, siman 4, he discusses a case of a man who divorced his wife by trickery against her will and concludes that although she may be divorced and permitted to marry, the husband may still be obligated to return and remarry her or remain in violation until she marries someone else. He compares this to the rule in Temurah that whatever the Torah says not to do, if one does it, it may not take effect for purposes of releasing the transgressor from the obligation. Therefore, beit din should rule that he continues to violate the cherem each day until he restores the situation or she marries another. Since she remains bound to him in this respect, he may also remain within the prohibition of marrying two wives, and no one should give him another wife because of the possible cherem.

Get Pashut, s.k. 24, writes that bedieved if he divorced her against her will, perhaps the get is not valid even bedieved, but he notes that the wording of the enactment quoted in siman 102 does not clearly say this. It appears even Maharam Mintz did not decide to void the get absolutely, but treated it as doubtful. See there also whether a woman who consented at the beginning of writing the get and then retracted must pay the expenses; he writes she need not pay, and he caused his own loss. Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 129, cites Knesset HaGedolah, who writes simply that if he divorced her against her will, although he violated the cherem, the get is a get. The phrase in Maharam Mintz that "the get is nothing" means regarding the husband’s obligations to her, not that the get is halachically void.
