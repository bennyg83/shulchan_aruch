(א) <b>One who divorced, etc.</b> This is Rambam's language. Maggid Mishneh wrote that this is also the Gemara's version. But our version is: she may not marry in his neighborhood, specifically when she remarried; and with a kohen, even if she is unmarried. Tosafot explained that a neighborhood is smaller than an alley, and we are more stringent with a kohen because she is unmarried and the matter is light for him. But the Rosh, Rashba, Ran, and other poskim explained that a neighborhood is larger than an alley, and we are more stringent with a married woman. Ran wrote that although with an Israelite, as long as she is unmarried, she is permitted even in an alley, nevertheless in a courtyard it is forbidden. This is what it says there: come and hear, "if it was a courtyard," etc.; what is this courtyard doing here? Rather, it is in such a case. But Ritva wrote that even in a courtyard it is permitted, and this is what it says, "she may not marry," without explaining residence without marriage. He also infers this from Rashi's language there, s.v. "with the divorcee of a kohen," which implies specifically with a kohen.
