<b>She should not remain under him, etc.</b> For it is written, "Do not devise evil against your fellow." Chelkat Mechokek wrote that perhaps in such a case, where she is already married to him, it does not help if he informs her, for what can she do? He also infers from the language "and she serves him" that the prohibition is specifically when he has relations with her while intending to divorce her. In Perishah, siman 25, he wrote that after marriage it does not help if he informs her, and it is forbidden because of the category of children of the nine defective unions, namely children of one whose husband intended divorce in his heart. At first glance his explanation is difficult: if she is forbidden to him because of "divorce in the heart," why did Rav Mesharshya ask Rava what the law is if his heart is to divorce her while she remains under him? Did he not know the teaching of the nine defective unions? And Rava answered that it is forbidden because of "Do not devise evil." If so, how does the teaching of the nine defective unions apply? From this it appears that the nine defective unions speak when he has relations with her, whereas here, where he has not had relations, it is forbidden only because of "Do not devise evil," unlike Chelkat Mechokek, who wrote that here it speaks specifically where he has relations with her. However, Perishah's words are not forced. One may say that the nine defective unions, including divorce in the heart, speak when at the time he has relations with her his intent is to divorce her; here it speaks where his intent is to divorce her at a fixed later time, and at the time he has relations with her he does not intend to divorce her, yet it is still forbidden because of "Do not devise evil." If he has not had relations with her, one can say there is no prohibition. One may also say that the nine defective unions refer to the children being blemished, while here he transgresses "Do not devise evil." Chelkat Mechokek's inference from the language "and she serves him" is not compelling, for the Tur did not write "and she serves him." At first glance one could distinguish further: the child of a woman divorced in the heart refers to those whom he is compelled to divorce, as implied by the Tur's language there. However, we have not found such a distinction, and the Tur's words there are explaining the Rosh in Nedarim, who wrote that he does not hate her and divorce her; the Tur wrote that this refers to those whom he is compelled to divorce, for otherwise how could the case occur. But regarding children of one divorced in the heart there is no distinction. I later found that Baal HaTurim on Parashat Va'etchanan explained likewise: children of a woman divorced in the heart means that he does not hate her, but he is among those compelled to divorce.

(א) <b>He should not divorce, etc.</b> The Tur wrote: "When a man takes a wife," etc. Beit Hillel hold that even if she burned his dish he may divorce her, for he expounds the verse this way: "he found in her ervat davar" means either ervah or a matter in which she sinned against him, etc. In what case is this said? With a second match. But with a first match one should not hasten to divorce her, for R. Eliezer said: whoever divorces his first wife, the altar sheds tears. This implies that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree regarding a second match; there Beit Hillel hold that it is permitted to divorce because of a "matter." But with a first match Beit Hillel agree that it is forbidden to divorce because of a "matter." The verse "ervat davar" speaks of a second match, and then it is permitted to divorce because of ervah or because of a matter. According to this, beit din should initially compel him not to divorce his first wife because of a mere matter.

(ה) <b>And she cannot guard herself.</b> This is the rule: if she cannot guard her get, then by Torah law she is not divorced, for it is written, "and he shall place it in her hand," and then according to all opinions he remains liable for every monetary obligation. If she can guard her get but cannot guard herself, then by Torah law she is divorced, but Chazal enacted that he should not divorce her, so that people should not treat her as ownerless. In this case Rambam holds that Chazal did not enact monetary obligations, since he is prepared to settle what is hers and is prevented only by the enactment of Chazal; therefore he is exempt from all monetary obligations. With this one can answer the difficulty raised by Rama in siman 70: there one may say it speaks where she became deranged and cannot guard her get, so he remains liable for every monetary obligation. As for medical expenses, which Rambam says he does not owe, it appears he refers to other medical expenses; but if it is possible to cure her of this illness, he is obligated in her treatment. Raavad challenged him without need. Tosafot and the Rosh in Perek HaCheresh cite the Yerushalmi that when her father is alive and she is betrothed, then even if she cannot guard the get, she is divorced by Torah law, because her father can guard it. Likewise, if she is sometimes sane and sometimes insane, it is as though she can guard her get; nevertheless, ab initio it is forbidden to divorce her, because we are concerned she may come to promiscuity, since her father cannot guard her at every moment, and when she is insane she may come to promiscuity. After the fact, however, she is divorced in such a case even if she cannot guard her get.

(ו) <b>And some say that he is obligated in her sustenance.</b> The Tur wrote in the name of Ramah that even if he already divorced her, he is obligated in sustenance and other obligations except marital relations. He wrote two reasons. First, since Chazal enacted that he not divorce her, even if he transgressed and divorced her he remains obligated in all obligations, for otherwise what did Chazal accomplish with their enactment? Second, she is no worse than one who is divorced and not divorced, where he is obligated in her sustenance. Since Chazal enacted that he not divorce her, the practical difference between these two reasons is nowadays after the ban of Rabbenu Gershom: if he divorced her against her will, according to the first reason one may say that here it is not relevant to ask what Chazal accomplished, since there is a ban in the matter and presumably he will not transgress; but according to the second reason one may say that he is obligated in all obligations.
