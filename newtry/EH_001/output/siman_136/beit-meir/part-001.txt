Beit Shmuel wrote that at first glance this is difficult, and similarly Magen Avraham wrote in siman 339. According to what I proved in siman 127, that according to Tosafot's approach every conditional get has publicity, this is not difficult, for a get of a dangerously ill man is undoubtedly given on condition that he die, and it has publicity. "If the get is in his domain, he should transfer ownership to her," etc. Rashi z"l explained that this is a chazakah sufficient to acquire, so that the seller cannot retract: now he gives her the courtyard with the get inside it, and we learned that property without liability is acquired together with property that has liability. Ran wrote: this does not appear to me, for if so why does he need to transfer the particular place where the get rests? We hold that with acquisition agav we do not require the movable item to be piled on the land. Rather, a woman is not divorced through agav, for we require "and he placed it in her hand." Here they relied on the law of her courtyard, as we say that her get and her courtyard come into her possession at once. At first I wished to support Rashi from the Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 198 s.k. 10, who wrote regarding one who rents the place of an item that he acquires the movables in it only where he first rented the place and afterward the movables were transferred to him, but not where both are transferred at once. Tosafot already wrote here that this is not like "take your get from the ground," because since the get comes from the husband's domain into her domain it is as though he gave it to her. Necessarily they mean that in the rejected case the get is already no longer in his domain but on the ground, whereas here the get comes from his domain; though see the beginning of siman 139. This is satisfactory if he transfers the courtyard and the get to her simultaneously. But if he first transfers the courtyard to her while the get is lying in it, then according to Rava's initial assumption, that we do not say "what a woman acquires her husband acquires," or according to what is known, that she was an arusah, the get is lying in her domain. If he then transfers the get to her, it would certainly be like "take your get from the ground." Therefore Rashi was forced to explain that he transferred the get and the courtyard at once, the get through agav with the courtyard. As for Ran's question, if so why is the particular place needed, and his further statement that a woman is not divorced through agav: in my humble opinion, one point answers the other. Certainly, for agav generally we do not require the movable item to be piled on the land. But for a get, he needs that particular place because if the get is elsewhere, even if she acquired it through agav, it still would not be "and he placed it in her hand." When it lies in the place he transfers to her, that place becomes her hand, and the get is transferred to her through agav; this properly fulfills "and he placed it in her hand." But under the law of courtyard, simultaneous transfer is impossible according to the Shach. Still, I see that the Shach's words cannot stand even according to Rashi's explanation, for on Gittin 21 Rava says: if he wrote a get for her and placed it in the hand of his slave, and wrote her a deed of gift for the slave, she acquires the slave and is divorced through him. Rashi explains on his own that the slave becomes like her courtyard, and her get and her courtyard come at once. Thus Rashi also holds that one can transfer movable property and courtyard simultaneously through the law of courtyard. What the Shach brought as support from siman 202, and there in s.k. 3 challenges the Sema and writes that from Ran and Maggid Mishneh it is clear that even in a protected courtyard it is not called "piled there," is, in my view, astonishing. Aside from the fact that here Ran proves explicitly the opposite, the very source from which he came proves explicitly like the Sema: they answered for the Rambam that he ruled that "piled there" is not needed for agav because then the acquisition works through his courtyard, and Ran asks only that according to this the Rambam should have explained that it must be protected. It is therefore clear that acquisition through a protected courtyard works even where both are transferred at once. So too the sugya in Bava Metzia 11b proves this, for Rav Papa establishes the incident of Rabban Gamliel and the elders as operating through courtyard, and there it is clear that he gave everything at one time. Thus the main view is like the Sema. To settle Rashi z"l, I saw that Pnei Yehoshua wrote that one cannot explain it through her courtyard, because at this stage Rava had not yet concluded that her get and her courtyard come at once, and he thought the courtyard had to be hers before the get was placed there. If so, what does he mean by "let her go," etc.; it would not help, since it was not her courtyard before the giving. Likewise, what does he mean by saying that if they said this for a married woman, would they say it for an arusah? Even for an arusah it would not help according to the initial assumption that her get and her courtyard do not come at once. Therefore Rashi had to explain here through agav. At first this is not understood: what of the fact that he had not concluded that with a woman her get and her courtyard come at once? That was because what a woman acquires her husband acquires, so even if she acquired the courtyard it is still not her domain. But according to the view that he had not concluded that what she acquires her husband acquires, and likewise by an arusah, it is like a courtyard about which he wrote "I have no claim or rights in it," as the Mishnah teaches: if he placed it in her courtyard, it is a get. But it seems his intention is that the two matters depend on one another: just as according to the view that we do not say her get and her courtyard come at once, so too we say the Shach's reasoning that even regarding another person one cannot transfer courtyard and movables simultaneously, because at the very moment he would acquire the movable item it is not yet truly his courtyard. According to this, however, it would imply that Rav Ashi in the Bava Metzia passage necessarily reached Rava's reasoning that her get and courtyard come at once, whereas the implication is that Rava originated this idea. On this understanding, Pnei Yehoshua's words are correct in settling Rashi, but what he wrote further, "and Rashi holds," etc., see there, is not understandable and is unnecessary, for it is already settled by what I have written. In any case it is also proven that according to the conclusion, the Shach's words cited above are not correct.
