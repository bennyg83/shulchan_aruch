<b>אבל איני בא עליה</b>. לדעת כמה פוסקים מורד מתשמיש הוי מורד אבל מורד ממזונות לא הוי מורד אלא כופין אותו ליתן לה מזונות או לגרש אותה וכן כשהיא מורדת ממלאכה אינה מורדת אלא כופין אותה כמ"ש בסי' פ' אלא בתו' כתבו דין זה תליא בפלוגת' אם י"ל איני ניזונית ואיני עושה לכן לפסק הלכה דקי"ל היא י"ל איני ניזונית ואיני עושה לא הוי מורדת אבל הוא א"י לומר איני נזונך הוי מורד וכ"כ הרא"ש והטור, מיהו לא הוי מורד כשאומר טול מ"י במזונתך ומספיק לה, וב"ח פוסק כשיטות הפוסקים מורד דוק' מתשמיש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But I will not have relations with her.</b> According to several decisors, one who rebels from marital relations is a mored, but one who rebels from support is not a mored; rather, we compel him to provide support or divorce her. Likewise, when she rebels from work, she is not a moredet; rather, we compel her, as written in siman 80. Tosafot write that this law depends on the dispute whether she may say, "I will not be supported and I will not work." Therefore, since the halacha is that she may say this, she is not a moredet; but he cannot say, "I will not support you," and he is a mored. So wrote the Rosh and Tur. However, he is not a mored if he says, "take your earnings for your food," and it suffices for her. Bach rules like the decisors that mored refers specifically to marital relations.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>We compel him immediately to divorce.</b> So wrote the Tur, implying that we do not give him seven days, although regarding one who forbids by vow we wait seven days, as written in siman 74, because one who vows has more "bread in his basket" than a mored. So wrote the Perishah. Here, according to everyone, we compel him to divorce her, as written by Tosafot and the Rosh. Do not ask from the Rama below that we do not compel her to accept a get within twelve months because of Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment; perhaps there it is different because there is a set limit of twelve months. The Rosh elsewhere cites the Yerushalmi that we compel only regarding women forbidden to him, and all the more where she withholds herself we do not compel him; but he also writes at the end of HaMadir that we compel one who says, "I will not feed or support." One must say that he cites the Yerushalmi only to show that where she prevents herself, we do not compel him; but in truth the Rosh holds we compel him. See Bach, seif 15.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say that if he wants to divorce immediately.</b> At first glance it is puzzling to prove from the Rambam, since he ruled according to Talmudic law where a man could divorce against her will. But nowadays, with Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, perhaps his law is like a mored even if he wants to pay the ketubah. The Chelkat Mechokek asks this. One can answer that the Rambam’s plain ruling that if he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah he is not a mored, without distinguishing even a first marriage where one may not divorce unless he found ervah, shows that even where it is forbidden lechatchilah to divorce, he is not a mored and we do not add to the ketubah. From this it follows that even for us, with Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, he is not a mored. Shiltei Gibborim also implies that even nowadays he is not a mored if he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah. Responsa Maharam Mizrachi, siman 30, ruled that he need not provide support because he is prevented by Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment, comparing it to one whose time to marry arrived but Chazal prevent the marriage. The Chelkat Mechokek objects that there he had not yet married her; Responsa Maharam ben Sasson raised this and still ruled that the husband can say kim li like Maharam Mizrachi, and Bach also rules like him. However, Responsa Rosh, Responsa Maharik, and below at the end of siman 117 imply that he must provide food and clothing, though it appears we do not compel him to have relations with her. Regarding inheritance, the Rif, Rosh, and other decisors write that even those whom we compel to divorce, whether by law or enactment, if she died before the get he inherits her. Shiltei Gibborim and Darkei Moshe write that where he is a mored or forbids his wife by vow he does not inherit, as in Shulchan Aruch below, siman 90. See the Bach’s distinction between one who finds her repulsive and one who wants her but torments her.

לפ"ז אפי' מורד' בעינ' ליה א"צ ליתן הנדוני' כשתצ' ממנו ואם הנדוני' מונח ביד השליש תליא בדינים אשר מבוארים בסי' נ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And she takes her worn-out property.</b> There are four approaches in this sugya: the Rambam’s, the Rosh’s as cited by the Tur, the Ramban and Rashba’s, and the Ran’s. According to the Rambam, the sugya of Rav Zevid’s daughter-in-law speaks of a moredet who says, "I want him but I want to pain him." The Gemara says that with nichsei tzon barzel, if she seized, we do not remove it from her, and if she did not seize, she did not acquire. Rivash writes that the husband need not seize; if she did not seize, it is as though he seized. Nichsei melog she receives even without seizure. The Rambam holds there are daily announcements for four weeks, with warnings before and after, and then the beit din consults her. Immediately after four weeks she has no ketubah, though the get is delayed twelve months. In "ma'is alai," he divorces immediately and the Rambam holds we compel him, though all decisors disagree. In nichsei tzon barzel and nichsei melog, whatever is extant she takes even without seizure.

According to the Rosh, after the final enactment both types are delayed twelve months. In "I want him but pain him," they announce her, and after twelve months she loses ketubah and conditions of ketubah; before then she has them, except no support and her earnings are hers. In both cases nichsei melog are hers without seizure, and so is land of nichsei tzon barzel; movable nichsei tzon barzel she receives only if she seized.

According to the Ramban and Rashba, "I want him but pain him" has only the Talmudic law: after four weeks’ announcement she loses; seizure does not help for nichsei tzon barzel, while nichsei melog need no seizure. Rav Zevid’s daughter-in-law speaks of "ma'is alai," where we wait twelve months; during that time she loses nothing except support, and afterward loses ketubah and conditions. For nichsei tzon barzel, seizure helps; for nichsei melog, seizure is not needed; and no announcement is made for "ma'is alai."

According to the Ran, in "ma'is alai" she even has ketubah, nichsei tzon barzel, and nichsei melog without seizure, and the get is delayed twelve months. Rav Zevid’s daughter-in-law speaks of "I want him," where seizure is required for nichsei tzon barzel, and after four weeks’ announcement she loses ketubah; if she returns it does not help, but Chazal enacted not to divorce her until twelve months. Regarding seizure, see siman 93: seizing a key does not help, nor seizure through an agent. The Chelkat Mechokek rules that if her father seized, it is like seizure through an agent, unless the dowry had not yet reached the son-in-law and remains with the father. The Ran’s responsum explains that seizure helps only for the very item she brought him, not another item; money of dowry is like another item, because "praise of her father’s house" does not apply to money, unlike the Bach. Hagahot Maimoniyot compares this to siman 53: if her father did not promise the dowry except for his daughter’s benefit, and she says "ma'is alai," he need not pay it; according to that reason it applies even if she says "I want him but pain him" when she leaves him. If the dowry is held by a third party, the laws depend on siman 53.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All this is when she gives no reason, etc.</b> This refers above to seif 2, where the law of "ma'is alai" was stated. There it speaks where she says "ma'is alai" without a clear reason. But if she gives a clear reason which beit din recognizes as true and fitting for her to find him repulsive, the Geonim enacted not to apply Talmudic law, and the husband must return the entire dowry. There are three categories: a clear claim, a false claim, and an unclear claim. Their sources are in the Rif and the laws of the Metivta. Where the claim is clear, we remove from him nichsei melog and nichsei tzon barzel and give them to her. Where the claim is not clear, if she seized, we do not remove it. Where it is shown false, everything is given to the husband, even what she brought in, according to the Rama’s explanation. The Bach inferred that the Metivta law does not speak about the dowry money and that she acquires only if she seized, but the Chelkat Mechokek disagrees, and the main law is like him. The Taz writes that we remove from him only at the time of the get; if he does not want to divorce and we do not compel him, we do not remove anything unless she seized. The law of the Metivta applies specifically to "ma'is alai," not to "I want him but I want to pain him." The reason for the enactment is to prevent Jewish women from going astray. I already wrote in siman 75 that if property was consumed because he did not guard it properly, or it was stolen or lost, then for nichsei melog he is exempt and for nichsei tzon barzel he must pay; but if he used it improperly, the reverse applies: for nichsei tzon barzel he is exempt, because it is his, but for nichsei melog he must pay if he did not use it properly and consume the fruits.

<b>An arusah who does not want, etc.</b> If she clarifies her claim, it is obvious that her father is not obligated, for this is no worse than a married woman, where we remove nichsei tzon barzel from the husband; all the more so he need not give it to him. Rather it speaks where she cannot clarify the claim. With a married woman, if she seized we do not remove it from her, but seizure by her father does not help; here, where the dowry is still with her father, he need not give it. The same applies if she is married and the dowry is still with her father. Even if he gave a debt document to the groom, he need not pay, because these laws resemble those in siman 53 regarding a daughter who died. Even if she says, "I want him but I want to pain him," the law is so, as written there. However, if he gave a mamarni note as customary in Poland, where the lender’s name is not stated, it is like money in the groom’s hand and he must pay, as the Taz writes there. If the dowry is held by a third party, I also wrote there. All the more so from these laws we learn regarding our shidduchim that if she refuses, her father need not give what he pledged.

A brief summary of the laws of mored and moredet: a husband who rebels against his wife has three dinarim added to her ketubah each week as a debt. According to most decisors, mored applies specifically to marital relations; if he refuses support, he is compelled to support her, though Tosafot, the Rosh, and Tur hold that refusing support also makes him a mored. If she wants, we compel him to divorce. If he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah, he is not a mored even after Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment, though there is dispute regarding support and clothing; we do not compel him to have relations, since according to his claim she is repulsive to him.

A moredet who says, "I want him but I want to pain him," or says "ma'is alai" while demanding the ketubah, follows Talmudic law, not the Metivta law. She is warned, announced about for four weeks, warned again, and told by beit din that she will lose the ketubah. If she returns, some reduce her ketubah by seven dinarim per week and some do not. If she persists, according to Rambam, Ramban, Rashba, and Ran she loses the ketubah after the warnings, though he is asked not to divorce for twelve months; according to the Tur she loses only after twelve months. During twelve months she has no support and her earnings are hers; he remains obligated in burial and eats the fruits. After twelve months she has no ketubah or conditions of ketubah, and he is not obligated in burial unless she had seized nichsei tzon barzel, in which case he inherits them and must bury her. All agree he inherits her. Nichsei melog return to her; nichsei tzon barzel depend on seizure, and seizure must be by her herself, not by an agent or father, except where the dowry never reached the groom. Gifts of the husband are returned. Nowadays he may be allowed to divorce her against her will or, where impossible, marry another.

A moredet who says true "ma'is alai" and does not demand the ketubah has three cases. If she gives a clear and verified reason, she receives nichsei melog and nichsei tzon barzel under the Metivta enactment. If her claim is visibly false, nichsei tzon barzel are given to him, while nichsei melog remain hers. If the claim is unclear but not fraudulent, he may divorce her immediately without ketubah; nichsei melog are returned, and nichsei tzon barzel depend on seizure. In such a case seizure of dowry money helps, because according to her claim the Metivta law applies. If she dies, burial and fruits depend on the doubts explained above.
