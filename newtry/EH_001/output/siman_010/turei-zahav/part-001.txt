**** ENGLISH ****
He may not return. In the Gemara there are two versions. In the first version the reason is because of degradation — lest she degrade herself against her husband; Rashi explained: after she marries another and he will say "had I known the matters were false or that the vow has annulment I would not have divorced her," and so I said at the time of the get "because of this I am divorcing you" — and then it was in my mind to return you if they are found false, and the get is found void; therefore they say "know" that you may never return her, and he is no longer believed to say so — since he knew it is forbidden to return her even if she did not marry, and he did not care to wait and investigate after the matters — he revealed his intent that she was not dear to him. But if he did not say so initially at the time of the get he cannot degrade — for he did not say initially that he divorces her because of bad reputation and vow; and since he cannot degrade, if she married they did not forbid him to return her until she married — end quote Rosh. And the latter version holds the reason he may not return is a penalty — lest the daughters of Israel be promiscuous in immorality or vows; therefore he must tell her "know that because of this I am divorcing." It turns out per this statement is not needed except to warn her — but he cannot return her even if he did not say so and did not warn her; nevertheless the penalty of him forbids her return, and this statement which is necessary for the prohibition — in the first version it must be with doubled condition, for Rava established this tanna holds like R' Meir who said we require doubled condition in every condition; and perforce the statement here does not forbid him because of degradation except if he doubled to say "were it not for this I would not be divorcing you" — but without doubling there is no insistence to fulfill his words. The Rosh ruled like the first version — and this first "some say" here in Shulchan Aruch; and the second is the Rambam's view that doubling is not required, for he did not mention it at all, but wrote in ch. 10 of Gerushin: one who divorces his wife because of bad reputation or because she was promiscuous in vows — they tell him: inform her for what reason you divorce her in order to warn her, and know you may never return her; and why may he never return her? A decree lest she marry another and repent and the first will say "had I known it is so I would not have divorced her," and it is found like divorcing conditionally and the condition was not fulfilled, and the get is void retroactively — end quote. And it is questioned how he ruled against Rava who concluded we require doubled condition for the Sages who hold like R' Meir that we require doubled condition; and in Perush HaMishnah the Rambam wrote halakhah is like R' Yehudah who does not require doubled condition — and so Maggid Mishneh brought it in Beit Yosef; except he challenged him — for the Rambam ruled in ch. 6 of Ishut halakhah like R' Meir that we require doubled condition; and previously Beit Yosef explained the sugya conversely — he did not say his establishment except as general sharpness, but in truth of the matter it deals even without doubling his condition; and if so there is wonder here that Beit Yosef's words contradict each other — since per truth it must require doubling, and this is against his words in ch. 6 of Ishut that Beit Yosef himself mentioned; aside from what is strained to say Rava's words are only for sharpness. And in truth it seems Beit Yosef's words here are only general sharpness; and Maharachal strained for this to explain that R' Yehudah in truth does not dispute R' Meir generally, but here from stringency they said even though he did not double doubly — all this is not worth it to me. Rather in my humble opinion certainly the Rambam in his composition retracted from what he wrote in Perush HaMishnah where he wrote halakhah is like R' Yehudah that doubling is required — rather halakhah is like R' Meir as he stated explicitly in Hilchot Ishut; and the closing the Rambam deals with is when he doubled; and that he did not mention this in Perush is because he gave the reason it is like divorcing conditionally, which is explained in his words in the place that is the source of the law of condition that we require doubling — and he did not need to mention it here since he mentioned condition, the whole law of condition applies; and that which is mentioned in the Gemara in four doublings supports our words — for where it deals without doubling they needed to explain, not so where stam was as explicit in doubling. Behold the Rambam brings in his language "and in order to warn her" — which is the latter version; and afterward he mentioned concern for degradation — and this is in the way that the latter version is stricter than the first version which said because of degradation; automatically the prohibition is only when he says at the time of divorce "because of bad reputation I am divorcing you" — meaning with doubling; and if he did not say so there is no prohibition. And in the first version they hold that nevertheless it is forbidden because of promiscuity; therefore one must inform her. And the Rambam ruled like the first that statement is required; and in truth statement suffices even not in her presence but before witnesses — for in this too there is degradation; except it is better that the statement be in her presence in order to warn her per the reason of the latter version — but the prohibition does not depend on this; and however the statement is, in any case doubling is required like every condition, and he need not mention it here. And therefore when the Tur brought the RaMaH's view who wrote doubling is not required — once suffices — the Tur did not mention that this is the Rambam's view for the reason I mentioned. It turns out the second "some say" mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch is (not) the Rambam's view.

that he vowed to divorce her. In the Gemara it states: in another case — when she vowed; but if he vowed that he divorce her — he may return if he wishes, for there is no concern for degradation. And R' Yossi bar Yehudah: an incident also in Tzaidan — one who said to his wife "Konam all fruits upon me if I do not divorce you" — and the Sages permitted him to return her because of tikkun olam. And we challenge: they permitted — plain — for what do we concern for her? Because of R' Natan — as it is taught: R' Natan says whoever vows is as if he builds a bamah, and one who fulfills it and is not asked to permit it is as if he offered a sacrifice on it — he completes his sin whole; and this one who fulfilled his vow and divorced her — they are penalized regarding return — teaches us. And we challenge: what tikkun olam is here? There is here, said Ravina: this is what it means — there is no here because of tikkun olam; and Rashi explained: they permitted me to return her because of tikkun olam that no prohibition is found here — end quote. And his explanation implies: even though initially when she was divorced because she vowed it is forbidden to return her because of tikkun olam — meaning because of degradation; but here when she vowed there is no concern of tikkun olam such as degradation — no prohibition is found here, and one must understand: if there is no concern of prohibition why did the tanna need to teach us this matter? Perforce one must say there is a side of prohibition because of degradation — meaning she will marry another and find an opening to annul his vow and he would not have needed to divorce her first, and he will say "had I known there is an opening for my vow I would not have divorced her and would have permitted the vow" — teaches us that specifically when she vowed there is this concern of degradation, since there is a defect in her we say he divorced with this intent; not so when he divorces because of his vow to divorce. And accordingly (question why he challenges) initially plain — why not answer that it teaches us this matter that we mentioned that there is a difference between her vow and his vow in this. And I saw in Perishah: he may return her — there is no concern for degradation — so much Rashi; and per his words: behold it was in his power to return her immediately after the get and he fulfilled his vow; and since he did not return her certainly he did not desire her; therefore if he said at the time of the get "had she not vowed I would not have divorced her" — nevertheless there is no concern for degradation. Accordingly if she vowed she is forbidden to him forever, and at the time of the get he also said he divorces because of this — also forbidden to return her because he can degrade saying "had I known there is an opening for my vow I would not have divorced her" — end quote. The author waives the honor of the rabbi — his words are not correct in what he wrote: since he did not return her certainly these matters cannot be said; certainly in concern if she marries another there is no difference whether she was betrothed immediately after or waited some time — and he will degrade saying "I desired to return her but she hurried to marry another and did not wait for me, and had I known this I would not have divorced her." And the matter is clear in the plain explanation that there is no proof at all from what he did not return after the get — neither in divorce because of her vow or his vow; rather the difference between them is that when she vowed — if bad reputation spread about her or she is an ailonit — meaning she did not bring signs — in these a blemish applies; but in his vow annulment is possible, and bad reputation is possible it will be explained it is false, as Rashi explained whom I brought at the beginning of this seif; and for the Rambam: lest she repent. And for an ailonit perhaps she will still bring signs — and these matters he remembered at the time of the get; and especially if he doubled them it was like giving a get on an actual condition, as the Rambam wrote; and it is plain that in every condition if it was not fulfilled the act of the get is nothing and it is as if it was not given at all. So too this matter in the eyes of the husband — since the get was given when he thought she was an hour because of bad reputation or she was an ailonit or she vowed and he does not desire a niddah; and since this is false and the vow has annulment — automatically it is as if she did not vow at all; if so he will say the get has no effect and she is still his wife — all this if she does not marry, the first returns her without new kiddushin. So was my opinion; and with this the words of Rashi are settled who wrote "and when I divorced you it was in my mind to return you" — for seemingly questioned: is it a decree in him lest she not agree to marry him again since he already shamed her? And per the words it is settled that she was compelled to return to him since the get did not take effect; and for this he relied at the time of giving the get. But regarding his vow to divorce her — he thought annulment is impossible and because of this he gave and fulfilled his vow; except that in the end it became known to him annulment is possible — nevertheless it took effect since he did not actually annul and was obligated to do so; and annulment of his act does not apply here, and condition does not apply here — perforce there is no degradation. With this — and in truth if he said at the time of the get "had I known there is annulment for my vow I would not have divorced you" — in this certainly similar to her vow, it was like conditionally; and whenever it became known to him there is annulment the get itself is void — not so here where he did not say at the time of the get but the vow brings him to this; even though he said "had there not been the vow I would not have divorced you" — this is not included that there will not be a get if annulment is possible; for it can be that even though annulment is effective nevertheless he does not want to nullify his words because of annulment; and also if in his heart annulment is impossible — matters of the heart are not matters. With this the plain explanation in the Gemara is settled that stated ma'aseh beTzaidan — one who vowed, etc., and they permitted him, etc. — for in truth also when she vowed or bad reputation and ailonit — if he divorced her she is permitted to return to him before she married another; except the Sages made a prohibition to return her since degradation is possible if she marries — not so when he vowed, the Sages did not make a prohibition since there is no concern for degradation as we said — rather it remains as Torah law. If so what does it teach that they permitted to return her? And it answers there is one opinion they are penalized since he did not seek to permit it; and afterward the tanna himself explained the matters we mentioned that he said there is no here because of tikkun olam; and Rashi explained no prohibition is found here for the Sages because there is no concern at all as we mentioned — and this is the tanna's main intent that stated in another case when she vowed; but if he vowed, etc. — it implies they are seemingly equal and we are from concern of degradation as I mentioned. And in what he concluded in Perishah: if she vowed she shall be forbidden to him forever, etc. — I do not know a place for his words; certainly if she forbade him eternal prohibition — meaning Konam your relations upon me — he cannot return after divorce without concern without degradation except because of his vow in his explanation. And if it deals that afterward he finds an opening on his vow — certainly he may return her, for there is no concern for degradation at all — the get is a complete get as we mentioned. All this seems clear and plain after examination, with God's help.
