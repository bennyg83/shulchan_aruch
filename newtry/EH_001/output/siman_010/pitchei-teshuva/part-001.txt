<b>המגרש את אשתו. </b> כתב בר"י אות א' יש להסתפק אשה שנתגרשה בגט כשר מה"ת ופסול מדבריהם והלכה וזינתה אי מותרת לחזור לבעלה לכתחלה אי אמרינן דקרינן בה ונטמאה מדרבנן כיון דאגידא ביה וגיטא מיפסל מדרבנן או דילמא הואיל והגט כשר מה"ת אף דזינתה מותרת לבעלה והאריך בזה והביא ראיות דמותרת לחזור לבעלה ע"ש באורך:
**** ENGLISH ****
One who divorces his wife. Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in note 1: there is room to question — a woman who was divorced with a valid get biblically and invalid rabbinically and she went and committed adultery — whether she is permitted to return to her husband ab initio; do we say we apply to her "and she became defiled" rabbinically since it was stated to him and the get is disqualified rabbinically? Or perhaps since the get is valid biblically, even though she committed adultery she is permitted to her husband? And he expanded on this and brought proofs that she is permitted to return to her husband — see there at length.

**** ENGLISH ****
And some say specifically, etc. See in responsum of Noda BiYehudah end of siman 11 — after he explained there in his case of the spit that it will be brought below siman 115 seif 6 seif kaf-lamed-vav that there is room to permit her to return to her husband, and we do not say she is established on one side because she revealed her intent before she admitted she wants to admit falsely — he wrote: however for another reason this woman is forbidden to return to her first husband — for behold he divorced her because of bad reputation; and it is explained in siman 10 seif 3 there are three views regarding one who divorces because of bad reputation, etc.; and if so per the Rambam who is "there is one who says" — plainly he cannot return and take her; and even per the middle view she is also forbidden — for perforce we do not require that he say "because of bad reputation I am divorcing you," but general bad reputation that she was not forbidden to him by beit din and he divorced her of his own accord. But in our case where he replied that he believes her words and the rabbi instructed him he is obligated to divorce her — this is more than speech of his own accord because of bad reputation; and not only that, but it is possible even the first view agrees here that the rabbi instructed to divorce her, and per the rabbi's instruction the woman became forbidden to him — he divorced more than doubling a matter; and as Tosafot Kiddushin 49 wrote: matters we are witnesses to do not require doubled condition, etc.; and so too there are no witnesses greater than this. And even though nevertheless one may not void the get now because of this — since he said at the time he divorced in explanation that he divorces without condition and nullified all conditions as is customary among us at the time of arranging the get — nevertheless it is no worse than doubling a matter, which also deals when he did not double his condition but his matter; therefore it seems the husband is forbidden to return this divorcee — end quote. Also in Yeshuot he wrote: and in one responsum I saw written that if beit din agreed he divorce her because of a rumor and afterward it became known it was false in this — we do not require he tell her, and he is not permitted to return her — for there is no greater revelation of intent than this. And perhaps in responsum of the Gaon Noda BiYehudah he means to say all this is only when he divorces of his own accord; but if beit din tells him "you are obligated to divorce her" — forbidden to return her according to all, and then they must also tell him "know," etc.; and it requires investigation, for the poskim do not appear so closed — and see siman 117 in Beit Yosef and Chelkat Mechokek (seif kaf-gimel). The Ran's question and his answer in the law of one who divorises a woman who has no regular period seems to imply the opposite — end quote. Also in responsum of Chatam Sofer siman 25 he wondered on Noda BiYehudah in this from the words of the Ran above; and in Tosafot Biur he wrote: and in Maharachal it was hidden from him the words of the Ran ch. 2 of Shevuot in the law of a woman who has no regular period, etc. — we learned from the Ran there are three laws: where they compel to divorce — even if he said explicitly "because of this I am divorcing you" she is not forbidden to return to him, for he cannot say "had I known I would not have divorced you" — since they compel to divorce; and where she is forbidden and they do not compel — he need not even statement, and nevertheless forbidden to return to him; and where there is no prohibition except he concerns for bad reputation or vow — then when he says "because of bad reputation," etc., forbidden to return her. And if so in the incident of Noda BiYehudah where the rabbi ruled he is obligated to divorce her — no need to say "had I known"; and so in Beit Meir — end quote. In my poverty I did not merit to understand their challenge on Noda BiYehudah above — certainly if the instruction of the mentioned rabbi was that they compel him to divorce, their wonder is proper; but as it appears from what the rabbi ruled that he is obligated to divorce — it was per the words of Shulchan Aruch in siman 115 seif 6: if he believes her and his mind relies on her words — he is obligated to divorce her; and if so behold he concludes there "but they do not compel him" — and presumably that rabbi too ruled so; and if so this is comparable to the second law that emerges from the Ran above, and there is support for the words of Noda BiYehudah from the Ran as above — and it requires investigation. And see further in responsum of Chatam Sofer who wrote that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch added and wrote "because of bad reputation that spread about her" — and their intent is to exclude if bad reputation did not spread but the husband has an evil spirit from his youth and suspects the innocent — Kapot Temanim ben Yehudah end of Gittin, etc. — certainly she is permitted to return to him — see there.
