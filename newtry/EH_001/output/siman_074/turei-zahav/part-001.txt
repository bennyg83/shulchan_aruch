**** ENGLISH ****
(ג) <b>Seven days.</b> A woman is normally in her days of niddah for seven days, so he waits that amount of time while she remains with him, perhaps he will regret the vow and find an opening for release. But where she vowed and he upheld it, what more does she have to wait? If she is content to take her ketubah and leave, she need not seek an opening for her vow. But when he is the one who vowed, he waits, perhaps he will find an opening for his vow.

ובזה לק"מ גם קושיות החמ"ח והב"ש אטו לא ידעינן דאיש יכול לגרש אשתו בע"כ כו' ע"ש ולפי מ"ש מיירי מתני' נמי באומר א"א באשה נדרנית ויוציא בע"כ עכד"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) <b>And some say that if he forbade her.</b> Meaning, he attached it to marital relations, for otherwise he cannot forbid her adornment. This "some say" is Rambam, as brought by the Tur. The language of the Mishnah is: with poor women, as long as he did not set a limit; with wealthy women, thirty days. This means that one who forbids his wife's adornment follows the rule that if he forbids it for a fixed period, up to twelve months, she waits; if for longer, he must divorce. If he said it without specification, according to Shmuel he divorces after twelve months. The first opinion is the Rosh, who rules that without specification he divorces immediately and gives the ketubah. See my words in Yoreh Deah siman 235 for the details.<br />
Words of the glossator, seif 2: "If she vowed," etc. Beit Shmuel s.k. 4 wrote that Taz says Rambam holds that she put her finger between her teeth, and Beit Shmuel objected that if so, why should he give her the ketubah? In my view Taz's words are correct. The Gemara on Ketubot 71 states: a woman who vowed to be a nazir and her husband heard and did not annul it: R. Meir and R. Yehudah say she put her finger between her teeth, therefore if the husband wishes to annul, he may annul, and if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," she leaves without a ketubah. R. Yose and R. Elazar say he put his finger between her teeth, therefore if he wishes to annul, he may annul, and if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," he divorces and gives the ketubah. Ran, cited by Beit Yosef, explains that the poskim understand: if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," and therefore did not annul, he divorces and gives the ketubah; Rambam, however, did not explain this way, and for that reason holds that we do not compel him to divorce. According to this, Taz can say that Rambam too accepts Ran's reading that because he said "I do not want a woman who vows" he did not annul; Rambam nevertheless holds that we do not compel him to divorce because he follows the view that she put her finger between her teeth. Even so, he gives her the ketubah, for the Gemara mentions only two alternatives: if he wishes to annul, he may annul; and if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," then according to R. Meir and R. Yehudah she leaves without a ketubah. A third case, where he says "I want her," is not mentioned. Therefore according to Rambam, where he does not wish to annul yet does not force divorce, the ketubah may still be due depending on how the case is framed. This resolves Beit Shmuel's difficulty.
