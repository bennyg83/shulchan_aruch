**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>We write the name of his city, etc.</b> According to the Rosh in chapter HaZorek and several poskim, if he did not write the names of their cities, the get is valid. But in chapter HaSholeach he wrote that one must write the name of the place from which he and she come. One must say that "must write" means ab initio, but after the fact it is valid if he did not change it. However, Beit Yosef, s.v. "and if he sends," brought the Rosh's view that he follows one approach with Rashba and Ran: if he did not write the place of residence, it is invalid. According to Beit Yosef's latter answer there, it can be somewhat reconciled. Perhaps if he wrote no place name at all it is better, but when he wrote the place where he stands, they hold that he must write his own place too, lest people say another man divorced, thinking he is from that standing place. According to this, however, it contradicts our custom, for we do not write the places of residence. See Responsa Rivash, siman 114. It appears that where there is no established custom to write it, omission does not invalidate; but if one writes and changes, it is invalid.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>And even if she married, she must leave.</b> I already wrote in the name of Rashi that with these changes the get is void by Torah law and the child from the second husband is a mamzer by Torah law. However, one may say that Rashi wrote this according to R. Meir, for the Mishnah follows R. Meir; but according to the Rabbis it is valid by Torah law. Although the Rabbis agree with R. Meir regarding a change of his name, her name, his city, and her city, nevertheless they hold that it is invalid only rabbinically, as proven from Tosafot, folio 20, s.v. "but we require." See Chiddushei Maharsha. However, Rambam wrote at the beginning of chapter 3 that it is not a get, meaning that the get is void by Torah law. So too Shiltei Gibborim wrote, and this requires study for practical law. See the Tur and Beit Yosef: if he uprooted his residence and came to another place and was there thirty days, that place is called his residence.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>His name was lost.</b> This means that the majority call him only by one name. Levush. This is specifically if the minor name was lost. But if the major name was lost, both names are mentioned. If it is doubtful, meaning some people call him by this name and some by that name, they write only the major name and do not write both names, because perhaps the major name is primary and it would be a change. But in the reverse there is no concern, for the major name is not displaced. Levush. Regarding the name of a river or city that is pronounced with a soft kaf, although in these countries we pronounce chet like a soft kaf, nevertheless one should write a soft kaf, because in some countries chet is pronounced like heh. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 231. See the end of siman 129. A river or city whose name is like two names, such as Regen-Shpurg and the like, is one name.
