**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>That she was betrothed.</b> Four elements are required: first, that they said "she was betrothed," in the past tense; second, that they said the kiddushin took place today; third, that they say she was betrothed to so-and-so; and fourth, that they said she was betrothed in this city. Bach and Chelkat Mechokek wrote that the women do not need to say "she was betrothed to so-and-so today here"; rather, the rumor emerges that way, while the women simply say that she was betrothed. However, the Tur wrote that they must say "so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so," and Rivash likewise wrote that this is the correct reading in Rambam. Still, they need not say where the kiddushin occurred, for from their words it is understood that she was betrothed in the place where candles were lit; so wrote Kesef Mishneh. See what I wrote in siman 6 regarding a rumor that she committed zenut.

**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>And similarly, if two came.</b> This means there is a rumor in a certain city that so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so; when beit din investigates the rumor, we require two people to come and testify that they heard from so-and-so, who heard, etc. But where there is no rumor and public commotion in the city, it is not called a rumor. Bach, in the name of Maharal of Prague. Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that the witnesses must be valid. At first glance, the Talmud implies that they need not be valid, for it says there that one wished to resolve from R. Chisda's statement, "until they hear from valid people," that one may nullify a rumor; and according to R. Shimon, who says that they need not be valid and even women suffice, it follows that one does not nullify a rumor. Since we rule that one does not nullify a rumor, it appears that they need not be valid. This is the reasoning of the Ramah brought by the Tur, who holds that even a woman who heard from two people creates a rumor. However, it seems that the rule that we do not nullify a rumor is based on custom rather than strict law; therefore the witnesses must specifically be valid. The Tur also implies that this testimony, that they heard from so-and-so, must include that she was betrothed today by so-and-so. However, Ran wrote that they need not testify "today," and perhaps they also need not testify that she was betrothed to so-and-so. So too it appears from Rambam's language, and this is the wording of the Mechaber. See Kesef Mishneh, who wrote in the name of Rivash.

<b>אם נראים הדברי' לב"ד</b>. במגיד מבואר היינו שיצא קול שזרק קדושין ואח"כ יצא אמתלא שהי' קרוב לו אם נראי' לב"ד לסמוך על אמתלא זו לא הוחזקה מקודשת אבל אם יצא הקול סתם פלוני קידש פלונית ואח"כ יצא אמתלא שהיה בזריקה וקרוב לו אין לסמוך על אמתלא זו כיון שלא יצא קול אמתלא מיד ח"מ וכן אם אמרו אח"כ שהי' ספק קרוב לו אין מתירין אותה הואיל שיצא קול הראשון שזרק לה סתם גם משמע אם יצא קול הראשון שזרק לה הקדושין ואח"כ אמרו שקידש על תנאי אפילו אמרו שלא נתקיים התנאי לא תלינן לקולא כי קידושין על תנאי לא נכלל בכלל הקול הראשון שיצא שזרק לה קידושין וכתב הר"ן אם בתחלה יצא קול שזרק קידושין ואח"כ נתברר שהיה קרוב לו מתירי' אותה ואם לא נתברר שהיה קרוב לו אסורה להנשא ואם נשאת לא תצא נראה דוקא בכה"ג לא תצא משום שיצא הקול שזרק לה הקדושין י"ל שהיה קרוב לו אבל אם יצא קול סתם דהיא מקודשת תצא מחמת הקול כמה שכתב בסעי' ה':
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>If the matters appear credible to beit din.</b> In Maggid Mishneh it is explained that this refers to a case where a rumor emerged that he threw kiddushin to her, and afterward an explanation emerged that it was closer to him. If it appears to beit din that they may rely on this explanation, she is not established as betrothed. But if the rumor emerged without qualification, that so-and-so betrothed so-and-so, and only afterward an explanation emerged that it was by throwing and was closer to him, we do not rely on this explanation, since the explanation did not emerge immediately together with the rumor; see Chelkat Mechokek. Likewise, if they later said that it was doubtful whether it was closer to him, we do not permit her, since the first rumor was that he threw kiddushin to her without qualification. It also appears that if the first rumor was that he threw kiddushin to her, and afterward they said that he betrothed her conditionally, even if they said the condition was not fulfilled, we do not attribute the matter leniently, because conditional kiddushin was not included in the first rumor that he threw kiddushin to her. Ran wrote that if at first a rumor emerged that he threw kiddushin to her and afterward it became clear that it was closer to him, we permit her; if it was not clarified that it was closer to him, she is forbidden to marry, but if she married she need not leave. It seems that only in such a case she need not leave, because the rumor was that he threw kiddushin to her and one can say it was closer to him. But if the rumor emerged without qualification that she was betrothed, she must leave because of the rumor, as written in seif 5.
