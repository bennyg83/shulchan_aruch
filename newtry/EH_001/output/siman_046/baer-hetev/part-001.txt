<b>היום. </b> ד' דברים צריכים להיות. א' שאמרו נתקדשת לשון עבר. ב' שאמרו דקדושין היו היום. ג' שאומרים נתקדשה לפלוני. ד' שאמרו נתקדשה בעיר זו. קלא דשטר כתוב וחתום שנתקדשה ומכח השטר לא הוי קדושין דמפיהם ולא מפי כתבם בעינן. לא הוי קלא. משפטי שמואל סי' ס"ח. ועיין במהר"ם אלשיך סי' ח':
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>Today.</b> Four elements are required: first, that they said "she was betrothed," in the past tense; second, that they said the kiddushin took place today; third, that they say she was betrothed to so-and-so; and fourth, that they said she was betrothed in this city. A rumor based on a written and signed document stating that she was betrothed does not constitute kiddushin through the force of the document, for we require testimony "from their mouths" and not from their writing; it is therefore not considered a rumor. Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 68. See also Maharam Alshikh, siman 8.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ג) <b>The candles.</b> These two must be valid witnesses. The statement of these two does not require them to testify in beit din on the very day she was betrothed; even after some time they may testify that on a certain day they saw lit candles in a certain house, etc. So wrote Bach; see there. As for what Beit Shmuel wrote, that this does not seem to follow from Rashba's responsum, where R. Abba wrote that he does not disagree with Ulla, for had it been at the time we would require it like Ulla, etc.; see there. He was not precise. The plain meaning is that "had it been at the time" means that they saw the candles lit and were in the city at the time of the celebration. But "after some time, when investigating the rumor" means that they did not see the candles lit and were not actually in the city at the time of the celebration, but came there later and heard, etc. See Beit Yosef and Keneset HaGedolah, folio 80a, seif 11. In any event one can say that if they saw it at the time, they may testify later about what they saw on a certain day. Examine carefully.

<b>פלונית. </b> לא הצריך שהנשים יאמרו לפלוני בעיר הזאת. רק שהנשים יאמרו סתם נתקדשה פלונית והקול יוצא מתוך דבריהם שנתקדשה לפלוני בעיר הזאת. והיום פשיטא שא"צ שיאמרו דהא ביום האירוסין עושין השמחה ח"מ וב"ח. מיהו הטור כתב דצ"ל נתקדשה פלונית לפלוני. וכנה"ג הביא דעת מהריב"ל דס"ל דהרמב"ם ורש"י וטור ס"ל הא דצריך שיאמרו נתקדשה לפלוני אלא בדליכ' רגלים לדבר דנרות דולקות ומטות מוצעות. אבל היכא דאיכ' רגלים לדבר דנרות דולקות ומטות מוצעות א"צ שיאמר לפלוני ע"ש דף פ' ע"א סעיף י"א. ודף פ"ב ע"א סעיף ו' ודלא כב"ש שכתב וכן כתב הריב"ש ע"ש. ולכ"ע א"צ לאמר היכן היו הקדושין כי מכלל דבריהן נשמע שנתקדשת במקום שנרות דולקות כ"כ כ"מ וב"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) <b>So-and-so.</b> He did not require the women to say "to so-and-so in this city"; rather, the women may say simply that so-and-so was betrothed, and the rumor emerges from their words that she was betrothed to so-and-so in this city. Today it is obvious that they need not say this, since on the day of erusin they make the celebration; see Chelkat Mechokek and Bach. However, the Tur wrote that they must say "so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so." Keneset HaGedolah brought the view of Maharival, who holds that Rambam, Rashi, and the Tur require them to say "she was betrothed to so-and-so" only where there are no supporting indications, such as lit candles and beds spread. But where there are supporting indications, lit candles and beds spread, they need not say "to so-and-so"; see there, folio 80a seif 11 and folio 82a seif 6. This is unlike Beit Shmuel, who wrote, "and so wrote Rivash"; see there. According to everyone, they need not say where the kiddushin took place, for from their words it is understood that she was betrothed in the place where the candles were lit. So wrote Kesef Mishneh and Ba'er Hetev.

<b>קול. </b> כ"כ הרמב"ם. והטור בשם הרמב"ן כתב ולדברי ב"י הוא הרמ"ה ע"ש דס"ל דוקא אם אומרים בשם שנים דחיים עדיין אלא הלכו למד"ה אז מרתת שמא יבואו ויכחישו אבל אם אומרים בשם העדים שמתו כבר לא הוי קול. ואפשר לומר דל"פ דהרמב"ם דס"ל אם מתו הוי קלא היינו מפני שהוא סובר שבאו שנים והעידו מפי א' ששמע מאחד שנתקדשה פלונית בפני פלוני ופלוני. וכיון דבין הכי לא מת מי שתלו הדבר בו מאי איכפת לן אם מתו העדים שנתקדשה בפניהם דאעפ"י שאמרו שמתו העדים שנתקדשה בפניהם יש להאמינם ששמעו מפי פלוני כיון שתולין הדבר בו ומרתתי שמא היום או למחר אתי אבל לפי פי' הטור וסייעתו דליכ' הכא אלא ב' ששמעו מפי ב' והב' האחרונים הם עידי הקדושין מסתבר' למימר דוקא שתלו בב' שהלכו למד"ה דמרתת שמא היום או למחר יבואו אבל אם תולין בשתים שמתו לא הוי קלא כנה"ג דף פ' ע"ב סעיף י"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) <b>A rumor.</b> So wrote Rambam. The Tur, in the name of Ramban, wrote, and according to Beit Yosef this is the Ramah; see there, that this applies specifically when they speak in the name of two witnesses who are still alive but have gone overseas, for then people are afraid lest they come and contradict them. But if they speak in the name of witnesses who have already died, it is not a rumor. One can say that they do not disagree. Rambam, who holds that if the witnesses died it is still a rumor, speaks because he understands that two people came and testified from the mouth of one who heard from another that so-and-so was betrothed before so-and-so and so-and-so. Since in any case the person on whom the report depends has not died, why should we care whether the witnesses before whom she was betrothed have died? Even though they said that the witnesses before whom she was betrothed had died, they can be believed that they heard from so-and-so, since the matter is attached to him and they are afraid he may come today or tomorrow. But according to the explanation of the Tur and those who support him, where there are only two who heard from two, and the latter two are the witnesses to the kiddushin, it is reasonable to say this applies only when they attach it to two who went overseas, for they are afraid lest they come today or tomorrow. But if they attach it to two who died, it is not a rumor. Keneset HaGedolah, folio 80b, seif 18.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ג) <b>Forever.</b> This means she is forbidden to both of them, to the husband and to the paramour, and this is where there were at least witnesses of seclusion, that she secluded herself with the second man. But if there was only chuppah, she is permitted, since the first kiddushin was doubtful. Rashdam, Even HaEzer, siman 22. However, Mahara Sasson, siman 173, wrote that this is not sufficient, even if she was a niddah; rather, only where witnesses came and testified that their hands did not leave their hands and that they were not secluded. Most of the sages of Salonika agreed to this.
