**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>They have the custom to be stringent, etc.</b> The reason is that the letter does not look proper when the letters touch one another. But by strict law, touching letters in a Get are valid, even though in a Torah scroll it is invalid, because regarding a Torah scroll the verse says, you shall write them, meaning complete writing; and we do not derive the law of a Get from a Torah scroll, just as we do not derive parchment and ink requirements from a Torah scroll. So wrote Semag and the Rosh in Gittin chapter 2. A practical difference follows: if one wrote a letter and it is not surrounded by blank parchment below, according to all opinions it is valid, for the letter then appears proper. However, the Rosh did not give this reason, but wrote only that the custom is to be stringent that it be surrounded by blank parchment. One may say the reason is that initially we make it like a Torah scroll; according to this, even below it must be surrounded by blank parchment. See Responsa Mishnat Binyamin, siman 23, which I cited at the end of seif 7. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 that only when a child can read it is it valid according to those who validate; but when a child cannot read it, all agree it is invalid. According to those who are stringent, even if a child can read it, it is invalid. So too it appears from Semag and from Responsa Rivash, siman 56, and so Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 101, ruled. At first glance this does not appear so from the Rosh and the Tur, since they wrote that if ink fell into a letter and it is no longer recognizable, he may not remove the ink; this implies that if letters touch one another, even if it is not recognizable, he may repair it.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Any smudge.</b> The reason is concern for forgery. Darkei Moshe wrote that one should be careful that no drip or dirt fall on the parchment, because when one writes over it, it appears erased. If rain falls on the writing, the get should not be given, for it turns black and is like something that can be forged. One should not write over an erasure for the same reason. Although by strict law it is permitted to write on something that can be forged, as written in siman 124, namely where the delivery witnesses are before us, nevertheless ab initio one must be careful, lest the delivery witnesses die. Likewise, if a fly dragged ink from a letter, one should not erase it; there should be no erased matter between word and word or between letter and letter, because that is something that can be forged. Therefore one does not write over something scraped. I have already written that in a case of agunah it is valid. Darkei Moshe wrote that if it was punctured beside the letter and not in the letter, it is valid.
