<b>אחר שגמר לכתוב תורף</b>. היינו תנאי ע"מ כשר אבל תנאי המבטל הגט כגון חוץ או תנאי שלא תשת' יין כל ימי חייה פסול ותנאי ע"מ דכשר היינו כשנתקיים התנאי או חזר ומחקו מיהו מחק קי"ל דפסול כמ"ש בסי' קכ"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>After he finished writing the essential text.</b> This means that a condition phrased as "on condition" is valid. But a condition that nullifies the get, such as "except," or a condition that she not drink wine all her life, is invalid. A valid condition phrased as "on condition" means when the condition was fulfilled, or he went back and erased it. However, regarding erasure, we hold that it is invalid, as written in siman 125.

(ב) <b>This is a safek divorce.</b> Rambam wrote this regarding a condition phrased "on condition" before the essential text, whether in writing or orally. Even if the condition was later fulfilled, nevertheless at the time of writing it was not known whether it would be fulfilled, so it was not a "scroll of severance," and we do not say bereirah. See Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Merubah; I do not know how he reconciles this law, for Rambam's reason must be bereirah, and we do not say bereirah. However, most poskim disagree with Rambam and hold that a written condition of "on condition" before the essential text invalidates only rabbinically, as a decree because of a condition of "except." According to Ran, Baal HaMaor, and several poskim, an oral condition of "on condition" before the essential text is valid, for they hold that even "except" orally before the essential text is invalid only rabbinically as a decree lest he write "except." Therefore an oral "on condition" is valid. Beit Shmuel then contrasts Rambam, Rashi, Ran, Baal HaMaor, and the other poskim, and explains the practical differences between written and oral conditions, before and after the toref, and conditions that reserve part of the divorce versus conditions that merely suspend it.

<b>כגון שהתנה לה וכו'</b>. היינו אלו תנאים אפי' אחר התורף או שהתנה שאר תנאים ע"מ קודם התורף וחזר ומחקו ס"ל לרמב"ם ספק גט הוא כ"כ המגיד אבל חוץ ולא נמחק הגט בטל ותנאי ע"מ אחר התורף כשר אפי' לא נמחק כמ"ש בש"ס הטעם הוא משום בעת הכתיבה נכתב שפיר וגם ליכא שיור אלא תנאי אבל חוץ אפי' אחר התורף איכ' שיור אף על גב דנכתב שפיר מ"מ איכ' שיור אף על גב חזר ומחק מ"מ בעת  הכתיבה היה שיור והוה ספק אם ימחק כל זה הוא לשיטות הרמב"ם ושיטות שאר הפוסקי' כתבתי בסמוך ואם היה התנאי ע"פ היינו תנאי חוץ, אף על גב דמבואר בש"ס לכולי עלמא חוץ לאחר  התורף אינו פוסל בע"פ היינו דוק' בע"פ לא בעת נתינת הגט:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>Such as where he stipulated to her, etc.</b> This means these conditions even after the essential text, or other conditions phrased "on condition" before the essential text where he then erased them. Rambam holds that it is a safek get; so wrote Maggid Mishneh. But if it is "except" and it was not erased, the get is void. A condition phrased "on condition" after the essential text is valid even if it was not erased, as written in the Shas; the reason is that at the time of writing it was written properly, and there is also no reservation, only a condition. But with "except," even after the essential text, there is a reservation. Although it was written properly, nevertheless there is a reservation; although he later erased it, nevertheless at the time of writing there was a reservation, and it is a safek if he erases it. All this is according to Rambam's approach. The approach of the other poskim I wrote nearby. If the condition was oral, meaning a condition of "except," although it is explained in the Shas according to all, etc.
