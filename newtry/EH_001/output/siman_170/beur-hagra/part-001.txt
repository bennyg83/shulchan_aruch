(ב) <b>And there is one who, etc.</b> This is the view of the Rambam and other decisors, and so is the view of Tosafot, as written below. Seif 8 is the view of Rashi, as written in the Tur, and as Rashi wrote there, s.v. "they require," etc.; and the latter clause teaches, etc. Below he explains the source of their dispute.

At first glance there is no distinction in the matter, for two yevamot are worse than two yevamin, as they said there: and if you wish, say, since, etc. But the Tur wrote: From the words of Rashi it appears that even according to the one who says that an inferior chalitzah requires, etc., with two yevamot and one brother, her rival is exempted through her chalitzah. Now Rashi wrote there: Rather, learn from this that they are equal, and her rival is exempt, unlike Rivan in Tosafot and the Rosh there. In this, Rashi’s words are very correct, for certainly according to Shmuel, when they are equal, she exempts her rival, unlike that language of "he began and he did not begin." This is as Rashi wrote in Perek Rabban Gamliel, 51a, s.v. "shall we say," etc., and rabbinically, etc. This is a great proof. There is another proof from what they said there, 52a: "How shall he say," etc. In all of them, it supports Shmuel, and in the middle clause should it say that it supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah, and is also not difficult for Shmuel? According to Shmuel it is simpler from the first clause. This is the view of all the decisors except Rivan.

But what the Tur wrote, that even Rabbah bar bar Chanah agrees in this, the sugya of the Gemara implies this, for it says there, 51b: the latter clause teaches, etc., and it does not challenge from the first clause. And there, 53a, "shall we say this supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah," it does not say: the latter clause teaches. Rather, according to the above words of the Tur, the support is all the more so from the first clause, unlike an objection. So too it appears from Rashi in the Mishnah of Perek Rabban Gamliel, where he explains the whole Mishnah as chalitzah to one of them, and he does not mention any dispute. See the second discussion, s.v. "and chalitzah," etc., s.v. "maamar to this one and he had relations," etc., and s.v. "they require," etc. From this the Tur inferred and wrote: From Rashi’s words, etc. So too it appears from the language of the Mishnah, which says throughout the Mishnah get and chalitzah.

But Tosafot there, s.v. "they require," etc., wrote like the Rambam, that according to Rabbah bar bar Chanah everyone agrees in two yevamot, and he says chalitzah incidentally to get. What is written on 52b, "the latter clause teaches," is comfortable for the Gemara to challenge from a comparable case, as the Ramban and Rashba wrote. And regarding what it says there, 53a, "shall we say this supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah," Tosafot on 51b, s.v. "Ben Azzai," wrote that it means from the latter clause: and likewise, etc. So too Rashi explains there, 53a, s.v. "they require," etc. Although according to Rashi it is also proven from the first answer, nevertheless Rashi did not want to derive by kal vachomer something explicit in the Mishnah: and likewise, etc. Furthermore, according to the questioner’s view, Rabbah bar bar Chanah also does not agree with two yevamot, for the first clause teaches "they require." But according to the conclusion, even according to Rabbah bar bar Chanah, one must explain "they require" in general in the first clause according to Rashi.

The simple reading of the sugya implies like Rashi, as above. However, it is forced to say that Shmuel and Rav are opposites. Therefore the words of the Rosh appear correct, for he ruled like Shmuel, and then there is no distinction at all between yevamin and yevamot: where they are equal, in both cases they exempt, and where they are inferior, in both cases they do not exempt, like the first Tanna, as above. It emerges that there are three approaches in ruling: like Rav, or like Shmuel according to the first language, or like the latter language. In the approaches regarding exempting the rival, there are also three approaches: Rivan, Ramban, and Rashi, and two further approaches, those of the Rambam and Ra'avad, as written there.

(ג) <b>And according to the one who says, etc.</b> As written there and there, and like Shmuel there, 56 and 27. The Rif wrote there: the authorities ruled that the halacha follows Shmuel. The Ra'avad explained this as the first statement of Shmuel, for in the latter statement there is no one who disputes. But the Rosh ruled like the first answer: What is "one," etc. So too the Ra'avad wrote that one should do this lechatchilah, but bedieved the latter answer is primary.

But the Ramban and Ramah rule like Rav. The Ramban wrote that even though we hold like Shmuel in the baraita, about this the Rif wrote that the authorities ruled like Shmuel, meaning the earlier authorities, because both are halacha. Even though we hold like the answer of Rav Ashi to Ulla, "as you said," etc., and he wrote that Rav Ashi applies only with an excellent chalitzah to exempt the divorcee. But where they are equal, we hold like Rav, who said that with the middle one, one performs chalitzah to both, and similarly where they fell at once before all three, as they said there. All the more so with rivals, where both are equal, one performs chalitzah to both, as written: "he began and he did not begin," etc. Even Rav Ashi disagrees only because they are not strong, etc., as written there: and if you wish, say, when Shmuel says, etc. This shows that it is stricter regarding exempting a rival. Since we hold like Rav, all the more so regarding exempting a rival.

Regarding what it says there: They asked, the one who received a get, etc.; rather, is it not, etc., the Ramban explains like the Rosh in the name of Ribam, that both require chalitzah. So too the Rosh wrote in his name. The Ramban, of blessed memory, wrote that according to the words of Rabbi Eliezer, of blessed memory, etc., it teaches us that when we say, etc., and all the more so with the rivals, etc.

But Rashi explained there, s.v. "they are equal," etc., that where both are equal, one chalitzah exempts, because specifically the inferior one does not exempt. This is as written in the first answer: since we have heard Shmuel, etc.; what is "one," etc. This shows that equal ones exempt. Even according to the latter answer, "and if you wish, say," etc., this reasoning remains regarding exempting a rival. What it says, "he began and did not begin," etc., was what he initially thought, and it is rejected in the conclusion. Even according to Rav, who says an inferior chalitzah requires, etc., even with equal ones, nevertheless regarding exempting a rival, equal ones do exempt, the opposite of Shmuel’s view according to the latter answer, for according to that answer a rival is more stringent.
