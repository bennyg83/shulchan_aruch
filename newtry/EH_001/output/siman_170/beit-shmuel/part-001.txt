**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say.</b> The source of this law is the sugya at the beginning of Perek Arba'ah Achin, where a woman with a maamar and a woman with a get are considered parallel. Rashi explains that the chalitzah of one exempts the other because they are equal, since a woman with maamar also requires a get. Tosafot and the Rosh connect this to the dispute of Rav and Shmuel about defective chalitzah. According to Shmuel, where the brothers are equal in prohibition, one defective chalitzah can exempt; according to Rav, each may require chalitzah. The Tur inferred that Rashi may be more lenient in two yevamot, but Tosafot and the Rosh imply otherwise. The Beit Shmuel notes that the later seifim must be read with this dispute.

<b>Maamar, etc.</b> A maamar with a minor or by a minor yavam does not have the same force as maamar by an adult. The yavam’s age and intent determine whether the act is meaningful.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He performs chalitzah to the second.</b> So wrote the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7, in the name of Halachot Gedolot. Although we hold that an invalid chalitzah does not exempt her rival, that is specifically when she became forbidden to him through the bond, such as where he performed maamar with her or gave her a get. But when she was forbidden to him from the outset, this is a valid chalitzah. So too wrote Tosafot at the beginning of Perek Arba'ah Achin. This is as written in the glosses at the beginning of siman 167, and so ruled the Shiltei Gibborim. The "there is one who says" is the view of the Rambam and the Tur, and so it also implies from Rashi at the beginning of Perek Arba'ah Achin, who holds that it is equivalent to the law of one who received a get and one with whom maamar was performed.

However, according to this, there is a difficulty from the baraita in chapter 2, page 20, which teaches that with a prohibition of mitzvah or a prohibition of sanctity, if he performed chalitzah with her, her rival is exempt. The Bach wrote that this baraita speaks on a Torah level, where she is exempt, but rabbinically they enacted that an invalid chalitzah does not exempt. This is not precise, for it teaches a prohibition of mitzvah and a prohibition of sanctity, and one of them means secondary relatives. If so, even rabbinically it exempts. Perhaps one can resolve this according to what is written there, that it was stated in different directions: "if he had relations with her" refers to a prohibition of mitzvah, which is rabbinic, while "if he performed chalitzah with her" refers to a prohibition of sanctity. One can also say that it speaks where her rival is also forbidden like her; therefore, with a prohibition of mitzvah, which is rabbinic, she nevertheless exempts through intercourse when she and her rival are equal, as written at the beginning of siman 171, while with a prohibition of sanctity she exempts by chalitzah on a Torah level.

According to those decisors who hold that with rival wives, when they are equal in prohibition, they exempt one another, as written at the beginning of siman 167, one can say that the baraita speaks where the rival is also subject to a prohibition of mitzvah. So too wrote the Maggid. But according to his explanation of the Rambam, that even when the rival wives are equal they nevertheless do not exempt one another, it is difficult for him to resolve; see there. This will be explained nearby. The Mechaber is surprising, because he brought the Rambam’s view in the name of "there is one who says," implying that the main view is like the first opinion, yet in siman 174 he rules plainly like the Rambam. The Rama too is difficult: in siman 167, regarding invalid chalitzah, he wrote that it is specifically where she was bound and then became disqualified, which follows the first view, yet at the beginning of siman 174 he did not gloss anything.
