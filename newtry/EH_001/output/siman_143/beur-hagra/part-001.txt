**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>Therefore, etc.</b> From what they said there, 76b: and let us be concerned that perhaps he appeased her. He explains: perhaps he nullified the get. Although it is also taught there on the Mishnah, the Rambam explains that this is what they said there: and according to the one who teaches it on the baraita, etc., "he did not come," meaning that even if he appeased her, it is nothing, since the condition was fulfilled retroactively. The Ramban and Ran have another approach there. See there, that even with "from now" or "on condition" there is a way to nullify it. Nevertheless, they learned this not from the Mishnah but according to the version in the baraita: on condition that I pass, etc. But I saw in the Rambam, chapter 9, halacha 10, that anything that is for her benefit she can waive. This is what is meant by "perhaps he appeased her." But in the Mishnah we are not concerned, because it is not the way, etc., and as written above, siman 144, seif 7. His proof is from what they said, 29b, "perhaps he appeased her," and the same applies here, since it has not yet taken effect, etc., as above. See Tosafot 18b, s.v. "perhaps," etc. Nevertheless, his view there is like the Rambam.

[Collection:] <b>Therefore he cannot nullify, etc.</b> And what they said there, 76b, on the Mishnah, "from now," etc.: perhaps he appeased her, and even according to the one who teaches it on the baraita, etc., because people do not usually come secretly. From what he wrote in chapter 9, halacha 9, the Rambam already explained there that any condition that is for her benefit she can waive, as written above, siman 144, end of seif 7. What is written in the first clause, regarding "if," that he can nullify, etc., is from what they said, 29b, "perhaps he appeased her," according to the explanation of the Rif and Tosafot above, that he nullified it. The reason is that since it does not take effect until after thirty days, he can nullify it. And there, 34a: Abaye said, from where do I say this? For that case, etc.; Rava said: is that so, etc. It implies that if he explicitly nullified, according to everyone it is nullified. See Mordechai, Perek Mi She'achazo, siman 609, and the Rif further wrote, etc. So too here, since it does not take effect until fulfillment of the condition, as above, he can nullify it. Only Perek Mi She'achazo, which speaks of "on condition," says specifically that it depends on her will. This is also the view of Tosafot 18b, s.v. "perhaps," etc. But the Ra'avad, chapter 9, halacha 9, in his glosses, holds that even with "from now" he can nullify it, like the view of Ba'al HaItur. This is also the view of the Rosh, klal 46, seif 2. See there, where he discusses this at length and brings proof from Perek HaMadir: Do not say that Rav’s reason is waiver, etc. He wrote that in divorce, even a matter that is for the woman’s benefit can be nullified, since he divorces her against her will. See above, siman 38. The Tur also brought this in this siman. This is what is written in the gloss: and some say, etc. See Beit Shmuel at the end of siman 144, and other approaches, and what I wrote in siman 16.
