**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there are those who wrote.</b> So wrote the Rosh: if he is willing to accept the money, it is a Get. Necessarily, one must say that the Rabbis and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagree where he is not willing. If so, it is difficult, for it is proven that giving against one's will is not considered giving according to the Rabbis, and we hold like them. So Derishah and Bach asked in the name of Maharshal, and they did not answer. One may say that giving against one's will means where the condition was to give money and she gave it against his will; but where the money is given in place of the article, and it is given against his will, we do not say that giving against one's will is considered giving. Therefore I wrote in Hilkhot Kiddushin at the beginning of siman 27 in the name of Maharshal. Do not object from the Rabbis' statement that he specifically meant the cloak; one could say that even if we did not say that he specifically meant it, giving against his will would still not count. Rather, certainly with money it is considered giving, which is difficult according to the Rosh, who holds that giving against one's will is a doubt. It is strained to say that this is what they mean: he specifically meant the cloak, and therefore it is not giving at all; but if he had conditioned on money, it would be a doubt. Perhaps the Rosh holds that giving into the hand of the beit din in his absence is considered giving when we do not know that he is unwilling, and she is permitted to marry, unlike the responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 73. Therefore the Rabbis say that he specifically meant the cloak, and therefore it is not giving even into the hand of the beit din in his absence. In Responsa Rashakh II, siman 99, he also ruled that giving into the hand of the beit din is considered giving. In truth, Rashba's proof is at first glance surprising, for he brought proof from Hillel's enactment to put the money in the chamber, implying that generally giving to the beit din is not considered giving. But there, he had revealed that he did not want it, which is different; if we do not know his view, one may say it is considered giving. However, it is difficult according to Rabbah there, who said that this enactment was not needed and explained that it follows the Rabbis. Even though according to the Rabbis he specifically meant the cloak because he intended only to distress her, and according to the Rosh the case concerns giving against his will, it would follow that even if she did not give the very thing he stipulated and gives against his will, it is nevertheless considered giving because he did not intend only to distress her. This is also difficult according to the words of Maharit that I cited at the beginning of siman 27.

This follows the approach of those poskim who hold that according to the Rabbis, if he stipulated without detail, it is a condition for his benefit; nevertheless, if the child died before she nursed, it is not a Get. So too according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who holds that even where he specified we say he stipulated for his benefit: if he died before she nursed, or before she served his father, it is not a Get. But according to the other poskim I cited, and Rashba nearby, who hold that according to the Rabbis, when he stipulated without detail, even if the child died before she nursed at all it is a Get, it appears that according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, if he died or said it is impossible for you to serve me before she nursed, it is a Get. They answered that here Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds he stipulated for his benefit, and since he died he no longer needed it, so it is as though the condition was fulfilled; but there, where he said that you give me, his intent was that he receive it, and ultimately when he does not wish to receive it, the condition was not fulfilled. (ג) <b>And there is no remedy to permit her.</b> Since he gave her a valid Get, it is not in his power to permit her unless he betroths her; so wrote the Tur. From this it follows that he cannot nullify the condition at all, even if both of them consent. But this is difficult from what the Tur wrote at the end of siman 38 and below in this siman, in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, that he can nullify the condition. It appears that this is not comparable to what is written at the end of siman 38, for there the condition is if he comes within twelve months, which is not in his power to fulfill, and the kiddushin have not yet taken effect. What is written at the end of seif 7 concerns a condition that if the divorcing husband does not come within twelve months, even though the condition is passive and the divorcing husband has the power to fulfill it, nevertheless she is forbidden to marry because it is not in her power but in the power of the divorcing husband; in any such case, they have the power to nullify it. But here it is a complete Get, and she is permitted to marry immediately, because the condition is in her power to fulfill passively; therefore he cannot nullify the condition. However, one may say that the Tur follows his own reasoning, for he holds that with a condition on condition that you give me two hundred zuz, waiver does not help even if he says let it be a Get without condition; therefore even with this condition there is no remedy by saying let it be a Get without condition. But according to the poskim he cited in siman 5, that it helps if he says let it be a Get without condition, one may say it would help here too. However, according to what I wrote, the condition that she give is not in her power to fulfill, because perhaps she will lose everything she owns; one may say that there the condition is not in her power, and therefore he can nullify it. The reason it does not help when he says I waive it to you is because he intended to distress her. But here the condition is in her power and the Get takes effect immediately; it is not in their power to nullify it, even if he says let it be in your hand without condition. According to this, one may say that the Tur holds that with a condition that she give, it helps if he says let it be in your hand without condition. Yet it is difficult: why did the Tur write regarding a condition on condition that you serve my father for two years, and he then went back and stipulated on condition that you give two hundred zuz, that he did not deliver the Get on the first condition, for otherwise he could not stipulate another condition? Why can he not stipulate another condition? The first condition is one that prevents her from marrying, since it is active and also not in her power. Perhaps there, the condition is intended to distress her, so he cannot waive the condition and stipulate another. The rule is: a condition intended to distress cannot be waived; a condition not intended to distress, if it is in her power and passive so that she is permitted to marry another, cannot be nullified. Bach answers that everything depends on whether the condition is intended to distress or not. At the end of siman 38 and at the end of seif 7, the condition is not intended to distress and he can nullify it; but the condition that she not marry this man is intended to distress, and likewise the condition that you serve my father is intended to distress. According to this, the Tur's statement here that there is no remedy proves that he holds it does not help, if he stipulated that she give two hundred zuz, for him to say let it be in your hand without condition; otherwise there is a remedy, namely to say let it be in her hand, etc. But the language of the Tur here does not imply as Bach says.
