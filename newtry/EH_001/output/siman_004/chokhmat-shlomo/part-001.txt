<small>Seif 19</small> <b>And if they had relations with a Jewish woman, whether unmarried or married, the child is valid, etc.</b> NB: Whether the child requires conversion or not, see Chemdat Shlomo, siman 2, where the sages of the generation struggled with this. In my responsum, second edition, in the addenda, I concluded that with a married woman the child requires conversion, while with an unmarried woman the child does not require conversion. In my responsum to the holy community of Tarnopol, Even HaEzer siman 4, in my fourth edition, siman 66, I concluded that rabbinically conversion is required in every case; see there, for it is good. In my work Chokhmat HaTorah, parashat Emor, year 5607, and in what I wrote in my responsum to the holy community of Chudnov, fixed in Even HaEzer siman 5, in my ninth edition, siman 259, see there with the help of Hashem, I showed arguments in both directions. Now too it appears possible to bring proof in both directions. First I will bring proof that conversion is required from what is written in Ketubot 44a in the Mishnah: "A female convert who converted... if her conception and birth were in sanctity, she is like a Jewish woman in all respects." This is difficult: how can the Tanna state categorically that she is like a Jewish woman in all respects? One can find a case where even though her conception and birth were in sanctity, she is not like a Jewish woman in all respects, such as where her mother had relations with a gentile. Although a gentile who has relations with a Jewish woman produces a valid child, nevertheless she has no father, since the child follows her; at least she has no "entrance of her father's house." Especially since the first clause presumably speaks of one born from a gentile; even though a gentile woman who had relations with a Jew has the same law, since the child of a maidservant or gentile woman follows her, nevertheless the plain wording implies an ordinary convert who gave birth from a gentile. If so, since it distinguishes between conception and birth in sanctity or not, it implies the same type of case, one where she had relations with a gentile. How then can it say she is like a Jewish woman in all respects? Moreover, it should have distinguished within conception and birth in sanctity itself: whether she had relations with a gentile or not, for from a gentile she has the fine but no entrance of her father's house, while from a Jew she has an entrance of her father's house. The difficulty is even greater in the Talmud, which asks: say it means her conception and birth were in sanctity; and answers: in that case she is a full Jewish woman. What answer is this? Perhaps it teaches that even if she had relations with a gentile, the law is so. It must be proven that in such a case conversion is required, and therefore her conception and birth were not in sanctity. This is worse than a convert whose daughter converted with her, where her conversion helps also for the child; therefore one can find conception not in sanctity and birth in sanctity. But with a Jewish woman, who is already Jewish, when a gentile has relations with her, the conception and birth are certainly not in sanctity if the child later requires conversion. Thus this is exactly the Mishnah's distinction between conception and birth not in sanctity and in sanctity. If it is in sanctity, it can only be where a Jew had relations with her, and therefore the Gemara properly says she is a full Jewish woman. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion; examine carefully. Yet now I see a clear proof to the opposite, that conversion is not required, from an explicit Talmudic passage in the Mishnah in Bekhorot, chapter Yesh Bekhor, and in the Talmud there 47a, and in Tur and Shulchan Aruch Yoreh Deah siman 305: when a gentile has relations with a Jewish woman, the child is obligated in pidyon haben because he is petter rechem. If one would think he requires conversion, then a convert who converts is like a newborn child; he has no brotherhood or kinship, and there is no petter rechem here. On the contrary, it is proven there in the Talmud, 47a, where it says, "Do not say this only according to the one who says they do not taint the child, but even according to the one who says they taint the child..." According to both Rashi and Tosafot there, it is proven that even according to the view that the child is invalid, nevertheless he does not require conversion, since he is obligated in pidyon haben; he is only a mamzer, as the Talmud says there, an invalid Levite is called a Levite. If he required conversion, he would not be a Levite at all. It must be that he does not require conversion and is obligated in pidyon haben. According to what I wrote in my fourth edition, cited here, that rabbinically he requires conversion, this is somewhat resolved, for one may say that since by Torah law he does not require conversion, he is obligated in pidyon haben. At least it is proven that by Torah law he does not require conversion, and this is clear. It is astonishing that Shaarei Teshuvah and Chemdat Shlomo discuss this law and did not notice the above Talmudic passage. Examine carefully.
