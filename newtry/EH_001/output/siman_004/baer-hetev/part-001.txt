<b>עולם. </b> בפרק הערל אמר רבא לא תתחתן דכתיב בז' אומות בגיותן אבל אחר שנתגיירו שרי אלא נתינים דוד גזר עליהם. הדר אמר רבא לא תתחתן איירי בגיירותן אבל בגיותן לית בהו חתנות ופסק הרמב"ם פי"ב מהא"ב כלישנא קמא וכל ז' אומות מותר להתחתן בהם אחר שנתגיירו אלא נתינים שהם מז' אומות גזר דוד עליהם שלא יכנסו בקהל לעולם וליכא איסור דאורייתא. אבל אלו דחשיב אפילו נתגיירו אסורים. ואפילו ספק ממזר אסור לעולם הר"מ מטראני ח"א סי' ל"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
Forever. In chapter HaAreil Rava said "do not intermarry" — Scripture speaks of the seven nations in their gentility; but after they converted it is permitted, except Netinim on whom David decreed. Again Rava said "do not intermarry" speaks in their conversion, but in their gentility there is no marriageability. Rambam chapter 12 Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah rules like the first version — all seven nations permitted to intermarry after conversion except Netinim from the seven nations on whom David decreed not to enter the congregation forever; there is no Torah prohibition. But those listed remain forbidden even if converted. Even safek mamzer is forbidden forever — Rambam of Trani part 1 siman 34.

**** ENGLISH ****
For kehunah. Even though their cohabitation was in transgression nevertheless the daughter is fit. Do not challenge according to what the Mechaber ruled here — Ammonite convert who married Israelite daughter, the daughter is fit even for kehunah — for then the a fortiori we derive for gentile and uncircumcised with Israelite daughter that the child is blemished for kehunah from widow-Kohen Gadol is concealed; Ammonite convert proves he blemishes in his cohabitation and there is transgression in his cohabitation yet his daughter is fit for kehunah, as Tosafot challenged Yevamot 87a. If so, why did the Mechaber rule gentile with Israelite daughter — child blemished for kehunah? Say we have another a fortiori from widow-Kohen Gadol: kiddushin take effect in her and blemish her son — all the more gentile and slave in whom kiddushin do not take effect. On this there is no refutation. Why Bach wrote gentile/slave child blemished because kiddushin do not take effect — and did not write it is an a fortiori from widow — certainly the challenge of Tosafot did not escape him; his intent as written, see and examine; not like Beit Shmuel. See Chiddushei Beit Yehuda Yevamot 45 who challenged there why Gemara derives gentile/slave child blemished from widow a fortiori because prohibition unequal — why not derive from widow a fortiori that kiddushin take effect; see there and answers. If so per our establishment that kiddushin take effect with those liable for negative commandments — we can derive from a fortiori of kiddushin taking effect; see and examine.

**** ENGLISH ****
Mamzer. The first view is Rambam's view. Some say it is Hagahot Maimoniyot's view — we decree lest he came through a name, as in the incident of Shmuel's father; Rosh wrote we attribute that he came through a name even though she does not say so. However Tosafot end Kiddushin wrote we do not attribute through a name unless she says he came. Beit Yosef siman 179 brought one responsum — per that responsum say Rambam does not dispute Hagahot Maimoniyot; rather Hagahot Maimoniyot discusses when the husband is not present and Rambam when he is present and does not say he came. The Mechaber appears to hold like Rambam — always the child is mamzer even when he is not present. If he is present and says he did not come — then definite mamzer, for he is believed to say he is not his son. If he says he came and she contradicts him — Choshen Mishpat wrote he is not believed to validate the child; say Rambam also concedes this; practically unresolved. Beit Shmuel; see Choshen Mishpat.

<b>בנו. </b> אם אשה א' תובעה לאחד ואמרה דהולד ממנו ויתן לה מזונות והוא כופר פטור אפי' משבועה. אע"ג אם תובע אותו שיתן לה האתנן חייב שבועה מ"מ על הולד הוא נאמן בלא שבועה ריב"ש סימן מ"א ועיין סימן קע"ז סעיף ה' בהג"ה וע"ל סימן כ"ב ס"ק ד'. וכ"כ בה"י ע"ש ועיין בתשובת חכם צבי שאלה קמ"ה קמ"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
His son. If one woman claims against one that the child is from him and he should give her sustenance and he denies — exempt even from oath.
