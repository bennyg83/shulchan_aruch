**** ENGLISH ****
And Netinim. In chapter HaAreil Rava said "do not intermarry" — Scripture speaks of the seven nations in their gentility; but after they converted it is permitted, except Netinim on whom David decreed. Again Rava said "do not intermarry" speaks in their conversion, but in their gentility there is no marriageability. Rambam chapter 12 Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah rules like the first version — all seven nations permitted to intermarry after conversion except Netinim from the seven nations on whom David decreed not to enter the congregation forever; there is no Torah prohibition. But those listed remain forbidden even if converted. Some say even safek mamzer is forbidden forever — Rambam of Trani part 1 siman 34.

Rif is uncertain whether they are blemished for kehunah; Rambam wrote they are fit; Rosh wrote they are blemished for kehunah from a fortiori — Kohen Gadol who cohabited with a widow blemishes his son though the prohibition is not equal in every respect; if one of these before conversion cohabited with an Israelite daughter the child is blemished for kehunah. For practical halakhah it appears bedi'eved we do not remove if she married a kohen — for Rambam fit, and likewise for Rif and Ramban we do not remove. See responsum Maharshal siman 17; see siman 67.

Therefore difficult on Rosh — how he learned gentile/slave with Israelite daughter blemishes child for kehunah from widow-Kohen Gadol a fortiori when Ammonite convert proves he blemishes in cohabitation yet daughter fit for kehunah — Rambam's reason: gentile/slave child fit for kehunah because holds a fortiori refuted; Tosafot challenged on sugya in HaAreil; Bach wrote on Ammonite convert who married Israelite daughter — daughter fit for kehunah; but gentile/slave because kiddushin do not take effect; also could derive from widow a fortiori because unequal prohibition and did not give reason kiddushin take effect. Appears he omitted Tosafot's words there.
