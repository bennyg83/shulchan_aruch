(א) <b>And give it to the agent.</b> In the Talmud, Rava asked: if he said, and give it to the agent, what is the law? Rashi explained: if they gave it to the agent and it was later lost, do we say they have already fulfilled their agency? It further states: Ravina said to Rav Ashi, if he said, and give it to the agent, and he shall bring it, what is the law? Rashi explained: if you say that when he did not say and he shall bring it, they have already fulfilled their agency, nevertheless if he said and he shall bring it, one may say they have not fulfilled their agency until the Get reaches her hand, and that is why he concluded and said and he shall bring it. Ri"v wrote regarding Rashi's words, who explained the question as where it was lost, that the same applies if it was found invalid. Rambam wrote if it was found invalid, and the same applies if it was lost. Beit Yosef challenged him and wrote that according to Rambam the Talmud's question is specifically if it was found invalid, for one may say that since their obligation was only to give it to the agent, even if they gave an invalid Get they fulfilled their agency; but if it was a valid Get and they gave it to the agent and it was lost, he did not ask, and they certainly fulfilled their agency. If they wrote another Get, the Get is void. At first glance it appears that Ri"v, Rashi, and Rambam do not disagree. When Rambam wrote and it was found invalid, he means that after they gave it to the agent a defect was found in it that occurred in the Get after delivery, which is like being lost. Likewise, according to Rashi, who explained if it was lost, the same applies if it was found invalid. But if the invalidity existed before delivery, they do not disagree, and according to all opinions they did not fulfill their agency, as the Tur wrote. This is also the Maggid Mishneh's intent when he wrote if it was found invalid or lost: he asks about invalidity after delivery, but if it was invalid from the outset, according to all opinions they may write another Get. In any event, it is explicit from the Maggid Mishneh that if it was a valid Get and was lost, that too is the question, unlike Beit Yosef, who wrote that if it was valid and lost there is no question and they certainly fulfilled their agency. If he did not say and he shall bring it, the law is not explicit here. The Tur wrote that they certainly were not made agents. Beit Yosef wrote that although he wrote certainly, his intent is only that it is doubtful, because the question was not resolved. Even though the second question was stated in the form if you say, and the Geonim rule according to an if you say, nevertheless since the if you say is not explicit, no one rules according to it. According to what I wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 346, that the Rosh does not rule according to the approach of the Geonim, this was unnecessary. See further below in siman 137, and see Rambam chapter 7 of Hilkhot Nachalot, chapter 2 of Hilkhot Sheluachin, and chapter 8 law 3. In Taz he wrote that the Tur holds that in the first question they certainly fulfilled their agency, and what Ravina said to Rav Ashi, what if he said and he shall bring it to her, means that he told him Rava did not ask when he did not say and he shall bring it; rather, specifically when he said and he shall bring it did he ask. For practical halakhah, the main ruling is that even if he did not say and he shall bring it, it is still doubtful. Beit Yosef wrote at the end of seif 7 that perhaps Rambam explains the first question as whether they can give the Get themselves to the woman: even though he appointed another agent, nevertheless he did not remove them from the agency. This is Rif's approach. In the novellae of Rabbi Shelomo Kohen on the eight chapters of Hilkhot Gittin, he wrote that Rambam's words in chapter 9 law 31 escaped Beit Yosef's attention, for there Rambam writes that there is no doubt in the matter and he cannot be his agent. Although there he wrote if he said and he shall bring it, there is no reason to distinguish. See siman 141.
