**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>One who went, etc.</b> The language of Shulchan Aruch from here until seif 12 is entirely the language of the Rambam, except for seif 7. There, 107a: it was stated, Rav said, etc.; come and hear: one who went, etc.; come and hear: one who went, etc.; and if he came, etc. There are many explanations there.

The version of the early authorities is: "And if he came and said, 'Let her labor go toward her maintenance,' he is permitted; if they preceded," etc. The explanation is that she borrowed and ate, and when he came he said to the beit din: she could have worked and eaten. The law is with him. But if they already went ahead and ruled, we do not listen to him. From this it appears that where one went overseas and his wife claims maintenance, one cannot tell her to work and eat, and we do not calculate her labor with her at all. So too it appears from what is written: if so, a minor and sufficient for her labor, etc. But according to Rav, in every case we rule for her, and even according to Shmuel, in the view of Rabbi Zeira.

Some explain that if she borrowed, it is like a beit din already ruled, and that baraita deals with a case where she did not borrow but rather sold her property and ate. But the Rif’s version is: "if he said to her, go out," etc., meaning that when he left he said this to her and she was silent. He is believed in this if she claims that she borrowed and denies that he said this to her. So too is the other baraita there: if he came and said, "I allotted," etc., he is believed with a heset oath, like the law of one who denies everything. If the beit din already preceded, etc., he is not believed, because then he is the claimant and she is in possession. So too he is not believed to say, "I allotted maintenance to her." The same applies if she sold his property to herself, that he is not believed.

Some say that if she borrowed, it is also like the beit din already ruled, as above. The baraita, "and if he came and said, 'I allotted,'" explains that when the beit din rules maintenance for her, they rule for her from the time of her claim, even if they delayed the judgment for some time. If he came before they ruled for her after her claim and said, "I allotted maintenance to you," he is believed retroactively from the time of her claim until now. So too in the other baraita, "and if he came and said to her, 'go out,'" etc., it means he said to her this way: "go out," etc., and you accepted it upon yourself. He is believed retroactively from the time of the claim until now. If the beit din preceded and ruled, etc., he is not believed, even regarding maintenance retroactively from the time of the claim, with any claim.

But the Rosh disagrees with all this and says that the beit din tells her to work and eat, for one can advocate on behalf of a person and collect from her not in his presence. Rather, it speaks where her labor is sufficient, according to his words, for major needs but not for minor needs, like the first answer in Tosafot there, s.v. "a minor." This is similar to what they said: if he came and said to her, "go out," etc., for she should have pressed herself, etc., according to Tosafot’s explanation there, s.v. "and if he came," etc. But if the beit din already preceded, etc., then she revealed her view that she does not want to press herself, etc. What is written there, "and if he came and said, 'I allotted,'" he is believed, is explained like Rashi: he is believed even to take from her hand, even what the beit din gave her, by oath, unlike Tosafot there, s.v. "and if he came," etc.

The Rambam’s version is like the first version: "and he said to her, let her labor go toward her maintenance." So too is the version in the Tosefta and Yerushalmi there. He explains that she did not claim, but rather pressured herself, worked, and ate, as written in seif 11. What is written there, "and if he came and said, 'I allotted,'" he explains as where she borrowed and ate, like the first explanation above, and as written in seif 10: "and if she did not claim," etc., "the debt remains," etc. This is unlike the Ran, who wrote that the Rambam explains according to the second explanation above. One must press there and say that he does not know she borrowed. This is what the Rav added there: "rather, she borrowed or," etc., meaning that it is known that she borrowed. So too it appears like the Rambam’s words there, where he wrote: "and the debt shall remain upon her." Now I will explain each item one by one. <b>One who went, etc.</b> There, that Rav agrees in this.
