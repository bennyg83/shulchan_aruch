<b>במציאתה. </b> כתב ב"י אעפ"י שמציאתה ומעשה ידיה אינם ענין לקדושין כתבם רבינו משום דבחדא מחתא מחתינהו. ובה"י כתב טעם אחר ע"ש. וב"ח כ' על דקדוקי הב"י דמש"ה הביא כאן דזכאי במציאתה דלפי שאמר כסף קדושיה שלו. דמשמע דאפי' הם מאה מנה הכל של אב ולא אמרינן דלא זכה האב אלא במה שראוי להתקדש בו דהיינו פרוטה אבל היתר מפרוטה הוי מתנה בעלמא ויהי' שלה מש"ה אמר וכן זכאי במציאתה וכו' ע"ש לכאורה יש ללמוד מזה מי שנותן מתנה לבתולה זוכה אביה. אבל באמת אינו מוכח דלא כתב הב"ח דהוי לאביה אלא המותרת על כסף קידושין. הואיל ובא מתחלה ע"י קידושין. והתורה זיכה לאב בכסף קידושין ולא חילק אם הרבה או בפרוטה לעולם הוי לאביה. משא"כ במתנה דעלמא אפשר דהוי כמו ירושה שמפורש במתני' בהדיא דאינו אוכל פירות בחייה אלא יעשו לה סגולה ולא הוי לאביה. ואין להביא ראיה מתוס' דכתובות דף מ"ו ע"ב ד"ה זכאי במציאתה וכו' שכתב שם דאין למילף מציאה מכסף קידושין וכו' משום מציאה מטריח את עצמה להגביה וכו' ע"ש. א"כ משמע מתנה שפיר יש למילף מכסף קידושין. דהא אינו דא"כ קשה נמי נילף ירושה מכסף קידושין דהוי לאביה דנמי אינה מטריח כמו מתנה ע"ש ובמהרש"א. וכן הח"מ מסופק במתנה. אבל הב"ש כתב בפשיטות דהוי לאביה ע"ש. ולא דק כמו שכתבתי. ועוד לדבריו הוי מתנה לאביה מדאורייתא כמו כסף קידושין א"כ קשה קושית התו' שם ונילף מציאה מתרווייהו וכו' וא"ל תירץ תוספת שם דמה להנך שהאב נפסד על ידיהן וכו' איכא למימר מתנה יוכיח ע"ש ודו"ק. גם י"ל לפי מ"ש תוס' שם לבסוף כסף קידושין שאני משום דיש לו פסיד' דיוצאת מרשותו וכו' א"כ גם מתנה אין למילף וא"ל כסברת המהרש"א דלענין חדא מחדא לא פרכינן הך פירכא והביא ראיה מפ"ק דקדושין ע"ש. אינו ראיה היא דשם רצה למילף מעשה ידיה מכסף קדושין אינו יוכל לומר הך פירכא שכן האב נפסד דבמעשה ידיה נמי מפסיד כסברת תוס' כאן. אבל לעולם אי ילפינן מציאתה מכסף קדושין שפיר פרכינן מה לכסף קדושין שכן האב נפסד וא"כ לא מצי למילף מתנה מכסף קדושין ובאמת יוכל לומר הך פירכא דהאב נפסד לעיל דלא כמהרש"א ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
In her finds. Beit Yosef wrote: although her finds and work of her hands are not the topic of kiddushin, our master wrote them because he strikes them with one blow. In Be'er Heitev another reason — see there. Bach on Beit Yosef's precision: therefore he brought that the father is entitled to her finds — since he said kiddushin money is his, even a hundred maneh belong to the father; we do not say the father acquires only a perutah's worth and the rest is a gift to her. At first glance one learns that one who gives a gift to a virgin — her father acquires. But Bach wrote only the surplus over kiddushin money belongs to the father, since it came through kiddushin; Torah gave the father kiddushin money without distinguishing amount. Unlike ordinary gift, possibly like inheritance (mishnah: father does not eat fruits in her lifetime). Tosafot Ketubot 46b — cannot learn finds from kiddushin because finds require effort; seemingly gift can be learned — but then inheritance too; Choshen Mishpat uncertain on gift; Beit Shmuel plainly father acquires. Tosafot's difficulty and answers — see there and Maharsha; examine.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or was married.</b> So wrote the Rosh; see Hagahot. A minor girl who betrothed herself, or married herself off, during her father's lifetime: her kiddushin is not kiddushin and her marriage is not marriage; see there. If so, the same should apply here, where she betrothed herself without her father's knowledge, or was also married, for both the kiddushin and the marriage were without her father's knowledge. Nevertheless it is nothing, for we are not concerned that perhaps the father became willing.

In truth, however, this requires analysis in practice. What we rule like Ulla, and are not concerned for Rav and Shmuel, is where she was only betrothed. But where she was betrothed and married, it is explicit in the Gemara 45b that Rav Huna and R. Yirmiyah disagree about this, and Rava concludes that Rav Huna's reason is that an act of orphanhood was done with her during her father's lifetime. See Rashi there, who wrote that he would not remain silent to such an extent, and he certainly became willing. So too Responsa Rashba, siman 1219, wrote that the words of Rav and Shmuel were rejected only where she was betrothed alone; but where she was betrothed without her father's knowledge and married without her father's knowledge, we certainly are concerned that perhaps the father became willing, and she requires both a get and mi'un, even if the father did not arrange the match; see there.

Bach wrote: it appears to me to bring proof that even if she was also married, it is nothing, for we challenge Ulla from the Mishnah, "and all of them, if they died or performed mi'un..." Who betrothed her? If her father betrothed her, would mi'un suffice? She would require a proper get. Rather, it must be that she betrothed herself, and it teaches that she requires mi'un. Yet the Gemara does not answer that the Mishnah speaks where she was married, and therefore we are concerned that perhaps the father became willing, while Ulla speaks where she was betrothed and not married. Therefore, he says, there is no distinction. This is not precise, according to Tosafot there, s.v. "rather, she betrothed herself," who write that she requires a get and mi'un. If so, here too, if the case is where she was also married without her father's knowledge, then according to Rashba's view she requires a get and mi'un; the same difficulty remains, that she requires a get and mi'un, as Tosafot hold. I am astonished, for Bach himself wrote that if the Mishnah is where she was married, then we are concerned that perhaps the father became willing and she requires a get and mi'un; how did these words of Tosafot escape his notice?

Moreover, according to his words there is the same difficulty for Mordechai, who explains that the dispute of Ulla and Rav and Shmuel is specifically where the father is in town, but if the father is not in town we are concerned that perhaps the father will become willing; see Beit Yosef. If so, one could ask likewise: why does the Gemara challenge Ulla, perhaps the Mishnah speaks where the father is not in town? Rather, certainly in that case she requires a get and mi'un, and Tosafot's position returns to its place. Examine this carefully. Nor can one bring proof from the other question, "he may not sell her to relatives... what is the case of this widow," for perhaps it speaks where she was betrothed and married without her father's knowledge; rather, there is no distinction. See Tosafot there, s.v. "rather, she betrothed herself, a widow," who wrote that where he arranged the match it is as though her father betrothed her; if so, here too one can say that where she was betrothed and married without her father's knowledge, it is as though her father betrothed her. Examine this carefully, and see 46a in Rashi, s.v. "since an act of orphanhood was done with her during her father's lifetime." See Maharsha in the name of Tosafot Yeshanim. According to the words of Mordechai above, it is difficult: why does the Gemara ask, "what is the case of this widow," perhaps it speaks where the father is not present? See Kenesset HaGedolah 67a and Beit Shmuel.
