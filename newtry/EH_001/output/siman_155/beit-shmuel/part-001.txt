**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>From six years old, etc.</b> This follows the version of Rif and Rambam in Perek HaMitkabel: the transactions of young children are valid, and corresponding to them a minor girl may be betrothed for purposes of mi'un. In Perek Beit Shammai, R. Chanina says that any girl who can guard her kiddushin requires mi'un. Rambam explains that R. Chanina refers to young children, namely from six to ten, each according to her sharpness, and that sharpness means that she knows how to guard kiddushin. But below age six R. Chanina did not speak, and even if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she does not require mi'un. Regarding a get, however, even below age six she can receive the get, namely if she can keep a pebble when it is thrown to her, and she is divorced from her father's kiddushin according to Rambam's version. The reason is explained by the Ran in Perek HaMitkabel: kiddushin are money and require guarding, so for kiddushin she must at least have the measure of young children. What Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 of Ishut, that according to Rambam even under six, if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she requires mi'un, is astonishing to me, for in chapter 11 of Geirushin he wrote explicitly that even if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she does not require mi'un, as the Mechaber wrote here. However, according to Ramakh, cited by Kesef Mishneh in chapter 4 of Ishut, even under six, whenever she knows how to guard her kiddushin, she requires mi'un. He holds these are two separate measures: young children generally require mi'un, and if she knows how to guard her kiddushin, even under six, she requires mi'un. So too according to the version of Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh there, the measure of keeping a pebble when thrown to her applies to mi'un; this is what R. Chanina meant by knowing how to guard her kiddushin, even under six, as Ran wrote in Perek HaMekabel, for mi'un is only rabbinic kiddushin and does not require the measure of young children. Above, in siman 141, the Mechaber brought their view according to their version, and accordingly for mi'un even a girl under six requires mi'un. Here he omitted their view; perhaps he holds that since it is only rabbinic kiddushin, we are lenient. Also according to Ritva's approach cited nearby in the gloss, which is the approach of Raavad, Baal HaMaor, and others, there are two separate measures, and R. Chanina speaks even of one under six, who requires mi'un when she was betrothed by her brothers. Therefore it appears one should be stringent that she requires mi'un even under six.

(א) <b>Or until she gives birth.</b> In the first chapter of Yevamot there is a dispute: according to Rav Safra, children are like signs of maturity; according to R. Zevid, there are no children without signs. Tosafot and the Rosh wrote that in most cases when she gives birth she has signs. The phrase "there are no children without signs" does not mean absolute impossibility, but that children are not commonly found without signs. Even if they examined her and she had no signs, we are concerned that perhaps they fell out. Even where there is no Torah doubt, namely where he did not have relations after she matured, nevertheless with children we are concerned that perhaps the signs fell out. According to Rif, Tosafot, and the Rosh, according to Rav Safra even if she gave birth while a minor, the children count as signs after she matures; pregnancy during minority likewise counts as signs after the time. Rama should not have needed to write this law in the name of Or Zarua, because it is already explicit in Tosafot and the Rosh. (א) <b>That he did not have relations after she was twelve years and one day old.</b> Then, even if she is an adult, there is no Torah-level kiddushin, and therefore we are not concerned that the hairs may have fallen out. Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that in such a case women are believed that she has no hairs and she may perform mi'un, since there is no Torah concern, as written above. Likewise, where he did not have relations and women testify about her years that she is still not twelve years and one day old, they are believed. This may also be inferred from Rashi in Perek Ba Siman, Niddah 48b, who wrote that if they said the hairs are absent she may not perform mi'un because we are concerned that they fell out. This implies specifically where we say they may have fallen out; but where he did not have relations, she may perform mi'un. There is no proof that Tosafot disagree from what they wrote there, s.v. "Granted," that one cannot explain that the hairs are a wart and she may perform mi'un, for the latter clause teaches that she is not believed to perform mi'un. It must be that the case is where he did not have relations; otherwise, how could one have thought she may perform mi'un, since even if she is believed that these are hairs, we still suspect that other hairs fell out. Even if there were no hairs at all, she could not perform mi'un. Rather, it must be where he did not have relations, and nevertheless Tosafot wrote that she is not believed to perform mi'un. One can answer that their meaning is this: since the latter clause teaches that she is not believed to perform mi'un, it contradicts the first clause whether the whole baraita speaks where he had relations or where he did not. Rather, the first clause speaks regarding chalitzah, and in truth it speaks where he had relations. One can also say that if these hairs remained and did not fall out, we no longer suspect that she produced other hairs and they fell out, for since we saw these hairs and they did not fall out, why should we say she produced others and they fell out? According to this, Tosafot speak where he had relations, and nevertheless one might say she is believed to say she did not produce other hairs and may perform mi'un, and we do not suspect that new hairs fell out; therefore they needed to bring proof from the latter clause. The practical difference is where she was examined by men before the age and they saw hairs, and those hairs remained after twelve years. We no longer suspect that she produced other new hairs and they fell out, since the old ones did not fall out. Then even if he had relations after she was twelve, she may perform mi'un. At first glance it appears that all doubts here have a remedy: where a minor was married off by her mother and brothers and then fell before a yavam, she should perform mi'un before two and then perform chalitzah. She is then permitted to another either way: if she is an adult, she leaves through chalitzah; if she is a minor, she leaves through mi'un. Just as we find in several laws that she requires both mi'un and chalitzah, here too with mi'un and chalitzah no examination is needed and there is no doubt at all.
