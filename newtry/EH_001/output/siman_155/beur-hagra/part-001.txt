**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>What is, etc.</b> According to the Rif’s version in Gittin: the young children, etc., and corresponding to this she can become betrothed for mi'un. There, 59a: and until how much, etc. According to the Rif’s version, which reads, “a pebble and she throws it,” and corresponding to this she can be divorced, one must say that “a pebble and she throws it, a nut and she takes it” is one measure. If she distinguishes between her get and something else, meaning that she guards it, it is not like the nut, etc. Just as she knows how to distinguish between a nut and a pebble, so she distinguishes between a get and a nut or the like. But according to Rashi’s version, distinguishing her get is a higher level, and it is the level of the young children. The Rambam explains that for a get it is enough that she knows how to guard it alone, while for mi'un she needs the age of the young children, and knowing how to guard is like in divorce. He could likewise have written “a pebble and she throws it,” but he used the language of the baraita there, for this is the meaning of guarding her get and her kiddushin. Rava said “a pebble and she throws it” as a test, for then she knows how to guard kiddushin as well. So too the Rambam writes regarding divorce that she distinguishes between her get and another object. There is no distinction between here and get except that here she also needs the age of the young children, while there knowing alone is enough. But according to Rashi’s version, the knowing how to guard here does not require such a distinction between kiddushin and another object as by get, but only between a pebble and a nut. Also, here the age of the young children is not required. See above, siman 141, seif 6, where he brought the two opinions.

Furthermore, we say in Niddah 46a: Rav Dimi of Nehardea said, etc. This shows that if she brought signs, she cannot perform mi'un even if he did not have relations. But the Ra'avad wrote that there is no uncertainty at all, for the answer is proper. Rav Sheshet accepted his words, as it says, “he is greater,” according to the commentators who explain that this refers to Ravin. The sugya of the Gemara there follows him. As for Niddah, even Rav agrees that rabbinically she requires a get. So wrote the Rambam, and so wrote the Ramban and other decisors. This also appears in Niddah 52a in the Mishnah, “a young girl,” etc., and in the Gemara there: and Rabbi Yehudah agrees, etc. So too at the end of the first chapter of Chullin: wherever there is mi'un, etc., and Rav said: this, etc. This shows that Rav agrees, as above.
