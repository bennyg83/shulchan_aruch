**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or, etc.</b> According to our text and the version of other decisors: "let it be in your hand." The Ran wrote that we read "for me, in your hand," but without this it does not imply that she made him an agent for receiving. So it is proven in the Yerushalmi, and in the Tosefta it is also taught: "This is your get; give it to so-and-so," she is not divorced. "Let him receive it for me," she is divorced. This is as written in Kiddushin, in the first chapter of Kiddushin. The same applies to "let it be in your hand." In the Mishnah too we learned: "Take it for me," and in the baraita: "Take it for me," "carry it for me," "let it be for me." But "let it be a deposit" does not require "for me," because the husband did not give it as a deposit. This is the view of the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, that with deposit he did not write "for me," while in the latter clause he wrote "for me." See Rashi there, s.v. "let it be," etc., who follows his own approach, for he wrote that even with "receive" one needs "for me," as written above, siman 140, seif 4. But the Maggid Mishneh wrote there in the name of Ramban and Rashba that with "receive" one does not need "for me." This is what is written in the gloss: "and some say," etc. But the Rambam holds according to the simple reading of the Mishnah, for throughout this chapter we learned: "receive for me."

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whether, etc.</b> See Rashi there, s.v. "to receive," etc., and s.v. "by implication," etc. The Rosh wrote there that we hold like Rabbi Yochanan, and it is invalid both for delivery and for bringing. But Ri Migash explains that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees with Rabbi Ami, etc., because one cannot learn receiving from bringing. But regarding bringing he agrees, according to Rabbi Elazar’s inference. See Tosafot there, s.v. "one does not," etc. The Ran wrote that in the Yerushalmi it is explicitly implied like their words, for we read there: a case came before Rabbi Ami regarding a slave who brought the get, and he validated it. Rabbi Abba said to him: But Rabbi Chiyya taught that a slave who brought the get is not valid. Rabbi Ami said: If not for Rabbi Abba, we would have permitted a married woman. And if you say Rabbi Ami did not hear this that Rabbi Chiyya taught, did he not hear the words of the Rabbis? Let us make him hear it from this: Rabbi Zeira said, Rabbi Chiyya in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: the words appear that a slave may receive his own get of emancipation, but may not receive, etc. The Rashba wrote that for practical halacha we act like Rashi, stringently. It sounds that he was uncertain. Beit Yosef.
