**** ENGLISH ****
(ח) <b>She is believed.</b> By migo, even if the get is in the agent's hand, and even in one city she is believed by the migo that she could say, "You gave it into my hand and I deposited it with the agent." Then she is believed, for a woman who says "you divorced me" is believed. Although the agent assists her, we do not say that she is brazen wherever there are not two witnesses assisting her. So wrote the Rosh. See the beginning of siman 17, where the Tur wrote that even if there is one who assists her, she is brazen, unlike the Rosh; this requires resolution. But if she does not say, "you gave it to the agent before me for divorce," and they are in one city, the agent is not believed according to these poskim. We do not say a migo that the agent could give the get into her hand, for one can say that she does not wish to receive the get whenever she does not know with certainty that the husband gave it to the agent for divorce. So it implies from the Rosh.

(ג) <b>Or the woman admits.</b> Certainly, if the Get is already in her hand and another man betrothed her, she is not believed to say that he canceled this agency so as to forbid herself to the second man. Also, when the divorcing husband is a kohen, she is not believed to permit herself to him. This speaks of a case where the Get came into her hand, for if it did not come into her hand, the earlier difficulty returns: why is he not believed without her statement? When one cancels the agent through another agent, Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that the agent counts as one of the two; see the Rosh in Perek HaSholeach. Ri"v wrote that he does not count even as one, because he stands in place of the husband. Beit Yosef wrote that the Yerushalmi implies that even when he cancels in the agent's presence, two are required, unlike Rambam, who rules that if he cancels in the agent's presence, two are not required. According to Beit Yosef's explanation, one can also answer that Ri"v does not disagree with the Yerushalmi: Ri"v speaks where he cancels not in the presence of the first agent, and then two are needed apart from the second agent, because the second agent stands in place of the husband. But the first agent does not stand in place of the husband, since he is canceling his agency; therefore the first agent joins when he cancels before him. Do not object from what Beit Yosef wrote, that the Yerushalmi holds that the agent does not join, as written at the beginning of the siman, for cancellation is different, as stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi.
