<b>ומשיגיע לידה תתגרש ודאי. </b> הוא לשון הרמב"ם בפ"ו וכתב ה"ה דפשוט הוא ועיין ט"ז סק"ב שכ' י"ל ולי הוא תימה רבתי מה מועיל מה שמגיע לידה כיון שכבר נתבטל השליחות של שליח הבעל במה שניתק משליחותו בשעה שקיבל עליו להיות שליח האשה לקבלה דהא א"א לו להיות בפעם אחת שלוחו ושלוחה א"ו דניתק משליחות הבעל כמ"ש רש"י בהדיא וא"כ ממי תתגרש כו' וזה דומה ממ"ש למ"ש בסי' ק"מ סעיף ח' כו' עכ"ל. ועי' בת' מהרי"מ מבריסק סי' ל"ח שכ' ליישב קושיא זו בטו"ט דכאן אף דקיבל דבריה להיות ש"ק מ"מ לא ביטל לגמרי השליחות אלא הוי רק כמו ג"ד בגיטא ולאו מילתא היא ועדיין יכול להיות שליח להולכה אם רוצה ושאני התם בסי' ק"מ כיון דבתחילת שליחותו גילה לבטל שרצונו דוקא להיות ש"ק הגם דלא ביטל בפירוש שליחות הולכה של האשה מ"מ שליח להולכה בבעל תליא והבעל סמך על דברי השליח ולא עשהו כלל ש"ה כיון דחזי דרצונו דוקא בשליח קבלה אבל הכא שכבר נעשה שליח הבעל אלא שאח"כ עשהו האשה ש"ק אמרינן ג"ד לאו מלתא היא כו' ומסיים וכיון דדעת הרמב"ם ז"ל הוקבע להלכה בש"ע והאחרונים סתמו כוותיה הכי נקטינן להלכה. ויצא לנו מזה דאף אי נימא דשליח יכול לבטל שליחותו בלי ידיעת הבעל היינו אם ביטל בפירוש אבל גילוי דעתא לבד גם בשליח לאו מלתא היא (לאפוקי מדעת הרב השואל שם) עכ"ד ע"ש
**** ENGLISH ****
And when it reaches her hand she is certainly divorced. This is Rambam's wording in ch. 6, and Maggid Mishneh wrote that it is obvious. See Taz s.k. 2, who wrote some say — but I have great wonder: what help does its reaching her hand provide, since the agency of the husband's agent was already voided when he accepted to be the wife's reception agent, for he cannot be both his agent and her agent at once — or [it means] the husband's agency was severed, as Rashi states explicitly — if so, from whom is she divorced? etc. This resembles what is written in siman 140, seif 8, etc. — end quote. See the responsum of Maharim of Brisk, siman 38, who resolves this difficulty by way of Tosafot: here, even though he accepted her words to be a reception agent, he did not fully void the [delivery] agency; rather, it is merely a declaration of intent (giluy da'at) regarding a get, which is of no effect — and he can still be a delivery agent if he wishes. Whereas there in siman 140 it is different, since from the outset of his agency he revealed that his will was specifically to be a reception agent; even though he did not explicitly void the wife's delivery agency, the delivery agency depends on the husband, and the husband relied on the agent's words and never made him a delivery agent at all, since he saw his will was specifically for a reception agent. But here, where the husband's agent was already made [such], and only afterward did the wife make him a reception agent, we say a declaration of intent is of no effect, etc. He concludes: since Rambam's view has been established as the ruling in the Shulchan Aruch and the later authorities followed him, this is how we hold. And we learn from this that even if one says an agent can void his agency without the husband's knowledge, that is only if he voided it explicitly — but a declaration of intent alone, even regarding an agent, is of no effect (against the view of the questioner there) — end quote — see there.

<b>שאין האשה יכולה. </b> עבה"ט דהריב"ש כ' דהעיקר כו' ועיין בס' גט מקושר בסג"ש אות כ' שכ' דלפום נוסחא דידן דאומר תן אותו לידה אפילו בשעת הדחק אינה יכולה לעשות שליח לקבלה דהיכא דאמר לידה ודאי איכא קפידא אפילו להפוסקים כר' חנינא כמפורש בת' הרשב"א שהביא הב"י ס"ס זה והריב"ש שלא חילק בזה לא ראה תשובת הרשב"א וא"כ הרב ב"ש נתן מכשול למעיינים כמה שהביא להלכה דברי הריב"ש דהא לפי נוסח דידן שאומר לידה הוי קפידא לכ"ע וא"א שיקבל שלוחה מיד שליח בעלה אם לא שפירש הבעל לידה או ליד שלוחה וגם היא נתנה רשות לשלוחה לקבל מיד הבעל או שלוחו אבל בלא"ה אפילו בדיעבד יש להחמיר ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
A woman cannot appoint a reception agent, etc. Ba'er Hetev — Rivash wrote that the main ruling is etc. See the Sefer Get Mekushar in Siddur HaGittin, letter 20, who wrote: according to our text, which reads "give it to her hand," even in a pressing situation she cannot appoint a reception agent, for wherever he says "to her hand" there is certainly an objection, even according to the authorities who hold like Rabbi Chanina — as is explicit in the responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. The Rivash, who did not make this distinction, had not seen the responsum of Rashba. Accordingly, Beit Shmuel misled students by citing Rivash as the ruling, for according to our text which reads "to her hand" there is an objection for all opinions, and it is impossible for her agent to receive [the get] from the husband's agent — unless the husband explicitly specified "to her hand or to her agent's hand," and she also authorized her agent to receive it from the husband or his agent. Otherwise, even after the fact one should be stringent — see there.

<b>ה"ז ספק מגורשת. </b> ומסיים הרמב"ם שאין השליח נאמן להתיר ערוה אע"פ שהוא עד אחד אלא מפני כתב העדים שחתמו שהן כנחקרה עדותן עד שיהא שם מערער כמו שבארנו. ואם היו שם שני עדים שיעידו שגט זה הבעל נתנו לשליח לגרש בו הרי זו מגורשת ע"כ. וכתב על זה בספר בית מאיר וז"ל נשמע מזה שגט בלא עדים חתומים עליו הבא ממקום למקום אי אפשר זולת בעידי קבלה מיד הבעל וזה ברור אף לר' אלעזר. ואפילו ירצה השליח לו' בפ"נ לא מהני כיון דלא שייך עדים החתומים על השטר כמי שנחקרה כו' ויש לתמוה על התוי"ט פ"א מ"ג המביא גט ואינו יכול לו' בפ"נ ובפ"נ אם יש עליו עדים יתקיים בחותמיו. כתב אם יש כו' דקיי"ל עדי מסירה כרתי נראה דקשה לו מה זה לשון אם דמשמע דאפשר בלא עדים הא לר"מ לא סגי בלאו הכי ופירש דקיי"ל כר"א והוא תימה דמשמע הא יכול לומר בפ"נ כשר אף אם אין עליו עדים וזה בלתי אפשר אף לר"א זולת ע"י עדי קבלה מן הבעל ואז א"צ לכלום (כבסי' קמ"ב סי"ב) אמנם מדברי הר"ן שם נראה שמפרש אם יש עליו עדים היינו עדי קיום כו' עכ"ד. וקצרה דעתי מהבין דבריו מ"ש ואפילו ירצה השליח לומר בפ"נ כו' וכן חזר ושנה לשון זה עוד בתמיהתו על התוי"ט וכ' משמע הא יכול לומר בפ"נ כו' ולא ידעתי איך שייך לומר שהשליח יכול לומר בפ"נ כיון שאין עדים חתומים בו איך יאמר ובפני נחתם והרי מבואר במשנה פ"ב ובש"ע סי' קמ"ב סי"ו דאם אמר בפני נכתב ולא אמר בפני נחתם אינו כלום וצ"ע. ובחידושי כתבתי בזה קצת ישוב לדברי התוי"ט הנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
This is a doubtful divorcée. Rambam concludes: the agent is not believed to permit a forbidden relationship even as a single witness, except by virtue of the written document with the witnesses' signatures — for they are considered as if their testimony has been examined [in court], so long as no one contests it. If there were two witnesses who testify that the husband gave this get to the agent for the purpose of divorce, then she is divorced. Beit Meir wrote on this: from this it follows that a get without signed witnesses that travels from place to place can only [be validated] with witnesses to the delivery from the husband's hand — and this is clear even according to Rabbi Elazar. And even if the agent wishes to say "in my presence it was written," it is of no effect, since the principle of "witnesses signed on a document are as if their testimony was examined" does not apply [without such signatures]. One should wonder at Tosafot Yom Tov (ch. 1, Mishnah 3): a bearer who cannot state "in my presence it was written and in my presence it was signed" — if the document has witnesses, it may be validated through its signatories. By writing "if it has witnesses" — given that we rule that witnesses of delivery are decisive — it seems he was troubled by the phrasing "if," implying it could be without witnesses; for according to Rabbi Meir this is inadequate; so he explains that we rule like Rabbi Elazar — but this is surprising, since it implies the agent can say "in my presence it was written" as valid even without signatories, which is impossible even for Rabbi Elazar except through witnesses of delivery from the husband — and then nothing else is needed (as in siman 142, seif 12). However, from the Ran it appears he explains "if it has witnesses" to mean witnesses for validation, etc. — end quote. I am perplexed by what Beit Meir means when he says "even if the agent wishes to say 'in my presence it was written' etc." — and he repeats this phrasing in his critique of the Tosafot Yom Tov, writing "it implies the agent can say 'in my presence it was written'..." — and I do not know how the agent could say "in my presence it was written and in my presence it was signed" when there are no signatures on the document; for the Mishnah (ch. 2) and the Shulchan Aruch (siman 142, seif 16) explicitly state that if one said "in my presence it was written" but did not say "in my presence it was signed," it is of no effect — and this requires study. In my novellae I have offered some resolution of this difficulty regarding the Tosafot Yom Tov's position.

**** ENGLISH ****
But only because it is written there, etc. See Maharival, siman 1, principle 4, siman 22, who infers from this that a written authorization is essential for this, and his word alone is not believed; and accordingly, if the authorization is not validated, he is not believed, etc. One must have an authorization whose witnesses' signatures are recognized, etc. — see there. See Beit Meir, who wrote: one who examines the responsa of Rashbatz will find grounds to be lenient if the authorization is a court document (ma'aseh beit din), as is customary. Also, Maharival's inference from the language of the Ran and the Shulchan Aruch here can be refuted — for even if it appears that his word alone is not believed, nevertheless when he holds an authorization and the permission is mentioned within it, there is already a presumption [regarding] the witnesses who signed, etc., and he is again believed to validate it, as the Rosh wrote; and especially for us, since the matter is already properly established in all documents, the majority supports him — see seif 38 — and this requires study — end quote. See the Sefer Get Mekushar in Siddur HaGittin, letter 20, who completely disagrees with the above Maharival and writes: in my humble view it is obvious — if he is believed regarding the main [validity of] the get and the agency, and we rely on him that the get is validated, all the more so should he be believed regarding the permission to appoint [another] agent, etc. What was enacted [requiring] an authorization document is because it states there that the husband grants permission, etc. — this was [done] because sometimes the agent does not specifically recall that the husband explicitly mentioned permission to appoint another agent; and when he has the authorization document, he can rely on it, etc. — see there.

But initially, etc. See the responsum of Bach, New Responsa (HaChadashot), siman 87, who wrote: regarding the validation of an authorization that was validated twice — because it was written close to the eve [of Shabbat] and due to the delivery date was written as "the twenty-third of the month," omitting the word "day" — even though there is no concern about invalidating it since there is no room for error about anything else, etc. — in any case it is not appropriate for us to release it from our hands with this deficiency, and we agreed to correct it and re-validate it a second time through the witnesses already signed on the authorization and through the court signed on the certification (hanhak), and we did not wish to write another authorization since the time had passed and we would have needed to change the standard text of authorizations — end quote — see there. His intention is that [otherwise] they would have had to write two dates: one for when the get was delivered to the agent, and one for when the authorization was signed — and it is not proper to change the format of the earlier authorities, as is noted in Darkei Moshe s.k. 15: there was an incident with witnesses who had signed the get who also signed the authorization, and Maharik required [them to] write another authorization with other witnesses, as he did not wish to change the wording of the authorization — see there. This is because the standard wording of all authorizations is "and its witnesses who signed it are so-and-so, in my presence and before so-and-so," whereas here it was written "and its witnesses who signed it are those signed below." Nevertheless, it is obvious that if there is no time to write it the same day, they may write it the next day and there is no impediment. **** ENGLISH ****
The entire agency. Ba'er Hetev wrote: the get is disqualified — Darkei Moshe; and Beit Shmuel concludes: perhaps because it is proven that the arrangers of the get were ignoramuses, which is why the get is disqualified, as I wrote below — end quote. See the Sefer Get Mekushar in Siddur HaGittin, letter 5, who writes on this: this seems incorrect. We are not dealing with a case where the disqualification became established afterward through evidence of theft or other disqualification or kinship; rather, it appears he holds that since the authorization was disqualified from the outset, the agent must be reappointed before valid witnesses. And since this agent stands in the wife's place, he holds that an agent cannot be appointed in writing, as stated in seif 35. But if the agent returns the get to the husband, the husband could certainly reappoint him; but then it would be antedated, and regarding an antedated get we are strict not to send it even through an agent, as the Rama writes in siman 127, seif 5. And one cannot say there were still other witnesses present at the time of appointment who were valid — perhaps they too intended to testify, and their combination was also voided; therefore it is disqualified in every situation. However, if [the place] is nearby so that it is possible (on the same day) to return it to the husband and have him reappoint [the agent] and give him an authorization before valid witnesses, it should certainly be validated, since returning it to the husband's hand does not make it antedated — so it seems to me — end quote.
