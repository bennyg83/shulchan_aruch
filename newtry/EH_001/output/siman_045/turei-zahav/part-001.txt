**** ENGLISH ****
Although he did not send the bridal gifts before witnesses. This implies that even in a place where only a minority first perform kiddushin and then send gifts, we are concerned even without witnesses; that is, even if both parties admit that no kiddushin took place. The Tur also implies this, for he wrote that according to Rabbenu Chananel, if there are witnesses that he did not betroth her after the matchmaking, we are not concerned; this sounds as though witnesses are specifically required, but the admission of both parties does not help, even where only a minority first betroth and then send gifts, since his conclusion is to be stringent even there. However, Terumat HaDeshen, siman 207, does not imply so. He wrote that even according to Rabbenu Chananel, if the sender of the gifts says, even under oath, and likewise the betrothed woman says, that he had not already betrothed her, they are not believed; only witnesses are believed. He explains that since the Sages established that in a place where people first betroth and then send gifts we are concerned lest he already betrothed her, he is not believed against what the Sages determined. Similarly, Or Zarua writes regarding one who has relations with his maidservant that wherever the Sages determined a person's intent, he is not believed to say, "I did not intend that." Moreover, since the local custom is to betroth first and then send gifts, if we believed him that he had not betrothed her yet nevertheless sent gifts, he would be claiming against the custom; one who claims against custom is not believed even with a miggo. From both of these reasons there is no concern where only a minority first betroth. The first reason, that the Sages determined his intent, does not apply where the place usually sends gifts first and only a minority betroth first, for we see that most townspeople do not have that intent, and the concern is only a stringency for the minority. The second reason, that he claims against the custom, likewise does not apply in a minority case. Responsa Maharshal, siman 21, also proves from Terumat HaDeshen that one should not be stringent where only a minority first betroth; although we are concerned for the minority when nothing is said, if he states explicitly that he did not betroth, there is no concern. He wrote even more in siman 101: if only one witness was present with the gifts there is no concern, for even according to one who is stringent about kiddushin before one witness, in the mere concern of gifts one should not be stringent. Rama soon cites an opinion that if he did not send the gifts before witnesses, etc.; even the second opinion that Rama cites as stringent agrees that if there were no witnesses either at the sending or at the giving, there is no concern. It is therefore surprising that Rama here copied "even though he did not send the gifts before witnesses." It is also surprising that the Tur wrote that according to Rabbenu Chananel we require witnesses that he did not betroth, implying that the admission of the man and woman does not help. Perhaps this applies specifically where most first betroth and then send gifts. Even though the Tur later rules stringently for both interpretations, we do not impose two stringencies at once: to be concerned for the minority, and also to say that the parties are not believed. Who says we go that far, especially since Maharik in siman 101 wrote that most poskim are not stringent in a minority case even where there are two witnesses to the gifts? There is certainly no proof from the Tur to be stringent and disbelieve their admission, for when he mentions witnesses that she was not betrothed, he may mean where someone says with certainty that she had been betrothed, not a case of mere uncertainty. Alternatively, the parties' own admission may be like witnesses, as at the beginning of Bava Metzia: "when they admit, or when there are witnesses," which equates the two; the Tur mentioned one of them. On this basis one can explain Beit Yosef's difficulty with Rashba. The words are straightforward: in a place where most send gifts first and only a minority first betroth, if there were no witnesses to the gifts there is no concern at all. According to Rashi there is no concern, because without witnesses there can be no kiddushin through the gifts. According to Rabbenu Chananel too, we need not fear that he had already betrothed her, because the same mouth that created the concern by saying he sent gifts is the mouth that permits by saying there was no kiddushin at all. This is specifically where only a minority first betroth and then send gifts. This appears correct and primary for halachah and practice, with the help of Heaven. I saw that my teacher and father-in-law wished to be stringent even with no witnesses at all and only their own admission, but he did not address what I have explained.
