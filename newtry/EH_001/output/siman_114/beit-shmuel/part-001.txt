**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>Specifically where they acquired from him by kinyan.</b> See siman 112 according to the view of Ramah brought there in seif 8: here an unspecified kinyan helps, because they acquired from him regarding all the conditions; regarding his daughters it does not help because of the concern for deposited funds. The reasons stated in chapter HaNizakin, that one does not collect from encumbered property, were said only regarding his daughters; but for his wife's daughter an unspecified kinyan helps. And what we rule, that these matters were not given to be written, so that even if he wrote them one does not collect from encumbered property, refers to where he wrote the matters as they occurred, "so-and-so obligated himself for such and such," as written in siman 51. But a full document, even if they wrote a document about all the conditions, is effective. According to Tosafot there, a kinyan is stronger than a document; if so, even for his wife's daughter a document does not help, only a kinyan, and for his daughters even a kinyan does not help. However, one can say that according to Tosafot, although a document does not help, an unspecified kinyan on all the conditions does help for his wife's daughter. All this follows Rif, Rosh, Rashba, and many poskim who rule like the view that the reason is because they are not written. Rambam, however, rules like the view that the reason is because they are not fixed, as written in Maggid Mishneh chapter 11. He also holds that one who obligates himself to his fellow to feed someone for five years is obligating himself in something without a fixed amount; even if they acquired from him, he is not obligated to pay, even from free property. However, when he obligates himself to his wife at the time of kiddushin, it is effective regarding free property even without a kinyan. But at the time of marriage it is ineffective unless they acquired from him or he wrote a document; then they collect from purchasers, as written at the end of chapter 23 of Hilchot Ishut. The Talmudic sugya in chapter HaNizakin appears to hold that Rav Huna bar Manoach's question applies both according to the view that the reason is that they are not written and according to the view that they are not fixed.

**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>In the place where her mother will be.</b> Why do we not say there is an assessment that he did not intend to give her daughter sustenance except as long as her mother is with him, just as we say an assessment regarding dowries of sons-in-law, as written in siman 53, that he did not intend to give the dowry unless his daughter would benefit? There it is different: the assessment is that his obligation was specifically to give to his daughter, and his daughter died, so he is exempt. But where a person obligates himself generally, we do not use an assessment to exempt him; what he obligated himself to do, he must fulfill. We do not say such an assessment, as written in siman 90 in the name of Mahariv. Taz, citing Terumat HaDeshen, distinguishes differently. There he ruled that one who obligates himself to provide sustenance for his son and daughter-in-law until a certain time, and his son dies within the time, must provide sustenance for his daughter-in-law until the time ends, just as here he must provide sustenance for his wife's daughter. If her mother must move from her place for some reason, Responsa Rashach, part 1, siman 177, is uncertain whether he gives her daughter sustenance in the place of her mother.

<b>ואם כתב לא' וכו'</b>. בתשובת רשב"א מבואר בוודאי אם מחייב את עצמו לזון אותה איזה זמן ומת תוך הזמן א"צ לשלם המזונו' להבא אבל זה היה מתחייב את עצמו ליתן לו איזה סך ולא קצב זמן של מזונו' אלא קצב זמן פרעון והיו יכולים להוצי' זה הסך אף ביום א' לכן חייב לשלם אף ע"פ שלא הגיע עדיין הז"פ, ומ"ש אם היה רוצה ליקח ממנו הסך ביחד וכו' כבר תמה ע"ז בח"מ ואפשר לומר דכוונתו אלו לא היה קבע זמנים לשלם היה הדין שצריך ליתן לו אותו הסך מיד לכן אפילו אם קבע זמנים מ"מ כבר חל החיוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>And if he wrote to one, etc.</b> In Responsa Rashba it is explained that certainly if he obligated himself to sustain her for a certain time and died within that time, he need not pay future sustenance. But in that case he obligated himself to give him a certain sum, and did not set a time for the sustenance; rather, he set a time for payment. They could have collected that sum even on the first day, and therefore he must pay even though the payment date had not yet arrived. As for what he wrote, "if he wanted to take the sum from him all at once," etc., Chelkat Mechokek already wondered about this. Perhaps his intent is that had he not fixed times for payment, the law would be that he must give him that sum immediately; therefore, even if he fixed times, nevertheless the obligation had already taken effect.
