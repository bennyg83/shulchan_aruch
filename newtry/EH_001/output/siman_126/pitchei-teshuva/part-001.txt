<b>רק במקום עיגון </b> כ' בס' ג"פ סק"י יש לחקור אם לא נמצא מי שיודע לכתוב אשורי כתיקונו אלא כתב אשורי משובש כגון כתב הנקרא אצל ספרדים משקי שהוא דומה לאשורי ויש מי שיודע לכתוב כתב פרובינצאל (הוא מה שקורין כתב רש"י כ"כ הוא ז"ל סק"ד יפה מאד והוא שעת הדחק ומקום עיגון כגון ש"מ שרוצה לגרש שלא תזקק אשתו ליבם קטן או שרוצה לפרוש במדה"י בהי מינייהו עדיף לכתוב גיטא ודבר זה הוא פלוגתא דרבוותא כפי מה שהביא מרן הב"י דלסברת יש מגמגמים טפי עדיף כתב משקייט שדומה לאשורי ולסברת מרן טפי עדיף כתב פרובינצל דכתב בפני עצמו הוא ולא גרע מכתב הכותים משא"כ כתב משקייט דכיון שהוא כתר אשורי צריך לכותבו כתיקון ממש ואפילו קוצו של יוד מעכב. והכי מסתבר כדברי מרן משום דבכתב משקייט כיון שאין האותיות כתיקונם שמא יקרא הקורא ב' במקום כ"ף או איפכא וכן בשאר אותיות תדמה אות לאות ונמצא הענין משתנה משא"כ בכתב פרובינצל אמנם אם הוא כתב אשורי ממש ואין האותיות דומה זו לזו אע"פ שאינו מיושר ויפה כאשורית שכותבי' בס"ת אפ"ה עדיף טפי מכתב פרובינצל עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only in a case of igun.</b> In Get Pashut, s.k. 10, he writes that one should investigate a case where no one is found who knows how to write proper Ashurit, but there is someone who can write defective Ashurit, such as the writing called among the Sephardim mashki, which resembles Ashurit, and there is someone who knows how to write Provencal script, which is what is called Rashi script. He wrote beautifully, and the case is one of urgency and igun, such as a deathly ill man who wants to give a get so that his wife not fall to a minor yavam, or one who wants to travel overseas. Which of these is preferable for writing the get? This matter is a dispute among the authorities, according to what Maran the Beit Yosef brought. According to the view of those who hesitate, mashki script is preferable because it resembles Ashurit. According to Maran, Provencal script is preferable, because it is an independent script and is no worse than Samaritan script. This is unlike mashki script, for since it is like Ashurit, it must be written with full precision, and even the point of a yud invalidates. It is logical like Maran, because in mashki script, since the letters are not properly formed, perhaps the reader will read a beit as a kaf or the reverse, and similarly with other letters, one letter may resemble another and the meaning will be changed. This is not so with Provencal script. However, if it is actual Ashurit script and the letters do not resemble one another, even though it is not straight and beautiful like the Ashurit written in a Sefer Torah, nevertheless it is preferable to Provencal script. End quote.

<b>באחד בשבת </b> עב"ש שכ' ולא יכתוב לשבת כי אז יש למטעי כו' וכ' בס' ג"פ ולפ"ז אם כתב הגט ביום ד' בשבוע וכתוב בגט בד' לשבת בלמ"ד דמשמעו דמשבת מתחיל המנין ויבינו שנכתב ביום ג' אלא שהוא ד' למנין שבת שעבר א"כ הוי גט מוקדם ופסול כמ"ש סי' קכ"ז ס"ב ומיהו יראה דיש להכשיר בדיעבד בכה"ג וראי' לדבר ממ"ש מרן סעיף כ"א אם כתב בד' עשרים יום כו' ה"נ יש לתלות כאן דטעה דחשב דפירוש לשבת הוי כמו בשבת ואפי' הריב"ש דפליג בההיא יראה דמכשיר בכה"ג עכ"ל. ומ"ש הב"ש ולשון ירח ע"ש גרש ירחים. הוא מדברי הלבוש וכ' בפ' ג"פ ויראה שאם כתב לחדש ולא נקיט לישנא בירח יש להכשיר בדיעבד. וכ' עוד בשם הרשב"ם סי' מ"ו בגט א' שכתוב בו במקום באחד בשבת בראשון בשבת והשיב דכשר בשעת הדחק כו' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On the first day of the week.</b> See Beit Shmuel, who wrote that one should not write "to the Shabbat," because then one may err, etc. In Get Pashut he writes that according to this, if the get was written on Wednesday and the get says "on the fourth to the Shabbat," with a lamed, which implies that the count begins from Shabbat, people may understand that it was written on Tuesday, since it is the fourth day from the previous Shabbat. If so, it is an antedated get and invalid, as written in siman 127, seif 2. However, it appears that bedieved one should validate it in such a case, and proof is from what Maran wrote in seif 21: if he wrote "on the fourth, twenty days," etc. Here too one can attribute the error to his thinking that "to the Shabbat" means the same as "in the Shabbat." Even the Rivash, who disagrees there, would appear to validate in this kind of case. End quote. What the Beit Shmuel wrote about the language of "yerach," see there, "geresh yerachim," is from the Levush. In Get Pashut he writes that it appears that if he wrote "to the month" and did not use the expression "in the yerach," one should validate bedieved. He further writes in the name of the Rashbam, siman 46, regarding a get in which instead of "on the first day of the week" it said "on the first in the week," and he responded that it is valid in a pressing circumstance, etc. See there.

<b>וכתב י"ב. </b> כ' בס' ג"פ ס"ק כ"א ויראה דהיינו דכתב נקודות על אותיות י"ב כו' ע"ש: וכ' עוד בס"ק ע"ג ויש להסתפק אם דוקא כה"ג י"ב לחודש וכיוצא דרגילי אינשי לכתוב או לומר כה"ג אבל אם כתב הגט בי"ז לחדש וכתב ביום טו"ב לחדש לא. ודעתי נוטה דגם בזה יש להכשיר דלפעמים רגילין לכתוב כה"ג בשטרות ואגרות שלומים. גם במספר השנים רגילים לכתוב פסוקים הרומזים למספר השנה כגון שנה זאת תל"ז כותבין שנת תז"ל כטל ועושין ב' נקודות על תיבת תזל וכיוצא בו כותבין בכל שנה ושנה אם אירע שנכתב בגט כיוצא בזה דעתי נוטה דכשר עכ"ד.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he wrote twelve.</b> In Get Pashut, s.k. 21, he writes that it appears this means where he placed dots over the letters yud-bet, etc. See there. He further writes in s.k. 73 that one may be uncertain whether this applies only in such a case as "yud-bet of the month," and similar expressions that people commonly write or say in this way, but if the get was written on the seventeenth of the month and he wrote "on the day tov of the month," perhaps not. My view inclines that even in this one should validate, because people sometimes write this way in shtarot and letters of greeting. Also in numbering years, people commonly write verses that allude to the number of the year, such as for the year tav-lamed-zayin they write the year "tizal katal," and they put two dots over the word. Similarly, they write in each year, and if something like this was written in a get, my view inclines that it is valid. These are his words.

<b>ניסן כו'. </b> עיין בס' ג"פ שכ' אם לא כתבו בגט שמות החדשים אשר עלו מבבל אלא כתבו לירח הא' לירח הב' דנשר גמור הוא כיון דמנין זה כתיב בתורה והכל יודעים דמניסן מנינן לחדשים כו' ע"ש. וכ' עוד דאם נכתב בגט לירח מנחם במקום לירח אב נ"ל דכשר בדיעבד כיון דהדבר מפורסם וידוע דחודש אב קרו לי' חדש מנחם וכן הדין במרחשון אם כתב חשון כשר בדיעבד. ועי' בה"ט ס"ק ב"ב דמרחשון חד תיבה וכ' בס' ג"פ שם ואם פסק שם מרחשון מר תיבה אחת וחשון תיבה אחרת צ"ע אם יש להכשיר ואת"ל כשר אם כתבו בשני שיטין יש להסתפק אם יהיה כשר ועי' סימן קכט סל"ב ול"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nissan, etc.</b> See Get Pashut, who writes that if they did not write in the get the names of the months that came up from Bavel, but wrote "the first month," "the second month," it is completely valid, since this count is written in the Torah and everyone knows that we count months from Nissan, etc. See there. He further writes that if the get was written "the month of Menachem" instead of "the month of Av," it appears to him that it is valid bedieved, since it is public and known that the month of Av is called the month of Menachem. The same law applies to MarCheshvan, that if he wrote Cheshvan, it is valid bedieved. See Ba'er Heitev, s.k. 22, that MarCheshvan is one word. Get Pashut writes there that if he split MarCheshvan there, "Mar" as one word and "Cheshvan" as another word, it requires study whether it may be validated. If you say it is valid, then if he wrote it on two lines there is room to be uncertain whether it is valid. See siman 129, seif 32 and 33.

<b>If he wrote "on the fourth," etc.</b> The reason is explained in the responsum of the Rosh, because "on the fourth" has no other meaning except the day of the week, since afterwards it says "twenty days of the month." In Perishah he further wrote a proof, that if it referred to the month it should have written "on four" and not "on the fourth." See there. See Get Pashut, who writes that the Perishah did not intend to rely on this proof alone, such that even if he also skipped the words "twenty days" and wrote "on the fourth of such-and-such month," it should be validated. See above, siman 127, seif 7. This is certainly not so, for in such a case one should not validate even in a place of igun. Rather, the Perishah's intent is that this reason is an additional support to the reason of the Rosh. He further writes that it appears to him that even if he wrote "on four" instead of "on the fourth," skipped the word "of the week," and then wrote "twenty days of the month," the get is valid, based on the reason of the Rosh alone, because "four" has no other meaning except the week, since afterwards it says "twenty days," etc. Also, it cannot be explained as a postdated get, see above, siman 127, seif 9, such that people might say the get was written on the twenty-fourth of the month, because if so he should have written "and twenty in it." Since he wrote the word "twenty" without a vav, it implies that four is the date of the week and twenty is the date of the month.

Also, if he skipped the word "year" and wrote "five thousand," etc., it is valid, because the plain wording explains itself that it is discussing the count of years. See Raanach, part 1, siman 106. A get that says "on the third day of the week, days of such-and-such month," and skipped the number of days, is valid. Maharam Alshich, siman 102. If the get was written in the month of Elul and the get says "on the fourth year five thousand," etc., and skipped the word "of the week," the number of the month, and the name of the month, there is room to say the get is invalid because it is antedated, since people may explain "on the fourth" as the name of the month Tammuz. However, my view inclines that it is valid, because the custom is not to write the months except by the names that came up from Bavel, and "on the fourth" has no meaning except the fourth day of the week. These are his words.

Regarding what Get Pashut wrote, "also if he skipped the word year," see the responsa in Gevurat Anashim, where two great authorities of the generation disagreed. The view of the Gaon Morenu HaRav Yehoshua, of blessed memory, was that it is obvious that if he entirely skipped the word "year" and wrote only "five thousand," etc., "to the creation of the world," it is valid, and no worse than if he skipped the word "of the week," where the Rosh, of blessed memory, wrote that it is valid because it has no other meaning. The same applies here: it is obvious that one cannot explain "five thousand," etc., as days or months. First, because that would be treating them as subordinate and making days into months and months into years, as stated in Perek Get Pashut, page 166, in the case of a shtar in which it was written "six hundred and a zuz," where we do not say six hundred perutot, because one treats them as subordinate to zuz, etc. Second, since the way of the world is to count years from creation, there is no concern, as Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah.

In Shulchan Aruch below, siman 127, seif 10, it is validated where he skipped "to the creation of the world," etc., and all the more so in our case, where no fool would err and count this as months, since it would then be before the giving of the Torah by several thousand years, etc. My grandfather, the Gaon Maharam Katz, of blessed memory, responded that he does not agree, and it appears to him that if he skipped the word "year," it is like a get that has no date, and one may say perhaps it refers to months and is antedated, etc. From the words of the Rosh, of blessed memory, regarding where he skipped the word "of the week," there is proof to the contrary, for he wrote the reason that "on the fourth" has no other meaning except the week, since afterwards it says "twenty days of the month." If so, in our case, where it has another meaning, it is invalid.

Furthermore, here one can say more strongly that "five thousand" refers forward to what is written in the get, "twenty-one days of such-and-such month, five thousand," meaning that the month is the five thousandth from creation. Also from the Gemara in the above Perek Get Pashut there is proof to the contrary, for Tosafot wrote there: It is difficult, for zuz too may be treated as subordinate to istira, etc. They answer that people commonly write shtarot in zuz, etc. If so, in our case, people commonly write months in shtarot, for the verse says "the twelfth month," so perhaps "five thousand" refers to months. I do not understand this, for in any case we do not find a count of months greater than twelve. Also, what he wrote that no fool would err in this, from the words of Tosafot in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah it is clear that we do not rely on this. They wrote there that if he skipped the thousands there is no concern because it says nearby "he left the first thousand," etc. This implies that without that reason it would be invalid, even though he skipped five thousand, etc.

There is another proof from what the Mordechai wrote, brought in the Beit Yosef, see Shulchan Aruch, siman 127, seif 12, and what I wrote there, that regarding a ketubah where the scribe erred and wrote "thousands" and skipped "four," Ri of Vienna validated it, because one cannot say a minority of two thousand years since he did not write "two thousand." This implies that without this reason we would invalidate it and say it was written two or three thousand years earlier. If so, in monetary law, and all the more so in gittin, etc. He discusses this at length. Nevertheless, it is explained at the end of the responsum there that in a great case of igun, one can possibly validate. See there.

See there further regarding a case where in the word shenat, the nun looked like a gimel, and a child who was neither wise nor foolish read it as nun. The Gaon Mahari wrote that this is simpler to validate, for even if we say that the word shenat is essential, it is valid in our case since we recognize the form of the matter, as the Ba'al HaItur wrote regarding "on the fourth" and "on the sixth," that if the reish of revi'i or the shin of shishi is missing, there is no concern, etc. The Gaon Maharam Katz, of blessed memory, wrote that the opposite is true: this is worse, because even if we say that if he did not write the word shenat at all it is not invalid, nevertheless a change invalidates it, as proven from Tosafot in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, etc. Furthermore, this is worse than an ordinary change, for it resembles what the Talmud says, not to write lemechak because it implies laughter. Here too it is like shigeg, lacking a root-letter gimel, as in the verse in Iyov, "if I have erred, my error remains with me." If so, here it implies that the five thousand is by error and not truthfully, and is like laughter. Therefore, even if a child who is neither wise nor foolish reads it as shenat, it does not help, as Maharik wrote that where it is obvious that the letter does not have its proper form, what does the child's reading help? Nevertheless, in a great place of igun, it appears to me to lean toward validating even in this case, etc. See there.

However, if she was already divorced with a get that does not contain "behold, you are permitted," but it does contain lemehach lehitnasba, and it is a place of igun where it is impossible to obtain another get, it appears to me that we can rely on most decisors, who hold that "behold, you are permitted" does not invalidate, since there are other expressions that are considered like "behold, you are permitted." See there further, where he wonders about Maran and Moram, of blessed memory, why they did not write that one must write "and this shall be for you from me." The word "this" must be written so that there are clear indicators that he divorces her with this get and not with speech, and the word "from me" must also be written so that there are clear indicators that he is divorcing his wife, etc. In truth, Maran the author mentioned this above, seif 9, and below, seif 44, but the intent of Get Pashut is why he did not mention that it is essential. He concludes that if he did not write "and this" or the word "from me," and she was divorced with that get, it appears to him that we judge her as doubtfully divorced. See there.

<b>דהוית ארוסתי. </b> כ' בס' ג"פ ס"ק ק"ב אשה נשואה נתגרשה בפני ב"ד הדיוטות ונפל ס' בגט אם היה כשר או לא וחזר המגרש וקידשה ואח"כ חזר בו ורוצה לגרשה ועתה שהגט הראשון הוא ספק אם הי' כשר א"כ חלו הקדושין ויש לכתוב עכשיו ארוסתי לכתחילה ואם לא הי' גט לא חלו הקדושין ויש לכתוב אנתתי ולכן לצאת מן הספק צריך לכתוב ב' גיטין אחד בלשון אנתתי ואחד בלשון ארוסתי כן עשה מעשה הרא"ש כמ"ש בתשובה ס"ס יוד וע"ש (והובא ג"כ במל"מ פ"ד מה"ג דין י"ב) ולי צ"ע דמדברי ת' הרא"ש שהובא בש"ע לק' סי' קמ"ג סי"ו דמ"כ אא"כ יקדשנה ויחזור ויגרשנה סתם ולא כתבו דצריך לתת ב' גיטין משמע דבגט א' סגי ולענין איך יכתבו דעתי נוטה דיכתבו ארוסתי (טעמא לא ידענא דלכאורה יותר נכון לכתוב אנתתי דאף אם באמת היא רק ארוסה כשר לכל הדיעות משא"כ אם יכתוב ארוסתי שמא באמת היא אשתו וע"ז יש מחמירים כמ"ש הב"ש ס"ק ט"ל גם הראי' שהביא מסי' קמ"ג איני מכיר דשם אין שום ספק על הגט הראשון ובודאי יש להחוב שם ארוסתי וצ"ע) וכ' עוד אם כתב בתחילת הגט אנת אנתתי פ' ובסוף הגט כתב דהוית ארוסתי או להפך שכתב בתחלה אנת ארוסתי ובסוף כתב דהוית אנתתי צ"ע אם כשר בדיעבד ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Who was my betrothed.</b> In Get Pashut, s.k. 102, he writes: A married woman was divorced before laymen, and there was a doubt about the get whether it was valid or not. The divorcing husband then betrothed her again, then later changed his mind and wished to divorce her. Now, since the first get is doubtful whether it was valid, if so the kiddushin took effect and one should now write arusati lechatchilah. But if it was not a get, the kiddushin did not take effect, and one should write antati. Therefore, to avoid doubt, one must write two gittin, one in the language antati and one in the language arusati. So the Rosh acted, as written in his responsum, end of siman 10. See there. This was also brought in Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchot Gerushin, law 12.

To me this requires study, for from the words of the responsum of the Rosh brought in Shulchan Aruch below, siman 143, seif 16, that he has no remedy unless he betroths her and then divorces her again plainly, and he did not write that he must give two gittin, it implies that one get is enough. As to how they should write it, my view inclines that they should write arusati. I do not know the reason, for at first glance it is more correct to write antati, since even if she is truly only betrothed, it is valid according to all views, unlike if he writes arusati and perhaps she is truly his wife, regarding which there are those who are stringent, as the Beit Shmuel wrote, s.k. 39. Also, I do not recognize the proof he brought from siman 143, for there is no doubt there regarding the first get, and certainly the obligation there is to write arusati, and this requires study.

He further writes that if he wrote at the beginning of the get "you are my wife so-and-so," and at the end of the get wrote "who was my betrothed," or the reverse, where he wrote at the beginning "you are my betrothed," and at the end wrote "who was my wife," it requires study whether it is valid bedieved. See there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One should validate.</b> In Get Pashut he writes: This requires study. Why did Maran, of blessed memory, not write that one should correct them by attaching them, as Maran wrote nearby regarding attachment of the legs of the hehs to their roofs? Perhaps because this matter, that someone else can correct even though he was not commanded by the husband, was said by the Rosh, of blessed memory, in that responsum regarding not lengthening the vavs, as the Tur wrote at the end of this siman. We see that the Rivash, siman 56 and 315, holds that it is preferable to give it without correction, since the child reads it as a vav. If correction were required, how could another person correct by lengthening the vavs? Maran held that regarding writing, such as lengthening the vavs or attaching the yuds of shin and ayin, it is preferable to give it without correction by another, since a child reads it properly. But regarding scraping away the attachment of the legs of hehs and kufs, it is preferable to give it after correction, and another may scrape them even though he was not commanded by the husband. He further writes: I saw in a handwritten responsum of Radbaz regarding a get that came from a distant land, where the legs of the tavs were distant from their roofs, and he validated the get through correction. In my humble opinion, it appears that they should give it without correction, then the agent should take it back, correct it, and give it to her a second time. End quote. See above, s.k. 44.
