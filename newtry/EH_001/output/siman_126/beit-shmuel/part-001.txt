<b>בא' לירח</b>. אף על גב בסעי' ד' פסק דכותבים ביום א' לירח כתב ב"ח אם ר"ח רק יום א' אז כותבים ביום א' שהוא מבורר טפי וכשר"ח שני ימים לא יכתוב ביום א' דהוה משמע יום הראשון של ר"ח אלא יכתוב בא' לחודש שלא בא להזכיר אלא מנין הימים שבחדש והרב רמ"א ע"כ לא ס"ל חילוק זה דאל"כ למה לא כותבין ביום השני דהא ליכא ספק כלל ולכ"ע לא כותבים יום והא דאין נותנים גט ביום א' דר"ח משום דכתבו המ' וכמה פוסקי' דאין ליתן גט ביום א' דר"ח והטעם הוא דצריך לכתוב שהוא יום ל' לחדש העבר ושמא יאמרו מוקדם הוא ולא כח"מ שכתב הטעם משום פלוגת' הר"ן והרשב"א דהא ס"ל דיכול ליתן גט ביום א' דר"ח וכתב בח"מ ביום א' דר"ח אין ליתן גט אלא במקום עיגון וביום ב' אינו אלא חששא בעלמא כמו אם ר"ח רק יום א':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On the first of the month.</b> Although in seif 4 he ruled that one writes "on day one of the month," Bach wrote that if Rosh Chodesh is only one day, one writes "on day one," because it is clearer. But when Rosh Chodesh is two days, one should not write "on day one," because that would imply the first day of Rosh Chodesh; rather, one writes "on the first of the month," because he intends only to mention the count of days in the month. The Rama evidently does not accept this distinction, for otherwise why would one not write on the second day, where there is no doubt at all? Rather, according to everyone one does not write "day." The reason a get is not given on the first day of Rosh Chodesh is that several decisors wrote not to give a get then, because one would need to write that it is day thirty of the previous month, and people might say it is predated. This is unlike the Chelkat Mechokek, who gave the reason as the dispute between the Ran and Rashba. In fact they hold one can give a get on the first day of Rosh Chodesh. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that on the first day of Rosh Chodesh one should not give a get except in a case of igun, while on the second day there is only a mere concern, like when Rosh Chodesh is one day.

<b>אדר סתם כשר</b>. כן ס"ל לרוב הפוסקי' וכ"כ בא"ח סימן תכ"ב, ובח"ה סימן מ"ג ואין חילוק בין אם ידע שהוא מעובר ובין אם לא ידע ובי"ד סי' רכ"ב הביא המחבר דעת הרמב"ם דמחלק בין אם ידע ובין אם לא ידע ועיין בש"ך שם הקשה למה לא חשש כן לדעת הרמב"ם וב"ח פסק ג"כ אם ידע שהוא מעובר ספק גט הוא ואין להקל, ולולי דבריה' היה נראה לומר דקי"ל כרוב הפוסקי' דס"ל סתם אדר הוא אדר ראשון ואין חילוק בין אם ידע ובין אם לא ידע אלא בנדרי' שהוא איסור דאורייתא חשש לדעת הרמב"ם אבל כאן ליכא א"ד דהא אף להרמב"ם אינו אלא מאוחר גם בגט מאוחר כשר לרוב הפוסקי' לכן לא חשש כאן להביא דעת הרמב"ם וכ"כ בט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he wrote "Adar" without specification, it is valid.</b> This is the view of most decisors, and so is written in Orach Chaim, siman 422, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 43. There is no distinction whether he knew it was a leap year or did not know. In Yoreh Deah, siman 222, the Mechaber cites the Rambam’s view distinguishing whether he knew or not. The Shach there asks why he was not concerned here for the Rambam’s view, and the Bach also ruled that if he knew it was a leap year, it is a doubtful get and one should not be lenient. Were it not for their words, it would appear that we hold like most decisors, that plain "Adar" means the first Adar and there is no distinction whether he knew or not. Only in vows, which involve a biblical prohibition, did he take the Rambam’s view into account. Here there is no biblical prohibition, for even according to the Rambam it is only postdated, and a postdated get is valid according to most decisors. Therefore he was not concerned to bring the Rambam’s view here. So wrote the Taz.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he wrote it with one yud.</b> Terumat HaDeshen wrote that if it was discovered immediately, he should give another get; if it was discovered only later, he need not give another get. So wrote Levush. Although with other invalidities there is no such distinction, here there is no real invalidity and it is different. Some are stringent to give two gittin in Iyar, as Darkei Moshe writes in the name of Maharil. The Shulchan Aruch omitted his view because he holds we do not give two gittin except under pressure, and then it is better to write with two yuds, because two gittin are written only with difficulty.

"Marcheshvan" is one word. Regarding "Kislev," Terumat HaDeshen wrote that in Scripture it is found lacking, while in Targum it is written full; we follow Scripture. Although most of the get is in Aramaic, the date is written in Hebrew: "two days," not "trei yomin." Therefore the date is written in Hebrew, the language of Scripture. This is not like "yimcha" with an alef, where we follow the Aramaic language, because "yimcha" is written among Aramaic wording, unlike the date. If he wrote "Kislev" full, it is valid, like "shemoneh," and so with all such cases.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he did not lengthen the vavs.</b> This means he did not write them in the form of vavs but in the form of yuds; therefore it is invalid. But if he wrote vavs and merely did not make them longer than other vavs, it is valid, as written by Tosafot and Responsa Rivash, siman 314. It also implies that the Rosh holds so, for in his rulings he did not write that they must be longer than other vavs. The Beit Yosef and Bach explain the Rosh’s responsum cited by the Tur as holding that if he did not lengthen them more than other vavs, it should not be given unless because of igun; if already given and the husband is far away, it is valid. This explanation does not appear correct. The Tur himself wrote earlier that the vavs need not be longer than other vavs, so he cannot later cite the Rosh as invalidating on that basis. Rather, the Rosh speaks where the vavs are like yuds, and even then he validated it. So ruled the Taz: even without pressing circumstances it is valid if he merely did not lengthen them.

The three views brought here are these: according to the Rambam, if he did not lengthen the vavs of "teruchin," "shevukin," and "piturin," or wrote the yud of "lemehach" or "piturin," or did not write the three yuds of "deyitehevyin" or "deyitzviyin," it is invalid even if the husband did not protest and even if he did not write it himself. If she remarried, she need not leave. The Itur, cited by the Rosh, Tur, and Maggid Mishneh, holds that it is invalid only if the husband protests that he wrote it that way intentionally. According to that view, if the husband wrote it or the scribe wrote from his mouth and the husband protests, she must leave her new husband; if the scribe did not write from his mouth and the husband does not protest, the get is valid and she may marry; and intermediate cases are invalid lechatchilah but if she married she need not leave. If the get came from elsewhere and we do not know who wrote it, lechatchilah it should not be given except in a case of igun, or if already given. If the foot of the hei in "lemehach" was not distanced, the law is the same. The "some say" here is not necessarily the Ba'al HaItur, as some small Shulchan Aruchs indicate. According to Ra'avad and Itur, the husband’s protest helps even if he did not write it; Rivash writes otherwise. The Rashba’s responsum explicitly states in the name of Rav Hai and the Geonim that when the husband wrote it, even if he does not protest, or the scribe wrote generally and the husband protests, the get is invalid. See the Rama in siman 155 regarding feet of heis touching. Regarding "monin" with a vav, the matter may depend on this dispute if the husband wrote it and protests, but the main view is that the dispute applies only to what is explicit in the Talmud; with "monin" everyone agrees she need not leave. In many places they write "monin" with a vav. If they want to correct before delivery, it is like the touching heis in siman 125; in a case of igun the scribe may correct even after the witnesses signed, because it is not a substantive correction.
