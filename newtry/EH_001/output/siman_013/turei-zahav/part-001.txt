**** ENGLISH ****
And he hired a wet-nurse for him. In the Tur the version is "or he hired"; and even with this version it would be "and he hired" like "or he hired" — for if per Beit Yosef's explanation "and he hired" refers to what precedes — we need two matters; why afterward "in her husband's lifetime" since they already wrote so — Maharach; and accordingly if he hired a wet-nurse alone it suffices even near her husband's death; and if so there is a question: what is written afterward — a different division: or she gave to a wet-nurse three months, etc.; and in the Tur truly this is not the division at all; rather there is a question: behold Beit Yosef wrote the Rosh in responsum was astonished at what he saw in the Geonim's words this division of three months before her husband's death; and on this the Rosh wrote: it seems they knew that within three months milk can return to the woman and not afterward; and so is his conclusion for practical law; also Rivash wrote in the name of responsum of the Ran this law of three months — therefore it seems primary as Beit Yosef wrote regarding the law of milk stopping for the child: not sufficient except specifically after three months; and that the Tur did not mention three months — because it is not a fixed matter; for Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rivash that what the Gaon wrote three months — he holds the majority's milk stops after three months; but there are those who stop after one or two months; and if so we explain the Tur's words: what is written "or her milk stopped" — meaning the known measure for her milk stopping; or "she hired a wet-nurse for him in her husband's lifetime" — meaning even though her milk did not stop but of good will she hired him a wet-nurse in her husband's lifetime as the poskim's measure for milk stopping; and per the version "and she hired" it also seems not sufficient if she hired less than three months or measure of milk stopping — only a question what is missing and she is not his wet-nurse entirely even a small amount but she hired him a wet-nurse — and from this the child's life; and this is in any case as the measure of her milk stopping; and one may not challenge this version "and she hired" — for the Tur cited in explanation in bet haba it seems his intent: if he did not write "in her husband's lifetime" only afterward "and she hired" — this means after her husband's death her milk stopped and before her husband's death she hired him a wet-nurse even a short time — it teaches us we require it stopped in her husband's lifetime — i.e., the poskim's measure; and in any case three months suffice for all women.
