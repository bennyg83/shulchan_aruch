**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>One who violates Dat Moshe, etc.</b> The rule is that she does not leave without a ketubah unless she caused him to stumble in a matter of prohibition, or she transgressed a sin for which children die, or she acted immodestly, in which case there is concern that she may commit zenut under him. But because of some other prohibition she committed, she does not leave without a ketubah. See Responsa Rosh, kelal 32 siman 3, where he wrote that a woman who apostatized is not considered one who violates Dat Moshe and Yehudit, since it is not among the above concerns. Rather, one can say that there is a stumbling block for her husband and for the children she will bear from him, for they will have great disgrace that their mother was among the uncircumcised; and it is also possible that there is danger for her husband to live with her in the place where she apostatized. Shiltei HaGibborim wrote that if he causes her to stumble in a matter of prohibition, or if he vows and does not fulfill, we compel him to divorce and give a get. This is when she comes with the claim "I need a staff for my hand"; where he vows and does not fulfill, since the children die for that sin, she must claim "I need a staff for my hand." If she vows and does not fulfill, and she is barren and has no child, Piskei Mahara'i, siman 68, was uncertain whether she leaves without a ketubah, since she has no children; even though perhaps if she had not violated vows a miracle would have occurred for her and she would have had children. See there.

<b>שגם הוא עובר וכו'</b>. כיון דהאי גברא לא בעי בנים לכן לא הפסידה ואם היא האכילו דבר איסור והוא אוכל גם כן דבר איסור לא הפסידה ואם היא האכילו דבר איסור והוא עובר על נדרים כתב במרדכי בשם מהר"מ דהפסידה הכתובה דקי"ל מומר לדבר אחד לא הוי מומר לדבר אחר וי"ל אף על פי שהוא עובר על נדרים מ"מ  אינו אוכל דבר איסור וכן להיפוך כתב בפסקי מהרא"י סי' ס"ח בזה"ז צריך דקדוק היטב להוצי' אשה בלא כתובה מחמת שעוברת על נדרים דרבים מקילין בנדרים, ואם תשבע היא על איזה דבר שעשתה שאם תעשה עוד מחויבת לקבל גט ממנו ועברה על השבועה נראה השבועה חל דהא חר"ג היה לטובתה שאל יגרש אותה בע"כ והיא יכולה למחול מכ"ש בזה"ז די"ל כבר כלו הזמן החרם, וא"כ דהשבועה היה חל עליה ועברה על השבועה נמצ' דהיא עוברת על הדת ויכול לגרש אותה בע"כ וכתב בח"מ ה"ה אם הוא עובר על שאר עבירות אשר בנים מתים לא הפסידה אם היא עוברת על הנדרים מטעם הנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>That he too transgresses, etc.</b> Since this man does not desire children, she has not lost her ketubah. If she fed him forbidden food and he himself also eats forbidden food, she has not lost it. But if she fed him forbidden food while he transgresses vows, Mordechai wrote in the name of Maharam that she has lost her ketubah, for we hold that an apostate regarding one matter is not an apostate regarding another. One can say that although he transgresses vows, nevertheless he does not eat forbidden food, and likewise the reverse. Piskei Mahara'i, siman 68, wrote that in our time one must examine very carefully before removing a woman without a ketubah because she transgresses vows, for many people are lenient with vows. If she swore regarding something she had done that if she does it again she must accept a get from him, and she violated the oath, it appears that the oath takes effect. The cherem of Rabbeinu Gershom was for her benefit, that he not divorce her against her will, and she can waive it; all the more in our time, when one can say that the time of the cherem has already ended. Since the oath took effect upon her and she violated it, she is found to be one who transgresses dat, and he may divorce her against her will. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that the same applies if he transgresses other sins because of which children die: if she transgresses vows, she has not lost her ketubah, for the above reason.

<b>אשה שהמירה דתה וכו'</b>. בתשו' הרא"ש כתב אם המירה באונס ולא קדשה השם לא הוי עוברת על דת דהא לא הכשילה לבעלה מיהו איכ' למימר בזה איכא תקלה לבעלה ולבנים אשר תלד ממנו גם אפשר שיהיה סכנה לבעלה לדור עמה במקום שהמירה וע"כ אפשר שהפסידה הכתובה ודוק' בהתראה אבל אם המירה ברצון תליא בפלוגת' שהבי' בסי' ז' ולהני פוסקים דס"ל דאסורה על בעלה משום דינה כזינתה אז א"צ התראה כמ"ש ברמב"ם אפילו בעידי כיעור א"צ התראה והרב רמ"א לטעמיה אזיל שהבי' שם סי' ז' דעת המקילין אפי' אם המיר' מרצון לא אמרי' שזנתה א"כ אפי' לענין עוברת עד"מ שוה המירה ברצון להמיר' באונס כל שאינ' חשש זנות, מיהו נראה עיקר אם המירה ברצון אסור' לבעל' דהא הרא"ש משאנ"ץ ס"ל כן כמ"ש בתשו' מהרי"ק סוף שורש ק"ס והג"מ ות"ה סי' רמ"א וכן משמע בתשו' הרא"ש אפי' אם היה פלוגת' א"י להוצי' הכתובה אפי' אם לא התרה בה אף על גב לדעת האוסרים אינו אלא ספק שמא זנתה לא הוי כאלו טוען הוא שמא פרעתיך והוי החוב בודאי והפרעון ספק הוא והאומר יודע אני שהלויתי וא"י אם פרעתיך חייב ש"ה דהיא אסורה לו והיא גרמה האיסור לכן לית לה הכתובה כמו בקינוי וסתירה כמ"ש סס"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>A woman who apostatized, etc.</b> In Responsa Rosh he wrote that if she apostatized under coercion and did not sanctify Hashem's name, she is not considered one who violates Dat, for she did not cause her husband to stumble. However, one can say that even here there is a stumbling block for her husband and for the children she will bear from him, and it is also possible that there is danger for her husband to live with her in the place where she apostatized. Therefore it is possible that she forfeited the ketubah, specifically with warning. But if she apostatized willingly, this depends on the dispute brought in siman 7. According to those poskim who hold that she is forbidden to her husband because her law is like one who committed zenut, she does not need warning, as written in Rambam: even with witnesses of ugly conduct, warning is not required. The Rav Rama follows his own reasoning, for there in siman 7 he brought the lenient view that even if she apostatized willingly we do not say she committed zenut. If so, even regarding one who violates Dat Moshe, voluntary apostasy is equivalent to coerced apostasy wherever there is no concern for zenut. However, the primary view appears to be that if she apostatized willingly she is forbidden to her husband, for the Rosh of Sens holds so, as written in Responsa Maharik at the end of shoresh 160, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot and Terumat HaDeshen, siman 241; so too it appears in Responsa Rosh. Even if there is a dispute, he cannot be required to pay the ketubah even without warning. Although according to those who forbid her it is only a doubt, lest she committed zenut, this is not like a case where he says "perhaps I paid you," while the debt is certain and payment doubtful, in which case one who says "I know I borrowed but do not know whether I repaid" is liable. There it is different: here she is forbidden to him and she caused the prohibition, and therefore she has no ketubah, as in the case of warning and seclusion, as I wrote at the end of the previous s.k.

He overlooked Tosafot in chapter Af Al Pi, Ketubot 63, who disagree with this and do not permit her on that basis when she says "I am impure"; see there. Piskei Mahara'i, siman 222, asked that these words of Tosafot contradict one another, and answered that what they wrote in chapter Af Al Pi is from the words of Rashba of Sens, who composed Tosafot. So too it appears from this gloss: where there are indications, she is forbidden, with no distinction. If a widow married a kohen and after the marriage it became clear that she had committed zenut before the marriage, and she said that she committed zenut with her sister's husband, she thereby became a zonah and is forbidden to her husband, who is a kohen. This is what Rif, Rosh in chapter HaOmer, and the Tur in siman 178 wrote: he is not obligated by law to divorce her, even though he was silent; we do not say silence is like admission, as written in Tosafot in chapter HaOmer. These laws should not be compared to the other cases.
