<b>Seif 4 (that he warned her).</b> Regarding what is explained in Even HaEzer siman 115, that a woman who violates dat Yehudit requires warning, it appears obvious that this is only with respect to forfeiting her ketubah. But regarding the point that Cherem deRabbeinu Gershom does not apply in such a case, no warning is needed. Whenever it becomes known that she violates dat Yehudit, he may divorce her against her will; all the more so where she violates dat Moshe, such as serving him while she is a niddah, as regrettably happened here. It became known from her own mouth, though she says it was only a stain on the lower part of her garment, and now says perhaps it was from scratching. She held herself as a niddah for two weeks, and they ate separately in the manner of a niddah before people living with them. Afterward she said she would go immerse, took a garment with which women wrap themselves to go to the market, and said she would take a knife to cut her nails. She then returned home and was not at the mikveh at all, as became known from the women appointed over the bathhouse; she also admitted before a beit din of three that she had not been at the mikveh. Yet before her husband she said that she had immersed. The matter was recognized because they saw that her nails were long, investigated, and the matter became known. Outside she said that she immersed on the night her husband came home, and this too became known as false from her own mouth and from the women at the mikveh. She offers as an excuse that it was only a stain, and she gives no other claim of excuse, only saying that she will not return again to her folly in such a sin. The husband and his father agreed not to rely on her repentance, because she is established as pursuing food, and who will guard her to make sure she fasts? Without fasting there is no repair. In particular, her husband's money is not precious in her eyes, so that she would be careful to guard it. She has also been established in many matters of laxity, in salting and in cooking groats that had passed six weeks, where they warned her about the concern of whitening, as is known. She said that her husband had inspected them in the sun and there was nothing in them, but afterward it became known that her husband had not inspected them at all. Also, when she told her husband that she had immersed at the proper time, and from then until her husband came to town the time of her veset had passed. Although she was nursing, she had nevertheless seen after the completion of a year from birth, and then a second time, which she now says was only a stain. For this reason her husband was concerned that the time of the veset had passed. She said that she had examined herself after the veset and was pure; now before the beit din she said that she had not examined herself, only that since she did not see actual blood at the time of the veset she was not concerned. They also now found large stains on her garment, which are impossible without sensation and her knowing of an actual seeing. In such a case it is obvious that even if in dat Yehudit warning would be needed even to divorce her against her will, with respect to violating dat Moshe, where there are opinions that no warning is needed at all even for her ketubah, regarding a get against her will a doubt would be enough to be lenient in a rabbinic matter according to all. But none of this is needed, because it is explicit in the Mordekhai, Perek HaMadir, that regarding a get against her will no warning is needed according to all, even for dat Yehudit and all the more so for dat Moshe. So too is it in the Beit Yosef at the beginning of the siman and, briefly, at the end of the siman. This is also the implication of the Shulchan Aruch: even where she has not lost her ketubah, it is proper to divorce her against her will. It is clear that in the case above the opinion in siman 178 about what causes the annulment of Cherem deRabbeinu Gershom does not apply at all; see there. Even there that is not the practical law, and I also heard from my father-in-law, may his light shine, that we do not rule that way. There he has no basis to believe her according to the words of Chazal and he chooses to believe her; here, by contrast, it was clarified from her own mouth and from the women as above. In the holy book Hafla'ah it is written that since the holy Torah believed women regarding niddah, women in this matter are like witnesses; see there. Nor does the concern apply that she set her eyes on another man, since in her view repentance would help her, and she did not say it on her own initiative but only after the matter became visible outside. From now on she is forbidden to him by her own words until she immerses according to all, for the holy Torah believed her regarding niddah. As for his unwillingness to rely on her future repentance, she has no claim against him, for this is like a man who does not wish to marry a woman upon whom there had been a concern of rumor, because of what passed; indeed, he has a mitzvah to divorce her. It is clear that what Tosafot write at the end of Nedarim, that some say he thereby removes Cherem deRabbeinu Gershom, applies only where the Talmud explicitly states decisively that we presume she has set her eyes on another man. Perhaps they knew that this is so in the majority of cases. But here, where we have no basis to say that she will make a proper repentance, and in any event regarding the past Chazal said decisively that it is a mitzvah to divorce her, and also because lashes are administered on the basis of established presumptions, since she was established as a niddah through actions as well, she is definitely included as a niddah. Afterward it is certainly known that she did not immerse. We rule that even one who violates a rabbinic law is included in one who violates dat Moshe. Here it is impossible that there was immersion after washing and inspection, with the washing by day and immersion at night, because we are witnesses that there was no woman who performed inspection for her, since there are women appointed in the city for this. At the very least she is included as one who violates dat Yehudit by changing the required washing and inspection. The rule that a person does not make himself wicked does not apply here, because on the contrary, she was established in wickedness not by her own mouth; from her own mouth some plausible excuse would have been required, and there is none.

<b>I also found</b> what I wrote elsewhere concerning a woman who had intercourse while a niddah: the cherem of Rabbeinu Gershom, z"l, does not apply here, neither regarding taking two wives nor regarding divorcing her against her will. She, and likewise her father and mother, are evading the matter, and now their answer is that she is pregnant. He wishes to assure her regarding the child's needs. It appears that this is no claim at all. Wherever the enactment of Rabbeinu Gershom does not apply, he may divorce her, and he need not wait any time because she says that afterward she will accept it willingly, even if it were known that there is no deception and that she has a proper claim. All the more so in the present case, where it is plainly deception. Since the enactment of Rabbeinu Gershom does not apply, one should not prevent him even for a moment when he wishes to send her away with a get of dismissal. The claim concerning the fetus also is not found as a ground to delay a get of dismissal. Only payment of her ketubah is incumbent upon him first, and nothing else. May the good God do what is good in His eyes.

<b>סעיף ו' (ואם הי' מאמינה)</b> פעם אחד עיינתי עם הרב החסיד המפורסם בש"ט מו"ה חיים נ"י. האבד"ק טשערנאוויץ יצ"ו אודו' מהימן לה למשנה אחרונה דנדרים אם הוא מהחוה"ק ועיינתי אז בשו"ת המיוחסו' להרמב"ן ז"ל. בזה שמשם יש כעין הכרע ע"ש והתוס' סוף נדרים שכתבו מצד חרם דרגמ"ה ז"ל על שמאמין אין הכרע כ"כ כי י"ל שאין אנו מאמינים לו שמאמין ונהנה י"ל עוד ואין ההלכה קבועה כך ג"כ ושמעתי ממו"ח נ"י בזה שנוטה אצלו שהוא מהתוה"ק וכמו שבחינת שויא אנפשיה חתיכה דאיסורא ברור אצלו שהוא מהתוה"ק ואמר שכ"ה במוהרימ"ט ז"ל וגם שאיננו מצד נדר. דאם כן הי' מועיל התרה מכל מקום הוא מצד חזקה שהחזיק כן על עצמו וכ"ה בזה והביא ראיה מהחזקה דגבי טבח שעשה סימן בראש כבש שהוא טריפה. עתב"ש שם ואמרתי לו שאולי יש חילוק כי שם החזיק כן לגבי כ"ע ושייך שפיר חזקה מה שאין כן שויא אנפשיה לבד וכן במאמין ולא החזיק כן עדיין וצלע"ע:
