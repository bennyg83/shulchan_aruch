**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) <b>He had a son and a wife, etc.</b> This law is exactly the law mentioned in seif 10, and it is in Beit Yosef in the name of Responsa HaGeonim of Rav Hai Gaon, except that the reason there is not as Rama wrote here. Rama wrote the reason that everything is his, and this is difficult: what of it? Perhaps he did not wish to make his wife ruler over everything, but wished only to give his son a little without granting any authority. But the reason mentioned there in Beit Yosef is not difficult at all. This is its language: if he had given part of his property to a stranger together with his wife, his wife would be only an apotropos. But now that he wrote to his sons and not to others, how can we say that he made her an apotropos over his property, when he gave his sons what he wished? End quote. The explanation appears to be that the reason we say he made her only an apotropos is that a person does not set aside his sons from his property entirely. Here, since he gave to his sons, that concern is removed.

Therefore he must have intended it as a gift; otherwise how could the principal become subordinate and the subordinate become principal? With the wording "after you," the first recipient is primary.
