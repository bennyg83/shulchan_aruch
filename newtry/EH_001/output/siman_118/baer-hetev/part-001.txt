**** ENGLISH ****
(א) <b>In the Yerushalmi.</b> The enactment was made specifically regarding the dowry because of anguish, and they supported it on the verse "your strength will be spent in vain." But regarding inheritance that fell to her, namely nichsei melog, they did not enact. Responsa Rosh. It implies that even if the inheritance had not yet come into the husband's hand, nevertheless he acquired it. However, it appears that with nichsei melog that her father gave her, the Rosh agrees that he must return it, for there is also anguish in that. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that nichsei melog that she had before marriage means inheritance that fell to her before marriage; that must be returned. But inheritance that fell after marriage need not be returned. This does not appear to me; rather, anything that her father did not give her need not be returned. In Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 64, he ruled like the Rosh, that the enactment was not about nichsei melog that she had.

(ד) <b>Themselves, etc.</b> For "your strength will be spent in vain" does not apply when they themselves brought in the dowry. Now the simple custom among orphans who have inherited money is to write explicitly that because of lack and quarrel it shall stand according to the enactment of Shum as though her father had married her off during his lifetime. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that even if they forgot to write it, or the conditions are not before us, we follow the enactment of Shum, since they are accustomed to write it and it is as though they wrote it. See Ranach, part 1 siman 2; Rashdam, siman 254; and Keneset HaGedolah, folio 133a seif 6. The enactments of Shum mean that it returns in the second year, for the first year is the enactment of Rabbenu Tam. So wrote Maharival, part 3 siman 59, and Maharit, part 2 Even HaEzer siman 47. See Mishnat Binyamin, simanim 12 and 13, and Responsa She'erit Yosef.
