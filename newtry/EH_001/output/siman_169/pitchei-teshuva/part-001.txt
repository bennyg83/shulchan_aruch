<b>מצות חליצ'. </b> עבה"ט וע' בס' זכור לאברהם אה"ע אות ח' כתב בשם הגאון חיד"א ז"ל דמותר לעשות ב' חליצות ביום א' ואין לחוש משום אין עושין מצות חבילות ואחר גמר הראשונה ושהלכו החולצת והיבם ימתינו מעט ויעשו החליצ' הב' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The mitzvah of chalitzah.</b> See Ba'er Heitev. See Sefer Zachor LeAvraham, Even HaEzer, letter chet, who wrote in the name of the Gaon Chida of blessed memory that it is permitted to perform two chalitzot on one day, and there is no concern of "we do not make mitzvot into bundles." After the first one is completed and the chalutzah and yavam have left, they should wait a little and then perform the second chalitzah. See there.

<b>נמצא אחד מהם קרוב. </b> עיין במל"מ פט"ז מהלכות עדות שחקר שם בהא דקיי"ל נמצא א' מהם קרוב או פסול דגבי עדות עדותן בטילה איך הדין בדיינים אם הי' ב"ד יותר משלשה ונמצא א' קא"פ וכ' דמדברי רש"י פ' מצות חליצה (יבמות דף ק"ד ע"ב) אמתני' דחליצה בשנים או בשלשה ונמצא אחד מהן קא"פ חליצתה פסול' ופרש"י דנמצא א' קא"פ הדר הוי לי' שנים מבואר דדוקא בשלשה משום דאי מסלקת הפסול הוי לי' שנים אבל אם הי' יותר משלשה חליצת' כשיר' ואמנם מדברי הנ"י מבואר דאף שיש שלשה בלעדו חליצת' פסול' עכ"ד ע"ש וע' בס' ישועות יעקב סק"ג מ"ש בזה וע' בס' נה"מ בח"מ סי' ל"ו מענין זה. וע' עוד בס' ישועות יעקב סק"ו במעשה באשה אחת שמת בעלה בלי ז"ק והי' לו שני אחים אחד גדול הראוי לחלוץ ואחד קטן בן ד' שנים ובקושי גדול שהשיגה חליצ' מאח הגדול ואחר החליצ' נתוודע ששנים מן השלשה דיינים היו קרובים שני בשני בחד בעל ורצו לכוף את היבם הגדול שיחלוץ שנית לפני דיינים כשרים אך נסתפקו מאחר שהחליצ' השנית היא חליצ' פסולה שאינה ראוי' ליבום מחמת החליצ' הראשונ' וא"כ צריכה לחזור על כל האחין ותצטרך להמתין עד כי יגדל האח הקטן ודבר זה בלתי אפשרי מכמה טעמים. והשיב ע"ז באריכות ומצדד להתיר להאשה הזאת לחלוץ שנית מיבם הזה וא"צ להמתין עד שיגדיל הקטן ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One of them was found to be a relative.</b> See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 16 of Hilchot Edut, where he investigates the law that we hold that if one of them was found to be a relative or disqualified, then regarding testimony their testimony is void. What is the law regarding judges if the beit din had more than three and one was found to be a relative or disqualified? He wrote that from Rashi’s words in Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, Yevamot 104b, on the Mishnah that chalitzah with two or three where one of them was found to be a relative or disqualified is invalid, and Rashi explains that when one is found to be a relative or disqualified it turns out there are only two, it is clear that this is specifically with three, because if you remove the disqualified one, there are only two. But if there were more than three, her chalitzah is valid. However, from the words of the Nimukei Yosef it is clear that even if there are three without him, her chalitzah is invalid. End quote. See there. See Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 3, what he wrote about this, and see Sefer Netivot HaMishpat in Choshen Mishpat, siman 36, regarding this matter.

See further in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 6, regarding an incident involving a woman whose husband died without children. He had two brothers, one adult fit to perform chalitzah and one minor of four years. With great difficulty she obtained chalitzah from the adult brother, and after the chalitzah it became known that two of the three judges were second-degree relatives through one husband. They wanted to compel the adult yavam to perform chalitzah again before valid judges, but they were uncertain, since the second chalitzah would be an invalid chalitzah that is not fit for yibbum because of the first chalitzah. If so, she would need to go back to all the brothers and would need to wait until the minor brother grows up, which was impossible for several reasons. He answered this at length and inclines to permit this woman to perform chalitzah a second time from this yavam, without needing to wait until the minor grows up. See there.

<b>או פסול. </b> עבה"ט מ"ש ואפי' אם קבלו ליבם כו' והוא מדברי הב"ש בשם ת"ה וע' בס' ישועות יעקב סק"ד שכ' דאף שהב"ש כתב דבר זה בפשיטות מ"מ במחלוקת שנוי' דמדברי הרא"ש פמ"ח מבואר דדוקא בדבר שפסלה התורה להדיא בב"ד של חליצה הוא דלא מהני קבל' אבל במה דלא מצינו להדיא רק משום דהוי כדיני ממונות לא עדיף מד"מ דמהני קבלה אך מדעת התו' שם מבואר דס"ל כדעת התה"ד ע"ש: (ד) <b>אביו ואמו מישראל. </b> עבה"ט מ"ש שתוקי כו' אבל אם רוב פסולים אצלה כו' וע' בתשו' חתם סופר חאה"ע ח"ב סי' צ"ד שכתב דאפילו בהיתה אמו במקום שכונת ישראל קשה לסמוך על זה בעיר שרובה א"י ואין דלתות השכונה נעולים כו' וע"ש הועלות השתוקי על כסא הרבנות בודאי לא נכון מטעם שכתב רמ"א בתשו' שם שהוא בזיון לתורה ונקל הוא בעיני הבריות כו' (ע"ש בת' הרמ"א דמ"מ אם עלה ברשות לא ירד וכדיעבד דמי דאם נשאת לא תצא) אבל בלא מינוי בראש רק לסמכו שהוא ראוי להורות ולדון הנה מ"ש הרמ"א שם מדברי הרמב"ם דאם אינו ראוי לכל אין סומכין לחצאין דבריו תמוהים דהרמב"ם מיירי במי שאינו יודע להורות בכל התורה וע"ד שאמרו סוף הוריות אבל לא במי שאינו ראוי לדין א' מפני פסול אלא דמטעם אחר י"ל שלא לסומכו כי איכא למיחש מחמת בושה יחפה על עצמו במקום שאין מכירין ויזדקק לחליצה ע"ש):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or disqualified.</b> See Ba'er Heitev, what he wrote, "even if they accepted them for the yavam," etc. This is from the words of the Beit Shmuel in the name of Terumat HaDeshen. See Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 4, who wrote that although the Beit Shmuel wrote this plainly, nevertheless it is subject to dispute. From the words of the Rosh in Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah it is clear that specifically regarding something the Torah explicitly disqualified for a beit din of chalitzah, acceptance is not effective. But regarding something that we do not find explicitly, and it is only because it is like monetary law, it is no worse than monetary cases, where acceptance is effective. However, from the view of Tosafot there it is clear that they hold like the Terumat HaDeshen. See there.

<b>His father and mother are from Israel.</b> See Ba'er Heitev, what he wrote regarding a shetuki, etc., but if most men are disqualified with respect to her, etc. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, part 2, siman 94, who wrote that even if his mother was in a Jewish neighborhood, it is difficult to rely on this in a city whose majority is non-Jewish and where the doors of the neighborhood are not locked, etc. See there regarding elevating the shetuki to the rabbinic seat, that it is certainly not proper for the reason the Rama wrote in that responsum, that it is a disgrace to the Torah and appears light in the eyes of people, etc. See there in the responsum of the Rama, that nevertheless if he ascended with permission, he does not descend, and it is like bedieved, that if she married she need not leave.

But without appointing him as head, only to ordain him as fit to rule and judge, what the Rama wrote there from the Rambam, that if one is not fit for everything, we do not ordain partially, his words are astonishing. The Rambam speaks of one who does not know how to rule in the entire Torah, like what they said at the end of Horayot, but not of one who is unfit for one judgment because of a disqualification. However, for another reason one can say not to ordain him, because there is concern that out of shame he will conceal his status in a place where people do not know him and will become involved in chalitzah. See there.

<b>ושומר נפשו ירחק </b> עיין בתשו' נו"ב תניינ' ס"ס קנ"ה שכ' לרב אחד אשר שאל אם אני נוטל שכר מחליצה ח"ו היה לא נהיית' ואפילו השנים המצטרפים עמי איני נותן להם רשות ליקח אפילו פרוטה אבל להשנים הנוספים אין אני מוחה ואירע לפעמים שאלו הנוספים מוותרים אח"כ איזה דבר לשנים הראשונים אני מעלים עין מהם אבל אני איני נוטל שום פרוטה לא בתחלה ולא בסוף עכ"ד. גם בס' ישועות יעקב סק"ב קרא תגר על שסמכו להתיר ליטול שכר מן החליצות דכיון דיש חשש ביטול החליצ' מכל וכל ראוי לגדור גדר בדבר וכתב דרבים נוהגין שהשנים הנוספים מקבלין שכר ואח"כ המה נותנים מזה לשלשה הדיינים ואם שגם זה לא יתכן מ"מ בזה ראוי לומר מוטב יהיו שוגגין אבל שיקבלו הדיינים בעצמן השכר חלילה להקל ע"ש וע' בע"ז לעיל סי' קנ"ד סק"ב ובס' גט מקושר בסג"ר אות י"א וגם בתשו' חתם סופר חי"ד סי' ר"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one who guards his soul should distance himself.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, end of siman 155, who wrote to one Rav who asked whether he takes payment for chalitzah: Heaven forbid, it never happened. Even the two who join me, I do not give them permission to take even one perutah. But regarding the two additional ones, I do not protest. Sometimes it happens that those additional ones afterward waive something to the first two, and I turn a blind eye to them, but I do not take any perutah, not at the beginning and not at the end. End quote.

Also in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 2, he protested against those who relied on permitting taking payment from chalitzot, because since there is concern that the chalitzah may be entirely void, it is proper to fence the matter. He wrote that many practice that the two additional ones receive payment and afterward give from it to the three judges. Although even this is not proper, nevertheless regarding this it is appropriate to say, "better that they be inadvertent," but for the judges themselves to receive the payment, Heaven forbid to be lenient. See there. See Even HaEzer above, siman 154, s.k. 2, Sefer Get Mekushar in Seder Get Rishon, letter 11, and also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Yoreh Deah, siman 230.

But in my humble opinion we do not have the power to change the explained laws based on reasons stated to explain the matter, for perhaps there is another reason hidden from us, etc. See there. <b>On the day before.</b> See Ba'er Heitev. But if from the beginning it was uncertain on which day he would perform chalitzah, why should the first fixing not be effective? See there.

<b>We do not rely, etc.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 93, who wondered about this law, because this matter has no root in the Gemara, nor in the Rif, Rambam, or Rosh. Although the Tur copied this law in the name of the Rambam, the Rambam did not write it regarding chalitzah at all, but only regarding lashes and punishments. In the Mordechai, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, brought in the Beit Yosef, this matter is explained in the name of many Geonim, and all his words there can be answered, etc.

What he wrote in that responsum, that even if we grant the Mordechai his view that he is not believed to testify that so-and-so is the deceased’s brother, see Beit Shmuel above, siman 127, s.k. 2, this is because most people are not the deceased’s brothers, and if so the witness comes to remove so-and-so from the majority, etc., end quote. I do not understand this, because in any case most people have a presumption of validity and would not say about themselves that they are the deceased’s brothers in order to perform chalitzah for her and cause her to stumble. Regarding this particular person, who also says of himself that he is the deceased’s brother, there is no majority of the world, and the witness is not coming to remove him from the majority at all. What he wondered there regarding the Mordechai, who wrote that permitting a yevamah to the public is a matter of ervah, see there in siman 54, where he discusses this more at length. To resolve the view of the above Geonim, it appears to me that they truly hold like the view of the Rosh, above, siman 158, seif 3 in the gloss, that one witness is not believed regarding a yevamah to permit her to the public because she hates him, etc. All this follows the Mordechai’s explanation that "revealing a matter," which they said in HaCholetz regarding the deceased’s brother, means that he is otherwise established. I already wrote that the three pillars of halachic ruling did not explain it that way. <b>If they have many hairs or they are long.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 33, regarding a minor yavam whose father testified that he is thirteen years old and who has many hairs, but among all of them there is not even one as wide as a finger called an amah; some are only enough to grasp with a fingernail, some enough to take with a scissors, and some enough to bend the head to the root. He wrote: the measure of the length of hairs is written in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 27, as the width of a finger called an amah. The view of the Bach, brought in the gloss of the Beit Shmuel there, is that if the yevamah is sitting as an agunah for several years, even this measure should not be relied on unless they are very long. In siman 155, seif 18, the Shulchan Aruch ruled that the measure of the length of hairs for chalitzah is enough to bend the head to the root. The Beit Shmuel there wrote in the name of the Taz that this does not contradict what is explained in Seder Chalitzah, that an amah’s width is required, because there it speaks where it is not known that he is thirteen years old, and there too an examination for depressions is not needed. According to this, where it is known that he is thirteen, hairs long enough to bend are sufficient, except that examination for depressions is required.

However, all this is where there are not many hairs. But where there are many hairs, Shiltei Gibborim, siman 169, wrote: if they have many hairs or they are long, etc., there is a presumption that they have reached the age of majority, and chalitzah is performed, and we rely on this not to examine whether there are depressions, etc. Thus, many hairs are effective even when they are not long, even without the years being known, and they also do not require examination for depressions. Since the author did not mention a minimum size for many hairs, it implies that his intent is ordinary hairs according to their measure for chalitzah according to the author, in siman 155, meaning enough to bend. See Shulchan Aruch above, siman 141, seif 28.

But the Bach in siman 155 ruled that when there are many hairs, there is no need to examine their length at all, for presumably he has reached majority. Nor is there need to examine whether there are depressions at their roots, nor even to check whether among them there are at least two hairs with the measure of bending. But all this can be said where the years are known through witnesses. It appears that he explains the Bach’s words, "presumably he has reached majority," not as referring to years, but rather that through these hairs he is considered an adult after we also know that he is thirteen. The language "presumably" somewhat does not imply this. But in the case of the question, where the years were not known through valid witnesses, and what his father testified about him, that he is thirteen, is subject to the plain law in the Tur and Shulchan Aruch that we do not rely on relatives for the count of years, etc., perhaps the fact that many hairs is effective to rely on this that he reached his years is specifically where all of them can be bent. <b>That he performs chalitzah with both.</b> See Ba'er Heitev up to where he brings that the Bach wrote that the custom is to perform chalitzah with one after the other, and the yavam and yevamah should intend, etc. The Bach concludes there: now there is no concern of invalid chalitzah, because since both of them intend that she should not be released from her zikah at all through the chalitzah of that foot which is not the proper one by law, it is not chalitzah at all, as though he had not performed chalitzah for her, and she is not disqualified to the brothers through this chalitzah. This is how we rule, and this is how I practice. End quote. This is explained in the Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letters 59, 60, 71, and 84. See there. So wrote Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 9, at length.

See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 155, who wrote regarding one who is left-footed: the matter is simple that he performs chalitzah with both feet, meaning one after the other, like the words of the Bach, and not from both at once. All the words of Maharam Lublin in responsum siman 9 are correct in this, that in every respect it is better to do it one after the other. In Responsa Maharam Lublin there it is explained that if we follow the Beit Yosef’s ruling, that she should remove both shoes at once with both hands, there is concern that perhaps she will intend for the one that is not truly the right foot, as Mahari Weil wrote regarding two gittin given at one time. But when she removes one after the other, there is no concern, like one who gives two gittin one after the other. See there, and see Sefer Get Pashut above, siman 129, s.k. 104. It appears to me there is also another reason: according to the Beit Yosef, it emerges that from one of them she removes with her left hand, while lechatchilah we require it to be with her right hand, as below, seif 30.

She should say explicitly that regarding the foot which the law treats as valid for chalitzah, she intends for the mitzvah of chalitzah, and regarding the one that is not considered valid for chalitzah according to the law of our Torah, she does not intend for the mitzvah of chalitzah at all, but only as one occupied with removing a shoe from a stranger. There is no concern of invalid chalitzah that would require returning to all the brothers, since she says explicitly that regarding the one that is not according to law, she does not do it for chalitzah at all. Furthermore, the main law that an invalid chalitzah requires returning to all the brothers is not agreed upon, and Tosafot wrote that this applies specifically where the invalidity is because of weakening of the zikah. See Sefer Beit Meir.

Nevertheless, where there is another brother, even a minor brother, rather than the left-footed one, she performs chalitzah from the other brother. So we acted here in practice. Why should we be lenient against the Ramban, brought in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, and the Rashba left the matter requiring study? Although there is room to discuss the Ramban’s essential words, that even according to him this stringency applies only to one who became left-footed, while one who was always left-footed, why should he not perform chalitzah? Nevertheless, my words count as nothing against the Torah of the Ramban of blessed memory. Therefore, where there is another brother, even several parsaot away, one should not act against the Ramban. See there.

At first glance, from the above language of the Noda BiYehudah, who wrote "nevertheless, where there is another brother," it implies that everything he wrote until then speaks where there is no other brother. This is astonishing, because if so, why did he need to say that the main view is like the Bach and Maharam Lublin? In such a case, where there are no brothers except him, there is no dispute at all, and the Beit Yosef too agrees that in such a case she removes one after the other, since there is no concern that she must return to all the brothers. This is explicit in the language of the Beit Yosef in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 40, where he wrote: it appears to me that if he has another brother, she must remove both feet at once, etc. See there. Furthermore, if so, why did he need to mention that she should say explicitly that regarding the one not considered valid according to law, etc., since the concern of returning to all the brothers does not apply? One must say that what he wrote, "nevertheless, where there is another brother," means another brother who is fit to perform chalitzah. Until then he was also speaking where there is another brother, but he is not fit to perform chalitzah, either because he is a minor and she would need to wait until he grows up, or because he is overseas. In the incident of Maharam Lublin too it is explained there that there was another brother, but he was sick in his foot. See there.

In the words of the Bach and the above Responsa Noda BiYehudah, they wrote simply that he removes one after the other and did not mention which one should be removed first, implying that there is no concern about this. But in Responsa Maharam Lublin there he wrote explicitly that one should first remove the right foot of ordinary people, and afterward the left, which is his right. He explained the reason there: because the view of the Semag is more plausible to us, that even one who is left-footed performs chalitzah with the right of ordinary people, etc. See there. So too it is explained in Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 59. See there. So too Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 16, concluded, and he brought there that Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo also wrote explicitly that although he removes from both feet, nevertheless the right foot is primary, and he removes first from the right and then from the left. See there. This is unlike the view of the Gaon author of Get Mekushar, who wrote in his glosses to Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 71, that she should first remove from his right, which is the left of ordinary people. See there. See what I wrote further in my commentary to Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letters 59 and 84.

What the Gaon author of Get Mekushar there wondered about the Bach, Maharam Lublin, the Beit Shmuel, and the Rav arranging the chalitzah, who all wrote that they should intend that if it is not according to law it should not count as chalitzah at all: he wondered that this is like a condition, and what benefit is there in a condition, since we hold that there is no condition in chalitzah? He concludes there that this is like an error before the ruler. At first I wanted to resolve his question, because the reason that there is no condition in chalitzah is explicit in the Gemara: because we require something that can be performed through an agent. If so, one could say based on what the Mishneh LaMelech discusses in chapter 6 of Hilchot Ishut, end of law 1, that the laws of conditions are required only for a condition about the future, such as if I do this or if you do this, but for a condition about the past, such as if I did this or if you did this, the laws of conditions are not required. See there, and Sefer Yad HaMelech there. Accordingly, one could say that we also do not require that it be possible through an agent. I found explicitly in Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 15, that he wrote this regarding one who betroths on condition that she had not had relations with one disqualified to her. See there, brought briefly above, siman 6, seif 17, s.k. 16. If so, here too, this condition, if this foot is valid for chalitzah, is not a future condition, and it need not be possible through an agent, so this condition should work well. According to this, a new law would emerge regarding what is below in seif 50: if he made the condition depend on the past, such as if you gave my father two hundred zuz in such-and-such place, or the like, the condition would effectively void the chalitzah.

However, the conclusion of the Mishneh LaMelech there is not so, but rather that even for a condition about the past, the laws of conditions are required. Furthermore, from Tosafot in Ketubot 74, s.v. "condition," it is clear that this matter, that it must be possible to perform through an agent, is not dependent on the other laws of conditions. See there. This is astonishing regarding the above Responsa Brit Avraham, who did not mention the conclusion of the Mishneh LaMelech or the above words of Tosafot. In truth, in his own case he did not need this at all, according to what is explained in Ketubot there regarding betrothal through relations, that all forms of becoming betrothed are compared to one another. If so, one can say the same regarding prohibited betrothal. So it is explicitly explained in Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh Deah, siman 147. See there. However, see Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov below, seif 50, s.k. 57, where he resolves the change between the sugyot in Yevamot 53 and 106, on which Tosafot there, s.v. "Ravina," already stood. According to his words there, one can also resolve the above question.

One can also say according to what the Ramban wrote in his novellae to Bava Batra 126, brought in Sefer Beit Meir above, siman 120, seif 4, that a condition is effective in nazir even though it is impossible to perform through an agent, see Tosafot Nazir 11a, s.v. "on condition," because we learn from the condition of Bnei Gad and Bnei Reuven only cases like it, namely a condition between one person and another. But a condition between himself and himself is a condition in every respect. The reason makes sense, for he stipulated of his own will and wishes to fulfill the condition; therefore even a doubled condition is not required. See there. This reasoning is sufficient here too: the rule that there is no condition in chalitzah applies to a condition that she give you two hundred zuz, as below in seif 50, which is a condition between one person and another. But here, "if this foot is valid for chalitzah," is like a condition between himself and himself. This requires study. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 90, where it appears that these words of the Ramban escaped him. See there.

See further in Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 156, where he was asked about an incident in which one performed chalitzah for his yevamah. Before the chalitzah they saw that he controlled both feet, and he performed chalitzah with the right according to Torah law. Afterward, when the yevamah became engaged, the chalutz challenged the chalitzah and said that in truth he was left-footed. What he had not said at the time of chalitzah, he did deceitfully, because he knew the law that the yavam and yevamah divide the deceased’s assets, and the yevamah had said that the deceased left nothing. Therefore he also acted deceitfully and performed an invalid chalitzah, so that she would make a match and the conditions would publicize how much she was bringing as dowry, and then he would go and reveal before the beit din that it was an invalid chalitzah. Then she would need another chalitzah and be forced to divide the assets with him. The questioner asked whether he is believed now to invalidate the chalitzah.

He answered at length and concluded that this woman should be permitted to the public, and we do not pay attention to the words of the chalutz who creates a rumor that he is left-footed. First, he is not believed because a person does not make himself wicked. If it were true that he is left-footed, he performed an evil act, because if he had died suddenly, a stumbling block would have come through him in the prohibition of a yevamah to the public, since there were other brothers. Although Tosafot in Bava Metzia 3 wrote that a person does not make himself wicked, nevertheless, since he does not want to bring non-sacred animals into the Temple courtyard, he is believed; so too here, one might say he is repenting and does not want to cause her to marry with an invalid chalitzah. This is not so. Tosafot wrote this reasoning only regarding himself, but he cannot ruin others, etc. Although this permission is not entirely clear, because perhaps "a person does not make himself wicked" applies only to something the Torah called wicked, etc., see Urim VeTumim, rules of migo, letter 105, and Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 13, brought somewhat above, siman 6, s.k. 16, there is another permission here: even if we believe this chalutz, who says he is left-footed, there is no certain invalidity in this chalitzah, because the Semag and Semak hold that even a left-footed person performs chalitzah with the right of ordinary people. Even the decisors who hold that he performs chalitzah with both do so because they are uncertain whether we follow his own right. Only the Tur holds that he performs chalitzah with his own right. If so, even if he is left-footed, there is only doubtful invalidity here. From now there is a double doubt: perhaps he lies in his right and is not left-footed at all; and if he is left-footed, perhaps we follow the right of ordinary people. It is also a reversible double doubt, etc. Although there is a presumption of prohibition, and many are stringent not to rely on a double doubt, nevertheless here there are three doubts, for even if she performed chalitzah with the left, there is no clear halacha that it is invalid, etc. He discusses this at length, part of which was brought above, s.k. 22, and see what I wrote there in the name of Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger. He concludes: since there are three doubts here to permit her to the public, she should be permitted, especially since I already wrote a reasoning not to believe him to contradict his first words at the time of chalitzah, that he is not left-footed, if the arrangers of the chalitzah acted as customary and asked him at the time of chalitzah whether he is left-footed. Nevertheless, although she is permitted, one should not rush to permit. He should instruct the chalutz’s neighbors to watch him when he goes out and comes in, in a way that he will not sense that they are paying attention to this, for without doubt he will be caught in his lie. If despite all this he always acts deceitfully, do not pay attention to him and permit the woman. But because of the seriousness of the matter, he should also ask another well-known Rav. End quote. See there.

See Yeshuot Yaakov here, s.k. 16, and in his short commentary to Seder Chalitzah, s.k. 11, where he too was asked about exactly such an incident and also concluded to permit her. First, he is not believed because a person does not make himself wicked, even regarding something that does not disqualify him from testimony. Furthermore, bedieved one may rely on the view of those who validate the right of ordinary people where there are additional factors. See there.

In Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 91, he was asked by his father-in-law, the Gaon Rabbi Akiva Eiger of blessed memory, about a similar case: a chalutz received a large sum of money to perform chalitzah and told the beit din he was not left-footed. The beit din tested him, and four times he stepped first with his right and two times he stepped first with his left, and they judged him to control both feet. He performed chalitzah with the right. After the chalitzah he spread a rumor that he was left-footed and wanted to pressure them to give him more money. Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote that his view inclines to leniency, because in that case there were no other brothers. If he had said he was left-footed, they would have let him perform chalitzah with both feet, and the beit din told him that they also had a left shoe prepared. If so, it was in his power to perform a proper chalitzah, and he was believed in his words that he was not left-footed, and afterward one does not retract and testify anew. Even if the credibility is based on "it was in his power," we say regarding it that one does not retract and testify anew, etc. Also, it is a double doubt: perhaps he is not left-footed, and perhaps the halacha follows the decisors that a left-footed person performs chalitzah with the right of ordinary people; and even according to those who prohibit, it is an unresolved question. Also, there is room to say that a person does not make himself wicked because he took money unlawfully. However, he did not want to decide to permit until his son-in-law, the Gaon, the author, agreed.

He, of blessed memory, answered at length, and the substance of his words was to agree to permit that woman to the public. What his father-in-law the Gaon wrote, that even one believed by reason of "since it was in his power" and "what would he gain by lying" is also subject to "one does not retract and testify anew," is certainly clear, for this is an explicit Talmudic passage in Kiddushin 64a, etc. What he wrote that there is a double doubt here, at first glance there is room to question this, because there are four views here, etc., see the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 40. If so, the doubt that perhaps the halacha is that a left-footed person performs chalitzah with the right of ordinary people has most decisors against it. On the contrary, there is a double doubt to be stringent: perhaps he has no remedy, and if he has a remedy, perhaps we follow his own right. However, the words of the Gaon Rabbi Zalman Emrich of blessed memory in his book Shishah Zera'onei Arugah are fit to join, for he wrote strong proofs that a left-footed person does not mean one who normally begins with his left foot, for that is of no concern at all. Rather, itter means blocked, from the force of that limb, like one whose right hand is weakened. Here too it means the strength of his right foot is weakened until he drags his right foot after his left, etc. See Rashi, Shoftim 3, on the verse "itter yad yemino." This appears to have hit the truth. Although Heaven forbid to act on this alone, nevertheless it is fit to join to a double doubt, that perhaps all the decisors hold this way, and in our case there is no left-footed person at all, etc. Also, what his father-in-law wrote, that there is room to say a person does not make himself wicked because he took money unlawfully, is correct. One can add that when he took his wage as a worker and must complete his work, he is no longer believed to say that he did not perform his work properly, unless he said so the first time he was found, as the Ran explains in Kiddushin 68b and the Shach hinted to in Yoreh Deah, siman 127, s.k. 20, etc.

Furthermore, it appears to me that here, since they tested him in beit din and four times he stepped first with his right and two times with his left, if one would think that he is left-footed and deceitfully lifted his right to mislead the beit din, then a deceiver is careful in his deceit and would always have been careful to lift his right. This is how we hold like Rav Zevid in Bava Batra 167b, and even Abaye said his view only where over time they called him and he answered. But here, in the very same session where he was engaged in his deceit, he certainly would have been careful. Therefore, from the fact that he was not careful, learn that he is not left-footed. This reasoning appears correct and is fit to join. He concludes: nevertheless, it appears to me it is good to say to him: You are not believed, and this woman is permitted. But if you know in yourself that the truth is with you and you are concerned for causing a stumbling block, and you want to perform chalitzah for her a second time without any money, without any expense, and without any delay to cause her suffering, then she will perform chalitzah for you. Then, if he listens and agrees to this, perhaps she is forbidden to marry by law until she performs chalitzah again, for he is believed, since what would he gain by lying and also by degrading himself? In Sanhedrin chapter 2, chalitzah is considered embarrassment and degradation. It is somewhat like "since you are believed to lose your payment," etc. It is also not a case of making himself wicked, because he speaks by way of repentance. Therefore one should be concerned. End quote. See there.

See Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 28, where he too was asked about a similar incident, except that in his case it was not relevant to discuss whether to believe him, because it was mentioned there that afterward it was clarified completely that he was left-footed and that the chalutz had intended to mislead them and invalidate the chalitzah so that the yevamah’s father would submit to judgment with him. The questioner was uncertain whether bedieved the chalitzah was valid for one left-footed, even with his right foot, and whether this is called bedieved or not, since the chalutzah had not yet married. He discusses at length all the views on this and concludes that according to all the decisors who mention the law of a left-footed person regarding chalitzah, if he performed chalitzah with the right of ordinary people, even bedieved it is invalid chalitzah. Only the Semag and Semak hold that chalitzah of a left-footed person is with the right of ordinary people. Who would place his head between the great mountains to act leniently, all the more so since those great authorities who are stringent are the majority in wisdom and number? One should not say that where there is pressing circumstance, as here, where the yavam is distant from the yevamah, we may rely on the Semag and Semak. This is not so. First, it is not called pressing circumstance unless there is no remedy for the matter, as the Radach wrote in bayit 3, that where travelers are common, it is not pressing circumstance merely because of waiting three or four months. If so, in our case, where the chalutzah has not yet married and the yavam wants to perform chalitzah, it is not a pressing circumstance. Furthermore, the Radach wrote there that specifically in rabbinic matters do we say that he is worthy to rely on in pressing circumstances to be lenient. If so, in our case, where those great authorities who invalidate hold that they invalidate biblically, it is not relevant to be lenient in pressing circumstances. All the more so in our case, where we see that he intended to invalidate the chalitzah so that she would submit to judgment, since they asked him at the time of chalitzah whether he was left-footed and he answered no and forced himself to mislead them by lifting his right foot. This is like "she intended and he did not intend," which is invalid chalitzah. It is also not called bedieved, since she has not married and the yavam wants to perform chalitzah. <b>That one should cut into the shoe.</b> See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 154, who wrote regarding the chalitzah shoe: When I came here, I found that their shoe was cut at its back into three pieces, and I changed it to two pieces, as I saw in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 12 of Yevamot, siman 7. He, of blessed memory, discusses this at length in explaining the words of the Mordechai, and also brought the words of the explanation of Seder Chalitzah in the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, seif 59, and the words of Maharshal, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, siman 7. He concludes that one should not make holes or notches on the sides, only two notches behind it. However, they should be somewhat long, so that they extend a little to both sides of the foot, because the fold made in the straps also extends somewhat to the sides of the foot. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>And she should hold his foot in her left hand.</b> The Bach, letter 19, wrote: This means that at the time she grasps the heel of the shoe with her right hand to remove it, she should grasp with her left hand the thickness of the flesh of the lower leg in the air, so that it should not sway or move, and she should slip the shoe from the heel with her right hand, etc. He concludes there that this is the custom, that everything is with the right, except that she holds the flesh of the lower leg with the left. This is what the Yerushalmi means by "she holds it with the left." See there.

However, in Sefer Get Mekushar, in his glosses to Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 83, he wrote: See in Kuntres Tov Chalitzah, where I wrote that she must grasp the place of the shoe and hold there, holding the shoe together with the foot so that it should not fall, even if the shoe was not tight. End quote. I found in Sefer Kerem Shlomo, in the responsa at the end of the book, siman 87, that one Rav wrote to him: It seems to me that the world practices that she holds the yavam’s heel with her left and removes the shoe with her right. In my humble opinion, this is not correct, because through this one can easily come to a stumbling block, that she will assist with her left hand, since she is holding the heel. Rather, the choicest way is what I saw in the Bach, etc. He also copied this in Sefer HaKovetz, that the left should not assist, but only hold the flesh of the lower leg, and this is proper practice and a mitzvah to publicize. He answered him and agreed that certainly she should not grasp the heel. See there.

In my humble opinion, the words of the above Get Mekushar are more correct, for so is precise from the language of the Yerushalmi, from which the decisors derived this matter, brought by Tosafot, the Rosh, the Mordechai, the Maggid Mishneh, and the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 71: How does she do it? She unties it with the right, holds it with the left, removes the heel with the right, and drags it with the right. In this there are variant readings, as explained in the Beit Yosef. But regarding "holds it," everyone agrees that the version is "with the left," as written in the Derishah. The language "unties it" and "drags it" refers to the shoe and not to the foot. Presumably, "holds it" also refers to the shoe, that she should hold the place of the shoe, as the above Get Mekushar wrote.

What Sefer Kerem Shlomo wrote, that one can easily come to a stumbling block, etc., is not so, because the beit din sees and warns about this. Furthermore, there is more concern if most of the heel falls by itself, because even bedieved it is invalid. This is unlike here, where even if she removed it entirely with her left, it is valid, and according to Rabbenu Tam it is permitted even lechatchilah when the left assists, because assisting has no substance. The Bach himself concludes that this is the main view, except that the custom already followed the Semak, etc. See there. Therefore it appears that in a place where there is no custom like the Bach, or the custom is not clear, one should do like the above Get Mekushar. See what I wrote further on this in Seder Chalitzah in brief, seif 52, s.k. 14.
