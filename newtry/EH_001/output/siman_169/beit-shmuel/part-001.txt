<b>מצוה ושיוסיפו שנים</b>. הטעם כדי לפרסם ופרש"י ונ"י לפ"א לפרסם שתהא חלוצה ואסורה לכהן לפ"ז אם היא גרושה כבר אין צריך חמשה אבל לפי' השני צריך ה' כדי דליתי אנשי ולקפצו עליה ובאלו שנים אין קפידא אם נוטלין שכר הרבה ד"מ, וכן אם הם קרוביו קורבה שאין פסול ע"פ הדין אלא מצד חומרא כמ"ש בסי' ס"ח ובנ"י כ' אם הזמינו ג' לב"ד בפי' וקראו שנים למילוי אז אפי' גרים אפי' פסולים כשרים באלו שנים והא דפוסל בש"ס לרב ושמואל שהיו בן גר למילוי שנים היינו דשם כל החמשה היה עושה חיבור א' שאין זה כבודו של ר"ש להקצות מן השלשה וכ"כ בא"ז דאין קפידא בשנים הנוספים אם אינם כשרים והיינו נמי בכה"ג לפי המנהג דאין עושין חיבור א' ואלו שנים יושבין נמשכין מן הג' אז אין קפידא כלל ובזה מיושב מה שהקשה בת"ה סי' רכ"ו על הא"ז מעובדא רב ושמואל בזה ניחא דל"פ כלל הפוסקים ומה שמדייקי' מרמב"ם וטור דס"ל דיש קפידא באלו שנים שאל יהיו קרובים היינו דס"ל כל החמשה יושבין בכנופי' חדא כדמשמע שם שלא כתבו דיושבין רחוק מן הג', גם מה שמדייק הת"ה מן הגמיי' אין מוכרח וגר שאמו מישראל פסול לכתחלה להיות תוך שנים כשעושין כנופי' אחת כמ"ש בש"ס בעובדא רב שמואל בר יהודא ובדיעבד כשר הואיל ואלו שנים רק כדי לפרסם הדבר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is a mitzvah that they add two.</b> The reason is publicizing the matter. Rashi and the Nimukei Yosef explain, according to the first explanation, that this publicizes that she is a chalutzah and forbidden to a kohen. According to this, if she is already divorced, five are not needed. But according to the second explanation, five are needed so that people will come and seek to marry her. With these two, there is no concern if they take much payment. Darkei Moshe. So too if they are his relatives with a closeness that is not disqualifying by strict law, but only by stringency, as written in siman 68.

The Nimukei Yosef wrote that if they explicitly designated three for the beit din and called two others only to fill out the number, then even converts and even disqualified people are valid for these two. That which the Talmud disqualifies regarding Rav and Shmuel, who were sons of converts for filling out the two, is because there all five formed one group, and it was not respectful to Rav Shmuel to exclude him from the three. So too the Or Zarua wrote that there is no concern with the two additional ones if they are not valid. This too is in such a case: according to the custom, where they do not make one group, and these two sit drawn away from the three, there is no concern at all. With this, what Terumat HaDeshen, siman 226, asked against Or Zarua from the incident of Rav and Shmuel is resolved, and there is no dispute at all among the decisors.

What is inferred from the Rambam and Tur, that they hold there is concern that these two not be relatives, is because they hold that all five sit in one assembly, as implied there, since they did not write that the two sit away from the three. Also, what Terumat HaDeshen inferred from Hagahot Maimoniyot is not compelling. A convert whose mother is from Israel is disqualified lechatchilah to be among the two when they form one assembly, as written in the Talmud in the incident of Rav Shmuel bar Yehudah. Bedieved he is valid, since these two are only to publicize the matter.

<b>דדים גדולים</b>. כתב בד"מ בשם המרדכי דכתב בשם התרומה בדיקה זו צריך להיות ע"י אנשים ובדיקה אם יש לה ריבוי שערות בודקים ע"י נשים וכ"כ ב"ח בשם הסמ"ק בדיקות דדים צריך להיות ע"י אנשים אא"כ הוחזקה גדולה בשנים אז בודקים ע"י נשים כי החזקה מסייע מסתמא אחר יב"ש הביאה סימנים א"כ בדיקת שערות הוא אפי' לא הוחזקה בשנים והקשה הד"מ א"כ למה מהני בדיקות נשים ותירוץ משום דהוא מילתא דעבידא לגלויי לא משקרי והא אין נשים נאמנים על ב' שערות כשלא הוחזקה בשנים ולא אמרי' מלתא דעבידא לגלויי משום בשני שערות לאו מלתא דעבידא לגלויי הוא כ"כ דהא מהאי טעמא כ' בה"ת דאין ממאנין בזמן הזה דאין אנו בקיאין לבדוק כל הגוף אם יש לה שערות מיהו קשה דדין נמי הוא מלתא דעבידא לגלויי וא"ל דדין לא עדיף מב' שערות דדין מהני משום א"א לבוא סימן עליון בלא תחתון מ"ה כשהיא לא הוחזקה בשנים אין בודקים נשים א"כ מה מהני בדיקת אנשים וצ"ל דד"מ לא תירץ זאת אלא לסברת המ' דס"ל באמת בדיקת דדין הוא ע"פ נשים, ולבה"ת דס"ל דל"מ ע"פ נשים צ"ע למה מהני רבוי שערות ע"פ נשים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Large breasts.</b> The Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of the Mordechai, who wrote in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, that this examination must be done by men. The examination whether she has many hairs is done by women. So too the Bach wrote in the name of the Semak: examination of breasts must be done by men, unless she has already been established as an adult by years, in which case women examine, because the presumption assists, since presumably after twelve years and one day she brought signs. If so, examination for hairs is even where she was not established by years. The Darkei Moshe asked: if so, why is women’s examination effective? He answered that since it is a matter that will be revealed, they do not lie. But women are not believed regarding two hairs where she was not established by years, and we do not say it is a matter that will be revealed, because two hairs are not so much a matter that will be revealed. For this reason Sefer HaTerumah wrote that we do not allow mi'un nowadays, because we are not expert to examine the whole body to see whether she has hairs.

However, this is difficult, because breasts are also a matter that will be revealed. One cannot say that breasts are no better than two hairs, for breasts are effective because an upper sign cannot come without a lower sign. Therefore, when she was not established by years, women do not examine. If so, why is the examination of men effective? One must say that the Darkei Moshe answered this only according to the view of the Maggid, who truly holds that examination of breasts is done by women. But according to Sefer HaTerumah, who holds that it is not effective through women, it requires study why many hairs are effective through women.

<b>וע"ז סומכין שלא לבדוק וכו'</b>. עיין סי' קנ"ה שכתב הטור בשם הבע"ת דאין אנו בקיאין לבדוק גומות א"כ לפ"ז לכאורה קשה כל בדיקות אחר השערות אפי' ע"י אנשים ל"מ דקי"ל שערות בלא גומא ל"מ ואין אנו בקיאין וכן הק' ב"ח וע"ש מ"ש ולדידי' לא נתיישב לפסק הלכה מנ"ל למיקל נגד דעת הבה"ת וכן הטור הביא דעתו בפשיטות בלי שום חולק אבל לכאורה נ"ל דלק"מ בודאי על בדיקות גומות כשאין שערות בכל הגוף אין אנו בקיאין מ"ה החמיר שם שלא תמאן אבל לבדוק אם יש גומא תחת השערות פשיטא בקיאין אנו בבדיקה זו וכן משמע מלשון המחבר שכתב ע"ז סומכין שלא לבדוק משמע דאנו יכולים לבדוק, שוב ראיתי גם בט"ז בסימן קנ"ה כתב כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And on this we rely not to examine, etc.</b> See siman 155, where the Tur wrote in the name of Ba'al HaTerumot that we are not expert in examining depressions. According to this, at first glance it is difficult: every examination for hairs, even by men, should not be effective, for we hold that hairs without a depression are not effective, and we are not expert. So the Bach also asked. See there what he wrote. According to him, this is not settled as a halachic ruling: from where do we know to be lenient against the view of Ba'al HaTerumot? The Tur also brought his view plainly without any dispute.

But at first glance it appears to me that this is not difficult. Certainly, regarding examination for depressions where there are no hairs on the entire body, we are not expert, and for this reason he was stringent there that she may not perform mi'un. But to examine whether there is a depression under the hairs, it is obvious that we are expert in this examination. So it also implies from the language of the author, who wrote: "on this we rely not to examine," implying that we are able to examine. Later I saw that the Taz in siman 155 also wrote this.

<b>שמצות חליצה מעומד</b>. משום דדמיא לדין ואם יבם חלש או זקן נותנין לו מקל בידו לסמוך עליו, ולרמב"ם ונ"י בשם הרבה פוסקים אפילו לכתחלה חליצת המנעל מות' בישיבה וא"ל להני פוסקים דס"ל לכתחלה מצוה בעמידה הוא וס"ל גם כן הא דאמר אמימר צריך למדחסי כרעיה היינו צריך לכתחלה ובדיעבד אין עיכוב אם כן מה מקשה שם על אמימר מברייתא בין עומד ובין יושב וס"ד כשיושב לא מדחי' כרעיה ומה ק"ל הא יש לומר הברייתא איירי בדיעבד וכן הקשה בנ"י, וצ"ל דס"ל ע"כ הברייתא איירי לכתחלה מדקתני בין עומד אם כן ע"כ מה דתני בברייתא יושב ומוטה נמי לכתחלה וס"ד כשמוט' לא מדחסי כרעיה וא"ל איך ס"ד יושב לכתחלה דהא מצות חליצה לכתחלה מעומד ויש לומר דסבר אף על גב לכתחלה בעינן שיעמדו היינו משום דהוי כדין וצריכים שיעמדו מחמת שהיא גובאת כתובה הוי כדין, אבל משום מצות חליצ' אפי' לכתחלה מותר וכן אם הדיינין עמדו כשר דחד טעם הוא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For the mitzvah of chalitzah is standing.</b> Because it resembles judgment. If the yavam is weak or old, they give him a cane in his hand to lean on. According to the Rambam and Nimukei Yosef in the name of many decisors, even lechatchilah the removal of the shoe may be done while sitting. One cannot ask according to those decisors who hold that lechatchilah the mitzvah is standing, and who also hold that what Ameimar said, that he must press his foot down, means lechatchilah but bedieved it is not indispensable: if so, what is the Gemara’s challenge against Ameimar from the baraita, "whether standing or sitting"? The Gemara initially thought that while sitting he does not press his foot down. What was difficult? One could say that the baraita speaks bedieved. So the Nimukei Yosef also asked. One must say that they hold the baraita necessarily speaks lechatchilah, since it teaches "whether standing." If so, what it teaches, "sitting and leaning," is also lechatchilah. The Gemara initially thought that while leaning he does not press his foot down. One should not ask how it could initially think that sitting is lechatchilah, since the mitzvah of chalitzah is lechatchilah standing. One can say that it thought that although lechatchilah they must stand, this is because it is like judgment, and they need to stand because she collects her ketubah, which is like judgment. But because of the mitzvah of chalitzah itself, even lechatchilah sitting would be permitted. So too, if the judges stood, it is valid, for it is one reason.

According to the Rosh, it is valid, and he wrote the reason: the derivation from "tachash," that it must be of leather, is only an asmachta. One should not ask, according to this, why cloth socks are invalid. One must say that he holds like Tosafot, who wrote there that cloth socks are not considered a shoe because people do not usually make them from cloth. Everything depends on whether it is the way to make such a shoe. The derivation of "tachash" is only an asmachta. A wooden shoe is also invalid because it is not usual to make a shoe of wood, as written in Tosafot there.

Rashi explained that according to the one who says socks are valid, the verse "tachash" is not precise. Learn from this that he too holds like these "some say." Tosafot there also implies that they hold the derivation of "tachash" is not an asmachta, but then one must explain why, in the dispute regarding the amputee’s wooden leg and socks, they wrote that the Rabbis invalidated because it is not the way to make such a shoe. Let them say that they hold the derivation of "tachash" is a full derivation. One must say as written nearby, that where it is made of wool that comes from leather, it is not excluded by "tachash." This explains what they wrote regarding socks. Perhaps since they were forced to explain socks as invalid because it is not the way to make a shoe, because it is not excluded by "tachash," they no longer needed to explain that according to Rabbi Meir, he does not derive from "tachash" regarding the amputee’s wooden leg. Even if he does not derive from "tachash," still, since it is not the way to make a shoe from them, why would it be valid? Rather, one must say the above reason.

For halacha, the main ruling follows these "some say," for this is the view of Rashi, Tosafot, Rambam, Ramah, and Rashba, as the Maggid wrote in his name, and so ruled the Shiltei Gibborim. According to this, what the Tur wrote, that if he performed chalitzah with a muk it is valid, is according to his reasoning that anything from which shoes are normally made is valid, and "tachash" is not a full derivation. But according to the above decisors, it is a full derivation, and then even with a muk it is invalid. However, one can say that although "tachash" is a full derivation, nevertheless a muk made of wool that comes from leather is not excluded by "tachash." Although one made of hair is invalid, nevertheless a muk is valid, because perhaps it is called a shoe, whereas one made of hair is not called a shoe but a korka. According to Rashi and Rashba, who explained that cloth socks are excluded by "tachash," even though they are made of wool that comes from leather like hair, if so there is a difficulty in the sugya where he asked: If it is of leather and its straps are of hair, what is the law? He said to him: do we not read "tachash" regarding it? He said to him: if so, all of it too. That one is a korka. If woolen cloth is excluded by "tachash," then hair too should be. Why do we need the reason that it is called a korka? Why not exclude it from "tachash"? One can answer.

Therefore, what the Rosh wrote, and what is brought in the gloss nearby, that if she removes both they are valid, appears to speak where she removes both together at one time. Otherwise, when she removes the upper one, it is "from upon that which is upon," an invalid chalitzah, and she must go back to all the brothers. If she tore the upper one, but it still remained and because of the tear she could remove the lower one, the question was not resolved, and it is an invalid chalitzah. It implies that even if she tore most of the heel or most of the shoe of the upper one, so that he cannot walk in it, nevertheless, since it remained and there was no exposure of his foot, this is the question. For even where most of the shoe was torn, we hold that it is chalitzah and disqualifies. Therefore, in this kind of case, one can say it is as though the upper shoe remains. See the above responsum.

According to the Rambam, the question in the Talmud is where she removed the upper one and tore the lower one. If so, it is chalitzah and exposure of the foot. Even if she did everything at one time, it appears that the question remains, since the chalitzah was with the upper one. However, if she tore the upper one entirely and removed the lower one, it is an excellent chalitzah. The Maggid and Beit Yosef wrote that the Rambam had another version. However, in truth, one can learn the Rambam’s law from our version too.

It seemingly implies that since he compares these two laws to one another, and spitting speaks before beit din, the same applies if she removed the shoe in beit din and did nothing else: we require specifically that both intended. It appears from his words that the Talmud said that if one of them intended it is chalitzah stringently only with a complete chalitzah, as the Talmud says, "remove the shoe, and thereby you will acquire her," where it is a complete chalitzah but he did not intend. But according to this, it is difficult why he needed to prove there that spitting which she spits among the brothers is not effective even to disqualify, for even in beit din it is not effective with intent of one of them, and all the more so among the brothers. Necessarily, among the brothers there is no concern that perhaps both intended, for the whole Talmudic discussion is whether perhaps she intended, but there is no concern at all regarding both intending. The words of the Rif and Rambam, chapter 4 of Gittin, according to the Nimukei Yosef, that they wrote "we are concerned perhaps they intended," and what the author wrote "perhaps they intended for chalitzah," imply specifically where there is concern that both intended. It appears that he wrote imprecisely.

It emerges that if she removed the shoe not before beit din and one of them did not intend chalitzah, the above decisors disagree. If she spit in beit din and he did not intend, according to the Rosh it is not effective at all, and perhaps the other decisors also agree to this. If she removed the shoe in beit din without intent, I wrote that according to the Rosh too it is not effective unless it was in a case of complete chalitzah. However, this can be pushed aside somewhat, and one can say that the Rosh intended to compare spitting in beit din without one of them intending to shoe removal outside beit din without one of them intending, but shoe removal in beit din, even without intent, disqualifies.

<b>Her chalitzah is invalid.</b> There are variant versions of this Mishnah. According to our version, the Tanna taught: a minor girl who performed chalitzah should perform chalitzah again when she grows up, and if she did not perform chalitzah again, her chalitzah is invalid. Tosafot inferred from this: since it teaches "invalid," why did it teach "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up"? Rather, so that you should not say it is only rabbinically invalid chalitzah, it taught the extra language "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up" to teach that the chalitzah of a minor girl is nothing, like the chalitzah of a minor boy. From this we learn that just as we hold that the chalitzah of a minor boy does not disqualify, so too the chalitzah of a minor girl does not disqualify. Although it is stated in the Talmud that the Sages say "man" is written in the section and we do not compare woman to man, nevertheless for final halacha we do not hold like the Sages according to the conclusion, for Rava said there: the halacha is until she brings two hairs, meaning we do not hold like the Sages in this. So it is in the Semag, page 137a. The Rosh took this version as primary, and therefore it is proven from his words that the chalitzah of a minor girl is nothing and does not disqualify. This is what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh, equating the chalitzah of a minor girl to that of a minor boy. This is also the intent of the Beit Yosef in inferring from the above Tosafot that they hold the chalitzah of a minor girl does not disqualify. Although Tosafot wrote this according to Rabbi Meir, who holds that even the chalitzah of a minor boy disqualifies, nevertheless from Rabbi Meir one learns for the Sages, whom we hold like in this, that since the chalitzah of a minor boy does not disqualify, that of a minor girl also does not disqualify, because we learn from the extra language of the Mishnah that this comparison of a minor girl to a minor boy is biblical in this matter. With this all the astonishments of the Bach and Derishah against the Beit Yosef are settled, and what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh is also clear. The Beit Yosef also understood this way; his only wonder was that we do not find it explicit in the Rosh.

According to the Rif, the version is: if she did not perform chalitzah after she grew up, it is valid. According to the Sages, who say we do not compare man to woman, then even lechatchilah one may perform chalitzah with a minor girl. What is concluded there, that the halacha is until she brings two hairs, means lechatchilah. The Rambam, who wrote that her chalitzah is invalid, appears to have taken our version, unlike the Maggid. To answer Tosafot’s question, why did it teach "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up," one can answer differently: although this comparison is rabbinic, nevertheless she requires another chalitzah when she grows up. According to this, there are three views: according to Tosafot’s approach, according to the Sages there is no chalitzah at all and it does not disqualify; according to the Rif, bedieved it is valid; according to the Rambam, it disqualifies and requires another chalitzah. But what the Maggid wrote, that even according to the Rif’s version, the conclusion there "the halacha is until she brings two hairs" means it is invalid even bedieved, appears very forced, since according to Rabbi Meir and according to the Rabbis, chalitzah is valid, so how would Rava rule like no one?

At first glance, the Rambam is difficult, because he rules regarding a deaf-mute man and woman that their chalitzah is nothing and does not disqualify. The Maggid wrote that the reason is because we hold that throughout the Torah, a deaf-mute, insane person, and minor are equivalent. Therefore, in chalitzah too they are equal, and it does not disqualify, like the chalitzah of a minor boy. If so, why does the chalitzah of a minor girl disqualify? If the comparison of woman to man is only rabbinic, then even the chalitzah of a deaf-mute woman should disqualify. One must say that it speaks of a minor girl who has understanding, and then the disqualification is because "man" is written in the section and not a minor, even if he has understanding. The comparison of woman to man is only rabbinic, and therefore she disqualifies. This is unlike a deaf-mute man or woman and an insane person, who lack understanding, and therefore their chalitzah does not disqualify, as the Rambam wrote there in law 13 regarding a deaf-mute man and woman, that they are not of understanding. A minor boy, even if he has understanding, does not disqualify, because the Torah excluded him by writing "man." I later saw that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 225, also wrote that the Rambam’s version is like our version.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>יש מי שאומר שחליצתה כשרה</b>. כתב המגיד הרמב"ם שכתב חלוץ לה שזו מצוה היא וכו' כמ"ש בסעי' הקודם חולק על זה והמחבר ס"ל דזה עדיף דאמרי' לו בפי' שיעקור הזיקה משא"כ שם שסובר בזה יכול לכנוס גם מ"ש לו ואם תרצה לייבם תייבם עדיין הזיקה קיימת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is one who says that her chalitzah is valid.</b> The Maggid wrote that the Rambam, who wrote "perform chalitzah for her, for this is a mitzvah," etc., as written in the previous seif, disagrees with this. The author holds that this case is stronger, because we tell him explicitly that he should uproot the zikah. This is unlike there, where he thinks that through this he can marry her. Also, what they said to him, "and if you want to perform yibbum, perform yibbum," leaves the zikah in place.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>חליצה פסולה הוא</b>. אף על גב בגט אם מוסר מודעה אז  הגט בטל ואין פוסל כלל כמ"ש בסימן קל"ד מ"מ חליצה פוסלת ועיין הטעם בתשובת ריב"ש סימן תפ"ב והובא בכ"מ פ"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is invalid chalitzah.</b> Although by get, if one delivers a moda'ah, the get is void and does not disqualify at all, as written in siman 134, nevertheless chalitzah disqualifies. See the reason in Responsa Rivash, siman 482, brought in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 4.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>כותבין לה ב"ד</b>. עיין מ"ש בסי' קל"ה וכתב זה צריכין לקיים לכ"ע אף מי שסובר בגט א"צ קיום משום חומר' שהחמרת בסופו משא"כ כאן עיין תשובות ריב"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Beit din writes for her.</b> See what I wrote in siman 135. This must be validated according to all opinions, even according to one who holds that a get does not need validation because of the stringency imposed at its end. This is unlike here. See Responsa Rivash.
