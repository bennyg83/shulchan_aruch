<b>Seif 2, if he began, etc.</b> The Yerushalmi there says: since we learned that one does not divide the Shema with fewer than ten, did we not also learn that one does not pass before the ark? Therefore it was necessary for that which we learned: one does not divide the Shema with fewer than ten; if they began with ten and some left, he finishes. One does not pass before the ark with fewer than ten; if they began with ten and some left, he finishes. One does not lift, etc.; if they began, etc. One does not read, etc. About all of them it says, Those who forsake Hashem will perish. Meaning, the Mishnah says that each one is a matter by itself, and they finish only that matter. This is what is meant by one does not pass, etc., even though at the start of dividing the Shema there were ten, and similarly for all of them. This is the meaning of that, etc.

<b>Seif 4, some permit, etc.</b> This is from what is stated in Berachos 47 to 48a: although they said that a minor lying, etc.; Rabbi Yochanan said, a minor who has sprouted, etc.; it was also taught likewise, etc.; and the halacha is not, etc. Rashi explained there that a minor who has sprouted means a minor who brought hairs. Tosafot rejected his explanation, for if so, what is it coming to include, etc. They explained in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that it refers to one in his thirteenth year who brought hairs, but not enough that their tips can be bent to their roots. This is why it says sprouted, for the hairs have begun to sprout; see there, s.v. a minor, etc. So too all the Geonim and commentators explained. They further explained that what it says there, and the halacha is not, etc., means that both are required. So wrote the Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, and the Rif in the name of the Geonim, except that he explained that sprouting hairs is not necessary. But the Rif explained that even years are not necessary, even if he is nine or ten, and so wrote the Rambam, who wrote eight or seven. The Rivash wrote that they chose the age of the young child, and the same applies to one older than six, for below that age his knowledge is not considered knowledge, as stated in Gittin 59a. Rabbeinu Tam explained that what is stated there, and the halacha is not, does not refer to the first two statements of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, since the Gemara interrupted after them with other matters; there it refers to ten, while here it refers to three. He wrote that the same applies to prayer, that a minor lying in a cradle combines, for they are equivalent, as the Gemara challenges there, they raised an objection, etc. However, the poskim disagree with Rabbeinu Tam. Ri explained that they do not combine either for prayer or for Birkas HaMazon, whether for ten or for three, until they bring two hairs, as stated in the Yerushalmi there: from when do we make him an adjunct? Rabbi Avina said: Rav Huna and Rabbi Yochanan disagreed, both in the name of Shmuel. One said, when he knows the nature of a blessing; the other said, when he knows to Whom he is blessing. Rabbi Yosei said: many times I ate with Tachlifa my father and with Rabbi Chanina bar Sisi my uncle, and they did not include me in zimun until I brought two hairs. There it is speaking of ten, as stated there explicitly; see there. Nevertheless, he wrote that one who has sprouted and knows to Whom they bless combines even for three, for in this there is no one who disagrees. See Tosafot there, s.v. and the halacha is not, etc. But from the words of the Rosh and Tur it appears that a minor does not combine at all, and they relied on the Yerushalmi cited above, interpreting it specifically after thirteen years, as stated in the chapter Yotzei Dofen. Although our Gemara disagrees with the Yerushalmi, the common practice follows the Yerushalmi, Hagahot Maimoniyot, as stated: whenever the halacha is uncertain, etc. One can also say that this entire sugya concerns ten and disagrees with Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as Tosafot wrote at the end of s.v. a minor, but not regarding three, as stated in the Yerushalmi there: Rabbi Simon in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, Rabbi Yosei ben Shaul in the name of Rabbi, a minor may be made an adjunct for ten, etc. Rabbi Berechiah said: Rabbi Yaakov bar Zavdi asked before Rabbi Yosei bar Abba: there, a minor is made an adjunct for ten; should we also say here that he is made an adjunct for three? There, where the Name of Hashem is mentioned, they make him an adjunct; here, where the Name of Hashem is not mentioned, should this not be all the more so? He answered: it is not all the more so. There, where the Name of Hashem is mentioned, they make him an adjunct; here, where the Name of Hashem is not mentioned, they do not make him an adjunct, etc. From when do they make him, etc., as above. All this follows Rabbi Ashi on 45b, but we rule like Mar Zutra, that ten is stricter than three, as the Rosh wrote in the name of Rabbi Chaim regarding Rabbeinu Tam's ruling cited above, and so ruled the gloss in siman 199, seif 10. But in the Shulchan Aruch there he ruled like the Rif and Rambam, that once he has reached the age of a young child and knows to Whom he blesses, he combines both for three and for ten, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who distinguishes as above. On the basis of this view, there are those who permit here, as Rabbeinu Tam wrote that prayer and Birkas HaMazon are equivalent.
