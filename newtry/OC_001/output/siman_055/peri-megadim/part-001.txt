<b>אומרים</b> קדיש עט״ז. אחר ישתבח אומרים קדיש וקאי על פסוקי דזמרה כי אין אומרים אותו אלא על תורה שבכתב או אחר הגדה עמ״א נ״ד ג׳. ומ״ש אחר תפלת י״ח י״ל שיש שם כמה פסוקים רפאנו ה׳ וכדומה. והז׳ קדישים בכל יום ע״ש שבע ביום הללתיך מנאם הלבוש א׳ אחר ישתבח ב׳ אחר תפלת י״ח ג׳ אחר סדר קדושה ד׳ אחר עלינו שיש בו פסוקים ה׳ אחר אשרי במנחה וי״ו אחר תפלת מנחה ח״י זיי״ן אחר ברכת ק״ש של ערבית. וקדיש אחר תפלת ח״י ערבית רשות והנה לי ההדיוט יש לי בכאן הרהורי דברים ובמנהגים שלי הארכנו וכאן אקצר והנני פורטם בעזה״י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They recite</b> Kaddish. See Taz. After Yishtabach they recite Kaddish, and it refers back to Pesukei DeZimrah, for Kaddish is recited only after verses from Scripture or after aggadah. See Magen Avraham 54:3. Regarding what is written that Kaddish is recited after the Amidah, one may say that the Amidah contains several verses, such as “Heal us, Hashem,” and the like. The Levush enumerates the seven Kaddishes recited each day, corresponding to “Seven times a day I praise You”: first, after Yishtabach; second, after the Amidah; third, after the order of Kedushah; fourth, after Aleinu, which contains verses; fifth, after Ashrei at Minchah; sixth, after the Minchah Amidah; and seventh, after the blessings of Krias Shema at Maariv. The Kaddish after the Maariv Amidah is optional. I, in my humble capacity, have several thoughts on this matter. I discussed them at length in my work on customs, but here I shall be brief and set them forth, with Hashem's help.

<b>דע</b> כי שלש קדישים יש לנו א׳ ק״ש עם תתקבל ב׳ ק״ש בלא תתקבל ג׳ ח״ק. והלבוש נתן כלל שא״א תתקבל רק אחר תפלת י״ח והט״ז כתב גם אחר סליחות. והנה בתרצ״ג למ״ד שאומרים ב״פ תתקבל א׳ אחר תפלת ח״י וא׳ אחר ואתה קדוש ליתא לכללא אלא הלבוש לשיטתיה שם שאומרים ח״ק אחר תפלת ח״י ואחר ואתה קדוש ק״ש עם תתקבל וה״נ דעת הט״ז שם. ואמנם מ״ש הלבוש כללא דכל שא״א תתקבל (הש״צ) אין משלים הקדיש ט״ב יוכיח שאומר הש״ץ אחר ואתה קדוש ק״ש בלא תתקבל. כמ״ש הלבוש גופא בתקנ״ט ועט״ז שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Know</b> that we have three forms of Kaddish: first, full Kaddish with Tiskabel; second, full Kaddish without Tiskabel; and third, half Kaddish. Levush gives a rule that Tiskabel is recited only after the Amidah, while Taz writes that it is also recited after Selichos. In siman 693, according to the view that Tiskabel is recited twice, once after the Amidah and once after Ve'Atah Kadosh, this rule does not hold. Levush, however, follows his own view there, that half Kaddish is recited after the Amidah and full Kaddish with Tiskabel after Ve'Atah Kadosh; Taz there likewise holds this view. Yet Levush's rule that whenever the chazzan cannot recite Tiskabel he does not complete the Kaddish is disproved by Tisha B'Av, when the chazzan recites full Kaddish without Tiskabel after Ve'Atah Kadosh, as Levush himself writes in siman 559. See Taz there.

<b>והנה</b> חצי הדיש איכא לעיונא ביה למה בחרו בח״ק לא בק״ש בלא תתקבל כמו שאומרים ק״ש בלא תתקבל אחר פסוקים או הגדה. ואומר אני כפי דעתי ההדיוטית דכללא הוא כ״מ שאומרים בשביל פסוקים או הגדה אומר ק״ש משא״כ כשאומר החזן להפסיק בין פסוקי דזמרה שנשלמו כאן (עב״י) ואמר ש״ע שהיא מצוה בפ״ע ואחר תהלה לדוד במנחה שכל האומר תהלה מובטח כו׳ ואומרים דרך תחנה לא כקורא בתורה אומרים רק חצי קדיש ואחר קריאת התורה בב׳ וה׳ אעפ״י שקורא בתורה מ״מ מצוה ותקנת עזרא אנו אומרים רק ח״ק מה״ט דאמרן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Now</b> half Kaddish requires examination. Why did they choose half Kaddish rather than full Kaddish without Tiskabel, as is recited after verses or aggadah? In my humble opinion, the rule is as follows. Whenever Kaddish is recited on account of verses or aggadah, full Kaddish is recited. By contrast, when the chazzan recites it to mark a separation after Pesukei DeZimrah, which ends at that point, as explained in Beit Yosef, before beginning the Amidah, which is an independent mitzvah, only half Kaddish is recited. Likewise, after Tehillah LeDavid at Minchah, although whoever recites it is assured of a portion in the World to Come, it is recited as supplication rather than as one reading Scripture, and therefore only half Kaddish is recited. After the Torah reading on Monday and Thursday, although Scripture has been read, it is nevertheless a distinct mitzvah and an enactment of Ezra, so only half Kaddish is recited, for the reason stated.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Know</b> that half Kaddish is always recited after the Torah reading, even at Shabbos Minchah and at Minchah on a public fast, because the Kaddish recited at the lectern before the Amidah refers back to the Torah reading. It is not recited on the bimah at Minchah, as Levush writes in siman 292 regarding the Monday and Thursday fasts, so that the congregation will know when to begin the Amidah. This concern does not apply when Ashrei is recited aloud. One might ask why, on Monday and Thursday mornings, Kaddish is recited on the bimah after the Torah reading. Full Kaddish with Tiskabel refers back even to the Amidah, and certainly it could refer to the Torah reading. Levush answers in siman 292 that public Torah reading is exceptionally important, and therefore they instituted a separate Kaddish for it. On Shabbos, in any event, it also separates between the seven aliyos and Maftir. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further. See my work on customs; this is not the place to elaborate.

<b>It does not.</b> See Taz, who inclines to say that although there was no minyan when the preceding section was recited, if a minyan arrived afterward they may recite Kaddish, and he cites siman 53. At first glance, Kaddish is not recited for Yishtabach alone, but only for the preceding verses, as I wrote in letter 1. If so, this ruling is explicit. Even were we to say that Kaddish may also be recited for Yishtabach, because it contains language resembling verses, nevertheless, since the reason given is that one should not interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish, the more basic reason should have been that a minyan is required at the time of Yishtabach. This indicates that it is not required. As for what he writes in the name of Rama, that one may wait before reciting Yishtabach, I do not know how long this waiting may continue. Certainly, after a lengthy interruption it is not appropriate to recite Kaddish for material studied long before. His comments on Levush require study. He apparently understood Levush to mean that there is no obligation to recite Kaddish after Aleinu because Aleinu contains no verses, or because it is recited as supplication. According to that understanding, Levush would concede regarding Tehillim or aggadah. Yet one who examines Levush sees from his conclusion, “Rather, the custom is to recite it because of the mourner's Kaddish that follows,” that his earlier words must also be understood differently. At Maariv, the Sages obligated the recital of the blessings after Krias Shema, and Kaddish consequently follows them. It therefore suffices if ten were present either for the blessings or for Kaddish. Aleinu, however, is not itself obligatory. His opening words may likewise be understood this way. See there. See Magen Avraham 69:4 and 234:1, who disagrees and holds like Levush. It appears there that even for Tehillah at Minchah, which one is obligated to recite three times daily, ten must be present during Tehillah. This obligation is not as absolute as the obligation of the Maariv blessings; rather, one is merely assured of reward for reciting it, even though it is obligatory. Moreover, one may sometimes discharge the obligation in the morning by reciting Tehillim that includes Tehillah. See Eliyah Rabbah here, letter 3. According to this, what is the Taz's proof from Kol Bo for his position? Rabbi Amram's proof that Yishtabach should be recited standing was so that there be no interruption, even when ten are already present. How does this prove anything from Ashrei at Minchah, where ten are not yet present? One may answer that Ashrei has the status of an obligation to recite it twice, and therefore the only possible reason is to avoid an interruption. Understand this. Regarding his statement that Pesukei DeZimrah was not mentioned, I do not understand its connection to what precedes. Rama in siman 53 explicitly states that one should wait before Yishtabach. One may say that Yishtabach is then recited and the Kaddish refers to Yishtabach. There is no need to elaborate. The proper course is that afterward they should recite three verses in the presence of the minyan and then recite Kaddish. Eliyah Rabbah 581:13 questions the words of Kol Bo, and his view there appears to accord with Levush.

<b>Or.</b> See Taz. One who examines Beit Yosef may say that Mahari Bei Rav did not derive the ruling regarding a sleeping person from the case of one who is praying. Rather, Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, letter 9, derives the case of one who is praying from Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. Even those who disagree with Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi regarding a minor may concede regarding a sleeping adult, because the Shechinah rests wherever ten adult Jews are present. Mahari Bei Rav holds that one who is praying cannot listen and remain silent, because listening is equivalent to responding and would constitute an interruption, as discussed in Beit Yosef in siman 104. According to our ruling, however, one who is praying certainly combines, because he can listen silently; perhaps the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed also applies. A sleeping person differs, yet the ruling is nevertheless derived from Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as stated. Regarding Taz's assertion that the principle of anything fit for mixing applies only after the fact and not initially, see Menachos 18. There too, the Torah initially required mixing. See also Kiddushin 25a. See Hilchos Tefillin, siman 32, in Beit Yosef, citing Rashba, that when letters are joined to one another no correction is required even initially, under the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed. According to our ruling, however, the writing remains invalid until the joining is scraped away, because each letter must be surrounded by blank parchment. With Hashem's help, I will explain this elsewhere. Regarding what Taz writes from siman 125, seif 4, Peri Chadash writes the same and agrees with Taz. This implies that if only one person is praying, the others may proceed, and he may remain silent at the end of the blessing and listen to the chazzan's conclusion. See siman 124, seif 4, Magen Avraham letter 8, and Eshel Avraham there; with Hashem's help, it will be explained there. Peri Chadash further adduces proof from Even HaEzer 136. According to Rosh, a sleeping woman cannot be divorced, for she is treated like one lacking legal competence or like a deaf-mute; the same should apply to counting her counterpart toward a minyan. According to Ran and Rashba there, however, the reason is that she cannot safeguard the get. Yet in Shulchan Aruch there, seif 3 in the gloss, if the get is placed in the hand of her agent, even Rosh agrees, as Beit Yosef writes at the end of that siman. Otherwise, how could one ever rely on an agent for acceptance, since perhaps she was asleep when he accepted it? There is no need to elaborate. Certainly, initially one should awaken him. If that is impossible, one who relies on Shulchan Aruch has not lost. Perhaps in places where Kaddish and Kedushah are recited aloud and the remainder of the repetition is said silently, the Amidah is not repeated. In that case, there is no contradiction from siman 124. See what I write, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham letter 8 on this subject.

<b>חציו</b> עט״ז ועפר״ח להמחבר שם דפורעין מקופת הקהל הכא העני גורם שיצא וצריכין לשכור אחר ולכן גם להמחבר חצי לפי נפשות גם למ״ד שם במקום שאין מנהג יגבו לפי ממון התם רוצים בחזן נעימות קול משא״כ כאן עא״ר ועמ״א אות י״ו ושם יבואר אי״ה. ומ״ש סברא לפי ממון שלא יצטרכו כו׳ כלומר העיקר התקנה שלא יצטרכו כו׳ ולבוש כתב מסתמא אין הליכת עני כעשיר להרויח ועא״ר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Half.</b> See Taz and Peri Chadash. According to the Mechaber there, the expense is paid from the communal fund. Here, however, the poor person's departure creates the need to hire another chazzan. Therefore, even according to the Mechaber, half is assessed per capita. Even according to the view there that where there is no established custom the collection is assessed according to wealth, there they seek a chazzan with a pleasant voice, unlike here. See Eliyah Rabbah and Magen Avraham, letter 16, where, with Hashem's help, this will be explained. Regarding the rationale that it should be assessed according to wealth so that the poor will not need to leave, meaning, the primary purpose of the enactment is that they should not need to leave. Levush writes that, ordinarily, a poor person's departure is not comparable to that of a wealthy person who leaves to pursue profit. See Eliyah Rabbah.

<b>לענין</b> בר מצוה עמ״א יו״ד ופלא ברמ״א משלי ח׳ פסוק י״ו יצויר קטנה א״א א׳ חייב מיתה והב׳ פטור באילו יע״ש היטב. ונולדה בשנה מעוברה כ״ח אדר א׳ ובג״ש מעובר׳ ובא עליה זו אי עשאה זונה ספק הוי ובשנייה כה״ג את״ל דזונה דרבנן ספק לקולא. אה״ע ז׳ וכ״ף יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Regarding</b> bar mitzvah, see Magen Avraham, letter 10. A remarkable case arises from Rama's comments to Proverbs 8:16: it is possible for intercourse with one girl to incur the death penalty while intercourse with the other is exempt, even if both acts occurred on the same day. Examine it carefully there. If a girl was born in a leap year on the twenty eighth of Adar I, and her third year is also a leap year, intercourse with her on that date creates a doubt whether she thereby acquires the status of a zonah. In a second comparable case, should one say that the status of zonah is Rabbinic, the doubt is resolved leniently. See Even HaEzer, simanim 7 and 20, and examine the discussion there.
