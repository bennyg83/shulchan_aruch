<b>על</b> שמן עט״ז ואם בירך ע״ב יצא עמ״א אות י׳ ועי׳ לבוש כתב שאין ראוי לאכילה לברך עליו בשם פירוש קשה דא״נ ראוי לאכילה מפני חשיבות (שגדל בא״י סביב יריחו ונקרא יריחו ע״ש כך ערש״י ברכות מ״ג א׳) קבעו ברכה בפ״ע וי״ל דהיה לקבוע ריח טוב בפירי אפרסמון כמו פה״ג ולא מצאתיו כעת שיאמר א׳ מהפוסקים זה וא״כ אם בירך ריח טוב בפירות לא יצא ולא ראיתיו ולא ידעתיו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Over</b> oil. See Taz. If one recited Borei Atzei Besamim, he fulfilled his obligation; see Magen Avraham, letter 10. See Levush, who writes that it is not fit for eating. His explanation is difficult, for even if it were fit for eating, because of its importance, since it grows in Eretz Yisrael around Yericho, which is named for this, as Rashi explains in Berachos 43a, they established a distinct blessing for it. One might say that they should have established the blessing “Who creates a pleasant fragrance in fruits” for balsam oil, just as they established Borei Peri HaGefen. I have not presently found any posek who says this. Accordingly, if one recited “Who creates a pleasant fragrance in fruits,” he would not fulfill his obligation. I have neither seen nor established this ruling.

<b>המריח</b> בפירות טובים כתב א״ר אות ו׳ בשם ע״ת דיברך פה״ע ואח״כ על הריח דברכת אכילה קודמת ע״ש ועמ״א כן ריש הסימן ונראה דמ״מ יאכל מיד שלא יהיה הפסק ואח״כ יברך להריח ומריח בו ולא יכוין ליהנות מריח עד שיאכל נ״ל. עא״ר ולמ״ש נכון הוא בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who smells</b> fragrant fruits. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 6, writes in the name of Olas Tamid that one should first recite the blessing for eating and afterward the blessing for fragrance, because the blessing for eating takes precedence. See there, and see Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Nevertheless, it appears that he should eat immediately so there is no interruption, then recite the blessing for fragrance and smell the fruit. He should not intend to derive benefit from its fragrance until after eating. So it appears to me. See Eliyah Rabbah. According to what I have written, this is correct.

<b>ואם</b> עט״ז וסיים הש״ע נכון ליזהר מלהריח בו ולבוש יריח על בושם אחר ויכוין לפטור זה ועא״ר אות י״ג דיקח עצי בשמים ויועיל לזה דבאפרסמון נמי דיעהד יוצא בעצי בשמים וא״י דאפרסמון גדל על עץ משא״כ סיננו דמברך שמן ערב איך יוצא בעצי בשמים ובהכרח ליקח אפרסמון ואין שכיח וכן אם היה עשבים בו ומ״מ י״ל דמברך בורא מיני בשמים דהיינו שיקח מיני בשמים ויברך ויוציא ג״ז נ״ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See Taz. The Shulchan Aruch concludes that it is proper to avoid smelling it. Levush writes that one should smell another fragrant substance and intend thereby to discharge this one. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 13, who writes that one should take a fragrant wood, and this will also cover it, because regarding balsam too, after the fact, one fulfills the obligation with Borei Atzei Besamim. I do not understand this. Balsam grows on a tree, unlike filtered perfumed oil, over which one recites Borei Shemen Areiv. How would Borei Atzei Besamim discharge it? One would necessarily need actual balsam, which is uncommon. The same issue applies if herbs were placed in the oil. Nevertheless, one may say that he should recite Borei Minei Besamim, meaning that he should take some type of fragrant substance, recite that blessing, and thereby discharge this as well. So it appears to me.

<b>ואם</b> עט״ז עי׳ באות הקדום. ומלת עשבי כו׳ אני העני לא ראיתי באיוב ובקונקורדסיוס בשורש עשב ובשורש הרר לא הביא זה הפסוק באיוב אבל במשלי כ״ז כ״ח עשבות הרים מצאתי נוסחא עי׳ בחיר״ק ושי״ן שוואית דגושה נעה ובית רפויה ולפ״ז עשבי בשמים ג״כ הבית רפויה של עשבי ושל בשמים אבל הרב המדקדק הגדול מהר״ר שלמה הענא ז״ל בשערי תפלה סי׳ שי״ד עשבי בשמי׳ השי״ן בסגו״ל כמו מן חלב חלבי יע״ש וכעת אין הדיוט כמוני מכריע ואי״ה יבואר עוד. וי״ל בין סמיכות לסמיכות וצ״ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See Taz and the preceding letter. Regarding the word isvei, “herbs,” I, in my limited knowledge, did not find the cited verse in Iyov. Nor did I find it in the concordance under either the root for “herb” or the root for “mountain.” In Mishlei 27:25, however, I found the reading “the herbs of the mountains,” vocalized with a chirik, a shin with a mobile sheva and dagesh, and a soft beis. According to this, in the phrase isvei besamim, both the beis of isvei and the beis of besamim are soft. The great grammarian Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Hanau, of blessed memory, however, writes in Shaarei Tefillah, siman 314, that in isvei besamim the shin has a segol, like the form chalvei from chalav; see there. At present, one as unqualified as I cannot decide the matter. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further. One may perhaps distinguish between one construct form and another. This requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An esrog.</b> See Taz, contrary to Magen Avraham, letter 22. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 22. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in simanim 653, 658, and 665, and there is no need to elaborate here. Eliyah Rabbah writes there in the name of Orchos Chayim that on Hoshana Rabbah, after its mitzvah has been completed, all agree that one may smell it. I do not understand this. According to Taz, it remains prohibited even on Shemini Atzeres, as stated in siman 665, because once it is forbidden for eating, its fragrance status is also removed. According to Magen Avraham, during all seven days one may smell it and recite a blessing when it is not being held for the mitzvah. It appears to me that this is a third rationale: since it is designated for the mitzvah, it is not designated for smelling. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in siman 665.
