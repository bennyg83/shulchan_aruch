<b>כ' מ"א וז"ל מהרי"ו כו'</b> משמע דוקא אלו ג' מלאכות כו' משמע קצת דגם הטחינה אפילו בריחים דיד או הלכה לטחינה מותר ע"י גוי וכן דעת הט"ז דאף דעכ"פ משעת טחינה בעי שימור כמ"ש סי' תנ"ג. היינו שימור שלא תחמיץ אבל לשם מצוה די בשימור של הני ג' מלאכות דלא כהב"ח ע"ש. וע בח"י שמקיל ברחים של יד בלא"ה כיון שנעשה ע"י כלי ל"ל בה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Magen Avraham wrote: and these are the words of Mahariv, etc.</b> It implies specifically these three labors, etc. It implies somewhat that milling too, even with a hand mill, or the law regarding milling, is permitted through a non-Jew; and so is the view of the Taz, that even though in any case from the time of milling one needs guarding, as written in siman 453, that means guarding that it not leaven; but for the sake of the mitzvah, guarding of these three labors suffices; not like the Bach; see there. And see Chok Yaakov, who is lenient with a hand mill even without this, since it is done through a vessel: what difference does it make to him?

<b>ומ"מ מוטב כו</b> ע"י קטן. ובח"י כתב טוב לעשות ע"י חש"ו כו' משמע דגם חרש ושוטה עדיף מגוי. ולענ"ד שוטה וחרש וקטן. חרש וקטן עדיפא. דשוטה לאו בר דעת כלל. אבל חרש וקטן נלע"ד דחרש עדיף. דהא ר"א ס"ל ביבמות דף קי"ג ע"א חרש שתרם תרומתו ספק דמספקא ליה אי א"ל דעת' צילותא או קלישתא. או מספקא ליה בעתים חלים ועתים שוטה כמבואר שם ואיכא אמוראי שם דס"ל כוותיה. ובקטן ליכא למ"ד דתרומתו תרומה אפי' מספק. וניהו דלא קי"ל בהא כר"א מ"מ אף לדידן י"ל דעדיף מקטן. ואע"ג דלעיל סי' רס"ו סעיף ה' חרש וקטן שוין. שם טעמא אחריני איכא כמבואר שם בשבת דף קנ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And nevertheless it is better, etc.</b> Through a minor. And in Chok Yaakov he wrote: it is good to do through a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor, etc., implying that a deaf-mute and a deranged person too are preferable to a non-Jew. And to my humble opinion: a deranged person, a deaf-mute, and a minor: a deaf-mute and a minor are preferable; for a deranged person has no understanding at all. But regarding a deaf-mute and a minor, it seems to my humble opinion that a deaf-mute is preferable; for behold R' Elazar holds in Yevamos, folio 113a, that a deaf-mute who separated terumah: his terumah is a matter of doubt, for he is unsure whether we say his mind is clear or weak, or he is unsure whether at times he is healthy and at times deranged, as explained there; and there are Amoraim there who hold like him. And regarding a minor there is no authority who says that his terumah is terumah even as a doubt. And although we do not hold in this like R' Elazar, nevertheless even according to us one may say that he is preferable to a minor. And even though above in siman 266, se'if 5, a deaf-mute and a minor are equal: there there is another reason, as explained there in Shabbos, folio 153.

**** ENGLISH ****
And a deaf-mute means one who does not hear and does not speak; and a deranged person means one who loses what they give him, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 1; so wrote the Taz. And it is further explained there the signs of a deranged person: he goes out alone at night, or tears his clothing, or lodges in a cemetery; and with one of these four things he is called deranged. And specifically when that is his way; but because of one time, even if he acted in a deranged manner, he is not called deranged. And even if that is his way, specifically when he does it in a deranged manner; for otherwise, even if he did all of them, he is not called deranged; so it is there in Yoreh Deah. And a minor: Maharil's view is that until he is bar mitzvah he is called a minor; and the Taz holds that once he has understanding he is no longer called a minor; and see the later authorities.

<b>(ס"ק ד) עבה כו'</b> דלמסקנא כו' דאמרינן דף ל"ז ת"ר אין אופין פת עבה בי"ט בפסח דברי ב"ש. וב"ה מתירים וכמה פת עבה א"ר הונא טפח שכן מצינו בלחם הפנים שהיה צ"ל מצה שעבה טפח. מתקיף לה ר' יוסף אם אמרו בזריזים יאמרו בשאינן זריזים כו' ומסיק אלא פת עבה ר"ל פת מרובה ומשום טרחא בי"ט ע"ש. א"כ למסקנא לא הוזכר כלל דין פת עבה. וע"כ צ"ל דאפילו פת עבה נמי אפשר לשומרו מן החימוץ. דאי ס"ד בעבה א"א לשמרו א"כ לא נדע כמה שיעור עוביו. דהא מלחם הפנים ליכא למילף ושמא כעובי אצבע גם כן מיקרי עבה וה"ל לחכמים לבאר שיעור עוביו. א"ו דבכל ענין שרי ואפילו עבה יותר מטפח ולחם הפנים לא משום חשש חימוץ לא נעשה עבה יותר מטפח אלא שמצותו בכך ופר"ח חולק. וע' במ"א סוף סי' תנ"ו צריך קצת ישוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 4) Thick, etc.</b> For according to the conclusion, etc.: for we say on folio 37: Our Rabbis taught: One does not bake thick bread on Yom Tov of Pesach; the words of Beis Shammai. And Beis Hillel permit. And how much is thick bread? Said Rav Huna: a tefach, for so we find with the lechem hapanim, which needed to be matzah a tefach thick. R' Yosef challenged this: if they said [this] regarding the meticulous, will they say [it] regarding those who are not meticulous, etc.? And it concludes: rather, "thick bread" means a large [quantity of] bread, and because of toil on Yom Tov; see there. If so, according to the conclusion, the law of thick bread was not mentioned at all. And therefore one must say that even thick bread too can be guarded from becoming chametz. For if you would think that with thick [bread] it is impossible to guard it, then we would not know how much the measure of its thickness is; for from the lechem hapanim one cannot learn, and perhaps even the thickness of a finger is also called thick, and the Sages would have had to explain the measure of its thickness. Rather, in every respect it is permitted, and even thicker than a tefach; and the lechem hapanim was not made thicker than a tefach not because of concern for chametz, but because its mitzvah is so; and Peri Chadash disagrees. And see Magen Avraham at the end of siman 456: it needs a bit of resolution.
