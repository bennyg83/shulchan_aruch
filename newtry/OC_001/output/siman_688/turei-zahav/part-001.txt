<b>כרכין המוקפים חומה. </b> הקשה הר"ן למה חלקו מצוה זו לימים חלוקים וקבעו יום מיוחד לפרזים משא"כ בשאר המצו' ותי' שלא נחו בשוה וכמ"ש ב"י  והקשה ב"י על תי' הר"ן מדפרכינן בגמ' אלא שושן כמאן עבדא כו' הא כל עיקרן של מוקפין לא עבדו בט"ו אלא להדמות לשושן ורבא מאי קאמר כו' ולפע"ד אין כאן קושיא כל עיקר דהרב"י הבין דמאי דפריך שושן כמאן עביד היינו דלא כמוקפי' ודלא כפרזין דאלו כמוקפין בעינן מימות יהושע ושושן לא ידעי אי מוקפת מימות יהושע ולא כפרזין דקורין בי"ד ושושן בט"ו מ"ה הקשה ב"י קושיות שלו אבל הר"ן לא פי' כן אלא דכשהקש' המקשן שושן דעביד כמאן הוקשה לו באמת  גם על המוקפין דהם תלוים בשושן אלא דקשה ושושן עצמו במאי תלוי והיינו כמאן פי' ממי למדו לעשות בט"ו ממילא קשה גם על המוקפים שתלוים בשושן ותי' רבא הואיל ונעשה בו נס ההריגה ביום י"ד ונחו בט"ו ע"כ קבעו שם בט"ו וא"כ כשיש חילוק בין שושן לפרזים סברא לדמות כל המוקפים לשושן ופרזים לשאר עיירות שהיו בזמן ההוא אלא שהי' ראוי לחלק במוקפין מן אחשורש בזמן שהי' הנס אלא דהי' א"י חוץ לחיוב הקריאה כמוקפין והם רצו שלא לעשות זילותא לא"י שהית' חריבה בימים ההם וכדי לכוללם לשושן שהי' הנס ביותר וראוי לתת לא"י כל מעל' שאפשר ע"כ תלוי במוקף מיהושע ממילא גם א"י יהי' לו מעלה דשושן ומזה נמשך החילוק דמוקפין לאין מוקפין. וז"ש הב"י בשם הרמב"ם ולמה תלוי הדבר בימי יהושע כדי לחלוק כבוד לא"י כו' ויהיה לזכרון לא"י בנס זה עכ"ל פי' דע"י שיראו הדבר הזה שתלאו במקדש ולא באחשורוש כמו שהי' ראוי וישאלו ע"ז וישובו ע"ז שעושין כן לכבוד א"י כנלע"ד נכון ביאור דברי הר"ן בלי ספק. והרב"י טרח בחנם לתרץ בע"א ולומר שעשו לזכרון שבח א"י בנס זה והוא תמוה דא"כ קשה גם בנס דחנוכה ה"ל לתקן איזו דבר לנס בשביל א"י וכמ"ש הר"ן בתחלה מ"ש משאר המצות:

<b>Walled cities surrounded by a wall.</b> The Ran asked why this mitzvah was divided into different days and a special day was fixed for unwalled towns, unlike other mitzvos. He answered that they did not rest equally, as Beis Yosef wrote. Beis Yosef asked against the Ran's answer from the fact that we ask in the Gemara: but Shushan, according to whom did it act, etc.? Since the entire essence of the walled cities did not act on the fifteenth except to resemble Shushan, what is Rava saying, etc.? In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty here at all. Beis Yosef understood that when it asks, "Shushan, according to whom did it act," it means that it is neither like walled cities nor like unwalled towns: not like walled cities, because we require from the days of Yehoshua, and regarding Shushan we do not know whether it was walled from the days of Yehoshua; and not like unwalled towns, for they read on the fourteenth, while Shushan reads on the fifteenth. Therefore Beis Yosef asked his questions. But the Ran did not explain this way. Rather, when the questioner asked, "Shushan, according to whom did it act," in truth the difficulty also applied to the walled cities that depend on Shushan; but the difficulty was: upon what does Shushan itself depend? This is the meaning of "according to whom," meaning from whom did they learn to act on the fifteenth? Automatically, there is also a difficulty regarding the walled cities that depend on Shushan. Rava's answer is that since the miracle of the killing occurred there on the fourteenth and they rested on the fifteenth, therefore they established there on the fifteenth. If so, once there is a distinction between Shushan and the unwalled towns, it is reasonable to compare all walled cities to Shushan and the unwalled towns to the other towns that existed at that time. It would have been proper to distinguish based on those walled from the days of Achashverosh, when the miracle occurred, but Eretz Yisrael would then have been outside the obligation of reading as walled cities, and they did not want to degrade Eretz Yisrael, which was desolate in those days. In order to include it with Shushan, where the miracle was greater, and because it is fitting to give Eretz Yisrael every possible distinction, they therefore made it depend on being walled from Yehoshua. Automatically, Eretz Yisrael too has the distinction of Shushan, and from this came the distinction between walled and unwalled places. This is what Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rambam: why does the matter depend on the days of Yehoshua? In order to apportion honor to Eretz Yisrael, etc., and so that there be a remembrance of Eretz Yisrael in this miracle; end quote. Meaning, through people seeing that they made it depend on the Temple-era Land and not on Achashverosh as would have been proper, they will ask about this and answer that they do so in honor of Eretz Yisrael. In my humble opinion, this is certainly the correct explanation of the Ran's words. Beis Yosef labored for nothing to answer differently and say that they made a remembrance of the praise of Eretz Yisrael in this miracle. This is astonishing, for if so, regarding the Chanukah miracle too they should have enacted some element in the miracle for the sake of Eretz Yisrael, as the Ran asked at the beginning, what makes this different from the other mitzvos?

<b>אבל מסתמא הוקפה. </b> לכאורה קשה ממ"ש בסי' ת"א בסי' שצ"א וסי' שכ"ה וסתם עיירות מוקפות לדירה שבונין בתים תחלה ואחר כך מקיפים אותה ולק"מ דהתם לא קפדי' אלא שיהיה היקף לדירה דהיינו לאפוקי מבצר שאין ההיקף שלו לדירה אלא לצורך מלחמה ובזה אמרינן דסתם עיירות בהתחלתה בונים איזה בתים דרך עראי תחלה והכנה בעלמא ואח"כ מקיפין חומה  ואח"כ מיישבין אותה בבנינים הרבה והוה שפיר הוקפה ולבסוף ישבה כי הבנינים הראשונים  לא היו רק התחלה בעלמא וה"ה נמי כאן אנו הולכים אחר ההקפה שתהיה קודם לישיבתה ואין הולכים אחר התחלת הבנינים כיון שעיקר הישיבה הוא אח"כ נמצא דמ"ש בסי' ת"א שתחלם בונין בתים היינו ההתחלה בעלמא שלא בא רק לסימן שיהי' כאן עיר. ומ"ש כאן שמן הסתם הוקפה ולבסוף ישבה היינו עיקר ישיבת וקביעת מקום היא אח"כ וכן מדוקדק לשון של הר"ן כאן בב"י שכ' שכן דרך רוב המדינות מקיפין חומה תחלה ואח"כ מיישבין אותה עכ"ל הרי שזכר ל' ישוב ולא בנין בתים שהישוב הוא עיקרי הבנינים לרובן כנלע"ד ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But presumably it was surrounded.</b> At first glance this is difficult from what is written in siman 401, siman 391, and siman 325, that ordinary towns are surrounded for dwelling, for they build houses first and afterward surround the town. This is not difficult at all. There, we are particular only that the enclosure be for dwelling, to exclude a fortress whose enclosure is not for dwelling but for war. In that context we say that ordinary towns, at their beginning, first build some temporary houses and mere preparation, and afterward surround with a wall, and afterward settle the town with many buildings. It is therefore properly surrounded and afterward settled, for the first buildings were only a beginning. The same applies here: we follow the surrounding that comes before its settlement, and we do not follow the beginning of the buildings, since the main settlement is afterward. It emerges that what is written in siman 401, that they first build houses, means only the initial beginning, which came only as a sign that a city will be here. And what is written here, that presumably it was surrounded and afterward settled, means the main settlement and fixing of the place afterward. So too the language of the Ran here in Beis Yosef is precise, for he wrote that this is the way of most countries: they surround with a wall first and afterward settle it; end quote. He mentioned the language of settlement, not building houses, because settlement is the main body of most of the buildings. This appears clear to me.

<b>Provided that they are not distant, etc.</b> In the Gemara this measure is stated, and Rashi explained that it applies to adjacent, for it explains until where it is called adjacent. This implies that regarding visible but not adjacent, they did not give a measure; rather, whenever it is visible, even from afar, it is included. For otherwise, if visible too required a measure, it should not have mentioned visible, only adjacent, since everything would depend on adjacent, meaning within a mil. But from the language of the Tur and Rambam it implies that it applies also to visible. The Maggid Mishneh already wondered about the Rambam because of the difficulty we mentioned, and he needed to emend his words so that it applies only to adjacent alone, as with Rashi. Beis Yosef elaborated on this in various ways. What appears to me clearest is this: they gave a measure of a mil for the level ground between city and city, but not for the slope of the mountain on which the city sits. About this they stated a measure even regarding visible: although we do not count the slope, nevertheless the level ground must not be more than a mil. This is with a mountain, where we count the mil only until the edge of the mountain on which the city sits, because we consider the entire mountain as part of the city, since through the mountain the walled city is visible to this town, and therefore we do not count the slope. But when it sits in a valley, we do count the slope, for there is only the advantage of adjacent, and therefore it must truly be adjacent to the walled city, not more than the measure. I will prove this distinction to you from the sugya. It says: granted, visible even though not adjacent can be found on a mountain; but adjacent even though not visible, how can one find it? Rabbi Yirmeyah said: where it sits in a valley. This has astonished me from my youth: what did the one who asked think? Why was he more comfortable with a mountain? Did he not know that we can find in a valley the same as on a mountain, until Rabbi Yirmeyah came and opened this possibility for him? According to my words, with Heaven's help this is settled. The questioner found it difficult as follows: granted, with a mountain, where the advantage comes from being visible, we properly do not count the slope of the mountain that causes the visibility, as I wrote. But regarding adjacent and not visible, it is difficult: how can one find it? If it sits in a valley and there is a slope down below, you certainly must count the slope toward the measure of a mil, since that slope has no advantage. If so, you must say that the city together with the slope is within the measure; but then it is difficult, for the walled city would certainly be visible to the town if it were in this manner, that is, within the measure of the mil. To this Rabbi Yirmeyah answers: in a valley, meaning that there is a depression without a slope, but like a vertical wall from top to bottom, and below sits the city, and the walled city is not visible to it because it sits above on the side of the wall. Nevertheless, the dwelling of the city is adjacent within a mil. A proof that "valley" means this can be brought from the verse "and the streams of Arnon," where Rashi explained that they were like walls on this side and that side, and the stream was deep between them, like what we mentioned. I am also satisfied regarding another difficulty I had in the Gemara's baraisa: until how far? Rabbi Yirmeyah said: like Chamasan to Teveria, a mil. Why not just say a mil? It teaches us how much the measure of a mil is, like Chamasan to Teveria. This is very difficult: do we not know that the measure of a mil is two thousand amos? The entire tractate Shabbos is full of this. According to my view it is well settled, for Teveria sat in a valley, as Rashi explained in the chapter Kol Kisvei: may my portion be with those who bring in Shabbos in Teveria, because it sits in a valley and it becomes dark while it is still day. This teaches us that the measure of a mil regarding megillah is if the ground slopes like from Chamasan to Teveria. With this everything is settled with Heaven's help. Let it not appear forced in your eyes that the measure of a mil was included regarding visible and adjacent in one measure; nevertheless, the words appear correct, to distinguish by simple reasoning as I wrote, with the matter and its reason. So it appears to me correct, with Heaven's help.

<b>כרך שהוא ספק. </b> כ' רש"ל ותימה ולמה אין מברכין עלי' בב' ימים כשאר ספיקא דיומא לענין ברכה ודוחק לחלק בין יו"ט דאוריי' למגילה דרבנן בשלמא בלא ספק היקף ליכא להקשות אמאי לא נקרא המגילה בי"ד וט"ו משום ספיקא דיומא כשופר ולולב דכבר תירץ המרדכי משום דכתיב ולא יעבור מאחר דאין בקיאין בקביעא דירחא אבל בספק ממש לא שייך לו' ולא יעבור דשמא היום עיקר זמנה ול"ל למיחש לספק ברכה לבטלה יותר מספיק' דיומא לענין י"ט ויש לחלק דהתם קדושת היום גורם לחייבו אף בברכה מכח ספק דאם לא תנהוג בו קדושת היום אף לענין ברכ' א"כ אתה עושיהו חול ועוד קדושת היום תלוי בב"ד והם כך קבלו עליהם עכ"ל. ושני התי' אין הקושיא מתורצת בהם דהא גם גבי מגיל' יש בו קדושת היום לענין כל הדינים התלוים בפורים וגם על התירוץ הב' קשה על הב"ד גופיה למה לא קבלו עליהם בשוה ונלע"ד לתרץ קושייתו דשאני גבי י"ט דיש צד א' לו' שלא יקיים מצות י"ט כלל דאם היום השני קודש לא יועיל מה שנהג אתמול קודש כי חול היה ע"כ מן הספק צריך לנהוג גם ביום השני קודש וכן להפך. אבל במגילם מצינו בלא"ה הרבה זמנים דיש למגילה דהיינו י"א י"ב י"ג וילפי' לה מדכתי' בזמניהם הרבה זמנים יש וגם במקום שעושין בט"ו מקדימין כגון אם חל בשבת. ויש בב"י בשם הירושלמי כל החודש עד ט"ו כשר למגילה וא"כ אפי' באותו צד שתאמר שביום ט"ו עיקר החיוב מ"מ מועיל מה שקראו בי"ד דמצינו בלא"ה קדימה לענין זה ע"כ אין חשש גמור וסגי בי"ד כיון שהיא לרוב העולם וזה נכון לע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A walled city that is uncertain.</b> Maharshal wrote: this is astonishing; why do they not bless over it on both days like every calendar doubt regarding a blessing? It is forced to distinguish between a Torah festival day and megillah, which is rabbinic. Granted, where there is no doubt about the wall, one cannot ask why we do not read the megillah on the fourteenth and fifteenth because of the calendar doubt, as with shofar and lulav, for Mordechai already answered that because it is written "and it shall not pass," after we are not expert in fixing the month. But with an actual doubt, it is not relevant to say "it shall not pass," for perhaps today is its main time. Why should we be more concerned for a doubtful blessing in vain than with a calendar doubt regarding a festival? One can distinguish that there, the sanctity of the day causes him to be obligated even in the blessing because of doubt, for if you do not treat it as the sanctity of the day even regarding the blessing, you make it a weekday. Furthermore, the sanctity of the day depends on the court, and they accepted it upon themselves this way; end quote. The difficulty is not resolved by these two answers, for regarding megillah too there is sanctity of the day regarding all the laws dependent on Purim. The second answer is also difficult: regarding the court itself, why did they not accept them equally? It appears to me to answer his difficulty as follows. It is different with a festival day, where there is one side on which he will not fulfill the mitzvah of the festival at all: if the second day is sacred, it will not help that he treated yesterday as sacred, for it was a weekday. Therefore because of the doubt he must treat the second day as sacred as well, and likewise the reverse. But with megillah, we find in any case that the megillah has many times, namely the eleventh, twelfth, and thirteenth, and we derive this from the phrase "in their times," meaning that there are many times. Also, in a place that observes on the fifteenth they advance it, such as if it falls on Shabbos. In Beis Yosef in the name of the Yerushalmi, the whole month until the fifteenth is valid for megillah. If so, even on the side that you say the main obligation is on the fifteenth, what they read on the fourteenth still helps, for in any event we find advancing in this matter. Therefore there is no full concern, and the fourteenth is enough, since it is for most of the world. This is correct in my humble opinion.

<b>And he was delayed and did not return, etc.</b> The approach of the Rosh and the Tur is that we follow his intent when he went from his place, and both cases are exactly equal. A resident of a walled city who went to an unwalled town and intended to remain there until the daylight of the fourteenth, and so it happened that he remained there at the beginning of the day, has the obligation of the town's reading fall upon him, and he is an unwalled-town resident for a day. But if he returned to his place, which was a walled city, before the day, it does not help that he initially thought to be in the town at the town's reading time. A resident of an unwalled town who went to a walled city and intended to remain there at the beginning of the fourteenth day, and so it happened that he remained there, according to Rashi this does not help to say that he becomes like a resident of a walled city, for he is sitting idle there and there is no reading there, and there is nothing to take effect upon him. Therefore he must read like his town. But according to the Rosh and Tur, in this case too the custom of his town is removed from him, since at the time the reading time of his town begins he is not there and he intended to be in the walled city. Therefore, even if he returns to his town on the fourteenth, he reads on the fifteenth, because once his obligation was removed at its beginning, it is entirely nullified. This is called a walled-city resident for a day. Beis Yosef and Kesef Mishneh wrote that according to this approach, the explanation of an unwalled-town resident for a day is not comparable to the explanation of a walled-city resident for a day. For walled-city resident for a day means that he lost the day of his town, which is the fourteenth, namely that he intended to remain there on the fourteenth, and he is called walled and is removed from the obligation of the people of his place. An unwalled-town resident for a day means that he remained in the unwalled town on the fourteenth, which is the reading day of the place to which he went, and the obligation of that day's reading fell upon him; end quote. I did not understand his speech, for what difference is there between them? In both we require that when he goes from his place he intends to remain in the place to which he is going at the beginning of the fourteenth day, and that this actually occurs. Through this, each one is changed from the custom of his place. The reason is not the same: with the resident of a walled city, the reason is that the obligation of the town takes effect upon him on the fourteenth, since they read then; with the resident of an unwalled town, the reason is also that since he intended to remain there at the beginning in the walled city, he is automatically removed from the law of his place. The words of the Rambam in this section also appear to be directed this way. If he did not intend to return until after the time of reading, meaning after the reading of the fourteenth, automatically the reading obligation of the town falls upon the resident of the walled city, and the resident of the unwalled town is removed from his own reading obligation. Therefore each reads with the people of the place where he is: the resident of the walled city reads on the fourteenth according to the place where he is, and the resident of the unwalled town reads on the fifteenth in the place where he is at the time of the reading of the fourteenth. This means even if he returned to his place after the removal, he reads on the fifteenth. Since the Rambam did not mention this explicitly, that even if he returned to his place, it appears that he holds that he must remain in the walled city until the fifteenth and read with them. According to this, the phrase "the people of the place where he is" is settled even better, meaning that he always reads with the people of the place: the resident of an unwalled town on the fifteenth in the walled city, and the resident of a walled city on the fourteenth in the town. That which the delay depends on the beginning of the fourteenth day after the time of reading, according to the language of the Rambam, is difficult: why should we not follow the night reading? If the resident of an unwalled town intended to be the whole night in the walled city through the beginning of the day, and he did so, automatically he was removed from the obligation of the night reading according to the custom of his town. Perhaps the Rambam truly intended the night reading, which is the whole night. So too it is difficult regarding the Tur, who wrote: but if he intended to stand there for part of the fourteenth, since he did not intend to return to his place at the time of the obligation of his place, he was removed from the reading of his place. In the Gemara as well, Rava mentioned only intent to return on the night of the fourteenth, and did not mention part of the day. It appears that the Tur too intended to say that this is enough: if he intended to remain there the whole night alone. But the measure of the night reading is perhaps, in pressing circumstances, the whole night until sunrise, as with Shema in siman 235. The "part of the fourteenth" that the Tur used means from dawn. This requires analysis.
