<b>1) [Seif 1] Cities surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, etc.  </b>The reason the matter was made dependent on Yehoshua rather than Moshe is that Yehoshua was the principal agent in the conquest of Eretz Yisrael. So wrote the Re'em in his Tosafos, and so too wrote Meiri. Birkei Yosef, letter 2. Erech HaShulchan, letter 2. Why was the matter made dependent on the days of Yehoshua? In order to accord honor to Eretz Yisrael, which was desolate at that time, so that its residents would read like the residents of Shushan and would be considered as though they were cities surrounded by a wall. Even though they are now desolate, since they were surrounded by a wall in the days of Yehoshua they read on the 15th, and there will thereby be a remembrance of Eretz Yisrael in connection with the miracle. Rambam, Hilchos Megillah 1:5. As for the reason they divided this mitzvah among different days and established a special day for unwalled cities and a special day for walled cities, unlike other mitzvos, the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran that the Jews dwelling in unwalled cities and the Jews in Shushan did not rest from their enemies on the same day. The Jews in the unwalled cities rested on the 14th, while the Jews in Shushan rested on the 15th, and each made feasting and rejoicing on the day of his rest. Therefore, when they established a festival upon themselves for future generations, they fixed it for each group on the day on which it rested, and they equated all cities surrounded by a wall with Shushan, because the miracle occurred there, see there. As for why in the present era the Megillah is not read on Purim on the 11th, 12th, or 13th, as stated at the beginning of tractate Megillah, it is because they said there in the Gemara: R' Yehudah said, when does this apply? When the years are properly fixed and the Jewish people dwell upon their land. But in the present era, since people calculate by it, they read it only at its appointed time. Rashi explains: But in the present era, when all of these have ceased and the Jewish people are dispersed, and the emissaries of beis din do not reach them, everyone watches the date of the Megillah reading and says, 'We read the Megillah on 14 Adar; 15 days then remain in Adar, and on 15 Nissan we celebrate Pesach.' If its reading were advanced, they would celebrate Pesach at the end of 30 days from the reading and would consequently eat chametz during the latter days of Pesach. End quote. Beit Yosef cited his words. So too wrote Rambam in the aforementioned chapter, that in the present era it is read only at its appointed time, which is the 14th and the 15th: villagers and residents of unwalled cities read on the 14th, and residents of walled cities read on the 15th. So too wrote the Tur. These are likewise the words of the Shulchan Aruch:

<b>3) Ibid. Even if they do not have ten batlanim.  </b>So wrote Tosafos and the Rosh there, that any city surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun reads on the 15th even if it does not have ten batlanim. However, the Maggid Mishneh wrote that there is another approach regarding this, namely, that even in a city surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua, ten batlanim are required; otherwise, it is considered like a town. This is the opinion of Ramban and Rashba. End quote. Beit Yosef cited his words and wrote that this approach was not accepted by the Ran. Nevertheless, he wrote that the present custom in Eretz Yisrael to read on the 15th, even though in most places there are not ten batlanim who refrain from work in order to remain in the synagogue, can be reconciled even according to that approach. It does not literally require men who are idle from work; rather, whenever ten men live there who go to the synagogue morning and evening, it is considered as though there are ten batlanim. End quote. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch ruled here in accordance with Tosafos and the Rosh, that even if they do not have ten batlanim, because ordinarily in a city there are ten men who go to the synagogue morning and evening:

<b>5) Ibid. Provided it was first surrounded and afterwards settled, etc.  </b>If you ask: Since it says that a city first settled and afterwards surrounded is judged like a village, how can a city surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun read on the 15th? Perhaps it was settled first and only afterwards surrounded. One can answer that the settlement of non-Jews is not considered settlement. It follows that when Yehoshua conquered it, it had a wall but was not considered settled until Jews settled there, etc. Yet there remains room to analyze cities surrounded by walls outside Eretz Yisrael. Perhaps their settlement preceded their walls, in which case they are considered as though they have no wall. One must therefore say that we follow the majority, for in most cases their walls precede their settlement, since the practice of most countries is first to surround a place with a wall and only afterwards settle it. <b>7) Ibid., Rama's gloss. But ordinarily it was surrounded and afterwards settled.  </b>As for the fact that in siman 401 he wrote, 'Ordinary cities are enclosed for residential purposes, for they first build houses and afterwards surround them,' Taz, s.k. 2, wrote that what is stated in siman 401, that they first build houses, refers merely to an initial beginning, which serves only as an indication that a city will be established there. Pri Chadash wrote that what is stated in siman 401, that they first build houses and afterwards surround them, means that they first build the houses with the intention of surrounding them afterwards.
