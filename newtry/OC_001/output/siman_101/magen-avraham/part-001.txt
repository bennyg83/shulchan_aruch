<b>ולא יתפלל בלבו</b>. צ"ע אם התפלל בלבו אם יצא דלכאורה משמע דיצא אף על גב דקי"ל דהרהור  לאו כד"ד כמ"ש סי' ס"ב וסי' קפ"ה שאני תפלה דגמרי' מדכתיב ולעבדו בכל לבבכם איזו עבודה שבלב זו תפלה א"כ עיקר תלי בכוונת הלב והקב"ה יודע מחשבות אלא דבגמר' משמע דלא יצא דאל"כ ה"ל לתקן שבעל קרי יהרהר בלבו התפלה דהא רשאי להרהר כיון דקי"ל הרהור לאו כד"ד אלא ע"כ דלא יצא בהרהור דבעי' שיחתוך בשפתיו כי הקול מעורר למעלה כידוע והא דכ' בסי' צ"ד דחולה יהרהר בלבו היינו לרווחא דמלתא אבל אינו יוצא ידי תפלה בזה וכ"מ בפוסקים דהא בגמ' דף ט"ו ע"ב אמרי' אם בירך ב"ה בלבו יצא וה"ה לשאר ברכות וכ' הרא"ש פ' מ"ש דמיירי שמוצי' בשפתיו דהרהור לאו כד"ד וכן פסק סי' קפ"ה א"כ ס"ל דכללא כייל בכל מילי דאל"כ יש לחלק בשלמא ק"ש כתיב ביה ושננתם משא"כ ב"ה דכתיב וברכת וכשמהרהר נמי מברך להקב"ה כי הוא יודע מחשבות אלא ע"כ אין לחלק וכן משמע בגמ' וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He shall not pray in his heart.</b> It requires study whether one who prayed only in his heart fulfilled his obligation. Seemingly he did, although we rule that thought is not equivalent to speech, as stated in simanim 62 and 185, because prayer differs. We derive it from the verse, "to serve Him with all your heart," and Chazal explain that service of the heart is prayer. Thus, the essential matter depends on the heart's concentration, and Hashem knows thoughts. The Gemara, however, implies that he did not fulfill his obligation, for otherwise Chazal should have ordained that a baal keri think the prayer in his heart. He is permitted to think it because thought is not equivalent to speech. It follows that thought does not fulfill the obligation, since one must articulate the words with his lips, for the voice arouses effects Above, as is known. The ruling in siman 94 that an ill person should think the prayer in his heart is merely an additional benefit, but he does not thereby fulfill the obligation of prayer. This also appears from the poskim. The Gemara on page 15b states that if one recited Bircas HaMazon in his heart he fulfilled his obligation, and the same applies to other blessings. The Rosh, in the chapter Mi SheMeiso, explains that the case concerns one who articulated the words with his lips, because thought is not equivalent to speech, and this is ruled in siman 185. He therefore holds that this principle applies universally. Otherwise one could distinguish Krias Shema, regarding which Scripture states "you shall teach them," from Bircas HaMazon, regarding which Scripture states "you shall bless," and even when one thinks he blesses Hashem, Who knows thoughts. It follows that no distinction may be drawn. The Gemara also implies this. The matter requires study.

<b>ומשמיע לאזניו</b>. כהרב"י בב"ה שהזוהר מסכים להתוספתא שלא ישמיע לאזניו ואין ראי' משם די"ל דכוונ' הזוהר שאיש אחר לא ישמע קולו וכמ"ש רמ"א סי' קמ"א ומ"מ פשוט דאם לא השמיע לאזניו יצא. כ' ב"ח דאף החזנים לא יגביהו קולם יותר אלא כדי צורך שישמיעו להצבור ואין דבריו מוכרחים: האר"י לא היה משמיע קולו אפילו בזמירות רק בשבת הרים קולו מעט [הכוונות]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he makes it audible to his ears.</b> Beit Yosef writes in Bedek HaBayis that the Zohar agrees with the Tosefta that one should not make it audible to his ears. There is no proof from there, however, for the Zohar may mean only that another person should not hear his voice, as Rama writes in siman 141. In any event, it is clear that if he did not make it audible to his ears, he fulfilled his obligation. Bach writes that even chazzanim should not raise their voices more than necessary to make themselves heard by the congregation, but his words are not compelling. The Arizal would not make his voice audible even during zemiros; only on Shabbos did he raise his voice slightly, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos.
