**** ENGLISH ****
(In Magen Avraham s.k. 3) And there is no proof from there, etc.; see in Beit Shemuel on Berachot, that it is proven from there regarding Urim and Tumim — prayer in a whisper was forbidden only actual raising of voice that all around him hear; but if he speaks and one standing opposite him or right next to him hears well, it is fine — see there. And somewhat requires study: for in Sotah 32b we say, why did they enact prayer in a whisper — so as not to embarrass oneself, etc.; see there. It implies they enacted that no person should hear; and as we find regarding the Temple: they stand crowded, etc., so one should not hear his fellow's confession — see in Beit Shemuel Chullin that Magen Avraham wrote that certainly it is fitting to publicize, only we do not rely on him perforce to publicize; and if they had not enacted prayer in a whisper but only aloud, one who prayed in a whisper would necessarily be embarrassed, for presumably it is fitting to pray somewhat aloud to awaken intent, etc.; therefore they enacted that all say in a whisper — see there. And per this one may say: even though from verses of Channah we derive it, nevertheless the essence is a rabbinic enactment, and they enacted in a whisper that one need not make heard to any person; and if this one wishes to awaken his intent and makes heard somewhat until his fellow right next to him hears, there is no objection, since the matter depends on his will. But to raise his voice is forbidden, lest he appear as one of little faith; and see Magen Avraham siman 141 on the words of the Zohar; and I saw in Beit Yosef that he wrote on this matter — see there; and so in his book Mahadura Batra; and see there that he wrote to reject the proof from Yoma: what we say "as Channah said in prayer" is not literally so, only that he should not contemplate and not raise his voice; but regarding hearing of one standing next to him — there the priest must hear, and here Channah speaks before Hashem; even though one must articulate with the lips per the plain meaning and truth to be voice and speech, nevertheless the voice must not be heard at all, for he answers in a whisper — see there; and the distinction is correct, though forced to explain "as she said," etc., that it implies her question of Urim and Tumim was such that the priest standing next to her heard. Nevertheless it is plain that to pray in a whisper such that even one next to him does not hear is fine, as proven from Channah that even Eli did not hear. And it is possible the custom was to pray not in whisper literally but cutting the words such that one next to him heard; and thus is the answer for Eli: at first glance it is difficult why Eli considered her a drunkard and did not consider she prayed in a whisper (and Rashi in Shmuel wrote they were not accustomed to pray in a whisper, and Taz brought it siman 102; and it appears they also were not accustomed to raise their voices in prayer even to make heard somewhat to awaken intent; therefore he considered her a drunkard): per the custom they would pray with some audible voice such that one next to him heard, and she exceeded and only her lips moved and her voice was not heard at all; therefore he wrote "and Eli watched her mouth," thinking she prayed and his ears would hear what she uttered from her mouth; but she wanted that even Eli not hear, and prayed in actual whisper; therefore he considered her a drunkard. But per this, what is said in the Gemara "as Channah did" means at least not in a loud voice nor in thought of the heart alone; but whether one next to him hears or complete whisper — both are included in "her lips moved and her voice was not heard"; only the custom was to pray somewhat with oral speech to awaken intent, as stated from words of Or Zaru'a in Beit Shemuel. And indeed per this one may say that afterward they enacted actual whisper so as not to embarrass, etc.; and requires study among the commentators; and see Beit Yosef from the language of the Zohar Vayakhel: if this prayer is heard by others, a man's words are not heeded above, etc. — it implies even one right next to him should not listen. Nevertheless one who cannot concentrate in one manner should do what he can concentrate on; for even though we hold if he did not have intent he need not return and pray, nevertheless if he fixes his prayer in a manner he cannot concentrate, certainly he does not fulfill his obligation and must return and pray, as appears to me.
