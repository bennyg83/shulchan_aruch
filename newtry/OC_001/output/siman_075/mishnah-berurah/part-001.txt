(א) במקום שדרכה - מפני שזה מביא לאדם לידי הרהור כשמסתכל בו בכלל ערוה היא ואסור לקרות או להזכיר שום דבר שבקדושה נגד זה כמו נגד ערוה ממש ולפי מה שביארנו לקמן סעיף ו' בשם האחרונים דנגד ערוה ממש אסור אפילו בעוצם עיניו עד שיחזיר פניו ה"ה בזה ויש מתירין בזה אם הוא נזהר מלראות כלל. וכשא"א בענין אחר נראה דיש לסמוך ע"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) In a place that is ordinarily covered. This is because looking at it brings a person to improper thoughts. It is considered ervah, and it is forbidden to recite Krias Shema or mention any sacred matter in its presence, just as in the presence of actual ervah. According to what we explain below in seif 6 in the name of the later authorities, that in the presence of actual ervah even closing one's eyes is ineffective until one turns his face away, the same applies here. Some permit this if he is careful not to look at all. When there is no other possibility, it appears that one may rely on this opinion.

(ב) לכסותו - אבל פניה וידיה כפי המנהג שדרך להיות מגולה באותו מקום וכן בפרסות רגל עד השוק [והוא עד המקום שנקרא קניא בל"א] במקום שדרכן לילך יחף מותר לקרות כנגדו שכיון שרגיל בהן אינו בא לידי הרהור ובמקום שדרכן לכסות שיעורן טפח כמו שאר גוף האשה אבל זרועותיה ושוקה אפילו רגילין לילך מגולה כדרך הפרוצות אסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
(2) To cover it. Her face and hands, however, according to the local practice by which they are ordinarily uncovered, are permitted. The same applies to the feet up to the shok, meaning up to the place called the knee in the vernacular, in a place where women ordinarily walk barefoot. One may recite Krias Shema in their presence, because familiarity with them prevents improper thoughts. In a place where they are ordinarily covered, their measure is one handbreadth, like the rest of a woman's body. Her upper arms and her shok, however, are forbidden even where women habitually expose them in the manner of the immodest.

(ז) פחות מטפח - ואם השוק מגולה י"א דאפילו באשתו ופחות מטפח אסור לקרות נגדה שהוא מקום הרהור יותר משאר איברים. וכ"ז לא איירי אלא לענין איסור ק"ש דהאיסור הוא להרבה פוסקים לקרות נגד המגולה אפילו בלא מכוין לאיסתכולי אבל לענין איסור הסתכלות לכו"ע המסתכל באשה אפילו באצבע קטנה כיון שמסתכל בה להנות עובר בלאו דלא תתורו אחרי עיניכם ואמרו שאפילו יש בידו תורה ומע"ט לא ינקה מדינה של גיהנם וראיה בעלמא לפי תומו בלא נהנה שרי אם לא מצד המוסר ובספר מנחת שמואל הוכיח דאדם חשוב יש לו ליזהר בכל גווני. וכתב הפמ"ג דבמקומות שדרך להיות מכוסה [כגון זרועותיה וכה"ג שאר מקומות הגוף] אף ראיה בעלמא אסור וכתבו הפוסקים דבתולות דידן בכלל נידות הם משיגיעו לזמן ווסת ובכלל עריות הם:
**** ENGLISH ****
(7) Less than one handbreadth. If the shok is exposed, some say that even with one's wife and even less than one handbreadth, it is forbidden to recite Krias Shema in its presence, because it is more likely to arouse improper thoughts than other limbs. All this concerns only the prohibition of reciting Krias Shema. According to many authorities, it is forbidden to recite in the presence of an exposed area even without intending to look at it. Regarding the prohibition against looking, however, all agree that one who looks at a woman, even at her little finger, when he does so for pleasure, violates the prohibition, “Do not stray after your eyes.” The Sages said that even if he possesses Torah and good deeds, he will not escape the judgment of Gehinnom. A casual glance without intent and without deriving pleasure is permitted as a matter of strict law, though it may still be improper from the standpoint of moral conduct. Minchas Shmuel proves that a distinguished person must be careful in every circumstance. Peri Megadim writes that in areas ordinarily covered, such as her upper arms and similar parts of the body, even a casual glance is forbidden. The authorities write that our unmarried girls are considered niddos once they reach the age when menstruation ordinarily begins, and are therefore included among the forbidden relations.

(ח) מדברי הרא"ש וכו' - אבל הרשב"א חולק ע"ז וטעמו דס"ל דכי היכי דבעצמה יכולה לקרות כשהיא ערומה אלמא דאין בגילוי כל גופה משום ולא יראה בך ערות דבר אלא לאנשים ומשום הרהור כן אף אשה אחרת מותרת לקרות ולהתפלל נגדה כשהיא ערומה והאחרונים מסכימים עם הרשב"א ודעתם דגם הרא"ש מודה לזה. ודע עוד דלכו"ע אין מותר לקרות נגדה כשהיא ערומה רק כשהיא יושבת כדי שלא יהא פניה שלמטה נראית אבל כשהיא עומדת דינה כמו נגד ערות איש ואפילו ברשות אחרת אסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
(8) From the words of the Rosh, etc. Rashba disagrees. His reasoning is that since a woman herself may recite when she is naked, it is evident that the exposure of her entire body is not included in “no naked matter shall be seen among you” with respect to women themselves, but is forbidden only to men because of improper thoughts. Accordingly, another woman may likewise recite and pray in her presence while she is naked. The later authorities agree with Rashba and maintain that the Rosh also concedes this. Know further that according to all opinions, she may recite in the presence of a naked woman only when that woman is sitting, so that her genital area is not visible. When she is standing, her law is like that of a man's ervah, and it is forbidden even when she is in another domain.

**** ENGLISH ****
(10) That she ordinarily covers. Even if she ordinarily covers it only in the marketplace and not in the house or courtyard, it is nevertheless considered ervah according to all opinions, even in the house. It is therefore forbidden there to recite in its presence if some of it becomes exposed. Know further that even if this woman and her companions in that locality ordinarily go bareheaded in the marketplace in the manner of the immodest, it remains forbidden, just as an exposed shok is forbidden in every circumstance, as explained above in seif katan 2. Since women are obligated by law to cover their hair, and this includes a Torah prohibition derived from “he shall uncover the woman's head,” implying that it was otherwise covered, and since all Jewish women who uphold the law of Moshe have observed this from the days of our ancestors until the present, the hair is considered ervah and one may not recite in its presence. The ruling excludes only unmarried girls, who are permitted to go with uncovered hair, or hair that emerges beyond the hair binding. The latter depends on local practice. If Jewish women in that place are careful that not even the smallest amount emerge beyond the binding, it is considered ervah and one may not recite in its presence. Otherwise it is permitted, because familiarity with it prevents improper thoughts, as stated below.

(14) To emerge, etc. Meaning, in addition to the hat on her head, she has a hair binding, a garment that constrains the hair so that it does not emerge. Rashba is lenient regarding the small amount that cannot be fully contained and extends beyond the binding. See Chasam Sofer, siman 36. The Zohar in Parashas Naso is exceedingly stringent that no hair of a woman be visible, stating that this causes poverty in the home, causes her children not to be esteemed in their generation, and causes the other side to dwell in the home. All the more so when she goes this way in the marketplace. Therefore a woman should ensure that even the beams of her house do not see a single hair of her head. If she does so, Scripture states, “Your children are like olive shoots.” Just as the olive, etc., so her children will be elevated in esteem above other people. Moreover, her husband will be blessed with all blessings above and below, with wealth, children, and grandchildren. This is an abbreviated rendering of the Zohar. Magen Avraham writes that it is proper to follow the Zohar. The Gemara in Yoma relates concerning Kimchis that in the merit of her extraordinary modesty, by which the beams of her house never saw the seams of her undergarment, she merited that Kohanim Gedolim descended from her.

(יז) זמר אשה - אפילו פנויה אבל שלא בשעת ק"ש שרי אך שלא יכוין להנות מזה כדי שלא יבוא לידי הרהור וזמר אשת איש וכן כל העריות לעולם אסור לשמוע וכן פנויה שהיא נדה מכלל עריות היא ובתולות דידן כולם בחזקת נדות הן משיגיע להן זמן וסת. וקול זמר פנויה נכרית היא ג"כ בכלל ערוה ואסור לשמוע בין כהן ובין ישראל. ומ"מ אם הוא בדרך בין העכו"ם או בעיר והוא אנוס שא"א לו למחות כיון דלא מצינו דמקרי ערוה מדאוריית' מותר לקרות ולברך דאל"כ כיון שאנו שרויין בין העכו"ם נתבטל מתורה ותפלה וע"ז נאמר עת לעשות לד' הפרו תורתך אך יתאמץ לבו לכוין להקדושה שהוא עוסק ולא יתן לבו לקול הזמר:
**** ENGLISH ****
(17) A woman's singing voice. This applies even to an unmarried woman. Outside the time of Krias Shema, it is permitted to hear her, provided he does not intend to derive pleasure, lest he come to improper thoughts. It is always forbidden to hear the singing voice of a married woman or of any woman forbidden to him. An unmarried woman who is a niddah is likewise among those forbidden to him, and our unmarried girls are all presumed to be niddos once they reach the age when menstruation ordinarily begins. The singing voice of an unmarried non-Jewish woman is also considered ervah and may not be heard, whether by a kohen or by an Israelite. Nevertheless, if he is traveling among non-Jews or is in a city and is compelled to hear it because he cannot prevent it, then since we have not found that a woman's voice is considered ervah by Torah law, he may recite Krias Shema and blessings. Otherwise, because we dwell among non-Jews, Torah study and prayer would be abolished. Concerning this it is said, “It is a time to act for Hashem; they have nullified Your Torah.” He should nevertheless strengthen his heart to concentrate on the sacred matter in which he is engaged and pay no attention to the singing voice.

(19) In the presence of ervah. Scripture states, “For Hashem your God walks in the midst of your camp; your camp shall be holy, and He shall see no naked matter among you.” From here the Sages learned that wherever Hashem our God walks with us, meaning when we engage in Krias Shema, prayer, or Torah study, we must ensure that Hashem not see among us a naked matter. This means that no ervah may be opposite the face of the person reciting or praying within his field of vision, as is evident from siman 79, seif 2. It also means that he himself may not be naked, because his own ervah is thereby exposed. This too is included in the verse.

**** ENGLISH ****
(29) Or he closed, etc. The later authorities agree that none of these remedies is effective except turning away, because Scripture does not say, “you shall not see,” but “it shall not be seen,” meaning that it must not be visible to the one who sees. Even turning away is effective only when he turns his entire body and stands facing another direction, so that the ervah is at his side. If he turns only his face, it is ineffective. It follows that when he himself is naked, there is no possible remedy that would permit him to speak words of Torah. Mishbetzos Zahav writes that if he is in one domain and a naked person is in another domain opposite him, and he closes his eyes so as not to see him, one could say that this is permitted according to all opinions. Derech HaChayim also somewhat implies this. In Eshel Avraham, siman 79, letter 8, however, it appears that he retracted this, and Chayei Adam, rule 4, letter 9, likewise does not imply it. If a glass window separates him from the ervah and he closes his eyes so as not to see it, this is effective according to all opinions, because there is at least some partition covering the ervah from him. One may think words of Torah while naked, and all the more so in the presence of another person's ervah, because Scripture says, “a naked matter.” Speech is forbidden, but thought is permitted. Nevertheless, he should not listen at that time to another person's blessing with intent to fulfill his obligation, because the principle that hearing is equivalent to responding cannot apply when he is unable to respond. This is stated by the later authorities.

(ל) דבראיה תלה - ר"ל אע"פ שהוא בסמוך לו תוך ד' אמותיו דבצואה קי"ל בסימן ע"ט דאסור אפילו אם הוא מלאחריו בזה גילה הכתוב דתלוי רק בראיה ונ"מ בכל זה אף לדידן דמחמירין בעצימת עינים וכנ"ל מ"מ אם החזיר פניו וגופו מן הערוה מהני לכו"ע אפילו בסמוך לו:

(30) Scripture made it dependent on seeing. Meaning, even though the ervah is near him within four cubits. Regarding excrement, we rule in siman 79 that it is forbidden even when it is behind him. Here Scripture revealed that the law depends only on sight. This distinction remains relevant even according to our practice of being stringent about merely closing the eyes, as explained above. If he turns his face and body away from the ervah, however, it is effective according to all opinions even when the ervah is near him.
