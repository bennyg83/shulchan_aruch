<b>וי״א</b> כו׳ בטו״ר כתב כו׳ עט״ז מהציון הלז אחשוב אני שאדונינו ז״ל כיון כוונה גדולה. כי בגמרא (ברכות כ״ד א׳) אר״י טפח באשה ערוה אר״ח שוק באשה ערוה שנאמר גלי שוק ותגל ערותך ויש ג׳ פירושים א׳ בחה״ר (ועב״י) לומר שוק הוי מקום צנוע באשה ב׳ הב״ח כתב דשוק באשתו פחות מטפח הוי ערוה לק״ש ג׳ דר״ח בא לומר דבאשה אחרת פחות מטפח נמי ונקיט שוק ה״ה שאר איברים המכוסים וכהגמי״י (ומרש״י ד״ה שוק בא״א יורה כחה״ר והב״ח כתב בזה עמ״ש לקמן אי״ה) א״כ בגמרא י״ל כפי׳ הג׳ ובטור א״א לומר כן דפסק באחרת נמי טפח בהכרח כפי׳ הב׳ דבשוק באשתו שוק פחות מטפח אסור לק״ש ויורה ע״ז הלשון שכתב טפח מגולה באשה אסור לקרות ק״ש אפילו אשתו וכן שוקה להסמיך שוקה לאשתו כאמור והמחבר הביא לשון הטור ושינה בכוונה טפח מגולה אפילו אשתו אסור כי סובר וכן שוקה כחה״י. ולכן השמיט בכאן שוקה והר״ב יפרש כפי׳ הג׳ והביאו והט״ז ז״ל כתב כי למה השמיטו שוק י״ל שוק באשתו פחות נמי והוי חומרא כי באפשר שניהם אמת כי שוק אאשתו כו׳ פחות מטפח והגמי״י נמי מוכרח מדאמר טפח באשת״ו ולא אמר אלא לק״ש ויהיה אפילו באשה אחר׳ לפיסול ק״ש דאפילו חוזר וקורא (עמ״ש במ״א אי״ה מזה על ח״מ פ״ג מק״ש הי״ו) ש״מ באחרת פחות מטפח נמי אסור בק״ש וחוזר וקורא והבן זה ועמ״א בזה והוי יודע דלשון שוק היא מארכובה (ערש״י ויקרא ז׳ ל״ב ובתי״ט פ״י דחולין מ״ד וכ״פ הר״מ ז״ל בה׳ מעה״ק פ״ט ה״י ושוק לפעמים נקרא ירך) נמצא כל הרגל עד ארכובה שקורין קניא שם במקום שהולכין יחף ומגולה אפשר אין חשש ובמקום שדרכן לכסותן טפח ומארכובה למעלת פחות מטפח נמי ועב״ח כתב שוק מטונף כו׳ היינו מחמת מלאכה יע״ש. עיין נ״ץ פי׳ דברי ר״ח כן חגיגה י״ג א׳ רש״י ד״ה רכובה יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> The Tur writes, etc. See the Taz. From this citation I believe that our master of blessed memory had a profound intention. In Berachos 24a Rabbi Yitzchak says that a tefach of a woman is ervah, and Rabbi Chisda says that a woman's lower leg is ervah, as it is written, “uncover the leg, expose the thigh.” There are three interpretations. First, the Chiddushei HaRashba, as cited by Beit Yosef, explains that the lower leg is a normally covered part of a woman. Second, the Bach writes that even less than a tefach of one's wife's lower leg is ervah for Krias Shema. Third, Rabbi Chisda teaches that with another woman even less than a tefach is prohibited, and he mentions the lower leg merely as an example, the same applying to all normally covered limbs. This is the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos. Rashi's wording concerning the lower leg of a married woman appears to support the first interpretation, though the Bach discusses this as I will explain below, with Hashem's help. The Gemara can therefore be understood according to the third interpretation. The Tur, however, cannot mean this, because he rules that even regarding another woman a tefach is required. He must follow the second interpretation, that even less than a tefach of one's wife's lower leg is prohibited for Krias Shema. His wording indicates this, for he writes that one may not recite Krias Shema opposite a tefach exposed on a woman, even his wife, “and likewise her lower leg,” linking the lower leg to his wife. The Mechaber quotes the Tur but deliberately changes the wording to, “a tefach exposed, even on his wife, is prohibited,” because he understands “and likewise her lower leg” according to the first interpretation. He therefore omits the lower leg here. The Rama understands it according to the third interpretation and includes it. The Taz writes that the omission of the lower leg may be explained because even less than a tefach of one's wife's lower leg is prohibited, which is a stringency. It is possible that both rulings are true: regarding one's wife, less than a tefach of the lower leg is prohibited, and Hagahos Maimoniyos is also necessarily correct. For the Gemara says, “a tefach regarding his wife,” rather than merely saying “for Krias Shema.” This implies that with another woman even less than a tefach invalidates Krias Shema and he must repeat it. See what I wrote, with Hashem's help, in the Magen Avraham regarding Chiddushei Maimoniyos, chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, halachah 16. Thus, regarding another woman even less than a tefach is prohibited for Krias Shema and requires repetition. Understand this well, and see the Magen Avraham. Know also that the term “lower leg” means from the knee downward. See Rashi to Vayikra 7:32, Tosafos Yom Tov to Chullin 10:4, and the Rambam in Hilchos Maaseh HaKorbanos 9:10, although at times “lower leg” denotes the thigh. Thus, the entire leg below the knee, called the shin, may raise no concern in a place where women walk barefoot and it is normally exposed. Where women normally cover it, a tefach is prohibited, while above the knee even less than a tefach is prohibited. The Bach's statement that the lower leg is soiled refers to soiling from work. See there. See Nechalas Tzvi, who explains Rabbi Chisda's statement similarly, and Rashi to Chagigah 13a, s.v. “knee.”

<b>אפלפל</b> קצת הנה אמר שבאשתו ולק״ש ולא סתם לק״ש מוכח כהגמי״י ועב״ח שהקשה על הטור איך כתב באשה אחרת טפח דאסור בלא ק״ש להסתכל ש״מ דס״ל אין חוזר וקורא ותי׳ דבהסתכל בלא נהנה שרי וכ״מ בא״ע כ״א ס״א ולק״ש אסור ולפ״ז הגמי״י והר״ב נמי בראי׳ בעלמא מיירי וא״צ לחזור ולקרות כמ״ש הפר״ח ע״ג בהרהור והא דלא אמר לק״ש מוכח כהגמי״י אלא מרש״י ברכות ד״ה לאיסתכולי אם אשת איש היא משמע פנויה שרי אמאי לא מוקי לק״ש סתם בפנויה ש״מ פנייה נמי פחות מטפח אסור כהגמי״י הן אמת שבאה״ע כ״א ס״א וב׳ מבואר דאסור בתולה מדרבנן עכ״פש עב״ש אות ב׳ וצ״ע כעת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>I will analyze</b> the matter somewhat. The Gemara says, “regarding his wife and Krias Shema,” rather than simply “for Krias Shema,” which supports Hagahos Maimoniyos. See the Bach, who asks how the Tur can write that a tefach of another woman is prohibited, when looking at another woman is forbidden even outside Krias Shema. This indicates that the Tur holds that one need not repeat Krias Shema. The Bach answers that mere looking without deriving pleasure is permitted, as also appears in Even HaEzer 21:1, but is prohibited during Krias Shema. According to this, Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Rama likewise address ordinary sight, and one need not repeat Krias Shema, as the Peri Chadash writes regarding thought. The fact that the Gemara does not simply say “for Krias Shema” may support Hagahos Maimoniyos. Yet Rashi on Berachos, s.v. “to gaze,” says, “if she is a married woman,” implying that looking at an unmarried woman is permitted. Why, then, did the Gemara not establish the case simply as Krias Shema opposite an unmarried woman? This indicates that even regarding an unmarried woman less than a tefach is prohibited, in accordance with Hagahos Maimoniyos. It is true that Even HaEzer 21:1 and 2 explains that even looking at an unmarried woman is at least Rabbinically prohibited. See Beis Shmuel, paragraph 2. This presently requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Regarding what</b> Rashi writes on Berachos 24a, s.v. “lower leg” and s.v. “ervah,” we wrote above, but further explanation is required. With a married woman, one who deliberately looks and derives pleasure violates a Torah prohibition even when the area is normally exposed, as is evident in Even HaEzer 21:1. Mere sight without deriving pleasure is permitted, as the Bach writes, and this is obvious. Regarding a normally covered area, however, even mere sight is prohibited. This is what Rashi means by specifying a married woman, and his words support the first interpretation. Understand this. See Beis Shmuel, Even HaEzer 169:32, Beer HaGolah there, paragraph 300, and what I recorded in my work on chalitzah.

<b>What</b> the Mechaber writes in seif 4, that one may not recite opposite the ervah of a non-Jew, implies that opposite the ervah of an animal it is permitted. So writes the Peri Chadash, and this is explicit in Berachos 25b from the verse, “their flesh is the flesh of donkeys,” showing that the nakedness of an animal creates no prohibition. It is permitted to think words of Torah opposite ervah, as stated in Shabbos 150a and Rashi there. I cited this in my remarks on Yoreh Deah. See there.
