<b> שיהא הוא מעורר השחר</b> - נכון מאד ליזהר לומר קודם כל ג' תפלות הודאת היה"ר המבואר בירושלמי פ' תפלת השחר דהיינו בשחרית מודה אני לפניך ד' או"א שהוצאתני מאפלה לאורה. ובמנחה מודה אני וכו' שכשם שזיכיתני לראות כשהחמה במזרח כך זכיתי לראותה במערב. בערבית יה"ר כו' כשם שהייתי באפילה והוצאתני לאורה כן תוציאני מאפילה לאורה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should awaken at dawn</b> — It is very proper to be careful, before each of the three prayers, to say the acknowledgments and “May it be Your will” formulas explained in Yerushalmi Tefillas HaShachar: for Shacharis—Modeh Ani before You, living and enduring Lord, who brought me from darkness to light; for Minchah—Modeh Ani … just as You privileged me to see [the sun] when it was in the east, so You privileged me to see it in the west; for Maariv—May it be Your will … just as I was in darkness and You brought me to light, so bring me from darkness to light.

<b>It is a great principle in the Torah …</b> — Whoever wishes to fulfill “I have set the Lord before me” properly should hasten to fulfill what we cite from Sefer HaChinuch; because of their preciousness many great authorities copied them into their works. Thus HaChinuch [in his introduction and in mitzvos 25, 26, 17, 18, 432, 387]: Six mitzvos whose obligation is constant never cease from a person even a single moment all his days; every moment he thinks of them he fulfills a positive command, and reward for mitzvos has no limit. They are:<br>(a) To believe there is one God in the world who brought all beings into being; by His will and desire is everything that is now, was, and will be forever; who took us from Egypt and gave us the Torah—this is the positive mitzvah of “I am the Lord your God who brought you out …”—meaning: know and believe there is a God who supervises the world, since “I brought you out of Egypt.”<br>(b) Not to believe in any power besides Him—“You shall have no other gods before Me”; even if one admits God rules all but imagines He entrusted the world’s governance to an angel or star—he admits idolatry and violates “no other gods”; rather he must believe the Holy One blessed be He Himself in His glory supervises all worlds and no creature can do anything against His will—hence He is called “God of gods.”<br>(c) To declare His unity—“Hear O Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One”: Hear, Israel, and know that the Lord who brings everything into being by His will, who supervises all worlds, is our God—the Lord is One without any partnership.<br>(d) To love God—“You shall love the Lord your God …” How does love come? Through Torah, as Sifrei explains: “You shall love”—I do not know how to love the Omnipresent; Scripture adds “These words … shall be upon your heart”—through them you recognize Him who spoke and the world came to be; through contemplating Torah one recognizes God’s greatness, incomparable and endless, and love settles necessarily in the heart. The mitzvah is that one should direct all ambition and thought toward love of God and assess in his heart that all worldly wealth, children, and honor are as nothing compared to His love; he should toil constantly to acquire Torah wisdom to gain knowledge of Him. One who fixes his mind on physical matters and vanities of the world not for Heaven’s sake but for pleasure and honor negates this positive mitzvah and its penalty is severe.<br>(e) To have fear of Heaven always before one so as not to sin—hence “The Lord your God you shall fear”; if sin presents itself one must rouse his spirit and consider at that moment that God supervises all human deeds even in darkness and repays evil accordingly—“Can a man hide in secret places …?” (Jeremiah 23:24).<br>(f) Not to stray after the imagination of the heart and what the eyes see—“You shall not stray after your hearts …”; the Sages said “after your hearts” is heresy, “after your eyes” is licentiousness; heresy includes all foreign thoughts contrary to Torah; licentiousness includes pursuing worldly lusts without intending any good purpose—not eating or acting for health and divine service but solely to increase bodily pleasure.<br>All this I gathered briefly from his pleasant wording—they are eye-salve; happy is one who fulfills them properly. See Chayei Adam, general rule 1, where he also elaborates.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should not be ashamed …</b> — See Mishnah Berurah citing Beis Yosef. Know: Beis Yosef speaks only of a mitzvah one performs for himself when people mock him—then he need not heed their mockery nor quarrel with them. But if he stands where heretics oppose Torah and wish to enact city ordinances by which they turn the public from God’s will, and he opens with peace and is not heard—Beis Yosef said nothing about such a case; it is a mitzvah to hate them, quarrel with them, and thwart their counsel in every way possible. David said, “Do I not hate Your enemies, O Lord? … I hate them with utmost hatred” (Psalms 139:21–22).

(שמ"ח) וכהן יוסיף פסוק וכל מנחת כהן כליל תהיה. ופרשת שלמים מן ואם זבח שלמים עד ואם מן הצאן ובפרשת צו מן וזאת תורת השלמים עד במדבר סיני והוא ג"כ פרשת תודה ואח"כ פרשת נסכים בפרשת שלח מן וידבר דכי תבואו עד אתכם. ופרשת חטאת בויקרא מן ונפש אחת תחטא עד ונסלח לו (ואם יש לו פנאי יקרא גם בפ' צו וזאת תורת החטאת עד באש תשרף) (שמ"ח) ופרשת אשם בפרשת צו מן וזאת תורת האשם עד אשם הוא. (ואם יש לו פנאי יקרא גם בויקרא) (שם). ופ' עולה ויורד ג"כ דהיא באה על שבועת ביטוי מן ונפש כי תחטא עד והיתה לכהן כמנחה (מ"א) ועיין בפמ"ג דמסתפק אם צריך לומר פרשת נסכים ג"פ לעולה ותודה ושלמים דחייב על כ"א בנסכים או דלענין קריאה די בפ"א וכן מצדד הארה"ח וטוב לומר כל אלו הפרשיות קודם התפלה ובבהכ"נ (ארה"ח בשם הזוהר) אך שלא יאחר עי"ז זמן ק"ש אפילו תפלה בצבור. וצריך להתודות קודם קריאת פרשת הקרבנות (ארה"ח בשם א"ר ופמ"ג) והוידוי יהיה במעומד. וכבר נדפס בימינו ספר עבודה תמה שמבואר בו כל ענייני אמירת הקרבנות בכל פרטיהם והעתיק ג"כ מן הרמב"ם סדר ההקרבה שהיה נצרך לזה וכבר מבואר בספרים גודל המצוה ומה טוב למי שנוהג בזה שילמוד מתחלה את ענינים האלו מן הגמרא או מספרי הרמב"ם כדי שיבין אח"כ מה שהוא אומר ובזה תחשב לו כאילו הקריב ממש קרבן וכן מה שאנו אומרין בכל יום אביי הוה מסדר וכו' וענייני עשיית הקטורת מצוה לראות להבין מה שהוא אומר ועיין מה שהאריך בעל השל"ה בהגהותיו על ספר יש נוחלין:

<b>And the portion Olah</b> — From the beginning of Vayikra through “Or if from the flock …” The portion Minchah—from “And when a soul offers …” through “you shall offer.” [If he has time he may also read in Parashas Tzav from “This is the law of the Minchah offering …” through “it is most holy.”] (Siman 48.) A kohen adds the verse “Every meal-offering of a kohen shall be wholly burnt.” The portion Shelamim—from “And if a sacrifice of peace-offering …” through “Or if of the flock …”; in Parashas Tzav from “This is the law of the Shelamim …” through “in the Sinai desert”—this is also Parashas Todah; afterward Parashas Nesachim in Parashas Shelach from “Speak to the children of Israel … through ‘I am the Lord your God.’” Parashas Chatas in Vayikra—from “If a soul sin …” through “and it shall be forgiven him” (if he has time also Parashas Tzav “This is the law of the Chatas … through shall be burned with fire”) (Siman 48). Parashas Asham in Parashas Tzav—from “This is the law of the Asham … through it is an asham” (if time, also in Vayikra) (there). Parashas Oleh ve’Yored as well—for a vain oath—from “If a soul sin …” through “it shall be to the kohen like a meal-offering” (Magen Avraham). See Pri Megadim—there is doubt whether one must say Parashas Nesachim three times for Olah, Todah, and Shelamim, since each requires libations, or once suffices for reading; Eshel Avraham inclines thus; it is good to say all these portions before prayer in synagogue (Eshel HaChayim citing Zohar)—provided one does not thereby delay Kerias Shema or even communal prayer. One must confess before reading Parashas HaKorbanos (Eshel HaChayim citing Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim); confession should be standing. Today Sefer Avodah Tamah is printed explaining every detail of saying the sacrifices and quoting Rambam’s order of offering needed for this; books elaborate the mitzvah’s greatness and how good it is for one who practices thus to learn these matters first from Gemara or Rambam so he later understands what he says—it is then considered as if he actually offered a sacrifice; likewise what we say daily “Abbaye would arrange …”; regarding preparing incense one should study to understand what one says—see Shelah’s glosses on Sefer Yesh Nohalin at length.
