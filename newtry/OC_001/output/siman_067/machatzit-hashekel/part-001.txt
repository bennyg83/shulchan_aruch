**** ENGLISH ****
And see Berakhot page 14 regarding yatziat Mitzrayim, and page 20 side 2 in Tosafot, etc. — it is implied, etc., that in them are two matters: acceptance of the mitzvot and the Exodus from Egypt. Meaning: regarding what we taught "a baal keri" — who in the time of the Gemara was forbidden in Keriat Shema and prayer — who contemplates Keriat Shema in his heart and does not bless before or after Keriat Shema, even through contemplation; and Tosafot wrote: per the one who says Keriat Shema is from the Torah, it fits: specifically regarding Keriat Shema contemplation was permitted for him, for it is from the Torah; unlike blessings, which are rabbinic. But per the one who says Keriat Shema is rabbinic — it is difficult. And they wrote: the reason is, since mentioning the Exodus from Egypt is certainly from the Torah — if so, he still did not say emet veyatziv and did not yet mention the Exodus from Egypt; if so, Keriat Shema is from the Torah on account of mentioning the Exodus from Egypt in parashat tzitzit. And again, the blessing emet veyatziv is rabbinic, since contemplation suffices — he contemplated Keriat Shema with parashat tzitzit. And they wrote, and these are their words: "Let him fix it that he say emet veyatziv and not Keriat Shema" — meaning the choice should be in his hand: either contemplate Keriat Shema and not emet veyatziv, or contemplate emet veyatziv and not Keriat Shema. And in the mishnah it is implied that specifically regarding Keriat Shema contemplation was permitted, and not regarding emet veyatziv — for it is preferable he say Keriat Shema, for in it is also acceptance of the kingdom of Heaven — until here. And Magen Avraham holds the same: parashat tzitzit, in which is also the yoke of mitzvot, is preferable to emet veyatziv, even though it lacks acceptance of the kingdom of Heaven as in parashat tzitzit. However, there is difficulty: behold, the words of Tosafot are the words of the Gemara, Arukh page 21 — for R. Eliezer said: if in doubt whether he recited Keriat Shema — he does not return and read; if in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv — he returns and says, because he holds Keriat Shema is rabbinic and emet veyatziv is on account of mentioning the Exodus from Egypt, which is from the Torah. And he challenges: if emet veyatziv is from the Torah, the mishnah of baal keri cited above is difficult — why does he not bless after Keriat Shema the blessing emet veyatziv through contemplation? And they answer: he already mentioned the Exodus from Egypt in contemplating Keriat Shema. And he challenges: let him say emet veyatziv through contemplation, and let him not say Keriat Shema. And they answer: Keriat Shema is preferable, for in it are two matters — until here is the Gemara; behold, the words of Tosafot are the words of the Gemara, and in their words there is only what Rashi and Tosafot explain in the mishnah — the words of the Gemara mentioned afterward. But Magen Avraham — I did not know why he needed proof from the words of Tosafot; he should have brought the words of the Gemara. And perhaps one could say: from the Gemara that plainly answers "Keriat Shema is preferable because of two" — one could distance the meaning of "two" besides the Exodus from Egypt, namely acceptance of the kingdom of Heaven and the yoke of mitzvot; and if so, parashat tzitzit alone, in which there is no advantage except one — namely the yoke of mitzvot — one could push that it does not have so much preference over emet veyatziv. Unlike Tosafot, who did not say Keriat Shema is preferable to emet veyatziv, but only "because of two" — acceptance of the kingdom of Heaven — and they did not mention the yoke of mitzvot; it implies that with one matter it suffices that there be preference over emet veyatziv; and if so, the same applies to parashat tzitzit, in which there is one matter — the yoke of mitzvot — preferable to emet veyatziv; since the proof is more explicit in Tosafot, Magen Avraham brought the words of Tosafot.

<b>אבל בדף כ"א בד"ה כו'</b> כצ"ל. שהקשו התוס' אהא דאר"י הנ"ל ספק אמר א"ו חוזר ואומר א"ו ע"כ מיירי שנסתפ' גם אם אמר ק"ש דאי ברור לו שאמר ק"ש א"כ כבר הזכיר יצ"מ וא"כ למה צריך לחזור ולומר אמת ויציב משום דהוי ספק דאורייתא. הא השתא לא הוי ספ' דאורייתא דהא כבר הזכיר יצ"מ בק"ש. וכיון שגם ק"ש מסתפק אי אמר' א"כ למה אמר שיאמר אמת ויציב טפי ה"ל לומר שיקרא ק"ש דאית ביה תרתי. ותי' התוס' דהא איכא שני ספיקות חדא שמא לא אמר לא ק"ש ולא אמת ויציב. שנית שמא לא אמר אלא אחד מהן. ובז' ליכ' להסתפ' שמא אמר אמת ויציב ולא ק"ש דמסתמא התפלל כדרך המתפללים תחלה ק"ש ואחר זה אמת ויציב אלא איפכא איכא לספוקי שמא קרא ק"ש ולא אמר אמת ויציב וא"כ עדיף טפי לו' אמת ויציב ולא ק"ש דאם אומר אמת ויציב ניהו דאם לא קרא ק"ש וגם לא אמר א"ו לא תיקן הכל במה שאומר עכשיו א"ו מ"מ שמא אמר וקרא ק"ש וכשיאמר עתה א"ו תיקן הכל שקרא ק"ש וגם אמת ויציב משא"כ אי יקרא ק"ש דבשום צד לא תיקן הכל דאי לא קרא ק"ש וגם לא אמר א"ו כשיקרא עתה ק"ש עדיין לא אמר א"ו ואי קרא ק"ש ולא אמר א"ו א"כ כשעתה שוב יקרא ק"ש לא תיקן כלל עכת"ד. ולפ"ז ה"ה היכי דנסתפק אי אמר א"ו וגם אי קרא פ' ציצית דג"כ ליכא להסתפק שאמר א"ו ולא אמר פ' ציצית אלא שמא לא אמר שניהם או שמא אמר פ' ציצית ולא אמר א"ו א"כ ג"כ עדיף לומר א"ו מלומר פ' ציצית מטעם שכתבו התוספות כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
But on page 21 s.v., etc. — thus it should read. Tosafot challenged on this that R. Eliezer said above: if in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv — he returns and says emet veyatziv; it must deal with one who is in doubt also whether he said Keriat Shema — for if it is clear to him that he said Keriat Shema, then he already mentioned the Exodus from Egypt; if so, why must he return and say emet veyatziv because of a Torah doubt? Now it is no longer a Torah doubt, for he already mentioned the Exodus from Egypt in Keriat Shema. And since he is also in doubt about Keriat Shema whether he said it — if so, why did he say he should say emet veyatziv rather than that he should read Keriat Shema, in which there are two matters? And Tosafot answered: there are two doubts — one, perhaps he said neither Keriat Shema nor emet veyatziv; second, perhaps he said only one of them. And it is more likely to doubt that he said emet veyatziv and not Keriat Shema, for presumably he prayed in the manner of those who pray — first Keriat Shema and afterward emet veyatziv; but the reverse — to doubt that he read Keriat Shema and did not say emet veyatziv — if so, it is preferable to say emet veyatziv and not Keriat Shema: for if he says emet veyatziv, behold if he did not read Keriat Shema and also did not say emet veyatziv, he has not fixed everything in what he now says — emet veyatziv; nevertheless, perhaps he said and read Keriat Shema, and when he now says emet veyatziv he fixes everything — that he read Keriat Shema and also emet veyatziv. Unlike if he reads Keriat Shema — in no way has he fixed everything: if he did not read Keriat Shema and also did not say emet veyatziv, when he now reads Keriat Shema he still has not said emet veyatziv; and if he read Keriat Shema and did not say emet veyatziv, then when he again reads Keriat Shema he has not fixed anything at all — until here. And accordingly, the same applies where he is in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv and also whether he read parashat tzitzit — for likewise one should not doubt that he said emet veyatziv and did not say parashat tzitzit, but rather that perhaps he said neither, or perhaps he said parashat tzitzit and did not say emet veyatziv; if so, here too it is preferable to say emet veyatziv than to say parashat tzitzit, for the reason Tosafot wrote as above.

**** ENGLISH ****
But per the explanation of Rosh, etc. — Tosafot wrote in the name of Rosh: the case is where it is clear to him that he said Shema and "and it shall be if you heed," and he is in doubt whether he said parashat tzitzit and also whether he said emet veyatziv. What he said — "if in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv" — is included in this, that he is also in doubt whether he said parashat tzitzit, since in both of them the Exodus from Egypt is mentioned; parashat tzitzit is included in the category of emet veyatziv. And if so, when R. Eliezer said in such a doubt that he says emet veyatziv — it means he should say parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv, for both are included in what is called emet veyatziv, as above. And furthermore, otherwise what did Rosh fix in his answer — the difficulty would still remain to say parashat tzitzit, in which are two matters: the yoke of mitzvot and the Exodus from Egypt; emet veyatziv — in truth he means he should say both: parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv. Behold, according to this, the doubt of Magen Avraham is explained in the Gemara — and they are the words of R. Eliezer in such a case, where he is in doubt about parashat tzitzit and about emet veyatziv — that he must say both.
