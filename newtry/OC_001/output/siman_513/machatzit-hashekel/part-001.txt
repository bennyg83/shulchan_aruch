**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 2) That is, etc.</b> For the prohibition of touching is from the Yerushalmi on that which is taught on folio 4 of Beitzah, etc.: but one may invert a vessel over it so that it not break. The Yerushalmi concludes: only that he not touch it. Beis Yosef brought it in siman 310, and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 25 of the laws of Shabbos, wrote, and this is his language: I do not know the reason, etc. And perhaps the egg will sway through the vessel (that is to say, because the egg is round it sways through the touching); end quote. And in Terumat HaDeshen he wrote that this reason is strained. And perhaps Terumat HaDeshen holds that one should not forbid touching for this reason, for it is something not intended, and also it is not a psik reisha. And even if it is a psik reisha, one can say that Terumat HaDeshen follows his view that regarding a rabbinic matter a psik reisha is permitted (as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 314, s.k. 5). And Terumat HaDeshen wrote, and this is his language: rather it appears to distinguish: there, since placing the vessel on the egg is for the need of the egg, which is a muktzeh item, therefore it is forbidden to touch it; but when the placing and use are for the need of something that is not muktzeh, when one touches the muktzeh through the placing it is permitted; end quote of Beis Yosef above in siman 314.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is implied that it discusses, etc.</b> That is to say, Or Zarua discusses when a permitted item rests on the lamp; therefore it is permitted to take a permitted item from the lamp even though thereby the lamp is swayed; nevertheless since he does not touch the lamp itself, only through a permitted item resting on it, it is only moving from the side, and therefore permitted. And with this Rama's words in siman 265 are well: certainly also in siman 265 he holds not like Terumat HaDeshen, and there is no distinction between touching for the need of a permitted matter or for the need of muktzeh. And when it sways, in every case it is forbidden. And that he wrote "and it is forbidden to touch a lit lamp," etc., "lest it sway," etc.: he stated a settled matter, for when the lamp is lit it is forbidden even if one touches it through a permitted item resting on it, for it is not merely moving from the side; but when it is not lit, the prohibition is not a settled matter, for sometimes it is permitted, namely when he does not touch except through a permitted item, that is, moving from the side. And even though in the Yerushalmi it wrote regarding "and he inverts a vessel over it" and "only that he not touch it with the vessel," as Beis Yosef wrote in siman 310 the wording of the Yerushalmi, and as is written here in seif 4: the reason is that Rama holds that swaying too is called moving muktzeh; nevertheless we hold in siman 311 that moving from the side is permitted, and that is specifically when the moving is for the need of a permitted matter; therefore Magen Avraham wrote that a permitted item rests [on it]. But in the Yerushalmi, which discusses inverting a vessel, which is for the need of the muktzeh egg: in such a case even moving from the side is forbidden.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 7) For appearance, etc.</b> even though it came to improve, etc.: for at the end of tractate Beitzah we learn: a woman who borrowed water and salt for her dough (brought above in Shulchan Aruch siman 397, seif 12): the dough is according to the feet of both of them, and they cannot carry the dough except to a place where both women can go, for all things are according to the feet of the owners; and the Gemara challenges: let the water and salt be nullified relative to the dough, and the dough should be according to the feet of the owner of the flour; and R' Ashi answers: because it is a davar sheyeish lo matirim and is not nullified. And Tosafos challenged: but a different kind, even a davar sheyeish lo matirim, is nullified, as is written in Yoreh Deah siman 102. And they answered: regarding techumin they were more stringent. Another answer: since water and salt come to improve the dough, it is called same kind in same kind (and Taz and Peri Chadash in Yoreh Deah wrote: specifically regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim it is called same kind in same kind where it came to improve, and not elsewhere). And therefore Magen Avraham wrote: from the fact that the Mechaber wrote "for appearance and taste," it is implied that we require both. But if it acts only for taste and is not recognizable in appearance, it is nullified (that is, this is not difficult, for we hold that whatever acts for taste is not nullified: Shach already wrote in siman 98, s.k. 28, that where the taste is not sensed it is nullified, and so Peri Chadash). Nevertheless one must say that the Mechaber does not hold like the second answer of Tosafos mentioned above, but even when it came to improve it is called a different kind, and therefore even a davar sheyeish lo matirim is nullified; and therefore we require both appearance and taste.
