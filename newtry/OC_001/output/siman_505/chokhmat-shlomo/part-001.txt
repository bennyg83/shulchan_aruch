<b>סעיף א'  </b>בהמה שהיא עומדת לאכילה ורוצה לחלוב אותה וכו'. נ"ב הנה ראיתי בספר מטה אפרים סי' ת"ר שכתב להקל בבהמות של עיירות דסתמא לחליבה ולא לעשות גבינה. ובאלף למטה הביא ראיה לזה מן הט"ז סי' תק"ה שכתב דספק דרבנן להקל ע"ש ולפענ"ד אינו נראה כן דהט"ז מיירי דידוע דעומדת רק לחליבה לאכול חלבה רק דהספק הוי בגוף הדין דפליגי בי' הפוסקים בזה שפיר י"ל דספק דרבנן להקל אף דהוי דבר שיל"מ מכל מקום בספיקא דפלוגתא לא אמרינן וכמ"ש בחיבורי בגדי יו"ט על מס' ביצה עודנו כת"י בשם הרש"ל ושו"ת שאג"א. אבל בנדון דידן דבסתם בהמות אם הוי ספק אם עומדת לחליבה או לחמאה וגבינות הוי ספק במעשה ובזה הו"ל ספק בדבר שיל"מ וקיי"ל לחומרא אף דהוי דרבנן ואף אם סתם הוי לחליבה מכל מקום כיון דיש מיעוט דהוי לגבינות א"כ הרי דעת הר"ן דלא אזלינן כ"ר בדבר שיל"מ ואף החולקים מכל מקום כאן הוי ס"ס דלמא לגבינות ואת"ל לחליבה דלמא כדעת האוסרים אף לחליבה וס"ס עדיף מרובא. גם י"ל דמצטרפין המיעוט לגבינות לדעת האוסרים ונעשה עכ"פ מחצה על מחצה ושוב הו"ל ספק בדבר שיל"מ כיון דהוי הספק גם על המעשה. גם י"ל כיון דאמרינן בש"ס דביצה דגזרינן אטו יו"ט אחר השבת ואף דקיי"ל הכנה דרבנן מכל מקום גזרו בזה גזירה לגזירה וא"כ ה"נ בעומדת לחליבה גזרינן אטו העומדת לגבינות דבשלמא בעומדת לאכילה בזה לא אתי לאחלופי דניכר מגופו דאין הדרך למכור ממנה החלב אבל בעומדת לחליבה אינו ניכר אם הוא לעשות גבינות או לאכול החלב לכך יש לגזור אטו הא. לכך העיקר כדעת האוסרים ואין להקל בזה ואתי שפיר ודו"ק: (שם בהג"ה) ואם מותר לומר לעכו"ם לחלוב בהמתו בשבת וכו'. נ"ב עיין **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1.</b> An animal that is designated for eating and he wishes to milk it, etc. Marginal note: Behold, I saw in the book Mateh Ephraim, siman 600, that he wrote to be lenient regarding animals of the towns, which are presumed for milking and not for making cheese. And in Elef LaMateh he brought a proof for this from the Taz, siman 505, who wrote that a rabbinic doubt is judged leniently; see there. And in my humble opinion it does not appear so, for the Taz is dealing where it is known that [the animal] is designated only for milking to eat its milk, and the doubt is only in the body of the law, about which the poskim dispute; regarding that one may well say that a rabbinic doubt is judged leniently, and even though it is something that has a permitted form, nevertheless regarding a doubt of dispute [among authorities] we do not say [to be stringent], as I wrote in my work Bigdei Yom Tov on tractate Beitzah, still in manuscript, in the name of the Maharshal and Responsa Shaagas Aryeh. But in our case, regarding ordinary animals, if there is a doubt whether [the animal] is designated for milking or for butter and cheeses, it is a doubt in the facts; and regarding this it is a doubt in something that has a permitted form, and we hold to be stringent even though it is rabbinic. And even if ordinarily it is for milking, nevertheless since there is a minority that is for cheeses, if so, behold the view of the Ran is that we do not follow the majority regarding something that has a permitted form; and even those who disagree, nevertheless here there is a double doubt: perhaps it is for cheeses, and even if you say it is for milking, perhaps [the law is] like the view of those who forbid even for milking; and a double doubt is stronger than a majority. One may also say that the minority [designated] for cheeses joins with the view of those who forbid, and it becomes at least half and half, and again it is a doubt in something that has a permitted form, since the doubt is also about the facts. One may also say: since we say in the Gemara of Beitzah that we decree on account of Yom Tov after Shabbos, and even though we hold that hachanah is rabbinic, nevertheless they decreed here a decree upon a decree; and if so, the same applies when [an animal is] designated for milking: we decree on account of [an animal] designated for cheeses. For granted, when designated for eating, one would not come to confuse them, for it is recognizable from its nature that it is not the way to sell milk from it; but when designated for milking it is not recognizable whether it is for making cheeses or for eating the milk, therefore one should decree on account of that. Therefore the main [ruling] is like the view of those who forbid, and one should not be lenient about this; and it fits well; examine carefully. (There in the gloss) And whether it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to milk his animal on Shabbos, etc. Marginal note: See in the Magen Avraham what is written in s.k. 3, that if it was milked on Shabbos it is forbidden on Sunday which is Yom Tov, for Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; see there. And it appears that the same applies conversely: if it was milked on Yom Tov that fell on Friday, it is forbidden on Shabbos; for just as Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov, so Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos, for we hold that they are two sanctities. And what is permitted for cooking, etc., is all by force of eiruv tavshilin, namely by force of the fact that according to the Torah the needs of Shabbos may be done on Yom Tov by virtue of "since guests may come and it is fit for him," etc. If so, that is fine for what is permitted that same day; but if it is forbidden on Yom Tov itself, "since guests may come" does not apply, and the heter of eiruvei tavshilin does not help for this; therefore it is forbidden even on Shabbos after Yom Tov. And likewise in siman 513 as well: wherever the poskim wrote that the egg is forbidden on Yom Tov after Shabbos, the same applies that it is forbidden on Shabbos after Yom Tov. And so I found exactly like my words in the book Eliyah Rabbah, siman 512, s.k. 15, in the name of the Rashba in the book Avodas HaKodesh, and this is his language there: A chick that was born and opened its eyes: there are those who permit, and the Geonim forbade, for it is muktzeh, and upon them we rely for prohibition; and therefore Yom Tov and Shabbos adjacent to it, whether before it or after it: what was born on this is forbidden on that, for the one does not prepare for its fellow; end quote. If so, it is explicit exactly according to our words. So it appears to me in my humble opinion correct and clear in this for the practical law; and examine carefully well. And what pertains to the law of Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos: see what I wrote in my work on Orach Chaim from the year 5616, in a responsum to the holy community of Lvov, folio 123; and it found its place in the laws of Shabbos, siman 252; see there; and examine carefully.
