<b>א) [סעיף א'] סדר תעניות שמתענים בא"י על הגשמים וכו'  </b>ואם יש לנהוג בזה"ז סדר תעניות וכסדר ששנינו במשנתינו. כפי מה ששמעתי מפי זקנים שכשהשנה מעוברת אין להתחיל התעניות כסדר המשנה ולעיקרא דמילתא קאמינא דשליחותייהו דקמאי קעבדינן. הלק"ט ח"א סי' ץ' י"א בהגה"ט. פה"א ח"ד דף ח' ע"א. ומיהו עיין ברכ"י או ה' מה שכתב להאריך בזה וכתב ששמע מזקני עה"ק ירושלים וחברון ת"ו דאירע בימיהם עצירת גשמים ונהגו ככל סדר שכתבו הטור והש"ע ובתחלה כתב לדחות זה משום דליכא סמוכים בזה"ז ואח"כ כתב שראה בתשו' מהר"ם בן חביב כ"י סי' ו' שכתב ג"כ דיש לנהוג בזה"ז בא"י סדר תעניות הכתוב במשנתינו וע"כ כתב ליישב וסיים והגם דמלתא כדנא א"י וחו"ל שוים כהדדי מ"מ בחו"ל לאו סדנא דארעא חד הוא דזמנין דמשכחת עצירת גשמים באיזו מדינה ויש כמה מדינות דאין שם עצירת גשמים כלל אלא מיעוטא דמיעוטא וכיון דלא שוו בשיעוריהן כל מקומות שבחו"ל השוו רבנן מדותיהם דאין ת"צ בחו"ל יעו"ש. וכ"כ בשו"ת נוב"י ח"א סי' ל"א שאין נוהגין במדינות אלו לעשות בעצירת גשמים י"ג תעניות כסדר המשנה רק גוזרים תענית א' או ב' ומרצים בתפלות ותחנונים יעו"ש. והב"ד השע"ת או' א' ועיין בתשו' נחפה בכסף ח"ב בסוף קונטריס פרי מפרי דף ל"ו ע"א בסופו שכתב וז"ל שמעתי מפי הרב הכולל כמהר"ר ירוחם ווילנא נר"ו שבצפת לא עשו אפי' באותם עשר תעניות חומרות אלו משום שאין בזה"ז סמוכים אפי' בא"י יעו"ש. וכ"כ בספר התקנות והמנהגים של ירושת"ו בדיני תעניות או' צ"ב דבזמן גדול הדור האחרון הרב מוהר"מ סוזין זצוק"ל אירע כזה ועלתה הסכמתו שלא לעשות כסדר מרן וכדעת הברכ"י עכ"ל ועיין לקמן סעי' ט':

<b>1) [Seif 1] The order of fasts observed in Eretz Yisrael because of the rains, etc. </b>Regarding whether the order of fasts should be practiced nowadays in the sequence taught in our Mishnah: According to what I heard from elders, when the year is a leap year, the fasts should not begin according to the order of the Mishnah. Regarding the essence of the matter, I say that we act as agents of the early authorities. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 90; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Pri HaAdamah, part 4, 8a. Nevertheless, see Birkei Yosef, os 5, who wrote at length concerning this. He wrote that he heard from the elders of the holy communities of Jerusalem and Chevron, may they be rebuilt and established, that in their days there was a drought, and they practiced the entire order written by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. At first he wrote to reject this because there are no ordained judges nowadays. He then wrote that he saw in the manuscript responsa of Maharam ben Chaviv, siman 6, that he likewise wrote that nowadays in Eretz Yisrael one should practice the order of fasts recorded in our Mishnah. He therefore wrote to reconcile the matter, and concluded: Although in a matter such as this Eretz Yisrael and the Diaspora are equal, nevertheless, in the Diaspora the nature of the land is not uniform. At times a drought is found in one country, while there are many countries where drought does not occur at all except very rarely. Since all places in the Diaspora are not equal in their required measures, the Rabbis standardized their measures, so that there is no communal fast in the Diaspora. See there. So too wrote Responsa Noda B'Yehuda, part 1, siman 31, that in these countries the practice is not to observe 13 fasts because of drought according to the order of the Mishnah. Rather, they decree one or two fasts and seek appeasement through prayers and supplications. See there. Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1, cited his words. See also Responsa Nechpeh BaKesef, part 2, at the end of the pamphlet Pri MiPri, 36a, at its conclusion, who wrote as follows: I heard from the comprehensive scholar, our teacher R' Yerucham Vilna, may his light shine, that in Tzfas they did not practice these stringencies even during those ten fasts, because nowadays there are no ordained judges even in Eretz Yisrael. See there. So too is written in Sefer HaTakanos VeHaMinhagim of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established, in the laws of fasts, os 92, that during the time of the last great authority of the generation, R' Moshe Suzin, of righteous and blessed memory, such an event occurred, and his conclusion was not to follow the order of the Mechaber, in accordance with the opinion of the Birkei Yosef. End quote. See below, seif 9.

<b>3) Ibid. </b>The 17th of Marcheshvan arrived and rain had not fallen, etc. When the 17th of Marcheshvan occurs on Monday, the consensus of the Rabbis of Jerusalem is not to begin fasting on that day, because the phrase 'the 17th arrived' means only after the 17th has passed. Pri HaAdamah, part 3, 21c; Mizbach Adamah, 8, citing Shulchan Melachim. Nevertheless, Birkei Yosef, os 1, wrote to prove that one should fast on that very day, and wrote that this is the ruling we adopt. However, in Sefer Birchos Mayim on this siman, 42, he decided through proofs from the manuscript of the gaon, the Rif, from the author of Orach Chaim, and from the actual conduct of his father, the author of Pri HaAdamah, at a gathering of all the Rabbis of the holy city, that they should not begin fasting on that day. He also answered the words of the aforementioned Birkei Yosef. See there. Ikarei HaDat, siman 29, os 7, cited his words.

<b>ד) בשנת  </b>התקכ"ד הגיע יום י"ז חשון ביום ב' וכבר אמרנו דהסכמת רבני ירושלים הוא עד שיעבור יום י"ז ולא ירדו אז גוזרים ת"צ בה"ב וכיון שאם היו ממתינים עד יום כ"ד חשון היה בא יום ג' של התענית ביום ר"מ והעיקר כי היה דוחק וצער מחמת המים כי עלה השער מאד והסכימו לסיבא זו באותה שנה להתחיל היחידים ביום י"ז חשון עצמו שהיה יום ב' מזבח אדמה על סי' זה דף ח' ע"ג וברכות מים דף מ"ב ע"ג. ועיין לקמן או' מ':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>4) In the year </b>5524, the 17th of Marcheshvan occurred on Monday. We have already stated that the consensus of the Rabbis of Jerusalem is to wait until the 17th has passed, and if rain has not fallen they decree a communal fast on Monday, Thursday, and Monday. Since, had they waited until the 24th of Marcheshvan, the third day of the fast would have occurred on the day of Rosh Chodesh, and primarily because there was great pressure and distress concerning the water, for its price had risen greatly, they agreed for this reason that in that year the individuals should begin on the 17th of Marcheshvan itself, which was Monday. Mizbach Adamah on this siman, 8c; Birchos Mayim, 42c. See below, os 40.

<b>5) In the year </b>5521, the 17th of Marcheshvan occurred on Monday, and the individuals did not begin until the 24th. The third day of the fast would then have occurred after Rosh Chodesh Kislev. Therefore, the individuals fasted only on Monday and Thursday, and on Rosh Chodesh Kislev they decreed a Monday, Thursday, Monday series. Pri HaAdamah, part 4, 7d. Birchos Mayim, ibid., wrote that the great authorities of the generation agreed to this. At that time the wondrous scholar R' Yaakov Abulafia, of blessed memory, was present there and agreed with them. He disputed Birkei Yosef, os 2, who wrote that they did not act properly. See there.

<b>ח) שם.  </b>וכל התלמידים ראויים לכך. ומשמע דאין חייבין להתענות. והקשה הרב"י דהא בגמ' משמע דחייבים להתענות ולק"מ דהתם קאי את"ח ששואלין אותו דבר הלכה בתלמודו ואומרו אבל אם לא הגיע לכלל כך אין מחויב להתענות אלא שאם רצה מתענה ולית ביה משום יוהרא. מ"א סק"א. א"א או' א' וכ"פ מ"ב או' ג'. ואף בזה"ז דלית ת"ח מ"מ מתענין. מש"ז או' א' ונראה דדינם כתלמידים דאין חייבין ואם רצו מתענין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>8) Ibid. </b>And all students are fit for this. This implies that they are not obligated to fast. The Beit Yosef asked that the Gemara implies that they are obligated to fast. This is not difficult at all, for there it refers to a Torah scholar who, when asked a matter of halacha concerning his studies, can answer it. However, one who has not reached that level is not obligated to fast; rather, if he wishes he may fast, and there is no concern of haughtiness. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eshel Avraham, os 1. So too ruled Mishna Berurah, os 3. Even nowadays, when there are no Torah scholars of that standard, they nevertheless fast. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1. It appears that their law is like that of students, who are not obligated, but may fast if they wish.

<b>12) [Seif 2] Rosh Chodesh Kislev arrived and rain had not fallen, the beit din decrees, etc. </b>Sefer Shulchan Melachim wrote that if the beit din did not decree fasts for rain, once Rosh Chodesh Kislev has passed the decree automatically takes effect and they are obligated to fast, for these fasts were already decreed by the Sages of the Mishnah. These are his words. This is incorrect, for the wording of the Mishnah and the poskim, who state that the beit din decrees them, refutes his view. Likewise, the statement of Shmuel in the Gemara, 'There is no communal fast in Babylonia,' breaks his argument. I also found in the manuscript responsa of the pious scholar R' Yosef Molcho that is in my possession, siman 21, that he wrote that if they were not decreed, they do not take effect. He brought a proof as I wrote, and he further wrote at length to prove that the beit din must decree them. I showed this responsum to the Rabbis of our time, and they rejoiced at his words. Birkei Yosef, os 3.
