<b>א) [סעיף א'] אין לרוץ בשבת.  </b>דכתיב אם תשיב משבת רגלך ודרשו חז"ל שלא יהא הילוכך של שבת כהילוכך של חול. טור. והגם דהש"ס מפיק ליה מעשות דרכיך י"ל דהטור נקט רישא דקרא וכוונתו לסוף המקרא. וכ"כ התו"ש או' א' יעו"ש. ואף פסיעות קצרות פחות מאמה כל שהוא בריצה אסור בשבת א"א או' א':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] One may not run on Shabbos.</b> It is written, 'If you restrain your foot because of Shabbos,' and our Sages expounded that one's manner of walking on Shabbos should not resemble his manner of walking during the week. Tur. Although the Talmud derives this from the words, 'pursuing your affairs,' one may say that Tur quotes the beginning of the verse while intending its conclusion. Tosefes Shabbos 1 writes likewise; see there. Even if one's strides are shorter than an amah, running is forbidden on Shabbos. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1.

<b>ב) שם. אין לרוץ בשבת.  </b>ואף בחול אסור פסיעה גסה דנוטלת אחת מת"ק ממאור עיניו. מ"א סק"א. וכתב מהרש"א בח"א דף קי"ג א"כ מה בין שבת לחול. ותירץ דבחול ליכא איסורא אלא הזיקא לעינים ונוסף בשבת דאיכא איסורא. גם י"ל נ"מ בסומא דאפ"ה בשבת אסור. מחה"ש. מיהו עיין בתו"ש או' ג' שכתב דאיכא מאן דפליג בהא דפסיעה גסה נוטלת וכו' וק"ל כוותייהו ורק בשבת הוא דאסור יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) There: One may not run on Shabbos.</b> Even during the week one may not take a large stride, for it removes one five-hundredth of one's eyesight. Magen Avraham 1. Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggados, page 113, asks: If so, what difference exists between Shabbos and weekdays? He answers that during the week there is no prohibition, only harm to the eyes; on Shabbos there is an additional prohibition. Another practical difference concerns a blind person, for whom it is nevertheless forbidden on Shabbos. Machatzis HaShekel. See Tosefes Shabbos 3, however, who writes that some dispute the assertion that a large stride diminishes eyesight. The halachah follows them leniently, and the prohibition applies only on Shabbos; see there.

<b>ה) שם בהגה. ואסור לפסוע יותר מאמה וכו'  </b>פי' שיהא חצי אמה בין רגל לרגל וכף הרגל הוא ג"כ חצי אמה כמ"ש סי' שצ"ז סעי' ב' בשם מהרי"ו. מ"א סק"ב. ור"ל דשם כתב דהאמה היינו אורך ב' מנעלים על אחד מקום מצב הרגל ומנעל הוי בין רגל לרגל יעו"ש וא"כ מ"ש כאן דכף הרגל הוא חצי אמה היינו עם המנעל. ובאגודה כתב בשם התו' פ"ק דתענית שלא יעקור רגלו השנית קודם שיניח הראשונה, מ"א שם, וכ"כ הראב"ן סי' ש"ס והביאו א"ר או' ד':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) There, in the gloss: It is forbidden to take a stride longer than an amah, etc.</b> This means that there should be one-half amah between one foot and the other, while the foot itself is also one-half amah, as stated in siman 397:2 in Mahari Weil's name. Magen Avraham 2. The passage there explains that an amah is the length of two shoes: one shoe occupies the place where the foot stands, and another shoe's length is between the feet; see there. Accordingly, the statement here that the foot is one-half amah includes the shoe. Agudah writes in Tosafos' name, first chapter of Taanis, that one should not lift his second foot before placing down the first. Magen Avraham, ibid. Raavan 360 writes likewise, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah 4.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>12) There: Likewise, to see anything, etc.</b> This means that one may also run or leap in order to see something from which people derive pleasure. Bach. Olas Shabbos 3 explains likewise. Taz 1, however, writes that one may not run in order to see it; the ruling permits only the viewing itself, meaning that one may see on Shabbos something he enjoys. Magen Avraham 4 explains similarly: Although one may not look at images, as stated in siman 307:15, he may look if the viewing gives him pleasure. Eliyah Rabbah 6, however, explains like Bach. Chemed Moshe 2 and Maamar Mordechai write likewise, and this appears to be the view of the later authorities.

<b>יג) שם הגה. וכן מותר לטייל וכו'  </b>פי' כוונתו להתעמל ולהתחמם בכך אבל אסור לרוץ כדי להתחמם אם עושה לרפואה. מ"א סק"ה. מיהו הא"ר או' ו' כתב מלבוש משמע אף שהולך ואינו רץ אם כוונתו להתעמל אסור וכתב דנראין דברי הלבוש דכ"מ מתוספתא וכ"מ מרמ"א יעו"ש אמנם הא"א או' ה' כתב דאין מוכרח כדברי הלבוש וי"ל כדברי המ"א יעו"ש וכן הפתה"ד או' ג' כתב להשיג על דברי הא"ר הנ"ז והסכים לדברי המ"א יעו"ש. וכ"נ דעת התו"ש או' ה':

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>14) There, in the gloss: It is likewise permitted to stroll, etc.</b> A responsum at the end of Chazeh HaTenufah states that strolling on Shabbos in an alley with an eruv without a head covering or outer cloak depends upon local custom. If the custom is not to stroll this way, it is a praiseworthy custom and should not be treated leniently, as proven by Pesachim 50b, chapter Makom Shenahagu. Beis Yosef. In my humble circumstances, these responsa reached me in manuscript. Chazeh HaTenufah is an abridgment of Rosh's responsa. At its end is an abridgment of responsa of Rosh that were not printed. This responsum is number 55, where the text contains a variation and addition: 'If this is their custom, it is a praiseworthy custom and should not be treated leniently, for it is a proper custom to make one's Shabbos manner of wrapping different from that of weekdays. The custom of our ancestors is Torah. Proof comes from the chapter Makom Shenahagu.' Machazik Berachah 2.

Hagahos Maharshal, however, cites a responsum of Maharam that rules like Rav Huna, permitting the shoe on the wounded foot. Bach therefore writes that because of the dispute among great authorities, one should be stringent and not go out wearing a single shoe, whether on the wounded foot or the unwounded foot. <b>44) Regarding going out on Shabbos</b> in the nail-studded sandal called kabkab in Arabic, although it is not the sandal mentioned in the chapter Bameh Isha, it should nevertheless be forbidden because it is a burden or because it may slip off and he may come to carry it. It can be remedied by constructing it so that it does not slip from his foot, like the sandals in Europe whose upper portion is covered. They then constitute shoes, do not slip off, and are not a burden. See Radvaz 3, Furth edition, siman 11, who rules that a wooden shoe not covered with leather, called sueikos - kabkab in Arabic - may not be worn outside on Shabbos even into an alley without an eruv. The leather placed over its top often slips off; he can no longer wear it, and there is a substantial concern that he will carry it in his hand. Ikrei HaDat 15:3. See Birkei Yosef 10, however, who writes in Maharikash's name that some permit it. Machazik Berachah 7 cites several poskim who prohibit it like Radvaz; see there. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>48) There: Whether it has an eye, etc.</b> Beis Yosef contains a scribal error here, but Shulchan Aruch is written correctly. Magen Avraham 15. See Taz 5 for his emendation and resolution of Beis Yosef's words. Regarding Bach's statement that no opinion holds a man liable for an eyed needle, Magen Avraham writes that this is not compelled. Taz similarly writes that Bach needlessly described Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch here as the words of an erring student. Everything is coherent; only Beis Yosef requires emendation. The general principle is that everything depends upon contemporary practice. If it is ordinarily carried this way during the week and is not an ornament, this is its normal mode of carrying and he is liable. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, he is exempt, but the act is forbidden because it is not an ornament. The authorities disagree only about which kind of needle was ordinarily carried in Talmudic times. If most people do not carry it this way, even though people in one locale do, they are exempt because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else, as the Gemara, page 52, states.
