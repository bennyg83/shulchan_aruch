(א) אין לרוץ בשבת - שנאמר וכבדתו מעשות דרכיך ודרשו שלא יהא הילוכך בשבת כהילוכך בחול שדרך האדם למהר ולרוץ אחר עסקו ואף בחול אין לפסוע פסיעה גסה דנוטלת אחד מת"ק ממאור עיניו אלא דבשבת איכא נמי איסורא משום מעשות דרכיך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) One may not run on Shabbos.</b> As it is stated, 'And you shall honor it by refraining from pursuing your affairs.' The Sages expounded that your manner of walking on Shabbos should not be like your manner of walking on a weekday, when a person normally hurries and runs after his business. Even on a weekday one should not take a broad stride, for it diminishes one five-hundredth of his eyesight; on Shabbos there is the additional prohibition derived from 'pursuing your affairs.'

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>33) With a pierced needle he is liable.</b> This view holds that a man's law is like a woman's below in siman 303:9: at times the normal manner of carrying a pierced needle is to stick it in one's garment. The ruling below in se'if 12 that even a craftsman is exempt is explained there in Mishnah Berurah. For an unpierced needle he is exempt, because it is normally carried by hand, not stuck in a garment. The opposite view holds that a man does not normally carry a pierced needle in his garment, since it is demeaning to be called a tailor, whereas he may sometimes stick an unpierced needle into his garment on a weekday after finding it in the street, when he needs it to pick his teeth or the like. Magen Avraham concludes that everything depends on contemporary practice: if people normally carry it this way on weekdays and it is not an ornament, one is liable; if they do not, he is exempt but it remains forbidden. If most people do not carry it this way, the practice of one locality does not create liability, because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else.

<b>109) One may go out.</b> Only if he tied the coin to the sole of his foot before Shabbos with an insignificant rag that is subordinate to it. If he used a significant material, he may not go out on Shabbos, as above in se'if 22.
