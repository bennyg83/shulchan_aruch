**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Having an eye.</b> Beis Yosef contains a scribal error here, but Shulchan Aruch is written correctly. Bach writes that no opinion holds a man liable for an eyed needle; see there. This is not compelled. The general principle is that everything depends upon contemporary practice. If people ordinarily go out with it this way during the week and it is not an ornament, this is its normal mode of carrying and he is liable. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, he is exempt, but the act is forbidden because it is not an ornament. The authorities disagree only about which kind of needle was ordinarily carried in Talmudic times. If most people do not carry it this way, even though people of another locale do, they are exempt because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else, as the Gemara, page 92, states.

Even Rabbi Eliezer, who holds that weapons are ornaments, says only that one is not liable to a sin-offering; a prohibition remains. Tosafos 62 likewise implies that initially carrying an ornament in one's hand is forbidden because of appearances. Raavan 349 writes likewise. The Yerushalmi's statement that Rabban Gamliel went with a key 'in his hand' cannot mean literally in his hand, for then the concern that he may remove and show it would be inapplicable. See Beis Yosef. It must mean, as Ran writes, that it was inserted on his finger like a ring. This proves that carrying an ornament in one's hand is forbidden; see Beis Yosef. Furthermore, it is made both as an ornament and for use, as stated in se'if katan 11. Where there is an eruv, however, it appears that an honored person may go out with it, since he carries it only for honor. An ordinary person who does not carry it for honor may not do so even where there is an eruv, because this demeans Shabbos, as stated in siman 522. It appears that Tur's ruling, 'One may not go out in a chair, etc.; if the public needs him, it is permitted,' likewise concerns a place with an eruv. This resolves Beis Yosef's question. Rambam writes that if an amputee went out with his prosthetic leg or wooden ankatmin, he is exempt.

<b>כובע</b>. ז"ל הרמב"ם פכ"ב כובע שיש לו שפה מקפת שהוא עושה צל כמו אוהל על לבושו מותר ללבשו ואם הוציא מן הבגד סביב לראשו או כנגד פניו כמו אוהל והיה מהודק על ראשו והיה השפה שהוציא' קשה ביותר כמו גג אסור מפני שהוא עושה אוהל עראי עכ"ל וכתב הכל בו דוקא אם הוא קשה ואינו נכפף ובהג"מ כתב וכובעים שלנו שרי דמשופעים והולכים כלפי מטה ומשמע בסה"ת דזהו מה שקורין בריט"ל בפולין עיין סי' שט"ו ס"ח וסי"א ויש נמנעים מלתת הטלית על ראשם כדי שלא יהיה הטלית מכאן ומכאן כמו דפנות כמ"ש סי' שט"ו וצ"ע לדידהו דכל הפוסקים שכתבו לאיסורא וכן בגמרא אטו מיירי דוקא כשיש לו טלית על ראשו וע"ק דה"ל לשנויי הכי בגמרא הא דאית ליה טלית בראשו הא דלית ליה אע"כ כמ"ש הרא"ש פרק המביא דמוכח בפרק ב' דעירובין כיון שעיקר כונתו לעשות אוהל להיות לצל אסור אפי' בלא דפנות והכי דייק לישנא דרמב"ם, ולכן נ"ל שאם הכובע קשה ביותר יניחנו בשיפוע כמש"ל והב"ח כתב היכא דנהוג היתר כרש"י נהוג וכ"כ במהרי"ל להתיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A hat.</b> Rambam, chapter 22, writes: 'A hat with a surrounding brim that shades like a tent over the wearer may be worn. If a projection extends from the garment around his head or opposite his face like a tent, is secured upon his head, and the projecting brim is extremely stiff like a roof, it is forbidden because he forms a temporary tent.' Kol Bo writes that this applies specifically when it is stiff and does not bend. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that our hats are permitted because they slope downward. Sefer HaTerumah implies that this is what is called a britel in Poland. See siman 315:8 and 11. Some refrain from placing a tallis over their head lest the tallis on either side form walls, as stated in siman 315. This requires examination according to them: Did all the poskim who prohibit the hat, and the Gemara itself, discuss specifically a person with a tallis over his head? Moreover, the Gemara should have answered by distinguishing between one who has a tallis over his head and one who does not. Rather, as Rosh writes in the chapter HaMeivi based upon Eruvin chapter 2, since his primary intent is to form a shelter for shade, it is forbidden even without walls. Rambam's wording is precise accordingly. It therefore appears to me that if the hat is extremely stiff, he should wear it at a slope, as stated above. Bach writes that where the practice follows Rashi leniently, it may continue. Maharil likewise permits it.

<b>זוג זוג</b>. הקשה בתוי"ט כיון דקי"ל דשבת לאו זמן תפילין א"כ יכניס ב' זוגות בבת אחת דלא עבר על בל תוסיף כיון דלאו זמניה הוא כדאית' בגמ' עכ"ל, וצ"ל כיון דאנן לא בקיאין לכוין המקום כמ"ש סי' ל"ד ואם יניח שלא במקומו יהי' משאוי לכן לא יניח אלא זוג א' ועמ"ש ססי' תרנ"א ורש"א כ' דאפי' למ"ד שבת לאו זמן תפילין הוא אסור ללבוש יותר מזוג א' דלא שרי ליה יותר ממלבושו בחול כדאי' בגמ' וכ"כ הר"ן והא דאוקימנ' כמ"ד שבת זמן תפילין הוא משום דמתני' ר"מ הוא דס"ל כן ע"ש, אבל אשה אסור' להכניסה ואף על גב דק"ל דמותר לצאת בשק מפני שהרועים רגילים לצאת בהם מקרי מלבוש לכל אדם  שאני נשים שהן עם בפ"ע (שבת דף ס"ב) ולכן נ"ל שאם יצאה בטלי' המצוייצ' חייב' חטאת אבל דבר שהוא מלבוש גמור לאיש מותרת אשה לצאת בו ועבי"ד סי' קפ"ב משא"כ טבעת לא מקרי מלבוש גמור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Pair by pair.</b> Tosafos Yom Tov asks: Since we rule that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, why not bring in two pairs at once? He does not violate adding to a mitzvah when it is not the mitzvah's time, as stated in the Gemara. One must answer that since we are not expert in positioning tefillin, as stated in siman 34, a pair placed outside its proper location would be a burden. He should therefore wear only one pair. See what I wrote at the end of siman 651. Maharsha writes that even according to the view that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, one may not wear more than one pair, since the Sages permitted only what he ordinarily wears during the week, as stated in the Gemara. Ran writes likewise. The Gemara establishes the Mishnah according to the view that Shabbos is a time for tefillin because the Mishnah follows Rabbi Meir, who holds that view; see there. A woman, however, may not bring them in by wearing them. Although we rule that one may go out wearing a sack because shepherds ordinarily do so and it is therefore considered clothing for everyone, women are a separate people for this purpose. Shabbos 62. It therefore appears to me that a woman who goes out wearing a fringed tallis is liable to a sin-offering. A woman may, however, go out wearing something that is fully considered a man's garment. See Yoreh Deah 182. A ring, by contrast, is not considered a complete garment.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to move them.</b> Mordechai writes: 'A person may dry himself with ten towels but may not bring them in his hand. It is nevertheless preferable to dry oneself with something whose retained water does not concern him.
