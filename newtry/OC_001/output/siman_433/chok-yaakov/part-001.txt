<b>צריך שתהיה לאור הנר. </b> ובליל י"ד ואף אם בדק יום י"ג לאור הנר צריך לחזור ולבדוק ליל י"ד כתקנת חכמים שאור הנר יפה לבדיקה ובני אדם מצויין בבתיהן כמבואר בב"י בשם הפוסקים ראשונים ובת"ה סימן קל"ג וכן הסכמת האחרונים אכן אם בדק ליל י"ג ונזהר היטב שלא להכניס עוד חמץ אין זה זריז ונפסד וא"צ לבדוק עוד כן מבואר במשמעות דברי הפוסקים ראשונים והמ"א ס"ס זה ובע"ש ר"ס תל"א וסי' זה אך הלבוש והב"ח הפריז על המדה דאף אם בדק ליל י"ג צריך לחזור ולבדוק הוציא כן מדברי המרדכי בפ"ק דפסחים בשם ירושלמי. באמת שאין דבריו מוכרחים דמיירי הירו' מליל י"ג ואדרבה הע"ש ר"ס תל"א מוכיח מתוך הירושלמי להיפך. ועוד דהמרדכי עצמו מסיק מיהו בתלמוד שלנו וכו' הרי להדיא המרדכי עצמו כ' דתלמוד שלנו חולק אירושלמי וכלל בידינו בכ"מ הלכתא כתלמודא דידן נגד הירושלמי ואף שב"ח מדחיק להשוות בזה תלמוד שלנו עם הירושלמי. מכל מקום נראה לי מוכח מכמה סוגיות דמהני בדיקה בליל י"ג חדא מהא דקאמר הש"ס דף ד' ע"ב אבעיא להו המשכי' בית לחבירו בחזקת בדוק ועל כרחך צריך לומר דבי"ג איירי דבי"ד על המשכיר לבדוק וא"כ ל"ל למימר בחזקת בדוק וכן כתבו שם תוס' להדיא ושאר מפרשים (ועיין בר"ן שכ' שיש פי' אחר בזה) ואי אמרת דמי שבדק בליל י"ג צריך לחזור ולבדוק מה מבעי ליה אי הוי כמקח טעות הלא בלא"ה צריך לחזור ולבדוק. ותו מהא דקאמר הש"ס שם דף ט' ע"ב אמר אביי ל"ק הא בי"ד והא בי"ג ואם איתא הא לא מהני בדיקת י"ג כדעת הירושלמי שלא לחלק בין בדיקה לבדיקה (דודאי אין סברא כלל לומר דמיירי ביוצא לדרך דוקא דמותר לבדוק קודם) אלא ודאי דהיכי דבדק ליל י"ג לאור הנר כדינו יצא ושוב א"צ לבדוק ואם רוצה להחמיר על עצמו עכ"פ לא יברך להכניס עצמו לחשש ספק ברכה אך יחזיר ומשתמש בתוכה חמץ ואז יוכל לחזור ולברך ולבדוק כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It must be by the light of a lamp.</b> And this means on the night of the fourteenth. Even if he checked on the day of the thirteenth by the light of a lamp, he must go back and check on the night of the fourteenth, in accordance with the enactment of the Sages, for lamplight is effective for checking and people are found in their homes, as is explained in the Beit Yosef in the name of the early poskim and in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 133, and this is also the consensus of the later authorities. However, if he checked on the night of the thirteenth and was very careful not to bring in any more chametz, he is not one who is diligent to his own loss, and he need not check again. This is clear from the implication of the early poskim, from the Magen Avraham at the end of this siman, and from the Olas Shabbos at the beginning of siman 431 and in this siman. But the Levush and the Bach went too far, saying that even if he checked on the night of the thirteenth he must go back and check. He derived this from the Mordechai in the first chapter of Pesachim in the name of the Yerushalmi. In truth, his words are not compelling, for the Yerushalmi is speaking of the night of the thirteenth; on the contrary, the Olas Shabbos at the beginning of siman 431 proves the opposite from the Yerushalmi. Furthermore, the Mordechai himself concludes, however, in our Talmud, etc.; thus the Mordechai himself explicitly wrote that our Talmud disagrees with the Yerushalmi, and we have a rule everywhere that the halacha follows our Talmud against the Yerushalmi. Although the Bach presses to reconcile our Talmud with the Yerushalmi, nevertheless it appears to me proven from several passages that checking on the night of the thirteenth is effective. First, from what the Gemara says on 4b: they asked, if one rents a house to his fellow with the presumption that it has been checked. Perforce this must be speaking on the thirteenth, for on the fourteenth the obligation to check is on the landlord. If so, why say with the presumption that it has been checked? Tosafot there explicitly wrote this, as did the other commentators, though see the Ran, who wrote that there is another explanation of this. Now, if you say that one who checked on the night of the thirteenth must check again, what was the question whether this is a mistaken transaction? In any event he must check again. Further, from what the Gemara says there on 9b: Abaye said, there is no difficulty, this is on the fourteenth and this is on the thirteenth. If so, according to the Yerushalmi's view that one does not distinguish between one checking and another, checking on the thirteenth would not be effective. There is certainly no basis to say that it speaks specifically of one leaving on a journey, who is permitted to check earlier. Rather, certainly, where he checked properly by lamplight on the night of the thirteenth, he has fulfilled the obligation and need not check again. If he wishes to be stringent upon himself, he should in any case not recite a blessing, so as not to enter a concern of a doubtful blessing. Rather, he should again use chametz inside it, and then he can go back, recite a blessing, and check. So it appears to me.

<b>ואכסדרה. </b> וכ' המ"א פי' הבנויה ע"ג עמודים ואין לה כותל עכ"ל והיינו אכסדרה רומייתא שבש"ס אבל סתם אכסדרה הוא שיש לה ג' דפנות כמבואר בש"ס דב"ב דף י"א ע"ב ובתוס' שם ד"ה הכא ובפ' הקומץ (מנחות דף ל"ג) ובי"ד הל' מזוזה סי' רפ"ו וא"כ הכא דנקט סתם אכסדרה מיירי ג"כ כה"ג ויש לה ג"כ אור הרבה כיון שדופן רביעי פתוחה לגמרי ולא ידעתי מאין יצא לו פי' אחר על אכסדרה שבכאן וכ"ה להדיא בריא"ז בש"ג הפי' של אכסדרא כמ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And an achsadra.</b> The Magen Avraham wrote that this means one built upon pillars and having no wall, end quote. That is the Roman achsadra mentioned in the Talmud. But an ordinary achsadra has three walls, as is explained in the Gemara in Bava Basra 11b, in Tosafot there, s.v. hacha, in the chapter HaKometz in Menachos 33, and in Yoreh De'ah, Hilchos Mezuzah, siman 286. If so, here, where it mentions an achsadra without qualification, it is also speaking of such a case. It too has much light, since the fourth side is entirely open. I do not know from where he derived another explanation of the achsadra here. So too it is explicit in Riaz in Shiltei HaGibborim, in the explanation of achsadra, as I wrote.

<b>וה"ה כנגד ארובה שבחדר. </b> ארובה מקרי מה שבאמצע גג וכנגד חלון שבכותל הובא במ"א שני דעות אי מותר לבדוק כנגדה לאורה או לא ול"נ עיקר דמותר לבדוק לאורה כמו בארובה וקצת ראיה לזה מהא דמפלפל הש"ס על הא דקתני מתני' ולא לאור החמה היכי דמי ולא מוקי בפשיטות דמיירי כנגד החלון שבחדר אלא ודאי דנבדקה לאורה כמו בארובה וכ' המ"א וז"ל ונ"ל דחלון שיש בו מחיצות של זכוכית לכ"ע אסור לבדוק כנגדה עכ"ל והא דלא מוקי הש"ס בהכי צ"ל דזהו מילתא דפשיטא ומה קמ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The same applies opposite a skylight in a room.</b> A skylight refers to an opening in the middle of the roof. Regarding opposite a window in the wall, the Magen Avraham cited two opinions whether one may check opposite it by its light or not. It appears to me that the primary view is that one may check by its light, as by a skylight. There is some proof to this from the Gemara's discussion of what the Mishnah taught, not by sunlight, where it asks, what is the case? It does not simply establish it as speaking opposite a window in a room. Rather, certainly, it was checked by its light, as by a skylight. The Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that if the window has glass partitions, all agree that it is forbidden to check opposite it. End quote. That the Gemara did not establish it this way must be because this is obvious, and what novelty would it be teaching us?

<b>לאור האבוקה. </b> ואפי' בדיעבד לא מהני בדיקה בזה משא"כ באינך נרות בדיעבד מהני הבדיקה אחרונים ושעת הדחק שאין לו נר של שעוה יבדוק לכתחלה בנר אחרינא דשעת הדחק כדיעבד דמי מ"מ אם אפשר לו בנר של שמן יבדוק דהוא ג"כ נכון אפי' לכתחלה לדעת כמה פוסקים כמ"ש הרא"ש והכלבו ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>By the light of a torch.</b> Even after the fact, such a checking is not effective. This is unlike the other lights, where after the fact the checking is effective, as the later authorities wrote. In pressing circumstances, when he has no wax candle, he may initially check with another lamp, for pressing circumstances are treated like after the fact. Nevertheless, if it is possible for him to use an oil lamp, he should check with that, for it too is proper even initially according to several poskim, as the Rosh and the Kol Bo wrote. See there.

<b>כל חדרי הבית. </b> כגון חדרי המשכנות וחדרי כבושים וגבינות ומרתפות ובית העצים וגחלים ותחת הספסלים ואוצרות יין ושכר שמסתפקין מהם צריכין בדיקה ואוצרות שמסתפקין מהם ויש קבע שנוטל מהם בפעם אחת א"צ בדיקה. אחרונים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All the rooms of the house.</b> Such as storage rooms, rooms for preserved foods and cheeses, cellars, woodsheds and coal rooms, beneath benches, and storehouses of wine and beer from which one regularly takes, all require checking. But storehouses from which one takes, where there is a fixed practice to take a single large amount at once, do not require checking. So write the later authorities.

<b>שאין יד האדם מגעת שם. </b> וכ' הטור בשם הירושלמי דהיינו אם גבוה י"ט והשיג עליו הטור דאדרבא עיקר התשמיש הוא בגבוה י"ט ועיין באחרונים שטרחו לתרץ הירושלמי ול"נ דהירושלמי לא קאמר אלא בגבוה י' למעלה מקומת איש כזה שאין יד האדם מגעת שם וכן משמע שם בירושלמי דומיא דהבור והדות דקתני התם דהוא למטה מקומת איש וע"ל סי' תל"ח גבי ככר בקורה וככר בבור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Where a person's hand cannot reach.</b> The Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that this means if it is ten handbreadths high, and the Tur objected that, on the contrary, the main use is at a height of ten handbreadths. See the later authorities, who labored to resolve the Yerushalmi. It appears to me that the Yerushalmi speaks only of something ten handbreadths above a person's stature, so that a person's hand cannot reach there. This is also implied there in the Yerushalmi, similar to the pit and cistern taught there, which are below a person's stature. See below, siman 438, regarding a loaf on a beam and a loaf in a pit.

<b>וכן אמצעי של חצר. </b> כן הוא ל' הרמב"ם וכ' הר"ן דאתי למעוטי חורי חצר וצ"ל הא דלא אמרינן דאתי למעוטי צדדים דא"כ תקשה על סוגיא דש"ש דריש פסחים דקאמר ולא לאור החמה היכי דמי אי נימא בחצר הא אמר רבא חצר א"צ בדיקה ואמאי לא משני דמיירי בחצר ומן הצדדים אלא ודאי דאף הרמב"ם מודה דמן הצדדים א"צ בדיקה רק דאתי למעוטי חורין שבחצר דצריך בדיקה והא דלא משני הש"ס הכי משום דהתם ליכא אור החמה כל זה נ"ל פשוט וברור בדברי הר"ן בכוונת דעת הרמב"ם דלא כהב"ח שפי' שדעת הרמב"ם נגד המשמעות הש"ס ושאר הפוסקים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise the middle of a courtyard.</b> This is the language of the Rambam. The Ran wrote that this comes to exclude holes in the courtyard. One must say that it does not come to exclude the sides, for if so there would be a difficulty on the sugya at the beginning of Pesachim, which says: not by sunlight, what is the case? If you say in a courtyard, but Rava said that a courtyard does not require checking. Why did it not answer that it is speaking of a courtyard and at the sides? Rather, certainly, even the Rambam agrees that the sides do not require checking. It only comes to exclude holes in the courtyard, which require checking. The reason the Gemara did not answer this way is that there sunlight is not present. All this appears to me simple and clear in the words of the Ran as the intent of the Rambam, unlike the Bach, who explained that the Rambam's view is against the implication of the Gemara and the other poskim.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And this applies only to doubtful chametz, but not to definite chametz.</b> So the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Semak, but I labored and did not find this in his words. Only in the Agudah, in the first chapter of Pesachim, is it written in this language: that a courtyard does not require checking means regarding doubtful chametz, but definite chametz must be removed. End quote. So too Mahari Weil wrote in siman 192. In Hagahos Maimoniyos it is written: Before the time of its prohibition, one may throw it wherever ravens are commonly found, since it is not yet forbidden in benefit, and this is the case in which the courtyard does not require checking. But if one finds chametz after the time of its prohibition, he may not leave it there so that the ravens will take it, for the essential removal of chametz is burning. End quote. At first glance there seem to be two contradictory opinions here, for from Hagahos Maimoniyos it is clear that even definite chametz does not require checking, and one may throw it there even initially, unlike the view of the Agudah. The Rama therefore noticed in his glosses on the Beit Yosef that the Beit Yosef's rulings here and in siman 445 contradict one another. The later authorities labored greatly to resolve the Beit Yosef's view and entered into great difficulties because of this question. It is even more astonishing regarding the Beit Yosef, for after citing in this siman the language of the Semak, he wrote the language of Hagahos Maimoniyos and did not notice any contradiction between them. Furthermore, according to the Agudah there is a difficulty: what did the Gemara ask, what is the case? If you say in a courtyard, but Rava said that a courtyard does not require checking. Perhaps it is indeed a courtyard and definite chametz. The Magen Avraham noticed this question and pressed to resolve it according to the Agudah, but he did not resolve it at all, for it would still be difficult: perhaps it speaks of definite chametz in a place where ravens are not commonly found, only birds. Examine what the Magen Avraham wrote. However, when one examines well the language of the Agudah and Mahari Weil, which is like the language of the Semak cited by the Beit Yosef, one sees that there is no difficulty at all and no contradiction whatever in the words of the early authorities. Their words must be read precisely: at first they wrote that chametz does not require checking, and at the end they wrote that it must be removed. Rather, the explanation is this: that a courtyard does not require checking refers to doubtful chametz, for we assume that the ravens and birds will eat it. But if there is definite chametz, and the time of removal has arrived, we do not say that they need not remove it at the time of removal and may rely on the birds eating it. Rather, they must remove it, exactly like the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos. But before the time of removal, it is certainly permitted to leave chametz there relying that they will eat it, as in the language of Hagahos Maimoniyos which he rules below in siman 445. The reason they mention doubtful chametz at the beginning is that the term checking applies only to a doubt, for with definite chametz, what language of checking applies? So too the Beit Yosef wrote below in siman 438; rather, there is only preparation to remove it the next day. This is also necessary and explicit in what Mahari Weil wrote in siman 153: the middle of the courtyard does not require checking, but if there is definite chametz there it must be removed, as stated in Hagahos Maimoniyos. Thus he explicitly combined the two opinions together, as I wrote. The later authorities erred in this because the Beit Yosef abbreviated the citation of the Semak, so they understood that they were divided opinions and challenged the Beit Yosef. In truth, it is all one opinion, joined together without any separation at all. Blessed is Hashem, Who enlightened my eyes in this to understand the words of the early authorities; they left me room to distinguish myself in this matter, and it is simple.

<b>בתי כנסיות ובתי מדרשות צריכין בדיקה. </b> והיינו בליל י"ד כ"כ האחרונים ומ"מ ונראה אם עבר ולא בדק בליל י"ד בודק אפילו לכתחלה ביום י"ד לאור החמה כמו אכסדרה שאורה ג"כ רב וכן מצאתי ראיתי במרדכי פ"ק דפסחים בשם הירושלמי וע"ל סי' ק"נ סעיף ד' ולא ראיתי נזהרים בזה לבדוק הבית הכנסת רק סגי להו בכיבוד בעלמא ביום וראוי להזהיר על זה השמשים של ב"ה וכ"כ מהרי"ל וכת' עוד דביש מקומות מכריזין אחר בדיקת בית הכנסת שלא יביאו עוד שם התינוקות עם חמץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Synagogues and batei midrash require checking.</b> This means on the night of the fourteenth, as the later authorities wrote. Nevertheless, it appears that if he transgressed and did not check on the night of the fourteenth, he may check even initially on the day of the fourteenth by sunlight, like an achsadra, whose light is also abundant. So I found explicitly in the Mordechai, first chapter of Pesachim, in the name of the Yerushalmi. See above, siman 150, seif 4. I have not seen people careful to check the synagogue; they suffice with mere sweeping by day. It is fitting to warn the synagogue attendants about this. So too Mahari Weil wrote, and he further wrote that in some places they announce after checking the synagogue that the children should no longer bring chametz there.

<b>ביום שלשה עשר. </b> ודוקא ביום אבל אם בדק ליל י"ג אין צריך לחזור ולבדוק כמו שכת' לעיל סק"א כ"כ הע"ש דלא כשאר אחרונים וע"ש:

<b>On the day of the thirteenth.</b> Specifically by day; but if he checked on the night of the thirteenth, he need not check again, as I wrote above in seif katan 1. So wrote the Olas Shabbos, unlike the other later authorities. See there.

<b>לכבד חדריו. </b> לכן נהגו ליטול נוצות ולכבד כ"כ המ"א ובאמת לא על כיבוד זה נאמר רק מכבדין ממש כל החדר והנוצות לוקחים רק בשעת בדיקה במקום שמוצא חמץ לגרור עם הנוצה מהסדקים והחורים או י"ל כוונתו על הנוצות גדולות מחוברות יחד דהיינו כנף של האווז שקודין פלעדר וויש בל"א שמכבדין בו כל המקומות קודם פסח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To sweep his rooms.</b> Therefore they have the custom to take feathers and sweep, as the Magen Avraham wrote. In truth, this was not said about such sweeping, but about actually sweeping the entire room. The feathers are taken only at the time of checking, when one finds chametz, to scrape with the feather from the cracks and holes. Or one can say that his intent is large feathers joined together, namely a goose wing, called a feather brush in the vernacular, with which they sweep all the places before Pesach.

<b>שנותנים בהם לפעמים חמץ. </b> אפילו אומר ברי לי שלא נתתי בהם חמץ אפשר דאפ"ה צריך בדיקה כיון שדרך לתת בו חמץ ומלתא דלא רמיא עליה דאינש לאו אדעתיה ועוד דלפעמים דבוק פירור חמץ בידים ונותן לתוך כיסוי ובתי יד שלו בלי ידיעתו כי ידים עסקניות הם וע"ל סימן תמ"ז סעיף ה' בהג"ה ומ"ש שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In which they sometimes place chametz.</b> Even if he says, I am certain that I did not place chametz in them, it is possible that nevertheless they require checking, since it is the way to place chametz in them, and something that is not imposed upon a person is not in his mind. Furthermore, sometimes a crumb of chametz is stuck to his hands and he puts it into his hood or sleeve without knowing it, for hands are busy. See below, siman 447, seif 5 in the gloss, and what I wrote there.

<b>צריכין בדיקה. </b> לכאורה משמע דבזמן בדיקה קאמר דהיינו ליל י"ד אבל באמת העולם אין נזהרין בזה ומנערים הכיסים בשעת הביעור ואפשר מטעם שאוכלין עוד חמץ ואולי נדבק בידיו דבר מה וחוזר ונותן לתוך כיסוי גם בלבוש משמע דאין צריך בדיקה בשעת הבדיקה והרוצה להחמיר עכ"פ צריך לחזור ולנער כיסו בשעת הביעור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They require checking.</b> At first glance this implies that it refers to the time of checking, namely the night of the fourteenth. But in truth, the world is not careful about this, and they shake out the pockets at the time of burning. Perhaps the reason is that they still eat chametz, and perhaps something sticks to their hands and they again put it into the hood. The Levush also implies that it does not need checking at the time of checking. One who wishes to be stringent must at least go back and shake out his pocket at the time of burning.
