נעקם לצדדים פסול כמו לפניו: והרמב"ם כו' שכ' ג"כ דצדדי' דינו כמו לאחריו: ועס"ס פ"א. זו ג"כ הוא ראיית הר"מ (אלא שיש ט"ס שם) שכ' בסי' פ"א היה קורא וראה צואה כנגדו ילך כדי שתשאר הצואה לאחריו ד"א ואם א"א לילך לפניו יסלקנה לצדדים וירחיק ד"א הרי דבמקום דאפשר שתהיה צואה לאחריו עדיף טפי ולא מהני במה שמסלקה לצדדי': דצדדים שלפניו. ר"ל שאינו פניו נגד ממש אלא לצדדים שלפניו: כמ"ש סי' קכ"ח כו' דאמרי' שם עה שאחורי כהנים אין בכלל הברכה אבל העומדי' לפני הכהנים אבל משוכים לצדדים שלפני כהני' הם בכלל ברכה וכאלו הם עומדים נגד פני הכהנים ממש אלא דלפ"ז דמדמה האי דהכ' למ"ש בסי' קכ"ח א"כ מאי דקי"ל שם דצדדי כהן ממש הוי ג"כ כשלפניו א"כ למה כ' בשם ר"מ דאם אפשר עדיף להניחו לאחוריו ד"א אבל בלא אפשר צדדים דינו כלאחריו: (ס"ק ד) של כו' דעת שומע כו' והוי כעונה והכא כיון דצואה מפסקת א"א להיות שומע כעונה דהא אסור לענות: (ס"ק ה) במקום כו' רחב ד' טפחי' על ד' טפחים. והט"ז חולק וס"ל דאפי' אינו רחב ד' טפחים: (ס"ק ח) כשאינו כו' שרי בזה לכ"ע דהא במעצים עיניו אין אסורו כ"א משום והיה מחניך קדוש והכא כיון דמחיצה מפסקת מקרי **** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) Even, etc.</b> See the end of siman 75. Meaning, regarding ervah in such a case there is a dispute between the Beit Yosef and the Bach. According to the Beit Yosef it is permitted, but regarding excrement even the Beit Yosef agrees that it is forbidden. (Seif katan 2) To his side, etc. Regarding a bent lulav, below at the end of siman 645, one bent toward the front is invalid, while one bent toward the back is valid. It is written there that if it is bent to the sides, it is invalid like one bent toward the front. And the Rambam, etc., who likewise wrote that the sides have the same law as behind him. See also the end of siman 81. This too is Rabbi Moshe's proof, although there is a textual error there, for he wrote in siman 81: If one was reciting and saw excrement opposite him, he should walk until the excrement remains four amos behind him. If he cannot walk forward, he should move it to the side and distance himself four amos. Thus, where it is possible for the excrement to be four amos behind him, that is preferable, and moving it to the side does not suffice. The sides in front of him, meaning that his face is not directly opposite it, but it is to the forward sides. As written in siman 128, etc., for we say there that those behind the kohanim are not included in the blessing, but those standing before the kohanim, though drawn to the forward sides of the kohanim, are included in the blessing, as though standing directly before the kohanim. According to this, however, since he compares the case here to that in siman 128, and we rule there that the kohen's direct sides are also like his front, why did he write in Rabbi Moshe's name that where possible it is preferable to place it four amos behind him, but where that is impossible the sides are treated like behind him? (Seif katan 4) Of, etc. The listener's intent, etc., and he is considered as one who responds. Here, since excrement intervenes, the principle that listening is equivalent to responding cannot apply, because responding is forbidden. (Seif katan 5) In a place, etc., four handbreadths wide by four handbreadths. The Taz disagrees and maintains that this applies even if it is not four handbreadths wide. (Seif katan 8) When he does not, etc. This is permitted according to all opinions, because when he closes his eyes the prohibition is only due to, "Your camp shall be holy." Here, since a partition intervenes, it is called, "Your camp shall be holy." The reason of those who prohibit is that he sees it, and since he closes his eyes, or in a comparable case in which he does not see it, it is permitted. (Seif katan 9) And some say, etc. This requires study, etc. Nevertheless, it may be said that here is different, etc., as written in seif 9. Meaning, we rule that a foul odor without a tangible source, namely flatulence, prohibits recitation until the odor dissipates. If so, according to the opinion that a partition intervenes even regarding odor, why should this be less severe than an odor without a tangible source? It must therefore be said that only when the excrement is in another room, even though the odor enters this room, does the partition intervene, because the excrement from which the odor came was never in this room. We associate the odor with the excrement in the other room. This differs from an odor without a tangible source, namely flatulence, where the person from whom it emerged was initially in this room when it emerged. That is worse and is forbidden, even though now it is as though the excrement has passed away, because after the flatulence has emerged the person is not himself considered excrement. The law also applies where the flatulence came from another person. The ruling in seif 9 is stated without qualification, implying that even if the other person left the room and only the odor remained, recitation is still forbidden. The same applies here when the excrement was removed and its odor remained. And also the Rashba, etc. It appears to me that there is a textual error and the order was reversed. The words, "And also the Rashba," should appear before, "Nevertheless, it may be said that here is different." He brings proof from the Rashba to permit it. The Rashba explains that an odor without a tangible source does not refer to flatulence, but even to odor coming from excrement when the excrement is covered. In such a case recitation is forbidden, but flatulence is permitted according to him because there is no excrement present. The same would apply here. Although we do not rule like the Rashba and prohibit flatulence, it is possible that this case is more lenient than flatulence. At the end, however, the Magen Avraham retracted and maintained that this case is comparable to flatulence.
