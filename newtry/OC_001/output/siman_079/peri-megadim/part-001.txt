**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Know</b> that the law of excrement is divided into ten categories: first, excrement behind him, including at his sides; second, in front of him; third, in a place ten handbreadths high or ten handbreadths low; fourth, excrement in another room with the doorway open; fifth, excrement behind glass; sixth, excrement in water; seventh, a foul odor that has a source and one that has no source; eighth, moving excrement; ninth, this also includes the law of urine, a chamber pot, and a refuse vessel; tenth, a garbage heap and the excrement of animals and wild beasts.

<b>הא׳</b> צואה מאחוריו תוך ד״א אסור מ״ה לקרות ק״ש ולדבר ד״ת שנאמר והיה מחנך קדוש (דברים כ״ג י״ד ט״ו) וקאי אצואה וכל תוך ד״א חנייה של אדם הוא כ״כ חה״ר ושאר פוסקים. ואם קרא אפילו במקום שראוי לספק חוזר וקורא כבסימן ע״ו ס״ח וכבר דברנו פעמים רבות מזה אם הטעם דכ״מ דאמר רחמנא לא תעביד לא מהני כרבא בתמורה ד׳ וכ״ת דוקא מעשה הא ליתא דהמקדים תרומה לבכורים דפריך מיניה דיבור ומחשבה הוי יע״ש. ומיהו המעיין בסמ״ע ח״מ ר״ח ובי״ד י״א השוחט ביוה״כ וי״ט ש״מ דכ״מ כו׳ דוקא אם לא יצויר בע״א משא״כ כאן שהמקום גורם ויראה דהוי דרבנן וכמ״ש רבא ברכות כ״ב זבח רשעים תועבה יע״ש ועיין קפ״ה ויבואר אי״ה לקמן גבי מי רגלים בזה יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The first</b>: when excrement is behind him within four amos, it is forbidden by Torah law to recite Krias Shema or speak words of Torah, as it is stated, “Your camp shall be holy,” which refers to excrement, and everything within four amos is considered a person’s camp. So wrote the Chidushei HaRashba and the other poskim. If he recited even in a place where the presence of excrement was only reasonably doubtful, he must repeat the recitation, as stated in siman 76, seif 8. We have already discussed many times whether the reason is that whenever the Torah says not to do something, the act is ineffective, following Rava in Temurah 4. One cannot answer that this applies only to an act, because the Gemara challenges from one who gives terumah before first fruits, which involves speech and intent. Nevertheless, one who studies Sema in Choshen Mishpat siman 208 and Yoreh Deah siman 11 concerning one who slaughters on Yom Kippur or Yom Tov will see that this principle applies only where the matter cannot otherwise take effect. Here, by contrast, the place causes the prohibition, and it appears that the requirement to repeat is Rabbinic, as Rava states in Berachos 22, “The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination.” See also siman 185. This will be explained, with Hashem’s help, below regarding urine.

<b>השני</b> צואה מלפניו מרחיק מלא עיניו וכבר ידעת מחלוקת הרא״ש ורשב״א כפי הנראה להרא״ש לא יראה קאי רק אערוה וכפשטא דסוגיין דשבת ק״ן א׳ ובצואה ליכא רק מחנך קדוש וא״כ חוץ לד״א למה אסור לפניו ויש לנו כמה דרכים או מדרבנן אסור לפניו כל שרואה. או מ״ה כל שרואה מלפניו בכלל מחנך הוי וכ״מ ראי׳ הוי בכלל חנייה אפי׳ חוץ לד״א ובחידושינו הארכנו עתה ראיתי להפר״ח כתב מדכתיב ולא יראה בוי״ו קאי נמי אצואה והיינו כל שהוא חוץ למחנהו ומ״מ לא ברשות אחרת הא ברשות אחרת גלי קרא דמחנך דכה״ג שרי ומש״ה שרי בחדר אחר אע״פ שרואה יע״ש. ואמנם לרשב״א מבואר דסובר דלא יראה קאי נמי אצואה ואפי׳ בחדר אחר כל שרואה אסור מ״ה. ולפי״ז להרא״ש אם נאמר כמלא עיניו דרבנן א״ש ברכות כ״ב ב׳ מצא צואה במקומה וכן הא דת״ר יסלקנה לאחוריו ואין חוזר ומתפלל דמלא עיניו דרבנן ספק לקולא כמו מ״ר בע״ו ס״ח ומיהו אף לרשב״א דמלא עיניו מ״ה פוסק מלא עיניו אין חוזר וקורא בפ״א במ״א ד׳ דמחנך קדוש איכא רק לא יראה וכיון שלא ידע ולא ראה לית כאן איסור תורה ולפ״ז אם נאמר לראש כל לפניו כמלא עיניו מקום חנייה הוי י״ל בזה ומיהו בלא״ה נראה מפסק המחבר כאן ס״ב דפסק כרשב״א וכ״פ בפ״א. ויש נ״מ לרא״ש י״ל כמלא עיניו אף הרהור אסור מדרבנן או לפניו הוי מקום חנייה כמלא עיניו ואלו לרשב״א הרהור חוץ לד״א כמלא עיניו מותר שהרי בערוה נמי הרהור מותר בשבת ק״ן א׳ ה״ה צואה דלית מחנך רק לא יראה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The second</b>: excrement in front of him requires distancing as far as he can see. You already know the dispute between the Rosh and Rashba. According to the apparent view of the Rosh, the verse “no nakedness shall be seen” refers only to ervah, in accordance with the plain meaning of the passage in Shabbos 150a, while concerning excrement there is only the requirement that “your camp shall be holy.” Why, then, is it forbidden in front of him beyond four amos? Several approaches are possible. It may be Rabbinically forbidden whenever he sees it, or it may be forbidden by Torah law because everything visible before him is included in his camp, and every visible area is regarded as part of his encampment even beyond four amos. In my novellae I discussed this at length. I have now seen that Peri Chadash writes that since the verse says “and no nakedness shall be seen,” with the conjunctive vav, it also refers to excrement, meaning whenever it is outside his camp. Nevertheless, this does not apply in another domain, because the verse “your camp” indicates that such a case is permitted. Therefore, excrement in another room is permitted even though he sees it. According to Rashba, however, “no nakedness shall be seen” also refers to excrement, and even in another room it is forbidden by Torah law whenever he sees it. According to this, if one says that the distance of eyesight is only Rabbinic according to the Rosh, the passages in Berachos 22b concerning one who found excrement in his place, and the baraisa requiring him to move it behind him but not repeat the Amidah, are readily understood. A doubtful Rabbinic prohibition is treated leniently, like urine in siman 76, seif 8. Yet even according to Rashba, who holds that the distance of eyesight is a Torah requirement, he rules in siman 81, cited in Magen Avraham seif katan 4, that one does not repeat the recitation, because “your camp shall be holy” was fulfilled and only “it shall not be seen” was violated. Since he did not know and did not see it, there was no Torah violation. According to the Rosh, if everything visible in front of him is considered his camp, this can also be explained. In any event, the Mechaber’s ruling here in seif 2 appears to follow Rashba, as he also rules in siman 81. A practical difference is that according to the Rosh, perhaps even contemplation is Rabbinically forbidden within eyesight, or else the entire visible area in front of him is considered his camp. According to Rashba, however, contemplation beyond four amos but within eyesight is permitted, because contemplation is permitted even before ervah, as stated in Shabbos 150a, and the same applies to excrement where only “it shall not be seen” applies and not “your camp.”

<b>והנה</b> פשיטנא לכאורה מהדא דירושלמי רבי לייא וחביריו הובא בחה״ר וט״ז ובפר״ח דודאי תוך ד״א לא היה צואה דאל״כ תיפוק מחנך רק כמלא עיניו וא״כ איך פסק הוראה שם ש״מ כמלא עיניו הרהור מותר ופס״ד הוי הרהור כבסי׳ פ״ה ב׳ ומ״ז ס״ד אלא דהא בורכא דהא פסק בנתינת טעם כיון דהוי יממא ולא היל״ל רק אסור והתם לאפרושי מאיסורא היה שרי כבסי׳ פ״ה ובהכרח דרבי לייא אמר רק סתם אסור ואח״כ כשהלך משם אמר הטעם. ומ״מ הדין אמת לרשב״א כמלא עיניו הרהור מותר וכדכתיבנא. גם מדיתבי רבנן התם הא ת״ח אל יהלך במבואות המטונפות ש״מ חוץ לד״א הרהור שרי. והנך רואה דרבי לייא וחברוהי ידעין בודאי שהיה שם צואה כמלא עיניו ואסור אפי׳ בלילה דאלו מספק הא פסק המחבר בפ״א כדיעה א׳ בסתם דאין חוזר לראש ומ״ש הט״ז אפילו בספק צואה אפשר דבתוך ד״א קאמר ומלתא באפי נפשיה היא. ומה שקשה מצואה עוברת לרשב״א יבואר לקמן אי״ה שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It would appear</b> at first that the Yerushalmi concerning Rabbi Ila and his colleagues, cited by Chidushei HaRashba, Taz, and Peri Chadash, resolves this. Certainly there was no excrement within four amos, for otherwise the prohibition would derive from “your camp.” It was only within eyesight. How, then, could Rabbi Ila issue a halachic ruling there? This would show that contemplation is permitted within eyesight, and rendering a ruling is considered contemplation, as stated in siman 85, seif 2, and Mishbetzos Zahav seif katan 4. But this proof is mistaken, because Rabbi Ila gave a reason for the prohibition, saying that had it been daytime they would have seen what was before them. He should simply have said that it was forbidden. Furthermore, there it would have been permitted to intervene to separate them from a prohibition, as in siman 85. We must therefore say that Rabbi Ila merely said in general that it was forbidden, and only after leaving the place explained the reason. Nevertheless, the law is correct according to Rashba, that contemplation within eyesight is permitted, as I wrote. The fact that the Sages sat there also shows this, for a Torah scholar may not walk through filthy alleyways, which implies that contemplation beyond four amos is permitted. You see that Rabbi Ila and his colleagues knew with certainty that excrement was present within eyesight and that it was forbidden even at night. Had it been only doubtful, the Mechaber rules in siman 81, following the first opinion stated without qualification, that one does not return to the beginning. What Taz wrote, that even doubtful excrement is forbidden, may refer to within four amos and be an independent point. The difficulty concerning moving excrement according to Rashba will be explained below, with Hashem’s help.

<b>הג׳</b> במקום גבוה יו״ד או נמוך יו״ד דהוי רשות אחרת לרא״ש אפילו רואה שרי דקרא לא יראה לא קאי עלה ולהרשב״א אסור כמבואר כאן ס״ב. ואם בעינן גבוה י״ט ורוחב ד״ט עט״ז אות ג׳ ועמ״ש אי״ה בצואה בעששית מזה. ומ״ש המ״א אות ח׳ בעוצם עיניו לכ״ע שרי לי העני א״י מקורו כעת ואי מצואה עוברת ברכות כ״ה א׳ דח״מ שרי דלאו מחנך הוי כמ״ש בחה״ר ואכתי לאו דלא יראה להרשב״א ש״מ דעוצם שרי וצ״ע מירושלמי ומסעיף א׳ כאן דמלא עיניו נמי לאו מחנך הוי רק מלא יראה ואפ״ה בלילה אסור וההיא דצואה עוברת י״ל מאחוריו מיירי. הן אמת שהר״מ ז״ל אסברה לן מלפניו בפ״ג מק״ש הי״ג שטה ע״פ המים כנגד״ה וכפירש״י כ״ה א׳ צואה עוברת מלפניו יע״ש. וראיתי שדברי האדון ז״ל בנוים על אדני האמת והשכל כמ״ש באות א׳ בלילה דבעינן מחנך קדוש והראה לע״ה כי להמחבר ודאי קשה דבע״ה ס״ז בערוה ד״ת עוצם מהני ובצואה די״א לא יראה רק אערוה ולרא״ש י״ל כמלא עיניו דרבנן יהיה חמור דרבנן מד״ת וע״כ סובר המחבר לפניו כמלא עיניו ורואה צואה לאו מחנך קדוש הוי ומש״ה עוצם לא מהני משא״כ בערוה דרק לא יראה כל שעוצם מהני עמ״ש בע״ה בט״ז אות ב׳ ולפ״ז ודאי הוא דבחדר אחר דליכא כ״א לא יראה עוצם מהני כמו ערוה כאמור ולב״ח ולאינך אה״נ דאסור כה״ג בחדר אחר כמו ערוה והמחבר למד זה מרש״י ור״מ בצואה עוברת דאיך שרי אביי לפניו הא רואה ע״כ בעוצם ועוברת סובר לאו מחנך קרינן ביה ושפיר והבן זה ועמ״ש במ״א א׳ בשם פרישה ולבוש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The third</b>: when the excrement is in a place ten handbreadths high or ten handbreadths low, it is in another domain. According to the Rosh, even if he sees it, it is permitted, because the verse “it shall not be seen” does not refer to excrement. According to Rashba, it is forbidden, as explained here in seif 2. Whether the elevated place must be ten handbreadths high and four by four handbreadths wide is discussed by Taz, seif katan 3. See what I will write, with Hashem’s help, concerning excrement behind glass. Regarding Magen Avraham’s statement in seif katan 8 that closing one’s eyes permits it according to all opinions, in my poverty I do not presently know his source. If it is derived from moving excrement in Berachos 25a, where Chidushei HaRashba says it is permitted because it is not within one’s camp, the prohibition of “it shall not be seen” according to Rashba still remains. This would prove that closing one’s eyes helps, but it requires study in light of the Yerushalmi and seif 1 here, where the distance of eyesight is also not part of one’s camp but rather derives only from “it shall not be seen,” yet it is forbidden at night. The case of moving excrement can perhaps be understood as being behind him. It is true that Rambam explains it as passing before him in Hilchos Krias Shema 3:13, floating upon the water opposite him, following Rashi on 25a. I have seen that the master’s words are founded on truth and reason, as he wrote in seif katan 1 concerning nighttime, that the requirement is “your camp shall be holy.” He thereby illuminated the matter. According to the Mechaber, siman 75, seif 7, closing one’s eyes helps regarding ervah by Torah law. Concerning excrement, there are opinions that “it shall not be seen” refers only to ervah. According to the Rosh, if the distance of eyesight were merely Rabbinic, the Rabbinic law would be more stringent than the Torah law. The Mechaber must therefore hold that visible excrement before him within eyesight is not a holy camp, and closing one’s eyes does not help. Regarding ervah, by contrast, only “it shall not be seen” applies, so closing one’s eyes helps. Accordingly, in another room, where only “it shall not be seen” applies, closing one’s eyes certainly helps, just as with ervah. According to Bach and the others, however, it is indeed forbidden in another room in such a case, as with ervah. The Mechaber learned this from Rashi and Rambam regarding moving excrement. How could Abaye permit excrement passing before him when he sees it? It must be that he closes his eyes, and moving excrement is not regarded as part of his camp. The ruling is therefore sound. Understand this, and see what I wrote in Magen Avraham seif katan 1 in the name of Perishah and Levush.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The fourth</b>: excrement in another room with the doorway open. One who studies the Rosh on Berachos 25a regarding a foul odor that has a source will see that he writes that an elevated place permits even when one sees the excrement, because “your camp shall be holy” is fulfilled. According to this, excrement before an open doorway is permitted, because an open doorway is considered as though closed, like excrement behind glass. Thus there is a distinction. Even if we assume that a place ten handbreadths high, though another domain, is forbidden when visible according to Rashba, an open doorway may nevertheless be treated as closed and like excrement behind glass. Although he sees it, it is permitted because it is covered. Scripture reveals that covering helps even according to Rashba, as we will explain, with Hashem’s help, regarding excrement behind glass. The same applies before an open doorway. We do not find that Rashba disagrees with this, only concerning a high or low place, where the concept of being as though closed does not apply. It may be that where there is a door, Rashba agrees that it is considered closed. The Mechaber in seif 2 follows his own view in his longer work, where he raised a difficulty from the case of a small courtyard adjoining a large one. See Peri Chadash, who answers that it concerns excrement behind him. Alternatively, it may be explained by closing one’s eyes, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 8. According to the Mechaber, this must be the explanation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The fifth</b>: excrement behind glass is permitted, because the Torah made the law dependent upon covering. Rif’s text reads that “your camp shall be holy” is fulfilled. See my novellae. Even according to Rashba, who holds that “it shall not be seen” refers to excrement, Scripture reveals that covering helps. As I wrote above, covering is not considered another domain, but rather like something buried in the ground, and so Rambam writes in Hilchos Krias Shema 3:9. Know that the glass mentioned in Berachos 25b is explained by Rashi as a glass partition separating between him and the excrement. Rambam likewise explains in halachah 10: “If there was a glass partition between him and the excrement, it is permitted.” The same applies to ervah behind glass. Accordingly, the excrement need not be covered on all sides. A glass partition separating between them is itself the covering. Even according to Bach, cited by Taz seif katan 3, who requires an elevated place to be ten handbreadths high and four by four handbreadths wide, such dimensions are not required for a partition. Whenever there is a separation between him and the excrement, it is permitted, and this is the covering. Therefore, if excrement is outside and he sees it through a glass window, it is permitted according to all opinions, because a glass partition separates between them. Even according to Rashba in seif 2, who forbids it in another domain when visible, it is permitted when there is a glass partition.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The seventh</b>: a foul odor that has a source is like the excrement itself and is forbidden by Torah law. Following Rav Chisda, one must distance four amos from the point where the odor ends. It is obvious that if he recited he must repeat, even were we to say that for a Rabbinic prohibition one does not repeat. See Peri Chadash at the end of seif 2. When the excrement is in another room, the Mechaber cites without qualification the first opinion that a partition does not help for a foul odor, and he rules likewise in seif 3, as Peri Chadash writes. Nevertheless, if the foul odor does not reach him, even though there is a foul odor in the room containing the excrement, he need not distance four amos from the place of the odor. This also appears from Beit Yosef and Perishah, based on what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the name of the French rabbis and that Rambam forbids it. According to Rambam, even in another domain he follows Rav Huna, who requires only that the foul odor not reach him. Since Rabbeinu Yonah wrote this, it follows that for us as well a partition at least helps in another domain, so that it is enough that the foul odor not reach him, as stated. We must say that this is considered a foul odor without a source. Excrement covered by a vessel is considered a foul odor without a source, as discussed by Magen Avraham here in seif katan 9, and the same applies when it is in another room. Accordingly, if the foul odor reaches him, the prohibition is only Rabbinic, like a foul odor without a source, which is permitted by Torah law. In seif 9, words of Torah are permitted when the odor has no source. Were it a Torah prohibition, words of Torah would certainly also be forbidden, as Taz writes in seif katan 1. Perishah writes that the Torah’s command “you shall cover it” implies that when there is no foul odor it is permitted by Torah law. This requires study, because it appears that the prohibition is Rabbinic, as I wrote. If so, the verse teaches that it is permitted by Torah law in every such case. The words of Levush in seifim 2 and 3 appear contradictory. In seif 2 he requires four amos from where the odor ends, while in seif 3 he writes that it is permitted if the odor does not reach him.
