<small>שם</small> והרמב"ם ר"ל דלהרמב"ם שכ' דין זה דצדדים כלאחריו מיירי כשא"א לו להחזיר לצדדין דבה"ג מבוא' בס"ס פ"א דדי בצדדים אבל היכ' דאפש' אז אין צדדים כלפניו כ"כ הכ"מ בשם ה"ר מנוח אבל הפוסקים חולקים ע"ז ולכן סיים המג"א דצדדים שלפניו. כ"ע מודו דהוי כלפניו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> “And the Rambam,” meaning that according to the Rambam, who writes that the sides are treated like behind him, the case concerns one who cannot turn it to the sides. In such a case, it is explained at the end of siman 81 that placing it to the sides is sufficient. Where it is possible to place it behind him, however, the sides are not treated like in front of him. This is what the Kesef Mishneh writes in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach. The other authorities disagree. The Magen Avraham therefore concludes that all agree that the sides extending toward his front are treated like the area in front of him.

<small>ט"ז סק"ב</small> שכלה דכך הדין כמבואר בריש הסעיף וע"ז סיים דבמי רגלים כו' ור"ל דאין להם ריח דאלו יש להם ריח דינם כצואה עיין סי' פ"ו. וכוונת הט"ז למה כתב דין זה בצואה ולא כתב דה"ה למ"ר לכן כ' דמ"ר מרחיקין כל הקהל ד"א כיון דא"צ להרחיק רק מהמ"ר עצמם דאין להם ריח משא"כ בצואה דצריך להרחיק ממקום הריח א"א להם להרחיק וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Taz, seif katan 2.</small> “Where the odor ceases,” for this is the law explained at the beginning of the seif. He then concludes, “but regarding urine,” and so forth. This means urine that has no odor, for if it has an odor its law is like excrement, as explained in siman 86. The Taz’s point is to ask why this law was written regarding excrement and not also regarding urine. He therefore writes that in the case of urine, the entire congregation can distance itself four amos, since one need distance himself only from the urine itself, as it has no odor. With excrement, however, one must distance himself from the place where its odor ceases, and they cannot all do so.
