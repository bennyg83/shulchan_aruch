<b>או פוררו כו'. </b> רבה ס"ל דחמץ בעי פירור גם בזור' לים ומחלק בין ים המלח דלא בעי פירור פירש"י משום דשם אין הולכין ספינו' משא"כ בשאר נהרות ור"י ס"ל חמץ לא בעי פירור  כיון דממאיס והא דתניא בעי פירור ל"ק הא בחיטי והא בנהמא פירש"י חטים שנחמצו לא יזרקם בשק על פני המים אלא יפזר' על פני המים ופסק הרא"ש כרבה ודברי הש"ע כאן הם ל' הרמב"ם ומשמע דס"ל כרב יוסף דמחלק בין חיטי לנהמ' וע"כ כ' ואם היה החמץ קשה וכו' וקשה למה פסק כר"י והא קי"ל כרבה לגבי ר"י לבר מתלת ותו דהטו' לא חילק בחמץ קשה ממיל' ס"ל כרבה וכ' וכ"כ הרמב"ם וזה אינו וב"י האריך להסכים דברי הטור והרמב"ם ולעד"נ דגם הרמב"ם פוסק כרבה וס"ל דהחילוק שבין ים המלח לשאר ימים דלא כפירש"י אלא דהים המלח מים שבו חדים בטבע המלח ומחתך החמץ במהרה משא"כ בשאר מים. ורב יוסף דמחלק בין חיטי לנהמ' היינו ג"כ דלא כרש"י אלא הרמב"ם מפ' דלר"י דבמיאוס תליא מלת' ע"כ אינו מיקל רק בנהמ' דממאיס בלחלוחית המים אבל חטים אינם מאיסי דאפשר להדיחם שפיר וא"כ גם הרמב"ם פוסק כרבה דלרבה החילוק בין ים המלח לשאר מים הוא משום חוזק המים ודבר זה א"א לכוין דלפעמים יש בשאר מים חילוק זה החזק הוא הרפה ע"כ נתן כלל לפי ענין החמץ אם יתחתך מהרה שם במקו' שיזרוק אותם והחילוק דנהמ' וחטי' לא זכר הרמב"ם כלל כיון דאינו אלא לר"י והשתא ניחא דהטור לא זכר חילוק דים המלח ושאר מים דלא פסיק' לן מלת' כמ"ש ע"כ החמיר בכל מים דבעי פירור כנלע"ד הגם שה' המגיד כ' שהרמב"ם פסק כר"י חלקי אמרה נפשי והוא נכון בס"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or he crumbles it, etc.</b> Rabbah holds that chametz requires crumbling even when throwing it into the sea, and he distinguishes between the Dead Sea, where it does not require crumbling, which Rashi explains is because ships do not travel there, and other rivers. Rav Yosef holds that chametz does not require crumbling, since it becomes repulsive; and that which the baraisa teaches that it requires crumbling is not difficult, for this refers to wheat kernels and that refers to bread. Rashi explains that wheat kernels that became chametz should not be thrown into the water in a sack, but should be scattered over the water. The Rosh ruled like Rabbah. The words of the Shulchan Aruch here are the language of the Rambam, and it implies that he holds like Rav Yosef, who distinguishes between wheat kernels and bread; therefore he wrote, and if the chametz was hard, etc. This is difficult: why did he rule like Rav Yosef, when we hold like Rabbah against Rav Yosef except in three cases? Furthermore, the Tur did not distinguish hard chametz, which means he holds like Rabbah, and he wrote that so wrote the Rambam, but this is not so. The Beis Yosef wrote at length to reconcile the words of the Tur and the Rambam. In my humble opinion, it appears that the Rambam too rules like Rabbah, but he holds that the distinction between the Dead Sea and other seas is not like Rashi's explanation; rather, the waters of the Dead Sea are sharp by the nature of the salt and cut the chametz quickly, unlike other waters. Rav Yosef, who distinguishes between wheat kernels and bread, also does not mean as Rashi explained. Rather, the Rambam explains that according to Rav Yosef, who makes the matter depend on repulsiveness, he is lenient only with bread, which becomes repulsive in the moisture of the water; but wheat kernels do not become repulsive, for they can be rinsed well. If so, the Rambam also rules like Rabbah: according to Rabbah the distinction between the Dead Sea and other waters is because of the strength of the water. This matter cannot be measured precisely, for sometimes there is such a distinction among other waters, this one strong and this one weak. Therefore he gave a rule according to the nature of the chametz, whether it will be cut apart quickly in the place where he throws it. The Rambam did not mention the distinction between bread and wheat kernels at all, since it belongs only to Rav Yosef. Now it is understandable that the Tur did not mention the distinction between the Dead Sea and other waters, because the matter is not fixed for us, as I wrote; therefore he was stringent with all waters, requiring crumbling. So it appears to me, although the Maggid Mishneh wrote that the Rambam ruled like Rav Yosef. My soul has said my portion, and it is correct with the help of Heaven.

<b>והמנהג לשרפו כו'. </b> נרא' טעם לשריפ' זו משום שיש מחלוקת בין ר"י לחכמים אם בעי' שריפה ורבו הפלוגת' בין הפוסקים ע"כ יש לנהוג כמחמירין ולכן נרא' דמי שמקיים מצות שריפ' בחמצו משעה ו' ואח"כ נמצא חתיכות חמץ שא"צ לשרפו אלא יבערו מן העולם שהרי משעה ו' ולמעלה אנו מקילין בחמץ כמו בשאר איסורין הן לענין משהו הן לענין נ"ט לפגם כמ"ש לקמן סי' תמ"ז ולכן כיון דאפי' בפסח עצמו אנן קי"ל כרבנן אלא משום מנהג בעלמ' אנו נוהגין כר"י משא"כ כאן דהוא קודם הליל' והוא כבר קיים מצות שריפה בשעה ו' לכן א"צ לשרפו כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the custom is to burn it, etc.</b> The reason for this burning appears to be that there is a dispute between Rabbi Yehuda and the Sages whether burning is required, and the dispute among the halachic decisors is extensive; therefore one should practice like the stringent authorities. It therefore appears that one who fulfilled the mitzvah of burning with his chametz from the sixth hour and afterward pieces of chametz were found does not need to burn them, but should destroy them from the world. For from the sixth hour and onward we are lenient with chametz as with other prohibitions, both regarding a mashehu and regarding a spoiled flavor, as I wrote below in siman 447. Therefore, since even on Pesach itself we hold like the Rabbis, only that because of mere custom we practice like Rabbi Yehuda, this is unlike here, where it is before night and he already fulfilled the mitzvah of burning in the sixth hour; therefore he need not burn it. So it appears to me.

<b>ואם שרפו קודם שעה ו' כו'. </b> בגמרא ד' י"ב ע"ב אר"י אימתי שלא בשעת ביעורו אבל בשעת ביעורו השבתתו בכל דבר ופירש"י שלא בשעת ביעורו בתחילת ו' וכל ו' דאכתי מדאוריי' שרי אבל בשעת ביעורו בז' כו' ובטור הביא דעת הרא"ש דדעת רש"י בשעה ה' השבתתו בכל דבר כיון שהוא מות' בהנא' והטור הקש' עליו ממה שפירש"י בגמ' דכ"ז ע"ב אהא דאמרו ליה לר"י לא מצא עצים לשרפו יהא יושב ובטל בתמי' וז"ל אע"ג דאמרי' בפ"ק לר"י אבל בשעת ביעורו השבתתו בכל דבר מיהו גבי פלוגתייהו קולא הוא במי שרוצה לצאת לשייר' כו' דאמרי' זקוק לבער וכשאין עצים לשרפו יהא יושב ובטל ע"כ והב"י רוצ' לישב קושיתו ע"ש ול"נ דכד מעיינת בפירש"י לא תקשי כלום על הרא"ש משום דק' למה לי' למנקט שייר' טפי ה"ל להקשו' משע' ו' שמי שאין לו עצים יהא יושב ובטל ולא יבערנו אע"כ צ"ל דה"פ שודאי לר"י עיקר הביעור שריפה מ"מ שמי שאין לו עצים עכ"פ צריך לבערו מן העולם כדי שלא יעבור בבל יראה ובבל ימצא וא"כ אין להקשות משעה ו' ממי שאין לו עצים כו' דאע' ג דלא יהיו לו עצים מ"מ חיוב ביעור דעלמ' עליו שלא יעבור בבל יראה ממילא לא יהיה יושב ובטל ולהכי נקט רש"י יוצא בשיירא דבזה ודאי אם לא יהיו לו עצים דיהי' יושב ובטל דמשום חובת הביעור דהיינו השריפה אינו מוטל עליו משום דאין לו עצים והביעור דעלמא משום בל יראה ג"כ אינו מוטל עליו דעדיין לא חלה עליו בל יראה כלל וא"כ יהי' יושב ובטל ולפ"ז לק"מ על הרא"ש דאם יבער בביעור דעלמא בשעה ה' זה לא מיקרי יושב ובטל כיון דמעיקר' כבר ביערו מן העולם ואין לו מה לשרוף ונסתלק קושיות הטור מעליו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he burned it before the sixth hour, etc.</b> In the Gemara, folio 12b, Rabbi Yehuda said: when is this so? When it is not at the time of its destruction; but at the time of its destruction, its destruction may be by any means. Rashi explained: not at the time of its destruction means at the beginning of the sixth hour and all of the sixth, for according to Torah law it is still permitted; but at the time of its destruction means in the seventh hour, etc. In the Tur he brought the view of the Rosh, that according to Rashi's view, in the fifth hour its destruction may be by any means, since it is permitted for benefit. The Tur objected to him from what Rashi explained in the Gemara on folio 27b, regarding what they said to Rabbi Yehuda: if he did not find wood to burn it, should he sit idle? Rashi wrote as follows: although we say in the first chapter, according to Rabbi Yehuda, that at the time of its destruction, its destruction may be by any means, nevertheless regarding their dispute this is a leniency, in the case of one who wants to leave in a caravan, etc., where we say he is required to destroy it, and when he has no wood to burn it, should he sit idle? End quote. The Beis Yosef wants to answer his difficulty; see there. To me it appears that when you examine Rashi's explanation, there is no difficulty at all against the Rosh. For there is a difficulty: why did Rashi need to mention a caravan? He should have asked from the sixth hour, that one who has no wood should sit idle and not destroy it. Rather, perforce this is the explanation: certainly according to Rabbi Yehuda, the primary destruction is burning; nevertheless, one who has no wood must in any event destroy it from the world so that he should not transgress bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei. If so, one cannot ask from the sixth hour, from one who has no wood, etc.; for although he will not have wood, nevertheless the general obligation of destruction is upon him so that he should not transgress bal yera'eh, and automatically he will not sit idle. Therefore Rashi mentioned one leaving in a caravan, for in that case, if he has no wood, he would certainly sit idle: the obligation of destruction, namely burning, is not upon him because he has no wood, and the general destruction because of bal yera'eh is also not upon him, since bal yera'eh has not yet taken effect at all. If so, he would sit idle. According to this, there is no difficulty at all against the Rosh, for if he destroys it by general destruction in the fifth hour, this is not called sitting idle, since he has already destroyed it from the world at the outset and has nothing to burn. The Tur's difficulties against him are removed.

<b>קודם זמן איסורו כו'. </b> לא הבנתי דעת הרב בזה דז"ל הג"מ שמביא ב"י ז"ל ריב"ק היה אוסר להשליך חמץ וראיה מירוש' דאמר לא יאכיל חמץ אפי' לכלבים אחרי' מיהו קודם איסורו יכול להשליכו כ"מ שהעורבים מצוים תל"ג לחלק בין חצר המשותפין להרבה ב"א דקרוי הפקר ובין חצר המיוחד לאחד ואינו מתישב בזה דשותפין חייבים ממש כמו אדם א' לכל מילי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Before its time of prohibition, etc.</b> I did not understand the Rav's view in this. This is the language of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the Beis Yosef brings: Rabbeinu Baruch Kohen forbade throwing chametz, and brought proof from the Yerushalmi, which says that one may not feed chametz even to other dogs. However, before its prohibition, one may throw it wherever ravens are found, since it is not yet forbidden for benefit; but if he finds it after its time of prohibition, he may not leave it there so that the ravens should take it, for the essential destruction of chametz is burning. End quote. In the next siman I will write the view of Rashi. End quote from the Beis Yosef. Now, what is written here, and if he finds chametz after its time of prohibition: if its meaning is that if he found it in the house after the time of prohibition, he may not place the chametz in his domain in a place where ravens eat it and have that be its destruction, because destruction is only by burning, then so it appears in the Beis Yosef in the next siman, where he wrote, in one explanation of Rashi's words according to the Tur's view, that removing it from his domain is sufficient after the time of prohibition, unlike the view of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that I wrote in the previous siman. This implies that this gloss also speaks of removing it from his domain to another ownerless domain. If so, one cannot settle what the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, and if he found it afterward, etc., that the ravens had not eaten it, which implies that it refers to the previous case, namely that he threw it before the prohibition and the ravens did not eat it, meaning that after he found that chametz in the place where he threw it; this contradicts his explanation in the next siman. Furthermore, according to the Shulchan Aruch's explanation, it is difficult, for it is not reasonable at all that after he threw it permissibly he would afterward become obligated, since the place is ownerless; it is as though he gave it to a non-Jew before the prohibition. Would one think that he must return and take it from the non-Jew after the prohibition? Perforce one must say that here in the Shulchan Aruch this is the explanation: the ownerless place he mentions does not mean truly ownerless, but it is his domain, and there is a place there that is ownerless for the ravens. Therefore he said that even if he found it in the place where he placed it before the prohibition, he must take it and destroy it. So too it appears well from the Levush, who wrote that although he made it ownerless, it is considered his, since it is not in the domain of others. If so, the Beis Yosef contradicts the explanation he wrote in the next siman, as I wrote. Furthermore, this explanation is not possible; for if so, the first clause of the gloss also speaks in this way, that the ravens will eat it in his domain, and nevertheless it is permitted because it is before the prohibition. This is not so, for then it contradicts an explicit Talmud at the first chapter, regarding the mouse, from which it is proven that it speaks even before the prohibition, and there is no permission on account of perhaps the mouse will eat it, for doubt does not remove certainty. Would we say here that it is permitted to throw it for this reason? The Beis Yosef already wrote in Shulchan Aruch, siman 433, that definite chametz requires checking, and the Rema challenged there from this case before us, but did not decide his view there. The main point appears to me to be that the Shulchan Aruch's ruling there is certainly true: definite chametz requires checking even before the time of prohibition. Here the Hagahos Maimoniyos does not speak of chametz in one's domain, to be lenient because mice may eat it; that is forbidden even before its prohibition unless one stands over them so they will eat it before the prohibition. Rather, the Hagahos Maimoniyos speaks where he throws it into a truly ownerless place. This he may do even close to the time of prohibition, when they certainly will not eat it before the prohibition, for what concern is it to him, since the prohibition will not be in his domain? And there is no prohibition of benefit, even to other dogs, because that applies specifically if he gives it before them after the prohibition. Therefore the latter clause of the Hagahos Maimoniyos says, and if he finds it after the time, meaning that he finds it in his house after the prohibition, he cannot exempt himself from destruction on the basis that ravens will take it in an ownerless place, even during the time of rabbinic prohibition, namely the sixth hour. Above, where it is forbidden with definite chametz, that is because it is in his domain; this is forbidden even before the prohibition, lest it remain after the prohibition. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, explained the words of the Hagahos Maimoniyos in siman 433 by distinguishing between a courtyard shared by many people, which is called ownerless, and a courtyard designated to one person. This does not settle the matter, for partners are fully obligated like one person in all matters.
