**** ENGLISH ****
To clarify the Magen Avraham’s intent, I will quote the Gemara, Berachos 29a: Rav Nachman said in the name of Shmuel that throughout the year one may recite Havineinu, except on Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov, because Havdalah must be recited in Atah Chonen. Mar Zutra challenged: Let him include in Havineinu, “Hashem, our God, Who distinguishes between sacred and mundane.” The Gemara leaves this difficult. Rabbi Bibi bar Abaye said that throughout the year a person may recite Havineinu, except during the rainy season, because the request for rain must be recited in Bircas HaShanim. Mar Zutra challenged: Let him include in “and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land,” the words “grant dew and rain.” The answer is that he may become confused. If so, the Gemara asks, regarding Havdalah in Atah Chonen he may likewise become confused. They answered that there, since it comes at the beginning of the prayer, he will not become confused. The Rashba writes that Rav Hai Gaon, the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh all ruled in accordance with Rav Nachman in the name of Shmuel. Although the Gemara concludes with a difficulty, since it did not say that his ruling was conclusively refuted, it was not rejected from halachah. This is the method followed throughout the Talmud.

ואנו תופסים כשני החומרות כשמואל ורב ביבי ופוסקים דלא יתפלל הביננו בין במ"ש וי"ט בין בימות הגשמים כן נראה לפרש דברי מ"א. אבל הב"ח כתב וז"ל אחר שכתב שיטתו בפי' דברי הרר"י הנ"ל כתב וז"ל ובהכי ניחא דדחק תלמודא גבי שאלה וקא משני הא דאין כוללים אותה בהביננו משום דאתי למטרד ולא קאמר האי טעמא דהי' נראה שהיא ברכה בפ"ע עכ"ל. לדידיה קשיא מה שהקשיתי לעיל וצל"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
Rabbeinu Yonah explains Mar Zutra’s question by saying that if Havdalah were incorporated into Havineinu, it would appear that Havdalah is an independent blessing, since a condensed form of Havdalah was instituted just as a condensed form of the other blessings was instituted. We can now understand the Magen Avraham’s question: “One might ask that regarding the seasonal request we should likewise give this reason.” Meaning, since this reason must in any event be stated regarding Havdalah, the same reason should have been stated regarding the request for rain, and there would have been no need to answer that one might become confused. Yet this is not a difficulty. First, we see that the Gemara itself did not answer Mar Zutra’s question regarding Havdalah by saying that it would appear to be an independent blessing, and instead left the matter difficult. Evidently that answer did not occur to the Gemara, or was rejected for some reason, so how can one ask why it did not offer that answer to Mar Zutra’s second question regarding rain? Moreover, when the Gemara asks, “If so, regarding Havdalah he may likewise become confused,” what is the difficulty? Perhaps that is indeed the reason, and Rav Nachman’s ruling that Havineinu is not recited on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov would thereby be explained. It is strained to say that the question means merely that the Gemara should not have left Rav Nachman’s ruling difficult, but should also have answered that confusion is the reason. It appears more likely that the Gemara’s question is based on Rabbi Bibi bar Abaye’s statement that throughout the year one may recite Havineinu except during the rainy season. Since he did not exclude Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov, he must disagree with Rav Nachman and hold that Havineinu is recited then with Havdalah included. The Gemara therefore properly asks that, if so, Havdalah too should create a concern of confusion. It answers that at the beginning of the prayer one does not become confused. Accordingly, there is no question why the Gemara did not answer that the rain request would appear to be an independent blessing, for that would revive the Gemara’s question regarding Havdalah. Rabbi Bibi evidently does not accept that concern. The Magen Avraham’s question must therefore be directed at the halachic authorities. They explain the exclusion of the rainy season as due to confusion, but since we rule like Shmuel that Havineinu is not recited on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov, we must accept Rabbeinu Yonah’s reason that Havdalah would appear to be an independent blessing. If so, they should have applied that same reason to the request for rain, giving one reason for both Havdalah and rain. Alternatively, if the Magen Avraham is questioning the Gemara itself, his question is directed to Rav Nachman in the name of Shmuel. Rav Nachman says that throughout the year one may recite Havineinu except on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov because of Havdalah. Since he did not exclude the rainy season, he evidently holds that Havineinu is recited then with the rain request included, and he is not concerned about confusion. Yet the rain request should still be impossible to include because it would appear to be an independent blessing, just as Shmuel is concerned regarding Havdalah according to Rabbeinu Yonah. According to this, Shmuel is not concerned about confusion but is concerned that it appear as an independent blessing, and therefore Havineinu is not recited on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov but is recited in the rainy season. Rabbi Bibi bar Abaye holds the reverse: he is not concerned that it appear to be an independent blessing, but he is concerned about confusion. Therefore, during the rainy season one does not recite Havineinu, while on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov one does. We adopt both stringencies, following both Shmuel and Rabbi Bibi, and rule that Havineinu is recited neither on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov nor during the rainy season. This appears to be the correct explanation of the Magen Avraham. The Bach, however, after presenting his understanding of Rabbeinu Yonah, writes that this explains why the Gemara, regarding the request for rain, was compelled to answer that it is not included in Havineinu because one may become confused, rather than giving the reason that it would appear to be an independent blessing. According to his approach, the difficulty I raised above remains and requires study.
