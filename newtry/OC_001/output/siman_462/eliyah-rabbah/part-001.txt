[ב] <b>עם מים וכו'.</b> והוא הדין נילוש תחילה עם מי פירות ואחר כך בא עליו מים קודם שנאפה או להיפך (ר"ן בתשובת נ"ט הרא"ש וסמ"ג והגהות מיימוני פרק ה'). כתב מגן אברהם אם נפל מעט פירות לתוך העיסה ולא נאפה מיד צריך עיון וברא"ש משמע דדבר מועט אינו אוסר, עד כאן, ובס"ק ג' כתב דאם נפל מים כל שהוא במי פירות בשעת לישה אסור (סמ"ג ור"ן ע"ב), ואין נראה לחלק בין מי פירות בסוף בין מים בסוף, ונראה דלעיל מיירי שנפל אחר שנילוש אבל בשעת לישה אוסר אפילו מעט ודלא כט"ז ועיין סימן תנ"ה ס"ק ט', אך צריך עיון מאי מייתי מגן אברהם ראיה מהרא"ש דקיטוף שהוא דבר מועט אינו אוסר הא כתב זה לר' עקיבא אבל הלכה כחכמים דף ל"ו דאיתא שאין לשין אין מקטפין ועיין בב"ח, ובזה מתורץ מה שהקשה בתשובת בית יעקב סימן ס"ח ועיין סימן תנ"ג ס"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
[2] <b>With water, etc.</b> And the same applies if it was kneaded first with fruit juice and afterward water came upon it before it was baked, or the reverse (Ran in the responsum of ninety of the Rosh, and Semag, and Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 5). Magen Avraham wrote: if a little fruit [juice] fell into the dough and it was not baked immediately, it requires further study; and in the Rosh it is implied that a small amount does not forbid; until here. And in s.k. 3 he wrote that if any amount of water fell into fruit juice at the time of kneading it is forbidden (Semag and Ran 72); and it does not appear correct to distinguish between fruit juice at the end and water at the end; and it appears that above he deals with where it fell after it was kneaded, but at the time of kneading even a little forbids, and not like Taz; and see siman 455 s.k. 9. But it requires further study what proof Magen Avraham brings from the Rosh that glazing, which is a small amount, does not forbid; for he wrote this according to R' Akiva, but the law is like the Sages on folio 36, where it is stated that what one does not knead one does not glaze; and see in Bach. And with this is resolved what was asked in Responsa Beis Yaakov siman 68; and see siman 453 s.k.

[ג] <b>יאפה מיד וכו'.</b> ואם לא נאפה מיד כיון שלא שהא שיעור מיל בלי עסק ולא ניכר בה שיעור חמץ וכהאי גוונא כתב גיסי בספר חק יעקב דיש להתירו בהנאה. והנה ראיה שמביא מכלבו בשם הראב"ד גבי מלח יש לחלק דיש לומר דזה גרע דהא יש פוסקים דישרוף מיד כמו שכתב הטור, גם נראה לי דעת הכלבו שם דאסור בהנאה והא דנקט באכילה היינו לאפוקי מה שכתב שם דמותר באכילה רק שאינו יוצא בליל ראשון עיין שם ודו"ק, אך יש לסמוך בזה על הרי"ף והרמב"ם ומגיד פרק ה' שפסקו דמותר ללוש דאין ממהר להחמיץ יותר משאר עיסה, ואף דלא קיימא לן כוותייהו מכל מקום יש לומר קצת כסברת תרומת הדשן בסימן קי"ג אינהו אכלי ואנן לא משהינן, ואף דמר' ירוחם משמע דמרמב"ם לא סבירא ליה כרי"ף צריך עיון, והמגיד ובית יוסף כתבו דסבירא ליה כהרי"ף כדאמרן. ועוד נראה לי דאפשר הרא"ש וטור שכתבו יאפה מיד לאו דוקא אלא כמו בלש במים לבד לאפוקי ממה שכתב גבי מי פירות דאפילו שהתה כל היום אינו מחמיץ ולא חלקו על הרי"ף אלא במה שמתיר ללוש לכתחילה ודו"ק, וכן נראה בספר צידה לדרך, ועוד דלגירסת רש"י דף ל"ו וחכמים אומרים יאכל פשיטא דמותר וכן משמע בתוס' דף ל"ה ד"ה מי פירות וכו', ואפילו באכילה היה נראה להתיר בהפסד מרובה:
**** ENGLISH ****
[3] <b>He should bake immediately, etc.</b> And if it was not baked immediately: since it did not wait the measure of a mil without activity, and the measure of chametz is not recognizable in it, and the like, my brother-in-law wrote in Sefer Chok Yaakov that one should permit it for benefit. And regarding the proof he brings from Kol Bo in the name of Raavad concerning salt, one may distinguish, for one may say that this is worse, for there are poskim who say he should burn immediately, as the Tur wrote; also it appears to me that the Kol Bo's view there is that it is forbidden for benefit, and that he mentioned eating is to exclude what he wrote there that it is permitted for eating only that one does not fulfill on the first night; see there and examine carefully. But one may rely in this on the Rif and the Rambam and Maggid chapter 5, who ruled that it is permitted to knead, for it does not hasten to leaven more than other dough; and even though we do not hold like them, nevertheless one may say somewhat like the reasoning of Terumat HaDeshen in siman 113: they eat and we do not keep; and even though from Rabbeinu Yeruham it is implied that from the Rambam he does not hold like the Rif, this requires further study; and Maggid and Beis Yosef wrote that he holds like the Rif, as we said. And furthermore it appears to me that possibly the Rosh and Tur who wrote "bake immediately" are not exact, but rather like when kneading with water alone, to exclude what he wrote regarding fruit juice that even if it waited the whole day it does not leaven; and they disagreed with the Rif only regarding that he permits to knead ab initio; examine carefully; and so it appears in Sefer Tzidah LaDerech. And furthermore, according to Rashi's version on folio 36, "and the Sages say he may eat," obviously it is permitted; and so is implied in Tosafos folio 35 s.v. Fruit juice, etc.; and even for eating it would appear to permit in a case of great loss.

[ו] <b>[לבוש] כגון יין וכו'.</b> אפילו שמרי יין (חק יעקב), וכן שמן זית ומי תפוחים ומי רימונים וכל כיוצא בהן ושאר יינות ושמנים (רמב"ם) ודבש דבורים ודלא כשלטי גיבורים כמו שכתבתי באליהו זוטא וכן הסכימו אחרונים. יין צמוקים הוא בין של ענבים שאין מחמיצין כי המים ששרו בהן הצמוקים נשתנו מברייתן (בדק הבית ומבי"ט ומהרי"ט וכנסת הגדולה), וכן דבש שעושין מצמוקים ששורין הצמוקים במים וסוחטין אותם ומבשלין אותם שהמים שבו נשתנו על ידי אור יותר משינוי שעל ידי היין (מהרי"ט), וכתב מגן אברהם וצריך עיון במי דבש שקורין מע"ד דגם כן נשתנו המים מברייתן. חיטה הנמצאת בשומן אווזא אוסר בשל"ה כיון שכבר שרוי במים וזקני הגאון זכרונו לברכה פסק להשהותה עד אחר פסח ובתשובת צמח צדק ומגן אברהם התירו באכילה, ועיין סימן תס"ז סעיף י"ד דמשמע נמי דמותר כששורין המצה במים וסוחטין אותה ואחר כך לשין אותה בשומן ונפל לתוכה חיטה יש להחמיר דנשארין בתוכה המים בעין דאי אפשר לסחטן כל כך:
**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>[Levush] For example wine, etc.</b> Even wine lees (Chok Yaakov); and likewise olive oil and apple liquid and pomegranate liquid and all of their kind, and other wines and oils (Rambam), and bee honey, and not like Shiltei Gibborim, as I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta; and so the Acharonim agreed. Raisin wine is among grape [products] that do not cause leavening, for the water in which the raisins soaked changed from their original nature (Bedek HaBayis and Mabit and Maharit and Kenesses HaGedolah); and likewise honey made from raisins, where they soak the raisins in water and squeeze them and cook them, for the water in it changed by fire more than the change through wine (Maharit). And Magen Avraham wrote: and it requires further study regarding mead called mead, for there too the water changed from its original nature. A wheat kernel found in goose fat: the Shelah forbids, since it was already soaked in water; and my grandfather the Gaon, of blessed memory, ruled to keep it until after Pesach; and in Responsa Tzemach Tzedek and Magen Avraham they permitted for eating; and see siman 467 se'if 14, where it is also implied that it is permitted; [but] when they soak the matzah in water and squeeze it and afterward knead it with fat and a wheat kernel fell into it, one should be stringent, for water remains in it visibly, for it is impossible to squeeze them so thoroughly.

[7] <b>[Levush] A little water, etc.</b> He wrote in the Shulchan Aruch: it is permitted to knead with wine even though it is impossible for him without a drop of water that falls at the time of the vintage, since the water was already nullified in the wine before they kneaded the dough; until here. And that is Hagahos Maimoniyos from Semag in Beis Yosef; and Levush who omitted it and on the contrary wrote that even a little water causes leavening appears to hold like one sage that Kenesses HaGedolah brought, who wrote from Semak that it is a solitary view and Rambam forbade; and in Kenesses HaGedolah he dealt at length to disagree with him, for Semak deals specifically at the time of vintage when it was nullified through fermentation, and "before they kneaded the dough" is not exact; and so wrote Maharit there, that specifically at the vintage they were nullified even though water is more than an imparting of taste; and so appears the conclusion of Magen Avraham. And in truth I am astonished at that sage, for in Semak folio 31 he also ruled like Rambam; and more astonishing is Kenesses HaGedolah who did not notice this; and I saw in the commentaries of Maharshal folio 66 that he wrote that Semak rules like Rambam and did not write that it is nullified except in the name of Riva, and he does not hold so; and that is forced. Also according to this it is astonishing regarding Hagahos Maimoniyos who ruled in the name of Semag that it is nullified, and likewise Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch. And also Kenesses HaGedolah's answer is not clear to me, for the language "since they were already nullified in the wine before they kneaded the dough" implies that even in absolute wine it is nullified; and so is implied in Shelah folio 141; and so my brother-in-law agreed with me in Sefer Chok Yaakov. But what he answered regarding the main difficulty, that a little water does not cause it to hasten to leaven and it is permitted to knead with it ab initio, but nevertheless the dough needs guarding, for in any case there is a concern of leavening in the place of water; and if so a wheat kernel found in it is forbidden like with water; until here are his words. And it is not clear in my eyes, for either way: if it was nullified there is no leavening in it at all, and "place of water" does not apply, for everything mixes together; and for this reason wet with wet does not revive again in siman 447 se'if 4; and from the Rosh chapter "These pass over" there is no proof at all, for he does not deal with a case where it was nullified. And furthermore it is difficult: for in Semag there it is implied that if he kneaded with fruit juice into which any amount of water mixed, he must burn it immediately; rather the main point is as I explained in Eliyahu Zuta, that this is the language of the Rambam and Semag: when do we say this? When water did not mix into them at all in the world; but if any water mixed into them they cause leavening; until here his language. And all the great authorities mentioned above understood "any amount" as a tiny amount. And in my humble opinion it is difficult: if so he should have said "even any amount" [and Maharshal copied thus, and it is not there]; also what is the language "any water in the world"? Rather it appears to me that this is its meaning: "any water in the world" means from anything called water that has the law of water, such as dew and other things in siman 466, or whether hot water or cold, drawn or natural; and for this he concluded: but if water of any amount mixed into them, that is, whatever water in the world, they cause leavening; but regarding a little that was nullified he admits that it is permitted. And likewise in the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter "They make partitions" he used the language "any amount" in the way I explained. But it requires further study in Rokeach at the end of siman 290, who forbids a drop of water in milk; and so he wrote in Terumat HaDeshen siman 113. And perhaps he deals with where water fell after the wheat fell, and in this it is forbidden like at the time of kneading above; and it still requires further study. And at first glance one should compare it to what the Rambam wrote at the end of the Laws of Tum'as Ochlin: fruit juice that mixed with water, everything is liquid and makes susceptible; until here; and in siman 466 we learned leavening from making susceptible; but Raavad there disagrees, that we follow the majority.
