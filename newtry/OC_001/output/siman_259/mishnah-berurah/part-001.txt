(כא) מותר ע"י א"י - אבל ע"י ישראל אסור לסתום דמכבה הגחלים ואע"פ שאין מתכוין לכך אסור משום פסיק רישא אבל באמירה לא"י לא קפדינן כולי האי כמ"ש סוף סימן רנ"ג. ואם אינו סותמו לגמרי אפילו ע"י ישראל שרי דאינו מכבה [אגור ומהרי"ל] וכתב המ"א דדוקא כשמניחו הרבה פתוח אבל אם מניחו רק מעט פתוח יש לאסור משום מבעיר דטבע הרוח להיות מפיח דרך נקב קטן ודמי למפוח ומבעיר יותר התנור מאלו היה פתוח לגמרי. כתב הא"ר דבמקום הדחק מותר הישראל לסתום התנור אפילו ביש גחלים לוחשות כי יש לסמוך על דעת תרומת הדשן שמיקל וס"ל דלא הוי פסיק רישא שיכבו הגחלים ע"י הסתימה ואם אש בוער בתוכו אף לתה"ד אסור דהסתימה גורם לאש שיכבה מהר. ודע דכ"ז לענין סתימת התנור אבל לענין פתיחת פי התנור לכו"ע אסור ע"י ישראל אם ידוע שיש גחלים לוחשות אפילו אם אינו שרוק בטיט דהרוח מבעיר את הגחלים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is permitted through a non-Jew.</b> A Jew, however, may not seal it, because doing so extinguishes the coals. Although he has no intention to extinguish them, it is forbidden as a psik reisha. We are not as stringent regarding instructing a non-Jew, as stated at the end of siman 253. If he does not close the oven completely, even a Jew may do so, because he is not extinguishing the fire, as stated by the Agur and Maharil. The Magen Avraham writes that this applies only when he leaves a substantial opening. If he leaves only a small opening, it is forbidden because the nature of wind is to blow forcefully through a narrow opening. This resembles bellows and causes the oven to burn more strongly than if it were fully open. Eliyah Rabbah writes that in pressing circumstances a Jew may seal the oven even when glowing coals remain, relying on Terumas HaDeshen, who is lenient because he maintains that extinguishing the coals through sealing is not inevitable. If a fire is burning inside, however, even Terumas HaDeshen agrees that it is forbidden, because sealing causes the fire to die more quickly. Know that all this concerns sealing the oven. Opening the oven is forbidden according to all opinions when it is known that glowing coals remain inside, even if the opening is not sealed with clay, because the wind fans the coals.

(כד) אסור לסתום התנור וכו' - דע"י סתימת פיו ואפילו בלא טיחה בטיט מוסיף חום ומתבשל במהרה ואסור אפילו אם התנור גרוף וקטום מן הגחלים. ואם התנור אינו גרוף וקטום מן הגחלים אסור לישראל לסתמו אף אם הקדרות כבר מבושלות כ"צ מבעוד יום דבלילה מסתמא עדיין לא נתאכלו הגחלים ולוחשות הן ויש חשש כיבוי ע"י הסתימה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to seal the oven, etc.</b> Closing the opening, even without smearing it with clay, increases the heat and causes the food to cook more quickly. It is therefore forbidden even if the oven was swept free of coals and its remaining coals were covered with ash. If the oven was not swept or covered, a Jew may not seal it even when all the pots were fully cooked before nightfall, because at night the coals have presumably not yet been consumed and remain glowing, so that sealing raises a concern of extinguishing.

דסמיכה לא אסרו בזה ועל התנור נהגו העולם היתר אם לא הוסק עדיין אבל אם הוסק ויש אש בתוכו לא ואם נתן לבנה או ד"א על התנור להפסק ואח"כ נותן הקדרה עליה שרי. וכ"ז בתבשיל חם קצת אבל בתבשיל קר לגמרי אפילו קודם שהוסק אסור אפילו לסמוך כ"א ע"י א"י קודם שהוסק ולאחר שהוסק אפילו ע"י א"י אסור דיש בזה חשש בישול. ולצורך חולה שאין בו סכנה או לצורך קטן שאין לו מה לאכול מותר לחמם ע"י א"י אפילו בתוך התנור. ודבר שאין בו רוטב אפילו קר כחם דמי דבדבר יבש שנצטנן קי"ל דאין בישול אחר בישול. ופירות חיין אף שנאכלים כך וכן מיני משקין דינם כתבשיל שיש בו רוטב ונצטנן לגמרי דשייך בהם בישול. וליתן דבר מה על הקדרה הקבוע בתנור דינו כמו על התנור ובתוך הקאכלי"ן. ולתת לתוך המים שבקדרה ההיא קנקן עם משקין פשיטא דאסור. וישראל אסור ליתן מים לתוך הקדרה ההיא בשבת אפילו קודם שהוסק אך אם נתנם הא"י קודם שהוסק שרי ליהנות מהם. גם <b>And even through a non-Jew it is forbidden.</b> It is forbidden to instruct him to seal the oven, even without smearing clay, because this directly hastens the cooking. All the more so is it forbidden to instruct him to smear clay over the closure, for this involves the melacha of smoothing, and it is forbidden even if the oven had already been closed. After the fact, if the non-Jew sealed the oven, the food is not forbidden, because his action did not benefit it greatly, since it would have cooked eventually in any case. The later authorities write that even in summer, when people commonly leave the synagogue while it is still broad daylight, before nightfall and before bein hashmashos, so that a Jew's prohibition is only rabbinic because he has accepted Shabbos, one must nevertheless avoid instructing a non-Jew then to seal the oven. Otherwise, he may come to instruct him during winter, when people leave the synagogue close to nightfall, and by the time he washes his hands, recites Kiddush, and speaks to the non-Jew about the oven, it will already be fully night. The principal laws of shehiyah and hatmanah, based on the Magen Avraham, Tosefes Shabbos, and the other later authorities, are summarized here. In our times we place pots uncovered inside the oven and do not surround their walls with insulating material. They therefore have only the law of shehiyah, not hatmanah in a substance that adds heat. Even if a pot stands on the coals, as long as it remains exposed above it is not considered hatmanah, as the Rama writes at the end of siman 253, se'if 1. Therefore, if the food reached ma'achal ben Derusai before nightfall, meaning approximately half cooked, although some say one third cooked, it may remain even if the oven was not swept free of coals and the coals were not covered with ash. Meat may likewise be left if it was placed raw in the pot immediately before nightfall, meaning before sunset. For roast meat, baked goods, legumes, and pie, however, placing them there raw does not suffice, and they must reach ma'achal ben Derusai before Shabbos. If a raw piece of meat is placed among them immediately before nightfall, they may remain even at the beginning of cooking. In an oven sealed with clay, everything is permitted. If one knows that glowing coals remain inside, the oven may be opened only by a non-Jew and not by a Jew, because opening it fans the fire and this is a psik reisha. If the coals surround the pot, only a non-Jew should remove it. If the pot stands directly on the coals, a Jew may remove it when there is no other way, provided he removes it gently and does not shake the coals. The same precautions apply on Friday night when removing the pot from the oven. Therefore, close to nightfall, when leaving a cooked pot in the oven for Friday night, people take care to separate it slightly from the fire before Shabbos so that it can later be removed without concern. On Shabbos it is permitted to place a hot, fully cooked dish next to the oven on its ledge, even if the oven was fired and was neither swept nor covered, because merely placing it alongside was not forbidden in this case. The common practice is to permit placing it on top of the oven if the oven has not yet been fired. If the oven has been fired and a fire burns inside, it may not be placed there. If a brick or another object was placed on the oven as a separation, the pot may be placed upon it. All this applies when the dish remains somewhat warm. A dish that is completely cold may not even be placed next to the oven before it is fired, except through a non-Jew. After the oven is fired, it is forbidden even through a non-Jew because of cooking. For a sick person not in mortal danger, or for a child who has nothing to eat, it may be warmed by a non-Jew even inside the oven. A dry food without liquid has the same status when cold as when hot, because regarding a dry food that cooled we rule that there is no prohibition of cooking after cooking. Raw fruit that is eaten as it is, and beverages, have the same status as a cooked food containing liquid that cooled completely, because cooking applies to them. Placing something on a pot fixed inside the oven has the same status as placing it on the oven or inside a tiled stove. It is plainly forbidden to place a pitcher of liquid into the water contained in that fixed pot. A Jew may not pour water into that pot on Shabbos even before the oven is fired. If a non-Jew poured the water in before the oven was fired, one may benefit from it. On erev Shabbos close to nightfall, a Jew may likewise not pour water into that pot when the oven is already fired, unless the water can become heated to half its intended heat before nightfall. In pressing circumstances, where he forgot to add the water earlier and fears that the pot will crack from the heat, there may be room to be lenient, provided he pours it in before sunset, as I wrote at the end of siman 254. On Shabbos one may not wrap a pot of hot food in pillows and cushions when this constitutes the initial hatmanah. Returning existing insulation, however, is permitted. This restriction applies specifically when the food remains in the same pot in which it was cooked. If he transferred it to another pot, he may wrap that second pot.
