**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se’if 3: Some say that even, etc.</b> This is also implied by the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 4: If he removed it before daytime ended, he may return it before daytime ended; if he removed it after nightfall, he may return it after nightfall. If he removed it before daytime ended and the day then became sanctified, R. Ba said in the name of Rav Yehudah: If the cavity was damaged, it is forbidden. Reish Lakish said: From R. Yehudah you can resolve the question. Just as he forbids it when the cavity was damaged, because this is considered insulating initially, so too, if it was removed before daytime ended, it is forbidden, because returning it is considered an initial act.

<b>ס"ז מותר לסתור.</b> שבת מ"ח וכי מה בין זו כו' ועיין רש"י ד"ה מגופה כו' ואף ע"ג דל"ד דשם כלי אבל בקרקע אמרי' בי"ט ל"ד ב' חותמות שבקרקע כו' ה"מ בדבר שעומד להתקיים כמ"ש בסי' שי"ד ס"י דגם בקרקע אינו רק גזירה דרבנן כמ"ש רש"י בעירובין ל"ה א' ד"ה ובעי כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se’if 7: It is permitted to dismantle.</b> See Shabbos 48a: “What difference is there between this,” etc. See Rashi, s.v. “the stopper,” etc. Although the cases are not entirely comparable, because there the discussion concerns a utensil, while regarding something attached to the ground Beitzah 34b states, “Seals attached to the ground,” etc., that applies only to something intended to remain in place, as stated in siman 314, se’if 10. Even regarding the ground, the prohibition is only a rabbinic decree, as Rashi states in Eruvin 35a, s.v. “and he requires,” etc.
