<b>אבל שומע כו'. </b>חגיגה ב' ב' גיטין מ"א א' ואע"ג דאמרינן האלם לא יתרום מפרש בירושלמי משום ברכה ואחר א"י לברך להם וכמ"ש בהג"א ריש חולין בד"ה ובא"ז כת' האלם כו' ואין אחר כו' אבל כו' ואע"פ שכ' שם מיהו א"י אם ה"ה כו' מ"מ כבר כתבו כל הפוסקים דה"ה באלם ודוקא כשאחר ג"כ נתחייב ולכן כאן יוכל לתקוע לכתחלה להוציא רבים י"ח כה"ג דאחר יברך ועי"ד סי' א' ס"ז וש"ך שם ס"ק י"ב ומשום הכי תנן דלא יתרום משום דאין מצוי אחר שיתחייב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But one who hears, etc.</b> Chagigah 2b; Gittin 41a. And even though we say a mute person may not separate terumah, the Yerushalmi explains that this is because of the blessing, and another cannot recite the blessing for them; and as is written in Hagahot Asheri at the beginning of Chullin, s.v. "And in the Or Zarua he wrote: a mute person, etc., and another may not, etc., but, etc." And even though he wrote there: however, I do not know if the same law applies, etc. nevertheless all the poskim have already written that the same law applies regarding a mute person; and specifically where another is also obligated. And therefore here he may blow l'chatchila to discharge the many of their obligation, in such a case where another will recite the blessing. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 1, se'if 7, and the Shach there, s.k. 12. And for this reason the Mishnah taught that he may not separate terumah, because another who is obligated is not commonly available.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise another, etc.</b> The Rosh in the name of the Ravyah, not like the Baal HaItur. And even though it is a shevus, nevertheless in such a case it is permitted, just as they permitted leaning [on a korban] for them, even though they rested their hands [upon it]; nevertheless it is similar to the Temple service, all the more so here, where it is not melachah. And see what I wrote in siman 254, se'if 5; and in Chullin 84b it is implied that there is a prohibition to blow for women, for it is a shevus. And see Tosafos there at the end of s.v. "Tekios," etc., only that according to Tosafos' first answer the prohibition is in carrying out; but the Ravyah holds that all this is according to R' Yehudah, whereas according to R' Yosi everything is permitted, as is written there: "And R' Yosi follows his reasoning," etc. And this is what is written in the Rosh of Rosh Hashanah there; and the Ravyah wrote: since, etc. And it is also permitted to carry out, etc. But that which he wrote "and so it appears to me that it is no worse," etc. requires further study. And see Tosafos of Chullin 85a, s.v. "Women," etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the custom.</b> This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, Rosh Hashanah 33: that even though she is exempt, nevertheless she may recite the blessing; for if not so, what did Rav Yosef say ("I would make a festive day for the rabbis") would he then forfeit the blessings of all the mitzvos and the other blessings?
