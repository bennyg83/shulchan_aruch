[4] <b>Levush: That we do not, etc.</b> This is not the real reason. Even in their time, when they would withdraw their hands from bread, wine drunk before the meal exempted wine drunk after the meal. His words are also difficult in light of siman 177, seif 2, where according to the Tur even nowadays people withdraw from bread at large meals. The Tur is also difficult, for he omitted what the Rosh wrote, that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not exclusive and that the matter depends on a person’s intention. Lechem Chamudos, folio 52, likewise expresses surprise at the Tur. In my humble opinion, however, there is no difficulty. This is included in what the Tur cites from the Raavad, that in places where wine is common there is no distinction between Shabbos, Yom Tov, and weekdays. It therefore follows that everything depends on the person’s intention. This is explicit in Pesachim, chapter Arvei Pesachim, and in Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 142. After they write that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not exclusive, they cite the Raavad in the same manner. The Levush may be reconciled as follows. His reason, that we do not withdraw from bread, was written regarding the latter clause, that wine drunk during the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal. In truth, at large meals where people withdraw from bread and drink wine, the wine drunk during the meal does not exempt the later wine; only wine drunk before the meal does so, because both are drunk for the sake of drinking. The Tur’s wording is: “Wine drunk before the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal.” This means a person who regularly establishes a session to drink wine after finishing his meal but before Bircas HaMazon. It appears to me that this necessarily refers to one who has withdrawn from bread and drinks wine, for if he had not withdrawn from bread, even wine drunk during the meal would exempt it. Rather, the case is one who withdrew from bread, and the Tur teaches that he must regularly establish such a drinking session after finishing his meal. If he does not regularly do so, but it happened on one occasion that he withdrew from bread and drank wine, the earlier wine certainly does not exempt it unless, when drinking before the meal, he intended to withdraw from bread later and drink wine, as explained above. This excludes Beit Yosef’s strained interpretation of the Tur, which does not withstand examination.

**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>Wine of Havdalah, etc.</b> Bach writes that according to Tosafos and Mordechai, even one who drinks wine before washing does not thereby exempt later wine. Perishah, however, writes that they refer specifically to Havdalah, which is not for the sake of the meal. Regarding ordinary wine drinking, although their practice was to drink only after washing, if one happened to drink before washing, it does exempt the later wine. Magen Avraham rules this way, and Beit Yosef also implies it. It appears to me that there is proof from the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Eilu Devarim: Beis Hillel say that one first mixes the cup and then washes his hands. Rashi explains that this means the wine served before the meal is mixed and drunk, and only afterward one washes his hands. This appears to refer to the Mishnah that wine drunk before the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal. The Mishnah is thus dealing with precisely this case. However, Tosafos on folio 42, s.v. veRav Sheshes, write that the wine of Havdalah is no worse than wine drunk before the meal, which exempts later wine. This supports Bach’s reading. Perishah further writes that Tosafos’ statement, that nowadays people drink the wine before the meal after washing, is not exact; it means after Hamotzi, since one may not interrupt with wine drinking between washing and Hamotzi. Even according to the Geonim cited above in siman 166, who permit an interruption by speaking, this refers only to speech, not an act of drinking. Magen Avraham, however, cites Hagahos Mordechai to Arvei Pesachim: “One might ask how it is permitted to interrupt with Havdalah between washing and Hamotzi. The answer is that it is not a diversion of attention, since he intends to eat.” According to this, even ordinary wine drinking may be discussed as occurring between washing and Hamotzi. Beit Yosef likewise writes this in the name of Maharam, and Tosafos there also imply that Havdalah and ordinary wine drinking are equivalent in this respect. Nevertheless, Bach writes in seif katan 8 that it is permitted to interrupt and recite a blessing over a drink between washing and Hamotzi because he does so for the sake of a mitzvah, to avoid the doubt discussed below in seif 4. This implies that otherwise one may not interrupt. It appears that all agree that if he recited the blessing after Hamotzi, it exempts the later wine. For this reason, it appears to me that at large meals the practice is to recite the blessing over wine immediately after Hamotzi, before eating, in order to exempt the wine drunk after the meal, as I wrote in seif katan 4. I also see support for Perishah from the Gemara on folio 52, where Beis Hillel challenges Beis Shammai, who say that one first washes and then mixes the cup: Is it not necessary to proceed directly from washing to the meal? See there. However, according to Tosafos in Pesachim 106b, who explain that the case concerns mixing with hot water and requires care not to diminish the quantity, there is no proof. See Birchas Avraham, folio 201, whose words are difficult in light of the Tosafos cited above. Examine carefully, for I have been brief. See also below, at the end of siman 211 and siman 231.

[7] <b>He washed his hands, etc.</b> This is not exact; the same applies if he has already established himself for the meal. Tosafos, s.v. ba lahem, imply this, for although they washed only one hand and must wash again, the earlier wine nevertheless exempts the later wine. Magen Avraham. This is difficult, for I wrote that Magen Avraham distinguishes between Havdalah and ordinary wine drinking, while Tosafos there discuss ordinary wine drinking. Moreover, Tosafos provide no proof. They say only that the wine exempts wine drunk after washing both hands, which is also before the meal; since the wine drunk after washing both hands exempts even wine drunk after the meal, this proves nothing further. Examine carefully. In addition, the phrase “one who recites Havdalah at the table” implies that even though he has established himself for the meal, the law still requires that he have washed his hands. Although Taz writes in siman 299 that “at the table” is not literal and means in the same room, he concludes that the reason is that the person intends to drink later during the meal that will follow. See there. Tosafos on folio 42 likewise write, “Since he washed and intends to eat and drink,” implying that if he did not wash, even though he intends to eat and drink, the earlier wine does not exempt the later wine. Perhaps merely establishing oneself for the meal is ineffective. See there. This requires study.
