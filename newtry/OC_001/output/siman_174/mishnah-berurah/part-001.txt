**** ENGLISH ****
(3) All kinds of beverages. This applies provided that they were before him on the table when he recited the blessing over the wine. If they were not before him at the time of the blessing, then even if they were brought while the wine was still before him, this does not suffice and he must bless over them. Some are lenient even when they were not before him, provided that he had them in mind. All this applies when he did not establish himself to drink wine, but intended to drink only this one cup. If he established himself to drink wine, several later authorities agree that the wine exempts even beverages that were not before him when he blessed, provided that they were brought while the wine was still before him. If they were brought only after he finished drinking the wine, he must bless over them, as Maharam Banet writes in his commentary, unless he had them in mind before completing his drinking. Ideally, it is always preferable that the other beverages be before him when he blesses over the wine. Know also that if he recited Kiddush over wine and discharged others with his blessing, but they did not taste from the Kiddush cup and now wish to drink other beverages, they must bless over those beverages even if they were before them when he blessed over the wine. The reason wine exempts other beverages is that they are subordinate to it, and this rationale does not apply to one who does not drink the wine. See Biur Halachah for further discussion.

**** ENGLISH ****
(7) During the meal. The same wine also exempts wine drunk after the meal but before Bircas HaMazon, in a place where people customarily drink wine after finishing their food, or when he explicitly intended to do so. If, however, he had no wine before the meal and blessed over wine during the meal, he must bless again over wine drunk after the meal. Wine during the meal comes only to settle the food in his stomach and is not sufficiently significant to exempt wine after the meal, which comes for drinking and enjoyment. Beit Yosef writes that if he is thirsty because of his eating, then even if much time has elapsed after the meal, so long as he has not recited Bircas HaMazon the wine is considered wine during the meal, since it comes to settle the food. Rashba also somewhat implies that if he drinks immediately after eating, it is presumed to come for that purpose. In practice, this law is uncommon among us, because we do not withdraw from bread until Bircas HaMazon, and everything is considered part of the meal. Consequently, wine during the meal exempts wine afterward, since both serve to settle the food.

(18) He did not intend, etc. Meaning, he intended to drink only that cup and no more, but afterward reconsidered and wished to drink more. Taz writes that the same applies to eating. For example, if he bought one loaf for himself intending to eat it all, blessed on that basis, and afterward desired more and sent someone to buy another loaf, he must recite Hamotzi again. His actions prove that he initially intended to eat only the loaf he purchased, for otherwise he would have bought more, and only afterward did he desire additional food. If, however, he has bread at home, cuts a piece to eat, and afterward wishes to cut another piece, this is not considered a new decision. People commonly think at the time of the blessing that one piece will suffice and then take more when they see that they are not satisfied.

Magen Avraham, however, writes that during the meal no blessing is required, because it then comes to stimulate the appetite and is therefore also considered to come because of the meal. In truth, it would have been proper to follow the Mechaber’s advice for other beverages, namely to bless over a small amount of liquor before washing with intent to exempt what he drinks during the meal, because there are differing opinions. The world, however, is accustomed to be lenient, and there is basis for this practice. All this applies during the meal. If he drinks liquor after the meal, when his sole intention is certainly to digest the food, all agree that it requires a blessing. Later authorities. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he ate a fatty food and takes a little liquor to remove the greasiness from his mouth, and this is his regular practice, the liquor is subordinate to the food. Therefore, even after completing the meal, he need not bless. Chayei Adam writes that coffee drunk after the meal is only for digestion and differs from other beverages, so it requires a first blessing. This is not conclusive, and it is proper to bless first over a small amount of sugar in order to exempt the coffee. All this applies only on weekdays. On Shabbos and Yom Tov morning, the issue does not arise, for he certainly already recited Kiddush over a cup. If he recited Kiddush over wine, the wine exempts all beverages, under the conditions explained above in seif katan 3. If he recited Kiddush over beer or the like, he recited Shehakol, which automatically exempts the coffee as well.
