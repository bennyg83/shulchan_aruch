**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And Maharam, etc.</b> And so wrote the Ran, etc. The wording is not exact, for Maharam and the Ran did not prophesy in one style. Maharam wrote as follows: the Sages upheld their words through sit and do not act, even to uproot a Torah matter, as we find regarding shofar-blowing on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos, etc. End quote; he elaborated there. But he did not give the reason that Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment. From Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos he brought proof only that the Sages have power, but there the reason is lest one carry, etc., whereas here there is another reason. The Ran wrote nothing regarding chametz, only in tractate Rosh Hashanah, chapter 4, folio 32, on that which we learned in the Mishnah: a shofar of Rosh Hashanah, one may not clear rubble from over it and one may not carry it beyond the techum. The Gemara concludes that the reason is that shofar is a positive commandment, and Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment. About this the Ran wrote that although this is only a shevus, nevertheless they strengthened, etc. It appears to me that with chametz, etc.; for if not, chametz too has a positive commandment and a negative commandment, the positive commandment of tashbisu se'or and the negative commandment of bal yera'eh, etc. If so, the positive and negative commandments of chametz should override the positive and negative commandments of Yom Tov. Rather, perforce with chametz the reason is that at the time he uproots the prohibition he does not fulfill the positive commandment. According to this, perforce chametz is not considered a positive commandment of the public, for with a positive commandment of the public we do not require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. See what I wrote below regarding the words of the Shelah.

<b>And this requires examination, for here too, etc.</b> For he speaks after its forbidden time. That is to say, it is impossible to give it as a gift to a non-Jew, for if so that would have been enough; since it is after its forbidden time, it is forbidden to give it as a gift, for it is forbidden for benefit. Perforce the intent is that the non-Jew remove it and destroy it. If so, with chametz too it is not at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. There is a further difficulty from what I proved, etc.; that is to say, as the Shelah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, that with a positive commandment of the public we do not require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. However, from this there is no proof, for the Ran himself wrote there about the Mishnah, where the Talmud gave the reason for shofar because Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, as follows: it stated the true reason; however, I do not need this, for even if Yom Tov were only a negative commandment, the preparations for shofar would not come and override it, because at the time he uproots the negative commandment he does not fulfill the positive commandment. End quote. Thus, perforce the Ran does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam, and even with a positive commandment of the public we specifically require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition. Rather, the difficulty is from the words of the Talmud itself, for it gave the reason that Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, etc.; if so, even where it is at the time he uproots, etc., it nevertheless does not override the negative commandment of Yom Tov.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Furthermore, according to him, even through a Jew, etc.</b> One cannot say that the Shelah permitted only through a non-Jew because it is a shevus of a shevus, unlike through a Jew, where it is one shevus. This is not so, for according to the reason the Shelah gave, that it comes, etc., and overrides a rabbinic negative commandment, this applies even to one shevus. Furthermore, perforce what the Shelah wrote, although regarding shofar telling, etc., does not apply, means according to Tosafos, as the Shelah wrote there explicitly, as follows: regarding shofar on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos, shofar does not override even a shevus of a shevus, as the Tur, Orach Chayim, at the end of siman 586, wrote in the name of Tosafos, because there it is different, since at the time, etc. End quote. But we do not rule this way at the end of siman 586, and a shevus of a shevus is permitted in a place of the mitzvah of shofar; see there. Rather, the Shelah's intent is to permit chametz according to everyone, even according to Tosafos, who are stringent regarding shofar. According to Tosafos there is no distinction between one shevus and a shevus of a shevus; if so, the same should be true that it is permitted even through a Jew, although there is only one shevus. Still, one might say that perhaps the Shelah permitted specifically through a non-Jew because in his view, when the non-Jew removes it, it is considered at the time he uproots the prohibition, unlike a Jew, where it is not at the time he uproots, etc. This is not so, for certainly it did not escape the Shelah that through the non-Jew's removal he has not yet fulfilled destruction, but only afterward, when the non-Jew throws it into the sea or the like. Nevertheless, he holds that it is considered at the time he uproots, etc., because the removal is the beginning of the destruction, unlike clearing the rubble for shofar-blowing, where one cannot say that the clearing is the beginning of the mitzvah of blowing. But the Magen Avraham disputes this reasoning above: it is not called fulfilling the positive commandment until the time of destruction, and removal is not part of the destruction at all. Here too it is not at the time, etc., just as with shofar. According to this, he properly challenged the Shelah here: according to the Shelah, who holds that in such a case it is at the time he uproots, etc., because removal is the beginning of destruction, the same should be true that through a Jew it should be permitted, for even with a Jew one can say that removing it in order to throw it into the sea is the beginning of destruction and is at that time, etc.

(ס"ק ד) דינו כח"ה ט' יטלנו לים ואם אפשר להשליכו לבית הכס' וכמ"ש מ"א סי' תמ"ה ס"ק ב' עדיף טפי לענ"ד דלים מלבד איסור טלטול איכא איסור הוצאה לים דהוי כרמלית ואע"ג דאמרינן מתוך בהוצאה כדאיתא רסי' תק"ח מ"מ הא לדעת הכ"מ שהביא פה ס"ק ג' זה לא מיקרי צורך קצת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if katan 4: Its law is like Chol HaMoed.</b> That is, he should take it to the sea. If it is possible to throw it into the privy, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 445, s.k. 2, that is better in my humble opinion, for with the sea, besides the prohibition of moving, there is the prohibition of carrying out to the sea, which is a karmelis. Although we say the principle of since regarding carrying, as stated at the beginning of siman 508, nevertheless according to the Kesef Mishneh, which he cited here in s.k. 3, this is not called even some need.
