<b>ויבערנו מיד</b>. נ"ל דיברך על ביעור חמץ אף על פי שכבר ביטל מ"מ מברך על תקנת חכמים וכ"ש  אם נתחמצה לו עיסה בפסח שזה לא היה בכלל הביטול שיברך על ביעורה ודוקא בחמץ גמור אבל חטה שנמצאת בתבשיל דאינה חמץ גמור נ"ל דלא יברך עליה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should destroy it immediately.</b> It appears to me that he recites the blessing over destroying chametz. Although he already nullified it, nevertheless he recites a blessing over the enactment of the Sages. All the more so if dough became chametz for him on Pesach, for that was not included in the nullification, he should recite a blessing over its destruction. This applies specifically to actual chametz, but with a wheat kernel found in a cooked dish, which is not actual chametz, it appears to me that he should not recite a blessing over it.

<b>שלא יוכל לטלטלו</b>. דמוקצ' הוא דהא אסור בהנא' (תו' פ"ק דכתובו') ואף על גב שלא הי'  בכלל הביטול כגון שנתחמץ בי"ט ועובר על בל יראה דאוריי' ומוקצ' הוי דרבנן כיון דשב ואל תעש' הוא אסרוהו וכיון דרצונו מקרי עשה דרבים דאל"כ חמץ נמי עשה ול"ת הוא ולידחי עשה ול"ת די"ט מיהו יש לו' כמ"ש רש"י והרי"ף פ"ב דב"מ דלענין לדחות לא מצרפי' לל"ת ע"ש: ובשל"ה כתב דיוציא החמץ ע"י עכו"ם דאתי עשה דרבנן דתשביתו ודחי ל"ת והפוסקים והטעם נ"ל כמ"ש התו' דף כ"ט שהמשהה חמצו ודעתו לבערו אינו עובר באותו שהי' וכו' וטעמא משום דלא יראה ניתק לעשה ולכן אינו עובר כשמבערו לבסוף וכ"כ דף צ"ה וא"כ א"צ לדוחק הטעמים הנ"ל: ונ"ל דשרי גשמים מבערו בי"ט כיון שלא היה בכלל הביטול וכן נוהגין העולם ותבשיל שנמצא בו חטה אפי' בי"ט שני ישהנו עד ח"ה כיון שאינו חמץ גמור ואם נמצא חטה בתבשיל בז' של פסח יבערנו מיד דאם ישהנו עד שנכנס יום האחרון יהי' מותר להשהותו ונמצא שהאיסור חוזר להיתרו עכ"ל ועססי' תמ"ז מ"ש לבער חמץ גמור בי"ט זה הוא מחלוק' הפוסקים ע' בטור והיכא דנהוג נהוג ובמקום שאין מנהג שב ואל תעשה עדיף גם מ"ש שבז' יבערנו כו' נ"ל שהוא סברא רחוקה שיעשה איסור ויבערו בי"ט משום שלא יחזור להתירו ונ"ל טעם אחר דהא כתבתי בשם התוספות דהמשהה כדי לבערו אינו עובר עליו אבל הכא משהה אותו כדי לקיימו דהא לא יבערנו אח"כ לכן יבערנו בז' ע"י עכו"ם וכ"מ מדכ' בסי' תס"ז ואם נמצא בח' מות' להשהותו משמע דבז' אסור להשותו /להשהותו/:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For he cannot move it.</b> For it is muktzeh, since it is forbidden for benefit. Tosafos, first chapter of Kesubos. Even though it was not included in the nullification, such as where it became chametz on Yom Tov and he transgresses bal yera'eh according to Torah law, while muktzeh is rabbinic, since this is a matter of sit and do not act, the Sages forbade it. Since his desire was to destroy it but the Sages forbade him, he does not transgress it, for he is prevented. The Rambam answered that they did not permit moving it in order to destroy it because at the time he moves it, he has not yet fulfilled the positive mitzvah of tashbisu until the time of burning. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3. Maharam of Lublin, siman 20, wrote similarly, and so wrote the Ran regarding shofar, for we learned at the end of Rosh Hashanah that one may not clear rubble from over it. What is the reason? Shofar is a positive mitzvah, and Yom Tov has a negative commandment and a positive commandment, and a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment and a positive commandment. Since according to Torah law it does not override, the same applies to rabbinic muktzeh, for they strengthened their words like Torah law. End quote. The Shelah asked there: why must one say that Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment? Even a negative commandment alone also should not be overridden, since at the time he uproots the prohibition he does not fulfill the positive commandment. Rabbeinu Tam answered that a positive commandment of the public overrides a negative commandment even though at the time he uproots the prohibition he does not fulfill the positive commandment. End quote. It appears to me that chametz is not called a positive commandment of the public; specifically shofar, where an individual enables the public to fulfill their obligation, is called a positive commandment of the public. For if not, chametz too is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and should override the positive and negative commandments of Yom Tov. However, one can say as Rashi and the Rif wrote in chapter 2 of Bava Metzia, that for purposes of overriding, we do not combine them with the negative commandment; see there. The Shelah wrote that one should remove the chametz through a non-Jew, for the rabbinic positive commandment of tashbisu comes and overrides the rabbinic negative commandment of telling a non-Jew, although regarding shofar we do not permit telling a non-Jew, because there it is different, since at the time he uproots the prohibition he does not fulfill the positive commandment, unlike here. End quote. This requires examination, for here too, although he gives it to the non-Jew, he still does not fulfill the positive commandment until the non-Jew destroys it from the world, since we are speaking after the time it became forbidden. There is a further difficulty, for I proved above that regarding shofar the reason is not because at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. Furthermore, according to him, even through a Jew it should be permitted to throw it into the sea, since there is only a rabbinic prohibition. Therefore it appears to me that he should cover it with a utensil, as is implied by the Gemara and the halachic decisors. The reason appears to me as Tosafos wrote on folio 29, that one who keeps his chametz while intending to destroy it does not transgress during that delay, etc.; the reason is that bal yera'eh is rectified through a positive commandment, and therefore he does not transgress when he destroys it at the end. So too they wrote on folio 95. If so, there is no need for the forced reasons above. It appears to me that it is permitted to throw it into the sea through a non-Jew, for this is a shevus of a shevus, which is permitted in a place of mitzvah, as written in siman 325 se'if 3, siman 444, siman 459 se'if 4, siman 587 se'if 21, and siman 307. But scattering it to the wind may be a Torah labor, as written at the end of siman 319. Nevertheless, all the halachic decisors wrote to cover it with a utensil, and Maharam of Lublin also wrote that this was his custom. Perhaps we are concerned that the non-Jew will not crumble it according to the law; and further, the main destruction is burning; and further, the non-Jew may say that he is doing sorcery, as in siman 433 se'if 7. The Bach ruled that actual chametz, such as flour on which rain fell, should be destroyed on Yom Tov since it was not included in the nullification, and this is the common practice. But a cooked dish in which a wheat kernel was found, even on the second day of Yom Tov, should be kept until Chol HaMoed, since it is not actual chametz. If a wheat kernel was found in a cooked dish on the seventh day of Pesach, he should destroy it immediately, for if he keeps it until the final day begins, it will be permitted to keep it, and the prohibition will be found returning to permission. End quote. See the end of siman 467. What he wrote about destroying actual chametz on Yom Tov is a dispute among the halachic decisors; see the Tur. Where the custom is practiced, it is practiced, but in a place without a custom, passive inaction is preferable. Also what he wrote that on the seventh day he should destroy it, etc., appears to me to be a distant reasoning, that one should commit a prohibition and destroy it on Yom Tov so that it should not return to permission. Another reason appears to me: I wrote in the name of Tosafos that one who keeps it in order to destroy it does not transgress, but here he keeps it in order to preserve it, for he will not destroy it afterward. Therefore he should destroy it on the seventh day through a non-Jew. So too it is implied from what he wrote in siman 467: if it was found on the eighth day, it is permitted to keep it, which implies that on the seventh day it is forbidden to keep it.

<b>גם לשרפו במקומו אסור</b>. וא"ת הא ק"ו ומה טלטול דרבנן אסור ק"ו הבערה דאוריית' וא"כ מאי גם וי"ל כמש"ל דשאני הכא דבעידנא דמיעקר לאו מקיים עשה וה"א דשרי קמ"ל דאסור וכ' הכ"מ כיון דלית ביה צורך היום כלל לא אמרינן מתוך כמ"ש סי' תקי"ד ס"ה ועוד דלא אמרי' מתוך אלא בהנאה השוה לכל הגוף ובזה אין הנא' לגוף כמש"ל סי' תקי"א ואף על גב דאיכא מצוה בשריפתו אפ"ה אסור מידי דהוי אשריפת קדשים דאין דוחה י"ט ע"כ ובתו' פ"ק דכתובות לא משמע כן ומהר"ם לובלין כ' הטעם אף על גב דקי"ל כלי ניטל  לצורך דבר שאינו ניטל היינו דבר שיש תורת כלי עליו אבל עצים ואש שאין תורת כלי עליהם אין נטלין לצורך חמץ עכ"ל עסי' תק"ב מיהו בתו' פסחים ד' ה' ע"ב משמע דמותר לשרפו כיון דיש מצוה בשריפתו מקרי צורך היום וצ"ל דזה כהס"ד דאך ביום הראשון תשביתו היינו בי"ט אבל להמסקנא ביום הראשון היינו מעי"ט א"כ אסור לשורפו בי"ט ע' בתשובת הרשב"א סי' ע"א: כתב ב"י בשם הריב"ש דאסור לטלטל החמץ משום דכל דבר האסור באכילה אפי' מדרבנן אסור לטלטלו בשבת וה"ה בי"ט ולכן אין מהפכין החלבים עסי' תצ"ט פ"ד עכ"ל הריב"ש וכ"כ הרמב"ם פכ"ה משבת פירות שאסורים לאכול כגון טבל כו' אסור לטלטל עכ"ל וא"כ בע"פ שחל בשבת אסור לטלטל חמץ בשעה ה' וכ"ה ברמב"ם ספ"ג מחמץ שכ' חמץ אחר ד' שעות כופה עליו כלי וצ"ע אטו מי גרע מנבילה שנתנבלה בשבת דשרי לטלטל דחזי' להשליכה לכלבים ולכן נ"ל דדוק' פירות טבל אסור לטלטל או חלבים דלאו דעתי' למשדא לכלבים וכמ"ש סי' ש"ח סל"א ע"ב אבל חמץ דעתיה למשדיה לכלבים ונ"ל דאפי' למ"ד נולד אסור בשבת מ"מ הכא שרי דמאתמול היה דעתו להשליכה לכלבים בשעה ה' כדאמרי' בשבת דף קמ"ג וכ"כ הטור דמותר לטלטלו בשעה ה' והא דאסר הריב"ש מיירי בחמצו של עכו"ם שנמצא דלא שייך לו' דעתו עליו ובי"ט דבאמת הוא אסור בהנאה אסור לטלטלו כמ"ש ס"ג ורמב"ם מיירי דליכא עכו"ם או כלב ליתן לו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even to burn it in its place is forbidden.</b> If you ask: is this not an a fortiori argument? If moving, which is rabbinic, is forbidden, all the more so kindling, which is according to the Torah. If so, what is the meaning of even? One can answer as I wrote above, that here it is different, because at the time he uproots the prohibition he fulfills the positive commandment, and one might have said it is permitted; he therefore teaches us that it is forbidden. The Kesef Mishneh wrote that since there is no need of the day at all, we do not say the principle of since, as written in siman 514 se'if 5. Furthermore, we say the principle of since only regarding a benefit equal for every person, and here there is no bodily benefit, as I wrote above in siman 511. Although there is a mitzvah in burning it, nevertheless it is forbidden, just like burning kodshim, which does not override Yom Tov. End quote. In Tosafos, first chapter of Kesubos, it does not imply so. Maharam of Lublin wrote the reason: although we hold that a utensil may be moved for the sake of something that may not be moved, that applies to an item that has the status of a utensil; but wood and fire, which do not have the status of utensils, may not be moved for the sake of chametz. End quote. See siman 502. However, in Tosafos, Pesachim folio 5b, it implies that it is permitted to burn it, since there is a mitzvah in burning it, and it is called a need of the day. One must say that this follows the initial assumption that on the first day you shall destroy it means on Yom Tov; but according to the conclusion, on the first day means from the eve of Yom Tov, and if so it is forbidden to burn it on Yom Tov. See Responsa Rashba, siman 71. The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash that it is forbidden to move chametz because anything forbidden for eating, even rabbinically, is forbidden to move on Shabbos, and the same applies on Yom Tov. Therefore we do not turn over the forbidden fats; see siman 499, chapter 4. End quote from the Rivash. So too the Rambam wrote in chapter 25 of Hilchos Shabbos: fruits that are forbidden to eat, such as tevel, etc., are forbidden to move. End quote. If so, when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, it should be forbidden to move chametz during the fifth hour. So too it is in the Rambam at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchos Chametz, where he wrote that after four hours he covers chametz with a utensil. This requires examination: is it worse than an animal that became a neveilah on Shabbos, which is permitted to move because it is fit to throw to dogs? Therefore it appears to me that specifically tevel fruits are forbidden to move, or forbidden fats, because one's mind is not to throw them to dogs, as I wrote in siman 308 se'if 31, but with chametz one's mind is to throw it to dogs. It appears to me that even according to the one who says nolad is forbidden on Shabbos, here it is permitted, for from yesterday his mind was to throw it to dogs in the fifth hour, as we say in Shabbos folio 143. So too the Tur wrote that it is permitted to move it in the fifth hour. As for what the Rivash forbade, he speaks of a non-Jew's chametz that was found, where it is not relevant to say that his mind was upon it; and on Yom Tov, when it is in truth forbidden for benefit, it is forbidden to move it, as written in se'if 3. The Rambam speaks where there is no non-Jew or dog to whom to give it.
