לא פירשו הר"ן אלא סבירא ליה דאף דבר שאין מתכוין נמי אסור, והא דמותר בבשר על גבי גחלים היינו משום שהוא אוכל נפש ממש וכן מבואר מפירוש רש"י שם. ועוד אפשר דגם לפירוש הר"ן דוקא התם דאיכא קצת אוכל נפש לעשן פירות אף שאפשר לאוכלן בלא זה, ואין עושה אלא למתקן מכל מקום הוי קצת אוכל נפש, לכן נראה לי להחמיר כרמ"א ולבוש אף שהט"ז סוף סימן תקי"א הסכים לרש"ל כמדומה שלא ראה כשכתב שם דברי רמ"א שבכאן, גם לא ירד לכל הנזכר לעיל, גם מי יקל ראש נגד רוקח ומהרי"ל בשם פרדס:
**** ENGLISH ****
[3] <b>Because he creates a fragrance, etc.</b> This is difficult, for the prohibition applies only when one places a cup there in order to perfume it. Where one does not intend to perfume it, it should be permitted, because the principle of an inevitable consequence applies only to a full melachah, whereas perfuming is not itself a melachah; only extinguishing and kindling are melachos (Yam Shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 2). His meaning is that when the Gemara, Beitzah 23, forbids placing spices even upon a shard because this creates fragrance in the shard, the prohibition is due to the fact that kindling is involved, which is a full melachah. Creating fragrance without intent is permitted. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham concludes that the Rama is correct, because there the kindling is for food preparation and is permitted, yet creating fragrance in the shard remains forbidden. I find this explanation difficult. Yam Shel Shlomo explicitly mentions both extinguishing and kindling, while upon a shard there is no extinguishing at all, as stated there in Beitzah. Rashi also explains that kindling upon a shard is not a full melachah, since it is done unusually and constitutes an indirect manner of performance. Yam Shel Shlomo therefore plainly refers to placing spices upon coals, where both extinguishing and kindling are present. The Magen Avraham's statement that there is no prohibition of kindling because it is for food preparation is likewise difficult, for the opinion that forbids using a shard maintains that this is an excessive luxury not needed by everyone, as explained by the Gemara and Rashi. According to the opinion that it is a food need, even placing the spices upon coals is permitted despite the extinguishing, kindling, and creation of fragrance, as Rashi explains. There is also an apparent contradiction, for in siman 511, seif katan 10, Magen Avraham writes that creating fragrance without intent is permitted, and Beit Yosef cites Ran likewise. The Gemara cited above would then also be difficult according to Ran. It therefore appears clear that Rav, who permits even upon coals, disagrees entirely with Rabbah, who forbids upon a shard because it creates fragrance. Rav maintains that creating fragrance without intent is permitted, and the halachah follows Rav, as stated at the end of siman 511. Yam Shel Shlomo undoubtedly relied upon Ran and therefore disagreed with the Rama. In my humble opinion, however, Rokeach and Maharil, from whom the Rama derived this ruling, did not interpret Ran this way and hold that even an unintended creation of fragrance is forbidden. The permission to roast meat upon coals applies because it is actual food preparation, as is also evident from Rashi. It is also possible that even according to Ran, permission applies there only because smoking fruit has some element of food preparation, although the fruit could be eaten without it and the act merely improves it. One should therefore be stringent in accordance with Rama and Levush. Although the Taz at the end of siman 511 agrees with Yam Shel Shlomo, it appears that he did not see the Rama's ruling here when he wrote there, nor did he address all the considerations stated above. One should not treat lightly the ruling of Rokeach and Maharil in the name of Pardes.

[ד] <b>אין אדם יוצא וכו'.</b> ואם התחילו לצאת בו דעת מגן אברהם דהוה ליה כאילו נתנו במתנה כמו בטבעת קידושין באבן העזר סימן כ"ח וכן כתב סימן י"ד וצריך עיון בסימן תרמ"ט סק"ט כתב דלא יצא ואפשר שיש טעות סופר שם, מיהו בט"ז שם ובתשובת בית יעקב סימן קי"ד משמע דלא יצא, אם אין האיש בביתו ונתנה האשה הלולב לאחד מאוהביו לא יצא, דשמא ניחא ליה למוכרו על יום אחד אם לא שהוא אדם חשוב דזילא ביה מילתא למוכרו (מגן אברהם). ולי נראה דאין הבעל מקפיד בזה כדאיתא ביו"ד סימן רמ"ח ומכל שכן כשהיא נושאת ונותנת בתוך הבית:
**** ENGLISH ****
[4] <b>A person does not fulfill his obligation, etc.</b> If one already began using it for the mitzvah, Magen Avraham considers this equivalent to having given it as a gift, as with a ring used for kiddushin in Even HaEzer, siman 28, and as he writes in siman 14. This requires study, for in siman 649, seif katan 9, he writes that one does not fulfill the obligation; perhaps there is a scribal error there. The Taz there and Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 114, imply that one does not fulfill the obligation. If the owner is not at home and his wife gives the lulav to one of his friends, the recipient does not fulfill the obligation, because the owner may have preferred to sell its use for one day, unless he is a distinguished person for whom such a sale would be demeaning (Magen Avraham). It appears to me, however, that the husband does not object to this, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 248, particularly where she conducts the household's business affairs.

[ו] <b>אפילו נאנס וכו'.</b> ואם נתנו סתם ונאנס מידו נותן דמים ויוצא (רבינו ירוחם), אף זה בשבלי הלקט ואחרונים לא הביאו, גם מהר"ן וטור לכאורה לא משמע הכי, מכל מקום נראה לי להקל כי ראיתי בשבלי הלקט וכלבו דמייתי פוסקים דאף באמר על מנת שתחזירו לי יוצא בנאנס דכוונתו היה שיחזירו לולב עצמו אם יהא בעין אבל בנאנס יוצא כשנותן דמים ואף דלא קיימא לן כוותייהו מכל מקום בסתם נקטינן הכי. ונראה בחול המועד סוכות יוצא אף באמר על מנת שתחזירו לי דהא הרבה פוסקים בסימן תרמ"ט דסבירא ליה דיוצאין בגזול:
**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>Even if an accident occurred, etc.</b> If it was given without qualification and was lost through circumstances beyond his control, he pays its value and fulfills the mitzvah (Rabbeinu Yerucham). This is also found in Shibbolei HaLeket, though later authorities did not cite it, and Ran and Tur do not appear to agree. Nevertheless, it seems proper to be lenient, because Shibbolei HaLeket and Kol Bo cite authorities who rule that even when one said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' the recipient fulfills the mitzvah if it was lost through unavoidable circumstances and he pays its value. Their intent is that the giver required the lulav itself to be returned only if it remained intact. Although the halachah does not follow them in that case, when it was given without qualification we may rely upon this view. It also appears that on Chol HaMoed Sukkos one fulfills the obligation even if the giver said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' since many authorities cited in siman 649 maintain that one fulfills the obligation with a stolen lulav on those days.

[ז] <b>נתנו לו סתם וכו' מסתמא וכו'.</b> הקשה בתשובת בית יעקב סימן קי"ד דשולחן ערוך סותר למה שכתב בחושן משפט סימן רמ"א סעיף ו' דאם אמר על מנת שתחזירו ולא אמר לי יכול להחזיר כשירצה, ואין לומר דסתם עדיף מאומר על מנת שתחזירהו ולא אמר לי דהא כתב בית יוסף דסתם לא עדיף ממתנה בפירוש ולא כפליה לתנאי. ותירץ דבחושן משפט מיירי שיש לו לולב אחר לצאת בו מכל מקום אם אמר על מנת שתחזירו לי צריך להחזירו מיד שרוצה לצאת בממון שלו ודחוק, ועוד תירץ דהתם לענין ממון קאמר דאין יכולין לכופו להחזיר מיד אבל ידי מצוה לא יצא, אבל במגן אברהם כתב דיצא וכן משמע בתשובת רשב"א סימן אל"ף. ולי היה נראה מדלא כתב בשולחן ערוך הכא וכן אם החזירו לאחר זמן מצותו וכו' כמו שכתב בסעיף דלעיל אלמא דסבירא ליה בסתם לא הוי כמו שאמר על מנת שתחזירו לי אלא לענין שצריך להחזיר אבל לא לענין שצריך להחזיר מיד וכמו דנשתנה הדין לרבינו ירוחם לענין נאנס דלעיל, אך מלבוש שכתב והא דאמרינן וכו' אחר דין דסתם מבואר דגם בסתם דינא הכי וכן משמע בר"ן, ומכל מקום בחול המועד סוכות נראה דיצא אף שלא החזירו מיד ובסתם וכדאמרן:
**** ENGLISH ****
[7] <b>He gave it to him without qualification, etc.; presumably, etc.</b> Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 114, asks that the Shulchan Aruch appears to contradict Choshen Mishpat 241:6, where it rules that if one said, 'on condition that you return it,' without saying 'to me,' the recipient may return it whenever he wishes. One cannot answer that an unspecified gift is stronger than an explicit condition lacking the words 'to me,' because Beit Yosef writes that an unspecified gift is no stronger than an expressly stated gift whose condition was not doubled. He answers that Choshen Mishpat speaks where the giver has another lulav with which to fulfill the mitzvah. If he said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' however, it must be returned immediately, because he wishes to perform the mitzvah with his own property. This answer is strained. He further answers that Choshen Mishpat concerns monetary enforcement, so the court cannot compel immediate return, but the recipient has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Magen Avraham, however, writes that he has fulfilled it, and Responsa Rashba, siman 1000, likewise implies this. I would have said that since the Shulchan Aruch does not write here, 'and likewise, if he returned it after the time of the mitzvah,' as it writes in the preceding seif, an unspecified gift is not equivalent to saying, 'on condition that you return it to me,' except insofar as it must eventually be returned, not that it must be returned immediately. This resembles Rabbeinu Yerucham's distinction above regarding unavoidable loss. Yet Levush, who places the statement 'that which we said, etc.' after the law of an unspecified gift, clearly indicates that the same immediate-return rule applies. Ran also implies this. Nevertheless, on Chol HaMoed Sukkos it appears that one fulfills the obligation even if he did not return it immediately when it was given without qualification, as stated above.

Darchei Moshe writes in the name of Ri that if one gave an esrog as a gift and said, 'after you, it shall belong to so-and-so,' the first recipient fulfills the mitzvah even if he ate or sold it, because the later recipient acquires only what the first leaves over.
