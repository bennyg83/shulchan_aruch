<b>ב ואסור. </b>ממ"שש מ"ב א' מ"ד הואיל כו' וע' רש"י שם ד"ה קמ"ל משא"כ כאן דאף לאנשים לא חזי. ושם ושבת קל"א א' אצטריך קרא למישרי טלטול וע' רש"י ד"ה אצטריך כו'. ובתוספתא ספ"ב רי"א י"ט הראשון של חג שחל להיות בשבת כיון שיצא י"ח בו אסור לטלטלו וכן בירושלמי סוף פ"ג הניחו בארץ אסור לטלטלו א"ר זאת אומרת שאסור בהנייה דאל"כ תורת כלי עליו הדס להריח בו וכן כולם וז"ש אבל האתרוג כו' וכמש"ל סימן תרנ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2. And it is prohibited.</b> From what is stated in Sukkah 42a: according to the one who says, 'since,' etc. See Rashi there, s.v. 'kamashma lan'; this is not so here, for it is not fit even for men. And there, and Shabbos 131a, a verse was needed to permit moving it; see Rashi, s.v. 'itztarich,' etc. In the Tosefta at the end of chapter 2, R. Yehudah says: if the first Yom Tov of the festival occurred on Shabbos, once he has fulfilled his obligation with it, it is prohibited to move it. So too in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3: if he placed it on the ground, it is prohibited to move it. R. Zerika said: this teaches that it is prohibited for benefit, for if not, it has the status of a utensil, as hadas is for smelling, and likewise all of them. This is what it says: but the esrog, etc.; and as I wrote above, siman 653.

<b>ס"ז דוקא שלא כו'. </b>כמ"ש בס"ט מה כו' וכ' המ"מ בשם הרשב"א כיון שידעו בשעת שאינו ראוי ליחלק וע"ד כן לקחוהו אמרינן יש ברירה בשעה שנוטל לצאת כולו שלו כמו שותפין שנדרו הנאה זה מזה שנכנסין לחצר שאין בו דין חלוקה דאמרינן יש ברירה שכשלקחוהו ע"מ כן שבשעה שכ"א מהן נכנס יהיה מקום דריסתו שלו וכן בלולב כיוצא וא"צ להקנות ובב"ק נ"א ב' פי' הרשב"א בשם ר"ח משימסור לו דליו. נטלא והוברר הדבר משמסר לו הדלי שהבור עומד ברשותו ומשל עצמו הוא דולה וז"ש שם ואזדי לטעמייהו כו' כמ"ש בנדרים דבאין בו דין חלוקה פליגי ואמרינן דהוברר הדבר בשעה שמשתמש שכולו הוא שלו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 7: Specifically that, etc.</b> As written in seif 9: what, etc. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of the Rashba: since they knew at the time that it was not fit to be divided, and they acquired it with that in mind, we say there is retroactive clarification; at the time he takes it to fulfill the obligation, it is entirely his. This is like partners who vowed not to benefit from one another, who may enter a courtyard that is not subject to division, because we say there is retroactive clarification: when they acquired it, they did so with this understanding, that at the time each of them enters, the place of his walking is his. So too with lulav and the like, and no transfer of ownership is required. In Bava Kamma 51b, the Rashba explained in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel: from when he hands him his bucket. He took it, and the matter became clarified from when he handed him the bucket, that the pit stands in his domain and he draws from his own. This is what it says there: and they follow their own reasoning, etc., as written in Nedarim, that they dispute where it is not subject to division, and we say that the matter is clarified at the time he uses it, that all of it is his.

This is the Ran's wording: and if you say, even if there are other esrogim there, who divided it for him, as written in chapter 5 of Bava Metzia? One can say that here it speaks of brothers who are not particular with one another, and even if this one took the beautiful one, they are not particular about it. But when they have only a quince or a pomegranate, they are certainly particular, for they want to fulfill the mitzvah just like him.
