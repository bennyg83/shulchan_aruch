<b>(ס"ק ב) דמוליד כו</b> להניח הכוס (שהוא מבושם ומריח) כדי לגמר היינו שמכוין שיגמרו ויתבשמו הבגדים ע"י כך. נקט שיראי. ר"ל דבגמ' במס' ביצה דף כ"ג אמרו סחופי כסא אשיראי אסור משום מוליד ריח'. אינו ממלאכה אלא הכיבוי וההבערה כ"ה שם ביש"ש. פי' ר"ל דכיבוי והבערה מאי שאטייהו הכא. לזה כתב דמהרש"ל בא ליישב מאי דקשיא עליו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 2) For he creates, etc.</b> To place the cup, which is perfumed and gives off fragrance, in order to perfume, means that he intends that the garments be perfumed and scented through it. He took the case of silk garments. Meaning, in the Gemara in tractate Beitzah, daf 23, they said: turning a cup over onto silk garments is prohibited because it creates fragrance. 'It is not a melachah, except for extinguishing and kindling.' So it is there in Yam Shel Shlomo. Explanation: what do extinguishing and kindling have to do here? Therefore he wrote that Maharshal came to resolve what was difficult to him.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For that which we say on daf 23, etc.</b> His words are very brief, and I must explain at length according to my humble understanding, and clarify what Maharshal and the Magen Avraham dispute. R. Yehudah said: smoking fruits, meaning placing spices on burning coals while fruits are suspended above the coal so that they absorb the taste and smell of the spices, is prohibited on Yom Tov. At the beginning, by placing the spices on the coal, he extinguishes, and afterward he kindles. Although this is for food preparation, nevertheless, since it is not equal for every person but only for delicate people, it is prohibited. But on a heated earthenware shard, it is permitted to place spices and smoke the fruits. This is the wording: there is no extinguishing, and the kindling is also through an unusual manner, backhanded, and there is no Torah prohibition. Rabbah said: even on an earthenware shard it is also prohibited, because he creates fragrance. Rashi wrote as follows: it enters the shard, which had no fragrance, and it is rabbinically prohibited, etc. End quote. Rabbah and R. Yosef both said: turning a cup over onto silk garments on Yom Tov is prohibited because it creates fragrance, etc. Rava said: even on coals it is permitted, just as with meat on coals, where there is also extinguishing, kindling, and creating fragrance in the coals, and nevertheless it is permitted [see above siman 511 in Magen Avraham, what he wrote]. This is the sugya of the Talmud. Maharshal wanted to resolve what is difficult regarding his words, on what he wrote that if one does not intend to create fragrance, as with an esrog, even though it is an inevitable consequence, it is nevertheless permitted. But Rabbah prohibits even on an earthenware shard because he creates fragrance in the shard, although he certainly does not intend to create fragrance in the shard, and nevertheless it is prohibited. Although we do not rule like him, that is because we rule like Rava, that even on a coal it is permitted because it is for food preparation, similar to meat on coals. But in a place where there is no need of food preparation, even Rava agrees with Rabbah that it is prohibited. Regarding this Maharshal wrote to resolve that there, in any event, there is kindling, etc. Meaning, he found it difficult why Rabbah prohibited on an earthenware shard because of creating fragrance, although creating fragrance is only a rabbinic prohibition, as Rashi wrote and as above. One must say that he holds that since it is only for delicate people, even a rabbinic prohibition was not permitted. If so, why mention a new prohibition, namely creating fragrance? Let him derive it from the prohibition of kindling. Although it is through an unusual manner and there is no Torah prohibition, nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition, as Rashi wrote. Rather, one must say that Rabbah holds that one rabbinic prohibition is permitted for food preparation even though it is only for delicate people, but two rabbinic prohibitions are not permitted. According to this, since there is also creating fragrance, for there is the prohibition of kindling in an unusual manner and also the prohibition of creating fragrance, therefore it is prohibited. According to this, Maharshal holds by reasoning that since the prohibition of creating fragrance is a light prohibition, when he does not intend to create fragrance, even though it is an inevitable consequence, it is permitted even where there is no need of food preparation. But on an earthenware shard, since even without this there is a complete prohibition, namely kindling in an unusual manner, although for that rabbinic prohibition alone we would permit it for food preparation that is only for delicate people, when another prohibition of creating fragrance joins it, even though it is a light prohibition and he does not intend it, nevertheless it is prohibited. But where there is no kindling, only the prohibition of creating fragrance, and he does not intend this, even if it is not for food preparation at all, it is permitted. This is Maharshal's view. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote: nevertheless, it appears to me, etc., for there the prohibition is not because of kindling, etc. Meaning, the Magen Avraham holds that it is impossible to say like Maharshal that Rabbah prohibits because there are two things, kindling in an unusual manner and creating fragrance; for if so, Rabbah should have mentioned the prohibition of kindling, which according to Maharshal is the main point, as above, and why did he mention only the prohibition of creating fragrance? Rather, it must be that Rabbah holds that although kindling, even in an unusual manner, is nevertheless rabbinically prohibited, we would permit it because it is for food preparation.

<b>Know what the Taz wrote in seif katan 3, that a lulav may be moved for use of its body or place, similar to shofar.</b> The book Chemdas Moshe challenged him, because specifically a shofar has the status of a utensil whose work is prohibited, and therefore it is permitted to move it for use of its body or place, as stated in siman 308. But a lulav does not have the status of a utensil, and even for use of its body or place it is prohibited, as the Rema wrote in siman 308:7 in the gloss; see there. So too it is implied at the beginning of chapter Lulav VaAravah, from what Tosafos wrote on the Talmud's question, 'but it is mere moving,' that it does not have the status of a utensil.

<b>(ס"ק ג) אין אדם כו'</b> צ"ע דבא"הע כו' וי"ל דהתם מיירי באינו יודע ר"ל שאינו יודע שאין אשה מתקדשת בשאול. וכוונת מ"א בתירוצו הוא דבשלמא בקדושין דלא כתיב לכם. ולכן לא בעינן שיהיה הדבר של הבעל. אלא בעי' שהאשה תקבל דבר מהבעל ולא יהיה הדבר ההוא גזל ביד האשה וא"כ כיון שלא ידע שאין מתקדשת בשאול אמרי מה"ט אמר לשון שאלה מחסרון ידיע' שהי' סבר שהי' די גם בשאול ואלו ידע שאינה מתקדשת בשאול היה נותן לו במתנה ולכן אע"פ שאומר לשון שאלה. וא"א לו' שלא דק בלשניה וכוונתו היה למתנה מ"מ מקודשת ניהו דעדיין אינו של בעל דהא לשאלה נתנו להבעל ולא במתנה. והבעל נתנו לאשה במתנה. עכ"פ לא הוי גזל ביד האשה. דהא כשעתה יוודע למשאיל דאין אשה מתקדשת בשאול יתננו במתנה ולכן אינו גזל ביד האשה אע"ג דלא קנה הבעל והאשה למפרע. כ"א משעת מתנה ואילך מ"מ סגי בכך ולא הוי גזל בידיה. אבל באתרוג דבעי' לכם ניהו דלבסוף כשיוודע למשאיל דאינו יוצא בשאול יתננו במתנה מ"מ בשעת נטילה לא הוי לכם ולא יצא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 3) A person does not, etc.</b> This requires investigation, for in Even HaEzer, etc. One may say that there it speaks where he does not know, meaning that he does not know that a woman cannot be betrothed with a borrowed item. The intent of the Magen Avraham in his answer is as follows: Granted, regarding betrothal, where 'yours' is not written, we do not require that the object belong to the husband. We require only that the woman receive something from the husband and that the object not be stolen in the woman's hand. If so, since he did not know that she cannot be betrothed with a borrowed item, we say that for this reason he used the language of borrowing out of lack of knowledge, for he thought that a borrowed item was also enough; and if he had known that she cannot be betrothed with a borrowed item, he would have given it as a gift. Therefore, although he used the language of borrowing, and we cannot say that he was imprecise in his wording and meant a gift, nevertheless she is betrothed. Although it still does not belong to the husband, for it was given to the husband as a loan and not as a gift, and the husband gave it to the woman as a gift, in any event it is not stolen in the woman's hand. For when the lender now learns that a woman cannot be betrothed with a borrowed item, he will give it as a gift, and therefore it is not stolen in the woman's hand, even though the husband and woman did not acquire it retroactively, but only from the time of the gift onward. Nevertheless this suffices, and it is not stolen in her hand. But with an esrog, where we require 'yours,' although in the end, when the lender learns that one does not fulfill the obligation with a borrowed item, he will give it as a gift, nevertheless at the time of taking it was not 'yours,' and he did not fulfill the obligation.

<b>ובאמת היודע כו'</b> ר"ל לפי זה דלשון שאלה שאמר הוא דוקא אלא דאמרי' שהוא מחסרון ידיעה כנ"ל ואלו ידע היה אומר לשון מתנה. א"כ היודע שאינה מתקדשת בשאול ואעפ"כ אמר לשון שאלה אינה מקודשת דהוי גזל בידה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And in truth, one who knows, etc.</b> Meaning, according to this, the language of borrowing that he used is precise, except that we say it was from lack of knowledge, as above, and if he had known, he would have used the language of a gift. If so, one who knows that she cannot be betrothed with a borrowed item and nevertheless used the language of borrowing, she is not betrothed, for it is stolen in her hand.

<b>וה"ה באתרוג.</b> ר"ל כיון דאפי' בידע מתקדשת ע"כ ל"ל הטעם הנ"ל אלא צ"ל דלשון שאלה שאמר לאו דוקא ולא דק בלשניה. ועכ"פ נתכוין למתנה. על מנת להחזיר. כיון דרצונו למלאות בקשת המבקש לקדש אשה ודאי כוונת המשאיל באופן המועיל לקדושין וא"כ ה"ה באתרוג מהני כה"ג דהוי לכם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the same applies to an esrog.</b> Meaning, since even when he knew, she is betrothed, necessarily the above reason does not apply. Rather, we must say that the language of borrowing that he used was not precise, and he was not exact in his wording. In any event, he intended a gift on condition to return, because he wants to fulfill the request of the one asking to betroth a woman, so the lender's intent is certainly in a way effective for betrothal. If so, the same applies to an esrog: in such a case it helps, for it is 'yours.'

וכ"מ לעיל סי' י"ד גבי השואל טלית מצוייצת דמברך עליו אע"ג דגם גבי ציצית בעינן שלכם. באתרוג מהני טפי מתנה עמ"ל. משום דהי' מצו' דא"א לפטור ממנה. ודאי בלב שלם נתנו במתנה עמ"ל עכ"פ משא"כ ציצית דאם אין לו בגד בת ד' כנפות פטור אפשר לא נתכוין למתנה ודעתו באמת שלא יברך עליו. עיין שם שהאריך. ועז"כ מ"א וצ"ע דמשמע בש"ס דבכ"מ מהני מתנה עמ"ל חוץ מקדושין דלא. משום דדמי לחלופין דאין האשה נקנית בחלופין כדאי' בקדושין:
**** ENGLISH ****
So too it is implied above in siman 14, regarding one who borrows a fringed garment, that he recites a berachah over it, although regarding tzitzis too we require 'yours.' With an esrog, a gift on condition to return helps more, because it is a mitzvah from which one cannot exempt himself; he certainly gave it wholeheartedly as a gift on condition to return, at least. This is not so with tzitzis, for if one does not have a four-cornered garment he is exempt; perhaps he did not intend a gift, and his true intent is that he should not recite a berachah over it. See there, where he discusses this at length. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote: this requires investigation, for it is implied in the Talmud that in every place a gift on condition to return helps, except for betrothal, where it does not, because it resembles exchange, and a woman is not acquired through exchange, as stated in Kiddushin.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 6) And he must, etc.</b> Even if he said explicitly, etc. One might have thought that where it is unspecified, he certainly intended to return it as a gift, even though he did not explicitly say that he returns it as a gift, for he certainly intended through his return to fulfill the condition that he explicitly stipulated. This teaches that nevertheless he must explicitly say it in the language of a gift.
