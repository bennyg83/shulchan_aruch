<b>א) [סעיף א'] במקום שנהגו שהעולה עצמו קורא וכו'  </b>כההוא דר"ע שקראו אותו לתורה ולא רצה לעלות לפי שלא סדר אותה תחלה ודריש ליה מקרא אז ראה ויספרה וכו' מדרש תנחומא פ' יתרו ומד"ר פ' כי תשא, טור וב"י, וכתב הב"י דהיינו לדידהו שהעולה היה קורא בקול רם אבל לדידן שהעולה אינו קורא בקול רם אעפ"י שלא סדר אותה לית לן בה, וכ"כ הב"ח. אלא שכתב מיהו מצוה מן המובחר שגם העולה יסדר אותה תחלה פעמים ושלש, וע"כ ראוי שכל אחד יסדר אותה תחלה דשמא יקראו אוחו לעלות למחר בשבת עכ"ד, והביאו שכנה"ג בהגב"י או' ב' וכתב ונ"ל דבקריאת הפ' שנים מקרא ואחד תרגום יוצא י"ח הסידור. וכ"כ העו"ת או' א' א"ר או' א' אמ"ל בדין עליית קס"ת או' נ"ב, לד"ת סי' ה' או' נ"ב, וכ"כ הפר"ח או' א' אלא שכתב שם הפר"ח דצריך לחזור ולסדרה פעם אחרת כדי שיהיה ג"פ יעו"ש והביאו א"א או' א' ומ"ש היפ"ל סוף או' ב' דמדברי הזוהר שהביא הסו"ב לקמן סי' קמ"א סק"א נראה שדי אפי' בפ"א אינו מוכרח כיעו"ש וא"כ למה לנו לחלוק השוים במקום שאין הכרע, ועיין בדברינו לקמן ריש סי' קמ"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] In a place where they have the custom that the oleh himself reads, etc.</b> Like that [incident] of Rabbi Akiva, whom they called to the Torah and he did not wish to ascend because he had not first arranged [reviewed] it, and he expounds it from the verse, 'then He saw it and declared it,' etc. Midrash Tanchuma, parashas Yisro, and Midrash Rabbah, parashas Ki Sisa. Tur and Beis Yosef. And the Beis Yosef wrote that this is for them, where the oleh would read aloud; but for us, where the oleh does not read aloud, even though he did not arrange it, it is of no concern to us. And so wrote the Bach; only that he wrote: however, it is the choicest [manner of] the mitzvah that the oleh too arrange it first, two and three times; and therefore it is fitting that each one arrange it first, for perhaps they will call him to ascend the next day on Shabbos. End of his words. And the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 2, and wrote: and it appears to me that with the reading of the parashah twice Scripture and once Targum one fulfills the obligation of arranging it. And so wrote the Olas Tamid, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Emes LeYaakov in the law of ascent to the Sefer Torah, ot 52; Ledovid Emes, siman 5, ot 52. And so wrote the Pri Chadash, ot 1; only that the Pri Chadash wrote there that one must return and arrange it another time, so that it be three times; see there, and the Eshel Avraham cited him, ot 1. And as for what the Yafeh LaLev wrote at the end of ot 2, that from the words of the Zohar cited by the Soles Belulah below in siman 141, s.k. 1, it appears that even once suffices -- this is not compelling, as seen there; and if so, why should we distinguish [between] the equal [views] in a place where there is no decisive [proof]? And see our words below at the beginning of siman 142.

<b>7) There, gloss: And in these countries they do not practice so, etc.</b> The Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 4, and this is his language: it appears to me that the customs do not conflict; for that which they practice in our places, that the shaliach tzibbur ascends on his own without being called, is on days when the segan does not call [the olim], but rather the olim ascend on their own, such as Monday and Thursday, Shabbos Minchah, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, and public fasts; and that which they practice in Ashkenaz, that the chazan does not ascend unless the segan calls him, is on days when the segan calls; and so it is the widespread custom in these places, that the chazan does not ascend on his own unless the segan calls him. And by this distinction, that which the world practices on the aforementioned days, ascending on their own, is settled: for since they have the custom on these days that the segan does not call, it is like granting permission, as though the segan said, 'whoever wishes to ascend and read, let him ascend.' And this is also the reason for that which they practice in Constantinople, that when there is a celebration in the congregation, after they call up a Kohen and a Levi and three [more], whoever wishes to ascend, ascends: since they have the custom not to call by name except a Kohen and a Levi and 'samuch' and 'mashlim,' and the rest, whoever wishes to ascend to honor the celebrant, ascends -- it is as though they called them. And in Turya, may God protect it, I saw that when there is a celebration in the congregation, after the celebrant calls up one and two [olim] and does not wish to call any more by name, the shamash stands and announces, 'whoever wishes to honor the celebrant, let him ascend and read.' And they practice so because in these places most call up all the olim at their celebration, and no oleh ascends on his own; therefore the shamash announces, 'whoever wishes to ascend, ascends,' which is a granting of permission. Also that which they practice in some places, that they do not call the Sefer Torah by name, but rather they ascend according to [where they are] seated, that one Shabbos begins from one [end] until seven, and on another Shabbos ascends the one who left off at his place for another Shabbos -- this too is like granting permission. And the same applies also to Simchas Torah, when the entire congregation ascends without being called except for a Kohen and a Levi and three and 'samuch' -- it is like granting permission. But indeed, those who ascend without being called on a day when the segan calls do not act properly; and perhaps there is no objection to the ascent itself, for even though he ascends on his own, as long as the chazan tells him to read, it suffices. And this is also the reason [that permits] the sage of the congregation or the head of the congregation, when they call him for the Shirah or for the Ten Commandments or for the Tochachah, to read himself without being granted permission: for since the custom is for the sage to read them, it is like granting permission. However, if they called a person who is not accustomed to read them, he does not read until they tell him to read, etc. End of his language. However, one who observes will see that this distinction that he wrote is only according to the custom of his place; but there are places that have the custom that even at Shabbos Minchah and Monday and Thursday and Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed and at a celebration and the like, they do not ascend without being called; and so, 'each river follows its own course.' And in a place where they have the custom that at all times they do not ascend without being called, the shaliach tzibbur too does not ascend without being called, for so the custom has spread in most places, like the Rama, of blessed memory, as the Ledovid Emes wrote, siman 5, ot 44, and the Yafeh LaLev, ot 3; and so is the custom here in the holy city of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established.

<b>ט) שם בהגה, שקוראים אותם בשמם וכו'  </b>והמנהג פשוט בירושלים ת"ו שהש"ץ אינו קורא לעולה לס"ת בשמו בקו"ר כמנהג כל המקום רק השמש קודם שיסיים העולה מקודם הולך אצלו ואומר לו שיעלה, ואם אותו יחיד אינו רוצה לעלות מחמת איזה סיבה אומר לשמש שיקרא אחר, וזה תקנת ראשונים שלא יהיה בכלל הקורין אותו לס"ת ואינו עולה, וגם בזה ניצולין מכמה מיני קפדות שנמשכות כשיקראו אותו בשם והוא מוכרח לעלות והוא לא ירצה מטעם ידוע אצלו, הרב חיים שאל בתשו' ח"א סי' י"ג, והביאו בס' התקנות בדין קס"ת או' י"ט, ויש מקומות נוהגים שמי שרוצים לכבדו לעלות לס"ת הולך השמש אצלו ונותן בידו טס של כסף וכן ראיתי נוהגין באר"ץ בעברי לשם כשבאתי לירושת"ו וגם זה המנהג הוא מטעם הנז':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>9) There, in the gloss: That they call them by their name, etc.</b> And the custom is widespread in Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, that the shaliach tzibbur does not call the oleh to the Sefer Torah by his name aloud, as is the custom in every place; rather the shamash, before the previous oleh finishes, goes over to him and tells him to ascend; and if that individual does not wish to ascend on account of some reason, he tells the shamash to call another. And this is an enactment of the earlier authorities, so that he not be included among those who are called to the Sefer Torah and do not ascend. And also by this they are saved from several kinds of resentment that ensue when they call him by name and he is compelled to ascend but does not wish to, for a reason known to him. The Rav Chaim Sha'al in a responsum, part 1, siman 13; and the sefer HaTakanos cited him in the law of Krias Sefer Torah, ot 19. And there are places that have the custom that one whom they wish to honor to ascend to the Sefer Torah -- the shamash goes over to him and places in his hand a plaque of silver; and so I saw them practice in [my] land when I passed through there, when I came to Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt; and this custom too is for the aforementioned reason.
