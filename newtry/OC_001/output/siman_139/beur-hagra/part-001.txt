**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise if, etc.</b> For they permitted assisting a non-Jewish woman in childbirth, and even on Shabbos they considered permitting it because of enmity, were it not possible to answer, “Our own,” etc. Likewise, Rabbi Yehudah Nesiah considered accepting a gift from that heretic on his festival, except that they devised a stratagem. See Josippon, which relates that they named kohanim after Alexander of Macedon because of the Kohen Gadol, and see Mordechai there. The same is true in many cases, and certainly here, where there is no prohibition at all. The statement, “The name of the wicked shall rot,” means that one should not name others after them, and likewise that one should curse them. But attributing a son to his father is permitted, whether the father was wicked, as in “Achav son of Omri” and many similar examples, or righteous, as in “King Chizkiyahu son of Achaz” and the like.
