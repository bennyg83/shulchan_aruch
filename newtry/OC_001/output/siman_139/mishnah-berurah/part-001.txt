(א) לא יעלה - היינו אע"פ שקראוהו לעלות ואחז"ל [פרק הרואה] ג' דברים מקצרים ימיו של אדם ואחד מהן מי שנותנין לו ס"ת לקרות ואינו קורא הכא לא יעלה שצריך מתחלה לסדר בינו לבין עצמו ואסמכוהו חכמים אקרא דכתיב אז ראה ויספרה הכינה וגם חקרה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) He should not go up. This means that even if he was called to go up, he should not do so. Chazal said in the chapter HaRoeh that three matters shorten a person's life, one of which is when he is given a Sefer Torah from which to read and does not read. Here, however, he should not go up because he must first review the reading privately. The Sages found support for this in the verse, “Then He saw it and declared it; He prepared it and also investigated it.”

**** ENGLISH ****
(14) All those who read, etc. Strictly speaking, only the first person needed to recite the opening blessing and the final person the concluding blessing, while those in the middle would be discharged by their blessings. Nevertheless, the Sages instituted this because of those who enter and leave during the reading, lest they fail to hear the blessing of the first or last reader and say that the Torah is read without a blessing. Therefore, every person receiving an aliyah recites a blessing before and after, as is practiced today.

**** ENGLISH ****
(15) They recite blessings before and after. The concluding blessing, Asher Nasan Lanu, is Rabbinic according to all opinions. Regarding the opening blessing, Asher Bachar Banu, the poskim disagree whether it is D'Oraisa or Rabbinic. This applies only if he has not yet recited Bircas HaTorah. If he already recited Bircas HaTorah, all agree that it is Rabbinic, instituted out of respect for the congregation, as stated in seif 8. If he erred in Bircas HaTorah and began Asher Nasan Lanu instead of Asher Bachar Banu, then if he remembers before saying Hashem's Name in the conclusion, meaning before Noten HaTorah, he should begin Asher Bachar Banu. If he already said Baruch Atah Hashem, even though he has not yet said Noten HaTorah, he should complete that blessing, and after the reading recite Asher Bachar Banu. Derech HaChayim. He further writes that if he recited the first blessing correctly, but in the second blessing erred and began Asher Bachar Banu, then if he remembers before saying Baruch Atah Hashem, Noten HaTorah, he should begin Asher Nasan Lanu. If he did not remember until after saying Baruch Atah Hashem, Noten HaTorah, he should begin the blessing again from its beginning. If he remembers after Baruch Atah Hashem but before Noten HaTorah, he should immediately say Elokeinu Melech HaOlam, Asher Nasan Lanu, etc.

(יז) ואח"כ יברך - ר"ל שאין צריך לגלול הס"ת בשעת ברכתו וליכא למיחש שמא יאמרו ברכות כתובות בתורה והוא מברך מן הספר דהכל יודעין שאין ברכות כתובות בס"ת. ודוקא בברכה ראשונה לא חששו לזה משום טרחא דצבורא שיצטרכו להמתין עד שיחזיר ויפתח אבל בברכה אחרונה דליכא טעם זה וגם דבלא"ה צריך לגלול הס"ת בין גברא לגברא בודאי עדיף יותר שיגלול קודם הברכה וזהו שמסיים המחבר דלאחר שקורא גולל ומברך:
**** ENGLISH ****
(17) And afterward he recites the blessing. Meaning, he need not roll the Sefer Torah closed while reciting the blessing. There is no concern that people will say the blessings are written in the Torah and that he is reading the blessing from the scroll, since everyone knows that the blessings are not written in a Sefer Torah. Specifically regarding the first blessing, they were not concerned because of burdening the congregation, who would have to wait until he rolls it back and opens it. Regarding the concluding blessing, however, that reason does not apply. Moreover, the Sefer Torah must in any event be rolled between one reader and the next. It is therefore certainly preferable to roll it before the blessing. This is what the Mechaber means when he concludes that after reading, he rolls it and recites the blessing.

(32) For it is no less than one who was exempted, etc. As explained above in siman 47, seif 7. He must complete the other blessings and afterward recite Bircas Kohanim, which is customarily said each day.

(לה) בס"ת בשעת ברכה - היינו דיאחוז בעמודי ס"ת ועיין בב"ח וט"ז שהסכימו דבעת הקריאה צריך ג"כ לאחוז בס"ת. יש נוהגין לאחוז אז ביריעות עצמן ע"י מפה ועיין במ"א. אחר הקריאה מנהג לנשק הס"ת וכשנזדמן לו רוק ירוק [אך מן הצד ולא כנגד הס"ת דאסור כמבואר ביו"ד סימן ר"ע] ואח"כ ינשק ולא ינשק ואח"כ ירוק [אחרונים]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(35) The Sefer Torah during the blessing. Meaning, he should hold the handles of the Sefer Torah. See Bach and Taz, who agree that during the reading he should likewise hold the Sefer Torah. Some have the custom then to hold the parchment itself through a cloth; see Magen Avraham. After the reading, the custom is to kiss the Sefer Torah. If saliva comes to his mouth, he should spit, but to the side and not opposite the Sefer Torah, which is forbidden as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 270, and afterward kiss it. He should not kiss it and then spit. Later authorities.
