[4] <b>And this requires examination, for in the Yerushalmi, etc.</b> It says that a non-Jew cannot make his fellow non-Jew an agent, etc. Regarding transferring the place, many were confused. In Sefer Chok Yaakov he wrote in the name of Responsa Even Shoham that the intent is not that by transferring the place it becomes the non-Jew's courtyard and his courtyard acquires the chametz for him, for courtyard is derived through agency, and there is no agency for a non-Jew. Rather, the intent must be by way of agav, that movable goods are acquired together with land. <small>According to this, he must use the language of agav and not the language of "with," as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 202, according to one view there.</small> However, Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, end of siman 120, wrote that for this reason the Tur in Yoreh De'ah, siman 320, regarding removing the sanctity of a firstborn, wrote that the clearest of the acquisitions is that he transfer the place to him. In that responsum he gave the reason why this acquisition is the clearest: with pulling, the non-Jew's intent is only to acquire with money, and whenever the buyer does not intend to acquire, the acquisition is ineffective. He brought proof from Bava Metzia 10, where a poor person fell on pe'ah and they remove him from it, for he did not acquire it. Although we hold that a person's four cubits acquire for him, nevertheless, since he fell on it, he revealed that he wants to acquire by falling and not through his four cubits, and therefore the four cubits do not acquire for him. Thus the acquisition depends on the buyer's intent. So too wrote Beis Yosef in siman 134, and also the Bach there, and he wrote that Responsa Maharam Mintz wrote likewise and brought many proofs. Therefore transferring the place is preferable, because his courtyard acquires for him without his knowledge; this is the content of his words. According to this, he necessarily holds that the reason is that a courtyard acquires for a non-Jew, not because of agav. For if it were because of agav, we would still require the non-Jew's intent, and on the contrary, acquisition by agav is difficult to explain to a non-Jew, and it is unlikely that the non-Jew intends to acquire by agav. Rather, according to Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, the reason must be through the law of courtyard. But then the difficulty of Responsa Even Shoham mentioned above applies to him, for there is no agency for a non-Jew. I found that in Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, end of siman 105, he sensed this and wrote: one can also say, regarding how a non-Jew's courtyard acquires for him, since courtyard is based on agency and we hold there is no agency for a non-Jew, and I wrote at length about this in another responsum. This is his language. I did not find anything about this in his printed responsa that came into my hands. Perhaps one can say that he holds like the Mas'as Binyamin brought by Magen Avraham, that a non-Jew can become an agent for a non-Jew, since both are not members of the covenant. Only when the sender is a member of the covenant must the agent also be a member of the covenant, and the same in reverse: when the agent is a member of the covenant, the sender must also be a member of the covenant. But when both sender and agent are not members of the covenant, it is acceptable. According to this, even though courtyard is based on agency, nevertheless a courtyard acquires for a non-Jew, because the courtyard is also not a member of the covenant and can become an agent for a non-Jew. Responsa Even Shoham follows its own approach, for it was not clear to him whether a non-Jew can become an agent for a non-Jew, as written in Sefer Chok Yaakov in his name, and therefore he wrote not to transfer to a non-Jew by way of courtyard, but only by way of agav. If so, since Magen Avraham proved from the Yerushalmi that a non-Jew cannot become an agent for a non-Jew, then we hold like Responsa Even Shoham, that courtyard does not acquire for a non-Jew, and one must transfer the chametz to him by way of agav.

[5] The same applies regarding challah in the Gemara, page 47, and below in siman 457, se'if 2, that one who separates challah from impure dough on Yom Tov of Pesach may not designate it as challah until it is baked. For if he were to designate it as challah while it is still dough, he would be forbidden to bake it, since it is not fit for any person, as it is impure. If the challah remains that way and he does not bake it, it will become chametz. Even though it does not belong to the Jew, and it also has not come into the kohen's possession, we nevertheless say that the Jew transgresses regarding it, since if he wishes he can request release from it; for it is like a vow, where one can ask a sage about vows and he releases him, and it returns to being untithed, and becomes his. Therefore, even if he did not request release from it, he transgresses because of "since," since it is in his power to request release from it. There it is different, for it is in the Jew's power to ask about his vow, and it is like his; since it is in his power, it is as though he did it.
