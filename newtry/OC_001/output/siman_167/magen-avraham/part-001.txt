**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the side of the bread.</b> This follows the other explanation, which holds that one breaks from the place where the bread begins to crust and bake. Some say this is the upper side, and some say it is the lower side; therefore one satisfies both views. The Darkei Moshe writes in the name of the Or Zarua that this means opposite the split, for that side began to bake and the dough was pressed until the opposite side split. Rashi writes that with a sliced loaf one should break only from the edge of the bread and not from its middle. With our bread one should cut above; see what I wrote at the end of siman 168, seif 3. The Maharil would cut around the loaf like a ring, leaving it attached in the middle, and would then slice from bottom to top. With a thin cake, he would place his eight fingers above and his two thumbs below, recite the blessing, and slice.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>HaMotzi.</b> What the Beit Yosef wrote, that there are variant readings, requires study, for at the end of daf 52 it is stated explicitly that one must bless for what has passed, and so too in Eruvin 19a. Perhaps there is a scribal error in the texts, and they wrote a form meaning brings forth in place of a form meaning brought forth, confusing the letters. For this reason one should bless with the proper past-tense pronunciation. The Chok Yaakov wrote to bless with a different vowelization because all blessings are in the present participle, like borei. But the Gemara states explicitly that borei can imply the past; nevertheless, we say borei because it is the scriptural wording. So wrote the Shiltei Gibborim, and so wrote the Knesses HaGedolah in siman 202. The Chok Yaakov also wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that even Beis Shammai disagreed only regarding fire, but regarding something renewed each year one blesses borei.

**** ENGLISH ****

So too wrote the Remazim, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the Piskei Rekanati, siman 80, that according to Rashi, if each one sits alone, even if the listener and the one reciting intended, he does not fulfill his obligation with his fellow's HaMotzi blessing, because they must be fixed together. There is no proof from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 213, for there, although there is no zimun for fruit, they had nevertheless established themselves together and fulfilled their obligation after the fact. This also appears from his novellae, where he asked that we rule that one who heard and did not answer fulfilled his obligation, and he left the matter requiring study. This implies that here even after the fact he did not fulfill his obligation. His question can be answered as in the Yerushalmi, that Birkas HaMazon was excluded from this rule, and the same is true of HaMotzi.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even on Shabbos.</b> This is implied by the Gemara, since they asked regarding the HaMotzi blessing over matzah whether one may bless for others, implying that on an ordinary Shabbos and Yom Tov it is obvious that one may not bless for others, as stated by the Beit Yosef.
