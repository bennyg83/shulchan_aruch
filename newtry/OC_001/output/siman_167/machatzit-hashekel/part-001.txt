**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 8: HaMotzi, etc.</b> Beit Yosef writes that there are variant readings. Berachos 38a states: “The Sages taught: HaMotzi lechem min haaretz; Rabbi Nechemiah says: Motzi.” Rava said: regarding Motzi, all agree that it means “He brought forth.” Rashi explains that this is certainly a proper blessing, because the wording must refer to the past, since Hashem has already brought forth the bread from which he is about to benefit. They disagree regarding HaMotzi: the Sages hold that it too means “He brought forth,” while Rabbi Nechemiah holds that it means “He will bring forth.” Beit Yosef's text of the Tur read: “He should bless HaMotzi, although Motzi is preferable, since all agree that it refers to the future and the blessing should refer to the future; nevertheless, the Talmud ruled HaMotzi.” The Tur thus states the reverse of the Gemara before us. The Gemara says the blessing must refer to the past and that Motzi means the past, while this reading of the Tur says it should refer to the future and that Motzi means the future. Beit Yosef therefore writes that the Tur had the opposite reading in the Gemara: regarding Motzi all agree that it means “He will bring forth,” that is, the future. He found this reading in Maareches Elokus, and Tosafos also appears to have had it, for Tosafos writes: “The halachah is HaMotzi, although regarding Motzi all agree that it means He will bring forth.”

Yet a blessing must refer to what Hashem has already done. According to Beit Yosef's reading, the Mishnah concerning fire states: Beis Shammai says, “Who created the light of fire,” and Beis Hillel says, “Who creates the lights of fire.” Rava said that all agree “created” refers to the past, and they disagree about “creates”: Beis Shammai understands it as future, while Beis Hillel understands it as past. Rav Yosef objects from “Who forms light and creates darkness.” Rabbi Yosef therefore concludes that all agree both “created” and “creates” refer to the past; they dispute only whether one says “light” or “lights.” It is thus clear that all agree the blessing must refer to the past.

The same appears in Eruvin 19a, where Reish Lakish says that the sinners of the nations do not repent even at the entrance of Gehinnom, based on the verse “the men who rebel against Me.” It does not say “who rebelled,” but “who rebel,” indicating that they continue rebelling. The Gemara objects: “But it is written HaMotzi and HaMaaleh. Do these too refer to the future?” According to the Tur's reading as explained by Beit Yosef, what is the objection from HaMotzi? On the contrary, the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Nechemiah provide support, for they hold HaMotzi refers to the future and the halachah follows them. Necessarily, the correct reading is the one before us: the required reference is to the past, and the Sages hold HaMotzi means that He already brought forth. The objection from the Sages, whose view is authoritative, is therefore sound.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And Chochmas Manoach wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi, etc.</b> The Yerushalmi asks that according to Beis Shammai one should say over wine, “Who created the fruit of the vine.” It answers that wine is renewed every year, whereas fire is not renewed every year. This means that regarding something renewed each year, Beis Shammai agrees one says borei, a participle that includes both past and future: the wine has already been created and will be created again, and we bless and praise Hashem for both. Fire is different. The blessing is not upon the particular fire before us, for then one would need to bless whenever producing fire. Rather, it is upon the fire created during the six days of Creation, which was created on Motzaei Shabbos, as Rashi explains. One therefore says “Who created,” referring to the past. This resolves Magen Avraham's earlier objection to Beit Yosef's reading that the blessing should refer to the future, for the fire blessing specifically must refer to the past because it concerns the primordial fire. According to this, one may likewise pronounce Shehakol nihyeh with a segol beneath the yud, as Chochmas Manoach writes, treating it as a participle. There is no proof against this from Borei Meorei HaEsh, because fire is exceptional and must refer to the past, as the Yerushalmi explains. Chochmas Manoach, however, brought his proof from borei, so his proof is invalid, although the ruling itself may be correct. This is also implied by Magen Avraham below, siman 204, seif katan 14, who cites Chochmas Manoach and refers back here, suggesting that he accepts the substance of the ruling. Responsa Meil Tzedakah, siman 42, likewise agrees that all blessings should use participial language and therefore nihyeh should have a segol. He concludes, however, that he heard that nihyah with a kamatz can also be participial, whereas with a segol it may be a language of request, as in “and may we and our descendants be.” This distinction requires further analysis.
