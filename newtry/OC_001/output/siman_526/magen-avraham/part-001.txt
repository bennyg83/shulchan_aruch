<b>לכסותו בעפר</b>. דגמר קבורה היא (ב"י) ולכאור' למנהג שלנו הוא אסור דהא כתב ת"ה שאנו נוהגין שלא לעשות מלאכה דאוריית' והרב"י כ' דסתימת הקבר הוי מלאכה דאוריית' ובס' ראב"ן כ' גבי יום טוב  ראשון שיכסוהו עכו"ם ובט"ז בי"ד כ' ראיתי קצת מקומות שישראלים מחזירים העפר לתוך הקבר והוא בעיני איסור גמור וכו' ע"ש ובלבוש כ' בי"ט שני יש אוסרין ויש מתירין ויש להקל עכ"ל אפש' דס"ל דלא הוי מלאכה דאורייתא דאמרי' בשבת דף פ"א אסו' לפנות בשדה ניר בשבת (פירש"י ור"ן בחרישה ראשונה ועומדת לזריעה) דלמ' שקיל צרור מעילאי ושדי ליה לתתאי והסותם גומא בשדה חייב משום חורש בבית חייב משום בונה ע"כ משמע דבשדה שאינו חרושה לא מחייב כלל מיהו אפשר דזה דמי לבית שמתקן בזה שלא יהא המת מגולה ולכן נ"ל דאסו' ובס' מ"ש החמיר מאד וכ' מקום שנהגו לקבור על ידי ישראל לא יחפרו אנשים אחרים זולת בני החבורה הרגילין לחפור וכ"ש שלא ישליכו אנשים אחרים עפר לסתום הקבר דכל מה דאפשר למעט בחלול ממעטי' עכ"ל ורש"מ סי' י"ו מתיר לכל ע"ש וכנ"ל דהא כחול שווי' רבנן וגדולה מזו אמרו גבי מילה בשבת דמותרים שני מוהלים למול כמ"ש בי"ד עסי' רס"ו ק"ו כאן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To cover it with earth.</b> For this is the completion of burial, as in the Beis Yosef. At first glance, according to our practice this is forbidden, for Terumas HaDeshen wrote that our practice is not to perform labor according to the Torah, and the Beis Yosef wrote that closing the grave is labor according to the Torah. In Sefer Raavan he wrote regarding the first day of Yom Tov that non-Jews should cover him. In the Taz in Yoreh Deah he wrote: I saw some places where Jews return the earth into the grave, and in my eyes this is a complete prohibition, etc.; see there. In Levush he wrote that on the second day of Yom Tov some forbid and some permit, and one may be lenient, end quote. Perhaps he holds that it is not labor according to the Torah, for we say in Shabbos, folio 81, that one may not relieve himself in a plowed field on Shabbos, for perhaps he will take a clod from above and throw it below; one who fills a hole in a field is liable because of plowing, and in a house because of building. This implies that in a field that is not plowed he is not liable at all. However, perhaps this is comparable to a house, since he repairs it thereby so that the dead will not be exposed. Therefore it seems to me that it is forbidden. In Sefer Masas Binyamin he was very stringent and wrote that in a place where the custom is to bury through Jews, no other men should dig except the members of the society accustomed to digging, and all the more so other people should not throw earth to close the grave, for whatever can be minimized in desecration, we minimize, end quote. Responsa of Shlomo ben Shimon Duran, siman 16, permits everyone; see there. This appears correct to me, for the Sages treated it like a weekday, and they said something even greater regarding circumcision on Shabbos, that two mohalim may circumcise, as written in Yoreh Deah; see siman 266. All the more so here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But moving is permitted.</b> But it is forbidden to take him out even to a karmelis. Although it implies from Rashi and the Rosh that it is permitted to wash him and cut his hair, nevertheless carrying out is more of a labor, for cutting hair on a corpse is not a labor at all, as in Terumas HaDeshen. In Yam Shel Shlomo he objected: the Rambam wrote in chapter 9 that one who shears, whether from a living being or from a corpse, is liable, and so it is in the Yerushalmi, chapter Klal Gadol. Rather, the view of the Rosh according to Rashi must be that a weighty labor is forbidden but carrying is permitted. Nevertheless, it seems to me that there is a textual error in Rashi and one must emend it; see there. Accordingly, it is forbidden to do anything for the need of tomorrow, end quote; see seif 8. It does not seem so to me, for what Terumas HaDeshen wrote, that cutting hair is not a labor at all, means because he has no need of the hair at all, whereas the Rambam speaks where he needs the hair; see what I wrote in siman 340. Although carrying out is also a labor not needed for its own sake, nevertheless the name carrying applies to it. But here it is not a labor at all, for we say in Shabbos that if one circumcised an eight-month infant on Shabbos, he has done nothing, for it is merely cutting flesh. All the more so hair. Nevertheless, it is good to be stringent, and so it appears from Terumas HaDeshen and the Shulchan Aruch.

This is no worse than cutting myrtle for him, which is for his honor. If there are graves there and he instructed that they bring him to the graves of his ancestors, although it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead, and especially in such a case, where it is his honor to be buried among his ancestors, nevertheless it is not his honor to desecrate Yom Tov for nothing. For if he had not instructed so, it would be forbidden to remove him from one grave to another, as the Or Zarua wrote in the first chapter of Moed Katan. Therefore his own actions and instruction caused this for him. Thus, where there is concern that he may putrefy, they bury him in his place; and if there is no concern that he may putrefy, they leave him overnight and bury him after Yom Tov in the graves of his ancestors, end quote of Yam Shel Shlomo. See Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 363, seif 2.

<b>אסור אפי' וכו'</b>. דאין מצוה לקבור נפל וא"כ אין י"ט נדחה לצורכי קבורתו ועוד דמה שנהגו להסירה אינו מנהג של תורה אלא מנהג בעלמא משום תקנו' הפושעים דאמרי בב"ר שהקב"ה מעביר הערל' מן הקטנים ונותנ' על פושעי ישראל (הג"מ) וכ' בבד"ה דזה מיירי דידעי' בודאי שלא כלו לו חדשיו דאל"כ קוברים אותו בי"ט כמ"ש ס"ט משמע דס"ל דקוברין אותו ואין מוהלין אותו, ובד"מ כ' דעכ"פ ימתין עד למחר וימול אותו אפי' גמרו שערו וצפרניו ומשמע דאם הוא אחר המיל' או שהי' נקבה מודה רמ"א דקוברין אותו בי"ט כמ"ש ס"ט ומ"ש בי"ד ססי' רס"ג דאין קוברים הנפלים בי"ט מיירי בנפל ודאי ובכ"ה כתב בשם ר"ש הלוי סי' י"ד דאם הוא מבן ל' ולמעלה שמתאבלין עליו מסירין ערלתו בי"ט שני של גליות ובתוספות שנ"ץ מתירין לקבור נפל גמור בי"ט עכ"ל ולי צ"ע מ"ש הג"מ דאין מצוה לקבור נפל ומביא ראי' מדאמרי' בגמ' שפחה שהטילה נפל לבור ואמרי' בור שמטילין לתוכו נפלים צ"ע דהא מ"מ קבורה היא כמ"ש הרא"ש פי"ד מ"ג באהלות ועי"ל דמיירי בנפל שהוא שפיר כדאי' בע"א ועוד דהא אמרי' בנדה דף נ"ז הכותים לא היו קוברים את הנפלים אלא משליכין מאי דרוש כל שיש לו נחלה יש לו גבול משמע דאנן לא קי"ל כוותייהו ועוד מדאיצטריך קרא בת"כ פ' אמור שאין הכהן מטמא לבנו ובתו הנפלים ש"מ שמצוה לקוברן שהרי אין הכהן מטמא אלא לצורך קבורה כמ"ש בי"ד סי' שע"ג ס"ה ועוד דבב"ב ספ"ז איתא שהיו עושין כוכין לנפלים וכ"ה בסנה' פ"ו וצ"ע דלכאורה משמע בנדה דף הנ"ל שגם על הנפלים קאי לאו דלא תלין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden even, etc.</b> For there is no mitzvah to bury a stillborn, and therefore Yom Tov is not overridden for the needs of his burial. Furthermore, what they customarily do to remove it is not a Torah custom, but merely an ordinary custom because of the rectification of sinners, for they say in Bereishis Rabbah that the Holy One, blessed is He, removes the foreskin from the children and places it upon the sinners of Israel, as in Hagahos Maimoniyos. In Bedek HaBayis he wrote that this speaks where we know with certainty that his months were not complete, for otherwise we bury him on Yom Tov, as written in seif 9. This implies that he holds that we bury him but do not circumcise him. In Darchei Moshe he wrote that in any event one should wait until tomorrow and circumcise him, even if his hair and nails were fully formed. It implies that if it is after the circumcision, or if the infant was female, the Rama agrees that we bury him on Yom Tov, as written in seif 9. What is written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 263, that one does not bury stillborn infants on Yom Tov, refers to a definite stillborn. In Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, siman 14, that if he is thirty days old or more and they mourn for him, they remove his foreskin on the second day of Yom Tov of the diaspora. In Tosafot Shantz they permit burying a complete stillborn on Yom Tov, end quote. To me, what Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote requires study, that there is no mitzvah to bury a stillborn, and he brings proof from what we say in the Gemara, that a maidservant cast a stillborn into a pit, and we say: a pit into which stillborn infants are cast. This requires study, for nevertheless it is burial, as the Rosh wrote in chapter 14, mishnah 3 of Ohalos. One can also answer that it speaks of a stillborn that is merely a shapeless mass, as stated elsewhere. Furthermore, we say in Niddah, folio 57, that the Cutheans would not bury stillborn infants but would cast them away. What did they expound? Whoever has inheritance has a boundary. This implies that we do not rule like them. Furthermore, since a verse was needed in Toras Kohanim, parshas Emor, that a kohen does not become impure for his stillborn son or daughter, this shows that it is a mitzvah to bury them, for a kohen becomes impure only for the need of burial, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 373, seif 5. Furthermore, at the end of chapter 7 of Bava Basra it states that they would make niches for stillborn infants, and so too in Sanhedrin, chapter 6. This requires study, for at first glance it implies in the above folio in Niddah that the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight also applies to stillborn infants.

<b>קודם תפלה</b>. כדי שיוכלו האוננים להתפלל ובזמנינו שעושין הכל ע"י עכו"ם אין לקוברן עד אחר אכילה (מ"ב סוף הספר) משמע דעתו כיון שקוברין ע"י עכו"ם מותרין האוננים להתפלל וצ"ע דהא אית' סי' ע"א דפטורין מתפלה ואפשר דס"ל כיון שאין רוצים לקברו עד אחר אכילה חייבים להתפלל כמו בלילה כמ"ש סי' תקמ"ח וביש"ש כ' ראיתי נוהג שקוברים קודם אכילה ואף על פי שכ'  הכל בו קודם תפילה במדינתינו א"א להקדים כ"כ ומ"ש לקברו אחר אכיל' ל"נ דאי משום שמחת י"ט קאמרי הא ליתא דאם מת מצוה קודם למגילה ק"ו לשמחת י"ט ובזמנינו הכל נקר' מ"מ ועוד דאיתא במדרש דאסור לאכול סעודת קבע קודם שנקבר המת ולכן אם הוא אדם חשוב קוברים אותו מיד אחר יציאת בה"כ ואם לאו מקבצים מנין וקוברין אותו בשעה שהש"ץ אומר פיוטים עכ"ל ועכשיו נוהגים לקוברו בשחרית וע"פ הרוב נמשך בשעת התפלה ויש ליזהר בזה עסי' ע"ב ס"ב ונ"ל דאם הוא אדם חשוב שצריכים רבים ללוותו יוציאהו לבה"ק אחר יציאת בה"כ ויאכלו ואח"כ יקברוהו כראוי כמ"ש סי' תמ"ג ועמ"ש סי' תקמ"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Before prayer.</b> In order that the onenim will be able to pray. In our times, when everything is done through non-Jews, they should not bury them until after eating, as in Mateh Binyamin at the end of the book. It implies that his view is that since they bury through non-Jews, the onenim are permitted to pray. This requires study, for it is stated in siman 71 that they are exempt from prayer. Perhaps he holds that since they do not want to bury him until after eating, they are obligated to pray, as at night, as written in siman 548. In Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote: I have seen the practice that they bury before eating. Although Kol Bo wrote before prayer, in our country it is impossible to do so early. But what they wrote, to bury him after eating, does not seem correct to me. If they say this because of the joy of Yom Tov, this is not so, for if a meis mitzvah precedes reading the Megillah, all the more so does he precede the joy of Yom Tov. In our times everyone is considered a meis mitzvah. Furthermore, it is stated in the Midrash that it is forbidden to eat a fixed meal before the dead is buried. Therefore, if he is an important person, they bury him immediately after leaving the synagogue; and if not, they gather a minyan and bury him while the shaliach tzibbur is saying piyyutim, end quote. Nowadays the practice is to bury him in the morning, and for the most part it extends into the time of prayer, and one must be careful about this; see siman 72, seif 2. It seems to me that if he is an important person whom many people must escort, they should take him out to the cemetery after leaving the synagogue, eat, and afterward bury him properly, as written in siman 443.
