<b> אסור להלינו עד יו"ט שני</b> -  עיין מ"ב מ"ש בשם האחרונים דאם השהו אותו שאין לקוברו ע"י ישראל והוא בת' מנחם עזריה סימן ט"ז והביאו גם בא"ר ומסיים ע"ז בא"ר ואם אין עממים צ"ע אם יש לקברו ע"י ישראל. ולענ"ד נראה פשוט בזה דבודאי אין משהין אותו עד אחר יו"ט וקוברין אותו ע"י ישראל דכי מה פשע המת שהשהו אותו. אלא דגם עיקר דינו דמנחם עזריה ג"כ אינו נראה דהא לכמה רבוותא קיי"ל כמר זוטרא ביו"ט א' שאף רב אשי מודה ליה וא"כ לדידהו מן הדין צריך להשהות אותו עד יו"ט ב' ונהי דאנן לא נקטינן כהנך פוסקים ואדרבה יותר טוב לקברו ביומו מ"מ לא מצינו דאם השהה אותו דליקנסיה למת שלא לקברו ע"י ישראל במקום דלכמה פוסקים היה צריך לעשות כן לכתחלה ותו דגם עיקר דינו דמחבר אינו מוסכם והראב"ד הלא עשה הלכה למעשה להשהות לת"ח ליו"ט שני כדי שיקבר על ידי ישראל אלא שהאו"ח כתב שחלקו עליו עי"ש בב"י אמנם במאירי הביא גם כן להא דראב"ד ולהני דחלקו עליו והוא הסכים להראב"ד וכן פסק בשיטה מקובצת [אלא דבשמ"ק מבואר דדוקא באדם כשר ונכבד שיש לו בזיון לקוברו ע"י נכרים והמאירי אף שהביא להא דראב"ד דמיירי בת"ח אמנם מסיום דבריו וז"ל ומדקאמר כחול שויוה רבנן לא אשתייר ביה ולא מידי לשום ענין של מת עכ"ל משמע דבכל מקום מותר להשהותן] ואם כן אף שאין להקל לכתחלה אפילו בת"ח נגד סתימת המחבר אבל לקנסו מנ"ל מה גם דהקנס הוא למת ולא שייך למקנסיה דלא פשע מידי ועיין בשער הציון מה שהבאנו בשם העולת שבת והגר"א דנראה מדבריהם דפירשו דמשום איסור הלנת המת נגעו בה ולדידהו בודאי דל"ש כלל לקנסו ולבזותו עוד יותר שלא לקברו כלל או לקברו דוקא ע"י נכרי וכן מצאתי להרב יעב"ץ כסברתם וחילק גם בעיקר דינו דמחבר וז"ל נראה דמיירי במת דאסור להלינו לאפוקי מת שמלינו לכבודו להביא לו ארון ותכריכין דודאי שרי דאפילו בחול מצוה להלינו וה"ה באב ואם כיון דהמדחה הרי זה מגונה א"כ מסתמא זהו כבודו אפילו בחול כ"ש ביו"ט דאיכא סברא בלא"ה דאי לא אישתהי שצריך להשהותו לקבור אותו בב' על ידי ישראל ולא בראשון ע"י עכו"ם ואף שיש סברא כנגד זה דכבוד יו"ט הוא למהר להוציאו מ"מ מאן לימא לן לידחי הא מקמי הא כ"ש אם האבלים רוצים בכך שיש כבוד החיים והמתים נראה דבכה"ג סברת ראב"ד עדיפא עכ"ל. היוצא מכל הדברים האלה דאף שקשה לחלוק על המחבר כסברת עו"ש גם לא לחלק בו כסברת המור וקציעה שכפי הנראה לא עמדו רק אטעמא דאיסור הלנת המת ולא עמדו אטעמא שהבאנו באיסור שהייה מ"מ בעבר והשהה נראה דאין לקנוס כלל שאף לטעמא דידן אם הוא פשע מת מה פשע ולמאירי ושמ"ק [אף שהם נחתו לטעמא דשבות שיש בו מעשה] למסקנא דיום טוב שני כחול וכו' מותר לכתחלה במקום שכבודו יותר בישראלים א"כ עכ"פ בדיעבד יש לסמוך ולקברו ע"י ישראל ובפרט אם הוא ת"ח דזילא מילתא לקוברו ע"י עממין כן נראה לענ"ד וכ"ז הוא לפי סברת המחבר דלרמ"א בלא"ה קוברין ע"י עממין אף ביו"ט שני:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to leave him overnight until the second day of Yom Tov</b> - See Mishnah Berurah for what he wrote in the name of the Acharonim: that if they delayed him so as not to bury him through a Jew, that is in Responsa Menahem Azariah, siman 16, and Eliyah Rabbah also cited it; and Eliyah Rabbah concludes on this: and if there are no non-Jews, it requires further study whether to bury him through a Jew. And in my humble opinion it seems simple in this that certainly one does not delay him until after Yom Tov, and they bury him through a Jew: for what sin did the dead commit, that they delayed him? Rather, even the main ruling of Menahem Azariah also does not seem [correct], for according to several early authorities we hold like Mar Zutra on the first day of Yom Tov, that even Rav Ashi concedes to him; and if so, according to them by law one must delay him until the second day of Yom Tov. And granted that we do not follow those posekim, and on the contrary it is better to bury him on his day: nevertheless we have not found that if someone delayed him we should penalize the dead by not burying him through a Jew, in a case where according to several posekim one needed to do so ideally. And furthermore, even the main ruling of the Mechaber is not agreed upon; and the Raavad in fact ruled in practice to delay a Torah scholar until the second day of Yom Tov so that he be buried through a Jew; only that Orchos Chaim wrote that they disputed him; see there in Beis Yosef. However, the Meiri also cited that of the Raavad and those who disputed him, and he agreed with the Raavad; and so ruled the Shittah Mekubetzes [only that in the Shittah Mekubetzes it is clear that this is specifically regarding a proper and honored person for whom it is a disgrace to bury him through non-Jews; and the Meiri, even though he cited that of the Raavad which deals with a Torah scholar, nevertheless from the end of his words (and this is his language: "and since they said the Rabbis made it like a weekday, nothing remains of it for any matter of the dead"; end quote) it is implied that everywhere it is permitted to delay them]. And if so, even though one should not be lenient ideally even regarding a Torah scholar against the plain ruling of the Mechaber, still: to penalize him, from where? Especially since the penalty is upon the dead, and it is not fitting to penalize him, for he committed no offense. And see Shaar HaTziyun for what we brought in the name of Olas Shabbos and the Gra, from whose words it appears that they explained that they touched upon the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight; and according to them it certainly does not apply at all to penalize him and disgrace him further by not burying him at all, or to bury him specifically through a non-Jew. And so I found in Yaavetz according to their reasoning, and he also distinguished regarding the main ruling of the Mechaber; and this is his language: it appears that it deals with a dead person whom it is forbidden to leave overnight, excluding a dead person whom they leave overnight for his honor to bring him a coffin and shrouds, for certainly it is permitted, for even on a weekday it is a mitzvah to leave him overnight; and the same applies regarding a father and mother, since thrusting them aside is disgraceful: if so, presumably this is his honor even on a weekday, all the more so on Yom Tov, where there is in any case a reason that if he has not lingered one must delay him to bury him on the second [day] through a Jew and not on the first through a non-Jew. And even though there is a contrary reason that the honor of Yom Tov is to hasten to remove him: nevertheless, who tells us to push aside one before the other? All the more so if the mourners want thus, that there is honor of the living and of the dead: it appears that in such a case the Raavad's reasoning is preferable; end quote. What emerges from all these words is that even though it is hard to dispute the Mechaber according to the reasoning of Olas Shabbos, and also not to distinguish in it according to the reasoning of Mor UKetzi'ah, who apparently stood only on the reason of the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight and did not stand on the reason we brought for the prohibition of delaying: nevertheless, where one transgressed and delayed, it appears that one should not penalize at all; for even according to our reason, if he is a sinner, what sin did the dead commit? And according to the Meiri and Shittah Mekubetzes [even though they descended to the reason of a shevus that involves an act], according to the conclusion that the second day of Yom Tov is like a weekday, etc., it is permitted ideally where his honor is greater through Jews: if so, at least after the fact one may rely and bury him through a Jew, and especially if he is a Torah scholar, for whom it is degrading to bury him through non-Jews. So it appears in my humble opinion. And all this is according to the Mechaber's reasoning; for according to the Rama, in any case they bury through non-Jews even on the second day of Yom Tov.

<b> וחוזרין למקומם בו ביום</b> -  עיין מ"ש במ"ב לענין אם יש שם מושב יהודים, וסמוכים לדברינו מדברי הרדב"ז ח"ד סימן ק"ז שכתב בביאור דברי הכלבו ד' מ"ו שהתירו חכמי בדרש במת שהיה בעיר אקד"י ללותו עד בדרש שנים מן הקרובים ולרכוב בדרך אך לא שבו אותו היום למקומם אך נשארו בעיר בדרש היום ההוא ע"כ וכתב ע"ז הרדב"ז דלהכי לא התירו להם לחזור כיון שהיו במקום מיושב ויותר טוב ממקומם מה צורך שיחזרו וליכא הכא טעמא שלא תהא מכשילן לע"ל אבל בנידן דידן שהביה"ק הוא במקום שמם ומדבר ואפילו אילו אין שם להסתר מן השמש ואין שם לא מאכל ומשתה ודאי דאיכא טעמא שלא תהא מכשילן עכ"ל ודע עוד דיש להסתפק במקומות שהביה"ק הוא רחוק מאד עד שאין שום אדם מלוה עד ביה"ק בשום פעם רק יש אנשים מחברה קדישא שקבועים תדיר לשאת המת שם ובאים על שכרם אם התירו בדידהו לחזור ואי שייך בהו טעמא כדי שלא תכשילם שהרי הם שכורים והולכים בשביל שכרם ואם בשביל שיצטרכו האבלים לשלם ביותר בעבור זה צ"ע אם מתירים להו בשביל זה לחזור כמה מילין מיהו ד' אלפים אמה בודאי יכולין לחזור שהרי עכ"פ לא גרע מכל היוצאין ברשות שיש להם ד' אלפים בהבלעה וכדלעיל בסימן ת"ה אבל ביותר מד' אלפים שמתירין אנו רק בשביל טעמא שלא תהא מכשילן צ"ע אם שייך בזה ועוד מסתפקנא אם גבי האבלים נמי אמרי' דחוזרין למקומם דלכאורה בדידהו לא שייך דמימנע דודאי לא ימנעו ללות אביהם בשביל זה ולא שייך אלא באנשים דעלמא ואפשר דזהו טעמא דעובדא דמייתי בכלבו הנ"ל שלא התירו להם לחזור משום שהיו קרובים למת ולא מימנעו מללוות ומסתימת הפוסקים משמע שאין לחלק בזה וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And they return to their place that same day</b> - See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah regarding if there is a Jewish settlement there; and supporting our words are the words of Radbaz, part 4, siman 107, who wrote in explaining the words of Kol Bo, folio 46, that the sages of Beziers permitted regarding a dead person who was in the city of Aqde to escort him to Beziers: two of the relatives, and to ride on the way; but they did not return that day to their place, but remained in the city of Beziers that day; end quote. And Radbaz wrote on this: therefore they did not permit them to return, since they were in an inhabited place and better than their place; what need have they to return? And here there is no reason of "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them in the future." But in our case, where the cemetery is in a desolate place and wilderness, and even if there is nowhere to shelter from the sun, and there is neither food nor drink: certainly there is the reason of "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them"; end quote. And know further that one should be in doubt in places where the cemetery is very far, such that no person ever escorts as far as the cemetery, but there are people of the Chevrah Kaddisha who are constantly appointed to carry the dead there and come for their wage: whether regarding them they permitted to return, and whether the reason "so that you not cause them to stumble" applies, for they are hired and go for their wage; and if because the mourners would have to pay more for this, it requires further study whether we permit them for this to return several miles. However, four thousand amos they can certainly return, for in any case they are no worse than all who go out with permission, who have four thousand with absorption, as above in siman 405; but beyond four thousand, which we permit only because of the reason "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them," it requires further study whether this applies. And I further doubt whether regarding the mourners too we say that they return to their place; for apparently regarding them "he will refrain" does not apply, for certainly they will not refrain from escorting their father because of this, and it applies only to ordinary people. And perhaps this is the reason of the incident cited in Kol Bo above, that they did not permit them to return because they were relatives of the dead and would not refrain from escorting. And from the plain wording of the posekim it is implied that one should not distinguish in this; and it requires further study.

<b> אם א"א וכו'</b> -  עיין בט"ז דר"ל שיש להם טורח קצת לילך ברגל ושרי ואזיל לשיטתיה שכתב בתחלה שעיקר דין המחבר מגומגם אצלו עיין שם אבל לפי מה שפירש המגן אברהם וא"ר דמשום זילותא דיו"ט הוא אין להתיר ברכיבה אלא א"כ יש טורח גדול לילך ברגליו וכן כתב בספר נהר שלום והגר"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it is impossible, etc.</b> - See Taz, meaning that they have some exertion to walk on foot and it is permitted; and he goes according to his view, for he wrote initially that the Mechaber's main ruling is unclear to him; see there. But according to what Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah explained, that it is because of the degradation of Yom Tov, one should not permit riding unless there is great exertion to walk on foot; and so wrote Sefer Nehar Shalom and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav.

<b>It is permitted to go out on the road, etc.</b> - And in the gloss: and it is permitted to bring a dead person, etc. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 17, who cited the view of the Rivash who disputes this, because one does not make a coffin except with the dead in the courtyard, and all the more so here, where one who sees will say he is going for his own need; and Magen Avraham disputed this, for it is explained there that in a publicized matter it is permitted (and Machatzis HaShekel explained: since it was told to them by other informants, if so the matter is publicized); and also one need not be concerned that Jews will encounter them on the way, for on Yom Tov Jewish wayfarers are not common. However, if he is not lying in the field but in a house, one should be stringent; end quote. And behold, Magen Avraham abbreviated greatly, and also contradicts his own words, for in s.k. 7 he wrote that going outside the techum is comparable to cutting cedars, which is forbidden even regarding a well-known person. Also the very reasoning of Machatzis HaShekel is not clear, for the Rivash in this responsum cited the law of a well-known person and did not descend to say that here too it is like a well-known person; and Magen Avraham himself also wrote in s.k. 7 that if it happened to become publicized by chance, it is nothing. And even if we say that Magen Avraham's intent is as its plain sense, because since we are few in our sins, our law is like a well-known person, as the posekim wrote: nevertheless the first difficulty remains in place, for regarding cutting cedars a well-known dead person does not help; and so the Rivash intended, as is explicit there, and as Magen Avraham also copied: "all the more so here, where one who sees will say he is going for his own need," and that is also the reason of cutting cedars. And what Magen Avraham further wrote, that one need not be concerned that Jews will encounter them, for wayfarers are not common, is also difficult from that s.k. above. And afterward I found that R' Akiva Eiger in his chiddushim also asked from this se'if against Magen Avraham's words in s.k. 7, and answered: since the dead is lying in disgrace in the field, they were more lenient. And it is astonishing, for one who examines this se'if in Beis Yosef will see that it stems from the words of Tashbetz, who disputed what was ruled in Responsa Rivash to be stringent in this (see there, part 1, siman 22); and in Responsa Rivash the incident was that the non-Jews shut him in a cave with his garments, as Beis Yosef copied, and even so the Shulchan Aruch ruled to be lenient. Indeed, when I examined Kenesses HaGedolah in all the places, and in Rivash and in Tashbetz (who even in such a case did not copy the content of his responsum), I saw that the law of s.k. 7, that going outside the techum is comparable to cutting cedars, stems from Responsa Rivash, siman 116; see there; and his words in s.k. 17 are like the reasoning of Tashbetz, who disputed the Rivash's incident. And even though Magen Avraham did not see the responsum, he intended part of it; and we will copy from there the content of the words: (1) that we are few in our places, and our law is like a well-known dead person, as Tosafos wrote; (2) that they forbade in public only where it is possible in private, or to delay him one day when he does not become so disgraced through this delay; but a matter like this, even in public, one should permit, and it resembles that of Rav Yosef regarding the beams, of which he said "privacy of these days," as Rashi explains there, since it is impossible; and here too likewise, for the hour presses and it is impossible to wait more because of concern of putrefaction or dogs eating or burning: we should not be concerned about publicity [and it seems that the source of the words of R' Eliezer of Jerusalem who permits even regarding cutting cedars where it is otherwise impossible, which Magen Avraham cited in siman 547, end of s.k. 7 (and one should read there "R' Eliezer of Jerusalem," and he is some posek, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote there), is from these words]; (3) that there is no publicity here, for those who go outside the techum [and see Moed Katan 12 regarding a contracted weekday job], and there are only the buryers, and going out of the city outside the techum is not called publicity, for it is permitted to go outside the techum by means of burganin; (4) one need be concerned about publicity only regarding making a coffin and making a grave, which are melachos of the Torah. He also wrote: since we have not found regarding this a prohibition, from where should we be stringent by our reasoning in a rabbinic prohibition? And all the more so in this, which touches upon human dignity; and especially in this, where there is none to tend to him, he is a met mitzvah, for whom even a High Priest becomes impure; and on a weekday certainly an obligation rests upon us to bury him; and if so, the same applies on the second day of Yom Tov, which is like a weekday; and on the contrary, such a dead person is more severe than if he were in the city. Also the claim that otherwise there will not be enough time to bury him is not a claim, for whenever a Yom Tov act hastens his burial we do not care, for all the time he delays he increases becoming disgraced; and all the more so if there is concern of dogs eating and of burning; end quote of Tashbetz; see there where he elaborates. And in the general of his words are included the words of Magen Avraham in s.k. 17 and in siman 547, s.k. 10 and s.k. 7; only that what Magen Avraham concludes, that one should be lenient when lying in the field, but when lying in a house, not: is not exact, for apparently it seems at first that he thought the Rivash's incident was of one lying in the field, and therefore the permissive views were concerned about dogs eating; but in truth in Responsa Rivash it is explicit that the idolaters placed him in a cave with his garments and closed the mouth of the cave; see there; only that since he was delivered into their hands, they were concerned that because of the greatness of the putrefaction they would burn him or leave him for dogs to eat; and even in such a case the Mechaber copied Tashbetz's view to permit. And if one should distinguish, he should have distinguished between one lying in the hands of non-Jews or guarded with his family, even if there is none there to bury him or there is no cemetery there [and perhaps after he first cited the view of Yam Shel Shlomo who is stringent in every case, he did not wish to be as lenient as Tashbetz except when lying in the field]. The short of the words is thus: according to the Rivash, even in his own incident which has several supporting factors he forbids, and all the more so regarding a dead person lying in a Jewish house, only that it is impossible to bury him there: certainly it is not proper to go there outside the techum to bring him, for he compares going outside the techum to cutting cedars. And according to Tashbetz's view, even regarding a dead person lying in his house outside the techum, if he is lying in a near place such that it may be possible to reach there to bury him afterward on Yom Tov, one should lean to permit, for it is not comparable to cutting cedars, which is a full melachah, and also going outside the techum is not called publicity, as above; and according to this, the same applies regarding a dead person lying here, and one needs to go outside the techum to bring shrouds and a coffin and the like from there: it is also permitted. And behold, the Mechaber appears to have held Tashbetz's view; and also the Rama, who copied Responsa Rashba in the gloss, and there the dead was lying far from the city and they went to bring him, implies likewise that according to him it is permitted to go outside the techum and bring a dead person from there. And from the Rashba himself there is no proof, for it is possible he permitted going lest they find the slain still twitching and they save him; see there in the responsum. But the Rama, who did not copy the content of the responsum, implies that regarding any dead person it is permitted to go and bring him [unless the Rama refers to the Mechaber's words, and there he was lying in the hands of non-Jews]. And I found in Kenesses HaGedolah that he stood on this and wrote that the Mechaber and the Rama follow Tashbetz's view; and he also cited Responsa Mahari Taitatzak who permitted in such a case even to ride on horses, and he disputed him there that riding on horses is certainly forbidden [however, where there is concern of dogs eating, Eliyah Rabbah wrote that it is permitted even to do melachah on the second day of Yom Tov in order to save him]; and also regarding going on foot he debates there at length whether it is permitted and concludes to forbid; and apparently Magen Avraham was concerned for his words. And in my humble opinion, where the dead has already lingered, one should be lenient to go outside the techum and bring him; and the same applies regarding a dead person lying here who has lingered, to go outside the techum and bring shrouds and the like of burial needs to bury him that same day: one should permit, like Tashbetz's reasoning; and especially since the Mechaber and Rama incline to this view. And know that all this that we wrote is on the second day of Yom Tov, but not on the first day of Yom Tov, for a met mitzvah does not override Shabbos and Yom Tov; however, through a non-Jew one should permit [Eliyah Rabbah].
