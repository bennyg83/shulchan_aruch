**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That one recites a blessing, etc.</b> As written in the Yerushalmi at the end of the sixth chapter of Berachos: It teaches the order of a meal. Guests enter and sit on benches and chairs until all have gathered. Wine was brought to them, each one recites a blessing for himself. Water was brought to them for the hands, each one washes one hand. This is a textual error, and one must read: before wine was brought to them, etc.; and so too in the latter clause. So it is also written in the Tosefta, and so it appears in our Gemara there. Refreshments were brought to them, each one recites a blessing for himself. They ascended and reclined. Wine was brought to them, and even though he recited a blessing over the first wine, he must recite a blessing over the second, and one recites the blessing on behalf of all of them. Water was brought to them for the hands, and even though he washed one hand, he must wash both hands, etc. Thus, he does not need to wash for refreshments, and these are types of mezonos, as Tosafot wrote on folio 42a, s.v. he recited a blessing, etc. This is what he wrote: but for bread, etc. This is unlike what is written in Terumas HaDeshen, that he washes one hand, and the Raah already challenged him; see there. For what relevance does all this have here? There, he washes only because of the wine.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 2, there are those, etc.</b> Because of uncertainty. This is from the Rokeach. His uncertainty is because in the second chapter of Sukkah they say: behold, an egg-size requires washing and a blessing, and they likewise seek to prove from there in chapter 25 regarding a cloth. However, one can say that perhaps Rabbi Tzadok follows his own view, for he maintains regarding Birkas HaMazon like Rabbi Yehudah, and the same would apply regarding washing. But according to us, since we rule like Rabbi Meir that an olive-size is sufficient, the same applies regarding washing, as written in seif 3. Alternatively, one can say that even according to Rabbi Meir, since less than an egg-size does not contract tumah, the Sages did not decree netilas yadayim, even though for itself it receives tumah with any amount, as Tosafot wrote in Sukkah 26b, s.v. they took, etc., from the Mishnah at the beginning of the second chapter of Taharos. Nevertheless, since according to the Torah it does not contract tumah, as written in Shabbos 91a, s.v. if, and Pesachim 33b, s.v. when. As to what the Magen Avraham wrote, that Tosafot and the Ran in the second chapter of Sukkah disagree with this, meaning the Tosafot there, s.v. they took, and so the Ran wrote there, I do not understand it; for with terumah, certainly even any amount requires washing, since hands are second-degree tumah and disqualify terumah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 3, if, etc.</b> See the Magen Avraham, that there is a textual error in the Rokeach and one must read: less than an egg-size. His proof is from what is written in Pesachim 116b: he washed his hands for dipping, etc.; it is not difficult because of matzah, for matzah suffices with an olive-size and does not require washing. This is unlike food dipped in liquid, which becomes tamei with any amount according to the Torah, as Tosafot wrote there, folio 14a, s.v. for there is, and even according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation, nevertheless it is because of the liquids themselves. But Tosafot there, s.v. requires, etc., wrote: it is not precise, etc. This is what the Magen Avraham wrote: and in Tosafot, Pesachim 116. It is not compelling, for Tosafot did not wish to force an explanation that throughout the whole meal he does not eat an egg-size of bread. But the essential halacha is that the measure for netilas yadayim is an olive-size, since it contracts tumah with any amount, except that they decreed only regarding something for which Birkas HaMazon is required, just as they did not decree regarding fruits or refreshments when he does not establish his meal upon them. Thus it depends on Birkas HaMazon. Therefore in Sukkah there they linked washing and blessing, and at the end of the eighth chapter of Berachos they supported them from one verse.

Tosafot's proof that this washing is not because of sanctity but so that the foods and liquids not be disqualified, from the fact that the Gemara challenges: but he did not touch, is not decisive. One can say that it initially assumed that they did not decree unless he touched the place of the liquid. What they wrote, like that case in Chullin 33a, means that it says: behold, it came out, etc., why are hands second-degree, etc.? But if they would make a third degree in chullin, washing would be required; and there it is so that the food not be disqualified, and here too. They also brought proof from the order of reclining in the sixth chapter of Berachos, that he washes one hand, and there it is so that the liquid not be disqualified. All this is not decisive, as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote, that he recites the blessing: Who commanded us, for He commanded us regarding cleanliness, and all this is included in the mitzvah to heed the words of Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah. It is all the more so from bread, where the reason is only because of a concern for terumah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And a cooked dish, etc.</b> That is to say, this does not refer specifically to fruits, but to anything that is not bread, as written in seif 1. And they are dry. This is specifically so. See Rabbeinu Shimshon, seventh chapter of Mikvaos, Mishnah 2, in the Yerushalmi, and see Beis Yosef. He maintains that gravy is considered a liquid, as written at the beginning of the third chapter of Taharos. Even though in Yoma, chapter 8, we say regarding brine that is upon, etc., according to the one who says, etc., behold, in Chullin 120a we say: there, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. and he answers, etc.: but here, etc. Rashi needed this, even though it comes out of the meat, as he wrote there, s.v. fat, etc.; nevertheless, since it comes out through cooking in water, it is a liquid, like sweat at the beginning of the second chapter of Machshirin. This is unlike the Taz and Magen Avraham, who wrote that liquid that is moist from itself is not considered liquid. The roasted item mentioned above is because it does not require rinsing at all. The words of Rashi in Berachos 52a, end of s.v. there is no, require study, even according to the Magen Avraham's explanation; for according to him, is there no concern for gravy?

<b>Seif 6, one who drinks, etc.</b> That is to say, even though we say in Berachos 43a: they brought, etc., Tosafot already answered there and in Chagigah, as above in seif 5. So too in Yoma 30a: the Sages taught, the halacha, etc.; Rav Chisda said, etc. Rashi already explained there, s.v. not, etc.: nevertheless, etc. The Rashba, however, requires him to wash one hand based on the above. Although in Yoma there it says: if he spoke with his fellow, etc., Terumas HaDeshen already guarded against this and said that what is stated, there is no difference, etc., applies only to urinating, that is, only to the latter clause, and when he washes, etc. But in truth he washes only one hand, unlike the Raah, who requires one who drinks in a fixed manner to wash both hands based on the above, and he wondered at the Rashba; but his words are not understandable. Therefore the Rashba wrote that this baraisa can be rejected, for perhaps washing is required there because of droplets, since it is referring to urinating. All this appears forced to Rashi, that the first clause of the baraisa should differ from the latter clause, and all the more so to say that the latter clause does not refer back to the first.

<b>Seif 8, one who was, etc.</b> He derived this from the end of the first chapter of Eruvin, regarding four matters, etc. But it is not compelling, as Rabbeinu Yehonasan wrote there: this applies specifically to a camp going out to war, even for an optional war, but not to a caravan traveling from place to place, for the law of a caravan regarding these four matters is like the law of an individual standing in his house, end quote. This is as written in the Gemara there, and as written in the Tosefta at the beginning of the first chapter of Eruvin: tents in a caravan are obligated in eruv, while in a camp they are exempt from eruv. This means a camp going out to war, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in the first chapter there. So too regarding netilas yadayim, as written in the third chapter of Pesachim, and derive from there regarding netilas yadayim, four mil, etc.

That is to say, as written at the end of the first chapter there, s.v. when not, etc.: wherever, etc. But the Rambam in chapter 11 of Hilchos Berachos wrote that he does not recite a blessing afterward, as stated there, 51a. What Hagahos Ashri distinguished between blessings over mitzvos and blessings of benefit is not implied there, for he brings proof from one who immersed and ascended, and it is rejected only because initially, etc. The proof of Hagahos Ashri is from what is written in Berachos there, that one recites the blessing of Yotzer Or after Shema. This is not decisive, for the blessings of Shema were not instituted over Shema, as the Rashba wrote. This is also the view of Tosafot in Pesachim there, s.v. at the time, and in Sukkah there; see there. But according to the first reason, he can recite a blessing, as written in Berachos there, and this reason also applies to lulav. See what I wrote in siman 651, and see Magen Avraham. In the gloss he ruled according to both reasons and leniently.

<b>Seif 13, one who immerses, etc.</b> Tosefta at the beginning of the second chapter of Yadayim. The Beis Yosef wrote: it appears to me that the reason drying is required is because of tamei water, unlike immersing, where there is no tamei water, as written above in siman 162, seif 1. This is what he wrote: and the same applies, etc., and as written there: and so, etc., for one who washes, etc. This too is for the same reason, that there is no tamei water, as above. A proof is that second waters are not required, as written at the beginning of the second chapter of Yadayim: if one washed his hand, etc., until he washes with a revi'is. Tosafot in Chullin there and the Rosh there explained that this means even if they came from remnants of purity, for with one hand it helps if the rinsing is like two rinsings; but a revi'is always helps and does not require two times. See siman 162, seif 4. This is the wording of the Tosefta there: one who washes his hands should not say regarding the first waters, behold I am washing the tamei ones; and if he did so, he must dry his hands. But one who immerses his hands need not dry his hands. This requires study. Perhaps instead of tamei ones one must read: halves, as Rabbeinu Shimshon wrote at the beginning of Yadayim, that one who washes half his hand and then the other half must dry his hands.
