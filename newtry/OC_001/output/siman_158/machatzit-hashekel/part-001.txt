<b>(סק"א) יטול כו'</b> אטו סרך תרומ' כו' דבתרומה שני עושה שלישי וכיון דגזרו על הידים שהן עסקניות שיהיו להם דין שני לטומאה ואם יגעו בתרומה יפסלו התרומ' שתעשה שלישי ולכן הצריכו אפי' לחולין לנטילה לטהר הידים ועי"ז יהיו נזהרים גם אוכלי תרומ':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 1) He should wash, etc.</b> Because of the trace of terumah, etc. For with terumah, second-degree impurity makes third-degree impurity. Since they decreed regarding the hands, which are busy, that they have the law of second-degree impurity, if they touch terumah they invalidate the terumah by making it third-degree. Therefore they required washing even for chullin in order to purify the hands, and through this those who eat terumah will also be careful.

<b>(סק"ד) פחות מכזית נוסחא מוטעת כו'</b> דסעיף זה מקורו מרוקח שהביא הרב"י וזה לשונו ונוטלין ידיהם(בליל פסח אחר סדר ההגדה קודם אכילה) ומברכים ענט"י מפני שצריך לטבל טיבול שני במרור (דס"ל כדעת רש"י דלקמן דצריך לברך ענ"י לצורך דבר שטיבולו במשק') אבל על אכילת מצה שהי' יוצא בכזית א"צ נטילה דעל פחות מכזית א"צ נטיל' כדאמרי' בסוכה ובברכות עכ"ל. כך הי' הגירסא לפני הרב"י עפ"ז כ' בש"ע דעל פחות מכזית אינו צריך נטיל' אע"ג דבסעיף שלפני זה כ' דעל פחות מכביצ' יטול ידיו ולא יברך משמע דפחות מכזי' קיל מפחות מכביצ' וכ' מ"א דט"ס הוא ברוקח וצ"ל דעל פחות מכביצ א"צ נטיל' דהכי אי' בסוכה דף כ"ו ע"ב במשנ' וכשנתנו לר' צדוק אוכל פחות כביצ' נטלו במפה ואכלו חוץ לסוכה כו' ופרש"י נטלו במפה לא נטל ידיו אלא כרך במפה ידיו משום נקיות עכ"ל דרש"י מן הסוברי' דפחות מכביצ' אינו מקבל טומאה מן התור' ולכך א"צ נטיל' כמ"ש בסעיף שלפני זה והתוס' חולקים וס"ל פחות מכביצה מקבל טומאה ובעי נטילה אלא דר' צדוק כהן הוי והותרה לו לאכול בלי נט"י ע"י כריכת מפה דלא נגע במאכל כדאמרי' בחולין דף ק"ז ע"ב משום דזריזי' הם אע"ג דלישראל לא הותר לאכול ע"י מפה וכדלקמן סי' קס"ג והרוקח ס"ל כרש"י ומוכח שם דפחות מכביצ' א"צ נטילה אבל פחות מכזית דא"צ נטיל' לזה אין ראיה מסוכה דיש לחלק בין פחות מכביצ' לפחות מכזית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 4) Less than an olive-bulk is an erroneous text, etc.</b> For this seif has its source in the Rokeach cited by the Beis Yosef. This is his language: They wash their hands on Pesach night after the order of the Haggadah, before eating, and recite the blessing of netilas yadayim, because one must dip a second dipping with maror, for he holds like the view of Rashi below, that one must recite the blessing of netilas yadayim for something dipped in liquid. But for eating matzah, with which he fulfills the obligation with an olive-bulk, he does not require washing, for less than an olive-bulk does not require washing, as we say in Sukkah and Berachos. End quote. This was the version before the Beis Yosef, and based on this he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch that for less than an olive-bulk one does not require washing. Although in the preceding seif he wrote that for less than an egg-bulk one should wash his hands and not recite a blessing, implying that less than an olive-bulk is more lenient than less than an egg-bulk, the Magen Avraham wrote that it is a scribal error in the Rokeach, and the text must read: for less than an egg-bulk one does not require washing. For so it is in Sukkah, folio 26b, in the Mishnah: When they gave Rabbi Tzadok food less than an egg-bulk, he took it in a cloth and ate it outside the sukkah, etc. Rashi explained: He took it in a cloth, meaning he did not wash his hands, but wrapped his hands in a cloth for cleanliness. End quote. Rashi is among those who hold that less than an egg-bulk does not receive impurity according to the Torah, and therefore it does not require washing, as written in the previous seif. Tosafot disagree and hold that less than an egg-bulk receives impurity and requires washing, except that Rabbi Tzadok was a kohen and it was permitted for him to eat without netilas yadayim by means of wrapping in a cloth, so that he did not touch the food, as we say in Chullin, folio 107b, because they are zealous, even though for a Yisrael it was not permitted to eat by means of a cloth, as below in siman 163. The Rokeach holds like Rashi, and it is proven there that less than an egg-bulk does not require washing. But that less than an olive-bulk does not require washing has no proof from Sukkah, for one can distinguish between less than an egg-bulk and less than an olive-bulk.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 6) Liquids, for even, etc.</b> That is to say, although one who washes his hands for fruit is considered among the arrogant, as written in seif 5, since according to the Torah one need not separate terumah from fruit, and since washing was instituted because of the trace of terumah, therefore with fruits, where terumah is only D'Rabbanan, they did not institute washing. Nevertheless, if the fruits, even if they are less than an olive-bulk, are dipped in liquid, they require washing. For specifically with bread they required washing only because of the trace of terumah, but because of chullin it would not have been necessary, since hands are second-degree impurity, and a second-degree impurity does not make a third-degree impurity with chullin. By contrast, with something dipped in liquid, washing is needed because of chullin alone. For we rule that whatever invalidates terumah, namely second-degree impurity, if it touches liquids, the liquids become first-degree impurity. Therefore, when he touches the liquids with his hands, which are second-degree impurity, the liquids become first-degree impurity, and the liquids will make the food second-degree impurity, for a first-degree impurity makes second-degree impurity even with chullin. Therefore washing is required.

So too in Shabbos, folio 19, it is stated there: Who is this Tanna who holds that anything that comes on its own is permitted? That is to say, although we forbid on Shabbos liquids that flowed out on Shabbos as a decree lest one squeeze, nevertheless, if one loaded beams and presses upon grapes and they were crushed before Shabbos, and through this the liquids come out on their own on Shabbos, they are permitted on Shabbos, for there is no concern lest he squeeze, since even if he squeezed them by hand there would be no liability for a sin-offering, for they were crushed before Shabbos and would in any case come out on their own. But regarding olives and grapes, which are now liquid, one can say there is a concern lest he squeeze. End quote of Rashi. Thus they called it food.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See there in the name of Tosafot in Pesachim, where Tosafot asked that in several places it implies that oil is called a liquid.</b> In Taharos we learned that Rabbi Shimon Shezuri disagrees with Rabbi Meir and the Sages, and holds that honey and oil are not called liquids, but wine is called a liquid. In Menachos, the Talmud rules that the halacha follows Rabbi Shimon Shezuri. Tosafot answered that what is said that honey and oil are not called liquids refers to when they congealed, even though they later melted again; but wine, even if it congealed, if it later melted it is a liquid. According to this, we have two grounds of permission for preserves: one, because it stands for eating, and second, if it was once congealed, even though now it has melted. But the Taz, s.k. 7, disagreed with the first permission. The Tosefta teaches that thought for eating helps only where it flowed out on its own; but if he removed it by hand, since he performed an act of removing it for the sake of liquid, the later thought by which he intends it for eating does not remove it from the first act.
