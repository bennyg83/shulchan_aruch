<b>And if he did not divert his attention, etc. </b>It appears to me that Rama was troubled by the fact that we rule in siman 159, seif 13, that intention is required only initially. Here, the first washing is no worse than a washing performed without intention. He therefore writes that the same rule applies here: so long as one did not divert his attention, he should wash again without a blessing, because the first washing is effective at least to prevent him from reciting another blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For drying as well, etc. </b>According to this reason, there is a difficulty, for there are many washings that do not require drying, as stated in the following seif. According to the first reason cited by the Tur, however, namely that his hands are not clean, the matter is understandable. Rama writes that if one forgot to recite the blessing until after drying, he may recite it afterward. This implies that he may not recite it after the blessing of Hamotzi. Maharshal writes in chapter Kol HaBasar: “If he forgot to recite the blessing until after drying, I wrote in the first chapter of Pesachim, siman 2, that he may recite it before Hamotzi, but not afterward.” I do not know his proof. In my humble opinion, however, it appears far more likely that once he has completed fulfillment of the mitzvah, namely the drying, he should no longer recite the blessing. All mitzvos require the blessing to be recited before their performance. In a mitzvah whose fulfillment extends over a period of time, if one forgot to recite the blessing before beginning, there remains time to recite it while he is still engaged in the mitzvah. Once the period of fulfillment has ended, however, he may no longer recite the blessing. For this reason, the Tur wrote that he should recite the blessing before drying, and did not write that he may do so even afterward. Proof may be brought from siman 167, seif 8, which states that if one did not remember until after completing his meal, he does not recite Hamotzi. One cannot answer that here is different because he has not yet eaten, for eating may be done with clean hands without touching the food, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 164 in the name of Kol Bo, that if one placed coverings over his hands he need not wash afterward. Moreover, if the blessing could still be recited because of the eating, its text should have been, “Who commanded us to eat with clean hands,” since the mitzvah of washing has already passed. Nor can proof be brought from Rama in siman 172, who rules that one expels the liquids from his mouth and recites the blessing. That case is different because he remembered while the liquids were still in his mouth, as the Rosh writes there and Beit Yosef cites. Indeed, that passage itself provides proof, for the Gemara says there, “Once it was deferred, it was deferred.” The same applies here: once the blessing was deferred from before drying, it is entirely deferred.

<b>בלי ניגוב. </b> במרדכי הביאו ב"י כתב המטביל ידיו לחוד ונתן בו הב"י טעם משום שהמים שעל ידו לא נטמאו ומ"ה כתב אם שופך רביעית בבת אחת שאין המים מטמאין שאין צריך ניגוב וכמו שסיים כאן וה"ה לנוטל ידיו כו' ורש"ל כ' ע"ז ולא דיבר נכונה כי הניגוב הוא תיקון חכמים ומשום מיאוס כמ"ש רש"י על לחמם טמא דדבר מיאוס קרוי טמא ולא תקנו אלא בנט"י כמו שתקנו מנא וכח גברא אבל בטבילת ידים שבא מצד טבילת גופו שהיא דאורייתא לא תקנו מידי עכ"ל. ודבריו נראים עיקר דהא אמרי' בגמרא בשם רבי אבהו כל האוכל בלא ניגוב ידים כאלו אוכל לחם טמא שנא' ככה יאכלו בני ישראל את לחמם טמא ופירש"י דיליף מקרא דמיאוס נקרא טמא דקרא קאי התם על דבר מיאוס ואם כן מאי מהני נטילה רביעית בבת אחת וא"ל א"כ המטביל ידיו אמאי אין צריך ניגוב דשאני מטביל דיש לו דין טבילת כל גופו וזה פשוט דהטובל כל גופו דנעשה טהור אפי' בעודו לח והפסוק לא מיירי אלא בדבר ששייך לאכילה דהא אמר יאכלו דהיינו נטילת ידים בזה הקפיד על מיאוס. ובלבוש כתוב שיש כאן ב' דיעות אם צריך ניגוב למטביל כיון דגם במטביל שייך מיאוס כיון שהם לחים וזה אינו דא"כ כל ידים לחות אפי' הם טהורים יהא אסור לאכול בהם אלא פשוט דבמקום שתקנו נט"י משום טומאה הקפידו ג"כ שבאותו פעם לא יהיה מיאוס ואסמכו' אקרא דככה יאכלו שמצינו גם כן שהקפידו על מיאוס ואין כאן אלא דיעה אחת דכ"ע מודים דמטביל דברייתא היא כמ"ש ב"י וכ"כ הוא עצמו אלא שכתב שר"א חולק על הברייתא וזה לא ניתן להיכתב שהיאך יחלוק ר"א שהוא אמורא על הברייתא כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Without drying. </b>Mordechai, cited by Beit Yosef, writes this only regarding one who immerses his hands. Beit Yosef explains that the water remaining upon his hands did not become impure. He therefore writes that one who pours a reviis over his hands all at once, so that the water does not become impure, does not require drying, as he concludes here: “The same applies to one who washes his hands,” etc. Maharshal writes concerning this: “He did not speak correctly, for drying is an enactment of the Sages because of repulsiveness, as Rashi explains regarding ‘their bread in impurity,’ that something repulsive is called impure. They enacted this only for washing of the hands, just as they enacted the requirement of a vessel and human force. Regarding immersion of the hands, however, which derives from immersion of the entire body and is D’Oraisa, they enacted nothing.” His words appear primary. The Gemara states in the name of Rabbi Abahu that whoever eats without drying his hands is considered as though he ate impure bread, as it says, “Thus shall the Children of Israel eat their bread impure.” Rashi explains that the verse teaches that something repulsive is called impure, for the verse there concerns a repulsive matter. If so, what is gained by pouring a reviis all at once? One cannot ask why one who immerses his hands does not require drying, for immersion is different, since it has the law of immersion of the entire body. It is obvious that one who immerses his entire body becomes pure even while still wet. The verse speaks only of something related to eating, as it says, “shall eat,” namely washing of the hands, and in that context the Sages were particular about repulsiveness. Levush writes that there are two opinions whether one who immerses his hands requires drying, since repulsiveness also applies when the immersed hands remain wet. This is incorrect. If so, all wet hands, even when pure, would be forbidden for eating. Rather, wherever the Sages instituted washing because of impurity, they were also particular that at that time there be no repulsiveness, and they supported this with the verse, “Thus shall they eat,” where we find that Scripture was likewise particular about repulsiveness. There is only one opinion here. Everyone agrees with the Baraisa that immersion suffices, as Beit Yosef writes, and Levush himself writes likewise. He merely states that Rabbi Abahu disputes the Baraisa, but such a statement should not be written. How could Rabbi Abahu, an Amora, dispute a Baraisa? This appears correct.
