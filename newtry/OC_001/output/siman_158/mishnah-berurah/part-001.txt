**** ENGLISH ****
(1) When one comes, etc. The enactment of washing has two reasons. First, it was instituted because of terumah. Since hands are active and touch everything, when the laws of tumah and taharah were observed and kohanim ate terumah, they were required by Rabbinic law to wash their hands before eating terumah so that they would not render it tamei through contact. To accustom the kohanim to this, the Sages also decreed that every Jew who eats bread may not eat until he washes his hands. Even now, when kohanim do not eat terumah because of tumah, this enactment was not annulled, so that the Jewish people will be accustomed to eating in a state of taharah when the Beis HaMikdash is rebuilt, speedily in our days. A second reason for the enactment is cleanliness and sanctity. The Gemara in Berachos 51 derives support from the verse, “You shall sanctify yourselves and be holy.” Semag.

**** ENGLISH ****
(9) Less than the volume of an egg, etc. The reason is that, regarding tumah, food measuring less than an egg does not contract tumah D’Oraisa and therefore will not become tamei through contact with hands. It may therefore be that the Sages did not decree washing for such an amount. Alternatively, since regarding Bircas HaMazon it is considered eating, for we rule that Bircas HaMazon is recited over an olive-sized amount, it may likewise be considered eating regarding washing, and the Sages did not distinguish within their enactment. He should therefore wash without reciting a blessing, since the blessing does not prevent fulfillment of the washing. Know that if the food measures an egg even without its shell, all agree that he must wash with a blessing. Dagul Merevavah and other Acharonim.

(10) Less than an olive-sized amount, etc. Such an amount is not considered eating even regarding Bircas HaMazon. Taz therefore rules that washing is unnecessary, and this is also the view of Bach. The Gra and Birkei Yosef agree, the latter citing Tashbetz. Lechem Chamudos, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and other Acharonim hold that even less than an olive-sized amount has the same law as less than an egg, and one should wash without a blessing. It is therefore proper initially to be stringent. Know that an olive-sized amount that requires washing according to all opinions includes even fine crumbs, which combine; if the crumbs together measure a full egg, he must also recite the blessing over washing. If one eats a small amount of bread to temper the sharpness of a drink, in the manner explained below in siman 212, so that HaMotzi is not required because the bread is secondary to the drink, some say that washing is likewise unnecessary even if the bread measures an egg, while others require it; see Eliyah Rabbah. If it is less than an olive-sized amount, it appears that one may certainly rely on the lenient view and not require washing even initially, since there are lenient opinions in any event, as stated above. For an olive-sized or egg-sized amount, however, he should wash without a blessing because of the doubt.

(מה) לחם טמא - כתב הב"י דהיינו משום דמים הראשונים ששופך על ידיו טמאים הם שנטמאו מחמת ידיו כדלקמן בסימן קס"ב ואף דשופך מים שניים לטהר המים כדאיתא שם מ"מ לכתחלה צריך להעביר את הראשונים לגמרי ע"י ניגוב ורש"ל כתב דעיקר הניגוב הוא משום נקיות דכשידיו לחים ממי הניגוב יש בו משום מיאוס וכ"מ ברש"י סוטה דף ד'. כתב המ"א בשם התשב"ץ לא ינגב ידיו בחלוקו שקשה לשכחה ועיין בפמ"ג שמסתפק אם דוקא חלוקו או כל בגדיו במשמע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(45) Tamei bread. Beit Yosef writes that this is because the first water poured on one’s hands becomes tamei through contact with the hands, as explained below in siman 162. Although he pours second water to purify the first water, as stated there, initially he must remove the first water completely by drying. Maharshal writes that the primary purpose of drying is cleanliness, because when one’s hands remain wet from the washing water it is repulsive. This also appears from Rashi in Sotah 4a. Magen Avraham cites Tashbetz that one should not dry his hands on his shirt, because this is detrimental to memory. See Peri Megadim, who is uncertain whether this applies specifically to one’s shirt or to all one’s garments.

(מו) בלא ניגוב וכו' - כן איתא בתוספתא וכתב הב"י דהטעם הוא משום דבמטביל אין כאן מים טמאים כלל ולכן אין צריך ניגוב ולמד מזה דה"ה בשופך רביעית בבת אחת על שתי ידיו או על כל יד ויד בפני עצמו דקי"ל נמי דהמים טהורים הם [כדלקמן בסי' קס"ב] אין צריך ניגוב. ורש"ל חולק על זה דס"ל דעיקר הניגוב שתקנו הוא משום מיאוס וכנ"ל וא"כ מה לי רביעית בבת אחת או לא ורק במטביל אין צריכין ניגוב דטבילת הידים הוא כעין טהרה דאורייתא של טבילת הגוף ושם בודאי אין צריך ניגוב ולכן גם בטבילת ידים לא תקנו בו ניגוב וכדבריו כתב גם הב"ח בסימן קס"ה וכן הסכימו שאר אחרונים. ואף במטביל דא"צ ניגוב מן הדין כתב המ"א דמי שדעתו קצה עליו יש בו משום מיאוס וצריך ניגוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
(46) Without drying, etc. — thus it is in the Tosefta; and Beit Yosef wrote that the reason is because in immersing there are no impure waters here at all, and therefore drying is not required; and from this we learn that the same applies when pouring a revi'it at once on both hands, or on each hand by itself — for we also hold the waters are pure [as below siman 162] — drying is not required. Rashal disagrees with this, for he holds that the essence of drying that they enacted is because of disgust, as above; and if so, what difference is there whether a revi'it at once or not — only in immersing they do not require drying, for immersion of the hands is like a Torah purity of immersing the body, and there certainly drying is not required; therefore also in immersion of the hands they did not enact drying — and in his words Bach also wrote in siman 165, and likewise other Acharonim agreed. And even in immersing, where drying is not required by law, Magen Avraham wrote that one whose intent is repulsive upon him — there is [reason] because of disgust and he requires drying.
