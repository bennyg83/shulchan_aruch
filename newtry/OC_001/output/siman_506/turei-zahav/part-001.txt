<b>אין מרקדין כו'. </b> דזה אפשר מאתמול וכל אוכל נפש עצמו שאפשר מאתמול אסור בי"ט מדרבנן כ"כ הר"ן הביאו ב"י סי' זה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One does not sift, etc.</b> For this is possible from yesterday; and any ochel nefesh itself that is possible from yesterday is forbidden on Yom Tov by rabbinic law. So wrote the Ran; Beit Yosef cited him on this siman.

<b>And a pebble fell into it, etc.</b> In the Gemara it is stated: Our Rabbis taught: one does not do a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov (that was sifted yesterday, and one comes to sift it again in a sieve to improve it: one does not do a second sifting, for it was possible for him yesterday). R' Papus and R' Yehuda ben Beteira said: one does a second sifting (for there is no [significant] effort, and it is an apparent matter that this is a second time, and it is not like sifting to select). And they agree that if a pebble or twig fell into it, one does a second sifting. A Tanna taught before Ravina: one does not do a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov; but if a pebble or twig fell, he selects it by hand (but doing a second sifting is forbidden even in this). He said to him: all the more so it is forbidden, for it is like borer, and borer is an av melacha like sifting; but one who does a second sifting is not meraked, for everything comes out and there is no bran here. The wife of Rav Yosef sifted flour over the back of a sieve; he said to her: see that I want fine bread. (You need not do a second sifting on Yom Tov, for one does a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov.) The wife of Rav Ashi sifted flour upon the back of a table (because of a change; meaning the underside of the table, and there is a cavity there). And Tosafos explained that even upon the table it is a change. Said Rav Ashi: this [wife] of ours is the daughter of Rami bar Chama, and he is master of the incident. And the Rosh, the Tur, and the Ran ruled like the one who says one does not do a second sifting for beauty unless by means of some change, like the wife of Rav Ashi; and one need not do as the wife of Rav Yosef. And Rav Yosef said to his wife that you need not a change as great as this, for the flour is not fine thereby; rather a small change suffices, namely upon the table. But if a pebble fell there, no change at all is needed; rather he does a second sifting as is. And they did not write at all that ruling of Ravina who forbids taking the pebble or twig by hand. And Beit Yosef wrote the reason: Ravina said so only according to what that Tanna said before him, to be more stringent regarding doing a second sifting than regarding taking by hand, for he holds one does not do a second sifting at all even by means of a change; but according to us, who hold one does a second sifting by means of a change, Ravina agrees that the hand is no better than doing a second sifting; and if so, according to the truth, also by hand it is permitted. Further Beit Yosef answered that certainly it is forbidden to take a pebble by hand, and the poskim did not write it regarding sifting flour because it is not a matter of sifting, and they relied on what is written in the law of borer; and this appears more [correct], end of his words. And in my humble opinion the first answer is more correct, for nothing of this is mentioned at all in the law of borer that there should be a prohibition to take a pebble or twig from a food item because of borer; and the Rashba explicitly permitted to take by hand, as Beit Yosef wrote; rather certainly according to the first explanation. <b>And the Raavad</b> in the Hasagot wrote in chapter 4, and this is his language: my master says the law follows the words of R' Papus and R' Yehuda who say one does a second sifting; and if a pebble or twig fell, it is forbidden; and if he changed, it is permitted, end of his words. At first glance it implies that what he wrote 'forbidden' means forbidden to do a second sifting, namely a second sifting, excluding the beginning; and upon this he said: and if he changed, it is permitted, meaning by means of a change a second sifting is permitted even if a pebble fell. And so the words of Kol Bo that Beit Yosef cites imply, and this is his language: the Raavad wrote: a second time one sifts it on Yom Tov if a pebble did not fall into it; but if a pebble fell into it, one does not do a second sifting to sift it, because it appears like borer; and if he changed, it is permitted, end of his words. And it is surprising upon him, for we explicitly say that they agree regarding a fallen pebble that doing a second sifting is permitted, which implies that a fallen pebble is better than doing a second sifting; and since he ruled like the one who says one does a second sifting even without a pebble, all the more so with a pebble. And further it is difficult: why should a change be forbidden because of borer, when borer is permitted on Yom Tov like cooking, as explained in siman 510 in the words of Maggid Mishneh; and Maggid Mishneh explained Rashi regarding what Ravina said: all the more so by hand it is forbidden because of borer, but doing a second sifting is not an av melacha; that is, it is preferable to do what is not an av melacha; but here where it is entirely forbidden, it is certainly difficult; and see what I wrote there. Therefore one must explain the Raavad's words as Maggid Mishneh wrote, and this is his language: the explanation of his words is that it is forbidden to remove the pebble by hand, but he may do a second sifting of the flour; but the Rashba explained that it is permitted to remove it by hand; and this appears to be the view of our Rabbi the Rambam, who did not write a prohibition of taking by hand, end of his words. If so, what the Raavad wrote 'and if he changed, it is permitted' means a second sifting in its usual way without a change; for he explains what Ravina said 'all the more so by hand it is forbidden' thus: if you come to be stringent regarding doing a second sifting and you rule like the first Tanna that one does not do a second sifting, all the more so you should forbid by hand, which is more similar to borer; and this is forbidden even according to the one who says one does a second sifting; and why do you permit by hand? And according to me he holds like the one who says one does a second sifting, and even so by hand it is forbidden since it is possible without an av melacha, as it appears to me according to the Raavad. And he holds that the fact that Rav Ashi's wife made a change upon the table was an excessive stringency, for no change at all is needed; rather one does a second sifting in the usual way; and therefore R' Yehuda said to his wife: see that I want fine bread, therefore do not make any change at all; and so the Ran wrote on this. And if so, by hand it is forbidden according to the conclusion to take a pebble or twig from the flour, for it is borer according to the Raavad, for it is like taking waste from within the food, which is forbidden in siman 319; which is not so when he does a second sifting of the flour, namely he sifts it again, then he takes the food from within the waste. And if so, when flies fall into a cup on Shabbos, it is forbidden according to the Raavad to remove the flies alone from there; rather he must take some of the drink with them as well; and this is the view of those who are stringent that the Rama mentioned in se'if 2. And my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote another explanation in the words of the Rosh and the Raavad; and what appears to me clear and simple I have written.

<b>אפי' תחלת הרקדה. </b> דרשב"ם פסק דשינוי מהני אפי' בהרקדה ראשונה אלא דרש"י פסק דלא מהני דהיא אב מלאכה ואפשר מאתמול ואסור מדרבנן ע"כ כתבו התוס' דע"י עכו"ם יש להקל בשינוי קצת והיינו כמו בהרקדה שנייה ע"י ישראלית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even a first sifting.</b> For Rashbam ruled that a change helps even with a first sifting; but Rashi ruled that it does not help, for it is an av melacha, and it is possible from yesterday, and it is forbidden by rabbinic law. Therefore Tosafos wrote that by means of a non-Jew one should be lenient with some change; and that is like a second sifting by means of a Jewish woman.

זה וע"ז נתן מהרי"א חילוק בין מתנות לחלה דמותר במתנות להפריש היום מה שנשחט מאמש משא"כ בחלה והטעם היא משום הנך תרתי שזכר אותם משא"כ בחלה ונראה פשוט דהסופר שהעתיק דברי מהרי"א טעה במקום בדברי הטור שמהרי"א כתב דבריו על דין העיסה של עי"ט דבתר הכי והסופר העתיק את דבריו לכאן לענין הולכה לכהן וזה גרם להטעות להרב"י מנוחתו כבוד ומ"מ תימה עליו שלא הרגיש במה שלא הוזכר בדברי מהרי"א כלל מן ההולכה לכהן ולא דק כלל בזה ובסוף הסי' כ' ב"י פעם שנית דברי מהרי"א אלו בשם הרשב"א כי שם מקומם באמת  וכתבו התוס' בפ"ק דביצה שם על מתני' בש"א דאין מוליכין לכהן חלה ומתנות כו' ה"ה בביתו שאסור ליתן לו אם לא שרגיל אצלו וראיה מירושלמי ורש"ל הביא מזה באריכות ומסיק דאף לב"ה דהם מתיריה ההולכה לכהן אם הוא בענין שאסור להוליך מצד שמפסיק ר"ה או כרמלית מ"מ אסור ליתן לו בביתו אם לא שרגיל אצלו ולא ידעתי מקור לדבריו דודאי אליבא דידן דלא קי"ל כמתני' אלא כר' יוסי ולב"ה מוליכין אפי' תרומה לא תמצא איסור נתינה לו בבית דלא גרע זה מהולכה ותו דאין איסור בהולכה דרך ר"ה כיון שהוא אוכל נפש לכהן ואין באוכל נפש משום הוצאה בי"ט ואע"ג דלישראל אין כאן אוכל נפש מ"מ הוא מוליכה לאכילה לכהן דאל"כ תהיה אסורה אפי' לטלטל בביתו כיון דלא חזיא ליה ע"כ לא הזכירו הפוסקים מזה כלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who kneads dough, etc.</b> In the first chapter of Beitzah, folio 12, we hold like R' Yosi who said: Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel did not disagree about the gifts of the foreleg, jaw, and maw, that one brings them to the kohen on Yom Tov; they disagreed only about terumah, for Beit Shammai say one does not bring, meaning specifically gifts one brings, for he is entitled to raise them, meaning he is permitted to raise them on Yom Tov; but terumah it is forbidden to raise on Yom Tov, as stated in chapter Mashilin: one does not raise terumos and maasros on Yom Tov; therefore what he raised of terumah on erev Yom Tov one does not bring. And Beit Hillel say: just as one brings the gifts, so one brings terumah. And it is simple that challah's law is like the gifts, and all separate it on Yom Tov if kneaded on Yom Tov; automatically its law is like the gifts and one brings it to the kohen even according to Beit Shammai. And Beit Yosef's words here are not precise, for he wrote: and it is known that the halachah follows Beit Hillel, and it implies that even terumah raised last night Beit Hillel permit to bring, for in the mishnah, etc.; why should he hang 'the halachah follows Beit Hillel' regarding challah, which is like the gifts and Beit Shammai also agree to it, and even if separated yesterday, since he can also separate on Yom Tov like the gifts according to R' Yosi whom we hold like. Further, in what he wrote 'even terumah raised last night,' what does 'even' mean, for it is impossible to separate terumah on Yom Tov but only from erev Yom Tov; rather in the rush of his words he was not precise, for he was comparing terumah to challah in this. The Tur wrote: one who kneads dough on Yom Tov may separate challah from it and bring it to the kohen; and likewise priestly gifts one may bring to the kohen even if raised last night; and Beit Yosef wrote that this 'even if raised last night' applies also to challah; but I found that the Gaon Mahari Weil wrote that there is a difference between gifts and dough: with gifts, even if slaughtered last night, he may separate them, which is not so with dough; and the reason is because with gifts, since they are not tevel (meaning there is no tevel prohibition if one eats the meat before separating the gifts), and moreover they are as if explained and standing, and they are recognizable before separation as after separation, for when he separates now he does nothing; which is not so with dough, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: therefore our Rabbi's language is explained simply: do not take 'even if raised last night' except regarding gifts; but regarding challah, specifically if raised today one is permitted to bring it, but if raised last night not; however it is difficult, for in the Gemara it does not imply so, as I wrote; therefore one must say that 'even if raised' that our Rabbi wrote applies also to challah, and not like that rabbi's words, end of Beit Yosef. And I say: although our Rabbi Yosef is the ruler in all the world in his explanations and rulings, nevertheless here an oversight came forth from before the ruler, for he attributed empty words, God forbid, to the Gaon Mahari Weil, to say it is forbidden to bring to the kohen what was raised of challah on erev Yom Tov; such should not be in Israel, for even Beit Shammai agree to it, for Beit Shammai's reason regarding gifts is since he is entitled to raise it today, and challah is like gifts regarding this that he is permitted to raise it on Yom Tov; and it is obvious its law is truly like gifts that Beit Shammai permit even if separated from erev Yom Tov, since today he has a right to separate it; likewise with challah there is no distinction except regarding terumah which cannot be separated today, as stated. And the Gaon Mahari Weil did not write his words except on this law of bringing to the kohen? For he did not mention bringing at all, only separation he mentioned, and he refers to the law afterward, namely if dough was kneaded from erev Yom Tov whether he may separate challah on Yom Tov; Tur wrote that the Rif and Rashi forbade this, and on this Mahari Weil gave the difference between gifts and challah, that with gifts it is permitted to separate today what was slaughtered last night, which is not so with challah; and the reason is those two reasons he mentioned, which is not so with challah. And it appears simple that the copyist who copied Mahari Weil's words erred in place in the Tur's words: Mahari Weil wrote his words on the law of dough of erev Yom Tov that comes after this, and the copyist copied his words here regarding bringing to the kohen, and this caused Beit Yosef's error, may his resting place be in honor. And nevertheless it is surprising upon him that he did not notice that Mahari Weil's words never mention bringing to the kohen at all, and he was not precise in this. And at the end of the siman Beit Yosef wrote again these words of Mahari Weil in the name of the Rashba, for there is truly their place. And Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Beitzah there on the mishnah: Beit Shammai say one does not bring to the kohen challah and gifts, etc.; likewise in his house it is forbidden to give him unless he is regularly accustomed with him; and proof from the Yerushalmi. And Maharshal brought from this at length and concludes that even for Beit Hillel who permit bringing to the kohen, if it is in a manner forbidden to bring because a public domain or karmelit interrupts, nevertheless it is forbidden to give him in his house unless he is regularly accustomed with him. And I did not know the source for his words, for certainly according to us we do not hold like the mishnah but like R' Yosi, and for Beit Hillel one brings even terumah; you will not find a prohibition of giving him in the house, for this is no worse than bringing. And further there is no prohibition in bringing by way of a public domain, since it is ochel nefesh for the kohen, and there is no issue of carrying out for ochel nefesh on Yom Tov; and although for a Yisrael there is no ochel nefesh here, nevertheless he brings it for eating by the kohen, for otherwise it would be forbidden even to move in his house since it is not fit for him; therefore the poskim did not mention this at all.

<b>ומותר לאפות הפת כו'. </b> המרדכי כ' שאין לאפותה בי"ט שאין יכול לאוכלה מיהו אם גלגלה ואפאה מעי"ט ולא הפריש חלה מצי לאכלה ובלבד שיניח כדי חלה כו' עכ"ל וכ' ב"י ע"ז ויש לתמוה אמאי אינו יכול לאוכלה הרי בח"ל אוכל והולך ואח"כ מפריש וכ"נ דעת הפוסקים שיכול לאפותה בי"ט עכ"ל ומ"ה פסק רמ"א שמותר לאפות כו' ול"נ לתרץ דהא לא מצי לאכול קאמר דהיינו שצריך לשייר חלק הכהן בשלמא בשאר עיסה אופה חלק הכהן בשביל הכהן משא"כ בזה שיום לא יאכל הכהן חלקו שהרי אסור להפריש א"כ אופה חלק א' שלא לצורך י"ט וקשה שהרי א"א לחלק החלה מן העיסה בי"ט נמצא דא"א בחלוקה א"כ דמיא לבהמה חצים של עכו"ם דמותר בשחיטה בי"ט לפי שא"א לכזית בשר בלא שחיטה כדאי' סימן תצ"א סי"א ה"נ בזה והא דעיסה חציה של עכו"ם דאסור לאפותה בי"ט כדאיתא בס"ה היינו משום דאפשר למפלגינהו בלישה וכאן לא שייך זה וי"ל כיון דבשעת הלישה היה חול ואפשר להפרישה והוא לא הפריש נמצא שהעיכוב מצדו שלא עשה כראוי לא מצד העיסה שפיר קרינן ביה אפשר למיפלגינהו בלישה ואסו' כנ"ל נכון טעמו ונראה דמודה הוא באם עבר ואפה דמותר לאכול ולשייר ואפשר שהפוסקים שכתבו שאסור להפריש חלה בזה ולא זכרו האפייה מיירי בענין זה שעבר ואפה ואע"פ שזה קצת דוחק כיון שדעת המרדכי בפי' כן ולא נמצא בפי' לסתור דבריו ראוי לחוש לדבריו כיון שיש לו טעם נכון וכ"כ מו"ח ז"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is permitted to bake the bread, etc.</b> The Mordechai wrote that one should not bake it on Yom Tov, for he cannot eat it; however, if he rolled it and baked it on erev Yom Tov and did not separate challah, he may eat it, provided he leaves enough for challah, etc., end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and one should wonder why he cannot eat it, for with challah of chutz la'aretz he eats and continues and afterward separates; and so appears the view of the poskim that he may bake it on Yom Tov, end of his words. And therefore the Rama ruled that it is permitted to bake, etc. And it appears to me to answer: what he said 'he cannot eat' means that he must leave the kohen's portion; fair enough with other dough one bakes the kohen's portion for the kohen, which is not so here, for today the kohen will not eat his portion, since it is forbidden to separate; if so he bakes one portion not for the need of Yom Tov. And it is difficult, for it is impossible to divide the challah from the dough on Yom Tov; it turns out that division is impossible; if so it is similar to an animal half of which belongs to a non-Jew, which is permitted in slaughter on Yom Tov because it is impossible [to get] a kezayis of meat without slaughter, as stated in siman 491, se'if 11; so too here. And that dough half of which belongs to a non-Jew, which is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov as stated in se'if 5, that is because it is possible to divide them in kneading; and here that does not apply. And one can say: since at the time of kneading it was a weekday and it was possible to separate it, and he did not separate, it turns out that the hindrance is from his side that he did not do properly, not from the side of the dough; we properly call it 'it was possible to divide them in kneading,' and it is forbidden, as it appears to me. His reason is correct; and it appears that he agrees that if he transgressed and baked, it is permitted to eat and leave over. And it is possible that the poskim who wrote that it is forbidden to separate challah in this and did not mention baking are discussing a case where he transgressed and baked; and although this is somewhat forced, since the Mordechai's view is explicitly so and we do not find an explicit [source] to contradict his words, it is proper to be concerned for his words since he has a correct reason; and so my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote.
