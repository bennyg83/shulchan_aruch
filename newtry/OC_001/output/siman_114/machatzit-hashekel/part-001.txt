<b>(ס"ק ב) שיכריז כו'.</b> דע דבירושלמי איתא ר' חגי בשם ר' פדת אמר אסור ליחיד להזכיר עד שיזכיר שליח ציבור. וכ' הרא"ש וז"ל נ"ל דהא קאי אמאי דפריך בירושלמי (הובא במ"א ס"ק א') דה"ל להתחיל בשחרית. וע"ז אמר שאין ליחיד להזכיר עד שיזכיר שליח ציבור פי' שיאמר קודם תפלת מוסף בקול רם משיב הרוח ומוריד הגשם והאי א"א בבקר לפי שאינו יכול להפסיק בין גל"ת עכ"ל. ולדבריו צ"ל מ"ש בירושלמי עד שיזכיר ש"ץ ר"ל בקול רם והיינו שיכריז קודם התפלה. אבל ראבי"ה כ' וז"ל אסור ליחיד כו' עד שיזכיר ש"ץ בין בלחש בין בקול רם עכ"ל ופי' הב"ח דס"ל מ"ש בירושלמי אסור כו' עד שיזכיר ש"ץ כפשוטו דאסור ליחיד להזכיר כשעדיין לא עמד הש"ץ להתפלל מוסף דאכתי לא הזכירו ש"ץ אפי בלחש אבל כשעמד החזן והתפלל בלחש ויודעים שהש"ץ יזכיר בלחש אף גם כל העם מזכירים בתפלת מוסף. והמחבר שכ' אסור כו' עד שיכריז ש"ץ אם נא' דס"ל כהרא"ש דצריך להכריז קודם התפלה ולכן כ' המחבר עד שיכריז א"כ תיקשי במאי פליג רמ"א שכ' וי"א שקודם כו' הא גם המחבר הכי ס"ל (ומה"ט באמת הט"ז הגיה דברי רמ"א ע"ש) אע"כ דרמ"א הבין מדברי המחבר מדלא כ' עד שיכריז קודם תפלה וכל' הרא"ש ע"כ כראבי"ה ס"ל דא"צ להכריז קודם התפלה אלא דהרב"י מסבר' דנפשי' כתב ע"צ היותר טוב שיאמר בקול רם תוך התפלה משיב כו' אף דלראבי"ה אפי' מזכיר בלחש סגי מ"מ עדיף שיכריז תוך התפלה בקול רם משיב כו' כדי שלא ישכח שום א'. אבל באמת מצד הדין סגי אפי' הזכיר ש"ץ בלחש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2, That the chazzan should announce, etc.</b> Know that the Yerushalmi states in the name of Rabbi Chaggai quoting Rabbi Pedas: an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it. The Rosh writes that this refers to the Yerushalmi’s question, cited by Magen Avraham in seif katan 1, why they do not begin at Shacharis. The answer is that an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it, meaning that before Mussaf the chazzan says aloud, “Who causes the wind to blow and the rain to descend.” This cannot be done in the morning because one may not interrupt between redemption and prayer. According to the Rosh, the Yerushalmi’s phrase “until the chazzan mentions it” means aloud, through an announcement before the prayer. Raavyah, however, writes that an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it, whether silently or aloud. Bach explains that Raavyah understands the Yerushalmi literally: an individual may not mention it before the chazzan has begun Mussaf, since the chazzan has not yet mentioned it even silently. Once the chazzan begins the silent Amidah and the congregation knows he will mention it silently, the entire congregation also mentions it in Mussaf. If the Mechaber, who writes that one may not mention it “until the chazzan announces it,” agrees with the Rosh that an announcement before prayer is required, then in what respect does Rama disagree when he writes, “Some say that before,” etc.? The Mechaber would hold the same view. For this reason Taz emends Rama’s words. It must therefore be that Rama understood from the Mechaber’s failure to write “until he announces it before prayer,” in the Rosh’s formulation, that the Mechaber follows Raavyah. No announcement before prayer is required. Beit Yosef added on his own that it is preferable for the chazzan to say aloud during the prayer, “Who causes the wind,” etc., so no one forgets, although according to Raavyah even a silent mention suffices. Strictly speaking, it is enough for the chazzan to mention it silently.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The Mechaber’s use of the word “forbidden,” etc.</b> refers only to the basic rule that an individual may not mention it before the chazzan does. It does not refer to the statement that the chazzan should announce it aloud, for the chazzan’s saying “Who causes the wind,” etc., aloud is not a strict requirement, as explained. This is also implied by the end of the Mechaber’s words: “Therefore,” etc., “because it is forbidden,” etc., “until the chazzan says,” etc. This implies that mere recitation suffices, even silently. Bach likewise infers from the Mechaber’s phrase “until he says” that he follows Raavyah, and asks how Beit Yosef’s statements can be reconciled, since at first he writes “until he announces,” which sounds like the Rosh. According to Magen Avraham’s explanation, however, there is no difficulty. The end of the Mechaber’s statement, “until he says,” refers to the strict law. Rama’s statement, “Some say that before,” etc., which is the Rosh’s view, is therefore understandable. Know that according to Raavyah and Beit Yosef, who follows him and holds that no announcement is made before prayer, one must explain why they do not begin at Shacharis, since according to them there would be no interruption between redemption and prayer. They must follow the Yerushalmi’s other answer: if they mentioned it at Shacharis, one who was absent from the synagogue at night would assume they had already begun at Maariv, like Yaaleh VeYavo and similar insertions, and the following year he would mention it at Maariv, again creating separate groups. When he sees that it is not mentioned at Shacharis, he knows it was not mentioned at Maariv either. Rabbi Chaggai did not come to resolve the question, but merely stated a law. Bach writes that this is according to the Rosh, and Rama follows his view, unlike Raavyah and Beit Yosef, who follow the other view.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 3, And likewise regarding dew.</b> Meaning, the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the wind to blow and the dew to descend” during the summer. According to our custom, the end of the Mechaber’s statement, that if one omitted dew during the summer he is not required to repeat, is inapplicable, since our initial custom is not to mention dew during summer. It must therefore refer to the Sephardic custom of mentioning dew in summer. The reason is as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 4. However, Magen Avraham’s statement that the Sephardic custom is to say during summer “Who causes the wind,” etc., requires explanation. If so, why does the Mechaber begin by saying that if one said “Who causes the wind” during summer he is not required to repeat? According to the Sephardic custom, this is the preferred practice. I would have thought the two words “Who causes the wind” are a scribal error, and it should read only that the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the dew to descend” during summer, as Beit Yosef and Levush write. Bach, however, also writes that the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the wind to blow and the dew to descend” during summer.
