<b>Even.</b> See Taz. It appears that Yom Tov differs from Shabbos, for reciting Barechu does not constitute acceptance of Yom Tov, whereas on Shabbos reciting Barechu does constitute acceptance; see simanim 261 and 263. Alternatively, the text should read “the fifth and sixth days,” and a stipulation is required. This implies that accepting Yom Tov through Barechu is like accepting Shabbos. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3, and siman 263, seif katan 35. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in Magen Avraham.

At first glance, according to the Mechaber's ruling in accordance with the Rosh, even after the fact one does not fulfill his obligation through the communal eruv if the one who made it intended to include deliberate offenders. Why, then, is it a mitzvah for the leading scholar of the city to make an eruv for everyone, since in Ezra's time people did not fail to make their own? According to Rashi, however, one may say that in Ezra's time the generation was worthy and people were not negligent, whereas now it is a mitzvah because some may be negligent. This requires study.

<b>שאלה</b> הניח ע״ת לחם עם ביצה מבושלת דמשמע דיי״ח ללפת במשנה כדג וביצה שעליו כו׳ ובי״ט נולד ביצה ובשלה ונתערבה בהרבה דשיל״מ י״ל כאן לא הוי דשיל״מ דא״כ יהא אסור לבשל בשבת עדיף מנתקלקל א״ד הואיל ובתבשיל זה לית הפסד אם ימתין אסור ולא הוי עירוב דהוי כנאבד הואיל ואסור לאכול בי״ט ומצה שבורה נתערב בע״פ חמץ במצה בי״ט נאסר יבש ביבש למ״ד חוזר וניער אסור כה״ג ואין סומכין משום עירוב וצ״ע ואם אין לי״ט כ״א דבר שנתערב כה״ג צ״ע אם הוי דשיל״מ עסי׳ תקכ״ט עונג י״ט שוה לשבת ולהתענות ג״כ אסור בי״ט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Question.</b> One set aside bread and a cooked egg for eruv tavshilin. It appears that this fulfills the requirement of a food eaten with bread, as in the Mishnah's case of a fish with an egg upon it. On Yom Tov, another egg was laid, cooked, and became mixed with many permitted eggs. Since an item that will later become permitted is not nullified, perhaps here it is nevertheless not treated as such, for otherwise he would be forbidden to cook for Shabbos, which would be more severe than a case in which the eruv was spoiled. Alternatively, since he loses nothing by waiting to eat this cooked food, it remains forbidden and cannot serve as an eruv; it is as though the eruv were lost, because it may not be eaten on Yom Tov. Similarly, if on Erev Pesach a broken matzah became mixed with chametz matzah, then on Yom Tov the dry mixture would be forbidden according to the opinion that the prohibition reawakens on Pesach. In such a case one may not rely upon it as an eruv. This requires study. If one has no Yom Tov food other than an item mixed in this manner, it also requires study whether it retains the status of an item that will later become permitted. See siman 529. The obligation of rejoicing on Yom Tov is equivalent to that of Shabbos, and fasting is likewise forbidden on Yom Tov.
