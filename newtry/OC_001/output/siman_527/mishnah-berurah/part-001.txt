{א} יו"ט שחל להיות בע"ש - ואין נ"מ בין יו"ט ראשון בין יו"ט ב' של גליות דאם חל יו"ט ב' בע"ש ג"כ אסור לבשל לשבת אם לא ע"י עירוב שיערב מעיו"ט הראשון:

{1} <b>Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos.</b> And there is no practical difference between the first day of Yom Tov and the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora: if the second day of Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbos, it is likewise forbidden to cook for Shabbos except by means of an eruv that he sets from the first erev Yom Tov.

{ג} וע"י עירוב מבשל בתחלה - כתבו הפוסקים דהיתר העירוב הוא אף למ"ד דמלאכות שבת אין נעשין ביו"ט מדאורייתא מ"מ מהני העירוב דמ"מ אין כאן אלא איסור מד"ס דמדאורייתא אמרינן הואיל ואלו מקלעי אורחים וחזי ליה ליו"ט גופא א"כ אין עושה איסור בזה ורק מדרבנן אסרו ובשביל שבת שהוא שעת הדחק התירו ע"י עירוב שנחשב בזה כאלו כבר התחיל להכין מעיו"ט לשבת ורק שגומר ביו"ט וכדלקמיה בהג"ה. וכתבו האחרונים דביו"ט שחל להיות בע"ש יזהר להקדים הכנת מאכליו לשבת בכדי שיגמר מלאכתו בעוד יום גדול דסמוך לחשיכה בזמן דלא שייך שיצטרך לו ביו"ט גופא הלא יש כאן לתא דמלאכה דאורייתא [וכן יש ליזהר בהמאכלים שמטמין לשבת שיטמינן בזמן שאפשר שיתבשלו שליש בישול מבעו"י] וכתבו דמטעם זה נהגו להקדים תפלת ערבית בליל שבת כשחל סמוך ליו"ט כדי שלא יתאחר מלאכת בישולו ביום טוב עד סמוך לחשיכה. ועיין בה"ל דבשעת הדחק יש להקל ביו"ט שני שחל בע"ש אם נתאחר בישולו לשבת עד סמוך לשבת ואף ביו"ט ראשון אפשר דיש להקל בשעת הדחק ולכתחלה בודאי צריך ליזהר בזה ובפרט ביו"ט ראשון שהוא דאורייתא:
**** ENGLISH ****
{3} <b>And by means of an eruv he cooks initially.</b> The poskim wrote that the permit of the eruv applies even according to the view that the melachos of Shabbos are not done on Yom Tov according to the Torah; nevertheless the eruv is effective, for nevertheless there is here only a rabbinic prohibition, for according to the Torah we say ho'il: since guests may happen to come and it is fit for him for Yom Tov itself, if so he does no prohibition in this, and only rabbinically they forbade, and for the sake of Shabbos which is a time of pressure they permitted by means of an eruv, which is considered in this as if he already began preparing from erev Yom Tov for Shabbos and only finishes on Yom Tov, as below in the gloss. And the later authorities wrote that on Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos one should be careful to prepare his foods for Shabbos early so that he finishes his work while it is still broad daylight, for close to nightfall, at a time when it is not relevant that he would need it for Yom Tov itself, there is a Torah-level melacha concern [and likewise one should be careful with foods that he buries to keep warm for Shabbos, that he bury them at a time when it is possible that they will cook one-third cooking while it is still day]. And they wrote that for this reason the custom is to pray Arvis early on Friday night when it falls beside Yom Tov, so that his cooking work on Yom Tov not be delayed until close to nightfall. And see Biur Halacha, that at a time of pressure one may be lenient on the second day of Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos if his cooking for Shabbos was delayed until close to Shabbos; and even on the first day of Yom Tov it is possible to be lenient at a time of pressure; and l'chatchilah one must certainly be careful about this, and especially on the first day of Yom Tov which is according to the Torah.

**** ENGLISH ****
{4} <b>Even doubtful nightfall.</b> The time of doubtful nightfall is explained in siman 261, and it is explained there that when three medium stars appear it is definite night. And the later authorities wrote that if he accepted Yom Tov upon himself by saying Barchu, he can no longer make an eruv, for by accepting Yom Tov the time is considered definite Yom Tov; and specifically by accepting with Barchu, which is a communal acceptance, and even if he himself did not say Barchu he is dragged after the congregation, as explained in siman 263, se'if 14; but his own personal acceptance, even if he accepted explicitly, possibly does not avail for this matter, and he may still make an eruv if it is not yet night. And at a time of pressure, even after Barchu, so long as the congregation has not prayed Arvis of Yom Tov, he may make an eruv if it is not yet night. If he came to pray Minchah and remembered that he had not made eruv tavshilin, and if he returns home to make an eruv he will miss the time for Minchah: he should pray Minchah, and regarding the eruv he should transfer ownership of his flour to others; however if he can make his eruv through an agent, he should send an agent to make the eruv before he prays Minchah.
