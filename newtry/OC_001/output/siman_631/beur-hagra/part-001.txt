**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is one, etc.</b> As the Ramban, Rashba, and Ran wrote, that the Mishnah is established where they do not have four, unlike Rashi and Tosafos, who wrote that an ordinary ceiling has four, and therefore it challenges Shmuel. For at the end of Bava Metzia 117 we find an ordinary ceiling that does not have four. Furthermore, if they have four, what does removing one help? And if you say like Rashi explained, the case is similar to loosening, where he comes to validate the whole sukkah; even according to the one who says invalid s'chach invalidates with four amos, he agrees that one does not sleep beneath it, and all the more so according to the one who says that in the middle it invalidates with four handbreadths. Why then does the Gemara challenge from the Baraisa below, 18a, and not challenge from the Mishnah? Furthermore, the reasoning would be reversed: that a ceiling made previously is valid by loosening even if it has four, and who proves this to the onlookers? Rather, it must be established when it does not have four. The one who holds that there is a decree of ceiling with four decreed here even with less than four, for certainly even less than four are used for ceilings; it is only things that are regularly used for s'chach as for ceilings that they did not decree upon. But when they have four, which is closer to a ceiling than to s'chach, they decreed about them. When it was already made as a ceiling, according to the one who decrees here, there is no distinction between four and less than four, since they are placed as the roofing of a house and become close to a ceiling. This is why it asks: granted according to Rav, for according to Rabbi Meir there is no decree at all; here too they did not decree. But according to Shmuel, where according to everyone there is a decree of ceiling with four, the same applies here, even with less than four, and according to everyone it should be invalid. It answers: the end is about nullifying the ceiling, etc.; according to Beis Hillel, even the old ceiling is repaired in this way, while according to Beis Shammai it is repaired only by removing one, as written at the beginning; and the same according to Rabbi Meir, according to all views. Their entire repair is to make them as at first, before they had become a ceiling. The sugya is explained this way, and this is the view of the Rambam. There is another path even according to Rashi and Tosafos, and according to the view of the Ra'avad, who ruled like Rav, for the halachah follows him in prohibitions. He wrote that the fact that Rav Papa explains Shmuel's matter is not decisive, for it is the way of the amoraim to explain. And the fact that the sugya assumes that four is invalid is because we rule like Rabbi Meir in his decrees, and here it is because of the decree of ceiling. All of it leads to one approach, as the Rashba wrote: according to Rav, where Rabbi Yehudah does not decree at all, then the halachah follows Rav in his decrees; but according to Shmuel, where Rabbi Yehudah too decrees and they disagree only about the measure, why should the halachah follow Rabbi Meir? We return to the rule, and there is no practical difference there. But here there is a practical difference: according to Rav, since the halachah follows Rabbi Meir in the first case, the same is true in the latter case. According to the Ra'avad, even according to Rashi's explanation here, it must not have four, for even Rabbi Meir, who validates by removing one, does so specifically with exactly one amah. So ruled Rabbi Yonah and the Rashba, of blessed memory, like Rav and like Rabbi Meir, except that according to their explanation nothing helps except removing one, and specifically when they do not have four, as it appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it must have, etc.</b> Like the measure of a sukkah that will be explained in siman 633:8 and 634:1. He teaches us not like the commentators who say that it needs only seven by seven on its floor and that any time it is ten handbreadths high, even if the tenth handbreadth narrows to a width of one handbreadth, that is enough. He teaches that it is not so. See Tosafos there, s.v. <i>ha'oseh</i>, unlike the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh, who wrote that the entire slope needs to be only sixteen besides the handbreadth. Rather, everything depends on the height of the wall. For example, if the wall is eighteen handbreadths high, the slope must be twenty-two and a half, and the distance from the wall fourteen and a half; this is if he distanced it one handbreadth from the wall. If he raised it one handbreadth from the ground and the wall is seventeen high, then the slope is twenty-two and a half and more, and the distance is sixteen. So everything depends on the wall and the distance.
