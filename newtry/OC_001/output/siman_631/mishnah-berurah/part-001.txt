(25) <b>Even if the boards are four wide.</b> Although if there had not been a ceiling here initially, and now he came to cover with boards of four handbreadths for the sake of sukkah, it would be rabbinically invalid, as above in siman 629:18, because of the decree of ceiling, lest he sit under the beam of his house and say: what difference is there between this and a new one? **** ENGLISH ****

<b>(29) If it had a roof even one tefach wide.</b> Whether he made it like a hut but did not slope it all the way to a sharp point, leaving an opening one tefach wide, or he leaned the top of the wall against another wall but left a space of one tefach between them, in all these cases we treat the open space as closed. Any gap of less than three tefachim is treated as lavud, and it is therefore considered to have a roof. See Magen Avraham, who writes that even if there is not a full tefach at the very top, it is permitted provided that within less than three tefachim of the top there is a width of one tefach.

Any gap of less than three tefachim is treated as lavud and is considered closed. That tefach is regarded as the wall of the sukkah, and its slope is regarded as the roof. Nevertheless, the sukkah must be ten tefachim high, as explained below.

(לג) בגובה י"ט - לאפוקי אם הוא ז' על ז' למטה מי"ט וכשמגיע השיפוע לגובה י"ט אינה רחבה ז' על ז' פסול ומודדין העשרה טפחים של הגובה ביושר ולא באלכסון:
**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(33) Ten tefachim high.</b> This excludes a case where the sukkah is seven by seven tefachim below the height of ten tefachim, but when the slope reaches a height of ten tefachim it is no longer seven by seven tefachim wide. Such a sukkah is invalid. The ten tefachim of height are measured vertically, not along the diagonal.

(לד) דהא הם הסכך - מסתברא דקאי ד"ז על שני הענינים הנ"ל דהיינו בין אם עשה למטה בדופן טפח או שהרחיק ראש הדופן למעלה מן הכותל ועשה הגג טפח בכל זה אמרינן דמן עשרה טפחים ולמעלה חשבינן השיפוע לסכך. כתב הפמ"ג כל מקום שיש פלוגתא דרבוותא בסוכה אם הוא בדאורייתא אזלינן לחומרא ואי לית ליה סוכה אחריתא אוכל שם בלי ברכה ואי בדרבנן ביש לו סוכה אחריתא ראוי להחמיר ולילך שם ובאין לו סוכה אחריתא יוצא בה י"ח וברוכי נמי מברך ועדיין צ"ע קצת עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(34) For they are the s'chach.</b> It appears that this ruling applies to both cases mentioned above, whether he made a tefach below as part of the wall or distanced the upper end of the wall from the adjacent wall and made a roof one tefach wide. In both cases, from the height of ten tefachim and upward, the sloping section is regarded as s'chach. Peri Megadim writes that whenever the early authorities dispute the validity of a sukkah, if the issue concerns a Torah law one must rule stringently. If he has no other sukkah, he should eat there without a blessing. If the issue is rabbinic, then when he has another sukkah it is proper to be stringent and use it, but when he has no other sukkah he fulfills his obligation in this sukkah and may also recite the blessing. This still requires some further study. End of his words.
