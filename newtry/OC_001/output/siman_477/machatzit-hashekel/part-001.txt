<b>(ס"ק ב) בלא ברכה דאתכ' כו'</b> ר"ל דהא קי"ל ר"ס קע"ט דאפי' לא אמר רק הב לן ונברך אם ירצה לאכול צ"ל ולברך. מ"מ הא כתב שם סעיף ב' דאורח הסמוך על שלחן בעה"ב אפי' אמר האורח ה"ל ונברך לא הוי היסח הדעת ועז"כ דבליל פסח אתכ' דרחמנא כו' דאפי' אמר ה"ל ונברך ל"מ היסח הדעת אולם שם בסי' קע"ט לא כתב באורח דלא הוי היסח הדעת כ"א באומר ה"ל ונברך אבל נטל ידיו אינו מבואר שם. ומשמע דאפי' באורח הוי היסח הדעת והכא אפילו נטל ידיו א"צ לברך י"ל דהתם הטעם לפי מ"ש מ"א שם ס"ק ג' דאם בשעת אמירת ה"ל ונברך הסיח דעתו בבירור. אפי' בארח הוי היסת הדעת ולכן בנטל ידיו הוי היסח הדעת בבירור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 2) Without a blessing, for we [rely on] the table, etc.</b> That is to say: for we hold, at the beginning of siman 179, that even if he said only "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless," if he wants to eat he must wash and recite a blessing. Nevertheless, there in se'if 2 it is written that a guest who relies on the host's table, even if the guest said "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless," it is not a diversion of attention; and for that reason [the Mechaber] wrote that on Pesach night we [rely on] the Table of the Merciful One, etc., so that even if he said "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless," it does not constitute a diversion of attention. However, there in siman 179 he did not write regarding a guest that it is not a diversion of attention except when he says "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless"; but if he washed his hands, it is not explained there. And the implication is that even for a guest it is a diversion of attention; yet here, even if he washed his hands, he need not recite a blessing. One may say that there the reason is according to what the Magen Avraham wrote there in s.k. 3: that if at the time of saying "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless" he clearly diverted his attention, even for a guest it is a diversion of attention; and therefore, when he washed his hands, it is clearly a diversion of attention.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Which is not so regarding the Table of the Merciful One: in every case it is not a diversion of attention.</b> For it does not depend on his intent, since he must fulfill Hashem's mitzvah; and certainly forgetfulness caused him to wash his hands, and he did not divert his attention entirely.

וב"ח כ' בשם רש"ל דלא יאכלנו והוא דעת בה"ע וכתב הב"ח הטעם דאם נזכר לאחר שבירך בהמ"ז ועדיין לא בירך בפה"ג אין לעשות כמ"ש בש"ע דלא יברך על הכוס ויטול ידיו ויברך המוציא כו' דא"ל דמעיקרא הכי תיקין בסתם שיברך בפה"ג על כוס ג' לאחר בהמ"ז אע"פ שלא גמר האכילה. דמברך על מזונו סתמא קתני ואם יברך על הכוס ואחר אכילת האפיקומן שוב יברך בהמ"ז על הכוס מוסיף על הכוסות וכן אפי' בהב לן ונברך לא יאכל. אפיקומן דהא איכא למ"ד סי' קעט דאפי' בה"ל ונברך אסור שוב לאכול אפי' רוצה לברך המוציא עד שיברך תחלה בה"מז על מה שכבר אכלו ואם יעשה כי יצטרך שוב לכום בהמ"ז כנ"ל לכן טוב שלא יאכל אפיקומן ויסמוך על מצה שאכל תוך הסעודה אע"ג דלא קי"ל הכי בה"ל ונברך כמ"ש לעיל סי' קעט מ"מ מה לנו להכניס א"ע במחלוקת הפוסקים ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
And the Bach wrote in the name of the Maharshal that he should not eat it; and that is the view of the Baal HaItur. And the Bach wrote the reason: that if he remembers after he recited Birkas HaMazon and has still not recited Borei Pri HaGafen, one should not do as is written in the Shulchan Aruch, namely that he should not bless over the cup, but should wash his hands and recite HaMotzi, etc. For one cannot say that from the outset they enacted in a general way that he recite Borei Pri HaGafen over the third cup after Birkas HaMazon even though he has not finished eating; for "one who recites a blessing over his food" is stated in a general way. And if he blesses over the cup and after eating the afikoman again recites Birkas HaMazon over the cup, he is adding to the cups. And likewise, even with "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless," he should not eat the afikoman; for there is an opinion in siman 179 that even with "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless" it is forbidden thereafter to eat, even if he wants to recite HaMotzi, until he first recites Birkas HaMazon over what he already ate. And if he does so, he will again need a cup for Birkas HaMazon, as above. Therefore it is better that he not eat the afikoman and rely on matzah that he ate during the meal. Even though we do not hold that way regarding "Bring [us a cup] and we will bless," as is written above in siman 179, nevertheless, why should we enter ourselves into a dispute among the poskim; see there.
