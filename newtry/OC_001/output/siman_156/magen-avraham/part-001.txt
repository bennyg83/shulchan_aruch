עיין בהלכות דיעות להרמב"ם וכלל הדבר שילך אדם בדרך המיצוע לא יקמץ יותר מדאי ולא יפזר יותר מדאי רק הגאוה והכעס יתרחק ממנה עד קצה האחרון, אמרינן בגמ' ו' דברים גנאי לת"ח ע' ברכות פ"ו: <b>מלאכתו עיקר</b>. פי' שיעשה רק כדי פרנסתו ואמרי' בגמרא הרבה עשו כרבי ישמעאל ועלתה בידן ע"ש ועיין בהג"מ פ"ג מה' ת"ת בענין יפה ת"ת עם דרך ארץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
See Rambam, Hilchos Deos. The general rule is that a person should follow the middle path. He should neither be excessively miserly nor excessively wasteful. Only pride and anger should he distance himself from to the furthest extreme. The Gemara lists six behaviors that are disgraceful for a Torah scholar; see Berachos, chapter 6. <b>His work primary.</b> Meaning, he should work only sufficiently for his livelihood. The Gemara states that many followed Rabbi Yishmael and succeeded; see there. See also Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 3 of Hilchos Talmud Torah, concerning the virtue of combining Torah study with worldly occupation.

<b>He may become obligated to take an oath to him.</b> See Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 95, that at times it is forbidden to accept the oath. Rambam writes in chapter 6 of Hilchos Deos that it is a positive mitzvah to cleave to Torah scholars and their students in order to learn from their conduct, as it says, “and to Him you shall cleave.” It is a mitzvah for every person to love every Jew as himself, as it says, “You shall love your fellow as yourself.” This applies specifically to one who is your fellow in Torah and mitzvos. But a wicked person who does not accept rebuke is to be hated, as stated in Pesachim 113. Hagahos Maimoniyos. Tosafos there implies that one must nevertheless assist him in loading, in order to subdue one's evil inclination so that the hatred not become complete. Nimukei Yosef rules oppositely, as do Tosafos in Bava Metzia; Rama rules likewise in Choshen Mishpat 272:10. One who gains honor through another's disgrace has no share in the World to Come. Loving a convert who converted entails two positive mitzvos, as it says, “You shall love the convert.” One who hates a Jew in his heart violates “You shall not hate your brother in your heart.” One who strikes or insults another, although forbidden, does not violate “You shall not hate”; nevertheless, striking violates “He shall not add to strike him.” When one person sins against another, the victim should not harbor resentment in silence, but should ask why he acted that way. See simanim 606 and 608 concerning rebuke. Nevertheless, he should not speak harshly enough to shame him, as it says, “and you shall not bear sin because of him.” One who humiliates another publicly has no share in the World to Come. Rather, rebuke should be private, gentle, and in soft language. If he does not repent after two or three occasions, one may shame him publicly. Re'em writes that although Bava Metzia, chapter 2, derives from “You shall surely rebuke” that even a student may rebuke his teacher, this applies where the teacher accepts the rebuke; regarding a fellow, rebuke is required even if he does not accept it. Hagahos Maimoniyos.

One who suspects innocent people is afflicted bodily. Yoma 19. It is forbidden to count Jews directly, even where the purpose is only to cast lots, even for a mitzvah. Rather, they extend their fingers and the fingers are counted. Yoma 22. A communal leader is appointed only if a “box of creeping creatures” hangs behind him, meaning that he has some discreditable ancestry, so that he not become arrogant. There. Permission to deviate from the truth for the sake of peace applies to something already past, not to the future. Sefer Chassidim 426. In three matters one may deviate from the truth: regarding one's knowledge of a tractate, marital privacy, and hospitality. See Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 262. Rash at the end of Sheviis writes, in the name of the Yerushalmi, that one who goes to a place where he is unknown and they assume he has studied extensively and honor him excessively must tell them that he has not studied so much. A guest should not praise his host, lest many guests descend upon him, as stated in Berachos. If one heard a ruling and believes it to be correct, he may state it in the name of a great authority so that people will accept it. Eruvin 51 and Pesachim 112. Yet at the end of Maseches Kallah it states that one who attributes a teaching to a sage from whom he did not learn it causes the Shechinah to depart. The same appears in Berachos 27; see Rabbeinu Yonah there. This requires study. Whoever does not state a teaching in the name of its author violates a prohibition. First chapter of Nedarim.

A person must be careful regarding orphans and widows, even if they are wealthy adults. Whoever taunts them, angers them, pains their hearts, dominates them, or destroys their property violates a prohibition, all the more so one who strikes or curses them, as it says, “You shall not afflict any widow or orphan.” This applies where one afflicts them for his own purpose. A teacher, however, may discipline them in order to teach Torah or a trade or guide them on the proper path. Even so, he should not treat them like everyone else, but should distinguish them and lead them gently and with great compassion. This applies equally to an orphan from the father and from the mother. Until when are they considered orphans for this law? Until they no longer need an adult upon whom to rely, to train and care for them, but can attend to all their needs themselves like other adults.

A talebearer violates “You shall not go as a talebearer among your people.” Who is a talebearer? One who carries matters and goes from one person to another saying, “So-and-so said this,” or “I heard such-and-such about so-and-so.” Even if true, he destroys the world. There is a still greater sin included in this prohibition, namely lashon hara, speaking disparagingly of another even when true. For example, saying, “Where is there fire? In so-and-so's house, where meat and fish are always found,” can be lashon hara when said with disparaging intent. One who says falsehood is called a motzi shem ra. A habitual speaker of lashon hara sits and says, “So-and-so does this,” “His ancestors were such-and-such,” or “I heard this about him.” The Yerushalmi states that one may speak lashon hara about those who maintain a quarrel, as derived from “I will come after you and complete your words.” Hagahos Maimoniyos. This applies specifically when speaking to a righteous king, as Nosson spoke to David. Hagahos Semak. One who accepts lashon hara is worse than the speaker. If he sees evident indications, however, he may believe it, as in the account concerning David in the chapter Bameh Behemah; see there.

What is “the dust” of lashon hara? Saying, “Who would have thought so-and-so would become what he is now?” or, “Be silent; I do not wish to reveal what occurred,” and similar expressions. One who praises another in front of his enemies also violates the dust of lashon hara, because he causes them to speak disparagingly of him. In front of his friends it is permitted. Sefer Chassidim. Rashi, however, explains that one should not praise another excessively, because while enumerating all his virtues he will inevitably come to mention a fault. Hagahos Maimoniyos. This requires study, for Bava Basra 164 relates that someone praised a document's handwriting, and Rabbi Shimon said, “I did not write it; Yehudah the tailor did,” to which he replied, “Stop speaking lashon hara.” Likewise, one who speaks lashon hara jokingly or frivolously, without hatred, is included in Shlomo's statement, “Like one who shoots firebrands, arrows, and death, and says, ‘I was only joking.’” The same applies to one who speaks lashon hara deceitfully. Whether spoken in the subject's presence or absence, any statement that, if passed from person to person, may harm another bodily or financially, distress him, or frighten him, is lashon hara. See Shabbos 33b in the incident of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai.

If such words were said before three people, the matter is already publicized. If one of the three repeats it, there is no lashon hara, provided he does not intend to spread it further. Yoma, chapter 1, asks: From where do we know that when someone tells another something, it remains under “do not say” until he says, “Go and say”? If the speaker warned him not to repeat it, then even if it was said before many people, repeating it is lashon hara, as in Sanhedrin regarding the student. Hagahos Maimoniyos. Tosafos in Bava Basra 39 explain that any statement ordinarily said before three people is not lashon hara for the original speaker, because it is as though he said it before the subject, which is not lashon hara. Tosafos in Arachin, chapter 6, limit this to a case like “Where is fire? In so-and-so's house,” where it can be understood non-disparagingly. But an insulting remark remains lashon hara even when said before the subject.

One who takes revenge violates “You shall not take revenge or bear a grudge.” How so? If one asks, “Lend me your axe,” and the other refuses, and the next day the latter asks, “Lend me your sickle,” and he answers, “I will not lend you, just as you did not lend me,” this is revenge. If he says, “Here, I will lend it to you, for I am not like you,” this is bearing a grudge. Rather, he must erase the matter from his heart and not remember it at all. Rambam. Yoma states that a Torah scholar who does not take revenge and bear a grudge like a serpent when his honor is insulted is not a Torah scholar. Nevertheless, he should not personally retaliate; if others avenge him, he may remain silent. If they appease him, he must forgive. Every person must be careful in this matter.

Rambam lists as positive mitzvah 73 the obligation to confess before Hashem for every sin one commits, as it says, “They shall confess their sin.” In the section of prohibitions, mitzvah 195 forbids eating and drinking in the manner of a glutton and drunkard, as it says, “This son of ours is a glutton and drunkard.” Mitzvah 299 forbids placing a stumbling block before an innocent person, meaning giving him improper advice, as it says, “You shall not place a stumbling block before the blind.” May the Omnipresent guide us on a true and upright path. One should beware of flattery and mockery, as is known, and also of anger. One who inspects his property daily will find a coin. One should divide one's money into thirds: one third in land, one third in commerce, and one third in ready cash. “You shall love your fellow as yourself.” Judge your fellow favorably; see Shabbos 127. A person should not utter an indecent expression, and therefore in Talmud study should say bei'im rather than beitzim. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 4. One should not kill a louse in front of another person. Chagigah, chapter 1; Maseches Derech Eretz, chapter 3. A host enters first and a guest leaves first. The zealous hasten to mitzvos, but not earlier than dawn. Pesachim 4. One who calls Avraham “Avram” violates a positive and a negative commandment. End of the first chapter of Berachos. One who benefits from the meal of a bridegroom and does not gladden him violates the five “voices.” Berachos, chapter 1. One who engages in idle talk violates a positive commandment, meaning indecent and frivolous speech. Yoma.

A person should not say, “I possess more than I deserve according to my deeds and service of Hashem,” for this would imply that the Holy One, blessed is He, overlooks sin. Rather, he should conclude, “apart from the merit of the Patriarchs that assists me.” Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 5. In my opinion, Hashem may grant more reward, but He does not overlook sin; He merely delays punishment.

Shabbos 31 states that when a person is brought to judgment, he is asked: Did you conduct business faithfully? Did you establish fixed times for Torah? Did you engage in procreation? Did you anticipate salvation, meaning the fulfillment of the prophets' words? Did you analyze wisdom? Did you understand one matter from another? Even so, if fear of Hashem was his treasure, yes; if not, no. See Midrash Mishlei, which elaborates greatly on what a person is asked, including whether he studied Shiur Komah. In our many sins we do not know the full extent; may Hashem grant wisdom. Rashi explains that because a person must engage in worldly occupation, and without worldly occupation Torah cannot endure, he must set fixed times for Torah so that worldly matters not occupy his whole day. This implies that one who does not need to work is obligated to fulfill “You shall meditate upon it day and night.” See Yoreh Deah 246. Kiddushin 30b states that a person must teach his son a trade or commerce, and if he does not, it is as though he taught him robbery.

Shabbos 129 states that one who underwent bloodletting and has nothing to eat should take a defective coin, go from shopkeeper to shopkeeper, taste wine at each, and when he offers to buy, the shopkeeper will refuse the coin, until he has tasted a revi'is. Although our bloodletting does not involve such danger, this may teach us regarding another sick person.

Sotah at the end of chapter 7 relates that when King Agrippa read, “You may not place over yourself a foreign man,” his eyes filled with tears because he was not of Jewish descent. They said to him, “You are our brother.” At that moment the Jewish people became liable to destruction because they flattered Agrippa. Although they could not protest, they should have remained silent rather than affirming him. This is the punishment of one who flatters a sinner out of fear of him without regard for fear of the Holy One, blessed is He. If, however, he fears that the sinner will kill him, he may say, “You acted properly,” even though the act was sinful. Tosafos there.

Berachos 7 teaches that one should not provoke a wicked person whose fortunes are favorable. Shabbos 33 states that women are of light understanding; perhaps they will be tortured and disclose our whereabouts, so Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his son hid in a cave. This implies that one may flee where there is danger to life, even though this may cause pain to another. It also implies that one must endure pain rather than cause another to be killed without cause. End of Berachos: Whoever presses the hour is pressed by the hour; whoever yields to the hour, the hour yields to him. Whoever benefits from a meal at which a Torah scholar is present is as though he benefited from the radiance of the Shechinah. Shabbos 30. In Torah matters, “Answer a fool according to his folly”; in ordinary matters, do not answer him, like the one who said to Rabbi, “Your wife committed adultery with me.” There, page 32: A person should not stand in a dangerous place. Rav would not cross in a ferry containing a non-Jew, lest judgment be exacted from him and Rav be caught with him. Shmuel crossed only in a ferry containing a non-Jew, saying that the accuser does not rule over two nations. Accordingly, all agree that one should not cross with a Jewish apostate or wicked person.
