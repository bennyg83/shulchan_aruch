(א) ילך לעסקיו - והנהגת האדם צריך להיות הכל בדרך המיצוע לא יקמץ יותר מדאי ולא יפזר יותר מדאי וכן בכל המדות רק הגאוה והכעס יתרחק מהם עד קצה האחרון וכמו שאמר התנא מאד הוי שפל רוח וכן בכעס הזהירו חז"ל הרבה עליה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) He should go to his business. A person's conduct should always follow the middle path. He should neither be excessively miserly nor excessively wasteful, and so regarding all character traits. Only pride and anger should he distance himself from to the furthest extreme, as the Tanna said, “Be exceedingly humble of spirit,” and Chazal issued many warnings concerning anger.

(ב) עיקר - אלא יעשה רק כדי פרנסתו אך בזה גופא צריך להזהר מפיתוי היצר שמפתהו שכל היום צריך השתדלות על הרוחה זו. והעיקר שיתבונן בעצמו מה הוא הכרח האמיתי שאי אפשר בלעדו ואז יכול להתקיים בידו שיהא מלאכתו עראי ותורתו עיקר:
**** ENGLISH ****
(2) Primary. He should work only sufficiently for his livelihood. Yet even here he must guard against the enticement of the evil inclination, which persuades him that he requires the entire day to pursue profit. His primary task is to reflect honestly upon what is truly indispensable. Then he can maintain his work as incidental and Torah study as primary.

(4) Faithfully. Meaning, his business should contain no theft or deceit. A person is questioned about this at judgment, as Chazal said. There are many constant mitzvos, positive and negative, that obligate a person at all times but are not recorded in Shulchan Aruch. Magen Avraham copied several from Rambam and other early authorities, together with proper forms of conduct mentioned by Chazal. I too will therefore copy some of them here.

Rambam writes in chapter 6 of Hilchos Deos, halachah 2: It is a positive mitzvah to cleave to Torah scholars and their students in order to learn from their conduct, as it says, “and to Him you shall cleave.” Can a person cleave to the Shechinah? Rather, Chazal explained this mitzvah: cleave to Torah scholars and their students. Therefore, a person should strive to marry the daughter of a Torah scholar, marry his daughter to a Torah scholar, eat and drink with Torah scholars, conduct commerce on behalf of Torah scholars, and associate with them in every form of association, as it says, “and to cleave to Him.” Chazal said that one who benefits from a meal at which a Torah scholar is present is as though he benefited from the radiance of the Shechinah. Berachos 64.

Rambam further writes in chapter 6, halachah 3, that it is a mitzvah for every person to love every Jew as himself, as it says, “You shall love your fellow as yourself.” Therefore, he must speak his praise, though not before his enemies lest they come to disparage him, and be concerned for his property as he is for his own and desire his own honor. One who gains honor through another's disgrace has no share in the World to Come. This applies specifically to one who is your fellow in Torah and mitzvos. But a wicked person whom one saw transgress a sin well known among Jews and who did not accept rebuke is to be hated. Pesachim 113. Loving a convert in the time the mitzvah applies entails two positive commandments, one because he is included among Israel and a separate commandment, “You shall love the convert.” One who hates a Jew in his heart violates “You shall not hate your brother in your heart.” One who strikes or insults another does not violate that prohibition, but striking violates “He shall not add to strike him,” and insult violates “You shall not wrong one another.” If he shames him, he also violates “You shall not bear sin because of him.”

Rambam writes in Sefer HaMitzvos, positive mitzvah 205, that included in the mitzvah of rebuke is that we rebuke one another when one person sins against another. We should not harbor resentment or regard him as guilty in our hearts, but must rebuke him verbally until nothing remains in our hearts. Nevertheless, one should not speak harshly enough to shame him, as it says, “and you shall not bear sin because of him.” Rather, he should rebuke him privately, gently, and in soft language. The principal laws of rebuke will be explained, with Hashem's help, in simanim 606 and 608.

A person must be careful regarding orphans and widows, even if they are wealthy adults. Whoever taunts them, angers them, pains their hearts, dominates them, or destroys their property violates a prohibition, all the more so one who strikes or curses them, as it says, “You shall not afflict any widow or orphan.” This applies where one afflicts them for his own purpose. A teacher, however, may discipline them to teach Torah or a trade or guide them on the proper path. Even so, he should not treat them like everyone else, but distinguish them and lead them gently and with great compassion. This applies equally to an orphan from the father and from the mother. Until when are they considered orphans? Until they no longer need an adult upon whom to rely, to train and care for them, but can attend to all their needs themselves like other adults.

A talebearer violates “You shall not go as a talebearer among your people.” A talebearer carries matters and goes from one person to another saying, “So-and-so said this,” or, “I heard such-and-such about so-and-so.” Even if true, he destroys the world. There is a still greater sin included in this prohibition, namely lashon hara, speaking disparagingly of another even when true. One who says falsehood is called a motzi shem ra. One who relates matters that, if passed from person to person, may harm another bodily or financially, distress him, or frighten him, speaks lashon hara. This prohibition has many detailed laws explained extensively by the early authorities. One who wishes to know all the details should study Sefer Chafetz Chaim, where they are explained at length.

One may not maintain a quarrel, as it says, “He shall not be like Korach and his assembly.” Semag, prohibition 157. One who takes revenge violates “You shall not take revenge or bear a grudge.” If one asks, “Lend me your axe,” and the other refuses, and the next day the latter asks, “Lend me your sickle,” and he answers, “I will not lend you, just as you did not lend me,” this is revenge. Rabbeinu Eliezer of Metz writes in Sefer Yereim that this is not limited to borrowing utensils, for the verse does not mention utensils. It includes any other property. Jews are thus warned not to withhold charity or monetary kindness because the other person did not do the same for him, for this is revenge. If he says, “Here, I will lend it to you, for I am not like you,” this is bearing a grudge. Rather, he must erase the matter from his heart and not remember it at all. Rambam.

Positive mitzvah 73 is to confess before Hashem for every sin, as it says, “They shall confess their sin.” In the section of prohibitions, mitzvah 195 forbids eating and drinking in a gluttonous and drunken manner in youth, as it says, “You shall not eat over the blood,” meaning do not eat in a manner that leads to bloodshed, namely the eating of the wayward and rebellious son, for which he is liable to death. Although after youth the Torah no longer imposes death for such eating, its disgrace is evident from the verse, “This son of ours is a glutton and drunkard,” and from, “Do not be among wine guzzlers, among gluttonous eaters of meat.” According to Ramban, this is included in the positive mitzvah, “You shall be holy.”

A person should not utter an indecent expression. Pesachim 3. Mitzvah 299 is not to place a stumbling block before an innocent person, meaning not to give him improper advice, as it says, “You shall not place a stumbling block before the blind.” One should beware of mockery and flattery and distance himself completely from falsehood, as discussed in Shevuos 31. For the sake of peace, however, one may deviate from the truth. See Magen Avraham citing Sefer Chassidim; this requires study. In three matters one may deviate from the truth: regarding one's knowledge of a tractate, marital privacy, and hospitality. Choshen Mishpat 262; see there.

Sotah at the end of chapter 7 relates that when King Agrippa read, “You may not place over yourself a foreign man,” his eyes filled with tears because he was not of Jewish descent. They said, “You are our brother.” At that moment the Jewish people became liable to destruction because they flattered Agrippa. Although they could not protest, they should have remained silent rather than affirming him. This is the punishment of one who flatters a sinner out of fear without regard for fear of the Holy One, blessed is He. If, however, he fears that the sinner will kill him, he may tell him, “You acted properly,” even if the act was sinful.

Chazal explained “Judge your fellow with righteousness” to mean: judge your fellow favorably. See Shabbos 127. One who suspects innocent people is afflicted bodily. Yoma 19. One who engages in idle conversation violates a positive commandment, meaning indecent and frivolous talk, as it says, “You shall speak of them,” and not of idle matters. A prohibition derived from a positive commandment has the force of a positive commandment. Yoma 19.

Rambam writes in Sefer HaMitzvos, mitzvah 8, that we are commanded to emulate Hashem to the extent possible, as it says, “You shall walk in His ways.” This command is repeated in “to walk in all His ways.” Chazal explained: Just as the Holy One, blessed is He, is called gracious, so should you be gracious; just as He is called merciful, so should you be merciful. The same idea is repeated in “After Hashem you shall walk,” which means emulating His good acts and noble attributes.

There are many more constant positive and negative commandments mentioned throughout the four sections of Shulchan Aruch, each in its place. Many more are not recorded in Shulchan Aruch and are found in the works of those who count the mitzvos, Rambam, Semag, and Sefer HaChinuch, and especially in Sefer Chareidim, which collected from all the earlier authorities the mitzvos that apply in practice today. It is very proper for every person to study them and know them well. Then he will be capable of fulfilling them. Chazal explained the verse, “You shall see it and remember all the commandments of Hashem and perform them,” that remembering leads to action. If one does not know that something is a mitzvah at all, what can he remember in order to fulfill it?
