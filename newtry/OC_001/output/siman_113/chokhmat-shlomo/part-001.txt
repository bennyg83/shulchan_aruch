<b>Seif 9.</b> One may not add, etc. Note: I saw in Responsa Gur Aryeh Yehudah, Orach Chayim, siman 105, that he cited in the name of a responsum of the Peri Megadim the question why we say ‘the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac’ without a connecting vav, but say ‘and the God of Jacob’ with a vav. See what he wrote there. The responsa of the Peri Megadim are not available to me for review. In truth, there is an explicit verse in Parashas Va'eira, ‘to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob,’ where Jacob is introduced with a vav and Isaac is not. See the Zohar there, which explains that this alludes to Jacob's primacy. From there the wording of the prayer clearly derives, with a vav before Jacob. See also what I previously wrote explaining this, and in my Torah novellae of 5592 on Parashas Vayeitzei, where I offered another explanation based on the words of the holy Zohar there and in Parashas Va'eira. See there. In the Zohar on Vayikra, on the verse ‘If a soul sins,’ there is another reason why Isaac is not introduced with a vav, namely, because Isaac must be included within Abraham. See there and examine it carefully. It now appears to me further that Hashem called Jacob the firstborn, as stated in the Midrash on Parashas Bo, on ‘Sanctify to Me every firstborn,’ and in the prayer, ‘the congregation of Jacob, Your son, Your firstborn.’ The meaning is as follows. Regarding the redemption from Egypt, Abraham is mentioned first, but regarding the future redemption it says, ‘I will remember My covenant with Jacob,’ placing Jacob first. Hashem thus made him the firstborn of the Patriarchs. This will not be realized until the future, when Jacob will become first among the Patriarchs; until now Abraham has been first. It is known that a vav adds to the preceding matter. We therefore begin, ‘the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac,’ reflecting the present order, and then say, ‘and the God of Jacob,’ who in the future will stand first, before Abraham. The vav separates him as a distinct matter, indicating that in the future the blessing will begin with him, since he will then be first among the Patriarchs. This is clear and readily understood. A further explanation may be offered based on Bava Metzia 85b, where Eliyahu said that all the Patriarchs cannot be awakened together, because they would increase mercy and bring Mashiach before his appointed time. So too, when we say ‘the God of Abraham,’ Abraham is aroused above, and likewise Isaac and Jacob. Were all three mentioned as one connected unit, all would be aroused together and would bring Mashiach before his time. Therefore Abraham is mentioned separately and Isaac separately. As for Jacob, even if Isaac and Jacob were to stand together, they would not bring Mashiach before his time, because they are one against one: Isaac represents judgment and Jacob mercy. But if Abraham were also joined, there would be two attributes of mercy and one of judgment. Mercy would prevail over judgment; moreover, with Abraham on one side, Jacob on the other, and Isaac in the middle, they would sweeten the judgment and bring Mashiach before his time. Isaac is therefore mentioned without a vav. This is clear and readily understood. See also my work Chochmas Shlomo on the Torah, with Hashem's help, Parashas Shemos, year 5620, where I wrote further on this basis. Examine it carefully.
