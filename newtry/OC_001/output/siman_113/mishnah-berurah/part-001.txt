**** ENGLISH ****
(9) Disgraceful: One should bow only where the Sages instituted bowing. For this reason, one should not bow at ‘and to Jerusalem’ or at Boneh Yerushalayim, contrary to Maharil. Darchei Moshe. Magen Avraham writes that perhaps one may bow slightly. All this applies during the Amidah. Outside the Amidah, however, one may bow and prostrate as he wishes. Thus, we find regarding Rabbi Akiva that a person would leave him in one corner and find him in another because of his many bows and prostrations. The poskim explain that this occurred after he had completed the Amidah. The Shulchan Aruch prohibits bowing in all the cases listed here because these expressions of thanksgiving are by way of praise and glorification, to which bowing and prostration do not pertain. Likewise, ‘every stature will bow before You’ does not refer to oneself but states that the entire world will bow before Him, may He be blessed, and is therefore also praise and glorification. But at ‘and we bow and prostrate,’ where one speaks about oneself, one may bow. Taz; see there further.
