(4) And bless on them — and immediately after Shema he removes them. But the tallit he may wear and bless on it before beginning the blessings; and he may also wear it all day, for a clean body is not required for tzitzit. However, if he did not put on the tallit beforehand and it came to him between Ahavah and Shema, he may put it on without a blessing, for donning is not an interruption. And all this is when this incident occurred on account of some food that harmed him and the like — so that in the evening he can certainly pray with a clean body. But one who is accustomed such that he has an illness on account of which he is compelled to belch every hour — and if he does not pray he is exempt from prayer forever — it is permitted for him to pray without a clean body, for the prohibition of belching, which is a bad odor that has no substance, is only rabbinic; and it yields to Keriat Shema, which is Torah, or to prayer, which is a public mitzvah. Therefore he recites Shema and prays [Rama in responsum siman 98]. And Peri Chadash also wrote thus, except that he added that when belching comes to him he stops until the odor passes, as with a bad odor that has no substance — he stops and returns to pray. And Peri Megadim was further uncertain: perhaps in such a case it is also permitted to put on tefillin, for how can he uproot forever a Torah positive commandment of tefillin on account of this? And it seems Peri Megadim's intent is only donning for a few moments, and only in order to fulfill the mitzvah; beyond that it is certainly forbidden. And if he cannot restrain himself from belching long enough to don the shel yad and shel rosh, he dons them one by one. And regarding a blessing it seems he should not bless on them. Rama wrote in responsum siman 98: one who has an illness such that he constantly drips urine against his will — if so he could never pray. One should permit making for him a garment around his organ, and the outer garments shall be clean, and he enters the synagogue and prays; at the time of dripping urine he stops, and afterward prays.
