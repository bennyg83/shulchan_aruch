<b>(ס"ק א) אינו כו'</b> כ"מ סי' קפ"ג שכת' משנתנו לו כוס לברך לא ישיח המברך ת"ל אפי' קודם נתינת הכוס אסור לשיח דהא כבר נטל ידיו. ועמ"ש בסי' קס"ו דאפי' במים ראשונים אסור לשיח כ"ש במים אחרונים דחמירים לענין הפסקה כמבואר שם ועמ"ש מ"א בס"ס קס"ה ובמ"ש שם בע"ה. וגם הראי' מסי' קפ"ג ס"ו דלעיל שהביא הכ"מ להתיר לשיח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 1, he may not, etc.</b> This is implied by siman 183, which states that once the cup has been given to him for Bircas HaMazon, the one reciting the blessing should not speak. This teaches that even before the cup is given, speaking is forbidden, since he has already washed his hands. See what I wrote in siman 166, that even after washing before the meal one may not speak. All the more so after mayim acharonim, which are stricter regarding interruption, as explained there. See also Magen Avraham at the end of siman 165 and what I wrote there, with Hashem’s help. The proof from siman 183, seif 6, cited by Kesef Mishneh to permit speaking, is likewise not a proof.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2, until, etc.</b> The halachah does not follow, etc. Berachos 42a records that Rav Huna son of Rav Nachman holds that once one has finished eating, he may no longer eat, whereas Rav Pappa holds that even after finishing he may still eat unless the table has been cleared, meaning that the bread and all foods were removed. The Gemara concludes that the halachah does not follow any of these teachings, for whether he merely finished or even cleared the table, he may still eat. Rather, it follows Rabbi Chiya bar Ashi in the name of Rav, who said that there are three matters requiring immediate succession, one of which is that Bircas HaMazon must immediately follow mayim acharonim. Rashi explains that only after washing may one no longer eat. The Rosh writes that if one said, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” then according to Rashi and Rashbam, who explain Rav’s statement in Pesachim 103b, he may no longer drink until he first recites Bircas HaMazon. Some distinguish between eating and drinking, namely Rabbeinu Yonah and Ran, whose view is cited here in Shulchan Aruch, but the Rosh rejects that distinction. The Rosh in the chapters Kisui HaDam and Arvei Pesachim disagrees with Rashi and Rashbam. He holds that after saying “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” one may not drink unless he first recites Borei Peri HaGafen, but he need not first recite Bircas HaMazon. The Rosh does not state explicitly what he holds after mayim acharonim, whether one must first recite Bircas HaMazon or whether a new blessing before the food or drink suffices. The Tur understands that even after mayim acharonim one need not first recite Bircas HaMazon. Beit Yosef, however, infers from the Rosh that after mayim acharonim he agrees that Bircas HaMazon must be recited first. The Rosh writes that only according to Rashi and Rashbam is saying “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon” equivalent to mayim acharonim. This implies that according to the Rosh, who holds that this declaration does not require Bircas HaMazon first, it is not equivalent to mayim acharonim. Were the Rosh to hold that even after mayim acharonim one need not recite Bircas HaMazon first, the two cases would be equivalent according to him as well. It must therefore be that he agrees that after mayim acharonim one must first recite Bircas HaMazon. Shulchan Aruch therefore rules here unlike the Tur.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>How could he contradict in the abridgment, etc.</b> Although the Tur also authored the abridgment of the Rosh’s rulings, it would not be so astonishing if, while writing the Tur, the Rosh’s language cited by Beit Yosef temporarily escaped him. But while writing the abridgment, the Rosh’s work stood before him and he was condensing it. How could he write the opposite of the Rosh’s own conclusion while those words were before his eyes? Moreover, the Rosh himself writes in Arvei Pesachim, siman 7, regarding the Rif’s ruling. Although the halachah follows Shmuel that members of a group eating on Friday afternoon need not interrupt when Shabbos begins, but merely cover the bread, recite Kiddush, and continue their meal, the Rif writes that if they finished their meal before Shabbos and also washed mayim acharonim, they must first recite Bircas HaMazon and only afterward Kiddush. The Rosh challenges this: since Kiddush must be recited in the place of the meal, and the meal eaten before Kiddush cannot reasonably establish that status, they must eat again after Kiddush. Why, then, must they first recite Bircas HaMazon? They should cover the bread, recite Kiddush, and continue eating. He concludes, “Although they washed their hands,” etc. Thus the Rosh clearly holds that even after mayim acharonim one need not first recite Bircas HaMazon, and a new blessing before eating suffices, contrary to Beit Yosef.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And what he wrote in the chapter Kisui HaDam, etc.</b> This refers to Beit Yosef’s proof cited above, from the Rosh’s statement that according to Rashi and Rashbam, saying “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon” is equivalent to mayim acharonim. The meaning is not, as Beit Yosef understood, that in both cases Bircas HaMazon must first be recited. Rather, the Rosh is discussing the requirement that Bircas HaMazon immediately follow mayim acharonim. The issue there is not eating between them, but interruption by any other activity, comparable to the other cases of immediate succession listed there, such as adjoining redemption to prayer. The Rosh means that saying “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon” is likewise equivalent to mayim acharonim in that one may not interrupt with another activity. This is specifically according to Rashi and Rashbam, who hold that if one wishes to eat, he must first recite Bircas HaMazon. According to them, the declaration is more than mere diversion of attention; it is a complete conclusion of the meal and is as though he has already begun Bircas HaMazon. Therefore he may not interrupt with any other activity. According to the Rosh, however, since the declaration does not require Bircas HaMazon first and is only diversion of attention, he may interrupt with another activity. Although according to the Rosh, after mayim acharonim one likewise need not first recite Bircas HaMazon, nevertheless mayim acharonim may still constitute a greater conclusion than the declaration.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some distinguish, etc.</b> One might ask: according to Magen Avraham’s explanation that the Rosh discusses only interruption by another activity, how does the Rosh’s concluding distinction between eating and drinking relate? Magen Avraham means that this follows the Rosh’s statement that, according to Rashi and Rashbam, the declaration is equivalent to mayim acharonim regarding interruption by another activity. From this we infer that according to the Rosh there is a distinction between the declaration and mayim acharonim regarding such an interruption. But regarding eating and drinking, the Rosh makes no distinction: in both cases one need not first recite Bircas HaMazon, and a new blessing before eating or drinking suffices. The Rosh then adds that some distinguish further: after the declaration, drinking requires a new blessing, but eating does not; after mayim acharonim, even eating requires a new blessing.

<b>And Beit Yosef abbreviated his citation.</b> Meaning, he did not cite the beginning of the Rosh’s statement, “Bircas HaMazon must immediately follow mayim acharonim,” but cited only: “The halachah is not so; rather, as long as he has not washed he may eat, and if he said, ‘Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,’ it is equivalent to mayim acharonim according to Rashi and Rashbam,” etc. Therefore Beit Yosef did not consider that the Rosh might be discussing interruption by another activity, and instead understood him to be discussing one who wishes to eat.

<b>This is implied by Tosafos in Berachos 42, etc.</b> Tosafos there, Tosafos in Pesachim 101, and the Rosh in chapter 6 of Chullin ask concerning the account in Berachos 42. Rava and Rabbi Zeira visited the house of the Exilarch. After the table had been cleared, food was sent to them from the Exilarch’s house. Rava ate, but Rabbi Zeira did not, because clearing the table ends the meal. They ask: even after saying “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” one need not first recite Bircas HaMazon; a new blessing before eating suffices. <b>(סק"ד) שיאמר כו'</b> דדוקא ששתקו כו' דאין עדיפת בע"ה יותר מאורח. אלא לגבי' נפשי' דאורח אפי' אמר ה"ל ונברך מ"מ מסתמ' דעתו אם יתן לו הבעה"ב עוד לאכול יאכל אבל בע"ה כיון דאמר ה"ל ונברך אפי' האורח רוצה לאכול אסור לבע"ה לאכול כי הבעה"ב אינו תלוי באורח אבל מכל מקום אם האורח דעתו לאכול אף שאמר בעה"ב ה"ל ונברך רשאי אורח לאכול:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 4, that the host should say, etc.</b> This applies specifically where the guests remained silent and agreed. The host has no greater authority over a guest than the guest has over himself. Even if the guest said, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” his presumed intent is that if the host offers him more food, he will eat. But once the host says it, the host himself may not eat even if the guest wishes to continue, since the host is not dependent upon the guest. Nevertheless, if the guest intends to continue eating, he may do so even though the host said, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon.”
