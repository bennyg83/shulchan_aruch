<b>א) [סעיף א'] כל אדם יש לו אלפים אמה וכו'  </b>כדרשינן אל יצא איש ממקומו אלו אלפים אמה. עירובין נ"א ע"א. ואינו אלא מדרבנן וקרא אסמכתא בעלמא. כ"כ הרי"ף והרא"ש בסוף פ"ק דעירובין. וכ"כ הטור. ומיהו חוץ לי"ב מיל כתב הרי"ף שם דבירושלמי קאמר דהוי דאורייתא יעו"ש. וכ"כ הרמב"ם ריש פכ"ז. וכתב שם המ"מ שכ"ה הסכמת הגאונים. אבל הרא"ש והמרדכי כתבו דלגמ' דידן אפי' חוץ לי"ב מיל לא מיתסר אלא מדרבנן וכתב המ"מ שם דלדיעה זו הסכימו הרמב"ן והרשב"א יעו"ש. והב"ד ב"י. ודע דגם לדעת הסוברים דחוץ לי"ב מיל דאורייתא אין בו איסור כרת רק לאו בעלמא דהא לא נמנה בט"ל מלאכות וכ"ה בהרמב"ם שם שלא כתב בו כ"א איסור מלקות. וכ"כ בס' החינוך ובס' מצות ה' פ' בשלח יעו"ש. ומ"ש הלבוש דחייבין עליו חטאת כבר השיג עליו המי"ט והביאו א"ר אות א' יעו"ש. ועיין לעיל סי' של"ד אות קל"ז ולקמן סי' ת"ד אות י"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] Every person has two thousand amos, etc. </b>This is derived from the verse, 'Let no man go out from his place,' which refers to two thousand amos. Eruvin 51a. This prohibition is only Rabbinic, and the verse is merely a Scriptural support. Rif and Rosh write this at the end of chapter 1 of Eruvin, as does the Tur. Regarding traveling beyond twelve mil, however, Rif writes there that the Yerushalmi states that the prohibition is of Torah origin. See there. Rambam likewise writes this at the beginning of chapter 27. Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is the consensus of the Geonim. Rosh and Mordechai, however, write that according to the Babylonian Gemara, even traveling beyond twelve mil is prohibited only by Rabbinic law. Maggid Mishneh writes that Ramban and Rashba agree with this opinion. See there. Beis Yosef cites their words. Know that even according to the authorities who maintain that traveling beyond twelve mil is prohibited by Torah law, the violation does not incur kareis but only transgresses a negative commandment, since it is not counted among the thirty-nine melachos. Rambam there accordingly mentions only liability for lashes. Sefer HaChinuch and Sefer Mitzvos Hashem, parashas Beshalach, write likewise. See there. Mateh Yehudah challenges Levush's statement that one is liable to bring a sin offering, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, cites the challenge. See there. See above, siman 334, subsection 137, and below, siman 404, subsection 14.

<b>ז) שם. פסיעות בינונות.  </b>דדרך הליכתו של אדם אמה. רש"י. והיינו אורך ב' מנעלים מנעל א' מקום מצב הרגל ומנעל הוי בין רגל לרגל. מהרי"ו סי' מ"ה. מ"א סק"א: א"ר שם. תו"ש אות ב' ר"ז או' ב' ח"א כלל ע"ו אות א' וכתב בחידושי הריטב"א וכ"ז היינו אם צריך לילך בשבת לאיזה צורך מצוה מונה אותו דהוה כמדידה של מצוה דשרי כמ"ש סי' ש"ו סעיף ז' יעו"ע. תו"ש שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>7) There. Medium steps. </b>A person's ordinary stride is one amah. Rashi. This equals the length of two shoes: one shoe corresponds to the place occupied by the foot, and one shoe to the space between one foot and the other. Mahari Weil, siman 45; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, there; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Chayei Adam, rule 76, subsection 1. Ritva writes in his novellae that all this applies when one needs to walk on Shabbos for a mitzvah purpose. One may count the steps because this is comparable to measuring for a mitzvah purpose, which is permitted, as stated in siman 306, se'if 7. See there. Tosefes Shabbos, there.

<b>14) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. </b>The same applies to his produce and anything else belonging to him. Beitzah 39b and Rashi there. Avodas HaKodesh, folio 35, writes: When does this apply? When the items are within his techum. Even if they are stored in warehouses outside the city, whenever they are within his techum they have the same techum as he does. If, however, they are situated in another city, then although the residents of that city established an eruv in his direction and can reach the place where he established his Shabbos residence, his produce has the status of that city and may not be moved from its place. <b>טו) שם כך כליו ובהמתו וכו'  </b>ונראה דבהמה וכלים של נכסי מלוג הרי הם כרגלי הבעל ואם נתן לה אחד מתנה ע"מ שאין לבעל רשות בהן ומה שתרצה תעשה הוי כרגלי שניהם. ונראה פשוט דאם א' בעיר אחרת רחוק יותר מאלפים אמה ופירותיו כאן מותר להוליך הפירות לצד עירו ולא חיישינן שמא עירב הוא לצד אחר רחוק אלפים ממנו דאז אין להם אלא ד"א דמוקמינן לה אחזקה שלא עירב גם נראה דאם היה רחוק מכאן ונודע שהוא גוסס כבר ג' ימים דאז הוא בחזקת מת וכליו קנו שביתה אצל יורשיו כמ"ש סי' שכ"ט עי"ש וביו"ד סי' של"ט ובאה"ע סי' י"ז תו"ש או' ג'.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>15) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. </b>It appears that an animal and utensils belonging to nichsei melog have the same techum as the husband. If someone gave the wife a gift on condition that her husband have no rights in it and that she may do with it whatever she wishes, it has the techum of both spouses. It also appears obvious that if a person is in another city more than two thousand amos away while his produce is here, the produce may be conveyed toward his city. We are not concerned that he might have established an eruv two thousand amos away in the opposite direction, which would leave the produce only four amos, because we maintain the presumption that he did not establish an eruv. It further appears that if the owner was distant from here and it became known that he had already been moribund for three days, he is presumed dead and his utensils establish their Shabbos residence with his heirs, as stated in siman 329. See there, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 339, and Even HaEzer, siman 17. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3.

<b>18) There. One who entrusts his animal to his son. </b>Beis Yosef writes this in the name of Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Yom Tov, and rules accordingly in the Shulchan Aruch. Avodas HaKodesh, folio 35, however, disagrees with Rambam and rules that a son has the same status as another person. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 5 of Beitzah, likewise disagrees with Rambam. Rambam himself rules in his Commentary on the Mishnah that a son is like another person. This also appears from the Tur, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 33, who do not distinguish between a son and another person. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Yom Tov, also writes that Rashi and the other commentators rule that a son is like another person. It therefore appears clear to me that one should not rule in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch and Levush on this matter. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, however, rules in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch. This also appears to be the opinion of Magen Avraham and Tosefes Shabbos, cited in the preceding subsection, since they explain the Shulchan Aruch's words without disagreeing.
