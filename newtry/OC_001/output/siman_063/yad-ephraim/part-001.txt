<b>בט"ז ס"ק ב') </b>וזה מוכח כו' נ"ל שיש כאן חיסו' לשון וכוונתו דשם אמרינן דאזדא ר"י לטעמיה ואמר הלכה כר"א משום ר"י דס"ל כיון שכיון לבו בפסוק ראשון שוב א"צ ואי ס"ד דגם במהלך ועומד יש קפידא דמחזי דעביד כב"ש א"כ איך אמר ר"י דכל הפרשה בעמידה וא"ל דר"י ס"ל ר"ל דס"ל כמד"א דעביד כב"ש עשוי. ז"א דהא גם בפרשת והיה א"ש ס"ל לב"ש בבוקר יעמדו. א"כ אם כדי לצאת ידי ב"ש גם והיה אם שמוע צריך לקרות בעמידה וז"א דר"י לא ס"ל רק בפרשה ראשונה ומשום כוונ' כדקאמ' ואזדא לטעמיה וכו' ועוד דמשמע מר"י דאף בערב הדין כן וב"ש ס"ל דדוקא בבוקר יעמדו. אלא ע"כ דאין קפידא אלא ביושב ועומד וכו' וכוונתו במלות קצרות הרחבתי ביאורם קצת:
**** ENGLISH ****
In Turei Zahav, se'if katan 2: “And this is proven,” etc. It seems to me that words are missing here. His intent is that there it is said that Rabbi Yochanan follows his own reasoning, and he ruled in accordance with Rabbi Eliezer in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, who holds that once one directed his heart during the first verse, he no longer needs to do so. If you would think that there is also an objection when one who is walking stops, because it appears that he is acting in accordance with Beit Shammai, how could Rabbi Yochanan say that the entire paragraph is recited while standing? You cannot answer that Rabbi Yochanan follows the opinion that one who acts in accordance with Beit Shammai has fulfilled his obligation, for regarding the paragraph Vehayah Im Shamoa as well, Beit Shammai hold that in the morning one must stand. If so, to satisfy the opinion of Beit Shammai, one would also have to recite Vehayah Im Shamoa while standing. Yet Rabbi Yochanan said this only regarding the first paragraph, because it requires intention, as the Gemara says, “He follows his own reasoning,” etc. Moreover, Rabbi Yochanan implies that the same law applies even in the evening, whereas Beit Shammai hold that one stands specifically in the morning. Rather, it must be that the objection applies only when one who is sitting stands up, etc. I have expanded somewhat upon the meaning that he expressed in brief words.

<b>(במג"א ס"ק א') </b>נראה שר"ל דלכאורה מרמ"א משמע שהטירחא הוא מחמת שכב' שוכב. לזה אמר דבתר"י מבוא' דהטירחא הוא הלבישה. ומ"ש וכן משמע בגמ' פרקדן כו'. ר"ל דבהש"ס שם קאמ' דמיקרי ק"ש כי מצלי אסו' ר"ל שהוא מוטה על צדו וע"ז הוקשו תר"י מהא דזה מחזי' פניו וקורא ותירצו דהתם איכא טירחא ללבוש בגדיו וס"ל להמג"א דוקא טרחא דלבישה אבל טרחא דעמידה לחוד לא דהא הא דפרקדן משמע שכב' שוכב פרקדן דהיינו שהוא מוטל על צדו ואפ"ה אסו' אבל הב"י ס"ל בשיטת הרמב"ם דיש חילוק בין שוכב על צדו ממש דשרי וזה האי דמחזי' פניו וקורא ודאמרינן מיקרי כי מצלי אסו' היינו ששוכב על גבו ומוטה מעט על צדו ולדידיה אין חילוק בין פשט בגדיו או לא:
**** ENGLISH ****
In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: It appears that he means as follows. At first glance, Rama implies that the exertion is due to the fact that he is already lying down. Magen Avraham therefore says that Rabbeinu Yonah explains that the exertion is that of putting on clothing. Regarding his words, “And so it is implied in the Gemara: one who lies supine,” etc., he means that the Talmud there says that reciting Keriat Shema while tilted is forbidden; that is, when he is tilted onto his side. Rabbeinu Yonah challenged this from the case of one who turns his face and recites, and answered that there the exertion of putting on his clothing is involved. Magen Avraham understands that specifically the exertion of dressing is a consideration, but the exertion of standing alone is not, for the case of one lying supine implies that he is already lying supine (that is, resting on his side) and it is nevertheless forbidden. Beit Yosef, however, understands Rambam's position to distinguish between one who is actually lying on his side, which is permitted and is the case of one who turns his face and recites, and the statement that reciting while tilted is forbidden, which refers to one lying on his back and tilting himself slightly onto his side. According to this view, there is no distinction between whether he removed his clothing or not.
