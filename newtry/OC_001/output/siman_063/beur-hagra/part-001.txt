<b>ס"ג היה כו'. </b>דקי"ל כר"מ וכ"פ בתוס' שם ד"ה על וירושלמי כו' וכן הלכה כו' וכן איתא בתנחומא כו' וכ"כ הרי"ף כמו דאמרינן ואזדא ר"י כו' ה"ה רב יהודה ס"ל מצות כוונה כר"א אבל הראב"ד פסק עד על לבבך דוקא דטעמיה משום דלא לשוי עראי כמ"ש בס"ו וכ"כ לעיל סי' נ"ח ס"ג בשם הרשב"א וכ"כ הרשב"א בחי' בשם הרי"צ גאות דבלא"ה ק' דרבא אדרבא מ"מ משם ואילך מותר בהליכה משום דכתיב ובלכתך וצ"ע דא"כ מאי ואזדא ר"י לטעמיה מ"מ הא כתיב ובלכתך ועתוס' שם ט"ז א' ד"ה הא וכ"כ רז"ה דה"ה להא דפ"א דיומא שלא ירמוז כו' אבל הרי"ף וכל הפוסקים פי' בשניהם פ' ראשונה ממש וצ"ל דכ"א כדאיתא וג' חילוקים בדבר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 3, if he was, etc.</b> For we hold like Rabbi Meir, and so Tosafot there, s.v. ‘al,’ ruled, and the Yerushalmi, etc., says, ‘and so is the halacha,’ etc. So too it is found in Tanchuma, etc., and so the Rif wrote, as we say, ‘and Rabbi Yehuda follows his reasoning,’ etc. The same applies: Rav Yehuda holds that the mitzvah requires intent like Rabbi Elazar. But the Raavad ruled that this applies specifically until ‘upon your heart,’ because the reason is so that he not make it casual, as written in seif 6. So too was written above, siman 58, seif 3, in the name of the Rashba, and the Rashba wrote in his novellae in the name of the Ritz Giat, for otherwise there is a difficulty from Rava against Rava. In any event, from there onward it is permitted while walking, because it is written, ‘and when you go.’ This requires study, for if so, what is meant by ‘and Rabbi Yehuda follows his reasoning’? In any event, the verse says, ‘and when you go.’ See Tosafot there, 16a, s.v. ‘ha.’ So too the Razah wrote that the same applies to the statement in the first chapter of Yoma, that one should not gesture, etc. But the Rif and all the poskim explained both cases as referring to the actual first parsha. One must say that each case is as it is, and there are three distinctions in the matter.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 7, if he was engaged, etc.</b> Although in the Gemara they said the reason is intent, and if so the first verse should suffice, and this is also the view of Tosafot there, nevertheless the view of the Rif and the other poskim is that this applies throughout the entire parsha, because Rava accepted Rav Mari’s answer. Although one could reject this and say that Rava accepted it only according to the reasoning of Rabbi Yochanan, who said ‘and he,’ etc., and Rabbi Yochanan follows his own view, and further, that perhaps the Gemara is speaking according to Rabbi Yochanan, according to the version ‘Rabbi Yochanan said,’ etc., nevertheless in the Yerushalmi it is explicit that the same reason applies to them as to the law above in seif 6. For it says there in chapter 2: it was taught, one should not gesture and read; it was taught, workers who were doing work for the homeowner, etc. Rabbi Mana said: this teaches that it is forbidden to perform work at the time one blesses, etc.
