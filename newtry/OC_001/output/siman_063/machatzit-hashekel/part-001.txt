הלביש' כמו שהוכיח מ"א. אע"כ דגם רמ"א ר"ל כששוכב ערום וקיצר בלשון כמ"ש ל"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) And there is, etc.</b> ‘And so it implies in the Gemara, one lying face upward, etc.;’ meaning that even though he is already lying, etc. At first glance this is no proof, for the language of the Rama implies that two things are required: that he actually be lying on his side, and also that there be burden. If so, what proof does he bring from one lying face upward? There, the case is that he only leans somewhat on his side, and therefore it does not help even though there is burden in standing. However, the intent of Magen Avraham is only to clarify the words of Rabbeinu Yonah. The Gemara, page 13, states that Rav Yosef said: one lying face upward may not read Krias Shema. It asks: even without reading Krias Shema it is forbidden to lie face upward, for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who lies face upward. It answers: regarding lying, if he tilts somewhat, it is permitted; but for reading, even in such a case it is forbidden. It asks: but Rabbi Yehuda tilted and read. It answers: Rabbi Yehuda was different, for he was heavyset and there was burden. Rabbeinu Yonah asked from the baraisa on page 24a regarding two who were covered by one tallis while naked: one turns his face and reads Krias Shema, and the other turns, etc. How may he read while lying tilted on his side? Some answer that when they said reading is forbidden even if he tilts, this means a slight tilt, but when he is fully on his side it is permitted. The more correct answer is that since we see that where there is burden it is permitted, as with Rabbi Yehuda because he was heavyset, so too here they permitted him while tilted, because there is burden since he removed his clothing and would have to dress again. From Rabbeinu Yonah’s phrase ‘since he removed his clothing,’ Magen Avraham wrote above that if he lies in his shirt he must stand, for standing involves no burden. To leave no room for one to argue that standing itself is always called burden, and Rabbeinu Yonah mentioned removing clothing only as an added point because in fact the baraisa of two covered with one tallis speaks of naked people, Magen Avraham wrote that the Gemara implies otherwise. According to Rabbeinu Yonah, only dressing is called burden, not standing, for in the second answer of Rabbeinu Yonah they permitted even one who leans only somewhat on his side, as implied by their language. Furthermore, whichever way one understands it, how could Rabbeinu Yonah in the second answer require both that he actually lie on his side and that there be burden? If lying on one’s side is not a matter of arrogance, it should be permitted even without burden; and if even actual lying on the side involves arrogance but they permitted because of burden, then why not permit slight leaning because of burden, since both involve arrogance? Rather, Rabbeinu Yonah’s second answer permits even slight leaning where there is burden. The slight tilt needed is not because of Krias Shema, but because Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who lies face upward. Therefore, since even slight leaning is permitted because of burden, standing cannot be called burden. This proves Magen Avraham’s question from ‘one lying face upward,’ for there too the Gemara must be speaking where he leans somewhat, and nevertheless it was not permitted although there is the burden of standing. Rather, standing is not called burden; only dressing after removing clothing is called burden. If Rama requires both actual lying on the side and burden, even though this is difficult according to Rabbeinu Yonah, one can say that Rama was uncertain which of Rabbeinu Yonah’s two answers is primary, and therefore adopted the stringency of both: actual lying on the side like the first answer, and burden like the second. Since he is stringent for the second answer, he must require that he be naked without a shirt, for according to that answer standing is not called burden, only dressing. Therefore Rama too means that he is lying naked, but abbreviated his wording, as Lechem Chamudos wrote.

<b>מיהו דעת הרב"י כו'</b> ר"ל דלא תימא דבין הרב ב"י ובין הרמ"א ס"ל כתי' ב' של תר"י דההיתר הוא משום טרחא גרידא וא"כ אפי' נוטה רק מעט על צדו שרי וכוונת הרב"י במ"ש והוא שוכב על צדו ר"ל מעט על צדו ובא רמ"א בהג"ה לפרש דבריו מאחר כו' ואיכא טרחא כו' ועז"כ מ"א דע"כ זה ליתא דבשלמא אי הי' אפשר לפרש דהעמיד' גרידא מקרי טרחא אז הי' אפשר לפרש כן דגם הרב"י ס"ל כתי' שני דתר"י הנ"ל אף דסתם הרב"י ולא כ' משום טרחא מה בכך כיון דבאמת איכא עכ"פ טרחא משא"כ השת' לפמ"ש מ"א דהטירחא היא הלביש' ולא הותר כ"א בשוכב ערום א"כ איך א"ל דהרב"י מתיר אפי' בנוט' מעט על צדו. וע"כ אין ההיתר כ"א משום ששוכב ערום והוא לא הזכיר מזה כלום (בשלמא לרמ"א צריך לדחו' במ"ש מאחר שכבר שוכב כו' רמז לזה דאפשר סתם שכיב' היא ערום בלי לבוש. ובאמת כ' הל"ח על רמ"א שנתן מקום לטעות) (ובלא"ה כיון דבחבורו הארוך כת' שמדברי הרמב"ם ורש"י וטור משמע כתי' א' של תר"י מסתמא גם בש"ע פסק כתי' א' דבעינן דוקא שוכב על צדו ממש ואז אפי' ליכא טרחא שרי) ורמ"א שכ' מאחר שכבר כו' משמע דאפי' שוכב על צדו ממש אפ"ה בעי' דוקא היכי דאיכא טרחא דהא קאי על דברי המחבר דמיירי בשוכב על צדו ממש היינו טעמא משום דחושש לחומרת שני תירוצי תר"י כנ"ל (וכן ס"ל להלבוש וע' מ"ש עליו הל"ח) ואע"ג דלהרב"י מותר אפי' ליכא טרחא. אפילו הכי לא כ' רמ"א ל' וי"א דהא לפי זה רמ"א חולק על הרב"י כיון דאפשר לדחוק דגם הרב"י רצה לומר דוקא כששוכב ערום אף דאין האמת כן מ"מ לא כ' ל' י"א. כי כן הוא דרך רמ"א בש"ע כנודע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>However, the view of the Beis Yosef, etc.</b> Meaning, do not say that both the Beis Yosef and the Rama hold like the second answer of Rabbeinu Yonah, that the permission is only because of burden, and if so even leaning only somewhat on his side is permitted; and that when the Beis Yosef wrote ‘and he is lying on his side,’ he means somewhat on his side, while the Rama came in the gloss to explain his words, ‘since,’ etc., and ‘there is burden,’ etc. Magen Avraham writes that this cannot be. If it were possible to explain that standing alone is called burden, one could explain that the Beis Yosef also holds like Rabbeinu Yonah’s second answer, even though the Beis Yosef did not mention burden, since in fact there is at least the burden of standing. But now that Magen Avraham wrote that the burden is dressing, and it was permitted only when he is lying naked, how can one say that the Beis Yosef permits even leaning somewhat on his side? The only basis for permission would be that he is naked, and he did not mention that at all. It is different with the Rama, where one can press his words ‘since he is already lying’ to allude to this, for perhaps ordinary lying is naked without clothing. In truth, Lechem Chamudos wrote that the Rama’s wording leaves room for error. In any case, since in his longer work he wrote that the words of the Rambam, Rashi, and Tur imply the first answer of Rabbeinu Yonah, presumably in the Shulchan Aruch too he ruled like the first answer, that one must actually lie on his side, and then it is permitted even without burden. The Rama, who wrote ‘since he already,’ etc., implies that even where he actually lies on his side, we require specifically that there be burden, for he comments on the Mechaber, who speaks of actually lying on his side. The reason is that he is concerned for the stringencies of both answers of Rabbeinu Yonah, as above, and so holds the Levush; see what Lechem Chamudos wrote against him. Although according to the Beis Yosef it is permitted even without burden, nevertheless Rama did not write ‘there are those who say,’ even though according to this Rama disagrees with the Beis Yosef, because it is possible to press the Beis Yosef’s words and say that he too meant specifically when he is lying naked. Although this is not the truth, he therefore did not write the language of ‘there are those who say.’ This is the way of the Rama in Shulchan Aruch, as is known.

<b>(ס"ק ג) נקרא כו'</b> דמאי טיבותיה כו' כיון דקרא ובקומך כו' ר"ל דמיניה יליף ב"ש דבעי' עמידה דבשכבך ר"ל דרך שכיבה. ובקומך ר"ל דרך קימה אבל ב"ה דרשי קרא ובלכתך בדרך אפי' הילוך. ובשכבך ובקומיך ר"ל על הזמן דהיינו בערב ובבקר דהוי זמן שכיבה וזמן קימה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 3) He is called, etc.</b> For what is his virtue, etc., since the verse says ‘and when you rise,’ etc. Meaning, from that Beis Shammai derive that standing is required: ‘when you lie down’ means in the manner of lying down, and ‘when you rise’ means in the manner of rising. But Beis Hillel expound the verse ‘and when you go on the way,’ even while walking; ‘when you lie down and when you rise’ refers to the time, that is, evening and morning, which are the time of lying down and the time of rising.

<b>אבל לענין בהמ"ז כו'</b> ר"ל דבר"פ אלו דברים פליגי ב"ש וב"ה מי שאכל ושכח ולא בירך בהמ"ז והלך לו ונזכר בש"א יחזור למקומו ויברך בהמ"ז ובה"א יברך במקו' שנזכ' ואמרינן שם דף נ"ג ע"ב רב ב"ח אזיל בשיירתא אכל ושכח ולא בירך ונזכר ואמר היכי איעבד אי אומר לשיירת' ששכחתי לברך יאמרו שאברך פה והוא רצה להחמיר כב"ש א"ל שכחתי יונה דדהבא (וע' שם הטעם למה אמר יונה) וחזר ובירך במקומו ובזכות זה באמת מצא יונה של זהב וכן איתא שם בתלמיד א' שהחמיר כב"ש ומצא ארנקי דדהבא הרי מן השמים הסכימו על ידם שטוב עשו במה שהחמירו כב"ש. עסי' קפ"ד. דיש פוסקים כב"ש לענין בהמ"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But regarding Birkas HaMazon, etc.</b> Meaning, at the beginning of the chapter Elu Devarim, Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel dispute one who ate, forgot to recite Birkas HaMazon, left, and then remembered. Beis Shammai say he returns to his place and recites Birkas HaMazon, while Beis Hillel say he recites it in the place where he remembered. It says there, page 53b, that Rabbah bar bar Chanah was traveling with a caravan; he ate, forgot to bless, and remembered. He said: What shall I do? If I tell the caravan that I forgot to bless, they will say that I should bless here, and he wished to be stringent like Beis Shammai. He told them: I forgot a golden dove, and returned and blessed in his place. Because of this he actually found a golden dove. So too it is stated there regarding a certain student who was stringent like Beis Shammai and found a golden purse. Thus, from Heaven they agreed with them that they did well in being stringent like Beis Shammai. See siman 184, for there are poskim who rule like Beis Shammai regarding Birkas HaMazon.

<b>(Seif katan 4) In the verse, etc.</b> For it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai. Although the prohibition is only when he is sitting and stands when he reads, for then it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai, whereas if he is standing before he begins reading it is permitted to stand during Krias Shema, since it does not appear that he acts like Beis Shammai, here he had already stood because of the first verse. Nevertheless, since he is walking on the road and stops and reads, it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it implies that if he remembered, etc.</b> Since he wrote in seif 8 that he should read the parsha twice, this implies that he had already read the whole parsha, and nevertheless he should again read the whole parsha. The reason is that if he would read only the first verse, it would be like reading out of order, for he first read from ‘And you shall love’ until the end of the parsha and afterward read Shema. Although he had also read Shema Yisrael at first, since he did not have intent when reading the verse Shema, that Krias Shema is considered as nonexistent, and he has read out of order. This is what Magen Avraham teaches, that this is called reading out of order.

<b>(ס"ק ט) לא ירמוז כו'</b> לצורך מצוה כו' הוא מדתנן התם בליל יה"כ היו קוראים לפני כהן גדול דברים הממשיכים את הלב כדי שלא יבא לידי שינה. ותנן ר' זכריה בן קבוטל אומר הרב' פעמים קריתי לפניו בדניאל. ואמרי' מתני לה ר' חנן בר רבא לחייא בר רב קמיה דרב. ר' זכריה בן קפוטל (בפ"א) ומחוו ליה רב בידו קבוטל (בבי"ת) ופריך הש"ס ונימ' ליה להדיא ולמה רמז לו ביד. ומשני רב הוי קרי אז ק"ש ולא הי' אפשר לו להפסיק ופריך א"כ האיך רמז לו ביד הא אסור לרמוז באצבעותיו ובעיניו כו'. ומשני רב הוי עוסק בפר' שני' של ק"ש ומאי קושיא אי לדבר מצוה שרי. הא רב רמז לו לדבר מצוה להצילו מן הטעות אלא ודאי דאפי' לדבר מצוה אסור. אך ראיה גמורה ליכא די"ל שאני התם דלא הוי מצוה ממש. דהא אפשר להמתין עד שיסיים ק"ש ואז יאמר לו שטעה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 9) One should not gesture, etc.</b> ‘For the need of a mitzvah,’ etc. This is from what we learned there: on the night of Yom Kippur they would read before the Kohen Gadol matters that draw the heart, so that he would not come to sleep. It was taught: Rabbi Zecharyah ben Kevutal said, many times I read before him from Daniel. They said that Rabbi Chanan bar Rava taught Chiyya bar Rav before Rav: Rabbi Zecharyah ben Kefutal, with a peh, and Rav showed him with his hand, Kevutal, with a beis. The Gemara asks: let him say it to him explicitly, and why did he gesture with his hand? It answers that Rav was then reading Krias Shema and could not interrupt. It asks: if so, how could he gesture with his hand, since it is forbidden to gesture with his fingers and eyes, etc.? It answers that Rav was engaged in the second parsha of Krias Shema. What is the question? If it is permitted for a mitzvah matter, Rav gestured to him for a mitzvah matter, to save him from an error. Rather, certainly even for a mitzvah matter it is forbidden. However, this is not a complete proof, for one can say that there it was different, since it was not an actual mitzvah, for it was possible to wait until he finished Krias Shema and then tell him that he erred.
