**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> And if it would enter your mind that Rabbi Yehuda holds like Beis Shammai, there is some textual error here. The intent is: how could he stand for the entire parsha? If so, it would appear that he acts like Beis Shammai. Rather, one must say that when walking there is no concern, for everyone understands that he is doing it for intent. So too according to us, even though we do not hold like Rabbi Yehuda and it suffices to stand for the first verse, nevertheless if he stands for the entire parsha, we have no objection, for it does not appear at all that he acts like Beis Shammai, but rather that he acts for intent.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif katan 9.</small> And see the end of siman 66. For there it is explained that for a mitzvah matter, such as putting on tzitzis, one interrupts between the sections. If so, the same applies that it is permitted there to gesture for a mitzvah matter. It is difficult, for if so, in Yoma there, where Rav gestured for a mitzvah matter and the Gemara asked that it is forbidden, and answered that he was standing in the second parsha, why did it not answer that he was between the sections? One can answer that ‘between the sections’ is not called so until ‘Vehayah,’ as written in siman 66, seif 5. Therefore it answered a greater novelty, that even in the middle of ‘Vehayah’ he could gesture. This is clear.
