{11} However, they customarily, etc. The reason is that there are stringent poskim who say that even regarding spices that dissipate flavor it is not permitted to pound them on Yom Tov in their usual way unless he did not know from yesterday that he would need them on Yom Tov; and we are concerned for their words ab initio. However, even they said this only regarding spices; but regarding garlic, onion, and cress, according to everyone he should not pound them from before Yom Tov even if he knew he would need them tomorrow, for they dissipate their flavor entirely and become spoiled. {טז} ובארץ ישראל וכו' - כמה פירושים יש בזה יש מפרשים דאסרו בא"י משום דהיו רגילים להחזיק עבדים ומזלזלי בזה שעושין כדרכן בחול ואומרים בקטנה עשינו לפיכך אסרו חכמים לגמרי וא"כ לדינא בזמנינו תלוי בזה היכי שמחזיקים עבדים כנענים אסור בכל גווני ויש שכתבו דבא"י היו החטים משובחים שאין מתקלקלים ולא נשתנה מראיתם אם יכתשם מעיו"ט ולפיכך לא התירו להם חכמים לכתוש ביו"ט וא"כ ה"ה בכל מקום ומקום שהחטים טובים ואינם נפגמים אם יהיו נידוכים מלפני יו"ט אסור ויש שכתבו דבבבל היו רגילים לאכול תדיר דייסא ומתוך שהיה צרכיהן מרובה לזה לא כתשו במכתשות קטנות וכשכותש ביו"ט בקטנה הוי שינוי משא"כ בא"י שלא היו רגילין הרבה בזה גם בימות החול מצוי שכותשים במכתשת קטנה וליכא שום שינוי ביו"ט ולהכי אסור וה"ה בזמנינו תלוי ג"כ בזה אם אין דרך לכתוש בקטנה בחול שרי ויש להחמיר ככל הפירושים:
**** ENGLISH ****
{16} And in Eretz Yisrael, etc. There are several explanations of this. Some explain that they forbade it in Eretz Yisrael because they were accustomed to keep servants and they were lax about this: they do it in their weekday way and say, we did it in a small [mortar]; therefore the Sages forbade it entirely. And if so, for practical law in our time it depends on this: wherever they keep Canaanite servants it is forbidden in every respect. And some wrote that in Eretz Yisrael the wheat was superior, not spoiled, and its appearance did not change if one pounded it from before Yom Tov; therefore the Sages did not permit them to pound on Yom Tov. And if so, the same law applies in every place where the wheat is good and is not damaged if pounded before Yom Tov: it is forbidden. And some wrote that in Babylon they were accustomed to eat porridge constantly, and since their need for this was great they would not pound in small mortars; and when one pounds on Yom Tov in a small one it is a change; which is not so in Eretz Yisrael, where they were not so accustomed to this, and even on weekdays it is common that they pound in a small mortar, and there is no change on Yom Tov, and therefore it is forbidden. And the same law applies in our time as well: it depends on this: if it is not the practice to pound in a small [mortar] on a weekday, it is permitted. And one should be stringent like all the explanations.
