He answered that the questioner held that at the time of Krias Shema they can be careful not to think improper thoughts, and therefore he understood that what was taught, 'because they are accustomed to impurity,' means an old impurity, for presumably they had already seen keri. If so, he should have said briefly that the reason is because of improper thought.

**** ENGLISH ****
Seif katan 5, obligated, implies that he is obligated immediately. But in Yoreh Deah siman 388 it says after sunrise; this is how it should read. His intent is to challenge the words of Rabbi Beis Yosef against each other. This is a dispute among the Rishonim. In chapter Eilu Megalchin we rule like Rabbi Yehoshua, who says that an avel is forbidden to put on tefillin during the first two days. It answers that the second day is included as permitted, and Rav ruled that if he put them on the second day, even if new visitors came, he does not remove them. The Rif holds that when we say that the second day is included as permitted, although it first said that two days are forbidden, this is because on the second day we say that part of the day is like the whole day. Some say that since night is not a time for tefillin, the night does not count, and part of the second day is required, meaning after sunrise.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That which Rabbi Shimon taught, etc.</b> In Sukkah 25b it is taught: a chasan, his attendants, and all members of the wedding party are exempt from prayer and from tefillin. Rashi explains: from prayer, because it requires concentration; from tefillin, because drunkenness and levity are common. They are obligated in Krias Shema. Rashi explains: because its mitzvah of concentration is only for the first verse, and they can settle their mind briefly to recite the first verse. In the name of Rabbi Sheila they said that the chasan is exempt, but his attendants and all members of the wedding party are obligated. Rashi explains that the chasan is exempt from Krias Shema because he is preoccupied, while the others are obligated, and these Tannaim do not hold that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah. The Rosh wrote as follows: these Tannaim do not hold that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah, unlike the aforementioned Rashi. Rather, the first Tanna obligates them in Krias Shema because its primary concentration is for the first verse and one can easily concentrate and settle his mind for one verse. Rabbi Shimon holds that a chasan cannot concentrate his mind at all. Rabbi Shimon says only that attendants and members of the wedding party are obligated in Krias Shema, but he concedes that they are exempt from prayer and tefillin, not because they are engaged in a mitzvah, for if so they should be exempt even from Krias Shema. Rather, they are not called engaged in a mitzvah, and they are exempt from prayer because due to their joy they cannot concentrate for prayer, since joy and levity are common at a wedding, end quote. The Bach explained his words: when he wrote that it is not because they are engaged, etc., for if so they should be exempt even from Krias Shema, although he wrote above that since the main concentration is for the first verse they can easily concentrate, nevertheless there is a difficulty according to Rabbi Shimon, who exempts a chasan from Krias Shema. If so, if they were called engaged in a mitzvah, they too should be exempt, for when they are reciting Krias Shema and the chasan is not reciting at that time, they cannot rejoice with the chasan. Rather, because of this reason they are not called engaged in a mitzvah, end of his words. I copied all of this, because through it all the words of the Magen Avraham in this seif katan will be understood.

<b>Seif katan 8, and their merchants, etc.</b> This is like one who returns a lost object, etc., even though he receives payment; see there. On that which was taught, 'he may return his lost object,' Rav Ami and Rav Asi dispute. One says this is specifically when the property of the returner is forbidden to the owner of the lost object, since he returns to him something that is his own and this is not called benefit. But when the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, it is forbidden to return it to him, because the owner of the lost object benefits him with the perutah of Rav Yosef, for he does not need to give a perutah to a poor person, since one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from the mitzvah of tzedakah. The other says that even if the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, it is permitted, because the perutos of Rav Yosef are uncommon. It is explained there in the Rosh and Ran that according to the one who says it speaks specifically when the property of the returner is forbidden to the owner of the lost object, the latter clause, which teaches 'in a place where people take payment,' speaks when the returner is an important person and does not want to take the payment from the owner of the lost object; he may not keep it for himself, and therefore it falls to hekdesh. In any event, it is explained there in the sugya that the latter clause, 'and in a place where people take payment,' refers back to the first clause, and it implies that there is no difference between the first and latter clauses except that the payment falls to hekdesh. If so, it should have distinguished in the latter clause and taught a novelty: that in a place where people take payment, even when the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, nevertheless he may return it, because since he receives payment there is no issue of the perutah of Rav Yosef, for he is not considered engaged in a mitzvah. Rather, it must be that even when he takes payment, he is still called engaged in a mitzvah.
