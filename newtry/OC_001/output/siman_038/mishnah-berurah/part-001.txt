**** ENGLISH ****
(16) On the first day: This means even on the day he was buried, which is not the day of death, since it is the first day of mourning and consolation. Therefore, even if he was buried at night, he should not put on tefillin that day. But if he died or was buried on Chol HaMoed, he puts on tefillin both on Chol HaMoed, though the Pri Megadim in siman 71 questions this because in any event the first day is a bitter day for him, and at least he must be careful not to recite a blessing over them, and in any case the custom is not to recite a blessing over tefillin on Chol HaMoed, and after the festival, even though it is the first day of mourning, nevertheless they already comforted him during the festival. So too, if he was buried on the second day of Yom Tov, he should put on tefillin in the Land of Israel, because the second day of Yom Tov counts toward the seven days, and the day after the festival is considered the second day. Similarly, if he heard a recent report, meaning within thirty days, even on the thirtieth day itself, its law is like the day of burial. Therefore, even if he heard it at night, he should not put on tefillin during the day. Even if a recent report came to him after he had already put on tefillin and begun to pray, he removes them. If he heard a distant report, meaning after thirty days, when mourning applies only for one hour, he may put on tefillin, and all the more so he need not remove them. Therefore, if the report came to him in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah or the like, he should not remove the tefillin, but only remove his shoes because of mourning. If the report causes him to cry, he must remove them, as is evident from Yoreh Deah siman 388, seif 2, and siman 402 in Shach, s.k. 2. See below in seif 9.

(לד) מהנחת תפילין - ואע"ג שמחוייב להפסיק מת"ת כדי לקיים כל המצות שחיובן עליו כדאיתא ביו"ד סימן ר"מ שאני מצות תפילין שעיקר תועלתה הוא לתורה כדכתיב ולזכרון בין עיניך למען תהיה תורת ה' בפיך ע"כ מכיון שכבר עוסק בתורה מקודם אין צריך לבטל תורה בשביל זה זולת בשעת ק"ש ותפלה כדי לקבל עליו עול מלכות שמים וגם שאז עדיין אינו עוסק בתורה ומחוייב להיות עליו תפילין. והגר"א פסק בביאורו דדוקא מי שתורתו אומנתו כגון רשב"י וחבריו אבל כגון אנו צריכין להפסיק אף לתפילין:
**** ENGLISH ****
(34) From putting on tefillin: Although one is obligated to interrupt Torah study in order to fulfill all mitzvos whose obligation rests upon him, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 240, the mitzvah of tefillin is different, for its primary benefit is for Torah, as it is written, 'and as a remembrance between your eyes, so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth.' Therefore, since he is already engaged in Torah beforehand, he need not suspend Torah for this, except at the time of Krias Shema and prayer, in order to accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, and also because then he is not yet engaged in Torah and is obligated to have tefillin upon him. The Gra ruled in his Biur that this applies specifically to one whose Torah is his occupation, such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues, but people such as we must interrupt even for tefillin.

(36) Because of his awe: That is to say, it is disrespectful that he uncovers his head before him. According to this, even if his rebbe has already removed his tefillin first, it is forbidden.
