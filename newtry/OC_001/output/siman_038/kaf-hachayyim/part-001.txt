<b>1) [Se'if 1] An intestinally ill person is exempt from tefillin, etc.</b> He is exempt even if he presently feels no pain and walks in the marketplaces and streets. It is forbidden for him to be stringent, except that he may don them during Krias Shema and prayer if he can keep his body clean, as stated in se'if 2. Magen Avraham 1; Soles Belulah 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of those obligated in tefillin 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 10:23. Responsa Rema MiPano 60 implies that even outside Krias Shema and prayer he may wear them at a suitable time and remove them when distress begins. If he was overtaken by circumstances and did not remove the arm-tefillin, he committed no prohibition. Eliyah Rabbah 1. See below, paragraph 5.

<b>3) A person who hears but cannot speak should don tefillin,</b> and so should one who speaks but cannot hear. Sefer Chasidim there, cited by Knesses HaGedolah. Knesses HaGedolah writes that Sefer Chasidim means these people are obligated, whereas the deaf-mute discussed by Chazal (who neither hears nor speaks) is exempt from tefillin because he is exempt from all mitzvos. Nevertheless, if he wishes to don them, he is not protested; Knesses HaGedolah saw several such people in Constantinople wearing tefillin without rabbinic protest. So also write Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah 4; Olas Tamid; Eliyah Rabbah 8; Shaliach Tzibbur, page 40d; and Shesilei Zeitim 2.

<b>10) Rema writes that women who wish to be stringent should be protested.</b> Birkei Yosef agrees. Beis Hillel on Yoreh De'ah 182:2 cites Targum Yonasan on 'A man's garment shall not be upon a woman': tzitzis and tefillin are male ornaments. This implies not merely exemption but prohibition. See Pischei HaDvir II. Shomer Emunim on Ki Seitzei writes that the numerical value of 'kli gever' through Atbash alludes to tefillin, which pertain to the male world; Michal's conduct is no proof. Yafeh LaLev explains that Michal was wise and knew that her soul derived from the male aspect, which is why she had no child. Shaar HaGilgulim, introduction 9, teaches that a woman whose soul is a reincarnated male soul cannot conceive unless, through great merit, an additional female soul enters her by ibbur. See siman 17, paragraph 5.

<b>13) Nevertheless, he is held accountable</b> for failing to do what he should: settle his mind and forcefully repel improper thoughts at all times, especially while wearing tefillin and praying. He bears a double obligation not to mix holy with mundane or impure with pure, like placing excrement in a king's food, which arouses great wrath. God is merciful and does not judge compulsion like willing conduct. Thoughts arise knowingly or unknowingly, and Chazal say no person escapes sinful thoughts every day. No claim is made against one who guards himself, battles to reject them, and exchanges evil thoughts for good ones. The author found that deeply and repeatedly reciting the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy is very helpful. But one who willingly prolongs such thoughts should fear punishment unless he repents. Chesed LaAlafim 2. Compare Kol Yaakov on Shoftim, regarding 'You shall not plant an asherah.'

<b>יג) שם  </b>מוטב שלא להניחם. עיין בשכנה"ג אות ב' שכתב דזו סברה תמוהה הרבה דמפני ההרהור תבטל מצות תפילין וכו', ומהירושלמי שהביא לראיה דמפרש גוף נקי ממחשבה רעה אין ראיה דאפשר לפרש דמיירי שלא להניחם כל היום כאלישע בעל כנפים אבל לפטור ממצות תפילין בשעת ק"ש מפני ההרהור לא עכ"ד, והב"ד הש"ץ דף מ' ע"ד, והרב יפה ללב אות ג', ועי"ש ביפה ללב שתמה על בי"ע שבדיני מי הם החייבים בתפילין אות ה' הביא דברי מרן ומור"ם ז"ל ולא הביא סברת החולקים שהיא סברת השכנה"ג יעו"ש ולא ידעתי מה יש תמיהא בזה דהיא הנותנת דבשביל שסברה זו חולקת על סברת מרן ומור"ם ז"ל לא הביאה, ולענין דינא כבר כתב מרן ז"ל בב"י שצריך לכופם בדברים ולמשוך לבם ליראת שמים כדי שיסיחו לבם מדברי הב"י המזיקים לגוף ולנפש ויפנו לבם לקבל עליהם עול מלכות שמים עכ"ד. ועיין מש"ל אות י"א ואות י"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>14) Knesses HaGedolah 2 calls this opinion astonishing:</b> should the mitzvah of tefillin be canceled because of thoughts? The Yerushalmi's definition of a clean body as clean thoughts may concern wearing tefillin all day like Elisha Baal Kenafayim, not exemption from tefillin during Krias Shema. Shaliach Tzibbur, page 40d; Yafeh LaLev 3. Yafeh LaLev questions why Beis Oved omitted this dissent. There is no difficulty: the dissent contradicts Maran and Rema and therefore was omitted. Beis Yosef's practical counsel is to persuade such people, draw their hearts toward fear of Heaven, remove vanity from them, and have them accept the heavenly yoke. See paragraphs 12-13.
