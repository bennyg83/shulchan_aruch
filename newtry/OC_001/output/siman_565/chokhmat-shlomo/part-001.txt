<b>סעיף ה'  </b>אין היחיד רשאי לומר שלש עשרה מדות וכו'. נ"ב עיין בהרא"ש פ"ק דר"ה מה שביאר מאמר חז"ל אני ה' בתירוץ הב' דקאי על מחשבת ע"ז. הנה מה שדבריו סותרים לדברי הש"ס בקידושין בג' מקומות עיין בחיבורי על או"ח משנת תרי"ב ד"פ ע"ב בתשובתי לק"ק באטישאן קנה מקומה בהלכות ר"ה סי' תר"נ שם ישבתי דברי הרא"ש על נכון בעזה"י ע"ש דרך אגב ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 5.</b> An individual may not say the Thirteen Attributes, etc. Marginal note: See in the Rosh, first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, what he explained regarding the statement of Chazal "I am Hashem," in the second answer, that it refers to the thought of idolatry. Now, regarding how his words contradict the words of the Gemara in Kiddushin in three places, see in my work on Orach Chaim of the year 1852, folio 80b, in my responsum to the holy community of Botoshan; its place was acquired in the laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 650; there I resolved the words of the Rosh properly, with the help of Hashem; see there incidentally, and study carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(There, se'if 6.)</b> One who fasts and publicizes himself to others so as to glory that he is fasting is punished for this. Marginal note: See in the Taz and in Magen Avraham what they discussed at length: if they asked him whether he is fasting, whether he should tell the truth or say a falsehood. And according to my humble opinion it appears that it is evident from the Gemara and from Rashi's commentary that even when they ask him he should lie. And this is from what we learn in tractate Sukkah, folio 49b: "and walk modestly with your God": this is carrying out the dead and bringing a bride into the chuppah. And these matters are an a fortiori: just as these matters, whose manner is to be done in public, the Torah said "and walk modestly," then matters whose manner is to be done in private, all the more so. And see Rashi there, s.v. "carrying out the dead," etc.: and some say, if he needs to spend for a poor dead person or for bringing in a poor bride, he should do it privately, and he should not say before someone who does not know that he did it. And afterward, s.v. "matters whose manner is to be done privately": such as charity given to a poor person in secret; which is not so regarding a gift for a bride, where others are involved who know from whom it is. And that is what R' Elazar said, that all matters require modesty, end quote. And according to Rashi, the reason of the a fortiori is at first not understood: what does it matter whether their manner is to be done in public or in private; ultimately, one who knows knows, and one who does not know does not know. But necessarily the intent is as follows: just as these, whose manner is to be done in public, and regarding them certainly many ask him who is doing so and who volunteered to do so, and even so the Torah said that even if they ask he should lie before them that he is not doing it, and he should do it privately; and even if people already knew from elsewhere that he is the one doing it, he should lie before them that it is not he; all the more so regarding matters whose manner is to be done privately, where askers will not multiply upon him, it is obvious that he should do it privately and not begin of his own accord to publicize himself that he is doing so. And with this the a fortiori comes out very properly: just as the Torah permitted lying even to those who begin with him by asking, all the more so that without being asked he should not glory in himself with this. And if so, it appears that it is permitted even to lie in the matter, since his intent is for the sake of Heaven, and it is a transgression for its own sake, and better than it, as it appears to me correctly according to my humble opinion in the explanation of this sugya; and study carefully. And now, both the Taz and Magen Avraham proved that when it is merely by way of a question it is permitted to answer the truth and he should not lie, from the fact that he first answered "I am sitting in a fast," etc.; see there. And I saw in the newly printed book Yaalas Chen on the Torah, on Parashas Beshalach, that he wrote to reject this proof because of the difficulty: why does it conclude "and R' Yehudah said, even on Shabbos"; and also it is difficult how at first they asked him, "Did not the master say that a person lends his fast and repays," perhaps he accepted upon himself this particular day, such that he cannot lend his fast; and necessarily the case is that it was on Shabbos, and therefore from the outset they sensed that they were on Shabbos; see there. And his words are puzzling, for the Magen Avraham and Taz were precise in their wording to prove from what he said afterward, "I am sitting as a Torah scholar," which implies that at first they did not know that he was a Torah scholar; and they are correct in this, for otherwise how did he say afterward "I am sitting as a Torah scholar," when they already knew from the outset that he was a Torah scholar? He would only have needed to say that he said, etc., "a Torah scholar is fine," etc., "like fire," etc. And necessarily, from what he answered afterward "I am sitting as a Torah scholar," it is implied that until then they did not know that he was a Torah scholar, and necessarily it was not on Shabbos. And as to what he raised as a difficulty, how they asked him "a person lends his fast and repays," perhaps he vowed this day: one may answer as follows. According to his view that the fast he lent was because of his vow, it would have been possible that he ask for release from his vow, and his opening would be that he did not vow with the intention of [being released before] a great man, as is written in Nedarim; and if so he could have asked him to seek release from his vow. But since we hold that we do not ask for release from a vow of a mitzvah except in a case of pressure, therefore he could not ask him to seek release from his vow in that way, for it is a vow of a mitzvah and it is not proper to ask about it. And therefore he said to him that in truth it is possible to ask about it; and even though it was a vow of a mitzvah, he can fast another day, and as to specifically fasting this day, that is not within the category of a vow of a mitzvah; and therefore release would have been permitted to him. Only it would have been possible to say that this too is a nullification of a mitzvah, for perhaps tomorrow he will die and will not fulfill his vow, and it is a vow of a mitzvah and he has no release, as we are concerned in the first chapter of tractate Nedarim lest he die. And therefore he brings him a proof from what we say, that a person lends his fast and repays, and it is evident that we are not concerned in this about perhaps he will die; and if so, it is again possible for him to ask for release from his vow that he vowed for this day, and this is not a nullification of a mitzvah, since he can fulfill it and fast afterward, and for this day he is not commanded; and therefore he was forced to answer them afterward that he is a Torah scholar. But now, regarding the matter that a fast is beneficial for a dream, one may say two intents here: either that the fast is a remedy for the dream, or the opposite, that eating is something that strengthens the decree of the dream, but when he does not eat, the dream is nullified of itself. And there is a practical difference if he eats a meal of a mitzvah: if we say that the fast is beneficial for the dream, then even if he eats a meal of a mitzvah, it is in any case not considered like the fast, and therefore a fast specifically is required; but if we say that optional eating harms the dream, then if he eats a meal of a mitzvah he is not harmed. And therefore without this it would have been possible to say that the benefit of the fast is only that eating not harm him; and if so, regarding a meal of a mitzvah he would have no concern of harm. And since if he hosts guests it is a meal of a mitzvah, as is written in Orach Chaim, siman 333; and even though it is implied from Magen Avraham that specifically for the host it is a mitzvah and not for the guests, behold from the words of the Taz there it is not so implied; also in siman 568 in Magen Avraham it is implied that a meal in which a Torah scholar dwells is a meal of a mitzvah; and in the laws of Chanukah it is clarified that if he says songs and praises it is a meal of a mitzvah; and if so it was in their hand to make this into a meal of a mitzvah, and therefore he would not need to fast; and therefore he brought him a proof from what Rav Yosef said, even on Shabbos, and there is no meal of a mitzvah greater than this, and even so the fasting is better than it; and therefore all the more so regarding other meals of a mitzvah, that they do not avail, and only a fast specifically is required; and therefore he cannot lend his fast and repay. And thus the words of the Taz and Magen Avraham are fine, and the difficulty of Yaalas Chen is resolved; and study well carefully.
