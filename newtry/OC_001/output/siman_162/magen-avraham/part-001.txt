<b>הנוטל ידיו וכו'</b>. כתוב בתורת הבית בית ג' אם שפך על ידו אחת שטיפה אחת גדול' ויש בה כדי ב' שטיפות הרי זה עולה לשתים ואינו צריך מים שניים אבל שפך על ב' ידיו שטיפה א' גדולה אינה טהורה אא"כ יש בו רביעית ובלא"ה לא מהני אפי' אתי משיורי טהרה וכ"כ ב"י סי' ק"ס בשם התו' והרא"ש וסמ"ק והר"ש וכ"כ התו' בגיטין פ"ב ובת"ה הקצר השמיט זה וכ' בפ"ב כדעת הראב"ד בשם הירוש' דאף ברביעי' צריך ב"פ ע"ש ובב"י וע' ברמב"ם פ"ז דין י':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who washes his hands, etc.</b> It is written in Toras HaBayis, bayis 3: if one poured over one hand one large pouring that contains enough for two pourings, this counts as two, and he does not need second waters. But if he poured over both hands one large pouring, it is not pure unless it contains a reviis, and otherwise it does not help even if it comes from leftover pure water. So too the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 160 in the name of Tosafot, the Rosh, the Semak, and the Rash, and so too Tosafot wrote in the second chapter of Gittin. In Toras HaBayis HaKatzar he omitted this, and in the second chapter he wrote like the opinion of the Raavad in the name of the Yerushalmi, that even with a reviis it requires two times; see there and in the Beit Yosef. See also Rambam, chapter 7, law 10.

With this, the matter is resolved: why, in the question of Ilfa, does he not bring the Mishnah that one does not add? Rather, the Mishnah is speaking of the initial law, where it is forbidden even when it is moist, whereas Rabbi Yannai is speaking after the fact, and the question of Ilfa is also after the fact. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he should pour for him.</b> Meaning, when he washes both hands together, he may rub them, as stated in seif 5. And if you ask: let him pour over each hand twice and afterward rub them. One may answer that he must rub during the first pouring in order to wash away the sweat properly; see the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba. The Bach wrote in the name of the Maharshal that when one pours over one hand and raises that hand, and the water goes beyond the joint, then when he goes back and takes the vessel with that hand to pour over the second hand, he will need to lower the hand that he washed first, and the water will return and render the hands impure, as written in seif 1. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Over the second.</b> If one touched, with an impure hand, one hand that had already received the second waters, he need not dry it; rather, he afterward puts water upon it, for since it had already become entirely pure, even though it became impure from the other hand, the second waters help. Yam Shel Shlomo, seif 33; see the end of seif 7. A vessel that has a hole in its bottom and a stopper inserted into it, and he presses it upward so that the water emerges onto his hand, is permitted. Therefore, he should wash with a reviis at once.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or any other matter.</b> This implies that he comes to include even something that is from the original creation of the water, unlike Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. But in siman 160, seif 10, it implies that the halacha follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Beit Yosef. And in Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 52, he ruled that although one may immerse in it, nevertheless it reduces the measure with respect to the tube of the waterskin. The Tosafot Yom Tov disagreed with him and wrote that according to the Rosh and the Tur, one may neither immerse in it nor wash with it, and it reduces the measure; and it is also impure if found on the hand. See there, sixth chapter of Mikvaos. To resolve the opinion of the Rav Beit Yosef, one must say that the ruling that one may immerse in small water creatures applies specifically when he crushed them, for they are no better than snow. Even according to the one who disagrees regarding snow, he agrees here that if he did not crush them, it is not called immersion at all. Therefore, if he did not crush them, they reduce the measure, and it is possible that they also render the hand impure. The same applies to snow and hail when they were not crushed and are found on the hand: it is impure. So one should rule.

<b>ורטיה עליה</b>. קשה דהא רטיה חוצצת כמ"ש סי' קס"א וי"ל דהכא מיירי כשאינו מקפיד עלייהו (ל"ח) וב"ח תי' דהכ' מיירי שיש מכה בידו וכמ"ש הרא"ש וז"ל אף על פי שאסור לאכול במפה גזרה שמא יגע שאני הכא דליכא למיחש שיגע בבשרו שתחת הרטיה שאין מסירו מחמת כאב המכה ע"כ ולעיל מיירי שיש לו מיחוש בעלמא ויש לחוש שיסיר הרטי' ויגע באוכלין וצריך ליטול תחת הרטי' ואיכא חציצה עכ"ל וכן נרא' עיקר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is a bandage upon it.</b> This is difficult, for a bandage interposes, as written in siman 161. One may answer that here it is speaking where he is not particular about it. Lechem Chamudos. The Bach answered that here it is speaking where there is a wound on his hand, as the Rosh wrote, and this is his language: Although it is forbidden to eat with a cloth, by decree lest he touch, here it is different, for there is no concern that he will touch the flesh beneath the bandage, since he does not remove it because of the pain of the wound. Until here. Earlier, however, it is speaking where he has a mere discomfort, and there is concern that he may remove the bandage and touch the food; therefore he must wash beneath the bandage, and there is an interposition. Until here is his language. This appears to be the primary explanation.
