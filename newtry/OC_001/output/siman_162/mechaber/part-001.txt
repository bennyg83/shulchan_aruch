**** ENGLISH ****
Raising and rubbing the hands during hand washing. Containing 10 paragraphs: One who washes their hands must raise them upward so that the water does not flow past the wrist joint and return to defile the hands. {Rama: (meaning the fingertips) Gloss: The same applies if one lowers them from the start of washing to the end — that is also valid — but one must be careful not to first raise the fingertips and then lower them, because then the water would flow past the wrist and return to defile the hands (Beit Yosef in the name of Maharai ibn Haviv). This applies when one does not wash the entire hand up to where the hand meets the forearm, but if one washes up to that point, there is no need to raise the hands (and some disagree). Similarly, if one poured a revi'it (a specific measure) over both hands at once, since there are no impure waters at all, there is no need to raise the hands. Similarly, one who immerses their hands need not raise them. (Some say that if one pours three times there is no need to be careful about all this, and this is the lenient custom) (Agur in the name of Or Zarua and Hagahot Asheiri, ch. 25.)}

**** ENGLISH ****
If one poured water on one hand and rubbed it against the other, the washing is invalid — even if afterward one poured water over both hands — because the water poured on one hand became impure, and when rubbed against the other hand, it defiled it. The second waters only purify water made impure by the hand itself, not water that came from the other hand. Therefore, one who poured from a vessel onto one hand and rubbed it against the other must dry their hands and wash again properly. Thus, it is preferable that someone else pour water for you; if there is no one, hold the vessel with your fingertips and pour over both hands at once, or pour a revi'it over each hand separately before rubbing — because pouring a revi'it at once is like immersion and does not require a second washing. If someone who has not washed their hands touches your hands while they are still wet, you must dry them and wash again, because they have been defiled through the water on them. Therefore, when pouring the third water (which serves as the "second" beyond the first), take care that one hand does not touch the other until the water has been poured on the second hand as well, or pour over both simultaneously. If one used a full revi'it for each hand, none of this is necessary.

One who has a wound on their hand with a bandage it is sufficient to wash the rest of the hand that is not covered by the bandage, and one must take care not to touch the bandage, lest the water on it return and defile the hand. Alternatively, one may pour a full revi'it over the hand at once, in which case the waters are not impure. {Rama: (ungüento in the vernacular) [One pours the first waters whether over a vessel or over the ground. — Tur]}
