<b>(ס"ק א) מסופק כו'</b> בסי' קי"ד. מבואר שם דבתפלת מוסף של י"ט אחרון של חג מתחילין לו' משיב הרוח. ואם נסתפק אם אמרו תוך שלשים יום סמוך לחג בחזקת שלא אמרו וצריך לחזור ולהתפלל. ולאחר שלשים יום כבר נתרגל לאומרו. וחזקה שאמר שעל הרגל לשונו הולך. וכתב שם סוף הסי' דאם אמר בי"ט אחרון צ' פעמים אתה גבור עד מוריד הגשם. הוי מכאן ואילך בחזקת שאמר. דהא בשלשים יום יש צ' תפלות. א"כ אם אמרו צ' פעמים נתרגל בפעם א' כאלו אמרו שלשים יום:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 1) Uncertain, etc.</b> In siman 114. It is explained there that in Musaf of the last day of Yom Tov of the festival they begin to say "mashiv haruach." And if he is uncertain whether he said it within thirty days adjacent to the festival, the presumption is that he did not say it, and he must return and pray. And after thirty days he has already become accustomed to saying it, and the presumption is that he said it, for a person follows the habit of his tongue. And he wrote there at the end of the siman that if on the last day of Yom Tov he said ninety times "Atah gibor" until "morid hageshem," from then on the presumption is that he said it; for in thirty days there are ninety prayers; if so, if he said it ninety times he becomes accustomed in one instance as if they said it for thirty days.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In the name of Abudirham.</b> Who disagrees with the aforementioned law. And the reason is that it is impossible that he begin from the beginning of the Amidah of eighteen and say it ninety times until after "mashiv haruach," because of a blessing in vain. And if he begins only before "mashiv haruach" and says it ninety times, then afterward when he begins to pray from the beginning of the Amidah he will follow his habit and not say "mashiv haruach." And granted that we do not hold like Abudirham in that matter, since there is no change in his saying it ninety times (whether he prays from the beginning of the Amidah or from the beginning of "Atah gibor," there is no change adjacent to "mashiv haruach"). But here everyone agrees that it does not help, since there is a change immediately adjacent to "HaMelekh HaKadosh," which he mentions in prayer with "Blessed are You, Hashem."

**** ENGLISH ****

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 3) To stand erect, etc.</b> Before he says "Blessed," for the reason of standing erect is that we hold it is forbidden to bow at the end of every blessing (but in the middle of a blessing it is permitted), as above in siman 113. And the reason is not to add upon the enactments of the Sages, who enacted to bow in Avos at the beginning and end and in Hoda'ah at the beginning and end; and the Sages enacted to bow when he says "Blessed," as written in siman 113: he bows at "Blessed" and stands erect at the Name. Therefore in another blessing aside from Avos and Hoda'ah he must stand erect before he says "Blessed," for there the enactments of the Sages are to bow in Avos and Hoda'ah. And the Taz wrote in siman 113 that in all the blessings it is enough if he stands erect before he says "Blessed"; but in Zochreinu and the like, where he must bow by virtue of the enactments of the Sages in that blessing, he must stand erect a few words before "Blessed," so that afterward when he bows at the end of the blessing it be recognizable that it is by virtue of the enactments of the Sages; and so wrote the Taz here. And Magen Avraham above wrote in the name of Agudah that in a place where one must bow by the enactment, he must stand erect beforehand as above, and afterward when he says "Blessed" he should bow, and when he mentions the Name he should stand erect, and afterward he should bow again.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>היינו לשנוי' קמא כו'</b> דבע"ז דף ד' איתא א"ר יוסף לא לצלי אינש מוסף בג' שעות הראשונות דר"ה אלא בצבור דאין תפלתם נדחית. אבל לא ביחיד. דלמא מפקיד דיניה ומעייני' בעובדיה וידחה תפלתו. וס"ד משום דבג' ראשונות יושב ודן ופריך והאמרי' ג' ראשונות יושב ועוסק בתורה ובג' שניות יושב ודן ומשני איפוך בג' ראשונות דן ובשניות עוסק בתורה ואב"א לעולם לא תיפוך ובג' ראשונות עושק בתורה אפ"ה לא יתפלל אז מוסף ביחיד. משום דתורה כתיב בה אמת דהיינו כפי שורת הדין. לכן אין הקב"ה נכנס לפנים משורת הדין. ע"כ דברי הגמ'. והתוספות דף ג' ע"ב כתב די"א דמה"ט אומרים במוסף ממקומו הוא יפן כו' משום דקי"ל בשניות יושב ודן. וע"ז כתבו ואין נראה דלמאי דמסקינן דבג' ראשונות יושב ודן. וזה לא יתכן. וע"כ מטבע תפלה כך הוא עכ"ל. אבל אנן קי"ל כשנויא בתרא דלתי' בתרא א"צ לומר איפוך והכי עדיף:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That is, according to the first answer, etc.</b> For in Avodah Zarah folio 4 it is stated: R' Yosef said, a person should not pray Musaf in the first three hours of Rosh Hashana except with the congregation, whose prayer is not pushed aside; but not as an individual, lest his judgment be visited and they examine his deeds and his prayer be pushed aside. And one might have thought that this is because in the first three hours He sits and judges; and it objects: but we say that in the first three hours He sits and engages in Torah, and in the second three hours He sits and judges; and it answers: reverse it: in the first three hours He judges and in the second He engages in Torah; and if you wish, say: never reverse it, and in the first three hours He engages in Torah, even so one should not then pray Musaf as an individual, because regarding Torah it is written "truth," namely according to the strict line of the law; therefore the Holy One, blessed be He, does not enter beyond the line of the law. Thus far the words of the Gemara. And Tosafos on folio 3b wrote that some say that for this reason they say in Musaf "From His place He will turn," etc., because we hold that in the second hours He sits and judges. And upon this they wrote: and it does not appear so, for according to what we conclude, that in the first three hours He sits and judges; and this would not fit. And necessarily the fixed wording of the prayer is thus, end quote. But we hold like the latter answer, for according to the latter answer one need not say "reverse it," and that is preferable.
