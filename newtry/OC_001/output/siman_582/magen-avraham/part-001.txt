**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He need not repeat.</b> And this requires further study, for he should have explained that this is specifically when he knew that these are the Days of Repentance and erred in his speech; but if he did not remember that they are the Days of Repentance and remembered afterward, he has not fulfilled his obligation, as written in siman 487 and siman 209. <b>אין מחזירין</b>. ואפי' רצה להחזיר אסור בת"ה סי' קמ"ד ועי' סי' ק"ז (ד"מ), זכרנו כתבנו בחטף קמ"ץ, בזכרנו לא יאמר לחיים טובי' רק לחיים עד וכתו' לחיים טובים כל וכו' (לבוש) אבל י"א שגם בוכתוב א"א טובי' עד בספר (מט"מ), לחיים הלמ"ד בשב"א ולא בפת"ח דלא לשתמע לא חיים ודוקא בר"ה קפדי' אבל בכל השנ' אומרים השכיבנו לחיים בפת"ח תחת הלמ"ד ע"פ דקדוק דכשהשב"א באה בתחיל' התיב' באותיות שאינן אח"הע קורין בפת"ח (ב"ח מט"מ) ועמ"ש סי' ח', שהשלטן בקמץ תחת שי"ן שני, ואין לומ' באהב' מקרא קדש רק יום תרועה מקרא קדש, ודברך אמת ואין לומר מלכנו, וכופלין לעילא בכל הקדישים שאומרים (כ"ז במנהגים ולבוש וב"ח):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They do not make him return.</b> And even if he wishes to return, it is forbidden, in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 144; and see siman 107 (Darkei Moshe). Zochreinu and Kosveinu with a chataf-kamatz. In Zochreinu one should not say "lechaim tovim," only "lechaim," until "and write for good life," all etc. (Levush); but some say that also in "and write" we do not say "tovim" until "in the book" (Mateh Moshe). "Lechaim": the lamed with a sheva and not with a patach, so that it not be heard as "not life"; and specifically on Rosh Hashana we are particular, but throughout the year we say in Hashkiveinu "lechaim" with a patach under the lamed according to grammar, for when the sheva comes at the beginning of the word with letters that are not alef-hei-ches-ayin, they read it with a patach (Bach; Mateh Moshe); and see what I wrote in siman 8. "Shehasultan" with a kamatz under the second shin. And one should not say "be'ahava mikra kodesh," but rather "yom teruah mikra kodesh." And "udevarecha emes," and one should not say "malkenu." And they double "le'eila" in all the Kaddishes that they say. (All this in the Minhagim, Levush, and Bach.)
