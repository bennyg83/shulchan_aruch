**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> This is an explicit Mishnah in the third chapter of Kerisus, 12b, that the measure for drinking is like the measure for eating, in the time of eating a peras. So too it is in the baraisa in the Gemara there, 13a. The words of the Rambam are a great error. Perhaps one can say that the Rambam explains that Rabbi Eliezer, who disagrees in Kerisus there and says, "if he paused in it," or "if he put, etc.," has one reason for both: since he put a little water in it, he did not drink a revi'is within the time of a revi'is; and we rule like Rabbi Eliezer, as stated there, 13b. He holds that the same applies everywhere. But this is not so, for specifically there it is because it is written "and strong drink," as stated there.

<b>מותר ליגע כו'. </b>יומא ע"ז ב' דלא כי"מ שדחקו שם בתינוק קטן שאין יכול ליטלו בעצמו והביאו ראיה מפ"ק דפסחים דאף חכמים ל"פ אלא משום דהוא עצמו מחזר כו' שאני התם שמותר בשאר דברים לא בדיל ועתוס' דע"א ו' ב' ד"ה מנין כו' דלא אסור אלא בנזיר דכ"ע שתי חמרא כו' וכמ"ש ביומא י"ד א'. ועודנ"ל דוקא בנזיר משום דסחור סחור לכרמא כו' והוא מבקש שיתן לו מסתמא ליושתי קבעי כמ"ש תוס' שם אבל אם נדור משאר דברים מותר ליתנו לו כ"ז שלא אמר בפירוש שיאכלנו וכמ"ש בתוספתא ספ"ב דדמאי ע"ה שאמר לחבר תן לי ככר זה ואוכלנו יין זה ואשתנו לא יתן לו שאין מאכילין טהרות לע"ה היה נדור מן הככר וא"ל תן לי ואוכלנו אבטיח וניקר וא"ל תן לי ואוכלנו יין ונתגלה וא"ל תן לי ואשתנו לא יתן שאין מאכילין את האדם דבר האסור לו כיוצא בו לא יושיט ישראל אבר מן החי לב"נ ולא כוס יין לנזיר שאין מאכילין את האדם דבר האסור לו אלמא נקט בכולם ואוכלנו חוץ מאלו שנים. והרשב"א והר"ן ומ"מ כתבו ב' טעמים על קניבת ירק דאסור קודם מנחה א' משום דלמא אתי למיכל אבל מן המנחה ולמעלה דומה כמי שיש לו פת בסלו ולפ"ז אסור כאן ועוד טעם אחר כ' משום דקודם המנחה נראה כמתקן לצורך היום ואע"ג דבירושלמי דפ"ד דפסחים הל' ד' הנ"ל משמע כטעם הראשון שם שאמרו שע"י שהוא דבר קל כו' ליתא דהיינו לר"א שם אבל לפי מאי דקי"ל כרב כמ"ש בסי' תרי"א ס"ב א"צ לזה אלא טעמא משום סכנה כקושיית הירושלמי הנ"ל וכמו שאסרו בכל שבת בתוספתא הנ"ל וקי"ל כרב כנ"ל ואע"פ שטעם של ר"א לכאורה ברור כיון שהטעם משום סכנה מה לי בחול כו' מ"מ הא מסיק שם דאסמכוה אקרא שבתון שבות וכן בגמ' שלנו משמע דלאו משום טעם הראשון ועמ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is permitted to touch, etc.</b> Yoma 77b, unlike some who explain by pressing it into a case of a small child who cannot take it himself. They brought proof from the first chapter of Pesachim, that even the Sages disagree only because he himself goes after it, etc. There it is different, for since other things are permitted, he is not separated from them. See Tosafos, Avodah Zarah 6b, comment beginning "from where," that it is forbidden only with a nazir because everyone drinks wine, etc., as written in Yoma 14a. It further seems to me that specifically with a nazir this is so because of "go around, go around, do not approach the vineyard," and because he asks that one give it to him, presumably he wants it to drink, as Tosafos wrote there. But if he is vowed away from other things, it is permitted to give them to him as long as he did not say explicitly that he will eat them, as written in the Tosefta at the end of chapter 2 of Demai: if an am ha'aretz said to a chaver, give me this loaf and I will eat it, this wine and I will drink it, he may not give it to him, for one does not feed pure foods to an am ha'aretz. If he was vowed from the loaf and said to him, give it to me and I will eat it; a watermelon was pecked and he said to him, give it to me and I will eat it; wine was exposed and he said to him, give it to me and I will drink it, he may not give it, for one does not feed a person something forbidden to him. Similarly, an Israelite may not hand a limb from a living animal to a Noahide, nor a cup of wine to a nazir, for one does not feed a person something forbidden to him. Thus in all of them it states "and I will eat it," except for these two. The Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh wrote two reasons why cutting vegetables is forbidden before Minchah. One reason is lest he come to eat, whereas from Minchah onward it is like one who has bread in his basket; according to this, it would be forbidden here. They wrote another reason, because before Minchah it appears as though he is preparing for the needs of the day. Although in the Yerushalmi, chapter 4 of Pesachim, halachah 4, mentioned above, it implies like the first reason, where they said that since it is a light matter, etc., this is not so, for that is according to Rabbi Eliezer there. But according to what we rule like Rav, as written in siman 611, se'if 2, this is unnecessary; rather, the reason is danger, as in the question of the Yerushalmi mentioned above, and just as they forbade it on every Shabbos in the Tosefta mentioned above. We rule like Rav, as above. And even though Rabbi Eliezer's reason appears clear at first glance, since the reason is danger, what difference is there between weekday, etc.? Nevertheless, it concludes there that they supported it on a verse, "Shabbason," meaning resting. So too our Gemara implies that it is not because of the first reason. See Magen Avraham.
