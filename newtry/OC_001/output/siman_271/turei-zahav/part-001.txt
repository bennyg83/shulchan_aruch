**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And they discharge men, etc.</b> Although in siman 689 the Shulchan Aruch ruled like Behag that women do not discharge men in reading the Megillah, even though they are obligated in reading the Megillah, that is not comparable to here. Regarding Megillah there are opinions that women should not bless over reading the Megillah but over hearing the Megillah, as the Beit Yosef wrote there; therefore it is certainly not proper initially for them to discharge men. Maharshal and my teacher, the Bach, ruled here too that they do not discharge, as with Megillah, but this is not compelling at all.

<b>בלא ברכת יין. </b> דכיון דבירך כבר עליו אין הקידוש מפסיק לומ' שיברך שנית (ובטור הביא חד דיעה דצריך לברך על היין ותמה ב"י שזה לא נמצא כלל  ובסמוך יתבאר בדברינו שהוא דעת הראב"ד) אלא דבברכת המוציא מביא כאן הפלוגתא שמביא הטור וטעם הדיעה הא' שצריך לברך המוציא ועל היין ל"צ כ' הרא"ש וז"ל דודאי איסור  האכילה גורם לקידוש דהוי הפסק להצריך המוציא אבל בורא פרי הגפן שהוא קודם הקידוש בשביל איסור שתיה לא הוי הפסק עכ"ל נראה פי' דבריו דאע"ג דשתיה אסורה כמו אכילה מ"מ תרתי בעי' דהיינו שיש איסור לאכול וגם עביד מעשה הפסק דעשה קידוש משא"כ ביין דאין בו אלא איסור לחוד ולא שום מעשה שהרי בשעה שיש לו לברך בפה"ג מחמת הקידוש עדיין לא אמר הקידוש ע"כ אותו הכוס שמקדש עליו א"צ לברכת היין מה שא"כ בברכת הלחם שזמנו אחר קידוש וס"ל להרא"ש דכאן נחשב הקידוש גמר וסילוק על האכילה שאכל כבר כיון שרובץ עליו האיסור לאכול עד שיעשה קידוש ול"ד למ"ש הרא"ש סי' קע"ח דתפילה לא הוי הפסק באכיל' כיון דלא הוה גמר וסילוק כמו ב"ה דהכא ודאי יש איסור כשהגיע העת של הקידוש והוא מוכרח לעשות מעשה חדש לומר הקידוש מה שא"כ בתפלה אף שעושה מעשה חדש מ"מ אין איסור אכילה בעת ההיא באותה שעה ממש שמתפלל כי ודאי לא מיירי שממתין עד סמוך לכלות היום שלא יהיה לו אפשרות לאכול מעט ע"כ לא הוה גמר וסילוק. והי"א ס"ל דגם הקידוש לא הוי הפסק לחזור ולברך המוציא דכאן לא נאסר עליו האכילה אלא מצד שיש עליו מונע מצד אחר דרמיא עליה לעשות קידוש וכל שהלך המונע חוזר לענינו הראשון וזה דומה למי שנשתתק בשעת אכילה ח"ו ונתרפא תכף ודאי א"צ לחזור ולעשות המוציא וע"כ לא הוה גמר וסילוק על אכילה ראשונה כנ"ל טעם נכון לאלו הדיעות וכיון דספק ברכות להקל ודאי לא יברך גם המוציא ועמ"ש בסי' רצ"ט בס"ד ופשוט דאין צריך להתפלל ערבית אם הוא פורס מפה ומקדש כיון דהתחיל לאכול בהיתר ממש כדין כל תפלת ערבית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Without the blessing over wine.</b> Since he already blessed over it, kiddush does not interrupt so as to require blessing again. In the Tur he brought one opinion that one must bless over the wine, and the Beit Yosef wondered that this is not found at all. Nearby it will be explained in our words that this is the view of the Raavad. Regarding HaMotzi, however, he brings here the dispute cited by the Tur. The reason of the first opinion, that one must bless HaMotzi while over wine he need not, was written by the Rosh: certainly the prohibition of eating causes kiddush to be an interruption requiring HaMotzi, but Borei peri hagefen, which precedes kiddush, is because of the prohibition of drinking and is not an interruption. The meaning appears to be that although drinking is forbidden like eating, two factors are needed: a prohibition to eat and an act of interruption, namely making kiddush. Regarding wine, there is only the prohibition and no act yet, for when he must bless Borei peri hagefen because of kiddush, he has not yet said kiddush. Therefore the cup over which he recites kiddush does not require a wine blessing. This is not so with the blessing over bread, whose time is after kiddush. The Rosh holds that here kiddush is considered completion and withdrawal from the eating he already ate, since the prohibition to eat rests upon him until he performs kiddush. This is unlike what the Rosh wrote in siman 178, that prayer is not an interruption in eating because it is not completion and withdrawal like Birkas HaMazon. Here there is certainly a prohibition when the time of kiddush arrives, and he is forced to perform a new act, saying kiddush. Prayer is different: although he performs a new act, there is no prohibition of eating at that very moment when he prays, for it certainly does not speak of one who waits until near the end of the day when he has no possibility to eat a little. Therefore it is not completion and withdrawal. The second opinion holds that kiddush too is not an interruption requiring a new HaMotzi, for here he is not forbidden to eat except because of another preventing obligation placed upon him, namely to perform kiddush; once the impediment is removed, he returns to his first matter. This is like one who became mute during eating, Heaven forbid, and was immediately healed; he certainly need not recite HaMotzi again. Therefore it is not completion and withdrawal from the first eating. This appears to me a correct reason for these opinions. Since in doubt regarding blessings we are lenient, he certainly should not bless HaMotzi either. See what I wrote in siman 299 with Heaven's help. It is obvious that he need not pray Maariv if he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush, since he began eating in complete permissibility, as is the law of every Maariv prayer.

<b>שנים שהיו שותים כו' </b> ז"ל הרמב"ם פ"ד דברכות וסיים ע"ז אבל אם אמרו בואו ונבדיל א"צ לחזור ולברך וכתב הראב"ד ע"ז ערבובי דברים יש כאן שהפסקת הקידוש אע"ג דלא אמרו בואו ונקדש כיון שקידש היום חייב להפסיק  משא"כ בהבדלה שאע"פ שחשיכה אינו מפסיק מ"מ אם אמר בואו ונבדיל הא סליק נפשיה משתיה ואסור למשתי בלא ברכה עכ"ל. וכתב בכ"מ פי' פלוגתייהו דבגמרא אמרי' שבת לקידוש קובע ולא להבדל' ופי' הראב"ד היינו בלא אמירת בואו ונקדש וממילא דכי אמרי' הבדלה אינה קובעת היינו בלא אמירתם בואו ונבדיל אבל באמירתם ה"ל היסח הדעת כמו הב לן ונברך והרמב"ם ס"ל דכי אמרינן שבת קובעת לקידוש היינו כשאמרו בואו וכו' וא"כ כי אמרי' להבדל' אינה קובעת אפי' באמירת בואו ונבדיל עכ"ל הנה אנו רואים בפי' לדעת הראב"ד דבקידוש אפי' בלא אמירת' מפסיק ומברך בפה"ג כמ"ש בהבדלה כשאומרים בואו כו' דבזה שוין הסוא"כ בחנ' תמה ב"י על רבינו הטור למה כ' י"א שמקדש ומברך על היין שזה לא נמצא והא לך  הראב"ד שכ"כ בפי' וקשה דהא כבר פסק הרמב' ספכ"ט  דמשקידש היו' אסור לשתו' ולא זכר כלום מאמיר' זו תי' בכ"מ דהתם משחשיכ' ודאי וכאן בספק חשיכה וק"ל דא"כ חשיכ' ודאי בלא אמירה יהיה לו דין א' עם ספק חשיכה עם אמירה וא"כ היה לו לרמב"ם לכתוב בפכ"ט משבת שצריך לברך בפה"ג קודם הקידוש כמ"ש כאן בספק חשיכה עם אמירה ותו דא"כ פסקי הש"ע סותרים זה את זה דבסמוך הביא דיעות לענין המוציא משמע דלענין בפה"ג ד"ה שא"צ וכאן כ' הרמב"ם שצריך ונ"ל דודאי לענין איסור שתיה שוה חשיכה ודאי בלא אמירה כמו ספק חשיכה עם אמירה אלא לענין בפה"ג אינם שוים דבחשיכה בלא אמירה אין האיסור שהוא מצד עצמו עושה היסח הדעת שיצטרך לברך שנית בפה"ג אבל כשיש אמירה אפי' בספק חשיכה וכ"ש בודאי חשיכה האמירה היא עושה היסח הדעת לברך שנית וכמ"ש בדעת י"א בסעיף שלפני זה שהוא הרא"ש לענין חילוק שבין בפה"ג ולברכת המוציא דלא סגי לעשות היסח הדעת מחמת האיסור לחוד כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Two who were drinking, etc.</b> This is the language of the Rambam in chapter 4 of Berachos, and he concluded that if they said, Come and let us recite havdalah, they need not bless again. The Raavad wrote on this: confused words are here. The interruption of kiddush, even if they did not say, Come and let us recite kiddush, since the day became sanctified, obligates them to interrupt. This is unlike havdalah, where although it became dark he does not interrupt. Nevertheless, if he said, Come and let us recite havdalah, he has removed himself from drinking and may not drink without a blessing. The Kesef Mishneh explained their dispute: the Gemara says that Shabbos establishes for kiddush but not for havdalah. The Raavad explains this as without saying, Come and let us recite kiddush, and automatically when we say havdalah does not establish, this too means without saying, Come and let us recite havdalah; but with such a statement it is diversion of mind, like saying, Let us bless. The Rambam holds that when we say Shabbos establishes for kiddush, this is when they said, Come, etc.; if so, when we say it does not establish for havdalah, it is even when saying, Come and let us recite havdalah. We see explicitly according to the Raavad that by kiddush, even without their statement, he interrupts and blesses Borei peri hagefen, just as by havdalah when they say, Come, etc., for in this they are equal. The Beit Yosef therefore wondered on the Tur: why did he write that some say he recites kiddush and blesses over the wine, claiming this is not found? Here the Raavad wrote it explicitly. There is a difficulty, for the Rambam already ruled at the end of chapter 29 of Shabbos that once the day became sanctified it is forbidden to drink, and he mentioned nothing of this statement. The Kesef Mishneh answered that there it is definite darkness, and here doubtful darkness. This is difficult: if so, definite darkness without a statement should have the same law as doubtful darkness with a statement, and the Rambam should have written in chapter 29 of Shabbos that one must bless Borei peri hagefen before kiddush, as he wrote here by doubtful darkness with a statement. Furthermore, the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch would contradict each other, for nearby he brought opinions regarding HaMotzi, implying that regarding Borei peri hagefen all agree he need not bless, while here the Rambam writes that he must. It seems to me that regarding the prohibition of drinking, definite darkness without a statement is the same as doubtful darkness with a statement. But regarding Borei peri hagefen they are not the same, for in darkness without a statement the prohibition itself does not create diversion of mind requiring another Borei peri hagefen; but where there is a statement, even in doubtful darkness and certainly in definite darkness, the statement creates diversion of mind requiring another blessing. This is as I wrote in the view of the Some say in the preceding seif, which is the Rosh's view regarding the distinction between Borei peri hagefen and HaMotzi: the prohibition alone is insufficient to create diversion of mind. So it appears to me.

<b>אע"פ שאינם רשאים. </b> משמע שאע"פ שמברכים שנית עבדי איסורא במה ששותים אחר שחל עליהם קידוש היום ולעיל סי' קע"ט פסק בש"ע דאם אמר הב לן ונבריך אסור לשתות עד שיברך כו' משמע שאחר הברכה אין איסור וס"ל דיש חילוק בין אמירה זו לזו דברמב"ם כאן נקט שניהם ועל שניהם סיים אע"פ שאינם רשאים ונ"ל דמ"מ לענין הלכה אנו מחלקים דלענין הב לן ונבריך אין איסור אחר הברכה שנית ולענין שבת דקובעת עצמה לקידוש יש איסור אפילו בברכה שנית עד שיקדש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Although they are not permitted.</b> This implies that although they bless again, they transgress by drinking after the sanctity of the day has taken effect. Above in siman 179 the Shulchan Aruch ruled that if one said, Let us bless, it is forbidden to drink until he blesses, which implies that after the blessing there is no prohibition. He holds that there is a distinction between this statement and that one. The Rambam here took both cases and concluded about both that they are not permitted. Nevertheless, it appears to me that regarding halacha we distinguish: regarding Let us bless, there is no prohibition after a second blessing; regarding Shabbos, which establishes itself for kiddush, there is a prohibition even with a second blessing until he recites kiddush.

<b>ונזכר קודם שאכל. </b> נראה פשוט דגם לאחר שאכל קצת הוה כן שיקדש ולא יצטרך לברך שנית המוציא ואפי' לאותה דיעה דסעיף ד' דצריך להמוציא היינו כיון שאכל כבר מבע"י אבל כאן שבירך המוציא אחר שנכנס שבת ודאי לא צריך שנית להמוציא ולא זכר כאן קודם שאכל אלא משום סיפא דבהבדלה לא יבדיל קודם האכילה כיון דאין מבדילין על הפת ה"ל הבדלה הפסק בין המוציא לאכילה אלא יאכל קצת תחלה ויבדיל ויסיים אכילתו משא"כ בקידוש דלא הוי הפסק אע"ג שתחלה בירך המוציא שלא ע"ד קידוש אלא אאכילה בעלמא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he remembered before he ate.</b> It appears obvious that even after he ate a little the same applies, that he recites kiddush and need not bless HaMotzi again. Even according to the opinion in seif 4 that he must recite HaMotzi, that is because he had already eaten while it was still day. Here, however, where he blessed HaMotzi after Shabbos entered, he certainly need not recite HaMotzi again. It mentioned here before he ate only because of the end of the case, that regarding havdalah he should not recite havdalah before eating, since one does not recite havdalah over bread, and havdalah would be an interruption between HaMotzi and eating. Rather, he should eat a little first, then recite havdalah, and finish his meal. This is unlike kiddush, which is not an interruption, even though at first he blessed HaMotzi not with kiddush in mind but merely for eating.

<b>וי"א דאף בגמר סעודתו כו'. </b> משמע כ"ש תוך סעודתו דדינא הכי וקשה דלמה כתב כאן ומברך המוציא דהא בס"ד הביא דיעות דא"צ ומשמע ספק ברכות להקל וכאן כתב שיברך ובאמת דברים של י"א אלו דאף בגמר כו' הם דברי הרא"ש כמ"ש הטור וכמו שהעתיק ב"י שהסכים עם מהר"ר ישעיה מטראנ"י לדידיה באמת גם בס"ד צריך לברך המוציא שהיא הדיעה הראשונה שם אלא דעל בעל הש"ע קשה ונ"ל דל' דאף בגמר לא קאי על כל מה שיזכר ע"ז אח"כ אלא אהאי מילתא לחוד דפורס מפה ומקדש אבל מה שסיים אח"כ ומברך המוציא זה קאי דוקא אגמר סעודתו ונוטל ידיו או אמר הב לן ונבריך דאז חייב לברך שנית המוציא אפי' בחול וא"ל א"כ גם בפה"ג צריך לכ"ע דאה"נ דהך ומקדש שכתב כאן פי' בסתם קידוש כל השנה דאומר בפה"ג תחלה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say that even when he finished his meal, etc.</b> This implies all the more so in the middle of his meal that the law is so. It is difficult: why does he write here that he blesses HaMotzi, when in seif 4 he brought opinions that he need not, and it implies that in doubt regarding blessings we are lenient? Here he writes that he should bless. In truth, the words of this Some say, that even when he finished, etc., are the words of the Rosh, as the Tur wrote and as the Beit Yosef copied, who agreed with Mahari of Trani. According to him, indeed in seif 4 as well one must bless HaMotzi, which is the first opinion there. But the difficulty is on the Shulchan Aruch. It appears to me that the phrase even when he finished does not refer to everything that follows, but only to this matter, that he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush. What he concludes afterward, that he blesses HaMotzi, applies specifically to where he finished his meal and washed his hands or said, Let us bless, for then he must bless HaMotzi a second time even on a weekday. If so, should he not also need Borei peri hagefen according to all views? Indeed, the word recites kiddush written here means the usual kiddush all year, that he first says Borei peri hagefen.

<b>If the cup spilled, etc.</b> An incident came before me in the lands of Moravia: I recited kiddush over a cup that I thought was wine, but it was water, and in a second vessel that was also before me there was wine. I had already said all of kiddush. At first I considered drinking the other wine in the second vessel, because kiddush over wine is only D'Rabbanan, derived as a support from Remember it over wine, while according to the Torah verbal recitation alone suffices, as Tosafot write at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Their enactment was only to attach the wine to the Torah kiddush, and thereby one fulfills the mitzvah; but if he already fulfilled the mitzvah according to the Torah, they did not require a new kiddush merely because of the wine. There is also a clear proof from what is written in seif 15, that if the cup spilled before he tasted from it, he brings another cup and blesses Borei peri hagefen, and need not repeat kiddush; rather, he fulfilled with the first kiddush. Although the cup spilled and he did not fulfill, for even if he drank less than the measure he did not fulfill, we nevertheless do not say that afterward he must recite kiddush again. So too here, what difference is there whether it spilled and he did not drink at all, or whether it was water? In the end he did not fulfill kiddush over wine. So too the Tur in siman 269 cites Rav Natronai Gaon, who wrote that one is obligated only to hear kiddush and fulfills even without tasting. Although the Tur wondered about him there, that is because it is not in the place of a meal, but not because one cannot fulfill by hearing alone. Then I saw this law in the rulings of Mahari, siman 115, where he wrote simply that one should recite kiddush again over wine. He argued: what of it if kiddush over wine is D'Rabbanan? There are many D'Rabbanan mitzvos over which we bless. Would one say that if he blew mistakenly on Rosh Hashanah during the standing blasts he need not repeat them, since he already fulfilled according to the Torah with the seated blasts and these are only D'Rabbanan? Certainly no one would say this. I am very astonished at that great rabbi for uttering such words, for what proof did he bring from shofar, where no additional blessing is required? What concern is there if he blows again? Certainly, if he blew mistakenly and finished the entire prayer, he need not repeat the prayer, for we rule that the blessings and blasts do not prevent one another, and this is an explicit Talmudic ruling. Here, however, he would need to bless the kiddush blessing a second time, and there is concern for a blessing in vain. However, after further analysis it appears that one must distinguish the matter, as I will explain with Heaven's help. This case resembles what is written in siman 272 regarding kiddush in the place of a meal, where we rule that if he recited kiddush intending to eat here, and some cause arose requiring him to uproot from that place, he must recite kiddush again. Although kiddush in the place of a meal is only D'Rabbanan, even according to Rashbam who derives it from You shall call Shabbos a delight it is only an asmachta, nevertheless he must recite kiddush again. So too here he must recite kiddush again over wine. One should not say that there, in the law of the place of the meal, perhaps the kiddush was proper and he fulfilled, but he is forbidden to eat away from the place of kiddush. That is not so, for on the contrary the Beit Yosef ruled there that even if he does not want to eat afterward at all, he did not fulfill kiddush, and he derived it from the Gemara that Rava's lamp went out, he went to Rabbah his son, recited kiddush again, and tasted something. Thus, since he did not fulfill the D'Rabbanan kiddush, he must recite kiddush again; the same applies here. But according to this, it is very difficult why, when the cup spilled, he need not repeat kiddush over another cup, since he did not fulfill D'Rabbanan kiddush, namely kiddush over the cup. It is also difficult from one who recited kiddush and spoke before tasting: he must bless Borei peri hagefen again but not kiddush, as written in this seif from Behag, cited by the Tur at the end of this siman. It is likewise difficult from what the Tur writes in the name of Behag in this siman, that the one who recites kiddush himself specifically must taste; if someone else drank the cup, they bring the reciter another cup and he blesses Borei peri hagefen but does not recite kiddush again. Why do we not say that since he at least did not fulfill, he should recite kiddush again? It seems to me that the essence depends on kiddush requiring tasting wine at the time of kiddush. About this they said, Remember it over wine, that there must be tasting of wine there and that kiddush be said over it. If a mishap occurred in the tasting of wine, he recited kiddush over it according to law, but since he cannot taste, he can repair the defect by tasting other wine. But regarding kiddush in the place of a meal, if it turns out that he cannot eat there, there is no repair for that defect, and therefore he must recite kiddush again where he will eat. In our case, since he did not recite kiddush properly, it is as though he did not recite kiddush at all, and therefore he must recite kiddush again over wine. However, there is a distinction: if at the time of kiddush there was wine on the table or on the bench near him prepared to drink, then certainly he need not recite either Borei peri hagefen or kiddush again, but should drink what was prepared before him, as Rama wrote in siman 209 from the responsum of Maharil, for it is as though he recited kiddush over that wine, and in this we say all cups are included in the blessing. So it appears to me correctly in this matter.
