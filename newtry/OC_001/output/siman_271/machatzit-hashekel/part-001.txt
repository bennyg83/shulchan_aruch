**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 1, to eat immediately means to recite kiddush, etc.</b> The Tur's reason is derived from remembering Shabbos at its entrance, which teaches primarily that kiddush should be said immediately. Since kiddush must be in the place of a meal, eating must also follow immediately. He also discusses the statement in Tikkunei Shabbos about reciting kiddush before night because of the planetary hour, and notes that this applies only when the day and night are equal. From the Yerushalmi and Rashi it appears that people would eat the Shabbos night meal after nightfall.

<b>Seif katan 13, he finished, etc.</b> The Rif rules that if they finished the meal before Shabbos began and while they were washing mayim acharonim Shabbos began, they first recite Birkas HaMazon on one cup and afterward kiddush on a second cup, because two sanctifications are not placed on one cup. The Rosh objects that since kiddush must be in the place of the meal, they should rather spread a cloth, recite kiddush and HaMotzi, and continue. The Ran answers for the Rif that the weekday meal can still count as the meal place for kiddush. The remaining issue depends on the laws of removal from the meal in siman 179. According to the Rif, once they washed, Birkas HaMazon is required first; according to the Rosh as understood by the Magen Avraham, even washing mayim acharonim need not force Birkas HaMazon before continuing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 14, and he must, etc.</b> There is no concern if he mentions Shabbos unnecessarily. Above in siman 188, when one began eating on Shabbos and finished after Shabbos, he mentions Shabbos because perhaps we follow the beginning of the meal. Here too, even if the mention may be unnecessary, it is not an interruption, as at the end of siman 108, where mentioning an event of another day in prayer is not an interruption. The Taz disputes this.
