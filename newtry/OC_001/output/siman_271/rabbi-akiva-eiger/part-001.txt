<small>מג"א ס"ק ב'</small> ועמ"ש רסי' קצ"ג. בס' דגול מרבבה כ' דאיש שכבר התפלל ערבית א"י להוציא בקידוש לאשה שלא התפללה ערבית דהוא אינו מחוייב בדבר מדאורייתא דאף דבברכות המצות אם יצא מוציא. הא הטעם משום דכולם עריבים זה בזה כמ"ש תוס' ר"ה כ"ט. והרי הרא"ש כ' בפ"ג דברכות דאשה אינה בכלל ערבות לכך אינה מוציאה אלא למי שחיובו מדרבנן. ובזה יש להסתפק די"ל נשים אין עריבים בעד אחרים. אבל אנשים ערבים גם על הנשים עיי"ש. ולענ"ד דאין חלוק דין אנשים לנשים לענין ערבות ולא מצינו בשום דוכתא דבנשים אין הדין דאם יצא מוציא והרי גבי ינאי מלכא ומלכתא דכריכו ריפתא משמע דאם היה אוכל שמעון בן שטח כזית שפיר עבד והא אם נשים מחוייבות בבהמ"ז דאורייתא. איך היה יכול להוציא לאשת ינאי דהיא מחוייבת מדאורייתא ורשב"ש מדרבנן והא ליכא ערבות. זולת שנאמר דנשים אין ערבים לאחרים. אבל אנשים ערבים גם לנשים. א"כ עכ"פ נפשט ספקו דהגאון הנ"ל. אבל נראה דכוונת הרא"ש דהערבות היה רק לגבי מי שמחוייב במצוה זו הוא ערב על אחרים. ואם נשים בבהמ"ז דאורייתא באמת אם יצאו מוציאים לאנשים. דכיון שהם שייכים במצוה זו ישנם ג"כ בכלל ערבות. אבל במצוה שאין חייבים בה דהיינו אם נשים בבהמ"ז דרבנן. אין להם דין ערבות במצוה א'. ובתשובות כתבתי ראיה ברורה לזה מהא דמבואר בסוגיא דמגלה אם חרש שא"י להשמיע לאזנו לא יצא א"י להוציא אחרים דהוא אינו מחוייב בדבר. וכן פסקי' לקמן סימן תקפ"ה ס"ב. ובזה יקשה ק' הרא"ש דהא יצא מוציא וליתא לתי' הרא"ש. דהא התם מיירי באנשים דישנן בכלל ערבות. אע"כ דאין כוונת הרא"ש לחלק בין נשים לאנשים לענין ערבות. אלא דמי שאינו בר חיוב במצוה זו אינו בכלל ערבות על אחרים ומש"ה בשופר כיון דאינו שומע. ואינו בחיוב מצות שופר אינו בכלל ערבות על אחרים ודו"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 2.</small> See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 193. In Dagul Merevavah he wrote that a man who already prayed Maariv cannot discharge through kiddush a woman who did not pray Maariv, for he is no longer obligated in the matter according to the Torah. Although regarding blessings over mitzvos one who has fulfilled can discharge another, the reason is that all are guarantors for one another, as Tosafot write in Rosh Hashanah 29. But the Rosh wrote in the third chapter of Berachos that a woman is not included in mutual responsibility, and therefore she discharges only one whose obligation is D'Rabbanan. In this there is room to doubt, for one may say women are not guarantors for others, but men are guarantors even for women. See there. In my humble opinion, there is no distinction between men and women regarding mutual responsibility, and we find nowhere that women do not have the law that if one fulfilled, she can discharge another. Regarding Yannai the king and the queen who ate bread, it implies that if Shimon ben Shetach had eaten a kezayis he would have acted properly. If women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon according to the Torah, how could he discharge Yannai's wife, since she would be obligated according to the Torah and Shimon ben Shetach only D'Rabbanan, unless there is mutual responsibility? Unless we say women are not guarantors for others, but men are guarantors even for women; if so, the doubt of the above Gaon is at least resolved. But it appears that the Rosh means that mutual responsibility applies only to one obligated in this mitzvah, who is responsible for others. If women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon according to the Torah, then if they already fulfilled, they truly can discharge men, for since they belong to this mitzvah they are also included in mutual responsibility. But in a mitzvah in which they are not obligated, meaning if women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon only D'Rabbanan, they do not have the law of mutual responsibility in that mitzvah. In responsa I wrote a clear proof from what is explained in the sugya of Megillah, that if a deaf person who cannot make himself hear has not fulfilled, he cannot discharge others, for he is not obligated in the matter. So we rule below in siman 585, seif 2. There the Rosh's question is difficult, for one who has fulfilled can discharge another, and the Rosh's answer does not apply, since there it speaks of men who are included in mutual responsibility. It must be that the Rosh did not intend to distinguish between women and men regarding mutual responsibility, but rather that one who is not subject to obligation in this mitzvah is not included in mutual responsibility for others. Therefore regarding shofar, since he does not hear and is not obligated in the mitzvah of shofar, he is not included in mutual responsibility for others. Examine this well.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif 4.</small> Only kiddush, without the blessing over wine. This is from the words of the Rosh, who derived it from the Yerushalmi. But the Ramban in Milchamos, in the sugya of One may not give him fewer than four cups, holds that he must also bless Borei peri hagefen, and the Yerushalmi follows Rabbi Eliezer, for even Birkas HaMazon is not an interruption. See there. Perhaps this is the view of the Some say cited by the Beit Yosef; see the Beit Yosef, who was pressed in this. See Bach, who wrote that the reasoning of the Rosh is forced. In my humble opinion, from the Mordechai's words it appears otherwise, for he inferred that one who recites kiddush in the synagogue may taste even though he did not fulfill kiddush, from Shmuel's statement, Even the obligation of kiddush he did not fulfill, which implies that the obligation of wine he also did not fulfill. How is it possible that he drank? It must be that he may taste. Now, if we say that kiddush is an interruption, what practical difference is there to Shmuel that he did not fulfill the wine obligation because of change of place? Let it follow because immediately after tasting he becomes forbidden to drink until he recites kiddush at home, and automatically he would need to bless Borei peri hagefen, since kiddush would be an interruption. Rather, this proves like the Rosh, that since the blessing Borei peri hagefen belongs before kiddush, kiddush is not an interruption. The Rosh follows his own view, that it is forbidden for the reciter of kiddush to taste, and according to Shmuel he truly should not drink; therefore he needed to bring proof from the Yerushalmi. See Tosafot 106b, s.v. ta'am, how he can recite kiddush again at home, and see Maharsha there, who holds that according to Shmuel he drank wine, as the Mordechai inferred. Nevertheless, it seems to me that in halacha Tosafot do not hold like the Mordechai that he may taste, for if so they asked nothing, since he tasted permissibly. Rather they hold like the Rosh, that it is forbidden to taste; only Shmuel's language implies a practical difference in that he did not fulfill the wine obligation where he drank in prohibition, and they answered that since he tasted only a minimal amount, it is not called tasting. This resolves the Maharsha's question on Tosafot, that perhaps he truly should not recite kiddush at home, for since we do not instruct him to taste, one can say that if he did taste he cannot recite kiddush at home. But the Maharsha's question is really not valid, for why then did Shmuel say he did not fulfill the wine obligation, since he would not drink at home at all because he cannot recite kiddush? According to this it is also proven that Tosafot's view is like the Rosh, for otherwise the question would still remain: why say he did not fulfill the wine obligation, since kiddush would in any case be an interruption? Examine this well.
