**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Magen Avraham seif katan 3</b> R. Yitzchak, Ran, etc. In my humble opinion this is a scribal error, and it should read: Beis Yosef, Ran. That means that the Beis Yosef wrote this in the name of the Ran, and concerning this the Magen Avraham wrote: according to what I wrote above, this is unnecessary, etc. Meaning, the Beis Yosef is difficult, for he brought the Ran's words here, and they were stated according to the one who says that he eats it immediately, so that we would say muktzeh for its mitzvah is more severe; and concerning this he wrote that it is as though he stipulated, see there. But according to the one who says that he eats it on the next day, there is no difficulty at all, for specifically a sukkah was set aside at twilight for its mitzvah, but an esrog was not set aside at twilight of the following day at all, since one does not need to take it at twilight. See above at the beginning of siman 652, where he wrote that from the Ran's words it is clear that one does not need to take it at twilight except on the first day. According to this, seemingly an esrog of the first day should be prohibited on the second. But one may say, according to what is implied here, that he is speaking regarding the law in Eretz Yisrael; for if in the diaspora, even the esrog of the seventh day is prohibited on the eighth. If so, even that of the first day is permitted on the second, for it is a weekday and there is no muktzeh. According to this, one may say that what the Magen Avraham wrote, 'and according to what I wrote,' etc., does not refer to what he wrote here in seif 1, that we do not say muktzeh because of the doubt concerning the previous day; rather, it refers to what he wrote in siman 652 in the name of the Ran. See the Ran at the beginning of Beitzah. One may say that only with sukkah does he hold that it is muktzeh for its mitzvah, which is severe enough to prohibit even because of the previous day; this is not so with an esrog regarding eating on the following day, since one does not need to take it at twilight. Even if you say that it is prohibited to eat it then because of the doubt that perhaps it is day, nevertheless it is like any other muktzeh because of the previous day, which we do not say. But from the Ran's words in Sukkah it implies that if the esrog were prohibited for eating at twilight because of the doubt concerning the previous day, we would indeed say that since it was set aside, it remains set aside. Rather, in truth it is not prohibited at twilight of the weekday when its prohibition is because it was set aside for its mitzvah, for this applies only during the hour that is fit for the mitzvah. If so, at twilight, when it is not fit for the mitzvah, muktzeh for its mitzvah does not take hold of it. Therefore at the twilight of the eighth itself it is permitted, and there is no basis at all to say that it became muktzeh at twilight; see there.
