**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 4) And to make, etc.</b> For it is a genuine derashah; and he brought proof from what we say in the first chapter of Chagigah: that even though each and every one of Israel fulfills the mitzvah of the simcha offering that he is obligated in on the festival even if he brings vows and freewill offerings that he vowed and pledged before Yom Tov from animal maaser, etc., nevertheless he does not fulfill simcha with menachos and a bird offering, for it is written "and you shall rejoice on your festival," that the joy should be on your festival with a species through which one fulfills the Chagigah offering that each person is obligated in on Yom Tov; with menachos and birds one does not fulfill Chagigah. Rav Ashi said: we do not need reliance [on juxtaposition]; and "and you shall rejoice on your festival" itself: rather from the word "and you shall rejoice" itself we learn that there is no joy except with the meat of an animal. And [the Gemara] asks: if so, according to Rav Ashi, why do I need the verse "and you shall rejoice on your festival"? And it answers: it is needed [to teach] that one does not marry women on the moed, for we expound "and you shall rejoice on your festival, and not with your wife." Behold, then, it is not an asmachta but a genuine derashah; for it answers that this [verse] is needed [to teach] that one does not marry women, etc. And this proof Tosafos also wrote in Kesubos folio 47.

<b>And further in Sukkah folio 25 it is explicitly implied, etc.</b> For we say there that a chasan and his shoshvinin are exempt from the sukkah, for there is no joy except under the chuppah, and it is impossible to make a chuppah in the sukkah; and this is clear below in siman 640, se'if 6, see there. And how will you find [this case]? Necessarily it deals with one who married on erev ha-regel, and the days of the chuppah continue into the festival; and so wrote Rashi there. And even if you say it deals with one who married some days before the festival and not on erev ha-regel, nevertheless this and that are one and the same: since it is permitted if he married two days before the festival even though the seven days of feasting continue into the festival, the same law applies to one who marries on erev ha-regel. In another responsum [regarding] a wedding meal on the festival: if so, Magen Avraham's first difficulty is on the Beis Yosef, who wrote that since these are rabbinic matters we take the lenient side; for it is proven that it is according to the Torah, and it is proper to be stringent if there were a doubt in the matter.
