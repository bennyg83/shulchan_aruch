<b>א) [סעיף א'] מקום שיש לו ג' מחיצות וכו'  </b>בטור כתב דמה"ת רה"י גמור הוא וכתב עליו ב"י דלא לחייב הזורק מרה"ר לתוכו קאמר אלא להתיר לטלטל בכולו שכך דינה של כרמלית מה"ת וכדברי הרמב"ם פי"ד ופי"ז אבל יש חולקים עליו ואומרים דמקום שיש לו ג' מחיצות הוי רה"י גמור לחייב הזורק מרה"ר לתוכו וכבר ביאר המ"מ בפי"ז שמחלוקתם תלוי בסוגית הגמ' דעירובין די"א ודצ"ד יעו"ש. אבל הב"ח כתב דדעת הטור דלא כהרמב"ם אלא לחייב גם הזורק מרה"ר לתוכו יעו"ש. וכ"כ הט"ז סק"א אלא שכתב לחלק דאי פתוח לרה"ר ודחקי בה רבים שאינו חייב הזורק לתוכו מרה"ר ואי לא דחקי בה רבים הוי רה"י גמור ותייב יעו"ש והביאו א"ר או' א' וכתב דמב"ח ומ"א משמע דבכל ענין חייב. וכ"כ החמ"מ או' א' דמסתמיות הטור משמע דבכל ענין חייב יעו"ש. וכ"כ הנה"ש. ובעיקר דין זה דעת רש"י בעירובין י"ב ע"ב וחו' בכמה מקומות והראב"ד בהשגות והרא"ש פ"ק דסוכה והרשב"א בחי' ובעבוה"ק והריטב"א דבג' מחיצות או בב' מחיצות ולחי רה"י גמור הוא מה"ת. ועיין לעיל סי' שמ"ה או' ה' וסי' שמ"ו או' ג' ודוק.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] A place that has three partitions, etc. </b>The Tur writes that under Torah law it is a complete private domain. Beis Yosef explains that the Tur does not mean that one who throws an object into it from a public domain is liable, but that it is permitted to carry throughout it, for this is the status of a karmelis under Torah law, in accordance with Rambam in chapters 14 and 17. Others disagree and maintain that a place with three partitions is a complete private domain, such that one who throws an object into it from a public domain is liable. Maggid Mishneh explains in chapter 17 that their dispute depends upon the interpretation of the discussions in Eruvin 11 and 94. See there. Bach, however, writes that the Tur disagrees with Rambam and means that one who throws into it from a public domain is also liable. Taz, subsection 1, writes likewise, but distinguishes between a place open to a public domain where the public presses through, in which case one who throws into it from a public domain is not liable, and a place where the public does not press through, which is a complete private domain and one is liable. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, cites him and writes that Bach and Magen Avraham imply that one is liable in every circumstance. Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, likewise writes that the unqualified wording of the Tur implies liability in every circumstance. See there. Nehar Shalom writes likewise. Regarding the principal ruling, the opinions of Rashi on Eruvin 12b, Tosafos in several places, Raavad in his strictures, Rosh in chapter 1 of Sukkah, Rashba in his novellae and Avodas HaKodesh, and Ritva are that three partitions, or two partitions and a lechi, form a complete private domain under Torah law. See above, siman 345, subsection 5, and siman 346, subsection 3, and examine the matter carefully.

<b>5) There. Until one makes some form of rectification, etc. </b>A tzuras hapesach is effective under Torah law to make it a complete private domain, as stated by Tosafos on Eruvin 6a, beginning with the words 'Rav,' and there on 22a, beginning with the words 'But did Rabbi Yochanan not say,' and by Rosh in chapter 1 of Sukkah, siman 34. A korah, however, is effective only by Rabbinic law, as stated by Tosafos on Eruvin 86a, beginning with the word 'Korah.' See also Tosafos there on 5b, beginning with the words 'Whose breach,' who write that it is a tradition from Sinai that the width of a lechi and korah is effective, while a courtyard requires a four-handbreadth strip. See the gloss of Chok Nosson there, who questions this, and Maharsha there regarding his explanation. See also Rosh there, chapter 8, siman 7, and Ritva on folio 86, who write that a korah four handbreadths wide constitutes a partition under Torah law, while one narrower than four handbreadths is effective only by Rabbinic law as a visual marker. Orach Chaim, subsection 1. See above, siman 362, subsection 88.

<b>6) A lechi </b>functions as a partition under Torah law, whereas a korah functions as a visual marker. Eruvin 12 states: If an alleyway was rendered permitted by a lechi, one who throws an object into it is liable; if it was rendered permitted by a korah, one is exempt. According to Rambam, this refers to an alleyway with three partitions; according to the other authorities, it refers to one with two partitions. A practical difference concerns disqualification from testimony or the status of an apostate through desecrating Shabbos: one who desecrates Shabbos in violation of Torah law is deemed an apostate with respect to the entire Torah. However, some say that nowadays we have no public domain; see siman 345, Taz, subsection 6. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. See our words there in se'if 7. Another practical difference concerns whether the Shabbos desecration violated a Torah or Rabbinic prohibition with respect to the required rectification. See the end of siman 334.

<b>8) There. A courtyard that was breached across its full width, etc. </b>For a square courtyard breached on its fourth side, a different rectification is required from that used for an alleyway that is longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side. A courtyard requires a four-handbreadth strip on one side, or two strips of any width, one on each side. An alleyway longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side, however, was permitted with a lechi of any width on one side, as stated below in se'if 3. Bach. The same applies to a courtyard that is longer than it is wide, whose law is that of an alleyway, as stated below in se'if 27. The reason for the distinction between a courtyard and an alleyway is that anything used with greater privacy requires a more substantial partition, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rashba. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3, writes likewise. See below, subsection 164.

<b>14) There. A handbreadth on this side and a handbreadth on that side. </b>Beis Yosef writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan, but comments that a handbreadth is not literal, since any amount on each side suffices. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 4, writes that this is explicit in Rabbeinu Yehonasan's words cited by Beis Yosef in siman 372. Since the Shulchan Aruch does not write so here, one must say that he reconsidered and ruled stringently in accordance with Tosafos. See there. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, likewise challenges Beis Yosef and writes that when part of the wall remains, it specifically must be one handbreadth wide. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules likewise. However, Rambam writes in chapter 15, halachah 12, that even when remnants remain, any amount on each side suffices. Ritva on Eruvin 10a and Rabbeinu Chananel there write likewise, as does Levush. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, cites him and writes that this also appears from Rashba's Avodas HaKodesh, folio 6, and that Rashba's resolution may be relied upon. See there. Chayei Adam, rule 71, subsection 3, and Nishmas Adam there, subsection 1, write that initially one should be stringent and prohibit carrying unless a handbreadth remains, but in pressing circumstances one may rely upon the lenient authorities, who hold that any remnant suffices. See there. It appears that this should be the practical ruling.
