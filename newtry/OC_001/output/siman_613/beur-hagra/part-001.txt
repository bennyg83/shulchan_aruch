<b>ס"ב נוטל כו'. </b>ע' תוס' שם וע"ל סי' תקנ"ד ס"י ומש"ש אבל בירושל' פ"ב דברכות אמרינן דבי"כ וט"ב אסור רחיצה אא"כ חולה שיש בו סכנה וז"ל א"ל דלא יסחיימות אין בעי אפי' בט"ב כו' אפי' ביה"כ וכ"כ הרמב"ם בפ"ז מה"ת ה"ח וכ"כ מ"ע שם בשם הר"פ שאין רחיצה כלל ביה"כ וט"ב אלא כמש"ש ואם היה מלוכלך כו' ובצואה רוחץ כדרכו כו' וכן עיקר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 2: He washes, etc.</b> See Tosafos there, and above, siman 554, seif 10, and what I wrote there. In the Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Berachos, however, it is stated that on Yom Kippur and Tishah B’Av washing is forbidden unless one is dangerously ill. Its wording is: “He said to him: If not for [a corrupt phrase], if he wishes, even on Tishah B’Av, etc., even on Yom Kippur.” Rambam likewise writes in chapter 7 of Hilchos Taaniyos, halachah 8, and the Maggid Mishneh there likewise writes in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz, that there is no washing at all on Yom Kippur or Tishah B’Av except as stated there: If he was soiled, etc., with excrement, he washes normally, etc. This is the primary ruling.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 3: If he urinated, etc., or relieved himself, etc.</b> All of this follows the principle stated above, that they prohibited only, etc. This is as stated in chapter 3 of Yoma: “This is the rule: anyone who anoints, etc.,” even on Yom Kippur, for it refers back to the opening clause. It is stated there: Granted, regarding his feet, etc., it is a mitzvah to rub them. See the old Tosafos in Yoma there, who explain that this applies even if he urinated and did not rub, because of “prepare,” see there at length, s.v. “Because,” etc.
