**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) Ibid. Soiled with mud or excrement, etc. </b>However, washing to remove perspiration is forbidden, as stated in the following siman regarding anointing, that it is forbidden in order to remove grime; the same applies to washing. Taz, s.k. 1. This is also the view of the Bach and Pri Chadash. However, the words of the Levush at the beginning of siman 614 below imply that washing is permitted to remove grime. This is also the view of the Magen Avraham there, s.k. 1. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah there, sec. 1, in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, and Maamar Mordechai there, sec. 1, in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach on the Rambam, chapter 3, see there. Mateh Ephraim, sec. 2, distinguished between the cases: whenever the perspiration causes him distress because he is delicate, it may be permitted in accordance with the Levush and Magen Avraham. One who is not delicate and does not have such a great need for this washing should be stringent not to wash merely to remove perspiration, just as it is forbidden to anoint with oil to remove grime, as explained in the following siman. So too wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 2. See above siman 4, seif 21 in the gloss, and our words there, sec. 95.

<b>12) [Seif 3] If he urinated and rubbed with his hand, etc. </b>This implies that without rubbing, he should not wash his hands unless he wishes to pray, as stated afterward. However, the Beit Yosef cited several great authorities who maintain that even without rubbing, he may wash his hands because of preparation, meaning in order to recite Asher Yatzar in holiness, see there. The Bach wrote that in practice he should bring himself into an obligation to wash by wiping and rubbing, for then he is obligated to wash his hands according to all opinions, see there. The Pri Chadash wrote that this is unnecessary, because any washing that is not intended to remove grime or the like, and that involves preparation, is permitted, see there. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, wrote in accordance with the Bach, that he should wipe and rub in order to remove himself from the dispute. So too wrote Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 4; Chayei Adam, rule 145, sec. 11; Mateh Ephraim, sec. 4; Mishna Berurah, sec. 4. This applies specifically at night. During the day, however, if he urinated or relieved himself, since prayers are recited throughout the day, he may wash his hands even if he did not wipe and rub. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 5; Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. All this concerns one who relieved himself or urinated in a field or the like. But if he entered a fixed privy, in any case he must wash his hands because of the harmful spirit that rests upon the hands in a privy, as stated above siman 4, sec. 65, see there. See Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 2.
