<b>סעיף א'  </b>המדליק בליל ראשון מברך ג' ברכות וכו'. נ"ב הנה בדין אם מותר להפסיק בין נר לנר לשאול מפני הכבוד או היראה עמ"ש בחיבורי על או"ח משנת תד"ר לפ"ק בהלכות ר"ח סי' תכ"ב מ"ש בזה באריכות קצת ודו"ק. דהנה גרסינן בשבת פ"ב דף כ"ג מה ממעט ממעט זמן ונמעט נס ומשני נס כל יומא איתא. ואם הכוונה כפשוטו יש לדייק מה פריך ונמעט נס מה אולמא דזמן מן הנס דאף לפי הס"ד דלא ידע דנס עדיף עכ"פ לא גרע מזמן. ותדע דאם יש צד לו' דיש מעלה לזמן על הנס א"כ ניהו דמשני דלנס יש מעלה דנס כל יומא איתא אכתי הרי לזמן יש נמי מעלה כפי הס"ד ואכתי לימא כולהו. ובע"כ דגם המקשן ידע דאין לזמן שייכות יותר מנס וא"כ מה פריך ונמעט נס וכי בשביל כך יהיה מעלה לזמן. ודוחק לפרש דהכוונה ולמעט נמי נס דאם כן ה"ל לו' כן ונמעט נמי נס ועוד קשה למה במגילה מברכין שהחיינו גם בשחרית ולא אמרינן דדי בשל לילה דכיון שהגיענו לתחלת הזמן הגיענו כפירש"י בחנוכה כאן. ולכך נראה דמזה יהיה ראיה למ"ש בחיבורי ספר החיים וכן מצאתי אח"כ בבינה לעתים כתב נמי כן דנר הראשון הוי זכר לגוף הנס הגאולה ושאר הימים הוי לנס השמן. וא"כ אתי שפיר דודאי הוי שייך לברך כל השלשה ברכות כל הימים רק בכוונה תקנו לפחות ברכה אחת בשאר הימים להראות ששאר הימים חלוקים משלפניו ויהי' למעלה ליום הראשון יתרון על שאר הימים. ולכך כיון דהוי רק לזכרון להיות חלוק יום הראשון מכל הימים פריך שפיר ולמעט נס וזמן יאמר ויהיה יותר היכר ליום הראשון על שאר הימים ולכך משני גם כל יומא איתא לכך שייך טפי לברך על הניסים וההיכר יהיה בהזמן משיעשה להיפוך לברך זמן ולעשות היכר בהנס ואתי שפיר ודו"ק היטב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 1.</b> One who kindles on the first night blesses three blessings, etc. Note: regarding whether it is permitted to interrupt between one lamp and another to inquire out of honor or fear, see what I wrote at some length in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5604, in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, siman 422; examine it. For we read in Shabbos, chapter 2, page 23: what does he reduce? He reduces the blessing of time. Let him reduce the blessing of the miracle. It answers: the miracle exists on every day. If the meaning were simple, one should examine: what was the question, let him reduce the miracle? What superiority does the blessing of time have over the blessing of the miracle? Even according to the initial assumption, before he knew that the miracle is preferable, at any rate it is no worse than the blessing of time. And know this: if there were room to say that the blessing of time has a superiority over the miracle, then even though it answers that the miracle has a superiority because the miracle exists on every day, still the blessing of time also has a superiority according to the initial assumption, and one should still say all of them. Perforce, even the questioner knew that time has no stronger connection than the miracle. If so, what did he ask, let him reduce the miracle? Is that enough to make time superior? It is forced to explain that the intent was, let him also reduce the miracle; but if so it should have said this explicitly, let him also reduce the miracle. There is another difficulty: why regarding Megillah do we bless Shehecheyanu also in the morning and not say that the nighttime blessing is enough, since once He brought us to the beginning of the time, He brought us, as Rashi explains here by Chanukah? Therefore it seems that from here there is proof for what I wrote in my work Sefer HaChayim, and afterward I also found that Binah LaItim wrote this as well: the first lamp commemorates the essential miracle of the salvation, and the other days commemorate the miracle of the oil. If so, it is well understood. Certainly it would have been relevant to bless all three blessings on all the days, but they intentionally instituted omitting one blessing on the remaining days to show that the remaining days differ from the day before them, and that the first day has an advantage over the other days. Therefore, since this is only for remembrance, to distinguish the first day from all the days, the Gemara properly asks: let him reduce the miracle and say the blessing of time, and there will be a greater distinction for the first day over the other days. It therefore answers that the miracle also exists on every day, and therefore it is more fitting to bless over the miracles, with the distinction being made through the blessing of time, rather than doing the opposite, blessing the blessing of time and making the distinction through the miracle. This is well understood; examine carefully.

<b>(שם סעיף ג') </b> ואם אח"כ בליל ב' או ג' בא להדליק אינו חוזר ומברך שהחיינו נ"ב עיין במג"א מ"ש בשם הב"ח דאם אשתו מדלקת עליו חייב לחזור ולברך שהחיינו דאשתו אינה פוטרת גופו רק ביתו וכו'. והנה לפי דברי הב"ח היה נדחה הראיה מ"ש הט"ז והמג"א לעיל דאשה מוציאה האיש ממה דאמרינן אשה וודאי מדלקת כיון דמ"ש הדליקה חש"ו לא עשה ולא כלום בע"כ הוי הכוונה להוציא אחרים א"כ מ"ש אשה וודאי מדלקת היינו להוציא אחרים ע"ש. מוציאה איש אפילו אם מדלקת בפניו ובשליחותו. ובגוף פלוגתת הב"ח והמג"א נראה לפענ"ד דראיית המג"א מר"ז לאו ראיה היא די"ל כיון דהראיה נמי מצוה היא שהרי הרואה מברך וכו'. אך הנה באמת בעלמא לא מצינו ברכה על שוא"ת רק מה שהוא בקום ועשה ולא על שוא"ת דעל ל"ת אין מברכין כמ"ש המג"א בהלכות סוכה בשם הלבוש דלכך אין מברכין כל שבעה על מצה משום דאין מצוה באכילת מצה ואין בו רק איסור חמץ ועל ל"ת אין מברכין וכן בסילוק תפילין בשבת ויו"ט כתבנו נמי כן וא"כ מוכח דעל שוא"ת אין מברכין מה שאינו בקום ועשה ולכך ה"נ בראיה שאינו עושה מעשה רק ראיה אינו מברך עליו רק נס שהחיינו ולכך אתי שפיר דלפטור גופו יוצא בראיה אבל לפטור ביתו צריכנא היינו להשתתף בפריטי לא צריכנא אבל ראיה מיהו צריך. אבל כאן בש"ע מיירי שאינו רואה גם כן דאל"כ הרי בירך בעת הראיה שהחיינו ובע"כ דלא ראה ג"כ עדיין ובזה חייב לברך אח"כ שהחיינו מכח הראיה דגופו עדיין <b>There, section 3.</b> And if afterward, on the second or third night, he comes to kindle, he does not go back and bless Shehecheyanu. Note: see Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Bach that if his wife kindles for him, he must go back and bless Shehecheyanu, because his wife exempts only his house, not his body, etc. According to Bach's words, the proof written by Turei Zahav and Magen Avraham above, that a woman can discharge a man, would be rejected. They proved this from what we say, a woman certainly kindles: since the statement that if a deaf-mute, minor, or mentally incompetent person kindled, he has done nothing, must mean that they do not discharge others, then what it says that a woman certainly kindles means that she discharges others. See there. According to the above, there is no proof, for one can say that there a woman never discharges a man, even if she kindles before him, but there it is speaking of exempting his house. Therefore it says that if a deaf-mute, minor, or mentally incompetent person kindled, he has done nothing, even to exempt his house; a woman certainly kindles. But to exempt his body, one may say that a woman does not discharge a man even if she kindles before him and as his agent. Regarding the actual dispute between Bach and Magen Avraham, it seems in my humble opinion that Magen Avraham's proof from Rabbi Zeira is no proof, for one may say that seeing is also a mitzvah, since one who sees blesses, etc. Yet in truth, generally we do not find a blessing on passive non-action, only on active performance, and not on passive non-action; for one does not bless on a negative prohibition, as Magen Avraham wrote in the laws of Sukkah in the name of Levush, that this is why one does not bless all seven days over matzah, because there is no mitzvah in eating matzah then, only the prohibition of chametz, and one does not bless over a negative prohibition. We also wrote similarly regarding removing tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov. If so, it is proven that one does not bless over passive non-action that is not active performance. Therefore here too, regarding seeing, where he performs no act but only sees, he blesses over it only the blessings of the miracle and Shehecheyanu. Therefore it is well understood: to exempt his body, he fulfills through seeing; but to exempt his house, he does not fulfill through seeing, only through kindling. The Sages obligated him in both, and placed the obligation both on his body and on his house. Therefore, as long as Rabbi Zeira was not married and had no one to exempt his house, seeing alone was not enough for him, and he therefore had to participate with coins. Then it was considered as though he himself kindled, and he was exempt from the mitzvah of seeing and the mitzvah of kindling. But when he married, his wife exempted him from the mitzvah of kindling and the obligation of his house. Nevertheless, his body was still obligated in seeing the Chanukah lamp, and for this he exempted himself through seeing. For this he did not need to participate with coins, since he himself was the one seeing and was fulfilling the mitzvah with his body. Granted, as long as his wife had not kindled for him, he was obligated in the mitzvah of kindling, and since he did not kindle himself but another did, he was not fulfilling the mitzvah himself; a mitzvah is greater when done by oneself than through one's agent. Therefore he had to participate with coins, so that he would be considered a partner, and a partner is preferable to an agent, as is proven in chapter 2 of Kiddushin; see there. But whenever he has a wife kindling for him, he is exempt from the mitzvah of kindling, only not from the mitzvah of seeing. That he fulfilled himself, for he saw it. Therefore he did not need to participate with coins. When he said, now I certainly do not need, he meant that he did not need to participate with coins; but seeing he still needed. Here in Shulchan Aruch, however, it is speaking where he also did not see. Otherwise he would already have blessed Shehecheyanu at the time of seeing. Perforce, he had not yet seen either, and in this case he must bless Shehecheyanu afterward because of seeing, for his body had not yet been exempted from seeing. The words of Bach are correct in my humble opinion; examine carefully.
