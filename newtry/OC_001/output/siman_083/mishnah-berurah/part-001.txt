(ה) אין מגיע - ואם מגיע ר"ר אסור עד שירחיק ד"א ממקום שכלה הריח ובזה די אפילו לפניו לדעת המחבר. ודע דדעת המחבר להתיר אפילו במחיצות שאין גבוהות יו"ד כדאיתא בב"י ובזה חולקים עליו כל האחרונים ובגבוהות יו"ד יש מן האחרונים שסוברין כוותיה והרבה מן האחרונים שחולקין גם בזה וטעמם דס"ל דכיון דהמחיצות מיוחדות לקבל צואה כמו צואה דיינינן להו וכגרף של רעי לקמן בסימן פ"ז דאסור אפילו אין בו ריח רע וכן מדברי הגר"א בביאורו משמע ג"כ שדעתו להחמיר בזה וכן בפמ"ג ובדה"ח ושאר הרבה אחרוני זמנינו כולם מחמירין בזה וס"ל דצריך להרחיק מהמחיצות ד"א ולפניו כמלא עיניו אפילו פינו ממנו הצואה ואין בו ריח רע ולכן צריך ליזהר שלא לקרות ולהתפלל ולדבר בד"ת בחצר כנגד בית הכסא כמלא עיניו אע"פ שהוא סגור ואי אפשר לראות הצואה שבו ולכן נכון ליזהר כשעושה סוכה בחצר שיעשה בענין שלא יהיה מכוון מחיצות בה"כ נגד פתח הסוכה כשהיא פתוח דאז יהיה צריך ליזהר בזה בעת שהוא מדבר ד"ת ושאר דברי קדושה וכן אם יש נגד חלון ביתו בית הכסא והחלון פתוח והוא עומד בביתו ופניו כלפי בית הכסא נכון ליזהר מלדבר אז דברי תורה ומ"מ לעת הדחק יש לסמוך להקל אם אין מגיע לו ריח רע דאיכא ס"ס [היינו הב"י והב"ח בסימן זה דמתירין במחיצות גבוהות עשרה והרא"ש בסימן ע"ט ס"ב דמתיר אפילו בצואה כשהוא ברשות אחרת אף דרואה אותה] ובעוצם עיניו לכו"ע מהני בכה"ג. עוד כתבו האחרונים דמה דאנו מחמירין במחיצות בה"כ לדונם מבחוץ כצואה דוקא במחיצות שאינם נעשים אלא בשביל בית הכסא כגון שעשה בחצר חדר מיוחד לזה אבל אם בנה במחיצה של בית שהסמיכה לאותה מחיצה אע"פ שאותה מחיצה הוא מחיצה של בה"כ ממש אין על אותה מחיצה דין בה"כ לענין זה ומותר לקרות אפילו בסמוך כיון שאין המחיצה מיוחדת לבה"כ לבד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(5) Does not reach him.</b> If a foul odor reaches him, it is forbidden until he distances himself four amos from the place where the odor ends. According to the Mechaber, that distance suffices even when the privy is in front of him. Know that the Mechaber permits this even when the partitions are not ten tefachim high, as stated in Beit Yosef, but all the later authorities disagree with him in that case. When the partitions are ten tefachim high, some later authorities agree with him, while many others disagree even then. Their reason is that since the partitions are designated to contain excrement, they are judged like excrement itself, like a chamber pot below in siman 87, which is forbidden even when it has no foul odor. Gra's commentary likewise implies that he rules stringently. Peri Megadim, Derech HaChaim, and many other recent authorities are all stringent as well. They hold that one must distance himself four amos from the partitions, and when they are in front of him, as far as he can see, even if the excrement has been removed and there is no foul odor. One must therefore take care not to recite Keriat Shema, pray, or speak words of Torah in a courtyard while facing a privy within his line of sight, even if it is closed and the excrement inside cannot be seen. Therefore, when building a sukkah in a courtyard, it is proper to ensure that the privy's walls are not directly opposite the open entrance of the sukkah, for he would then have to observe this restriction while speaking words of Torah or other sacred words. Likewise, if a privy is opposite a window of one's house, the window is open, and he stands inside facing the privy, it is proper to refrain from speaking words of Torah then. Nevertheless, in pressing circumstances one may rely on the lenient view when no foul odor reaches him, because there is a double doubt: Beit Yosef and Bach in this siman permit partitions ten tefachim high, and Rosh in siman 79, seif 2, permits even excrement when it is in a different domain although one sees it. Closing one's eyes is effective according to all opinions in such a case. The later authorities further write that our stringency of judging privy walls externally like excrement applies specifically to walls made solely for the privy, such as a room in a courtyard built especially for that purpose. If, however, one built a house wall adjacent to that wall, then even though the wall itself is actually a wall of the privy, it does not have the status of a privy wall for this law, because it is not designated solely for the privy. It is therefore permitted to recite even adjacent to it.

(9) <b>But not inside it</b> - Meaning, if that place has no partitions, only the specific area that he designated is forbidden. If the designated sitting area is enclosed by partitions, the designation forbids the entire enclosed area within the partitions. See nearby, seif katan 7. Outside it, however, it is permitted even adjacent to it. Baer Heitev writes that it is permitted to think words of Torah inside a new privy that has not yet been used, and Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise. It is obvious that builders constructing a privy may not recite any blessing inside the area of the partitions, even before the partitions have been built, because once the place has been marked out for them, that area has been set aside as a privy and is forbidden.

**** ENGLISH ****
(12) <b>But if they urinate</b> - Meaning, even when no urine is presently there and there is no foul odor, because the area outside the pit where people urinate has the designation of a privy. Taz writes that the partitions of this privy nevertheless do not have the law of privy partitions, requiring distance from the area opposite them, as explained above in seif katan 5. The Torah prohibition concerning urine applies only directly opposite the stream; after it falls to the ground, its prohibition is only D'Rabbanan. These partitions are therefore not considered privy partitions on that account. It appears, however, that in places where a special public urinal is made, and it is known that those places and their walls smell extremely foul, the walls also have the full status of a privy. This is comparable to a urine receptacle, which many poskim explain in siman 87 is prohibited D'Oraisa. Although urine itself is only D'Rabbanan, because the receptacle is designated for it and becomes especially foul, it is more severe; the same applies here. See Peri Megadim in siman 79, who maintains that foul-smelling urine is prohibited D'Oraisa.

**** ENGLISH ****
(13) <b>Krias Shema opposite it</b> - He maintains that it does not have the law of an old privy, which we rule is forbidden in front of a person as far as he can see even after the excrement has been removed. Rather, it has only the law of a house designated as a privy, explained in seif 2, because excrement was never upon the chair itself and the chamber pot stands beneath it only when needed. Many later authorities disagree and maintain that it has the law of an old privy, since people relieve themselves upon it. Therefore, one who wishes to recite Krias Shema, pray, or speak words of holiness must be careful to remove the chair from the house, place it four amos behind him, or cover the entire chair with a garment or another object. This applies even when it is clean, has no foul odor, the chamber pot is not beneath it, and the opening above is covered by a board. One must likewise be careful in homes containing small perforated chairs for the children, covering them with a garment during Krias Shema or Torah study even when they contain no excrement, or removing them from the room in which words of holiness are spoken. This is especially so because a child commonly soils the chair while relieving himself, in which case all agree that it has the designation of a privy even when clean. Derech HaChaim writes regarding a perforated bench that if the bench is principally designated for sitting, like a chair used for ordinary sitting and covered with a cushion, and when needed one removes the cushion and relieves himself into a vessel placed beneath it, one may recite and pray while it is covered by the cushion, even if it is not otherwise covered by another object, because it has no designation as a privy. See there for his reasoning. See Biur Halachah for what we wrote further on this matter.
