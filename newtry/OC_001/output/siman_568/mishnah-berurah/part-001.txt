<b>(20) To Fast, etc.</b> And if day 31 of his son's birth falls on Shabbos, so that he must do the redemption on Sunday, which is a pidyon not in its time: Magen Avraham is in doubt whether it is a seudas mitzvah and it is permitted to eat when it falls during the Ten Days of Repentance or not (and it is not comparable to a bris milah, which is a seudas mitzvah even not in its time; for there every moment is its time so that he not remain uncircumcised; which is not so regarding pidyon haben). And therefore he leans that he should make the meal afterwards at night. [And in Dagul Merevavah he wrote that it is better that he do the redemption and the meal on the night of Motzaei Shabbos; see there.] And the Rama wrote that all this is specifically in such a case; but on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah. <b>(21) And if he accepted upon himself, etc.</b> That is to say: all this is specifically when he did not accept the Fast upon himself, but only relied on the custom, or answered Amen when they bless Mondays and Thursdays; for then he did not accept it upon himself as a vow, and if he wishes not to Fast he is permitted. And the view of Magen Avraham and some later authorities is that even when he accepted upon himself at Minchah it is permitted, since his acceptance was for the sake of the custom; and the Rama is dealing with one who does not Fast all Mondays and Thursdays or all of the Ten Days of Repentance, but fasts only one day or two days; and it turns out that this one must by law accept at Minchah, and his law is like one who fasts in the middle of the year, for it does not depend on custom; and therefore he is obligated to Fast. And behold, from the Gra it is implied that he explains like the Levush; and nevertheless it appears that in this he may borrow and repay another Fast; for even without this there are several Rishonim who hold that even when he says, "Behold I accept to Fast tomorrow," he may borrow and repay.
