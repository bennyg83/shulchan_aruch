**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is a mitzvah to eat.</b> It appears to me that if he knew before Rosh Hashanah that he will eat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at a bris milah, he is obligated to fast one more day before Rosh Hashanah; for for that reason they fast four days before Rosh Hashanah corresponding to the four days on which they eat; and all the more so if a milah occurs in the four days before Rosh Hashanah, that they fast one day corresponding to it. And if he fasts until after the congregation leaves the synagogue, it is called a fast, as is written in siman 562, se'if 2; but not earlier than that, for originally they accepted upon themselves thus. And it is obvious that when a pidyon haben or other seudos mitzvah fall on Mondays and Thursdays or during the Ten Days, it is likewise a mitzvah to eat.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For they were not accustomed.</b> But on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah; and so it is implied in siman 572, se'if 2. And see what I wrote in siman 559, and in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, in the Shach, s.k. 12, who wrote: if a pidyon haben falls on a communal fast day, they practice to redeem on the day of the fast and to make the meal at night; end quote. And this requires further study: if day 31 falls on Shabbos and Sunday is a fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, whether they are permitted to eat; for Tosafos wrote in Moed Katan at the end of folio 8 that milah is permitted to be done during chol hamoed since its time is fixed, and one should not cancel this because of that; but a meal of pidyon haben requires further study; and one should not say: behold its time is fixed; that is fine in its time; not in its time, how would it be permitted; end quote. And it is difficult: do we not also find milah not in its time? Rather, necessarily milah, even though its time has passed, every moment is its time, for it is forbidden to remain uncircumcised; but pidyon haben, once its time has passed, they can postpone it further, as appears to me according to the view of Tosafos; and if so one may say it is not called a seudas mitzvah; therefore it appears to me that he should make the meal at night; but when it is in its time it is permitted to eat there. And this requires further study regarding when they fast the Twentieth of Sivan in the kingdom of Poland, whether it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah. However, in our times for the most part they make all the meals at night; however, once the Twentieth of Sivan fell on Erev Shabbos and there was a meal of a bris milah, and the rav ordered [them] to eat by day because people would not be found who would go at night. And it is obvious that in a place where it is permitted to eat at the meal, it is not a fast at all, and afterwards it is permitted to eat and drink even in his house; however, before the meal it appears to me that it is forbidden to eat and drink in his house; and so it is implied from what I wrote above in the name of Terumas HaDeshen. However, the baalei bris are permitted to eat immediately, for it is their festival.

And the reason is: even though he answered Amen he is not obligated to fast; therefore it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah; and even if he began to fast and did not know that they would invite him to a meal, nevertheless it is permitted. But it appears to me another explanation: for this is the wording of Rabbeinu Beis Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos: but if he accepted at Minchah on one of the Days of Repentance, he needs annulment; end quote. And it is difficult: he should have said simply, if he accepted at Minchah he needs annulment, for it also refers to Mondays and Thursdays; rather this is the meaning: if they all fast because of the custom, then even though he accepted the fast upon himself at Minchah it is permitted to eat, for he fasts with the intention of the custom; but if he fasts on one of the Days of Repentance, for then he is not fasting because of the custom but only accepted upon himself now to fast one day, it is forbidden to eat, for it is like one who fasts in the middle of the year, as appears to me. And see siman 562.
