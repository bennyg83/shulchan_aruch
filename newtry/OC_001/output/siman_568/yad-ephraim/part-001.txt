י"ל כמ"ש ואם מי שעושה הסעודה לשמוח שזכה מאת ה' לבית וחינוכו לש"ש הוי קצת סעודת מצוה ומ"מ לענין נדר נראה דאין להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Magen Avraham, s.k. 5.</b> That chanukas habayis in Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah, but not in chutz la'aretz, etc. See in Tashbetz Katan, siman 170, in the laws of Chanukah, where he wrote: and Maharam of blessed memory says specifically to praise and give thanks, but not for feasting and rejoicing, and all the more so for chanukas habayis, which is optional; end quote. And it implies even in Eretz Yisrael. And see below siman 609, and what is written in the name of Yam Shel Shlomo; see there, where he wrote as follows: but meals of inaugurating the house are certainly a path of friendship, especially in our many sins; and so I found that Maharam ruled. Nevertheless it appears that one who fears God and wishes to inaugurate his house with Torah, etc.; see there. And in my humble opinion Maharam [holds] according to his reason, that a seudas mitzvah is like there, when they are the days of the dedication of the altar; however Maharshal there rules that a Chanukah meal is a seudas mitzvah, for they established a festival because of the dedication of the altar; see there. (And see above siman 444, that a meal of sivlonos on the night before the chuppah: even the meal they make a long time before the wedding when they send sivlonos, called a meal of kinyan, is a seudas mitzvah. And see above siman 558 and below siman 640 regarding the meal on the night before the milah; see there.) And one may also distinguish: even if we say that a Chanukah meal is not a seudas mitzvah according to Maharam's view, nevertheless that is because it is only a remembrance of what was in ancient times, and all the more so now that it is destroyed in our many sins; and even though they established to make Chanukah days each year as a remembrance, they were established only to give thanks and to praise. Which is not so regarding one who inaugurates his house now and makes a feast in the joy of his heart that Hashem repaid him and he merited to build himself a house: it is possible that it is a seudas mitzvah. But Maharam wrote "and all the more so for inaugurating a house," and it appears that for that reason Maharshal decided that nevertheless one who fears Hashem and wishes to inaugurate his house with Torah, etc. And even though Maharam learned an a fortiori from Chanukah, and there is no greater inauguration-mitzvah than that, nevertheless this is not comparable to there, for there it is a remembrance of the past; which is not so here, where now his heart is toward Heaven, and a sign of the matter is that he inaugurates his house with Torah. And all the more so according to what Maharshal holds, that a Chanukah meal is a seudas mitzvah; and at the building of Shlomo he made a feast of seven days and they did not fast on Yom Kippur, as is stated in Moed Katan. And even though one should not compare to the inauguration of the Beis HaMikdash, nevertheless there is some support to say that a meal of inaugurating a house for the sake of a mitzvah is somewhat a seudas mitzvah regarding being able to borrow a fast that is not fixed. And it is stated in the Yerushalmi of Sotah, chapter "Mashuach milchamah": Can one who builds a house in chutz la'aretz return [from war]? Scripture teaches "and he has not inaugurated it": that which it is a mitzvah to inaugurate, excluding this which it is not a mitzvah to inaugurate. And Korban HaEdah wrote there: from here we learn that a meal of inaugurating a house is not a mitzvah, and one who vowed not to eat from a meal that is not of a mitzvah is forbidden to eat. However in the Bavli in our chapter it expounds "and he has not inaugurated it" to exclude a robber; but in the Rambam he wrote both, etc.; see there. And possibly this is specifically at a time when Israel was dwelling [in the land], then there is no mitzvah in chutz la'aretz, for they said whoever dwells in chutz la'aretz is like, etc.; which is not so in this time, for R' Yehudah said all who ascend from Babylonia, etc., and the view of several poskim is that in any event there is no mitzvah to ascend to Eretz Yisrael: one may properly say as I wrote. And if one who makes the meal to rejoice that he merited from Hashem a house, and his inauguration is for the sake of Heaven, it is somewhat a seudas mitzvah; nevertheless regarding a vow it appears that one should not be lenient.

<b>בט"ז ס"ק ד' </b>שאחר כלות י"ב חודש מותרין גם כו' בת"ה שם נסתפק אי אמרינן באבילות יב"ח מקצת יום ככולו ורצה מתחלה לומר דאמרינן ושוב חזר לומר דלא אמרינן וצריך להמתין עד שיכנס ניסן וע"ז כ' שהעולם נוהגין דאפי' בר"ח כו' ור"ל דלפי"ז לכאורה אין נ"מ בדין מקצת היום כיון שנוהגין אבילות אף אחר כלות יב"ח וע"ז אמר ואפשר משום דאמרינן קדיש כו' אבל מן הדין א"צ כו' ר"ל א"כ שפיר נ"מ בשאלה זו ועכ"פ לפי הדין אם צריך להמתין עד שיכנס ניסן או מקצתו סגי דמקצתו ככולו וס"ל לפסוק דצריך להמתין אבל לפי המנהג אין נ"מ שהרי הם ממשיכים האבילות עד יום א' אחרי כלות היב"ח לגמרי ושוב כ' דגם נ"מ למי שמתענה כו' דגם לפי המנהג יש נ"מ בפסק זה דלא אמרינן מקצתו ככולו כגון מי שנוהג להתענות ביום המיתה (ר"ל דשם בסי' רצ"ג כ' שיש נוהגין להתענות ביום המיתה וגם ביום הקבורה) ואומר קדיש כו' והא נקבר למחר ר"ל ביום ב' שאחריו כגון אם מת בר"ח ניסן ונקבר בג' בניסן שאז היא"צ הוא ר"ח ניסן והשלמת האבילות דמנינן ליה מיום קבורה ביום ב' בניסן שאז שלמו היב"ח ובזה יש נ"מ בפסק זה דלא סגי ליה במקצת יום ב' בניסן אלא שלמו לו הי"ב חודש שמיום קבורה עד ג' בניסן ומ"ש בתה"ד ונקבר למחר הוא לאו דוקא אלא ר"ל ביום שלאחר יום המיתה כן מבואר למעיין בתה"ד בהמשך דבריו שם ומעתה מ"ש הט"ז אל תטעה ללמוד מזה כו' אלא דשם בתה"ד לא הוי שום הפסק כו' דבריו תמוהים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In the Taz, s.k. 4.</b> That after twelve months are completed they are permitted, also, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen there he was in doubt whether we say regarding twelve-month mourning that part of the day is like the whole; and at first he wished to say that we do say so, and afterwards he retracted to say that we do not say so, and one must wait until Nisan enters; and upon this he wrote that the world practices that even on Rosh Chodesh, etc. And that is to say: according to this, at first glance there is no practical difference in the law of part of the day, since they practice mourning even after twelve months are completed; and upon this he said: and possibly because they say Kaddish, etc.; but by law one need not, etc. That is to say: if so, there is properly a practical difference in this question; and in any event according to the law, whether one must wait until Nisan enters or part of it suffices because part of it is like the whole; and he holds to rule that one must wait; but according to the custom there is no practical difference, for they extend mourning until one day after twelve months are completed entirely. And afterwards he wrote that there is also a practical difference for one who fasts, etc.; that even according to the custom there is a practical difference in this ruling that we do not say part of it is like the whole: for example, one who is accustomed to fast on the day of death (that is to say, that there in siman 293 he wrote that there are those who practice fasting on the day of death and also on the day of burial) and says Kaddish, etc., and he was buried the next day, that is to say on the second day after it: for example, if he died on Rosh Chodesh Nisan and was buried on the 3rd of Nisan, then the yahrzeit is Rosh Chodesh Nisan, and the completion of mourning that we count from the day of burial is on the 2nd of Nisan, when twelve months were completed; and in this there is a practical difference in this ruling, that it does not suffice for him with part of the 2nd of Nisan, but rather the twelve months from the day of burial are completed for him until the 3rd of Nisan. And what Terumas HaDeshen wrote "and he was buried the next day" is not precise, but rather means on the day after the day of death; so it is clear to one who examines Terumas HaDeshen in the continuation of his words there. And from now on, what the Taz wrote "Do not err to learn from this," etc., "but rather there in Terumas HaDeshen there was no interruption," etc.: his words are puzzling.
