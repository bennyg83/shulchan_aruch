<b>Monday.</b> It is difficult for me: here he said that in this case one cannot borrow even when distressed, for it is "this day"; and above, siman 552, se'if 11, if Erev Tishah B'Av falls then, he borrows and repays. And in truth there is a dispute among the poskim whether we say even regarding "this day" that one borrows and repays. And one must say that that [ruling] above in siman 552 is not the law, but rather specifically where he fasts up to a large measure of the day and afterwards borrows and repays, as I wrote above; also here it avails if they fast part of the day and afterwards borrow and repay, even on "this day." <b>מצוה. </b> נ"ל דאם יודע לפני ר"ה שיאכל בין ר"ה לי"כ על ברית מילה מחויב להתענות עוד יום אחד לפני ר"ה דהא מה"ט מתענין ד' ימים לפני ר"ה נגד ד' ימים שאוכלין וכ"ש אם אירע מילה בד' ימים שלפני ר"ה שמתענין יום א' נגדו מיהו נ"ל אם ר"ה הוא ביום ה' דליכא אלא ד' ימים לפני ר"ה אין צריך להתענות יום אחד קודם שבת דמעיקרא לא קיבלו עלייהו אלא להתענות בימי הסליחות ולא בימים האחרים. ופשוט דכשחל פדיון הבן או שאר סעודות מצוה בבה"ב או ביו"ד ימים ג"כ מצוה לאכול. אבל בשאר תענית שגוזרים הצבור אסור לאכול אפי' בסעודת מצוה. ואם חל פדיון הבן בתענית ציבור נוהגים לפדות הבן ביום התענית ולעשות הסעודה בלילה ש"ך בי"ד סימן ש"ה ס"ק י"ב וכתב המ"א אם חל יום ל"א בשבת וביום א' הוא תענית עשרת ימי תשובה מאחר דהוי מצוה שלא בזמנו ויוכל לדחותו יותר לא מיקרי סעודת מצוה ויעשה הסעודה בלילה אבל בזמנה מותר לאכול כמ"ש. וצ"ע כשמתענין כ' סיון אם מותר לאכול על סעודת מצוה מיהו פעם אחד חל בערב שבת והיה מילה וציוה הרב לאכול מפני שלא ימצאו אנשים שילכו בלילה. ופשוט דבמקום דמותר לאכול על הסעוד' אינו תענית כלל ומותר אחר כך לאכול ולשתות אפי' בביתו מיהו קודם הסעוד' נראה לי דאסור לאכול בביתו ומיהו הבעלי ברית מותרים לאכול מיד די"ט שלהם הוא מ"א וע' סי' תקנ"ט סעיף קטן ט"ו מש"ש ע' מ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A mitzvah.</b> It appears to me that if he knows before Rosh Hashanah that he will eat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at a bris milah, he is obligated to fast one more day before Rosh Hashanah; for for that reason they fast four days before Rosh Hashanah corresponding to the four days on which they eat; and all the more so if a milah occurs in the four days before Rosh Hashanah, that they fast one day corresponding to it. However, it appears to me that if Rosh Hashanah is on Thursday, so that there are only four days before Rosh Hashanah, he need not fast one day before Shabbos; for originally they accepted upon themselves only to fast during the days of Selichos and not on the other days. And it is obvious that when a pidyon haben or other seudos mitzvah fall on Mondays and Thursdays or on the Ten Days, it is likewise a mitzvah to eat. But on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah. And if a pidyon haben falls on a communal fast, the practice is to redeem the son on the day of the fast and to make the meal at night (Shach in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, s.k. 12). And the Magen Avraham wrote: if day 31 falls on Shabbos and Sunday is a fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, since it is a mitzvah not in its time and one can postpone it further, it is not called a seudas mitzvah, and he should make the meal at night; but in its time it is permitted to eat, as I wrote. And this requires further study regarding when they fast on the Twentieth of Sivan, whether it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah; however, once it fell on Erev Shabbos and there was a milah, and the rav ordered [them] to eat because people would not be found to go at night. And it is obvious that in a place where it is permitted to eat at the meal, it is not a fast at all, and afterwards it is permitted to eat and drink even in his house; however, before the meal it appears to me that it is forbidden to eat in his house; however, the baalei bris are permitted to eat immediately, for it is their festival. Magen Avraham. And see siman 559, s.k. 15, what is written there; see Magen Avraham.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Adar.</b> One whose father died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and the following year Cheshvan is short and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day: this requires further study when he should fast; whether on the 29th of Cheshvan, which is the day before Rosh Chodesh Kislev, for always the second day is primary, since we count the moados from the second; or we say that in any event the name of Rosh Chodesh is upon it. And it appears to me that if the first year was short, he should fix it on the 29th of Cheshvan, for the thirtieth day is counted to the past month; but when the first year was also full, then he must fix the day on Rosh Chodesh, and therefore even in the years that follow after them, even if they are short, he should fix it on Rosh Chodesh, for it is like a vow, in which we follow the language of people. And if he died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar, he should fast the following year on the first day of Rosh Chodesh of first Adar, and not on the 29th of it; for always first Adar stands in place of second Adar.
