<b>א) [סעיף א'] ברכת התורה צריך ליזהר בה מאד.  </b>כדאיתא בנדרים דף פ"א מפני מה אין מצויין ת"ח לצאת ת"ח מבניהן רבינא אמר שאין מברכין בתורה תחילה וכו' ופי' שם המפרש שאין מברכין כשמשכימין לתלמוד תורה. ותלמידי חכמים מתוך שהן זהירין לעסוק בתורה ורגילין בה אינן זהירין לברך כשפותחין ולא מקיימא ברכתא נהיה אנחנו וצאצאינו מלומדי תורה עכ"ל. ועיין טור וב"י. וז"ל הלבוש מאד מאד צריך האדם ליזהר בה להראות שהתורה היא חשובה בעיניו ונהנה ממנה כמו שמברך על כל הנאותיו. ואם אינו מברך עליה אינו זוכה לבן ת"ח שזה עונשו מדה במדה שמאחר שאינו מברך עליה מורה שאינו קורא בה לשמה ואינו משמח עמה ולא נהנה בה אלא היה בעיניו כאומנות בעלמא לפיכך אינו זוכה לשלשלת הנמשך לעוסק בה לשמה דכתיב לא ימושו מפיך וכו' עכ"ל ומהעיקר הזהירות הוא שצריך להבין סי' המילות ולאמרה בנחת מילה במילה שלא יחסר שום תיבה או אות כמש"ל סי' ה' אות ב'. וגם צריך לאומרה בחשק נמרץ וישים אל לבו אשר הוא מברך על התורה אשר היא חיינו ואורך ימינו בעוה"ז ובעוה"ב:

<b>1) [Se'if 1] One must be exceedingly careful with Birchos HaTorah.</b> Nedarim 81a asks: Why is it uncommon for talmidei chachamim to have sons who are talmidei chachamim? Ravina said: Because they do not first recite a berachah over the Torah, etc. The commentator there explains that they do not recite a berachah when they rise early to study Torah. Because talmidei chachamim are diligent in Torah study and accustomed to it, they are not careful to recite the berachah when they begin, and the blessing 'May we and our descendants be students of Torah' is not fulfilled. See Tur and Beis Yosef. Levush writes: A person must be exceedingly careful with this berachah, thereby demonstrating that Torah is precious in his eyes and that he derives pleasure from it, just as he recites a berachah over all his pleasures. If he does not recite a berachah over Torah, he does not merit a son who is a talmid chacham. This punishment is measure for measure: since he does not bless over it, he demonstrates that he does not study it for its own sake, does not rejoice in it, and derives no pleasure from it, but regards it as an ordinary trade. He therefore does not merit the continuing chain promised to one who studies it for its own sake, as it is written, 'They shall not depart from your mouth,' etc. The principal care required is to understand the meaning of the words and recite the berachah slowly, word by word, omitting no word or letter, as stated above in siman 5:2. One must also say it with intense desire and take to heart that he is blessing over the Torah, which is our life and the length of our days in this world and in the World to Come.

<b>ב) שם.  </b>ברכת התורה צריך וכו' בענין ברכת התורה אם היה דאורייתא אי דרבנן. ונפקא מינה דאם היא דאורייתא אם נסתפק לו אם בירך או לא צריך לברך ואת היא דרבנן א"צ לברך וכמ"ש מרן ז"ל בסי' ר"ט סעי' ג' יעו"ש. הנה בדבר זה נחלקו הפוסקים דהרב שאגת אריה סי' כ"ד ס"ל דהיא דאורייתא ובספק בירך ספק לא בירך חוזר ומברך יעו"ש. וכן ס"ל להמו"ק. וכן הסכים הפר"ח ע"ז הסעיף. והב"ד יד אהרן בהגה"ט וכן דעת מהר"א הלוי בס' זקן אהרן סי' ס' יעו"ש אמנם הרב פני משה בתשו' ח"א סי' א' כתב דמדברי הרמב"ם והטור דסתמו לן סתומי ולא כתבו דהוי דאורייתא משמע דס"ל דהוי דרבנן יעו"ש. וכן משמע מדברי מרן ז"ל לקמן סי' ר"ט סעי' ג' שכתב וז"ל בכל הברכות אם נסתפק אם בירך אה לאו אינו מברך וכו' חוץ מברכת המזון שהיא של תורה עכ"ל. מדקאמר חוץ מברה"מ משמע דברכת התורה היא בכלל ברכות דרבנן. וכ"מ מדברי האגור שהביא ב"י בזה הסי' יעו"ש. וכ"מ מדברי הלבוש סעי' א' שכתב ואסמכוה אקרא וכו' ומסעי' י"ב שכתב דברכה אינה מעכבת יעו"ש משמע דס"ל שהיא דרבנן. וכן ס"ל להעו"ת שכתב בסעי' ט' דאם הפסיק בין ברכה ללימוד שאין לו לחזור ולברך דברכות דרבנן. הרי לך בהדיא דס"ל דברכת התורה דרבנן. וכן הסכים מהר"י עייאש בס' מטה יהודה אות א' שכתב דאם נסתפק אם בירך ברכת התורה דלא יברך יעו"ש. ועי"ש במ"ש על דברי הפר"ח, ועוד עיין להחיד"א בברכ"י אות ח' שדחה ראיית הפר"ח. ועוד לו במחב"ר אות א' ואות ב' והסכים שאין לברך מספק משום דכלל גדול הוא ספק ברכות להקל יעו"ש, והב"ד הרב המגיה בש"ץ דנ"ו ע"א, וכן פסק בקש"ג סי' ה' אות כ"ה, סידור בי"ע בדיני ברכת השחר אות כ"ב. וכן דעת המאמ"ר אות א' אלא שכתב דאם נסתפק יכוין לפטור אותה באהבת עולם יעו"ש, וכן דעת כס"א ויפה ללב אות א', וכתב ודלא כמ"ש באה"ט בשם הפר"ח והיד אהרן יעו"ש, ומ"מ הנכון הוא מי שנסתפק לו אם בירך ברכת התורה אם לאו ישתדל לשמוע הברכה מאחר שעדיין לא יצא ויתכוין לצאת וגם יאמר לו להמברך שיתכוין להוציאו כידוע או יברך בלא שם ומלכות וכשיגיע לברכת אהבת עולם יתכוין לצאת בה נמי ברה"ת כדי לצאת ידי המחמירים, ועיין מש"ל סי' מ"ו אות נ"ב:

<b>2) There: Birchos HaTorah require, etc.</b> There is a dispute whether Birchos HaTorah are a Torah obligation or a rabbinic obligation. The practical difference is that, if they are a Torah obligation, one who is uncertain whether he recited them must recite them; if they are rabbinic, he need not do so, as Maran writes in siman 209:3; see there. The poskim dispute this matter. Shaagas Aryeh 24 holds that the obligation is from the Torah, and one who is uncertain whether he recited the berachah must recite it; see there. Mor U'Ketziah rules likewise, and Pri Chadash agrees in his comments to this se'if. Yad Aharon cites this in Hagahos HaTur. This is also the view of Rabbi Aharon HaLevi in Zekan Aharon 60; see there. Pnei Moshe, Responsa 1:1, however, writes that Rambam and Tur state the law without saying it is a Torah obligation, implying that they consider it rabbinic; see there. This is also implied by Maran below, siman 209:3: 'Regarding all berachos, if one is uncertain whether he recited the berachah, he does not recite it, etc., except Birkas HaMazon, which is from the Torah.' By singling out Birkas HaMazon, he implies that Birchos HaTorah are included among the rabbinic berachos. This is also implied by Agur, cited by Beis Yosef in this siman. Levush 1 writes that the Sages attached it to a verse, and in se'if 12 that omission of the berachah does not prevent fulfillment, implying that he considers it rabbinic. Olas Tamid likewise writes in se'if 9 that if one interrupted between the berachah and learning, he does not repeat the berachah because berachos are rabbinic. Thus, he states explicitly that Birchos HaTorah are rabbinic. Mahari Ayash agrees in Mateh Yehudah 1, writing that one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah should not recite them; see there and his comments on Pri Chadash. See also Chida, Birkei Yosef 8, who rejects Pri Chadash's proof, and Machazik Berachah 1–2, where he agrees that one should not recite the berachah in a case of doubt, because the great principle is that doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently. The glossator to Shalmei Tzibbur 56a cites him. Kesher Gudal 5:25 and Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of Birchos HaShachar 22, rule likewise. Maamar Mordechai 1 also agrees, but writes that one who is uncertain should intend that Ahavas Olam exempt the obligation; see there. This is also the view of Kisei Eliyahu and Yafeh LaLev 1, who write that this is unlike Be'er Heitev in the names of Pri Chadash and Yad Aharon; see there. Nevertheless, the proper course for one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah is to endeavor to hear the berachah from someone who has not yet fulfilled his obligation. He should intend to fulfill his obligation, and tell the person reciting it to intend to discharge it for him, as is known. Alternatively, he may recite it without God's Name and kingship; and when he reaches Ahavas Olam, he should intend that it also discharge Birchos HaTorah, thereby satisfying the stringent opinions. See what was stated above in siman 46:52.

<b>2) Know</b> that our statement that one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah does not repeat them applies whether he is uncertain about all of them or only some of them; he does not recite whichever berachah is in doubt. Pnei Moshe writes, as Pri Chadash cites there, that even according to those who consider Birchos HaTorah a Torah obligation, only one berachah is required by the Torah. Shaagas Aryeh 25 likewise writes that one berachah (whichever it may be) is a Torah obligation, though he inclines toward reciting 'Who chose us'; see there. Accordingly, for us, who rule in accordance with those who consider the obligation rabbinic because doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently, he does not repeat any berachah about which he is uncertain, whether all the berachos or only some. Pri Chadash also holds that the berachah recited over the public Torah reading is rabbinic, as he writes in siman 139:8; see there. Birkei Yosef 8 in this siman writes likewise.

<b>3) [Se'if 2] One must recite the berachah whether studying Scripture or Mishnah, etc.</b> For all of them are called Torah. Levush. The same is true according to the Zohar and Kabbalah, and it is obvious. The kabbalists write that these four categories (Scripture, Mishnah, Gemara, and Kabbalah) correspond to the four worlds of Atzilus, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah. See the newly printed Nehar Shalom, page 37b. It is known that through the berachah one draws down an encompassing light, and through performance of the mitzvah an inner light, as explained in Etz Chaim, gate 40, derush 2; Shaar HaKavanos, derushim 11–12 concerning Sefiras HaOmer; and many other places. It is also known that all the sparks clarified and mochin drawn down through a berachah and performance of a mitzvah pertain specifically to that berachah and mitzvah. Since Scripture, Mishnah, Gemara, and Kabbalah correspond to four distinct aspects, and one recites the morning berachah over everything he will study that day, possibly including all four, he must therefore intend at the time of the berachah to include them all. The clarifications and the drawing down of mochin appropriate to each will thereby occur. Understand this well.

<b>5) [Se'if 3] One who writes words of Torah must recite the berachah even if he does not read them.</b> Taz 2 questions this ruling: How does writing differ from thought, regarding which the next se'if rules that no berachah is recited because thought is not equivalent to speech, as Beis Yosef writes at the end of this siman? Writing, too, involves only thought. He concludes that 'one who writes words of Torah' means one who writes or copies them intending to learn; such a person properly recites the berachah. A scribe who copies a book merely as paid work, however, should not recite a berachah beforehand; see there. Magen Avraham 1 writes similarly, but concludes that even one who writes as a mode of study requires further consideration, for it is nevertheless only thought; see there. Solel Boneh 2 and Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, rule likewise. Levush 4, however, explains that a writer recites the berachah even though he does not read because he performs an action through writing. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 2, writes likewise; see there. According to this, even if he does not intend to study what he writes, he must recite a berachah because he performs an act of writing. Mateh Yehudah 2 similarly writes that the unqualified language of Shulchan Aruch implies no distinction: whether one writes to study or copies for pay, writing is equivalent to speech and requires a berachah; see there. Chayei Adam 9:11 explains that a writer ordinarily articulates what he writes and might utter it unintentionally. He writes that this applies all the more to one writing a Sefer Torah, tefillin, or mezuzos, who is obligated to articulate the words and therefore must recite the berachah. Amar Shmuel 1:2 likewise writes that one may not write sacred scrolls before reciting Birchos HaTorah, because he is obligated to articulate what he writes; see there. In practice, to avoid every doubt, both a scribe writing sacred scrolls and a scribe copying a book as a mode of study, or anyone writing words of Torah, should first recite Birchos HaTorah and then read the passage of Birkas Kohanim, as is customary. His berachah will then primarily apply to Birkas Kohanim, after which he may write whatever words of Torah he wishes. One who listens to words of Torah from others should do likewise: he should recite Birchos HaTorah, read the passage of Birkas Kohanim, and then listen, because this matter is disputed. See Machazik Berachah 4 and Erech HaShulchan 2. If, however, one merely writes several words from verses in a friendly letter for elegance of expression, no berachah is required, as Taz and Magen Avraham write there. See above, siman 46:56, and below, paragraph 7.
