<small>Taz, seif katan 6.</small> Asher Bachar Banu is the principal blessing and exempts Torah study even if one does not study at all immediately after reciting it. Its effect continues until the next morning.

This is the basis of our custom not to study immediately after Birchas HaTorah and not immediately after Ahavah Rabbah.

Taz then asks why this differs from sukkah, where one must recite a blessing for every meal. Here too, one might have required a blessing each time he studies, and all the more so immediate study after the blessing.

This is the sequence of his argument.

<small>There.</small> “For according to Rabbeinu Tam.”

Meaning, Rabbeinu Tam disagrees with seif 13, which says that one who rises before daylight to study recites Birchas HaTorah.

Rabbeinu Tam holds that even nighttime sleep is not an interruption. Nevertheless, when daylight arrives one recites the blessing. This shows that the arrival of a new day itself creates a new obligation.

Likewise, according to our view that nighttime sleep is an interruption, we may still hold that the new day independently creates a new obligation.
