**** ENGLISH ****
Before beginning the explanation of the Shulchan Aruch, we will cite the baraita stated on this subject in the Gemara, Shabbos 19a: “The Sages taught: One may not embark on a ship fewer than three days before Shabbos. When does this apply? For a discretionary purpose. For the sake of a mitzvah, however, it is permitted, and he stipulates with the shipmaster that he rest on Shabbos, although the shipmaster does not rest; these are the words of Rabbi. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that no stipulation is necessary. From Tyre to Sidon, which is a journey of one day, it is permitted even on Friday.” This is the language of the baraita. The authorities offer many explanations for this prohibition. Some say that the concern is that the Jew may have to perform labor himself on Shabbos because of danger to life, since sea travel is dangerous, or that the non-Jewish shipowner may perform labor for him by adjusting the sail and through other Shabbos desecrations. Others explain that the concern is the prohibition of techumin, as the Beit Yosef discusses at length. The Rif maintains that if the concern were labor or techumin, departure would certainly be forbidden even more than three days beforehand, and it would not be permitted even for a mitzvah. Rather, the principal prohibition is the loss of Shabbos enjoyment: during the first three days the passengers suffer from the ship's motion, which causes bodily distress, as the verse states, “They reel and stagger like a drunkard,” and from the foul odor arising from salt water. Their equilibrium does not return until after three days, once they have become accustomed to it, as stated in seif 2. Others say that embarking was forbidden because it appears as though one is floating and swimming on the water on Shabbos. This is forbidden according to all opinions because of the decree lest one make a swimmer's barrel, as explained below in siman 339. Departure more than three days beforehand is permitted because beginning the voyage so much earlier provides a recognizable distinction, and he will not come to make a swimmer's barrel. According to the authorities who explain the prohibition as labor or techumin, the baraita distinguishes between the three days immediately before Shabbos and earlier days because the three days before Shabbos are considered the beginning of the coming Shabbos. The obligation to avoid entering a situation that may cause Shabbos desecration has therefore already taken effect. Earlier days, by contrast, are associated with the preceding Shabbos, and the warning concerning the coming Shabbos has not yet taken effect. He may therefore depart then, and if he later must desecrate Shabbos because of danger to life, it is permitted. Although the Mechaber follows the Rif in seif 2 and cites his explanation, he also agrees with the authorities who permit departure more than three days beforehand even where the traveler may later be compelled to perform labor and desecrate Shabbos, as stated in seif 4; see what we write there. He nevertheless holds that even where there is no concern of labor or techumin, such as when the ship belongs entirely to non-Jews and any labor performed is presumably done for their own purposes, and it sails in water deeper than ten handbreadths, so that many authorities hold there is no prohibition of techumin, as explained below in siman 404, departure is still forbidden solely because it will impair Shabbos enjoyment. We will now, with God's help, explain the words of the Shulchan Aruch.

**** ENGLISH ****
(1) Even on Friday: Meaning that although he will certainly lack Shabbos enjoyment as a result, as the Mechaber writes in seif 2, it is nevertheless permitted because the journey is for a mitzvah, and we follow the rule that one occupied with one mitzvah is exempt from another. This is obvious where the ship travels in water deeper than ten handbreadths, for there is no concern of leaving the techum because we rule that the prohibition of techumin does not apply above ten handbreadths. Even where some places have shallow water less than ten handbreadths deep, however, it is permitted to embark on Friday when traveling for a mitzvah. Eliyah Rabbah.
