**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> In order to escape the doubt. It is surprising: how does it harm this doubt if he blesses over washing the hands before relieving himself? One may say that according to the writings he blesses over washing the hands, while with Asher Yatzar he may wait until he relieves himself. Therefore he wrote that he should not wait, but is obligated to recite Asher Yatzar immediately to escape the doubt, and therefore it is proper to relieve himself immediately. If so, according to Magen Avraham he should bless over washing the hands beforehand, for the writings said to bless over washing the hands first only so that he may then wait. Alternatively, Magen Avraham holds that the blessing over washing the hands is an interruption and that he does not satisfy the view requiring Asher Yatzar without relieving himself. The first answer is primary.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, s.k. 6</small> Meaning after the fact. He intends this because the language of the Rama could be misunderstood: since he wrote, however a vessel, etc., is not indispensable, it might imply that the Mechaber did not speak of a vessel, and that the Rama means it is not indispensable even lechatchilah. Since the Mechaber wrote that it is good to be careful, the Rama would be saying that a vessel and human force do not prevent even lechatchilah, and that the Mechaber spoke only of water invalid in itself, where one must be careful lechatchilah. This is not correct, for then how did he bring in seif 1 an opinion not to bless? Where do we find such a thing if even water invalid in itself is only something good to be careful about? Therefore Magen Avraham explained the Rama as though he wrote that after the fact it does not invalidate. In truth the Mechaber too speaks of a vessel and human force, where the invalidity is not in the water itself; there it is only good to be careful. But invalidity in the water itself is where there is an opinion that one does not bless. This is why Magen Avraham concludes by referring to what he wrote in seif 1.

<small>(שם)</small> דלא גרע מצרור. תימה דצרור אינו מועיל רק לתפלה והרי לתפלה אפילו שכשך לתוך מעט הים שאובים בכלי מהני כמבואר בש"ע ומה ענין מ' סאה לתפלה. ותו מה השיג על הע"ת דאיהו ודאי מיירי להעביר ר"ר אז צריך מ"ס שיהיה בו שיעור מקוה שמטהר טמא מטומאתו דאז מהני אף לר"ר וכן הקשו אליה רבא ופרי מגדים. והברור שיש כאן ט"ס ותיבות אילו דלא גרע מצרור בסכ"ב מקומו בס"ק הקודם גבי שכשך ידיו שכתב המג"א לברך על נקיות וע"ז כתב דלא גרע דכיון שמועיל לתפלה מברך על נקיות ה"ה בזה השכשוך וכאן כ' המג"א ופשוט דלא בעי מ"ס ר"ל מי גשמים מכונסים בקרקע אפילו אין בהם מ"ס מועיל אפילו לר"ר וראייתו מסימן קנ"ט סעיף י"ד דנקטינן כדברי המקיל וס"ל דאפילו פחות ממ"ס מטהר כלים מש"ה מועיל לנט"י לאכילה ואף שכתב שם דיש להחמיר לכתחלה הכא בנט"י שחרית דקיל טפי א"צ להחמיר וכיון דיש בהם לטהר טומאת כלים וידים אינם נטמאים מהר"ר ומש"ה משיג שפיר על הע"ת ומה שמצוה לעיין בסימן הק"ס סעיף ז' אינו להשיג על הע"ת דא"כ היה כותבו קודם תיבות דלא כע"ת אלא כוונתו לדין מחודש דשם מבואר דבחמי טבריא דבה"ג שאין בו מ"ס אין מטבילין בהם הידים משום גזירה ע"ש במ"א ס"ק י' מש"ה גם בנט"י שחרית לא מהני וכ"ז ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> For it is no worse than a pebble. This is surprising, for a pebble helps only for prayer, and for prayer even shaking into a little drawn water in a vessel helps, as explained in Shulchan Aruch; what then has forty se'ah to do with prayer? Further, what was his challenge to Olas Tamid, who certainly spoke of removing evil spirit, for which forty se'ah are needed so that it has the measure of a mikveh that purifies an impure person? Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim also asked this. The clear truth is that there is a scribal error: the words for it is no worse than a pebble in seif 22 belong in the previous s.k., regarding shaking his hands, where Magen Avraham wrote to bless over cleanliness. On that he wrote that it is no worse, for since it helps for prayer he blesses over cleanliness, and the same applies to this shaking. Here Magen Avraham wrote that it is obvious that forty se'ah are not required, meaning rainwater gathered in the ground, even without forty se'ah, helps even for evil spirit. His proof is from siman 159, seif 14, where we follow the lenient view that even less than forty se'ah purifies utensils; therefore it helps for washing hands for eating. Although there the Rama wrote that one should be stringent lechatchilah, here, in morning washing, which is lighter, one need not be stringent. Since such water can purify the impurity of utensils, hands do not become impure from the evil spirit, and therefore he properly challenges Olas Tamid. His direction to see siman 160, seif 12, is not meant as a challenge to Olas Tamid, for then he would have written it before the words unlike Olas Tamid; rather, he intends a new law, that with the hot springs of Tiberias, according to Baal Halachos Gedolos, if there are not forty se'ah one does not immerse the hands in them because of a decree, and therefore morning washing would also not help. All this is clear.
