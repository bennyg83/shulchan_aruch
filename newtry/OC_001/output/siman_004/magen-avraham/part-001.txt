<b>ויברך ענט"י</b>. לפי מאי דקי"ל אפי' עשה צרכיו ועומד להתפלל אינו מברך ענט"י ובשחרית מברכין צ"ל הטעם כמ"ש הרשב"א דבבוקר נעשה כבריה חדשה וצריכים אנו להודות לו ועד"ז תקנו כל ברכות השחר לפיכך צריכין אנו להתקדש בקדושתו וליטול ידיו מן הכלי ככהן שמקדש ידיו מן הכיור קודם עבודתו: כתב ב"ח בשם תולעת יעקב כל ההולך ד"א בלא נט"י שחרית חייב מיתה ובסדה"י כתוב שלא יגע במלבושיו עד שיטול ובג' לא משמע כן עסי' מ"ו ובברכות דף נ"א אל תטול חלוקך שחרית מיד השמש ותלבש, ומשמע בכתבי' שיברך נ"י תיכף ואח"כ יעשה צרכיו ויאמר אשר יצר ול"נ דטוב לעשות צרכיו תחלה כדי לברך א"י תיכף כדי לצאת מן הספק די"א דמברך א"י בלא עשיית צרכיו ומכ"ש אם צריך לנקביו שאסור לו לברך, אמרי' בגמ' רוחץ אדם פניו ידיו ורגליו בכל יום משום כבוד קונו כ"כ הרמב"ם פ"ד מה' תפלה וכ"כ בהגמ"נ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“And he blesses netilas yadayim.”</b> According to what we hold—even if he relieved himself and is about to pray—he does not bless netilas yadayim; yet in the morning we do bless, so a reason is needed: as the Rashba explains, in the morning one becomes like a new creature and we must give thanks; accordingly they instituted all the morning berachos; therefore we must sanctify ourselves in His holiness and wash the hands from a vessel, like a kohen who sanctifies his hands from the laver before his service. Bach cites Tolaas Yaakov: anyone who goes four cubits without morning netilas yadayim is liable to death. Seder Hayom writes not to touch garments until he washes; siman 46 does not read that way. Berachos 51a: “Do not take your robe in the morning from the sun-heated pot and put it on,” which implies in some writings that he should bless netilas yadayim immediately, then relieve himself, then say Asher Yatzar. It appears to us better to relieve himself first so he may say Asher Yatzar right away and avoid a doubt—some say one may bless Asher Yatzar without relieving himself, and all the more so if he must use the bathroom it is forbidden for him to bless. The Gemara says a person washes face, hands, and feet every day for the honor of his Maker; so too Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah ch. 4, and Hagahos Maimoniyos.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Parashas HaYashan.”</b> This requires study: we do not find that formulation there except regarding the Amoraim, and for all opinions it is forbidden to sleep excessively by day, as stated in siman 231; but David was careful even at night, as Rabbeinu Yerucham cites the Zohar. Berachos 3a states explicitly: until midnight at night he dozed like a horse—that is sixty breaths; and what they say that he had a harp to wake him from sleep means if midnight arrived within those sixty breaths, for in truth he slept several times sixty breaths before midnight of the first night. We must say that Rema’s ruling “what difference does it make—Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that David was careful,” etc., means only that an especially pious person may impose a stringency on himself, and that is what he means; and in the Gemara, Parashas HaYashan, etc., means that an especially pious person should not be stringent except by day.
