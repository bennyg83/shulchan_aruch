**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"And some say."</b> This follows the Rashba in siman 191, who wrote that morning differs from Minchah and Maariv because one has become like a new creature, as stated in the Midrash on "new every morning," etc.; therefore one must thank Him and serve Him. They instituted "Elokai Neshamah" for the soul and "Asher Yatzar" for the body. Thus the wording of all the early authorities places "Elokai Neshamah" immediately after "Asher Yatzar," and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 6; the blessing on Torah follows all the morning blessings before the passage of the Tamid, and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, simanim 46, 47, and 48. The Rav, however, changed the order at the end of siman 46 and wrote, "the custom is," etc. The reason is that in the synagogue they begin from "Elokai Neshamah"; see Magen Avraham, siman 6, small paragraph 8. At home they recite only "al netilas yadayim" and "Asher Yatzar," as the Tur wrote there; at times one recites verses and supplications before going to the synagogue. This is what he wrote at the end of siman 46: "one should not read," etc.; "but the custom," etc.; "and they are accustomed," etc., meaning to satisfy the first view. That accords with the second opinion in the Tur, siman 6, that one need not juxtapose "Asher Yatzar" to "Elokai Neshamah"; but the first opinion there requires juxtaposition, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of rule 4.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>והנוגע בכנה והנוגע כו'. </b>דלדעת הרא"ש להכי תקינו נטילה בשחרית וגם לתרומה בפ"א דשבת וכן כל הני דחשיב שהן מקום הטנופת ובכלל מ"ש ידים עסקניות הן וע' רש"י דנזיר נ"ט א' ד"ה א"ד וד"ה א"ל כו' וז"ש בסכ"א צריך כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"And one who touches a louse, and one who touches," etc.</b> According to the Rosh, for this reason they instituted washing in the morning, and also for terumah in Shabbos chapter 1. So too all those listed are places of filth and are included in what was said: hands are busy. See Rashi, Nazir 59a, gloss beginning "i de'amar" and gloss beginning "amar lei," etc. This is what he wrote in seif 21: "one must," etc.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>ס"כב ויברך כו'. </b>כ"כ הרא"ש והוא כשיטת מרדכי וסמ"ג שכ' דאין מברכין ענט"י אלא כשנוטל מן הכלי וכמ"ש בס"ס קנ"ט ס"כ בהג"ה אבל הרשב"א והר"י כ' שם שמברך לעולם ענט"י שמחמת הנטילה שצונו עליה ובכלל מאתים מנה וכ"כ בש"ע שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 22, "and he recites the blessing," etc.</b> So wrote the Rosh. This follows the Mordechai and Semag, who wrote that one recites "al netilas yadayim" only when washing from a vessel, as written at the end of siman 159, seif 20, in the gloss. But the Rashba and Rabbenu Yonah wrote there that one always recites "al netilas yadayim," because of the washing that we were commanded about; the larger amount includes the smaller. So wrote the Shulchan Aruch there.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>אבל ברכות כו'. </b>דכולהו נתקנו קודם הנטילה כמש"ש ס"ב ואמרינן בחגיגה י"ח ב' הנוטל ידיו לפירות כו' והא דאמרי' בברכית מ"ג אורחין כו' התם כשאין ידו נקי וכמ"ש תוס' שם בחגיגה ולקמן סי' קנ"ח ס"ה בהג"ה וז"ש שם אלא א"כ כו' וההיא דברכות ס"ב דמסתמא ישן ערום ע' ברשב"א וכ"ש לשיטת הרא"ש שכתב דסתם ידים בשינה אינן נקיות וצ"ל ההיא דברכות הנ"ל שא"צ לברכות אלא נקיון וז"ש עד כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"But blessings," etc.</b> For all of them were instituted before washing, as written there in seif 2. And we say in Chagigah 18b: one who washes his hands for fruit, etc. What is said in Berakhot 43 about guests, etc., is where his hand is not clean, as Tosafos wrote there in Chagigah, and below siman 158, seif 5, in the gloss. This is what is written there: unless, etc. And that case in Berakhot 62, presumably he slept naked; see the Rashba. All the more according to the Rosh, who wrote that ordinary hands after sleep are unclean. One must explain the above Berakhot passage as meaning that for blessings only cleanliness is needed; this is what he wrote: "until," etc.
