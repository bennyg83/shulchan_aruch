But according to what we hold, that even if he relieved himself, etc., one must say the reason is as the Rashba wrote, etc.

Therefore for Shacharis more than for Minchah one must make it resemble the Temple service, where the kohen required sanctification. Although the kohen also had to sanctify his feet, nevertheless they made it only a resemblance and it is not equal to sanctification in every respect.

<b>כ' הב"ח בשם ת"י כו'</b> חייב מיתה ותמה הב"ח על הרב"י שהשמיטו ואולי משום מוטב יהיו שוגגים כו'. ובס' א"ר סי' א' הביא בשם גיסו ה"ה מהרר"י בעל מנחת יעקב שאמר ליישב מנהג העולם שסומכים ע"ד המרדכי שהביא ש"ך י"ד סימן שד"מ בהא דאמרינן אין אומרים בפני המת אלא דבריו של מת. וכ' רב האי גאון דוקא תוך ד' אמות של מת וכ' המרדכי דכל תחתיו נחשב כד"א וא"כ ה"ה כאן מותר לילך בכל החדר קודם נטילה. ובעל א"ר דחה דהא קי"ל בכל דוכתי גם לענין צואה בבית דאין כל החדר נחשב לד' אמות א"כ קשיין דברי המרדכי אעכצ"ל דהמרדכי לא אמרה אלא לגבי מת דלא הוזכר בפי' בגמ' שיעור ד' אמות אלא רב האי וכיון דהטעם הוא שהוא בזיון למת דנראה כאלו מתייאש לקוברו הואיל ומדבר דברים אחרים לכן להאי טעמא מסתבר למרדכי דכל החדר נחשב לענין זה כד"א אבל במקום שהוזכר ד' להדיא אמות מודה מרדכי דהוא דוקא ולא אמרינן דכל החדר נחשב לד"א וא"כ אין לעולם על מה לסמוך והאריך. ואח"ז כ' שמצא בס' דמשק אליעזר שכ' שאפשר בזה"ז אין כחו של רוח רעה כל כך חזק שיזיק להולך ד"א בלי נטילה כמו שיבתא ויתר ענינים שנשתנו בזה"ז ואעפ"כ סיים בספר א"ר שירא שמים יחמיר על עצמו בלי לילך ד"א בלי נטילה ויכין סמוך למטתו כלי אחד עם מים וכלי א' ריקן ליטול לתוכו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The Bach wrote in the name of Tosafos Yeshanim, etc.</b> That he is liable to death. The Bach wondered at the Beis Yosef for omitting it, perhaps because it is better that they be inadvertent, etc. In Sefer Eliyah Rabbah, siman 1, he brought in the name of his brother-in-law, Rabbi Yaakov, author of Minchas Yaakov, who sought to justify the common custom by relying on the Mordechai brought by the Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 344 regarding the rule that one may not say matters before the dead except matters of the dead. Rav Hai Gaon wrote that this is only within four amos of the dead, and the Mordechai wrote that the whole area under the same roof is considered four amos. If so, here too it would be permitted to walk anywhere in the room before washing. The author of Eliyah Rabbah rejected this, for we hold everywhere, even regarding excrement in a house, that the entire room is not considered four amos. Therefore the words of the Mordechai must be limited to a dead person, where the measure of four amos is not stated explicitly in the Gemara but only by Rav Hai. Since the reason is disgrace to the dead, for it appears as though he despairs of burying him when he speaks of other matters, the Mordechai reasonably held that for this purpose the whole room is like four amos. But where four amos is stated explicitly, the Mordechai agrees that it is specific and we do not say the whole room is like four amos. Thus the world has nothing to rely on, and he wrote at length. Afterward he wrote that he found in Sefer Damesek Eliezer that perhaps nowadays the power of the evil spirit is not so strong as to harm one who walks four amos without washing, just as with shivta and other matters that have changed nowadays. Nevertheless, Eliyah Rabbah concludes that one who fears Heaven should be stringent and not walk four amos without washing, and should prepare next to his bed one vessel with water and one empty vessel into which to wash.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And in Seder HaYom he wrote, etc.</b> But the Gemara does not imply so; see siman 46. That is, there the order of the morning blessings is arranged, and so it is in the Gemara: when he dresses, he blesses Who clothes the naked; when he puts on his shoes, he blesses; when he girds himself, he blesses; when he places a cap on his head, he blesses; when he washes his hands, he blesses. Thus washing is arranged at the end, after putting on all his garments. Some say that he blesses, etc., as the Rama wrote, that it is forbidden for him to bless, as written in siman 92. We say in Shabbos 49b: so wrote the Rambam. The Raavad objected: why does he need washing the feet for prayer? The author of Migdal Oz wrote that the Raavad overlooked the words of that Gemara in Shabbos. The Kesef Mishneh defended the Raavad, writing that he explains the beraisa not as taught regarding prayer, but regarding whether it is permitted to wash one's face, hands, and feet to beautify oneself, as Rashi explained.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And perhaps it speaks, etc.</b> His intention is to justify the opinion that one should not bless, against the Levush's difficulty that it is no worse than a pebble. He writes that it speaks where he does not wish to pray now and is washing only to remove the evil spirit. Since they are uncertain whether these waters help remove the evil spirit, and even the pebble in seif 22 helps only for prayer, if he does not wish to pray now he should not bless. It implies that if it would help remove the evil spirit, he would bless even though one need not bless merely for removing evil spirit, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 13, because he will pray later and could bless now, since some purpose is achieved by the washing, namely removing the evil spirit. But if it does not help remove the evil spirit and he does not intend to pray now, there is no present purpose in the washing, so he should not bless.

<b>(סק"ז) כלי כו'</b> אבל ע"ג קסמים אסור עסי' קפ"א ס"ב דמים אחרונים אף שאין נוטלים ע"ג קרק' דרוח רעה שורה עליהן מ"מ ע"ג קסמים מותר התם ה"ט דטעם הנטילה הוא משום מלח סדומית או ט"א ע"ש בב"י א"כ המים מצד עצמן ע"י הנטילה עדיין אין ר"ר שורה עליהן אלא בהגיען לארץ אז רוח רעה שורה עליהן וכיון שיש דבר מפסיק בין המים לארץ אין רשות לר"ר לשרות עליהן כמ"ש מ"א לקמן לכן שרי על גבי קסמים משא"כ כאן דהרוח רעה שורה על הידים וע"י הנטילה מעביר הר"ר מן הידים ושורה הר"ר על מי הרחיצה וא"כ מאי מהני ע"ג קסמים ניהו שיש הפסק בינם לקרקע הא כבר שורה עליהן ר"ר ולכן צריך כלי ליטול לתוכן ולשופכן במקום שאין אנשים עוברים ומה"ט כתב הרב"י בשם הזוהר שאין ליתן אותם המים למכשפות והב"ח כ' דאפילו לכל אדם השואל אותן המים אין ליתן דודאי דעתו לכישוף:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>S.k. 7, a vessel, etc.</b> But on chips of wood it is forbidden. See siman 181, seif 2: final waters, although they are not poured on the ground because an evil spirit rests on them, may nevertheless be poured on chips of wood. There the reason is that the washing is because of Sodomite salt or another reason, and thus the water itself does not yet have an evil spirit upon it through the washing; only when it reaches the ground does the evil spirit rest on it. Since something separates between the water and the ground, the evil spirit has no permission to rest upon it, as Magen Avraham writes below, and therefore it is permitted on chips. Here, however, the evil spirit rests on the hands, and through washing it is removed from the hands and rests on the washing water. If so, what help are chips of wood? Although there is a separation between them and the ground, the evil spirit already rests on the water. Therefore a vessel is needed into which to wash, and it should be poured where people do not pass. For this reason the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Zohar that one should not give those waters to sorceresses, and the Bach wrote that even to any person asking for those waters one should not give them, for certainly his intent is for sorcery.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>S.k. 10, three waters, and the same applies in a river, etc.</b> The reason for three changed waters is that if one shakes his hands in one vessel three times it certainly does not help, because at the first shaking the water already became impure, and the later shaking does not help. This is not so with three changed waters in three vessels, where that concern does not apply. Nevertheless, he is uncertain lest the evil spirit is removed only when the water comes by pouring and not by shaking. If so, the same applies in a flowing river: even if he shakes his hands in one place in the river, it has the law of three changed waters, because the first water passes away. In any event, one could also have said that a river, being attached to the ground, is not subject to the water becoming impure through the first shaking, for attached water does not receive impurity.

<b>S.k. 12, without a blessing.</b> The Bach wrote that he should bless because the Sages did not differentiate. That is, the Beis Yosef wrote that according to the Rashba's reason, that one becomes a new creation, if he was awake, although he did not become a new creation, perhaps because the Sages did not differentiate he requires washing and a blessing. By contrast, according to the Rosh's reason, where he was awake and did not touch, they certainly did not institute it. In my humble opinion, the reason to distinguish is this: according to the Rashba, morning washing is a new enactment because one became a new creation, so perhaps even if he did not become a new creation he needs washing and a blessing because the Sages did not differentiate. But according to the Rosh, the concern is that he may have touched a soiled place; this is not a special morning enactment, for even at Minchah, if he relieved himself, he requires washing and a blessing. If so, why should morning, even when he was awake and did not touch, require washing and a blessing because the Sages did not differentiate? Then all day, even for Minchah when he did not relieve himself, should require washing and a blessing, which is certainly not so. The Bach disagreed, for necessarily according to the Rosh too the rule that the Sages did not differentiate requires washing and a blessing, since the Rosh was asked about one who rises before dawn to learn whether he needs washing and a blessing, and he answered that he does. If so, the words of the Rosh seem contradictory, for the Rosh holds that if one relieved himself and then wishes to learn, he does not need a blessing. Necessarily, what he wrote about rising to learn is not precise, but that was the incident, and the same applies if he rose to do his work; he holds that he needs washing and a blessing because if he rose and wished to pray immediately he would need washing and a blessing for the prayer. Therefore even if he rose and does not yet wish to pray, he still needs washing and a blessing because the Sages did not differentiate. Once we say they did not differentiate, even if he was awake and certainly did not touch a soiled place, he still needs washing and a blessing. Nevertheless this is not such a proof from the Rosh, meaning that according to the Rosh one can always say there is no rule of not differentiating, and that when he ruled for one who arose to learn that he blesses, the reason is not learning, as the Bach said it was not precise, but because eventually he will pray and will then need to bless. Therefore he blesses immediately now. But if he was awake, even when he prays he does not bless, for he certainly did not touch and there is no doubt. The Beis Yosef was uncertain whether he should bless because for us the main reason is the Rashba's, as written in s.k. 1, that he became a new creation; although this applies only where he slept, nevertheless washing and a blessing are required because the Sages did not differentiate.
