<b>ויברך</b> עט״ז הביא טעם הרא״ש ורשב״א לענין ברכה (זוהר וא״ח אין שייכים לברכה כמ״ש במ״א א׳) ותופס דברי הרא״ש עיקר דמדינא א״צ כלי כלל ומים הפסולים לנט״י לסעודה כשרים כאן לכתחלה וכ״נ דעת המחבר בס״א סתם כשירים ובס״ז לחוש לדברי הרשב׳א כתב טוב כו׳ ולהמ״א א׳ דעיקר כרשב׳א ודאי לכתחילה יזהר מדינא בכל הדברים כמו לכיור ומה״ט מברך ענט״י נטלא הכלי כו׳ יע״ש בב״י. ואזיל לשיטתיה בסימן זיי״ן אות א׳ תירץ קושיית מ״א דוקא בשחר תקנו דרוב פעמים ידיו מטונפין ועל הרוב תקנו ברכה משא״כ מנח׳ דרוב פעמים אין מטונף ומיעוטא דל״ש שיגע במטונף וילך מיד להתפלל לא תקנו ועל סתם ידים בהיסח הדעת וודאי א״צ ברכה ולפ״ז יצא לנו דין הדש באם ישן בבתי ידים ויודע וודאי שלא נגע במטונף לרא״ש אין מברך ולהרשב״א מברך כמ״ש במ״א מזה א׳: ועמ״ש בט״ז אות ט׳ ובמ״א אות י״ג מזה עוד וכאן אין להאריך יותר: ועמ״ש עוד בסעיף כ״ג במ״א כ״ו ז׳ ח׳ מזה: והוי יודע די״ל בבוקר חייבו ענ״י להר״מ ז״ל פ״א מתפלה וכ״ד המחבר בק״ו ס״ב דמ״ה הוה ודי בפ״א הלכך בבוקר דתפלה א׳ מ״ה תקנו חז״ל נ״י הא במנחה דרבנן לא תקנו חז״ל נ״י בברכה ואע״ג דהתפלל ערבית בלילה והולך אחר היום וכ״ת דווקא ביום מצוה מ״ה ולעבדו הוה עבודת היום הא בורכא דא״כ נשים פטורי׳ כמו ציצית והרי הר״מ ז״ל פ״א מתפלה והמחבר בק״ז כתבו דמ״ה נשים חייבות בתפלה אלא ר״ל ערבית רשות בזמן גמרא ותפלה א׳ בבוקר ד״ת והאידנא נמי דעשאוהו חוב לא זזה ממקומה כמו קידוש בבה״כ וכדומה אלא דהט״ז בק״ו ב, כתב תפלה דרבנן: <b>ומ״ש</b> הט״ז אי מיד מברך עיין סימן ו׳ בט״ז ד׳ שם ואע״ג דניגוב אין מעכב כאן כמ״ש המ״א כאן אות ו׳ א״כ לאחר נטילה קודם הניגוב הוה אחר עשייה יבואר במ״א ו׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should bless.</b> See Taz, who brought the reasons of the Rosh and Rashba regarding the blessing. The Zohar and Orchos Chayim do not relate to the blessing, as written in Magen Avraham s.k. 1. He takes the words of the Rosh as primary, for according to the law no vessel is needed at all, and waters invalid for washing hands for a meal are valid here lechatchilah. So too appears to be the view of the Mechaber, who in seif 1 wrote without qualification that they are valid, and in seif 7 wrote that it is good to be concerned for the Rashba. According to Magen Avraham s.k. 1, however, that the primary view is like the Rashba, one certainly must be careful lechatchilah according to the law in all matters, like the laver; for this reason one blesses over washing the hands, using a vessel, etc.; see the Beis Yosef. The Taz follows his view in siman 7, s.k. 1, where he answers Magen Avraham's question: specifically in the morning they instituted the blessing because most times one's hands are soiled, and they instituted the blessing based on the majority. This differs from Minchah, where most times one is not soiled, and the uncommon case that he touched a soiled place and immediately goes to pray was not included in the enactment. For ordinary hands that were merely unattended, one certainly does not need a blessing. According to this, a new law emerges: if one slept in gloves and knows certainly that he did not touch any soiled place, according to the Rosh he does not bless, while according to the Rashba he blesses, as Magen Avraham wrote from this in s.k. 1. See what I wrote on Taz s.k. 9 and Magen Avraham s.k. 13 about this further; here is not the place to elaborate. See also what I wrote in seif 23 on Magen Avraham s.k. 26, 7, and 8 about this. Know that one can say that in the morning the obligation of washing hands according to the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah, and the view of the Mechaber in siman 106, seif 2, is according to the Torah, and one time is enough. Therefore, in the morning, when one prayer is according to the Torah, the Sages instituted washing hands; but for Minchah, which is D'Rabbanan, the Sages did not institute washing hands with a blessing. Although one prays Maariv at night and the day follows the night, and should you say that specifically by day the mitzvah according to the Torah is to serve Him as the service of the day, this is mistaken, for if so women would be exempt like tzitzis, yet the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah and the Mechaber in siman 107 wrote that women are obligated in prayer according to the Torah. Rather, Maariv was optional in the time of the Gemara, and one prayer in the morning is according to the Torah. Even nowadays, when they made Maariv obligatory, the original enactment did not move from its place, like kiddush in the synagogue and the like. However, the Taz in siman 106, s.k. 2, wrote that prayer is D'Rabbanan. <b>And what the Taz wrote</b> regarding whether one blesses immediately, see siman 6 in Taz s.k. 4. Although drying does not prevent validity here, as Magen Avraham wrote here in s.k. 6, so after washing and before drying it is already after the act, this will be explained in Magen Avraham s.k. 6.

<b>במקום</b> עט״ז פירש דהקזה וגיגית קודם נטילת שחרית מיירי דוקא. והנה מ״ש גיגית ל״ל לטותא תקצץ אם רוצה להפסיד ממונו כ״כ הב״ח: ומ״ש דהוכחת רש״י דהנך מצינו בהו איסור כל היום י״ל אמה בנשוי ומעטרה ולמטה מותר כמבואר בסימן ג׳ וקודם נטילה שחרית תקצץ גם אי כפי׳ אמאי הפסיק בין חסודה לגיגית באמה ופה טבעת דהן כל היום ומ״ש תחתוניות לא מצאתיו שבת פ״א א׳ חשב עשרה דברים ולא הא יע״ש: בא״ר ג׳ כתב קיצור פסקי הרא׳ש בשמונה שרצים סעיף ג׳ י״ד לעין ופה ואמ״ה וחוטם קודם נטילת שחרית תקצץ אני תמה דודאי ט״ס הוא אמה וצ״ל אוז״ן ולמה השמיט אוזן ומשם ראיה כפי׳ הב״י דאל״כ מאי לא נקיט כל הח׳ דברים רק ד׳ עין פה וחוטם ואוז״ן (במקום אמ״ה) וזה כב״י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In a place.</b> See Taz, who explained that bloodletting and a vat refer specifically before morning washing. Regarding what he wrote about a vat, why would there be a curse of should be cut off if one wishes to damage his own money, the Bach also wrote this. What he wrote as Rashi's proof, that regarding those matters we find a prohibition all day, one may say that regarding the member, for a married man and below the crown it is permitted, as explained in siman 3; before morning washing it should be cut off. Also, if so, why did the Gemara interrupt between the pious woman and the vat with member, mouth, and ring, which apply all day? What he wrote about hemorrhoids I did not find; in Shabbos 81a it counts ten matters and not this; see there. Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 3 cited the abridged rulings of the Rosh in Shemonah Sheratzim, seif 14: to the eye, mouth, member, and nose before morning washing should be cut off. I wonder, for it is certainly a scribal error: it should read ear, not member. Why did he omit ear? From there there is proof like the Beis Yosef's explanation, for otherwise why did it not mention all eight matters, but only four, eye, mouth, nose, and ear, in place of member? This accords with the Beis Yosef.

<b>ממי</b> עט״ז. והנה עכו״ם י״ל אין מקבל טומאה ולטעם הזוהר מאן דנאים כו׳ העד על זה קברי עכו״ם למ״ד אפילו במגע אין מטמא והעולם באמת אין נזהרין מזה. אבל נשים יזהרו מאוד שתיקון מאכלים הם עושים שלא יטמאו בידיהם ועיין עט״ז: גם קטנים א״י למה אין נזהרים בזה וראוי לרחוץ ידיהם בכל בוקר כי חמירא סכנתא מאיסורא ומ״ש נ״י לסעודה הקשה בא״ר אות ז׳ וכ״כ של״ה וס״ה דבנ״י לסעודה שאין רק לתוס׳ קדושה רשאי ליטול ממי שלא נטל ידיו וכ״מ בקנ״ט סי״א וי״ב וע״ש בט״ז אין כ״כ ראיה משם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From someone.</b> See Taz. Regarding a non-Jew, one may say that he does not receive impurity; according to the reason of the Zohar, whoever sleeps, etc., the proof is from non-Jewish graves according to the opinion that even by touch they do not convey impurity. In truth, the world is not careful about this. But women should be very careful, since they prepare foods, lest they make them impure with their hands; see Taz. I also do not know why people are not careful with minors, and it is proper to wash their hands every morning, for danger is more severe than prohibition. Regarding what he wrote about washing hands for a meal, Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 7 challenged it, and so wrote Shelah and Seder HaYom, that for washing hands for a meal, which is only for added sanctity, one may wash from someone who has not washed. So it also implies in siman 159, seif 11 and seif 12; see there in the Taz, though there is not such a strong proof from there.

<b>לק״ש</b> עט״ז ומ״ש מזוהר משמע דבעי כלי ועירוי דווקא ולפ״ז אפילו נטל ידיו מזוחלין ממקום גבוה וכ״ש ע״י תחיבת ידו ג״פ בשלג או ממי נהר יש לספק לר״ר אי מועיל ומיהו ברוכי מברך אכל הני ומשמע ענ״י לא נקיות ידים ועמ״א אות ט׳ מ״ש שם ועיין סכ״ב שם:

<b>אם</b> עט״ז מקום הספק להעביר ר״ר הוא אי טעם זוהר או טעם אורחות חיים. וספק לתפלה אי טעם הרשב״א ולא פלוג או טעם הרא״ש ול״ש ל״פ בזה היכא (עיין באר הגולה צ׳ גם לדעת הרא״ש) דלא נגע וסובר דלרשב״א דעת הב״י אע״ג שכתב ואפשר הכין הוא דל״פ וספק שמא הלכה כרא״ש ואזיל לשיטתיה באות א׳ וסימן זיין א׳ אף לדידן שייך טעם הרא״ש: ומ״ש להרשב״א ל״פ דלאו להנצ״ל מעבירה אף דלרשב״א נמי הטעם דנגע במטונף או סתם ידים והא דלא תקנו בתפלת מנחה דכאן משום דנעשה כבריה חדשה צריך ככהן כלי כמו כיור מש״ה תקנו ברכה משא״כ בכל יום די בנקיון עפר עמ״א לקמן אות כ״ה סובר די״ל מטעם בריה חדשה לחוד נמי תקנו לברך: והנך רואה דלפ״ז אף ישן עראי דלא חיישינן דנגע במטונף ולרשב״א בריה חדשה אפ״ה אין לברך דשמא הלכה כרא״ש ומיהו אם עשה צרכיו וישן עראי לכ״ע מברך ואע״ג דלדידן בסי׳ ז׳ כל היום לא מ״מ משכחת כה״ג וודאי ל״פ או דרוב פעמים אין נקיים כט״ז בזיי״ן א׳ (וכן בליל שבועות אם ישן עראי יפנה ויברך ענ״י עיין תצ״ד) אבל למ״א אות א׳ וב׳ דדחה טעם הרא״ש לדידן אף בישן עראי ולא עשה צרכיו יברך וודאי ענ״י. ועמ״ש במ״א אות י״ב ולמ״א י״ג פ״א בריה ולא ב״פ ויודה הט״ז לזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If.</b> See Taz. The place of doubt regarding removing evil spirit is whether the reason is the Zohar or Orchos Chayim. The doubt regarding prayer is whether the reason is the Rashba and the Sages did not differentiate, or the Rosh and the rule of not differentiating does not apply here, where he did not touch. See Be'er HaGolah s.k. 90, even according to the Rosh. He holds that according to the Rashba, in the Beis Yosef's view, although he wrote perhaps, it is indeed because they did not differentiate. The doubt is perhaps the halachah follows the Rosh, and he follows his own view in s.k. 1 and siman 7, s.k. 1, that even for us the reason of the Rosh applies. What he wrote that according to the Rashba they did not differentiate, not merely to save one from sin, although the Rashba too gives the reason that he touched a soiled place or ordinary hands, is because here, since he became a new creation, he needs a vessel like a kohen from the laver; therefore they instituted a blessing, unlike every day where cleaning with earth is enough. See Magen Avraham below s.k. 25, who holds that perhaps because of the reason of new creation alone they instituted a blessing. You see that according to this, even one who slept a temporary sleep, where we are not concerned that he touched a soiled place, and according to the Rashba he is a new creation, nevertheless one should not bless, lest the halachah follow the Rosh. However, if he relieved himself and slept a temporary sleep, according to everyone he blesses. Although for us in siman 7 one does not bless all day, such a case can exist, either because they certainly did not differentiate or because most times one's hands are not clean, as the Taz wrote in siman 7, s.k. 1. Likewise on Shavuos night, if he slept a temporary sleep he should relieve himself and bless over washing the hands; see siman 494. But according to Magen Avraham s.k. 1 and 2, who rejects the reason of the Rosh for us, even if one slept a temporary sleep and did not relieve himself, he certainly blesses over washing the hands. See what I wrote in Magen Avraham s.k. 12; and according to Magen Avraham s.k. 13, one new creation, not twice, and the Taz will concede this.

כשיאיר עט״ז ומקום הספק אי שורה כל הלילה עד היום או לאו ועוד דשמא כזוהר דווקא מאן דנאים והאי ניעור הוא ויטלם דס״ס לא מהני לסכנה דס״ס י״א דלא עדיף כמו חזקה ובסכנה חזקה לא מהני כדמסיק חולין ט׳ בגילוי חמירא סכנה מאיסורא ואף למ״ד כרוב מ״מ י״ל דלא מהני ס״ס לסכנה ואי״ה במקום אחר יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
When it becomes light; see Taz. The doubt is whether the evil spirit rests the whole night until day or not. Also, perhaps like the Zohar, it rests specifically on one who sleeps, and this one was awake. He should wash, for a double doubt does not help regarding danger; some say a double doubt is no better than a presumption, and a presumption does not help in danger, as Chullin 9 concludes regarding exposed liquids: danger is more severe than prohibition. Even according to the opinion that it is like a majority, one may say that a double doubt does not help in danger. With the help of Heaven this will be explained further elsewhere.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>ישן</b> עט״ז הספק אי כזוהר ואפי׳ ביום צריך או כא״ח לילה גורם: ומ״ש הר״ב בזה וכן במשכים קודם אור היום דיטלם בלא ברכה לאו משום ספיקא דא״כ וודאי לר״ר ל״ש ברכה כמ״ש המ״א אות י״ג ומלבוש אין ראיה להיפוך יע״ש בזה:

<b>Particles of sweat.</b> See Taz. This refers specifically to covered places of the body. The humble writer says: because the ways of wisdom are hidden from me, I will briefly investigate several questions relevant to this siman and other simanim. There are six washings for added sanctity: meal, morning, Minchah, Shema, Torah study, and dipping food into liquid. Washing for a meal is explained in simanim 159 and 160, including what invalidates and what is proper lechatchilah; see there. For morning washing, lechatchilah one should be careful about all matters that invalidate washing for a meal, as the Mechaber wrote here in seif 7. When I say matters that invalidate, it may be that what is only lechatchilah there, such as in siman 159, seif 8 and seif 13, is not needed here for prayer; for example, shaking in a vessel, which according to the Rashba himself is lechatchilah here, while there it is acceptable. Regarding intent, one may say that here intent is not required, as we will say below with the help of Heaven. Know that if rabbinic mitzvos also require intent, then for non-sacred slaughter, which in Yoreh Deah does not require intent; washing for non-sacred food in siman 159, seif 13, which does not require intent; and a niddah immersing for her husband, which does not require intent, although these are Torah laws, one must necessarily say that any mitzvah from which one cannot exempt himself requires the intent of the one performing it, whereas slaughter, immersion, and the like are different, for he need not slaughter and eat, or immerse and have relations. In those, intent is not needed. If so, morning washing, since one cannot be without washing and prayer, might require intent. However, Magen Avraham in siman 60, s.k. 3, brought several opinions that D'Rabbanan mitzvos do not require intent; if so, according to everyone morning washing does not require intent. But water is required for two reasons: first because of evil spirit, and second because for prayer we require water specifically when possible. Minchah is explained in siman 233; see there, that if he has no water he cleans with a pebble. See siman 7 there, s.k. 2, and siman 92, s.k. 1, where he holds that the rulings of the Mechaber contradict one another; with the help of Heaven this will be explained there. Regarding Shema it appears that according to everyone one need not go back four mil to find water; only for prayer must one go back. See Beis Yosef, simanim 92 and 98, from Berachos 15a: these words apply to Shema, etc. The reason for the distinction is that Shema is Torah law while prayer is D'Rabbanan; see Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Shema, halachah 1, and chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2. I do not know, for according to the Rambam prayer is according to the Torah at least once; how can he distinguish between Shema and prayer, and not within prayer itself between one prayer and a second? See chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah and the view of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 106, as Magen Avraham wrote there, that prayer is according to the Torah. With this one may say that Minchah is D'Rabbanan, Shacharis is according to the Torah, and Maariv is optional; therefore they were stricter regarding Shacharis. There is another reason to distinguish Shema: its time is brief, three hours, and afterward he is only like one reading in the Torah, whereas prayer has four hours and afterward at least there is reward for prayer; see Rashi, Berachos 15a. In our novellae we wrote that according to this, all the more so for evening Shema, whose time is all night according to the Torah, and even if he passed midnight he fulfills the mitzvah of Shema, it is superior to prayer, where there is only reward for prayer not in its time, and it is obvious that he must go back after water. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Shema, regarding what he wrote that according to the Torah its time is all day; see chapter 1 of Hilchos Shema, halachah 13. For all these, Shema, prayer, and a meal, even ordinary unattended hands invalidate, as explained in siman 93, and it appears the same for Shema. But for Torah study, ordinary hands are fit for Torah study, for a blessing, and for mentioning the Name, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 92, s.k. 4. There is also a distinction: for Torah study and blessings, according to everyone cleaning with earth is enough lechatchilah and water is not needed. Dipping into liquid is explained in siman 158, seif 4, that it requires washing without a blessing. The Taz there, s.k. 6, implies that it is only for cleanliness and not because of a connection to terumah. According to this its law is like other blessings: with filth, cleaning with earth is enough lechatchilah, and the same here with ordinary hands. But one who examines the Beis Yosef and Levush there will see many reasons because of a connection to terumah or because liquids are prone to impurity; if so, certainly cleaning is not enough even for ordinary hands, but specifically water is required, and one does not bless. There are two kinds of dirt. One is grime in covered places and those matters listed here in seif 18; for this, specifically water is needed because of evil spirit, although for Shema and prayer, if he has none, earth is enough, and for Torah study and blessings earth is also enough lechatchilah. But dirt from mud or mire, which is not repulsive grime and has no evil spirit, for Torah study and blessings earth or anything that cleans is enough lechatchilah; for Shema and prayer, lechatchilah water is required and after the fact cleaning suffices. If one is standing in prayer and touched, it is explained in siman 92, seif 6, that lechatchilah cleaning with earth is enough. There is a further distinction: for dirt from mud or mire, for Torah study and blessings one needs to clean only that place; but regarding the matters listed here in seif 18, because of evil spirit, lechatchilah one certainly needs the entire joint until the arm, and at least until the finger joints; see siman 613 and Magen Avraham here s.k. 7 and what I wrote there. It appears to me that ordinary hands are invalid for prayer and one must clean even the back of the hand; do not say only the palm, which usually touches, but he cleans everything if he has water, and if not he cleans with earth until the joint. Whether to distinguish between ordinary hands and soiled hands regarding going back for water will be explained with the help of Heaven in siman 92. Know that any revi'is of water in the ground, if not drawn, is fit for vessels and has the law of a mikveh; needless to say a spring purifies with any amount. See siman 159, seif 14, Taz s.k. 18 and Magen Avraham s.k. 26. Such waters do not receive impurity at all; see Pesachim 17b, there is no difference when it is in the ground, etc. According to this, what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 10 against Olas Tamid is good, and we wrote there that now we see that the law is with him. The explanation is that a pebble is enough for prayer, and as for receiving impurity, they do not receive impurity at all; we are not concerned for evil spirit, as explained in siman 159, seif 14. He also referred to siman 160, seif 12, that snow too has the law of a mikveh. See Yoreh Deah siman 201, seif 30, where some say even if it has not melted, and some are stringent. For evil spirit, if it has the law of a mikveh the evil spirit does not rest in it; otherwise there is a concern of evil spirit even if snow is not capable of receiving impurity. See the Rambam, Hilchos Keilim 21. There is no need to elaborate, and with the help of Heaven this will be explained more in Magen Avraham.
