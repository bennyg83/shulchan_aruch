**** ENGLISH ****
The blessing of individual thanksgiving. 9 sections: Four people must give thanks: those who descend to the sea when they have come up from it; those who travel through deserts when they reach an inhabited area; one who was ill and recovered; and one who was imprisoned and was released. **** ENGLISH ****
One must recite this blessing before ten people, two of whom should be Torah scholars, as it is written "let them exalt Him in the congregation of the people and praise Him in the assembly of elders." If Torah scholars are not available he should not refrain from blessing. The custom is to bless after the Torah reading since ten are present. If he blessed before fewer than ten, some say he has fulfilled his obligation and some say he has not — it is best to bless again before ten without mentioning God's name and kingship.

**** ENGLISH ****
If another person blessed and said "Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who has bestowed all goodness upon you" and he answered Amen, he has fulfilled his obligation. Similarly if one said "Blessed is the Merciful One, King of the universe, who has given you to us" and he answered Amen, he has fulfilled his obligation. {Rama: Rema: This is not a blessing in vain for the one blessing, even though he is not personally obligated in this blessing, since he is only blessing as an expression of praise and thanksgiving for the good that befell his fellow in which he rejoices. (Tur)}

**** ENGLISH ****
In Ashkenaz and France they do not bless when traveling from city to city, since the obligation was only imposed for those who travel through deserts where wild animals and bandits are common. In Spain the custom is to bless since all roads are presumed to be dangerous — however for less than a parasang one does not bless, unless it is a place known to be especially dangerous, in which case even less than a parasang requires a blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
For any illness one must bless, even if it is not a life-threatening illness or an internal wound — rather any time one took to bed and recovered, since it is like one who was brought up to the tribunal to be judged.
