<b> ותרי מינייהו רבנן</b> -  עיין פמ"ג שמגמגם בדברי המחבר שהשמיט דעת התוס' דבעינן תרי רבנן לבד העשרה מאחר שהרי"ף והרמב"ם מסכימים ג"כ לשיטה זו ובאמת נמשך בשגגה אחר דברי הב"י וכבר כתב הפרישה שט"ס הוא בב"י ואדרבה הרי"ף קיים בשיטת הרמב"ם דס"ל להיפך מהתוס' וכן באשכול ובהלכות ראבי"ה [שיצא עתה לאור] העתיק ג"כ הכי ומצאתי שכבר העיר בזה המפרש לחידושי הרא"ה ע"ש ובעיקר הדבר אין תמיה כלל עליהם דידוע דכמה ראשונים סוברים דכל היכי דלא אסיק הגמרא בתיובתא לא נדחה דינא דאמורא [והתוס' אפשר דקיימי בשיטת הרשב"ם פ' חזקת הבתים (בבא בתרא נ"ב ע"ב) בד"ה קשיא דלא סבר לה כהראשונים הנ"ל אח"כ מצאתי שהגר"א העיר כל זה] וי"ל גם בעניננו דמר זוטרא סובר דאין סברא להצריך ב' לבד העשרה דהיכן מצינו בכל התורה להצריך מספר י"ב אנשים ובודאי הני ב' מתוך עשרה הם. ודע עוד דלשון התוס' בזה כפי מה שהוא לפנינו הוא מקוצר ביותר ועיין באחרונים שנדחקו בהבנתו ובתוס' רבינו יהודה הלשון מבורר ביותר וז"ל אימא בי עשרה ותרי רבנן קשיא וכיון דלא איפשטא לן עבדינא לחומרא ואי ליכא יברך אפילו ליכא תרי רבנן עכ"ל ומפורש הוא ששני דברים הם והגר"א בביאורו כוון ג"כ לזה מדעתו הרחבה עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And two of them are Torah scholars</b> - See Peri Megadim, who questions the Mechaber for omitting the view of Tosafos that two scholars are required in addition to the ten, since Rif and Rambam also agree with this approach. In truth, he was mistakenly drawn after the words of Beit Yosef. Perishah already wrote that there is a scribal error in Beit Yosef. On the contrary, Rif supports the view of Rambam, who holds the opposite of Tosafos. Eshkol and Hilchos Raavyah, which has recently been published, likewise record it this way. I found that the commentator to Chiddushei HaRaah already noted this; see there. Fundamentally, there is no difficulty with their position, for it is known that several Rishonim hold that wherever the Gemara does not conclude with a conclusive refutation, the ruling of the Amora is not rejected. Tosafos may follow the view of Rashbam in the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, Bava Basra 52b, s.v. kashya, who does not agree with those Rishonim. I later found that the Gra noted all this. One may also explain our case by saying that Mar Zutra held there is no reason to require two scholars in addition to the ten, for where do we find anywhere in the Torah a requirement of twelve people? Certainly, the two scholars are included among the ten. Know further that the wording of Tosafos as it appears before us is extremely abbreviated. See the later authorities, who struggled to explain it. In Tosafos Rabbeinu Yehudah the wording is much clearer: “Say that there must be ten and two scholars. This remains difficult. Since the matter was not resolved, we act stringently; but if they are unavailable, he should recite the blessing even without two scholars.” It is explicit that these are two distinct points. The Gra, in his explanation, independently reached the same conclusion; see there.

<b> לא שכיחי רבנן וכו'</b> -  עיין בביאור הגר"א דמשמע מניה דלדעת המחבר דפסק מתחלה דאין צריך כ"א עשרה ליכא מקור לדין זה [וכן בחמד משה פקפק בזה עי"ש] ומ"מ למעשה נראה כפסק המחבר ואפשר דאף הגר"א מודה בזה דהוא לא לעיכובא דהמעיין בבה"ג יראה שלא העתיק כלל מימרא דמר זוטרא דבעינן תרי רבנן ואפשר דס"ל דמאחר דרב אשי מותיב עלה קושיא אחר קושיא מסתמא לא חש לה כלל וס"ל דמדאמוראי קמאי כרב יהודה ואביי לא הזכירו זה כלל ס"ל דקרא אורחא דמלתא נקט דמהדר אחרי זקנים כדי שיברכו אותו וכדכתב הרמב"ם דהשומעים אומרים מי שגמלך וכו' אבל לא דדינא הכי וכמו בברכת חתנים לר"נ דיליף [כתובות ז'] מויקח בועז עשרה אנשים מזקני וגו' ודאי זקנים לאו דוקא ולא לקחם אלא כדי שיברכו אותו וכמו שמבואר שם באמת בקרא דברכו אותו [וע"כ צ"ל כן לר"נ דלא ס"ל כאידך דלקח זקנים למדרש עמוני וכו' עי"ש היטב] כמו כן הכא וכן בנוסחת הרי"ף שהיה לפני רבינו ירוחם לא הביא ג"כ הא דמר זוטרא וכן הרא"ה בהלכותיו כתב דהרי"ף לא הביא הא דמר זוטרא וכתב ע"ז ומשמע משום דבגמרא מקשינן עליה אימא וכו' עי"ש וכן כתב אחריו בשיטה מקובצת עי"ש וכמו שכתבנו ג"כ בטעם דבה"ג ולכן אף שפסקינן דבעינן תרי רבנן אעפ"כ אי לא שכיחי רבנן מסתברא שאינו מעכב מאחר שבה"ג והרי"ף [לפי גירסת הרא"ה והרי"ו] לא העתיקו כלל מימרא זו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If Torah scholars are not available, etc.</b> - See Biur HaGra, which implies that according to the Mechaber, who first ruled that only ten are required, there is no source for this law. Chemed Moshe likewise questioned it; see there. Nevertheless, in practice the Mechaber’s ruling appears correct. It is possible that even the Gra concedes that the presence of scholars is not indispensable. One who examines Halachos Gedolos will see that it does not quote Mar Zutra’s statement requiring two scholars at all. Perhaps it holds that since Rav Ashi challenged the statement repeatedly, he did not accept it. It may also hold that since earlier Amoraim, such as Rav Yehudah and Abaye, did not mention this requirement, the verse merely describes the ordinary practice of seeking elders so they would bless him. Rambam similarly writes that the listeners say, “May He Who bestowed good upon you,” and so forth, but not that elders are legally required. This parallels Bircas Chasanim according to Rav Nachman, who derives from “Boaz took ten men from the elders,” in Kesubos 7b. Certainly, “elders” there is not literal. He chose them so they would bless him, as the verse itself says that they blessed him. Rav Nachman must explain it this way because he does not accept the alternative derivation that elders were taken to expound the law concerning an Ammonite; examine the passage carefully. The same applies here. The version of Rif before Rabbeinu Yerucham also omitted Mar Zutra’s statement. HaRaah writes in his halachos that Rif did not include it and explains that this is apparently because the Gemara challenges it with “say instead,” and so forth; see there. Shitah Mekubetzes writes similarly, as does the explanation we gave for Halachos Gedolos. Therefore, although we rule that two Torah scholars are required, if scholars are unavailable it is reasonable that their absence does not invalidate the blessing, since Halachos Gedolos and Rif, according to the versions of HaRaah and Rabbeinu Yerucham, did not record this statement at all.

<b> וי"א שלא יצא</b> -  עיין בטור דהביא ראיה מדקאמר אביי בלשון צריך משמע דהוא רק למצוה בעלמא ובבה"ג משמע דחיובא יש בזה ע"ש אמנם הרא"ה דעתו בהלכותיו כהטור ע"ש. ודע דמדברי המחצית השקל ברי"ש הסימן משמע דלדעה אחרונה אם מברך בלא עשרה הוי ברכה לבטלה דלא נתקנה כלל בלא עשרה וא"כ לדבריו כיון שהמחבר לא הכריע בפלוגתא זו א"כ ממילא אינו רשאי לברך בלא עשרה אבל דעת רע"א בחידושיו דאפילו לדעה אחרונה אינו אסור לברך בלא עשרה דרך שבח והודאה דלא גרע מאדם דעלמא שמברך על חבירו ברכה זו אע"ג דלא נתחייב כלל וכמ"ש בהג"ה בס"ד וכ"ש איהו גופיה אלא דאם יזדמנו לו אח"כ עשרה צריך לברך עוד הפעם דרך חובה לדעה אחרונה ואמנם לכתחלה יש ליזהר שלא לברך בלא עשרה אפילו דרך שבח והודאה דאם יזדמן לו אח"כ עשרה לא יוכל לברך פעם שני דרך חובה דשמא קי"ל כדעה ראשונה וכבר יצא ע"ש [אכן אם כוון בהדיא שלא לצאת בהברכה אפשר דלכו"ע יכול לחזור ולברך דאפילו למ"ד מצות א"צ כוונה היינו בסתמא אבל לא בכוון בהדיא שלא לצאת ועיין]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say that he has not fulfilled his obligation</b> - See Tur, who proves from Abaye’s expression “he must” that the requirement is only the preferred manner of fulfilling the mitzvah. Halachos Gedolos, however, implies that there is an actual obligation; see there. HaRaah, in his halachos, agrees with Tur. Know that Machatzis HaShekel, at the beginning of the siman, implies that according to the latter opinion, one who recites the blessing without ten has recited a blessing in vain, because the blessing was never instituted without ten. According to him, since the Mechaber did not decide this dispute, one may not recite the blessing without ten. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, however, writes in his novellae that even according to the latter opinion it is not forbidden to recite it without ten as praise and thanksgiving. It is no less than an ordinary person reciting this blessing over another, although he has no obligation at all, as stated in the gloss in seif 4, and certainly the person himself may do so. If ten later become available, however, according to the latter opinion he must repeat the blessing as an obligation. Nevertheless, initially one should be careful not to recite it without ten, even merely as praise and thanksgiving, because if ten later become available he will not be able to repeat it as an obligation, since perhaps the halachah follows the first opinion and he already fulfilled his duty. See there. If, however, he explicitly intended not to fulfill his obligation with the first blessing, it is possible that all agree he may repeat it. Even according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent, that rule applies where one had no contrary intent, but not where one explicitly intended not to fulfill the mitzvah. This requires further examination.

<b>And he answered amen</b> - See Mishnah Berurah. Although elsewhere we rule that hearing is equivalent to responding, even without answering amen, as explained in siman 213, this case is different. The person reciting the blessing is himself not obligated in it and says it only as praise and thanksgiving for his fellow’s deliverance. Therefore, if the rescued person did not answer amen, he has not fulfilled his obligation. This is Tur in the name of Rosh. All this follows the view that one may recite the blessing as praise and thanksgiving for another’s deliverance even though one is not personally obligated. See below in Biur Halachah, s.v. “and this is not,” etc. Shitah Mekubetzes offers another explanation: since the one reciting the blessing does not intend to discharge the rescued person’s obligation, the latter must at least answer amen within the brief interval required for speech after the blessing. See there. According to this reasoning, it would appear that elsewhere too, if one answered amen, the person reciting the blessing need not intend to discharge him. In truth this is not so; see the end of siman 213. Perhaps this case is preferable because it is impossible to intend to discharge him using this wording, as we wrote in Shaar HaTziyun. Rabbi Akiva Eiger gives another reason. The principle that hearing is equivalent to responding cannot apply here, because even if the rescued person himself explicitly recited the blessing in this wording it would not help. The blessing is addressed to the rescued person and is appropriate only for another person thanking Hashem for his fellow’s benefit. He must therefore answer amen to his fellow’s blessing. By doing so, he too at least acknowledges Hashem for the goodness bestowed upon him. Without this, the blessing is entirely ineffective for him. This explanation is very correct.

<b>And this is not a blessing in vain</b> - Magen Avraham agrees and writes that this also appears to be the Mechaber’s view, since he states the ruling without qualification, unlike Beit Yosef, who wrote that one should rebuke a person who recites Bircas HaGomel over his fellow. Eliyah Rabbah, however, writes that Beit Yosef’s view is primary, and that apart from a son or student, others may not recite it. Olas Tamid likewise writes that this is the view of most poskim; see there. This also appears correct. The gloss, which quotes Tur in the name of Rosh, is inherently difficult. Where do we find blessings that one is entirely unrequired to recite, yet is nevertheless permitted to say? Although Shehecheyanu is described as optional, that does not mean entirely optional. Rather, it is not an absolute obligation, but there remains a mitzvah to recite it. Moreover, why should this blessing differ from the blessing over a miracle in siman 218? Throughout that siman the authorities investigate over which events one may recite a blessing and over which one may not, until it is ruled that one may not even bless over a miracle performed for the tribes unless it affected most of Israel. No authority there suggests that even absent an obligation one may recite the blessing because he rejoices over the rescue of thousands of Jews. Necessarily, they reject Tur’s reasoning. Rav Chanan and the Sages recited the blessing over Rav Yehudah only because he was their teacher, as Ramban, Rashba, and HaRaah explain at the beginning of the sugya, because one is likewise obligated to bless over a miracle performed for his teacher. Although HaRaah later offers another reason, that one who rejoices over another must recite Bircas HaGomel, he also writes that one who rejoices over another may recite the blessing over a miracle. In his view, the requirement of a father or teacher applies only when one reaches the place where the miracle occurred, not when seeing the person himself. Since we do not rule this way in siman 218, seif 6, in the gloss, and the place of the miracle requires specifically one’s father, teacher, or most of Israel, we should not rule according to that view for Bircas HaGomel either. It is unreasonable to give Bircas HaGomel greater scope than the blessing “Who performed miracles,” which was instituted specifically over a miracle. It therefore appears that without the relationship of father or teacher, it is improper to recite the blessing, even a father over his son and similar cases. See what Shaarei Teshuvah cites in the name of Birkei Yosef; perhaps his reasoning is as we have written. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 12, relies upon the gloss regarding a husband reciting it for his wife because one’s wife is like his own body. This too requires study. Just as a husband does not recite the blessing over miracles for his wife, as we wrote in siman 218, the same should apply here. For one’s father or teacher, however, it is reasonable that one must recite Bircas HaGomel, just as one must bless over a miracle performed for them, as in the Gemara’s account of Rav Chana of Baghdad, who blessed over his teacher Rav Yehudah. I saw that Eliyah Rabbah cites Ramban as holding that Bircas HaGomel is weaker: even for one’s father or teacher it is only optional, because the blessing for recovery rests upon the recovered person himself, not upon others. Meaning, this resembles the other ordinary human needs for which a person blesses every morning, such as “Who clothes the naked” and “Who releases the bound.” Could a student or son be required to bless because his teacher or father has clothing? A complete miracle is different, for one’s son or student must bless over it. Although elsewhere we do not find that the Sages permitted blessings where no obligation exists, perhaps because a complete miracle to one’s father or teacher itself creates an obligation, they also permitted Bircas HaGomel for them. Rambam entirely omits the incident of Rav Chana, and his reason requires study.
