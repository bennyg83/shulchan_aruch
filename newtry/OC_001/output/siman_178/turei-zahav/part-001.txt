<b>And likewise if they were reclining, etc. </b>Here there is no distinction whether some members remained behind or not, for even if some remained, another blessing is required, since fruit does not create a fixed meal, as explained below. According to Rav Sheshes there is no distinction in this respect. The word “likewise” does not equate every detail of the two cases; it means only that a change of location likewise requires a new blessing. This applies in every case. Although it is more obvious regarding fruit than regarding the bread discussed in the first clause that a new blessing is required, the wording “likewise” is nevertheless appropriate because here one is liable even when some members remained behind.

<b>From one corner, etc., he need not return and recite another blessing. </b>The phrase “need not return” might appear to mean that he also eats in the other corner, and then need not return here to recite the after-blessing but may recite it in the other corner. This cannot be correct, for if he eats in the second place, even when moving from one house to another he need not return to the first place, as stated in seif 4. The wording is somewhat imprecise; it should have said that he need not recite an after-blessing before moving.

<b>ודוקא שלא הניח כו'. </b> תמוה הוא כיון דקי"ל כר"ח עכשיו ממילא אין חילוק בזה כמש"ל דבפירות אפי' בהניח חייב לברך ובפת אפי' לא הניח א"צ לברך ובאמת הג"ה זו מן הסמ"ק שהעתיק ב"י וז"ל חבורה שהיו יושבים מסובים ועקרו רגליהם ללכת כשהניחו מקצת חברים אין טעונים ברכה לא למפרע ב"ה ולא המוציא אפילו בדברים שאינם טעונים ברכ' לאחריהם במקומן דאלו בדברים הטעונים אפי' הניחו נמי דשינוי מקום א"צ לברך לפניהם בדברים הטעונים כו' עכ"ל. ודבריו תמוהים דהא הסוגיא משמע דבדברי' שאינם טעונים דהיינו פירות אין חילוק בין הניח או לא וכמש"ל ומלבד זה כ' ב"י דאין הדברים של הסמ"ק מכוונים דכתב לא למפרע ב"ה משמע דאיירי בפת ואח"כ העמיד בדברים שאינ' טעונים ברכה במקומ' עכ"ל. ועוד תמוה מאוד שכ' תחלה אפי' בדברים שאינ' טעונים משמע שעכ"פ מיירי גם בדברים הטעונים אלא שלענין רבותא אמר אפי' באינם טעונים וזה סותר מ"ש אחר כך. ונ"ל דחסרון תיב' א' יש שם דהיינו תיבת הניח וההצעה כ"ה דמ"ש הסמ"ק עד אפי' הוא לשונו מן הפנים ומן אפילו והולך הוא הגהת סמ"ק שהוא רבינו פרץ דהפנים ס"ל כר"ש וע"כ כתב תנא דהניח לענין פת ובהג"ה חולק על הפנים וכת' שלא קי"ל כך אלא אפי' הניח בדברים שאינם טעונים לאחריהם במקומ' דאלו כו' ור"ל דלא קי"ל כר"ש אלא כר"ח ומ"ה באינם טעונים אפי' הניח צריך ברכה ובטעונים אפילו לא הניח א"צ ברכה. כלל העולה שאליבא שיטת התוספות והרא"ש לא איכפת לן כלל בהניח או לא ובסמ"ג יש ג"כ חילוק דהניח  ויתבאר בסמוך פירושו ע"כ אין מקום לדברי רמ"א בזו לחלק בין הניח או לא אלא בין טעוני' לאין טעוני':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Specifically when he did not leave, etc. </b>This is difficult. Since we now rule like Rav Chisda, there should be no distinction of this kind. Regarding fruit, even if some remained behind, one must recite another blessing; regarding bread, even if none remained, no new blessing is required. This gloss is taken from Semak, as cited by Beit Yosef: “A group who were reclining and then rose to leave, if they left some members behind, do not require an after-blessing, neither Bircas HaMazon nor HaMotzi, even for foods that do not require an after-blessing in their original place; for foods that do require an after-blessing in their original place, even if they left none behind, a change of location does not require another blessing before eating.” These words are difficult, for the sugya implies that regarding foods not requiring an after-blessing in their original place, namely fruit, there is no distinction whether some remained or not. Beit Yosef also writes that Semak’s wording is imprecise, since his mention of Bircas HaMazon implies bread, while he then applies the ruling to foods that do not require an after-blessing in their place. There is a further difficulty: he first says “even foods that do not require,” implying that the ruling certainly applies to foods that do require, yet afterward he says the opposite. It appears to me that one word is missing, namely, “remained.” The correct structure is as follows. Semak’s main text, through the word “even,” follows Rav Sheshes and therefore discusses leaving some members behind with respect to bread. The gloss, written by Rabbeinu Peretz, disagrees with the main text and says that this is not the accepted ruling. Rather, “even if some remained behind, for foods that do not require an after-blessing in their original place,” another blessing is required; “whereas for foods that do require,” even if none remained, no new blessing is needed. In other words, we rule like Rav Chisda rather than Rav Sheshes. Thus, for foods not requiring an after-blessing in their place, another blessing is required even if some remained; for foods requiring it, no new blessing is required even if none remained. The general conclusion is that according to Tosafos and the Rosh, whether some remained is irrelevant. Semag, however, also distinguishes based on whether some remained, as will be explained below. Rama therefore has no basis here to distinguish between leaving some behind and leaving none. The distinction is instead between foods that require an after-blessing in their original place and those that do not.

בגן אחד ואינו מוקף מחיצה סביב הוי כמגן לגן שכ"כ ב"י בפי' דברי הרמב"ם כמ"ש בס"ב אכל במזרחה של תאנה כו' ואף שכ' אח"כ הטעם דברואה מקומו הראשון תליא מילתא מ"מ כאן הוא דמן אילן שני רוצה לאכול הוי  דינו כמגן לגן דלא מהני רואה המקום הראשון כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Rather, in the second place, etc. </b>It appears that this concerns one who originally intended to eat there, similar to travelers, whom the Rama mentions immediately afterward. This poses a difficulty to Rama’s ruling in seif 1 that prior intention helps only within one house, whereas between two houses, according to the first opinion, one must recite an after-blessing before leaving. Why is the present case different? Necessarily, even the first opinion, which follows Rav Sheshes, agrees that if one intended to eat elsewhere, no new blessing is required, because this is not considered a change of location. This is contrary to Rama’s gloss. Yet there remains a difficulty from the rule below concerning moving from one garden to another, where prior intention does not help. It appears to me that even the first opinion distinguishes between bread and fruit regarding prior intention to eat elsewhere. Since bread creates a fixed meal, prior intention to eat there is effective and no new blessing is needed. Regarding fruit, prior intention is ineffective because the two acts of eating are not joined. This contradicts Rama’s earlier gloss. There is no difficulty, however, from the Shulchan Aruch’s statement in seif 2 that when they return they must recite another blessing, because there the case is not that they eat in the second place; rather, they return to finish the meal in the first place. If one eats in the second place and had intended to do so, no new blessing is required. Rama’s requirement that both places be in one house is therefore difficult. His proof from siman 273 is not comparable. There, one does not eat at all in the place where Kiddush is recited but intends to eat only in the second place, so the two are considered one place of the meal only if they are in one house. Here, however, one eats in the first place as well. If he intended to continue eating elsewhere, the places need not be in one house. This appears correct to me. As practical halachah, since we rule like Tosafos, Rashbam, and the Rosh, whom we generally follow, the law is as follows. Ideally, a person should not leave a meal at all before Bircas HaMazon, unless a passing mitzvah requires it, such as when the time for prayer has arrived. In that case, even if he did not originally intend to leave, our practice is to do so. At a seudas mitzvah, such as a wedding feast, people commonly eat a little in their homes and then go to the wedding feast to finish the meal, provided that they originally intended to go there. Travelers likewise recite the after-blessing where they finish eating. If a person went elsewhere without intending to eat there and then returned to his meal, if he had eaten something requiring an after-blessing in its original place, as described in seif 5, he need not recite another initial blessing, and certainly need not first recite Bircas HaMazon. If he ate fruit or drank wine, left, and returned, he must recite a new initial blessing over what he drinks upon returning. If he diverted his attention from eating when he left, then in every case he must recite a new blessing over what he subsequently eats. Regarding fruit in a garden, the matter depends on whether it is one garden. If it is one garden, prior intention helps for everything in the garden. It appears that even one garden, if it is not enclosed by a surrounding wall, is treated like moving from one garden to another. Beit Yosef writes this in explaining Rambam’s case of eating on the east side of a fig tree, as stated in seif 2. Although he later writes that the rule depends on seeing the original place, here, since one wishes to eat from a second tree, the law is like moving from one garden to another, and seeing the original place does not help. This appears correct to me.
