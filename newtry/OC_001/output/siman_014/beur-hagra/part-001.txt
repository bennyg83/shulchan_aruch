<b>ס"א ויש מחמירין כו'. </b>כמ"ש בפ' השולח בתפלין וקשרתם וכתבתם כו'. וס' הראשונה ס"ל דוקא בתפלין דנתיב עיכובא וראיה ממה שהצריכו ב"י למעוטי עו"ג ועוד סוכת גנב"ך ובפ' הספינה דביתהו דר"ח בן דוסא אבל הג"מ כ' בשם מהר"מ דמבני ישראל ממעטינן בנות ישראל כמ"ש בכמה מקומות והא דפ' הספינה היינו בטוית ציצית דאף בתפלין בכה"ג כשר באשה כמ"ש בע"ג ל"ט א' ע"ש פ"א מהלכות ציצית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 1, "And there are those who are stringent, etc."</b> As is stated in the chapter "HaSholeach" regarding tefillin: "and you shall bind them" and "and you shall write them," etc. The first opinion holds that this applies specifically to tefillin, where Scripture wrote an indispensable requirement. A proof is that the Beit Yosef needed a derivation to exclude a gentile. Also, there is the case of a sukkah made by gentiles, women, animals, or Cutheans, and in the chapter "HaSefinah" the case of the wife of Rabbi Chanina ben Dosa. But the Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Maharam that from "the children of Israel" we exclude the daughters of Israel, as in several places. And the case in the chapter "HaSefinah" concerns spinning tzitzit, for even regarding tefillin, in such a case, a woman is valid, as stated in Avodah Zarah 39a; see there, chapter 1 of the Laws of Tzitzit.

<b>ס"ב הטיל כו'. </b>דרמב"ם אזיל לשיטתו שפי' מש"ש מ"ב ב' מן הגרדין כו' לאו משום לשמה כמש"ל סי"א ס"ה וראיה מדס"ל לרב ציצית בנכרי כשרה וגט בנכרי פסול משום דבעינן לשמה כמש"ש כ"ג א' ואף ללישנא בתרא דוקא משום דכתיב מיעוטא וז"ש בסוכה ט' א' אלמא בעינן טויה לשמה ל"פ אלא מטויה וכן לפירש"י. וע"ל סי' ט"ו ס"ד אבל לפי' תוס' מן הגרדין כו' צריך לשמה וכ"כ הרא"ש ואזיל לשיטתו דס"ל דאין ראיה מגט וכמ"ש בע"ג כ"ז במילה דכשרה לר' יהודה ועמש"ל סי' י"א ס"ב וס"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 2, "If he inserted, etc."</b> The Rambam follows his own view, for he explained what is stated there, 42b, "from the remnants, etc.," as not being due to the requirement of being made for its own sake, as I wrote above in siman 11, section 5. The proof is that Rav holds that tzitzit made by a gentile are valid, while a bill of divorce written by a gentile is invalid because it must be written for its own sake, as stated there, 23a. Even according to the second version, this is specifically because an exclusion is written. This is what is stated in Sukkah 9a: "Evidently, we require spinning for its own sake"; they disagree only about spinning. So too according to Rashi's explanation. See above, siman 15, section 4. But according to the explanation of Tosafot, "from the remnants, etc." means that it must be made for its own sake, and the Rosh wrote likewise. He follows his own view, holding that there is no proof from a bill of divorce, as is stated in Avodah Zarah 27 regarding circumcision, which is valid according to Rabbi Yehudah. See what I wrote above in siman 11, sections 2 and 5.

<b>"And the same applies to tefillin."</b> This means: although we say in Bava Metzia 29b, "One who finds tefillin, etc.," that does not mean that otherwise it would be forbidden to put them on. Rather, it comes to teach a permission, unlike the Nimukei Yosef.
