<small>מג"א ס"ק א' </small>פי' שהטילן וכו'. בנז"ש כ' ולא ידעתי מנ"ל הא כיון בטוי' כל הפוסקי' ס"ל דבעי' לשמה כשר להרא"ש בעע"ג. מכ"ש בעשיי' דלהרמב"ם ל"צ שיהי' לשמה דכשר להרא"ש בעע"ג. ומחכ"ת לא דק דשאני עשיי' דאין פסולן משום שלא לשמה אלא דגזה"כ הוא דממעט' מקרא בני ישראל ועשו ולא נכרי מש"ה לא מהני עע"ג. ופשוט. ובזה מיושב להרא"ש דס"ל עשיי' צריך לשמה למ"ל קרא בני ישראל הא ממילא פסול משום שלא לשמה ע' ט"ז סק"ב. דלהרא"ש לשיטתי' דבעע"ג מקרי לשמה צריך קרא ב"י ועשו למעט נכרי בעע"ג והרמב"ם לטעמא דס"ל היכי דבעי' לשמה לא מהני עע"ג שפיר מוכח שטתו דעשיי' ל"צ לשמה מדאצטריך קרא בני ישראל למעט נכרי:
**** ENGLISH ****
Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: “This means that he attached them,” etc. Nezer HaKodesh writes: I do not know his source. Since all authorities hold that the spinning must be performed lishmah, yet according to the Rosh it is valid when a Jew stands over a non-Jew and supervises him, all the more so should this apply to the making of the tzitzit, which according to the Rambam need not be performed lishmah; according to the Rosh, it should be valid under a Jew's supervision. With due respect, he was not precise. Making the tzitzit is different: its invalidity is not due to a lack of lishmah. Rather, it is a specific scriptural decree derived from “The children of Israel shall make,” excluding a non-Jew. Therefore, a Jew's supervision does not help. This is straightforward. This also resolves a question regarding the Rosh, who holds that making the tzitzit requires lishmah: why is the verse “The children of Israel shall make” needed, when a non-Jew's work should already be invalid because it was not performed lishmah? See Turei Zahav, se'if katan 2. According to the Rosh's own view, supervision by a Jew renders the act lishmah; the verse is therefore needed to exclude a non-Jew even when a Jew supervises him. The Rambam, for his part, holds that where lishmah is required, a Jew's supervision does not help. His view that the making of tzitzit does not require lishmah is therefore correctly demonstrated by the fact that a verse was needed to exclude a non-Jew.

<small>מג"א ס"ק ד' </small>כמ"ש רסי' ט"ו. כאן א"צ לזה דאף אם הי' הדין דמותר להחזירם לבגד כיון דכבר הי' עשוי בפעם א' בהכשר מ"מ ניחא הכא. דאם ניחוש לדעת הרא"ש ממילא לא הי' עשיי' מעולם בהכשר. כיון דהי' בלא כוונה ממילא הוי תחלת עשיי' ובציר לי' שיעורא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 4.</small> “As stated at the beginning of siman 15.” Here that reason is unnecessary. Even if the law permitted returning them to the garment because they had once been validly made, the present case is still understandable. If we take Rosh's view into account, the tzitzis were never validly made at all, since they were attached without intention. Reattaching them would therefore constitute the initial making, at which point the required length would be lacking.

**** ENGLISH ****
There, in the same passage: “And it must be said that the case concerns Shabbat.” Note: This means that if he unties them and then makes them again, the making will take place on Shabbat; that is, it is so close to Shabbat that there is time only to make one set. See Tav-Shin.
