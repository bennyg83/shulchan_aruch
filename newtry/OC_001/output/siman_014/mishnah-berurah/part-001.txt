(א) שעשאן - פי' שהטילן בבגד פסול אפילו בישראל עומד על גבו ומלמדהו לעשות לשמה דאלו אם טוואן א"י או שזרן באופן זה כשר להרא"ש כמש"כ לעיל בסימן י"א ס"ב ודוקא אם תחבו בכנף או עשה החוליא או הקשר ראשון אבל אם החוליא והקשר ראשון עשה ישראל והא"י גמר שאר החוליות והקשרים דאינן רק למצוה בעלמא כשר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(1) That they made.</b> Meaning, they attached the strings to the garment. It is invalid even if a Jew stands over the non-Jew and teaches him to act for the sake of the mitzvah. If, however, the non-Jew merely spun or twisted the strings under such supervision, it is valid according to Rosh, as explained above in siman 11, seif 2. The invalidity applies specifically if the non-Jew inserted the strings into the corner, made the first winding, or tied the first knot. If the Jew made the first winding and knot and the non-Jew completed the remaining windings and knots, which are only an enhancement of the mitzvah, it is valid.

<b>(7) Regarding Rambam, who validates.</b> This applies even if he inserted the strings and made all the knots and windings without intention. Rambam maintains that although spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah, attaching the strings need not be. He derives this from the verse's exclusion of a non-Jew: if a non-Jew is excluded specifically, a Jew who acts without intention is valid. One might ask that the strings should be removed and reattached with proper intention. The answer is that the case concerns one close to Shabbos, without enough time to remove and retie them. Alternatively, one end of a string may have been severed, so that if the strings were removed it would be forbidden to reattach them, as explained in siman 12.

<b>(8) He should not recite a blessing over it.</b> This applies even if only the insertion into the corner was done without intention, and all the more so if the upper knot was tied without intention. (יא) מברך עליה - ויש מחמירין וסוברין שלא לברך אא"כ נתן לו בפירוש במתנה ע"מ להחזיר אך רוב האחרונים מסכימים לפסק השו"ע והטעם הוא דהא אדעתא דהכי השאילה לו שיברך עליה וכיון שא"א לו לברך אא"כ תהיה שלו הוי כאילו נתנה לו במתנה ע"מ להחזיר. ולכתחלה בודאי יותר טוב לבקש מהמשאיל שיתן לו במתנ' ע"מ להחזיר ולא לעיכובא. ודוקא אם שאל ממנו טלית המיוחד למצוה אבל אם הוא בגד העומד ללבישה ורק כיון שפתוח רובו עושין בו ציצית ולא נעשה בשביל מצות ציצית וכן אם שאל טלית לעלות לתורה או לעבור לפני התיבה או לדוכן שאינו אלא משום הכבוד אין מברכין עליו דאולי היה רצונו להשאילו רק למלבוש לחוד ולא להקנות לו ויש חולקין בזה וס"ל דבכל גווני מברכין ע"כ כתב בדרך החיים דיותר טוב שיכוין בכל אלו שלא לקנות כדי שלא יצטרך לברך לכו"ע לבד מהטלית ששאל בעת התפלה לצאת בו. וכ"ז בטליתות של איש פרטי אבל בטליתות של קהל כעין שלנו המצויות בבתי כנסיות צריך לברך עליו לכו"ע אפילו כשלובשו רק לעבור לפני התיבה או לעלות לתורה משום דטלית של קהל אדעתא דהכי קנוהו מתחלה שכל מי שלובש אותו שיהיה שלו כמו באתרוג ועיין מש"כ בבה"ל בשם השערי אפרים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(11) He recites a blessing over it.</b> Some are stringent and maintain that he should not recite a blessing unless the lender explicitly gave it as a gift on condition that it be returned. Most later authorities agree with Shulchan Aruch. Since the lender lent it so that the borrower could recite the blessing, and the borrower cannot recite the blessing unless it belongs to him, it is treated as though given as a gift on condition that it be returned.

Initially it is certainly preferable to ask the lender to give it explicitly as a gift on condition that it be returned, but this is not indispensable. This applies when he borrowed a tallis specifically designated for the mitzvah. If it is an ordinary garment worn as clothing, with tzitzis attached only because most of it is open and it has four corners, or if he borrowed a tallis merely to receive an aliyah, lead the prayers, or ascend to duchan, all of which are matters of honor, he does not recite a blessing. Perhaps the owner intended to lend it only as clothing, not transfer ownership. Some disagree and maintain that one recites a blessing in every case. Derech HaChayim therefore writes that in all such cases it is preferable to intend not to acquire it, so that according to all opinions no blessing is required, except when borrowing a tallis during prayer in order to fulfill the mitzvah.

This discussion concerns a privately owned tallis. Regarding communal talleisim, such as those commonly found in synagogues, everyone agrees that one recites a blessing even when wearing it only to lead the prayers or receive an aliyah. The community purchased it with the intention that each wearer acquire it, like a communal esrog. See Biur Halachah in the name of Shaarei Ephraim.

<b>(12) Immediately.</b> See siman 658, seif 5, regarding a lulav, and Peri Megadim there. The same principle applies here. He must return the tallis to the giver at the proper time. If the owner already fulfilled the mitzvah that day, he must at least return it before prayer the next morning, so that the owner will have his own tallis with which to fulfill the mitzvah. If he did not return it and the owner has no other tallis, the gift appears to be retroactively void and it is not called “your garment.” Even if the owner has a tallis katan, it is reasonable that he did not give the tallis on that understanding, unlike Nehar Shalom.

It likewise appears obvious that if the tallis or one of its tzitzis was damaged while in the borrower's possession, even through unavoidable circumstances, and he did not return it in the condition in which he received it, he does not fulfill the mitzvah. <b>(18) Is obligated.</b> This refers to a case where the other partner consents to his wearing it. If partners own a tallis with tzitzis and one objects to the other's wearing it, then even if the garment is divisible and each half contains the minimum tallis size, one who wears it through theft and recites a blessing violates the prohibition against taking Hashem's Name in vain, because the half containing two of the tzitzis does not belong to him.
