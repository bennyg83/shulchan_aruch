<b>(1) Rosh Chodesh Adar, etc.</b> I will briefly explain the four special parshiyos. Chazal instituted the reading of four parshiyos each year from Rosh Chodesh Adar until Rosh Chodesh Nisan in remembrance of four matters. The first is Parshas Shekalim, in remembrance of the mitzvah of the half shekel, which will be explained below. Each year the Jewish people were obligated to contribute it to the chamber for the daily offerings. The second is Parshas Zachor, to remember what Amalek did. It is read on the Shabbos before Purim so that it is adjacent to the event involving Haman, who descended from Amalek, and so that the remembrance of eradicating Amalek precedes its performance, as the verse states, “These days are remembered and performed.” The third is Parshas Parah Adumah, read on the Shabbos before Parshas HaChodesh. The red cow was burned in the wilderness shortly before Nisan so that the Jewish people could be sprinkled with the ashes of the sin offering immediately after the Mishkan was erected. They would thereby be pure and able to offer the Pesach sacrifice at its proper time. We therefore read this parshah and pray before Hashem that He should likewise sprinkle pure water upon us speedily. The fourth is Parshas HaChodesh, read on the Shabbos near Rosh Chodesh Nisan in order to sanctify the month of Nisan, as the Torah states, “This month shall be for you the first of the months.” This reading is not the essential sanctification of the month, because the essential sanctification occurs when the new moon is seen and the beis din sanctifies the month. This reading is only a rabbinic enactment.

(ב) קורין פרשת שקלים - דאמר קרא זאת עולת חודש בחדשו לחדשי השנה והאי לחדשי יתירא הוא אלא אמרה תורה יש לך חודש שאתה צריך לחדשו בהבאת עולות תמידין ומוספין מתרומה חדשה וזהו ניסן דגמרינן בגמרא בג"ש שחודש זה הוא חודש ניסן וכיון דבניסן בעי לאקרובי מתרומה חדשה לכך מקדמינן ומשמיעין על השקלים באדר הסמוך לו שיביאו שקליהם בר"ח ניסן ואנו משלמין פרים שפתינו בקריאת הפרשה של כי תשא דכתיב בה ענין השקלים. יוצר יש לשחרית וגם למוסף בפרשת שקלים ובפרשת החודש אבל בזכור ופרה לא תקנו במוסף [פמ"ג] אם אירע שלא קראו הפרשה מענינו של יום אין לה תשלומין לשבת הבאה [שערי אפרים]. אם שכחו הצבור לקרות פרשת שקלים וה"ה בשארי השלשה פרשיות אם כבר אמר הפטרה של סדר השבוע בברכותיה ונזכרו צריכים לחזור ולהוציא ס"ת ולקרות הפרשה בברכה לפניה ולאחריה ויאמר אח"כ קדיש ויפטיר בלא ברכה לפניה ולאחריה. ואם נזכרו אחר שהתחיל לברך על הפטרה ואמר השם יגמור הברכה ויאמר קצת פסוקים מהפטרת השבוע [כדי שתהיה לברכה מקום לחול] ובלא ברכה אחריה ויוציאו ס"ת ויקרא עולה אחד מענינו של יום ואחר ברכה אחרונה יאמר קדיש ויקרא הפטרה בלא ברכה לפניה והברכות שלאחריה יברך. ואם נזכרו מיד אחר שבירך ברכה אחרונה על התורה יוציאו ס"ת אחרת ויניח אצל הראשונה ויאמר קדיש ואח"כ יקרא אחר שיפטיר מענינו של יום בברכותיה [שם] אם טעו וקראו הפרשיות והמגילה באדר ראשון צריך לחזור ולקרות המגילה בשני ולענין הפרשיות בב"י משמע דא"צ לחזור אבל בד"מ ובא"ר כתבו דצריך לחזור:

<b>(2) Parshas Shekalim is read.</b> The verse states, “This is the burnt offering of each month in its month, for the months of the year.” The word “for the months” is superfluous. The Torah thereby teaches that there is a month whose offerings must be renewed through the use of a new contribution for the daily and Mussaf offerings. This is Nisan, as the Gemara derives through a gezeirah shavah that the month referred to here is Nisan. Since the offerings in Nisan must come from the new contribution, the announcement concerning the shekalim is made in the preceding month of Adar so that the people will bring their shekalim by Rosh Chodesh Nisan. We fulfill the verse, “Our lips will substitute for bulls,” by reading the portion of Ki Sisa, which discusses the shekalim. Yotzer piyyutim are recited at Shacharis and also at Mussaf on Parshas Shekalim and Parshas HaChodesh, but no Mussaf piyyutim were instituted for Parshas Zachor or Parshas Parah, as stated by Peri Megadim. If the special parshah of that day was not read, it cannot be made up on the following Shabbos, as stated by Shaarei Ephraim. If the congregation forgot to read Parshas Shekalim, and the same applies to the other three special parshiyos, and they already recited the weekly haftorah with its blessings before remembering, they must again take out a Sefer Torah and read the special parshah with the blessings before and after it. They then recite Kaddish and read the special haftorah without a blessing before or after it. If they remembered after the reader began the opening blessing over the haftorah and had already pronounced Hashem's Name, he should complete that blessing and recite a few verses from the weekly haftorah so that the blessing has a subject upon which to take effect, but he should not recite its concluding blessing. They should then take out a Sefer Torah, one person should read the special parshah, and after the concluding Torah blessing Kaddish should be recited. The special haftorah should then be read without its opening blessing, but the concluding blessings should be recited. If they remembered immediately after the concluding blessing over the Torah reading, they should take out another Sefer Torah, place it beside the first, and recite Kaddish. Another person should then read the special parshah as maftir and recite its haftorah with the blessings, as stated there. If they mistakenly read the special parshiyos and the Megillah during the first Adar of a leap year, the Megillah must be read again during the second Adar. Regarding the special parshiyos, Beis Yosef implies that they need not be repeated, but Darkei Moshe and Eliyah Rabbah write that they must be repeated.

16) <b>To a place where there is a minyan, etc.</b> This means even if he has a Torah scroll in his house but does not have a minyan in his house. Even if we say that a minyan for Parashas Zachor is only rabbinic, in any case the main reading of Zachor is Torah-level. Terumas HaDeshen wrote that hearing Parashas Zachor read with ten is more important than reading the megillah publicly, for according to most poskim, reading the megillah in its proper time suffices individually. Therefore, if he cannot fulfill both, he should seek to fulfill Parashas Zachor publicly. But Magen Avraham inclines that it is better to come to a place where they read the megillah publicly, and regarding Parashas Zachor he can fulfill, in pressing circumstances, through hearing the reading on Purim of Parashas Vayavo Amalek, for through this too he remembers the deed of Amalek and fulfills his obligation. That is to say, he fulfills Torah-level. Since we hold above in siman 60 that mitzvos require intent, at the time of hearing this section he would need to intend to fulfill the positive commandment of Zachor through it. But in my humble opinion, the main ruling of Magen Avraham requires analysis, for in the Torah it is written: "Remember what Amalek did to you, etc., how he met you on the way and cut off your rear, etc.; you shall erase, etc." The intent is not to forget what Amalek did to us, and that we tell this to our children and to our generations, saying to them, "such the wicked one did to us," and therefore we were commanded to erase his name, as the Ramban wrote in his explanation. This is not mentioned in Parashas Vayavo Amalek.
