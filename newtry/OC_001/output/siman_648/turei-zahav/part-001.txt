<b>ע"י שיעביר בו מחט. </b> כ"כ הרא"ש וטור וק' הא יהי' בו נקב מפולש והוא פסול לדעת הרא"ש והטור וכ"פ בש"ע בס"ב לדיע' קמייתא י"ל דאין פוסל מפולש אלא כשניקב מעבר זה לעבר שכנגדו או לחדרי הזרע אבל אם הנקב בחד צד מפולש דהיינו בעובי שלה  ודאי אין פוסל. לכ"ע בלא חסרון דהא דיני אתרוג אנו ילפי' מטריפות של בהמה ובטריפות אם יש נקב מפולש בעובי האבר כגון בלב או מעיים ואינו מגיע לחלל ודאי אין טריפות ה"נ כאן באתרוג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>By passing a needle through it.</b> So wrote the Rosh and the Tur. It is difficult: this will make a through-and-through hole in it, and it is invalid according to the Rosh and the Tur, and so the Shulchan Aruch ruled in se'if 2 according to the first view. One can say that a through-and-through hole invalidates only when it is punctured from this side to the opposite side, or to the seed chambers. But if the hole on one side goes through, meaning through its thickness, it certainly does not invalidate according to all opinions without deficiency. For the laws of esrog are learned from the laws of tereifot of an animal; and regarding tereifot, if there is a through-and-through hole in the thickness of an organ, such as in the heart or intestines, and it does not reach the cavity, it certainly is not a tereifah. So too here with an esrog.

<b>משנה שעברה כו'. </b> משמע בזה לא מהני בדיקת חוט הנזכר וז"ל תשו' מהרי"ל סי' ה' ואתרוג ישן של אשתקד לשנה זו א"א שלא נתייבש דשיעור יבשות פי' הראב"ד שאינו מוציא ליחה ע"י חוט עכ"ל ולפי משמעות לשונו נראה דה"ק דודאי בכלל יבשות הוא דשיעורו שאינו מוציא ליחה וע"כ צריך בדיקה ויש תימה על רמ"א דמשמע דלא מהני בדיקה מדכתב ע"ז ופסול ואיני יודע למה לא מהני בדיקה כיון שיש בו לחלוחית בפנינו ואפשר דרמ"א נמי אמר דאז צריך בדיקה ובגמרא דף ל"א איתא אתרוג הישן פסול ור"י מכשיר והתם טעמא דת"ק משום דלאו הדר הוא מבואר שם והיינו שנתכווין ומפסיד הנוי שלו ולפ"ז אם אנו רואין שנשאר בנוי שלו מהני בו בדיקת חוט וע"ל בסי' זה כתבתי דין אם מקיימין אותו בדבש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From the previous year, etc.</b> This implies that the aforementioned thread test is not effective for it. This is the language of Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 5: an old esrog from last year for this year cannot possibly have failed to dry out, for the Ra'avad explained the measure of dryness as that it does not produce moisture by means of a thread. End quote. According to the implication of his language, it appears that this is what he means: certainly it is included in dryness, whose measure is that it does not produce moisture, and therefore it requires checking. There is surprise regarding the Rema, who implies that checking is ineffective, since he wrote about this: and it is invalid. I do not know why checking should not help when it has moisture before us. Perhaps the Rema too means that then it requires checking. In the Gemara, daf 31, it states that an old esrog is invalid, while Rabbi Yehudah validates; and there the reason of the first tanna is because it is not beautiful, as explained there, meaning that it has shriveled and lost its beauty. According to this, if we see that it remains in its beauty, the thread test is effective for it. See below in this siman, where I wrote the law if they preserve it in honey.

<b>אם היה כאיסר. </b> אבל פחות מאיסר ואין שם חסרון כגון שנעץ שם יתד כשר ואם חסר כל שהוא אפי' באינו מפולש וי"א הוא הראב"ד ס"ל דתרתי לריעותא בעינן מפולש וחסרון דבלא חסרון כשר בכל גווני אלא ביש חסרון יש חלוק דאם הוא מפולש פסול אפי' בחסרון כל שהוא ובאינו מפולש דוק' כאיסר וקיי"ל להחמיר זולת בשעת הדחק כמ"ש רמ"א אח"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it was the size of an issar.</b> But if it is less than an issar and there is no deficiency there, such as if he inserted a peg there, it is valid. If any amount is missing, even if the hole does not go through, it is invalid. The 'some say' is the Ra'avad, who holds that we require two defects: a through-and-through hole and deficiency, for without deficiency it is valid in every case. Only where there is deficiency is there a distinction: if it is through-and-through, it is invalid even with any amount of deficiency; and if it is not through-and-through, specifically the size of an issar. We rule stringently except in pressing circumstances, as the Rema writes afterward.

<b>אע"פ שיש בהם חסרון. שזהו דרך גדילתן. </b> יליף לה בת"ה מטריפות דריאה אם יש ריאה חסירה שיש בה  קמט וסדק אם עולה בנפיחה ועור ובשר קיים כשרה והיינו כזה באתרוג הנעש' לו בשעת גידולו עור ובשרו קיים כשרה אבל כל מה שיחסר לו אחר שנתלש א"א שיהיה עורו וגופו קיים עכ"ל מבואר כוונתו דאנו רואים שנקרם עור האתרוג במקום החסרון ההוא רק שיש חסרון וגומא שם בזה התיר אבל אם אנו רואים שלא נקרם אלא יש שם נקב וחסרון אע"פ שנעשה ע"י קוצים בדרך גדילתו פסול וזה שסיים ומיהו אם רואה כו' והנהו קוצים לא מעלין ולא מורידין לענין נקב אלא שזכר אות' בת"ה לפי שאז רגיל להיות שם בנקב עורו קיים והמון עם טועים בכך שסוברים שיש קול' כשנעשה הנקב ע"י קוצים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even though there is deficiency in them, for this is their manner of growth.</b> He derives this in Terumat HaDeshen from the tereifah law of a lung: if there is a deficient lung that has a wrinkle and a crack, if it rises with inflation and the skin and flesh remain intact, it is valid. This is like an esrog that became this way at the time of its growth, with its skin and flesh intact, and it is valid. But anything missing from it after it was detached cannot have its skin and body intact. End quote. His intent is clear: we see that the skin of the esrog has formed over the place of that deficiency, only that there is a deficiency and depression there; in this case he permitted it. But if we see that it has not formed over, but rather there is a hole and deficiency there, even though it was made by thorns in its manner of growth, it is invalid. This is why he concluded: however, if he sees, etc. Those thorns neither raise nor lower anything regarding the law of a hole; he mentioned them in Terumat HaDeshen only because then it is common that the skin remains intact in that hole. The masses err in this, thinking there is a leniency when the hole was made by thorns.

<b>שאין העור והבשר קיים. </b> משמע דאם יש ספק בזה אם הבשר קיים אזלינן לקולא כיון שהדיעה האחרונה שהיא הראב"ד ס"ל אפי' בוודאי יש חסרון כשר כל שאינו מפולש ואין החסרון כאיסר שפיר נסמוך עליה בספק לכל הפחות ומ"ה נ"ל שכתב רמ"א כאן ובשעת הדחק יש להקל כסברא אחרונה ולא כתבו תכף אחר הפלוגתא אלא להורות כאן דאין איסור אלא ברואין שאין הבשר קיים  אבל בספק כשר שהרי בשעת הדחק סמכינן אסברא אחרונה אפי' בודאי אין הבשר קיים ונ"ל פשוט דזה מיירי שעל כל פנים לא ניקב עד חדרי הזרע  דאל"כ אפי' הבשר קיים יש פסול מטעם נקב מפולש כמו בסמוך  ואם יש ספק אם מגיע לחדרי זרע ג"כ כשר כיון שאין חסרון כלל מטעם שזכרתי כיון שלהראב"ד כשר אפי' במפולש כל שאין בו חסרון כלל כנ"ל בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That the skin and flesh are not intact.</b> This implies that if there is doubt whether the flesh remains intact, we go leniently, since the latter view, which is the Ra'avad, holds that even if there is certainly a deficiency it is valid whenever it is not through-and-through and the deficiency is not the size of an issar. We may therefore properly rely on it in a case of doubt at least. For this reason it appears to me that the Rema wrote here, 'and in pressing circumstances one may be lenient like the latter view,' and did not write it immediately after the dispute, in order to teach here that there is prohibition only when one sees that the flesh is not intact; but in a case of doubt it is valid. For in pressing circumstances we rely on the latter view even when the flesh is certainly not intact. It appears obvious to me that this speaks where, in any case, it was not punctured until the seed chambers; for otherwise, even if the flesh remains intact, there is invalidity because of a through-and-through hole, as nearby. And if there is doubt whether it reaches the seed chambers, it is also valid, since there is no deficiency at all, for the reason I mentioned: according to the Ra'avad it is valid even with a through-and-through hole whenever there is no deficiency at all, as it appears to me in this matter.

אפשר לפרש דכל עובי האתרוג עד חדרי זרע קרוי קליפה כמ"ש ב"י דלא משתמע הכי בלשון כלל אלא הקליפה החיצונה היא שיש בה חריפות אם ניקבה כולה מצד אחד הוה מפולש ע"כ ולא דק בזה דכל עובי האתרוג עד חדרי זרע זולת הקליפה הדקה שיש בה חריפות ולא מקרי מפולש אלא עד חדרי זרע וא"צ להאריך מזה עוד כי דבר ברור הוא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That it was punctured from one side to the other.</b> This is the view of the Ba'al Halachot Gedolot in Tosafot, that it is called through-and-through only in that case. But the view of Tosafot is that if it was punctured until the seed chambers it is invalid even without deficiency. That which we require through-and-through means when it is not opposite those chambers; but if it was punctured in the place of the chambers, it is through-and-through, for those chambers are like the bronchi in a lung. This is the 'some explain' that he brings here. In the Ran it is written as follows: Some are stringent and say that the peel is a distinct entity, and whenever all of it was punctured from one side, it is called a through-and-through hole. End quote. This is the view of Tosafot that I mentioned, that the entire thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers is called peel. This is as is found earlier there in the Gemara regarding terumah, that one may not take an esrog of terumah because he ruins it. Rashi explained that the outer peel becomes repulsive through handling by the hands, and it is prohibited to ruin terumah. They say there: if he designated it as terumah except for its outer peel, then according to one opinion it is permitted. That means that the thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers is the outer peel. For we see that there is an inner peel in the seed chambers themselves: there is a chamber between each portion of the esrog's seeds and its fellow, and those chambers are called the inner peel, with the seed resting inside it. But the entire thickness of the esrog until the chambers from the outside is called the outer peel. A parallel is the gizzard, where they speak of its outer skin, meaning the entire thickness of the flesh of the gizzard. The same is literally true regarding an esrog. One should not err and say that outer peel refers to the thin glaze above, as I will write in the name of the Ran nearby regarding peeling, for that is very thin and is not part of the esrog; even if most of it was peeled from the esrog, it is valid, as is stated afterward. How could they say regarding this in the Gemara that he designated the esrog as terumah but not that thin peel? Certainly without this, that thin peel is not included in terumah in the sense that it is included in eating and that it is prohibited to ruin terumah, for what loss is there in it, since it is not at all part of the flesh of the esrog? And the Rambam writes in chapter 11 of Terumot that esrog peels, even though they have no food and householders discard them, are nevertheless prohibited to non-kohanim. He is certainly speaking of the thin peels that we mentioned; but the thickness of the esrog, which is included in the flesh of the esrog, certainly is not discarded. Rather, they eat it, for it has sharpness, as I will write nearby in the name of the Ran. Furthermore, if you say that the outer peel in the Gemara regarding esrog is the thin one, one will still come to ruin terumah, for since it becomes repulsive through handling, one who wants to eat it afterward will cut somewhat into its thickness and discard from there the handled place, and it will not suffice for him to cut off only a hairbreadth. If so, he will come to ruin terumah. Rather, certainly it is speaking of the thickness of the esrog, which is certainly from the flesh of the esrog, and he does not designate it as terumah. This is what the Ran is speaking about here: if that thickness was punctured until the seed chambers. It follows that three peels belong to the esrog. <b>The first</b> is the very thin one, which is like a glaze above; there is no invalidation in it except if all of it was peeled, as stated afterward. <b>The second</b> is called the outer peel, meaning the entire thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers, and it has a sharp taste when one eats it. Regarding this we say that if a little is missing from it, it is invalid according to the Rambam even without being through-and-through, and according to the Ra'avad through being through-and-through, as above. The law of splitting nearby also concerns this peel. <b>The third</b> is the inner peel, which is between seed and seed within the esrog itself. But in the outer peel, meaning the thickness of the esrog that we mentioned, there is no further distinction; all of it is one, for what boundary is there to divide it? I wrote all this because the Beit Yosef wrote regarding the Ran: perhaps the entire thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers is called peel, etc. End quote. He wrote with somewhat uncertain language in this. My father-in-law and teacher, of blessed memory, was drawn from this to say that in truth there is a distinction in the thickness of the esrog between peel and the rest of the thickness until the seed chambers. He wrote that one cannot explain that the entire thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers is called peel, as the Beit Yosef wrote, for the language does not sound that way at all; rather, the outer peel is that which has sharpness, and if all of it was punctured from one side, it is through-and-through. End quote. But he was not precise in this, for the entire thickness of the esrog until the seed chambers, aside from the thin peel, has sharpness, and it is not called through-and-through except until the seed chambers. There is no need to elaborate further, for it is clear.

<b>אתרוג שנימוח כו'. </b> זהו בעי' דרבא בגמ' נולדו בו סימני טרפה מהו ולא נפשט הבעי'   ופסק בה לקולא וכתב הרא"ש טעמא דהנך פסולים אין עיקרם אלא מד"ס ולכן לא הביא הרי"ף הבעיא. וכתב הטור יש מחמירין והכי מסתברא  כיון דספק דאורייתא הוא עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>An esrog whose flesh dissolved, etc.</b> This is Rava's question in the Gemara: if signs of tereifah developed in it, what is the law? The question was not resolved, and he ruled leniently. The Rosh wrote the reason: these invalidations are essentially only rabbinic, and therefore the Rif did not bring the question. This is where it is not at its nose, for at its nose any amount is invalid, as the Ran wrote. This appears clear to me, with Heaven's help.

End quote. The Beit Yosef wrote: since the Rosh distinguishes regarding the stem between whether some of it remained in the esrog and whether none remained, but does not distinguish regarding the pitma, it implies that whenever a little of the pitma was removed, even though the depression remained full, it is invalid. I do not recognize this implication, for the Rosh wrote: and likewise, if the stem was removed and none of it remained at all in the esrog, it is invalid, but if any amount of it remained, it is valid. End quote. This implies that the removal of both is equal. The reason he did not mention by the pitma that none of it remained at all in the esrog is because it is not a fixed matter that there is a depression there; many times there is no depression at the head of the esrog, but it is sloped and sharp, and at its head the dad, which is the pitma, is attached. Plain 'removed' means all of it. Furthermore, by the stem there is certainly removal in every esrog, for they cut it there from the tree, and therefore he needed to mention until what point the removal invalidates. So the Beit Yosef wrote explicitly in the name of Orchot Chayim: but if the shoshana alone was removed, or the stem alone, or both, and the two pestles remain, it is valid; so it is proper to rule and to act, and no more. End quote. The Beit Yosef also wrote that this is implied by the words of the Ran. So too the words of the Rambam, which are the language of the Shulchan Aruch here, 'its dad was removed,' imply that plain 'removed' means all of it. So too the language of the Maggid Mishneh, who wrote: from the language of our master it implies that it is not invalid through removal of the shoshana alone, but through removal of the dad, which is the wood upon which the shoshana is; and this is primary, and so it is proper to rule, etc. End quote. This implies that he means removal of the whole dad, for if it were invalid when only part was removed, then it would be invalid when the shoshana was removed, since the shoshana is part of the body of the dad; if it is removed, some of the dad has been removed. Why should removal of a little of the dad after the shoshana be more severe than that part upon which the shoshana sits? Rather, certainly he is speaking of removal of the entire dad as invalidating, not removal of part of it. So too is the implication of the Rema's words here, that he invalidates when the dad is removed, meaning all of it, as it appears to my humble opinion.

<b>And some are stringent that if the shoshanta was removed, etc.</b> There is no explicit opinion about this, except that the Rif wrote: the explanation of its pitom is the dad of the esrog, and it is its nose, as we learned regarding the pitma of a pomegranate, and it is the shoshanta. End quote. The Ran wrote that according to the Rif the pitma is the shoshana itself, but this does not appear correct to me, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote that even according to the Rif, the invalidating matter is the dad itself and not the shoshana, for he says 'dad,' and it is its nose, and the shoshana is not called dad, but nose. Therefore, that which the Rif said, 'and it is its shoshanta,' means that the dad itself is called its shoshanta because it has a shoshana, and it is called by its name. So too one can explain the language of the Tur, who wrote: its pitma was removed, and this is the flower at its head, etc.; meaning the dad itself is called by the name of the flower that is at the head of the dad. This also appears from his language in the remazim, where he wrote: if its pitma was removed it is invalid, and it is the flower at its head that protrudes from the head of the esrog, etc. If he meant the flower itself, which is the shoshana, he should not have said 'that protrudes from the head of the esrog,' but should have said 'that protrudes from the head of the dad.' Rather, certainly he is speaking of the dad itself and calls it flower. It appears to my humble opinion that if we have before us an esrog that is beautiful in its other qualities, except that it lacks the shoshana, or a little of the upper dad was removed, and another esrog that is not beautiful in its other qualities but has a shoshana, one should not take that shoshana into account. All the more so where there is doubt whether it ever had a shoshana at all. Since the Ran, the Maggid Mishneh, and the Beit Yosef in the name of Orchot Chayim wrote that this is the proper practical ruling, they certainly are fit to rely upon for practical halachah.
