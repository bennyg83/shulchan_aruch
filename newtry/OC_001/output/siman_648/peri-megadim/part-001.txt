<b>ע״י</b> שיעביר בו מחט עט״ז עב״ח דהראב׳ד כתב בדיקה זו ולדידיה שפיר כבס״ב וי״א משא״כ הרא״ש וטור דסוברים כדיעה א׳ בס״ב קשה וביותר המחבר דמגיה מחטט וב״ו חוט עב״י וא״ר א׳ ב׳ ומ״ש בעובי אין פוסל מפולש כמו בלב עי״ד סי׳ מ״ם בש״ך וב״ח ובפר״ח ופריי שם כשר אבל כאן במ״א אות ב׳ וט״ז אות ו׳ קשה דמפולש שלא נגד החדרים פסול ועיין עט״ז כאן וא״ר ב׳ ואות ו׳ וצ״ל שיש בפנים מחיצות מפסיקות בין חדרי הזרע ושם אם ניקב מפולש מעל״ע ברוחב האתרוג ממש (דבר עגול ברוחב ממש) פסול ונגד חדרי הזרע ג״כ מפולש מקרא אבל בצד האתרוג במקום שמתקצר שאין ברוחב ממש אז אין מזיק לדידי׳ מעל״ע ואי״ה במ״א ב׳ יבואר עוד. ובעשוי דפא דפא וניקב א׳ מהבליטות י״ל לכ״ע כשר דמצד לצד מפולש היינו מעבר לעבר לא חד בליטה כאמור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>By passing a needle through it.</b> See Taz and Bach. Raavad prescribed this test, and according to his view it is understandable, as stated in seif 2 under “others say.” According to Rosh and Tur, however, who follow the first opinion in seif 2, it is difficult, and all the more so according to Mechaber, who emends the text to read “he probes it,” and according to the reading “a thread.” See Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah, seifim katanim 1 and 2. What is written concerning its thickness is that a through-perforation does not invalidate it, as with the heart; see Yoreh Deah, siman 40, Shach, Bach, Pri Chadash, and my commentary there, where it is ruled kosher. Here, however, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, and Taz, seif katan 6, imply that a through-perforation even where it is not opposite the seed chambers invalidates it. See Taz here, Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2, and below, seif katan 6. One must therefore say that partitions inside the esrog separate the seed chambers. If a perforation passes completely through the true width of the esrog, meaning through the width of a round object, it is invalid. A perforation opposite the seed chambers is likewise termed through-going. At the side of the esrog, however, where it narrows and does not constitute its true width, a perforation from side to side does not harm it according to this view. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. If the esrog is formed in separate ridges and only one ridge was perforated, it may be said that all authorities agree that it is kosher, because “from side to side” means from one side of the esrog to the other, not merely through a single ridge, as explained.

<b>משנה</b> עט״ז הב״ח פוסל ולא מהני בדיקה ופי׳ אתרוג הישן משנה שעברה אבל משו״ת מהרי״ל דהביא שיעור יבשות כהראב״ד ליתא ואי הוי מפרש כהב״ח ז״ל א״צ לזה אלא היה להביא גמרא דסוכה ל״א ישן פסול כו׳ אלא ש״מ דפי׳ להגמרא ישן שנתכווץ ושו״ת מהרי״ל הוי בלא נתכווץ ואמר דמשנה לשנה עכ״פ הגיע לספק יובש דשיעורו ליחה וצריך בדיקה ומהני כיון שרואין שיש ליחה ל״ש לחז״ל כו׳ קים להו. ובא״ר ג׳ כב״ח וכ״מ לשון הר״ב ז״ל דלא מהני בדיקה ועיין אות י״ו כבוש בדבש פסול ועמ״א כ״ג ובא״ר ג׳ דהב״ח ס״ל באולי דשאני איסורא מאתרוג ולפ״ז בדקדוק נקט המחבר דוקא חומץ לא כבוש במשקה מעל״ע דאין פוסל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From one year.</b> See Taz. Bach invalidates it and maintains that the test does not help. He explains “an old esrog” as one remaining from the previous year. This cannot be proved from Maharil's responsum, which gives the measure of dryness in accordance with Raavad. Had Maharil understood it as Bach does, he would not have needed that measure; he could simply have cited the Gemara in Sukkah 31a, which states that an old esrog is invalid. It must therefore be that he understood the Gemara's “old” esrog as one that had shriveled, whereas Maharil's responsum concerns one that had not shriveled. Maharil states that after a full year it has at least entered a doubt of dryness, whose measure depends on whether moisture remains, and it therefore requires examination. The examination is effective, because when we see that moisture remains, the principle that the Sages knew otherwise does not apply. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3, follows Bach, and Rama's wording also implies that examination does not help. See below, seif katan 16, that an esrog pickled in honey is invalid, Magen Avraham, seif katan 23, and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3. Bach may maintain that the laws of forbidden mixtures differ from those of an esrog. According to this, Mechaber deliberately mentioned specifically vinegar, because soaking in another liquid for a full twenty-four hours does not invalidate it.

<b>אם</b> עט״ז עב״י ותליא בפי׳ הסוגיא ל״ו א׳ אי עולא ב״ח ארישא או אסיפא קאי ובפי׳ המשנה. ולפ״ז בדיעה א׳ מפולש בלא חסרון כלל פסול ואין מפולש כל שיש נקב כאיסר ובלא חסרון מסמר רחב פסול אבל חסר כ״ש מבשר וקליפת אתרוג העבה כדלקמן בט״ז אות י׳ פסול בי״ט א׳ כדלקמן תרמ״ט ה״ה בהג״ה ושם יבואר אי״ה. ודיעה שניה הוא לקולא דכל שאין חסרון כלל אין פוסל אף מפולש וכל שיש חסרון מפולש חסר כ״ש פסול ואין מפולש כאיסר חסרון הא באין חסרון אף כאיסר אין פוסל וכ״מ בהשגות הר״א ז״ל פ״ח ה״ו דלעולם בעי חסרון דוקא והר״ב שכתב בשעת הדחק כסברא ב׳ להכשיר שאין מפולש חסר כ״ש משמע הא מפולש באין חסרון או אין מפולש ואין חסרון ויש כאיסר אף י בשעת הדחק פסול ובתלת פליגי ב׳ דיעות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If.</b> See Taz and Beit Yosef. The matter depends upon the interpretation of the sugya on 36a, whether the statement of Ulla bar Chanina refers to the first clause or the latter clause, and upon the interpretation of the Mishnah. According to the first opinion, a through-perforation invalidates even where no substance is missing. A non-through perforation invalidates when it is the size of an issar, even where no substance is missing, such as one made by a broad nail. If any amount of the flesh or thick peel of the esrog is missing, as explained below in Taz, seif katan 10, it is invalid on the first day of Yom Tov, as stated below in siman 649, seif 5 in the gloss; with Hashem's help it will be explained there. The second opinion is lenient: where no substance is missing, even a through-perforation does not invalidate. Where substance is missing, any amount invalidates if the perforation passes through; if it does not pass through, the missing area must be the size of an issar. If nothing is missing, even a hole the size of an issar does not invalidate. This is also implied by Raavad's glosses, chapter 8, halachah 6, that actual missing substance is always required. Rama writes that in pressing circumstances one may rely upon the second opinion to validate a non-through perforation missing only a minute amount. This implies that a through-perforation with no missing substance, or a non-through perforation the size of an issar with no missing substance, remains invalid even in pressing circumstances. Thus the two opinions disagree concerning three cases.

<b>אע״פ</b> עט״ז עד״מ ול״ד לבלא״ט פלע״ק סעיף י״ב ובמ״א אות ך׳ דהתם מתחלת ברייתו הדר הוא משא״כ ניקב וחסר הוא כעין טרפות דסופו לירקב הכל עתה״ד שם א״כ אין לחלק אף מתחלת ברייתו כל שניקב ואין עור קיים פסול ועב״י משמע ניקב נסדק נקלף מדרבנן אזלינן בספק לקולא ועד״מ ואי״ה באות ה׳ יבואר בזה:

<b>שאין</b> עט״ז לי ההדיוט יש בכאן כוונה מיוחדת כמו שאגיד. הנה בר״מ כתב דספק כשר מטעם דרגילות הוא במחובר להיות עור ובשר קיים ואין חוששין אלא שרואין שאין קיים הא היכא דליכא לברורי לעיין בפנים היטב שרי דרגיל הוא כו׳ או י״ל מטעם ספק דרבנן לקולא עיין אות ד׳ אבל בהג״ה חזר ביה ומטעם ס״ס מכשיר ליה כאמור (ובפריי לי״ד כל שבה״מ כשר מצטרפין לס״ס באין ה״מ) ובחסרון כאיסר ע״י קוצים בספק י״ל ביה והבן ומ״ש דנקב מפולש פסול כמו בסמוך יראה דנתכוין לאות ד׳ דלמדה מריאה חסרון בתולדה ל״ש חסרון הא נקב מפולש אף בתולדה פוסל ומטריף שם ה״ה כאן מפולש ע״י קוצים ועורו קיים פסול ועי״ד סימן מ״ג אות ב׳ שם מכשיר הט״ז נקב בתולדה ועור קיים צ״ע ה״ה כאן לכשר כה״ג. ובספק אם הגיע הנקב לחדרי הזרע כשר מטעם ס״ס שמא לא הגיע ואת״ל הגיע שמא הלכה כדיעה ב׳ בכל תלת מילי ומפולש באין חסרון כשר להראב״ד ומחובר בעור ובשר קיים לאו חסרון הוי וה״ה בתלוש באין חסרון כלל כה״ג וה״ה מפולש ממש מעל״ע במחובר בשעת הדחק י״ל אראב״ד ולעיל דפוסל באין שעת הדחק. ומשמע ביש חסרון לא הוי ס״ס שמא לא הגיע לזרע ושמא הלכה מעל״ע בעינן וצ״ע ואפשר מדהביא המחבר י״א באחרונה בס״ג ועב״י ובא״ר ו׳ וצ״ע כעת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Where there is no.</b> See Taz. In my humble opinion there is a particular point intended here, as I shall explain. Rema of Fano writes that in a doubtful case it is kosher because ordinarily, while the esrog is still attached to the tree, its skin and flesh remain intact. We are concerned only when we actually see that they are not intact. Thus, where it is impossible to clarify the matter by examining the interior carefully, it is permitted because the usual condition is that they remain intact. Alternatively, the reason may be that a doubt concerning a D'Rabbanan law is treated leniently; see the preceding seif katan. In his gloss, however, he retracts this and validates it because of a double doubt, as explained. In my commentary to Yoreh Deah I wrote that any opinion relied upon in cases of substantial loss may combine to form a double doubt even where there is no substantial loss. The same may be said where there is doubt whether thorns caused a missing area the size of an issar. His statement that a through-perforation is invalid, as stated immediately afterward, appears to refer to seif katan 4, where the law was derived from a lung: a naturally occurring absence is not considered missing substance, but a naturally occurring through-hole does invalidate and render a lung tereifah. The same would therefore apply here, so that a through-hole caused by thorns is invalid even though its skin remains intact. Yet in Yoreh Deah, siman 43, seif katan 2, Taz validates a naturally occurring hole where the skin remains intact. This requires study, for the same should apply here. If there is doubt whether the hole reached the seed chambers, the esrog is kosher because of a double doubt: perhaps it did not reach them, and even if it did, perhaps the halachah follows the second opinion in all three disputed cases. According to Raavad, a through-hole without missing substance is kosher, and while the esrog is attached, intact skin and flesh are not considered a deficiency. The same applies after it is detached where no substance is missing. In pressing circumstances one may likewise rely upon Raavad even for a hole that truly passes completely through an attached esrog, whereas above it was invalidated when there was no pressing circumstance. It appears that where substance is actually missing there is no double doubt of perhaps it did not reach the seed chambers and perhaps the law requires a hole passing completely through. This requires study. Perhaps this follows because Mechaber cites the “others say” opinion last in seif 3. See Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 6. The matter presently requires study.

<b>שניקב</b> עט״ז עמ״ש באות א׳ מצד לצד שלא נגד חדרים לאו מפולש אין סותר לכאן כי מיירי במקום שיש מחיצה עבה בין החדרים אז הוי מפולש. ועתוס׳ סוכה ל״ד א׳ ד״ה אי ניקב וברא״ש שם הביא דעת בה״ג הוא דיעה א׳ כאן. ולדינא צ״ע כי בב״י משמע שהרי״ף והרמב״ם השמיטו בעי׳ דירושלמי ומפולש מצד לצד דוקא וא״כ משמע שכן הלכה כפי הכלל להמחבר. ומ״ש הט״ז על הב״ח שרצה לפרש שלש מחלוקת ולהר״ן קליפ׳ חיצונה לבד עד בשר האתרוג נמי מפולש מקרי והט״ז פי׳ דהר״ן כתב עד חדרי הזרע. ומ״ש מתרומה כו׳ קורקבן עמ״ש בפריי לי״ד סי׳ מ״ט דעור חיצון דק מאד אפ״ה עם כל הבשר הוי בכלל עור א׳ ועור ב׳ הוא הלובן בפנים הקשה הדבוק לבשר נקב מפולש טריפה אע״פ שכיס שהמאכל בתוכו הנקלף מעצמו ובתרנגולת מונח בפ״ע לא הוי בכלל יע״ש. ושם התבאר זה. ועיין אות יו״ד בט״ז מזה. ומ״ש בפי״א דתרומה ה״י שמשליכין נקובת ירק וסובר הט״ז ז״ל דקאי נמי אקליפת אתרוג יע״ש. ועמ״ש אי״ה באות ז׳ וי׳ יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That it was perforated.</b> See Taz and what I wrote in seif katan 1. The statement there that a hole passing from side to side outside the seed chambers is not considered through-going does not contradict the present discussion, because there the case is where a thick partition between the chambers was perforated, and that is considered through-going. See Tosafos, Sukkah 34a, s.v. “If it was perforated,” and Rosh there, who cites the view of Behag, corresponding to the first opinion here. The practical halachah requires study. Beit Yosef implies that Rif and Rambam omitted the Yerushalmi's question and require specifically a perforation from one side to the other. According to Mechaber's usual rule, this would imply that the halachah follows them. Taz comments on Bach, who sought to identify three opinions. According to Ran, penetrating only the outer peel until the flesh of the esrog is also called a through-perforation, whereas Taz explains that Ran means a hole reaching the seed chambers. Regarding the comparison to terumah and to the gizzard, see what I wrote in my commentary to Yoreh Deah, siman 49: although the outer membrane is extremely thin, it combines with all the flesh as one membrane, while the second membrane is the hard white layer inside that adheres to the flesh. A through-hole renders the gizzard tereifah even though the pouch containing the food, which peels away on its own and lies separately in a chicken, is not included. This was explained there. See Taz, seif katan 10, on this point. Rambam writes in chapter 11 of Hilchos Terumos, halachah 10, that perforated vegetables are discarded, and Taz understands this as applying also to the peel of an esrog. See there. With Hashem's help see also what I write below in seifim katanim 7 and 10.

<b>שנימוח</b> עט״ז הרי״ף והר״מ ז״ל השמיטו בעיא דרבא ל״ו א׳ ופסקו לקולא דהוי ספק דרבנן וי״א טור דסובר דהוי ספק תורה ועב״ח דאף דהרא״ש כתב דהנך פסולים עיקרן מד״ס מ״מ במידי דאורייחא הוא יע״ש (היינו נטילת אתרוג ד״ת וצ״ע) וסיים הב״ח די״ל כל הפוסקים מודים דפסול יע״ש. והנה המחבר כאן הכריע כדיעה א׳ בסתם דכשר ובס״ג לא הכריע בניקב עד חדרי הזרע כו׳ והלבוש שינה וכתב גם כאן י״מ וי״א יע״ש. וי״ל כמ״ש בס׳ כפות תמרים דת״ש סרוח מבפנים ולמיפשט הא לא סרוח כשר ודחי לה ומש״ה הכריע לקולא דאדחו ולא סמכינן אבל הר״ב דשתיק קשה קצת דפסק בי״ד ל״ו ס״ז ול״ז ס״א רבים מכשירים שם בסרוחים א״כ הפשיטות כאן לחומרא והוי יודע דבנימוח בפנים ונסרח י״ל דפסול להמחבר וגם הר״ב אפשר יודה לזה וצ״ע)(:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That its interior dissolved.</b> See Taz. Rif and Rambam omit Rava's unresolved question on 36a and rule leniently because it is a D'Rabbanan doubt. The “others say” is Tur's view, that this is a doubt concerning a Torah law. See Bach, who writes that although Rosh states that these invalidities are fundamentally from the words of the Sages, the matter nevertheless concerns a Torah mitzvah. See there. He evidently means the Torah obligation of taking an esrog, though this requires study. Bach concludes that it may be that all the poskim agree that it is invalid. Mechaber here rules like the first opinion without qualification, that it is kosher, whereas in seif 3 he did not decide the dispute concerning a hole reaching the seed chambers. Levush therefore altered the formulation and writes here too, “some permit and some forbid.” One may explain, as Kappos Temarim writes, that the Gemara sought to resolve the question from an esrog whose interior had decayed, implying that if it had not decayed it would be kosher, but rejected the proof. Mechaber therefore ruled leniently because the proposed proof was rejected and cannot be relied upon. Rama's silence is somewhat difficult, since in Yoreh Deah 36:7 and 37:1 he rules that many authorities validate a decayed organ. If so, the attempted proof here would point toward stringency. Know that where the interior dissolved and decayed, Mechaber may agree that it is invalid, and perhaps Rama would agree as well. This requires study.

<b>And some are stringent.</b> See Taz and Bach. Even according to Beit Yosef's reading, the stringent opinion invalidates an esrog split completely along one side. According to the first opinion, however, a majority split on both sides is required, which is more extensive than a complete split along one side. This is unlike Rama's ruling; see there. One may say that an esrog is more stringent than a windpipe, because a windpipe stretches when the animal extends its neck and then heals, unlike an esrog; see Yoreh Deah, siman 34. According to the first opinion and Beit Yosef's reading, a windpipe split along most of both sides is also kosher. The esrog must therefore be invalid because a split extending fully from the bottom is like a windpipe with no complete ring remaining below, for there one requires a complete ring both above and below. The comparison to a tereifah windpipe is thus exact. The Mishnah's unqualified statement that an esrog split is invalid can then refer even to a majority split, meaning on both sides. Necessarily, however, the stringent opinion invalidates a majority split on one side even if a small part remains below and the chotem is intact. In this respect it is unlike a windpipe, which stretches and heals as explained.

Taz's reading is: “It split; some explain along its length, along its width, on both sides,” meaning that the opinions differ in the same division found in Behag and Rosh cited in seif katan 6. The Mishnah teaches “it was perforated, it split,” so the split is comparable to the perforation. According to the opinion that a perforation must pass completely through, the split must likewise extend through the full length from one side to the other, beginning below near the uketz and continuing lengthwise until the opposite side. It is not invalid because of a perforation, since this opinion follows Raavad in seif 2 that a through-hole requires missing substance, whereas a split invalidates even without missing substance. A split on the chotem certainly invalidates with even the slightest measure, though perhaps a minute amount must remain outside the chotem and also below it, just as a windpipe requires a margin toward the lung. According to this, “along its width” means through from side to side, which he calls its width. Although a windpipe fully split along one side is tereifah, an esrog is more lenient in this respect.

The stringent opinion corresponds to Rosh's view in seif 3 that a perforation reaching the seed chambers is invalid. Similarly, a lengthwise split along one side that reaches the seed chambers invalidates. It is not invalidated because of perforation, since this opinion follows Raavad in seif 2 that a hole requires missing substance, whereas a split does not. Were one to say that a hole reaching the chambers invalidates even without missing substance, what difference would a split along one side make, since it would already be invalid as a perforation? One must therefore say that a split through most of the peel's thickness along one full side invalidates even though it did not reach the seed chambers.

According to this, Ran agrees with Tosafos that the entire length must be split, not merely most of it. Ran only cites a dispute whether the split must pass from side to side beginning at the uketz, which is called its width, or whether a complete split along one side invalidates. Beit Yosef, however, reads Ran as saying: “Some explain that it split lengthwise along most of both sides, while some explain along one side.” This can be explained following Taz: the view that defines a perforation as passing completely through similarly requires a split on both sides, while the other view requires only one side. Beit Yosef therefore writes “but Ran,” because Ran disagrees with Tosafos, who require a complete split with nothing remaining above or below. According to Ran's first opinion, a majority split on both sides invalidates. The stringent opinion invalidates a majority split on one side, following the view that a perforation reaching the seed chambers is enough. It presumably refers to the same majority measure and does not follow Bach on this point. Mechaber rules without qualification like Tosafos that a complete split on one side is required, not merely a majority, even though in seif 3 he cites both views, because this resembles the law of a windpipe. It remains difficult why he did not mention that most of the peel's thickness must be split, in accordance with his rulings in seifim 2 and 3.

One may alternatively say that Beit Yosef's words “but Ran” refer to the chotem, because Tosafos imply that a split does not invalidate specifically at the chotem. See Magen Avraham, whose reading is: “It split; some explain along its length as along its width, on both sides.” The comparative word “as” means that just as a split around the circumference invalidates only if it forms a complete ring and leaves nothing connected, so too a lengthwise split requires both sides. A windpipe split across its width is invalid when most is split because it stretches, and similarly a lengthwise split in an esrog requires both sides. See below, seif katan 6. Our reading in Ran is: “Some explain along its length or along its width.” See Eliyah Rabbah. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.

This is somewhat difficult, because the Gemara concerning terumah and Rambam in chapter 11 may indeed refer to the somewhat thick peel.
