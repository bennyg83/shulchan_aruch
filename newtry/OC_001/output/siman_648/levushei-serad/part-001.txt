<small>שם</small> אלא מחמת יובש וכו'. יבש הוא וקרי ליה כמושות לפי שהוא רק במקצתו וס"ל לש"ג דיבש מקצת כשר ולא קי"ל כך כמ"ש המ"א סקי"ז מש"ה פוסלו הג"א ועכ"פ מביא ראיה מהש"ג דיובש במקצת קרוי כמוש ובההוא דוקא פוסלו הג"א ומסיק די"ל דאע"ג דכמוש כשר הוא דוקא במקצתו וכמו בריאה דיובש פוסל בכל שהוא וצמקה דהיינו כמוש דהכא פוסל דוקא בכולה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> But due to dryness, etc. It is dry, and he calls it withered because it is only in part. The Shiltei Gibborim holds that partial dryness is valid, but we do not rule that way, as the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 17. Therefore Hagahos Ashri invalidates it. In any event, he brings proof from the Shiltei Gibborim that partial dryness is called withering, and only in that case does Hagahos Ashri invalidate it. He concludes that one can say that although withering is valid, that is specifically in part; and just as with a lung, dryness invalidates even in any amount, while shriveling, which is the withering here, invalidates only when it is entirely affected.
**** END BLOCK ****

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> There is a doubt, etc. This is a separate matter, and it deals with a hole that has no deficiency, and it is understandable. But where there is a deficiency, since even according to the Raavad it is not clearly valid, for if it reaches the seed chambers it is invalid if in such a case it is called perforated through, therefore we do not validate it. Even though according to the Raavad there is a double doubt, perhaps it does not reach the seed chambers, and perhaps reaching the seed chambers is not called perforated through, and if so perhaps according to the Raavad it is valid because of this double doubt, nevertheless, since according to those who disagree with the Raavad it is invalid, and even according to the Raavad himself it is only a double doubt, perhaps in such a case even in pressing circumstances one should not be lenient.
**** END BLOCK ****

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>S.k. 6.</small> Opposite those chambers, etc. What the Taz validated in s.k. 1 is a different matter: he inserts the needle even opposite the seed chambers, but only through a bit of the peel across its width on one side of the esrog, making a slight hole, and the hole has two openings. This is neither perforated through nor from side to side. Here, however, it discusses a case where above the seed chambers it was pierced from side to side. In one responsum I wrote at length about various matters, but here is not the place to elaborate.
