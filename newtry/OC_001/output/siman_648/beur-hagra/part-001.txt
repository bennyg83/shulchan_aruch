<b>Section 11. That applies to matters done by Heaven, but in matters done by human hands exact measurement is possible, from that which is stated regarding a breach equal to the standing wall, and in the third chapter of Chullin: half and half, etc. There they say regarding tereifot that half and half is not like a majority to render it tereifah; rather, we require a majority visible to the eyes. Here we compare it to tereifot. And even though in Bekhorot 18a we conclude that according to the Rabbis even in matters done by human hands exact measurement is impossible, nevertheless regarding matters done by human hands we rule like Rabbi Yose HaGelili. See Tosafot there, who elaborated on this, and in the Rosh there, who concludes as I wrote; see there, chapter 2, se'if 4. <b>And some invalidate.</b> For they hold that the Rabbis maintain that even in matters done by human hands exact measurement is impossible, as stated in the first chapter of Eruvin: what will you say, that the two are equal? It is a doubt, etc. This implies that it is impossible to clarify. They hold that we rule entirely like the Rabbis, even in matters done by human hands. And that case in Chullin, they conclude there that according to all opinions half and half is not like a majority, because we require a majority visible to the eyes; and this is because exact measurement is impossible. This is what the Ra'avad wrote, that it is impossible to be exact, because we require a majority visible to the eyes. The case of a breach equal to the standing wall is because this is how the Merciful One taught it, etc.: that it should not appear as a majority visible to the eyes that is breached. With this the question of Tosafot in Chullin there, s.v. de-kulei alma, is resolved. Likewise by tereifot, half is valid because this is how they received the tradition, that we require a majority visible to the eyes. So too was the initial assumption regarding slaughter, that this is what the Merciful One said: do not leave over a majority visible to the eyes, as stated there.

<b>ויש מי כו'. </b>כמ"ש הרא"ש דפסול החזזית משום דאינה ממראה האתרוג ולכך נקלף כשר אפילו ברובו הואיל והוא מראה האתרוג אף על פי שאין מראיהן שווין ועסי"ז והני כיון שרגילין הם ממראה האתרוג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is one who says, etc.</b> As the Rosh wrote, that boil-like discoloration is invalid because it is not of the appearance of an esrog. Therefore, one that was peeled is valid even on most of it, since it is the appearance of an esrog, even though their appearances are not identical. See se'if 17. And these, since they are common, are considered of the appearance of an esrog.

<b>סי"ד אם עלתה כו'. </b>דטעמא דפסולים אלו משום הדר ואינן פסולים בגופו וכמו ענביו מרובים מעליו שפסולו משום הדר ואם מיעטן כשר וזהו הטעם מ"ש ביבמות ע"ו א' וזהו כו' כיון שאינו מחמת עצמו אלא שאין הזרע יכול לקלח מחמת הנקב למעוטי כו' ששם פסולו בגופו וערא"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 14. If it arose, etc.</b> For the reason of these invalidations is because of beauty, and they are not invalidations in its body, like berries more numerous than its leaves, whose invalidity is because of beauty; if he reduced them, it is valid. This is the reason for what is written in Yevamot 76a: and this is, etc., since it is not because of itself, but because the seed cannot flow because of the hole, to exclude, etc.; for there its invalidity is in its body. See the Rosh.

<b>סט"ז אם הוא כו'. </b>שם מנומר ופי' בגוונין הפסולין כגין כושי לבן אבל ממראה האתרוג או כשר כשר ממ"ש בהד' א"ל רבא שנים כו' אלא כו' וערש"י שם ד"ה מיני כו' אלמא ממראיו לא פסיל מנומר ואמר שם אדומות כשחורות דא"ר חנינא כו' ול"ל לטעמא דר' חנינא הא בלא"ה כיון שאינו ממראה ההדס וז"ש הרא"ש דחזזית אינו ממראה האתרוג דאל"כ לא הוי אמרי' אבל בשניה כו' ואם כן הא דתניא כושי לבן דאף במקום א' פסול ברובו וכמו חזזית דבב' ובג' מקומות משום מנומר ובמקום א' ברובו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 16. If it is, etc.</b> There it means spotted, and he explains that it is with invalid colors, such as black or white; but if it is of the appearance of an esrog, or valid, it is valid. This follows from what is written regarding hadas: Rava said to him, two, etc., rather, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. minei, etc.; this shows that spottedness from its own appearances does not invalidate. And it says there that red ones are like black ones, for Rabbi Chanina said, etc. Why do we need Rabbi Chanina's reason? In any event, it is not of the appearance of hadas. This is what the Rosh wrote, that boil-like discoloration is not of the appearance of an esrog, for otherwise we would not say: but in two, etc. If so, that which is taught regarding a black or white one is that even in one place it is invalid on most of it, like boil-like discoloration, which in two or three places is invalid because it is spotted, and in one place on most of it.
