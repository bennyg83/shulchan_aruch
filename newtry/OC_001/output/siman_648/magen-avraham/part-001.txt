אפילו כמוש שקורין וועל"ק פסול (תשובת מהרי"ל סי' ה' ד"מ) וכ"כ הרוקח וצ"ע דאי' בהדיא בסוכ' דף ל"א וכולן כמושים כשרים והביאו הרא"ש ורי"ו ובאמ' במרדכי משמע דכמוש פסול שכ' שכשר בשעת הדחק אבל בב"י סוף סי' תרמ"ט כ' הגי' כבוש ע"ש וכ"מ דאי כמוש היאך כתב המרדכי יבש בשעת הדחק ופי' ראבי"ה דה"ה לכמוש וכו' הא בכלל ר' ק' דכיון דיבש כשר כמוש בכלל אלא כמ"ש הב"י עיקר דהגי' הוא כבוש אבל כמוש כשר לעולם ובהג"א פ"ג כ' ע"ש ראבי"ה אתרוג כמוש ומנומר שהוא פסול אם חתך מה שהיה יבש כמוש ומנומר פסול עכ"ל ויש לדקדק דבתחלה כ' כמוש וסיים יבש כמוש אלא ע"כ מיירי שיבש והא דקרי ליה כמוש היינו משום שאינו יבש כולו אלא מקצתו וכ"כ בש"ג חזזית העולה בתלוש אינה אלא מחמת יובש וכמושות וכשר עכ"ל ואפשר לו' דכשנכמשה כולה פסול מקצתו כשר דהא בגמר' מדמי אתרוג לטריפות דריאה ובריאה נמי דינא הכי ומ"מ נ"ל להקל מאחר שכל הפוסקים כתבו דיבש פסול משמע דכמוש כשר ובלבד שיבדקנו: <b>מחט</b>. ויזהר שלא יעשה נקב מפולש דהיינו שיתחב בחזקה המחט עס' ב"ג (ב"ח):
**** ENGLISH ****
Even if it is withered, which is called welk, it is invalid, Responsa Maharil, siman 5, cited in Darkei Moshe, and so the Rokeach wrote. This requires study, for it is explicit in Sukkah, folio 31, that all of them, if withered, are valid, and the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham cite this. Indeed, in the Mordechai it implies that withered is invalid, for he wrote that it is valid in pressing circumstances. But in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 649 he wrote that the correct version is pickled; see there. This also follows logically, for if the text were withered, how could the Mordechai write dry in pressing circumstances, and the Raavyah explained that the same applies to withered, etc.? That is included in his words, for since dry is valid, withered is included. Rather, as the Beis Yosef wrote, the primary reading is pickled, but withered is always valid. In Hagahos Ashri, chapter 3, he wrote in the name of the Raavyah: an esrog that is withered and speckled, which is invalid, if one cut away what was dry, withered, and speckled, it is invalid, end quote. One must analyze this, for at first he wrote withered, and he concluded dry, withered. Rather, it must be that he is discussing where it dried, and the reason he called it withered is that it is not entirely dry but only partly so. The Shiltei Gibborim likewise wrote: a scab that arises after it was detached is only due to dryness and withering, and it is valid, end quote. It is possible to say that if all of it withered it is invalid, but if part of it withered it is valid, for in the Gemara they compare an esrog to the tereifah laws of a lung, and the law of a lung is also so. Nevertheless, it seems to me to be lenient, since all the poskim wrote that dry is invalid, which implies that withered is valid, provided that one examines it. <b>Needle.</b> He should be careful not to make a hole that is perforated through, namely by inserting the needle forcefully; see seif 2 and 3, Bach.

<b>כאיסר</b>. כגון שתחב בו יתד רחב ועבי"ד סימן ל"ד ס"ג בענין שיעור כאיסר: נ"ל דאם הנקב מרובע או ארוך רואין אי כד מעגלת לה הוי כאיסר פסול וכ"ה בי"ד סימן ל"ד לענין גרגרת דלא כמ"ש בכ"ה בשם רדב"ז שדקדק מלשון הרמב"ם להכשיר אם הוא מרובע עד שיכנס לתוכו איסר ע"ש ואין זה כדאי להקל מדקדוק קל דיש לומר דמה דנקט הרמב"ם או יתר לאשמועינן דאף שיתר כשר בשאר הימים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Like an issar.</b> For example, where he inserted into it a broad peg. See Yoreh Deah, siman 34, seif 3, regarding the measure of an issar. It seems to me that if the hole is square or long, we see whether, if you would round it, it would be like an issar; if so, it is invalid. So too in Yoreh Deah, siman 34, regarding the windpipe, unlike what the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of the Radbaz, who inferred from the Rambam's wording to validate it if it is square until an issar can enter into it; see there. This is not sufficient grounds to be lenient based on a slight inference, for one can say that the reason the Rambam wrote or more is to teach that even if it is larger, it is valid on the other days.

<b>בנסדק רובו</b>. משמע אפי' נסדק מצד א' ולא דק דדוקא למ"ד בב' צדדין הוא דפסול ברובו אבל למ"ד מצד א' בעי' כולו [ב"ח] ולי צ"ע דהא מדמי לה לגרגרת ובגרגרת בעינן שנסדק כולו וגירסתנו בר"ן הוא לארכו ברחבו משני צדדין ונ"ל דה"פ בין שנסדק לארכו ובין שנסדק ברחבו בעינן משני צדדין ואף על גב דבגרגרת אם נפסק לרחבו טריפ' ברובו היינו משום דכשהבהמה פושטת צווארה מתקרע משא"כ כאן ואם כן ליכא למ"ד דטריפה ברובו ע"ש ולכן יש להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Where most of it cracked.</b> This implies even if it cracked on one side, but this is not precise, for only according to the opinion that it must be on two sides is it invalid if most of it cracked, but according to the opinion that one side suffices, we require all of it, Bach. To me this requires study, for he compares it to the windpipe, and regarding the windpipe we require that all of it cracked. Our version in the Ran is: along its length, across its width, on two sides. It seems to me that this is the explanation: whether it cracked along its length or whether it cracked across its width, we require two sides. Although with a windpipe, if it was split across its width the animal is a tereifah when most of it split, that is because when the animal stretches its neck it tears further, which is not so here. If so, there is no opinion that it is a tereifah by most of it; see there. Therefore one may be lenient.

<b>וכל שינוי מרא'</b>. משמע דוקא שינוי מרא' אבל לא שאר פסולים וכ"כ רי"ו נ"ח ח"ג והרא"ש דלא כהר"ן שפוסל גם בנסדק כל שהוא בחוטמו (ד"מ) ועס"ה ולדידיה אם נסדק בחוטמו פסול כל ז' דאין זה הדר כנ"ל וה"ה לכל פסולי האתרוג כ"כ הר"ן וצ"ע על הרב"י שכתב וי"א דה"ה ליבש כיון שכתב בס"ה דנסדק פוסל שם אם כן כל פסולי האתרוג בכלל וכ"ש יבש שפסול משום הדר דבזה אפי' הרא"ש מודה שפסול: ונ"ל דאתא לאפוקי מש"ג דס"ל דאם לא יבש כולו מקרי כמוש וכשר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And any change of appearance.</b> This implies specifically a change of appearance, but not other disqualifications. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 8, part 3, and the Rosh, unlike the Ran, who invalidates even if any amount of the nose cracked, Darkei Moshe. See seif 5. According to him, if its nose cracked it is invalid all seven days, for this is not hadar, as it seems to me; and the same applies to all disqualifications of an esrog, as the Ran wrote. It requires study regarding the Rav Beis Yosef, who wrote that some say the same applies to dryness. Since he wrote in seif 5 that a crack invalidates there, if so all disqualifications of an esrog are included, and all the more so dryness, which is invalid because of hadar, for in this even the Rosh agrees that it is invalid. It seems to me that he came to exclude the Shiltei Gibborim, who holds that if it did not dry entirely it is called withered and is valid.

<b>The green one.</b> Maharil would invalidate a green one, lest it remain that color, unless it had begun to turn yellow like saffron, end quote. This implies that he holds the reason is that its fruit has not finished developing, and therefore once it turns somewhat yellow like saffron it is valid. But the Bach wrote that it is invalid also because of hadar, and therefore we require that all of it return to the appearance of an esrog, unlike the common practice, which validates it when only part of it has changed, end quote. It is possible that he did not examine the Maharil. Also from Tosafot and the Rosh it implies that the reason is that its fruit has not finished developing, but nevertheless it is hadar, as the Gemara concludes. One can also say that Maharil holds that Tosafot and the Rosh maintain that even a leek-green esrog is valid if we know that its way is to turn yellow, and so it appears from the wording of Tosafot. But we do not rely on this unless it has begun, and so the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies, for he wrote that one leaves it. Therefore one should not overturn the practice. An esrog grafted from a lemon and an esrog is invalid, and it has three signs. First, the grafted one is smooth, while an esrog has small protrusions over its whole body, and they are raised. Second, the grafted one has a protruding stem, while the stem of an esrog is sunken. Third, the inside of the grafted one, namely the pulp, is broad, its juice is abundant, and its middle peel is narrow; in an esrog it is the reverse, for the peel is broad and the inside is narrow and almost dry. These signs are definitive for the grafted esrogim that grow among us. But from the district of Puglia there come esrogim about which we are uncertain, though they cannot bring them to you, for the distance from you is great, etc. A grafted esrog is invalid even in pressing circumstances, end quote of Rabbi Yehudah Padua in the Responsa of the Rama, siman 126. In Olas Shabbos he wrote another sign, that in an esrog the seed stands upright along the length of the esrog, while in a grafted one the seed lies across the width of the esrog. The Levush wrote that the one called Greski is invalid because a transgression was committed with it, like an animal of kilayim, which is invalid for offering, end quote. This is difficult, for if so, the horn of a goat produced through kilayim should be invalid for shofar. Also, why do we need a verse that kilayim is invalid for an offering? Let it be derived from the fact that a transgression was committed with it. See Bava Kamma, folio 77b, and Chullin, folio 115, and what I wrote in siman 586. It seems to me that it is invalid because it is not called an esrog at all. See Tosafot at the beginning of folio 33.
