[3] <b>Only garments of their own material, etc.</b> Levush explains that the verse says “the corner,” with the definite article, implying that the threads must be of the material of the corner. Rashi on Shabbos 27a does not explain it this way. Rather, the word “the corner” is entirely superfluous, since the verse already says “upon the corners of their garments”; it should have said only, “They shall place upon the tzitzis a thread of techeiles.” See Tosafos, Menachos 39b, s.v. “Even.” Olas Tamid cites Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Radbaz that a tallis whose warp is wool and woof is silk, or the reverse, is exempt according to the Torah and obligated D'Rabbanan even if most is wool. He further cites Maharam of Rothenburg that such a tallis has no remedy, and leaves the matter requiring study.

I examined Radbaz, responsum 199. His reason is that Hagahos Maimoniyos writes in the name of Maharam that wool and linen exempt other materials only when used together, but wool alone does not. Therefore the garment has no remedy: wool tzitzis alone do not exempt it, and silk tzitzis do not exempt the woolen warp. Olas Tamid was not precise in leaving this unresolved, because he himself rules in this siman that we do not follow Hagahos Maimoniyos and that wool alone exempts every material. The garment therefore has a complete remedy through woolen tzitzis. Shulchan Aruch and Levush likewise rule without qualification that wool exempts all materials, clearly rejecting Maharam's view, as Beit Yosef explicitly states.

Moreover, Hagahos Maimoniyos's citation of Maharam is astonishing. Maharam of Rothenburg, responsum 444, explicitly writes that linen tzitzis do not help a silk tallis because they are not the material of the corner. The Torah said “a thread of techeiles,” meaning wool, but did not say ordinary tzitzis. The verse “You shall make twisted cords for yourself from them” refers to two techeiles threads together with two white threads. Where both cannot be placed together, as in our time, the tzitzis must be either wool, because the verse says “a thread of techeiles,” or the material of the garment, because “the corner” teaches that the threads must match the corner. Maharam thus clearly maintains that wool exempts every material. His statement that wool and linen exempt only together concerns linen: linen exempts only its own material, or another material when wool and linen threads are used together. In our time linen helps only a linen garment, but wool exempts every material.

This responsum also clarifies Tur's citation of Semak that one should avoid linen tzitzis on a silk garment. Semak evidently takes Maharam's concern into account that linen does not exempt other materials, whereas wool certainly does because of “a thread of techeiles.” Tur therefore also rules that linen exempts linen, based upon “the corner,” meaning the material of the corner. Beit Yosef and Bach offered many strained explanations of Tur's words, leaving room for this explanation.

The practical conclusion is that the garment described above should specifically have woolen tzitzis. Silk tzitzis do not exempt it, even if most of it is silk, because warp without woof, or the reverse, is not a garment. If wool and silk fibers were themselves blended and woven, the majority governs: if most is wool it is treated as entirely wool, and if most is silk it is treated as entirely silk. A tallis blended from linen and silk, or linen and another material, has no remedy.

[4] <b>Levush: white tzitzis, etc.</b> This is not because white threads are required, but because they are permitted. Levush's statement that two white wool threads together with two techeiles threads are valid in a linen garment means that since wool is required for techeiles, white wool is also valid; it is therefore all the more permitted. This follows the first view below. Levush further writes that in our time, when we lack techeiles, a partial positive commandment is not strong enough to override the prohibition of shaatnez. I do not know why he did not invoke Reish Lakish's rule in Menachos 40a: whenever a positive and negative commandment conflict, if both can be fulfilled, one must do so; only when that is impossible does the positive commandment override the negative. Today, without techeiles, both can be fulfilled, so the positive commandment does not override the negative. Levush himself gives this reason in the name of the second view. One need not object that this reasoning should also prohibit it when techeiles exists. Since techeiles must then be placed, its accompanying white wool is different, as the Gemara implies.

Malbushei Yom Tov writes that he does not know who the “some say” are, and that the Gemara resolved the question that even linen tzitzis in wool are permitted. I later found Beit Yosef, siman 11, citing Rambam, who rules otherwise because the question was not conclusively resolved; this is astonishing regarding Malbushei Yom Tov. He further writes that the later “some say” is the rule of Reish Lakish, and that there is no dispute: according to the Torah the law follows the first view, but in practice one should not do so because of Reish Lakish. This interpretation is correct and is also implied by Beit Yosef, Tur, Semag, and Semak. The rule follows the first view at the Torah level, but because we can fulfill both commandments without shaatnez, linen tzitzis should not be used in wool garments and wool tzitzis should not be used in linen garments in our time.
