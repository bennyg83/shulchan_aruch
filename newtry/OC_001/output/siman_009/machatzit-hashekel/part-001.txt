<b>(ס"ק א) צמר כו'</b> אילים עבי"ד סי' רצ"ח. ר"ל דמה"ט לא מקרי צמר אלא צמר רחלים דבעינן שלא יהיה לו שם לווי וסתם צמר הוא צמר רחלים משא"כ צמר גמלים או צמר ארנבים יש להם שם לווי וכדאיתא בירושלמי שהביא הרב"י. וא"כ ה"ה צמר אילים יש לו דין צמר רחלים. דגם צמר אילים אין לו שם לווי. וגם צמר אילים מקרי צמר סתם. וכ"ה בירושלמי הנ"ל וגם בי"ד סי' רצ"ח לענין כלאים בעינן ג"כ דוקא צמר שאין לו שם לווי כתב שם דבין צמר רחלים ובין צמר אילים מקרי צמר סתם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) Wool, etc.</b> Rams. See Yoreh Deah 298. The reason only sheep's wool is called wool is that the Torah requires a material without a qualifying name. Ordinary “wool” means sheep's wool. Camel wool or rabbit wool has a qualifying name, as stated in the Yerushalmi cited by Beit Yosef. Ram's wool has the same law as ewe's wool, because it too has no qualifying name and is ordinarily called wool. This is stated in that Yerushalmi and in Yoreh Deah 298 regarding kilayim, where wool without a qualifying name includes both ewe's and ram's wool.

<b>רחל מרחל.</b> משא"כ רחל מעז דנשתנ' צמרה פסול לציצית. וכיון דלאו מין צמר דקרא הוא יש לו דין שאר מיני' ואינו פוטר אלא במינו. דהיינו בגד הנעשה מצמר רחל בת עז. ודע דמחלוקת הרב"י ורמ"א אי שאר מינים חייבים מן התורה. הוא מחלוקת רב נחמן ורבא. דרב נחמן ס"ל כתנא דבי ר"י דס"ל דכתיב בציצית סתם על כנפי בגדיהם ילפינן מנגעי בגדים דכתיב בבגד צמר או בבגד פשתים. אף כאן דכתיב בגדיהם ר"ל דוקא צמר ופשתים חייבי' מן התורה (וה"ה דכ"מ דכתיב סתם בגדים ר"ל דוקא בגד צמר ופשתים) אבל שאר מינים פטורים מן התורה ומכל מקום מדרבנן חייבים ורבא דייק מדכתיב לא תלבש שעטנז צמר ופשתי' יחדיו גדילים תעש' לך ומדסמכינהו קרא. דרשינן תעשה לך מהן היינו מצמר ופשתים משמע דבעינן ציצית דוקא מצמר ופשתים. ותו דרשינן מדכתיב הכנף פתיל דתיבת הכנף מיותר. למדרש שהציצית יהיו דוקא ממין הכנף. דהיינו ממין הבגד ואם כן קשיין קראי אהדדי. ולכן דרשינן צמר ופשתים פוטרים בכל מין בגדים ושאר מיני' אין פיטרים אלא במינן. וא"כ הא דכתיב הכנף לאשמעי' דבעינן מין כנף קאי אשאר מינים: ולפ"ז ע"כ צ"ל דגם שאר מינים חייבים מן התורה דהא מוקמינן קרא דהכנף על שאר מינים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A ewe from a ewe.</b> Unlike a ewe born from a goat, whose changed wool is invalid for tzitzis. Since it is not the wool specified by the verse, it has the law of other materials and exempts only its own kind, namely a garment made from the wool of a ewe born from a goat.

Know that the dispute between Beit Yosef and Rama whether other materials are obligated according to the Torah is the dispute of Rabbi Nachman and Rava. Rabbi Nachman follows the academy of Rabbi Yishmael, which derives ordinary references to “garment” from the laws of garment afflictions, where the Torah specifies wool and linen. Thus only wool and linen garments are obligated according to the Torah, while other materials are obligated D'Rabbanan. Rava derives from the juxtaposition, “You shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together; you shall make twisted cords for yourself from them,” that tzitzis may be made from wool and linen. He also derives from the superfluous word “the corner” that the threads must be of the material of the corner. These verses are reconciled by teaching that wool and linen exempt garments of every material, whereas other materials exempt only their own kind. The phrase “the corner” therefore concerns other materials. It necessarily follows that garments of other materials are also obligated according to the Torah, because the verse itself discusses their tzitzis.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 2) At all. The reason, etc.</b> Beit Yosef cites Mahari Abuhav's explanation of Semak's warning not to make linen tzitzis. There is concern that one may place linen tzitzis on a wool garment because silk may appear to be a type of wool. Beit Yosef understood this to mean that silk resembles wool, and objected that a silk tallis does not resemble a wool one. Magen Avraham therefore explains the reverse: there is a type of wool that resembles silk. The statement that silk appears to be wool means that a silk garment resembles a known variety of sea wool.

Tosefes Shabbos asks why silk tzitzis should then exempt a silk garment, since one might mistake a wool garment for silk and use silk tzitzis upon it. He answers that when both materials are before us together, their distinction is apparent, comparable to the law in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 45. This is well said. Chavos Yair, responsa 28 and 30, adduces further proofs. In my humble opinion, however, this does not fully resolve the matter. It should then also be forbidden to sew a silk garment with linen thread, lest one mistake the sea wool for silk and sew it with linen, thereby creating shaatnez. One must therefore say that this is too remote a concern. The matter presently requires further study, as I cannot examine it fully now.

בס"ק ט"ז בשם אגודה כיצד יתנהג בברכה על טלית של משי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 3) They do not exempt, and Bach, etc.</b> The text should read, “and some say that with silk, etc.” This is a distinct, more lenient opinion than Bach. There are three views. Bach is stringent in order to satisfy Baal HaIttur. He explains the “some say” in seif 6, who prohibit making a linen tallis, as following Baal HaIttur.

Rabbi and the Sages dispute in Menachos whether techeiles and white threads prevent one another. Rabbi understands “the corner” as threads matching the corner, usually white, followed by “a thread of techeiles,” and the singular phrase “you shall see it” combines them into one mitzvah, so they prevent one another. The Sages consider them two mitzvos and apply “it” separately to each. Baal HaIttur understands that the Sages require, when techeiles is unavailable, threads both of the material of the corner and of the material of techeiles, namely wool. Therefore only a wool garment with wool tzitzis can be fully remedied in our time. Linen and other garments cannot satisfy both requirements. This is Bach's reason for reciting a blessing only over a wool tallis with wool tzitzis.

Bach further explains that the Gemara's answer to Rabbi Nachman's view, that wool or linen separately exempts all materials, applies only because other materials are obligated D'Rabbanan. Baal HaIttur rules like Rava that other materials are obligated according to the Torah, and therefore follows Maharam of Rothenburg's view that only wool and linen together exempt other materials. This explains Bach's position that even a silk tallis with silk tzitzis should not receive a blessing.

The more lenient opinion cited by Magen Avraham is Maharam of Rothenburg: wool and linen exempt other materials only together, but each separately exempts only its own kind. Thus a silk tallis cannot be remedied with wool tzitzis, but can be remedied with silk tzitzis because they match the corner, even though they are not the material of techeiles.

Terumas HaDeshen then reports the common practice to place wool tzitzis on silk garments, disregarding both Baal HaIttur and Maharam and following the plain conclusion of the Gemara that wool alone or linen alone exempts all materials. He adds that one who uses silk tzitzis must not purchase already spun and twisted silk thread from a shop, because spinning and twisting must be performed by a Jew for the sake of tzitzis. Rather, he should purchase raw silk and have a Jew spin and twist it for the mitzvah. See the beginning of siman 11 regarding the stricter view that even combing must be for the sake of the mitzvah, though Rama records the custom to be lenient. See siman 8:13, seif katan 16, in the name of Agudah, regarding the blessing over a silk tallis.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For this reason, etc.</b> Regarding cotton and linen. This assumes the view that rejects Rama's “some say” in seif 2. According to those who disagree with Maharam, linen tzitzis could exempt the cotton portion as well, because wool or linen separately exempts every material. According to Maharam, however, only wool and linen together exempt other materials, so such a tallis has no remedy. Nevertheless, according to those who permit a silk tallis, one should use silk threads rather than wool threads. Meaning, although they disregard Baal HaIttur, they should at least take Maharam's view into account and use threads matching the corner, since according to Maharam wool alone does not exempt other materials.

<b>In siman 24 he wrote, etc.</b> He gives another reason, based upon “You shall walk in His ways,” to resemble Him, as it were.
