**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When the kohanim.</b> In the Mordechai it is written that they should leave before they begin Retzeh, and it appears to me that his reason is so that people not say that they are blemished when they do not uproot their feet at Retzeh; and see the Beis Yosef at the end of this siman. The Bach wrote that those disqualified only by rabbinic law need not go outside, for when the chazzan calls, "Kohanim," his intent is not upon those disqualified. This implies that if they were told to ascend they must ascend, for they transgress a positive commandment if they do not ascend. But from the wording of the Beis Yosef, who wrote in se'if 2, it implies that if one of the matters that prevent him applies to him, even if he was told to ascend he does not transgress. This also appears correct, for he is not refraining on his own; rather, the Sages prevented him, and they have authority to do this, as stated in siman 191.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he recited the blessing over washing the hands.</b> One may examine this, for he should have said briefly that one does not recite a blessing over this washing. Do we not already know that if he did not recite the blessing in the morning, he blesses over his later washing even if he does not wish to raise his hands? Perhaps his intent is specifically when his hands are clean and he did not touch a filthy place, in which case he need not bless a second time; but if he touched a filthy place, he must bless, for the Tur and Rashi wrote that the blessing must be immediately adjacent to the washing. Although elsewhere Rashi wrote the opposite, Tosafos already wrote that a mistaken student wrote it, unlike what the Bach wrote, that the Beis Yosef wrote so; see there. Even in the Rambam there is no implication against this. On the contrary, the simple reading of his language implies that washing of the hands is required even when he washed in the morning, only that in some places they were accustomed not to wash a second time, as the Beis Yosef wrote, for if a kohen in his service sanctified his hands in the morning, it suffices for the whole day. But if he touched a filthy place, according to everyone he must bless, for this washing is written in the Gemara as obligatory. Yet what can I do, since they were not accustomed this way? Therefore every God-fearing kohen should guard his hands from the time of his morning washing not to touch a soiled place, as appears to me. It is forbidden to delay the walking distance of twenty-two amos between the washing and the blessing (Tosafos); therefore one must wash close to Retzeh, and the chazzan should not prolong Retzeh.

<b>מחל על כך</b>. דכהונה שלהם ומצי מחל [שם] דהרי מצינו בגמ' פרק הזרוע שרבא היה לו עבד כהן וגם בב"ק דף כ' איתא שרמי ב"ח היה משמש ברב חסדא שהיה כהן ע"ש, כתב רי"ו נ"ב ח"ג הכהן חייב לעשות שום דבר פעם א' בשנה להחזיק עצמו בכהונה או ליקח מתנות או לפרוש כפיו כך פשוט בחולין פרק הזרוע עכ"ל ולפי מ"ש התוס' שם דמשום שבעי"ה היה שוחטין הרבה בהמו' ואם לא היה נוטל היו אומרים שאינו כהן לכן נטל משמע דבלא"ה אין צריך להחזיק עצמו בכהן: אבל בטי"ד ס"א משמע כמ"ש רי"ו ובגמרא פריך ולפרוס ידיה ומשני אנסי ליה עידניה ופירש"י שהיה לו עת קבוע ללמוד באותה שעה, ורי"ף פי' שחלה במעיו הי' ולא היה יכול לשהות עצמו כ"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He waived it.</b> For it is their priesthood, and he can waive it. For we find in the Gemara, chapter HaZeroa, that Rava had a kohen servant, and also in Bava Kamma, folio 20, it states that Rami bar Chama served Rav Chisda, who was a kohen; see there. Rabbi Yerucham wrote, Nesiv 2, part 3: A kohen must do something once a year to maintain himself in the priesthood, either to take the gifts or to spread his hands; so it is clear in Chullin, chapter HaZeroa, end quote. According to what Tosafos wrote there, that because on the eve of Yom Tov they slaughtered many animals, and if he did not take they would say that he was not a kohen, therefore he took, it implies that otherwise he need not maintain himself as a kohen. But in Tur Yoreh Deah, se'if 1, it implies as Rabbi Yerucham wrote. In the Gemara it asks: Let him spread his hands. It answers: his time prevented him. Rashi explained that he had a fixed time to learn at that hour. The Rif explained that he had an intestinal illness and could not hold himself that long.
