(ג) ואין לזר - דכתיב כה תברכו וגו' אתם ולא זרים ולאו הבא מכלל עשה עשה ועיין בב"ח שכתב דדוקא עם נשיאת כפים ואפילו אם לא בירך מתחלה אקב"ו וכו' אבל אם יברך ברכת כהנים בלא נשיאת כפים אינו עובר בעשה ומפמ"ג משמע דאיסור יש בכל גווני. כתב בספר מגן גבורים דהא דאמרה תורה אתם ולא זרים אינו רק במתכוין לכוונת המצוה אבל אי לא מכוין כלל לכוונת המצוה רק שלא לעבור על דברי חבריו שחשבו שהוא כהן ואמרו לו עלה לדוכן פשיטא דאין כאן איסור עשה כלל עי"ש ונ"ל דכ"ז דוקא למאי דקי"ל לעיל בסימן ס' ס"ד דמצות צריכות כונה דאל"ה אינו מותר רק דוקא אם מכוין בפירוש שלא לשם מצוה או שאינו מכוין כלל לברכה ועיין בבה"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) A non-kohen may not.</b> The verse says, 'So shall you bless, etc.'; you, and not non-kohanim. A prohibition inferred from a positive command has the status of a positive command. Bach writes that this applies specifically when he raises his hands, even if he omitted the preliminary blessing 'Who sanctified us, etc.' If he recites the Priestly Blessing without raising his hands, Bach holds that he does not violate the positive command, while Peri Megadim implies that some prohibition exists in every case. Magen Gibborim writes that the Torah's exclusion 'you, and not non-kohanim' applies only when he intends to fulfill the mitzvah. If he has no mitzvah intent and ascends merely to avoid contradicting companions who mistakenly believe he is a kohen and tell him to ascend, there is plainly no violation of the positive command; see there. It appears to me that this is true only under our ruling above, siman 60:4, that mitzvos require intent. Under the contrary view, it would be permitted only if he explicitly intends not to fulfill the mitzvah, or does not intend to bestow a blessing at all. See Biur Halachah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>8) Like one who violates three.</b> These are: 'So shall you bless'; 'Say to them'; and 'They shall place My Name, etc.' Nevertheless, there is essentially only one positive command, 'So shall you bless,' which is phrased as a command to bless. The poskim write that the Priestly Blessing does not override the prohibition against a kohen contracting impurity, because impurity entails both a positive and a negative command; 'He shall not become impure for a person'; 'They shall be holy.' Therefore, even if someone suddenly dies after the prayer leader has already called 'Kohanim,' so the positive mitzvah of the Priestly Blessing has taken effect upon them, they must immediately leave. If a kohen does not know of the death, he must be informed. If, however, the impurity is in a house adjoining the synagogue and the synagogue's openings and windows, or those of the house containing the corpse, can be closed, then even after learning of it he need not leave until completing the Priestly Blessing. The Priestly Blessing is a Torah command, whereas impurity transmitted in this manner is only rabbinic. If the kohen learns of it before washing his hands to ascend, however, it is better that he leave immediately.

<b>9) When 'Kohanim' was called.</b> Without that call he does not ascend, for the verse says, 'Say to them,' which Targum renders, 'when they say to them.' This is the call 'Kohanim' that we recite. See below, se'if katan 10. Magen Avraham writes that this applies only if he moved his feet during Avodah. <b>12) When the kohanim do not wish.</b> For example, he is weak or the like. Magen Avraham cites Mordechai that one who leaves should do so before Retzeh begins, lest people think the kohanim are disqualified when they do not move during Retzeh, and also so that the Levites who bring water will not approach and tell them to ascend. Eliyah Rabbah. The poskim write that kohanim disqualified from ascending, even rabbinically, need not leave: When the cantor calls 'Kohanim,' he does not intend the disqualified. They further agree that even if someone explicitly tells such a kohen to ascend, or if only disqualified kohanim are present so the cantor certainly intends them, they nevertheless need not ascend and do not violate a positive command. They do not refrain on their own; the Sages barred them and possess authority to do so. Still, when no other kohanim are present and only those disqualified rabbinically remain, Eliyah Rabbah inclines that they should leave before Retzeh. Rabbi Akiva Eiger's gloss also implies this.

**** ENGLISH ****
(101) And the ten answer, etc. — for even though we hold above in seif 1 that nesiut kafim requires ten and kohanim are from the number, and it is found there are not ten answering here — nevertheless it is fine, for that case is different: since the essence of Birkat Kohanim was stated over Israel, they count even with fewer than ten, and even if there is only one Israelite all ascend the duchan and bless that Israelite; not so in our case where all are kohanim — they do not count to bless themselves unless there are ten who will receive the blessing and answer Amen.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Not to say them.</b> So too the later authorities wrote that this is primary. The Magen Avraham and Taz wrote that one who says them should say them while the shaliach tzibbur prompts the kohanim, but the Eliyah Rabbah is uncertain even about this; see there. So too the Vilna Gaon instituted not to say them, Maaseh Rav. All the more so one must be careful not to say the verses aloud, as the masses do, and especially not to repeat the words from “Yevarechecha” onward; this is a mistake, for this custom is not mentioned anywhere, Eliyah Rabbah.

(קד) כהן אינו וכו' - לשון הגמרא שלא יאמר הואיל ונתנה תורה רשות לברך אוסיף ברכה אחת משלי כגון ה' אלהי אבותיכם יוסף עליכם ככם אלף פעמים וגו' ת"ל לא תוסיפו ומסיק שם הגמרא דאפילו כבר סיים כל ברכותיו ג"כ עובר בלאו וכ"ש אם הוסיפה באמצע הברכות. ודוקא להוסיף פסוק אחר אסור אבל לומר ברכת כהנים כמה פעמים אינו משום בל תוסיף דלא שייך בל תוסיף בעשיית המצוה שתי פעמים [אחרונים]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>104) A kohen may not, etc.</b> The Gemara says he should not reason: Since the Torah authorized me to bless, I will add a blessing of my own, such as, 'May Hashem, the God of your fathers, increase you a thousandfold, etc.' The Torah therefore says, 'You shall not add.' The Gemara concludes that even after completing all three blessings he violates the negative command; all the more so if he adds within them. Only adding another verse is forbidden. Repeating the Priestly Blessing several times does not violate bal tosif, because performing a mitzvah twice is not considered adding to it. Later authorities.
