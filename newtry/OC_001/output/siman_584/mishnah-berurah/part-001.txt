(א) אין אומרים הלל וכו' - לפי שספרי החיים והמתים פתוחים ואיך יאמר שירה ואע"ג שאנו בטוחים שנצא זכאים בדין מ"מ צ"ל ירא וחרד מאימת הדין ועי"כ נזכר לזכות:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) One does not say Hallel, etc. Because the books of the living and the dead are open; and how could one say song? And even though we are confident that we will emerge righteous in judgment, nevertheless one must be fearful and trembling from the awe of judgment; and through this one is remembered for merit.

(ג) על הסדר - לאפוקי מדעת הרב"י שמדלגין בכ"מ שמזכיר חטא ולדידיה א"א א"מ חטאנו לפניך מפני שא"א וידוי בר"ה (כדי שלא ליתן פ"פ למקטרג) ולומר פסוקים שיש בהם הזכרת חטא אין קפידא לכו"ע כיון שאינו דרך וידוי. ובפירוש המחזור מפרש א"מ חטאנו לפניך כלומר אבותינו חטאו שעבדו ע"ג (ואנו צריכין להתודות עליהם כמ"ש והתודו את עונם ואת עון אבותם) ואנו אין לנו מלך אלא אתה לכן עשה עמנו למען שמך ובזה נתיישב מנהגינו שאומרים אותו בר"ה. צ"ל מחה והעבר חטאינו ופשעינו כי פשע גדול הרבה מחטא וצריך לבקש תחלה על הקל ואח"כ על החמור. יש לומר כלה הכ"ף בפת"ח והלמ"ד בציר"י. הט"ז בסימן תרכ"ב כתב שיש לומר רוע גזר בנשימה אחת דהיינו שיקרע הוא ית' הרוע שבגזירה ומה שנשאר בגזירה יהיה לרחמים:
**** ENGLISH ****
(3) In order. To exclude the view of Beis Yosef who skip wherever it mentions sin; and according to him one does not say "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You," because one does not say vidui on Rosh Hashanah (so as not to give an opening of the mouth to the Accuser). But to say verses that mention sin there is no concern according to all opinions, since it is not in the manner of vidui. And in a commentary on the machzor it explains "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You" to mean: our fathers sinned in that they served idolatry (and we must confess for them, as it is written "and they shall confess their iniquity and the iniquity of their fathers"), and we have no king but You; therefore deal kindly with us for Your name's sake. And with this our custom of saying it on Rosh Hashanah is settled. One should say "erase and remove our sins and our willful transgressions," for pesha is far greater than chet, and one must first ask about the lighter and afterward about the more severe. One should say "kaleh" with the kaf with a patach and the lamed with a tzeirei. Taz in siman 622 wrote that one should say "roa gezar" in one breath; that is, that He, may He be blessed, tear away the evil that is in the decree, and what remains in the decree should be for mercy.

**** ENGLISH ****
(8) The seventh. Until "a wife for Hashem." And if he erred and completed the section with four who were called up, or three, and he has not yet said Kaddish, he should complete the number of those called up in the Akeidah section that follows it. And if he said Kaddish: if only one is missing from the number, he need not read, and may rely on the Maftir; so wrote Shaarei Ephraim. And in Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 29, he wrote that if he completed the section with four, he should not say Kaddish until after they read in the second Sefer Torah, and the Maftir will be the fifth who goes up; for we hold that the Maftir counts toward the number of those called up. If he erred and on the first day read the Akeidah section, he has fulfilled his obligation, and on the second day he reads the section "And Hashem visited" etc., and also the Akeidah section until the end of the sedra. Nevertheless, if he remembers while still reading the Akeidah section that he erred, and the last one has not yet recited the concluding berachah, he should roll [the scroll] and read with him the section "And Hashem visited" until its end, and afterward recite the concluding berachah (Mateh Ephraim and Shaarei Ephraim). Mateh Ephraim further wrote: it is proper for every man who fears Hashem to strive to have an aliyah on the Days of Awe; and even in places where they sell mitzvos, he should buy it with money according to his ability; and on the contrary there is more elevation in a mitzvah that comes to him with money than in a mitzvah that comes to him free, without silver.
