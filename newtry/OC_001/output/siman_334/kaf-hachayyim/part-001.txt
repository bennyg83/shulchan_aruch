<b>א) [סעיף א'] אם הוא בלילה קודם סעודה וכו'  </b>וכל שהוא קודם אכילה אפי' בסוף הלילה מציל מזון ג' סעודות משא"כ שחרית דוקא קודם סעודה כלומר זמן סעודה עד סוף שעה ששית אבל אחר שעה ששית עבר זמנה ואינו מציל ב' סעודות אעפ"י שהוא קודם אכילה. ב"ח מיהו העו"ש או' א' כתב על דברי הב"ח הנז' צ"ע וכ"כ המש"ז או' א' דהא ג' סעודות מחויב אעפ"י שעבר זמנם כמ"ש סי' רצ"א יעו"ש.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] If it is nighttime before the meal, etc.</b> Whenever it is before eating, even at the end of the night, one may save food for three meals. In the morning, however, this applies specifically before the time of the meal, meaning until the end of the sixth hour. After the sixth hour, its time has passed and one may not save food for two meals even if he has not yet eaten. Bach. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1, writes that Bach's ruling requires examination. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, writes the same, because one remains obligated in three meals even after their proper times have passed, as stated in siman 291; see there.

<b>ג) שם. והראוי לבהמה לבהמה.  </b>משום דאסור לאכול קודם שיתן מאכל לבהמתו ולכך מציל גם לבהמה מזון ג' סעודות. תו"ש או' א' וכ"כ בס' תפארת ישראל בקו' נפלאות על המשניות פט"ז דשבת מ"ב יעו"ש. וכ"כ המו"ק והב"ד הפתה"ד או' ב' וכתב דזהו דרך דרש אבל לפום דינא אין להתיר להציל לבהמה ג' סעודות אם אין רגילה לאכול ג"פ ביום אלא כשיעור אכילתה של אותו יום ולא יותר ומ"ש אסור לאכול קודם שיתן לבהמתו היינו כששניהם רעבים והביא סמוכות לדבר יעו"ש.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) There: And what is fit for an animal, for the animal.</b> Since one may not eat before feeding his animal, he may also save three meals of food for the animal. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Tiferes Yisrael writes the same in Kuntres Niflaos on the Mishnah, chapter 16 of Shabbos, number 42; see there. Mor U'Ketziah writes likewise. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 2, cites him and writes that this is a homiletic interpretation. In law, one may not save three meals for an animal that does not ordinarily eat three times a day, but only the amount it eats that day. The rule forbidding a person to eat before feeding his animal applies when both are hungry. He adduces support for this; see there.

<b>ד) שם. ובשחרית מזון ב' סעודות.  </b>ופשוט דשרי להציל לחם משנה לכל סעודה. תו"ש או' ב' ובענין המשקין כתב שם התו"ש דאפי' במקום דשכיח יין טובא נמי לא התירו יותר מג"ס יין דאזלינן בתר רובא דעלמא אבל בשאר משקין נראה פשוט דשרי להציל לצורך שתיית כל היום כולו יעו"ש. ועיין לקמן רס"י של"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>4) There: In the morning, food for two meals.</b> It is obvious that one may save two whole loaves for each meal. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. Regarding beverages, Tosefes Shabbos writes that even where wine is very common, one may save no more than enough wine for three meals, because we follow the majority of the world. Other beverages, however, may plainly be saved in the amount needed to drink throughout the entire day; see there. See below at the beginning of siman 335.

<b>ה) שם. ובשחרית מזון ב' סעודות.  </b>ועיין במש"ז או' א' שכתב שאעפ"י שכבר אכל כל שהוא בבקר מציל ב' סעודות שאותה אכילה של שחרית לאו עיקר סעודה ועיקר סעודה בצהרים יעו"ש והסכים על ידו הפתה"ד או' א' וכתב ומכאן סמך גדול לנוהגים ביום ש"ק כי בצאתם בבקר מבהכ"נ אוכלין אכילת עראי בפת הבא בכסנין בלי נט"י והמוציא ומברכין אחריה מעין ג' ובצהרים עורכין שלחן וקובעין שם סעודה שנייה אלא שאח"כ הביא דעת הפ"ו ומהאר"י ז"ל דצריך לעשותה תיכף אתר התפלה וסיים דלכל מנהג יש סמך ורק שהאוכל עראי בבקר יזהר שלא ימלא כריסו במעדנים ואח"כ לא יוכל לעשות סעודת העיקר בצהרים ואם אינו יכול להעמיד על עצמו ישנה מנהגו ויקבע הסעודה בבקר ויאכל מעדנים לשובע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) There: In the morning, food for two meals.</b> Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, writes that even if one ate a small amount in the morning, he may save food for two meals, because that morning eating was not the principal meal, which is eaten at midday; see there. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, agrees and writes that this provides strong support for those whose Shabbos practice is to eat a light snack of baked grain products upon leaving the synagogue in the morning, without washing their hands or reciting HaMotzi, recite the after-blessing comprising the essence of three blessings, and set the table at midday for the second meal. He subsequently cites Pri Chadash and Arizal, who hold that the meal should be eaten immediately after prayer, and concludes that every custom has support. One who eats a light snack in the morning must take care not to fill himself with delicacies and thereby become unable to eat the principal meal at midday. If he cannot restrain himself, he should change his practice, establish the meal in the morning, and eat delicacies to satiety.

<b>ו) שם. ודוקא בני הבית וכו'  </b>כ"כ הרא"ש בשם ר"ת. מיהו בתו' ר"פ כל כתבי כתבו בשם ר"ת באותו בית או באותו חצר וכ"כ הר"ן בשמו וכ"נ מלשון הרמב"ם כמ"ש המ"מ. וכתב הב"ח דהרא"ש מיירי שהבתים עומדים כל א' בפ"ע כבתי דידן והר"ן איירי שהרבה בתים עומדים בחצר אחד דאז כולם חשובים כבית אחד. והתו' איירי בשניהם ולכן כתבו באותו בית או באותו חצר ע"כ. ועיין סי' תקל"ה שיש חילוק בין בית לחצר. תו"ש או' ג':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>6) There: Specifically members of the household, etc.</b> Rosh writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter Kol Kisvei, however, writes in Rabbeinu Tam's name that this applies within the same house or courtyard. Ran writes the same in his name, and Rambam's wording also appears to imply this, as Maggid Mishneh notes. Bach explains that Rosh discusses separate houses like ours, while Ran discusses several houses standing within one courtyard, where all are considered one house. Tosafos discusses both and therefore writes 'in the same house or courtyard.' These are his words. See siman 535, which distinguishes between a house and courtyard. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3.

<b>ז) שם. משום דאיכא למיחש וכו'  </b>ואע"ג דלגבי מת מתירין להציל כדי שלא יכבה כמ"ש רס"י שי"א והכא אסרינן להציל כדי שלא יכבה הטעם כתבו התו' דמ"ד ע"א משום שאין אדם מצטער על ממונו כמו על מתו ואי שרית ליה מתוך שיהא טרוד להציל ישכח שהיום שבת ויבא לכבות בשוגג אבל על מיתו מצטער ביותר ואי לא שרית ליה אתי לכבויי במזיד עכ"ל והביאו הע"ז סק"א והחו"ש או' ד' יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>7) There: Because there is concern, etc.</b> Regarding a corpse, the Sages permit saving it lest one extinguish the fire, as stated at the beginning of siman 311, whereas here they forbid saving property lest one extinguish. Tosafos on page 44a explains that a person does not suffer over his property as he does over his deceased relative. If permitted to save property, his preoccupation with saving it may cause him to forget that it is Shabbos and inadvertently extinguish. He suffers much more over his deceased relative, and if not permitted to save the corpse he may deliberately extinguish. These are their words. Taz, paragraph 1, and Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, cite this; see there.

<b>11) [Seif 2] Some permit moving, etc.</b> This is Sefer HaTerumah's view, cited by Tur, while the prohibiting opinion is Tur's disagreement. Bach writes that Sefer HaTerumah addresses Rabbeinu Tam's permission for nearby houses to save more than needed. Accordingly, they may even save money and muktzeh objects, because they are not so panicked since the fire is in another house; see there. Perishah, paragraph 1, writes the same, and this also appears to be Beis Yosef's view. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 45, paragraph 6. Mahari Abuhav, however, infers from Ran in the chapter Mi Shehechshich that Sefer HaTerumah permits this even in the burning house itself. Tur objects only regarding other houses not burning, but raises no objection concerning the burning house because moving muktzeh may properly be permitted lest the owner extinguish the fire. Beis Yosef cites him, and Taz, paragraph 2, agrees; see there. Beis Yosef, however, questions Mahari Abuhav's words and offers a forced resolution; see there.

<b>12) Issur VeHeter</b>, klal 59, siman 41, writes: Today, when we have no public domain, though see below, siman 345, seif 7, where this is disputed, and there is no concern for a Torah prohibition, if a fire breaks out in one's house on Shabbos he may save anything so that he will not extinguish, because a person is distraught over his property. Certainly, if the fire began in another courtyard, he may save anything. Even money and other muktzeh items may be saved in a case of loss, and the prohibition of muktzeh need not be considered. This applies only to rabbinic prohibitions. One may not dig them into the ground or desecrate Shabbos through any Torah-prohibited primary or derivative labor for monetary property. These are his words. The gloss there writes that since the reason is that one is distraught over his property and might extinguish, this applies to the owner, his sons, and his wife, who need not account for the prohibitions of a karmelis, techum, or a courtyard without an eruv. A friend, for whom this reason does not apply, may certainly not carry the property out. Regarding muktzeh, however, which has no Torah root at all, it appears that anyone may help him move and carry it into a courtyard even without an eruv. The work there is uncertain whether another person may even help carry it into a karmelis; see there. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 45, paragraph 8. Chayei Adam writes there, however, that all these words of Issur VeHeter require great examination, because they are found neither in Sefer HaTerumah nor in any halachic authority.

<b>14) There: Or from violent confiscators, etc.</b> Maadanei Yom Tov writes that Maharash likewise permitted carrying property outside and removing it, because our alleys are not public domains; only a found object that never belonged to him was forbidden. Olas Shabbos writes that if a sack containing money is tied at its opening when enemies come to take it, one may cut it with a knife and break the knot to save it even on Shabbos, but may not untie the knot. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, writes that one may be lenient only regarding muktzeh, not carrying through a karmelis, and only in a house near the fire, not the burning house; see there. All this follows those who permit. According to those who forbid, even moving muktzeh is forbidden.

<b>25) [Seif 4] But not</b> from Shabbos for Yom Kippur, etc. For example, if Yom Kippur fell on Sunday, he would not eat until evening and could exert himself and bring food then. Rashi. Tosafos writes that even to feed his children on Yom Kippur, he may not save food. Although Yom Kippur and Shabbos cannot occur consecutively, as stated below at the beginning of siman 428, the ruling teaches that if Yom Kippur falls on Thursday and one knows food will not be available for purchase on Friday, food may be saved. Magen Avraham, paragraph 6. Conversely, if Yom Kippur falls on Monday and one knows food will not be available on Sunday, one still may not save food on Shabbos for Yom Kippur. Machatzis HaShekel. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 9, writes that Magen Avraham's explanation is not entirely clear. It appears more relevant to those who observe two days of Yom Kippur and are forbidden to perform labor on the additional day, as stated at the end of siman 624; see there. Alternatively, since the Baraisa wished to say 'but not from Shabbos for Yom Tov or the following Shabbos,' which applies today, it cited the entire language of the Baraisa in Shabbos 117b.
