[ב] <b>ויחל כשקורין ויחל בתענית שותק החזן והקורא עד שיאמרו הקהל שוב מחרון אפך וגו' וגם ה' ה' וגו' כי הקהל אומרים אותו דרך בקשה, והחזן והקורא אינו רשאי לקרות בלשון בקשה ספר חסידים [סימן] ר"ן, משמע שהחזן והקורא אין אומר אותו עם הקהל כלל רק אחר כך קורין ועיין רוקח סימן רי"א.</b> כתב מטה משה סימן תשנ"ו מנהג ליתן צדקה במנחה דתענית דאגרא דתעניתא צדקתא, ויש אדם שחביב ממונו מגופו תענית אותיות ת"ת ענ"י ובגליון מצאתי כתוב צדק ומשפט מכון כסאך ראשי תיבות צום, ביום הצום יש להרבות צדק ומשפט, עד כאן, ומהאי טעמא מפטירין דרשו במנחה אחר נתינת הצדקה דכתיב בו דרשו משפט ועשו צדקה תוס' פרק הקורא. יש נוהגים לשער מה שהיה אוכל ביום התענית ליתן לעניים בערב. כתבו המקובלים לקרוא ביום התענית בפרשת נצבים מן והיה כי יבואו עליך וגו' עד סוף, עד כאן, וכוונתי שכתבו אין דעתי מסכמת אלא ליודעי נסתרות:
**** ENGLISH ****
[2] <b>Vayechal: when they read Vayechal on a fast, the chazzan and the reader are silent until the congregation says "Turn from Your fierce anger" etc., and also "Hashem, Hashem" etc., for the congregation say it by way of petition, and the chazzan and the reader are not permitted to read it in the language of petition (Sefer Chasidim, siman 250). This implies that the chazzan and the reader do not say it with the congregation at all; only afterward they read; and see Rokeach, siman 211.</b> Mateh Moshe, siman 756, wrote: the custom is to give charity at Minchah of a fast, for the reward of a fast is charity; and there is a person whose money is dearer to him than his body. "Taanis" [spelled with letters of] "tanis," and "ani" [poor]. And in a gloss I found written: "Righteousness and justice are the foundation of Your throne," the initials of which are "tzom" [fast]; on a fast day one should increase righteousness and justice; end quote. And for this reason they recite the haftarah "Dirshu" at Minchah after giving charity, for it is written therein "Seek justice and practice righteousness" (Tosafos, chapter HaKore). Some have the custom to estimate what one would have eaten on the fast day and give it to the poor in the evening. The kabbalists wrote to read on a fast day in Parashas Nitzavim from "And it shall be, when all these things come upon you" etc. until the end; end quote. And their intention as written, my mind does not agree except for those who know the hidden matters.

[ג] <b>ומיהו אין דוחין וכו'.</b> כן כתב רמ"א, ומלבושי יום טוב האריך לתמוה עליו שפסק כמהרי"ל נגד הטור, ושכן משמע במסכת סופרים פרק י"ד הלכה ד', וסיים ז"ל לכן פשוט בעיני להורות להלכה דלא כמהרי"ל אבל למעשה דכבר נתפשט המנהג כמהרי"ל לא מלאני לבי עד היום יום ב' כ' סיון תי"ב תענית שגזרו ארבע ארצות וקבעוה לדורות הוריתי למעשה על זה היום שאירע ביום שני לקרות ויחל אף בשחרית, ודוקא לזה היום לכשיחול עוד כן, ובזה אינני סותר המנהג עד כאן לשונו. ולעניות דעתי ליישב דברי הרמ"א ולבוש כי מהרי"ל כתב שמצא כן במנהגים לכך לא חש להסכמת הטור, גם מצאתי כן באגודה פרק קמא דתענית שכתב שראה כן מרבותיו, ואין ספק שאילו ראהו מלבושי יום טוב לא היה משיג על רמ"א ומכל מקום בכ' סיון יש לקרות ויחל, וכמו שכתב הט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
[3] <b>However, they do not push aside, etc.</b> So wrote the Rama; and Malbushei Yom Tov elaborates at length to wonder at him for ruling like Maharil against the Tur, and that so it is implied in Maseches Sofrim, chapter 14, halachah 4, and he concluded, in his words: therefore it is obvious in my eyes to rule as law not like Maharil; but in practice, since the custom has already spread like Maharil, my heart did not fill [me with courage] until today, Monday the 20th of Sivan 5412, a fast that the Council of Four Lands decreed and established for generations, I ruled in practice for that day which fell on Monday to read Vayechal even at Shacharis, and specifically for that day when it falls so again, and in this I do not contradict the custom; end of his language. And in my humble opinion, to settle the words of the Rama and the Levush: for Maharil wrote that he found so in the customs, therefore he was not concerned about the agreement of the Tur; I also found so in the Agudah, first chapter of Taanis, who wrote that he saw so from his teachers, and there is no doubt that had Malbushei Yom Tov seen him he would not have challenged the Rama. Nevertheless on the 20th of Sivan one should read Vayechal, as the Taz wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
[4] <b>Of its own, etc.</b> This implies that he should say it in Shomea Tefillah since it is a communal fast, and so it is implied in Responsa Rashba, siman 81. The Bach wrote: an individual who is not fasting who prays with the congregation must say Aneinu, and he should say "on this day of our fast"; end quote. On a communal fast they say "Our God and God of our fathers"; one who did not fast should also say [it] (Maharil). An individual who prays with the chazzan should not say Aneinu with him before Refaenu (responsum of Maharil); but the chazzan should say it before Refaenu even if he has not yet prayed [silently], and a chazzan who is fasting an individual fast should not say Aneinu when he prays aloud even if he did not pray silently; rather when he says silently "Elokai netzor" he should also say Aneinu (Maharshal, plain, and not like Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah); and in Eliyah Zuta I wrote the reason. And regarding Vayechal: if it is on Monday or Thursday at Shacharis, it is enough if there are in the synagogue six who are fasting; there is no added blessing, only that they push aside the weekly portion; but at Minchah or on other days of the week there must be specifically ten; and see siman 492.

[6] <b>[Levush] But a fast, etc.</b> Such as erev Rosh Chodesh, some fast for the sake of repentance; and Magen Avraham wrote that where there is no custom on erev Rosh Chodesh to read [Vayechal], one should not practice so ideally; end quote. And the same law applies that one should not say Aneinu between Goel and Rofei. The Tashbetz wrote that he saw from his teachers that they decreed a communal fast and prayed a communal prayer because one dreamed that the whole congregation should fast. Sefer Chasidim, siman 60, wrote: when the moon is eclipsed it is a bad omen for the Jewish people; therefore one must fast; end quote. A congregation that decreed a fast and decreed that whoever eats should pay a fine: one who ate unwittingly is exempt from the fine, but he may not eat until dark (responsum of Maharil 95).
