(ב) On the eighth day from departure — but on the ninth forbidden, for although it is not usual to leave on erev Shabbat, perhaps a caravan happened and he left on erev Shabbat — then the ninth day from departure is Shabbat; but on the eighteenth and likewise at night permitted; Acharonim wrote: if he can do melacha on that day enough to support himself for the whole week — forbidden to do melacha all week. Until this siman we clarified in Mishna Berurah all primary melachot and their derivatives [and also shevut matters the Sages fenced as a safeguard to the Torah] mentioned in Shulchan Aruch [except melacha of carrying out, explained later in the next siman] — only to copy here several primary melachot not mentioned in Shulchan Aruch: (1) The carder of wool or flax or other things as flax and wool are carded, or as wool is beaten to make felt — beating sinews with a rod until like threads to spin, as scribes do — is called carding and one is liable; beating flax stalks and hemp is liable as "combing." (2) Spinning four handbreadths of anything spun is liable — whether wool, flax, feathers, hair, sinews, or similar, not specifically with a tool [as they call spindle wheel] but even spinning by hand [as women spin by hand on a spindle]; and one who buys threads and twists them [twine] is also liable as spinning; and making felt [capuchin or felt] is a derivative of spinning and liable. (3) Making two eyelets is liable — see Tosafos Shabbos chapter 7 and chapter 13 s.v. "in eyelets" and Tiferet Yisrael who expanded; making a sieve, strainer, basket, mat, or weaving a bed with ropes — likewise with reeds — is a derivative of making eyelets; once two eyelets are made one is liable; Rashi explained these are not literally eyelets but interlacing one thread below and one above and setting the warp — (4) The way of weavers stretching threads lengthwise and breadthwise, two holding each side and one beating with a rod aligning threads side by side until all warp is made, or woof — stretching threads as weavers do is called stretching; when folded and beginning to insert warp into woof it is called weaving. (5) Stretching is liable; beating on threads until they separate and align is a derivative of stretching; combing threads and separating while weaving is a derivative of weaving. (6) Whether weaving in a garment, reeds, or window slats like sukkah covering — liable; and braiding threads, hair, or anything is a derivative of weaving and liable; in detached state; but braiding hair on the head attached is not like weaving, for weaving is not usual on attached hair, and it will not endure — nevertheless there is prohibition to braid or untie braids, as above siman 303 s.k. 26 in Mishna Berurah. (7) Splitting two threads — for Rambam drawing warp from woof or vice versa; for Ra'avad cutting two threads after weaving from the fabric — liable; Jewish doctors must beware when needing separated threads [bandages] placed on a wound — taking a linen piece and separating threads is Torah melacha [collected from Rambam, Chayei Adam, and Torah of Moses noted]:
