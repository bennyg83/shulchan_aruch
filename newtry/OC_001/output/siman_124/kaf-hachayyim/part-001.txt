<b>א) [סעיף א'] לאחר שסיימו הצבור תפלתם וכו'  </b>ואם גמר תפלתו בלחש ועדיין הש"ץ אינו מתחיל החזרה חטא גמור לדבר בין לחש לחזרה, גורי האר"י ז"ל, קש"ג סי' ח"י אות א"ך בי"ע בדיני חזרת הש"ץ או' יו"ד, פתה"ד סי' קי"א אות ג' חס"ל אות ו' כף החיים להרח"ף סי' ט"ו אות ב"ן, וכתב שם הפתה"ד דכ"ש הש"ץ דאסור לדבר בין לחש לחזרה יעו"ש, וכ"כ כף החיים שם. וכ"מ מדברי שער הכוו' דרוש ד' דיוה"כ שכתב על ענין פסוק ה' הוא האלהים היה ראוי לאומרו אחר תפלת לחש דנעילה ולא אחר החזרה והטעם שאומרים אותו אחר החזרה הוא שלא להפסיק בין הלחש והחזרה יעו"ש. וא"כ לפ"ז משמע כיון שפסוק זה יש צורך לאומרו בין לחש לחזרה ואפ"ה אין אומרים אותו כדי שלא להפסיק וא"כ כ"ש שאסור להפסיק בדיבור בין לחש לחזרה ולפ"ז נראה דאפי' ללמוד אסור בין לחש לחזרה, וכ"כ בן א"ח פ' תרומה אות יו"ד, ועיין בדברינו לעיל סי' ג"ן או' ט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] After the congregation has finished their tefillah, etc.</b> And if one finished his silent tefillah and the shaliach tzibbur has not yet begun the repetition, it is an outright sin to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition. The disciples of the Arizal, of blessed memory; Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 21; Beis Oved, in the laws of the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, ot 10; Pesach HaDvir, siman 111, ot 3; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 6; Kaf HaChaim of R' Chaim Palagi, siman 15, ot 52. And the Pesach HaDvir wrote there that all the more so the shaliach tzibbur himself is forbidden to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. So too wrote the Kaf HaChaim there. And so is implied from the words of Shaar HaKavanos, the fourth discourse on Yom Kippur, which wrote concerning the verse 'Hashem Hu HaElokim' that it would have been fitting to say it after the silent tefillah of Neilah and not after the repetition, and the reason they say it after the repetition is so as not to interrupt between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. Accordingly, this implies that since there is a need to say this verse between the silent prayer and the repetition and even so it is not said, in order not to interrupt, then all the more so it is forbidden to interrupt with speech between the silent prayer and the repetition; and according to this it appears that even to study Torah is forbidden between the silent prayer and the repetition. So too wrote the Ben Ish Chai, parashas Terumah, ot 10. And see our words above in siman 53, ot 16.

<b>2) There: For if there is someone who does not know, etc.</b> This is according to the plain meaning; and it is known that all the words of the Sages, of blessed memory, although they contain wondrous secrets, they do not reveal them, and they clothe them in the language of the plain meaning. But in the days of our master the Arizal, of blessed memory, permission was granted to reveal, and he revealed the secret of the repetition: that its level is greater than that of the silent prayer, and both are obligatory, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Chazaras HaAmidah, chapters 1 and 2; see there. Therefore the Arizal, of blessed memory, during the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, would close his eyes and listen and concentrate on the words of the shaliach tzibbur, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 4a, and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaKorbanos, chapter 2; and so wrote the Mishnas Chassidim in the discourse of She'eiris HaAtzilus, ot 1. Therefore one should never cancel the repetition, at Shacharis, Musaf, or Minchah, except specifically in a situation of duress; and so wrote the Ben Ish Chai there, ot 2; see there. And the order of the aspects of the tefillos I have already written in a responsum in my small holy work Be'er Mayim Chaim, with Heaven's help; take it from there.

<b>8) [Seif 2] A shaliach tzibbur who enters the synagogue, etc., descends before the ark, etc.</b> So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Kol Bo, siman 27, who wrote it in the name of the R' Shimshon. But the Rav [Chaim Vital], in the sefer Pri Etz Chaim, at the end of chapter 2 of Shaar HaAmidah, wrote that one should not say the Amidah at Shacharis and Minchah except first silently, and afterward he says the repetition aloud. End of his words. And so is implied from the words of Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, as seen there. So too the Rav Kaf HaKohen wrote in siman 109, from the responsa of the Rav Yaavetz, that if he did not pray silently and found a congregation that had finished the silent prayer, he cannot say the repetition and have it count for both, for there is no repetition tefillah unless the silent tefillah preceded it. And this is what David, King of Israel, peace be upon him, alluded to: 'I will thank Hashem greatly with my mouth': first 'with my mouth,' as an individual; and then afterward, 'amid the many I will praise Him.' End of his words. And the Ruach Chaim cited him, ot 2; see there. And see our words above in siman 25, ot 75, where we wrote that in any matter not mentioned explicitly in the Talmud, about which the poskim disputed, the view of the mekubalim decides; see there. If so, all the more in this matter, where sitting passively is preferable.

<b>ט) שם יורד לפני התיבה וכו'  </b>כתב ב"י דעכשיו נהגו ברוב המקומות להתפלל לכתחלה ש"ץ תפלת המנחה בקול רם כדי שיענו קדושה אחריו ולא יצטרך לחזור ולהתפלל בקול רם עכ"ד והיינו שאומר הקדושה וברכת אתה קדוש ואח"כ אומר האמצעיות בלחש עם הצבור ומתחיל רצה בקול רם וגומר תפלתו בקול רם כמ"ש בב"י סי' רל"ד יעו"ש, אבל מור"ם ז"ל בד"מ אות ג' כתב דאין אנו נוהגין כן אלא לעולם הש"ץ מתפלל תחלה בלחש מלבד היכא שהדבר נחוץ שחיישינן שיעבור זמן המנחה דאז מתחיל מיד להתפלל בקול רם קדושה כדי שיענו קדושה בשעתה עכ"ל וכ"כ מרן ז"ל לקמן בב"י סי' רל"ד דמנהג האשכנזים הוא הנכון וכן הנהיגו חכמים שבדור שלפנינו בצפת תוב"ב וגזרו נידוי לעובר על תקנתם יעו"ש והב"ד הש"ץ דקל"ו ע"ג וכתב דכ"ש בשחרית ומוסף דליכא משום שעת הדחק דאסור לפרוץ גדר ולעבור על תקנת חכמים יעו"ש קש"ג סי' ח"י אות א' בי"ע אות א' וכן היא ע"פ דברי האר"י ז"ל שצ"ל דוקא תפלת לחש ואח"כ תפלת החזרה בין בשחרית בין במוסף בין במנחה ולא יש חילוק ביניהם דבכל אחת מג' תפילות הללו החזרה היא יותר מעלתה מהלחש כמבואר בשער הכוו' ובדברינו לעיל אות ב' ואות ח' וכ"כ בשער הכו' דרוש ב' דתפלת המנחה על האר"י ז"ל שהיה נזהר מאד בתכלית שלא לאומרה בלא חזרה אלא בתחלה בלחש ואח"כ בקו"ר כמו בתפלת שחרית ומכ"ש בתפלת שחרית ומוסף ואף אם היה סמוך לשקיעת החמה מעט ולא עוד אלא אפי' אם היה בע"ש קודם לשקיעת החמה היה אומר בלחש ואח"כ אומר בקו"ר עכ"ל וכן נתפשט המנהג עכשיו בכל המקומות כדברי האר"י ז"ל שאין מבטלין שום חזרה שחרית מוסף ומנחה אלא רק אם הוא שעת הדחק כגין שיראים שיעבור זמן התפלה ר"ל שלא ישאר זמן לומר החזרה בזמן תפלת מנחה וכמ"ש לקמן סי' רל"ב אז כשמתחילין לומר התפלה בלחש מגביה קולו הש"ץ בג' ראשונות עד אחר האל הקדוש והם אומרים עמו מלה במלה כדי לומר קדושה ואח"כ גם הש"ץ משלים תפלתו בלחש מן אתה חונן וכו' עד סיום תפלת י"ח משום דאסור להשמיע קול בתפלת הלחש כמ"ש לעיל סי' ק"א או' ז' בשם הזוה"ק והאר"י ז"ל אלא רק בג' ראשונות כיון שהוא מגביה קולו לצורך כדי לומר קדושה מותר כיעו"ש, ועיין בדברינו לסי' ס"ט אות א':

<b>9) There: Descends before the ark, etc.</b> The Beis Yosef wrote that now the custom in most places is for the shaliach tzibbur to pray the Minchah tefillah aloud from the outset, so that they answer Kedushah after him and he need not go back and pray aloud. End of his words. That is, he says the Kedushah and the berachah of 'Atah Kadosh,' and afterward says the middle berachos silently with the congregation, and begins Retzeh aloud and finishes his tefillah aloud, as written in the Beis Yosef, siman 234; see there. But Moreinu HaRav Moshe [Isserles], of blessed memory, in Darkei Moshe, ot 3, wrote: We do not practice thus; rather, the shaliach tzibbur always prays first silently, except where the matter is urgent, when we are concerned that the time of Minchah will pass; then he begins immediately to pray aloud the Kedushah, so that they answer Kedushah in its proper time. End of his language. And Maran, of blessed memory, wrote below in the Beis Yosef, siman 234, that the custom of the Ashkenazim is the correct one, and so did the sages of the generation before us institute in Tzfas, may it be rebuilt and established, and they decreed excommunication upon one who violates their enactment; see there. And the Shalmei Tzibbur cited this, folio 136c, and wrote that all the more so at Shacharis and Musaf, where there is no pressing circumstance, it is forbidden to breach the fence and violate the enactment of the sages; see there. Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 1; Beis Oved, ot 1. And so it is according to the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that one must say specifically the silent tefillah first and afterward the repetition tefillah, whether at Shacharis, at Musaf, or at Minchah, with no distinction among them, for in each one of these three tefillos the repetition is of greater level than the silent prayer, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos and in our words above, ot 2 and ot 8. And so is written in Shaar HaKavanos, the second discourse on the Minchah tefillah, about the Arizal, of blessed memory, that he was exceedingly careful, in the utmost, not to say it without a repetition, but rather first silently and afterward aloud, as with the Shacharis tefillah, and all the more so with the Shacharis and Musaf tefillos; and even if it was slightly close to sunset. Moreover, even if it was on erev Shabbos before sunset, he would say it silently and afterward say it aloud. End of his language. And so has the custom now spread in all places, in accordance with the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that no repetition is canceled, at Shacharis, Musaf, or Minchah, except only if it is a pressed time, such as when they fear that the time of the tefillah will pass, that is to say, that no time will remain to say the repetition within the time of the Minchah tefillah, as stated below in siman 232. Then, when they begin to say the tefillah silently, the shaliach tzibbur raises his voice in the first three [berachos] until after 'HaKel HaKadosh,' and they say it with him word for word, in order to say Kedushah; and afterward the shaliach tzibbur as well completes his tefillah silently, from 'Atah Chonein,' etc., until the conclusion of the Shemoneh Esreh, because it is forbidden to make one's voice heard in the silent tefillah, as we wrote above in siman 101, ot 7, in the name of the holy Zohar and the Arizal, of blessed memory; only in the first three, since he raises his voice for a need, in order to say Kedushah, it is permitted, as seen there. And see our words on siman 69, ot 1.

<b>י) שם הגה, וכן אם הוא שעת הדחק וכו'  </b>ואם אין השעה דחוקה כ"כ כתב הל"ח בפ' תה"ש או' ט"ו והעו"ת אות ג' דלא יתחילו הצבור רק עד לאחר שאמר הש"ץ האל הקדוש יעו"ש, והב"ד הבאה"ט אות ד' אבל הא"ר או' ד' חלק ע"ז וכתב דאף אם אין השעה דחוקה כ"כ יתחילו תכף וכתב כמדומה שכן נוהגים יעו"ש, וכן הסכים הפתה"ד אות א' יעו"ש. ועוד נ"ל לתת טעם לזה מפני שכיון שכל הצבור מכוונין עליו ועדיין לא התפללו לחש נראה כמו שאומרים חזרה קודם הלחש וכבר אמרנו לעיל אות ח' ע"ש האר"י ז"ל שאין לומר חזרה בלא לחש כיעו"ש, ועוד כיון שעדיין הצבור לא התפללו בלחש ואין מועיל חזרה בלא לחש הרי הוא כמתפלל ביחיד וע"כ לא נהגו כן אלא אם לא נשאר זמן מתפללים כולם ביחד והש"ץ בלבד מגביה קולו בג' ראשונות בלבד מפני ששלושה ראשונות חשובים כאחד עד לאחר האל הקדוש כדי לענות קדושה ואחר כך מסיים בלחש עם הצבור כנ"ז באו' הקודם אמנם מ"ש שם הא"ר והפרמ"ג א"א אות ה' שיתפללו הצבור עם הש"ץ מלה במלה עד שומע תפלה ומודים והם בלחש והש"ץ בקול וכ"מ מדברי הלבוש סי' רל"ג ססעי' א' יעו"ש מדברי הזוה"ק והאר"י ז"ל נראה שזה לא יעשה דכיון שאין חזרה בלא לחש וגם שאין ט' מכוונין לברכותיו זה לא הוי כ"א תפלת לחש והמגביה קולו בתפלת לחש אסור מפני שיתאחזו בה החיצונים כמש"ל סי' ק"א אות ז' אלא יש לזהר שאין להגביה קולו כ"א בג' ראשונות דוקא כנז' וגם זה הוא ע"י הדחק מפני כבוד מצות הקדושה שלא תתבטל מהם ודו"ק:

<b>10) There, in the gloss: And likewise if it is a pressed time, etc.</b> And if the hour is not so pressed, the Lechem Chamudos, in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar, ot 15, and the Olas Tamid, ot 3, wrote that the congregation should not begin until after the shaliach tzibbur has said 'HaKel HaKadosh'; see there. And the Baer Heitev cited this, ot 4. But the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 4, disputed this and wrote that even if the hour is not so pressed they should begin immediately, and wrote, 'it seems to me that such is the custom'; see there. And the Pesach HaDvir, ot 1, agreed with this; see there. And it further appears to me to give a reason for this: since the entire congregation direct their attention to him and they have not yet prayed the silent prayer, it appears as though they are saying the repetition before the silent prayer; and we have already said above, ot 8, in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that one may not say a repetition without a silent prayer, as seen there. Furthermore, since the congregation have not yet prayed silently, and a repetition without a silent prayer is of no avail, he is like one praying as an individual. Therefore the custom is not so; rather, if no time remains, they all pray together, and the shaliach tzibbur alone raises his voice in the first three [berachos] only, because the first three are reckoned as one, until after 'HaKel HaKadosh,' in order to answer Kedushah, and afterward he concludes silently with the congregation, as mentioned in the preceding ot. However, as for what the Eliyah Rabbah and the Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 5, wrote there, that the congregation should pray with the shaliach tzibbur word for word until Shomea Tefillah and Modim, they silently and the shaliach tzibbur aloud, and so is implied from the words of the Levush, siman 233, end of seif 1, as seen there: from the words of the holy Zohar and the Arizal, of blessed memory, it appears that this should not be done; for since there is no repetition without a silent prayer, and also there are not nine concentrating on his berachos, this is nothing but a silent tefillah, and one who raises his voice in the silent tefillah, it is forbidden, because the external forces take hold of it, as we wrote above in siman 101, ot 7. Rather, one must take care not to raise the voice except in the first three specifically, as mentioned; and this too is [only] under duress, for the honor of the mitzvah of Kedushah, that it not be lost to them; and examine this well.

<b>12) There, in the gloss: And if there are individuals in the congregation, etc., the shaliach tzibbur should not wait, etc.</b> And now the custom is to wait for the av beis din, and it appears to me that the reason is that most people pray hurriedly, and if individuals pray word by word they would not be able to say Kedushah with the congregation; therefore they wait. And therefore it appears to me that even if there is no av beis din in the city, one should wait for one who prays word by word; but when he prays at great length, one should not wait for him. Magen Avraham, s.k. 7; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 6; Soles Belulah, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 137d; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 2; Mishnah Berurah, ot 13. And so wrote the Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur, siman 90, that the custom now is to wait for the chacham of the community and for all the notables of the community, and there is no burden upon the congregation in this, for they forgo their honor. End of his words. And the Eliyah Rabbah cited him there and wrote that nevertheless it appears that the rav should not pray at such length, as we find with R' Akiva, who would shorten [when praying with the congregation]; and all the more so in a place where they wait for the chacham who has not yet come, that he should come early and be among the first ten. End of his language. And the Chesed LaAlafim wrote there that if he prays exceedingly long, it is fitting for him to be considerate of the honor of the congregation and to instruct concerning himself that they not wait for him; see there. And see above siman 55, seif 7, and our words there, ot 50.
