כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise the Bach copied.</b> However I did not understand their words in what they wrote "and kindling too is not present"; therefore their intent is not what ends up kindling after the extinguishing. On this the Ran wrote, and likewise in Milchemes: since there is no extinguishing here, there is also no kindling, for it is as if it never was extinguished. And also for this, what they wrote that the fire was made for cooking need does not help; rather he necessarily intended at the beginning of the kindling, before he placed the meat upon them; and therefore he wrote that it deals with one who made the fire for cooking need. And I did not understand why it should not be permitted to make a fire in order to roast on coals, for behold it is actual ochel nefesh; and what difference is there to me whether he makes a fire for cooking need or for roasting need? And necessarily they did not write that it is not a need of ochel nefesh, but rather because he can roast it on a spit; and behold even if he roasted it on a spit he would need to make a fire; only it would have been thought forbidden to roast on coals because he extinguishes, and this extinguishing is not a need of ochel nefesh, for he can roast on a spit. And on this the Gemara answers that he does not extinguish, since in the end it kindles. And if because by roasting it on coals he must add extra kindling beyond what would be needed if he roasted on a spit, that is, what ends up kindling after the extinguishing: for this the Ramban and Ran wrote that since there is no extinguishing here, there is also no kindling, as explained. But to forbid making a fire ideally in order to roast on coals: I did not know a reason. And what they wrote in the name of the Ramban that it deals with a fire he made for cooking need: he did not write thus about meat roasted on coals, but about smoking fruits mentioned in siman 511, seif 4; and he came to reject the explanation of the Baal HaMaor, who had the text like Rashi's version, that the Gemara answers the challenge from meat roasted on coals, and answers there: it is not possible, meaning regarding meat roasted on coals it is not possible, for it is the choicest roasting and is as if impossible in another way, and it is a need of ochel nefesh; which is not so for sweetening mustard, which is possible, meaning possible without sweetening. And therefore the Baal HaMaor holds that meat roasted on coals is a need of ochel nefesh; and the Ramban came to reject this: that necessarily meat roasted on coals also is not called an actual need of ochel nefesh, for it is possible to roast it on a spit; rather the permission on coals is because there is no extinguishing, according to the Rif's version. And one of the Ramban's proofs is from what we say in Beitzah, folio 22b, regarding smoking fruits: Rav said it is forbidden because he extinguishes and in the end kindles, and there are two melachos, and he holds it is not called ochel nefesh; and Shmuel said it is permitted, for it is ochel nefesh. R' Yehudah said: on a coal it is forbidden; on pottery it is permitted; for on pottery there is no extinguishing, and kindling the spices is not present, for it is kindling in an unusual manner, and there is only a rabbinic prohibition. Rava said: even on a coal too it is permitted, similar to meat roasted on coals, which also extinguishes and kindles and produces fragrance, and nevertheless is permitted. And from this the Ramban proved that necessarily meat roasted on coals is not a need of ochel nefesh, for it is possible to roast on a spit, as explained. And these are his words at the end: and moreover, if you examine carefully, one should not have said "even on a coal it is permitted"; for one who forbids on a coal and permits on pottery certainly would not permit making a fire by itself for it, for he holds it is not ochel nefesh and not a need of ochel nefesh; and since that is so, Rava, who added a permission because of ochel nefesh, certainly need not have said "even on a coal it is permitted," which implies that Rava's permission on a coal is similar to R' Yehudah's permission on pottery, without making a fire by itself for it; rather he should have said: Rava said, it is a need of ochel nefesh, etc. Rather certainly this is what he meant: even though this is not an actual need of ochel nefesh, it is permitted to place it on a fire burning for a pot, because there is no extinguishing here, for in the end it kindles; and since extinguishing is not present here, kindling is not present here, etc., similar to meat roasted on coals, in which all of these are present, and nevertheless it is permitted even though it is not ochel nefesh, etc., end quote. From this he proved not like the Baal HaMaor: that necessarily meat roasted on coals is not a need of ochel nefesh; for otherwise what proof would Rava bring to permit smoking fruits on coals from meat roasted on coals? Meat roasted on coals would be different, for it is a need of ochel nefesh, which is not so for smoking fruits, which is not a need of ochel nefesh; and for that reason it is forbidden to make a fire by itself for smoking fruits, as explained. Rather, also regarding meat roasted on coals it is not an actual need of ochel nefesh, for it is possible to hang it on a spit, as explained. Behold: the Ramban did not forbid making a fire by itself except for smoking fruits; but regarding meat roasted on coals we find nothing from the Ramban. And by reasoning, certainly it is permitted to make a fire for meat roasted on coals, as explained.
