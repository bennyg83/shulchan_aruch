<b>מוללין מלילות. </b> פי' שבידו ימלול החטים כשהם רכים דהוה דש כלאחר יד ומפרכין קטניות היינו השרביטין ומוציא הזרע ובשבת צריך שינוי במלילה כמ"ש סי' שי"ט בי"ט לא גזרו. אבל לא בטבל' דמיחזי כמאן דעביד לצורך מחר שאין דרך לעשות בכלים הללו אלא הרבה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One may rub heads of grain.</b> Meaning that by hand he rubs the wheat while soft, for that is threshing in an unusual manner; and one may crumble legumes, that is the pods, and remove the seed. And on Shabbos an alteration is required in rubbing, as written in siman 319; on Yom Tov they did not decree. But not on a board, for it appears like one acting for tomorrow's need, since with these vessels the usual way is only to prepare a large amount.

<b>בד"א כשהאוכל מרובה כו'. </b> אז בורר כדרכו לזרוק הפסולת אבל אם היה הפסולת מרובה כו' בטור כתוב בזה דאסור אפי' לטלטלו שהאוכל בטל ברוב הפסולת כדפרכי' בגמ' אוכל מרובה מי איכא מאן דשרי פי' אפי' לטלטל ע"כ צריך שיהיה עכ"פ אוכל מרוב' ואז בטורח תליא מלתא דאם יש טורח בברירת הפסולת יותר מן ברירת האוכל דהיינו שהפסולת הוא דק אז לא יברור הפסולת אלא האוכל אבל כאן הוא ל' הרמב"ם דאף ברוב פסולת שרי לברור האוכל ובשני פעמים מצינו בורר אוכל א' אם אוכל מועט והשני אפי' באוכל מרובה כגון שהפוסלת דק וטירחתו מרוב' ולד"ה אם האוכל והפסולת שוין בכמותן ובטירחתן בורר כדרכו דהיינו זורק הפסולת אלא דנראה דהרשות לברור איזה שרוצה אבל אם כמות הפסולת והאוכל שוין והאוכל הוא דק אז אסור לברור האוכל כמו שיש איסור לברור הפסולת אם הוא דק ובכ"מ מיישב הגמ' אליבא דהרמב"ם במה דפרכי' פסולת מרובה מי איכא מאן דשרי ה"ק פשיטא דצריך לברור האוכל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When is this said? When the food is the majority, etc.</b> Then one selects in the usual way, casting off the refuse; but if the refuse were the majority, etc.: in the Tur it is written about this that it is forbidden even to move it, for the food is nullified in the majority of refuse, as they asked in the Gemara: when the food is the majority, is there anyone who permits? Meaning, even to move. Therefore there must at least be food that is the majority, and then the matter depends on toil: if there is more toil in selecting the refuse than in selecting the food, namely when the refuse is fine, then one should not select the refuse but rather the food. But here the Rambam's wording is that even when refuse is the majority it is permitted to select the food. And in two places we find selecting food: one, when the food is the lesser amount; and second, even when the food is the majority, for example when the refuse is fine and its toil is great. And according to all opinions, if the food and the refuse are equal in quantity and in toil, one selects in the usual way, namely casting off the refuse; only it appears that one has leave to select whichever he wishes. But if the quantity of refuse and food are equal and the food is fine, then it is forbidden to select the food, just as it is forbidden to select the refuse if it is fine. And in Kesef Mishneh he resolves the Gemara according to the Rambam regarding their question "when refuse is the majority, is there anyone who permits?": this is what it means: it is obvious that one must select the food.

כיון שא"א בע"א ודאי מותר כמו מבשל ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is not called refuse since their manner is, etc.</b> This gloss is not precise; for even though in the Asheri the same wording appears as well, nevertheless the meaning is not that they are not refuse at all, for certainly the shells are called refuse. Rather the Tur wrote according to his view that when refuse is the majority it is forbidden to move, because the food is nullified in the majority and it is as if entirely refuse; and on this he wrote that with almonds and pistachios moving is in any case permitted, and the food is not nullified, and one must specifically select the food. So the Tur wrote explicitly here and in siman 319: it is not called refuse-in-the-majority, but in any case it is called refuse. And here in the Shulchan Aruch, who ruled like the Rambam that even with other refuse the food is not nullified in the majority of refuse, there is no distinction at all between almonds and pistachios and other refuse; and in the course of his running remarks the Rama was not precise about this. Afterward I saw that Maharshal also followed the wording of the Rosh and wrote that there is no name of refuse upon it at all, and he even gave it a higher status than food from within food, and in whatever manner one wants to prepare it it is permitted; and there is no rationale for this, for since the matter depends on toil, certainly one must select that which involves less toil. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 3 that the Rashba wrote: whatever on Shabbos incurs a chatas is forbidden on Yom Tov; and they learned this from their forbidding a sieve and a sifter; and from this he concluded that it is forbidden to select on Yom Tov except to eat immediately, and not to leave for later that day, as on Shabbos in siman 319; and likewise with two kinds of food. And his honor rests in its place: for selecting refuse from food, even by hand, incurs a chatas, and on Yom Tov it is permitted; and they forbade a sieve and a sifter only because it appears like selecting for many days; and so Rashi explained; and this is why they did not mention on Yom Tov at all that one must select for immediate eating, but rather its law is like baking and cooking, which are permitted for that same day, end quote. And one can ask: cooking is different, for it is impossible from erev Yom Tov since it spoils its taste, which is not so for selecting, which is possible from erev Yom Tov. And even though ochel nefesh itself is permitted even when one could do it on erev Yom Tov, that is according to the Torah; but rabbinically it is forbidden, as the Ran wrote, brought in Beis Yosef siman 506 on the ruling that one may not sift flour a second time; Rashi explained: because it is possible from yesterday; and the Ran wrote an explanation of his explanation, and from Tosafos it is rabbinic, for according to the Torah they do not distinguish within ochel nefesh itself between possible and not possible. Also his remark is puzzling to me: from their not mentioning on Yom Tov that one should select only to eat immediately; and further, from their permitting on Yom Tov to select refuse from food, which is forbidden on Shabbos, these two proofs are rejected with one word: namely, the poskim did not write the prohibition of selecting on Yom Tov because they relied on what was mentioned regarding Shabbos, and presumably the same applies on Yom Tov whenever it was possible from erev Yom Tov. And if so, when they permitted here to take refuse from food, that is to eat immediately, as its law on Shabbos; and with this they distinguished between refuse-in-the-majority or the reverse. And so it appears from the nearby ruling that one may not strain mustard in a strainer; and Rashi explained: it appears like selecting, for he casts off its bran. And there is further proof from the words of the Maharil that I mentioned at the beginning of siman 495, that selecting is forbidden on Yom Tov. And Maharshal wrote in the first chapter of Beitzah, siman 42, on the words of the Maharil: and I do not believe these words came from his mouth; behold Beis Hillel permit selecting on Yom Tov, only that one should be careful ideally to minimize toil; and if so it is good to take the large crumbs of matzah, which minimize toil, end quote. And the view of the Rashba was hidden from him, for he wrote explicitly that selecting is forbidden on Yom Tov, only that the Maggid Mishneh disagrees with him. And therefore it appears to my humble opinion that the Rashba's words are primary. Nevertheless, the difficulty that I raised in siman 506 on the words of the Kol Bo in explaining the Raavad from the Maggid Mishneh's words here is difficult even according to the Rashba; for there it refers to a pebble that fell today, and if so it was impossible from erev Yom Tov; so why was sifting flour forbidden there, since it was impossible otherwise? Certainly it should be permitted like cooking; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With a coal of wood.</b> In the Gemara they ask: what differs from meat roasted on coals, which is permitted? And they answer: here there is extinguishing, here there is no extinguishing; meaning because of extinguishing the coal; but with metal, extinguishing does not apply, for it does not become charcoal. And the Baal HaMaor challenged this: for every extinguishing that is for the need of ochel nefesh is permitted; what difference is there to me between kindling and extinguishing? Rather the reading is: here it is possible, here it is not possible; meaning that mustard is only for the pampered and is not equal for every person. And the Ramban rejected his words, for certainly it is needed and equal for every person. And the Baal HaMaor's difficulty: its answer is that sweetening mustard is a preparation and is not included in baking and cooking, but is like grinding wheat; and in the manner they said in Avos: everything needs preparation; mustard needs sweetening, wheat needs grinding, even a person needs preparation; and because of this they forbade it, end quote. And I did not reach the end of his view: for if you compare it to grinding wheat, baking too is preparation, and even more than grinding wheat; and if so, what differs regarding sweetening mustard? And in truth, grinding wheat: the prohibition is not because of preparation, but as written at the beginning of siman 495. And regarding the Baal HaMaor's difficulty, it appears to me that the truth is like his reading "here it is possible," only the explanation is in this manner: namely that sweetening the mustard should be done from erev Yom Tov, which is not so for meat roasted on coals, which spoils the taste; and so it is explained in Beis Yosef siman 511.
