מבלבן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 1) The strings, the wording of the Rambam, etc.</b> See siman 301, seif 42, where he wrote as follows: One who finds tefillin on Shabbos in a disgraceful place, etc.; if they have straps, by which it is recognizable that they are tefillin and not amulets, and they are tied so that he can wear them, he brings them in pair by pair in the manner of wearing. This too is the wording of the Rambam in chapter 19 of Hilchos Shabbos, and its source is in Eruvin, folio 96b: Rabbi Elazar said, one who finds techeiles in the marketplace, if they are strings, they are valid. The Shas asks, perhaps they were spun with a garment in mind, and thus not for their required purpose. It answers that they were twisted, since it is not usual to weave a garment with twisted threads. It asks further, perhaps he twisted them for the edge of a garment, for at the edge of a garment one does weave with twisted threads. It answers that they were cut into strings in the manner of tzitzis strings, for people certainly do not trouble themselves to that extent. Rava said: does a person trouble himself to make an amulet resembling tefillin, and nevertheless we are concerned for this? The same applies to tzitzis strings, for we learned in a Mishnah: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair. When is this said? With old ones, but with new ones he is exempt. According to the Rambam, one must say that the conclusion is that new means that they do not have straps, or are not tied, unlike what Tosafos wrote there. Thus, regarding new ones we are concerned lest one troubled himself to make an amulet resembling tefillin. Rava said, if we are concerned that a person troubles himself, etc., it is a dispute of Tannaim, for it was taught: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair, whether new or old; these are the words of Rabbi Meir, showing that he does not say that one troubles himself, etc. Rabbi Yehudah forbids new ones and permits old ones, showing that Rabbi Yehudah is concerned that people trouble themselves, and the Mishnah follows Rabbi Yehudah, while Rabbi Elazar holds like Rabbi Meir. Since regarding Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah we rule that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, and also the anonymous Mishnah is like Rabbi Yehudah, it follows that we say a person troubles himself, etc. Therefore he ruled regarding Shabbos that new ones are forbidden, because we are concerned for an amulet, and likewise regarding tzitzis he ruled not like Rabbi Elazar, that even if they are twisted and cut we are concerned that he troubled himself, etc., and they are invalid. The Raavad challenged him, and the sages of Lunel also asked him about this, since that is not the conclusion of the Shas there. For it says there: now that the father of Shmuel bar Yehudah taught, which are old? Any that have straps and are tied. New means that they have straps but are not tied. According to all views, a person does not trouble himself; rather, Rabbi Yehudah follows his own reasoning, that a bow is considered a knot and one is liable for it on Shabbos, and therefore he forbids new ones, since one cannot bring them in by wearing them. Rabbi Meir holds that a bow is not a knot, and therefore one can bring them in by wearing them through a bow. If so, the ruling of Rabbi Elazar concerning techeiles is according to all views, that when they are twisted and cut they are valid. He responded to them: I erred, and correct your books as follows: cut ones are invalid; twisted ones are valid. So wrote the Kesef Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchos Tzitzis. See the Beit Yosef, siman 20, who wrote that the Rif and the Rosh omitted this law of Rabbi Elazar, one who finds techeiles, and it appears that their reason is because they hold it was stated regarding techeiles, but regarding white strings we do not have it; and perhaps the Rambam also holds so. The intent of the Beit Yosef is to say that regarding white strings they were more stringent, and according to all views they are invalid, for only techeiles is expensive, and presumably was made for the sake of tzitzis and certainly fell from an expert, as Tosafos wrote there, since a non-expert is not accustomed to deal with them because he knows people will not buy from him; therefore we assume they are certainly valid. This is not so with white strings. However, one can argue the opposite, that regarding white strings all agree they are valid, for specifically by techeiles, where there is concern that the spinning was not for its required purpose and also concern that the dyeing was not for its required purpose, they were more stringent than with white strings.

<b>(ס"ק ב') נכרי כו'</b> בסי' ת"ס בב"י דדעת רשב"א אע"ג דיש לפסול בגוי בעע"ג דגוי אדעתא דנפשיה עביד ולא ציית למה שמצוהו ישראל לעשות לשמה מ"מ בחרש וקטן מקלי' יותר (וחרש היינו שאינו שומע ואינו מדבר ושוטה היינו שמאבד מה שנותנין לו וקטן היינו פחות מי"ג שנה וכדאיתא בי"ד סי' א' סעיף ה') דחרש וקטן דעכ"פ אית להו דעת' קלישתא מהני עומד ע"ג אבל שוטה דלית ליה דעת כלל לא מהני עע"ג ומצוהו רק בגט דמוכח מלתא שיש לו דעת קצת ושומע ומכוין לקיים מה שנצטוו' דהא מצוהו לכתוב לשמה והוא כותב שם הבעל והאשה אבל בשחיטה ועשיית מצת מצוה דליכא הוכח' לא מהני עומד על גבו וא"כ הוא הדין הכא לענין טוויה דליכא הוכחה לא מהני בשוט' עומד על גבו אבל דעת הרר"י דמידי דאפשר לעשות ע"י שליח מהני עומד על גבו דהוי ליה ע"י העמידה ע"ג כאלו הוא שלוחו ולפ"ז אף דשוטה לאו בר שליחות הוא מ"מ כה"ג שעע"ג עדיפא משליחות דעלמא וא"כ גם בשחיטה ומצת מצוה וה"ה טוי' מהני עע"ג בשוטה משא"כ חליצה דא"א ע"י שליח ל"מ עע"ג בשוטה וע"ש בפר"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 2) A non-Jew, etc.</b> In siman 460 in the Beit Yosef, the opinion of the Rashba is that although one should invalidate when a non-Jew performs it while a Jew stands over him, because the non-Jew acts with his own intention and does not obey what the Jew commands him to do for its required purpose, nevertheless regarding a deaf-mute and a minor we are more lenient. A deaf-mute means one who neither hears nor speaks, an insane person means one who loses what is given to him, and a minor means one less than thirteen years old, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, seif 5. For a deaf-mute and a minor have at least a weak degree of understanding, and standing over them helps; but for an insane person, who has no understanding at all, standing over him does not help. It helps only by a get, where the matter proves that he has some understanding and that he hears and intends to fulfill what he was commanded, for he is commanded to write it for its required purpose and he writes the names of the husband and the wife. But by shechitah and the making of matzah for the mitzvah, where there is no proof, standing over him does not help. If so, the same applies here regarding spinning; since there is no proof, standing over an insane person does not help. But the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah is that for something that can be done through an agent, standing over him helps, for through standing over him it is as though he is his agent. According to this, although an insane person is not subject to agency, nevertheless this case, where one stands over him, is stronger than ordinary agency. If so, also for shechitah, matzah for the mitzvah, and likewise spinning, standing over an insane person helps, unlike chalitzah, which cannot be done through an agent, where standing over an insane person does not help. See there in Pri Chadash.

He asks it both regarding kohanim and regarding tzitzis, and this is the question of Reish Lakish. See siman 587. These are his words: from here the Re’em learned that one is not liable for tying one knot, since it does not endure; for if one were liable, then in sewing two stitches you could not find a case without a knot, and why say he is liable for sewing? Since the proof of the Rosh regarding shaatnez, that two stitches require a knot, is only from what we found there regarding Shabbos, and since regarding Shabbos it must be that two stitches suffice with one knot, the same applies to shaatnez, for their laws are equal, with one knot. See there. And what follows should read: and it emerges for us from this, etc. From the words, but in Tosafos Yom Tov, etc., until see there, should be at the end of this seif katan. This is its meaning: according to what he proved from the words of the Tur, it must be that the Tur holds that two stitches suffice with one knot. This is what Magen Avraham wrote: and it emerges for us from this that according to all views, with two holes, meaning two stitches, only one knot is needed. According to the Rambam no knot is needed at all, and according to the Tur, who requires a knot, it must be that one knot suffices, as above. If so, those who make two holes and two knots contradict themselves, for we know that a knot is required in tzitzis only because otherwise one could not find shaatnez in tzitzis. Since they make two holes, which are like two stitches, even with one knot it is shaatnez, even according to the Tur. From where do they know that two knots one on top of the other are required? Granted, for one who makes only one hole, one can properly say that two knots are required, for with one knot it is not shaatnez in one stitch, as the Tur wrote and as above. But for one who makes two holes, which are like two stitches, even according to the Tur one knot makes it shaatnez, so from where is there a requirement for more?

It is permitted. But in Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 7 of Shabbos, Mishnah 2, under the heading The Primary Labors, he wrote that it is proven in the Gemara like the explanation of the Bartenura, that one is liable only with two knots. See there. This is how it should read, and here is the end of the seif katan. One must say that the Tosafos Yom Tov proved there not like the Ran, for necessarily with two stitches we specifically require two knots. For there on folio 74b, Abaye said: one who makes a beehive on Shabbos is liable for eleven sin-offerings, and if he sewed its mouth, Rashi explains, to make an edge for it, he added here sewing and tying, for he must tie after the sewing, he is liable for thirteen. If two stitches with one knot sufficed, he would be liable for only twelve. And if it refers to tying two knots, how could he state it as an absolute matter, that if he sewed its mouth he is liable for thirteen? One can find a case where he is liable for only twelve. Rather, necessarily one cannot be liable for sewing unless he ties two knots. As for the difficulty of the Ran, who holds that one would be liable for tying, one can say that indeed, whenever he is liable for sewing, he is liable for two, one for sewing and one for tying. Since regarding Shabbos one is not liable for two stitches unless there are two knots, if so according to them the same applies to shaatnez according to the Rosh, who holds that it is equal to Shabbos; it is not a connection unless there are two stitches and two knots. But the Tur certainly does not hold so, for if not, the difficulty of Magen Avraham against the Tur returns to its place, that according to the Tur one could not find shaatnez in tzitzis. If so, those who make two holes and tie two knots have whom to rely upon, namely the Bartenura and Tosafos Yom Tov.
