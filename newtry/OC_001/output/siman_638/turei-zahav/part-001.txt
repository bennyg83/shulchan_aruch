<b>Whether the wood of the walls.</b> We learn this in the Gemara from the verse, "the festival of Sukkot": just as the Name of Heaven takes effect on the festival offering, so it takes effect on the sukkah; just as the festival offering is for Hashem, so the sukkah is for Hashem. The Rosh wrote that this is specifically regarding the roofing, as we say in siman 630 that it is permitted to make the walls even from things that are not from the threshing-floor and winepress, because the verse "when you gather" refers specifically to the roofing. So too here with the prohibition of benefit, for that very verse, in which "when you gather" is written, is itself expounded from "festival." How can one word be divided into two matters? It is understandable at the beginning of siman 630, where we expound an extra word for walls, which is another word. He wondered about the Rambam, who prohibited even the wood of the walls. One must say that the Rambam's view is that he prohibited the walls because of muktzeh. The Rambam was troubled by Tosafot's question in the first chapter, page 9: here sukkah wood is forbidden by Torah law, while in chapter Kirah it implies the reason is muktzeh, which is rabbinic. He resolved that chapter Kirah speaks of the walls, while here speaks of the roofing. The Rosh's wonder about the Rambam is because the Rambam combined the roofing and the walls together, implying that both are Torah law, which is certainly not so. The Rosh answers Tosafot's above question in another way, as the Beit Yosef wrote. If so, it implies that even rabbinically the walls are permitted where there is no dismantling of a tent, but it fell on its own. So too the Beit Yosef's words imply in the Rosh's view, that regarding the wood of walls, where there is no element of Torah prohibition, the rabbis did not decree. But this cannot be said, for in the first chapter, page 10, we say: let us say this supports R. Chisda, for we say that if he spread sheets under the roofing as decoration it is permitted, as taught in a baraita: if he roofed it properly and adorned it with painted sheets, it is forbidden to make use of them, etc. This implies that it is permitted to spread a sheet, and the Gemara rejects: perhaps it means from the side, at the walls; but above it is forbidden to spread a sheet because it receives impurity. We thus see that even sukkah decoration next to the walls is forbidden because of muktzeh, unless he stipulated about them. How then should there be no prohibition of muktzeh in the walls themselves? Rather, it is simple that even according to the Rosh there is a prohibition of muktzeh with the walls, but here he is speaking of a Torah prohibition because of "the festival of Sukkot," etc.; in that he distinguished between roofing and walls. This is unlike the Rambam as the Rosh understood him, wanting to equate them to the roofing and make them Torah-forbidden too. About that the Rosh wondered, for there are certainly many leniencies and distinctions between a Torah prohibition and a rabbinic prohibition. In truth, however, the Rambam too prohibited the walls only because of muktzeh. There is only a distinction regarding a condition, for a condition does not help with walls even if they fell, as the Rema writes nearby. The reason is that the statement that he does not separate himself from them throughout twilight so that sanctity should not take effect on them does not apply to walls, for if he wished to dismantle them he would not be permitted to do so, unlike decorations, which can be taken on Yom Tov; therefore a condition helps there. So it appears to me. There is no view here that permits the wall wood even rabbinically; there is only a dispute between the Rosh and the Rambam regarding a Torah prohibition with the walls, according to the Rosh's explanation of the Rambam. But we rule that even the Rambam prohibited the walls only rabbinically, as appears to me.

<b>דהזמנ' לאו מלתא היא. </b> ק"ל הא מקשי' סוכה לחג כדאי' לעיל שחל עליה שם שמים וא"כ נימא כאן בסוכה ג"כ דהזמנה מילתא היא כמו בקרבן חגיגה דקי"ל בקרבן אם אמר ה"ז קרבן דהוה קדושת הגוף ה"נ נימא בסוכ' ומנ"ל לחלק בינייהו דהא קיי"ל אין היקש למחצה כ"ש כאן דרחמנא אקשינהו בפירוש ונראה דדעתו בזה דודאי אין לך לומר שכל הדינים שישנם בחגיגה יהיו בסוכה דא"כ נימא שיש גם בסוכה קדושת הגוף אלא ע"כ דה"ק קרא חג הסוכות תעשה לך ז' ימים דדוקא בזה יהיו שוים לענין ז' ימים שתהא קדושת הסוכה לזמן זה כמו חג ולא תטול ממנו להנאתך שום דבר לבטל ממנה קדושה שלה בזה דוקא שוים ולא לדבר אחר ואף ע"ג דאמרי' לעיל דלרבנן דרשינן עשה סוכה אפי' ביום ז' היינו לענין תעשה לך אבל לענין ההיקש לחג הוי היקש לכל ז' הימים וע"כ נראה פשוט שמ"ש אין נאותין מהם לדבר אחר  היינו דרך ליטול משום שבזה תבטל קדושתה אבל בעוד שהיא קיימת אין איסור הנאה ממנה כדי לסמוך עלי' או להניח עלי' שום דבר כיון שעדיין קדושת' עלי' וכ"כ סי' תקי"ח בש"ע אלא דכאן כ' בשם הר"ן שני דיעות אם מותר להריח בהדס ואי נימא דמריחא אקצייה כמו בהדס שבלולב א"ד דשאני הדס שבלולב שהוא צריך למצותו ואי אפשר בו אלא לריחיה שהוא עומד אקצייה אבל הדס שבסוכה כיון דאפשר ליה בעצים בעלמא מעשה עץ בלבד משמש ומהבערה בלבד אקצייה שלא יפחות אבל מריחי' לא אקצייה עכ"ל ורמ"א מביא בזה בסוף הסי' וממ"ש מוכח להיתר בזה נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For designation is not legally effective.</b> This is difficult for me: we compare sukkah to the festival offering, as above, that the Name of Heaven takes effect upon it. If so, let us say here too with sukkah that designation is legally effective, as with a festival offering, for we rule regarding an offering that if he said "this is an offering," it has bodily sanctity. So too let us say with sukkah. From where do we distinguish between them? We rule that an analogy is not partial, all the more so here where the Merciful One explicitly compared them. It appears that his view is that certainly one cannot say that all laws that apply to a festival offering apply to a sukkah, for if so we would say that the sukkah too has bodily sanctity. Rather, perforce, this is what the verse says: "the festival of Sukkot you shall make for yourself seven days"; only in this matter are they equal, regarding the seven days, that the sanctity of the sukkah should be for this time like the festival offering, and one should not take from it for his benefit any item that would nullify its sanctity. In this alone they are equal, and not in another matter. Although above we say that according to the sages we expound "make a sukkah" even on the seventh day, that is regarding "you shall make for yourself"; but regarding the comparison to the festival offering, the comparison applies to all seven days. Therefore it appears simple that what it says, "one may not benefit from them for another matter," means by way of taking from it, because through this one would nullify its sanctity. But while it stands, there is no prohibition of benefit from it, such as to lean on it or place something on it, since its sanctity is still upon it. So too in siman 518 in the Shulchan Aruch. Here, however, he wrote in the name of the Ran two views about whether it is permitted to smell myrtle. Is it that he set it aside from fragrance, as with myrtle of the lulav, or perhaps myrtle of the lulav is different, for it is needed for its mitzvah and can only be set aside from the fragrance for which it stands; but myrtle in the sukkah, since ordinary wood would suffice, functions only as wood, and he set it aside only from burning, so that he not diminish it, but did not set it aside from its smell. End quote. The Rema brings this at the end of the siman. From what I wrote, the permissive view in this matter is proven, as appears to me.

<b>And on Yom Tov and Shabbat it is forbidden, etc.</b> In the Tur, in the name of Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, it is written that on Yom Tov it is permitted to move them, and on Shabbat it is forbidden to move them. Our teacher the Tur wrote about this: I do not understand what distinction there is regarding moving them in their place between Yom Tov and Shabbat. The Beit Yosef further objected that Ba'al Halachot Gedolot ruled like R. Shimon, who has no muktzeh on Yom Tov and Shabbat, as he wrote in the laws of Yom Tov in his name; and he found no resolution to this difficulty. Therefore the Rema ruled here that on Yom Tov too it is forbidden. In my humble opinion, this can be compared to what is stated in Yevamot, chapter Cheresh, page 114: Abba Shaul says, we practiced nursing from a kosher animal on Yom Tov. The Gemara asks: if there is danger, then even on Shabbat too; if there is no danger, even on Yom Tov it should be forbidden. It answers: it is a case of distress, and he holds that extracting in an unusual manner applies. Meaning: specifically milking by hand is extracting something from its place of growth and is a Torah liability, but nursing is a change, meaning an unusual manner. Regarding Shabbat, where the ordinary extraction prohibition is stoning, the rabbis decreed regarding nursing; regarding Yom Tov, where the prohibition is a negative commandment, the rabbis did not decree. We see that in a matter dependent on a decree, Shabbat is stricter than Yom Tov. So too Ba'al Halachot Gedolot holds regarding decorations that fell, with respect to moving: there is no disgrace of the mitzvah here, and nevertheless it is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef wrote nearby in the name of the Ran: since he made them subordinate to the sukkah, he made them like it, etc.; and if he benefits from them even after falling, there is disgrace of the mitzvah; therefore a condition is nothing, for just as the sukkah is forbidden, so too its decorations. End quote. Ba'al Halachot Gedolot holds that with sukkah wood, where there is a connection to a Torah prohibition because of "festival," etc., it is obvious one must be stringent even after it fell, even regarding moving. But decorations, where the prohibition is only rabbinic because of disgrace of a mitzvah even while they are standing, and they were stringent also after they fell regarding eating, nevertheless it is not fitting to prohibit moving them after falling, except that the sages made their measures uniform with sukkah wood and decreed against moving the decorations because of moving sukkah wood, where there is a connection to Torah prohibition. Therefore it is understandable that there is a distinction between Shabbat and Yom Tov, for Shabbat is stricter regarding decrees. But regarding something that depends only on muktzeh, with no connection to a Torah prohibition, it is permitted both on Shabbat and on Yom Tov according to Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, who rules like R. Shimon, as the Beit Yosef wrote in his question. This seems correct and clear to resolve the difficulty of our teacher the Tur and the Beit Yosef's difficulty against Ba'al Halachot Gedolot.

די"א דאין אסור אפילו בדפנות עצמן כו'. דברים הללו אינן מחוורין דאין שום פוסק ס"ל להתיר אפילו בנוי דפנות כ"ש בדפנות עצמן דהיא תלמוד ערוך בפ"ק דסוכה כמ"ש רסי' זה אלא דהב"י כ"כ בפי' דברי הרא"ש והוא שלא בדקדוק כמ"ש שם אלא דנ"ל להקל בסדינים המצויירים שיש חשש מפני גנבים אנן סהדי שלא תלאן שם מתחלה להיותן שם תמיד ע"כ לא אתקצו כלל ואפי' לא התנו והרבה פעמים מצינו כיוצא בזה ואפי' לענין איסור ריבית כתב רמ"א בי"ד סי' קס"ט ס"ח ואפשר אפי' מסתמא הוה כהתנו בכך כיון שהמנהג כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
That there are those who say it is not forbidden even with the walls themselves, etc. These words are not clear, for no decisor holds that even wall decorations are permitted, all the more so the walls themselves, which is an explicit Talmud in the first chapter of Sukkah, as I wrote at the beginning of this siman. Rather, the Beit Yosef wrote so in explaining the words of the Rosh, but not precisely, as I wrote there. However, it seems to me to be lenient with painted sheets where there is concern because of thieves: we are witnesses that he did not hang them there from the outset to remain there permanently, and therefore they were not set aside at all, even if he did not stipulate. We find many similar cases. Even regarding the prohibition of interest, the Rema wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 169, seif 8: it is possible that even without specification it is as though they stipulated so, since that is the custom.
