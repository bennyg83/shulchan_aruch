<b>במקום השמש. </b> אבל שלא במקום השמש מותר ללוש ולא חיישינן שמא יהיה מעונן כיון שהוא במקום מגולה תחת אויר הרקיע ודאי ירגיש כשיהיה מעונן ומבואר בש"ס ופוסקים דכל שאינו נתקרב אל השמש ממש רק שהוא קרוב אל השמש אין לחוש דל"ד לפי התנור שחומו מרובה ושולט בקרוב משא"כ חום השמש אינה שולטת רק במקום שהיא זורח וכל זה פשוט ומבואר דלא כראש יוסף שמפקפק בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In a place of the sun.</b> But not in a place of the sun it is permitted to knead, and we are not concerned lest it become cloudy, since he is in an uncovered place under the open sky and will certainly notice when it becomes cloudy. And it is clear in the Talmud and poskim that so long as one is not drawn right up to the sun itself, but is only near the sun, there is no concern; for it is not comparable to near the oven, whose heat is abundant and takes effect nearby, which is not so of the sun's heat, which takes effect only where it shines. And all this is simple and clear, not like Rosh Yosef who is doubtful about this.

<b>נגד החלון. </b> משא"כ מן הצד של חלון או רחוק מן החלון בענין שא"א לשמש לזרוח אל מקום הלישה אף אם יהיה השמש בכל שטח הרקיע כיומא דעיבא דכולא שמשא כגון שהחלון עומד במקום צר בין החומות שאר בתים וכיוצא בזה מותר ללוש וזה כוונת הרוקח סימן רע"ג ע"ש ופן משמע במהרי"ל ועיין בב"ח שפי' כן דברי הטור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Opposite the window.</b> Which is not so from the side of a window, or far from the window, in such a way that the sun cannot shine onto the place of kneading, even if the sun were over the whole expanse of the sky like "a cloudy day is entirely sun," for example when the window stands in a narrow place between the walls of other houses and the like: it is permitted to knead. And this is the intent of the Rokeach, siman 273, see there; and so it is somewhat implied in Maharil; and see Bach, who so explained the words of the Tur.

<b>החלון הפתוח. </b> אבל אם החלון אינו פתוח אף אם היא של זכוכית כדרך סתם חלונות אפ"ה מותר ללוש נגדה. ול"ד למ"ש לעיל סימן תנ"ה ס"ק ד' לענין המים דכל שהשמש זורח תוך הזכיכות מבעור ומחמם יותר היינו דוקא אצל מים אמרינן הכי וכל' הש"ס דביצה דף ל"ג אין מוציאין האש מן המים וכן משמע בכלבו וכן ראיתי נוהגין ללוש נגד חלון סתים של זכוכית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The open window.</b> But if the window is not open, even if it is of glass as ordinary windows are, even so it is permitted to knead opposite it. And it is not comparable to what I wrote above in siman 455, s.k. 4, regarding water, that whenever the sun shines through the glass it burns and warms more: that is said specifically regarding water, and as the language of the Talmud in Beitza folio 33, "one does not take fire out of the water"; and so it is implied in Kol Bo; and so I have seen practiced to knead opposite a closed glass window.

<b>אצל התנור. </b> בטור כ' אצל פי התנור משמע דכל שאינו אצל פי התנור אין לחוש אכן המחבר העתיק ל' הרא"ש שכ' סתם שאין ללוש קרוב לתנור מ"מ נ"ל דהיא היא דכל שאינו קרוב לפי התנור אלא מאחוריו אין לחוש בתנורים שלנו שאופין בהם שעשוים מלבנים עבים שאין החום שולט לחוץ כמו בתנור בית החורף רק דרך פי התנור יש לחוש משא"כ אם אינו קרוב לפי התנור וכן מצאתי בתו' פ' כ"ש דף ל"ו ע"א ד"ה במקום וז"ל מאפה תנור היו לשין בעזרה סמוך לתנור של קודש שלא תחמיץ עכ"ל. ובב"ח כ' וז"ל היכי דמשימין עצים בוערות וגחלים לוחשות לפני פי התנור אסור ללוש בכל אותו חדר דהכל הוא אצל האש וכל החדר הוא חם וכ"כ באגודה בפ' אלו עוברין בשם הגאונים שיעור מיל דוקא בבית שאין התנור בו אבל בבית שהתנור בו הוא חם ביותר ואפילו פחות ממיל הוא מחמיץ עכ"ל הב"ח ולא ראיתי נזהרים בזה והטעם נראה לי עיקר דהאגודה לא קאמר אלא בתנורים של בית החורף שע"י ההסקה מחזיק חומן וכה"ג מקום שמרגישין החמימות של אש קרוב לפי התנור או לאש וכ"מ להדיא מדברי הרוקח בסימן רע"ג ומהרי"ל (וזה ג"כ דעת הגה"מ פרק אלו עוברין הובאו בב"י) משא"כ כשאופין במקום שאין מרגישין החמימות ולא סמוך לתנור של בית החורף שמחזיק החמימות או שאין הבית סתום ואינו קרוב לפי התנור סמוך לאש אף שאופה סמוך לתנור שאופין בו אין קפידא וכמ"ש להדיא בשם התוס' להיפך ופיק חזי מאי עמא דבר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Beside the oven.</b> In the Tur he wrote "beside the mouth of the oven," which implies that so long as one is not beside the mouth of the oven there is no concern; however, the Mechaber copied the language of the Rosh, who wrote simply that one should not knead near the oven. Nevertheless it appears to me that it is one and the same: so long as one is not near the mouth of the oven but behind it, there is no concern with our ovens in which they bake, which are made of thick bricks so that the heat does not take effect outside, as with a winter-house oven; only by way of the mouth of the oven is there concern, which is not so if one is not near the mouth of the oven. And so I found in Tosafos, chapter Kol She'asek, folio 36a, s.v. "bemakom," and this is their language: bread of the oven, they would knead in the Azarah near the oven of consecrated [use] so that it not become chametz, end quote. And in Bach he wrote, and this is his language: where they put burning wood and glowing coals before the mouth of the oven, it is forbidden to knead in that entire room, for everything is beside the fire and the whole room is warm; and so wrote Agudah in the chapter "These pass," in the name of the Geonim: the measure of a mil is specifically in a house that has no oven in it, but in a house that has the oven in it it is excessively warm, and even less than a mil it becomes chametz, end quote of Bach. And I have not seen people careful about this; and the reason appears to me essential: that Agudah said this only regarding winter-house ovens that through heating hold their warmth, and similarly a place where one feels the warmth of the fire near the mouth of the oven or fire; and so it is clearly implied from the words of the Rokeach in siman 273 and Maharil (and this is also the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter "These pass," brought in Beis Yosef); which is not so when one bakes in a place where one does not feel the warmth and is not near a winter-house oven that holds warmth, or when the house is not sealed and one is not near the mouth of the oven beside the fire: even if one bakes near the oven in which they bake, there is no objection, and as I wrote explicitly in the name of Tosafos to the contrary; and go out and see what the people do.

<b>And the measure of a mil is a quarter of an hour and a twentieth of an hour.</b> So wrote Terumat HaDeshen, siman 223, and Mahariv, siman 193: that is, according to what we say in the chapter "One who was impure," an average person walks ten parasangs in a day; and his intent, since in every parasang there are four milin, ten parasangs are forty milin; and when you divide ten hours into forty parts, each mil gets a quarter of an hour; and afterward divide the two hours, each hour into twenty parts, so each mil gets a further twentieth of an hour. Or go by this path: divide forty mil into eight parts, and afterward divide the twelve hours of the day into eight parts, so that for each hour and a half there are five milin; and in an hour and a half there are six quarters of an hour, so each mil gets a quarter of an hour and a fifth of the remaining quarter-hour, which is a twentieth. And regarding salting it is clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 69, and it too is from Terumat HaDeshen, siman 160, that the measure of salting is like the measure of a mil, namely a third of an hour minus a thirtieth of an hour; and certainly both are one measure: for five milin to an hour and a half, and in an hour and a half there are four thirds of an hour and half a third. And if there were five full thirds, each mil would get a third of an hour; and now that half a third is lacking, which is a sixth of an hour, and if you divide that part among five milin, a thirtieth of an hour is lacking when you divide a sixth of an hour into five parts; and this is sufficient. Or go by this path: if you divide twelve hours among forty parts, each part gets a third of an hour; but still four thirds are lacking, for in twelve hours there are only thirty-six thirds; and when you lack and divide four thirds of an hour among forty parts, you lack from every ten mil one third of an hour; and when you divide a third of an hour into ten parts, you lack from each part a thirtieth of an hour; so the reckoning is exact and precise here and there. And it appears to me that for this reason he used a different language there: regarding leaving in salt he used "a third of an hour minus a thirtieth," so as not to be lenient regarding salting if one does not know how to be so exact and leave it a third of an hour by estimate to be stringent; and here he used "a quarter," so as to be stringent if one does not know how to be exact, that if he left without work a quarter of an hour it became chametz: all this is correct and clear according to Terumat HaDeshen and his company, and after him the later authorities followed. However, I saw that the Rambam and Bartenura wrote in the Mishnah of "These pass" that the measure of a mil is two-fifths of an hour of equal hours, end quote (meaning all hours are equal, each a twenty-fourth of day and night, not seasonal hours); and that is a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour, for when you divide the hour into fifteen parts, two-fifths of them are six parts, so it is a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour. And it appears that according to their view Maharil meant what he wrote, that it is a third of an hour and a bit more, for that is like the Rambam's view. But the matter is astonishing regarding such great ones, that they did not aim the reckoning according to the sugya of the Talmud that an average person walks ten parasangs in a day, as I wrote; at any rate the reckoning I wrote is a little less than a third of an hour. And the later authorities already noticed this astonishment: Maggid Mishneh, first chapter of Berachos, folio 7a; and Tiferes Yisrael, chapter 13 of Pesachim, Mishnah 2; and Chut HaShani at the end of the sefer in the accounting pamphlet. And Maggid Mishneh there answered that the Rambam is of the school who hold that the day is reckoned from sunrise to sunset, like the view of the Levush in siman 233 and siman 266; and when you deduct ten milin that Ulla said there in the Gemara an average person walks, namely five milin from dawn until sunrise and five milin from sunset until nightfall, thirty milin remain from sunrise until sunset, the twelve hours being reckoned thus according to his view; so the reckoning comes out to two-fifths, for there are six fifths in twelve hours, each hour into five parts, so each mil gets two-fifths. And one must say that the Rambam ruled like Ulla; for according to R' Yehuda, who holds that before sunrise and after sunset they walk only eight mil, thirty-two milin remain from sunrise until sunset, and then each mil does not get two-fifths, end quote of the content of his words there, though he wrote at length, see there. Now what he wrote, that the Rambam ruled like Ulla, his words are correct; and even though in the Talmud they left his words as a refutation, as he explained the matter there at length, and so it appears from Tosafos of the chapter "They would examine" (Sanhedrin folio 41) and Pesachim 11, and from Ra'am in Sefer Yere'im, and from Rashi at the beginning of Berachos. But what he wrote, that the Rambam's view is like the school who hold that the day is reckoned from sunrise to sunset: that is a great stretch, as Chut HaShani at the end of the responsa argued against this at length; and furthermore, on the contrary the Rambam's view is the opposite in several places in the commentary on the Mishnah and in his sefer Yad HaChazakah, as Menachat Kohen discussed at length in Chelek Mavo HaShemesh, maamar 1, chapter 2, see there. And in truth I have not fathomed the view of Maggid Mishneh and Responsa Chut HaShani and Magen Avraham in s.k. 3 who followed him and wrote that this depends on the dispute whether the day is reckoned from sunrise or from dawn. And anyone who examines and is precise in the sugya of the Talmud will see explicitly that the reckoning of the Rambam and Bartenura is a true reckoning according to the sugya, and the later authorities erred in this according to my humble opinion. And I will present the language of the Talmud and Rashi's explanation there on folio 93: we learn, Ulla said: from Modi'in to Jerusalem is fifteen milin (Rashi: since the Mishnah said regarding the Pesach offering, what is a distant journey: from Modi'in outward, that one cannot reach there from the time of slaughtering the Pesach, which is midday, until sunset). He holds like the statement of Rabbah bar bar Chanah who said in the name of R' Yochanan: how much does an average person walk in a day? Ten parasangs; from dawn until sunrise five milin; from sunset until nightfall five milin; thirty remain: fifteen from morning to midday and fifteen from midday to evening (Rashi: an average person walks on an average day of the tekufah of Nisan and Tishrei when day and night are equal). Thus it is clear explicitly that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei when days and nights are equal, an average person walks from sunrise until sunset thirty milin; and this is a peg that will not budge: for what we say that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei day and night are equal means specifically from sunrise until sunset, for from dawn until nightfall the day will be longer than the night by some hours, as the matter is visible to the eye by proof and by sense; and so it is in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Berachos; and so Menachat Kohen discussed at length in Chelek Mavo HaShemesh, maamar 1, chapter 4, and that such is according to astronomy and nature, see there in maamar 1, chapter 11 at the end; and it is simple. And accordingly the Rambam's view aligns with the sugya of the Talmud and is precise according to the reckoning: for it is clear from this sugya that on the days of Nisan the day from sunrise until sunset, which is twelve equal hours, one can walk thirty mil, so each mil gets two-fifths of an hour. But if there is a difficulty upon the Rambam, it is this: since he ruled like Ulla, it turns out that from dawn until sunrise one can walk five milin, which is two hours according to the Rambam's reckoning; yet at the beginning of Berachos in the commentary on the Mishnah he wrote that it is only an hour and a fifth of an hour; and Responsa Chut HaShani similarly wonders at him there, see there. And one may say that the Rambam holds that the dispute of R' Yehuda and Ulla at first glance is difficult: how do they dispute with opposite reasonings against reality, that R' Yehuda holds that from sunrise until sunset they walk thirty-two milin, which proves he holds they walk faster than Ulla who holds they walk only thirty milin; and regarding before sunrise and after sunset R' Yehuda would hold they walk only four milin while Ulla holds five milin. Therefore the Rambam interprets that Ulla too holds one cannot walk five milin, and that he said five milin is according to those who rise early and stay late; and that he said "dawn" is only a fixed time, but in truth they rise early and stay late as R' Yehuda said there; and there are further ways to distinguish, see Menachat Kohen in Mavo HaShemesh. And he disagrees with R' Yehuda only regarding the walk from sunrise until sunset: according to R' Yehuda one can walk thirty-two milin and according to Ulla thirty milin. So the reckoning of the Rambam and Bartenura who ruled like Ulla is precise according to the sugya: two-fifths of an hour, namely a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour; and this is Maharil's view who wrote "a third of an hour and a bit more." And that he did not use exactly the Rambam's language "two-fifths of an hour" is perhaps because he is concerned for the view of poskim like R' Yehuda that an average person walks in twelve hours thirty-two milin; and see Menachat Kohen, who also wrote that many poskim hold like R' Yehuda. And if so, when you divide thirty-two milin among twelve hours of the day, for an hour and a half there are four milin; and in an hour and a half there are four thirds of an hour and half a third, so each mil gets a third of an hour; and afterward divide half a third, which is a sixth of an hour, into four parts, so it is a twenty-fourth of an hour; so each mil gets a third of an hour and a twenty-fourth of an hour. Therefore Maharil wrote simply that it is a bit more than a third of an hour and did not wish to write exactly in the Rambam's language. And however it may be, at any rate it is proven explicitly from what I wrote that the measure of a mil, both according to Ulla and according to R' Yehuda, is more than a third of an hour, both to be lenient as here regarding chametz and to be stringent regarding salting; and the view of the author of Terumat HaDeshen and Maharil, after whom the later authorities followed, cannot be settled at all according to the sugya (and it is even more astonishing regarding Menachat Kohen, that even though he saw and knew that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei when day and night are equal means from sunrise until sunset, as I wrote above in his name, even so he followed Terumat HaDeshen in this in maamar 8, chapter 1, see there; and this requires further study), unless we wish to say that Terumat HaDeshen stated his measure regarding seasonal hours that exist on the days of Nisan, namely from dawn until nightfall that time divided into twelve hours, and if so the reckoning also aligns with the Rambam's view in equal hours; but the plain language of Terumat HaDeshen here, and all the more so regarding salting, and all the other later authorities, does not so imply, and their words do not work at all according to the sugya. And regarding the Rambam we say that Moshe spoke well; and blessed is Hashem who guided me on the true path, and they left me room to elaborate on this, and I know a mere reckoning. And if one says, what need is there for all this length: let us see regarding an average person in what time he walks a mil: Tosafos already noticed such a difficulty in Shabbos folio 35a and answered that they were uncertain what is an average person; see there in Tosafos, and examine carefully. And a twentieth of an hour, etc. And this measure was stated only in a house that has no fire; but if there is fire in it, or any excess of warmth, it hastens to become chametz, and one must give heed according to the matter (Ateres Zekenim).

<b>שלא יהיה בו שום גומא. </b> וז"ל מ"א יש מצפין העמילה בטס של מתכות ויש שעושין אות' מאבן חלק' כדי שתתקרר (זכרונות דף כ"ב כ"ה) . ומזרק שנסדק בשפתו צוה מהרי"ל להרחיב הסדק ולגרדו יפה ולהגעיל ולסתום הסדקים ויחליקם להשוותם יפה או ירחיב' כרוחב אצבע אבל לא ידביק עליו חתיכה נחושת (פי' במסמורים) אלא ע"י היתוך וכמ"ש לעיל סי' תנ"א סקמ"א וגם במרד' לא יהיה סדק או חריץ (מהרי"ו) הסדין צריך להחליף בין לישה ללישה ואם עושה על דף בלא סדין ויש נקבים בדף צריך לסתמן שלא יכנס בה הבצק עכ"ל מ"א וגיסי הרב כתב בסא"ז דטוב יותר לעשות על דף מלעשות על סדין עכ"ל ורוב העולם נוהגין לעשו' על סדין לפי שרוב שלחנות אינם חלקים וע"ל סי' תנ"א ס"ק נ"ב מדין הסדין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That there not be in it any depression.</b> And this is the language of Magen Avraham: Some plate the kneading-board with a metal sheet, and some make it of smooth stone so that it cool (Zichronos, folios 22, 25). And a vessel that cracked at its rim: Maharil ordered to widen the crack and scrape it well and hagalah, and to seal the cracks and smooth them to make them even, or widen it to the width of a finger; but one should not stick a copper piece onto it (meaning with nails) but rather by melting, as I wrote above in siman 451, s.k. 41. And also in the peel there should not be a crack or groove (Mahariv). The sheet must be changed between one kneading and the next; and if one works on a board without a sheet and there are holes in the board, one must seal them so that dough not enter them, end quote of Magen Avraham. And my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Sefer Ateres Zekenim that it is better to make on a board than on a sheet, end quote. And most of the world is accustomed to make on a sheet, because most tables are not smooth. And see above, siman 451, s.k. 52, regarding the law of the sheet.

<b>He should not add flour to it.</b> And if he transgressed and added flour to it, it is permitted to eat it as is, only that one not put them into something liquid; such is the consensus of the later authorities.
