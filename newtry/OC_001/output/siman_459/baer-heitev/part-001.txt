<b>השמש. </b> אבל שלא במקום השמש מותר ללוש ולא חיישינן שמא יהיה מעונן כיון שהוא במקום מגולה תחת אויר הרקיע ודאי ירגיש כשיהיה מעונן ומבואר בש"ס ובפוסקים דכל שאינו נתקרב אל השמש ממש רק שהוא קרוב אל השמש אין לחוש דל"ד לקרוב לפי התנור שחומו מרובה. ופשוט דבית החורף שהסיקו בתנור גרע טפי מיום המעונן. ולא להסיק בו ביום או לפתוח הדלת וחלונות עד שיצא החום:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The sun.</b> But not in a place of sun it is permitted to knead, and we are not concerned lest it become cloudy, since he is in an uncovered place under the open sky and will certainly notice when it becomes cloudy. And it is clear in the Talmud and the poskim that so long as one is not drawn right up to the sun itself, but is only near the sun, there is no concern; for it is not comparable to being near the oven, whose heat is abundant. And it is obvious that a winter-house that was heated with an oven is worse than a cloudy day. And one should not heat it that day, or should open the door and windows until the heat leaves.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A quarter.</b> That is, according to what we say, an average person walks ten parasangs in a day, and each parasang is four milin, so ten parasangs are forty milin; when you divide ten hours into forty parts, each mil gets a quarter of an hour; and afterward divide the two hours, each hour into twenty parts, so each mil gets a further twentieth of an hour. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>They combine.</b> And Terumat HaDeshen wrote that working the hands [into the dough] and rolling with which they roll cancels the chimutz; but regarding piercing with which they pierce the matzos, even though it is work so that it not become chametz, nevertheless it does not cancel the idle periods like real work; see the later authorities. And one should not pass his hands over the matzos and rub them. And Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: and all the more so they do not do well when the multitude sticks a knife or a stick into the dough and leaves it without work. Also those who press their hands hard on the matzos are not called [doing] work, and it is worse. And Sefer Zichronos wrote: and one should not begin to shape and roll the matzos until they first sweep the oven, so that he can bake immediately and not leave even one moment idle. And see Chok Yaakov (who wrote explicitly that everything is according to the abundance of the oven's heat). And because of our many sins, most of Israel, even the learners, are not careful about this; rather they leave several doughs on the table before beginning to sweep the oven, and it is a criminal iniquity.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A crack.</b> Some plate the table and the kneading-board with a metal sheet, and some make [it] of smooth stone so that it cool; Sefer Zichronos. And after every dough one should wash his hands and the basin well and dry them with a cloth, unless he kneads immediately without interruption. And in the Shelah he wrote that it is proper to prepare two vessels, and immediately after the first kneading one should be ready to wash the vessel thoroughly, and the second dough should be measured in the second vessel, and so he alternates, end quote. (And Chok Yaakov wrote that I have not seen people practicing thus, for if so, between matzah and matzah and piercing and piercing one should also be concerned, see there, until [he says] the Torah was not given to the ministering angels.) And it appears to me that this applies specifically with a copper vessel, which one can wipe dry; but with wood, where one cannot wipe so that there not still be rinse-water in it, this does not apply. Maharil wrote not to begin kneading a second [dough] until the oven has been heated a second time, and in the meantime wash the basin and all the utensils. And Mahariv wrote: the one who puts the flour should wash his hands, and likewise the one who shaped the matzos. The sheet must be changed between one kneading and the next; and if one works on a board without a sheet and there are holes in the board, one must seal them so that dough not enter them. And also in the peel there should not be a crack or groove; and see above, siman 451, se'if 18. And Or Zarua wrote that it is better to make the matzos on a board than on a sheet; and so wrote the Taz in siman 453, s.k. 5, see there. And the world is accustomed to make them on a sheet, because most tables are not smooth.
