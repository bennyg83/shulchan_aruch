<b>The law of the blessing <i>Asher Yatzar</i> and <i>Elohai Neshamah</i> and their explanations — it contains four clauses.</b><br>When one leaves a privy one blesses [<i>Asher Yatzar</i>] “Who formed man with wisdom,” for man’s creation is with wondrous wisdom. Some explain [“with wisdom”] because the body resembles a skin bottle full of wind and it is full of openings, as below. Some explain “with wisdom” that He prepared the food of the first man and afterward created him, and He created in him openings upon openings, hollows upon hollows — meaning many openings such as mouth, nose, and anus, and He also created in him many hollow organs such as heart, stomach, and intestines. For if one of them were sealed — that is, among the openings there is one, the mouth, that in his mother’s womb is sealed and when he emerges into the world it opens, and if when emerging into the world it remained sealed he could not survive even one hour; and the hollow organs — if one of them opened he could not survive even one hour. Another explanation: man has a limit such that his openings can seal and he will not die, but once he passes that limit he cannot survive even one hour; and since among the openings are the anus and urinary opening, and among the hollow organs such that if one of them opened he could not survive are the stomach and intestines — this praise fits the matter of relieving oneself; also possibly because if one goes to his openings excessively until he passes the limit he dies — if one of them opens. And if one of them opens it is also of the very matter of relieving oneself. “Healer of all flesh” — through the openings He created in him and removing the waste of his food, for if it rotted in the belly he would die, and its removal is healing. “And acts wondrously” — because man resembles a skin bottle full of wind: if one makes in the bottle a hole like a needle point the wind escapes; man is full of openings yet his spirit is preserved within him — that is a marvel. One may also explain [“acts wondrously”] that He selects the good of food and rejects the waste. Rema: One may also explain “acts wondrously” regarding guarding man’s spirit within him and binding something spiritual to something physical — all through His being “Healer of all flesh,” for then man is in health and his soul is preserved within him (his own words).

**** ENGLISH ****
Some have the custom to wait to bless on hand-washing until they come to the synagogue and they arrange them with the other blessings; Sephardim do not do so. In any case one does not bless twice: one who blessed them at home does not bless in synagogue, and one who blessed in synagogue does not bless at home (<i>Kol Bo</i> siman 2). One who learns before entering synagogue or prays before [going] blesses [hand-washing] at home and does not bless in synagogue; even in such cases some arrange it with the other blessings in the synagogue and do not bless at home (Maharil, Laws of Prayer).
