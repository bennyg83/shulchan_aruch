(ג) באקראי - ועזרות שלנו לא עדיפי מבתים וחצירות שאינן מתפללין בהם אלא כשהם נדחקים ומ"מ אפשר לומר הואיל ומתפללין שם תדיר כ"ז שהם נדחקים אע"פ שהוא אקראי לגבי זה המתפלל המקום נעשה קבע לעצלנים [מהרי"ט ח"ב סימן ד'] ובעיירות גדולות שהדרך להתפלל שם בעזרה בקביעות פשוט דכל קדושת ביהכ"נ עליו אבל גן ופרדס ואיצטבא שסביבה אע"פ שפתוח לביהכ"נ אין בה קדושה ואם היה פתוח נגד ההיכל יש להחמיר שלא לנהוג שם קלות ראש ואע"פ שהמקום חול הואיל והוא מכוון כנגד הקודש איכא זילותא [שם]:
**** ENGLISH ****
Occasionally: Our courtyards are no better than houses and courtyards in which people pray only when they are crowded. Nevertheless, one may say that since they pray there regularly whenever they are crowded, although it is occasional with respect to this worshipper, the place becomes fixed for those who are lax. Maharit, part 2, siman 4. In large cities where the practice is to pray regularly in the courtyard, it is obvious that it has all the sanctity of a synagogue. But a garden, orchard, or surrounding bench, even if open to the synagogue, has no sanctity. If it was open opposite the heichal, one should be stringent not to act there with levity, for although the place is non-sacred, since it is aligned opposite the sacred area there is a degradation. Ibid.

(ו) תשמישי קדושה וכו' - והיינו דאף דבתשמישי מצוה קי"ל בסימן כ"א דלאחר שעבר מצותן מותר לזורקן בתשמישי קדושה אינו כן אלא אף לאחר שנתבלו ואין ראויין עוד לתשמישן מ"מ יש בהן קדושה וצריך לגנזן וגם אסור להשתמש בהן שום תשמיש ודוקא תשמיש קדושה עצמה אבל דבר המשמש להתשמיש קדושה והוא הנקרא תשמיש דתשמיש אין בו קדושה ומותר להשתמש בהן אף בעודן קיימין [פמ"ג]:
**** ENGLISH ****
Accessories of sanctity, etc.: This means that although with accessories of a mitzvah we hold in siman 21 that after their mitzvah has passed it is permitted to discard them, this is not so with accessories of sanctity. Rather, even after they are worn out and no longer fit for their use, they still have sanctity and must be placed in genizah. It is also forbidden to use them for any use. This applies specifically to an accessory of sanctity itself, but something that serves the accessory of sanctity, which is called an accessory of an accessory, has no sanctity, and it is permitted to use it even while it still exists. Peri Megadim.

In which they place a Torah scroll: This teaches that we should not say it was made merely for protection and not for honor, and that the chest therefore does not have the status of an accessory of sanctity. Sefer Shiurei Berachah in the name of the responsum of Rabbi Yehudah ben HaRosh. Although regarding a chest in which a Torah scroll is placed we hold that even if the Torah scroll rests in a case the chest is nevertheless an accessory of sanctity, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 282, seif 12, perhaps a chest is different, since it is made for the honor of the Torah scroll, as stated in the Gemara, unlike these boxes, which are made only for mere protection.

(יד) ודוקא הדבר וכו' - זה הכלל כל שנוגע ממש בקדושה בלי הפסק אע"פ שאין כ"כ לכבוד רק קצת שמירה כמו ארגז וכו' (לאפוקי ארון בחומה שהוא רק לשמירה בעלמא) ולכבוד אע"פ שאין נוגע כך כמו טסי כסף ומכסה על הקרשים הואיל ומונח על הקדושה אע"פ שיש הפסק תשמישי קדושה הוה משא"כ פרוכת שלנו בסעיף ו' אע"ג דדבר יקר וכבוד הוא מ"מ הוי כמחיצה ואין מונח כלל על הקדושה [פמ"ג] ועיין בבה"ל מה שכתבנו בשם הגר"א בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
Specifically the item, etc.: This is the rule: anything that actually touches the sanctity without an intervening object, even if it is not so much for honor but only for some protection, such as a chest and the like, excluding a cabinet in a wall, which is merely for protection; and anything made for honor, even if it does not touch in that way, such as silver plates and a cover over the boards, since it rests on the sanctity even with an intervening object, is an accessory of sanctity. This is unlike our paroches in seif 6, for although it is an expensive and honorable item, nevertheless it is like a partition and does not rest on the sanctity at all. Peri Megadim. (נב) מותר לקרות - היינו בד"ת ולא בקריאה של חול ולא דמי לנר חנוכה דלרוב הפוסקים אפילו ללמוד אסור לאורן וכדלקמן בסימן תרע"ג שאני התם דבעינן שיהא ניכר שהודלק לשם מצות נר חנוכה ולא לשום דבר אחר משא"כ הכא דכונת הנודר היה רק כדי להרבות מאור ביהכ"נ לפיכך כל כמה דקרו אינשי לאורו בד"ת טפי איכא מצוה ומקבל שכר. ומיהו זה דוקא במי שנדר נדר לביהכ"נ וכנ"ל אבל במקומות שעושין הגזברים נרות והדרך ליקח במו"ש כ"א נר לביתו אפילו משתמש בהן תשמיש חול שרי דה"ל לב ב"ד מתנה עליהן אבל באותם שעומדים על המנורה אסור לקרות בהן דבר של חול [אחרונים]:
**** ENGLISH ****
It is permitted to read: This means words of Torah, but not mundane reading. This is not comparable to a Chanukah candle, for according to most poskim even learning by its light is forbidden, as below in siman 673. There it is different, because it must be recognizable that it was lit for the mitzvah of the Chanukah candle and not for anything else. Here, however, the intention of the one who vowed was only to increase light in the synagogue; therefore, the more people read words of Torah by its light, the more mitzvah there is, and he receives reward. However, this applies specifically to one who vowed a vow to the synagogue, as above. But in places where the treasurers make candles and the practice is that after Shabbos each person takes a candle to his home, even if he uses them for mundane use it is permitted, for this is considered as though the condition of beis din was made concerning them. But those candles that stand on the menorah may not be used for mundane reading.

(נח) אלא מה שנתן - דמסתמא לא התנו אלא לחלק מבורר באותה שעה ולא לעשות שומא אחרת ואף אם היה ראוי להסתפק בלשונם למה נתכונו נמי דינא הכי כיון דממ"נ הס"ת נשאר להנשארים אלא שצריכין להחזיר הדמים ויש ספק כמה יחזירו מצו למימר הס"ת ממ"נ דידן היא ואי משום דמי אייתי ראיה ושקול [ב"י]. ולפי טעם זה האחרון אפילו הוזלו הספרים א"צ ליתן לו אלא כשער הזול [לבוש] ואם היה התנאי ביניהם שאם ירצה הוא יתן להם דמיהם ויקח הס"ת אע"פ שנתרצה אח"כ ליתן להם הס"ת מ"מ הם אינם נקראים מוחזקים כיון שיש בידו לסלקן [מ"א]:
**** ENGLISH ****
Only what he gave: Presumably they stipulated only with respect to the defined portion at that time, not to make another appraisal. Even if there were room to doubt what they meant by their wording, the law is also so, since in any event the Torah scroll remains with those who remain; they only need to return money, and there is doubt how much they must return. They can say, the Torah scroll is certainly ours, and if you claim money, bring proof and take it. Beit Yosef. According to this latter reason, even if the books decreased in value, they need give him only according to the lower price. Levush. If their condition was that if he wished, he could give them their money and take the Torah scroll, then even if he later agreed to give them the Torah scroll, nevertheless they are not called the ones in possession, since he has the ability to remove them by payment. Only: All this is in a case like this, where without the condition he would have no right at all to take money for his share, as above, and therefore the Torah scroll is in the possession of those who remain, except that the condition causes them to give him money. They can say, bring proof how much is due to you because of your condition, and take it. But partners who bought a house together, and there was a condition between them that they would give him his share whenever he wished, and afterward the value increased, and one of them wants to withdraw and take his share from those who remain, they certainly must give him according to what it is worth now. The condition does not weaken his power; it came only to improve his power, so that he would not need to wait until the time they wanted. This is unlike here, regarding a Torah scroll, where by law he has no right in the Torah scroll apart from the condition.
