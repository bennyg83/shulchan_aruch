<b>Seif 8.</b> In the gloss: “The practice is to derive various forms of benefit from sacred articles.” Note: See Taz, seif katan 7, who writes, “Were I not afraid,” etc. See my novellae to Yoma 12b concerning the opinion there in the Gemara that the second kohen is fit neither to serve as Kohen Gadol nor as an ordinary kohen because one ascends in sanctity and does not descend. I proved there that the halachah does not follow that view. This is especially evident from the supposition there that the Kohen Gadol might have a rival wife during his lifetime, in which case the second kohen might never serve again, and yet the rule that one ascends in sanctity and does not descend would still apply to him. See there and examine carefully. Taz cites Maharshal as holding that it is not considered a descent so long as the Sefer Torah remains inside the receptacle. Magen Avraham likewise cites this in his name, while he cites Bach as holding that even this constitutes a descent from sanctity. See there. See also my novellae to the laws of Chanukah, siman 671, where I brought proof for Bach that even this constitutes a descent from sanctity. See what I wrote there and examine carefully. In my humble opinion, proof may be brought for Maharshal’s view, cited by Taz, that a temporary descent in sanctity is not regarded as a descent. Sukkah 44b asks: Since you say that the aravah is taken separately, it is obvious that one does not fulfill the obligation with the aravah bound together with the lulav. This is difficult. Perhaps the teaching comes to tell us that even if one removes that aravah from the lulav, he still does not fulfill his obligation with it. One might ask why, in truth, he should not fulfill the obligation with it once it is taken separately. It may be answered that since the halachah is that one may not lower an object from greater sanctity to lesser sanctity, this proves that doing so is a degradation of the mitzvah. Although articles used for a mitzvah may be discarded after their mitzvah has ended, while their mitzvah is still current they may not be used for benefit because of degradation of the mitzvah, as is stated regarding decorations of the sukkah and similar cases. Using them for a lesser mitzvah should likewise constitute degradation. The lulav obligation on the first day outside the Mikdash is D’Oraisa, whereas aravah has no D’Oraisa basis outside the Mikdash. Lulav is observed all seven days as a remembrance of the Mikdash, whereas aravah is observed for only one day. Aravah is therefore a lesser mitzvah. It follows that one may not lower the aravah from the greater sanctity of lulav to the lesser sanctity of aravah. If it remains with the lulav, there is no degradation, as is evident here regarding books, that while the sacred books remain in the receptacle other books may be placed there as well. Yet he cannot then fulfill the aravah obligation, because aravah must be taken separately. If he removes it from the lulav, he temporarily lowers it from greater sanctity to lesser sanctity while it is still fit for the greater mitzvah that day. One cannot answer that this should matter only initially, whereas after the fact he should fulfill his obligation, for the Tanna states that one does not fulfill the obligation with the aravah of the lulav, implying even after the fact. The act would be a mitzvah performed through a transgression, which most poskim invalidate even after the fact. Why, then, does the Gemara ask that the ruling is obvious? Perhaps, while the object remains fit for its mitzvah, just as it is forbidden to use it for a mundane purpose, it is also forbidden to divert it to a lesser sanctity, since that too is included in degradation of the mitzvah. Necessarily, a temporary lowering is not regarded as a lowering. Since it can still be returned to the lulav, it is not deemed a descent. If this is permitted even with sanctity, it is certainly permitted with articles used for a mitzvah. This supports Maharshal against Beit Yosef. Examine this carefully.
