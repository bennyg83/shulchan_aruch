**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 8: Made for honor and placed, etc.</b> Meaning that it is permanently attached to the Sefer Torah. This refers to leather or silk covering the wooden boards of the scroll. A curtain, however, is not attached to the scroll. This differs from Olas Tamid, who holds that even though the board covering serves an article that itself serves the Sefer Torah, it acquires sanctity because it was made for honor. According to him, a curtain made for honor should likewise be sacred, and Beit Yosef’s distinction is difficult: he rules stringently concerning the board covering, yet says that a curtain is sacred only because the Sefer Torah is placed upon it, while our paroches is not sacred despite being made for honor. According to Magen Avraham, the law does not depend on whether it was made for honor.

<b>Seif katan 11: He raises an objection, etc.</b> Mordechai implies that even an ark built into a wall, as in seif 1, may not be converted into a chair. <b>And in Megillah 26b, etc.</b> Rava initially thought that where a corpse lay in a room adjoining the synagogue and an open window connected them, preventing kohanim from entering the synagogue, one could place the ark in the window to block tumah. He thought that the ark containing the Sefer Torah was a wooden vessel made to remain stationary, meaning it stayed permanently in one place and was not moved, and therefore did not contract tumah because a wooden vessel must resemble a movable sack. Anything incapable of contracting tumah can interpose against tumah. The Gemara rejected Rava’s view because the ark is sometimes moved and therefore does contract tumah.

Tosafos must therefore hold that such placement, where the ark is not used for anything, is not considered lowering.

<b>They said in Shabbos, etc.</b> Rashi explains that “arna” was the term they used for their towers, but “aron,” etc. Although aron and arna differ only in that aron is Hebrew and arna is Aramaic, the word arna became specifically associated with towers. It is therefore degrading to call the aron kodesh an arna. The term aron is not uniquely associated with towers.
