**** ENGLISH ****
For they are fit to chew, all the more so these; and the later authorities challenged: why need this reason? Let [him] say that barley is animal food, and animal food may be moved and is not muktzeh. And one can say that this is explained above in siman 308, se'if 31, in Magen Avraham s.k. 56: raw meat that is hard, for it is not fit as raw meat (umza), is muktzeh even though one can throw it to dogs; nevertheless, since at Motza'ei Shabbos it will be fit for himself through cooking, his mind is not to throw it to dogs, and it is muktzeh; and the same applies in such a case regarding a chick, and likewise at the beginning of siman 515. And if so, here with barley and rye: if he ground them and made bread from them, the matter is revealed that his mind was not to give them to an animal; and if not that they are fit raw for a person, they would be muktzeh even though they are fit for an animal, as above.

<b>(ס"ק ג) שרוב' כו'</b> ונ"ל דאם ישראל קנה כו' בלא חותם ואפי' חתמו ולמחר מצא שהחותם מקולקל ונראה שנעשה בכונה ע"י אדם אפ"ה מותר כמ"ש שם בי"ד כיון דאינו נהנה בחליפין דהא יפה כמו הראשון ולהכשיל ל"ח. דוקא בנדון דהמהרי"ל דהיה קמח של גוי ועדיין לא קנה ישראל יש לחוש לחליפין דרך מקרה ולא נתכוין להכשיל. מה שאין כן כשכבר קנה ישראל. לא יחליף אם אין לו הנאה. ובספר חמד משה השיג על מ"א. וכתב דהואיל והוא חנוני אפי' שהוא יפה כראשון אכתי א"ל לחליפין ולא נתכוין להכשיל אלא שבאו ב"א לקנות קמח ולא הספיק קמחו שה"ל ומכר של ישראל. ואח"ז טחן היום ונתן אותם לישראל תמורת קמחו של ישראל הראשון עיין שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 3) Whose majority, etc.</b> And it appears to me that if a Jew bought, etc., without a seal, and even if he sealed it and the next day found that the seal was spoiled and it appears that it was done deliberately by a person, even so it is permitted, as written there in Yoreh De'ah, since he does not benefit from the exchange, for it is as fine as the first, and we are not concerned that he intends to cause a stumbling. Only in the case of the Maharil, where it was flour of a non-Jew and a Jew had not yet bought it, is there reason to fear an incidental exchange without intent to cause a stumbling. Which is not so when a Jew has already bought: he will not exchange if he has no benefit. And in the book Chemed Moshe he challenged the Magen Avraham, and wrote that since he is a shopkeeper, even if it is as fine as the first, still there is reason for exchange without intent to cause a stumbling: rather, people came to buy flour and his own flour that he had was not enough, and he sold the Jew's [flour]; and afterward he ground today and gave those [bags] to the Jew in place of the Jew's first flour; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it appears to me in a place where the appraisal, etc.</b> It is permitted to take it without giving money. And likewise it is permitted to mention that [amount of] money, such as "give me for a perutah" or "for two perutos," for this is not naming a sum of money in the manner of buying and selling; rather he informs the seller how much he needs. Which is not so when the appraisal of the bread is not known: one cannot say that he intended to inform him how much he needs, for even if he says "for a perutah" or "two perutos," [the seller] still will not know how much they will give him and whether it will suffice for him. And all this is explained above in siman 323; and see there further details of the laws on this.
