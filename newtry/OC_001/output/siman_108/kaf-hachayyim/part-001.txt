<b>ב) שם. הראשונה מנתה והשנייה לתשלומין וכו'  </b>והטעם כתב בלבוש משום דק"ל תדיר ושאינו תדיר תדיר קודם וילפינן מקראי מיהו המי"ט פ' תפה"ש או' ת' הקשה על טעם זה וכתב הוא טעם אחר לפי שיש לחוש שע"י כן תתבטל תקנתא ויבא להתפללי אותה אפי' שלא בזמן תפלה שנייה לה אבל מכיון שאפי' אם התפלל קודם לשל חובה צריך שיחזור ויתפלל אותה של תשלומין לא אתי לידי פשיעה זו שיתפלל אותה לאחר זמנה וקודם זמן תפלה שנייה עכ"ד, והא"ר או' א' הקשה גם על טעם זה וכתב הוא הטעם משום דאין תפלת תשלומין מתקבלת אלא אגב תפלת העתיד אבל כשמתפלל התשלומין קודם תפלת העתיד אינה מתקבלת כיון שעבר זמנה עכ"ל, ול"נ בס"ד לתת טעם בסוד ע"פ מ"ש הרב ז"ל בשער הכוו' בדרוש שינוי התפלות דנ"ט ע"ב והוא כי בתפלת שחרית תהיה המשכת המוחין בכליה פנימיים ובמנחה בכלים אמצעיים ובערבית בכלים חיצוניים, וע"כ אם יתפלל תפלת תשלומין קודם זה לא יכון כיון שעכשיו היא בחי' אחרת אבל אם יתפלל תפלת אותה הבחי' של עכשיו אז ימשכו המוחין של בחי' זו וא"כ מכ"ש שצריך שימשכו המוחין של בחי' שקודם לזה ואז עי"ז יהיה לו פתח שיוכל להתפלל גם תפלה שעברה כדי שיהיו המשכת המוחין בשלימות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) There: The first counts for the present obligation and the second as compensation, etc.</b> Levush explains that the frequent takes precedence over the infrequent, a principle derived from Scripture. Maadanei Yom Tov, chapter Tefillas HaShachar, paragraph 400, questions this and gives another reason: otherwise the enactment may be undermined, and a person may come to pray the compensation even when it is not yet time for the second prayer. Since one who puts the compensation first must repeat it, he will not negligently pray it after its own time but before the time of the second prayer. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, challenges this reason as well and explains that the compensatory prayer is accepted only together with the coming prayer; if one prays the compensation before the coming prayer, it is not accepted because its time has passed. With Hashem's help, it appears to me that a mystical reason can be given from Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the changing prayers, folio 59b. In Shacharis the mochin are drawn into the inner vessels, at Minchah into the middle vessels, and at Maariv into the outer vessels. If one first prays the compensatory prayer, it is inappropriate because the present spiritual aspect is different. If he first prays the prayer corresponding to the present aspect, the mochin of that aspect are drawn down; all the more should the mochin of the preceding aspect then be drawn. This opens the way for him also to pray the missed prayer, so the mochin will be drawn in completeness.

<b>ג) שם, ואם היפך לא יצא וכו'  </b>פי' שהיה בדעת שהראשונה תהא לתשלומין לא יצא ד"מ וא' א' א"ר או' א' סידור בי"ע בדיני תפלת תשלומין שאחר תפלת ערבית או' א' ר"ז או' א' בן א"ח פ' משפטים או' ט' וכן הסכים הנה"ש או' ב' מיהו י"א שא"צ לחזור ולהתפלל אא"כ גילה דעתו בפירוש בתפלתו שהשנייה היא לשם חובה והראשונה לתשלומין וכגון שהוא מוצאי שבת והבדיל בשנייה ולא הבדיל בראשונה אבל אם היפך במחשבה לחוד יצא, ט"ז סק"ב, מ"א סק"ב סו"ב או' ב' ח"א כלל כ"ז או' ד' וכ"נ דעת החיד"א בקש"ג סי' כ"ב או' י"א, וסברה זו הביאה ר"ז שם, וכתב דיש לחוש לדבריהם לחדש איזה דבר כשחוזר ומתפלל שאז אין חשש ברכה לבטלה לד"ה כמ"ש בסי' ק"ז סעי' א' וכ"כ הפרמ"ג בא"א או' ב' מיהו אם התפללל סתם ולא כיון אפי' בדעתו איזה לחובה ואיזה לתשלומין כגון שאינו יודע רק שיתפללו תשלומין אבל אינו יודע איזה צריך לחובה ואיזה צריך לתשלומין נראה דאין צריך לחזור דמסתמא דעתו לעשות כפי הדין וכיון שלפי הדין הראשונה צריכה להיות לחובה אמרינן כך נתקבלו לפני אבינו שבשמים, ומה שנסתפק שם הפרמ"ג בספק התפלל שחרית ועבר זמנה אם משלימה במנחה כבר כתבנו ע"ז לעיל סי' ק"ז או' ב' קחנו משם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) There: If he reversed them, he did not fulfill his obligation, etc.</b> This means that he intended the first prayer as compensation; he did not fulfill his obligation. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of compensation after Maariv, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Mishpatim, paragraph 9; Nahar Shalom, paragraph 2. Some say, however, that he need not repeat the prayer unless he explicitly revealed in his prayer that the second was the present obligation and the first compensation; for example, on Motzaei Shabbos, if he recited Havdalah in the second but not the first. If he merely reversed them in thought, he fulfilled his obligation. Taz, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Soles Belulah, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, rule 27, paragraph 4. Chida in Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 11, appears to agree. Shulchan Aruch HaRav cites this view and writes that one should take account of it by introducing a novel request when repeating the prayer. All opinions then agree there is no concern of a blessing in vain, as stated in siman 107, se'if 1. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, writes likewise. If he prayed without any intention which prayer was obligatory and which compensatory; for example, he knew only that a compensation must be prayed but not which comes first; it appears that he need not repeat. His presumed intent is to follow the law, and since the law assigns the first prayer to the present obligation, we say that this is how the prayers were accepted before our Father in Heaven. Pri Megadim remains uncertain whether one who is unsure if he prayed Shacharis may compensate after its time at Minchah. We already addressed this above in siman 107, paragraph 2; consult that discussion.

<b>5) [Se'if 2] If one erred and did not pray Maariv, he prays Shacharis twice, etc.</b> When the next day's shaliach tzibbur must pray twice, he need not pray three prayers. He prays two: the silent prayer for the day's obligation, and the aloud repetition as his compensation and to discharge one who is not proficient. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 174; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos on the Tur; Olas Tamid, paragraph 5; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Soles Belulah, paragraph 8; Halachah Berurah, paragraph 2; Pri Chadash, paragraph 2, who writes that the law is clear; Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 14, who writes that this is the principal ruling, unlike the glossator to Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 171b, who questions it; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 9; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6; Chayei Adam there, paragraph 3; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 21, paragraph 9. According to Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 1 concerning the repetition of the Amidah, the repetition is a distinct spiritual aspect and is necessary independently, not merely to discharge one who is not proficient. The shaliach tzibbur must therefore intend in the repetition three things: his compensatory prayer, discharging one who is not proficient, and the spiritual aspect produced by the repetition.

<b>6) If one did not pray Maariv, prayed Shacharis, and intended during the shaliach tzibbur's repetition that it serve as compensation, he fulfilled his obligation.</b> Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, and Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 15; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 10; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 2; Kaf HaChaim, siman 15, paragraph 56. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, finds this difficult: the listener and speaker must both intend, while the shaliach tzibbur certainly does not intend during the repetition to discharge his compensation. Moreover, a person cannot silently follow the shaliach tzibbur, as Tur writes in siman 59 in the name of the Rosh. If, however, the shaliach tzibbur intended to discharge his compensatory prayer and he silently followed the entire prayer, perhaps even a proficient person fulfills it after the fact. The shaliach tzibbur must therefore intend during the repetition to discharge this person's compensation, to discharge one who is not proficient, and for the spiritual aspect created by the repetition, as stated above. The listener should likewise intend compensation, fulfilling the alternative opinion, and the aspect created by the repetition. This applies only when the shaliach tzibbur prays calmly, word by word, and aloud; otherwise the listener cannot follow him, as stated below in siman 124, paragraph 56.

<b>7) Know that even ten people who did not pray Shacharis or Maariv pray twice, and the repetition suffices for them.</b> It appears this is the correct text; the printed reading "and it does not suffice" is a scribal error. The passage returns to Magen Avraham's ruling in the name of Radbaz that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills his compensation through the repetition, and teaches that the same applies not only to a shaliach tzibbur but to ten people. This is the evident flow of the language. If ten people did not pray Minchah and pray Maariv twice, however, the shaliach tzibbur certainly does not repeat Maariv for the missed Minchah. The repetition was instituted only in the prayer's own time; no repetition of a compensatory prayer was instituted. This is obvious. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. They must have the intentions stated in the preceding paragraph.

<b>11) Rema MiPano writes</b> in Alfasi Zuta at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar: Rabbi Yochanan says that one who erred and did not pray Maariv should pray Shacharis twice; it appears this is only through four hours, no later. One who missed Shacharis prays Minchah twice, and likewise only until plag haminchah, no later. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2, cites him and writes that the unqualified language of the poskim does not imply this, nor is common practice so. Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 169b; Zechor LeAvraham, volume 1, paragraph 400; and Ruach Chaim, paragraph 2, cite this. Alfasi Zuta means that one who missed Maariv can compensate after Shacharis only if he prays Shacharis within its preferred fixed time, through four hours, as stated in siman 89, se'if 1. If he prays Shacharis after four hours, he cannot then compensate Maariv. Likewise, one who missed Shacharis can compensate after Minchah only if he prays Minchah before plag haminchah, as Zechor LeAvraham explains. Machazik Berachah rejects this because the unqualified words of the poskim do not imply it. Rather, whenever the current prayer may be prayed, the preceding prayer may also be compensated after it; even Shacharis after four hours and Minchah after plag haminchah.

<b>12) Mussaf has no compensation;</b> one does not compensate it, and no other prayer compensates for it. The same applies to Ne'ilah. Neither prayer interrupts the sequence for compensation: the missed prayer may still be compensated from Shacharis to Minchah or from Minchah to Maariv. Rema MiPano there; Machazik Berachah, paragraph 3; Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 6; Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 169b; Zechor LeAvraham, paragraph 400. See the following paragraph.
