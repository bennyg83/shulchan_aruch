See siman 93, Magen Avraham seif katan 5, and the Eliyah Rabbah there, seif katan 4, in the name of the Nekudos HaKesef, like the Perishah. <b>And if.</b> See the Taz, who questioned the Beit Yosef for learning this from seif 10, for it is not comparable: there he made an actual change, unlike here. See the Rambam, chapter 3, halacha 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, who did not mention that if he reversed them he has not fulfilled; and in chapter 10 the Taz wrote the law of reversal, see there. See the Pri Chadash, who wrote that nevertheless the halacha is that here too he has not fulfilled. Rabbi Shmuel Narmizan wrote: if one did not pray Minchah and prays two Ma'ariv prayers, and did not mention Tal U'Matar in the first, he goes back and prays two, for otherwise he will have put the makeup first. The Pri Chadash disagrees and says that he repeats only one. According to the Levush here, the reason is that the frequent precedes and that this invalidates after the fact; if so, it invalidates here as well. But the Taz below, letter 10, wrote that frequency does not invalidate after the fact; see siman 684, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. If so, one may say that if he reversed them he has not fulfilled because he violated the enactment of Chazal that the obligation of the present time must be put first, which is not the case here. Still, one may say that even if he reversed them inadvertently he has not fulfilled, and this is also implied by the plain words of the poskim. If so, the same applies here. See Magen Avraham, letter 2.

<b>יאמר</b> אשרי עט״ז בסמ״ק מביאו ב״י בשחרית שתים יאמר אשרי קודם תפלה שנייה וכן בערבית שתים יאמר אשרי קודם תפלה שנייה והשמיט במנחה שתים דיאמר אשרי קודם שנייה וסוברים הפרישה וב״ח כה״ג לא יאמר אשרי בשנייה (וכ״נ דעת המחבר דשינה סדר הטור דכתב טעה ולא התפלל שחרית או לא התפלל ערבית וכן לא התפלל מנחה דהוי לא זו אף זו כמ״ש הב״ח והמחבר הקדים טעה מנחה ואח״כ טעה מעריב אלא להסמיך אמירת אשרי לשחרית אמר כן וכ״ש טעה מנחה שיאמר אשרי בערבית שייך למנחה והר״ב שכתב וכן כו׳ שלא תטעה לומר בשחרית יש אשרי אומ׳ אותו קודם ח״י שנייה משא״כ ערבית) והט״ז חולק עליהם וסובר טעה שחרית אומר במנחה ג״כ אשרי קודם ח״י שנייה והנה בטעם אשרי יש ג׳ טעמים טעם הב״ח וט״ז כדי לעמוד מתוך ד״ת ויש עוד טעם כדי להפסיק בין תפלה לתפלה (אע״ג דדי בד״א כבסי׳ ק״ה מ״מ לכתחלה יאמר אשרי) עד״מ כאן וב״ח בזה הפעם הג׳ שיהא ניכר שהוא תפלת מנחה כ״כ הב״י ברל״ד בשם הר״י ב״ש ולפ״ז טעה ערבית יאמר שחרית אשרי דיש בלא״ה אשרי קודם למנצח יאמר אותו קודם ח״י שנייה אע״פ שאין שייך היכר וטעה מנחה יאמר אשרי בערבית משום היכר וטעה בשחרית משמע דאין אומר במנחה פ״ש אשרי כב״ח ופרישה. והנה הט״ז סובר דטעה שחרית יאמר במנחה ב״פ אשרי ומצריך צריכות לדברי סמ״ק שבהכרח נקיט תרתי מעריב ומנחה ודי בכך. דאי אמר רק מעריב ה״א בשחרית אומר בלא״ה אשרי יאמר אותו בין תפלה שנייה אבל טעה במנחה א״א אשרי במעריב ואי אמר מנחה אומר במעריב אשרי ה״א ה״ה בשחרית די בחד אשרי ומיתורא שמעינן דצריך בשחרית ב״פ אשרי א׳ בין תפלה שנייה וא׳ אח״כ קודם למנצח ואי הוי נקיט רק טעה בשחרית יאמר במנחה ב״פ אשרי ה״א דאשרי שנייה לתשלומין. כל האומר תהלה ג״פ בכל יום כו׳ ואף אי הוי נקיט טעה בשחרית ובמנחה לא הוי ידעינן דטעה במעריב אומר בשחרית ב״פ אשרי ולכך נקיט סמ״ק מנחה ומעריב ודי בכך. ומכל הפוסקים משמע בשחרית אומר פ״א אשרי ועולה ללמנצח ג״כ ובפשיטות י״ל צריכותא דאי אמר מנחה אומר מעריב אשרי להשלמה של אשרי או שיהא ניכר משא״כ בשחרית וע׳ פרישה וב״י כאן ורל״ד ולענין תחנון עמ״א אות ד׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should say.</b> Ashrei. See the Taz. In the Semak, brought by the Beit Yosef, it says that if one prays two Shacharis prayers he should say Ashrei before the second prayer, and likewise if he prays two Ma'ariv prayers he should say Ashrei before the second prayer. It omits the case of two Minchah prayers, that he should say Ashrei before the second. The Perishah and Bach understand from this that in such a case he should not say Ashrei before the second prayer. This also appears to be the view of the Mechaber, who changed the order of the Tur. The Tur wrote: if he erred and did not pray Shacharis, or did not pray Ma'ariv, and likewise if he did not pray Minchah, which is a progression of not only this but even that, as the Bach wrote. The Mechaber, however, first wrote one who erred at Minchah and then one who erred at Ma'ariv, in order to attach the saying of Ashrei to Shacharis; and certainly one who erred at Minchah says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, for it relates to Minchah. The Rama wrote and likewise, etc., so that one not mistakenly say that at Shacharis, where there is Ashrei in any event, he should say it before the second Shemoneh Esrei, unlike Ma'ariv. The Taz disagrees with them and holds that one who erred at Shacharis also says Ashrei at Minchah before the second Shemoneh Esrei. There are three reasons for Ashrei: the reason of the Bach and Taz, that one should stand for prayer from words of Torah; another reason, to separate between one prayer and the next, and although the measure of four amos suffices, as in siman 105, nevertheless initially he should say Ashrei; see Darchei Moshe here and Bach. The third reason is that it should be recognizable that it is the Minchah prayer, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 234 in the name of the Rivash. According to this, if one erred at Ma'ariv, he says Ashrei at Shacharis, for there is already Ashrei before Lamnatzeach, and he should say it before the second Shemoneh Esrei even though recognition is not relevant; if he erred at Minchah, he says Ashrei at Ma'ariv because of recognition; if he erred at Shacharis, it implies that he does not say Ashrei twice at Minchah, like the Bach and Perishah. The Taz, however, holds that one who erred at Shacharis says Ashrei twice at Minchah, and he explains the need for the Semak's words, that it necessarily mentioned both Ma'ariv and Minchah, and that suffices. Had it said only Ma'ariv, one might think that at Shacharis, where he says Ashrei in any event, he should say it between the two prayers, but when he erred at Minchah, Ashrei is not possible at Ma'ariv. Had it said only Minchah, that he says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, one might think the same applies at Shacharis and that one Ashrei suffices; from the extra wording we learn that at Shacharis he needs two Ashreis, one between the two prayers and one afterward before Lamnatzeach. Had it mentioned only one who erred at Shacharis and says Ashrei twice at Minchah, one might say the second Ashrei is for makeup of whoever says Tehillah three times each day, etc.; and even had it mentioned one who erred at Shacharis and Minchah, we would not know that one who erred at Ma'ariv says Ashrei twice at Shacharis. Therefore the Semak mentioned Minchah and Ma'ariv, and that suffices. From all the poskim it implies that at Shacharis one says Ashrei once, and it counts also for Lamnatzeach. Simply, one can explain the need: had it said that one who erred at Minchah says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, that may be to make up Ashrei or so that it be recognizable, unlike Shacharis. See Perishah and Beit Yosef here and in siman 234. Regarding Tachanun, see Magen Avraham, letter 4.

<b>מ״ש</b> המחבר בס״ג הוא דברי הרשב״א הביאו הב״י כאן ובפ״ט אבל הט״ז שם ג׳ והפרישה ושאר אחרונים פירשו דוקא מיד אחר תפלת זמנה משלים אבל לא מופלג ממנה תו אין לה תשלומין ועא״ר אות ד׳ וסיים אם התפלל החובה והלך לעסקי׳ ורוצה להשלים התפלה ששכח יתפלל נדבה יע״ש ונכון הוא. וכללא הוא כל ספק פלוגתא דרבוותא יתפלל נדבה כבסעיף י״א והיינו שיתנה ויאמר אם חייב אני הרי חובה וא״ל נדבה כבסי׳ ק״ז במ״א אות א׳. ובתנאי שיהא ספק פלוגתא ממש לא יחיד נגד רבים ולא קטן נגד גדול דכה״ג שוה ספק פלוגתא לספק גוף המעשה ובשבת וי״ט אין מתפלל תפלת ספק דאיך יתנה דאין מביאין נדבה בשבת עסי׳ ק״ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>What.</b> The Mechaber wrote in seif 3 are the words of the Rashba brought by the Beit Yosef here and in siman 89. But the Taz there, letter 3, and the Perishah and other later authorities explained that one makes it up specifically immediately after the prayer of its time, but if it is distant from it, it no longer has makeup. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, who concludes that if one prayed the obligatory prayer and went about his business, and then wishes to make up the prayer he forgot, he should pray it as a voluntary prayer; see there, and this is correct. The rule is that in every doubtful dispute among major poskim, one prays as a voluntary prayer, as in seif 11, meaning that he stipulates and says: if I am obligated, this is an obligation, and if not, it is voluntary, as in siman 107, Magen Avraham letter 1. This is provided that it is a real doubtful dispute, not an individual against the many or a lesser authority against a greater one, for in such a case a doubtful dispute is equivalent to a doubt in the factual event itself. On Shabbos and Yom Tov one does not pray a doubtful prayer, for how can he stipulate, since voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbos; see siman 107.

<b>נסתפקתי</b> מי שלא התפלל ערבית ועבר ד׳ שעות מהיום ונזכר מיד שסיים שחרית סוף ד׳ שעות אי ישלים ערבית כיון דאי לא התפלל שחרית מתפלל עד חצות כבסי׳ פ״ט (ושם חיובא הוא דאיכא דצריך להתפלל עד חצות כמ״ש שם) א״כ עדיין לא עבר זמן ב׳ תפלות מיקרי ומשלימה לערבית קודם חצות. ופשיטנא לה מהדא שכתב הלבוש דאין משלים מנחה אחר כלות ערבית כל הלילה והיינו אחר חצות לילה כמ״ש הא״ר אות ד׳ א״כ כ״ש כאן דעבר זמן ב׳ תפלות מיקרי כה״ג ודברי א״ר קשים דבר״מ פ״ג מתפלה ו׳ יבואר ערבית זמנה לכתחלה כל הלילה והתי׳ הב׳ שכתב א״ר לאחר שעלה ע״ה זה נראה ואי״ה ברל״ה יבואר מזה ול״ד למ״ש הנ״ץ דטעה ולא התפלל שחרית והתפלל מוסף משלים שחרית במנחה דמוסף זמנה כל היום אע״ג דנקרא פושע אחר שבע כבסי׳ רפ״ו מ״מ זמנה כל היום משא״כ שחרית אחר ד״ש שכר תפלה בזמנה לא יהבי ליה כבסי׳ פ״ט ועמ״א כאן וי״ו ומזה נלמוד מכ״ש דמשלים מנחה אחר חצות דערבית כאמור נ״ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>I was uncertain.</b> Regarding one who did not pray Ma'ariv, and four hours of the day passed, and he remembered immediately after completing Shacharis at the end of four hours: may he make up Ma'ariv? Since if he had not prayed Shacharis he may pray until midday, as in siman 89, and there it is an obligation, for there are those who say one must pray until midday, perhaps this is still not called that two prayer-times have passed, and he may make up Ma'ariv before midday. I resolved it from what the Levush wrote, that one does not make up Minchah after the time of Ma'ariv has ended for the whole night, meaning after midnight, as the Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, wrote. If so, certainly here it is called that two prayer-times have passed in such a case. The words of the Eliyah Rabbah are difficult, for in the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah, halacha 6, it is explained that the time of Ma'ariv is initially the whole night. His second answer, that it means after dawn rose, appears correct; with Hashem's help this will be explained in siman 235. This is not comparable to what the Nachalas Tzvi wrote, that if one erred and did not pray Shacharis but prayed Mussaf, he makes up Shacharis at Minchah, for Mussaf's time is the whole day. Although after seven hours he is called negligent, as in siman 286, nevertheless its time is the whole day. But Shacharis after four hours does not give him the reward of prayer in its time, as in siman 89. See the Magen Avraham here, letter 6. From this we learn all the more that he may make up Minchah after midnight during the time of Ma'ariv, as stated. So it seems to me.

<b>Answer.</b> If one erred with Minchah of Yom Kippur, he prays two weekday Ma'ariv prayers, as in seif 10. Do not say that two prayer-times have passed, for Minchah's time is the whole day. The proof is from Tosafot 26a, s.v. Miba'ya, who say that Mussaf has no makeup at night; why not simply say that two prayer-times, Mussaf and Minchah, have passed? Since they hold that makeup is only during the time of the adjacent prayer, necessarily, because its time is all day, two times have not passed. See the Beit Yosef, who wrote that although the Rashba holds that there is makeup for two prayers, etc. See there. This is his meaning: the Rashba also wrote that there is no korban at night, as cited by the Taz, letter 4. On this it is difficult: according to the Rashba, night is not a sufficient reason, for let him wait until Shacharis. To this he answered that nevertheless they gave the reason of night, for whenever it is possible immediately it can also be done later; but when it is impossible immediately, since at night there is no korban, they did not institute it further. If so, since the time of Minchah is until dark, two prayer-times have not passed, for Minchah and Ne'ilah share one time. See siman 233, where some say Minchah is until plag, and Ne'ilah is when the sun is at the tops of the trees; if so, at first glance two times have passed. Nevertheless, after the fact he fulfills until night, and at Ma'ariv it is the adjacent prayer. Certainly if he forgot Ne'ilah, he makes it up at night with two weekday prayers. If he forgot both, one may also say that he makes them up at Ma'ariv, even though in seif 4 there is no makeup, for the main reason there is that two times have passed, whereas here two times have not passed. If he intentionally omitted Ne'ilah, he prays voluntarily if he wishes and need not introduce something new, but he should not stipulate that it be obligatory and be obligated to do so. Although Rav and Rabbi Yochanan dispute this, see siman 623; the halacha is that Ne'ilah is only by day, and if so he is exempt, and it is only a voluntary permission. If he forgot Minchah and Ne'ilah, it is reasonable that he should pray the obligatory prayer, then Ne'ilah, and then Minchah, in order, as in seif 4. Perhaps, since the time of both is the same, the frequent one comes first; nevertheless Ne'ilah is later for us. See siman 233.
