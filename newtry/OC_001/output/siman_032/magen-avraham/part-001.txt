<b>מעשן עצים</b>. לא ראיתי לא' מהגדולים שנהג כן, וכן בי"ד כתב שלכתחלה יעשה דיו מדבר הבא מן העץ משמע דלא קפדי' על עשן, והרמב"ם כתב מצוה לכתוב מדיו שנמחק, ובתשו' מהרי"ל ד"ה סי' קכ"ז כתב שדיו שלנו כמה רבוותא אכשרו לה ותמצא בהג"מ אמנם הואיל ור"ת פוסל עפצי' ורש"י פוסל אדרמינ"ט ויתר רבותינו פסלו גלוטשטיי"ן נכון להחמיר לצאת ידי כולן ולכתוב בשטופטינ"ט שהוא של קוצים או בדיו של עשבים כמ"ש המיימוני עכ"ל וע' פ"ב בשבת דמשמע שהיו עושין דיו מעשן שמני':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From the smoke of wood.</b> I have not seen any of the great authorities who practiced this. Also, in Yoreh Deah he wrote that initially one should make ink from something that comes from a tree, which implies that they were not particular about smoke. The Rambam wrote that it is a mitzvah to write with ink that can be erased. In Responsa Maharil, Dapim Heh, siman 127, he wrote that many great authorities validated our ink, and you will find this in Hagahos Maimoniyos. Nevertheless, since Rabbeinu Tam invalidates gallnut ink, Rashi invalidates adramint, and our other rabbis invalidated glotshteyn, it is proper to be stringent in order to satisfy all opinions and to write with shtoftint, which is made from thorns, or with herbal ink, as the Maimonides wrote, end quote. See chapter 2 of Shabbos, where it implies that they made ink from the smoke of oils.

<b>שלא תדבק</b>. אם האות גדול ונדבק בסופו באופן שאם נגרר מה שדבוק מ"מ ישאר צורת אות כשר (רד"ך בא"ח ל"ב) ואם אינו דבוק בעצמו רק התג א' דבוקה בחברתה כשר (הרב המאירי) וכתב הרלב"ח שנראה שהרמב"ם חולק ועיין בב"י בד"ה וא"ת והיכי מכשרי וכו' (כ"ז בכ"ה) ונ"ל דגם בדין הראשון יש להחמיר עסי' ל"ו עסי"ח ובב"י שם דלא כע"ת ועמ"ש ס"ק ל"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that it not stick.</b> If the letter is large and it became attached at its end in such a way that, even if the attached part were scraped away, the form of a valid letter would still remain, it is valid; Radach in Orach Chaim 32. If it is not the letter itself that became attached, but only one crown that became attached to another, it is valid; Rav HaMeiri. Ralbach wrote that it appears the Rambam disagrees. See Beis Yosef, s.v. Ve'im tomar veheichi machsherei, etc.; all this is in Knesses HaGedolah. It seems to me that even in the first ruling one should be stringent; see siman 36, se'if 18, and Beis Yosef there, unlike Olas Tamid. See what I wrote in s.k. 35.

<b>שטה עליונה</b>. משום דאסור לכתוב פסוק בלא שרטוט וכשמשרטט שטה עליונה דיו, והקשה בס' ברכת הזבח במנחות דף ל"ב א"כ למה שירטט מר זוטרא לכולהו פרשתא כדאיתא ספי"ב דיבמות עכ"ל, ול"נ שהטור והרא"ש נזהרו מזה שכתבו שסתם בני אדם יודעים לאמן ידם אחר שכתבו שטה עליונה ומר זוטרא לא היה בקי לאמן ידיו אחר שטה עליונ' לכן שירטט הכל ועוד דלישנ' דתלמוד' ה"פ מר זוטרא משרטט וכ' לכולה פ' י"ל שלא שרטט רק שטה עליונה וכ' לכל הפ' לאפוקי ממ"ד דלא כתבי' כולה: ועוד הק' על מ"ש הרא"ש ביבמות שם הא דכ' אביי בגט חליצה ואקרינוהו לדידי' מן לא עד לקחתה לא ידענא למה דהא ק"ל ג' כותבין עכ"ל, והקשה דהא הרא"ש גופיה כ' במגילה ב' כותבין בלא שירטוט ג' אין כותבי' וכו' ע"ש, ול"נ דהרא"ש ס"ל דהא דלא כתב אביי הכל דס"ל כמר בר רב אשי דלא ניתן לכתוב אפילו בשירטוט ע"ש בגמרא ולזה הקשה הרא"ש דהא ק"ל ג' תיבות כותבין בשירטוט עכ"פ וא"כ למה לא כתב אביי לא חפצתי לקחת':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The upper line.</b> This is because it is forbidden to write a verse without scoring, and when he scores the upper line that is sufficient. The author of Birkas HaZevach asked in Menachos, folio 32: if so, why did Mar Zutra score all of the passage, as stated at the end of chapter 12 of Yevamos? End quote. It seems to me that the Tur and the Rosh guarded against this, for they wrote that ordinary people know how to train their hand after writing the upper line, whereas Mar Zutra was not expert in training his hand after the upper line, and therefore he scored it all. Furthermore, the language of the Talmud can be explained as follows: Mar Zutra would score and write the whole passage. One may say that he scored only the upper line and then wrote the whole passage, to exclude the view that we do not write the whole passage. He further challenged what the Rosh wrote there in Yevamos regarding what Abaye wrote in the chalitzah document, 'And we read to him from lo until lekachtah; I do not know why, for we maintain that three words may be written,' end quote. He asked that the Rosh himself wrote in Megillah that two words may be written without scoring, but three may not be written, etc.; see there. It seems to me that the Rosh holds that Abaye did not write the entire text because he holds like Mar bar Rav Ashi, that it may not be written even with scoring; see the Gemara there. On this the Rosh asked, for we maintain that three words may at least be written with scoring, and if so why did Abaye not write 'lo chafatzti lekachtah'?

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he does not know, etc.</b> The Yerushalmi said that one who is exempt from something yet does it is called a commoner only when he does so as a stringency. Here, however, he does not intend that, since this is not a matter of prohibition to which stringency applies; he scores only in order to write more straightly, and he is not a commoner; Beis Yosef. See siman 639, se'if 7. In truth this ruling requires consideration, for we find many stringencies that we accept upon ourselves and we do not say that one is called a commoner. The book Beis Shmuel also wondered about this. See Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 8 of Chullin, regarding eating meat after cheese, where he wrote in the name of Maharam that it appears like disagreeing with the Talmud unless one does it as a fence and separation. See what I wrote in siman 63, se'if 2, and siman 232.

<b>או לשם ס"ת</b>. ז"ל ב"י אם עבד לשם ס"ת מהני לכתוב בו תפילין דקדושת ס"ת חמירא מקדוש' תפילין עכ"ל, וכתב הלבוש אף על גב דאסור לשנות מקדושה חמורה לקדושה קלה כמ"ש סי'  מ"ב הכא שאני שאינו אלא הזמנה ומותר לשנותו לקדושה קלה אבל לדבר חול אסור לשנותו עכ"ל וכ"מ רסי' מ"ב שכתב מותר לעשות מתפילין חדשים של ראש לש"י אף על גב שנכתבו לשם  ש"ר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or for the sake of a Sefer Torah.</b> This is the language of Beis Yosef: If he processed it for the sake of a Sefer Torah, it is effective for writing tefillin on it, because the sanctity of a Sefer Torah is more severe than the sanctity of tefillin, end quote. The Levush wrote: although it is forbidden to change from a more severe sanctity to a lighter sanctity, as written in siman 42, here it is different, for it is only designation, and it is permitted to change it to a lighter sanctity. This is also implied at the beginning of siman 42, where he wrote that it is permitted to make new tefillin of the head into tefillin of the hand, even though they were written for the sake of the head.

**** ENGLISH ****

Regarding what the Olas Tamid wrote, that there is no need for the rule of fixed, because most animals are non-kosher, one can answer that, in any event, most animals that are eaten are kosher, and from those that are not eaten one does not take sinews, for a non-Jew certainly does not slaughter his animal merely for the sinews; carcasses and tereifos are a minority. It seems to me that this applies specifically when it is known that some make them from non-kosher animals, in which case it is fixed; but ordinarily we follow the majority, as written in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 86, that one may buy eggs from a non-Jew and need not be concerned that they came from a carcass or tereifah, and this is an explicit Talmudic ruling in Chullin. It seems to me there is another reason why it is forbidden to buy from a non-Jew: since these are a halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai, they require spinning for the sake of the mitzvah, as written in siman 33, seif 4, and non-Jews are not fit for acting for the sake of the mitzvah.
