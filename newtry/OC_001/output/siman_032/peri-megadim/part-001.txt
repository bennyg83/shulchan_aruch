<b>יכתוב</b> עט״ז. והוא בב״י גבי אם חיסר אין תקנה בשם הר״י אכסנדרי אבל הב״ח כתב בריש סי׳ דמטור משמע דש״ר קדושתו חמורה יכתוב קודם והפרישה כתב מ״ש הטור כ״א בקלף לבדה אש״י קאי ולבדה שלא יערבב אלא יכתוב כל פרשה בעמוד א׳ וא״כ אין חולק על הרי״א ועיין עט״ז הם דברי הב״ח. ובא״ר כ׳ שמהר״י לוריא נהג ש״ר קודם ואנו פוסקין כמ״ש הר״ב בהג״ה ודיעבד אין פסול אע״ג דשלא כסדרן דיעבד פסול אפי׳ אות א׳ כמבואר בסעיף כ״ג והיו בהוייתן יהא אבל ש״י קודם לש״ר לאו מוהיו נפיק אלא מדהקדים קרא (ומניחין כן) ש״י קודם א״כ כשר דיעבד ע״ץ אות ג׳: אם נפסל פ׳ קדש נפסלו ג׳ אחרים ואם יש לו פרשת קדש מתפילין אחרים ויודע שנכתב קודם אלו יצרפם ומספק פסול א״ר אות א׳ עה״ג ס׳ והכין ודאי ספק תורה לחומרא וכמדומה בבאר היטב לא כתב כן. וש״י וש״ר מספק ודאי אין קפידא כי דיעבד כשר ש״ר קודם עמ״ש באות ב׳ מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should write.</b> See Taz. This is in the Beit Yosef regarding the rule that if he omitted something there is no remedy, in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. However, the Bach wrote at the beginning of the siman that from the Tur it implies that the head tefillin, whose sanctity is more severe, should be written first. The Perishah wrote that the Tur’s statement that each one is on its own parchment refers to the hand tefillin, and “on its own” means that he should not mix them, but should write each parsha in one column. If so, no one disagrees with Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. See the Taz, whose words follow the Bach. The Eliyah Rabbah writes that Rabbi Yitzchak Luria would write the head tefillin first, but we rule as the Rav writes in the gloss; after the fact there is no invalidation. Although writing out of order invalidates even after the fact, even for one letter, as explained in seif 23, because “they shall be” teaches that they must remain as written, nevertheless the precedence of hand tefillin before head tefillin is not derived from “they shall be,” but from the verse placing it first, and we therefore put them on in that order. Accordingly, after the fact it is valid; see Atzei Shittim, letter 3. If the parsha of Kadesh became invalid, the other three became invalid as well. If he has a parsha of Kadesh from other tefillin and knows that it was written before these, he may combine it with them; in a case of doubt it is invalid, as in Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1, and Beer Hagolah. This is correct, for a doubt in a Torah matter is ruled stringently, and it seems that the Baer Heitev did not write this way. Regarding doubt between hand tefillin and head tefillin, however, there is certainly no concern, for after the fact it is valid if the head tefillin were written first, as I wrote in letter 2 concerning this.

<b>כולם</b> עט״ז עי׳ לקמן סעיף ל״ח ובט״ז ל״ב וסעיף מ״ז והעיקר בש״י אות א׳ בית א׳ אפי׳ דיעבד מעכב וא״י אם הניח בד׳ בתים (עסי׳ מ״ב) אבל הפרשיות מצוה לכתחלה להיותן בקלף א׳ כמו שאות א׳ בחוץ כן בפנים ודיעבד כשר והמנהג לדבקן כבסעיף מ״ז ובש״ר צריך ד׳ בתים לטטפת טט כו׳ ויבואר אי״ה לקמן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All of them.</b> See Taz. See below, seif 38, and Taz 32 and seif 47. The essential rule for hand tefillin is that one letter, one compartment, even after the fact, is indispensable. I do not know whether, if he placed them in four compartments, this is valid; see siman 42. Regarding the parshiyos, the mitzvah initially is that they be on one parchment, just as it is one letter on the outside so it is one unit within. After the fact it is valid, and the practice is to paste them together as in seif 47. For head tefillin, four compartments are required because of the word “totafos,” and this will be explained below with the help of Heaven.

<b>ומ״ש</b> הש״ע בס״ג אם זרק זהב מעביר ואע״ג כ״ז דלא מעביר פסול לא הוי שלא כסדרן כיון שאין כותב כ״א מעביר ונשאר תחתון ממילא. ובא״ר ה׳ כתב בדיו וזרק זהב וחיפה דיו עליו אפ״ה פסול דאל״כ יש תקנה לאזכרות יע״ש וי״ל כשמעביר דיו על מקצת אות ועדיין לא השלימה נמי הוי כמוחק. וממ״ש הנ״י הביאו הב״י זרק זהב על אזכרות ע״ג דיו וכב״י דמש״ה נקיט אזכרות א״ל מנא ליה ואזכרות נקיט דשאר אותיות מעביר דיו וי״ל דהנ״י לאו מכח קושיא כתב כן די״ל כתב אס״ת קאי כפירש״י בשבת ק״ג ב׳ ד״ה כתבה כשירה לס״ת ושם שאר אותיות תיקן לגרור ולכתוב ובאזכרות אסור ועב״ח כאן. עי׳ לבוש י״ד רע״ז וס״ה ובש׳ך ו׳ וכבר יש לתרץ דחיסור לשון יש שם דשאר אותיות מהני גרירה. גם מ״ש שם דאם גריר וכותב בדיו היינו בכתב זהב משא״כ זר״ק זהב מעביר ונשאר הדיו כמ״ש כאן. שוב ראיתי בא״ר כאן אות ו׳ ז׳ הרגיש בזה יע״ש ולפ״ז בתפילין זר״ק יש תיקון לא כתב והנה מ״ש כאן כמוחק השם דמוחק ממש לא הוי דנשאר כתב תחתון ומשמע מכאן אפי׳ זרק זהב שלא בקדושת השם אסור למחוק דלא כש״ך י״ד רע״ו י״ב יע״ש וצ״ע. עמ״ש באות ט״ו מזה ובמ״א אות ל״ח. עוד אשוב אל מ״ש באות א׳ להצטרף פרשה מתפילין אחרים בספק הט״ז בי״ד רפ״א א׳ ור״ץ א׳ בשם התוס׳ מנחות ל״ב ד״ה דלמא דמכשרי שלא כסדרן פרשיות שאין סמוכין בתורה ולדידהו קדש אחר והיה כי יביאך פסול סמוכות אבל אחר שמע והיה אם שמוע אין סמוכות וכשר וכן שמע אחר והיה אם שמוע כשר כמו קראה למפרע בק״ש (לקמן סי׳ ס״ד) לפ״ז הפרש יש קדש והיה כי יביאך פסול בספק אבל שמע והיה אם שמוע הוי ס״ס שמא נכתבו כסדר ושמא הלכה כתוס׳ דאין סמוכות וכשר אלא שמאחר דבש״ע כאן וי״ד רפ״ח לא זכר כלל דעת התוס׳ פשיטא שאין לחשוב ס״ס כה״ג וכמ״ש בי״ד בהנהגות או״ה יע״ש ובסי׳ ס״ד כתבנו מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And what is written.</b> The Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 3 that if he cast gold over it, he may pass ink over it. Although so long as he has not passed ink over it, it is invalid, this is not considered out of order, since he is not writing but only passing over it, and the lower writing remains automatically. In Eliyah Rabbah 5 he wrote that if one wrote with ink, cast gold over it, and then covered it with ink, it is nevertheless invalid, for otherwise there would be a remedy for Divine Names; see there. One can say that when he passes ink over part of a letter and has not yet completed it, this too is like erasing. What the Nimukei Yosef, cited by the Beit Yosef, wrote regarding casting gold over Divine Names that were written over ink, and the Beit Yosef wrote that this is why he specifically mentioned Divine Names, should not be challenged by asking how he knew this, for he mentioned Divine Names because with other letters one may pass ink over them. One can answer that the Nimukei Yosef did not write this because of the difficulty, for it can be said that the statement refers to a Torah scroll, as Rashi explains in Shabbos 103b, s.v. “if he wrote it, it is valid,” and there other letters can be corrected by scraping and rewriting, whereas for Divine Names this is forbidden. See the Bach here. See Levush, Yoreh Deah 277:5, and the Shach there, seif katan 6. It can already be resolved that there is an omission in the wording there, for with other letters scraping helps. Also, what is written there that if he scraped and wrote in ink, this refers to writing in gold, unlike where he cast gold over it and passed ink over it, where the ink remains, as stated here. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah here, letters 6 and 7, sensed this; see there. According to this, in tefillin, if he cast gold there is a remedy, not writing. Now, what is written here that it is like erasing the Divine Name, it is not actual erasure, since the lower writing remains. It implies from here that even if he cast gold not with the sanctity of the Divine Name, it is forbidden to erase, unlike the Shach.

<b>כתיבה</b> עט״ז. בטור כתב כתיבה תמה שיהא כל א׳ מוקף גויל ומתויג כראוי ושלא יחסר קוצו של יו״ד הכל בכלל כתיבה תמה הוי עב״ח ומ״ש על הב״ח דודאי כל שאין כותב כי אם גורר לבד לא מקרי שלא כסדרן ואפי׳ פסול כך מ״מ לא הוי שלא כסדרן וכמ״ש באות ב׳ זרק זהב מהני לגרור הזהב וענין קוצו של יו״ד עסי׳ ל״ו בטור ולבוש וצורתה כזה. [] דהיינו רגל ימין קצת עקום ומשמאל על פניה תג למעלה בפניה ממש (לא באמצע) ועוקץ קטן יורד למטה קצר מתג לבוש וא״צ לעקם קוצו השמאלי כדמוכח בב״י ל״ו ושמה״ט יעקם ימין ושמאל קצר שלא תדמה לחי״ת וכפוף היינו התג יורד למטה עוקץ כו׳ ומשמע בב״י ל״ו אף לרש״י פסול בלא עוקץ שמאלי. ובט״ז ל״ו מכשיר לתקן אח״כ ולא הוי שלא כסדרן דלא כלבוש דמחמיר. וא״ר בל״ו אות כ״ג הסכים לתקן אח״כ וכן שעטנ״ז ג״ץ יכול לתקן אחר כך מ״א ל״ו ג׳. והטעם כל שאותיות ניכרות תו לא הוי שלא כסדרן אם כן אני תמה על עצמי אמאי לא יתקנו כל התפילין היודי״ן בעוקצין שמאלית וראוי להזהר בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Writing.</b> See Taz. In the Tur it is written that perfect writing means that each letter must be surrounded by parchment, properly crowned, and not missing the point of a yud; all of this is included in perfect writing. See the Bach. Regarding what was written against the Bach, certainly whenever one does not write but only scrapes, it is not called out of order, and even if it is invalid in that state, it is still not out of order. As I wrote in letter 2, where gold was cast, scraping the gold helps. The matter of the point of a yud is discussed in siman 36 in the Tur and Levush, and its form is as follows: the right leg is somewhat bent, and on the left face there is a tag above, on the face itself, not in the middle, with a small point descending below, shorter than the tag, as in the Levush. It is not necessary to bend the left point, as is clear from Beit Yosef 36. For that reason he bends the right side and keeps the left short, so that it should not resemble a ches. “Bent” means that the tag descends downward as a point. It appears from Beit Yosef 36 that even according to Rashi it is invalid without the left point. In Taz 36 he validates correcting it afterward, and it is not considered out of order, unlike the Levush, who is stringent. Eliyah Rabbah in siman 36, letter 23, agrees that it may be corrected afterward, and similarly the letters that require crowns may be corrected afterward, as in Magen Avraham 36:3. The reason is that whenever the letters are recognizable, it is no longer considered out of order. If so, I wonder why all the yuds of the tefillin should not be corrected with left points; one should be careful about this.

מ״ש בסעיף זיי״ן הל״מ כו׳ הנה ד׳ שמות יש עו״ר דלא מעובד גויל מעובד ולא מגרר ממנו כלום רק השיעור נפל ממנו קלף ודוכסוסטוס ועט״ז י״ד רע״א ד׳. ושם מכשיר הר״ב דיעבד בקלף מקום שיער ועא״ר כאן י״ד דבתפילין פסול בקלפים שלנו ג״כ יע״ש ושאני ס״ת ושם רפ״ח ס״ו מזוזה לכתחלה על דוכסוסטוס וקלפים שלנו כשירים לכל ויש לעיין במזוזה אם כתב על קלף שלנו במקום שיער אי כשר כיש מקילין בס״ת רע״א שוב ראיתי בי״ד רע״א בב״י דמזוזה נמי כשר ליש מקילין כמו ס״ת דהנך כשירים בין גויל וקלף ודוכסוסטוס ולפ״ז ה״ה גויל במקום בשר כשר וכ״מ בלבוש י״ד רע״א ס״י יע״ש. ויש עוד שם חמישי דיפתרא דמעובד ולא בסיד בעפצים פסול לס״ת תפילין מזוזות. ועמ״ש באות ד׳ מדין עיבוד ושרטוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
What is written in seif 7, that it is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, etc. There are four names: hide, which is not processed; gevil, which is processed and nothing has been scraped from it, but only the required amount has fallen from it; parchment; and duchsustus. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 271:4. There the Rav validates, after the fact, parchment written on the hair side. See Eliyah Rabbah here, 14, that in tefillin it is invalid even with our parchments; see there. A Torah scroll is different. There, in siman 288:6, a mezuzah is initially written on duchsustus, and our parchments are valid for all. One must examine whether a mezuzah written on our parchment on the hair side is valid according to those who are lenient regarding a Torah scroll in 271. I later saw in Beit Yosef, Yoreh Deah 271, that a mezuzah is also valid according to those who are lenient, as with a Torah scroll, for these are valid whether on gevil, parchment, or duchsustus. According to this, gevil written on the flesh side is also valid, and so it appears from Levush, Yoreh Deah 271:10; see there. There is also a fifth type, diftera, which was processed but not with lime and gallnuts; it is invalid for Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos. See what I wrote in letter 4 regarding processing and scoring lines.

<b>או</b> עט״ז עיבוד ס״ת מהני לכל (ולא הוה הורדה דהזמנה לאו מילתא הוא ומותר עכ״פ מקדושה חמורה לקלה ולא בחול עסי׳ מ״ב ובמ״א כאן אות יו״ד) ותפילין מתני למזוזה ומזוזה רק למזוזה. והנה יש להסתפק אם עיבד קלף לעור לבית ש״ר דכתיב בו שי״ן דהוי ממש כקדושת תפילין עצמן אם מהני לכתוב פרשיות עליו עי׳ לקמן סעיף ל״ז די״א דא״צ עיבוד לשמה א״כ אף מעבדין לשם בית ש״ר כמאן דליתא דמי עב״י כאן דלמאן דאמר מזוזה א״צ עיבוד דכמאן דליתא דמי. ואם עיבדן לשם רצועות פסול לפרשיות דחמורה ולעור בתים י״ל נמי דפסול אם עשה אח״כ קלף מהן קדושתן חמורה מרצועה ואי״ה בסי׳ ל״ג ס״ג יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or.</b> See Taz. Processing for a Torah scroll is effective for all, and this is not considered lowering sanctity, for designation is not legally substantive, and in any case it is permitted from a more severe sanctity to a lighter one, though not to mundane use; see siman 42 and Magen Avraham here, letter 10. Processing for tefillin is effective for a mezuzah, and processing for a mezuzah is effective only for a mezuzah. Now, one may be uncertain: if he processed hide for the leather of the head tefillin compartment, upon which the shin is written, which has the actual sanctity of the tefillin themselves, does this help for writing the parshiyos upon it? See below, seif 37, where some say that processing for its required purpose is not needed. If so, even if it was processed for the head tefillin compartment, it is as though nothing was done. See Beit Yosef here, that according to the view that a mezuzah does not require processing, it is as though nothing was done. If he processed them for straps, they are invalid for parshiyos, for the parshiyos have greater sanctity. Regarding leather for compartments, one can also say that it is invalid if he later made parchment from it, since their sanctity is more severe than that of a strap. With the help of Heaven, this will be further explained in siman 33:3.

<b>מ״ש</b> הב״ח אהא דפריך בהקומץ תפילין שבלו אין עושין מזוזה דלמא במשרטט דלא מהני שיטוט דתפילין דא״צ לשרטט דמזוזה משמע שרטוט לשמה בעינן ובי״ד רע״א פסק הטור דא״צ שרטוט בס״ת לשמה הביאו הש״ך שם ב׳. וא״ל במזוזה לכ״ע בעינן שרטוט משא״כ בס״ת נחלקו בה כמבואר בי״ד רע״א ורפ״ח הא בב״י שם ברע״א דקיי״ל כרבא הזמנה לאו מילתא (היינו כה״ג שאין עושה מעשה חשוב כ״א שרטוט בעלמא וא״ש אף לפיר״ת דקיי״ל צריך עיבוד לשמה שזה מעשה חשוב מהני יותר מהזמנה משא״כ שרטוט הוי רק הזמנה וא״צ בה) וא״כ אף מזוזה א״צ שרטוט לשמה. נמצינו למדין ג׳ שרטוטין יש תפילין א״צ שרטוט ואם עבדו עור לס״ת ונמלך ושרטט לשם תפילין ואח״כ נמלך בס״ת כשר כי א״צ לשמה אבל שרטט אפי׳ לס״ת ואח״כ נמלך למזוזה להב״ח פסול דסבירא ליה מזוזה צריך שרטוט לשמה ולדידן כשר. ואפשר א״צ שרטוט לשמה היינו סתמא הא שלא לשמה גרע. ועמ״א אות יו״ד מ״ש על באר הגולה אות רי״ש אי״ה. ויש שרטוט ד׳ בגט באה״ע קכ״ה ב״ש אות יו״ד בשם רש״י מנחות דשרטוט בגט ד״ת ועי׳ לבוש י״ד רפ״ח בשרטוט תפילין הלמ״מ הוא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>What is written.</b> The Bach, on the Gemara’s question in HaKometz, “Worn tefillin are not made into a mezuzah,” asks why not answer that the case is where he scored lines, and the scoring for tefillin does not help because tefillin do not require scoring while a mezuzah does. This implies that scoring must be for its required purpose. But in Yoreh Deah 271 the Tur rules that a Torah scroll does not require scoring for its required purpose, as the Shach there, seif katan 2, cites. One should not say that for a mezuzah everyone agrees scoring must be for its required purpose, unlike a Torah scroll, over which there is a dispute, as explained in Yoreh Deah 271 and 288. For in the Beit Yosef there in 271 it is stated that we rule like Rava, that designation is not legally substantive, meaning in such a case, where he does not perform a significant act but only scoring, and this is sound even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that processing must be for its required purpose, since processing is a significant act and is stronger than designation, whereas scoring is only designation and does not require this. If so, even a mezuzah does not require scoring for its required purpose. We have learned that there are three types of scoring: tefillin do not require scoring; if one processed hide for a Torah scroll, then changed his mind and scored it for tefillin, and afterward changed his mind back to a Torah scroll, it is valid, because scoring does not require its own purpose. But if he scored even for a Torah scroll and afterward changed his mind to use it for a mezuzah, according to the Bach it is invalid, for he holds that a mezuzah requires scoring for its required purpose; according to us, it is valid. Perhaps “it does not require scoring for its required purpose” means when done neutrally, but if it was done explicitly not for its purpose, that is worse. See Magen Avraham, letter 10, regarding what is written about Beer Hagolah, letter reish, with the help of Heaven. There is a fourth scoring, in a get, Even HaEzer 125, Beit Shmuel letter 10 in the name of Rashi on Menachos, that scoring in a get is according to the Torah. See Levush, Yoreh Deah 288, regarding scoring tefillin.

<b>And some.</b> See Taz. See Tosafot, Menachos 32a, s.v. “and now,” who cite the arrangement of the early authorities, and what is written in the Yerushalmi. The general rule is that between one parsha and another there must be the measure of nine letters; less than this is invalid, whether open or closed. There are many disputes regarding the form of an open and closed parsha, and we will record only the dispute of the Rambam and the Rosh. According to both, there are two forms of an open parsha and three forms of a closed parsha. According to the Rambam, laws of Torah scroll 8:1, an open parsha must begin at the start of the line, and a closed parsha is always in the middle of a line; this is the distinguishing sign. Thus there are two forms of open parsha: one leaves blank space of nine letters at the end of the previous parsha and begins the next at the start of the next line, or he ends the previous parsha at the end of a line, leaves a blank line, meaning nine letters, and begins the open parsha at the start of the third line. The forms of a closed parsha are three: first, blank space of nine letters in the middle of a line, and he writes one word of that closed parsha on that line; second, if there is not enough room for nine letters and one word, he begins writing the closed parsha somewhat in the middle of a line; third, he leaves blank space of nine letters and begins in the middle of a line. He states them in a “not only this but even this” manner. According to the Rosh, there are two forms of an open parsha: one leaves blank space at the end, nine letters, and begins the open parsha at the start of the next line; second, he leaves nine letters and begins in the middle of a line. There are three closed forms: two like the Rambam’s illustrations, and the third, if he leaves an entire blank line, even if he begins afterward at the start of the third line, it is closed. If so, regarding hand tefillin, whereas in head tefillin see Magen Avraham 49, since one must write specifically in four columns and not two in one column, as in seif 47, how shall one act in order to satisfy both the Rambam and the Rosh? He concludes that one should make the second form of closed parsha, to which the Rosh agrees, for wherever there is not a blank space of nine letters in one place but only through combination, the Rosh agrees that it is closed. When the Rosh says that beginning in the middle of a line is open, that is where the blank space of nine letters precedes it in one place, not such a case through combination. What he wrote that according to the Rambam it is closed because there is not a full measure of blank space in one place is difficult, for according to the Rambam, whenever it does not begin at the head of the line, even if there is more than nine letters in one place, it is closed. Also, what he wrote according to the Rambam regarding a closed parsha when he left only nine letters of blank space and not enough to write one word, that since this is the measure of closure, therefore when he leaves a little blank on the second line it is through combination, I do not understand, for it seems that the Rambam’s reason is that whenever it does not begin at the start of a line it is closed. In any case, the practice that he wrote is correct, for now Kadesh is recognizable as being at the beginning with none before it, like Bereishis, though one who examines the Rambam sees that he did not count Bereishis as either open or closed; Ve-hayah ki yeviacha and Shema are recognizable from the nine-letter blank and begin at the head of the line; and at the end of Shema there is less than nine letters, and Ve-hayah im shamoa also has less than nine letters, so one satisfies everyone. Perhaps even according to the Rosh, although one leaves below a blank space of nine letters, as long as there is not at its beginning nine letters in one place, it is considered closed. Still, one should not do this, as the Taz wrote. At the end of Ve-hayah im shamoa one should not leave a blank space of nine letters, because some say this is the sign of an open parsha before it; see Maharam Padua, who elaborated on this. See Yoreh Deah 275 in Taz 5. This requires study.

What he wrote that we rule like Rav Dimi but like Abaye is apparently a textual error, and it should read “not like Abaye.” He said it according to the Bach’s method, that we rule like Rav Dimi, that initially it is forbidden but after the fact it is permitted, and not like Abaye, who is stringent even after the fact, and not like the Beit Yosef; see there. “He wrote them on one parchment” is speaking after the fact. According to this, what the Semag wrote, that the shin must extend until the seam, is also not indispensable but only initial practice, for this is the explanation of “some say” in Rav Dimi’s statement in Menachos 35a, as in Beer Hagolah, letter samech. If so, after the fact it is not indispensable. The Rav’s statement, “and likewise the yud must be,” is not so exact, for one is indispensable and the other only initial. Darkei Moshe answered in another way, and in Magen Avraham, letter 62, he explained his interpretation. I did not understand the Darkei Moshe before us, which the Derishah also cites, and I therefore did not want to elaborate on it.

One can say that their dispute is about placing the four parshiyos in one compartment: according to Rabbi Yehudah, after the fact one must paste them together, while according to Rabbi Yosei it is not necessary, even initially.
