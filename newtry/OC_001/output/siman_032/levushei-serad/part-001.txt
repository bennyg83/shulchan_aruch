<small>Shulchan Aruch, seif 15.</small> If after it was written. This is also the language of the Tur. See Taz, seif katan 14, who writes that the Tur does not accept the Beis Yosef's second answer, and that the Mechaber in this seif also follows the Tur's approach. If so, it requires study why they wrote here, 'if after it was written,' since there is no distinction, and according to the Bavli it is valid even if the hole existed first before the writing and then he filled the entire hollow of the hole. Both cases concern even where the hole existed before it was written. Afterward the Mechaber wrote, 'that which we validate,' etc., in accordance with the Beis Yosef's second answer, and some disagree whether it is inside the letter or outside it. In truth, his words contradict each other, as stated in Taz, seif katan 14.

On this he writes, 'and the reason appears,' etc. Meaning, the blank parchment adjacent to half the letter is considered as though it also surrounds the other half, since the whole is one part.

<small>(שם)</small> ע"כ דס"ל כתירוץ הראשון כלומר דס"ל להטור כתירוץ הראשון דאין סברא כלל לחלק בין נכתב בכשרות אי לא אלא דסביר' ליה לבבלי דלא קפדינן אלא בדיבוק אות לאות אבל נקב לית לן בה מש"ה שפיר עשה הטור מחלוק' דלבבלי אין פסול אלא בדיבוק אותיות אבל בנקב לא ואפילו היה הנקב קודם הכתיב' כיון דאין סברא כלל לחלק בין בכשרות או לא. ולהירושלמי דבעי מוקף גויל גם מתוכו פסול אף אחר שנכתב. והרב פרי מגדים שגג בכאן גם הרב נודע ביהוד' ח"א סימן ע"ה שגג בזה שהבין מ"ש הט"ז דס"ל התירוץ ראשון ר"ל דירושלמי ס"ל כתי' ראשון. ולכן תמה דהא לתירוץ ראשון אינו פסול אלא בדיבוק אות לאות ואין מקום לדין הירושלמי ע"ש ושגיא' הוא דהט"ז ס"ל כתי' ראשון של הב"י והיינו אליבא דבבלי דמכשיר בנפסק מש"ה עשה הטור מחלוקת משום דהירוש' אין מחלק בין דיבוק אותיות ובין ניקב מדמצריך היקף גויל גם בתוך האות וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> It must be that he holds like the first answer. Meaning, the Tur holds like the first answer, that there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not. Rather, according to the Bavli, we are particular only about one letter adhering to another, but a hole does not matter. Therefore the Tur properly formulated this as a dispute: according to the Bavli, the only invalidation is adhesion of letters, but not a hole, even if the hole existed before the writing, since there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not. According to the Yerushalmi, which requires surrounding blank parchment even within the letter, it is invalid even after it was written. The Pri Megadim erred here, and the Noda BiYehuda, first edition, siman 75, also erred, for he understood the Taz's statement that he holds like the first answer to mean that the Yerushalmi holds like the first answer. Therefore he wondered, for according to the first answer there is invalidation only in adhesion of one letter to another, and there is no room for the Yerushalmi's law; see there. This is a mistake. The Taz means that the Tur holds like the Beis Yosef's first answer, namely according to the Bavli, which validates when it was severed. Therefore the Tur made it a dispute, because the Yerushalmi does not distinguish between adhesion of letters and a hole, since it requires surrounding blank parchment even inside the letter. This is clear.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> They contradict each other. At first glance one could say that the Mechaber was concerned for the stringency of both answers. Perhaps the law follows the second answer, that one must distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not; therefore here he validates only when it was written in validity, and likewise in seif 15 he wrote, 'if after it was written,' meaning specifically after it was written. He was also concerned that perhaps the law follows the first answer, that there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not, but that the Bavli holds that surrounding blank parchment is needed only between one letter and another. Consequently, the Yerushalmi, which requires surrounding blank parchment inside the letter, invalidates even after it was written. Therefore he wrote there, in seif 15, to be concerned for the Yerushalmi where its entire hollow was pierced, even after it was written. Yet this is certainly against the Bavli, for according to both answers, even a hole on the outside is valid after it was written. How much more so inside the letter, which is more lenient, as explained in the responsum of Maharlanch cited in the Beis Yosef. If so, he should not have been concerned for the Yerushalmi at all in this matter. This is clear. The Noda BiYehuda, siman 5, answers this difficulty of the Taz by saying that according to the Beis Yosef's first answer, a hole inside the letter is more severe than a hole outside it; see there. In my humble opinion he erred, and the responsum of Maharlanch in the Beis Yosef escaped him. There Maharlanch refutes the Beis Yosef's first answer from the Yerushalmi, which invalidates a hole inside the letter, and all the more so outside it; see there. Also, his underlying reasoning there, that a hole between letters is comparable to one letter's touching another, is refuted by the end of Maharlanch's words; see there. Thus all the words of the Taz are correct. One who examines the Beis Yosef will see that for this reason the Shulchan Aruch wrote the second answer here, for in the Beis Yosef, after writing the two answers, he brought the words of Maharlanch, who refuted the first answer from the Yerushalmi and proved the second answer. According to that, the Yerushalmi, which invalidates a hole inside the letter, is speaking specifically before writing. Therefore the Taz wondered about seif 15, where he wrote that the Yerushalmi invalidates even after it was written. There is no need to elaborate further here; examine it carefully.

<small>There.</small> It emerges that the first three passages, according to the Rambam. From what he writes here, 'according to the Rambam,' and from what he writes afterward, that it is like Bereishis, which is open according to the Rambam, and afterward, regarding the two following passages, he writes, 'and this is so both according to the Rosh,' etc., it implies that the first passage is open only according to the Rambam. This law is not found in the Rosh, but nevertheless we can say that the Rosh also agrees. Therefore we begin at the start of a line and satisfy both opinions, whereas if we began in the middle of a line we would not satisfy the Rambam.
