<b> בסדר הזה</b> -  עיין במ"ב והוא מהא"ר והפמ"ג ושארי אחרונים ודעת הט"ז ביו"ד סימן ר"ץ דלדברי התוספות מנחות ל"ב ע"א ד"ה דילמא כשר אם אין סידור בהפרשיות של שמע והא"ש אחרי שאין סמוכות בתורה ועיין שם בנה"כ שחולק עליו ועוד ראיתי בס' קדושת יו"ט שכ' ג"כ דלא כט"ז ואין חילוק בענין סידור בין הפרשיות והאותיות וכ' דהתוספות ס"ל דכונת המכילתא הוא לענין סידור המקום שלא יהא נסדרת בהקלף והיה קודם לקדש אבל אם נסדרת אחר קדש אפי' אם היא נכתבת תחילה כשר ואנו להלכה לא ס"ל להקל אפילו באופן זה כיון דנכתבת שלא כסדרה וכדמוכח לקמן בסכ"ג וביו"ד בסימן רפ"ח גם מדברי הגר"א בכל אלו מקומות מוכח דס"ל כפסק השו"ע ולא כהתוספות ועיין לקמן בסימן ס"ד בבה"ל שתירצנו בעזה"י את קושית הט"ז בטוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In this order.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah. This is from the Eliyah Rabbah, the Pri Megadim, and the other later authorities. The Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 290, holds that according to Tosafos, Menachos 32a, s.v. dilma, it is valid if there is no order in the passages of Shema and Vehayah im shamoa, since they are not adjacent in the Torah. See there in the Nekudos HaKesef, who disagrees with him. I also saw in the book Kedushas Yom Tov that he likewise writes not like the Taz, and that there is no distinction regarding order between passages and letters. He writes that Tosafos holds that the intent of the Mechilta concerns the order of placement, that Vehayah ki yevi’acha should not be arranged on the parchment before Kadesh; but if it is arranged after Kadesh, even if it was written first, it is valid. We, however, do not rule leniently even in that manner, since it was written out of order, as is evident below in seif 23 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 288. From the words of the Gra in all these places it is also evident that he holds like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and not like Tosafos. See below in siman 64 in Biur Halacha, where with Hashem’s help we answered the Taz’s difficulty well.

<b> יכתבם בדיו שחור</b> -  ועיין ברמב"ם פ"א מהלכות תפילין שכתב דההלמ"מ דנאמר שיהיו כותבין בדיו לא נאמר רק למעט שאר מיני צבעונין כגון האדום והירוק וכיוצא בהן משמע דבכל דבר שיעשה ממנו מראה שחור כדיו כשר ורק שיהיה מתקיים וכן כתב בהלכות ס"ת א' מכ' דברים הפוסלים ס"ת הוא אם נכתב בלא שחור העומד. ואם היה המראה דומה למראה הכחול [שקורין בל"א בלאה] נלענ"ד שיש להחמיר בזה וראיה מחולין מ"ז ע"ב ככוחלא כשירה כדיותא טריפה דאף דמהרמב"ם משמע דהלכה לא נאמר רק שיהיה מראה שחור וביררנו לקמן בסימן ל"ג דהרצועות שיש עליה גם כן הלמ"מ דצריכה להיות שחור די בשחרות שהיא ככחול שאני הכא שכיון שהיתה ההלכה שיכתוב בדיו אף דדיו לאו דוקא להרמב"ם וה"ה כל מראה שחור אבל עכ"פ שיהיה מראיתה שחור כמו דיו. וכ"ש לפירוש הרא"ש וש"פ דסוברים שדיו ממש בעינן מראה כחול בודאי איננו בכלל דיו אך צל"ע קצת דא"כ אם היה מראית הדיו דומה למראית העורב ג"כ איננו בכלל דיו וכדמוכח בנדה כ' ע"א דמראית הדיו שחור יותר ממראית העורב דאמר שם שחור כדיו טמאה כעורב הוא בכלל דיהא מכן וטהור וא"כ אמאי אמר במדרש שחורות כעורב אלו האותיות אך אין למדין הלכה מן האגדות א"נ י"ל בדוחק דשחור כעורב הוא שחור ג"כ כדיו אבל כדיו הלח והוא ג"כ טהור כדמוכח שם בגמרא. ואפילו אם טבעו להיות נשחר לגמרי אחר הכתיבה יש לעיין בזה טובא אי כשר בדיעבד דהלא ההלכה נאמר שיהיו כותבין בדיו כדאיתא בירושלמי ולא נאמר שיהיה כתוב הסת"מ בדיו ולשון השו"ע ג"כ משמע דבעת הכתיבה יהיה הדיו שחור ויש לפרש עוד דכוונת השו"ע כ"ש שחור אפילו ממי עפצים וקנקנתום ודלא כר"ת וכמו שביאר בב"י וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should write them in black ink.</b> See Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, who writes that the halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai that they be written in ink was stated only to exclude other kinds of colors, such as red, green, and the like. This implies that anything from which one makes a black appearance like ink is valid, provided only that it endures. Similarly, in Hilchos Sefer Torah he wrote that one of the twenty things that disqualify a Sefer Torah is if it was written without enduring black. If the appearance is similar to the appearance of blue, called blau in German, it seems to my humble opinion that one should be stringent in this. There is proof from Chullin 47b: if it is like blue pigment it is kosher, but if like ink it is tereifah. Although from the Rambam it appears that the halachah was stated only that there be a black appearance, and we clarified below in siman 33 that straps, which also have a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai that they must be black, are sufficient with a blackness like blue, here it is different, since the halachah was that he should write with ink. Even though according to the Rambam ink is not precise, and the same applies to any black appearance, nevertheless its appearance must at least be black like ink. All the more according to the explanation of the Rosh and other poskim, who maintain that actual ink is required, a blue appearance is certainly not included in ink. Yet this requires some study, for if so, if the appearance of the ink resembles the appearance of a raven, it too is not included in ink, as is evident from Niddah 20a that the appearance of ink is blacker than the appearance of a raven, for it says there that black like ink is tamei, but like a raven is included in the thereafter category and is tahor. If so, why does the Midrash say, black like a raven, these are the letters? However, we do not derive halachah from aggados. Alternatively, one may say with difficulty that black like a raven is also black like ink, but like wet ink, and that too is tahor, as is evident there in the Gemara. Even if its nature is to become entirely black after the writing, this requires much examination whether it is valid after the fact, for the halachah was stated that they should write with ink, as appears in the Yerushalmi, and it was not stated that the sacred texts should be written with ink. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch also implies that at the time of writing the ink must be black. One may further explain that the intent of the Shulchan Aruch is any black substance, even gall water and vitriol, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, as the Beis Yosef explained. This requires study.

<b> בין שיש בו מי עפצים</b> -  המחבר סתם דבריו ולא פירש ממה לעשותו משום דאפילו אם הוא עשוי מקנקנתום ומי עפצים או מגומא ומי עפצים כשר דמשני מינים ביחד נקרא דיו ודלא כר"ת דאוסר בזה וכמו שביאר בב"י [כן ביאר הגר"א בסימן תרצ"א לקמן וגם בסי' זה רמז לזה דלזה לא כתב השו"ע רק שיש בו ולא כתב אפילו אם הדיו הוא מעפצים בלבד ומיושב בזה קושית הפמ"ג] ולזה בא הרמ"א והוסיף דלכתחלה יש להחמיר שלא לעשותו מעורב בקנקנתום או בגומא משום דהוי כתב שאין יוכל להמחק וכמו שנתבאר ביו"ד בסימן רע"א ס"ו דדוקא בדיעבד אם כתבו במי עפצים וקנקנתום כשר [וגומא הוא ג"כ כתב שאין יוכל להמחק וכדאיתא בסוטה דף י"ז במשנה ] וכן מוכח ברמב"ם פ"א מהלכות תפילין ה"ד ע"ש ואין כונת הרמ"א לומר דלכתחלה דוקא מעשן ולא מעצים גופייהו [ועיין מ"א וא"ר] דהרי ביו"ד בסי' רע"א הרמ"א גופא כתב דלכתחלה יותר טוב שיעשה מדבר הבא מן העץ אח"כ מצאתי בעזה"י בעולת תמיד שפירש כן. א"נ י"ל דכונת הרמ"א למעט מה שכתב הב"י סתם בדיו שחור דמשמע כ"ש שחור כיון דהוא מתקיים והוא ניכר על הקלף דיו הוא וכמו שכתב בב"י בשם רבינו שמחה ולזה בא הרמ"א והוסיף דלכתחלה יחמיר כדברי רבינו תם דדוקא בא מן העץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whether it has gall water.</b> The Mechaber spoke generally and did not specify from what to make it, because even if it is made from vitriol and gall water, or from gum and gall water, it is valid, since two kinds together are called ink, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who prohibits this, as the Beis Yosef explained. The Gra explained this below in siman 691, and also in this siman he alluded to it, for therefore the Shulchan Aruch wrote only that it has gall water, and did not write, even if the ink is from gall-nuts alone. With this the difficulty of the Pri Megadim is resolved. Therefore the Rama came and added that ab initio one should be stringent not to make it mixed with vitriol or gum, because that is writing that cannot be erased, as was explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, seif 6, that only after the fact, if they wrote with gall water and vitriol, it is valid. Gum too is writing that cannot be erased, as appears in the Mishnah in Sotah 17. This is also evident in Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 4; see there. The intent of the Rama is not to say that ab initio it must specifically be from smoke and not from the trees themselves, and see Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah, for in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, the Rama himself wrote that ab initio it is better to make it from something that comes from a tree. Afterward, with Hashem’s help, I found in Olas Tamid that he explained it this way. Alternatively, one may say that the Rama’s intent is to exclude what the Beis Yosef wrote generally, black ink, which implies any black substance, since if it endures and is recognizable on the parchment it is ink, as he wrote in the Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Simchah. Therefore the Rama came and added that ab initio one should be stringent like Rabbeinu Tam, that it must specifically come from a tree.

<b> בין שלא וכו'</b> -  עיין לקמן בסימן תרצ"א וביו"ד בסימן רע"א שכתב השו"ע דאם כתב במי עפצים וקנקנתום כשר. כתב הפמ"ג מדלא הזכיר גומא משמע דאף אם לא הטיל בהן גומא כשר ועיין עוד בדבריו שמסתפק לדינא אם דוקא ע"י שניהן ביחד כיון דאין בהן גומא או אפילו בכל אחד יכול לעשות דיו כיון שהוא שחור והגר"א בביאורו בסימן זה ובסימן תרצ"א פוסק דממי עפצים בלבד או מקנקנתום בלבד פסול אפילו דיעבד דלא מיקרי דיו כ"א משני מינים ביחד מי עפצים וגומא או מי עפצים וקנקנתום ומגומא וקנקנתום יחד בלי תערובת מי עפצים או עשן עצים ושמנים לא ברירא לי דעת הגר"א בזה והגאון מהר"מ בנעט בביאורו על המרדכי מחמיר בזה לכתחלה ובקנקנתום לבד או בגומא לבד מחמיר אפילו בדיעבד כדעת הגר"א ובתשובת משכנות יעקב סימן ל"ז מחמיר אפילו במי עפצים וקנקנתום יחד בלי תערובת גומא עי"ש. והגם דבדיעבד בודאי אין להחמיר ולפסול במי עפצים וקנקנתום יחד נגד פסק השו"ע בסי' תרצ"א וביו"ד סימן רע"א עכ"פ לכתחלה בודאי יש ליזהר כדבריו שאם הוא עושה דיו ממי עפצים אפילו אם הוא מערבו עם קנקנתום לא יעשהו בלתי תערובת גומא וה"ה דממי עפצים וגומא בלחוד נמי שפיר דמי ועי"ש בתשובת משכנות יעקב שמביא הרבה פוסקים סייעתא לדבריו גם כתב שם בתשובה דלכתחלה יש לדקדק לעשותו ע"י בישול כדעת הרמב"ן והר"ן הואיל דאפשר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whether it does not, etc.</b> See below in siman 691 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, where the Shulchan Aruch wrote that if one wrote with gall water and vitriol, it is valid. The Pri Megadim wrote that since he did not mention gum, this implies that even if he did not put gum in them, it is valid. See further in his words, where he is uncertain in practice whether this is specifically through both of them together, since there is no gum in them, or even with each one alone one can make ink, since it is black. The Gra, in his commentary in this siman and in siman 691, rules that from gall water alone or from vitriol alone it is invalid even after the fact, for it is not called ink except from two kinds together, gall water and gum, or gall water and vitriol. Regarding gum and vitriol together without a mixture of gall water, or smoke of wood and oils, the opinion of the Gra is not clear to me. The Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet, in his commentary on the Mordechai, is stringent ab initio, and with vitriol alone or gum alone he is stringent even after the fact, like the opinion of the Gra. In Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 37, he is stringent even with gall water and vitriol together without a mixture of gum; see there. Although after the fact one certainly should not be stringent to invalidate gall water and vitriol together against the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 691 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, nevertheless ab initio one certainly should be careful according to his words, so that if he makes ink from gall water, even if he mixes it with vitriol, he should not make it without a mixture of gum. The same applies that gall water and gum alone is also perfectly acceptable. See there in Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, who brings many poskim as support for his words. He also wrote there in the responsum that ab initio one should be precise to make it through cooking, according to the opinion of Ramban and the Ran, since it is possible.

<b> עפרות זהב</b> -  וה"ה שאר מיני צבעונין על הדיו דפוסל בזה להכתב ובזה לא שייך תקנתא דמעביר ואף אם ירצה אח"כ להעביר בדיו על הצבעונין והזהב פסק הע"ת והא"ר דלא מהני וזהו שכתב אחר כך על אות מאזכרה אין לו תקנה כלומר אפילו רוצה לחפות בדיו מלמעלה פסול וראיה זו דחה הפמ"ג עי"ש ובאמת לענ"ד יש לעיין בזה אם יש בזה אפילו פסול ספק כמו דיו ע"ג צבע אחרי דכתב התחתון וגם העליון הוא כתב דיו ואין כאן מקום להאריך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Gold dust.</b> The same applies to other kinds of colors placed upon the ink, for this disqualifies it from being written, and here the remedy of passing ink over it is not relevant. Even if afterward he wants to pass ink over the colors and the gold, the Olas Tamid and the Eliyah Rabbah ruled that it does not help. This is what he wrote afterward, that for a letter of a Divine Name there is no remedy, meaning that even if he wants to cover it with ink from above it is invalid. The Pri Megadim rejected this proof; see there. In truth, in my humble opinion, this requires examination, whether there is even doubtful invalidity here like ink over color, since the lower writing and also the upper writing are writing in ink. This is not the place to elaborate.

<b> מעביר הזהב</b> -  הפרישה כתב עיין ביו"ד סימן רע"ו כתבתי שם בשם ריב"ש דיש לחוש משום כתב מנומר אם יש הרבה אותיות כן בכמה דפין וכן כתב הל"ח והא"ר השיג עליהם דהריב"ש לא קאי כלל על דברי הנ"י כשזרק זהב על הדיו דבזה לא שייך ענין מנומר כיון דכתב דלמטה קיים אלא הריב"ש קאי על עיקר הדין אם כתב בזהב או בצבעונים עצמן וגררן וכתבן בדיו דבזה שייך ענין מנומר והאריך בזה ועיין בברכי יוסף סי' רע"ו שהסכים לדברי הא"ר וכן הבני יונה ג"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He removes the gold.</b> The Perishah wrote: see Yoreh Deah, siman 276, where I wrote in the name of Rivash that one should be concerned for speckled writing if there are many letters like this on several leaves. So too wrote the Lechem Chamudos. The Eliyah Rabbah challenged them, for the Rivash is not at all referring to the words of the Nimukei Yosef when one threw gold onto the ink, since in that case the issue of speckled writing does not apply, because the writing underneath remains. Rather, the Rivash refers to the basic law where one wrote with gold or with colors themselves, then scraped them off and wrote them with ink; in that case the issue of speckled writing applies. He elaborated on this. See Birkei Yosef, siman 276, who agreed with the words of the Eliyah Rabbah, and so too the Bnei Yonah.

<b>At the beginning of the processing.</b> If only the end of the processing was for their sake, see below in seif 9, where we clarified that the Taz in Yoreh Deah 271, seif 3, holds that it helps, while Perach Mateh Aharon, volume 1, siman 45, disagrees and holds that in practice there is a doubt whether we say that the end proves the beginning. See there further, that scoring of tefillin and mezuzah certainly does not help according to the Bach, and even if one scored them for their sake, it is as if it does not exist. However, we have already concluded that one should not be stringent after the fact. See there in Pri Megadim, who wrote that all this is in an unspecified case, but if one explicitly thinks during the scoring not for its sake, perhaps it is worse according to all opinions.

<b>According to the Rambam, etc., and he assisted.</b> To explain the law of assistance one must elaborate somewhat. The Taz brought here and in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, that assistance is effective even at the end, and he brought proof from returning the parchment to the lime; see there. The Perach Mateh Aharon, siman 45, challenged him: first, how can we say here that the end proves the beginning, since the beginning was done by a non-Jew, and how can we say that because the Jew came at the end and assisted for the sake of the mitzvah, this proves that the beginning was for the sake of the mitzvah. Furthermore, even regarding a Jew himself he brought proof that, as a matter of law, there is doubt whether we say that the end proves the beginning; see there carefully. In truth, according to his understanding of the Taz, there is an even greater difficulty with the Taz, for how did the Taz bring proof from returning the lime into the parchment? Perforce, that case is inferior, for with returning the lime, even if there was no instruction to the non-Jew, it is effective, as appears from the words of the Tur in Yoreh Deah there; whereas here instruction to the non-Jew is certainly required, since assistance alone has no substance. Rather, it is clear that when the Taz wrote that he assists him, he did not mean that he performs some act together with the non-Jew, for such assistance certainly has no substance. Rather, he means that he too performs some act of processing without the non-Jew. The reasoning of the Taz is that this certainly counts as a significant designation for sanctity, since without the Jew finishing it the hide would not yet have been processed. The Tosafos were stringent only because preparing the parchment and scoring it do not count as designation for sanctity, since the work of processing had already been completed. But here, where he performs a complete act in the processing, without which the hide would not have been completed in its processing, it is effective. For this he brought proof from returning the thin layer, which is parchment, as stated in the Tur. That is to say, regarding Shabbos there is certainly no processing after processing, as is explained in the laws of Shabbos concerning other labors; yet here it is effective. Perforce, here we require only that he perform an act demonstrating that it is a significant designation for sanctity. If so, all the more so when he performs the completion of the processing itself; this is certainly called processing even regarding Shabbos, as is proven in Shabbos, folio 75, where it asks: this is salting, this is processing. Thus, even though he did only salting, while it still has the status of hide and not parchment, as stated in Shabbos, folio 79, he is liable for processing. If so, all the more so where he did the tanning alone, through which the processing is completed so that it is called parchment and not diftera, that he is liable. All the more so it is called processing regarding doing it for its sake. If so, we do not need to say that the end proves the beginning, for the end alone is sufficient, and consequently we also do not need to say that he must instruct the non-Jew at the beginning to do it for its sake. With this all the difficulties in the words of the Taz are removed. What emerges from our words is that there are two kinds of assistance. If he performed an action in partnership with the non-Jew, even at the beginning, this certainly is not effective except when he told the non-Jew to put it into the lime for the sake of the mitzvah, and even that is effective only according to the Rosh. This is the case discussed by the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote that he assisted him somewhat in its processing. But certainly, if he performed an action by himself in the work of processing, even though the non-Jew performed the beginning of the processing, since without the Jew it would not have been completed, the Taz holds that it is effective even if he did not tell the non-Jew at the beginning to do it for its sake. Even the Rambam and Tosafos, who hold that instructing the non-Jew does not help at all, would agree according to the Taz that this is effective. Afterward, with Hashem’s help, I found in the explanation of the Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai, at the end of Hilchos Sefer Torah, s.v. or he assists, that his words there explicitly indicate that he too holds as we have written, that even if he did not tell the non-Jew anything about doing it for its sake, it is effective, since the Jew performed an action himself in the work of processing for the sake of the mitzvah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But in the Yerushalmi.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, who wrote: Know, etc. See the Taz, who wrote a novel law, that even according to the Yerushalmi there is invalidity only when the entire interior is punctured, but not when its interior is punctured on one side near the letter. See the Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim, that this applies only according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef; see there. But according to his second resolution, which the Mechaber rules like just below in seif 16, the law according to the Yerushalmi would be that if its interior was punctured before the writing near the letter, even partially, it is invalid. In truth, this is stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1 of Megillah: if the middle of the beis was punctured, if parchment surrounds it on every side it is valid, and if not, it is invalid. It seems clear to me that this is also the intent of the Tur, except that when he wrote the entire interior he meant to exclude a case where the actual interior of the letter was punctured and there remained only a hairbreadth surrounded by blank parchment on all sides, which is valid. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, in a pressing situation where no scribe is available who can properly peel away a little near the hole so that it will be surrounded by parchment, it seems to me that one may rely on the poskim who validate even when the entire interior was punctured. For although the Taz wrote that it is possible that the Rama’s view follows the stringency of both explanations, one who examines the long Darchei Moshe will see explicitly that his view is to be lenient when the entire interior was punctured.

<b> כשנכתב בכשרות</b> -  ומ"מ דוקא בנפסק ע"י נקב דהב"י בתירוצו השני הוסיף דע"י נקב בעינן ג"כ שנפסק אח"כ אבל בנגיעה אות לאות אף שנכתב בכשרות ונפסק אח"כ פסול כדמשמע בסעיף י"ח. דבר משה חא"ח בסי' י"ג הביאו הגרע"א בחידושיו והדה"ח בהלכות קה"ת בדין כ"ה סובר דגם זה תליא בב' התירוצים שבב"י ולתירוץ ב' כשר אך שצריך לחוש לחומרת ב' התירוצים ונמצא שיש בזה לדבריו ספיקא דדינא. ומהסעיף י"ח אין ראיה כלל דשם מיירי בעת הכתיבה גופא אחר שנגמר צורת האות נגע אות בחבירו משא"כ בזה שכבר סילק ידו. וסברא זו מוכרחת ממ"ש השו"ע מגיע לסוף הקלף ע"כ דאין סובר כהירושלמי דמכשיר בנגיעת אות לאות אחר שנגמרה צורתה וכמ"ש הפרישה וביאור הגר"א ואעפ"כ כתב לבסוף בלי היקף קלף מתחלתה דוקא משמע דאם נחתך הקלף אח"כ ועי"ז אין מוקף גויל כשר וע"כ הטעם כמו שכתבנו ומתוך מה שציין הגר"א בסי' י"ח סק"נ עי"ש משמע ג"כ שמפרש להשו"ע דמיירי בעת הכתיבה ואין סותר כלל לסברת תירוץ הב' שזכרו הש"ע בסט"ז. היוצא מדברינו דאין ראיה להחמיר יותר בזה ממה שסובר בעל דה"ח שם עי"ש דהוא ספיקא דדינא ונ"מ לענין להוציא אחרת עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When it was written validly.</b> Nevertheless, this is specifically where it was severed by a hole, for the Beis Yosef, in his second resolution, added that even by a hole we require that it was severed afterward. But regarding a letter touching another letter, even if it was written validly and was separated afterward, it is invalid, as implied in seif 18. The Devar Moshe, Orach Chaim, siman 13, brought by Rabbi Akiva Eiger in his novellae, says this. The Derech HaChaim, Hilchos Kerias HaTorah, law 25, holds that this too depends on the two resolutions in the Beis Yosef, and according to the second resolution it is valid, but one must be concerned for the stringency of both resolutions; according to him, this is a legal doubt. There is no proof at all from seif 18, for there it speaks at the time of the writing itself, where after the form of the letter was completed one letter touched another, unlike here where he had already removed his hand. This distinction is necessarily implied from what the Shulchan Aruch wrote about reaching the end of the parchment. Perforce, he does not hold like the Yerushalmi, which validates a letter touching another letter after its form was completed, as the Perishah and the Biur HaGra wrote; and nevertheless, he wrote at the end that only if it lacked surrounding parchment from the beginning is it invalid. This implies that if the parchment was cut afterward and thereby it is not surrounded by blank parchment, it is valid. Perforce, the reason is as we wrote. From what the Gra cited in siman 18, s.k. 50, see there, it also implies that he explains the Shulchan Aruch as speaking at the time of writing, and it does not at all contradict the reasoning of the second resolution mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch in seif 16. What emerges from our words is that there is no proof to be more stringent in this than the view of the Derech HaChaim there, see there, that it is a legal doubt, with a practical difference regarding whether to take out another Torah scroll; see there.

באופן שאפילו אם ינכה מקום הנקב ושיעור דהקפת גויל ג"כ ישאר שיעור הראוי לאותו אות יש לצדד להקל ולצרף לזה דעת הרד"ך שהובא בסימן זה במג"א סק"ג כי אפילו לדעת הדה"ח והנו"ב הנ"ל איננו רק ספיקא דדינא דשמא **** ENGLISH ****
<b>And afterward it was severed.</b> See the Beis Yosef, and see Derech HaChaim, Hilchos Kerias HaTorah, law 26, who wrote there that even according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef, lack of surrounding blank parchment caused by a hole invalidates, and the Mechaber was concerned for the second resolution only stringently. According to his words, the Mechaber does not distinguish between a puncture before or after writing except where there is at least surrounding blank parchment around the hole near the letter, for the hole does not annul the surrounding parchment around it. Thus there is some surrounding blank parchment at least. But if the hole reaches from one letter to the adjacent letter, it is invalid in every case, for this is considered like one letter touching another, in accordance with the first resolution, which we too follow stringently. Therefore the Mechaber invalidates above, according to the Yerushalmi, even if it was punctured after being written, for puncturing its entire cavity is like one letter touching another; see there. All this is according to his view. But the Pri Megadim, in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 8, holds that the essential law of the Mechaber applies only before it was written; see there. The words of the Derech HaChaim are astonishing. If so, why did the Mechaber write, or if the leg, etc., was without surrounding parchment from the beginning? Even if the parchment adjacent to the letter was cut afterward, it should be invalid according to him, for perhaps the halachah follows the first resolution and we do not distinguish between before and afterward; only by a hole would we be lenient because there is at least surrounding blank parchment adjacent to the hole near the letter, unlike where it was completely cut. Rather, it is certain that the Beis Yosef holds that if the hole was made after the writing it is valid in every case, in accordance with the second resolution, that at that point we do not require surrounding blank parchment. Furthermore, it appears to me that all the more so according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef, lack of surrounding blank parchment caused by a hole does not invalidate. For the Beis Yosef derived this law written in the Shulchan Aruch because he found difficult how we validate any letter severed by a hole, since we say that any letter not surrounded by blank parchment on all four sides is invalid. He answered that we say this only where it occurred by one letter touching another, but not by a hole; or alternatively, that we invalidate due to surrounding blank parchment only if at the time of writing it was not surrounded, unlike where it was written validly. The Beis Yosef here copied the second resolution as law. According to the Derech HaChaim’s new position, that even according to the first resolution one requires surrounding blank parchment and the reason it is valid with a hole is that there is parchment beyond the hole adjacent to the other letter and this counts as surrounding parchment, I am astonished. If so, when the leg of a final kaf reaches the end without surrounding parchment, it should be invalid even according to the first resolution. This is not so, as is proven in the Beis Yosef, that the responsum of the Ralbach, which invalidates this, fits only according to the second resolution. Even if one would force this into the Beis Yosef and say that he wrote the law of the Ralbach according to all views, nevertheless the long Darchei Moshe explicitly proves that according to the first resolution it is valid in this case; see there. Afterward, with Hashem’s help, I found that the Levushei Serad also challenges the Noda BiYehuda, who likewise wrote like the Derech HaChaim. The Pri Megadim too, in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 14, wrote explicitly that according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef we do not require surrounding blank parchment regarding a hole, and even if from the beginning of the writing the leg of the final kaf was without surrounding parchment, it is valid. If so, according to this, even if the hole reaches from one letter to the adjacent letter it is valid for us when it occurred after the writing. Although all this is clear with Hashem’s help, nevertheless practical ruling requires study, since the Derech HaChaim and the Noda BiYehuda are stringent in the matter. At least where another lenient factor joins, such as where the letter is larger than its required measure and the hole was made at its end in such a way that even if one deducts the place of the hole and the measure of surrounding blank parchment the proper measure for that letter remains, there is room to be lenient and to add the view of the Radach cited in this siman in Magen Avraham s.k. 3. For even according to the Derech HaChaim and Noda BiYehuda, it is only a legal doubt, perhaps the halachah follows the first resolution. It is better if he can scrape away a little from the thickness of the line of the letter so that it will be surrounded by blank parchment, for this is effective in every case, even in tefillin and mezuzos, where we require writing in order, as the Magen Avraham permitted scraping the edge of the leg of a final kaf that touches the end without surrounding parchment.

<b> טיפת דיו</b> -  עיין במ"ב בסק"א וז"ל סד"ח בדיני קריאת התורה דין ל"ה נטף שעוה על אות בחול יסירנה [ואם הנטיפה היא על אותיות משמות שאין נמחקין אף בחול אסור שהרי יש לחוש שיתקלף קצת מהאות עמו כיצד יעשה יראה לחמם היטיב הגויל מבחוץ נגד מקום הנטיפה ועי"ז יוסר השעוה בנקל ולא יודבק בו מהכתב כלל ש"א בשער ה' דין ז'] ואם בשבת אם נראה האות מתוכה ויכול לקרות כך כשר אך אם הוא מכוסה שאינו נראה מתוך השעוה אם נמצא זה בין גברא לגברא כיון שאין לקרות בע"פ מה שמכוסה בשעוה ואין קורין בחומשים מפני כבוד הציבור וליכא פגמא של ספר דתרי גברא בתרי ספרא לאו פגמא הוא יש להוציא אחרת ואם נמצא באמצע הקריאה יש לקרות המכוסה בע"פ ואחר כך יסיים בה מנין הקרואים כיון דכבר קרא מה שמכוסה בשעוה מותר לקרות להלן בס"ת הזה דאין פסול בזה מה שמכוסה בשעוה עכ"ל ועיין בספר ש"א הנ"ל ובחידושי רע"א בסימן זה. וכתב עוד שם הגרע"א דאומן ישראל המתקן סידור הבלוי ומוכרח לו להניח נייר ולדבקו עם (פא"פ) על מקום היפה ופוגע בשמות הקדושים שאינם נמחקים דיש בזה חשש איסור מחיקה כיון דא"א לקלפו שיהיה כל האותיות קיימים וגרע יותר משעוה עי"ש שהאריך בזה ומסיק דיעשה האומן כך יניח נייר על כל שם ושם נייר בלי דבק או ידבק מעט בשעוה דלא הוי מחיקה וכנ"ל [ומה דאסור בשבת גרירת השעוה מעל הס"ת הוא מטעם אחר עי"ש] ואח"כ ידבק הנייר (בפא"פ) דיהיה הנייר הב' מדובק על הנייר ראשון שמונח על השם וזה לא הוי מחיקה דאם יקלפו הנייר השני יהיה השם שלם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A drop of ink.</b> See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 1. This is the language of the Derech HaChaim, laws of Kerias HaTorah, law 35: If wax dripped on a letter on a weekday, he should remove it. If the drip is on letters of the Names that may not be erased, even on a weekday it is forbidden, for one must be concerned that some of the letter will peel off with it. What should he do? He should heat the parchment well from the outside opposite the place of the drip, and through this the wax will come off easily and none of the writing will adhere to it at all. Shaarei Ephraim, gate 5, law 7. If this occurred on Shabbos, then if the letter is visible through it and can be read that way, it is valid. But if it is covered and is not visible through the wax, if this is found between one aliyah and another, since one may not read by heart what is covered by the wax, and one may not read from printed chumashim because of the honor of the congregation, and there is no defect to the scroll since two readers reading from two scrolls is not considered a defect, another scroll should be taken out. If it is found in the middle of the reading, one should read what is covered by heart and afterward complete with that scroll the required number of readers. Since what was covered by wax has already been read, one may continue reading further in this Torah scroll, for the fact that it is covered by wax does not make it invalid. End quote. See the aforementioned Shaarei Ephraim and Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger on this siman. Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote further there that when a Jewish craftsman repairs a worn siddur and must place paper and glue it with paste onto the nice area, and he comes upon sacred Names that may not be erased, there is concern for the prohibition of erasing, since it is impossible to peel it off while all the letters remain intact, and this is worse than wax; see there, where he discusses it at length. He concludes that the craftsman should do as follows: he should place paper over each Name, a paper without glue, or glue it slightly with wax, which is not erasing, as above, for the prohibition on Shabbos to scrape wax from a Torah scroll is for another reason; see there. Afterward he should glue the paper with paste, so that the second paper is glued onto the first paper that lies over the Name. This is not erasing, for if the second paper is peeled off, the Name will remain whole.

<b> ואינה ניכרת</b> -  עיין במ"ב בס"ק ס"ב לענין אם נפל על קווי האות ממש וניכר האות דכתבנו שם להקל בזה אף דהפמ"ג במ"ז בס"ק ט"ו מצדד להחמיר בזה מטעם כתב על כתב אם לאו שבעת נפילת הטיפה כבר נתייבש האות עי"ש כבר הושג ע"ז מחמת כמה קושיות עיין בפ"ת אה"ע בסדר גיטין ואף שמיישבו שם קצת מלבד שהוא דוחק גדול אין להחמיר בזה אחרי שרוב הפוסקים מקילין אפילו בכתב על גבי כתב ממש וכ"ש בזה הטפת דיו שנפלה מעצמה שאין שייך שם כתב עליו. ועוד נ"ל בזה עצה אחרת אפילו בכתב על גבי כתב ממש והשני היה שלא לשמה דיעביר עוד קולמוס עליו לשמה וממ"נ מהני ולא מיקרי שלא כסדרן לכאורה מאחר דמעת כתיבה הראשונה צורתו עליו וכמש"כ בס"ק ל"ט במ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is not recognizable.</b> See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 62 regarding where it fell directly on the lines of the letter and the letter is recognizable; we wrote there to be lenient in this, although the Pri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 15 inclines to be stringent because of writing over writing, unless the letter had already dried when the drop fell; see there. This has already been challenged because of several difficulties; see Pischei Teshuvah, Even HaEzer, Seder Gittin. Although he somewhat resolves it there, aside from its being very forced, one should not be stringent in this after most poskim are lenient even regarding actual writing over writing, and all the more so with this drop of ink that fell on its own, where the term writing does not apply to it. It further appears to me that there is another remedy even in actual writing over writing where the second writing was not for the sake of the mitzvah: he should pass the pen over it again for the sake of the mitzvah, and in either possibility it is effective. It seemingly is not considered not in order, since from the time of the first writing its form was upon it, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 39.

<b> ורי"ש שעשאה כמין ד' וכו'</b> -  ואם עשה גג רחב לך' פשוטה עד שנראה כמו רי"ש כתב המ"א דימשוך הרגל עד שיהיה כפלים כמו הגג [ואם הכפלים הוא לעיכובא או לכתחלה עיין לקמן בסימן ל"ו בדיני צורת אותיות] ואם אין לו מקום למשוך כתב המ"א בשם הרי"ל וכן סתם הפמ"ג בסי' ל"ו בצורת האותיות דתקונו הוא שימחוק כל הגג דגרירת היתר בודאי לא מהני בזה דהוי ח"ת ממש ואפילו במלת אלהיך יכול למוחקו אך יזהר שלא ימחק הרגל וצ"ע הלא דמי לרי"ש שעשאו כמין ד' דפסק השו"ע דצריך לגרור כולו משום דנכתב בפ"א וה"נ דכוותיה ועיין ביד אפרים שמחלק קצת בזה ודוחק אח"כ מצאתי בחידושי רע"א שכתב בפשיטות דדמי לרי"ש שעשאו כמין ד' [ועיין בש"ת בס"ק כ"ב במה שהעתיק בזה בשם תשובת גינת ורדים וצ"ע אם יש לסמוך ע"ז אחרי שכל האחרונים מחמירין דצריך עכ"פ למחוק הגג] אך אי אתרמי כן בך' של אלהיך שעשאו רחב כמין ד' או רי"ש נראה דיש לסמוך להקל דדי במחיקת הגג אם אין לו מקום למשוך הרגל אחרי שגם ברי"ש שעשאו כמין ד' מיקל הפר"ח והביאו המה"ש דדי שיבטלנו מצורת אות וגם הגרע"א בחידושיו הניח דברי השו"ע בזה בצ"ע מחמת אותה הקושיא של הפר"ח (ותמיה שלא זכרו):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And a reish that he made like a dalet, etc.</b> If he made a wide roof for a final kaf until it appears like a reish, the Magen Avraham wrote that he should extend the leg until it is twice the length of the roof. Whether that double length is indispensable or only initial practice, see below, siman 36, in the laws of the forms of the letters. If he has no room to extend it, the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Maharil, and the Pri Megadim likewise ruled simply in siman 36 in the forms of the letters, that its correction is to erase the entire roof, for scraping away the excess certainly does not help here, as it is actual engraving of the interior. Even in the word “your God” he may erase it, but he must be careful not to erase the leg. This requires study, for is it not comparable to a reish that he made like a dalet, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled that one must scrape all of it because it was written at one time? Here too it is similar. See Yad Ephraim, who distinguishes this somewhat, but it is forced. Afterward I found in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger that he wrote simply that it is comparable to a reish that he made like a dalet. See Shaarei Teshuvah, s.k. 22, in what he copied on this from Responsa Ginas Veradim; it requires study whether one may rely on this after all the Acharonim are stringent that he must at least erase the roof. However, if such a case occurred in the final kaf of “your God,” where he made it wide like a dalet or a reish, it appears that one may rely leniently, that erasing the roof suffices if he has no room to extend the leg. This is because even with a reish that he made like a dalet, the Pri Chadash is lenient, and the Machatzis HaShekel brought him, that it is enough to annul its form as a letter. Rabbi Akiva Eiger too, in his novellae, left the words of the Shulchan Aruch here requiring study because of that question of the Pri Chadash, and it is surprising that they did not mention him.
