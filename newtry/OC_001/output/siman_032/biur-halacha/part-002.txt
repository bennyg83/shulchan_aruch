<b> משום דבין הגג ובין הירך כו'</b> -  כי בפעם א' נכתב הכל כדרך הכותבין ולפ"ז אם מתחלה כתב ריש ואח"כ טעה ועשה בו תג כעין דלי"ת סגי ליה כשיגרור הגג או הירך עם התג ויחזור ויכתבנו כמין ריש. לבוש ופמ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because both the roof and the thigh, etc.</b> For all of it was written at one time in the normal manner of writers. According to this, if he first wrote a reish and afterward erred and made a tag on it like a dalet, it is enough that he scrape the roof or the thigh together with the tag and then write it again like a reish. Levush and Pri Megadim.

<b> ואם גרר והפרידה וכו'</b> -  אף אם נעשה הדביקות קודם שנגמר האות ועיין בביאור הגר"א ובס' שערי אפרים למהרא"ז מרגליות דדין זה דשו"ע יש לפקפק בו ומוכח שם מדעת ש"א בפתחי שערים שלו דין להקל בו אלא בתפילין ומזוזות דכדיעבד דמי דאם היה צריך מחיקה היה נפסל משום שלא כסדרן אבל בס"ת וכן בתו"מ אם לא כתב עדיין התיבות שאח"ז לא מהני גרירת הדביקות לבד אם נדבק קודם שנגמר האות וגמר האות בפסול:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he scraped and separated it, etc.</b> This applies even if the adhesion occurred before the letter was completed. See Biur HaGra and Sefer Shaarei Ephraim by Rabbi Efraim Zalman Margoliyos, who write that this law of the Shulchan Aruch can be questioned. It is evident there from the view of Shaarei Ephraim in his Pischei Shearim that he rules leniently in it only regarding tefillin and mezuzos, which are like an after-the-fact case, for if erasure were required it would be invalid because it was not written in order. But regarding a Torah scroll, and likewise tefillin and mezuzos if he had not yet written the words after it, scraping the adhesion alone is not effective if it became attached before the letter was completed and he completed the letter invalidly.

ולכך פסול דז"א דהרי השו"ע מחמיר אפילו בקו"ף שע"י נגיעת הרגל להגג אינו משתנה מצורתו כלל וכ"ש בזה ובמקום הדחק ואין תקנה במחיקת האות כגון שכתב שם הקודש אח"ז ובידוע שנעשה נגיעה דקה זה אחר שנגמר האות בהכשר אפשר דיש לצרף לזה דעת הסמ"ק [המובא בטור בדין נפלה טיפת דיו ע"ש] דסובר להקל בזה ע"י גרירה וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The legs of the heh, etc.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote that whenever the form of the letter is not present, etc.; this is the language of the Chelkas Mechokek in Even HaEzer, see there. Regarding what I wrote, that even if it became attached in such a way that the child knows it is a heh, etc., so it appears clear from the fact that the Beis Yosef brought in this siman and in siman 36 the source for this from the words of Maharik, shoresh 95, and not as he wrote in shoresh 78; there he is explicitly discussing such a case, as explained there at the end of the shoresh. He also brought this from Terumas HaDeshen, and it is likewise proven there, for otherwise there would be no question at all from the Ram against the Rosh; see there. So too it is explained explicitly in Even HaEzer, siman 125, seif 16, in the gloss, that although the child reads it as a heh, nevertheless it should be invalidated except in a case of agunah; see there. The Pri Chadash is stringent there even in a case of agunah for the reason we wrote. So too the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radach, and so too it is proven in Yoreh Deah, siman 276, in the Beis Yosef; see there. However, if this was found in a Torah scroll, where the leg of a heh or kuf touched above and a child who is neither wise nor foolish reads it properly, it appears to me that another scroll should not be taken out because of this. In truth, this law is subject to a dispute among great authorities, as is evident to one who examines Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 125. The proof is that they validate it in a case of agunah; see there. If so, regarding taking out another scroll, one may join to this the view of the Rambam, who validates reading from an invalid Torah scroll, making it a double doubt, as the Pri Megadim ruled in a similar case in siman 143, and the Derech HaChaim in Hilchos Kerias HaTorah. But regarding tefillin it is at least a doubt concerning Torah law, and a doubt concerning Torah law is treated stringently. Know further that the Gra in his commentary agrees with the view of the Mordechai, that suspending the heh, and all the more so the kuf as is proven to one who examines it, is only for the mitzvah in a place where the scribal custom is to make the ches with humps, so there is a clear distinction between ches and heh. It seems obvious to me that what he wrote to be lenient is specifically where it touches only like a hairbreadth and from a distance it has the form of a heh, since the child also reads it as a heh. In truth, in such a manner it has the form of a heh and not a ches; it is only because it should not appear like a ches, which is included in the case of making chesin into hehin taught in the baraisa, about which it says it must be put away. Therefore, if they make the ches with humps, there is again a distinction, and one should not invalidate after the fact. But if the leg of the heh adhered to the roof with a thick adhesion until it actually appears like a ches, certainly the name ches is not changed to heh by the scribes. I later found, with Hashem’s help, in the explanation of the Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai that he too wrote this in the view of the Mordechai. The Tashbetz also holds so, and he is brought in the Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 126, and in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger below in siman 36; see there. Nevertheless, it requires study whether one may rely on the Mordechai’s view to be lenient here even by separating it, since the Shulchan Aruch is stringent in this. One should not reject this by saying that the Shulchan Aruch speaks in a place where the scribal custom is to make a ches without humps, which is valid in that case as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 36 and the Acharonim agreed, and therefore he is also stringent there in Even HaEzer regarding a get, for there the custom is not to make a ches with humps, so there is no distinction between heh and ches, and therefore it is invalid. This is not so, for the Shulchan Aruch is stringent even with a kuf, where through the leg touching the roof its form is not changed at all, and all the more so here. In a pressing situation where there is no remedy by erasing the letter, such as where he wrote the sacred Name after it, and it is known that this thin touch occurred after the letter was validly completed, perhaps one can combine the view of the Semak, brought in the Tur in the law of a drop of ink that fell, see there, who holds leniently here by scraping. This requires study.

<b> יגרור הרגל</b> -  עיין במ"ב במ"ש כולו וכו' וה"מ שנעשה הנגיעה בעת הכתיבה אבל אם נעשה לאחר הכתיבה אין דין הה"א והקו"ף שוה דבה"א צריך למחוק כל הרגל דאם ישתייר כ"ש עדיין שם ה"א הראשונה עליו להרא"ש כדאיתא בסט"ז משא"כ בהקו"ף די שיבטלנו מצורת אות. ודע עוד דמ"ש הפמ"ג בסימן ל"ו בא"א אות א' עוד עצה אחרת דהיינו שיגרור קצת מהגג עד שהרגל יהיה חוץ לגג ועי"ז יתבטל צורתו ואח"כ ימשוך גג הקו"ף למעלה מהרגל דהיינו שיעשה מעט הפרש ושפיר דמי כל שאין בזה משום שלא כסדרן עכ"ד הוא מיירי בנעשה הנגיעה אח"כ דאל"ה נעשה הרגל בפסול וצריך לגוררו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should scrape the leg.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, in what he wrote: all of it, etc. This applies only where the touch occurred at the time of writing. But if it occurred after the writing, the law of heh and kuf is not the same. With a heh, he must erase the entire leg, for if any amount remains, the name of the first heh is still upon it according to the Rosh, as stated in seif 16. By contrast, with a kuf it is enough that he annul it from the form of a letter. Know further that what the Pri Megadim wrote in siman 36, Eshel Avraham, letter alef, as another remedy, namely that he should scrape a little from the roof until the leg is outside the roof and thereby its form will be annulled, and afterward extend the roof of the kuf above the leg, meaning that he should make a small separation, and this is proper whenever there is no issue of not in order, end quote, speaks of where the touch occurred afterward. Otherwise the leg was made invalidly and he must scrape it.

<b> נגע רגל האל"ף וכו'</b> -  דע דאין חילוק בין אם נגע בעת הכתיבה או לאח"כ דומיא דנפלה טיפת דיו בסי"ז רק דאם נגע בעת הכתיבה צריך למחוק הרגל כולו ואם נגע אחר שהשלים האות כראוי אין צריך למחוק כל הרגל רק שיבטלנו מצורת אות דומיא דמה שכתב הלבוש והביאו המ"א סוף ס"ק כ"ה עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The leg of the alef touched, etc.</b> Know that there is no distinction whether it touched at the time of writing or afterward, like the case of a drop of ink that fell in seif 17. However, if it touched at the time of writing, he must erase the entire leg; and if it touched after he completed the letter properly, he need not erase the whole leg, but only annul it from the form of a letter, like what the Levush wrote and the Magen Avraham brought at the end of s.k. 25; see there.

<b> או פני האל"ף וכו'</b> -  עיין במ"א דדוקא אם נדבק עצם היו"ד להאל"ף משא"כ אם רק נגע עוקצה השמאלית של היו"ד להאל"ף כשר דהא אפילו בלא הקוצה מיקרי יו"ד פי' דאף דע"י הנגיעה נחשב כאלו אין לו קוץ להיו"ד הא אין מעכב בזה לענין תיקון וכ' ע"ז הפמ"ג ועכ"פ גרירה בעי ודי וא"צ למחוק כל היו"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or the face of the alef, etc.</b> See the Magen Avraham, that this is specifically if the body of the yud adhered to the alef. But if only the left point of the yud touched the alef, it is valid, for even without the point it is called a yud. That is, although because of the touching it is considered as though the yud has no point, this is not indispensable regarding correction. The Pri Megadim wrote about this that in any event scraping is required, and that suffices; it is not necessary to erase the entire yud.

<b>He should scrape all that was made invalidly, etc.</b> See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that this means everything he wrote in that letter after he made the touch. See the Pri Megadim, who explained his words regarding the upper yud, that if the upper yud touched the alef and he completed the letter afterward, he must scrape the entire alef, and scraping the yud does not help. The Magen Avraham inferred this from the word “all” written in the Shulchan Aruch. It is very astonishing to derive such a new law from such a slight inference. Moreover, his essential law is refutable, for the Beis Yosef wrote as follows: If the leg of the alef touched the roof of the alef, or the face of the alef touched the face of the roof beneath it, the Agur wrote that all of them are invalid and cannot be corrected by erasing or scraping, for this is called engraving the interior. However, by scraping what was made invalidly it is enough, as I wrote nearby regarding the leg of a heh or kuf that touches above. End quote. The plain language of “however” proves that it refers to both cases, and that scraping the yud and the leg suffices, similar to the leg of a heh or kuf. This is also proven more strongly from his language in siman 36, where he wrote that the Agur says that if the upper yud of the alef touched, etc.; and so he wrote in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. He wrote further there that if they adhered and thereby became invalid, it is not enough to erase the adhesion, for that is engraving the interior; rather, one must scrape the entire yud that adhered and correct it initially. This too was copied in the long Darchei Moshe. It is clear that even with the adhesion of the upper yud, scraping the yud is enough. So too, from what he concluded there in Beis Yosef, siman 36, with this very law of scraping the yud when the yud of a shin or ayin adhered, etc., it is also proven as we wrote. According to the Pri Megadim, one would need in those cases too to scrape the entire letter, as with the adhesion of the yud on the alef. One cannot say that the Beis Yosef is speaking in all of this where it adhered after he had completed the letter properly, for if so, why must he erase the whole yud? It would be enough to scrape part of it until he annuls the alef from the form of a letter, as above, and likewise with yuds that touched other letters. The Levush too wrote explicitly that the law of the upper and lower yud is the same, as the Pri Megadim himself brought, unless we push aside the Pri Megadim’s words and say that the Beis Yosef, Darchei Moshe, and Levush speak where he first wrote the body of the letter and afterward the upper yud, and upon completing it it adhered to the bent line of the alef. Were it not for the words of the Pri Megadim, it would seem to me that the Magen Avraham refers to the lower leg of the alef and comes to explain the Shulchan Aruch’s intent in writing “all,” namely that he must scrape the entire leg, since it was written in one act of writing after the touch was made, and it is not enough to scrape part of it until he annuls the alef from the form of a letter. The same applies where the upper yud adhered, but in any event it suffices to scrape the entire yud, and he need not scrape what was written afterward, since it was written in two acts of writing. The Magen Avraham did not concern himself to explain this, for the Mechaber had already explained it at the beginning of the seif. In particular, what the Pri Megadim wrote in the laws of the forms of the letters, letter alef, that the Taz in Even HaEzer, siman 125, also holds like the Magen Avraham, that there is a distinction between the upper yud adhering and the lower yud, meaning the leg of the alef. His intent is to the Taz there, s.k. 10, who disagrees with the Beis Yosef and Rama in the law of a drop of ink that fell and one completed the letter through writing, and holds that the entire letter must be made through writing and not made on its own. From this the Pri Megadim infers that the same applies, that according to the Taz one must be stringent in this law too. In my humble opinion this is no proof at all, for there his reason is that the entire letter must be formed through writing and not on its own. But here, where it was not made on its own, it may be comparable to a case where one wrote the roof and leg and afterward wrote the yud. It is not comparable to a dalet in place of a reish, where one must scrape all of it, because there he made everything in one act of writing, as the Taz wrote in s.k. 17; unlike our case, which appears to be two acts of writing. Also in Sefer LeDavid Emes by the author of Birkei Yosef, he does not distinguish between the adhesion of the upper yud and the lower yud. However, afterward I saw in some later Acharonim after the Pri Megadim that their view also seems like the Pri Megadim, that the Magen Avraham’s view distinguishes between the upper and lower yud. Therefore initially one certainly must be stringent and scrape everything made invalidly after the touch occurred, meaning even the lower leg of the alef and the like in the other letters listed here in the gloss. After the fact, it requires examination whether to be stringent in this, since the plain language of the Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe in siman 36 and the Levush does not imply so, as above. Afterward I found in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger that he hinted to examine the responsum of Rabbi Yehoshua HaLevi, brother of the Taz. I examined it and found that he too takes it as obvious that when the yuds on the alefs touch, one need not scrape the lower leg beneath the line; see there, regarding what he wrote about the upper yud. But regarding the bent line, which the Shulchan Aruch calls the roof of the alef, he holds there that one must scrape it, for it is called something made invalidly through the adhesion to the yud; see there. This is unless he first wrote the bent line and the lower leg and afterward completed the upper yud and it adhered to it, in which case everyone agrees that he need only scrape the yud, for the line was written validly. Returning to our matter: initially it is proper to be concerned for the stringent view and scrape even the lower leg; after the fact, if he did not scrape the lower leg, it appears that one should not be stringent. Regarding the bent line, one must examine the matter, for from the plain language of the Beis Yosef, Darchei Moshe, and Levush cited above it implies that the yud must be erased, but not the line. The practical ruling requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One letter, etc.</b> I have seen some people invalidate in this case when there is a very fine break, so fine that it is not recognizable until one looks at it carefully. Their source is the Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, who wrote that in all such cases repair does not help even if their separation is not plainly recognizable; see also there under letter 36. In my humble opinion, as a matter of law one should not be stringent about this, and I will clarify it, with Hashem's help, through several proofs. First, from the words of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari, from whom this law originates. His language in the Beis Yosef is that if one wrote in tefillin or mezuzos one letter divided into two letters, for example a tzadi written as a yud and a nun, etc., and similarly the Rosh wrote about a hei and a kuf that became attached in tefillin, that they have no remedy through correction, etc. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 36, who wrote that in all these cases, even if a child can read it, it is invalid, since it looks like two letters; and see the Pri Megadim there. According to his words, Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari and the Magen Avraham are speaking even where their separation is not plainly recognizable. Besides this being very forced in the words of the Magen Avraham, as I will clarify at the end, it creates a great difficulty in Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari's words. If so, what proof did he bring from kuf? The reason repair does not help for kuf, while for other letters that are not touching but have not lost their essential form repair helps, as Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari himself wrote, must be as the Beis Yosef first wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, that its repair is through erasure, and therefore its essential form will be cancelled before everyone's eyes. If so, that is no proof to a tzadi written as yud and nun where the separation was not plainly recognizable until one looked closely, and a child read it as a letter. Although one could force a distinction between tzadi and a yud and shin that are not touching, since until they are joined they are intrinsically two other letters, at any rate there is no proof from kuf, and certainly no a fortiori proof. Rather, Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari meant to invalidate specifically when it was divided until it was recognizable and appeared to the eye as two letters. Then he properly brings proof from kuf: at the time of writing it did not resemble another letter because of its attachment, and even at the time of repair, when it must first be erased until its form is cancelled, it still will not thereby resemble another letter, yet it is invalid to repair. All the more so a tzadi written as yud and nun, which appears to all eyes as two letters and certainly does not yet have the form of a tzadi, may not be joined afterward. This is also the case addressed by the Magen Avraham: a child's reading does not help where it appears to all eyes as two letters, for the tzadi has lost its essential form and is like an alef missing one yud. But if the separation is not recognizable until one looks carefully, it may be repaired, for its essential form remains and a child reads it as a letter, like a yud and shin that are not touching, where the child's reading helps. This also resolves his wording, "in all these cases, even a child," because it looks like two letters; he did not write, "because they are two letters." The implication is that when the separation is not plainly recognizable and the child reads it as a letter, it is still called retaining its essential form and may be repaired. The language of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari and the Shulchan Aruch also implies this, for they speak of a tzadi written as yud and nun, not merely of a break at the point where the yud joins the nun. Olas Tamid also implies that the case is one where it appears to everyone as two letters; see there. Know also that Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari's explanation, that before repair they still had the name of that letter and not another name because a child neither wise nor foolish could read them, is one reason: the second clause clarifies the first, for it proves that the name of the letter is still upon it. This is unlike a tzadi written as yud and nun, where the separation is plainly recognizable; presumably a child will not read it as a tzadi, since it looks like two letters and its essential form has been cancelled. As proof that this is correct, I copy the language of the long Darchei Moshe: Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari wrote that if he wrote one letter and divided it into two letters, etc.; but if the break was in other letters where the break does not make them resemble another letter, such as an alef or tav whose parts were not joined, it is permitted to repair them, since without repair a child neither wise nor foolish can read them. Thus the main reason to validate a yud and shin that did not touch is that a child can read it, unlike a tzadi written as yud and nun, where presumably the child could not read it because it appears to all as two letters. The Magen Avraham's statement means that even if it happened that a child read it as a tzadi, it is still invalid because our eyes see that its form is not upon it at all. Although the Taz in s.k. 21 explains the case as one where he did not make its roof, implying that the Mechaber's case of a ches made of two zayins applies even where the separation is not plainly recognizable, for it is parallel to the latter case, and there it speaks where it is not plainly recognizable except that there is a roof, therefore repair helps there and not here, nevertheless the words of the Taz themselves are very difficult. First, the Mechaber's language implies that at the beginning the invalidity of the ches is because its separation is plainly recognizable, since at the end he says, "but it is not recognizable," implying that first he speaks of where it is plainly recognizable. Second, one who studies the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 36, under "I found written, if the roof," will see explicitly that it is invalid even when it has a roof, and his language there implies that even a child would not help, because it has no name of a letter at all, since its separation is plainly recognizable and it looks like two zayins. If so, this itself is proof to our words: in all the earlier cases the Mechaber also speaks specifically when their separation is plainly recognizable, for because of this the letter has lost its essential form and appears like two other letters. But where the separation is not plainly recognizable, its name still remains upon it. This is the reason of the opinion that is lenient regarding the roof of a ches whose separation was not recognizable. One may also say, as a matter of law, that regarding a tzadi and similar cases, even the Taz agrees to all we have proven, that where the separation is not plainly recognizable it can be repaired, for its name remains upon it. Only regarding ches does the Taz hold that since it has no roof it is evidently two zayins, and therefore even if they were made so close that the separation was not plainly recognizable, it lost its name. Terumas HaDeshen in siman 48 also somewhat implies this. He writes that the Rosh's ruling about a leg of a hei or kuf that touched above in tefillin does not prove that the Rosh disagrees with Maharam, who validates repairing the yuds on alefs that touch the body of the letter, for those cases are different since they cannot be repaired by erasure alone, etc. He further writes that perhaps the Rosh also refers to what he wrote afterward, that the yuds on alefs and shins must be attached, meaning that those too cannot be repaired in tefillin and the Rosh disagrees with Maharam. The Beis Yosef writes that one who studies his words will see that Terumas HaDeshen found the first answer more reasonable, that the Rosh does not disagree with Maharam and was not referring to what follows. If so, we see that the Rosh later included in this law also the yuds behind the tzadi, writing that the point on the alef shaped like a yud must touch it, and likewise the yud within the shin and ayin and behind the tzadi; if it does not touch, the form of the letter is altered. The plain language of Terumas HaDeshen and the Beis Yosef implies that the Rosh's view that there is no remedy does not apply at all to what he wrote afterward. Terumas HaDeshen mentioned only yuds on alefs and shins because he was not listing every item; proof is that he also omitted yuds on ayins and legs of tavs. Hashem enlightened my eyes and I found this still more explicit in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 230, where he is uncertain about a get in which the yud in an alef, the yud in a shin, and the yud behind a tzadi were not touching, but were so close that a child read them properly. It is explicit there that in all these cases the name of the letter is upon it, since the letter is recognizable even to a child; they are invalid only for tefillin and mezuzos, and he is uncertain whether to invalidate them even in a get because the letters must be written as given to Moshe at Sinai, each letter as one connected body except for hei and kuf. Accordingly, it seems clear that since we rule regarding the rule of writing in order for tefillin and mezuzos that it is permitted to repair yuds of shin and ayin that are not touching, because once a child reads it as a letter the name of the letter is upon it and its essential form remains, the same applies to the yud behind a tzadi. Returning to our matter, all this proves explicitly that if the break was fine, so that the separation was not plainly recognizable until one looked carefully, and a child reads it properly, even if such a case occurred in a tzadi between the yud behind it and its nun, or the like, it may be repaired, for it is not called a letter divided into two letters. Perhaps in such a case one need not even show it to a child, for he will certainly read it properly, like the ches about which the Shulchan Aruch wrote that seeing a child is not indispensable; although initially it is certainly proper to show it to a child. I have also seen some later authorities who incline to leniency where the separation is not plainly recognizable and the child reads it as a letter. Therefore, in pressing circumstances it appears to me that one may rely on all this to be lenient and permit repair, not like the Pri Megadim. Know further that if the break is extremely fine, visible only against the sun, it is valid even according to the Pri Megadim and needs no repair at all. Certainly a break is no worse than such a hole in that place, and regarding a hole we rule that a hole over which the ink passes is not considered a hole, even if visible against the sun, as written above in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 32. Certainly even if this occurred in a tzadi between the yud and nun, or the like, it is not considered a hole; otherwise, how could they say in Shabbos 108a that tefillin may be written on the skin of a kosher bird, even though the skin is perforated, because it is only a hole over which the ink passes? We should be concerned that the hole might occur in a tzadi between the yud and nun, or in a shin where it divides it into an ayin and zayin, or the like. Rather, such a hole is not considered a hole at all. One who wishes to be stringent about such a fine break should be stringent only to require repair, but Heaven forbid to invalidate the tefillin because of this.

<b> מכירם שאע"פ וכו'</b> -  הנה מפשטא דלישנא דהשו"ע מוכח דמיירי אף בניכר להדיא הפרדתו וכמו שהוכיח הגרע"א בחידושיו דלא כהפמ"ג בא"א בסוף צורת האותיות ומה שהביא הפמ"ג ראיה לזה ממה שכתב הב"י בסוף סימן ל"ו בד"ה מ"כ וכו' ודאי אי הוה מופרד להדיא עד שנראה כשני זייני"ן וכו' המעיין יראה דאין ראיה כלל דשם ה"ט כיון שנראה כשני זייני"ן בטל ממנה עיקר תמונתה והוא אות שחלוק לשתי אותיות הכתוב בשו"ע מקודם ולא אמרינן דהחטוטרות מוכיח עליה משא"כ בזה דע"י שניכר להדיא הפרדתה לא אבד עדיין ממנה עיקר תמונתה כ"ז שאינה חלוקה כ"כ עד שהתינוק לא יכיר תמונתה גם לפי מה שהוכחנו למעלה דאות שחלוק לשני אותיות מיירי דוקא בניכר להדיא הפרדתה ממילא מוכח דדין זה דסיפא איירי ג"כ בכה"ג דאפילו ניכר להדיא מהני תינוק לענין תיקון ועכ"פ נראה לסמוך ע"ז בשעת הדחק להקל דמהני תינוק וכמו שכתב הגרע"א בחידושיו דהורה כן למעשה בשעת הדחק ומה שכתב לבסוף וצ"ע לדינא נ"ל שהוא מלשון הפמ"ג שם בפתיחה בסוף דבריו שמסיים שם וצ"ע דאל"כ יסתור א"ע שכתב שהורה כן למעשה. אך מ"מ נ"ל בפשיטות דאף הגרע"א מודה היכא שהפסקו גדול כ"כ עד שניכר ונראה לעין כל מרחוק דבזה הרי אנו רואין שאבד צורתו לגמרי ולא עדיף מצד"י שכתבו יו"ד נו"ן ולא מהני לזה תינוק. וכעין זה ראיתי באחרונים שהביאו בשם תשובת פמ"א ח"ב סימן ט"ז שכתב אם ברחוק מכירים שהוא ריוח ואינו מגיע לגוף האות לדעת הב"י בסימן ל"ו ברחוק ד"א ולדעת הב"ח ברחוק אמה בזה ודאי אין לסמוך על תינוק אבל אם נרחק לכל מר כדאית ליה אנו רואין שהוא אל"ף ושי"ן רק כשמקרבין עינינו רואין ריוח ותינוק קורא אל"ף או שי"ן שפיר נוכל לסמוך עליו עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Recognizes them, even though, etc.</b> From the plain wording of the Shulchan Aruch it is proven that it speaks even where the separation is plainly recognizable, as Rabbi Akiva Eiger proved in his novellae, unlike the Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham at the end of the forms of the letters. As for the Pri Megadim's proof from what the Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 36, under "I found written, etc.," that if it were certainly plainly separated until it looked like two zayins, etc., one who studies it will see that there is no proof at all. There the reason is that since it looks like two zayins, its essential form is cancelled; it is the earlier Shulchan Aruch case of a letter divided into two letters, and we do not say that the roof proves its identity. This is not so here, where even though the separation is plainly recognizable, its essential form has not yet been lost, so long as it is not so divided that the child no longer recognizes its form. Also according to what we proved above, that a letter divided into two letters refers specifically to where the separation is plainly recognizable, it follows that the latter clause also speaks in such a case, where even if the separation is plainly recognizable, a child's reading helps for repair. In any event, in pressing circumstances it appears one may rely on this leniently, that a child helps, as Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote in his novellae that he ruled so in practice in pressing circumstances. What he wrote at the end, "and this requires study as law," appears to be copied from the Pri Megadim's language in the introduction at the end of his words, for otherwise he would contradict himself after writing that he ruled so in practice. Nevertheless, it seems obvious to me that Rabbi Akiva Eiger also agrees that where the gap is so large that it is recognizable and visible to every eye from a distance, then we see that its form has been entirely lost, and it is no better than a tzadi written as yud and nun, for which a child does not help. I saw something similar in later authorities, who cite Responsa Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 16: if from a distance one recognizes that there is a space and it does not reach the body of the letter, according to the Beis Yosef in siman 36 at a distance of four cubits, and according to the Bach at a distance of one cubit, certainly one should not rely on a child. But if, at each authority's relevant distance, we see that it is an alef or shin, and only when we bring our eyes close do we see a space, and a child reads it as an alef or shin, we can properly rely on him.

<b> אם אותיות של שם וכו'</b> -  עיין במ"ב שכתב וה"מ שנדבקו וכו' נוב"י בחיו"ד בסימן קס"ט וכמ"ש שם הטעם דשמא הלכה כתי' ב' של הב"י דלא בעי מוקף גויל רק בעת הכתיבה ונ"ל פשוט דאם מסתפק אימת נעשה הדיבוק יכול להפרידו מטעם ס"ס דשמא נעשה בעת הכתיבה ואת"ל דנעשה אח"כ שמא הלכה כתירוץ א' של הב"י לעיל בסט"ז דבנגיעה אין חילוק ואפילו להמחמירין בס"ס באיסור מחיקה יש להקל בזה לפי מה שכתב שם הנוב"י דבמוחק מקצת אות ועדיין נשאר צורתו עליו אין חייב בזה משום מוחק ורק מדרבנן ע"ש. אח"כ מצאתי כן בעזה"י בספר ישועות יעקב עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If the letters of a Divine Name, etc.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, which wrote that this applies only when they became attached, etc. This is from Noda BiYehuda, Yoreh Deah, siman 169, and as he wrote there, the reason is that perhaps the law follows the Beis Yosef's second answer, that surrounding blank parchment is required only at the time of writing. Afterward I found this, with Hashem's help, in Sefer Yeshuos Yaakov; see there.

See Magen Avraham; I omitted his answer because of the difficulty raised by the Baer Heitev, see there. The Pri Megadim also raised this difficulty. But two letters of a three-letter word, which are most of the word, one might have thought is forbidden, as the Gra indeed wrote according to the Rambam; therefore the Mechaber teaches that two letters are permitted in every case. This appears to be his intent.

<b> יעשה כל פרשיותיה</b> -  עיין במ"ב מש"כ בשם האחרונים לענין תפי' של ראש וא"כ לפי דבריהם אפילו אם התחיל ראש כל פרשה באמצע שיטה כשר אף דבעלמא היא סתומה להרמב"ם אבל לפי דברי הפמ"ג בא"א אות מ"ט עי"ש אפשר דיש להחמיר בזה אפילו בדיעבד דהלא עי"ז באותו קלף גופא ניכר שהיא סתומה ומה לנו לקלף פרשה הקודמת לה אך אם לא הניח חלק ט' אותיות בסוף כל פרשה בתש"ר נל"פ דיש להקל בזה כסתימת שארי אחרונים אף דגם בזה מפקפק הפמ"ג שם ומטעם דכיון דכל פרשה הוא בקלף בפ"ע נחשבת כפרשת קדש בתש"י דנחשבת פתוחה מצד שאין כתוב לפניה כלום בתפילין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should make all its passages.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, which cites the later authorities regarding head tefillin. According to their words, even if he began the start of each passage in the middle of a line it is valid, although generally, according to the Rambam, that makes it a closed passage. But according to the words of the Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, letter 49, see there, perhaps one should be stringent in this even after the fact, since on that very parchment it is recognizable as a closed passage, and what difference does the parchment of the previous passage make to us? <b> מניחים חלק כדי לכתוב ט"א</b> -  ואם שכח להניח כמלא ט"א בסוף פרשת קדש ופרשת והיה כי יביאך מבואר בלבוש דפסול וכתב הא"ר דמיירי הכא בשעבר והתחיל שיטה ראשונה בראש הדף לכן הוא פסול דתו אין כאן פתוחה אבל לכתחלה עצתו כשנזדמן לו כן שיתחיל לכתוב בתחלת שיטה שניה שהיא נמי פתוחה לדעת הרמב"ם ואף דלא ישתייר מנין השורות הנזכרים בסימן ל"ה הא קי"ל שם דאם שינה לא פסל. אכן לפי מה שראיתי עתה בספר משנת אברהם שכתב דהיכא דמשייר ריוח שני שיטין בין פרשה לפרשה אף לדעת הרא"ש ז"ל היא פתוחה נ"ל בזה שישייר ריוח בתחלת הפרשה שני שיטין דאם ישייר רק שיטה א' הלא לדעת הרא"ש תפילין שלו הם פסולין דהיא פרשה סתומה והשלשה פרשיות בעינן שיהא פתוחות לעיכובא לד"ה כמו שכתבו האחרונים ואף דבדיעבד אם לא שייר רק ריוח שיטה א' אין לפסול התפילין דבעיקר הדין קי"ל כהרמב"ם בזה מ"מ לכתחלה בודאי י"ש יצא ידי הכל. כתב הפמ"ג יש לשאול אם הניח חלק ט"א ואכלוה עכברים או תולעת מקום החלק ואין שם קלף חלק כלל אם כשר כה"ג או לאו ע"ש דמסתפק בזה גם בתש"ר ולפי מה שכ' לעיל בתחלת הסעיף בשם האחרונים בש"ר בודאי יש להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They leave blank space enough to write nine letters.</b> If he forgot to leave a full nine-letter space at the end of the passage "Kadesh" and the passage "Vehayah ki yeviacha," the Levush explains that it is invalid. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that here he speaks where he already went on and began the first line at the top of the page; therefore it is invalid, for there is no open passage here anymore. But initially, when this happens, his solution is to begin writing at the start of the second line, which is also open according to the Rambam. Even though the number of lines mentioned in siman 35 will not remain, we rule there that if he changed it, he has not invalidated it. However, from what I have now seen in Sefer Mishnas Avraham, who wrote that where one leaves a space of two lines between one passage and another it is open even according to the Rosh, it appears to me in this case that he should leave a space of two lines at the beginning of the passage. If he leaves only one line, according to the Rosh his tefillin are invalid, for it is a closed passage, and the three passages must be open indispensably according to all opinions, as the later authorities wrote. Although after the fact, if he left only a one-line space, the tefillin should not be invalidated, for in the main law we rule here like the Rambam, nevertheless initially one who fears Heaven should certainly fulfill all opinions. The Pri Megadim wrote that one may ask: if he left a nine-letter space and mice or worms ate the blank area, so that no blank parchment remains there at all, is it valid in such a case or not? See there, where he is uncertain about this even regarding head tefillin. According to what I wrote above at the beginning of the se'if in the name of the later authorities regarding head tefillin, one should certainly be lenient.

<b> ואם מניחים הוא פחות וכו'</b> -  לאו לעיכובא קאמר אלא דלכתחלה ס"ל דלא נכון לעשות כן דזה מורה קצת על פרשה שלאחריה שתהיה פתוחה אבל בדיעבד אפילו אם הניח בה חלק כדי ג"פ אשר אפ"ה לא נתבטל שם סתומה מפרשת והיה א"ש כיון שלא התחילה בראש שיטה דכלל הדבר להרמב"ם דנקטינן כוותיה העיקר להלכה דסתומה היא כל שמתחלת מאמצע שיטה כן כתוב בספר מאמר מרדכי ושארי אחרונים וכן מוכח בב"י ביו"ד סימן ער"ה במש"כ בשם הר"י אכסנדרני שם וכן בביאור הגר"א כאן. ודע דבזה דאיירינן השתא ששייר בסוף שמע כדי ט' אותיות דעת רוב האחרונים וכמעט כולם [אפילו אותם החולקים על הט"ז וס"ל דלא מהני צירוף] שאפילו אם לא הניח ריוח בתחלת והיה א"ש כדי ט' אותיות אפ"ה מקרי סתומה לדעת הרמב"ם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if they leave it less, etc.</b> He does not say this as indispensable, but rather that initially he holds it is not proper to do so, because this somewhat indicates that the following passage will be open. But after the fact, even if he left in it a blank space enough for three words of "asher," nevertheless the status of closed passage has not been removed from "Vehayah im shamo'a," since it did not begin at the head of the line. The general rule, according to the Rambam, whom we follow as the main law, is that a closed passage is any passage that begins in the middle of a line. So it is written in Sefer Maamar Mordechai and the other later authorities, and so is proven in the Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 275, from what he wrote there in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari, and likewise in the Gra's commentary here. Know that in the case now under discussion, where he left at the end of Shema enough space for nine letters, the opinion of most later authorities, almost all of them, even those who disagree with the Taz and hold that combining spaces does not help, is that even if he did not leave a nine-letter space at the beginning of "Vehayah im shamo'a," it is nevertheless called closed according to the Rambam.

<b> לדעת הרמב"ם</b> -  עיין במ"ב במה שכתבנו בשם הט"ז בזה והגר"ז כתב דלכתחלה יש למנוע מלעשות כעצת הט"ז וכתב הטעם דמהרבה פוסקים מוכח דלא ס"ל כלל הסברא דצירוף [ור"ל מדכתבו שאין תקנה לעשות סתומה לד"ה וגם מה שהקשה הב"י בהלכות מזוזה סי' רפ"ח על הא דהרא"ש והטור שם אבל א"כ יהיה ראיה מהרא"ש והטור לט"ז וע"כ דכוונתו מהאחרונים שבסימן זה שלא מצאו עצה לעשות כד"ה] וא"כ אין כאן פתוחה ולא סתומה ופסולה ומוטב לעשות כעצת השו"ע שיהיה עכ"פ כשר בדיעבד בודאי וכן בספר מאמר מרדכי מפקפק בדינו של הט"ז מטעם דמסתפק שם אם מהני צירוף. ומ"מ בודאי אין למנוע הנוהג כהט"ז כי יש ראיה לדינו מהרא"ש והטור כמו שכתב הגר"א וכן הגאון רמ"ע מפאנו כ"כ והובא בחידושי רע"א ושארי אחרונים כמו שכתבתי במ"ב. והנה כ"ז שכ' בהשו"ע הוא לענין תפילין דרש"י ולענין תפילין של ר"ת בענין פתוחות וסתומות דלדידיה פרשה ד' הוא קודם לפ' ג' ג"כ יתנהג באחד מאלו הג' אופנים דהיינו או שיעשה כולן פתוחות וכמש"כ במ"ב ס"ק קס"א וכמו שאנו נוהגין במדינתינו לענין תפילין דרש"י וכמו שכתב בהג"ה והנוהג כהמחבר יעשה כך פרשת קדש והיה כי יביאך מתחיל בראש שיטה ובסוף קדש לי מניחים חלק ט' אותיות ובסוף והיה כי יביאך אין מניחים חלק ואם מניחים הוא פחות מט' אותיות ואח"כ מתחיל פרשת שמע בקצה הקלף דהוא בעמוד הרביעי דהא בעינן כסדרן וכשכותבה הוא מתחילה בראש שיטה ואח"כ חוזר לעמוד ג' לכתוב פרשת והיה א"ש ומתחילה באמצע שיטה עליונה שמניח לפניה חלק ט' אותיות ובסופה משייר חלק כדי ט' אותיות כדי שפרשת שמע שאחריה תהיה פתוחה עי"ז נמצא שפרשת קדש לי ופרשת והיה כי יביאך ופרשת שמע הם פתוחות בין להרמב"ם ובין להרא"ש ופרשת והיה א"ש היא סתומה לדעת הרמב"ם ולהרב ט"ז ג"כ הכל כנ"ל רק דלדידיה ישייר בסוף והיה כי יביאך פחות מכדי ט' אותיות קטנות וכן בריש פרשת והיה א"ש יניח ריוח פחות מכדי ט' אותיות קטנות שעי"ז תקרא פרשת והיה אם שמוע סתומה לכו"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>According to the Rambam.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote in the name of the Taz about this. The GraZ wrote that initially one should avoid following the Taz's advice. He gives the reason that it is clear from many poskim that they do not accept the concept of combining spaces at all, meaning from the fact that they wrote that there is no way to make a closed passage according to all opinions. Also, the difficulty that the Beis Yosef raised in Hilchos Mezuzah, siman 288, against the Rosh and Tur there would otherwise be a proof from the Rosh and Tur to the Taz. Therefore his intent must be from the later authorities in this siman, who found no solution that satisfies all opinions. If so, there is here neither an open nor a closed passage, and it is invalid; it is better to follow the Shulchan Aruch's advice, so that at least it is certainly valid after the fact. Sefer Maamar Mordechai also questions the Taz's ruling because he is uncertain whether combining helps. Nevertheless, one should certainly not prevent someone who follows the Taz, for the Gra wrote that there is proof to his ruling from the Rosh and Tur, and so too the Gaon Rabbi Menachem Azariah of Fano wrote, as cited in Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae, and other later authorities, as I wrote in the Mishnah Berurah. All that is written in the Shulchan Aruch concerns Rashi tefillin. Regarding Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, with respect to open and closed passages, since according to him the fourth passage precedes the third, one should likewise follow one of these three methods. Either he should make them all open, as written in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 161, which is our practice in our country for Rashi tefillin and as written in the gloss. One who follows the Mechaber should do as follows: the passages "Kadesh" and "Vehayah ki yeviacha" begin at the head of a line; at the end of "Kadesh li" one leaves a nine-letter space; at the end of "Vehayah ki yeviacha" one leaves no space, and if he leaves any, it is less than nine letters. Then he begins the passage "Shema" at the edge of the parchment, which is the fourth column, since we require the writing to be in order; when he writes it, he begins at the head of a line. Afterward he returns to the third column to write the passage "Vehayah im shamo'a," beginning in the middle of the upper line, leaving before it a nine-letter space, and at its end leaving a nine-letter space so that the following passage, "Shema," will thereby be open. It turns out that "Kadesh li," "Vehayah ki yeviacha," and "Shema" are open both according to the Rambam and according to the Rosh, while "Vehayah im shamo'a" is closed according to the Rambam. According to the Taz as well everything is as above, except that according to him one should leave at the end of "Vehayah ki yeviacha" less than a nine-letter space, and likewise at the beginning of "Vehayah im shamo'a" leave less than a nine-letter space, through which "Vehayah im shamo'a" is called closed according to all opinions.

<b> עור הבתים</b> -  עיין במ"ב במש"כ וה"ה עור התיתורא וכו' ע"כ הסופרים שמניחים בין הבית להתיתורא עורות ישנים אשר מאשפות ירימו וממנעלים קרועים ידעו כי עבירה היא בידם אחרי שהוא מחובר להבית צריך להיות דוקא מעור כשר כמוהו ממש כ"כ בספר לשכת הסופר ולכתחלה בודאי הדין עמו אך לענין דיעבד יש לעיין לפי מה דכתב הפמ"ג לקמן בסעיף מ"ז דמה דהצריך רמ"א לדבק כשר הוא למצוה ולא לעיכובא כיון דהדיבוק אינו מעכב ממילא אין קפידא במה מדבק וא"כ לכאורה ה"נ בענינינו לא דמי לעור התיתורא והמעברתא דחיובן הוא מצד הדין ע"כ בעינן שיהיו טהורים משא"כ בזה וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The leather of the compartments.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote: and the same applies to the leather of the titura, etc. Therefore scribes who place between the compartment and the titura old pieces of leather that they have lifted from refuse piles or from torn shoes should know that they are committing a transgression. Since this leather is attached to the compartment, it too must specifically be from valid leather exactly like it. So wrote Sefer Lishkas HaSofer, and initially the law is certainly with him. But after the fact this requires study, according to what the Pri Megadim wrote below in se'if 47, that the Rama's requirement that the glue be valid is for the mitzvah but not indispensable, since the gluing itself is not indispensable, and therefore there is no concern with what he glues with. If so, seemingly the same applies in our case; it is not comparable to the leather of the titura and ma'avarta, whose obligation is by law and therefore they must be from a kosher species. This requires study.

<b> מעור בהמה וכו'</b> -  ועיין בד"מ שכתב דאין עושין מעור הדג משום דאיבעיא דלא איפשטא בשבת ק"ח ולכאורה יש לעיין דהלא הרמב"ם כתב הטעם שאין כותבין על עור הדג מפני שאין הזוהמא פוסקת בעיבודה משמע מזה דשאר עור פוסק הזוהמא על ידי העיבוד וא"כ הרמב"ם שפוסק דעור הבית מותר על עור שאינו מעובד יהיה מותר אפילו על עור הדג ויש לדחות:

<b> טהורים</b> -  עיין במ"ב ע"כ אם נתהוה לו איזה ספק בעור הבתים אם הוא טהור יש להחמיר דספיקא דאורייתא הוא ואפילו ברצועות יש להחמיר פמ"ג בס"ק כ"ז ומשמע שחזר ממה שכתב בפתיחתו להלכות תפילין בענין זה ע"ש. גם מה שכתב שם בפתיחה דעור הבית ש"י שצריך להיות מעור טהורה הוא מדרבנן בעלמא גם זה אינו לענ"ד דלפ"ז הו"ל להגמרא לתרץ דקאי לש"י דאין בו שי"ן ומש"כ הפמ"ג בזה הוא דוחק גדול אלא נראה פשוט דס"ל להגמרא דכיון דהל"מ הוא אפילו על התפירות שיהיו נתפרין הבתים דוקא בגידין טהורין כ"ש על הבתים גופייהו שיהיו מעור טהור ולא צריך לזה הא דרב יוסף וכעין זה כתב רש"י מנחות מ"ב ע"ב ד"ה פסולות עי"ש ואין זה בכלל אין דנין ק"ו מהלכה דגילוי מילתא בעלמא הוא ואחר דקמקשה הגמרא והאמר אביי שי"ן של תפילין וכו' היה יכול לתרץ דקאי על ש"י ולהקשות ע"ז מהברייתא דקמקשה השתא אך לא רצה להאריך בזה וכמו שכתבו כעין זה בכללי הש"ס עיין בגליון הש"ס להגרע"א והנלענ"ד כתבתי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Kosher species.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah. Therefore, if any doubt arises regarding the leather of the compartments, whether it is from a kosher species, one should be stringent, for it is a biblical doubt. Even regarding the straps one should be stringent, as the Pri Megadim wrote in s.k. 27, and it appears that he retracted what he wrote in his introduction to Hilchos Tefillin on this matter; see there. Also what he wrote there in the introduction, that the leather of the hand tefillin compartment must be from a kosher species only rabbinically, is not correct in my humble opinion. According to that, the Gemara should have answered that it refers to hand tefillin, which do not have a shin. What the Pri Megadim wrote about this is very forced. Rather, it seems obvious that the Gemara holds that since it is a halachah leMoshe miSinai even regarding the stitching, that the compartments must specifically be sewn with sinews from kosher animals, all the more so the compartments themselves must be from the leather of a kosher animal. For this we do not need Rav Yosef's statement. Rashi wrote similarly in Menachos 42b, under "invalid"; see there. This is not included in the rule that we do not derive an a fortiori inference from a halachah leMoshe miSinai, for it is merely revealing the matter. After the Gemara challenges, "but did Abaye not say: the shin of tefillin, etc.," it could have answered that the case concerns hand tefillin, and then challenged from the baraisa as it does now; it simply did not wish to lengthen the discussion, as similar points are written in the rules of the Talmud. See Gilyon HaShas of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. I have written what appears to me.

<b> היכא דאפשר</b> -  עיין במ"א שכתב דאם אין לו קלף אחר יכול לברך ע"ז דכדאי הוא הרמב"ם לסמוך עליו ולברך דגם רש"י ס"ל כן עי"ש. ולכאורה אין מיקל המ"א לענין ברכה רק באם הי' מעובד ושלא לשמה אבל לא בשאינו מעובד כלל דזה לא מצינו לרש"י. איברא ממה דגילה לנו הגר"א את מקורו דהרמב"ם מהא דעירובין צ"ו דעור הבתים הוא כעין עור קמיע שהוא עור שאינו מעובד כלל וחזינן מזה דסוגיין כת"ק דרשב"ג ולא כרשב"ג עי"ש א"כ ממילא מוכח דלרש"י שהוא פוסק כת"ק דרשב"ג מותר אפילו עור שאינו מעובד כלל דאל"ה אמאן תרמיי' האי סוגיא דעירובין. מ"מ לענ"ד צ"ע אעיקרא דדינא אם יש לסמוך על המ"א שמיקל לענין ברכה אף שהברכי יוסף הסכים עמו ודלא כב"ח וא"ר דהלא הגר"א כתב בביאורו בסימן ל"ג סוף ס"ג דדברי הרמב"ם שמיקל בזה לענין לשמה לכאורה צ"ע דהלא הוא עצמו כתב אם חיפן עו"ג להבתים או תפרן פסול מפני שעשייתן ככתיבתן מפני השי"ן [ור"ל א"כ הכא נמי יהיה העור מעובד לשמה מפני השי"ן] ותירץ ע"ז דהרמב"ם אזיל לשיטתו דלא מצריך עיבוד לכל כתב שבקדושה שהרי במזוזה לא מצריך עיבוד אלא בס"ת ובתפילין [וכוונת הגר"א עיבוד לשמה אינו מצריך במזוזה אבל עיבוד מצריך כמו שכתב הרמב"ם בהדיא בהלכות תפילין בפ"א] וא"כ לפ"ז לפי מאי דאנן פסקינן לקמן בסימן ל"ט ס"ב דעשיית הבתים הוא ככתיבה מפני השי"ן וכדאיתא בטור ופסקינן נמי ביו"ד רפ"ח לענין מזוזה דלא כוותי' דהרמב"ם דאף דהשו"ע שם כתב רק דצריכה עיבוד לשמה לכתחילה כל האחרונים חלקו ע"ז עיין בט"ז שם ואפילו להש"ך שם עכ"פ בלא ברכה וגם הגר"א כתב שם דכל הפוסקים חולקים אהרמב"ם א"כ למה נקיל הכא דיהא מותר לברך בעור שאינו מעובד לשמה וצ"ע למעשה. ואם יזדמן איזה ספק בהעיבוד דעור הבתים יש להקל דהוי כעין ס"ס כן משמע מהפמ"ג במ"ז ס"ק ל"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Where possible.</b> See Magen Avraham, who wrote that if he has no other parchment, he may recite a blessing over this, for the Rambam is worthy of being relied upon for a blessing, and Rashi also holds so; see there. At first glance, Magen Avraham is lenient about the blessing only where the leather was processed but not for its sake, but not where it was not processed at all, for this we do not find in Rashi. In truth, from what the Gra revealed to us, that the Rambam's source is from Eruvin 96, where the leather of the compartments is like the leather of an amulet, which is entirely unprocessed leather, and we see from this that our sugya follows the first Tanna of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and not Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, see there, it follows that according to Rashi, who rules like the first Tanna of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, even entirely unprocessed leather is permitted. Otherwise, to whom would that sugya in Eruvin apply? Nevertheless, in my humble opinion, the main law requires study: whether one may rely on the Magen Avraham who is lenient regarding a blessing, although the Birkei Yosef agreed with him and not like the Bach and Eliyah Rabbah. The Gra wrote in his commentary at the end of siman 33, se'if 3, that the Rambam's leniency regarding lishmah seemingly requires study, for the Rambam himself wrote that if a non-Jew covered the compartments or sewed them, it is invalid, because their making is like their writing on account of the shin. That is to say, if so, here too the leather should require processing for its sake on account of the shin. He answers that the Rambam follows his own position, that he does not require processing for every sacred writing, for regarding mezuzah he does not require processing, only regarding a Sefer Torah and tefillin. The Gra's intent is that he does not require processing for its sake for mezuzah, although he does require processing, as the Rambam explicitly wrote in Hilchos Tefillin, chapter 1. If so, according to what we rule below in siman 39, se'if 2, that making the compartments is like writing because of the shin, as stated in the Tur, and we also rule in Yoreh Deah 288 regarding mezuzah not like the Rambam, for although the Shulchan Aruch there wrote only that it requires processing for its sake initially, all the later authorities disagreed, see the Taz there; and even according to the Shach there, at least it would be without a blessing. The Gra also wrote there that all the poskim disagree with the Rambam. If so, why should we be lenient here to allow a blessing over leather not processed for its sake? This requires study in practice. If some doubt arises regarding the processing of the leather of the compartments, one may be lenient, for it is like a double doubt. So it implies from the Pri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, s.k. 31.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should make four compartments, etc.</b> See Magen Avraham in the name of the Bach, that through sewing it is not considered one piece of leather. See Machatzis HaShekel, that the same applies to gluing according to the Bach. In the glosses of Rabbi Chaim Sanzer, he wrote that gluing is effective according to all opinions, while in Chasam Sofer, which I cited in the Mishnah Berurah, he wrote the opposite. According to what is found in the Or Zarua it appears that the two are equal, as will be explained later. Machatzis HaShekel wrote that the same applies to compartments made from one piece of leather, but strips are cut from it while they remain attached in the middle, and then the strips are joined together again, thereby forming the compartments: according to the Bach, they are invalid, since at any rate they are cut on every side of the compartment, and in his view sewing, and likewise gluing, does not create a connection. However, the Pri Megadim wrote in s.k. 52: It is proper to make everything from literally one piece of leather; after the fact, the scribes cut above and below while leaving it attached. It appears to me to bring proof for the scribes that this is called one piece of leather and is not included in "two or three memorials." The entire force of those who are stringent and hold that one piece of leather is indispensable is because perhaps tefillin are comparable to a get. Regarding a get, Even HaEzer, siman 130, se'if 7, in the gloss, rules that gluing is a possible invalidation because the Torah said "one scroll," not two or three scrolls; the same would apply in our matter. Yet regarding gittin themselves, the Gemara on folio 20b concludes that where it remains attached it is permitted, because it is still called one scroll. Therefore it appears that here, according to all opinions, sewing or gluing helps, and it is not like the case Machatzis HaShekel cited at the beginning of siman 33 in the name of Mordechai, for there it remained that way without repair. I found in Pischei Teshuvah on Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 33 that he too inclines this way, though he did not bring our proof. Accordingly it is obvious that even if a small hole was made in the leather at the place of the square, as commonly happens, nevertheless the name of one piece of leather is not lost through this, and even if the square was thereby spoiled, later repair helps.

<b>From one piece of leather.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that there are those who are stringent that it is not considered a connection. This refers to the Bach, and likewise Sefer Biur Mordechai and Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 11, incline to say that it is uncertain whether gluing is a connection. Their proof is from the Rama's ruling in Even HaEzer, siman 130, in the gloss, in the name of Or Zarua, regarding a get, that it is a doubtful divorce; presumably the same applies in our case. But now that we have merited the light of the great Or Zarua printed in our days, I found in siman 176 that if one wrote tefillin on two pages on one piece of leather, and even on two pieces of leather and joined them together either by sewing or by glue, it is valid. There too it is in the name of Or Zarua. It must be that he distinguishes between a get and tefillin. Afterward I searched further and found in this siman in Darchei Moshe that he cites Or Zarua as follows: Or Zarua wrote that if one wrote tefillin on two pages on one piece of leather, and even on two pieces of leather and glued them together, it is valid. Yet there in Even HaEzer 130, se'if 7, the Rama in his Darchei Moshe and in the Shulchan Aruch cites Or Zarua regarding a get, that it is uncertain. It must be that there is a distinction between a get and tefillin, or that there, because of the stringency of a married woman, Or Zarua did not want to rely on the proofs he brought in siman 176. Rashi in Megillah 8b also seems, at first glance, to imply that sewing with sinews helps for tefillin and mezuzos, and this must concern the passages, for if it concerns sewing the tefillin themselves after the passages have been placed inside, what relevance would mezuzah have? Perhaps, however, Rashi mentioned mezuzah not precisely. But in Ritva there in Megillah I found explicitly that sewing two skins helps both for tefillin and for mezuzah. Certainly in our matter, making the compartments is no worse than the passages themselves, for which joining helps, as is proven in Biur HaGra. Therefore, although in Yoreh Deah, siman 288, se'if 4, the Mechaber ruled anonymously like the Rambam that sewing does not help for mezuzah, nevertheless one who makes the compartments by sewing or gluing has authorities upon whom to rely: Sefer HaTerumah, who concludes that it is only good and proper to make them from one piece, the great Or Zarua cited by the Darchei Moshe as law in this siman, the Ritva in Megillah, and also according to one answer of Tosafos in Menachos. It may be that even the Rambam, who holds that sewing does not help for the passages of a mezuzah, nevertheless agrees regarding making the compartments, that joining them by sewing is not considered two or three memorials. In my humble opinion, one could further say that when the Torah excluded "one memorial" and not two or three memorials, it excluded only making four compartments from four skins, each skin entirely by itself, meaning the compartment with its titura for one passage, and so for each passage, so that each compartment is a separate memorial for the person. That is what the Torah excluded. The intent of Chazal in saying "one leather" is that all the compartments be fixed in one leather, and that each compartment not be an independent pouch. This is somewhat implied by the wording of the Mechilta. But this does not seem to follow from the Rishonim; for if so, even without sewing one compartment to another, merely fixing them in one titura would be considered one memorial, and no posek mentions such a leniency. On the contrary, Rashi and the other poskim each depict making it from literally one piece of leather, which indicates that once the Gemara mentions one leather, they hold it must be literally one leather, or at least joined by sewing according to the other poskim. The Gra in his commentary to Kelim, chapter 18, also wrote that sewing can make a connection, although in his commentary in this siman he seems somewhat inclined to stringency. See Kovetz on the Rambam, who discusses this from a baraisa in Gittin, where it is a dispute between Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar and the Rabbis, and the law follows the Rabbis that it is not considered a connection. See Yeshuos Yaakov in the gloss of his grandson, who wrote about this. One may also reject that proof, for it is an anonymous mishnah against a dispute in a baraisa, and the law follows an anonymous mishnah; this requires study in the rules of halachah. Nevertheless, initially, one who can do so should certainly be stringent and make it from one piece of leather in order to satisfy all concerns in a biblical positive commandment.

<b>Their squareness was spoiled.</b> See the Mishnah Berurah. Know that the boundary for spoiling the squareness of the compartments is obvious to me: we do not require that most of their height be spoiled; even a minority suffices, even if he made them very large. We do not say: remove the excess from here and the required measure of tefillin remains without it, similar to those who are lenient regarding surrounding blank parchment when there is enough of the letter without it. I say this from what we rule in Zevachim 62a, that the squareness of the altar is indispensable, and we also rule that even a nick in the altar invalidates it, because of squareness, as Tosafos in Sukkah 49a, under "for all," writes. We do not require specifically that most of the altar be nicked, but even an olive-bulk or a handbreadth, each master according to his view, as in Chullin 18a; see the Gemara there. One should not say that there the reason is because the altar has a fixed measure, unlike tefillin whose height has no measure, for this is not so. There too we rule that the measure of its height is not indispensable, as stated in that Gemara. If so, if he had made its height only up to above the place of the surrounding ledge and made the horns there as well, he would have fulfilled after the fact; nevertheless, once he built it to this height, its top must be square, as is proven in Zevachim 54a, Tosafos under "Rav said." The same applies in our matter regarding tefillin. It is also obvious to me that this is not limited to a spoilage at the upper corner-point; the same applies if it was spoiled or nicked elsewhere on the outer walls of the compartment, so that its squareness was lost thereby. This too follows from the above matter of the altar, for it is obvious that an invalidating nick in the altar applies anywhere in its walls, not only at a corner-point. Accordingly, if because of the tefillin's great age it is evident to all that one of its walls has sunk inward and does not stand in its proper place, it appears that they must be repaired. It is also obvious to me that a small nick formed in the compartments, the size of a fingernail-catching nick, and perhaps even somewhat more, does not thereby remove the name of square from them. Even if we say that at the time of making, the scribe must be careful about even the slightest deficiency anywhere, for at any rate it is not perfectly square, nevertheless after the fact, once it was spoiled, the name of square is not lost because of the slightest deficiency. The proof is from Chullin 18, where the Gemara answers: this is with stone, that is with lime. With stone, since the verse says "whole stones," even a fingernail-catching nick invalidates; but with lime, where "whole" is not written, a significant deficiency such as a handbreadth or olive-bulk is required; see there. Thus even regarding the altar, where we rule that squareness is indispensable and where it must be fully square, equal even in its diagonal, as proven in Zevachim 62b from "around," meaning all its surroundings must be equal, as Rashi explains there, nevertheless it is valid with lime. Therefore, a fingernail-catching nick does not cause it to lose the name square. This is so even if the small nick in the tefillin occurred at the upper corner-point; otherwise the Gemara could have answered that both cases were with lime, and here it speaks at the corner-point. Although one could distinguish that regarding the large altar a minor nick is not considered sufficient to remove its status as square, unlike in our matter, nevertheless in my humble opinion the law is as I have stated. Otherwise, Rav Chisda there should have listed another nick that invalidates at the size of a fingernail-catching nick, namely the square of tefillin, similar to what the Taz wrote in siman 645, se'if 4; see there. What remains uncertain to me is the actual measure of spoilage of the square, how much is called spoilage in our matter. It is clear that regarding tefillin we cannot require that the deficiency of the square be a handbreadth, as the Rambam ruled there regarding the altar, for the entire tefillin do not have that size. Perhaps if the square of the tefillin became crushed slightly more than a fingernail-catching nick, in our matter this is also called spoilage of the square; this requires study.

<b> חריץ שבין בית לבית וכו' ואם לא הגיע כשרה</b> -  עיין בח"א דמשמע מיניה דאפילו הנוהגין לעשות התפילין ע"י דיבוק הד' עורות אפ"ה מהני בדיעבד אם מדבק לבסוף הד' בתים ביחד ומשייר מלמעלה רק מעט שיהיו החריצין ניכרין אך דמצריך הח"א ליזהר שיהיו החריצין עכ"פ בעצם הבדלת הבתים ולא בהגלאנץ לבד ובתשובת ח"ס סימן ה' משמע דהנוהגין לעשות תפיליהן ע"י דיבוק לא מהני אפילו החריצין ניכרות אלא יהיו מובדלות לגמרי ואז יזהר שיהיה ריבוען קיים כי מצוי שע"י ההבדל נאבד הריבוע ומנהג העולם במדינתינו כהח"א וכן משמע בספר ישועות יעקב ומ"מ כל י"ש יזהר לכתחלה לעשות הבתים נפרדים וכמו שכתב הח"א גופא דלכתחילה פשיטא דנכון לעשות כן. והנה אם לא יהיו מדובקים כלל מצוי לפעמים שנתקלקל הריבוע בהמשך הזמן עי"ז ע"כ אפשר שטוב יותר שידבק מעט בשיפולי הבית אצל תיתורא בין בית לבית והנה יש עוד אופן שידבק מעט העורות שבין בית לבית ואם העורות לא יגיעו זה לזה יתן לתוכם חתיכת קלף דק עם דבק שיחברם יחדיו וצדי הבתים וכן בראשי הבתים לא יהיה מדובק כלל ובזה יהיה עדיין כל החריצים מינכר היטב וגם ריבוען יהיה קיים אך באופן זה צריך ליזהר מאוד תמיד שלא לנתק בחוזק בית אחד מחבירו כדי שלא יקרע עי"ז העור שבין בית לבית:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The groove between one compartment and another, etc.; and if it did not reach, it is valid.</b> See Chayei Adam, which implies that even those who customarily make tefillin by gluing the four pieces of leather, nevertheless after the fact it helps if one finally glues the four compartments together and leaves only a little at the top so that the grooves are recognizable. However, Chayei Adam requires care that the grooves at least be in the actual separation of the compartments, not in the glaze alone. In Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 5, it implies that for those who make their tefillin by gluing, recognizable grooves do not help; rather, they must be completely separated, and then one must be careful that their square remains intact, for it is common that through separation the square is lost. The practice of the world in our country follows Chayei Adam, and so it also implies in Sefer Yeshuos Yaakov. Nevertheless, every person who fears Heaven should initially be careful to make the compartments separate, as Chayei Adam himself wrote, that initially this is certainly proper. If they are not glued at all, sometimes the square is spoiled over time because of this; therefore perhaps it is better to glue a little at the lower edge of the compartment near the titura, between one compartment and another. There is also another method: to glue slightly the leathers that are between one compartment and another, and if the leathers do not reach each other, to place between them a thin piece of parchment with glue that connects them together, while the sides of the compartments and the tops of the compartments are not glued at all. In this way all the grooves are still clearly recognizable and their square remains intact. But in this method one must always be very careful not to pull one compartment forcefully away from another, so that the leather between the compartments not tear thereby.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The length and width of the compartments, etc.</b> See Eruvin 93b in the Gemara, and study carefully the Rosh in Hilchos Ketanos, in the brief order for preparing tefillin, what he wrote regarding the measure of tefillin, and the Biur HaGra in this se'if. In any event, in my humble opinion, even according to the Shulchan Aruch, who ruled anonymously as law that they have no measure, nevertheless since it is proven there in the Gemara that on the head, and likewise on the arm, there is room only to place two tefillin and not three, one must at least be careful not to wear very small tefillin at one time and tefillin three times as large as the first ones at another time. For example, if the first time they were one and a half fingerbreadths by one and a half fingerbreadths, and the second time they were four and a half fingerbreadths by four and a half fingerbreadths, and the like, then either way there is a problem: either the first tefillin were smaller than the required measure, or the second tefillin were placed outside the place of tefillin. See below, siman 233, se'if 1, that one should not create two contradictory practices even not on one day. Although one could reject this by saying that in the time of the Gemara their tefillin were medium-sized, about two fingerbreadths or more, unlike this case, if so the Rosh's proof there falls away, and regarding the Shulchan Aruch's ruling not like the Rosh, the later authorities did not mention that it was for this reason, but rather for another reason; see Beis Yosef and Biur HaGra. The proper practice is that a person's tefillin should always be medium-sized, that is, not less than two fingerbreadths by two fingerbreadths and not more than four by four fingerbreadths, including the titura and ma'avarta. In this way, at least as a matter of law, he fulfills all opinions.
