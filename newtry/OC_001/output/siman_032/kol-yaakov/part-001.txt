<b>ב) שם ופרשת שמע וכו'</b> כתב בספר הכונות בדרושי ק"ש דרוש ו' בשם האר"י ז"ל שצריך לכתוב בתפילין ובמזוזה דל"ת זו דאחד גדולה ועבה כשיעור ד' דלתי"ן קטנות. והביאו מג"א סע"ק א', ואם לא כתב דל"ת זו גדולה כשיעור ד' דלתי"ן מאותו כתב, רק כתבה גדולה משאר אותיות, כל שיש בה שיעור ד' דלתי"ן קטנים מאד סגי. פרמ"ג, אשל אברהם אות א', ועיין בדברינו ליו"ד סי' ער"ד אות י"ז וסי' ער"ה אות כ"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There, and the passage of Shema, etc.</b> In Sefer HaKavvanos, in the discourses on the recitation of Shema, discourse 6, it is written in the name of the Arizal that in tefillin and mezuzah one must write this dalet of the word Echad large and thick, the size of four small dalets. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, cites this. If one did not write this dalet so large as four dalets of that same script, but only wrote it larger than the other letters, it is sufficient so long as it contains the measure of four very small dalets. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 1. See our words in Yoreh De'ah, siman 274, letter 17, and siman 275, letter 24.

<b>ג) שם בהגה וצריך לכותבם בסדר הזה וכו'</b> ואין חילוק בין ש"י לש"ר, כן פסק מרן בב"י להלכה, ודלא כהגהות סמ"ק דכתב בשם רבינו יחיאל דבש"ר שהם ד' קלפים אין להקפיד בסדר כתיבתן, ואע"ג דהרשב"ש סי' תרל"ב החזיק בדברי הגהות סמ"ק, אין הלכה כן. מחב"ר אות ב', חק"ל סי' ז', זכ"ל אות ת', שע"ת אות ג', וכ"כ מלא"ש כלל ח' אות א' בחכמה קה"ס סי' ט' אות א', אמרי שפר כלל כ' אות א' בסתם ולא חילקו בין ש"י לש"ר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There in the gloss, and he must write them in this order, etc.</b> There is no distinction between the hand tefillin and the head tefillin. So Maran ruled as halachah in Beis Yosef, unlike Hagahos Semak, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yechiel that regarding head tefillin, since they are four parchments, one need not be particular about the order of their writing. Although Rashbash, siman 632, upheld the words of Hagahos Semak, the halachah is not so. Machazik Berachah, letter 2; Chikrei Lev, siman 7; Zecher LeAvraham, letter tav; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 3; and so wrote Meleches Shamayim, rule 8, letter 1, in Chochmah; Keset HaSofer, siman 9, letter 1; and Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1, in plain terms, without distinguishing between hand tefillin and head tefillin.

<b>ד) סופר</b> שכתב כמה פרשיות לזוגות תפילין ש"ר בב"א ונזהר בכתיבתן לכותבם על הסדר שיהיו כל ד' פרשיות של כל ראש כס' אך נתערבו אח"כ כל הפרשיות ולא ניכר בכל ד' שיקח מהם אם אלו שלקח כתובו' כס' דמשכחת לה דפ' ראשונ' שלקח היא כתובה אחרונ' לג' אלה המצורפות עמה וכיוצא, טעונים גניזה, מחב"ר אות ג', שע"ת אות ב', זכ"ל אמ"ש כלל כ' אות א', ואין להתיר מכח ס"ס, ספק שמא נזדמנו בידו כל הד' פרשיו' כסדרן ואת"ל לא נזדמנו כל הד' פרשיות כס' שמא הלכה כמ"ד לא בעינן ב"פ ש"ר כס' דכיון שנתערבו הפ' א"א שיזדמנו ד"פ יחד שנכתבו כסדר והוי האיסור מוחזק יותר מן ההיתר, וכה"ג לא עבדינן ס"ס כמ"ש הש"ך סי' ק"י בדיני ס"ס אות ל"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A scribe.</b> If he wrote many passages for pairs of head tefillin at one time, and was careful to write them in order so that all four passages of each head-tefillin set would be in order, but afterward all the passages became mixed together, and it is not recognizable in any group of four that he takes from them whether those he took were written in order, for it may be that the first passage he took was written last relative to the three joined with it, and the like, they require burial. Machazik Berachah, letter 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2; Zecher LeAvraham in Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1. One may not permit this by force of a double doubt, namely perhaps all four passages came to his hand in order, and even if not all four came in order, perhaps the halachah follows the view that we do not require the passages of head tefillin to be written in order. Since the passages became mixed, it is impossible that four passages written in order should come together, and the prohibition is more established than the permissibility. In such a case we do not make a double doubt, as the Shach writes in siman 110, in the laws of double doubt, letter 33.

<b>ז) שם כסדר הזה וכו'.</b> ואם לאחר שכ' כסדר נפסלו אחת או שתים מהפרשיות הראשונות, וידוע לו בברור שיש לו פרשיות אחרות דוגמת אלו שנפסלו שנכתבו קודם אלו האחרונות מותר לצרפם, ואע"פ שלא נכתבו הפרשיות מתחלה לצרפם לאלו הפרשיות כי אם לפרשיות אחרות, אבל אם יש לספק אם נכתבו מקודם אם לאו פסול, עבודת הגרשוכי סי' ס', א"ר אות א' י"א הגה"ט פרמ"ג מש"ז או' א' ישועות יעקב אות ח', שע"ת אות ב' מאמ"ר אות א' מלא"ש כלל ח' או' ב' בחכמה, קה"ס סי' ט' או' ב' בלשכ"ה אמ"ש כלל כ' או' א'. ואע"ג דהרב חק"ל בא"ח סי' ז' כתב דיש להשיב על עבודת הגרשוני מ"מ כיון דכל הפו' הנז' סברי כותיה הכי נקטינן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There, in this order, etc.</b> If after he wrote them in order one or two of the first passages became invalid, and he knows clearly that he has other passages corresponding to those that became invalid, which were written before these latter passages, he may join them. This is so even though at the outset those passages were not written in order to be joined to these passages, but to other passages. But if there is doubt whether they were written earlier or not, it is invalid. Avodas HaGershuni, siman 60; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Tur; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 1; Yeshuos Yaakov, letter 8; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2; Maamar Mordechai, letter 1; Meleches Shamayim, rule 8, letter 2, in Chochmah; Keset HaSofer, siman 9, letter 2, in Lishkas HaSofer; Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1. Although Chikrei Lev in Orach Chaim, siman 7, wrote that there is room to challenge Avodas HaGershuni, nevertheless, since all the later authorities mentioned agree with him, this is how we rule.

<b>There, and if he changed it, it is invalid.</b> If he wrote a passage that appears earlier in the Torah last, for example he wrote the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before the passage of Kadesh, he may write additional passages that are later than it and join them to it, that is, he writes Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and joins them to Kadesh. Likewise, if he wrote later passages first, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and afterward wrote Kadesh, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. If he cannot join them, they require burial. GraZ, letter 1. Some say that if he reversed the passages, writing Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh intending to place them in tefillin that way, even the passage of Kadesh is invalidated, and similarly with the second and third or third and fourth passages: from the reversal downward, all the passages are invalid and he can no longer join other passages to them. What was written before the invalidating act, however, such as where he wrote Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and then wrote Vehayah Im Shamoa before Shema, remains valid, and only from the reversal downward is invalidated. Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim, siman 7; Zecher LeAvraham wrote this briefly. It follows from the rabbi's words that the same applies if an error was found in the first or second passage of tefillin: from the error downward is invalid, and one cannot join other earlier-written passages to the final passages, since the latter passages were written in invalidity; there is no distinction between reversing one passage with another and writing after the error, for in both cases it was written in invalidity. This is our practice here in Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it, that if an error or invalidity is found in the first passage or in the first two, even if he has matching passages that were written earlier, we do not join them to the later passages because those later passages were written in invalidity. But if all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in the first passage or in the first two, and he has corresponding passages that were written before the latter ones, they may be joined, as stated in the preceding letter. If one of the earlier passages of head tefillin became invalid through adhesion or separation, and we do not know whether this occurred at the time of writing or after all four passages had been written validly, one may permit joining earlier-written replacement passages to the later passages on account of a double doubt: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages had been written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that head-tefillin passages need not be written in order, as above, letter 3. This double doubt is reversible. In addition, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ. It appears that for hand tefillin as well one may permit them in such a case, for there is an opinion in Mordechai that even with hand tefillin, if they were not written in order, one need not be particular, and Beis Yosef copied it in siman 34, as did the later authorities there. Thus one can make a double doubt in such a case: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages were written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that hand-tefillin passages need not be written in order; furthermore, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who does not accept the concept that they were written in invalidity, as he wrote that where earlier passages in the Torah were written last, one may write other later passages and join them to it, and where later passages were written first, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. This is specifically according to our custom. But in a place that has no custom, one may permit in all cases: whether all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in them, whether an error was found in the earlier passages such that the later passages were written in invalidity, or whether the passages were reversed, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh and has a Kadesh passage written before these passages, he may join it to Vehayah Ki Yeviacha; and if Kadesh was written last, he may write the later passages and join them to it. When an error is found in the earlier passages and he has corresponding passages written before these tefillin, he may join them to the later passages by force of a double doubt: perhaps the halachah follows the view that writing in order is not required, and even if the halachah follows the view that it is required, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who permits joining in all cases. This double doubt is reversible. Furthermore, perhaps Avodas HaGershuni permits joining in all cases, and the later authorities cited above brought him, leaving Chikrei Lev alone in the matter. Although we do not establish the halachah according to those views, nevertheless for a double doubt we act as Rav HaGaon wrote in Zivchei Tzedek, part 2, siman 110, in the laws of double doubt, letter 158, that we make a double doubt even from a rejected opinion and even against Maran and Rama. However, where there is a custom one should not be lenient, for it is difficult to annul a custom. See Baer Heitev, siman 551, s.k. 7, siman 690, s.k. 15, and Shulchan Aruch, siman 114.

<b>And what is written.</b> Regarding what Sefer HaKavvanos wrote, that if he interrupted between passages he should pass the quill over the first word of each passage already written, Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 3, in Shulei HaYeri'ah, wrote in the name of Bigdei Yesha that one should not do so; see there for the reason. It appears that this is how one should rule. The reason is that even from the rabbi's own words it appears that there is not such an obligation to pass the quill again over the earlier passages, for he wrote that it is considered somewhat joined, implying that it is not a complete joining. In addition, there is concern lest by passing the quill over, a letter change into another letter, for example a reish become a dalet, and he then fix it, so that he will have written out of order; he will come to repair and will end up ruining, Heaven forbid. Passing the quill over tefillin requires great craftsmanship, for the letters are fine, and a person can easily stumble, Heaven forbid. Therefore it is preferable to sit and not act. Rather, the correction is that if an event occurred and he could not complete all four passages together, then if he knows how to direct the intentions in writing tefillin, he should return and direct the general intentions of the passages that were written earlier. For example, if he wrote two passages of the head tefillin and two remained, when he returns to write the two, he should again have in mind that the 31 verses in the four tefillin passages correspond to etc., and the 21 mentions of the Divine Name correspond to etc.; the first passage has ten verses corresponding to etc., the second passage corresponds to etc.; and he should again have in mind that the four passages correspond to the four letters of the Name Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei, the first passage corresponding to the yud of Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei and to 72 and 161, the second passage to the hei and to 63 and 143, etc. Through this all four passages will be joined together. Keset HaSofer there wrote: In our time, however, it seems that they do not do even this, and no one is concerned about it. End quote. So too now in our time, we have not seen any scribes who are concerned about it. It appears that they are not particular about this because there is another correction: each passage must be wrapped with one piece of parchment and placed in its compartment, as Maran wrote in seif 44. The compartments can be filled in this way: the passage of Kadesh is wrapped with very thin parchment, the passage of VeHayah Ki is wrapped with thicker parchment, and likewise the passage of Shema is wrapped with thick parchment, and one also wraps more around it; through this all the compartments will be filled. The scribes rely on this when placing the passages into the compartments.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>51) There. That also on the inside it must be surrounded by blank parchment.</b> If the hole does not fill the entire hollow, even though the hole is immediately adjacent to the letter and no blank parchment at all remains between the letter and the hole inside it, nevertheless it is considered surrounded by blank parchment even at the place of the hole, by means of the blank parchment inside it on the other side of the hole. Taz, end of seif katan 8; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 22; GraZ, letter 19; Noda BiYehuda, first edition, Yoreh De'ah, siman 75. Some say that blank parchment on one side does not help for the other side, and we require the letter to be surrounded by blank parchment on all its sides; whenever the hole reaches the thickness of the letter, it is invalid according to this view. Maamar Mordechai, letter 13. So wrote Nahar Shalom, letter 15; Keset HaSofer, inquiry 4, s.v. hen. See below, letter 69.
