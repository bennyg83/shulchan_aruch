(קז) על ידו - וכשטובל הקולמוס לכתוב בו את השם לא יתחיל לכתוב אותו מיד שלא יפסידנו ברבוי דיו או אולי יש עליו שער ולא יצא הכתב מיושר וגם משום שצריך לקדש הדיו שעל הקולמוס טרם שיכתוב השם לכן יכוין להניח אות אחת מלכתוב קודם השם ובאותו אות יתחיל לכתוב ואם לא עשה כן יחפש אחר אות או תג שצריכה דיו וימלאנה וכותב את השם ואם צריך דיו קודם שיגמור השם אז יטבול באותיות שלפני השם שהם לחים עדיין ויגמור השם אבל לא יטבול באותיות השם עצמן ויש מקילין לטבול באותיות השם עצמן דביזוי ליכא כיון שהוא כדי להשלים בו את השם. ואם האותיות שלפניו אינם לחין אזי יטבול מחדש ויחפש אחר אות או תג שצריכין דיו וכנ"ל וכ"ז הוא למצוה ולא לעיכובא בדיעבד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>By means of it.</b> When he dips the quill in order to write the Divine Name, he should not begin writing it immediately, lest he spoil it with too much ink, or perhaps there is a hair on it and the writing will not come out straight. Also, because he must sanctify the ink on the quill before writing the Name, he should have in mind to leave one letter unwritten before the Name, and begin writing with that letter. If he did not do so, he should search for a letter or tag that needs ink, fill it in, and then write the Name. If he needs ink before finishing the Name, he should dip into the letters before the Name, if they are still wet, and finish the Name; but he should not dip into the letters of the Name itself. Some are lenient to dip into the letters of the Name itself, for there is no disgrace since it is for the purpose of completing the Name with it. If the preceding letters are not wet, he should dip anew and search for a letter or tag that needs ink, as above. All this is for the preferred performance of the mitzvah, but it does not prevent validity after the fact.

(קי) אות אחת - ואם כתב תיבה יתירה יגררנה ויוכל להניח המקום חלק [אם אין לו אותיות מהתיבות שלפניה שיהיה יוכל להמשיכן במקום ההוא] ואין החלק פוסל כל שאין במקומו כשיעור הריוח שבין פרשה לפרשה שהוא כדי ט' אותיות. ולפעמים יש תקנה אפילו בזה כגון שיכול להמשיך האות מן התיבה שלפניה כדי למעט הרויח של ט' אותיות. ויותר מזה דבאופן זה שיפסל התפילין מחמת הרויח אז אפילו אם בסיום התיבה שלפניה יש שם ה"א או קו"ף יוכל להמשיך גגן כדי למעט הריוח ואע"פ שע"י ההמשכה לא יהיה הרגל של הה"א והקו"ף בסופה אין קפידא בדיעבד. וכתב הפמ"ג דבתיבה כפולה טוב יותר למחוק השניה דתיבה הראשונה כדין נכתב. ואם קודם הראשונה יש אות שיכול למושכו טוב יותר שימחוק תיבה הראשונה כדי לחוש אף לדעת ר"ת דשיעור פרשה הוא ג' אותיות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One letter.</b> If he wrote an extra word, he should scrape it away and may leave the place blank, if he has no letters from the preceding words that he can extend into that place. The blank space does not invalidate so long as in its place there is not the measure of the space between one passage and another, which is nine letters. Sometimes there is a remedy even for this, such as if he can extend a letter from the preceding word in order to reduce the space of nine letters. More than this: in a case where the tefillin would be invalidated because of the space, even if at the end of the preceding word there is a hei or kuf, he may extend their roofs to reduce the space, and although through the extension the leg of the hei or kuf will not be at its end, this is not a concern after the fact. Pri Megadim wrote that with a doubled word it is better to erase the second one, for the first word was written properly. If before the first word there is a letter that can be extended, it is better to erase the first word, in order to be concerned even for Rabbeinu Tam’s view that the measure of a passage break is three letters.

(קיב) נראה כשתי תיבות - ופעמים שתיקון מועיל ע"י שיגררנה וימשיך האות שלפניה שתמלא מקומה כגון לאבותיך שכתב מלא וי"ו אחר הב' יגררנה וימשוך הב' שלפניה שתמלא מקומה. וכן אם התיקון תלוי בכ"ף או דלי"ת או רי"ש שאפשר להמשיכן מעט למלא מקום אות היתרה שנגררה אבל אם האותיות שאפשר להמשיכן הן אחר האות היתרה שא"א להמשיכן לאחוריהן עד שיגרר מהם תחלה כגון שאר שכתוב בתורה שכתבה מלא וי"ו אחר האל"ף שא"א למשוך הרי"ש לאחריה עד שיגרר רגלה תחלה ונמצא שביטל צורתה וכשמתקנה אח"כ נמצא כותב שלא כסדרן ואין תקנה אא"כ יכול למלאות מקומו ע"י שיעבה קצת האות שלפניה ושלאחריה שאין בזה משום שינוי האות משא"כ כשהאותיות שאפשר להמשיכן הן לפני האות היתרה אזי אפשר להמשיכן הרבה מבלי שיגרע מהם כלום. אך יש להסתפק במצות הראשון שדינו להיות חסר וכתבו מלא ומחק אח"כ הוי"ו אם מועיל אריכת הצדי"ק למטה אח"כ או דילמא כל עוד שאינו מאריך האות למעלה נחשב כשני תיבות אע"פ שסמכה מלמטה וכן נתן שמשפטו להיות חסר וכן הוצאך אם כתבו מלא ביו"ד בין צדי"ק לאל"ף בכל אלו יש להסתפק אם יש להם תיקון בהאריך האות שלפניו למטה ומ"מ כל שנראה כב' תיבות פסולה אבל אם בתחלת הכתיבה ממשיך רגל התחתון של הנו"ן והצד"י וכותב האות הסמוכה לתוכה כגון פני או ארצי כשר כיון דבתיבה אחת נכתבו ומ"מ לכתחלה אין נכון לעשות כן להבליע אות בתוך אות כי יש מחמירין בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It appears as two words.</b> Sometimes correction helps by scraping it away and extending the preceding letter so that it fills its place. For example, if one wrote la’avosecha plene, with a vav after the beis, he should scrape it away and extend the preceding beis so that it fills its place. So too if the correction depends on a kaf, dalet, or reish, which can be extended slightly to fill the place of the extra letter that was scraped away. But if the letters that can be extended are after the extra letter, and they cannot be extended backward until one first scrapes from them, such as the word she’er written in the Torah, if he wrote it plene with a vav after the alef, he cannot draw the reish backward until he first scrapes its leg. It follows that he has nullified its form, and when he afterward corrects it he is writing out of order. There is no remedy unless he can fill its place by thickening slightly the letter before it and the letter after it, in a way that does not constitute a change in the letter. This is not so where the letters that can be extended are before the extra letter; then he can extend them greatly without detracting from them at all. However, one should be uncertain regarding the first occurrence of mitzvos, whose law is to be defective, if he wrote it plene and afterward erased the vav: does lengthening the tzadi below afterward help, or perhaps as long as he does not lengthen the letter above, it is considered like two words even though he joined it below? So too with nasan, whose law is to be defective, and with hotzi’acha, if he wrote it plene with a yud between the tzadi and the alef. In all these cases one should be uncertain whether they have a remedy by lengthening the preceding letter below. In any event, whenever it appears as two words, it is invalid. But if at the beginning of the writing he extends the lower leg of the nun or tzadi and writes the adjacent letter into it, such as in the words penei or artzi, it is valid, since they were written as one word. Nevertheless, initially it is not proper to do so, to swallow one letter into another, because some are stringent in this.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Every letter, etc..</b> This seif will explain the law of writing out of order, and its matters are lengthy. I will include the law briefly. There are three details in it. First: if there is a defect in some letter, whether it occurred at the time of writing or after writing, as Pri Megadim writes in the introduction, if it is visible to all that its form is not upon it, such as a yud missing its right leg, or a shin missing one yud, and similar cases where some part is missing from the letter so that its form is not upon it, even if because of this it does not resemble another letter, and he wrote afterward, no correction helps. Even if it happened that a child read it as the letter, it is nevertheless invalid because it was written out of order. All the more so if through its defect and lack it resembles another letter, such as a dalet resembling a reish, or a beis resembling a kaf, or a ches whose left leg has a break between the leg and the roof and appears like a hei, or a tzadi that has a break at the place where the yud joins its body and appears to all as a yud and nun; in all such cases we do not rely on the child and correction does not help. Second: if through its defect and lack it did not completely lose its form, such as where there is a thin break in the middle of some letter and the essential form is still upon it, or where the yuds on alefs and shins, or the legs of tavs, did not touch the body of the letter, then if a child who is neither wise nor foolish recognizes them, although the letter is not thereby rendered valid because its form is not upon it completely, nevertheless this helps, for its essential form remains upon it, so that it is permitted to correct it and it is not called out of order. There is also a third case: although its essential form is upon it and a child reads it as a letter, nevertheless no correction helps, such as a letter invalidated by something whose correction is to scrape part of it and write it anew, for example the leg of a hei or kuf that touched above, even with a thin contact like a hairbreadth, whose correction is to scrape the whole leg and write it anew, since mere separation does not help because of chak tochos; or likewise where the leg of the alef touched the roof of the alef, and similar cases, as above in seif 18. If, before correcting it, he wrote afterward, no correction can be devised, for he must scrape everything written invalidly, and if so, what he corrects afterward is out of order. Now, with the help of God, we will begin to explain the seif: “Every letter that is written,” etc.; the same applies if it became ruined afterward.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That were somewhat erased.</b> Meaning, some of the appearance of their ink has left the letters. If at the beginning of the writing the ink was not black, but appeared like whitened ink whose color had faded, or like red ink, and one must pass a pen over it, this is considered writing out of order. But in the case of the Shulchan Aruch it is not called writing out of order, since even now the writing is valid, and what he adds merely preserves it from being erased further. This is only where some of the ink color remains. But if all the ink has sprung away from the parchment and only a reddish trace of the ink rust remains, when he passes a pen over it he is writing out of order. Therefore, if some ink peeled from part of the length of a vav, and only the rust-trace remained at that spot, and it must be shown to a child, then if the upper part has the measure of a vav, one must cover the lower part so that the child not combine it with the upper part, similar to what we wrote above in seif katan 48. Know further that the Pri Megadim holds that even if actual ink remains, but the upper blackness peeled away and red remained, this is writing out of order, since red is invalid for tefillin. The Chasam Sofer, in Yoreh Deah, siman 256, disagrees and holds that if the change to reddishness came from age, it is valid, for it was written with ink. The halachah requires writing with ink to teach only that it must be written with ink, and it is the way of most ink, as it ages, for its appearance to fade and turn somewhat reddish, becoming like the color called brown. Nevertheless, it is proper to pass a pen over it. Even over the sacred Names it is almost certainly permitted, like ink over ink, for there is no erasure here. But if it changed to actual red, which is not the ordinary way of ink, or even only to reddishness but it changed quickly right after writing, that Torah scroll has no remedy, for it appears that this is not from age. Perforce there is a defect in the essence of the ink, since it was made from other ingredients, and it was invalid from the outset because it was not written with ink; regarding the Divine Names, it is forbidden to pass ink over them. See there, where he discusses all this at length with reasons.

Some great authorities practiced this way ab initio; each river follows its own course. See Biur Halacha.

(קעב) מעור אחד - וחתיכת עור תפורים יחד מצדד המ"א דחשובים כעור אחד וכ"כ בתשובת ח"ס או"ח סימן ה' שיש להקל בתפורות ואם היו רק דבוקות בדבק מחמיר שם אבל בח"א משמע דה"ה אם הם דבוקות בדבק וכן משמע באו"ז וכן נתפשט עתה המנהג במדינותינו ומ"מ לכתחילה ראוי ונכון לעשותו מעור אחד ממש כי יש מחמירין וסוברין דלא מהני בחתיכות תפורות יחד או מדובקות בדבק ועיין בח"א דעכ"פ צריכין שיהיו הד' בתים פרודות זה מזה ולא יהיו מדובקין ולא כמו שעושין הסופרים שמדבקין ע"י דבק רק שלמעלה עושין סימנים כמו חריץ בעלמא דצ"ע אם להכשיר אפילו בדיעבד דממ"נ אם אנו חושבין ע"י דיבוק עור אחד א"כ אין לנו אלא בית אחת ובתוכו ד' מחיצות דפסול ואם אין הדבק עושה עור אחד ויש כאן ד' בתים עם ד' חריצים דהדיבוק כנפרד דמי א"כ התפילין גופייהו נעשו משתי עורות ופסולים ובאמת טוב הוא שאפילו העושין מעור אחד ממש יהיו תפיליהן פרודות דשמא הדיבוק חבור וכן התפילין של הגר"א היו פרודות ועיין בבה"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From one piece of leather.</b> Regarding pieces of leather sewn together, the Magen Avraham tends to say that they are considered one piece of leather. So too, Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 5, writes that one may be lenient with pieces that are sewn together; if they were only joined with glue, he is stringent there. However, Chayei Adam implies that the same applies when they are joined with glue, and so it also appears from the Or Zarua, and this is now the widespread custom in our countries. Nevertheless, initially it is proper and correct to make it from an actual single piece of leather, for some are stringent and hold that sewn-together pieces, or pieces glued together, do not help. See Chayei Adam, who writes that in any case the four housings must be separated from one another and not glued together. It should not be like what scribes do, gluing them with glue while merely making marks above like a groove, for it requires study whether this may be validated even after the fact. For whichever way you look at it, if by gluing we consider it one piece of leather, then we have only one housing containing four partitions, which is invalid; and if the glue does not make it one piece of leather, and there are here four housings with four grooves because the glued part is deemed separate, then the tefillin themselves were made from two pieces of leather and are invalid. In truth, it is good that even those who make them from an actual single piece of leather have their tefillin separated, lest the gluing be considered a connection; and the tefillin of the Gra were separated. See Biur Halacha.

(קעה) כדי שיהי' וכו' - פי' בריבוע גמור שארכו כרחבו ממש שיערו חכמים כל השיעור שיש לו יש לו באלכסון ב' חומשים נוסף על אותו השיעור וגם כאן צריך שיהיה מרובע ממש אבל אם לא יהיה מרובע ממש לא יהיה באלכסון שלו כשיעור ב' חומשים תוספות אלא שיעור אחר. וע"כ ימדדנו בד' קוים וידע אם הוא מרובע דהיינו תחלה ימדוד קו אחד באורך וקו אחד ברוחב שיהי' שוה אך שמא באמצעו שוה ובצדדיו מתמעט לכן ימדדנו עוד בשני קוים באלכסונו שיהיו שוים ב' קוי האלכסון ג"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that it shall be, etc.</b> Meaning, in a complete square, whose length is exactly equal to its width, the Sages estimated that whatever measure it has, its diagonal is two fifths more than that measure. Here too it must be an actual square, but if it is not an actual square, its diagonal will not have the measure of an additional two fifths, but a different measure. Therefore he should measure it with four lines and know whether it is square; namely, he should first measure one line lengthwise and one line widthwise so that they are equal. Yet perhaps it is equal in the middle but narrows at the sides; therefore he should also measure it along the two diagonal lines, so that the two diagonal lines are also equal.
