**** ENGLISH ****
(8) <b>From the smoke of wood.</b> Meaning, he should not put into it gum or copper sulfate, called kuper vasser in the vernacular, because initially we require writing that can be erased; through gum or copper sulfate the writing remains and is not erased. All this is only for the optimum performance of the mitzvah, but as a matter of law even the Rama agrees that it is permitted to make ink from gallnut-water, gum, and copper sulfate, and this is the custom today, to make it from a mixture of all three and by cooking, for this is preferable, as written in Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 37. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that in his days as well he did not see any of the great authorities accustomed to write with ink made from the smoke of wood and oils, though perhaps they made it from ink of thorns, as he concluded in the name of Maharil. So ruled Sefer Get Mekushar and Sefer Birkei Yosef, to follow our present custom, because ink made from the smoke of wood and oils spoils and is erased easily; therefore we do not use it now. Ink made only from gallnut-water without gum, called gumi in the vernacular, or only from copper sulfate, is invalid even after the fact. So wrote the Gra in his commentary below, siman 691; see there, and see Biur Halacha. It is permitted to write tefillin and mezuzos with ink made from ordinary non-Jewish wine; see Shaarei Teshuvah, seif katan 4. Ink need not be made for the sake of the mitzvah.

<b>And not by hollowing.</b> This too is called engraving, since he is not performing an act on the body of the letter. Rather, he must also scrape away some of the letter until its form no longer remains upon it, and afterward correct it, provided that he has not yet written after this letter; otherwise it is invalid for tefillin and mezuzos, where we require writing in order. All this law of the Shulchan Aruch is because the letter is not recognizable and the original writing is thereby nullified. But if the form of the letter was not changed, he may erase the drop of ink. There is no distinction whether the ink fell only into the hollow of the letter or also upon the body of the letter and thereby thickened the roof or even the leg; nevertheless he may correct it, and it is not called chak tochos, since the letter is still recognizable. Strictly speaking, in such a case no correction is even required, only it is better that he correct it. Therefore he may make this correction even in tefillin and mezuzos, where writing in order is required. See Biur Halacha. Know further that the Shulchan Aruch speaks even where the drop fell after the letter was completed; all the more so if it fell before the letter was completed properly, and he completed the letter while it was still unrecognizable. In that case everyone agrees that scraping the drop does not repair it, for it never had the name of a letter upon it, and only now, through scraping, it becomes a letter, which is actual chak tochos. But if he first scraped away the drop and now wishes to complete the letter, there are differing opinions among the poskim. The Radach holds that it does not help, for since the drop fell upon part of the letter, the status of writing was removed from it and it is mere engraving; what then is gained even if he completes the letter by writing? All the more so, if a drop of ink actually fell on the parchment and became like half a letter, one may not complete the letter by writing. The Beis Yosef and Rama, in Even HaEzer siman 125, seif 4, are lenient even in this latter case, and hold that it is called chak tochos only when one completes the letter by engraving. The later authorities, however, incline to be stringent in this last case, unless he draws the wet drop of ink and moves it from place to place until he makes from it a complete letter, for that is considered writing.

(סח) אין תקנה - פי' ע"י המחיקה אין תקנה אבל מותר להוסיף דיו ולעשותו עגול. וכן אם טעה וכתב רי"ש במקום ד' וכ' במקום ב' והוו אותיות עבין אין תקנה שיגררן ויעמידן בתמונת ד' וב' אבל מותר להוסיף עליהן דיו ולעשותן בתמונת ד' וב'. וכן אם טעה וכתב במקום זיי"ן נו"ן פשוטה אין תקנה שיגררו ויעמידו על זיי"ן דזה הוי חק תוכות ממש אלא צריך למוחקו לגמרי וכן אם טעה וכתב ה"א במקום ד' אין תקנה לגרור הרגל של הה"א וישאר ד' דדומה לסמ"ך וב' שנפל טפת דיו שם ונתקלקל תמונתו עי"ז דלא מהני גרירת הטפה משום דאין עושה מעשה בגוף האות וה"נ כה"ג אלא צריך לגרור ג"כ קצת הגג עד שישאר כמו וי"ו או לגרור הירך עד שלא ישאר צורת ד' עליו ואח"כ יתקננו כללא דמילתא גורר לא מהני להיות כתיבה בכך ומיקרי חק תוכות אבל כותב מהני שמעבה האות מדלי"ת רי"ש או מרי"ש ד' שכותב התג. ולענין שלא כסדרן נהפוך הוא גורר כשר כגון בדיבוק אות לאות וכדלקמן דזה לא מיקרי מעשה בגוף האות וכותב פסול. ומה שהתרנו להוסיף דיו ולהעמידן על תמונתן היינו כשלא כתב האותיות שאחר זה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is no remedy.</b> Meaning, there is no remedy by erasing, but one may add ink and make it round. Similarly, if one erred and wrote a reish instead of a dalet, or a kaf instead of a beis, and the letters are thick, there is no remedy by scraping them and setting them in the form of a dalet and beis; but one may add ink to them and make them into the form of a dalet and beis. Likewise, if he erred and wrote a final nun in place of a zayin, there is no remedy by scraping it and setting it as a zayin, for this is actual chak tochos; rather, he must erase it completely. So too, if he erred and wrote a hei instead of a dalet, there is no remedy by scraping off the leg of the hei so that a dalet remains, for it is like a samech or beis into which a drop of ink fell and whose form was thereby ruined, where scraping the drop does not help because he is not performing an act on the body of the letter. Here too it is the same. Rather, he must also scrape some of the roof until it remains like a vav, or scrape the thigh until the form of a dalet no longer remains upon it, and afterward correct it. The general rule is: scraping does not help to make that an act of writing, and it is called chak tochos; but writing helps, as when he thickens a letter from a dalet into a reish, or from a reish into a dalet by writing the tag. Regarding writing out of order, the reverse is true: scraping is valid, as in the case of a letter adhering to another letter, as below, for this is not called an act on the body of the letter; but writing is invalid. What we permitted, to add ink and restore the letters to their form, is only where he has not written the subsequent letters.

(פ) חק תוכות - ומיירי שלא נשתנה האות מצורתה ע"י הדביקות אבל אם נשתנית האות מצורתה ואין התינוק יכול לקרותה וכ"ש אם נשתנית לצורת אות אחרת כגון וי"ו שנדבק בסופו לנו"ן כפופה דיבוק עב שנראית כצורת טי"ת עד שתינוק דלא חכים ולא טיפש יקרא אותו לטי"ת לא מהני גרירת הדיבוק בזה דהוי כמ"ם שנסתמה ומקרי חק תוכות וע"כ צריך לגרור גם הנו"ן שגם הוא נשתנה מצורתו ונפסל ע"י הדיבוק וכן כל כיוצא בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Chak tochos.</b> This refers to a case where the letter did not change from its form because of the adhesion. But if the letter changed from its form and the child cannot read it, and all the more so if it changed into the form of another letter, such as a vav whose end adhered to a bent nun with a thick adhesion so that it appears like a tes, to the point that a child who is neither wise nor foolish would read it as a tes, scraping the adhesion does not help, for it is like a mem that became closed, and is called chak tochos. Therefore, he must also scrape the nun, which also changed from its form and was invalidated by the adhesion, and the same applies to all similar cases.
