**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 1) Mixtures, etc.</b> See siman 612 regarding the measure of eating a peras: some say it is four eggs, and some say it is three large eggs. 'For there is a kezayis of chametz there' means that only with regard to eating do we require a kezayis within the time of eating a peras. If the chametz were present by itself and one ate a little, paused, and ate a little more until he completed a kezayis, but more than the time of eating a peras passed from the first eating to the last, the amounts do not combine. All the more so where it is mixed: if within four eggs, according to one view, or within three eggs, according to the other view, there is less than a kezayis, then to eat a kezayis of chametz he must eat more than four or three eggs, and the eating of the kezayis of chametz takes longer than the time of eating a peras, so it does not combine. But regarding bal yera'eh, in any event he has a kezayis in his house, even if it is mixed among many eggs, and therefore he transgresses.

<b>אע"ג דוודאי אסור באכילה.</b> אולם לענין ב"י דעת הע"ש דעובר על ב"י וח"י כ' דאינו עובר ולכן אחר הפסח דאפילו נחמיר לאסרו באכילה אבל עכ"פ בהנאה מותר. וכ' שמטעם זה התיר חמיו ה"ה מהר"ש מפראג בחמאה ששרה בו פת וקמח ועבר ושהה עד אחר הפסח דיוליך הנאת הפת וקמח לים והחמאה מותר למכור לגוי עכ"ל. והיינו שדרך העולם כשמבשלים החמאה להוציא צחצוחי זוהמא נותנים בו פת וקמח לשאוב הזוהמ'. ואח"כ מסנני' החמאה במסננת והמסננת קולט הזוהמ' והחמא' יורדת מתוך המסננת. והח"י כתב לקמן סי' תמ"ז ס"ק י"ז בנפלו פירורי פת תוך יין וסננו קודם פסח דמותר בפסח לכ"ע. ואפי' את"ל כמאן דאמר דע"י סינון מ"מ נכנם מעט מפרורי חמץ לתוך היין מ"מ כיון שהוא דק כ"כ ומתערב הוי לח בלח דקי"ל אם נתבטל קודם פסח אינו חוזר וניעור בפסח ע"ש. ואם כן ה"ה בחמאה הנ"ל דרכן לסנן קודם פסח ניהי דקודם סינון לא היה ששים בחמאה נגד הפת וקמח מ"מ אחר הסינון אפי' עבר מעט בפת וקמח עם החמאה נגד אותו מעט החמאה ודאי ששים וכיון דמקרי לח בלח ונתבטל קודם פסח אינו חוזר וניעור בפסח א"כ אם אתה בא לאסור החמאה ע"כ משום הטעם שקבלה קודם הסינון דאז לא היה ששים בחמאה וזה הוי טעם ולא ממש דהא סננו ובכה"ג ניהו דלכתחלה אסור להשהותה וצריך לבערו מדרבנן. מ"מ אם לא בערו לא עבר על ב"י ומותר אחר הפסח עכ"פ בהנאה ע"י פדיון ועס"ס תמ"ז. וכ' הטור כו' מותר לערבו כו' ר"ל ל"מ אם כבר נתערב למ"ד תערובת חמץ מותר מן התורה באכילה אע"ג דמ"מ אסור מדרבנן באכילה מ"מ מותר לשהותו אפי' לכתחלה אלא אפי' לכתחלה מותר לערבו כדי לשהותו ול"מ מבטל איסור לכתחלה כיון דעדיין זמן היתר הוא וכמ"ש מ"א לקמן סי' תרכ"ו ס"ק ג' וה"ה נוקשה בעינה למ"ד שאין באכילתו איסור תורה מותר לשהותו בעין לדעת הטור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Although it is certainly forbidden to eat.</b> However, regarding bal yera'eh, the Eliyah Rabbah holds that one does not transgress. Therefore after Pesach, even if one is stringent to forbid eating it, it is at least permitted for benefit. He wrote that for this reason his father-in-law, Maharash of Prague, permitted butter in which bread and flour had been soaked and that had been kept until after Pesach: one should cast the benefit of the bread and flour into the sea, and the butter may be sold to a non-Jew. This refers to the practice of cooking butter to remove the scum, placing bread and flour into it to absorb the scum, and then straining the butter. Chok Yaakov wrote below, siman 447, s.k. 17, that if crumbs fell into wine and one strained it before Pesach, it is permitted on Pesach according to all. Even if some fine crumbs enter the wine through the straining, since they are very fine and mixed as liquid with liquid, if they were nullified before Pesach they do not reawaken on Pesach. The same applies to this butter, which is normally strained before Pesach. Although before straining there was not sixty in the butter against the bread and flour, after straining, even if a little passed through, there is certainly sixty against that little. Since it is considered liquid with liquid and was nullified before Pesach, it does not reawaken on Pesach. If one would forbid the butter, it would have to be only because of the taste it absorbed before straining, when there was not sixty; that is taste without substance. In such a case, initially it is rabbinically forbidden to keep and must be destroyed, but if he did not destroy it he did not transgress bal yera'eh, and after Pesach it is at least permitted for benefit through redemption; see the end of siman 447. The Tur's statement that it is permitted to mix means not only that if it was already mixed it may be kept according to the view that a chametz mixture is permitted to eat according to the Torah, though rabbinically forbidden, but that one may even initially mix it in order to keep it, since it is still a time of permissibility, as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 626, s.k. 3. The same applies, according to the Tur, to chametz nukshah in its own substance where its eating is not forbidden by Torah law.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The words of the Beis Yosef here require examination.</b> He wrote on the Tur's statement that according to Rabbi Eliezer a chametz mixture is under a negative commandment without kareis, and according to that it is forbidden to keep on Pesach; according to the Sages, even though there is no negative commandment, there is still some prohibition, and according to them it may be kept. The Beis Yosef understood from this wording that according to the Sages there is no negative commandment but there is nevertheless a Torah prohibition in eating, and therefore he wondered why the Tur permitted keeping it. Thus it is clear that when he first wrote 'only a prohibition,' he meant a rabbinic prohibition. The Taz already noted this and added further challenges; see there.
