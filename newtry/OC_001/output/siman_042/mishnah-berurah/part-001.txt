**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(3) From them.</b> Magen Avraham writes that if the arm-tefillin strap snapped near the knot, one may not reverse the intact lower end upward and make the arm-tefillin knot there, then tie the piece that formerly held the knot below it. This lowers the former knot section from its sanctity, since it held the knot and yod and would now be used for the finger wraps. That piece requires genizah.

If the remaining strap is too short, he should make fewer arm wraps.

The same applies to head tefillin. If the encircling section snapped, one may not reverse the portion formerly inside the knot so that it now lies outside.

If the strap snapped outside the knot and he wishes to pull the break downward so that the strap retains the required measure, some permit this. Although the place of the knot moves downward, the same section still holds the knot.

Others hold that this too improperly lowers its sanctity.

According to the latter view, even where an arm-tefillin strap snapped near the knot and one does not intend to reattach the broken piece below, he should not reverse the strap and make the knot from its lower end, because the formerly upper section would move farther from the knot.

One should therefore be stringent and reverse the strap only in pressing circumstances, where no other strap is available and the broken section is soft and weak and liable to break if used for tying upon the arm. See Biur Halachah.

<b>(11) Tefillin.</b> A tallis bag is only an accessory of a mitzvah and may be used for mundane items.

Magen Avraham cites authorities who disagree and hold that even such designation is ineffective. In practice, one should be stringent like the first view.

A condition is effective before writing begins. If, however, one began writing a Sefer Torah, tefillin, mezuzah, or another sacred text upon parchment or even paper, everyone agrees that he may not afterward write mundane matters there. This is an act involving the sacred object itself, and even a condition does not help.

<b>(24) He wrapped in it.</b> Meaning temporary use without prior designation, even repeated many times, provided each use was temporary.

If he used it permanently, or even once with an intention at that moment to use it permanently, it becomes forbidden even without prior designation.

If he expressly inserted the tefillin intending to remove them later, it remains permitted even after repeated use, so long as the pouch itself was not made for tefillin, as explained in seif katan 14.
