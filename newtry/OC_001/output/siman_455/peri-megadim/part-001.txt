<b>Beis Yosef.</b> What he wrote: see Taz; it needs explanation. In the Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah, siman 593: an incident occurred when Pesach fell on Sunday, and also a scribal error: the tekufah fell on the night before it, and even so they were not concerned and kneaded with water drawn at night; and Rabbeinu Yehuda HaChasid wrote that one need not take care: "he who guards a mitzvah will know no evil"; and all of them baked only for the need of the next day, for one Yom Tov does not prepare for its fellow; also on the second night they should bake only for the need of that day, for one does not prepare for a weekday. And according to this reading one may say: even so they were not concerned for the tekufah that fell on Motzei Shabbos night, and they drew water at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos as the law of mayim shelanu, and they held that there is no prohibition, for there is not so much preparation in this drawing, and they did not spill the water and kneaded with them for the need of the first day, and likewise on the second night; and they hold that when Pesach falls on Sunday one bakes the matzos on Pesach night so that there not be old matzah; see siman 458 in Taz 1; and Rabbeinu Yehuda HaChasid is not arguing, but said that they did well not to spill the water, for "he who guards a mitzvah," etc.; and this is the language of Beis Yosef; and it is obvious that he is like those who say that when it falls on Sunday one bakes on Pesach night; for if one bakes from Friday this does not apply; and even according to those who say one bakes on Pesach night there is reason to wonder why they did not draw from Friday; and perhaps they hold that in such a case they are not considered mayim shelanu; and still it is difficult: even though the tekufah had not fallen on the Thursday night before it, how was it permitted to draw on Shabbos for the need of Yom Tov; and if you say that drawing which is not such great exertion is permitted, if so when the tekufah fell on the Thursday night before it, what was [the issue]: they should have drawn on Friday at bein hashemashot. And the words are hard to fit if the reading is "on the Thursday night before it"; however, if the reading is "on the night before it" (and they erred and moved the letter hei away from "night" and put a mark on it), or one may explain Beis Yosef's words thus: that the tekufah fell on the night before it, Motzei Shabbos, for Pesach is on Sunday, so "the night before it" properly means Motzei Shabbos; and they also hold like R' Eliezer of Metz that if they drew near sunset they are permitted immediately and need not twelve hours, unlike Rashi; and Rabbeinu Yehuda HaChasid said they did well not to spill: "he who guards a mitzvah," etc.; and Beis Yosef asked why they did not draw from Friday by day rather than drawing at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos; see there; and he answered that in such a case they hold it is forbidden: whenever they drew by day there is no remedy by waiting twelve hours (and nevertheless it is difficult: they should have drawn on Thursday night belonging to Friday at bein hashemashot rather than drawing at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos); see there; and according to this it appears one must say "and still it is difficult, even though the tekufah was falling," and delete the word "that it had not"; however, Heaven forfend for me to emend and delete the word "that it had not."

<b>However.</b> The words of the Bach, of blessed memory, require a bit of further study, for he too explains that Beis Yosef had before him the reading that the tekufah fell on the night before it, that is Motzei Shabbos, as written here; however, what he wrote there, that they needed to draw water for the need of the second night of Pesach, and even though one Yom Tov does not prepare for its fellow they are also fit for the first Yom Tov, requires a bit of further study, for it implies that they drew and waited twelve hours; if so, for the first night of Pesach whence did they have water? And necessarily that they drew at bein hashemashot of Thursday night belonging to Friday, they should have drawn also for the second night; and further, according to what he concludes, that all that is detached harms them the water for day one; and if so it would appear to explain that they drew water for day one of Pesach after bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos immediately at the beginning of the night before the springs boil, like R' Eliezer of Metz, and kneaded immediately, as implied by the Tur there. And Beis Yosef asked: why specifically where the tekufah fell on the night before it, that is at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos, and even so they were not concerned and drew immediately after sunset and kneaded immediately like R' Eliezer of Metz; for if the tekufah had not fallen at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos they would have drawn at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos as the law of mayim shelanu ab initio at bein hashemashot itself. And Rabbeinu Yehuda HaChasid disagrees and said they did not do well, for one should draw at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos, for "he who guards a mitzvah," etc.; and Beis Yosef asked: since the tekufah had not fallen they would have drawn at bein hashemashot of Motzei Shabbos; now too they should draw at bein hashemashot of Friday, since they did not draw at the hour of the tekufah and it does not harm; upon this he answered: all that is detached harms; therefore they waited until after the tekufah passed. And Taz was not settled with this because of several difficulties, as he wrote. Therefore he explained in another way. However, the reading in the Mordechai is "on the Thursday night before it," and in the Rokeach the reading is "on the night of the tenth"; one may say they are one: sometimes he calls Thursday night the night of Thursday belonging to Friday, etc., as Rama wrote "on Thursday night"; see Magen Avraham letter 4. And what is written "on Shabbos" is a scribal error, for it is apparently proven they baked on Friday after midday as in siman 451; and the tekufah was on Thursday night belonging to Friday, and they were not concerned that the water was detached at the tekufah and they did not put iron; and R' Yehuda HaChasid said they did well, for he concludes in the Mordechai incident: and all of them baked only for the first night, that is at Motzei Shabbos, not on Friday; and Pesach was on Sunday; and "on Shabbos" is a scribal error, as is proven.

<b>And Taz,</b> of blessed memory, explained that the reading in the Mordechai is: and the tekufah fell on the night before it, that is on Friday night; and what is written "before it" is literal, for according to the Bach "before it" means that of Sunday. And it fits well with the Rokeach's reading "on the second night," that is the one belonging to Friday. And they were in doubt because of the tekufah that fell after bein hashemashot how to draw water at the hour of the tekufah, danger; and even so they were not concerned for the tekufah and kneaded with water drawn at night, meaning they drew water at night itself after the tekufah; and Rabbeinu Yehuda HaChasid wrote that they did well, etc.; upon this Beis Yosef asked: and still it is difficult: how does it depend on the tekufah; without a tekufah how is it permitted to draw on Friday night for Yom Tov; and if you say that when it is not exertion it is permitted, only because of the tekufah that fell on Friday night they doubted, and they were not concerned for the tekufah and drew on Friday night: they should have drawn at bein hashemashot of Friday, see there, which is better as the precise law ab initio, and not to transgress preparing against the tekufah, etc.; and the last answer is only on the last difficulty, etc. And know that the phrase "and they kneaded with water drawn at night": one may say the word "at night" is a pause between the word "drawn"; and the meaning is: those drawn at bein hashemashot as the law, they kneaded at night on Pesach night. And Taz reads in the Mordechai: Pesach on Sunday, and also the tekufah fell on the night before it, meaning on the night before Pesach, whether Friday or Thursday; and in the Rokeach he explains on Friday night of Shabbos; only there is no practical difference except that they drew as the law on Thursday night as explained in the gloss se'if 1 and baked on Pesach night, holding that in such a case one bakes on Pesach night itself; see siman 458; and even though the water was detached they were not concerned and baked with them; and R' Yehuda HaChasid said they did well; and all the difficulties are removed, namely what Beis Yosef wrote regarding Shabbos how they drew, etc.; but the difficulty that it does not harm: they hold that what is detached harms; and on Shabbos, certainly on Friday night one may not draw, as the Rokeach wrote, for it is like filling a garden; see siman 338 (and there is a scribal error in the print, and one must say the reading: on the night before it, and on Shabbos one may not draw); and if one asks what relevance "and also the tekufah fell": alternatively, Pesach fell on other days and they were detached and they kneaded on Erev Pesach after midday; one may say on the contrary the word "also" indicates like Taz, for if the meaning is that they drew on Friday night after the tekufah it is one matter: he should have said Pesach fell on Sunday and the tekufah fell; and "also" implies they are two laws, as said.
