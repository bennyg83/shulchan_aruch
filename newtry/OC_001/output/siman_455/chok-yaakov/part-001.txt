<b>או בין השמשות. </b> דעיקר הזמן לשאוב בה שהוא בין שקיעת החמה לצאת הכוכבים טרם שתזרח הלבנה באדמה וכ"כ מהרי"ל וע"ל סי' רס"א שיעור בין השמשות ולפי שאין הכל יודעין לכוין שיעור בין השמשות לכן שואבין אותו סמוך לבין השמשות אחר שקיעת החמה ובדיעבד אם שואבין בעוד יום או ששאבן אחר תחילת הלילה נראה מדברי האחרונים שיש להקל ללוש בהם אחר י"ב שעות. ובתשובה מ"ע סי' ל"ב כתב דאין להקל אלא א"כ בשעת הדחק או הפסד מרובה ע"ל סעיף ג' ועיין בט"ז שכת' שיותר טוב לאחר השאיב' מלהקדים. אכן מהרי"ל כתב להיפך וז"ל יותר טוב להקדים קצת משיאחר כי אין הלינה הוזכר בתלמוד להדיא עכ"ל ודבריו נראים נכונים וכן משמעות מ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or at bein hashemashot.</b> For the main time to draw is that which is between sunset and the appearance of the stars, before the moon shines upon the earth; and so wrote Maharil; and see above, siman 261, for the measure of bein hashemashot; and because not everyone knows how to aim the measure of bein hashemashot, therefore they draw near bein hashemashot after sunset; and after the fact, if they draw while it is still day, or if they drew after the beginning of the night, it appears from the words of the later authorities that one may be lenient to knead with them after twelve hours. And in Responsa Meir Einei Chachamim, siman 32, he wrote that one should not be lenient except in a time of pressure or great loss; see above, se'if 3; and see Taz, who wrote that it is better to delay the drawing than to advance it. However, Maharil wrote the opposite, and this is his language: it is better to advance a bit than to delay, for overnight resting is not mentioned explicitly in the Talmud; end quote; and his words appear correct; and likewise is the implication of Magen Avraham.

<b>תחת התקר'. </b> והא דלא נקט לכסותו במפה כמ"ש בסמוך משום דצריכין להיות במקום מגולה שאויר שולט כמ"ש המחבר בשם א"ח ואם יכסנו במפה נתחממו ולפי זה אינו נכון מה שנזהרין העולם לכסות המים דוקא אף בעת הקר וצריכין לומר דלכך לא מהני הכסוי בלילה דחיישינן שמא ישכח להכניסם עד זמן רב ביום ויתחמם דרך המפה או הכיסוי אם לא יכסנו בטוב מה שא"כ בסמוך שמוליך המים רק דרך העבר' בעלמא מהני כיסוי כל דהוא:

<b>Under the ceiling.</b> And the reason he did not state to cover it with a cloth as he wrote nearby is because they must be in an open place where air has sway, as the Mechaber wrote in the name of Orchos Chaim; and if one covers it with a cloth they become heated; and according to this, what the world is careful specifically to cover the water even in cold time is not correct; and one must say that therefore covering at night does not help: we are concerned lest one forget to bring them in until long into the day and they become heated through the cloth or the cover if one does not cover them well; which is not so nearby, when one only carries the water mere transit, any covering helps.

<b>And when one carries the water under the open sky.</b> And it refers to what was written above: it is good to set it under the ceiling so that if one forgets to bring them in at dawn the sun not shine upon it. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>The night of the thirteenth, that is, Thursday night.</b> For if on Friday night we are concerned lest one advance before sunset or delay and desecrate Shabbos; so is the language of the Mordechai at the end of the first chapter of Pesachim; it is implied from this that after the fact, if one forgot to draw on the night of the thirteenth, it is permitted to draw on Friday while it is still day near bein hashemashot, or even at bein hashemashot, for we are not concerned for desecration of Shabbos, for anything that is only a shevut they did not decree upon at bein hashemashot in a place of mitzvah, as is explained above in siman 261 and 307; see there. However, from the words of the Rokeach, siman 265, brought in the words of the later authorities, who wrote, and this is his language: when the fourteenth falls on Shabbos, one draws on the night of the thirteenth from the river or well, and one does not draw from the cistern in his courtyard on Shabbos for kneading, for one needs much and it appears like filling his garden and his ruin, as is in Eruvin folio 104; end quote; it is implied that even after the fact drawing is forbidden; and in truth at first glance his words are astonishing: what concern of prohibition is there in this of appearing like filling, for there it is explained that specifically by means of a wheel it is forbidden, because one draws much and draws easily; and even with our wheel some permit, as is explained above in siman 338, se'if 6; and all the more so what they draw nowadays for an individual in his house, one or two jugs, which is a small matter, and it is also at bein hashemashot, when they did not decree upon a shevut; if so, what concern of prohibition is there in this? And in truth one may perhaps force a reconciliation of the Rokeach's words: that his intent is if one wants to draw on Shabbos itself, and so is slightly implied by his language, and he is not discussing bein hashemashot; and he also discusses when many courtyard residents want to draw and the like, but not where an individual forgot, when it is permitted to draw one or two jugs in his courtyard at bein hashemashot, or even from the river in a place where carrying is permitted (however, from the words of the later authorities who cited the Rokeach this is not implied). And it seemed more to me that there is a concern of prohibition of preparing from Shabbos for Yom Tov: (so what is written on Friday at the end of this siman permitting through small vessels and many people does not help for this reason.) And this concern is also mentioned in Beis Yosef and in Magen Avraham, and he concludes that one who forgot to draw on Thursday night should draw on Friday while it is still day, or command a gentile to draw at bein hashemashot, or take water that was drawn in his house even though it was not drawn for the sake of the mitzvah, since it is impossible otherwise; end quote. And the best is to draw oneself or through one's agent that same day near bein hashemashot, for there is no concern of prohibition as in the other ways; and see above, s.k. 1; and in Responsa Meir Einei Chachamim, siman 32, he wrote, and this is his language: the custom spread not to knead matzah after Shabbos except with water drawn on the night toward Friday, for then one can aim to draw them between sunsets and stars, which is not so with the two bein hashemashot of Friday and Motzei Shabbos; and in a place where they customarily practiced, one should not be lenient except in a time of pressure or great loss; end quote. And nevertheless, where there is no custom, one should not be stringent and practice as he wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And nevertheless it is good ab initio to put iron in them.</b> And one should take new iron and hang it by a string (Maharil and later authorities). And Magen Avraham wrote: Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that on all other days of the year one should place salt or a seal; end quote. And Taz wrote, and this is his language: it appears that nevertheless on other nights, if one did not put iron in it to guard against the tekufah, one may spill them if one can easily do without that water; which is not so of these waters of matzas mitzvah, which it is forbidden to spill, for it appears like belittling the verse's statement, "he who guards a mitzvah will know no evil"; end quote. And similarly wrote Maharshal in his commentary on Semag; and my brother-in-law the Rav in Sefer Ateres Zekenim challenged him regarding what is implied from his words that specifically for matzas mitzvah one may permit the waters, but rather even for waters for other matzos one need not take care on account of the tekufah if it dies; and nevertheless he concluded in the name of Agudah that even for matzas mitzvah one may permit only when it is impossible to borrow mayim shelanu from him in that city; end quote. And it appears simple to me that even Agudah who is stringent said so only in a small city with few people; but in a large city we are certainly not obligated to go door to door asking for water, only among one's neighbors and acquaintances and the like; and so is implied from Taz's language, that even on all other days of the year one should not be stringent if one cannot easily do without that water; and all the more so of these waters; and the custom is to be lenient in every case.

<b>אין ללוש במים חמין. </b> הלבוש כ' אפילו לנו כל הלילה ולא נתחממו אלא בחמה ע"כ כוונתו אף שלנו כל שעדיין הם חמין אסור ללוש בהם מה שא"כ אם הם צוננין לפנינו דמה"ט בעי מים לינה להפיג חמימתן מהחמה כמבואר בש"ס ופוסקים וה"ה בחמי האור אם נצטננו כמשמעות הפוסקים והמחבר שכת' ומים שבתוכו פושרין משמע דוקא כשהן פושרין עדיין אכן בתשובת רמ"א סימן ל"ה כ' וז"ל דחמי האור אפילו לאחר צינון יש לאסור דעבד לא מבעיא להר"א ממיץ דאפילו מים שלא לנו לא מהני צינון וכ"ש מים חמין ואף לדעת רש"י מכל מקום בחמי האור אין לו תקנה ולכן לא מצינו לשום פוסק שכת' שיש תקנה לחמי האור עכ"ל ובאמת משמעות המחבר אינו כן וכמ"ש הע"ש וכ"כ גיסי הרב בסא"ז משם מי"ט ולא הוצרכו הפוסקים לכתו' תקנה דהא חמי האור קתני וכל שנצטננו תו לא מיקרי חמי אור וכיון שהם צוננים לפנינו מה חשש איסור יש בזה ואדרבא שמעתי שלפעמים מים שנתבשלו ונצטננו הם צוננים יותר משאר מים גם מ"ש דלהר"א ממיץ לא מהני צינון למים שלא לנו אין זה מוכרח ועיין בב"י אף שהמי"ט שהביא גיסי הרב בסא"ז סיים וז"ל אלא חלילה לסתור דבריהם בלי ראיה עכ"ל נראה מדבריו שהבין שגם דעת הלבוש במ"ש אפילו לנו כונתו אפילו נצטננו לכן לא בא לסתור גם דבריו ובאמת כוונת הלבוש פשוט כמ"ש וגם הראי' ברור' מלשון הש"ס והפוסקים והמחבר דנקטו דוקא חמין או פושרין והרוצה להחמיר עכ"פ יש להתיר בה"מ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One may not knead with hot water.</b> The Levush wrote: even if they rested the whole night and were heated only by the sun; therefore his intent is: even if they rested, as long as they are still hot it is forbidden to knead with them; which is not so if they are cool before us; for for that reason we require overnight resting of water to dispel their heat from the sun, as is explained in the Gemara and poskim; and the same applies to fire-heated water if they cooled, as implied by the poskim and the Mechaber who wrote "and the water in it is lukewarm," implying specifically when they are still lukewarm; however, in Responsa of the Rama, siman 35, he wrote, and this is his language: that fire-heated water even after cooling one should forbid, for it is not even a need to say according to R' Eliezer of Metz that even water that did not rest overnight cooling does not help, and all the more so hot water; and even according to Rashi's view, nevertheless fire-heated water has no remedy; and therefore we find no posek who wrote that fire-heated water has a remedy; end quote. And in truth the Mechaber's implication is not so, as Olas Shabbas wrote; and so wrote my brother-in-law the Rav in Sefer Ateres Zekenim from the work cited as mem-yud-tet; and the poskim did not need to write a remedy, for it says fire-heated, and whenever they cooled they are no longer called fire-heated; and once they are cool before us, what concern of prohibition is there in this? And on the contrary, I have heard that sometimes water that was cooked and cooled is cooler than other water; also what he wrote that according to R' Eliezer of Metz cooling does not help for water that did not rest overnight is not compelling; and see Beis Yosef. Even though the work mem-yud-tet that my brother-in-law the Rav cited in Sefer Ateres Zekenim concluded, and this is his language: but Heaven forfend to overturn their words without proof; end quote; it appears from his words that he understood that the Levush's intent too in writing "even if they rested" was even if they cooled, therefore he did not come to overturn his words as well; and in truth the Levush's intent is simple as I wrote; and the proof is also clear from the language of the Gemara and the poskim and the Mechaber, who stated specifically hot or lukewarm; and one who wishes to be stringent should at least permit in a case of great loss.
