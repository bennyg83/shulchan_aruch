<b>שקורין</b> לאמפ״א עט״ז פמוטות שלנו ונחושת קלל וכדומה הכל עגול ול״ש נר אמצעי ובמ״א ד׳ בצד אמצעי הסמוך לפתח כלומר עיקר המצוה להתחיל בנר הסמוך טפי לפתח ועסי׳ תרע״ו בט״ז אות ו׳ בשם רש״ל להניח באורך הטפח הנרות מדליק משמאל לימין א״כ המדליק בפמוטות ידליק בנר היותר סמוך לפתח ויסבב דרך ימין משמאל לימין ועדיין צ״ע קצת. ומ״ש המדקדקים אין מדליקין בלאמפין אף בפמוטות שלנו ובפר״ח הביא עששיות של זכוכית וסיים שראה שמדליקין בהם וא״י פירוש אם לומר שנותנין נר חלב ושעוה בתוך מחיצה של זכוכית או שמן כה״ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Which are called.</b> Lampa. See the Taz. Our candelabras, made of polished copper and the like, are all round, and an middle lamp is not applicable. In Magen Avraham, s.k. 4, 'on the side of the middle one adjacent to the entrance' means that the main mitzvah is to begin with the lamp closest to the entrance. See siman 676, in the Taz, letter 6, in the name of the Maharshal, that he should place the lamps along the length of the tefach and light from left to right. If so, one who lights in candelabras should light the lamp closest to the entrance and turn rightward, from left to right; this still requires some investigation. What he wrote, that the meticulous do not light in lampas, applies even to our candelabras. The Pri Chadash brought glass lanterns and concluded that he saw people lighting in them, but I do not know the explanation: whether this means that they put a tallow or wax candle inside a glass partition, or oil in such a manner.

<b>אמר</b> ההדיוט הכותב בדברי הרב בהג״ה ראיתי לבאר קצת מ״ש ולא בעגול בד״מ כתב כמו שעושין בב״ה שנועצים מסמורים א״י אם ר״ל נועצים מסמורים בכותל בעיגול או סביב לוח א׳ עגול נועצים מסמורים בעיגול ואיך שיהיה איזה הפרש יש בין פמוט לזה וי״ל וע׳ פר״ח. והנה תראה שהר״ב ז״ל בד״מ כתב שכל א׳ מובדל מחבירו לא מיקרי מדורה וכאן כ׳ מובדל הרבה מחבירו ובתה״ד ק״ה כ״ב דלא דמי פמוט לקערה שכל קנה מובדל במחיצה ועוד לבעל העיטור בקערה רחוק ב׳ אצבעות שרי וכאן רחוק הרבה יע״ש. והמחבר אוסר בקערה אפילו רחוק הרבה כהטור והר״ב מדשתיק אודויי אודי ליה אלא בפמוט החמיר דבעינן שיהיו מובדלין הקנים וגם הרבה רחוקים יותר מב׳ אצבעות הא בחסר א׳ דהיינו שאין מובדלין במחיצות או מובדלין ואין רחוקים הקנים זה מזה ב׳ אצבעות אסור דהוי כמדורה. וא״כ צ״ע כשמדבקין נרות שעוה בכותל באין רחוקים ב׳ אצבעות די״ל דאסור ועא״ר בטור אצבע ובתה״ד ב׳ אצבעות ואי״ה במ״א ג״ד יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Said</b> the ordinary writer: In the words of the Rav in the gloss I saw fit to explain somewhat what he wrote, 'and not in a circle.' In the Darkei Moshe he wrote: as they do in the synagogue, where they insert nails. I do not know if he means that they insert nails in the wall in a circle, or around one round board they insert nails in a circle. In any case, there is some distinction between a candelabrum and this; one can say so, and see Pri Chadash. You will see that the Rav, of blessed memory, wrote in the Darkei Moshe that wherever each one is separated from the other, it is not called a bonfire; but here he wrote that it must be greatly separated from the other. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 105, he wrote that a candelabrum is not comparable to a bowl, because each branch is separated by a partition, and furthermore, according to the author of the Ittur, with a bowl, a distance of two fingerbreadths is permitted, while here they are very far apart; see there. The Mechaber forbids with a bowl even when they are very far apart, like the Tur, and from the Rav's silence he admitted to him. But with a candelabrum he was stringent that we require the branches to be separated and also much more distant than two fingerbreadths. Thus, if one of these is missing, meaning they are not separated by partitions, or they are separated but the branches are not two fingerbreadths apart from one another, it is forbidden, for it is like a bonfire. If so, it requires investigation when one attaches wax candles to the wall and they are not two fingerbreadths apart, for one can say it is forbidden. See Eliyah Rabbah: in the Tur, one fingerbreadth, and in Terumas HaDeshen, two fingerbreadths. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, s.k. 3 and 4, this will be explained further.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one needs.</b> See the Taz, and so the Magen Avraham wrote in his name, letter 5. See siman 673, letters 4 and 5, for certainly one benefits more when there are many lamps; see there. In the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 4, halachah 8: in a time of danger he places it inside, and even on his table is enough, and another lamp is needed inside his house to use its light. Meaning: even if he placed it inside near the entrance and window, where it is recognizable as a Chanukah lamp, nevertheless another lamp is needed in order to use its light. This is what he wrote, 'inside,' and also 'table,' to equate them. See the Levush and Eliyah Rabbah about this. Now, when Rava said that another lamp is needed, one can say this applies even to an important person who always uses two lamps on his table and the like, as ministers do; if so, even two lamps would not be a recognition on the first night. Therefore he said that another lamp is needed so that he not use the light of Chanukah, even though there will be no recognition, and another lamp is sufficient. With Hashem's help, in siman 673 in the Magen Avraham and Taz, s.v., and below in the Taz, letter 11, and Magen Avraham, letter 14, this will be explained further. <b>למעלה</b> מך׳ עט״ז וכ״פ הב״ח ועתוס׳ סוכה ב׳ ד״ה וכיון דקורת מבוי אף שמונח על כותלי המבוי דטפח לא מינכרא ה״ה נ״ח ועב״ח. וז״ש בשבת כ״ב נ״ח פסולה למעלה מך׳ אמה כסוכה וכמבוי לומר מה מבוי אף במגיע הכותלים לקורה פסול ה״ה כאן דסוכה נמי לרבא דהלכתא כוותיה אף מגיע לסכך פסולה עסי׳ תרל״ג ועד״מ בשם מהר״א מפראג ז״ל. ובס״ה מניחו בחלון הסמוך לרה״ר עפר״ח אם גבוה ך׳ אמה אין מעלה כלל ומיהו לדידן דמדליקין בפנים רשאי להניח בחלון ומ״מ יש היכר קצת לבני רה״ר ועדיף כן ואי״ה במ״א י׳ וי״ד אבאר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Higher</b> than twenty. See the Taz, and so ruled the Bach. See Tosafos in Sukkah 2, s.v. 'and since,' that with the crossbeam of a mavoi, even though it rests on the walls of the mavoi, if it is a tefach, it is not recognizable; the same applies to the Chanukah lamp. See the Bach. This is what it means in Shabbos 22: a Chanukah lamp is invalid higher than twenty amos, like a sukkah and like a mavoi. This teaches: just as with a mavoi, even if the walls reach the crossbeam it is invalid, the same applies here. A sukkah too, according to Rava, whom the halachah follows, is invalid even if the walls reach the s'chach; see siman 633 and Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahara of Prague, of blessed memory. In se'if 5, he places it in the window adjacent to the public domain. See the Pri Chadash: if it is twenty amos high, it has no advantage at all. However, for us, who light inside, one may place it in the window, and nevertheless there is some recognition for the people of the public domain, so this is preferable. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letters 10 and 14, I will explain further.

<b>From its midpoint.</b> See the Taz and the Beis Yosef. As it appears, according to the Tur's version in the Semak, it speaks of a person standing inside the house with his face toward the door; the opening is the hollow of the door, the panel that closes the house. The person's right is toward the left of the door, the place of the hinge, and the person's left is toward the right of the door, toward the mezuzah. He made two conditions: first, that the door is divided into two. For example, if the door is four amos wide, we say that two amos belong to the right of the mezuzah and two amos to the left, where the hinge is. In those two amos toward the hinge he should place the Chanukah lamp toward the left, meaning at the end of the third amah from the mezuzah, which is the beginning of the fourth amah, so that the Chanukah lamp will be only one amah from the hinge of the door and the threshold of the house. But according to the second explanation, it speaks of a person standing outside with his face toward the door, with the person's right toward the right of the mezuzah and his left toward the hinge of the door. There is one condition: that he place the Chanukah lamp at the end of two amos from the mezuzah, and it is also permitted even if the Chanukah lamp is two amos away from the hinge. According to the Tur's explanation, he calls the part of the door close to the hinge 'entrance,' etc.; see Eliyah Rabbah there. It appears to me that one can say as we wrote, that even according to the first explanation, if he places it one amah close to the hinge it is enough, and he need not place it within a tefach adjacent to the threshold and hinge. See the Bach, who explained differently, and see Pri Chadash. I did not have time to examine it properly. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letter 8, it will be explained further; here is not the place to elaborate.
