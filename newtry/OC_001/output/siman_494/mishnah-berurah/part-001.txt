{א} ביום נ' לספירת העומר וכו' - ומאחרין להתפלל ערבית בכניסת שבועות בצאת הכוכבים כדי שיהיו ימי הספירה מ"ט יום תמימות. איתא בזוהר שחסידים הראשונים היו נעורים כל הלילה ועוסקים בתורה וכבר נהגו רוב הלומדים לעשות כן ואיתא בשו"ע האר"י ז"ל דע שכל מי שבלילה לא ישן כלל ועיקר והיה עוסק בתורה מובטח לו שישלים שנתו ולא יארע לו שום נזק. והטעם כתב מ"א ע"פ פשוטו שישראל היו ישנים כל הלילה והוצרך הקב"ה להעיר אותם לקבל התורה [כדאיתא במדרש] לכך אנו צריכין לתקן זה. וצריכין ליזהר אותם הנעורים כל הלילה שלא לברך על נטילת ידים בבוקר רק אחר שעשה צרכיו קודם התפלה ואז מברך על נט"י ואשר יצר וכמבואר לעיל בסימן ד' סק"ל ע"ש ולענין ברכה על טלית קטן יש דעות בין הפוסקים וע"כ יראה לכוין לפטור אותה בברכה שמברך על טלית גדול כמבואר לעיל סימן ח' במ"ב ס"ק מ"ב ולענין ברכת התורה עיין לעיל בסימן מ"ז במ"ב סקכ"ח ולענין ברכת אלהי נשמה וברכת המעביר שנה עיין בסימן מ"ו במ"ב סקכ"ד. נכון ליזהר ע"פ קבלה מלשמש בליל שבועות אם לא בליל טבילה ועיין מה שכתבנו לעיל בסימן ר"מ במ"ב סק"ז ובה"ל שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) On the 50th day of Sefiras HaOmer, etc.: And they delay praying Ma'ariv at the entry of Shavuot until nightfall when the stars appear, so that the days of the counting shall be 49 complete days. It is stated in the Zohar that the early chassidim would stay awake all night and occupy themselves in Torah; and already most of those who learn have practiced to do so; and it is stated in the Shulchan Aruch of the Arizal, of blessed memory: know that anyone who on that night did not sleep at all whatsoever and was occupied in Torah is assured that he will complete his year and that no harm will befall him. And the reason the Magen Avraham wrote according to its plain sense is that Yisrael were sleeping all night and the Holy One, blessed be He, had to awaken them to receive the Torah [as is stated in the Midrash]; therefore we must correct this. And one must caution those who stay awake all night not to recite the blessing on washing the hands in the morning except after he has relieved himself before prayer, and then he recites the blessing on washing the hands and Asher Yatzar, as is explained above in siman 4, s.k. 30; see there. And regarding a blessing over a tallis katan there are disagreements among the poskim; therefore he should see to intend to exempt it with the blessing that he recites over a tallis gadol, as is explained above in siman 8 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 42. And regarding Birkas HaTorah, see above in siman 47 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 28. And regarding the blessing Elokai Neshamah and the blessing who removes sleep, see in siman 46 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 24. It is proper to be careful according to Kabbalah not to have relations on the night of Shavuot unless it is the night of immersion; and see what we wrote above in siman 240 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 7, and in the Beur Halacha there.

(12) Dairy foods: See Magen Avraham; and I further heard in the name of one great authority who said a proper reason for this: for at the time they stood at Mount Sinai and received the Torah [for in the Ten Commandments all parts of the Torah were revealed to them through this, as Rav Saadiah Gaon wrote that in the Ten Commandments the entire Torah is included], and they went down from the mountain to their homes, they did not find what to eat immediately except dairy foods; for meat requires much preparation: to slaughter with a checked knife as Hashem commanded, and to remove the cheilev strands and the blood, and to rinse and salt, and to cook in new vessels, for the vessels they had beforehand, in which they had cooked during that day-and-night period, became forbidden to them; therefore they chose for the moment dairy foods, and we make a memorial for this.

(17) A memorial for the two breads: And if so, they should be of wheat, like the two breads. The Peri Megadim wrote: one must be careful regarding meat and dairy foods in all that one is careful throughout the year, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, simanim 88 and 89, and that their reward not go out in their loss. He further wrote: in the formula of the haftarah blessing, "over the Torah and over the Avodah," etc., "this day of the festival of Shavuot"; but they do not mention to say "mikra kodesh," etc. And they mention souls and say Av HaRachamim on the second day; and in every place where they read "All the firstborn" they mention souls, for it contains "the gift of the hand," and they vow tzedakah. When Shavuot falls on Friday-and-Shabbos they do not say Chapters [of Avos]; and when Shavuot falls on Motzaei Shabbos they say Chapters at Mincha on Shabbos.
