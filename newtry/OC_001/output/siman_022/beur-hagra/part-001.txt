**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 1: One who purchased, etc.</b> The Tosefta at the end of Berachos states: one who makes tzitzis for himself says, “Blessed is He Who has enabled us to reach this time.” There it also teaches that one who makes tefillin for himself recites Shehecheyanu. Rambam likewise writes in chapter 11 of Hilchos Berachos: every mitzvah that comes at fixed intervals, such as shofar, sukkah, lulav, Megillah, and Chanukah lights; every mitzvah that constitutes an acquisition for a person, such as tzitzis, tefillin, mezuzah, and a parapet; and every mitzvah that is uncommon and not available at all times, and therefore resembles a mitzvah that comes at fixed intervals, such as circumcising one's son and redeeming a firstborn son, requires Shehecheyanu at the time it is performed.

Rambam derived the first category from sukkah, lulav, Megillah, and Chanukah lights, as stated in the fourth chapter of Sukkah, the second chapter of Shabbos, and the third chapter of Megillah. He derived the second category from the Tosefta cited above. He derived the third category from redemption of the firstborn at the end of Pesachim, and he calls it a mitzvah that comes at fixed intervals, as stated at the end of the third chapter of Eruvin.

Tosafos disagree. On Sukkah 46a, s.v. “One who makes,” they write that one does not recite a blessing when making tzitzis or tefillin. They prove this from Menachos 42b: Rabbi Yochanan does not list these among the mitzvos upon whose making one recites a blessing. The same logic would show that Shehecheyanu is not recited, since he did not list it. Likewise, no blessing is recited over circumcision, since it is not listed at the end of chapter 19 of Shabbos. Tosafos therefore disagree with Rambam's latter two categories and write that the Sages instituted Shehecheyanu only for mitzvos involving joy; see there. They maintain that the Tosefta cited above must be set aside in favor of our Gemara.

See Tosafos on Menachos 42b, s.v. “And these,” where they cite the Tosefta. The structure of their words is that the Tosefta supports the Gemara: it lists only Shehecheyanu, but not the blessing “to perform.” This is as Tosafos write in Sukkah in the preceding passage, s.v. “One who makes,” that one does not recite “to perform.” Although Shehecheyanu is also not mentioned in the Gemara, while the Tosefta says that it is recited, that is because of the new garment, and the law of new utensils was already mentioned in Berachos. When Tosafos cite Rav Sherira Gaon, their meaning is that he explains the Tosefta in this manner.

Only one difficulty remains: Tosafos contradict themselves. In Menachos they write that Rabbi Yochanan knew that he had been asked about the matter, rather than deriving the law merely from his omission. If so, the entire argument of Tosafos in Sukkah, proving from the omission that Shehecheyanu is not recited, falls away.

According to the Tosefta, Shehecheyanu is recited over tefillin as well, whether according to Tosafos' explanation or according to Rambam. According to Tosafos in Sukkah, however, it is not recited, in accordance with Baal HaIttur and Shulchan Aruch here.

See Yoreh Deah, siman 265, in Tur: Rambam writes that the father recites Shehecheyanu at every circumcision, while Baal HaIttur writes that he should not, and Rabbeinu Yitzchak writes the same. These are the views of Tosafos and Baal HaIttur mentioned above. Shulchan Aruch there records Rambam's view, while Rama records Tosafos and Baal HaIttur. Yet in Rama's gloss to siman 28, seif 2, he rules like Rambam.

Rambam's position is primary. Tosafos' proof is not compelling, as explained, and certainly cannot override the Tosefta's explicit statement. Beit Yosef likewise writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 265, in the name of Rashba that according to the strict law one should recite Shehecheyanu, as with redemption of the firstborn. The difficulty from Hallel remains, as Tosafos note in Sukkah, and their answer is insufficient, for Hallel certainly involves joy. One must therefore say that Hallel is not an independent mitzvah, as stated in Arvei Pesachim: “Could Israel slaughter their Pesach offerings and take their lulavim, etc.?”

Tur here likewise follows Rambam, for he copies the Tosefta word for word. Beit Yosef asks why Tur did not write the same concerning tefillin, and Mahari Abuhav and Beit Yosef infer from this that Tur follows Baal HaIttur. This is incorrect. Shehecheyanu is recited specifically by one who makes the item for himself, as Rambam writes, and as Tosafos and Rashi write in Sukkah, following the Gemara's phrase “one who makes for himself.” The Tosefta likewise says “for himself” regarding every mitzvah, and Tur writes the same here. This situation is uncommon with tefillin, because a scribe ordinarily makes them, as indicated on Chullin 9a. Regarding tzitzis, the matter must be understood as Rashi explains there.

<b>And if he did not, etc.</b> Rambam and Nimukei Yosef write likewise. This follows the passage in Sukkah: if the sukkah was already made, etc., and the account of Rav Kahana.
