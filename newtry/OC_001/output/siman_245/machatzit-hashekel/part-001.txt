<b>טול לעצמך כך וכך ימי' כו'</b> אין זה לשון הג"א וכן הוא ל' הג"א אפי' התנו אסור אלא שהרב"י לפי הבנתו דברי הג"א כ' כן מדעתו אבל לפמ"ש מ"א אח"ז אין הפי' כן. ונראה מתוך דבריהם דלא קי"ל כוותי'. דז"ל התוס' ספ"ק דע"ז מעשה כך הי' בימי ר"ת שגבה ישראל אחד בחובו תנור א' מגוי והיה לאופה המסיק חלקו בתנור וה"ל ליהודי ליטול כך וכך ימים בחלקו ולאופה כ"וכ ימים ולא התנו בתחלה כו' והיה ר"י ר"ל דמותר דל"ד שדה כו' ולא הודה לו ר"ת ונר' לה"ר אלחנן דבתנור אפי' התנה מתחלה לא יועיל כיון שכל התנור של ישראל וה"ל כמשכי' כו' טול שכר תנור בשבת כשתסיקנו לי בחול ול"ד לישראל וגוי כו' שיש לגוי חלק בגוף הקרקע וגם ר"י חזר בו והביא ראיה לר"ת כו' וגם ר"ת באותו מעש' דתנור צוה לקבל החוב מן הגוי ולחזור ולהלות ולהתנות מתחלה עכ"ל התוס' הרי במעשה דתנור שהאופה לא ה"ל חלק בו צוה ר"ת להתנות משמע דחולק על הרר"א הנ"ל דמהני תנאי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Take for yourself such-and-such days, etc.</b> This is not the wording of Hagahos Ashiri. Its wording is: “Even if they stipulated, it is prohibited.” The Beit Yosef, according to his understanding of Hagahos Ashiri, wrote this formulation on his own. According to Magen Avraham's explanation below, however, this is not the meaning. It appears from their words that we do not rule in accordance with that view. Tosafos at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah writes that in Rabbeinu Tam's time a Jew collected an oven from a non-Jew in payment of a debt. The baker who fired it had a share in the oven, and the Jew was to receive a certain number of days for his share and the baker a certain number of days for his share, without any prior stipulation. Rabbeinu Yitzchak wished to permit it, because it was unlike a field, but Rabbeinu Tam did not agree. Rabbeinu Elchanan held that regarding an oven even an initial stipulation would not help, because the entire oven belonged to the Jew, and it would be as though he said, “Take the oven's Shabbos earnings when you fire it for me on a weekday.” This differs from a Jew and non-Jew who jointly own land, where the non-Jew has a share in the land itself. Rabbeinu Yitzchak later retracted and brought proof for Rabbeinu Tam. In that very case Rabbeinu Tam instructed the Jew to accept payment of the debt, lend the money back, and stipulate from the outset. Thus, in the oven case, where the baker had no share in the oven itself, Rabbeinu Tam nevertheless instructed that an initial stipulation be made, indicating that he disagrees with Rabbeinu Elchanan and holds that a stipulation is effective.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It appears to me, on the contrary, from the fact that he wrote, etc.</b> The Rosh wrote that he collected half an oven. One cannot say that he merely reported the facts, because the incident cited by Tosafos in Rabbeinu Tam's time involved the Jew collecting the entire oven, while the baker had no share in it. The Rosh deliberately altered the case and wrote “half an oven,” meaning that half remained with the non-Jew, who therefore had an ownership share. In that case an initial stipulation is effective. If the Jew collected the entire oven and the non-Jew retained no share, the Rosh agrees with Rabbeinu Elchanan that a stipulation does not help. Hagahos Ashiri should therefore be understood in this manner, not as the Beit Yosef understood it to disagree with the Rosh. Rather, Hagahos Ashiri explains the Rosh: the Rosh mentioned specifically half an oven because where the non-Jew has no ownership share, he agrees that an initial stipulation is ineffective.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It appears to me that Tosafos, meaning Rabbeinu Elchanan, and Hagahos Ashiri prohibited only, etc.</b> Magen Avraham first rejected the Beit Yosef's inference that because the Rosh did not mention this law, he must disagree with it; Magen Avraham argued the opposite. He now rejects the Beit Yosef's further claim that Tosafos disagrees with Rabbeinu Elchanan because Rabbeinu Tam instructed the Jew to accept repayment and then stipulate from the outset. Magen Avraham explains that Tosafos and Hagahos Ashiri prohibited only where the arrangement resembles employing a worker. The Beit Yosef understood their prohibition, where the non-Jew has no ownership share, to be because the non-Jew acts as the Jew's agent. One cannot say that an initial stipulation removes the Jew's interest in the oven on Shabbos and leaves it exclusively to the non-Jew, since the non-Jew owns no part of it. According to this, even if the non-Jew receives the earnings of several days including Shabbos, it would remain prohibited. The Beit Yosef held that Rabbeinu Tam and the other poskim reject this reasoning. Magen Avraham, however, holds that even Rabbeinu Elchanan and Hagahos Ashiri agree that where the non-Jew has no ownership share, an initial stipulation prevents the arrangement from being considered employment. Their concern is instead that it constitutes direct Shabbos wages, as Magen Avraham explains. Therefore, it is prohibited only when the stipulation assigns the non-Jew the earnings of Shabbos alone. If the non-Jew receives the earnings of several days including Shabbos, it is absorbed Shabbos wages and is permitted. This is the type of stipulation Rabbeinu Tam allowed, as the wording of Tosafos cited above indicates. Thus, Rabbeinu Tam does not disagree with the Rosh and Hagahos Ashiri.
