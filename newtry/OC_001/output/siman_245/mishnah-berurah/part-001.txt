(א) ישראל וא"י וכו' - דע ההיתר גמור הוא באם הישראל יש לו איזה עסק או מלאכה בשותפות עם הא"י שיתנו מתחלה קודם שלוקחין בשותפות עבוד עתה בשבת ותקח כפי מלאכתך הן רב או מעט ואני אעבוד כנגדך יום אחד בחול ואטול כפי מלאכתי ובשעת חלוקה יטול הא"י שכר עבור השבת והישראל יטול יום א' כנגדו הן רב או מעט אבל אם לא התנו מתחלה ובשעת חלוקה אומר לא"י טול אתה כנגד עמלך בשבת ואני בחול זהו ודאי אסור ואם לא התנו מתחלה ובשעת חלוקה חלקו סתם בשוה זהו בעיא בגמרא ולא אפשיטא ופסק הרמב"ם לחומרא והרא"ש לקולא והמחבר סתם כהרמב"ם והרמ"א פסק בהג"ה כהרא"ש במקום הפסד גדול. ואם התנו קודם שבאו להשתתף כנ"ל ובשעת חלוקה נתרצו לחלוק בשוה זהו מותר אף להרמב"ם וכמש"כ בס"ב זהו עיקרי הדינים שבסעיפים הראשונים ויתר הפרטים וגם טעמיהן יבוארו לקמיה. שדה או תנור וכו' - טעם הדברים אלו דבעינן התנו דאל"ה כיון דמתחלה בשעת עשיית השותפות היה מוטל המלאכה על שניהם יחדו ולהכי אם אח"כ יאמר להא"י עשה אתה לבדך בשבת וטול לך כל הריוח של שבת ואני אעשה לבדי יום אחד בחול כנגדו ואטול אותו לעצמי הריוח של אותו יום הו"ל כאלו מעמיד את הא"י לפועל בידים כאלו אומר לו עשה אתה עבורי בשבת ואני אעשה נגד זה עבורך בחול והוי שלוחו ממש [תוספות] ולפ"ז אם שותפו הא"י בעצמו אינו עושה כלום רק שא"י אחרים הבאים לאפות מסיקין בו בתנור של השותפות ונותנים סך מה בשכרם יכול הישראל לקבל משותפו שכרו המגיע לו לפי חלקו אפילו אם לא התנו ובלבד שלא יקח ממנו משכר שבת לבדו אלא יקח ממנו סתם בהבלעת שאר ימים כדי שלא יהיה שכר שבת ולא חיישינן משום מראית העין שיאמרו לצורך ישראל הוא עושה כיון שידוע שיש לא"י חלק בו [מ"א וש"א]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) A Jew and a non-Jew, etc. The fully permissible arrangement is where a Jew and non-Jew jointly own a business or undertaking and stipulate before entering the partnership: “You work on Shabbos and take whatever your labor produces, whether much or little, and I will work one weekday in exchange and take whatever my labor produces.” At the division, the non-Jew receives the Shabbos earnings and the Jew receives the weekday earnings, whether much or little. If they made no initial stipulation and only at the time of division the Jew says, “Take the amount corresponding to your Shabbos labor and I will take the weekday earnings,” this is certainly prohibited. If they made no initial stipulation but later divided the profits equally without specification, the Gemara leaves the question unresolved. Rambam rules stringently and the Rosh leniently. The Mechaber follows Rambam, while Rama relies on the Rosh in a case of substantial loss. If they stipulated before entering the partnership and later agreed to divide equally, this is permitted even according to Rambam, as stated in seif 2. These are the basic rules of the opening seifim; the details and reasons follow. The reason an initial stipulation is required regarding a field or oven is that otherwise, when the partnership was formed, the work rested upon both of them together. If the Jew later tells the non-Jew, “You alone work on Shabbos and take all its profits, while I alone will work one weekday and take that day's profits,” it is as though he actively appoints the non-Jew as his worker, saying, “Work for me on Shabbos and I will work for you during the week.” The non-Jew is then truly his agent. According to this, if the non-Jewish partner performs no labor himself, but other non-Jews come to bake in the jointly owned oven and pay a fee, the Jew may receive from his partner the amount due according to his ownership share even without an initial stipulation. He must not, however, take payment specifically for Shabbos; he should receive it without specification together with the earnings of other days, so it is not direct Shabbos wages. There is no concern of appearance that the work is being performed for the Jew, since it is known that the non-Jew has an ownership share.

**** ENGLISH ****
(8) And they divided without specification. At the time of division the Jew did not mention Shabbos by saying, “You take the amount corresponding to your Shabbos labor and I will take the amount corresponding to my weekday labor,” but they divided equally without specification. The case also assumes that the Jew did not initially tell the non-Jew, “You work on Shabbos and I will work a corresponding weekday,” but the non-Jew worked on his own initiative. Otherwise all authorities prohibit it, because the Jew thereby revealed that the non-Jew was his Shabbos agent and that he labored for him during the week, since the work originally rested upon both. This entire seif concerns a case where the Jew and non-Jew jointly own the oven or bathhouse. If the non-Jew owns no share in the oven itself and merely receives a portion of the profits for baking and firing it, some hold that even an initial stipulation, “Take your portion on Shabbos and I one weekday,” is ineffective. Since the oven itself belongs to the Jew, it resembles renting the oven for Shabbos in exchange for the non-Jew firing it on a weekday, and the Jew thereby receives direct Shabbos wages. Magen Avraham concludes that this stringent view should be taken into account. This applies only where the payment is not absorbed with other days. If the Jew tells the baker, “Take two or three days,” it is permitted when this was stipulated at the start of the partnership. Some later authorities hold that where the non-Jew has no ownership share, an initial stipulation is unnecessary so long as the payment is absorbed with other days.

(13) The non-Jew agreed. This means that according to their initial stipulation, whether the amount was much or little, the non-Jew's Shabbos portion exceeded the Jew's one weekday portion, and at the time of division the non-Jew nevertheless agreed to divide equally. We do not say that it thereby becomes evident retroactively that the non-Jew had been the Jew's Shabbos agent. Rather, because they stipulated initially that Shabbos belonged exclusively to the non-Jew, the extra amount is merely a gift. This applies where the non-Jew agreed to equal division without calculating the profits of the two days. If the agreement followed an explicit calculation, with the Jew saying, “You earned one hundred zuz on Shabbos and I earned fifty on the weekday; make up my deficiency,” and the non-Jew then agreed to supplement him, the dispute noted above in seif katan 5 applies.

(15) If he wishes, etc. See Magen Avraham and Taz, who question this ruling sharply: why should it be permitted, when the leniency in siman 244 applies specifically because of financial loss? Magen Avraham concludes that the case concerns renting the Jew's share to the non-Jew with absorbed payment, meaning that he rents him all his Shabbosos together with two or three weekdays, so direct Shabbos wages are no longer involved. The allowance through contracted work likewise applies only where Shabbos was not specified, but the Jew says generally, “Whenever you work and earn such-and-such for me, I will pay you such-and-such.” Rama's principal novelty is that there is no concern of appearance that the non-Jew works for the Jew, because it is known that the non-Jew has an ownership share. The phrase “as explained” refers to the earlier case, where appearance was not a concern; all the more so here, where none exists because of the partnership. Taz likewise concludes that the arrangement is permitted only with absorbed payment. Nevertheless, the Jew should avoid discussing the business with the non-Jew on Shabbos, as the later authorities write at the end of siman 244.

(18) Likewise, there is no, etc. Meaning, there is also no concern of appearance that people will think the non-Jew is the Jew's employee, because the business is not publicly identifiable as the Jew's. If it were identifiable and called by the Jew's name, it would be prohibited, as stated in siman 243, seif 1, regarding a bathhouse. Even this leniency applies only when the Jew does not instruct the non-Jew to deal with the property on Shabbos, but the non-Jew does so on his own initiative, as stated similarly in seif 5.

**** ENGLISH ****
(23) If non-Jews baked. Bach, at the end of siman 243, writes that this refers to a case where they baked only on Shabbos. Therefore, even if they baked against the Jew's will, accepting the loaf would constitute receiving Shabbos wages. If they also baked on other days, he may receive payment for Shabbos together with the other days. Biur Halachah explains that this is permitted even if the baking was not against his will. Although he violated the law by allowing them to bake, as explained at the beginning of siman 243, their payment is not prohibited after the fact because the prohibition there is only due to appearance, as stated at the end of siman 243, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 16.
