<b>(ס"ק א) לשקיעת כו'</b> היינו סוף שקיעה כו' כוונתו דהטור כ וז"ל וזמנה סמוך לשקיעת החמה כשהחמה בראש האילנות ופי' הב"ח דסמוך לשקיעה היינו חצי שעה קודם השקיעה כדאמרי' גבי סמוך למנחה דר"ל חצי שעה קודם מנחה מ"מ מוכח מדברי הטור דסמוך לשקיעה וכשהחמה בראש האילנות חד זמן הוא וזמניהם שוה ולכן הקשה הב"ח על לשון הש"ע שכ' זמן תפלת נעילה כשהחמה כו' כדי שישלים אותה סמוך לשקיעת החמה הא זמן שניהם א' כנ"ל וע בט"ז מה שרצה לדחוק בזה ולכן תי' מ"א דהטור מיירי בתחלת שקיעה ובש"ע מיירי סוף השקיעה דהוא זמן ארוך אחר זמן שהחמה בראש האילנות שהוא חצי שעה קודם תחילת השקיעה ומתחלת השקיעה עד סוף השקיעה הוא שיעור ד' או ה' מיל לכל מר כדא"ל כדאי' בפסחים דף צד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if katan 1) To sunset, etc.</b> This means the end of sunset, etc. His intent is that the Tur wrote as follows: Its time is close to sunset, when the sun is at the tops of the trees. The Bach explained that close to sunset means half an hour before sunset, as we say regarding close to Minchah, meaning half an hour before Minchah. In any case, it is clear from the words of the Tur that close to sunset and when the sun is at the tops of the trees are one time, and their times are equal. Therefore the Bach challenged the language of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote that the time of the Ne'ilah prayer is when the sun is at the tops of the trees, so that he will complete it close to sunset; but according to what was said above, these are the same time. See the Taz, who tried to force an explanation of this. Therefore the Magen Avraham answered that the Tur is speaking of the beginning of sunset, whereas the Shulchan Aruch is speaking of the end of sunset, which is a long time after the time when the sun is at the tops of the trees, which is half an hour before the beginning of sunset. From the beginning of sunset until the end of sunset is the measure of four or five mil, each authority according to his view, as is found in Pesachim 94.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the Bach wrote that nowadays the practice is, etc.</b> For in the Yerushalmi, Rav and Rabbi Yochanan disagree, since for this reason they called this prayer Ne'ilah, after its time. Rav holds that its time is at the time of the closing of the gates of heaven; the Rambam wrote at the beginning of Hilchos Tefillah that this means that the gates of heaven are locked before the sun and it is hidden, namely at night. Rabbi Yochanan holds that its time is at the time of the closing of the gates of the Sanctuary, which is during the day. The Bach concludes that their dispute concerns the time of the end of the prayer, which is the main matter; according to Rabbi Yochanan, the conclusion of the prayer must be by day, namely when the sun is at the tops of the trees. That is the beginning of the prayer according to Rav, and the end of its time according to Rav is at night. Nevertheless, Rav agrees that initially, if possible, one should begin the prayer during the day, only that its conclusion should be at night.

**** ENGLISH ****
It is a day that obligated him in eating. Meaning: although when Rosh Chodesh falls during the week, if he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkas HaMazon, we do not send him back, because he is not obligated to eat bread on Rosh Chodesh; this differs from Shabbos, where if he forgot to mention Retzeh, we send him back, because on Shabbos he is obligated to eat bread, as above in siman 188. Nevertheless, when Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, even because he did not mention Rosh Chodesh we send him back, since the day is obligated in eating. Rabbi Eliezer would say that we do not send him back, as it says here, that were it not Shabbos, there would be no haftarah from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh. Meaning, as we say in Shabbos 24: Rav Gidel said in the name of Rav: When Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, the one who reads the haftarah from the Prophets on Shabbos need not mention Rosh Chodesh, for were it not Shabbos, there would be no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh. Therefore one does not mention Rosh Chodesh. So too here, were it not Shabbos there would be no eating today. Although it requires mention, nevertheless we do not send him back. End quote from the Hagahos Mordechai. The Beis Yosef understood that Rabbi Eliezer's statement that we do not send him back does not refer only to Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos, but to everything the Hagahos Mordechai wrote that we send him back; Rabbi Eliezer holds that we do not send him back, and he disagrees also about mentioning Shabbos in Ne'ilah. Although Rabbi Eliezer concluded, 'So too here, were it not Shabbos there would be no eating today,' which implies that he is speaking of mentioning Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos in Birkas HaMazon, nevertheless the Beis Yosef held that he disagrees also about the mention in Ne'ilah. The reason he used language relevant to Birkas HaMazon of Rosh Chodesh is that his proof is from the haftarah on Rosh Chodesh, so he too mentioned Rosh Chodesh; but the same can be said regarding Ne'ilah, that were it not Yom Kippur, there would be no Ne'ilah on Shabbos. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham disagrees and holds that Rabbi Eliezer disputes only the mention of Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos in Birkas HaMazon, but regarding the mention in Ne'ilah he agrees that if one did not mention Shabbos, we send him back. For Rabbi Eliezer's proof is from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., the one who reads the haftarah, etc. There the Gemara asks whether one should mention Chanukah in Mussaf: since there is no Mussaf on Chanukah, perhaps one does not mention it, or perhaps when Chanukah falls on Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh, it is the day that obligated him in four prayers, and he should mention it. The Gemara sought to resolve this from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., the one who reads the haftarah, etc., and so too one should not mention Chanukah in Mussaf. It rejects: Are they comparable? There, there is no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh at all; but here, on Chanukah, there is mention in the prayers of Ma'ariv, Shacharis, and Minchah. Therefore he mentions it also in Mussaf, and so we rule. If so, regarding Ne'ilah too, Rabbi Eliezer has no proof from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., for one can also say: since there is mention of Shabbos in the other prayers, Rabbi Eliezer must agree that also in Ne'ilah we send him back, for it is reasonable to say that this is the day that obligated him in four prayers.

דהא ב"ה אין שיעורו כ"א לערך רביע שעה כמ"ש לעיל סי' רס"א ור"ל כיון דהוי ב"ה ול"ג על שבות ב"ה במקום מצוה ואע"ג דבסי' שמ"ב מסתפק מ"א אי לא גזרו במוצאי שבת על שבות ב"ה דשאני אפוקי יומא כו' וצ"ל דהכא הקילו דכמה טעמים יש על תקיעה זו. ועס"ס רצ"ט. וגם שם רמז מ"א לעיין כאן. ור"ל אע"ג דבמוצאי שבת קודם שהבדיל בתפלה אסור אפי' טלטול נר מ"מ הקילו כאן:
**** ENGLISH ****
For bein hashmashos is only approximately a quarter of an hour, as written above in siman 261. His meaning is that since it is bein hashmashos, and we do not decree against a rabbinic prohibition during bein hashmashos in a case of mitzvah, it is permitted. Although in siman 342 the Magen Avraham is uncertain whether they refrained from decreeing on Motza'ei Shabbos against a rabbinic prohibition during bein hashmashos, for the departure of the day may be different, etc., one must say that here they were lenient, because there are several reasons for this blowing. See the end of siman 299. There too the Magen Avraham hinted to see here. His meaning is that although on Motza'ei Shabbos, before one has made havdalah in the prayer, even moving a candle is forbidden, nevertheless they were lenient here.
