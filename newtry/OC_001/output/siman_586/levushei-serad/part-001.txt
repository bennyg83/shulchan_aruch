**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham seif katan 4</small> And see above at the end of siman 454. It seems to me that his intention is to ask one question, as follows. In the Yerushalmi there are two reasons why one fulfills the obligation with a stolen shofar. One reason, brought in the Taz, seif katan 2, is that there is no derivation forbidding a stolen object. A second reason is that theft does not apply to sound. The practical difference between them is with a mitzvah performed through an act and not through sound, where there is no derivation excluding a stolen object. According to the first reason, one fulfills the obligation with a stolen object; according to the second reason, one does not, even though there is no derivation excluding it. The Rambam brought the reason that theft does not apply to sound, implying that in any mitzvah performed through an act one does not fulfill the obligation with a stolen object, even where there is no derivation excluding a stolen object. This also appears from the words of the Beit Yosef and the poskim in siman 691, who wrote that one fulfills the obligation with a stolen Megillah because theft does not apply to sound, implying that without this reason he would not fulfill the obligation. On this the Magen Avraham asked that at the end of siman 454, regarding stolen matzah, the Rosh did not say that one fails to fulfill the obligation with it except because there is a derivation from a verse, brought in the Taz there, which the Gemara derives by a gezeirah shavah from matzah to challah; see there. This seems to me to be the intention of the Magen Avraham, and it seems to me that this is itself the objection of the Raavad against the Rambam, brought in the Beit Yosef here; see there. This is easy to understand.

For they asked there, and in Yevamos, why one fulfills the obligation with that of a non-Jew, since this too should be considered as though crushed away, for from the moment he lifts it he acquires it. They therefore had to answer that he does not intend to acquire it, and they did not answer that it has owners. Perforce, nevertheless, since the Jew intends to acquire it and keeps it under his responsibility, and becomes liable for accidents, it is considered his, just as chametz with respect to the prohibition of it being seen, where he transgresses if he is liable for its responsibility, above in siman 440. Therefore they had to answer that he does not intend to acquire it, meaning that he does not intend at all to steal it, but only wishes to blow it. For if he intends to steal it, it immediately stands under his responsibility. This is somewhat difficult, for it appears that even if he took it only to blow it and wishes to return it after the blowing, nevertheless, since it is without the non-Jew's consent, he is considered a robber with respect to being liable for its responsibility. If so, this means that the case is where the non-Jew permitted him to blow it, and if so the words of the Tur brought by the Taz in seif katan 3 are not resolved. This is easy to understand, but requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> There is benefit in dwelling. The explanation of their words appears to be as follows: where one says, 'benefit from a shofar or sukkah is konam upon me,' we explain that his intent refers to the primary benefit. By contrast, blowing or dwelling for the sake of a mitzvah, which were not given for benefit, are not included in the benefit that he forbade upon himself. But when he did not mention benefit, but rather said, 'the blowing of a shofar is upon me,' or 'dwelling in a sukkah is upon me,' then we also explain that his intent is that he should not benefit from them. Although he did not mention benefit, nevertheless an unspecified vow is from benefit. For this reason there is a distinction: with dwelling in a sukkah, his wording includes even dwelling for a mitzvah, for even though its primary dwelling is only for the mitzvah, nevertheless, since there is also benefit, it is included in the category of dwelling in a sukkah which he forbade upon himself. Since he did not say, 'benefit from a sukkah is upon me,' but rather, 'dwelling in a sukkah is upon me,' even benefit from dwelling whose primary intent is not benefit, such as for a mitzvah, is included. This is not so with a shofar, for when he blows for the mitzvah there is no benefit at all, and we explain that his intent was to forbid blowing a shofar as music, but blowing for the mitzvah is not included. This, in my humble opinion, is the explanation of their words. Examine it carefully.
