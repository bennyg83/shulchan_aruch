<b>(In Magen Avraham s.k. 5)</b> As it is in Avodah Zarah folio 42, etc. and Rashi there wrote: perhaps he lifts it, for every ownerless thing (such as a find) is acquired by lifting; behold it is clear like Bach. But one may say that Magen Avraham's difficulty is from what we hold: that even an idol that has gentile owners, a Jew cannot nullify it; and therefore also Rashi means: since an ownerless thing is acquired by lifting, automatically even when it has owners we decree on account of [a case] where they have no owners; or perhaps he declares it ownerless and a Jew lifts it and afterward nullifies it. But the language of Magen Avraham does not imply so; and also from the reason he gives (because he is liable for its responsibility) it is clear that he holds that lifting helps him to be like his own; and according to what I wrote this is only because of a decree; and also what I wrote to explain that we decree on account of when it has no owners; this has no place, for still if he nullifies a gentile's before lifting, it is a decree upon a decree: perhaps he lifts before nullification, and perhaps he nullifies even when it has no owners. Therefore it appears to me in this like the view of Bach: that even though from the reason of a decree we say that nullification does not help, nevertheless since by Torah law nullification helps in such a case, "its measure is crushed" does not apply. And regarding Rashi's view it appears that he explains such as where a gentile lost [it] and a Jew found it, that it was acquired by him through lifting. And see Yoreh Deah siman 116, the view of Bach: that even if he did not acquire it (such as he found it before despair, or stole it) nevertheless it has no nullification by rabbinic law; and therefore he holds that nevertheless we do not say "its measure is crushed" in such a case, since he did not acquire it. And this requires further study according to what Taz wrote in Yoreh Deah there: that also when he acquired it only by rabbinic law, nullification does not help (see there. And what Magen Avraham wrote as a reason because of responsibility, and his proof from chametz) there is no proof, for there we amplify from "it shall not be found"; and also according to what I wrote in a responsum, regarding theft from a gentile there is no liability of responsibility even if stealing from a gentile is forbidden, for nevertheless "and he shall restore the stolen item" does not apply to it; and even though whatever the Merciful One said "you shall not do," if one did it it does not help; nevertheless if it was stolen or made [into something else] he is exempt; and so wrote Noda BiYehuda, Orach Chaim part, siman 88, and I elaborated there; if so it is obvious that there is no liability of responsibility here.

<b>(In Magen Avraham s.k. 6)</b> When it was nullified before Yom Tov, etc. his words need clarification. And it appears to me that his intent is: initially he wrote to explain the words of Rama, that whatever is not in his hand is dichuy, and here it is not in his hand; and in truth even the first opinion does not dispute this, only that they hold that even if it was not nullified at all we do not say regarding it "its measure is crushed," since it is possible that it be nullified by a gentile; but if nullification were needed, they too admit regarding this, specifically when we know that it will be nullified before Yom Tov, for otherwise we would have to be concerned for dichuy. And this matter is necessary for the method of the Tur and those with him: for the Gemara brings the inquiry of Resh Lakish whether a lulav of an idol is permitted for a mitzvah, and says: where it is forbidden to a commoner, you need not ask; when you ask is where they planted it and afterward worshipped it according to the Rabbis, lest it be repulsive for the Most High; when Rav Dimi came he said: an asherah that was nullified is what is inquired for him (whether there is dichuy or there is no dichuy; and that same [type of] inquiry applies for R' Papa regarding covering and uncovering, etc.) that R' Papa himself inquires, perhaps it is specifically for stringency (see there). And behold according to the Tur and those with him it is apparently difficult: how did he write plainly to permit even without nullification, for even with nullification it is inquired whether its lulav is permitted for a mitzvah because it is repulsive, even though permitted to a commoner; and all the more so when forbidden to a commoner and he did not nullify. And one should not say that he holds that according to what Rav Dimi said regarding an asherah that was nullified it is inquired (he holds there is no [concern of] repulsive here) for nevertheless it is proven that we require nullification, and therefore he holds that there is dichuy; and according to the Tur who holds that we do not require nullification, if so there is no dichuy here at all, for it was never deferred; and so Tosafos asked in Sukkah folio 32 on this method. And one must say that he holds that certainly Rav Dimi explains Resh Lakish's inquiry of an asherah that was nullified, and he holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and therefore before nullification it is dichuy, for it is forbidden in benefit, and he inquires whether there is dichuy regarding mitzvos, which is not so for the Tur and those with him, who hold like what Rava said in Rosh Hashana and in Sukkah, that if he took it he has fulfilled [his obligation] because mitzvos were not given for benefit, if so there is no dichuy here at all, and even without nullification he has fulfilled. (But one should not explain that Rav Dimi holds that so long as he did not nullify we say "its measure is crushed" even though it is of a gentile and it is possible to nullify it, for if so whence does the Tur rule not like him, since we do not find anyone who disputes him on this; rather certainly the Tur holds that everyone admits on this, only that Rav Dimi's reason is because he holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and we find Amoraim who dispute this, and we hold like Rava.) And accordingly it is fine that the Tur explains Resh Lakish's inquiry like Rav Dimi, and he holds there is no concern of repulsive at all, only that he requires nullification because of the prohibition of benefit, and we hold that mitzvos were not given for benefit. And one should not say that the Tur holds that Resh Lakish's inquiry is like the first wording because of repulsive, only that Rava disputes and holds there is no concern of repulsive at all; this is not so, for whence that Rava disputes? Perhaps Rava holds like Rav Dimi's wording that regarding an asherah that was nullified we inquire; rather certainly in truth the Tur holds to rule as law like Rav Dimi's wording that we are not concerned for repulsive, only regarding that he holds "a mitzvah was given for benefit" we do not hold like him. And now thus is the order of Magen Avraham's words: after he explained the Mordechai that Rama brought (that here it is "not in his hand," since it is not in his hand to nullify it, etc.) he wrote that this requires further study in Yoreh Deah: that is, regarding both the Tur and Rama; why did they rule there that there is no dichuy, for according to what he ruled here, all of them necessarily hold that Resh Lakish's inquiry was stated regarding dichuy (whether there is dichuy for leniency or not) and they should have ruled also there so; and without the words of the poskim here we would have said that they hold the main [view] is like Resh Lakish's first wording, that he inquired regarding repulsive, but regarding dichuy the matter of R' Papa comes as its plain sense both for leniency and for stringency, and therefore he ruled there regarding covering plainly that there is no dichuy; but after they ruled here that if they blew with a shofar of an idol he has fulfilled [his obligation] even without nullification, they necessarily hold that Resh Lakish's inquiry is as Rav Dimi said, if so it requires further study why he ruled in Yoreh Deah that certainly there is no dichuy, and he should have said he should cover it only out of doubt, etc. And upon this he wrote that the view of the Tur and the poskim is as Tosafos wrote in Avodah Zarah: that Resh Lakish's inquiry is only l'chatchila; and the difficulties of Tosafos in Sukkah are answered, meaning: forever he holds like the first wording that Resh Lakish's inquiry is regarding repulsive, but regarding dichuy the matter of R' Papa comes as its plain sense that there is no dichuy even for leniency; and therefore he properly ruled in Yoreh Deah that there is no dichuy; and nevertheless what they ruled here is fine; that without nullification it is also permitted, and if so dichuy does not apply; and from the reason of repulsive one need not be concerned, for repulsive is only l'chatchila alone; and all the difficulties of Tosafos in Sukkah are answered (including also this difficulty they asked: if also without nullification [it is valid], then what did he inquire regarding dichuy) for this is not so, for one may say that Rav Dimi holds like Rav Yehudah in Rosh Hashana who holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and we do not hold those. And upon this Magen Avraham wrote: and if so the law is thus, etc. and l'chatchila he should not take even after it was nullified because it is repulsive, etc., meaning: since we now stand like the first wording, that this inquiry deals even where it is permitted to a commoner, nevertheless l'chatchila he inquired because it is repulsive, if so even though it was nullified it is also repulsive; and so wrote Tosafos there that Resh Lakish deals even when it was nullified, and even so he inquires perhaps l'chatchila it is forbidden because it is repulsive.

<b>In Taz s.k. 10.</b> And behold regarding the explanation of the Rosh's words in Rosh Hashana concerning pierced and he sealed it, they struggled with this; that his words contradict: what he wrote "alternatively like R' Natan, and its majority was diminished," and again he asked according to the latter wording that the Rabbis admit when its majority remained, and he was not comfortable establishing it as "its majority was diminished." See Responsa Mahari Halevi, brother of Taz, siman 28; and my ancestor Maharsha emended that it should read as Tosafos wrote: "alternatively R' Natan, and with what is not of its kind" and this requires further study, for in Asheri he wrote in his difficulty that presumably it deals even of its kind, and if so one cannot answer that it comes like R' Natan and with what is not of its kind; and Tosafos in truth did not write this in their difficulty. Therefore it appears to my humble opinion that the Rosh there wrote that if he did not seal it, it is kosher, for all sounds are kosher, only that the sealing detracts, for it is the sound of a shofar and another matter; and accordingly it appears to me that this is the reason of R' Natan who distinguishes between of its kind and not of its kind: that of its kind it is not detracted because of the sealing and is not called another matter, since it is of its kind, which is not so for not of its kind, for it is the sound of a shofar and another matter. But all this is when its majority remained, which produces a shofar sound (only that it is not as it had been) [then] sealing of its kind helps, since without the sealing it was kosher and the sealing does not detract since it is of its kind. If so, when its majority was diminished, without the sealing there is no shofar sound here but mere puffing, as we say that it makes a barking sound; even of its kind it is invalid. And accordingly the Mishnah is fine like R' Natan: that whenever its majority remained, this is not called "impeding the blast" (what do we care that before the hole there was another sound, since all sounds are kosher) we have only to judge as it is now after the hole, that this sound was kosher and did not impede the blast at all, for the sound as it is after it was pierced is called a shofar sound, and it is not called "impeding the blast" except when its majority was diminished. (And what the Rosh wrote that even if its majority remained it is "impeding the blast" that is when we explain as its plain sense that "impeding the blast" means the sound is not as it was before it was pierced.) And accordingly the Mishnah properly comes like R' Natan: for the Mishnah that says "impeding the blast" means its majority was diminished (for if its majority remained, we do not call it "impeding the blast"; and he properly says that if it impedes the blast it is invalid) namely even of its kind, for R' Natan deals only specifically when its majority remained, then it is kosher of its kind; and that is what he means: if not that it does not impede the blast (namely that its majority remained) and all sounds are kosher, and there is no [problem] because of shofar sound and another matter that is of its kind, then it is kosher. But according to the other wording, that when its majority remained even not of its kind they are kosher, if so necessarily the sealing does not detract to disqualify because of shofar sound and another matter, for they permit even not of its kind; and if so even when its majority was diminished one cannot say that the disqualification is that the sealing detracts, for the sealing does not detract, for otherwise it would be invalid also when its majority remained; and since the sealing does not detract, one may say that it helps to permit, for through it a shofar sound is made; and therefore the reason is that since its majority was diminished it went out from the category of shofar and repair does not help it. And if so one cannot explain "if it impedes the blast" as "its majority was diminished," for we do not come upon it because of impeding the blast, but because it itself is not in the category of shofar; from the principle that all sounds are kosher in a shofar; and therefore when its majority remained is what is dealt with; and if so when its majority remained the Rabbis hold it is kosher. And also one may say that certainly it is not reasonable to permit sealing of its kind if its majority was diminished and there is no shofar sound at all here, that we should say it helps to permit it because of the sealing; and specifically regarding whether the sound through the sealing assists, then we say of its kind is not called shofar sound and another matter; but that the essence of the sound be through of its kind that seals it; it is not reasonable to permit. And that is in truth the reason of the first wording: that of its kind does not help when its majority did not remain; for in the first wording they hold that if its majority did not remain the shofar sound departed from here; and it is not reasonable that the latter wording dispute this; and therefore when he establishes in the latter wording "its majority was diminished," and even so permits of its kind; necessarily in the latter wording they hold that also when its majority was diminished and the sounds are not spoiled so much that it not be considered a shofar sound; rather also when its majority was diminished one may say all sounds are kosher; and therefore he distinguishes between of its kind and not of its kind, for assistance of what is not of its kind causes spoilage; but specifically when its majority was diminished, since it was broken so much, and its breaking proves upon it that it was broken so much that [it is like] the breaking of a vessel, the sealing is considered like the body of the shofar and is not nullified to it, which is not so when its majority remained, which is still a vessel: even though the hole impedes the blast, nevertheless its importance has not departed from it, for all sounds are kosher, and the sealing is nullified to the body of the shofar and we do not reckon it as shofar sound and another matter. And this is necessary: for if you say that a shofar sound whose majority was diminished is not considered a sound like in the first wording (if so why does of its kind help for R' Natan, who is reasonable like the first wording, to make from an invalid sound a kosher sound) for this sealing does not help at all; and therefore in the latter wording he holds that there well is a sound when its majority was diminished, only when its majority was diminished it is a shofar and another matter. And one should not say that the Rabbis hold not like R' Natan in this, and hold that when its majority was diminished its sound is not considered a shofar sound and therefore invalid even of its kind according to the Rabbis, for why make a dispute in this between the Rabbis and R' Natan, for simply one may say they hold that of its kind also detracts; and also if so it would be a dispute in fact: the Rabbis hold this is not a shofar sound and R' Natan holds this too is a shofar sound. It is fine to distinguish in this between the wordings: one holds that R' Natan and the Rabbis both hold it is not a shofar sound when its majority was diminished, and the latter wording holds that the Rabbis and R' Natan both hold it is a shofar sound; but if we establish the dispute of the Rabbis and R' Natan thus, it would be like a dispute of fact. And since we must say in the latter wording that there well is a shofar sound when its majority was diminished, only that they hold that since it has no importance the sealing detracts; (and accordingly one cannot establish the Mishnah like the Rabbis and when its majority was diminished..) one cannot establish the Mishnah like the Rabbis and when its majority was diminished, for if so what is this that it takes "impeding the blast" for it does not depend on this at all, for this is not called "impeding the blast," since all sounds are kosher; and if you say that "impeding the blast" means the blast that was before it was pierced, if so even when its majority remained it impedes the blast that was before the hole, and the Mishnah disqualified "impeding the blast"; and therefore [in] the wording the Rabbis permit when its majority remained, and if so it is difficult: the Mishnah is like whom? And the words of the Rosh are fine: that in the first wording we can well say that the Mishnah is like R' Natan and when its majority was diminished, for "its majority was diminished" and "impeding the blast" are one matter; which is not so in the latter wording, one must explain that "its majority was diminished" does not detract the sound more than when [majority] remained, and in both one may say all sounds are kosher, and the inferiority of diminished versus remaining is regarding that it is not considered important relative to the sealing, if so one cannot establish the Mishnah like the Rabbis, for the Mishnah makes the matter depend on impeding the blast.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>S.k. 12.</b> And it is similar to gold plating, etc. it appears he means: like there, through the gold its sound was changed, so too here through wrapping outside it a thread or cord one may say it causes a change of sound. Nevertheless if there is a [sufficient] measure, since there is the status of a shofar upon it without the lower part, one should not compare it to gold plating, for it appears that merely remaining a measure of tekiah above the plating toward the mouth does not help; whereas here we do not attribute the change of sound to the thread or cord to say that it is the sound of a shofar and another matter; rather we say the change of sound is because of the crack and we do not care, as written in seif 9 regarding cracked across its width and remaining, that it is kosher even if it impedes the blast; which is not so if a measure of tekiah did not remain: since without the thread and cord upon it the status of shofar departs from it, and only through the thread and cord with which it was tightened does the status of shofar appear again upon it; and since its sound did not return to what it had been, we are concerned for the change of sound like sealed with what is not of its kind and the like (so wrote Magen Avraham s).k. 11.
