<b>But if, etc.</b> See Nedarim 16b and Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah. As Ran and Rosh explain there, Rava disagrees with Abaye only when one prohibits benefit, because mitzvos were not given for personal benefit. But if he says, “the sitting,” etc., the prohibition takes effect, since he expressly prohibited the act upon himself, just as one may prohibit himself from throwing a stone into the sea even though he derives no benefit from it. Ran writes that if he merely says, “This sukkah is forbidden to me,” he may sit in it for the mitzvah, because he did not expressly prohibit the act of sitting. This is why the ruling here speaks of “its sounding being forbidden to me,” for he expressly mentioned its sounding. Ran further asks that vows do not take effect upon something intangible, and answers that the case is where he said, “This sukkah is forbidden with respect to its sitting.” Tosafos write that even if he says, “Sitting in this sukkah is forbidden to me,” it is effective because he mentions the tangible object. This depends upon the ruling at the end of the first chapter, seif 13, concerning one who says, “My mouth shall be forbidden,” etc. Ran likewise writes that the statement is treated as though he said it in that form, and this is the ruling in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 213. See the gloss there concerning one who says, “My speech,” etc. This is also implied by the words of Tosafos cited by Ran concerning sitting in a sukkah. The same applies here: he must say, “The sounding of this shofar is forbidden to me.” See Beit Yosef in siman 239. Nevertheless, it is prohibited D’Rabbanan even if he did not mention the tangible object, as stated there on 14a and as Ran writes there at the end of the first chapter.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he sealed it, etc.</b> This is Rambam’s view, that Rabbi Nasan refers to the latter clause of the Mishnah, and the other poskim explain Rambam accordingly. In a pressing situation one may rely upon Tosafos. Rambam’s proof is from the Yerushalmi there: “A shofar that split and was joined is invalid. For whom was this teaching necessary? For Rabbi Nasan, who says that joining fragments of shofaros is invalid. This too is Rabbi Nasan’s law concerning a shofar that was punctured and sealed.” Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that it is Rabbi Nasan’s view, for it was taught: if it was punctured and sealed, whether with its own material or with another material, if it impedes the sounding it is invalid, and if not it is valid; Rabbi Nasan says, with its own material it is valid, with another material it is invalid. Since the Yerushalmi had to interpret the Mishnah as referring to another material and according to Rabbi Nasan, it follows that Rabbi Nasan addresses the latter clause, while the Sages invalidate it in every case. Likewise, joining fragments is considered another material when glue is used, according to Rashi. Tosafos object that the first Tanna’s ruling, “if it impedes the sounding,” is stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi and likewise clearly in the Tosefta. One must say that Rambam did not have that reading. Tosafos therefore explain that Rabbi Nasan refers to the first clause, as the baraisa explicitly states: the first Tanna says, “whether with its own material,” etc., and Rabbi Nasan says, “with its own material,” etc. According to this, the majority of the shofar must always remain, for otherwise it is considered removed. Tosafos understand the Yerushalmi to mean that the ruling applies even according to Rabbi Nasan, and all the more so according to the first Tanna. Since the first clause asks, “For whom was this teaching necessary?” it means that according to the Sages, who hold that a repair impeding the sounding is invalid, there was no need to state separately that a split shofar or joined fragments are invalid. According to Rabbi Nasan, however, it was necessary, since he validates a repair with the same material even where it impedes the sounding, and a joined split is considered the same material, as Rosh writes, and certainly according to Ramban, who explains that it was heated in fire until its edges adhered. The Yerushalmi then challenges from the latter clause, “It was punctured and sealed,” which reflects the first Tanna’s view in the baraisa, and Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that this too is Rabbi Nasan’s view. It then cites the baraisa: “It was punctured and sealed, whether with its own material or another material; Rabbi Nasan says, with its own material,” etc. Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: “This is the proper reading of our Mishnah: if it impedes the sounding it is invalid, and if not it is valid.” Meaning, not as initially assumed that Rabbi Nasan makes no distinction between whether it impedes the sounding or not. Rather, Rabbi Nasan’s invalidation with another material follows the Mishnah only where it impedes the sounding. This is why Rabbi Chiya says, “This is the proper reading of our Mishnah,” and follows his view that the Mishnah should be established according to Rabbi Nasan. Their interpretation is primary.

<b>וי"א. </b>דרובו ככולו. מ"מ וכדעת תוס' ורא"ש ור"ן בשם הרשב"א דמשום הכי ל"ק ונשתייר כו' דוקא לכל ארכו אלא דס"ל דרובו ככולו וכמ"ש שם בניקב והוא שנשתייר רובו אבל דעת תוס' וש"פ דוקא כל ארכו ממש וכ"כ הרוקח וכת' וכמו גרגרת דפסיקתו לרחבו ברובה ולארכו אפי' לא נשאר אלא משהו למעלה ולמטה כשר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Some say.</b> Because the majority is treated as the whole. Maggid Mishneh, following Tosafos, Rosh, and Ran in the name of Rashba. Accordingly, the requirement that “there remained,” etc., is not limited to a split along its entire length; rather, they hold that the majority is treated as the whole, as stated there regarding a puncture, provided that the majority remained. Tosafos and the other poskim, however, require specifically that the entire length remain intact. Rokeach likewise writes that it resembles the windpipe, whose severance across its width is determined by the majority, whereas lengthwise it remains valid even if only a minimal amount remains above and below.

<b>ס"כב אם א"י. </b>להג"א דוקא בכה"ג לפי שאינו מוקצה הואיל שמותר לישראל אחר ומותר בטלטול כמש"ל סי' תקט"ו ולכן מותר ג"כ לתקוע דמצות לאו ליהנות ניתנו לפי' רש"י דאוסר משום שלא יהנה ממלאכת י"ט וגם לפי' תוס' דאוסר משום שלא יאמר ליכא למיחש שיחטא ויעשה מצוה בעבירה אבל המרדכי מתיר אפי' במוקצה מהא דפ"ג דפסחים כמש"ש למטה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 22: If a non-Jew, etc.</b> According to Hagahos Ashri, this applies specifically in such a case, because it is not muktzeh, since it is permitted to another Jew, and it may therefore be moved, as explained above in siman 515. It may likewise be sounded, because mitzvos were not given for personal benefit. This accords with Rashi, who forbids use because one may not benefit from melachah performed on Yom Tov, and also with Tosafos, who forbids it lest one instruct the non-Jew, for there is no concern that one will sin in order to perform a mitzvah through a transgression. Mordechai, however, permits it even where it is muktzeh, based upon the third chapter of Pesachim, as explained below.
