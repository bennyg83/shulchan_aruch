נקט איל דשאר כפופים נמי שרי לכתחלה אלא טעמא דמנהגא קאמר  וה"ק למה תוקעין בשופר ומחזירין עליו לעשו' מצוה מן המובחר כדי שיזכור עקידת יצחק עכ"ל ונ"ל דל"ד של איל אלא ה"ה כל מיני כבש הם נכללים בכלל זה נכלל בכלל כבש כרמב"ם ואע"ג שכתב הכ"מ שאין זה במשמעות דברי הרמב"ם ופירש בדרך אחר הנלע"ד כתבתי והו' מוכח דאל"כ למה לא זכר מדין איל כלום שזה זכר לעקיד' יצחק אלא ודאי שכל אלו נכללי' בכלל איל שהוא כבש ולא בא להוציא אלא פרה ושאר בהמות ואם כן הוה שיטת הרמב"ם כשיטת הרא"ש והטור אלא שהמהדרין מן המהדרין נוהגי' לחזור אחר של איל דוקא דהוא זכר טפי לאילו של יצחק כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Of a ram and bent, etc.</b> Even though the Shulchan Aruch grouped these two in one category, nevertheless they are not equal: for bent is the essence of the mitzvah l'chatchila, and they have not fulfilled their obligation l'chatchila with a straight [shofar]; but of a ram is not called l'chatchila, but rather a preferred mitzvah. So it is implied in the Tur, who wrote: it turns out there are three distinctions in it, namely, of a cow is entirely invalid even b'dieved: this is part one; and other species such as goats and ibex that are straight are kosher b'dieved: this is part two; and bent ones are kosher l'chatchila: this is part three. And afterward he wrote: nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah to seek one of a ram, to remember the Binding of Yitzchak, and that is outside the count of the three he took first, for the three are from the side of law, but of a ram does not enter at all into the category of law except from the side of preferred mitzvah and extra carefulness. And so wrote Beis Yosef. And that the Tur wrote first, "A shofar fit to blow with (how so? That it be of a ram and bent, etc.") in this he also included preferred mitzvah in the general rule; and what he wrote afterward, "or that he has no ram and bent," he said by way of "not only this but also that": not only if he has no ram (obviously it is permitted b'dieved) but even if he has no bent; and therefore he concluded at the end: and l'chatchila we require that it be bent, even of goats. And nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah to seek one of a ram, behold they are not equal, and one should not err and say that this "nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah" is the third part, as some wrote, for that is not so: if so he should not have said "and nevertheless a preferred mitzvah etc." but "and a preferred mitzvah etc.," since this is one of the parts; rather certainly he taught us that even though this is not from the side of law, nevertheless it is from the side of fine choice for one who can. And so it is implied explicitly in the Asheri, who wrote: and l'chatchila the mitzvah is with bent ones, and with all bent ones they blow, etc. And that R' Abahu said, "Why do they blow with a shofar of a ram?" he did not take "ram" specifically, for other bent ones are also permitted l'chatchila; rather he stated the reason of the custom: and thus he meant: why do they blow with a shofar and seek after it to make a preferred mitzvah (so that He remember the Binding of Yitzchak) end quote. And it appears to me that not only of a ram, but likewise all kinds of sheep are included in this general rule to recall the Binding of Yitzchak; for in siman 583 he wrote that they eat a sheep's head, namely as a remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. And with this the words of the Rambam are fine to me, who wrote as follows: The shofar with which they blow, whether on Rosh Hashana or in the Jubilee, is the horn of sheep, bent; and all shofaros are invalid except the horn of a sheep (end quote. And it is difficult: why did he need to mention "except the horn of a sheep," since he already mentioned it first) he should have said "and all other shofaros are invalid"; and further, why did he not also mention in the latter clause the bent sheep? Rather it appears to me that he said two things: first he said l'chatchila it should be a bent sheep; and afterward he said b'dieved the rest are invalid except the horn of a sheep, namely even straight, which is kosher b'dieved; and in this "sheep" he included all species such as goats and ibex (and there is a proof for this from the verse "ox, sheep of lambs and sheep of goats; deer and gazelle and yachmur," and yachmur means ibex; and just as he counted ram after mentioning sheep of lambs, so he also mentioned yachmur which is in the category of goats, and its name proves it, for they call it Ashtenbak as Rashi explains, namely like goats, whose males are called bak; so too these are called Shtenbok, for its growth is on the rock which is stone). So wrote Beis Yosef, and so it is implied in the name of Kol Bo. And if you say that everything is included in the category of sheep like the Rambam (and even though Kesef Mishneh wrote that this is not in the implication of the Rambam's words and explained another way) what appears to me I wrote; and it is proven: for otherwise why did he not mention anything of the law of a ram as a remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak? Rather certainly all these are included in the category of ram, which is a sheep, and he came only to exclude a cow and other animals. And if so the Rambam's method is like the method of the Rosh and the Tur; except that the most meticulous of the meticulous custom to seek specifically one of a ram, for it is more of a remembrance of the ram of Yitzchak, as appears to me.

<b>הגוזל שופר כו'. </b> בירושלמי מה בין זה ללולב בלולב כתיב ולקחתם לכם משלכם ברם הכא כתיב יום תרועה יהיה לכם מ"מ נראה פירושו דגבי לולב כתיב אצל לקיחה משמע שהלקיח' תהיה משלכם וכן בציצית כתיב ועשו להם דרשינן העשיה משלהם ולא גזול משא"כ כאן כתיב יום תרועה יהיה לכם סמוך לכם לתרועה ולא נזכר עשית השופר כלל שפי' שתרועה תהיה מ"מ לכם יהיה מאיזה צד שיהיה בלא עשייה כלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who steals a shofar, etc.</b> In the Yerushalmi: what is the difference between this and a lulav? Regarding a lulav it is written "And you shall take for yourselves" of your own; but here it is written "A day of teruah shall be for you." Nevertheless its meaning appears: that regarding a lulav it is written next to the taking, implying that the taking should be of your own; and likewise regarding tzitzis it is written "And they shall make for them" we expound that the making be of theirs and not stolen; which is not so here, where it is written "A day of teruah shall be for you," with "for you" adjacent to teruah, and the making of the shofar is not mentioned at all, meaning that the teruah should in any case be for you from whatever side it may be, without any making at all.

<b>קונם לתקיעתו עלי. </b> זהו ל' נדר דאסר החפץ עליו והנדרים חלים ע"ד מצוה כדבר הרשות כמ"ש בי"ד סי' רט"ו  ממילא אם אמר שלא אשמע קול שופר הוא ל' שבועה שאוסר נפשו על החפץ ואין שבועה חלה ע"ד מצוה  וב"י הביא בשם התו' שכתבו בפ"ב דנדרים גבי סוכה  שאם או'  ישיבת סוכה עלי קונם אפילו אם אומר שלא אשב בסוכה אסור לישב בסוכה של מצוה ה"נ בשופר דאם אמר תקיעות שופר עלי קונם אסור לתקוע אם לא לחלק דגבי סוכה בלא מצוה  יש הנאה בישיבה אבל שופר אי לאו מצוה אין הנאה בתקיע' דאינו מתכוין לשיר עכ"ל ואם אומ' על אדם אחר שלא אשמע קול שופר ממנו או תקיעתו עלי (קונם) נראה פשוט דחל אליבא דכ"ע דהא אפשר לשמוע מאחר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Konam regarding its blowing upon me.</b> This is the language of a neder, forbidding the object upon him; and nedarim take effect regarding a mitzvah-matter as regarding a voluntary matter, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 215. Automatically, if he said that he will not hear the sound of a shofar, that is the language of an oath, forbidding himself regarding the object, and an oath does not take effect regarding a mitzvah-matter. And Beis Yosef brought in the name of Tosafos who wrote in the second chapter of Nedarim regarding a sukkah: that if he says, "Sitting in a sukkah upon me as a konam," even if he says that he will not sit in a sukkah, it is forbidden to sit in a sukkah of mitzvah (so too regarding a shofar: if he said, "Shofar blasts upon me as a konam," it is forbidden to blow) unless one distinguish that regarding a sukkah even without a mitzvah there is enjoyment in sitting, but a shofar without a mitzvah has no enjoyment in blowing, for he does not intend song (end quote). And if he says regarding another person that he will not hear the sound of a shofar from him, or "his blowing upon me (as a konam)," it appears simple that it takes effect according to everyone, for it is possible to hear from another.

<b>אם ניקב כו'. </b> יש בזה חילוק דיעות בין הרמב"ם להרא"ש דאי' בגמ' ת"ר ניקב וסתמו בין במינו בין שלא במינו פסול ר"נ או' במינו כשר שלא במינו פסול וקי"ל כר"נ ואמרי' אליביה דהא דמכשר במינו היינו דוקא שנשתייר רובו ורמב"ם מפ' דר"נ קאי אאם אין מעכב התקיעה דהיינו אחר סתימה חזר קולו לכמות שהיה תחלה ואפ"ה אין כשר אלא במינו נמצא בעי' ג' למעליותא אינו מעכב התקיעה ובמינו ונשתייר רובו אבל אם חסר א' מאלו אסור זו היא הדיע' הראשונ' שמביא כאן והרא"ש ס"ל דר"נ קאי אמעכב התקיעה ואפ"ה כשר במינו ממילא הוה לקולא דלא בעינן אלא תרתי דהיינו מינו ורובו ואפי' מעכב או רובו ואינו מעכב ואפי' אינו מינו וזהו שסיים כאן ואם הוא שעת הדחק כו' שיש לסמוך על הרא"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it was pierced, etc.</b> There is a difference of opinions on this between the Rambam and the Rosh. For it is taught in the Gemara: Our Rabbis taught: If it was pierced and he sealed it, whether with of its kind or not of its kind, it is invalid; R' Natan says: of its kind it is kosher, not of its kind it is invalid. And we hold like R' Natan; and we say according to him that that which he permits of its kind is specifically when its majority remained. And the Rambam explains that R' Natan refers to [a case] where it does not impede the blast (namely after sealing, its sound returned to what it had been initially) and even so it is kosher only of its kind; it turns out we require three [conditions] for excellence: it does not impede the blast, and of its kind, and its majority remained; but if one of these is lacking it is forbidden; this is the first opinion that he brings here. And the Rosh holds that R' Natan refers to [a case] where it impedes the blast, and even so it is kosher of its kind; automatically this is a leniency, for we require only two (namely of its kind and its majority) and even if it impedes; or its majority and it does not impede, even if not of its kind. And this is what he concluded here: and if it is a time of urgency, etc. that one may rely on the Rosh.

It is fine if now it returned exactly to the first sound: we say that through the sealing that was of its kind it became exactly the first creature, since the sound truly returned thus; which is not so if no [new] sound was born except as it was before the sealing (then it was not made like the first creature, and automatically the sound born now is another matter, and therefore invalid) as appears to me.
