<b>1) [Seif 1] The shofar of Rosh Hashanah, etc. </b>This excludes a communal fast, on which they also sound a shofar, for there it need not be from a ram or curved. Mishna Berurah, s.k. 1. Alternatively, it excludes Yom Kippur of the Jubilee year, regarding which there is an opinion that it must be straight, as explained in the Beit Yosef.

<b>ג) שם. מצותו בשל איל וכפוף.  </b>וה"ה כל מיני כבש (ר"ל אפי' נקבה) הם נכללים בכלל זה להזכיר עקידת יצחק שהרי בסי' תקע"ג כתב ואוכלין ראש כבש והיינו לזכר עקידת יצחק אלא שהמהדרין נוהגין לחזר אחר של איל דוקא שהוא זכר טפי לאילו של יצחק. ט"ז סק"א. א"ר או' א' מט"א או' א' מ"ב או' ב'.

Indeed, in siman 583 it is written that one eats the head of a sheep, and that is in remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. Nevertheless, those who are meticulous customarily seek specifically a ram's horn, since a male more closely resembles the ram of Yitzchak. <b>ד) שם. מצותו בשל איל וכפוף.  </b>אע"ג דנקט הש"ע הני תרתי בחדא מחתי מ"מ אינם שוים דכפוף הוא עיקר המצוה לכתחלה ולא יצא י"ח לכתחלה בפשוט אבל בשל איל אינו מקרי לכתחלה אלא מצוה מן המובחר. ט"ז שם. מיהו המאמ"ר או' א' כתב הגם שמדברי מרן בב"י בביאור דברי הטור והפו' מבואר דהא דבעינן של איל אינו אלא למצוה מן המובחר ואינו מעכב אפי' לכתחלה כל שהוא כפוף אמנם מסתמיות דבריו כאן בש"ע משמע דכל שאר השופרות אינם כשרים אלא בדיעבד יעו"ש. וכ"ה דעת הב"ח דשאר שופרות שאינם של איל אפי' הם כפופים אינם כשרים אלא בדיעבד יעו"ש. ונ"מ דאם יש שופר של איל ואינו כפוף ושופר שאינו של איל וכפוף לדעת הט"ז כפוף שאינו של איל הוא יותר מוקדם כיון דשל איל אינו אלא למצוה מן המובחר ושאינו כפוף בדיעבד ולדעת הב"ח והמאמ"ר של איל ואינו כפוף ושאינו של איל וכפוף הם שוים כיון שזה בדיעבד וזה בדיעבד. וכ"מ ממ"ש לעיל או' ב' דכל חד אית ביה חדא לטיבותא אבל כיון דלהב"ח והמאמ"ר הם שוים ולהט"ז כפוף שאינו של איל כשר אפי' לכתחלה יש להקדים הכפוף דהוי אליבא דכ"ע. וכ"כ מ"ב או' ה' דמסקנת רוב הפו' כפוף עדיף יעו"ש.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>4) Ibid. Its mitzvah is with a ram's horn, and curved. </b>Although the Shulchan Aruch mentions these two requirements together, they are nevertheless not equal. A curved horn is part of the primary mitzvah l'chatchila, and one does not fulfill the obligation l'chatchila with a straight horn. By contrast, a ram's horn is not called a l'chatchila requirement, but rather the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah. Taz, ibid. However, Maamar Mordechai, letter 1, wrote that although it is evident from the words of Maran in the Beit Yosef, in his explanation of the Tur and the poskim, that the requirement of a ram's horn is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah and does not invalidate even l'chatchila so long as the horn is curved, nevertheless, the plain implication of his words here in the Shulchan Aruch is that all other shofaros are valid only b'dieved; see there. This is also the view of the Bach, that other shofaros which are not from a ram, even when curved, are valid only b'dieved; see there. A practical difference arises where one has a straight ram's horn and a curved horn that is not from a ram. According to the Taz, the curved horn that is not from a ram takes precedence, since being from a ram is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah, whereas a straight horn is valid only b'dieved. According to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai, the straight ram's horn and the curved horn that is not from a ram are equal, since each is valid only b'dieved. This also appears from what was written above in letter 2, that each one possesses one advantage. Yet since according to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai they are equal, while according to the Taz the curved horn that is not from a ram is valid even l'chatchila, the curved horn should be given precedence, since this accords with all opinions. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, s.k. 5, that the conclusion of most poskim is that a curved horn is preferable; see there.

<b>5) All </b>of this applies only on Rosh Hashanah itself, when the time for sounding has arrived and they are deliberating as to which shofar is more fit to sound. Before Rosh Hashanah, however, when the obligation of sounding has not yet taken effect, one may purchase a goat's horn even though a ram's horn is also available, but it must be purchased for a high price, and he knows that there will be no ram's horn in his city on Rosh Hashanah. Even so, there is no obligation to purchase the ram's horn for a high price. Rather, it is governed by the law of enhancing a mitzvah up to one-third, but no more, as explained below regarding the lulav. Bach; Ateres Zekeinim. Mateh Ephraim, letter 1; Mishna Berurah, s.k. 5. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>6) Ibid. B'dieved, all shofaros are valid. </b>For example, those of ibexes and goats. Tur; Levush. An ibex is a species of wild animal, as stated in Parashas Re'eh, 'the wild ox, the ibex, and the antelope,' which Onkelos translates, 'the wild ox and the ibex.' The Ran likewise concluded in the chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din, unlike the Aruch, who explained it as a female sheep; see there. This is also explicitly proven in chapter 1 of Kilayim, Mishnah 6, unlike the Aruch; see there. Nevertheless, if one has the horn of a male goat or a female goat, he should give it precedence over the horn of an ibex or other wild animal, for it is closer to the species called seh, as it is written, 'a seh from the sheep and a seh from the goats,' and there is at least some remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. Mishna Berurah, s.k. 4. One can further say that this is because goats are fit to be offered upon the Altar, unlike wild animals.

<b>ז) שם. ובדיעבד כל השופרות כשרים.  </b>והרמב"ם (ריש פ"א דשופר) כתב כל השופרות פסולים חוץ מקרן הכבשים והכל תפשו עליו בזה. טור. מיהו באו"ח כתב אפשר שדעת הרמב"ם אינו אלא למעט של פרה אבל של עז ותיש בכלל כבש הם וכן של יעלים עכ"ל והב"ד ב"י. וכ"כ בכ"מ שם אלא שכתב שאין זה במשמעות דברי הרמב"ם והב"ד הט"ז סק"א יעו"ש. וכתב בס' נחפה בכסף דף קפ"ד דבמקום שקבלו עליהם הוראות הרמב"ם אם אין להם שופר של כבש כפוף אין להם לברך בשאר שופרות יעו"ש. והב"ד הברכ"י או' א' וכתב דיש לפקפק דכיון דאיכא דמאן דמפרש בדברי הרמב"ם דשאר שופרות כשרים יכולין לברך דהגם דפירוש מרן ודעמיה בדעת הרמב"ם נראה נכון מ"מ מידי ספיקא לא נפיק וכיון דשאר פו' פשיטא להו דיברך ויש להסתפק קצת שדעת הרמב"ם כן כמו שפירשו שאר מפרשים אינו בכלל קבלתם לסמוך על הרמב"ם עכ"ל.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>7) Ibid. B'dieved, all shofaros are valid. </b>The Rambam, at the beginning of chapter 1 of Hilchos Shofar, wrote that all shofaros are invalid except for the horn of sheep, and everyone challenged him regarding this. Tur. However, the Orchos Chaim wrote: It is possible that the Rambam's intent was only to exclude the horn of a cow, while the horns of a goat and a male goat are included in the category of sheep, and likewise the horns of ibexes; end quote. The Beit Yosef cited his words. So too wrote the Kesef Mishneh there, although he wrote that this is not the implication of the Rambam's words. The Taz, s.k. 1, cited his words; see there. The sefer Nechpeh BaKesef, folio 184, wrote that in a place where they accepted the rulings of the Rambam, if they do not have a curved sheep's horn they should not recite a blessing over other shofaros; see there. Birkei Yosef, letter 1, cited his words and wrote that this may be questioned. Since there are those who explain the Rambam's words as holding that other shofaros are valid, they may recite a blessing. Although Maran's explanation, and that of those who agree with him, regarding the Rambam's view appears correct, nevertheless the matter has not left the realm of doubt. Since the other poskim plainly hold that one recites a blessing, and there is at least some doubt that the Rambam also holds so, as the other commentators explained him, this is not included in their acceptance to rely upon the Rambam; end quote.

<b>17) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Likewise, a shofar from a non-kosher animal is invalid. </b>This is because it is stated, 'So that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,' meaning that everything used for the Torah of Hashem must be from that which is permitted in your mouth. Although it comes from a neveilah or another prohibition, it is nevertheless called 'in your mouth,' because its species is permitted in your mouth, as we say regarding tefillin and a Sefer Torah. The same applies to every sacred article, which must be from that which is permitted in your mouth. Levush; Ateres Zekeinim; Magen Avraham, s.k. 3. However, the Ran in the chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din wrote that the statement in the chapter Bameh Madlikin, folio 28, that only the hide of a kosher animal was deemed fit for sacred articles, and a shofar is also a sacred article since it comes for remembrance, is not a conclusive proof. Therefore, the matter requires further study; see there. The Beit Yosef and Olas Shabbos, letter 1, cited his words. Therefore, Olas Shabbos wrote there that if no other shofar is available, one should sound such a shofar without a blessing. So too wrote Mateh Ephraim, letter 3, and Mishna Berurah, s.k. 8.

<b>23) It appears </b>that one may take another person's shofar without his knowledge and recite a blessing over it, as written in siman 649, seif 5. Although a shofar is somewhat damaged through sounding it, a tallis is also somewhat damaged through its use, and nevertheless it is permitted, as written above in siman 14. There is no concern that he will sound it excessively, as there is regarding sefarim, above siman 14, letter 31, for why would he sound more than is required for his obligation?
