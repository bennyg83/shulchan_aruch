(ה) ומצוה בכפופים וכו' - ר"ל דלאו דוקא באיל אלא אפילו בשאר בהמות וחיות ג"כ כפוף עדיף מפשוט. והנה המחבר סתם ולא ביאר איל פשוט וכפוף משאר מינים איזה מהן עדיף אכן מסקנת רוב הפוסקים דמעלת כפוף עדיף לפיכך אם נזדמן לו של יעל כפוף ושל כבש פשוט יתקע בשל יעלים וכפוף דתקון רבנן לתקוע בכפוף לסימן שיכפפו לבם למקום בתפלה משא"כ שופר של איל אינו תיקון רבנן לתקוע דוקא בשל איל אלא מנהג שנהגו כל ישראל מעולם לזכר עקידה ואיכא בזה מצוה מן המובחר. ומ"מ כתבו המפרשים דגם בזה יש עכ"פ דינה דהידור מצוה וע"כ אם מבקשים ממנו להוסיף על שופר של איל יוסיף עד שליש וכעין המבואר לקמן בסימן תרנ"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(5) And the mitzvah is with bent ones, etc.</b> Meaning: not specifically of a ram, but even of other domestic and wild animals, bent is also preferable to straight. And behold the Mechaber stated plainly and did not clarify: a straight ram versus bent of other species, which of them is preferable; however the conclusion of most poskim is that the virtue of bent is preferable; therefore if an ibex bent and a sheep straight happen to him, he should blow with the bent ibex, for the Rabbis enacted to blow with bent as a sign that they bend their hearts toward the Place in prayer, which is not so for a shofar of a ram: it is not a rabbinic enactment to blow specifically of a ram, but a custom that all Israel practiced from always as a remembrance of the Binding, and in this there is a preferred mitzvah. Nevertheless the commentators wrote that in this too there is at least the law of hiddur mitzvah; and therefore if they ask him to add for a shofar of a ram he should add up to a third, as of the kind explained below in siman 656.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(9) One who steals a shofar, etc.</b> And it is not similar to a stolen lulav, matzah, and tzitzis, because the mitzvah of shofar is only the hearing alone, and in hearing a sound there are no laws of theft, for in his hearing he does not touch the shofar at all; therefore even though he blew with it with a prohibition of theft, since in the essence of the mitzvah (namely the hearing) there is no prohibition of theft, he has fulfilled his obligation. And all this is when he already blew; but l'chatchila it is forbidden to blow with it against the will of the owner of the shofar, and even if the owners despaired of it; and all the more so one should not recite a blessing over it because of "and the robber blesses..," etc. And see above siman 11 seif 6 in Biur Halacha: that even if there was despair and change of domain or change of deed, it is also not appropriate to recite a blessing. And the Acharonim wrote that it is permitted to take one's fellow's shofar without his knowledge and blow with it, for it is pleasing to a person that a mitzvah be done with his property, and certainly he would consent; and he may blow with it all the hundred sounds even though that is not by law, since they so customed; certainly he will not protest this.

<b>(24) It is forbidden to blow, etc.</b> And even if he specified in his neder a tekiah of mitzvah; and the reason is that nedarim are a prohibition of the object and take effect even on a matter of mitzvah; and even hearing the blowing of a shofar from another is also included in this prohibition. And know that there are poskim who mean that the neder does not take effect unless he forbade the shofar upon himself (such as if he said, "A shofar upon me as a konam to blow with it"; but if he forbade upon himself the blowing) such as if he said, "Blowing of a shofar upon me as a konam" it does not take effect, for the blowing has no substance, and nedarim do not take effect on something without substance. However several poskim hold that it takes effect even in this, since in any case he mentioned in his neder an object; ordinarily his intention is to forbid the object upon himself. And all this regarding a Torah prohibition; but by rabbinic law there is a prohibition in every manner, even in a neder that has no substance, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 213; and therefore it is forbidden to blow with it before he has the neder permitted. And it is explicit in the poskim that all this is regarding a neder; but regarding an oath he cannot release himself from the mitzvah of blowing the shofar, for he is sworn and standing from Mount Sinai to fulfill the mitzvos of the Torah, unless he included in his oath to forbid himself from hearing every tekiah, which implies even a voluntary tekiah; and in this his oath takes effect comprehensively to forbid also one of mitzvah, and he should strive to permit his oath. And even regarding nedarim: if he forbade himself to hear a shofar from so-and-so, he is not released thereby from the mitzvah of blowing the shofar, and he must seek another person to hear a shofar from him; and if he does not find another, it is forbidden to hear from this person (even if he forbade himself by an oath) for he is not like one sworn to cancel the mitzvah, since when the oath left his mouth it was not to cancel, for behold he is not commanded to hear specifically from this person.

<b>(26) If it was pierced, etc.</b> I preface this seif with a short introduction, as follows. It is taught in the Gemara: Our Rabbis taught: If it was pierced and he sealed it, whether with of its kind or not of its kind, it is invalid; R' Natan says: of its kind it is kosher, not of its kind it is invalid. And the poskim wrote that we hold like R' Natan; and we say there that that which R' Natan permits of its kind is specifically when its majority remained that was not pierced. And behold regarding the explanation of the sugya there are opinions among the Rishonim: the Rambam and those who assist him explain that R' Natan refers to [a case] where it does not impede the blast (namely after sealing, its sound returned to what it had been initially before it was pierced) and even so it is kosher only of its kind; it turns out we require three [conditions] for excellence: it does not impede the blast, and of its kind, and its majority remained; but if one of these is lacking it is invalid; this is the first opinion brought here. And the Rosh and those who assist him hold that R' Natan refers only to [a case] where it impedes the blast, and even so it is kosher of its kind; and when it does not impede, even not of its kind it is kosher if its majority remained. And automatically according to this explanation it is a leniency, for we require only two (namely of its kind and its majority) and even if it impedes; or its majority and it does not impede, even if not of its kind. And this is what the Mechaber concluded below: and if it is a time of urgency, etc., namely then one may rely on the Rosh and those who assist him.

<b>(87) Outside the techum.</b> That is, even beyond twelve mil, which according to several poskim is according to the Torah; and if so, according to them it is forbidden to tell a gentile to bring it, for in the previous seif they permitted only something that is rabbinic. <b>(88) If a gentile made, etc.</b> Even for a Jew; and it deals with a case where the gentile made [it] from his own horn, for it is not muktzeh, since he did not divert his mind from it; but from a Jew's horn it is forbidden to carry it, and even to blow with it, for it is muktzeh of nolad; and even if he made it on the first day of Rosh Hashana it is forbidden also on the second day, like the law of an egg laid on the first [day], which is forbidden also on the second day. The Acharonim wrote that all those shofaros of which we said they are invalid to blow with are invalid both on the first day and on the second day.
