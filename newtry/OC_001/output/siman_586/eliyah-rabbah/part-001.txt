[ד] <b>מבהמה וכו'.</b> כן כתב הר"ן והקשה ר"מ רבקש דהא בהמה טמאה לית ליה קרנים כדאיתא במשנה פרק בא סימן, ותירץ מגן אברהם דהתם קאי אחיה, עד כאן. ולפי זה תיקשי על הלבוש שכתב נמי הדין כן בחיה. ואני הארכתי בספרי ליו"ד סימן פ' דטעו בתרתי, חדא דר"ן בחולין פירש דרבנן פליגי על מתניתין וכן פסקו הרשב"א ובית יוסף, ועוד הוכחתי שם דהרמב"ם והרא"ש פירשו המתניתין בסימן בין חיה לבהמה אבל טמאה יש לו קרניים, גם תוספות יום טוב בנדה לא דק בזה. גם מה שהניח בצריך עיון על רש"י שלא פירשו שם על קרניים כמו בחולין לא דק, דסבירא ליה כמו שכתבו תוס' לר"ת בחולין דף נ"ט דגם בבהמה טמאה אין קרניים כלל וכמו שכתב מהרש"א שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
[4] <b>From a non-kosher animal, etc.</b> Ran writes this. R. Moshe Rivkash objects that a non-kosher domesticated animal has no horns, as stated in the Mishnah in the chapter “Ba Siman.” Magen Avraham answers that the Mishnah there refers to a wild animal. According to this, however, the difficulty applies to Levush as well, for he states the same law regarding a wild animal. In my work on Yoreh Deah, siman 80, I explained at length that they erred in two respects. First, Ran in Chullin explains that the Sages disagree with that Mishnah, and Rashba and Beit Yosef rule accordingly. Second, I proved there that Rambam and Rosh understand the Mishnah as giving a sign distinguishing a wild animal from a domesticated animal, but a non-kosher species can have horns. Tosafos Yom Tov in Niddah was also imprecise in this matter. His difficulty with Rashi for not explaining the Mishnah there as referring to horns, as Rashi does in Chullin, is likewise unfounded, for Rashi follows Rabbeinu Tam’s view in Tosafos, Chullin 59, that even a non-kosher domesticated animal has no horns at all, as Maharsha explains there.

**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>It is invalid, etc.</b> This requires study. Darkei Moshe writes: “Ran leaves the matter requiring study, and it is proper to be stringent.” This implies that one may be lenient when no other shofar is available. Why, then, did Rama later rule without qualification that it is invalid? It appears to me that Ran’s essential uncertainty is whether the rule that only material fit for sacred service may be used can be derived for shofar, because Shabbos 28a asks what conclusion was reached concerning the tachash that existed in Moshe’s time. One may answer that a temporary case is not derived from a law applying for generations, namely tefillin, whereas shofar can be derived from it because shofar too applies for generations. Olas Shabbos questions this ruling, arguing that if a verse was needed for tefillin, “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,” we evidently do not accept the rule that only fit material may be used. His question itself is difficult. If so, why does the Gemara ask, after concluding that this rule concerns tefillin, that tefillin are explicitly written? It should have asked earlier, when it assumed that the rule concerned other matters, that this cannot be so because a verse was required for tefillin. Rather, it is clear that the general rule is itself derived from the verse concerning tefillin, as Levush implies.

[8] <b>Levush: He should not sound it, etc.</b> In a pressing circumstance he may sound it himself, as Bach writes in Yoreh Deah 221:13. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: if one said, “This shofar is forbidden to me by a vow,” he may sound it. This resolves Ran’s question why Rava in Rosh Hashanah 28 also stated the law of one prohibited by vow from a shofar, after already ruling that one prohibited from benefiting from another person may use that person’s shofar. According to what I wrote, the novelty is that he himself may sound it. According to the reading that forbids it, one can say that this itself is the novelty. Ran in Nedarim 16 also writes that if one says, “A sukkah is forbidden to me,” since he did not mention sitting in the sukkah, he prohibited benefit and it is permitted. The same should apply to a shofar, and this requires study. See Beis Hillel to Yoreh Deah 221, where neither he nor his son in the gloss noticed Bach here.

[ט] <b>[לבוש] קודם שופר וכו'.</b> כן כתב בית יוסף מר"ן וכלבו, וכתב עלה וזה לשונו ותוס' ומרדכי והגהות אשירי כתבו שם בפרק ב' דנדרים מסיק רבא דאם אמר ישיבת סוכה עלי קונם או אפילו אמר קונם שלא אשב בסוכה אסור, ונראה דהוא הדין גבי שופר דאם אמר תקיעת שופר עלי קונם אסור לתקוע אם לא נחלק דגבי סוכה בלא מצוה יש הנאה בישיבה אבל שופר אי לאו מצוה אין הנאה בתקיעה עד כאן לשונו. ופשוט דמאי שכתב בית יוסף כתבו שם רצה לומר בפרק ראוהו בית דין דהזכיר למעלה ומה שכתב בפרק ב' דנדרים הוא לשון תוס' ומרדכי ולאפוקי מט"ז ועולת שבת שהבינו שתוס' ומרדכי כתבו כן בפרק ב' דנדרים ושגגה בידם. עוד הבינו דתוס' וסייעתו מודים לר"ן וכלבו דקונם שופר לתקיעתו עלי אסור, והר"ן מודה לתוס' באומר תקיעה שופר עלי קונם. ולעניות דעתי ברור למעיין בדבריהם דלר"ן וכלבו הכל אסור ולתוס' וסייעתו מסתפקין בכולם, ועוד דטעם דאי לאו מצוה אין הנאה הוא נמי באומר קונם שופר לתקיעתו, (וכן משמע באגודה), אלא דבית יוסף פסק כר"ן וכלבו בכולם לאסור דלא שבקינן מאי דפשיטא להו משום ספיקא דתוס' וסייעתו וכן עיקר שכן ראיתי שפסק רבינו ירוחם דף מ"ח וכן כתב כנסת הגדולה מהג"ה וכן מוכח בדרכי משה שהביא דברי ר"ן וכלבו וכתב עליהם זה לשונו, אבל תוס' ומרדכי והגהות אשירי מסתפקים בדבר עד כאן לשונו. הרי להדיא דמסופקים בדין דר"ן וכן נראה לי בכולם לאסור. כתב ט"ז אם אומר על אדם אחד שלא אשמע קול שופר ממנו או תקיעתו עלי נראה דחל אליבא דכולי עלמא דהא אפשר לשמוע מאחר, עד כאן. וצריך עיון הא באמר תקיעתו עלי כבר כתב רמ"א בסימן תקפ"ט סעיף י' ואף שאין אחר נראה דאסור דנדר חל על דבר מצוה ורישא שלא אשמע קול שופר נמי אסור בכל ענין כמו שמבאר הלבוש שמבטל בכלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
[9] <b>Levush: Before shofar, etc.</b> Beit Yosef writes this from Ran and Kol Bo. He then adds that Tosafos, Mordechai, and Hagahos Ashri write that Rava concludes in the second chapter of Nedarim that if one says, “Sitting in a sukkah is forbidden to me,” or even, “It is forbidden to me that I sit in a sukkah,” it is forbidden. Beit Yosef writes that the same would appear to apply to shofar if one says, “The sounding of a shofar is forbidden to me,” unless one distinguishes that sitting in a sukkah gives ordinary benefit even without the mitzvah, whereas sounding a shofar gives no benefit apart from the mitzvah. Beit Yosef’s reference to “they wrote there” plainly means the chapter “Ra’uhu Beis Din,” which he cited above; the words “in the second chapter of Nedarim” are part of the citation from Tosafos and Mordechai. This excludes the understanding of Taz and Olas Shabbos that Tosafos and Mordechai wrote this in the second chapter of Nedarim. They also understood that Tosafos and the other authorities agree with Ran and Kol Bo that if one says, “This shofar is forbidden to me for its sounding,” it is prohibited, while Ran agrees with Tosafos where one says, “The sounding of a shofar is forbidden to me.” In my humble opinion, one who examines their words will see clearly that according to Ran and Kol Bo all these formulations are forbidden, whereas Tosafos and the other authorities remain uncertain in all of them. The rationale that there is no benefit apart from the mitzvah applies equally where one says, “This shofar is forbidden to me for its sounding,” as is also implied by Agudah. Beit Yosef therefore rules in accordance with Ran and Kol Bo that all are forbidden, for one does not set aside their definite ruling because of the uncertainty of Tosafos and the other authorities. This is the primary ruling. Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 48, rules likewise, Knesses HaGedolah cites the same from a gloss, and Darkei Moshe explicitly writes that Tosafos, Mordechai, and Hagahos Ashri are uncertain regarding Ran’s law. Taz writes that if one says regarding a particular person, “I shall not hear the sound of a shofar from him,” or, “His sounding is forbidden to me,” the vow certainly takes effect according to all opinions, because he can hear it from another person. This requires study, for Rama already writes in siman 589, seif 10, that if he says, “His sounding is forbidden to me,” it is prohibited even where no other person is available, because a vow takes effect upon a mitzvah. The first formulation, “I shall not hear the sound of a shofar,” is likewise forbidden in all circumstances, as Levush explains, because it negates the mitzvah in general.

[12] <b>Levush: But if it split, etc.</b> Malbushei Yom Tov writes that all the more so it is invalid if it became notched, for the Mishnah in the chapter “Eilu Mumin” teaches that a notch involves a deficiency whereas a split does not. It therefore did not need to state the law of a notch regarding shofar. One must consequently inspect shofaros carefully, because many have a slight notch at the mouthpiece due to saliva, and such a shofar is invalid. In my humble opinion, however, it is possible that a split without a deficiency is worse here than a hole with a deficiency, because where material is missing we do not fear that the defect will continue to widen, even when it runs lengthwise. This is also implied by Taz. Although Levush writes that the law concerns a round hole, perhaps he means a hole without a deficiency; this wording is absent from Shulchan Aruch. Malbushei Yom Tov’s proof from blemishes is certainly not decisive, because there all holes are disqualifying.
