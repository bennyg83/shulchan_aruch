(ס"ק ג) מבהמה כ' כדאיתא בנדה דף נ"א ע"ב תנן כל שי"ל קרנים יש לו טלפים. ויש שי"ל טלפים ואין לו קרנים. וניהו דשני סימני טהרה יש. טלפים ומעלה גרה מכל מקום האמרי' בחולין אם יש לו טלפים די וא"צ לידע אם מעלה גרה ובלבד שמכיר חזיר. דאין בהמה טמאה שי"ל טלפים ואינו מעלה גרה כ"א חזיר וכיון שמכיר שאינו חזיר די בסימן טלפים וכיון שכל שי"ל קרנים יש לו טלפים. הרי יצא מספק טמאה. דהא טמאה אין לה טלפים כ"א חזיר. וחזיר הא אין לו קרנים. אם כן לא משכחת קרן מבהמה טמאה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(s.k. 3) From an animal; he wrote: as is stated in Niddah folio 51b: We learned: Whatever has horns has hooves; and there are [animals] that have hooves and do not have horns. And even though there are two signs of taharah (cloven hooves and chewing the cud) nevertheless we say in Chullin that if it has cloven hooves, that suffices, and one need not know whether it chews the cud, provided he recognizes a pig: for there is no non-kosher animal that has cloven hooves and does not chew the cud except a pig; and once he recognizes that it is not a pig, the sign of cloven hooves suffices. And since whatever has horns has cloven hooves, it has left the doubt of tumah; for a non-kosher [animal] has no cloven hooves except a pig, and a pig has no horns. If so, you do not find a horn from a non-kosher animal.

<b>And he did not look in Chullin.</b> Certainly he looked in Chullin; for R' Moshe Rivkash concluded: "And it implies there that it is [accepted by] everyone, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam in Chullin folio 59" (end quote). Thus the source of his difficulty is Tosafos of Chullin; rather Magen Avraham meant that he did not look in Chullin in the Ran. And that Magen Avraham contradicted his own words and did not mention the Ran is because this law (that a shofar of a non-kosher animal is invalid) is the words of the Ran; so R' Moshe Rivkash's difficulty is against the Ran. Therefore he wrote that he did not look in his words in Chullin. And this is what is stated there: Our Rabbis taught: These are the signs of a wild animal to permit its fat and to require covering [of the blood]: whatever has horns and cloven hooves. R' Dosa says: If it has horns, certainly it also has cloven hooves; but if it has cloven hooves, one must inquire whether it has horns. And we conclude there that those horns must be different from the horns of a domestic animal; but regarding cloven hooves it is not mentioned in the Talmud whether they must be different. And Tosafos wrote in the name of Rivam that the cloven hooves need not be different. And the first Tanna requires two things: horns, which are a sign that it is a wild animal, and cloven hooves to remove it from the category of a non-kosher wild animal. And R' Dosa holds that distinctive horns alone suffice, for it is certainly a kosher wild animal. And Tosafos wrote that according to this, the Mishnah of Niddah mentioned above necessarily comes only like R' Dosa and not like the first Tanna; for according to the first Tanna, even if it has distinctive horns one must know that it has cloven hooves, and we do not say that it certainly has cloven hooves; yet in the Mishnah of Niddah it teaches "whatever has horns has cloven hooves." Further they wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam according to the conclusion of their words: that the cloven hooves mentioned in the beraisa as well are distinctive cloven hooves different from those of a domestic animal; and according to the first Tanna one of them suffices to recognize that it is a kosher wild animal (either distinctive horns or distinctive cloven hooves; and what the first Tanna wrote "whatever has horns and cloven hooves") the vav of "and cloven hooves" that the first Tanna said is a disjunctive vav, as if he said "horns or cloven hooves." And R' Dosa is more stringent and said: whatever has distinctive horns certainly has distinctive cloven hooves; but if it has distinctive cloven hooves, that does not remove it from the doubt of being a domestic animal, and you must know whether it has horns to establish it as a wild animal. And according to this, neither the first Tanna nor R' Dosa spoke of signs that remove from a presumption of tumah; and one can say that everyone holds that with horns alone it leaves the presumption of tumah. And so Tosafos concluded, and this is their language: "And now the Mishnah of Niddah mentioned above comes according to everyone, for the Sages concede: whatever has horns has cloven hooves (that is, split and parted") end quote. And Maharsha wrote, and this is his language: "And know that according to this, horns and cloven hooves there (meaning in tractate Niddah) refer to any horns and cloven hooves (even if not distinctive), and regarding tumah and taharah; and this is what it means: whatever has horns, even of a domestic animal, has split cloven hooves and is tahor" (end quote. And he is right; for go and see) also if one says that the Mishnah of Niddah deals with distinctive horns and cloven hooves: how then did Tosafos write that the Mishnah of Niddah comes according to everyone? It could not come like the first Tanna, for the first Tanna holds that distinctive cloven hooves alone suffice. And anyway it is difficult: if the Mishnah deals with distinctive horns and cloven hooves, how does the end teach "and there are [animals] that have cloven hooves and do not have horns," and it is possible that it is non-kosher? For a non-kosher animal has no cloven hooves except a pig, and a pig's cloven hooves are not distinctive; and if so, since it has distinctive cloven hooves it has left the doubt of a pig and is certainly tahor. Therefore the Mishnah of Niddah must deal with cloven hooves that are not distinctive, and similarly those horns are not distinctive, and then it properly comes according to everyone. And according to this, per Rivam's explanation, Rivkash's difficulty does not arise; for according to Rivam the Mishnah of Niddah necessarily comes like R' Dosa. But according to the first Tanna we require two things: distinctive horns to remove the doubt that it is a domestic animal, and cloven hooves that are not distinctive to remove the doubt of tumah. If so, we find according to the first Tanna horns of a non-kosher [animal] if it has no cloven hooves. And if so, one can say that the Ran ruled like the first Tanna, and there is no difficulty at all. Therefore R' Moshe Rivkash concluded "and it implies there that it is [accepted by] everyone, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam," etc. it implies that R' Moshe Rivkash took the main [view] like Rabbeinu Tam and asks well (not like what the Peles wrote in siman 79, who attributed to R' Moshe Rivkash an intention that does not appear so from his language (see there). And on this Magen Avraham wrote "and he did not look in Chullin") meaning in the Ran in Chullin, as above: that the Ran in Chullin brought only Rivam's explanation and did not bring Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at all. And if so, the Ran according to his own method is fine, and there is no difficulty at all.

And he came to resolve the Peri Chadash's difficulty in siman 80 at least against the Shulchan Aruch: he asked, since according to Rivam's explanation Rabbi stated a plain Mishnah in Niddah like R' Dosa, why did the poskim rule like the first Tanna (see there).
