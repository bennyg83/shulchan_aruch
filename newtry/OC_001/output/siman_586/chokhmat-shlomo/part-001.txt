<b>Se'if 3.</b> But with that of avodah zarah of a non-Jew, and likewise with accessories of avodah zarah of a non-Jew, one should not blow; and if he blew with it he has fulfilled, etc. Marginal note: The reason is explained in the Talmud; that it is by force of [the principle] that mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit. And behold, the words of the Baal HaMaor are well known, that he holds that rabbinic mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit. And from the words of Tosafos in the second chapter of Pesachim, in the sugya of Chizkiyah and R' Elazar, where it objects there from terumah, that we make an eruv for a nazir with wine, and Tosafos wrote there that there he does not go according to the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, for mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, etc. And according to the Baal HaMaor's words it comes out well: for certainly even according to the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless even for a rabbinic mitzvah we make an eruv, for eruvin are rabbinic, and one way or the other; according to the Torah one does not need eruvin, and by rabbinic law this day itself is a mitzvah; and if so, a rabbinic mitzvah [was] given in order to derive benefit; necessarily he does not hold like the Razah. And Tosafos's words are astonishing: why must they say that he goes according to the view that we make an eruv for an optional matter; better they should have said that he goes according to the view that mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit, as R' Yochanan and Rava initially hold in tractate Rosh Hashanah (see there). And their core difficulty likewise is not difficult: one can say that if it were so that he holds we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, why did it cite "we make an eruv for a nazir with wine," etc.? It should have cited that we make an eruv for one who vowed benefit from something else, who is likewise permitted to another and forbidden in benefit to himself, and nevertheless we make an eruv; and from the fact that it did not cite so, it is proved that regarding something forbidden to him in benefit we do not make an eruv, and necessarily he holds we make an eruv for an optional matter and it is forbidden among forbidden benefits, and it comes out well; and examine this carefully with Hashem's help. <b>(Ibid., se'if 5.)</b> One who is forbidden by neder from benefit of a shofar; another person blows with it, etc. Marginal note: Behold Tosafos asked in the sixth chapter of Shevuos: why do we say mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit (behold he benefits in that he is exempt from the prutah of Rav Yosef) see there. And in my humble opinion it comes out well: for what exempts him from giving a prutah to a poor person is only akin to "driving away a lion" and like one who pays his fellow's debt without his knowledge; and therefore, since regarding one forbidden by neder from benefit it is permitted to pay his debt without his knowledge, likewise here what exempts him from a prutah to a poor person is not reckoned as benefit. And even though one who pays his debt with his knowledge is forbidden; that is because there, since nothing forces him to do so and he does it of his own will, he reveals his mind that it is agreeable to him and he acts as his agent, which is not so when performing a mitzvah: he is forced to fulfill the mitzvah, and if he were not forced it is possible he would rather give the prutah to the poor person and not do the mitzvah, only that the Torah forced him to do the mitzvah, and thereby he is automatically exempted from the poor person; since it is through the Torah's compulsion, it is like [doing it] without his knowledge. But a paid watchman, since he has this benefit, it is like with his knowledge; therefore even "driving away a lion" is benefit since it is with his knowledge. But regarding a mitzvah it is like without his knowledge. And further: regarding one forbidden by neder from benefit the matter depends on the Torah's viewpoint, and in the Torah's viewpoint driving away a lion is not reckoned as benefit; but regarding a paid watchman the matter depends on his own viewpoint, as we find generally regarding that benefit that because he trusts him he commits himself, and therefore in his viewpoint paying a debt is also benefit, so he is a paid watchman; and it comes out well. And even though regarding a watchman of a lost object he is likewise forced to accept the lost object and to watch it; nevertheless for the watching alone he does not become a paid watchman, as is known, but only through spreading and shaking [the garment]; and spreading and shaking it is in his power to do through another, and then he would be obligated to give a prutah to a poor person, for he himself does nothing and is exempt only when he himself does it, and that too is with his knowledge entirely; therefore he is a paid watchman. And with this Bach's answer comes out well (what Magen Avraham wrote in his name regarding what is difficult from here to what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 221) and Bach answers that there is a distinction between whether there is another [person] or not; and apparently it is not understood, and one must say that in a time of need we rely on those who hold that bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit is also not reckoned as benefit, etc. And according to the above we need not that: only if there is another, then if the other blows (since he himself does nothing) he is obligated in a prutah to a poor person and does not benefit except when he himself blows, [when] he is exempt; and if so, if it is with his knowledge and he benefits with the prutah of Rav Yosef, therefore it is forbidden. But if there is no other, then since he is obligated to blow it is again like without his knowledge and is only "driving away a lion," therefore it is permitted; and it comes out well with Hashem's help; and examine carefully, for it is correct. And behold the dispute of the Rishonim is well known regarding what we hold that mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, whether when there is bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit we also say they were not given in order to derive benefit, or in that [case] mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit. And now it appears to me a proof from Pesachim, chapter "Kol Sha'ah," in the sugya of Chizkiyah and R' Elazar, where it objects there from a nazir (the Merciful One said he shall not eat, etc., and we learned we make an eruv for a nazir with wine, etc.) and it is difficult: what does it ask? Behold the view of the Rabbis in tractate Eruvin is that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter; and if so one can say that this Mishnah comes like the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, and mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, therefore we make an eruv for him with wine, so what does it ask? And necessarily it is proved that where there is bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit, [mitzvos] were given in order to derive benefit; and if so, likewise here, even though the beginning of the eruv is for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless once he placed the eruv he can again go there even for an optional matter wherever he wishes; therefore in that [case mitzvos] were given in order to derive benefit, and it asks well; and examine carefully. However one can say that those who hold that even when bodily benefit is together with a mitzvah we also say they were not given in order to derive benefit; they hold like the Razah's view that regarding rabbinic mitzvos [they] were given in order to derive benefit; and if so even those who hold we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter; nevertheless for a rabbinic mitzvah too we make an eruv, by force of one way or the other: according to the Torah one does not need an eruv, and by rabbinic law an eruv is required, and by rabbinic law too it is a mitzvah; and if so it asks well that a rabbinic mitzvah was given in order to derive benefit; and it comes out well; and examine carefully; and see what I wrote above on this in another matter. Only even according to the Rama's view in siman 495, who brought those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov and wrote that according to their words "born" is forbidden even on Shabbos; nevertheless he did not gloss anything here; and that is because until here "born" is more severe than muktzeh only regarding "born" that is recognizable from its body, that we saw with the eye that yesterday it was not as it is now, and as it is now it has no permit (therefore it is truly "born" and more severe than muktzeh; but "born" of this sort) if the non-Jew had made it for himself it would be permitted, as Magen Avraham himself wrote, only if they made it for a Jew it is forbidden; and if so, regarding this, from the body of the act it is not recognizable for whom he is making it, whether for a Jew or a non-Jew; therefore whatever is not recognizable from its body does not have the law of "born" but [only] muktzeh, and is permitted on Shabbos and on Yom Tov according to this view. And what is written in siman 501 that it is forbidden; that is the Mechaber; and examine carefully. And even if it is forbidden on Shabbos, it is only by force of [concern of] substituting for a complete "born," so that they not come to treat truly-"born" lightly; and this is not a decree upon a decree, for sometimes we do decree so; therefore at any rate regarding this they were again not stringent to give it the law of a positive and a negative; therefore the positive commandment of shofar overrides it; and it is fine one way or the other, whether according to the Mechaber or according to the Rama. And one can explain the matter further: for necessarily it is proved that regarding this they did not make a reinforcement as of the Torah; for if they made a reinforcement as of the Torah, how is it permitted on Shabbos; from the Torah we do not find anything that is permitted on Shabbos and forbidden on Yom Tov; therefore what they made more stringent on Yom Tov than on Shabbos is not as of the Torah, for if as of the Torah it would be forbidden even on Shabbos; and since they did not make [it] as of the Torah, therefore automatically it is again only like a negative commandment and not like a negative and a positive; and examine carefully, for this is clear as a matter of reasoning.
