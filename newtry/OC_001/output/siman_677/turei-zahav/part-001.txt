הבני בית עם אשתו יכוונו וידליקו עליו ג"כ אלא דניחא טפי לעשות הברכה בעצמו כנ"ל בדבר זה גם רמ"א פסק כן בסמוך:

<b>Must give a perutah to the homeowner, etc.</b> In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 101, he wrote regarding a married lodger who wants to kindle with a blessing away from his home, that one great authority wrote that he may do so. Although we say in the Gemara, once I married, I said, now I certainly do not need, that means only that he is not obligated; but if he wants, he may kindle, and it is not a blessing in vain, for he is included among the mehadrin. Another great authority wrote that since he does not need it, he is exempt, and if he does it he is called a commoner, and all the more so it is a blessing in vain, since we did not find this kind of mehadrin in the Talmud. Terumas HaDeshen concludes that he is properly called included among the mehadrin, for just as there is beautification in a lamp for each person in one house, so too there is beautification in a lamp for the husband and a lamp for his wife in two places. The Tanna did not definitively teach this kind of mehadrin because it is not so common. Since we find in the Talmud mehadrin and mehadrin min hamehadrin, this is not like adding to the Talmud in a way that detracts. Until here. Beis Yosef wrote on this: it does not seem to me that one may rely on this to recite an unnecessary blessing. Until here. Maharshal wrote in his abbreviated rulings as follows: a man and his wife certainly are sufficiently covered by one lamp. A married lodger, if he does not know that his wife is kindling for him, may kindle and bless. But if he knows clearly that she is kindling for him, he should not kindle, unless he is in a separate house because of one who sees; then he should kindle but not bless. Where he did not know that she kindled for him and came home that night, he must go back and kindle and bless, because the obligation of the Sages has taken effect upon him. But where he does not want to fulfill through his wife's lighting, which she kindles for him at home, it appears that this is not in his power, and he cannot bless. Until here. Now we have not yet merited to see the longer words of Maharshal, to know the source of his words. But when we look into it ourselves, the words of Terumas HaDeshen appear correct. What the one great authority wrote, that we did not find this beautification in the Talmud, does not seem correct to me at all, for when the Gemara said mehadrin, it meant that presumably each person would do so, and it is pleasant for every person to be included among mehadrin. This is not so in this matter, which is similar to other blessings: here the choice is given to a person to do as he wishes. If he wants, he fulfills with the blessing that his wife recites when she kindles, and if he does not want, he blesses for himself. It seems appropriate to compare this to what is written at the beginning of the third chapter of Berachos: Abaye said, two who ate have a mitzvah to divide, and Rashi explained this both regarding the blessing of zimun and the blessing of Hamotzi. Rosh wrote that the world did not practice this way, and also in Rashi we did not hear that they practiced this way regarding the blessing of Hamotzi, and so too in the incident of Bar Kappara, etc. Thus, in any event there is no prohibition if each one blesses Hamotzi; it is only that Rosh wrote that they did not practice this way. Heaven forbid that Rosh should say it is a blessing in vain, for Rashi even included this matter under the category of mitzvah. Furthermore, we find regarding the blessing of Yotzer Or in the commentary of Nimmukei Yosef, Responsa Rosh: I say it quietly with the prayer leader, although if I heard it from the prayer leader I would fulfill. The reason is lest I not concentrate properly on the words of the one blessing. Likewise regarding Birkat Hamazon, Beis Yosef in siman 183 wrote in the name of Orchot Chayim in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz that each person should recite Birkat Hamazon even when they recite zimun, because he may not be able to concentrate, etc.; but Maharam would not bless quietly, etc. Until here. We see that in a matter where one can fulfill through his friend's blessing but does not want to, he has no prohibition in his hand, since his intent is for the sake of Heaven. And the one who does not want to bless for himself, as Maharam did, also has intent for the sake of Heaven, not to multiply blessings when it is possible to fulfill through his friend's blessing. The same applies in this matter, and all the more so, since he is not with his wife and does not hear the blessing, he can say: I do not fulfill with my wife's blessing when I am not present, for the intent of the hearer and the one who recites is required. Even if he is in his home and does not want to fulfill with his wife's blessing, I do not know what prohibition there is if he blesses for himself. How is this different from a minor who reached the age of education, who kindles for himself with a blessing, while it is obvious that he fulfills through his father's blessing? We have already seen the practice that anyone who goes away from his home during Chanukah takes Chanukah lamps with him to kindle in the place where the spirit leads him to go, even though the members of his household who are with his wife kindle in his home. Although there is no proof from this, since the household members may not intend to discharge the homeowner's obligation but only their own, nevertheless that itself is difficult: why must he take the lamps with him? Let the household members with his wife intend and kindle for him as well. Rather, it is more pleasant to recite the blessing himself, as it seems to me in this matter. Rema also ruled this way nearby.

<b>וי"א דבזמן הזה כו' ידליק במקום שאוכל. </b> בתשו' רשב"א סי' תקמ"א כ' מי שאוכל על שלחן בע"ה ואפילו שוכב בבית שני בפ"ע א"צ להדליק אבל צריך להשתתף עם בע"ה כאכסנאי שאין לו בית במקו' אחר עכ"ל מזה יליף רמ"א דלא אזלינן בתר מקום השינה דא"כ ה"ל לזה חיוב להדליק במקום ששוכב ונראה דגם רמ"א לא מיירי אלא במי שיש לו תמיד חדר מיוחד לאכילה וחדר אחר לשכיבה אך העיקר הוא חדר האכילה וכנדון דהרשב"א שזכרתי משא"כ במי שיש לו דירה בעיר והולך פעם א' חוץ לביתו וסועד אצל חבירו פשיטא שלא יניח ביתו וידליק בבית שהוא אוכל שם פ"א אצל חבירו אלא צריך לילך לביתו ולהדליק שם ואם אחזו תאות האכילה ואינו רוצה לזוז משם עכשיו יצוה לבני ביתו שידלקו עליו בביתו דאותו בית שהוא סועד שם אין דינו  אלא כאכסנאי שמדליקין עליו בבית והוא אינו מדליק במקום שהוא ואפי' למ"ש בסמוך דאכסנאי רשאי להדליק גם במקום שהוא שם היינו שעכשיו תהיה גם שכיבתו שם משא"כ בזה שיאכל כאן שעה או שתים וישוב למקומו אין שום סברא לו' שידליק שם ולא בביתו דזה הוה כאלו עומד בשעת הדלק' על רחוב העיר דאין שיך לו שם הדלקה הגם שראיתי קצת בני אדם בשע' שאוכלי' בסעוד' אצל אדם אחר שולחים אחר נרותיהם ודולקים שם וסומכים על הג"ה זו וטעות הוא בידם ואינם מבינים דבר זה וז"ל רש"ל חתן האוכל בבית חמיו אם יש יכולת בידו  אפי' ישן בבית חמיו לא ישתתף אלא לעצמו משום המהדרין שהרי הוא איש וביתו ואם ישן בבית אחר פשיטא שחייב להדליק במקום שישן עכ"ל והוא נגד דעת הרשב"א שזכרתי ולע"ד דברי הרשב"א עיקר בזה וראיה מעירוב חצירות סי' ש"ע ס"ה מי שאוכל במקום א' וישן במקום אחר מקום האכילה עיקר כו' ואף שכתב הטור בשם הרא"ש דחייב להדליק במקום השינה אין סותר זה דשם מיירי לדין התלמוד והפוסקי' הראשונים שצריך להזהר מחשד וכמ"ש הטור כאן מפני השכנים כו' מה שאין כן לדידן שמדליקין בפיים ואין היכר אלא לבני הבית כמ"ש לעיל ססי' תרע"א והוא מבואר ברשב"א סימן תקמ"א לא איכפת לן במקום השינה שהרי בני הבית הם יודעים שקבוע דירתו לאכילה במקום אחר ושם הוא מדליק כנלע"ד ברור בזה:

<b>And some say that in this time, etc., he should kindle in the place where he eats.</b> In Responsa Rashba, siman 541, he wrote: one who eats at the homeowner's table, even if he sleeps in a second house by himself, need not kindle, but must participate with the homeowner like a lodger who has no house in another place. Until here. From this Rema learned that we do not follow the place of sleeping, for if so this person would have an obligation to kindle in the place where he sleeps. It appears that Rema too is speaking only of one who always has a room designated for eating and another room for sleeping, but the main one is the eating room, as in the case of Rashba that I mentioned. This is not so for one who has a dwelling in the city and one time goes outside his home and dines at his friend's. It is obvious that he should not leave his home and kindle in the house where he eats one time at his friend's; rather, he must go to his home and kindle there. If the desire to eat seized him and he does not want to move from there now, he should command the members of his household to kindle for him in his home. The house where he is dining has the law only of a lodger for whom they kindle at home, and he does not kindle in the place where he is. Even according to what I wrote nearby, that a lodger may kindle also in the place where he is, that is where his sleeping will now also be there. This is not so in this case, where he will eat here for an hour or two and return to his place. There is no logic to say that he should kindle there and not in his home, for this is as though he were standing at the time of kindling in the street of the city, where no kindling belongs to him there. Although I have seen some people who, when eating a meal at someone else's home, send for their lamps and light there, relying on this gloss, this is an error in their hands, and they do not understand this matter. This is the language of Maharshal: a groom who eats in his father-in-law's house, if he has the means, even if he sleeps in his father-in-law's house, should not participate but should kindle for himself because of mehadrin, for he is a man and his household. If he sleeps in another house, it is obvious that he is obligated to kindle in the place where he sleeps. Until here. This is against the view of Rashba that I cited. In my humble opinion, the words of Rashba are primary in this. There is proof from Eruvei Chatzeiros, siman 370, section 5: one who eats in one place and sleeps in another, the place of eating is primary, etc. Although the Tur wrote in the name of Rosh that he must kindle in the place where he sleeps, this does not contradict it, for there he is speaking according to the law of the Talmud and the early authorities, where one must be careful because of suspicion, as the Tur wrote here, because of the neighbors, etc. This is not so for us, who kindle indoors and where recognition is only for the household members, as I wrote above at the end of siman 671. This is explained in Rashba, siman 541. We are not concerned about the place of sleeping, for the household members know that his fixed dwelling for eating is elsewhere, and there he kindles. This seems clear to me.
