<b>ס"ב ואפילו בביתו. </b>טור. ולמד ממ"ש כ"ט א' והנחתום כדי כו' הלא"ה אסור דלא כירושלמי שם תני רשבג"א אף מפיס אדם את עצמו בליטרא לידע כמה הגיע לו כהדא ר' מנא זבן חרובין דשמיטתא אתא שאיל לר' חזקיה א"ל נהגין רבנן כהדא דרשב"ג ועב"י ונר' לי שמירושל' מוכח להיפך שאמר שם ר"י בשם שמואל אפי' לתלותו בכף מאזנים מפני העכברים אסור ר"ל שז"ש אין משגיחין אר"י בר' בון לא מטעם זה ר"ל לא משום כן אמר אין משגיחין דבכה"ג מותר אלא בגין דתנינן רי"א שוקל אדם בשר כנגד הכלי או כנגד הקופיץ וחכמים אומרים אין משגיחין בכף מאזנים כל עיקר הא תניא קדמייא סבר משגיחין ר"ל דת"ק סובר שמשגיחין דהיינו נגד כלי וקופיץ ובכה"ג אמרי רבנן אין משגיחין. הא לתלותו בכף מאזנים מפני העכברים מותר תני רשב"ג אומר אף מפיס אדם כו' כנ"ל וכיון דלא קי"ל כירושלמי דמסיק דמפני עכברים דמותר כ"ש דלא קי"ל כרשב"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 2: And even in his house.</b> Tur. And he learned it from what is written on 29a: "and the baker, in order," etc.; otherwise it is forbidden. This is unlike the Yerushalmi there, which teaches: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that a person may even settle himself with a litra to know how much reached him, like this: R. Mana bought carobs of shemittah and came to ask R. Chizkiyah, who said to him: the rabbis practice like this of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. And see Beis Yosef. And it appears to me that from the Yerushalmi the opposite is proven, for it says there: R. Yosi in the name of Shmuel: even to hang it on a scale-pan because of mice is forbidden. Meaning, that is what it says, "we do not attend." R. Yosi bar Bun said: not for this reason; meaning, he did not say "we do not attend" because in such a case it is permitted, but because we learned: R. Yehudah says a person may weigh meat against a vessel or against a cleaver, and the Sages say we do not attend to a scale-pan at all. Thus the earlier beraisa held that we do attend, meaning against a vessel or cleaver, and regarding such a case the Rabbis said we do not attend. It follows that to hang it on a scale-pan because of mice is permitted. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel teaches: a person may even settle himself, etc., as above. And since we do not rule like the Yerushalmi, which concludes that because of mice it is permitted, all the more so we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 6: They are accustomed, etc.</b> Hagahos Ashiri there s.v. "and because of loss," etc.; see there. And so wrote Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1, in the name of the Rashba. But according to what is written that regarding salting it is permitted because it dulls the taste, it would be forbidden here, and there is no proof from salting; rather one may say that his proof is from how they were lenient to salt a great deal when only one [piece] is needed; and see the Rosh there; and see what I wrote in siman 567. And in Hagahos Maimoniyos he wrote that because it is impossible to eat otherwise, it is permitted even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, and proof from what is written there: if one rolled dough on erev Yom Tov, he separates challah from it on Yom Tov. And he wrote: however, we wrote that the halachah is not like Rabbah but like the father of Shmuel; and so he ruled in siman 506, se'if 3. And what he wrote "because it is impossible," etc., so wrote Raavan there. And Eliyah Rabbah [on] "he rolled," etc.: it appears to me that because of covering [the blood] he does not refrain, etc., for it is permitted to eat without covering, which is not so regarding challah; and for this reason it appears to me that it is permitted to nikkur meat on Yom Tov even though it is like selecting, etc., and even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, since he cannot eat without nikkur. And he wrote: and that is what is written there, 37a: we do not lift, etc.; what is the reason? R. Yehudah taught: it was needed only, etc. And the explanation: why is it forbidden, for it is impossible to eat it without this? And he answers: it was needed only, etc., that specifically to give to a kohen is forbidden, but he sets his eye on this side and eats on another side. And it is clear from his words that challah is not rectified by setting his eye, etc., and therefore it is permitted.
