**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Behind him.”</b> — The Taz notes Bach cites a reversed text: a handbreadth before him and two behind; a woman—a handbreadth behind and nothing before. The reason is she need not be careful before her body; behind her a handbreadth suffices—unlike a man, who must be careful before himself too, hence two handbreadths behind. One could raise that we should worry about two textual traditions and [hold] only a handbreadth before and after—see there. We rule like the Mechaber and Rabbi Yochanan’s silence [toward the stringency] concedes agreement. For us, [we give] children two handbreadths even without adults present, for per Beit Yosef’s reading there is no difficulty for Bach, and we need not divide between needing caution from two sides or not.

<b>“But.”</b> — The Taz forces me to expand here. See Gemara <i>Berachot</i> 61b. In my lay view there are three readings. (1) Simply: for Rabbi Akiva, east–west in <i>chutz laAretz</i> is forbidden in Bavel, which lies east of Israel; from that, a country lying north–south [of Israel] would invert [the permitted axes]. (2) As Maharsha explains there with a diagram—see well—and he holds Rashi and Tosafos <i>s.v.</i> Rabbi Akiva agree: for Rabbi Akiva in <i>chutz laAretz</i> specifically east–west is forbidden in Bavel because two factors apply—(i) sanctity of Israel, (ii) west—Shechinah there—hence forbidden; if one factor is absent it is permitted; hence north–south while seated toward Israel is permitted since <i>chutz laAretz</i> is most of the world and far from Jerusalem. The same for <i>terumah</i> lands in Judea and Galilee: prohibition applies only when both factors combine; hence in Judea east–west, where most of Judea lies, is forbidden even at corners between east and west because both reasons apply—western sanctity and the strip sanctified from Jerusalem to the end—unlike north–south where only one reason applies; understand this there. Accordingly, what Rashi writes (<i>Berachot</i> 62a <i>s.v.</i> Rabbi Yochanan “was in Judea”)—if in <i>chutz laAretz</i> how could he say one does not relieve east–west but only north–south in a country lying north–south, and one turned between east–west and north–south where the two reasons do not combine—per reading (1) it works simply in Judea; if in <i>chutz laAretz</i> sometimes the axes invert for a north–south country. (3) Rambam’s view and the Tur here: Rabbi Akiva forbids everywhere in <i>chutz laAretz</i> east–west whether the country lies north–south—east–west forbidden, north–south permitted. Tosafos <i>s.v.</i> Rabbi Akiva “east behind, face west,” and we conclude “even in <i>chutz laAretz</i>,” raises a difficulty: why specify east–west? Maharsha resolves this per reading (2). But Tosafos’ “we conclude even in <i>chutz laAretz</i>” is redundant—the Taz sensed this here; one can say they explain like Tur and Rambam that in <i>chutz laAretz</i> it is not specifically Bavel as Rashi <i>s.v.</i> “was” seemed to restrict to Bavel, but all <i>chutz laAretz</i> follows that law—supported by the later baraita “I followed Rabbi Yochanan…” unlike Rashi who placed it in Judea, implying also <i>chutz laAretz</i>—hence “we conclude…” The Tur’s wording “he shall not put his face to the east and his back to the west,” and they did not shorten “he shall not relieve between east and west,” admits two aspects: either concern is only exposure behind, not before—but that cannot be, for then why specify north–south; east–west with back to east would also [need mention]. The second [reading]: they state a stringency—even exposure behind a handbreadth is forbidden toward the west, all the more two handbreadths; yet toward the east for exposure we do not care, only that exposure not face west. Eshel Avraham’s pilpul on Yerushalmi is tangled and not for here. In the Taz’s words there are obscurities, as I will say: he wrote the reason “because the Shechinah is in the west,” which sounds like scruple is only for the west, not the east—then a woman [needs] nothing before her, perhaps by <i>lo plug</i>; why not say like Rambam <i>Hilchot Beit Habechirah</i> 7, reason Beit Yosef cites here—that one does not relieve east–west means neither toward west nor toward east that is opposite the west—so east too has sanctity (see <i>Bava Batra</i> on this); it is strained to say one stands toward west with back [only] without exposure also forbidden—Eshel Avraham [calls] that a great strain. There is much to compare whether east is sanctified; see Magen Avraham letter 5 and letter 6. The other reason—toward Jerusalem—lacks clear explanation on its face, for that is the first <i>tanna</i>’s view in Judea specifically; one can say that with this fits what Rashi wrote on <i>Berachot</i> 62a: Rabbi Akiva learned from Rabbi Yochanan “do not relieve east–west in Judea” when that faced Jerusalem—in <i>chutz laAretz</i> no proof, not like Tur and Rambam and Beit Yosef’s explanation of them that “do not relieve east–west” means everywhere—they derived their ruling from that—and Eshel Avraham—understand this.

<b>“And what the Tur says.”</b> — “The Tur’s words are compelling to me” matches “We learned in a baraita… I learned that one does not relieve east–west”—the reason must be Shechinah in the west; for if not, Rabbi Akiva disputes the first <i>tanna</i> and forbids everywhere outside Israel east–west even at corners [not toward Jerusalem], while the first <i>tanna</i> also forbids corners in Judea—Rabbi Akiva would then forbid in <i>chutz laAretz</i> east–west at corners of Israel (not “toward Jerusalem” but “toward Israel”)—but those corners are the majority—why should they follow the minority? Granted that [relieving] toward Israel does not follow the majority because it is directly toward Israel—even so the majority’s corners should not follow the minority. Proof: in Judea north–south is permitted even though some north–south corners face Jerusalem—[see] diagram thus []—see Maharsha on <i>Berachot</i> and understand, for he explained that two causes combine as brought above. Hence for Rabbi Akiva the reason is not “toward Israel” but Shechinah in the west. They answered that in the conclusion that Rabbi Akiva forbids <i>chutz laAretz</i>, it follows automatically—see Maharsha and Magen Avraham on this; no need to lengthen—see Magen Avraham letters 5 and 6.

<b>Eshel Avraham</b> letter 3 cites Natz: a city north of Jerusalem—forbidden north–south, permitted east–west. Since Rambam in <i>Hilchot Beit Habechirah</i> 7 brought only east–west, it implies he holds for Rabbi Akiva only east is forbidden everywhere, north–south permitted—the main point is Shechinah in the west; also “east–west permitted” means there is no preference—and if for that reason partitions help, Magen Avraham letter 7 will explain further; in Beit Yosef 2 it sounds like Natz. <b>The Levush</b> 65 at first glance is jarring—he wrote east is also sanctified and concluded one partition suffices, scruple only for the west; I later saw his words are sound and built on “thresholds of fine gold”: surely exposure before him is also forbidden, as the first <i>tanna</i>’s south [rule] proves and Rashi there; Rambam in <i>Beit Habechirah</i> gave a reason east is sanctified—for a woman or one who does not expose before himself so children do not see (for adults it is not certain under strain; for children it is—Eshel Avraham letter 4); even so one partition suffices even for Rambam, as Bach [notes] Rambam does not argue on Yerushalmi—the reason east [matters] is it faces the Shechinah; unlike a partition to the west, there is no place there where east sees “west’s face” in that spot—permitted; no distinction even for exposure behind toward east. In siman 67 he gave a reason for urinating alone that one does not expose as much (see letter 2 in Taz and letter 4 in Magen Avraham); the Levush wrote well—“and her garments are like snow” (<i>Job</i>).

בקצרה כמה הוא שיעור מחיצה אם גובה עשרה ורחב ד׳ טפחים (עיין שמ״ה ושאר דוכתי ור׳י בזה) וכמה יהא מותר להתרחק ממקום המחיצה. הנה מדלא נתנו שיעור משמע כל שאין פירועו מגולה שרי. ויראה דהפרש יש כי בחצר גדול רחוק כמה שרי לכמה דיעות אבל מחיצה א׳ וודאי כל שרחוק הרבה לא מהני ומיהו תוך ד״א יראה דשרי:
**** ENGLISH ****
Briefly: what is the measure of a partition—ten <i>tefachim</i> high and four wide (see Shulchan Aruch <i>Harav</i> and elsewhere and R. Y. on this)—and how far may one distance from the partition? Since no measure was given, it implies wherever exposure is not uncovered it is permitted. It appears there is a difference: in a large courtyard, how far is permitted varies by opinion; with a single partition, certainly if one sits very far away it does not help; yet within four <i>amot</i> it seems permitted.
