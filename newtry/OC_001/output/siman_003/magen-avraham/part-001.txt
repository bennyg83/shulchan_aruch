<b>מגולה</b>. אבל בחצר בכל ענין שרי (ב"י) ומ"מ נ"ל דעיר המוקפת חומ' לא מקרי מחיצו' לענין זה בב"י ולבוש כתב דמחיצה א' לצד מערב סגי ע"כ ומדנקט בש"ע מחיצות משמע דב' מחיצות בעי דהא י"א גם למזרח אסור שהוא רוח שכנגד מערב עב"י ובגמרא דב"ב פ"ק אמרינן דמזרח נקרא אויר יה ומ"מ לפי מ"ש בהג"ה דלהטיל מים שרי א"כ לא חיישינן בפירועו לפניו וא"כ שרי במחיצה שלאחריו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Exposed” [no partitions].</b> In a courtyard, any orientation is permitted (<i>Beit Yosef</i>). Still, it appears to me [<i>nireh li</i>] that a city surrounded by a wall is not called “partitions” for this purpose in <i>Beit Yosef</i>; the Levush wrote that one partition toward the west suffices. Because the <i>Shulchan Aruch</i> says “partitions” (plural), it implies two are required—for some hold east is also forbidden, as the wind opposite west [<i>ruach shekeged ma’arav</i>] (<i>Beit Yosef</i>). In <i>Bava Batra</i> ch. 1 they say the east is called “air of G-d” [<i>avir YaH</i>]. Yet according to the Rema’s gloss that urinating [in any direction] is permitted, we are not concerned about exposure before him while relieving, and therefore one partition behind him suffices.

<b>בין מזרח למערב</b>. פירש"י יכוין שיהא ראשה ומרגלותיה זה לצפון וזה לדרום וכ"כ ס"ס ר"מ ובמ"ע סימן ג' פסק כמ"ש הזוהר פ' במדבר שיהא ראשה ומרגלותיה זה למזרח וזה למערב ותהא מטה בין צפון לדרום וכתב שיהא הראש למערב והטעם ע"פ סוד ע"כ: וכתב הלבוש אף על גב דישן במקום מחיצות אסור דגנאי גדול הוא ע"כ וצ"ע דאטו מי איכא גנאי טפי בשינה מב"ה ול"נ דבשינה ליכא הכירא במחיצות דדרכו בכך משא"כ בב"ה דדרך לפנות בשדה ואי איכא מחיצות איכא היכרא ועי"ל דבחדר ששוכב שם ג"כ מקום השראת שכינה ולאפוקי בב"ה שאפילו מלאכים אין נכנסין שם כמ"ש ס"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Between east and west.”</b> <i>Rashi</i> explains one should arrange the bed so head and feet lie one toward north and the other toward south. So too <i>Sefer Hasichos</i> of Rabbi Moshe of Coucy; Magen Avraham in siman 3 ruled like the Zohar, <i>Parashat Bamidbar</i>: head and feet one east and one west, the bed between north and south; he wrote the head should be west—for a kabbalistic reason. The Levush wrote that even when one sleeps in a partitioned place it is forbidden, for it is a great disgrace—this needs study: is there really more disgrace in sleeping than in a privy? It appears to me [<i>vele’aniyas da’ati</i>] that during sleep one is not as aware of partitions, for that is one’s usual way—unlike a privy, where one typically relieves in a field: if there are partitions, there is “recognizability.” Alternatively: the bedroom is also a place for the Divine Presence to rest, unlike a privy—even angels do not enter there, as explained in siman 1.

<b>ביד ימין</b>. מפני שבימין מראה טעמי התורה ועוד ט"א בגמרא ולכולהו טעמא איטר יד מקנח בשמאל דידיה שהוא ימין דעלמא אך רבא אמר מפני שהתור' נתנה בימין שנאמר מימינו אש דת למו א"כ לפ"ז אפילו איטר אין לקנח בימין דעלמא וצ"ע אי הלכה כמותו דהוא בתרא או שמא כיון דכולהו תנאי דלא כוותיה לית הלכתא כוותיה וכנ"ל, ומ"ש הט"ז דאיטר מקנח בשמאל דעלמא כמ"ש הב"י סימן מ"ג ליתא דלא הוזכר שם איטר ועוד נ"ל דאם כותב בשמאל ושאר מעשיו עושה בימין דיש לקנח בימין דאיכא תרי טעמי שכותב בשמאל וקושר בה תפילין, וכתב בשל"ה שלא יקנח באצבע אמצעי שכורך עליה רצועה של תפילין כמ"ש סי' כ"ז ס"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“With the right hand.”</b> Because with the right one indicates Torah trop, and the Gemara gives another reason; for all those reasons a left-handed person [<i>it’ter</i>] wipes with his left hand, which counts as most people’s “right.” But Rava said: because the Torah was given from the right—“From His right hand a fiery law for them”—therefore even a left-handed person may not wipe with “the world’s right” [his own left hand, which for him is the primary hand, parallel to others’ right]. This requires study: does halacha follow Rava as the later authority, or perhaps since all the <i>Tannaim</i> disagree with him the halacha does not follow him—so it appears to me [<i>kenireh li</i>]. What the Taz wrote—that a lefty wipes with most people’s left, as <i>Beit Yosef</i> siman 43—is not correct: “lefty” is not mentioned there. Moreover it appears to me [<i>ve’od nireh li</i>]: if one writes with his left and does other tasks with his right, he should wipe with his right [most people’s right], for two reasons apply—he writes with that hand and ties tefillin on it. Shelah wrote: one should not wipe with the middle finger around which he wraps a tefillin strap—as in siman 27 s. 8.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Married.”</b> — It appears to me that if his wife is not with him or she is a <i>niddah</i>, it is forbidden. Tosafos: when we say our holy Rabbi did not place his hands below his belt, that was his greatness—he was married and stringent on himself—implying “below the belt” is not literal but means he did not place his hands on his organ; for if it meant literally below the belt, that would be mere ordinary piety for Rabbi… (and from Taz <i>Even HaEzer</i> 182 it seems he did not see these Tosafos). Similarly <i>Avodah Zarah</i> 17a: “Hand to his mother—cut off” for placing his hand below his navel—answered, a thorn lodged in his belly—should he not remove it? Yet his belly would split open. Tosafos: if he does not remove the thorn his organ may rupture—so “belly” means his organ; likewise the Gemara that began with “organ” and ended with “belly.” In <i>Even HaEzer</i> 23 we rule like Rambam—see there. The Gemara: where one is afraid it is permitted to hold, since fear does not lead to [forbidden] thoughts; the <i>poskim</i> omitted this because we are not expert as to which degree of anxiety prevents thoughts—it depends on the person (<i>Beit Yosef</i>). This needs study: <i>Even HaEzer</i> 184 rules anxiety stops menstrual flow, yet we do not say “we are not expert in anxiety”; it is strained to distinguish fear from anxiety, unless rabbinic <i>veset</i> rules are lenient unlike here where the sin is severe. See <i>Shabbat</i> 41a.
