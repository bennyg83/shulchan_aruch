<b>א) [סעיף א'] כשיכנס וכו'  </b>ועכשיו לא נהגו לאומרו. דאין אנחנו מחזיקים עצמנו ליר"ש שהמלאכים מלווים אותנו. ב"ח. ט"ז סעק"א. ר"ז או' א' אבל המצת שמורים דף כ"ח ע"ד כתב צריך להזהר שבכל פעם שיכנס בבה"כ שיאמר התכבדו וכו' היפך ממ"ש הרד"א כי הוא טוב לאומרה כדי להעביר ממנו רוח הטומאה ומחשבות רעות ויהיה לבו פתוח תמיד לד"ת ללמוד אותה בקדושה ובטהרה עי"ש והביאו מחב"ר או' ב' וכתב עוד משם הרב המקובל הג' מוהר"ם פאפיראס ז"ל בס' אור צדיקים וז"ל יאמר התכבדו וכו' ואף שכתב בש"ע שלא לאומרו עכשיו עכ"ז חידש האר"י לאומרו גם עכשיו ובזה ניצול שלא יהרהר בד"ת בבה"כ עכ"ל וכ"כ הרו"ח או' א' בשם ס' מגיד משרים למרן ז"ל בפ מטות ומסעי דא"ל המגיד למרן הקדוש שיאמר התכבדו מכובדים. וכ"כ יפה ללב ח"ג או' א' וכ' שע"ת או' א' ואולילא נאמרו הדברים אלא ליחידים אשר תורתם אומנותם ולא זולתם דמחזי כרמות רוחא אבל הפ"ת ביו"ד סי' רמ"ו או' יוד כתב דלא שייך מחזי כיוהרא אלא בדבר שעושה בפני רבים משא"כ בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
a) [Section 1] When he enters, etc.—nowadays people do not recite it, for we do not presume ourselves to be <i>yerei Shamayim</i> such that angels escort us (Bach, Taz s.k. 1). Raavad holds 1—but <i>Matzat Shemura</i> 28d wrote one should be careful every time he enters a privy to say <i>Hitchabdu</i>, etc., contrary to Radak; it is good to say it to remove from him an impure spirit and evil thoughts and keep his heart open always to Torah—to learn it in holiness and purity—see there; they cited from <i>Chavruta</i> responsa 2, and wrote further from the great kabbalist Maharam Papirash in <i>Or Tzadikim</i>: one should say <i>Hitchabdu</i>, etc., even though <i>Shulchan Aruch</i> says we do not say it nowadays; Arizal innovated to say it now too, and by this one is saved from Torah thoughts in the privy—see there. So too <i>Ruach Chaim</i> 1 in the name of <i>Maggid Meisharim</i> to Maran in <i>Metzot u-Masa’ei</i>: the <i>maggid</i> told the holy Maran to say <i>Hitchabdu mechubadim</i>. So <i>Yafeh Lev</i> ch. 3 s. 1—but they wrote these words only for individuals whose craft is Torah, not others, for it looks arrogant; yet <i>Pri To’ah</i> <i>Yoreh De’ah</i> 246 wrote arrogance applies only to something done before many—not here.

**** ENGLISH ****
f) There in <i>hagaha</i>: He should not speak there, for there is a kind of spirit that incites a person if he speaks there. <i>Or Yisrael</i> in the name of the Arizal. <i>Amudei HaShulchan</i> on <i>Kitzur Shulchan Aruch</i> siman 4 s. 3, and Rav Salat Balula s. 2, wrote that speaking in the privy is forbidden so as not to give external forces a hold on a person’s voice and speech—for he is holy. For great need before having relieved, it may be permitted—see <i>Eliyahu Rabbah</i> s. 2 with a case there. Many works of <i>mussar</i> wrote it is good to think other thoughts in the privy so Torah thoughts do not intrude. <i>Ruach Chaim</i> wrote it is good on weekdays to think of one’s livelihood and household needs; on Shabbat to think of fine buildings and forms. Salat Balula s. 8.

15) There: When there are no partitions—but if there are partitions, even a single partition to the west suffices, for the <i>Shechinah</i> is west (Beit Yosef); Levush <i>Orach Chayim</i> s. 3; <i>Sheyarei Knesset Hagedolah</i> s. 1—he should turn his back to the west. <i>Yeshu’ot Yaakov</i> there <i>Even HaEzer</i> s. 5, Raavad s. 8; some permit even if the partition is on the east when he turns his back east (Magen Avraham, Raavad there). <i>Tosafos Shabbat</i> “<i>Keitzad</i>” “<i>S. Plaim</i>”; it also fits <i>Kitzur Shulchan Aruch</i> siman 4 s. 2—the partition mentioned is not precise but conventional, one partition also suffices, <i>Tosafos</i> there. But Perisha/Magen and <i>Yeshu’ot Yaakov</i> there forbid if the partition is on the east—and their view warrants concern. See Rav Neuwirth (<i>Shemirat Shabbat Kehilchata</i>) s. 5; partition height—Rav Ben Ohev Chayim there s. 9: it need not be ten handbreadths high provided the exposure is not visible, so if short he relieves right next to it so it shields him. Some say even with partitions avoid east-west <i>lechatchila</i>; it is proper to heed them where possible. Where one can build the privy initially, do not place it east-west but north-south (Raavad s. 7, Ben Ohev Chayim there); see Perisha/Magen s. 5. Some are careful not to build a privy except north-south.

16) [Section 6] It is forbidden to sleep between east and west, etc.—Rashi explains aligning head and feet north-south; so <i>Shulchan Aruch</i> at the end of siman 240. However <i>Zohar</i> Bamidbar writes the opposite—the one who lays his bed north to south means head east feet west—Bach cites. <i>Shaar HaMitzvos</i> Bereishit: bed placement north-south parallels Adam’s creation and the upper stature—head east toward da'at called “east … I will bring your seed,” feet west <i>Yismach Moshe</i> as we note for <i>lulav</i>, right south <i>chesed</i> left north <i>gevurah</i>. Rashi in <i>Likutei Pardes</i> withdrew that view and agreed with kabbalists—Birkei Yosef s. 2; Tashbetz Yaakov siman 3 rules like <i>Zohar</i>. Though ShKH wrote in Hagahot Beit Yosef s. 4 that nowadays the world is not careful—see there—and <i>Olas Tamid</i> s. 6 “<i>ke-Mar</i> …”—still one should be careful like <i>Zohar</i> and Arizal.
