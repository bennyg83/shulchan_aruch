**** ENGLISH ****
(s.k. 8) “With the right hand,” etc.—more reasons in <i>Berachot</i> 62a: Rabbi Eliezer—because he eats with it; Rabbi Yehoshua—because he writes; Rabbi Akiva—because he indicates Torah trop with it—three <i>Tannaim</i>. Rava—because the Torah was given from the right—“From His right hand a fiery law for them.” Rabbi Elazar—because he ties tefillin with it. For the first reasons a lefty wipes with his left (= society’s “right”). Were we to rule like Rava, even a lefty could not wipe with most people’s “right” [his left]; Magen Avraham ruled like the last view, not like Rava, since all <i>Tannaim</i> disagree with Rava—and the same applies to Rabbi Elazar’s reason, for no <i>Tanna</i> gives it, yet Magen Avraham still requires wiping with most people’s right. Hence his main reason is “no distinction” between this lefty and other lefties who do everything with the left; if none of the five reasons applied to this lefty we would not invoke “no distinction.” Therefore at least two reasons apply, and again we say “no distinction” between him and other lefties.

**** ENGLISH ****
What the Taz wrote—a lefty wipes with most people’s left, as <i>Beit Yosef</i> siman 43—is not correct as it stands: Magen Avraham had a different Taz text; our Taz may contain a scribal error. In substance the Taz agrees with Magen Avraham that a lefty wipes with most people’s right [his physical right hand]. At first the Taz wrote a lefty wipes with most people’s left, since although all reasons would require wiping with most people’s right, we still worry for Rava’s reason, so he wipes with most people’s left—hence even though the left is generally designated for wiping, one may still show trop with it; proof: a lefty wipes with his left even when showing trop with it. Yet <i>Beit Yosef</i> siman 43 forbids wiping with the “right”—so a lefty must wipe with most people’s right, i.e. his physical right, which also satisfies Rava (verse “from His right hand…” applies to his right even if the verse speaks of G-d). See Taz <i>Orach Chayim</i> 43 s.k. 5: entering a fixed privy—he removes tefillin at a distance, rolls the straps, and holds them in his right hand—Taz apparently holds for that reason he holds specifically with the right, not the left, lest while wiping with the left he improperly hold tefillin in that hand; hence one should not show trop with the left—so how could a lefty manage? He must wipe with most people’s right—then Taz and Magen Avraham do not disagree in practice. This proof is still unclear: there the issue is not holding tefillin in the wiping hand when wiping—Bei’ur Halacha below cites Taz 43:6 briefly; per Taz’s wording Bei’ur Halacha explains: because the left is designated for wiping it is unfit to hold tefillin—but reading Bei’ur Halacha itself, the reason seems to be a rabbinic decree as stated.
