**** ENGLISH ****
[ד] <b>“Between east and west,” etc.</b> <i>Rashi</i>: feet toward east and head toward west—or the reverse—but [the bed’s width lies] between north and south. The Bach and Taz cite the Zohar, <i>Parashat Bamidbar</i>: “between north and south” means head east and feet west—end of quotation. <i>Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah</i> wrote that therefore people are not careful about this nowadays at all—end of quotation. <i>Olas Tamid</i> wrote: “some do this, some do that”—end of quotation; see <i>Tosafos Shabbat</i> 61a <i>s.v.</i> <i>dav</i>… I found in Responsa Menachem Azariah siman 3 that early authorities explained the bed’s <i>width</i> should run north–south so that conception resembles the formation of Adam; the reason is not the Shechinah—on the contrary, one should think holy thoughts at relations. Moreover, even in a privy the prohibition applies only where there is no fence; relations are permitted only where there is a fence and the whole body is covered. For practice he concluded the head of the bed should be west and the northwest corner highest—end of quotation. For <i>Rashi</i> and the plain <i>Shulchan Aruch</i> his head is north or south—so wrote Bach explicitly. In <i>Lechem Chamudos</i> I found he requires the head specifically toward north—hence he mentions north before south even though south is to one’s right—end of quotation. This needs reconciliation with <i>Tehillim</i> 89: “North and right You have created”—where north is listed first. Rabbenu Chananel explained: in the Gemara “north” is called cold and “south” heat—meaning one should use his bed about midnight, when the body has cooled from the food’s heat and temperament is calm: one born from a cold drop is a fool; from a hot drop, wrathful; from an average drop, wise and sociable—“You have given your maidservant men’s offspring.” The Levush wrote that even where there are partitions one must be strict—unlike relieving above, for it is more disgraceful to stretch one’s legs toward the Shechinah—and the Bach answered that at relations one must sanctify oneself more.

[ו] <b>“He sits with his face [toward the Sanctuary],” etc.</b> It implies one’s back may not face the Sanctuary, as Beit Yosef wrote. One may explain the Levush that way, or say he held the main point is Beit Yosef’s first reading: he stated a stringency—even the front while exposing two <i>tefachim</i> is permitted, all the more so the back; still the Levush overlooked <i>Kesef Mishneh</i> <i>Hilchot Bechirah</i> ch. 4, who retracted—there was a scribal error—and one must read Rambam and <i>Shulchan Aruch</i> as: he does not sit facing the Sanctuary but north or south, or he turns the Sanctuary direction aside—end of quotation. <i>Olas Tamid</i> wrote that even now there is a practical difference of “holiness stands in its place” as in <i>Megillah</i>. <i>Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah</i> wrote he did not need to record this ruling and his wording to that point was imprecise. As for his difficulty from <i>Megillah</i>: one should say “holiness stands in its place” applies only to items forbidden in the Temple itself. **** ENGLISH ****
[ח] <b>“Not with the right [hand],” etc.</b> So too not with the middle finger of the left, because one lays tefillin on it (<i>Shelah</i>)—all this for wiping. Rubbing off urine splatter is explained in <i>Beit Yosef</i> siman 43 as permitted even with the right; the same may permit killing a louse with the right. The prohibition on wiping with the right is because tefillin are tied with the right and because one indicates Torah trop with it. For those reasons a left-handed person wipes with his right; for the reason “Torah was given from the right,” even a lefty follows everyone’s right hand and wipes with his left. It appears to me one may show trop with the left even though it is designated for wiping—a lefty wipes with his left while showing trop with it—so I thought; but <i>Beit Yosef</i> siman 43 forbids wiping with the “right,” so a lefty must wipe with his own right, which counts as most people’s left, even for the “Torah from the right” reason. So wrote Taz; Responsa <i>Beit Yaakov</i> 149 disputes the Taz’s wording but not his reason—and did not study Taz’s conclusion. This needs study: perhaps <i>Beit Yosef</i>’s “forbidden to wipe with the right” means everyone’s right, and he read the Gemara about tefillin in a privy—“he holds them in his right”—as most people’s right, not the lefty’s “right”—and that is no proof, for <i>Berachot</i> 23a parallels “holding in his hand” to “holding in his garment.” Magen Avraham wrote: if he writes with his left and does other work with his right, he wipes with his right—two reasons: writing and tying tefillin with the left—end of quotation. That is not obvious, for the left also has two reasons—feeding mouth and trop. The “Torah from the right” of most people is as he himself wrote there.

[יג] <b>“From the corona downward,” etc.</b> Even with an erection he may hold from the corona downward. Scripture therefore teaches that “from the corona downward” is the lower segment of the organ [<i>tzad ha’aretz</i>]; though when he first grasps he may be above the corona, “lower/higher” is judged relative to the organ when not erect—so Beit Yosef.

[טו] <b>להתחכך וכו'.</b> כתב חלקת מחוקק באבן העזר סימן כהן גדול בשם ספר חסידים סימן קע"ו שכתב שאם אחד מתיירא שלא יכשל באשת איש או באשתו נדה חס ושלום טוב לו להוציא זרע לבטלה משיבוא חס ושלום באיסור אשת איש או בנדה רק שיתענה ארבעים יום בימי הקיץ או ישב בקרח בימי החורף עד כאן לשונו. והא דקיימא לן שם דעוון זה חמור מכל העבירות שבתורה רצה לומר דהיינו אם מגרה יצר הרע אנפשיה אבל הכא דבא לו התאוה מוטב לו שיוציא זרע לבטלה משיעבור חס ושלום על אשת איש או נדה כנזכר לעיל ודלא כבית שמואל שם ועיין לקמן סימן ר"מ עוד מדינים אלו:
**** ENGLISH ****
[טו] <b>“To rub [for pleasure],” etc.</b> <i>Chelkas Mechokek</i> in <i>Even HaEzer</i> cites <i>Sefer Chasidim</i> 176: if one fears he might sin with another’s wife or his own <i>niddah</i> Heaven forbid, better to emit seed wastefully than come to <i>eshet ish</i> or <i>niddah</i>—only he should fast forty summer days or sit in the cold in winter—end of his wording. Our ruling there that this sin is graver than all Torah sins means when the yetzer incites him deliberately; here where desire comes on him, better wasteful emission than transgressing with another’s wife or a <i>niddah</i> as above—not like <i>Beit Shmuel</i> there. See later siman 240 on these laws.

[טז] <b>המשהה וכו'.</b> היינו כשמשהה לגדולים או לקטנים. אבל להפחה לא כדמשמע לקמן סימן ק"ג וכן כתב לחם חמודות דף כ"ד. כתב ט"ז דבקטנים נמי הטעם משום שלא יהיה עקר. וכתב מעדני מלך שם דלא נאמר בל תשקצו אלא כשנדחק להוציא הנקבים ולא בהתעוררות בעלמא. ועיין לקמן סימן צ"ב ס"ק ב' עוד מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
[טז] <b>“One who restrains [relief],” etc.</b> That is when he delays defecation or urination; “to press [urine] out” is not as implied later siman 103—so <i>Lechem Chamudos</i> 24a. Taz wrote: for children the reason is also lest they become unable to bear children. <i>Ma’adanei Melech</i> wrote <i>bal tishaktzu</i> applies only when he is forced to void the openings, not to ordinary arousal. See later siman 92 s. 2.
