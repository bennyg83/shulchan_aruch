**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The law of a non-Jew's chametz deposited with a Jew. It contains four se'ifim.</b><br>A non-Jew who deposited his chametz with a Jew: if [the Jew] is responsible for it against theft and loss — whether it is in his house or anywhere within his domain — he is obligated to remove it. {Rama: And even if he in turn deposited it with another non-Jew (Hagahos Alfasi, first chapter of Pesachim).} And some say that even if he is only an unpaid guardian, who is liable for negligence, he is obligated to remove it. And even if he is not legally responsible for it, but he knows that the non-Jew is forceful and will compel him to pay if it is lost, he is obligated to remove it; and some disagree.

**** ENGLISH ****
If he is not responsible for it, he is not obligated to remove it—even if it is under his control, such as in the case of a resident convert living with him in the courtyard; however, he must make a partition ten handbreadths high so that he not forget and eat it. Rema: if a non-Jew placed chametz in a Jew’s house without permission, he covers it with a vessel (Rivash §401). This applies specifically on Yom Tov; but if it is before Yom Tov, he must make a partition (see above §446:1).
