<b>התמיד </b> עבה"ט. ועיין מג"א שכ' פרשת הקרבנות יאמר בעמידה כו' ובבכור שור כתב דא"צ לעמוד אפי' בפ' התמיד שהביא המ"א מע"ה ומעולם לא ראיתי מאן דחש ליה וכ"כ בא"ר ובמח"ב כתב שרבים בתורה לא חשו לזה וכן העלה מור וקציעה וכתב שראה להגאון אביו ז"ל שאומרו מיושב. ובש"צ כתב להוכיח כן מדברי האר"י ז"ל גבי פסוק ה' מלך ע"ש בבכ"ש שכ' מה שאומר כאלו הקרבתי קאי אבעלי' שנתן לכהן וא"כ א"צ לעמוד ובפ"מ כתב ומ"מ בפ' התמיד ראוי לעמוד שקורין בצבור בקול רם ובבכ"ש כתב שם דמ"ש בא"ז שיקרא בבית הכנסת דוגמת העבודה ג"כ אין לחוש לזה אך הואיל ונפיק מפומיה דר' כרוספדאי לשון כבתי כנסיות כו' טוב לעשות כן וע' לקמן מ"ש על דברי הדרך חכמה בזה וע"ש מ"ש בסידורים כאלו הקרבתי או כאלו הקרבנו אינו נכון כי הקרבתי בפ' חטאת אבעלים קאי משא"כ בתמיד אין יחיד מיקרי בעלים ובטור כתב כאלו קרב כו' וזה נכון ע"ש ומשום הא לא אריא וכדאמרינן בתענית שלכך תיקנו מעמדות לפי שאין קרבנו של אדם קרב והוא אינו עומד ע"ג לפיכך תיקנו כו' והיינו לפי שכל אדם מישראל יש לו חלק בתמידי צבור שנקחין מתרומת הלשכה וה"ל כאלו הם נתנו לכהן בעדם קרבן התמיד וא"כ כשאומר יהי רצון בפ' התמיד נמי שייך לו' כאלו הקרבנו או הקרבתי והיינו ע"י הכהן שהוא שלוחא דידן ודרחמנא ומ"ש בבה"ט בשם דרך חכמה שיזהר לאומר' בצבור עם הש"ץ כי קרבן תמיד נקרבת בצבור צ"ע דאטו הצבור עומדים בשעת הקרבה רק אנשי מעמד וכיון שכ"א אומר לעצמו יה"ר כאלו קרב כו' אין שייכות לצבור בזה כלל ומ"ש הרמ"א ולחזור פ' התמיד לבד עם הצבור ויכוין כו' לאו חיובא הוא רק כוונת הדברים מבואר בטור שכתכ שראוי לכל אחד לומר רבון כו' ואם אין יכול לאומרו מפני שהוא עם הצבור טוב הוא שיקרא פ' התמיד לבדו ויאמר אותן ואין לחוש אם יקרא שנית עם הצבור ולרווחא דמלתא יכוין שהוא כקורא בתורה עכ"ל ור"ל שלפי שדרכם היה שכל הצבור אמרו הכל יחד בקול רם א"כ כשיאמר אותו לבדו אז יצטרך לשתוק בהגיעו לפ' התמיד עם הצבור ואין זה נכון לכן כתב שאין לחוש אם יקרא שנית כו' אבל פשיטא שאם אינו מתפלל עם הצבור יחיד א"צ לאומרה בצבור דוקא שהרי כ' שאף שיאמר שנית בציבור יאמר כקורא בתורה וא"כ אינו עולה לו האמירה בצבור דוגמת קרבן כלל:

<b>The Tamid.</b> See Baer Heitev. Magen Avraham writes that the passages of the offerings should be recited while standing, etc. Bechor Shor rules that one need not stand even for the passage of the Tamid cited by Magen Avraham from Emek HaBerachah, and says he has never seen anyone show concern for this. Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise. Machazik Berachah reports that many Torah scholars were not concerned, and Mor U'Ketziah reaches the same conclusion, writing that he saw his father, the Gaon, recite it seated. Shalmei Tzibbur brings a proof from Arizal's teaching concerning the verse 'Hashem reigns'; see there. Bechor Shor explains that the phrase 'as though I offered' applies to an owner who gave his offering to a kohen, so he need not stand. Peri Megadim nevertheless writes that it is proper to stand for the Tamid passage because it is recited aloud by the congregation. Bechor Shor further writes that one need not be concerned for Or Zarua's statement that it should be read in the synagogue in imitation of the Temple service. Still, since R' Kruspedai used the expression 'in the synagogues, etc.,' it is preferable to do so. See below regarding Derech Chochmah's statement on this matter. Bechor Shor also writes that the wording in siddurim, 'as though I offered' or 'as though we offered,' is incorrect. Regarding a sin-offering, 'I offered' refers to the owner; regarding the Tamid, an individual is not called its owner. Tur writes, 'as though it were offered,' which is correct; see there. This argument is not conclusive. Taanis explains that the ma'amados were instituted because a person's offering should not be brought while he is not standing over it. Every Jew has a share in the communal Tamid offerings purchased from the chamber funds. It is therefore as though all Israel gave the kohen the Tamid on their behalf. When reciting Yehi Ratzon after the Tamid passage, one may consequently say 'as though we offered' or 'as though I offered'; through the kohen, who is the agent both of us and of Heaven. Baer Heitev cites Derech Chochmah that one should be careful to recite it with the congregation and prayer leader because the Tamid is a communal offering. This requires study. Did the entire congregation stand at the offering? Only the men of the ma'amad did so. Since each person privately says, 'May it be Your will that it be as though it were offered, etc.,' the congregation has no role here. Rama's instruction to repeat the Tamid passage alone with the congregation and intend, etc., is not an obligation. Tur makes the intent clear: 'It is proper for each person to say, Master of the universe, etc. If he cannot say it because he is with the congregation, it is preferable that he read the Tamid passage privately and say these words. He need not be concerned if he reads it a second time with the congregation; for completeness he should intend then that he is reading Torah.' Their custom was for the entire congregation to recite everything aloud together. If he recited it privately, he would otherwise need to remain silent when the congregation reached the Tamid passage, which is improper. Tur therefore says there is no concern if he reads it again. Plainly, an individual not praying with the congregation need not recite it specifically in a congregation. Indeed, Tur writes that even the second public recital should be intended merely as Torah study, so the public recital does not count as an imitation of the communal offering.

<b>Se'if 1: The Shulchan Aruch says that the verses of the Musaf are recited.</b> Arizal's view is that the Musaf verses should not be recited after the Tamid passage, and certainly not the verses of Rosh Chodesh. See Shalmei Tzibbur for several precise readings in Pitum HaKetores. According to Arizal's teaching, one should say, 'You are Hashem, God,' and the word hatzori should have a chataf kamatz. See Knesset HaGedolah in the name of Radbaz. One should say, 'He returns them to the mortar,' not 'and he returns them.' One should say, 'also Jordan amber,' without adding a mem to the word, for its letters correspond to the word katef, etc. One should say, 'any amount,' rather than merely 'which is,' as Arizal writes. The text is, 'with which to rub the onycha,' not 'to beautify it,' as printed in some works. The text should read: 'This is the rule: If prepared in its proper measure, it is valid in halves; and if he omitted one, etc.' So writes R' Yisrael Sarug. Regarding 'Abaye arranged, etc.,' Arizal writes that one need not say 'precedes' in each clause. One says simply, 'and the incense precedes the limbs,' continuing this way through the bowls; only then does one say, 'and the bowls precede, etc.' In Ana B'Koach, each pair of words should be recited together, followed by a pause: 'Ana b'koach,' pause, 'gedulas yeminecha,' and so forth. This follows the pattern of 'with two he covers his face, etc.' Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, siman 51, records that some stand while reciting Ana B'Koach. Beit David explains why after the incense passage we do not say, 'as though we burned the incense': We have just recited that if one omitted, etc., and there is concern that we may have omitted something. See below, siman 132; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5. Birkei Yosef cites R' Menachem di Lonzano that in the phrase 'speedily, when the Beis HaMikdash will be built,' one should carefully say 'when the Beis HaMikdash will be built' in the passive form, because the future Temple will be fashioned by the Creator; 'the Sanctuary, Hashem, that Your hands established.' The same form should be used before Eizehu Mekoman and after Shemoneh Esrei. This is not conclusive. See Tosafos Yom Tov, the second chapter of Maaser, citing the Yerushalmi. Even the ordinary phrase can mean that it will visibly be built by Heaven. He further writes that textual errors entered the passage 'Give thanks to Hashem, etc.,' which appears in Divrei HaYamim and Tehillim. One error concerns the phrase 'the land of Canaan'; a second version reads 'God, the God of Israel.' This is not the reading in Divrei HaYamim, and our text follows Divrei HaYamim. One must pause slightly between 'in the waters' and 'the mighty.' A tipcha appears beneath 'in the waters,' and 'the mighty' has a phrase-ending accent. 'The mighty' does not modify the waters but the Egyptians, as in the teaching: 'Let the Mighty One; the Holy One, blessed is He; come, etc., from among the mighty; the Egyptians, of whom it is written, They sank, etc.' The meaning is: 'The mighty sank like lead in the waters.' Although 'mighty' can also describe waters, since it can refer to the Egyptians one should pause slightly rather than recite the words in one breath.
