<b>וא"ל דהיינו כו'</b> קאי על מ"ש שם דאין לברך על הדלקת נר ביוה"כ (דלא כמ"ש פה בש"ע סעיף ב') דדוקא בשבת שהדלקת נר בשבת חובה יש לברך. אבל ביוה"כ דהדלקת נר תליא במנהגא. א"כ אפילו במקום שנוהגים להדליק אין לברך עליו דאין לברך על דבר שאינו אלא משום מנהג וע"ז כ' וא"ל דוקא בחדר כו' תליא במנהג אבל בבית חובה להדליק וא"כ שפיר יש לברך על נר שמדליק בבית. ודחה זה דע"כ גם בבית תליא במנהג. דהא תנן מדליקין בבתי כנסיו' כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one should not say that this is, etc.</b> This refers to what is written there, that one does not recite a blessing over lighting the Yom Kippur candle, unlike what is written here in the Shulchan Aruch, se'if 2. Specifically on Shabbos, where lighting the Shabbos candle is an obligation, one recites a blessing. But on Yom Kippur, where lighting a candle depends on custom, even in a place where they customarily kindle, one should not recite a blessing over it, for one does not recite a blessing over something that is only because of custom. About this he wrote: and one should not say that specifically in the room, etc., it depends on custom, but in the house it is an obligation to kindle, and if so one may properly recite a blessing over the candle one kindles in the house. He rejected this, for necessarily also in the house it depends on custom, since we learned: they kindle in synagogues, etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is difficult, for if so, etc.</b> That is, regarding what the Mordechai wrote at the beginning of Yoma, that some kindle on the table and not in the room, one should ask, according to the truth: if so, when he sees her he will desire her, etc. And regarding what the Mordechai wrote at the end of Yoma, it is difficult: why did he need to reject it by saying that necessarily also in the house it depends on custom, from the Mishnah, "they kindle in synagogues, etc."? If so, in a place where the custom is not to kindle in the room but in the house it is obligatory to kindle, then when he sees her he will desire her, etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Subparagraph 4) And if they were extinguished, etc.</b> See what I wrote in siman 276, se'if 3, that it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to take a lit candle and walk with him, since there is only the prohibition of moving muktzeh and the non-Jew also needs it, for he walks with him. But it is forbidden to tell him to kindle a candle, even if the non-Jew walks with him. If so, regarding moving the dripping wax, in a place where the custom is that that non-Jew also has a share in the dripping wax, it would have been possible to permit telling the non-Jew to catch the dripping wax, since the non-Jew also acts for his own benefit, as above where the non-Jew walks with him. However, the Magen Avraham wrote there in the name of Radbaz that this should be permitted only for Torah scholars, lest people come to draw improper conclusions. In Eliyah Rabbah he wrote as follows: In the old Levushim he wrote, see siman 276, se'if 2, and siman 307, se'if 5. The Peri Megadim explained that he means that according to one opinion there, one can learn a reason for the custom to be lenient and tell a non-Jew to repair the candles, for he holds that telling a non-Jew is permitted even for full labor in a mitzvah situation. The Eliyah Rabbah concluded: and I wrote there that all the Acharonim agreed to forbid, end quote. The Magen Avraham there also wrote that one should not protest against those who permit it, for it is better that they be inadvertent sinners. The Magen Avraham also wrote there: specifically a mitzvah from which his body benefits, such as a candle for eating; see there, and see what the Eliyah Rabbah wrote about him.
