**** ENGLISH ****
“The kindling of lights (lamps) on Yom Kippur” - Containing four paragraphs. In a place where it is customary to kindle a light on the night of Yom Kippur, one does so; 68a The following comment is given by Magen Avraham , (see footnote 33.): 610:1 - "We light": So that one will not have intercourse by the light of the candle. And for those whose custom it is not to light (a candle in the bedroom) their reasoning is so that one will not see her (one's wife) and desire her. in a place 68b The following comment is given by Turei Zahav , (see footnote 19a.): 610:1 - "In a place (location or town) where it was customary to light": And the two places (locations, towns) had the same one intention, to prevent intercourse. The one who lights (a candle), his intention is (to observe the law) that it is forbidden to have intercourse by the light of a candle; and he who does not light (a candle), his intention is that he should not see her (his wife) and desire her, even though it is forbidden for him to have intercourse with her, nevertheless he would not avoid thinking about her and this will avoid (nullify, prevent) his clean thoughts on this day (Yom Kippur). where it is not customary to kindle a light, one does not do so. (If there is a lamp in the house one is obligated to kindle a light also in the room where he sleeps so that he does not due to over-stimulation have relations with his wife, since he sees her in the light (i.e., illuminating) of the house, ( Maharil (R' Yaakov Moelin) ), 69 Maharil , Maharil (R' Yaakov Moelin) ; see footnote 8. (Marital relations must take place in total darkness). But if it (Yom Kippur) falls on the Sabbath, all are obligated to kindle lights (for the Sabbath). {Rama: Hagah : And one blesses (finishes the blessing over the candles with) “to light the candle of Shabbat and Yom Kippur.”}

**** ENGLISH ****
The custom everywhere is to increase lamps in the synagogues and to spread out fine cloths in the synagogue. {Rama: And the custom is that for every person, adult or minor, a lamp is made (Mordechai; Mahari Weil); also a soul-lamp for one's father and mother who have died (Kol Bo), and so is proper, and so wrote some of the [early] authorities. And if these lamps went out on Yom Kippur, one should not tell a non-Jew to relight them (Maharil). One whose lamp went out on Yom Kippur should relight it on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, and not extinguish it again, but leave it to burn to the end; and he should also accept upon himself that all his days he will not extinguish his lamp on Motza'ei Yom Kippur — neither he nor another (so it is found in old Minhagim). Some say that the tables should be spread on Yom Kippur as on Shabbos (Mordechai; Minhagim), and so is the custom. Some have written that the custom is to wear white and clean garments on Yom Kippur, resembling the ministering angels; and likewise the custom is to wear the kittel, which is white and clean, and is also a garment of the dead — and through this a person's heart is humbled and broken (his own opinion, from Hagahos Maimoni, chapter 7 of Shevisas Asor).}
