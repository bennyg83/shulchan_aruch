Just as there is a dispute about the pole, meaning the wood attached to it at its beginning or at its end, whether it belongs at the beginning or the end, so too there is a dispute about the blank space. <b>And behold.</b> The Mechaber wrote that it requires parchment surrounding each letter, so every letter must be surrounded by parchment; the humps of the ches like two zayins, and so on; and the hanging of the heh and kuf, all like a sefer Torah, according to Rabbeinu Tam, except for what is explicitly mentioned as not being equal to a sefer Torah. But regarding making the tagin of Shaatnez Getz, he brought two opinions. There is no difficulty from processing the parchment, for the proof is from a Megillah among the Writings, as I wrote above. In the Beis Yosef, regarding what the Tur wrote that the Baal Halachos Gedolos wrote that one need not make tagin on it, meaning unlike Rabbeinu Tam, perhaps his intent is not specifically tagin; the same applies to all laws of a sefer Torah, that it has none of them except what is explicitly mentioned as equal to a sefer Torah. This is difficult here, as we wrote. Also, regarding what he wrote about the humps of the ches letters, scribes are not accustomed to make humps even in a sefer Torah, all the more so in a Megillah. See siman 32, that exacting scribes wrote humps, not ordinary scribes. There is something like proof from what is written that one should not make heh letters into ches letters. In Yoreh De'ah 274, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch use the term humps there; see Magen Avraham, siman 36, letter 3.

<b>Further.</b> I will explain to you: in Yoreh De'ah 281, seifim 3 and 4, regarding a sefer Torah, a slave, a woman, and a minor are invalid, as is learned from tefillin; see there. Regarding Megillah, a woman and a slave are obligated at least in hearing it, and therefore they can write it. But a minor, even if he has reached the age of chinuch, is not comparable to an adult, as explained in siman 689, and is invalid to write it. Regarding a mamzer and a convert, some invalidate them for writing a sefer Torah. See the Shach there, letter 8, who gives the reason in the name of the Mordechai, that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation and is therefore invalid to write. According to this, the same applies to Megillah, and it appears that it should be written by someone who can discharge others through performance of the mitzvah.

Know that what the Mordechai wrote, that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation, is astonishing in my eyes, for a mamzer is a full Jew and is obligated in the matter. To say that those invalid to enter the congregation are not included in mutual responsibility, but only those who are valid, would apply also to an Egyptian and an Edomite. However, perhaps a fourth-generation Egyptian has no deficiency on the side of his soul; see the Tosafos Yom Tov in Rosh Hashanah, that nowadays one discharges others through mutual responsibility. If so, this can be resolved, but it requires analysis, for I found this in no authority. See above, simanim 53 and 55, regarding a mamzer serving as shaliach tzibbur; see there. Also, hearing is like responding.

Know that the Shach in Yoreh De'ah there wrote the reason that a mamzer and a convert are not obligated in all mitzvos, and therefore they are invalid; see there. This means a mamzer is permitted to a maidservant; see Even HaEzer, siman 4, and the Beis Shmuel there. A convert is permitted to a mamzeres, an Egyptian, and an Edomite. But if not being fit to be a king or a judge for chalitzah were enough, then a person blind in one eye should also be invalid for writing a sefer Torah. However, in the Shulchan Aruch there it says ger toshav, meaning one who accepted not to worship idolatry in partnership; I explained the matter of partnership in my Pri on Yoreh De'ah. This implies that an ordinary righteous convert is valid for a sefer Torah. If so, according to the Taz there, letter 4, a mamzer is valid for Megillah, while according to the Mordechai he is invalid. See Magen Avraham 39, letter 9, in the name of Darkei Moshe, that a mamzer and a ger toshav are valid for tefillin. This is difficult: tefillin require writing for their own sake, so how can this be valid? See Magen Avraham 32. In the Fuerth printing of Darkei Moshe, the word toshav was omitted; see there.

Again Hashem enlightened my eyes, and I examined the Mordechai, Krakow edition, at the end of Bava Kamma, page 56. In the print there is an error; see the lower number there, 7 and 8. He brought from Maseches Sofrim the reading “convert” without qualification, and the Mordechai, according to his approach, explains it as a ger toshav. But according to the reason that he cannot discharge the public obligation, as the Mordechai wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Simchah, who did not have the reading mamzer, for with a sefer Torah whoever can discharge the public writes it, and a mamzer can discharge, while the Mordechai does read mamzer and learns that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation, either from Torah law or from the words of the Sages. According to this, the same applies to a righteous convert, based on what the Rosh wrote in Berachos, that women are not included in mutual responsibility; see there, page 20b. Also according to the Shach’s reason, the same applies to a righteous convert. In Maseches Sofrim the reading is simply convert, as we wrote. See what I wrote in simanim 39, 53, and 55. This requires analysis.

<b>Vav.</b> See the Taz. This is the explanation of the Ran, of blessed memory. See the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chayim in the name of the Rosh. In the Rosh it implies that one should lengthen it in writing, so that the vav is among the large letters; see there. I did not understand the explanation of the Ran, of blessed memory. If he means that the vav should be only a straight line without a bent head, this is not the form of a vav, as the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 36 in the name of HaManhig, that it must be like hooks, like pegs for posts, so that the curtains should not fall. If he means that the somewhat bent head should stand upright diagonally upward, nevertheless I have not seen the scribes of our time do this; they only write it among the large letters. This requires analysis.

Regarding missing and extra letters, see Eliyah Rabbah, who elaborated and brought different versions. Although after the fact it is valid immediately, for it is like where the scribe omitted up to half of it, nevertheless initially it is proper and correct to write according to the law. In Shulchan Aruch, seif 8: publicly, one does not fulfill the obligation even after the fact with a Megillah written among the Writings, while an individual reads from it. This implies that even initially, even if he has a Megillah written separately, he may read from this one. But in the Tur he wrote: “but an individual who reads from it has fulfilled the obligation,” which implies after the fact. See the Rosh, chapter 2, page 19. From there it also implies that if it is missing or extra, the public fulfills with it after the fact, though perhaps not initially. The Mechaber ruled like the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 2 of Megillah, halachah 1, that publicly one has not fulfilled the obligation from it even after the fact, while for an individual, even initially he reads from it. Perhaps what the Tur wrote, that an individual fulfills after the fact, is because initially an individual is not common, for a Megillah in its proper time initially requires ten. Nevertheless, he should have explained that if there are not ten and he has a Megillah written separately, he may initially read from one written among the Writings. See Megillah 19a, regarding the one who read from a Megillah written among the Writings; see there. See the Beis Yosef regarding what the Mordechai wrote, that it is not comparable to a Megillah written among the Writings. From the fact that it is not comparable to a Megillah written among the Writings, there is no proof that an individual may read initially; therefore the Mordechai needed to distinguish between them regarding the initial law.

In the Rema, seif 11: one does not recite a blessing over a stolen Megillah. See siman 649, seif 1, that even if he acquired it through a physical change and the like, nevertheless he should not bless over it. The same applies here. Although there is no theft of sound, nevertheless there too he acquired it and still does not bless, since it came through theft; the same applies here. The same applies to shofar, that he does not bless; see siman 586 in the Magen Avraham there. With Hashem’s help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham here. What is written that with a stolen Megillah he fulfilled the obligation because there is no theft of sound: were it not for that reason, even though “yours” is not written, nevertheless it would be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, like shofar. If not for the fact that there is no theft of sound, he would not fulfill the obligation. See the Beis Yosef here and in siman 586. According to the Mechaber in siman 649, seif 5, which is the view of the Rambam, of blessed memory, that in Rabbinic matters one fulfills even with a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, one must say that Megillah is words of Kabbalah and is like Torah law, and therefore the reason that there is no theft of sound is needed. See the Beis Yosef, siman 686, in the name of the Baal HaMaor; see the Beis Yosef, siman 649, and here, regarding what he wrote in the name of the Rashba, of blessed memory.

If one wrote a Megillah on something from which benefit is forbidden, he has fulfilled the obligation, for mitzvos, even Rabbinic mitzvos, were not given for benefit; see Rosh Hashanah 28 in the Ran, and see siman 649. I am uncertain if he wrote it on hide of idolatry or of an idolatrous city, which must be burned. Regarding shofar in siman 586, since it requires a minimum measure, he does not fulfill with it. Regarding Megillah, does he fulfill, or perhaps he does not, because it is like reading by heart? See Even HaEzer regarding a get written on items forbidden for benefit and idolatry. With Hashem’s help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham.
