<b>Seif 1: On gevil or, and so on, like a sefer Torah.</b> There it says “on a sefer,” and in the Gemara there: from where do we know this? It is derived by the word “writing” from “writing,” and so on. This follows his own view, for he ruled in chapter 1 of the laws of tefillin that a sefer Torah may be written only on gevil or parchment. So too the Shulchan Aruch ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, seif 3. This is as written in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, that one must write on gevil on the hair side and on parchment on the flesh side, and if one changed this it is invalid. This implies that only these are valid. So too they said in the first chapter of Maseches Sofrim, and in Bava Basra 14a: He said to him, with gevil it is six, with parchment how much, and so on. What is written in Shabbos 79b, that the academy of Menashya taught, and so on, and they established it as referring to a sefer Torah, is an individual opinion.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he wrote it, and so on.</b> So too he ruled regarding a sefer Torah. This does not mean with each one by itself, for kankantum is invalid in the Mishnah, and it implies that it is not ink; the same applies to a sefer Torah, as written in Shabbos 103b, “if he wrote it not with ink,” and so on. In the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, it is a halachah to Moshe from Sinai that they be written with ink. Likewise, gall-nut water in Gittin 19a implies that it is not ink except when both are together, for then it is ink. It teaches us that although it cannot be erased, as written in Eruvin 13a, nevertheless it is permitted. He ruled like Rabbi Yehudah in the name of Rabbi Meir, who said: into all of them one may place, and so on, as written in chapter 1 of the laws of tefillin.

This is their language: How is ink made? They gather the soot of oils, and so on, soak it in gall-nut water or the like, and write with it, so that if one erases it, it is erased. This is the ink with which it is the choicest mitzvah to write a sefer Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos. If one wrote all three with gall-nut water and kankantum, which endures and is not erased, they are valid. If so, what did the halachah said to Moshe at Sinai, that they be written with ink, exclude? It excludes other kinds of colors, such as red, green, and the like; if one wrote even one letter in a sefer Torah, tefillin, or mezuzah with other kinds of colors or with gold, they are invalid. So too the Rosh wrote in Hilchos Ketanos. This is what is meant here by “if he wrote it with other,” and so on. This is his language in his commentary to the second chapter of Sotah: I saw in Eretz Yisrael that they distance themselves from writing a sefer Torah with one type of ink called in Arabic chibr, and write only with actual ink. He erred in the halachic ruling from the Gemara, for the last statement said in the Gemara on this matter is that one may place kankantum into all of them except the Sotah passage in the Mikdash. The copyist wrote there: I, the copyist, saw fit to explain what chibr is, as the Geonim explained. Chibr is gall-nut ink, into which they place kankantum, a kind of blackening earth that is not erased. So too wrote Rabbeinu Chananel, of blessed memory, and so on; see there.
