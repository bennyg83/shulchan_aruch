<b>And some are stringent.</b> This follows Rashi's approach, that a kid is permitted because the draft is harmful to it. Accordingly, the prohibition stated in se'if 2 regarding meat on coals is not limited to meat directly on the coals. The same applies near the coals, and even inside an oven whenever it is open, because the rationale that the draft is harmful does not apply when the oven is open regardless. This is the meaning of, 'and on the fire, where it is exposed': whenever it is exposed, it is called meat on coals. The wording is intended to prevent one from understanding the Gemara's phrase, 'meat on coals,' as referring only to meat directly on the fire. The Tur likewise writes, 'near the fire.' Tosafos there, s.v. hasam, proves this as well. They ask: according to Rav Ashi, who permits it, how can the Mishnah state, 'One may not place bread,' etc., and, 'One may lower,' etc., since otherwise it would be forbidden? They answer that the Mishnah concerns an open oven, which is like meat on coals. The Shulchan Aruch does not write that it is permitted once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, as stated in se'if 2, because it rules according to the first opinion in siman 253, se'if 1; without that concern it is permitted.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 5: Its surfaces, etc., but not, etc.</b> The Rambam wrote this regarding both bread and a cake, referring to the surfaces attached to the oven or to the fire. The Beit Yosef distinguished between them and held that the Rambam ruled like R' Eliezer, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote, but he gained nothing by this distinction, as explained by the Taz. It appears to me that the Rambam ruled like the first Tanna, as is evident from his commentary to the Mishnah, for it is not his practice to rule like a lone opinion merely because the Gemara raised a question according to that opinion, as I have written in many places. Rather, he was troubled why the Gemara did not raise the question according to the first Tanna. He therefore explained unlike Rashi and Tosafos, that this is neither necessarily a leniency nor necessarily a stringency, for sometimes one surface forms a crust first and sometimes the other. The Gemara did not ask according to the first Tanna because according to him one surface certainly suffices, since he stated without qualification that its surfaces should form a crust. This is why the Rambam wrote, 'in the oven or by the fire.' The Tur wrote likewise.

They rule like R' Eliezer and explain, in accordance with Tosafos, that his statement is lenient, as is explicit in their words there regarding the blessing of Hamotzi. Only with regard to a pie does R' Eliezer agree, as Tosafos wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: 'R' Eliezer agrees regarding the showbread,' etc. That is because it is thick and does not bake quickly, as stated in the second chapter of Pesachim and at the end of the second chapter of Beitza; the same applies here. It should not have been necessary to mention specifically the lower surface, but they adopted the wording of Tosafos in the name of the Yerushalmi, for here even the upper surface must crust. It appears more likely that they too agree that the upper surface may sometimes crust first, but ordinarily the lower one crusts first. Therefore, here, where a full crust is required, both surfaces are needed, whereas elsewhere, where we rule like R' Eliezer, there is no concern. In this way their words accord properly with the Rambam's ruling that one surface suffices in every case, although they differ in explanation, as stated above.

<b>אפי' בשוגג.</b> ירושלמי והביאו סמ"ג ע"ש ועב"י וז"ל הירושלמי שם פ"ג הלכה א' תני לא תמלא אשה עססיות ותורמסין כו' ואם נתנה מ"ש אסורין בכדי שיעשו ר' אחא ר' ייסא אמר בשוגג כר"י א"ר מנא יאות אמר ר' ייסא דתני הנוטע בשבת שוגג יקיים מזיד יעקור ובשביעית בין בשוגג בין במזיד יעקור ר"י אומר חילוף הדברים הנוטע בשבת בין בשוגג בין במזיד יעקור בשביעית שוגג יקיים ומזיד יעקור למה מפני שהניית שבת עליו וכאן מכיון שאת אומר ימתין למ"ש בכדי שיעשו כמי שלא נהנה מחמת שבת כלל כו' ע"ש ואנן קי"ל כר"י במבשל וכמש"ש כר"י דמבשל וה"ה לנוטע ועמש"ל סי' ש"א ע"ש ומש"ל הטעם מפני שדברים אלו אינו צריכין כו' הוא דלא כרש"י והזקיקו לפרש כן דומיא דמים ורש"י נזהר שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even unintentionally.</b> This is stated in the Yerushalmi and cited by the Semag; see there and see the Beit Yosef. The Yerushalmi, chapter 3, halacha 1, states: 'It was taught: A woman may not fill a pot with beans and lupines, etc. If she placed them there, after Shabbos they are forbidden until the time of b'chedi she'ya'asu has elapsed. R' Acha said in the name of R' Yasa: This applies when it was done unintentionally, in accordance with R' Yehuda. R' Mana said: R' Yasa spoke correctly, for it was taught: One who plants on Shabbos unintentionally may leave it in place; intentionally, he must uproot it. During Shemittah, whether unintentionally or intentionally, he must uproot it. R' Yehuda reverses the rulings: One who plants on Shabbos, whether unintentionally or intentionally, must uproot it. During Shemittah, if unintentionally he may leave it, and if intentionally he must uproot it. Why? Because the benefit of Shabbos remains upon it. Here, however, once you say that he must wait after Shabbos for the time of b'chedi she'ya'asu, it is as though he derived no benefit from Shabbos at all, etc.' See there. We rule like R' Yehuda regarding one who cooks, as explained there concerning R' Yehuda's ruling about cooking, and the same applies to planting. See what I wrote above in siman 301, and what I wrote there. The reason stated above, that these matters do not require, etc., does not accord with Rashi, and he was compelled to explain it that way because the case is comparable to water. Rashi took care to avoid this difficulty there.
