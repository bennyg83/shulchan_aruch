<b>(ס"ק א') שאצל כו'</b> כדי להבין דברי ש"ע צריך להציע דברי הש"ס ופוסקים המצטרכים פה דאיתא דף י"ח ע"ב השתא דאמר מר כל מידי דקשה ליה זיקא לא מגלי ל"ל לחיתוי ולכן בשר גדי אפי' לא שריק פי התנור בטיט שרי וברחא היינו עז וטח פי התנור ג"כ שרי לכ"ע אבל עז ולא שריק פי התנור פליגי ר"י מדפתי אוסר ור"א שרי ופריך לר"א והתנן אין צולין בשר בצל וביצה אלא כדי שיצולו מבע"י כמב"ד ולר"א הא בכל ענין שרי ולמה הצריכו מתני' שיצולו מבעוד יום ומשני התם בבישרא אגומרא דמוד' ר"א וכ' התוס' והרא"ש בשם בה"ג דאע"ג דחי לגמרי מותר סמוך לחשיכ' כדלעיל סי' רנ"ג מ"מ סוגיא דהכא דמיירי בצלי אפי' חי אסור ויש פוסקים כר"א אבל רוב הפוסקים פסקו כר"י מדפתי וכן פסק בש"ע ונחלקו רש"י והעומדים בשיטתו עם הרמב"ם ועומדים בשיטתו בפירוש' דשמעת' הרמב"ם ס"ל הא דאמרי' והשתא דאמרי' כו' דקשיא ליה זיקא כו' האי התירא לא אצטריך לגדי דבגדי הטעם כיון דבשרו רך ובחימום מיעוט סגי לכן ל"ח שיחתה דאם יחת' יתחרך מרוב החום:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if katan 1: Which is near, etc.</b> To understand the words of the Shulchan Aruch, one must first set out the relevant statements of the Gemara and poskim. Shabbos 18b states: 'Now that the master said that anything harmed by air will not be uncovered, there is no concern that one may stoke the coals.' Therefore kid meat is permitted even when the mouth of the oven was not sealed with clay. With regard to a goat, if the mouth of the oven was sealed, all agree it is permitted. If it was not sealed, Rav Yirmiyah of Difti forbids it and R' Ashi permits it. The Gemara challenges R' Ashi from the Mishnah: one may not roast meat, an onion, or an egg unless they will roast before nightfall to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. According to R' Ashi, however, it should be permitted in every case, so why did the Mishnah require that they roast before nightfall? The Gemara answers that the Mishnah concerns meat placed directly on coals, where R' Ashi agrees that it is forbidden. Tosafos and the Rosh write in the name of Halachos Gedolos that although completely raw food may be placed shortly before nightfall, as stated above in siman 253, the present discussion concerns roasting, for which even completely raw meat is forbidden. Some poskim rule like R' Ashi, but most rule like Rav Yirmiyah of Difti, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly. Rashi and those who follow his approach disagree with the Rambam and those who follow his approach regarding the explanation of the passage. The Rambam holds that the permission based on the food being harmed by air was not needed for kid meat, because its flesh is tender and a small amount of heat suffices. Therefore we are not concerned that he may stoke, since stoking would scorch it from excessive heat.

<b>והתירא דקשיא ליה זיקא אצטריך משום עז דבשרו קשה.</b> ויש לחוש לחיתוי לכן לר"י מדפתי לא הותר כ"א בשריק פי התנור ואז ל"ל שיחת' דאי יחת' היה צריך לגלות פי התנור ולאשר קודם הפתיח' הי' החום מתקמץ וע"י הפתיח' יכנס האויר ויקרר התנור ויתקשה הבשר וכמ"ש בש"ע. ולפ"ז גדיא מותר אפי' פי התנור מגולה לגמרי ואינו מכוסה כלל דהא ל"ל שיחת' כיון דא"צ חיתוי אם יחתה יתחרך ויפסיד ולר"א דגדי ועז דיניהם שוה ומותר בכל ענין א"כ הא דתנן אין צולין בשר כו' ומשני התם בבישרא אגומרא ופי' הרמב"ם דמונח על הגחלים ממש וכה"ג אפי' בשר גדי אסור שמא יחתה ואי ע"י חיתוי יתחרך מ"מ חזינן דניחא ליה במהירת הצלי אע"ג דמתחרך דהא הניחו על גבי גחלים ובודאי יתחרך ע"כ שיטת הרמב"ם והוא שיטת הרב"י פה. אבל רש"י ס"ל דהא דאמרי' כ"מ דקשיא ליה זיקא כו' קאי אהתירא דגדי דמה"ט בגדי ל"ח שמא יחתה משום דקשי ליה זיקא. וא"כ בגדי ג"כ לא הותר אא"כ פי התנור סתום דאי פתוח למה לא ניחוש לחיתוי הא בלא"ה הרוח יכנס בתוכו אלא דעז דלא קשיא ליה זיקא אפי' התנור סתום. אסור דחיישי' לחיתוי דחיישי' שמא יגלה פי התנור ולכן בעינן לר"י מדפתי שיהי' פי התנור שריק בטיט וכה"ג אית ליה הכירא ולא אתי לחיתוי ולפ"ז הא דמשני הש"ס לר"א דמתני' מיירי בבשרא אגומרא פירוש שאין ניצלה תוך התנור אלא בחוץ על גבי גחלים ולאו דוקא אלא ה"ה אצל הגחלים וכתבו התוס' דה"ה אם מונחים תוך התנור אלא שפי התנור מגולה ואין לו כסוי כלל ג"כ אסור דניהו דר"א לא בעי בעז טיחת פה התנור בטיט מכל מקום עכ"פ כיסוי בעי דלא עדיף מגדי דבעי כיסוי דאל"כ ל"ל דקשה ליה זיקא כנ"ל ורמ"א בהג"ה פסק כשיטת רש"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The permission based on its being harmed by air was needed for goat meat, whose flesh is tough.</b> Since there is concern that one may stoke, Rav Yirmiyah of Difti permits it only when the mouth of the oven is sealed with clay. Then there is no concern that he will stoke, for he would first have to uncover the oven. Before opening it, the heat is concentrated, while opening it allows air to enter, cools the oven, and causes the meat to toughen, as the Shulchan Aruch explains. According to this, kid meat is permitted even when the oven is completely open and uncovered, because there is no concern that he will stoke: it does not require stoking, and if he does stoke it will scorch and be ruined. According to R' Ashi, who treats kid and goat meat alike and permits both in every case, the Mishnah's rule that one may not roast meat, etc., is explained as meat placed directly upon the coals. The Rambam explains that it lies upon the coals themselves. In such a case even kid meat is forbidden lest he stoke. Although stoking would scorch it, we see that he prefers rapid roasting despite scorching, for he placed it directly on the coals, where it will certainly scorch. This is the Rambam's approach and that of the Beit Yosef here. Rashi, however, holds that the statement that anything harmed by air will not be uncovered refers to the permission of kid meat. For this reason we are not concerned that he may stoke, because air harms it. Accordingly, kid meat too is permitted only when the oven is closed, for if it is open, why should we not be concerned that he may stoke when air is entering in any event? Goat meat, however, is not harmed by air and is forbidden even when the oven is closed, because we are concerned that he may uncover the oven and stoke. Therefore, according to Rav Yirmiyah of Difti, the mouth of the oven must be sealed with clay, which serves as a reminder and prevents him from stoking. According to this, when the Gemara answers R' Ashi that the Mishnah concerns meat on coals, it means that it is roasted outside the oven upon coals. This is not necessarily limited to direct placement on the coals; the same applies when it is near them. Tosafos write that the same law applies if the meat is inside the oven but the oven is completely uncovered, for although R' Ashi does not require a clay seal for goat meat, he at least requires a cover. It cannot be better than kid meat, which requires a cover; otherwise the reason that air harms it would be irrelevant. The Rama in his gloss rules like Rashi.

**** ENGLISH ****
Nevertheless, once it has roasted to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted according to the first view here, meaning the Rambam's view regarding goat meat, and according to the Rama, who follows Rashi, both kid and goat meat are permitted once they have reached ma'achal ben Derusai, even according to those who forbid at the beginning of siman 253, namely the Rif. The reason will be explained below. They interpret the Mishnah's rule that one may not roast meat, etc., as follows. The Rif is the stringent view at the beginning of siman 253, forbidding leaving food even after it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai because he holds that the Mishnah teaches leaving food and rules like the Sages who disagree with Chananya. Yet the Rif quotes the Mishnah that one may not roast meat, etc., unless it can roast before nightfall, and the Gemara explains that this means ma'achal ben Derusai. This proves that once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted. The Ran explains that the Sages agree in this case, because nothing separates the meat from the coals. If he stokes after it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, smoke rises into it and scorches and ruins it. Before it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, however, stoking does not ruin it, and therefore the Sages decreed. Where a pot separates the food from the fire, stoking after it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai does not harm it; on the contrary, it hastens the cooking. These are his words; see there. Accordingly, when the Gemara challenges R' Ashi from the Mishnah and answers that it concerns meat on coals, according to the Rama and Rashi this means an uncovered oven. Even so, once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted, necessarily for the Ran's reason, since nothing separates the meat from the fire. Although this answer was needed only according to R' Ashi, whereas according to Rav Yirmiyah of Difti the Mishnah could concern goat meat in an oven whose mouth was covered but not sealed with clay, nevertheless there is no basis to create a further dispute between them on this point. Still, Magen Avraham's proof works only according to the Ran's explanation at the beginning of the chapter Kirah. The Rosh, citing Rabbeinu Yonah, resolves the Rif differently. He explains that the Mishnah is needed even according to the Sages, to teach that if the food reached ma'achal ben Derusai before nightfall it may be left on a stove that is grufa u'ketumah. Thus the Mishnah accords with everyone: according to Chananya even if the stove was not swept or covered, and according to the Sages only if it was swept or covered before nightfall. According to this, for the stringent view of the Rif at the beginning of siman 253, we find permission for meat on coals only when it is left on a stove that is grufa u'ketumah, with no distinction whether something separates the meat from the fire. Perhaps Magen Avraham did not wish to be stringent to this extent, since the Rama wrote above in siman 253 that the custom follows the latter, lenient opinion there, that once food reaches ma'achal ben Derusai it may be left even when the stove is not grufa u'ketumah. The Rama writes here only that one who wishes to be stringent may follow the Rif in siman 253. It therefore suffices to be stringent according to the Rif as explained by the Ran.
