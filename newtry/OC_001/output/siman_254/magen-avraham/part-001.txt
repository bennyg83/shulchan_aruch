**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For it is uncovered.</b> This implies that even if it is in an oven, whenever it is not covered at all, it is forbidden, unlike the first view, which holds that kid meat is permitted. For they explain that which we learned, one may not roast, etc., unless it will be roasted while it is still day to the level of the food of Ben Derusai, as referring to meat on coals, meaning that it is uncovered. If so, once it reached the level of the food of Ben Derusai, at least it is permitted. This is unlike the first view, for which there is no permission at the level of the food of Ben Derusai unless it is placed directly on the coals, as written in seif 2. However, it appears from the Baal HaMaor that even according to the second view, this is specifically in their ovens, which were small, and they were concerned that it might become scorched, as stated in tractate Beitzah. But in our ovens, there is no permission for roasted meat at the level of the food of Ben Derusai according to those who prohibit in a pot in siman 253.

<b>של מים</b>. אף על גב דמים ראוין לשתו' חיין מ"מ אינן טובים כ"כ כפירות כמ"ש ס"ד או  י"ל דהכא מיירי דקא בעי ליה להדחה ואז צריכים להיות חמין ולפ"ז אסור ליתן מים לתוך הקדירות שבתנורים אף ע"ג דמתכוין שלא יבקע הקדירה מ"מ הא קבעי ליה להדחה וחיישי' שמא יחת' לכן צריך ליתנן מבע"י ועמ"ש ססי' רנ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Of water.</b> Although water is fit to be drunk raw, nevertheless it is not as good as fruit, as written in seif 4. Alternatively, one may say that here it is speaking where he needs it for rinsing, and then it must be hot. According to this, it is forbidden to put water into the pots that are in the ovens, even though he intends that the pot not crack, since he needs it for rinsing and we are concerned lest he stoke. Therefore he must put it in while it is still day. See what I wrote at the end of siman 253.
