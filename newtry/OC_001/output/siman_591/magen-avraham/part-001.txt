אף על פי שש"ץ מוציא בר"ה ויה"כ אפי' הבקי מ"מ אין כל אדם יכול לשמוע מתחלה ועד  סוף כמ"ש סי' קכ"ד (טור) וגם לפי פירש"י ותו' לא פטר אלא מי שהיה אנוס ולא הי' יכול לסדר תפלתו ולפי' הר"ן אינו מוציא אלא משני ברכות אבל ז' ברכות מחויב להתפלל כשאר י"ט לכן מוטב להתפלל כל א' בלחש כל הט' ברכות ועוד נ"ל דאפי' בדיעבד לא יצא בתפלת הש"ץ כיון שמפסיקים עכשיו בפיוטים לא יצא י"ח כלל עמ"ש סי' ס"ח ועסי' קכ"ד ס"י וסי' תקצ"ב ס"ב: <b>וא"א פסוקי כו'</b>. אפי' קודם הקרבנות א"א אותו [טור מנהגים לבוש סי' תקפ"ב]:
**** ENGLISH ****
Although the shaliach tzibbur discharges others on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, even one who is expert, nevertheless not every person can listen from beginning to end, as written in siman 124 [Tur]. Also, according to Rashi and Tosafos, he exempted only one who was under constraint and could not arrange his prayer. According to the explanation of the Ran, he discharges only from two blessings, but the seven blessings one is obligated to pray like the rest of Yom Tov. Therefore it is better for each person to pray silently all nine blessings. It also seems to me that even after the fact he did not fulfill his obligation with the shaliach tzibbur's prayer, since nowadays we interrupt with piyyutim; he has not fulfilled his obligation at all. See what I wrote in siman 68, and see siman 124, seif 10, and siman 592, seif 2. <b>And we do not say the verses, etc.</b> Even before the offerings, we do not say it [Tur, Minhagim, Levush, siman 582].

<b>ואומרי' ג"כ מוספי כו'</b>. אף על גב שהרב"י כתבו בסמוך ס"ג כתבו רמ"א כאן להודיענו דאף למנהגנו שאומרים הפסוקים יאמר מוספי דלא כב"ח שנדחק שלא לו' מוספי ע"ש, וכ"מ בר"ן שכ' שלא לומר פסוקי מוסף ר"ח משמע פסוקי ר"ה אומרים ואפ"ה כ' לו' את מוספי כו' ע"ש: במקום שהקהל מתפללין עם הש"ץ והש"ץ תוקע וטעה אסור לשוח ולגעור בו דתקיעות שעל סדר הברכות אין מעכבות כ"ש שהיחידים א"צ לשמוע על סדר הברכות ואף אם לא שמעו תקיעות דמיושב יחזרו ויתקעו אחר התפל' אותו סי' שטעה ודיו [רשב"א סי' רצ"ה]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And we also say 'the Mussaf offerings,' etc.</b> Although the Beis Yosef wrote this nearby in seif 3, the Rema wrote it here to inform us that even according to our custom, where we say the verses, one should say 'Mussaf offerings,' unlike the Bach, who was pressed not to say 'Mussaf offerings'; see there. This is also implied by the Ran, who wrote not to say the verses of the Mussaf of Rosh Chodesh, implying that the verses of Rosh Hashanah are said, and nevertheless he wrote to say 'the Mussaf offerings,' etc.; see there. In a place where the congregation prays together with the shaliach tzibbur and the shaliach tzibbur blows and errs, it is forbidden to speak and rebuke him, for the blasts according to the order of the blessings are not indispensable. All the more so, individuals need not hear them according to the order of the blessings. Even if they did not hear the sitting blasts, they should go back and blow after the prayer the set in which he erred, and that is sufficient [Rashba, siman 295].

<b>לזרע יעקב</b>. דאם או' לזרעו יהיה עשו בכלל טוע' שהרי שנינו האומר קונם מזרע אברהם מותר באומות שנא' כי ביצחק יקרא לך זרע עכ"ל ריב"ש וכ"כ המפרש בנדרים ר"ל דאמרי' ביצחק ולא כל יצחק וצריך עיון דהא מילתא אמר הקדוש ברוך הוא לאברהם שעשו לא יקרא  על שמו אבל נקרא על שם יצחק והנודר מזרע יצחק אסור בעשו וי"ל מדקאמר רחמנא ליצחק לזרעך אתן את כל הארצות האל ולעשו לא נתן ש"מ אין עשו בכלל כ"כ בירושלמי בנדרים, וקשה דהא נתן לעשו קיני קניזי קדמוני כמ"ש רש"י פרשת דברים וי"ל דאין זה ירושה ממש כמו בבני לוט ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For the seed of Yaakov.</b> For if one says 'for his seed,' Esav would be included. One who errs in this should know that we learned: one who says, 'Konam from the seed of Avraham,' is permitted to benefit from the nations, as it says, 'for through Yitzchak seed will be called yours,' end quote of the Rivash. So too wrote the commentator in Nedarim, meaning that we say 'through Yitzchak,' but not all of Yitzchak. This requires analysis, for the Holy One, blessed is He, said that statement to Avraham, that Esav would not be called by Avraham's name; but he is called by Yitzchak's name, and one who vows not to benefit from the seed of Yitzchak is forbidden to benefit from Esav. One may answer that since the Merciful One said to Yitzchak, 'To your seed I will give all these lands,' and He did not give them to Esav, we learn that Esav is not included; so it is written in the Yerushalmi in Nedarim. But this is difficult, for He gave Esav the Keini, Kenizi, and Kadmoni, as Rashi wrote in Parashas Devarim. One may answer that this was not an actual inheritance, as with the descendants of Lot; see there.

<b>לא יתפלל</b>. וה"ה שלא יתקע ביחיד עמ"ש סי' תקפ"ט ס"ו אבל שחרית מותר לו להתפלל כיון דאיכא צבורא דמצלי שחרית בכל מקומות לא נדחית תפלתו [עמ"ש סי' צ' ס"ט] אבל מוסף הואיל וזמנה כל היום יש שמתפללין אותה עליונים ותחתונים מה שיהיה לשנים רבות כגון ז' שני רעב כו' ע"ש: י"ל ואין לפר' עילום שמך ולא יאמר ואין פירו' לעילום שמך כי יש פי' לעילום שמו [תשב"ץ בשם מהר"מ]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should not pray.</b> The same applies that he should not blow shofar as an individual; see what I wrote in siman 589, seif 6. But he may pray Shacharis, since there is a congregation praying Shacharis in all places, and his prayer is not rejected [see what I wrote in siman 90, seif 9]. Mussaf, however, since its time is the whole day, some pray it in the morning and some delay it [Rashi]. If so, it appears that nowadays, when the custom throughout the dispersions of Yisrael is to pray Shacharis early, an individual should not pray Shacharis after the first hour, for certainly the congregation has already prayed. Although the shaliach tzibbur may still be saying piyyutim, this is not called the time when the congregation is praying, for even if they are praying Shacharis and he is praying Mussaf it is not called the time of the congregation, and all the more so piyyutim. If we would say that he should wait until after three hours, the time for Shema will already pass; and if he reads Shema at the end of the third hour and prays immediately, it is impossible to be precise. Therefore it seems to me that he should be very careful to pray in the first hour, and if he did not do so, he should pray in the second hour, for Rashi wrote that in the first three hours it is a time of anger, while Tosafos wrote that the anger is in the third hour; see there. If so, it implies that in the first and second hours it is permitted to pray, and this is also implied in Beis Yosef, who brought Tosafos. But the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch hold that he should not pray during all three hours, because it is difficult on Rashi's words: why did the Gemara need to say, 'If you say Torah, of which it is written truth,' etc.? Let it derive from the fact that it is a time of anger. Rather, necessarily the reason is not because of the anger, but because when a person prays and requests his needs, his deeds are examined; and since the Holy One, blessed is He, is engaged in Torah, He does not act beyond the letter of the law, and his prayer will be rejected. Therefore in a case like this one may rely on Rashi, as it seems to me. If you ask: do we not say at the end of siman 98 that only when one says, 'It is fitting that the Holy One, blessed is He, should fulfill my prayer,' are his sins mentioned, implying that when he prays simply, his sins are not mentioned? How then do we say here that perhaps his deeds will be examined? One may answer: there they certainly mention his sins, while here it is only a possibility. Rashi explained that the Mussaf prayers of the whole year are praise and narration of events, but on Rosh Hashanah, since he says Malchiyos, Zichronos, and Shofaros, the judgment is examined more, end quote. According to this, one must say that the Gemara's question, 'If so, in the morning too,' refers to Shacharis of the whole year, which is also a request for one's needs and is not praise and narration of events; one should likewise be concerned that the judgment is examined. It answers: since there is a congregation, etc. If so, one must be careful the entire year not to pray Shacharis alone in the first hour, for certainly the congregation does not pray at that hour nowadays in summer; and in winter the reverse is true, for after two hours all places have certainly prayed. This requires analysis for practical halacha. But Tosafos at the beginning of chapter 4 of Berachos wrote that the whole year it is permitted. Alternatively, one may say that Rashi wrote this according to the second answer, which answers: Torah, of which it is written truth, etc. We learn from there that although He is not engaged in judgment, nevertheless, since the person is praying, they examine his deeds; if so, the same would apply the whole year. But according to the first answer, specifically on Rosh Hashanah, when He is actually judging, they examine his deeds; on the rest of the days of the year He judges only for assignment or mere examination, and there is no concern at all, as it seems to me. If so, the Gemara properly asks, 'If so, in the morning too.' Sefer Chasidim, siman 763, wrote: since the Holy One, blessed is He, judges every day for three hours, and so too on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, what is the difference between them and other days? Rather, on Rosh Hashanah He judges the upper beings and lower beings regarding what will be for many years, such as seven years of famine, etc.; see there. One should say, 'and there is no explaining the concealment of Your Name,' and not say, 'and there is no explanation for the concealment of Your Name,' because there is an explanation for the concealment of His Name [Tashbetz in the name of Maharam].
