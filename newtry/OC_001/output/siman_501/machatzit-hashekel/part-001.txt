**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 2) And close, etc.</b> For even according to what was written in siman 324, se'if 7, etc.: regarding the dispute of R' Yehuda and R' Shimon concerning an animal that became a neveilah on Shabbos, that R' Yehuda forbids and R' Shimon permits, there is a dispute of Amoraim. One said that R' Shimon permits only when it was in danger from erev Shabbos, for he sits and waits when it will die, and it resembles leftover oil in a lamp that R' Shimon permits, for he sits and waits when his lamp will go out; but when it was sound from erev Shabbos, R' Shimon concedes that if it became a neveilah on Shabbos it is forbidden. And one said that even if it was sound from erev Shabbos, R' Shimon holds it is permitted. And we hold above in siman 324 like the one who says that even if it was sound from erev Shabbos it is permitted. And the aforementioned dispute is on Shabbos and in Beitza folio 27b. And if so, why did the Tur write "but according to R' Shimon it is permitted when weak," for according to us even when sound it is permitted according to R' Shimon?

**** ENGLISH ****
He sits and waits, hoping that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor will slaughter it, as Tosafos wrote in Chullin folio 14a on that which is taught: one who slaughters on Shabbos or Yom Kippur, even though he is liable to death, his slaughter is valid. And we say that the colleagues took it to say it is R' Yehuda, who holds of muktzeh, from its teaching Shabbos as similar to Yom Kippur: just as on Yom Kippur in any case it is forbidden today because of the fast, so on Shabbos it is forbidden on its day; and therefore the reason must be muktzeh, and it is R' Yehuda. And Tosafos raised a difficulty: for R' Shimon too permits only when one sits and waits, and there certainly he was not sitting and waiting that it be slaughtered with a Shabbos prohibition. And they answered: he sits and waits hoping that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor will slaughter it, and others see them. And Magen Avraham did not bring the conclusion of Tosafos' words, "and others see them," because Tosafos wrote that according to R' Shimon it is permitted on its day for human eating, and therefore it does not suffice that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor slaughter it, for their slaughter is a neveilah; therefore they concluded "and others see them," for then their slaughter is valid. But Magen Avraham came only to give a reason for a sound animal that became a neveilah on Shabbos, that it is permitted for dogs; for him it suffices that he waits that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor slaughter it, even if others do not see them, and nevertheless it will at least be fit for dogs.

The anonymous Talmud in Beitza folio 30b on that which is taught: one may not take wood from the sukkah on Yom Tov, and R' Shimon permits. And it poses against R' Shimon: but he is demolishing a tent; and Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak answers that it is dealing where it fell on Yom Tov, and R' Shimon according to his view that he does not hold of muktzeh. And it further raises: until now R' Shimon permitted only leftover oil in a lamp that went out, for he sits and waits when his lamp will go out; but here, does he wait for his sukkah to fall? And Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak answers: here we are dealing with a weak sukkah from erev Yom Tov, for then too he sits and waits when it will fall. And if you say that a beam has the law of an animal, and according to the one who holds that R' Shimon permits even a sound animal, likewise he permits even a sound beam, and likewise a sound sukkah; then the questioner's difficulty, "until now R' Shimon permitted only leftover oil," etc., goes only according to the one who says R' Shimon concedes regarding a sound animal that it is forbidden; but according to the one who says R' Shimon permits even a sound animal there is no difficulty and one need not establish it as a weak sukkah. And granted that the answerer established "here we are dealing with a weak sukkah" in order to settle also according to the one who says R' Shimon concedes regarding a sound one; but the questioner should have said, "this works for the one who says R' Shimon permits even a sound one, but according to the one who says," etc. Rather, necessarily regarding a sukkah and a sound beam, even according to the one who says R' Shimon permits even a sound animal, nevertheless regarding a sukkah and a beam he concedes that it is forbidden even according to R' Shimon, as Magen Avraham's view. And I saw in a new book called Chemdas Moshe that wrote against these words of Magen Avraham as follows: and with apology to his Torah honor, he did not fulfill his duty of study in this, for in Shabbos folio 156b after the Mishnah "one may cut up a neveilah" they said, and even R' Yehuda held the law is like R' Shimon. And it poses: did R' Yehuda say thus? But R' Yehuda said the law is like an anonymous Mishnah, and we learned, "one may not split," etc., "and not from a beam that broke on Yom Tov"; and it answers, etc. And according to Magen Avraham's words, what difficulty is there from a beam that broke, since in this even R' Shimon concedes that it is forbidden? But truth will show its way. And it seems to me that when he wrote this he forgot the Talmud at the beginning of Beitza, where we say there regarding Yom Tov that the Tanna taught anonymously like R' Yehuda, for we learned, "one may not split," etc., "and not from a beam that broke on Yom Tov." And Tosafos there, s.v. "Ein mevak'in," etc., raised a difficulty: and if you say, but R' Shimon permits only when one sits and waits? And one can say that here too, since it broke on Yom Tov, presumably it was weak from yesterday, and it is like one who sits and waits when it will break, etc.; end quote of Tosafos. And according to this they properly raised a difficulty there against R' Yochanan; and now Magen Avraham's words are a mistake; end quote. And see there his further words; with easy seeing one will refute them. And in my humble opinion he necessarily understood from Tosafos' words that they hold that even if the beam was sound to the eye that saw it as strong and sound, nevertheless afterward when it broke on Shabbos the matter is revealed retroactively that the seers erred and already from erev Shabbos it was weak. And in my humble opinion it is impossible to explain Tosafos' intent thus, for if so how can Tosafos say that thereby he sits and waits; is he a prophet that he should know it will break on Shabbos? And yet today from erev Shabbos it is weak and he was sitting and waiting, while it is completely sound to our eyes. And it is forced to say that sometimes even what appears sound today will break tomorrow, and therefore they erred in their seeing yesterday and thought it was sound while in truth it was weak; and therefore in every case, even when truly sound, he sits and waits lest it be weak and not noticeable and tomorrow it will break; that is forced. And also the language of Tosafos does not imply thus. Therefore in my humble opinion they intended one matter, and on the contrary from their words it appears as Magen Avraham wrote. For at first glance what difficulty do Tosafos have: Rav Nachman said this statement that on Yom Tov Rabbi taught anonymously like R' Yehuda. And perhaps Rav Nachman holds like the one who says R' Shimon permits even a sound animal, and therefore he properly inferred that the Mishnah "one may not split" comes like R' Yehuda, for according to R' Shimon in every case it is permitted. And if their difficulty was why R' Shimon in truth permits when sound, since he is not sitting and waiting, that difficulty does not depend on Rav Nachman; he should have raised it against the one who says that even when sound R' Shimon permits. Rather, necessarily they hold like Magen Avraham's view, that regarding a sound beam, according to all R' Shimon concedes that it is forbidden, for he is not sitting and waiting, which is not so regarding an animal, as Tosafos wrote in Chullin as above. And if so, they properly have a difficulty: from where does Rav Nachman know that the Mishnah is R' Yehuda; perhaps it deals with a sound one, for so is the anonymous language, and also similar to the opening clause "one may not split wood from beams arranged for building," which are certainly sound; and similarly R' Shimon concedes regarding a sound beam. And on this Tosafos answered that Rav Nachman holds that even though the Mishnah taught anonymously, nevertheless it deals with a weak one, for so is the natural course of the matter: if it broke on Yom Tov it was certainly weak from erev Yom Tov and appeared so to every eye, and presumably the Mishnah spoke of the usual case. And therefore he properly sits and waits. And what Tosafos wrote, "and it is like one who sits and waits," with a comparative kaf, means like one who sits and waits regarding a lamp; and the matters are simple in my humble opinion. And for those who forbid nolad, etc.: that is to say, that Magen Avraham needed proof that regarding a sound beam even according to R' Shimon it is forbidden even for us who hold regarding a sound animal it is also permitted: that is for the poskim who ruled like R' Shimon even regarding nolad. But according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 495, that even though we hold on Yom Tov like R' Shimon regarding muktzeh, nevertheless nolad one should forbid; if so one does not need the aforementioned words of Magen Avraham, for it is obvious regarding a sound beam that broke that it is forbidden, for it is nolad. And it appears to me that even if it was weak it is called nolad. And the Tur who wrote that weak is permitted according to R' Shimon is because R' Shimon himself holds that even nolad is permitted; which is not so for us, who do not hold like R' Shimon regarding nolad.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it has the form of a utensil and broke on Yom Tov.</b> That is to say: even though it already broke from erev Yom Tov, nevertheless since the form of a utensil remained upon it, even if it does not perform a use similar to its original work, such as fragments of a trough to cover the mouth of a barrel, and fragments of glass to cover the mouth of a flask, and on Yom Tov it broke completely so that it is no longer fit for anything, they are forbidden on Yom Tov, for it is nolad. And a utensil that was weak from erev Yom Tov and broke on Yom Tov is no less called nolad than fragments of utensils that were fit for some use from erev Yom Tov and broke completely on Yom Tov, which are nolad. And even though the Yerushalmi said its words on the Mishnah "one may not split wood," etc., "and not from a beam that broke on Yom Tov," which comes like R' Yehuda who holds of muktzeh, nevertheless Magen Avraham properly inferred that even for us who hold like R' Shimon regarding muktzeh, nevertheless such a case is forbidden because of nolad according to us. For if you would think it is not called nolad, since it was weak from yesterday and is already as if broken into fragments that have no form of a utensil at all from yesterday, then according to that it would also not be muktzeh, for it is already completely broken from yesterday and permitted even according to R' Yehuda.
