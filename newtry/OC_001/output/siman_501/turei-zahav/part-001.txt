<b>And even to break them by hand, etc.</b> So the Tur wrote, that from the language of the Rosh it appears that it is forbidden; and Beis Yosef wrote that he learned it from the Rosh's writing "one should not split them," since one can cook and bake with them without splitting, so it is unnecessary exertion, and it is an av melacha, like that of one who shreds shreddings, who is liable on account of grinding, etc. And our master holds that even by hand one is liable on account of grinding. And Maharshal disagrees with this proof and said: on the contrary, since the Rosh hung the reason on its being an av melacha, it implies that by hand it is permitted, for there is no grinding except with a vessel; and even pepper, regarding which grinding applies, you will not find a prohibition by hand but only with a vessel. Therefore it appears that it is permitted according to all opinions; until here his words in chapter HaMevi. And it appears to me that the Tur learned it from the first reason that the Rosh wrote, that it is unnecessary exertion; in this there is reason to forbid even by hand. And this fits the language of the Rosh, for one may scrutinize why he needed to write that it is unnecessary exertion, which is rabbinic, and then conclude upon it "and it is an av melacha," for among two hundred there is a maneh; and if he meant to say in the manner of "not only this but also that," he should have said "and not only that, but it even involves an av melacha." Rather, it appears that his intent with these two reasons is that even in a place where one cannot say it is an av melacha, namely where one is not splitting into very fine pieces and it is unlike that of shredding shreddings (meaning fine wood to kindle the fire), which is an av melacha on account of grinding, and that is only with very fine wood in the manner of kindling a fire, which is not so here where one does not split into very fine pieces, for it is not the way to cook with such fine pieces, but only that they not be very thick, in the manner of placing on the fire after the fire has been kindled, to cook the pot, and there grinding does not apply; therefore he wrote that it is forbidden at least on account of exertion. And if so, he included in this two matters: if one splits into somewhat fine pieces there is unnecessary exertion, and if very fine there is an av melacha. And this is proper and reasonable to include in the Rosh's words; and if so one should forbid even by hand on account of the first reason. And thereby I am settled regarding what one may say from that of Rav Yehuda who strips bark into strips; and that is the law the Tur wrote in siman 519, that it is permitted to snap spice wood, and likewise on Shabbos in siman 322 that it is permitted to snap even with a knife straw to pick between one's teeth, for there it is for a need, which is not so here where we consider it unnecessary exertion. And according to this I am also settled regarding what the Tur wrote, that from Rashi it appears that it is permitted to break by hand; and Beis Yosef wrote that he did not know from where [the Tur] learned this from Rashi's words, and also Rashi weighed this and it did not appear to him from Rashi to forbid. And according to me it is fine: the main prohibition according to the Rosh is on account of exertion, and Rashi explained explicitly that according to the questioner the reason was exertion, but according to the conclusion we do not forbid on account of exertion but on account of muktzeh; and if so there is no prohibition by hand in something that is not muktzeh, namely what broke from erev Yom Tov. This appears to me in the Tur's words; and it is proper to be concerned in practice to forbid breaking even by hand in a place where it is forbidden with a knife. And Maharshal wrote: and the meticulous were accustomed to forbid even on Chol HaMoed, and they prepare all the small wood needed for cooking fish before Yom Tov, for they are concerned about exertion, since one can burn it thus without splitting, and one should not be lenient; but in a place where wood is expensive they were not concerned about exertion, for there is great loss; end quote. And it appears that to break by hand one should not be stringent at all on Chol HaMoed. <b>He wrote</b> the Ran: and they raise a difficulty here, why did they permit to split by means of a change, for these are preparations that one could do from erev Yom Tov; and Raavad answered that since one cannot cook without wood, they treated it like pounding salt, which they permitted by means of a change. And it appears to me that whatever one benefits from the body of the thing itself is like ochel nefesh itself, and it is unlike preparations from which one does not benefit, such as a knife or a spit, but only from what was prepared by their means; which is not so regarding wood, from which one benefits in themselves, for they make a fire and one warms oneself opposite them. And since splitting them to make a fire is permitted even though it was possible from yesterday, even to cook with them is permitted by way of "since" (mitoch). However, since people split for making vessels, they permitted only by means of a change; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote that Mahari Abuhav raised a difficulty: for warming by a fire was permitted only by way of "since," that burning was permitted for the need of ochel nefesh; where then do we say "since burning was permitted for a fire, we permit splitting wood," and likewise permit them to cook, given that the fire was permitted for warming only by way of "since" cooking was permitted; end quote. And it appears to me that the Ran himself holds that in truth there is no permission for warming by way of "since," etc., but that it itself is ochel nefesh, as he wrote here. And that the Ran wrote, brought by Beis Yosef in siman 511, that these two matters, namely the benefits of warming by a fire and hot water for his feet, are by force of "since": he wrote thus even according to Raavad, who here does not hold that warming is like ochel nefesh itself; but according to the Ran's own reason here, one needs the reason of "since" only regarding hot water for the feet, not regarding warming opposite [the fire].

<b>אין מביאין עצים כו'. </b> זה דברי רמב"ם ובמשנה איתא ר"א אומר נוטל אדם קיסם משלפניו (לפי' הרמב"ם היינו בסמוך לו) לחצוץ בו שיניו ומגבב מן החצר ומדליק שכל מה שבחצר מוכן הוא וחכ"א מגבב משלפניו ומדליק. וברייתא איתא ת"ר מגבב מן החצר ומדליק שכל מה שבחצר מוכן הוא ובלבד שלא יעשה ציבורין ציבורין ור"ש מתיר במאי קמפלגי ת"ק סבר מיחזי כמאן דמכין למחר ור"ש ס"ל קדירתו מוכיח עליו ופסק הרמב"ם כחכמים דמתניתין ויש כאן חומרא וקולא חומרא דאפי' בחצר אין היתר אלא מלפניו דהיינו בסמוך לו דלא כחכמים דברייתא וקולא מדלא הזכירו חכמים דמתני' בפי' חצר ש"מ אפי' בשדה מותר משלפניו אם מדליק שם אבל הטור משמע ליה דפוסק כת"ק דברייתא ויש חילוק בין חצר לשדה דבחצר מותר מכל החצר רק שלא יעשה צבורין אבל מן השדה בכל גווני אסור ולא מחלק הטור בחצר בין עצים דקים לגסים דאע"ג דצריכים לחלק כן כי היכי דלא תקשה חכמים דמתני' דלא התירו אלא מלפניו והיינו אפי' בחצר על חכמים דברייתא  דס"ל כל מה שבחצר אלא ודאי צריך לחלק דחכמים דמתני' מיירי מדקים דבטילי אצל הקרקע ובברייתא איירי מגסים וכן חילק הר"ן מ"מ לא ס"ל להטור כן אלא אין חילוק בין דקים לגסים לחכמים דברייתא דכל שבחצר מוכן הוא כמו ר"א במשנה ופליגי חכמים דברייתא על חכמים דמשנה ולא ס"ל כל שבחצר מוכן ומ"ה פסק הטור דאסור לעשות צבורין והיינו בין גסים בין דקים כחכמים דברייתא ובזה מתורץ תמיהת ב"י על הטור על שלא הביא חילוק דדקים וכן על שלא הביא הרמב"ם ורי"ף הדין שלא יעשה ציבורין דזה שייך בהבאה ממקום למקום אבל בנוטל משלפניו במקום שמדליק אין שייך כלל כן ע"כ לא הביאו זה דלדידהו אין היתר רק משלפניו:

<b>One may not bring wood, etc.</b> These are the words of the Rambam; and in the Mishnah it is stated: R' Eliezer says, a person may take a twig from in front of him (according to the Rambam's explanation, that is, next to him) to pick between his teeth, and may gather from the courtyard and kindle, for everything in the courtyard is prepared; and the Sages say he gathers from in front of him and kindles. And in a beraisa it is stated: Our Rabbis taught: he gathers from the courtyard and kindles, for everything in the courtyard is prepared, provided he does not make piles upon piles; and R' Shimon permits. In what do they argue? The first Tanna holds that it appears as one who prepares for tomorrow, and R' Shimon holds that his pot proves about him. And the Rambam ruled like the Sages of the Mishnah; and here there is a stringency and a leniency. A stringency: that even in a courtyard there is no permission except from in front of him, that is, next to him, not like the Sages of the beraisa. And a leniency: that since the Sages of the Mishnah did not mention a courtyard explicitly, it is implied that even in a field it is permitted from in front of him if he kindles there. But the Tur implies that he rules like the first Tanna of the beraisa, and there is a distinction between a courtyard and a field: in a courtyard it is permitted from the whole courtyard, provided he does not make piles, but from the field in all ways it is forbidden. And the Tur does not distinguish in a courtyard between fine wood and coarse wood; even though one needs to distinguish thus so that the Sages of the Mishnah, who permitted only from in front of him (and that even in a courtyard), not contradict the Sages of the beraisa who hold everything in the courtyard [is prepared]. Rather, certainly one must distinguish: the Sages of the Mishnah deal with fine wood that are nullified to the ground, and in the beraisa they deal with coarse wood; and so the Ran distinguished. Nevertheless the Tur does not hold so; rather there is no distinction between fine and coarse according to the Sages of the beraisa, that everything in the courtyard is prepared, like R' Eliezer in the Mishnah; and the Sages of the beraisa disagree with the Sages of the Mishnah and do not hold that everything in the courtyard is prepared. And therefore the Tur ruled that it is forbidden to make piles, and that is both coarse and fine, like the Sages of the beraisa. And thereby Beis Yosef's wonder at the Tur is answered, that he did not bring the distinction of fine wood, and likewise that the Rambam and Rif did not bring the law that one should not make piles: for that applies when bringing from place to place, but when taking from in front of him in the place where he kindles, it does not apply at all; therefore they did not bring this, for according to them there is no permission except from in front of him.

<b>ולפידים שנשארו כו'. </b> ב"י הביא דברי הרא"ש דמתיר בזה דאין בזה הכנה וכ"כ המרדכי בשם ר"י דכיון דגוף של הפתילה ועץ של המדור' הוא בעולם ואין כאן אלא תיקון הוה כמו אפיה ובישול ובשם בעה"ת כתב הרא"ש לאסור משום הכנה שיותר טוב להדליק כשהודלקו כבר וכבו הלכך בשבת וי"ט וב' י"ט של ר"ה אסור  להדליק בליל י"ט שכבו בראשונה אם לא שנדלקו ונכבו בחול וכן הלפידים שכבו בראשון אם לא שירבה עליה עצים אחרים ויבטלם ברוב ובלבד שלא יגע בהם אבל יכול להפוך בהם אחר שנתבטלו ברוב עכ"ל והקשה ב"י ע"ז דלפ"ד ה"ל למיסר בי"ט של ר"ה שיורי פתילה ולפידים שכבו בו ביום בין שתאמר שהאיסור בי"ט שני משום קדוש' א' להם בין שתאמר משום הכנה כשם שביצה שנולדה בו ביום אסור וכן ה"ל למיסר להדליק בכל י"ט הפתילו' שכבו בו ביום וצ"ע עכ"ל ותמהתי מאוד על גברא רבא דכוותיה דהשאיר קושיא זו בצ"ע דלפע"ד אין לה התחלה דודאי האיסור משום הכנה כמ"ש בעה"ת דאין שבת מכין לי"ט כן שני ימים של ר"ה למ"ד שני קדושות הן אין מכין מקדושה א' לחברת' אבל לבו ביום עצמו פשיטא דמכין מי"ט לאותו יום עצמו דאין זה בכלל איסור הכנה ול"ד לביצה דאסורה בו ביום מטעם דאיתא פ"ק דביצה אליבא דרבה דאסור משום הכנה ביצה שנולדה בי"ט דהיינו בי"ט שחל אחר השבת דאמרי' דכל ביצה שנולדה היום נגמרה מאתמול ממילא שבת מכין לי"ט ופרכינן שם א"כ י"ט בעלמא תשתרי ומשני גזירה משום י"ט שאחר השבת פי' אם נתיר בנולד בי"ט בעלמא יבוא להתיר ג"כ במה שנולד בי"ט שאחר השבת והכל בנולד בי"ט וכאן בלפידים ופתילה אין שייך גזירה דבאמת מותר אפי' בי"ט שאחר השבת אם כבה היום דההכנה היא היום ולא מאתמול ולא גזרי' מה שנולד בי"ט אטו נולד בשבת שלפניו וכעין זה כתבתי בסי' תקי"ב סוף הסימן גבי אפרוח. זה נראה פשוט וברור ובלי עיון כתב ב"י צ"ע. ולענין הלכה פסק רמ"א לקולא וכ"כ המרדכי שהביא ב"י שנוהגים שמדליקים שיעור פתילה ושמן שדלקו בי"ט של ר"ה להדליק בליל ב' ובת"ה מראה פנים לאיסור להדליק אפי' בצד השני שלא הדליק עדיין מטעם גזירה שמא ידלק בצד השני כמ"ש גבי שחיטה בסכין שצד אחד פגום ורש"ל החליט לאיסור בזה וכן מו"ח ז"ל וב"י בסי' תקי"ד פסק להיתר ובשני י"ט של גליות מותר לכ"ע להדליק בליל ב' מה שכבה ביום הראשון רק בי"ט שאחר שבת ור"ה דאין הטעם משום ספק בזה פליגי הפוסקים דלעיל ובודאי ראוי לכל א' לצאת ידי הכל ולעשו' פתילות חדשות ויכול לטלטלן בליל שני להפתיל' הישנה ואע"פ שהמרדכי בשם ראבי"ה אוסר גם הטלטול בהא ודאי נסמוך על המקילים כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And torches that remained, etc.</b> Beis Yosef brought the words of the Rosh who permits this, for there is no preparation in this; and so the Mordechai wrote in the name of R' Yitzchak, that since the body of the wick and the wood of the fire already exist in the world and there is here only a repair, it is like baking and cooking. And in the name of Ba'al HaItur the Rosh wrote to forbid on account of preparation, for it is better to light when they were already lit and went out; therefore on Shabbos and Yom Tov and the two days of Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden to light on the night of Yom Tov what went out on the first, unless they were lit and went out on a weekday; and likewise the torches that went out on the first, unless he adds other wood upon them and nullifies them in the majority, provided he does not touch them, but he may turn them after they were nullified in the majority; end quote. And Beis Yosef raised a difficulty on this: according to his words he should have forbidden on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah leftover wick and torches that went out on that very day, whether you say the prohibition on the second day is because they have one sanctity, or whether you say it is because of preparation, just as an egg laid on that day is forbidden; and likewise he should have forbidden lighting on every Yom Tov the wicks that went out on that day; and this requires study; end quote. And I wondered greatly at a great man like him that he left this difficulty as requiring study, for in my humble opinion it has no beginning: for certainly the prohibition is on account of preparation, as Ba'al HaItur wrote, that Shabbos may not prepare for Yom Tov; likewise the two days of Rosh Hashanah, according to the one who holds they are two sanctities, one may not prepare from one sanctity for its fellow; but for that very day itself it is obvious that one prepares from Yom Tov for that same day itself, for this is not within the prohibition of preparation. And it is unlike an egg, which is forbidden on that day, for the reason stated in the first chapter of Beitza according to Rabbah, that an egg laid on Yom Tov is forbidden on account of preparation, namely on Yom Tov that falls after Shabbos, for we say that every egg laid today was finished from yesterday, so of its own accord Shabbos prepares for Yom Tov; and we raise there: if so, ordinary Yom Tov should be permitted; and it answers: a decree on account of Yom Tov after Shabbos, meaning if we permit what was laid on ordinary Yom Tov they will come to permit also what was laid on Yom Tov after Shabbos; and all this is regarding what was laid on Yom Tov. But here with torches and a wick, a decree does not apply, for in truth it is permitted even on Yom Tov after Shabbos if it went out today, for the preparation is today and not from yesterday; and we do not decree what was "born" on Yom Tov on account of what was "born" on the Shabbos before it. And something like this I wrote in siman 512 at the end of the siman regarding a chick. This appears simple and clear; and without examination Beis Yosef wrote that it requires study. And regarding practical law the Rama ruled leniently; and so the Mordechai that Beis Yosef brought wrote that the custom is that they light a measure of wick and oil that burned on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah to light on the second night. And in Terumas HaDeshen he shows reason to forbid lighting even on the other side that has not yet been lit, on account of a decree lest one light on the other side, as written regarding slaughter with a knife of which one side is notched; and Maharshal decided to forbid in this, and so my master and father-in-law of blessed memory; and Beis Yosef in siman 514 ruled to permit. And on the two days of diaspora Yom Tov it is permitted according to all to light on the second night what went out on the first day; only on Yom Tov after Shabbos and Rosh Hashanah, where the reason is not doubt, do the aforementioned poskim disagree. And certainly it is proper for each person to fulfill all views and make new wicks; and he may move them on the second night toward the old wick; and even though the Mordechai in the name of Ravyah forbids even the moving, in this we certainly rely on those who are lenient, as it appears to my humble opinion.
