<b>וקרובה להשבר</b>. זהו אליבא דר"י אבל לר"ש מותר ברעוע' (טור) ונ"ל דאפי' לפי מ"ש סי' שכ"ד ס"ז דבהמה בריאה שמתה מותר לר"ש היינו משום דמ"מ יושב ומצפה שמא ישחטנו חש"ו כמ"ש התו' בחולין (וערסי' שי"ח) אבל קורה בריאה שנשברה לכ"ע אסור דהא קפריך סתמא דתלמודא דף ל' ולא קאמר הניחא למ"ד כמ"ש דף כ"ו אלא ע"כ כ"ע מודים בזה דאסור ולהאוסרים נולד בי"ט כמ"ש סי' תצ"ה פשיטא דאסור דהא נולד הוא וכן כל שברי כלים עסי' ש"ח ס"ו: ונ"ל דאפי' היתה רעועה מקרי נולד דמעיקרא כלי והשתא שבר כלי וכ"כ הרב"י בשם הירושלמי אפי' כלי שנשברה מערב יום טוב ויש בו תואר כלי ונשבר בי"ט  אסור, ומיהו בירושלמי דפוס קראקא הביא אם יש עליו תואר כלי אפי' נשבר בי"ט מותר להסיקו וכ"כ הב"י עצמו ססי' זה ומיירי שעושין עדיין מעין מלאכתן ראשונ' כגון שברי עריבה לצוק לתוכן מקפה שברי זכוכית לצוק לתוכן שמן כדאיתא בגמ', ונראה לי דבכלי אפילו היה שלם אתמול ונשבר ביום טוב שרי לר"ש דכלי עשוי להשבר משא"כ בקורה וכ"מ בגמרא דף ל"ב דפריך שתי הברייתו' אהדדי ולא קאמר כאן שהיה בריא מעי"ט כאן שהיה רעועה אלא ע"כ בכלי אין חילוק וכמ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And close to breaking.</b> This is according to R' Yehuda; but according to R' Shimon it is permitted when weak (Tur). And it appears to me that even according to what I wrote in siman 324, se'if 7, that a sound animal that died is permitted according to R' Shimon, that is because nevertheless he sits and waits, hoping that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor will slaughter it, as Tosafos wrote in Chullin (and see siman 318); but a sound beam that broke is forbidden according to all, for the anonymous Talmud on folio 30 raises a difficulty and does not say "this works for the one who holds," as it wrote on folio 26; rather, necessarily all agree here that it is forbidden. And for those who forbid nolad on Yom Tov, as written in siman 495, it is obvious that it is forbidden, for it is nolad; and likewise all broken utensils; see siman 308, se'if 6. And it appears to me that even if it was weak it is called nolad, for originally it was a utensil and now it is a broken utensil; and so Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: even a utensil that broke from erev Yom Tov and still has the form of a utensil, and then broke on Yom Tov, is forbidden. However, in the Yerushalmi of the Krakow printing it brought: if it still has the form of a utensil, even if it broke on Yom Tov it is permitted to kindle with it; and so Beis Yosef himself wrote at the end of this siman; and it is dealing where they still perform a function similar to their original use, such as fragments of a trough to pour porridge into them, or fragments of glass to pour oil into them, as stated in the Gemara. And it appears to me that regarding a utensil, even if it was whole yesterday and broke on Yom Tov, it is permitted according to R' Shimon, for a utensil is made to break, which is not so regarding a beam; and so it is implied in the Gemara on folio 32, where it poses a contradiction between the two beraisos against each other, and it does not say "here where it was sound from erev Yom Tov, here where it was weak," but rather necessarily regarding a utensil there is no distinction, as I wrote.

<b>גדולות</b>. ואף על גב דהוי מכשירין שאפשר לעשותן מעי"ט שרי דלאו מלאכה היא אלא עובדא דחול אבל דקות אסור דחייב משום טוחן כדאמרי' פ"ז דשבת (מרדכי וכ"מ ברא"ש ערסי' תקי"ט) והר"ן כתב דהוי כאוכל נפש עצמו דהא ראוי להתחמם בהם ומתוך דשרי לבקען לצורך להתחמם בהם שרי נמי לבקען לבשל בהם ומיהו כיון שרגילין לבקעם לעשות מהם כלי צריך שינוי עכ"ל והקשה מהרי"א הא מדורה להתחמם לא הותרה אלא משום מתוך כמ"ש הר"ן גופא ש"מ דלאו אוכל נפש הוא [ב"י] ולא ידעתי מאי קא קשיא ליה דהא הר"ן כתב דהוי כאוכל נפש, כלו' דבאמת מדורה להתחמם לאו אוכל נפש הוא אלא שהותרה משום מתוך וא"כ כיון שמדורה מותרת מותר לבקע עצים למדורה כיון שנהנה מהעצים עצמן ול"ד למכשירין כגון סכין ושפוד שאין נהנה מהן עצמן רק ממה שנעשה על ידיהן כ"כ הר"ן בהדיא וכיון שהותרה בקיעה לצורך חימום מותר נמי לצורך בישול כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Large ones.</b> And even though these are preparations that one could make from erev Yom Tov, it is permitted, for it is not a melacha but only weekday labor; but fine ones are forbidden, for one is liable on account of grinding, as we say in chapter 7 of Shabbos (Mordechai; and so it is implied in the Rosh; see siman 519). And the Ran wrote that it is like ochel nefesh itself, for one may warm oneself with them; and since splitting them is permitted for warming oneself with them, it is likewise permitted to split them to cook with them. However, since people commonly split them to make vessels from them, a change is needed; end quote. And Mahari Abuhav raised a difficulty: for a fire to warm oneself was permitted only by way of "since" (mitoch), as the Ran himself wrote, which implies that it is not ochel nefesh (Beis Yosef). And I do not know what difficulty he has, for the Ran wrote that it is like ochel nefesh, meaning that in truth a fire to warm oneself is not ochel nefesh, but it was permitted by way of "since"; and if so, since a fire is permitted, it is permitted to split wood for a fire, because one benefits from the wood itself, and it is unlike preparations such as a knife or a spit, from which one does not benefit in themselves but only from what is done by their means, as the Ran wrote explicitly. And since splitting was permitted for the need of warming, it is likewise permitted for the need of cooking, as it appears to me.

<b>שלמים</b>. הרא"ם בס' יראים דייק מזה דאפי' סתירה גמורה לא שייך בכלים ולדידן דקי"ל דיש סתירה גמורה בכלי' (כמ"ש סי' שי"ד) צ"ל מיירי בכלי רפוי (ט"א) וצ"ע דאע"ג דמותר לשמוט כלי רפוי מ"מ אסור לשברו לגמרי ועוד דעכו"ם שחקק קב בבקעת לאו רפוי הוא וא"כ למה לי טעמא דנולד ול"נ דקושיא מעיקרא ליכ' דהבער' גופה אב מלאכ' היא והותרה לצורך ה"ה סתירה ונהי דאסור לבקע כלי לצורך אוכל נפש היינו משום דמלאכ' בפ"ע היא ואפש' דמות' לבקעו כמו שהתירו ביקוע עצים בס"א דהא סותר אינו חייב אלא א"כ עושה לבנות ואם כן לצורך שרי לגמרי כנ"ל ועל כל פנים צ"ל שאין לו עצים דאם לא כן עבר על בל תשחית כדאיתא בשבת דף קכ"ט, עץ שמחתין בו האש שנשבר אסור להסיקו דה"ל נולד (יש"ש גמרא) ודוקא שהי' מיוחד לכך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whole ones.</b> The Ra'em in Sefer Yereim inferred from this that even absolute demolishing does not apply to utensils; but for us, who hold that there is absolute demolishing regarding utensils (as written in siman 314), one must say it is dealing with a loosely joined utensil (Turei Even); and this requires study, for even though it is permitted to remove a loosely joined utensil, nevertheless it is forbidden to break it completely. And further, a non-Jew who carved a kav into a log is not a loosely joined case; so why do we need the reason of nolad? And it appears to me that there is no difficulty from the outset, for kindling itself is an av melacha and was permitted for a need; the same applies to demolishing. And granted that it is forbidden to split a utensil for the need of ochel nefesh, that is because it is a melacha in its own right; and it is possible that it is permitted to split it just as they permitted splitting wood in se'if 1, for one who demolishes is not liable unless he does so in order to build; and if so, for a need it is entirely permitted, as it appears to me. And in any case one must say that he has no wood, for otherwise he transgresses "do not destroy," as stated in Shabbos folio 129. A stick with which one stirs the fire that broke: it is forbidden to kindle with it, for it is nolad (Yam Shel Shlomo, Gemara); and specifically where it was designated for that purpose.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Torches.</b> For preparation does not apply, since the body [of the wick or wood] already existed in the world, and it is like baking and cooking, which are not called preparation (Mordechai). And it appears to me that even according to what I wrote in siman 527, that preparation applies regarding cooking, that means it is forbidden to cook from one Yom Tov for its fellow; but if one already cooked, it is not forbidden in benefit, for preparation is not called [forbidden preparation] except regarding an egg that was not in the world at all. And Yam Shel Shlomo ruled that torches are forbidden, and the same applies to wicks that went out; and he wrote in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 81, that if it was lit on Shabbos and went out, it is forbidden to move it on Yom Tov even for the need of its body or its place; even though a wick is a utensil, nevertheless it is considered as if he actively set it aside by hand from the Yom Tov that follows Shabbos, on account of preparation; until here. And it appears to me that this is not called actively setting aside by hand except on Shabbos, when it is forbidden to move a burning lamp; but on Yom Tov it is not called actively setting aside by hand; end quote, Yam Shel Shlomo. And so the Bach wrote. And regarding what Beis Yosef wrote, that if it is considered preparation, then even on that very day it should be forbidden to light it, like an egg that was laid: he answered in Darkei Moshe that since it is an inferior preparation, it is not forbidden on that very day; end quote. This implies that he holds it is not Torah-level preparation. And it appears to me that from the outset there is no difficulty, for according to the strict law even an egg laid on Yom Tov is permitted, except that we decree because sometimes Yom Tov falls on Sunday and they would also permit the egg and would not know to distinguish that it was finished from yesterday; but here there is no place to decree that if they come to light those that went out today they will also light those that went out yesterday on Shabbos, for people do not err between Yom Tov and Shabbos. For if you do not say so, regarding wood that fell on Yom Tov, why do we need the reason "lest one go up and detach"; we should have said the reason is a decree regarding Yom Tov after Shabbos (see siman 507, se'if 2)? Rather, necessarily it is as I wrote. And it is written in Terumas HaDeshen that on Yom Tov after Shabbos it is forbidden to light it even on the other side, for since it is Torah-level we decree lest one come to do so on the other side; but on the second day of Yom Tov that follows Shabbos, or on the second day of Rosh Hashanah, it is permitted to light it on the other side, and therefore it is permitted to move them; until here.
