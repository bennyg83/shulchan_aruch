There is an additional difficulty, for in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341, he ruled that even on Shabbos, if one wishes not to eat meat, he has permission to do so, as Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: he is not obligated to eat meat and wine, and so on; and what they forbade on a weekday is because if he is drawn after pleasures he will be distracted from burying the deceased. See siman 552, seif 10, that when he refrains because of mourning it is forbidden. If so, there is no positive commandment at all, even by day. Perhaps one can say that Purim is different, for joy is written regarding it, and there is no joy except with meat. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>The burial of his deceased comes first.</b> See what I wrote in siman 687, that there are those who disagree, and the custom is to bury him after leaving the beis haknesses. It is difficult: how is he obligated in reading the Megillah, since he is exempt from all mitzvos? One can answer that the reason he is exempt is so that he should be occupied with the needs of the deceased, but here it is forbidden to bury him until he first reads. If so, he is obligated even in davening and Krias Shema, similar to Pesach, where he is obligated even though he is an onen, as the Rosh wrote in the name of the Maharam. The Mordechai wrote that they even suspend the Avodah in order to hear the Megillah reading with the community, all the more so mourning; if so, the same applies to an onen. Furthermore, there is no prohibition for an onen to go to the beis haknesses, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he is exempt from davening and Krias Shema. It also appears to me that even though he heard the Megillah with the community, he should read it a second time after the deceased is buried, for it is an explicit Talmudic teaching that an onen is exempt from all mitzvos. Although we say that Megillah reading precedes, that is with regard to someone else; but the onen is exempt until he buries him. However, according to what I wrote in siman 71, that if he wishes to be stringent he is permitted, he fulfills his obligation with what he heard in the community, for the essential law is that Megillah reading precedes the deceased, and if so he is obligated in Megillah reading. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he should not put on tefillin, for they are called glory; and see the beginning of siman 71.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is permitted to marry.</b> For regarding the festival it is written, “and you shall rejoice in your festival,” and not with your wife, but this is not so regarding Purim. Also, Purim is one day, and because of one day a person does not delay himself; and this follows the one who says it is because of neglecting the mitzvah of being fruitful and multiplying, as cited by the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rashba. At first glance it implies that this is the explanation: granted, on a regel we decree lest he delay until the regel in order to profit by having the seven days of feasting fall within the seven days of the festival. But for Purim, because it is one day, he will not delay. This requires analysis, for the Gemara says that it is permitted to marry on Erev Yom Tov because, for one day, a person does not delay himself. Rashi and the Ran explain that since if they delay it further they cannot do it on the moed. If so, he will not schedule the marriage on Erev Yom Tov, and that does not apply to Purim. Perhaps the Rashba stated his own reasoning, but adopted the language of the Gemara. Nevertheless, it is difficult: from where does he know this? Perhaps his proof is from the fact that it is permitted to marry on Erev Shavuos, and we are not concerned that he will delay the marriage, because there he is not concerned if it is postponed until after Shavuos, since it is only one day. Rather, it must be that since it is only one day, he does not care to delay for this, for in any event he will still need to make the seven days of feasting. The Levush wrote: according to what I wrote in siman 688, seif 6, that one should not make the Purim meal on Shabbos, here too it is forbidden to make the wedding meal on the fourteenth. This is the end of his words. But it does not appear to me so, for the cases are not comparable. There the reason is that if so, the Purim meal will not be recognizable, but here what does it matter if the wedding meal is not recognizable? See what I wrote there. Rather, if there is a difficulty, this is the difficulty: we rule that one does not mix one joy with another joy, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 546, and we derive this from a verse. If so, how is it permitted to mix the joy of marriage with the joy of Purim? One may say that for this reason the Rashba concluded that these matters are Rabbinic and we follow the lenient view; that is, according to the opinion that one does not mix joys, it is indeed forbidden, but since according to the other view it is permitted, we follow the lenient view. If so, according to what I wrote at the beginning of siman 546, that we rule according to the one who says that one does not mix one joy with another joy, here too it is forbidden. Therefore it appears to me that he should make the chuppah on the thirteenth. So it appears to me.
