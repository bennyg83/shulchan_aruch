The Mechaber, who copied the words of the Rokeach, follows his view in seif 4 that mourning applies on Purim. But the Rema has already written above that we follow the authorities who say that mourning does not apply on Purim. According to this, even if he has a minyan in his house, he goes to the beis haknesses to hear the Megillah reading with the community. To leave the entrance of his house for optional matters, it implies from the Pri Megadim that one should be stringent. All of this is by day, for days of feasting and joy are written. But at night, if he has a minyan, he should daven and read the Megillah in his house; and if he does not have a minyan, he should daven in his house and go to hear the Megillah reading in the beis haknesses, but other than the reading he should not leave the entrance of his house. See the Derech HaChaim, that on the fifteenth day he should not go from his house to the beis haknesses even to daven Shacharis. Nevertheless, regarding removing shoes and sitting on the ground, it implies from the authorities that even on the fifteenth he need not do so. The Levush wrote: one does not recite Tzidduk HaDin or Kaddish except for a sage in his presence after they expound about him; and although one does not recite Tzidduk HaDin, they do say Kaddish.

This is similar to an onen on the night of the second day of Yom Tov, where many authorities obligated him in mitzvos for this reason. But there is room to question this. First, the night of the second day of Yom Tov itself is subject to differing opinions among the authorities. Furthermore, there at any rate it is Yom Tov, and therefore aninus does not take effect because it is not the way to bury him at night. This is unlike Purim, where the primary joy is by day, while at night it resembles an ordinary weekday night, when aninus certainly takes effect even though it is not the way to bury him then. Therefore the Derech HaChaim wrote that on the night of the fourteenth he should hear the Megillah reading from someone else.

<b>The burial of his deceased comes first, as was explained, and so on.</b> This means that there it is proven that it is not only a meis mitzvah that comes before Megillah reading, but the same applies to any deceased person who does not have enough people to bury him: one who encounters him buries him first and afterward reads. In truth, the Magen Avraham already wrote there that many authorities disagree with this and hold that with any deceased person, Megillah reading comes before his burial, and the custom is to bury him after leaving the beis haknesses. The Magen Avraham asked: this is understandable regarding other people, but how is the onen obligated in Megillah reading? Is he not exempt from all mitzvos, and how did he fulfill his obligation with what he heard in the community, when at that time he was not obligated at all? Therefore the Magen Avraham concludes that even though he heard the reading with the community, he should read it a second time after the deceased is buried. Regarding Krias Shema and davening, he is like any other onen on weekdays: before burial he is exempt, and after burial see above in siman 71, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 4, where all the details are explained. Regarding tefillin, the view of several later authorities is that he should not put them on, as above in siman 38, seif 5.
