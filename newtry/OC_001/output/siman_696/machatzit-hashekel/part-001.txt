<b>(ס"ק ב) אינו כו'</b> עיין ברמב"ם דמשמע כו' שכתב וז"ל ומותר בעשיית מלאכה ואעפ"כ אין ראוי לעשות בו מלאכה. אמרו חכמים כל העושה כו' א"ר סי' כו' משמע דקאי אדסמוך ליה ונתן טעם על מ"ש שאין רואה וגם לא הזכיר כלל שם ממנהג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 2) It is not, etc.</b> See the Rambam, who implies, etc., for he wrote as follows: It is permitted to do work, and nevertheless it is not proper to do work on it. The Sages said: anyone who does, etc. Eliyah Rabbah, siman, etc. This implies that it refers to what is adjacent to it, and gives a reason for what he wrote, that he does not see blessing; and he also did not mention there any custom at all.
**** END BLOCK ****

<b>אבל בב"י משמע כו'</b> שכתב דראיית הרמב"ם הוא מעובדא דרב דחזי להאי גברא דזרע פשתן בפורים. ולטיי' ולא צמח הפשתן הרי נראה דאינו רואה ס"ב. א"כ אין ראיה כ"א למקום שנהגו איסור דפריך הש"ס התם למאי דמסיק שם דאיסור מלאכה לא קבלו ומותר מצד הדין. א"כ אמאי קלל רב להאי גברא. ומשני משום דברים המותרים ואחרים נהגו בו איסור. א"כ ע"כ במקום דזרע האי גברא הי' מקו' שנהגו בו איסור ומקום שלא נהגו איסור אין ראיה שאינו רואה ס"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But in the Beit Yosef it implies, etc.</b> For he wrote that the Rambam's proof is from the incident of Rav, who saw a certain man planting flax on Purim, cursed him, and the flax did not grow. Thus it appears that he does not see a sign of blessing. If so, there is proof only for a place where they were accustomed to prohibit work. For the Talmud asks there according to what it concludes, that the prohibition of work was not accepted and is permitted according to the law: if so, why did Rav curse that man? It answers that it was because of matters that are permitted, but others practiced a prohibition regarding them. If so, necessarily the place where that man planted was a place where they had the custom to prohibit work; but regarding a place where they did not have the custom to prohibit work, there is no proof that he does not see a sign of blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 3) Siman, etc.</b> It does not sprout. If so, he loses even the seed, for the Beit Yosef wrote that the Rambam's proof is from this Gemara, where he planted flax, as above, and it did not grow. It implies there that he had another textual reading, for according to our reading it is proven that he lost the seed.
