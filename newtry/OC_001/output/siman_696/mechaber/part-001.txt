<b>The laws of eulogy, fasting, and doing work on Purim. It contains eight seifim:</b><br>On Purim, it is permitted to do work. **** ENGLISH ****
The fourteenth day and the fifteenth day are forbidden in eulogy and fasting for every person in every place, both for residents of walled cities, who observe only the fifteenth, and for residents of towns, who observe only the fourteenth. Women may wail on them, meaning that all answer together, and they may clap, striking palm against palm. But they may not lament, meaning that one woman speaks and all the others answer after her. Once the deceased has been buried, they may neither wail nor lament.

**** ENGLISH ****
All matters of mourning are observed on Chanukah and Purim. <small>Gloss: Nevertheless, he should go to the beis haknesses to hear the Megillah, as Tosafos writes in the first chapter of Moed Katan. If he can gather a minyan in his house to read the Megillah, that is preferable, as the Maharil writes. Some say that mourning does not apply on Purim, neither on the fourteenth nor on the fifteenth, as in the Rosh and Minhagim, and so is the custom. Even the mourning of the first day is deferred because of Purim, as appears to me. But matters done privately do apply. And even though mourning is not observed on Purim, it counts for him toward the seven days of mourning, like Shabbos; and the Rav himself ruled so in Tur Yoreh De'ah, siman 401.</small>

**** ENGLISH ****
It is permitted to marry a woman on Purim. <small>Gloss: This is both on the fourteenth and on the fifteenth. All the more so, it is permitted to make a pidyon haben, as Tosafos writes in the first chapter of Moed Katan. Regarding the custom to wear costumes on Purim, and for a man to wear a woman's garment and a woman men's clothing, there is no prohibition in the matter, since they intend only ordinary joy. The same applies to wearing Rabbinic kilayim. Some say it is forbidden, but the custom follows the first view. Likewise, people who snatch from one another in a joyous manner do not thereby violate “You shall not steal,” and this is the custom, provided that one does not do anything improper according to the leaders of the city, as in Responsa Mahari Mintz, siman 17.</small>
