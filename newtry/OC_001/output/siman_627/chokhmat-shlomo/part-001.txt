**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1</b> One who sleeps under a bed in a sukkah. Note: We read in the Talmud, Sukkah chapter 2, that one who sleeps under a bed in a sukkah has not fulfilled his obligation. Rabbi Yehudah said: it was our practice that we would sleep under the bed, etc. Rashi and the Bartenura explained that the reason is that a bed is a temporary ohel, for it is moved from place to place. They showed me the Tosafot Yom Tov there, who asked why Rabbi Yehudah does not disagree regarding kinofot above at the beginning of chapter 1, Mishnah 3, and he left it requiring examination. In my opinion this is understandable, for it resembles what is written in Shabbos regarding one who threw and it landed in a dog's mouth: he is liable, even though we require resting on an area of four, because his intention gives it the status of a significant place. This proves that treating something as significant can make it like an important place. But treating something as significant helps only where it is something people normally do; where people do not normally do so, his intention is nullified relative to everyone else, as written at the beginning of the third chapter of Pesachim regarding Babylonian kutach: leave Babylonian kutach aside; if he wants to eat it, his intent is nullified relative to everyone. Therefore it works well. With kinofot, since he treated it as an ohel and people normally do this, his intent is effective, and Rabbi Yehudah agrees that his thought gives it the status of an ohel. But this is only if he sleeps on the bed, which people normally do. One who sleeps under a bed does something people do not normally do, so his intent is nullified relative to everyone and it is only a temporary ohel, which does not nullify a fixed ohel. Someone asked me: it is explicit there that some say, and the Tosafot Yom Tov cites it in its place, that Rabbi Yehudah also disputes regarding a bridal canopy, and that is its normal manner; if so, why does he not dispute regarding kinofot? At first I thought to answer that with a bridal canopy, one can say he does not intend it as an ohel, only for privacy, like what is stated in Shulchan Aruch, Laws of Shabbos, siman 315, that where it is only for privacy there is no prohibition of ohel. So too here it is only for privacy, and there is no proof that he treated it as an ohel. But where he sleeps alone under the bed or under a kinof, this is not for privacy but for an ohel; therefore in that case there is a distinction between where this is the normal manner and where it is not. However, I returned and saw that it works better this way: the Mishnah in the first chapter explicitly says that he spread a sheet over a kinof, so it is proven that he chooses this and wants to make an ohel; therefore he treated it as significant. But with a bridal canopy, the canopy was already made and he is not now making it by hand; it is possible that he would have slept even without the canopy, and therefore treating it as significant does not apply, so Rabbi Yehudah disagrees. With a bed, although he places himself to sleep under the bed and we see that he chooses this ohel, nevertheless, since this is not the normal manner, his intent is nullified relative to everyone. This is understandable, with God's help. What I wrote above in siman 626:1 regarding if he lowered the branches below, see the source of that law in the Talmud, first chapter of Sukkah 9, in Rashi s.v. beshechavtan, who wrote that by Torah law it is nullified by majority. Regarding the question I was asked about the wording by Torah law, and what I answered about this, see the omissions to the Laws of Vows, siman 215, where I wrote about this incidentally to an insightful person in Lvov; see there, for that is its proper place.
