<b>(ס"ק א) אם כו'</b> ועיין סי' שט"ו ותראה שיש ט"ס בדבריו. ר"ל דהאגודה הביא ראיה מסי' שט"ו ושם כ' להיפך בסעיף י"א וז"ל שם והוא שלא תהי' משולשלת מעל המטה טפח. אולם הטעם מבואר שם כיון שהכילה פרוסה לצל הוי המטה עשוי' כעין גג באמצע המחיצה של הכילה. מה שמשולשל למטה הוי מחיצה. מכל מקום אין ענינו שייך לכאן. בשלמא לעיל סי' שט"ו דמיירי לענין איסור עשיית אהל בשבת א"ש דכה"ג אסור דהוי אהל. אבל הכא דמיירי לענין הישן שם דמיקרי אינו ישן בסכה דאהל מפסיק. א"כ מה בכך דתחת המטה נקרא אהל. הא הוא ישן ע"ג המטה ושם ליכא אהל מפסיק. ואי בישן תחת המטה בלא"ה אסור כדאי' ריש הסי':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if katan 1) If, etc.</b> See siman 315 and you will see that there is a scribal error in his words. Meaning, the Agudah brought a proof from siman 315, but there it is written the opposite in se'if 11, in this wording: provided it is not lowered from the bed one tefach. However, the reason is explained there: since the canopy is spread for shade, the bed is made like a roof in the middle of the canopy's partition. What is lowered below is a partition. Nevertheless, that matter is not relevant here. It works well above in siman 315, where the subject is the prohibition of making an ohel on Shabbos; in such a case it is forbidden because it is an ohel. But here, where the subject is one who sleeps there and whether he is called not sleeping in a sukkah because an ohel interrupts, what difference does it make that under the bed is called an ohel? He is sleeping on top of the bed, and there no ohel interrupts. And if he sleeps under the bed, that is forbidden in any case, as stated at the beginning of the siman.

<b>(ס"ק ג) והוא שלא כו'</b> נ"ל דהנך י"ט כו' וכ"מ בתוס' סוף דף כ"א אהא דתנן התם הסומך סוכתו בכרעי המטה. ר"ל שסיכך ע"ג מטה וקרקע המטה היא קרקע סכה וגם דופני המטה הם דופני הסכה כשרה ר' יהודה אומר אם <b>(Se'if katan 3) And this is provided that, etc.</b> It appears to me that these ten tefachim, etc.; so it also appears in Tosafot at the end of page 21, concerning the Mishnah there: one who supports his sukkah on the legs of the bed. This means that he placed s'chach on top of a bed, so that the bed floor is the sukkah floor and the bed walls are the sukkah walls. It is valid. Rabbi Yehudah says: if it cannot stand on its own, it is invalid. According to one opinion in the Talmud there, the reason is that it lacks fixedness. Tosafot explained, based on the Yerushalmi, that from the bed floor to the s'chach there are not ten tefachim. Tosafot asked: if so, why does Rabbi Yehudah validate where it can stand on its own, meaning that he placed s'chach on pegs standing in the ground? In any event, from the bed floor to the s'chach there are not ten tefachim. They answered that nevertheless, since if the bed were removed the s'chach would still remain resting on the pegs, we measure the ten tefachim from the ground, not from the bed floor. See there. However, I did not understand his words, for it is explicit in the Talmud to the contrary on 10b. The baraita there says: two naklitin, etc., which is our case here; if he spread it over naklitin it is valid, provided that the naklitin are not ten tefachim higher than the bed. Thus we measure the ten tefachim from the bed. This is also explicit in Rashi there. The words of Tosafot that he brought are, on the contrary, against him, for they imply the opposite. Here, where the naklitin are fixed in the bed and he spread it over the naklitin, it resembles placing s'chach on top of a bed and not on pegs, where Rabbi Yehudah requires ten tefachim from the bed floor. I have now seen that the Netiv Chayim was aroused to this, and on this basis rejected the Magen Avraham's words. In my humble opinion one can say that the Magen Avraham was precise in the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which changed from the Gemara's wording. In the Gemara it says: provided that the naklitin are not ten tefachim higher than the bed. In the Shulchan Aruch it says: provided that they are not ten high. This implies that it refers to the naklitin themselves, that they should not be ten high. The Magen Avraham was troubled: the Gemara's wording works well, that the naklitin should not be ten higher than the bed, implying that if they are not ten higher than the bed, even though the naklitin themselves are ten high, for example where the naklitin are fixed in their middle and part of the naklitin is below the bed, it is still permitted, since in any event they do not extend above the bed ten tefachim and do not have the law of an ohel. But the Shulchan Aruch wrote simply, provided they are not ten high, meaning the naklitin, even though they are not ten higher than the bed, as where they are fixed in the middle as above; nevertheless it has the name of an ohel. On this the Magen Avraham was troubled: why should it have the name of an ohel? Therefore the Magen Avraham was forced to say that each source took the usual case of its time. In the time of the Talmud, the naklitin were fixed in the bed and had the law of one who supported his sukkah on the legs of the bed, where it cannot stand on its own. Then we do not measure from the ground but from the bed floor, and therefore the baraita required that the naklitin be ten tefachim higher than the bed floor. But in the time of the Beit Yosef, the way was that the naklitin were not fixed in the bed but in the ground next to the bed. Then they have the law of a sukkah that can stand on its own, like one who placed s'chach on pegs, in which case the ten tefachim are measured from the ground. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch made the measure depend on the naklitin themselves, whether they are ten high, even if there are not ten from the bed floor. The Magen Avraham's words are thus understandable.
