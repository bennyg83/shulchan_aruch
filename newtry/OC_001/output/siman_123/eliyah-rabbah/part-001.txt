[ד] <b>יעמוד וכו'.</b> וחייב לכוין רגליו כמו בתפילה כנסת הגדולה בשם רד"ך, ומשמע קצת מזה דיש לעשות כל פסיעה בשני רגלים כמו שכתב בשם לחם חמודות, וסיים בכנסת הגדולה זה לשונו ועיין עוד שם ובתשובת בנימין זאב סימן קס"ז, עד כאן. ורצה לומר משאר דינים בענין זה אבל דין זה דחייב לכוין רגליו ליתא מידי בתשובת בנימין זאב עיין שם, ועולת תמיד עינו הטעתו וחשב דעל דין זה קאמר ולא דק:
**** ENGLISH ****
[4] <b>He should stand, etc.</b> He must align his feet as during the Amidah, Knesses HaGedolah writes in the name of Radach. This somewhat implies that each step should be completed with both feet, as he writes in the name of Lechem Chamudos. Knesses HaGedolah concludes, “See further there and in responsum Binyamin Ze’ev 167.” He means the other laws of this subject, for the requirement to align one’s feet does not appear in Binyamin Ze’ev. Olas Tamid was misled and thought that the reference applied to this law, but he was not precise.

[ה] <b>ולפחות וכו'.</b> רצה לומר לכתחילה הלכה עד קדושה אלא כשיש המקום צר ואתו לנצויי ודחוק יש להקל ולחזור כשיתחיל השליח ציבור, וכן פסק הב"ח, ולחם חמודות מחלק דכשאומרים פיוטים יחזור כשיתחיל השליח ציבור, ואם חוזר מיד למקומו קאמר בש"ס דומה לכלב ששב על קיאו ועיין בית יוסף לבוש פירושו, אבל בתשובת רשב"א סימן תל"ו ותרע"ה ותשובת בנימין זאב סימן קס"ז פירשו דכיון שחוזר מיד למקומו נראה כאילו רוצה להתוודות שנית כדאיתא לקמן בהלכות יום כיפור שהתודה ביום כיפור זה וכו' דומה לכלב וכו' ועיין בליקוטי מהרי"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>At least, etc.</b> This means that ideally one should remain until Kedushah. Where space is narrow, conflict may arise, and there is pressure, one may be lenient and return when the chazzan begins, as Bach rules. Lechem Chamudos distinguishes that when piyutim are recited, one may return when the chazzan begins. The Gemara says that one who immediately returns to his place resembles a dog returning to its vomit. See Beit Yosef and Levush for their explanation. Rashba, responsa 436 and 675, and Binyamin Ze’ev 167 explain that because he immediately returns to his place, it appears as though he wishes to confess a second time, similar to the law below in Hilchos Yom Kippur that one who confesses again on a later Yom Kippur resembles a dog returning to its vomit. See Likkutei Maharil.

[6] <b>The time needed, etc.</b> This requires extensive study. Beit Yosef quotes the Rashba: “You asked whether a chazzan may return immediately to his place when he wishes to repeat the Amidah aloud. I have not seen any of the great authorities object, although every chazzan does so. On the contrary, it appears necessary. I heard from great Ashkenazic authorities that their chazzanim do so, except that they wait there briefly, the time needed to walk four steps.” On this basis Rama and Levush write that the chazzan should wait. However, in responsum Rashba 436 the wording is: “On the contrary, it appears that they are obligated to do so, for when he prayed privately he was required to take three steps, and if he did not, they said that it is as though he did not pray. I heard from one of the great Ashkenazic authorities that their chazzanim do so, waiting in place the time needed to walk four steps. The distinction between an individual and a chazzan is that it is known that the chazzan returns to discharge others, so he does not resemble a dog returning to its vomit.” This distinction implies that in the Rashba’s view he may return immediately and need not wait the time of four steps. The words “I heard that their chazzanim do so” apparently refer upward, meaning that they return immediately. Beit Yosef therefore added the words “except” and “briefly,” although those words are not in the responsum. It is also difficult to understand why this should be considered brief or why any delay is necessary if the concern of returning like a dog does not apply. Rather, the statement that their chazzanim “do so” refers downward, meaning that what they do is wait the time needed to walk four steps. They therefore hold that a chazzan too must wait like an individual, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rambam. Malbushei Yom Tov likewise writes in the name of Derashas Maharash that he should remain until the congregation reaches Atah Kadosh. Nevertheless, that Ashkenazic authority apparently permitted the shorter delay of four steps because of burdening the congregation. According to the Rashba’s distinction, however, the concern does not apply to a chazzan and he may return immediately. Furthermore, the Rashba’s words as printed earlier are themselves unclear: “On the contrary, they are obligated, for when he prayed privately he had to take three steps.” How does this prove that he must return immediately? The responsum must contain an omission or error. I also found this responsum reproduced verbatim in Rashba 675, but the entire passage from “On the contrary” through “there is a distinction” is absent, and there it is clear that he may return immediately. Beit Yosef also writes this in the name of Ohel Moed. It appears that the passage in the first responsum is an interpolation and not the Rashba’s own words. I found the same in responsum Binyamin Ze’ev 164, and I am astonished that the great later authorities did not mention it.
