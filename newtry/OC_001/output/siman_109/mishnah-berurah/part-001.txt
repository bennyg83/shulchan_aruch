(4) Or for Kaddish. Seemingly, since he must finish before Kedushah, he will certainly hear the Kaddish after the Amidah. One may answer that this refers to Ma'ariv, where there is no Kedushah, or to one who has already heard Kedushah and therefore need not wait for it. Nevertheless, he must finish before the Kaddish after the Amidah, meaning at least before the chazzan reaches yehei shemei rabba. He should not answer Amen then, but only yehei shemei rabba, because if he does not know what the Amen addresses it is an orphaned Amen. If he paid attention and knows what he is answering, he may answer Amen. Yehei shemei rabba takes precedence over Kedushah. Therefore, if one arrives close to Kedushah, and waiting until after Kedushah will prevent him from finishing before Kaddish, while waiting until after Kaddish will cause the time for prayer to pass, it is preferable to begin before Kedushah so that he can answer yehei shemei rabba. Kedushah takes precedence over Modim. Therefore, if beginning immediately will prevent him from reciting Kedushah, while beginning after Kedushah will prevent him from saying Modim with the congregation, and waiting until after Modim risks missing the prayer time, he should begin after Kedushah. According to what we explain below in seif katan 14 in the name of Magen Giborim, it is better for him to begin the Amidah together with the chazzan and thereby have both Kedushah and Modim. Certainly, yehei shemei rabba takes precedence over Modim. Therefore, one who arrives immediately after Kedushah, and if he waits until after Modim will miss yehei shemei rabba after the Amidah, while waiting until after Kaddish will cause the prayer time to pass, should begin immediately. It is preferable to forgo Modim rather than yehei shemei rabba, and he can bow in the middle of a blessing. Yehei shemei rabba also takes precedence over communal prayer. Therefore, if he arrives close to Kaddish, and praying immediately will cause him to miss yehei shemei rabba, while waiting until after Kaddish will prevent him from praying Ma'ariv with the congregation, yehei shemei rabba takes precedence. Immediately afterward he should pray the Amidah as Minchah and need not be concerned that the congregation is beginning Ma'ariv, since it is still daytime. See further in Biur Halachah in the name of Peri Megadim.

(5) The same applies to Amen, etc. Regarding the Amen after Shome'a Tefillah, there is a practical difference, since he must wait to begin even after Kedushah unless he knows he can finish before Shome'a Tefillah. What practical difference is there regarding Amen after HaKel HaKadosh, since he must already finish before Kedushah and will naturally answer it? It applies even if he already heard Kedushah, for he must still wait for the Amen after HaKel HaKadosh if he has not yet heard it. It also means that after Kedushah he should not immediately begin praying, but wait until answering Amen after that blessing, which is itself connected to Kedushah. The same applies if he knows the congregation will say Barechu and he has not yet heard it. If he can finish before Barechu, he may pray; otherwise, he should wait at Shirah Chadashah until he hears Barechu. If he has already heard Kedushah or Barechu, or knows he will hear it later, he need not wait. For Modim, however, even if he has heard Modim with the congregation several times, he must still wait, because otherwise he appears to deny when he does not bow. For Kaddish too he must wait, because there is no fixed limit. It is possible that the Kaddish recited from Aleinu onward is not included. Magen Avraham and other Acharonim write this. Derech HaChaim and Chayei Adam write that if he has already heard any Kaddish and answered yehei shemei rabba, he need not wait further.
