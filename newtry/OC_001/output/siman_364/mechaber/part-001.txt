**** ENGLISH ****
A public domain itself can only be rectified with doors, and they must be locked at night. Some say that even if they are not locked, they must at least be fit to be locked — so if they are sunken in the ground, one clears them and fixes them so they are fit to be locked. Once the door arrangement is made, the entire area is considered a single courtyard and its alleyways require no further arrangement.

**** ENGLISH ****
A bent alleyway shaped like the letter dalet is treated as if it were a pass-through alleyway at the bend, and requires a doorframe shape at the bend. At each of its two ends it needs either a lechi, beam, or doorframe shape — and a lechi or beam at the bend. If it is shaped like the letter chet, it requires a doorframe shape at each of its two bends, and a lechi or beam at each of its two ends.

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="י" data-order="10"></i>מבואות <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="יד"></i>הפתוחי' אלו לאלו ואין העיקום של א' מהם נוטה לרה"ר כיון שפתיחת המבוי החיצון לר"ה הרי אותו מבוי שפתוח אליו בעקמימותו חשיב כפתוח לרשות הרבים וכן מבוי הפתוח לאותו מבוי וכן כולם:
**** ENGLISH ****
Alleyways that open into one another — when the bend of one of them does not face the public domain — since the outer alleyway opens to the public domain, the alleyway that opens into it at its bend is considered as if it opens to the public domain. And so too the alleyway that opens into that one, and so on for all of them.

**** ENGLISH ****
An alleyway shaped like the letter nun meaning a large alleyway into which many small alleyways open, with the other ends of the small ones opening to the public domain — even if the openings of the small alleyways on each side do not directly face one another: one makes a doorframe shape for each small alleyway at the point where it opens into the large alleyway, and a lechi or beam at its other end which opens to the public domain. As for the large alleyway itself: if it is a pass-through, one makes a doorframe shape on one side and a lechi or beam on the other; if it is closed at one end, one makes a lechi or beam at its open end.
