<b>ומשיירין</b> עט״ז. מ״ש דת״ק דאמר מבערין הכל שמשייר מזון ב״ס כ״כ רש״י פסחים מ״ט א׳ ומיהו פי׳ כן דלא ליהוי ד׳ מחלוקת וכר״א איש ברתותא ופסחים י״ג א׳) אבל אין מוכרח דיש לקיים ב׳ סעודות במצה עשירה עמ״א ר״פ או למ״ד דאין איסור מצה בע״פ כ״א לאחר איסור חמץ ועב״י עמ״ש הטור כן הנהיג רש״י לבער הכל מלפני השבת ולא לאכול מצה כתב הב״י דיאכל מצה לתיאבון וזה כהרא״ש הב״י תע״א דקודם ה׳ מותר במצה גם לא הטריחו במצה עשירה יע״ש. ומ״ש הט״ז הך ביעור כו׳ כדמוכח בסי׳ תמ״ו אות ב׳ דקודם ה׳ צריך ליתן לעכו״ם דאח״כ אסור בטילטול ומיהו אמירה לעכו״ם בטילטול מוקצה עסי׳ רע״ו במ״א י״א וגם שבות דשבות במקום מצוה כבסי׳ ש״ז ס״ה. ומ״ש לא שאר השבתות לא מוכח בתמ״ו עמ״א שם ב׳ לזרות לרוח אסור כבסי׳ שי״ט סי״ז יע״ש. עמ״ל פ״א ה״ג דמפרר דק ממש י״ל הוי טוחן יע״ש ועב״ח ס״ג בשם בעל המאור דמותר לפרר בשבת ומאן דאוסר דהוי עובדין דחול לא מ״ה. ואפשר בתמ״ו מוכח דאסור לפרר ואי״ה שם יבואר ועמ״א תמ״ו אות ג׳ וה׳ כשיש עכו״ם שרי בטילטול ועובדא דזונין פסחים מ״ט א׳ י״ל בד׳ או ה׳ כמ״ש המ״א בתמ״ו. וע׳ אות ה׳ כאן ואי״ה יבואר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And we leave over.</b> See the Taz. What he wrote, that the first tanna who says we destroy everything still leaves food for two meals, so wrote Rashi in Pesachim folio 49a. However, he explained it this way so that there should not be four disputes, and like Rabbi Elazar of Bartosa, and Pesachim folio 13a. But this is not compelled, for one can fulfill the two meals with egg matzah; see Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Or according to the one who says that the prohibition of matzah on the eve of Pesach applies only after the prohibition of chametz. See the Beis Yosef on what the Tur wrote, that Rashi practiced this way, to destroy everything before Shabbos and not eat matzah. The Beis Yosef wrote that he should eat matzah with appetite. This follows the Rosh, as in Beis Yosef siman 471, that before the fifth hour matzah is permitted; and they also did not trouble him with egg matzah; see there. What the Taz wrote, this destruction, etc., is as is proven in siman 446, letter 2, that before the fifth hour one must give it to a non-Jew, because afterward it is forbidden to move. However, regarding telling a non-Jew to move muktzeh, see siman 276, Magen Avraham s.k. 11; and also a shevus of a shevus in a place of mitzvah, as in siman 307 se'if 5. What he wrote, not on other Shabbosos, is not proven in siman 446; see Magen Avraham there, s.k. 2. Scattering to the wind is forbidden, as in siman 319 se'if 17; see there. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 1, law 3, that crumbling it very finely may be considered grinding; see there. See the Bach, se'if 3, in the name of the Baal HaMaor, that it is permitted to crumble on Shabbos; and the one who forbids because it is weekday activity did not forbid according to Torah law. It is possible that in siman 446 it is proven that crumbling is forbidden, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. See Magen Avraham siman 446, letters 3 and 5, that where there is a non-Jew it is permitted with moving. The incident of Zunin in Pesachim folio 49a can be said to have been in the fourth or fifth hour, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 446. See letter 5 here, and with Hashem's help it will be explained.

<b>אם</b> שכח להפריש חלה מע״ש עמ״א תק״ו אות ח׳ יע״ש וע׳ באר היטב ולהפקירו ג״כ א״א. ולמ״ד ח״ש לית ביה איסור להשהותה חלת האור אין לה שיעור אלא דקי״ל דאסור ח״ש עסי׳ תמ״ז במ״א. ואם הפריש חלה מע״ש ושכח לשרוף והגיע שבת שעה ד׳ לעכו״ם אסור ליתן אלא לקטן כהן אבל לגדול ולבטל ברוב אין מבטלין בשבת עמ״א שכ״ג י״ד וי״ד צ״ט ובפריי שם ושכ״ד. ומיהו י״ל מפקירו או חלה דלאו דידיה אין עובר עליו ע׳ פסחים כיצד מפרישין חלה בטומאה וליתן לכלבו י״ל שרי וע׳ ביצה כ״ז ב׳ די״ל אין מבערין קדשים בי״ט ושבת. וחלה בדיל מיניה ער״מ פי״א מתרומות ישראל אסור בהנאה של כילוי וה״ה טבל אסור בהנאה של כילוי עתוס׳ פסחים ט׳ א׳ וצ״ע א״כ צריך להשליך הכל לים ולא לעכו״ם ואפשר חלת ח״ל ל״ג כ״כ ושרי בהנאה ואם הפריש חלה במזיד לא יאכל ובשוגג יאכל ער״מ ז״ל ה׳ שבת ועמ״ש בשכ״ג במ״א י״א ובט״ז ה׳ סיים שם דרבנן שרי א״כ שאני חלת א״י דהוי ד״ה משא״כ חלת האור דרבנן י״ל דלא הוי תיקון כ״כ אף דא״א לאכול כך כמ״ש המ״מ ולדינא באין לו פת אחר כ״א זה ששכח ליטול חלה אפשר לצדד למ״ש הט״ז שם אי״ה בתק״ו יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If.</b> If he forgot to separate challah from Friday, see Magen Avraham siman 506, letter 8; see there, and see Baer Heitev. Declaring it ownerless is also impossible. According to the one who says that a half-measure has no prohibition, keeping the challah of fire has no minimum measure; but we rule that a half-measure is forbidden; see siman 447 in Magen Avraham. If he separated challah on Friday and forgot to burn it, and Shabbos arrived at the fourth hour, it is forbidden to give it to a non-Jew, only to a minor kohen; but giving it to an adult, or nullifying it in a majority, is not allowed, for one may not nullify on Shabbos. See Magen Avraham siman 323, s.k. 14, and Yoreh Deah siman 99, and my Peri Megadim there and in siman 324. However, one may say that he should make it ownerless; or perhaps challah that is not his, he does not transgress over it. See Pesachim regarding how one separates challah in impurity. To give it to his dog, one may say it is permitted; and see Beitzah folio 27b, where one can say that we do not destroy kodshim on Yom Tov and Shabbos. Challah is something from which one keeps away; see the Rambam, chapter 11 of Terumos, that a Yisrael is forbidden to benefit from it by way of destruction. The same applies to tevel, which is forbidden for benefit by way of destruction; see Tosafos, Pesachim folio 9a. This requires examination: if so, one must throw everything into the sea and not give it to a non-Jew. Perhaps challah of the Diaspora is not so stringent and is permitted for benefit. If he separated challah intentionally, he may not eat; if unintentionally, he may eat. See the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Shabbos, and what I wrote in siman 323 on Magen Avraham s.k. 11. In the Taz, s.k. 5, he concluded there that a rabbinic matter is permitted. If so, challah of Eretz Yisrael is different, for it is Torah law, unlike challah of fire, which is rabbinic; one may say this is not so much an act of fixing, although it is impossible to eat it as is, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote. As practical law, where he has no other bread except this from which he forgot to take challah, perhaps one may incline to what the Taz wrote there; with Hashem's help it will be explained further in siman 506.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See.</b> See Responsa Maharil, siman 99, regarding the third meal, that there is no positive commandment to override, etc. One may say it is a positive commandment that arose through negligence; see Tosafos, Eruvin folio 100a, s.v. matan. See what I wrote about this in siman 291, and see siman 446, Magen Avraham s.k. 2. In my Sefer HaMaggid I wrote the laws of Pesach when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, and I marked them in the Magen Avraham. I will also mention here that charoses and other things must be prepared on Friday, for on Yom Tov pounding in the normal way is somewhat forbidden; after the fact, see siman 504. Regarding roasting meat and an egg as a remembrance of the chagigah and the Pesach offering, which are left for the second night: when the eve of Pesach is Shabbos, he should roast them on Friday, for on the first night of Yom Tov it is forbidden to prepare from one Yom Tov day for the next. Some say that we do not apply the principle of since regarding cooking. Filling a pot to cook many eggs is not relevant; that applies specifically to meat, which improves thereby. See Taz siman 473, s.k. 4; see there. Whether it is permitted to place the maror in water on Shabbos so that it not wither, see Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 3 and siman 473, s.k. 14, that it is permitted. This requires examination, for moving and watering, since they are not for the need of Shabbos, should be forbidden; see siman 321 in Magen Avraham s.k. 13 and siman 654. This requires examination if he inserted the maror into the ground so that it not wither and it did not take root; see Mahariv, Hilchos Pesach. With Hashem's help it will be explained in siman 473. See Magen Avraham there, letter 8, regarding what one should do on the night of Yom Tov if he forgot to roast the egg and meat; see there.

They wrote, of blessed memory, that the shaliach tzibbur should not prolong the morning prayer. The Rav should expound on the Shabbos before it. In Responsa Kenesses Yechezkel, at the end of Orach Chayim, he wrote that on Shabbos that is the eve of Pesach one should warn members of the household on Friday about separating challah, for the reason we do not mention now is that with challah of the Diaspora one may continue eating and afterward separate; this does not apply on Shabbos that is the eve of Pesach. See there. See siman 260 and siman 506, Magen Avraham s.k. 8.
