**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 8) One hundred blessings.</b> Beit Yosef's calculation of one hundred and five is as follows.

At night one recites HaMapil. In the morning he recites Al Netilas Yadayim and Asher Yatzar, fifteen blessings from Elokai Neshamah through HaGomel Chasadim Tovim, and three blessings over Torah, because he regards VeHaarev Na as a separate blessing. This totals twenty-one.

The blessings over tzitzis and tefillin add two, totaling twenty-three. Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach add two. The blessings of Krias Shema at Shacharis and Maariv add seven, totaling thirty-two. The three weekday Amidah prayers contain fifty-seven blessings, bringing the total to eighty-nine.

Two meals, one at night and one in the morning, contain sixteen blessings, eight at each: Al Netilas Yadayim, HaMotzi, the four blessings of Bircas HaMazon, and blessings before and after drinking. Beit Yosef assumes Bircas HaMazon requires a cup even for an individual, and therefore counts the blessings before and after the cup. The total is one hundred and five.

On a fast day eight are missing from the daytime meal, since the preceding night he eats. If he stops eating before nightfall, another eight are missing.

Magen Avraham writes that “ninety-eight” is a scribal error and should read ninety-seven.

This creates a difficulty with Beit Yosef's instruction to complete the total by putting on tzitzis and tefillin at Minchah. Beit Yosef counted only two blessings for both, since he holds that head and arm tefillin require only one blessing. Even after adding them, one blessing would still be missing.

Perhaps Beit Yosef means that one blessing nevertheless remains missing, but he found no remedy for it, just as he later writes regarding Shabbos.
