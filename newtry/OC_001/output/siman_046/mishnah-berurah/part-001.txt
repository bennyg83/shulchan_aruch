**** ENGLISH ****
Before entering the synagogue, while he is still in its courtyard, he should say, “In the house of God we shall walk with the multitude.” He should feel awe and trembling upon entering, pause briefly, and say, “But I, through Your abundant kindness, shall enter Your house; I shall bow toward Your holy sanctuary in fear of You.” He should then enter.

<b>(1) At the time, etc.</b> All these blessings were instituted because a person may not benefit from this world without a blessing. Whoever benefits without a blessing is considered to have misused sacred property.

Two verses appear contradictory: “The earth and its fullness belong to Hashem,” and, “The earth He gave to mankind.” The Sages resolve them: before a blessing it belongs to Hashem and is forbidden like consecrated property; after a blessing it is permitted to mankind.

Since before the blessing everything belongs to Hashem, it is sacred and subject to misuse. The Sages therefore instituted a blessing for each benefit a person derives from the world's regular order.

(יד) ברכות - הטעם לפי שהיו מתים בכל יום מאה נפשות מישראל תיקן דוד ע"ז לברך מאה ברכות בכל יום. והנה בכל יום אנו מברכין עוד יותר ממאה ברכות כיצד בלילה כשהולך לישן מברך המפיל ובשחר מברך ענט"י ואשר יצר ועוד ט"ז ברכות בברכת השחר וג' ברכות על התורה [לדעת הרמ"א לקמן בסי' מ"ז סעיף ו' בהג"ה] הרי כ"ב וברכת ציצית ותפילין הרי עוד שלש לפי מנהגינו שמברכין שתים על התפילין הרי כ"ה וברוך שאמר וישתבח הרי עוד שתים וברכת ק"ש שחרית וערבית עם ברכת יראו עינינו שמנה הרי ל"ה ונ"ז ברכות דג' תפלות הרי צ"ב ובשתי סעודות שסועד אחת ערבית ואחת שחרית יש ט"ז ברכות כי בכל סעודה יש ח' ברכות על נט"י והמוציא וד' ברכות שבבהמ"ז וכשהוא שותה כוס בבהמ"ז מברך לפניו ולאחריו הרי בס"ה ק"ח ברכות וא"כ אף ביום התענית שחסר לו סעודה אחת ג"כ מקיים ק' ברכות. וביום השבת חסר לו י"ג ברכות מהמאה וכדאיתא במ"א עי"ש ע"כ יראה להשלימם במיני פירות ומגדים ואם אין לו יוצא ע"פ הדחק במה שיכוין לשמוע ברכת התורה והמפטיר ויענה אמן וכדלקמן בסימן רפ"ד וכתב המ"א דלא יכניס עצמו בחשש ברכה שאינה צריכה משום מצות ק' ברכות. וביוהכ"פ ג"כ יוצא בשמיעת הברכות כמו בשבת אך ביוה"כ אחר כל החשבונות חסר לו עוד שלש ברכות וכתב המ"א דישלים זה בברכת הריח על הבשמים אך כ"ז שלא הסיח דעתו מלהריח אסור לחזור ולברך משום ברכה שאינה צריכה. וכן יוכל להשלימם בברכת אשר יצר אם נזדמן לו. ואפשר דיוצא ע"פ הדחק במה שמכוין לשמוע חזרת הש"ץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(14) Blessings.</b> One hundred Jewish souls were dying each day, and David therefore instituted one hundred daily blessings.

In fact, we recite more than one hundred each day. At night one recites HaMapil. In the morning he recites Al Netilas Yadayim, Asher Yatzar, sixteen morning blessings, and three blessings over Torah according to Rama below in siman 47, seif 6, totaling twenty-two.

The blessings over tzitzis and tefillin add three according to our custom of reciting two blessings upon tefillin, totaling twenty-five. Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach add two. The blessings of Krias Shema at Shacharis and Maariv, together with Yir'u Eineinu, add eight, totaling thirty-five.

The three Amidah prayers add fifty-seven, totaling ninety-two. Two meals, one at night and one in the morning, add sixteen, because each contains eight blessings: Al Netilas Yadayim, HaMotzi, the four blessings of Bircas HaMazon, and blessings before and after the cup. The total is one hundred and eight.

Even on a fast day, when one meal is missing, the total remains one hundred.

On Shabbos thirteen blessings are missing from the hundred, as Magen Avraham explains. One should complete them with fruit and delicacies.

If he has none, in pressing circumstances he may intend to hear the blessings over the Torah and Haftarah and answer amen, as explained below in siman 284.

Magen Avraham writes that one should not create a possible unnecessary blessing merely to complete one hundred blessings.

On Yom Kippur he likewise relies upon listening to blessings as on Shabbos. After all calculations, three blessings still remain missing. Magen Avraham writes that he may complete them with blessings over fragrances.

So long as he has not diverted his attention from smelling, however, he may not repeat the blessing, because that would be an unnecessary blessing.

He may likewise complete the total with Asher Yatzar if the occasion arises. In pressing circumstances, perhaps intending to hear the chazzan's repetition also suffices.

<b>(18) He may recite the blessing.</b> Meaning he should say, “Who made me a convert,” because conversion is called making, as in “the souls they made in Charan.”

Others disagree because his conversion resulted from his own good choice to accept the true faith, rather than Hashem creating him as a convert.

Everyone agrees that he may recite “Who did not make me a slave” and “Who did not make me a woman.” Although a non-Jew has a lower status than a slave, had he been born a slave his master might never have freed him, whereas his conversion depended upon his own will.

A mamzer may recite all these blessings because he is fully Jewish. The same applies to a blind person, whom we rule is obligated in all mitzvos.

A tumtum and androgynous person, who are obligated in mitzvos because of doubt, should not recite “Who did not make me a woman,” because their status is uncertain. Peri Megadim.

<b>(20) He should not recite it.</b> By thanking Hashem for fully straightening him, he has included Matir Asurim, whose obligation arose immediately upon sitting up.

Some disagree. The later authorities conclude leniently.

Peri Megadim writes that it is proper to hear the blessing afterward from another person and intend to fulfill the obligation, thereby satisfying both possibilities.

If, when saying Hashem's Name, he intended to recite Zokef Kefufim, he should conclude Zokef Kefufim even though he will thereby be unable to recite Matir Asurim. All the more so, with the other blessings he should not be concerned about order, but should conclude according to his intention when mentioning Hashem's Name. Chayei Adam.

If he reached Pokeach Ivrim, intended to conclude it, but misspoke and said Malbish Arumim, then remembered within the time of an ordinary utterance and added Pokeach Ivrim, some say he fulfilled Pokeach Ivrim because he canceled the first conclusion. Others remain uncertain.

If, however, when he said “Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe,” he intended Malbish Arumim, completed it, and then within an ordinary utterance added Pokeach Ivrim, everyone agrees he fulfilled Malbish Arumim and must recite Pokeach Ivrim again. The order does not prevent fulfillment.

The same applies to all morning blessings except Matir Asurim and Zokef Kefufim. If he recited Zokef Kefufim first and within an ordinary utterance added Matir Asurim, he has fulfilled only the first blessing, Zokef Kefufim, but should not recite Matir Asurim again. Peri Megadim, siman 209.
