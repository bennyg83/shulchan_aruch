ופרש"י דגן כל ה' מינים קרוים דגן מרחיקין מצואתו משאכל כזית מאלו בכל ענין אכילה שצואת מינים הללו מסרחת עכ"ל. וכתב ב"י שנראה מדבריו שאם לא אכל קטן זה עדיין כזית דגן אין מרחיקין מצואתו ואפילו הוא בן כמה בכא"פ אע"פ שעדיין לא אכל דגן כלל מעולם מרחיקין מצואתו ומי רגליו שכל שהגיע לכלל זה צואתו מסרחת עכ"ל. וכן הם דבריו כאן בש"ע ואיכא למידק מהיכן משמע לו מלשון הפוסקים דלא כרש"י והלא דברי הטור ורמב"ם היו אכל דגן מעולם או אכל מימים רבים א"צ להרחיק מצואתו וזה אינו נראה כלל דגדול יוכיח כמו שזכרתי ופסק הש"ע ברור וקיים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A child who reached, etc.</b> In the gemara it is stated in this language: a child who can eat a kezayit of grain — we distance from his excrement, etc. R' Chiya said: and that he can eat it in the time of eating a peras. R' Chiya son of Rav Avya: in an adult, even though he cannot eat it in the time of eating a peras, as it is written "he will add knowledge, he will add pain"; and Rashi explained: grain — all five species are called grain; we distance from his excrement from when he ate a kezayit of these in any manner of eating, for the excrement of these species putrefies — end quote. And Beit Yosef wrote: it appears from his words that if this child has not yet eaten a kezayit of grain, we do not distance from his excrement, even if he is many years old. And it further appears from his words that even if he ate a kezayit of grain, if many days passed such that it is impossible to say this excrement is from that grain he ate, we do not distance from it, etc. But this does not appear so from the poskim' words; rather his intent is that he reached the stage of being able to eat a kezayit of grain in the time of eating a peras — even though he has not yet eaten grain at all — we distance from his excrement and urine, for once he reached this stage his excrement putrefies — end quote. And so are his words here in Shulchan Aruch. And one may question: whence does he infer from the poskim' language, not like Rashi? Behold the Tur's and Rambam's words were exactly the gemara's language. And one must say his intent is that the poskim are accustomed to explain their language more than the Talmud's way, for their words are abbreviated; and if so, the poskim should not have written "can eat," which implies but did not eat — in truth it is certainly so. But I wondered greatly at what he understood from Rashi's view — that all who ate from days ago a kezayit of grain need not now distance from his excrement; this is impossible to say at all, for if so, that which the gemara said "and in an adult even though he cannot eat in the time of eating a peras" — his law would be likewise, that if he did not eat grain types for many days we need not distance from his excrement; and behold an adult is not stricter than a child except in the measure of time of eating a peras, but not in the matter of eating itself that an adult is stricter than a child in this — rather they are certainly equal in this. And what Beit Yosef wondered on Rashi at length — that Shulchan Aruch ruled well regarding a child, and Rashi's view too is thus, and what he wrote "from when he ate" — Rashi deals where we know from this child's eating that we saw; and in truth, if we do not see from him any eating of grain types for the reason they did not give him, but if it is clear by human sense of the infant's nature that he could eat grain in the measure of eating a peras — it is as if we saw he ate thus, and we distance from his excrement. And in vain Beit Yosef made a dispute between Rashi and the poskim in this. And moreover I wondered at Magen Avraham z"l who disputed Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef on the basis that all poskim hold like Rashi — that all who did not eat grain ever, or ate from days ago, need not distance from his excrement; this does not appear at all, for an adult proves as I said, and Shulchan Aruch's ruling is clear and established:
