[ג] <b>יזהר ליקח החוט וכו'.</b> זה לשון אגודה לא יכרוך גמי סביב ההדס דעובר על בל תוסיף אך במין עלי לולב יכרוך, עד כאן. וצריך עיון דהא קיימא לן כרבי מאיר דאין צריך אגד ולא הוי בל תוסיף והיינו שכתבו רמ"א ולבוש טעם דהסרת חוט משום חציצה ולא כתבו משום בל תוסיף וכן משמע בש"ס דכל שהוא לנוי אין בו משום בל תוסיף. ולדעת אגודה נראה לי למחות למה שיש נוהגין לצבוע לבן הלולב במין שקורין פיטר זיל, ואפשר דסבירא ליה דאין בו ממשות לחול עליו בל תוסיף ודחוק. עוד בסימן תקי"ג סעיף ב' דמה דעביד לחזותא לא בטל ועיין בתו' סוכה דף ל"א ד"ה הואיל וכו':
**** ENGLISH ****
[3] <b>One should be careful to take the string, etc.</b> This is the language of the Agudah: one should not wrap reed-grass around the hadas, for he violates bal tosif; but with a species of lulav leaves he may wrap it, end quote. This requires analysis, for we maintain like Rabbi Meir that binding is not required, and it is not bal tosif. This is why the Rema and the Levush wrote that the reason for removing the string is because of interposition, and did not write that it is because of bal tosif. So too it implies in the Talmud that anything done for beauty has no concern of bal tosif. According to the Agudah, it appears to me that one should protest against what some practice, to dye the white part of the lulav with the substance called petersil. Perhaps he holds that it has no substance upon which bal tosif can take effect, but this is forced. Also in siman 513 se'if 2, something done for appearance is not nullified; and see Tosafos, Sukkah 31, s.v. "since," etc.

[ו] <b>בלולב שלושה וכו'.</b> משמע בלא קשר הדס וערבה עם הלולב קושרים שלושה למעלה בלולב וכן מסתבר דלמטה אין העלים צריכין לכפות כל כך, ולאפוקי מט"ז שכתב זה לשונו, במרדכי כפת חסר וי"ו דששה ימים קשור ודומה לכף אחת וביום שביעי מתירין אותו, עד כאן. ותמיה לי דהא צריך לכסכס עלין וקשר זה מעכב הכסכוס על כן צריך לעשות בענין שיוכל לכסכס היטב ועל כל פנים נראה דקשר שמקשר שלושה מינים יחד נחשב לאחד ויעשה למעלה עוד שנים עד כאן דברי ט"ז. גם לא ידעתי מאי תמה על המרדכי דיש לומר דסבירא ליה דאין צריך לכסכס כלל כמו שכתב בסעיף ט' גם אישתמיטתיה מה שכתב מהרי"ל דיהא פתוח למעלה רק טפח ובהושענא רבה יתיר עד חציו. כתב מהרי"ו שלושה אגודות נגד שלוש אבות ולולב בגימטריא ס"ח נגד ע"א סנהדרין ובדיעבד סגי באחד:
**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>On the lulav, three, etc.</b> It implies that aside from the knot of the hadas and aravah with the lulav, one ties three knots above on the lulav. This is also reasonable, for below, the leaves do not need to be folded so much. This excludes the Turei Zahav, who wrote as follows: in the Mordechai, "kapos" is written missing a vav, meaning that for six days it is tied and resembles one palm, and on the seventh day they untie it, end quote. This is astonishing to me, for one must rustle the leaves, and this knot prevents the rustling. Therefore one must make it in a manner that he can rustle well. In any event, it appears that the knot that binds the three species together is counted as one, and he should make two more above. Until here are the words of the Turei Zahav. I also do not know what he wondered about the Mordechai, for one may say that the Mordechai holds that one need not rustle at all, as he wrote in se'if 9. He also overlooked what the Maharil wrote, that it should be open at the top only one tefach, and on Hoshana Rabbah he should untie it until halfway. Mahari Weil wrote: three bindings corresponding to the three forefathers; and lulav has the numerical value 68, corresponding to the seventy-one of the Sanhedrin. After the fact, one suffices.

[ח] <b>[לבוש] הני תלתא וכו'.</b> מקשים תיפוק ליה דכל דבר שמברך צריך ליטול בימין, ותירץ מגן אברהם דקא משמע לן דאף אחר ברכה כגון בשעת אמירת הושענא יטול בימין, ולי נראה דאי לאו האי טעמא דהני תלתא באמת לא היה מברך אלולב וזה שכתב הלבוש ומהאי טעמא וכו' וכן משמע במהרי"ל שהקשה אמאי לא מברכינן על אתרוג דמוקדם בקרא ותירץ משום דהני תלתא. ולכאורה קשה על מהרי"ל דהא בש"ס [סוכה] דף ל"ו קאמר טעם אחר משום דגבוה במינו, ויש לומר דמכל מקום טעם דמוקדם נקרא עדיף לכן אמר עוד טעם בלולב דהני תלת לחוד נמי לא סגי נגד טעם דמוקדם. אך קשה על הלבוש שמחשב נמי מעלה שאתרוג הוא פרי וזה עדיף מטעם לולב גבוה אם כן נשאר קשה לברוך על אתרוג דמוקדם, ובאבודרהם כתב עוד טעם שאתרוג דומה ללב והלב בגוף נוטה לצד שמאל. כתב בספר אמרכל בשם ספר עתים דאם נוטלין בזה אחר זה יטול גם אתרוג בימין:
**** ENGLISH ****
[8] <b>[Levush] These three, etc.</b> They ask: let him derive it from the rule that anything over which one recites a blessing must be taken in the right hand. The Magen Avraham answered that this teaches us that even after the blessing, such as at the time of saying Hoshanah, he should take it in the right hand. To me it appears that if not for this reason, that these are three, in truth he would not have recited the blessing over the lulav. This is what the Levush wrote: and for this reason, etc. So too it implies in the Maharil, who asked why we do not recite the blessing over the esrog, since it is earlier in the verse, and answered because these are three. At first glance, there is difficulty with the Maharil, for in the Talmud, Sukkah 36, it gives a different reason, because it is higher among its species. One may say that nevertheless the reason that it is earlier in the verse is considered preferable, and therefore he gave another reason regarding lulav, that these three alone would also not suffice against the reason of precedence. But there is difficulty with the Levush, who also counts as an advantage that the esrog is a fruit, and this is preferable to the reason that the lulav is taller. If so, the difficulty remains that one should recite the blessing over the esrog, which is earlier. In Abudraham he wrote another reason, that the esrog resembles the heart, and the heart in the body inclines toward the left side. It is written in Sefer Imarkal in the name of Sefer HaItim that if one takes them one after another, he should also take the esrog in the right hand.

[12] <b>[Levush] And after the fact he fulfills, etc.</b> The view of the Nachalas Tzvi is that specifically a left-handed person fulfills his obligation if he reversed them, but one who is not left-handed does not fulfill his obligation if he takes the lulav in his left hand. I already wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that he did not see the Darkei Moshe, who wrote in the name of the Kol Bo that even one who is not left-handed fulfills his obligation. I also saw this in Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 118, and Tanyei Maharil; and so I found in Sefer Imarkal in the name of Sefer Baruch. The proof that the Turei Zahav brought from the Talmud, page 43, where Abaye answers that he turned it upside down and did not say that he took the lulav in his left hand, see there. So too Chut HaShani, siman 28, wrote; this proof had already been brought in Sefer Shibbolei HaLeket. What Chut HaShani wrote according to the explanation of the Baal HaItur is forced. Rather, the primary explanation is as I explained in Eliyahu Zuta; see there. However, in Shibbolei HaLeket there, in the name of Rabbi Yaakov, it implies that he did not fulfill his obligation. Nevertheless, it is clear that Shibbolei HaLeket did not rule like him in this. The Magen Avraham wrote that Rabbeinu Chananel explained "when he turned it upside down" to mean that he takes the lulav in his left hand; therefore it is good to go back and take it without a blessing, end quote. In my humble opinion, one need not be concerned against the authorities mentioned above, and the Magen Avraham did not see the authorities mentioned above. Furthermore, I found that Imarkal brought this explanation and wrote about it: this is not clear, for it should have said "when he exchanged them" and not "when he turned them upside down," end quote. And likewise on page 36 the Talmud says: sometimes they become exchanged for him.
