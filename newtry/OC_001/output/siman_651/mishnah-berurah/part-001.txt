(א) שיטול - ביו"ט ראשון יותר טוב לילך למקום שיש בו לולב מלילך למקום שמתפללין דלולב ביום א' היא דאורייתא ותפלה דרבנן ואפילו לולב ספק אם ישיג שם ולתפלה ודאי ילך ביום א' למקום שיש בו לולב וביו"ט ב' ילך למקום שודאי מתפללין [פמ"ג]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) <b>That he take</b> - On the first day of Yom Tov it is better to go to a place that has a lulav than to go to a place where people pray, for lulav on the first day is a Torah obligation, while prayer is rabbinic. Even if it is uncertain whether he will obtain a lulav there, whereas he will certainly have prayer, on the first day he should go to the place that has a lulav. On the second day of Yom Tov he should go to the place where people certainly pray. Peri Megadim.

(ב) כל אחד - ולא שאחד יטול בשביל כולם דכתיב ולקחתם לשון רבים ולא לקחת להורות שהחיוב על הרבים ולא מיבעי ביו"ט ראשון ואפילו בשאר ימים בעינן לקיחה לכל אחד [ולא כמו שצוה אחד במעשה שלא היה בבהכ"נ אלא אתרוג אחד שיקח הש"ץ עבור כולם והם ישמעו הברכה ויענו אמן] [תשובת ח"ס חאו"ח סי' קפ"ב]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(2) <b>Each person</b> - And not that one person should take it on behalf of everyone, for it is written, "and you shall take," in the plural, and not "you shall take" in the singular, to teach that the obligation is upon the many. This is not only on the first day of Yom Tov; even on the other days, taking is required for each person. This is unlike what one person instructed in a case where there was only one esrog in the synagogue, that the prayer leader should take it on behalf of everyone and they should hear the blessing and answer amen. Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 182.

(11) <b>There are those who wrote, etc.</b> - This refers to Yom Tov, for the Shulchan Aruch brought the permissibility of a bow, and the Rema, in the name of the Tur, brings the permissibility of wrapping. But what the Rema concludes, "and so is the practice," and the Magen Avraham explains that this applies even on a weekday, requires analysis. For although the Mordechai mentions the permissibility of wrapping even on a weekday, it is not in this manner, but rather that at first he ties once and afterward wraps, etc., and through this it is as though he ties two knots one upon the other. But without a tie it is no better than a bow. Afterward I found that the Gra also wonders about this. Therefore it appears, according to what is explained just ahead, that since the practice is to make three knots, the lower knot, namely the one that binds the three species together, should first be made with a tie.

(47) <b>He should go around toward the right in his shaking: east, south, etc.</b> - So too the Derech HaChayim and the Chayei Adam copied, that this is the common custom in our countries. The reason is that the Sages always warned us that you should always take the path that is to your right; where there is right and left before you, choose your right. There is no difference between a left-handed person and one who is not left-handed. Magen Avraham. Now I will briefly arrange the order of the shakings according to what is customary in our countries. For the order of shakings toward the directions, he should turn his face eastward, with the spine of the lulav toward his face, and perform three outward movements to the east and three inward movements. That is, he extends his hand with the lulav from opposite himself outward three times. It is proper that he also rustle the leaves during the outward and inward movement. During the inward movement he should bring it very close to his body, opposite the chest. Afterward he should incline the head of the lulav to the south and likewise perform three outward and inward movements, all as toward the east; but he need not turn his face to the side toward which he shakes, only incline the head of the lulav toward the side to which he shakes. Likewise toward the west, he should incline the head of the lulav over his shoulder and perform three outward and inward movements; afterward toward the north, he extends outward and brings inward three times; afterward upward, and afterward downward, all by extending outward and bringing inward three times, as toward the east.
