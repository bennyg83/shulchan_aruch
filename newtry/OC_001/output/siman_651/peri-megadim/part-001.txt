<b>לעשות</b> עט״ז והקשר עליון שבלולב עכ״פ יוצא לולב טפח למעלה שיעור נענוע כבסי׳ תרנ״א ועא״ר ז׳ משמע ג׳ קשרים בלולב עצמו לבד הקשר הד׳ שאוגדם ג׳ מינים יחד יהא למעלה הלולב פחות טפח. ב׳ ראשיהם עט״ז סוכה ל״ז ב׳ י״ג רבא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To make.</b> See the Taz. The upper knot of the lulav, in any event, must leave a tefach of lulav projecting above it, the measure required for shaking, as in siman 651. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 7, which implies that there are three knots on the lulav itself, besides the fourth knot that binds the three species together, and above them there should be less than a tefach of lulav. Regarding the two heads, see Taz, Sukkah 37b, and Rava there.

<b>עמ״א</b> ו׳ ואי״ה שם יבואר ובאבודרהם אתרוג דומה ללב בשמאל ועא״ר. ומברכין על נטילת לולב בלשון חז״ל נטילה כמו לקיחה וע״ש שצריך להניף הלולב ונטל לשון הרמה וינטלם וינשאם אבודרהם שם ועמ״נ בשורש נטל משא יע״ש וי״ל אמאי לא מברכין אקב״ו על ד׳ מינים וי״ל ועחה״ר כפות תמרים זה הקב״ה וצדיק יסוד עולם יע״ש ואין לנו ידיעה בזה. אולי יזכני ה, ולהבין את זאת בעזה״י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See Magen Avraham.</b> Seif katan 6, and with G-d's help it will be explained there. In Avudraham it states that the esrog resembles the heart and is taken in the left hand; see Eliyah Rabbah. We bless 'al netilas lulav' in the language of the Sages, for 'netilah' is like taking. See there, for one must wave the lulav; 'netal' is a term of lifting, as in 'He took them and carried them.' See Avudraham there, and see Menachem ben Saruk under the root 'natal,' meaning burden, see there. One may ask why we do not bless, 'Who commanded us concerning the four species.' This may be answered. See the beginning of the explanation that 'branches of date palms' alludes to the Holy One, blessed is He, and to the righteous one, the foundation of the world, see there. We have no clear knowledge of this. Perhaps Hashem will grant me merit to understand this, with the help of Heaven.

<b>ואם</b> עט״ז ובמ״א שמפרש דהיפוך יצא אף מי שאין אטר ובד״מ בשם הכל בו משמע כל אדם היפוך יצא ובש״ע מדלא הגיה הר״ב בס״ב כן משמע דוקא אטר וי״ל הטעם הואיל ורבנן תקנו כן לא יי״ח מדרבנן עכ״פ או אפשר אף מ״ה עיין ברבינו יונה ריש ברכות ותוס׳ ברכות י״א א׳ וסוכה גבי ראשו ורובו כו׳ דעבר מדרבנן מ״ה לא יי״ח די״ל לא תסור כו׳ משא״כ אטר דיש ספק פלוגתא איך שיעשה יי״ח דספק דרבנן לקולא כי כן התנו עיין בשורש הא׳ במגילת אסתר ולכאורה אטר והיפוך יטול בשתי ידיו ויצא ממ״נ. ועיין אות י״ד אם נטלן בידו אחת ד׳ מינים יצא דיעבד היינו שאין לחזור ולברך אף אם סח ומ״מ אין הפסד אם יטלם אחר כך כדינם ג׳ בימין ואתרוג בשמאל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See Taz and Magen Avraham, who explain that if one reversed the hands he fulfilled the obligation even if he is not left-handed. In Darkei Moshe, in the name of Kol Bo, it implies that any person who reversed them has fulfilled the obligation. From the fact that in Shulchan Aruch the Rav did not gloss so in seif 2, it implies that this applies specifically to a left-handed person. The reason may be that since the Sages instituted the matter this way, he has not fulfilled his obligation D'Rabbanan in any event, or perhaps even according to the Torah; see Rabbeinu Yonah at the beginning of Berachos, Tosafot, Berachos 11a, and Sukkah regarding one whose head and majority, etc., where since he transgressed D'Rabbanan he has not fulfilled according to the Torah, for one may say this is included in 'do not turn aside,' etc. This is unlike a left-handed person, where there is a doubtful dispute, and however he acts he fulfills, for a D'Rabbanan doubt is ruled leniently, since this was their stipulation; see the first root in Megillas Esther. At first glance, a left-handed person, or one who reverses the species, should take them in both hands and thereby fulfill according to all possibilities. See note 14: if he took all four species in one hand, he has fulfilled after the fact. This means that he need not return and bless, even if he spoke. Nevertheless, there is no loss if he then takes them afterward in their proper manner, three in the right hand and the esrog in the left.

<b>קודם</b> עט״ז תוס׳ סוכה ל״ט א׳ ד״ה עובר ובלבוש נקיט דרך ג׳ שיכוין שלא לצאת דבזה כ״ע מודים דלא יי״ח תי׳ הט״ז שישכח. והמ״א אות י״ב דפלוגתא היא בסימן סמ״ך ס״ד אפשר כמ״ש הב״ח בשם הב״י ס״ס תקפ״ט בשם הרא״ה והרשב״א ז״ל די״א אפי׳ עומד וצווח שא״י בה בע״כ יצא בה יע״ש אף דלא קיי״ל כן ועב״י תקפ״ט משמע דקיי״ל דצריך כוונה לחומרא א״כ לא יברך שנית ובצווח ודאי דלא יי״ח מברך שנית ומצה אכילה שאני דמ״מ נהנה. ובדרבנן עמ״א סמ״ך אות ג׳ בדרבנן מצות א״צ כוונה א״כ ביום ב׳ לא מהני הך תקנה וי״ל להיפך בדרבנן אף הרשב״א והרא״ה ז״ל מודים דצווח לא יי״ח דמ״ה בע״כ גזירת מלך הוא. ועמ״ש במקום אחר עלה דהאומר לולב שאין נוטל מכין עד שתצא נפשו אי בדרבנן כן ובש״ה כמדומה בכלל מכות מרדות כתבנו מזה אין מקומו פה ואי״ה לקמן בה׳ אלו יבואר מזה קצת עיין סי׳ תרנ״ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Before.</b> See Taz and Tosafot, Sukkah 39a, s.v. over. The Levush takes a third approach, that one should intend not to fulfill, for in that case all agree that he has not fulfilled. The Taz answers that he may forget. The Magen Avraham, note 12, writes that this is a dispute in siman 60, seif 4. It is possible as the Bach wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 589, in the name of the Raah and Rashba, that some say even if one stands and cries out that he does not want it, against his will he has fulfilled; see there. Although we do not rule this way, see Beit Yosef 589, which implies that we rule that intent is required as a stringency, and therefore he would not bless a second time. But if he explicitly protests, he certainly has not fulfilled and should bless a second time. Eating matzah is different, for in any event he derives benefit. Regarding D'Rabbanan matters, see Magen Avraham, siman 60, note 3, that D'Rabbanan mitzvos do not require intent; if so, on the second day this remedy would not help. One may say the opposite, that in D'Rabbanan matters even the Rashba and Raah agree that if he protests he has not fulfilled, for according to the Torah, fulfillment against his will is a decree of the King. I have written elsewhere regarding one who says, 'I will not take the lulav,' that he is beaten until his soul departs, whether this applies D'Rabbanan as well. In Sifsei Kohen, I believe under the rules of lashes for rebellious conduct, we wrote of this. This is not its place, and with G-d's help below in these laws something of this will be explained; see siman 656.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That there is not.</b> See Taz. See Be'er Heitev and Magen Avraham, note 18, who answer that there, whenever the hand is not covered, the intervening item is nullified relative to the hand; see there. Regarding practice, it seems at first glance from here that one removes tefillin before Mussaf, after Hallel and Torah reading. See siman 31 in the Taz, who writes that on Chol HaMoed Pesach as well one should remove them before Hallel, and Magen Avraham, siman 25, note 31, that on Pesach the shaliach tzibbur removes them after Hallel, and on Sukkos before Hallel, so as not to burden the congregation. But if there is no burden, it is proper for the shaliach tzibbur also to remove them before Hallel, and the congregation should remove them during the repetition of the prayer after Kedushah, and this is the proper practice. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 25, note 25, that on Rosh Chodesh this is after the Kedushah of U'va LeTziyon and before the Kaddishim. It implies there that the shaliach tzibbur should not remove them on Chol HaMoed Pesach while before the amud, for that is like removing them before his teacher. But on Sukkos, when they sell the esrog, he can step away from before the amud to one side and remove tefillin before Hallel; see there. In Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 28, page 2, he writes that it is proper to remove tefillin on Rosh Chodesh when one reaches 'May it be Your will that we observe,' not at the moment he wants to pray Mussaf; see there.

<b>The shaking.</b> See Taz. In the Ran, citing the Yerushalmi, it says that one must shake three times for each matter. Rabbi Zeira asked whether this direction is counted as one and that direction as one, or whether this and that together are counted as one. R. Yitzchak Giyyas explained that the Yerushalmi follows our Gemara, Sukkah 38a, where Rava says that the same applies to the lulav, one raises and lowers it, brings it and takes it out. Rabbi Zeira's question about three times for each matter may mean each place where we shake, namely the blessing, Hodu, Ana, and the final Hodu, and those are the three times; or it may mean whether taking out and bringing back count as two, while raising and lowering count as only one, since once he raises it he necessarily lowers it afterward, and therefore there are three, not four. Or perhaps taking out and bringing back also count as one, and according to this one needs two outward motions with two inward motions, plus raising and lowering, making three. The matter was not resolved. Shaking means taking out and bringing back, and does not necessarily require rustling or fluttering the leaves. See Rashi, Menachos 62a, regarding harmful winds to the east, west, north, and south, and harmful dews above and below. According to the view that the waving is to the One to Whom the world belongs, perhaps only one outward and inward motion is needed. But according to the explanation that it is to restrain harmful winds, four outward and inward motions are needed for the four directions, as Rashi explained in Menachos. In my humble opinion, Rabbi Zeira's question may be explained homiletically: does this direction count as one and that direction as one, meaning outward and inward, while with raising and lowering the main intention is only the raising, since lowering is unavoidable, or are all the motions one expression that the one Creator created everything. Still, the explanations of the earlier authorities are certainly true, each according to his approach. The difficulty is that if so, for a lulav 'long enough to shake,' mere outward and inward movement does not require that measure. The Rif and Rambam therefore explain that the teaching means one must shake for each motion, namely for every outward motion, inward motion, raising, and lowering, one must rustle the leaves three times. Rabbi Zeira asked whether outward and inward are counted as one, and likewise raising and lowering as one, requiring six rustlings, or whether each is counted separately, requiring twelve rustlings; we rule stringently, because of love for the mitzvah, or because there is no burden in doing so. A third view is that the rustling itself should be in the form of small outward and inward movements. First one performs large outward and inward movements, and likewise raises and lowers, perhaps in each of the four directions and above and below, as Rashi explained in Menachos, and afterward in each direction one again performs three small paired outward and inward movements. The Rosh, after citing the Rif, writes that this shaking is besides the outward and inward movement, and explains that for every direction, besides the large motion, there must be small paired movements within it. It emerges that there are different methods: according to one, leaf rustling alone suffices; according to another, small paired movements are required; according to the Rosh's short rulings, after one large outward motion he makes three small paired motions, and similarly after the large inward motion, totaling thirty-six small pairs for the six directions. This remains difficult, for perhaps only rustling of the leaves is required and Rabbi Zeira's question concerns whether the large outward and inward movement counts as one.

These appear contradictory, and this seems also to be the Magen Avraham's question in note 20, and with G-d's help it will be explained there. Maharshal concludes that one should do as follows: toward the east, make three large outward motions, perhaps because this is the meaning of three times for each matter, and because Rabbi Zeira's question is treated stringently. He should also rustle the leaves at least during one outward and inward motion, three rustlings outward and three inward, to be concerned for the second view. He should also, during one outward and inward motion, make six small paired movements in the form of outward and inward motions, to be concerned for the third explanation. But during the second and third large outward and inward motions toward the east, no shaking is needed, neither rustling nor small pairs, for this is merely extra according to all explanations, and he has fulfilled according to all views as stated. The Taz also writes stringently that the large inward motions should bring the lulav closer than where it was when he began the outward motion, for bringing it only back to its original place is not considered a genuine inward motion; since he took it out, he must bring it back to its place, so only bringing it closer to his heart is considered a proper inward motion. Perhaps this is hinted in Rabbi Zeira's question and in the Tur's version, according to the Sephardic reading, 'and brings it toward himself,' meaning closer than the lulav initially was. I have written at length and still have not fulfilled the obligation of explaining the matter; see Lechem Mishneh, chapter 4, halacha 10, in his explanation of the Maggid Mishneh, and Sefer Kappos Temarim.

First, it is comparable to the two loaves, and it appears so because Rava did not state a separate rule, 'a lulav is taken out and brought back,' but placed it in comparison to the two lambs; or because he did not say, 'and likewise a lulav,' as Rashi notes, but used the prefix meaning 'with,' which implies this.
