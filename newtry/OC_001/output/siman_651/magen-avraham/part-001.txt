**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In his right hand.</b> Because these three species constitute one mitzvah unit, while the esrog is a single species. Gemara. Some ask why this reason is needed, since every object over which one recites a blessing should be held in the right hand, as stated in siman 206, seif 4, and in chapter 8 of Berachos concerning oil and a myrtle. One may answer that the meaning is: the lulav is held in the right hand and the esrog in the left. Why are they not both held in the right hand? The answer is that these three, etc. Meaning, the verse grouped these three species separately, for it states, “a branch of a thick-leaved tree and willows of the brook,” whereas it does not place the conjunction “and” before “branches of date palms.” See what I wrote in seif 4. Alternatively, this teaches that even if he already recited the blessing and afterward takes it a second time, he must take it in his right hand, such as during the recitation of Hoshanos. See Maharil for further discussion.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The lulav in his arm, etc.</b> This resembles the law of one who lacks a thumb in Sanhedrin 45b, where the substance is placed upon the location of the missing thumb and he fulfills the requirement. This is preferable to taking the lulav and afterward the esrog, or applies where he has only a left hand or is left-handed. These are the words of Rokeach. This requires study. Since he has no hand at all, what does it mean that this is preferable to taking the lulav, etc.? In any event he would need to take it with his arm. Beit Yosef, citing Rokeach, emends the text to read: “An amputee who has only one hand,” etc. Beit Yosef understands that the word “only” is a scribal error, but in Rokeach the reading is: “An amputee who has only one hand,” etc. The explanation is that he takes the lulav in his hand and the esrog in his arm, and this is preferable, etc. Or, if he has only a left hand, he likewise takes the lulav in his left hand, which is now his right, and the esrog in his arm. If he is left-handed, he likewise takes the esrog in his own left hand, which is the right hand of ordinary people. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Orchos Chaim that if he took both species in one hand he did not fulfill his obligation, because the verse does not write “and branches of date palms”; see there. Nevertheless, Rokeach’s proof from Rabbi Eliezer requires study, because we rule like the first Tanna that one who lacks a thumb can never attain purification, as Rambam rules in chapter 5 of Hilchos Mechusrei Kapparah. Perhaps Rokeach follows the conclusion at the end of chapter 6 of Nazir that the positions are reversed; see Tosafos there, s.v. “as was taught.” He also follows Rabbeinu Tam that taking the species one after another does not fulfill the obligation, as is implied by his subsequent words. According to Rambam, however, it is preferable that he take them one after another in his hand, since this fulfills the obligation as stated in seif 12. Perhaps Rambam concedes here that taking it with the arm suffices, for does the verse state, “You shall take them in your hand”? A similar principle is stated regarding chalitzah in chapter 2 of Yevamos.

<b>And the extending and bringing back, etc.</b> This requires study, because above the ruling follows Ran that one shakes the lulav, etc., and Beit Yosef already wrote that these are differing opinions. Darkei Moshe implies that the meaning is that one need not shake it three times at the point to which it was extended. Rather, one extends and brings it back three times, and while extending and bringing it back he shakes the lulav, etc. He writes this there in the name of Raavyah. Bach maintains that it suffices to rustle the leaves while extending it, and this is the custom.

<b>דרך ימין</b>. ע' בלבוש והסי' ימין ה' רוממה ובכתבים איתא מזרח דרום צפון מעלה ומטה מערב ולעולם יעמוד בפניו למזרח [מ"צ] נ"ל דאיטר יקיף ג"כ כסדר הזה דאזלי' בתר ימין דעלמא וראיה ממשנה ב' פ"ב דמדות דאמרי' כל הנכנסין בהר הבית נכנסין דרך ימין וכו' חוץ ממי שאירעו דבר שהיה מקיף לשמאל וא"ל מה לך מקיף לשמאל מפני שאני אבל השוכן בבית זה הוא ינחמך וקשה מאי שאלה היא זו דלמא איטר הוא וע"ק דגם במקיף דרך ימין ה"ל לשואלו דלמא אבל הוא וזהו שמאל דידי' אלא ע"כ לא אזלינן בתר ימין דידי':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Turning to the right.</b> See Levush. The mnemonic is, “The right hand of Hashem is exalted.” The writings of the Arizal give the order: east, south, north, upward, downward, and west, while the person always faces east. Mateh Moshe. It appears to me that a left-handed person should follow this same order, because we follow the ordinary right side. A proof is the Mishnah in Middos 2:2: Everyone who enters the Temple Mount proceeds to the right, except one to whom an adverse event occurred, who circles to the left. They ask him, “Why are you circling to the left?” If he answers, “Because I am a mourner,” they say, “May the One Who dwells in this House comfort you.” One might ask: perhaps he is merely left-handed. Furthermore, even one circling to the right should be questioned, for perhaps he is a mourner and that direction is his left. It necessarily follows that we do not follow the individual’s own right side.

<b>ואם סח</b>. ז"ל הג"מ וע"ז כ' רבי' שמחה שאם שח בין מין למין חוזר ומברך עכ"ל מזה הלשון מוכח דר"ש לשיטתי' אזיל שפסק ג"כ שופר שאם סח חוזר ומברך כמ"ש הג"מ שם בשמו אבל לדידן קי"ל בסי' תקצ"ב דאינו מברך משום דחדא מצוה היא א"כ ה"נ אינו חוזר ומברך דהא חדא מצוה היא כדאיתא במנחות ואפשר כיון דהראב"ד ס"ל דאפי' לא סח מברך על כל אחד בפ"ע לכן פסק רמ"א לברך עכ"פ אם סח בנתיים: כתוב בא"ח מעשה בהר"מ שנטל לולב ובירך ואח"כ ראה שלא היה בו ערב' וחזר ונטלו עם הערבה ובירך על נטילת ערבה ושהחיינו משום ערבה נהי שא"צ ליטול אלא הערב' בלבד מ"מ מצוה מן המובחר ליטול הד' מינים בבת א' וכ"כ הראב"ד עכ"ל ב"י וצ"ע דבסי' תרע"ב כ' בשם א"ח אם לא הדליק בליל ד' אלא ג' נרות יחזור וידליק א' ולא יברך כי הברכה שעשה בתחלה על חיוב כל הנרות עשהו עכ"ל וכ"מ סי' ר"ו ס"ה וקע"ז ס"ד דאע"פ שלא היה לפניו בשעת ברכ' כיון שדעתו עליו ועסי' ר"ט ס"ב בהג"ה ולכן נ"ל דהא"ח וראב"ד לשיטתם אזלי דס"ל דצריך לברך על כל א' בפ"ע אבל לדידן א"צ לברך אא"כ סח בנתים ולכן השמיטו דין זה בש"ע וכ"כ הרד"א בשם הרמב"ם שאם בשעה שהדליק נר הראשון היה כוונתו להדליק אחרות א"צ לברך ואם בירך והדליק ואח"כ נזדמן לו נרות מברך בכל פעם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he spoke.</b> Hagahos Maimoniyos writes: Regarding this, Rabbeinu Simchah ruled that if one spoke between taking one species and the next, he must recite another blessing. This wording demonstrates that Rabbeinu Simchah follows his own view, for he also rules concerning shofar that if one spoke he must recite another blessing, as Hagahos Maimoniyos records there in his name. We, however, rule in siman 592 that he does not repeat the blessing because it is one mitzvah. The same should apply here, since the four species constitute one mitzvah, as stated in Menachos. Perhaps, because Raavad maintains that even without an interruption one recites a separate blessing over each species, Rama ruled that at least when one spoke between them he should recite another blessing. Orchos Chaim records an incident involving Maharam, who took the lulav and recited the blessing, and afterward discovered that it lacked a willow. He took it again with the willow and recited “concerning the taking of the willow” and Shehecheyanu because of the willow. Although strictly speaking he needed to take only the willow, it is the preferred manner of the mitzvah to take all four species together. Raavad writes likewise. These are the words cited by Beit Yosef. This requires study, because in siman 672 he writes in the name of Orchos Chaim that if on the fourth night one lit only three Chanukah lamps, he should return and light one more without a blessing, since the original blessing was recited over the obligation to light all the lamps. This is also implied by siman 206, seif 5, and siman 177, seif 4: even if the item was not before him when he recited the blessing, his blessing covers it when he had it in mind. See the gloss in siman 209, seif 2. It therefore appears to me that Orchos Chaim and Raavad follow their own view that one must recite a blessing over each species separately. According to our ruling, no additional blessing is needed unless he spoke in the interim. This is why this law was omitted from Shulchan Aruch. Abudraham likewise writes in Rambam’s name that if, when lighting the first lamp, he intended to light others, he need not recite another blessing. If he recited the blessing and lit, and only afterward additional lamps became available, he recites a blessing each time.

<b>משום בל תוסיף</b>. בטור כתוב דוקא אם נטלו לשם מצוה והקשה הרב"י דהא איתא בר"ה דף כ"ח דלעבור בזמנו לא בעי כונה עכ"ל ולכן סתם פה בש"ע לומר דאפי' לא נטלו לשם מצוה עובר ולא ראה מ"ש רי"ו נ"ג ח"ג דהנ"מ אליבא דמ"ד מצות א"צ כוונ' אבל לדידן דצריכים כוונה כמ"ש סי' ס' סעיף ד' אינו עובר עד שיכוין וכ"ה ר"פ י' דערובין בגמ' ודעת המ"מ והר"ן דאין עובר משום בל תוסיף דהאי לחודיא קאי והאי לחודיא קאי אלא דלכתחלה לא יעשה כן דנראה כמוסיף ואפשר שזהו דעת הטור והב"י ומ"ש הטור אם נטלו לשם מצות לולב כוונתו לתרץ מ"ש מאגד דשרי שלא במינו וע"ז כתב דהתם אינו עושה למצוה אלא לנוי בעלמא והתו' כתבו דשאני אגד דאינו נוטלו דרך גדילתו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because of adding to the mitzvah.</b> Tur writes that this applies specifically when he takes the additional species for the sake of the mitzvah. Beit Yosef asks that Rosh Hashanah 28b states that, during the proper time of a mitzvah, intent is not required in order to violate the prohibition against adding to it. Therefore, Shulchan Aruch writes without qualification that he violates the prohibition even if he did not take the additional species for the sake of the mitzvah. Beit Yosef did not see what Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in nesiv 3, part 3: that statement follows the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent. According to our ruling that mitzvos require intent, as stated in siman 60, seif 4, one does not violate the prohibition until he intends to add. The Gemara at the beginning of chapter 10 of Eruvin states the same. Maggid Mishneh and Ran maintain that one does not violate the prohibition against adding, because each species stands independently. Nevertheless, one should not initially do so, because it appears that he is adding to the mitzvah. Perhaps this is the position of Tur and Beit Yosef. When Tur states, “if he took it for the mitzvah of lulav,” his intention is to answer why binding the lulav with another species is permitted. There, the additional material is not used for the mitzvah but merely for beautification. Tosafos write that a binding is different because the additional material is not held in its manner of growth.
