<b>Subparagraph 12.</b> As is written in siman 60 se'if 4; and see the Bach. However, it appears to me that he should first take the lulav in his left hand and recite the blessing, and afterward also take the esrog. That is to say, the Bach wrote so, for according to the one who says mitzvos do not require intent, one may say that even if he cries out that he does not want to fulfill the mitzvah, he has fulfilled it. This is the view of the Rashba and the Ra'ah, as the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 589. The Bach also wrote regarding the two enactments written in the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, namely that he should take the lulav before the blessing without the esrog, or that he should turn the esrog upside down, that according to the view of Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Tur, who rule that if all of them are before him they do not prevent fulfillment and one may take them one after another, this too is not called before the performance. With this the view of the Rambam is settled, for he holds that one recites the blessing before taking the lulav. In truth, the view of Tosafos, the Rosh, and the other authorities is that it is not reasonable to recite the blessing before he takes it in his hand at all. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote: however, it appears to me that he should first take the lulav in his left hand. This is not considered taking so as to say that, since all of it is before him and they do not prevent fulfillment, it would count. In this case, it helps properly that his intent is not to fulfill with this taking, since he takes it in the left hand and his intent in this taking is to put it into his right hand. Therefore he properly recites the blessing afterward, and it is called before the performance. For even though they do not prevent fulfillment of one another, nevertheless this taking of the lulav itself has not yet been completed until he returns it to the right hand. This is all the more so according to what the Magen Avraham wrote above, that the view of Rabbeinu Chananel is that even after the fact he has not fulfilled his obligation with the lulav in the left hand. Nevertheless, there is no objection because it is not yet in his hand, for in any event it is already in his left hand, like tefillin, over which one recites the blessing from the time of placement, when the mitzvah is ready in his hand, even though he has not yet fulfilled it. So too, the fact that the lulav is in the left hand is the beginning of the mitzvah, and he properly recites the blessing over it. After the blessing he takes the esrog in his left hand and returns the lulav to the right hand, and the mitzvah is completed. This is what the Magen Avraham wrote, "and it turns out that he satisfies both of them," meaning that by doing so he satisfies the view of Tosafos and the Rosh, who hold that at the time of the blessing the mitzvah must be ready before him, and also the view of the Rambam, who holds that since they do not prevent fulfillment of one another, it is not before the performance even if he takes the lulav or turns the esrog over. That concern applies only when he takes the lulav in the manner of taking and in the right hand; but here, where he takes the lulav in the left hand so that the mitzvah should be ready before him, nevertheless there is not even completion of the mitzvah of taking the lulav alone, since it is not in the place of its mitzvah of taking, which is in the right hand, until the end when he gives it to the right hand. Thus the words of both are fulfilled. He then wrote: see the Shelah in the name of Even Shoev. This means what he brought above in subparagraph 1, that he should take the esrog first. This also works well according to this enactment, for even though he takes the lulav first, since he takes it in his left hand it is not considered taking until he returns it to the right hand. It follows that the esrog in the left hand precedes the taking of the lulav. This is unlike the enactment of the Shulchan Aruch for the blessing to be before the performance, which contradicts the words of Even Shoev that one should take the esrog first because the verse opened with it, "the fruit of a beautiful tree," since he first recites the blessing "on taking the lulav." And if he recites the blessing after taking the lulav, that too is not before the performance. Although one could say that after taking the esrog he recites the blessing "on taking the lulav," since it is ready before him and he takes it immediately, nevertheless this is not so, as is explained below regarding taking them one after another, that one must take the lulav first; and the reason appears to be because the blessing is "on taking the lulav." The enactment of the Magen Avraham is preferable, for although he already took the lulav, it is not considered taking, only prepared for the blessing; and when he takes the esrog in the left hand, which is its proper mitzvah, he fulfills the verse as it is written, with the esrog preceding the lulav that he returns to the right hand. I have been brief. If we wish to uphold the text before us, which says that he should take from his fellow, etc., nevertheless it is obvious that one must also add the word "and recite the blessing," as I wrote above. See Hagahos She'arei HaGaon, by the author of Mekor Chayim, who wrote that this phrase, "however, it appears to me," is not in its place but belongs above in subparagraph 8. According to this, one must greatly press the meaning of what is written, "and he satisfies both of them." Also, we see that he concludes there, "and see the Shelah in the name of Even Shoev," and the Shelah then repeats these matters in subparagraph 8, which are in the name of Mateh Moshe, and they are the words of Even Shoev. The correct text should be: he should first take the lulav from his fellow in his left hand and recite the blessing, and afterward take the esrog, etc. My intent is that it is possible to repair what the Bach wrote, that since they do not prevent fulfillment of one another, it was not before the performance. However, this applies only to one who recites the blessing over his own lulav and esrog, for since it is before him, it does not prevent fulfillment. But when he takes from his fellow in order to recite the blessing over it, this concern is removed, for at first his fellow gives him only the lulav and he recites the blessing over it, and afterward he goes back and gives him the esrog. Since the esrog is not yet his, it certainly prevents fulfillment until he also takes the esrog. If so, even with his own esrog there is this remedy: he should give the esrog to his fellow, and his fellow should first return to him the lulav, and he should recite the blessing over it, and afterward the esrog. Yet there is still some hesitation in the matter according to what Even Shoev wrote, that one must take the esrog first; if so, he cannot recite the blessing "on taking the lulav" until he also receives the lulav, and it is not before the performance. Therefore he said that he should first take the lulav from his fellow in his left hand and recite the blessing, so that he satisfies both views: he recited the blessing before the performance, since he still has not received the esrog from his fellow, and because it is still in his fellow's hand it properly prevents fulfillment; and it is also considered as though he took the esrog first, in accordance with Even Shoev, since receiving the lulav in the left hand is not considered precedence. According to this, it is close to say that in the words of the Magen Avraham, the phrase "however, it appears to me" belongs after what he wrote, "and see the Shelah, what he wrote in the name of Even Shoev." That is to say, here he wrote an enactment that one should take the lulav first, while according to the words of Even Shoev one must take the esrog first; about this he wrote: however, it appears to me that he should first take the lulav in the left hand and recite the blessing, and afterward, etc. And because the Shelah added to the words of Even Shoev that the prayer leader who hands it to the one who purchased the mitzvah must move the lulav first, as he wrote in subparagraph 8, therefore here too he used the case of taking it from his fellow. Nevertheless, the same applies when one recites the blessing for himself; he must do so. It appears more likely that the word "from his fellow" is a scribal error, as I wrote, and this is why he concludes that through this he satisfies both of them. See the book Divrei Malkiel, who wondered about the Magen Avraham in subparagraph 8, that he did not pay attention to what the Rambam, Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Tur wrote, and here it is explicit that one should take the lulav first. According to what I wrote, it appears that the Magen Avraham here sensed that the words of these authorities contradict what the Shelah wrote in the name of Even Shoev, and therefore he wrote an enactment to satisfy both of them. See above se'if 12 regarding if he took them one after another; the Rema also wrote, "and he should take the lulav first," etc., and this is from the words of the Rosh. There it is obvious that the intent is that he should take the lulav in the right hand; according to Even Shoev he should have preceded the esrog. One must therefore say that in this case even Even Shoev agrees that one must take the lulav first, since he is not now taking the esrog and the wording of the blessing is "on taking the lulav"; therefore he must take the lulav first. And what the Darkei Moshe wrote about the words of the Mateh Moshe, that what he wrote that one removes the lulav first is an error, for in Menachos regarding tefillin a derivation is needed that one removes the head tefillin first, see the Mateh Moshe there, who concludes: as is written regarding tefillin, "and it shall be for a sign upon your hand," all the time that the totafos are between your eyes; and likewise all the time that the lulav is in the right hand. They would have both been equal in holiness. In truth this is not forced, for regarding tefillin it is derived from the word "and it shall be," which implies two, as Rashi wrote there.
