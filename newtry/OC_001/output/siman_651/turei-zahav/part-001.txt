<b>And if he wrapped a cloth around it, etc.</b> In Tosafos, page 37, in the dispute of Rabbah and Rava regarding taking by means of another item, they wrote that what we require here, complete taking, cannot be explained as meaning that there should be no interposition, as above. For above it is speaking when he binds the lulav, and here it is when he wraps his hand; and with something wrapped around his hand, interposition does not apply. They rejected this explanation, and one may say that it is speaking where he made from the cloth a kind of handle on the lulav, and interposition does not apply here, for the entire lulav is outside his hand and he holds the protruding handle made from the cloth; and Rava considers this complete taking. And the lulav that he carried out in a vessel at the end of the chapter is not where he held it by the sides of the vessel, but where he placed his hand under its bottom or held the handle of the vessel, end quote. The explanation of their words, in what they conclude, "and the lulav that he carried out," etc., appears as follows. What they wrote earlier, that it is speaking where he made the cloth like a handle, means that he benefits from the cloth in holding the lulav. That is, the lulav itself with the hadas and aravah is difficult for him to hold, as the Tur wrote at the end of siman 651, that they practiced taking 68 aravos. Therefore he wraps a cloth around the lulav and the end of that cloth is in his hand, and through this he holds the lulav like tongs, whether part of the lulav is inside the cloth at the place he holds, or even if all of it emerges from the place he holds. In any case, by holding the cloth he has assistance in holding the lulav, and it is permitted, for it is considered a handle. This is also explicit in their words at the end of chapter Makom Shenahagu, where they wrote: Rabbeinu Yitzchak answered that there it is speaking where he holds the lulav by means of the cloth like tongs, and he holds the cloth, for the cloth assists him in holding it, and in such a case it is not an interposition, etc. So too here, this is the intent of Tosafos. About this they said that it is complete taking according to Rava, and it is also not an interposition. About this they had difficulty: why do we say at the end of the chapter that if he carried it out in a vessel, it is not complete taking? How is the cloth here different from the vessel there? Granted, according to the first answer it is fine, for here it is speaking specifically where the hand is wrapped. But according to the concluding answer it is difficult. About this they wrote that if it were speaking there where he holds the sides of the vessel, meaning that the lulav is inside the hollow of the vessel and he holds it by the sides of the vessel from above, where this is useful for him in holding it, it would be like holding the lulav, since he benefits from this, and it would be a handle. In that case it would certainly be permitted, like here with the cloth. Rather, there it is speaking where he places his hand under its bottom, and in this he has no benefit from the vessel; and likewise if he holds the handle of the vessel, then if you say that the vessel is a handle, he holds a handle for a handle, and this does not look like tongs. In this case it certainly is not taking, and that is what is being discussed there. But if he held the sides of the vessel itself from above, it would be like tongs. It follows that there is no difference between a cloth and a vessel: wherever it is like a handle it is permitted, even with a vessel; and where it is not like a handle it is forbidden, even with a cloth. Therefore the Tur brought this, and this is the distinction in the Gemara, and the Tur copied it: taking by means of another item is called taking; therefore, even if he made a handle and placed the lulav in it and took it, it is permitted, even if the entire lulav emerges from beneath his hand, provided that it is in a respectful manner. But if it is not in a respectful manner, such as if he placed it in a vessel and took it, he has not fulfilled his obligation, end quote. The explanation of his words is that since we maintain generally that taking by means of another item is called taking, therefore if he took it by means of another item in a way that it is a handle, it is permitted, even though the whole lulav is outside his hand. One might have said that there is no taking here; nevertheless it is permitted. This is specifically in a respectful manner, meaning that it is useful for holding it, and he holds that it is a handle. But if it is not a handle, rather he simply placed it in a vessel and not for the benefit of holding the lulav, this is not respect and is forbidden. Therefore the Tur did not distinguish within the vessel itself, since he had already written that it is specifically in the manner of a handle. According to this, there is no permission in the world except where it is made like tongs. But if he wrapped his hand in a cloth and took the lulav, it is certainly forbidden because of interposition, and because of this Yissachar of Kefar Barkai was punished; it is not as Tosafos needed to press themselves according to the first explanation. So too they wrote explicitly in chapter Hotziu Lo and in chapter Makom Shenahagu, and there they distinguish between holding the rim of the vessel and holding its bottom. Their intent here is also the same. All this appears clear to me with the help of Heaven. But the Beis Yosef explains the words of Tosafos in another way. He wrote: it is not speaking where he holds the sides of the vessel, for if so the Gemara would exclude it because of interposition; rather, it is speaking where he places his hand under its bottom or holds the handle of the vessel, where there is no interposition, but only because it is not complete taking even according to Rava, because it is a disgraceful manner. Therefore with any vessel, whether he holds the sides of the vessel or places his hand under its bottom, he has not fulfilled his obligation: if he holds the sides of the vessel, also because of interposition; and if he places his hand under its bottom, because of complete taking. Therefore our master the Tur did not bring a distinction between holding the sides of the vessel and placing his hand under its bottom, end quote. With pardon for the honor of our master the Beis Yosef, he did not descend to the truth of the words of Tosafos in this. According to his explanation, why did Tosafos write that there it is not speaking of holding the sides of the vessel, because in that case it would be invalid not because of incomplete taking but because of interposition? If so, it will be difficult on the Talmud: who brought it into this matter, to establish Rava's statement as a case where he carried it out in its vessel, so that he did not fulfill his obligation, and on this it asks there that he should have fulfilled it, since taking by means of another item is called taking? It should have answered that Rava is speaking in every case where he carried it out in its vessel and did not fulfill his obligation, whether because it is an interposition or because it is not complete taking. Rava did not mention either the reason of interposition or the reason of incomplete taking. What forced the Talmud to establish Rava's reason specifically as incomplete taking, so that it is speaking only where he did not hold the sides of the vessel? Why not say that Rava is truly speaking in every case, and in every manner with a vessel he has not fulfilled his obligation? Furthermore, there is no place for his explanation, for why should there be more interposition when he holds the sides of the vessel than when he holds the handle of the vessel or its bottom? This is only astonishing. According to what I wrote, everything is fine. What it writes here, "and if he wrapped a cloth around it and took it," means not in the manner of a handle, and about this the view of Tosafos and the Tur is that he has not fulfilled his obligation. And what the Tur says, "even if the entire lulav is outside his hand," is a novelty regarding taking, that it is considered taking even in this case. But regarding interposition, there is more advantage if it is outside his hand than if it is partly in his hand. Therefore Tosafos wrote that there is no interposition here, for the entire lulav is not in his hand. Because of this it would seem that there is only a concern of taking, and they conclude that even so it is permitted because it is like a handle.

<b>The shaking is, etc.</b> In the Yerushalmi we say: it was taught that one must shake three times for each matter. There are opinions about this. What the Ran wrote is that Rav Yehudai Gaon holds that one need not rustle the leaves at all, only extend outward and bring inward, raise and lower. With this there are three times, for raising and lowering are one, since it is impossible to raise without lowering, and extending outward and bringing inward are counted as two. The Rif and the Rambam hold that one must rustle the leaves apart from extending outward and bringing inward, and regarding rustling the leaves it says that it should be three times. In each direction there is one outward movement and one inward movement, without rustling the leaves; and during that outward movement that he does, he should make three small motions by rustling the leaves, and likewise during the inward movement. It follows that in each direction there is one outward movement and one inward movement and six small motions. Raising and lowering are also done in this manner. A third opinion is that those small motions that we mentioned should themselves contain outward and inward movement. That refers to outward and inward movement, and here there is only one outward and inward movement, while the minimal shaking alone is three times. Rather, certainly the three times refers to the outward and inward movement, meaning that it must be three times in each direction. About this Rabbi Zeira asked whether in each direction three outward movements and three inward movements are required, with some minimal shaking in each outward and inward movement, or whether an outward movement, inward movement, and outward movement in each direction suffice, and this is called three times, with a small shaking in each one. We go stringently, that in each direction he should perform three outward movements and three inward movements, and each time make a small shaking. But the language of the Tur does not imply that he explains the Rosh this way, for he wrote: "and he shakes there three times during the outward movement and three times during the inward movement," implying that the shaking itself contains outward and inward movement. According to our books of the Tur, where it is not written "and brings it toward himself and does so three times," we can explain well that the phrase "three times" has no attachment to what precedes it, but to what follows it, and this is what he says: he extends his hand from opposite himself outward and shakes there, meaning whatever shaking, and does so three times in outward movement and three times in inward movement, meaning he does so three times in outward movement, extending three times and shaking each time, and likewise inward movement three times, shaking each time. What the Tur writes afterward, "and raises his hand upward and does so three times," means that he raises three times and each time shakes a little. However, in the abbreviated rulings of the Rosh he did not write this way. It appears that the version "and brings it toward himself," etc., can also be settled: three outward movements and three inward movements are required in each direction, but if he only extends outward and there makes three outward and inward movements, the inward movements are not counted, since it is impossible to extend outward unless he afterward brings inward, and we maintain that outward and inward movement are one. Therefore he must afterward make an inward movement after completing the outward movement, and in that inward movement he should also make three outward and inward movements. Only the inward movements are counted, because it is impossible to make an inward movement without an outward movement. It follows that we have only the three upper outward movements and the three lower inward movements on one side. According to this, one need make in one direction only one large outward movement with three small pairs in it, and likewise one large inward movement with three pairs in it, and no more. In Responsa Maharshal, siman 83, he wrote on the words of the Rosh as follows: the explanation of his explanation is whether he needs outward movement together with inward movement, or three times altogether, meaning outward, inward, and outward. About this he wrote that we go stringently and require three times outward movement with inward movement. According to his explanation, the shaking is the outward and inward movement, and so it is explicit in Orchos Chayim, end quote. But the Tur does not imply this at all, for he wrote that he extends his hand from opposite himself outward and shakes there three times, implying that outward movement and shaking are two separate matters. This is how the Beis Yosef understood it, and this is correct. Furthermore, the Rosh already brought the words of the Rif, who wrote that the shaking is apart from extending outward and bringing inward. According to the Maharshal's explanation, there would be no shaking here except the three outward and inward movements, which are the extending outward and bringing inward mentioned in our Gemara. In what manner, then, do you find shaking apart from extending outward and bringing inward?

<b>לא יוסיף כו'. </b> בטור כתוב לא יפחות מהן ולא יוסיף כתב ב"י אינו מדוקדק לשון רבינו ל"ל לכתוב לא יפחות דהא כבר כתב ד' מינים מעכבין זא"ז בשלמא בגמרא אמר כשם שאין פוחתין כך אין מוסיפין וקמ"ל רבותא לענין אין מוסיפין אפי' בלא אגד אבל אין פוחתין ליכא רבותא ונ"ל דקמ"ל רבותא דמיירי אפי' כבר נטל כל הד' מינים ויצא בהן בשעת נטילה אלא שאח"כ ביום משום חבוב מצוה רוצה לתפוס הלולב בידו כדאית' לקמן סי' תרנ"ב שיש חבוב מצוה בזה עז"ק שלא יקח ג' מינים לחוד לשם מצוה דעובר על לא תגרעו ובגמ' הוצרך לרבותא אחרת ולא אמר רבותא שזכרנו דאמר שם כשם שאין פוחתין כך אין מוסיפין משמע דיש בהוספה רבותא אחריתא ממה שיש בפחת כ"ז נ"ל נכון מאד: <b>אחר </b> שכתבתי זה ראיתי שהקש' ב"י על רבינו הטו' במ"ש אם יאחזנו בידו עמהם לשם מצות לולב איכ' בל תוסיף הא קי"ל בס"פ ראוהו ב"ד דלעבור בזמנו לא צריך כוונה פי' דלענין לעבור על בל תוסיף עובר אפי' אם לא נתכוין לשם מצוה ולמה כתב הטור דוקא נתכוין לשם מצות לולב עובר הא אפי' בלא נתכוין עובר: <b>אמרתי </b> ע"ז שישו בני מעי אם על ספיקו' שלכם כך ק"ו על הודאי כי במ"ש ליישב הקושי' ראשונה של הב"י שהיא מכח דקדוק הלשון הנה מוכח כן בפירוש מכח קושיא השניה מצד הדין ובאמת שניהם מתורצים בדבר אחד דגם כאן נתכוין הטור למ"ש דמיירי אחר הברכה רק מכח חבוב מצוה אז ודאי צריך כוונה כדאיתא בס"פ ראוהו ב"ד והא דזכר רבינו הטור בזה איכא בל תוסיף ולא זכר בל תגרע לפי שלפעמים נוטל אפי' פחות מד' מינים דהיינו בשעה שאין לו כולם אז נוטל את מה שיש לו כדי שלא תשתכח תורת לולב כדאיתא לקמן אבל במוסיף יש איסור אם כוון לשם מצוה ואם יש לו כל ד' מינים ונוטל פחות משום חיבוב מצוה אה"נ דעובר על לא תגרע והטור נקט מילתא דפסיקא דהיינו במוסיף: <b>ראיתי </b> למו"ח ז"ל שטרח הרבה לתרץ קושיית ב"י והוא ללא צורך ומ"ש נראה ברור בס"ד. ונ"ל דאם אין נתכוין להוסיף דהיינו לשם מצוה דאז אינו עובר אבל מ"מ יזהר שאפי' בלא מתכוין לשם מצוה לא יוסיף דמ"מ נראה כמוסיף ודומה לאוכל בסוכה בח' כדלקמן סי' תרס"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should not add, etc.</b> In the Tur it is written: one should not subtract from them and should not add. The Beis Yosef wrote: our master's language is not precise; why did he need to write "one should not subtract," since he already wrote that the four species prevent fulfillment of one another? Granted, in the Gemara it says, "just as one may not subtract, so one may not add," and it teaches us a novelty regarding not adding, even without binding; but with not subtracting there is no novelty. It appears to me that he teaches us a novelty, that it is speaking even where one already took all four species and fulfilled his obligation with them at the time of taking, but afterward during the day, because of affection for the mitzvah, he wants to hold the lulav in his hand, as is stated below in siman 652 that there is affection for the mitzvah in this. About this he states that he should not take three species alone for the sake of the mitzvah, for he transgresses "do not subtract." In the Gemara another novelty was needed, and it did not state the novelty that we mentioned, for it says there, "just as one may not subtract, so one may not add," implying that with adding there is another novelty beyond what there is with subtracting. All this appears very correct to me. <b>After</b> I wrote this, I saw that the Beis Yosef challenged our master the Tur regarding what he wrote: if he holds it in his hand with them for the sake of the mitzvah of lulav, there is bal tosif. But we maintain at the end of chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din that to transgress in its time one does not need intent. That means that regarding transgressing bal tosif, one transgresses even if he did not intend for the sake of the mitzvah. Why, then, did the Tur write specifically that if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah of lulav he transgresses, since even without intent he transgresses? <b>I said</b> about this: rejoice, my innards, if this is so for your doubts, all the more so for certainty. For in what I wrote to settle the first difficulty of the Beis Yosef, which was from precision in the wording, this is now proven explicitly from the second difficulty from the legal side. In truth, both are answered with one matter: here too the Tur intended what I wrote, that it is speaking after the blessing, only because of affection for the mitzvah. Then certainly intent is required, as is stated at the end of chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din. And the reason our master the Tur mentioned that in this case there is bal tosif and did not mention bal tigra is because sometimes one takes even fewer than four species, namely when he does not have all of them, he takes what he has so that the Torah of lulav should not be forgotten, as is stated below. But with adding there is a prohibition if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah. And if he has all four species and takes fewer because of affection for the mitzvah, indeed he transgresses "do not subtract." The Tur took a definite case, namely adding. <b>I saw</b> that my father-in-law, of blessed memory, labored greatly to answer the Beis Yosef's difficulty, but this was unnecessary. What I wrote appears clear with Heaven's help. It appears to me that if he did not intend to add, meaning for the sake of the mitzvah, then he does not transgress. Nevertheless, he should be careful that even without intending for the sake of the mitzvah, he should not add, for in any event it appears like adding, and it resembles one who eats in the sukkah on the eighth day, as below in siman 666.
