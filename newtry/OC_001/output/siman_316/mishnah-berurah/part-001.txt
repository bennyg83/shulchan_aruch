(א) הצד וכו' - היינו שפתח שער המגדול ועשה תחבולות עד שנכנס הצפור לתוכו וסגר עליו אפילו לא תפסו עדיין בידו חייב כי בסגרו המגדול נגמרה הצידה אבל במה שהכניסו לבית אפילו הבית קטן וגם חלונותיו סתומין עדיין אינו נצוד שטבעו של הצפור דרור שאינו מקבל מרות ודר בבית כבשדה שנשמט מזוית לזוית ואינם יכולים לתפסו ולכן אינו נקרא צידה מדאורייתא:

(א) One who traps, etc. — namely that he opened the gate of the tower and made snares until the bird entered into it and he closed upon it — even if he did not yet seize it in his hand he is liable, for in closing the tower the trapping was completed. But in what he brought it into a house — even a small house and also its windows closed — it is still not trapped, for the nature of the dror bird is that it does not accept authority and dwells in a house like in a field, slipping from corner to corner, and they cannot catch it; and therefore it is not called trapping d'oraisa:

(ד) That they are trapped — to exclude if the house and vivarium are large so they cannot catch it in one stride — namely one run — but must catch breath before reaching it; it is not called trapping d'oraisa even if it is roofed; and automatically one who traps it there in a house or vivarium is liable. See below in Mishna Berurah s.k. 58. One who scoops a fish from the sea into a bowl of water is liable, for it is trapping; but if he uprooted it and chased it into a pool of water — it is not trapping, for there too it slips into holes and cracks. (ח) But a cow and horse, etc. — meaning that regarding cow and horse that are species of domestic animals — trapping does not apply at all, even rabbinically, even if he acquired them anew and they are not yet accustomed to come to his house, for they are not made to slip from a person's hand; and permitted to seize them and close them in a narrow place. And regarding other beast and bird in his possession that are made to slip from a person's hand — even though they do not need a trap to catch them, for they will come on their own to their house in the evening — nevertheless there is rabbinic prohibition of trapping; and all this when they did not rebel. But when they rebelled — even cow and horse are liable to chatas for trapping them, even if they grew up from the outset in his house; and this is what he wrote "and all the more so other beast and bird," etc. — namely when the cow and horse rebelled completely and fled from his possession until he must seek a trap or some snares to catch them; and also they do not come in the evening to his house:

(ט) And all the more so, etc. that rebelled — it also deals when they rebelled and fled and do not return to his house in the evening but sleep in the field like geese and gander that sleep on the water; and difficult to catch them without trap and snare; and also the Mechaber agrees that in this one who traps them is liable to chatas for trapping. And later authorities wrote that from this we learn that the same applies if he bought geese and chickens anew and they are not yet accustomed at all in his house — if they went out they will not return to the house but peck in the garbage — one who traps them is liable.
