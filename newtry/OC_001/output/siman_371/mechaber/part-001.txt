**** ENGLISH ****
When One of the Courtyard Residents Leaves or Dies — 6 Sections One of the courtyard residents who left his house and went to spend Shabbat in another courtyard — even one adjacent to his own — if he put it out of his mind and does not intend to return to his house on Shabbat, he does not restrict them. This applies to a Jew, but a non-Jew — even if he went to spend Shabbat in another city — still restricts them until they rent his place from him, if it is within one day's journey, since it is possible he may come on Shabbat. But if it is further away, he does not restrict. Some say that a non-Jew too, if he went to spend Shabbat in another courtyard, does not restrict. [And this appears to be the primary ruling] [And if the non-Jew does come on Shabbat, see above Siman 383]

**** ENGLISH ****
An outsider who had a house in the courtyard and died, leaving his domain to one of the courtyard residents — if he died while it was still day, the inheritor does not restrict, since the eruv he made covers his own house and also permits what he inherited. But if he died after nightfall, he restricts, and even though the inheritor had made an eruv with them, it does not help for what he inherited afterward on Shabbat.

**** ENGLISH ****
One of the courtyard residents who died on Shabbat — if he had made an eruv, even if an outsider inherits from him, he does not restrict. If he had not made an eruv: if an outsider inherits from him, as long as the outsider does not come to live with them he does not restrict; if he comes to live on Shabbat in the house of his benefactor, he restricts. If one of the courtyard residents inherits from him — if the inheritor had made an eruv and was living together with the deceased in his house, he does not restrict even though the deceased had not made an eruv. But if he was not living with the deceased but in another house in the courtyard, he restricts even though the inheritor had made an eruv, since the deceased had not made an eruv. If the deceased died while it was still day and an outsider inherits from him, the outsider restricts — provided he comes to the deceased's house on Shabbat.

**** ENGLISH ****
A Jew and a convert who were living in a courtyard, and the convert died while it was still day and a Jew took possession of his property — even if he did not take possession until after nightfall, he restricts until he renounces his domain like any other inheritor. If the convert died after nightfall, the one who took possession does not restrict, since the original permitted status remains in force.

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="נ" data-order="14"></i>א' מבני חצר שהיה גוסס <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="לב"></i>אף על פי שאינו יכול לחיות בו ביום אוסר עד שיזכו לו בפת <i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="8"></i><i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="4"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="לג"></i>ויערבו עליו <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="לד"></i>וכן קטן אוסר אע"פ שאינו יכול לאכול כזית אבל האורח אינו אוסר כמו שנתבאר בסי' ש"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
One of the courtyard residents who was dying — even though he cannot survive the day — he restricts until bread is acquired on his behalf and an eruv is made for him. Similarly, a minor restricts even though he cannot eat an olive's worth. But a guest does not restrict, as explained in Siman 370.
