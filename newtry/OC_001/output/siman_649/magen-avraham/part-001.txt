**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Prohibited for eating.</b> This implies that he holds that even though it is permitted for benefit, since it is prohibited for eating it is invalid all seven days. As for Tosafot's question, why is a reason needed by a lulav of idolatry because its required measure is considered crushed; let him derive it from the fact that it has no monetary law? The Mechaber answered this by writing that that esrog, etc., is prohibited for eating; meaning that specifically by esrog, since it is fit for eating, we expound 'for yourselves' to mean it must be yours to eat. But by lulav, which is not fit to eat, such an exposition is not relevant, and since it is his, even though it is prohibited for benefit, we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it. An esrog of orlah is invalid. [Gemara] Orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is valid, for he can ab initio feed it to his fellow who does not recognize it. [Shiltei Gibborim] So too appears to be the view of the poskim, who omitted this case. But according to what the Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 294, that it is prohibited to feed it to his fellow, if so it is invalid. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should validate it, since the plain meaning of the Talmud implies that he may feed it to his fellow, and one may rely on that regarding esrog. An esrog that became prohibited because it absorbed a prohibition is invalid. [Shiltei Gibborim] This is not compelling, for one can say that only when its prohibition comes from itself is it invalid. Therefore it appears to me that one should validate it on the other days, for Tosafot, Rashi, and Kol Bo validate even one of orlah on the other days, and therefore one may rely on them in such a case. An esrog of demai is valid; regarding tevel, see the Ran.

<b>לולב יבש</b>. אבל אתרוג יבש לא וכ"מ מלשון המרדכי [ד"מ] אבל במ"ב סימן י"ז כתב דהטור והרא"ש ורי"ו וכל בו ס"ל דכל ד' מינים יבשים כשרים בשעת הדחק וה"ה שאר פסולים והא דלא כ' המרדכי הדין באתרוג יבש משום דשכיח בכל השנ' אתרוגי' חדשים והא דמברכין אלולבים והדסים אפי' איכא אחרים לחים משום שהלחים אינן לחים גמורים רק שיש בהם מיעוט לחות וא"כ הלחים והיבשים כמעט דומין זה לזה אבל אי איכא לחי' גמורים אפי' היבשים אינן יבשים גמורים אין לברך ובדיעבד אם בירך על יבש איכא לספוקי אם יצא ולכן יחזור ויטול לח בלא ברכה אבל אם בירך על יבשים גמורי' ואיכ' לחי' גמורי' משמע שם אפי' בדיעבד לא יצא עכ"ל ודבריו בנוים ע"פ הפוסקים האחרונים לכן אין להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A dry lulav.</b> But not a dry esrog, and so it appears from the language of Mordechai. [Darkei Moshe] But in the Mishnah Berurah, siman 17, he wrote that the Tur, the Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Kol Bo hold that all four dry species are valid in pressing circumstances, and the same applies to other invalidations. The fact that the Mordechai did not write the law regarding a dry esrog is because throughout the year new esrogim are common. And the reason one blesses over lulavim and hadasim even when there are other moist ones is because the moist ones are not fully moist, only having a small amount of moisture; if so, the moist and dry ones are almost similar to one another. But if there are fully moist ones, even if the dry ones are not completely dry, one should not bless. After the fact, if one blessed over a dry one, there is doubt whether he fulfilled the obligation; therefore he should return and take a moist one without a blessing. But if he blessed over completely dry ones while fully moist ones were available, it appears there that even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation. End quote. His words are built on the later poskim; therefore one should not be lenient.

<b>דחסר כשר</b>. אם נשאר בו כשיעור כדי לצאת בו [טור] וכת' הר"ן דוק' כשנשתייר רובו ושמו עליו וכו' אבל בפסקי מהרא"י סי' צ"ב משמע אפי' נשאר מיעוטא סגי עכ"ל ד"מ וז"ל מהרא"י אף על גב דחסר כשר בשעת הדחק היינו שנחסר מאליו אבל לא שרי לחתוך האתרוג  ולברך על אות' חתיכ' ותו הא דחסר כשר היינו שחותך לאכילה ומברך על השאר דהוי שיורי מצו' כדאשכחן גבי ציצית אבל לחתוך מן האתרוג ולברך על חתיכ' אסור דלאו אתרוג מיקרי אלא חתיכת אתרוג עכ"ל משמע דאפי' לא נשתייר רובו כשר רק שישאר כביצ' וכ"ד הטו' ורמ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For a deficient one is valid.</b> If enough of it remains for the required measure with which to fulfill the obligation. [Tur] The Ran wrote: specifically when most of it remains and its name is still upon it, etc. But in the rulings of Mahari, siman 92, it implies that even if a minority remains it is sufficient. End quote of Darkei Moshe. And this is the language of Mahari: even though a deficient one is valid in pressing circumstances, that is when it became deficient on its own, but it is not permitted to cut the esrog and bless over that piece. Furthermore, that which a deficient one is valid means that he cuts it for eating and blesses over the remainder, which is the remnant of a mitzvah, as we find regarding tzitzit. But to cut from the esrog and bless over the piece is prohibited, for it is not called an esrog but a piece of esrog. End quote. This implies that even if most of it does not remain it is valid, provided only that an egg-bulk remains. This is the view of the Tur and Rema.
