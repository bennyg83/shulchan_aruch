<b>From the first day onward.</b> These are the words of Rava in the Gemara, who said on page 31 that a lulav of idolatry, which was used for idolatry, should not be taken, and if one took it he has fulfilled his obligation. The Ran and the Maggid explained that this is on the second day of Yom Tov, when a person fulfills with a borrowed one. This is difficult to me, for there they challenge Rava from the Mishnah that teaches that one from an asherah is invalid, and it answers that it is an asherah of Moshe, etc. According to this, there is no challenge at all, for before this, at the beginning of the chapter, Rava holds like Rabbi Yitzchak bar Nachmani, who said regarding the Mishnah that teaches a stolen one is invalid: they taught this only regarding the first day of Yom Tov, but on the second day of Yom Tov, since one fulfills with a borrowed one, one fulfills with a stolen one. It is stated there: Rava said: actually, on the first day of Yom Tov, etc. If so, here too regarding asherah, it is certainly speaking of the first day of Yom Tov, for they are in the same Mishnah. From the words of the Ran it implies that he did not read "Rava said: actually, on Yom Tov," but rather "Rav Ashi," for he wrote that the Razah rules like Rabbi Yitzchak bar Nachmani because Rav Ashi answered according to him, meaning the answer above, "actually, on the first day of Yom Tov." And the reason it is valid after the first day, even though it is forbidden for benefit, is that mitzvos were not given for benefit. <b>It is written</b> in the Levush: there are those who want to validate those esrogim that come from the orchard called Gertiza. I say that they are invalid by Torah law, for it is known that they are grafted, where a branch from an esrog tree was grafted into a marantz or lemon tree, or the reverse. A transgression of grafting was therefore performed with it. Even though a gentile grafted it, there is one who says in chapter Dalet Misos that even a Noachide is commanded regarding grafting a tree. Since a transgression was performed with it, even though it is permitted for eating it is repulsive to the Most High, similar to a crossbred animal, which, even though it is permitted for eating, is forbidden as an offering because a transgression was performed with it and it is repulsive to the Most High. So too here, so it appears to me, end quote. This is difficult for me. If so, what good is that which Rava said to those merchants who sold hadas, that when you buy myrtle from gentiles, you should not cut it yourselves, but they should cut it and give it to you? What is the reason? Ordinary gentiles are robbers of land, etc., as stated on page 30. Ultimately, according to his words, the gentile performed a transgression and it is repulsive to the Most High, for Noachides were commanded regarding robbery. Furthermore, in siman 11 regarding the law of tzitzis, it says that flax planted for idolatrous worship is valid for tzitzis, for it changed; all the more so here, where there is no greater change than this, that after the grafting was done the esrog grew and became a new entity. Furthermore, there is difficulty from what is written in chapter Merubah, page 78, regarding consecrated animals: "or a sheep" excludes crossbreeds. Why do we need a verse? Let us derive that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, according to his words. Rather, certainly a mitzvah that comes through a transgression does not apply here, for the reason we mentioned. It appears to me to forbid it for another reason. In chapter Merubah there, we say: if it comes to include, why do I need two inclusions? Now that a crossbreed is included, need a resembling one be included? Learn from this that there is more reason to forbid a crossbreed than a resembling one. Here we see that an esrog that resembles another is forbidden, for it is stated on page 36 that one resembling a kushite is invalid. Rashi explained: it grew here and resembles a kushite; it is a resembling one and invalid. If so, all the more so a crossbreed is invalid for an esrog, and this Gertiza is a tree graft, so it is obvious that it is invalid. But there is difficulty: from where do we know the prohibition of crossbreed and resembling one regarding esrog? Regarding consecrated animals, firstborn, and tithe, a verse is needed there in chapter Merubah; if so, from where do we know it regarding esrog? One may say that the Rosh wrote in chapter Lulav HaGazul regarding the question whether if signs of tereifah were born in it, what is its law, that the disqualifications of esrog are essentially only from the words of the Sages, and now that the Rabbis enacted regarding esrog, they enacted similar to Torah law regarding consecrated items. So too the Ran wrote at the beginning of the chapter, that regarding the disqualification of beauty, the verse handed it over to the Sages to determine what is beautiful. According to the Tur, who wrote in siman 648 that it is a Torah doubt, one must say that the disqualifications of esrog are a halachah le-Moshe mi-Sinai. If so, regarding crossbreeding too it is so. However, there is difficulty with this: if a grafted esrog is invalid, why do we say on page 31, if he did not find an esrog he should not bring a pomegranate, nor a quince, nor another item, and it did not say the greater novelty, that he should not bring even a grafted esrog? A similar difficulty appears in the Rosh regarding an invalid esrog, that there are those who are precise and ask why it said quince and pomegranate and not another invalid species. One can reject this, that it mentioned quince and pomegranate as a greater novelty, even though they are beautiful and whole, etc. But this cannot answer regarding a grafted one, for a grafted one is also beautiful and whole. Furthermore, is esrog written in the verse? It is written only "the fruit of a beautiful tree," and we derive esrog from it, as is stated in the Gemara through many derivations. This grafted one has all the virtues that an esrog has. And if it is because something that lacks the virtue of an esrog is mixed into it, why should we not compare it to that in Oso VeEs Beno, page 79, in the sugya of a goat that came upon a female deer, where the Sages there hold that even part sheep is called sheep? Here too, let us say that since it has part of the fruit of a beautiful tree, the mixture of another thing within it should not harm it. As for what I brought from a resembling one, that it is invalid, one can reject this, that a resembling one is different, for it is not beautiful since that is not its normal way. In my humble opinion, on the first day one should certainly not take it, since deficiency invalidates then, for we consider that part of the other species as though it is not present, and it follows that what remains of the esrog portion is deficient. According to this, there is no reason to invalidate it on the other days, for a deficient one is valid then. Nevertheless, there is concern that perhaps the majority is from the part that is not the species of esrog, and there is not the required measure remaining from the esrog portion; and it is forbidden even on the other days. It appears that even to take it without a blessing so that the Torah of esrog not be forgotten is seemingly not proper, for we say in the Gemara: if he did not find an esrog, he should not bring another item. Is that not obvious? One might have said he should bring one so that the Torah of esrog not be forgotten; it teaches us that sometimes ruin may result from it, for he may come to be drawn after it. This is said regarding other species that are not esrog at all; all the more so with this grafted one, where people will think it is a valid esrog. Perhaps for this reason it said quince or pomegranate, as a greater novelty, that even with this we decree lest he come to be drawn after it, and all the more so with a grafted one. Nevertheless, since the prohibition is not clear, we do not decree concerning it, and one should take it all seven days without a blessing, so it appears to me. <b>Later</b> I saw in Responsa Rema, siman 126, that Mahari Padua wrote: go read it to schoolchildren in our country, that we do not fulfill with it even in pressing circumstances. He gave signs for them there. First, the grafted ones are smooth, whereas esrogim have small protrusions over their entire body and height. Second, on a grafted one the stem protrudes, whereas on an esrog the stem is sunken. He gave another sign regarding the membrane inside it.
