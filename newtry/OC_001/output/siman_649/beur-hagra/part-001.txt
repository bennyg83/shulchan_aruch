ביום ראשון ולר"ל אפילו בשאר ימים משום מצוה הבאה בעבירה. מה פליגין בשגזלו משופה אבל אם גזלו ושיפהו דמים הוא חייב לו ר"ל ולכם קרינן ואין בו משום מה"ב וז"ש כגון גזל כו' ועתוס' ט' א' ד"ה ההוא כו' ל' א' ד"ה משום כו' וד"ה הא כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1. But if one stole, etc.</b> For there Rabbi Yochanan said: only after despair, etc. It appears that he holds that despair effects acquisition. And in Bava Kamma 68b Rabbi Yochanan challenges Reish Lakish: and if you would think that despair effects acquisition, etc.; and likewise there, 114a, he holds that despair effects acquisition. Yet there, 67a, Ulla said: from where do we know that despair does not effect acquisition? And likewise in Gittin 54a Ulla holds that despair does not effect acquisition. One should not answer that everywhere Ulla holds that it does acquire, only with respect to consecration it is not fit for offering because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for in Gittin there Rava inquired according to Ulla regarding shearings and offspring. Moreover, it is difficult, for there they challenge Ulla from the baraita, if one stole and consecrated, while in Merubah Rabbi Yochanan challenges Reish Lakish from that baraita in the opposite direction. And here one cannot answer as he answers there, that the owners consecrated it in the thief's house, for if so, what is the challenge to Ulla? Rather, everywhere Ulla and Rabbi Yochanan hold that despair by itself does not acquire; only with consecration they hold that it acquires, because it is despair together with a change of name: at first it was unconsecrated, and now it is consecrated, as stated in Bava Kamma 67a. And that case in Bava Kamma 114 is also with a change of name, for at first it was hides and now it is coverlets, as stated there 66b. And that which Abaye challenges Rabbah there, from 'his offering' and not stolen property, etc., and derives from it that despair does not acquire, is because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as written in Sukkah. That is, through the consecration he acquires it and fulfills the mitzvah with it; but if he had acquired it without the consecration, it would not be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Now it proves well that despair without consecration necessarily does not acquire. Ulla and Rava likewise prove this there, 67b, in this manner, and this is Ulla's reason in Gittin. Another proof is from that which they ask in Sukkah: let him acquire them by a physical change, and let him acquire them by a change of name; meaning that he bound them in a bundle and it is called a hosha'ana. This shows that if he had acquired it, it would not be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. But now that he acquires it only because of the change of name that made it into a lulav for the mitzvah, it is different. And they further said there: but if he stole wood, etc., he acquired it by a change of name; and likewise a beam of a sukkah, the Sages made an enactment, etc., that he acquires it and it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, because he acquired it without the mitzvah of sukkah. So too the Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, etc. And what is different from a stolen sukkah, which the Rabbis validate, etc.? If he stole wood and roofed with it, he acquired it by a physical change; if so, by lulav also, he holds that a lulav does not require binding, and if you would say, etc., as stated in the Gemara there. Furthermore, we invalidate one of an asherah and of an idolatrous city because its required measure is considered crushed, and we do not invalidate because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. And in the Yerushalmi, Sukkah there: Rabbi Chiyya taught, 'and you shall take for yourselves,' from your own, and not stolen. Rabbi Levi said: one who takes a stolen lulav, to what may this be compared? To one who honored the ruler with a tray, and it was found not to be his. They said: woe to this one, whose advocate has become his accuser. They disagree, for according to Rabbi Chiyya this applies specifically on the first day, and according to Rabbi Levi even on the other days, because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. In what case do they disagree? When he stole it smoothed; but if he stole it and smoothed it, he owes him only money, meaning that we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it, and it does not involve a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. This is what he wrote: such as if he stole, etc. See Tosafot 9a, s.v. hahu, and 30a, s.v. mishum, and s.v. ha.

<b>ויש מי כו'. </b>לפני ייאוש מיירי דלאחר ייאוש לא אסור אף ביום הראשון אלא משום מצוה הבאה בעבירה כמ"ש שם בשלמא כו' והאי לאו דידיה הוא אלא כו' ואין חילוק בין יום ראשון לשאר ימים כמ"ש שם אלא לפני ייאוש דביום ראשון אסור משום דלאו דידיה הוא וה"ה לאחרים וכי היאך יכול להקנות לו של חבירו אבל בשאר ימים משום מצוה הבאה בעבירה לאחרים ליכא וכ"כ בה"ג דה"ל מצוה הבאה בעבירה אבל אקצייה לאינש אחרינא קני ונפיק ביה ול"ש בי"ט ראשון ול"ש בי"ט שני אלא דשם מיירי לאחר ייאוש וכמ"ש בגמ' בייאוש ושינוי רשות שם בהני אונכרי וס"ל לא"ח דה"ה קודם ייאוש ליכא משום מה"ב דאמרו בירושלמי שנעשה סניגורו קטיגורו כנ"ל משא"כ שהוא לא גזלו ומה שהקשה למה ליה למימר בגמרא ייאוש ושינוי רשות התם משום יום ראשון דבעינן לכם וכמ"ש כאן חוץ מיום ראשון ועיין רש"י שם ד"ה וקרקע כו' וקסבר ייאוש גרידא כו' ואם כן קני כו'. מ' לס' הראשונה דייאוש לא קני א"צ לס' משום מה"ב אלא משום לכם ובזה ניחא דאימעיט סוכה גזולה מלך ת"ל דה"ל מה"ב דהא ע"כ בענין דלא קני דאל"כ הוי לך וכשר כמו גזל עצים אלא דמשום מה"ב היה כשר לאחרים ובזה מתורץ קושית תוס' ל' א' ד"ה משום כו' והדתניא לקמן כו' והוא דוחק דגמ' אמר בהדיא להוציא את הגזול ואין אסור אלא ביום ראשון כמו שאול ומשום מה"ב אסור בכל הימים ועוד לך דסוכה א"א לומר כן דהא שאול כשר שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is one who says, etc.</b> He is speaking before despair, for after despair it is not prohibited even on the first day except because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as stated there: granted, etc., but this is not his; rather, etc. And there is no distinction between the first day and the other days, as stated there. Rather, it is before despair, where on the first day it is prohibited because it is not his, and the same applies to others, for how can he transfer to him property belonging to another? But on the other days, because of the concern of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, that does not apply to others. So the Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote: for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; but if he assigned it to another person, he acquires it and fulfills the obligation with it, whether on the first day of Yom Tov or on the second day of Yom Tov. However, there he is speaking after despair, as stated in the Gemara regarding despair and change of domain, in the case of those merchants. The Orchot Chayim holds that the same applies before despair, that there is no issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for the Yerushalmi said that his advocate becomes his accuser, as above, which does not apply when he did not steal it. And regarding the difficulty he raised, why the Gemara needed to say despair and change of domain, there it is because of the first day, when we require 'for yourselves,' as written here: except for the first day. And see Rashi there, s.v. vekarka, who writes: and he holds that despair alone, etc.; and if so he acquired it, etc. This implies according to the first view, that despair does not acquire, there is no need for the reason of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, but rather because of 'for yourselves.' With this it is understandable why a stolen sukkah is excluded from 'for yourself.' Why is that needed? Let it be because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. For necessarily it is in a case where he did not acquire it, since otherwise it would be yours and valid, as with one who stole wood. Rather, because of the issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression it would be valid for others. This resolves Tosafot's question on 30a, s.v. mishum, where they ask from that which is taught below, etc.; their answer is forced, for the Gemara says explicitly: to exclude stolen property, and it is prohibited only on the first day, like borrowed property, whereas because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression it would be prohibited all the days. Moreover, 'for yourself' by sukkah cannot be interpreted that way, for a borrowed sukkah is valid there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 2. And one who is prohibited by vow from benefit, etc.</b> Since they are prohibited for benefit, we do not read 'for yourselves' with respect to them. See Rashi 35a, s.v. lefi she-ein bah din mamon, etc. And that which must be said regarding an asherah and an idolatrous city, that they are considered crushed, etc., is because of the remaining days, as Tosafot wrote there, s.v. lefi; but on the first day they are prohibited because of 'for yourselves.' The same applies here: even though he gave it to him as a gift, nevertheless it is prohibited, as stated in Nedarim 34b, and it is not his. If he betrothed a woman with them she is not betrothed, as stated there 47: Rami bar Chama inquired; come and hear, one who betroths, etc. This shows that if it is prohibited for benefit, she is not betrothed. There.

A proof is that for us also, the first day of Yom Tov does not override Shabbat, just as for them, because we are not expert in the fixing of the month, as stated at the beginning of the fourth chapter. Because of this, even for them it does not override, since for us, etc., as stated there. And just as they, because of doubt, would take it and not bless, so too we do the same. Even according to those who explain that we act like them only to be stringent and not to be lenient, this is not leniency, since we do take it. The Rif wrote in the first chapter of Megillah that this is the reason shofar is deferred because of Rabbah's reason: also because we do not know the fixing of the month. The Rashba in Sukkah there wrote likewise, and wrote that therefore circumcision is not deferred because of Rabbah's reason, since its time is clear; see there, and see the Ran in Megillah there. Also, from the fact that we bless on the second day of Yom Tov even though we do not bless over a doubt, we bless so that people not treat it lightly; all the more so he should not bless here, so that people not treat it lightly. This is not comparable to counting the Omer, for there it is biblical, unlike here. See the Ran, chapter 5.
