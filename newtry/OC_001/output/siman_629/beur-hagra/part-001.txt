<b>Se'if 7: One should be uncertain, etc.</b> He was uncertain whether it is considered a receptacle, since it is made to be filled. As it says there on 12b, something that is susceptible to tumah is invalid. Although in Torat Kohanim, parashat Shemini, it says that a ladder is pure, and likewise in the Tosefta of Kelim, this refers to where the rungs are on top of its sidepieces, unlike where its sidepieces are perforated to receive the heads of the rungs. However, in Kelim chapter 17 we learned: the base of goldsmiths is impure, and that of blacksmiths is pure; the Rambam explained that the blacksmiths' base is a receptacle made to be filled. See there in Kelim 2:3 and in the Raavad there. The Rambam's words require examination, for in the first chapter of Sukkah it explains that female arrows are invalid, and even though they are made to be filled, they are susceptible to tumah, etc. But the Rash there on the Mishnah of the base explained that it is an implement placed beneath the feet of goldsmiths and blacksmiths; see there. Similarly Rashi in Sanhedrin 68a explained that Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages dispute about a receptacle made to be filled, and according to the Sages it is pure. Tosafot there and in Sukkah there challenged him; see there. But what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, whether it is permitted to place, etc., is not what is written in the Responsa of the Ramban there or in the Responsa of the Rashba, siman 195. They discuss whether it is permitted to use the ladders themselves as s'chach. However, in Terumat HaDeshen siman 90, the question is written as whether it is permitted to place the ladder in order to put the s'chach on top of the ladder. He answered that even to use the ladders themselves as s'chach is permitted, because they are flat wooden implements, and he brought a proof from Torat Kohanim as above, as the Rashba wrote in the first chapter of Sukkah. The correct explanation is as Rabbeinu Yitzchak wrote: all flat wooden implements that are fit for lying or treading upon, if one designated them for such use, receive every kind of tumah and can even become a primary source of tumah, as written in Bava Kamma, Bechorot, Niddah, Sukkah, Shabbos, and other places. But otherwise, if they serve a person and serve a person's use, they are pure by Torah law but receive tumah rabbinically. If not, they are pure even rabbinically. This is the ladder excluded in Torat Kohanim, since it serves the person and does not serve the person's accessories; see there in chapter 6. Also, ladders are not designated for lying or treading, but for climbing up and down. Likewise according to Tosafot's other answer, that because they are somewhat wide and fit to place something on them, they decreed rabbinic tumah upon them because they resemble a receptacle, and so it also appears in the Gemara in Bechorot and several places according to that reasoning, nevertheless with a ladder everyone agrees that it is pure, as above from Torat Kohanim and the Tosefta. There the statement is rabbinic, and although it derives it from a verse, it is only an asmachta, as is clear from its mention of a table, and a table is pure by Torah law, as stated in Menachot chapter 11. See Tosafot there 96b, s.v. uledvarim; Sukkah 5a, s.v. misgarto; Bava Batra 66a, s.v. veshani; and Eruvin 31a, s.v. bifshutei. See also Yoreh De'ah 198:31. The Beit Yosef understands that since the Terumat HaDeshen permits placing s'chach on top of a ladder only because he holds that a ladder is pure, according to the Rashba, who is uncertain, the Terumat HaDeshen would agree that it is forbidden. But this is not precise, for the Terumat HaDeshen wrote this only to strengthen the point, that even the ladder itself may be used as s'chach. But even according to those who forbid using it as s'chach, placing s'chach on top of it is certainly permitted, as he wrote there in siman 91 and as the Shulchan Aruch writes below in se'if 8. He wrote that although we rule like Rabbi Yehudah in Sukkah 21b, since the Amoraim there discuss his view, nevertheless the Rosh wrote there that the main reason is fixedness, as the Yerushalmi there concludes. See Tosafot there, s.v. she'ein, etc.; and so it is ruled below in Shulchan Aruch, end of siman 630, se'if 13. Although one could answer that the case of se'if 8 is not difficult, for even though the Terumat HaDeshen wrote specifically according to the Rosh's ruling that the reason is fixedness, he did not see the words of the Rashba, who ruled like the one who says that the reason is that he supports it with something susceptible to tumah. The Rashba there challenged from the rule that one who makes his sukkah on top of a tree has a valid sukkah, even though a tree is invalid for s'chach. He also challenged Rashi's explanation there, that if he placed the s'chach on top of the bed it is valid, meaning that he inserted pegs outside and did not rest the s'chach on the bed; if so, what novelty is there? He therefore wrote that the correct explanation is as the Ramban wrote: he supports his sukkah with the legs of the bed, because he overturned the bed and its legs are above, and placed s'chach on them. One says it is invalid because it lacks fixedness, for people can tell him: stand up so we can do our work, since it is not standing for that purpose, for its use is while inverted. The other says it is invalid because, etc. Abaye said: this applies only, etc., meaning where he placed the bed in its usual position, inserted pegs upon it, and rested the s'chach on the pegs. Although it moves with the bed, it is valid according to everyone: according to the one who says the problem is lack of fixedness, here it stands in its usual way; and according to the one who says because, etc., that applies only when he rested the s'chach itself on something susceptible to tumah, but supports are valid. Otherwise, every sukkah wall would have to stand on the ground. This is what is meant by one who makes his sukkah at the top of a tree: the floor of the sukkah, as Rashi wrote there. See there, where he answers Tosafot's questions against Rashi. The Ran wrote similarly. According to this, the case of se'if 8 is not difficult. But the end of siman 630 is difficult. The Magen Avraham answered that there it is after the fact, while here he forbids initially. This is forced. See Magen Avraham se'if katan 9.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 17: But a bundle, etc.</b> As is written there, 11a. All the more so according to the Rashba's explanation, that attached material is also excluded from this verse, make and not, etc. This is all the more true of attached material, with which no act at all was done, even for the sake of dwelling. This is what the Gemara challenges there: why specifically because of, etc.? See Tosafot there, 12b, s.v. ein mesachchin, and from this, etc. But one can say that nothing can be proven from this, for there too, 11a, the Mishnah teaches plainly: or if he cut them, they are valid, and the Gemara says that he must shake them.

<b>או שהוא שרוי כו'. </b>עמ"א ס"ק כ"ה וכמש"ל בסי' תרכ"ו ס"ב. אבל הטור סתם לפי שכתב זה לדעת ר"ת כמ"ש כדי להגין או כו' ולדעת ר"ת שכל שאינו מסייע בהכשר סוכה אינו פוסל והוכיח הרשב"א והר"ן כסברא ראשונה ממ"ש שם מ"ד ה"ה לנאותה כו' אורחא דמילתא ואי לפיר"ת לאו אורחא דמילתא שיהיו כל כך נושרין עד שתשוב חמחה מרובה מצילתה ועוד דקאמר שם דלא לימרו קמסככו כו' ואם איתא אמאי יהיו סבורין שיהיה הסוכה חמתה מרובה מצלתה בלא סדין וכן סתם לעיל בסי' תרכ"ו ס"א כסברא ראשונה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or that it is soaked, etc.</b> See Magen Avraham se'if katan 25, and as I wrote above in siman 626:2. But the Tur wrote the matter without qualification because he wrote this according to Rabbeinu Tam's view, as he wrote: in order to protect, or, etc. According to Rabbeinu Tam, anything that does not assist in the sukkah's validity does not invalidate it. The Rashba and Ran proved like the first view from what is said there: the same would apply to beautifying it, etc.; that is only the usual manner of the matter. But according to Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation, it is not usual that they would shed so much that its sun would become greater than its shade. Furthermore, the Gemara says there that people should not say that he is using it as s'chach, etc. If so, why would they think that the sukkah would have more sun than shade without the sheet? He likewise ruled plainly above in siman 626:1 like the first view.
