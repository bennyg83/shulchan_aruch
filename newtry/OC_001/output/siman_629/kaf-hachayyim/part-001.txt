<b>א) [סעיף א'] דבר שמסככין בו צריך שיהיה צומח וכו'  </b>דכתיב חג הסוכות תעשה לך וכו' באספך מגרנך ומיקבך הילכך אין מסככין אלא בדבר שצומח מן הארץ והוא עכשיו תלוש ואינו מקבל טומאה דומיא דפסולת גורן ויקב (שהן קשין ואשכולות ריקנין) שיש בהם כל הדברים הללו. לבוש. ועיין לעיל ריש סימן תרכ"ו. ונהגו לסכך בענפי אילן מפני כי אילן עולה מספר סוכה ולכן ענפי אילן הם מצוה מן המובחר. בן א"ח ריש פ' האזינו. וגם סוכה היא אותיות כ"ו ה"ס כ"ו גי' שם הוי"ה וה"ס גי' שם אדנ"י ולכן כשמסכך בענפי אילן. יכוין כי אילן גי' סוכה וגם גי' הוי"ה אדנ"י וגם יכוין כי סוכה בחי' אור מקיף כמ"ש לעיל סי' תרכ"ה או' א' ועוד עי"ש או' י"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] An item used for s'chach must grow from the ground, etc. </b>For it is written, You shall make for yourself the festival of Sukkos, etc., when you gather from your threshing floor and your winepress. Therefore, one may use for s'chach only something that grows from the ground, is presently detached, and is not susceptible to tumah, similar to the waste of the threshing floor and winepress, namely straw and empty clusters, which possess all these characteristics. Levush. See above, at the beginning of siman 626. The custom is to use tree branches for s'chach, because the numerical value of the word ilan, tree, equals that of sukkah. Therefore, tree branches are the preferred manner of performing the mitzvah. Ben Ish Chai, beginning of Parashas Haazinu. Also, the word sukkah contains the letters that allude to 26 and 65. Twenty-six is the numerical value of the Name Havayah, and 65 is the numerical value of the Name Adnus. Therefore, when one uses tree branches for s'chach, he should have in mind that ilan has the same numerical value as sukkah and also as Havayah combined with Adnus. He should also have in mind that the sukkah is an aspect of surrounding light, as written above, siman 625, sec. 1. See further there, sec. 11.

<b>ב) שם. צריך שיהיה צומח וכו'  </b>וכ"ז דרשינן מקראי הלכך דאו' נינהו ולעיכובא דהיינו דבר שאינו צומח מארץ או מחובר או דבר שראוי לקבל טומאה מה"ת פסול מדאו' אכן דבר שראוי לקבל טומאה מדרבנן פסול רק מדרבנן וע"כ אם אין לו לסכך רק בדבר זה י"ל דיסכך בו ולא יברך. מש"ז אות א' מ"ב בב"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) Ibid. It must grow, etc. </b>All these requirements are derived from verses. Therefore, they are Torah laws and indispensable. Thus, something that does not grow from the ground, something attached to the ground, or something fit to contract tumah according to the Torah, is invalid according to the Torah. However, something fit to contract tumah only rabbinically is invalid only rabbinically. Therefore, if one has no other material for s'chach, it appears that he should use this material but should not recite a blessing. Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha.

<b>ה) וסכך  </b>הסוכה או הדפנות העשוי מאיסורי הנאה אם יש לומר בזה מצות לא ליהנות ניתנו או לא כיון שהגוף נהנה מהם וכן מעצי אשירה עיין בית השואבה אות ה' ואו' ו' מה שהאריך בזה והסכים שאפילו בדיעבד פסולה יעו"ש אבל הער"ה אות א' כתב דאם א"א בסכך של היתר בזה יש לסמוך על המתירין בכל ז' ימי החג אבל ביום ח' יש להחמיר שלא לישב בה כיון דאיכא הנאת הגוף ואיכא מ"ד דבמצוה דרבנן לא אמרינן מצות לא ליהנות ניתנו יעו"ש. ועיין לקמן סי' תר"ל או' ג' ועוד עיין לעיל סי' תקפ"ו סעיף ג' ובדברינו לשם ומ"מ נראה דלא יברך משום סב"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) Regarding s'chach </b>or walls of a sukkah made from items from which benefit is forbidden, whether we say in this case that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, or whether we do not say so because the body derives benefit from them, and likewise regarding wood from an asheirah tree, see Beit HaShoevah, secs. 5 and 6, where he discussed this at length and concluded that the sukkah is invalid even after the fact, see there. However, the Erech HaShulchan, sec. 1, wrote that if permitted s'chach is unavailable, one may rely on the lenient opinions throughout all seven days of the festival. On the eighth day, however, one should be stringent and not sit in it, since there is bodily benefit and there is an opinion that, regarding a rabbinic mitzvah, we do not say that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, see there. See below, siman 630, sec. 3. See also above, siman 586, seif 3, and our words there. Nevertheless, it appears that one should not recite a blessing, because of the principle that in a case of uncertainty regarding blessings we are lenient.

<b>ו) נסתפק  </b>בס' בכורי יעקב ברסי' זה אם מותר לסכך בנייר אפילו העשוי מעשבים וכתב דנייר העשוי מבגדים של צמר פסול מדאו' לסיכוך ואותו הנעשה מעשבים או מבגדי פשתן אינו פסול רק מדרבנן ויש נפקותא בזה בשעת הדחק שאין לו רק נייר לסכך יעו"ש והב"ד הרו"ח או' ב' וכתב וצריכה רבה חדא דאם יפלו גשמים על הנייר הרי היא מכלהו ויפול ואם יהיה כ"כ עב ביותר הרי אין הכוכבים נראים מתוכן כלל ואם יעשה בהם איזה נקבים אפשר דיהיה מותר בשעת הדחק יעו"ש. וכבר כתבנו לעיל או' ב' דכל שהוא איסור דרבנן בשעת הדחק לא יברך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>6) The author </b>of Sefer Bikkurei Yaakov, at the beginning of this siman, was uncertain whether it is permitted to use paper for s'chach, even paper made from vegetation. He wrote that paper made from woolen garments is invalid for s'chach according to the Torah, whereas paper made from vegetation or linen garments is invalid only rabbinically. There is a practical difference in a pressing circumstance when one has only paper for s'chach, see there. Ruach Chaim, sec. 2, cited his words and wrote that this requires great consideration. First, if rain falls on the paper, it will destroy it and it will fall. If it is exceedingly thick, the stars will not be visible through it at all. If one makes holes in it, it is possible that it would be permitted in a pressing circumstance, see there. We already wrote above, sec. 2, that whenever the prohibition is rabbinic, in a pressing circumstance one should not recite a blessing.

<b>10) [Seif 2] Such as spits. </b>It appears that this refers to wooden spits, for if they were metal, they would already be excluded because they do not grow from the ground. Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 4. So too the Levush wrote: wooden spits. However, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, wrote that it refers to iron spits, whereas wooden spits are not susceptible to tumah even rabbinically, like male arrows, since they are flat wooden utensils. He left the words of the Beit Yosef requiring further study, see there. The Bach wrote that it refers even to metal spits whose handles are wooden. Although the handles have no receptacle and are like male arrows, one may not use even their wooden portion as s'chach because of the spit, which was susceptible to tumah, see there. However, the Shiltei Gibborim, sec. 4, wrote that the words of the Beit Yosef are primary, since the Mishnah teaches spits together with bedposts, all of which are wooden. So too the Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, wrote that although flat wooden utensils are not susceptible to tumah, they are nevertheless forbidden rabbinically, and they are inferior to the male arrows of seif 3. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, sec. 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 4, that wooden spits are susceptible to tumah rabbinically because they serve a person and serve in the manner of a table, see there. So too Sefer Bigdei Yesha wrote that the case concerns spits designated for roasting and other uses, which serve a person. Utensils that serve a person's needs are susceptible to tumah even though they are flat wooden utensils, as explained at length in Tosafos, Sukkah 5a, and in Toras Kohanim, see there. See below, sec. 14.

<b>טז) הקנים  </b>אעפ"י שיש להם בית קיבול אין מקבלין טומאה כיון שלא נעשה הקיבול שלהם לצורך קבלה ומסככין בהם. תשו' הרא"ש כלל כ"ד סימן י"ב. ב"י. עט"ז. כנה"ג בהגב"י. מ"א שם. ר"ז אות ד' ח"א כלל קמ"ו או' י"א. מ"ב שם. וה"ה דמותר לסכך במרזב (צינור) לפי שלא נעשה לקבלם. מ"א שם. ר"ז שם. ח"א שם. מ"ב שם. ועיין ביו"ד סי' ר"א סעי' ל"ו דאם חקק במרזב גומא לקבל צרורות מקבל טומאה ולעניין שיעור גודל הגומא יש חילוק בין עץ לחרס יעו"ש. מחה"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>16) Reeds, </b>although they have a hollow receptacle, are not susceptible to tumah, because their hollow was not made for the purpose of receiving. Therefore, they may be used for s'chach. Responsa of the Rosh, rule 24, siman 12; Beit Yosef; Ateres Zahav; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 4; Chayei Adam, rule 146, sec. 11; Mishna Berurah, ibid. The same applies to a gutter or pipe, which may be used for s'chach because it was not made to receive. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Chayei Adam, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. See Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 36, which states that if one carved a hollow in a gutter to catch pebbles, it is susceptible to tumah. Regarding the required size of the hollow, there is a distinction between wood and earthenware, see there. Machatzis HaShekel.

<b>17) Know </b>that every wooden utensil that is somewhat broad and fit for placing something upon it is susceptible to tumah, because it resembles a receptacle. So wrote Tosafos, Sukkah 5a. Accordingly, one should not place a shovel or rake upon the sukkah, even if it broke. Magen Avraham, s.k. 11; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 11; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 14. Nevertheless, see Bikkurei Yaakov, whose view inclines toward leniency in a pressing circumstance, permitting use of it for s'chach after it broke. Since even while intact it was susceptible to tumah only rabbinically, there is no reason to decree once it is broken, see there. The Mishna Berurah, sec. 10, cited his words. This is also the view of Beit HaShoevah, sec. 11, see there. If they are not fit for placing anything upon them, they are permitted even initially. Beit HaShoevah, ibid. <b>19) [Seif 4] If he covered it with flax that was not crushed and not combed, etc. </b>The Gemara is uncertain regarding flax that was soaked in water, though it was neither crushed nor combed, whether one may use it for s'chach. The poskim rule leniently, and the Ran wrote that the reason is that the prohibition is merely rabbinic. Beit Yosef. However, the Riaz wrote stringently regarding this uncertainty, and the Shiltei Gibborim cited his words in the first chapter of Sukkah. So too the Ritva wrote, according to Rashi and those who follow his explanation that tufts of flax are invalid according to the Torah because they are susceptible to the tumah of tzaraas, that even if they were merely soaked and not crushed, we rule stringently. So too wrote Orchos Chaim, folio 110c, in the name of Mahariag, that even if it was soaked and not crushed, it is invalid. So too wrote the Baal HaIttur and Shibolei HaLeket. However, from the words of the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch, who wrote, that was not crushed and not combed, etc., it is implied that if it was soaked, it is permitted. This is also implied by the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 8, part 1. So too the Ritva wrote in his novellae to Sukkah that it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently. Beit HaShoevah, sec. 21, cited his words and wrote that, as a matter of law, this is how we rule, since the majority of the early and later poskim, namely the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Tur, Ran, and Ritva, ruled that flax which was soaked but neither crushed nor combed is valid. Maran also ruled so in the Shulchan Aruch. In practice, however, since according to the Riaz, Orchos Chaim in the name of Mahariag, and the Ritva according to Rashi, even mere soaking renders it invalid, one should at least be stringent initially not to use flax stalks that came from the soaking vat as s'chach, even if they were not crushed, see there. So too ruled the Mishna Berurah, sec. 11.
