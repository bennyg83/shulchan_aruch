<b>סולם</b>. משום שיש לו בית קיבול שנקוב' במקום שתוחבין בו השליבות ואף על פי שהוא  עשוי למלאות שמו בית קיבול כמ"ש ברשב"א סי' קצ"ה ומ"ש בת"ה דסולמות טהורות היינו באותן הנקובים מעבר לעבר כעין שלנו וסולמות של עגלות או אותן שקובעין בכותל להניח עליהן מאכל לבהמ' טמאים לכן יש לאסור כל סולמות [ב"ח] ועס"ח דלא גזרינן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A ladder.</b> Because it has a receptacle, being perforated in the place where the rungs are inserted. Even though it is made to be filled, it is called a receptacle, as written in the Rashba, siman 195. What the Terumat HaDeshen wrote, that ladders are pure, refers to those that are perforated from side to side, like ours. But ladders of wagons, or those fixed in the wall on which to place animal food, are impure; therefore all ladders should be forbidden, as the Bach writes. See se'if 8, where we do not decree.

<b>על גביו</b>. דאין מעמידין הסכך בדבר המקבל טומאה שמא יבא לסכך בו אף על גב שמעמידין  על כותל אבנים משום דלא שכיח שיסכך בהם ועוד דכ"ע ידעי דכה"ג בית דירה מקרי ויש נוהגים להעמיד קנים תחת הסכך ומדת חסידות הוא (ר"ן) ובהכי מתיישבת מ"ש סימן תרל"א ס"ח שאע"פ שהסכך כשר נתון על השפודים כשר כיון דלא התירו לו אא"כ נתן סכך כשר עליהם לא גזרי' שיסכך בשפודים וכמ"ש שם ס"ט והב"ח כת' שמדינא אסור להעמיד ולא משום גזיר' ע"ש שנדחק ולדבריו קשיא הך דשפודין מ"מ צ"ע דהא בססי' תר"ל פסק הרב"י בהדיא דמותר להעמיד בדבר המקבל טומא' דאל"כ אפי' גבוה עשרה פסול' ע"ש וגם בס"ח פסק לחבר כו' אין קפיד' א"כ מוכח דס"ל כת"ה שמותר להעמיד בדבר המקבל טומאה וכ"ה בלבוש בהדיא לכן נ"ל דמה שאסר בסולם היינו משום שהיא עצמ' סכך פסול [ועסי' תרל"ב] וכ"ה ברשב"א סי' קצ"ה וידוע שתשוב' להרמב"ן הוא ג"כ מהרשב"א וא"כ שניה' לדבר א' נתכוונו אבל להעמיד הסכך בדבר המקבל טומא' שרי וכמ"ש הרא"ש ות"ה ומהרי"ל בתשו' סי' פ"ז ועי"ל דבדיעבד שרי וכמ"ש ססי' תר"ל ולכתחל' אסור וכמ"ש כאן ס"ז ומ"ש ס"ח שמותר לקשרם בבלאו' היינו כמ"ש ססי' תר"ל בשם הר"ן דכיון שאין הסכך עצמו נסמך עליה' אלא שקונדסין נסמכין עליהם שרי וכן עיקר דלא כלבוש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On top of it.</b> For one may not support the s'chach with something susceptible to tumah, lest one come to use it itself as s'chach. Although one may support it on a stone wall, that is because it is uncommon to use stones as s'chach; furthermore, everyone knows that in such a case it is called a dwelling house. Some have the custom to place reeds beneath the s'chach, and this is an act of piety, as the Ran writes. With this, what is written in siman 631:8 is resolved: although the valid s'chach is placed upon spits, it is valid, since they permitted it only if he placed valid s'chach upon them, and we therefore do not decree that he might use the spits as s'chach, as written there in se'if 9. The Bach wrote that by law it is forbidden to support the s'chach this way, and not because of a decree; see there, where he was forced. According to his words, the case of the spits is difficult. In any event this requires examination, for at the end of siman 630 the Beit Yosef ruled explicitly that it is permitted to support with something susceptible to tumah, for otherwise even if it is ten tefachim high it would be invalid; see there. Also in se'if 8 he ruled that to connect, etc., there is no concern. It is therefore proven that he holds like the Terumat HaDeshen, that one may support s'chach with something susceptible to tumah. So too the Levush writes explicitly. Therefore it appears to me that what he forbade with a ladder is because the ladder itself is invalid s'chach; see siman 632. So it is in the Rashba, siman 195. It is known that the responsum attributed to the Ramban is also by the Rashba, so both intended one point. But to support the s'chach with something susceptible to tumah is permitted, as the Rosh, Terumat HaDeshen, and Mahari Weil wrote in responsum 87. One can also say that after the fact it is permitted, as written at the end of siman 630, but initially it is forbidden, as written here in se'if 7. What is written in se'if 8, that it is permitted to tie them with worn-out cloths, is as written at the end of siman 630 in the name of the Ran: since the s'chach itself does not rest upon them, but the poles rest upon them, it is permitted. This is the main ruling, not like the Levush.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That are susceptible to tumah.</b> In Mahari Weil it is written that we are concerned that they may have become impure through midras. This implies that if we know with certainty that they did not become impure, for example if they are new, they are permitted. This requires examination, for in any case they are susceptible to tumah. So too the wording of the Rema implies that they are forbidden even when new. The Bach asked how we place poles on top of an attached wall, but he did not recall what he himself wrote in the name of the Terumat HaDeshen, that something detached and later attached is valid for s'chach. So too the Beit Yosef wrote at the end of siman 626, and so it implies in siman 638:9. See what I wrote at the end of siman 628. Know that any wooden implement that is somewhat wide and fit to place something on it is susceptible to tumah, because it resembles a receptacle, as Tosafot wrote on page 5. Therefore one should not place a spade or rake on the sukkah, even if it broke.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the custom is.</b> In the Hagahot Maimoniyot it is written that it is invalid, since nowadays people roof their houses with boards that are less than four tefachim wide, so the decree of a ceiling applies, as the Darchei Moshe writes. In pressing circumstances, when they have nothing with which to make s'chach, they use boards as s'chach even if they are four tefachim wide, as the Shiltei Gibborim writes, and so it appears from the Gemara. The same applies to anything forbidden because of a decree. The Bach wrote that laths, although they are not subject to the decree of a ceiling because people do not roof their houses with them, nevertheless one must be concerned that he will place so much s'chach that rain will not descend inside, as the Semak wrote. Therefore one may not use them as s'chach, and certainly shingles.
