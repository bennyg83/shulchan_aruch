<b>ס"ק א' כל דבר כו'</b> ובב"י סימן תקל"ב. ר"ל דמשם יש קצת ראיה שהביא בשם הג"א דאשה שאירע טבילתה בחה"מ אסור ליטול צפורניה בכלי. אלא תאמר לגוי ליטול צפורניה. וסיים אף על גב דאמירה לגוי שבות במקום מצוה לא גזרו עכ"ל. וניהו דאנן קי"ל שמותרת בעצמה ליטלן. וגם להרב"י מותר ליטול צפורנים אפי' שלא במקום מצוה. מכל מקום מדברי הג"א דאוסר ליטול אפי' במקום מצוה ואעפ"כ התיר ע"י גוי. יש ללמוד לדידן מלאכה אחרת במקום מצוה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>s.k. 1: Any matter, etc.</b> And in Beis Yosef, siman 532. That is to say, from there there is some proof: for he brought in the name of Hagahos Asheri that a woman whose immersion occurred on Chol HaMoed is forbidden to cut her nails with a utensil; rather she should tell a non-Jew to cut her nails. And he concluded: even though telling a non-Jew is a shevus, in a situation of mitzvah they did not decree, end quote. And granted that we hold that she herself is permitted to cut them; and also according to Beis Yosef it is permitted to cut nails even not in a situation of mitzvah. Nevertheless, from the words of Hagahos Asheri, who forbids cutting even in a situation of mitzvah and even so permitted through a non-Jew, one may learn for our case [to permit] another melacha in a situation of mitzvah.

<b>(s.k. 2) Forbidden, etc.</b> It implies that it is permitted to enter it. For Nimukei Yosef wrote that the incident of Mar Zutra is dealing with Yom Tov. That is to say: we say in tractate Moed Katan, folio 12: Shmuel said: receivers of a contract (that is, one who hired a non-Jew to build his house by contract), within the techum it is forbidden; outside the techum it is permitted. And the reason is that by contract, according to the strict law it is permitted, for the non-Jew acts of his own accord; and even so, within the techum it is forbidden because of appearance of impropriety, for those who see will suspect him that the non-Jew is a day-laborer, and it turns out that a Jew is hiring him to build on Shabbos. But outside the techum (and the house is not near another within the techum) it is permitted, since Jews do not go on Shabbos, and outside [the techum] there is no appearance of impropriety. Rav Mesharshaya said: and that which is permitted outside the techum was said only regarding Shabbos and Yom Tov, when people are not commonly going there; but on Chol HaMoed, when people commonly go there, it is forbidden. And afterward we say: Mar Zutra had a mansion built for him by receivers of a contract outside the techum. Rav Safra and Rav Huna bar Chinena happened to be there and did not enter it. Some say Mar Zutra also did not enter it. And the Gemara challenges: but Shmuel said receivers of a contract outside the techum are permitted; so why did they not enter there? And it answers: a distinguished person is different. And if you want, say: there was assistance; men of Mar Zutra's household, without his knowledge, assisted the non-Jews with straw for the building, and it was done in a forbidden manner. And the Tur wrote, and this is his language: and even if the house was built in a forbidden manner through non-Jews, it is proper to be stringent that they not enter it. And Beis Yosef wrote that he holds, from what we say that these Amoraim did not enter it, and we do not say that it is forbidden to enter it, that there is no prohibition; rather they stringented upon themselves. And therefore the Tur also wrote that it is proper to be stringent, and did not write that it is forbidden. And Beis Yosef further wrote: and since our master wrote simply that it is proper to be stringent and did not distinguish between a distinguished person and one who is not distinguished, it appears that he holds we take according to the latter wording, that there was assistance: see there. And the Bach understood that Beis Yosef found a difficulty: that the Tur should have distinguished that specifically for a distinguished person it is forbidden and one must be stringent, but one who is not distinguished need not be stringent, if it were that he holds like the first wording; therefore he must hold like the latter wording. And on this the Bach wonders: for the Tur is dealing with a case where they built in a forbidden manner; and in that case, even according to the first answer that a distinguished person is different, nevertheless when they built in a forbidden manner it is proper even for one who is not distinguished to be stringent. And the first answer did not say that a distinguished person is different except according to what it holds that the case is dealing with building in a permitted manner, for it was outside the techum and it did not conclude that they assisted with straw. And if so, even if the Tur holds like the first answer, even so it is fine that he did not distinguish between a distinguished person or not. Therefore the Bach wrote that Beis Yosef was explaining that Shmuel, who said outside the techum is permitted, is dealing even with Chol HaMoed: for even on Chol HaMoed, although it is permitted to go outside the techum, nevertheless people are not as commonly going on Chol HaMoed as on weekdays. And so wrote the Rosh. And a proof is that the Gemara placed Shmuel's law in the laws of Chol HaMoed and did not place it in [the laws of] Shabbos. And Rav Mesharshaya disagrees with Shmuel and holds that on Chol HaMoed even outside the techum it is forbidden; and the incident of Mar Zutra, that they built for him outside the techum, was on Chol HaMoed. And the Gemara challenges: but Shmuel said outside the techum is permitted even on Chol HaMoed. And granted that Mar Zutra and these Rabbis hold like Rav Mesharshaya, that on Chol HaMoed even outside the techum it is forbidden; nevertheless they should not have stringented even after the fact not to enter it, for according to Shmuel it is permitted even ideally. And to this it answers: a distinguished person is different; or [the answer of] assistance applies, that it is forbidden according to everyone, and therefore even after the fact he did not enter it. And according to this, Beis Yosef proved well that necessarily the Tur does not hold like the first answer: for according to the first answer, one who is not distinguished need not be stringent when it was built outside the techum, since Shmuel permits ideally; so how did the Tur write simply regarding one built in a forbidden manner that it is proper to be stringent, and he should have distinguished that outside the techum, if there was no assistance, there is a distinction between a distinguished person or not. And all the more it appears that what the Tur wrote "if they built in a forbidden manner" means outside the techum, as appears from the language of the Tur: see there. Therefore he must hold like the latter answer: these are the words of the Bach; see there. But you have not understood: according to this, what did Beis Yosef gain by writing that the Tur holds the latter answer is primary? On the contrary, it is more difficult. For the questioner held that outside the techum one should permit after the fact, since Shmuel permits ideally; so at least according to Rav Mesharshaya it is permitted after the fact even for a distinguished person. And since the latter answer is that there was assistance, etc., it implies that if there was no assistance he concedes to the questioner that after the fact it is permitted even for a distinguished person. So how did the Tur write regarding one built in a forbidden manner that it is proper to be stringent, etc., and he is dealing with outside the techum as above? And see the Taz; and so holds Magen Avraham: that Beis Yosef was explaining that the Tur holds the incident of Mar Zutra was on Chol HaMoed, and Beis Yosef's proof is from the fact that the Tur did not write the law at all in the laws of Shabbos, and relied on what he wrote regarding Chol HaMoed. Granted that one can learn the law of Shabbos from Chol HaMoed by a fortiori, as Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 244: see there. Nevertheless, if the incident in the Gemara had been on Shabbos, he should have transcribed it in the laws of Shabbos. Therefore the incident of Mar Zutra was on Chol HaMoed, and therefore he transcribed it regarding Chol HaMoed as the incident was in the Gemara. And from it we learn regarding Shabbos when it was built within the techum or with another prohibition. And further: if you say the incident of Mar Zutra was on Shabbos, from where would the Tur learn from it to be stringent also on Chol HaMoed? Perhaps specifically on Shabbos they stringented, because its prohibition is severe with stoning; which is not so of Chol HaMoed, which according to several poskim is only rabbinic. Therefore he holds that the incident of Mar Zutra was on Chol HaMoed, and even so they stringented. And therefore Magen Avraham wrote: since Beis Yosef omitted the words of the Tur that if it was built in a forbidden manner it is proper to be stringent, it implies that he holds one need not be stringent; for he holds as Nimukei Yosef wrote, that the incident of Mar Zutra was on Yom Tov (and so wrote Beis Yosef above at the end of siman 244 in the name of the Ra'avad: see there); and if so, one cannot learn from it to be stringent also on Chol HaMoed.
