**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And as he wrote in siman 677, the Tur wrote.</b> (And it is a "there are those who say" there in the Shulchan Aruch:) Oil of a Chanukah lamp, even if mixed with permitted oil, it is forbidden to add upon it until sixty; and that is the view of Maharam, who holds that even regarding a rabbinic issur one may not add upon it in order to cancel it. And Maharam wrote: and this is not comparable to wood that fell from the palm into the oven on Yom Tov, upon which one adds, etc.; for that case is different, in that he does not benefit from them until after their burning; but here he benefits at the time the lamp is burning; end quote. And if so, from this Magen Avraham learns that even according to the view of Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 99, who holds that regarding every rabbinic issur it is permitted to add, as above: nevertheless here, since it is something that has a permitted form, it would have been proper to forbid; except that it is permitted to add because the issur is burned away. And Magen Avraham now holds that likewise it is forbidden to warm oneself opposite it, for this is comparable to benefiting from its light; for the warming comes while the issur is still present.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one can say that it is permitted to warm oneself, etc.</b> At first glance his intent in this is not to resolve the words of Tosafos of Pesachim. That is to say: even though Tosafos wrote that whatever one cannot benefit from at the beginning of the burning is forbidden to destroy, nevertheless here it is permitted. For even though it is forbidden to benefit from its light, as Maharam wrote as above, nevertheless it is permitted to warm oneself opposite it. And even though the reason of the permission of warming is that the heat comes from that little that has already been finished, nevertheless it is called that benefit comes to him from the beginning of the burning, and it is permitted to destroy it. If so, the difficulty of Tosafos returns to its place: for if so, also with chametz it is permitted to warm oneself opposite it immediately, for this very reason; and if so, he can still destroy the chametz on Yom Tov, since he can benefit from it immediately by warming himself; regarding muktzeh which is also forbidden for benefit. Rather, necessarily he did not come in this to resolve the words of Tosafos, but stated a law: that above it was obvious to him that from Maharam forbidding benefit from its light, likewise it is forbidden to warm oneself opposite it; and upon this he now wrote that it is not comparable: for even though benefiting from its light is forbidden, nevertheless warming oneself is permitted. However, his language implies that he came to resolve the words of Tosafos. For certainly the words of Tosafos are true, that whatever one cannot benefit from at the beginning of its destruction is forbidden to destroy. And even so, here it is permitted to heat with them, for it is possible to benefit from them from its beginning, namely to warm oneself; even though the reason of the permission is that the heat comes from that portion that has already burned, nevertheless it is called benefiting immediately, like one who heats with permitted wood to cook or bake, for that too is not fit to bake and cook immediately. And even so, that of chametz is fine, that it is forbidden to destroy it on Yom Tov; and why not say he can benefit immediately by warming himself opposite it? And one can say that Magen Avraham follows his own view, which he wrote in siman 501, s.k. 3, in the name of the Ran; and so he wrote in siman 511, that a fire to warm oneself was permitted only because of "since it was permitted for a need," etc.; and if so, Rabbi Akiva according to his own view, who does not hold "since," as is stated there in that sugya where Rava said, learn from Rabbi Akiva three things, one of them that we do not say "since"; see there.
