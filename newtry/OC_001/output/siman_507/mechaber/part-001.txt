**** ENGLISH ****
Even though wood that fell from a palm tree on Yom Tov or on the Shabbat preceding it is forbidden to kindle, if the wood fell on Yom Tov into the oven, one may add prepared wood upon it and nullify it, provided that one does not touch it until it is nullified in the majority. **** ENGLISH ****
It is forbidden to take wood from among logs to stoke the fire in an oven, for this constitutes repairing a vessel. If an oven has plaster-clay fall into it: if it is possible to bake and roast in it without sweeping and the bread or roast will not be scorched, it is forbidden to sweep it out because one is moving it unnecessarily. But it is permitted to flatten the fire and the ash in it so that it will be smooth and will not touch the bread at all, even though if it were touching it, it would not be enough to scorch it. However, if there is enough of the plaster that fell into it to scorch the bread or roast if it were touching them, even though without sweeping it would be possible to bake and roast in it, since it scorches, it is permitted to sweep it out, for this is considered moving for a purpose. This applies specifically to their ovens, where they would attach the bread around the sides and only needed to sweep out the plaster that fell into it. But our ovens, since it is impossible to bake in them without sweeping, it is permitted to sweep it from the ash and coals, and even though one extinguishes [fire in the process], it is impossible otherwise. And just as it is permitted to kindle for the purpose of food preparation, so too it is permitted to extinguish for the purpose of food preparation. This is like placing meat on coals, and such is the custom.

A new oven and new stoves may not be coated with oil nor wiped with a cloth, but it is permitted to heat them even for the first heating, provided that one does not cool them with cold water in order to seal them.
