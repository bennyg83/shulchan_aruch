**** ENGLISH ****
An oven into which there fell, etc. This se'if is the language of the Tur, and into it entered the explanation of the Beis Yosef; and in my humble opinion the explanation is in another way, as I wrote with Hashem's help. We learned in the mishnah: one does not break pottery, and one does not cut paper to roast salted fish on it, and one does not scrape an oven and stove, but one may press; and we say in the Gemara: what is the reason? Because he is fixing a vessel. And Rashi explained that this reason applies also to "one does not scrape," and it follows the Rabbis, who hold that preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul are forbidden. And further in the Gemara: "one does not scrape an oven"; Rabbi Chiya bar Yosef taught: and if it is impossible to bake unless he scrapes it, it is permitted. Rashi explained: this follows Rabbi Yehuda, who holds that preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul are permitted whenever it was impossible to do from before Yom Tov. And the Rosh wrote that this scraping deals with case where plaster and oven-lining fell into the oven, and one does not need so much of this scraping except so that the bread be nice, that it not touch the bread and the roasting and be scorched; and even so they permitted it, for there is in it only mere moving, etc.; end quote. And upon this the Tur wrote, and this is his language: An oven into which there fell of the clay lining: if it is possible to bake and roast in it without scraping, it is forbidden to scrape it; but it is permitted to lay down the fire and the ash in it so that it be even and not touch the bread; but if he needs to scrape it, even if it is possible to bake and roast in it without scraping, only in order to beautify the bread and the roasting so that the clay not touch it and scorch it, it is permitted; end quote. And Beis Yosef explained that the Tur and the Rosh disagree with Rashi, who explained the scraping prohibition as fixing a vessel, whereas according to the Rosh and Tur it is because of needless moving. And it appears to me that Rashi also holds the reason of the Rosh, and he stated in the mishnah the reason of fixing a vessel only for the Rabbis, who forbid preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul in every circumstance; but according to Rabbi Yehuda, who holds that preparations are permitted when impossible from before Yom Tov, likewise here it was impossible from before Yom Tov, for it fell on Yom Tov, or it fell on the eve of Yom Tov and he did not know of it: there is here no prohibition of fixing a vessel, only because of moving; and that is the beraisa of Rabbi Chiya bar Yosef. And Rashi was compelled to this: for in the mishnah they taught oven-scraping together with the others, whose reason is certainly fixing a vessel; and further, in the mishnah it is implied that even where it is impossible without scraping it is forbidden to scrape, and that is because of fixing a vessel, for the permission of food-preparation needs of the soul does not apply to it; but the beraisa that permits when it is impossible necessarily does not hold because of fixing a vessel, and that is like Rabbi Yehuda; and even so it is forbidden where it is possible without scraping, and therefore the reason is moving. And we hold like Rabbi Yehuda, as he wrote in siman 509; therefore Rashi also holds this law like the Rosh and Tur, except that the explanation of the mishnah is not the same, whether according to Rashi or according to the Rosh. Beis Yosef further wrote to explain the words of the Tur on three divisions: if it is possible to bake without scraping and it will not be scorched, it is forbidden to scrape because of moving, but to lay down the fire and ash is permitted so that it not touch it at all, even though from the force of the touch there would not be scorching; but if there is in the fallen plaster enough to scorch bread by way of touch, it is permitted, for it is considered moving for a need. And it implies that the laying down is fire and ash, but not fallen plaster-dust, and not like Rashi who wrote "he lays ash and dust," etc.; end quote. And it is astonishing to me how one can distinguish regarding touch with respect to not being scorched; for certainly it will be scorched if the fire touches it, and there is a full verse: "Can a man take fire in his bosom," etc. And further, why in truth should it be permitted to lay down in a place where the damage will not reach the bread at all? And further, why did he not state a stringency at the outset, that even where there is concern of touch of fire or clay it is forbidden to scrape, since there is not enough for scorching? And it appears to me that there are here only two divisions: whatever touches will certainly scorch; except that there are here two defects: one from the clay that fell there, if it touches it will scorch, and for this there is no remedy except scraping; and the second from the fire that will touch, and for this there is a remedy without scraping, only laying alone. Therefore the Tur said that if it is possible to bake without scraping, for there is no concern of clay touching the bread, it is forbidden to scrape the clay; and included in this, the fire and ash too are forbidden to scrape; and this is even if there is concern of fire-touch and it would burn: nevertheless it is forbidden to scrape, since there is a permission by laying down the fire and ash; we will not permit him more. And for this reason he did not mention here laying the fallen clay so as to forbid scraping even where there is concern of touch; for laying is of no help for clay, which is a coarse thing and specifically needs scraping; which is not so of fire and ash, for which laying helps, being a fine thing. And not for nothing did Rashi state "they lay the ash and the dust" and not state the clay as he began with there; rather, certainly he deals with dust that became fine from what fell from the oven plaster and became like ash, and laying helps it; its law is like ash and fire, and scraping is forbidden even if it is a case where he is concerned that it will touch. But regarding a coarse thing like a chunk of clay, laying does not apply, only scraping; and in this there is a distinction: if there is no concern of touch, it is forbidden to scrape; that is the law of the opening clause. But if there is concern of clay-touch, then automatically it will certainly scorch; that is the law of the closing clause, that because of concern of touch which is scorching it is permitted even to scrape, since laying is of no help for this. So the main distinction between the opening and closing clauses is whether there is concern of clay-touch or not; except that he inserted in between the law of laying, which deals even with a whole oven where he fears fire-touch, to teach that there the law is different, since laying helps him; and therefore scraping is forbidden even if there is concern of touch; which is not so regarding concern of clay-touch, which is a coarse thing and has no remedy except scraping: the closing clause teaches us that this is permitted even to scrape in order to be saved from the touch which is certainly scorching. Thus it appears to me in explaining the words of the Tur.

כיון שא"א לאפות כו'. בטור כתוב אבל בתנורים שלנו שמכבים הגחלים בגריפתן אם אפשר לאפות זולתה אלא שרוצה לגרוף כדי לייפות הפת אסור אבל אם א"א לאפות בלא גריפ' מותר עכ"ל ותמה ב"י כיון שסתם תנורי' דידן א"א בלא גריפה לא היה לכתוב אם אפשר בלא גריפה וע"כ כתב כאן בש"ע באמת כן גם רש"ל הקשה כן ותי' דמיירי בגחלים דקים שנשארו אחר כיבוי הראשון ול"נ דלק"מ דודאי אין אפשרות להושיב הפת על הגחלים אלא צריך לפנות מקום למושב הפת ולכבות שם הגחלים אלא דהטור מיירי במה שגורף כל הגחלים שבתנור ומכבה אותן ובזה חילוק שאם אין הכרח להאפיה בשביל זה אסור אפי' אם מהני גריפת כל התנור כדי ליפותו ואע"ג דמצינו בגמ' דא"ל ר"ח לדביתהו חזי דאנא ריפתי מעלי' בעי ורמז לה שתגרף התם הי' הפת מתחרך מחמת הטיט שנפל משא"כ בתנורים שלנו שאנו גורפין התנור בשביל יפוי לחוד בלי חשש חירוך וזהו שמסיק הטור אבל אם א"א לאפות בלא גריפ' מותר פי' שיש חשש חירוך מחמת הגחלים שיש סביבו והשכב' לא שייך בתנורים דידן כיון שהלחם על קרקע התנור מותר לגרוף כל התנור. והקש' מו"ח ז"ל דלמה יאס' הכיבוי כדי לייפותו כיון דמותר בשביל אוכל נפש נימא ביה מתוך שהותרו לצורך הותר נמי שלא לצורך כיון שיש בו צורך קצת כדאמרי' בהבער' מתוך כו' כמ"ש סי' חצ"ה ותי' ע"פ דברי התוס' בפ"ב דף כ"ג ד"ה ע"ג חרס דדוקא גבי הבער' אמרינן מתוך כו'. כיון שיש לפעמים לצורך תיקון אוכל נפש מה שאין כן בכיבוי שלעולם אינו לצורך תיקון אוכל נפש ואפי' להרא"ש דכתב כאן דכיבוי לצורך אוכל נפש מותר מ"מ אין זה קרוי אוכל נפש. דאינו אלא להציל שלא יזיקנו חום האש אבל הכריע בשולחן ערוך יפה כאן שמותר הכיבוי גם בשביל הייפוי לחוד דהכל היא צורך אוכל נפש והוא דעת הרשב"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
Since it is impossible to bake, etc. In the Tur it is written: but in our ovens, where they extinguish the coals by scraping them, if it is possible to bake without that, only that he wishes to scrape in order to beautify the bread, it is forbidden; but if it is impossible to bake without scraping, it is permitted; end quote. And Beis Yosef wondered: since our ovens as a rule are impossible without scraping, he should not have written "if it is possible without scraping." And therefore here in the Shulchan Aruch he in truth wrote accordingly. Maharshal also asked thus, and answered that it deals with thin coals that remained after the first extinguishing. And it appears to me that there is no difficulty: for certainly there is no possibility of sitting the bread on the coals; rather one must clear a place for the seat of the bread and extinguish the coals there. Rather, the Tur deals with scraping all the coals in the oven and extinguishing them; and in this there is a distinction: if there is no necessity for the baking because of this, it is forbidden even if scraping the whole oven helps to beautify it. And even though we find in the Gemara that Rabbi Chiya said to his wife, "See that I want nice bread," and hinted to her that she scrape, there the bread was being scorched because of the clay that fell; which is not so in our ovens, where we scrape the oven only for beautification alone without concern of scorching. And that is what the Tur concludes: but if it is impossible to bake without scraping, it is permitted; meaning, where there is concern of scorching because of the coals around it; and laying does not apply in our ovens, since the bread is on the floor of the oven; it is permitted to scrape the whole oven. And my master and father-in-law of blessed memory asked: why should extinguishing be forbidden in order to beautify it, since it is permitted for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul; let us say regarding it "since they were permitted for a need, they were also permitted not for a need," since there is some need in it, as we say regarding burning "since," etc., as he wrote in siman 495. And he answered according to the words of Tosafos in chapter 2, folio 23, s.v. "upon a pottery shard": that specifically regarding burning we say "since," etc., because sometimes there is need for preparing food-preparation needs of the soul; which is not so regarding extinguishing, which is never for the sake of preparing food-preparation needs of the soul. And even according to the Rosh who wrote here that extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul is permitted, nevertheless this is not called food-preparation needs of the soul, for it is only to save so that the heat of the fire not damage it. But the Shulchan Aruch decided well here that extinguishing is also permitted for beautification alone, for all of it is a need of food-preparation needs of the soul; and that is the view of the Rashba.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>ומותר לגבל האפר. </b> כ"כ הרמב"ם ורי"ף ור"ת הקש' ע"ז דהא אפר בר גיבול הוא וכמ"ש סי' שכ"ד תי' ה' המגיד דכיון דא"א לעשות גיבול זה מעי"ט שמתיבש לשעתו הלכך מותר לגבלו בי"ט משום אוכל נפש ורש"ל פסק כיון דלר"ת והרא"ש אסו' אין להתיר בגיבול האפר במים בי"ט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is permitted to knead the ash.</b> So wrote the Rambam and the Rif; and Rabbeinu Tam asked on this, for ash is subject to kneading, as he wrote in siman 324. The Maggid answered: since it is impossible to do this kneading from before Yom Tov, for it dries at its time, therefore it is permitted to knead it on Yom Tov because of food-preparation needs of the soul. And Maharshal ruled that since according to Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh it is forbidden, one should not permit kneading ash with water on Yom Tov.
