**** ENGLISH ****
{8} He adds upon them prepared wood, etc. - And even though this wood has a permitted form tomorrow, and something that has a permitted form is not canceled even in a thousand, and even regarding rabbinic issurim: this issur is lighter still, for the primary benefit of the wood comes after heating, when he bakes with it [or warms his house], and then the issur is already not present; and in such a case the Sages were not stringent because it is something that has a permitted form. And also if after heating he wishes to set a pot to cook opposite the fire [or to warm his body, or even to benefit from the light of the fire] while the wood is burning, it is permitted; for muktzeh is not forbidden for benefit that comes of itself, only that it is forbidden to move muktzeh or to use it even without moving, as above in siman 501 in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 23 and 24. And here, since he uses it with permission for baking bread, automatically it is permitted to benefit also in other benefits, even though were it not for the benefits the wood would be muktzeh. And there are those who are lenient also to heat l'chatchilah in order to set a pot then, or to warm oneself opposite the fire; for even though the wood is still burning, nevertheless the primary benefit is the eating, and at the time he eats he benefits only from heat in the cooked food, and that heat is already separated from the fire in the wood that is present; and likewise one who warms himself opposite the fire: the primary benefit comes to him from the heat, and the issur has already been finished. However, to heat in order to benefit from its light is certainly forbidden, for he benefits from the fire that is present; as below in siman 677, se'if 4, regarding a Chanukah lamp; and see Biur Halacha.

{22} And specifically in ovens, etc. - The language is somewhat murky. And in the Rosh, from whom these words derive, it is stated thus: that the Sages permitted to scrape only oven plaster, which is only moving muktzeh; but to scrape coals, by which one comes to extinguishing, they did not permit. And all this is in their ovens, where they stuck the bread to the walls of the oven; and if so, the coals at its bottom cannot burn the bread, only scorch a little so that the bread not be nice; and for this reason they did not permit scraping coals which comes to extinguishing. But in our ovens, where we bake on the bottom of the oven, even coals are permitted to scrape, because it is entirely impossible to bake. This is the content of the words of the Rosh, and so is the intent of the Mechaber.
