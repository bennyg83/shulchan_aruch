**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1.</b> The time of blowing the shofar is by day and not by night, and its mitzvah is from the time of sunrise etc. Note: So it is in the Tur; and one should examine why in the Mishnah of Megillah, in the latter clause (regarding every matter whose mitzvah is by day, it is valid all day) it also listed there taking the lulav and shofar; while in the first clause, regarding "they do not circumcise and do not sprinkle etc.," it did not list shofar and lulav, only milah and sprinkling and immersion. Also the Tur's words have no understanding: here he wrote this law, and in Hilchos Lulav he wrote simply that its mitzvah is from when dawn rises, and did not write at all that ideally its mitzvah is from sunrise. And to reconcile the Tur's words it would appear that he holds that since the main concern of before sunrise is by force of a doubt of night (as Rashi explained, and it is brought in Magen Avraham in this siman) and one may say the intent is: by force of "the diligent advance to mitzvos," there is concern that because he is eager to be among the diligent he will not examine carefully and it will seem to him that it is day while it is still night; therefore they set him a fixed time at sunrise, for it is impossible to err. And accordingly one may say that because of concern of a wasted blessing they would not have enacted, for they hold like the view of Tosafos that every [ordinary] blessing is only rabbinic and it is a rabbinic doubt; rather, for concern of nullifying the body of the mitzvah they so enacted; and Megillah too, since it is words of kabbalah [Prophets], is like words of Torah, and its doubt is to be stringent. One may say: specifically regarding Megillah and milah and sprinkling and immersion (where people do not tend to do so several times in one day (for milah is impossible; and likewise sprinkling) he would become tamei again with a second sprinkling; and likewise immersion (people do not tend to do so several times in one day; and likewise Megillah) therefore there is concern that they may err that it is day and fulfill with that reading and not read a second time and be nullified from their mitzvah; therefore they enacted with sunrise. But regarding lulav, where the meticulous tend to hold the lulav in their hand all day, and likewise even other people tend to take it several times a day, if so there is no concern of nullifying the mitzvah, for even if it was still night his end is to take it again in actual day; therefore they did not decree on this to wait until sunrise. And with this one may explain why the Mishnah did not list shofar: one may say that specifically Megillah and sprinkling and immersion (which people do not enjoy and certainly will not add to do so many times for nothing) therefore they were concerned lest he take it before day and be nullified from the mitzvah; but regarding shofar, where many people enjoy when they blow, as Kol Bo wrote, if so, automatically even if he already fulfilled, his end is to blow further, and there is no basis to decree to wait until sunrise; only according to the view of several that after sunset one should not blow further for nothing, if so again it is proper to be concerned as above. And the She'elas Yaavetz in a responsum of Hilchos Rosh Hashanah discusses at length that one may say it is permitted to blow all day by force of "mitochn"; but in my opinion this is not so, for "mitochn" applies only to what was permitted by way of optional [use], for example melachah of Yom Tov that one is not obligated to do on Yom Tov and is only optional and was permitted, therefore "mitochn" applies; but what is not optional but obligatory. "mitochn" does not apply to it, and not for the sake of the mitzvah is forbidden; however that is according to the view that women are forbidden to blow; but according to the view that women are permitted to blow, if so in that respect it is optional and permitted, and "mitochn" applies, and automatically it is permitted to blow entirely; and therefore the Mishnah comes like the view that women are permitted to blow (that women may lean [on a korban] as optional) and automatically every person may blow as he wishes; therefore they did not decree on this to wait until sunrise by force of something like a double doubt: perhaps it is already day, and even if you say it is night, perhaps he will blow further and afterward fulfill his obligation, which is not so regarding Megillah and the others, where people do not tend to do so for nothing, and anything uncommon does not enter the category of a double doubt; which is not so regarding shofar, where this is the way, since many people enjoy when they blow, and it is properly a double doubt. But the Tur goes according to his view that it is forbidden to blow for nothing, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name in siman 596; and therefore if he has fulfilled his obligation he will not blow further; therefore ideally he must wait until sunrise; and regarding lulav, where certainly his way is to take it several times, it is an excellent double doubt (therefore he does not require waiting until sunrise; and he proved so from the Mishnah, since it did not list lulav and not shofar) perforce as above; and it fits well. And according to the above one may also reconcile what is in the Yerushalmi of Megillah on the Mishnah "they do not read etc. until sunrise etc.," and these are its words: "But do not the Rabbis advance for intercalation of the year?" and it answers: "It is a mitzvah to advance in order to spur on in mitzvos" (end quote) and it has no understanding. And it appears in intent according to Rashi's explanation that the concern of sunrise is because not everyone is expert; and if so there is no difficulty from intercalation of the month, for there one may say the Sages are meticulous and will not err, as written in the Talmud in Pesachim and Eruvin that one holds "members of a fellowship are meticulous"; and even though we do not hold so, one may say regarding the Sages who go up for intercalation of the year that certainly one may say so, for they are meticulous; only the Yerushalmi's assumption was that the main reason we ideally require sunrise is because until then it is night by rabbinic law, and not by force of concern of error, and therefore there is no reason to distinguish between intercalation of the year and other matters, and therefore it challenges from its intercalation; and to this it answers that the reason is not because until sunrise it is night, but because it is a mitzvah to advance and spur on in mitzvos; and therefore by force of being eager we are concerned that he will come to err and do before dawn; since the concern is only by force of error, automatically there is no challenge from intercalation, for the Rabbis are meticulous, and it fits well; and study carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(There, se'if 3)</b> He heard nine tekios from nine people who all blew as one; he has not fulfilled etc. Note: Here there are variant readings in the Talmud: some read "he has fulfilled," and there is a version that he has not fulfilled. And in my humble opinion the essential [reading] is like that version that he has not fulfilled, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote; and it is compelled in the Talmud from what we read there in tractate Rosh Hashanah, folio 34: R' Yochanan said, one who heard nine tekios from nine people in nine hours of the day has fulfilled etc. and did R' Yochanan say thus, for R' Yochanan said regarding Hallel and Megillah: once he waited long enough to complete all of it, he returns to the beginning etc.? And it is very difficult for me: what does he challenge according to those who hold (brought in the Shulchan Aruch, Hilchos Krias Shema, siman 64) that waiting long enough to complete all of it [causing] return to the beginning is only when he waited because of coercion, but when not because of coercion he does not return, if so what does he challenge: perhaps R' Yochanan here deals with interruption not because of coercion, therefore he has fulfilled? And this is a great wonder according to that view, and I have not seen anyone who noticed this; therefore it appears to answer thus: the reason of the view that distinguishes between waiting because of coercion or not must perforce be the reason that it resembles what we hold; whatever is fit for kneading, kneading does not prevent it, and what is not fit for kneading, kneading prevents it; so too here, if it was not under coercion and it was fit to be together, therefore even though it is not together it also helps; but where it was under coercion and not fit for kneading, kneading prevents it; and therefore it fits well there; but regarding tekios, since he heard nine tekios from nine people as one he has not fulfilled by force of there not being a plain sound before it and a plain sound after it, and why should we not say: behold it is fit for kneading and fit for them to be heard one after another? It is proven from this that regarding plain before it and plain after it, being fit for kneading does not help; rather we require that it actually be before him and after him; and if so here too, if there was interruption of another matter, this is not within "before it and after it." Only granted according to the view that waiting long enough to complete all of it does not [require] return even where it was under coercion and not fit for kneading; perforce according to him waiting is not considered an interruption by itself; if so automatically it is also considered "before it and after it"; but according to the view that waiting long enough to complete all of it [requires] return to its beginning (perforce this is considered an interruption; if so, granted that where it was not under coercion it is not an interruption) that is for another reason, that it is fit for kneading; but if so regarding tekios, since he heard from nine people as one he has not fulfilled, for we require before it and after it, and behold it is fit for kneading, and perforce regarding before it and after it being fit for kneading does not help, and we require actually before it and after it; if so where there is interruption it is not actually before it and after it, and being fit for kneading does not help on this; and he challenges well. Therefore the matters compel that we read here "he has not fulfilled" for if the reading were "he has fulfilled," our difficulty stands in its place: what does he challenge? Therefore in any case for those who hold there that specifically through coercion he has not fulfilled, this reading is compelled; and even for those who hold that in every case he has not fulfilled, nevertheless our difficulty is hard, since the Talmud wants to prove from this that in every case of waiting he does not return, and if so, whence do we know this: perhaps in truth there is a distinction between where it is fit for kneading or not, as we hold in several places, and we may say that what R' Yochanan said that he returns is where it was not possible at all, but where possible he does not return, and what does he challenge? <b>(There, se'if 5)</b> A Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos; one does not blow the shofar. Note: I was in doubt regarding Rosh Hashanah that fell on Shabbos, and one transgressed or forgot and blew one tekiah or two and did not complete the third; do we say that he should complete the third or not? For one may say: granted that the Sages upheld their words in the face of a mitzvah not to blow; that is by passive abstinence, then it is comfortable for them to uphold their enactment and they uprooted words of Torah by passive abstinence; but in this case where he has already transgressed a positive commandment of their words by active deed and has already blown, and if he blows further, granted that he transgresses their words, nevertheless he performs a mitzvah and fulfills the Torah mitzvah of blowing; but if he does not blow further, then he has not fulfilled the mitzvah at all, and in the end he has transgressed their prohibition and already blown, and if so he loses: he has already done a prohibition and not fulfilled any mitzvah, since he did not complete the tekios, for every tekiah we require a plain sound before it and a plain sound after it; in such a case perhaps the Sages did not uphold their words, since in the end their rabbinic enactment was not fulfilled, for he has already blown, whether we say that in this they permitted him to complete the tekios or not. And apparently I wanted to resolve this doubt from Beitzah, folio 21, from the beraita of the sheep of Atzeres that one slaughtered not for their sake or slaughtered before their time etc. and if it was Shabbos he should not throw [the blood], and if he threw, it is accepted for burning the emurim in the evening; and there the Talmud says: if he threw; after the fact yes, but ideally no; and behold there if he will not throw, a Torah prohibition is made retroactively, and even so they upheld their words not to throw on Shabbos, even if by not throwing he has not fulfilled the mitzvah at all, and if he throws he has fulfilled the mitzvah of the korban which is valid at least; but when we examine carefully there is still room to distinguish: there, even if the korban would be valid, he has done a Torah transgression (for ideally it was forbidden to offer this korban) and he has still desecrated Shabbos according to the Torah even if he throws; and further, there it is not the day's obligation since he slaughtered them not for their sake or before their time, which is not so here, where it is the mitzvah of the day according to the Torah thereby, and if he blows according to law he fulfills his obligation according to the Torah and has done no transgression except that he transgressed a decree of their words. Rather certainly it is proven from this that on Shabbos even if he took of the permitted and blew of the permitted he has not fulfilled, and he is obligated to recite Shehecheyanu on the second day according to law. One may also say on this that therefore it does not help for him even after the fact by force of it being a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since he transgressed a rabbinic [law]; and behold even regarding a rabbinic prohibition it is considered a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as is proven in chapter 2 of Pesachim regarding tevel that is tevel by rabbinic law, and Rashi explained there (see there); and Eshel Avraham there did not notice this, and there is no leisure to elaborate. And one should note on this: why then did Rava in Beitzah folio 19 give another reason for immersing vessels (because of repairing) and not say the reason "lest he carry it" and behold that reason is compelled regarding shofar and lulav; and see the commentators; and according to what I wrote one may say: for it is implied according to Rava in all these that even after the fact it does not help, for if not so no Tanna would have omitted teaching us that it is specifically ideally, and after the fact he has fulfilled; and perforce after the fact also it does not help; and accordingly it fits well: granted regarding these that are a mitzvah it is fine, since they forbade ideally because of "lest he carry it," automatically after the fact he has not fulfilled, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; but regarding immersing vessels, which is not a mitzvah, by force of the decree of "lest he carry it" it would be proper that after the fact it help, since what was was, and even if we say he has not fulfilled, the concern we already had is still not removed for us; and therefore it would be proper to say that after the fact it helps; therefore he needed the reason of "repairing," and with this after the fact properly does not help, for by this the prohibition is repaired, since if it does not help he has repaired nothing; but if after the fact it helps he has properly repaired; therefore to repair the prohibition we say that even after the fact it does not help; but by force of "lest he carry it," that concern is not repaired even if we say that after the fact it does not help; and since it would not be repaired it would be proper to say that after the fact it helps; therefore Rava did not want to answer on this "lest he carry it" why should it not help after the fact? But there regarding a mitzvah, that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, that reason properly suffices, since ideally at least it is forbidden by force of "lest he carry it," and automatically even after the fact it does not help since it was a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and therefore the aforementioned answer fits better: according to Rava to his view who answers in Beitzah by force of "lest he repair," if so perforce he holds that after the fact it does not help in these; and therefore he challenges from "if he blew he has not fulfilled," and it is impossible to answer regarding Shabbos that on Shabbos even with the permitted he has not fulfilled, and the aforementioned law is compelled that after the fact also he has not fulfilled; and not by force of Eshel Avraham's reason that after the fact it does not help, for if so Rava still would not have needed another reason for immersing vessels, only as our words by force of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and therefore regarding vessels he needed another reason by force of repairing; and it fits; and study carefully on this; and in my work on Orach Chayim of the year 5592 in siman 588 I elaborated on this (see there and study carefully).
