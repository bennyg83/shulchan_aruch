**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For Rabbeinu Tam and the Rashba etc.</b> Another sound etc. (regarding that which we say at the end of Rosh Hashanah: R' Abbahu enacted in Caesarea that they blow tekiah, shevarim, teruah, tekiah) for he was in doubt what a teruah is: whether moans, namely shevarim, or wails, namely teruah, and he does both. Rav Aveira challenged: perhaps it is moans, and the teruah interrupts between the shevarim and the final tekiah. And the Ran asked: what of it; did not R' Yochanan say that one who heard nine tekios in a day has fulfilled, even though there is interruption between them? And Rabbeinu Tam answered, and so the Rashba holds for one answer: granted that after the fact he has fulfilled, nevertheless ideally one should not interrupt. And the Ramban answered: granted that waiting is not an interruption; nevertheless if he blows between them a tekiah for the sake of the mitzvah it is an interruption; and we hold like the Ramban below in siman 590. If so, according to Rabbeinu Tam and the Rashba, even though he heard another sound for the sake of the mitzvah, nevertheless he has fulfilled, for this addresses Rav Aveira's challenge that the teruah blown out of doubt is an interruption; and even so he concludes that specifically ideally [one should not], but after the fact he has fulfilled, like one who heard nine tekios, as above, behold, even another sound, namely a teruah that is irrelevant if it is moans, nevertheless after the fact is not an interruption.

And Magen Avraham explained their words in what they wrote "there is an act of skill") namely, lest he repair a musical instrument. And that is to say: because of one shevus alone they would not uphold their words; therefore regarding lulav there is only one shevus ("lest he carry it") therefore the first day that falls on Shabbos overrides Shabbos; but regarding shofar there are two shevusim (lest he repair a musical instrument and lest he carry it) therefore they upheld their words when it falls on Shabbos, that it does not override Shabbos, even though it is according to the Torah in the provinces. And accordingly, even without this, Maharam Padua's difficulty is not difficult (that one should have said [the reason is] lest he repair a musical instrument) for in truth that is the Gemara's intention in what it wrote "lest he carry it": that is to say, there is also the decree of "lest he carry it" in addition to "lest he repair a musical instrument," so that now there are two shevusim; but because of one shevus they would not uphold their words.
