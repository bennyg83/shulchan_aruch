<b>However.</b> See Taz. His words are founded upon truth and sound reasoning. In siman 202, seif 1, everything follows the majority, while in letter 9 the Taz asks: if so, when vegetables are cooked in water, the water is the majority. He answers there that water is not significant and is subordinate to the flavor of the vegetables. There is support for this, since water has no independent flavor; therefore, the vegetable flavor within it is primary even though the water is the majority. This differs from wine mixed into beer and the like, where even though the wine's taste is perceptible, the majority component has an independent flavor. Accordingly, when vegetables are cooked with meat, the water receives a good flavor from the meat, so the vegetable flavor becomes subordinate to the majority water, since the water now has a good meat flavor. From this he derives that if vegetables are cooked in vinegar or in the beverage called borscht, each of which has an independent flavor, the blessing follows the majority component, namely the vinegar or borscht. Even if cooked with meat, one blesses over the liquid according to the blessing of the beverage, which is primary. If this borscht is made from red beets, called burkes, its blessing outside a meal is Borei Peri HaAdamah; the meat requires Shehakol. Borscht made from bran water requires Shehakol, even if made from wheat or barley flour, as stated in siman 208, seif 5. Regarding wine vinegar, one might initially think that if it was cooked with meat and sweetened for drinking its blessing would be Borei Peri HaGafen, but this is mistaken, for vinegar requires Shehakol since it was not planted for this purpose and changed for the worse; see Magen Avraham, siman 204, letter 12. It must therefore refer to a borscht beverage made from red beets. From here it follows that when meat and vegetables are cooked in a pot without added water, and much liquid is released from the vegetables, one should bless Borei Peri HaAdamah over the vegetable liquid, for it is not nullified to the small amount of fat released from the meat. See Magen Avraham, letter 5. With Hashem's help, this will be explained there.
