(ה) כשהם חיים בפה"א - כתבו האחרונים דדוקא בשומים רכים דאז דרך בני אדם לאוכלן חיים אבל שומים שהזקינו שהם חריפים מאד ואינו ראוי לאוכלן חי [בלא פת] אם אכלו מברך שהכל וכן בבצלים דינא הכי דאם אוכלן רכים בפה"א הזקינו שהכל ועיין בשע"ת שמצדד לומר דאפילו רכים אינו מברך בפה"א רק במדינות שדרך לאוכלן רכים בלא פת אבל במדינותינו שלעולם אין דרך בנ"א לאכול שומים ובצלים חיים אף כשהם רכים בלא פת אין מברך עליהם אלא שהכל וכן מצדד בנשמת אדם לברך שהכל. וע"ש עוד בשע"ת שכתב דבמבושלים בין שומים ובצלים רכים ובין כשהזקינו לעולם אינו מברך אלא שהכל ואפילו בישלן אם בשר:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) When raw borei peri haAdamah — the latter authorities wrote: specifically soft garlic, for then it is people's way to eat them raw; but garlic that has aged, which is very sharp and not fit to eat raw [without bread] — if one ate it, one blesses shehakol. And the same law applies to onions: if one eats them soft — borei peri haAdamah; aged — shehakol. And see Shaarei Teshuvah, who tends to say that even soft ones one does not bless borei peri haAdamah except in countries where the way is to eat them soft without bread; but in our countries it is never people's way to eat garlic and onions raw — even when soft, without bread — one does not bless on them except shehakol. And Nishmat Adam likewise tends to bless shehakol. And see there further in Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that when cooked — whether garlic and onions are soft or aged — one never blesses except shehakol, even if cooked with meat.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ז) From themselves — see the latter authorities who wrote that if one was cooking vegetables with meat [or frying in fat] and his intention is certainly also to eat the vegetables themselves, he certainly must bless on them borei peri haAdamah — even if those vegetables are by nature accustomed to change for the worse when cooked without meat. And this case is different, for the fats were not put in the pot for themselves but only to give taste in the meat; but they automatically also receive taste from the meat and are improved — therefore one blesses on them only with the meat's blessing. And accordingly, if one fried onions in oil or butter in order to improve them, one blesses on them borei peri haAdamah, for the onions changed for the better through frying and they are the main item for him; and the same applies in all such cases.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ח) On the water in which they cooked — and if one soaked vegetables in water, Beit Yosef wrote in siman 202 that its law is like cooked, and as is accepted universally that pickled is like cooked; nevertheless, in the sauce of pickled cucumbers — the pickling is only for the cucumbers themselves to sour, and he has no intention at all for the pickling liquid — one blesses on the sauce shehakol [Taz in siman 202 seif 10, and so Eshel Avraham] [and in a place where the custom of most people of the city is to dip bread in cucumber-pickle water, one must bless borei peri haAdamah even when drinking the liquid alone]. He also wrote there: Eshel Avraham — the same applies to compote sauce; and in our countries they call them kraut, and one blesses shehakol [and this does not seem correct per the custom of our countries, who eat the sauce like the compote itself]. And all this when eating sauce of raw kraut; but when cooked with the kraut and one wants to drink the sauce alone, Taz agrees one blesses borei peri haAdamah like other water of cooked vegetables. And sauce of pickled beets that they call rassel — even if cooked alone, it appears from Taz one blesses borei peri haAdamah, since the beets were pickled in them and the intention of pickling is also for them, and it is customary to dip bread in it; and Chayei Adam tends likewise. Nevertheless, when drinking them raw it is reasonable to bless on them shehakol, because most of their eating is through cooking. And a beverage of borscht made from bran water and flour — one blesses on it shehakol whether drinking it raw or cooked [latter authorities].

(11) That one blesses, etc. — and all this regarding types of vegetables; but dumplings cooked in water [farfele lokshn kneidlach] and he does not want to eat the dumplings but only to drink the water alone — it is uncertain whether he should bless on them mezonot like the dumplings or shehakol. Therefore he should take a little of the dumplings and bless mezonot, and also bless on the rest whose blessing is shehakol, and eat from both types in the measure so he can bless al hamichya and also borei nefashot [and this is specifically in this case where his main desire is only to eat the sauce of the dumplings; but if he also wants to eat the dumplings themselves as is customary, he blesses on the dumplings mezonot and thereby exempts the sauce as well per all views, because it is secondary]. And Chayei Adam wrote that also in this case it is better to bless shehakol on the rest, as above.

(12) Even though there is only, etc. — specifically when he cooked the vegetable in plain water that has no taste in itself and one senses in it only the taste of the vegetable; but if he cooked the vegetable in vinegar or in a beverage made from bran water and flour [called kvass] that have taste in themselves, the beverage is not drawn after the vegetable, and one blesses on it shehakol when drinking them alone. And the same applies to borscht beverage, which is water in which sour beets were soaked — if he cooked vegetables with meat in them and wants to drink them alone, one does not bless on the borscht shehakol because of meat taste absorbed in it, as will be explained — rather borei peri haAdamah, since the beverage has its own taste apart from the meat [Taz]. Nevertheless, when he also eats the meat it appears better to bless on the meat shehakol and intend to exempt the sauce too, for it is reasonable that when eating together the sauce is secondary relative to the meat.

(יח) הלפת כשהוא חי וכו' - הלפת טוב שיותר מבושל מחי [טור] ולפת הוא מה שאנו קורין מייארי"ן וריבי"ן ועיין במ"א שמפקפק בדין זה ועיין בטור שמביא ג"כ שיש גאונים שסוברין לברך על חי בפה"א ועיין בח"א שכתב דתלוי אם דרך בנ"א באותו מקום לאוכלן חיין ועיין בנ"א שהאריך בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(18) Turnip when it is raw, etc. — turnip is better cooked than raw [Tur]; turnip is what we call meyerin and rubin; see Magen Avraham who is uncertain about this law; see Tur who also brings that some Geonim hold to bless on raw borei peri haAdamah; see Chayei Adam who wrote that it depends whether the custom of people in that place is to eat them raw; see Nishmat Adam who expanded on this.
