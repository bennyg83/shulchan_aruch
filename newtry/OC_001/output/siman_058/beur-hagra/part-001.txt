**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The end of three hours, etc.</b> Rambam, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and the other poskim understand that the phrase “three hours” means all three hours, for otherwise it should have said, “until the third hour.” In the Yerushalmi, chapter 1, R' Zeira said in the name of R' Ami: “In the days of R' Yehoshua, we would go out to a fast and recite Keriat Shema after three hours, and he did not protest.” R' Yose and R' Acha went out to a fast. The congregation came and recited Keriat Shema after three hours. R' Acha wished to protest, but R' Yose said to him: “Have they not already recited it at its proper time? Do they recite it now for any reason other than to stand in prayer after words of Torah?” He replied: “It is because of the uninformed, lest they say that they are now reciting it at its proper time.” In chapter 4 there, R' Yose prayed at three hours, R' Chiyya bar Abba prayed at three hours, and R' Berechiah saw him recite Keriat Shema and pray after three hours. The Yerushalmi asks from the Mishnah that one who recites from then onward has not lost, etc., and answers that he had already accepted the yoke of Heaven at its proper time. Likewise, the Gemara on 7a speaks of the first three hours of the day. This is unlike Re'em and Rashi, who wrote that the time extends only until the very beginning of the third hour, based on the passage on 3b concerning two hours of the day. See Tosafos to Avodah Zarah 4b, s.v. Bitlas. Their proof is thereby refuted, for the reference is to the entire third hour. Hagahot Maimoniyot offers another answer; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 3, And one who, etc. </b>This follows from the Gemara there on 8b, where the halachah is ruled in accordance with Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. The commentators ask that we otherwise rule like the Others and like the vasikin. The Rif and the other poskim explain that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai refers to an after-the-fact case, or alternatively to pressing circumstances, such as one who must arise early to travel and similar cases, just as the Gemara says regarding Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's other ruling that it applies in pressing circumstances. They challenged this from the ruling concerning one who arose early, where it says that when the proper time arrives he should recite. The Rashba answers that the case in chapter 1 concerns one who departs from an inhabited place, while Rabbeinu Yonah answers that the members of the caravan will not wait for him, as they explain. The statement in Yoma that one who recites with the men of the watch has not fulfilled his obligation means that he has not fulfilled the choicest manner of the mitzvah, as Beit Yosef explains. See Tosafos 12a, s.v. Mishum, where there is a scribal error and the text should read, ‘as stated in Yoma, one who recites, etc.’ Rashi there likewise explains that the baraisa can be understood as referring to the baraisa in Yoma, meaning that it does not accept Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's ruling. This is also explicit in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1 of Berachos, where that baraisa is shown to disagree with Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. The Yerushalmi says regarding Rabban Gamliel that his ruling follows Rabbi Shimon, who taught in Rabbi Shimon's name that one may recite one before dawn and one after dawn. It then asks whether, just as Rabban Gamliel follows Rabbi Shimon regarding the evening, the same applies in the morning, or whether the ruling follows Rabbi Zeira, brother of Rav Chana, that one who recites with the men of the watch has not fulfilled his obligation. This answers the Baal HaMaor's objection to the Rif from that baraisa, for the Rif ruled like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, who disagrees with it. The Bavli also indicates this, for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi says that the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. Since a ruling of halachah implies a dispute, the opposing view must be the aforementioned baraisa. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi ruled like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, and the Rif therefore did so as well. The Tur, however, explains that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai applies only occasionally, but one who acts this way regularly has not fulfilled his obligation even after the fact. In that type of case the ruling in Yoma applies. This is why the Tur writes that if he recited it after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation and need not recite it again, provided that he is not accustomed to doing so. The Rashba and Shulchan Aruch also appear to rule this way.

<b>אם נאנס כו'. </b>כמ"ש שם בגמ' להדיא כי אתא כו' בשעת הדחק וכ' הרא"ש והשתא לא פליגי הא דרשב"י אהדדי וכן הא דפסק שם כר"ג דבלא אונס עד ע"ה ובאונס עד הנץ החמה וכן בק"ש של שחרית בלא אונס כנ"ל ובאונס מע"ה והקשה תוס' שם בד"ה לעולם כו' וביומא דהא קי"ל כאחרים ור"ע עצמו אמר בין חמור לערוד וכאן אמר בלא אונס מנץ החמה ותי' שם ושם דהקמ"ל דבלילה אינו יוצא אחר הנץ החמה אפילו באונס וז"ש כיון שעדיין כו' וכ"כ הרא"ש ועוד תי' דקמ"ל דאינו יכול לקרות שניהם וכ' ברמזים וב"י שקורא של ערבית דשחרית אפשר לקרות אח"כ וז"ש ואם היה כו' ובטור השמיטו דס"ל כתי' ראשון וב"י ס"ל דשניהם אמת לדינא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he was prevented by circumstances, etc. </b>This is explicit in the Gemara there, which says that when Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai came, his ruling was accepted in pressing circumstances. The Rosh writes that the two rulings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai therefore do not conflict. Likewise, the ruling there in accordance with Rabban Gamliel means that without compelling circumstances the evening Krias Shema may be recited only until dawn, but under compulsion until sunrise; similarly, the morning Krias Shema begins at its normal time as explained above, but under compulsion it may be recited from dawn. Tosafos there, s.v. Le'olam, and in Yoma asks that we otherwise rule like the Others, and that Rabbi Akiva himself stated that the time is when one can distinguish between a donkey and a wild donkey, whereas here the time without compulsion is said to begin at sunrise. Tosafos answers that the novelty is that the evening Krias Shema cannot be recited after sunrise even under compulsion. This is the meaning of the words, ‘since it is still, etc.,’ and the Rosh writes likewise. Tosafos further answers that the novelty is that one cannot recite both the evening and morning Krias Shema during the same period. The Rosh writes in his abridged rulings, and Beit Yosef explains, that one should then recite the evening Krias Shema, since the morning Krias Shema can still be recited afterward. This is the meaning of, ‘And if he was, etc.’ The Tur omitted this because he follows the first answer, while Beit Yosef holds that both answers are accepted as halachah.
