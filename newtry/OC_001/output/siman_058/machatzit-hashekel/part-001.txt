**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In the Gemara, page 3a, in Tosafot, etc.</b> For it was taught: Rabbi Eliezer says the night consists of three watches, and there is a sign for the matter: in the first watch the donkey brays, etc.; in the second watch, etc.; in the third watch, etc. The Gemara asks what he is counting: if he counts the beginning of the watch, why does the first watch need a sign, it is evening and is recognizable; and if he counts the end of the watch, why does the end of the third watch need a sign, it is daytime and the beginning of the day is recognizable. It answers that he always counts the end of the watch, and the sign for the end of the third watch is needed for one who sleeps in a dark house and does not know when the time of Krias Shema is; when he sees the sign of the end of the third watch he should rise and read. Tosafot wrote: but Rabbi Eliezer requires until one can distinguish between blue and green; and they answer that nevertheless, since he knows when alot hashachar rises, before he rises and prepares himself that time has arrived. Certainly the Magen Avraham's intent is not to prove that we count the three hours from alot hashachar in order to resolve Tosafot's question, for it is not the way of the Magen Avraham to disagree with Tosafot without necessity. Rather, his intent is that from our own reasoning we would not know whether the end of the third watch is at alot hashachar or at netz hachamah; therefore he hinted to examine the Gemara and Tosafot, for from Tosafot's language it is clear that the end of the third watch is alot hashachar. This is preparation for what the Magen Avraham writes afterward regarding the Gemara's question from David. Since the end of the third watch is alot hashachar, the Gemara regarding David proves that the three hours are counted from alot hashachar: Rabbi says there are four watches in the night, because one verse says 'At midnight I arise,' and another says 'My eyes preceded the watches.' According to Rabbi Nosson, who says there are three watches, the verses are difficult, for from midnight onward there is only one and a half watches. The Gemara answers that he holds like Rabbi Yehoshua, that the time of Krias Shema is until three hours, for such is the way of princes to rise then; thus six hours of night and two hours of day are two watches. If those hours were counted from netz, there would be more than two hours from alot hashachar, but since the watches end at alot hashachar, the proof is that the three hours are counted from alot hashachar.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>This is the language of the Tur: And the mitzvah in its preferred form, etc.</b> is to read it a little before netz hachamah, so that he finishes it and its blessings with netz hachamah and joins prayer to it. The Rambam likewise wrote that its mitzvah is to begin before netz hachamah, so that he finishes reading and blessing with netz hachamah, and this measure is like the measure of one hour before the sun rises. According to the simple meaning, 'before the sun rises' means the beginning of its rising, namely netz hachamah; and the Beis Yosef asked in the name of Maharai how the Tur called this 'a little before' if it is an hour. Therefore Maharai explained that the Rambam means the end of the sun's rising. The Bach and Darkei Moshe discuss the textual readings and explanations, whether it is an hour or a tenth of an hour, and whether netz means the beginning or the end of the sun's rising.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 5) It is called, etc.</b> And like the men of the mishmar, etc. It is stated in Berachos 11b: there we learned that the appointed officer said to them, meaning to the men of the mishmar, the kohanim serving in the Temple, who were occupied all day with the sacrificial service and therefore read Krias Shema early: bless one blessing, namely one blessing before Krias Shema, and they blessed and read Shema, Vehayah im shamoa, and Vayomer. The Gemara asks which one blessing it was. Rav Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: Ahavah Rabbah. Rabbi Zerika said in the name of Rabbi Ami in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish: Yotzer Or. Rabbi Yitzchak bar Yosef said that the statement of Rabbi Zerika was said by inference, for Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said regarding that Mishnah: this teaches that the blessings do not prevent one another. If one says that they said Yotzer Or, it is well proven that the blessings do not prevent, since if they said Yotzer Or, the time for Ahavah Rabbah had certainly arrived, for even at night it is the time for Ahavah Rabbah, and nevertheless they did not say Ahavah Rabbah, evidently because they lacked time due to the sacrificial service. If so, certainly afterward they did not say Ahavah Rabbah, since they had no time, and nevertheless they said Yotzer Or, so they fulfilled the blessing of Yotzer Or. But if one says they said Ahavah Rabbah, perhaps the blessings do prevent one another, and the men of the mishmar in fact later read Yotzer Or as well; only at the time they read Krias Shema and said Ahavah Rabbah, the time for Yotzer Or had not yet arrived because they were reading early. The Gemara concludes that even if this is by inference, what of it; and it rejects that they may always have said Ahavah Rabbah, and what is meant by this teaches that the blessings do not prevent is that the order of the blessings does not prevent. Although they advanced Ahavah Rabbah before Yotzer Or, nevertheless they fulfilled. On this the Rashba asked: according to the Gemara's rejection, they said both Ahavah Rabbah and Yotzer Or; on the contrary, according to that rejection they were not permitted to say Yotzer Or then, because its time had not yet arrived, even though alot hashachar had already risen. To this the Rashba answered that this is only a mere rejection, showing that no proof can be brought from inference. But in truth, since Rabbi Zerika said in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish that they said Yotzer Or, presumably he learned it from his teacher that Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said so explicitly.

For if so, even after the fourth hour he should read the blessings. **** ENGLISH ****
It is a positive commandment not caused by time, like prayer. Meaning, do not say that it is still a time-caused positive commandment, since in any event the nighttime Shema is not during the day and the daytime Shema is not during the night. This is not so, for the time of Krias Shema, combining night and day, is both by day and by night. Prayer is also this way, for the time of night prayer is not by day and the time of day prayer is not by night, yet it is called not time-caused, since the times of prayer together are at all times.
