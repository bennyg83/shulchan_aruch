<b>ואע"פ שאין השמנים כו'. </b> פי' דבנר של שבת  אסור משום שמא תכבה ויחזור וידליקנ' ובנר של חנוכה היה לנו לחוש אפי' בחול ג"כ שמא תכבה ויפשע ולא יתקן קמ"ל דאין חשש בזה דקי"ל כבתה אין זקוק לה ותו היה לנו לחוש בנר חנוכה ובשבת חנוכה שמא לא תדלק יפה ויטה הנר כמו שאנו חוששין בנר של שבת קמ"ל דזה אין חשש דהא אסור להשתמש לאורה א"כ לא אכפת לי' במה שלא תדליק יפה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And even though the oils, etc.</b> This means that with a Shabbos lamp it is forbidden because perhaps it will go out and he will go back and relight it. With a Chanukah lamp, we also should have been concerned even on a weekday that perhaps it will go out and he will be negligent and not fix it; therefore it teaches us that there is no concern about this, for we hold that if it went out, he is not bound to it. Furthermore, we should have been concerned regarding a Chanukah lamp on Shabbos Chanukah that perhaps it will not burn well and he will tilt the lamp, as we are concerned with a Shabbos lamp. Therefore it teaches us that this is not a concern, since it is forbidden to use its light; if so, he does not care that it does not burn well.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or to count them by its light.</b> In the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rosh, he wrote about this that even a degrading casual use, such as examining coins, is forbidden, because his hands are near the lamp in order to examine them carefully. So too the language implies, for it says, 'opposite the Chanukah lamp,' and it did not say, 'it is forbidden to examine coins by its light.' End quote. The Beis Yosef wrote: It is implied from his words that a casual use that is not degrading, meaning one that does not require his hands to be near the lamp, is permitted, and I do not know why our teacher did not write this. End quote. I am astonished that the Beis Yosef wrote specifically 'that is not degrading,' for even if it is degrading, like the example of examining coins, wherever he does not need to bring his hands near the lamp it is permitted. He should not have mentioned degradation regarding the use at all, but only degradation to the lamp when he brings his hands close to it. This indeed appears in the rulings of the Maharshal, who wrote as follows: Even the casual use of examining coins is forbidden, since he brings his hands near the lamp, but other casual uses are permitted. End quote. But I have difficulty with the words of the Rosh: what forced him to write 'degrading'? What was difficult to him? He came only to answer why Rav Asi had to say that examining coins is forbidden, since it was already ruled that all use of its light is forbidden. About this he answered that the earlier statement referred to fixed use, where one who sees it says that he lit it for that use and not for the sake of the mitzvah, but not to casual use; therefore Rav Asi teaches that even casual use is forbidden just like fixed use. Why, then, did he need to mention 'degrading' and that his hands are near the lamp? Furthermore, what degradation is there in examining coins? It appears to me that one word is missing in the Ashri, and it should read: 'and even a casual use that is not degrading, such as examining coins,' etc. For the reason they stated in the Gemara about this is degradation of the mitzvah, and this applies even when the use is not degrading, since regarding sukkah decorations they also say there that the prohibition is because of degradation of the mitzvah, that one may not benefit from them. So too in siman 674 it is forbidden by means of a splinter because of degradation of the mitzvah, and even to light from one lamp to another there is an opinion that it is forbidden if placement performs the mitzvah. So too he wrote in the remazim, where he did not mention 'degrading.' And since the Tur did not write 'opposite the Chanukah lamp,' but wrote that it is forbidden to use its light, this implies that even if his hands are not near the lamp it is forbidden, just as the Rosh inferred from Rav Asi's language, for he did not say 'by its light.' Therefore, in every manner it is forbidden to use its light, even if his hands are not near the lamp, as is implied by the Shulchan Aruch.

<b>ויש מי שמתיר תשמיש כו'. </b> הוא בעל העיטור בטור ק"ל הא הטעם דאסור להשתמש לאורה היא מפני שיהא ניכר שהוא נר של מצוה כדפירש"י בגמ' וא"כ מ"ט שרי ללמוד אצלו דהרי הרואה סבור שלא לשם מצות נ"ח הדליקו אלא ללמוד ותו דבמאי התירו בחנוכה בשמנים שפסולי' בנר של שבת משו' דלא חיישי' שמא יטה דהא אסור להשתמש לאורן אכתי ניחוש שמא יטה בשביל לימוד שהוא מותר וא"ל דהא לימוד אסור לאור הנר אפי' בנר של שבת משום שמא יטה אא"כ יש אחר עמו שיזכירנו כדלעיל סי' ער"ה וע"כ גם כאן אין חשש כיון שיש אחר עמו   זה אינו דא"כ למה פסלו השמנים שאין נמשכים אחר הפתיל' בשבת היה להם להתיר באם יש אחר עמו אלא פשוט דלענין השמנים הפסולים ל"מ אחר עמו שא"א להזהר מלהטות א"כ ה"נ כאן בנ"ח יהא פסול משום שמא יטה להשתמש לתשמיש מצוה וע"כ נראה דגם בעל העיטור לא התיר בתשמיש מצוה אלא דרך עראי דבכה"ג לא מיגרע ההיכר של חנוכה שהכל יודעין שלא הודלק בשביל תשמיש עראי וגם חיישי' שמא יטה השמנים הפסולים בחנוכה דלא גזרי' בעראי ומ"ש ב"י בשם ש"ל דלקרות בספר מותר היינו גם כן דרך עראי בעלמא וא"כ קיצר כאן ש"ע במה שה"ל לבאר דיש מי שמתיר בתשמיש של מצוה היינו דרך עראי דוקא ולא ללמוד דרך קבע וכאן לא מהני אחר עמו דאפי' בחול אסור משום דלא יהיה היכר שנעשה לשם חנוכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there is one who permits use, etc.</b> This is the author of the Ittur, cited in the Tur. This is difficult for me: the reason it is forbidden to use its light is so that it should be recognizable that it is a mitzvah lamp, as Rashi explained in the Gemara. If so, why is it permitted to learn near it? One who sees will think that he lit it not for the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp but to learn. Furthermore, why did they permit on Chanukah the oils that are invalid for a Shabbos lamp, because we are not concerned that he might tilt, since it is forbidden to use their light? We should still be concerned that he might tilt for the sake of learning, which is permitted. One cannot answer that learning by lamplight is forbidden even by a Shabbos lamp because perhaps he will tilt, unless another person is with him to remind him, as above in siman 275, and therefore here too there is no concern since another person is with him. This is not so, for if so, why did they invalidate on Shabbos oils that are not drawn after the wick? They should have permitted them when another person is with him. Rather, it is clear that regarding invalid oils, another person with him does not help, because one cannot be careful not to tilt. If so, here too, with a Chanukah lamp, it should be invalid because perhaps he will tilt in order to use it for a mitzvah use. Therefore it appears that even the author of the Ittur permitted mitzvah use only in a casual manner, for in such a case the recognition of Chanukah is not diminished, since everyone knows that it was not lit for a casual use. Also, we are not concerned that he might tilt the invalid oils on Chanukah, because we do not decree regarding casual use. What the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, that it is permitted to read in a book, also means only in a merely casual manner. If so, the Shulchan Aruch was brief here where he should have explained that 'there is one who permits use for a mitzvah' means specifically casual use, but not fixed learning. And here another person being with him does not help, for even on a weekday it is forbidden, because there would be no recognition that it was made for the sake of Chanukah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For it is an item that is counted.</b> It is written there in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 103, that although when they are sold they are sold by weight, nevertheless a mitzvah item is a counted item. Since they became mixed after they were lit for the mitzvah, and now they are counted items, they remain in their status, even though regarding weekday use they would not be a counted item. He brings proof from what Tosafos wrote in chapter Gid HaNasheh, folio 100: If you will ask, why do we say in chapter HaArel that a piece of an impure sin-offering that became mixed among one hundred pure pieces becomes nullified? They answer that consecrated items are not called fit to be honored, since they are fit only for kohanim, and they are accustomed to eat from one another without holding a debt of gratitude. Thus, even though in general it would be fit to be honored, nevertheless we follow the present state even leniently; all the more so here stringently. End quote. I am very astonished at this great man, for he wrote imprecisely. On the contrary, from there is proof to the opposite, for Tosafos wrote there and in chapter HaArel that we follow what it will be after the nullification. Therefore, there in chapter HaArel, Yevamos folio 81, in the latter clause regarding a sin-offering that became mixed with non-sacred meat, it does not become nullified, because after it is nullified it is fit to be honored with it. But in the first clause, where it became mixed with pure pieces of sin-offering, even if it becomes nullified it is not fit to be honored, because honor for kohanim does not apply in the Mikdash; therefore it becomes nullified, for it is only 'anything whose way is to be counted,' etc. End quote. Meaning: we hold that only something whose way is to be counted everywhere is not nullified. Here regarding Chanukah, if we say that the Chanukah lamp becomes nullified in the majority of weekday lamps, it will not be a counted item, for for weekday use they are not used by count. This is similar to the first clause, that if an impure piece became mixed among pure pieces, it is indeed nullified, as written. However, it appears to me that for another reason it is not nullified. There in Terumas HaDeshen, the question was where the lamps were placed and burning, and one Chanukah lamp became mixed among two shamash lamps. Regarding this I say there is a distinction: if the Chanukah lamp was fixed in its place and two shamash lamps became mixed with it there, it is not nullified, since we hold that something fixed is like half and half. But if the Chanukah lamp was moved from its fixed place and became mixed elsewhere among the shamash lamps, then it is indeed nullified in the majority. I also saw in the rulings of the Maharshal that he wrote as follows: It appears that one need not add one lamp as a shamash, as is the custom of Ashkenaz, since he has a lamp on his table, and their enactment is their ruin; I discussed this at length. Therefore, if a Chanukah lamp became mixed with two shamash lamps, one is nullified in two. End quote. His reason is not known to us. In my opinion, he intended what I wrote, that there is ruin through the shamash lamp. It appears to me that his intent is that they will benefit from the light of the shamash and come to benefit from the other Chanukah lamps; or perhaps he meant that one benefits from the added light from the Chanukah lamps. I have already written that there is no prohibition on account of this.

<b>Rather, he should light from the mixture, etc.</b> It appears to me that the reason of the Rav in this is that he holds that the lamps are not stricter when they are placed in a mixture than when they are burning for their mitzvah. Since at the time of lighting there is no prohibition if there is another lamp near them, even though there is greater light from them, nevertheless it is permitted for the reason I mentioned immediately above. Therefore, now as well, certainly the only prohibition is to use each lamp by itself. About this he wrote nearby that even one in a thousand is not nullified, meaning to take one of them by itself. With other dry mixtures, one dry item is nullified in two and it is permitted to eat each one separately, but here, because it is a counted item, even in a thousand it is not nullified to light one lamp by itself. This appears to me to be the reason for the Rama's words. But I have no way to settle his words, for certainly he is discussing extinguished lamps, and in that case even the Terumas HaDeshen agrees that it is nullified in the majority. In Terumas HaDeshen he mentioned there that they are placed and burning, and then they have the status of a counted item according to his view; but if they are extinguished, the importance of being counted has departed from them. This law is explicit in what is written at the end of siman 677, that leftover oil that became mixed on the eighth day with other oil is nullified in sixty. Consequently, if it is a wax candle, its law is like dry in dry, and it is nullified in the majority, unless we say that he is discussing the day of Chanukah, when it became mixed and is then fit to be joined with others to light it on the second night; therefore it is a counted item. But I have already written that there is no counted item here, and there is no foundation here to forbid at all.

<b>And even if it went out on erev Shabbos, etc.</b> I have room to investigate these words, which are in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 103, and I will copy them. This is his language: It appears that he is not bound to it even in such a case. Although the main mitzvah is from nightfall, as the Mordechai wrote, and therefore we could have said that if it went out before the mitzvah began to be fulfilled he is bound to it, nevertheless, from the fact that we recite the blessing while it is still day, 'to light the Chanukah lamp,' even though the mitzvah is not yet present, since it is impossible in another way it is considered preparation for a mitzvah, as Tosafos explained at the beginning of Yevamos regarding preparation for the mitzvah of honoring father and mother, etc. Therefore one recites a blessing over it. Here too, if it went out, he is not bound to it, for the mitzvah has already begun through its preparation. And there is proof from the fact that they permitted on Chanukah that falls on Shabbos the oils that are forbidden for a Shabbos lamp, the reason being that it is forbidden to use its light, as above. Now, if you would say in our case that he is bound to it, why is it permitted? Let us be concerned that perhaps it will go out after accepting Shabbos, for that acceptance must be before bein hashmashos, and throughout the entire duration of bein hashmashos there is still no fulfillment of the mitzvah, as written above in the name of the Mordechai, and he will therefore be prevented from the mitzvah; there is reason to decree in this case, since he cannot light. End of his words. These words do not settle for me. According to what appears, certainly what they said, that if it went out he is not bound to it, means if it burned at least during the time of the obligation, namely from sunset. But if it burned a little before this and went out during that little time, he is certainly bound to it. Only on Shabbos, when it is impossible to wait until sunset to light and one must begin earlier, that time is called the time of beginning, just as on a weekday from the end of sunset; for so the Sages enacted, that he should light at the time when he is permitted to light. This is not called preparation for a mitzvah, but is an actual mitzvah, since that time is like after sunset. Therefore it is permitted to light with invalid oils on Shabbos, and we are not concerned that perhaps it will go out after accepting Shabbos, for then it is truly like Shabbos itself. But if he lights with them before accepting Shabbos, indeed he must light again, and even with all oils the law is so. This is unlike before accepting Shabbos, when he still has permission to light; who tells us that that time is like afterward? Certainly we do not derive what is possible from what is impossible. Although the Sages gave him permission to recite a blessing before the time of accepting Shabbos, nevertheless it is not then actual Shabbos. This matter is comparable to the case of Rav, who would pray the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos, and certainly it did not become actual Shabbos to obligate him if he performed labor at that time that he had advanced, as though he had performed it on Shabbos itself. Even though in prayer he had already made it Shabbos, it is really so. In the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 672 he brings in the name of Mahari Abuhav that from plag haminchah one may advance the lighting, comparable to Rav praying the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos. Furthermore, even regarding lighting the Shabbos lamp, it appears above in siman 263 that there is permission to perform labor until Barchu, even though he already recited the blessing to light the Shabbos lamp. So too here, regarding the Chanukah lamp, should we really say that he fulfilled the mitzvah by its burning during that time? For this reason, what the Terumas HaDeshen wrote from the case of 'slaughter for me, cook for me,' that it is considered fulfillment of a mitzvah, is also rejected. There too, since he wants to feed his father, he must first slaughter for him, and this is like the time after accepting Shabbos here. I then saw that the Maharshal also wrote in his brief laws that he is bound to light a second time on erev Shabbos, not like Mahari Abuhav. End quote. In any event, it appears to me that he should not recite a second blessing when he lights if it went out before accepting Shabbos, meaning so long as there is still permission because of the halachic time of accepting Shabbos; rather, he should go back and light without a blessing, for he already recited a blessing permissibly. This appears clear to me. Although the Rama wrote this regarding one who wants to be stringent, etc., it appears to me that according to the law one should rule this way.
