**** ENGLISH ****
(13) Water and wine, and to eat fruit - The reason is that eating fruit, even a large amount in a fixed manner, is considered only like a temporary eating of bread. The same applies to eating meat, fish, cheese, and other important foods: according to the Shulchan Aruch they are like fruit, and at the end he was stringent only regarding a cooked dish made from the five species of grain. What the Rema glossed, "even if he established himself over them," also refers to wine, as is evident in the Darchei Moshe; his reason is that wine is considered only like eating fruit, and certainly other beverages are so, and therefore their fixedness is not considered fixedness. Know that several later authorities question this law, and their view is that if one drinks wine in a fixed manner he is obligated in sukkah, especially members of a group who established themselves to drink wine, which is certainly complete fixedness. Nevertheless, regarding the blessing, several later authorities agreed that one should recite "to sit in the sukkah" only over bread made from the five species of grain, or over a cooked dish made from the five species if he establishes a meal on it, as below at the end of the seif. The law of pat haba'ah bekisnin will be explained there. Still, it is proper and correct at the outset not to drink in a fixed manner, even other beverages such as mead and beer, except during the meal, or at least first to eat more than an egg's bulk of bread [or another cooked dish made from the five species, if he establishes his meal on it], recite "to sit in the sukkah," and thereby satisfy all opinions.

(16) And it requires sukkah - Meaning by law, and therefore one must recite "to sit in the sukkah." The Ma'amar Mordechai wrote that one who ate pat haba'ah bekisnin in the morning with coffee or the like, as we are accustomed throughout the year, even though he does not recite hamotzi because he is not eating the measure on which people ordinarily establish a meal, nevertheless requires a sukkah, for he is establishing his meal on it, etc.; and so we have practiced, to recite over it "to sit in the sukkah"; see there. He mentioned a common case, but the same applies even without drinking coffee, since he established a meal on the pat haba'ah bekisnin. If he did not establish his meal on it, but merely ate more than an egg's bulk, there are different views among the later authorities whether he must recite "to sit in the sukkah." See Sha'arei Teshuvah, who wrote that regarding Shabbat and Yom Tov morning, when one recites kiddush and eats pat haba'ah bekisnin in the place of a meal [even if afterward he goes outside to receive his teacher's presence and the like, and several hours pass until mealtime], according to all opinions he may recite "to sit in the sukkah," since he eats it as the meal required for kiddush, and it is proper that he recite the sukkah blessing, for his intention makes it fixed. On a weekday it is not worthwhile to recite the blessing, for in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently; nevertheless, the common practice is to recite the blessing even on weekdays. To avoid concern for a blessing in vain, he should see to it not to leave immediately after eating, but to sit there for some time, and at the time of his blessing "to sit in the sukkah" he should intend for the eating and for the sitting afterward.

(35) But the night, etc. - Although he is distressed because of the rain, and anyone distressed is exempt from the sukkah, the Rema holds according to the view of the decisors who maintain that on the first night even one who is distressed is obligated. Their reason is that since we derive "the fifteenth" from "the fifteenth" of the festival of matzah, we derive it completely: just as there he is obligated in all circumstances, so too on Sukkot. Know that there are other great early authorities who hold that this verbal analogy does not come for this, and one should not distinguish between the first night and the other days. Therefore several later authorities concluded that although he is obligated to eat in the sukkah, because of doubt concerning the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" he should not recite it, for in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently. They further wrote that it is proper to wait until midnight lest the rain pass; if it passes, he should enter the sukkah, recite "to sit in the sukkah," eat, and thereby fulfill the positive commandment according to all opinions. Some later authorities wrote that it is not worthwhile to wait so long, for if he waits with the eating of the adult and young members of his household, there is no greater prevention of Yom Tov joy than this [Sha'arei Teshuvah]. Likewise in Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim they did not mention the measure of midnight, but only about an hour or two; and if the rain has not stopped, he should then recite kiddush in the sukkah as the Rema wrote.

(36) And he should recite kiddush in the sukkah so that he says Shehecheyanu, etc. - Meaning: seemingly he should have done the reverse, reciting kiddush in the house and also saying there the blessing of Shehecheyanu for this time, eating his whole meal there, and only at the end of the meal entering the sukkah and eating an olive's bulk there. However, in that case he would need to recite another Shehecheyanu over the sukkah, and it is not proper to multiply blessings. Therefore it is better to recite kiddush first in the sukkah, so that the blessing of Shehecheyanu refers also to the sukkah. Even if we say that he is then exempt from sukkah, nevertheless he fulfills it thereby, for this is no worse than if he recited Shehecheyanu during the weekday at the time of making the sukkah, where he fulfills it. He should eat an olive's bulk in the sukkah, and the rest of his meal eat in his house. If the rain stops, even if he already recited Birkat HaMazon over the earlier eating, he must eat again the measure of an egg's bulk and a bit more in the sukkah and recite "to sit in the sukkah," in order to satisfy the view of the decisors who hold that he did not fulfill his obligation with what he ate while it was raining. This is when he has not yet lain down; but if he already lay down, he need not rise from bed, for if one who is distressed is obligated, he already fulfilled his obligation with his eating, and if one who is distressed is exempt, he need not rise from bed, as explained in seif 7. What the Rema wrote, "the first night," means that on the second night he recites kiddush in his house, recites Shehecheyanu over the Yom Tov, eats his meal, and at the end of the meal eats an olive's bulk in the sukkah, but does not recite "to sit in the sukkah" and does not recite Shehecheyanu over the sukkah; he relies on the Shehecheyanu that he recited on the first night. At the outset, it is good to wait a little on the second night too until the rain stops. If he ate in his house and only an olive's bulk at the end of the meal in the sukkah as we wrote, and afterward the rain stopped, he must enter the sukkah, eat the measure, namely more than an egg's bulk, and recite "to sit in the sukkah" [later authorities]. See below in siman 640, seif 4, what we wrote there regarding this matter.

**** ENGLISH ****
(46) The custom is, etc. - Meaning: according to the law of the Gemara, in the view of the early decisors, when one recited the blessing in the sukkah and then left to attend to his affairs, without returning immediately, in a manner that constituted a complete departure, he has diverted his mind from the mitzvah; when he returns afterward he must recite the blessing again, even one hundred times in a day. Whenever he enters, even though he does not eat there, he recites the blessing, since sitting and standing there are also mitzvot, for it is in the manner that one lives. This applies specifically when he left completely, as we wrote; but if he left to relieve himself or to bring something to the sukkah, he need not recite the blessing, for he did not divert his mind. Nevertheless, the universal custom follows the view of the decisors who hold that one recites the blessing only at the time of eating. Even if they sit in the sukkah for an hour before eating, they do not recite the blessing, for they hold that the blessing recited afterward over the eating exempts everything, since eating is primary and exempts sleeping, strolling, and study, all of which are subordinate to it. The later authorities wrote that it is correct to satisfy also the view of the early decisors and not sit in this way without a blessing. Therefore, immediately upon coming from synagogue, he should recite a blessing over something from the five species, eat slightly more than an egg's bulk of it, recite "to sit in the sukkah," and then not recite the blessing later at the time of the meal.

(מח) אכילה - והמתענה בסוכות או שאין דעתו לאכול פת באותו יום אז לכו"ע כל אימת שיצא יציאה גמורה חייב לברך דדוקא כשאוכל פת ס"ל להנהו פוסקים שמברך על עיקר חיוב הסוכה ופוטר כל הדברים הטפלים אבל כשאינו אוכל לא שייך זה [ט"ז וש"א] וכתב הח"א דה"ה כשיצא יציאה גמורה לאחר אכילה וחוזר ונכנס ולא יאכל עד הערב וקודם האכילה יצטרך עוד הפעם לצאת לביהכ"נ דבזה ג"כ לכו"ע צריך לברך. ומי שהולך באמצע סעודתו לסוכת חבירו דעת המ"א שאפילו אם היה בדעתו בשעת ברכה שילך באמצע הסעודה לשם ולשוב אח"כ מיד אפ"ה צריך לברך שם לישב בסוכה דהליכה הוי הפסק וע"כ אם רוצה לאכול בסוכה זו דבר שאסור לאכול חוץ לסוכה צריך לברך שם לישב בסוכה אמנם הוא לשיטתו אזיל בסימן ח' אבל לפי מה שכתבנו שם דדעת כמה אחרונים דהליכה לא הוי הפסק אם היה בדעתו א"צ לברך וספק ברכות להקל [לבושי שרד] ואם שכח לברך לישב בסוכה עד שהתחיל לאכול יברך אח"כ על מה שרוצה לאכול ואפילו אכל כבר ובירך בהמ"ז יכול לברך שגם הישיבה היא מן המצוה דמאכילה ואילך כ"ז שיושב הוי הכל קביעות אחד ומ"מ כל שעדיין לא בירך בהמ"ז יברך ויאכל מעט. ומי שקידש בליל סוכה והתחיל לאכול ואח"כ ראה שהלאדי"ן לא היו נפתחין עדיין נ"ל שיאכל שוב כזית ויברך לישב בסוכה אבל קידוש פשיטא שא"צ לברך שנית ואפילו זמן לא יברך עוד הפעם (כיון דלדין התלמוד מהני הזמן שבשעת עשיית הסוכה למצות סוכה כ"ש שמהני בזה מה שבירך כשהיו הלאדי"ן סתומים) [בכורי יעקב ע"ש]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(48) Eating - One who fasts on Sukkot, or who does not intend to eat bread that day, according to all opinions must recite the blessing whenever he leaves in a complete departure, for only when he eats bread do those decisors hold that he blesses over the primary obligation of sukkah and exempts all subordinate matters; when he does not eat, that does not apply [Taz and other authorities]. The Chayei Adam wrote that the same applies when he left in a complete departure after eating and then returns and enters, but will not eat until evening, and before eating will need once again to leave for synagogue: in this case too, according to all opinions he must recite the blessing. One who goes in the middle of his meal to his fellow's sukkah: the Magen Avraham's view is that even if, at the time of the blessing, he intended to go there in the middle of the meal and then return immediately, he must nevertheless recite "to sit in the sukkah" there, because walking is an interruption. Therefore, if he wants to eat in that sukkah something that is forbidden to eat outside a sukkah, he must recite "to sit in the sukkah" there. However, this follows his own approach in siman 8; but according to what we wrote there, that the view of several later authorities is that walking is not an interruption if he had it in mind, he need not recite the blessing, and in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently [Levushei Serad]. If he forgot to recite "to sit in the sukkah" until he began eating, he recites it afterward over what he still wants to eat; and even if he already ate and recited Birkat HaMazon, he may recite the blessing, for the sitting too is part of the mitzvah, since from the eating onward, as long as he sits, it is all one fixed act. Nevertheless, as long as he has not yet recited Birkat HaMazon, he should recite the blessing and eat a little. One who recited kiddush on the night of Sukkot and began to eat, and then saw that the shutters had not yet been opened, it seems to me that he should again eat an olive's bulk and recite "to sit in the sukkah"; but it is obvious that he need not recite kiddush a second time, and he should not recite Shehecheyanu again either, since according to Talmudic law the Shehecheyanu recited at the time of making the sukkah is effective for the mitzvah of sukkah, and all the more so the one he recited when the shutters were closed is effective in this case [Bikkurei Yaakov; see there].
