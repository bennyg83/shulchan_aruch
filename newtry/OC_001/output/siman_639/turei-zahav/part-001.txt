<b>סוכה קטנה. </b> פירש"י של שיעור מצומצם ז' טפחים משמע דטעם האיסור משום דבזה ממעט' משיעור' וכעין ההיא דסכך פסול פחות מד' ואויר אצלו דסי' תרל"ב ס"ג דפוסל בסוכה קטנ' וק"ל מ"ש מהאי דסי' תר"ל סי"ג דאם סמך הסכך על העמודי' כו' דכשר כיון שיש י"ט מהארץ עד הסכך והמט' אינה ממעטת השיעור כיון שיש שיעור אם תנטל המט' כ"ש כאן אם תנטל המנורה ותו דא"כ מאי גריעותא דמנורה משאר כלי' שמות' להכניסם לסוכה ואפי' קטנה ונ"ל דמיירי במנורה שהוא תלוי' ונקשרת למעל' בסכך כמו בנר למאפ"ן שלנו וע"כ ממעט הנר כשיעור שלו מן הסכך ונשאר פחות מכשיעור לפ"ז אין איסור אלא בנקשר ושיעור מצומצם אבל התוספות כתבו בדף כ"ט הטעם דסוכה קטנה דחיישינן שמא תשרף סיכתו לפ"ז אין חילוק בנקשר למעלה וגם במצומצם ז"ט אלא שראוי לשייר שלא תשרף סוכתו כנלע"ד בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A small sukkah.</b> Rashi explained: one of the exact minimum measure, seven handbreadths. This implies that the reason for the prohibition is that by this it reduces it from its measure, similar to the case of invalid roofing less than four handbreadths with open air next to it in siman 632, seif 3, which disqualifies a small sukkah. This is difficult for me: how is this different from the case in siman 630, seif 13, that if one supported the roofing on posts, etc., it is valid, since there are ten handbreadths from the ground to the roofing, and the bed does not reduce the measure since there is the measure if the bed were removed? All the more so here, if the lamp were removed. Further, if so, what is worse about a lamp than other utensils that may be brought into a sukkah, even a small one? It seems to me that it speaks of a lamp that is hanging and tied above in the roofing, like our lamp called a lampa; therefore the lamp reduces from the roofing by its own measure, and less than the measure remains. According to this, the prohibition is only when it is tied and the measure is exact. But Tosafot wrote on page 29 that the reason for a small sukkah is that we are concerned lest his sukkah burn. According to that, there is no distinction whether it is tied above or whether it is exactly seven handbreadths, except that it is proper to leave enough so that his sukkah not burn, as appears to my humble opinion.

<b>ויין כו'. </b> ברא"ש וטור כ' אפי' את"ל דפרי בעי סוכה היינו דוקא מי שקובע עליהם אבל מי שאוכלן דרך עראי ודאי א"צ סוכה ושתיית יין עראי הוא עכ"ל ומשמע דאפי' קבע עצמו לשתות יין פטור ובהג"א פ"ב דסוכה כת' וז"ל ומיהו שתית יין ודבש ושכר בהדי פירות או בלא פירות אלא יין בלבד וכגון שלא אבל לחם ולפתן אין לי לברר אם מותר חוץ לסוכה אם לאו דתשבו כעין תדורו אמר רחמנא ואין שתיה בלא אכילה או שמא פעמים אוכלין אגוזים ושאר פירות וקובעין עצמן לשתיה ואסור עכ"ל. ובלבוש כ' על שתיית יין וה"מ ששותה לצמאו אבל אם קבע לשתות הרבה הוה קביעות  כדלעיל סי קי"ד ורי"ב לפכך נראה דגם לסוכה חשיב קבע וצריך סוכה וטוב להחמיר ולענין הברכה נראה שצריך לברך על השתיה של קבע שהרי מן הדין היה לו לברך בכל פעם שנכנס אלא שקבעוה על האכילה כמ"ש בסמוך א"כ למה לא יברך גם על השתי' של קבע כמו שמברכין על האכילה עכ"ל ול"נ דאין לברך לישב בסוכה כלל על השתיה אפי' בקביעות דאפי' את"ל דחייב בסוכה מ"מ אותה ברכה שמברך על האכילה פוטרת אותו מכל וכל וראיה ממ"ש התו' והרא"ש בפ"ק דברכות וז"ל כיוצא בזה שאל ר"י מר"ת אם צריך לברך על השינה בסוכה דיותר חמירא שינה מאכילה דמותר לאכול אכילת עראי חוץ לסוכה ושינת עראי אסור חוץ לסוכ' והי' ר"י ר"ל משום דשמא לא יוכל לישן וכשנרדם בשינה אינו יכול לברך אבל ר"ת השיב לו שכל מצות סוכה שחייב אדם מסעודה לסעודה כגון שינה וטיול ולימוד ברכת לישב בסוכה שבירך על הסעודה פוטרתו מלברך עליהם עכ"ל הרי לפניך שאפי' שונה שהיא חמורה מאכילה ברכת האכילה פוטרת אותו מן השינ' כ"ש שתיה שהיא אינה מבוררת א"צ ברכה עליה דברכת האכילה פוטרתו ואפי' אם קובע עצמו לשתות קודם האכילה כגון שתיית יי"ש במדינות אלו אפ"ה ברכה שיברך אח"כ בשעת האכילה כמ"ש ססי' זה פוטרתו ע"כ אין לברך כלל רק על האכילה כמ"ש בסוף הסימן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And wine, etc.</b> In the Rosh and Tur it is written: even if you say that fruit requires a sukkah, that is only one who establishes himself over them; but one who eats them casually certainly does not need a sukkah, and drinking wine is temporary. End quote. This implies that even if he established himself to drink wine, he is exempt. In Hagahot Ashiri, chapter 2 of Sukkah, it is written: However, drinking wine, honey-beverage, or beer together with fruit, or without fruit but only wine, and where he did not eat bread and relish, I cannot clarify whether it is permitted outside the sukkah or not, for the Merciful One said, "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live, and there is no drinking without eating; or perhaps sometimes people eat nuts and other fruit and establish themselves for drinking, and it is prohibited. End quote. The Levush wrote about drinking wine: this applies when he drinks to quench his thirst, but if he established himself to drink much, it is fixedness, as above in simans 174 and 212; therefore it seems that for sukkah too it is considered fixed and requires a sukkah. Regarding the blessing, it seems that one must bless over fixed drinking, since by law one should bless every time he enters, except that they fixed it over eating, as written nearby; if so, why should he not bless also over fixed drinking, just as one blesses over eating? End quote. This does not appear to me. One should not recite "to sit in the sukkah" at all over drinking, even fixed drinking, for even if you say that it requires a sukkah, nevertheless the blessing that he recites over eating exempts it entirely. The proof is from what Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Berachot: In a similar matter, R. Yitzchak asked Rabbeinu Tam whether one must bless over sleeping in a sukkah, for sleep is more stringent than eating, since it is permitted to eat a temporary eating outside the sukkah, but temporary sleep is forbidden outside the sukkah. R. Yitzchak would have wished to say that perhaps one cannot sleep, and when he falls asleep he cannot bless. But Rabbeinu Tam answered him that all mitzvot of sukkah that a person is obligated in from meal to meal, such as sleeping, strolling, and study, are exempted from blessing by the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" that he recited over the meal. End quote. You see that even sleep, which is more stringent than eating, is exempted by the eating blessing; all the more so drinking, whose obligation is not clear, does not need its own blessing, for the eating blessing exempts it. Even if he establishes himself to drink before eating, such as drinking spirits in these lands, nevertheless the blessing he will recite afterward at the time of eating, as written at the end of this siman, exempts it. Therefore one should bless only over eating, as written at the end of the siman.

<b>ולאכול פירות. </b> הרא"ש כ' שר"ת היה מחמיר אפי' בפירות שלא לאוכלן חוץ לסוכה כי היה סבור כההוא לישנא דפירות בעי סוכה כו ותמהתי שאין שם ב' לשונות ביומא דף ע"ט אלא ב' תירוצים יש על מילתא דרבא דאמר פירא לא בעי סוכה ומאן דלא משני התם כרבא לא פליג על רבא בזה ע"ש וצ"ע וכ' הטור בשם הר"פ דבשר וגבינה וכיוצא בהן בעי סוכה וטעמא מדאמרי' בגמרא שלר"א אם השלים סעודתו במיני תרגימא יצא והיינו בשר וגבינה ואח"כ כ' הטור במסקנ' ומיהו כיון שאין דרך לקבוע עליהם אפילו הקובע סעודתו עליהם לא חשיב קבע דלא עדיף מאכילת עראי דפת שאין צריך סוכה ובקצת ספרים ב' במקום ומיהו ומים וכ"ה נוסחת ב"י והוא ט"ס:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And to eat fruit.</b> The Rosh wrote that Rabbeinu Tam was stringent even with fruit, not to eat them outside the sukkah, because he held according to that version that fruit requires a sukkah, etc. I was astonished, for there are not two versions there in Yoma 79, but two answers to Rava's statement that fruit does not require a sukkah; and the one who does not answer there like Rava does not disagree with Rava about this. See there, and this requires examination. The Tur wrote in the name of R. Peretz that meat and cheese and the like require a sukkah, and the reason is from what the Gemara says, that according to R. Eliezer, if he completed his meal with kinds of targima, he fulfilled his obligation; this means meat and cheese. Afterward the Tur wrote in the conclusion: However, since it is not the way to establish oneself over them, even one who establishes his meal over them is not considered fixed, for it is no better than a temporary eating of bread, which does not require a sukkah. In some books the word "and water" appears instead of "however," and so is the text of the Beit Yosef, but it is a scribal error.

<b>ול"נ משום דמצות סוכה כו'. </b> על הטעם הזה ק' א"כ גם על האכילה לא יצא עד שתהיה לו סוכה מיוחדת כמ"ש המרדכי הביאו רמ"א בסי' תר"מ דאם עשה סוכה במקום המצטער בשינה אף ידי אכילה לא יצא ונלע"ד לתת טעם לקולת השינה דאיתא בגמר' דשלוחי מצוה פטורים דהיינו רבה בר רב הונא ור"ח שהלכו לקבל פני ר"ג היו ישנים חוץ לסוכה וה"נ למי שנשוי אשה שחייב לשמח את אשתו ברגל כדלעיל סימן תקנ"ט ונשים פטורות מן הסוכה א"כ אם אינו רוצה לפרוש מאשתו מקרי דבר מצוה וע"כ פטור מן הסוכה בלילה בשינה ואפי' אם אשתו איננה טהורה מ"מ שייך שמחה כשבעלה עמה בחדר כדאית' בגמ' שאסור לישן בחדר שאיש ואשתו לנין בו וא"ר יוסף אפילו באשתו נדה שנאמר נשי עמי תגרשון מבית תענוגיה כנ"ל טעם הקולא בזה למי שמיקל בו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it does not appear to me, because the mitzvah of sukkah, etc.</b> This reason is difficult: if so, even eating would not be fulfilled unless he had a private sukkah, as the Mordechai wrote and the Rema brought in siman 640, that if one made a sukkah in a place where he is distressed in sleeping, he has not fulfilled even eating. It appears to my humble opinion to give a reason for the leniency regarding sleep: it is stated in the Gemara that agents of a mitzvah are exempt, meaning Rabbah bar Rav Huna and R. Chisda, who went to receive Rabban Gamliel's presence, slept outside the sukkah. So too one who is married to a woman and is obligated to gladden his wife on the festival, as above in siman 559, and women are exempt from sukkah: if he does not want to separate from his wife, it is called a mitzvah matter, and therefore he is exempt from the sukkah at night with respect to sleep. Even if his wife is not ritually pure, nevertheless there is joy when her husband is with her in the room, as stated in the Gemara that it is forbidden to sleep in a room where a man and his wife are sleeping; and Rav Yosef said: even with his menstruant wife, as it says, "the women of My people you drive out from their houses of delight." This appears to me to be the reason for the leniency for one who is lenient in this.

<b>בליל י"ט הראשון חובה. </b> דאר"י משוה ר"ש בן יהוצדק נא' כאן ט"ו ונאמ' ט"ו בחג המצות מה להלן לילה הראשון חובה מכאן ואילך רשות אף כאן לילה הראשון חובה מכאן ואילך רשות ופי' הסמ"ג ורא"ש דאירדו גשמים קאי דאל"כ הא חובה בי"ט לאכול פת וממילא חייב בסוכה אף מכאן ואילך וסמ"ק וא"ז מוכיחים מזה דאין חובה בי"ט לאכול פת מדאמר מכאן ואילך רשות והם לא מפרשים דקאי אירדו גשמים ופסק בפסקי מהרא"י סי' ק"ס דבליל ב'  של סוכות דא"צ לאכול כזית בסוכה אם ירדו גשמים דסמכינן אסמ"ק וא"ז דלא כאשר"י דס"ל לדידן י"ט ב' כראשון אלא אנן בקיאין בקביעא דירחא כו' ומסיק שם תלמיד מהרא"י וז"ל אמנם שמעתי שדרש בב"ה דבלילה הראשונה ושניה צריך לאכול כזית בסוכה אם ירדו גשמים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On the first night of Yom Tov it is obligatory.</b> For R. Yochanan said in the name of R. Shimon ben Yehotzadak: "the fifteenth" is stated here, and "the fifteenth" is stated regarding the festival of matzah. Just as there the first night is obligatory and from then onward optional, so too here the first night is obligatory and from then onward optional. The Semag and Rosh explained that this refers to when rain fell, for otherwise one is obligated on Yom Tov to eat bread, and automatically obligated in sukkah even from then onward. The Semak and Or Zarua prove from this that there is no obligation on Yom Tov to eat bread, since it says that from then onward it is optional, and they do not explain that it refers to when rain fell. In Piskei Maharai, siman 160, he ruled that on the second night of Sukkot one need not eat an olive's bulk in the sukkah if rain fell, for we rely on the Semak and Or Zarua and not on the Ashiri, who holds that for us the second Yom Tov is like the first; rather, we are expert in the fixing of the month, etc. The student of Maharai concludes there: However, I heard that he expounded in the synagogue that on the first and second nights one must eat an olive's bulk in the sukkah if rain fell.

<b>Even if he ate an olive's bulk.</b> The Tur wrote: although the measure of eating without a sukkah is an egg's bulk, the first night is different, for it is more obligatory, and even if he wishes to eat only an olive's bulk, it is forbidden to eat it outside the sukkah; therefore he also fulfills the sukkah obligation with it. End quote. Thus, in the Tur's view, the first night is more stringent than all the days of Sukkot also in this respect, that even an olive's bulk, which is less than an egg's bulk, requires a sukkah. The Beit Yosef objected: My heart hesitates, for he says "even if he wishes"; is it dependent on his wishing? He should have said "he cannot" rather than "he does not wish." Further, how does he know this very point, that the first night differs from other nights? He should rather have stated the reason that since it is derived from the festival of matzah, its measure is an olive's bulk like Pesach. End quote. I did not understand the first objection at all, for he certainly said well "if he wishes," meaning that there is a stringency on the first night, that even an olive's bulk requires a sukkah. The second objection is not an objection, for our teacher the Tur learns it from what the Rosh brings from the Yerushalmi: The colleagues asked: perhaps just as the festival of matzah requires that one enter the mitzvah while hungry, so too sukkah? R. Zeira asked: perhaps just as there, only until he eats an olive's bulk of grain? He explains that R. Zeira was uncertain whether the obligation of sukkah on the first night extends to an olive's bulk and exempts only less than an olive's bulk, and is more stringent than other nights. We rule stringently that an olive's bulk requires a sukkah. From this our teacher learned that on the first night an olive's bulk requires a sukkah even if rain fell, and that is why an olive's bulk suffices.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The custom is that we do not recite the blessing, etc.</b> By the law, we rule like Rabbah bar bar Chanah in the name of R. Yochanan, that a sukkah all seven days requires a blessing. The Rif, the Rosh, and the Tur wrote that one should bless each time he enters it. So too Tosafot in chapter Lulav VeAravah wrote that whenever one enters it to eat, drink, and sleep, even ten times in a day, he blesses over each time, just as with tefillin whenever he puts them on. The reason with sukkah is that if a meal happens to come to him, he cannot eat it outside the sukkah, even though he already ate that day in the sukkah. End quote. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Maggid: I saw written that this applies specifically when he left in a complete departure to attend to his affairs and not to return immediately, but if he left only to speak with his fellow or to bring something to the sukkah for that moment, it is not considered a departure to obligate him in another blessing when he returns; and this is a correct matter. End quote. The Tur concluded: Rabbeinu Tam explained that since the main fixed act that a person does inside the sukkah is eating, he blesses over the eating and exempts all things, even sleeping, etc.; and our rabbis established the practice like Rabbeinu Tam. In Tosafot at the beginning of Berachot 11, Tosafot wrote: If you ask, how is sukkah different, where one must bless over each and every meal "to sit in the sukkah," whereas for the blessing over Torah one time suffices? One may answer that Torah is different, for he does not remove his mind from it, since every hour a person is obligated to learn, as it is written, "you shall meditate on it day and night," and it is like sitting all day without interruption; but eating in the sukkah has a fixed time. End quote. This implies that even if he intended today to eat twice in the sukkah, he must bless a second time, for otherwise what was Tosafot's question? With Torah he certainly intends to learn afterward. Rather, it is clear that with sukkah he must bless again in every case, and because of this they had to distinguish between them. It is difficult for me: how is this different from a perfume shop, where in siman 217 it is stated that one who enters and exits recites the fragrance blessing each time? The Tur wrote there in the name of the Ramah that this is specifically when he did not intend to return, but if he intended to return he does not bless, for he did not divert his mind. There we are not particular whether he leaves for an hour or not, but even for a long time, as long as he intends to return that same day; whereas here we require that he left only to speak with his fellow. And according to Rabbeinu Tam, who ruled that the matter depends on eating, even if he intended to return and eat afterward he must bless a second time. How is this different from the fragrance blessing? Why should we not say that just as the fragrance blessing helps for what he will smell all day, even if he left in between for many hours, so too it should help with sukkah? It appears that the answer is that specifically with sukkah there is a fixed time, namely at the time of eating, and afterward he has permission to leave. Regarding this we say that whenever he delayed for a long time, according to the essential law, between departure and entry, and according to Rabbeinu Tam even if he did not go out of the sukkah but immediately after reciting Birkat HaMazon finished the first meal, he must bless again over the second meal, even if he originally intended it. This is like a night intervening, where it is obvious that his intention today does not help for what he will eat tomorrow, for tomorrow is another matter; so too on the same day after completing the first eating. This is unlike the fragrance blessing, where there is no completion of an act and removal of the first act; if he did not leave the shop all day and all night until tomorrow, he would not need to bless again. Therefore his intent to return that day is effective, as appears to my humble opinion. <b>And it is written</b> in the Levush regarding those who establish themselves to drink after Birkat HaMazon, and after drinking for some time they return and eat a second meal without interruption, and recite "to sit in the sukkah" a second time: It seems to me that they are making a complete error, for they did not divert their minds from sitting in the sukkah, over which they already blessed at the first meal. What they practiced, to fix the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" over eating, is because they divert their minds from eating, leave the sukkah several times, and return to it; but here, where they sat inside it all day, why should they bless over it another time? End quote. I say that the practice is correct, and I am very astonished at the author of the Levush, who ruled from his own reasoning and did not seek to examine the source of the law at all. First, the Rosh wrote in chapter Lulav VeAravah: "to sit in the sukkah" means to remain in the sukkah, and for this reason he disagrees with the Rambam, who wrote that one should recite kiddush standing, etc. If so, there is no practical difference whether they remained there or left and returned, for the main obligation is because of the fixedness of eating. Since the first fixed eating ended, he must bless again over the second fixed eating, since if he wanted to go outside the sukkah for that eating it would be forbidden to him, and the obligation of sitting in the sukkah applies to him then as well; he must bless over the second obligation, and the first blessing already ended with Birkat HaMazon. How is this different from the blessing of hamotzi, where he must bless a second time and the first blessing before the first meal does not help? The Levush wrote to distinguish that there they diverted their minds from eating, but why should he bless "to sit in the sukkah," since he did not divert his mind from it? This is nothing. Even if you say that drinking requires a sukkah, nevertheless it is subordinate to eating, as the Rosh wrote in chapter Lulav VeAravah: Rabbeinu Tam says that since the primary fixed act that a person does in the sukkah is eating, the other strolling and sleeping that he does in the sukkah are subordinate to eating and it exempts them. End quote. Now it is a fortiori: strolling and sleeping are explicit in the Gemara as obligations of sukkah, and nevertheless we consider them subordinate to eating; all the more so drinking, which is not mentioned in the Gemara, and on the contrary, it is explicit in the decisors we mentioned above that it does not require a sukkah. Although we are stringent and require a sukkah for them, nevertheless it is obvious that they are subordinate to eating. By contrast, eating in a different meal is certainly and obviously not considered subordinate to the first. Furthermore, for drinking in between we do not require a sukkah blessing even if he went out to the market and returned to drink, because eating exempts it; but for eating, it is clear that if he goes out and returns and enters to eat, he requires a sukkah blessing. If so, how can you say that there is no obligation on the one who returns to eat because he already occupied himself with drinking in between? Drinking has no significance at all in relation to eating. One should not err from the language of Tosafot that I cited, where they wrote: But with sukkah, whenever he enters it in order to eat, drink, and sleep, even ten times, etc.; and mistakenly say that this is only when he enters from outside in order to eat, etc., but not when he was already there. That is complete error. Did Tosafot speak of one who sits and drinks in between, and who forced them into that case? Rather, plainly they stated the normal manner: he enters to eat, because a person does not sit in the sukkah from one eating to the next. On the contrary, they wrote afterward that if a meal happens to come to him, he cannot eat outside the sukkah, even though he already ate that day in the sukkah; and likewise tefillin all day, etc. End quote. This implies even if he did not leave in between, for they wrote without qualification. Further, what special force is there in drinking in between? If he sits idle in between, he is also engaged in sukkah, for we rule that strolling also requires a sukkah. If so, how could he bless afterward over the eating? Rather, eating is a separate matter, as appears to me clearly and simply, and many practice so, and the law is with them. I found that my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote so plainly. I also saw fit to mention a simple matter: everything that one drinks before eating, such as spirits in a fixed manner, does not require a sukkah blessing, for the eating over which he will bless afterward exempts even what preceded it. The proof is from what the Rosh wrote in chapter Lulav VeAravah: since the mitzvah of sukkah is eating and strolling, if he blesses after he has sat, it is proper and is called "before performing it." He brings proof from tzitzit, where we say that after one has wrapped himself he says "to wrap in tzitzit"; thus, although he has already wrapped himself, he blesses because the mitzvah continues as long as he is wrapped and standing. So too with sukkah, etc. End quote. Thus even the sitting, strolling, and sleeping before eating are exempted by the force of the eating afterward; all the more so drinking. All this appears simple in my humble opinion, and one who wishes to bless over drinking must be concerned for a blessing in vain, without doubt. <b>It seems to me</b> that one who intends not to eat bread all day, and certainly one who fasts a dream-fast on Sukkot, must bless over the sukkah each time he enters it that day to stroll, since on that day there is no eating of bread. We return to the Talmudic law, that one blesses each time he enters to sit there, for the main reason is that eating bread is primary and exempts what is subordinate.
