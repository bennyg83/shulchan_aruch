**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And all this applies specifically, etc.</b> From what is written: "if he wishes, he eats," but is it not obligatory to eat bread on Yom Tov, from what is written in Berachot 49b, "Sabbaths and festivals, where it does not suffice, etc."? Perforce it refers to bread, for fasting is forbidden even on Rosh Chodesh. R. Yitzchak answered that the practical difference is when rain fell, for on the first night, even if rain fell, there is an obligation. See the Rosh there, and so wrote the Semak. But in Tosafot it is written there: for example, if rain fell and he ate outside the sukkah, and then the rain stopped, he is obligated, as the Rashba wrote, for while rain is falling it does not have the status of a sukkah for him. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this because the Rashba, Semag, Or Zarua, and other decisors disagree with this and say that because of Yom Tov an olive's bulk suffices, and even with an egg's bulk of bread we do not require sukkah. But the view of Tosafot is primary.
