<b>מי שאינו כו'</b> ואם עבר בדיעבד כו' יש לסמוך כו' ר"ל כיון דדיעבד הוא ובמקום מצוה ודחק יש לסמוך על הפוסקים המתירים ולא מיקרי עבד אסורא. ולכן רשאי לברך על הסוכה. דלהאוסרים אין לברך על הסכה דאין זה מברך אלא מנאץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who is not, etc.</b> And if he transgressed after the fact, etc., there is basis to rely, etc. That is to say, since it is after the fact, and in a place of mitzvah and pressing need, one may rely on the decisors who permit it, and it is not called an act of prohibition. Therefore he may recite the blessing over the sukkah; for according to those who prohibit, one may not recite the blessing over the sukkah, for such a blessing is not blessing but blaspheming.

If so, even before eating it is forbidden. But after eating, although the Birkei Yosef cited in the name of the Ra'avyah that bowls are permitted, nevertheless we do not have license to be lenient against the Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch, who ruled not like him. In any case, whether before eating or after eating, whether a pot or a bowl, we have only the ruling of the Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch, and by its light we shall walk.

<b>וא"כ איך אפשר דכותבת אחד עם הגרעין יהיה יותר מביצה.</b> ומשני רבא הא דאמר שני כותבות מצד הדין פטורים מן הסכה. אין הטעם משום דחסר מן השיעור. אלא משום דהוי פירי ופירי לא בעי סכה. ופריך מדתניא אמר רבי כשהיינו לומדים תורה אצל ר"א ב"ש הביאו לפנינו תאנים וענבים ואכלנום אכילת עראי חוץ לסכה. ודייק הש"ס אכילת עראי אין אכילת קבע לא אלמא פרי בעי סכה ומשני ע"ש. ואחר זה מסיק לימא מסייע ליה דפירי לא בעי סכה. דתנן ר"א אומר י"ד סעודות חייב לאכול בסכה ומי שלא אכל בי"ט ראשון ישלים בי"ט אחרון דהיינו בשמיני. ותני בברייתא אם השלים במיני תרגימ' יצא משום דבעינן שישלים בדבר שצריך לאכול בסכה ס"ל דמיני תרגימ' בעי סכה. ואי ס"ד דפירי בעי סכה הל"ל רבות' דמשלים אפי' בפירי. ודחי לה ע' שם. ומעתה אבא לבאר דברי מ"א מ"ש מ"א ולמד כן מדאמרי' אכילת עראי אין כו' ר"ל מדקתני ואכלנום אכילת עראי משמע שאכלום דרך עראי ולא דרך קבע. דאי ס"ד דהפי' שאכלו שיעור אכילת עראי דהיינו פחות כביצה ה"ל למימר אכלנו מהם אכילת עראי. ועל זה דייק הש"ס אכילת עראי אין אכילת קבע לא. ע"כ הכוונה דוקא דרך עראי אין אבל דרך קבע לא. הרי דאפי' א"א פירי בעי סכה. אינו חייב בסכה אלא אם כן יאכלם דרך קבע. דהא השתא לפי האי דיוקא בעי למידק דפירי בעי סכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If so, how is it possible that one date with its pit is more than an egg's bulk?</b> Rava answers: that which it says that two dates are exempt from sukkah by law is not because the measure is lacking, but because they are fruit, and fruit does not require sukkah. It objects from a baraita: Rabbi said, when we studied Torah before R. Elazar ben Shammua, they brought before us figs and grapes, and we ate them as temporary eating outside the sukkah. The Talmud infers: temporary eating, yes; fixed eating, no; apparently fruit requires sukkah. It answers; see there. After this it concludes: let us say this supports the view that fruit does not require sukkah, for we learned that R. Eliezer says one must eat fourteen meals in the sukkah, and one who did not eat on the first Yom Tov completes it on the last Yom Tov, meaning on the eighth day. It was taught in a baraita: if he completed with kinds of targima, he fulfilled his obligation, because we require that he complete with something that requires eating in the sukkah; he holds that kinds of targima require sukkah. But if it enters your mind that fruit requires sukkah, it should have taught a greater novelty, that he completes even with fruit. It rejects this; see there. Now I come to explain the words of the Magen Avraham: what the Magen Avraham wrote, "and he learned so from what we say: temporary eating, yes, etc.," means from the fact that it taught, "and we ate them as temporary eating," which implies that they ate them in a temporary manner and not in a fixed manner. For if one would think that the meaning is that they ate a measure of temporary eating, namely less than an egg's bulk, it should have said, "we ate from them a temporary eating." About this the Talmud inferred: temporary eating, yes; fixed eating, no. Therefore the intent is specifically that in a temporary manner, yes, but in a fixed manner, no. Thus even if you say fruit requires sukkah, he is obligated in sukkah only if he eats them in a fixed manner, for now, according to this inference, it wants to infer that fruit requires sukkah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Similar to tasting a cooked dish.</b> He comes to explain how one can say that the reason it was not by law is because he ate them in a temporary manner, when nothing of this was mentioned in the Mishnah. The Mishnah wanted to bring proof that if one wished to be stringent even with a temporary eating, he may do so, and it brought the incidents of R. Yochanan ben Zakkai and Rabban Gamliel. If so, the essential point is missing, namely that Rabban Gamliel ate them in a temporary manner. It is understandable if the initial assumption of the questioner is correct, that two dates without pits are less than more than an egg's bulk and fall short of the measure, for that was well mentioned, since it taught "two dates." Likewise according to Rava's answer, that fruit does not require sukkah, this too is clear in the Mishnah. But if the reason is that he ate them in a temporary manner, the Mishnah should have explained so, for that is the main proof. The Magen Avraham wrote the reason for this: it is similar to R. Yochanan ben Zakkai tasting a cooked dish, which is in a temporary manner. If so, the Mishnah need not explain that he ate them in a temporary manner, since these two incidents of R. Yochanan ben Zakkai and Rabban Gamliel are taught together without qualification and are similar to each other, and in both they were eating in a temporary manner.

<b>ואפי' אכל הרב' וקבע עלייהו וכ"מ בגמרא.</b> אין כוונת מ"א מדקתני ואכלנום אכילת עראי כו' אלמא למ"ד פירי בעי סכה צריך לקבוע עליהן וממילא למ"ד פירי לא בעי סכה אפי' אכלן דרך קבע לא בעי סוכה. ז"א דהא מ"א חולק על הרא"ש וס"ל למ"ד פירי בעי סכה היינו אפי' אכלן דרך עראי. וא"כ ע"כ מ"א לא דייק לישנ' דואכלנום אלא ר"ל מדמסייע ליה מדתני' אם השלים במיני תרגימ' יצא ולא קתני אם השלים בפירי ע"כ פירי לא בעי סכה כנ"ל. ותיקשי ולטעמיך אפ"ת פירי לא בעי סכה. אי ס"ד דבאכלן דרך קבע מודה דחייב בסכה אכתי תיקשי ליתני ישלים בפירי ויאכלם דרך קבע. וגם סתמ' דמלתא כן דהא הוא השלמת סעודה אע"כ למ"ד פירי לא בעי סכה אפי' אכל הרבה ודרך קבע מ"מ לא בעי סכה. היינו כשאכל כביצה (לאו דוקא אלא ר"ל יותר מכביצ' מעט) ואפי' אכלו דרך עראי דלא גרע מפירי למ"ד פרי לא בעי סכה וכמ"ש מ"א דלא כהרא"ש. ודע דמיני תרגימ' היינו תבשיל מחמשת מיני דגן. וכמ"ש רמ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And even if he ate much and established himself over them, and so it appears in the Gemara.</b> The Magen Avraham's intent is not from the fact that it taught, "and we ate them as temporary eating," etc., that according to the one who says fruit requires sukkah, one must establish himself over them, and automatically according to the one who says fruit does not require sukkah, even if he ate them in a fixed manner he does not require sukkah. That cannot be, for the Magen Avraham disagrees with the Rosh and holds that according to the one who says fruit requires sukkah, this means even if he ate them in a temporary manner. If so, perforce the Magen Avraham did not make an inference from the wording "and we ate them," but rather means from the support brought from the teaching: if he completed with kinds of targima, he fulfilled his obligation, and it did not teach: if he completed with fruit. Therefore fruit does not require sukkah, as above. And it is difficult: according to your reasoning, even if you say fruit does not require sukkah, if you think that when he ate them in a fixed manner he admits that he is obligated in sukkah, it should still be difficult: let it teach that he completes with fruit, eating them in a fixed manner. The plain assumption is also so, for it is the completion of a meal. Rather, perforce, according to the one who says fruit does not require sukkah, even if he ate much and in a fixed manner, nevertheless it does not require sukkah. This is when he ate an egg's bulk, not precisely, but meaning slightly more than an egg's bulk, and even if he ate it casually, it is no worse than fruit according to the one who says fruit does not require sukkah, as the Magen Avraham wrote, unlike the Rosh. Know that "kinds of targima" means a cooked dish from the five species of grain, as the Rema wrote.
