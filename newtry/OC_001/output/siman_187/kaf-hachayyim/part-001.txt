**** ENGLISH ****
(1) <b>[Se'if 1] Some say, 'Blessed is He Who satisfies,' etc.</b> Berachos 48b teaches that Birkas HaMazon consists in order of HaZan, Birkas HaAretz, Boneh Yerushalayim, and HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Moshe instituted HaZan when the manna descended; Yehoshua instituted Birkas HaAretz upon entry into the land; David and Shlomo instituted Boneh Yerushalayim; David for Israel and Jerusalem, Shlomo for the great and holy Temple. HaTov VeHaMeitiv was instituted at Yavneh for the slain of Beitar: 'HaTov,' that they did not decay, and 'VeHaMeitiv,' that they were given burial. Rashba asks how these figures could institute blessings that the Gemara soon calls Torah law. He answers that they fixed the blessing texts. Torah law allowed any wording; Moshe, Yehoshua, and David established a form appropriate to each era. Before conquest and construction they necessarily spoke differently than afterward, just as we now pray to restore the kingdom and rebuild the Temple, whereas they prayed to preserve them and continue the land's tranquility. Rosh and Tur, siman 188, agree. Ramban writes that Torah law gives no fixed Birkas HaMazon text but commands each person to bless after eating according to his ability, like Binyamin the shepherd's 'Blessed is the Merciful One, Master of this bread.' The prophets instituted polished language, changing with the eras; Shlomo and his court instituted the subject, while each era uses fitting wording. Beis Yosef and Prishah cite him. Rashba, Rosh, Tur, Prishah, Bach, Tosafos, Levush, and Magen Avraham understand that the number of three blessings is Torah law, supported by the verses in the Gemara. Maran in Beis Yosef, siman 191, however, says Torah law does not fix their number, as Rambam in Sefer HaMitzvos lists only the command to bless after eating; the Gemara's verses are supports. Kesef Mishneh understands Rambam and Ramban likewise, and Midrash Rabbah, Mas'ei, implies the same. The practical difference: if three blessings are Torah-required, one who said Al HaMichyah instead of Birkas HaMazon, or omitted one blessing, did not fulfill the obligation; the two-blessing allowance for laborers in siman 191 is exceptional. Eliyah Rabbah 268:18 and Chayei Adam likewise rule that Al HaMichyah does not suffice. If their number is rabbinic, omission or Al HaMichyah does suffice. Taz, siman 168:6, appears to say so; Meshivas Nefesh 4 says three texts are rabbinic and Torah law is fulfilled with Al HaMichyah, making doubt lenient; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 191, agrees. Because of the dispute, one who mistakenly said Al HaMichyah after eating to satiation should, if possible, eat more bread, bless Hamotzi, and recite Birkas HaMazon intending also for the first eating; alternatively, hear Birkas HaMazon from another and intend to fulfill his obligation. If impossible, he should contemplate the blessing in his heart, as above in siman 184, paragraph 22. If he had not eaten to satiation and the obligation was rabbinic, one may be lenient after the fact and consider him to have fulfilled it. See below, paragraph 18.

<b>ג) וזה  </b>נוסח התחלת הברכה בסידור הכוו' של הרש"ש ז"ל בא"י אמ"ה האל הזן אותנו וכו' בטובו גי' הוי"ה בחסד גי' י יה יהו יהוה. נותן גי' אבגית"ץ. כי הוא אל זן. יכוין כי הוא אל זה הוא אל שדי שבבריאה והוא ישפיע אל זן שהוא גי' אל הוי"ה שביצירה פ'תח א'ת י'דיך יכוין ר"ת פא"י גי' יאהדונה"י וס"ת חת"ך ובא"ת ב"ש סא"ל בגי' יאהדונה"י ומשביע גי' חת"ך. לכל חי רצון יכוין להמשיך שפע וברכה רבה לכל העולמות. ע"כ. ועי"ש כוונת פסוק זה בארוכה ובשה"מ פ' עקב יעו"ש. ונראה שבשעה שיכיון להוריד שפע וכו' לכל העולמות יכוין להמשיך ג"כ לבחי' הנר"ן שלו ולגופו. וסדר כוונת ד' ברכות של בהמ"ז של חול ושבת כבר כתבנו אותם בסימן זק"ן אות כ"ד ואות כ"ה. קחנו משם. ועיין לקמן אות ט':
**** ENGLISH ****
(3) This is the opening text in the Rashash's Siddur HaKavanos: 'Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe, the God Who nourishes us ... in His goodness.' The Divine Name has the numerical value associated with chesed; 'nosen' has the value of the Name Avag Yatatz. At 'for He is God Who nourishes,' intend that the Divine Name Shakkai of Beriah influences 'zan,' whose value is Kel Havayah of Yetzirah. At 'Pose'ach es yadecha,' intend the initials, whose value is the combined Name Y-ah-Adonai, and the final letters chasakh; through Atbash, sa'al, likewise has that value. 'Masbia' has the value chasakh. At 'every living thing with favor,' intend to draw abundant flow and blessing to all worlds. See there at length and Shaar HaMitzvos, Eikev. When intending to bring this flow to all worlds, one should apparently include his own nefesh, ruach, neshamah, and body. The intentions for the four weekday and Shabbos blessings were recorded in siman 157, paragraphs 24–25. See below, paragraph 9.

(4) <b>First blessing.</b> Some begin, 'Blessed is He and blessed is His Name'; one should not begin so even with ten present; see below, siman 192, paragraph 7. Others begin, 'Nourish us ... Who bestows His goodness abundantly upon us.' This addition causes a defect, for the letter peh is absent from Birkas HaMazon to teach that af and ketzef (anger and wrath) do not rule over one who recites it with concentration [Yad Aharon; Chesed LaAlafim]. (4) <b>'He has not deprived us, and may He not deprive us.'</b> In chaser, the ches has a kamatz and samekh a patach. In yechsar, the yud has a sheva, ches a patach, and samekh a tzerei. The numerical value of the vowel points then equals lechem: every dot is ten, every line six, and a kamatz is sixteen [Shelah, Shaar HaOsiyos 83a; Magen Avraham; Eliyah Rabbah].

<b>ד) ברכה ג'.  </b>עלינו ועל כל ישראל עמך. רענו זוננו מ"א סי' פק"ח ססק"ג. ואל תצריכנו לידי מתנת ב"ו. ולא לידי הלואתם אלא לידך וכו'. יהי רצון שלא נבוש בעוה"ז וכו' בנוסח רצה. נשבות בו וננוח בו. ונתענג בו וכו'. (ובלילה נשבות בה וננוח בה וכו'. כמ"ש בשער הכוו') אחר רצה או אחר יעלה ויבא אומר ותבנה ירושלים עה"ק במהרה בימינו בא"י בונה ירושלים חס"ל שם. בן א"ח שם אות ט"ז. ועיין לקמן סי' פק"ח סעי' ד'. גם בשבת אומר רענו זוננו כמו בחול. ואין לחוש שהוא שאלת צרכיו דאסור בשבת דטופס ברכות הוא ושרי. ירושלמי והביאו הטור סי' קפ"ח. מ"א שם סק"ג. בי"מ אות ה':
**** ENGLISH ****
(4) <b>Third blessing.</b> Say, 'upon us and upon all Your people Israel'; 'tend us, nourish us'; 'do not make us dependent upon gifts of flesh and blood, nor upon their loans, but only upon Your hand'; and 'May it be Your will that we not be ashamed in this world.' In Retzei say, 'that we may cease work on it, rest on it, and delight in it'; at night, use the feminine forms, as Shaar HaKavanos states. After Retzei or Yaaleh VeYavo say, 'and rebuild Jerusalem, the holy city, speedily in our days. Blessed are You, Hashem, Who rebuilds Jerusalem' [Chesed LaAlafim; Ben Ish Chai 16]. See below, siman 188:4. Even on Shabbos say 'tend us, nourish us' as on weekdays. This is not forbidden personal petition, since it is the fixed blessing text [Yerushalmi; Tur and Magen Avraham, siman 188; Beis Menuchah].

<b>ד) ברכה ד'.  </b>בא"י אמ"ה אבינו מלכנו וכו'. ועיין לקמן סי' קפ"ט אות ד' ואות ה'. ואחר סיום הברכה ורוח והצלה וכל טוב השומע יענה אמן חס"ל סי' רט"ו אות ג'. והיינו למנהג הספרדים אבל מנהג אשכנזים מסיימת ומכל טוב תמיד אל יחסרנו והשומע שהמברך מסיים כך צריך לענות אמן" כמ"ש ח"א כלל מ"ז אות א' יעו"ש. ועיין לקמן סי' קפ"ט אות א'. הרחמן הוא יפרנסנו וכו'. בריוח ולא בצמצום. (ולא יאמר בהתר ולא באיסור) הרחמן הוא יצליח את דרכינו. הרחמן הוא יצליחנו בלימודנו. הרחמן הוא ירפאנו רפואה שלימה רפואת הנפש ורפואת הגוף. אם יש עכו"ם בבית יאמר הרחמן הוא יברך כל אחד ואחד ממנו בני ברית. (4) <b>Fourth blessing.</b> Begin, 'Blessed are You, Hashem ... our Father, our King.' See below, siman 189, paragraphs 4–5. Under the Sephardi text, the listener answers amen after 'relief, deliverance, and every good.' Under the Ashkenazi text, which ends 'and may He never deprive us of any good,' the listener answers amen there [Chayei Adam 47:1]. See below, siman 189, paragraph 1. Among the Harachaman passages say: 'May He sustain us ... with abundance and not restriction'; but not 'permissibly and not prohibitively'; 'May He grant success to our paths'; 'May He grant us success in our learning'; and 'May He heal us completely, in soul and body.' If a gentile is in the house, say, 'May He bless each one of us, members of the covenant.' Continue, 'as our holy forefathers were blessed: Avraham with everything, Yitzchak from everything, and Yaakov everything'; 'May He implant His Torah and love,' and 'may all our deeds be for the sake of Heaven' [Chesed LaAlafim; Ben Ish Chai 18]. Harachaman is said on Shabbos as on weekdays, since it is the fixed text [Beis Yosef and Darkei Moshe, siman 188; Shibolei HaLeket; Or Zarua; Shulchan Aruch HaRav]. A guest likewise blesses the host on Shabbos [Birkei Yosef]. Amen is answered after each Harachaman even without God's Name, and after every supplication and request even if not a formal blessing [Shelah; Magen Avraham, siman 189; Chesed LaAlafim; Ben Ish Chai].
