And if Pesach has passed over them, they are forbidden in benefit; see Chok Yaakov.

<b>עכבר. </b> דמעט רוק שבפי עכבר אין מחמיץ חטה קשה ולכן אפילו אין ס' נגדו שרי בדיעבד <small>(ולא הוי כמבטל איסור לכתחלה דאין כוונתו לבטלו אלא לטחנן)</small> רשב"א ורמב"ן והקשה פר"ח הלא מבטלו ק"פ וכולה היתר הוא. ומסיק הואיל ומערבו על מנת לאכול בפסח הוי כמבטל איסור ע"ש וח"י כתב ע"ש רמב"ן דנשיכת עכבר הוי כמי פירות ואפשר דדוקא חטה אבל שעורה ממהר להחמיץ מ"א וע"ל סי' תס"ו סעיף ד':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A mouse.</b> For the little saliva in a mouse's mouth does not leaven hard wheat; therefore even if there is not sixty against it, it is permitted after the fact <small>(and this is not like nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, for his intent is not to nullify it, but only to mill it)</small> (Rashba and Ramban). And the Peri Chadash challenged: is he not nullifying it before Pesach, and it is entirely permitted? And he concludes that since he mixes it in order to eat it on Pesach, it is like nullifying a prohibition; see there. And Chok Yaakov wrote in the name of the Ramban that a mouse-bite is like fruit juice; and it is possible that this is specifically wheat, but barley quickly becomes chametz (Magen Avraham); and see above, siman 466 se'if 4.

**** ENGLISH ****

Nevertheless it is certainly permitted to cook from those matzos during Pesach; see Magen Avraham in siman 458. And if the flour became moist and turned into crumbs, nullification does not help even for baking before Pesach, as is explained below in siman 467 (Chok Yaakov).

<b>השוק. </b> דלא מחזקי' איסורא שמא נגע במים ונראה דאפי' במקום שנוהגי' ללתות החטים אימר לא לתת בדרך שנתחמץ ט"ז. אכן בתצ"צ סי' ל"ב החמיר בענין זה וכתב כשידוע בבירור בודאי שמקצתן מכבסין תמיד החטין שהוא גרע מלתית' אף שרוב המוכרים אין מכבסין אסור משום קבוע כמחצה עמח"צ דמי ע"ש <small>(ואם המוכר מביא לבית ישראל מותר דכל דפריש כו'. ועיין פר"ח)</small> וכ"כ המ"א בשם גדול א' דבמדינתנו שרגילין לרחוץ החטין ושוהין אותן במי' אסור ליקח קמח מן השוק דהוי ממש כדגן שנטבע כמ"ש סי' תס"ז ס"ב וכ"פ כל חכמי פראג ע"ש ובח"י כתב נראה לחלק דדוקא מן אותן הטוחנין שבעיר או המוכרי קמח בקביעות אין ליקח וכ"ש מן הנחתומים שאין ליקח מהם אבל מותר ליקנות מן בני הכפרים המביאים קמח בכל יום מן הכפרים ע"ש הטעם ושמעתי שגם בימי מוח"ז הגאון מהר"ש ז"ל היה פ"א דחוק בקמחא דפסחא והתיר לקנות מן השוק ואפשר דג"כ בדרך שכתבתי ואין להחמיר בפרט במצה שאינ' של מצוה כמ"ש ס"ק ח'. וכתב הט"ז דבמקום שטוחנין החיטים ברחיים שטוחנין המלצי"ן אפי' בשעת הדחק אסור כי חמץ גמור הוא והח"י כ' עליו לעד"נ דבשעת הדחק אף כה"ג יש להתיר דהא אף אם נשאר איזה דבר מן המלצי"ן ונתערב. ודאי דנתערב ונתבטל ק"פ. מ"מ הנכון הוא בשעת הדחק לאפות הכל ק"פ. והח"י מביא ראיה כשנפל פרורי פת בקמח דמהני ליה הרקד' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The market.</b> For we do not presume a prohibition, lest it touched water; and it appears that even in a place where they practice soaking the wheat, we say perhaps they did not soak it in a way that it became chametz (Taz). However, in Tzemach Tzedek siman 32 he was stringent in this matter, and wrote that when it is known clearly that some of them always wash the wheat, which is worse than soaking, even though most sellers do not wash, it is forbidden because of kevu'a, which is like half and half; see Tzemach Tzedek there. <small>(And if the seller brings it to a Jew's house, it is permitted, for whatever separates [from a mixture is presumed from the majority]; and see Peri Chadash.)</small> And so wrote the Magen Avraham in the name of a certain great authority, that in our country, where they are accustomed to wash the wheat and leave it soaking in water, it is forbidden to take flour from the market, for it is literally like grain that was submerged, as is written in siman 467 se'if 2; and so ruled all the sages of Prague; see there. And Chok Yaakov wrote that it appears one should distinguish: specifically from those millers in the city, or those who sell flour regularly, one should not buy, and all the more so one should not buy from the bakers; but it is permitted to buy from villagers who bring flour every day from the villages; see there the reason. And I heard that also in the days of my maternal grandfather, the Gaon Maharash of blessed memory, there was once a pressing need for Pesach flour, and he permitted buying from the market; and it is possible that it was also in the manner I wrote; and one should not be stringent, especially regarding matzah that is not of the mitzvah, as I wrote in s.k. 8. And the Taz wrote that in a place where they grind the wheat in mills that grind maltzin (malt), even under pressing circumstances it is forbidden, for it is absolute chametz. And Chok Yaakov wrote upon him: in my humble opinion, under pressing circumstances even in such a case there is room to permit, for even if some remnant of the maltzin remained and became mixed, it certainly became mixed and was nullified before Pesach. Nevertheless the correct practice under pressing circumstances is to bake everything before Pesach. And Chok Yaakov brings a proof from when crumbs of bread fell into flour, that sifting helps for it; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To soak.</b> Meaning letiah: the Rambam wrote that they soak the wheat in water. And the Aruch explained [it as] placing them for soaking; and because we are not expert in soaking, therefore the Geonim forbade it. Nevertheless it appears that it is permitted to keep such wheat in their houses, since by the law of the Gemara the wheat is permitted with soaking so long as it is not close to splitting, as is written in siman 467 se'if 9; and that is specifically with a soaking or a slight washing, but with a heavy soaking it is absolute chametz, like grain that was submerged in a river (Chok Yaakov). And the Taz wrote that it is forbidden to buy the wheat from the miller, for they are accustomed to rinse the wheat first.
