<b>והמנהג ליקח לכתחלה חטים. </b> לכאורה היה נראה דאם אין לו חטים יקח כפי הסדר שהם שנוים במשנה וכה"ג כתב הטור לקמן סי' תע"ג לגבי מרור אבל באמת אין ראיה מזו כלל דהתנא נקיט כסדר המקרא והמוקדם בפסוק וכן יש קצת הוכחה לזה מלשון מהרי"ל שכת' וז"ל מהרי"ל דרש עיקר המצוה ליטול חטין דלא משתמיט שום תנא בלישנא כ"א חטין לפסח עכ"ל ולא קאמר מדהתנא נקט חטין ברישא אלא ודאי דהתנא נקט רק הסדר המינים דכתיבי בקרא וע"ל בטור סי' ר"ח דכוסמין בכלל מין חטין הוא ובסי' רי"א דכל הקודם בפסוק מוקדם לברכה מ"מ נ"ל דכאן אליבא דכ"ע מין החביב עליו קודם כדי לקיים יותר מצות אכילת מצה לתיאבון ואפשר דמהאי טעמא נקט ג"כ התנא בכל מקום חטין לפסח לפי שמסתמא הוא החביב לרוב בני אדם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the practice is initially to take wheat.</b> At first glance it would seem that if one has no wheat he should take them according to the order in which they are taught in the Mishnah, and similarly the Tur wrote below in siman 473 regarding maror. But in truth there is no proof from there at all, for the tanna lists them according to the order of the verse and what comes earlier in the verse. There is also some proof to this from the language of the Maharil, who wrote: The Maharil expounded that the essence of the mitzvah is to take wheat, for no tanna ever uses any wording other than wheat for Pesach. He did not say that it is because the tanna listed wheat first. Rather, it is certainly that the tanna merely listed the species in the order in which they are written in the verse. See above in the Tur, siman 208, that spelt is included in the species of wheat, and in siman 211, that whatever comes earlier in the verse takes precedence for a blessing. Nevertheless, it appears to me that here, according to all opinions, the species that is most beloved to him takes precedence, so that he may better fulfill the mitzvah of eating matzah with appetite. Perhaps for this reason as well the tanna everywhere says wheat for Pesach, because presumably it is beloved to most people.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They do not come to leavening.</b> The Rambam wrote in chapter 5 of Hilchos Chametz, halacha 1: There is no prohibition of chametz on Pesach even if one kneaded rice flour and it swelled like dough that became chametz; it is permitted to eat. In the Venice edition of the Rambam, the gloss of the Raavad is recorded there: There is no prohibition. The Raavad says: This is not a simple matter, and not everyone agrees with him. Granted, it does not become complete chametz and one is not liable to kareis for its leavening, but it is nevertheless chametz nuksheh and prohibited. This requires study, for the words of the Raavad are explicitly against the conclusion of the Gemara and all the poskim. See Pesachim 114. From the Maggid Mishneh there it appears clear that he equated the Raavad's view with what the Rambam wrote there in halacha 2 regarding fruit juice. But in truth, according to how the Raavad's glosses are recorded there, and according to the implication of the wording, it seems that he is referring to types of kitniyos, and this requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there are those who prohibit.</b> The reason is explained in the name of the poskim: they prohibited it because grain is made as cooked dishes and kitniyos are made as cooked dishes, so there is a decree lest one confuse one with the other. Also, sometimes they make bread from them, and one may come to confuse it with porridge. Also, sometimes grain is mixed into them and it is impossible to clarify it well. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 113, he wrote that they prohibited it because they are things gathered into piles, and one may come to confuse them with grain. According to this, rice should have been permitted, because it is not something gathered into piles and is not comparable to kitniyos in this regard, as is stated in the Gemara in Nedarim 55 regarding one who vows from dagan. See there. But from the implication of the poskim, one should not distinguish between rice and types of kitniyos. See the Bach, who writes that reason would give greater basis to be stringent with rice according to Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as stated in the chapter Arvei Pesachim. Yet no one is concerned for that. The agreement of the poskim is that all of this is merely a stringency; however, where the prohibition is practiced, it is practiced, and one should not change. So too wrote the Taz.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise it is permitted to keep types of kitniyos in the house.</b> This is somewhat difficult, for what novelty does this teach? Even the five species of grain may be kept in the house. One cannot say that it comes to teach that even if they were soaked and immersed in water it is still permitted to keep them, for this too is already heard from the fact that he permitted them even for benefit and for lighting from the oils, even though they were first soaked, as written in seif katan 7. Also, the main novelty is missing from the text. Perhaps it was necessary to teach that it is permitted to keep them in the house and we do not fear that he may err and come to a stumbling block by cooking and eating them, since they are not prohibited by the law itself. With the five species of grain, where we do not fear that he will cook them, this is because they are complete chametz and people separate themselves from them. Also, it is not the normal way to cook and eat them in that manner, except for kinds of groats called gropen, and these in any case are forbidden to keep, as the Magen Avraham wrote, because they moisten them with water before crushing. From what I heard, immediately after they are moistened with water they crush them, and the bran and flour dust scatter from them; thus it is complete chametz, and if Pesach passed over it, they are forbidden. As for what my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Soles Belulah in the name of what he heard, that his grandfather the gaon permitted selling them to a non-Jew, I already discussed this matter with him, and he said that he too investigated and concluded that it is complete chametz, and that the listener heard and erred.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it has the taste of grain.</b> Even if there is not an olive's volume within the time of eating a half-loaf. This follows the view of the Rambam, Ramban, Rosh, Tur, and Maggid Mishneh, unlike the Rashba and the Raavad, who hold that an olive's volume within the time of eating a half-loaf is specifically required. In the Magen Avraham, siman 208, seif katan 15, there is a printing error, for he wrote the opposite in the name of the Rashba. See the Beit Yosef. My brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Soles Belulah: Since the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that with rice there specifically must be an olive's volume within the time of eating a half-loaf, and in Yoreh Deah siman 324 he wrote in his name that all this is specifically with rice and wheat, according to this, with other species one would need a majority of grain specifically, unlike the Olas Shabbos, and this requires study. In truth, according to the Rashba his words are correct, and I likewise saw this explicitly in the words of the Rashba in his rulings on challah, where he derived this from the Yerushalmi. See there. However, according to the Rambam and those who support him, that with rice he fulfills the obligation even when there is not an olive's volume within the time of eating a half-loaf, then with other species it is sufficient to have that measure. This is explicit and clearly implied from the language of the Maggid Mishneh cited in the Beit Yosef, and this is the intent of the author of Olas Shabbos. See there. The Magen Avraham ruled that one should be stringent and require an olive's volume within the time of eating a half-loaf even with rice. This is an excessive stringency against the implication of the Rishonim and the agreement of the later authorities here and in Yoreh Deah, siman 324.

See above at the beginning of siman 567. In such a case, even if the baking was also done by a non-Jew while a Jew stood over him, it is also permitted, only it is not guarded matzah with which to discharge the obligation of the first night, as explained below in siman 460. This may also be proven from the sugya at the beginning of Chullin regarding the matzos of a Kusi. See there. Only a Jew must be present at the kneading and baking so that chametz is not mixed into it. All this is simple and clear, unlike the Olas Shabbos, who was not precise. However, all this is according to the law. In practice, Jews are holy, and they have the custom to make guarding for all matzos from the time of grinding, or at least from the time of kneading. Therefore the poskim wrote that all our matzos have the presumption of being guarded, as explained below in siman 477.

<b>Flour from the market.</b> This implies specifically flour, but clean semolina is forbidden to take, for it is complete chametz, as written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 119, because they soak it and are not careful with their soaking. This is cited in Beit Yosef, siman 442. However, the language of the Kol Bo, page 51b, says that one may take semolinas from the market. More difficult at first glance is how they ruled simply that in pressing circumstances one may take flour from the market because we do not presume a prohibition, when it is explicit in the tenth chapter of Machshirin, mishnah 2, and in the Rambam, chapter 7 of Tum'as Ochlin, that all flour and semolinas from the market are presumed to have been soaked. One must say that this is the meaning: we do not presume that they soaked it in a way that made it chametz, for according to the law of the Gemara it is permitted to soak, only the Geonim decreed and prohibited it, and in pressing circumstances they did not decree. Similarly wrote the Taz. However, in the responsa of the Tzemach Tzedek, siman 32, he is stringent in this matter. He wrote that when it is certainly known that some of them always wash the wheat, which is worse than soaking, then even if most sellers do not wash, it is forbidden because a fixed source is treated as half and half, and even keeping it is forbidden. From bakers one should not buy at all, for they certainly wash. He concluded: However, my practice is not to prohibit keeping the flour, and also not to prohibit eating it on Pesach in pressing circumstances, because it is not certainly known that some flour sellers always wash, so the fixed source is not identifiable in its place. But from bakers one should not buy, for they certainly wash, though grain flour may be bought from them. So too the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of one great authority, that in our country, where they regularly wash the wheat and leave it in water, it is forbidden to take flour from the market, for it is exactly like grain that sank in a river. So ruled all the sages of Prague, and once a certain decisor erred and ruled to take flour from the market, and he decreed a fast upon himself for his error. In my humble opinion, it appears that one should distinguish: specifically from those millers in the city or those who sell flour on a fixed basis one should not buy, and certainly not from bakers, who have clean semolina. But it is permitted to buy from villagers, who do not have clean semolina, as is explicit in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, Pesachim 40a: What was the reason at first? Because they had not soaked. And if they did soak, we do not presume that they soaked in a way that became chametz. Also, the reason of the Tzemach Tzedek based on a fixed source does not apply to them, for they are all mobile each day; these go and others come, and all have separated from their fixed place. The matter is also visible to the eye, for their semolina is not so clean. I heard that in the days of my grandfather, the gaon Maharshal of blessed memory, there was once a shortage of flour for Pesach and he permitted buying from the market. Possibly this too was in the manner I wrote. One should not be so stringent against the implication of the early and later poskim, especially for matzos that are not for the mitzvah. See above, seif katan 15. Maharil wrote that the flour made by the peddlers and called kraft meal is complete chametz.

<b>ומכבסין אותם. </b> וכ' הט"ז וטוב לקנות חדשים גם מה"ד נ"ל דאסור ע"י כיבוס וראיה מ"ש הרא"ש שאם שפשפו אווז במורסן כיון דאית ביה נקבים אסור וכ"ש בשקים שעשוי נקבים באריגתם וכן המפות שעושין עליהן המצות ע"כ תוכן דבריו. ולא ידעתי למה ממרחק הביא לחמו ואינו מביא ראיה מנפה ורי"ב איי"זן וסלים שמביא הרב עצמו לעיל סי' תנ"א סעיף י"ט בהג"ה שאינו מועיל הגעלה וכ' שם ובשקים נוהגין היתר אלא ודאי דשאני שקים שמתכבסים היטב זה בזה במשמוש היד מבפנים ומבחוץ מה שא"כ בנפה וכה"ג וכ"ש באווזא דלא שייך כיבוס כיון שהחמץ נכנס מבפנים ע"י שפשוף וק"ל. ועמש"ל סי' תנ"א ס"ק נ"ב ונ"ג ופוק חזי מה עמא דבר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And they launder them.</b> The Taz wrote that it is good to buy new ones, and according to the law as well it appears to him that laundering is prohibited. His proof is from what the Rosh wrote, that if one rubbed a goose with bran, since it has holes it is forbidden, and certainly this applies to sacks, which have holes in their weave, and likewise to the cloths on which matzos are made. This is the substance of his words. I do not know why he brought his proof from afar and did not bring proof from a sieve, a grater, and baskets, which the Rav himself cited above in siman 451, seif 19 in the gloss, where hagalah does not help. There he wrote that with sacks the practice is to permit. Rather, certainly sacks are different, since they are laundered well against each other by hand from inside and outside, unlike a sieve and similar items, and certainly unlike a goose, where laundering is not relevant, since the chametz enters inside through rubbing. This is clear. See what I wrote above, siman 451, seif katan 52 and 53, and go out and see what the people do.

<b>אסור להניח ע"ג בהמה. </b> הטעם כתב בת"ה סי' קי"ח לפי שהקמח מתחמם בגוף הבהמה ועוד דלא ברירא לן אי זיעת בהמה מכשרת ומחמצת אף ע"ג דלפי הסברא אינו מכשיר ומחמצת כמו זיעת האדם מ"מ אין להקל במלתא דלא פשיטא עכ"ל ולפ"ז היה נראה בדיעבד יש לאסור הקמח שנתלחלח מזיעת הבהמה מספק אכן המ"א כתב וז"ל ונ"ל דבדעבד יש להורות דזיעת הבהמה אינה מחמצת עכ"ל נ"ל עיקר דמה שמסתפק בת"ה אי זיעת הבהמ' מכשיר זה ודאי אינו להמעיין בפ"ב ופ"ו דמכשירין וברמב"ם פ"י מהל' טומאת אוכלין וע"ל סי' תס"ז ומ"מ צ"ע אם מותר ללוש מהן מצות דמי פירות כשמערבין בה מים מחמיץ אף דאפשר לומר דהוא תולדה בפני עצמו וגם כשנתייבש המי פירות בתוכו שוב אינו מחמצת ונ"ל זה טעם הי"א שבטור בסי' זה דבאכילת עכבר דהוי מי פירות ואינו מחמצת אכן בהגהות סמ"ק שהביא בב"י בשם הר"פ משמע דאף כה"ג מחמיץ וע"ל סי' תס"ב ותס"ו ומ"ש שם ס"ק ז' וח' ע"ש.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to place it upon an animal.</b> The reason is written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 118: because the flour becomes heated by the animal's body, and also because it is not clear to us whether an animal's sweat renders food susceptible to impurity and causes leavening. Although logically it should not render susceptible or cause leavening, like human sweat, nevertheless one should not be lenient in a matter that is unresolved. According to this, it would seem that after the fact one should prohibit flour that became moistened from an animal's sweat because of doubt. However, the Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that after the fact one should rule that an animal's sweat does not cause leavening. This appears to me to be primary, for what Terumas HaDeshen was uncertain about, whether animal sweat renders susceptible, is certainly not so for one who examines chapter 2 and chapter 6 of Machshirin and the Rambam, chapter 10 of Tum'as Ochlin. See below, siman 467. Nevertheless, it requires study whether it is permitted to knead matzos from it, since fruit juice mixed with water causes leavening. One might say that it is an independent derivative, and also that once the fruit juice inside it has dried, it no longer causes leavening. It appears to me that this is the reason of the opinion cited in the Tur in this siman regarding a mouse's eating, that it is fruit juice and does not cause leavening. However, in the glosses of the Semak brought by the Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz, it implies that even in such a case it causes leavening. See below, simanim 462 and 466, and what I wrote there in seif katan 7 and 8.

<b>שומרין אותו. </b> וכתב גיסי הרב בסא"ז ועתה נוהגין לכסות הריחים בפשתן ולפ"ו אפשר להתיר אף קמח הראשון עכ"ל וכתב המ"א בשם מ"צ כשטוחנין ברחים תבואה לתות' לא יטחנו באותו בית באותו פעם אפילו ברחים אחרת כי האבק פורח ומתערב וקטן לא ישמור הרחים עכ"ל. ולא ראיתי נזהרים בזה וטוחנין באותו בית לפסח שטוחנין בו קמח אחר וצריך לומר משום דאיכא לספוקי שמא התבואה אחרת ג"כ אינה לתותה ואת"ל שהיא לתותה שמא לא לתת בדרך שנתחמץ וכמ"ש לעיל ס"ק ט"ו גם בדיעבד ליכא אסור כיון שנתבטל קודם פסח כמבואר לעיל סעיף ג' אכן תוך הפסח יש ליזהר בזה נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They guard it.</b> My brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Soles Belulah: Now the practice is to cover the mill with linen, and according to this it may be possible to permit even the first flour. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Matzas Mitzvah: When grinding soaked grain in a mill, they should not grind in that house at that time even with another mill, because the dust flies and mixes in; and a minor should not guard the mill. I have not seen people careful about this, and they grind for Pesach in the same house in which other flour is ground. One must say this is because there is a doubt, perhaps the other grain was also not soaked, and even if you say that it was soaked, perhaps it was not soaked in a way that became chametz, as I wrote above in seif katan 15. Also, after the fact there is no prohibition, since it was nullified before Pesach, as explained above in seif 3. However, during Pesach one should be careful about this, so it appears to me.

<b>יום או יומים. </b> וכתב מהרי"ו בתשובה סי' קצ"ג מנהג כשר שלא לטחון לצורך פסח תוך ג' ימים קודם ע"פ והמחבר שכתב יום או יומים כונתו מע"ל וכמו שפירש"י בפ' משפטים גבי יום או יומים יעמוד וכה"ג דקדקו בל' הפוסקי' כמבואר ביו"ד סי' ע' ובת"ה כלל ו' וע"ש בספרי מ"י סק"ט וע' בח"מ סי' ש"ו ס"ח בסמ"ע כל זה נ"ל ברור ודלא כמ"א שכתב דלא בעי מע"ל רק לינת לילה ונראה דמדמה למים דבעינן לינה ולע"ד נראה מלשון הפוסקי' כמ"ש ואם עבר ולש נראה דאין לאסור בדעבד ומשמר היטב ועיין בס"ק כ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A day or two.</b> Mahariv wrote in responsa, siman 193, that it is a proper custom not to grind for Pesach within three days before the eve of Pesach. The Mechaber, who wrote a day or two, means a full twenty-four-hour period, as Rashi explained in Parshas Mishpatim regarding the verse, if he stands a day or two. Similar precision is made in the language of the poskim, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 70, and in Terumas HaDeshen, klal 6. See there in my work Minchas Yaakov, seif katan 9, and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 306, seif 8, in the Sema. All this appears clear to me, unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote that a full twenty-four-hour period is not needed, only that it stay overnight. It seems that he compares it to water, which requires staying overnight. In my humble opinion, the language of the poskim appears as I wrote. If one transgressed and kneaded, it seems one should not prohibit it after the fact, provided he guards it well. See seif katan 23.
