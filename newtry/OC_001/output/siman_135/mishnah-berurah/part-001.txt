The Rambam wrote in chapter 12 of Hilkhot Tefillah: Moshe Rabbeinu enacted for Israel that they read from the Torah publicly on Shabbat, and on Monday and Thursday mornings, so that they should not remain three days without hearing Torah. Ezra enacted that they also read at Minchah every Shabbat because of the idlers, and he also enacted that on Monday and Thursday three people read and that they not read fewer than ten verses. In Moshe Rabbeinu's time the enactment was not specifically that three read and that exactly ten verses be read; Ezra came and enacted this. In the Yerushalmi it says: Moshe enacted for Israel that they read from the Torah on Shabbatot, Yomim Tovim, Rosh Chodesh, and Chol HaMoed; the Rif brings this in Perek HaKorei Omed, and so it is in the Rambam, chapter 13, halakhah 8.

(5) If they failed, etc. This refers to a case where the reading was omitted the entire day because of a quarrel or the like. If they omitted only the Shacharis reading, but by Minchah calm had been restored, then if there is time to read the entire parashah they should read the entire parashah at Minchah and call seven men, because the entire Shabbos day is a time for the reading. Rather, they read only the passage from the coming Shabbos’s order, not from the previous Shabbos. Dagul Merevavah; see there for the reasoning.

**** ENGLISH ****
(7) The reading of the parashah, etc. If two parashiyos were joined on the Shabbos when the reading was omitted, Maharam Mintz holds that they are not made up at all, because we never find three parashiyos read on one Shabbos. Nor do we say to read only one of them, because there is no partial remedy. The same applies if two parashiyos are joined on the present Shabbos: the previous Shabbos’s parashah is not made up, for the same reason. Hagahos Minhagim disputes all this, and Eliyah Rabbah agrees that whatever was omitted on the previous Shabbos must always be made up. Magen Giborim also inclines to this view. Some say that if Parashas Vayechi was omitted, it should not be read together with Parashas Shemos, because when two parashiyos are read one man must read from the end of the first into the beginning of the second so that they are joined, and this should not be done from two separate Sifrei Torah. The same applies to Pekudei and Vayikra and similar cases. Others disagree and hold that it is always made up. Shulchan Atzei Shittim writes that one who follows this practice should not be protested. Shaarei Ephraim writes that if the reading was omitted in one synagogue but most of that congregation heard Krias HaTorah in another synagogue, they need not make it up. If most of that congregation did not hear the reading at all, then even though other synagogues in the city read properly, those who did not hear it and comprise the majority of this congregation must make up the reading.

**** ENGLISH ****
(9) A kohen reads first in the Torah. The verse states, “You shall sanctify him,” and Chazal received that this means that in every sacred matter he is first to open, first to recite a blessing, and first to take a choice portion. Nevertheless, if a kohen wishes to honor his rebbe or someone greater than himself, he may do so. Regarding Krias HaTorah in the synagogue, however, Chazal enacted for the sake of peace that the kohen and levi may not waive their precedence. Rather, a kohen reads first, then a levi, then a Yisrael, so that the matter not lead to dispute, with each person saying, “I am greater and should read first.” There is no distinction between Shabbos and Yom Tov, Monday and Thursday, or any other time of public Torah reading. Peri Megadim. If there is no kohen in the synagogue, precedence follows greatness in wisdom and years of study, even if another person is older in age. Magen Avraham, seif katan 7.

(18) It is preferable that the kohen leave. Strictly speaking, even if he does not leave, a Yisrael may be called in his place because he is unable to receive the aliyah. Initially, however, it is preferable that he leave the synagogue, lest there be concern for his status since not everyone knows that he is not fasting. If another kohen who is fasting is present, all agree that he need not leave; he should merely inform the chazzan not to call him. A congregation had an ancient practice that on Shabbos Bereishis a congregant donated money for synagogue needs in order to be called first at the beginning of the Torah. If the kohen refuses to waive his precedence, his objection is disregarded and the congregation follows its custom, since it is an ancient practice instituted for the honor of Torah. Maharik, shoresh 9; see there at length in praise of this custom. Knesses HaGedolah and Peri Chadash agree that even if the kohen refuses to leave, he is disregarded and the donating Yisrael is called. This applies specifically where the custom was ancient, universally accepted, and established with the consent of the kohanim, whose longstanding practice was to leave when the Yisrael was called, because of the enhancement and honor of Torah created by large donations for the opening aliyah. It does not apply where a city seeks to establish a fixed practice of selling the mitzvos without the kohanim’s consent and requiring them to leave during Krias HaTorah, which they have no authority to impose. Responsa Chasam Sofer 24; likewise Responsa Shivas Tzion.

(36) And similarly is the practice, etc. Levush writes that nowadays, even after the seven required aliyos, the practice is to call a kohen or levi only for the final aliyah or for maftir, and the other later authorities rule likewise. Levush explains that there is no concern about the final aliyah casting doubt on him. On the contrary, in these generations the final aliyah is considered the most distinguished because it completes the parashah, and the greatest person in the congregation is called for it. Although maftir follows, Kaddish intervenes between them. There is also no objection to a kohen receiving maftir, because it is treated like the beginning of a new reading. Even if the final aliyah was also given to a kohen, there is no concern about kohen after kohen because Kaddish intervenes. The completion occurs in the second Sefer Torah, from which the Rosh Chodesh reading is read. In the second Sefer Torah, where the final aliyah completes the reading, and in the third, from which the Chanukah maftir is read, a kohen may be called for the final aliyah and another kohen for maftir.
