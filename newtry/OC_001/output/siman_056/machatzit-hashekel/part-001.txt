**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It appears to me that he holds, etc.</b> The Shach wrote in the margin, ‘See Tosafos there.’ His meaning is that one should not say that we do not rule like Rav Safra, but rather are stringent like Rava. That is incorrect, for Tosafos there, s.v. Amar Rava, write: ‘This is difficult, for at the end of Mitzvas Chalitzah Abaye said that one who prompts the yevamah, when she performs chalitzah and must recite “lo avah yevami,” and the yavam must say “lo chafatzti lekachtah,” should not prompt her with “lo” separately and “avah yevami” separately, for that would imply “my brother-in-law desired”; rather, he must prompt her with “lo avah yevami” together. Likewise, he should not prompt him with “lo” separately and “chafatzti lekachtah” separately, for that would imply “I desired to take her”; rather, “lo chafatzti lekachtah” together. Rava said that completing the phrase is unnecessary. One may answer that Rava said this after hearing Rav Safra’s teaching here.’ Thus Rava himself retracted. See Tosafos in Yevamos, who offer another answer according to which Rava did not retract.

דעכ"פ י"ל בנשימ' א' לא אבה יבמי ולא חפצתי לקחת' דאע"ג דמן הדין א"צ נשימה אחת כדאסיק רבא כנ"ל אפ"ה אנו מחמירים וס"ל לרמ"א דה"ה דיש להחמיר ביש"ר מברך. ואע"ג דבחליצה מחמירים שצ"ל בנשימה א'. וכאן קתני שאין להפסיק משמע דעכ"פ רשאי להנשים ביניהן צ"ל דרמ"א ס"ל דהא קיל מחליצה ונחתינן דרגא. ולעלמי בוי"ו. שיש רוצים לו' שי"ל לעלמי בלא ו' כדי שלא יהי' בשבע תיבות מן יהא עד עלמיא כ"א כ"ח אותיות. ועז"א כל העונה איש"ר בכל כחו קורעין לו כו' גזר דינו כו' ור"ל כ"ח אותיות ועז"כ הרב"י דאין לשנות מטבע שטבעו חכמים כאשר קבלנו מקדמונינו לו' ולעלמי בו' בשביל שום דרשה גם כ' הרב"י דמה"ט י"א שמי' בלא י' כדי שיהיה כ"ח אותיות ולהאומרי' עד דאמירן בעלמ' הם כ"ח תיבו':
**** ENGLISH ****
Nevertheless, ‘lo avah yevami’ and ‘lo chafatzti lekachtah’ should be said in one breath. Although by strict law one breath is unnecessary, as Rava concludes, nevertheless we are stringent. The Rama holds that the same stringency applies to ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah mevorach.’ Although regarding chalitzah we are stringent that it be recited in one breath, whereas here the teaching says only that one may not interrupt, implying that breathing between the words is at least permitted, one must say that the Rama holds this case to be more lenient than chalitzah and therefore lowers the requirement by one degree. One should say ‘ule’almei’ with a vav. Some wish to say ‘le’almei’ without a vav so that from ‘yehei’ through ‘almaya’ there will be exactly twenty-eight letters. Concerning this it is said that whoever answers ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ with all his strength has his harsh decree torn up, meaning with the twenty-eight letters. The Beit Yosef therefore writes that one should not alter the formula established by the Sages, as received from our predecessors, by omitting the vav because of any homiletical interpretation. The Beit Yosef also writes that for this reason some say ‘shemei’ without a yud so that there will be twenty-eight letters, while according to those who recite through ‘da’amiran be’alma’ there are twenty-eight words.

<b>(ס"ק ד) ויש לעמוד כו'</b> מיהו נ"ל דמ"ש שט"ס כו' ר"ל דלפי המובן הפשוט שהאר"י אומר שט"ס בירוש' אפשר להבין דהדין לא מסתבר ליה או היה קבל' בידו שהדין שבירושלמי ט"ס. וא"א לחלוק על האר"י בלי טעם. לכ"כ מ"א דנ"ל שטעמו מ"ש שהדין של ירושלמי ט"ס משום שהראיה שמביא בירושלמי שנאמר בשופטים סי' ג' שאמר אהוד לעגלון מלך מואב קום כי דבר אלקים לי אליך ויקם משל הכסא וכיון שצוהו לעמוד ילפי' מיני' עכ"פ בקדוש' שיש לעמוד (אף דבהזכרת השם אין עומדים דדברי נבוא' שאני) וכיון דלא כתיב בקרא שהנביא צוהו לעמוד ליתא לראיית הירושלמי וע"כ גם דינו של הירושלמי ליתא וכל הדין ט"ס וכיון שנודע טעמו של האר"י יש בידינו לתרץ קושייתו ולקיים דינו של הירושלמי כמ"ש מ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 4) One should stand, etc.</b> Nevertheless, it appears to me that the statement that this is a scribal error means as follows. The plain understanding is that the Arizal said the Yerushalmi contains a scribal error, which could be understood to mean that he considered the law itself unreasonable or had a tradition that the Yerushalmi’s ruling was erroneous. One cannot disagree with the Arizal without explanation. Therefore the Magen Avraham writes that, in his view, the Arizal’s reason for saying that the Yerushalmi’s ruling was a scribal error is that the Yerushalmi adduces proof from Judges, chapter 3, where Ehud said to Eglon king of Moav, ‘Arise, for I have a word of God for you,’ and he arose from the throne. Since Ehud commanded him to stand, the Yerushalmi derives that at least for a matter of holiness one must stand, even though one does not stand merely upon mention of Hashem’s Name, because words of prophecy are different. Yet Scripture does not state that the prophet commanded him to stand. Therefore the Yerushalmi’s proof is absent, and consequently its ruling too is absent, with the whole passage being a scribal error. Once the Arizal’s reason is known, we can answer his difficulty and uphold the Yerushalmi’s ruling, as the Magen Avraham writes.

**** ENGLISH ****
This proves that he commanded him to stand. Sefer Yereim is not available to me. Yet in my humble opinion Sanhedrin 60a does not imply this. The Mishnah there teaches that when the blasphemer’s trial is concluded, the greatest of the witnesses must state before the Sanhedrin the Name that he blasphemed, and the judges then stand. The Gemara adduces proof from the verse, ‘He arose from the throne,’ and derives an a fortiori argument: if Eglon king of Moav, who was a non-Jew, stood, then Jews certainly must stand. This implies that Ehud did not command him, for otherwise the proof should have been brought from Ehud’s words. It is possible that on this point the Bavli disagrees with the Yerushalmi.
