(ח) That he does not make a vessel — meaning that he does not make a hole for an opening but breaks it; and afterward he wrote a further novelty that even a mere hole — meaning a hole that is not fit for an opening — there are two improvements in it: he does not break the barrel and also it is not an opening — nevertheless it is forbidden. The Rambam wrote chapter 23: One who makes a hole that is fit for inserting and removing — such as the hole in a rooster coop that is fit for inserting light and removing smoke — is liable for makeh b'patish [which is there the general term for every completion of melacha that is a toldah of this av melacha, that the artisan strikes with a hammer on the vessel at its completion to straighten its crookedness]; therefore they decreed upon every hole, even if it is only fit for inserting or removing alone, lest he come to make a hole for which one is liable; and see in Magen Avraham, who explained that even if it is a coop that is not attached to the ground, where there is no concern of building in making the hole — nevertheless he is liable for makeh b'patish, which is completion of the coop's melacha; and he wrote further in chapter 10: Whoever does a thing that is completion of melacha — this is a toldah of makeh b'patish and he is liable. One who makes any hole, whether in wood, in metal, in building, or in vessels — this is a toldah of makeh b'patish and he is liable; and every opening that is not fit for inserting and removing — one is not liable for making it, end of his words. It is in the Gemara: if one drives a nail into a wall [to hang upon it] he is liable for makeh b'patish.
