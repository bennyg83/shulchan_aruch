**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To be stringent.</b> This is so that the non-Jew not become accustomed to doing so. It appears there in the Hagahot Mordechai that the reason is that the non-Jew has not yet acquired it until he removes it; consequently, it is as though the Jew sells it to him on Shabbos. See also what I wrote in siman 246, seif 3, where we rule accordingly. Even regarding chametz, we rule in siman 440 that designating a place does not suffice, and in siman 347 we likewise rule stringently. One should therefore not rely on this leniency.

**** ENGLISH ****

This implies that on Wednesday or Thursday it is permitted even when the work cannot be completed before Shabbos. This excludes a mission, since the messenger may not find the person at home and may have to follow him.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he was doing it, etc.</b> If he performs the work for payment, even when no price was fixed, the Jew need not protest (Beit Yosef and Bach). This requires study, for paid work without a fixed price is worse than gratuitous work, since one may not initially give it to him. It therefore appears to me that the Rokeach is correct: only where a price was fixed is there no need to protest. The Yerushalmi likewise concerns a fixed-price arrangement, for without a fixed price it is prohibited to give it to him at all, and all the more so must one protest. See what I wrote at the end of siman 276, seif 4, and in siman 334, seif 25. This applies specifically where one gives garments to a launderer. If, however, he ordered a non-Jew to make shoes for him and sees him working on Shabbos, he need not protest, because the Jew's name has not yet become attached to those shoes; if the non-Jew wishes, he may give those shoes to someone else and make another pair for the Jew. The Hagahot Mordechai, chapter one, states: We say that the non-Jew acts on his own behalf only when he performs labor with his own property. To kindle a fire with a Jew's wood, however, he must obtain permission from the owner of the wood. If the Jew sees and remains silent, the non-Jew acts on his behalf, and the Jew must protest.

Accordingly, garments that are recognizably Jewish-owned and that non-Jews wash at the river require protest when the Jew sees the work being done on Shabbos. The truth, however, points to the conclusion that here and in siman 244 the authorities intended the same rule. The Beit Yosef's formulation here, that one should be stringent, therefore requires study.

<b>שעושה מנעלים</b>. ז"ל הרב"י כתוב בהג"א פ"ג די"ט עכו"ם שעשה מנעלים בי"ט לית ביה משום מוקצה שירצה ובא יהודי ולקחם מהחנות ואסרם הר"ר י"ט משום מוקצה. והר"ר אליה התיר' הואיל וגמרו בידי אדם ויכול לגמרו בי"ט בסתם למכור לכל מי ע"כ, וצ"ע דליתסר משום דאין מביאין כלים מבית האומן וכמ"ש בסמוך ושמא י"ל דשאני מנעלים דאין דרך בני אדם לתת לו מלאכה לעשות אלא הוא עושה משלו ומוכר הלכך אפי' עשאם בי"ט לית לן בה דבדידיה קא טרח עכ"ל ועפ"ז כ' רמ"א הג"ה זו והמעיין בב"י יראה שכל דבריו דברי תימה הם דכ"ע ס"ל דאין מביאין כלים מבית האומן (עסי' תקל"ד מ"ש) ואם הביא להר"ן שרי ליהנות ממנו ולהג"א אסור הואיל ועשאו בשביל ישראל אבל בעכו"ם שעושה על המקח אם הביא שרי ליהנות דהא לא עשאו אדעתא דישראל ואם עשאו בשבת בעיר שרוב' ישראל אסור כמ"ש סי' תקט"ו ס"ו אבל לכתחל' אסור לילך וליקח ממנו אם לא בעכו"ם חנוני שאינו אומן כלל אפשר דמותר כנ"ל ועב"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Who makes shoes.</b> The Beit Yosef writes: The Hagahot Ashri, chapter three of Beitzah, states that shoes made by a non-Jew on a festival are not inherently muktzeh. A Jew came and took them from the shop. Rabbeinu Yom Tov prohibited them because of muktzeh, while Rabbeinu Eliyahu permitted them, since their completion depends upon human action, they could be completed on the festival, and they were made generally for sale to anyone. This requires study, for they should be prohibited because one may not bring articles from a craftsman's house, as stated below. Perhaps shoes are different, because people do not ordinarily give the craftsman materials to fashion them; rather, he makes them from his own materials and sells them. Therefore, even if he made them on the festival, it is of no concern, because he labored for himself. Based on this, the Rama wrote this gloss. Yet anyone who examines the Beit Yosef will see that his words are surprising. All agree that one may not bring articles from a craftsman's house, as discussed in siman 534. If the non-Jew brought them, according to the Ran one may benefit from them, whereas according to the Hagahot Ashri it is prohibited because he made them for a Jew. If, however, a non-Jew makes goods for general sale and brings them, one may benefit from them, since he did not make them for the Jew. If he made them on Shabbos in a city whose population is mostly Jewish, they are prohibited, as stated in siman 515, seif 6. Initially, however, it is prohibited to go and take them from him. Perhaps only from a non-Jewish shopkeeper who is not a craftsman at all would it be permitted. This appears correct. See Bach.

<b>בוסר ומלילות</b>. צ"ל דיותר עומדין למשקין משאר פירות דהא קי"ל דסוחטין שאר פירות לכתחלה וכמ"ש סי' ש"כ (תוס') וצ"ע דמ"מ אי סחיט להו לא חייב חטאת דד"ת לא חייב אלא על דריכת זתים וענבים בלבד כדאי' בשבת דף קמ"ה סוף ע"א וכ"פ הרמב"ם פ"ו וא"כ המשקין היוצאין מהן יהיו מותרין אפי' לא ריסקן כמ"ש התוס' בעצמן אח"כ כל היכא דליכא חיוב חטאת המשקין מותרין, וע"ק למה דילג הטור שום המוזכר בגמ' ונקט בוסר ומלילות וכן ברמזים לא נקט שום וצ"ע למה ונ"ל דס"ל דמאי דאתמר בגמרא שום היינו שאם לא ריסקן מבע"י אסור לגמרו בידים בשבת כמ"ש ס"ס שכ"א אבל משקין היוצאים ממנו מותרים אפי' לא ריסקן כמש"ל אבל בוסר הוא ענבים ומלילות הוא שבלים וידוע דסחיטה היא תולדה דדש וא"כ במלילות איכא נמי חיובא דאורייתא כשמפרק אותן משבלים ולכן אם לא רסקן משקין היוצאין אסורין כנ"ל ברור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Unripe grapes and ears of grain.</b> We must say that these are more commonly intended for their liquid than other fruits, for we rule that one may initially squeeze other fruits, as stated in siman 320 (Tosafot). This requires study, because even if one squeezes them there is no liability to bring a sin-offering; by Torah law one is liable only for pressing olives and grapes, as stated in Shabbos 145a and ruled by Rambam in chapter 8. Accordingly, the liquids that flow from them should be permitted even if they were not crushed beforehand, as Tosafot themselves write subsequently that wherever there is no liability to bring a sin-offering, the liquid is permitted. A further difficulty is why the Tur omitted garlic, which is mentioned in the Gemara, and listed unripe grapes and ears of grain; in his abridgment as well he did not list garlic. This requires study. It appears to me that he understood the Gemara's reference to garlic to mean that if it was not crushed before Shabbos, one may not finish crushing it by hand on Shabbos, as stated at the end of siman 321. The liquid that flows from it, however, is permitted even when it was not crushed, as explained above. Unripe grapes are grapes, and ears of grain are stalks. It is known that squeezing is a derivative of threshing; separating kernels from stalks therefore also entails a Torah prohibition. Consequently, if they were not crushed beforehand, the liquid that flows from them is prohibited. This appears clearly correct.

<b>להשמעת קול</b>. ואיכא זילותא דשבת ואם הרחיים של עכו"ם פשיטא דשרי ליתן לתוכה מבע"י ואם הישראל נותן לעכו"ם החטים מע"ש אפי' העכו"ם טוחנן בשבת שרי כמ"ש ס"ב ובלבד שלא יעמוד הישראל אצלו דאז מיחזי כשלוחו וכמ"ש ס"ס ש"ז אבל אם השע' צריכה לכך כגון סמוך לפסח רשאי הישראל לשמור הרחיים שלא יתחמץ דהא שבות דשבות היא אפי' נותן הישראל להרחיים בשבת ליכא חיוב חטאת עד שיטחון ברחיים של יד וכ"מ בתוס' דף י"ח ד"ה ולימא מר כו' דטעמא דב"ש דגזרינן כו' וק' דמ"מ קשה לימא גבי רחיים נמי משום גזירה אלא ע"כ אפי' נתנו בשבת ליכא חיוב חטאת וכ"כ הסמ"ג וסמ"ק בהדיא (וע' בתוס' ספ"ק דע"א מיהו ממ"ש סי' שט"ז גבי מצוד' בשם התוס' משמע דחייב חטאת בדבר דאתי ממילא וכ"פ התוס' במנחות דף נ"ו דומיא דמדביק פת בתנור ונאפה) וכ"מ בפ"ח ברמב"ם א"כ כשאומר לעכו"ם ה"ל שבות דשבות דשרי במקום מצוה כמ"ש סי' ש"ז ס"ה וכ"ש היכא דלא א"ל כלל רק יושב ומשמר ועססי' ש"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because it produces sound.</b> This degrades the dignity of Shabbos. If the mill belongs to a non-Jew, it is obviously permitted to place grain into it before Shabbos. If a Jew gives grain to a non-Jew before Shabbos, it is permitted even if the non-Jew grinds it on Shabbos, as stated in seif 2, provided the Jew does not stand beside him, for then he appears to be his agent, as stated at the end of siman 307. If circumstances require it, such as shortly before Pesach, the Jew may guard the mill so that the grain not become leavened, because this is a rabbinic prohibition compounded with another rabbinic prohibition. Even if the Jew places the grain into the mill on Shabbos, he is not liable to bring a sin-offering unless he grinds with a hand mill. This is also implied by Tosafot on Shabbos 18a, s.v. “Let the master say,” who explain Beit Shammai's reason as a decree. One might ask why the Gemara does not likewise explain the mill by way of a decree. It must be that even if he placed it there on Shabbos, he would not be liable to bring a sin-offering. Semag and Semak state this explicitly. See, however, Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah. Moreover, from what is written in siman 316 regarding a trap in the name of Tosafot, it appears that one is liable to bring a sin-offering for an action that proceeds automatically, and Tosafot in Menachot 56b likewise rule so, comparable to attaching bread in an oven and its subsequently baking. This is also implied in chapter 8 of Rambam. Accordingly, instructing a non-Jew is a rabbinic prohibition compounded with another rabbinic prohibition, which is permitted for the sake of a mitzvah, as stated in siman 307, seif 5. All the more so is it permitted where he gives no instruction at all and merely sits and guards it. See the end of siman 307.

<b>בתפילין בראשו</b>. ולא גזרינן שמא יצטרך לפנות ויסירן כמ"ש סי' ש"א ס"ז לפי שאינו שוכחן בראשו שאסור להסיח דעתו מהן אבל בידו אסור דמסיח דעתו מהן וחיישי' שמא ישכח להצניען עד שתחשך ולבסוף כשיזכור שיש חפץ בידו ישכח שהיא שבת ויצא אבל אין לפרש שישכח החפץ בידו ויצא דבזה ליכא חיוב חטאת אא"כ שכוונתו לעשות מלאכה אלא ששכח שהוא שבת או שלא ידע שמלאכה זו אסורה (תוס' ועיין בב"ח דלא כב"י):
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With tefillin on his head.</b> We do not decree lest he need to relieve himself and remove them, as stated in siman 301, seif 7, because he does not forget them on his head, since it is prohibited to divert one's attention from them. In his hand, however, it is prohibited, because he does divert his attention from them, and we are concerned that he may forget to put them away until dark. Ultimately, when he remembers that he has an object in his hand, he may forget that it is Shabbos and carry it out. One should not explain that he may forget the object in his hand and carry it out, because there is no liability to bring a sin-offering unless he intends to perform the act but forgot that it was Shabbos, or did not know that this labor was prohibited (Tosafot; see Bach, contrary to Beit Yosef).
