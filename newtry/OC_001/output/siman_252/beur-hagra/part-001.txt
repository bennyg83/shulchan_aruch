**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If one saw him... if.</b> This is from the Jerusalem Talmud. From here Beit Yosef proved that work performed in exchange for mere goodwill is permitted only if the Jew sees the non-Jew on Shabbos, as stated there; otherwise, how could he leave the item in the non-Jew’s possession on Shabbos? This applies specifically according to Beit Yosef’s position that, when no price was fixed, it is always forbidden. According to Rama’s position, however, that it is permitted when given on Wednesday or Thursday, one may say that he gave it to him on Wednesday or Thursday. This is why Rama writes, “Even if he gave it to him...” He writes this lest one infer from his earlier statement, “Some disagree if he performs...”

<b>אבל מותר לצאת בתפילין.</b> שם בגמ' י"ב א' תנא דבי רבי ישמעאל כו' והקשו ב"י וט"ז דהא בלא רבה בר רב הונא נמי מותר לצאת דהא לא גזרו אלא אם היה בשבת חייב אם יוצא ובתפילין לא היה חייב ועיין מה שנדחקו ונ"ל דנקט לפי שאינו שוכחן דוקא דבלא"ה אסור אף לרבא דמודה רבא דאיכא דוכתא דגזרינן גזירה לגזירה והיינו שקרוב לבא לידי פשיעה וכמ"ש כל שאסור לצאת לר"ה אסור לצאת לחצר ואפי' בדברים שפטור אבל אסור וה"ט שהאשה עשויה לפשט תכשיטיה וכן האיש להניח כליו והעיקר נראה דלא גזרו סמוך לחשיכה אבל עם חשיכה גזרו ולכן אמר שם תניא חנניא אומר חייב כו' למשמש כו' עם חשיכה שאף אם היה יוצא בשבת לא היה חייב מכל מקום חייב למשמש שעם חשיכה גזרו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is permitted to go out wearing tefillin.</b> This is stated in the Gemara there, 12a: “The school of Rabbi Yishmael taught...” Beit Yosef and Taz ask that even without Rabbah bar Rav Huna it should be permitted to go out wearing them, since the Sages decreed only where going out with the item on Shabbos would incur liability, whereas one would not incur liability for going out with tefillin. See the strained answers they offer. It appears to me that the Gemara specifically relies on the fact that one does not forget tefillin. Were that not so, it would be forbidden even according to Rava, for Rava concedes that there are cases in which the Sages enact a decree upon a decree, namely where one is likely to come to negligence. This is as stated: anything with which one may not go out into the public domain may not be worn even in a courtyard, including items for which one is exempt but whose carriage is forbidden. The reason is that a woman is likely to remove her ornaments and a man to set down his articles. The primary explanation appears to be that the Sages did not decree shortly before nightfall, but they did decree at nightfall itself. Therefore it states there in a baraita: “Chananya says: A person is obligated... to feel his garments... at nightfall.” Although one who went out with the item on Shabbos would not incur liability, he must nevertheless inspect himself, because at nightfall the decree applies.
