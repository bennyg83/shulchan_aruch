(6) Eruvei chatzerot; this is even for a matter of reshus, as is proven from Levush. And the reason is that all eruvei chatzerot is included in a mitzvah, as is written at the end of siman 260 and in siman 366 seif 13 that it is a mitzvah to seek out eruvei chatzerot, and they did not decree during bein hashemashot; and Korban Minchah wrote that nevertheless it is not called on account of this a mitzvah except that they permitted it because the reason is that he is preoccupied and hurries because of it [Tosafos Shabbos]. (2) From the beginning of sunset; the reason for all this is that in Pesachim 94 it states that from sunset until the emergence of the stars is the measure of 4 milin; and in Shabbos [34-35] it states that from sunset bein hashemashot begins [for Rabba according to R. Yehudah whom we rule like]; and it says there that bein hashemashot lasts three-quarters of a mil and afterward it becomes night; and we say there that three medium-sized stars constitute night. If so, there is a contradiction, and perforce Rabbeinu Tam and his camp answer to divide between sunset in Pesachim and sunset in Shabbos there, that the one in Pesachim is the beginning of sunset (from when the body of the sun is hidden when it sets from our eyes) and from then until the emergence of the stars is 4 milin; and when the sun sets in Shabbos there it is the end of sunset, which is when the light of the sun also sets from most of the sky toward the west alone; and from then bein hashemashot begins, which is three-quarters of a mil before the emergence of the stars. And from the beginning of sunset, which is the sunset of the body of the sun, until the end of the aforesaid sunset is day, and its duration is 3 milin and a quarter; and then is the time of tosefet Shabbos, as is written here in Shulchan Aruch; and among all of them they are 4 milin, and until 4 milin from the beginning of sunset it is not certainly night; this is the view of Rabbeinu Tam and his camp; and see below in siman 331 seif 5 in Magen Avraham, it appears there that his view for halachah is like this view; but the Gra z"l disagrees on this view and extended with several proofs that sunset in Shabbos regarding bein hashemashot is also the beginning of sunset like sunset in Pesachim there, and immediately after sunset of the body of the sun bein hashemashot begins according to R. Yehudah whom R. Yochanan ruled like stringently on Shabbos there; and the duration of bein hashemashot is three-quarters of a mil; and the aforesaid difficulty he answers to divide between the emergence of the stars of Shabbos and the emergence of the stars of Pesachim, that the emergence of the stars of Shabbos is the time of night which is three medium-sized stars, and the emergence of the stars of Pesachim is the emergence of all the stars visible at night, which is a much later time, and is 4 milin after the beginning of sunset. And even though it appears to the eyes that the duration of bein hashemashot until the emergence of the stars is much more than three-quarters of a mil, it is as the Gra wrote in his Biur that the measure of the Gemara is stated only regarding the horizon of Babylonia or Eretz Yisrael; and in our countries which are more inclined toward the north it lengthens more; and therefore regarding the end of bein hashemashot we do not have a fixed duration from the Gemara on our horizon, and it depends only according to sight when it is three medium-sized stars, but not large ones; and because we are not expert which are medium, one must wait until small ones as is written siman 293, and see there what we wrote further on this matter. And per the aforesaid view of the Gra, immediately after sunset of the body of the sun over the earth it is complete bein hashemashot, as stated. And in truth this view of his is not new, for Maharam Alshakar already agreed thus in responsum siman 96 that immediately after sunset of the body of the sun it is complete bein hashemashot, and he brought there that thus explicitly wrote the early Geonim in their responsa; they are Rabbeinu Sherira Gaon and Rav Hai Gaon and Rabbeinu Nissim Gaon; also R' Avraham HaChasid son of Rambam z"l also wrote there that thus is implied from the Rif and Rambam and Rashi, see there; and Shach brought it for halachah in Yoreh De'ah siman 266 seif katan 11 regarding the matter of bein hashemashot; and see also in the commentary of Maharalbach on chapter 2 of Hilchot Kiddush HaChodesh of Rambam s.v. klal ha'oleh that he also wrote that from the Rif and Rambam it is implied that he holds that from the beginning of sunset [namely when the sun is hidden from our eyes] it is bein hashemashot; and also Maharal of Prague in his chiddushim on Shabbos explained exactly like the aforesaid view of the Gra; and also Ra'avan holds thus that immediately when the body of the sun sets and is hidden from our eyes the time of bein hashemashot begins, see Hagahot Mordechai chapter 2 of Shabbos; and also in Sefer Seder Zmanim he extended and ruled for practical law like the Gra z"l that immediately after the beginning of sunset it is bein hashemashot. And behold for practical law one must certainly be careful per the view of the Gra and many of the Rishonim and poskim mentioned above that immediately after the beginning of sunset (namely from the hour when the sun is hidden from our eyes) it is a complete prohibition not to perform melacha, and Heaven forbid to be lenient in this, for it is safek issur skilah per the view of all these great authorities [and especially in our days when the world is not expert in the time of bein hashemashot; and in this manner for all there is to be stringent to refrain from melacha from the beginning of sunset as is stated in the Gemara and brought in Shulchan Aruch seif 3]; and it is not specifically regarding Shabbos, for the same applies to every matter in the Torah where there is a difference between day and night; we go to be stringent that immediately at the beginning of sunset bein hashemashot begins and the time of bein hashemashot continues until the emergence of three medium-sized stars, as stated. And know that in Sefer Yere'im of Rabbeinu Eliezer MiMitz was even more stringent regarding the beginning of bein hashemashot, and he holds that bein hashemashot begins three-quarters of a mil before the beginning of sunset, and his words are brought in Agudah and in Mordechai chapter 2 of Shabbos; and see in Bach who extended on this, and his view is that one should be careful l'chatchila to practice like the view of the Yere'im, see there, and he brought that thus was the custom of the communities from ancient times; and see in Magen Avraham who also brought the words of Bach, and it is implied from him that stringently he was concerned for the words of Bach [even though in the fundamental law he holds like Magen Avraham like the view of Rabbeinu Tam as is proven below siman 331 seif 5 in what is written there regarding milah on erev Shabbos]; however the Gra rejected the view of the Ra'avan mentioned above, and see in Sefer Seder Zmanim what he wrote explicitly on the words of the Ra'avan; and nevertheless l'chatchila it is certainly good to be concerned for the words of the Ra'avan and to advance a little more in kindling the candles in order to satisfy his view as well, as I wrote in Mishna Berurah seif katan 23, and as Bach wrote that thus was the custom of the communities from ancient times:

<b> אין מערבין</b> -  היינו עירובי חצרות כמו שפירשתי במ"ב וכן מפורש במרדכי סוף פרק במה מדליקין דהדין הזה נובע ממנו [ולפלא על הפמ"ג שרוצה לומר דהכא איירי המחבר בעירובי תחומין] ועיין במרדכי שם שרבינו יואל חולק ע"ז ומתיר והמחבר סתם פה כדעת המחמירין בזה [וכן הרמ"א בסי' רנ"ג ס"ב בהג"ה סתם להחמיר] והנה בשו"ע סי' שצ"ג ס"ב הביא פלוגתא בזה ומשמע שם דהוא נוטה יותר לדעת המתירים שסוברים דמה דהתירו בין השמשות הוא אפילו אם קבל עליו שבת ג"כ וכדי שלא יסתרו אהדדי מחלקים רבים מן האחרונים [הלא המה הא"ר לחד תירוצא והביאו הפמ"ג והמח"ב שהובא בשע"ת והשלחן עצי שטים] ואומרים דשם איירי ביחיד שקבל עליו שבת והכא דקבלת שבת של ברכו שהוא ע"י צבור חמיר טפי. ועיין בט"ז בסימן תר"א דפוסק דמותר לערב אפילו אחר שקבל עליו שבת וכן בדה"ח מצדד לפסוק דכל שבות לצורך מצוה מותר אפילו אחר שקיבלו הצבור שבת ואפילו בחצי שעה שקודם חשיכה והטעם דלא חמיר הזמן הזה מבין השמשות ובודאי לא היה דעת הצבור לקבל על עצמם התוספת שיהא חמיר מבה"ש עצמו עי"ש ומ"מ התוספתא שהביאו הפוסקים ראיה ממנה להחמיר אינה מתורצת בסברתו עיין במרדכי ועיין בבאור הגר"א בסימן שצ"ג ומשמע מדבריו דהוא מצדד יותר לדעת האוסרין והביא דגם דעת הרמ"א כן בסימן רנ"ג ס"ב בהג"ה. ומ"מ נראה לענ"ד דבהצטרף עוד איזה ספק יש להורות להקל בענין זה כגון שהוא שתי שעות קודם חשכה דלכמה פוסקים הוא קודם פלג המנחה ואין בקבלתו כלום ואף דמהרי"ו נקט לעיל בהג"ה שעה או ב' אפשר דלא נקטו רק לענין מלאכה דאורייתא של הדלקה דחל קבלתו ואין מותר רק ע"י א"י אבל לענין שבות במקום מצוה יש להקל אפילו ע"י עצמו אם הוא שתי שעות קודם חשכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One may not establish an eruv.</b> This means an eruv chatzeiros, as I explained in the Mishna Berurah. It is likewise explicit in the Mordechai at the end of the chapter Bameh Madlikin, from which this ruling is derived. It is surprising that the Peri Megadim wishes to say that the Mechaber is discussing an eruv techumin here. See the Mordechai there, where Rabbeinu Yoel disagrees and permits it. The Mechaber rules here without qualification in accordance with those who are stringent, and the Rama likewise rules without qualification to be stringent in siman 253, se'if 2, in his gloss. Now, in Shulchan Aruch, siman 393, se'if 2, the Mechaber cites a dispute concerning this matter, and it appears there that he inclines more toward the permissive view. They maintain that the permission granted during bein hashmashos applies even when one has already accepted Shabbos upon himself. To avoid a contradiction between the rulings, many later authorities, namely the Eliyah Rabbah in one of his resolutions, as cited by the Peri Megadim, the Machazik Berachah cited in Shaarei Teshuvah, and the Shulchan Atzei Shittim, distinguish between the cases. They explain that there the case concerns an individual who accepted Shabbos upon himself, whereas here it concerns the communal acceptance of Shabbos through Barechu, which is more stringent. See the Taz in siman 601, who rules that one may establish an eruv even after accepting Shabbos. The Derech HaChaim likewise inclines to rule that any shevus performed for the sake of a mitzvah is permitted even after the community has accepted Shabbos, and even during the half hour before nightfall. His reason is that this period is no more stringent than bein hashmashos, and the community certainly did not intend to accept an addition to Shabbos that would be more stringent than bein hashmashos itself; see there. Nevertheless, the Tosefta from which the poskim derive proof for the stringent ruling is not resolved by his reasoning; see the Mordechai. See also the Biur HaGra in siman 393. It appears from his words that he inclines more toward the prohibitive view, and he notes that this is also the Rama's view in siman 253, se'if 2, in the gloss. Nevertheless, it appears to me that when an additional uncertainty is present, one may rule leniently in this matter. An example is where it is two hours before nightfall, which according to many poskim is before plag haminchah, so that his acceptance of Shabbos has no effect. Although Mahariv above in the gloss mentions one or two hours, it is possible that he referred only to the Torah-prohibited melacha of kindling, regarding which the acceptance takes effect and the act is permitted only through a non-Jew. Regarding a shevus performed for the sake of a mitzvah, however, one may be lenient even to perform it himself when it is two hours before nightfall.
