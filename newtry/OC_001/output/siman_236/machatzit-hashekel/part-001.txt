<b>כ' מ"א כ' בע"ב שיש להפסיק בין ברקיע כו'</b> ר"ל שיש להמשיך תיבת כרצונו אדלעיל מיניה ומסדר את הכוכבים במשמרותיהם ברקיע כרצונו. כדאמרי' סוף שבת אין מזל לישראל וכתבו התוספות דלפעמים ע"י זכות גדול משנה ה' המזל וכן מצינו שאמר הקב"ה לאברהם מאי דעתך דקאי צדק במערב אוקמי ליה במזרח. הרי דלפעמים הרצון לפניו יתברך לשדד המזלות והכוכבים ושייך שפיר שכרצונו הוא יתברך מסדרן ברקיע. אבל אי ממשיכים תיבת כרצונו אדלמטה כרצונו בורא יום ולילה לא יתכן דודאי יש ביכולתו לשנות יום לליל' או להיפך לילה ליום האו' לחרס ולא יזרח אבל כבר הבטיח הוא יתברך יום ולילה לא ישבותו כ"א על דרך המקרה כמו בחשך מצרים ואז לא היה בכל העולם כ"א במצרים וכן העמדת חמה למשה ויהושע וחזקיה היה רק ע"ד המקר'. משא"כ שידד מערכו' הכוכבים ודאי נעשה בכל דור לצדיקי הדורות והם מן הנסים הנסתרים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Magen Avraham wrote, citing Olas Tamid, that one should pause between “in the firmament,” etc.</b> Meaning, the word “as He wills” should be connected to what precedes it: “and arranges the stars in their watches in the firmament as He wills.” For we say at the end of Shabbos that Israel is not subject to mazal, and Tosafos write that at times, through great merit, Hashem changes a person's mazal. Thus we find that the Holy One, blessed is He, said to Avraham: “What is your view, that Jupiter stands in the west? I shall place it in the east.” We see that at times it is His will, blessed is He, to overturn the constellations and stars, and it is therefore fitting to say that He arranges them in the firmament as He wills. But if the words “as He wills” are connected to what follows, “as He wills, He creates day and night,” this is inappropriate. Certainly He has the power to change day into night or night into day, to command the sun not to rise, but He has already promised that “day and night shall not cease,” except as an exceptional occurrence, as with the darkness in Egypt, which was not throughout the world but only in Egypt, and likewise the stopping of the sun for Moshe, Yehoshua, and Chizkiyahu, which occurred only exceptionally. By contrast, overturning the order of the stars certainly occurs in every generation for the righteous of the generation, and these are among the hidden miracles.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2: They are accustomed, etc.; and Ma'amar Mordechai writes that one must sit, etc.</b> Those who stand reason as follows. In earlier times, synagogues were in the fields, and people feared prolonging Maariv at night because the synagogue was far from inhabited areas. They therefore instituted, in place of the Amidah, the eighteen verses beginning Baruch Hashem Le'olam, which contain eighteen mentions of the Name Havayah corresponding to the eighteen blessings, and together with them they instituted the concluding blessing Yir'u Eineinu. Even now, when our synagogues are within the city and we recite the full Amidah, the original enactment to recite these eighteen verses in place of the eighteen blessings remains in force. This is the reason Rama writes that meticulous people stand, since these verses were originally instituted in place of the Amidah itself. Those who disagree argue that for this very reason one should sit. If one stands, it appears that he intends to fulfill the Amidah through these verses; when he subsequently recites the Amidah, it then appears as though he prays the Amidah twice. Nor can it be considered one extended prayer, because Kaddish intervenes between them. So writes Taz. Eliyah Rabbah writes that even those who stand should not stand facing the wall, for then it appears exactly as though they are praying the Amidah.
