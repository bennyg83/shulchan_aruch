<b>סעיף ב'  </b>יחידים הצריכין למטר וכו' אין שואלין אותו בברכת השנים אלא בשומע תפלה וכו'. נ"ב עיין בט"ז מ"ש לראיה מלשון רבי שהשיב כיחידים דמי ובש"ת ובע"כ הכוונה דאף בש"ת הוי כיחידים ע"ש. ואני אומר אף אם נדייק כן יהי' מוכח מן הש"ס שם להיפוך שהרי סיים הש"ס מאי הוי עלה ומסיק שם בשומע תפלה ולא סיים שם הך לישנא דכיחידים דמי ובשומע תפלה ולמה שינה מלשון של רבי בע"כ כיון דמסיק הש"ס דיש פלוגתא בדבר ורבי תנא הוא ופליג ולכך כיון דיש חד דיעה דדין ציבור ממש להם ומזכירין בברכת השנים ורבי ס"ל להיפוך דאף בשומע תפלה הוי כיחידים וכמ"ש הט"ז. ולכך לדינא מכריע הש"ס כתרוייהו דלענין זה הלכתא כרבי דמזכירין בשומע תפלה אבל לענין הזכרה בקול רם הוי הדין כאידך דמזכירין בקול רם ויש להם דין ציבור ולכך השמיט לשון כיחידים דמי ואמר רק דבזה הלכתא כרבי בשומע תפלה אבל לא במ"ש כיחידים ואתי שפיר ונכון ודו"ק היטב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 2.</b> Individuals who require rain, etc., do not request it in Bircas HaShanim, but in Shome'a Tefillah, etc. Note: See the Taz, who derives proof from Rabbi's response, “They are considered like individuals, and therefore request it in Shome'a Tefillah,” and necessarily understands that even in Shome'a Tefillah they retain the status of individuals. I say that even if this inference is accepted, the Gemara there proves the opposite. The Gemara concludes by asking, “What is the practical ruling?” and rules that the request is made in Shome'a Tefillah, but it does not repeat Rabbi's phrase, “They are considered like individuals and request it in Shome'a Tefillah.” Why did it depart from Rabbi's wording? Necessarily, since the Gemara concludes that there is a dispute, and Rabbi, as a Tanna, may disagree, one opinion holds that they have the full status of a congregation and mention rain in Bircas HaShanim, whereas Rabbi holds the opposite, namely that even in Shome'a Tefillah they are treated as individuals, as the Taz explains. The Gemara therefore rules in accordance with both positions: it follows Rabbi in requiring the request to be made in Shome'a Tefillah, but regarding audible recitation it follows the other opinion, allowing the request to be said aloud because they retain the status of a congregation. It therefore omitted the words, “They are considered like individuals,” and stated only that the halachah follows Rabbi with respect to Shome'a Tefillah, but not with respect to their being considered individuals. This is correct; examine it carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 4.</b> If one did not request rain during the rainy season, he must repeat the Amidah, etc. Note: I saw that Baer Heitev cites Halachos Ketanos concerning one who continued with a weekday Amidah on Shabbos, reached Bircas HaShanim, then remembered that it was Shabbos, and during the summer mistakenly requested rain. Halachos Ketanos rules that he must return and recite Bircas HaShanim correctly, and only afterward proceed with the Shabbos prayer. I say that this ruling should be rejected. Initially, one may not pray the weekday Amidah on Shabbos. Only if he already began a weekday blessing must he complete that blessing, but no more. This applies where he already began the blessing. Here, however, because he requested rain in Bircas HaShanim, it is as though he never recited that blessing, and he would need to begin it anew. Yet if he is beginning anew, it is Shabbos and he should not begin a weekday blessing at all. This question appears to depend on the dispute in siman 108 concerning one who prayed on Shabbos without mentioning Shabbos, or on Rosh Chodesh without Ya'aleh VeYavo, whether he must later recite a compensatory prayer on a weekday. Some hold that since the later prayer would also omit the special insertion, there is no purpose in repeating it. They understand that although his prayer was defective, it is not regarded as though he did not pray at all. Others require a compensatory prayer on a weekday because they regard the original prayer as no prayer at all. Accordingly, where one requested rain during the summer, those who hold that an improperly recited prayer is treated as no prayer would likewise regard Bircas HaShanim as never having been recited. Since, had he remembered before beginning it, he would not begin a weekday blessing on Shabbos, here too he should not repeat Bircas HaShanim, but should immediately begin the Shabbos prayer. According to those who hold that a defective prayer is nevertheless considered a prayer, he has begun Bircas HaShanim but not completed it correctly, and must therefore repeat that blessing before beginning the Shabbos prayer. In practice, however, all should agree here that it is treated as though he did not pray. There, the weekday prayer is fundamentally appropriate even on Shabbos, and the defect was merely omission of the Shabbos insertion. Likewise on Rosh Chodesh, he did not actively corrupt his prayer but merely failed to make the required insertion. Here, however, he actively inserted something inappropriate, and it is certainly regarded as though he did not recite the blessing. Since he is considered not to have begun it, he need not begin it anew on Shabbos. This appears correct in practice, especially because in a case of doubt concerning a blessing we rule leniently, and because the weekday blessing is not appropriate to the day except by virtue of having begun it, which itself is doubtful. The ruling of Halachos Ketanos is therefore astonishing and should be rejected. I do not possess that work to examine his source. May Hashem enable me to obtain it and clarify the matter further. This appears correct and clear.
