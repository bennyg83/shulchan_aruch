<b>If so, even an individual should not request it at Maariv, etc.</b> Meaning, we see that were it not for the concern of forming factions, it would have been proper to begin mentioning rain at night. For this reason, with respect to the request, where this concern does not apply at the beginning, they begin at night. The same should apply to cessation: where there is no concern of factions, it is proper to cease at night. Since the congregation actually prays the Yom Tov Amidah at Maariv and does not request rain, an individual who mistakenly prays the weekday Amidah should likewise not request it. On the contrary, by not requesting rain he does not form a faction, because the congregation also did not request it. This is explicit in Taanis 4a. The Mishnah states that Rabbi Yehudah says: On the first day of Pesach, the one who leads Shacharis mentions rain, while the latter one, namely the one who leads Mussaf, no longer mentions it. On 4b the Gemara challenges this from a Baraisa in which Rabbi Yehudah states that rain is requested until Pesach has passed. The Gemara initially assumes that mention and request cease at the same time. Rav Yosef explains that “until Pesach has passed” means until the chazzan who leads Shacharis on the first day of Pesach has passed before the ark. Abaye objects: Is there a request for rain on Yom Tov? The Baraisa discusses requesting rain, yet neither at Maariv nor at Shacharis is the request made because the Yom Tov Amidah contains no such request. According to the Bach, the Gemara could have answered that the practical difference concerns an individual who mistakenly prayed the weekday Amidah through Barech Aleinu and would need to request rain. Since it did not answer this way, the Bach's ruling is necessarily incorrect. The text “as stated above, that they went out, etc.” contains a scribal error. It should read: Moreover, according to what the Beit Yosef stated above in siman 114 in the name of the Yerushalmi, Rabbi Avin said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that Rabbi Yehudah's reason is so that the festivals should conclude with dew, because dew is beneficial to the world. Meaning, even though on Yom Tov rain is not an adverse sign once they have left the sukkah, and the comparison to a servant whose master pours the cup in his face no longer applies, nevertheless a person's heart rejoices more when the sky is clear and rain does not fall. The Rosh explains that this gives the reason why we do not begin mentioning rain at Maariv of Shemini Atzeres. The earlier Yerushalmi explanation, that not everyone attends shul at Maariv, suffices only for the cessation at Mussaf on the first day of Pesach, since before Yom Tov people are occupied with preparing matzah and maror. On the night of Shemini Atzeres, however, Chol HaMoed preceded it and people are not occupied, so everyone attends shul and rain should have been mentioned at Maariv. Therefore the Yerushalmi gives the additional reason that the festivals should conclude with dew, showing that they did not wish even to mention rain. How, then, could the Bach rule that an individual who erred should request “Grant dew and rain”? This also appears from the Tur. Although an individual might otherwise have been fit to mention or request rain, because the congregation did not do so he follows the congregation. The Tur cites Raavyah that the sixty days after the tekufah must be complete twenty-four-hour periods. Therefore, if the tekufah fell three hours into the day and the congregation prays on the sixtieth day before those three hours, they do not request rain. Even if an individual prays after those three hours, when the time for the request has already arrived, since the congregation did not request it earlier, he follows the congregation and does not request it. Although we do not follow Raavyah regarding the need for complete twenty-four-hour periods, no one disputes the principle that an individual follows the congregation in such a case. The same applies here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 4, those who require, etc.</b> This requires study, etc. Meaning, the basis of this ruling is Responsa Rosh, where the Rosh expresses wonder that we do not request rain from the seventh of Marcheshvan as the residents of Eretz Yisrael do. We follow Babylonia only in disputes of law between the scholars of the West and the Babylonian Talmud, or between residents outside Eretz Yisrael and those in Eretz Yisrael. This matter, however, depends on the natural conditions of the lands. In Eretz Yisrael rain was needed immediately from the seventh of Marcheshvan, so they requested it then. Babylonia did not need rain until sixty days after the tekufah, so they waited until then. Since these lands require rain in Marcheshvan, it would have been proper for them to follow Eretz Yisrael and request it from the seventh of Marcheshvan. The Rosh writes that his view was not accepted. Based on this, the Beit Yosef writes that although initially we do not follow the Rosh, since he himself states that his view was not accepted, nevertheless an individual who mistakenly mentioned rain during the summer need not repeat the Amidah except as a voluntary prayer. Accordingly, even without a drought, an individual who mistakenly requested rain from the seventh of Marcheshvan onward should not need to repeat the Amidah. Yet the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies that this applies only when there is a drought.
