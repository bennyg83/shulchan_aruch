<b>(Seif katan 2) Not, and Tosafos implies, etc.</b> This ruling, that it is forbidden to remove them without placing them immediately upon another garment, is derived from Tosafos on Shabbos 22a. Rav and Shmuel disagree there: Rav maintains that one may not remove tzitzis from one garment for another, while Shmuel maintains that one may remove them, and the halachah follows Shmuel in this matter.

Tosafos write that according to Rav, even if he maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person rather than upon the garment, his ruling that one may not remove them is nevertheless understandable, just as we find regarding mezuzah that one who removed it was punished, in Bava Metzia 101b, even though mezuzah is an obligation upon the resident. Tosafos then write according to Shmuel, who permits removal, that although Shmuel maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment, he nevertheless permits removal because the person does so for the sake of another garment.

From this the Shulchan Aruch writes that even according to our ruling like Shmuel, removal was permitted only when one places the tzitzis upon another tallis. Admittedly, this passage alone is not conclusive, because Tosafos wrote it according to Shmuel, who maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment. According to our ruling that it is an obligation upon the person, as explained below in siman 19, one might say that removal is permitted even if he does not place them upon another tallis. Nevertheless, Tosafos conclude there according to Shmuel: “Regarding the mezuzah for whose removal he was punished, perhaps he did not intend to place it in another house.” Although mezuzah is an obligation upon the resident, they write that he was punished because he did not intend to place it in another house. The same therefore applies to tzitzis according to our ruling that it is an obligation upon the person: it is forbidden to remove them if he does not intend to place them upon another tallis.

Although Tosafos do not explicitly state Shmuel's rationale, Magen Avraham understands that the reason Tosafos give according to Rav for forbidding removal, even where he intends to place them upon another garment, applies in this respect according to Shmuel as well. In truth, Tosafos on Shabbos do not state Rav's reason either. Tosafos on Menachos 41b, however, write: “One may not nullify the mitzvah of tzitzis.” Meaning, although according to our ruling there is no obligation to attach tzitzis to a garment that is not being worn, once tzitzis have already been attached, it is improper to remove the mitzvah from the tallis.

The Gemara on Menachos 43a likewise implies this. It teaches: “One who purchases a tallis with tzitzis, etc., from a non-Jewish merchant, it is valid,” even though the Sages said that one may not sell a tallis with tzitzis to a non-Jew until he removes its tzitzis. The reason a garment purchased from a merchant is valid is that when he says he bought it in that state from a Jew, he is believed that it was properly made, because a merchant does not damage his professional reputation and certainly does not lie. The baraisa then says, “Although they said that one may not sell, etc.” One might think that his statement must therefore be false. This is not so, because the presumption that a merchant does not damage his reputation is stronger, and we assume that a Jew in fact acted improperly and sold it to him. This is also explained below in siman 20.

The baraisa says that one may not sell it until he removes its tzitzis. It does not say that he must remove the tzitzis and place them upon another tallis. This shows that because he is selling it to a non-Jew, who is not obligated in the mitzvah, it is permitted to remove them even without placing them upon another tallis. One could seemingly have brought the same proof from the permission to remove tzitzis from the garment of a deceased person, even though they are not placed upon another tallis.

<b>(Seif katan 3) It was not. Beit Yosef's words imply, etc.</b> Beit Yosef cites the baraisa in the chapter HaTecheiles: all agree that one may not bring even a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit from another tallis, with tzitzis attached, and sew it onto this tallis. Rashi and Rosh explain that the reason is the verse, “upon the corners of their garments,” and this corner was not a corner of this garment.

Beit Yosef writes: “I did not understand what our great teacher Mahari Abuhav wrote: ‘But he may not take the corner, etc. Rosh explained the reason as “you shall make,” and not something already made.’ It appears to me that since it was already made for the sake of the mitzvah, this is not so. Rosh's words are straightforward. He does not invalidate it because of something already made, but because the Torah requires ‘the corners of their garments,’ and that condition is absent.”

Mordechai writes that this applies specifically when bringing a piece from elsewhere. If the garment tore more than three fingerbreadths from the edge, it may be sewn, because it is still properly called “the corners of their garments.” Nimukei Yosef, however, explains that the reason is “you shall make,” and not something already made. He writes: “All agree, even the authority who permits sewing a tear outside three fingerbreadths, because the corner was not nullified, that here one may not bring, etc., because it is something already made.”

Magen Avraham understands that because Beit Yosef did not write, “But Nimukei Yosef writes,” or, “And Nimukei Yosef writes,” which would indicate that he cites a dissenting explanation, but instead wrote, “Nevertheless, Nimukei Yosef writes,” he refers back to Beit Yosef's objection to Mahari Abuhav. Beit Yosef had said that Mahari Abuhav understood Rosh's reason as something already made, while Rosh gives a different reason. Beit Yosef then adds that Nimukei Yosef explicitly gives the reason of something already made. Therefore, in order not to create an unnecessary dispute, one should explain that Rosh too intended something already made. Mahari Abuhav's understanding is therefore correct. Although Rosh writes that the reason is the verse, “upon the corners of their garments,” his intent is as Magen Avraham explains here.

<b>אע"פ שתפר הכנפות כו'</b> משמע דהתפיר' אינה מבטלת הכנף אע"ג דשם הטעם דסופו להתיר התפירה וכמ"ש רש"י שם דא"א דלא בעי להו ה"ל לחתכן א"ו דסופו להתירן וכ"כ הרב"י שם וגם פה. משא"כ הכא דאין דעתו להתיר התפיר' אולם אדרב' היא הנותנת וזה ראיית מ"א דבסי' יו"ד אע"ג דתפר הכנפות אפ"ה לא בטל הכנף הואיל וסופו להתירן הרי דנדון ע"ש סופו ה"ה הכא יש לידון ע"ש סופו ודעתו לתפרו כולו ויהיה בגד א' בת ד' כנפות וא"כ לא נתבטל הכנף מעולם. ודע דמ"ש הט"ז בס"ק ג' בתפר שני חלקי הטלית שנחלקו תחלה אף דמוד' למ"ש מ"א דלא כהב"ח מ"מ כ' הט"ז בתפר ב' חלקי הטלית יחד כבראשונה צריך להתיר הציצית של חלק א' ע"ש הטעם. ודבריו אין מובנים לי גם בס' חמד משה חלק עליו ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even though he sewed the corners, etc.</b> This implies that sewing does not nullify the corner, although there the reason is that he ultimately intends to undo the sewing. Rashi writes there that if he did not need the corners, he should have cut them off; since he did not, he evidently intends to undo the sewing. Beit Yosef writes the same there and here.

Our case is different, because he does not intend to undo the sewing. Yet this itself is Magen Avraham's proof. In siman 10, although he sewed the corners, the corner is not nullified because he ultimately intends to undo them. Thus the present status is judged according to the intended final result. The same applies here: we judge according to the intended final result, and he intends to sew the entire garment so that it will become one four-cornered garment. The corner was therefore never nullified.

Know that Taz, seif katan 3, discusses sewing together the two halves of a tallis that was previously divided. Although he agrees with Magen Avraham against Bach, Taz nevertheless writes that when sewing the two halves back together as originally, one must first remove the tzitzis from one half; see there for his reason. I do not understand his words. Chemed Moshe likewise disputes him; see there.

(ס"ק ה') תפירה שלא נטוה לשמה עסי' י"ד ס"ב באמת רש"י נתן טעם משום תולמ"ה אלא שהתוס' מנחות דף מ' ע"ב ד"ה שמא יקרע כו' כ' על פרש"י וז"ל ה"ל למנקט משום דבעינן או טויה או תליה לשמה אבל תולמ"ה ליכ' לפי מאי שאפרש לקמן גבי עשא' מן הקוצים עכ"ל. ולכן באמת כ' מ"א הטעם משום דבעינן טויה לשמה ואף דהתוס' כ' גם משום דבעי' תליה לשמה שביק מ"א טעם דבעי' תליה לשמה. וע"ז רמז במ"ש עסי' י"ד ס"ב ר"ל דשם הובאו ב' דעות אי בעי' תלי' לשמה לכן ניח' ליה לומר משום דבעי' טויה לשמה דבטוי' אין חולק דבעי' לשמה ואע"ג דמה שדחו התוס' פרש"י שכ' משום תולמ"ה גבי עשאן מן הקוצים בדף מ"ב ע"ב היינו משום קושייתם שם דהא מרא דמימר' דעשא' מן הקוצים פסולה הוא רב ולרב לית ליה משום תולמ"ה וז"ל התו' שם ד"ה הקוצים כו' פי' בקונט' משום תולמ"ה ול"נ דמאי דעביד אח"כ היינו העשייה דאמר רב גופיה תלאן ואח"כ פסק ראשי החוטין שלהן כשר דזו היא עשייתו כו' אלמ' טעמ' דבעינן תליה לשמה עכ"ל והיינו דוקא לרב אבל לדידן דלא קי"ל בהא כרב אדרבה קי"ל תלאן ואח"כ פסקן פסולים כדלעיל סי' י"א סעיף י"ג. מ"מ הא מבואר לעיל דוקא כשכבר כרך חוליא א' וקשר קשר א' עכ"פ ואח"כ פסק פסולים משום תולמ"ה משא"כ כאן דעדיין לא כרך חוליא ולא קשר והתו' שהקשו מרב עדיפא מקשה. וכן נראה מדברי התו' בדף מ' ע"ב ד"ה שמא יקרן כו' הנ"ל שהקשו כן אליביה דכ"ע כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 5) Sewing thread that was not spun for the sake of the mitzvah. See siman 14, seif 2.</b> Rashi indeed gives the reason of “you shall make,” and not something already made. Tosafos on Menachos 40b, s.v. “lest it tear,” object to Rashi's explanation: “He should have said that either spinning or attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah. But there is no issue of something already made, according to what we explain below concerning strings made from loose tufts.”

Magen Avraham therefore writes the reason that spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah. Although Tosafos also write that attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah, Magen Avraham omits that reason. He alludes to this by writing, “See siman 14, seif 2,” where two views are cited whether attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah. He therefore prefers the reason based upon spinning, regarding which no authority disputes that intention is required.

Tosafos rejected Rashi's explanation of something already made concerning strings made from loose tufts on Menachos 42b because the authority who states that such strings are invalid is Rav, and Rav does not accept the disqualification of something already made in this context. Tosafos write there: “Rashi explains that it is because of something already made. This is difficult, because the later act is the making itself, as Rav himself says: if one attached them and afterward cut the ends of the strings, it is valid, because that is their making. Rather, the reason is that attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah.”

This applies specifically according to Rav. According to our ruling, we do not follow Rav in this matter. On the contrary, we rule that if he attached them and afterward cut them, they are invalid, as stated above in siman 11, seif 13. Nevertheless, it is explained there that they are invalid because of something already made only if he already made at least one winding and one knot before cutting. Here, however, he has not yet made any winding or knot. Tosafos' objection from Rav is therefore the stronger objection. The words of Tosafos on 40b, s.v. “lest it tear,” likewise show that they raised the objection according to all opinions, as explained.
