(א) וליתנם וכו' - אפילו מטלית חדש לטלית חדש וכ"ש מטלית ישן שמותר להתיר וליתנם בטלית חדש שרוצה ללבשו ולקיים בו המצוה. גם אפילו מטלית גדול לט"ק שט"ג וטלית קטן שוים אבל אין להתיר ציצית מטלית של גדול וליתנם בטלית של קטן דחיובו רק מדרבנן ובפמ"ג נסתפק אם מותר ציצית מטלית של צמר ליתנם בטלית של שאר מינים להפוסקים לעיל בסי' ט' ס"א דשאר מינים הוא רק מדרבנן והארצות החיים מיקל בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(1) And place them, etc.</b> This is permitted even from one new tallis to another new tallis, and certainly from an old tallis to a new tallis that he wishes to wear and use for the mitzvah. It is likewise permitted to transfer them from a tallis gadol to a tallis katan, because a tallis gadol and tallis katan have equal status. One may not, however, remove tzitzis from an adult's tallis and place them upon a minor's tallis, whose obligation is only D'Rabbanan. Peri Megadim remains uncertain whether one may transfer tzitzis from a wool tallis to a garment of other materials according to the authorities cited above in siman 9, seif 1, who maintain that other materials are obligated only D'Rabbanan. Artzos HaChayim is lenient.

(ג) בבגד אחר לא - כי יש איסור שמבזה טלית של מצוה בחנם אם לא שנתבלה הטלית אז מותר להתיר ציציותיו בכל גווני או שרוצה למכרו לנכרי או לעשות מהטלית בגד שלא יהיה ד' כנפות מותר ליטלן אבל אם רוצה למכור הטלית לישראל אסור ליקח ציציותיו אף שישראל השני יוכל להטיל ציצית בעצמו אפ"ה אסור. ומותר להסיר הציצית ולתת תחתיהם יותר נאים משום הידור מצוה או כשהם ישנים ומסירם כדי לחדשם או כשנפסק חוט א' ולא נשאר בו אלא כדי עניבה אע"פ שעדיין הציצית כשר אעפ"כ מותר להסיר ולתת תחתיה שלמה. ודע עוד דבמקום שהציצית הם שלמים וראויים להנתן לבגד אחר [לאפוקי אם הם רק כדי עניבה] יש מן האחרונים שכתבו דיזהר להתיר הקשרים והכריכות שלהם ולא להפסיקן ולקרוע אותן כדי שלא יכלה אותן וכבר אחז"ל לא ישפוך אדם מי בורו ואחרים צריכין להם והחיי אדם כתב במקום שקשה בעיניו הטרחה להתיר מותר לנתקם ואין בזה משום בל תשחית כיון שאין עושה דרך השחתה עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(3) Not upon another garment.</b> This is forbidden because he needlessly degrades a tallis used for a mitzvah. If the tallis became worn out, he may remove its tzitzis in every case. The same applies if he wishes to sell it to a non-Jew, or convert it into a garment that will not have four corners. If he wishes to sell the tallis to a Jew, however, he may not remove its tzitzis, even though the purchaser could attach tzitzis himself.

It is permitted to remove tzitzis and replace them with more beautiful ones because of beautifying the mitzvah, or to replace old strings with new ones. Likewise, if one string was severed and only enough remained to tie a bow, although the tzitzis are still valid, he may remove them and replace them with complete strings.

Where the strings are complete and fit to be used upon another garment, unlike strings that retain only enough to tie a bow, some later authorities write that one should carefully untie their knots and windings rather than cut or tear them, so that they are not destroyed. The Sages said that one should not spill out water from his cistern when others need it. Chayei Adam writes that where untying them is excessively burdensome, one may cut them. This does not violate bal tashchis because he is not acting destructively.

One should be stringent. Derech HaChayim likewise rules this way.

<b>(18) From the hole.</b> This concerns a case where he did not sew the tear. The ruling teaches that although less than the distance of a thumb joint remained, it is valid, as explained above in siman 11, seif 10, because that measurement is required only at the time the tzitzis are made.

If he wishes to sew the tear and the garment is wool, he should sew it with thread of other materials. He need not remove the tzitzis from the corner and reattach them after sewing, because the corner never became invalid. If the garment is made of other materials, according to Rashi he may not sew it with thread of the same material as the garment, lest some thread remain and be used for tzitzis. This concern applies even though tzitzis are already present, because the Sages did not distinguish. He should use thread of another material. It is preferable not to sew it with white thread, but rather with colored thread, because nowadays the custom is to use only white tzitzis, so there is no concern that colored sewing thread will be used for tzitzis.

<b>(21) If it tore, etc.</b> Meaning, it tore completely from the hole to the edge. There is therefore room for doubt. Even if the garment is wool and he sews it with thread of other materials, which is certainly permitted according to Rashi, perhaps it is nevertheless invalid according to Rav Amram. Since no connection at all remained, the preceding seif explains that according to the first opinion it is treated as completely severed, and sewing may not help even if he attaches tzitzis afterward.

Perhaps this case is better, because the corner remains whole on both sides and only the middle was split. This is the doubt of Shulchan Aruch. Taz disagrees and rules that there is no doubt: all agree that if he attaches tzitzis after sewing, it is valid.

Nevertheless, it is preferable before sewing to place beneath the corner a patch measuring three by three fingerbreadths, thereby satisfying all opinions. If this is impossible, one may be lenient in accordance with Taz. In any event, we wrote in seif katan 15 that the later authorities maintain that sewing creates a connection and helps even when the corner was entirely torn from its place.
