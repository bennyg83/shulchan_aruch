<b>(סק"א) משתחשך כו'</b> אפי' ספק חשיכה בפסחים דף ק"ה ע"א אמרי' משמיה דרב שבת קבעה לקידוש. (ר"ל כשעומד תוך אכילתו וקידש היום צריך להפסיק) אבל להבדלה אינה קובעת ואמרי' ה"מ לאפסוקי דאין צריך להפסיק אכילתו ולהבדיל אבל להתחיל אסור והא דא"צ להפסיק היינו כשאוכל אבל כשלא קבע לאכילה כ"א לשתיה צריך להפסיק וס"ל להרי"ף הא דאמרי' דא"צ להפסיק דוקא בספק חשכה אבל ודאי חשכה צריך להפסיק גם מאכילה והיא דעת י"א שהובא פה בש"ע וכ' על זה הרא"ש וז"ל ואין אלו דברים של טעם ורחוק מן הדעת לו' דשקלי' וטרי' דהש"ס כו' איירי בספק חשיכה דוקא עכ"ל הרא"ש הרי דעת הרא"ש דאפי' ודאי חשכה א"צ להפסיק מאכיל' והוא דעה א' פה בש"ע וס"ל להט"ז דהרי"ף ורא"ש פליגי בתרתי חדא לענין הפסקה דלהרי"ף בודאי חשיכה צריך להפסיק ולהרא"ש א"צ להפסיק. שני' כיון דלהרי"ף מיירי הש"ס בספק חשיכה א"כ הא דאמר הש"ס וה"מ לאפסוקי אבל להתחיל אסור היינו אפי' בספק חשיכה אסור להתחיל משא"כ להרא"ש דהש"ס מיירי בודאי חשכה אבל ספק חשכה אפי' להתחיל מותר אבל אין כן דעת מ"א דלענין התחלה מודה הרא"ש דאפי' בספק חשכה אסור דאלת"ה אמאי לא הזכיר הרב"י בש"ע מחלוקת הרי"ף ורא"ש כ"א לענין הפסקה ה"ל להזכיר גם כן מחלקותם לענין התחלה א"ו ס"ל להרב ב"י דהרא"ש מודה דאסור להתחיל אפי' בספק חשכה וראיית הרב"י הוא מדברי הרא"ש שכתב ע"ד הרי"ף ואין דברים של טעם כו' דשקלי' וטרי' כו' בספק חשכה דוקא אם כן ה"ה לשטת הרא"ש דוחק לומר דהש"ס מיירי בודאי חשכה דוקא א"ו ס"ל להרא"ש דהש"ס מיירי בכל גווני בין בספק ובין בודאי חשכה ולהפסיק אפי' ודאי חשכה א"צ ולהתחיל אפי' ספק חשכה אסור וכן דעת הב"ח וא"כ עכצ"ל מ"ש בש"ע משתחשך הוא ל"ד אלא ה"ה ספק חשכה וכבר כתבתי כ"ז לעיל סי' רע"א ס"ק י':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) From nightfall, etc.</b> Even doubtful darkness is included. In Pesachim 105a they say in Rav's name that Shabbos establishes the meal with respect to kiddush, meaning that if one is in the middle of eating and the day became sanctified, he must interrupt. But with respect to havdalah it does not establish the meal. When the Gemara says this applies only to interrupting, meaning that he need not interrupt his meal in order to recite havdalah, but it is forbidden to begin, that applies when he is eating. If he had not established himself for eating, but only for drinking, he must interrupt. The Rif holds that the Gemara's rule that he need not interrupt applies specifically during doubtful darkness, but once it is certainly dark he must interrupt even from eating. This is the view cited here in the Shulchan Aruch as 'some say.' The Rosh objected that these words have no reason, and it is far from the mind to say that the give and take of the Talmud concerns only doubtful darkness. Thus according to the Rosh, even once it is certainly dark he need not interrupt eating, and this is the first opinion here in the Shulchan Aruch. The Taz understands that the Rif and the Rosh disagree in two matters: first, whether he must interrupt once it is certainly dark; second, whether, according to the Rosh, during doubtful darkness it is permitted even to begin. But this is not the Magen Avraham's view. He holds that regarding beginning, the Rosh agrees that even during doubtful darkness it is forbidden. Otherwise, the Beit Yosef should have mentioned their dispute also about beginning, not only about interruption. Rather, the Rosh holds that the Talmud speaks in all cases, whether doubtful or certain darkness: even in certain darkness one need not interrupt eating, but even in doubtful darkness one may not begin. This is also the Bach's view. Therefore, what the Shulchan Aruch writes, 'from nightfall,' is not precise, and the same applies during doubtful darkness. I already wrote all this above in siman 271, seif katan 10.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It implies in Tosafot, Shabbos 9, etc.</b> There the Gemara discusses Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who says that one may not taste anything before Minchah, and infers that even if he began eating he must interrupt. Tosafot write that this refers only to one who began in prohibition, but if he began permissibly he need not interrupt, for regarding havdalah too the language is that one may not taste before havdalah, and nevertheless Pesachim 105 teaches that if he began permissibly he need not interrupt eating. This is the law mentioned here in the Shulchan Aruch. From this it is proven that if he began in prohibition, then regarding havdalah too he must interrupt, since the same language is used. Although with prayer we rule that even if he began in prohibition he does not interrupt, that is because we do not rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. But regarding havdalah, where we do rule that it is forbidden to taste before havdalah, the rule remains that if he began in prohibition he interrupts. The reason is that prayer is D'Rabbanan, while havdalah is according to the Torah, as the Magen Avraham writes afterward. So too it appears from siman 271, seif 5 in the gloss, where one who forgot havdalah until after HaMotzi should eat first. Since he did not write that he should finish the meal first, it implies that he should eat only the slice of HaMotzi so his blessing not be in vain, and then interrupt and recite havdalah, as the Magen Avraham wrote there.

<b>(Seif katan 3) And some say this applies only, etc.</b> And so it appears in the Gemara, etc., unlike the Olas Shabbos, etc. The wording is awkward, and I do not know how to correct it. But his intent is to challenge the Olas Shabbos, who wrote in the name of Shiltei Gibborim that during doubtful darkness it is permitted to drink, and drinking is forbidden only once it is certainly dark. The Magen Avraham disagrees, for the Gemara proves the opposite. As he wrote in seif katan 1, even according to the first opinion, the Rosh's view, that the Talmud applies even to certain darkness and nevertheless one need not interrupt eating, the Rosh must agree that the Talmud also speaks of doubtful darkness, for it speaks in all cases. Therefore, when it says that for drinking one interrupts, this means even during doubtful darkness, and certainly one may not begin drinking then. This is all the more true according to the 'some say,' the Rif's view, that the entire sugya speaks specifically of doubtful darkness.

Although the Magen Avraham above in siman 272 disagrees with the Rama, his main reason there is concern for the Geonim in siman 271, seif 14, that if the one making kiddush did not taste, he did not fulfill the obligation. That applies specifically to kiddush, not havdalah, for there in siman 271 it is explained that with other matters requiring a cup, the Geonim agree that another person's tasting suffices. However, according to the Taz in siman 271, seif katan 18, the distinction is that kiddush has an asmachta according to the Torah to sanctify over wine, and havdalah also has an asmachta. According to the Levush there, however, it works somewhat, for he says kiddush is according to the Torah and supported by a verse; one may then rely on the view that havdalah is D'Rabbanan.

<b>For even the Rosh, etc.</b> This is the second 'some say' in siman 296, seif 2. Although there we rule that the primary view is the first opinion, that he recites both kiddush and havdalah over bread, that is specifically when Motzaei Shabbos is Yom Tov, where he says Yaknehaz and the matters are one, so once he is reciting kiddush over it he also recites havdalah over it. <b>ועמ"ש סי' קס"ז.</b> ר"ל על מ"ש מ"א ואח"כ יברך המוציא ויאכל אע"ג שכבר יצא בברכת המוציא של המקדש על הפת ואע"ג שהפסיק בין ברכת המוציא לטעימה בהבדלה מ"מ הא כתב רמ"א בסי' קס"ז דאם טעם הבוצע אע"ג שאחרים השומעים הפסיקו בדיבור בין שמיעת ברכת המוציא לטעימתם אין בכך כלום ולא הוי הפסק. אולם המ"א שם (וכן הט"ז) חלקו עליו דהא השומעים אין יוצאים כ"א מטעם שומע כעונה ולא יהיה השומע עדיף מהעונה ואלו השומע היה מברך בעצמו והפסיק בדיבור הוי הפסק וכשיצא ע"י שמיעה לא יהיה הדיבור הפסק ולכן לפי שיטות מ"א דלעיל ה"ה הכא כיון דהפסיק השומע בהבדלה לכן צריך לחזור ולברך המוציא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And see what I wrote in siman 167.</b> This refers to the Magen Avraham's statement that afterward he should bless HaMotzi and eat. Although he already fulfilled the blessing of HaMotzi through the one who recited kiddush over bread, and although he interrupted between hearing HaMotzi and tasting by reciting havdalah, the Rama wrote in siman 167 that if the one who broke bread tasted, then even if the listeners interrupted by speaking between hearing HaMotzi and their own tasting, it is nothing and is not an interruption. However, the Magen Avraham there, and likewise the Taz, disagree, since the listeners fulfill only through hearing as though answering, and the listener cannot be better than the one who recites. If the listener had blessed himself and interrupted with speech, it would be an interruption; so too when he fulfills by hearing, speech should interrupt. Therefore, according to the Magen Avraham's approach above, here too, since the listener interrupted with havdalah, he must repeat HaMotzi.
