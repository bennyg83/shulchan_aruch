<b>א"צ להפסיק</b>. ואפשר דאם התפלל תוך הסעודה חל עליו חובת הבדלה ואסור לאכול עד שיבדיל עסי' רע"א ס"ד וכתב הבחיי בס' שלחן ארבע וז"ל שאם היה מפסיק נראה כמגרש המלך ודומה לזה דרשו במכילת' זכור ושמו' שמריהו ביציאתו כאדם שאין רוצה שילך אוהבו מאצלו כל זמן שיכול ובהגהת י"נ כתב שמהרש"א הפסיק בסעודת נשואין והלך לבה"כ ואח"כ חזר לסעודה ואפשר דס"ל כדעת הי"א ומשמע בתו' בשבת דף ט' דאם התחיל באיסור מפסיק וכ"מ סי' ער"א ס"ה בהג"ה וצ"ל דס"ל דהבדלה דאורייתא ולמ"ד הבדלה דרבנן אם נטל ידיו מבדיל קודם אכילה כיון דכוליה ליליא זמניה הוא חיישי' דלמא מפשע וכמ"ש סי' רל"ה ס"ב אבל אם התחיל לאכול אינו פוסק ע"ש ואם לא קרא קריאת שמע עדיין והתחיל באיסור לכ"ע פוסק ואין לסמוך על קריאה לבית הכנסת אלא מי שרגיל לילך במ"ש לבה"כ ומשום הבדלה אין איסור אלא כשיגיע זמנה דהיינו ספק חשיכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He need not interrupt.</b> It is possible that if he prayed during the meal, the obligation of Havdalah has taken effect upon him and it is forbidden to eat until he recites Havdalah; see siman 271, seif 4. Bachya wrote in Sefer Shulchan Arba that if he were to interrupt, he would appear as one who drives away the King. Similar to this they expounded in the Mechilta on Zachor and Shamor, that one should guard it at its departure like a person who does not want his beloved to leave him as long as he can. In Hagahos Yesh Nochalin it is written that the Maharsha interrupted a wedding meal, went to the synagogue, and afterward returned to the meal. Perhaps he held like the opinion of the “some say.” It implies in Tosafot in Shabbos 9 that if one began in a prohibited manner, he interrupts, and so it appears in siman 271, seif 5, in the Rama. One must say that he holds that Havdalah is according to the Torah. According to the one who says Havdalah is D’Rabbanan, if he washed his hands, he recites Havdalah before eating, since its time is the entire night and we are concerned lest he be negligent, as written in siman 235, seif 2. But if he began eating, he does not interrupt; see there. If he has not yet recited Krias Shema and began in a prohibited manner, according to all opinions he interrupts. One may not rely on the call to synagogue except someone who is accustomed to go on Motzaei Shabbos to the synagogue. Because of Havdalah, the prohibition applies only when its time arrives, namely doubtful darkness.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there are those who say that he must.</b> This is the view of Maharik, shoresh 96. It requires study, for in siman 271 he ruled plainly that regarding kiddush one need not recite another blessing. The Bach wrote that there it is different, since they had not yet recited Birkas HaMazon, and the blessing he initially recited over the wine exempts all the wine. But Maharik speaks where they were reclining only for drinking, and therefore once he must interrupt for kiddush or Havdalah, it is a conclusion to what he drank, and he must bless Borei peri hagafen. This requires study, for if so, why did Maharik write that according to the Rif and SemaG one interrupts at definite darkness? For regarding drinking, according to all opinions one interrupts even at doubtful darkness. Furthermore, he wrote there explicitly that he was in the middle of a meal; see there, for it is explicit that he is speaking of eating. What the Beit Yosef wrote, that they were reclining for drinking, means the drinking after the meal before Birkas HaMazon. As for the halacha, the Bach ruled that even if they were occupied with drinking, one need not bless again. It seems to me according to its plain sense that he holds the cup of Havdalah is worse, because it is not connected to the meal, as written in seif 8 and siman 174, seif 4, and therefore he must bless. According to him, when one recites Havdalah over beer in the middle of the meal, he must also bless, for the blessing of HaMotzi does not exempt it, just as Birkas HaMazon does not exempt it from the after-blessing, as written in seif 8. Therefore one should be careful not to recite Havdalah in the middle of the meal. There is also a dispute whether he afterward blesses HaMotzi again, as written in siman 271, seif 4.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>While it is still day.</b> We do not say that since he recited Havdalah he accepted the fast upon himself, for we say this only regarding an obligatory fast, such as Yom Kippur or Tishah B’Av, and so it is explicit in the Gemara. The Olas Shabbos did not know this and objected to the Levush improperly; these are exactly the words of the Terumas HaDeshen. It speaks of one who vowed to fast every Sunday for three days and three nights, or every Erev Rosh Chodesh, and the like. See what I wrote in siman 556. But here, with vows, we follow the language of people, and the fast begins only at night, as stated there in Terumas HaDeshen. It implies that he prays Maariv before reciting Havdalah, and nevertheless he may eat afterward. The same applies on a weekday: one may eat after Maariv while it is still day. In any case, he prays Maariv before Havdalah, and so it appears from what is written at the end of siman 293, that because of Havdalah he also prays Maariv while it is still day. See siman 293, seif 3, and see the Gemara there, where prayer is obvious and Havdalah is the question. If so, wherever it is permitted to recite Havdalah, all the more so it is permitted to pray. The Shelah, laws of Rosh Hashanah, wrote that one who fasts two days and two nights before Rosh Hashanah recites Havdalah on the night after the second day. If Rosh Hashanah falls on Tuesday, he should not wait until Tuesday night, for then he would need to recite Yaknehaz, and it would be a strange matter. It seems to me that if it happened that he waited, he should not say kiddush and Havdalah over one cup, for we say in Pesachim 102 that one does not recite two sanctifications over one cup, but rather sanctification and Havdalah, because they are one matter. Rashi explained that both are because of the sanctity of Yom Tov, and in Havdalah itself he mentions the sanctity of Yom Tov, for he blesses “Who distinguishes between holy and holy.” So too wrote the Rashbam. But Havdalah from weekday has no connection. Even more than this, Tosafot wrote, s.v. and let us say both over one cup, etc., that even according to the questioner who holds that one may say two sanctifications over one cup, nevertheless if Birkas HaMazon relates to a weekday meal and kiddush relates to Shabbos, one cannot say both over one cup. One cannot say that here too he should say “between holy and holy,” for that would be false. Therefore it seems to me that he should recite kiddush over one cup and afterward recite Havdalah over one cup and drink, as is done at a wedding meal. If he has no wine, he recites kiddush over bread and eats HaMotzi, and afterward recites Havdalah over beer, since there are those who say that it is permitted to eat before Havdalah, as written in siman 296, seif 3. Although Tosafot and the Rosh in Arvei Pesachim wrote that it is astonishing how they can say half of Yaknehaz over bread and half over beer, that refers to Motzaei Shabbos that is Yom Tov, where both are one matter, as written above. But here they agree, for this has no connection to that. However, there is a dispute whether he should bless over beer, as written in seif 3, and there are also those who say that if he tasted, he does not recite Havdalah. Therefore it is better to make the kiddush also over one cup of beer, as written above regarding wine, and as written in siman 296, seif 3. For even the Rosh, who holds that one does not recite kiddush over beer, as written in siman 272, seif 9, nevertheless holds that on Motzaei Shabbos that is Yom Tov it is better to say both over beer. Alternatively, he should hear kiddush from others and not taste HaMotzi until after he recites Havdalah over beer and drinks, because it is forbidden to eat before Havdalah. Afterward he blesses HaMotzi and eats, for he has fulfilled the obligation of kiddush even though he has not yet tasted. See what I wrote in siman 167, seif 16, in the Rama, and what is written in siman 271, seif 6.

<b>שצריך לברך</b>. משמע ברא"ש פ' ע"פ דאם היה לו יין בתוך המזון אף על פי שבירך בלא כוס מכל מקום אין צריך לברך ברכה אחרונ' על כוס של הבדלה דב"ה פוטרתו דכולה שתיה א' היא ואף על גב דבסי' קע"ד ס"ו כ' דאפילו לא היה לו יין תוך המזון אין צריך ברכה שאני  הכא שאין היין בא לצורך אכילה אלא לצורך מצוה, ול"ד לקידוש בססי' ער"ב דקידוש צורך סעודה דאין הקידוש אלא במקום סעודה כנ"ל לדעת הטור ועמ"ש סי' קע"ד לענין שאר משקין אבל קשה דהא הרא"ש כתב גבי ארבע כוסות דאי איתא שצריך ברכ' אחרונה כשאין לו יין תוך המזון א"כ אפי' אית ליה יין לא יפטור ליה ע"ש וא"כ ה"נ כן ודוחק לומר כיון דמצוה הוא שאני:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he must bless.</b> It implies in the Rosh, chapter Arvei Pesachim, that if he had wine during the meal, even though he recited Birkas HaMazon without a cup, nevertheless he need not recite an after-blessing over the cup of Havdalah, for Birkas HaMazon exempts it, since it is all one drinking. Although in siman 174, seif 6, it is written that even if he did not have wine during the meal, he need not recite an after-blessing, here is different, because the wine does not come for the need of eating but for the need of a mitzvah. This is not comparable to kiddush at the end of siman 272, for kiddush is for the need of the meal, since kiddush is only in the place of a meal. So it seems in the view of the Tur. See what I wrote in siman 174 regarding other beverages. But it is difficult, for the Rosh wrote regarding the four cups that if it is so that an after-blessing is required when he has no wine during the meal, then even if he has wine, it should not exempt him; see there. If so, the same should apply here. It is forced to say that since it is a mitzvah, it is different.

<b>הדלקת הנר</b>. ז"ל הד"מ ססי' רצ"ד כתוב בא"ז צריכים ליזהר שלא לעשות מלאכה עד אחר סדר קדושה אף על פי שכבר הבדילו מיהו חזן הכנסת מדליק הנרות בבית הכנסת כשהבדיל בתפלה אפילו קודם סדר קדושה ובזוהר בראשית כ' עונש גדול שלא להדליק עד אחר שהבדיל על הכוס ולא ראיתי מי שחושש לזה אלא המנהג פשוט להדליק אחר שהבדיל בתפלה וכ"ה סימן תרפ"א עכ"ל ובאמת בזוהר במדבר ע' ת"ן כתב גם כן שלא להדליק עד אחר סדר קדושה ומ"מ צ"ע מ"ש רמ"א פה דהמנהג להקל להדליק אחר ברכו וזה דלא כמ"ש בד"מ וצ"ל דמ"ש פה מיירי בנשים כיון שאין מבדילו' בתפלה וגם אין בקיאות לומר המבדיל נוהגין להדליק אחר ברכו אבל אנשים אסורים עד אחר שהבדילו בתפלה וכן המנהג פשוט וכ"מ ססי' רס"ג והרב"י חלק על דברי רי"ו ותמה עליו מנין לו זה וגם בעיני יפלא ואפשר לפרש דברי רי"ו דקאי אמ"ש לעיל מניה בשם הרמב"ם דס"ל דאע"ג דהבדיל בתפלה אסור במלאכה עד שיבדיל על הכוס וע"ז קאמר דוקא במלאכ' גמורה וכו' אבל הדלקת הנר כו' שרי דהא מותר להדליק נר להבדלה וכ"מ בשבת דף ק"ן ברש"י ור"ן שכתבו ואף על גב דלא אבדיל אסיפ'  פריך דשרי לקצוץ מן המחובר וכו' משמע דעל יציאתו חוץ לתחום או מה שמעביר ד"א בר"ה ומוציא מרשות לרשות לא קפדינן והיינו כיון שהבדיל בתפלה אבל כשלא הבדיל בתפלה כולי עלמא מודו שאסור ולכן יש לדרוש ברבים שילמדו בנותיהם לומר המבדיל ומכ"ש אותן שמבעירין עצים ואש ומחממין מים דהוי מלאכות גמורות ובעל נפש יעשה כמש"ל בשם ד"מ והזוהר שלא להדליק עד אחר סדר קדושה אבל טלטול הנר פשיטא דשרי אחר שהבדיל בתפלה ועסי' תרכ"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Lighting the lamp.</b> This is the language of the Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 294: It is written in Or Zarua that one must be careful not to perform labor until after Seder Kedushah, even though he has already recited Havdalah. However, the synagogue attendant lights the lamps in the synagogue once he has recited Havdalah in prayer, even before Seder Kedushah. In the Zohar, Bereishis, it is written that there is a great punishment for not lighting until after he recites Havdalah over the cup. I have not seen anyone concerned for this; rather, the simple custom is to light after one recited Havdalah in prayer, and so it is in siman 681. These are his words. In truth, in Zohar Bamidbar, page 450, it is also written not to light until after Seder Kedushah. Nevertheless, what the Rama wrote here requires study, that the custom is to be lenient and light after Barchu, for this is unlike what he wrote in Darkei Moshe. One must say that what he wrote here speaks of women, since they do not recite Havdalah in prayer and are also not expert in saying Hamavdil, so they practice lighting after Barchu. But men are forbidden until after they have recited Havdalah in prayer, and so is the simple custom. So it also appears at the end of siman 263. The Beit Yosef disagreed with the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham and wondered at him, from where did he derive this? It is also wondrous in my eyes. Perhaps the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham can be explained as referring to what he wrote above in the name of the Rambam, who holds that although one recited Havdalah in prayer, he is forbidden to perform labor until he recites Havdalah over the cup. On this he says that this applies only to complete labor, etc., but lighting a lamp, etc., is permitted, for it is permitted to light a lamp for Havdalah. So it also appears in Shabbos 150 in Rashi and Ran, who wrote that although he has not recited Havdalah, the question is from the latter clause, which permits cutting from what is attached, etc. This implies that they are not particular about his leaving the techum, or about carrying four cubits in the public domain and taking out from one domain to another. This is because he recited Havdalah in prayer. But when he did not recite Havdalah in prayer, all agree that it is forbidden. Therefore one must teach publicly that they should teach their daughters to say Hamavdil, and all the more so those who kindle wood and fire and heat water, which are complete labors. A spiritually sensitive person should act as I wrote above in the name of Darkei Moshe and the Zohar, not to light until after Seder Kedushah. But moving the lamp is obviously permitted after he recited Havdalah in prayer. See siman 623.
