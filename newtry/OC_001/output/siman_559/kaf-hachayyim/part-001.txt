<b>א) [סעיף א'] אין אומרים צדקתך וכו'  </b>מידי דהוה אר"ח שחל להיות באחד בשבת שאין אומרים צו"ץ בשבת דט"ב נמי איקרי מועד. טור וב"י. ונקרא מועד משום דכתיב תקרא כיום מועד דפסוק קרא עלי מועד דרשו אותו רז"ל על ר"ח כמ"ש בשבועות דף יו"ד ע"א ופסחים ע"ז ע"א ותענית כ"ט ע"א יעו"ש. כר"ש. ומיהו עיין מט"י ססי' תקנ"ב וח"א כלל קל"ה או' כ"א שהביאו ראיה מפסוק קרא עלי מועד יעו"ש. והוא מדברי התשב"ץ שהביא ב"י ססי' זה יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] Tzidkascha is not recited, etc.  </b>This is like Rosh Chodesh that falls on Sunday, when Tzidkasecha Tzedek is not recited on Shabbos, for Tisha B’Av is also called a festival. Tur and Beit Yosef. It is called a festival because it is written, “You have called as on a festival day.” The verse, “He proclaimed a festival against me,” was expounded by our Sages regarding Rosh Chodesh, as stated in Shevuos 10a, Pesachim 77a, and Taanis 29a, see there. Kerem Shlomo. However, see Mateh Yehudah at the end of siman 552 and Chayei Adam, klal 135, entry 21, who brought a proof from the verse, “He proclaimed a festival against me,” see there. This is from the words of the Tashbetz cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, see there.

<b>3) Ibid.  </b>Tzidkascha is not recited, etc., and all the more so Tzidduk HaDin is not recited if a death occurred on Tisha B’Av. So too wrote the Rama at the end of this siman in the gloss. Taz, s.k. 1. Maharshal wrote in responsum 32 that Tzidduk HaDin is recited on Tisha B’Av even though Tachanun is not recited, see there. The Taz cited his words there. However, Mateh Moshe, siman 734, wrote regarding the aforementioned words of the Maharshal that the custom not to recite it has already become widespread, see there. Eliyah Rabbah, entry 14, cited his words. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah, siman 554, entry 18, that the custom is not to recite it, and so too wrote Chayei Adam there and Derech HaChaim, entry 9. <b>5) Ibid., </b>in the gloss. The same is done during the reading of Eichah. One should pause briefly between every verse, and somewhat longer between each chapter of Eichah. The final verse of each chapter is recited aloud. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Before beginning Eichah, one recites the blessing Al Mikra Megillah. Derashos Maharil there; Levush; Magen Avraham there; Ateres Zekeinim; Chayei Adam, klal 131, entry 23. Even when it is printed and written on paper, the blessing is recited. Levush; Machatzis HaShekel. Chayei Adam there wrote that the blessing is recited only when it is written on parchment and rolled in accordance with the law. Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman wrote that even if it is written on parchment, one should not recite the blessing, see there. Nevertheless, see above siman 490, entry 81, where we cited the dispute concerning this. Although we rule that where there is a dispute regarding blessings one should not recite a blessing because of the principle of uncertainty regarding blessings, as stated above in siman 18, entry 7, nevertheless, where the custom to recite the blessing has already been established, we do not take this principle into account, as stated above in siman 17, entry 2, see there. Therefore, each place should follow its custom. Where there is no established custom, one should not recite the blessing because of the principle of uncertainty regarding blessings.

<b>9) [Seif 2] On the night of Tisha B’Av, one prays Maariv and recites Kaddish with Tiskabel after the Maariv Amidah.  </b>Tur and Beit Yosef. So too wrote the Avudraham, 95a; Levush. Some say that only half-Kaddish should be recited, meaning until “da’amiran b’alma.” Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer. However, the view of the later authorities follows the Tur, that Tiskabel is recited after completing the eighteen blessings of the Maariv Amidah. Taz, s.k. 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Mateh Yehudah; Eshel Avraham, entry 1; Chayei Adam, klal 135, entry 19; Derech HaChaim, entry 2; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 124, entry 1; Mishna Berurah, entry 4. But after Eichah, until Minchah the next day, Kaddish is recited without Tiskabel. Levush, seif 2; Taz there; Mateh Yehudah; Eshel Avraham there. So too is stated below in seif 4 in the gloss, see there. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the reason is that Eichah already states, “You have shut out my prayer.” So too wrote the Levush there. There are places that omit Tiskabel only at night after the Amidah, but recite it at Shacharis, since they have already finished and concluded the kinnot and recite several verses of consolation at their end. Beit Yosef in the name of the aforementioned authority; Levush there. So too, the Rokeach, siman 311, wrote that some require Kaddish with Tiskabel to be recited during the day after Kedushah D’Sidra. He wrote that their reason is that the primary mourning applies only at night and no longer, and on the next day, part of the day is considered like the whole day, see there. So too is stated in the Avudraham, 95b. This is also the custom of Beit El, may it be rebuilt speedily in our days, in our holy city Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established, whose members follow the words of the Arizal, to recite Tiskabel at Shacharis on Tisha B’Av as on other days.
