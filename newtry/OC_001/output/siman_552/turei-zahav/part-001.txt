<b>In the Tur</b> he brought the view of Ravyah, that what the Gemara said on erev Tisha B'Av that one should not eat meat that is like shelamim, the reason is that regarding shelamim "joy" is written, and since the time of shelamim has already passed for them, there is no joy in it; and we also say regarding a stubborn and rebellious son that he is liable only for meat that is like shelamim, in which there is joy; and it continues after that: And Rava said, if he ate poultry he does not become a stubborn and rebellious son, etc. And we recite in the first chapter of Chagigah: Yisrael pour a hin of oil for vows, etc., but not for birds, because there is no joy in them. From all these it is implied that likewise at the seudah hamafseket it is permitted. And the Tur wrote on this: It appears that even though there is no joy in it, it is forbidden; for it is not for joy alone that they forbade meat, but in order to increase mourning. Know this, for behold for a mourner afterward there is a mitzvah to give him wine to drink, for we say wine was created only to comfort mourners, for there is a mitzvah to comfort him so that he forget his sorrow; and here they forbade wine, for if he drinks he will forget, and it is a mitzvah that he remember the destruction of the Temple and grieve over it; therefore for this reason even poultry is forbidden, like meat, end quote. And that they permitted salted meat that is more than the measure of shelamim, the reason is that its taste is nullified and there is no good food in it. And at first glance one may say regarding what Ravyah wrote, that a stubborn and rebellious son is exempt if he eats meat that is not like shelamim, that is after two days and one night; for in the chapter of the stubborn and rebellious son we say: Rabbah and Rav Yosef both say, if he ate salted meat he does not become a stubborn and rebellious son. We learned there: On erev Tisha B'Av one should not eat meat, etc., but he may eat salted meat; until when? Said R' Chanina bar Kahana: As long as it is like shelamim. Here what? There on Tisha B'Av it is because of joy; as long as it is like shelamim there is also joy in it. Here regarding a stubborn and rebellious son it is because of drawing after [appetite], and with anything [after a night] he is not drawn after. Rashi explains: Once a night has passed, its taste is dulled and it is not significant. This implies according to the conclusion that a stubborn and rebellious son is exempt even with less than the measure of shelamim. And one must say that Ravyah explains the Gemara that there is no conclusion here; rather all is from the questioner's words who said "here what," whether we should say that there the reason is joy, etc., and regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we also follow joy, for then he is drawn after it, or we should not say so, but regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we do not follow joy but only drawing after; and the question was not resolved. Therefore Ravyah mentioned only the simple matter without doubt, that with more than the measure of shelamim he is certainly exempt, and the reason is that there is no joy in it; and we learn from this that on erev Tisha B'Av that is permitted. So it appears to me simply. And my father-in-law, of blessed memory, strained needlessly to resolve the Gemara according to Ravyah. And there is a difficulty on the Tur, who explained the reason on Tisha B'Av not as joy but as nullification of taste, while in this Gemara we say explicitly that the reason is joy on Tisha B'Av and not nullification of taste; for nullification of taste is even after one night, as written regarding a stubborn and rebellious son according to one side. And according to the Tur's explanation one would permit on Tisha B'Av even at the measure of a night as well, since the taste is nullified. And one may say that the Tur explains that this is what it means: there on Tisha B'Av the reason is also because of joy; meaning there are two nullifications of taste: one is a great nullification, namely after the measure of shelamim, and the second is a small nullification, which is even after one night. And on this the Gemara said "there because of joy," meaning that on Tisha B'Av there is no prohibition [permission?] except after the measure of shelamim, and there are two advantages: namely that there is no joy, and there is a great nullification; but with one advantage it is forbidden; whereas regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we do not follow joy at all but only taste alone, and therefore it is permitted even with a small nullification. It turns out that wherever there is one side, namely joy alone, even though there is a small nullification of taste, we follow stringency, since there is at least taste and there is joy; therefore it is forbidden on Tisha B'Av within the measure of shelamim. And here with poultry there is taste and no joy; we also follow stringency, and there is no permission except where there is no joy and no taste, namely meat after the measure of shelamim where there is a great nullification. Thus the Tur's words are correct. And even though there is no practical difference for us, for all meat is forbidden to us, nevertheless there is a difference: according to the Tur's reason, that we also follow taste and we require that he not drink and forget the destruction of the Temple, therefore even drinking mead called "med" or distilled spirits is forbidden at the seudah hamafseket; even though there is no joy in them, nevertheless since they intoxicate there is a concern, and this is certainly forbidden according to the Tur, as it appears to me. And so I saw in Responsa Maharshal, siman 31, that he forbids even beer, unless one is accustomed at every meal to beer, or he is a weak person.<br><b>(1)</b> <b>And he need not remove his shoes.</b> And that we require sitting on the ground is not for the reason of mourning, but so that the meal be poor and lowly; for the lowliness is recognized only in this, for for us all year we do not eat reclining, and that meal is like aninut before burial of the dead, when mourning has not yet begun.

<b>כ"ז בסעודה המפסקת כו'. </b> הרמב"ם כ' מימינו לא אכלנו אפי' תבשיל של עדשים בעט"ב והטור הביא זה ואח"כ כ' וכ"ז בסעודה המפסקת כו' משמע ל' הרמב"ם אסעודה המפסקת דוקא ותמהני מאי רבותי' דאם אכל כבר מאכלים טובים בסעודה המפסקת לא אכל תבשיל וצ"ל דהוא לא אכל ב"פ כמו שאנו עושים שאוכלים קודם מנחה סעודה קבועה ואחר תפלת מנחה סעודה מפסקת אלא סעוד' א' לבד אכל והיא היתה הסעודה המפסק' ועל אות' הסעודה שהוא קבע שלו לא אכל אפי' תבשיל עדשים אבל קודם לזה דהיינו קודם חצות אכל הרבה תבשילין וז"ל הטור בשם הרמב"ן ומסתבר סעוד' המפסיק בה שאין דעתו עוד לאכול אחריה סעודת קבע אע"פ שדעתו לאכול אחריה עראי לא כאלו שבטנם בטן רשעים אוכלים בשר ושותין יין ומשתכרין ואח"כ אוכלים עראי כדי להפסיק באכילה שאין בה בשר ויין עכ"ל וכ' רש"ל בתשוב' סי' ל' דהא דנקט רמב"ן בשר ויין ל"ת דוקא משום בשר ויין קרי להו בטן רשעים אלא הרמב"ן מיירי לפי הגמ' שהיה מותר בבשר ויין קודם סעודה המפסקת מ"מ הוא בטן רשעים שאוכלים מעדנים כל צרכם ולאכול אחר כך עראי וה"ה שאר אכילות שעושין מיני טיגונים כמו לחג שבועות בארצינו כו' ואו' אני גדול עונם מנשוא בפרט ליודעי תורה עכ"ל וכן מסיק מו"ח ז"ל דכל ירא שמים לא יאכל אחר חצות סעודה ברבוי תבשילין שמה שאוכלין אחר כך סעודה המפסקת בפירות על הקרקע הוא אכילת עראי ובעל נפש שאינו רוצה לשנות המנהג שכ' בהגהות ש"ע ירבה בסעוד' קודם חצות עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All of this is at the seudah hamafseket, etc.</b> The Rambam wrote: In our days we never ate even a dish of lentils on erev Tisha B'Av; and the Tur brought this, and afterward wrote "and all of this is at the seudah hamafseket," etc., implying that the Rambam's language refers specifically to the seudah hamafseket. And I wonder what the novelty is: if he already ate good foods at the seudah hamafseket, he did not eat a cooked dish? One must say that he did not eat twice, as we do, eating before Minchah a fixed meal and after Minchah prayer a seudah hamafseket; rather he ate only one meal, and it was the seudah hamafseket; and at that meal which was his fixed meal he did not even eat a dish of lentils; but before that, namely before midday, he ate many cooked dishes. And these are the words of the Tur in the name of the Ramban: And it is reasonable regarding the meal with which one interrupts, that he has no intention to eat a fixed meal after it afterward, even though he intends to eat incidental food afterward; not like those whose belly is the belly of the wicked, who eat meat and drink wine and become drunk, and afterward eat incidental food in order to interrupt with an eating that has no meat and wine, end quote. And Maharshal wrote in Responsa, siman 30, that that the Ramban mentioned meat and wine is not precisely because of meat and wine that he calls them the belly of the wicked; rather the Ramban deals according to the Gemara when meat and wine were permitted before the seudah hamafseket; nevertheless it is the belly of the wicked when they eat delicacies to full need and then eat incidental food afterward; and the same applies to other eatings that they make of kinds of fried foods as for the festival of Shavuos in our country, etc.; and I say their iniquity is too great to bear, especially for those who know Torah, end quote. And so my father-in-law, of blessed memory, concluded that every God-fearing person should not eat after midday a meal with many cooked dishes, for what they eat afterward as the seudah hamafseket with fruit on the ground is incidental eating; and a conscientious person who does not wish to change the custom that the glosses of the Shulchan Aruch wrote, should increase in the meal before midday, end quote.

<b>And the Geonim wrote, etc.</b> It appears that they hold that one may not borrow the fast and repay, for the reason we will write nearby, namely to eat immediately on this day and repay afterward. And these are the words of Terumat HaDeshen, siman 275, in the name of Or Zarua: An individual who accepted upon himself a Monday-Thursday fast all the days of the year, and erev Tisha B'Av occurred on Monday: what should he do? To feast twice is forbidden because of his vow; rather he feasts once before the sun sets, for we hold that one may fast by hours, and he prays Minchah the fast prayer, and afterward he feasts, end quote. And there is a difficulty: since he accepted upon himself a plain fast, he must complete it as written in siman 562, se'if 3; if so, how may he eat before sunset? It is well regarding a dream-fast and a yahrzeit, which do not depend on his acceptance; which is not so here, where it depends on what he accepted upon himself, and he is obligated to complete, and whoever does not complete is as if he did not fast at all. And what he wrote that we hold that one may fast by hours: that is not so; that is if he accepted explicitly by hours, but if he accepted plainly, that is a full day, no. One must say that they hold that each day separately needs acceptance, and it teaches us that on this day before Tisha B'Av he should accept the fast explicitly that he will not complete; but this is not so, for whenever he accepted upon himself the whole year, the Rama wrote in siman 562, se'if 8, that one acceptance suffices; and if so he is obligated in every fast to complete it. And it appears that the Geonim intended that in any case he become obligated to repay by fasting another day. And proof from the words of the Rosh in the first chapter of Taanis, who brings that a person may borrow his fast and repay, and wrote on this in the name of Ravyah: And I saw my teachers who would require permitting the vow of his fast with three [judges], for since he said "behold I am fasting tomorrow," it is like a vow; and it appears that since he must repay, the vow is not nullified; rather by estimation of intent we say that his intent was to fast one day and not to fix one day as obligatory. And also from the Yerushalmi there is proof, that something Rav said implies one may fast by hours, for Rav said a person may borrow his fast and repay; and according to our Talmud, that every fast upon which the sun has not set is not a fast, it is impossible to explain except that what he fasted part of the day is called a fast for this purpose, that he need not seek annulment of his vow, for what he fasted part of the day is called a fast, end quote. And in the concise rulings of the Rosh he also wrote, and these are his words: And a person may borrow his fast and repay after he has fasted part of the day, end quote. If so, the Geonim's view is likewise on erev Tisha B'Av that falls on Monday, that he is obligated to fast because of the vow at least a fast of hours, and afterward he borrows the fast and pays on another full day after Tisha B'Av; for only in this manner, that he fasted part of the day, can he afterward make a loan; and that is that they permitted borrowing only when he is in pain, and then he need not fast at all that day; and here when he is not in pain, his permission is after he has fasted part of the day. Also in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 275, he concludes, and these are his words: And reason inclines in our case, namely that he vowed to fast all year Monday-Thursday, that one may not borrow the fast except for duress or real pain or for a meal of mitzvah that applies to them; and even so he must fast part of the day a short time more than he is accustomed, end quote. Even though according to the beginning of his words it does not imply as I wrote, nevertheless according to his conclusion it appears so; rather his view is to be stringent even when he has pain, that nevertheless he should wait a bit; and automatically when he has no pain he should wait until close to sunset, and afterward repay another day. Nevertheless it appears that the Geonim's view is as I wrote, as it appears to me in this; and see what I wrote in siman 568; and as I recalled, so it is also in Mordechai, first chapter of Taanis, on this, and these are his words: However he is obligated to repay it, for he accepted upon himself a full day.
