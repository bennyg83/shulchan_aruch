A homeowner who has many houses in a courtyard and lent or rented them to others — if he has in each of them items that cannot be moved on Shabbat due to their weight or due to prohibition (meaning things forbidden to move even for the sake of their place) — the residents of those houses do not restrict him, since they are all considered like guests in his home. But if the houses do not belong to him — neither owned nor rented — even if he has items in them that are forbidden to move, they restrict one another. {Rama: Rema: Some say all this applies when there are no other residents in the courtyard besides them, but when there are other residents who bring their eruv to them, each one must make an eruv. **** ENGLISH ****
Five groups who spent Shabbat in a large hall and divided it with partitions — if they all pass through one another's sections since there is only one opening to the courtyard, through the outermost section, and all pass through it — only the two innermost need contribute to the eruv, and all the others are considered a gatehouse for them. If each one has an opening to the courtyard — if the partitions reach the ceiling or come within three handbreadths of it, making each one a separate room even if they are made of curtains: if the courtyard residents place the eruv in one of the other courtyard houses, each and every group in the hall must contribute an eruv. Similarly, if there are no other residents in the courtyard, they need no eruv.
