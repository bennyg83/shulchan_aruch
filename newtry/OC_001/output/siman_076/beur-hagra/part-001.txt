<b>ס"ד וי"א כו'. </b>דאזלינן לחומרא וי"א כו' מדפריך ביומא שם ה"ד אי דנראית פשיטא ואם איתא מאי פשיטא והא ר"ה שרי ואכתי לא אסיק אדעתיה במקומה כו' כמו שפריך שם ומ"ש מדר"ה כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 4, And there are those who say, etc.</b> For we rule stringently. The second opinion is derived from the Gemara's challenge there in Yoma: What are the circumstances? If it is visible, the ruling is obvious. Were this not so, why would it be obvious, since Rav Huna permits it? At that point the Gemara had not yet considered the distinction that it is in its place, etc., as it later asks there: In what way is this different from Rav Huna's case, etc.?

<b>ס"ח במקום כו'. </b>ממ"ש הואיל וחטא כו' ואי במקום שאין ראוי להסתפק לא הל"ל הואיל וחטא כו' ועוד ממש"ש וראה צואה כנגדו כו' ודי בזה ול"ק שצריך לחזור ולהתפלל אלא דשם במקום שאין ראוי להסתפק תר"י ורא"ש וע"ל סי' פ"א ס"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 8, In a place, etc.</b> This follows from the statement, "Since he sinned, etc." Had it been a place where there was no reason to suspect excrement, the Gemara should not have said, "Since he sinned, etc." Moreover, this follows from what is stated there: "And he saw excrement opposite him, etc." This is sufficient. The Gemara's ruling that he must repeat the Amidah does not present a difficulty, because there it concerns a place where there was no reason to suspect excrement. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh. See above, siman 81, seif 2.
