<b>(ס"ק ב) לקרות כו'</b> וב"ח פסק מדסתם הרב"י בש"ע וכ' אסור לקרות כנגדה. משמע שדינו כדין צואה ממש שמבואר בסי' ע"ט שלפניו צריך להרחיק מלא עיניו משום דאזיל לשיטתי' שכ' בחבורו על מ"ש הרמב"ם בצוא' עוברת ופי חזיר אסור לקרות כנגדן עד שיעברו ממנו ד"א דמשמע מלשונו דאפי' לפניו סגי בד"א דצואה עוברת קיל בזה מצואה קבוע' וכ"כ מהרי"א אלא לפי שלא מצא הרב"י ראיה לחילוק זה בגמ' לכן פי' דהרמב"ם מיירי לאחריו אבל לפניו אפי' בצואה עוברת אסור כמל' עינו אבל הב"ח כ' דל' הרמב"ם שם משמע כפי' מהרי"א דאפי' לפניו סגי בד"א ואי משום שלא נמצא ראיה לזה בגמ'. אולי הרמב"ם מצא כן בתוספת' והבי' ג"כ קצת סמך מן הגמרא ע"ש. ולכן פסק הב"ח לדינ' כוותיה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2, To recite, etc.</b> The Bach rules this way because the Beit Yosef writes without qualification in Shulchan Aruch, "It is forbidden to recite opposite it." This implies that its law is like actual excrement, regarding which siman 79 explains that in front of him he must distance himself as far as he can see. The Beit Yosef follows his own approach in his commentary on Rambam's ruling that when excrement is passing by, or a pig's mouth passes by, it is forbidden to recite opposite them until they have moved four amos away. Rambam's wording implies that even in front of him four amos suffice, because passing excrement is treated more leniently than stationary excrement. Mahari likewise writes this. However, because Beit Yosef found no source in the Gemara for this distinction, he explains that Rambam refers to excrement behind him. In front of him, even passing excrement is forbidden as far as he can see. The Bach writes that Rambam's wording there supports Mahari's explanation that even in front of him four amos suffice. As for the absence of an explicit source in the Gemara, perhaps Rambam found this in a Tosefta. The Bach also cites some support from the Gemara. See there. He therefore rules in practice in accordance with that view.

<b>(ס"ק ג) ומכוסה כו'</b> על ידו כו' שרי כו' ב"י תר"י ורשב"א דע בגמ' איתא צואה על בשרו או שהיו ידיו בב"ה רב הונא אמר מותר לקרות ק"ש ורב חסדא אמר אסור. וטעם פלוגתייהו מבואר שם. וכמ"ש מ"א ס"ק ה' ו'. ויש פוסקים כר"ה ויש פוסקי' כר"ח והן ב' דעות שהובאו פה בש"ע. והרי"ף ורמב"ם גורסים צואה על בשרו. או ידיו מטונפות מב"ה. והקשה הרא"ש על גרסא זו דא"כ היכי שרי רב הונא כיון שהצואה נראית ואפי' יבשה אסור. וכת' תר"י אף שהרמב"ם יישב זה בלשונו דמיירי שאין בו ממשות אלא לכלוך בעלמ'. ומתוך קטנותו ויבשותו נידון אותו כאלו אין שם צואה. אלא שע"ז קשה דכה"ג אמאי יחלוק רב חסדא מדכתיב כל עצמותי כו' הא כיון דנידן כאלו אין שם צוא' הרי כל עצמותיו נקיים כו' עכת"ד תר"י. ורשב"א כ' ג"כ דהרמב"ם מיירי שאין בו צוא' ממש אלא כעין מלמולי זיעה אלא שהוסיף רשב"א דאפי' בלח שרי משום דאין בו ריח וליכא אלא משום והיה מחניך קדוש. וכיון דמכוס' שרי עכת"ד. א"כ אי מיירי מ"א ביבישה שרי לכ"ע אפי' להי"א דפסקו כר"ח דהא תר"י הקשו כן על גרסת הרמב"ם דכה"ג למה יאסר ר"ח ולגרסת הרמב"ם צריך יישוב אבל לפי גרסתינו שהיה בב"ה. באמת נשארה סבר' חיצונה דכה"ג דהוי כעין מלמולי זיעה מותר לכ"ע אפי' לר"ח ופליגי בדבר אחר וכן בל"ח דלא התירו רשב"א אלא משום שהן מכוסים י"ל כמו שהקשו תר"י דא"כ במאי פליג ר"ח ואסר. וניהו דלגרסת הרמב"ם צריך לדחוק אבל לגרסתנו נשארה סבר' החיצונה דאפי' לר"ח שרי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 3, And covered, etc.</b> "On his hand, etc., it is permitted, etc." Beit Yosef, Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashba. Know that the Gemara states: If there is excrement on his flesh, or his hands are inside a privy, Rav Huna says that he may recite Krias Shema, while Rav Chisda says that he may not. The basis of their disagreement is explained there, as Magen Avraham writes in seifim ketanim 5 and 6. Some authorities rule like Rav Huna and others like Rav Chisda. These are the two opinions cited here in Shulchan Aruch. Rif and Rambam read the text as: "Excrement is on his flesh, or his hands are soiled from a privy." Rosh challenges this reading, because if so, how can Rav Huna permit it when the excrement is visible, since even dry excrement is forbidden? Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah write that although Rambam resolves this through his wording, explaining that there is no actual substance but only a superficial stain, and because it is minute and dry it is treated as though no excrement were present, a difficulty remains. Why would Rav Chisda disagree in such a case because of the verse, "All my bones shall say, etc."? Since it is treated as though no excrement is present, all his limbs are clean. This is the substance of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah's words. Rashba likewise writes that Rambam refers to a case where no actual excrement is present, but only something like accumulated sweat residue. Rashba adds that even when it is moist, it is permitted because it has no odor, and the only concern is "your camp shall be holy." Since it is covered, it is permitted. Accordingly, if Magen Avraham refers to a dry stain, it is permitted according to all opinions, even according to those who rule like Rav Chisda. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah themselves challenged Rambam's reading by asking why Rav Chisda would forbid such a case. Rambam's reading requires a forced resolution, but according to our reading, which states that his hands were in a privy, the straightforward reasoning remains that a residue like accumulated sweat is permitted even according to Rav Chisda, and their disagreement concerns something else. Regarding a moist residue, Rashba permitted it only because it is covered. One might raise Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah's challenge and ask, if so, on what basis does Rav Chisda disagree and forbid it? Although Rambam's reading requires a forced answer, according to our reading the straightforward reasoning remains that even Rav Chisda permits it.

(אם כי אינה ראיה גמורה. די"ל דרבא אורח' דמלת' נקט. והרא"ש נקט סנדל כיון דרבא איירי בסנדל) וא"כ ניהו דסנדל אע"ג דבטל לגבי גוף מ"מ הוי כיסוי מ"מ לענין נוגע יש לחלק מה"ט דוק' סנדל דבטיל לגוף לכך אי סנדל נוגע אסור דהוי כאלו נגע גופו בצואה משא"כ שאר בגדים דלא בטילי לגוף אף דהבגדים נוגעים בצואה לא הוי כאלו הגוף נוגע בצואה ומה"ט שרי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the Rosh's wording somewhat implies, etc.</b> The Rosh writes regarding Rava's statement in the Gemara, "If excrement is in a cavity, he places his shoe over it and recites Krias Shema": "We do not say that the shoe is subordinate to his body and therefore is not considered a covering." This implies that this is Rava's novel point. Since Rava specifically mentions a shoe, and the Rosh likewise mentions a shoe, it appears that other garments are not subordinate to the body and therefore obviously constitute a covering. This is not a conclusive proof, because Rava may merely have described the ordinary case, and the Rosh may have mentioned a shoe because Rava's case concerned a shoe. Nevertheless, although a shoe constitutes a covering despite being subordinate to the body, contact may be treated differently precisely because the shoe is subordinate to the body. If the shoe touches the excrement, it is forbidden because it is considered as though his body touched the excrement. Other garments, however, are not subordinate to the body. Therefore, even if they touch the excrement, it is not considered as though his body touched it, and it is permitted.

<b>ועי"ל כו'</b> כמ"ש תר"י לפי דרכם דהאי וי"א שלא התיר המתיר כו' אצילי ידיו כו' הם תר"י והמאור וכתבו תר"י הטעם דכיסוי בגדים לא מהני לפי שפעמים מתגלה ותהיה נראה לכך אסור משא"כ תחת אצילי ידיו שנשאר לעולם מכוסה וא"כ ה"ה אפי' למאן דמתיר אפי' בכיסוי בגדים היינו דוק' בגדים דע"פ הרוב נשאר מכוס' משא"כ סנדל דע"כ ע"י הלוכו יתגלה מודה דלא הוי כיסוי. ובהמאור מבואר יותר שכ' וז"ל צואה על בשרו במקום שהוא במסתרים כו' ולא עביד לגלויי דאי בדוכתי דעביד לגלויי לא גרע מצוא' דבוק' בסנדלו כו' עכ"ל מבואר להדיא דטעם סנדל נוגע דאסור משום דעביד לגלויי אלא דהמאור השוה גם שאר בגדים שלפעמים מתגלה לסנדל שודאי יתגלה. ולדעה קמא דמתיר אפילו ע"י כיסוי בגדים ס"ל לחלק דוק' סנדל שבודאי מתגלה משא"כ בגדים שאין מתגלה כי אם ע"פ מקרה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One may further explain, etc.</b> Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah follow this approach in explaining the opinion cited as, "Some say that the permissive opinion permitted it only, etc., at the joints of his arms, etc." This is the view of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah and Baal HaMaor. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah explain that covering it with clothing is ineffective because the area may sometimes become uncovered and visible. It is therefore forbidden. Beneath the joints of his arms, however, it remains permanently covered. Accordingly, even the opinion that permits covering with clothing may do so only regarding garments that generally remain covered. A shoe, which will necessarily become exposed through walking, is not considered a covering even according to that view. Baal HaMaor states this more explicitly: "Excrement on his flesh in a concealed place, etc., which is not ordinarily exposed, for in a place ordinarily exposed it is no better than excrement attached to his shoe, etc." His words clearly show that contact with the shoe is forbidden because it is ordinarily exposed. Baal HaMaor equates other garments that sometimes become uncovered with a shoe that will certainly become exposed. The first opinion, which permits even when covered by clothing, distinguishes between a shoe that will certainly become exposed and garments that become uncovered only incidentally.

<b>ובשבת דף קי"ז כו'</b> זה ראיה לדין א' דעל בגדיו ומכוסה שרי ע"כ לכ"ע. דהא מבואר כן בש"ס וע"כ לא יחלוק ע"ז שום פוסק והתם מיירי במכוסה דאמרי' שם ארחב"א אר"י כל ת"ח הנמצ' רבב על בגדיו חייב מיתה (פרש"י רבב שומן וחלב) שנא' כל משנאי אהבו מות אל תקרי משנאי אלא משניאי (פירש"י שממאיסים עצמם בעיני הבריות כו' נמצ' זה משניא את התורה) רבינא אמר רבד אתמר (פירש"י ש"ז) ולא פליגי הא בגלימ' (שהוא עליון אפי' רבב) הא בלבושא (שהוא תחתון רבד אין רבב לא) הרי דש"ז מיירי בלבוש תחתיו דשם מכוסה ע"י בגד עליון ומדאיצטריך לטעם דמשניאי ת"ל דצריך להתפלל וללמוד אע"כ דלענין תורה ותפלה אין קפיד' כיון שהוא על בגדיו ומכוסה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And in Shabbos 117, etc.</b> This is proof for the first ruling, that when it is on his clothing and covered it is necessarily permitted according to all opinions. The Gemara explicitly states this, and no authority can disagree. The case there concerns something covered. The Gemara states that Rabbi Chiya bar Abba said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: Any Torah scholar upon whose clothing a grease stain is found is liable to death, as it is stated, "All who hate Me love death." Do not read it as "those who hate Me," but as "those who cause Me to be hated," because they make themselves repulsive in the eyes of others and thereby cause the Torah to be hated. Ravina says that the text refers to a semen stain. They do not disagree: the first statement concerns an outer garment, where even a grease stain is objectionable, while the second concerns an undergarment, where a semen stain is objectionable but a grease stain is not. Thus, the semen stain discussed there is on an undergarment and covered by an outer garment. Since the Gemara must invoke the concern that he causes the Torah to be hated, it follows that there is no objection regarding Torah study or prayer when the stain is on his clothing and covered.

<b>Only that he may wash it, etc.</b> Meaning, since according to the strict law there is no objection, why is washing permitted on Yom Kippur, when washing is forbidden? Magen Avraham answers that this is comparable to one whose body is soiled with mud, who may wash it, because only washing for pleasure was prohibited, as stated below at the beginning of siman 613 and in the Rama there, seif 3. Rashi at the end of Yoma likewise implies this. However, Rashi does not write that he should scrub away the semen. Rather, he writes that he should scrub because of a chatzitzah. This refers to the baraisa: "The Sages taught: One who experiences a seminal emission on Yom Kippur descends and immerses, and in the evening he scrubs himself." Rashi explains that he scrubs because of a chatzitzah. The Gemara asks: In the evening, meaning after Yom Kippur, what has happened has happened. How does scrubbing then help regarding the chatzitzah that existed on Yom Kippur? Rather, read: Before evening he should scrub, meaning on Erev Yom Kippur each person should scrub himself with hot water so that if he experiences a seminal emission the next day he can immerse without a chatzitzah. Magen Avraham understands that Rashi's explanation according to the Gemara's initial assumption, that he scrubs after Yom Kippur because of a chatzitzah, necessarily means that the semen itself creates a chatzitzah. This implies that he scrubs only because of the chatzitzah, but not because of prayer, since it is covered even though it is on his flesh. One cannot answer that even according to the initial assumption the scrubbing was not specifically because of semen, and that the semen itself would prevent prayer, but that it would suffice to wipe only the affected area, whereas the baraisa's "scrubbing" means scrubbing the entire body lest some attached substance create a chatzitzah, as Rashi explains in the conclusion. Such an answer remains difficult, because from where did Rashi know that according to the initial assumption "scrubbing" meant the entire body lest some substance be attached? Perhaps it meant scrubbing only the area soiled with semen. Even then the Gemara could properly ask, "What has happened has happened," and the reason would be prayer rather than chatzitzah. Since Rashi nevertheless explains it as a chatzitzah, it necessarily follows that semen is not objectionable for prayer when it is covered, even though it is on his flesh. The reason must therefore be chatzitzah. Thus, Rashi also agrees with the permissive opinion regarding excrement on one's flesh.
