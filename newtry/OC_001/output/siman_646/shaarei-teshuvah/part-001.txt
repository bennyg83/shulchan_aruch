<b>בש"ע נשרו רוב עליו כו' </b> וע' בשו"ת פ"י סימן ג' בנשרו רוב עליו דפסול אפילו בשאר ימים פסול למאן דס"ל הדר כל ז' בעי' ואפילו בלולב וערבה פסול שאר ימים כמו ביום א' וכן כל הפסולים חוץ מחסר ושאלה ואצ"ל נשרו עלי הדס דכתיב ביה עבות כו' ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Shulchan Aruch: most of its leaves fell, etc.</b> See Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, siman 3, regarding a case where most of its leaves fell. It is invalid even on the other days according to the one who holds that we require hadar all seven days. Even a lulav and willow are invalid on the other days as on the first day, and so are all invalidations except for missing and borrowed. Needless to say, this applies to fallen myrtle leaves, for regarding the myrtle the Torah writes avos, etc.; see there.

<b>שליקט עבה"ט </b> וע' בספרי שו"ת בית אפרים סימן נ"ה וסימן נ"ט שם נתבאר באר היטב דברי הרמב"ם וגם כתבתי ליישב דלא תיקשי אהא דאמרינן אם מיעטן כשר והא קי"ל כל מלתא דאמר רחמנא ל"ת אי עביד לא מהני עפ"י דברי התוספת דתמורה בצורם אוזן בכור כו' ע"ש וע' במרדכי דסוכה שהובא לקמן סימן תרמ"ז דאם קצצו עכו"ם ערבה בשבת דמייתי ראיה מהך דעבר וליקטן דלית ביה משום מה"ב ובבר"י כאן ובמח"ב סי' תרס"ז האריך בזה ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he picked; see Be'er Heitev.</b> See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 55 and siman 59. There the Rambam's words are explained very well. I also wrote there to resolve why it is not difficult that we say, 'if he reduced them it is valid,' even though we rule that anything the Merciful One said not to do, if one did it, it does not take effect. This is based on the words of Tosafos in Temurah regarding one who blemishes the ear of a firstborn animal, etc.; see there. See the Mordechai on Sukkah, brought below in siman 647, that if a non-Jew cut willows on Shabbos, he brings proof from this law of one who transgressed and picked them that there is no issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. In Birkei Yosef here and in Machazik Berachah, siman 667, he discusses this at length; see there.

<b> (בש"ע ס"ג) ענף עץ עבות כו' </b> וכ' במור וקציעה הדס שאין עלים שלו ראשו של זה מגיע לעוקצו של זה פסול מק"ו דלולב כו' כ"ש בזה דילפינן מקרא דבעי' ענפים חופים את עצו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 3) A branch of a thick tree, etc.</b> The Mor U'Ketziah wrote that a myrtle whose leaves are not arranged so that the top of one reaches the stem of the next is invalid, by an a fortiori inference from lulav, etc. All the more so here, where we derive from the verse that we require branches that cover its wood.

<b>שוטה. </b> עבה"ט ומ"ש אותן הבאים מאיטליא הם פסולים ר"ל שהח"צ כ"כ כלפי מ"ש השבו"י שאין לברך על הדסים הנמצאים בפרא"ג (והם גם כן נמצאים באשמטרדם ובהמבורג) לצאת בהם מחשש מורכבים לפי שבישנים הבאים שהביא מתשובת רמ"א סי' קי"ז. וכתב מח"ב דאם הוא ודאי מורכב יש לפסול ע"ש וגם לטעם הלבוש דפוסל אתרוג מורכב משום שנעבדה בו עבירה יש לפסול כאן דשמא הורכב באילן מאכל וזה האות דגרעינים שלו גדולים קצת וכמש"ל מדברי השבו"י בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Shoteh.</b> See Be'er Heitev. What he wrote, that those that come from Italy are invalid, means that the Chacham Tzvi wrote so in response to what the Shevus Yaakov wrote, namely that one should not recite a berachah over the myrtles found in Prague, which are also found in Amsterdam and Hamburg, in order to fulfill the mitzvah with them, because of a concern that they are grafted. The older ones brought from Italy have small round berries, while the newer ones are elongated, and some larger seeds are also found. Therefore one should recite the berachah over the customary myrtles brought from afar, and as long as they are still green one fulfills the mitzvah with them. But in pressing circumstances, when the customary myrtles are not found, perhaps one may permit fulfilling the mitzvah with these, etc.; see there. Because of this the Chacham Tzvi wrote that the opposite is true: those brought from afar, namely from Italy, should be used only in a place and time when it is impossible to find the valid ones; see there. See my Beis Ephraim on lung tereifos at the beginning of siman 35, on the phrase 'even like a myrtle leaf,' where I brought the words of Machazik Bedek, wondered about him, and also explained this in my Responsa Beis Ephraim, Yoreh De'ah, siman 7. There I brought the above words of the Chacham Tzvi and wondered about the Kapos Temarim, who wanted to force into the words of the Rosh the idea that if one leaf is above, it is not a hadas shoteh. This is not so, for it is clear that specifically the three standing in a circle are required. I brought there the words of the Remazim, that they must stand in the same circle, except that one leaf is mounted over the other two and extends somewhat across their width; this is the avos of the verse. See there, where I wrote that according to this one can properly resolve the words of Machazik Berachah. What the Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of the Panim Meiros, that one violates bal tosif, see there: he did not write this decisively, but wrote only that they should have been more concerned that they might violate bal tosif according to some great authorities brought by the Rosh. It appears from there that according to the Rabbis, and it seems there is a scribal error and it should read that according to the Rabbis who hold that lulav does not require binding, one can say that even according to the Rabbis we do not say each item stands alone, but rather one violates bal tosif; see there. According to the view that lulav requires binding, one violates the Torah prohibition of bal tosif with a non-species, even if he intended it for beauty; see there. See Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 108, who brought the words of the Chacham Tzvi and wrote that his words are not compelling, and that the main ruling is as he wrote in part 1, that my grandfather, the Gaon Rabbi Shmuel Shapiro, did not want to change the custom at all. However, regarding taking old myrtles together with a new one, he also wrote that they did not act properly, because there is a decree on account of observers, due to bal tosif. In my humble opinion, a decree on account of observers does not apply here, since no change is recognizable except for the seeds, and who can determine that this is not its species? See also Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 9, and see Magen Avraham, siman 655, subparagraph 16, that intent is needed for this: he intends to fulfill the obligation only with one, whichever is correct according to law. So too we rule regarding chalitzah, that for a left-handed man they perform chalitzah with both left and right. Nevertheless, what forces me into all this? See there. His proof from chalitzah requires analysis. See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 1, and the Turei Zahav above, siman 39. See Mor U'Ketziah, who wrote that regarding a myrtle, grafting does not invalidate at all, since the change is not recognizable; see there. His words require analysis. See Machazik Berachah, who brought from Responsa Rema, siman 117. Machazik Berachah wrote that if it is certainly grafted, it must be invalidated; see there. Also according to the reason of the Levush, who invalidates a grafted esrog because a transgression was committed with it, one should invalidate here, for perhaps it was grafted onto a food tree, and this is the sign, that its seeds are somewhat large, as we wrote above from the words of the Shevus Yaakov about this.

<b> (ס"י) בש"ע ויש פוסלין כו' </b> וע' בהלק"ט ח"א סימן ר"ך בענף אחד של הדס שיש בו סעיפים הרבה שהוא נחשב אחד ונקטם האמצעי וסעיף אחד נשאר שלם יש תקנה לדבר שיחתוך האמצעי וישאר כמו בית יד לסעיף העליון ולא יתראה שהוא למעט כמו כן הכא ולפ"ז אסור גם כאן לעשות כן ביו"ט ופשוט שעכ"פ צריך אורך ג' טפחים ואע"פ שצריך למדוד כפיפתו ושפועו שבאמצע הקנה לית לן בה כל שעליו חופין את עצו כדינו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if 10) In Shulchan Aruch: and some invalidate, etc.</b> See Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 220, regarding one branch of myrtle that has many offshoots and is considered one. If the middle one was cut off and one offshoot remained whole, there is a remedy: he should cut the middle one so that it remains like a handle for the upper offshoot, and it will not appear that he is reducing it. The same applies here. According to this, it is also forbidden here to do this on Yom Tov. It is obvious that in any event it needs a length of three tefachim. Although one must measure its bend and the slope in the middle of the reed, we have no issue with this as long as its leaves cover its wood as required.
