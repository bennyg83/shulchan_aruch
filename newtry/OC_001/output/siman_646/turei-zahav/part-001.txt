**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Green are valid.</b> For the myrtle itself is also green, so there is no change of appearance because of the berries. This is unlike black berries. Red berries are also considered black, for we say that this black is really red that has deteriorated.

<b>א' א' לאכילה. </b> גירסא זו אינה נכונה ברמב"ם דבגמ' אמרינן רשב"א אומר ממעטין בי"ט ופריך והא מתקן מנא דמתחלה היה פסול ומכשירו ומשני רב אשי כגון שלקטן  לאכיל' ולא להכשירו ופריך והא פסיק רישא הוא  ומשני דאית ליה הושענא אחריתא וקשה על הרמב"ם דמשמע דרוצה  להתיר לכתחל' למעט כשמלקט לאכילה וה"ל להזכיר דאית ליה  הושענא אחריתא והמגיד גורס או שליקט אחר לאכילה ור"ל אחר שא"צ לתקון זה דמסתמא יש לו אחר שלו והיינו כמו שמשני בגמרא והב"י הסכים גם כן לזה וע"כ תימה דהעתיק כאן גירסא זו א' א' כו' שהיא גירסא אינה נכונה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One by one for eating.</b> This reading is not correct in the Rambam. In the Gemara we say: Rabbi Shimon bar Elazar says, one may reduce them on Yom Tov. The Gemara asks: but he is repairing a utensil, for at first it was invalid and he is making it valid. Rav Ashi answers: this is where he picked them for eating and not to make it valid. The Gemara asks: but it is a pesik reisha. It answers: he has another hosha'ana. The Rambam is difficult, for it sounds as if he wants to permit reducing them initially when he picks them for eating, and he should have mentioned that he has another hosha'ana. The Maggid Mishneh reads: or another person picked them for eating. This means another person who does not need this repair, for presumably he has another one of his own. This is like the Gemara's answer. The Beis Yosef also agreed to this. Therefore it is surprising that here he copied this reading, 'one by one, etc.,' which is not a correct reading.

<b>ומיום ראשון ואילך כו'. </b> לא ידעתי שום מעלה בפסול זה דענביו מרובין מעליו יותר משאר פסולים כגון נפרצו עליו ושאר פסולים של הדס דבכל הפסולים אמרי' בסי' תרמ"ט דכשרים מיום ראשון ואילך ובי"ט שני גם כן חצי נזק צרורות דמספקא ליה לר"פ ולרבא פשיטא ליה ופסקו הפוסקים שם כרבא דפשיטא ליה ה"נ בזה וע"כ מותר אפי' באשחור בי"ט ואע"ג דהר"ן כתב כאן דאין מותר אלא באשחור בעי"ט הרמב"ם והרא"ש והטור לא ס"ל כן כנלע"ד **** ENGLISH ****
<b>And from the first day onward, etc.</b> I do not know any special quality in this invalidation of its berries being more numerous than its leaves over other invalidations, such as its leaves being split apart and the other invalidations of myrtle. For regarding all invalid items we say in siman 649 that they are valid from the first day onward, and on the second day of Yom Tov as well its law is the same as the other invalid items, that they are taken without a berachah, as explained there below. Why did the Rema need to write it here? Further, it is difficult for me: why is there leniency here on the other days? It has a remedy, for he can reduce them on Chol HaMoed. This is rejection from the outset, and we say explicitly in Perek Lulav HaGazul, Sukkah 33, that rejection applies to this. It says there regarding what the Mishnah teaches, 'if he reduced them it is valid': when did they blacken? Meaning, when did they become black and invalid? If they blackened before Yom Tov, that is rejection from the outset. Rashi explains: the time of the mitzvah arrived and it was not fit for him, so it is rejection from the outset, and it is stronger than fit and then rejected and then fit again. Shall we resolve from this that rejection from the outset is not rejection? Rather, it means they blackened on Yom Tov, which is fit and then rejected. Thus you learn that there is never rejection when they blackened from the outset, and you may resolve that this is not rejection, but you cannot resolve from here that fit and then rejected is not rejection. If so, here, where the berries were more numerous, it is rejection from the outset; why should he not reduce them on Chol HaMoed and take them? As to what is written in this sugya, that the question of fit and then rejected was not resolved, namely when they blackened on Yom Tov, and there is permission only when they blackened before Yom Tov: why did the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur write without qualification that if he reduced them it is valid, and not write that this is specifically when they blackened before Yom Tov, but if they blackened on Yom Tov one should be stringent, since it is a Torah doubt? This is the question Rabbi Yirmeyah asked nearby, whether rejection applies to mitzvos, whether it should be compared to korbanos, where rejection is from the Torah. The Beis Yosef answered that since it is in his power to repair it, it is not rejection, as stated in Zevachim that wherever it is in his power, it is not rejection. The repair is that he can reduce them, as stated in the Gemara, where he picks them for eating and has another hosha'ana. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct, for on Yom Tov he cannot repair it so that it becomes valid. The Gemara's answer, that he picked them for eating, means that his intent is only for eating, unlike here, where you say that he can repair it, which means he will reduce them for the sake of repair, and that is forbidden on Yom Tov. Further, if so, the Rosh should have mentioned that it speaks specifically where he has another hosha'ana, as the Gemara says. Further, if so, even fit and then rejected should also be permitted, as the Ran wrote; the Beis Yosef wrote about this that in truth it is permitted and the question was resolved, except that at first they did not know this reasoning. This is not at all implied in the sugya. It appears to me that there is no difficulty, for there above in the sugya Rav Papa said, 'This teaches that there is no rejection regarding mitzvos,' implying completely, even leniently, and that rejection of a mitzvah is not like rejection of a korban. Rabbi Yirmeyah was merely uncertain about this inference of Rav Papa, whether it applies even leniently. We do not leave Rav Papa's certainty and follow Rabbi Yirmeyah's uncertainty, as we say similarly at the beginning of Bava Kamma regarding half-damages of pebbles, where Rav Papa was uncertain and Rava was certain, and the poskim rule there like Rava, who was certain. So too here. Therefore it is permitted even if they blackened on Yom Tov. Although the Ran wrote here that it is permitted only if they blackened before Yom Tov, the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur do not agree with him. This appears clear to me.

<b>אבל אם היו שני העלין כו'. </b> בגמרא אמרינן דעבות היינו דקיימי ג' עלין בקינא רב כהנא אמר אפי' תרי וחד פירש"י ב' עלין בעוקץ אחד ועלה א' מלמעלה ועול' ורוכב על השנים רב אחא בריה דרבא מהדר אתרי וחד הואיל ונפק מפומי' דרב כהנא א"ל מר בר אמימר לרב אשי אבא קרי לההיא הדס שוט' וע"כ פסלוהו הפוסקים וטרח רמ"א לקיים מנהגנו שמברכין על הדסים כאלו ונ"ל דיש סמך וראיי' בדרך זה דרב אחא טרח לקיים דברי רב כהנא אע"ג דאינו מצו' מן המובחר דהא קאמר בלשון אפי' אלא משום כבודו דרב כהנא עביד כי וע"ז אמר מר בר אמימר דאבא קרי ליה הדס שוט' כלומר אינו מן המובחר וחולק על מה שמהדר דוקא ע"ז אבל לדינא הוא כשר כנ"ל לבקש סמך למנהג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if there were two leaves, etc.</b> In the Gemara we say that avos means that three leaves stand in one nest. Rav Kahana said: even two and one. Rashi explains: two leaves on one stem, and one leaf above, rising and riding over the two. Rav Acha son of Rava would seek out two and one, since it came from Rav Kahana's mouth. Mar bar Ameimar said to Rav Ashi: my father called that a hadas shoteh. Therefore the poskim invalidated it. The Rema labored to uphold our custom, that we recite a berachah over such myrtles. It appears to me that there is basis and proof in this way: Rav Acha labored to uphold Rav Kahana's words, even though this is not the choicest performance of the mitzvah, for he said it with the expression 'even,' but he did so out of honor for Rav Kahana. About this Mar bar Ameimar said that his father called it a hadas shoteh, meaning that it is not the choicest form, and he disagrees with specifically seeking it out. But as a matter of law, it is valid. This seems to me a basis for the custom.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if most of them fell, etc.</b> In the Gemara the Rabbis taught: if most of its leaves fell and a minority remained, it is valid, provided that its avos remains. This itself is difficult: you said that if most of its leaves fell it is valid, and then you said, provided that its avos remains. Once two have fallen, how can avos be found, since altogether there are only three? Abaye said: it is found with an Egyptian myrtle, where seven stand in one nest; when four fall, three remain. The Ran wrote: regarding an ordinary myrtle, its law was not explained. There is one who says that it must all be avos, and if even one leaf from its entire required measure remains missing, it is invalid, etc. This is what we say, that its avos must remain, and we established it where three remained, proving that with fewer than three it is not called avos. But the Ra'ah holds that when we say three in a nest, it means that the species must be of this type. If it is of this species, but some of its leaves fell, as long as the majority remains in each nest it is valid and is properly called avos. He infers this, etc. One should not be lenient this way, for his proof is not clear. These are the Ran's words. The Rosh wrote that what the baraisa teaches, that most of its leaves fell, refers to the leaves beyond the required avos. 'Nifrezu' in the Mishnah means that most of the avos of the myrtle's required measure fell and the minority remained. These are his words. The Beis Yosef wrote that this implies that if the entire required length of the myrtle was avos and most of the leaves fell, meaning most of the nests, even if only one leaf fell from each nest, it is invalid. This follows the words of the Geonim who hold that avos is required throughout the whole required length of the myrtle. These are his words. I do not know the basis for his implication, for the Rosh distinguishes between the falling of leaves in the baraisa, which refers to the surplus beyond the avos, that is, from valid avos in one place, namely an Egyptian myrtle that has seven leaves, four of which are surplus, and the 'nifrezu' of the Mishnah, which speaks of most of the measure being broken in one place. With an ordinary myrtle, whose measure is three leaves, most of them being broken means two. The Rosh wrote this explicitly regarding willow: the final clause teaches that if some of its leaves fell it is valid, to teach that 'its leaves were split' means the majority; he taught it in the last clause, and the same applies to all of them. Regarding the invalid case he used the expression 'nifrezu' because it is the majority. These are his words. This also refers to an ordinary myrtle, all of whose leaves are only three, and nevertheless it is invalidated only when the majority was broken. Do not ask: what is the real difference, that we do not require all the avos, meaning all three, as the baraisa said, provided that its avos remains? One can answer that the Mishnah speaks of an ordinary myrtle that has only three leaves, but the baraisa speaks of an Egyptian myrtle that has seven. When most fell, we require that the whole measure of avos remain, similar to an ordinary myrtle, which is not validated when only one remains, since the majority fell from it, but rather the majority must remain. So too with an Egyptian myrtle, whenever the majority fell, three must remain, which is the measure of avos. So the Tur also wrote: first he wrote about an Egyptian myrtle of seven leaves that three must remain, and afterward he wrote that if most of the leaves of the avos measure fell, it is invalid. It is clear that he speaks of an ordinary myrtle, whose measure is three, and it is invalid specifically when two fell from it. It follows that no one is stringent to invalidate an ordinary myrtle when one of the three leaves fell except the Ran alone. Therefore, for us, where our myrtle has two leaves above two, if one leaf from the required myrtle measure, which must be avos, meaning three tefachim, fell, it is invalid according to the Ran; but according to the Rosh, if two leaves fell from most of this required measure, then it is invalid. One must be careful about this, because many times many leaves fall from the myrtle and people do not pay attention to it.

<b>ולעיכובא ברובו. </b> פי' דאם אין הרוב של שיעור הדס מכוס' בעבות פסול ול"ד ליבש דסגי בג' עלין לחין דשם איתנהו לכולהו אע"פ שאינו כ"כ הדר אבל בנשרו העבות בצר להו שיעורא אם לא נשאר הרוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And for indispensability, in its majority.</b> Meaning, if the majority of the required myrtle measure is not covered with avos, it is invalid. This is not comparable to a dried myrtle, where three moist leaves suffice, for there all the leaves are present, even though it is not so hadar. But when the avos leaves fell, the required measure is lacking unless the majority remains.
