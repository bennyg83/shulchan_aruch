**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1.</b> A myrtle whose top was cut off is valid, etc. Note: The Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Ran two opinions regarding a myrtle whose top was cut off before Yom Tov and a new shoot grew on it on Yom Tov, whether it is valid. Some validate it because rejection from the outset is not considered rejection regarding mitzvos, as the Talmud concludes regarding the case, 'and if he reduced them, it is valid.' From there it appears that our question about its top being cut off and a new shoot growing was resolved. Others hold that our question was not resolved. Granted, where he transgressed and reduced them, it is in his power to repair it; although it is forbidden, it is nevertheless in his power to repair it, and therefore it is not rejection. But where it is not in his power to repair it, even rejection from the outset is rejection. These are the words of the Beis Yosef. I say that this reasoning, whether something that is in his power to repair through a prohibition is considered 'in his power' regarding rejection or not, is the subject of a dispute among the Amoraim in Yoma 57b. There we read: if some of the bloods became mixed and some did not become mixed, it is obvious that when he sprinkles, he sprinkles from the certain bloods. But what is the law regarding those mixed bloods as remainders? Do they go to the base of the altar, or perhaps they are rejected and go to the channel? Rav Papa said: even according to the one who says that one cup renders its fellow cup remainders, that applies only where, if he wanted to sprinkle from it, he could sprinkle from it. But regarding this mixture, if he wanted to sprinkle from it, he could not sprinkle from it. Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua said to Rav Papa: on the contrary, even according to the one who says that one cup renders its fellow rejected, that applies only there, where he rejected it actively, but where he did not reject it actively, no, etc. The reason for their dispute appears as follows. Certainly, when Rav Papa says that if he wanted to sprinkle from it, he could not sprinkle from it, this is only initially, because since there are certain bloods here, it is not proper to sprinkle from the mixture. But after the fact, if he sprinkled from the mixture according to the law explained earlier there, in a way that escapes all uncertainties, certainly it helps, since he has already sprinkled everything according to its law and he has certainly fulfilled it. Only initially, since there are certain bloods here, he must sprinkle from the certain bloods. If so, Rav Papa holds that the fact that he is unable to sprinkle initially is considered rejection. Therefore he properly says that even according to the one who says that one cup renders its fellow remainders, that is only where he could sprinkle from it and there was no rejection. But this one, from which he cannot sprinkle, has been rejected; and since it was rejected, it no longer has the status of remainders. Although he did not reject it actively, nevertheless, since it is not in his power to repair it, a case without active rejection and not in his power to repair is like active rejection despite being in his power to repair, and therefore it is rejection. But Rav Huna holds that something in his power to repair through a prohibition is called in his power and is not rejection. Therefore he properly says: even according to the one who says that one cup renders its fellow rejected, that applies only there, where he rejected it actively. Since it was in his power to sprinkle in a fully permitted way, if he did not sprinkle from it and separated them, that is active rejection; therefore, although it was in his power to sprinkle from it, it is rejection. But here, with the mixture, either way it is not active rejection, because it is forbidden initially to sprinkle from it. Since it is forbidden initially to sprinkle from it, it is not called active rejection. Nevertheless it is called in his power to repair, since it is in his power to do this. Therefore it is not active rejection and it is in his power to repair, so it is not rejection. Since the Amoraim dispute this and the Rambam ruled like Rav Papa, this is therefore considered rejection even though it is in his power to repair it. Perforce, the reason in the case of 'if he reduced them it is valid' is that there is no rejection regarding mitzvos; if so, the same is true when its top was cut off. However, according to what Tosafos wrote in Sukkah 33, that regarding rejection from the outset, something in his power to repair through a prohibition is not rejection, but regarding fit and then rejected it is rejection even though it is in his power to repair through a prohibition, there would be no proof from this. For here, regarding the blood, it is fit and then rejected, since before the mixture it was fit for sprinkling. Therefore it is fit and then rejected, and for that reason Rav Papa holds that it is rejection even though it is in his power to repair. There would be no proof about rejection from the outset. Yet I reconsidered and saw that this is not so. It is proven there in the Talmud, and afterward stated explicitly in the Mishnah, that if he put the blood of the goat before the blood of the bull, he has done nothing in all the applications. If so, regarding the blood of the bull it was fit and then rejected, but regarding the blood of the goat it is rejection from the outset, for it was not yet fit to be placed in the Sanctuary as long as he had not placed the blood of the bull. If so, it is rejection from the outset. And if, for rejection from the outset, something in his power to repair through a prohibition is considered in his power, the blood should still have the status of remainders because of the goat's blood, and it should be placed on the base of the altar because of the goat's blood within it. Why should it be poured into the channel? After all, it is implied in the Talmud that with a mixture one sprinkles three times, even though the third time includes one blood that is extra; nevertheless, one sprinkles it because of the obligatory blood within it. If so, the same should apply regarding pouring the remainders, since that is a mitzvah and part of the remnants of the service, whereas pouring into the channel is not part of the service at all. It should therefore be placed on the base because of the goat's blood, since in that respect it has the status of remainders, for this is rejection from the outset. Perforce, Rav Papa holds that even with rejection from the outset, it is rejection, even though it is in his power to repair through a prohibition. The same applies to lulav. Perforce, there is no rejection regarding mitzvos, and the same is true where a new shoot grew on it. With this I have found room to resolve the view of the poskim who ruled that where the wind covered the blood and it became uncovered again, one is obligated to cover it, and they did not write that this is because of a doubt whether rejection applies to mitzvos; see Magen Avraham, siman 586, and my work there. Now it appears as follows. One may say that when the Talmud rejects the proof by saying that rejection from the outset is not rejection, and that one cannot resolve from it the case of fit and then rejected, its meaning is this: certainly, regarding consecrated items even rejection from the outset is rejection, as Tosafos wrote here. Only because mitzvos are different, and rejection does not apply to a mitzvah, do we come to this. If the reason is that rejection does not apply to a mitzvah, then although the item has the formal status of rejection, it is valid for another reason, namely that rejection does not apply to mitzvos; in that case there is no distinction between rejection from the outset and fit and then rejected. But if the reason is that it is in his power to repair, even though only through a prohibition, and therefore it does not have the name of rejection at all, in that case one can distinguish between rejection from the outset and fit and then rejected, just as one might have thought to distinguish regarding consecrated items between fit and then rejected and rejection from the outset, holding that rejection from the outset is not rejection but fit and then rejected is rejection. So too here: something in his power to repair through a prohibition, regarding rejection from the outset, one can say that it is not rejection, even though the repair is through a prohibition; but regarding fit and then rejected, even if it is in his power to repair through a prohibition, it is rejection. Therefore this is well understood. When the Talmud says: really, it blackened before Yom Tov, and rejection from the outset is not rejection; you may resolve this from it, but you cannot resolve from it that fit and then rejected is not rejection, this is only according to Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua in the above sugya, who holds that something in his power to repair through a prohibition is not rejection with rejection from the outset. Therefore, he pours it to the base because of the goat's blood within it. According to him, one can say that the case is where it blackened from the outset, and rejection from the outset is not rejection if it is in his power to repair through a prohibition, while fit and then rejected is rejection even though it is in his power to repair through a prohibition, as Tosafos wrote in the sugya in Sukkah. All this is according to Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, in the manner of what Tosafos wrote in Nedarim regarding 'these kohanim are agents of the Merciful One,' where the Talmud wants to resolve the matter; see there. So too here, the Talmud wants to resolve from the baraisa itself that fit and then rejected is not rejection regarding mitzvos. Therefore it rejects the proof: from the baraisa alone there is no proof, for one can say that since it is in his power to repair through a prohibition, it does not have the name of rejection. According to this, fit and then rejected differs from rejection from the outset, for regarding the reason that it does not have the name of rejection, one may distinguish between rejection from the outset and fit and then rejected. All this is only the Talmud's rejection that there is no proof from the baraisa. But for us, according to the truth, since we rule like Rav Papa in Yoma that it is poured into the channel, he holds that something in his power to repair through a prohibition is not considered in his power regarding consecrated items, and it has the formal status of rejection. If so, since it has the formal status of rejection, perforce the reason that where he transgressed and reduced them it is valid is that there is no rejection regarding mitzvos. If the thing itself is considered rejection but is valid for another reason, namely that rejection does not apply to mitzvos, then there is no longer a reason to distinguish between rejection from the outset and fit and then rejected. Everything is not invalidated by reason of rejection, since rejection does not apply to mitzvos; all are equal, rejection from the outset and fit and then rejected. Therefore the question of fit and then rejected is indeed resolved from the law that if he transgressed and reduced them it is valid. The view of those poskim is thus properly established. Examine this well.
