<small>ט"ז סק"ו </small>ראוי להחמיר כיון דהוי ספיקא דאורייתא. לענ"ד הא תוס' כתב דאף דבדבר שבידו לא מקרי דיחוי אפי' בקרבנות. דלא הוי כ"כ דיחוי. והיינו כמ"ש תוס' זבחים כיון דאסור למעט ע"ש. וא"כ לא הוי ס' בדאורייתא דהא פשיטא דמדאורייתא מותר ללקטן כמ"ש תוס' להדיא ביומא דל"ד ב' ד"ה הנ"מ בסה"ד דאותו תקון דמיעוט ענביו אינו נראה תיקון כ"כ שיוחשב תיקון מה"ת עיי"ש. וכן בלבוש כאן כתב דנראה כמתקן וא"כ מדאורייתא ולא הוי דיחוי דהוא בידו. אלא כיון דמדרבנן אסור ללוקטו הוי דיחוי מדרבנן וא"כ הוי ספק דרבנן. אולם במרדכי ריש פ"ג דסוכה כתב דתיקון זה דמיעוט ענביו הוא דאורייתא. ועמש"ל בגליון המג"א סי' שי"ד סק"ה. ונ"מ אם שרי למעט ע"י נכרי דלתוספות שרי דהוא שבות דשבות במקום מצוה כההיא רסי' תקפ"ו סכ"א. ולהמרדכי אסור. ואפשר דגם להמרדכי שרי באומר לנכרי שימעטם לאכילה דבזה אף דהוי פ"ר מ"מ אמירה לנכרי שריא. ע' מג"א סי' רנ"ג סקי"ח. וכעין זה תמוה לי בסוכה דל"ג ב' דמעטינהו אימת וכו' דיחוי מעיקרא הוא. הא בעיו"ט דהוי בידו לגמרי פשיטא דלא הוי דיחוי דאפי' בקרבנות לא הוי דיחוי וצע"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Taz se'if katan 6</small> It is proper to be stringent since it is a Torah doubt. In my humble opinion, Tosafot wrote that even though something in his power is not called rejection, even regarding offerings, it is not so much a rejection. This is as Tosafot wrote in Zevachim, because it is prohibited to reduce them; see there. If so, this is not a doubt in a Torah matter, for it is obvious that by Torah law it is permitted to pick them, as Tosafot wrote explicitly in Yoma 34b, s.v. hanach milei, at the end of the discussion, that this repair of reducing its berries does not appear so much like repair that it should be considered repair by Torah law; see there. Likewise the Levush here wrote that it appears like repairing. If so, by Torah law it is not rejection, because it is in his power. Rather, since rabbinically it is prohibited to pick them, it is rejection rabbinically; if so, it is a rabbinic doubt. However, the Mordechai at the beginning of the third chapter of Sukkah wrote that this repair of reducing its berries is biblical. See what I wrote above in the margin of Magen Avraham, siman 314, se'if katan 5. The practical difference is whether it is permitted to reduce them through a gentile: according to Tosafot it is permitted, for it is a double rabbinic prohibition in the place of a mitzvah, like that case at the beginning of siman 586:21; but according to the Mordechai it is prohibited. Perhaps even according to the Mordechai it is permitted if one tells a gentile to reduce them for eating, for in that case, even though it is an inevitable consequence, nevertheless instructing a gentile is permitted. See Magen Avraham, siman 253, se'if katan 18. Similarly, I am puzzled by Sukkah 33b: when did he reduce them, etc.; it is initial rejection. But before Yom Tov, when it is entirely in his power, it is obvious that it is not rejection, for even by offerings it is not rejection. This requires great investigation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Se'if 8</small> That even if from the three that he chose, a nest, etc. This is puzzling to me, for from the words of the Rosh it appears that we require that three nests remain on the hadas branch, and in each nest the leaf at the top of the one nest should be one leaf that is set over the two of them. This requires great investigation. Afterward I found that the Gaon Rabbi Eliyahu of Vilna, of blessed memory, wrote so. I am further puzzled: if one leaf is set over the two of them, this is a wild hadas. The Korban Netanel addressed this. According to his words, the difficulty still remains against the Shulchan Aruch, which ruled in se'if 3 that such a case is called a wild hadas.
