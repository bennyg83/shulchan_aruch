<b>(Seif katan 1) To discard them, etc.</b> Since one mitzvah was performed with them, etc. Taz writes that the same applies to s'chach from a sukkah: after the festival, one should not use it for something degrading. The same applies to every mitzvah object, such as a lulav and the like.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 2) Not to, etc.</b> The writings of the Arizal, etc. Taz writes that the permission to enter the privy while wearing tzitzis applies specifically to a tallis katan. A tallis gadol, whose initial making was for the sake of the mitzvah, is forbidden. The same applies to a kittel; see there.

“He had a mezuzah, etc.” The Gemara likewise implies this. Meaning, one should not say that David was particular only about a mitzvah upon his body, unlike mezuzah, which is not upon his body. The Gemara implies that even if he had had a mezuzah, that would have sufficed for David.

<b>(ס"ק ג) שלא כו'</b> וכ"מ ממ"ש ס"ס י' ר"ל מ"ש מ"א שם ס"ק י"ג בשם הרב"י שנתן טעם שנהגו להקל שלא לעשות ציצית בבגדים ארוכים שלנו דא"כ היו נפסקים בכל עת והיו צריך לתקנן כל עת י"ל דלא חייבה תורה בכך ע"ש. ות"ל שהיו הציצית נגררים על הארץ והי' עושה איסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 3) Not to, etc.</b> The same is implied by what is written at the end of siman 10. Meaning, Magen Avraham there, seif katan 13, cites Beit Yosef's reason for the custom not to place tzitzis upon our long garments: if they had tzitzis, the strings would constantly break and require constant repair. One may say that the Torah did not obligate a person in such a case. According to Magen Avraham's present concern, however, another reason should suffice: the tzitzis would drag upon the ground and cause a prohibition.
