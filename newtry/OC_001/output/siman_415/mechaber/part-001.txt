**** ENGLISH ****
That one may only make an eruv of boundaries for a matter of mitzva. Contains 4 sections One may only make an eruv of boundaries for a matter of mitzva — such as wishing to go to a house of mourning, or to a wedding feast, or to greet his teacher or friend who came from a journey, and similar cases. And if he made an eruv for none of these but for an optional matter, it is nonetheless a valid eruv. {Rama: Rema: Or if he wishes to go for a stroll on Yom Tov or Shabbat in an orchard, which brings joy — this is considered a matter of mitzva. Or out of fear, such as wishing to flee from non-Jews or bandits and the like, in which case he is permitted to go even for an optional matter.}

**** ENGLISH ****
If two people said to someone "go and make an eruv for us" — one made an eruv for him while it was still day and one made an eruv for him during twilight: the eruv made for him while it was still day was eaten during twilight, and the eruv made for him during twilight was eaten after nightfall — both have acquired a valid eruv. Some disagree.

**** ENGLISH ****
When placing an eruv of boundaries one recites the blessing on the mitzva of eruv and says "through this eruv I shall be permitted to walk from such-and-such place two thousand cubits in each direction." If he is making an eruv for many he says "so-and-so and so-and-so shall be permitted" or "the residents of such-and-such place shall be permitted."
