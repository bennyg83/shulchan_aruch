<b>בראש. </b>ר"ל בגובה העצם כו'. וז"ל סמ"ק ואמרו רבותינו שצריך להניח במקום גובה של זרוע כו' וכ' בהג"ה ומיהו אין כולו כשר להניח כ"א חצי הזרוע של צד הקובד"ו כדאיתא בשימושא רבא פלג זרוע. ור"ל על הגבוה שבחצי עצם הנ"ל דהא אמרינן קיבורת דוקא וכ"כ כל הפוסקים שבמקום גבוה דוקא: בד"ה. ודברי הגהת סמ"ק צ"ע מנ"ל לחלק דהא בגמ' אמרינן סתמא ידך זו קיבורת ולולי דבריו נ"ל דמ"ש בש"ר פלג זרוע קאי על כל הקיבורת שהוא פלג זרוע ול"ד פלג אלא ר"ל מקצתו וכשיעור מקום הנחת שני תפילין כמ"ש בריש פ' בתרא דעירובין וכן נראה לי עיקר דכל הקיבורת כשר להניח בו תפילין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the top.</b> Meaning on the raised portion of the bone. Semak writes that our Rabbis required placement on the raised portion of the arm. Its gloss says that not the entire arm is valid, but only the half toward the elbow, based on Shimusha Rabba's phrase 'half the arm'; it means the raised part of that half, since the Gemara specifies the kibores and all poskim require the raised portion. This gloss requires examination: the Gemara says without qualification, 'your hand; this is the kibores.' Were it not for the gloss, I would explain 'half the arm' as referring to the entire kibores, which is the arm's relevant section; 'half' is not exact but means part of it, sufficient to place two pairs of tefillin, as stated at the beginning of Eruvin's final chapter. This appears primary: the entire kibores is valid for tefillin.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One whose hand is amputated, etc.</b> Darkei Moshe cites Or Zarua that an amputee is exempt even if some upper arm remains, while Tosafos implies he is obligated. Magen Avraham says Or Zarua had Ra'em's reading, 'one who has only an arm,' and ruled like the first tanna. It appears that all agree an amputee does not place it on the left; the dispute is whether he places it on the right. Mechilta derives inclusion of an amputee whose tefillin are placed on the right. Our reading is preferable: why exempt an amputee when the whole arm and kibores are called 'hand'? Rather, the amputee in the sugya lacks even the kibores, as Bach writes; he is exempt according to the first tanna and Rav Ashi. If the kibores remains, all agree he is obligated and places it on the left. Thus Tosafos is primary according to our reading, while Or Zarua ruled like the first tanna according to Ra'em's reading. Tosafos was pressed to say the views do not disagree because it understood the case as amputation of the left hand with some arm remaining. According to the explanation above, the dispute concerns placement on the right, but there is no practical difference in the ruling.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 8: The length, etc.</b> See Tosafos there, s.v. Vekamah. Aruch and the other explanation understand the measure as stated here; all the Geonim and Rif explain so, and Rambam's wording matches the Shulchan Aruch. The Gemara's statement that Rabbah tied them means upon the finger, requiring extra length. Rav Acha wrapped three times and tied. Mordechai explains the order as incidental. More likely 'tied' means fastening on the arm: Rabbah first fastened them, then stretched and let the straps fall, without wrapping the middle finger; Rav Acha wrapped. Rambam mentions tying because otherwise the wraps would not remain, but we do not tie there because the wraps are not indispensable. Mordechai says the halachah follows Rav Acha. Elsewhere the halachah follows Mar son of Ravina; here we act stringently, unlike there where stringency would create bal tosif.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 9: The place, etc.</b> Menachos 37b calls it a place where the hair can be removed and which can contract impurity. 'Where a child's brain is soft' means up to that point; certainly below it is even more literally 'between your eyes.' Eruvin's final chapter says there is room there for two pairs. The Yerushalmi similarly says that the upper part of the head has room for two, and Rav Zeira explains: up to where a child's brain is soft. 'Up to the upper part' and 'up to where the brain is soft' imply from the beginning of the head until that point; 'has room' likewise means beginning at the hairline. See Sefer HaTerumah.
