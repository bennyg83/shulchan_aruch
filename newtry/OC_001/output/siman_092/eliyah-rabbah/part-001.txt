[א] <b>צריך לנקביו וכו'.</b> שזה גם כן כלול בפסוק הכון לקראת אלהיך ועוד כתיב שמור רגליך באשר תלך אל בית אלקים אי מיירי שישמור רגליו שלא יגוף באבן כשילך לבית אלקים בכל מקום שילך נמי אלא שמור נקביו קאמר, (לבוש). ובספר חסידים סימן תתק"ד למד מזה שיעשה צרכיו קודם שהקהל הולכין לבית הכנסת כדי שלא יצטרך לשהות אחר כך. עוד כתב לעיין שלא ידבק במנעליו צואה כשהולך לבית הכנסת שנאמר שמור רגליך וכו' ולכך כתיב של נעליך מעל רגליך. ומה שכתב הלבוש בכל מקום שילך נמי וכו' קשה דילמא קא משמע לן דאף דקיימא לן סימן צ' סעיף י"ב מצוה לרוץ כשהולך לבית הכנסת צריך לשמור, דלא תימא שלוחי מצוה אינם ניזוקין קא משמע לן היכא דשכיח היזיקא שאני כדאיתא בפסחים דף ח', ואפשר דהכי קאמר מאי איריא בית אלקים אפילו שאר דבר מצוה נמי ודחוק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who needs to relieve himself, etc.</b> This too is included in the verse, 'Prepare to meet your G-d.' It is also written, 'Guard your feet when you go to the House of G-d.' If this meant that he should guard his feet so that he not strike them on a stone when going to the House of G-d, that would apply wherever he goes. Rather, it means that he should guard his bodily needs, as the Levush explains. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 904, he learned from this that one should relieve himself before the congregation goes to the synagogue, so that he will not need to delay afterward. He also wrote that one should be careful that excrement not adhere to his shoes when he goes to the synagogue, as it says, 'Guard your feet, etc.' Therefore it is written, 'Remove your shoes from upon your feet.' What the Levush wrote, that it would apply wherever he goes, etc., is difficult, for perhaps the verse teaches that although we rule in siman 90, seif 12, that it is a mitzvah to run when going to the synagogue, he still must be careful. One should not say that agents of a mitzvah are not harmed; the verse teaches that where danger is common, the matter is different, as stated in Pesachim 8a. Possibly this is what he means: why specifically the House of G-d, the same should apply to any other mitzvah matter. But this is forced.

[ב] <b>שיעור הילוך וכו'.</b> הוא שיעור שעה (ב"ח), ועיין לעיל סימן נ"ח ס"ק ה' וצריך עיון. ודע דהא דנצרך לנקביו דנקט הש"ס ופוסקים כל נקביו במשמע בין גדולים בין קטנים כדמשמע בסעיף ג' וכן מבואר בתשובת רשב"א בסימן קל"א, וכן כתב בתשובת רמ"א סימן צ"ח ומעדני מלך דף כ"ד. ותימא על מגן אברהם שכתב בפשיטות דאין צריך לחזור בקטנים ואישתמיטתיה גדולים הנזכרים לעיל, גם ראיה מש"ס [ברכות] דף כ"ג שהביא לא דק דשם לא נצרך לנקביו קודם תפילה אלא בתוך התפילה. אך קצת קשה מלקמן סוף סימן ת"ו בהלכות תחומין דיש אומרים דוקא גדולים נקראו נקבים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The measure of walking, etc.</b> This is the measure of an hour, as the Bach writes. See above, siman 58, seif katan 5, and this requires study. Know that when the Gemara and poskim refer to one who needs to relieve himself, all bodily needs are implied, whether major or minor, as is implied in seif 3, and as is explicit in the responsum of the Rashba, siman 131. So too wrote the responsa of the Rama, siman 98, and Maadanei Melech, page 24. It is therefore astonishing that the Magen Avraham wrote simply that with minor needs he need not repeat the prayer, for the major needs mentioned above escaped him. Also, the proof he brought from the Gemara in Berachos 23 is not precise, for there the person did not need to relieve himself before prayer, but during prayer. However, there is some difficulty from below, at the end of siman 406 in the laws of techumin, where some say that only major needs are called nekavim.

[ג] <b>אבל לכתחילה וכו'.</b> כתב עולת תמיד בשם הב"ח דאם יבדוק קודם תפילה יעבור זמן תפילה מותר אף לכתחילה כיון שיכול לעמוד על עצמו שיעור פרסה, עד כאן. ולא דק דדוקא לגירסת הרי"ף דגריס בש"ס מותר לכתחילה דפסק הב"ח כוותיה, כתב הב"ח דמיירי כשעובר זמן התפילה, אבל לגירסת הרא"ש והרבינו יונה דאסור והכי קיימא לן, אוסר הב"ח לכתחילה אף אם יעבור זמן תפילה וזה ברור. מיהו לדינא כתב מגן אברהם דאפשר לסמוך ארי"ף בזה, וכן נראה לי שראיתי באגודה שפסק כרי"ף. ונראה לי דמכל מקום דאף אם יעבור תפילת ציבור יפנה קודם, ועיין לעיל סימן נ"ח ס"ק ז' דתפילה בזמנה עדיף מתפילת ציבור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But initially, etc.</b> The Olas Tamid wrote in the name of the Bach that if checking before prayer will cause the time for prayer to pass, it is permitted even initially, since he can restrain himself for the measure of a parsah. This is not precise. Only according to the Rif's text, which reads in the Gemara that it is permitted initially, and since the Bach rules like him, did the Bach write that the case is where the time for prayer will pass. But according to the text of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah, that it is forbidden, and this is how we rule, the Bach forbids it initially even if the time for prayer will pass. This is clear. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halacha, the Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps one may rely on the Rif in this matter, and it appears to me that I saw in the Agudah that he ruled like the Rif. It appears to me, however, that even if prayer with the congregation will pass, he should relieve himself first. See above, siman 58, seif katan 7, that prayer in its proper time is preferable to prayer with the congregation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is better, etc.</b> This implies that if he wishes he need not interrupt. At first glance, he is obligated to interrupt so that he not violate Bal Teshaktzu. Perhaps because Bal Teshaktzu is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, we do not obligate him to interrupt prayer, for that too is a prohibition. Similarly, Terumas HaDeshen, siman 16, wrote that where there is disgrace and degradation, we are not concerned because of Bal Teshaktzu. It appears more correct that he wrote, 'it is better to interrupt,' in order to teach that if he transgressed and prayed, his prayer is valid, since the need arose in the middle of prayer; but initially he is obligated to interrupt. However, I examined the responsum of the Rashba itself, siman 131, and it is explicit there that even if his urge is so strong that Bal Teshaktzu applies, he should restrain himself. This is astonishing regarding the Rama, who explained the Rashba as speaking where he does not have such a strong urge. Therefore it appears to me that the first approach is primary. Possibly because the Rama was uncertain in his interpretation of the Rashba, he wrote the language, 'it is better, etc.' Afterward the Magen Avraham was printed, and I found that he disagreed with the Rama, maintaining that it is always forbidden to interrupt based on the above responsum of the Rashba. But what he wrote, that Terumas HaDeshen also holds this way, does not appear so from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 16, and this requires study.

[ה] <b>יותר ממיל וכו'.</b> ומשמע דמיל עצמו חוזר, ותמהו עליו לחם חמודות ונחלת צבי דף י"ד דשביק גירסת רי"ף והרא"ש דמיל עצמו אינו חוזר ופסק דנראה ממשמעות הרמב"ם פרק ד' מהלכות תפילה, ועוד דברמב"ם גופיה יש לפרש כדברי רי"ף וכמו שכתב כסף משנה גופיה ואם כן אין מי שפסק דמיל עצמו חוזר ולכן פסק כן להלכה נגד השולחן ערוך עיין שם. אבל אני מצאתי בכלבו דף ז' ובספר צידה לדרך דף מ"ג שפסקו דמיל עצמו חוזר וכן משמע בספר חסידים סימן י"ח וכן עיקר, וכן פירש לחם משנה דברי רמב"ם, אך צריך עיון רב בדבריו הא גריס שם כגירסת הרי"ף ודע דבכלבו מיירי בשיכול לבוא למחוז חפצו וכו' כדלעיל סימן צ' סעיף י"ז. כתב בתשובת בית יעקב סימן ל"ה דרוכב דינו בזה כמהלך ברגליו ואין חילוק בין דרך רע לטוב, ועיין סימן רמ"ט ס"ק ג':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>More than a mil, etc.</b> This implies that for exactly a mil he must return. Lechem Chamudos and Nachalas Tzvi, page 14, wondered at him, for he abandoned the text of the Rif and the Rosh, according to which for exactly a mil he does not return, and ruled based on what appears from Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah. Furthermore, even Rambam himself may be explained like the Rif, as the Kesef Mishneh himself wrote. If so, there is no one who ruled that for exactly a mil he must return. Therefore they ruled halachically against the Shulchan Aruch; see there. But I found in Kol Bo, page 7, and in Seidah LaDerech, page 43, that they ruled that for exactly a mil he must return. So too is implied in Sefer Chasidim, siman 18, and this is primary. So too the Lechem Mishneh explained Rambam's words, though his words require great study, for he reads there like the Rif's text. Know that the Kol Bo is speaking where he can still arrive at his desired destination, etc., as above in siman 90, seif 17. Beis Yaakov responsa, siman 35, wrote that a rider's law in this respect is like one walking on foot, and there is no distinction between a bad road and a good one. See siman 249, seif katan 3.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he fears, etc.</b> In Nachalas Tzvi he wondered about the Levush and Shulchan Aruch, for the Rif and Rambam, who rule that one must go back four mil, hold that even if there is no time and the time for prayer will pass, he must go back after water; the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush therefore contradict each other. I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that the words of Lechem Chamudos there escaped him, for he resolves this well, namely, not to rule like them when there is no time. Afterward I saw in the Taz that he wondered that, on the contrary, according to the Rif there is a stringency that it is forbidden to pray until he has water. In my humble opinion, it is a greater stringency that he not let the time pass, since cleaning with something that cleans is possible; therefore the Shulchan Aruch relied in this matter on Tosafot and the Rosh. It also appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch holds that when only the beginning of the time for prayer will pass, he must return, but when he fears that the entire time for prayer will pass, even the Rif and Rambam agree that he should clean his hands with a pebble. Here the Shulchan Aruch is speaking where the entire time for prayer will pass. So too is implied in the Bach below, siman 233, regarding the opinion of Rav Amram, although it does not seem so from the Beit Yosef. Now the Bach disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch and wrote that since one need not go back after water for Krias Shema according to everyone, one should not go back for Shacharis prayer either, since one must join redemption to prayer. It appears that he follows his own reasoning below in siman 233, where he wrote that the primary view follows Rashi, that one need not go back for prayer, only that stringently we are concerned for the Rif and Rambam. Whenever something is not a mitzvah by law, one may not interrupt at Shirah Chadashah, for only for the need of a mitzvah, such as answering Amen Yehei Shemei Rabbah and Kedushah, may one interrupt, as above in siman 66. Consider this carefully. However, according to the Beis Yaakov, that the primary view follows the Rif and Rambam, perhaps one may interrupt at Shirah Chadashah. It is also possible that he holds that even according to the Rif and Rambam one may not interrupt there, because of joining redemption to prayer.

[ז] <b>ואם אין מים מזומנים וכו'.</b> משמע דאף ביש בהן לכלוך אם אין לו מים מזומנים מנקה וכו', וכן נראה דעת לחם חמודות דף י"ד דאף בלכלוך אין צריך לחזור ארבע מילין כיון דכבר נטל שחרית סמכינן על הפוסקים דאין צריך לחזור ארבע מילין. אבל בעולת תמיד לא ראה דבריו והבין דברי שולחן ערוך דמיירי שאין לכלוך דוקא, גם הקשה על השולחן ערוך דלא מצינו לאחד מפוסקים שחילקו בכך. ובאמת לדינא אין להקל בלכלוך כי משמע בשולחן ערוך דלכלוך חמירא מהסיח הדעת כדעת יש אומרים בטור ואם כן דוקא בהסיח הדעת סמך השולחן ערוך על הפוסקים דאין צריך לחזור ד' מילין, והנה במגן אברהם סימן ק"ג משמע שגם בהסיח הדעת צריך לחזור כשיש שהות ואינו מכוין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if water is not available, etc.</b> This implies that even if there is dirt on his hands, if water is not available he cleans them, etc. So too appears to be the view of Lechem Chamudos, page 14, that even where there is dirt he need not go back four mil, since he already washed in the morning and we rely on the poskim who hold that one need not go back four mil. But the Olas Tamid did not see his words, and understood the Shulchan Aruch as speaking specifically where there is no dirt. He also challenged the Shulchan Aruch, saying that we did not find any posek who made such a distinction. In truth, as a matter of practical halacha, one should not be lenient where there is dirt, for the Shulchan Aruch implies that dirt is more severe than mere distraction, in accordance with the view cited as 'some say' in the Tur. If so, only where there was distraction did the Shulchan Aruch rely on the poskim who say that one need not go back four mil. Now in Magen Avraham, siman 103, it is implied that even with mere distraction he must go back when there is time, and he is not intending otherwise.

[ט] <b>כדי הילוך וכו'.</b> פירש מעדני מלך דף כ"ד אפילו יבלעו בקרקע, עד כאן ודחוק. אבל בכלבו ראיתי דף ח' ז"ל המשתין בכלי או במקום גבוה ולמטה שאין צריך להרחיק בשביל מים שהוא וכו'. מותר להשתין במקום שהתפלל ומכל מקום מדת חסידות שלא להשתין בתוך ארבע של תפילה, מגילה דף כ"ז ר"י ט"ו. כתב בספר חסידים סימן תתכ"ח שאין להשתין או לרקוק במקום שכיסה דם חיה ועוף בברכה בעוד הדם מכוסה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The time it takes to walk, etc.</b> Maadanei Melech, page 24, explained that this applies even if they are absorbed into the ground. This is forced. But in Kol Bo, page 8, I saw the following wording: One who urinates into a vessel, or on an elevated place with something lower beneath it, need not distance himself because of the water, etc. It is permitted to urinate in the place where one prayed. Nevertheless, it is an attribute of piety not to urinate within four cubits of the place of prayer, Megillah 27, R. Y. 15. Sefer Chasidim, siman 828, wrote that one should not urinate or spit in the place where he covered the blood of a wild animal or bird with a blessing while the blood remains covered.

[י] <b>משום ניצוצי וכו'.</b> כתב מלבושי יום טוב פשיטא וצריכא למימרא, ועוד כדי ארבע אמות ומיגרע ממים שותתים בסימן ע"ח שממתין עד שיכלה, ורש"י פירש שלא יטנפו בגדים בניצוצות שבאמתו, עד כאן. ונראה לי דזה קא משמע לן דאחר שהשתין מקלחות ניצוצות כדי הילוך ארבע אמות ועד שיכלו דלעיל נמי פירשו שיכלו לגמרי דהיינו שימתין מתחילת השתנתו עד סוף עיין אליהו זוטא מזה עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because of the droplets, etc.</b> Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: This is obvious, and did it need to be stated? Also, why four cubits? Is it worse than flowing water in siman 78, where he waits until it ceases? Rashi explained that the concern is that his garments may become soiled by droplets from his organ. It appears to me that this teaches that after he urinates in streams, there are droplets for the time it takes to walk four cubits. And what was said above, 'until they cease,' is also explained to mean that they cease completely, meaning that he waits from the beginning of his urination until the end. See more on this in Eliyahu Zuta.
