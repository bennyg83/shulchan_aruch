<b> היה צריך לנקביו וכו'</b> -  וש"ץ שהוא צריך לנקביו קודם חזרת הש"ץ אם יכול להעמיד עצמו עד פרסה כיון דבדיעבד תפלתו תפלה ויש בזה משום כבוד הבריות פשוט דמותר לסמוך בזה על שיטת הרי"ף המיקל לכתחילה בשיעור פרסה ונראה דאף המחמירין מודין כאן מפני כבוד הבריות אבל אם אין יכול להעמיד עצמו בשיעור פרסה דלכו"ע תפלתו תועבה מהו ואין להביא ראיה להקל ממה דהסכים המגן אברהם באם נתעורר לו תאוה בצבור באמצע התפלה עד דאית ביה משום בל תשקצו דאעפ"כ מותר לו לגמור התפלה משום דכבוד הבריות דוחה להאיסור דבל תשקצו וא"כ ה"נ בענינינו דלכאורה איסור הנצרך לנקביו לתפלה הוא ג"כ רק איסור דרבנן מ"מ אינו דומה דשאני התם דאיסור שאסרו חז"ל על הנצרך לנקביו להתפלל ואמרו דתפלתו תועבה אין שם כיון דבדק עצמו מתחלה והתחיל בהיתר ותדע דאף רמ"א המחמיר מודה שם דבדיעבד אם לא הפסיק תפלתו תפלה משום האי טעמא וכמש"כ הע"ת משא"כ בענינינו דצריך לנקביו קודם שמתחיל להתפלל דגזרו חז"ל ע"ז ואמרו דתפלתו תועבה וא"כ כל ברכותיו לבטלה ויש בהן משום לא תשא אפשר דאין להקל משום כבוד הבריות ואולי סלקו חז"ל איסרם מזה וממילא אין הברכה לבטלה וצע"ג. ואם נזדמן לו כן להקורא בס"ת כשעלה לבימה פשוט דמותר לו לקרוא דשם בדיעבד קריאתו קריאה וכדלקמן וכ"ש אם כבר התחיל לקרוא ונתעורר לו תאוה דמותר לו לגמור את כל הקריאה וכדלקמן בס"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he needed to relieve himself, etc.</b> If a chazzan needs to relieve himself before the repetition of the Amidah, and he can restrain himself for the time required to walk a parsah, then since after the fact his prayer is valid, and since human dignity is involved, it is clearly permissible to rely here upon the Rif's lenient view, which permits this initially when he can restrain himself for a parsah. It appears that even those who are stringent would agree here because of human dignity. However, if he cannot restrain himself for the time required to walk a parsah, in which case all agree that his prayer is an abomination, the matter is uncertain. One cannot prove leniency from the Magen Avraham's agreement that if the urge arises while one is praying with the congregation, to the point that remaining would violate bal teshaktzu, he may nevertheless finish the prayer because human dignity overrides the prohibition of bal teshaktzu. Although the prohibition against praying when one needs to relieve himself also appears to be only Rabbinic, the cases are not comparable. There, the prohibition instituted by the Sages against praying while needing to relieve oneself, and their statement that such a prayer is an abomination, do not apply, because he checked himself beforehand and began permissibly. Indeed, even the Rama, who is stringent, agrees there that after the fact, if he did not interrupt, his prayer is valid for this reason, as the Olas Tamid writes. Here, however, he needs to relieve himself before beginning to pray, which is the case regarding which the Sages decreed and said that his prayer is an abomination. Consequently, all his blessings may be in vain and may involve the prohibition of taking Hashem's Name in vain. It is therefore possible that one may not be lenient because of human dignity. Perhaps, however, the Sages removed their prohibition in such a case, in which event the blessings would not be in vain. This requires great study. If this occurs to one called to the Torah after he has ascended the bimah, it is clear that he may read, because after the fact his reading is valid, as explained below. Certainly, if he already began reading and the urge then arose, he may complete the entire reading, as explained below in seif 2.

<b> וצריך לחזור ולהתפלל</b> -  עיין מ"ב לענין קטנים דהי"א קמא והוא המגן אברהם וח"א סוברין שא"צ לחזור ולברך בדיעבד והי"א בתרא והוא הא"ר והדה"ח הסכימו דצריך לחזור ולברך ואין כח בידינו להכריע ביניהם כי אף דהא"ר הביא בשם תשובת הרשב"א ותשובת רמ"א ומע"מ דסוברין כוותיה עיין במגן גיבורים ובספר מאמר מרדכי שהביאו הרבה שיטות הראשונים דסוברים כוותיה דהמ"א ולדעתם אפילו אם לא יכול לגמור תפלתו דבאמצע התחילו מי רגליו להיות שותתין על ברכיו אפ"ה אח"כ כשפוסקין המי רגלים וצריך לחזור לתפלתו אין צריך לחזור רק למקום שפסק אפילו אם היה יודע מתחלה שצריך לנקביו עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He must repeat the prayer.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding urination. The first opinion, namely the Magen Avraham and Chayei Adam, maintains that after the fact he need not repeat the blessing. The latter opinion, namely the Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChaim, agrees that he must repeat the blessing. We lack the ability to decide between them. Although the Eliyah Rabbah cites Responsa Rashba, Responsa Rama, and Maadanei Yom Tov as supporting his view, see Magen Gibborim and Maamar Mordechai, who cite many Rishonim who support the Magen Avraham. According to them, even if he could not finish his prayer because urine began flowing down his knees in the middle, when the flow ceases and he must resume his prayer, he returns only to the place where he stopped, even if he knew beforehand that he needed to relieve himself. See there.

<b> שיעור פרסה</b> -  עיין מ"ב והא דלא נקט בהדיא שיעור שעה וחומש כתב בחידושי רע"א בשם הגינת ורדים דבא להורות דאף אם ע"י שיושב במקום אחד יכול להעמיד עצמו מעט יותר מ"מ משערינן כאלו הוא מהלך שאין יכול להעמיד עצמו ולילך עד פרסה. וטעם לשיעור פרסה עיין בב"ח וע"ת ופרישה ובח"א ראיתי טעם חדש בזה והוא דשיערו חז"ל דאם אינו יכול להעמיד עצמו שיעור זה בודאי כבר נהפך המאכל במעיו לצואה וכתיב וכל קרבי את שם קדשו לכך צריך לחזור ולהתפלל ולא נהירא דלפי דבריו מה יעשה עם תשובת הרשב"א דסובר דאפילו לקטנים בדיעבד תפלתו תועבה ועוד דא"כ אם שיער בעצמו שיכול לעצור שיעור פרסה ועבר והתפלל ואחר התפלה תיכף הלך ועשה צרכיו יתבטל התפלה משום זה ולא שמענו לאחד שיסבור כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The time required to walk a parsah.</b> See Mishnah Berurah. Regarding why the Mechaber did not explicitly state one hour and one fifth, Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes in his novellae, citing Ginas Veradim, that this teaches that even if, by sitting in one place, he could restrain himself somewhat longer, we assess him as though he were walking and could not restrain himself for the distance of a parsah. For the reason behind the measure of a parsah, see Bach, Olas Tamid, and Perishah. I saw a novel explanation in Chayei Adam: the Sages assessed that if he cannot restrain himself for this length of time, the food in his intestines has certainly already become excrement, and the verse states, “and all my innards shall bless His holy Name.” Therefore he must repeat the prayer. This is not convincing. According to him, how can one account for Responsa Rashba, which maintains that even regarding urination, after the fact his prayer is an abomination? Furthermore, if he assessed that he could restrain himself for a parsah, transgressed and prayed, and immediately after prayer relieved himself, would the prayer thereby be invalidated? We have not heard of anyone who maintains this.

<b> אפילו בד"ת</b> -  עיין מ"ב לענין ק"ש בדיעבד וממילא דכ"ש לשאר ברכות ואפילו לבהמ"ז ואף דבסימן קפ"ה במ"ז אות ב' משמע דבק"ש פשיטא לי' לבעל לחם יהודא דחוזר אף בדיעבד אם אין יכול להעמיד שיעור פרסה ונסתפק לו שם רק לענין בהמ"ז האמת הוא כמו שכתב הפמ"ג כאן דכן מצאתי בעזה"י במלחמות בסוגיא דשהה כדי לגמור את כולה ברכות כ"ג עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even in words of Torah.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding Krias Shema after the fact. It follows that this certainly applies to other blessings, even Bircas HaMazon. Although in siman 185, Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 2, it appears that the Lechem Yehudah considered it obvious that one must repeat Krias Shema after the fact if he could not restrain himself for a parsah, and was uncertain there only regarding Bircas HaMazon, the truth is as Peri Megadim writes here. With Hashem's help, I found this stated in Milchamos in the sugya concerning one who paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, Berachos 23. See there.

<b> אם באמצע תפלתו נתעורר וכו'</b> -  עיין בחידושי רע"א שנשאר בקושיא על ראיית הרשב"א דאינו ראיה וקוטב קושיתו אזלא לפי מה דפסיקא ליה דאם התפלל נגד מי רגלים בדיעבד תפלתו תפלה אולם לפי מה שהבאנו לעיל בסימן ע"ט הרבה דיעות שחולקים ע"ז ניחא בפשיטות. אולם אי קשיא לי על השו"ע קשיא לי דלמה הביאו לרשב"א לדינא דהלא עיקר ראייתו ממה דפשיטא ליה דאין לו לזוז ממקומו באמצע תפלתו אפילו אם נזדמן שמי רגלים אצלו באמצע תפלתו וכמו שמובא במ"א וביאור הגר"א וא"כ לפי מה שפסק השו"ע בסימן צ' סעיף כ"ז בזה דלא כהרשב"א וכמו שכתבו האחרונים דדין זה הוא דלא כהרשב"א וחזר הב"י ממה שהסכים עמו בסימן ע"ח א"כ אזיל ליה ממילא ראייתו דהרשב"א לדין זה ואמאי העתיקו השו"ע בסימן זה לדינא ואולי דהב"י מסתבר ליה טעמא דהרשב"א דכיון שכבר התחיל בהיתר בלי ראיה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If the urge arose in the middle of his prayer, etc.</b> See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who leaves the Rashba's proof as a difficulty because he considers it no proof. The central point of his question depends upon his assumption that if one prayed in the presence of urine, his prayer is valid after the fact. According to the many opinions cited above in siman 79 that disagree, however, the matter is readily resolved. Yet I have a difficulty with the Shulchan Aruch. Why did it cite the Rashba's ruling? His principal proof rests upon what he considered obvious, that one may not move from his place during the Amidah even if urine appears near him in the middle of the prayer, as cited by Magen Avraham and Biur HaGra. But the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 90, seif 27, contrary to the Rashba on this point, as the Acharonim write, and the Beit Yosef retracted there from what he had accepted in siman 78. The Rashba's proof for this ruling therefore falls away. Why, then, did the Shulchan Aruch reproduce it here as law? Perhaps the Beit Yosef found the Rashba's reasoning independently compelling, since he began permissibly, even without the proof.

<b> יעמיד עצמו עד שיגמור</b> -  עיין מ"ב דאין מותר מן הדין רק ק"ש וברכותיה ולא יותר ופשוט הוא דהוא כעין מה ששנינו במגילה כ"ג אין פורסין על שמע ואין עוברין לפני התיבה ואין נושאין כפים ואין קורין בתורה ואין מפטירין בנביא פחות מעשרה ועי"ש בר"ן שכתב בשם הירושלמי דאתא לאשמועינן דכל אלו הם כ"א ענין בפני עצמו ולכך אם התחיל אחד מהן בעשרה ויצאו מקצתן דקי"ל דגומרין אין רשאי רק לגמור אותו הדבר ולא להתחיל ענין אחר ומנה תידוק גם בענינינו דסובר הרשב"א דכיון דהתחיל כבר אותו הדבר כהוגן שבדק עצמו מתחלה מותר לו שוב לגמור את הדבר דעכ"פ אסור לו להתחיל דבר אחר מכל אלו הנזכרים במשנה וכל כה"ג וממילא פשוט דבק"ש דהתיר הרשב"א לקרוא כדרכו כיון שהתחיל בהיתר הוא רק עד שמ"ע ולא שמ"ע גופא דהוא ענין אחר וכנ"ל במשנה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should restrain himself until he finishes.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, that by law the permission extends only through Krias Shema and its blessings, and no further. This is clear, for it resembles what we learned in Megillah 23: one does not lead the responsive recitation of Shema, pass before the ark, recite Bircas Kohanim, read from the Torah, or read the Haftarah with fewer than ten. See Ran there, who writes in the name of the Yerushalmi that each of these is a separate matter. Therefore, if one of them began with ten and some departed, although the law is that they finish, they may finish only that matter and may not begin another. From this one may infer similarly here. The Rashba holds that because he began that matter properly, having checked himself beforehand, he may finish that same matter, but may not begin another of the matters listed in the Mishnah or anything comparable. It is therefore clear that when the Rashba permits him to continue Krias Shema in the ordinary manner because he began permissibly, the permission extends only until the Amidah, but not to the Amidah itself, which is a different matter, as explained from the Mishnah.

<b> קורא כדרכו</b> -  פשוט דה"ה אם התחיל לשמוע קריאת התורה וקודם לזה לא היה צריך לנקביו ובאמצע נתעורר לו תאוה קצת באופן שאין בו משום בל תשקצו מותר לו לשמוע כל קה"ת ולענות ברוך ד' הלע"ו [עד מפטיר שנקרא ענין אחר וכדלעיל בסימן נ"ה במ"ב] כיון שהתחיל בהיתר וכן ה"ה באמצע הלל ומגילה וברכת המזון וכל כי האי גוונא מותר לו לגמור את הענין כדרכו כיון שאינו מתאוה כל כך שיהא בו משום בל תשקצו ונראה דהוא הדין בדברי תורה אם הוא לומד לעצמו וקודם הלימוד לא היה צריך לנקביו ובאמצע ענינא נתעורר לו תאוה קצת אף שהוא משער בעצמו שאין יכול לעצור א"ע עד שיעור פרסה אפ"ה מותר לו לגמור את הענין ואין לדחות דשם בק"ש עם ברכותיה הוא הכל ענין אחד ושייך לומר דמותר לגמור הענין כיון שכבר התחילו וכן בקה"ת ובכל הנ"ל משא"כ בלימוד התורה מה שייך בזה גמר ואין שייך לומר דיהיה בזה עדיף מחמת התחלה דז"א דגם בד"ת מצינו שיש בזה יתרון מחמת שהתחיל כבר עיין שבת י"א ע"א בגמרא ובמ"א סימן ק"ו סק"ו ועכ"פ נ"ל דאם עי"ז שיפסיק עתה יוגרם לו אח"כ ביטול תורה בודאי יגמור את הענין ובפרט אם הוא רק התעוררות בעלמא ויכול לעצור עצמו עד פרסה דאין לו להחמיר בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He reads in the usual manner.</b> It is clear that the same applies if one began listening to the Torah reading, did not need to relieve himself beforehand, and then a mild urge arose during the reading, in a manner that does not involve bal teshaktzu. He may listen to the entire Torah reading and answer “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever,” until Maftir, which is considered a separate matter, as explained above in siman 55 in Mishnah Berurah, because he began permissibly. The same applies in the middle of Hallel, Megillah, Bircas HaMazon, and similar matters. He may finish that matter in the usual manner, provided his urge is not so strong that bal teshaktzu applies. It appears that the same is true of Torah study. If he is studying privately, did not need to relieve himself before beginning, and a mild urge arose in the middle of the subject, then even if he estimates that he cannot restrain himself for the time required to walk a parsah, he may nevertheless finish the subject. One cannot reject this by saying that Krias Shema and its blessings constitute one matter and may therefore be completed once begun, and the same is true of Torah reading and the other cases, whereas Torah study has no defined completion and therefore gains no advantage from having begun. That is incorrect, because even regarding Torah study we find an advantage in having already begun; see Shabbos 11a and Magen Avraham, siman 106, seif katan 6. At minimum, it appears to me that if interrupting now will cause subsequent neglect of Torah study, he may certainly complete the subject, especially where it is only a mild urge and he can restrain himself for a parsah.

<b>It is better to interrupt.</b> If he interrupted, relieved himself, and thereby paused long enough to complete the entire Amidah, all agree that he returns to the beginning. The same applies to Krias Shema according to the Rama's ruling above in siman 65. If he did not pause that long, however, the matter is not fully clear to me. Seemingly, he should afterward return to the beginning. Even the opinion in Berachos 23a that when urine is flowing down his knees he returns only to the place where he stopped explains that this is because the person remains fit. The Rosh and Rashba explain in a responsum that even while the urine is flowing, he is fit by Torah law. This implies that otherwise, since he was disqualified for a time, he may no longer resume. In our case, while actually relieving himself, he is forbidden by Torah law to recite sacred words. He should therefore be considered disqualified according to all opinions. This is unlike walking through filthy alleyways, where one returns to the beginning only if he paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, as stated in siman 85, because there the person remains fit and only the place is unfit, as the Rosh writes. In truth, this can be rejected. The reasoning that the person is disqualified may apply only together with the fact that he should not initially have begun praying because he already needed to relieve himself, as is evident in the Rosh. Here, however, he checked himself beforehand. This answer may resolve the matter according to the Rosh, but not according to the Rashba, who interprets the baraisa concerning urine flowing down one's knees as a case where he had not needed to relieve himself beforehand and the condition arose only in the middle, yet the Gemara still requires the reasoning that he is fit by Torah law. One may further argue that Rabbeinu Yonah implies there that according to the opinion that one who paused long enough to complete the entire prayer does not return to the beginning, no Amora holds that one who paused for less than that returns to the beginning because of compulsion. The Rosh likewise appears to understand this. If so, the same applies here according to our accepted ruling. All this pressure is necessary according to their interpretation. According to Rashi, however, who explains that “the person was fit” means that he was fit when he initially began to pray, it is possible that even if he interrupted in the middle for a condition that, by Torah law, forbade him from reciting sacred words, he is nevertheless called a fit person. This also appears from Chayei Adam, who writes that one should not speak while relieving himself in the middle of prayer, implying that he does not return to the beginning. It appears that he derived this from the general language of the Poskim, who write that if one paused long enough to complete the entire prayer because of compulsion, whether because the person was unfit or the place was unfit, he returns to the beginning. They do not limit this to a case where the person's unfitness was only Rabbinic, like urine flowing down his knees. This implies that even where the prohibition is Torah-level, he does not return to the beginning unless the interruption lasted long enough to complete the entire prayer.

<b>He must wash, etc.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote in the name of Rambam concerning the law after the fact. With hands of unspecified status, it is clear that he need not repeat the prayer. Although Rambam appears to hold that the requirement is indispensable even for hands of unspecified status, as Peri Megadim writes, this makes no practical difference for us. Beit Yosef already explained that we do not recite a blessing when washing hands of unspecified status because Ran and the alternative opinion cited by Tur maintain that such hands are fit for prayer, and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we are lenient. Certainly, then, one should not repeat the prayer. Apart from this, it is difficult to determine Rambam's precise intent. Regarding each of the other four prerequisites, Rambam explicitly explains that even after the fact one must repeat the prayer, whereas here he says nothing. This indicates that when he calls it indispensable, he means that the prayer was not conducted properly because the hands were soiled, but not that one must repeat it. It therefore appears to me that even according to Rambam, if one's hands were soiled with traces of perspiration because he scratched his head or touched a covered part of the body, although initially he may not recite Krias Shema, pray, or even recite a blessing, as explained above at the end of siman 4, after the fact he does not repeat. I found some support for this in Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, halachah 11: “There was excrement, etc., or his hands were soiled from a privy, etc.” See there. This ruling is cited above by Magen Avraham in siman 76, seif katan 3. Krias Shema and prayer have the same law regarding soiled hands, as appears from Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 2, and chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema. That passage certainly concerns a case without washing, yet Krias Shema is not invalidated. Although initially one may certainly not read because he has not washed his hands, Rambam does not address that point there, because he is discussing only the law of Krias Shema and its after-the-fact validity. If the lack of washing invalidated the reading even after the fact, the ruling would have no application. Rambam concludes there that several Geonim ruled that it is forbidden and that this is the proper practice, but this too refers only to the initial requirement, as Kesef Mishneh proves there. I saw that Peri Megadim above, siman 76, seif katan 3, wished to innovate that only another person facing him may read, but the person himself may not. According to that, it is possible that even after the fact he must repeat because he lacked washing. This does not appear from Rambam, who is the source of the ruling. See there. This is what appears correct to me.

<b>But if he must go back, etc.</b> If one is sitting at home, it is clear that his law is the same as with washing the hands, where Chayei Adam rules that it is treated like water behind him and he need seek it only up to one mil. See there. The Gemara states one teaching concerning both matters, and many laws for our subject are learned from washing the hands; see Divrei Chamudos in the chapter Hayah Korei. This is also evident from Perishah. Only according to Tosafos need one not seek water at home if he has none. According to Rif and Rambam, whose view the Shulchan Aruch follows, one at home must also seek water, with the same limit as water behind him. The words of Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 2, suggesting in one approach that the Mechaber's leniency in siman 233, seif 2, is because the person is at home, following Perishah, are not convincing. If so, one traveling would likewise not have to turn back up to one mil, for the two laws are interdependent, as Perishah explains. In letter 3 of Mishbetzos Zahav, Peri Megadim also appears to retract this, writing that if one touches a covered part of the body in the middle of Krias Shema or Pesukei DeZimrah, he must go to water available in the city and wash his hands. This is presented as obvious. It implies that even if water is not immediately before him, since his hands are soiled and he knows that water exists in the city, he must seek it. Yet the opposite question then arises: should he not have to go back for water up to one mil? Perishah's leniency for one at home was written only according to Tosafos, whereas according to the Mechaber's ruling like Rif and Rambam, he should have to go back up to one mil. Perhaps Peri Megadim adopted a compromise between these opinions, ruling that one must seek water available in the city. The common practice is not to follow Peri Megadim's stringency by interrupting Pesukei DeZimrah to seek water available somewhere in the city, since in most cases this would cause him to lose prayer with the congregation. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah have already been lenient in this matter, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 20.

<b> ועיין בסי' צ"ז</b> -  עיין במ"ב ובא"ר מסתפק דאפשר תיכף לאחר התפלה חמירא טפי ואסור בכל גווני עד שישהא דבזה לא שייך הטעם משום טרדת הלב ומסתימת לשון השו"ע משמע להקל בזה כהתר"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See siman 97.</b> See Mishnah Berurah. Eliyah Rabbah is uncertain whether immediately after prayer the law may be more stringent, so that it is forbidden in all circumstances until one waits, since the reason of distraction does not apply then. The plain language of the Shulchan Aruch implies leniency in this matter, following Rabbeinu Yonah.
