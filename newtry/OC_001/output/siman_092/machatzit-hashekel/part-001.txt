(Comment 1) And one must [return] — as our master taught in the Gemara on page 23: the Tanna taught, If one was standing in prayer and urine was dripping onto his garments, he interrupts until the urine stops. And they said: When he returns, to which place must he return? Rav Huna and Rav Chisda — one said to the place where he stopped, and one said to the beginning of the prayer. And they conclude: If he waited long enough to finish the entire [prayer], all agree he returns to the beginning; they disagree only when he did not wait — one master holds he is a person set aside (Rashi: when he began to pray and needed to relieve himself, he is set aside from praying; therefore what he prayed is not prayer and he returns to the beginning, etc.), and one master holds he is a fit person and his prayer counts (Rashi: even though he cannot wait to urinate until he finishes his prayer, what he prayed before urinating is valid prayer, etc.). And we rule like the one who said he returns to the place where he stopped. Above in siman 78 it is clear that even if he felt before prayer that he needs to urinate — so why would the other view think he returns to the beginning? Nevertheless we rule he does not return except to the place he stopped, and what he prayed initially counts for him. On the Rif who has this text in the Gemara, see Beit Yosef.

And it seems to me that one should rely on the Rashba — meaning: since now, according to Magen Avraham's view, the Rashba and Terumat HaDeshen disagree, and per the Rashba even when there is bal teshaktzu he does not interrupt whereas Terumat HaDeshen interrupts — therefore he wrote that one should rely on the Rashba and not interrupt. And it is possible that even Terumat HaDeshen forbade only sneezing, which is permitted, etc. — meaning it is not a full interruption; but attending to one's needs, which is a full interruption, he agrees with the Rashba that one does not interrupt. If so, it is also possible the reverse regarding sneezing: the Rashba agrees with Terumat HaDeshen — but in siman 103 Magen Avraham did not write thus; see what he wrote in Be'er Heitev. Such is the presentation of Magen Avraham's words; but apparently in Terumat HaDeshen's words the opposite is clear (and I likewise found in Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote, etc., that what Magen Avraham wrote — that Terumat HaDeshen also holds thus — is not implied thus in Terumat HaDeshen siman 57, etc.) — that he wrote regarding the fact that even meticulous people do not act thus when one wishes to sneeze during prayer: to distance four cubits and say Master of the universe, as explained below in siman 103. And he wrote that one of the great authorities said: when one feels an urge to sneeze during prayer, he restrains himself so as not to sneeze; and the author of Terumat HaDeshen objected: we say that one who restrains his orifices violates bal teshaktzu. And it is satisfactory to me, for the Rambam wrote: if one wished to expel wind from below, etc., and cannot restrain himself for the walk of four cubits, etc. — behold we learned explicitly that if he can restrain himself he does well. And the reason may be that they were not concerned for bal teshaktzu except when relieving oneself for defecation or urination, but for passing gas they did not decree and forbid; nevertheless, one who cannot restrain himself — I do not know a clear side of permission not to distance four cubits, except because he would be disgraced and ashamed before the public that they would perceive, etc. Therefore the matter in which the Sages obligated to distance four cubits and say Master of the universe applies specifically to one praying alone, etc. And there is also some support that on account of such disgrace and contempt they were not concerned for a rabbinic prohibition, for we find several Amoraim who restrained their orifices when sitting before their teacher — there is disgrace to go attend to one's needs while before their teacher; they were not concerned for the prohibition of bal teshaktzu. The same applies in our case. Nevertheless the matter is very difficult — to invent distinctions from one's own reasoning that are not hinted at in the Talmud and rulings of the Geonim from this word alone, etc., and I copied most of Terumat HaDeshen's words.

Also in Beit Yosef below in siman 103 they copied briefly, explained from the beginning of Terumat HaDeshen's words: after he brought proof from the Rambam to permit restraining oneself from passing gas, he wrote the reason — perhaps there is no bal teshaktzu except when relieving oneself for defecation or urination, but for passing gas they did not forbid. Behold he wrote that in sneezing there is no bal teshaktzu except for defecation or urination. And how did Magen Avraham write the reverse — that for defecation and urination Terumat HaDeshen agrees not to interrupt and restrain himself, and forbade only restraining oneself from passing gas? However, in my humble opinion Magen Avraham understood from Terumat HaDeshen's words: even though to restrain oneself for defecation or urination there is certainly a prohibition of bal teshaktzu, nevertheless Terumat HaDeshen agrees with the Rashba that it is permitted to restrain oneself for defecation and urination when standing in prayer — for if he interrupts to attend to his needs there is a great interruption, and the prohibition of interrupting prayer is more severe than the prohibition of restraining oneself, as Magen Avraham wrote from Semag's words. If so, it is better to set aside the prohibition of restraining oneself than to set aside the prohibition of interruption; but to restrain oneself from passing gas — according to what Terumat HaDeshen holds, that even in sneezing there is bal teshaktzu — on the contrary one should be stringent in sneezing, and it is forbidden to restrain oneself from passing gas even when standing in prayer, since there is no other prohibition counterbalancing it; for if he passes gas during prayer there is no interruption, nor is walking four cubits an interruption. If so, why did they permit him to restrain himself from passing gas and transgress bal teshaktzu, until he needed to bring proof from the Rambam's wording that when restraining oneself from passing gas there is no bal teshaktzu at all — therefore he is permitted to restrain himself from passing gas in prayer even though it is not an interruption.

And what Magen Avraham wrote — that even Terumat HaDeshen forbade only sneezing, etc. — even though this is only a hypothesis in his reasoning, but in the conclusion he also permits sneezing, and as he brought proof from the Rambam, it must be according to Terumat HaDeshen's conclusion; and there is also some support from disgrace, etc., and he concluded: nevertheless the matter is difficult to invent, etc. For certainly, since Terumat HaDeshen wrote that the custom is not to distance in this, he did not doubt Terumat HaDeshen to forbid even though there is no complete proof that nothing follows custom — leave Israel alone, etc., as stated in the Yerushalmi: if the law is weak in your hand, go see how the people practice. Rather, what that great authority said — that one restrains himself from passing gas in this — he did not find a custom; and he brought proof from the Rambam's words, and Terumat HaDeshen did not have this proof from the Rambam sufficiently; therefore he wrote and there is also some support, etc., that it is permitted to restrain oneself for defecation and urination in a place of human dignity — all the more so permitted to restrain oneself from passing gas; and this is proof for that great authority's words that he would restrain himself. And on this he concluded: nevertheless the matter is difficult, etc. — meaning to permit restraining oneself from passing gas. And so it appears Rama understood, who brought below in siman 103 that in a congregation one need not distance four cubits. And it is impossible that Terumat HaDeshen also doubted this — for Rama would not have needed to write a clear permission in this; or that the doubt was only regarding that great authority's view to permit restraining oneself from passing gas — and so it appears from Magen Avraham's words below in siman 103. Therefore one should not say what Magen Avraham wrote here — that even Terumat HaDeshen forbade only sneezing, etc. — for also in Terumat HaDeshen's conclusion the matter is doubtful in Terumat HaDeshen's hand to permit restraining oneself from passing gas.
