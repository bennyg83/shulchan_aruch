**** ENGLISH ****
Even if it is made of wood and covered with leather. I have not seen anyone who practices leniently to go out with a wooden sandal (Rabbeinu Yerucham; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 342). Initially one should be stringent even regarding cloth socks, unless he has some bodily ailment. So too I saw from my teachers, who went entirely barefoot, except in a place of mitzvah, or for a delicate person, or where there is concern for danger from a scorpion. Even a delicate person should not walk in cloth socks in a courtyard that has no eruv, and certainly not in the public domain, because anything that is not a shoe is forbidden as a burden (Nimukei Yosef, chapter 48, in the name of the Ra'ah). Although the words of the Ran in the last chapter of Yoma appear to disagree with the Ra'ah, nevertheless it is proper to be stringent. There is one who wrote that even for the need of a mitzvah we do not permit an actual shoe if there is no danger from a scorpion or the like. One should not compare it to washing, where they permitted crossing through water for the need of a mitzvah, because there it is impossible otherwise, but without wearing shoes it is possible with difficulty. There is one who permits walking in a torn shoe, even if it is torn only substantially on top (Or Zarua).
