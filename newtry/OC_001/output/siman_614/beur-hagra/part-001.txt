<b>Even of wood, etc.</b> The view of Rashi is that Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosi disagree regarding the amputee's wooden leg, whether it is a shoe or not a shoe, as is written in the sugya in Shabbos and the sugya in Yevamos. And although in Yoma Rava said that according to everyone it is a shoe, the Baal ha-Maor wrote that that passage disagrees with the others. Although Rava himself said in Yevamos, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," he said that according to the reasoning of the earlier amoraim. The Baal ha-Maor wrote that since it is a dispute, we adopt stringency in all respects: for chalitzah, that it is not a shoe, and for Yom Kippur, that it is a shoe, unlike Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who went out with a sandal of cork. But this is difficult, for Rava went out with diblei. Rashi there was careful about this and wrote in the comment beginning "a shoe, etc.": "but, etc." The view of Tosafos is that the sugyos do not disagree, and their dispute is whether we decree lest it slip off, as written in Yoma. And what it says in Shabbos there, that Shmuel said, "it is Rabbi Meir," also depends on this: if it falls off, it is not fit for chalitzah, as they said, "an adult who cannot walk, etc." And what is stated in Yevamos there about covered with leather, and the comment beginning "this is the reason," is because when covered with leather it is tightened. But there is a difficulty from what is stated in Yevamos there regarding a cloth anpilya: "we have reached the Rabbis." Surely it is tightened. They answered that certainly the questioner thought they disagreed about whether it is a shoe or not a shoe, for if not, the difficulty would be: to whom will you assign an anpilya? But after Rava answered that even Rabbi Meir agrees regarding an anpilya, this is no longer necessary. Even so, in Tosafos in Yoma and in the Rashba they answered that that question and that answer in Yevamos are all according to Abaye, who said in Yoma that they disagree about this. Therefore Rava came and answered, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," since it is impossible to say that an anpilya follows only the Rabbis, as above. The view of the Ramban is that Shmuel and Rav Huna in Shabbos hold like Abaye, that they disagree whether it is a shoe, but Rava, who said there "who is not, etc.," follows his own view in Yoma. And what is stated in Yoma, "according to everyone," means Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosi, but the Rabbis disagree with them, namely Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri's Rabbis, who did not agree with him; and we rule like them. Therefore Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi and all these amoraim went out, and Rava himself in Yoma there as well. So too the sugya in Yevamos says that leather specifically is required, and "I shod you with tachash," and they ask there about straps of hair, etc. And what is stated in Yevamos, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," is not precise, for the same is true of Rabbi Yosi; it is only to exclude the Rabbis, as is stated there, "the Sifra is the Rabbis," meaning the Rabbis mentioned above. Since Rabbi Meir is mentioned explicitly there, it stated Rabbi Meir. And so what is stated there about covered with leather, and the comment beginning "this is Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri," and we rule like him, as above. The view of the Rashba, from the fact that Rava in Shabbos spoke on Rav Huna's statement and not on Shmuel's statement, is that even Rabbi Yosi is included. One must therefore say as Tosafos said, that Rabbi Yosi forbids chalitzah because it falls off unless it is covered with leather, as above. But with a lime-burners' sandal, since it is of straw, it is well tightened; and even according to that explanation that it is of wood, nevertheless it has an inside and does not fall off. Therefore he needed to establish it like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as the Ramban said and as above. With this it is resolved how they went out with them according to Rav Huna, for one might ask that it slips off, and we rule like Rabbi Yosi, and we do not find that Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri disagrees with him about this. But according to what I wrote it is fine, because Rabbi Yosi forbade only a sandal, specifically one of wood. Similarly, Abaye, who established their dispute as whether it is a shoe, also holds that the reason is that we decree lest it fall off, except that with a garment we do not decree, for if so we would decree about every garment. Rather, according to everyone we hold that we decree, but they disagree whether it is a shoe. Yet with something that is tightened, even though it is not a shoe, it is permitted. Therefore Abaye went out with dehutzei. Rava holds that they disagree even about a shoe. One should not ask: in any event, what does the Gemara ask there from "and they agree, etc.," forcing Abaye there into a difficult answer? According to Abaye, whether according to Rabbi Meir or according to Rabbi Yosi, it is forbidden to go out into the public domain on Yom Kippur. One can say that since it said "and they agree," it implies for one and the same reason. Therefore Abaye said it is because of pads, and Rava said it is because of a shoe. With this it is understandable that in Yevamos there it says that the amputee's wooden leg is specifically Rabbi Meir and not Rabbi Yosi, but one of cork or bast is even according to Rabbi Yosi, and an anpilya is only according to the Rabbis, namely Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as above. We rule like him. The practical difference is that with cork and the like it is permitted to go out into the public domain on Shabbos and on Yom Kippur. Nevertheless, the entire proof of the Ramban and the Rashba from Rava's explanation in Shabbos is not compelling, for in the Yerushalmi in Yevamos there they said: Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rabbi Yosi said, even if it is entirely of wood, it is valid. The Mishnah supports Rabbi Yosi: a sandal of straw is susceptible to midras impurity and a woman performs chalitzah with it, the words of Rabbi Akiva; but the Sages did not agree with him regarding midras. Therefore Rava in Shabbos turned aside from Rav Huna's answer. According to this, for everyone it is a shoe, and as for Rava going out with them, one can say as Rashi wrote, as above. See Milchamos there.

The Rashba wrote there that that Tosefta of Yoma mentioned above is corrupt, since it was not brought either in the Gemara or in the Yerushalmi; see there.

<b>ס"ד ואם ירדו כו' והוא אסטניס כו'. </b>ירושלמי יומא שם ותענית שם ר' יצחק בר נחמני סליק גבי ריב"ל בלילי צומא רבא נפיק לגביה לביש סולייסיה א"ל מהו הכין א"ל אסטניס אני כו' ע"ש. ומכאן כ"ש למי שיש לו מכה ואף אם נאמר דנעילת הסנדל אף שלא לשם תענוג אסור כמו בסיכה מ"מ כה"ג מותר. וכ' הרשב"א ביבמות שם דדוקא משום סכנה דאסטניס כמ"ש בירושלמי שם חד תלמיד מן דר' מנא הורי לחד מן קריבוי דנשיאה מלבש סולייסי' א"ל מן הדא א"ל אן מן דריב"ל א"ל ריב"ל אסטניס הוה ובתוספתא פרק בתרא דיומא לא ינעול אדם סנדל ויטייל בתוך הבית אפי' ממטה למטה רשב"ג מתיר וכן היה רשב"ג אומר אם היה רגליו מלוכלכות בטיט ובצואה מדיחן במים כדי שלא יטנפו רגליו את בגדיו ור"ל מטעם זה הוא ג"כ מתיר בנעילת הסנדל בכה"ג וכ' הרשב"א שם דמש"ש אסטניס ר"ל מצונן ומסוכן מחמת הצנה וכמו שהתיר ר"א לחיה ועמ"א דלא כלבוש וכ' שם דההיא תלמידא דס"ד להתיר משום דמנעל שא"ל עקב הוא וא"ל דלא כן ומה דנפיק ריב"ל משום סכנה הוא דנפיק וע' רשב"א שם ועמ"א ס"ק ה' ומ"ש פי' איש כו' ואם נטנפו כו' כ"ז כ' הרשב"א שם להדיא בפי' הירושלמי והתוספתא כנ"ל. ומ"ש דאין כל אדם כו' ע' רא"ש בספ"ב דברכות ובי"ד סי' שפ"א ס"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 4. And if rain fell, etc., and he is delicate, etc.</b> This is from the Yerushalmi in Yoma there and Ta'anis there: Rabbi Yitzchak bar Nachmani went up to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi on the night of the great fast. He went out to him wearing his soles. He said to him: What is this? He said to him: I am delicate, etc.; see there. From here there is all the more reason for one who has a wound. And even if we say that wearing shoes is forbidden even when it is not for pleasure, as with anointing, nevertheless in such a case it is permitted. The Rashba wrote in Yevamos there that this is specifically because of danger, for a delicate person, as is written in the Yerushalmi there: one of Rabbi Mana's students ruled for one of the household of the Nasi's relatives to wear soles. He said to him: From where did you get this? He said to him: From Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. He said to him: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi was delicate. And in the Tosefta, last chapter of Yoma: A person may not wear a sandal and stroll in the house, even from bed to bed. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits it. And so Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel would say: if his feet were soiled with mud or excrement, he may rinse them with water so that they should not soil his clothes. That is to say, for this reason he also permits wearing shoes in such a case. The Rashba wrote there that what it says there, "delicate," means one who is chilled and in danger because of the cold, just as Rabbi Eliezer permitted it for a woman after childbirth. See Magen Avraham, that this is unlike the Levush. And he wrote there that that student initially thought to permit because it is a shoe without a heel, and he said to him that this is not so, and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi went out because of danger. See the Rashba there and Magen Avraham subparagraph 5. And what he wrote, "the explanation is a person," etc., and "if they became soiled," etc., all of this the Rashba wrote there explicitly in his explanation of the Yerushalmi and the Tosefta, as above. And what he wrote, that not every person, etc., see the Rosh at the end of the second chapter of Berachos and in Yoreh De'ah siman 381, se'if 3.
