<b>דיני סיכה ונעיל' הסנדל. ובו ד סעיפים:</b><br><i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="א" data-order="1"></i>אסור לסוך אפילו מקצת גופו <i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="1"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="א"></i>ואפילו אינו אלא להעביר <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="1"></i>הזוהמא אבל אם הוא חולה <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ב" data-order="2"></i>אפילו אין בו סכנה או שיש לו <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ב"></i>חטטין בראשו מותר:
**** ENGLISH ****
“The laws of annointment and wearing shoes” - Containing four paragraphs. It is forbidden to annoint oneself even a little (part) of one’s body and even if it is only to remove dirt (sweat), but if one is sick, even if there is no danger or if he has rashes (scabs) on his head he is permitted (to annoint himself).

**** ENGLISH ****
It is forbidden to wear a sandal or shoe of leather — even the [prosthetic] support of an amputee and the like; even one of wood covered with leather is forbidden. But [one] of reed, or straw, or cloth, or other kinds is permitted, even to go out with into the public domain {and it is permitted to stand upon pillows and cushions of leather; nevertheless, one who is stringent — a blessing will come upon him (Mordechai, Yoma; Terumas HaDeshen, siman 149)}.

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ד" data-order="4"></i><i data-commentator="Ateret Zekenim" data-label="♦" data-order="3"></i>החיה <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="י"></i>כל שלשים יום מותרת לנעול את הסנדל <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ה" data-order="5"></i><i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="5"></i>והחולה כיוצא בו אע"פ שאין בו סכנה <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ו" data-order="6"></i>וכן מי שיש לו מכה ברגליו:
**** ENGLISH ****
One who has just given birth, during the first thirty days (of the recovery) she is permitted to wear shoes, and the sick person is just like that even though (the sick person) is not dangerously sick, and so the person who has a sore on his foot (is also allowed to put on shoes).
