**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) It is forbidden, etc.</b> “But temporarily, etc.” If the tefillin themselves are hanging, it is forbidden even temporarily.

Taz, seif katan 1, writes that when putting on the head-tefillin, one holds them by the straps while the bayis hangs in the air until it is placed upon the head. This appears permitted because it is necessary for putting them on. Magen Avraham may also agree with Taz.

Maharit writes that it is permitted to write verses upon paper and hang them at the entrance to a synagogue.

His responsum, part 2, siman 3, discusses the custom on Yom Kippur of writing verses upon paper to awaken the people to repentance, such as “For on this day He shall atone for you,” or “Hear, Israel,” and hanging them above the bimah or at the entrances of the synagogue.

He rules that there is no concern of degrading sacred verses through suspension. This is unlike hanging tefillin, which is degrading because tefillin were made to be worn upon the head and arm rather than hung. These verses, however, were written from the outset for suspension so that those who see them will look upward and direct their hearts to Heaven.

He nevertheless raises another prohibition: one may not write isolated Scriptural passages in scroll form for a child to study, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 283.

Even according to Rif, who permits this because “It is a time to act for Hashem; they have nullified Your Torah,” since it is impossible for everyone to write an entire Torah, that leniency does not necessarily apply to these displayed verses.

Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 283, seif katan 2, likewise protests against those who write verses upon walls as a favorable sign.

A practical difference remains. If one writes only three words in a single line, which is permitted under the law against writing isolated passages, it may also be suspended.

Likewise, one may write upon a synagogue wall texts that appear in siddurim, such as the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy or the verses beginning “When the Ark traveled.” Since they may be written in siddurim under the principle “It is a time to act,” relying upon Rif, they may likewise be written upon a wall. A wall and a siddur are alike in this respect, for both are partial written collections. This is the accepted custom.

As written in siman 75: although one may recite Krias Shema opposite excrement enclosed in glass, meaning a transparent vessel, one may not recite opposite nakedness enclosed in glass.

Regarding nakedness, the Torah made the prohibition dependent upon sight, as the verse states, “No naked matter shall be seen among you,” and it remains visible through the glass.

Know that Rama writes in the gloss to seif 2 that it is permitted when the inner container is not designated for the tefillin while the outer one is designated. Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Beit Yosef and Lechem Chamudos that the reverse is certainly also permitted: when the inner container is designated and the outer one is not.

<b>והב"ח ר"ל כו'</b> היינו בכלי תוך כלי וקאי על מ"ש דכנגד ראשו אפי' כלי תוך כלי אסור עז"כ אבל שלא כנגד כו' מותר והיינו בלי תוך כלי וקאי על מ"ש תחלה מ"ש בש"ע אח"ז ואם אשתו עמו צריך כלי תוך כלי הא אפי' אין אשתו עמו צריך כלי תוך כלי מפרש הב"ח דר"ל אם אשתו עמו צריך כלי תוך כלי אפי' אין התפילין במטה שישן רק תוך החדר שישן שם מ"מ כיון דאשתו עמו צריך כלי תוך כלי. ומ"ש הב"ח שכ"מ בגמרא דאמרי' בברכות דף כ"ד ע"א והיכי מנח להו (ר"ל אם התפילין מראשותיו) א"ר ירמיה בין כר לכסת שלא כנגד ראשו והתני ר' חייא מניחן בכובע תחת מראשותיו ומשני דמפיק למורשא דכובע לבר (כובע היא כיס תפילין ומורשא הוא קצהו ששם התפילין) והקשו התוס' והרר"י דמאי פריך מדתני ר"ח תחת מראשותיו דלמא ר"ח ר"ל תחת מראשותיו אבל לא כנגד ראשו כדאמר ר"י ותי' התו' והרר"י דא"כ שהם שלא כנגד ראשו למה הצריכו ר"ח ליתנן בכובע אלא ע"כ דמונחים תחת מראשותיו והבין הב"ח דמאי דמשני דאפיק למורשא דכובע לבר ואעפ"כ מונחים בין כר לכסת אלא שהתפילין לצד חוץ וקשיא ליה דאכתי קשיא ל"ל כובע כיון דאפיק למורשא כו' א"כ אינן תחת ראשו אע"כ אפי' אינן כנגד ראשו כיון דהם מראשותיו בעינן כלי תוך כלי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And Bach means, etc.</b> Meaning, with one container inside another.

The Mechaber first says that directly opposite one's head it is forbidden even with one container inside another. He then says that when not directly opposite the head it is permitted, which Bach interprets as permitted with one container inside another.

Bach then addresses the Mechaber's later phrase, “If his wife is with him, one container inside another is required.” Since, according to Bach, even when his wife is not with him a double covering is required, he explains that the Mechaber means: if his wife is with him, one container inside another is required even where the tefillin are not in the bed in which he sleeps, but only in the room.

Bach writes that this also appears from the Gemara in Berachos 24a.

The Gemara asks how tefillin should be placed at one's head. Rabbi Yirmiyah says: between the pillow and cushion, not directly opposite the head. Yet Rabbi Chiya taught that they may be placed in their pouch beneath one's pillow.

The Gemara answers that the projecting end of the pouch extends outward. The “pouch” is the tefillin bag, and the protrusion is the end containing the tefillin.

Tosafos and Rabbeinu Yonah ask why Rabbi Chiya's teaching cannot simply mean beneath the pillow but not directly opposite the head, like Rabbi Yirmiyah.

They answer that if the tefillin were not directly opposite the head, why did Rabbi Chiya require them to be in their pouch? It must therefore be that they lie beneath the pillow itself.

Bach understands the Gemara's answer, that the pouch protrudes outward, as meaning that it still lies between the pillow and cushion but the tefillin are positioned toward the outside.

He then asks: why is the pouch still required? Since the tefillin protrude outward, they are no longer directly beneath the head.

He concludes that even when they are not directly opposite the head, because they are at the head of the bed, one container inside another is required.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>This is also implied by Tosafos, page 24, at the end of s.v. “But was it not taught,” etc.</b>

After Tosafos gives the answer to its question, that perhaps Rabbi Chiya refers to tefillin not directly opposite the head and that in such a case the pouch would be unnecessary, Tosafos writes:

“Nevertheless, it is difficult. Let the Gemara answer that Rabbi Chiya refers to a case where they are not directly opposite the head and his wife is with him, so that one container within another is required, and that is why the pouch is needed. This requires study.”

If the pillow were not considered a container, what would the pouch accomplish? There would still be only one container.

It is therefore clear that Tosafos holds that the pillow counts as a container.

Magen Avraham could also have brought proof from the beginning of Tosafos's words. Tosafos says that if the tefillin are not directly opposite the head, why is the pouch necessary? Yet at least one container is required. If the pillow were not considered a container, the pouch would still be necessary.

This is not conclusive, however. A vessel designated for the tefillin counts as a vessel when only one vessel is required, but when one container within another is required, two designated containers do not suffice.

Magen Avraham therefore preferred the proof from the end of Tosafos, which shows that the pillow counts as a container even toward the requirement of one container within another.
