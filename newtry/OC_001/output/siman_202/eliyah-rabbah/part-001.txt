<b>[Levush] One blesses on the bread, etc.</b>: even with much oil and little bread, and even when he is concerned for his throat: so Bach proved from the Gemara and poskim; and Olas Tamid challenged him from siman 212 that if the bread is mixed in the main ingredient he does not bless on the bread: see there. And one who examines Bach himself will see that he himself raised this difficulty in this siman and in siman 212, and even so he did not budge from this ruling, for on account of a difficulty one does not reject a ruling: a difficulty can be resolved; and so I saw that Nachalat Tzvi ruled and resolved the difficulty that salted fish can eat without bread and enjoy it, only in order that it not harm his throat he eats bread; but oil alone he cannot eat and one who eats does not enjoy: up to here. And in my humble opinion, to resolve: since there is no way to heal through eating oil and bread, his mind is voided relative to every person and the bread is primary; and in this manner we say in the Gemara and poskim in many places, and so it appears from Rabbeinu Yerucham. Again, the book Magen Avraham was printed and I saw in it other matters, and our mishnah did not move from its place: and so is primary.

<b>[Levush] And it is Shehakol, etc.</b>: in Magen Avraham he wondered from below siman 205 seif 2 that one blesses on mei silka Borei Peri HaAdamah: up to here. And I wonder more about Bach who wrote also in the name of BeHaG that one blesses Shehakol, and I found in the chapter "How does one bless" in this manner that he wrote one blesses Borei Peri HaAdamah. And it appears in my humble opinion that Shulchan Aruch did not write that one blesses Shehakol except when there is much oil: for then in any case the taste of vinegar is nullified even though vinegar is primary, and it is no worse than vegetables cooked in meat in siman 205: and not as implied from Bach and Nachalat Tzvi that one blesses Shehakol even when there is much oil in it. And if one puts in it a little oil and drinks it for healing, Bach wrote that for everyone one blesses Borei Peri HaEtz and he erases the word "much" that Tur wrote, and all the latter authorities followed him. And in my humble opinion this requires study, for in the Gemara [Berakhot] daf 36 it challenges: if he drinks it by means of vinegar, if so vinegar is primary; and it answers: here with what are we dealing? With one who is concerned for his throat: Rashi explained: and he must put in it much oil, for then oil is primary and vinegar is ancillary: up to here. It implies that healing is specifically by means of much oil and therefore oil is primary, and so it implies in Tashbetz siman 322. And regarding one who does not drink it for healing: even with much oil vinegar would be primary: it is possible that Rashi holds like R' Yosef who wrote in Tur that with much oil always oil is primary; or one may say that since it is impossible to drink without vinegar and he drinks it on account of vinegar, vinegar is primary. Or also, since there is no way to put much oil if he is not doing it for healing, his mind is voided relative to every person, as I explained somewhat [note 18]. Also it appears to me proof for this: for if not, why did the Gemara bring up the baraita "One who is concerned for his throat," etc.: does it wish to bring proof that oil is medicine for the throat? Rather it cites on account of what the tanna said "he puts much oil," etc.: examine this well. And Taz wrote that since there is much oil in it, even though vinegar is most of it, oil is primary since there is also a significant amount of it. And incidentally I raised a difficulty in the Gemara there, for it challenges: it is obvious: lest I say one blesses borei peri etz zayit like wine that changed also for the better, as the Gemara said there "from it we learn" Borei Peri HaEtz in the conclusion of the sugya there: and one may resolve with difficulty.

<b>[13] The fallen (novelet) and they, etc. So Rashi explained in the Gemara Berakhot 40, and Rambam, R' Yitzchak, and Bertenora explained that they are fruits that fell from the trees unripe before they were cooked; and it appears both are true; and so is implied in Kolbo 98, who wrote</b>: fruits that were burned and fell before their cooking: one blesses Shehakol: end of his language, meaning either they were burned or they fell: easy to understand. And behold in Demai chapter 1 Rambam explained on fallen dates of the palm: those that fall from the palm when the wind blows, and it is also mostly hefker: end of his words. And his explanation appears to me even if it falls after they were cooked: and as he concludes the reason "mostly hefker": meaning he explained here like the one who says "dates of wind"; but in Berakhot where the tanna taught fallen fruits anonymously, the explanation is like the one who says "cooked like myrtle," as the Gemara concludes. And I saw in Taz at the beginning of siman 204 that he wrote: since the Gemara in the latter language says "cooked": for the one who says dates of wind, this is, etc., and for the one who says cooked like myrtle it is difficult: therefore Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham ruled like the one who says dates of wind, see there. And I did not descend to the end of his view, for one who examines the Gemara will see this refers to fallen dates mentioned in Demai; but regarding fallen fruits taught anonymously by the tanna in Berakhot it concludes it is cooked like myrtle; and so I found explicitly in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot: this is his language: fallen fruits that the tanna taught here: cooked like myrtle. And behold what I explained in Rambam, that he explained dates of wind after they were cooked, appears proved in the Gemara: for if the explanation of dates of wind is before they were cooked, what does it challenge for the one who says dates of wind: what species of curse is this, behold this is a species of curse that fell before ripening; also the Gemara challenges "Borei Peri HaEtz": he needs to bless, implying it deals after they were cooked. And Bertenora wrote in Demai: fallen dates: dates that do not cook on the tree, and one picks them and places one on another so they cook; and some say fallen dates of the palm that the wind cast before their cooking: end of his words. Behold the first explanation is taken from Rosh, who explained thus the one who says cooked like myrtle; and if so, for blessing too one blesses Shehakol, as explained in the Gemara there; and per this explanation Bertenora explained in Berakhot and the first explanation he explained in Demai are all one: everything not cooked is a sign of curse, and the same for unripe that fell from trees that one cooks to ripen: and so is implied at the beginning of the first chapter of Orlah; and therefore Bach and Perishah did not mention Rosh's explanation in the law of blessing Shehakol: understand; and so implies Lechem Mishnah in the chapter "How does one bless." And all this is to exclude from Sefer Be'er Avraham, who did not delve into the depth of the matters: one who wishes may study it, and I did not wish to extend. However one can wonder on Bertenora: behold the latter explanation, perforce like the one who says dates of wind; for if not, why did he also bring the explanation of dates of wind; and so is implied in Rosh: and if so one can wonder: behold it deals even if they fell after they were cooked, as I clarified above from the Gemara; and it is close to me that he followed Rambam's explanation afterward, and he holds Rambam's words in Berakhot and Demai are one. And it is not so, but in Berakhot he explained per the one who says cooked like myrtle, and in Demai per the one who says dates of wind: meaning the wind dried them after they were cooked.
