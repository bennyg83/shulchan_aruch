<b>ואם</b> עט״ז. דעתו ז״ל דל״ד שכר תאנים וכ״מ בב״י בשם הרשב״א דכל שעיקר ועמו טפילה ומיהו המ״א אות ב׳ לא פירש כן (ורשב״ם תשב״ץ וב״י רשב״א) ועיין לבוש וא״ר ב׳ והקשה מי״ד שכ״ד ס״ט (ושם באות ט׳ וש״ך י״ז י״ל דוקא אורז דנגררים אחר המים אבל ברא״ש משמע הטעם טכ״ע ד״ת) וכ״ת ה׳ מינים חשובים המה א״כ אף באין טעם כמו בר״ח בט״ז אות ג׳ דאפי׳ אין נ״ט ושם יבואר אי״ה וע״כ צ״ל דלענין חלה לא מהני חשיבותם רק טכ״ע א״כ כ״ש לענין ברכה דליהני טכ״ע ותי׳ דהא יש ברכה על השכר שהכל ועל היין פה״ג הולכין בתר הרוב משא״כ התם ולכאורה הטעם דרוב עדיף מטעם דהרישורפין וסוקלין על רובא כו׳ וטעם כעיקר הוי כמו ח״ש לי״א. ומ״מ קשה ברכה אחרונה למ״ד מעין ג׳ ד״ת כמ״ש הטור בר״ט (עמ״א קע״ב ג׳ א״כ מעין שלש ד״ת ואיך יפטור בנ״ר דרבנן של שכר למעין שלש דיין וטכ״ע ד״ת שוב ראיתי מההוא דר״ח ס״ט קמ״ח מה׳ מינים עם שאר קמח ואין כזית כא״פ אין מברך בהמ״ז ומשמע אף ניכר ונרגש טעם הדגן ועמ״א שם ט״ז ותנ״ג אות ד׳ ע״ש א״כ כ״ש כאן דבהמ״ז ודאי ד״ת ולפ״ז ביין מועט בשאר משקין אף שיש בערך כזית כא״פ הא מבואר בתרי״ב סעיף י׳ דשתייה בעינן רביעית מתחלתה וסופה כדיעה א׳ בסתם ועמ״א ריש י׳ אות א׳ ושו״ת פ״מ ח״ב סי׳ כ״ז ואי״ה יבואר בר״י ובפשיטות י״ל דלגבי ברכה הולכין אחר הרוב וכל שעיקר ועמו טפילה מברך על העיקר ופוטר הטפילה ועסי׳ ר״ד סי״ב ואי״ה יבואר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See Taz. His view is that the ruling is not limited to fig beer. This also appears from Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba, that whenever there is a primary item together with a secondary one, the blessing follows the primary item. Magen Avraham, however, did not explain it this way. See also Levush and Eliyah Rabbah, who challenge this from Yoreh Deah 324:9. There it may be answered that rice is drawn after the water, whereas the Rosh implies that the reason is that taste is equivalent to substance by Torah law. One might argue that the five species are especially significant. Yet if so, even where they impart no taste they should retain their blessing, as in siman 208 according to Taz, seif katan 3. It must therefore be that for challah their importance helps only where taste is present. All the more so, for blessings, taste should be effective. Eliyah Rabbah answers that beer has its own blessing of Shehakol and wine has Borei Peri HaGefen, so one follows the majority, unlike there. Apparently the reason is that majority is stronger than taste, since capital punishments and similar laws follow majority, whereas taste as substance may be comparable only to half a forbidden measure according to some opinions. Nevertheless, the after-blessing remains difficult according to the view that Me'ein Shalosh is D'Oraisa. How can the Rabbinic Borei Nefashos of beer exempt the Torah-level Me'ein Shalosh of wine when taste as substance is D'Oraisa? I later saw that in siman 208, seif 9, regarding one of the five species mixed with other flour, if there is not an olive-volume within the time of eating a loaf, one does not recite Bircas HaMazon, even though the grain taste is perceptible. If so, all the more so here. Regarding a small quantity of wine mixed into other beverages, even if there is an olive-volume within that time, drinking requires a revi'is from beginning to end, as explained in siman 612:10. The simple resolution is that for blessings one follows the majority, and where there is a primary item with a secondary item, one blesses on the primary and thereby exempts the secondary. See siman 204:12. This will be explained further, with Hashem's help.

<b>ועדיין</b> צ״ע מר״ד ס״ו ומ״א ט״ז דיין במים כל שאין ששה חלקים מים מברך פה״ג וכ״ש בשאר משקין כל שיש טעם יין ולט״ז עכ״פ שוה למים ולמה זה כתב כאן הולכין אחר הרוב אם לא שנחלק בין נתערב ממילא לנותן בכוונה יין ליתן טעם במים כל שיש טעם יין והוא א׳ מחמשה במים מברך פה״ג (עסי׳ תמ״ב בטור וב״י ובש״ע ואחרונים שי״ל בין הנך דעיקר עשייתן כך יע״ש) ולפי שהדברים ארוכים ולי העני דרכי החכמה נעלמו ממני אמרתי לקצר כאן ואי״ה בפחיחה ובר״ד ס״ו וסי״ב ור״ח סא״ב וס״ט יבואר עוד וכאן אין להאריך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Still.</b> This requires study from siman 204:6 and Magen Avraham and Taz there, who rule that wine diluted with water retains Borei Peri HaGefen so long as there are not six parts water. All the more so, when mixed with other beverages, if the taste of wine remains, why does the ruling here say to follow the majority? Perhaps one must distinguish between a mixture that occurred on its own and one in which wine was intentionally added to impart taste. Where wine was intentionally added to water for its taste, and it constitutes one-fifth of the water, one recites Borei Peri HaGefen. See siman 442 in Tur, Beit Yosef, Shulchan Aruch, and the later authorities, where there may be a distinction because those products are ordinarily made in that manner. Since the matter is lengthy and the paths of wisdom are hidden from me, I have abbreviated here. With Hashem's help it will be explained further in the introduction, in siman 204:6 and 12, and in siman 208:2 and 9.

What he wrote regarding fat, that even though it gives much flavor, follows his position in Yoreh Deah 98, seif katan 11, that fat is not considered an ingredient added for taste. When there is a large quantity of fat, however, one nevertheless recites the blessing appropriate to the primary food. Regarding blessings, one follows the principal item. Thus, one who eats whole wheat kernels with fat recites Borei Peri HaAdamah and not Shehakol. <b>ואם</b> עט״ז דעת הב״ח ז״ל לרפוא׳ אף שמן מעט באניגרו״ן כ״ע (הרר״י ובה״ג) מודים דשמן עיקר ומברך רק פה״ע ואם מכוין לאכילה בנותן שמן מעט הרר״י מודים דאניגרון עיקר ובשמן הרבה יותר מהרגילות מ״מ (אניגרו״ן הרוב) להרר״י פה״ע ולבה״ג שהכל ומחק מ״ש הטור שזה רפואתו שנותן שמן הרבה מוחק תיבות הרבה יע״ש. וכ״נ מסתימת המחבר כאן ומ״ש רש״י ברכות ל״ו א׳ ד״ה החושש בגרונו וצריך שמן הרבה ושמן עיקר צריך לדחוק לענין איסור שבת קאמר דאפ״ה שרי ומש״ה ציין כאן ולא בחושש בגרונו אבל הט״ז סובר דלרפואה נמי דוקא כשנותן שמן הרבה יותר משיעור אז מברך פה״ע הא לא״ה לא ויש ראיה לזה דאל״כ למה לא אמר הרר״י דבגמרא נקיט בחושש לומר אפילו מעט שמן ולא לדוגמא. ותדע דדקדוק גדול הוא זה בדברי הרר״י דאל״כ מנא ליה להטור לאשוויי מחלוקת בין הרר״י ובה״ג הלא מבואר בס״א בהג״ה דבתר רוב אזלינן וכמ״ש הט״ז א׳ דכל שיש ב׳ ברכות אזלינן בתר רוב וא״כ הרר״י מיירי בבירך כבר על אניגרו״ן או על ד״א שהכל ומערב שמן עם אניגרו״ן בזה סברתו ברור מללו דכל שאין מזיק ונרגש טעמו מה״ת לא יברך פה״ע אדרבה הפטור לא יפטור החיוב ובה״ג מיירי שיש ב׳ ברכות לפניו שהכל ופה״ע בזה דוקא רפואה השמן עיקר ומברך רק פה״ע ואם שלח לרפואה אזלינן בתר רובא ומברך שהכל ממה שיברך פה״ע אלא דקשיא ליה להטור א״צ להרר״י לדחוק לומר הגמרא דוגמא נקיט כו׳ ש״מ הרר״י סובר אף בשיש ב׳ ברכות לפניו שמן עיקר והטור הסכים לדבריו ולמ״ש הרשב״א דאזלינן בתר כוונתו כו׳ אפשר להמתיק הענין כי רמז לקושיית ד״מ ופרישה למה תלה הב״י ברשב״א גם בה״ג כ״כ וי״ל דרשב״א הביאו הב״י בריש הסימן דאזלינן בתר רובא (עמ״ש ס״א בהג״ה נרשם תשב״ץ וב״י רשב״א) וא״כ אזלא ראיית הטור שאין ראיה ואי כן הוא נמצא עולה מדבריו ז״ל דברים מחודשים הא׳ לרפואה צריך שמן הרבה יותר מהרגילות. ב׳ אם נתערב מעט ברוב דאזלינן בתר רוב היינו בשיש ב׳ ברכות לפניו הא בירך מקודם על השכר שהכל ונתערב אח״כ בשכר וטעם יין מברך פה״ג דלא יפטור הפטור לחיוב. ג׳ דאפי׳ בכוונת שעירב על שיש רוב אזלינן בתר רובא וזה קשה מסי׳ ר״ד מ״א ט״ז כמ״ש באות א׳ ולי העני כ״ז צ״ע עמ״ש באות א׳ מזה. ובחידושינו לו א׳ הארכנו ואי״ה במ״א אות חטי״א יבואר עוד בזה וכאן אין להאריך עי׳ אות ט׳ ור״ה ה׳ ואי״ה יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See Taz. Bach's view is that for medicinal use, even a small amount of oil in anigron is considered primary according to all opinions, and one recites only Borei Peri HaEitz. If one's intent is eating, and only a small amount of oil is added, Rabbeinu Yonah agrees that the anigron is primary. If one adds more oil than usual, although the anigron remains the majority, Rabbeinu Yonah requires Borei Peri HaEitz, whereas Bahag requires Shehakol. Bach deletes the word “much” from the Tur's statement that this is its medicinal manner, because one adds much oil. This also appears from the Mechaber's unqualified wording here. Rashi's statement in Berachos 36a that one with a sore throat requires much oil and therefore the oil is primary must be pressed to mean that he was addressing the permissibility on Shabbos. Taz, however, holds that even for medicinal use Borei Peri HaEitz is recited only when one adds more oil than usual. Otherwise not. There is proof for this, for if not, why did Rabbeinu Yonah not say that the Gemara mentioned a sore throat to teach that even a small amount of oil is primary? Know that this precision is substantial, for otherwise how did the Tur derive a dispute between Rabbeinu Yonah and Bahag? It is explicit in seif 1 that one follows the majority where two blessings are involved. Rabbeinu Yonah could be speaking where one already blessed Shehakol on the anigron or on another item and then mixed in oil. In that case his reasoning is clear: so long as the oil is not harmful and its taste is perceptible, why should he not recite Borei Peri HaEitz? The exempt item should not exempt the obligated item. Bahag, by contrast, could be discussing a case where both blessings are before him. Then specifically for medicine the oil is primary and he recites only Borei Peri HaEitz, but if it is sent for food one follows the majority and recites Shehakol rather than Borei Peri HaEitz. Yet the Tur found it difficult to say that Rabbeinu Yonah used the Gemara only by way of example. This indicates that Rabbeinu Yonah holds that even where both blessings are before him, the oil is primary, and the Tur agrees. According to Rashba's rule that one follows intent, the matter can be clarified further, and this may answer the question of Darchei Moshe and Perishah why Beit Yosef associated this with Rashba, since Bahag said the same. Rashba, as cited by Beit Yosef at the beginning of the siman, holds that one follows the majority. If so, the Tur's proof falls away. Several new rulings emerge from Taz's words: first, medicinal intent requires more oil than usual. Second, where a small amount is mixed into a majority, following the majority applies only when both blessings are before him; but if he already blessed Shehakol on the beer and wine is then mixed into it and its taste is perceptible, he recites Borei Peri HaGefen, for the exempt item does not exempt the obligated item. Third, even when the mixture was intentional, one follows the majority. This last point is difficult from siman 204 and Magen Avraham and Taz there, as I wrote in letter 1. All this requires study.

<b>עוד</b> רגע אדבר כי לכאורה בשמן הרוב ואניגרון מעט י״ל לכ״ע פה״ע אף לב״ג די״ל דכה״ג אזוקי מזיק וכבעין דמי. ועי׳ חה״ר בשם רב הא״י חולק בין חול בעינן שמתכוין לרפואה הא סתמא אף שחושש לא מוכחא מילתא דלרפואה עביד דאדרבא בעיניה מעליא טפי (אף דמזיק מ״מ לרפואה הוא) ובשבת שא״א בעיניה אפילו סתמא נמי פה״ע יע״ש ולשון המחבר צריך התבוננות בו כי בה״ג כתב אי כייבין ליה חינכי דמכוין לרפואה משמע סתמא לא ובלבוש כ״כ והוא מתכוין לרפואה אבל המחבר כתב סתמא בחושש בגרונו כו׳ אפשר אף סתמא כעין ל׳ הר״מ ז״ל פ״ה מה״ב ה״ב ואמנם הר״מ סיים אבל אין חושש בגרונו והמחבר סיים אבל אין מתכוין לרפואה רבותא אפילו חושש שהכל וקצת סתרי הדיוקים ואם אין מתכוין לרפואה אלא לאכילה וצ״ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>I will add.</b> It appears that if oil is the majority and anigron the minority, all agree that one recites Borei Peri HaEitz, even Bahag, because in such a case it may be harmful and therefore treated as though eaten alone. See Chiddushei HaRashba in the name of Rav Hai, who distinguishes between weekdays, when medicinal intent must be explicit, since if left unstated it is not evident that he drinks it for medicine and indeed oil alone would be better, and Shabbos, when oil alone is impossible, so even without explicit intent one recites Borei Peri HaEitz. The wording of the Mechaber requires careful examination. Bahag wrote, “if his gums hurt,” implying that medicinal intent must be explicit, and Levush likewise says that he intends it for medicine. The Mechaber, however, writes without qualification about one whose throat hurts, perhaps following Rambam's wording. Yet Rambam concludes, “but if his throat does not hurt,” while the Mechaber concludes, “but if he does not intend it for medicine,” which teaches that even if his throat hurts, if he does not intend medicine he recites Shehakol. The implications conflict somewhat. This requires study.

<b>However.</b> See Taz. Levush and apparently Derishah distinguish between dates mashed by hand, where some form of the fruit remains and therefore, according to Rambam, Borei Peri HaEitz is recited, and fruit preserves whose form is no longer recognizable. The Gemara says that terima is permitted but beer is prohibited with respect to terumah. One might therefore have thought that so long as it remains food, even when its form is entirely unrecognizable, it retains its original status, and that terumah and blessings are equivalent. The Gemara also states that wheat flour receives Borei Peri HaAdamah like olive oil, concluding that there is no further elevation possible. We thus see that even where the form of the fruit is wholly unrecognizable, it retains its status. Likewise, the liquid of cooked vegetables is treated as the vegetables themselves. It must be that those items are not ordinarily crushed, and on the contrary are generally eaten in their original form, so if one changes them he recites Shehakol. Olive oil, wheat flour according to Rabbi Yehudah, and the liquid of cooked vegetables are ordinarily prepared in that form. If so, what proof does Taz bring from ground spices in siman 203:7? Grinding is their normal manner, as Magen Avraham writes in his name in seif katan 18, and as stated in Terumas HaDeshen 29. There it also implies that the reason one should perhaps recite Shehakol on preserves is that honey and spices are mixed in, creating two disadvantages: the fruit's form has entirely changed and its taste has also changed. Where fruit has completely dissolved but its fruit taste remains recognizable, Terumas HaDeshen may agree that Borei Peri HaEitz is recited. Even if one brings proof from cooked fruit according to the Rosh, where Borei Peri HaEitz is recited although this is not its usual manner, one can answer that its taste remains recognizable, unlike preserves mixed with honey and spices. All this concerns the first blessing. The after-blessing is more difficult. If terima, a paste mashed by hand, receives Borei Peri HaEitz, its after-blessing is Me'ein Shalosh; if its first blessing is Shehakol, its after-blessing is Borei Nefashos. Siman 208 explains that Borei Nefashos and Me'ein Shalosh do not exempt one another. One solution is to eat another fruit of the seven species and also an item requiring Shehakol, and thereby cover both after-blessings. If only this terima is available, according to the Tur in siman 209, who holds that Me'ein Shalosh is D'Oraisa, one might recite it because of the doubt. According to us, however, since the matter remains doubtful, one should not recite either Borei Nefashos or Me'ein Shalosh. This requires study.

<b>Honey.</b> See Taz. The Rosh writes regarding date honey that one recites Shehakol. The Gaon said that this applies only when it was mixed with water, but alone it receives Borei Peri HaEitz. The Rosh writes that this distinction is not correct. Beit Yosef understood the Rosh to hold that squeezing and crushing are inferior even to liquid that flows out by itself, whereas cooking is superior to both. Therefore, according to our ruling here that date honey flowing by itself receives Shehakol, all the more so juice extracted by squeezing or crushing receives Shehakol. By contrast, in seif 10, according to the Rosh, fruit liquid produced through cooking receives Borei Peri HaEitz, while squeezed juice receives Shehakol. This is certainly the Rosh's view, because squeezed and crushed liquid is inferior to cooked liquid. Chiddushei HaRashba, however, implies that squeezing is equivalent to cooking, and he distinguishes between fruits ordinarily cooked or squeezed, whose liquid retains the fruit's blessing, and fruits not ordinarily prepared that way. The Mechaber in seif 10 follows the first opinion without qualification, namely Rashba, yet in siman 205:3 he rules that squeezed fruit juice receives Shehakol. He thus appears to adopt two conflicting approaches. I discussed this at length in my novellae. It is also reasonable that squeezing should be superior to liquid that flows out by itself. Dates, however, naturally exude liquid when they contain much oil, and that exudate is then like the essence of the fruit. This was the Gaon's argument from Scripture's praise of Eretz Yisrael as flowing with honey. When dates lack abundant oil and are squeezed, their juice is not equivalent. In any event, according to the Rosh, neither spontaneous exudation nor squeezing and crushing is equivalent to cooking. According to Rashba, squeezing is equivalent to cooking. Wine and oil, even when they flow by themselves, receive Borei Peri HaGefen and Borei Peri HaEitz. See also the Mishnah in Terumos 11:3, which lists date honey, vinegar made from late grapes, apple wine, and other fruit liquids. Why list these individually? According to the Rosh, this follows his view that squeezing and crushing are not equivalent to cooking. According to Rashba, apple wine is listed because apples are not ordinarily cooked or squeezed; but fruits ordinarily cooked or squeezed yield liquid with the fruit's blessing. Date honey excludes the Gaon's opinion, and vinegar from late grapes excludes the thought that the exudate is the fruit's essence. See Magen Avraham seif katan 20. I discussed this at length in my novellae.

<b>And the Rosh.</b> See Taz. The Rosh was not uncertain. The Tur writes in siman 205 that fruit liquid produced by cooking is different and superior, so the Rosh clearly distinguished. Taz's conclusion can be framed as follows: where it is not usual to cook or crush the fruit, one recites Shehakol, following Rashba; where it is usual to cook, one recites Borei Peri HaEitz; but where it is usual to squeeze, he did not decide, because even when it is usual, the juice may still be regarded as sweat. Taz asks why cooked-vegetable liquid receives the vegetable's blessing when, in seif 1, where wine is mixed with beer, one follows the majority rather than taste. Here too, the water is the majority and only the vegetable taste remains, so one should recite Shehakol. He answers that water is not significant and one follows the taste, whereas when one important beverage is mixed with another, one follows the majority. This also explains the gloss in siman 204:5 and Magen Avraham and Taz there, that with fewer than six parts water one does not follow the majority because water is involved, whereas with another important beverage one does. According to what I wrote above, however, one may distinguish between an accidental mixture and one intentionally prepared for its taste, without distinguishing water from other beverages. Taz in siman 205:5 derives from this that vegetables cooked in vinegar or another beverage cause the liquid to retain Shehakol, because that liquid is significant and one follows the majority, not the taste, even when meat is also present.
