**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> There: “Beis Hillel say,” etc. The Rosh, however, writes: “And all other trees, from when they produce fruit.” In his explanation, he writes that one cannot derive this law of blessings from the law of Shemittah, for carobs remain bitter even after their fruit clusters form and are not fit to eat. This is the meaning of the Shulchan Aruch’s words, “provided that,” etc. His words are difficult, for if so, all the more so when they merely begin to emerge they are still inedible, since that stage precedes the formation of the fruit clusters. It appears that the Rosh also agrees that carobs are judged from when their clusters form, but he mentioned only the other trees, following the concluding words of Beis Hillel: “And all other trees, from when they produce fruit.” See Rosh, chapter 6 of Berachos, seif 5. The fundamental ruling nevertheless requires study. What connection does this have to Shemittah? Even regarding Shemittah, one may not eat until the produce has become food, meaning when it reaches the stage of liability for maaser, at which point it is considered fruit. Shemittah itself, however, is not dependent upon the item’s being a fruit. It appears more correct that the law should depend upon the time of liability for maaser, when it is considered fruit, even regarding the blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 3, And if, etc.</b> Rosh there, as explained above. Rashba, however, disagrees with Tosafos and writes that, on the contrary, pits are not considered fruit, since Scripture had to include them through the phrase “es hatafel,” just as it includes peels. He further writes that no blessing is recited over bitter pits, for trees are not planted with them in mind; if one sweetens them, however, etc., as stated in the Shulchan Aruch. This requires study, for the Shulchan Aruch combines the views of Tosafos and Rashba. See Magen Avraham. It is certain that the statement, “if one sweetens them,” reflects the view of Rashba, who disagrees with Tosafos. The principal view appears to be that of Rashba, for the Mishnayos in chapter 11 of Terumos, in Bikkurim, and in Uktzin all state without qualification that pits are not fruit at all. A further proof is from the law of neta revai. The fact that pits are forbidden as orlah necessarily derives from the scriptural inclusion of “es,” etc. This is therefore the principal ruling.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 6, The caper, etc.</b> See Tosafos there, s.v. vehilchesa, “and it was,” etc. The Rosh raises the same difficulty. It appears to me that at first glance one may answer the Gemara’s question, “Let the master act like Beis Shammai,” etc.: there it says that the teaching comes to inform us that the halachah follows whoever is lenient, etc. That differs from Beis Shammai, who is lenient only outside Eretz Yisrael. One must therefore say that the Gemara mentions only Rabbi Akiva because he is lenient, and his ruling is stated without qualification as the words of the lenient authority, not because of the rule that the one who is lenient in Eretz Yisrael, etc. This is the meaning of, “The halachah follows Mar bar Rav Ashi,” and not Rav Yehudah. See Yoreh Deah 294:3. Lechem Chamudos explains Tosafos’ question differently: since regarding orlah, etc. Meaning, because we are uncertain, we rule stringently in Eretz Yisrael and leniently outside the Land, and therefore the one who is lenient in Eretz Yisrael, etc., because a doubt regarding orlah outside the Land is permitted. The same applies regarding blessings, where we follow the lenient opinion because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we are lenient. As explained above, this is what is meant by following Rabbi Akiva, who is lenient.

This is the meaning of, “and the water,” etc. Rashba, however, writes that there it is not customary to boil or squeeze them, unlike vegetables, most of which are eaten after boiling. See Magen Avraham.
