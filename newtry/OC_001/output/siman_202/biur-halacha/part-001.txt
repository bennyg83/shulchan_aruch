<b> ואם הרוב שכר</b> -  עיין במ"ב ועיין בפמ"ג שהקשה מדברי רמ"א אלו על מה שכתב במגן אברהם בסימן ר"ד סקט"ז דיין במשקין אף לדברי הט"ז עכ"פ צריך ששה נגדו שיתבטל בו ולדידיה אף ששה לא מהני ע"ש והכא אמרינן דברובא מתבטל [ומה שרצה לתרץ דהכא דוקא בשנתערב ממילא ולא בשמכוין למזוג לא משמע כן בתשב"ץ סימן פ"ז שממנו מקור הדין הזה] וראיתי בדברי הגר"ז בסימן ר"ד וכן בח"א כלל נ"ה ס"ג שהשמיטו לגמרי דברי המ"א דשם והעתיקו להלכה כמו שכתב הרמ"א בשם התשב"ץ בסימן זה. אמנם לענ"ד נראה שיש חילוק בזה דלא כל המשקין שוין לענין ברכה דאם דרך העולם לערב אותן משקין ביין להשביח טעמו אז אפי' הם רובא נגד היין [כ"ז שאינם ששה נגדו] נמי אינם מבטלין היין ואפילו נשתנה טעם היין עי"ז מכמות שהיה מקודם וכמו ההיא דקונדיטון שמבואר בירושלמי שהוא שליש דבש ושליש פלפלין הרי דהיין הוא מיעוט לגבי הבשמים והדבש ואפ"ה מברכין בפה"ג והטעם כמש"כ הרשב"א בתשובה והעתיקו התשב"ץ שהרי עלויו בו ולא שייך בזה לומר דהיין הוא טפל דאדרבה הם טפלים לגבי היין להשביח טעמו ודברי הרמ"א מיירי בשכר וכיו"ב שאין דרך העולם לערב אותן ביין כדי להשביח טעמו דהגם דמשמע דשכר מחזק כח היין מ"מ אין דרך העולם לערב בזה כדי להשביחו ולכן לא הוי טפל לגבי היין וע"כ תלוי הדבר ברובא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(5) And if the majority is beer</b>: see in Mishnah Berurah; and see in Pri Megadim that he challenged from these words of Rama upon what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 204 note 96 that wine in beverages: even per the words of Taz: in any case requires six against it to be nullified, and per his view even six do not help: see there; and here we say that by majority it is nullified [and what he wished to answer that here specifically when it mixed by itself and not with intent to dilute: it does not appear so in Tashbetz siman 87 from which is the source of this law]; and I saw in the words of Gra Zal in siman 204 and likewise in Chayei Adam general 55 note 3 that they omitted entirely the words of Magen Avraham there and brought to law as Rama wrote in name of Tashbetz in this siman. However, in my humble opinion it appears there is a distinction here, for not all beverages are equal for blessing: if the way of the world is to mix those beverages in wine to improve its taste, then even if they are majority against the wine [provided they are not six against it] they also do not nullify the wine, and even if the taste of the wine changed thereby from what it was before: and like that case of condiment explained in Yerushalmi that is one-third honey and one-third spices: behold the wine is minority relative to the spices and honey, and nevertheless they bless Borei Peri HaGefen; and the reason as Rashba wrote in responsum and Tashbetz brought: behold its importance is in it, and it is not applicable here to say the wine is ancillary, for on the contrary they are ancillary relative to the wine to improve its taste; and the words of Rama deal with beer and the like that the way of the world is not to mix them in wine in order to improve its taste, for although it appears beer strengthens the power of the wine, nevertheless the way of the world is not to mix for this in order to improve it: therefore it is not ancillary relative to the wine, and thus the matter depends on majority:

<b> מברך שהכל</b> -  עיין במ"ב שהגר"א חולק על פסק זה ודעתו דמברך בפה"ג דמזיגת יין בשכר שוה למזיגת יין במים ולא אזלינן בזה בתר רובא אכן כמה אחרונים העתיקו דברי הרמ"א לפסק הלכה [וקושית הגר"א ממה שאמרו כל חמרא דלא דרי וכו' יישב הפמ"ג וש"א דדוקא במים אמרו שהוא דרך מזיגה וע"כ לא נתבטל ולא בשאר משקין ומה שכתב עוד דמתניתין לא איירי כלל בנתערב וכו' השיב במגן גבורים דלדינא הלא קי"ל דאף במעורב אמרינן עיקר פוטר הטפל וכדלקמן בסימן רי"ב ס"א וכן נתקשה בדבריו בספר דמשק אליעזר וע"ש מה שכתב בישובו. והעיקר דהגר"א ס"ל דלגבי יין אף שהשכר הוא רוב לא חשבינן ליה לעיקר דמדמה אותו למים שמוזג היין בם] וע"כ לענין ברכה ראשונה בודאי מהנכון לכתחלה לברך שהכל [ואם בירך בפה"ג יצא] ולענין ברכה אחרונה יראה ליקח יין אחר רביעית ויפטור בברכה אחרונה גם את זה אכן לפי המבואר בסימן ר"ד באחרונים שם לענין מזיגת יין במים דעכ"פ תלוי בחוזק היין ואף אם המים פחות משיעור המבואר שם לבטל היין מ"מ אין מברך בפה"ג אלא ביש בו טעם יין ודרך העולם לשתות יין זה במזיגה כזו נראה דה"נ בעירב בשכר אם הוא יין חלוש ואין דרך העולם למזוג אף ברוב מים גם בעירב עם שכר מברך שהכל ואחריו ב"נ אף לדעת הגר"א דמהגר"א משמע דשכר ומים דין אחד להם וכ"ש אם אבד טעם יין דמברך שהכל לכו"ע וכמ"ש במ"ב:

<b>(6) One blesses Shehakol</b>: see in Mishnah Berurah that Gra disagrees with this ruling and his view is one blesses Borei Peri HaGefen, for mixing wine in beer equals mixing wine in water, and we do not follow majority here; however, several latter authorities brought Rama's words as halakhic ruling [and Gra's difficulty from what they said "all wine that is not aged," etc.: Pri Megadim and Shulchan Aruch Harav resolve that specifically in water they said it is the way of dilution, and therefore it was not nullified, and not in other beverages; and what he further wrote that the mishnah does not deal at all with mixture, etc.: Magen Giborim answered that for the law we hold that even in mixture we say the primary exempts the ancillary, as below siman 212 seif 1; and likewise he challenged his words in Sefer Damasek Eliezer: see there what he wrote in his answer. And the essential point is that Gra holds that regarding wine, even if beer is majority we do not consider it primary, for he compares it to water in which wine is diluted]: and therefore for the first blessing certainly it is correct initially to bless Shehakol [and if he blessed Borei Peri HaGefen he fulfilled]; and for the concluding blessing it appears he should take another revi'it of wine and exempt this too with the concluding blessing; however, per what is explained in siman 204 in the latter authorities there regarding mixing wine in water, in any case it depends on the strength of the wine, and even if the water is less than the measure explained there to nullify the wine, nevertheless one does not bless Borei Peri HaGefen unless there is in it taste of wine and the way of the world is to drink this wine in such a mixture: it appears the same applies in mixture with beer if it is weak wine and the way of the world is not to dilute even with majority water; also in mixture with beer one blesses Shehakol and afterward Borei Nefashos, even per the view of Gra, for from Gra it appears beer and water share one law, and all the more if the taste of wine was lost one blesses Shehakol for all opinions, as written in Mishnah Berurah:

<b>And every other tree from when it brings forth, etc., like a kind of chains, etc.</b>: see in Biur HaGra who disagrees with Shulchan Aruch's ruling and proved that all these signs were not mentioned in the Gemara except for the prohibition of cutting trees in shevi'it, but for blessing it is reasonable that we require specifically when they reach the season of tithes, and before that it is not considered fruit fit for eating; and so is also Pri Megadim's view: and per their words all the laws in this seif change, namely: for grapes we require specifically that the seeds be visible inside; for olives, when they bring a third; for carobs, when black spots are seen on them, which is a sign it begins to blacken; and in every species there is a special measure for this, as all is explained in Rambam chapter 2 of Hilchot Ma'aser, see there: and before they reach this measure one blesses only Borei Peri HaAdamah; and there is support for their words from Rambam chapter 5 of Hilchot Shemittah and chapter 8 of Hilchot Berakhot, see there; and Acharonim already debated this. And behold per the Gra and Pri Megadim it is reasonable that even sweetening by fire does not help, since it is not considered fruit before it reaches the season of tithes, and one always blesses Borei Peri HaAdamah; and one should practice thus in action, ostensibly, for through this one exits from doubt either way; nevertheless one who practices like the ruling of the Acharonim who followed Shulchan Aruch: one should not protest against him, for he has whom to rely on; however if they were not fit to eat except through pressing, one should not bless Borei Peri HaEtz except when cooked or fixed by fire, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Chayei Adam and Olelot Ephraim. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Concerning that water, etc.</b> This implies that one recites the appropriate blessing over the fruits themselves. This is also evident from Magen Avraham in the name of Rashba. It must therefore refer to fruits that are good both raw and cooked, for otherwise one recites only Shehakol even over the fruits themselves, as explained below in seif 12; see Mishnah Berurah there. Even so, regarding the liquid, we do not say that their liquid shares the fruit's blessing unless most consumption of those fruits is through cooking. This is how the words of the Shulchan Aruch must be understood. There is clear proof for this, since apples are described in seif 12 as good both raw and cooked, yet Rashba explicitly writes that one recites Shehakol over their liquid because they are ordinarily eaten raw and not boiled. Necessarily, regarding their liquid, most of the fruit must specifically be intended for that use. It is possible that Rashba agrees with the Rosh where the ordinary practice is equally to eat the fruit cooked and raw, and that he disputes him only where the fruit is good cooked but is ordinarily eaten only raw. See Rashba's novellae to the entire sugya of vegetable broth, which somewhat imply this. Know further that even according to the Rosh, who holds that one always recites Borei Peri HaEtz over their liquid, this means only that most consumption need not be through cooking. Nevertheless, the fruits must at least be good both raw and cooked. If they are good only raw, then even over the fruits themselves one recites only Shehakol when eating them cooked, as explained immediately below in seif 12 in Mishnah Berurah. Accordingly, it is clear that according to all opinions one recites only Shehakol over water in which apples were soaked, known as apple kvass. The apples themselves are not ordinarily eaten after soaking, since soaking spoils them, and one recites Shehakol over them. Their liquid cannot be superior to the fruit itself. Apart from this reason, there is another reason why its blessing is Shehakol; see Magen Avraham, siman 205, seif 2. Chayei Adam rules likewise.
