**** ENGLISH ****

From here Taz derives proof below, siman 204, seif katan 15, that one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah over orange peel preserved in honey. Magen Avraham, however, in this siman, seif katan 17, disagrees. He writes that the cases are not comparable: here the peel falls away from the fruit, whereas orange peel is part of the fruit itself and is fit to be eaten, although it is not especially good without preparation and the addition of sugar. Since the inside of the fruit is likewise not good without preparation, one should recite Borei Peri HaEtz. See there. Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 65, likewise proves that one recites Borei Peri HaEtz, contrary to Taz. I found that one should recite Shehakol over preserved orange peel, and this is the practice throughout the lands of Ishmael. So states the Baer Heitev before me. <small>Eliyah Rabbah likewise rules that Shehakol is recited over preserved orange peel.</small>

<b>שהכל. </b> ול"ד למיא דסלקא בסי' ר"ה ס"ב דהנהו כיון דרוב אכילתן ע"י שליקה מי שליקתן כמותן משא"כ פירות דאין דרכן למישלקיה או למסחטיה אלא למיכלא בעינייהו בהנהו לא אמרינן שיהא מימיהן כמותן ב"י הרשב"א. ומיהו בפירות יבשים כגון תפוחים ואגסים גודגדניות פלומין ד"ה דמברך בפה"ע דהא אין דרכן אלא למשליקנהו ולא למיכלא בעינייהו וכן אני נוהג ועיקר ב"ח ט"ז. וכן פסק הע"ת דפירות שרוב אכילתם במבושל ודאי מים שלהם כמותן שאם הם פרי האדמה מברכין בפה"א ואם הם פרי העץ מברכין בפה"ע. ומג"א מסופק בזה. ועל קאוו"י וט"ע מברך שהכל פרח מטה אהרן ח"א סי' מ' וכ"פ בתשובת שבות יעקב ח"ב סי' ה' שמברכין שהכל על הקאוו"י וט"ע אבל בהלק"ט סי' ט' ובתשובת פנים מאירות סי' צ"ה כתבו דמדינא יש לברך בפה"א אלא שנהגו לברך שהכל ע"ש ועיין בס' בני חייא ואחריו בנ"ר עיין סי' ר"ד ס"ז מש"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Shehakol.</b> This is not comparable to vegetable broth discussed in siman 205, seif 2. Since those vegetables are ordinarily eaten after cooking, their cooking liquid is treated like the vegetables themselves. Fruits, however, are not ordinarily cooked or pressed, but eaten as they are; therefore, their liquid is not treated like the fruit itself. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. Regarding dried fruits, however, such as apples, pears, cherries, and plums, all agree that one recites Borei Peri HaEtz, since they are ordinarily cooked rather than eaten as they are. This is my practice and is the principal ruling. Bach and Taz. Olas Tamid likewise rules that when most of a fruit is eaten cooked, its cooking liquid is certainly treated like the fruit itself: if it is a fruit of the ground, one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah, and if it is a tree fruit, one recites Borei Peri HaEtz. Magen Avraham remains uncertain about this. Over coffee and tea one recites Shehakol. Perach Mateh Aharon, part 1, siman 40. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 5, likewise rules that Shehakol is recited over coffee and tea. Halachos Ketanos, siman 9, and Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 95, however, write that strictly speaking one should recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, although the custom is to recite Shehakol. See there. See also Bnei Chayei and the authority who follows him, and see siman 204, seif 7, regarding what is written there.

<b>Was drawn off.</b> Beit Yosef writes: This applies specifically when the raisins remain mixed with the water; if the water was drawn away from the raisins, it is wine. Rama explains his words as follows: When the raisins remain mixed with the water and one both eats the raisins and drinks the water, the liquid is like the broth of any other fruit. Physical drawing-off is not actually required. This is also implied by all the poskim who discuss raisin wine without distinguishing whether it was drawn off. Magen Avraham; see there. Taz likewise writes that after three days it is fully considered wine even if it was not drawn off. See there.
