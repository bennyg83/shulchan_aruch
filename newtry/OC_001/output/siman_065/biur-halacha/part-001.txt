He read it out of order, etc. — know that the author's view [which is Rif's and Rambam's view] is the same regarding tekios in siman 588 seif 2 and Birkat Hamazon in siman 183 seif 6; and so in Hallel in siman 422 seif 5; and so in Megillah in siman 490 seif 5; and the same for all other blessings except prayer: if he waited in the middle long enough to finish the entire thing he does not return to the beginning even if the interruption was due to coercion; and in prayer if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing, even if the interruption was not due to coercion, he returns to the beginning. And know the "some say" view — which is Tosafot's and Rosh's view — that one should not distinguish between prayer and other matters; rather in every matter if he waited due to coercion he returns to the beginning [except Birkat Hamazon which is not in their words explicitly]. And Darkei Moshe's ruling in siman 422: for a rabbinic matter alone apart from prayer one may be lenient and rule like Rif not to return to the beginning even if he waited due to coercion; but Levush and Bach and Agur disagree there — see there. And see Magen Avraham in siman 422 who requires returning to read Hallel without a blessing because of Darkei Moshe's concern; if so the same in Megillah — he should return to read it without a blessing; and in Pri Megadim siman 490 letter 7 he doubts there regarding the blessing — it seems it is not clear to him for practice like Magen Avraham; if so accordingly in every rabbinic matter, if it concerns concern of a blessing in vain, one should examine if he waited in it long enough to finish the entire thing even in complete coercion whether he returns to the beginning. And similarly one should examine regarding the blessing of Keriat Shema before or after it which is also rabbinic, since he already mentioned the Exodus in the section of tzitzit; and afterward I found in Nishmat Adam general rule 5 who doubts regarding all blessings; if so accordingly the same in the blessing of Keriat Shema; but from the words of Dagul MeRevavah in what he wrote regarding Keriat Shema law 8 it seems the law in the blessing of Keriat Shema is also like Keriat Shema itself, even though he himself ruled in Laws of Hallel like Magen Avraham that he returns to read it without a blessing; perhaps he holds the Sages equated the blessing of Keriat Shema to Keriat Shema. And see Magen Avraham in siman 183 letter 11 who doubts regarding Birkat Hamazon if he waited in it long enough to finish the entire thing due to coercion whether he returns to the beginning because of that which they were lenient in it also regarding what is explained in siman 185 seif 5. However according to the words of Dagul MeRevavah above that even in the blessing of Keriat Shema which is rabbinic, also bedieved if he found feces opposite him he need not return and bless as explained in Chayei Adam general rule 3 law 33 — see there — and nevertheless he returns to the beginning; if so all the more in Birkat Hamazon which is from the Torah; and so ruled in Nishmat Adam general rule 5 law 13 that when the man or place is not fit he returns to the beginning — see there. [Gloss: And in truth Magen Avraham's words are not understood at all, for what Tosafot and Rosh doubted regarding one who prayed and found feces opposite him is not specifically regarding Birkat Hamazon — the same for all matters except Keriat Shema and prayer as explained in the words of the poskim; and nevertheless the known view of Tosafot and Rosh regarding waiting long enough to finish the entire thing is that they do not hold like Rif that this law is only in prayer alone; rather the same for Hallel and Megillah and tekios — if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing due to coercion he returns; if so automatically the same for other matters. And let not the examiner wonder how coercion where the place is not fit is conceived in every matter except prayer — according to what Shulchan Aruch doubts when he blessed and there was feces opposite him, and their view that this was said only for prayer; but this is a blessing of God's name — the intent is after he blessed he afterward found feces in his place — on this they doubt perhaps this law was said only regarding prayer where they decreed and said his prayer is an abomination; and so seems in Levush and Sha'agat Arye; but all agree every holy matter is forbidden to say in a place that is not clean even only from urine, and all the more from feces. Therefore, gloss.] And know further that in Birkat Hamazon where they are stringent to return to the beginning — it is even if he waited between blessing and blessing, for in this manner Magen Avraham deals there; and although regarding the blessing of Keriat Shema they are lenient in this in Mishnah Berurah there that the blessings do not hinder one another — not so in Birkat Hamazon all three blessings are considered as one as explained in Tur siman 188 and Shulchan Aruch end seif 6 — see there in Magen Avraham; and it appears simple the same for Birkat Kohanim — all this law of waiting long enough to finish the entire thing applies; and if the waiting was due to coercion he returns to the beginning of the priests' blessing; and the measure of "to finish the entire thing" means the essence of the three blessings from beginning to end without saying Ribono shel olam and yehi ratzon that the congregation says; and it is possible that in places where they chant the kohanim during the congregation's utterance — this too should be counted within the measure — requires further study.

<b> בין בדיבור</b> -  עיין במ"ב במה שכתב אפילו שח במזיד עיין במגן אברהם בסימן ק"ד סק"ו דמתחלה מסתפק בזה לענין ק"ש או ברכותיה דאפשר דחוזר לראש ואפילו לא שהה כדי לגמור כולה אמנם במסקנתו מצדד שם דבדיעבד לא יחזור לראש דדיינינן להו כשוגגין מכיון שרוב העולם אין נזהרין משמע דבמקומות שזהירין העולם או אמר בעצמו שידע את הדין דשוב הו"ל מזיד גמור חוזר לראש כמו בתפלה לדעתו וכן פסק באמת בסימן קפ"ג בסקי"א לענין בהמ"ז עי"ש ומדברי הגר"א בשם הרשב"א בסימן ק"ד הנ"ל בס"ה ובס"ו משמע דחוזר למקום שפסק ואפילו כשפסק באמצע ברכה וממילא ה"ה לענין בהמ"ז דאף שהוא דאורייתא מה חמירא מק"ש וכן בא"ר בסימן קפ"ג לענין בהמ"ז כתב דדין זה דהמ"א שהחמיר בשח במזיד לחזור לראש צ"ע ע"כ לענ"ד קשה מאוד לעשות מעשה בזה כדברי המגן אברהם ועוד דבתפלה גופא יש הרבה דעות בין הראשונים והאחרונים לענין שח במזיד ומן התורה יוצא בזה בבהמ"ז וק"ש כמבואר בפמ"ג ע"כ סתמתי דבדיעבד אין לו לחזור ומ"מ מהנכון דלענין פרשיות ק"ש יחזור עוד הפעם אבל בבהמ"ז וכ"ש בשאר ברכות לא יחזור בדיעבד:
**** ENGLISH ****
Between utterances — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: even if he erred intentionally — see Magen Avraham in siman 104 comment 6 who initially doubted this regarding Keriat Shema or its blessings that perhaps he returns to the beginning even if he did not wait long enough to finish the entire thing; however in his conclusion he leans there that bedieved he does not return to the beginning, for we judge them as unwitting since most of the world is not careful — it seems in places where the world is careful, or if he himself said he knew the law — again it is fully intentional; he returns to the beginning as in prayer according to his view; and so he ruled in truth in siman 183 comment 11 regarding Birkat Hamazon — see there. And from the Gra's words in the name of Rashba in siman 104 above in seif 5 and seif 6 it seems he returns to the place he stopped even if he stopped in the middle of a blessing; and automatically the same regarding Birkat Hamazon — even though it is from the Torah, what is stricter than Keriat Shema? And so in Agur siman 183 regarding Birkat Hamazon he wrote that this law of Magen Avraham who was stringent regarding intentional error to return to the beginning — requires further study; therefore in my humble opinion it is very difficult to act on this according to Magen Avraham's words; and further in prayer itself there are many views among Rishonim and Achronim regarding intentional error; and from the Torah one fulfills through this in Birkat Hamazon and Keriat Shema as explained in Pri Megadim; therefore I sealed that bedieved he should not return; nevertheless it is proper that regarding the sections of Keriat Shema he return again; but in Birkat Hamazon and all the more other blessings he does not return bedieved.

He returns to the beginning — see Mishnah Berurah comment 5. And the reason for all these matters: for at first glance one should doubt three details. One: whether the law that if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing he returns to the beginning is specifically when he waited in the middle of a section or even between section and section. Two: whether the law of waiting long enough to finish the entire thing is specifically in Keriat Shema or even if he waited in the blessing of Keriat Shema. Three: the law of returning to the beginning — does he also return to the blessings, or perhaps since we hold blessings do not hinder Keriat Shema and they are a mitzvah by themselves, and proof that we bless them even after the time for Keriat Shema has passed — why should he return to the blessings? And the same when he interrupted the blessing of Keriat Shema such as in "emet veyatziv" — must he also return to Keriat Shema and the other blessings, or perhaps blessings with Keriat Shema are two matters; therefore since generally they do not hinder — the same also if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing in Ahavah Rabbah he need not return to Yotzer Ohr, since we hold blessings do not hinder one another as stated in Berakhot 12a; if so at first glance each blessing is a matter by itself and is not comparable to prayer and Hallel and Megillah — why should he return to Yotzer Ohr? And behold the first law I found with God's help in Rashba beginning of chapter 2 of Berakhot [his words are brought below siman 104 in Beur HaGra] that it is proven waiting applies even between section and section; and the second law I found in Dagul MeRevavah who wrote waiting applies even in the blessing of Keriat Shema [and there it seems from him that even if he did not wait only long enough to finish that blessing it is called waiting long enough to finish the entire thing — and we will speak of this below]; and only the third question remains for us; and it appears to depend on what is stated in Yerushalmi chapter 2 of Berakhot [brought in novelae of R' Akiva Eiger and other Achronim]: R' Bun bar Chiya asked: such as Keriat Shema and its blessings, or Keriat Shema without its blessings, or its blessings without it? And Peni Moshe explained: that which we say if he interrupted and waited long enough to finish the entire thing he does not fulfill and must return to the beginning — what "entire thing" does he mean? Such as Keriat Shema with its blessings, or it without its blessings, or even long enough to finish its blessings without it, end quote; and one depends on the other: if we say he must wait until long enough for Keriat Shema and its blessings specifically because they are interrelated — the same when he returns to the beginning he also returns with its blessings; and if we say "to finish the entire thing" means Keriat Shema itself — the same when he returns he need only return Keriat Shema; and it appears that although it is proven from this Yerushalmi that the law of waiting long enough to finish the entire thing is only rabbinic — since he asked whether with blessings or not — nevertheless regarding Keriat Shema itself it is correct to be stringent since it is from the Torah and there is no concern of blessing in vain; not so regarding blessings; therefore if he waited in Keriat Shema even only long enough to finish the sections he must return and only Keriat Shema alone; but the blessings — even if he waited in Keriat Shema long enough to finish the sections and also the blessings — he need not return, for we may go to leniency and say blessings are not related to Keriat Shema. And if he waited in the blessing of Keriat Shema he need not return to the beginning unless he waited long enough to finish from the beginning of Yotzer Ohr until Ga'al Yisrael — the Yerushalmi asks whether we say its blessings without it or not; if so we may go to leniency that we do not say its blessings without it; rather blessings with Keriat Shema are one matter; and so Chayei Adam — not like Dagul MeRevavah above. And to which blessing does he return? Behold if he stopped in Ahavah Rabbah he need not return to Yotzer Ohr in any manner — perhaps we hold its blessings without it; and automatically the same that blessings also have no connection one with another as we hold generally they do not hinder one another; and he need only return to the beginning of this blessing itself; only if he stopped in the middle of emet veyatziv it appears he returns to Keriat Shema for the reason above; and automatically if he stopped between blessing and blessing in all manners he need not return at all for the reason above — so appears to me with God's help; and accordingly I wrote all these matters in Mishnah Berurah.
