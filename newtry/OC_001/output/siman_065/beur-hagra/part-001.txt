**** ENGLISH ****
And there are those who say, etc. — for it was difficult for them to distinguish between all these and prayer, and they hold that all are equal; and Tosafot explain that in prayer there it deals with compulsion; and further it was difficult for them, for it says in Rosh HaShanah there "for me," etc., that it is not customary for a teacher to answer a student thus since he asks from him; and further, from where do we know that Rabbi Eliezer holds thus? Rather this is what it means: for me he does not hold that one may interrupt at all, as written in Berakhot 24b — therefore he need not return; but you, who interrupted — for you, because of compulsion, you must return to the beginning. And see Tosafot there 22b, s.v. ella, etc., and another view of Rabbenu Yonah there in Tosafot that even in prayer one need not return, unlike Rif, even because of compulsion; and thus is the view of Tosafot on Megillah cited above, who concluded and did not distinguish at all. But in Tosafot on Rosh HaShanah there, s.v. le-didi, etc., is another view that in all of them one must return even without compulsion; and according to all three of their views Rif's distinction does not hold. See in Milchamot.
