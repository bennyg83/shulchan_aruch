(8) <b>Several verses of Pesukei DeZimrah.</b> If he did not recite verses, he should not say Kaddish, but should begin Barechu without Kaddish, for one never finds Kaddish without verses preceding it, except when the chazzan says it after the repetition of the Amidah, or after the silent Amidah at Maariv, where the Kaddish refers back to the prayer. Magen Avraham writes that this is why the custom developed to conduct the shortened repetition of Krias Shema, meaning Barechu, as explained below in siman 69, after completion of the silent Amidah, so that Kaddish may refer back to the Amidah. Afterward, however, Kaddish has no basis unless several verses were first recited. Recently, some have begun conducting the shortened repetition after the final Kaddish and then reciting Kaddish again. This is improper, for what purpose is served by saying two Kaddishes consecutively? At minimum, they should be taught to recite several verses between the two Kaddishes.

(9) <b>Without praise.</b> Alternatively, it may follow prayer, as written in the preceding seif katan. Likewise, after one has studied a subject of the Oral Torah, Kaddish D'Rabbanan may be recited. See Magen Avraham, who concludes that this applies specifically if one afterward recites at least some aggadic passage, for this Kaddish was instituted primarily upon aggadah. Therefore, the universal Jewish custom is to recite after Pirkei Avos and Bameh Madlikin the teaching of Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya, or 'Rabbi Elazar said in the name of Rabbi Chanina: Torah scholars increase peace,' etc. Accordingly, when Mishnayos are studied, care should be taken to recite at the end the teaching of Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya or the like, so that Kaddish D'Rabbanan may then be recited. After Tanach, full Kaddish is recited even after only three verses. Kaddish may be said only if ten men were present in the synagogue during the recitation of the verses or the study, as will be explained in siman 55, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 2. (12) <b>And they were brought to him.</b> This applies both to an individual and to the chazzan. Although it is disrespectful to make the congregation wait, since there is no alternative it is permitted. In this case, the chazzan need not repeat verses, because the delay is brief. [Derech HaChaim] It appears that regarding an individual, the case is one in which he can finish putting on the tallis and tefillin before the chazzan reaches 'Yehei Shemei Rabbah' and Barechu, or where he has already heard Kaddish and Barechu. Otherwise, we rule in siman 25 that one may not interrupt between the arm tefillin and the head tefillin even to answer 'Yehei Shemei Rabbah' or Barechu; rather, he remains silent, listens, and has in mind what is being said. It is improper to arrange matters initially so that he must rely on this, even according to the rule that listening is equivalent to answering, as is evident in Berachos 21b, Tosafos beginning 'Ad,' and Rosh there. In such a case it is better to delay putting on the tefillin until the chazzan finishes Barechu, and then put them on. Regarding the blessings, see the next seif katan.

(13) <b>Between Kaddish.</b> Meaning, from the moment Kaddish begins, because Kaddish is connected to Barechu. See the later authorities, who write that the interval between Kaddish and Barechu has the same status in all respects as between sections. Immediately after Barechu, even before the blessing of Yotzer has begun, it has the status of the middle of a section, because from that point onward it is considered the beginning of Yotzer. The chazzan's essential intent in saying 'Bless Hashem' is that the congregation should then bless Him with the blessing of Yotzer. Therefore, whether an individual or the chazzan, if a tallis did not become available until after Kaddish began, he may put it on immediately but should not recite the blessing until after the prayer; then he should handle it and recite the blessing. If tefillin became available before Barechu, he may recite the blessings over them according to the consensus of the later authorities in siman 66 that one may recite the blessing over tefillin between sections. But if they did not become available until Barechu began, he may no longer recite the blessings. He should put them on without a blessing, and before reaching Ahavah Rabbah he should handle them and recite the blessings. See the next passage.

(14) <b>After he has said, etc.</b> This wording proves that even an individual may not interrupt. See the preceding seif katan. It appears that all this applies only to the case discussed in the text, where he has already completed Yishtabach and is about to begin Yotzer Or. But if he is still engaged in Pesukei DeZimrah, and certainly if he has just arrived to pray and wishes to pray in order without skipping Pesukei DeZimrah and beginning from Yotzer, and he hears Kaddish and Barechu, he does not have the status of being in the middle of a section. According to all opinions he may put on a tallis and tefillin and recite the blessings over them. See the glosses of the gaon Rabbi Chaim Tzanzar.
