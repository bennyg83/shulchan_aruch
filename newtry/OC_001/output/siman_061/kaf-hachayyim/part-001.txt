<b>1) At another window on the southern side stand six lintels, admitting those who engage in Krias Shema willingly and with concentration every day.</b> Rabbi Perachyah said: 'By God, as I gazed at that nearby window, the appointed angel named Na'aniel, who oversees the gate on the southern side, approached me. He said to me: Remain at your station and behold this window. No one has permission to enter there or look through it except those who engage in Krias Shema properly. For all who engage in Krias Shema properly, sixty appointed angels enter before Mashiach, crown him with holy crowns, and say to him: This is the crown of so-and-so, who engaged in Krias Shema properly. I asked him: My master, what constitutes Krias Shema properly? He answered: Pious one, a supernal secret lies here in Krias Shema. It contains four sections. The first is the section of unification, containing two unifications in twelve words: six words in the supernal unification and six in the other unification. The second section, "These words shall be," has forty-two words, in the mystery of the Name of forty-two letters' - this is Ana BeKoach, etc., as Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 24, writes in Zohar's name; see there, and Shaar HaKavanos, discourses 5 and 9 on the night. 'The third section, "It shall be if you heed," incorporates the right within the left and the left within the right. In the secret and mystery of the holy Chariot it extends through "You shall place them." This section rules over all, in the mystery of seventy-two words within the mystery of the seventy-two Names of the holy Chariot' - this is the Name of seventy-two derived from Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet, as Pri Etz Chaim, chapter 23, and Shaar HaKavanos there explain. 'The fourth section, "You shall place them," contains fifty words corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah. The section of tzitzis incorporates them all, and the sections ascend to the number of the limbs of man, etc. Come and see: Whoever properly completes the unification and concentrates upon these sections every day merits beholding these lintels and windows. He has dominion here, and none can impede him, provided he reads properly, concentrates upon these supernal mysteries, and takes hold of my right hand. He said to me: Pious one, come and see the honor of those who properly unify the holy Name and engage in Krias Shema in this manner. I entered one palace, and within it were 365 palaces, corresponding to the days of the year. Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov rule in that palace, with Adam HaRishon among them. I saw many companies of souls clothed in joy, in the embroidered designs of the Tree of Life and many hues of supernal glory, entering the palace. Yofiel, the great appointed angel over them all and the great appointed angel of Torah, was with them. They began and proclaimed: Adorn yourselves, holy ones of the world! Behold your children, who unify the holy Name and concentrate willingly upon the sections of Krias Shema. I saw Adam HaRishon attending to them. When the Krias Shema recited by a person enters, it first ascends to this palace, and Adam HaRishon senses its fragrance. If one letter is missing from Krias Shema, he does not accept it, for the structure of Adam, which has 248 limbs corresponding to the words including those repeated by the shaliach tzibbur, is incomplete. If he finds it proper, he accepts it; Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov take it from him and kiss it. Who has seen the joy? Who has seen the commotion among all the righteous in Gan Eden who come amid that joy? When that person comes to this world, who can behold his joy? The Patriarchs seat him beside them twice each day. Every day, dew from the King's head descends upon him. Through that dew they perceive and eat the food eaten by supernal angels, and know what was, what will be, and what is destined to be, until the Holy One, blessed is He, resurrects the bodies that decayed in this world and raises them in a properly perfected structure.' You now know the great virtue of one who recites Krias Shema with concentration. A person must therefore take care always to remember these teachings whenever he recites Krias Shema, evening and morning, an established statute that shall not pass. At minimum, he should apply his heart to understand the meaning of the words, omit no word or letter, and be especially mindful of the kavanos of the Names.

<b>2) One should bear in mind</b> that the first section, from VeAhavta through its end, excluding the verses Shema Yisrael, etc., and Baruch Shem, etc., contains forty-two words, corresponding to the forty-two-letter Name of Ana BeKoach, etc. The second section, from Vehayah Im, etc., through Vesamtem inclusive, contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet. From Es Devarai Eileh, etc., to the end of the section are fifty words, corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah. The section of tzitzis also contains seventy-two words when combined with the three repeated by the shaliach tzibbur. These correspond to the seventy-two words in the verses Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet, as Pri Etz Chaim and Shaar HaKavanos there explain. The Names in Vehayah appear in straight, reverse, and straight order, while those in the tzitzis section are all in straight order, as arranged in Rabbi Rashash's siddur. See Zohar, Beshalach 52a; Shaar Maamarei Razal, page 14a at the end; and Zohar Chadash, page 10, beginning of column 3. Rabbi Rashash's siddur sets them out as follows: In the first section, which contains forty-two words, one letter of the initials of Ana BeKoach - alef, beis, gimel, etc. - appears above each word. In the second section, one of the seventy-two Names derived from Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet appears above each word through Vesamtem. From there onward, one letter of the simple Name Ehyeh appears over each word until completing the forty letters contained in ten instances of Ehyeh; above each of the final ten words appears one complete simple Name Ehyeh. In the tzitzis section, one of the seventy-two Names derived from Vayisa, etc., appears above each word. See below, paragraph 16. Together with Shema Yisrael and Baruch Shem, etc., there are 248 words, corresponding to a person's 248 limbs, as Zohar Chadash, Acharei Mos 59a, states. It further says: 'Every person who recites Krias Shema properly causes each word to bestow influence upon its corresponding limb. If a person does not recite Krias Shema by day and by night, each of his limbs becomes filled with an evil spirit and with all the evil and harmful forces in the world.' One should therefore take care to recite the bedtime Krias Shema before becoming drowsy, so that he can say it properly. This will be explained in its place, with God's help.

<b>3) There: One should recite Krias Shema with concentration, etc.</b> Yerushalmi states that the three sections instituted for Krias Shema were chosen because they contain the Ten Commandments; examine them and you will find all ten. Tosafos, end of the first chapter of Berachos 12b. Zohar Chadash, Midrash Rus 95a, likewise states that the early chassidim instituted Krias Shema corresponding to the Ten Commandments and to the number of a person's limbs; see there. Yad Aharon writes in Hagahos HaTur: 'Since the works state that one must contemplate the Commandments while reciting Krias Shema, I saw fit to copy the correspondences here. At "Hashem Elokeinu," one accepts the commandment "I am Hashem your God"; at "Hashem Echad," "You shall have no other gods"; in the verse VeAhavta, "You shall not take Hashem's Name in vain"; in UChesavtam, "You shall not covet"; in VeAsafta, "You shall not steal"; in VaAvadtem Meheirah, "You shall not murder"; in Leman Yirbu Yemeichem, "Honor your father"; in VeLo Sasuru, "You shall not commit adultery"; in Leman Tizkeru, "Remember the Shabbos day," which is equivalent to the entire Torah; and at "I am Hashem your God," "You shall not bear false witness against your fellow."' Eliyah Rabbah 1 writes likewise in Kol Bo's name, as do Moreh BeEtzba 2:23 and Chesed LaAlafim 4.

<b>4) Abudraham writes</b> that the ayin of Shema and the dalet of Echad form the word eid, 'witness,' alluding to testimony. Another interpretation: Shema is an acronym for 'Raise your eyes on high - to Whom?' and for 'Shaddai, King of the world.' When should this be done? Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv. If you do so, you accept upon yourself the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, the initial letters of which spell Shema in reverse. Eliyah Rabbah 1 and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 95b, cite this.

<b>8) [Se'if 2] 'Which I command you today.'</b> This means that each day they should be as new in your eyes, etc. Maharchu writes in Shaar Maamarei Razal, in his commentary to Pirkei Avos: 'Our Sages established three times for prayer: Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv. Although they arranged an order for prayer and Krias Shema, these differ according to their respective times, for each has a new aspect and does not resemble another. One therefore should not pray as a merely fixed routine, Heaven forbid, but with his full concentration, like one praying a new prayer and reciting a new Krias Shema. Entirely new mochin are renewed with every prayer. There is no prayer in which a new light and flow are not generated, and one is not like another, etc. There is a great difference between a weekday prayer and a Shabbos prayer, between a Rosh Chodesh prayer and those of Yom Tov and Chol HaMoed. Not only that, but the Yom Tov prayers themselves differ - the prayer of Sukkos from that of Pesach, and so forth. Within each day, Shacharis does not resemble Minchah or Maariv, and so forth. Even on weekdays, the prayer of one day does not resemble that of the next; one is not like another. From the creation of the world through the future, no prayer has ever resembled another in any respect. The secret, briefly, is that the prayers refine the refinements of the seven kings. Each day and each prayer involves new and different refinements, not the same ones. In every prayer, sparks and refinements ascend that had never previously ascended. The order of the supernal union, yenikah, mochin, and their extension corresponds precisely to the order and value of the ascending sparks. This cannot be discussed at greater length here. This is the secret of our praying every day and at every time; one prayer a day does not suffice, because each prayer accomplishes a new aspect that no other prayer accomplished. This is sufficient. The same applies to every Krias Shema: each differs from another. All this concerns the mystery of the particulars. Regarding the general principles, however, all weekday morning recitations of Krias Shema have a single kavanah, all weekday evening recitations have a single kavanah, and so forth.' <b>9) There: Each day they should be as new in your eyes, etc.</b> Berachos 63b, chapter HaRoeh, says: 'Listen and hear, O Israel; this day you have become a people.' Was the Torah given to Israel that day? It was already the end of the forty years. Rather, this teaches that Torah is as beloved to those who study it each day as on the day it was given at Mount Sinai. Know this, for a person may recite Krias Shema morning and evening, yet if he fails to recite it one evening, he resembles one who never recited Krias Shema. The words, 'This day - behold, the day has come,' also teach that in serving Hashem, a person should place before his eyes only the light of the present day. A person has nothing in his world except the present day and hour, for tomorrow is an entirely different world. <b>12) There: The shaliach tzibbur concludes Hashem Elokeichem emes and repeats aloud, 'Hashem Elokeichem emes.'</b> This implies that the shaliach tzibbur should say emes twice: once upon concluding and once when repeating. Asarah Maamaros, maamar Em Kol Chai 1:17, however, writes that the shaliach tzibbur does not say emes quietly. An individual says emes quietly and does not repeat it. There are thereby precisely 248 words; if the shaliach tzibbur says emes quietly and again aloud, there are 249. Magen Avraham 1 and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99b, cite this. Rama MiPano likewise writes in Alfasi Zuta, chapter Hayah Korei: 'The halachah is that he does not repeat emes. An individual says it only at the end of the section, while the shaliach tzibbur says it only in the repetition of Hashem Elokeichem. Each person thus has emes, and it is never repeated.' Machazik Berachah 3 cites this. The universal custom is for the shaliach tzibbur to conclude Hashem Elokeichem aloud; the congregation answers emes, but he does not say emes. He then repeats Hashem Elokeichem emes aloud. The congregation does not repeat emes but continues VeYatziv, etc., so each person has only one emes. Chesed LaAlafim 3 writes that if one finishes before the chazzan, he should join Hashem Elokeichem immediately to emes, for the Sages said that one may not interrupt between Vayomer and Emes VeYatziv. He should not follow the common practice of waiting to say emes together with the chazzan, thereby interrupting between Elokeichem and emes. Those who do so err, since even an interruption due to fear is not permitted there, etc. This does not appear correct. The prohibition concerns interruption through speech; a silent pause alone is not an interruption, as Matteh Yehudah, siman 66:2, and Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 133, write; see there. Moreover, hearing is equivalent to answering. Since he says emes after the chazzan's reading, it is considered as though he says it after his own reading, and there is no concern.

<b>16) There: If he recites it alone, he should concentrate upon the fifteen vavs, etc.</b> If an individual is still reciting Krias Shema when the shaliach tzibbur finishes, the individual may remain silent and intend to hear Hashem Elokeichem from the shaliach tzibbur. This benefits him even though he subsequently completes Krias Shema. Mahari Ayash, Matteh Yehudah 2, Likkutei Orach Chaim, page 60c, cited by Machazik Berachah in the Kuntres Acharon to this siman 2. Maamar Mordechai 2 writes likewise in Seder HaYom's name, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99b; Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 135; Shaarei Teshuvah 2; and Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi, ibid. 50. According to the Arizal's words in Shaar HaKavanos, end of discourse 5 and discourses 8 on Krias Shema and 9 on the night, and in Rabbi Rashash's siddur, however, the first section from VeAhavta contains forty-two words corresponding to the forty-two-letter Name of alef-beis-gimel, etc., and to Chochmah, Binah, and Daas. The second section through Vesamtem contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet - Vayisa in straight order, Vayavo in reverse, and Vayet in straight order - and to Chesed, Gevurah, and Tiferes. From Vesamtem to the end of the section are fifty words, corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah. Forty correspond to ten Names Ehyeh containing forty letters; ten correspond to the aggregate of the ten Names Ehyeh. They correspond to the lower two-thirds of Tiferes. The tzitzis section contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet, all in straight order, and to Netzach, Hod, and Yesod. Repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes draws influence from the upper joints of the Netzach, Hod, and Yesod of Zeir Anpin, and from the moach within them, into Zeir Anpin's Netzach, Hod, and Yesod, thereby forming the Chochmah, Binah, and Daas of Malchus; see there at length. Those familiar with the mystical wisdom know that this kavanah can occur only after one recites the passages in order - after completing all three sections through Hashem Elokeichem and then repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes. Otherwise he reverses and confuses the order effected above, Heaven forbid. The only remedy is therefore either to hear the shaliach tzibbur and intend to fulfill the obligation when he is at the same point, or to repeat the words himself. Hearing them out of their proper place with intent to fulfill cannot accord with the kabbalists. Nor, according to them, can concentration upon the fifteen vavs substitute for repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes, because it confuses the order. Rather, he must hear them from the shaliach tzibbur or say Hashem Elokeichem emes himself, as stated. All the more does saying El Melech Neeman instead confuse the order, for those words have no place here, as the Arizal's words demonstrate and as stated below in paragraph 19. Although we do not perform the kavanos, we have written in many places that one must follow the order appropriate to them. Independently, we already wrote above that an individual may repeat Hashem Elokeichem, and nothing is lost thereby. Maamar Mordechai further writes that if one did not finish Krias Shema at precisely the same time as the shaliach tzibbur but reaches that point while the shaliach tzibbur repeats these three words, he should remain silent and hear them from the shaliach tzibbur, then complete his reading. They count toward his 248 words, for what difference does it make whether the shaliach tzibbur says them first or he says them first? This appears incorrect, for the repetition has a separate kavanah, as explained above. How can it precede his completion? He reverses the order. If this occurs, he should not intend to fulfill through the shaliach tzibbur but repeat the words himself, as stated. See also paragraphs 1 and 2 above.

<b>26) There: He should prolong the dalet of Echad, etc.</b> This means that when articulating the dalet, he should prolong the breath issuing from his mouth for the time needed to contemplate in his heart, etc. He should not act like those who prolong the dalet by pronouncing it with a tzeirei beneath it, thereby ruining the meaning.
