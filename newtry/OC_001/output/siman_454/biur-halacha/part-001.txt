<b> שאינו קרוי לחם</b> -  עיין במ"ב. ולא נתבאר כמה מיקרי מורסן מרובין וגם היכי מיירי אם עירב מורסן אחר או אפילו במורסנן כל שיש הרבה בטל עי"ז תורת לחם ולא דמי להא דס"א דכ"ז שלא פירשו אינם מגרעין כח הלחם ואדרבה עוד מצטרפין להשלים השיעור דשם מיירי בשהיה מעט מורסן עיין במחה"ש מה שכתב בזה ומצאתי להרשב"א שכתב בזה וז"ל דהא דאמר רשב"ל בירושלמי כל שלא עירב בה מורסן אין לפרש שכל שיש בה מורסן אינה חייבת בחלה שהרי שנינו ה' רבעים הן וסובנן ומורסנן אלא לומר שפעמים מעורבין בה מורסנן כ"כ שאינה ראויה לרועים א"נ יש לפרש שעירב בתוכה מורסן לאחר שניטל מתוכה לפי שאין דרך ב"א לעשות כן וכדתנן נטל מורסנן וכו' עכ"ל מובא בב"י יורה דעה סימן ש"ל ומתבאר מדבריו דהשיעור של מורסן מרובין כל שאין הרועה אוכל מחמתן את הפת ונראה דדין זה לא תלי בטעם כעיקר כההיא דסימן תנ"ג גבי אורז דשאני התם דגם אורז הוא מין אוכל ולהכי אם יש בו טעם דגן ומכ"ש בשיעור אכילת פרס יוצאין בו ולכמה פוסקים אפילו בחדא כזית וכמו שביארנו שם משא"כ הכא אפילו אכל ככר שלם אינו יוצא דהסובין והמורסן מפסידין הפת ועל ידם אינו ראוי לאכילה ולא דמי נמי להא דסימן תנ"ה ס"ו לענין תבלין דסגי בנתינת טעם דשאני הכא דאין דרך בני אדם לאכול פת כזה וכמו שכתב הרשב"א הנ"ל ובפמ"ג משמע דגם בעניננו סגי באכילת פרס ולענ"ד נראה כמו שכתבתי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That it is not called bread.</b> See in the Mishnah Berurah. And it was not explained how much is called "much mursan," and also in what case it deals: whether he mixed other mursan, or even with its own mursan, whenever there is much, the status of bread is nullified by that. And it is not comparable to that of se'if 1, where so long as they have not been separated they do not diminish the power of the bread, and on the contrary they even join to complete the measure; for there it deals with there having been little mursan; see in Machatzit HaShekel what he wrote on this. And I found that the Rashba wrote on this, and this is his language: that what Resh Lakish said in the Yerushalmi, "all that he did not mix mursan into it," one should not explain that whenever there is mursan in it it is not liable for challah, for behold we learned: five quarters they are, and their sovin and their mursan; rather, to say that sometimes their mursan is so mixed into it that it is not fit for shepherds; alternatively, one can explain that he mixed mursan into it after it had been removed from it, because it is not the way of people to do so, and as we learned in the mishnah: he removed their mursan, etc.; end quote. Brought in the Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 330. And from his words it is explained that the measure of much mursan is whenever the shepherd does not eat of the bread because of them. And it appears that this law does not depend on taste-as-essence like that of siman 453 regarding rice; for that case is different, for rice too is a kind of food, and therefore if there is in it the taste of grain, and all the more so in the measure of eating a pras, one fulfills with it, and according to several posekim even with one kezayit, as we explained there; which is not so here: even if he ate a whole loaf he does not fulfill, for the sovin and the mursan ruin the bread, and through them it is not fit for eating. And it is also not comparable to that of siman 455, se'if 6, regarding spices, where giving taste suffices; for here it is different, for it is not the way of people to eat such bread, as the aforementioned Rashba wrote. And in the Peri Megadim it is implied that also in our matter the measure of eating a pras suffices; and in my humble opinion it appears as I wrote.

<b>There is none in this time, etc. Forbidden.</b> See in the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that since we are not expert we treat it as absolute chametz; and so it is in truth explained in Hilchos Ritz Giat in the name of Rav Sherira, and this is his language on folio 91 there: even though the Sages said chalut, etc., and boiling water with a strong boil are those that bubble, etc.: we do not rely on this to act in practice, for we do not know how it will be guarded that it not come to chimutz, and perhaps it will cool from this corner or from the edge and come to chimutz; and we have not heard in the two yeshivos of anyone who permitted to do so except for a sick person and for a woman in childbirth alone, according to experts who say that he has a remedy in this and if not he comes to danger, or at a time when they say if they feed him matzah he will be endangered, etc.; and he has nothing to eat but chalut in boiling water: they feed him immediately, and he must eat immediately, and they leave over nothing of it at all; and see there that he also wrote a responsum of Rav Hai Gaon on this, and this is his language there: chalita in boiling water is certainly matzah, etc.; however, all the early Rabbis of many many generations say: this chalita, we do not know how it is, and they did not permit at all to do chalita with dough; and many many labored to permit for them barley water, that they boil boiling water and cast barley into it, whether in dough or in chalita, and the Geonim did not permit for them, for they say we do not know the permitted chalita how it is, etc. And regarding a sick person the early Geonim ruled: when the illness is hard upon him and he cannot taste anything on Pesach and he is endangered, it is permitted to do for him what he needs according to physicians, for with everything one heals except idolatry; end quote there. And so wrote Maharam Chalawah on the sugya of chalita; and see there: after bringing the aforementioned words of Rav Hai he wrote: and Rav Alfasi explained his words further and forbade for everyone like other issurim that stand on their law, that for a sick person who has danger it is permitted and not for another; end quote there on folio 39b; see there. And from these words it is proven that one must forbid chalita even after the fact and even in benefit and in keeping, since it is like other issurim; and it is also explicit that for a sick person without danger we are not lenient. And we elaborated on this to exclude the words of the Magen Avraham who is in doubt here that possibly it is only ideally as a mere matter; and had he seen the words of the aforementioned early authorities he would not have been in doubt here. And as for the proof he brought from liver, see in Eliyah Rabbah who rejected this. And further I wonder at the Radbaz, part 1, siman 241, whom they asked whether it is permitted to give a sick person barley water to drink on Pesach; and see there that he argued that possibly the Geonim's stringency was only with dough that hastens to become chametz before it is cooked by the boiling of the water, but with barley that is hard they did not decree on chalita; and he further wrote that even if they decreed also in such a case, nevertheless for a sick person, etc., we rely on our expertise, for we see that the barley is dry and strong, etc.; and even for a sick person without danger I see to permit a matter like this, etc.; see there. And in the aforementioned responsa of the Geonim it is explicit that also with barley water this enactment applies, and it is also explicit that for illness without danger they did not permit. And see in Machatzit HaShekel what he wrote on this, that necessarily it deals with where there is no danger; and his words are astonishing. And see in Shaarei Teshuvah who also leans to be lenient to place chaluted barley on the stomach even without danger, and he supported his pegs with the words of Chok Yaakov; and as it appears he held that Chok Yaakov permits keeping it after Pesach even intact; and that is not the intent of Chok Yaakov, who refers only to a mixture of any amount, as we copied in the Mishnah Berurah, and so the Shulchan Aruch HaRav copied. And with chalut intact it is certainly forbidden to keep it, as is explained in the aforementioned responsum of Rav Sherira who wrote, "and they leave over nothing of it at all." Therefore for the practical law one should not be lenient against all the aforementioned Geonim, and one should not give a sick person barley water to drink unless he is in danger; and likewise regarding chaluting barley and placing them on the stomach, as we copied in the Mishnah Berurah. Except that one can say that the Geonim themselves were stringent only regarding drinking, where there is concern of kares; but regarding placing on the stomach, where there is in this only concern of a mere negative commandment of bal yeira'eh, possibly they would not have been stringent in this and for every sick person they permitted; but it is difficult to say so, for what difference is there between a negative-commandment issur and a kares issur? And this requires further study.

<b> אין אדם יוצא י"ח במצה וכו'</b> -  עיין במ"ב ועיין בחידושי רע"א ובש"א שהביאו שיטת הריטב"א דדעתו דאף בגזל מצה יש שינוי מעשה דכיון דלעסיה קנייה בשינוי מעשה והמצוה מתקיימת אח"כ בבליעת המצה דהא בלע מצה יצא וא"כ דומה לגזל חטים או קמח ועשאו מצה דיצא ואף דלדינא בודאי נקטינן כשיטת השו"ע שהוא דעת רוב הפוסקים דס"ל דלא יצא וצריך לחזור ולאכול מצה אחרת משלו (ואפשר דס"ל דזה מקרי שהקנין בא ע"י המצוה גופא דקי"ל בסי' תרמ"ט דלא מהני דאף שאם בלע בלא לעיסה יצא הא עכ"פ לכתחלה צריך לעיסה כדי שיהיה טעם מצה בפיו) מ"מ לענין שיהיה צריך לחזור ולברך כשאוכלה אפשר דיש לחוש לדעת הריטב"א שכבר יצא בדיעבד וע"כ לא יברך אח"כ מצאתי שכן הסכים במגן אלף. ולענין מרור אם יוצא בדיעבד בגזול דעת הכנה"ג בשם מהרי"ו דיצא והפר"ח חולק עליו דאתקוש מצה ומרור להדדי כמבואר בפרק כל שעה ועיין בש"א סימן צ"ד דדעתו דגם במצה גזולה הפסול הוא משום מצוה הבאה בעבירה (והיא ג"כ דעת הלבוש) ולהכי גבי מרור דאינו אלא מדרבנן הו"ל מצוה הבאה בעבירה בדרבנן דלדעת המחבר בסימן תרמ"ט ס"ה יוצא בדיעבד וכ"כ במקו"ח ע"ש אכן לדעת הרמ"א שם בהג"ה שכתב שאנו נוהגין כהפוסקים דסוברין דגם בדרבנן מצוה הבאה בעבירה פסול בודאי גם במרור לא יצא [וראיתי דבר פלא שכנה"ג כתב בשם מהרי"ו בסימן קצ"ג דיוצא במרור גזול ועיינתי שם ומבואר להיפוך וזה לשונו שם מהר"ר אנשיל ממארפורק נסתפק כיון דמרור אתקש למצה וגזול פסול אם כן ישראל לא יעקור מרור (בעצמו) מן הקרקע מידי דהוי אאסא דאמר להו וכו' להני דמזבני אסא וכו' עי"ש בפרק לולב הגזול ונ"ל דלא דמי דהתם גבי אסא מתקיים בקרקע משו"ה הוי כקרקע ע"ש הרי מבואר להדיא ממהרי"ו דגם הוא מודה שאם אחד גוזל מרור מיד חבירו דלא יצא ורק לענין קציצה מן הקרקע ס"ל דלא מקרי מחובר מקודם ולא דמי לאסא] ועכ"פ לענין ברכה בודאי יש ליזהר כשנוטל מרור אחר לאכול שלא לברך עוד מחדש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A person does not fulfill his obligation with matzah, etc.</b> See in the Mishnah Berurah; and see in the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger and in Shaagas Aryeh, who brought the view of the Ritva that his opinion is that even with stolen matzah there is shinui maaseh, for since he chewed it he acquired it by shinui maaseh, and the mitzvah is fulfilled afterward with the swallowing of the matzah, for behold one who swallowed matzah fulfilled; and if so it is similar to one who stole wheat or flour and made it into matzah, who fulfilled. And even though for the practical law we certainly hold like the view of the Shulchan Aruch, which is the view of most of the posekim who hold that he did not fulfill and must go back and eat other matzah of his own (and possibly they hold that this is called that the acquisition comes through the mitzvah itself, for we hold in siman 649 that it does not help; for even though if he swallowed without chewing he fulfilled, nevertheless ideally chewing is required so that there be the taste of matzah in his mouth): nevertheless, regarding whether he must go back and recite a blessing when he eats it, possibly one should be concerned for the view of the Ritva that he already fulfilled after the fact, and therefore he should not bless afterward. I found that Magen Elef agreed so. And regarding maror, whether one fulfills after the fact with stolen: the view of Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of Mahariv is that he fulfilled, and the Peri Chadash disagrees with him, for matzah and maror are equated to each other, as is explained in chapter Kol Shaah. And see in Shaagas Aryeh, siman 94, whose view is that also with stolen matzah the disqualification is because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression (and that is also the view of the Levush), and therefore regarding maror, which is only rabbinic, it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression in a rabbinic [matter], for according to the Mechaber in siman 649, se'if 5, he fulfills after the fact; and so wrote Mekor Chaim; see there. However, according to the Rama there in the gloss, who wrote that we practice like the posekim who hold that also in a rabbinic [matter] a mitzvah that comes through a transgression is invalid, certainly also with maror he did not fulfill. [And I saw a wondrous thing: that Kenesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahariv in siman 193 that one fulfills with stolen maror; and I looked there and it is explained to the contrary, and this is his language there: Moreinu HaRav R' Anshil of Marpork was in doubt: since maror is equated to matzah and stolen is invalid, if so a Jew should not uproot maror (himself) from the ground, just as with myrtle, of which he said to them, etc., to those who sell myrtle, etc.; see there in chapter Lulav HaGazul. And it appears to me that it is not comparable, for there regarding myrtle it continues to live in the ground, therefore it is like ground; see there. Behold it is explained explicitly from Mahariv that he too admits that if one steals maror from his fellow's hand he did not fulfill, and only regarding cutting from the ground he holds that it is not called attached beforehand, and it is not comparable to myrtle.] And in any event regarding blessing one must certainly be careful, when he takes other maror to eat, not to recite a blessing again anew.

<b>And regarding blessing, etc.</b> The posekim wrote that specifically with stolen one does not fulfill, because the matzah itself comes through a transgression; which is not so if he carried matzah from a private domain to a public domain when Pesach fell on Shabbos: he fulfills his obligation with it and also recites a blessing over it, because he transgressed a transgression and the matzah itself is not involved in a transgression.
