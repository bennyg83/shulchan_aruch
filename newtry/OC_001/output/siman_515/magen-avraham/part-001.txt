<b>לטלטלן אסור</b>. דלא חזי לאכילה וכמוקצה דמי דכיון דאסורין באכילה מחמת איסור היום אסורין בטלטול (הרי"ף והרא"ש) וביש"ש חולק ע"ז והאריך וכ' דטעם איסור טלטול משום שהוא מוקצה ולכן מליל שני ואילך מותר בטלטול אף על פי שאסור באכילה עכ"ל וכל ראיותיו אין מוכרחות דמ"ש מדרשב"ג סותר שם בעצמו בהג"ה סי' ה' ומ"ש מפירות שיצאו חוץ למקומן דשרי בטלטול כמ"ש ססי' ת"ה שאני התם דיש להם היתר אכילה אם חזרו למקומן אבל בדבר שאין לו היתר אכילה אסור בטלטול כמ"ש סס"ד בהג"ה וכ"מ בב"י סי' תקפ"ו בשם הג"א והפוסקים וכ"מ ברשב"א סימן תתמ"ז גם מ"ש היש"ש והוסיף בדברי הרי"ף והרא"ש דכיון דאסור באכילה מחמת איסור היום אסור בטלטול כוונתו לתרץ דהא נבילה אסורה באכילה ושרי בטלטול והטעם משום דאין איסורו מחמת איסור היום ול"נ דטעמא משום דנבילה חזי לכלבים אבל זה לאו דעתיה למשדיי' לכלבים ואסור דומיא דטבל וכמ"ש סי' ש"ח סל"א ל"ב וכ"כ התו' בביצה דף ו' ע"ב דבעי' מוכן ועומד ועמ"ש סי' תמ"ו וסי' תקי"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to move them.</b> Since they are unfit for eating, they are regarded as muktzeh. Because they are forbidden for consumption due to the prohibition of the day, they are also forbidden to be moved, as stated by Rif and Rosh. Yam Shel Shlomo disagrees at length and writes that the prohibition against moving them is because they are muktzeh; therefore, from the second night onward they may be moved even though they remain forbidden for consumption. None of his proofs is conclusive. What he writes from Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is contradicted by his own gloss there in siman 5. His proof from produce that left its permitted area, which may be moved as stated at the end of siman 405, is not comparable, because there it may be eaten if it returns to its original area. An item that has no possibility of permitted consumption may not be moved, as stated in the gloss at the end of seif 4. This is also implied by Beit Yosef, siman 586, in the name of Hagahos Ashri and the poskim, and by Rashba, siman 847. Yam Shel Shlomo further explains the words of Rif and Rosh, that because the item is forbidden for consumption due to the prohibition of the day it is forbidden to move, as answering the objection that neveilah is forbidden for consumption yet may be moved; its prohibition is not caused by the prohibition of the day. In my view, however, the reason is that neveilah is fit for dogs, whereas one does not intend to throw this item to dogs. It is therefore forbidden like tevel, as stated in siman 308, seifim 31 and 32. Tosafot likewise write on Beitzah 6b that the item must be prepared and standing ready for use. See what I wrote in simanim 446 and 513.

<b>בכדי שיעשו</b>. שלא יהנה ממלאכת י"ט ויש לדקדק מדנקטו הגמ' והפוסקים עכו"ם שהביא דורון וכו' משמע דאם אין מביא בחנם רק הישראל קצץ לו מעות שיביא לו דגים מעי"ט והעכו"ם שהה והביאן בי"ט מותרין לערב מיד ואם הוא דבר שאין במינו במחובר ומחוסר צידה מותר אפי' בי"ט אפי' הביאו חוץ לתחום דהא אפי' עשה העכו"ם מלבוש בי"ט מותר ללבוש אם קצץ דעכו"ם אדעתיה דנפשיה קעביד כמ"ש סי' רנ"ב ס"ז ק"ו כשבא מח"ל ואפי' להמחמירים התם היינו במלאכה גמורה אבל בתחומין מודה וה"ה במלאכה גמורה שרי במוצאי י"ט ראשון בכ"ש וכ"מ בב"י סי' תקי"ז שכ' בשם המרדכי דההוא עובדא דליפת' דמחוז' ודיכרי דאיתא פ"ג דעירובין חנוני ישראל אמר קודם י"ט להביאן להם ופסקו את דמיהם ואיחרו העכו"ם והביאום בי"ט וכו' וכ"כ הראב"ן וא"כ ק' היאך התיר רבא ואמר אדעתיה דעכו"ם קאתי אלא ה"ק עכו"ם אדעת' דנפשיה קעביד כיון שקצץ עמו קודם י"ט ועסי' רמ"ז מיהו י"ל דדמי לעכו"ם שהדליק נר בשבת שאסור להשתמש בו אפי' קצץ משום דהעכו"ם עשאו אדעתא דהכי שהישראל ישתמש בו בשבת משא"כ מנעלים שלא נעשו דוקא להשתמש בו בשבת א"כ אפשר דמיני מאכל דמי לנר וצ"ע די"ל דוקא נר צריך להשתמש בו עכשיו דמחר בא ואיננו משא"כ מיני מאכל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For the time needed for it to be done.</b> This is so that he will not benefit from melachah performed on Yom Tov. One may infer from the Gemara and the poskim, who discuss a non-Jew who brought a gift, that if he does not bring it gratuitously, but the Jew fixed a payment with him before Yom Tov to bring fish, and the non-Jew delayed and brought them on Yom Tov, they are permitted immediately that evening. If the item is neither attached to the ground nor in need of trapping, it is permitted even on Yom Tov, even if brought from outside the techum. After all, if a non-Jew made a garment on Yom Tov, it may be worn where a fixed payment was agreed upon, because the non-Jew acts for his own benefit, as stated in siman 252, seif 7. This is certainly so where he merely brought it from outside the techum. Even those who are stringent there are stringent only regarding full melachah, but they agree regarding techum. Likewise, after full melachah it is permitted after the first day of Yom Tov once the time needed to perform it has elapsed. This is also implied by Beit Yosef, siman 517, who cites Mordechai concerning the incident of the turnips of Mechoza and the rams mentioned in the third chapter of Eruvin. Jewish shopkeepers told the non-Jews before Yom Tov to bring them, and fixed the price; the non-Jews delayed and brought them on Yom Tov. Raavan writes similarly. If so, how did Rava permit them by saying that they came for the non-Jews? Rather, the meaning is that the non-Jews acted for their own benefit because the price had been fixed before Yom Tov. See siman 247. One may nevertheless argue that this resembles a non-Jew who lights a lamp on Shabbos, which may not be used even where a fixed payment was agreed upon, because he made it with the intention that the Jew use it on Shabbos. Shoes, by contrast, were not made specifically for Shabbos use. Food may therefore resemble a lamp. This requires study, for perhaps a lamp is different because it must be used now, since tomorrow it will be gone, unlike food.

<b>ויש מחמירין</b>. דאם יהא מותר בי"ט שני יאמר לעכו"ם להביא בי"ט ראשון כדי שיאכל בי"ט שני ולפי טעם זה מותר לאחר במוצאי י"ט ראשון מיד דלא גזרינן שיאמר לעכו"ם להביא כדי שיאכל חבירו דאין אדם חוטא ולא לו אבל לסברא קמייתא הטעם שלא יהנה ממלאכת י"ט אף אחר צריך להמתין בכ"ש ואח"כ מותר לכ"ע וכ"פ יש"ש אבל הסמ"ק פסק דאף לאחר אסור עד מוצאי י"ט שני וכ"פ הב"ח וכ"ד רמ"א אם לא לצורך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some are stringent.</b> Their concern is that if it were permitted on the second day of Yom Tov, one might tell a non-Jew to bring it on the first day so that he could eat it on the second. According to this reason, it is permitted immediately after the first day to another person, because we do not decree that one might tell a non-Jew to bring it so that his fellow could eat it; a person does not sin for no benefit to himself. According to the first view, however, the reason is that one may not benefit from melachah performed on Yom Tov. Another person must therefore also wait the time needed for it to be done, after which it is permitted according to all opinions. Yam Shel Shlomo rules this way. Semak, however, rules that even another person may not use it until after the second day of Yom Tov. Bach rules likewise, and this is also Rama's position except in a case of need.

<b>להקל לאחרים</b>. פי' כשאותו אחר הזמין אורחים אז מתירי' לו דסמכי' אכל הפוסקים שפסקו דלא כהסמ"ק ולזה נתכוין מהרא"י וכ"כ היש"ש דלא כמה שהבין הרב"י ע"ש, ומ"מ אפשר שגם דין הרב"י אמת דדוקא לבני ביתו אסור אבל כשזימן אורחים אחר שהביאו לו הדורון כאחר דמי, והב"ח כתב מיהו במהרי"ל נסתפק במי שזימן אורחים אם יש להם דין בני ביתו אם לאו עכ"ל, ובכ"ה כ' וז"ל רדב"ז ח"ב סי' רנ"ג כ' דאפי' זימן אורחים במקרה אסורים לאכול בביתו אבל בביתם מותר ואם הם סמוכי' עליו באותו י"ט בכל ענין אסו' עכ"ל ול"נ דכשזימן אורחים שאינן מעיר הזאת יש להקל במוצי"ט ראשון בכ"ש דסמכי' אסברא קמיית' ועסי' שנ"ג סס"א, ואפשר דאף הוא בעצמו מותר לאכול עמה דאין זה כבודם שלא יאכל עמה' עסי' קס"ח ס"ה, ואפשר דאף רדב"ז לא אסר אלא בדבר שבא מח"ל דאסור לכ"ע בי"ט לדידי' ולאחרי' שרי דאז אורחי' חשיבי כבני ביתו אבל הכא סמכי' אסבר' קמיית' כנ"ל ואם יש לו מאותו המין לא מקרי לצורך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To be lenient for others.</b> Meaning, when that other person invited guests, it is permitted for him because we rely upon all the poskim who rule unlike Semak. This is what Terumas HaDeshen intended, and Yam Shel Shlomo writes likewise, contrary to Beit Yosef's understanding; see there. Nevertheless, Beit Yosef's ruling may also be correct. It is forbidden specifically for members of the recipient's household, but if he invited guests after the gift had been brought, they may be regarded as other people. Bach writes that Maharil was uncertain whether invited guests have the status of household members. Knesses HaGedolah cites Radbaz, part 2, siman 253, as ruling that even guests invited incidentally may not eat it in the recipient's house, though it is permitted in their own homes. If they depend upon him for their food throughout that Yom Tov, it is forbidden in all circumstances. In my view, where he invited guests who are not residents of that city, one may be lenient after the first day of Yom Tov once the time needed for it to be done has elapsed, because we may rely upon the first view. See siman 353, end of seif 1. It is possible that the host himself may also eat with them, since it would be disrespectful for him not to eat with them; see siman 168, seif 5. Perhaps even Radbaz was stringent only with an item brought from outside the techum, which is forbidden on Yom Tov to the person for whom it was brought but permitted to others, in which case guests are regarded as household members. Here, however, we may rely upon the first view, as explained. If he already has some of that same kind, the case is not considered one of need.

<b>שהובאו בשבילו</b>. פי' בתחלה צדן לעצמו ואח"כ נמלך והביאן לישראל, מותרין לו אף להיש מחמירין דלעיל ומיירי בתוך התחו' עס"ה, כ' הב"ח סי' שכ"ה אף על פי שהעכו"ם מביא דורון לישראל לא אמרינן דודאי לקטינהו בשבילו אלא הוי ספק עד שיודע שלקטו בשבילו עכ"ל ופשט' דתלמוד' לא משמע כן דהא אמרי' עכו"ם שהביא דורון לישראל אם יש במינו במחוב' משמע אף על גב דלא ידעי' שנתלשו היום וגם לא ידעי' שנתלשו בשביל ישראל בעי' בכ"ש דאמרי' כיון שהביאו לו מסתמא לקטן בשבילו והתו' בעירובין דף ל"ט כתבו דההו' עובדא דבר טבי' דאת' לבי ריש גלות' דשרי בי"ט מיירי שאיתצד מאיליו ע"י שהיו מצודות פרוסי' מעי"ט ולא עכו"ם שהבי' דורון לישראל הוי עכ"ל משמע דאי הוי עכו"ם שהביא דורון הוי אסור בי"ט שני אפי' לא ידעי' שניצוד בשביל ישראל ואפשר דגם הב"ח לא הקיל אלא בעי בכ"ש במוצי"ט שני:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That were brought for him.</b> Meaning, the non-Jew first trapped them for himself and later decided to bring them to the Jew. They are permitted to him even according to the stringent opinion cited above. The case concerns an item brought from within the techum; see seif 5. Bach writes in siman 325 that although a non-Jew brings a gift to a Jew, we do not say that he certainly gathered it for him. Rather, the matter remains doubtful until it is known that he gathered it for the Jew. The plain sense of the Gemara does not support this. It states that if a non-Jew brought a gift to a Jew and there is a species like it attached to the ground, one must wait the time needed for it to be done, even though we do not know that it was detached that day or that it was detached for the Jew. We presume that since he brought it to him, he gathered it for him. Tosafot on Eruvin 39b write that the incident of the deer brought to the house of the Exilarch and permitted on Yom Tov concerned an animal trapped on its own in nets that had been spread before Yom Tov. It was not a gift brought by a non-Jew to a Jew. This implies that had it been such a gift, it would have been forbidden on the second day of Yom Tov even without knowledge that it was trapped for the Jew. It is possible that Bach was lenient only regarding whether one must wait the time needed for it to be done after the second day of Yom Tov.

<b>אסורים</b>. ואם הביאן לישראל יש מחלוקת אי בעי' בכ"ש עסי' שכ"ה ס"ז ומקשה בסמ"ק הא אמרי' בגמ' אם יש מאותו המין במחובר בעי' בכ"ש משמע דאפי' הוא ספק בעי' בכ"ש ול"נ דהכא מיירי שיש ספק אם יש מאותו המין במחוב' דהוי קצת כעין ס"ס ולא בעינן בכ"ש (יש"ש) ודוחק דל' הסמ"ג לא משמע כן ועב"י וע"ק למה הביא הטור והרב"י בסי' שכ"ה ס"ז ב' דיעות סתם ולא כ' שיש ספק אם יש באותו המין במחובר לכן נ"ל דהכא מיירי בעכו"ם שמביא דורון אמרי' שמתכוין להביא מן המשובח ומביא מאותן שליקט או צד היום ה"ה המביא למכור וכמ"ש הר"ן ולעיל מיירי שהעכו"ם א"ל מעי"ט ליתן לו פירות והלך לבית העכו"ם ונתן לו וא"כ יש לספק שמא לקטן בשביל ישראל והיה בדעתו להביא לו אותן פירות או שמא לא לקטן בשביל ישראל והיה בדעתו להביא לו פירות אחרי' ולכן מתיר אותן מספק ולא בעי' בכ"ש מה"ט נ"ל דאפי' למאן דשרי ספק מוכן בי"ט שני כמ"ש סי' תצ"ז ס"ד היינו דוק' במצודות פרוסות מעי"ט ויש ספק אימתי ניצודו וכמ"ש הר"ן אבל בעכו"ם שהבי' דורון אסור דכודאי משוינן להו וכמש"ל ומכ"ש לר"ת דהטעם דאסור שמא יאמר לעכו"ם להביאם א"כ אין חילוק בין ראשון לשני כנ"ל דלא כמ"ש הב"י כאן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They are forbidden.</b> If the non-Jew brought them to a Jew, there is a dispute whether one must wait the time needed for them to be done; see siman 325, seif 7. Semak asks that the Gemara states that if there is a species like it attached to the ground one must wait that period, which implies that even in a doubtful case one must wait. Yam Shel Shlomo answers that here the uncertainty is whether there is any such species attached to the ground, creating something like a double doubt, and therefore no waiting is required. This is difficult, for Semag's wording does not imply that distinction; see Beit Yosef. It is also difficult why Tur and Beit Yosef cite two opinions without qualification in siman 325, seif 7, and do not state that the case is one of uncertainty whether such a species is attached to the ground. It therefore appears to me that when a non-Jew brings a gift, we assume that he intends to bring the finest produce and therefore brings items gathered or trapped that day. The same applies when he brings them to sell, as Ran writes. Above, however, the case is that the non-Jew told the Jew before Yom Tov that he would give him fruit, and the Jew then went to the non-Jew's house and received it. There is therefore doubt whether he gathered it for the Jew, intending from the outset to give him those fruits, or whether he did not gather it for him and initially intended to give him other fruit. We therefore permit these fruits because of the doubt and do not require waiting. For this reason, it appears to me that even according to the view permitting doubtful preparation on the second day of Yom Tov, as stated in siman 497, seif 4, that applies only where nets were spread before Yom Tov and there is doubt when the animal was trapped, as Ran writes. When a non-Jew brings a gift, however, it is forbidden because we treat it as certain. This is all the more so according to Rabbeinu Tam, whose reason for the prohibition is lest one tell the non-Jew to bring it. According to that reason there is no distinction between the first and second days, contrary to Beit Yosef's ruling here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And specifically where he had it in mind, etc.</b> I shall present the source of this ruling, from which the law will become clear. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashba that the permission for an item brought from outside the techum applies specifically to produce belonging to a non-Jew. Produce belonging to a Jew is forbidden to everyone because it is muktzeh. Since it was outside the techum, its owners consciously set it aside. Property belonging to a Jew requires preparation, whereas property belonging to a non-Jew does not. At the end of the siman, Beit Yosef cites from Rashba the law of a Jew who sent a gift. Rama therefore distinguishes here by requiring that the recipient had it in mind. This is difficult. The question presented to Rashba concerned an ordinary case of a Jew who sent a gift, and Rashba should therefore have answered that it is permitted only where the recipient had it in mind. It is also difficult that if the item were muktzeh because it was outside the techum, having it in mind would not help, as stated in siman 498, seif 3. Furthermore, at the end of siman 405 we rule that produce that left the techum may be eaten by the residents of the city to which it was brought, as Rashi writes. That teaching concerns Yom Tov, when muktzeh applies, as Ran and Maggid Mishneh explain, yet it is permitted. One may answer that there the produce was within its owner's techum at twilight and therefore was not set aside. All Jews may eat it with him. It is obvious that if a person brought an item with him from outside the techum, his fellow may eat it with him, and Raavan writes likewise. This is also the reason that property belonging to a non-Jew does not require preparation. A non-Jew does not consciously set anything aside, and since it is prepared for its owner, it is prepared for everyone. Here, however, the owner had already set it aside consciously, while the recipient knew nothing of it, and therefore it is forbidden. Levush appears to rule similarly. Nevertheless, the matter requires study. It should be no worse than ownerless produce that came from outside the techum, which may be eaten even though the recipient had not had it in mind, as stated in Eruvin 45b. Ran writes similarly at the end of Beitzah regarding an animal belonging to a fattener. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 2, writes that where an item was brought from outside the techum there is no issue of muktzeh; see there. Thus, even though a Jew sent it, there is no muktzeh. His reasoning is that only with animals located outside the techum do we say that the owners consciously set them aside, because they did not bring them within the techum before Yom Tov. Here, however, the owners did not consciously set it aside. It resembles one who transferred ownership of portions to others through a third party, as stated in siman 397. Although ownership cannot be transferred through a non-Jew, there is nevertheless no muktzeh, for it is no worse than finding ownerless produce, as explained above. This is also implied by siman 586, seif 22. According to what I wrote in siman 498, that Rabbi Shimon permits it, none of this analysis is necessary. See siman 495, seif 4.
