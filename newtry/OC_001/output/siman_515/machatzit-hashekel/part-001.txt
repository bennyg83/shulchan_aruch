<b>(ס"ק א) אסור כו'</b> משום מוקצה אף על גב דאפי' במוצאי יום טוב אסור בכדי שיעשה אי משום שלא יהנה ממלאכת י"ט לדעת רש"י או לר"ת שלא יאמר לגוי להביאן לו. כ"ש שבי"ט עצמו אסור בלי טעם מוקצה. ודאי לדעת יש"ש בס"ק שאח"ז איכא נ"מ במאי שאסור משום מוקצה דעי"ז אפילו בטלטול אסור. אבל לדעת הרי"ף והרא"ש דס"ל דכל שאסור באכילה גם בטלטול אסור תיקשי ל"ל טעם מוקצה. וכן לדעת רוב הפוסקים אליביה דר"ת דס"ל דלמי שלא הובא בשבילו מותר דל"ל שמא יאמר לגוי כו' דאין אדם חוטא בשביל אחר. א"ש דאיצטריך לטעם מוקצה. אבל דברים אלו שאסור משום מוקצה הם דברי רש"י שם אהא דר"פ לדידיה תיקשי ל"ל לטעם מוקצה. וצ"ל דנ"מ אם תלשן או צדן גוי לצורך עצמו דמותרים לישראל כמ"ש סעיף ב'. מכל מקום ביום א' אסורים משום מוקצה כמבואר שם וכ"כ הר"ן שם בפרק א"צ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(s.k. 1) Forbidden etc.</b> Because of muktzeh; even though even at the departure of Yom Tov it is forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, whether because he should not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov according to Rashi, or according to Rabbeinu Tam lest he tell a non-Jew to bring them for him: all the more so on Yom Tov itself it is forbidden even without a reason of muktzeh. Certainly according to Yam Shel Shlomo in the following s.k. there is a practical difference in that it is forbidden because of muktzeh, for thereby even handling is forbidden. But according to the Rif and the Rosh who hold that whatever is forbidden in eating is also forbidden in handling, the difficulty is: why do we need a reason of muktzeh? And likewise according to most poskim per Rabbeinu Tam who hold that for one for whom it was not brought it is permitted, for why would he say to a non-Jew etc., since a person does not sin for another: then it is fine that the reason of muktzeh is needed. But these words that it is forbidden because of muktzeh are Rashi's words there on that of Rav Papa; for him the difficulty remains why the reason of muktzeh is needed. And one must say that the practical difference is if a non-Jew detached or trapped them for his own need, that they are permitted for a Jew as written in seif 2; nevertheless on day one they are forbidden because of muktzeh as clarified there; and so wrote the Ran there in chapter Ein Tzadin.

**** ENGLISH ****
For such muktzeh even R' Shimon agrees; and if so, according to those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov in siman 495 who ruled on Yom Tov like R' Shimon, nevertheless such muktzeh is forbidden even according to R' Shimon. And the reason Rashi wrote there, folio 24b, and this is his language: and even according to Rashi there is muktzeh in dried figs and raisins (that he pushed them away by hand, as clarified above in siman 310, seif 2), and what is attached is like dried figs and raisins, for since he did not pick them yesterday he set them aside intentionally; and you should not distinguish in what is attached between his own and a non-Jew's; end quote. And Beis Yosef explained his words in siman 310, and this is his language: since through their picking they become permitted, and it is forbidden to benefit from them while attached; and since he did not pick them, it is as if he pushed them away by hand and showed that he does not wish to benefit from them; therefore even if a non-Jew picked them they are forbidden etc.; and for this reason they decreed regarding all that is attached even where it was not in his hand to pick them, for the Sages did not distinguish etc.; and it appears that for this reason there is muktzeh in a matter that lacks trapping even according to R' Shimon; end quote of Beis Yosef.

**** ENGLISH ****

And all the more so one should not forbid them in handling on the second day even according to Rabbeinu Tam who forbids even in eating also on the second day. Nevertheless handling is permitted, for since the muktzeh prohibition of pushing them away by hand departs from it immediately at the beginning of the second night; and from the side of the eating prohibition there is no muktzeh.

And so is implied in Beis Yosef, siman 586, who wrote: a non-Jew who brought a shofar from outside the techum, even the one for whom it was brought is permitted to blow a mitzvah blast on it and fulfills his obligation with it; and so we hold in siman 586. And Hagahos Ashri gave a reason for this because we hold that a thing brought from outside the techum is permitted for one for whom it was not brought, as written here in seif 5; and since it is permitted in benefit for one person, it is permitted in handling for every person, even the one for whom it was brought; and if so even the one for whom it was brought is permitted to blow on it to fulfill his obligation, for this is not benefit, since mitzvos were not given for benefit. It implies that if the law were that a thing coming from outside the techum is forbidden for the whole world, even for one for whom it was not brought, then handling too would be forbidden, for it would be muktzeh according to the view of the Rif and the Rosh.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Similar to tevel.</b> Meaning that we hold that tevel is muktzeh, as stated in Shabbos at the beginning of the chapter "They clear out"; and even though it is permitted to feed tevel to an animal, nevertheless it is muktzeh, for he does not intend to feed it to an animal, since it is fit for himself after he tithes. But clarification is needed regarding which tevel we deal with: if regarding a matter whose work is completed, behold Shabbos is one of the six things that fix for maaser, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 3 of the Laws of Maaser, laws 3 and 4, and so it is in the Gemara at the end of the chapter "One who brings" in Beitza; and if so it is forbidden to feed it to his animal. And if he means a matter whose work is not completed, such that Shabbos does not fix and it is permitted to feed it to his animal: if so, granted that its fitness for an animal does not help and nevertheless it is muktzeh because he does not intend to give it to an animal, still why is it muktzeh? For in such a case even a person eats a casual eating. And one must say that since a person is not allowed to eat a fixed eating, it is muktzeh.
