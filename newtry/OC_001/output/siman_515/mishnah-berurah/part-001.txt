הנה קודם שנכנס בביאור זה הסימן אעתיק לשון הגמרא וביאורו בדרך קצרה. איתא [ביצה כ"ד ע"ב] אמר רב פפא הלכתא נכרי שהביא דורון לישראל ביו"ט אם יש מאותו המין במחובר אסור ולערב נמי אסורין בכדי שיעשו ופירש רש"י דהאי אסורין היינו משום מוקצה מדלא לקטן מאתמול אקצינהו מדעתיה ואפילו לר"ש דלית ליה מוקצה בעלמא מודה בזה עי"ש. ומאי דקאמר ולערב אסורין בכדי שיעשו פירש רש"י כדי שלא יהנה ממלאכת יום טוב ומה דקאמר בכדי שיעשו אסורים ומשמע דלאחר כן מותרים היינו אפילו לערב ראשון נמי אע"ג דליל י"ט שני הוא ממתין רק בכדי שיעשו ומותר אח"כ ממ"נ אם הלילה חול הוא הרי המתין בכדי שיעשו ואם קודש הוא נמצא שנלקטו בחול ולזו השיטה הסכימו רוב ראשונים ויש מן הראשונים שפירשו מדקאמר כדי שיעשו משמע דבעינן זמן הראוי לעשייה דהיינו לערב יו"ט שני בכדי שיעשה דאז הוא זמן חול וראוי לעשייה וטעמם דלפיכך החמירו חכמים לאסור כל היום שני וגם אח"כ בכדי עשייה דאי אמרת דמותרין במוצאי יו"ט ראשון חיישינן שמא יאמר לנכרי ביום טוב ראשון לך ולקוט לי כדי שיוכל לאכול מהן בשני ויש עוד נ"מ בין אלו השיטות דלרש"י וסייעתא שלו דפירשו דהטעם שהחמירו בכדי שיעשה כדי שלא יהנה ממלאכת יו"ט שייך דין זה בין למי שהובא בשבילו ובין לאחרים אבל לדעה האחרונה דהטעם שהחמירו כדי שיעשה הוא כדי שלא יבוא לומר לכתחלה לעכו"ם לך ולקוט לי יש מן הפוסקים דס"ל דזה הטעם אינו שייך רק לענין לאסור בכדי שיעשה למי שהובא בשבילו אבל לא לאחרים דבודאי לא יאמר לעכו"ם לך ולקוט לי כדי להנות לאחרים דאין אדם חוטא ולא לו. וממילא לשיטה זו יש קולא לענין אחרים דאינו אסור לאחרים רק יום מותר חוץ לתחום אסור. והאי אסור הסכימו הפוסקים דהוא רק לאכול ולהנות מזה אבל בטלטול מותר [והנה מדלא הזכיר הגמרא דצריך להמתין אח"כ בכדי שיעשה ש"מ דבתחומין הקילו דא"צ להמתין לערב בכדי שיעשו אבל רוב הראשונים הסכימו דגם בזה צריך להמתין לערב בכדי שיעשו אך בזה אפילו אותן הנוהגין להחמיר לענין מחובר כדעה השניה לענין תחומין נהגינן להקל כדעה ראשונה דדי להמתין כדי שיעשו לערב יו"ט ראשון בלבד] עוד יש קולא לענין תחומין דהבא בשביל ישראל זה מותר לישראל אחר לאכול אותו הדבר אפילו בו ביום [ומ"מ אין לטלטל חוץ לד"א ממקום שהניח הנכרי את הדבר לבד אם הניח בבית או בעיר המתוקנת בעירובין דחשיב כולה כארבע אמות], ועתה נבוא לבאר את הסימן הזה בעזה"י: <br>{א} דורון לישראל - וה"ה בהביא למכור בעיר שרוב ישראל דרים בה דמן הסתם למכור לישראל הביאו וכן בקצץ להעכו"ם מעות להביא לו פירות או דגים מעיו"ט והביאן ביו"ט בכולהו אסור ולא אמרינן בעכו"ם אדעתיה דנפשיה עביד כדי להרויח או להשליך פעולתו כיון דעכ"פ עושה מלאכתו כדי שיהנה הישראל ביו"ט ועיין בה"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
Behold, before we enter the explanation of this siman I will copy the wording of the Gemara and its explanation briefly. It is stated [Beitza 24b]: Rav Papa said the halacha is that a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, if there is of that species among what is attached, is forbidden, and in the evening too they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And Rashi explained that this "forbidden" is because of muktzeh: since he did not pick them yesterday he set them aside intentionally; and even according to R' Shimon who generally does not hold of muktzeh, he agrees in this; see there. And that which it says "and in the evening they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done," Rashi explained: so that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and that which it says they are forbidden for the time it takes for them to be done, implying that afterward they are permitted, means even on the evening of the first as well: even though it is the night of the second Yom Tov, he waits only the time it takes for them to be done and afterward it is permitted, whichever way you turn: if the night is weekday, behold he waited the time it takes for them to be done; and if it is holy, it turns out they were picked on a weekday. And with this approach most Rishonim agreed. And there are among the Rishonim who explained that from its saying "the time it takes for them to be done" it implies we require a time fit for doing, namely the evening of the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done, for then it is weekday time and fit for doing; and their reason is that therefore the Sages were stringent to forbid the entire second day and also afterward the time of doing, for if you say they are permitted at the departure of the first Yom Tov we are concerned lest he tell a non-Jew on the first Yom Tov, "go and pick for me," so that he can eat of them on the second. And there is a further practical difference between these approaches: according to Rashi and those who support him who explained that the reason they were stringent with waiting the time it takes for them to be done is so that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov, this law applies both to the one for whom it was brought and to others; but according to the later view that the reason they were stringent with waiting the time it takes for them to be done is so that he not come to tell a non-Jew ab initio, "go and pick for me," there are among the poskim who hold that this reason applies only to forbid waiting the time it takes for them to be done for the one for whom it was brought, but not for others, for certainly he will not tell a non-Jew "go and pick for me" in order to benefit others, since a person does not sin and not for himself. And automatically according to this approach there is a leniency regarding others: it is forbidden for others only the first day alone because of muktzeh, and afterward it is permitted immediately and one need not wait even the time it takes for them to be done. And there are among the poskim who hold that even according to this approach the prohibition of waiting the time it takes for them to be done includes both for him and for others. Further it is stated there in the Gemara: if there is not of that species among what is attached within the techum it is permitted; outside the techum it is forbidden. And this "forbidden," the poskim agreed, is only to eat and to benefit from this, but in handling it is permitted [and behold from the Gemara's not mentioning that afterward one must wait the time it takes for them to be done, it is implied that regarding techumim they were lenient that one need not wait in the evening the time it takes for them to be done; but most Rishonim agreed that also in this one must wait in the evening the time it takes for them to be done; however in this, even those who practice to be stringent regarding what is attached like the second view, regarding techumim we practice to be lenient like the first view, that it suffices to wait the time it takes for them to be done on the evening of the first Yom Tov alone]. There is a further leniency regarding techumim: what was brought for this Jew is permitted for another Jew to eat that thing even on that very day [nevertheless one may not handle outside four amos from the place where the non-Jew set the thing down, except if he set it in a house or in a city prepared with eruvin, which is considered entirely like four amos]. And now we come to explain this siman with Hashem's help. <br>{1} A gift to a Jew: and the same law applies when he brought to sell in a city where most Jews live, for as a matter of course he brought to sell to Jews; and likewise if he fixed a price with the non-Jew beforehand to bring him fruit or fish from erev Yom Tov and he brought them on Yom Tov: in all of these it is forbidden, and we do not say regarding a non-Jew that he acts with his own intent in order to profit or to cast off his work, since in any case he does his melacha so that the Jew benefit on Yom Tov; and see Biur Halacha.

{9} And there are those who are stringent: who hold that the main prohibition is that if you permit him, perhaps he will tell the non-Jew to act; and therefore even on the second Yom Tov it is forbidden until evening at the hour fit for doing, for if not so there is still room to decree that he will tell a non-Jew to act on the first day in order to prepare for the second Yom Tov [because he knows that if he tells the non-Jew on the second Yom Tov itself it will be forbidden to benefit on that day until evening after waiting the time it takes for them to be done]. **** ENGLISH ****
{12} For the need of guests etc. that was not brought etc.: meaning, the reason is since it was not brought for them; namely, for the need of guests which is a mitzvah we rely on those who are lenient according to this view for others on the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; but aside from this one should not be lenient even for others, for the poskim do not distinguish in this. And know that regarding our reckoning guests as others, some explain that this is specifically when he invited them after they brought the gift, for then it was not brought with their intent; for guests whom he invited beforehand, one can say they are considered like members of his household and it is forbidden. And the later authorities further wrote to be lenient for guests from another city even if he invited them beforehand, and even he himself eats with them because of their honor, for this is included in the mitzvah of hospitality to guests; and we rely in this on the first view that permits on the second Diaspora Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and before waiting the time it takes for them to be done one should not be lenient in any manner even for others.

{60} Brings: and in a city whose majority are Jews, as long as we do not know that he brought for a non-Jew, as a matter of course we assume he brought for Jews who are the majority; and if it is a thing that has of its species among what is attached or that lacks trapping, we say that he picked and trapped for Jews to sell to them, and one must wait until the time it takes for them to be done as above in seif 1. And the later authorities leaned that all this is when the non-Jew is a city resident who went outside the city to trap and pick; but regarding a village non-Jew who brought his merchandise to the city, it appears that it does not apply at all to measure by the city into which he brought whether it is a majority of non-Jews or Jews; rather even if they are Jews we follow the majority of the world, and most are non-Jews, and with their intent he picked and trapped; and even though he brought them to sell to the city we say that afterward he reconsidered and brought them here [and accordingly they do not have the law of trapping for a Jew, and they are forbidden only on the first Yom Tov because of muktzeh, and on the second night they are permitted immediately; and if brought from outside the techum, even though the bringing was certainly for a Jew, in any case it suffices if he waits on the night of the departure of the first Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done]. And in the book Mor UKetziah he questions this, that the final act follows the initial thought, and certainly with intent for this city he picked and trapped; however if the non-Jew was known always to bring his merchandise to this city to sell, it is obvious that according to everyone we say that with intent for this city he picked and trapped.
