**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“One who rises early.”</b> The Kabbalists expanded at length on midnight prayer; Rekanti, Parashas Bereishis, wrote as follows: Some say the time for pleading mercy is the end of the night; I rule that at midnight it is fitting to plead for mercy upon the Community of Israel and her children, etc., and at the end of the night one should ask for one’s personal needs—and this request should be with prostration (nefilas apayim)—see siman 131:2. In our Gemara, the first explanation, which gave a sign for the middle of the “middle”—i.e., midnight—implies that it is a time of Divine favor; the second explanation does not read that way. See Zohar, Vayakhel, p. 242: we always reckon the night as twelve hours, summer and winter, even though for prayer it is not so, as stated in siman 233; likewise Shaarei Tziyun.

<b>עולה</b>. לאחר עולה ושלמים ותודה יאמר פ' נסכים והיא בסדר שלח לך כי אין זבח בלא נסכים אבל חטאת ואשם אין טעון נסכים ופ' צו את אהרן לא שייכא לעול' רק שייכא לקרבן תמיד וכמ"ש ס"ט ויאמר פ' ושמע' קול אלה בסוף ויקרא שהיא באה על שבועת ביטוי ועל שבועת העדות ואשם ודאי ואשם תלוי: נשאלתי למה כ' הטור והרב"י בש"ע לומר עולה כו' חטאת הלא קי"ל פ"י דזבחים דחטאת קודם לעולה וכ"כ רש"י פ' תזריע ע"ש ברא"ם ונ"ל דאמרי' בזבחי' דף ז' למה חטאת קודמת לעול' משל לפרקליט שנכנס למלך ריצה הפרקליט נכנס דורון אחריו ע"ש והתו' כתבו עולה דורון הוא אף על גב דאעשה מכפרת  יותר מחטאת וכו' ע"ש פי' דאיתא בגמ' חטאת מכפרת על חייבי עשה מקופיא ועולה מכפר מקיבעא אעפ"כ כשקרב חטאת ראשונה א"כ כבר כיפר' על חייבי כריתות ועשה א"כ עולה הוי דורון אבל באמירתינו כתב הטור שלא יאמר יה"ר אחר פ' החטאת שמא אינו חייב חטאת והוי כקורא בתורה ועולה מכפרת אעשה דאמרי' בגמרא אין לך אדם שאינו מחויב עשה א"כ יאמר עולה קודם שהיא מכפרת כדאיתא בזבחים שאין לך אדם בישראל שאינו מחויב עשה כנ"ל אבל כשיודע שנתחייב חטאת יאמר חטאת קודם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Olah.”</b> After olah, shelamim, and todah one says Parashas Nesachim—it is in the order Shelach lecha—for there is no sacrifice without libations; but chatas and asham do not require libations. Parashas Tzav es Aharon does not apply to olah but to the tamid offering, as stated in siman 69; and one says Parashas Vayikra, “If a soul shall sin,” at the end of Vayikra, which deals with a vain oath, witness oath, definite asham, and doubtful asham. I was asked: why did the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham in Shulchan Aruch list “olah … chatas,” for we hold (Zevachim ch. 10) that chatas precedes olah, and likewise Rashi, Parashas Tazria, there in Rosh. It appears to me: in Zevachim 7a, why does chatas come before olah? It is likened to an advocate who enters before the king—the advocate enters, the gift follows him—see there; Tosafos wrote that olah is the gift, even though chatas atones more, etc.—see there. The Gemara explains that chatas atones for deliberate violation of a positive command, and olah for failure to perform a positive command; nevertheless, when chatas is offered first, atonement has already been effected for kares-related and positive-command lapses, so olah is then a gift. But in our recitation the Tur wrote that one should not say “Yehi ratzon” after Parashas Chatas, lest one not be liable for a chatas and it be like reading Torah without obligation; and olah atones for a positive command, as the Gemara says: there is no Jew who is not obligated in some positive precept—so one says olah before it “atones,” as in Zevachim: there is no Jew without some positive obligation. But if one knows he is liable for chatas, he says chatas first.

מקום להאריך בזה עיין ברמב"ם פ"א מנזירות: והב"ח כתב בשם רש"ל שאחר חטאת יאמר אם נתחייבתי חטאת יהי רצון שיהא כו' וקשה לי שהרי תלמוד ערוך הוא בשבת דף ע"ב דחטאת בעי ידיעה בתחלה דכתיב או הודע אליו חטאתו ואף רבש"ע אם נתחייבתי אשם יה"ר וכו' דאשם לא בעי ידיעה בתחלה אבל אחר חטאת לא יאמר כלום ובהכי מתיישבה דעת הטור ע"פ ההלכה וכתוב בשל"ה דשבת וי"ט לא יאמר יהי רצון דאין קרבן נדבה בא בהם אבל הפרשיות אין הפסד לאומרן כקורא בתורה ומ"מ אם ת"ח הוא מוטב שיעסוק בפ' דיומא ע"כ ועיין סי' ק"ז ס"א ובע"פ וח"ה ועי"כ לא יאמר פ' תודה עסי' נ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Maharam.”</b> But after Parashas Chatas one should not say “Yehi ratzon” thus, because chatas does not come as a voluntary offering except when one inadvertently violated something whose deliberate violation carries kares (Tur). Rabbeinu Yerucham’s view seems to be that even after asham talui one should not say so, for we rule like the Sages that it does not come voluntarily except when a piece of fat and a piece of lean were before him, he ate one, and does not know which—and see Yoreh Deah siman 5. The Tur wrote that after asham one says “Yehi ratzon,” and Beis Yosef cites Mahar”i and Mahar”i”ch that asham too can come voluntarily, since Nedarim says the pious would vow nezirus to bring a korban—so it would seem it should apply like a nedavah—his words imply not like the Magen Avraham. I was greatly astonished at the geonim: in Nedarim 10a they volunteered nezirus to bring a chatas, and asham is not mentioned there—so “Yehi ratzon” would fit chatas, not asham. Moreover Scripture is explicit that a nazir brings chatas, not asham, unless he became tamei—then it is not called a nedavah and is like all chataos and ashamos for sin. One might answer that nazir’s asham is for deliberate sin (Kerisus ch. 2), so one could vow nezirus, become tamei deliberately, and bring asham—unlike other korbanos that come only for inadvertence—but that fails, for asham of a defamed maidservant, asham of deposit, and chatas of one who must bring sliding-scale offerings also come for deliberate sin; Heaven forbid that the righteous become tamei on purpose and violate a negative command; and in any case chatas is not called “coming voluntarily” when a tahor nazir brings it. I asked the great gaon R. Yitzchok, av beis din of Poznan, 5368; he argued sharply and learnedly that nazir’s chatas is not for atonement, so one cannot say it over another sin—unlike nazir’s asham, which is for atonement, and saying it would imply atonement for another sin—and Nedarim with the commentator’s explanation does not read that way: they feared sin and wished to bring a korban for atonement and therefore vowed nezirus—hence they were atoned by that chatas; no room to expand—see Rambam, Nezirus ch. 1. Bach cites Shlah that after chatas one says “If I became liable for chatas, may it be accepted,” etc.—but I find this difficult, for Shabbos 72b is clear that chatas requires prior knowledge, as it says “or his sin becomes known to him”—and though Rashi end of 71a suggests no prior knowledge from “this is a ram,” etc., the halachah requires knowledge and Rashi needs resolution—so a condition does not help if he does not know he sinned. Therefore my practice: after Parashas Asham say “Master of the Universe, if I became liable for asham, may it be accepted,” etc., for asham does not require prior knowledge; after chatas say nothing. Thus the Tur aligns with halachah. Shelah writes that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one does not say “Yehi ratzon,” for voluntary korban is not brought then; the parshiyos may still be said like Torah reading, and if he is a scholar it is better he study Yoma—see siman 107:1 and Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah; therefore one does not say Parashas Todah—see Magen Avraham there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Offering incense.”</b> For after the tamid they would offer the ketores (Beis Yosef, Orach Chayim). Hagahos Maimoniyos writes to say ketores before tamid, because the Mishnah (Yoma ch. 3) says the morning ketores was done between [sprinkling] the blood and the limbs. Seder Hayom, however, writes to say tamid before ketores, for the main atonement is through blood; so too Rabbeinu Yerocham ch. 4 of Berachos regarding Minchah—that the essence is opposite the blood—hence from the start of the tamid slaughter period (six and a half hours into the day), while Shacharis is from alos hashachar; for Minchah all agree tamid precedes ketores as in Yoma. A printed gloss (הע"ש) that says to recite Minchah before ketores is not precise, for Abaye arranged the pyre, etc.—see there.
