**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 1. In the Shulchan Aruch: 'Also to walk modestly.'</b> At first glance this means that he should act privately, thereby fulfilling the statement, 'and to walk modestly, etc.' See Sukkah 49b and Rashi there. In the Shlah's kuntres, however, it states, 'when he sits in his house and when he lies down, etc.' This suggests somewhat that he interprets it to mean that even while a person is privately at home, his conduct should be with Hashem his God, as stated in siman 2: even in the innermost rooms he should not say, 'Who sees me?' Yet the expression 'to walk modestly' does not imply this.

<b>There: 'Like the sanctity of Israel.'</b> The word 'like' is a scribal error and should read 'that is to say, etc.' The phrase 'the sanctity of Israel' means the quality of Knesset Yisrael, which is their sanctity, with the Divine Presence dwelling among them. See Peri Megadim, whose explanation is not clear. In any event, even if we emend the word 'sanctity' and write 'Knesset' in its place, the word 'like' remains a scribal error; it must read, 'that is to say, Knesset Yisrael.' In my humble opinion, however, the word 'sanctity' also fits well.

<b>(במ"א ס"ק ד') </b>הלילה לי"ב שעות. ר"ל שאחר שעברו י"ב שעות הלילה הוא נחשב ליום בחורף ובקיץ הוא להיפך והיינו לענין חצות דאחר שעברו ששה שעות מתחל' הלילה הוא זמן חצות. ואא"ז בת"ש אין דעתו כן ע"ש ועי' מ"ש בסימן מ"ז וע' בבכור שור ברכות שכתב בשם לקוטי האר"י שזמן חצות בין בקיץ ובין בחורף באמצע חשכת הלילה והוא ז"ל הסכים עמו דלא כשערי ציון. אך בס' שלמי צבור הביא בשם הרב הק' האליף מהו' שלום מזרחי שערבי זצ"ל שכ' כי בג"ע היום והלילה שוים שהזמן הוא אחר שש שעות מי"ב שעות הלילה שהוא אחר ב' שעות מחצות היום כשהחמה בראש כל אדם שאז הוא חצות היום בודאי בכ"מ בג"ע ובכל האקלימים כנודע כי בג"ע היום והלילה שוין בקיץ ובחורף ומשיתחילו לנטות צללי ערב עד תשלום י"ב שעות אז הוא ודאי חצות לילה ממש כי בחורף הלילה י"ב שעות מתחלת היום. ומסופו כמו ב' שלישי תוספות בקירוב מסוף הלילה וכנזכר בזוהר ויקהל כו'. וכן היה הזמן הקרבן תמיד של בין הערבים מכי יצטו צללי ערב בין בחורף ובין בקיץ ואע"פ שבקיץ משנטו צללי ערב עד שקיע' החמה יש יותר מו' שעות אותו הזמן העודף על שש שעות הוא מתחלת הלילה הבאה וכן בחורף להיפוך באופן שאחר שש שעות מי"ב שעות הלילה כנ"ל אז הוא חצות לילה כו' ותיכף ומיד יתגבר כארי לקום משנתו ויזהר מאד שלא יעבור חצות לילה ממש והוא ישן כי אז הוא טעים טעמא דמותא כו' ואפי' ישן אחר חצות אחר שיאמר הברכות והתיקון חצות אין בכך כלום ולא שלט עליו רוח מסאבא ולא טעם טעם דמותא אחר שנתעורר בחצות להמשיך המוחין לז"א כו' ע"ש כי האריך עוד באמרי נועם. וכן כ' הגאון בעל שב יעקב וע' בשערי תשובה בזה בשם תשובות אחרות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4: 'The night into twelve hours.'</b> This means that after the night's twelve hours have passed, the time is counted as day in winter; in summer the reverse applies. This concerns midnight: after six hours from the beginning of the night have passed, it is midnight. My revered grandfather in Tevuot Shor does not hold this view; see there and what I wrote in siman 47. See Bechor Shor on Berachos, who writes in the name of Likkutei HaArizal that both in summer and winter midnight is the midpoint of the darkness of night. He agreed with this, unlike Shaarei Tziyon. Shalmei Tzibbur, however, cites the holy master and scholar R' Shalom Mizrachi Sharabi, of blessed memory: In Gan Eden day and night are equal. The relevant time is after six of the night's twelve hours. These are measured from midday, when the sun is directly overhead, for that is certainly midday everywhere, in Gan Eden and in every climate. From when the evening shadows begin to incline until twelve hours are complete is certainly actual midnight. In winter the night borrows hours from the beginning and end of the day, approximately two thirds of the added time from the beginning of the day and one third from its end, as stated in Zohar, Vayakhel, etc. The afternoon tamid was likewise offered from when the evening shadows began to incline, in both winter and summer. Although in summer more than six hours elapse from that point until sunset, the excess beyond six hours belongs to the beginning of the coming night; in winter the reverse is true. Thus, after six of the aforementioned twelve hours of night it is midnight. One must immediately strengthen himself like a lion and rise from sleep, taking great care that actual midnight not pass while he sleeps, because he then tastes the taste of death, etc. If, however, after awakening at midnight he recites the blessings and Tikkun Chatzot and then sleeps, there is no concern. The impure spirit no longer rules over him and he does not taste death after awakening at midnight to draw the mochin to Zeir Anpin, etc. See there, where he elaborates with pleasant words. The gaon, author of Shav Yaakov, writes likewise. See Shaarei Teshuvah concerning this, citing other responsa.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 7: 'Rabbeinu Bachya writes,' etc.</b> Rabbeinu Bachya did not write this concerning the passage of the Akedah, but concerning the Sages' statement, 'Anyone who engages in the passage of the burnt offering, etc.' He means that the person should intend to understand what the passage signifies. Through this, the eyes of his intellect will be opened and he will understand wonders from the Torah of the offerings. He will consequently exert himself more in Torah and mitzvos, and his sins will be forgiven as though he had brought an offering. It cannot mean merely that he should utter and repeat the wording of the passage. The Sages likewise said, 'Anyone who recites Tehillah LeDavid, etc.' Their intent is that by contemplating what the verses teach and signify, he will recognize and discern the wonders of the One perfect in knowledge. His heart will be strengthened in faith in Hashem and His service, and through this he will inherit life in the World to Come. End of Rabbeinu Bachya's words. It therefore appears that the citation mark should be placed upon the words 'and the passage of the burnt offering.'

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There: 'Unless he became impure, and if so it is not called voluntary.'</b> In my humble opinion this is difficult. Whether a sin offering or a guilt offering is involved, he has not presently vowed to become a nazir and has incurred no offering. We therefore allow the prayer only where one can voluntarily bring that very offering, such as a burnt offering or peace offering; there he can properly say, 'May it be Your will,' because he could volunteer to bring it. This differs from an offering that cannot itself be brought voluntarily except through a vow of naziriteship, when the person knows that he did not vow to become a nazir. What difference is there between not knowing whether he sinned and knowing that he did not vow naziriteship? It therefore appears that the intent is certainly not that his 'May it be Your will' refer to a nazir's guilt offering. Rather, the view is that since some other sin can be atoned through a guilt offering occasioned by his voluntary resolve, he may properly say, 'May it be Your will, etc., as though I had brought a guilt offering.' A nazir's guilt offering, for example, incidentally atones for another sin in his possession. The offering's primary cause nevertheless arose from his voluntary resolve: he vowed to become a nazir and afterward became impure through circumstances beyond his control. Another sin is thereby atoned incidentally. He may therefore say, 'May it be Your will, etc., as though I had brought a guilt offering' occasioned by his voluntary resolve, through which an incidental sin is atoned. This cannot be said of a sin offering. Other sin offerings cannot be caused by his voluntary resolve and desire, but only if Hashem brings a sin to his hand. A nazir's sin offering, since it does not come for atonement, atones for no sin at all. This is the intent of the renowned gaon R' Yitzchak of Pozna, of blessed memory. Regarding Magen Avraham's objection that a sin offering requires prior awareness, my revered grandfather writes in Tevuot Shor that a guilt offering likewise requires prior awareness, and the later authorities discuss this at length. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. Regarding the possibility that he has no obligation at all, it is understandable that he should not say the prayer, since the offering cannot be donated. When he recites it conditionally, however, his lack of awareness is no impediment. This recitation does not require every condition and detail stated regarding a sin offering, because it is not an actual sacrifice. He merely recites the subject of the offering and prays that it be accepted to atone for any sin he committed. What difference does it make that the sin is unknown? The sin would indeed not be atoned through an actual offering; the Torah's wisdom decreed that a sin offering may not be brought without awareness. But this person is not attempting to offer a sacrifice without awareness. He recites the passage and requests mercy: if he sinned but does not know it, so that the burden of sin nevertheless hangs upon his neck, may this recitation be accepted as though the sin had become known to him and he had offered the sacrifice according to its law, in which case it would properly atone. May he now likewise be atoned through his good intention; that had he initially known and been permitted to bring an offering, he would have done so. As stated above in Rabbeinu Bachya's name, this thought is counted as an act: it is as though his sin had become known to him and he had become obligated to bring an offering. This appears correct in my humble opinion.

<b>ס"ק י"ב </b>יש מגיהים ובתפלת המנחה כ"ע מודים דתמיד קודם לקטורת כדאיתא ביומא. אבל פרשת מנחה י"ל אחר קטורת כצ"ל. ובאמת אם יגיה כן אינו מדוקדק מ"ש דלא כע"ש דהא הע"ש באמת כ"כ לחלוק על דברי הש"ע שכ' תמיד ואח"כ קטורת וע"ז כ' בע"ש ולי נראה שאין הסדר כן אלא תמיד ואח"כ מנח' ואח"כ קטורת ע"ש והכי הו"ל להמג"א למימר ובע"ש כתב די"ל מנחה קודם כו' ואין נראה דהא אביי כו' ולישנא דלא כע"ש משמע שנמשך לדבריו הקודמים שמזה מוכח דלא כע"ש וכן הוא ברוב המקומות במג"א ונלע"ד לפרש דהנה באמת צריך ליתן תבלין בדברי הע"ש דלמה מקדים פרשת מנחה לקטורת ומ"ש בע"ש וע' ברמב"ם הלכות תמידין לא מצאתי דבר ובודאי אין חולק שהקטרת הקטרת היו קודם למנחה והוא ש"ס ערוך. ונראה דהע"ש ס"ל ג"כ דמה שאנו אומרים פרשת תמיד הוא נגד אברים ואף שקטורת קודמין לאברים מ"מ אמרינן פ' תמיד תחלה שהוא מין זבח. וא"כ כיון דבלא"ה אין אנו אומרים כסדר הקרבה בבה"מ ממילא דיש לסמוך פ' מנחה לפרשת התמיד שהמנחה ג"כ היא שייך לקרבן משא"כ הקטורת. ואף שהקטורת קודמים למנחה מ"מ הא בלא"ה אין אנו אומרים כסדר דאל"כ היה צריך לאומרו קודם תמיד ג"כ כאשר הוא באמת הגה' מיי"נ. ולפי מ"ש המג"א דאמירת הפרשה הוא נגד דם התמיד ממילא שהאמירה הוא כסדר ההקרבה וא"כ פשיטא שאין לשנות הסדר וי"ל קטורת קודם מנחה ולכן אחר שהכריע המג"א שהוא כנגד דם התמיד כתב דבמנחה כ"ע מודו שתמיד קודם משום דאיתא ביומא דקטורת של בין הערבים בין אברים לנסכים ועכ"פ האמיר' צריך להיות כסדר ודלא כע"ש שכתב דמנחה קודם לקטורת והיינו משום דס"ל דפ' התמיד נגד האברים והאמירה שלא כסדר דז"א אביי הוה מסדר כו' דם התמיד קודם לקטורת וקטורת לאברים ואברים למנחה. וממ"נ אם הפ' נגד אברים יש להקדים הקטורת קודם לפ' התמיד ג"כ אבל מ"מ אין לומר שלא כסדר א"כ באמת הכפרה בדם ושפיר אמירה שלנו כסדר ודו"ק:<br>ומה שצ"ע בזה לפע"ד כיון דהמג"א וע"ש מיירי מן המנחה שהיו מקריבין עם תמיד של שחר איזה פ' מנחה יש כאן דפ' מנחת נסכים שבשלח לך אינה ענין למנחת התמיד כלל ע"ש ובמ"ש אא"ז ת"ש ובמנחת התמיד היא אמירה באותו פ' של קרבן התמיד גופו דקאמר ועשירית האיפה סולת למנחה ועיין בש"ס דיומא שם וברש"י שם ופשיטא שאינו עולה על הדעת שיפסיק בפ' קודם ועשירית האיפה ויאמר פ' קטורת באמצע הא ודאי ליתא. וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if katan 12.</b> Some emend the text to read: 'Regarding the Minchah prayer, everyone agrees that the tamid precedes the incense, as stated in Yoma; but the passage of the meal offering can be recited after the incense.' In truth, if this is the emendation, the words 'unlike Olat Shabbat' are imprecise. Olat Shabbat itself writes this in disagreement with the Shulchan Aruch, which places the tamid and then the incense. Olat Shabbat writes: 'It appears to me that this is not the order; rather, the tamid is followed by the meal offering and then the incense.' Magen Avraham should therefore have said: 'Olat Shabbat writes that the meal offering should precede, etc., but this does not appear correct, for Abaye, etc.' The phrase 'unlike Olat Shabbat' implies that it follows from Magen Avraham's preceding words that Olat Shabbat is wrong, as is Magen Avraham's usual style. It appears to me as follows. Olat Shabbat's words require explanation: why does he place the passage of the meal offering before the incense? His reference to Rambam's Hilchot Temidin supplies nothing, and no one disputes the explicit Talmudic rule that burning the incense preceded the meal offering. It appears that Olat Shabbat also holds that our recitation of the tamid passage corresponds to the limbs. Although the incense preceded the limbs, we nevertheless recite the tamid passage first because it is a type of sacrifice. Since in any case we do not recite the passages in the Temple's sacrificial order, the meal-offering passage may be placed beside the tamid passage, because the meal offering also belongs to the category of offerings, unlike the incense. Although the incense preceded the meal offering, we are already not following the order; otherwise the incense would also have to be recited before the tamid passage, as Hagahot Maimoniyot indeed states. According to Magen Avraham, however, reciting the tamid passage corresponds to the tamid's blood. The recitation consequently follows the sacrificial order, and it is obvious that the order may not be changed: the incense must precede the meal offering. After Magen Avraham concludes that the passage corresponds to the tamid's blood, he writes that at Minchah everyone agrees the tamid comes first, because Yoma states that the afternoon incense came between the limbs and the libations. In any event, the recitation must follow the order, unlike Olat Shabbat, who places the meal offering before the incense because he holds that the tamid passage corresponds to the limbs and that the recitation does not follow the order. This is incorrect, for Abaye arranged it as follows: the tamid's blood preceded the incense, the incense preceded the limbs, and the limbs preceded the meal offering. Whichever way one turns, if the passage corresponds to the limbs, the incense should also precede the tamid passage; one cannot recite them out of order. The atonement is in fact effected by the blood, so our recitation properly follows the order. Understand this carefully. What remains difficult in my humble opinion is that Magen Avraham and Olat Shabbat discuss the meal offering brought with the morning tamid. What separate meal-offering passage is there? The passage of the libation offering in Parashat Shelach has no relevance to the tamid's meal offering; see there and what my revered grandfather writes in Tevuot Shor. The tamid's meal offering is recited within the tamid passage itself: 'and a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a meal offering.' See the Talmud in Yoma there and Rashi there. It is obviously inconceivable that one should interrupt the passage before 'and a tenth of an ephah' and insert the incense passage in the middle. This certainly cannot be correct and requires further study.
