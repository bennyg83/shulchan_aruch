**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is a great principle in the Torah, etc.</b> One who wishes to fulfill “I have set Hashem before me always” properly should be diligent to observe what we shall write in the name of Sefer HaChinuch. Because of their great preciousness, many leading authorities copied these matters into their works. These are the words of Sefer HaChinuch, in its introduction and in mitzvos 25, 26, 417, 418, 432, and 387: Six mitzvos are constantly obligatory. Their obligation never departs from a person for even a single moment throughout his life. At every time and every moment that he reflects upon them, he fulfills a positive commandment, and there is no limit to the reward for these mitzvos. They are as follows.<br>1. To believe that there is one God in the world, Who brought all beings into existence, and that everything that presently exists, that existed, and that will exist forever is through His desire and will. He brought us out of Egypt and gave us the Torah. This is the positive commandment expressed in the verse, “I am Hashem your God, Who brought you out, etc.” Its meaning is: Know and believe that the world has a God Who exercises providence over it, as demonstrated by the fact that He brought you out of the land of Egypt.<br>2. Not to believe in any deity other than Him, as it is stated, “You shall have no other gods before Me.” Even if one acknowledges that the Holy One, blessed is He, rules over everything, but imagines that He entrusted the governance of the world to an angel or star, he thereby acknowledges idolatry and transgresses “You shall have no other gods before Me.” Rather, one must believe that the Holy One, blessed is He, Himself, in His glory, exercises providence over all the worlds, and that no created being has power to do anything contrary to His will. He is therefore called the God of gods.<br>3. To affirm His unity, as it is stated, “Hear, O Israel, Hashem is our God, Hashem is One.” Its meaning is: Hear, O Israel, and know that Hashem, Who brought everything into being through His will, and Who is our God exercising providence over all the worlds, is one Hashem, without any partnership whatsoever.<br>4. To love the Omnipresent, blessed is He, as it is stated, “You shall love Hashem your God, etc.” How does a person attain this love? Through the Torah. Thus it is taught in Sifrei: Since it says, “You shall love,” I do not know how one is to love the Omnipresent. The verse therefore states, “These words that I command you today shall be upon your heart,” for through this you come to recognize the One Who spoke and the world came into being. Meaning, through contemplation of the Torah, one recognizes the greatness of the Holy One, blessed is He, which is beyond measure and without end, and love will necessarily become established in his heart. The mitzvah requires a person to direct all his aspirations and thoughts toward love of Hashem. He should assess in his heart that all that exists in the world, including wealth, children, and honor, is as nothing compared with love of Him, blessed is He. He should constantly exert himself in seeking the wisdom of Torah so that he may attain knowledge of Hashem. One who fixes his thoughts upon physical matters and the vanities of the world, not for the sake of Heaven but merely to indulge himself and attain honor, has neglected this positive commandment, and his punishment is severe.<br>5. To have fear of Hashem constantly before him so that he will not sin. Concerning this it is stated, “You shall fear Hashem your God.” When an opportunity to sin comes before a person, he must awaken his spirit and take to heart at that moment that the Holy One, blessed is He, watches all human deeds, even if their acts are performed in darkness, and that He will exact punishment from them in accordance with the evil of their conduct. Thus it is written, “Can a person hide in concealed places and I not see him? says Hashem, etc.”<br>6. Not to stray after the thoughts of the heart and the sight of the eyes, as it is stated, “You shall not stray after your hearts, etc.” The Sages said: “After your hearts” refers to heresy, and “after your eyes” refers to immorality. Heresy includes all alien thoughts that contradict the Torah’s outlook. Immorality includes one who pursues the desires of the world without directing them toward any proper purpose, meaning that he does not engage in them so that he will remain healthy and be able to apply himself to the service of his Creator, but his constant intent is merely to increase the pleasures of his soul.<br>All this I have gathered briefly from his beautiful language. These words are healing for the eyes, and fortunate is one who fulfills them properly. See Chayei Adam, klal 1, who also discusses them at length.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should not be ashamed, etc.</b> See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Beit Yosef. Know that Beit Yosef speaks only of a mitzvah that a person performs for himself while others mock him. In that case he certainly should pay no attention at all to their ridicule, nor should he quarrel with them. If, however, he is in a place where heretics rise against the Torah and seek to institute regulations concerning the affairs of the city that will cause the people to depart from Hashem’s will, and he first approached them peacefully but they did not heed his words, Beit Yosef said nothing about such a case. It is a mitzvah to hate them, contend with them, and frustrate their counsel to the fullest extent possible. David HaMelech said, “Do I not hate those who hate You, Hashem, and contend with those who rise against You? I hate them with the utmost hatred, etc.”<br>
