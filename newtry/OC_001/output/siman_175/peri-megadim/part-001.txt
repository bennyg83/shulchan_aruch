**** ENGLISH ****
<b>During.</b> See Taz, Beit Yosef, and Terumas HaDeshen, siman 34, regarding this. See Beit Yosef and Bach, who infer from the fact that Rabbi recited a blessing over each barrel that even when there are many varieties of wine one may recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv several times. It appears to me that this applies specifically when all were not on the table simultaneously. If all were present when he recited HaTov VeHaMeitiv, one blessing covers them all. Even Borei Peri HaGafen and HaTov VeHaMeitiv may both be recited when two wines are on the table, as in seif 1, because those are two distinct blessings. That is unlike the present case. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 7. This requires study.

<b>ועל</b> החביב תחלה עט״ז וכ״כ המ״א אות ב׳ עמ״ש אות א׳. וכבר כתב באות א׳ דרמ״א מיישב ס״א וס״ג וב״ח תי׳ דעבר כו׳ אבל הט״ז ז״ל תי׳ דבס״א במסופק וכאן בידוע שהוא גרוע. ותראה מלשונו הצח דדבר חכמה דיבר אדונינו ז״ל כדרכו הטוב והוא דהב״י הביא על דברי הטור אבל המרדכי כתב כו׳ החליט דחלוקין המה ולמה זה בש״ע חזר וכתבם בס״א וס״ג וע״כ חזרה יש כאן וזה דוחק ע״כ כתב דהטור כתב אפי׳ שתיהן לפניו ופי׳ רשב״ם דוקא בידוע שא׳ חשוב יותר והתוס׳ אפי׳ במסופק ולרשב״ם ודאי חולק אמרדכי ומיהו הטור סבירא ליה דקושיית המרדכי אין קושיא דאין למעט בברכות כה״ג כמו ברי״א ס״ג ומיהו יש לומר התם לכתחלה ראוי לברך ברכה השייך לכ״א (ועסי׳ ר״ו ואי״ה שם יבואר) אבל המחבר דפסק כתוס׳ דמסופק נמי מברך א״ש דאין סתירה מס״א שמסופק ל״ש החביב דא״י איזה עדיף ויש לפנינו ריבוי יינות כה״ג י״ל דראוי לתת שבחים הרבה כעין רי״א ס״ג ולא לבחון מי הגרוע כו׳ ובס״ג דברור בא׳ חשוב מברך פה״ג על החשוב שאני ההיא דרי״א ס״ג כמש״ל זה תוכן כוונתו ז״ל וא״כ ספק עדיף בודאי בהיו שתיהן לפניו ולרשב״ם ודאי עדיף כדאמרן. ואם ידוע ששניהן שווין אין מברך הטוב כו׳ כ״א בסתם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And over.</b> The preferred wine first. See Taz, and Magen Avraham, letter 2. See what I wrote in letter 1. I already wrote there that Rama reconciles seif 1 and seif 3, while Bach explains that seif 1 concerns one who transgressed, etc. Taz, however, explains that seif 1 concerns uncertainty and this seif a case in which it is known to be inferior. His lucid wording reflects profound wisdom, as is his way. Beit Yosef, after citing the Tur, writes, “But Mordechai wrote,” etc., treating them as conflicting. Why, then, did he repeat both rulings in seifim 1 and 3 of the Shulchan Aruch? This would imply a retraction, which is difficult. Taz therefore explains that the Tur rules that even if both wines are before him, Rashbam limits the blessing to a case where one is known to be superior, whereas Tosafos apply it even in a case of uncertainty. According to Rashbam there is certainly a dispute with Mordechai. The Tur, however, holds that Mordechai's question is not compelling, since in such a case we do not minimize blessings, similar to siman 211, seif 3. One might distinguish that there it is initially proper to recite the blessing appropriate to each item. See siman 206, where, with Hashem's help, this will be explained. But according to the Mechaber, who follows Tosafos that the blessing is recited even in uncertainty, there is no contradiction. In a case of uncertainty, preference is inapplicable because he does not know which is superior. Since there is an abundance of wines, it is proper to offer multiple praises, analogous to siman 211, seif 3, rather than determine which is inferior. In seif 3, however, where one wine is clearly superior, he recites Borei Peri HaGafen over the superior wine. This differs from siman 211, seif 3, as explained above. This is the substance of Taz's intent. Accordingly, where both wines were before him, uncertainty is certainly preferable. According to Rashbam, known superiority is certainly preferable, as explained. If both are known to be equal, he does not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv; he does so only where their quality is unknown.
