**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 1: They complete, etc.</b> It is implied there that they read only seven aliyos. Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 8:4, writes that specifically in the period of the Mishnah, when only the first oleh recited the opening blessing and only the last oleh recited the concluding blessing, while the intermediate olim recited no blessings, all were considered like one person and therefore they completed the reading. Nowadays, when every oleh recites a blessing before and after, perhaps only the reader who had already begun and blessed before some people left may finish his aliyah and bless afterward, but no additional olim should be called. Nevertheless, Kesef Mishneh concludes that since the requisite number of olim has not yet ascended, the mitzvah is not complete, and once one person began and blessed before some left, all the required olim must ascend. Since he remained uncertain, even the seven required olim should perhaps not ascend; yet because he concludes that all seven may ascend, being considered one unit, and the mitzvah is incomplete until all seven ascend, no more than seven may be called. Although on ordinary Shabbasos one may add aliyos, once some people have left one should not add. Since the halachah is that the maftir may count toward the seven, although the custom is to call seven besides maftir, that extra aliyah should be omitted. Six should be called, and maftir should be the seventh. The phrase “together with maftir” implies that maftir reads the Haftarah and recites its blessings as usual. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees, arguing that the Haftarah is a separate mitzvah and a separate enactment. Once seven have read from the Torah, that mitzvah is complete, and one should not begin the Haftarah blessings when ten are no longer present. The Haftarah should be read without blessings. According to that, Magen Avraham would agree with him.

Tur and Beit Yosef, siman 141, cite Rosh, who initially wished to explain our custom of having the chazzan read because of unlearned people, so as not to embarrass them, like the Mishnah concerning bikkurim. Because of embarrassment, people refrained from bringing bikkurim, so they instituted that everyone be prompted.

<b>כיון דהרמב"ם מתיר כו'</b> ר"ל בתשוב' הובא באבודרהם דף מ"א וז"ל ואין הברכה על הקריאה בס' כמו הברכה על נטילת לולב או ישיבת סוכה שעליה מברך ואם היו פסולים לא עשה מצוה אבל בקריאת התורה מצוה היא הקריאה בין שקרא בס' כשר ובין שקרא בס' פסול כו' וראיה לדבר זה מה שארז"ל אין קורין בחומשים בצבור מפני כבוד הצבור וכי יש פסלנות בעולם כמו החומשים ואפי' היה הס"ת חסר אות אחת פסול וכ"ש חומש וה"ל ליתן טעם מפני שהיא פסול שנמצאת ברכה לבטלה כו' ועל דיוקן סמכו כל אנשי המערב והיו קורין בספרי קלף בלא עיבוד כלל ומברכים לפני' ולאחריה בפני גאוני עולם כו' ואעפ"כ ראוי להם לכל ציבור להיו' להם ספר תורה כשר ככל הלכותיו ובו ראוי לקרות לכתחלה בצבור כו' עכ"ל אלא שהרשב"א חלק עליו וכ' שאח"כ הרמב"ם גופיה בחבורו הגדול חזר בו שכ' פ' י' מהל' ס"ת ך' דברים הן שבכ"א מהן פוסל ס"ת כו' ואין קורין ברבים וא' מהן הוא שחסר בו אפי' אות א' או הוסיף בו אפי' אות א' אולם מהר"י בי רב כ' שזה דוחק לו' שחזר בו בחבורו דא"כ צ"ל שכל גאוני עולם שכ' הרמב"ם שנהגו כן ה"ל טועים אע"כ מ"ש בחבורו הוא לענין קיום מ"ע שחייב כל א' מישראל לכתוב לו ס"ת וכמ"ש בי"ד ריש הלכות ס"ת ובס"ת שחסר בה אפי' אות א' או א' מכ' דברים הנ"ל הפוסלים ס"ת לא קיים המ"ע ומ"ש ואין קורין בו ברבים כתב בס' ב"ה וז"ל כיון שלא קיים המ"ע אין ראוי שיקרא בו כו' עכ"ל והכ"מ כתב מדכתב ואין קורין בו ברבים ולא כתב ואם קראו בו לא יצאו אע"כ דבחיבור מיירי בדאפשר להו ולכן לכתחלה אין קורין בו ובתשובה מיירי בדלא אפשר להו בס"ת כשר ולכן אפי' באפשר להו בדיעבד אם קראו בו יצאו דהוי כלא אפשר ולכן כ' הרמב"ם אין קורין בו היינו לכתחל' כנ"ל לכן דיעבד סמכינן על דעת הרמב"ם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Since Rambam permits, etc.</b> Rambam wrote in a responsum cited by Abudraham: The blessing is not upon reading from the physical scroll in the manner of the blessing over taking a lulav or sitting in a sukkah, where if the object is invalid no mitzvah was performed. The mitzvah of Torah reading is the reading itself, whether from a valid or invalid scroll. A proof is the statement that one may not read publicly from chumashim because of the congregation’s honor. Is there any greater invalidity than a chumash? Even a Sefer Torah missing one letter is invalid, all the more so a chumash. The Gemara should have said that it is invalid and the blessing would be in vain. All the communities of the West relied on this precision and read from parchment scrolls that had not been processed properly, reciting blessings before and after in the presence of leading geonim. Nevertheless, every congregation should possess a fully valid Sefer Torah and initially read from it publicly. Rashba disagreed and wrote that Rambam himself later retracted in Mishneh Torah, where he lists twenty conditions that invalidate a Sefer Torah and states that one may not read from it publicly, including the omission or addition of even one letter. Rabbi Yosef Beirav wrote that it is difficult to say Rambam retracted, for then all the great geonim whom Rambam says followed this practice would have been mistaken. Rather, Mishneh Torah addresses fulfillment of the mitzvah incumbent upon every Jew to write a Sefer Torah. With a scroll containing even one of those defects, one has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Sefer Bedek HaBayis explains that since one has not fulfilled that mitzvah, it is not proper to read from it publicly. Kesef Mishneh notes that Rambam wrote, “One may not read from it publicly,” rather than, “If they read from it, they did not fulfill their obligation.” Thus Mishneh Torah addresses a case where a valid scroll is available, and initially one may not use the defective scroll. The responsum concerns a case where no valid scroll is available. Therefore, even where a valid scroll exists, if they already read from the defective one they have fulfilled their obligation, as though no alternative existed. After the fact we therefore rely on Rambam.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>His words require study, etc.</b> Yoma chapter 7 explicitly states that on Yom Kippur the Kohen Gadol reads from the Sefer Torah the section Acharei Mos and then rolls it to Ach BeAsor in Emor. Since both concern one subject and the sections are close enough that the scroll can be rolled while the translator renders the final verse of Acharei, there is no affront to the congregation through waiting. He then reads by heart the passage UveAsor in Pinchas, because it is too distant to roll there within the translator’s pause. The Gemara asks why another scroll should not be brought, already rolled to UveAsor. Rav Huna son of Yehudah answers that this would cast discredit upon the first scroll, and Reish Lakish answers that it would require an unnecessary blessing. The two answers are complementary. This proves that even after reading from a valid scroll, if another is brought one must bless again. According to Rabbi Yosef Beirav, although we rely on Rambam that the reading from the invalid scroll counted, it should be no better than reading from a valid scroll, and taking out another would require a new blessing.

<b>אלא מפני כ"ה כו'</b> וכדאית' במגיל' דף כ"ג ע"א ופירש"י שלא יהיה כבוד התור' וכבוד נביא שוין עכ"ל ר"ל דבשניהם קוראים ועל שניה' מברכים תחלה וסוף לכן גם המפטיר קורא תחלה בתורה עי"ז ניכר שהקריאה בתורה עיקר לפיכך די במה שקראו בס"ת פסולה משא"כ כשמיציאין שני ספרים וקריאת המפטיר היא מתקנת רבנן סבוראי או גאונים כמ"ש התו' והרא"ש סוף מגלה וא"כ חיוב קריאת המפטיר בתורה מצד עצמו לא משום נביאים לכן יש להחמיר בו אם נמצא טעות כנ"ל. וא"כ פשוט כו' ר"ל כיון דרמ"א מה"ט דקדק וכ' בס"ת שניה כו' להורות במפטיר של כל שבת אין הדין כן משום ספיקו של רשב"ץ א"כ י"ל דוקא רשב"ץ לטעמי' דפוסק כהרשב"א דס"ל אפי' דיעבד אין יוצאים בקריאה בס"ת פסולה אבל לדידן דסמכינן דיעבד על הרמב"ם שיוצאים בקריאה בס"ת פסולה אלא מצד מנהג שנהגו כהר"י ב"ר מוציאים אחרת כנ"ל אבל במפטיר של כל שבת דאפי להרשב"א מספקא לרשב"ץ אפשר שיצאו בקריאת ס"ת פסולה ודאי יש לסמוך על הרמב"ם והעומדים בשיטתו שלא להוציא אחרת וליכא בזה משום שינוי מנהג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Rather, because of the honor of the Torah, etc.</b> Megillah 23a explains that the purpose is that the honor of Torah and the honor of the Prophets should not appear equal. Since both involve reading and blessings before and after, maftir first reads from the Torah, thereby demonstrating that Torah reading is primary. Therefore, once the obligatory reading has been completed, reading maftir from the invalid scroll may suffice. When two scrolls are taken out, however, maftir’s Torah reading is an independent obligation, instituted by the Savoraim or Geonim, as Tosafos and Rosh write at the end of Megillah. One should therefore be stringent if an error is found. It follows simply, etc. Rama’s careful wording, “in the second Sefer Torah,” indicates that the ordinary maftir case differs because of Rashbatz’s uncertainty. Yet Rashbatz follows Rashba, who holds that even after the fact reading from an invalid scroll does not count. We, however, rely after the fact on Rambam, and remove another scroll only because of the practice following Rabbi Yosef Beirav. For ordinary maftir, where even Rashbatz is uncertain whether the invalid reading counts, one may certainly rely on Rambam and those who agree with him not to take out another scroll, without concern for changing established practice.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So as not to enter, etc.</b> If the error is found during an earlier aliyah, another scroll must in any event be taken out for the remaining olim, and the practice follows Rabbi Yosef Beirav to take it out during the aliyah, without concern for the dispute over the blessing. If the error is found during the seventh aliyah and only maftir remains, it is preferable for the seventh reader himself to serve as maftir and not take out another scroll, thereby avoiding the dispute concerning a new blessing. Although this explains only why another scroll should not be taken out during the seventh aliyah, one might still take out another for maftir after the seventh reader’s concluding blessing, as Taz rules. Magen Avraham must therefore hold that this would burden the congregation, in accordance with Rav Huna’s answer. The congregation should not be burdened merely for maftir, which is an addition, when the seventh reader can serve as maftir. Had it been possible to take out another scroll during the seventh aliyah, burdening the congregation would not prevent correcting the seventh aliyah with a valid scroll.
