<b>אחרת. </b> הב"ח כתב דפסק ש"ע אינו נכון והמנהג הוא שאם עדיין לא קרא שלשה פסוקים קורא הטעות על פה עד שיקרא ג"פ ומברך לאחריה ואם הוא פחות מג"פ סמוך לפרשה גומר הפרשה ואם כבר קרא ג"פ ונשאר ג' פסוקים עד סוף הפרשה פוסקים שם ומברך לאחריה ומשלימין המנין בס"ת הכשר וכך נהגו גדולי עולם שלפנינו עכ"ל. וכתב המ"א במקום שאין מנהג יש לנהוג כמ"ש הב"ח דיקרא בס"ת הפסול ג"פ והטעות יקרא בע"פ או להשלים הפרשה אם לא אפשר דהיינו שהוא ב' פסוקים סמוך לפרשה גומר הפרשה עם זה שנמצא הטעות ומברך לאחריה אבל במקום שיש מנהג קשה לשנות מנהגם ועכ"פ יש לנהוג שאם מתחיל רק תיבה א' בפסוק הג' יגמור אותו ויפסיק שם ואם הוא ב' פסוקים סמוך לפרשה יקרא עד הפרשה ע"ש והש"ץ והעט"ז מסכימים לדעת המחבר וכתבו שכן נתפשט המנהג גם הט"ז נדחק ליישב דעת המחבר עיין בי"ד סי' רע"ט. כתב רמ"א בי"ד סי' רע"ט ואין חילוק בזה בין שנמצא הטעות בס"ת ראשונה בין שנמצא הטעות בשניה שקורין בה חובת היום למפטיר עכ"ל וכתב המ"א דהרמ"א דקדק וכתב בס"ת שניה שקורין בה חובת היום למפטיר משמע אבל מפטיר שבכל שבת אין צריך להוציא אחרת אלא יקרא ג' פסוקים בס"ת הפסולה בלא ברכה כיון דאין קריאת המפטיר אלא מפני כבוד התורה ולא עוד אלא אפי' נמצא הטעות בשביעי יגמור קריאתו בס"ת זה ויברך אחריו ויפטיר הוא וט"ז ביו"ד שם כתב דאם נמצא בשביעי יגמור קריאתו ויוציא ס"ת אחרת למפטיר בשעת אמירת הקדיש. ואם נמצא הטעות במפטיר יקרא הטעות בע"פ ע"ש. ונ"ל כמ"ש המ"א. <small>(ועיין בספר אליהו רבה שהשיב על מ"א הרבה דברים. ומעשה היה בסוף פרשת וירא בפסוק מה לך הגר אל תראי חסר יו"ד והיה נ"ל להוציא אחרת דהוי שינוי דבלא יו"ד אינו לשון יראה אלא לשון ראה ועוד איזה מעשיות וע"ש שהאריך בענינים האלו)</small>:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Another.</b> Bach writes that the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch is incorrect. The practice is that if the reader has not yet read three verses, he reads the erroneous word by heart until he has read three verses and then recites the concluding blessing. If fewer than three verses remain before the end of the passage, he completes the passage. If he has already read three verses and three verses remain until the end of the passage, they stop there, he recites the concluding blessing, and they complete the required number of aliyos from a valid Sefer Torah. This was the practice of the great authorities who preceded us. Magen Avraham writes that where there is no established custom, one should follow Bach: the reader should read three verses from the invalid Sefer Torah and read the erroneous word by heart, or complete the passage if interruption is impossible, namely, if he is within two verses of the end of the passage. He then recites the concluding blessing, another Sefer Torah is brought, and they complete the required number of aliyos from it. Where there is an established custom, however, it is difficult to change it. At a minimum, if the reader has begun even one word of the third verse, he should complete that verse and stop there; if he is within two verses of the end of the passage, he should read until the end of the passage. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid agree with the Mechaber and write that this practice has spread. Taz likewise labors to justify the Mechaber. See Yoreh Deah, siman 279. Rama writes there that there is no distinction whether the error is found in the first Sefer Torah or in the second Sefer Torah used for the obligatory reading of the day for maftir. Magen Avraham infers from Rama’s precise phrase, “the second Sefer Torah used for the obligatory reading of the day for maftir,” that for the ordinary maftir of every Shabbos one need not bring another Sefer Torah. Rather, he reads three verses from the invalid Sefer Torah without a blessing, since the maftir reading exists only for the honor of the Torah. Furthermore, even if the error is found during the seventh aliyah, the reader completes his reading from that Sefer Torah, recites the concluding blessing, and he himself reads the haftarah. Taz there in Yoreh Deah writes that if the error is found during the seventh aliyah, the reader completes his reading, and another Sefer Torah is brought for maftir when Kaddish is recited. If the error is found during maftir, he reads the erroneous word by heart. See there. It appears to me that Magen Avraham’s ruling is correct. <small>See Eliyah Rabbah, who responds at length to Magen Avraham. An incident occurred at the end of Parashas Vayeira, in the verse “What is with you, Hagar? Do not fear,” where the yud was missing. It appeared to me that another Sefer Torah should be brought, for without the yud the word is not an expression of fear but of seeing. He also records several other incidents and discusses these matters at length.</small>

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And extra letters.</b> This refers to extra or missing vavs and yuds. If, however, the error changes the reading of the Torah, such as kesev and keves, another Sefer Torah is brought even though both words have the same meaning. Shelah. If rechavah was written instead of rochbo, another Sefer Torah must be brought, for this is a complete error. Maharam of Lublin; Magen Avraham. Ateres Zekeinim writes that this is not a change, because the word may be read with a cholam. See there. If migrasheihen was written instead of migrasheihem, another Sefer Torah need not be brought. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 119. See Minchas Yaakov, siman 18. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 54, rules that another Sefer Torah must be brought, and Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 3, likewise rules that whenever the error is not merely one of deficient or plene spelling, another Sefer Torah must be brought. Rabbi David Oppenheim agreed in Sefer Nishal David, and Sefer Chinuch Beis Yehudah, siman 5, is cited in Shevus Yaakov there. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 82. If one letter is attached to another, another Sefer Torah need not be brought. Bach. Magen Avraham writes that this applies only when the letter was properly formed and became attached at its end. If it is attached in its middle, and certainly if attached at its head, the Sefer Torah is invalid. On a day when two Sifrei Torah are taken out, if an error is found in the first, one should not use the second for that reading; another should immediately be brought. Know that Maharal of Prague instituted that another Sefer Torah should never be brought, even when a word is missing, because Rambam permits reading from an invalid Sefer Torah, as Beit Yosef writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 279, in the name of several poskim. I heard from the renowned Gaon Rabbi Aryeh Leib, av beis din and rosh yeshivah of Amsterdam, that he had a tradition from the Geonim that they instituted not to bring another Sefer Torah for an extra word, but to bring another for a missing word. The reason is that when a word is missing it must be read by heart, and it is forbidden to recite written Torah by heart; with an extra word this concern does not apply. It appears to me that for an extra letter one may rely upon Bach, cited above in seif katan 5, complete the three verses or the passage with the Sefer Torah in which the error was found, recite the concluding blessing, and then bring another Sefer Torah and complete the required aliyos from it. In this manner all poskim may agree. If a drop of wax fell upon letters of a Sefer Torah, it is valid if the letters remain visible. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 99. If this occurred on Shabbos and the letters are not visible, Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 4, cites Likkutei Pardes as holding that the Sefer Torah does not become invalid on this account. He rejects that view and rules that it is invalid, and cites the same ruling from Chinuch Beis Yehudah, siman 5. Responsa Perach Shoshan, principle 2, siman 2, rules likewise. If the wax has dried and can be removed indirectly, namely by bending the parchment so that it falls away, Shevus Yaakov writes that this is permitted and does not involve scraping. The Baer Heitev before me, siman 340, seif katan 4, validates the Sefer Torah in every case without giving any reason or proof. See there.

<b>הדחק. </b> ומהר"ם מלובלין פסק דאם נמצא טעות בס"ת ואין שם אחרת ישלים ז' קרואים בס"ת זו ולא יוסיף עליהם ולמנחה אין להוציאה דאינה חובת היום. ומ"א כתב דאפילו במקום שהוא חובה אין להוציאה לכתחלה אלא א"כ חומש א' שלם וכמ"ש רמ"א כאן. <small>(ובספר אליהו רבה חולק על מ"א ופוסק דאף בשחרית מוציאין אותה ע"ש)</small>:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In pressing circumstances.</b> Maharam of Lublin rules that if an error is found in a Sefer Torah and no other is available, they complete the seven aliyos from that Sefer Torah but do not add aliyos. At Minchah they should not take it out, because that reading is not the obligation of the day. Magen Avraham writes that even where the reading is obligatory, it should not initially be taken out unless one complete Chumash is valid, as Rama writes here. <small>Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with Magen Avraham and rules that it may be taken out even for Shacharis. See there.</small>
