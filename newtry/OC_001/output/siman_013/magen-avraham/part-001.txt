**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Human dignity.”</b> Even though tzitzis is a biblical positive mitzvah, we do not set aside a biblical prohibition for human dignity; still it is permitted because the Torah did not say “do not wear a garment without tzitzis,” rather a positive command to tie tzitzis on it—and since he cannot tie on Shabbos he does not violate a negative command; yet obviously a rabbinic concern remains, therefore it is permitted only for dignity—not like Eliyah Rabbah; but on a weekday he certainly violates continuously the positive “tie tzitzis on it” while wearing it. Proof: would we forbid entering a house without a mezuzah [or parapet] except for an unavoidable one-time situation—end Beis Yosef citing Mordechai. Question: Shabbos 131b says according to the view that tzitzis is the garment’s obligation, if it sits at home without tzitzis he violates a positive command even on Shabbos—so according to the view that tzitzis is the person’s obligation, why is Torah-level wearing on Shabbos without tzitzis permitted? Answer: there follows only R. Eliezer who holds tzitzis defers Shabbos, so the obligation applies even on Shabbos—see there; but we hold tzitzis does not defer Shabbos, so the positive obligation does not take effect on Shabbos—as I wrote. Another question: why forbid wearing on a weekday—Berachos 20 says human dignity pushes off a biblical positive when framed as shev ve’al ta’aseh, and there it implies wearing linen garments without tzitzis is shev ve’al ta’aseh; Yevamos 90 Tosafos similarly. If not like Mordechai that wearing itself is not a violation—as cited in his name—then weekdays too should be permitted for dignity; Mordechai mentioned Shabbos only at the start where Rabbeinu Shimon forbade even Shabbos, but he could mean he permitted even weekdays only that one violates a positive if he does not fix, yet is not obligated to remove for that reason since he wrote afterward “on a weekday,” etc.—end quote from his name implies weekdays too there is no prohibition in mere wearing; Rabbeinu Yerucham’s cited responsum is also about weekdays—Maharam of Rothenburg part 4 responsum 286—and we must say like Pesakim Tosafos Menachos siman 129 and Agur siman 24: specifically Shabbos is permitted, weekdays forbidden. The reason appears to me like Tosafos Shavuos 30 in one version: only great embarrassment permits overriding a biblical positive through shev ve’al ta’aseh, not minor embarrassment—as Choshen Mishpat 28:5: a scholar must go to court to testify about a prohibition even if embarrassing for him; sitting without a tallis is minor embarrassment; still if tzitzis snapped on Yom Tov on a small tallis he need not remove even on a weekday because removal is great embarrassment. Shulchan Aruch mentions Shabbos to teach that even with a Shabbos issue he need not remove; on Shabbos he need not hurry from synagogue—see Yoreh Deah siman 303; on a weekday he should hurry home to remove; one who sees a friend’s tzitzis snapped should not tell him until he gets home—Yoreh Deah 372 implies calling him home to tell him to remove; Shabbos 82: if he knew from yesterday it was invalid he may not wear on Shabbos—he should have fixed yesterday—so the responsum above, siman 252:2, gloss 158:3, siman 218; on a weekday if no tzitzis are available in town the law is like Shabbos. My late brother Maharar Yehudah Segal of Krakow asked: why permit wearing on Shabbos for dignity—he could give it as a gift to another and borrow it back and then be exempt from tzitzis, as siman 323 for tevilas keilim? It appears to me: still rabbinically obligated because it looks like his own—as siman 14:3 borrowed tallis thirty days, all the more here it is really his; if so why not on Shabbos—answer: otherwise on a weekday too he could do so and play tricks to exempt himself from tzitzis; but when he sits without tzitzis he knows we permit only on Shabbos for dignity—as I wrote—for per siman 306 it is forbidden to give a gift on Shabbos except for Shabbos need, so likewise forbidden to give as a gift; Shabbos 131: “since he could declare ownerless” but it does not say “since he could give to a gentile or minor”—see there.
