<b>Meaning, even, etc.</b> Unlike Beis Yosef's strained explanation. Beis Yosef understood 'even without a scroll' to mean without a Torah scroll (that the Torah had not been read) and writes that this applies only where Torah was read, even from an invalid scroll; if no Torah was read at all, the Haftarah is not recited with blessings, as in the responsum attributed to Ramban. He concludes that they should not be protested. Although a Haftarah ordinarily requires twenty-one verses, where the custom is to translate it ten verses suffice, as Tur writes, because translated verses combine due to the congregation's burden. Thus ten Hebrew and ten translated verses total twenty; the final verse is repeated in Hebrew after its translation to conclude with Scripture, totaling twenty-one. At Minchah only three people are called and ten Torah verses suffice, as above siman 137; therefore ten Haftarah verses suffice, just as Rema in se'if 5 requires only fifteen on Yom Tov when five are called. Six Hebrew verses (three from Yonah and three from Michah) together with their translations total twelve and suffice. Greek has the law of Hebrew. Although Rambam says the Greek language was lost, see Beis Yosef's comments in Orach Chaim 690. Moreover, he holds that listeners fulfill the obligation in any other language they understand; see his lengthy discussion.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Paragraph 2: After them, etc.</b> After the word vatzedek; that is, the vatzedek in the blessings after the Haftarah. Although printed texts place a break between vatzedek and the following word ne'eman as if vatzedek ends a blessing, it does not. Seven blessings were instituted for the maftir corresponding to the seven Torah readers: two over his Torah reading, one before the Haftarah, and four afterward. If vatzedek ended a blessing, there would be five afterward. In earlier times they paused after vatzedek to recite praises of God, but the word always remains in the middle of a blessing. This applies only to vatzedek after the Haftarah. One certainly answers amen after vatzedek in the blessing before the Haftarah, because it concludes that blessing.
