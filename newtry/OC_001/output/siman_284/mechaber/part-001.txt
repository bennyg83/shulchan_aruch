**** ENGLISH ****
The Laws Pertaining to the Haftarah and Its Blessings, 7 Seifim: We conclude in the Prophets based upon the subject of the parashah, and we do not decrease [the length of the haftarah] from 21 verses other than if the matter is settled in less than that, for example, “add your burnt offerings to your sacrifices” [in Jeremiah 7:21]. {Rama: Rema: And only on Shabbat on the matter of 21 verses: three verses for each one of the [seven Torah] readings, but on a Festival on which five read [the Torah], fifteen verses suffice (Maharam). And it was decreed that the haftarah is only [read] in public after they read the Torah (Ramban’s Responsa, Siman 199), but nevertheless it is forbidden to read with the blessings before it and after it; but with no blessing it is permitted (Beit Yosef).}

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ב" data-order="2"></i>אם חל ראש חדש בשבת <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ד"></i>אין המפטיר מזכיר של ר"ח כלל <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ג" data-order="3"></i>וי"א שאע"פ שאינו מזכיר בחתימה של ראש חדש מזכירין אותו בתוך הברכה שאומר את יום המנוח הזה ואת יום ראש החודש הזה והמנהג כסברא ראשונה:
**** ENGLISH ****
If Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, the maftir does not mention Rosh Chodesh at all [in the blessing over the Haftarah]. And some say that even though he does not mention Rosh Chodesh in the concluding [formula], he mentions it within the blessing, where he says, "this day of rest and this day of Rosh Chodesh." And the custom is in accordance with the first opinion.

One must concentrate on listening to the blessings recited by those reading from the Torah and the blessings of the maftir, and respond Amen to them. [This will benefit the one who responds, since his responses] enable him to reach the required sum of 100 blessings [to be recited daily]. (On Shabbos, he is lacking the required number of blessings,29 and responding Amen to these blessings can compensate for that lack).

**** ENGLISH ****
If the maftir lost his ability to speak in the middle of the Haftarah, the person who comes to replace him and complete [the reading] should not begin from the place where [the reader] ceased. Instead, he should begin from the place where the first began, as is done with the Torah reading. {Rama: Rema: Two people may not say the haftarah at the same time, because (of the principle) 'two people should not use the same keli'. (Rivash)}

<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ח" data-order="8"></i>בשבת שהפרשיות מחוברות מפטירין בהפטרת פ' <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="יג"></i>שניה: <small>הגה <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="יד"></i>וע"ל סי' תכ"ח ונהגו להזכיר <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="טו"></i>אחר קריאת התורה נשמות המתים ולברך <i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="7"></i>העוסקים בצרכי <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="5"></i>צבור כל מקום לפי מנהגו (שבולי הלקט והגהות מרדכי פ"ק דשבת) ונוהגים לומר יקום פורקן ואין בזה משום איסור תחינה בשבת גם נוהגים לומר אב הרחמים <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="טז"></i>ובכל יום) שאין אומרים בו צדקתך צדק אין אומרים אותו וכן כשיש חתונה או מילה ויש מקומות שאין אומרים אותו <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="יז"></i>כשמברכין <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="6"></i>החדש <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="•" data-order="9"></i>מלבד <i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="8"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="יח"></i>בימי <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="7"></i>הספירה והולכין בכל זה אחר המנהג:</small>
**** ENGLISH ****
On a Shabbos when the parashiyos are joined, we recite the Haftarah of the second parashah. {Rama: And see below, siman 428. And the custom is, after the reading of the Torah, to make mention of the souls of the departed, and to bless those who occupy themselves with the needs of the community — each place according to its custom (Shibbolei HaLeket; Hagahos Mordechai, first chapter of Shabbos). And the custom is to say "Yekum Purkan," and there is no violation of [reciting] supplication on Shabbos in this. Likewise the custom is to say "Av HaRachamim"; and on any day on which "Tzidkascha Tzedek" is not said, it is not said, and likewise when there is a wedding or a circumcision; and there are places where it is not said when the [new] month is blessed, except during the days of the [Omer] counting; and in all of this one follows the custom.}
