כל הט"ו שבחים שבישתבח מן שיר ושבחה וכו' עד מעתה יאמר בנשימ' אחת (של"ה): <b>מעומד</b>. כדי שיסמוך לו הקדיש מיד משמע דכשמתפלל ביחיד א"צ לעמוד וה"ה בשבת שהחזן מנגן בא"י א"צ היחיד לעמוד כשאומר ישתבח (ב"ח) אבל בס"ס נ"א משמע דצריך לעמוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
All the fifteen praises in Yishtabach, from Shir and Shvachah, etc., until from now, should be said in one breath, Shelah. <b>Standing.</b> In order that he join Kaddish to it immediately. But at the end of siman 51 it implies that one must stand.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even in his youth.</b> This means that he should not be appointed initially, but we do not remove him because of this. When a bad reputation comes out about him now, and it is a rumor that has not ceased, even an individual may object in order to remove him, Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 41. See below, seif 25. In any case where they already removed him, and he remained so for days and years with the consent of the community, and they now wish to appoint him, this is presently like an initial acceptance, and even a minority may object, Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 27, Maharshdam, and Mishpetei Tzedek, part 10, siman 77. If those who object are the communal leaders, it is as though the entire community objects, unless it is a place where there are appointed arbiters and there is a ban not to alter their decree. In that case, the objection of the parnasim is ineffective, Mishpetei Tzedek there. It is proper that a shaliach tzibbur have long garments so that his legs not be visible, and that he enter the beis haknesses first and leave last, Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 8.

<b>מותר להיות ש"ץ</b>. דלא מקרי אין פרקו נאה אלא דוקא שהיה מועד לעשות דברים שאינם מהוגנים אבל מי שבאה לידו שגגת מעשה ומתחרט הרי זה צדיק גמור לכל (ד"מ א"ז) ומזה הלשון משמע דאפי' עבר פעם א' במזיד ושב שרי עד שיהא מועד לכך ועבי"ד סי' ב' וצ"ע למה כ' רמ"א דבמזיד לא ור"א ששון סי' קנ"ז כ' בשם רא"ם סי' פ"ז דאפי' המיר ושב יכול להיות ש"צ קבוע וכ"כ בכ"ה בשם מהר"א בתשו' ב"י סי' ג ועיין ראב"ח ח"ב סי' מ"ב והרי"ט ובמ"צ ח"א סי"ח כ' דמי שנעשה תוגר וחזר יכול להיות ש"ץ לכ"ע ורש"ל בתשו' סי' כ' כתב דלא בעינן כל המדות אלא בת"צ ע"ש ועיין בתשו' ר"ש הלוי חי"ד סי' נ"ה ורש"ך ח"ב סי' ק"ז ש"ץ שיצא פעמים רבות טריפה מתחת ידו מסלקין אותו מש"ץ (מ"ץ ח"ב סי' ע"ה) ועיין ברש"ל סי' כ' ונ"ל דאם עבר עבירה באונס לכ"ע מותר וכמ"ש סי' קכ"ח, טוב לחזור אחר ש"ץ בן צדיק כי אינו דומה תפלת צדיק בן צדיק כו' (ב"ח רש"ל) מי שנפלו זרועותיו מותר להיות ש"ץ שדרך הקדוש ברוך הוא להשתמש בכלים שבורים כדאמר  במדרש ויראו שלא יהיה ש"ץ טיפש (רש"ל פ"ק דחולין) אבל בזוהר אמור ע' קע"ג אי' דהא דקב"ה משתמש בכלים שבורים היינו דכא ושפל רוח אבל מי שיש בו מום הוא פגום ע"ש שהאריך בזה ולכן כהן בעל מום פסול לעבודה וא"כ גם בש"ץ יש ליזהר, ועוד הקריבהו נא לפחתך וגו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He is permitted to be shaliach tzibbur.</b> For the phrase, his past is not becoming, applies specifically where he was habitually prone to do improper things. But one who had an accidental misdeed come to his hand and regrets it is a complete tzaddik in every respect, Darkei Moshe in the name of the Or Zarua. From this wording it implies that even if he sinned once intentionally and repented, he is permitted, until he becomes habitually prone to this. See Yoreh Deah, siman 2. It requires study why the Rama wrote that if it was intentional, he may not. Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 157, wrote in the name of Re'em, siman 87, that even one who apostatized and returned may be a fixed shaliach tzibbur. So too the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahara in the responsa of the Beis Yosef, siman 3. See Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 42, the Maharit, and Mishpetei Tzedek, part 1, siman 18, who wrote that one who became a Muslim and returned may be shaliach tzibbur according to all opinions. Maharshal, responsa siman 20, wrote that we require all these qualities only for a communal fast; see there. See also Responsa Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, Yoreh Deah, siman 55, and the Roshach, part 2, siman 107. A shaliach tzibbur from whose hand non-kosher meat was found to have gone out many times is removed from being shaliach tzibbur, Mishpetei Tzedek, part 2, siman 75. See Maharshal, siman 20. It seems to me that if he transgressed under compulsion, he is permitted according to all opinions, as written in siman 128. It is good to seek a shaliach tzibbur who is the son of a tzaddik, for the prayer of a tzaddik who is the son of a tzaddik is not comparable, etc., Bach and Maharshal. One whose arms fell off may be shaliach tzibbur, for it is the way of the Holy One, blessed be He, to make use of broken vessels, as stated in the Midrash. They should see to it that the shaliach tzibbur not be foolish, Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin. But in the Zohar, Emor, page 173, it is stated that the Holy One, blessed be He, makes use of broken vessels means one who is crushed and lowly of spirit, but one who has a physical blemish is considered defective; see there, where he elaborates on this. Therefore, a kohen who has a blemish is invalid for the service. If so, with regard to a shaliach tzibbur also one should be careful. Furthermore, the verse says, offer it now to your governor, etc.

In Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Chullin, siman 51, he wrote that this applies specifically where there is another person to hire for the wages of this one. Then an individual may object. The Levush wrote that nowadays we follow the majority of those who pay taxes, even if relatives who are invalid as witnesses are among them. This is not so now, when all are fluent, and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the piyyutim. Although he says Kaddish and Barchu, there is not such a great concern. Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 41, wrote that even according to the opinion that an individual may object, if he comes with a claim of disqualification, he may not object, for there is concern for damaging the person's reputation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the congregation cannot object.</b> This speaks of a case where they accepted him as shaliach tzibbur for all the days of his life. If so, he certainly stipulated with this in mind, that if at times he would be unable to pray, he could appoint another to assist him. Therefore his son takes precedence. Furthermore, even if the father had not been fit for this, the line of law is that his son takes precedence, for the custom of that place was that sons are appointed in place of fathers. They stated a great principle regarding all appointments, that the son takes precedence, even regarding a Kohen Gadol, and even if there are others greater than he, end quote from the Rashba, siman 300. So too it is in the Sifrei, that for all appointments, their sons take precedence. So too wrote the Knesses HaGedolah. Maharshdam in Yoreh Deah, siman 81, wrote that with a scholar appointed to teach Torah publicly or to judge, we do not say this, end quote. In the book Yefeh Mareh, from Chelek Daled, part 2, chapter 19, he wrote that all appointments that are the crown of Torah, their sons do not take precedence, and only from Hillel the Nasi onward did they practice that their sons take precedence. See chapter 13 of Kesubos.
