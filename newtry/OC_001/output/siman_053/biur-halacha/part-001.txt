<b> אמר ב"ש</b> -  ובדיעבד מי שדילג ברכת ב"ש ונזכר אחר שכבר אמר פסוקי דזמרה ב"ש כי היא ניתקנה קודם פסוקי דזמרה דוקא וכבר יצא ידי חובתו באמירתן מ"מ יאמר ישתבח דאטו מי שלא שלא בירך קודם האכילה לא יברך אח"כ [מפמ"ג] ופשוט דה"ה בברכות שלפניה ושלאחריה דהלל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He recited Baruch She'amar.</b> After the fact, if one omitted the blessing of Baruch She'amar and remembered only after he had already recited Pesukei DeZimrah, he should not then recite Baruch She'amar, because it was instituted specifically before Pesukei DeZimrah, and he has already fulfilled his obligation by reciting them. Nevertheless, he should recite Yishtabach. Is it conceivable that one who did not recite a blessing before eating should therefore not recite the blessing afterward? (Peri Megadim). It is obvious that the same applies to the blessings before and after Hallel.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should not recite the blessing.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote concerning between the sections. It is from the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger in the name of Levush. Although he concludes that the practical ruling requires study, Chayei Adam likewise writes that one should put on the tallis without a blessing until after Yishtabach, when he should touch it and recite the blessing. See Birkei Yosef and Magen Gibborim, who concurred with this as the practical ruling, because they found an explicit responsum of Rambam on this matter. According to this, it is certainly permitted at least to recite Asher Yatzar between the sections. See Chayei Adam, who remains uncertain about this. Therefore, one who needed to relieve himself in the middle of a section of Pesukei DeZimrah, and went and attended to his needs, should wash his hands, finish that section of Pesukei DeZimrah, recite Asher Yatzar, put on his tallis without reciting a blessing, as explained in siman 8, seif 14 and Mishnah Berurah there, and put on tefillin with their blessings. Since one may not enter a privy while wearing tefillin, the original blessing was displaced, as explained above in siman 25, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 47.

<b> דמ"מ יצא עליו שם רע</b> -  עיין במ"ב במה שכתבתי בשם המגן אברהם וא"ר בזה וסתמתי כדבריהם אף דיש עוד הרבה פוסקים דסוברין דדין שלא יצא עליו ש"ר הוא אפילו בכל השנה כדמשמע בכנה"ג וביותר מזה בביאור הגר"א מ"מ סתמתי כמותם משום דדין זה דהרמ"א דסובר דבפ"א שעובר אדם עבירה במזיד אף ששב מקרי שיצא עליו ש"ר ג"כ אינו מבורר דכבר הקשה עליו המגן אברהם וכן משמע שסובר הגר"א בביאורו עיי"ש וכן משמע מתשובת א"ז גופא להמעיין בו ודברי הרמ"א הוא דעת עצמו [ולפלא שהדפיסו בפנים בשם תשובת א"ז מה שכתוב שם בהיפך לענין מזיד עיי"ש] או אולי מצא באיזה פוסק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, an adverse reputation has attached to him.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote in the name of Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah. I ruled without qualification in accordance with them, even though many other poskim hold that the requirement that no adverse reputation have attached to him applies even throughout the year, as is implied by Knesses HaGedolah and, even more clearly, by Biur HaGra. I nevertheless ruled in accordance with Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah because Rama's ruling, that a person who intentionally committed a transgression even once is deemed to have acquired an adverse reputation despite having repented, is itself unclear. Magen Avraham already questioned it, and Biur HaGra likewise appears to disagree in his commentary; see there. The responsum of Or Zarua itself also appears to disagree, upon examination. Rama's words are therefore his own view. It is astonishing that the printers inserted into the text, in the name of Responsa Or Zarua, the opposite of what is written there concerning an intentional transgression; see there. Alternatively, Rama may have found this view in some other posek.

<b> אבל באקראי</b> -  עיין בר"ן [הובא בב"י] שכתב דדוקא בשאין שם אלא הוא וכן איתא בחידושי הרמב"ן ושארי הראשונים לא הזכירו דבר מזה ולכאורה יש להביא ראיה דהשו"ע אין סובר זה דא"כ הלא באופן זה מותר לו ג"כ להיות שם ש"ץ בקבע כדאיתא בסעיף ז' דמותר לו להיות ש"ץ והיינו אפילו בקבע כמו שכתב הלבוש שם וכן משמע במאמר מרדכי דבאקראי מותר אפילו ביש אחר אבל יש לדחות הראיה דבס"ז מיירי כשאנו יודעין לפי ענין המקום שבכל השנה לא יזדמן להם ש"ץ קבוע שנתמלא זקנו לכן מותר למנות לבן י"ג אפילו לקבע משא"כ בסתמא אין למנותו לקבע כי פן יזדמן להם מחר ש"ץ שנתמלא זקנו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But on an occasional basis.</b> See Ran, cited in Beit Yosef, who writes that this applies specifically when no one else is present. The same appears in the novellae of Ramban, while the other Rishonim make no mention of this limitation. At first glance, one may bring proof that Shulchan Aruch does not accept it. Were it so, under those circumstances he could also serve there as a permanent chazzan, as stated in seif 7, which permits him to serve as chazzan, even permanently, as Levush writes there. Maamar Mordechai likewise implies that occasional service is permitted even when another person is present. This proof may be rejected, however, because seif 7 addresses a place where, given the local circumstances, we know that throughout the year they will not find a permanent chazzan whose beard has fully grown. It is therefore permitted to appoint a thirteen-year-old even permanently. Ordinarily, however, one should not appoint him permanently, lest the following day they find a chazzan whose beard has fully grown.

<b> יוכל לירד וכו'</b> -  כתב בפמ"ג סימן קל"ב דאפילו אם הוא אבל לא ירד לפני התיבה בקבע אם לא נתמלא זקנו וכו' עיי"ש ונ"ל דלענין תפלת ערבית יש להקל אפילו בקבע דהא אין מחזירין התפלה רק שאומר קדיש וברכו וידוע דעת הרמב"ם שמחלק בין פריסת שמע לירידה לפני התיבה כמו שכתב הלח"מ דלדעת הרמב"ם אין לחלק בין קבע לארעי רק כמו שכתבנו וכן כתב הפר"ח והפמ"ג וישועת יעקב לדעת הרמב"ם ואח"כ מצאתי שכן ג"כ דעת בה"ג וגם מסתמא מוחלין הצבור להאבל ויש לצרף בזה ג"כ דעת הב"י והש"ג שמקילין בזה כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He may descend, etc.</b> Peri Megadim writes in siman 132 that even when he is a mourner, he should not regularly descend before the lectern if his beard has not fully grown, etc.; see there. It appears to me that regarding Maariv one may be lenient even on a regular basis, since the repetition of the Amidah is not recited, and he merely says Kaddish and Barechu. Rambam's view is well known, distinguishing between leading the responsive recitation of Shema and descending before the lectern. As Lechem Mishneh explains, according to Rambam the distinction is not between permanent and occasional service, but rather as we have written. Peri Chadash, Peri Megadim, and Yeshuos Yaakov also explain Rambam this way. I subsequently found that this is also the view of Halachos Gedolos. Moreover, the congregation presumably waives its honor for the mourner. One may also combine here the views of Beit Yosef and Shiltei Gibborim, who are lenient in this matter, as explained above.

<b> ולא ימתין</b> -  ובביאור הגר"א סותר לזה מערכין י"א ע"ב דש"ץ מימלך לתפלה עיי"ש. ומהטור ג"כ אינו מוכח רק דאינו צריך לסרב אבל לא דאין צריך להמתין כלל. ומ"מ בדוחק יש ליישב דעת השו"ע דמיירי הגמרא בש"ץ שאינו קבוע לענין התפלה רק דהוא ממונה לשארי דברים ולפי מה שפי' רש"י בחולין כ"ד ע"ב דיש ש"ץ שהוא ממונה לתקיעת שופר ולנדות ושארי דברים עיי"ש ואע"ג דבודאי מתרצים הצבור שיהיה הוא שלוחם ג"כ לתפלה מ"מ עביד דמימלך משא"כ בש"ץ שהתמנותו היה העיקר שהוא יהיה הש"ץ לתפלה א"צ למימלך כלל ובלוים שם כיון שזה היום הא' שנתמנו לעבודתם כדמוכח בקרא שם אפשר דגרע מש"ץ קבוע שעומד תמיד לכך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should not wait.</b> Biur HaGra contradicts this from Arachin 11b, which states that a chazzan seeks authorization before beginning the prayer; see there. Nor is it proven from Tur that he need not wait at all, only that he need not initially refuse. Nevertheless, with difficulty one can explain Shulchan Aruch's view by saying that the Gemara refers to a chazzan who was not permanently appointed for prayer, but was appointed for other communal functions. Rashi in Chullin 24b explains that there can be a chazzan appointed to sound the shofar, pronounce bans, and perform other functions; see there. Although the congregation certainly agrees that he should also serve as its agent for prayer, he nevertheless seeks authorization. By contrast, a chazzan whose primary appointment was to lead the prayers need not seek authorization at all. Regarding the leviim there, since this was their first day of appointment to their service, as is evident from the verse, perhaps their situation was inferior to that of a permanent chazzan who always stands ready for this purpose.

<b> שליח צבור</b> -  עיין במשנה ברורה והוא נובע מתשובת הרא"ש ומזה יראה האדם גודל החיוב על כל עיר לשכור להם רב שיורה להם במצות התורה ומשפטיה איך להתנהג ולא יהיו כעורים המגששים באפילה דאפילו אם עי"ז נדחית לגמרי מצות תפלה אצלם ג"כ החיוב הזה הוא קודם ובפרט אם אנשי העיר יכולין ג"כ להתפלל לעצמן בודאי זה קודם ומזה ימלאו רתת ופחד איזה מהעיירות הנמצאים מחוץ למדינה שאין להם רב ומ"ץ בעירם ועון גדול הוא כמו שביארנו הגם שלוקחין להם שו"ב עכ"ז הלא צריכין לאיש מו"צ שיורה להם דיני שבת ויו"ט והלכות פסח החמורה ודיני טבילת מקוה וכל פרטי הלכות נדה ושאר דיני תורתנו הקדושה אשר יעשה אותם האדם וחי בהם [אם לא שהשו"ב שלהם הוא למדן גדול ובקי בחדרי התורה ויש לו סמיכה על הוראות מהגדולים המפורסמים דמדינה] וכ"ש שיש ששוכרין להם ש"ץ מפורסם למנגן גדול בעד כמה מאות רו"כ לשנה שינגן להם בקול נעים ובשיעור כזה היו יכולין ליקח להם רב ומ"ץ וגם ש"ץ שיהיה יוכל לעבור לפני התיבה בשבת ויו"ט ור"ה ויוהכ"פ כאשר במדינתינו ובודאי היה מתקבל עבור זה תפלתם למרום משא"כ עתה שהתמנות הש"ץ הוא שלא לש"ש ע"ז נאמר נתנה עלי בקולה ע"כ שנאתיה וסוף דבר הוא שעי"ז הולכת העיר מדחי לדחי שאין להם מורה ומנהיג בדרכי ד' ובאים עי"ז לידי חילול שבת ויו"ט ואכילת חשש חמץ ח"ו ומי יודע עוד כמה איסורים מחייבי כריתות ח"ו השם ישמרנו מאנשים כאלו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Chazzan.</b> See Mishnah Berurah. This ruling is derived from a responsum of the Rosh, and from it a person should recognize the magnitude of the obligation upon every city to hire a rabbi who will instruct them in the mitzvos and laws of the Torah and teach them how to conduct themselves, so that they not be like blind people groping in darkness. Even if, as a result, the mitzvah of communal prayer would be entirely displaced for them, this obligation nevertheless takes precedence, especially where the residents can also pray on their own, in which case it certainly takes precedence. This should fill with trembling and fear those towns outside our country that have no rabbi and halachic decisor in their city. This is a grave sin, as we have explained. Although they hire a shochet and bodek, they still require a halachic decisor to teach them the laws of Shabbos and Yom Tov, the stringent laws of Pesach, the laws of immersion in a mikveh, all the details of the laws of niddah, and the other laws of our holy Torah, which a person shall perform and live by them. This is unless their shochet and bodek is a great Torah scholar, expert in the chambers of Torah, and has ordination to render halachic rulings from the renowned Torah authorities of the country. All the more so is this true of places that hire a celebrated chazzan, a great singer, for several hundred rubles a year so that he may sing for them in a pleasant voice, when for that sum they could hire both a rabbi and halachic decisor and a chazzan capable of leading the prayers on Shabbos, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur, as is done in our country. Their prayers would certainly be accepted on high on account of this. By contrast, when the appointment of the chazzan is not for the sake of Heaven, of this it is said, “She raised her voice against Me; therefore I hated her.” Ultimately, because of this the city deteriorates from one failing to another, for they have no teacher or guide in the ways of Hashem, and thereby come to desecrate Shabbos and Yom Tov and to eat food subject to a concern of chametz, Heaven forbid. Who knows how many other prohibitions punishable by kares they may transgress, Heaven forbid. May Hashem protect us from such people.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If witnesses came against him.</b> See Mishnah Berurah regarding an unceasing rumor. See also Biur HaGra, who cites Gittin 89a and Kesubos 36b. This refers to the Gemara's statement there that if a rumor circulated in the city that a woman had committed adultery, we do not take it into account. At first glance, this implies that even an unceasing rumor is disregarded, for otherwise the Gemara's question there would arise; see there. In Kol Bo, from which this ruling is derived, the text says “valid witnesses,” which apparently implies that an unceasing rumor alone is certainly insufficient. In Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 11, he cites these words of Kol Bo and concludes that even where there is an unceasing rumor, if the entire congregation is satisfied to retain him as chazzan, one need not protest against them. If, however, one member objects and says, “I believe this unceasing rumor,” he may object and have him removed. He further writes that at times one removes him because of an unceasing rumor even when no one objects, such as where the rumor is that the indecent act was committed before witnesses who subsequently traveled overseas. Although the witnesses are not before us, since there is a rumor that witnesses exist elsewhere, and the rumor initially emerged through valid witnesses, he is removed from his position. But where the accusation did not originate from witnesses, and the rumor initially emerged through a slave, maidservant, or women, he is not removed unless some individuals object to him, as stated above. An unceasing rumor is defined as one concerning which the entire city spoke for a day and a half, where he has no enemies in the city of whom we can say that they spread the rumor, as stated in Yevamos 25a. Responsa Maharik, siman 188, implies that it is called an unceasing rumor only where the public agrees upon it and it does not cease, for we may presume that such a rumor did not arise without cause. But if we know that only one person initially spread the rumor and it then circulated throughout the city, it is not termed an unceasing rumor. Possibly the same applies here. I later found that Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 25, implies this; see there. Yet there is seemingly a major difficulty: how can any unceasing rumor suffice to remove a chazzan, when he thereby also suffers a monetary loss, as is evident from Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin, at the end of the first chapter; see there. With regard to an ordinary monetary loss, it is obvious and clear that no rumor is effective, as is implied by the unqualified language of the Gemara in Gittin 89a at the beginning of the page, and likewise by the statements there concerning a rumor that one consecrated or rendered ownerless his property; see there. Even a compelling presumption is ineffective to extract money, as implied in Choshen Mishpat, siman 408, and all the more so a mere rumor. Perhaps the reason is that Gittin 89a states that we disregard a rumor that a woman committed adultery because it is possible that people merely saw her acting immodestly, and this gave rise to the rumor. Regarding a chazzan, however, people presumably would not initially wish to appoint a person who had acted with such immodesty that an unceasing rumor of adultery arose concerning him. He was obligated to guard himself against coming to such a situation. Where several signs of immorality are observed in him, it is obvious that he must be warned, as Rama writes regarding a chazzan who uses obscene language.
