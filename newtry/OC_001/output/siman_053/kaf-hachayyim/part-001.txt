<b>א) [סעיף א'] אומר ש"ץ ישתבח מעומד.  </b>כדי שיסמוך לו הקדיש מיד. ב"י. ב"ח. ט"ז סק"א. מ"א סק"א. אבל היחיד א"צ לעמוד באמירת ישתבח כמש"ל סי' נ"א אות מ"ב. ומ"ש המ"א דבסוף סי' נ"א משמע דצריך לעמוד כתב הא"ר אות א' צ"ל דהתם ממנהגא והכא מדינא יעו"ש. והביאו פרמ"ג במש"ז אות א' וכבר כתבנו לעיל דהמנהג פשוט בינינו שאין היחיד עומד באמירת ישתבח יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] The shaliach tzibbur recites Yishtabach while standing.</b> This is so that he may immediately follow it with Kaddish. Beis Yosef; Bach; Taz 1; and Magen Avraham 1. An individual, however, need not stand while reciting Yishtabach, as stated above in siman 51:42. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that the end of siman 51 implies one must stand, Eliyah Rabbah 1 writes that the ruling there must concern custom, while the ruling here concerns the strict law; see there. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 1, cites him. We already wrote above that the universal custom among us is that an individual does not stand while reciting Yishtabach; see there.

<b>ב) כתב  </b>מ"א בריש הסי' בשם השל"ה דכל הט"ו שבחים שבישתבח מן שיר ושבחה וכו' עד מעתה יאמר בנשימא אחת. אמנם בזוהר פ' תרומה דקל"ב ע"א לא משמע כן אלא דהאיסור הוא אם מפסיק בדיבור ואינם אלא י"ג שבחים מן שיר ושבחה וכו' עד ומלכות כאשר יעו"ש. וכ"כ הש"ץ דף ע' ע"ד. וכן האר"י ז"ל לא זכר זה בשער הכוו' שיהיה בנשימה אחת כאשר הבאנו לשונו לעיל סי' מ"ח יעו"ש. וכ"כ החיד"א בקש"ג סי' ז' אות מ"ז בשם סידור הרב יעב"ץ דלא יאמרם בנשימה אחת כי צ"ל בנעימה כמסדר שבחו של מלך עכ"ל. וכ"כ סידור בי"ע אות י"ג. שע"ת אות א' וכ"כ ח"א כלל י"ח אות ד' בשם הגר"א וזוה"ק. ונוסח ישתבח ומי תיקנו כבר כתבנו לעיל בסי' מ"ח בשם שער הכוו' יעו"ש וכ"כ ש"ץ דף ע' ע"ד ודף ע"א ע"א. סידור בי"ע אות י"ד. ולענין עניית אמן וכל דבר שבקדושה דינו כמו ב"ש עיין לעיל סי' נ"א אות י"א וסי' ה' אות ד' רק בי"ג שבחים יזהר שלא להביא עצמו לידי כך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) Magen Avraham writes</b> at the beginning of the siman, in Shelah's name, that all fifteen praises in Yishtabach (from 'song and praise,' etc., through 'from now') should be said in one breath. Zohar, Terumah 132a, however, does not imply this. Rather, the prohibition is to interrupt with speech, and there are only thirteen praises, from 'song and praise' through 'and kingship'; see there. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 70d, writes likewise. The Arizal also does not mention in Shaar HaKavanos that they should be recited in one breath, as we quoted him above in siman 48; see there. Chida, Kesher Gudal 7:47, writes in the name of Yaavetz's siddur that they should not be recited in one breath, for they must be said melodiously, like one setting forth the praise of a king. Siddur Beis Yaakov 13; Shaarei Teshuvah 1; and Chayei Adam 18:4 in the names of Gra and Zohar rule likewise. We already discussed the Yishtabach text and its author above in siman 48 in the name of Shaar HaKavanos; see there. See also Shalmei Tzibbur, pages 70d and 71a, and Siddur Beis Yaakov 14. Regarding answering Amen and any sacred response, its law is like Baruch She'amar; see above, siman 51:11 and siman 5:4. During the thirteen praises, however, one should take care not to place himself in such a situation.

<b>4) [Se'if 2] Yishtabach should not be recited unless one said Baruch She'amar and part of Pesukei DeZimrah.</b> Yishtabach is the concluding berachah over Pesukei DeZimrah, as stated at the beginning of siman 51. Magen Avraham 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 2. Many err while traveling, when they have no time to pray at length, and begin with Yishtabach. One who does so first recites a berachah in vain. Eliyah Rabbah 2 in the name of Netz HaChaim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid. Likewise, some people delay coming to the synagogue on Shabbos until the congregation reaches Nishmas Kol Chai and begin Nishmas with them. They should not say Yishtabach because of concern for a berachah in vain; rather, they should recite Nishmas Kol Chai without Yishtabach. This is obvious.

<b>7) There: Rather, between Yishtabach and Yotzer.</b> The same applies between one psalm and another. Mahari Binyamin, cited by Mahari Zayin in Responsa Gevuras Anashim, Orach Chaim 1:51; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Birkei Yosef 4. Mahara HaLevi, siman 52, writes that this applies specifically when one was compelled by bodily needs. If he was not so compelled but tzitzis and tefillin became available to him, it is proper not to put them on or recite their berachos within Pesukei DeZimrah, but to wait until between Yishtabach and Yotzer; see there. Birkei Yosef cites him and comments: 'The Rav went too far in this ruling. See Rambam's responsa in the newly printed Pe'er HaDor 147, which rules the opposite: A person who obtains a tallis and tefillin while in Pesukei DeZimrah; may he put them on and recite the berachos? Answer: There is no prohibition whatsoever against reciting the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin during the psalms. Such an interruption is not forbidden in Pesukei DeZimrah, which is neither the Amidah nor Krias Shema, where such an interruption would be forbidden.' Thus, three opinions exist. Rambam permits interrupting for the berachos over tallis and tefillin during Pesukei DeZimrah, apparently even in the middle of a psalm. Mahari Binyamin permits it only between psalms. Mahara HaLevi permits it only if one was compelled by bodily needs; if tzitzis and tefillin merely became available, it is proper not to put them on or recite the berachos until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Maran's unqualified wording also implies no distinction: the berachos should be recited only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Kisei Eliyahu 3; Siddur Beis Yaakov 9; and Yafeh LaLev 3, unlike Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 4. The rationale given by Magen Avraham and the later authorities cited above in paragraph 5 likewise implies no difference between the middle of a psalm and between psalms. The distinction is only between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach on the one hand and between Yishtabach and Yotzer on the other; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3 and Erech HaShulchan 1 rule likewise. It is astonishing that Birkei Yosef says Gevuras Anashim went too far. See Zechor LeYitzchak Harari 8, who also questions Birkei Yosef. This does not resemble Maran's ruling in siman 66:2 that between sections one may interrupt to put on tzitzis and tefillin. There, they are required for Krias Shema so that one not resemble a person bearing false testimony against himself, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Shibolei HaLeket. They are not needed for Pesukei DeZimrah. Moreover, here he can put them on between Yishtabach and Yotzer, which is more lenient; there is no other place available in Krias Shema. Even according to the Arizal's words cited above, that they must be put on before the passage of the Tamid, once that time has already passed and the mitzvah was not performed in its ideal order, one should not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah. One who lacked tzitzis and tefillin and obtained them after Baruch She'amar should therefore act stringently and put them on with their berachos only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Gevuras Anashim also writes that one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah should wash and recite Asher Yatzar. The proper practice, however, is not to interrupt for that berachah until after Yishtabach, as stated above in siman 51:28. Regarding Gevuras Anashim's underlying ruling that one who attended to bodily needs and entered the lavatory must repeat the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin, we wrote above in siman 8:52 and siman 25:78 that the matter is disputed and doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently; see there. Accordingly, one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah, removed his tzitzis and tefillin, and entered the lavatory need not repeat their berachos after leaving and washing his hands. He should put them on without a berachah, because doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently.

<b>13) A group</b> reciting Tehillim in the synagogue that did not have a minyan before Baruch She'amar may not recite Kaddish during Pesukei DeZimrah. Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, Orach Chaim 5; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67c; Kesher Gudal 7:36; and Siddur Beis Yaakov 12. Similarly, if no minyan was present before Hodu and the Kaddish preceding Hodu was not recited, but Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimrah have begun, Kaddish should no longer be recited during Pesukei DeZimrah. This is implied by Shaar HaKavanos, Derush HaKaddish, which says that Kaddish was instituted between each world and the next; see there. Since its time has passed, one need not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah to recite it. Pnei Yehoshua, ibid., writes likewise. Siddur Beis Yaakov 13 agrees, unlike Mahari Azulai; Shaarei Teshuvah 51:3. Likewise, in a place where orphans customarily recite the Kaddish after Yishtabach, if one of them has not yet recited Yishtabach and is still in Pesukei DeZimrah, he should not interrupt to recite Kaddish. The principal Kaddish that benefits the deceased is specifically the final Kaddish before Aleinu, as Shaar HaKavanos states there. He should therefore not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah for this Kaddish. Moreover, Kaddish was instituted only between one world and the next. He has not yet completed the aspect of that world in order to elevate it, as is known. Rav Pe'alim 2:14 writes that since he recites it for the congregation, doing so is permitted. In my humble opinion, this is incorrect. Since he has not yet completed the rectification of his own prayer in order to elevate it, how can he elevate the prayers of others? Even the shaliach tzibbur elevates others' prayers only together with his own; if his has not yet been rectified, how can he rectify others? Rav Pe'alim further writes that because he began the songs, he has a connection to the world of Yetzirah and may recite Kaddish. This too appears incorrect. The ascent through the seven palaces of Yetzirah to their Holy of Holies occurs through the five Halleluyahs, and through 'Praise God in His holiness' they ascend to the Holy of Holies, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaZemiros, chapter 6, and explained in Rashash's siddur. Since their rectification to ascend to their Holy of Holies is not yet complete, how can they ascend to Beriah through Kaddish? Moreover, since the rectification of Yetzirah is not complete, how can it ascend? The principal ruling is therefore not to interrupt.

<b>17) There: No bad reputation should have emerged concerning him, even in his youth.</b> Re'em, Responsa 1:86, writes that Rambam disagrees with Baal HaTurim. Rambam holds that an ordinary shaliach tzibbur (not one serving during a fast) need not have an unblemished past. If he now possesses good deeds, then even if a bad reputation emerged in his youth, and even if he worshipped idolatry, once he repented he is fit to be a permanent shaliach tzibbur. This is the primary ruling. Rashach 2:107; Rabbi Aharon Sasson 157–158; Mahara di Boton, handwritten responsum 3; and Maharshal, Responsa 20, rule likewise. Maharshal writes that all these qualifications are required specifically on fasts and during maamados. On other days, even for a permanent appointment, anyone who is not wicked, is acceptable to the people, has a pleasant voice, and is accustomed to read may be appointed shaliach tzibbur; see there. Rashdam, Orach Chaim 32 and Yoreh Deah 113, however, writes that Rambam does not disagree with Baal HaTurim; even if he does, Baal HaTurim's view is primary. Mishpat Tzedek 1:18 writes that perhaps even according to Rashdam, one who became an Ishmaelite and then fully repented may serve as shaliach tzibbur, although the question posed to him did not concern an apostate; see there. Raavad 13 writes that this is also evident from Rashdam; see there. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur and Shiyurei Hagahos Beis Yosef 2. Magen Avraham 8 writes that if he committed a transgression under compulsion, everyone agrees he is permitted. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 73c, rules likewise. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, however, questions Rashdam's aforementioned words and concludes: For an ordinary shaliach tzibbur we do not require an unblemished past. Anyone whom we see has repented is called fit. This is the primary ruling, and most later authorities rule accordingly. Pri Chadash 25; Birkei Yosef 2 in the name of the later authorities, who writes that he also found this explicit in the handwritten Responsa of the great master Mahari ibn Migash 97; Maamar Mordechai 1; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 73b. Olas Tamid 5 likewise concludes this, adding that if the congregation decrees a fast, then even if it is not presently an established public fast, such a person should not serve; all the more so on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Eliyah Rabbah 6 rules likewise, but concludes that during the rest of the year too it is proper to be stringent in accordance with Shulchan Aruch and Levush.

<b>23) The poskim write</b> that among the qualities required of a shaliach tzibbur is that he forgo his claims against others, as the Talmud states in Taanis 25b: Rabbi Eliezer descended before the lectern and was not answered. Rabbi Akiva descended, said Avinu Malkeinu, and was answered. A heavenly voice declared: It is not because this one is greater than the other, but because this one forgoes his claims and the other does not. Solel Boneh 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 71b. He must also not be a flatterer. Sotah 41b states: Rabbi Elazar said that anyone who possesses flattery brings wrath to the world, as it is written, 'Those who flatter in heart incur wrath.' Moreover, his prayer is not heard, as it says, 'They do not cry out when He binds them.' It is also written, 'For what is the hope of a flatterer when he gains,' and, 'Will God hear his cry when distress comes upon him?'
