<b>Seif 13, and he is, etc.</b> This is the language of the Rif there: it is one whose garments are torn and whose shoulders and arms are exposed, so that the garments upon him are like a belt below his armpits and lower. This is as it is written, 'and you shall loosen the sackcloth, etc.,' and Yonasan translates: go and remove the sackcloth from your loins. And it is written, 'and he did so, walking naked and barefoot,' and Hashem said, 'as, etc.' Yonasan translates this as puchi'ach, etc. Thus it is one who is girded at his loins while his shoulders and arms are uncovered. And in the Yerushalmi at the end of the first chapter of Moed Katan: if one relative died, he tears; if another relative died, he distances three fingerbreadths and tears. If the front became full, he begins from the back; if the top became full, he begins from below; if both this and that became full, Rav Chiyya bar Abba of Yaffo said: he becomes like a puchi'ach. So too it is stated explicitly in Yoma 77a, for if you do not say so, etc. It is implied from the words of the Rif that this is specifically when his shoulders too are exposed, and such is the language of the Tur.
