<b>(ס"ק א) מעומד כו'</b> משמע כשמתפלל ביחיד. דודאי כשמתפלל בצבור גם היחיד צריך לעמוד כמו הש"ץ אע"ג דבש"ץ הטעם שיסמוך לו הקדיש דאם ישב בישתבח ויצטרך לעמוד להקדיש יהי' קצת שהי' והפסק בין ישתבח לקדיש מ"מ גם היחיד צריך לעמוד בשעת הקדיש (עכ"פ בשעת אמירת איש"ר) כדלקמן סי' נ"ו ס"א מה שאין כן כשהיחיד מתפלל שלא בצבור משום ישתבח גופיה א"צ לעמוד וכן בשבת שהחזן מנגן ומאריך בסוף ישתבח באמירת בא"י אז יש שהות ליחיד המתפלל בצבור לעמוד בשעה שהחזן מנגן ואז אין היחיד צריך לעמוד בישתבח. אבל בס"ס נ"א (ס"ז בהג"ה) משמע דצריך לעמוד דהא כלל בחד בבא ישתבח עם ב"ש ויברך דוד משמע דדיניהן שוה וגם בישתבח צריך לעמוד משום ישתבח גופי' כמו ב"ש ויברך דוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 1: “Standing,” etc.</b> This implies that the ruling concerns one who prays alone. Certainly, when praying with a congregation, an individual must stand just as the chazzan does. Although the reason for the chazzan is that Kaddish should follow immediately, since if he sat during Yishtabach and then had to stand for Kaddish there would be a slight delay and interruption between Yishtabach and Kaddish, nevertheless the individual must also stand during Kaddish, at least while reciting “Amen, yehei Shemei rabbah,” as stated below in siman 56, seif 1. By contrast, when an individual prays without a congregation, he need not stand because of Yishtabach itself. Likewise, on Shabbos, when the chazzan sings and prolongs the conclusion of Yishtabach while saying “Blessed are You, Hashem,” there is time for the individual praying with the congregation to stand while the chazzan is singing, and the individual therefore need not stand during Yishtabach. However, at the end of siman 51, seif 7 in the gloss, it appears that one must stand, since Yishtabach is included in a single clause together with Baruch She'amar and Vayevarech David. This implies that their laws are the same, and that one must stand during Yishtabach because of Yishtabach itself, just as during Baruch She'amar and Vayevarech David.

<b>(ס"ק יז) אינו כו'</b> וכ' התו' סוף פ"ז דפסחים ד' פ"ו ע"ב אהא דאמרי' התם ר"ה ברי' דרב נתן אקלע לדבי רב נחמן בר יצחק אמרי ניתב אפוריא יתיב יהבו ליה כסא קבלו' בחד זימנא א"ל מ"ט כי אמרי לך ניתב אפורי' יתבת א"ל כל מה שיאמ' לך בעה"ב עשה כו' מ"ט כי יהבי לך כסא קבלת בחד זימנא א"ל מסרבין לקטן ואין מסרבין לגדול וכ' התו' וז"ל אמאי דיתיב אפוריא לא שייך למימר משום אין מסרבי' דישיבת פורי' דבר גסות ושררה (דקטנים יושבים ע"ג ספסלים כ"כ רש"י שם) ולא ה"ל לישב אי לאו משום כ"מ שיאמר בעה"ב כו' עכ"ל ומה שהוצרך בקבלת הכוס לו' משום דאין מסרבין כו' ולא הוי סגי בהא דכל מה שאומר בעה"ב עשה עיין במ"א לק' סי' ק"ע ובמהרש"א בח"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 17: “He should not,” etc.</b> Tosafos at the end of the seventh chapter of Pesachim, 86b, comments on the incident in which Rav Huna son of Rav Nassan visited Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak. They told him, “Sit on the bed,” and he sat. They gave him a cup, and he accepted it immediately. They asked why he had sat when told to do so, and he answered, “Whatever the host tells you, do.” They asked why he accepted the cup immediately, and he answered, “One may initially refuse a lesser person, but one may not refuse a great person.” Tosafos writes that his sitting on the bed could not be explained by the rule against refusing, because sitting on a bed is an act of haughtiness and authority, as younger people sit on benches, as Rashi explains there. He should therefore not have sat had it not been for the rule, “Whatever the host tells you, do.” Regarding acceptance of the cup, however, the Gemara needed the separate reason that one may not refuse a great person. Why the rule concerning the host was insufficient for accepting the cup is discussed by Magen Avraham below, siman 170, and by Maharsha in Chiddushei Aggados.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 20: “Even,” etc.</b> Yam Shel Shlomo means specifically when another candidate can be hired for the same amount. If the congregation would need to pay another candidate more, even when the other is superior by an amount corresponding to the additional pay, the individual cannot compel the congregation to pay more. He may do so only if he personally wishes to pay the additional wage. His wording there implies that even if the congregation had previously been accustomed to pay a higher wage, but now found a chazzan who requests less, an individual who wishes them to hire a superior candidate and pay him the amount they formerly paid cannot compel them to do so, since they have now found someone who accepts less than the former chazzan.

<b>For it can be said that they would damage his reputation.</b> The case necessarily concerns an individual who has no witnesses to the chazzan's disqualification. If he had witnesses, he would produce them and the chazzan would certainly be removed. Since he has no witnesses, even though he objects because of the alleged disqualification, he is not heard, although he would be heard were he to advance another legitimate claim, for even one individual may initially prevent an appointment when he has a sound reason. Here, however, we are concerned for damage to the chazzan's reputation. If they remove him, or decline to appoint him initially, and a report spreads that this was because of the alleged disqualification, the rumor of disqualification will thereby be strengthened, although it may be false.

<b>“It appears to me that it is better,” etc.</b> “Those who forsake Hashem will perish,” etc. At first glance there is no practical difference between Knesses HaGedolah and Magen Avraham, and they disagree only about the rationale. Knesses HaGedolah invokes “Those who forsake Hashem,” while Magen Avraham invokes shortening of one's life. The wording, however, does not indicate this. They also disagree in practice. Even after one has been called to the Torah, it is still between one aliyah and the next, since the next reading has not yet begun. Because Knesses HaGedolah writes that he must ascend lest he be included among those who forsake Hashem, he necessarily maintains that even leaving between aliyos falls under that prohibition. Accordingly, even before he has been called, if he knows he will be called, he may not leave. Yet since Maharil writes that even after being called he should not ascend, Knesses HaGedolah disputes him, and from Knesses HaGedolah's rationale we automatically know that even before being called he may not leave. Magen Avraham, however, maintains that leaving between aliyos does not involve forsaking Hashem, as he writes below in siman 146, seif 1. Therefore, even after being called it would still be permitted to leave on that ground, since it remains between aliyos. Once he has been called, however, he must ascend for another reason, as stated in Berachos 55a: Rabbi Yehudah said that three things shorten a person's days and years, including being given a Sefer Torah from which to read and refusing to read, as it is written, “For it is your life and the length of your days.” Before he has been called, this consideration does not apply. Therefore, if he merely knows that he will be called, he may leave before they call him.
