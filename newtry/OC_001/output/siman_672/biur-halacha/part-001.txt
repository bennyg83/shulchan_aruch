<b> עם סוף שקיעתה</b> -  כ"כ הטור וכ"כ הרא"ש בפ"א דתענית אבל ברשב"א ור"ן משמע בהדיא שהוא תחלת השקיעה אלא דמשמע שם בשקיעה שניה [הגר"א] וכ"כ במאירי כרשב"א ור"ן וכתב עוד ועיין במרדכי ספ"ב דשבת שכתב דלשון משתשקע מוכח שהוא תחלת השקיעה וכו' וכן עיקר עכ"ל וכ"כ הפר"ח.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the end of its sunset.</b> So wrote the Tur, and so wrote the Rosh in chapter 1 of Taanis. But in the Rashba and Ran it is explicitly implied that it is the beginning of sunset, though it is implied there that this means the second sunset; Gra. So too the Meiri wrote like the Rashba and Ran. He wrote further: See the Mordechai at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, who wrote that the language 'from when the sun sets' proves that it is the beginning of sunset, etc., and this is the main view. End quote. So too wrote the Pri Chadash.

<b> לא מאחרים</b> -  ולפ"ז צריך להדליק תיכף אחר צה"כ וא"כ צ"ע מי שנוהג תמיד להתפלל מעריב בזמנו דהיינו אחר צה"כ איך יעשה [פמ"ג] והנה לדחות תפילת ערבית משום זה ג"כ אינו כדאי דיש בה מצות ק"ש שהיא דאורייתא וגם דתדיר קודם כמו שכתבו האחרונים וע"כ נ"ל דאיש כזה בודאי מן הנכון שידליק קודם מעריב אחד דהרבה ראשונים ס"ל דלכתחלה יש לנהוג כן כמו שהביא הגר"א ועוד דאף הטור שכתב סוף שקיעה נוכח בסמוך דגם הוא ס"ל דיכול להקדים עד קרוב לחצי שעה מקודם ובפרט אם נסבור בדעת הרמב"ם במה שכתב דמדליקין עם שקיעתה היינו ממש עם הזמן שנתכסה השמש מעינינו וידוע שדעתו דמצות הדלקה הוא רק כחצי שעה או מעט יותר אח"כ ואם עבר הזמן שוב לא ידליק עוד א"כ אם ידליק בשעת צה"כ אינו יוצא כלל מצות הדלקה לדעת הרמב"ם והעומדים בשיטתו בזמן השקיעה. ואפילו המקילים שיכול להדליק אח"כ הוא ג"כ רק מטעם ספק לכמה פוסקים וע"כ נראה דטוב ונכון לכתחלה להדליק קודם מעריב ועיין בשע"ת שכתב בשם מח"ב ג"כ שמי שעושה כן לא הפסיד ולפי מה שהוכחנו יש לנהוג כן לכתחלה.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One does not delay.</b> According to this, one must light immediately after tzeis hakochavim. If so, it requires investigation what one who always practices praying Maariv at its proper time, meaning after tzeis hakochavim, should do; Pri Megadim. To postpone the Maariv prayer because of this is also not worthwhile, for it contains the mitzvah of reciting Shema, which is according to the Torah, and also that which is frequent takes precedence, as the later authorities wrote. Therefore, it appears to me that such a person should certainly, as the correct practice, light before Maariv. First, many early authorities hold that ab initio one should practice this way, as the Gra brought. Furthermore, even the Tur, who wrote 'the end of sunset,' proves nearby that he too holds that one may light early, up to nearly half an hour beforehand. This is especially so if we understand the Rambam's view, in what he wrote that one lights with its sunset, to mean literally at the time when the sun is covered from our eyes; and it is known that his view is that the mitzvah of lighting lasts only about half an hour, or a little more afterward, and if the time passed, he no longer lights. If so, if he lights at tzeis hakochavim he does not fulfill the mitzvah of lighting at all according to the Rambam and those who follow his view regarding the time of sunset. Even according to those who are lenient that he may light afterward, this too is only because of doubt according to several poskim. Therefore it appears that it is good and proper ab initio to light before Maariv. See Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote in the name of Machazik Berachah as well that one who does so has lost nothing; according to what we have proven, one should practice this way ab initio.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one does not light early.</b> This is from the language of the Rambam. See the Ran and Rashba, who disagree with this; Gra. The source of the Rambam was written by the Maggid Mishneh: it is from what appears in Shabbos 23b, 'provided that he does not light early and does not light late,' and he explains it as referring to Chanukah. According to this, for most poskim, who explain that it refers to the Shabbos lamp - Rabbeinu Chananel, Rashi, Tosafos, the Rosh, Rabbeinu Yonah, and the Mordechai at the beginning of chapter Tefillas HaShachar - we no longer have a source for the Rambam's law. The Tur too, in siman 263, took it regarding Shabbos. It is somewhat surprising that the Shulchan Aruch wrote this without qualification in siman 263, se'if 4, regarding Shabbos, and also here regarding Chanukah. Perhaps here the Tur holds, and perhaps the Rambam also, that it is based on reasoning: it is not recognizable for publicizing the miracle when one lights early, for what benefit is a lamp at noon? But this is difficult. Granted, if he explains 'from when the sun sets' according to its simple meaning, we could say that before sunset, when the daylight is strong, the light of the lamp is not recognizable and there is no publicizing of the miracle. But since the Shulchan Aruch copied the Tur's and Mordechai's explanation, namely from the end of sunset, which is tzeis hakochavim, certainly when he lights a little earlier, about a quarter of an hour, which is the time of bein hashmashos even according to Rabbeinu Tam and those who agree with him, or a little more, there is recognition that it is for night. Know that regarding Shabbos as well, the Shulchan Aruch ruled there that one should not light early because it is not recognizable that he is lighting in honor of Shabbos, and nevertheless it is known that according to everyone one must light before bein hashmashos. Perforce we must say that what they said, 'provided that he does not light early,' means specifically that he lights while the day is still great, when it is not recognizable that it is in honor of Shabbos. So too in our case, that it is for the sake of Chanukah. The language of the Shulchan Aruch there also proves this; see there. But this does not apply when he lights a quarter of an hour, or even approximately half an hour, before tzeis hakochavim. Now, when the Tur copied 'from the end of sunset,' he did not copy 'provided that he does not light early,' and the Rambam, who copied 'provided that he does not light early,' did not copy 'from the end of sunset,' but wrote that one lights with sunset. This is his language in chapter 4 of Hilchos Chanukah, halachah 5: One does not light the Chanukah lamps before the sun sets, but with its sunset; one does not light late and one does not light early. His intent is actually before sunset, as in the matter of what they said in Shabbos: three things a person must say in his house on erev Shabbos with nightfall, etc., 'light the lamp'; thus the language 'with' means some time before. Even if we explain that the Rambam holds like Rabbeinu Tam and those who agree with him, meaning the second sunset, we are still forced to say that the time of bein hashmashos and a little more, about ten minutes beforehand, which altogether is approximately twenty-five minutes before tzeis hakochavim, is not included in 'provided that he does not light early.' The proof is from Shabbos. This is all the more so according to what we clarified above in siman 261 from several poskim, that the Rambam holds that one must light the Shabbos lamps on erev Shabbos before the sun is covered from our eyes, and he does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam that there are two sunsets; and that is called sunset. If so, certainly when one lights even before sunset, this too is not included in 'he does not light early,' provided that he does not light much earlier before sunset, for that is what they meant by 'provided that he does not light early.' The conclusion of our words is that the Mechaber, who copied the Tur's phrase 'from the end of sunset' and joined to it the Rambam's words that one should not light early, requires great investigation. Incidentally, since we are dealing with this matter, I will write what is difficult to me in the words of the Beis Yosef, who wrote: The words of the Rambam are like the words of Baal Halachos Gedolos, who wrote that one does not light the lamps before the sun sets, but with its sunset; one does not light late and one does not light early. End quote. This is difficult, for the Ran challenges the words of Baal Halachos Gedolos from what we hold regarding Shabbos, that after sunset it is forbidden to light, and there too the baraisa teaches 'from when the sun sets.' Perforce, in our case too, if he wants to light earlier, he may light. He answered that Baal Halachos Gedolos will hold like Rav Yosef, and there too it is permitted after sunset; see there. According to this, what will he answer for the Rambam, who rules like Rabbah, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 5 of Hilchos Shabbos? Perforce we must say as we wrote earlier, that 'with its sunset' also includes some time before, similar to what appears in Shabbos: three things a person must say in his house on erev Shabbos with nightfall, etc., 'light the lamp.' What he wrote, that one does not light the lamps before the sun sets, means much earlier.

<b> ובלבד שיתן בה שמן עד שתכלה וכו'</b> -  לכמה פוסקים הוא כחצי שעה אחר צאת הכוכבים שהוא בס"ה שעה וג' רבע שעה מזמן הדלקה ואסור להשתמש לאורה כל אותו הזמן (אף שהדליקה בעוד היום גדול) ואם כבתה בחול קודם צאת הכוכבים צריך לחזור ולהדליק אבל לא יברך דע"פ הדחק אמרינן דמפלג המנחה ולמעלה כבר הותחל המצוה [פמ"ג].
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Provided that he puts into it enough oil until there are no more, etc.</b> According to several poskim, this is about half an hour after tzeis hakochavim, which is altogether an hour and three quarters from the time of lighting, and it is forbidden to use its light during that entire time, even though he lit it while the day was still great. If it went out on a weekday before tzeis hakochavim, he must go back and light it, but he should not recite a blessing, for under pressing circumstances we say that from plag haminchah and onward the mitzvah has already begun; Pri Megadim.
