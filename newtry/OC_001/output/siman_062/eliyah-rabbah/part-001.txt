[א] <b>ולא דקדק בהן וכו'.</b> כתב בספר החינוך פרשת ואתחנן אין הענין שלא להזכיר התיבות והאותיות שכל שלא קרא קריאת שמע כולו לא יצא, אבל ענין לא דקדק יצא הוא שלא נתן ריוח בין הדבקים, וכן שלא התיז הז' דתזכרו או שלא האריך בד' דאחד כפי הראוי לכתחילה לעשות הכל, עד כאן, ולתשובת בית יעקב סימן ע"ו אשתמיט זה. איתא בש"ס כל המדקדק בהן שכרו שמצננין לו גיהנום וכו' שמעתי סימן לדבר ש' משמע וא' מאחד הוא א"ש נשאר מ"ע משמ"ע ח"ד מאח"ד ראשי תיבות 'חנניה 'מישאל 'עזריה 'דניאל, לרמז מי שקורא קריאת שמע בכוונה ניצול מאש כחנניה מישאל ועזריה ודניאל, כן כתב מגן אברהם:
**** ENGLISH ****
[1] <b>And he was not precise in them, etc.</b> Sefer HaChinuch, in parashas Vaeschanan, writes that this does not mean that he omitted words or letters, for if he did not recite all of Krias Shema he certainly did not fulfill his obligation. Rather, "he was not precise, yet fulfilled his obligation" means that he did not leave space between adjoining words, did not sharply pronounce the letter zayin of "tizkeru," or did not lengthen the dalet of "echad" as one should ideally do. This passage escaped the notice of Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 76. The Gemara states that whoever is precise in reciting them is rewarded by having Gehinnom cooled for him. I heard the following mnemonic: the shin of Shema and the alef of echad form eish, "fire." The remaining letters, mem-ayin of Shema and chet-dalet of echad, form the initials of Chananyah, Mishael, Azaryah, and Daniel. This alludes that one who recites Krias Shema with intent is saved from fire like Chananyah, Mishael, Azaryah, and Daniel. So writes Magen Avraham.

[ב] <b>בכל לשון דשמע וכו'.</b> בפרק היה קורא, תנו רבנן קריאת שמע ככתבה דכתיב והיו בהוייתן דברי רבי וחכמים אומרים בכל לשון דכתיב שמע בכל לשון שאתה שומע וידוע דהלכה כחכמים. אך תמיהני איך אמר רבינא בפרק מי שמתו על מתניתין דבעל קרי מהרהר בלבו קריאת שמע, זאת אומרת הרהור כדיבור דמי, הא הוא דלא כמאן דאם יוצא בהרהור פשיטא ודאי דקריאת שמע בכל לשון דבהרהור לא שייך לשון כמו שכתב בחידושי רשב"א ברכות דף י"ד. והנראה לעניות דעתי דרבינא נמי לא קאמר אלא בבעל קרי דחשוב אונס, אבל שלא באונס לכולי עלמא לא יצא, ולפי זה לדידן דקיימא לן כרב חסדא דפליג ארבינא כמו שכתב בית יוסף אף באונס לא יצא, וכן מצאתי שפסק בהדיא בספר תמים דעים סימן ר"מ, ואם כן תימא על השולחן ערוך ולבוש סעיף ד' וה' שפסקו דיוצא באונס. ולדינא נראה לי דאם אי אפשר להוציא בשפתיו יהרהר, אבל עבר האונס ועדיין זמן קריאת שמע צריך לחזור ולקרותה, ועיין לקמן סימן ק"א, ועיין מה שכתבתי סוף סימן זה. כתב כנסת הגדולה השומע קריאת שמע ולא קרא יצא ידי חובתו, הרא"ם חלק א' סימן מ"א ובסימן מ"ב, כתב בשם הר"מ אלשקר שלא יצא, עד כאן. ועיינתי בתשובה גופיה וראיתי שהשיג הר"ם אלשקר על הרא"ם, ומטעם דמצות קריאת שמע היא הקריאה ולא השמיעה דכתיב ודברתם בם והביא הרבה פוסקים שפסקו הכי ודבריו נכונים, וכן נראה מספר תמים דעים שם. מיהו דוקא אם הוא בקי אינו יוצא או אפילו כשאינו בקיאים הוא ביחיד, אבל בעשרה כתב הר"ם אלשקר שם גופיה בשם אבודרהם דשליח ציבור מוציא את מי שאינו בקי, וכן כתב מגן אברהם סימן נ"ט. וגם בזה תמיהני הא כתב אבודרהם דף ל"א זה לשונו יש לשאול למה אומר השליח ציבור ברכות קריאת שמע בקול רם כדי להוציא את מי שאינו יודע וקריאת שמע אומר בלחש. ויש לומר כי מברכות קריאת שמע יכול השליח ציבור ליפטור את מי שאינו יודע מפני שהדבר תלוי בשמיעה כמו בקריאה, אבל מקריאת שמע אינו יכול לפוטרו משום שנאמר ודברת בם, עד כאן. הרי דאפילו שאינו בקי אינו מוציא השליח ציבור, ואם כן לדברי הר"ם יהיו דברי אבודרהם סותרים, וצריך עיון. ומכל מקום לדינא נראה דברי הר"מ דשליח ציבור מוציא את מי שאינו בקי דנראה דכיון שהוא בעשרה ועונה אמן הוי כמו שמדבר עצמו כיון שאינו בקי וקרינן ביה ודברת בם. וראיה לזה מדברי רבינו יונה שהביא בית יוסף בסימן ס"ט עיין שם. וצריך עיון עוד הא דכתב אבודרהם שם שאין לך בור בעולם שאינו יודע לומר פסוק שמע ישראל, ובכך יצא ידי חובתו:
**** ENGLISH ****
[2] <b>In any language, because of "Shema," etc.</b> In the chapter Hayah Korei the Sages taught: Krias Shema must be recited as written, as it says, "and these words shall be," meaning in their original form. This is Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi's view. The Sages say it may be recited in any language, as it says, "Shema," in any language that you understand. It is well known that the halachah follows the Sages. I am nevertheless surprised how Ravina, in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, inferred from the Mishnah stating that a baal keri mentally recites Krias Shema that mental recitation is equivalent to speech. If one fulfills his obligation through mental recitation, it is obvious that Krias Shema may be recited in any language, for language is inapplicable to thought, as the Rashba writes in his novellae to Berachos 14. It appears to me that even Ravina said this only regarding a baal keri, who is considered constrained, but without a constraint everyone agrees that he does not fulfill his obligation. Accordingly, since we rule like Rabbi Chisda, who disagrees with Ravina, as Beit Yosef writes, even under constraint he does not fulfill his obligation. I found that Sefer Tamim Deim, siman 240, rules this explicitly. The rulings of Shulchan Aruch and Levush in seifim 4 and 5, that one fulfills his obligation under constraint, are therefore surprising. As practical halachah, it appears to me that if he cannot articulate the words with his lips, he should contemplate them. If the constraint passes while the time for Krias Shema remains, he must recite it again. See below, siman 101, and what I write at the end of this siman. Knesses HaGedolah writes that one who heard Krias Shema without reciting it fulfilled his obligation, citing Re'em, part 1, siman 41. In siman 42, however, he cites Maharam Alashkar as ruling that he did not fulfill his obligation. I examined the responsum itself and saw that Maharam Alashkar challenges the Re'em because the mitzvah of Krias Shema is recitation, not hearing, as it says, "and you shall speak of them." He cites many poskim who rule this way, and his words are correct. Sefer Tamim Deim there also appears to hold this way. This applies specifically when he is proficient, or even when the listeners are not proficient if they are individuals. In the presence of ten, however, Maharam Alashkar himself cites Abudraham as saying that the shaliach tzibbur can discharge the obligation of one who is not proficient, as Magen Avraham also writes in siman 59. This too surprises me, for Abudraham, page 31, writes: One may ask why the shaliach tzibbur recites the blessings of Krias Shema aloud, to discharge the obligation of one who does not know them, while he recites Krias Shema quietly. The answer is that through the blessings of Krias Shema the shaliach tzibbur can discharge the obligation of one who does not know them, because the matter depends upon hearing just as upon recitation. He cannot discharge him from Krias Shema itself, because it says, "and you shall speak of them." Thus, even for one who is not proficient, the shaliach tzibbur cannot discharge his obligation. According to Maharam Alashkar, Abudraham's words would contradict one another. This requires study. Nevertheless, as practical halachah, Maharam's view appears correct, that the shaliach tzibbur discharges one who is not proficient. Since there are ten and he answers amen, it is as though he spoke himself, because he is not proficient, and "you shall speak of them" may be applied to him. A proof is found in Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 69; see there. Further study is needed regarding Abudraham's statement there that there is no ignoramus in the world who does not know how to say the verse "Shema Yisrael," through which he fulfills his obligation.

[4] <b>That he is not clean, etc.</b> Beit Yosef thus explains Rabbeinu Yonah's permission to contemplate. I found, however, that Perishah in siman 85 disagrees with Beit Yosef. He explains that "he is not clean" means that he is in a filthy place, where even contemplation is forbidden. Rabbeinu Yonah's statement that he may contemplate does not mean that he should mentally recite the text of Krias Shema and its blessings. Rather, he should think that he is obligated to recite Krias Shema and its blessings and feel distress that he cannot do so. The Holy One, blessed is He, then accepts this thought as though he had prayed in a clean place. This is permitted even in a thoroughly filthy place. I also saw Shelah, page 66, explain Rabbeinu Yonah this way. Magen Avraham writes that Rabbeinu Yonah's language does not imply this. Perhaps had he seen that Perishah also explained it this way, he would have agreed. Regarding an ill person or another constraint, Perishah likewise implies that he should contemplate in this manner and not mentally recite the blessing's text. This can be questioned, because if he is not in a filthy place, why should he not contemplate the text of the blessing? This supports what I wrote above, that mental recitation is not equivalent to speech even under constraint. From Rama's words, Taz derives that one who is thirsty at night while in bed, and cannot wash his hands and recite a blessing, should contemplate the blessing in his heart and drink. One can distinguish between Krias Shema, which is an obligation, and a blessing over enjoyment. It also appears to me that in such a case it suffices to clean his hands on a wall, a pebble, or anything that cleans, and he may then recite the blessing, as explained above in siman 4 and siman 92, seif 6. Beit Yosef writes that Kol Bo states that Krias Shema may be recited in any language only with a congregation, but not by an individual, as Rabbi Yochanan said that a person should not request his needs in Aramaic. Beit Yosef responds that he does not know what requesting one's needs has to do with Krias Shema, and therefore sees no basis for distinguishing between an individual and a congregation, as all the poskim likewise make no distinction. To avoid regarding Kol Bo as mistaken, I examined his words and found a novel explanation. Kol Bo writes concerning Rabbi Yochanan's statement that every intelligent person must contemplate it and not mistakenly think that Rabbi Yochanan meant that one should direct his request to an angel, asking the angel to join his prayer and present it before Hashem, contrary to the statement, "Do not cry out to Michael or Gabriel; rather, cry out to Me and I will answer you." Instead, the statement comes to awaken a person to concentrate in prayer. Since Aramaic is foreign and not fluent in a person's mouth, the soul naturally becomes confused by it and proper intent is not readily attained when praying in that language. When the Sages said that the ministering angels do not recognize it, they meant that the good faculties from which good qualities flow are called ministering angels, as in, "If there is an angel over him, an advocate." The evil faculties are called destructive angels. The warning is that because the soul's faculties are not accustomed to that language, one will not concentrate in prayer due to the language's unfamiliarity. Accordingly, Kol Bo's view is understandable. Since his reason is lack of intent, he holds that certainly accepting the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, which requires still greater intent, should not be performed in Aramaic. The other poskim, however, understood the statement literally, that actual ministering angels do not recognize Aramaic, as explained below in siman 101, and therefore did not distinguish regarding Krias Shema. Kol Bo's permission with a congregation must be because people then habituate one another to the language and concentrate better, and also because their collective merit is greater.
