**** ENGLISH ****
We read of the sacrifices of the nesi'im, which are in Parshat Nasso (Numbers 7:1 – 8:4), three each day; and we start reading from the blessing of the priests And it is read with a kohen and a Levite, and an Israelite reads on the first day. On the second day, the Kohen reads "on the second day" until "one young bullock," the Levite reads until "on the third day," and the Israelite repeats from "on the second day", and the portions are read accordingly, each day. On the eight day [of Hanukkah], we start "on the eighth day", and we finish the whole portion, and we read the first portion of [parshat] Beha'a lotcha. {Rama: [And there are those who say that we start from 'on the day that Moses had made an end' (ibid. 7:1) [Tur] and such is our practice.] [And there are those who say that the kohen reads the whole portion until 'on the first day' and the Levite and Israelite read 'on the first day', and such is our practice.] [And there are those who say that the Israelite reads the portion of the following day, that is, from 'on the third day', and accordingly each day, and such is our practice (Haggahot Maimuniyyot). (And it is our practice to finish with 'so he made the candlestick')(Minhagim)."}

**** ENGLISH ****
If Rosh Chodesh Teves falls on Shabbos, three Torah scrolls are taken out. Six read from the weekly parashah; from the second scroll, one reads the portion of Rosh Chodesh, beginning with On the Shabbos day; and from the third scroll, the maftir reads the portion of Chanukah, and he reads the haftarah of Rani Vesimchi. If Rosh Chodesh falls on a weekday, two Torah scrolls are taken out. From one, three read the portion of Rosh Chodesh, and from the second, one reads the portion of Chanukah. If the chazan erred and read the fourth aliyah from the portion of Rosh Chodesh, then if they had not taken out the second scroll, he need not read more. But if they had taken out the second scroll, because of possible blemish to it, he must read a fifth aliyah from the portion of Chanukah. Gloss: If he erred and began to read the portion of Chanukah, he must interrupt and read the portion of Rosh Chodesh. Abudarham, Laws of Blessings. Whether he must bless over the reading of Rosh Chodesh, see above, siman 140.
