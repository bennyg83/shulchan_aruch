<b>אם טעה כו'. </b> הש"ע הוסיף איזה דברים על לשון הטור ולענין דינא כל דבריו אמת רק בענין הביאור של לשון הטור נ"ל  קצת בע"א דנ"ל דרבינו ס"ל דמ"ש בגמרא הכל הולך אחר  החיתום הוה פי' דאם סיים מ"ע בשחרית בכל גווני לא יצא אע"פ שאמר בפתיח' וגם באמצע יוצר אור אבל אם סיים יוצר המאורות אז בעי' שיהיה מזכיר עכ"פ מברכ' יוצר אור או בפתיחה או באמצע מלבד הסיום וזה הוכיח רבינו מדאמר התלמוד פתח במ"ע וסיים בי"מ יצא מדלא נקט אמר מ"ע וסיים בי"מ אלא ש"מ דבזה לא יצא אלא בעי' פתח לחוד במ"ע ותכף נזכר ואמר יוצר אור גם סיים כדין אז יצא והטעם דבעינן בחתימה מעין הפתיחה או מעין סמוך לחתימה וא"ל א"כ גם מ"ש התלמוד פתח ביוצר אור וסיים במ"ע לא יצא דהיינו דוק' אם הפתיח' לחוד ביוצר אור וכל השאר במ"ע אבל אם אמר הכל ביוצר אור וסיים כמ"ע לחוד אימא ה"נ דיצא הא ל"ק דאדרבא זהו רבותא דאע"פ שאחר הפתיח' אמר מ"ע וגם סיים במעריב ערבים דהוה סמוך לחתימה אפ"ה לא יצא וכ"ש אם אמר הכל ביוצר אור והסיו' לחוד במ"ע דלא הוה בחתימ' מעין מה שנזכר תחלה כלל דלא יצא ומ"ה אמר רבינו דאם פתח ביוצר אור ואח"כ אמר מ"ע וגם סיים במ"ע דלא יצא דהיינו לרבותא כמ"ש וכ"ש אם הסיום לחוד במ"ע ומש"כ דאם פתח וסיים ביוצר אור דיצא אע"ג דבאמצע אמר של מעריב ולא הוה סמוך לחתימ' מעין החתימה ומנ"ל לרבינו הא ואע"ג דהוה מעין פתיח' מנ"ל דמהני י"ל דא"כ הי' לו לתלמודא לומר רבותא במקו' שאומר פתח ביוצר אור וסיים במ"ע לא יצא אפי' סיי' ביוצר אור לא יצא כיון שלא אמר באמצע של יוצר אור אלא ש"מ  דבכה"ג יצא דפשיטא דעדיף טפי כיון דהפתיחה והסיום כהוגן כן נ"ל כוונת רבינו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he erred, and so forth.</b> Shulchan Aruch adds certain points to the Tur's wording. As a matter of practical halachah, all its rulings are correct. Regarding the explanation of the Tur's language, however, it appears to me somewhat differently. It seems that Rabbeinu understands the Gemara's statement, ‘Everything follows the conclusion,’ to mean that if one concludes in the morning with ‘Who brings on evenings,’ he has not fulfilled his obligation under any circumstances, even if he said ‘Who forms light’ both at the opening and in the middle. But if he concludes with ‘Who forms the luminaries,’ he must at least have mentioned the formulation of Yotzer Or either at the opening or in the middle, in addition to the conclusion. Rabbeinu derives this from the Gemara's wording, ‘He opened with “Who brings on evenings” and concluded with “Who forms the luminaries,” he has fulfilled his obligation.’ The Gemara does not say merely, ‘He said “Who brings on evenings” and concluded with “Who forms the luminaries.”’ This indicates that in the latter case he would not fulfill his obligation. Rather, the case is specifically that he began only with ‘Who brings on evenings,’ immediately remembered and said ‘Who forms light,’ and also concluded properly. He then fulfills his obligation. The reason is that the conclusion must relate either to the opening or to the subject stated immediately before the conclusion. One might object that when the Gemara says, ‘He opened with “Who forms light” and concluded with “Who brings on evenings,” he has not fulfilled his obligation,’ perhaps this applies only when the opening alone was ‘Who forms light’ and everything else was from ‘Who brings on evenings.’ But if he recited the entire text of Yotzer Or and only concluded with ‘Who brings on evenings,’ perhaps he would indeed fulfill his obligation. This is not difficult. On the contrary, the Gemara teaches a greater novelty: even though after the opening he said the text of ‘Who brings on evenings’ and also concluded with ‘Who brings on evenings,’ so that the material immediately before the conclusion matched the conclusion, he nevertheless did not fulfill his obligation. All the more so, if he recited the entire text of Yotzer Or and only the conclusion was ‘Who brings on evenings,’ in which case the conclusion does not relate at all to what was mentioned previously, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Rabbeinu therefore writes that if one began with Yotzer Or, then said the text of ‘Who brings on evenings,’ and also concluded with ‘Who brings on evenings,’ he has not fulfilled his obligation. This is stated as the greater novelty just explained, and certainly if only the conclusion was ‘Who brings on evenings.’ He further writes that if one began and concluded with Yotzer Or, he has fulfilled his obligation even though in the middle he recited the text of ‘Who brings on evenings,’ despite the fact that the material immediately before the conclusion did not match the conclusion. From where does Rabbeinu derive this? Although it matches the opening, from where do we know that this suffices? The answer is that otherwise the Gemara could have stated an even greater novelty. Instead of saying that if one opened with Yotzer Or and concluded with ‘Who brings on evenings’ he did not fulfill his obligation, it could have taught that even if he concluded with Yotzer Or he did not fulfill his obligation when he did not recite the text of Yotzer Or in the middle. Since the Gemara did not say so, it follows that in such a case he has fulfilled his obligation. This is self-evidently preferable, since both the opening and conclusion were proper. This appears to me to be Rabbeinu's intent.
