<b>ומוטב שימעט בה. </b> ומבואר בדברי הראשונים אפילו אם יש לו עוזרים רבים וגם שני תנורים וכה"ג אפילו הכי לא ירבה שיעור העיס' וכ' הכל בו וז"ל כי קפדינן שלא ללוש יותר משיעור זה כשהוא לש מדות הרבה אבל אם לא בא ללוש רק מדה אחת אין לצמצם כ"כ שמא לא יהיה השיעור וכשמברך יברך ברכה לבטלה עכ"ל וכ"כ הלבוש וכ' עוד הכל בו ובש"ל ותניא דאם היה יותר ה' או ו' ביצים אין קפידא וכ"כ מהרי"ל אפי' לענין מצת של מצוה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is better that he reduce it.</b> And it is clarified in the words of the Rishonim: even if he has many helpers and also two ovens and the like, even so he should not increase the measure of the dough. And Kol Bo wrote, and this is his language: for we are particular not to knead more than this measure when he kneads many measures; but if he comes to knead only one measure, one should not narrow so much, lest there not be the measure, and when he blesses he will have recited a blessing in vain, end quote. And so wrote Levush. And Kol Bo, Shlah, and Tanya further wrote that if it was more by five or six eggs there is no concern; and so wrote Maharil even regarding matzah of the mitzvah.

<b>שימעט בה. </b> כ' הב"ח ויש חשש שמא יבא אח"כ לידי צירוף ע"י סל ולא יפריש ויבא לאכול טבל לכן טוב לקרב העיסות עכ"ד. ולא ידעתי מאין יצא לו טעם זה וחששה רחוקה היא בחלה בזמן הזה דלמא יבא לידי צירוף ודילמא לא יפריש ממנה דהלא קי"ל חלת ח"ל אוכל והולך ואח"כ מפריש ולא חיישינן שמא ישכח ולא יפריש ממנה והרא"ש פ"ק שהזכיר חששא זו היינו באם הפריש חלה ולא יצא בהפרשתה איכא חשש זו אבל בלא"ה לא חיישינן בחלה בזמן הזה דרבנן ובאמת הטעם פשוט ומבואר להדיא בלשון הטור והמחבר כיון שמדקדק בשיעור עיסה על כן חיישינן שמא יש בהם עיסה כשיעור שמחויב בחלה ושמא אין בהם כשיעור נמצא מברך ברכה לבטלה ואף אם רוצה למעט בכל עיסה ועיסה שיהא בודאי פחות מכשיעור זה ג"כ אסור דמפקיע מחלה ומצוה להפריש מן העיסה לכן טוב לקרב העיסות יחד וכן מבואר טעם זה להדיא בש"ס פרק אלו עוברין (פסחים דף מח ע"ב) ובי"ד ס"ס שכ"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he reduce it.</b> The Bach wrote: and there is concern lest afterward he come to combination by a basket and not separate and come to eat tevel; therefore it is good to bring the doughs close, end of his words. And I do not know from where this reason came to him; and this concern is remote regarding challah nowadays: perhaps he will come to combination, and perhaps he will not separate from it; for we hold that challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously and afterward one separates, and we are not concerned lest he forget and not separate from it. And the Rosh in the first chapter who mentioned this concern: that is where he separated challah and did not fulfill his obligation by that separation, then there is this concern; but otherwise we are not concerned regarding challah nowadays, which is rabbinic. And in truth the reason is simple and explicit in the language of the Tur and the Mechaber: since he is precise about the measure of dough, therefore we are concerned lest among them there be a dough of the measure that is obligated in challah, and lest there not be among them the measure, and he be found reciting a blessing in vain. And even if he wishes to reduce each and every dough so that it be certainly less than the measure, this too is forbidden, for he uproots [it] from challah, and it is a mitzvah to separate from the dough; therefore it is good to bring the doughs close together. And so this reason is explicit in the Talmud, chapter Eilu Ovrin (Pesachim folio 48b), and in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 324.

<b>טוב לקרב העיסות. </b> ואין להניח העיס' בכפל בשעת הפרשתם אלא מפריש מקצה אחד ואחד יערוך במגלגל בקצה השני (של"ה) והמנהג להפריש מכל עיסה ועיסה לבדו (ד"מ) וצ"ל דהוא ע"פ המנהג שנוהגין לעשות עיסות גדולות כמ"ש לעיל ס"ס תנ"ו וע"ש. וכתב במ"א ודי בברכה אחת לכולן אם לא הפסיק ביניהם בשיח' שלא מענין הלישה ועכשיו נהגו ע"פ הרוב להפריש מכל עיסה ועיסה אשה אחרת ומברכת לעצמה ונוהגין לעשותה תחלה כעין מצה ואחר כך משליכה לאור ויש נהגו לשרפה בלי תיקון ואם נפלה לתוך התבשיל הנה מלבד דין תערובת חלה שנתבאר בי"ד סי' שכד יש לדון בזה דין איסור חמץ וכמש"ל סי' תנד ס"ק ד':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is good to bring the doughs close.</b> And one should not leave the dough folded at the time of their separating; rather one separates from one end, and one arranges with the rolling-pin at the other end (Shlah). And the custom is to separate from each dough by itself (Darkei Moshe); and one must say that this follows the custom of making large doughs, as written above at the end of siman 456; and see there. And Magen Avraham wrote that one blessing suffices for all of them if he did not interrupt between them with speech unrelated to kneading. And now they mostly practice that from each dough a different woman separates and recites the blessing for herself. And they practice to make it first like matzah and afterward throw it into the fire; and some practiced to burn it without preparation. And if it fell into a cooked dish: besides the law of a mixture of challah that is clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 324, one must discuss here the law of the prohibition of chametz, as I wrote above in siman 454, s.k. 4.

<b>לקרב העיסות יחד. </b> כדי שיתדבקו זו בזו כדי שירצה להתפרד זו מזו יתלש אחת מחברתה מעט ואז נחשב הכל כעיסה אחת וכשנוטל חלה מא' מהן סגי. אכן ראיתי שנוהגין שחותכין חתיכה אחת משניהם יחד והוא לכאורה מנהג בלא טעם. (וע' בב"י במ"ש בשם תשוב' אשכנזית והיא בתשובה להרשב"א ובתשו' מהר"מ ב"ב סי' שכב דמשמע קצת דבעינן לחתוך מכל עיסה ועיסה גם בזה השיגו עליו וכל שכן בבה"ג שהכל נחשב כחתיכה אחת ועיסה אחת) ומאמ"ו האלוף המרומם מוהר"ר יוסף שמעתי הטעם שלפעמים העיסות הם קשים ואינן נדבקין יחד ע"י קירוב וכמ"ש הט"ז והראש יוסף וע"י חוזק החיתוך שחותכין משניהם נדבקין זה בזה והוא טעם נכון:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To bring the doughs close together.</b> So that they adhere one to the other so that when one wishes to separate them one from the other, one tears a little from its fellow; and then everything is considered as one dough, and when he takes challah from one of them it suffices. However I have seen that they practice to cut one piece from both of them together, and this at first glance is a custom without reason. (And see Beis Yosef in what he wrote in the name of an Ashkenazic responsum, which is in a responsum of the Rashba, and in the responsa of Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 322, which somewhat implies that one must cut from each and every dough; and even on this they disputed him; and all the more so in Beis HaGadol where everything is considered as one piece and one dough.) And from my father and master, the exalted champion Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Yosef, I heard the reason: that sometimes the doughs are hard and do not adhere together through bringing close, as Taz and Rosh Yosef wrote; and through the force of the cutting that they cut from both of them they adhere to one another; and that is a proper reason.

<b>יפרישנה אחר האפיה מיד. </b> דאע"ג דקיימא לן חלת ח"ל אוכל והולך ואח"כ מפריש היינו בעיסה שנילושה יחד והיתה כבר מחוברת הקילו בחלת ח"ל דלא בעינן לפרוש מן המוקף רק אוכל והולך ואח"כ מפריש משא"כ כאן וכן הוא לשון התוס' פ"ק דנדה דף ז' ע"א סוף ד"ה ומקפת ע"ש ומשמע שם מהתוס' אלו דאפי' בשני עיסות שבכל אחד היה כשיעור אם רוצה להפריש אחת על כולן בעינן הקפה אף בחלת ח"ל דלא אמר שמואל אוכל והולך אלא בעיסה אחת שנתחייב בחלה וכ"כ הב"ח בס"ק ד' ובזה מיושב היטב דברי הרא"ש והטור וניצל מכל הקושיות שהקשה בט"ז ס"ק ד' דברי הרא"ש והטור אהדדי דהא דהצריך הרא"ש והטור הקפה אף בחלת ח"ל לא מיירי רק בשני עיסות שיש בכל אחד שיעור חלה ודלא כמ"א בס"ק ג' שהשיג על הב"ח בזה שכתב דמשמעות לשון הרא"ש דאלו עוברין דמיירי בעיס' א' וזה אינו אף דנקט בלשונו עיסה היינו דבעיסה אחת היתה שיעור חלה מ"מ מיירי דאח"כ רוצה לצרף עוד עיסה אחרת וכן מבואר להדיא בקיצור פסקי הרא"ש דשם לכך הצריך שם הקפה (ומ"מ בדיעבד אם שכח ואכל מפריש אח"כ אף בשני עיסות והיה בשניהם כשיעור רק דבעינן הקפה לכתחלה לדעת הטור והרא"ש פרק אלו עוברין כמו שפי' הב"ח והוא נכון להמעיין שם אבל אם אין העיסות כשיעור ואכל אף בדיעבד לא מהני הפרשה אח"כ כיון דלא צירפם יחד א"כ לא נתחייב בחלה כלל אא"כ שייר מכל אחד חתיכה חתיכה ויצטרפם) וכן נתבאר בי"ד סי' שכג בש"ע שם בהג"ה ובב"י שם בשם סמ"ג ובהג"ה מיימוני ומדברי תוס' והרא"ש פ"ק דביצה הובא בב"י לקמן סי' תקו. וכ"כ בהגהת סמ"ק ומרדכי ואגודה פרק א"ט. ובש"ל בשם סה"ת וכל זה פשוט וברור ומיושב בדברי הרא"ש והטור בדבריהם ובפרטות שכן הוא להדיא בקיצור פסקי הרא"ש ובחנם טרחו בכל זה האחרונים ובפרטות הט"ז בסק"ד שהשיג על הרא"ש והטור בכל זה ובאמת לק"מ וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should separate it immediately after the baking.</b> For even though we hold that challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously and afterward one separates, that is for dough that was kneaded together and was already joined: they were lenient in challah of chutz la'aretz that we do not require separating from the surrounding batch, only eating continuously and afterward separating; which is not so here. And so is the language of Tosafot in the first chapter of Niddah, folio 7a, end of s.v. "u'makefes"; see there. And it is implied there from these Tosafot that even with two doughs each of which had the measure, if he wishes to separate one upon all of them we require surrounding even for challah of chutz la'aretz; for Shmuel said "one eats continuously" only regarding one dough that became obligated in challah. And so wrote the Bach in s.k. 4. And with this the words of the Rosh and the Tur are well resolved, and he is saved from all the difficulties that Taz in s.k. 4 raised of the Rosh and the Tur against one another; for that the Rosh and the Tur required surrounding even for challah of chutz la'aretz deals only with two doughs each of which has the measure of challah. And not like Magen Avraham in s.k. 3 who challenged the Bach in this, writing that the implication of the Rosh's language in Eilu Ovrin is that he deals with one dough: and this is not so; even though he used the language "dough," meaning that in one dough there was the measure of challah, nevertheless he deals where afterward he wishes to combine another dough as well; and so it is explicit in the Kitzur Piskei HaRosh there, for that reason he required surrounding there. (And nevertheless after the fact, if he forgot and ate, he separates afterward even with two doughs that both had the measure; only surrounding is required l'chatchilah according to the Tur and the Rosh in chapter Eilu Ovrin, as the Bach explained, and that is correct for one who studies there. But if the doughs do not have the measure and he ate, even after the fact separating afterward does not help, since he did not combine them together, so they never became obligated in challah at all, unless he left a piece from each one and combines them.) And so it was clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 323, in Shulchan Aruch there in the gloss, and in Beis Yosef there in the name of Semag and Hagahos Maimoniyos, and from the words of Tosafot and the Rosh in the first chapter of Beitzah, brought in Beis Yosef below in siman 506. And so wrote in Hagahos Semak and Mordechai and Agudah in chapter Eilu Temutos. And in Shlah in the name of Sefer HaTerumah. And all this is simple and clear, and is resolved in the words of the Rosh and the Tur in their words, and particularly that so it is explicit in the Kitzur Piskei HaRosh; and in vain did the later authorities toil over all this, and particularly Taz in s.k. 4 who challenged the Rosh and the Tur in all this; and in truth there is no difficulty, and this is light.

<b>All the matzos in a basket.</b> And the Bach wrote that according to the Rosh in the laws of challah and Tosafot in chapter Eilu Ovrin (Pesachim folio 47), Tosafot s.v. "ho'il," touching one another is also required; and one must say that the Tur abbreviated in this. And Taz lengthened to challenge him in this from the words of Tosafot in chapter Eilu Ovrin; and what caused all this is that Tosafot in the first chapter of Niddah, folio 7a, at length escaped his notice (for in Tosafot of chapter Eilu Ovrin the Tosafot abbreviated greatly, therefore one can explain like Taz's view, which is not so in the Tosafot of Niddah), who wrote explicitly, and this is their language: and it is proper on Pesach that all the matzos touch in the basket when he separates one upon all of them, for the basket does not combine them; and even though we say challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously, etc. Behold it is explicit from the words of these Tosafot that even with matzos whose obligation comes through combination, even so touching one another is required. And so it is clarified there in Piskei Tosafot and in Agudah in the name of R' Yitzchak. Even though there it is clarified from the beginning of their words that touching is not required where it is not the measure and the basket combines them: nevertheless that is if he wants only combination of the measure; which is not so with matzos, after he baked three or four doughs and combines them together, for through combination there are here two or three doughs of the measure of challah and he wishes to take terumah from the surrounding batch from one upon its fellow: therefore touching one another is required to exempt by one challah, as above, to explain the view of Tosafot; and particularly for one who studies Piskei Tosafot and Agudah. And not as Chiddushei Maharam explained with a strange and forced explanation of Tosafot's view; also Maharsha appears to have emended somewhat in Tosafot and forced their view there; and in truth the words of Tosafot are simple and clear as I wrote. It emerges that the Bach aimed well at Tosafot's view. And nevertheless the views of Tosafot and the Rosh are not the same as the Bach implied; for the Rosh in the laws of challah ruled explicitly the opposite: that if he wishes to combine for the measure he requires touching; which is not so if in two doughs each had the measure of challah and he wishes to take terumah from the surrounding batch from one upon its fellow: neither touching nor basket-combination is required, only that they be adjacent one to the other; and he rejects there Tosafot's proof in chapter Eilu Ovrin. And so wrote Shlah, siman 60, in the name of Sefer HaTerumah; and so ruled the Rashba in his rulings of challah; and so it is implied in the responsa of the Rashba, siman 834; and so wrote Agudah in chapter Eilu Ovrin in the name of HaTerumah and Raavyah: specifically where he is particular about the mixture, such as one dough tamei and tahor and the like, touching is required. (And in Agudah he wrote there that basket-combination helps according to the Geonim even without touching.) And so wrote Kol Bo, folio 49 column 4, that the air of the vessel combines them and touching is not required at all. And this is the view of the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, who did not mention touching at all. Therefore it appears to me that even though l'chatchilah touching is required, after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation and we may rely on the great poskim who did not require touching at all. And similarly if in two doughs there was the measure of challah, after the fact even basket-combination is not required, and all the more so touching: only that they be adjacent one to the other, as the Rosh's language explicitly in the laws of challah. But l'chatchilah one should practice and require the touching. And so wrote Maharshal in responsa, siman 28: even if the dough had the measure and he forgot to separate, l'chatchilah we require basket-combination and touching. However the Rav's view in the gloss is not so, as he wrote explicitly that in such a case even basket-combination is not required. And see there in Tosafot of Niddah who left it as requiring further study whether touching alone counts like basket-combination or like biting; and so wrote in Agudah.
