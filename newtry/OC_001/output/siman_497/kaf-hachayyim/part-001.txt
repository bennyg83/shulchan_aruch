<b>4) There, gloss: Even where they do not require catching, etc.</b> Even in a small enclosure where one need not say, 'Bring a net and let us catch it.' For animals and birds this is considered not requiring catching, as below in se'if 7, but fish are forbidden because they evade capture. This differs from the channel in se'if 5, which is extremely narrow, so the fish cannot evade as much. Maamar Mordechai 1; Mishnah Berurah 2. See below, paragraph 24. Rambam, chapter 2, law 7, permits a small enclosure whenever the fish do not require catching, as does Pri Chadash in se'if 5. Bach rules unlike Rambam and says Raavad and Rashba agree with him. The later authorities are stringent like Rema. See below, paragraph 25.

<b>ה) שם בהגה.  </b>והדגים נשמטים וכו' לפי שהם מכוסים מן העין. הראב"ד מל' הרמב"ם פ"ב דין ז' וא"כ אם המים צלולים שרואים הדגים מותר אם אין מחוסרים צידה. מ"א סק"א. א"ר או' ג' א"א או' א' מיהו דעת הר"ז בקרא או' א' לאסור גם אם המים צלולים יעו"ש. וע"כ נראה כיון דדעת הרמב"ם להתיר בביבר קטן כל שאינו מחוסר צידה כנז"ל אם הזמינם מאתמול והביבר סתום מכל צדדיו וגם המים צלולים שיכול לתפשם במהרם בלי השמטה יש להקל. וית' עוד מזה לקמן סעי' ה' יעו"ש.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) There, gloss: And the fish evade, etc.</b> Because they are hidden from view. Raavad on Rambam, chapter 2, law 7. Thus, if the water is clear and the fish visible, it is permitted when they do not require catching. Magen Avraham 1; Eliyah Rabbah 3; Eshel Avraham 1. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, however, forbids even clear water in Kuntres Acharon 1. Since Rambam permits a small enclosure whenever no catching is needed, if the fish were designated before Yom Tov, the enclosure is closed on all sides, and the water is clear enough to seize them quickly without evasion, one may be lenient. More is explained below in se'if 5.

<b>6) [Se'if 2] Fish, birds, and animals that are muktzeh, etc.</b> This is Rambam's wording in chapter 2, law 17. It shows that even without a catching prohibition, one may not feed muktzeh creatures, as a decree lest he move one. Although moving is only rabbinically forbidden and this would seem one decree upon another, the concern that he may take one is evidently not a formal decree but a likely concern, which applies even where the underlying prohibition is rabbinic. <b>יא) שם.  </b>ואין נותנין לפניהם מזונות וכו' ואין נותנים לפני הדגים מזונות כתב רש"י ז"ל בביצה כ"ג ע"ב משום דאפשר להם בלא מזונות שהם אוכלים שרשי עשבים וקרקע וגדול אוכל הקטן אבל התו' שם כתבו הטעם דאין נותנים לפני הדגים מזונות גזירה שמא יצודם יעו"ש. והב"ד ב"י וכתב שסובר הטור דדגים אפי' היכא דמותר ליקח מהם אין נותנים לפניהם מזונות מטעמא דפירש"י דאפשר להם בלא מזונות יעו"ש, וכתב בד"מ או' א' דאפי' לדעת רש"י הא דנוהגים האידנא דקונים דגים מבע"י והולכים במים תוך כלי דמותר ליתן לפניהם מזונות שהרי אין להם מזונות ממקום אחר עכ"ל, וכ"כ הלבוש. וכ"כ העט"ז, וית' עוד מזה לקמן סעי' ז' יעו"ש.:

<b>11) There: And one may not place food before them, etc.</b> Rashi on Beitzah 23b explains that fish can live without supplied food: they eat plant roots and earth, and larger fish eat smaller ones. Tosafos say feeding fish is forbidden lest one catch them. Beis Yosef cites both and says Tur holds that even where taking fish is permitted, one may not feed them because, as Rashi says, they can live without it. Darkei Moshe 1 writes that even according to Rashi, fish purchased before Yom Tov and kept in water inside a vessel may be fed, since they have no other food. Levush and Ateres Zekenim agree. More is explained below in se'if 7.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>12) [Se'if 3] Even doubtful catching is forbidden, etc.</b> Although a rabbinic doubt is generally lenient, this is forbidden because it is something that will become permitted later, or because the Sages chose stringency here. Mordechai in Yevamos notes many rabbinic doubts treated stringently, so cases cannot be analogized. Magen Avraham 3. Calling the matter rabbinic follows Rambam and those who hold these Yom Tov labors rabbinically forbidden; others hold they are Torah-prohibited, as above siman 495, paragraph 24. The reasons differ practically: if the prohibition is because the item will later become permitted, fish that will spoil by evening may be eaten that day, as in Yoreh De'ah 102:4. Under the second reason they remain forbidden. Beis Meir extensively proves the second reason and forbids every case. Nevertheless, in great loss and for Yom Tov rejoicing one may perhaps rely on the first.

<b>13) There: Doubtful catching is forbidden, etc.</b> All laws of muktzeh and nolad are included, because the item will become permitted later or because the Sages were stringent with this doubt. Rashba disagrees: since muktzeh and nolad are rabbinic, their doubts are lenient; only a matter close to a Torah prohibition is forbidden, such as uncertainty whether it was caught on Yom Tov or beforehand. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, law 6, questions Rashba: why then is an egg of uncertain Yom Tov origin forbidden? He favors Rambam, who draws no distinction. Beis Yosef, Levush 5, and Pri Chadash agree, as do Chemed Moshe 5, Shulchan Aruch HaRav 8, and Mikra Kodesh 37:18. Bach distinguishes where a prohibition was previously established, but Pri Chadash and Maamar Mordechai 4 reject this. The unqualified rulings below in siman 513:2 and 517:1 and the later authorities imply all doubtful muktzeh and nolad is forbidden, whether or not prohibition was established. The lenient statement below in siman 498:3 will be explained there, with God's help.

<b>17) There: If he found the traps damaged, etc.</b> Bach rules stringently, but Eliyah Rabbah 6 challenges him from several Rishonim and the Yerushalmi, all of whom rule like Shulchan Aruch. Pri Chadash agrees, as do the later authorities.
