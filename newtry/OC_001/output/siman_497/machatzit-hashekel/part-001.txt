<b>(ס"ק א) והדגים כו'</b> לפי שהן מכוסים כו' תנן ר"פ א"צ. אין צדין דגים מן הביברים בי"ט אבל צדין חי' ועוף מן הביברין רשב"ג אומר לא כל הביברין שוין זה הכלל כל המחוסר צידה אסור וכל שאינו מחוסר ציד' מותר ואיפסק' הלכת' כרשב"ג. ורמי הש"ס דתני' ביברין של חיה ושל עוף אין צדין מהן בי"ט. ומשני חיה אחיה לא קשי' הא בביבר קטן הא בביבר גדול. עופות אעופות ל"ק הא בביבר מקורה הא בשאינה מקורה. והוכיח שם דציפור דרור אפילו בביבר מקורה לא הוי כניצוד ע"כ תמצית דברי הש"ס המצטרך פה. ונחלקו הרמב"ם והראב"ד דהרמב"ם ס"ל דרשב"ג פליג גם על רישא. וגם בדגים אם הם בביבר קטן מותר לצודן דהוי כניצודים וחיה ועוף ודגים שוין. אבל הראב"ד ס"ל דרשב"ג לא פליג אלא על חיה ועוף אבל בדגים מודה לת"ק דאפילו בביבר קטן אסור לצודן ופסק רמ"א בש"ע כהראב"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 1, and the fish, etc.</b> Because they are covered, etc. We learned at the beginning of the chapter Ein Tzadin: one may not trap fish from ponds on Yom Tov, but one may trap wild animals and birds from enclosures. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: not all enclosures are the same; this is the rule: anything still lacking trapping is forbidden, and anything not lacking trapping is permitted. The halacha was ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. The Gemara challenges this from a baraisa that states that one may not trap from enclosures of wild animals and birds on Yom Tov. It answers: wild animal against wild animal is not difficult; this speaks of a small enclosure and that of a large enclosure. Bird against bird is not difficult; this speaks of a roofed enclosure and that of an unroofed enclosure. It proves there that a deror bird, even in a roofed enclosure, is not considered already trapped. This is the summary of the Gemara needed here. The Rambam and Raavad disagree. The Rambam holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees also with the first clause, and that even with fish, if they are in a small enclosure, one may trap them, for they are considered already trapped, and wild animals, birds, and fish are the same. But the Raavad holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees only regarding wild animals and birds, but regarding fish he agrees with the first Tanna that even in a small enclosure it is forbidden to trap them. The Rama in Shulchan Aruch ruled like the Raavad.

**** ENGLISH ****
However, the Maggid Mishneh challenged the Raavad from what we say on 25a: one who dammed a water channel before Yom Tov, the fish are permitted, as written in seif 5. But according to the Raavad, even in a small enclosure fish are forbidden. The Beis Yosef answered that the reason a water channel is permitted is that since it is narrow, even though it is very long, they are nevertheless considered trapped. This is unlike an enclosure, which is wide, and the fish are also hidden from sight and can more easily slip away; therefore it is forbidden even in an enclosure. Perforce, even according to the Rambam one must say this, for he permitted only a small enclosure, yet in a water channel it is permitted even if it is very long. It must be because a water channel is narrow. Through this the words of the Rama and Magen Avraham, and other places in this siman, will be understood.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For if it was not trapped today, there is no prohibition at all.</b> Likewise, if today is weekday, there is no prohibition at all. But with a double doubt through a mixture, meaning a third mixture according to the Beis Yosef, and according to the Shach even a second mixture, it is forbidden, for this is how we rule in Yoreh De'ah regarding important items that are not nullified if they became mixed even in a thousand. If afterward one of the mixture fell into two other permitted items, and from those three one fell into two other permitted items, the third mixture is permitted because of a double doubt. But in such a case regarding something that has a permitted alternative, the Rama wrote that some say it is forbidden, and he concludes that it is good to be stringent unless there is need or significant loss. Therefore the Taz asked: if so, why did the Rama state here plainly that a doubtful case of prepared status is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov? The Shach there answered with his main resolution, distinguishing between a double doubt generated by a mixture, as there, where the Rama was stringent because there is a definite prohibition present, and here, where both doubts concern whether there is any prohibition at all, in which case even something with a permitted alternative is permitted. Know that the Ran wrote: although for us the second day of Yom Tov is not observed because of doubt, since we are expert in the calendar and know that the first day is primary, nevertheless because of the custom of our fathers we treat it as holy; even so, it has the status of a doubt, for our fathers, who were not expert, had a doubt, and we too do not treat it more stringently than our fathers, since it is only because of their custom.

Since it included wild animal and bird in one clause, it implies that just as the wild animal case speaks of a small enclosure, so too the bird case speaks of a small enclosure, even though perforce it also speaks of a roofed one. This implies that even when roofed, it must be a small enclosure. But Maharsha there wrote that according to Tosafot, once it answers regarding wild animals that this is a small enclosure, etc., it no longer needs to establish the bird case as roofed or unroofed; rather, it too can be explained as a small enclosure where one does not say bring a trap, etc. Thus the proof I wrote is gone. However, the Magen Avraham follows his own view, for he wrote above that without a roof, even a small house is not considered trapped. Therefore even according to the conclusion one must say that the Mishnah permitting birds to be trapped speaks of a roofed enclosure; only the Mishnah saying they may not be trapped need not speak of an unroofed enclosure, but even if roofed, it is a large enclosure. According to this, the Magen Avraham's proof above is sound, that even when roofed one requires a small enclosure, as above.

But coins do not go by themselves unless a person took them, and when he took them he took both, as Rashi explained. According to this, since the Sages agree regarding fledglings in the latter clause, where he designated three and found two, the same applies in the first clause, where he designated two and found three: they agree with Rabbi that we do not say they all went away and these are others, but since they hop, they agree with Rabbi that we say only that one is mixed among them, even though in the first clause regarding second tithe, where he left one hundred and found two hundred, they disagree with Rabbi and hold that all is chullin. Nevertheless, with fledglings they agree.

<b>And see seif 13 in the gloss, that even when tied, we say they tend to hop.</b> What he wrote, siman 439, perhaps intends to resolve a slight difficulty in the Bach's explanation. For it is stated regarding the answer that fledglings hop that Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Elazar said about it: one says the dispute is with two pouches, but with one pouch all agree that it is chullin; and one says the dispute is with one pouch, but with two pouches all agree that one hundred remains and one hundred was taken. If so, according to the one who says that with two pouches the Sages agree that one hundred remains and one hundred was taken, why did Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Elazar need to answer that fledglings are different because they hop? Fledglings resemble two pouches. Rabbi Elazar said: here we deal with tied fledglings and tied pouches. Meaning, when we say the dispute is with one pouch, this does not mean literally one pouch, but two pouches similar to one pouch, namely where they are tied, and the Sages hold that in such a case all is chullin. They wanted to establish our Mishnah here as speaking of tied fledglings. Therefore according to the Sages one must say that fledglings are different because they hop and become separated from one another. These are the Gemara's words. If so, the Gemara's words on the first clause, with two found as three, where it says whichever way you look at it, if they are others, etc., according to the Bach's explanation mean according to the Sages. But since the Gemara had not yet thought of the distinction that fledglings hop, certainly it had not yet thought of Rabbi Elazar's answer that it speaks of tied fledglings. If so, according to the one who says that with two pouches the Sages agree, then with untied fledglings, which are like two pouches, the Sages agree with Rabbi that we do not say these are others. How then could it say, if they are others, etc., according to the Sages? It would have had to say that the Gemara follows specifically the one who says the dispute is even with two pouches. To this he hinted by saying to see what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 439, seif katan 7, that the statement that the dispute is with one pouch, meaning two pouches similar to one pouch, that is, two tied pouches, while with truly two pouches that are not tied the Sages agree, applies specifically in the latter clause, where he left two hundred and found one hundred. But in the first clause, where he left one hundred and found two hundred, the Sages disagree even with two untied pouches. This resolves what was stated according to the initial assumption: if they are others, etc., according to the Sages, even according to the one who says that with two pouches the Sages agree. See what I wrote, with Heaven's help, there in siman 439. The Bach asked: since Rabbi Elazar establishes the latter clause as speaking of tied ones, the same should apply to the first clause, where he designated two and found three. If so, whichever way you look at it, if he found two tied and the third not tied, why are the two forbidden? We know that the untied one is the one that came from elsewhere and the two tied ones are those he designated. If he found all three tied, it is obvious they are forbidden, for we see that all three are not those he designated. And if he found all of them untied, why speak specifically of two found as three? Even if he found only two untied, they would be forbidden, for perforce these are others, since he designated tied ones. He answered that in truth even if he found two they are forbidden because they are untied, and it mentioned finding three because it speaks in all cases, whether he designated tied ones or untied ones; when he designated untied ones, they are forbidden only if he found three, and therefore it mentioned finding three. But the Taz, with whom the Peri Chadash agrees, disagrees and holds that if he designated two tied fledglings and found two untied, they are permitted, for they became separated from one another and are the same ones.
