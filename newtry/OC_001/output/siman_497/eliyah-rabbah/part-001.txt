[ב] <b>שהביבר רחב וכו'.</b> כתב מגן אברהם ורש"ל תפס לו שיטה אחרת שכתב בדגים אינו חייב משום צידה דלא הוי דומיא דמשכן אלא חייב משום קוצר דהוי עוקר דבר מגידולו כדאיתא בירושלמי ומשום הכי חייב אפילו ביום טוב ודוקא בביבר שעל הארץ אבל ארגז מנוקב בתוך המים לא שייך בהן צידה כלל אפילו צריך מצודה קטנה, עד כאן, ולא ידעתי וכו' דירושלמי מיירי כשגורם לדג שימות אבל פשיטא דשייך בדגים צידה כמו שכתבו כל הפוסקים. ומה שכתב ומשום הכי חייב אפילו ביום טוב כלומר דקצירה אסורה ביום טוב אינו ראיה דאף צידה אסורה ביום טוב, עד כאן, ולעניות דעתי ודאי גם רבי שמעון מודה דשייך בדגים צידה אלא קאי להדיא על ביבר זה שאין מחוסר צידה דצידה כזה לא הוי במשכן דחיות ועופות במשכן היו מחוסרין צידה אלא חייב משום קצירה ולכך אסור אפילו ביום טוב דצידה כזה סבירא ליה דודאי לא הוי אסרו ביום טוב דדוקא צידה גמורה אסרו, גם מה שכתב בפירוש ירושלמי כבר דחה רש"ל שם וכן משמע בכלבו דף י"ט, וכל זה ברור למעיין בים של שלמה סימן ב' וקיצרתי. ובדין דארגז הנזכר לעיל הסכים בט"ז אף שמושיט על ידי מצודה לתיבה מרובעת ללוקחו אפילו מושיט איזה פעמים עד שיוציאנו אין קפידא, וכן נראה לי להקל דהא לרמב"ם אפילו בביבר מותר כתב רש"ל שם דוקא אם רוצה ליקח כולן ולבשלן או שאין שם דגים הרבה אבל כשיש שם דגים מרובים וביום טוב רוצה ליקח אחד או שתים ירא שמים יזהר בדבר לכתחילה לקשרם בחוט אותו שצריך לו, עד כאן, והט"ז השיג זה לשונו ותמהתי פשיטא דשרי ליטול הכל מאי שנא מסוכר אמת המים סעיף ה', עד כאן, ואשתמיט ליה דברי רש"ל סימן י"א שכתב גם באמת המים דמיירי כשרוצה ליטול כולן ועיין ס"ק ז', שוב ראיתי בט"ז ס"ק ז' שהביא דברי רש"ל ונדחק לפרשו וגם בזה דבריו דהתם סותרין לדהכא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That the fishpond is wide, etc.</b> The Magen Avraham wrote: and the Maharshal took a different view for himself, for he wrote that regarding fish one is not liable because of trapping, for it is not comparable to the Mishkan; rather one is liable because of reaping, for it is uprooting a thing from its growth, as is in the Yerushalmi; and therefore one is liable even on Yom Tov; and specifically in a fishpond on the ground, but a perforated crate in the water: trapping does not apply to them at all, even if one needs a small net; until here. And I do not know, etc., for the Yerushalmi deals with when one causes a fish to die; but it is obvious that trapping applies to fish, as all the poskim wrote. And what he wrote, "and therefore one is liable even on Yom Tov," meaning that reaping is forbidden on Yom Tov, is not a proof, for trapping too is forbidden on Yom Tov; until here. And according to my humble opinion, certainly even R' Shimon agrees that trapping applies to fish; rather he refers explicitly to this fishpond that does not lack trapping, for trapping of this sort was not in the Mishkan, for wild animals and birds in the Mishkan lacked trapping; rather one is liable because of reaping, and therefore it is forbidden even on Yom Tov, for trapping of this sort he holds they certainly did not forbid on Yom Tov, for specifically complete trapping they forbade. Also what he wrote in explaining the Yerushalmi, the Maharshal already rejected there; and so it is implied in the Kol Bo, folio 19. And all this is clear to one who examines Yam shel Shlomo, siman 2, and I abbreviated. And regarding the law of the crate mentioned above, the Taz agreed that even if one reaches with a net into a square box to take it, even if one reaches several times until one removes it, there is no insistence. And so it appears to me to be lenient, for according to the Rambam even in a fishpond it is permitted. The Maharshal wrote there: specifically if one wants to take all of them and cook them, or there are not many fish there; but when there are many fish there and on Yom Tov one wants to take one or two, a God-fearing person should be careful in the matter l'chatchila to tie with a string the one he needs; until here. And the Taz challenged this; this is his wording: And I wondered: it is obvious that it is permitted to take everything; what difference is there from damming an irrigation channel in se'if 5?; until here. And the words of the Maharshal in siman 11 escaped him, for he wrote also regarding an irrigation channel that it deals with when one wants to take all of them; and see s.k. 7. Afterward I saw in the Taz, s.k. 7, that he brought the words of the Maharshal and strained to explain them; and also in this his words there contradict [his words] here.

<b>Lest one come, etc.</b> In Nachalas Tzvi he challenged the Levush, for he ruled in siman 495, se'if 2, that trapping is rabbinic like the Rambam and the Ran; if so it is a decree upon a decree, as Tosafos wrote s.v. "ve'tanya"; until here. And it appears to me that this is no difficulty at all, for the Levush was precise in "to trap and to take," meaning that since it is forbidden to trap, it is muktzeh, and as he wrote in se'if 7: a decree lest one take; and as the Rambam and the Ran themselves wrote. And if one asks, [regarding] that which the Beis Yosef in siman 498 explained in the Rambam as a decree lest one trap, which the Magen Avraham brought: therefore the main reason of the Rambam appears to be because of muktzeh, and so wrote the Bach; and if so, even regarding muktzeh animals one does not water them and does not place food. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>The traps are disordered, etc.</b> The Bach wrote: It is astonishing regarding the Maggid who permits when one finds them disordered, like R' Shimon ben Elazar, and so ruled the Shulchan Aruch; and it is not clear, for the law follows the first Tanna who disagrees with R' Shimon ben Elazar (Beitzah folio 24), that it is always forbidden until they see a wild animal in the trap before Yom Tov; and so wrote all the authors; until here his wording. And [this is] astonishing regarding his astonishment, for I checked in all the authors and I found only that they wrote in the wording of the Shulchan Aruch: unless it is known that they were trapped today; and the Maggid already explained that when they became disordered it is as if known; and therefore the Rif omitted [the opposing view]. And it is all the more astonishing that I found in Avodas HaKodesh, folio 39, siman 296, that they ruled explicitly like the Maggid and the Shulchan Aruch. Also what he wrote in Yam shel Shlomo, siman 4, escaped him: that the first Tanna also agrees with R' Shimon ben Elazar, and on the contrary he holds that even a doubtful case of something prepared is permitted; and that which he forbids from nets is where it is not known whether the trap was spread before Yom Tov; and on this R' Shimon ben Elazar disagrees: that even when it is known that it was spread before Yom Tov it is forbidden; but when they became disordered before Yom Tov, everyone agrees that it is permitted. And R' Shimon ben Elazar said it only incidentally beside the prohibition. And I found a proof for this in the Tosefta: R' Shimon ben Elazar says: whatever they spread before Yom Tov, and one came and found them disordered, etc.; for it is difficult: why does he need "whatever they spread before Yom Tov," if not to exclude the first Tanna who permits, and he says it is forbidden unless they became disordered before Yom Tov. And likewise in the Yerushalmi, law 20, he ruled simply without dispute that it is permitted when they became disordered. And now it is fine that Rashi explained that the law follows R' Shimon ben Elazar, that a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden, etc.; and examine carefully. And Maharsha did not delve into all this. But it is somewhat difficult that the first Tanna should have distinguished within his own [category] regarding nets between known that it was spread before Yom Tov and not known, and not between anigron; and one can answer. And with this I merited to understand the words of the great HaMaor, of blessed memory: that which R' Shimon ben Elazar said, one may slaughter from anigron on Yom Tov but not from the nets: that is law, even though the Rif did not write it; until here his wording. The explanation appears to me regarding the law above, that he found them disordered, which R' Shimon ben Elazar said in the law of anigron: that this the Rif omitted, as I wrote above; but the law of anigron itself R' Shimon ben Elazar did not say. And Milchamos Hashem challenged HaMaor, of blessed memory: is the Rif a hunter, etc.? But the main law he already wrote, that a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden; until here his wording. And that is because he understood that it refers to anigron, and that is not so. And in order not to equate him with one who errs, it appears to me to reconcile that he too understood so, except that he wrote a reason for the Rif's omission of "hunter": that this is obvious, that if they became disordered while it was still day, presumably they were trapped, as the Maggid wrote; and that which he wrote "is he a hunter" is to learn the manner of trapping; and that is what he wrote, "but the main law he already wrote," etc., meaning that R' Shimon ben Elazar said this only incidentally beside [the rule that] a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden. The general principle of the matter is that the great Geonim among the authors ruled explicitly like the Shulchan Aruch, and the words of the Bach are rejected in this. And in passing I will explain the Yerushalmi mentioned above; this is its wording: Who informs [us] if the trap became disordered: something certain that they were trapped while it was still day; and even if it became disordered, and one is concerned to say maybe they were not trapped while it was still day; until here. And at first glance it is puzzling, and in the gloss on the Yerushalmi there he emends "but if it did not become disordered," etc., and he fixed nothing, for it is still difficult: what is "and even if it became disordered," etc.? And according to my humble opinion, to explain it without any emendation: that which he wrote "if it became disordered," etc., deals with while it was still day; and that which he wrote "if it became disordered," etc., means on Yom Tov; and that which he wrote "even if it became disordered," etc., means even though there is a doubt maybe it became disordered while it was still day, it is as if trapped on Yom Tov; and see in our Talmud and examine carefully.
