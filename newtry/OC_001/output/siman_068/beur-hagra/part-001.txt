Section 61 and 'proper.' As written in chapter 1 in the mishnah — in a place where they told to shorten, one is not permitted to lengthen, etc. And likewise Rambam in chapter 1 of Berakhot: these blessings one may not reduce from them nor add to them. And they said in chapter 6: whoever changes from the coin that the Sages stamped, etc. — Tur there. And thus Rambam in chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema: these blessings with all other blessings, etc., and one may not reduce from them nor add to them; a place where they enacted to seal, etc.; a place where they enacted not to open, etc.; the rule of the matter — whoever changes, etc., this one errs and returns and blesses as the coin; and all who did not say Emet veYatziv in the morning, etc. But in chapter 1 of the five blessings he returned and wrote that all the blessings Ezra and the court enacted, and it is not proper to change them nor add to one of them nor detract from it; and whoever changes from the coin is only in error; and all the blessings are said, etc., and that one should say as the Sages enacted; and if one changed the coin, since one mentioned the Name and malchut and the matter of the blessing, even in vernacular one has fulfilled — even though he pressed in Kesef Mishneh there to explain his words, they are not correct. And behold what he wrote that all the blessings are Ezra, etc. — there thirty-three words; and what he wrote 'a place where they enacted,' etc. — mishnah eleven words; and as written in the commentary there. And the long blessing is Yotzer Or and Maariv Aravim; and the short one is Ahavat Olam, and it is called long Yotzer Or and short Ahavat Olam because every blessing that has at its beginning Barukh and at its end Barukh is called long, and one that is not so is called short. And thus the Jerusalem Talmud on the mishnah above: R' Yudan — short coin opens with Barukh and does not seal with Barukh; long coin opens with Barukh and seals with Barukh. And he explains that likewise the opposite — one that seals and does not open is called short; and this is what they called in the mishnah 'short' for shortening, even though in the commentary he does not explain so. And what he wrote 'the rule of the matter whoever changes,' etc. — chapter 2, R' Yose there forty-two; but in the five blessings he returned and explains like R' Chiya, that in Berakhot like R' Meir; and likewise in the Gemara that says Benjamin evi, etc. — one might have thought it applies only where he said, etc.; and even according to R' Yochanan it follows not like R' Yose, as written; and R' Yose said to him, etc. And 'all the blessings,' etc., and 'if one changed,' etc., and 'since,' etc., like R' Yochanan who said every blessing that does not have, etc.; and all this is bedieved, for lekhatchilah not except bedieved; and according to R' Meir too there is no difficulty except bedieved one fulfills; and likewise Rosh Hashanah and R' Yehudah — lekhatchilah not except bedieved; and it says let us say Rosh Hashanah, etc. And in Tosafot chapter 1: why did they say one long and one short — a place where they said to lengthen is not permitted to shorten; to shorten is not permitted to lengthen; to seal is not permitted not to seal; not to seal is not permitted to seal; to open with Barukh is not permitted not to open with Barukh; not to open with Barukh is not permitted to open with Barukh; to bow is not permitted not to bow; not to bow is not permitted to bow. These are blessings in which the blesser abbreviates: on fruits and on mitzvot, Birkat haZimun, and the final blessing in Birkat haMazon. These are blessings in which one lengthens: fast blessings, and blessings of Rosh Hashanah, and the Yom Kippur blessing — meaning of the Jubilee that is equal to Rosh Hashanah. From a person's blessings it is recognized if he is a Torah scholar or an ignoramus. These are blessings in which one does not seal with Barukh: one who blesses on fruits and on mitzvot, Birkat haZimun, and the final blessing in Birkat haMazon — R' Yehudah haGalili would seal in the final blessing in Birkat haMazon and lengthen in it. These are blessings in which one opens with Barukh: all blessings one opens with Barukh except a blessing adjacent to Shema and a blessing adjacent to another blessing, in which one does not open with Barukh. These are blessings in which one bows, etc. — forty from here for lengthening and shortening per their plain sense, that one is not permitted to add or detract, for behold it counts opening and sealing; and further that it says 'these blessings in which one abbreviates' and 'these blessings in which one does not seal with Barukh' — evidently these are two matters, and in both it says on fruits and on mitzvot; and it says R' Hai Gaon would seal, etc., and lengthen in it — meaning he argues on both. And further that it says 'these blessings in which one lengthens,' etc. — evidently from what it did not count except these, we learn to lengthen actually. And further from what it says 'these blessings in which one opens with Barukh,' etc. — evidently it is another matter. And what it wrote in the Jerusalem Talmud above 'long coin,' etc. — not because of this is it called long and short, but it explains their laws: long opens and seals, short opens and does not seal. And what it wrote in the Tosafot above on mitzvah blessings that they are short and one does not seal in them, and R' Hai Gaon would seal, etc., and lengthen — this depends on that, but not because of this is it called short; therefore it does not say the opposite that short seals and does not open, for this does not depend on that but on adjacency. And what it wrote in the mishnah 'one long,' etc. — according to Rambam's explanation, but not from his reason; rather relative to Yotzer Or it calls it short, and there is no difficulty of long and short except because of the end 'a place where,' etc., and as written in the Tosafot above: why did they say one long, etc. — meaning not that they are truly long and short, but because of the end 'a place where,' etc., that one may not change them; and nevertheless bedieved one fulfills as explained. And likewise from what he wrote 'all who did not say Emet veYatziv,' etc., and 'all who did not say brit vatorah,' etc., and the like — specifically these; but the rest fulfill. And likewise it says in the Tosafot 'from his blessings,' etc. — there is no difficulty except 'behold this one is an ignoramus.' And thus Rambam wrote 'this one errs.'

And some say, etc. — Tosafot there, s.v. 'one long,' etc., and s.v. 'a place where,' etc., and Hagahot there s.v. 'and Perat,' etc., and Rashba also raised Tosafot's difficulty from 'modim,' etc., and further raised that they said 'he opened in deshekra,' etc., 'he opened in Yotzer Or,' etc. And per Rif's explanation who added in it and said 'both of them,' and further that they said 'Benjamin evi,' etc. — rather the explanation is as Rambam wrote in the commentary 'to open,' etc., as above, and as written in the Jerusalem Talmud above: R' Yudan, coin, etc. And it is difficult for him the Tosafot above that teaches to lengthen and to open, and he pressed in explaining the Tosafot: 'to lengthen is not permitted to shorten' — meaning not to open or not to seal; and 'to open,' etc. — meaning not to do the opposite, to seal and not open; and likewise 'to seal,' etc. — also not to do the opposite, to open and not seal; and he brought proof from the Jerusalem Talmud there that the Tosafot above brings: 'these blessings in which one lengthens — Rosh Hashanah blessings,' etc., 'from his blessings,' etc., 'these blessings in which one abbreviates — one who blesses,' etc. — behold all other blessings of a person one lengthens; Hezekiah said from what the mishnah teaches 'one who lengthens — this one is repugnant; one who shortens — this one is praiseworthy' — this teaches that this is not a general rule. And it seems to me from this Jerusalem Talmud forty to the opposite, that he explains to lengthen actually and to shorten actually; except that he said that in all other blessings where it was not said long and short, one is permitted to lengthen and shorten — except that one who lengthens is repugnant, etc., and not as he thought that the rest are long. And thus what he said that this is not a general rule that it said 'these that abbreviate' — do not read 'these' but that in these one is not permitted to lengthen. And further he said there: it teaches 'one must lengthen in Ga'al Yisrael on a fast' — behold in the six where he adds, he does not lengthen; R' Yosei said there is no difficulty, since it is like the eighteen he should not lengthen in it; therefore one must say 'one must lengthen in Ga'al Yisrael on a fast' — evidently he explains lengthening actually. And also Tosafot's explanation is not clear in the Tosafot above: 'why did they say one,' etc., as above; and likewise it seems Rambam in his composition returned from what he wrote in the commentary and explained to lengthen and shorten actually; and what he wrote 'to add or detract' and 'to open,' etc. follows the Tosafot above, and he counts the three matters as in the Tosafot; and all the difficulties above are not difficult, for there everything is bedieved, as explained. And what they raised from 'modim,' etc. — there is no difficulty, for 'one long' refers to Yotzer Or per Rambam's explanation; and it seems one may rely on this Jerusalem Talmud that they said only 'this one is repugnant,' and specifically gratuitously; but like the matter of the day, two opinions, and as written in chapter 1 of tractate Avodah Zarah 8a: a person asks, etc., 'if he comes,' etc.; and they said at the end of Berakhot 'does one lengthen at all,' etc.; and nevertheless 'one who shortens is praiseworthy' as written in the Jerusalem Talmud above. And thus 'and some say,' etc., and 'one who is lenient,' etc. — all the more so in Ahavah Rabbah, which the mishnah called short; and likewise in the prayer of the eighteen, for it says in the Jerusalem Talmud above 'there is no difficulty, since it is like the eighteen,' etc. — forty that one may not lengthen in them except what the mishnah wrote; and likewise from what is written in tractate Avodah Zarah there; but 'if he comes,' etc. — forty that in the middle, even in Shemoneh Esreh, one may not lengthen. And Tosafot there: kerovatz is different, for it is a need of the congregation.
