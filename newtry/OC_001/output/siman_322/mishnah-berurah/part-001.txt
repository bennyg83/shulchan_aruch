(א) אפילו לטלטלה - דכיון דלא חזי לאכילה כמוקצה דמיא והאי דאסורה באכילה [היינו לגומעה חיה] הוא משום גזירה שבת דמיקלע אחר יו"ט שאז ביצה הנולדה בה אסורה מדאורייתא באכילה דכל ביצה דמתילדא האידנא מאתמול גמרה לה ונמצא כשנאכלנה דיו"ט הכינה לשבת וזה אסור מדכתיב והיה ביום הששי והכינו את אשר יביאו וסתם ששי חול הוא להורות לנו דדוקא בחול צריך להכין לסעודת שבת וכן ליו"ט דגם הוא איקרי שבת אבל ביו"ט אסור להכין לסעודת שבת וכ"ש דבשבת אסור להכין לסעודת יו"ט וע"כ כשנולדה הביצה ביו"ט שחל יום א' ג"כ אסור מן התורה לאכלה דבודאי נגמרה במעי התרנגולת מאתמול ונמצא דשבת הכינה ליו"ט כשנאכלנה ואע"ג דהכנה בידי שמים היא ולא בידי אדם גם זה הוא בכלל הכנה ואסור וע"כ גזרו רבנן בכל שבת ויו"ט כשנולדה שאסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) Even to move it — since it is not fit for eating it is like muktzeh; and this that it is forbidden in eating [namely swallowing it raw] is on account of the decree of Shabbat, lest it happen after Yom Tov when an egg born then is forbidden d'oraisa in eating, for every egg born nowadays finished yesterday and it is found that when we eat it on Yom Tov it prepared for Shabbat — and this is forbidden, as it is written "and it shall be on the sixth day that they shall prepare what they bring," and the default of "the sixth" is a weekday to teach us that specifically on a weekday one must prepare for the Shabbat meal; and likewise for Yom Tov, for it too is called Shabbat; but on Yom Tov forbidden to prepare for the Shabbat meal; and all the more on Shabbat forbidden to prepare for a Yom Tov meal; and therefore when the egg was born on a Yom Tov that Sunday also falls on — forbidden from the Torah to eat it, for certainly it finished in the hen's innards yesterday and it is found that Shabbat prepared for Yom Tov when we eat it; and even though preparation through Heaven is not through a person's hand — this too is included in preparation and forbidden; and therefore the Sages decreed in every Shabbat and Yom Tov when it was born that it is forbidden:

**** ENGLISH ****
(ד) Both tough — meaning even tough ones that are not at all fit for animal food — nevertheless permitted to cut them off and to split them as much as he wishes, whether he splits a large or small piece [Rambam]; and specifically in all this where it is only to add scent through rubbing and cutting off; but forbidden to create a scent, such as to place spices in a garment and the like so the garment smell — forbidden [Acharonim]. And incidentally, since we spoke in Shulchan Aruch regarding cutting and cutting off in the matter of animal food and fragrant woods, we will also set forth here the law of the melacha of cutting (mechatech) in other matters. (1) One who cuts leather or anything a person cuts and is particular about its measure — whether wood or metal or even feathers of a bird, since he is particular about its length and width and cuts with intent — liable; but one who cuts in a destructive manner or without intent for its measure but as one who acts unawares or as one who plays — exempt [Rambam chapter 11]. And how well did the prophets (Nechemiah 10) and our Sages z"l profit us in what they forbade us commerce and trade on Shabbat, for sometimes one can come to liability for a chatas, such as when he sells some merchandise and cuts from it per the measure his customer needs — for he is liable on account of cutting [Chayei Adam in general rule 36]. (2) The melacha of cutting does not apply in foods, as we wrote above in s.k. 12. (3) Whether the melacha of cutting applies specifically with a utensil or even by hand — see what we wrote in Biur Halacha that it depends on each thing according to its nature: a thing whose manner is to cut specifically with a utensil — one is liable only with a utensil; and a thing whose manner is to separate it into two by hand — liable even by hand; and it is reasonable that there is a rabbinic prohibition in every manner, as we wrote in Biur Halacha, see there. One who cuts a splinter with a knife to pick his teeth with it — liable for this on account of fixing a utensil according to Rosh, and according to Rambam on account of cutting; and it is possible he is liable according to him for two:
