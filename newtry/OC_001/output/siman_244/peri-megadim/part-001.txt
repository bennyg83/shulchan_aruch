<b>לבנות</b> לו חצירו עט״ז ע״ז דף כ״א ב׳ תוספות ויש לראות הא דאסור מפני הרואי׳ היינו דוקא באיסור מלאכה תורה אף דהוי גזירה לגזירה דאמירה לעכו״ם רק מדרבנן מ״מ הואיל ואסמכיה אקרא דלא יעשה בהם בציר״י ואסמכתא י״א דהוי כשל תורה במ״ש בפתיחה כוללת משא״כ בשבות דרבנן אף דאמירה לעכו״ם אף בשבות אסור כל דליכא מצוה מ״מ לא חמיר ולא גזרו מפני הרואים ומותר בקבלנות ועם זה יש ליישב מ״ש ר״י ברי״ב בשם רב נטוראי ברחיים דמיא מותר ליתן לעכו״ם בקבלנות היינו כמ״ש המ״א ברנ״ב אות כ׳ דרחייא דמיא לית איסור תורה כ״א שבות וכה״ג י״ל דלא גזרו מפני הרואים ומיהו הב״י לא ס״ל כן שכתב דזה לדברי המתירים יע״ש באות וי״ו בט״ז אי״ה יבואר עוד: ומ״ש הטעם כו׳ בא״רר י״א תמה עמ״ש אות ג׳ עסי׳ רמ״ז אות ב׳ מבואר דאסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To build.</b> His courtyard for him. See Taz and Avodah Zarah 21b, Tosafos. It requires examination whether the prohibition because of onlookers applies specifically to labor prohibited by the Torah. Although instructing a non-Jew is itself only Rabbinic, the prohibition was supported by the verse, “It shall not be done among them,” with a tzeirei, and some hold that an asmachta has the force of Torah law, as I wrote in the general introduction. By contrast, regarding a Rabbinic shvus, although instructing a non-Jew is prohibited even for a shvus when no mitzvah is involved, it is not as severe, and perhaps the Sages did not prohibit contracted work because of onlookers. This may explain what Rabbi Yitzchak bar Yosef wrote in the name of Rav Natronai, that one may give a watermill to a non-Jew under a contract. This follows Magen Avraham, siman 252, seif katan 20, that operating a watermill involves no Torah prohibition, only a shvus; in such a case perhaps they did not decree because of onlookers. Beit Yosef, however, does not agree, for he writes that this is only according to those who permit it. See Taz, seif katan 6, where, with Hashem’s help, this will be explained further. Regarding the reason, etc., Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 11, challenges what Taz wrote in seif katan 3. See siman 247, seif katan 2, where it is explained that it is prohibited.

<b>או</b> עט״ז כמ״ש ברנ״ג אות ב׳ כ׳ הר״ן כתב מיחזי כשכיר יום היינו שהעולם א״י לחלק בין קבלנות לשכיר יום משא״כ התוס׳ כתב דיאמרו שכירי יום ובשדה דרכן בקבלנות ועב״ח ולא הבינותים על בוריו דהר״מ והוא ג״כ לשון המחבר שכתב דהרואה יאמר שפלוני שכיר היינו דלא כר״ן שכתב מיחזי כשכיר יום יע״ש וע״ז כתב הט״ז דמוכח גם מתוס׳ כר״מ דהביאו ירושלמי אומנין בתלוש מותר ש״מ מחובר אף בשדה אסור דהם סוברים לסתת אבנים ג״כ אסור מירושל׳ (משא״כ למאן דמפרש סיתות אבנים אין רחיים לאסור י״ל ירושלמי תלוש סיתות אבנים מיירי עב״ח בזה ובמ״א ט׳ אי״ה יבואר עוד (עוד תירץ אע״ג דרובן לקבלנות כל דליכא שני דרכים להיתר אסור יע״ש: ומ״ש רחייא דמי״א אסור עמ״ש באות א׳ מזה ועסי׳ רנ״ג בב״י ומ״א אות ב׳ כה״ג י״ל מדינא אסור ונ״מ אף חוץ לתחום אסור כה״ג: ועמ״א ה׳ וא״ר ה׳ ואי״ה שם יבואר עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or.</b> See Taz. As written in siman 253, seif katan 2, Ran explains that it appears like a day laborer because people do not know how to distinguish between contracted work and day labor. Tosafos, however, writes that people will say they are day laborers, whereas fieldwork is ordinarily done by contract. See Bach. I have not fully understood their words. Rambam, whose language is also used by the Mechaber, writes that an observer will say that so-and-so is a hired laborer, which does not follow Ran, who writes only that it appears like day labor. Taz writes that Tosafos also supports Rambam, for they cite the Yerushalmi that craftsmen working on detached materials are permitted, implying that work attached to the ground is prohibited even in a field. Tosafos understand that stonecutting is likewise prohibited by the Yerushalmi. According to the view that the stonecutting case does not establish a prohibition for a mill, one could say that the Yerushalmi’s law concerning detached work refers specifically to stonecutting. See Bach and Magen Avraham, seif katan 9; with Hashem’s help this will be explained further. Taz further answers that although most such work is contracted, whenever there are not two independent grounds for leniency it remains prohibited; see there. Regarding his statement that a watermill is prohibited, see what I wrote in seif katan 1, and see siman 253 in Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. In such a case it may be prohibited by law, with the practical result that it would remain prohibited even outside the techum. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 5, and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 5; with Hashem’s help this will be explained further there.

<b>מ״ש</b> הט״ז באות א׳ הטעם מפני הרואים בא״ר י״א תמה בזה עליו דשם מבואר דמלאכת עצמן שרי בבית ישראל יע״ש והוה לפחות ודאי מלאכת עצמן אוסר בהגמ״ר דאי מלאכת ישראל פשיטא דלא עדיפי מאומנין וכ״ש דמשתרשי בהו בשבת ומ״ש הגמ״ר דמלאכת עצמן שרי בצינעא תוך בית ישראל ול״ש רואים וכל דליכא כ״א איסור דרבנן לא גזרו בחדרי חדרים כמ״ש הט״ז ברמ״ג אות ג׳ יע״ש דמהרש״ל כ״כ בשם הגמי״י לאסור שפחת ישראל דיש חשש רואים שיאמרו מלאכת ישראל הוא וההיא דהגמ״ר ספי״ד עכו״ם מחוץ אין למחות הא שפחות דידיה יש למחות וצ״ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>What the Taz wrote.</b> In seif katan 1, that the reason is because of onlookers, is challenged by Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 11, for there it is explained that a non-Jew’s own work is permitted even in a Jew’s house; see there. At the very least, Hagahos Mordechai must be prohibiting the non-Jew’s own work, for if it referred to the Jew’s work it would be obvious that craftsmen are no better, especially since the Jew benefits from their Shabbos labor. Hagahos Mordechai’s statement that the non-Jew’s own work is permitted privately within a Jew’s house presents no concern of onlookers. Whenever there is only a Rabbinic prohibition, the Sages did not prohibit it in private, as Taz writes in siman 243, seif katan 3. See there, where Maharshal writes in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos that a Jewish maidservant is prohibited because onlookers may say she is doing the Jew’s work. The statement of Hagahos Mordechai at the end of chapter 14, that one need not protest a non-Jew from outside, implies that one must protest one’s own maidservants. This requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See.</b> Nechalas Tzvi, who writes that according to Rashba’s explanation of the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Shabbos, halachah 8, even contracted fieldwork is prohibited. I do not understand this. It is true that Rashba prohibits even sharecropping of a field in Avodah Zarah 21b. Nevertheless, some say that according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, whose view we follow regarding sharecropping of a field, contracted fieldwork is likewise permitted; while regarding contracted construction of a house, the halachah follows Rashba. In practice, however, even contracted fieldwork is prohibited. If it is publicly known that the field is worked by contract, one could say it is permitted, because the Mechaber gives the reason that people will say they are day laborers, not merely that it appears like day labor. Contracted work involving an oven, bathhouse, or mill, however, remains prohibited even when the arrangement is known, because the Jew benefits from the Shabbos labor, as Mahari Abuhav writes, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 253. See Magen Avraham there, seifim katanim 1 and 2, and what I wrote there in Peri Megadim.

<b>נכון</b> עט״ז ובסי׳ תקמ״ג (ט״ס תקמ״ד) כתב עוד הט״ז בזה: ובמ״ק י״ב א׳ יע״ש ועיקר קושיית הב״ח בתקמ״ג אמ״ש הב״י דלא כתב הטור דוקא אדם חשוב שאני שפסק כלישנא בתרא כו׳ דבחה״מ חוץ לתחום מדינא אסור (מ״ע) ודאי אף לל״ק כל אדם אסור דהרי הונח לו להמקשן אי הוה תוך התחום דאסור לבנות בו בקיבולת אף דהוה רק מ״ע אלא קשיא ליה דחוץ לתחום היתר גמור הוא ומשני לל״ק אדם חשוב אסור אף חוץ לתחום והיינו בשבת או י״ט אבל חה״מ לל״ק ג״כ כל אדם אסור: והט״ז תירץ דאה״נ דכוונת הב״י מדלא כתב בהלכות שבת דחוץ לתחום חשוב אסור ש״מ כל״ב וסמך על כאן דאין חילוק וכל שנבנה באיסור מ״ע לכ״ע נכון לחוד לא מדינא ובהיתר חשוב נמי א״צ להחמיר: וביאור הסוגיא לל״ק ב׳ חומרות א׳ חשוב אף בהיתר גמור ראוי להחמיר ב׳ נבנה באיסור מ״ע לכ״ע אסור מדינא מדפריך והא חוץ לתחום ש״מ תוך התחום הונח לו דאסור וי״ל מדינא ולל״ב ב׳ קולות בהיתר א״צ אדם חשוב להחמיר ב׳ באיסור רק נכון לא מדינא דהני רבנן לא עיילי דמחמרי כו׳ ופסק הטור כל״ב בשל סופרים לקולא. ר״ל ל״ב מחמיר דל״ק לשאר אדם אף באיסור שרי רק א״ח ופסק כל״ב לחומרא בזה דל״ב עיקר וי״ל עוד ל״ב דוקא סייעי׳ בתיבנא איסור גמור מדינא ממ״ש נכון להחמיר הא בקיבולת מ״ע א״צ להחמיר כלל (ולפ״ז מ״ש הטור ואפיל״ו נבנה באיסור נכון להחמיר י״ל דה״ק לא מבעיא נבנה במ״ע דרק נכון לא מדינא אפילו נבנה באיסור ממש שכירי יום וסייעי׳ בתיבנא רק נכון לא מדינא אלא שזה דוחק גם י״ל במ״ע אין בו חומרא כלל לל״ב לכן עשה הב״י כלומר אפיל״ו נבנה (כב״ר) נכון להחמיר דיעבד: והב״ח כתב כאן דכדאי ר״ת לסמוך עליו בדיעבד וחולק על הט״ז בתרתי א׳ דבקיבולת נכו״ן להחמיר עכ״פ ולא מדינא ומש״ה כתב הטור בתקמ״ג נכון ולא מדינא דבקיבולת איירי ובנבנה באיסור ממש שכירי יום מדינא אסור לדור בו ולט״ז בנבנה ממ״ש באיסור מדינא שרי לכנוס בו דהני רבנן החמירו מדלא אסר בגמרא איסור כו׳ ונכון להחמיר לכל אדם כה״ג: ובמ״ע בקיבולת אף להחמיר לכתחלה א״צ דסייעי׳ בתיבנא הוה איסור ממ״ש לא מראית עין ועא״ר אות א׳ ואי״ה במ״א אות יו״ד אבאר עוד בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is proper.</b> See Taz. In siman 543, which is a scribal error and should read siman 544, Taz writes further on this subject. See Moed Katan 12a. The principal difficulty of Bach in siman 543 concerns Beit Yosef’s statement that the Tur did not write that only an important person is different because he ruled like the second version. On Chol HaMoed, work outside the techum is prohibited by law because of maris ayin. Certainly, even according to the first version, every person is prohibited, for the questioner accepted that construction within the techum is prohibited under contract even though the concern is only maris ayin. His difficulty was that outside the techum it is completely permitted. The Gemara answers according to the first version that an important person should be stringent even outside the techum. This refers to Shabbos or Yom Tov, but on Chol HaMoed, even according to the first version, every person is prohibited. Taz answers that Beit Yosef’s intent is indeed inferred from the fact that the Tur did not rule in Hilchos Shabbos that an important person is prohibited outside the techum. This shows that he follows the second version, relying on the ruling here that there is no distinction. Whenever the building was constructed through a maris ayin prohibition, all agree that it is merely proper to be stringent, not prohibited by law; where it was built permissibly, even an important person need not be stringent. The sugya may be explained as follows. According to the first version there are two stringencies: first, an important person should be stringent even where the act is entirely permitted; second, where the building was constructed in a manner prohibited because of maris ayin, everyone is prohibited by law. This follows from the Gemara’s question, “But it was outside the techum,” implying that construction within the techum would indeed be prohibited. According to the second version there are two leniencies: where the act is permitted, even an important person need not be stringent; and where there was a prohibition, it is only proper to be stringent, not legally prohibited, as indicated by the fact that the Sages did not enter the building because they were stringent. The Tur rules leniently in this Rabbinic matter according to the second version. This means that the second version is actually more stringent regarding an ordinary person where there was a prohibition, for according to the first version an ordinary person might be permitted and only an important person would need to be stringent. The Tur nevertheless follows the second version as primary. One may further explain that according to the second version, only assistance with straw, which is an actual prohibition, warrants stringency, whereas contracted work involving only maris ayin would require no stringency at all. According to this, the Tur’s statement, “and even if it was built in a prohibited manner, it is proper to be stringent,” means: not only where it was built under contract, where there is only maris ayin and the matter is merely proper rather than prohibited by law, but even where it was built through an actual prohibition, by day laborers or by assisting with straw, it is only proper and not legally prohibited. This is strained. One could also say that according to the second version there is no stringency at all where the concern is only maris ayin, and therefore Beit Yosef explains the Tur to mean that even where it was built in an actually prohibited manner, it is merely proper to be stringent after the fact. Bach writes here that Rabbeinu Tam is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon after the fact, and disagrees with Taz on two points. First, where work was contracted it is at least proper to be stringent, though not legally prohibited; this is why the Tur in siman 543 writes “proper” and not “prohibited,” since he discusses contracted work. If it was built through an actual prohibition, by day laborers, it is legally prohibited to occupy it. According to Taz, even where it was built through actual prohibited labor it is legally permitted to enter, because those Sages merely acted stringently, and the Gemara did not formulate an actual prohibition. It is nevertheless proper for every person to be stringent in such a case. Regarding maris ayin in contracted work, there is no need even for initial stringency, because assisting with straw was an actual prohibition and not merely maris ayin. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1; with Hashem’s help I will explain this further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 10.

<b>כאלו</b> עט״ז החמיר כשקובע לו זמן דאדעתא דישראל עביד לגמור תוך הזמן עא״ר אות י״ב מתיר כה״ג דאם לא יעשה בשבת יעשה בחול יע״ש וסברת הט״ז נראה נכון כיון שקובע לו שצריך לו הבגד חיך הזמן וידוע שא״א לגמור אם לא שעושה בשבת ג״כ הוה כמצווה לעשות בשבת ואף אם יאנוס העכו״ם עצמו וישב בלילה יוכל לגמור בחול מ״מ אדעתא דישראל עביד ועיין לבוש מ״ש בכל יום ובא״ר שם וי״ל דמפרש מחלוקת הר״מ והר״א ז״ל כה״ג דכתב הט״ז וכדכתיבנא: ועכו״ם המושכר לשנה לכבות דליקה יש להתיר עב״י כאן ומ״א של״ד אות כ״ח דבדליקה התירו: ואי״ה שם יבואר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>As though.</b> See Taz, who rules stringently where the Jew sets a deadline, because the non-Jew then works for the Jew’s purpose in order to finish within the allotted time. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 12, permits such a case, reasoning that if the non-Jew does not perform the work on Shabbos he will do it during the week. See there. Taz’s reasoning appears correct: when the Jew sets a deadline and needs the garment within that time, and it is known that it cannot be completed unless work is also done on Shabbos, it is as though he instructed the non-Jew to work on Shabbos. Even if the non-Jew could force himself to sit through the night and complete it during the week, he is still working for the Jew’s purpose. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>If.</b> See Taz. His statement that public work is prohibited even in the non-Jew’s house is like the ruling in seif 4 concerning a ship. Magen Avraham, seif katan 13, writes that the prohibition applies specifically in a public place, not in the non-Jew’s private house; see seif 3 and what I explained there. Beit Yosef, however, writes that for this reason it is prohibited in “his house,” meaning the Jew’s house; see there. Taz further writes that even regarding a Rabbinic prohibition the Sages enacted safeguards lest one come to a more severe prohibition. In Shabbos 154b, regarding glassware, they did not enact a safeguard for a minor loss. See Magen Avraham, siman 266, seif katan 14. The Mechaber omitted the case of a sack of glass according to his own approach, but Ran writes that the permission applies even in a Rabbinic prohibition; see there. See also siman 334, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. The Mechaber in siman 266, seif 1, rules without qualification, implying that it applies even nowadays; and in siman 334 he likewise presents the first view without qualification, permitting even a Rabbinic prohibition. This is difficult from siman 266, seif 9: why did he omit the case of a sack of glass? With Hashem’s help it will be explained there. Magen Avraham, seif katan 17, also agrees that where writing is not customary it is permitted. Levush implies that even where writing is not customary it is prohibited, and he also writes that dropping the burden is Rabbinic. Nevertheless, one could say that in a Rabbinic matter and the like, the established custom is lenient. Regarding the distinction between a major and minor loss, see Levush, who appears to agree. With Hashem’s help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
