<b> בצינעה</b> -  עיין במ"ב ועיין ביאור הגר"א שציין ע"ז ירושלמי הנ"ל דבתלוש בביתו של א"י דוקא ולכאורה משמע מיניה דהוא מחמיר אם עושה אותה בפרהסיא אף שהיא מלאכת תלוש שאין ידוע שהוא של ישראל אבל לישנא דשו"ע שכתב שאין הכל מכירים וכו' אבל אם היתה ידועה וכו' משמע דהעיקר תלוי אם המלאכה מפורסמת שהיא של הישראל וכ"כ הגר"א בעצמו בביאורו על דברי הג"ה שבסעיף ב' ע"ש והא דאיתא בירושלמי בבית א"י מותר היינו משום דכל כה"ג מסתמא לא מינכרא מלתא שהיא של ישראל ואפי' אם היא מלאכת פרהסיא שרי ואה"נ דאפילו אם הוא עושה אותה בר"ה שרי אם אינו מנכר לסתם ב"א שהיא של ישראל כמו שכתב ב"י שם בשם רבינו ירוחם [ואפשר עוד דבא הירושלמי שם לאפוקי אם הוא בבית ישראל] ונראה דכונת הגר"א ג"כ הכי אלא שחסר תיבת וכו' אחר תיבת בצינעה ור"ל על מה דכתב בשו"ע שאין הכל מכירים וציין המקור לזה מדמצריך הירושלמי בביתו של א"י והיינו משום דהתם לא מינכרא מלתא שהיא של ישראל וכן מדמתיר במחובר בעיר אחרת והיינו הכל מטעם זה וה"ה כל כה"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Privately.</b> See Mishnah Berurah and Biur HaGra, who cites the Yerushalmi above, which permits detached work specifically in the non-Jew's house. At first glance, this implies that he is stringent when it is done publicly, even though it is detached work and it is not known to belong to a Jew. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch, however, which says that not everyone recognizes it, but if it was known, indicates that the primary factor is whether the work is publicly identified as belonging to the Jew. The Gra himself writes likewise in his explanation of the gloss in seif 2. The Yerushalmi's permission when it is done in the non-Jew's house is because in such a case it is ordinarily not recognizable as the Jew's work. Even public work is permitted when ordinary observers cannot recognize it as belonging to a Jew. The same applies even if it is done in the public domain, as Beit Yosef writes there in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. It is also possible that the Yerushalmi mentions the non-Jew's house merely to exclude the Jew's house. It appears that this too is the Gra's intent, except that the word “etc.” is missing after “privately.” He refers to the Shulchan Aruch's statement that not everyone recognizes it, and cites the Yerushalmi's requirement that it be done in the non-Jew's house because there it is not recognizable as the Jew's work. The same is shown by the permission for attached work in another city. All these cases depend upon the same principle.

דאותו בעה"ב דרכו לשכור לפעמים בעצמו את השכירים שתחתיו לשכירי יום ולכך לא נקרא תו שם קבלנות על הבנין אבל אם הדרך הוא תמיד שאין בעה"ב יודע כלל מכל עסק רק שהוא שוכר האדריכל והאדריכל בעצמו דרכו לשכור <b>Or to harvest his field for him.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, citing Taz, that even where the townspeople customarily hire workers as contractors, it is still forbidden, because observers may suspect them of being day laborers, which is also common. Peri Megadim understands that even if most of the town hires by contract, it remains forbidden so long as day labor is also common. In my humble opinion, this may be challenged from the Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah. Rabbeinu Tam permitted a Jew to hire a craftsman to build a house by contract. The Rosh objected that the statement that a sharecropper works for himself in a field is no proof regarding building contracts, because fields are ordinarily worked only through sharecropping or tenancy, whereas most houses are built by day laborers, so an observer assumes they are day laborers. This implies that if most of the town builds by contract, there is no need to be stringent even regarding houses. The Tosafos quoted by Taz are the words of Ri against Rabbeinu Tam, and the Rosh's statement likewise follows Ri. Old Tosafos to Shabbos 18a, however, supports Taz, holding that even where contracting is common it is forbidden if day labor is also common. Peri Megadim writes that according to Ran, who forbids even contracted field work because it appears like day labor, it is forbidden even when observers know that he is a contractor, since unless the non-Jew shares in the profit, people may come to permit actual day labor. According to that approach, even if the matter is publicized and the local custom is exclusively contracting, it remains forbidden. This is stricter than the Tosafos approach. According to the Shulchan Aruch's reason, however, that an observer does not know a price was fixed and assumes the Jew hired the non-Jew to work on Shabbos, it would appear permitted where it is publicized that this person hired by contract, or where the local custom is to hire workers by contract. Peri Megadim inclines toward leniency in that case. In my view, this requires much study, because Ran is not alone. Maharam in Tosafos to Avodah Zarah 21b holds that the distinction between sharecropping and contracting is not merely that houses are usually built by day labor, but that a sharecropper owns a share in the actual produce and resembles a partner, whereas a contractor receives money and therefore appears to be the Jew's hired worker. According to this, it is forbidden even where contracting is public knowledge. The same implication appears in Baal HaMaor, Hagahos Semak, Kol Bo, Hagahos Ashri, and Sefer HaTerumah. All make the essential distinction depend upon whether the non-Jew has a share in the object itself. Therefore, even if the town works only by contract, it remains forbidden according to Ran. This also explains why the Gemara in Moed Katan rules without qualification that contractors working within the techum are forbidden. On the other hand, Rambam's wording, adopted by the Shulchan Aruch, explains that an observer does not know a price was fixed. This implies that where most of the town hires by contract, even house construction may be permitted, as I demonstrated above at the beginning of siman 243 from Rambam himself, where a majority is required. Rabbi Akiva Eiger cites Responsa Mahari HaLevi as agreeing with the stringent view, though he himself permits where the local custom is contracting. Regarding a field, if it is publicized that this person hires by contract, or that this is the local custom, one should not protest against those who are lenient. Several Rishonim permit contracted field work because observers may assume sharecropping, and although we follow the stringent ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, their view may be combined with these circumstances. Regarding house construction, however, Rabbeinu Tam's lenient view is isolated, and Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that he himself later retracted it. It therefore requires study whether publicity suffices. I later found that Nishmas Adam, rule 3, is likewise stringent, and the practical ruling therefore requires study. What Mishnah Berurah writes, that it is considered contracting only when the entire building is contracted, but not when only the architect is the contractor and the workers beneath him are sometimes day laborers, follows the later authorities. It appears that this means where the owner himself sometimes hires the workers beneath the architect as day laborers, so the building can no longer be characterized as a contracted project. If, however, the owner has no involvement in the work and hires only the architect, while the architect himself sometimes hires day laborers beneath him, the project is still considered contracting.

<b> לא ישקעם בבנין</b> -  הנה בטור כתב דאסור לשקעם בבנין והא דלענין בית כתב הטור בסימן תקמ"ג וממנו העתיק המחבר בס"ג דרק נכון להחמיר תירץ בתו"ש דהכא שרוצה לשקעם בבנין חשוב כלכתחילה וממ"א בסק"י משמע דג"ז חשוב כדיעבד דיש הפסד עי"ז ולענ"ד נראה דבכונה שינה המחבר מלשון הטור ולא כתב בלשון אסור וכונתו דרק נכון להחמיר בזה ולא כדעת הטור וכן מוכח מהגר"א ביאורו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should not set them into the building.</b> Tur writes that it is forbidden to set them into the building. Regarding a house, however, Tur writes in siman 543, and the Mechaber copies in seif 3, only that it is proper to be stringent. Tosefes Shabbos answers that here, where he wishes to set the stones into the building, it is treated as an initial act. Magen Avraham, seif katan 10, implies that even this is considered after the fact because loss would result. In my humble opinion, the Mechaber deliberately departed from Tur's wording and did not write “forbidden.” His intent is that it is merely proper to be stringent, unlike Tur's position. This is also evident from Biur HaGra.

<b> והא"י עשה בעל כרחו</b> -  עיין במ"ב סוף ס"ק כ"ב במה שהבאנו בשם הא"ר והסברנו טעמו דה"ל להתנות מתחלה ולכאורה יש להביא ראיה מע"ג כ"ב דמשמע שם דרק לרשב"א אמרינן ליה לא"י וציית אבל לרשב"ג א"י לא ציית ולכך אוסר שם לשכור לו מרחצו בתחלה שבודאי לא ישמע לו אח"כ כשיאמר לו שלא יחממנו בשבת וא"כ ה"נ לענין קיבולת פשע במה שלא התנה מתחלה עמו דלבסוף לא יציית וכן משמע מרש"י מו"ק י"ב ע"א דפירש דמקבלי קיבולת בתוך התחום אסור היינו שאסור ליתנו לו בקיבולת כי עבדי ליה בשבתא אלמא דלא סמכינן על מה שימחנו אח"כ אם לא שיתנה עמו מתחלה וזהו גם לרש"י בודאי שרי זהו ביארנו סברת א"ר ומ"מ לדינא צ"ע דמהרמב"ם [וכן מהשו"ע שהעתיקו דבריו בס"א] משמע דקיבולת מותר למסור לו לכתחלה אלא דכשיגיע יום שבת ימחנו ולא יניחנו לעשות מלאכה ובעניני מרחץ גם הרמב"ם כתב דאסור להשכיר לו כלישנא דגמרא וע"כ דצריך לחלק דלענין מרחץ שהוא שכור לא"י לגמרי ס"ל לרשב"ג דלא יציית לנו לשבות ביום השבת משא"כ בקיבולת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the non-Jew did it against his will.</b> See Mishnah Berurah at the end of seif katan 22, where we cited Eliyah Rabbah and explained that the Jew should have stipulated this initially. There is apparent proof from Avodah Zarah 22a. The Gemara implies that only according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar can one instruct the non-Jew and expect compliance, whereas according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel the non-Jew will not comply. Therefore, it is forbidden initially to rent him a bathhouse, because he certainly will not listen later when told not to heat it on Shabbos. Likewise regarding contracting, the Jew was negligent by failing to stipulate initially, because the non-Jew will not comply afterward. This also appears from Rashi to Moed Katan 12a, who explains that contracted work within the techum is forbidden, meaning that one may not give it to him by contract when he will perform it on Shabbos. We do not rely upon protesting afterward unless a stipulation was made initially. This explains Eliyah Rabbah's reasoning. Nevertheless, the practical law requires study. Rambam, and the Shulchan Aruch that copies him in seif 1, imply that contracted work may be given initially, but when Shabbos arrives one must protest and not allow the work. Rambam also rules that a bathhouse may not be rented, following the Gemara's language. We must therefore distinguish between a bathhouse, which is entirely rented to the non-Jew and concerning which Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that he will not listen to a demand to cease on Shabbos, and ordinary contracted work.

<b> דבמקום פסידא וכו'</b> -  הקשה מ"א דהלא משום שכר שבת לא מקילינן אפילו במקום פסידא כדמוכח בסימן רמ"ג בתנור ומרחץ דשם איכא לפעמים פסידא יותר ממכס ע"ש ותירץ דמ"מ באותו היום יש פסידא במכס דחשבינן לכל יום בפ"ע ור"ל משא"כ במרחץ דשם הוא רק מניעת הריוח וע"ש עוד מה שתירץ לבסוף והעתקנוהו במ"ב ומשמע דבעצם הענין לא פסיקא ליה להמ"א לפי המסקנא שלו אם מקילינן כלל משום פסידא לענין שכר שבת אבל הט"ז בסימן רמ"ה סק"ו סובר בהדיא דבמכס שהוא עסק גדול [וכן גבי מטבע] לא אסרינן משום שכר שבת דאדם בהול על ממונו ואי לא שרית ליה אתי לידי איסור יותר גדול וכן דעת התוספות שבת ג"כ דבהפסד גדול מקילינן לענין שכר שבת ג"כ ועיין בביאור הגר"א דמשמע מיניה דמה שכתב הרמ"א דבמקום פסידא כה"ג וכו' הוא ג"כ תירוץ מה שלא חששו כאן לשכר שבת ומשמע מזה דהוא סובר ג"כ כהט"ז ותוספת שבת הנ"ל דיש בזה שכר שבת אך שהתירו משום פסידא [ומה שכתב המגן אברהם דזה דומה למי שיודע שיביאו לו סחורה וכו' עיין בתו"ש שהקשה דבעניננו אין יכול הישראל להקנות לו דהוי דשלב"ל ונראה דזה היה סברת הגר"א ג"כ ומה שיישבו הפמ"ג קצת היה נראה לו לדוחק] נמצא לפ"ז דהט"ז והתו"ש והגר"א כולם בחדא שיטתא קיימי וכן משמע בלבוש ועיין בא"ר שגם בתשובת רי"ט צהלון משמע כן וע"כ יש להקל בזה אך לדברי כולם משמע דבשכיר יום אין להקל בשום גוונא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In a case of loss, etc.</b> Magen Avraham asks that Shabbos wages are not permitted even in a case of loss, as is evident in siman 243 concerning an oven and bathhouse, where the loss may sometimes exceed that of customs revenue. He answers that regarding customs there is a loss on that very day, since each day is assessed separately, unlike a bathhouse where it is merely lost profit. See there for his further answer, copied in Mishnah Berurah. His conclusion implies that Magen Avraham was not certain whether actual Shabbos wages are ever permitted because of loss. Taz in siman 245, seif katan 6, explicitly holds that for customs, which is a major enterprise, and likewise for coinage, Shabbos wages are not prohibited. A person is anxious over his money, and if it is not permitted he may come to a greater prohibition. Tosefes Shabbos likewise holds that a major loss permits Shabbos wages. Biur HaGra implies that Rama's phrase “in a case of loss such as this” also explains why Shabbos wages were not a concern, indicating agreement with Taz and Tosefes Shabbos that Shabbos wages exist here but were permitted because of loss. Magen Avraham compares it to a case where one knows that non-Jews will bring merchandise on Shabbos and he sells it before Shabbos to another non-Jew who will receive it then. Tosefes Shabbos objects that here the Jew cannot transfer ownership because the subject matter does not yet exist. This appears also to have been the Gra's reasoning. Peri Megadim's resolution is somewhat forced. Thus Taz, Tosefes Shabbos, and the Gra follow one approach, and Levush likewise appears to agree. Eliyah Rabbah notes that Responsa Maharit Tzahalon also implies this. One may therefore be lenient, but all agree that no leniency exists for an actual day laborer.
