**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2: He dilutes it, etc.</b> In his country, etc. This is intended to demonstrate the praise of Eretz Yisrael, whose wine is so strong that it requires dilution with water. Although the person reciting the blessing is outside Eretz Yisrael, since he waits to dilute it until Bircas HaAretz, it is evident that he does so to demonstrate the praise of Eretz Yisrael. So writes Beit Yosef. There is also support for that custom in the wording of Rambam; see Beit Yosef. He further writes that one must dilute it immediately upon beginning Bircas HaAretz, unlike those who err and do not dilute it until the end of that blessing. Accordingly, at the beginning of Bircas HaMazon one should not fill the cup completely to its brim. See there. Seif katan 3: For the sake of the blessing, etc. For “alive” is one of the ten matters stated regarding a cup of blessing, as taught in Berachos 51a, and means undiluted wine, called fresko. The Mechaber’s statement in seif 3 that the cup must be whole follows those who explain “alive” as referring to the vessel, meaning that it must be whole, since, as we say elsewhere regarding vessels, their breaking is their death. Thus, a whole vessel is called alive.

<b>Seif katan 4: Full, etc.</b> With a crown around the inside of the cup. Meaning, they explain the “adornment” mentioned among the ten matters stated regarding a cup of blessing to mean that it should not be completely full, leaving a crown-like rim within the cup. Magen Avraham rejects this, for on the contrary, the cup must be full to its brim, and fullness itself is one of the ten matters. “Adornment” therefore refers to something else, as explained in the Gemara. Taz, seif katan 4, further rejects this because the halachah is that adornment is not required. Taz writes that those who nevertheless leave space do so lest the cup spill and become degraded, as stated at the beginning of siman 171 that one may not pass a full cup over food for this reason.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 6: Beneath, etc.</b> Berachos 51a states: Rabbi Chanan bar Abba said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: The early authorities asked whether the left hand may assist the right. Rabbi Asi said: Since the early authorities did not resolve it, we should act stringently. Bach writes that Shibolei HaLeket implies that they prohibited only holding the cup with the left hand from the opposite side, just as the right hand holds it from one side. But if one does not hold the cup at mid-height with the left hand, and merely places the left beneath the cup, so that the left is beneath the right and assists it, this assistance has no substance and is permitted. The Rama, however, understood that it is permitted only to place the left hand beneath the right hand, but placing it beneath the cup is forbidden because one is then holding the cup with the left. Bach rejects this interpretation.

<b>According to what is written at the beginning of siman 65, etc.</b> This refers to Krias Shema. Tosafos there hold that Krias Shema and shofar follow the same rule. This compelled them to distinguish between an ordinary delay and a delay caused by circumstances beyond one’s control. They were troubled that regarding shofar the Gemara says that even a delay of nine hours is not an interruption, while regarding prayer it says that if one delayed long enough to complete the entire prayer he returns to the beginning. Tosafos hold that prayer, Krias Shema, and shofar follow one rule. They therefore distinguish that a delay caused by circumstances beyond one’s control constitutes an interruption in all three. Accordingly, the statement that one returns to the beginning after delaying long enough to complete the whole refers to a delay caused by circumstances beyond one’s control. Since according to them even regarding shofar such a delay requires returning, and the present law is derived from shofar, the same applies here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the end of the passage beginning “To where does he return,” etc.</b> The Gemara asks where he returns. Rav Zevid in the name of Abaye says that he returns to the beginning, while the Sages say that he returns to the place where he stopped, and the halachah follows the Sages. Rashi explains that this concerns one who interrupts his meal for two others, as discussed below in siman 200: three ate together, two interrupt their meal to recite Bircas HaMazon with zimmun, while the third still wishes to eat. The third must stop eating until the end of the zimmun and then resumes his meal. We rule like Rabbi Sheshes that the zimmun extends through Bircas HaZan and the beginning of Nodeh Lecha. The question is where the third begins Bircas HaMazon after finishing his meal. Rav Zevid says from the beginning of HaZan; the Sages say from where he stopped, namely Nodeh Lecha. Tosafos object that according to the Sages, since he ate after hearing Bircas HaZan from the other two, he would lack Bircas HaZan for what he ate afterward. Tosafos therefore explain that the case concerns three who ate and one went to the marketplace, and they call him and recite zimmun with him, as explained below in siman 194, seif 2. He must remain with the other two until after Bircas HaZimun. If he then leaves and later wishes to recite Bircas HaMazon for what he ate before being called, the Sages properly rule that he begins with Nodeh Lecha, since he fulfilled Bircas HaZan by hearing it from the other two. Tosafos conclude that he fulfilled it because he did not speak in the interim. This implies that if he spoke, even the Sages agree that he returns to the beginning, supporting Magen Avraham’s ruling that intentional speech requires returning to the beginning.
