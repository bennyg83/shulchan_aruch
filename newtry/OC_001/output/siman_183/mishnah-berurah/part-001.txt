(25) Even. Speech in the interim does not invalidate the blessings after the fact. Know that Magen Avraham inclines to rule that this applies only to inadvertent speech. If one spoke intentionally, even briefly and even between one blessing and the next, he returns to the beginning of Bircas HaMazon, as regarding prayer in siman 114, seif 7. Eliyah Rabbah, however, leaves this ruling unresolved. We ruled above in the Mishnah Berurah to siman 65 that after the fact one should not repeat even if he spoke intentionally; see Biur Halachah there. Initially, however, one must be very careful. If he paused between blessings for the time needed to recite all of Bircas HaMazon from beginning to end, even without speaking, some say that he must return to the beginning, provided the delay resulted from circumstances beyond his control, such as needing to relieve himself or the place being unclean, as explained in siman 65 regarding Krias Shema. See Biur Halachah, where we wrote that in practice it is not clear that he should repeat because of such a delay between blessings. If such a delay occurred in the middle of a blessing, however, he must return to the beginning of that blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
(26) They did not fulfill their obligation. If the person reciting the blessing has not yet completed that blessing, the diners resume reciting for themselves from the point at which they ceased hearing him. If he already completed the blessing and they had intended to fulfill their obligation by hearing him, they must return to the beginning of that blessing and recite it themselves, because omitting its middle is equivalent to not reciting it at all. This applies specifically if their conversation caused them to miss words essential to the blessing, such as covenant and Torah and similar matters that are indispensable, as explained in siman 187. If they missed only nonessential wording, they fulfilled their obligation after the fact and need not repeat. All the more so if they missed nothing, such as where the person reciting the blessing himself spoke in the middle and they spoke with him. Although they certainly acted improperly, after the fact they fulfilled their obligation and need not repeat.

(27) It is proper. Strictly speaking, it would be preferable for the diners to hear all of Bircas HaMazon from the leader of zimmun, who would discharge their obligation, and for them not to recite it themselves. Because it is unfortunately common for diners to lose concentration and fail to attend to his words, with the result that they entirely lack Bircas HaMazon and actively neglect a positive Torah commandment, it is preferable nowadays that they quietly recite every word together with him. In this manner they bless together, and it is considered Bircas HaZimun, fulfilling the verse, “Declare Hashem’s greatness with me, and let us exalt His Name together,” from which Bircas HaZimun is derived, as explained below in siman 192.

**** ENGLISH ****
(28) Every blessing. At the very least, they must be careful to recite the first blessing quietly together with him. Otherwise, according to many poskim it is not considered zimmun at all, as explained below in siman 200. Accordingly, the widespread practice in which, after reciting “Blessed is He from Whose food we have eaten,” each person recites Bircas HaMazon aloud by himself is improper. The leader must recite at least the first blessing aloud so the diners hear him, while they recite it quietly word for word with him. Only at the conclusion should they finish slightly before him so they can answer amen, as Rama writes. See Magen Avraham, who inclines to rule like Tashbetz that they must remain silent, listen, and intend to fulfill their obligation through the leader until HaZan Es HaKol, because until there is Bircas HaZimun. From that point onward they may recite quietly with him. We do not follow this practice. Nevertheless, where one knows that the diners will concentrate on his words, it is proper and upright to follow Tashbetz, provided he first informs them to intend to fulfill their obligation and he likewise intends to discharge them. This applies only when all listeners understand Lashon HaKodesh. Otherwise, it is certainly preferable for them to recite all of Bircas HaMazon themselves rather than attempt to fulfill their obligation through the leader. Eliyah Rabbah.
