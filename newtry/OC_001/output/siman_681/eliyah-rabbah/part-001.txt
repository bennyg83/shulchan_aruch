ובמרדכי פרק במה מדליקים כתב ואף על גב דתדיר קודם והוה ליה להקדים ההיא דראש חודש מכל מקום פרסום הנס עדיף אף לקדם עד כאן לשונו. דבר זה אין לו ביאור רחב, וכבר כתב שם בגליון דבר זה אינו בשני נוסחאות של המרדכי ועל כן אין ללמוד שום דבר מדברים אלו עד כאן לשון הט"ז. ובמחילה מכבודו לא דק כלל שהרי קודם זה ממש נמי מבואר להדיא במרדכי דפרסום נס עדיף אף לקדם שהרי כתב זה לשונו, וקורין בתורה דראש חודש קודם ואחר שמחקו הט"ז ומהר"ם מטיקטין, וגם בתשובת רמ"א סימן ל"ח אות ב' האריך בפירושו ובשיירי כנסת הגדולה סימן תרפ"ד תמה עליו ודחה פירושו. ולעניות דעתי יש לו ביאור רחב, דמה שכתב אף על גב דתדיר קודם והוה ליה לאקדומי ההוא דראש חודש היינו דמקשה עצמו למה לי טעם משום הפטרה תיפוק ליה משום תדיר כתירוץ ראשון דתוס', ותירץ מכל מקום פרסומי ניסא עדיף אף לקדם, והיינו נגד תירוץ ראשון דתוס' הנזכר לעיל ואם כן היה מן וראב"ד נקט רבותא דאף דמדליקים נרות למגילה ואחר כך מבדילין, מכל מקום לא בירכו עליהם, עד כאן. והנה נפל טעות גדול בהבנת דברי ראב"ד משום שבירך על נר חנוכה, כי מצאתי בכלבו דף מ"ד זה לשונו, והראב"ד כתב שאין כתבתי אלא שאין למחות ביד הנוהגין כן, ועתה ישבתי תודה לאל הכל לכן אומר אני שאין לשנות כלל מנהג אפילו בבית:

<b>Levush: In order to delay, etc.</b> So wrote Terumas HaDeshen, and his proof is from the whole order of Havdalah, where we delay the blessing of Havdalah and the other blessings, and from the order of wine, kiddush, lamp, Havdalah, and time. Rabbi David Abudarham wrote that one recites Havdalah and afterward kindles. Our teacher Rabbi Loew, of blessed memory, instituted this here in Prague, and he wrote in the Ner Mitzvah that he authored that kindling is different, for it is impossible unless he recited Havdalah in prayer, and sometimes he did not recite Havdalah and will commit a transgression; and the words seem correct. This is from Malbushei Yom Tov. But from the day I began to understand, I have seen no custom here in Prague other than that they kindle and afterward recite Havdalah. Therefore I took to heart that the aforementioned reason may be rejected, for the whole world is already accustomed throughout the year to kindle the lamps given to them by the shamash after the order of Kedushah. And if one forgot Atah Chonantanu, he says Blessed is the One Who separates, etc. If so, Motzaei Shabbos Chanukah is no worse, especially in the synagogue, where the chazan kindles the Chanukah lamp, and the chazan is more careful in his prayer not to forget Atah Chonantanu. Magen Avraham wrote that one who forgot Atah should recite Havdalah and afterward kindle, but this is not clear. He also did not see in Ner Mitzvah there, where it wrote about this that we have not found that one should sometimes act this way. Now Turei Zahav wrote at great length with a new reason to put Havdalah first, from what Tosafos wrote in chapter Bameh Madlikin, as follows: the reason we first read the Rosh Chodesh portion before that of Chanukah is that in Torah reading one can do both, what is frequent and publicizing the miracle. Until here. If so, the same applies regarding the Chanukah lamp and Havdalah: we have clear proof to put the frequent first, since it is possible to fulfill both. Until here are his words. I am astonished at him, for Tosafos there wrote two further answers: one, that in Torah reading the publicizing of the miracle is not recognizable; second, in order to read the haftarah from the subject with which he concluded, the lamps of Zechariah. If so, according to these two answers the opposite is explicit, that publicizing the miracle comes before what is frequent, even when it is possible to fulfill both. Thus Terumas HaDeshen and the authorities who say that the Chanukah lamp precedes Havdalah hold like the last two answers of Tosafos. Turei Zahav further wrote as follows: Mordechai in chapter Bameh Madlikin wrote, Although what is frequent comes first and he should have put that of Rosh Chodesh first, nevertheless publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. Until here. This matter does not have a broad explanation, and it has already been written there in the margin that this text is not in two versions of Mordechai, and therefore nothing should be learned from these words. Until here are the words of Turei Zahav. With pardon to his honor, he was not precise at all, for immediately before this it is also explicitly explained in Mordechai that publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. He wrote as follows: and we read in the Torah that of Rosh Chodesh first and afterward that of Chanukah, so that the last reader will read the haftarah from the lamps of Zechariah, in the subject with which he concluded. Until here. This implies that if not for the reason of the haftarah, Chanukah would come first because publicizing the miracle is preferable to what is frequent, even though both are fulfilled. With this Hashem illuminated my eyes in the words of the aforementioned Mordechai, which Turei Zahav and Rabbi Menachem of Tiktin deleted. Also in Responsa Rema, siman 38, paragraph 2, he wrote at length in explaining it, and in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 684, he wondered about him and rejected his explanation. But in my humble opinion it has a broad explanation: what Mordechai wrote, Although what is frequent comes first and he should have put that of Rosh Chodesh first, means that he is asking himself: why do I need the reason of the haftarah? Let it derive from the rule of frequency, like the first answer of Tosafos. He answered: nevertheless, publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. This is against the aforementioned first answer of Tosafos, and if so it would have been proper to read Chanukah first, because it publicizes the miracle; therefore the reason of the haftarah was needed. The rule that emerges is that there is clear proof from Mordechai to put the Chanukah lamp first. Turei Zahav further wrote: I saw in Shiltei Giborim that some kindle before Havdalah, and Raavad wrote that it is not proper to do so because of the blessing of the lights of fire. Thus even the blessing over fire did not seem proper to him to precede, for there is no logic that one should first recite a blessing over a mitzvah dependent on fire and afterward bless over its original creation; all the more so regarding the blessing of Havdalah, how can he recite Havdalah when he has already interrupted Shabbos with his blessing? Raavad mentioned a greater novelty, that even though we kindle lamps for the Megillah and afterward recite Havdalah, nevertheless they did not bless over them. Until here. Now a great error fell into the understanding of Raavad's words, as if the problem were because he blessed over the Chanukah lamp. For I found in Kol Bo, page 44, as follows: Raavad wrote that it is not proper to do so, and likewise regarding the Megillah one should not read until after Havdalah, for it is not proper to bless over light after one has filled his eyes with it. Until here. It is thus explicit that he holds Megillah and the Chanukah lamp to be equal. What he wrote because of the blessing of the lights of fire means that one should not bless over fire after he has filled his eyes with it. According to this, since we hold regarding the Megillah that it is read before Havdalah, the same applies to the Chanukah lamp. According to this, it seems to me that there is no proof either from Rabbi David Abudarham, cited by Malbushei Yom Tov and Mateh Moshe, for he may likewise hold regarding the Megillah that Havdalah is recited first. Mateh Moshe wondered: how can we delay Havdalah because of the departure of the day, when by kindling the Chanukah lamp one shows that it is weekday? It seems to me that this is not difficult at all, for every Motzaei Shabbos we recite Borei Me'orei Ha'eish over fire that was lit before Havdalah, and the practice is always to kindle lamps before Havdalah. Rather, certainly, nevertheless, there is still a trace of the sanctity of the Shabbos soul until Havdalah over the cup. Now in Eliyahu Zuta I brought my view to recite Havdalah at home first, because I saw the wonder of Ner Mitzvah, Mateh Moshe, and Malbushei Yom Tov. Nevertheless, I myself did not practice this way, and I wrote only that one should not protest against those who practice so. Now, thank God, I have settled everything; therefore I say that one should not change the custom at all, even at home.

[ב] <b>[לבוש] ויש אומרים בביתו נר חנוכה וכו'.</b> ורמ"א כתב דביתו כל שכן הוא ונראה פירושו דכיון שכבר הבדיל בבית הכנסת אין איסור כלל להדליק, ולפי זה אין מקור ליש אומרים הנזכרים לעיל אף דתרומת הדשן נקט בית הכנסת יש לומר דרבותא נקט בית הכנסת, וכן הכלבו ובשיירי כנסת הגדולה תמה יותר על הלבוש דאיך כתב יש אומרים שהוא תרומת הדשן הא סברא ראשונה נמי הוא סברת תרומת הדשן, דלר' דוד אבודרהם לעולם בתחילה מבדילין ולא ראה במנהגים שכתב להדיא מדליקין קודם בין בבית הכנסת בין בביתו, ועוד מצאתי במהרי"ל שכתב מדליקין נר חנוכה ואחר כך מבדיל ואפילו בבית, עד כאן, ואם כן פשוט שסברא ראשונה הוא ממהרי"ל שכתב מדליקין. והשתא נמי ניחא דמייתי יש אומרים דלמדו מתרומת הדשן וכלבו דנקט בית הכנסת דוקא קאמרי דהא בית רבותא יותר במהרי"ל, ודוחק לומר דפליגי הפוסקים איזה רבותא יותר, גם תיקשי מנא לן כיון שאין מפורש מתרומת הדשן וכלבו רק בבדק הבית, ומזה קשיא על רמ"א שכתב וכל שכן וכו'. גם סברא שכתבתי משום היתר לדלוק יש לדחות דכבר מותר הבדלה בתפילה, או כשאומר המבדיל וכו' כדפירשתי בריש סימן זה, ועיין באגור סימן תתרמ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Levush: And some say that in his home, the Chanukah lamp, etc.</b> Rema wrote that in one's home it is all the more so, and it seems that his explanation is that since he already recited Havdalah in the synagogue, there is no prohibition at all to kindle. According to this, there is no source for the aforementioned some say, even though Terumas HaDeshen mentioned the synagogue, for one may say that he mentioned the synagogue as a novelty. So too Kol Bo. In Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah he wondered even more about Levush: how did he write some say, meaning Terumas HaDeshen, when the first view is also the view of Terumas HaDeshen? For according to Rabbi David Abudarham, one always recites Havdalah first. He did not see in Minhagim, where it is written explicitly that they kindle first, both in the synagogue and in his home. I further found in Maharil that he wrote: one kindles the Chanukah lamp and afterward recites Havdalah, even at home. Until here. If so, it is clear that the first view is from Maharil, who wrote that they kindle. Now it is also understandable that he brings some say, for they learned from Terumas HaDeshen and Kol Bo, who specifically mentioned the synagogue, that they speak only of the synagogue; after all, home is a greater novelty according to Maharil. It is forced to say that the authorities disagree about which case is the greater novelty. It is also difficult: from where do we know this, since it is explicit from Terumas HaDeshen and Kol Bo only regarding Bedek Habayis? From this there is a difficulty on Rema, who wrote and all the more so, etc. Also the reasoning that I wrote, that it is because it is permitted to kindle, may be rejected, for it is already permitted by Havdalah in prayer, or when he says Blessed is the One Who separates, etc., as I explained at the beginning of this siman. See Agur, siman 1041.
