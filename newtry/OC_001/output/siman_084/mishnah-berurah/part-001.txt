(3) And some naked — some say that even if now all are dressed or there is no person at all, nevertheless since the building is designated for being there naked and dressed, it has some law of bathhouse; and some are lenient in this. According to their words, in a room where the bath attendant dwells and some people also stand there naked, it is permitted to speak words of Torah and Kiddush and prayer when there is no person there. And all this is regarding the middle room; but the inner room, where all stand naked and bathe there, is more repulsive, and according to all its law is like a privy — forbidden even when there is no person there, and even if that bathhouse's custom is to bathe only in summer and in winter it is closed — nevertheless then too all bathhouse laws apply to it, for its name has not yet been uprooted from it. But if they made a physical change to it — e.g. they removed the oven there and the like and made it a dwelling — it is permitted to place a mezuzah at its entrance and bless on it and recite and pray there; and the same law applies to a privy.

(4) But not, etc. — and likewise all blessings and words of Torah are forbidden there. And it is implied from Taz's words that after the fact, if one blessed there, he does not return and bless; and likewise for Keriat Shema he does not return and read, even if a naked person was there in the middle room at the time of reading — provided he did not stand opposite him at the time of reading, or e.g. he turned his face and body from him — for otherwise he returns and reads because he read opposite nakedness [Peri Megadim]. Regarding an immersion room, Taz concludes that all its law is like the middle room of a bathhouse, and one should not bless there any blessing except the immersion blessing, which is necessary there; and after the fact it is considered [valid]. And all this is when the one blessing is not naked and also does not stand opposite naked people at the time of the blessing — for then it is forbidden on account of nakedness unless he turned his face and body from them; and the same applies when a woman blesses in the immersion room. Peri Megadim wrote it is better she bless in the water [and see above siman 74 s.k. 16, who wrote it is good that she muddy the water with her feet so her limbs are not visible in the water]. The reason is that there in the place of water it is more reasonable it is not considered like the middle room of a bathhouse; and it is plain that there too at the time of blessing she must turn her face and body from naked women standing in the immersion room, as proved above siman 79 s.k. 2 — see there. And behold according to Taz's view, even when there is no naked person there, one should not bless there another blessing according to the stringent view above regarding the middle room; but from Magen Avraham above siman 45 it is implied to be lenient, that there is no filth there; and likewise wrote Birkhei Yosef. And behold in a mikveh into which hot water is poured, one may say there is steam; and nevertheless one may say there is not so much filth and steam as in a bathhouse. And these mikva'ot standing in the bathhouse itself are certainly in the bathhouse — forbidden to bless there the immersion blessing; and even in the water it requires study, for one may say the bathhouse steam is greater and in water too it is forbidden [Peri Megadim; and see what he wrote further on this in siman 45 in Eshel Avraham]:

(6) Even greeting with Shalom is forbidden — for Shalom is the name of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it says: and he called it to the Lord Shalom.
