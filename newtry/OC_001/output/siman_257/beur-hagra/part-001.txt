<b>ס"ד ונתגלה משחשיכה.</b> כ"כ סמ"ג וסמ"ק וסה"ת ומ"ש ונתגלה ל"ד דהא אף לכתחלה מותר כמ"ש למטה אלא דאף בכה"ג דוקא משחשיכה והביא סמ"ג ירושלמי דדוקא משחשיכה וז"ל הירושלמי ספ"ד נתגלה מבע"י מכסהו מבע"י נתגלה משחשיכה מכסהו משחשיכה נתגלה מבע"י וקידש עליו היום תני אין טומנין משחשיכה ובגמרא דילן ספ"ב דשבת וא"ד מ"מ אמרו אין טומנין כו' וגריס שם הרי"ף להיפך וכן הרמב"ם ומפרש שמא ירתיח שתרתח הקדירה ויצטרך לגלותה ולחזור ולכסותה ובדבר המוסיף ודאי אסור אף שהיה מכוסה מבע"י ופריך א"ה בה"ש נמי כו' ופי' הרמב"ם דבה"ש מותר אפי' בדבר המוסיף הבל והקשה עליו הראב"ד דבה"ש יהיה קל מבע"י ע"כ פי' הר"ן דה"ק דבה"ש ליתסר אפילו בדבר שאינו מוסיף הבל מה"ט שמא ירתיח ויגלה בה"ש כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 4: And it became uncovered after nightfall.</b> So wrote the Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, Sefer Mitzvos Katan, and Sefer HaTerumah. The wording 'and it became uncovered' is not exact, for even initially it is permitted, as stated below. Rather, even in such a case it is permitted specifically when it became uncovered after nightfall. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol cites the Yerushalmi, which states that this applies specifically after nightfall. Its wording at the end of chapter 4 is: 'If it became uncovered while it was still day, he may cover it while it is still day. If it became uncovered after nightfall, he may cover it after nightfall. If it became uncovered while it was still day and the day became sanctified upon him, it was taught: one may not insulate after nightfall.' In our Gemara, at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, there are those who say: 'Nevertheless, they said that one may not insulate,' etc. The Rif reads the passage in the reverse order, and so does the Rambam, explaining that the concern is lest it boil, meaning that the pot may come to a boil and he will need to uncover it and then cover it again. With something that adds heat, it is certainly forbidden even if it was covered before Shabbos. The Gemara asks: if so, it should also be forbidden during bein hashmashos, etc. The Rambam explains that during bein hashmashos it is permitted even with something that adds heat. The Ra'avad objected that bein hashmashos should not be more lenient than before Shabbos. Therefore, the Ran explains that the Gemara means: during bein hashmashos it should be forbidden even with something that does not add heat, because of the concern that it may boil and he may uncover it during bein hashmashos, etc.

'What is different': 'This is difficult,' etc. Rabbeinu Yitzchak, however, wrote that the incident there concerned insulation. The proof is that the Gemara brings the case of one hot-water urn atop another, which concerns insulation, as is evident from its inclusion among the laws of insulation. The Tosefta there explicitly teaches: 'One may insulate a hot-water urn atop another hot-water urn, and a pot atop another pot.' If the incident there concerned mere placement, the Gemara should have cited the Baraisa: 'A person may bring a jug of water and place it opposite the fire,' etc. Rather, he wished to insulate both vessels in something that does not add heat, such as where it became uncovered on Shabbos, when it is permitted to cover it. He thought it was permitted because we rule that one may insulate a cold item, but Rabbah forbade it because placing it atop the hot-water urn is like insulating it in something that adds heat. On Shabbos they permitted only placing one hot-water urn atop another in order to preserve its heat, as explained above.
