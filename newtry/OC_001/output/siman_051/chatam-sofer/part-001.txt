**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 9: I am astonished.</b> See my Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayyim, siman 10. There I cite my relative by marriage, R' Yosef Lehmann of Amsterdam, of blessed memory, who resolved Rashba's question on Berachos 14b. In Eretz Yisrael they recited at Ma'ariv: 'Speak to the Children of Israel and say to them: I am Hashem your God.' How could they do so, when we may not divide a verse at a point where Moshe did not divide it? He answered that this is itself a complete verse at the beginning of the section of forbidden relationships in Parashas Acharei: 'Hashem spoke to Moshe, saying: Speak to the Children of Israel and say to them: I am Hashem your God.' Chasam Sofer praised this and wrote that the answer is undoubtedly correct for us, while also explaining why Rashba did not give it. See there further regarding the custom of beginning Friday-night Kiddush with 'It was evening and it was morning, the sixth day,' although this starts in the middle of a verse. This is done to form Hashem's Name from the initial letters of 'the sixth day; the heavens were completed.' Beginning with merely 'the sixth day' would be meaningless, so 'it was evening, etc.' was added. Beginning at the verse's start; 'God saw, etc., and behold it was very good'; is inappropriate on Friday night because the Sages expound 'very good' as referring to death. Since there is no suitable alternative, dividing the verse in this manner is permitted. This explains the custom.
