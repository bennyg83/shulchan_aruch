<b>(2) If he finished.</b> If he did not finish, he should not answer amen. This applies only once he has begun “Blessed are You, Hashem,” etc.; before that point the passage is merely praise.

This concerns amen after Baruch SheAmar or Yishtabach. Other amens may be answered even in the middle of these blessings, since they are not mentioned in the Gemara. However, after “Blessed are You, Hashem,” before completing “King praised through praises,” he may not answer, because he would disrupt the blessing.

<b>(3) The chazzan.</b> If he finishes together with the chazzan, he should not answer amen, because it appears that he is answering after his own blessing.

The same applies to all blessings, except Yishtabach, Yehalelucha after Hallel, and Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad. In those cases, one may rely upon the authorities who permit answering amen after one's own blessing.

If he concludes one blessing while the chazzan concludes another, he may answer amen.

**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(10) For the sake of a mitzvah.</b> It is obvious that if one fears that the time for Krias Shema will pass before he reaches it in the order of prayer, and he forgot to recite it before Baruch She'amar, he may interrupt and recite it. Likewise, if he forgot to recite Birkat HaTorah before prayer, he may recite it during Pesukei DeZimrah and afterward say the customary verses, because some hold that even Pesukei DeZimrah may not be recited before Birkat HaTorah. One who is in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah should not initially be called to the Torah, except when he is a kohen and no other kohen is present, or a levi and he is the only levi. He may read quietly together with the Torah reader, but he should not interrupt to ask the gabbai to recite a Mi Sheberach. If the gabbai began it on his own, forgot his name, and asks him, he may answer because of honor. If he is near the end of a section and can finish it without delay before going up, he should do so. If finishing would require delay, he should not wait, because of the burden upon the congregation. This is from Shaarei Ephraim. One may not recite the verse “Who can recount the mighty acts of Hashem,” etc., after Baruch She'amar and before Hodu. Some have the practice of reciting it after completing the psalms. Some recite Kaddish before Baruch She'amar, and others before Hodu.

During “Hashem reigns, Hashem reigned, Hashem shall reign forever and ever,” one should not interrupt, just as one may not interrupt between “Hashem your God” and “true.”

<b>(17) Forever and ever.</b> It is written in the name of the Arizal that one should also recite the Aramaic translation of this verse, meaning twice in Scripture and once in translation. One should also recite the verse “For Pharaoh's horse came,” because that passage is part of the essential Song. In the name of the Gra, however, they write not to say “For Pharaoh's horse came,” etc. In the second occurrence of Mi Chamocha, the kaf is pronounced with a dagesh. In the first Mi Chamocha, and in “all my bones shall say, Hashem, who is like You,” the kaf is soft. In “this people You redeemed,” the gimel is pronounced with a dagesh. In “they sank like stone,” the kaf is pronounced with a dagesh. One should pause between “in the waters” and “the mighty,” because “the mighty” refers to the Egyptians. One should recite the Song at the Sea joyfully and imagine that on that very day he crossed the sea. One who recites it with joy is forgiven for his sins.

(כב) משום חמץ - כי עם התודה היו צריכין להביא עשרה לחמי חמץ גם בע"פ לא היו יכולין להביא תודה שמא לא יוכלו לאכלו עד זמן איסור חמץ ויצטרכו לשרפו ואסור לגרום לקדשים שיבואו לידי שריפה ומטעם זה ג"כ לא בעיו"כ מפני שממעט זמן אכילתם ומביאם לידי פסול. ומטעם זה ג"כ הנוהג לומר בכל יום פרשת קרבנות השייכים ליחיד לא יאמר בעיוה"כ דאין מביאין אותם בעיוה"כ כמו תודה זולת פרשת עולה יכול לומר [פמ"ג בשם תבו"ש] ואומרים מזמור לתודה גם בעט"ב ובט"ב [אחרונים]. אין לדלג ביו"ט הפסוק מזמור שיר ליום השבת דגם יו"ט נקרא שבת [בה"ט]:
**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(22) Because of chametz.</b> A thanksgiving offering required ten loaves of chametz. It could not be brought on erev Pesach, lest there be insufficient time to eat it before chametz became forbidden, forcing the sacred food to be burned. One may not cause sacred food to become subject to burning. For the same reason, it was not brought on erev Yom Kippur, because doing so would shorten its permitted eating time and bring it to disqualification. For this reason, one who customarily recites each day the Torah sections concerning private offerings should not recite them on erev Yom Kippur, because such offerings were not brought then, just as a thanksgiving offering was not brought. He may, however, recite the section of the burnt offering. This is from Peri Megadim in the name of Tevuot Shor. Mizmor LeTodah is recited on erev Tisha B'Av and on Tisha B'Av, according to the later authorities. On Yom Tov one should not omit the verse “A psalm, a song for the day of Shabbos,” because Yom Tov is also called Shabbos, according to Baer Heitev.
