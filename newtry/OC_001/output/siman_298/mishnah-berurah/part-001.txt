(א) מברך על הנר וכו' - משום דתחלת ברייתו הוי במו"ש כדאמרינן בפסחים דף נ"ד במו"ש נתן הקב"ה דעה באדה"ר וטחן ב' אבנים זו בזו ויצא מהן אור והא דמברכין במוצאי יוה"כ אף כשאינו חל במו"ש מפני שהוא כעין הבדלה שכל היום היה אסור להשתמש בזה האור אף לאוכל נפש ולא כמו בשאר יו"ט ועכשיו מותר ולכך דעת הי"א דצריך לחזור אחריו כמו להבדלה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One blesses over the lamp, etc.</b> Because the beginning of its creation was on Motzaei Shabbos, as we say in Pesachim 54: on Motzaei Shabbos the Holy One, blessed is He, gave understanding to Adam HaRishon, and he struck two stones against one another and fire emerged from them. That which we bless on Motzaei Yom Kippur even when it does not fall on Motzaei Shabbos is because it is like havdalah, for the entire day it was forbidden to use this fire even for food preparation, unlike other festivals, and now it is permitted. Therefore, according to the opinion of the some say, one must seek after it just as for havdalah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Over a torch.</b> Because its light is great. It is good to beautify the mitzvah by using wax. The Magen Avraham wrote this in the name of the Kavanos of the Arizal. He further wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim that one should not take resinous wood, called kein in the vernacular, because its smell is bad. It appears that this refers to those that give off a bad smell of pitch. Above, in siman 264, it is stated that even a pitch lamp itself may be lit on Shabbos night, and likewise with itran, which certainly smells bad, they would have lit with it were it not for the concern that he might leave and go out in the middle of the meal. Certainly the light of havdalah, for which one need not seek, is no better than the Shabbos lamp, which is an obligation. Perhaps the intent of Sefer Chassidim is that this is not the preferred manner, even though its light is great, because of its bad smell; but it is not worse than another lamp that is not a torch. The Peri Megadim also wrote that if he has no other lamp, he may bless over it.

This is not so on Motzaei Yom Kippur, where it is explained below in seif 8 that one does not bless over fire produced from wood and stones.
