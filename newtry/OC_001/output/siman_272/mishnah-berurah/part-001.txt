(א) אין מקדשין - אפילו אין לו יין אחר ואפילו בדיעבד אם קידש משמע מדברי הרמב"ן שהובא בב"י דלא יצא ועיין בבה"ל. ואם מותר להבדיל על היין כזה עיין בחדושי רע"א שמסתפק בזה ול"נ פשוט הדבר לאיסור כיון שהטעם הוא משום הקריבהו נא לפחתך אח"כ מצאתי בברכי יוסף שכ"כ בשם ספר בית יהודה. ופשוט דה"ה על השכר שריחו רע אין מקדשין:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) <b>One may not recite Kiddush.</b> Even if he has no other wine. Even after the fact, if he recited Kiddush over it, the Ramban's words cited by the Beis Yosef imply that he did not fulfill his obligation; see Biur Halachah. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger questions whether one may recite Havdalah over such wine. It appears plainly forbidden, since the reason is, 'Offer it, please, to your governor.' I later found that Birkei Yosef rules likewise in the name of Beis Yehudah. Plainly the same applies to beer with a foul odor: one may not recite Kiddush over it.

(3) <b>For we are not particular about uncovered wine.</b> Since snakes are not common among us. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because of, 'Offer it, please, to your governor.' If it stood uncovered only briefly, however, there is no present concern so long as its taste and odor have not deteriorated. Chayei Adam writes that this applies specifically where wine is costly and people are not particular about such brief exposure; one using beer for Kiddush should be careful. It appears that after the fact, when he has no other beverage, one need not be particular even with beer, just as with wine.

**** ENGLISH ****
(16) <b>One may recite Kiddush over it.</b> Bechor Shor writes that since siman 204:5, gloss, establishes that one part wine in six parts water is nullified and no Borei Pri HaGefen is recited, here too one must put enough raisins into the water that they comprise slightly more than one-sixth of the water; otherwise the blessing is in vain, since this is no better than if all the raisins were wine. He writes that the raisins are measured as they stand after swelling from the water, not when first placed in it. Yad Ephraim there questions this, while Derech HaChaim plainly disagrees and measures them at the time they are put into the water, since their later swelling comes only from absorbed water. Some poskim hold that whenever it tastes like wine it is considered wine [see Yoreh De'ah 123:8, gloss]. Nevertheless, every God-fearing person should be careful, since Yoreh De'ah 134:5 and siman 204:5, gloss, rule that it is nullified in six parts water. Ordinary wine certainly contains the required proportion and may be used for Kiddush. See Biur Halachah for what we wrote in the name of Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov.

(כט) טוב לקדש - היינו דבזה הוא יותר טוב מעל הפת כמו שמפרש הטעם אבל יין במקום שהוא מצוי ודאי יברך עליו אפילו ביום וכדמוכח בסימן רפ"ט ס"ב ומ"מ במדינתנו שהיין ביוקר ורוב שתיית המדינה הוא משאר משקין לא נהגו אפילו הגדולים להדר אחר יין ביום שהקידוש שלו הוא רק מדרבנן לכו"ע וסומכין עצמן על דברי המקילין בזה ומי שמברך גם ביום על היין ודאי עושה מצוה מן המובחר:
**** ENGLISH ****
(29) <b>It is good to recite Kiddush.</b> Meaning that by day beer is preferable to bread, as the stated reason explains; but where wine is available, he should certainly bless over wine even by day, as proven in siman 289:2. Nevertheless, in our lands, where wine is expensive and the country's principal drink is other beverages, even prominent people have not customarily sought wine for daytime Kiddush, which according to all opinions is rabbinic. They rely on the lenient authorities. One who also blesses over wine by day certainly performs the mitzvah in its preferred manner.

(ל) על השכר - ואם חביב לו יין שרוף יכול לקדש עליו ביום לכתחלה במדינתנו שהוא חמר מדינה אך שיזהר ליקח כוס מחזיק רביעית ולשתות ממנו מלא לוגמיו שהוא רוב רביעית ובדיעבד או בשעת הדחק שאין יכול לשתות כמלא לוגמיו ואין לו יין ושאר משקין אפילו שתיית כל המסובין מצטרפין למלא לוגמיו וכדלעיל סימן רע"א סי"ד. ולענין מי דבש ושאר משקים עיין במה שכתבנו לקמן בסימן רצ"ו במ"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
(30) <b>Over beer.</b> If distilled spirits are dear to him, in our countries where they are chamar medinah he may initially use them for daytime Kiddush. He must take a cup holding a revi'is and drink melo lugmav, which is most of a revi'is. After the fact, or under pressure when he cannot drink melo lugmav and has neither wine nor another beverage, the drinking of all those assembled may combine to melo lugmav, as above in siman 271:14. Regarding mead and other beverages, see what we wrote below in siman 296 in the Mishnah Berurah.

(31) <b>Shehakol.</b> This additional blessing before the meal makes it evident that it is for the honor of Shabbos. If he blessed over bread, there would be no such indication, since no Kiddush formula is recited in the morning.

(לב) לא יקדש על הפת - כדי לחוש לדעת ר"ת שסובר דאין מקדשין על הפת כלל [מ"א]. ודע דאפילו להרא"ש ויתר הפוסקים דנקטינן כוותייהו דמותר לקדש על הפת ג"כ מודו דבמקום שיש יין בעיר אין מקדשין על הפת דעיקר מצוה הוא על היין אך דלדידהו אם הפת חביב לו יותר מיין אז מותר לקדש על הפת אפילו יש לו יין וכן פסק בדה"ח ואפשר שע"ז סומכין העולם להקל בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(32) <b>He should not recite Kiddush over bread.</b> This takes account of Rabbeinu Tam's opinion that Kiddush may never be recited over bread [Magen Avraham]. Know that even the Rosh and the other poskim whose view we follow, permitting Kiddush over bread, agree that where wine is available in the city one should not use bread, since the primary mitzvah is over wine. In their view, however, if bread is dearer to him than wine he may use bread even when he has wine; Derech HaChaim rules so. Perhaps the common lenient practice relies on this.

(33) <b>He may recite Kiddush over it, etc.</b> Magen Avraham decides that he may not recite Kiddush intending that others drink, since they know how to recite Borei Pri HaGefen themselves, as proven below in siman 273:4. Rather, they should recite Kiddush themselves, and he should recite Kiddush over bread.
