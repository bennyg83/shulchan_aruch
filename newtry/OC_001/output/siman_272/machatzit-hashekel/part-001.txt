**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And see the Bach, etc.</b> He challenged what the Tur wrote: if its smell is vinegar but its taste is wine, it is wine; but if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar, it is vinegar, for we follow its taste, end quote. The Bach objected: if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar, it is obvious that one may not recite kiddush over it, for it was explained above that one does not even bless Borei peri hagafen over it. Therefore, he answered that the rule that one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over it speaks specifically when a film formed upon it, meaning a kind of film grew on it; but if no film formed upon it, even though its taste is vinegar, one blesses Borei peri hagafen over it. Here it teaches us that nevertheless one may not recite kiddush over it. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote that his words are not correct; rather, even if no film formed upon it, one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over it. As to the Bach's question, one must say as the Taz wrote, that since the Tur wrote that if its smell is vinegar and its taste is wine one may recite kiddush over it, he also wrote the reverse, that if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar one may not recite kiddush over it.

<b>וכ"מ בגמרא דהא אמימר כו'</b> והוא בפסחים דף ק"ז ע"א אמימר איקלע לההוא אתרא והביאו לו במ"ש שכר להבדלה ולא רצה להבדיל עליו ובת טוות (היינו לן בתענית כי אסור לאכול קודם הבדלה) למחר טרחו והביאו לו יין והבדיל וטעם. שנה אחרת שוב איקלע להאי אתרא ושוב הביאו לו שכר להבדלה אמר אי הכי חמר מדינה והבדיל עליה וטעם ע"כ ומסתמא בשנה הא' כשראו שלא טעם מידי ודאי טרחו בכל כחם להמציא לו יין ולא מצאו וגם אמימר מסתמא אסיק אדעתיה שאין יין מצוי וא"כ גם בשנה א' הא הוי חמר מדינה ולמה לא הבדיל עליו אע"כ דלא מיקרי חמר מדינה אלא שבכל השנה אין יין שם ולכן אמימר בשנה ראשונ' טעה דכל השנה הי' להם יין אלא דהיום דרך מקרה לא ה"ל יין ולכן לא רצה להבדיל על שכר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so it appears from the Gemara, for Ameimar, etc.</b> This is in Pesachim, folio 107a: Ameimar came to a certain place, and on Motzaei Shabbos they brought him beer for Havdalah. He did not want to recite Havdalah over it, and he passed the night fasting, meaning he slept in a fast, because it is forbidden to eat before Havdalah. The next day they exerted themselves and brought him wine, and he recited Havdalah and tasted. Another year he again came to that place, and again they brought him beer for Havdalah. He said, if so, this is the beverage of the locale, and he recited Havdalah over it and tasted; thus far. Presumably, in the first year, when they saw that he did not taste anything, they certainly exerted themselves with all their strength to obtain wine for him and did not find any. Ameimar also presumably realized that wine was not available. If so, even in the first year it was the beverage of the locale, and why did he not recite Havdalah over it? Rather, it must be that it is not called the beverage of the locale unless throughout the whole year there is no wine there. Therefore, in the first year Ameimar erred, thinking that throughout the whole year they had wine, only that on that day, by chance, they had no wine, and therefore he did not want to recite Havdalah over beer.

<b>ונ"ל דאין מקדשין על יי"ש כו'</b> ועסי' רע"א סי"ג. ר"ל עוד טעם שאין מקדשין על יי"ש כו' דהא מבואר בסי' רע"א דצריך המקדש לשתות מלא לוגמיו דהיינו רוב רביעית ואין דרך לשתות יי"ש כ"כ הרבה בפעם אחד אף על גב די"א שם דמצטרפים שתיית כל המסובים למלא לוגמיו היינו דיעבד אבל לכתחל' צריך המקדש לבד לשתות מלא לוגמיו וכ"כ בתשובת בית יעקב סי' נ"ז ואף על גב דהטורי זהב כתב לעיל סי' ר"י דביי"ש אין צריך שיעור רביעית לברכה אחרונ' ודי במה שהוא שיעור שתיה לרוב ב"א וא"כ ה"ה לענין קידוש אולם מ"א לטעמיה אזיל שכתב בסי' ק"ץ סק"ד דגם ביי"ש שעורו רביעית וכ"כ בתשובת ב"י הנ"ל ודחה שם ראיית ט"ז אלא כתב שם אם אפשר לו לשתות כשיעור שלא בהפסק גדול (דהיינו תוך שיעור שתיית רביעית או תוך כדי אכילת פרס לכל מר כדאית ליה כמ"ש סי' תרי"ב) מצטרף. אך בתשובת נ"ש סי' ל"ה כתב דאפי' במקום דלא הוי יי"ש חמר מדינה מותר לקדש עליו בשחרית כיון דקידוש שחרית אינו אלא משום הכירא לא בעינן שיהי' חמר מדינ' כ"ה דעת הטור סי' רפ"ט הובא במ"א שם אבל אין כן דעת הרב"י שם אדרבה עדיף טפי לקדש עליו מלקדש בשחר על שכר כי אליבא ריקנא ודאי חביב עליו היי"ש יותר מן השכר וראוי לברך על החביב אבל בזה שניהם ה"ה בעל תשובת ב"י הנ"ל ותשובת נ"ש הנ"ל מודים דאינו זיעה אלא הוי משקה גמור ע"ש. ובס' ידי משה על מדרש רבה כתב ראיה בשם גדול אחד דע"כ יי"ש לא מקרי משקה מדאמרינן למה נמשלו ישראל לשמן מה שמן עולה על כל המשקין ר"ל אם תתן שמן על כל המשקים השמן צף למעל' כן ישראל יעלו על כל או"ה וביי"ש אם ניתן לתוך המשקין היי"ש שוקע למטה ואם כן ע"כ אינו בכלל משקין לפ"ד המדרש הובא גם כן בספר ת"ש אלא דסיים שאין למדין הלכה מפי אגדה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it seems to me that one does not recite kiddush over liquor, etc.</b> See siman 271, seif 13. That is to say, there is another reason one does not recite kiddush over liquor, etc., for it is explained in siman 271 that the one reciting kiddush must drink a cheekful, meaning the majority of a reviis, and it is not the way to drink so much liquor at one time. Although there are those who say there that the drinking of all those reclining joins to make up a cheekful, that is after the fact; initially the one reciting kiddush himself must drink a cheekful. So too wrote the responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 57. Although the Turei Zahav wrote above, in siman 210, that for liquor one does not require the measure of a reviis for the after-blessing, and whatever is the measure of drinking for most people suffices, and if so the same should apply regarding kiddush, nevertheless the Magen Avraham follows his own reasoning, for he wrote in siman 190, seif katan 4, that liquor too has the measure of a reviis. So too wrote the above responsa Beit Yaakov, and there he rejected the proof of the Taz. Rather, he wrote there that if one can drink the required measure without a large interruption, meaning within the time needed to drink a reviis or within the time needed to eat a pras, each authority according to his view, as written in siman 612, it combines. However, in the responsa Nahar Shalom, siman 35, he wrote that even in a place where liquor is not the beverage of the locale, it is permitted to recite kiddush over it in the morning, since the morning kiddush is only for recognition, and we do not require it to be the beverage of the locale. This is the opinion of the Tur in siman 289, cited in the Magen Avraham there. But this is not the opinion of the Beit Yosef there. On the contrary, it is more preferable to recite kiddush over it than to recite the morning kiddush over beer, for on an empty stomach liquor is certainly more beloved to him than beer, and it is proper to bless over what is beloved. But in this, both the author of the above responsa Beit Yaakov and the above responsa Nahar Shalom agree that it is not mere vapor, but a full beverage; see there. In the sefer Yedei Moshe on Midrash Rabbah, he wrote a proof in the name of one great man that liquor must not be called a beverage, for they said: why were Yisrael compared to oil? Just as oil rises above all liquids, meaning that if you place oil upon all liquids, the oil floats above, so Yisrael will rise above all the nations of the world. But with liquor, if it is placed into liquids, the liquor sinks downward. If so, according to the words of the midrash it is necessarily not included among liquids. This is also brought in the sefer Tosafos Shabbos, but he concluded that we do not derive halacha from aggadah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 9) And one who is not, etc.</b> This is the wording of the Darchei Moshe, etc. The Magen Avraham's intent is to challenge the Rama's distinction, as he writes afterward. Since in the Darchei Moshe the Rama proved his distinction, he first brings the wording of the Darchei Moshe and its proof, and then rejects his proof. The Beit Yosef speaks where wine is not beloved to him. That is to say, he did not vow off wine, and if he wishes he can drink wine, only it is not beloved to him. Although in the Gemara they say: when bread is beloved, etc., this is the statement of Rav Brona, who said: if he washed his hands he should not recite kiddush, for since he washed his hands, he revealed his mind that bread is beloved to him, and it is proper to recite kiddush over what is beloved. However, Rav Brona holds that one does not recite kiddush over bread, according to the Rif's understanding. Rabbi Yitzchak said to him: many times I stood before Rav and bread was beloved to him, and he recited kiddush over bread. Thus one sees that one may recite kiddush over bread. This is according to the Rif's view. See above, siman 271, in the Magen Avraham, seif katan 26, where I brought the words of the Talmud and its commentators. It follows that where bread is beloved to him, he should recite kiddush over bread and not over wine.

<b>Since others know, etc.</b> That is to say, we rule in siman 271 that initially the one reciting kiddush must drink a cheekful. Although after the fact he fulfills the obligation if the others reclining tasted, nevertheless initially one should not do so. But do not say: it is written in siman 273, seif 4, that a person may recite kiddush for others, for for them it is the place of the meal, and it concludes there: if he has not yet recited kiddush for himself, he should be careful not to taste with them, etc. Thus it is permitted initially to recite kiddush for others while they taste and not he. One must say that since his primary intent is to discharge others, it is as though they themselves recited kiddush; if they drink, it is like the one who recited kiddush tasted. If so, here too perhaps he can do so, intending to discharge those reclining, and it would suffice that the others whom he discharged taste. Nevertheless, here he too may eat through this kiddush, for for him too it is kiddush in the place of a meal. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote that this applies specifically when they do not know how to recite kiddush; but if they know, they should recite kiddush themselves. Even more, one may say that the Magen Avraham's intent is to raise the difficulty from siman 271, that initially the one reciting kiddush must taste, as above. If we say that the Rama's intent, when one vowed, is that he should recite kiddush over the wine and others should drink, speaks specifically of the daytime kiddush, then his intent in reciting kiddush over wine is to discharge the others, while he in truth fulfills his own obligation only by blessing HaMotzi over bread. Still, he can recite kiddush over wine; but when others are not with him, he cannot at all bless Borei peri hagafen over wine. This the Magen Avraham rejects, because one cannot discharge others if he himself is not fulfilling his obligation, unless they do not know how to recite kiddush. Since in the daytime the kiddush consists only of Borei peri hagafen, and presumably they know that blessing, see the end of the responsum of the Chacham Tzvi, siman 168.

But in the evening one cannot do so. Specifically in the morning, when the kiddush has no additional blessing except the blessing over wine, beer, or bread, one can properly say that he fulfills the obligation whichever way. This is not so in the evening, when the Sages instituted a special blessing for kiddush in addition to the blessings of benefit. If he recites kiddush over beer and says the additional blessing after Shehakol, perhaps he did not fulfill with beer. One cannot say that he fulfills with bread, for he cannot go back and say a second time the special blessing of kiddush, since one of them would be in vain. What the Bach wrote, that the Geonim, etc., is as written in siman 174. That is to say, there is there a dispute among the poskim whether one must bless over drinks other than wine during a meal, or whether we say that bread exempts them because they are needs of the meal. Therefore, the Beit Yosef wrote there that in order to avoid the doubt, one should sit before washing in the place of his meal, drink less than the required measure, bless over it, and intend to exempt also the drinks during the meal. The Bach concluded: if they would recite kiddush over wine, they would not avoid the doubt, end quote. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote: I do not know, etc., for wine exempts, etc. In the sefer Eliyah Rabbah and the sefer Tosafos Shabbos, both intended the same point: that the Geonim were concerned for the view of the Rokeach, brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 174, who holds that wine exempts other drinks only from the after-blessing, not from the first blessing. The Rama alluded to this at the beginning of siman 174, where the Mechaber wrote that wine exempts all kinds of beverages, and the Rama wrote: even from the first blessing, end quote. He used the language 'even' regarding the first blessing because the Rokeach agrees only regarding the after-blessing. These are their words. In my humble opinion one may say that in any event there is a difficulty with the Bach's answer: they wanted to avoid the dispute among the poskim about whether one must bless over drinks, but they entered into the dispute about whether one may recite kiddush over beer. Also, according to all the poskim, the main rule is that if there is wine in the city one should not recite kiddush over beer. What did they gain by reciting kiddush over beer? The Bach resolves this with his precise language, for he wrote as follows: Do not say that the Geonim were accustomed, etc., to bless each time over other drinks in the place of a meal with intent to drink during the meal; therefore even on Shabbos morning they would recite kiddush, etc., end quote. That is to say, since they had already established this good practice, they did not want to change their practice, and that practice was for them like a vow that could not be changed, even though by this they entered themselves into the dispute among the poskim whether one may recite kiddush over beer. Therefore the Magen Avraham asked that with wine too they would exempt the drinks during the meal. And if they were concerned for the view of the Rokeach, as above, this creates the difficulty stated above: is it worthwhile to satisfy the Rokeach, who is a lone opinion in the matter, and enter into a dispute about whether one may recite kiddush over beer, when many poskim hold that even where it is the beverage of the locale one may not recite kiddush over it, all the more so where wine is available in the city? Regarding this one cannot answer as above that they had already practiced to satisfy the Rokeach, for practice does not apply to this, since it is not the way to drink wine on weekdays. On the contrary, every Friday night they acted not according to the Rokeach, for at night they recited kiddush over wine. With what did they exempt the drinks during the meal if not through the wine? One cannot say that they blessed over beer; when would they do so? Before kiddush over wine it is forbidden to drink beer. According to the Bach, one must say with difficulty that since their holy practice was to remove themselves from dispute among the poskim concerning the blessing over drinks during the meal, therefore wherever it was possible for them to satisfy the Rokeach, they would do so, since in any event their practice in the essential matter was to satisfy the dispute regarding the blessing. But this is forced.
