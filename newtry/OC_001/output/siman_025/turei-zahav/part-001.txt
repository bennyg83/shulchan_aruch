<b>כדי שלא יעבור על המצות. </b> דאע"ג דגבי תפילין אם פגע בשל ראש תחלה יסלקנה ויניח של יד תחלה כמ"ש סי' זה ס"ו שאני התם דכתיב והי' לאות כו' כמ"ש בסמוך אבל הכא דאין לנו קדימה לציצית אלא מאסמכתא בעלמא כיון שהיא  תדירה בשבת ובחול כמה שכ' ב"י ר"ס זה מוטב שתדחה אסמכתא זאת משיעבור על המצוה כן כתב ב"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that he should not pass over the mitzvos.</b> Although regarding tefillin, if he encountered the tefillin of the head first, he should remove it and put on the tefillin of the hand first, as written in this siman, seif 6, that is different, for there it is written, and it shall be as a sign, etc., as written nearby. But here, where the precedence of tzitzis is only from a mere asmachta, since it is more frequent on Shabbos and weekday, as the Beit Yosef wrote at the beginning of this siman, it is better that this asmachta be pushed aside than that he pass over the mitzvah. So wrote the Beit Yosef.

<b>ושם יתעטף וכו'. </b> פי' דהזוהר כתב שצריך לברך על ציצית קודם תפילין כי תחלה צריך לפרוס על ראשו פרוס' דמצו' ואח"כ יתקשר בקישור' דיחודא דאינהו תפילין שמורין על יחוד שמו ית'. והנ"י כתב ג"כ הכי מט"א רק שכ' אח"כ ומ"מ אותם שמתעטפים בטלית גדול כיון שאין דרך להתעטף אלא אחר כל המלבושים כשנתנו מקטוריהן בראשיהם מצות תפילין באה לפניו תחלה ומברכין עליהם תחלה לפי שאין מעבירין על המצו' עכ"ל ונראה פי' דבריו דכיון דכבר יש המקטורן שהוא בגד כמין כובע על ראשו והטלי' מעוטף עליו מלמעל' וכשירצ' אח"כ להניח תפילין יצטרך להסיר הטלית עם המקטורן מראשו וזהו כמעבירין על המצות דהיינו מצות ציצית שהוא כבר על ראשו והוא ס"ל דעיקר עטיפת הטלית על הראש כעטיפת הישמעאלים שזכר הטור בסי' ח' מ"ה מסיק בשם גדולי עולם שיש לצאת ידי שניהם ללבוש טלית קטן ולהניח תפילין ובב"ה אח"כ יתעטף בטלית גדול. ולפמ"ש רמ"א שהעולם נהגו להתעטף בטלית גדול ג"כ בביתו  אין קושיא מההיא דינים שזכרנו דהא אע"פ שמעביר קצת הטלית מעל ראשו מ"מ אינו מעביר על מצות ציצית שעליו דהא כבר פסק רבינו בסי' ח' דאע"פ דלפעמים הוא בגילוי הראש מהטלית לית לן בה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And there he should wrap himself, etc.</b> Meaning, the Zohar wrote that one must bless over tzitzis before tefillin, because first one must spread over his head a covering of mitzvah, and afterward he should bind himself with the bond of unity, which is tefillin, that point to the unity of His blessed Name. The Nimukei Yosef also wrote so for another reason, but afterward wrote: nevertheless, those who wrap themselves in a large tallis, since the way is not to wrap oneself except after all the garments, when they have placed their caps on their heads, the mitzvah of tefillin comes before him first, and they bless over them first, because one does not pass over mitzvos. End quote. It seems the explanation of his words is that since the cap, which is a garment like a hat, is already on his head, and the tallis is wrapped over it from above, when he afterward wants to put on tefillin he will need to remove the tallis together with the cap from his head. This is like passing over the mitzvos, namely the mitzvah of tzitzis that is already upon his head. He holds that the essential wrapping of the tallis is on the head, like the wrapping of the Ishmaelites mentioned by the Tur in siman 8. Therefore he concludes in the name of the great authorities that one should satisfy both: wear a tallis katan and put on tefillin, and afterward in the synagogue wrap himself in a large tallis. According to what the Rama wrote, that the world practiced to wrap in a large tallis also at home, one must be careful to take the tallis first, so as not to pass over the mitzvos.

<b>צריך להעביר כו'. </b> דכתיב והיה לך לאות על ידך והדר ולטוטפות בין עיניך ודרשינן נמי בגמ' כ"ז שבין עיניך יהיו שתים ומ"ה מוטב שיעבור על המצות משיעבור על מ"ש בתורה משא"כ לענין ציצית ותפילין כמ"ש בסי' זה ס"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Two blessings.</b> The reason is that although the tefillin of the hand and tefillin of the head are two mitzvos, the Sages instituted that when he puts on both together, the blessings should be joined to both of them. Therefore if he spoke between them, he has separated them and must bless again over the tefillin of the head.

<b>Lehaniach with a kamatz.</b> Although there is no difference in meaning, nevertheless it is fitting to bless in the language of the verse, to cause blessing to rest in your house, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Agur. Copy from the responsum of my brother, the gaon Rabbi Yitzchak HaLevi, of blessed memory, regarding the blessings over the mitzvah of tefillin, whether one should say mitzvas or mitzvos. This is his wording: Question: those who practice like the Behag, Rabbeinu Tam, and their associates, who hold that over the tefillin of the hand one blesses lehaniach tefillin and over the tefillin of the head al mitzvas tefillin, when they put on both, should they say al mitzvas with the vav pronounced with a patach, or al mitzvos with a cholam, in the plural? I have seen many divided, one saying this and one saying that, and each group shows a reason for its words. Let our master teach us the truth in this. Answer: at the beginning of Perek HaTecheles we learned in the Mishnah: the tefillin of the hand do not prevent the tefillin of the head, and the tefillin of the head do not prevent the tefillin of the hand. This proves that they are two mitzvos, for if they were one mitzvah they would prevent one another, as we learned at the end of Perek HaKometz Rabbah that the four tzitzis prevent one another because they are one mitzvah. So too it is explicit there at the beginning of Perek HaTecheles, where Rav Chisda said that the statement that they do not prevent one another applies only when he has them, meaning both tefillin, but if he does not have them, they prevent. He said to him: did you say this? He said to him: no, rather, one who does not have two mitzvos, should he also not perform one mitzvah? So too the Rif wrote in tractate Rosh Hashanah, that this is not comparable to tefillin, where Rav Chisda said that if one spoke he goes back and blesses, for those are two mitzvos, as we learned: the tefillin of the hand do not prevent, etc. So too wrote the Rosh there and in the first chapter of Pesachim. So too wrote the Rambam in the fourth chapter of Hilchos Tefillin, because this is a mitzvah by itself and this is a mitzvah by itself. So too wrote the Tur and the other poskim, and in the count of the 613 mitzvos they counted them as two mitzvos. From here there is difficulty with what Tosafot wrote in Perek HaKometz Rabbah, that one who spoke between one tefillin and the other goes back and blesses, and from here people infer that when a person slaughters many birds or animals and speaks between them, he goes back and blesses. Nevertheless it is a transgression in his hand, as they say here that he returns from the battle arrangements. Perhaps tefillin are different, for they are one mitzvah, but there, if he wants he slaughters and if he wants he does not slaughter. End quote. Behold, they considered them one mitzvah. So too wrote Tosafot and the Rosh in Perek Kisui HaDam. If so, these words of the Rosh contradict his words that we mentioned above. Also in the first chapter of Pesachim, the words of the Rosh contradict one another, for at first he wrote that even according to Rashi, since they are two mitzvos, etc., and afterward he wrote that what we bless over the tefillin of the hand lehaniach and over the tefillin of the head al mitzvas is because the Sages did not want to institute two identical blessings one after another for one mitzvah, even if it is divided, as found in the Mordechai at the end of Perek Bnei HaIr in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim, that one who reads in the Torah and speaks does not go back and bless, and it is not comparable to tefillin, for they are sometimes one mitzvah and sometimes two mitzvos: if he did not speak, one mitzvah; if he spoke, two mitzvos. End quote. Nevertheless it is difficult, since it is explicit in the Mishnah and Gemara above that they are two mitzvos, from where did Tosafot, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Ephraim derive from their own reasoning to say that without speech they are one mitzvah? Moreover, from their words it appears as though it is obvious and explicit that they are one mitzvah when he did not speak, for they brought no proof for this. From where did they derive this certainty? Also, according to their view, which holds like Rabbeinu Tam that one recites two blessings even when putting on both, how did they write that they are one mitzvah? Do we not say that one does not bundle mitzvos together, and that one does not close a blessing with two matters for this reason? Without this also, this difficulty applies to what the Rosh and Mordechai wrote, that according to Rabbeinu Tam, if he has only the tefillin of the head, he recites two blessings. The words of Rabbeinu Ephraim also require explanation: why do we say with tefillin that if he spoke they are two mitzvos, and if he did not speak they are one mitzvah, but we do not say so regarding Torah reading? What is the difference? If it is because there is a difference between the tefillin, this one in one compartment and its place on the hand, and this one in four compartments and its place on the head, then even if he did not speak they should be two mitzvos for this reason. It appears that they are certainly two mitzvos even without speech, for regarding the tefillin of the hand a separate command was stated, and regarding the tefillin of the head a command was stated by itself. This is with respect to their tying and placement, this one on the hand and this one on the head. In the bodies of the tefillin as well they are divided, this one in one compartment and this one in four compartments. Rather, because it would have been proper to recite over each of them two blessings, for the reason that will be explained nearby, the Sages commanded that they be joined to one another and that over both one should bless the two blessings, so that the blessing lehaniach should apply to the tefillin of the hand and also to the tefillin of the head, and the blessing al mitzvas should apply to both. They found support for this from the Torah, as the Razah wrote in the last chapter of Rosh Hashanah: it is a transgression in his hand, because although they are two mitzvos, nevertheless, since regarding the tefillin of the hand and the tefillin of the head it is written, and it shall be for a sign upon your hand and for a remembrance between your eyes, a remembrance is required, that the tefillin of the head be immediately adjacent to the tefillin of the hand, so that there should be one being for both of them. So too it implies in Tur Orach Chaim, siman 25: I saw my father, the Rosh, of blessed memory, that immediately after he tied the tefillin of the hand on the arm he put on the tefillin of the head before wrapping the strap around the arm, for he would say that since the second blessing returns also upon the tefillin of the hand, etc., one should minimize interruption. The Beit Yosef wrote that in a responsum of the Rosh, rule 3, law 2, he wrote that one should not wrap the strap in between because the blessing over the tefillin of the hand returns also upon the tefillin of the head, etc., and I do not know why our master changed from the words of the Rosh. Maharshal of Prague answered that what the Tur wrote, returns also upon the tefillin of the hand, means also upon the blessing of the hand, but this is forced. According to what we have said there is no difficulty, for both statements are true and go to one place: each of the two blessings returns upon both. So it is explicit in Midrash Tanchuma at the end of Parshas Bo: it is a mitzvah to put on the tefillin of the hand first and recite lehaniach, and then he puts on the tefillin of the head and recites al mitzvas. If he spoke, etc., and if he interrupted between the tefillin for yehei shemei rabbah or Kedushah, he returns from the battle arrangements. He only goes back and recites the blessing over the tefillin of the head, because each tefillin requires two blessings. If he blessed them together, the blessings apply to this one and to this one; if he interrupted, the first blessing is voided and he goes back and recites both. End quote. The reason they said that it would be proper to bless over each of them two blessings seems to me to be that each of them contains two matters. One is the body of the mitzvah, which is remembrance, to believe in the existence of Hashem, His unity, the Exodus from Egypt, and reward and punishment. Even if one held them in his hand it would be possible to fulfill this mitzvah, and for this the blessing al mitzvas tefillin was instituted. The second is the placement and tying of the tefillin in the special place for them, as it is written, and you shall bind them as a sign upon your hand, and they shall be totafos between your eyes. For this the blessing lehaniach tefillin was instituted. Therefore they commanded to join them, so that each blessing should apply to both. Now it is well understood why they recite two blessings over the tefillin of the head when he interrupted between them, and there is no issue of bundling mitzvos. For this reason too they said that it is a transgression in his hand when he speaks between them, for he must join them to one another so that the two blessings apply to both. They did not say so regarding other two separate mitzvos, nor regarding one who slaughters many birds or animals and speaks between them, for that is like interrupting in the middle of his meal; he is not obligated to join the eating after the speech to the eating before it. So too he need not join the second slaughter to the first slaughter, therefore speech between them is not a diversion of the mind. This is what Tosafot, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Ephraim meant when they said that the two tefillin are one mitzvah if he did not speak. They did not mean that they are literally one mitzvah, for if so, even if he spoke between them it should be like speaking in the middle of one mitzvah, since their main matter is one. Rather, each of the two comes to indicate two matters, therefore the blessing lehaniach returns also upon the tefillin of the head, and the blessing al mitzvas also upon the tefillin of the hand. This was obvious to them from the fact that it is forbidden to interrupt between the two tefillin and that one returns because of it from the battle arrangements, unlike other mitzvos, as explained. But if he spoke, automatically they become entirely two mitzvos, for he did not have adjacency and immediacy, and he must bless two blessings over the tefillin of the head. This is clear. Now it is well understood what can be examined in the words of Rabbeinu Ephraim that we mentioned above, for he disputes Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi, whom the Mordechai there cites in his name, that one who reads several verses in the Torah and spoke goes back and blesses, and he brings proof from tefillin. These are his words: behold here, although the tefillin of the hand does not prevent the tefillin of the head and they are two mitzvos, when he spoke it is a diversion of the mind from the first blessing. So too one who reads three verses in the Torah has fulfilled the reading, and if he spoke in between, he blesses the preceding blessing. End quote. Since he says although tefillin are two mitzvos, etc., it implies that certainly if they were one mitzvah it would be a diversion of the mind and he would go back and bless. If so, what does Rabbeinu Ephraim answer him, that it is not comparable to tefillin because sometimes they are one mitzvah and sometimes two? In any event Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi brought proof that he goes back and blesses. Also, if so, why did Rabbi Yehudah need to say that one who read three verses in the Torah has fulfilled his reading, meaning that the mitzvah was completed? If he had not fulfilled his reading and the mitzvah had not been completed, certainly it would be an interruption and diversion of the mind. According to what we wrote, their words are well explained. Rabbi Yehudah's intent was to say that you should not say that only in one mitzvah is speech an interruption and diversion of the mind, since it completely cancels the first blessing, which was with intent to complete the mitzvah, and when he interrupted he did not complete it and the first blessing is retroactively void, therefore he must go back and bless. But when he already completed the mitzvah and the first blessing was not voided, it returns also upon what he will do further, another mitzvah to which that same blessing belongs. For this Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi brings proof from tefillin, that although they are two mitzvos and he already completed the first mitzvah, and the first blessing was not retroactively voided, nevertheless he goes back and blesses the first blessing over the tefillin of the head. So too, one who reads three verses in the Torah, where the mitzvah has been completed, nevertheless goes back and blesses, even though the first blessing was not retroactively voided. Rabbeinu Ephraim answered that even if it is an interruption and diversion of the mind when one spoke after three verses, he need not go back and bless, with proof from Barchu and Krias Shema, etc. Tefillin are different, for sometimes they are one mitzvah, in that one must join them to one another, and the blessing over this one applies also to this one, since their essential matter is one, as explained. Therefore if he spoke he made them two mitzvos and must go back and bless, since the first blessing was with this intent, that he would not speak between them and that it would also apply to the tefillin of the head, and afterward he retracted from his intent. But one who reads in the Torah did not bless initially with intent that he would not interrupt his reading after three verses, for he is not obligated to do so. Therefore even if he spoke and interrupted after three verses, he need not go back and bless, since the intent of the first blessing was not voided. This is also the reason of the Rosh, who wrote in Perek Kisui HaDam that one need not go back and bless when he spoke between one bird and another, etc., and it is not comparable to tefillin, for both are one mitzvah. At first glance this is difficult, for the opposite seems reasonable, that with two mitzvos there is more interruption and diversion of the mind than when he spoke in the middle of one mitzvah. According to what we wrote, it is well understood and there is no difficulty. Examine this well. We have learned that those who practice reciting two blessings, one over the tefillin of the hand and one over the tefillin of the head, should say al mitzvos with a cholam, in the plural, since it refers to both, for they are two mitzvos, as explained in the Mishnah and Gemara of Perek HaTecheles. Even if he spoke between them and recites two blessings over the tefillin of the head, he should say al mitzvos with a cholam. It is not necessary to say this according to the Razah, whom the Ran cites in his name in the last chapter of Rosh Hashanah, that if he spoke he goes back and recites lehaniach, and he then handles the tefillin of his hand and strengthens the knot, and further recites al mitzvas, etc., and joins the two mitzvos to one another, where it is obvious that al mitzvos properly refers to both. Rather, even according to the Rosh, Mordechai, and Tur, who did not mention that he should handle the tefillin of the hand and strengthen the knot, nevertheless, since through putting on the tefillin of the head he completes and perfects the two mitzvos of tefillin that join to one another, al mitzvos properly refers also to the tefillin of the hand that he already put on. This is as we say at the end of the third chapter of Berachos, that if one did not recite HaMotzi, as long as he has not completed the meal he recites HaMotzi. Although it is not before performing it, even when he puts on both without interruption in between he also does not recite al mitzvos before performing the putting on of the tefillin of the hand, but recites al mitzvos afterward, and it also refers to the tefillin of the hand, even though it is not before performing it. Therefore one should say al mitzvos with a cholam, in the plural. So too it implies in the Rambam, chapter 2, to say al mitzvos, for he wrote because they are two mitzvos. However, if he has only the tefillin of the head alone, it is obvious that he should bless al mitzvas with the vav pronounced with a patach. From what I wrote it emerges that in such a case he recites two blessings, as the Rosh, Mordechai, and Tur wrote, since each one requires two blessings, and not like the Nimukei Yosef in Hilchos Ketanos, who wrote that he recites al mitzvas alone. The Beit Yosef already wondered about him in Orach Chaim, siman 26, for here he holds like Rabbeinu Tam, that if one spoke he recites two blessings, which refers to the tefillin of the head. Although I can resolve that the Nimukei Yosef holds that when one has only the tefillin of the head he should not recite two blessings, because how can one perform mitzvos in bundles, whereas when he spoke between them it nevertheless also refers here to the tefillin of the hand, as explained above. Still, we have already proven that the issue of performing mitzvos in bundles does not apply here, and therefore we do not rule like the Nimukei Yosef, but like the poskim mentioned above, and as written in the Shulchan Aruch in the cited siman. Furthermore, it emerges from what we wrote that if he has only the tefillin of the hand, he recites two blessings, since each one requires two blessings, as explained. So ruled the Semak, the glosses of the Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah. I do not know why the Tur ruled that over the tefillin of the hand alone he recites lehaniach and not al mitzvas, against the poskim mentioned, without any reason or proof, and he did not mention their view at all. If it is because we say that one who spoke recites two blessings over the tefillin of the head, and if so the tefillin of the hand remains with only the blessing of lehaniach, that case is different, for it is no longer possible to fix it. Alternatively, the blessing al mitzvos that he recites afterward over the tefillin of the head also refers to the tefillin of the hand, although he interrupted in between, for the reason we said above. If the Tur understood this from what the Rosh wrote, and so too the Mordechai in Hilchos Ketanos in the name of the Ri, that one who spoke also recites two blessings over the tefillin of the head, and so too if he has only the tefillin of the head, etc., and they did not write generally, if he has only one of them, which would imply both the tefillin of the hand and the tefillin of the head, even from this there is not such proof. Perhaps because they were speaking there about the tefillin of the head, they also mentioned the law of one who has only the tefillin of the head, and indeed the same applies when he has only the tefillin of the hand, as the Semag, the glosses of the Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah wrote explicitly. Perhaps because the Rif, Rashi, and Rambam ruled to bless over both only lehaniach, and Rabbeinu Tam did not explicitly disagree with them regarding the tefillin of the hand, but mentioned the tefillin of the head, he treated it as a doubt in blessings and ruled leniently. What appears to my humble opinion I have written, Yitzchak Levi.

<b>קדושת כתר. </b> פי' במקום שאנו אומרים נקדש במוסף אומרים במקצת מדינות כתר יתנו לך וכו' וכתב ב"י הטעם דאין נכון להיות באותה שעה כתר דתפילין עליו כדאיתא בשם הזוהר מביאו ב"י לענין תפילין בח"ה בסי' ל"א. ותמהני על מ"ש רמ"א שהמנהג לסלקם בכ"מ והלא בח"ה חמור טפי לענין תפילין שלא להניחם כמו שהאריך על זה ב"י בשם הזוהר ואפ"ה מניחין תפילין במדינות אלו ואפי' בברכה ואלו כאן משום קדושת כתר שיש במדינות אחרות נחלוץ תפילין ואדרבא  בהרבה דוכתי שאין מניחין תפילין מ"מ אין חולצין אם היו עליו כבר  וכאן נימא להיפך. ושמעתי בשם גדול הדור אחד שלא היה חולצם במוסף כיון שא"א כתר ע"כ נראה דהנוהג כן שאינו חולצן אין עליו תלונה על שהוא סותר המנהג כי יש לו על מי לסמוך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The Kedushah of Keser.</b> Meaning, in a place where we say Nekadesh in Mussaf, in some countries they say Keser yitnu lecha, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote the reason, that it is not proper at that time for the crown of tefillin to be upon him, as is found in the name of the Zohar, which the Beit Yosef brings regarding tefillin on Chol HaMoed in siman 31. I am astonished at what the Rama wrote, that the custom is to remove them in every place. Is not Chol HaMoed more stringent regarding tefillin, not to put them on, as the Beit Yosef elaborated on this in the name of the Zohar? Nevertheless, they put on tefillin in these countries, even with a blessing. Yet here, because of the Kedushah of Keser that exists in other countries, should we remove tefillin? On the contrary, in many places where one does not put on tefillin, nevertheless one does not remove them if they are already upon him, and here should we say the opposite? I heard in the name of one of the great men of the generation that he did not remove them during Mussaf, since we do not say Keser. Therefore it appears that one who practices this way and does not remove them has no complaint against him that he contradicts the custom, for he has upon whom to rely.
