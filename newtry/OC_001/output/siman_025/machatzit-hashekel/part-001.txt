(פי' דהגדול הוא במדינת הים ואומר הקטן שימתינו עד שיבא הגדול ממדינת הים) אין שומעים לקטן. אלא צריך הקטן לייבם או לחלוץ מיד. אע"ג דאם היינו ממתיני' עד שיבא הגדול היה נעשה המצוה מן המובחר דהא לכתחל' המצוה בגדול מ"מ עדיף שייבם או יחלוץ הקטן מיד דשהויי מצוה לא משהינן וכל שיש לקרב עשיי' המצוה עדיף טפי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 2) Available, and so it is in Yevamos.</b> Meaning, although if he were to wait until tzitzis were brought to him, he would perform the preferred mitzvah by blessing on the tzitzis before the tefillin, as written at the beginning of the siman, nevertheless he need not wait. Regarding this he wrote that so it is in Yevamos, where we learned: the mitzvah is for the eldest to perform yibbum, as it is written, “and the firstborn,” etc.; if there is no eldest, the younger performs yibbum. And we learned: if the younger made the matter depend on the elder until he comes from overseas, meaning the elder is overseas and the younger says to wait until he comes, we do not listen to the younger. Rather, the younger must perform yibbum or chalitzah immediately. Although if we waited until the elder came the mitzvah would be done in the preferred way, for initially the mitzvah is with the elder, nevertheless it is preferable for the younger to perform yibbum or chalitzah immediately, for we do not delay a mitzvah; whenever one can bring the performance of the mitzvah closer, that is preferable.

<b>And see Yoreh Deah, siman 232, seif 10.</b> Meaning, from this Maharam, in the responsum mentioned above, brought proof for his words. There it states in seif 7: if one saw from afar people eating his fruit and forbade it to them, and when he came near he saw that they were his father or his brothers, his vow is not a vow, even though he did not first explain at the time he prohibited it that this was his reason. Since they were far away and he thought they were not his father or brothers, nevertheless the matter itself proves that a person does not prohibit his fruit to his father and brothers. In seif 10 it states: if one swore not to do something because he thought it was a grave prohibition, and afterward it became known to him that it was not so severe a prohibition, the oath does not take effect at all, for it was in error. End quote. Maharam compared the case of his question to these two laws. Therefore the Magen Avraham concluded: it seems to me that release is required, since Maharam compared it also to seif 10, and there in seif 10 the Taz wrote that it implies from the Shulchan Aruch that even if he did not explain at the time of the oath that he swore because he thought it was a grave prohibition, nevertheless he does not need release. The Taz wondered why this differs from what is written there in seif 6, that if one said, “konam, my wife shall not benefit from me because she stole my purse or struck my son,” and it was found that she did not steal and did not strike, he does not need release. There we require specifically that he explain at the time of the vow that she stole, etc.; but if he vowed without specifying, even if he says that in his heart it was because she stole, etc., nevertheless he needs release. The same should apply in seif 10: if he did not explain that it was because he thought it was a grave prohibition, he should afterward need release. It is not comparable to seif 7, where even though he did not explain that he forbade them because they were not his father or brothers, nevertheless he needs no release, because that case is different, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote, since the matter itself proves that a person does not prohibit his fruit to his father and brothers, and it is as if he had explained. But in seif 6 and seif 10 we require specifically that he explain at the time of the vow, for the matter does not prove that one who vows against his wife does so because he suspected she stole; perhaps it was for another reason. So too in seif 10, perhaps even had he known it was only a light prohibition, he would still have sworn, and since he did not explain, he needs release. These are his words. Since Maharam compared the present law also to seif 10 there, according to the Taz here too release is required, since here also the matter does not prove. Although the Magen Avraham agrees that regarding the penalty money we do not collect from him, nevertheless regarding the prohibition he is concerned for the view of the Taz and requires release. In Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 6, he disagrees with Maharam’s law, for the intent of the Zohar that the Holy One, blessed is He, asks “where is My awe” applies only when one leaves the entrance of his house after day has already dawned and the mitzvah of tefillin and tzitzis already applies to him. But when he leaves before dawn, when it is still impossible to put on tzitzis and tefillin, there is no concern. He brings proof from the words of the author of Reishis Chochmah in his book Totzaos Chaim, and the verse “I will come to Your house... in Your awe” can be explained another way, as referring to the merit of the three Avos; see there. These are his words. One must say that the fact that the Ari of blessed memory was not among the first ten, his main reason was as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 90, seif katan 27: because he needed to relieve himself due to illness, and also so that he should go wrapped in tallis and tefillin. Thus one may say that his primary reason was that he needed to relieve himself, and the reason of going wrapped, etc., he made only as an additional support. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah.

<b>(ס"ק ט) על מצות בפת"ח.</b> דאי הוי בפת"ח הוא ל' יחיד ובחול"ם הוא לשון רבים כדאית' סי' כ"ו שכ' רמ"א לדידן דמברכים שתי ברכות על של ראש ושל יד ואם אינו מניח רק של ראש לבד מברך שתי ברכות להניח וגם ברכת על מצות דאי מניח שתיהן אז קאי ברכת להניח שבירך על של יד גם על של ראש ודי כשמברך על של ראש על מצות אבל כשאינו מניח רק של ראש כיון שעדיין לא בירך צריך לברך על של ראש גם ברכת להניח וכתב שם עוד אבל אם אינו מניח רק של יד לבד אינו מברך רק ברכת להניח לבד וא"א דברכת על מצות קאי גם על של יד א"כ היה ראוי כשמניח של יד לבד ג"כ לברך שתיהן להניח ועל מצות אע"כ דעל מצות לא קאי על של יד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 9) Al mitzvas with a patach.</b> For if it is with a patach it is singular, and with a cholam it is plural, as stated in siman 26, where the Rama wrote that according to us, who bless two blessings on the head tefillin and arm tefillin, if one puts on only the head tefillin alone, he blesses two blessings: lehaniach and al mitzvas. When he puts on both, then the blessing lehaniach that he recited on the arm tefillin also applies to the head tefillin, and it is enough that he blesses al mitzvas on the head tefillin. But when he puts on only the head tefillin, since he has not yet blessed, he must bless on the head tefillin also the blessing lehaniach. There he further wrote: but if one puts on only the arm tefillin alone, he blesses only the blessing lehaniach. If one could say that the blessing al mitzvas also applies to the arm tefillin, then when putting on only the arm tefillin he should also have to bless both, lehaniach and al mitzvas. Rather, it must be that al mitzvas does not apply to the arm tefillin.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The commandment of Hashem is clear, etc.</b> It uses mitzvas with a patach, and it refers to all mitzvos.
