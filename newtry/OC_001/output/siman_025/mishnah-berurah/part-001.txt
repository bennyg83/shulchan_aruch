**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the time of Shema and prayer.</b> Meaning, at least at the time of Shema and prayer, as below in siman 37, seif 2. The Gemara says that anyone who reads Shema without tefillin is as though he bears false testimony about himself, Heaven forbid. Tosafot explained that this is because he says “and you shall bind them as a sign,” etc., and does not bind. Although after the fact he fulfilled Shema, nevertheless he has another transgression, for he shows about himself that he does not want to fulfill the will of Hashem; this is the false testimony he bears about himself. There is another explanation; see the Levush. Sefer Chareidim wrote that from this we learn that when one says “and you shall love Hashem,” etc., he should see to it that he brings love of Hashem into his heart, so that he not be like one speaking falsely, Heaven forbid. Know that they said this only when he does so intentionally, being lazy about putting on tefillin before Shema. But one who has no tefillin, or is on the road and because of cold and chill cannot put on tefillin, and similar cases, certainly should not delay Shema past its time because of this. Levush in siman 58, and I copied his language there; see there.

<b>And it is good to say.</b> Because of the concern of a possible blessing in vain, as written in siman 206. This is not a complete doubt, for if so, how could one bless in a doubtful case of a blessing in vain and rely on saying Baruch shem kevod malchuso? Rather, we Ashkenazim hold as halacha like Rabbeinu Tam; only as an added measure, to remove ourselves from any possible question, we are accustomed to say Baruch shem kevod malchuso. One must be very careful not to say Baruch shem until after he has tightened the head tefillin properly on his head, for otherwise it will be an interruption between the blessing and the placement, and this blessing will certainly be in vain, and he will have to bless again. People stumble in this.

The Acharonim disagree and wrote that since the blessing lehaniach that he recited before putting on the arm tefillin also applies to the head tefillin, as stated above, as long as he has not yet put on the head tefillin he is still engaged in the mitzvah that belongs to that blessing, and the mitzvah over which he blessed is not yet completed until he puts on the head tefillin as well. Therefore, just as if the knot of the arm tefillin came loose before placing the head tefillin the Shulchan Aruch ruled that he need not bless again, the same applies if the knot broke, that he need not bless again. If the knot of the head tefillin or of the arm tefillin broke after he already put on and tightened the head tefillin, he must bless again. If no one there knows how to fix the knot and he needed to take other tefillin, in every case he must bless again, for it is comparable to the law in siman 206, seif 6.
