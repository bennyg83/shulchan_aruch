**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> This follows the explanation of Tosafot there, and depends on the variant readings. According to the reading in our books, “he returns and recites a blessing,” it can be explained only like Tosafot, for otherwise what is the question and what is the answer? So too in Tanchuma. But according to the reading of the Rif and Rambam, who do not read “he returns,” but rather “he recites a blessing,” it must be like Rashi. The view of Rashi, Rif, and Rambam is primary, and so the Rashba wrote in a responsum that their text is very correct, for it does not read “he returns.” There is decisive proof from what is stated in the Tosefta of Berachos, chapter 6: “When he puts them on, he says, Who sanctified us, etc., to put on tefillin.” This is a very strong difficulty against the explanation of Tosafot from the above Tosefta. There is a further difficulty from the same chapter of the Tosefta regarding the blessing over terumah and maaser: “If he was going to separate terumah and maaser, he recites one blessing,” and so the Rambam rules in the first chapter.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it became untied, etc., but, etc.</b> For it is like they slipped out of place, as above. But before placing the tefillin shel rosh, even if they slipped out of place, he need not recite the blessing again, for the first blessing still applies to the tefillin shel rosh. That is why if he spoke, etc. If so, the same applies where the knot broke, even after tightening, before placing the shel rosh; he need not recite the blessing again. This is unlike what was written initially, “and at the beginning of tightening,” etc. So too the Taz wrote. What the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 24, that making the knot is an interruption, is not so. If so, all the more at the beginning of tightening, as stated in Berachos 40a. The interruption between the blessing and the beginning of the mitzvah is greater than the interruption between one mitzvah and its companion or in the middle of the mitzvah. See above, siman 8, seif 14. Rather, certainly making the knot is not an interruption, for only speech or distraction is considered an interruption. If so, its law is like one who moved them.
