<b>אסרו גם בהנאה. </b> בטור כתב ואם קידש בו אשה אינה מקודשת   ונ"ל דזהו שמיירי בחמץ בין לרש"י ורמב"ם ובין לר"ת שמביא ב"י דאין פלוגתא בין רש"י לר"ת אלא בתרתי דרבנן דהיינו קודם סוף ו' שהזמן ההיא מדרבנן וחמץ נוקשה שהוא דרבנן אפי' בזה  לא חיישינן לקדושין ולר"ת דוקא בחדא דרבנן דהיינו או הזמן או החמץ וחדא דאורייתא אבל בתרתי דרבנן חיישינן לקידושין וא"כ הוה דברי הטור כאן לכ"ע דלא כב"י שכתב דכאן כתב אליבא דרש"י ורמב"ם אלא שהטור בא"ע ס"ס כ"ח כתב דאליב' דהרא"ש לשיטת ר"ת הזמן עיקר ואזלינן בתריה אי הוי דאורייתא או דרבנן והוא צריך עיון ויתבאר שם בסייעתא דשמיא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They forbade it also in benefit.</b> In the Tur it is written: if he betrothed a woman with it, she is not betrothed. It seems to me that this is because he is speaking of actual chametz, whether according to Rashi and the Rambam or according to Rabbeinu Tam as brought by the Beis Yosef. There is no dispute between Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam except where there are two rabbinic factors, namely before the end of the sixth hour, when that time is rabbinic, and chametz nukshah, which is rabbinic. Even there, according to Rashi and the Rambam we are not concerned for kiddushin; according to Rabbeinu Tam, only where there is one rabbinic factor, either the time or the chametz, and one Torah-law factor, are we not concerned for kiddushin, but where there are two rabbinic factors we are concerned for kiddushin. If so, the Tur's words here are according to all opinions, unlike the Beis Yosef, who wrote that here he wrote according to Rashi and the Rambam. However, the Tur in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 28 wrote that according to the Rosh, following the approach of Rabbeinu Tam, the time is the main factor and we follow it whether it is Torah-law or rabbinic. This requires analysis, and will be explained there with help from Heaven.

<b>ר"א כו'. </b> זה דעת ת"ה סי' קכ"א ודברי הרב תמוהין בזה דהכי איתא בת"ה שאלה הא דאוכלין כל ד' ולא יותר בשנת העיבור שהיום מאריך בגבולינו שבסוף ד' יש עדיין כמו ג' שעות עד חצות עד אימתי שרי לאכול חמץ אם <b>Some say, etc.</b> This is the view of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 121, and the Rav's words in this are astonishing. For this is what appears in Terumas HaDeshen: the question was that they eat all of the fourth hour and no longer; in a leap year, when the day is longer in our regions, at the end of the fourth hour there are still about three hours until midday. Until when is it permitted to eat chametz? Is it always until two hours before midday, or perhaps when four hours from the beginning of the day are completed, it is always forbidden to eat chametz? He answered: it appears from the halachah that it is always permitted to eat until two hours before midday. Although Rava concludes that the reason is because of a cloudy day, and the fourth hour is mealtime for everyone, and in this a person does not err, according to that answer one could say that even on long days the mealtime is always at the fourth hour from the beginning of the day, and after four hours mealtime is over and there is reason to decree because of a cloudy day. Nevertheless, since according to the first answer a person errs by two hours, one should never forbid except two hours before midday, as is proven there, etc., end quote. It is implied from his words that he means two of our hours, meaning that we calculate our hour as one twenty-fourth of the day and night. So too it appears from his words in siman 167 regarding the measure for salting meat, where he compares our hours to the hours in the Talmud. This is difficult, for if so, that which we learned that one may eat all of the fourth hour would also mean our ordinary hours. But are hours written in the Torah, that we should follow them? Rather, certainly the hours are proportional, as written in the first opinion, and this is the view of the Rambam. This is very strongly proven, for the prohibition of chametz and the slaughter of the Pesach offering are connected to one another, as the verse says, 'You shall not slaughter the blood of My offering upon chametz,' and the slaughter of the Pesach offering depends on the nature of the day, as stated at the beginning of the chapter Tamid Nishchat. There we say that on erev Pesach the tamid is slaughtered at seven and a half hours, and Rava explains that the time of the mitzvah of the tamid is from when the evening shadows incline, when the sun begins toward evening, etc. It is therefore explicit that we follow the day. Since midday according to the sun is called six hours, for we calculate every day, whether long or short, as twelve hours, those are the hours about which the Sages said that one may eat all of the fourth. Also, what the Sages said, that the fourth hour is everyone's mealtime, likewise means according to the nature of the day, for a person is accustomed to eat his meal at a third of the day, each day according to what it is, as is people's practice. Certainly the mealtime on a long day is not the same as on a short day, but depends on the nature of the day. If so, there is no question at all, for when the Gemara says a person errs by two hours, it means according to the hours of that day, as is explicit where it says: if so, let us also eat in the sixth hour; Rav Ada bar Ahavah said: in the sixth hour the sun stands at the horns, meaning in the middle of the sky, etc. Our ordinary hours have no relevance to the position of the sun. Therefore, also according to the first answer, that a person errs by two hours, it means proportional hours. When on a long day we calculate two proportional hours before midday, that is exactly a third of the day, and both are one and the same. The words of Terumas HaDeshen are therefore very astonishing, since he made the prohibition depend on our ordinary hours, and this certainly is not so. The Beis Yosef in siman 431 wrote that the words of Terumas HaDeshen accord with the Rambam's view. If so, he should have explained that the two hours mentioned by Terumas HaDeshen are proportional hours, but this certainly is not so, for then there would not have been two sides to his doubt, and Terumas HaDeshen would not have needed to analyze it. The Rema understood, as written in Terumas HaDeshen, that he was speaking of our ordinary hours; therefore he made from this two opinions here. But the truth is that the words of Terumas HaDeshen in this matter are not clear, for according to his view the words of the Tanna would depend on our ordinary hours. What relevance is there for us to make the prohibition depend on two of our hours before midday? Why did the Tanna give the measure of eating until four hours and no more? He should also have said until two hours before midday, which would be the same in all seasons. Rather, this is certainly a void matter, and all the hours are proportional. Heaven forbid that we should eat after a third of the day from dawn. So too my father-in-law, the Bach, ruled in siman 431, that one should be stringent like the first view here. In my humble opinion, the second view has no support at all, and the essential law follows the first view.

<b>ימכרנו לעכו"ם כו'. </b> ואלו הי' הבעל כאן עליו לבער כדלעיל סי' ת"מ בסופו ואם הלוה ישראל לחבירו על חמץ נר א' לכאורה דעל המלוה לבער דדוקא בעכו"ם אמרי' בסי' תמ"א דעכו"ם מישראל לא קנה משכון וכן להיפך אבל ישראל מישראל קנה משכון כדאי' בפ' כל שעה מדכתי' ולך תהי' צדקה ואם אינו קונה משכון צדקה מנ"ל וא"ה הוה של המלו' וראייה עוד דהא אם קידש אשה במשכון שהלוה עליו הרי היא מקודשת כמ"ש בא"ע אלא דנלע"ד דמ"מ הוה גוף המשכון של הלוה דהא אי' בפ' הפרה מביא בח"מ סי' ע"ב במשכנו של ישראל אצל גר ומת הגר ובא ישראל אחר והחזיק במשכון דחייב להחזירו לישראל מטעם שגוף המשכון של ישראל רק להגר היה שיעבוד עליו וכיון שמת פקע שיעבודו א"כ גם כאן חייב הלוה לבערו דהגוף שלו וכ"ת דאין בידו לבערו כיון שזה יש לו עליו חוב לא יתננו לו בלא מעות מ"מ אם אירע איזה ענין שלא ביערוהו והגיע זמן איסורו נראה דחייב הלוה לשלם מה שהלו' לו וההיזק של החמץ על הלוה כיון שהגוף שלו וכן אם מלוה מוכרו לעכו"ם בע"פ שאז החמץ בזול כמ"ש רש"י בפ"ק ד' י"ד במעשה דיוחנן חקוקה הוה ההיזק על הלוה כנלע"ד:

<b>He should sell it to a non-Jew, etc.</b> If the owner were here, he would have to destroy it, as stated above at the end of siman 440. If a Jew lent money to another Jew on chametz as a pledge, it appears at first glance that the lender must destroy it. For specifically regarding a non-Jew we say in siman 441 that a non-Jew does not acquire a pledge from a Jew, and likewise the reverse; but a Jew from a Jew does acquire a pledge, as stated in the chapter Kol Sha'ah from the verse, 'And to you it shall be righteousness.' If he does not acquire the pledge, from where would there be righteousness? If so, it belongs to the lender. There is further proof, for if he betrothed a woman with a pledge upon which he lent money, she is betrothed, as written in Even HaEzer. However, in my humble opinion it seems that nevertheless the body of the pledge belongs to the borrower, for it says in the chapter HaParah, cited in Choshen Mishpat siman 72, regarding a Jew's pledge that was with a ger, and the ger died, and another Jew came and took possession of the pledge, that he is obligated to return it to the Jew, because the body of the pledge belongs to the Jew; the ger merely had a lien upon it, and once he died, his lien lapsed. If so, here too the borrower is obligated to destroy it, for the body of it is his. And if you will say that it is not in his power to destroy it, since the lender has a debt upon it and will not give it to him without money, nevertheless, if some circumstance occurred such that they did not destroy it and the time of its prohibition arrived, it appears that the borrower is obligated to pay what the lender lent him, and the loss of the chametz is upon the borrower, since its body is his. Similarly, if the lender sells it to a non-Jew on erev Pesach, when chametz is cheap, as Rashi writes in the first chapter, folio 14, in the incident of Yochanan Chakukah, the loss is upon the borrower, as appears to me.
