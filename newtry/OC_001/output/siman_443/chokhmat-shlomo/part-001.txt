<b>Se'if 1.</b> Chametz from six hours and onward on the fourteenth is forbidden in benefit, etc. Note: the Tur concludes, 'And if he betrothed a woman with it, she is not betrothed.' The later authorities discussed this at length; see Chok Yaakov, who elaborated on it. I am astonished at all of them: how did they not see the Yerushalmi on this matter? This is the language of the Yerushalmi in Pesachim chapter 2, halachah 4: Rabbi Meir says, from six hours and onward it is rabbinic; Rabbi Yehudah says, from six hours and onward it is Torah law. What is Rabbi Meir's reason? For it is written, 'But on the first day' - this means the fifteenth. Could it mean from when it gets dark? The verse teaches, 'but on the first day.' How is this? Give him one hour before sunset, etc. From this there is a proof for the Rambam's view that the positive mitzvah of tashbisu applies before six, for just as according to Rabbi Meir the positive mitzvah of tashbisu applies before night, before the time of its prohibition, so too according to us, where it is forbidden according to the Torah at six, tashbisu applies before the time of its prohibition. Rav said: according to Rabbi Meir, one who betroths from six hours and onward has done nothing. And it is proper: these are hard desert-wheats, which the Korban HaEidah explains as chametz nukshah, etc. From the Yerushalmi it is explicit that nukshah is only rabbinic, and it is also explicit that one who betroths with chametz during rabbinic hours is not betrothed. It is also explicit that Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours and rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours are equal regarding kiddushin, and in both she is not betrothed. I am therefore astonished at the Ramban cited by the Beis Yosef and the Chok Yaakov, who holds that with rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours she is not betrothed, but with Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours we are concerned for kiddushin. If so, what proof does the Yerushalmi bring from rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours, where she is not betrothed, to Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours, where she is not betrothed? One could distinguish as the Ramban did, and this requires great analysis. While dealing with this I saw the Korban HaEidah there and the Shiurei Korban, s.v. 'and consecrated things do not effect kiddushin, for we do not redeem consecrated items in order to feed them to dogs.' He wonders there that it is hekdesh for Temple maintenance, etc. I am astonished at him: how did he miss the words of our Shas in Pesachim folio 29b, which says there that they dispute whether one redeems consecrated items in order to feed them to dogs, and Tosafos there asked the Korban HaEidah's difficulty and answered that rabbinically one does not redeem. This is astonishing. I have another difficulty with the Korban HaEidah's words from what it says there: if he made it terumah, it is not terumah, because terumah was given only for eating. I am astonished how he will explain the Mishnah cited by the Shas in Pesachim chapter 2, folio 33b, which is a Mishnah in Terumos: one may not separate from the tamei for the tahor, but if he separated unwittingly, his terumah is terumah. The answer of the Shas there does not apply here, for it speaks of ordinary chametz and according to Rabbi Meir. This all requires analysis. See Chok Yaakov in this siman, where he discussed at length Rashi's language in Bava Kamma folio 98b, why Rashi says the Gemara should have disputed regarding theft and did not mention a guardian. See what I wrote on this, with Hashem's help, in my work on Bava Kamma folio 98b; examine there carefully. Regarding the law of one from whom chametz was stolen, whether his nullification helps, see Noda BiYehuda Mahadura Kama, siman 20, which implies that his nullification helps. In truth, however, it is not so; see what I wrote to explain his words in my responsum to my friend Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Horowitz, in passing, in my work on Yoreh De'ah, Mahadura 5, siman 103. See also what I wrote about these words of Rashi cited by the Chok Yaakov in my novellae to Choshen Mishpat siman 363 in Magen Avraham, year 5603, where I discussed this sugya at length and rejected the words of the Dodi there at length; examine there carefully.

<b>There, se'if 3.</b> If they bought some other thing after six hours, it is permitted, because chametz does not seize its proceeds. Note: I have reflections here, with Hashem's help, and I will set them out. We learn in Bechoros folio 9a, where it asks: what is Rabbi Shimon's reason? We have not found anything that is forbidden while its redemption is permitted. It asks: but shevi'is, etc. It answers there that the last item alone is seized. I am astonished: why did it not ask from the Mishnah in Kiddushin chapter 2, that if he sold benefit-prohibitions and betrothed with their money, she is betrothed? If so, it is proven that the money of benefit-prohibitions is permitted while they themselves are forbidden. So too Rashi in the first chapter of Chullin, folio 2b, regarding 'because they exchange,' wrote there that there is nothing that seizes its money except hekdesh, idolatry, and shevi'is; but chametz, its money is permitted while it remains forbidden. At first I wanted to say that the intent is that we have not found an item that is forbidden and through redemption becomes permitted while its redemption is also permitted. That we have not found. If so, there is no proof from all benefit-prohibitions, because they remain in their prohibition. But if so, it is difficult why the Gemara asks from shevi'is, for shevi'is also remains in its prohibition forever, as Rashi explained there, that the fruit itself remains in its prohibition. At first it arose in my mind to resolve that from here there is a proof that benefit-prohibitions have no monetary value and there is no sale at all, as the Sha'ar HaMelech wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that they have no money; see there at length. If so, the matter works well: with petter chamor, since it is permitted through redemption, it has monetary value; likewise shevi'is, since at least until the time of biur it is permitted in eating, it has monetary value. Therefore the Gemara properly asks that it is forbidden while its redemption is permitted. But these items that are forbidden forever and are forbidden even now have no monetary value at all; therefore this is not called their money, and the money is permitted. But this is difficult from the sale of a sefer Torah by the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the people of the city, where the money is permitted, and yet it has value and a sale, while the sefer itself is forbidden and the money is permitted. This requires analysis. See what I wrote in Tashmishei Kedushah in the laws of a synagogue, siman 153, in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5612, in a responsum to the community of Borshtin; examine well. Regarding the law explained in Shulchan Aruch, that if they bought something with chametz after six hours it is permitted because chametz does not seize its proceeds, this is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, that chametz does not seize its proceeds. His proof is from the sugya in Chullin, that the chametz of transgressors is immediately permitted because they exchange, etc. In my earlier novellae I wondered about him according to what Tosafos wrote in Avodah Zarah, and as we rule in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah simanim 132 and 144, that where the non-Jew first performed meshichah on the wine and afterward gave money, the money is permitted. If so, what proof is there? Perhaps chametz always seizes its proceeds, and there we say he would not leave permitted food and eat forbidden food, so presumably the non-Jew first performed meshichah on the chametz and only afterward the Jew took the exchange. Now, however, I saw that Rabbeinu Tam's words are compelled in the Shas, for necessarily 'because they exchange' means its simple sense. To give the non-Jew the chametz first is not included in 'he does not leave permitted and eat forbidden,' because he is afraid that the non-Jew will not pay him afterward, and he does not trust the non-Jew in this. Know that otherwise it is difficult from Nedarim folio 58b, regarding one who weeds with a non-Jew, etc., where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: if the Jew is suspected regarding shevi'is, etc. The Gemara suggests perhaps it refers to crushed produce suspected of shevi'is. At first glance, why does it matter that he is suspected? Since it is possible to use crushed produce, we should say he does not leave permitted and eat forbidden, and he is no worse than an apostate out of appetite, where we say he does not leave permitted, etc. But there is no difficulty, for one does not trouble himself where there is effort, as written in Chullin; if so, to crush is an effort and he will not trouble himself. So too it appears from the precise wording of the Ran there. Yet it remains difficult: there is an explicit baraisa regarding the chametz of transgressors, that it is permitted because they exchange; this proves that their way is to exchange in this way because he does not leave permitted. If so, what proof is there regarding growths of permitted produce nullifying the prohibited produce? Perhaps they never nullify, and there the reason they are permitted, even though he is suspected regarding shevi'is, is that we say he exchanged it with produce of a non-Jew, as with the chametz of transgressors, and therefore we assume that he exchanged it with a non-Jew's produce. But this is an error. Regarding chametz, the permissive argument that they exchange helps because chametz does not seize its proceeds. But with shevi'is, which seizes its proceeds, the exchange would be like the original item; why would the fact that they exchange help? If, however, we say that what is stated regarding the chametz of transgressors, that it is permitted because they exchange, means that the non-Jew first takes the chametz and afterward gives the money, then shevi'is too would be permitted in such a case. After all, the main proof of the poskim and Tosafos in Avodah Zarah that if the non-Jew first performed meshichah on the prohibited item the money is permitted is from this sugya, where the house of Rabbi Yannai would borrow produce in the seventh year and repay in the eighth. If so, it is proven that with shevi'is too it is permitted where he first took the shevi'is item and afterward took the exchange. Then let us say that here too this is what happened, that he exchanged it by first giving the shevi'is item to the non-Jew and afterward taking other produce; what proof is there that growths of permitted produce nullify the prohibited item? Perforce, in Avodah Zarah they are suspected, and their way is not to give the produce until they receive the money; therefore with shevi'is he is suspected of this, and the exchange does not help because shevi'is seizes its proceeds. With chametz, which is permitted, it must be because it does not seize its proceeds. Rabbeinu Tam's proof is therefore sound. If his intent were that he gives the chametz first, it would be difficult: what is the difference between chametz and shevi'is, and from where does the Shas know that growths of permitted produce nullify, etc.? Perhaps the intent is that they exchange, as above, like chametz. Thus Rabbeinu Tam's proof works well; examine carefully. Regarding the law stated in the Shas that the chametz of transgressors is immediately permitted because they exchange, I wrote in my earlier novellae why the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh omitted it. Now I saw in Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 115, at the end, s.v. 'Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote,' that he brings in the name of Baal HaItur a novel explanation of this baraisa, but his words are not understandable at all. See my novellae to Yoreh De'ah there, where I explained his words anew and also settled the Rif and Rambam, who omitted it; examine there. See Chok Yaakov, who distinguishes between clothing, utensils, and fruit, and money, saying that the money is permitted according to all opinions. See my responsum to Russia, printed in the laws of Pesach, siman 448, in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5616, folio 128a, where I brought a clear proof unlike the Chok Yaakov, that there is no distinction: all are exchanges of chametz and are forbidden. The questioner also wrote to me that the Peri Megadim disagrees with him, and likewise the Gaon Rabbi Shlomo Zalman in Shulchan Aruch disagrees with him. I have not examined their words, but what is correct and clear is as I wrote, with Hashem's help; examine well.
