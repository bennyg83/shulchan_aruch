<b>אסרוהו</b> עט״ז הב״י כתב דהטור סותר מכאן לאה״ע סי׳ כ״ח דכאן כ׳ כרש״י ורמב״ם ושם כר״ת והט״ז כתב דכאן מיירי בחמץ גמור (חסר תיבת גמור) וכ״כ הב״ח. וסיים הט״ז דמ״מ צ״ע מ״ש באה״ע כ״ח דלר״ת דמפרש משש ולמעלה משבע עיקר הוא הזמן ושעות דרבנן אף בחמץ ד״ה מקודשת זה צ״ע. ואפשר שזה מ״ש הב״י כאן ובשם הרמב״ן יע״ש. עח״י ו׳ ובע״ש נוקשה בה׳ ואסור באכילה ובשש בהנאה הניח בצ״ע. עח״י ג׳ ורש״י ז׳ א׳ אם נאמר דחיטי קורדנייתא נוקשה מבואר זה ועמ״א. וערש״י כ״א ב׳ ד״ה משש ח״י ו׳ ופני יהושע שם וי״ל דה״פ דר״י אמר הלכה כסתם משנה (וסובר דהלכה כמשנה ריש א״ע בנוקשה ותערובות ומתני׳ שיאור ישרוף מ״ח ב׳ לאו סתמא ר״י קאמר לה) וכתב שפיר דאי משבע קאמר פשיטא ומאי קמ״ל וכבר אמר הלכה כסתם משנה ונוקשה אע״ג בפסח גופא לדידן דרבנן ואף למ״ד ד״ת לפני זמנו דרבנן עתוס׳ ריש פסחים אפ״ה גזרו שש וחמש משום ל״פ. ותערובות פחות מס׳ בה׳ ודאי אסור באכילה ואי שרי להוסיף עד ס׳ עי״ד צ״ט בט״ז י״ב ולקמן תרע״ז. ומשש עד הלילה בפחות מס׳ אם מוכר חון מדמי איסור שבו צ״ע עסי׳ תמ״ז במ״א מ״ה לאחר הפסח מקילין הואיל וקנסא משא״כ קודם זמנו עד הלילה דהוי מ״ה ואף בו׳ איסור דרבנן א״ד דאסור ויבואר אי״ה לקמן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They forbade it.</b> See the Taz. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Tur contradicts from here to Even HaEzer siman 28, for here he wrote like Rashi and the Rambam, and there like Rabbeinu Tam. The Taz wrote that here he speaks of actual chametz; the word 'actual' is missing. So too wrote the Bach. The Taz concluded that nevertheless what is written in Even HaEzer 28 requires analysis, for according to Rabbeinu Tam, who explains 'from six and onward' as from seven, the time is the main factor, and rabbinic hours even by actual chametz would make the kiddushin valid. This requires analysis. Perhaps this is what the Beis Yosef wrote here and in the name of the Ramban; see there. See Chok Yaakov s.k. 6 and Olas Shabbos. Regarding nukshah in the fifth hour, which is forbidden in eating, and in the sixth in benefit, he left the matter requiring analysis. See Chok Yaakov s.k. 3 and Rashi 7a: if we say that wheat of Kordanya is nukshah, this is explained, and see the Magen Avraham. See Rashi 21b, s.v. 'from six,' Chok Yaakov s.k. 6, and Pnei Yehoshua there. One can say that according to Rabbi Yehudah he stated the halachah like an anonymous Mishnah, and he holds that the halachah follows the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter Ein Ovrin regarding nukshah and mixtures, and that the Mishnah 'si'ur must be burned' follows Rabbi Yehudah; and he wrote well that if it meant from seven this would be obvious and what would he teach us? He already said the halachah follows an anonymous Mishnah. Nukshah, even on Pesach itself, is rabbinic for us, and even according to the view that a half-measure before its time is rabbinic, see Tosafos at the beginning of Pesachim, nevertheless they decreed six and five because of 'do not confuse.' A mixture that has less than sixty is certainly forbidden in eating in the fifth hour; whether one may add to make sixty, see Yoreh De'ah 99 in Taz s.k. 12 and below siman 677. From six until night, where there is less than sixty, if he sells it excluding the value of the forbidden part, this requires analysis; see siman 447 in the Magen Avraham s.k. 45. After Pesach we are lenient because it is a penalty, unlike before its time until night, where benefit is forbidden. Even with an item whose prohibition is rabbinic, one should say that it is forbidden, and this will be explained below, with Hashem's help.

<b>והוי</b> יודע ב׳ קדירות בחמש א׳ איסור דרבנן ואחד היתר ונפל חמץ פחות מס׳ וא״י להיכן בה׳ מותר לאכול שאני אומר ובשש שתיהן אסורות דכל שאתה מקלקלו מעט מה חזית וה״נ בשש מקלקלו בהנאה ואם נשאר התבשיל מה׳ לשש צ״ע אי אמרינן כבר הותר די״ל דדמי להלך בא׳ ועשה טהרות ואכלו והלך בב׳ טהורים שתיהן קיימות שתיהן תלויות אע״פ שכבר נטהרו הא חזרו לאיסורן ער״מ ז״ל מה׳ שא״ה פי״ט ה״ה כאן עמ״ש בפריי לי״ד קי״ד ואי״ה בסי׳ תמ״ז אבאר עוד בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is.</b> One knows of two pots in the fifth hour: one contains a rabbinic prohibition and one contains permitted food, and chametz less than sixty fell in, and it is not known into which one it fell. In the fifth hour it is permitted to eat, for I say it fell into the prohibited pot. But in the sixth hour, when both are forbidden, for any small amount with which you spoil it - what did you see to distinguish? So too here in the sixth hour, one spoils it with respect to benefit. If the dish remained from the fifth hour into the sixth, it requires analysis whether we say it was already permitted. One can say that it is like a case where one walked on one path and handled taharos and ate them, and then walked on the second path; when both pure items still exist, both remain in suspense. Even though they had already been declared pure, they return to their prohibition. See the words of the Rama there, and this is not like the Pri Chadash. See what I wrote in Pri Megadim to Yoreh De'ah siman 114, and in siman 447 I will explain this further, with Hashem's help.

**** ENGLISH ****
And some say. See the Taz. The words of Terumas HaDeshen can be understood as his doubt: when four hours are completed, where each hour is one twenty-fourth of a day and night, is it forbidden to eat; or is it permitted to eat until two hours before midday, ordinary equal hours; or until four proportional hours? For example, if the day is long, eighteen hours, perhaps it is permitted to eat until the fifth hour and no more, or until the end of the seventh, or until the end of the sixth. He concluded that until two ordinary hours before midday it is permitted to eat, namely two hours each of which is one twenty-fourth of a full day. Although according to the second answer, the fourth hour is mealtime, etc., nevertheless according to the first answer, that a person errs by two hours, meaning according to Abbaye in Rava's view, according to Rashi, testimony is given over to careful people but chametz is given over to everyone, and all of this is that they do not err more than two hours between the sun in the east and west, etc. This requires analysis, for we rule regarding testimony like the Rama wrote in chapter 2 of the laws of testimony, halachah 5, and as the Kesef Mishneh wrote there, that according to Rabbi Yehudah in Sanhedrin 40a, by testimony it is only two hours. If so, even according to the latter answer, two ordinary hours before midday should be permitted, as stated. See what is written in the Magen Avraham s.k. 3 and Taz s.k. 1. In the Beis Yosef the reason is because of a cloudy day, and he did not conclude that the fourth hour is mealtime for everyone. The Taz questioned him: how can the Mishnah teach that one may eat all of the fourth and leave it pending all of the fifth, meaning two ordinary hours before midday; if so, one may eat more than four hours? One can answer that he spoke of an average day, and the difficulty is somewhat like what the Pri Chadash wrote there and resolved: are hours written in the Torah? Once the Torah divided the day, half of it is forbidden and half of it is permitted, whether it is a long day or a short day, everything depends on the inclination of the sun. The same is true for the two hours that Chazal added: they too are proportional hours. One can say this somewhat. What the Beis Yosef wrote, that Terumas HaDeshen is like the Rambam and holds that hours are proportional, whether the day is long or short, as the first view here, means that Terumas HaDeshen's conclusion, that until two hours before midday is permitted, refers to proportional hours, to exclude the notion that once four ordinary hours have been completed it is forbidden. According to this, Terumas HaDeshen is exactly like the first view here. The Taz's objection, that what was doubtful to Terumas HaDeshen was obvious, for all hours mentioned in the Talmud are proportional and no proof from dialectic was necessary, is addressed by the Pri Chadash, who wrote that the Beis Yosef meant what Terumas HaDeshen wrote in siman 1 in the name of Tosafos regarding Ma'ariv, that hours mentioned in the Talmud are proportional; see there. What the Taz wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen 167 is the proof there regarding salting, that an hour is one twenty-fourth of a day and night, from the fact that in the beginning of Tamid Nishchat we say that on erev Pesach they advance one hour for the Pesach because of roasting. He also challenged that ordinary hours in the Shas are proportional. One can say his proof is correct, for we see that even when the day is short in Nisan, one ordinary hour, one twenty-fourth of a day and night, suffices; hence the measure of roasting is one twenty-fourth of a full day. As a matter of halachah, see Magen Avraham s.k. 3 regarding the sale of chametz: if there is more than an hour, one twenty-fourth of a day and night, until midday, he may sell, since it is only rabbinic. However, one can say that it is a Torah doubt, as written in Terumas HaDeshen and the Pri Chadash there in Responsa Rabanei Venezia, that one who sells in the sixth hour and one who keeps chametz on Pesach transgresses bal yera'eh even though it is not his, and see Knesses HaGedolah in Talmud Torah. One can distinguish: regarding nullification it does not help, but with a sale, since there are hands acquiring it, he no longer transgresses on Pesach, as written in the Magen Avraham s.k. 6, and all the more so if he sells the room and the chametz to a non-Jew. According to the latter view, and as the Pri Chadash wrote in Toras Moshe, it is like Jewish chametz in a non-Jew's house, etc. Although we do not rule that way, nevertheless it is a kind of double doubt and is permitted. That they did not make a safeguard for the positive mitzvah of bal yera'eh was written there in the Magen Avraham. See Rashi, Pesachim 4b, the Rosh there, and Pnei Yehoshua. Also, one who keeps it with the intention to destroy it may not transgress; see Tosafos, Pesachim 29b, s.v. 'Rav Ashi.' As stated, they did not make a fence for destruction.
