(א) אסור בהנאה - מדאורייתא ויליף בגמרא מדכתיב לא תאכל עליו חמץ כלומר על קרבן הפסח וקבלו חז"ל דר"ל לא תאכל חמץ משעה שראויה לשחיטת הפסח דהיינו מחצות היום ואילך שהוא בין הערבים ומשעה שאסור באכילה אסור בהנאה אכן עדיין אין על אכילתו חיוב כרת עד הלילה וכן לענין בל יראה ובל ימצא הסכימו כמה פוסקים דאין עובר במה שמשהה החמץ משש שעות ומעלה כ"ז שלא הגיע ימי הפסח גופא מדכתיב שבעת ימים שאור לא ימצא בבתיכם וגו' ולא יראה לך שאור בכל גבולך שבעת ימים ומ"מ אף שאינו עובר בבל יראה עובר בכל רגע ורגע שמשהה החמץ בביתו על מ"ע דחשביתו שאור מבתיכם דקאי על ע"פ משש שעות ולמעלה כמבואר בש"ס:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) <b>Forbidden in benefit.</b> This is according to the Torah. The Gemara derives it from the verse, 'You shall not eat chametz upon it,' meaning upon the Pesach offering, and Chazal received that this means: you shall not eat chametz from the time that is fit for slaughtering the Pesach offering, that is, from midday onward, which is bein ha'arbayim. From the time that it is forbidden in eating it is forbidden in benefit. However, there is still no liability of kareis for eating it until night. Likewise regarding bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, several poskim agreed that one does not transgress by keeping chametz from six hours and onward so long as the days of Pesach themselves have not arrived, for it is written, 'For seven days leaven shall not be found in your houses,' etc., and, 'Leaven shall not be seen by you in all your borders for seven days.' Nevertheless, even though he does not transgress bal yera'eh, he transgresses every moment that he keeps chametz in his house the positive mitzvah of tashbisu se'or mibateichem, which refers to erev Pesach from six hours and onward, as explained in the Shas.

(6) <b>They forbade it also in benefit.</b> This is a decree because of the seventh hour, when it is forbidden in benefit according to the Torah. Therefore at that point it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew, to give it to him as a gift, or to feed it to an animal, beast, or bird. If there is a deceased person to bury on erev Pesach in the morning, if there is time to bury him and time will remain for eating chametz, they should bury the deceased before eating, for it is not proper for the members of the burial group to eat before burying the deceased, as explained in siman 526 in the Taz, s.k. 6. But if the time is pressed and it is possible that no time will remain for eating, it is better to eat first, then perform the destruction of chametz at its time, and afterward attend to the needs of burying the deceased.

(ז) לפי ענין היום - היינו דלעולם חשבינן היום לשתים עשרה שעות וממילא בימים הארוכים משערים בשעות גדולות ולפיכך בשנת העיבור מותר לאכול עד שליש היום אע"פ שהיום ארוך יותר משתים עשרה שעות שלנו כגון שמגיע לשליש היום חמש שעות ודע דלפ"ז ה"ה במדינות שהימים שלפני הפסח קצרים מחלקין הי"ב שעות לשעות קטנות ואינו רשאי לאכול רק עד שליש אותו היום:
**** ENGLISH ****
(7) <b>According to the nature of the day.</b> This means that we always calculate the day as twelve hours, and consequently on long days we reckon with long hours. Therefore, in a leap year, it is permitted to eat until a third of the day even though the day is longer than twelve of our hours, such as when a third of the day arrives after five hours. Know that according to this, the same applies in countries where the days before Pesach are short: the twelve hours are divided into small hours, and one is permitted to eat only until a third of that day.

(ט) וי"א עד ב' שעות וכו' - היינו שעות בינוניות שכל אחת א' מכ"ד במעל"ע וע"כ אפילו אם היום ארוך מותר לאכול יותר משליש היום והיינו עד סוף שעה עשירית מחצות הלילה וכן לענין מכירת החמץ מותר לדעה זו עד סוף שעה אחת עשרה וכתבו הפוסקים שהעיקר כדעה הראשונה ואין לאכול כ"א עד שליש היום וכן לענין מכירת חמץ שזמנו שעה אחת יותר חשבינן שעות זמניות ומ"מ בדיעבד אם לא מכר עדיין וההפסד מרובה יש לסמוך אדעה אחרונה ויש לו שהות למכרו עד שעה קודם חצות:
**** ENGLISH ****
(9) <b>And some say until two hours, etc.</b> This means ordinary equal hours, each one being one twenty-fourth of a full day and night. Therefore, even if the day is long, it is permitted to eat more than a third of the day, namely until the end of the tenth hour from midnight. Likewise, regarding the sale of chametz, according to this view it is permitted until the end of the eleventh hour. The poskim wrote that the main law follows the first view, and one may eat only until a third of the day. Likewise regarding the sale of chametz, whose time is one hour longer, we calculate proportional hours. Nevertheless, after the fact, if he has still not sold and there is a great loss, one may rely on the latter view, and he has time to sell it until one hour before midday.

(יב) חייב לבערו וכו' - דעת מ"א דאם היה באפשר לו למכרו ונתעצל ולא מכרו עד שהוצרך לבערו חייב לשלם אח"כ דמי הפקדון למפקיד אף שהוא ש"ח דפשיעה הוא דהו"ל למוכרו קודם זמן איסורו ובח"י מחלק בזה בין אם הוא ש"ח או שומר שכר ורוב אחרונים מסכימים דבין שומר חנם ובין שומר שכר פטור דזה לא נכנס בכלל שמירה שלא קיבל עליו אלא לשמור את החפץ שיהא ברשות בעליו ושיחזירנו לו בשלימות אבל לא קיבל עליו למוכרו שהרי אדרבה במכירה זו מוציאו מרשות בעליו ואינו צריך למוכרו אלא מטעם מצות השבת אבידה ולא מצינו שמחוייב לשלם מי שאינו משיב אבידה. מיהו אם החמץ הוא ממושכן אצלו ועבר שעה ה' ולא מכרו ואח"כ כשהגיע זמן איסורו הוכרח לשרפו בזה מסתברא דהפסיד חובו עכ"פ וכדעת המ"א דדעת כל ממשכן הוא בודאי שכל זמן שלא יחזיר לו המלוה משכונו לא יוכל לתבוע ממנו חובו אא"כ יהיה המלוה אנוס באבדת המשכון כגון שיגזלו ממנו לסטים וכי"ב משאר אונסים אבל כאן שהיה אפשר להמלוה למכרו קודם הפסח ולא מכרו הרי עצלותו גרמה הפסד המשכון שנאסר בהנאה ואין זה אונס [ופשוט דאם מכרו בע"פ אע"פ שמכרו בזול אין עליו כלום וההפסד הוא על הלוה. ט"ז] מיהו אם המשכון הוא בעין כגון שלא בערו המלוה לאחר זמן הביעור והשהה אותו עד לאחר הפסח אפשר דיכול לומר לו הרי שלך לפניך אע"פ שאינו שוה עכשיו כלום דחסרון שאין ניכר הוא ולא הפסיד חובו:
**** ENGLISH ****
(12) <b>He is obligated to destroy it, etc.</b> The view of the Magen Avraham is that if it was possible for him to sell it and he was lazy and did not sell it until he had to destroy it, he is afterward obligated to pay the value of the deposit to the depositor, even though he is an unpaid guardian, for this is negligence, since he should have sold it before the time of its prohibition. Chok Yaakov distinguishes in this matter between an unpaid guardian and a paid guardian, but most later authorities agree that whether he is an unpaid guardian or a paid guardian, he is exempt, for this was not included in the guardianship: he accepted upon himself only to guard the object so that it should remain in its owner's domain and that he should return it to him intact, but he did not accept upon himself to sell it. On the contrary, through this sale he removes it from its owner's domain. He must sell it only because of the mitzvah of returning a lost object, and we have not found that one who does not return a lost object is obligated to pay. However, if the chametz was pledged with him, and the fifth hour passed and he did not sell it, and afterward when the time of its prohibition arrived he was forced to burn it, in this case it stands to reason that he has lost his debt in any event, as the Magen Avraham holds. For the understanding of anyone who takes a pledge is certainly that as long as the lender does not return the pledge to him, he cannot claim his debt from him, unless the lender was forced by circumstances beyond his control in the loss of the pledge, such as if bandits stole it from him, or the like among other unavoidable circumstances. But here, where it was possible for the lender to sell it before Pesach and he did not sell it, his laziness caused the loss of the pledge, which became forbidden in benefit, and this is not an unavoidable circumstance. It is obvious that if he sold it on erev Pesach, even though he sold it cheaply, there is no claim against him, and the loss is upon the borrower, as the Taz writes. However, if the pledge is still intact, such as where the lender did not destroy it after the time of destruction and kept it until after Pesach, perhaps he can say to him, 'Here is yours before you,' even though it is now worth nothing, for this is a deficiency that is not visible, and he has not lost his debt.

(15) <b>After six hours.</b> That is to say, even though at that point it is forbidden in benefit according to the Torah according to most poskim, and if so by buying it he transgresses a Torah prohibition, nevertheless after the fact it does not seize its proceeds. For we have not found that a prohibition takes effect on the item exchanged for it except regarding idolatry, hekdesh, and shevi'is. According to this, the same is true where the prohibition is only rabbinic, as explained in Chullin folio 4b in Rashi and Tosafos. It also stands to reason, according to this, that if he sold it at the beginning of the sixth hour, when its prohibition is also rabbinic, or even on Pesach itself, it likewise does not seize its proceeds.

**** ENGLISH ****
(16) <b>It is permitted.</b> This means even for the very person who bought it, and all the more so that others may benefit from that item. Know that the view of the Mechaber is not agreed to by all, for there are among the great poskim those who maintain that even though others may benefit from the exchange of chametz, because chametz does not seize its proceeds after the fact, nevertheless the Sages penalized the owner of the chametz himself that he should not benefit from the money, since he committed a prohibition. It is permitted for him only where others sold or exchanged his chametz without his knowledge. One should be stringent like these poskim where there is no great loss. Know further that even according to this view, if he transgressed and bought some item with the money, it is permitted to benefit from that item, for it is an exchange of an exchange, and is permitted. The later authorities further wrote that even according to the stringent view regarding chametz, forbidding its exchange to the owner himself, this applies only when he took the exchange for the chametz at the time that he gave the chametz. But if he took the exchange for the chametz later, according to all opinions it is permitted even for him, for once the non-Jew took the chametz into his domain, the chametz was acquired by him immediately through his taking it, and what was given to the Jew afterward, whether its money or its exchange, is merely a gift and has no connection at all to the chametz, and is not called its exchange.

(יז) אינו תופס דמיו - והחמץ עצמו לעולם עומד באיסורו בכל מקום שהוא ואסור לכל אדם ליקח אותו מהעכו"ם אפילו חזר העכו"ם והחליפו באחר ואפילו מכר הישראל להעכו"ם אחר הפסח דכיון דחכמים הטילו איסור עליו הרי הוא כחתיכה דאיסורא. כתב ח"י דגן שנתחמץ ועבר עליו הפסח ונזרע גדוליו מותרין דגדולין הוי כחליפין ובספר מקור חיים חולק עליו ודעתו דגדולין גרע מחליפין ולכו"ע אסור ועיין בתשובת ח"ס או"ח סימן ק"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
(17) <b>It does not seize its proceeds.</b> But the chametz itself always remains in its prohibition wherever it is, and it is forbidden for anyone to take it from the non-Jew, even if the non-Jew went back and exchanged it for something else, and even if the Jew sold it to the non-Jew after Pesach. Since the Sages placed a prohibition upon it, it is like a piece of forbidden food. The Chok Yaakov wrote that grain that became chametz, Pesach passed over it, and it was then planted, its growths are permitted, for growths are like exchanges. In the work Mekor Chaim he disagrees with him, and his view is that growths are worse than exchanges and are forbidden according to all opinions. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim siman 104.
