<b>ס"א חמץ משש כו' בהנאה. </b>הרמב"ם והרא"ש וכ' המ"מ שכן הסכימו כל הגאונים ואע"ג דלאחר זמנו קי"ל כר"ש בלפני זמנו קי"ל כר' יהודה דסתם מתני' כוותיה בר"פ כל שעה עבר זמנו אסור בהנאתו וכן רבי פסק כוותיה שאמר בחמישית צא ומוכרה לא"י ש"מ שמכאן ואילך אסור דאל"כ ה"ל להמתין עוד דאוקמיה שם כת"ק דאמר אפי' הן אבודין כו'. ועברא"ש וראיה דר"נ ורבא פסקו בפ"ב כר"ש לאחר זמנו בלפני זמנו פסקו כר"י דתולין כל חמש וע"ש י"ג א' ואע"ג דר"נ משמיה דרב קאמר ורב אף לאחר זמנו כר' יהודה ע"ל שם כ"ט ב' מ"מ ר"נ לא ס"ל אלא כר"ש ועפ"ג דשבת מ"ז א' ומי א"ר יוחנן הכי כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1, chametz from six hours, etc., in benefit.</b> The Rambam and the Rosh; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that all the Geonim likewise agreed. Although after its time we rule like Rabbi Shimon, before its time we rule like Rabbi Yehudah, for the anonymous Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Kol Sha'ah follows him: once its time passed, it is forbidden in benefit. Rebbe also ruled like him, for he said: in the fifth hour go out and sell it to a non-Jew. This implies that from then onward it is forbidden, for otherwise he should have waited longer. Also, the Gemara there establishes it like the first Tanna, who says even if they are lost, etc. See the Rosh. A proof is that Rav Nachman and Rava ruled in chapter 2 like Rabbi Shimon after its time, while before its time they ruled like Rabbi Yehudah, for one may leave it pending the whole fifth hour; see there, 13a. Although Rav Nachman said it in the name of Rav, and Rav himself holds even after its time like Rabbi Yehudah, see there 29b, nevertheless Rav Nachman himself holds only like Rabbi Shimon; see chapter 3 of Shabbos 47a, 'But who said that Rabbi Yochanan holds so,' etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even his chametz, etc.</b> Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halachah 2: During Pesach, what is the law? Rabbi Yirmiyah said it is permitted; Rabbi Yose said it is forbidden. Rabbi Yose challenged: but was it not taught that a Jew may not rent his animal to a non-Jew to bring chametz upon it? He answered him: that is where he comes with him. But was it not taught that a Jew may not rent his house to a non-Jew to place chametz in it? You can say: that is where he lives with him. The sugya concludes like Rabbi Yose. Even according to the Tosefta in chapter 2, where it is taught there that a Jew may rent his donkey to a non-Jew to carry chametz on it from place to place, nevertheless it is evident that it is forbidden in benefit, for otherwise what would it teach us? After all, the wage for benefit-prohibitions is permitted, except for wine of nesach, as stated in Avodah Zarah 62; see there. So too it appears in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, 28 and 29, where it says there: who is this? If it is Rabbi Yehudah, he speaks of chametz generally, even of a non-Jew. The same is true according to Rabbi Shimon within its time, for within its time he does not dispute Rabbi Yehudah, and he too speaks of chametz generally. Rav Assi bar Yaakov, who holds there that a non-Jew's chametz is permitted during Pesach, retracted, as stated there: he retracted, etc. Rabbi Abba bar Yaakov said that according to all opinions, something that causes, etc. And the one who says that one commits me'ilah holds like Rabbi Shimon; hence even according to Rabbi Shimon, one commits me'ilah only because it is something that causes, but during Pesach it is forbidden.

<b>Se'if 3, if they bought, etc., for chametz.</b> As written in Chullin folio 4 regarding the chametz of those who transgress, etc.; and even according to Rabbi Yehudah, as written there. Although they said there, perhaps it is Rabbi Shimon, that is because we do not assume that they exchanged it. Rashi explained there that even on Pesach it is permitted, as written in the s.v. 'because.' And 'some say,' etc.: initially it is certainly forbidden, as Tosafos writes there, and the Ran in Nedarim, from that which is taught in chapter 2 of Pesachim: one who eats terumah that is chametz, etc. The Rosh in Nedarim there wrote from what is stated in Pesachim 28b, 'wherever it is said,' etc., in the same way that it specified regarding a neveilah to a resident alien through sale; hence by prohibitions of benefit it is forbidden. But after the fact, according to Torah law it is certainly permitted, as written in Kiddushin 58a and Avodah Zarah 54b, 62a. Rabbinically, however, it is forbidden to himself, as written on 47b in Rami bar Chama's question, etc. The Rosh and the Ran wrote that the same applies to all prohibitions of benefit, from the fact that they wanted to resolve it from the case where one sold them and betrothed, etc. Although in kiddushin he benefits from them himself, see Tosafos, Avodah Zarah 54b. The Rosh wrote in Kiddushin there that kiddushin with their money is an exchange of an exchange. So too wrote the Ran in Nedarim 57a, s.v. 'with an item,' and he proved from here that an exchange of an exchange is permitted. This is what is stated on 47b: perhaps initially, etc.; meaning, initially to exchange the exchange, but if he exchanged them, they are permitted even to himself. But for another person even the first exchange is permitted, as written in Chullin, as above. This is what the Tur means when he says that the maidservant bought, etc., and he brought proof from Chullin and did not bring proof from Kiddushin. But the Ran in Chullin there wrote that even to himself it is permitted, and he brought proof from the Yerushalmi, which said that if he betrothed with their money she is betrothed, because their money is not forbidden. He means where the money came into his hand by theft, and it was not considered proceeds of the prohibition at all. They said there: Rabbi Chaggai said, this teaches that one can betroth with stolen property. So ruled the Rambam in chapter 8 of the laws of forbidden foods, halachah 16, and in chapter 13, halachah 15. Although the Ran's proof is not decisive, for one can say that nevertheless to himself it is forbidden because of a penalty, the Ran follows his own approach, for in Nedarim there he resolved the question leniently, and the question there concerns the person himself, as stated above. So too the Ran wrote there, and regarding another person he was not even in doubt, etc.; this is unlike the Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah siman 216, who wrote that according to the Ran it is forbidden even to his fellow. He wrote that only about one who vowed a prohibition upon himself; see there. So too the Semag wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak. But the Rambam at the end of chapter 5 of the laws of vows wrote: this is a doubt; therefore it is forbidden to use their exchanges, but if he transgressed and benefited, he has benefited. One must say that what is written there, 'initially,' etc., refers only to vows, but with other prohibitions of benefit, even initially it is permitted to benefit from the exchanges, as Tosafos asked, for in Kiddushin he benefits initially. The questioner thought that even after the fact it is forbidden because it is an exchange of prohibitions of benefit; therefore he resolved it from Kiddushin. He rejected that the question concerns initial conduct, because it is similar to the exchanges of hekdesh, as the Ran writes. This is the meaning of 'initially.' If it referred to the Mishnah in Kiddushin, it should have said: perhaps after the fact yes, but initially no. Therefore the Rambam ruled simply that with vows it is forbidden even to another person who did not exchange it, and so the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh De'ah siman 216 se'if 2 ruled simply, etc. But with other prohibitions, even to himself it is permitted. See Tosafos, Pesachim 21a, s.v. 'all,' and there 22b, s.v. 'and Ever.' So too the Shulchan Aruch ruled simply here, and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch below in siman 450 se'if 4.
