**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Things that invalidate <i>tzitzis</i> — it contains three clauses.</b><br>If all the strings of the corner tore but enough remained to tie all the torn strings together, it is valid; if there did not remain enough to tie — even for one string that tore entirely — it is invalid. Therefore since each [strand] is folded to two, if two ends tore it is invalid lest one string tore entirely. According to our practice at making <i>tzitzis</i> to mark [such that] the four ends are always on one side of the knot and four ends on the other — if two ends on one side tore it is valid, for surely they are two strings and more than <i>kedei aniva</i> remains from the second end of each. Rabbenu Tam validates only if two complete strings remain — four ends each twelve thumbbreadths long — then when the other two strings tear, if enough remains to tie it is valid; but if three strings tore even if enough remains to tie it is invalid. Therefore when three ends were cut, if one did not take care when making the <i>tzitzis</i> that the four ends on one side of the knot be recognizable, we worry perhaps each end is from one string and only one complete string remains — hence [with] doubt it is invalid. Rema: All the more so if one marked the four ends on one side and three ends on one side tore — invalid, for then surely three strings tore; if three tore on two sides also invalid lest three strings [tore]. But if only two ends tore we validate with <i>kedei aniva</i>; the halacha follows the first view, but where possible it is good to heed Rabbenu Tam. Rema: And we practice like Rabbenu Tam (<i>Agudah</i> siman 15).

**** ENGLISH ****
Where <i>kedei aniva</i> is required but because the threads are thick one cannot tie them — were they thin there would be enough to tie — it is valid. Rema: We gauge with medium threads (his own words).

**** ENGLISH ****
<i>Kedei aniva</i> for Rashi is measured from the <i>anaf</i> [free-hanging threads]; for Ri even if the entire <i>anaf</i> was cut and only from the <i>gdil</i> [<i>knot windings</i>] enough remains to tie, it is valid; the world’s custom follows Rashi, and where impossible one may rely on Ri.
