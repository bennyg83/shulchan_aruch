**** HEBREW ****
<small>(ט"ז סק"א)</small> וקשה למה אמר יש בהם. דאלו בשני ראשים שיש כ"ע ע"י צירוף לא מהני וא"כ אין לפרש לשון בהם ע"י צירוף די"א אין לחוש שמא חוט א' מהם אע"כ כוונת הטור דצריך בכ"א כ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Taz, seif katan 1.</small> It is difficult why the Tur says, “they contain.” If two ends together retain enough to tie a bow, that does not help. The phrase therefore cannot mean by combining them, even according to the view that one need not suspect that they are ends of one string. It must mean that the Tur requires each one independently to retain enough to tie a bow.

<small>שם</small> וע"ק לשון אפילו. ר"ל מ"ש הטור ולפ"ז אפילו כו' הא לדיעה קמייתא פשיטא דכשר אפילו נפסק ח' חוטין ונשתייר כ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> There is an additional difficulty with the word “even.” Meaning, the Tur writes, “According to this, even, etc.” Yet according to the first view it is obviously valid even if all eight ends were severed, provided that enough remained to tie a bow.

**** HEBREW ****
<small>מ"א סק"ג</small> יש להסתפק. היינו הב"י עצמו נסתפק בזה ולכן כ' בש"ע שנפסק כולו דבה"ג פסול ודאי ובנשאר ע"י צירוף פסול רק מספק ועיין ט"ז סק"א ומש"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 3.</small> “The matter remains doubtful.” Beit Yosef himself was uncertain about this. He therefore writes in the Shulchan Aruch that if it was entirely severed it is certainly invalid, whereas if the two ends together retain enough to tie a bow it is invalid only because of doubt. See Taz, seif katan 1, and what is written there.
