<b>(12) And Hallel is recited with omissions.</b> The entire Hallel is not recited because Scripture states, "The song shall be for you as on the night when a festival is sanctified." We expound that a day sanctified as a festival, meaning that melacha is forbidden, requires song, while a day not sanctified as a festival does not require song. Arachin 10a. Our ancestors nevertheless customarily recited it, and in order to make it recognizable that its recitation is not required by law, passages are omitted. The widespread custom is to omit from "Not to us" until "Hashem remembered us," and from "I love, for Hashem hears" until "What shall I return." Some omit different passages. Rambam. See Eliyah Rabbah for the reason for our custom.

**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(16) Nevertheless, a person should be careful, etc.</b> This is in order to satisfy the opinion of the poskim who hold that an individual does not recite a blessing over it. The later authorities therefore write that if one arrives at the synagogue near the time of Hallel, he should first recite Hallel with the congregation and afterward pray. They further write that if he is in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, he should interrupt to recite Hallel with the congregation, and this is not considered an improper interruption, for it is no worse than the psalms added on Shabbos. However, he should not recite a blessing over Hallel, either before or after it. He fulfills the need for blessings through Baruch SheAmar, which he already recited at the beginning, and Yishtabach at the end. All this applies to Hallel on Rosh Chodesh, regarding which some poskim hold that no blessing is recited. On days when the entire Hallel is recited, however, when all agree that he is obligated to recite a blessing, he should not recite it in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, because he would thereby lose its blessings.

(ב) To the place he erred — meaning if he skipped one verse and remembered afterward, he should not say it at the place he remembered, for that is reading out of order; rather he begins from that verse and says in order until the end; and if he does not know where he erred, he returns to the beginning of the section where doubt fell.
