(א) המבשל וכו' - אחד המבשל את המאכל או את הסממנין או המחמם את המים [ר"ל אף דמים ראוי לשתיה בלא בישול וה"ה חלב מ"מ חייב כיון דמשתבח ע"י הבישול] ואחד האופה את הפת הכל ענין אחד הוא [דאפיה הוא מענין בישול אלא שאפיה הוא בפת ובישול הוא בשארי דברים]. המתיך אחד ממיני מתכות כל שהוא או המחמם את המתכות עד שתעשה גחלת ה"ז תולדת מבשל [שהרי בחימומו הוא מרככו שיהא ראוי לתקנו ולעשות ממנו כלי והרי הוא מרפה דבר הקשה] וכן הממסס את הדונג [שעוה] או את החלב או את הזפת והכופר והגפרית וכיוצא בהן ה"ז תולדת מבשל וחייב וכן המבשל כלי אדמה עד שיעשו חרס חייב משום מבשל [דקודם שמתחזק הכלי ונצרף בהכבשן ונעשה חרס כראוי מתבשל בה טיט של הכלי] וכן הנותן חתיכת עץ בתנור כדי שיתייבש וידוע שמתחלה הוא מתרפה ויוצא ממנו הלחלוחית חייב משום מבשל כללו של דבר בין שרפה גוף קשה באש או שהקשה גוף רך ה"ז חייב משום מבשל [רמב"ם בפ"ט] וע"כ יש ליזהר מאד שלא להניח עצים לחים על התנור ליבשן אחר שקיעת החמה דהוא חשש דאורייתא. הניח בשר ע"ג גחלים אם נצלה צליה גמורה כשיעור גרוגרת חייב ואפילו הגרוגרת אינה במקום אחד אלא מתלקט משנים ושלשה מקומות ג"כ חייב ואפילו אם לא היה צליה גמורה רק כמאב"ד ג"כ חייב אלא דבזה שאינו צליה גמורה צריך שיהיה הבשר צלוי משני הצדדים של הבשר דוקא וא"ל פטור [כן כתב הרמב"ם שם בהלכה ה'] וכ"ז הוא לענין חטאת אבל איסורא יש בכל גווני וכן מה שכתב הרמב"ם מתחלה לענין צליה השיעור גרוגרת הוא ג"כ רק לענין חיוב חטאת אבל איסור דאורייתא יש בכל גווני כדקי"ל חצי שיעור אסור מן התורה [שבת ע"ד גמ' ורש"י שם]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(א) One who cooks, etc. — whether one cooks the food or the spices, or heats water [meaning even though water is fit to drink without cooking, and the same applies to milk — nevertheless he is liable since it improves through cooking], and whether one bakes bread — all are one matter [for baking is a matter of cooking, except baking is in bread and cooking is in other things]. One who melts any kind of metal, or heats metals until they become coals — this is a toldah of cooking [for through heating he softens it so it is fit to be worked and made into a vessel, and behold he softens something hard], and likewise one who melts wax [tallow], or milk, or pitch, sulfur, and the like — this is a toldah of cooking and he is liable; and likewise one who cooks an earthenware vessel until it becomes pottery is liable for cooking [for before the vessel is strengthened and fired in the kiln and becomes proper pottery, the clay of the vessel is cooked therein], and likewise one who places a piece of wood in an oven so it will dry — and it is known that at first it softens and moisture comes out of it — he is liable for cooking. The general principle: whether he hardened something hard with fire or hardened something soft — he is liable for cooking [Rambam ch. 9]. Therefore one must be very careful not to place wet wood on the oven after sunset to dry them, for this is a d'oraisa concern. If he placed meat on coals: if it was fully roasted to the measure of a dried fig he is liable — even if the dried fig is not in one place but collected from two or three places he is also liable; and even if it was not fully roasted but only as cooked food he is also liable — except that when it is not fully roasted the meat must be roasted on both sides of the meat specifically, and if not he is exempt [as Rambam wrote there in halacha 5]. All this is for a chatas; but there is prohibition in every case. And likewise what Rambam wrote initially regarding roasting — the measure of a dried fig is also only for chatas liability; but there is d'oraisa prohibition in every case, as we rule that half a measure is forbidden from the Torah [Shabbos 74b Gemara and Rashi there]:

(ה) מותר למו"ש מיד - ואפילו למי שנתבשל בשבילו דלא בעינן להמתין בכדי שיעשו אלא במלאכה הנעשית ע"י א"י בשביל ישראל משום דקל בעיניו איסור דאמירה לעו"ג ויבוא לעשות כן פעם אחרת כדי שיהיה מוכן לו במו"ש מיד אבל דבר שנעשה ע"י ישראל בידים ודאי ליכא למיחש דעי"ז שנתיר למו"ש מיד יבא פעם אחרת לומר לישראל לבשל לו בשבת בשביל זה ועוד שהישראל לא ישמע לו דאין אדם חוטא ולא לו. אם היה במו"ש יו"ט אפילו בישל בהיתר כגון שבישל בשבת לחולה מ"מ אסור לבריא גם ביו"ט ראשון דאין שבת מכין ליו"ט. אם נתערב בשבת אותו דבר שנתבשל במזיד באחרים דעת המ"א דאינו מתבטל ואסור לו בשבת ליהנות מזה דכיון דלמו"ש יהיה מותר לאחרים מקרי דבר שיש לו מתירין דאינו בטל אפילו באלף ומה שכתב ביו"ד סוף סימן קי"ב היפוך זה נדחק המ"א לישבו ועיין בחות דעת שם שחולק עליו וכ"ז להמבשל בעצמו אבל לענין אחרים לכו"ע מקרי דבר שיש לו מתירין:
**** ENGLISH ****
(ה) Permitted at Motzei Shabbos immediately — even for one for whom it was cooked on Shabbat, for we do not require waiting until it is done; only in melacha done through a non-Jew for an Israelite do we fear, because it is light in his eyes — prohibition of telling a non-Jew, and he will come to do so another time so it will be ready for him at Motzei Shabbos immediately. But something done through an Israelite with his hands — certainly there is no concern that because they permitted it at Motzei Shabbos immediately he will come another time to tell an Israelite to cook for him on Shabbat for this reason; and moreover the Israelite will not listen to him, for a person does not sin when it is not for his benefit. If it was Motzei Shabbos of Yom Tov — even if he cooked permissibly, such as he cooked on Shabbat for a sick person — nevertheless for a healthy person it is forbidden also on the first day of Yom Tov, for Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov. If on Shabbat that thing cooked intentionally became mixed with others — Magen Avraham's view is it is not nullified and forbidden for him on Shabbat to benefit from this, since at Motzei Shabbos it will be permitted for others it is called something that has permitters and is not nullified even in a thousand. And what was written in Yoreh De'ah end of siman 112 is the opposite — Magen Avraham strained to reconcile it; see Chavat Da'at there who disagrees. All this is for one who cooked himself; but regarding others, according to all it is called something that has permitters:

(צ) That forbidden to soak it — even in kli sheini; and the same if it will become fit to eat through rinsing in hot water alone — rinsing is also forbidden, as above regarding salted fish. Now we will explain the law of cooking tea leaves relevant in several matters in this seif. Tea on Shabbat — obvious in poskim that there is cooking concern; in mezid there is stoning prohibition, and in shogeg chatas liability; therefore one must be very careful, and unfortunately many stumble on leniencies without substance; therefore I must explain modes of permission and prohibition there's help. Pouring from kli rishon onto tea leaves — certainly primary melacha concern, for we rule pouring cooks to the depth of a peel as below seif 10; and all the more if he then stands it on the oven or in coals until yad soledes bo — certainly all will come to stoning prohibition thereby. Even if he wishes to place the tea leaves into the vessel after pouring the hot water so it will be kli sheini — also forbidden as we rule in this seif that something not in hot water before Shabbat may not be soaked on Shabbat even in kli sheini; and all the more according to what is explained in this seif that there are soft things easy to cook even through rinsing from kli sheini — possible there is d'oraisa prohibition concern in submerging even this way; therefore later authorities agreed one should pour erev boiling from kli rishon so through this the tea will be called partially cooked, for pouring cooks to the depth of a peel and at the time of pouring will turn the tea well inside the boiling from top to bottom [better that it be truly cooked through standing in a cooking place]. Then pour the infusion into another vessel so the tea leaves remain dry and it will be permitted to pour hot water from kli rishon on them on Shabbat since they were already cooked erev, as we rule in this seif for dry things there is no cooking after cooking even if cooled [and afterward permitted to return also this cold infusion water into this vessel itself, as we wrote above that something moist that cooled may be placed in boiling kli sheini], for if the infusion liquid remains, we rule that in moist things there is cooking after cooking if cooled, unless the cold infusion waters are abundant so they will not heat through the water poured tomorrow. The same law for coffee — be careful not to pour hot water from kli rishon unless he poured yesterday and poured off the water from upon them, for the reason as all above [see responsa Chatam Sofer siman 74 and Pithei Teshuvah on Orach Chayim]. Know that even though we permitted pouring hot water on tea leaves and coffee if he placed water from kli rishon yesterday — nevertheless be careful not to stand the tea and coffee on the oven or in coals after pouring water, for pouring only cooks to the depth of a peel and therefore it was not fully cooked yesterday but only to a peel, and now through standing in the oven it will cook fully. This mode we explained — even though one should not protest those who practice it — nevertheless later authorities wrote better advice: prepare the infusion completely erev so he need not pour more boiling on Shabbat; tomorrow when he needs to drink he places cold infusion into the cup he drinks from after they poured hot water into it and it becomes kli sheini; and the same permitted to place in this kli sheini cup milk that cooled; but forbidden to pour from kli rishon on them, as below in this seif. When his infusion is not cold it is certainly good to satisfy all views this way [Pithei Teshuvah on Orach Chayim]:
