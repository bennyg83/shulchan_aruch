<b>סעיף א'  </b>בסוכות תשבו שבעת ימים וכו'. נ"ב הנה יש לספק בדין אם הי' לפניו שתי עיירות באחת יש סוכה ובאחת לולב לאיזה עיר הולך או אם הי' במאסר ונתנו לו רשות לצאת לקיים מצוה אחת אם יקיים סוכה או לולב. והנה לכאורה הי' נראה דסוכה קודם דהוי קודם בזמן דזה חל בלילה וזה ביום. אך בחידושי לתורה בדרוש לסוכות שנת תקצ"ז העליתי דלולב קודם מכח דהוא פרסומי ניסא כמ"ש במדרש פ' אמור שבו נודע שישראל זכו בדין והוכחתי כן מן התורה דהקדים לולב לישיבת סוכה להראות כן ע"ש שהארכתי בזה בעזה"י ודו"ק ועיין בט"ז כאן סק"א מ"ש בטעם מ"ש רש"י כר"א ולא כר"ע ועיין בחידושי תורה שלי בשנת תק"צ בדרוש לסוכות מ"ש סמוכין לזה מן הש"ס ממה דקיי"ל סוכה שבעה לברכה ע"ש ודו"ק: (שם) הם ענני כבוד שהקיפם בהם לבל יכם שרב ושמש. נ"ב הנה ראיתי דבר תמוה בפירש"י במס' סוכה די"ו ע"ב ד"ה ואד יעלה מן הארץ וז"ל וסוכה מענני יליף שהרי זכר לענני כבוד הוא כדכתיב כי בסוכות הושבתי ומתרגמינן ארי כמטלית עננא וכו' והוא תמוה למה הוצרך רש"י להביא התרגום הרי זה מפורש אח"כ בברייתא שהביא הש"ס תוך כ"ד שר"ע אומר כי בסוכות הושבתי היינו ענני כבוד ולמה לי' לרש"י **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1</b> You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days, etc. Note: There is room to be uncertain about the law if two cities were before him, in one there is a sukkah and in one there is a lulav: to which city should he go? Or if he was imprisoned and they gave him permission to leave to fulfill one mitzvah, should he fulfill sukkah or lulav? At first glance it would seem that sukkah comes first, because it comes first in time, for this obligation begins at night and that one by day. However, in my Torah novellae, in the derashah for Sukkos in the year 5597, I concluded that lulav comes first because it publicizes the miracle, as written in the Midrash, parashas Emor, that through it it became known that Israel won in judgment. I proved this from the Torah, for it placed lulav before sitting in the sukkah to show this; see there, where I discussed this at length with God's help. Consider it. See the Taz here, s.k. 1, regarding the reason Rashi wrote like Rabbi Eliezer and not Rabbi Akiva. See my Torah novellae from the year 5590, in the derashah for Sukkos, where I wrote support for this from the Talmud, from what we rule that sukkah is seven days for blessing; see there and consider it. There, they are the clouds of glory with which He surrounded them so that heat and sun not strike them. Note: I saw a puzzling matter in Rashi's commentary in Sukkah 16b, s.v. and a mist would rise from the earth. This is his wording: and sukkah is learned from clouds, for it is a remembrance of the clouds of glory, as it is written, for I caused them to dwell in sukkos, and we translate: for with clouds of glory, etc. This is puzzling: why did Rashi need to bring the Targum? It is explicit immediately afterwards in the baraita that the Talmud brings within a few words, that Rabbi Akiva says, for I caused them to dwell in sukkos, means the clouds of glory. Why did Rashi need to rely on the Targum and bring proof from afar? This requires much examination. See the Magen Avraham in this siman, what he wrote in the name of the Knesses HaGedolah, that one must have intention when sitting in it. See what the Peri Megadim wrote on the Shulchan Aruch, and see my work on Orach Chaim, still in manuscript, in siman 639, where I wrote about this; see there and consider it well. There, in the gloss: and it is a mitzvah to prepare the sukkah immediately after Yom Kippur. Note: The reason he attached this to the previous point appears to be that they are connected. In the Yerushalmi in the first chapter of Yoma it says: and the cloud of incense shall cover; I do not know what this covering is, but when it says, You covered all their sins, selah, this is a covering of the eye. According to this, everything Israel does for the Holy One, blessed be He, the Holy One, blessed be He pays them measure for measure, as written in the Talmud and Midrash Vayeira and Yalkut. So too here: He, may He be blessed, cannot look upon wrongdoing, for it is not His will, so to speak, to bear the sins of the house of Israel. But on Yom Kippur, when Israel repents, they cover their sins from Him, may He be blessed, and He casts them into the depths. Therefore it says, and the cloud of incense shall cover the kappores, for Israel covers their sins from Him, may He be blessed. Corresponding to this covering that Israel covers before Him, may He be blessed, with the cloud of incense, the Holy One, blessed be He, immediately repays them on Sukkos, to cover them from stream and rain and from the punishment of Gehinnom. Therefore it is proper to make the sukkah immediately after Yom Kippur, for this flows from that. Since the sukkah alludes to the clouds of glory that protected them from heat and sun, with which He, may He be blessed, protected them, it is fitting to make it after Yom Kippur, because this flows from Yom Kippur, measure for measure. This works well; consider it carefully.
