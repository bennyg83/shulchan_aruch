<b>(ס"ק א) לומר כו'</b> שהוא נהנה כו' ב"י. עמ"ש הטור בשם רבינו יהור' שמותר לומר לגוי לרחוץ התינוק אפי' בחמין שהוחמו בו ביום. דסתם תינוק חולה אצל חמין. ודבר שאין בו סכנה אומר לגוי ועושה עכ"ל. ועז"כ הרב"י משמע דישראל עצמו אסור לרחצן. אף על גב דלהאכילו מותר לישראל בעצמו. שאני רחיצה דאם רחצן ישראל הרי גם הגדול שרוחצן נהנה. ואם כן ה"ה לסוכן ישראל בידים אסור. והא מותרים לכתחלה כו' דאי' ביומא דף ע"ח ע"ב ת"ר תינוקו' מותרים בכלן חוץ מנעילת סנדל. ופריך מ"ש נעילת סנדל דאמרי אינשי עבדי לי'. ר"ל גדולים עשו לו. וגדולים מוזהרים על הקטנים שלא יעשו להם דבר האסור הנך נמי אמאי מותרים אמרי גדולים עבדו להו. ומשני הנך אמרי מאתמול עבדו להו ר"ל שרחצן וסכן אתמול ועדין הם נקיים. אבל סנדל א"א לומר דמאתמול נעלוהו ועדין הם ברגלי הקטן. דע"כ בליל' חלץ מנעליו דאין לישן אם מנעליו ברגליו דטועם טעם מיתה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if katan 1) To tell, etc.</b> That he derives benefit, etc. Beis Yosef. See what the Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yehudah, that it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to wash an infant even with hot water heated that day, because an ordinary infant is considered sick regarding hot water, and for something involving no danger one may tell a non-Jew and he does it. End quote. About this the Beis Yosef wrote that it implies that a Jew himself may not wash them. Although feeding him is permitted for a Jew himself, washing is different, for if a Jew washes him, the adult who washes him also derives benefit. If so, the same applies to anointing: it is forbidden for a Jew to do it with his hands. But they are initially permitted, etc. It is stated in Yoma 78b: The Rabbis taught, children are permitted in all of them except wearing shoes. It asks: what is different about wearing shoes? People will say, adults did it for him, meaning adults put them on him, and adults are warned regarding minors not to do for them a forbidden act. But these other matters too, why are they permitted? People will say adults did it for them. It answers: regarding those, people will say they did it for them yesterday, meaning they washed or anointed them yesterday and they are still clean. But regarding shoes, one cannot say that they put them on yesterday and they are still on the child's feet, for necessarily he removed his shoes at night, since one should not sleep with shoes on his feet, for he tastes the taste of death.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it asks: But it teaches that they are initially permitted.</b> Rashi explained: if one comes to ask, we tell him, 'feed him and wash him,' and then it is well known that he did it today. End quote. It concludes: rather, those things that are not the child's growth, the Rabbis decreed regarding them; those that are the child's growth, the Rabbis did not decree. End quote of the Gemara. Necessarily the Talmud is not speaking through a non-Jew. First, the phrase 'initially permitted' does not imply that. Second, it is impossible that it speaks through a non-Jew, for what would the question have thought, that for this reason wearing shoes is forbidden because it was necessarily done for him today and people will assume adults did it for him? It is possible to attribute it to being done by a non-Jew. Rather, necessarily the Talmud is not speaking of a non-Jew. The initial thought was that it was permitted only when the child does it himself, and adults do not stop him; only the Sages were concerned that people would err and think adults did it for them, since the child himself has no understanding to do it alone. To this it responds that they are initially permitted, which implies even an adult may do it.

**** ENGLISH ****
A negative prohibition is more severe than a positive commandment, as stated in the first chapter of Yevamos in the sugya of 'aleha,' and as is also proven in the second chapter of Yevamos. End quote of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 94. Therefore, since eating in a sukkah is only a positive commandment, even giving them food is permitted. However, the Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 269 rejected this, according to what we rule that even a rabbinic prohibition may not be fed directly to a minor, all the more so a Torah positive commandment. Therefore one must say that what he wrote here, 'furthermore, there it is a positive commandment,' leaves his first answer primary: sukkah is different, because in the eating itself there is no prohibition. In truth, directly feeding a minor outside the sukkah is forbidden even for a woman, and so he wrote explicitly below in siman 640. Rather, after he previously distinguished that on Yom Kippur the eating itself is prohibited, he now writes another reasoning: even if Yom Kippur were exactly comparable to sukkah in this respect, there is more room for leniency with sukkah, because it is a positive commandment, than with Yom Kippur. However, the Terumas HaDeshen at the end of the responsum challenged the Tosafos mentioned above and proved from Eruvin that the mother too is obligated to train her son, and he concluded that one should not be lenient; see there.

<b>משום דהכל לומדים כו'</b> וסמכינן על. דעת רש"י בכתובות דף נו"ן ע"א כפי פרושו של הב"ח דברי רש"י ע"ש אבל כשהוא בן י"ב שנים שלימים ונכנס לשנת י"ג מודה רש"י וגם הב"ח דצריך להשלים מדרבנן אם לא בנער כחוש דסמכינן על דעת י"א שהביא רמ"א. ובס' א"ר כתב בשם הב"ח על מ"ש רמ"א ויש לסמוך עלייהו בנער שהוא כחוש. וז"ל ומנהגנו בכל התינוקות כן כיון שלומדים כלם. וגם בזה"ז מסתמא חשיבי חולים (ב"ח) באמת הב"ח לא הקיל כ"א כשהו' בן י"ח כנ"ל. ואפשר דבס' א"ד כתב כן מסברא דנפשיה ע"פ סברת הב"ח. ועוד דסחמא חשיבי חולים. ר"ל בזמן הזה דירדה חולשה לעולם. וסיים הב"ח וכיון דלא בקיאים אימת ספק נפשות להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because everyone studies, etc.</b> We rely on Rashi's view in Kesubos 50a according to the Bach's explanation of Rashi's words; see there. But when he is twelve complete years old and enters his thirteenth year, Rashi, and also the Bach, agree that he must complete the fast rabbinically, except for a thin youth, where we rely on the 'some say' opinion brought by the Rema. In Eliyah Rabbah he wrote in the name of the Bach on what the Rema wrote, 'and one may rely on them regarding a youth who is thin': our custom is so with all children, since they all study, and also nowadays they are ordinarily considered sick. In truth, the Bach was lenient only when he is eleven, as above. It is possible that in Eliyah Rabbah he wrote this from his own reasoning based on the Bach's reasoning. And also because ordinarily they are considered sick, meaning nowadays, when weakness has descended to the world. The Bach concluded: since we are not expert when it is danger to life, in a doubt involving life we rule leniently.
