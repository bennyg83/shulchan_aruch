**** ENGLISH ****
[3] <b>Levush: Each according to, etc.</b> So too the Turei Zahav and Magen Avraham explained what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, 'at five and at six,' that each child is according to his sharpness. I am astonished, for this is the language of the Rambam in chapter 6, and the Kesef Mishneh explained: at five when his father is in the city, and at six when his father is not in the city. This implies even if he is not sharp, for if he is sharp, even at four he is not drawn after his mother, as written at the end of siman 414 and as explained in Eruvin 82. With this, everything the Turei Zahav wrote in explaining the Tur is rejected, and it also does not fit the sugya there; examine carefully, for I have been brief.

[ח] <b>[לבוש] ומכל מקום בלילה ראשונה וכו'.</b> הקשה מורי ורבי מוהר"ר הלמן זכרונו לברכה, אם כן מאי פריך בגמרא על רבא דאמר מצטער פטור מסוכה והתנן חולין ומשמשיהן פטורים חולה אין ומצטער לא, דילמא לכך תני חולה דאפילו בלילה ראשונה פטור. ונראה לי דהכי פירושו ליתני נמי מצטער ובשאר לילות כמו דתנא בסיפא אוכלין עראי חוץ לסוכה וזה על כרחך אינו בלילה ראשונה דחייב אפילו בכזית. ועוד נראה לי אפשר דבאמת חולה פטור בלילה ראשונה ומצטער לאו דוקא נקט דזיל בתר טעמא דיליף מחג המצות ואפילו בחולה שיש בו סכנה הוא מצד עצמו ולא מצד סוכה ואם הוא מצד הסוכה אין לך דבר שאינו עומד בפני פיקוח נפש. אך קשה לי על הפוסקים שהביא בית יוסף דמצטער פטור באכילה רק בשינה אם כן מאי פריך בגמרא הנזכר לעיל דילמא לכך תנא חולה דפטור אף מאכילה:
**** ENGLISH ****
[8] <b>Levush: Nevertheless, on the first night, etc.</b> My teacher and master Rabbi Helman, of blessed memory, asked: if so, what does the Gemara ask on Rava, who says that one who is distressed is exempt from sukkah, from the Mishnah that sick people and their attendants are exempt? It says: a sick person yes, but one who is distressed no. Perhaps it taught a sick person because even on the first night he is exempt. It appears to me that this is the explanation: it should also teach one who is distressed, with regard to the other nights, just as it teaches in the final clause that casual eating is permitted outside the sukkah, which perforce does not apply on the first night, for then one is obligated even in an olive-volume. It further appears to me that perhaps in truth a sick person is exempt on the first night, and 'one who is distressed' was stated imprecisely: follow the reason learned from the festival of matzos. Even with a dangerously ill person, the danger is from his own condition and not from the sukkah, and if it is because of the sukkah, nothing stands before saving life. However, it is difficult for me regarding the poskim brought by the Beis Yosef who say that one who is distressed is exempt in eating only regarding sleeping. If so, what does the Gemara mentioned above ask? Perhaps it taught a sick person because he is exempt even from eating.

[ט] <b>מי שכבו וכו'.</b> נראה לי דזה נמי בלילה ראשונה חייב לילך לסוכת חבירו אף שיש טורח דלא עדיף ממצטער, ועל פי זה נראה לי ליישב מה שהניח מגן אברהם ריש סימן תרל"ט בצריך עיון על כנסת הגדולה שכתב דבדיעבד ואונס ושעת הדחק יש לסמוך לעבור בספינה לילך בסוכה ותמה הא אין מחויב לילך לסוכת חבירו. ולעניות דעתי מיירי בלילה ראשונה, ועוד נראה לי דאפשר דשם מיירי נמי לסוכה שלו אלא שאי אפשר לעבור שם כי אם על ידי ספינה. כתב הדרישה מזה הוכחתי דגם כן מותר לבנות לו סוכה מיוחדת בחול המועד לעת הצורך ואין צריך להביא סעודתו לסוכה האחרת תחת הסכך וכו' משום אופל אבל על הסכך אפילו בחול אסור אם לא שנראה שמכוין להגן דאז אף ביום טוב שרי כיון דליכא חלל בינו לסכך ועיין סוף סימן תרכ"ט (מגן אברהם):
**** ENGLISH ****
[9] <b>One whose candles went out, etc.</b> It appears to me that on the first night too, he must go to his friend's sukkah even if there is exertion, for this is no better than one who is distressed. Based on this, it appears to me to resolve what the Magen Avraham left with a need for analysis at the beginning of siman 639 regarding the Kenesses HaGedolah, who wrote that after the fact, under compulsion and pressing circumstances, one may rely on crossing by boat to go to a sukkah. The Magen Avraham wondered: is he not exempt from going to his friend's sukkah? In my humble opinion, it speaks of the first night. It also appears to me possible that there it speaks of his own sukkah, except that it is impossible to cross there except by boat. The Derishah wrote: from this I proved that one may also build a special sukkah for himself on Chol HaMoed when necessary, and he need not bring his meal to the other sukkah under the sechach, etc. Because of darkness; but on top of the sechach, even on a weekday, it is forbidden unless it is evident that he intends to protect, for then even on Yom Tov it is permitted, since there is no empty space between it and the sechach. See the end of siman 629; this is from the Magen Avraham.

**** ENGLISH ****
[10] <b>One for whom it is too narrow, etc.</b> The Magen Avraham did not examine Terumas HaDeshen, siman 92, well in this matter; see there, and there is no need to elaborate. As for the Turei Zahav's astonishment about this, and his distinction between where one made it this way from the beginning, which is permitted, and where it later became narrow, there is no difficulty. Certainly everything is permitted. If he would be considered distressed even when he made it so from the beginning, he would not fulfill, for with any distress that applies to most people, even if for him it is not distress, he is nevertheless exempt. Terumas HaDeshen brings proof from making a sukkah seven by seven, and according to his calculation, even if one's feet are outside the sukkah, nevertheless his head and most of his body, which must be in the sukkah, are more than ten tefachim. Rather, perforce this is not considered distress.
