**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For he must bend.</b> The length of a small sukkah is seven tefachim, enough for his head and most of his body. For his head and most of his body we require at least ten tefachim; therefore, perforce he bends his height; this is from Terumas HaDeshen. This requires analysis, for one can say that at the time of sleeping he removes the table and lies diagonally in the sukkah, which is ten tefachim less a fifth, with his feet outside the sukkah in the house; see Rashi at the beginning of Sukkah. According to what Tosafos wrote in Eruvin 48, that a person's body is three cubits excluding his head, then for his head and most of his body more than ten tefachim is required, and the proof is sound. The Gemara says that the wind would drop splinters of sechach onto the food, and Rav Yosef left. Abaye said to him: but we learned, until the dish spoils. He answered: for me, since I am delicate, it is like the dish spoiling. Rashi explains that Rav Yosef was among the delicate, as stated in Perek Arvei Pesachim, where it is taught, etc. This means specifically Rav Yosef, who was known to be delicate, but ordinary people cannot say, 'I am delicate'; rather, we require until the dish spoils, unless he is known to be delicate. See siman 629, se'if 14, and the end of siman 614. It is stated on page 26 that if there are mosquitoes in the sukkah, one may sleep in a canopy even though it has a roof ten tefachim high; see siman 627. This implies that this is preferable to sleeping outside the sukkah, for there is one opinion that he fulfills in this.

<b>חייבים בסוכ'</b>. ול"ד לסעודת חתן שהיא מצו' גדולה לשמח חתן וכלה ונקראת סעודת מצוה בכל מקום אבל הכא אינו אלא מנהג בעלמא עכ"ל מהרי"ק פירוש אף על גב שצר להם המקום  לשבת שם וא"א להם לשמוח שם כ"כ מ"מ חייבין בסוכ' דאע"ג דסעודת מצוה הוא כמ"ש בי"ד סי' רס"ה סי"ב מ"מ אינה מצוה כ"כ דלא מצינו בגמ' בהדיא כמו סעודת נישואין דאי' פ"ג דפסחים בהדיא ע"ש וא"כ די להם בי' אנשים וע' בספר נחלת שבעה שכ' שגם הסעודה שעושין באשכנז בלילה שלפני המילה שמזמנין המוהל והסנדק ושאר קרובים ואוהבים נקרא סעודת מצוה לענין נדר ואינו מוכרח אדרבה מוכח במהרר"מ שהביא דדוקא כששולח סבלונות מקרי הלילה שלפני החופ' סעודת מצוה משמע דבמקום שאין שולחין סבלונות לא מקרי סעודת מצוה וכמ"ש סי' תקנ"ח א"כ ה"ה הלילה שלפני המיל' ע"ש דף נ"ג ולכן יש להחמיר לענין נדר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Are obligated in the sukkah.</b> This is not comparable to a wedding meal, which is a great mitzvah, to gladden groom and bride, and is called a mitzvah meal everywhere. But here it is only a mere custom. These are the words of Maharik. Meaning: even though the place is too narrow for them to sit there, and they cannot rejoice there so much, nevertheless they are obligated in sukkah. Although it is a mitzvah meal, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 265, se'if 12, nevertheless it is not so much of a mitzvah, for we do not find it explicit in the Gemara like a wedding meal, which is explicit in chapter 3 of Pesachim; see there. If so, ten people suffice for them. See Nachalas Shivah, who wrote that the meal made in Ashkenaz on the night before circumcision, where they invite the mohel, sandak, and other relatives and friends, is called a mitzvah meal regarding vows. This is not compelling. On the contrary, it is proven from Maharam, whom he brought, that specifically when they send gifts is the night before the chuppah called a mitzvah meal, implying that where gifts are not sent it is not called a mitzvah meal, as written in siman 558. If so, the same applies to the night before a circumcision; see there, page 53. Therefore one should be stringent regarding vows.

<b>וחייבים בליל'</b>. כשלן בלילה בישוב [תוס' ור"ן] משמע דצריך לעשות לו שם סוכה וכ"מ בא"ח וז"ל וכיון דהולכים ביום חייבים בליל' י"ל שהולכים בכפרי' יחמירו לשוב לבתיהם אם אין סוכ' באותו העיר ואף ע"פ שיש מקום לבעל דין לחלוק המחמיר תע"ב עכ"ל בב"י פי' דמדינא חייבים לעשות שם סוכה ואם אין עושין חייבין לשוב לבתיהם אף על פי שי"ל אם אין להם סוכ' בעת האוכל פטורין כמו אם ירדו גשמי' שא"צ להמתין מ"מ המחמיר תע"ב כי זה מקרי פושע כיון שהיה לו לעשות שם סוכה ולא עשה וכל זה בהולכין מכפר לכפר לתבוע חובותיהם אבל כששוהה בכפר א' ג' או ד' ימים חייב לעשות שם סוכה וכמ"ש ס"ס זה והלבוש כ' דאין חייב לעשות שם סוכה בין העכו"ם והא דקאמר חייבים בלילה היינו כשיש שם סוכה במקום שלן ולא דק דהא כתבו התוס' כשמגיע ליישוב חייב ומקום עכו"ם נמי קרי ישוב ועוד דלפי' הא דקאמר פטורים ביום היינו שא"צ להמתין עד שיגיע לסוכה וא"כ יום ולילה שוין והל"ל בסתמא דאם רוצה לאכול ואין שם סוכה א"צ להמתין ומה לי הולך בדרך או יושב בביתו אלא ע"כ ה"ק הולכי דרכים ביום פטורים ביום אפי' מגיעין למלון אין חייבין לטרוח ולעשות סוכה שם כיון שדעתם לילך לאלתר וחייבים בלילה לעשות שם סוכה וכ"מ מל' רש"י ור"ן ותו' דוק ותשכח ולכן יש להחמיר וכ"כ ב"י בשם א"ח דאין פטורין אלא כשלן בדרך או במקום שאין ישוב ע"ש אך אם בא לכפר סמוך לעת האוכל א"צ להמתין על עשית סוכה כמו בירידת גשמים ואם הוא בשדה אפי' כל ימי החג א"צ לטרוח ולעשות שם סוכה כדאיתא בגמ' ריש דף כ"ו דאין שם ישוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And are obligated at night.</b> This is when he lodges at night in an inhabited place; this is from Tosafos and the Ran. It implies that he must make a sukkah for himself there. So too it appears from Orchot Chaim, who wrote: since those who travel by day are obligated at night, one can say that those who travel in villages should be stringent to return home if there is no sukkah in that city; although there is room for a litigant to dispute, one who is stringent will be blessed. These are his words. In the Beis Yosef he explains that by law they are obligated to make a sukkah there, and if they do not make one they must return to their homes. Although one could say that if they have no sukkah at eating time they are exempt, just as if rain fell and one need not wait, nevertheless one who is stringent will be blessed, for this is called negligence, since he should have made a sukkah there and did not. All this applies when they travel from village to village to collect debts. But when one stays in one village for three or four days, he must make a sukkah there, as written at the end of this siman. The Levush wrote that he is not obligated to make a sukkah among the non-Jews, and that what it says, 'they are obligated at night,' means when there is a sukkah in the place where he lodges. He was not precise, for Tosafos wrote that when he reaches a settlement he is obligated, and a place of non-Jews is also called a settlement. Furthermore, according to him, what it says that they are exempt by day means that they need not wait until they reach a sukkah; if so, day and night are equal, and it should have stated plainly that if one wants to eat and there is no sukkah there, he need not wait. What difference is there whether he is traveling on the road or sitting at home? Rather, perforce this is what it means: travelers by day are exempt by day; even if they reach an inn, they are not obligated to exert themselves and make a sukkah there, since they intend to leave immediately. But at night they are obligated to make a sukkah there. This also appears from the language of Rashi, the Ran, and Tosafos; examine and you will find it. Therefore one should be stringent. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchot Chaim, that they are exempt only when lodging on the road or in a place without settlement; see there. However, if he comes to a village close to eating time, he need not wait for a sukkah to be made, just as with rain. If he is in the field, even all the days of the festival, he need not exert himself and make a sukkah there, as stated in the Gemara at the beginning of page 26, for there is no settlement there.

<b>שומרי העיר וכו'</b>. צריכים לילך סביב העיר כ"מ בגמ' ולכן לא פריך וליעבדו סוכה התם ובירושל' איתא דוקא בשומרי גייסות אבל בשומרי ממון הוי כשומרי גנות ופרדסין אבדומא הוי ישן תוך חנות ושאל לר"ח וא"ל שישן תוך הסוכה: אותן היושבים בחנות אף  על פי שרגילין ברוב הפעמים לאכול ביום שם מ"מ בסוכות צריכים לאכול בסוכה דומיא דשומרים ביום היו צריכין לעשות סוכה שם ה"נ מחוייבין לעשות סוכה שם אפי' אם דרים חוץ לעיר וחניותיה' בעיר מחוייבים לעשות שם סוכה בעיר כי שם ביתו: בירושלמי רב הונא היה הולך בדרך והיה צמא ולא רצה לשתות עד שנכנס לסוכה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The guards of the city, etc.</b> They must go around the city, as appears in the Gemara; therefore it does not ask that they should make a sukkah there. In the Yerushalmi it says that this is specifically regarding guards against troops, but guards of property are like guards of gardens and orchards. Avdoma would sleep inside a store and asked Rabbi Chiyya, and he told him to sleep inside the sukkah. Those who sit in a store, although they are usually accustomed to eat there by day most times, nevertheless on Sukkos they must eat in the sukkah, similar to guards by day, who would need to make a sukkah there. So too they are obligated to make a sukkah there. Even if they live outside the city and their stores are in the city, they are obligated to make a sukkah there in the city, because there is his home. In the Yerushalmi, Rav Huna was traveling on the road and was thirsty, and he did not want to drink until he entered a sukkah.
