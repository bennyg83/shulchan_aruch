<b> וצריך שיהא מחזיק רביעית</b> -  וא"ת פשיטא הלא צריך ליטול ידיו מרביעית וי"ל כגון דאתו משירי טהרה שאז אין צריך רביעית וכדלקמן בסימן ק"ס סי"ג אפ"ה צריך שיהא הכלי מחזיק רביעית [פרישה ע"ש עוד]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it must hold a revi'is</b> - One might ask: Is this not obvious, since one must wash his hands with a revi'is? The answer is that the case is where the water comes from remnants of purification, in which case a revi'is is not required, as explained below in siman 160, seif 13. Even so, the vessel itself must be capable of holding a revi'is. Perishah; see there further.

<b> והוא גדול וכו'</b> -  עיין במ"ב דאם היה רק כמוציא משקה מותר דרך פיו כן הוא מסקנת אחרונים ודלא כמה שכתב בב"י בשם סמ"ג וסמ"ק דהם סוברין דדוקא דרך הנקב מותר ועל עיקר דעתם קשה לי טובא דנקב קטן כזה דהוא רק כמוציא משקה ואינו הולך אפילו טיף אחר טיף איך התירו לכתחלה ליטול באופן זה לפי מה שכתב המגן אברהם לקמן בסימן קס"ב ס"ה ובא"ר שם דהיכא שאינו שופך הקילוח בבת אחת הוא כעין נטילה לחצאין ועל כן אין להתיר בזה אלא דרך פיו דוקא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is large, etc.</b> - See Mishnah Berurah, which explains that if the hole is only large enough to let liquid out, one may wash through the mouth of the vessel. This is the conclusion of the later authorities, unlike what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag and Semak, who hold that one may wash only through the hole. Their essential position is very difficult to understand. A hole of this size merely lets liquid out and does not produce even a drop-by-drop flow. How could they permit washing this way initially, in light of Magen Avraham below, siman 162, seif katan 5, and Eliyah Rabbah there, who write that when the stream is not poured all at once it resembles washing the hands in separate stages? One should therefore permit this only through the vessel's mouth.

<b> בטל מתורת כלי</b> -  ולאו דוקא בכלי חרס המיוחד למשקין דגם לענין טומאה סברי כמה פוסקים דבטל מטומאתו ע"י נקב דכונס משקה דאינו ראוי לתשמישו אלא אפילו בשאר כלים דלכו"ע לא נטהרו מטומאתן ע"י נקב זה [משום דחשובין הן ומצניעין אותן לתשמישי אוכלין הראויין להן] וכן בכלי חרס המיוחדין לאוכלין דשיעורן לענין טומאה עד שינקבו נקב כמוציא זית מ"מ לענין נט"י בטלי מתורת כלי מליטול דרך פיה בנקב ככונס משקה. והטעם דבשיעור זה כיון שאינו מחזיק מימיו כלל [דיוצא טיף אחר טיף] לא חשיב כלי לענין זה [דרישה ע"ש] או כיון דעתה משתמשין בה במים לנטילה דיינינן לזה לעולם כשיעור כלי חרס המיוחד למשקה דלתשמיש מים אינה ראויה וכמו שכתב במטה יהודה [כן כתבו התוספות הובא בב"י דאף בכלי שטף הדין כן ופשוט דה"ה בכלי חרס המיוחד לאוכלין] וכן מוכח דעת הרי"ף [כמ"ש הט"ז] וכ"כ רש"ל הובא בט"ז סק"ז דבכולם השיעור ככונס משקה ואף דדעת הסמ"ק הובא בטור דבכלי המיוחד לזיתים שיעורו כמוציא זית וכתב ב"י דכן הוא ג"כ דעת הרמב"ם ובביאור הגר"א משמע שהוא מסכים כן להלכה ופחות מזה לא חשיב נקב מ"מ פשוט שאין להקל בזה יותר מדעת הסמ"ק עצמו דהיינו שלא ליטול רק דרך הנקב אם מחזיק רביעית מן הנקב ולמטה ולא דרך פיו [וכן משמע בביאור הגר"א שלא נקט דעת הסמ"ק רק לענין זה ע"ש]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It loses the status of a vessel</b> - This is not limited to an earthenware vessel designated for liquids, regarding which several poskim hold that even for tumah it loses its status through a hole large enough to admit liquid, since it is no longer fit for its intended use. It applies even to other vessels, which according to all opinions do not become tahor through such a hole, because they are valuable and are retained for food uses suited to them, and likewise to earthenware vessels designated for food, whose measure regarding tumah is a hole large enough to let out an olive. Nevertheless, regarding netilas yadayim, they lose the status of a vessel for washing through their mouth once there is a hole large enough to admit liquid. The reason is that at this measure the vessel no longer retains its water at all, since it leaks drop by drop, and is therefore not regarded as a vessel for this purpose. Perishah; see there. Alternatively, since it is now being used with water for washing, it is always judged according to the measure of an earthenware vessel designated for liquids, for it is unfit for water use, as Mateh Yehudah writes. Tosafos, cited by Beit Yosef, write similarly that the same law applies even to a non-earthenware vessel, and it is obvious that the same applies to an earthenware vessel designated for food. This is also implied by the Rif, as the Taz writes. Maharshal, cited by Taz, seif katan 7, likewise rules that in all of them the measure is a hole large enough to admit liquid. Although Semak, cited by the Tur, holds that for a vessel designated for olives the measure is a hole large enough to let out an olive, and Beit Yosef writes that Rambam also holds this way, while Biur HaGra appears to agree with this ruling and holds that a smaller hole is not significant, it is nevertheless obvious that one should not be more lenient than Semak himself. Thus, one may wash only through the hole, provided the vessel holds a revi'is below the hole, but not through its mouth. Biur HaGra also appears to cite Semak only for this point; see there.

<b> כיון שמחזיק רביעית</b> -  עיין מ"ב מש"כ בשם הב"ח לענין נסדק והטעם כמו שכתב הח"א משום דסופו להסדק כולה והט"ז מפקפק מאד בדבר דמנ"ל לחלק בזה. והנה במרדכי פרק אלו דברים מוכח בהדיא דאין חילוק בין ניקב לנסדק דז"ל שם כלי שניקב או נסדק ומחזיק למטה רביעית כשר וכן הוא בספר התרומה ג"כ ובאמת משמע כן גם מתוספתא כלים בב"ק ספ"ג דאיתא שם הקוז והקוד הבבלי שנסדקו מלמעלה ומקבלין כשיעור מלמטה וכו' טמאין אמנם בתוספתא סוף כלים נמצא חילוק בין ניקב לנסדק לענין טומאה דז"ל שם תמחוי וקערה שניקבו מלמעלה טמאין מלמטה טהורין נסדקו אם יכולין לקבל את החמין כצונן טמאין וא"ל טהורין והובא בר"ש שם וגם הרמב"ם העתיקו סוף פי"ב ע"ש והנה מסתימת הברייתא משמע דבנסדק אין חילוק בין למעלה או למטה אלא העיקר תלוי דאם ע"י הסדק אינה יכולה לקבל חמין [כמו שמצוי בכלי זכוכית שנסדק דכולה הך ברייתא משמע שם לכאורה דמיירי בכלי זכוכית ע"ש] חשיב כשבר כלי ופסול אף אם הוא דק מן הדק שאינו מוציא משקין אבל ביכולה לקבל כשר ולפ"ז נראה דבכלי מתכות דחזקים הם לא נפסלו ע"י סדק מצד אחד דבודאי יכולין לקבל חמין עדיין אם לא כשנוטף טיף טיף ע"י הסדק דאז בודאי חשיב כנקב בכונס משקה ואפשר דגם בכלי חרס יש להקל דכמה פעמים יכולין לקבל חמין אף כשיש בהן סדק והרמב"ם ותוספתא דקערה הנ"ל יהיה מיירי בכלי זכוכית [וי"ל דזהו טעם המרדכי דאינו מחלק בין סדק לנקב דמיירי בכ"ח וכן התוספתא דקוז וקוד מיירי בכלי חרס ע"ש] ועוד יש לעיין דאפשר דהא דמשערינן בחמין היינו דוקא בדבר שתשמישו בחמין כמו קערה [וכמ"ש הר"ש חילוק זה בכלים פ"ג משנה ז' לענין אם סתם נקב בזפת דבדבר שתשמישו בחמין לא מהני ובצונן מהני] אבל בדבר שתשמישו תמיד בצונן לא משערינן בחמין כלל וצ"ע בכל זה. היוצא מדברינו לענין כלי זכוכית אם ע"י הסדק שוב אינו יכול להחזיק חמין בתוכה פסול לגמרי אף כשהסדק הוא דק מן הדק ואינו מוציא משקה ואם יכול להחזיק בו חמין הרי הוא כלי עדיין ובשאר כלים כגון כלי מתכות ואפשר דה"ה בכלי חרס דין הסדק כמו הנקב. ולענין סתימה עיין במש"כ במ"ב בשם הט"ז וכ"כ בח"א ודלא כמ"א ע"ש בנ"א והנה פשוט דכל אחד במינו בודאי חשיב סתימה ומה דלא מהני טיט בכלי חרס הוא משום דלא נצרף בכבשן עיין ר"ש כלים פ"ג משנה ב' ועיין נ"א דלאו דוקא זפת דיש עוד דברים דמועיל הסתימה לענין טומאה וי"ל דה"ה כאן ואף בשאר כלים יש דברים שמועיל בהן סתימה עיין ר"ה דף י"ט לענין כלי זכוכית שסתמה באבר ורק זפת לא מהני אלא בכלי חרס וגם זה צ"ע. דהר"ש פ"ג דכלים הביא תוספתא דבכלי מתכות שתשמישו בצונן מהני סתימה בזפת אלא דלפירוש הגר"א בתוספתא שם כונה אחרת בתוספתא זו ועיין ברמב"ם פרק כ' מהלכות כלים דמשמע דמפרש אותה בכלי חרס ועיין בראב"ד שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Since it holds a revi'is</b> - See Mishnah Berurah regarding what it cites from Bach concerning a cracked vessel. Chayei Adam explains that the reason is that the entire vessel will ultimately split, while Taz strongly questions the source for distinguishing this case. Mordechai, in the chapter Eilu Devarim, explicitly indicates that there is no distinction between a perforation and a crack, writing: “A vessel that was perforated or cracked and holds a revi'is below is valid.” The same appears in Sefer HaTerumah. This is also implied by the Tosefta, Keilim, Bava Kamma, end of chapter 3, which states that a koz and the Babylonian kod that cracked above but retain the required measure below remain susceptible to tumah. However, the Tosefta at the end of Keilim distinguishes between a hole and a crack regarding tumah: a serving dish or bowl perforated above remains tamei above but is tahor below; if it cracked, then if it can hold hot liquid as it holds cold liquid it remains tamei, and if not it is tahor. Rash cites this, and Rambam codifies it at the end of chapter 12. The plain wording implies that regarding a crack there is no distinction between above and below. Rather, everything depends on whether, because of the crack, it can no longer hold hot liquid. This is common with cracked glass vessels, and the entire baraisa appears to refer to glass vessels. If it cannot hold hot liquid, it is regarded as a broken vessel and is invalid even if the crack is exceedingly fine and does not let liquid out. If it can hold hot liquid, it remains valid. Accordingly, metal vessels, which are strong, are not invalidated by a crack on one side, since they can certainly still hold hot liquid, unless the crack causes a drop-by-drop leak, in which case it is certainly equivalent to a hole large enough to admit liquid. It may be possible to be lenient even with earthenware vessels, for they can often hold hot liquid despite a crack, while Rambam and the Tosefta's case of the bowl refer to glass. This may also explain why Mordechai does not distinguish between a crack and a hole, since he refers to earthenware, and the Tosefta's koz and kod likewise refer to earthenware. One must also consider whether measurement by hot liquid applies only to an article ordinarily used with hot liquid, such as a bowl, as Rash distinguishes in Keilim 3:7 regarding sealing a hole with pitch: sealing is ineffective for an article used with hot liquid but effective for one used with cold liquid. For an article always used with cold liquid, perhaps hot liquid is not the standard at all. All this requires study. The practical conclusion is that a glass vessel that, because of the crack, can no longer hold hot liquid is entirely invalid even if the crack is extremely fine and does not let liquid out. If it can hold hot liquid, it still has the status of a vessel. For other vessels, such as metal, and perhaps also earthenware, the law of a crack is like that of a hole. Regarding sealing, see Mishnah Berurah in the name of Taz; Chayei Adam likewise rules this way, unlike Magen Avraham. See Nishmas Adam. It is obvious that sealing each material with its own kind is considered a valid repair. Clay is ineffective for an earthenware vessel because it was not fired together with the vessel in a kiln. See Rash, Keilim 3:2. Nishmas Adam explains that pitch is not exclusive; there are other substances whose sealing is effective regarding tumah, and the same may apply here. There are also materials that effectively seal other types of vessels. See Rosh Hashanah 19 regarding a glass vessel sealed with lead. Pitch is effective only for earthenware, though this too requires study, for Rash in Keilim chapter 3 cites a Tosefta that pitch effectively seals a metal vessel used with cold liquid. According to Biur HaGra's explanation of that Tosefta, however, it has a different meaning. See Rambam, chapter 20 of Hilchos Keilim, who appears to interpret it as referring to earthenware, and see Raavad there.

<b> מלא נקבים וכו'</b> -  עיין מ"ב מש"כ לענין דעת הרמב"ם וכבר התעוררו בזה המטה יהודה והגר"א. ועיין בט"ז וא"ר שהביאו בשם רש"ל שדעתו להחמיר בכל טיטרוס לענין נטילה [ומחלק בין טומאה לנטילה דבנטילה כלי חשובה בעינן] ומ"מ למעשה נראה דיש לסמוך על השו"ע אחרי דהרא"ש והטור מסכימים להקל וכ"כ רבינו ירוחם בשם המפרשים אך אם הנקבים הם גדולים או בליווע"ר שלנו נראה דיש להחמיר לכתחלה אחרי דלדעת הרמב"ם גם לענין טומאה לא חשיב כלי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Full of holes, etc.</b> - See Mishnah Berurah regarding Rambam's view; Mateh Yehudah and Biur HaGra already raised this point. See Taz and Eliyah Rabbah, who cite Maharshal as ruling stringently regarding every titeros for netilas yadayim, distinguishing between tumah and washing because washing requires a significant vessel. In practice, however, it appears one may rely on the Shulchan Aruch, since Rosh and Tur agree to be lenient, and Rabbeinu Yerucham writes likewise in the name of the commentators. If the holes are large, however, or in our type of lever vessel, one should be stringent initially, since according to Rambam it is not considered a vessel even regarding tumah.

<b> ור"ת מתיר בזו</b> -  ר"ל בדין השני ולא בדין הראשון אכן מצאתי פלוגתא בענין זה דדעת הלבוש וע"ת דבדין הראשון אסור גם לר"ת אף אם ידיו של חבירו היו טהורות מקודם [דהיינו שנטלן ע"י כלי] כיון דלגבי האיש הזה לא היה נטילה כלל מעולם בכלי ודעת הגר"א דלר"ת אם היו ידיו של חבירו טהורות מקודם מהני דבר זה גם לדידיה שיוכל ליתן על ידו בחפניו וכן משמע בשב"ל ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And Rabbeinu Tam permits in this case</b> - Meaning, in the second case, not the first. I nevertheless found a dispute on this point. Levush and Olas Tamid hold that in the first case even Rabbeinu Tam prohibits, even if the other person's hands had previously been purified, meaning washed from a vessel, because for this person there was never any washing from a vessel. Biur HaGra holds that according to Rabbeinu Tam, if the other person's hands had previously been purified, this also helps for the present person, so that the other may pour onto his hand from his cupped hands. Shibolei HaLeket likewise implies this; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because they are drawn water</b> - See Mishnah Berurah. They are considered drawn water here because they were drawn with a bucket, as Rabbeinu Yonah writes. The pipe itself does not render them drawn, because the pipe is not a vessel, as Tosafos, Rosh, Eshkol, and Ritva write. This is also evident from Rabbeinu Yonah and from Raah, who validates such water through hamshachah, and the Mechaber codifies this below. Magen Avraham explains that according to the Mechaber the pipe is a vessel, but the reason for invalidity is that it is attached to the ground and therefore resembles immersion, while immersion is ineffective in drawn water. If so, however, it is difficult to understand why he permits it when the drawn water is conducted into the pipe by hamshachah because drawn water conducted over the ground is valid, for the pipe would still be a vessel. Rabbi Akiva Eiger raises this question, and Peri Megadim struggles greatly with it and leaves it unresolved. According to what we wrote, however, the Mechaber follows the majority of poskim that the pipe is not a vessel, and the difficulty disappears. It may appear from seif 8 that the Mechaber considers a pipe a vessel, since otherwise there is no source for his ruling according to Behag that immersion is ineffective in a vessel attached to the ground, namely one hollowed out and afterward affixed, as Perishah and Peri Megadim explain. This can be rejected, because the Mechaber found only Ramban and Rashba supporting Behag, and they explicitly write this, so he did not wish to be lenient against them. I later found that Shulchan Aruch HaRav also answers this way. All this forced interpretation is necessary only according to Perishah and Peri Megadim's explanation of the Mechaber below in seif 8. According to Biur HaGra there, however, Rashba, Ramban, and the Mechaber refer to a vessel first affixed and then hollowed out, which is invalid according to Behag for agitating one's hands in it because it is like the ground. The channel of drawn water was likewise first affixed and then hollowed out. It then follows simply that the Mechaber validates drawn water conducted into the pipe, because that pipe is literally like the ground. Its invalidity for immersion because of drawn water is due to the water first having been drawn in a bucket, as explained above.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If the vessel is attached to the ground, etc.</b> - See Mishnah Berurah for the reason, which is from Olas Tamid and Perishah, cited by Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim. Olas Tamid writes that Levush implies this applies only when the vessel was attached before it was hollowed out, since it never acquired the status of a vessel. Therefore immersion in it cannot substitute for washing, while immersion itself is ineffective, perhaps because the water initially entered this place through a vessel and is therefore drawn, or because it lacks forty se'ah. If it was hollowed out first and then attached, however, just as one may wash from it through the faucet, as explained above in seif 7, because it is a vessel, one may likewise immerse within it. Although Olas Tamid rejects Levush, I found Biur HaGra explicitly ruling this way. In truth, although the Mechaber's wording somewhat implies the later authorities' explanation, the words of Ramban and Rashba, who are the source of the ruling, are better understood according to Biur HaGra. They did not create a new decree because it appears like immersion. Rather, since an object hollowed out while attached to the ground does not acquire the status of a vessel for washing, its validity would have to come from immersion, and immersion is ineffective because the water is drawn, having been brought by bucket into this vessel. They compare it to bodily immersion, which is ineffective in drawn water. Ritva states this explicitly; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if it was lying on the ground</b> - Rabbi Akiva Eiger leaves the Mechaber's unqualified prohibition here unresolved, for according to the opinion in seif 8 that human force is not required, this case too should be valid, and above the Mechaber ruled that in a pressing circumstance one may rely on that lenient opinion. Rashba, who is the source of that opinion, explicitly permits this case as well. Peri Megadim raises the same difficulty, and Biur HaGra likewise notes that according to that opinion it is permitted. Practically, however, the Mechaber's ruling here appears primary. Mordechai explains that Behag validates immersion within a vessel because agitating one's hands in the vessel is itself considered human force. Ritva writes the same, and I found this also in Eshkol and Rokeach. According to them, Behag does not disagree at all with the other poskim who require human force, in keeping with the plain meaning of Rava's statement in Chullin 107. Peri Megadim inclines this way. One should therefore not be lenient, in accordance with the Mechaber's ruling. This is especially so because Maharshal and Biur HaGra are stringent even regarding immersion in a vessel in a pressing circumstance, as explained above, all the more so here. The later authorities likewise rule without qualification.
