In the Tosefta, first chapter, broken earthenware vessels that can hold a reviis may be used for netilas yadayim; and there, a skin flask and a basket, even though defective, may be used. Certainly a rag is not a connection to make it into a vessel, as in chapter 8 of Keilim and chapter 9 of Ohalos regarding a broken and plugged beehive. See the Taz at the end of s.k. 1.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if, etc.</b> This is like the case of a spigot in Terumas HaDeshen. But according to what is written in the gloss to seif 5, and all the more so a vessel, etc., there is no proof from a spigot; so I also found in the Bach. See Rabbeinu Shimshon at the end of the first chapter of Yadayim: if one opened a boiler and washed from it, or washed from a pipe that holds a reviis, his hands are tahor, while Rabbi Yosei says they are tamei. Rabbi Yosei agrees that if one placed a barrel between his knees or under his arms and washed, his hands are tahor. The opening of the boiler means that he made a crack and the water went out, namely after the first force of his opening. The sugya there follows Rabbi Yosei, and we rule like Rabbi Yosei, as written below. This is the meaning of seif 9, that he must return it, etc. Still, we learn that the issue is specifically human force; without that, it is valid, for the first Tanna and Rabbi Yosei dispute human force, as with a pipe. Semag and Hagahos Maimoniyos hold that even through the hole it is forbidden when the hole is large enough to admit liquid; when it is only large enough to let liquid out, it is permitted through the hole. Sefer Mitzvos Katan writes that if it was designated for olives, only a hole large enough to let out an olive matters, but with less than that one may wash through the hole if it holds a reviis below that point. See Beis Yosef. This is also the Rambam, that one may not wash from a vessel broken with a break that purifies it from tumah. So it also appears in Terumas HaDeshen, the Taz, and Magen Avraham. So too from Tosafot brought by Rabbeinu Shimshon in Yadayim, first chapter, Mishnah 2: the bottoms of vessels, etc., may not be used; but if one trimmed or smoothed them and made them into vessels, one may wash from them if they hold a reviis. Although the Beis Yosef strained to explain that trimming means that they can sit unsupported to hold a reviis, the wording does not imply this, and the Magen Avraham already challenged him from the Mishnah in chapter 30 of Keilim.

In the Tosefta, chapter 2: four or five may wash side by side and there is no concern for the four matters, namely, perhaps the water became tamei from the hands of the first one, since when all wash at once it is like two hands of one person washed together; perhaps work was done with the water; perhaps it was not taken from a vessel; and perhaps it was taken from less than a reviis. But one who washes and gives to another in his cupped hands has tamei hands, because at the outset he did not wash from a vessel.

<b>Seif 14, if he immersed, etc.; and if, etc.; and some say that it requires, etc.</b> As written in Chullin 106a: now if his whole body, etc.; and in the second chapter of Chagigah regarding holy things they immerse, which shows immersion helps. The Tosefta also speaks of one who immerses his hands, as in seif 19. The opinion that it requires a mikveh follows Rashi there, who holds that it requires the law of a mikveh like the whole body. This is also implied by Keilim, first chapter, Mishnah 7, and by the Rambam, who writes explicitly that if the mikveh does not contain forty seah one may not immerse the hands in it. The opposing view requires study. It appears their reason is that Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah explain the case as fully separated from the mikveh, even without forty seah, like vessels for which a reviis suffices according to the Torah; although the Sages later abolished that leniency for vessels, they did not abolish it for netilas yadayim, which is D’Rabbanan. Proof is brought from the irrigation channel of the buckets, which ordinarily lacks forty seah, and if not for being drawn water, immersion would work there. Even if it is connected to a mikveh, the fact that we are lenient about a slope and about a stream indicates a leniency for hands. The argument from now if his whole body, etc., refers to bitterness of the water and not to the measure. The Tosefta is no proof against this, for it also mentions vessels. A spring, however, suffices with any amount even for a person according to many sources; all the more so for hands. The Beis Yosef writes that since netilas yadayim is D’Rabbanan we follow the lenient view, especially as the Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah validate even a mikveh with a reviis. The Gra concludes that here all would agree regarding hands, which are no stricter than vessels, and with a spring the original law for vessels was not abolished according to all.
