<b>מגופה של חבית</b>. והר"ש והרשב"א פי' דמגופה פסולה משום דלאו לתשמיש לתוכה עבידא אלא לכיסוי חבית עמ"ש ס"ק ג', וכ' הב"ח שהם מפרשי' מאי דאמרי' מגופת חבית שתקנה היינו שיחדה מכאן והלאה לקבלה עכ"ל. אבל בר"ש פי' בהדיא שהתקינ' שחקק בה כדי לקבל רביעית משמע דיחוד לא מהני וע' פ"ל דכלים: כתוב בשל"ה דאין ליטול ידיו מהקנקן שקורין גיסקא"ן דרך הדד שבולט כי לא נעשה לקבלה ע"ש וצ"ע דעיקר נט"י בכלי ילפינן מכיור והכיור היו לו דדים כדאיתא ביומא, וצ"ל דאותם היו עשוים כמין שפופרת שמקבלין מים אבל כשעושים כמין צינור הפתוח למעלה והם כמו חצי עגולה לא (וכן מ"ב בשם גאון אחד וכ"מ בשל"ה) וכמ"ש ס"ז וס"ח בב"י בשם תוספ' ורא"ש דצנור לא מקרי כלי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A barrel cover.</b> Rabbeinu Shimshon and the Rashba explain that a cover is invalid because it is not made to serve as a receptacle but to cover a barrel. The Bach writes that when they say he repaired a cover, they mean he designated it from then on to receive. But Rabbeinu Shimshon states explicitly that he repaired it by carving it to hold a reviis, implying designation alone does not help. The Shelah writes that one should not wash from the protruding spout of a vessel called giskan, because it was not made as a receptacle. This requires study from the laver, which had spouts; one must say that those were tube-like receptacles, whereas an open half-channel is not a vessel, as stated regarding a pipe.

<b>If he tilted.</b> Although in seif 9 only the first stream is considered human force, there he did not act on the water itself but merely removed the spigot preventing it from going out. So it is implied in Chullin 16, where with a pottery wheel even the second rotation is considered human force.

<b>If a monkey.</b> At first glance one should ask why the dispute was not stated by a barrel, where the first Tanna and Rabbi Yosei disagree. One can answer that according to the Rambam, who rules like the first Tanna, the Mishnah about a tilted barrel refers to one tilted by hand; if it was tilted by itself, it is invalid because force of giving is required. The Tur, who rules like Rabbi Yosei, explains the Mishnah as a barrel tilted by itself. But when a person tilted it, perhaps even Rabbi Yosei agrees that it is human force. Therefore Beis Yosef ruled this plainly without dispute, while a pipe is invalid according to all because it is not force of giving.
