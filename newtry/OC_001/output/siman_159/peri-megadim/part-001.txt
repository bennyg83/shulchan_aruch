**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if.</b> See the Taz; in Magen Avraham s.k. 3 I discussed this at length, and here I will explain briefly. Rava in Chullin 106a, the Rambam in Hilchos Berachos 6:11, and Shabbos 95 discuss the measure of vessels for tumah and taharah. For earthenware, wood, bone, leather, and metal vessels, see the Rambam in Hilchos Keilim. The Beis Yosef's discussion of a gistra, a vessel designated to receive drippings, requires analysis: a whole vessel designated for drippings is not thereby purified, whereas a broken vessel may be. The main rule is that one does not bring a shard to support a shard; a whole vessel, even if designated for drippings and perforated only enough to emit liquid, remains tamei and valid for netilas yadayim. The Beis Yosef's two answers can both be true: designation alone may not help for netilas yadayim, but vessels of wood and bone may retain their tumah measure until a pomegranate-sized hole, and the same may apply to washing. Still, according to the Rif's version in Chullin, even a wooden bucket perforated enough to admit liquid is invalid for washing, and the matter remains difficult.

<b>Now.</b> The Taz disputes the Beis Yosef. The Beis Yosef inferred from the Rambam's wording, a vessel that broke, that if a broken piece remains capable of holding a reviis below the hole it may be used for washing, and he included the case of a gistra. The Taz challenges this: if a broken piece is treated as a vessel for washing because it is tamei, then the smaller tumah measures for earthenware shards should also suffice; yet for washing one needs at least a reviis. The Taz therefore distinguishes between a vessel that still retains its form, such as a shortened skin flask or earthenware vessel standing on its bottom, which is valid if it holds a reviis, and detached walls or shards, which even if tamei are not vessels for netilas yadayim. He concludes against the Beis Yosef that for all vessels, wood, bone, metal, or earthenware, a hole large enough to admit liquid invalidates for netilas yadayim, as Rava and Semag imply, while a gistra that is tamei with a smaller measure may still be valid for washing if it is designated for that use.

<b>ובמ״ש</b> הב״י דצריך להיות שיש כו׳ עט״ז יש בכאן התבוננות כמו שאגיד בעזה״י הנה הקשה על הב״י איך אפשר לומר מ״ש הר״מ כלי שנשבר לכלול גיסטרא ולהוכיח מזה שיש עוד שיעור קטן איך יצויר כלי של הגיסטרא וזה הרגשנו בתחלת אות זה למעלה. ותמצית דברי הט״ז דאף שבר כלי כל שלא יחדו לקבל נוטפין או לשאר תשמיש אין מטמא אע״פ שנשאר כשיעו׳ כ״פ רש״י בס״פ המצניע צ״ה ב׳ ד״ה ועדיין רימונים אם ייחדו וכ״כ התוס׳ בחולין צ״ה א׳ ד״ה שיעורן יע״ש וא״כ ל״מ אם ייחדן לנט״י ולא לקבל נוטפין פשיטא שאין נקב כמ״מ פוסל בו דלא יחדו לקבל נוטפין ואפי׳ לא יחדו לנט״י ממש רק לשאוב מים וכדומה כל שלא יחדו לגיסטרא נמי אין כמ״מ פוסל לנט״י ול״ת דטומאה ונט״י שוין וכל שבטומאה אין כלי ה״ה לנט״י לזה הוכיח ממ״ש פ״ב דכלים מ״ב הדקין כו׳ שיעור שבר כ״א לפי מה שהיה גדול תחלה א״כ נימא לנט״י נמי כן ואם היה תחלה סאה ליהוי שבר חצי לוג ולא לשתמיש לומר כן ש״מ דבנט״י כל שאין בכ״מ כשר הוא ול״ד לטומאה וה״ה שבר כלי די כל שמחזיק רביעית אע״ג דלטומאה בעינן יותר אם הוא סאה ורבא דאמר בחולין ק״ז מגופת חבית שתיקנה נט״י משמע כשאר כלי ממש ומינה לשברי כלים כה״ג הוי ממש ככלי שלם ואין פסול כמ״מ (ולפי׳ הב״י רבא חדא מינייהו נקיט ומ״ש הט״ז דבעינן ייחדו בשברים אין זה ברור כ״כ ועיין ר״ש פ״ב דכלים מ״ב משמע אף בלא ייחדו וע׳ ר״מ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And regarding what.</b> Regarding the Beis Yosef's requirement that there be a reviis below the hole, the Taz asks how the Rambam could include a gistra, for a shard is not tamei unless designated to receive drippings or another use. If it was designated only for netilas yadayim and not for drippings, a hole large enough to emit liquid would not invalidate it; even if it was designated merely to draw water, as long as it was not designated for drippings, its law must be clarified. The essence is that the tumah status of shards depends on designation and on the type of use, and one cannot automatically derive the law of netilas yadayim from tumah.

<b>And regarding what.</b> The Beis Yosef's reading of Toras Kohanim and Toras HaAdam is difficult. The Magen Avraham follows the Beis Yosef, but the Taz understands that Toras HaAdam does not pose a difficulty to Sefer HaTerumah. According to the Taz, with earthenware, a hole large enough to admit liquid invalidates even if a reviis remains, whereas if the hole only lets liquid out and a reviis remains below it, one may wash from it. The dispute turns on whether the laws of tumah for shards are being applied directly to netilas yadayim.

<b>One may not.</b> See the Taz. The Rambam rules like the Sages that something fixed while detached remains a vessel according to the Torah even after attachment, as by a baker's board in a wall. Regarding mikveh, however, since drawn water is D’Rabbanan, the Sages were lenient if it was fixed and then carved, but not if it was carved and then fixed. The Agudah derived from the unresolved question in Bava Basra that one should not wash from stones fixed in a wall and having receptacles. The Taz asks why we should be stringent in a doubt concerning netilas yadayim, which is D’Rabbanan. Peri Megadim answers that one should not create a doubt initially when other water is available, and the Sages may have given an attached item the status of ground both leniently and stringently. If one carved a receptacle in a mountain, washing from it is certainly not washing at all, though immersion there helps because it is ground. If a detached stone was fixed and afterward carved, immersion may help for netilas yadayim, but if he washed from it he should wash again without a blessing.

<b>And according to the Raavad.</b> See the Taz. Since netilas yadayim is D’Rabbanan, we are lenient, though he should wash without a blessing if he has water. The Bach's discussion of snow can mean taking snow in a vessel and throwing it on one hand and then on the other, where there is a dispute if it is not melted; or it can refer to immersion in snow. The Taz agrees with the first explanation. For immersion, one does not require melted water for netilas yadayim. But taking snow in one hand and rubbing it onto the other is forbidden even with water, because the second hand did not receive water from a vessel. Since snow can be considered liquid once he intends it for washing, it can become tamei and the hand must be dried.
