(18) With intent that they remain, etc. — but if he placed them intentionally on a weekday and his intent was not explicitly that they remain there on Shabbat and afterward he forgot them there — it is called forgetting [writings of Mahariv]; and Beit Yosef's view — even if he placed them on erev Shabbat, as long as his intent was not explicitly that they remain there at entry of Shabbat, and at bein hashemashos he forgot to remove — this too is in the category of forgetting. And behold regarding the essence of the law of base, Magen Avraham concludes it is not called base unless he placed a muktzeh object on it in order that it settle well upon it; but what people place incidentally, as is customary to place objects in a box one on another because he has no room to clear a place for each object at the edges of the box — such a case is not called placing but forgetting, and permitted to move the permitted object after shaking the muktzeh off it [and nevertheless the box itself according to all is a base for a forbidden object if the muktzeh is more important and forbidden to move it — for in any case his intent was that muktzeh rest in the box. Chayei Adam]; and accordingly what people do on Shabbat — remove the upper tablecloth from the table after removing the forbidden object, the menorah, from upon it — as is proper to do, for the menorah need not be specifically on the tablecloth but on the table, and he does not stand it on the tablecloth except because he cannot clear space for the menorah on the table itself, or because he is not concerned to clear space for it; therefore the tablecloth did not become a base for the menorah upon it that it be forbidden to move [and with the lower tablecloth all this is unnecessary — for behold in our time the way is to place lechem on it on erev Shabbat and it becomes a base for this, which is more important]; and some are strict in all this; and in a place of need it appears one may be lenient, for many Acharonim hold like Magen Avraham.
