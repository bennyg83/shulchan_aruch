<b>1) [Seif 1] He should wait one hour, etc.</b> So it is taught in tractate Berachos at the beginning of the chapter Ein Omdin, page 30b: "The early pietists would wait one hour and then pray." There, on page 32b, they also said: "One who prays must wait one hour after his prayer," etc.; see there. Rambam likewise writes this in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 16. Immediately beforehand, however, he writes: "One must sit for a short while before prayer in order to direct his heart," etc., and for a short while after prayer. In Kesef Mishneh there, Maran writes concerning these words of Rambam that although one cannot wait a full hour like the early pietists, one must nevertheless sit for a short while. End quote. Lechem Mishneh there likewise writes that for us it is sufficient to learn from them to wait a short time; see there. Perishah, paragraph 1, similarly explains the words of Tur and writes that a "short time" means the measure of two doorways, namely the time required to walk eight tefachim, as stated in siman 90, seif 20; see there. So too Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 22, paragraph 2; and Lechem Chamudos on the aforementioned chapter, paragraph 37. See the latter source's objection to Shulchan Aruch for using the expression "one hour," and his further ruling that the waiting should be done while seated, not standing; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1; and Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, write likewise. Nehar Shalom writes there as follows: "In truth, however, the plain meaning of Maran's words in Shulchan Aruch does not imply this. Rather, he holds that an actual hour is required and makes no distinction between pietists and the rest of the people. Semag and the students of Rabbeinu Yonah likewise make no distinction between pietists and other people. Their reason appears to me from what the Gemara states there on page 32b: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said, 'One who prays must wait,' etc. It is likewise taught there: 'One who prays must wait one hour before his prayer and one hour after his prayer,' etc. It does not say there that the pietists did so; rather, it states without qualification that one 'must,' implying that every person is required to do so. This also appears to be the view of Rif and Rosh, who cite Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's statement using the word 'must' after the teaching concerning the early pietists, thereby indicating that every person is required. Tur likewise states the ruling without qualification in Kitzur Piskei HaRosh, siman 11." End quote. See further there what he writes to reject the words of Lechem Yehudah on chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, who explains that the "one hour" written by Shulchan Aruch is not literal but means a short time. Lechem Yehudah also writes that this is implied by Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, who states: "For most people, it is sufficient to wait a short while. Rabbi Yishai Almuli likewise writes that this is Maran's view; his words are cited in Merkavas HaMishneh." End quote; see there. See also Shulchan Aruch HaRav there and Kitzur Shelah in the laws governing conduct during prayer, cited by Yafeh LaLev there. Nevertheless, since some explain Maran's words to require an actual hour from every person, one should be stringent where possible. Thus, one who is able should come to study in the place where he prays approximately one hour before Minchah and Maariv. He thereby gains twice: he establishes fixed times for Torah study and also waits an hour before praying. At Shacharis, in any event, the order of the offerings, Pesukei DeZimra, and Yotzer precede the prayer and take approximately an hour. After prayer as well, Ashrei, etc., is recited. Rishon LeTzion likewise writes that this is effective for the hour before prayer, etc.; Yad Aharon, second edition, paragraph 1, cites his ruling.

<b>ד) שם, ושעה א' אחר התפלה וכו'  </b>ואם יכול להתעכב עד שלא ישארו יו"ד בבה"כ טוב לו שלא גרם הוא להרוס הבנין להסתלק השכינה שהרי נתעכב עד שלא נשאר מקום לשכינה לנוח, ועוד כי הישיבה בבהכ"נ היא מצוה בפני עצמה כדכתיב אשרי יושבי ביתך ומכאן סמכו שצריך האדם לשהות שעה אחת קודם תפלה ושעה אחת אחר התפלה, אשרי יושבי ביתך לקודם התפלה, עוד יהללוך סלה לאחר התפלה, סדה"י א"ר אות א' ש"ץ דקנ"ח ע"ב, חס"ל אות ב' וכתב ומכ"ש שלא יצא ראשין מעשרה האחרונים שהוא עיקר הגורם לסילוק השכינה ע"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>4) There: And one hour after prayer, etc.</b> If one can remain until fewer than ten people are left in the synagogue, it is beneficial for him, because he did not cause the building to be demolished and the Divine Presence to depart. He remained until there was no longer a place for the Divine Presence to rest. Furthermore, sitting in the synagogue is a mitzvah in its own right, as it is written, "Praiseworthy are those who dwell in Your house." From here they found support for the requirement that a person wait one hour before prayer and one hour after prayer: "Praiseworthy are those who dwell in Your house" refers to before prayer, and "they will yet praise You, Selah" refers to after prayer. Seder HaYom; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 158b; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2. Chesed LaAlafim writes: "All the more so, he should not be the first of the final ten to leave, for he is the primary cause of the Divine Presence's departure." End quote.
