**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 7) Of joy.</b> It also refers to buying and selling, etc. Rav Beis Yosef wrote thus: since we see that the world is not careful to reduce buying and selling, therefore he holds like the "some say" that Tosafos brought, who explain that buying and selling as well is forbidden only that of joy; but the Taz in s.k. 1 rejected this, for behold we see that even regarding buying and selling of joy, namely needs of a chuppah, they do not refrain; and further, that Tosafos wrote on that interpretation that it is not clear to him, and this is their language: "that only the meal itself is forbidden," end of their words; that is to say: if it were that buying and selling is forbidden only that of joy, then why do we forbid needs of a chuppah, for that too is not buying and selling of joy, only a wedding meal; therefore the Taz wrote that by law in truth all increase of buying and selling is forbidden except for his livelihood. But for us, who have upon us the tax of the king and the officers, everything enters within "for his livelihood"; and for this reason, even though by the law of the Gemara it is forbidden to lend to a non-Jew with interest except for his living needs, for we are concerned lest he learn from his deeds, and nevertheless Tosafos wrote that in this time everything is permitted for the aforementioned reason.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That is to say, it is explained that a stringency they practiced because of a mistake does not need annulment.</b> But something they know is permitted and they were stringent as a fence needs annulment. And in the Beis Yosef it implies that they practice thus because of a mistake, for they explain that just as Tosafos wrote "they reduce joy," meaning that they do not rejoice at all, so too "they reduce buying and selling," meaning that they do not buy and sell at all; and he wrote that this explanation is not true; if so their custom is a mistake and would not have needed annulment; and even so Rav Beis Yosef wrote that they need annulment. One must say: even though Rav Beis Yosef wrote to teach merit for them, that they explain that by law all buying and selling is forbidden by analogy to joy, nevertheless one may also say that they too knew the truth that the meaning is not thus, only that they were stringent upon themselves to forbid upon themselves all buying and selling; therefore out of doubt it needs annulment; unless we say that Rav Beis Yosef holds like the "one who says" that he brought there in Yoreh De'ah, that a mistaken custom needs annulment. And if so, according to what he brought there in Yoreh De'ah as the first view, which was always the main one for Rav Beis Yosef, as is known his way, that a mistaken custom does not need annulment, and the Rama wrote there that such is the custom, annulment would not have been needed here; and that is forced.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 13) On Tisha B'Av, etc.</b> But in Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef, siman 5, it implies, etc.: Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef also permits bathing for the need of whitening through a slight change. For Rav Beis Yosef wrote that in Or Zarua he permitted to immerse on the night of Tisha B'Av, and likewise during the seven days of mourning. And even to bathe in order to whiten, Or Zarua wrote that it is permitted, only that she changes the bathing a bit; but a white undershirt she should certainly wear. And she should spread a white sheet so that she not come to a doubt; end of his words. And the Rama in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381: for us, who hold that immersion is forbidden, all the more so bathing for the need of whitening. And Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef disagreed with this: since the bathing is not for pleasure but for the need of a mitzvah, why should it be forbidden? Granted that we forbid immersion not like Or Zarua: that is because Or Zarua is consistent with his reason that immersion in its time is a mitzvah; but we, who hold that it is not a mitzvah, for what need should she immerse? For to her husband she is forbidden, for Tisha B'Av, and likewise a mourner, is forbidden in marital relations, and likewise on Yom Kippur. But bathing for whitening, since it is not for pleasure, why should she enter a doubt; and therefore it is permitted; end of his words. And the Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381, brought his words and concluded, and this is his language: and nevertheless for practical law it appears to me that she should not bathe for wearing the whitening, rather she should wash her lower face and wear a clean undershirt, not actually white, etc.; so it appears to me clear and proper.

<b>דדוקא גבי כו'</b> דזה ראיית מהרש"ל דב"ש ס"ל בבקר יעמוד בשעת ק"ש ובערב יטה דכתיב בשכבך ובקומך בעי' דרך שכיבה ודרך קימה. וב"ה ס"ל דקורא כ"א כדרכו דכתיב בלכתך בדרך שאינו לא דרך קימה ולא דרך שכיבה. ובשכבך ובקומך ר"ל בזמן שכיבה ובזמן קימה ואר"ט פ"א אני הייתי בא בדרך והטיתי לקרות כב"ש וסכנתי בעצמי מפני הלסטים וא"ל כדאי היית לחוב בעצמך שעברת על דברי ב"ה. הרי כיון דאפסק' הילכת' בש"ס כב"ה נגד ב"ש העושה כדברי ב"ש עושה שלא כדין. וכאן פליגי ר"מ ורשב"ג דר"מ דס"ל דאחר התענית מותר מיד ורשב"ג סבירא ליה דאסור לאחר התענית כל השבת ופסק רבא לקולא כר"מ א"כ העושה כרשב"ג עושה שלא כדין ולכן א"צ התר'. אבל הב"ח כתב דאין הנדון דהכא דומה להתם. דבשלמא בק"ש לב"ה ליכא שום סרך מצוה בהטי' כיון דלא דרש בשכבך ובקומך כדדרש ב"ש א"כ כשמטה א"ע בשעת ק"ש ע"כ נראה משום דס"ל כב"ש. וזה שלא כדין נגד הש"ס דפסק כב"ה. משא"כ הכא לענין ט"ב. דגם לר"מ אי מחמיר ואוסר ע"ע גם אחר התענית עביד שפיר דכדאי הוא בית אלהינו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That specifically regarding, etc.</b> For this is the proof of Maharshal: that Beis Shammai hold that in the morning he should stand at the time of Kerias Shema and in the evening he should recline, for it is written "when you lie down and when you rise," we require the manner of lying down and the manner of rising; and Beis Hillel hold that each reads in his usual way, for it is written "when you go on the way," which is neither the manner of rising nor the manner of lying down; and "when you lie down and when you rise" means at the time of lying down and at the time of rising; and Rabbi Tarfon said in chapter 1: I was coming on the way and I reclined to read like Beis Shammai, and I endangered myself because of bandits; and they said to him: you were worthy to be liable against yourself, for you transgressed the words of Beis Hillel. Behold, since the halacha was decided in the Gemara like Beis Hillel against Beis Shammai, one who does like the words of Beis Shammai does improperly. And here Rabbi Meir and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagree: Rabbi Meir holds that after the fast it is immediately permitted, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that after the fast the entire week is forbidden; and Rava ruled toward leniency like Rabbi Meir; if so, one who does like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel does improperly, and therefore he does not need annulment. But the Bach wrote that the case here is not similar to there: for regarding Kerias Shema, according to Beis Hillel there is no vestige of a mitzvah in reclining, since he did not expound "when you lie down and when you rise" as Beis Shammai expounded; if so, when he reclines himself at the time of Kerias Shema, necessarily it appears that he holds like Beis Shammai; and that is improper against the Gemara that ruled like Beis Hillel. Which is not so here regarding Tisha B'Av: for even according to Rabbi Meir, if he is stringent and forbids upon himself also after the fast, he does well, for worthy is the House of our God.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And further it appears to me that since Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds, etc.</b> They practiced like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel; so it should read. And the Magen Avraham's intent in this is not to resolve the difficulty of Maharshal; on the contrary, that is the difficulty of Maharshal: how may one rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel against the ruling of Rava. Rather the Magen Avraham also maintains the answer of the Bach. Only that it appears from the words of the Bach that it is only a mere stringency; and if so, if the fifteenth of Av is before Shabbos they are permitted to eat meat on the fifteenth day, since it is among the days on which they do not say tachanun, as above in siman 131. To this the Magen Avraham wrote that presumably the reason of those who are stringent, even though they admit that by law it is permitted after the fast as Rava ruled like Rabbi Meir, nevertheless they add a stringency like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as the Bach wrote. And since they are stringent like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that it is forbidden by law, certainly even on the fifteenth of Av it is forbidden, if so they who are stringent like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for them too it is forbidden on the fifteenth; and that is by presumption.
