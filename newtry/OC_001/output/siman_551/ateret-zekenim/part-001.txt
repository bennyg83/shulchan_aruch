מר"ח עד התענית כו' ובבנין של שמחה כיון דבגמרא דידן אוסר בנין סתם כל בנין קאמר כדרך שאסרו כל מו"מ כדי שיהיו נראין כמתאבלין על ירושלים (כ"כ הטור) ס"ל דבגמ' דידן בפ' החולץ (יבמות דף מ"ג) אוסר בבנין סתם מר"ח עד התענית ולא פירש שם דדוקא בנין של שמחה כמ"ש בגמ' דמגילה ותענית גבי תענית ציבור ש"מ דגמרא דידן ס"ל דוקא בתענית ציבור יש חילוק בין בנין של שמחה לאינו של שמחה אבל מר"ח עד ת"ב בכל ענין אסור מ"מ אם שכר עכו"ם קודם ר"ח אב לבנות לו בית בקבלנות דעכו"ם אדעתא דנפשיה קעביד אין בו איסור דעיקר טעמי' למעט שמח' הרי הישראל יושב בטל ואינו עוסק בשמחה אפילו בשבת מותר מן הדין ואינו אסור אלא משום מראית עין:
**** ENGLISH ****
From Rosh Chodesh until the fast, etc. And regarding a building of joy: since in our Gemara it forbids building without specification, it means any building, in the manner that they forbade all buying and selling so that they should appear as mourners over Jerusalem (so wrote the Tur). He holds that in our Gemara in chapter "HaCholutz" (Yevamos folio 43) it forbids building without specification from Rosh Chodesh until the fast, and it did not explain there that it is specifically a building of joy, as is written in the Gemara of Megillah and Ta'anis regarding a communal fast; learn from this that our Gemara holds that specifically regarding a communal fast there is a distinction between a building of joy and one that is not of joy, but from Rosh Chodesh until Tisha B'Av it is forbidden in every case. Nevertheless, if he hired a non-Jew before Rosh Chodesh Av to build him a house under contract, for a non-Jew acts for his own interest, there is no prohibition in it; for the main reason is to reduce joy, and here the Jew sits idle and is not occupied with joy; even on Shabbos it is permitted by law, and it is forbidden only because of appearance.

**** ENGLISH ****
And there is one who says, etc. And in Tosafos they wrote: if it falls on Thursday, it is permitted to launder them from midday onward, etc.; that is to say, on Wednesday from midday onward, for on Thursday itself it is obviously forbidden, and even for the honor of Shabbos; and so ruled the Beis Yosef, and not like the words of Tosafos in Ta'anis who permit. And based on this ruling my teacher in the Bach ruled: one who goes out on a distant journey during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, it is permitted to launder a linen garment for him even for several Shabbosos that he needs, for certainly it is impossible to wait for him until after Tisha B'Av; rather [do it] through a non-Jew, and a Jew should not launder, for through a non-Jew there is no prohibition, only a custom.

**** ENGLISH ****
And from Rosh Chodesh they put away the slaughtering knife, etc. My teacher wrote in the Bach: one who has lambs whose time has come to slaughter them and to sell the skins from Rosh Chodesh onward, and he wants to give them to a non-Jew to cut their throats: it appears that since a Jew is forbidden to cut their throats, as is stated in Avodah Zarah regarding uprooting that involves tereifos is forbidden, and whatever is forbidden for a Jew himself it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to do it with what is his; all the more so here, where in the end he will benefit from it, it is forbidden to do business in neveilah and tereifah, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 114; and see in Massas Binyamin, siman 25, he wrote to permit this matter; see there, that even his son the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Avraham cast an axe at it, and he himself conceded to him; see there.

**** ENGLISH ****

However, this requires further study in the words of the Beis Yosef himself: in Yoreh De'ah, siman 390, he rules like the words of the Rif Geonim, and he wrote that we hold like the Raavad and the Rambam that it is forbidden all thirty days, even what impedes eating; this requires further study.

יש נוהגין שלא לרחוץ מר"ח. אבל אם אירע ר"ח ביום ו' כתב מורי בב"ח דרוחצין לכבוד שבת וכ"כ במנהגים שכן נהגו ונכון הוא שהרי אף בשבת של חזון הוא חומרא יתירא שהרי אף הכיבוס התירו חכמי התלמוד לכבוד השבת (וכ"כ מהרש"ל סי' צ"ב) מצאתי כתוב בס' כנה"ג וז"ל נהגתי למנוע עצמי מתשמיש המטה בשבוע שחל ט"ב להיות בתוכו:
**** ENGLISH ****
There are those who are accustomed not to bathe from Rosh Chodesh. But if Rosh Chodesh happens to fall on Friday, my teacher wrote in the Bach that they bathe for the honor of Shabbos; and so he wrote in the Minhagim that so they practiced, and it is proper; for even on Shabbos Chazon it is an excessive stringency, since even laundry the Sages of the Talmud permitted for the honor of Shabbos (and so wrote Maharshal, siman 92). I found written in Sefer Kenesses HaGedolah, and this is its language: I practiced to refrain from marital relations during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, to be within it.
