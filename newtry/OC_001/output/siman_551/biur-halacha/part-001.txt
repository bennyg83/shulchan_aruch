<b> שהרי יש מי שכתב וכו'</b> -  לפי טעם זה אסור אפילו בכיבוס של כלי פשתן דמה שאמרו כלי פשתן אין בהם משום גיהוץ דוקא גיהוץ לית בהו אבל כיבוס שייך בהו בא"י לפי שיטת רבינו יהודא בר ראובן וכיון דכל הארצות שוות לא"י לבד מבבל נמצא דכיבוס אסור בכל מקום אפילו בפשתן כן איתא בר"ן בהדיא ומה שכתב השו"ע ועוד יש מי שפירש טעם זה הוא רק לעורר לנו דכיבוס שלנו הוי כגיהוץ וא"כ אסור עכ"פ מדינא בכלי צמר אף להניח. ומה שסיים עוד אפילו בכלי פשתן ללבשן מיהא עיין בביאור הגר"א שמתמיה דללבשן בודאי אסור לכו"ע מדינא וכל מה שכתב המחבר ונהגו וכו' הוא רק לעורר לנו דאף להניח אסור. ולענ"ד אפשר שצ"ל ואפילו וכו' ור"ל דאפילו בכלי פשתן שאין לנו לאסור לפי טעם השני הזה עכ"פ ללבשן בודאי אסור מדינא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For behold there is one who wrote, etc.</b> According to this reason it is forbidden even regarding laundry of linen garments; for what they said, "linen garments have in them no [issue] of pressing," is specifically that they have no pressing, but laundry applies to them in Eretz Yisrael according to the view of Rabbeinu Yehudah bar Reuven; and since all the lands are equal to Eretz Yisrael except for Bavel, it emerges that laundry is forbidden in every place even regarding linen; so it is stated in the Ran explicitly. And what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "and further there is one who explained this reason," is only to awaken us that our laundry is like pressing, and if so it is in any case forbidden by law regarding woolen garments even to leave [them]. And what he concluded further, "even regarding linen garments, at least to wear them": see in Beur HaGra, who is astonished that to wear them is certainly forbidden according to everyone by law; and all that the Mechaber wrote, "and they practiced," etc., is only to awaken us that even to leave [them] is forbidden. And in my humble opinion it is possible that one should read "and even," etc., meaning that even regarding linen garments, which according to this second reason we should not forbid, at least to wear them is certainly forbidden by law.

<b> וכן לכבוד שבת וכו'</b> -  עיין במ"ב שכתבנו לענין תספורת דאסור והנה המגן אברהם הביא זה בשם הגהת אשרי והוא הביא זה מאו"ז ועיינתי בא"ז בהלכות ת"ב וז"ל שם ובחמישי מותרין מפני כבוד השבת פי' מותרין לכבס וללבוש בשבת אבל לספר בחמישי אסור כדאמר בירושלמי ובחמישי מותרין מפני כבוד השבת רב הונא בשם רב המנונא אמר לתספורת חייש בה פי' שאסור לספר עכ"ל והנה המעיין בירושלמי גופא לפנינו הגירסא לתספורת הושבה ואף לפי גרסתו ג"כ אי אפשר לומר דכונת הירושלמי להחמיר כי לפי הענין מוכח שם להיפך דתספורת מותר בחמישי בשבת וצע"ג. ועיין בחידושי רע"א שהביא גם בשם התוספות דכביסה ותספורת שניהן שוין להקל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise for the honor of Shabbos, etc.</b> See in Mishnah Berurah that we wrote regarding haircutting that it is forbidden; and behold the Magen Avraham brought this in the name of Hagahos Asheri, and he brought this from Or Zarua; and I examined in Or Zarua in the laws of Tisha B'Av, and this is its language there: "And on Thursday they are permitted because of the honor of Shabbos"; meaning they are permitted to launder and to wear on Shabbos, but to cut hair on Thursday is forbidden, as it says in the Yerushalmi: "And on Thursday they are permitted because of the honor of Shabbos"; Rav Huna in the name of Rav Hamnuna said: regarding haircutting he is concerned in it; meaning that it is forbidden to cut hair; end of his words. And behold, one who examines the Yerushalmi itself: before us the reading is "regarding haircutting it was restored"; and even according to his reading it is also impossible to say that the intent of the Yerushalmi is to be stringent, for according to the context it is proven there to the contrary, that haircutting is permitted on Thursday before Shabbos; and this requires great further study. And see in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who also brought in the name of Tosafos that laundry and haircutting are both equal toward leniency.

<b>And they practiced to be lenient in this.</b> And in Beur HaGra he casts doubt on this: that even though one should not learn fixing garments from that of the Yerushalmi as Darkei Moshe wrote, nevertheless for the reason that it at least resembles laundry one should not be lenient; and likewise what he wrote, "but if it is known and publicized," etc.: in all of this there is room to cast doubt for the reason of laundry; and likewise regarding what he wrote, "and so they practiced to give to non-Jewish craftsmen," etc.: on all of it the Gra marked, and it implies that his view is to be stringent in this; and it appears to me obvious that one should be stringent only during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and not earlier, for regarding laundry itself it is only a custom, and since they practiced to be lenient in this, then one should not be stringent.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And a siyum of a tractate.</b> See what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, that even those who did not learn with them are permitted to eat; and that is from Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah; and see in the siddur of Rabbi Yaakov Emden, whose view is that that means those who support the learners with their money, and also the gabba'im who oversee them and their attendants: all these are permitted even if they are many; but to invite others besides these because of honor and love or for some benefit of his is an absolute prohibition. And nevertheless it is obvious that if those who come assist in making the meal, it is certainly permitted, for he is included in what the Gemara said: "May it come to me that when the Rabbis would finish a tractate I would make a festive day for the Rabbis"; and as the Magen Avraham wrote.
