<b>א) [סעיף א'] משנכנס אב ממעטין בשמחה.  </b>היינו מר"ח ועד עשרה בו בכלל וכמ"ש בס' נחמד למראה בח"ב דר"ז ע"א יעו"ש. ומי שנזהר בזה שלא לשמוח בעשרת ימים אלו יזכה לעשרת דברים שעתיד הקב"ה לחדש לעת"ל כדאיתא במד"ר סדר בא פט"ו. יפ"ל ח"ב או' א' יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(1) [Seif 1] When Av enters, we reduce joy.</b> This means from Rosh Chodesh until the tenth of the month, inclusive, as written in Nechmad L'Mareh vol. 2 p. 207a, see there. One who is careful about this not to rejoice during these ten days will merit the ten things that God will renew in the future, as found in Midrash Rabbah Parshas Bo ch. 15. Yafeh L'Lev vol. 2 os 1, see there.

<b>ב) שם.  </b>משנכנס אב ממעטין בשמחה. פי' שאין שמחין כלל כמ"ש התו' פ"ק דמגילה דף ה' ע"ב. מ"א סק"א. א"ר או' א' שו"ג או' ב' א"א או' א' ח"א כלל קל"ג או' ט' מ"ב או' א' והמט"י כתב ממעטין בשמחה ר"ל עניני שמחה שיתבארו בסמוך סעי' ב' לענין משא ומתן ובנין ונטיעה וכיוצא והכא כללא והתם פרטא וכתב שכ"כ הר"ן בפ' בתרא דתענית יעו"ש. מיהו מדברי הריא"ז שם משמע כדברי מ"א שכתב משנכנס אב ממעטין בשמחה וממעטין העם מעסקיהם וכו' משמע דהם תרי מילי שמחה לבד ומשא ומתן של שמחה לבד ובשניהם צריך למעט אלא שדעת מ"א ושאר האחרונים שאין שמחין כלל כנז':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(2) Ibid.</b> When Av enters, we reduce joy. This means that one does not rejoice at all, as written by Tosafos in the first chapter of Megillah 5b. Magen Avraham s.k. 1. Eliyah Rabbah os 1, Shulchan Gavoah os 2, Eishel Avraham os 1, Chayei Adam rule 133 os 9, Mishna Berurah os 1. The Mateh Yehuda wrote that "we reduce joy" means the types of joy that will be explained shortly in seif 2 regarding commerce, building, planting, and the like, the general principle is here and the specifics are there. He wrote that this is what the Ran wrote in the last chapter of Taanis, see there. However, from the words of the Ria"z there it is implied like the Magen Avraham who wrote: "When Av enters, we reduce joy, and the people reduce their business activities, etc.", implying that these are two separate matters: joy is one thing and commerce of joy is another, and both must be reduced; but the view of the Magen Avraham and the other later authorities is that one does not rejoice at all, as mentioned.

<b>ג) שם.  </b>ובר ישראל דאית ליה דינא בהדי עכו"ם לישתמיט וכו' כ"ה בגמ' תענית (כ"ט ע"ב) וכתוב שם לשתמיט מיניה באב דריע מזליה ע"כ. וכתבו שם התו' פי' משום דאמר לעיל מגלגלין חובה ליום החייב עכ"ל משמע דא"צ לשתמיט מיניה רק עד לאחר ט"ב שהם ימי הרעה וכ"כ ק"נ פ' בתרא דתענית או' ה' מיהו המ"א סק"ב כתב בשם רי"ו עד ר"ח אלול וכתב שכ"מ בגמ' וכ"כ א"ר או' א' א"א או' ב' מחב"ר או' א' והיפ"ל ח"ב או' ג' כתב דלא לישתמיט אלא עד ט"ו באב אבל מכאן ואילך לא לישתמיט דמזל טוב לישראל כדתנן סוף פ"ד דתענית לא היו ימים טובים לישראל כט"ו באב וכו' יעו"ש. וח"א כלל קל"ג או' ט' כתב דאם אפשר ישתמיט כל החדש ועכ"פ עד אחר ט"ב. וכ"כ קיצור ש"ע סי' קכ"ב או' ז' מ"ב או' ב' והרב בן א"ח פ' דברים או' א' כתב דסברא זו עד אחר עשרה באב היא העיקר וא"צ לשתמיט עד ר"ח אלול יעו"ש. וע"כ נראה דאם לא אפשר לישתמיט יותר יש לסמוך ע"ז וכמ"ש ג"כ הח"א ודעמיה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(3) Ibid.</b> A Jew who has a lawsuit with a non-Jew should evade him, etc. This is the language of the Gemara in Taanis (29b), and it says there: "He should evade him during Av, for his mazal is bad." Tosafos there explained: because it says above that unfavorable matters are moved to an unfavorable day. This implies that one need only evade him until after Tisha B'Av, which are the days of misfortune. So too wrote Korban Nesanel in the last chapter of Taanis os 5. However, the Magen Avraham s.k. 2 wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham: until Rosh Chodesh Elul, and wrote that this is implied by the Gemara. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah os 1, Eishel Avraham os 2, Machberes os 1. The Yafeh L'Lev vol. 2 os 3 wrote that one should only evade him until the fifteenth of Av, but from then on one should not evade, for the mazal is good for Jews, as we learned at the end of chapter 4 of Taanis: "There were no days as joyous for Israel as the fifteenth of Av, etc.," see there. The Chayei Adam rule 133 os 9 wrote that if possible one should evade the entire month, and in any case until after Tisha B'Av. So too wrote Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 7, Mishna Berurah os 2. The author of Ben Ish Chai Parshas Devarim os 1 wrote that this view, until after the tenth of Av, is the primary one, and one need not evade until Rosh Chodesh Elul, see there. Therefore it seems that if one cannot evade any longer, one may rely on this, as also written by the Chayei Adam and those who agree with him.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(6) In the Rama's gloss:</b> The custom is that the mohel, the sandak, etc., specifically the one who holds the infant on his knees at the time of the circumcision, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 265 seif 11. But the one who brings the infant in and out is not included in this. Even though the women (i.e., the woman who carries the infant to the synagogue for the bris) also customarily wear Shabbos garments, that is because this is the primary form of their mitzvah. All the more so on Shabbos when Tisha B'Av falls, where it is by law forbidden to wear white garments, only the one holding the infant is permitted. Magen Avraham s.k. 3. Chayei Adam rule 133 os 21. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 15. However, the Eliyah Rabbah os 27 wrote regarding the words of the Magen Avraham that it is strained to distinguish between men and women, and furthermore the Beit Yosef in siman 559 wrote "the mohel and his sandakim, the baalei bris," and so wrote the Knesset HaGedolah on this siman; since he used the plural "sandakim," it implies that all of them are included, and it seems that this is the common practice. The Machatzis HaShekel s.k. 3 cited his words. This is also the view of the Beis Meir, that the one who brings in and takes out is also called a sandak, just like the one who sits and holds the infant, see there. See further on this in Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(8) In the Rama's gloss:</b> They wear Shabbos garments. Even though the Shulchan Aruch rules below in siman 559 seif 8 that a baal bris wears other [non-Shabbos] garments on Tisha B'Av itself, which has the status of a festival, all the more so from Rosh Chodesh until Tisha B'Av. Nevertheless, here it teaches us that even actual white garments [are permitted], for we only forbid white garments on the day of Tisha B'Av itself. Furthermore, it teaches us that even according to the Ashkenazic custom of being strict on Shabbos Chazon not to change [garments], one might think that the festivity of a bris should not override Shabbos and he should not change his garments according to the custom, therefore it teaches us that the custom is to be lenient for a bris. Mateh Yehuda. The Machberes os 5 cited his words and wrote that this is unlike the Zera Avraham siman 76 who wrote the opposite in the name of this authority, see there.

<b>י) שם  </b>בהגה. לובשים בגדי שבת. ולענין תספורת לבעל ברית לא מיבעיא ביום ט"ב עצמו חלילה להתיר אלא אפי' מר"ח עד התענית אסור. בית יהודה ח"ב סי' פ"ה וכ"כ בספרו מט"י על סי' זה. וכ"כ א"ר או כ"ח אלא שכתב דקודם שבא שבוע שחל ט"ב אפשר להתיר. וכ"כ המש"ז או' י"ג וכ"כ הזר"א שם. והב"ד המחב"ר או ב' וכ"כ הנוב"י מה"ק סי' כ"ח. וכן הרב יעב"ץ בתשו' ח"ג סי' ב' ג' החליט לאסור יעו"ש. מיהו בתשו' חת"ס סי' קנ"ח השיג על דברי הנוב"י הנז' ומסיק להיתר גמור אפי' בשבוע שחל ט"ב בתוכו וכתב וכן עמא דבר יעו"ש, ובתשו' בנין ציון סי' ל"א כתב דבחל ט"ב בשבת ודאי יש לסמוך לגלח אפי' בע"ש יעו"ש. וכ"כ בשו"ת אור נעלם סי' יו"ד דט"ב שחל ביום א' והמילה ביום ה' שלפניו כיון שאין שבוע שחל ט"ב ואין איסור מדינא דגמ' יש להתיר למוהל ולבעל ברית להסתפר לכבוד המילה יעו"ש והב"ד השע"ת או' א' וכ"כ קיצור ש"ע סי' קכ"ב או' ט"ו דקודם שבת חזון יש להתיר אבל אח"כ יש לאסור. ונראה כיון דרבו הדעות בזה בשבוע שחל בו ט"ב יש להחמיר כיון שהוא מדינא דגמ' אבל מי"ז בתמוז ואילך וכן ט"ב שחל ביום שבת או ביום א' דאין לו דין שבוע שחל בו ט"ב לסתם הש"ע כמ"ש לקמן סעי' ד' יש להקל אם נצרכין לכך. וכ"ז לנוהגין להחמיר בתספורת מי"ז בתמוז כמ"ש לקמן סעי' ד' בהגה או לנוהגין להחמיר מר"ח אבל לנוהגין כדברי הש"ע דאין לאסור כ"א בשבוע שחל בו ט"ב בלא"ה שרי אם אינו בשבוע שחל בו ט"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(10) In the Rama's gloss:</b> They wear Shabbos garments. Regarding a haircut for a baal bris, it goes without saying that on Tisha B'Av itself it is forbidden, God forbid, to permit it; even from Rosh Chodesh until the fast it is forbidden. Beis Yehuda vol. 2 siman 85, and so he wrote in his work Mateh Yehuda on this siman. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah os 28, except that he wrote that before the week in which Tisha B'Av falls it is perhaps possible to permit. So too wrote Mish'meres Zahav os 13, and so wrote the Zera Avraham ibid. The Machberes os 2 cited their words. So too wrote the Noda B'Yehuda, first edition, siman 28. R' Yaakov Emden in his responsa vol. 3 siman 2, 3 decisively ruled it forbidden, see there. However, in the responsa of the Chasam Sofer siman 158 he challenged the words of the Noda B'Yehuda and concluded that it is fully permitted even during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and wrote that this is the common practice, see there. In the responsa Binyan Tziyon siman 31 he wrote that when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos one may certainly rely on permission to take a haircut even on erev Shabbos, see there. So too wrote in the responsa Or Ne'elam siman 10 that when Tisha B'Av falls on Sunday and the bris is on the preceding Thursday, since there is no "week in which Tisha B'Av falls" and there is no prohibition by the strict law of the Gemara, it is permitted for the mohel and the baal bris to take a haircut in honor of the bris, see there; the Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1 cited his words. So too wrote Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 15 that before Shabbos Chazon it is permitted, but afterward it is forbidden. It seems that since there are many opinions on this matter, during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls one should be strict since it is the strict law of the Gemara. But from the seventeenth of Tammuz onward, and likewise when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos or Sunday, which does not have the status of "the week in which Tisha B'Av falls" according to the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, as stated below in seif 4, one may be lenient if necessary. All this applies to those who have the custom to be strict regarding haircuts from the seventeenth of Tammuz as stated below in seif 4 in the Rama's gloss, or those who have the custom to be strict from Rosh Chodesh. But for those who follow the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch that the prohibition only applies during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, in any case it is permitted if it is not that week.

<b>יב) שם  </b>בהגה. כי אם הכתונת לבד. וכתב בהגמ"ר במס' תענית ה' ט"ב פי' כתונת הוא מעיל ולא חלוק וכן פי' הרד"ק ומ"ש כתונת המעיל פעמים קורא שניהם כאחד עכ"ל וכתוב עו"ש בהגה אשר סביב המרדכי שנדפס בקראקא שכ"כ בס' צל"ד ובס' התניא אבל הוא כתב שראה נוהגין שאין מחליפין אלא החלוק הפנימי. כנה"ג בהגה"ט. וכ"כ מ"א סי' תקנ"ט סק"י בשם מהר"ם טיקטין דמעיל אסור משום דדבר המפורסם חמור בט"ב מאבל וחלוק מותר יעו"ש, וכ"נ דעת האחרונים כמ"ש באו' שאח"ז:

<b>(12) In the Rama's gloss:</b> Only the undershirt. The Hagahos Maimuniyos on Tractate Taanis, Laws of Tisha B'Av, explained that "undershirt" means the outer coat, not the undergarment; so too explained the Radak, and what Scripture calls "the tunic of the coat", sometimes it calls both as one. He further wrote in the Hagahos Asheri around the Mordechai printed in Kraków that so wrote Sefer Tzeidah L'Derech and Sefer HaTanya, but he himself wrote that he observed the practice of only changing the innermost undergarment. Knesset HaGedolah in the glosses. So too wrote Magen Avraham in siman 559 s.k. 10 in the name of Maharam Tiktin that the outer coat is forbidden because a conspicuous matter is more stringent on Tisha B'Av than during mourning, but the undergarment is permitted, see there. This appears to be the view of the later authorities as stated in the following paragraph.

<b>יג) שם  </b>בהגה. כי אם הכתונת לבד. ומותר אפי' חל ט"ב בשבת. לבוש סעי' ט"ז. והמחמיר שלא להחליפו מנהג שטות הוא שאינו לבוש רק מפני הזיעה. תשו' רש"ל סי' כ"ז. מט"מ סי' תשל"ו, מ"א סק"ד. מיהו הא"ר או' ט"ל כתב דהנוהגים שלא להחליף גם הכתונת יש להם על מה לסמוך יעו"ש. וכ"ז לדברי מור"ם ז"ל אמנם מרן ז"ל בב"י ססי' תקנ"ב אחר שהביא דברי הגמ"י והמרדכי כתב דלא נהגו כן. דכן הרדב"ז בתשו' ח"ב סי' תרצ"ג ערער על מנהג זה וכתב דהא דנהגו באשכנז שלא להחליף בגדים בשבת איכה אלא לובשים בגדי חול כל גלילות ישראל לא נהגו מנהג זה ויפה עשו שהוא נגד הדין ועוד האריך יעו"ש. וכן הגאון מהר"ר צבי הגם שהיה אשכנזי היה נוהג שלא לשנות מאומה משאר שבתות אף כשחל ט"ב בשבת. כ"כ בנו הרב יעב"ץ בסידור שלו דף ס"ח יעו"ש. והב"ד המחב"ר או ד' וכ"כ ח"א כלל קל"ג סוף או' כ"א דבק"ק ווילנא נוהגין ע"פ הגר"א ללבוש בגדי שבת ויש משנין בגד אחר עכ"ל. וכ"כ או' חיים על ח"א שם דבהרבה קהלות נוהגים ללבוש כל בגדי שבת ולא לשנות בו מאומה אפי' בשבת שחל בו ט"ב יעו"ש. ופשוט דמ"ש דבהרבה קהלות וכו' היינו לק"ק אשכנזים אבל קהלות הספרדים כולם נוהגים שלא לשנות שבת זה משאר שבתות וכמ"ש ב"י והרדב"ז. ומיהו כתב השו"ג או' ד' דהמנהג שלא ללבוש בגדים לבנים או חשובים כבגדי מועד. וכ"כ היפ"ל ח"ג או' ב' דמנהגם שאין לובשים לבנים כשאר שבתות הקיץ כ"א מצבעונים אחרים כדרך הלובשים בשבתות החורף עכ"ל וזהו שכתבנו לפי מנהגם אבל יש נוהגים שאין לשנות כלל ולובשים גם לבנים כמו שאר שבתות אך לא בגדי מועד. ועיין לקמן סי' תקנ"ב סעי' יו"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(13) In the Rama's gloss:</b> Only the undershirt. And it is permitted even when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos. Levush seif 16. One who is strict about not changing it, that is a foolish custom, for one wears it only because of sweat. Responsa of Maharshal siman 27. Mateh Moshe siman 736. Magen Avraham s.k. 4. However, the Eliyah Rabbah os 39 wrote that those who have the practice not to change even the undergarment have what to rely upon, see there. All this follows the view of the Rama. However, Maran [the Mechaber] in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 552, after citing the words of the Hagahos Maimuniyos and the Mordechai, wrote that this is not the custom. So too the Radbaz in responsa vol. 2 siman 693 challenged this custom and wrote that the practice in Ashkenaz of not changing garments on Shabbos [Chazon], all the regions of Israel did not follow this custom, and rightly so, for it is against the law; and he elaborated further, see there. Similarly, the Gaon R' Tzvi, even though he was Ashkenazic, had the practice of not changing anything from other Shabbosos, even when Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbos. So wrote his son R' Yaakov Emden in his siddur p. 68, see there. The Machberes os 4 cited his words. So too wrote the Chayei Adam rule 133, end of os 21, that in the community of Vilna they practice according to the Gra to wear Shabbos garments, and some change one garment. So too wrote Or Chayyim on Chayei Adam ibid. that in many communities they practice wearing all Shabbos garments and do not change anything, even on Shabbos when Tisha B'Av falls, see there. Obviously, when he wrote "in many communities, etc." he meant Ashkenazic communities, but the Sephardic communities all practice not to change this Shabbos from other Shabbosos, as written by the Beit Yosef and the Radbaz. However, the Shulchan Gavoah os 4 wrote that the custom is not to wear white or distinguished garments like festival garments. So too wrote the Yafeh L'Lev vol. 3 os 2 that their custom is not to wear white as on other summer Shabbosos, but rather other colored garments as is done on winter Shabbosos. This is what we have written according to their custom, but some have the practice not to change at all and wear even white as on other Shabbosos, just not festival garments. See below siman 552 seif 10.

<b>(14) The custom</b> in Eretz Yisrael is not to say any kinah on Shabbos Chazon, as stated in the siddurim, and this is the custom of Egypt and the large cities and towns of Turkey. For even on Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos, one sets his table with fatty meat, the table of the final meal, to honor the day of Shabbos. Who placed a mouth to cry and mourn? He too brought [the kinah] as he lamented, and all the more so in public before the congregation on the holy day of Shabbos, it is certainly proper to nullify this custom in honor of Shabbos. Birke Yosef os 2. Likewise in the responsa Zaken Aharon siman 38 he discussed this at length and wrote that it is forbidden, see there. So too wrote the Radbaz in his responsa vol. 3 siman 645. The Machberes os 6 and the Sha'arei Teshuvah os 6 cited their words.
