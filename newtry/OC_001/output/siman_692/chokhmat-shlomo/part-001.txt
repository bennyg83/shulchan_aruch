**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 1.</b> One who reads the Megillah recites three blessings before it, and so on. Note: I will copy here what I was asked about an incident that occurred here in our community: while one was reading the Megillah he became unable to speak. Must the second reader begin from the place where the first stopped, or begin from the start of the Megillah? It is known that the Magen Avraham wrote that if one became unable to speak, the second must begin from the start. But in truth the words of the Magen Avraham are difficult, for he himself concludes immediately that the second does not need to recite a new blessing, because the first reader had intended from the start to discharge the entire congregation. If so, one who examines the laws of Torah reading, siman 140, and the Yerushalmi, which is the source for the law that when a Torah reader became unable to speak the next reader must begin from the beginning, will see that the reason is lest people say that the first reading was not blessed afterward and the final reading was not blessed before. The Rosh and those who follow him wrote that this speaks after the enactment that each person recites a blessing. This implies that if not for the reason that these verses were blessed before but not after, and those verses were blessed after but not before, he would begin from the place where the first stopped. If so, regarding Megillah, where he does not need to bless a second time because the blessing discharged the entire congregation, why should he need to begin anew?

One cannot say that the Magen Avraham intended to bring proof from the Rambam's view there, that the second does not need to bless, and nevertheless must begin from the start, and that the reason must be as the Beis Yosef explained in siman 140 according to the Rambam's understanding of the Yerushalmi, that if he begins from the place where the first stopped, it appears like a break of attention in the middle. If so, it is proven that even when he does not bless before it, he begins from the start. This is not so. First, since most authorities disagree with the Rambam, why should the Magen Avraham be stringent and be concerned for the Rambam's opinion in a Rabbinic matter, where it is proper to follow the lenient view? According to most authorities, who hold that the matter depends on an actual blessing, this is understandable regarding a sefer Torah, where the reader does not intend to discharge the public, and therefore he must go back and bless, and for that reason he begins from the start. But regarding Megillah, where he does discharge the entire congregation and is exempt from blessing, he does not need to return to the beginning. Why should one be stringent in a Rabbinic matter to return to the beginning out of concern for the Rambam?

Furthermore, even according to the Rambam, perhaps it appears like a break of attention only regarding Torah reading, where the one who recites the blessing does not discharge the public. If so, this second person who now stands to read is not considered as though he himself recited the first blessing; therefore it resembles a break of attention and is as though the latter verses were not blessed before them. But where the whole congregation must fulfill their obligation with one blessing, this second reader is like one who himself recited the blessing at the beginning, and for the original reader himself there is no concern if he paused in the middle. Furthermore, it is proven in the Yerushalmi that only because of the two concerns together, that people may say the first verses were blessed before but not after and the latter verses after but not before, are we concerned. But because of one concern alone, namely the latter verses not being blessed before, if there were no concern that the first verses lacked a blessing after them, we would not be particular. If so, this is understandable regarding Torah, which has a blessing after it, so one can say the first verses were not blessed after them. But regarding Megillah, there is no true blessing after it, for the blessing Harav Es Riveinu is not a blessing over the Megillah but over the miracle, as I wrote at length in my work Sefer HaChayim. If so, it is not relevant to say that people will say the first verses were not blessed after them. Since there is no concern for the first verses, one can say that for the latter verses alone we are not particular enough to require returning to the beginning.

Especially according to the Rambam, for even if he returns to the beginning he will not bless before it and will not thereby repair the latter verses. The concern is only that it appears like a break of attention. One can say that this was mentioned only incidentally, because once the Yerushalmi mentioned that the first verses were not blessed after them, which is something actually repaired if he begins again and blesses afterward, it also mentioned this concern that the latter verses were not blessed before them, which is also repaired. But where there is no blessing afterward, and there is no concern for the first verses, because regarding the latter verses there is no blessing before them and even reading from the beginning would not repair it, it is proper that we do not require returning to the beginning for this. If so, one can say that even the Rambam agrees here. Since according to the other authorities he certainly does not need to return to the beginning, and according to the Rambam it is doubtful and possible that he too agrees here, a doubt cannot remove a certainty. One should not abandon the majority of authorities to be concerned for a doubtful understanding of the Rambam.

Even though regarding this too there are those who disagree and hold that the final blessing of the Megillah is over the Megillah, nevertheless this is a double doubt. Perhaps the law follows most authorities in siman 140, who hold there that the reason is an actual blessing, in which case it is obvious that here he need not return to the beginning. And if you say the law follows the Rambam there, perhaps the law follows the Ran and the Levush that the Megillah has no final blessing over the Megillah itself, only over the miracle, and one may say that even the Rambam would not require returning to the beginning. A double doubt is effective everywhere, especially in a Rabbinic matter. Although Megillah is words of Kabbalah and is like Torah law, nevertheless the main concern requiring return to the beginning is because of the blessing, which is certainly Rabbinic. It is certainly improper to be stringent in this and require returning to the beginning. And although regarding Megillah there is a requirement to read it all like a letter, nevertheless because of that one should not be stringent after the fact to return to the beginning.

Therefore one who is stringent in this is only astonishing. Especially at night after the fast, one certainly should not be stringent to return to the beginning. Also, what the Magen Avraham brought from siman 284 regarding maftir, and not from siman 140 regarding the Torah reading itself, requires analysis. What appears to my humble opinion I have written as law. Afterward I saw in Eliyah Rabbah and Degul Merevavah that they sensed part of our words, and I have aligned with their view. But they did not explain it sufficiently as we have explained; see their works and you will find the difference between their words and mine. Examine this carefully.

<b>(שם סעיף ב') </b> אין לשוח בעוד שקורין אותה. נ"ב עיין במג"א מה שכתב בשם הש"ג פ"ב דברכות ולא העתיק יפה דהו"ל לסיים מה שהניח הש"ג בצ"ע מה נחשב בין הפרקים במגלה כיון דקורין אותה כאגרת ע"ש. ולדעתי י"ל כיון דיש דיעות במשנה מאימת קורין את המגילה ניהו דאנן קי"ל מתחלתה עכ"פ זה נחשב בין הפרקים דהיינו בין תחלתה לאיש יהודי ובין איש יהודי לבין בלילה ההוא וכו'. ועמ"ש בחיבורי על או"ח משנת תד"ר בהלכות ר"ח סי' תכ"ב שם כתבתי דיש פלוגתא בזה בין הפוסקים והטעם למה השמיטו הרמב"ם והש"ע דין זה עיין שם ודוק היטב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There, seif 2.</b> One may not speak while it is being read. Note: see the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim in the second chapter of Berachos, but he did not copy it well. He should have concluded with what the Shiltei HaGibborim left in need of analysis: what is considered “between the sections” in the Megillah, since it is read like a letter? See there. In my opinion, one can say that since there are opinions in the Mishnah about from where the Megillah is read, although we rule that it must be read from the beginning, in any event this is considered “between the sections,” meaning between the beginning and “There was a Jewish man,” and between “There was a Jewish man” and “On that night,” and so on. See what I wrote in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5404, in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, siman 422. There I wrote that there is a dispute about this among the authorities, and the reason why the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch omitted this law; see there and examine it carefully.
