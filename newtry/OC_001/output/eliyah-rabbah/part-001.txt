[A] Later authorities cite Zohar: one should not say “I lie on my bed and will study Torah”; one must stand and dress appropriately—“prepare to meet your God, Israel”—and if possible rise on one’s feet, so as not to fall asleep—very good. They also paired morning and evening, day and night, in Torah or prayer. Reshis Chochmah, Shelah, and other musar works elaborate the mitzvah of rising at halachic midnight and punishment for neglect—see there. One who rises at midnight for study should engage in Oral Torah (Shaarei Tzion citing Zohar). Chatzos: Shaarei Tzion cites Zohar—whether summer short nights or winter long—from twelve seasonal hours, Chatzos six hours after actual nightfall; thus night always begins for everyone from true nightfall—“there was evening and there was morning.” Derech Chachmah p. 46 seems to understand summer night beginning some hours before nightfall—hence his question how we pray Minchah in summer within six hours before Chatzos, and why melachah is not forbidden erev Shabbat summer six hours before nightfall—his reasoning is unclear. Zohar and Shaarei Tzion: in winter after twelve hours from nightfall it still belongs to the day for Chatzos and watches. If so we should permit Shacharis before daylight yet in summer delay Shacharis until twelve hours after nightfall—I answer prayer follows instituted times; Shacharis corresponds to seeing daylight as explained later; perhaps hidden reasons exist. In any case Gemara Berachos opening argues with Zohar and holds we reckon night with seasonal hours always—Chatzos is literally half the night—from the Gemara’s question about end of watch… See there. If Zohar—no difficulty—we need a sign for long nights; what does Gemara challenge “Chatzos I arise” vs “my eyes anticipate the watches”? Even rising at Chatzos after six hours from nightfall there are two watches until day on long nights—investigate. Lechem Chamudos, chapter “HaRo’eh”: even if one must rise promptly without laziness, pause briefly and do not stand suddenly after sleep—for Master said end of chapter “One removes five things close to death: ate and rose, drank and rose, slept and rose…” — pause a moment then rise.

[B] “Watchmen for morning” must precede daylight even in summer. If near sunrise say Kinah first then begin “watchmen for morning,” skipping Kinah in between—Rav Hai Gaon; see Responsa Be’er Eshek §124. Kenesses Gedolah siman 219: people who early attend synagogue but doze in prayer—even during Kerias Shema—those are “early risers” in the wrong sense. Properly: if they can intend in prayer they should rise at the watch; but if on Shabbat they come early only to sleep during prayer, better intend when rising in the morning—end quote. [D] “Measure of his sleep …” Shelah p. 124: if he does not wish to sleep he washes hands even while lying down; but it is forbidden to be awake on his bed lying unless thinking Torah. Bach siman 4 cites Tololas Yaakov from Zohar: walking four cubits in the morning without washing is liable for death—why did Beis Yosef not write? Apparently most cannot be careful—better unintentional than intentional—therefore they omitted—his words are strained. Needs study why people are lenient even learned Jews; Levush siman 25 start: immediately upon rising one relieves himself and washes… ordinary people’s facilities are not within four amos of bed—implies leniency. Also Levush siman 8 seif 11: wrapping tallis before washing to avoid four amos without tzitzis—easy to understand. My brother-in-law Rav Yaakov relied on Shach Y.D. §344 citing Mordechai: entire room where body lies counts as four amos—but leniency beyond that room is unclear; Mordechai Berachos 25a Rava: halacha not like Rabbi Shimon—the whole house is four amos—but we hold below siman 79 regarding feces in room… implies specifically room… Likutei Maharil: wherever Gemara says “house” means room. I resolve Mordechai: Berachos opening discusses speaking before the dead only funeral matters—four amos not mentioned—therefore entire room forbidden like within four amos; reason as Beis Yosef—appears hopeless when speaking before dead; where four amos explicit—outside four amos forbidden even in room. Incidentally I summarized in Eliyah Rabbah Y.D. resolving Beis Yosef’s wonder at Mordechai—why tie to first version forbidding entire room—because Mordechai switches Gemara’s wording: first version even mundane speech forbidden before dead—not mockery of poor; appears hopeless—entire room; second version Torah words forbidden because mockery—only within four amos like Kerias Shema near corpse; walking in cemetery with tzitzis/tefillin siman 366 there. This explains Tosafos Berachos 3a s.v. ein… “specifically his four amos”—investigate. I found Damasek Eliezer p. 126a on Tololas Yaakov: perhaps in their time like demons/pairs—so Bach’s wonder why Beis Yosef omitted—people rely; nevertheless God-fearing should be stringent per musar sefarim: prepare two vessels by bed… Etz Chaim: empty vessel should be chipped—it rules the Other Side. Mekor Chayim: do not touch clothes until washing. Seder HaYom: upon rising immediately Modeh Ani… no hand-washing needed even dirty hands—no Name—see end siman 5 what I wrote.
