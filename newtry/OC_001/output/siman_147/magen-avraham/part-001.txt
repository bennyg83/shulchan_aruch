**** ENGLISH ****
Beit Yosef writes in the name of Mordechai that if one washed his hands and did not divert his attention, he may hold the Sefer Torah bare. On this basis the Ashkenazim rely when they hold a Sefer Torah bare while raising it. Darkei Moshe writes: “We are their descendants, yet I have never seen this practice. On the contrary, people are careful not to touch a Sefer Torah bare. Regarding other sacred writings, however, I have never seen anyone concerned about this.” It is possible that any cleansing substance suffices; see the end of siman 4. Bach explains that even the poles may not be held except through a cloth or another intervening item. Responsa Maharit, siman 136, writes likewise. Ashkenazim are lenient regarding the poles, but one should be stringent. Rambam likewise writes in Hilchos Avos HaTumah, chapter 9: “Strings and straps sewn to a sacred scroll, although one may not retain them, impart impurity to the hands as long as they are attached to the scroll. A scroll-case, a scroll-box, and cloths of sacred scrolls impart impurity to the hands while sewn to them. Written blessings do not impart impurity to the hands even though they contain letters of the Divine Name.” Rosh writes the same in the name of the Tosefta. If so, all the more so the poles. Shabbos 14a states that the principal reason sacred writings impart impurity to the hands is to prevent one from holding them bare. It is therefore evident that even the poles may not be held bare. From Rambam’s statement that one may not retain unnecessary strings and straps, we learn that one may not sew anything onto the body of a sacred scroll unless it is needed for its protection, such as a case or the like. <b>Sacred writings.</b> So write Tosafos, proving it from the fact that sacred writings impart impurity to the hands. Accordingly, it should likewise be forbidden to hold tefillin straps bare, and certainly the tefillin themselves, since they impart impurity to the hands. However, Tosafos in Chagigah 24b write that Rabbi Parnach’s rule does not apply to tefillin straps together with the tefillin. Rash writes similarly in tractate Yadayim. This implies that they may be held bare. Sacred writings, however, are subject to this rule only when written in Ashuris script, on parchment, and with ink, as stated in the Mishnah and ruled by Rambam. Rama therefore wrote correctly that he had never seen anyone concerned about this, for our printed sefarim are not written in that manner. His statement here that it is proper to be stringent is therefore unnecessary. Nevertheless, one must be careful with a Megillah written according to halachah, since Rambam’s unqualified ruling shows that we do not rule like Shmuel, who says Megillas Esther does not impart impurity to the hands.

<b>המשי לצד הספר</b>. דומי' דמזבח הקטרת (מרדכי) וצ"ע דאדרבה המזבח היה מצופה בזהב מבחוץ ובאגודה סוף מגילה כ' דמעיל התחתון דהיינו המפה יהיה המשי לצד הס"ת ומעיל העליון יעשה המשי לצד חוץ דומיא דארון שהיה מבית ומבחוץ בזהב ובאמצעיתו עץ עכ"ל ונ"ל דט"ס הוא במרדכי וצ"ל דומי' דארון וכן יש לנהוג וכ"כ בס"ח סי' תתקל"ב ומביא ראיה מקרשים שהיו מצופין זהב מבפנים והיריעות מבחוץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The silk toward the Sefer Torah.</b> Because it resembles the incense altar. Mordechai. This requires study, for on the contrary, the altar was covered with gold on the outside. Agudah at the end of Megillah writes that on the lower mantle, meaning the cloth directly around the Sefer Torah, the silk should face the Sefer Torah; on the outer mantle, the silk should face outward, like the Ark, which was gold within and without with wood in the middle. It appears to me that Mordechai contains a scribal error and should read, “like the Ark.” This is the proper practice. Sefer Chassidim, siman 932, writes likewise and brings proof from the boards of the Mishkan, which were covered with gold on the inside while the curtains were outside.

<b>מדברים ישנים</b>. כתוב בט"ז בי"ד סי' רפ"ג תשמיש דתשמיש שרי עכ"ל ומיהו בתשו' מהרי"ל סי' קכ"א כתב דאף פרוכת אסור לעשות מדברים ישנים ופרוכת שלנו תשמיש דתשמיש הוא כמ"ש סי' קנ"ד ס"ו ובתוס' פ"ב דמגילה אי' כלים שנעשו מתחלתו להדיוט אין עושין אותן לגבוה אבנים וקורות שחצבו מתחלה להדיוט אין בונין אותן בהר הבית וכתב הרמב"ם ספ"א מה' ב"ה משמע דאפי' הם חדשים כל שנעשו להדיוט אסור ומיהו העולם נוהגים לעשות אפי' מישנים וכמ"ש מהרי"ל ונ"ל דס"ל דבה"מ שאני ועוד נ"ל דוקא כמות שהן אסור להשתמש בהן אבל אם שינה צורתן ועש' מהן כלי אחר שרי דהרי הכיור נעשה ממראות הצובאות ואף על גב דבמזבח בעי' עצים שלא נשתמש בהם הדיוט י"ל דמזבח שאני וכ"מ בגמ' ועיין בילקוט דמגילה ועוד דהרי הקרבנות קרבי' מבהמות שנשתמש בהם הדיוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From old materials.</b> Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 283, writes that a secondary accessory may be made from them. However, Responsa Maharil, siman 121, writes that even a paroches may not be made from old materials, although our paroches is only a secondary accessory, as explained in siman 154, seif 6. Tosafos in Megillah chapter 2 state that utensils initially made for ordinary use may not be used for sacred purposes, and that stones and beams initially quarried for ordinary use may not be used to build on the Temple Mount. Rambam rules this at the end of chapter 1 of Hilchos Beis HaBechirah. This implies that even new items are forbidden once they were made for ordinary use. Nevertheless, the common practice is to make sacred accessories even from old materials, as Maharil notes. It appears that they hold that the Beis HaMikdash is different. It further appears that such items are forbidden only while retaining their original form. If one changes their form and makes a different utensil from them, it is permitted, for the laver was made from the mirrors of the women who assembled. Although wood used for the altar had to be unused by an ordinary person, the altar may be different, as is also implied by the Gemara. See Yalkut on Megillah. Moreover, offerings are brought from animals that had been used by ordinary people.
