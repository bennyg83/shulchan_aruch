The Laws of Rolling the Torah Scroll. Containing 8 S'ifim: It is forbidden to grasp a Torah scroll bare [handed], without a hand-cloth. The greatest among those who read the Torah [i.e. received an aliyah] rolls [the scroll closed], and it is typical to buy it [i.e. this honor] with a large sum of money to foster love of the mitzvah [i.e. the rolling]. {Rama: Gloss: And there are those who say that the same applies to the rest of the sacred writings (Agur and Tosefot - first chapter of Tractate Shabbat), but we do not practice this way; and it is good to be stringent if one has not washed his hands; but regarding a Torah scroll, even in this case [where one has washed] it is forbidden. (His own opinion and the Agudah) Gloss: There are those who say that if the [Torah] cover is on one side [made] of linen and on the other side, silk, it is necessary to turn the silk towards the side of the scroll and to roll [it around the scroll that way] (Mordechai - end of [Tractate] Megillah), but we do not practice such. (Beit Yosef - siman 123). The Torah scroll should not be rolled using a torn material if one has another [un-torn one that can be used]. (Mahari"l). And we do not make a Torah scroll covers from old things which were [initially] made for a ordinary use; see below in siman 153. **** ENGLISH ****
Anyone who wants, may purchase [the privilege] of handing the covers to the one who rolls [the Torah scroll], and the one rolling may not protest because even though [the latter] purchased the [privilege of] rolling, [the latter] did not purchase [the privilege] of the taking of the covers. (And similarly, in those places that practice to purchase [the privilege] of removing and returning [the Torah from/to the ark], the prayer leader may not protest, because that [privilege] does not belong to chazzanim). (Mordechai - end of Tractate Megillah; and Ohr Zaruah)

**** ENGLISH ****
The one who rolls the Torah scroll rolls it from outside; and when one fastens it, one should fasten it from the inside. {Rama: Gloss: meaning: when the [Torah] scroll is standing before one, the writing should be opposite one's face, and one should start to roll from the outside. And after one finishes the rolling, one should fasten the end of the cloth on the inside so that when they come to read from it [the next time], they will find the fastening on the inside and they won't need to flip over the Torah scroll [to unfasten it]. (Tur and the Rosh - end of chapter "B'nei Ha-ir"). And it seems that all of this is when one person does the entire rolling [process]. But nowadays when the custom is that one person raises [the Torah] and another person rolls it, the writing should be facing the one raising it. And that is the custom, since he (ie. the one raising it) is the main "roller" and he is the one holding the Torah.}
