<b>וכ"כ התוס' דף ג' כו'</b> זה ראיית הב"ח דאי' ריש סכה לכמה אמוראי הא דפליגי ר"י ורבנן בסכ' שהיא גבוה למעל' מכ' אמה. דרבנן פסלי ור"י מכשיר. פליגי בכל גווני בין שתהי' רחבה הרב' או כשיעור מצומצם. אלא דרב הונא אמר רב דל"פ אלא בסכ' שהיא ד' אמות על ד"א. אבל בגדול' יותר מזה מודו רבנן דכשרה ורב חנן בר רבא אמר רב דלא פליגי רק באינ' מחזקת אלא ראשו ורובו ושלחנו. אבל בגרול' יותר מזה מודו רבנן דכשרה. ופריך הש"ס מדתני' א"ל ר"י לרבנן מעש' בהילני המלכ' שהית' יושבת בסכה גבוה יותר מכ' וגם שבעה בניה ישבו עמה. ולא אמרו לה רבנן דבר וניהו דאשה פטור' מ"מ ה"ל לרבנן למונעה משום ז' בניה בשלמא לאמוראי דפליגי בכל גווני בין בסכה גדולה ובין בקטנה שפיר אלא למ"ד דוקא בסכה קטנה פליגי וכי דרכה של מלכה לישב בשכה קטנה. ואי גדולה הוי הא מודו רבנן דכשרה ומאי ראי' מייתי ר"י לדבדיו ומשני שסכה גדול' הי' לה לצורך בני ביתה אלא שהסכ' גדולה הית' פתוחה לקיטונית. דהיינו סכות קטנות והיו גבוהים יותר מכ' והלני משום צניעות ישבה בקיטונית. ובהא פליגי דרבנן סברו דבניה בגדול' היו יושבים. והיא שישבה בקיטונית והית' גבוה יותר מכ'. מה בכך הא אשה פטור' מן הסכ' ור"י סבר בניה אצלה היו יושבים בקיטונית והיו הבנים חייביש בסכה ואפ"ה לא אמרו לה דבר ע"כ צריך לומר דלמעל' מכ' כשרה. והקשה רש"י דאכתי תיקשי לר' חנן ב"ר דס"ל דל"פ אלא באינה מחזקת אלא כדי ראשו ורובו ושלחנו. אם כן איך א"ל דבניה גבי דידה היו יושבים. וכי אפשר לישב ת' בני אדם בסכה שאינה מחזקת אלא כדי ראשו ורובו ושלחנו של אדם א'. וע"ז תי' התוס' דהית' ארוכ' הרב' ואינה רחבה שבעה טפחים ובניה באותו אורך היו יושבים עכת"ד. הרי אפילו אינה רחבה שבע' מכל מקום כיון דהית' ארוכה הרב' כשרה. דאפשר לעשות ממנה מ"ט רצועות וכ"א טפח מ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so Tosafos wrote on page 3, etc.</b> This is the proof of the Bach. It is stated at the beginning of Sukkah, according to several amoraim, that the dispute of Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages regarding a sukkah that is higher than twenty amos, where the Sages invalidate it and Rabbi Yehudah validates it, applies in every manner, whether it is very wide or of an exact minimal measure. But Rav Huna said in the name of Rav that they disagree only about a sukkah that is four amos by four amos; if it is larger than this, the Sages agree that it is valid. And Rav Chanan bar Rabbah said in the name of Rav that they disagree only when it holds only his head, most of him, and his table; but if it is larger than this, the Sages agree that it is valid. The Talmud challenges from a Baraisa: Rabbi Yehudah said to the Sages, there was an incident with Queen Helene, who was sitting in a sukkah higher than twenty, and her seven sons sat with her, and the Sages said nothing to her. Although a woman is exempt, nevertheless the Sages should have stopped her because of her seven sons. Granted, according to the amoraim who say that they disagree in every manner, both in a large sukkah and in a small one, this is fine. But according to the one who says that they disagree specifically in a small sukkah, is it the way of a queen to sit in a small sukkah? And if it was a large one, the Sages agree that it is valid, so what proof did Rabbi Yehudah bring for his words? The Talmud answers that she had a large sukkah for the members of her household, but the large sukkah opened to a small chamber, meaning small sukkos, and they were higher than twenty; Helene, because of modesty, sat in the small chamber. This is the point of their dispute: the Sages held that her sons sat in the large one, and she sat in the small chamber, which was higher than twenty. What of it? A woman is exempt from sukkah. Rabbi Yehudah held that her sons sat with her in the small chamber, and the sons were obligated in sukkah, yet they said nothing to her; therefore one must say that higher than twenty is valid. Rashi asked that it still remains difficult for Rabbi Chanan bar Rabbah, who holds that they disagree only when it holds only his head, most of him, and his table. If so, how can one say that her sons sat with her? Is it possible for eight people to sit in a sukkah that holds only the head, most of him, and table of one person? About this Tosafos answered that it was very long and was not seven handbreadths wide, and her sons sat along that length. These are the contents of their words. Thus, even if it is not seven wide, nevertheless since it was very long it is valid, because it is possible to make from it forty-nine strips, each one a handbreadth. This is what he wrote.

If so, one can well say that it was very long, until it held much more than the measure of seven by seven handbreadths.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And on the contrary, it is proven from there, etc.</b> For they wrote that it was only seven wide. This was unnecessary, for to answer their difficulty it would have sufficed had they answered that it was very long. Rather, they wrote this to exclude the reasoning of the Bach. Twenty-eight handbreadths and two fifths: since the Bach holds that we require a square of seven by seven, the diagonal, which is two fifths greater according to the rule that every amah in a square has an amah and two fifths in its diagonal, therefore the diagonal totals nine handbreadths and four fifths. If one makes such a thing in a circle, the circle must contain the entire diagonal of the square, for in a circle the entire matter is the diagonal, meaning nine handbreadths and four fifths. According to the rule that anything with a width of one handbreadth has a circumference of three handbreadths, the cord surrounding the circle is three times nine handbreadths and four fifths. This equals twenty-nine handbreadths and two fifths.

<b>וכ"מ בגמרא דף ג' בקיטונית.</b> הוא גמרא הנ"ל. ולפי מה שפירש מ"א דברי התו' הנ"ל דהעתיקו דוקא שלא הית' רחבה אלא ז' טפחים דאם הית' קצרה מז' טפחים הית' פסולה. אף ע"ג דארוכה הרב' לאפוקי מדעת הב"ח כנ"ל. ותיקשי ניהו דליתא לדברי הב"ח מ"מ אין צריך שתהי' הקיטונית רחבים ז'. ואפ"ה כשרים מטעם פסל היוצא מן הסוכ'. דהא הקיטונית היו פתוחים לסכה הגדול'. וכמשמעות לשון הש"ס לא נצרכה אלא לקיטונית שבה א"ו צ"ל כדברי מ"א דכה"ג אינו מתכשר משום פסל היוצא מ"ה כיון שהמקום צר לשבת. עוד י"ל ראיית מ"א מדאייתי ר"י ראי' מהילני דס"ל דבניה אצלה היו יושבים בסכה גבוה למעל' מכ'. ומאי ראי' דלמא הוכשרו הקיטונית משום פסל היוצא מ"ה דלמעל' מכ' נכשר ע"י פסל היוצא מ"ה כמ"ש מ"א סי' תרל"ג סעיף קטן ה'. אלא צ"ל דוקא לדידן דטעם למעל' מכ' משום דירת עראי אבל לר' חנן דפליגי רבנן ור"י דוקא באין מחזקת רק ראשו ורובו ושלחנו. דהטעם כמ"ש התוספות דהוי כלול של תרנגולים ע"ש א"כ למעל' מכ' סכך פסול מיקרי ואינו ניתר ע"י פסל היוצא מ"ה. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so it is implied in the Gemara, page 3, regarding the small chamber.</b> This is the Gemara mentioned above. According to the way the Magen Avraham explained the words of Tosafos mentioned above, that they specifically copied that it was only seven handbreadths wide, for if it was narrower than seven handbreadths it would be invalid even though it was very long, in order to exclude the view of the Bach as above, it is difficult: granted, the Bach's words are not correct, nevertheless the small chambers did not need to be seven wide, and even so they should be valid by reason of "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah," for the small chambers opened to the large sukkah, as implied by the language of the Talmud, "it was necessary only for the small chamber in it." Rather, perforce one must say like the words of the Magen Avraham, that in such a case it is not validated because of "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah," since the place is narrow for sitting. One can also say that the proof of the Magen Avraham is from the fact that Rabbi Yehudah brought proof from Helene, for he held that her sons sat with her in a sukkah higher than twenty. What proof is there? Perhaps the small chambers were validated because of "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah," for an area higher than twenty is validated through "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah," as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 633, small paragraph 5. Rather, one must say that this applies specifically according to us, where the reason for invalidating higher than twenty is because of a temporary dwelling. But according to Rabbi Chanan, that the Sages and Rabbi Yehudah disagree specifically about one that holds only his head, most of him, and his table, where the reason is, as Tosafos wrote, that it is like a chicken coop; see there. If so, above twenty is called invalid s'chach, and it is not permitted by means of "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah." [And so one must say according to the one who says that they disagree specifically about four amos by four amos, where the reason for invalidity is because there is no shade from the s'chach: above twenty is called invalid s'chach.] From this the Magen Avraham learned regarding our case, a corner that does not have seven by seven, that it is not validated through "the s'chach that extends from the sukkah." This proof is primary.
