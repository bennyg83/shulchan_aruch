**** ENGLISH ****
One whose wife gave birth, whose benefactor died, who built a new house, and who acquired new vessels — and what blessing he recites. 6 sections: If one's wife gave birth to a male, he recites HaTov veHaMeitiv, and she too must recite it. {Rama: Rema: If his wife died in childbirth he recites Shehecheyanu, since there is no benefit to others. Some have written that the custom is to be lenient with this blessing since it is optional rather than obligatory, and from this it has spread that many are lenient with these blessings.}

**** ENGLISH ****
If one's father died, he recites Dayan HaEmet. If the father left money that he inherits and he has no brothers, he also recites Shehecheyanu. If he has brothers, in place of Shehecheyanu he recites HaTov veHaMeitiv. {Rama: Rema: One only recites HaTov veHaMeitiv when others share in the benefit. (Tur)}

If one built a new house or acquired new vessels — even if he had similar ones before, or if he bought, sold, and bought again — he recites Shehecheyanu each time. **** ENGLISH ****
For something not particularly significant — such as a shirt, shoes, or light slippers — one should not recite a blessing. But if he is poor and rejoices in them, he should bless. {Rama: Rema: Some say that even a poor person does not recite a blessing over a shirt, shoes, and similar items, and this is the custom. (Tur in the name of Tosafot and Terumat HaDeshen §36) The custom is to say to one who wears a new garment: "May you wear it out and renew it." Some have written that one should not say this regarding shoes or garments made from animal hides, since that would require killing another animal first to make a new garment from it, and it is written "His mercy is upon all His creatures." (Mahariw in his rulings) The reasoning is very weak and does not seem compelling — nonetheless people are careful not to say it.}
