(א) ויתן ע"ג מכתו וכו' - ר"ל שע"י לעיסה בפיו מתערבים החטים ברוק ויחמיצו כשישהו ע"ג מכה בשיעור מיל ויעבור עליהן בבל יראה ובל ימצא ואפילו לעסן והניחן ע"ג מכתו קודם פסח צריך להסירן כשתגיע שעה ששית בע"פ אא"כ כבר נסרחה עד שנפסלה מאכילת כלב וכמו שנתבאר בסימן תמ"ב וכתבו הפוסקים דכל זה בחטים של ישראל אבל חטים של גוי שלעסן הגוי והשאילן לישראל להניחן ע"ג מכתו בפסח ה"ז מותר אם אין האחריות של הרטיה על הישראל דעל רטיה של עכו"ם אינו עובר בב"י ואם אין לגוי חטים יכול הישראל להקנות לו חטים משלו וילעסן הגוי ושוב יקבל הישראל ממנו את הרטיה רק בתורת שאלה. ואע"פ שחמץ אסור בהנאה ואפילו חמצו של עכו"ם וכדלעיל בסימן תמ"ג שאני הכא שהוא נהנה מחמץ זה שלא כדרך הנאתו ואין בו איסור רק מד"ס ובמקום חולי ואפילו אין בו סכנה לא גזרו ומ"מ אם יוכל להתרפא כשירכך רטיה זו של חטין ע"י מי פירות אין נכון לעשותו ע"י רוק:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) And place upon his wound, etc.: that is to say, that through chewing in his mouth the wheat mixes with saliva and they will cause chimutz when they stand upon a wound for the measure of a mil, and he will transgress regarding them bal yera'eh and bal yimatze. And even if he chewed them and placed them upon his wound before Pesach, he must remove them when the sixth hour of erev Pesach arrives, unless they already spoiled such that they became unfit for a dog's food, as explained in siman 442. And the poskim wrote that all this is regarding a Jew's wheat; but a non-Jew's wheat that the non-Jew chewed and lent to a Jew to place upon his wound on Pesach is permitted if the responsibility for the poultice is not upon the Jew, for regarding a non-Jew's poultice he does not transgress bal yera'eh. And if the non-Jew has no wheat, the Jew may transfer wheat of his own to him, and the non-Jew chew them, and then the Jew receive from him the poultice only by way of a loan. And even though chametz is forbidden for benefit, and even a non-Jew's chametz, as above in siman 443: this case is different, for he benefits from this chametz not in the normal way of deriving benefit, and there is prohibition in it only by rabbinic law, and in a place of illness, even when there is no danger, they did not decree. And nevertheless if he can be healed when he softens this wheat poultice with fruit juice, it is not proper to do it with saliva.

(ז) מותר - ואין חילוק בין אם הזיעה באה מפני מיעוט אויר כמו שמצוי במרתפים ובמערות שהכותלים והאבנים מזיעים בין אם באה מכח מים שיש בה כגון בחומה של לבנים חדשים שלפעמים מזיעים מחמת שלא נתייבשו עדיין הכתלים או בכותל של עץ שהעצים לחים ומזיעים בכל גווני אין הזיעה מחמיץ כמו דמצינו לענין הכשר טומאה דזיעה אינה חשובה כמים להכשיר. ומ"מ לא עדיף ממי פירות וא"כ לפי מה דפסקינן לעיל בסימן תס"ב דמי פירות עם מים ממהרים להחמיץ אין ללוש בהם לכתחלה ואם הוא מלוחלח עדיין ירקדנו ויקיים המלוחלח עד אח"פ והנותר ילוש ויאפה לפסח וכתבו הפוסקים דכל זה בזיעה הבאה מכתלים אבל אם הזיעה באה מחום והבל דמים חמין הנמצא בה כגון שיש שם גיגית של מים חמין ועי"ז נתלחלח השק לכו"ע הוי תולדות המים ומחמיץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
(7) Permitted: and there is no distinction whether the sweat comes because of scant air, as is common in cellars and caves whose walls and stones sweat, or whether it comes from water that is in it, for example a wall of new bricks that sometimes sweat because the walls have not yet dried, or a wooden wall whose woods are moist and sweat: in every case the sweat does not cause chimutz, as we find regarding hechsher of tumah that sweat is not considered like water to be machshir. And nevertheless it is no better than fruit juice; and if so, according to what we rule above in siman 462 that fruit juice with water hastens chimutz, one should not knead with them l'chatchila; and if it is still moist, he should sift it and keep the moistened until after Pesach, and with the remainder he should knead and bake for Pesach. And the poskim wrote that all this is regarding sweat that comes from walls; but if the sweat comes from the heat and vapor of hot water found in it, for example there is a tub of hot water and through this the sack became moistened: according to all it is a toldah of water and causes chimutz.

(ח) ויש אוסרים - ס"ל דלא ילפינן חמץ מהכשר טומאה ולגבי חמץ כל שהוא רטיבות של מים יש בו כח להחמיץ ויש שכתבו טעם סברא זו לפי שהחומה מזיע מכח המים שיש בה ואף זיעת כותל של עץ גם כן דינו כמים כי ע"פ רוב יש בו ג"כ בליעה ממי גשמים שנוטפים על הכתלים ולפעמים ג"כ נוטפים עליהם מי גשמים לאחר שנקצצו מן הארץ ולפי טעם זה בזיעה של אבנים או מתכות או זכוכית גם לסברא זו איננה מחמיץ וכמי פירות דמי. ולדינא העיקר כדעה הראשונה וכמו שסתם המחבר בתחלה ומי פירות הן ומ"מ יש לחוש לכתחלה לדעת האוסרין ואעפ"כ להשהותו לאחר הפסח שרי ואם רוצה להשתמש בקמח זו לפסח יש להתירו ע"י ריקוד ואפילו נתייבש והקמח המלוכלך יזרקנו והשאר ישתמש בו:
**** ENGLISH ****
(8) And some forbid: they hold that we do not learn chametz from hechsher of tumah, and regarding chametz whatever is moisture of water has power to cause chimutz. And there are who wrote the reason for this view: because the wall sweats from the water that is in it; and even sweat of a wooden wall also has the law of water, for for the most part it too has absorption from rainwater that drips on the walls, and sometimes also rainwater drips upon them after they were cut from the ground. And according to this reason, sweat of stones or metals or glass even according to this view does not cause chimutz and is like fruit juice. And for practical law the primary [view] is like the first view, as the Mechaber wrote without qualification at first, and they are fruit juice. And nevertheless l'chatchila one should be concerned for the view of those who forbid; and even so, to keep it until after Pesach is permitted. And if he wishes to use this flour for Pesach, one should permit it through sifting, and even if it dried: and the soiled flour he should throw away, and the rest he may use.

(18) On Pesach: and regarding mice that bit the sacks: if it is recognizable that they ate from the flour and the moisture dried, some say that if it is during Pesach, all of it is forbidden, for we attribute that they went in several places in the sack [and saliva in a mouse's mouth causes chimutz like saliva of other animals and beasts]; and therefore taking hold of that place does not help. And if it is before Pesach, he should sift the flour and keep it until after Pesach, or sell it, and like the law of moistened with water above. But many later authorities hold for practical law that also in this it helps to take hold around the place of the bite with the flour next to it carefully, and the rest he should sift and eat on Pesach; and this counsel helps even if it is during Pesach. And nevertheless it is proper to be stringent like those who say, except in a place of pressing need or loss. And all this is when the sack lies in its place without shaking and the flour did not mix; but if he moved the sack from place to place, or they bit in several places in the sack and it is impossible to grasp and sift, this counsel does not help even according to the view of those who are lenient, and its law is like the law of moistened with water, as above. However according to what we wrote at the end of the previous s.k., that also when the sack became moistened with water, sifting helps in a place of loss to bake before Pesach, it is obvious that the same applies also in this; and see Shaarei Teshuvah what he wrote regarding this.

**** ENGLISH ****
(20) And if it dried on Pesach, etc.: and the reason is that we hold that chametz that mixed on Pesach is in any amount, and if it mixed not in its time it is by giving taste. And therefore if it dried on Pesach, even though the moisture was beforehand, nevertheless so long as it was moist it did not mix, and when did it begin to mix? When it dried and crumbled crumbs into the flour: if so the mixture was made on Pesach, which is in any amount, and it is forbidden to keep it [and even if he sifts it, behold we are concerned that tiny crumbs go down through the holes of the sieve, as above]; which is not so if it dried before Pesach, for we hold that it is in the measure of giving taste in all the flour, and here it does not have that measure. The later authorities wrote that this entire se'if deals with a case where we know that the sack became moistened from water; but if there is doubt that perhaps its moisture is from fruit juice, one should be lenient to eat it on Pesach through sifting even if it dried during Pesach, for it is a double doubt: perhaps it was moistened from fruit juice; and even if you say from things that cause chimutz, perhaps nothing of the dried moisture mixed into the flour. And nevertheless if he can easily find other flour for Pesach, it is not worthwhile l'chatchila to rely on a double doubt to eat it on Pesach; rather he should put it away until after Pesach. And the same applies if it became moistened with some thing about which there are disputes among the poskim whether it is in the category of fruit juice or water: it too is permitted through sifting.
