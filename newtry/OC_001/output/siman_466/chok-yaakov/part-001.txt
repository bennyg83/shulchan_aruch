<b>לא ילעוס אדם. </b> והוא הפי' של מלוגמא כמ"ש לעיל סימן תמ"ב ס"ק ז' וכת' הרוקח סימן רע"א דאפילו אם כבר נלעס והניח ע"ג מכתו קודם פסח צריך ביעור ויסירם עכ"ל וכן נתבאר לעיל סימן תמ"ב דכל שלא נסרחה המלוגמא קודם פסח צריך ביעור וזה פשוט מש"ס ופוסקים ע"ל סי' תמ"ב וכל זה דלא כבית הלל בי"ד סי' קנ"ה ס"ק ג' שכת' הא דלא ילעוס היינו דוקא בתוך הפסח אבל אם היה חמץ כבר מותר להניח בפסח על איזה רפואה וכ"כ בכסף משנה בשם הרשב"א בהל' פסח עכ"ל ובאמת כל זה אינו ומ"ש שכ"כ בכ"מ יגעתי ולא מצאתי חילוק זה בדבריו. ועיקר הקושיא שהכריחו לחילוק זה משום דמקשה ממ"ש הרמב"ם פ"ה מהלכות יסודי התורה דשלא כדרך הנאתן כגון מלוגמא למכה מותר להתרפאות מחמץ בפסח מעיקרא לק"מ דהתם בחמצו של עכו"ם מיירי מה שא"כ בחמץ של ישראל כמ"ש שם הכ"מ וכ"כ המ"א ובתשובת מהר"ש קאיידנבר סי' כ"ז. וכל זה בחולה שאין בו סכנה אבל בחולה שיש בו סכנה אפילו ע"י ישראל בעצמו מחמצו ג"כ מותר וכ"ה במ"א בשם הירושלמי וכ"כ בתשובת מהרש"ק שם ע"ל סי' תס"ה ס"ק ב' ובחמץ שעבר עליו הפסח מותר וכת' בתורת אדם להרשב"א דף י"א ע"ב וז"ל ומיהו לענין אכילה אפשר שעשהו כשל תורה ואין להתרפאות אלא במקום סכנה עכ"ל וכ"כ הר"ן ר"פ כל שעה ובריב"ש סי' רנ"ה וכן מתבאר בי"ד סי' קנ"ה דלא כמשמע קצת בירושלמי פרק כל שעה הלכה א' דאפי' שלא כדרך הנאתו אסור ע"ש: כת' פ"ח נשאלתי על מי שיש לו כאב ראש תדיר ומניחין קילור של קמח ומים וסממנים אחרים בצדעיו כדי להקל הכאב אס מותר לעשות כן בפסח והשיב לאסור ומסיק שם אכן לעשות עם מי פירות שפיר דמי עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A person should not chew.</b> And that is the meaning of malugma, as written above in siman 442 s.k. 7. And the Rokeach wrote in siman 271 that even if it was already chewed and placed upon his wound before Pesach, it requires biur and he should remove them; end quote. And so it was explained above in siman 442 that whenever the malugma did not spoil before Pesach, it requires biur; and this is simple from the Talmud and the poskim; see above siman 442. And all this is not like Beis Hillel in Yoreh De'ah siman 155 s.k. 3, who wrote that "he should not chew" applies specifically during Pesach, but if it was already chametz it is permitted to place it on Pesach for some remedy; and so he wrote in the Kesef Mishnah in the name of the Rashba in the laws of Pesach; end quote. And in truth all this is not so; and what he wrote that so [is written] in the Kesef Mishnah, I toiled and did not find this distinction in his words. And the main difficulty that forced this distinction, because he challenges from what the Rambam wrote in Yesodei HaTorah chapter 5 that when not in the normal way of deriving benefit, such as a malugma for a wound, it is permitted to heal with chametz on Pesach: from the outset that is no difficulty, for there it deals with a non-Jew's chametz, which is not so regarding a Jew's chametz, as the Kesef Mishnah wrote there, and so wrote Magen Avraham and in the responsa of Maharshak, siman 27. And all this is regarding a sick person who is not in danger; but regarding a sick person who is in danger, even by a Jew himself from his own chametz it is likewise permitted; and so it is in Magen Avraham in the name of the Yerushalmi, and so wrote in the responsa of Maharshak there; see above siman 465 s.k. 2. And regarding chametz that Pesach passed over it, it is permitted. And he wrote in Toras HaAdam of the Rashba, folio 11b, and this is its wording: However regarding eating it is possible that he treated it like Torah [law], and one may heal only in a place of danger; end quote. And so wrote the Ran at the beginning of chapter Kol Sha'ah, and in the Rivash siman 255; and so it is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 155, not like what is somewhat implied in the Yerushalmi, chapter Kol Sha'ah, law 1, that even not in the normal way of deriving benefit it is forbidden; see there. Peri Chadash wrote: I was asked about one who has a constant headache and they place a kolir of flour and water and other ingredients on his temples to ease the pain, whether it is permitted to do so on Pesach; and he replied to forbid; and he concludes there that nevertheless to make it with fruit juice is fine; end quote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Water fell upon the flour.</b> And in a similar case the Bach is also stringent in siman 453 regarding flour from which mice ate; but if they bit only the sack and there is room to doubt that perhaps it ate nothing of the flour, even if found during Pesach one should permit keeping it. And in the responsa of Maharam of Lublin, siman 119, he is somewhat lenient regarding sacks bitten by mice in a time of pressing need; see there. Also Taz permits taking around the place of the bite, and the rest he should sift and eat on Pesach even if it dried; end quote. And Magen Avraham wrote, and this is its wording: and it appears to me that regarding mice one should be more stringent, and these mice are wicked and go throughout the entire sack and eat; and all the more so when it lies in barrels, for then it certainly goes through all the flour; therefore the aforementioned Geonim were stringent; and so wrote the Mabit to be stringent for this reason; see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that one should not be stringent at all regarding mice that ate from flour, and [should] permit through sifting in every case: because the core law of flour that mice ate is not mentioned at all in any early posek; only the later authorities derived this law from wheat that mice bit, where we hold like the view that mouse saliva causes chimutz, as explained above in siman 453. And in my humble opinion, even though mouse saliva causes chimutz, nevertheless when they ate from flour there is no concern of chimutz at all. And a clear proof for this is from the mishnah in Parah chapter 9 mishnah 3: that all creeping things that drank waters of purification do not disqualify, because no saliva comes from their mouths, except a weasel; R' Eliezer says, even a mouse; and the law is not like R' Eliezer, as the Rambam and the Rash and Rabbeinu Ovadiah of Bartenura wrote there; and so the Rambam ruled explicitly in Parah chapter 9 law 12, that no moisture comes from its mouth; and so wrote Tosafos in Chullin chapter 1. Behold even regarding liquids that are apt to receive moisture they do not disqualify, all the more so flour, where every grain is separate from its fellow, and there is no concern at all that it moistens from what it is not eating. And it is not comparable to wheat, which is one mass, and because of the strength of the bite we are concerned that some moisture remains in what remains of the wheat kernel; which is not so regarding flour, where one should be lenient in every case; and this is clear. But my opinion is not decisive against the great later authorities, and especially since they are stringent and I am lenient: in any case one should permit tying the place of the bite and permitting the rest in every case through sifting, as Taz ruled; and one who is stringent in this does not lose for his flour. And all this is when one sees no moisture at the place of the bite; but if one sees any moisture at the place of the bite, one should attribute it to a common thing: perhaps the mice urinated on the flour; and see nearby s.k. 14 whether there is concern of chimutz from urine, and it is like having become moistened with water.

<b>וכן מי רגלים. </b> כ' הב"י בשם הא"ח דכל תולדות המים כגון מי הפה והחוטם והעין ואוזן ומי רגלים מחמיצין עכ"ל וכיון שתלה הדבר בתולדה למים אם כן אין חילוק בין מי רגלים דקטנים או גדולים היוצא מפי הטבעת וכדאיתא פ"ו דמכשירין משנה ה' מה נקרא תולדה למים ואף ע"ג דצואת תרנגולים ובהמה אינו מחמיץ כדאיתא בטור וב"י (וירקד הצואה לחוץ משום בל תשקצו כמ"ש הב"ח) וצ"ל דשאני מי רגלים דבהמה דלא מקרי תולדה למים כמ"ש הרמב"ם פ"י מהל' טומאות אוכלין מדהוי מי פירות לענין הכשר ולהכי כ' המרדכי בעכברים שהשתינו על החטים דאין מחזיקין לחמץ עד שיראה החימוץ לפנינו ולפ"ז נראה דגם רי"ו והא"ח לא פליגי כלל עם המרדכי דלא כמשמעות הב"י דהרי הא"ח כתב להדיא דמה שנקרא תולדה למים מחמיץ דהיינו מי רגלים דאדם אכן באגודה פכ"ש בעכברים שהשתינו על החטים משמע דמחמיצין ע"ש ומי הפה מחמיץ כמו באדם. כדאיתא לעיל ס"ב. ודם אף ע"ג דבלא"ה אסור משום איסור דם מ"מ בטל בס' משא"כ איסור חמץ במשהו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise urine.</b> The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchot Chayim that all toldos of water, such as water of the mouth and nose and eye and ear and urine, cause chimutz; end quote. And since he hinged the matter on being a toldah of water, if so there is no distinction between urine of minors or of adults coming from the orifice of the rectum, as stated in Machshirin chapter 6 mishnah 5 regarding what is called a toldah of water. And even though chicken and animal dung do not cause chimutz, as stated in the Tur and Beis Yosef (and he should sift the dung outside because of bal teshaktzu, as the Bach wrote), one must say that animal urine is different, for it is not called a toldah of water, as the Rambam wrote in Tum'as Ochalin chapter 10, since it is fruit juice regarding hechsher; and therefore the Mordechai wrote regarding mice that urinated on wheat that we do not presume chametz until the chimutz is seen before us. And according to this it appears that Rabbeinu Yeruham and Orchot Chayim do not dispute the Mordechai at all, not as the Beis Yosef implies: for Orchot Chayim wrote explicitly that what is called a toldah of water causes chimutz, namely human urine. However in the Agudah, chapter Kol Sha'ah, regarding mice that urinated on wheat, it implies that they cause chimutz; see there. And water of the mouth causes chimutz as with a human, as stated above in se'if 2. And blood, even though it is otherwise forbidden because of the blood prohibition, nevertheless is nullified in sixty; which is not so for the chametz prohibition, which is in any amount.
