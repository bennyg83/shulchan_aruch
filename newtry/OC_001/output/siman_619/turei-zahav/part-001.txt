<b>שאומר ש"ץ כו'. </b> לא זכר כאן שיהיו עוד שנים עמו ובמנהגי' כתוב שצריך עוד לצרף שנים עמו ונ"ל לחלק דאם הש"ץ שהוא מתפלל הוא אומר נוסח זה א"צ לצירוף כי הוא משולח מן הקהל והוי כאלו כל הקהל ביחד אומרים כן אבל אם הרב או הגדול שבקהל אומר כן לא נקרא משולח אלא הוא עצמו אומר כן ע"כ צריך לצירוף שנים עמו שהם במקום כל הקהל ובמהרי"ל כתב וז"ל טרם שירד ש"ץ לפני התיבה היה עומד הוא לפני התיבה ואמר בישיבה כו' וכן עמא דבר לומר הגדול שבכל עיר עכ"ל משמע שמהרי"ל עצמו בלי צירוף אמר כן והיינו שמהרי"ל היה ש"ץ בקהל ורמייז"א ודי בו לבד וכמ"ש ומ"ש וכן עמא דבר שיאמר הגדול לא דק בזה כי אז צריך צירוף ב' אלא שזכר אמירת הגדול כנלע"ד וכתב בלבוש והולכים ב' חשובים מקהל ועומדים אצל החזן שהוא שלישי כדי להתיר להתפלל עם העבריינים אבל משום כל נדרי א"צ ג' שאינו אלא תנאי להבא כו' עכ"ל וא"כ היה לאלו הג' לילך משם אחר אמירת בישיבה כו' ולפי מ"ש שצריך צירוף משום שיהיה מפורסם שכל הקהל מסכימים בתנאי זה וכאלו אמרו הם עצמם פה א' כן ע"כ צריכים להיות עד גמר כל נדרי ומה שנוהגים להשהות עד אחר ברכו נראה דאם היו הולכים קודם אפשר שיאמר הש"ץ ברכו בעודם הולכים ועדיין לא באו למקומם ואינם יכולים לכוין לדברי הש"ץ כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That the shaliach tzibbur says, etc.</b> He did not mention here that there should be another two with him. In the Minhagim it is written that two more must be joined with him. It seems to me to distinguish: if the shaliach tzibbur who leads the prayer says this text, he does not need them to join, because he is the agent of the congregation and it is as though the entire congregation together says this. But if the rabbi or the great man of the congregation says this, he is not called an agent; rather, he himself is saying it. Therefore he needs another two to join with him, who stand in place of the entire congregation. In the Maharil it is written as follows: Before the shaliach tzibbur descended before the ark, he would stand before the ark and say, 'By the authority of the heavenly yeshivah,' etc.; and so is the people's practice, that the greatest person in each city says it. End quote. It implies that Maharil himself said it without anyone joining him. This is because Maharil was the shaliach tzibbur in the community of Worms, and he alone was sufficient, as I wrote. What he wrote, 'and so is the people's practice, that the great person says it,' is not precise, for then two others must join him; he mentioned only the saying by the great person, as it seems to me. The Levush wrote: Two important people from the congregation go and stand by the cantor, so that he is the third, in order to permit praying with the transgressors. But because of Kol Nidrei, three are not needed, for it is only a stipulation for the future, etc. End quote. If so, those three should have gone away after saying 'By the authority,' etc. But according to what I wrote, that the joining is needed so it should be publicized that the entire congregation agrees to this stipulation, and it is as though they themselves said it with one mouth, therefore they must remain until the end of Kol Nidrei. The custom to remain until after Barechu appears to be because if they left earlier, the shaliach tzibbur might say Barechu while they were walking and before they came to their places, and they would be unable to concentrate on the words of the shaliach tzibbur, as it seems to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>By the authority of the Omnipresent, etc.</b> My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, explained that we say this so the transgressors should not think they have already been exempted, since they have now prayed with them. Therefore we say 'by the authority of the Omnipresent,' etc., meaning that they should return in repentance to the Omnipresent. This is difficult: it would have been enough for them to hear that we say, 'we permit praying,' etc., which implies prayer alone. Furthermore, if so, in the act of drawing them close there would be an allusion to distancing them if they do not repent; and before Heaven the future is revealed, so this would become an accusation. Rather, it appears as we wrote: these three announce that they stand in place of the entire congregation, and they act with their authority as though the congregation acted themselves. Only one must first join the Omnipresent, blessed be He, to this, that He agrees with this declaration. Therefore it seems to me that the correct version is to say the text as written: first 'in the yeshivah,' etc., and afterward 'by the authority of the Omnipresent,' etc., and not as printed in the machzorim, first 'by the authority of the Omnipresent' and afterward 'in the yeshivah,' etc. For how could one mention first the agreement of the congregation and afterward mention the agreement of the yeshivos? The reverse is more reasonable: that the yeshivos agree to join the view of the Omnipresent to the view of the congregation to permit. <b>The Tur wrote:</b> And he says Kol Nidrei, etc., 'di nedarna, ve-di ishtabana, ve-di acharimna,' from last Yom Kippur until this Yom Kippur. They intend to release the vows, bans, and oaths that have passed, perhaps they transgressed them, and thereby to be saved from punishment. Rabbeinu Tam challenged this: what does it help to release that which they already transgressed? He instituted saying, 'from this Yom Kippur until the coming Yom Kippur, upon us for good.' He also instituted saying 'di nedirna' and 'de-ishtabana,' for he said this derives from what we learned in Nedarim: one who wants his vows of the entire year not to be upheld should stand on Rosh Hashanah and say, 'Any vows that I am destined to vow this year shall be null,' provided that he does not remember the stipulation at the time of the vow. For if he remembers his stipulation and nevertheless vows, he has annulled the stipulation. But if he does not remember his stipulation, then the stipulation is effective. Therefore one must say 'all of them I regret,' meaning: if I forget and vow, from now I regret them and stipulate that they should be null. End quote. The Beis Yosef challenged Rabbeinu Tam's difficulty: surely release is effective for past vows. Rather, the difficulty is that release requires regret and three laymen or one expert, and furthermore we rule that one must specify the vow, and furthermore, the cantor who says Kol Nidrei, who will release his vow? It seems to me that the Tur intended correctly, for the Rosh wrote answers to Rabbeinu Tam's question. Regarding what Rabbeinu Tam said, that we require regret, the Rosh answered: We are witnesses that anyone who transgresses his vow regrets it from the beginning in order to be saved from punishment. We have expanded somewhat in Yoreh De'ah, siman 323, in explaining these matters, and here we will mention it briefly. Certainly there is no release to uproot a vow retroactively because of fear of punishment, for if so, the entire liability for slaughtering outside the Temple would be nullified, since he could regret before coming to kares. Rather, a valid opening is needed, etc. Therefore Rabbeinu Tam asked well: What does this help to save from past punishment? There is certainly no opening of regret here for the whole congregation, so the release accomplishes nothing. Therefore the Tur wrote 'and it was difficult for Rabbeinu Tam,' and did not write 'Rabbeinu Tam asked,' because he did not ask it explicitly this way, but the intent of his question was in this manner: what does it help to release that which they have already transgressed? Meaning, in the manner I mentioned. The Rosh answered that this itself is the opening: had he known the fear of the punishment that follows from the vow, he would not have vowed. See there in Yoreh De'ah regarding this. Our custom follows Rabbeinu Tam, that we say from this Yom Kippur until the coming Yom Kippur, upon us for good. Regarding the meaning of the version mentioned by Rabbeinu Tam, it seems to me as follows: if it refers to the past, we say 'denadarna,' with a kamatz under the nun and a patach under the dalet, in the pattern of 'we said,' which is past. But for the future tense, one must vocalize a chirik under the dalet. Regarding 'ishtabana,' if it certainly refers to the past, the vowel is a patach under the tav and under the beis, and a sheva under the ayin; but for the future tense one must vocalize a chirik under the ayin. <b>The Levush wrote</b> that the text of Kol Nidrei contains an error, for it begins in the singular, 'de-indarna,' as is proper, and ends in the plural, 'our souls' and 'our oaths,' in plural form. Rather, one must say 'upon myself,' and at the end he should say, 'my vows are not vows, my prohibitions are not prohibitions, and my oath is not oaths.' He also needs to say 'de-indarna,' meaning, 'that I will vow, I.' To me it appears that all of it is plural. The expression 'indarna' is like 'our vow,' for this is indicated by the ending 'na'; and the alef of 'indarna' does not indicate first person usage, but the alef belongs to the root, like 'ishtakach,' 'anukva,' or 'ipachas darga,' and many such forms in Targum. So too, 'indar' is the root, and it does not mean 'ours.' The meaning of the word is thus 'our vow,' 'our indar.' So too with 'ishtabana,' where the root is 'ishtaba,' and the meaning is 'our ishtaba,' meaning our oath. So too with prohibition. This is proven, for if you explain the alef of 'and we forbade' as a prefix, the root would be 'sar,' which is no root at all. Rather, necessarily the alef belongs to the root. It is therefore properly interpreted entirely in the plural, meaning 'our prohibition.' Thus the entire text of Kol Nidrei is in the plural, and the Levush needlessly complained against our text of Kol Nidrei. One should not change it at all.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He says Shehecheyanu, etc.</b> The reason is lest he come to be drawn after it, meaning to drink when he becomes an adult. One should not ask: if so, it should be forbidden to give a child food on Yom Kippur for this reason. It seems to me that here he will think that a mitzvah is different, and that one may drink for it, unlike other eating that is not for a mitzvah, as it seems to me.
