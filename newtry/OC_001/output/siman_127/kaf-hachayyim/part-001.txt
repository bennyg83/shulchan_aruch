<b>א) [סעיף א'] כשיגיע ש"ץ למודים וכו'  </b>פי' דוקא למודים שוחין עם ש"ץ אבל לא בשאר ברכות שש"ץ שיחה. עו"ת או' א':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] When the shaliach tzibbur reaches Modim, etc.</b> That is, specifically at Modim they bow with the shaliach tzibbur, but not at the other berachos in which the shaliach tzibbur bows. Olas Tamid, ot 1.

<b>2) There: The congregation bows with him.</b> For we learn in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Berachos: All bow with the shaliach tzibbur at Hoda'ah. Until here. Tur and Beis Yosef. And it is implied from this that they bow like him, and its measure is like the other bows of the Shemoneh Esreh written in siman 113. So is implied in the Beis Yosef, and so wrote the Taz and the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Olas Tamid, ot 2, who wrote that such is the view of Maran, of blessed memory; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1; Chayei Adam, klal 30, ot 10. However, the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 9 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 4, wrote, and this is his language: When the shaliach tzibbur reaches Modim and bows, all the people bow slightly, but should not bow excessively. End quote. And the Kesef Mishneh wrote the reason there: because they have already prayed, and they bow only so as not to appear as heretics; for this it is enough that they bow their heads slightly, and one who bows excessively appears haughty. End of his words. And it is implied there in the Kesef Mishneh that the shaliach tzibbur and the congregation are equal, in that they bow only slightly, as seen there. But the Bach wrote that it is implied from the aforementioned words of the Rambam that the shaliach tzibbur bows according to the law of bowing, only that all the people bow slightly; and he wrote the reason: since the congregation are not now obligated to bow, and they bow only so as not to appear as heretics toward the One to Whom the shaliach tzibbur bows, therefore the Sages cautioned that they bow only slightly, so that they should know that there is no complete obligation in this bowing, but only so as not to appear as heretics; see there. And the Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 1; Ateres Zekeinim, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2. And so it is written in Shaar HaKavanos, at the end of the fourth discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, that at Modim d'Rabbanan there is nothing but the bowing and straightening of the head alone; and so it is written in the second discourse on the Amidah, folio 29a, and in the sixth discourse, folio 37b; and so it is written in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaAmidah, chapter 20; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 139b; Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 20; Beis Oved, ot 5. However, this is according to the plain sense of the words of Shaar HaKavanos, which wrote that at the Modim of the silent prayer one performs the bowing of the body and the straightening of the body alone, and at Modim d'Rabbanan one performs the bowing of the head and the straightening of the head alone. But according to the words of the Rashash, of blessed memory, who inferred from the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, from other places, and wrote that one must perform at the Modim of the silent prayer the bowing of the body and the bowing of the head and the straightening of the body and the straightening of the head, and likewise the shaliach tzibbur in the repetition, as stated above in siman 113, ot 21: if so, there remains nothing to perform at Modim d'Rabbanan the bowing and straightening of the head [alone], since we did everything at the silent prayer; rather one must do as the shaliach tzibbur does, the bowing of the body and the head and the straightening of the body and the head. And this accords with the view of Maran, of blessed memory, and those who agree with him, who hold that at Modim d'Rabbanan one does as at the silent prayer and as the shaliach tzibbur does. And so wrote the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, that the world does not practice like the Bach; see there. And so, since there is a dispute in this, whether the bowing of the head alone suffices or one needs [to do] as at the silent prayer, and also those who hold that the bowing of the head suffices did not say that it is forbidden to do as at the silent prayer, and what they said about there being haughtiness is only if one does excessively, as explained in siman 113 and as the poskim wrote and below in ot 4: therefore one should be stringent and perform at Modim d'Rabbanan as the shaliach tzibbur does, namely at 'Modim anachnu lach' he performs the bowing of the body, and at 'she'Atah Hu' the bowing of the head, and at 'Hashem' he performs the straightening of the body and the straightening of the head, as stated above in siman 113, ot 21; see there, and examine this well. However, it appears that this requirement to perform the bowing of the body and the bowing of the head, etc., is only if one is directing his attention to the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, and therefore he must do as he does; but if he is engaged in zemiros and the like, or has already prayed and heard the repetition, only that afterward he heard the shaliach tzibbur saying Modim, for this the bowing of the head alone suffices.
