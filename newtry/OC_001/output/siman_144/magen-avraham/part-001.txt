<b>שיש בו חתן</b>. ובכ"ה סי' שפ"ב /רפ"א/ האריך במנהגם שמוציאין לחתן ספר שני וקורא ואברהם זקן וגו' עד ולקחת אשה לבני משם מיהו אם הוא יום שבלא"ה מוציאין ב' ספרים קורא אותה בס"ת השני ואין מוציאין ס"ת שלישית וגם מנהגם לתרגם הפ' הנ"ל ואפי' ש"ץ יכול לתרגמה ודוקא הנושא בתולה אבל לנושא אלמנה אין קורין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Where there is a groom.</b> Knesses HaGedolah, siman 281, discusses at length their custom to take out a second Sefer Torah for a groom and read, “And Avraham was old,” through “and you shall take a wife for my son.” If, however, it is a day on which two Sifrei Torah are taken out in any event, they read this passage from the second Sefer Torah and do not take out a third. Their custom is also to translate that passage, and even the chazzan may translate it. This applies specifically to one marrying a virgin, but not to one marrying a widow.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the honor of the congregation is set aside.</b> This requires study. In the case of the Kohen Gadol they permitted reading by heart, but did not permit rolling the Sefer Torah in the presence of the congregation. This proves that we do not say that the congregation’s honor is set aside even where no other alternative exists. If so, here too he should read by heart, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Rashba. One must say that in the Beis HaMikdash all Israel were present and did not waive their honor, whereas in a synagogue only a small number are present and they presumably waive their honor in order to fulfill the reading of the Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh maftir and the Yom Tov maftir, which are enactments of the Geonim. See Maseches Sofrim, chapter 11, and what I wrote in siman 143, seif 4. See Bach, whose words require study. He confused “Ube’asor” with “Ach be’asor,” for we read “Ube’asor,” and in that passage we never skip. See there, and see Beit Yosef, siman 143, and what I wrote in siman 53, seif 6.

<b>ג' גברי</b>. משמע דהראשון אסור לקרות בס"ת השלישית ותימה דאנו רואין נוהגין דאף מי שקרא בתורה עולה למפטיר בס"ת שניה ונ"ל דלאו מנהג הגון הוא ויש למחות בידן עכ"ל הב"ח ולא ראיתי שום הוכחה לדבריו די"ל דאה"נ אם אין שם אחר היודע להפטיר דרשאי הראשון לקרות למפטיר (ועסי' רפ"ב ס"ה) אלא דהגמ' איירי כשיש שם יודעים ומשמיענו מנין הקרואין כדחשיב התם טובא במגילה דף כ"ט ועו"ק דלדבריו הל"ל מאי דמקשה לעיל מיניה למה נקט הטור ג' גברי דאתא לאשמועינן דהראשון אסור לקרות בס"ת השלישי ע"ש בב"ח ולכן נ"ל דשרי דליכא פגמא אלא כשקורא בשניהם זה אח"ז דאמרי' למה לא קרא בראשון אלא שחסר הפרשה בו אבל כשהפסיק בנתים ה"ל כקורא מחדש אך עכ"פ השביעי לא יקרא למפטיר בס"ת אחרת אלא יגללו הס"ת עד שיגיעו לשם כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Three men.</b> This implies that the first reader may not read from the third Sefer Torah. This is surprising, because we see that the custom is for one who already read from the Torah to be called for maftir from the second Sefer Torah. Bach writes that this is an improper custom and should be protested. I have seen no proof for his words. It may be that if no one else knows how to read the haftarah, the first reader may indeed be called for maftir. See siman 282, seif 5. The Gemara is discussing a case where others are capable, and its purpose is to teach the number of readers, as it enumerates many such cases in Megillah 29. There is also another difficulty: according to Bach, he should have said in the earlier challenge why the Tur specified three men, namely to teach that the first reader may not read from the third Sefer Torah. See Bach. It therefore appears to me that it is permitted. There is concern of aspersion only when one person reads from both scrolls consecutively, for people may say that he did not read the first passage from the first scroll because that passage was missing from it. When there is an intervening reader, however, it is like a new reading. Nevertheless, the seventh reader should not be called for maftir from another Sefer Torah. Rather, they should roll the first Sefer Torah until they reach the required passage. So it appears to me.
