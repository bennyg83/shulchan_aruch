(א) בנביא - היינו לפי מה שידוע דמדינא אין על הפטרה בנביא פסוקים מיוחדים רק שצריך להיות מעין הסדרא כדלקמן בסוף סימן תכ"ח בהג"ה ואשמועינן דמותר להמפטיר בנביא לדלג מפרשה זו לפרשה אחרת וכן המנהג בכמה הפטרות שמוסיפין להם פסוקים מפרשיות אחרות:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) In the Prophets. Since, as is known, the law does not prescribe specific verses for the haftarah, but only requires that it relate to the parashah, as stated below at the end of siman 428 in the gloss, this teaches that the maftir may skip in the Prophets from one passage to another. This is indeed the custom in several haftaros, where verses from other passages are added.

(ו) שלא ישהא וכו' - בזמניהם היו רגילין לתרגם בין פסוק לפסוק ואשמועינן שיהיו קרובים הענינים זה לזה כ"כ עד שלא ישהא הדלוג יותר מכדי שיפסוק התורגמן שאין כבוד לצבור שיעמדו בשתיקה וימתינו. וכתבו הפוסקים דבזמנינו שאין רגילין לתרגם נמי שרי כיון שכל ההפטרות כתובים בזמנינו בקונטרס בפני עצמן או בחומשין הלא יכול לסמן ולחפש מהר כל הפסוקים השייכים להפטרה זו ולא יהיו הצבור צריכין לעמוד בשתיקה עי"ז ולכן שרי ומטעם זה מותר בזמנינו אפילו כשהפסוקים הם משני נביאים ואפילו רחוקים זה מזה הרבה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(6) That he not delay, etc. In their times it was customary to translate between each verse and the next. This teaches that the passages must be close enough that the skipping does not take longer than the meturgeman needs to complete his translation, because it is disrespectful to the congregation for them to stand silently and wait. The poskim write that nowadays, when translation is not customary, it is nevertheless permitted, since all the haftaros are written in separate booklets or in Chumashim. One can mark and quickly locate all the verses belonging to that haftarah, so the congregation will not need to stand silently. For this reason, nowadays it is permitted even when the verses come from two different prophets and are very far apart.

(ט) לתחלתו - ר"ל לצד תחלתו שהוא למפרע ואפילו הם סמוכים זה לזה. והנה יש דעות בין הפוסקים י"א דדוקא מנביא לנביא אין מדלגין בזה [ואפילו בנביא של תרי עשר] אבל בנביא אחד מדלגין אפילו למפרע אם הם סמוכין ולא יצטרכו הצבור לעמוד בשתיקה והמ"א מצדד וכן פסק בא"ר דאפילו בנביא אחד אין לדלג למפרע ועיין בפמ"ג שמצדד דבענין אחד בנביא אחד מותר לקרות למפרע:
**** ENGLISH ****
(9) Toward its beginning. Meaning, toward the beginning, namely backward, even when the passages are adjacent. There is a dispute among the poskim. Some say that only from one prophet to another may one not skip backward, even within Trei Asar, but within a single prophet one may skip backward if the passages are close and the congregation will not need to stand silently. Magen Avraham inclines to prohibit skipping backward even within one prophet, and Eliyah Rabbah rules accordingly. Peri Megadim, however, inclines to permit reading backward within one prophet when the passages concern one subject.

**** ENGLISH ****
(17) With two Torah scrolls — whether regarding one subject or two subjects, such as on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh or on Yom Tov, where the law is that they read the haftarah maftir from another scroll already rolled and prepared for that place, so they need not roll the first scroll in the presence of the congregation, as likewise in siman 63. One should not call for maftir one who was called up last, that he too ascend for maftir on the second scroll, even if there is no other who knows how to read the haftarah — for it appears to cast a defect on the first scroll as though it is missing [a portion] or otherwise pasul; rather they roll the first Torah scroll until they reach that place. And if they wish to call for maftir from among those already called up, before the last one — Magen Avraham permits, for a defect applies only when one reads in both one after the other, for they will say: why did he not read in the first, except that the portion was missing in it; but when he interrupted in between with another person, it is now like reading anew. Bach forbids; and Eshel Avraham sides with the law per Bach's reason, lest they now say the defect of the first scroll is known, and therefore he read from another; but on Simchat Torah Eshel Avraham sides to be lenient in this, like Magen Avraham, that it is permitted to call for Bereishit one who already ascended in the first Torah scroll, since he has further branches to permit there.
