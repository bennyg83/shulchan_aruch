<b>וממ"ש סי' תכ"ב בשם רד"א כו'</b> דס"ל דדבר הבא משלשים לשלשים מברכים עליו זמן ולכן הקש' למה אין מברכים זמן על הלל דר"ח ודאי דאם החדש חסר דאז ר"ח ביום שלשים ראוי שלא לברך זמן אבל כשהחדש מלא דר"ח ביום שלשים וביום ל"א וכיון דבקיאים בקביעא דירחא וידעי' דיומ' בתרא עיקר וממנו מונים למועדים א"כ הלל שאמרו ביום ל' כמאן דליתא וכאלו לא אמרו רק מזמורי תהלים כיון שאינו אלא מצד מנהג אבותינו וא"כ ההלל שאומרים ביום ב' דר"ח דהיינו ביום ל"א היה ראוי לברך עליו אלא משום לא פלוג בין חדש מלא לחסר הרי דבחדש חסר ניח' ליה דאין מברכים זמן משום דאין בין אמירת הלל מן ר"ח העבר לאמירת הלל בר"ח זה כ"א כ"ח יום ולא מקרי בא מזמן לזמן לברך עליו שהחיינו וה"ה הכא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And from what is written in siman 422 in the name of Abudraham, etc.</b> Abudraham holds that one recites Shehecheyanu over something that recurs from thirty days to thirty days. He therefore asks why Shehecheyanu is not recited over Hallel on Rosh Chodesh. Certainly, when the preceding month is deficient and Rosh Chodesh occurs on day thirty, it is understandable that Shehecheyanu is not recited. But when the month is full and Rosh Chodesh is observed on both day thirty and day thirty-one, since we are expert in establishing the calendar and know that the second day is the principal day from which the festivals are counted, the Hallel recited on day thirty is regarded as though it had not been recited, or as though ordinary chapters of Tehillim had been said, since its recitation is only due to ancestral custom. Accordingly, one should recite Shehecheyanu over the Hallel said on the second day of Rosh Chodesh, day thirty-one. The reason one nevertheless does not is that the Sages did not distinguish between a full month and a deficient month. This shows that, regarding a deficient month, Abudraham accepts that no Shehecheyanu is recited because only twenty-eight days separate the Hallel of the previous Rosh Chodesh from the Hallel of the present Rosh Chodesh, and this is not considered an event recurring from time to time for which Shehecheyanu is recited. The same applies here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It appears to me that this depends upon the dispute, etc.</b> At the end of tractate Rosh Hashanah it is stated that one must review a prayer recited only at intervals before praying it, so that it will be fluent in his mouth. Rosh holds that this applies only to the prayers of the festivals, whereas the Rosh Chodesh prayer is not considered a prayer recited only at intervals and need not be reviewed. Rambam holds that even the Rosh Chodesh prayer is considered one recited only at intervals and must be reviewed. Beit Yosef ruled above in siman 100 in accordance with Rambam. The same applies here, for Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter HaRo’eh write, s.v. one who sees: “It appears to Rabbeinu that this applies specifically to one who sees it once in thirty days, like one who sees the Great Sea at intervals, for the Yerushalmi states that ‘at intervals’ means thirty days.” Thus, however the term “at intervals” is defined regarding prayer in siman 100, the same definition applies here.

<b>(ס"ק ו) יוצא כו'</b> שנולדו קודם לכן דלא תימ' דוקא הנולדים אחר כן דהם שותפים בנס האב ולולי שניצל האב הם לא באו לעולם משא"כ הנולדים קודם לכן קמ"ל דאפ"ה צריכים לברך דזהו מכלל דכיבוד אב ואייתי ראי' ממ"ש בסעיף וי"ו דעל נס רבו צ"ל וע"כ משום דזהו בכלל כיבוד ומורא רבו וה"ה באביו ואפי' בן בנו הנולד קודם לכן יש לברך דהא חייב גם בכבוד אבי אביו וכמ"ש בי"ד סי' ר"מ סעיף כ"ד בהג"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 6: His descendants, etc.</b> This includes those born before the miracle. One might otherwise have thought that only children born afterward must bless, since they share in their father’s miracle, for had their father not been saved they would never have come into the world, whereas children already born before the miracle would not. The ruling teaches that they too must bless, because this is included in honoring one’s father. Proof is brought from seif 6, which states that one must bless for a miracle performed for one’s teacher, necessarily because this is included in honoring and fearing one’s teacher. The same applies to one’s father. Even a grandson born before the miracle must bless, since he too is obligated to honor his paternal grandfather, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 240, seif 24, in the gloss.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 9: A person, etc.</b> The Yerushalmi, etc., and it resolved that if, etc. The Yerushalmi states, according to the Tur’s version: “What blessing should a person recite for a miracle performed for his teacher? If he was a distinguished person, such as Yoav son of Tzeruyah and his companions, or a person through whom Hashem’s Name was sanctified, such as Daniel and his companions,” and so forth. Magen Avraham understands that the Yerushalmi’s inquiry regarding a teacher was definitely resolved only when the teacher was distinguished or when Hashem’s Name was sanctified through him, in which case one certainly blesses. Since the Yerushalmi does not explicitly conclude, “But if his teacher was not distinguished, he does not bless,” it implies that this case remained doubtful and was not resolved.

However, according to Beit Yosef’s explanation of the Yerushalmi, the meaning is as follows. Beit Yosef challenges the Tur, who writes that one blesses for a miracle performed for one’s teacher, implying that this applies to every teacher, and cites proof from the Yerushalmi above. On the contrary, the Yerushalmi appears to limit the blessing to a teacher who was distinguished. Beit Yosef answers that the Tur understood the Yerushalmi as resolving that, since we already hold that one blesses for a distinguished person even if he is not one’s teacher, it follows logically that one blesses for one’s teacher even if he is not distinguished. According to this explanation, it is also understandable that one blesses for a distinguished person even if he is not one’s teacher. The same applies according to the version that Beit Yosef writes was before the Rosh; see there.

This requires study, for it contradicts all the poskim. Eliyah Rabbah and Chok Yaakov write that Magen Avraham overlooked Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter HaRo’eh, 54b, s.v. the stones, cited by Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. Tosafos write: “The same applies to the place where Sancheriv’s camp fell, for Chizkiyahu is no less than a distinguished person,” and later: “It again appeared to Rabbeinu Yitzchak that since the miracle is not recognizable from the place, unlike these other cases, no one blesses there except those for whom the miracle occurred.” Thus, had the event of Sancheriv been recognizable from the place, others would bless, even though the miracle was not performed for all Israel, because Chizkiyahu is no less than a distinguished person. Necessarily, one blesses for a miracle performed for a distinguished person when the miracle is recognizable from the place. This is the substance of their expanded explanation of Tosafos.

ולענ"ד המ"א לא הבין כן כוונת התוס' דא"א דכוונתם של התוס' במ"ש דל"ג מאדם מסוים ר"ל כיואב בן צרוי' מאין פשיט' להתוס' דמברכים על נס של אדם מסוים הא לא הוזכר מזה בש"ס דילן ואי כוונתם על ירוש' הנ"ל עכ"פ ה"ל להתוס' להביא דברי הירושלמי ולפרשן כי גם פשט ל' ירושלמי אינו מבואר כן להדי' כנ"ל א"ו כוונת התוס' אדם מסוים שנתקדש ש"ש על ידו כגון דניאל וזה פשוט ומבואר כן בש"ס דילן דף נ"ז ע"ב דרש ר"ה כו' ראה גוב של אריות או כבשן אש (של דניאל חנני' מ"ו) מברך כו' וע"ז כתבו דלא גרע חזקי' מאדם מסוים דהא גם באותו נס נתקדש ש"ש וכדאי' בסדר עולם הובא בילקוט מלכים ב' סי' רל"ו ד"ה שלום הרג וגו' הנני נותן בו רוח ושמע וגו' אליך ישתחוו ואליך יתפללו שהם מתנים שבחו של מקום ואומרים אך בך אל ואין עוד אפס אלהים וגו' אחר מפלתו של סנחריב עמד חזקי' ופטר האוכלסין שבאו עמו בקולרין וקבלו עליהן עול מלכות שמים שנ' ביום ההוא וגו' הביאו רש"י בישעי' סי' מ"ה פ' י"ד בקוצר אבל מסוים כיואב לא שמענו מדברי התוס':
**** ENGLISH ****
In my humble opinion, Magen Avraham did not understand Tosafos in that manner. It is impossible that Tosafos’ statement that Chizkiyahu was “no less than a distinguished person” means a person like Yoav son of Tzeruyah. From where would Tosafos know with certainty that one blesses for a miracle performed for a distinguished person? This is not mentioned in our Talmud. If their intent was based upon the Yerushalmi above, Tosafos should at least have cited and explained the Yerushalmi, especially since the plain wording of the Yerushalmi is not clearly explained that way, as noted above. Rather, Tosafos mean a distinguished person through whom Hashem’s Name was sanctified, such as Daniel. This is explicit in our Talmud, 57b, where Rav Hamnuna teaches that one who sees a lions’ den or a fiery furnace, referring to the miracles of Daniel, Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah, recites a blessing. Tosafos therefore write that Chizkiyahu was no less than such a distinguished person, since Hashem’s Name was likewise sanctified through his miracle. This is stated in Seder Olam, cited in Yalkut Shimoni to II Kings, siman 236: after Sancheriv’s downfall, Chizkiyahu released the armies who had come with him in chains, and they accepted the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven. Rashi cites this briefly on Yeshayah 45:14. But Tosafos provide no proof concerning an ordinary distinguished person such as Yoav.
