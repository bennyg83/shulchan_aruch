<b>וכן</b> עט״ז מסיק דדילג ב׳ תיבות צום תעניתי או תעניתנו כיון דלרוב הפוסקים לא הוי תענית נמצא שקרן בתפלתו וכ״כ באות ה׳ משמע אף בלא קבלו במנחה וגם תענית שעות יאמר עננו בדילוג ב׳ תיבות ומשמע דטוב יותר לומר אחר התפלה באלקי נצור מלהפסיק בשומע תפלה דלרוב הפוסקים הוי הפסקה שלא לצורך וי״ל והנה בעשי״ת שמתענין תענית שעות עד אחר מנחה גדולה וכדומה יראה דאסור לאכול עד שיתפלל מנחה תחלה כמו חתן בס״ב עמ״א ב׳ והוא מתפ״ד סימן קנ״ז אמנם לכאורה זה למ״ד דיאמר צום תעניתנו דלא יהיה שקרן בתפלה אחר שכבר אכל משא״כ אם דולג ב׳ תיבות אפשר דיכול לאכול אחר חצות בלא תפלת מנחה ואח״כ כשמתפלל עם הציבור מנחה יאמר עננו ביום צרה אף שאכל כיון דאין מזכיר צום תעניתנו אם התנה בכך לאחר חצות יאכל אי כוונתו כך היה וצ״ע כעת וע׳ תה״ד קנ״ז ואי״ה במ״א יבואר עוד. ובט״ז ב׳ מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Likewise.</b> See Taz. He concludes that one should omit the two words ‘the fast of my fasting’ or ‘the fast of our fasting,’ because according to most poskim it is not considered a fast, and he would consequently speak falsely in his prayer. He writes the same in paragraph 5. This implies that even without accepting it at Minchah, and even for a partial-day fast, he should recite Aneinu while omitting those two words. It also appears preferable to recite it after the prayer in Elokai Netzor rather than interrupt in Shomea Tefillah, because according to most poskim it would be an unnecessary interruption. This can be answered. During the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, when people observe a partial-day fast until after Minchah Gedolah or the like, it appears that they may not eat until they first pray Minchah, like a chassan in seif 2; see Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, citing Terumas HaDeshen, siman 157. At first glance, however, this applies only according to the opinion that he should say ‘the fast of our fasting,’ so that he not speak falsely in his prayer after he has already eaten. If he omits those two words, perhaps he may eat after midday without first praying Minchah, and afterward, when he prays Minchah with the congregation, he may say, ‘Answer us on the day of distress,’ even though he ate, since he does not mention ‘the fast of our fasting,’ provided that he stipulated that he would eat after midday and this was his intention. This presently requires study. See Terumas HaDeshen, siman 157. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham. See also Taz, seif katan 2, on this matter.

<b>שאין</b> עט״ז משמע דעתו ז״ל האידנא אע״ג אותן שמתענים רק ב׳ או ג״י בעשי״ת אין לקבלם במנחה שלפניו כי נוהגים מעולם כך ומש״ה ת״צ והשלים ג״כ אף כה״ג כיון דנהגו כך הוי כמו שמתענים כל עשי״ת ומ״ש כאלו פי׳ שאין רוצה להשלים היינו מחמת קושיא שהקשה אח״כ מסי״ב דאם הם מקובלים ועומדים צריך להשלים אלא הפירוש בטור שאין רגילות כלומר בכוונה הם עושים שלא לקבל רק צערא בעלמא א״כ אין חילוק בין כל י׳ ימי תשובה או רק ב׳ או ג״י. אבל בתה״ד סי׳ קמ״ו פירש דתרתי טעמי בעינן שאין מקבלים וגם רק צערא וע׳ אות שאח״ז ובמ״א ואי״ה שם יבואר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That they do not.</b> See Taz. His view appears to be that nowadays, even those who fast only two or three days during the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah should not accept those fasts at the preceding Minchah, because this has always been the practice. Therefore, a communal fast and its completion likewise apply in such a case, for once this is the established practice, it is as though they fast throughout the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah. His words, ‘as though he expressly stated that he does not wish to complete it,’ arise from the question he later asks from seif 12: if these fasts are already accepted and standing, they should require completion. Rather, the Tur’s expression that ‘it is not customary’ means that they intentionally refrain from accepting them and intend only discomfort. If so, there is no distinction between fasting all ten days of repentance and fasting only two or three days. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 146, however, explains that both reasons are required: they do not formally accept the fast, and their purpose is merely discomfort. See the following paragraph and Magen Avraham; with Hashem’s help, this will be explained there.

<b>מי״ז</b> בתמז עט״ז בתה״ד סי׳ קמ״ו כתב דתרתי טעמי בעינן בעשי״ת דאין משלימין א׳ שאין רגילות לקבלם רק מקובלים ועומדים ממנהג וגם י״ל לצעורי מכוין ומש״ה המתענה ב׳ או ג״י צריך לקבל דלאו מנהגא הוא וממילא צריך להשלים וה״ה מי״ז תמוז אע״ג דאין רגילות לקבלם מ״מ ל״ש לצעורי על העבר ומש״ה משלימין מי״ז בתמוז ועל זה קשה מתענית י״ב א׳ והב״ח הקשה על זה דעל החורבן לא שייך לצעורי על העבר ואמנם הט״ז ז״ל רמז עוד קושיא דודאי ת״ב נדחה ליום א׳ א״צ קבלת תענית במנחה ומקובלין ועומדין דברים וע׳ אות ב׳ כיון דא״צ לקבל א״כ הוא רק טעם צער לחודיה ואיך הקילו שם מפני שי״ט שלהם היה הא קיי״ל בטלה מגילת תענית ומותר להתענות באותן הימים ואי דלצעורי מכוין הא צער לחוד לא מהני ע״כ תירץ תרי גווני צער יש והתם הא לא כוונו כלל שיהא נחשב לתענית בט״ב ביום א׳ רק לזכר בעלמא ולא לשם תענית כלל משא״כ כשמכוין שיהא תענית ולצעוריה נפשיה אז י״ל בין עבר לעתיד. ובזה מתורץ מ״ש התוס׳ בתענית שם ד״ה התם דהקשו דבטלה ושרי להתענות בי״ט האלו ותירץ דסבר דלא בטלה ואסור להתענות ולכן לא השלימו התענית וא״כ קשה מ״ש המחבר לעיל בתקנ״ט ס״ט בעל ברית כו׳ מראב״צ דהוא סובר לא בטלה ויהיה י״ט שלהם ואסור משא״כ בעל ברית לית כאן י״ט מדבריהם ולכך פירש הט״ז דודאי התוס׳ שהקשו דבטלה היינו כיון שבטלוה אחר החורבן ורשאים להתענות בהם כי ערבה כל שמחה מסתמא ראב״צ היה ראוי לו שיתענה וישלים ומצוה קעביד אף שי״ל מדינא הואיל ונדחה מקילין כמו בעל ברית כו׳ ותירצו דסובר ראב״צ לא בטלה ואסור להתענות אבל הט״ז פירש די״ל דבטל אלא דמדינא כיון דנדחה והיה י״ט שלהם א״צ להתענות כלל רק לצעורי לזכר בעלמא לא לשם תענית כלל וזה לא היה כאשר להם מעולם אף קודם החורבן לזכר בעלמא תענית שעות וממילא לא היה בכלל ביטול דליהוי מצוה לאדם גדול להתענות אח״כ ומש״ה לא השלימו וא״ש ההיא דתקנ״ט ועדיין צ״ע קצת. ובתוס׳ ציינו התם לצעורי ולא ציינו מפני שי״ט שלנו י״ל ע״פ זה קצת ועמ״א תקנ״ט אות י״א ותקע״ב ס״ב ואי״ה שם יבואר. ועמ״א ו׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From the seventeenth of Tammuz.</b> See Taz. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 146, writes that two reasons are required regarding the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, when the fasts are not completed: first, it is not customary to accept them formally, for they are already accepted by force of custom; and second, one may say that the intention is merely to cause oneself discomfort. Therefore, one who fasts only two or three days must accept the fast, since this is not the established custom, and consequently must complete it. The same applies from the seventeenth of Tammuz: although it is not customary to accept that fast formally, the notion of causing oneself discomfort over the past is inapplicable, and therefore the fast from the seventeenth of Tammuz is completed. This is difficult in light of Taanis 12a. Bach also objects that, regarding the destruction, causing oneself discomfort over the past is indeed relevant. Taz hints at a further difficulty. Certainly when Tishah B’Av is postponed to Sunday, no acceptance at Minchah is required, because the fast is already accepted and standing; see paragraph 2. Since no acceptance is required, only the rationale of discomfort remains. How, then, were they lenient there because it was their Yom Tov? We rule that Megillas Taanis was annulled, and fasting on those days is permitted. If the explanation is that they intended merely discomfort, discomfort alone is insufficient. He therefore answers that there are two kinds of discomfort. In that case, they did not intend at all that the observance on Sunday should be regarded as the Tishah B’Av fast, but only as a remembrance, not as a fast at all. By contrast, when one intends it to be a fast and to cause oneself discomfort, one may distinguish between distress over the past and distress concerning the future. This resolves Tosafos in Taanis there, s.v. ‘There,’ who ask that Megillas Taanis was annulled and fasting on those festive days is permitted. They answer that the authority there holds it was not annulled and that fasting was forbidden, and therefore they did not complete the fast. If so, the Mechaber’s ruling above in siman 559, seif 9, regarding one involved in a bris, etc., is difficult. Rabbi Elazar ben Tzadok held that Megillas Taanis was not annulled, so that day was their Yom Tov and fasting was forbidden, whereas for one involved in a bris there is no such rabbinically ordained Yom Tov. Taz therefore explains that Tosafos’ question from the annulment means that, since Megillas Taanis was annulled after the destruction and they were permitted to fast on those days because all joy had ceased, Rabbi Elazar ben Tzadok presumably ought to have fasted and completed the fast, thereby performing a mitzvah. Although one could say that, as a matter of law, because the fast was postponed they were lenient, as with one involved in a bris, Tosafos answer that Rabbi Elazar ben Tzadok held Megillas Taanis was not annulled and fasting was forbidden. Taz, however, explains that it may indeed have been annulled, but because the fast was postponed and the day had formerly been their Yom Tov, they were not required to fast at all. They observed only a measure of discomfort as a remembrance, not as a fast. Such a partial-day observance had never been prohibited to them, even before the destruction, and therefore it was not included in the annulment in a manner that would later make it a mitzvah for a distinguished person to fast. They therefore did not complete it, and the ruling in siman 559 is understandable. The matter still requires some study. Tosafos cite the phrase ‘to cause discomfort’ rather than ‘because it was our Yom Tov’; this may be somewhat explained according to the foregoing. See Magen Avraham, siman 559, seif katan 11, and siman 572, seif 2. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there. See also Magen Avraham, seif katan 6.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If.</b> See Taz, who discusses this at length. At first glance, one could say that the halachah follows Rosh in his responsum, that a firm mental resolve is ineffective regarding charity, and that fasting is different because it closely resembles an offering. If so, Rama’s statement in his gloss in Choshen Mishpat and in Yoreh Deah 258:13, that the primary view is the first opinion because Rosh ruled this way regarding a fast, is not a proof, as explained. It would follow that if one merely contemplated fasting for several hours as a measure of discomfort, so as not to separate from the congregation, as discussed in paragraph 3, perhaps this does not resemble an offering and thought is not equivalent to speech. This too requires study. Maharik, however, maintains that Rosh’s responsum, which rules that thought is not equivalent to speech regarding charity, disagrees with what Rosh wrote in Taanis in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam. Therefore, although Rosh rules in the responsum that thought is not equivalent to speech for charity, the principal ruling follows what he wrote in his halachic rulings regarding a fast, and the same applies to charity: thought is equivalent to speech, because his rulings take precedence over his responsum where they conflict. Mordechai also cites Rashi that, regarding charity, thought is equivalent to speech. End quote from Maharik. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Yoreh Deah, siman 18, that even regarding a vow, thought is not equivalent to speech and one’s mouth and heart must agree. See paragraph 9 regarding when thought is effective. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there.

<b>בשעת</b> עט״ז עיין אות ז׳ ואות ח׳ ואע״ג דתענית כנדר קדשים מ״מ בעינן בשעת תפלה דוקא דאל״כ אין ההרהור קובע עליו חובה דדרך להתנחם ועא״ר אות ה׳ חוכך בזה ועסי׳ תקס״ג משמע כהט״ז דהיינו באם הוציא בשפתיו אף קודם חצות או זמן הרבה קודם עכ״פ מחוייב לשלם הנדר ולהתענות יום זה משא״כ בהרהור כ״ז שלא הרהר בשעתו הקבוע לא הוי כדיבור ורשאי לחזור אח״כ ולאכול ביום המחרת ואי״ה יבואר לקמן עוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the time.</b> See Taz, and see paragraphs 7 and 8. Although a fast is like a vow concerning sacred matters, it nevertheless must be undertaken specifically during prayer. Otherwise, the thought does not establish an obligation upon him, since a person is prone to reconsider. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, who is uncertain about this. See siman 563, which appears to agree with Taz: if he expressed it verbally, even before midday or a long time beforehand, he is in any event obligated to fulfill the vow and fast that day. By contrast, where there was only thought, so long as he did not contemplate it at the prescribed time, thought is not equivalent to speech, and he may later retract and eat on the following day. <b>שני</b> וחמישי עט״ז בשם מהר״מ מינץ ז״ל מי שקבל סתם להתענות בה״ב באיזה ימים ולא פי׳ יכולין להתענות ג״פ ה׳ ב׳ ה׳ ג״כ יי״ח וה״ה סירוגין דהיינו בכמה שבועות ישלים בה״ב ודי ויי״ח. ומ״ש המחבר יש מי שמסתפק (כצ״ל) עיין סעיף י״ב בט״ז אות י״ו יע״ש ואי״ה שם יבואר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Monday and Thursday.</b> See Taz in the name of Maharam Mintz. One who accepted without specification to observe a Monday-Thursday-Monday fast on certain days, but did not specify which days, may fulfill his obligation by fasting Thursday-Monday-Thursday three times as well. The same applies if he observes them intermittently, meaning that over the course of several weeks he completes the Monday-Thursday-Monday sequence; this suffices and he fulfills his obligation. The Mechaber’s wording should read, ‘There is one who is uncertain.’ See seif 12, Taz, paragraph 16; see there. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there.
