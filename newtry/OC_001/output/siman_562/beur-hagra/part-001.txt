**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or that the moon, etc.</b> Yerushalmi, first chapter of Berachos: It was taught: as long as the face of the sun is reddening, that is day; when they pale, that is bein hashemashot; when they blacken and the upper becomes equal to the lower, that is night. Rebbi says: the moon in its tekufah: when the solar disk begins to set and the lunar disk begins to rise, that is bein hashemashot. Said R' Chanina: [when] the end of the solar disk [begins] to set and the lunar disk begins to rise. Meaning, that R' Chanina disagrees and says that the moon does not begin to rise until the end of the sun's setting; and he brings a support there, and Shmuel taught similarly: the moon does not shine at the hour the sun sets, and does not set at the hour the sun rises. Meaning, that it does not shine at the time of [the sun's] setting, but only after its setting is finished. And even though Tosafos HaRosh explained the Yerushalmi regarding the two shkiyos, that the first Tanna holds that the first shkiyah is bein hashemashot and R' Chanina said the second shkiyah, his interpretation is not aligned, for he said "as long as the face, etc.," and that is after the second shkiyah, as is written at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos; rather everything refers to the second shkiyah, for there is no view that during the first shkiyah the measure of four mil is all bein hashemashot; rather the first Tanna holds that the beginning of the lunar disk rising is bein hashemashot, which is the beginning of shkiyah, and R' Chanina said that it is night, which is the end of shkiyah; and "to rise" means to shine, for it rises even by day.
