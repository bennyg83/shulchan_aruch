**** ENGLISH ****
Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote, etc., the stringency of Tisha B'Av, etc., and the same applies to other communal fasts in Eretz Yisrael, [that] if not for [that stringency] its bein hashemashot would be permitted. Meaning, bein hashemashot of entering the fast. "Its doubt is permitted": meaning, since specifically on Tisha B'Av it is severe to forbid bein hashemashot of entering the fast, but other fasts, even though they are forbidden in eating on the night before the fast day, nevertheless bein hashemashot, which is a doubt of night, is permitted. If so, the same applies at the exit of the fast day: we should permit bein hashemashot even though it is a doubt of day; for example, erev Tisha B'Av. Meaning, by the force of the law, if not for the stringency of Tisha B'Av, as above.

**** ENGLISH ****
(s.k. 3) The chazzan, etc. For the main ruling we hold, etc., as is written in se'if 11: that what we say in the Gemara "they fast for hours" is specifically when he did not eat all day, and in those manners explained in se'if 10 and 11; but if he ate before nightfall it is not called a fast regarding saying Aneinu. Only regarding an individual's fast do we rely on the view of the Rambam, who holds that a fast of an hour is called [a fast] even if he did not complete the fast and ate after midday; and since anyway an individual does not fix for Aneinu a blessing by itself but includes it in Shomea Tefillah, if so even according to those who disagree with the Rambam there is nothing wrong in this, for in Shomea Tefillah he may add whatever he wishes, as above in siman 119. And what the Taz wrote in s.k. 1 to skip the two words "the fast of our affliction": and if they skip, they do well. But what the Taz wrote to cast doubt on the Rama's ruling for two reasons: first, since the primary [view] is like those who disagree with the Rambam, if so when he says "the fast of our affliction" he is speaking lies, since according to those who disagree with the Rambam there is no designation of a fast upon him; of what avail is it that he may add in Shomea Tefillah? Nevertheless he is bringing a lie out of his mouth. And further, according to those who disagree it is an interruption in the middle of a blessing; and he brought a proof from the Tur's words, who wrote in siman 563 that because of a doubt of the law one should not accept a fast in Shomea Tefillah because of interruption; his words are brought in the Taz in this siman, s.k. 7; see there. In my humble opinion one may dispute his words. What he wrote, that if he says "the fast of our affliction" he is speaking lies: one may say, granted that according to those who disagree with the Rambam it is not called a fast, that is regarding saying Aneinu and fixing it as a blessing by itself, for whenever he does not complete the fast the Sages did not enact fixing it as a blessing by itself; but nevertheless it is called a fast, for the root of the name "fast" is affliction, and he in any case afflicted and pained himself. And a proof for this is from what is stated in Taanis, folio 12a, regarding that which Rav Chisda said: any fast upon which the sun did not set (that is, tzeit hakochavim) is not called a fast.

**** ENGLISH ****
And Terumat HaDeshen disagrees specifically regarding the Ten Days of Repentance, where their intent in their fast is for the sake of teshuvah, that they wish to return to Hashem: in that we say that he accepted to afflict himself, and it resembles the men of the mishmar who were likewise fasting for the future, that their korban be accepted. But those who fast from the 17th of Tammuz until the 9th of Av, who fast for the past, resemble the fast of the 17th of Tammuz and Tisha B'Av: we do not say he intends only to afflict himself, but rather for the sake of a fast, and completion is required. And upon this the Bach asked: but afterward the Gemara asks against Rav Chisda from that which R' Elazar ben Tzadok said: I was of the sons of Sena'ah ben Binyamin, for whom the 10th of Av was their Yom Tov, when they would bring the wood-offering when the Beis HaMikdash stood, on the 10th of Av; and once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbos and was deferred until Sunday, that is, the 10th of Av; and since it was the Yom Tov of his family members they would fast on it and not complete it; and it answers: there too it is mere affliction: hence R' Elazar ben Tzadok fasted a deferred Tisha B'Av which was for the past, and even so he did not complete because it was mere affliction. And it appears to me that there is no difficulty, etc.; but an individual, etc. Meaning, that in Terumat HaDeshen there it is explained that this is the reason that one who fasts from the 17th of Tammuz until Tisha B'Av must complete; and this is his language: for there they did not accept mere affliction upon themselves; on the contrary they resemble the four fasts of the 17th of Tammuz and Tisha B'Av and the 3rd of Tishrei and the 10th of Teves, on which they fast for the churban; and these Three Weeks too are alluded to in Daniel, to fast in them for the churban, etc.; end of his words. His intent appears: presumably the intent of one who fasts in the Three Weeks, since they are for the churban, is to fast like the fasts that Chazal fixed for the churban, that is, to complete; which is not so regarding R' Elazar ben Tzadok: since it was his Yom Tov he was not obligated to fast at all, and what he fasted part of the day was not because of the churban but to share in the congregation's affliction as they fast; if so R' Elazar ben Tzadok's fast was not for the past, that is, the churban, but only now to share in the congregation's trouble; and similarly the Taz answered.
