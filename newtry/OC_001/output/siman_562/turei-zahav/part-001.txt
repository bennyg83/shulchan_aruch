<b>וכן דעת מקצת רבוואת' </b> הוא דעת הרמב"ם שכתב אפי' אכל ושתה ואח"כ התחיל להתענות מקרי  תעני' לשעות והוא ע"פ הירושלמי ונגד תלמודינו דאר"ח והוא שלא אכל כל אותו היום וכבר כתבו בשם הרשב"א ששמע שהרמב"ם חזר בו והצריך דוקא שלא טעם כל היום וע"כ תמוה מה שכ' רמ"א שהיחיד יאמר ענינו דהיאך יאמר ביום צום תעניתנו ותעניתו אינו תענית ומו"ח זכרונו לברכה כתב שמכל מקום רשות הוא לומר ענינו וכת' שהרמב"ם ורמב"ן והרז"ה והר"ן ס"ל דקבלת תענית קודם שעות התענית מהני כו' ובאמת לא ס"ל כן אלא אם על כל פנים התענה כל היום רק שקצת היום היה בלא קבלת תענית אבל מ"מ לא אכל כל היום וכמ"ש הטור ומוזכר לקמן ס"י רק שהרמב"ם היה סובר כן דלא בעי' תענית כל היום וראבי"ה ביקש לתרצו ומה לנו לתירוצו כיון שהוא עצמו חוזר בו כמ"ש הרשב"א רק שמרדכי בשם ראב"ן והג"א ס"ל דאפ"ה מתפלל ענינו ונלע"ד דודאי אין אחר מנהג כלום מ"מ מאן דלא מתפלל ענינו בתענית שאינו משלים מרויח ספק ברכה לרוב פוסקים ואע"ג דאין לזה ברכה בפ"ע ביחיד אלא כוללה בש"ת מ"מ לשון צום תעניתנו אין הגון לדידהו דסתם תענית הוא יום שלם ותו דכיון דלפוסקים שלא לומר ענינו הוי הפסק בברכה של ש"ת כמ"ש הטור סי' תקס"ג לענין קבלת תענית בש"ת טוב שלא להפסיק בתפלה והדולג אז ב' תיבות אלו  צום תעניתנו יפה עושה לע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so is the view of some of the authorities.</b> This is the view of the Rambam, who wrote that even if he ate and drank and afterward began to fast, it is called a fast for hours, and that is according to the Yerushalmi, and against our Talmud, where Rav Chisda said: and that is provided he did not eat that entire day. And they have already written in the name of the Rashba that he heard that the Rambam retracted and required specifically that he not taste all day. And therefore what the Rama wrote, that an individual should say Aneinu, is astonishing: how can he say "on this day of the fast of our affliction" when his fast is not a fast? And my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that nevertheless one has license to say Aneinu, and he wrote that the Rambam, Ramban, Razah, and Ran hold that accepting a fast before the hours of the fast helps, etc. And in truth they do not hold so except if in any case he fasted the entire day, only that part of the day was without acceptance of a fast, but nevertheless he did not eat all day, as the Tur wrote, and as is mentioned below in se'if 10; only that the Rambam held so, that we do not require a fast for the entire day, and Ravyah sought to resolve him; and what do we need his resolution, since he himself retracted, as the Rashba wrote? Only that the Mordechai in the name of Ra'avan, and the Hagahos Asheri, hold that even so he prays Aneinu. And it appears to my humble opinion that certainly custom overrides nothing; nevertheless, one who does not pray Aneinu on a fast that he does not complete gains [avoidance of] a doubtful blessing according to most poskim; and even though for this there is no separate blessing for an individual, but rather he includes it in Shomea Tefillah, nevertheless the wording "the fast of our affliction" is not fitting according to them, for a plain "fast" means a full day; and furthermore, since according to the poskim who say not to say Aneinu it is an interruption in the blessing of Shomea Tefillah, as the Tur wrote in siman 563 regarding accepting a fast in Shomea Tefillah, it is good not to interrupt in the prayer; and one who then skips these two words, "the fast of our affliction," does well, in my humble opinion.

<b>שאין רגילות לקבלם. </b> כ"כ הטור ומרדכי ומפרש בת"ה דמקובלים ועומדים מכח מנהגם אבל המתענה רק ב' או ג' ימים לחוד צריכים לקבל וכמ"ש רמ"א בסמוך ונלע"ד אע"ג דעכשיו אין מקבלים  המתענים בכך מ"מ כל המתענה קצת ימים דעתו על כך כי המנהג כן הוא והוי כאלו פי' שאינו רוצה להשלים  וק"ל דאם הם מקובלים ועומדים ה"ל ממש קבל עליו כמ"ש בסי"ב אלא נרא' דהך שאין רגילים לקבל פי' שבכוונה אין מחזיקין זה לתענית אלא צער בעלמא וכמ"ש אח"ז  אפי' לדעת ת"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That it is not customary to accept them.</b> So wrote the Tur and Mordechai; and Terumat HaDeshen explains that they stand already accepted by force of their custom; but one who fasts only two or three days alone needs to accept, as the Rama wrote nearby. And it appears to my humble opinion: even though nowadays they do not accept those who fast in that way, nevertheless whoever fasts some days has his intent accordingly, for such is the custom, and it is as if he spelled out that he does not wish to complete. And it is difficult: if they stand already accepted, he has truly accepted upon himself, as is written in se'if 12; rather it appears that this "that it is not customary to accept" means that intentionally they do not maintain this as a fast, but only mere affliction, as he wrote afterward, even according to the view of Terumat HaDeshen.

<b>מי"ז בתמוז כו'. </b> כ' בת"ה דהנהו דמי לד' צומות של החורבן ול"ד לי' ימי תשובה שהתענית  לצערא בעלמא על העתיד כדי לקבל התפלה ויקרע גזר דינם וא"ל על בעל הת"ה ממה דפריך בגמ' על רב חסדא דבעי תעני' כל היום ת"ש דאר"א ב"צ פעם א'  חל ט"ב בשבת כו' ולא השלמנו מפני שי"ט שלנו היה ומשני  התם נמי לצעוריה נפשיה קא מכוין והא ר"א ב"צ לא התפלל על העתי' לק"מ  דמה שהקילו בשביל קבלת  צער לחוד היינו שלא כיוונו כלל שיהא זה תענית אלא צער וזכר בעלמא אבל בת"ה מיירי שהוא תענית ובזה מחלק בין עבר לעתיד ובזה מתורץ מה שמקשי' התוס' שם על מ"ש התם לצעורי כו' ואע"ג דאמרי' משחרב ב"ה  בטל' מגילת תענית כו' האי תנא ס"ל דלא בטל' ולפ"ד ניחא דתעני' שהוא בתורת תענית בטל האיסור מה שגזרו במגילת תענית דלא להתענות בי"ט  אבל קבלת צער בעלמא לא היה נאסר מעולם וע"כ לא היה בכלל הביטול כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>From the 17th of Tammuz, etc.</b> Terumat HaDeshen wrote that these resemble the four fasts of the churban, and are not comparable to the Ten Days of Repentance, where the fast is mere affliction for the future so that the prayer be accepted and their decree be torn up. And one cannot challenge the author of Terumat HaDeshen from what the Gemara asks against Rav Chisda, who required a fast for the entire day: Come and hear, for R' Elazar ben Tzadok said, Once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbos, etc., and we did not complete because it was our Yom Tov; and it answers, There too he intends to afflict himself; and behold R' Elazar ben Tzadok did not pray for the future: there is no difficulty, for what they were lenient about because of accepting affliction alone is that they did not intend at all that this be a fast, but only affliction and mere remembrance; but in Terumat HaDeshen he is dealing with a fast, and with this he distinguishes between past and future. And with this is resolved what Tosafos ask there on what is said there "to afflict, etc."; and even though we say that since the Beis HaMikdash was destroyed, Megillas Taanis was annulled, etc., this tanna holds that it was not annulled; and according to our words it works well: a fast that is in the category of a fast annulled the prohibition that they decreed in Megillas Taanis not to fast on Yom Tov; but accepting mere affliction was never forbidden, and therefore was not included in the annulment; as it appears to my humble opinion.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so it appears to me to practice regarding an individual's fast.</b> We have already written nearby regarding [a fast] upon which the sun did not set, concerning the Aneinu prayer; and so it is also here, for these two matters are equivalent: namely, completion of the fast and acceptance of the fast. And in a place where the Rama required an individual to say Aneinu, he should skip two words, namely "the fast of our affliction," for they are only mere affliction; and with this he fulfills according to all views, as it appears to my humble opinion.

<b>אימתי מקבלו. </b> בגמ' פליגי רב ס"ל במנח' דהיינו כשיגיע זמן המנחה וס"ל להרא"ש דקודם לזה אפי' יוציא בפיו לא מהני וראבי"ה ס"ל דכ"ש דמהני קודם למנח' ושמואל ס"ל בתפלת המנחה פר"ח שיאמר בש"ת הריני בתענית יחיד למחר יר"מ שתקבלני באהבה עכ"ל רי"ף. וכת' הטור וכיון דאיכא פלוגתא דרבוואתא הרי"ף פוסק כשמואל והראב"ד כרב טוב שלא להפסיק בתפלה פי' דכיון שיש סברא לפסוק כרב או כשמואל יש לנו לבחור כרב שלא להפסיק והב"י הביא בשם המגיד דיש בקצת ספרי הרמב"ם בלשון זה כשיתפלל תפלת המנחה או אחר המנחה וכן העתיק כאן ודבר פשוט שיותר טוב לאמרו אחר התפילה דהיינו קודם אלהי נצור ולא להפסיק בתפלה עצמה בש"ת וא"ל דהא קי"ל שואל אדם כל צרכיו בש"ת דהכא שאני שאומר אהא בתענית שזהו ענין נדר לא ענין תפלה והוה הפסק לאומרו קודם נצור וכ"כ בסמ"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When does he accept it?</b> In the Gemara they disagree: Rav holds [it is] at Minchah, that is, when the time of Minchah arrives, and the Rosh holds that before that, even if he utters it with his mouth, it does not help; and Ravyah holds that all the more so acceptance before Minchah helps; and Shmuel holds [it is] during the Minchah prayer. Rashi explained: that he should say in Shomea Tefillah, "Behold I am on an individual's fast tomorrow; may it be [Your] will that You accept me with love"; end of the Rif's words. And the Tur wrote: and since there is a dispute among the authorities, the Rif rules like Shmuel and the Ra'avad like Rav, it is good not to interrupt in the prayer; meaning, since there is reason to rule like Rav or like Shmuel, we should choose like Rav not to interrupt. And Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Maggid that in some texts of the Rambam the wording is: when he prays Minchah or after Minchah; and so he copied here; and it is obvious that it is better to say it after the prayer, that is, before Elokai Netzor, and not to interrupt in the prayer itself in Shomea Tefillah. And one cannot say that we hold a person may ask for all his needs in Shomea Tefillah, for here it is different: he says "I will be fasting," and that is a matter of neder, not a matter of prayer, and it is an interruption to say it before Netzor; and so wrote the Semak.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he contemplated in his heart.</b> This is the language of the Rosh, first chapter of Taanis: R' Yehudah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: if it was in his mind to fast from yesterday and he did not accept it upon himself at Minchah, but in his heart he was [resolved] to fast from yesterday, only that he did not bring it out of his mouth, it is properly called a fast, for it is included in "nediv lev," as is stated in the third chapter of Shevuos: that if he resolved in his heart, even though he did not bring it out with his lips, it helps; and even though it is said there that regarding a shevuah, resolving does not help until he brings it out with his lips, as it is written "to utter with the lips," it is logical that all matters of neder are learned one from another; for regarding tzedakah we expound "what goes out of your lips you shall keep," and resolving in the heart helps; and that we require acceptance in prayer is ideally required. And the Rosh concluded on this: and it appears to me that Rabbeinu Tam's proof was only for one who contemplated acceptance of a fast during Minchah and did not bring it out, from his bringing a proof from "nediv lev" and from tzedakah, that intent of the heart is as if he brought it out with his mouth; but hirhur of the entire day is not better than bringing it out with his mouth; end of his words. And the Tur rules so here; and it is difficult, for in siman 212 in Choshen Mishpat the Tur brings a responsum of the Rosh regarding one who buys land with the intent to make it hekdesh: if he brought nothing out of his mouth, they are unspoken matters of the heart; and Shmuel said, if he resolved in his heart he must bring it out with his lips; even though it is written "every nediv lev for olos," we say we do not learn chullin from kodashim; and nowadays every hekdesh has the law of chullin, for there is no hekdesh for bedek habayis now, and it is only for tzedakah; therefore he must bring it out with his lips, etc. If so, he does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam, and the words of the Rosh and the Tur contradict each other. And Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 228, brought in the name of Maharik, root 161, that the Rosh in this responsum does not take hold like Rabbeinu Tam, for in the first chapter of Taanis he wrote that regarding tzedakah resolving in the heart helps, and therefore he ruled that regarding tzedakah resolving in the heart does not help; end of his words. And also the Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Basra, wrote in the name of a responsum of Rashi that tzedakah is like nedarim and nedavos; therefore one who resolved in his heart to give tzedakah is obligated as if he brought it out of his mouth; end of his words. And it appears to my humble opinion that the Rosh and Tur agreed to Rabbeinu Tam's ruling regarding a fast; but nevertheless regarding the plain meaning of the text they did not agree. For it is stated in the third chapter of Shevuos: Shmuel said, if he resolved in his heart he must bring it out with his lips, as it is said "for whatever a person will utter in a shevuah"; they objected: "what goes out of your lips you shall keep and do": I have only [the case] where he brought it out with his lips; from where [do we know] if he resolved in his heart? The verse states "every nediv lev." That case is different, for it is written "every nediv lev"; and shall we learn from it? And they conclude: we do not learn chullin from kodashim. And one may ask against Rabbeinu Tam, who distinguishes shevuah from neder: what does the Gemara ask from a neder, where "what goes out of your lips" refers to a neder, for it is written regarding a neder, and Shmuel deals with a shevuah? And one cannot say that it asks we should learn shevuah from neder, and with this it answers that we do not learn chullin from kodashim; for if so, Rabbeinu Tam should not have distinguished between shevuah and neder, but between chullin and kodashim, whether in a shevuah or in a neder. And it appears to me that Rabbeinu Tam holds that certainly regarding a shevuah it never helps in any place, since we do not learn from kodashim; but regarding a neder we learn every neder from this verse, and we need no derivation for this, but rather everything was included in this verse, since the verse revealed to us regarding a plain neder, and we find regarding another neder that hirhur helps, namely regarding kodashim, and presumably everything is included, even chullin; which is not so of a shevuah, which was not included in this verse, but rather one now comes to learn from this verse: regarding that we say we do not learn chullin from kodashim. So it appears to me is Rabbeinu Tam's view. But the Rosh does not hold this rationale, that every neder is included in this verse; rather the verse is written specifically regarding kodashim, and you come to learn from it other nedarim; therefore he said we do not learn chullin from kodashim even regarding a neder itself; except that here regarding a fast it is considered truly like kodashim, for a fast stands in place of a korban, in that he offers his body to Heaven, better than tzedakah which is for the needs of people. And a proof for this is from the words of the Rosh, first chapter of Taanis, where he brought there the words of the Ra'avad who wrote that acceptance of a fast is only like tzedakah, as if he volunteers of his fat and blood to the Most High, etc.; and the Rosh there disputed him on what he wrote that it is like tzedakah, namely that it has a redemption, but rather he pays specifically with his body, for so he vowed his body; hence at any rate a fast stands in place of a korban; therefore he properly decided here that regarding a fast we learn from a neder of kodashim; for this reason hirhur helps here; which is not so in Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, regarding tzedakah. And furthermore the Rosh disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam: for Rabbeinu Tam interprets that verse "what goes out of your lips" and the verse of "nediv lev" regarding tzedakah, and holds as written nearby in the name of the Mordechai [citing] a responsum of Rashi, that tzedakah is like nedarim and nedavos, namely that everything is included in the neder of the verse; and the Rosh interprets it as referring specifically to true nedarim and nedavos, and automatically the same applies to a fast, but not to tzedakah. And even though the Rosh wrote here "from his bringing a proof from 'nediv lev' and from tzedakah, that intent of the heart, etc.," that is according to Rabbeinu Tam's view, who equates tzedakah to nedarim; but he himself does not hold so regarding tzedakah. And if so, everything is resolved, with Heaven's help.
