**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Paragraph 2: Under pressing circumstances; and the proof, etc.</b> Magen Avraham means that the early authorities appear to hold four opinions. Shulchan Aruch copies Rambam's phrase 'under pressing circumstances,' implying that even a Torah scholar may eat or drink there only under pressure. Tur omits those words. Beis Yosef says Tur agrees that pressure is required, but omitted it because it is obvious; why would permission be granted without need? Bach says Rif, Rosh, and Tur, who omit the phrase, dispute Rambam and permit it even without pressure. Rema's gloss indicates that Ran holds a third opinion, distinguishing a synagogue from a beis midrash: pressure is required in a synagogue, but not in a beis midrash. Hagahos Ashri appears to present a fourth view, writing that Rav Adda and Ravina cited here by Magen Avraham were personally stringent in saying, 'A teaching requires clarity,' although it would have been permitted even while their places were inhabited; eating and drinking are permitted in synagogues and study halls even for people who are not Torah scholars. Thus, he permits even a non-scholar, apparently without pressure. Bach struggles to reconcile Hagahos Ashri with the baraisa on Megillah 28, which says like Shulchan Aruch that one may neither eat nor drink there. All this arose because they understood Rambam's 'under pressing circumstances' as an indispensable condition and did not consider distinguishing eating and drinking from other uses. Beis Yosef supports Rambam's requirement from the incident of Rav Adda and Ravina, who were standing, etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
Ran writes concerning the incident of Rav Adda and Ravina: 'Although we say that Sages and their disciples are permitted, it appears to me that this refers to a beis midrash where they remain all day, as the expression bei rabbanan means the house of the Sages, but not a synagogue.' Rema understood Ran to dispute Rambam, who equates synagogue and beis midrash. According to Magen Avraham, however, there is no dispute. Rambam addresses a synagogue where they study, as was their practice in Talmudic times; eating and drinking were permitted so they would not be interrupted from study. Ran's language appears to address a synagogue where people do not study, and he therefore forbids eating and drinking there. The same would apply to sheltering from rain in such a case.

This is Rambam's meaning in saying that they may eat and drink 'under pressing circumstances.' The phrase is not a condition, because every eating and drinking by a Torah scholar in a beis midrash is compelled by the need not to interrupt his learning. Rather, Rambam gives the reason why eating and drinking were permitted. Rif, Rosh, and Tur consequently omit the phrase because it is not a condition; all their eating and drinking there is deemed compelled and permitted in every case.

<b>Paragraph 4: And to sleep, etc.</b> Tamid at the beginning of chapter 1 teaches that the Chamber of the Hearth was built half within the Temple and half outside it. Its non-sacred half was surrounded by stone ledges upon which the elders of the priestly watch guarding the Temple slept. The Gemara asks why they did not bring beds in (meaning, why sleep on stones) and Abaye answers that bringing beds into the Temple is improper. Magen Avraham holds that a synagogue's sanctity is greater than that of the Chamber of the Hearth, or at least no less: the half built outside had only the sanctity of the Temple Mount, while Mordechai and Maharik write that a synagogue's sanctity may resemble that of the Sanctuary. For this reason they forbid use of the roof above a synagogue, as explained at this siman's end.
