<b>עד</b> עט״ז דמצה כתיב בערב תאכלו מצות אין ראיה דמשבע נמי בערב מיקרי שם תזבח את הפסח בערב וכמ״ש במ״כ בס׳ מבוא השמש רק פסח כתיב ואכלו הבשר בלילה היינו צ״ה ומצה אתקיש לפסח כמ״ש התוס׳ ורא״ש ריש ערבי פסחים. ולפ״ז אם אכל מצה ביה״ש לא יצא י״ח ובלילה יאכל וצ״ע אם לברך אכילת מצה בלילה עסי׳ ס״ז בספק קרא ק״ש חוזר ומברך וקורא תליא בשני הפירושים שם להפרישה וכ״ג דמחלקין בין גברא בר חיובא וספק אם עשה המצוה כתקנה חוזר ומברך ובין טומטום כו׳ אבל למ״ש הכ״מ בשם הרשב״א הביאו המ״א שם א׳ אין לברך כאן ועא״ר שם ועשו״ת הרשב״א פ״א וש״ך ופר״ח שם. והנה בהיקש מצה לפסח י״ל כמ״ש בתע״א בפר״ח שי״א דאסור לאכול מצה ד״ת בע״פ מ״ע כמו פסח בלילה אין ביום לא ואף אם נאמר דהלכה מד״ס וכן הלכה מ״מ איסורא איכא מד״ס לאכול ביה״ש מצה אף בלא ברכה ומילקי נמי לקי מכות מרדות ול״ת ספק דרבנן לקולא דטעם ככלה בלא ברכה כו׳ וכ״ש זה שאין ממתין זמן מועט ומראה רעבתנותו. ואם ביה״ש בברכה י״ל עובר לא תשא עסי׳ רט״ו ובזה צ״ע אם לוקה מכות מרדות ומ״ש הט״ז הקידוש צריך שיהא בשעה הראוי למצה היינו דאין קידוש אלא במקום סעודה עסי׳ תפ״ט במ״א י״ד. א״נ כמ״ש הב״י בשם תה״ד קל״ו עמ״א א׳ דהקידוש א׳ מד׳ כוסות צריך שיהיה גם כן בלילה. ומ״ש בשם מהר״ל מפראג ע׳ תוס׳ ריש ע״פ ד״ה עד וברא״ש שם ועסי׳ רצ״א בט״ז אות ו׳ כתב דאף דלא אכל כזית פת בלילה יצא דתוספת שבת ד״ת יע״ש וכ״כ הע״ש ברס״ז ועמ״א רס״ז א׳ ועמ״מ פ״א משביתות עשור להר״מ ז״ל תו״ש לאו ד״ת ועב״י רס״א ופר״ח כאן. ומשמע אף ביה״ש י״ל דא״י י״ח בסעודת שבת ומיהו י״ל דפ״א בשבת לאכול הוי ד״ת ותו דרבנן. וממ״ש ובשבילה בא הקידוש משמע דאין קידוש אלא במקום סעודה ואי״ה במ״א א׳ יבואר עוד. ומ״ש וחדא מדאורייתא לכאורה ט״ס וצ״ל חדא מדאורייתא עב״ח ועסי׳ קפ״ח. עסי׳ תצ״ד מ״ש שם:

<b>Until.</b> See Taz, that regarding matzah it is written "in the evening you shall eat matzos": there is no proof, for from "seven" it is also called evening: "there you shall slaughter the Pesach in the evening," as Mahari Kolon wrote in Sefer Mevo HaShemesh; only regarding Pesach it is written "and they shall eat the flesh at night," meaning midnight (chatzos), and matzah is juxtaposed to Pesach as Tosafos and the Rosh wrote at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim. And according to this, if he ate matzah at bein hashemashot he has not fulfilled his obligation, and at night he should eat; and it requires further study whether to recite the blessing Al Achilas Matzah at night; see siman 67 regarding a doubt about Kerias Shema, where he returns and recites a blessing and reads; it depends on the two explanations there of the Perishah; and likewise those who distinguish between a person who is definitely obligated and a doubt whether he performed the mitzvah properly (he returns and recites a blessing) versus a tumtum, etc.; but according to what Kesef Mishneh wrote in the name of the Rashba, which Magen Avraham brought there note 1, one should not recite a blessing here; and see Eliyah Rabbah there; and see Responsa of the Rashba part 1, and Shach and Peri Chadash there. And behold, regarding the juxtaposition of matzah to Pesach one may say as is written in Eshel Avraham on Peri Chadash, that some say it is forbidden to eat matzah according to the Torah on erev Pesach from midday, like Pesach: at night yes, by day no; and even if we say the law is from the Sages and so is the law, nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition to eat matzah at bein hashemashot even without a blessing, and lashes of rebellion also apply; and do not say a doubt of a rabbinic matter is lenient, for the reason is like a bride without a blessing, etc.; and all the more so this one, who does not wait a short time and shows gluttony. And if at bein hashemashot with a blessing, one may say he transgresses "You shall not take [the Name in vain]"; see siman 215; and on this it requires further study whether he is liable to lashes of rebellion. And what the Taz wrote, that Kiddush must be at the time fit for matzah, that is because there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal; see siman 489 in Magen Avraham 14. Or alternatively as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen 136, on Magen Avraham note 1, that Kiddush, being one of the four cups, must also be at night. And what he wrote in the name of Maharal of Prague, see Tosafos at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim s.v. "ad," and the Rosh there; and see siman 291 in Taz letter 6, who wrote that even if he did not eat a kezayit of bread at night he fulfilled, for the addition to Shabbos is according to the Torah; see there; and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah at the beginning of siman 267; and see Magen Avraham 267:1; and see Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1 of Laws of Resting on the Tenth, to the Rambam of blessed memory, that addition is not according to the Torah; and see Beis Yosef 261 and Peri Chadash here. And it is implied that even at bein hashemashot one may say he has not fulfilled his obligation with a Shabbos meal; however one may say that eating once on Shabbos is according to the Torah and the further [addition] is rabbinic. And from what is written "and for its sake Kiddush comes," it is implied that there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal; and God willing in Magen Avraham note 1 it will be explained further. And what he wrote "and one is according to the Torah," apparently a scribal error, and it should say "one is according to the Torah"; see Bach; and see siman 188. See siman 494 what is written there.

<b>For in this time.</b> See Taz: his view [is that of a mourner]; but within twelve months for a father or mother, or within thirty for other relatives when he did not observe seven days before the festival, he does not recline, but he does wear the kittel; and in Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah he wrote thus regarding Yom Kippur; and see Eliyah Rabbah 3; and Chok Yaakov letter 5 ruled that he is obligated in heseiba; see there; and see Eliyah Rabbah. And see Chok Yaakov in the name of Pesach Me'ubin: if the mourner after seven days is obligated to recline, it is implied that within seven he is not; and if so it cannot be pictured for other relatives. <b>And it appears.</b> See Taz. The Rosh likewise wrote in chapter Arvei Pesachim 108a; and see Magen Avraham letter 7, who explained that whenever one must recite a blessing it is like adding to the cups; therefore between the first and the second, where one is permitted to drink as clarified in the Gemara Pesachim 117a and siman 473 se'if 3, it does not appear like adding, for he drinks without a blessing; and even for us who recite a blessing over each cup, the reason is that each is a mitzvah by itself; see siman 474 and in Tur and Magen Avraham; which is not so between the third and the fourth, where it is forbidden to drink, and if he drinks he would need to recite a blessing, it is like adding. And on this the Rosh concludes that there is no concern of adding to the cups since it was clarified, etc.; and on this the Taz challenged: granted that the Rosh initially holds it is like adding, nevertheless according to what he wrote afterward, whenever he acts because he did not initially fulfill, there is no adding here at all; if so, a further difficulty: he should rather have said the reason between third and fourth is that he should not drink, lest he become drunk and not say the Haggadah, as clarified in the mishnah 117b and in the Yerushalmi; which is not so for the first two cups, wine before the meal, with which one does not become drunk; and see siman 479. And so wrote the Agudah, chapter Arvei Pesachim siman 32, this concern; and why did he mention the concern of adding, which the mishnah did not mention, and it is not so clear that whenever one acts from doubt lest he not have fulfilled there is no concern of adding; and see Chok Yaakov letter 14; and according to what I wrote it is fine. And I have some difficulty in what the Rosh wrote: the fourth cup, if he drank without heseiba, he must recite Borei Pri HaGafen over a fifth cup: it is implied that over the third cup he need not recite a blessing; but it is forbidden to drink between the cups and he must recite a blessing also over the third cup, for initially the Rosh likewise wrote that for the third and fourth he should not drink, for it is like adding for this reason, as Magen Avraham wrote letter 7; and how did he conclude regarding the fourth cup that he recites a blessing and not regarding the third? One may resolve that the Rosh of blessed memory holds that for the third cup, if he first drank without heseiba, he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen when he returns and drinks, since his intention is to drink the fourth cup; and according to the Rosh over the fourth cup he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen; see siman 474; the same applies when he returns and drinks the third cup. And what he wrote initially, between third and fourth one should not return and drink: the reason is that between third and fourth he should not drink, as the Agudah wrote; and after the fourth cup the reason is that it is like adding, for he would need to recite Borei Pri HaGafen; and if so the Taz's challenge is well placed, why he did not give a shared reason for both. And see Magen Avraham 7.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>על</b> הסדר עט״ז פירוש בלי הגדה בנתיים ואם שתה בלא הגדה צריך לחזור ולשתות ג׳ כוסות על סדר הגדה כ״כ הרשב״ם והר״ן ז״ל עפר״ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In the order.</b> See Taz: meaning without the Haggadah in between; and if he drank without the Haggadah he must return and drink three cups on the order of the Haggadah; so wrote Rashbam and the Ran of blessed memory; see Peri Chadash.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>כולו</b> עט״ז לכתחלה טוב ליקח כוס קטן מחזיק רביעית בריוח וישתה לכתחלה כולו ויוצא לכ״ע ממה שיקח כוס גדול כו׳ ועב״ח ומ״א ודיעבד רובו רוב רביעית כו׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All of it.</b> See Taz: ideally it is good to take a small cup that holds a revi'is with room to spare and drink all of it ideally, and he fulfills according to all opinions, better than taking a large cup, etc.; and see Bach and Magen Avraham; and after the fact, most of it [means] most of a revi'is, etc.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>אפילו</b> עט״ז עסי׳ רע״ב במ״א ה׳ מי צמוקים דוקא שיש לחלוחית קצת וראוי לחזור אחר יין גמור אף ע״י טורח ומ״ש כל הפטור ועושה נקרא הדיוט עמ״א ו׳ ובח״י תי׳ כסוכה יש לו צער כו׳. ומיהו כל שרוצה להחמיר כחד דיעה אף דהלכה כאידך אין נקרא הדיוט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even.</b> See Taz; see siman 272 in Magen Avraham 5: raisin-water specifically when there is some moisture; and it is proper to pursue genuine wine even with effort. And what is written, whoever is exempt and does [the act] is called a hedyot: see Magen Avraham 6; and in Chok Yaakov he answered like sukkah, where he has distress, etc. However, whenever one wishes to be stringent like one opinion even though the law follows the other, he is not called a hedyot.
