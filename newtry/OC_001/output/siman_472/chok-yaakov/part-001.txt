<b>ואף אם הוא בבית המדרש בלילה יקום. </b> וכן הוא בש"ס דפסחים דף ק"ט אמרו עליו על ר"ע מימיו לא אמר הגיע עת לעמוד בבית המדרש חוץ מליל פסחים ועי"כ כן הוא הגירסא בהרא"ש והתוס' אכן בגירסא שלפנינו איתא חוץ מערבי פסחים ופירש רשב"ם דאף ביום אמר הגיע כו' וע"ש הטעם:

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With fine utensils according to his ability.</b> The Maharil wrote in the laws of the Haggadah: “All year it is good to refrain from using fine utensils as a remembrance of the destruction, except on the nights of the Seder, as a remembrance of freedom. Even utensils held by him as collateral from non-Jews may be placed on a special table in order to rejoice at the sight of them.” It is explicit that even on Pesach, in any event, one should not use them, as the Maharil wrote explicitly in the laws of hagalah, but only place them on a special table for beauty alone. This is unlike what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Maharil, that he meant to use them. That is not so. Also, what is implied in the Magen Avraham, that there is a prohibition of theft in using their collateral, is not precise, for there is no prohibition of theft here at all, as is clear in the Gemara at the end of the chapter HaSocher Es HaPoel. Rather, the Maharil was concerned only for deception, and even regarding this concern the Taz and Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 120, seif 9, challenged the Maharil and permitted it; see there, for their words are clear, unlike the Be’er HaGolah there, who distinguishes in another way.

<b>שיסב בהסיבה דרך חרות. </b> כתב הב"ח דאבל כל י"ב חודש לא יסב וכת' בפסח מעובין סי' כ"ז הב"ח אזיל לשטתו דפוסק כראבי"ה דבזמן הזה אין צריך להסב אבל לדידן דפסקינן דצריך היסבה אם האבל אחר ז' כיון דכבר פסקה אבילתו חייב להסב וכן המנהג עכ"ל הנה אף שהוא ז"ל נמשך אחר דעתו דעת הספרדים וכפסק הב"י בסעיף ז' דבדעבד לא יצא בלא הסיבה ודלא כהכרעת האחרונים אשר אנחנו נמשכין אחריהם כמ"ש שם סי' קס"ג להדיא. מ"מ לענין דין האבל נ"ל דבריו נכונים כי מנין לנו להחמיר באבילות דקולא ולבטל ממצות הסיבה דבר שלא נזכר כלל בפוסקים ראשונים ואחרונים ואדרבא בזמן הש"ס שהיו רגילין להסב על מטתן כל השנה לא מצינו איסור באבל רק כל שבעה כופה מטתו ואחר כך מיסב על מטתו כשאר כל אדם ואי משום דנראה דרך חירות ושררה מה בכך כיון שהוא זכר לנס ואדרבה כן מצינו להדיא גבי אבל ס"פ מציאת אשה מנין לאבל שמיסב בראש שנ' ואשכון כמלך וגו' ועיין במתני' דר"פ כהן גדול כל העם מסובין על הארץ והוא מיסב על הספסל ע"ש אף על גב דיש לדחות כל הנו ראיות בכמה גווני מ"מ כיון שלא מצינו בשום פוסק שיש נדנוד איסור בזה באבל אין לבדות מלבו איסור כזה באבילות דקולא מסברא דנפשיה בלי ראיות כלל ואפשר דהוי כאבילות בפרהסיא לכן אין לבטל מצות הסיבה וע"ל ס"ס תר"ס בדין הקפה שבלולב שכת' הכלבו הובא בב"י שם שסיים ודברי תימא הם למה יפסיד האבל הקפה וע"ש בט"ז שטרח למצוא טעם ליישב המנהג גבי לולב וכאן אין סברא כלל לאסור מ"מ אם אפשר לשנות קצת ישנה: <b>ועכ"פ </b> ילבוש הקיטל שהוא בגד מתים וכן מסקנת הט"ז ודלא כמ"א ע"ש נ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he recline with reclining in the manner of freedom.</b> The Bach wrote that a mourner during the entire twelve months should not recline. The Pesach Me’ubin, siman 27, wrote that the Bach follows his own view, for he rules like the Raavyah that in our time reclining is not required. But according to us, who rule that reclining is required, if the mourner is after the seven days, since his mourning has already ceased, he is obligated to recline, and so is the custom. Now, although he followed his own view, the view of the Sephardim and the ruling of the Beit Yosef in seif 7, that after the fact one has not fulfilled the obligation without reclining, unlike the conclusion of the later authorities whom we follow, as I wrote explicitly there in siman 163, nevertheless regarding the law of a mourner his words appear correct to me. For where do we know to be stringent in mourning, which is treated leniently, and to nullify the mitzvah of reclining, something not mentioned at all by the early or later authorities? On the contrary, in the time of the Gemara, when people were accustomed to recline on their beds all year, we find no prohibition for a mourner except that all seven days he overturns his bed, and afterward he reclines on his bed like everyone else. And if the concern is that it appears like a manner of freedom and authority, what of it, since it is a remembrance of the miracle? On the contrary, we find explicitly regarding a mourner at the end of Perek Metzi’as Ishah: from where do we know that a mourner reclines at the head? As it is stated, “And I dwelt like a king,” etc. See also the Mishnah at the beginning of Perek Kohen Gadol: all the people recline on the ground and he reclines on the bench; see there. Although all these proofs can be rejected in several ways, nevertheless since we find no posek who mentions even a trace of prohibition in this for a mourner, one should not invent such a prohibition in mourning, which is treated leniently, from one’s own reasoning without proofs at all. Perhaps it is considered public mourning. Therefore one should not nullify the mitzvah of reclining. See also the end of siman 660 regarding the circuit with the lulav, where the Kol Bo, cited in the Beit Yosef there, concludes: “These words are astonishing, for why should the mourner lose the circuit?” See there in the Taz, who worked to find a reason to justify the custom regarding lulav; here there is no reasoning at all to prohibit. Nevertheless, if he can alter it slightly, he should alter it. <b>In any event,</b> he should wear the kittel, which is a garment of the dead, and this is also the conclusion of the Taz, unlike the Magen Avraham. This appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He does not need reclining.</b> From this wording it initially seems that he does not need reclining, but there is no prohibition if he reclines. However, from the wording of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Chametz, and the Kol Bo, it implies that it is forbidden for him to recline because of awe and honor of his rebbe. This is also clear in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 138. See the Bach, who explained the language of the Tur and the Gemara, which is like the wording of the Mechaber, in this way as well, and this is primary. See what I wrote in seif katan 9. See also the Taz and the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of the Maharil that anyone who reclines while exempt is called a commoner. He derived this law from the Yerushalmi in the first chapter of Shabbos. In truth, the Be’er Sheva wrote that this matter requires consideration, for many times we find that people are stringent in matters from which they are exempt. These are the words of the Magen Avraham. See my work Minchas Yaakov, question 15, where I also wrote that one can distinguish. Although below, at the end of siman 639, the Rav writes in the gloss regarding rain that if one does not leave his sukkah he is called a commoner, that case is different, since the Torah exempted him so that his body not suffer, for its ways are ways of pleasantness. Therefore it is forbidden for him to be stringent and afflict his body, all the more so on Yom Tov. This is also explicit in the responsum of Mahariv, siman 191, in the laws of Sukkos; see there. In other matters, however, perhaps it is permitted to be stringent where there is no side of prohibition. Yet Tosafot in Sotah 17b somewhat implies that even in such a case he is called a commoner, and this requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Unless his rebbe gives him permission.</b> Although the Beit Yosef wrote that whenever his rebbe sees and remains silent, it is as though his rebbe gave him permission, nevertheless here he writes specifically that he gives him permission, for otherwise how could he initially recline, perhaps his rebbe will be particular with him? In the manuscript it was written regarding the students of Rabbah, who did not know the law; therefore they reclined, and Rabbah saw and remained silent, thereby waiving it for them. But one who knows the law is forbidden to recline until his rebbe gives him permission. All this appears simple to me, unlike the Pesach Me’ubin, siman 172, who makes this a dispute; see there, but he was not precise. All this applies to a student in Torah, but a trade apprentice reclines in every case.
