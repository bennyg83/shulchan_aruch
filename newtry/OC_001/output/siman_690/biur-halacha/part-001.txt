**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if he omitted its beginning or its end, etc., and even in its middle, etc.</b> In this matter there is confusion among the poskim. From the words of the Rosh in chapter 2, siman 4, it implies that one may be lenient after the fact in every case, whether the beginning of the scroll is missing or whether a complete matter is missing, so long as most of the scroll is not missing; see there. The Bach inclines to his view. In the words of the Rashba and the Orchos Chaim as well, one must examine whether they do not disagree with each other. It is possible that according to the Rashba, only at the beginning of the scroll is there a concern, but in the middle, even if a complete matter is missing, we are not concerned after the fact, as the Pri Chadash indeed inclined; and so too it seems, at first glance, from the Rashba and Ran; see there. It is further possible to say that the Rashba himself is stringent even at the beginning of the scroll only when an entire section is missing, and there is an implication of this in his words; see his novellae and the Ran. According to this, it is clear that he disagrees with the Orchos Chaim. And even if we say that according to the Rashba, at the beginning of the scroll even one missing verse invalidates it, as the author of Matteh Yehudah wrote in explaining his view, and that he also agrees with the Orchos Chaim that if a complete matter is missing, even in the middle, it is invalid, as it appears at first glance that this is the opinion of the gloss, for otherwise it should have said, "And some say further, that even in its middle, etc."; in any case it is not proven whether the Orchos Chaim agrees with the Rashba. For it is possible that he holds that specifically when a complete matter is missing it has lost the status of a megillah, but not when a verse is missing, even the first or last verse. In truth, in Meiri, chapter 2 of Megillah, he brings only this view of a complete matter, and this is his language: There is one who says that it was not permitted even for part of it to be missing when a complete matter is missing from there in sequence, for it says that it must be written in its entirety, excluding where a section or a complete matter is missing, even if it is small; end quote. He did not bring the Rashba's view at all. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law, we must not turn away from the opinion of the gloss, as we explained in Mishnah Berurah. However, one must clarify that according to all the views mentioned above, it seems at first glance that if the beginning and end were not missing, and also in the middle no complete matter was missing, it is valid so long as most of it is not missing. According to this, one could find a case where in every word of the megillah some letters were missing, until it would be impossible to read the megillah at all because of this, and how could one have thought that it would be valid? It is not a scroll at all. Perforce, when we calculate all of it or part of it, we do not calculate by the number of letters, but by the number of words. And even if a minority of the letters are blurred, as long as in every word, or in most words, some letter is blurred, it is invalid.

<b> דוקא דלא השמיט ענין שלם</b> -  עיין במ"ב דבכל זה הטעם דבעינן שיקרא מתוך ספר ואם חסר מתחלתו או שחסר באמצע ענין שלם אף שהוא רק מקצתו לא מקרי ספר ולפ"ז נראה פשוט דזה שייך דוקא לענין אם השמיט מתוך הספר ולאפוקי לענין קריאה בע"פ דיצא במקצתו אין לחלק בזה ובבגדי ישע ראיתי שהחמיר בזה ולא נהירא כלל וכן מוכח מסתימת כל הפוסקים שכתבו דמקצתו כשר ולא חילקו בזה אח"כ מצאתי כדברי במטה יהודא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Specifically where he did not omit a complete matter.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where in all this the reason is that we require that he read from a scroll, and if the beginning is missing, or a complete matter is missing in the middle, even though it is only part of it, it is not called a scroll. According to this it appears obvious that this applies specifically regarding a case where he omitted from the scroll. This excludes reading by heart, where he fulfilled his obligation when only part of it was by heart, for there is no distinction in this. I saw that Bigdei Yesha was stringent about this, but this is not at all correct. So too it is proven from the plain wording of all the poskim, who wrote that part of it is valid and did not distinguish this. Afterwards I found my words in Matteh Yehudah.
