**** ENGLISH ****
The custom of all Israel is that the reader reads and spreads it out like a letter, to show the miracle; and when he finishes, he goes back and rolls it all up, and blesses. <small>Gloss: Some wrote that the custom is to say four verses of redemption aloud, namely: "There was a Jewish man, etc.," "And Mordechai went out, etc.," "For the Jews there was light, etc.," and "For Mordechai the Jew, etc." So is the custom in these countries, Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 1, Kol Bo, and Avudraham. The cantor then goes back and reads them. They wrote further that the children had the custom to form an image of Haman on pieces of wood and stones, or to write Haman's name on them, and to strike them against each other so that his name should be erased, in the manner of "you shall surely erase the memory of Amalek" and "the name of the wicked shall rot." From this the custom spread that they strike Haman when the megillah is read in the synagogue, Avudraham. And one should not abolish any custom or mock it, for it was not established for nothing, Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim.</small>
