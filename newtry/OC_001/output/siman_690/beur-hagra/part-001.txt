<b>Section 9: If it was, etc.</b> The Rambam ruled that Greek foreign language is valid for everyone, like Rav and Shmuel. The Rif omitted what is written there, that Greek foreign language is valid for everyone. See the Rosh there, who wrote the reason he omitted it: because they hold like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and we do not rule this way, but like the Sages. His words are astonishing and at first glance are not understandable, for Rabbi Yochanan ruled in the first chapter there, 9b, like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Rather, one can say that we do not rule like Rav and Shmuel, who ruled like him even regarding megillah; and likewise the plain flow of the sugya there, 9a, is unlike Rav and Shmuel, for it says there: if it is Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, there is, etc.; rather, it is not difficult: here with megillah, etc. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel came to be stringent. So too Rav Papa and Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak hold there. And that which Rabbi Yochanan ruled that the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is because Rabbi Yochanan holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees only regarding other books, but not regarding megillah. See Milchamos. The Ran rejected his words and wrote that they omitted it because Greek has already been forgotten and its memory lost, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchos Sefer Torah. So too the Rambam ruled here like Rav and Shmuel, and so ruled the Baal HaMaor; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that many poskim ruled this way. Nevertheless, the words of the Ramban are correct, for certainly the sugya there on 9a is unlike Rav and Shmuel, as above. So too in the Yerushalmi there: what is the difference between books and Megillas Esther? The books may be written in any language, but Megillas Esther may be written only in Ashuris. But the Rambam holds like Rav Ashi there, as Tosafos wrote there under "here, etc.," and we rule like Rav Ashi.

<b>ס"י מי שיודע. </b>שהקשו והא אמרינן בפ"ק ט' א' דבמגילה אינו יוצא רק בלה"ק ופי' ללועזות שאינן מכירין בלה"ק כמו בפ"ק דמכות הנהו לעוזי כו' ומפני שאינן יודעין התירו להם בקושי כדי שידעו ידיעה ממש וגם כן יוכלו לקרות בעצמן. וכ"ה בירושלמי רפ"ב דמגילה סוף הלכה א' ר' אבהו בשם ר"א יודע אשורית ויודע לעז יוצא באשורית ור"ל רק באשורית דבאשורית לצאת בה אף אם אינו יודע כלל יוצא בה. בלעז יוצא בה בלעז. יודע אשורית ויודע לעז מהו שיוציא אחרים בלעז. ר"ל שהאחרים אין מכירין רק לעז ייבק כהדא כל שאינו חייב בדבר אינו מוציא את הרבים ידי חובתן כ"ה הנוסחא המדוייקת וע' ר"ן ומ"מ שכן פסקו הרמב"ן והרשב"א וכ"ה בסוטה בריש פ' ואלו נאמרין ר' לוי בר חיתא אזיל לקסרן שמע מלין קריין שמע אלוניסתון ר"ל באיזה לעז (ובערוך כ' שהוא לשון יוני) בעא מעכבתון שמע ר' יוסי ואיקפד אמר כך אני אומר מי שאינו יודע לקרות (אשורית) לא יקראנה כל עיקר השיב ר' ברכיה הרי מגילת אסתר היה יודע לקרות אשורית ולעז אינו יוצא אלא אשורית ר"ל מה אתה משיב ממי שאינו יודע ליודע ושפיר עכבם כו' ע"ש. וכן עיקר כי פשטא דירושל' משום שיודע ג"כ בלשון אשורית וכן פשטא דמתני' דקרי להו לועזות וכן מדקאמר והלועז כו' ופריך בגמ' והא לא ידע מאי קא' ואף לאותן שדחו הירושלמי ופירשו דירושלמי מיירי שכתובה בלשון אשורית דלא כגמרא דידן וכמ"ש למטה מ"מ מדידהו נשמע לדידן היכא שכתובה בלשון לעז והגמ' שלנו דקאמר וקראן כו' כמתורגמן המתרגם יצא ור"ל בלשון תרגום וכמ"ש למטה. ודאי הפי' כן אבל מ"מ אין ראיה מזה דדוקא מקצתה יצא כמו שיוצא במה שקורא בע"פ ואף דבירושלמי לא משמע הכי דפריך שם היאך יוצא בתרגום ומשני שמואל כדעתיה כו' ר' אבוה כו' משמע דלר' אבוה לא יצא בזה. י"ל דגמרא דידן פליג בזה דהא לא פריך כלום מתרגום. אלא שי"ל דגמרא דידן ס"ל כשמואל דזו הברייתא השמיט הסופר כו' דשמואל היא כמש"ש בירושל' ולפ"ז קי"ל כשמואל וזהו סברא שני' דיצא: אבל לפרש הירושלמי כמו שפי' ולחלק דאם כתובה בלעז אף ר' אבוה מודה לא נהירי כנ"ל דהא ודאי דהירושלמי פליג אגמ' דידן ומכשיר ללועזות אפי' כתובה אשורית אבל מ"מ ה"ה לדידן אם כתובה בלעז כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Section 10: One who knows.</b> They asked: do we not say in the first chapter, 9a, that regarding megillah one fulfills only in the Holy Tongue? They explained "for foreign-language speakers" as referring to those who do not know the Holy Tongue, like in the first chapter of Makkos, "those foreign-language speakers, etc." Because they do not know, it was permitted to them with difficulty, so that they should have actual knowledge, and also be able to read for themselves. So too it is in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 2 of Megillah, end of halachah 1: Rabbi Abahu said in the name of Rabbi Elazar: one who knows Ashuris and knows a foreign language fulfills with Ashuris, meaning only with Ashuris, for with Ashuris one fulfills even if he knows nothing at all. With a foreign language he fulfills in the foreign language. If one knows Ashuris and knows a foreign language, what is the law regarding discharging others in the foreign language? That is to say, the others know only the foreign language. It remains unresolved, like this rule: anyone who is not obligated in the matter does not discharge the public from their obligation. This is the precise text. See the Ran and Maggid Mishneh, who say that so ruled the Ramban and the Rashba. So too it is in Sotah at the beginning of the chapter "These are said": Rabbi Levi bar Chisa went to Caesarea and heard them reciting Shema in a foreign language, apparently some foreign language, and in the Aruch he wrote that it is Greek. He wanted to stop them. Rabbi Yose heard and became angry. He said: do I say that one who does not know how to read Ashuris should not read it at all? Rabbi Berechyah answered: regarding Megillas Esther, if he knew how to read Ashuris and a foreign language, he fulfills only with Ashuris. That is to say, what are you answering from one who does not know to one who does know? He properly stopped them, etc.; see there. So is the main view, because the plain meaning of the Yerushalmi is because he also knows the Ashuris language, and so too is the plain meaning of the mishnah, which calls them foreign-language speakers, and from the fact that it says "and the foreign-language speaker, etc.," and the Gemara asks, but he does not know what it is saying. Even according to those who rejected the Yerushalmi and explained that the Yerushalmi speaks where it is written in Ashuris language, unlike our Gemara, as written below, nevertheless from them we learn for us where it is written in a foreign language. And our Gemara, which says, "and he read them, etc., like an interpreter who translates, he has fulfilled," means in the language of translation, as written below. Certainly the explanation is so. But nevertheless there is no proof from this, for only regarding part of it did he fulfill, as one fulfills with what he reads by heart. And although the Yerushalmi does not imply so, for it asks there how one fulfills in translation, and answers: Shmuel follows his own view, etc.; Rabbi Abahu, etc. This implies that according to Rabbi Abahu one has not fulfilled in this case. One can say that our Gemara disagrees with this, for it does not ask anything from translation. But one can say that our Gemara holds like Shmuel, that this baraisa of "if the scribe omitted, etc." is Shmuel's, as stated there in the Yerushalmi. According to this, we rule like Shmuel, and this is the second view, that he fulfills. But to explain the Yerushalmi as they explained, and to distinguish that if it is written in a foreign language even Rabbi Abahu agrees, is not clear to me, as above. For certainly the Yerushalmi disagrees with our Gemara and validates it for foreign-language speakers even if it is written in Ashuris; nevertheless the same law applies for us if it is written in a foreign language, as above.
