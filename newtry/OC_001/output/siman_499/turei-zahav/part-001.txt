**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Unless he scalds it in order to eat the skin.</b> So wrote the Tur in the name of Bahag; and the Rosh wrote that Bahag forbids scalding the entire kid in order to eat the skin, because it is easier to flay. And Maharshal wrote: And it is astonishing regarding the Tur, who wrote that milug is lighter than flaying; he also did not bring Bahag's words as the Rosh wrote them. Therefore it appears that there is a scribal error, and one must read: even if he wishes to scald it in order to eat its skin, it is forbidden, for there is more toil in it than in flaying; and that is like the Rosh, and that is primary; end quote. And so wrote my late father and teacher. He wrote in Semak, siman 194: but regarding a bird, where flaying does not apply, it is permitted to scald and singe the entire body; end quote.
