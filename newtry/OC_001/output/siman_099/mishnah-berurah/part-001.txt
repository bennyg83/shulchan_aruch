(א) שתה יין - ונראה דה"ה שאר משקין המשכרים וכפי מה שכתב הט"ז לקמן בסימן קכ"ח ס"ק ל"ה לענין נשיאת כפים ואפילו לדעת המ"א שם דמיקל בנשיאת כפים מסתברא דתפילה חמירא ואסור כמו ביין כ"כ בספר מגן גיבורים עי"ש ולפי דעת הט"ז לקמן הנ"ל לא בעינן בשאר משקין שיעור רביעית עי"ש ולי נראה דמ"מ בעינן שיהא בו כדי לשכר ברביעית יין:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) One drank wine. It appears that the same applies to other intoxicating beverages, as Taz writes below, siman 128, seif katan 35, regarding Bircas Kohanim. Even according to Magen Avraham there, who is lenient regarding Bircas Kohanim, prayer is reasonably more stringent and is forbidden just as with wine. Magen Giborim writes this; see there. According to Taz there, other beverages do not require the measure of a revi'is. In my view, however, they must at least contain enough alcohol to intoxicate to the degree produced by a revi'is of wine.

(ב) כדי רביעית - דוקא אם שתאו בבת אחת אבל בשני פעמים או שמזגו במעט מים מותר ואם שתה יותר מרביעית בכל גווני אסור עד שיסיר יינו מעליו וכדלקמן בסימן קכ"ח סל"ח. וכ"ז כשיכול לדבר לפני המלך דאל"ה אין שייך לענינינו דאיירי בתפלה שום חילוק בזה. כתב המ"א בשם הסמ"ק יין שבתוך הסעודה אינו משכר ועיין עוד שם וכתב הפמ"ג דכ"ז הכל לפי מה שמבין בדעתו:
**** ENGLISH ****
(2) The measure of a revi'is. This applies specifically when he drank it at one time. If he drank it in two installments or diluted it with a little water, it is permitted. If he drank more than a revi'is, it is forbidden in every case until the wine has dissipated, as below, siman 128, seif 38. All this applies when he can speak before a king. Otherwise, in the context of prayer there is no relevant distinction. Magen Avraham cites Semak that wine drunk during a meal does not intoxicate. See further there. Peri Megadim writes that all depends on his own assessment of his condition.

(6) As in the case of an inadvertent omission. Even if he began drinking after the time arrived, because he thought there would remain time to pray once the small amount of wine dissipated, and he began drinking with a clear mind, but was then drawn into the feast, forgot the prayer, and became so intoxicated that insufficient time remained for the wine to dissipate before the prayer time ended, he is still considered inadvertent. Certainly, however, if Minchah is near its end and he becomes intoxicated when it is clear that the wine cannot dissipate within so short a time, all agree that he is deliberate and has no compensatory prayer.

(ח) כדין תפלה - היינו דאף שתוי אסור לכתחלה בק"ש והלבוש כתב דיש מקילין בק"ש אף אם הוא שכור דשאני תפלה שהוא בקשת צרכיו וכ"כ בלקוטי הרמב"ן בסופו אכן בביאור הגר"א הרחיב לבאר דברי הירושלמי שיש בענין זה ולפי דבריו מסקנת הירושלמי לאסור שכור בק"ש ע"כ יש ליזהר לכתחילה שלא לבוא לידי כך ובדיעבד אם נשתכר אין לפטור את עצמו מק"ש משום זה וכעין שכתב המ"א לקמן בסימן קפ"ה לענין בהמ"ז ואם יפוג יינו עד שלא עבר זמן ק"ש יחזור ויקרא הפרשיות:
**** ENGLISH ****
(8) The same law as prayer. Meaning, even one who has merely drunk may not initially recite Krias Shema. Levush writes that some are lenient regarding Krias Shema even when he is intoxicated, because prayer differs in that it is a request for one's needs. Likkutei Ramban at the end writes likewise. Biur HaGra, however, explains at length the Yerushalmi's discussion, and according to him its conclusion is that an intoxicated person may not recite Krias Shema. One should therefore initially take care not to reach such a state. After the fact, however, if he became intoxicated, he should not exempt himself from Krias Shema for this reason, similar to Magen Avraham's ruling below, siman 185, regarding Bircas HaMazon. If the wine dissipates before the time for Krias Shema passes, he should recite the sections again.

(11) While intoxicated. See Biur HaGra. According to his explanation, one should initially be stringent even regarding other blessings when the intoxicated person cannot speak before a king. Peri Megadim, Yoreh Deah, siman 1, seif katan 35 in Mishbetzos Zahav, writes likewise. All this applies when he has not reached the intoxication of Lot. If he has, he is regarded as fully insane according to all opinions and is then exempt from all mitzvos. Therefore, even after the fact, if he recited Krias Shema or a blessing in that state, he did not discharge his obligation, because he was exempt at that time, and afterward must recite it again.
