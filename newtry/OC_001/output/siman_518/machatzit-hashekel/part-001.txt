<b>Magen Avraham wrote.</b> Beis Yosef wrote in siman 495, etc. Since Magen Avraham's words here are for people of my level difficult to understand, I must therefore elaborate and explain my discourse according to the weakness of my understanding. What Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Beis Yosef, siman 495: this is not Beis Yosef's language, and Magen Avraham added explanation to his words. This is the language of the Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov 1:4: Any melacha for which one is liable on Shabbos, if he did it on Yom Tov not for the need of eating, he is liable to lashes, except for carrying out and kindling, for since they were permitted, etc.; and other melachos: whatever has a need of eating is permitted, such as slaughtering, baking, and kneading and the like; and whatever does not have a need of eating, such as writing and weaving and building and the like, is forbidden; end quote. And this is the language of the Maggid Mishneh: the intent from our teacher's view is that any melacha, etc., and that melacha is not for the need of eating: if he did it on Yom Tov he is liable to lashes, except carrying out and kindling, etc., even if he did them not for the need of eating. And the meaning of a melacha not for the need of eating is explained in our teacher's words, and it includes any melacha that is not done with eating and drinking. And if so, by law carrying out and kindling should have been forbidden like writing and weaving; rather, since they were permitted, etc. And it appears there is a reason for these two: carrying out, because it too is done with food and drink; and even though it is [also] with other things we say "mitoch," etc. And kindling, even though it is not with foods and drinks, Scripture wrote "You shall not kindle a fire in any of your dwellings on the day of Shabbos": on Shabbos it is forbidden, but on Yom Tov it is permitted. And so they expounded, because "on the day of Shabbos" is superfluous, for the verse above it already deals with Shabbos, etc. But other melachos that are not for the need of eating, such as writing and demolishing and weaving and building: even if he did them for eating he is liable to lashes. And other melachos that are with eating, such as slaughtering and baking: even if he did them not to eat them he is not liable to lashes, etc. And if he cooked for a non-Jew or for a weekday he is not liable to lashes, etc.; end quote of Maggid Mishneh. And so it is brought in Beis Yosef above at the beginning of siman 495; end quote of Maggid Mishneh. And I learned from their words and from the words of Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash at the beginning of siman 495 that there are three distinctions. (1) A melacha whose essence is for the need of eating and also prepares the food, such as baking and cooking: we say "mitoch," and even if it is not for the need of eating it is permitted. (2) A melacha whose essence is not for the need of eating: even if he did them for eating he is liable; that is, building, for one can find it with eating, such as one who makes cheese, of which we say in Shabbos folio 95 that it is a toladah of building; nevertheless, since the main melacha of building is not for the need of eating, even cheese-making which is for the need of eating, the Rambam holds he is liable, like the Sages there in Shabbos; see there. (3) We require that that melacha be a preparation for eating and drinking; therefore by law carrying out should have been forbidden, for it does not prepare the food through the carrying out; even so it is permitted and we say regarding it "mitoch," since at least it is done with the food itself, for he carries out the food itself; it has a law as if he prepares the food. And kindling, which is not done with the food itself: granted that cooking is with eating and prepares it; but kindling is only with wood, which are preparations for food, and preparations are permitted according to everyone, for they are preparations that cannot be done from the eve of Yom Tov, and even according to the Rabbis who dispute R' Yehuda they are permitted, as written above at the beginning of siman 495.

<b>אעפ"כ מותר ואמרי' ביה מתוך מייתור' דביום השבת.</b> ואע"ג דמייתור' דביום השבת הותרה הבערה בי"ט לגמרי אפי' אין בה צורך קצת. ומדברי מ"מ שכתב דהבערה מותר משום מתוך. והא גבי מתוך קי"ל דבעינן צורך קצת. לק"מ דהמ"מ אזיל לשיטתיה דס"ל דהרמב"ם ס"ל כשיטת רש"י דהא דבעי' גבי מתוך קצת אינו אלא מדרבנן ומן התורה מותר אפי' ליכא צורך כלל. וא"כ הבערה ושחיטה ואפיה וכדומה שוין דמן התורה מותרים אפי' ליכא צורך כלל. אבל מדרבנן בעינן צורך קצת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless it is permitted, and we say regarding it "mitoch" from the superfluity of "on the day of Shabbos."</b> And even though from the superfluity of "on the day of Shabbos" kindling was permitted on Yom Tov entirely, even if there is no slight need in it; and from the Maggid Mishneh's words who wrote that kindling is permitted because of mitoch; and yet regarding mitoch we hold that we require some slight need: there is no difficulty, for the Maggid Mishneh follows his view that he holds the Rambam holds like Rashi's position, that what we require regarding mitoch some slight need is only by rabbinic law, and according to the Torah it is permitted even if there is no need at all. And if so, kindling and slaughtering and baking and the like are equal: according to the Torah they are permitted even if there is no need at all; but by rabbinic law we require some slight need.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And see what I wrote in siman 501 in the name of the Ran, etc.</b> Like ochel nefesh itself, for the Ran holds that a fire to warm oneself is ochel nefesh, because it is enjoyment of the whole body. If so, we find kindling that is done with ochel nefesh, for a fire to warm oneself: the wood are like the food. <b>And it is astonishing, for in Pesachim, etc.</b> And so it is stated there on folio 5a: that which Scripture wrote, "But on the first day you shall make chametz cease from your houses": necessarily it means from the eve of the first day of Yom Tov, meaning from its essence; for if you think [it means] on Yom Tov itself, behold it is written "You shall not do any melacha," and we find that kindling is an av melacha. Rava said: we learn from R' Akiva three things, etc.; and we learn that kindling went out to divide (for in this R' Yehuda and R' Nosson disagree). For the verse "You shall not kindle" is superfluous, since it is included in "You shall not do any melacha"; and R' Nosson holds that it went out to divide, so that you should not say one is only liable if he did all thirty-nine melachos (and in Pesachim Rashi explains this on folio 5a); therefore kindling went out from the general rule; and it did not go out for itself alone but for the general rule; and just as one is liable for kindling alone, so too all thirty-nine melachos: if he did one of them he is liable; but in any case it is an av like all the other thirty-eight melachos. And R' Yehuda holds that it went out as a negative commandment: that all thirty-eight melachos are avos and one is liable for them without warning to kares, and with warning to stoning; but kindling is only with a negative-commandment prohibition. And R' Akiva necessarily holds like R' Nosson: for if like R' Yehuda that it went out as a negative commandment, why does Scripture call kindling an av melacha? But Tosafos wrote in the name of the Riva: if it went out as a negative commandment, Scripture removed kindling from the general rule of "You shall not do any melacha"; if so, on Yom Tov where only "You shall not do any melacha" is written, kindling is not included and is permitted on Yom Tov; and we learn that we do not say "since it was permitted," etc.; thus far the substance of the Gemara. And upon this Magen Avraham wrote: we learn that even though the negative commandment "You shall not kindle" was not said regarding Yom Tov, etc.; and his words are puzzling, for Maggid Mishneh only wrote his words that from the word "on the day of Shabbos" we exclude kindling on Yom Tov according to Beis Hillel who say mitoch; but Beis Shammai who do not say mitoch also regarding kindling, as stated in the Gemara, therefore did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov and establish it for another exposition. And if so, what difficulty is there for Magen Avraham from R' Akiva? For R' Akiva also holds like Beis Shammai, who do not say mitoch, and he too did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov; and if so kindly kindling is included in "You shall not do any melacha." And perhaps one can say that it appeared to Magen Avraham forced to say: that by external reasoning one would have said that in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov kindling is also included; and according to Beis Hillel the verse "on the day of Shabbos" comes to take kindling out of "You shall not do any melacha" written regarding Yom Tov; and Beis Shammai who did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" leave kindling included in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov. This is not the way of Scripture: to write a superfluous verse regarding Shabbos to explain a verse written regarding Yom Tov; and how can we say that it was written in the Shabbos portion "on the day of Shabbos" to explain the verse "You shall not do any melacha" written regarding Yom Tov, that kindling is not included? Rather Maggid Mishneh necessarily holds that by external reasoning kindling is not included in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov. For on Shabbos the Torah took kindling out of "You shall not do any melacha" written for Shabbos, for whatever reason it may be; if so regarding Yom Tov where "You shall not do any melacha" is written and it did not detail kindling, by reasoning kindling should be permitted on Yom Tov; rather, since the Torah forbade kindling on Shabbos and we do not find explicitly that the Torah permitted more on Yom Tov than on Shabbos except melacha of ochel nefesh, if so kindling too is forbidden on Yom Tov from the verse written regarding Shabbos "You shall not kindle." And therefore it works well for Beis Hillel who expounded "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov: that this verse "You shall not kindle" does not apply to Yom Tov. And if so for Beis Shammai, granted that they did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov and kindling is forbidden on Yom Tov: its prohibition is not because "You shall not do any melacha" is written regarding Yom Tov, for kindling is not included; rather from what is written on Shabbos "You shall not kindle," Beis Shammai hold that the same applies to Yom Tov as above, since they did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov. If so R' Akiva's words are difficult who said "and behold it is written 'You shall not do any melacha'": granted that he holds like Beis Shammai, nevertheless kindling is not forbidden on Yom Tov from the verse "You shall not do any melacha" but from the verse "You shall not kindle" written on Shabbos; and he should have said "and behold it is written 'You shall not kindle.'"

For in Beitzah folio 9 we say: that which Beis Hillel permitted there to move a ladder is specifically a dovecote ladder, for he does not need to plaster his roof with it; but an attic ladder that he needs to plaster his roof with is forbidden in a public domain because of marit ayin. Therefore even in the house it is forbidden to move it, for whatever they forbade because of marit ayin is forbidden even in innermost rooms. And Tosafos challenged: behold in Eruvin we say that even a ladder that is not of a dovecote it is permitted to move on Shabbos. And Rabbeinu Tam answered that in Eruvin it deals with a house ladder, which is smaller than an attic ladder, and we are not concerned that they will say he needs it to plaster his roof. And R' Eliezer of Metz answered that in Eruvin it deals with Shabbos, when he need not take them out to a public domain; therefore in his house it is permitted even an attic one, for in the house he does not need to plaster his roof; but on Yom Tov, since it is permitted to take it out to a public domain and there he needs to plaster his roof, therefore even inside the house it is forbidden, for whatever is forbidden in a public domain because of marit ayin is also forbidden in innermost rooms. And if so, according to R' Eliezer of Metz on Yom Tov even a house ladder is forbidden; for Rabbeinu Tam only distinguished between house and attic from the necessity of their question from tractate Eruvin; and since according to R' Eliezer of Metz in any case there is no difficulty from tractate Eruvin, why from the Torah should we distinguish between house and attic? And since the Tur in siman 308 ruled like R' Eliezer of Metz and permits on Shabbos even an attic ladder, automatically on Yom Tov even a house ladder is forbidden. And one who is lenient in the house has not lost out. So Yam Shel Shlomo wrote, and he explained the reason: even though according to R' Eliezer of Metz there is no necessity from the Eruvin sugya to distinguish between house and attic, nevertheless it is possible by reasoning without necessity to be lenient regarding a house ladder according to R' Eliezer of Metz.
