<b>הצריכים לו קצת. </b> דאמרי' מתוך שהותרה הוצאה לצורך אוכל נפש הותרה נמי שלא לצורך והיינו דעכ"פ יש בו קצת צורך היום כגון קטן ולולב אבל אבנים לא ובירושלמי אמרינן מותר לטלטל המפתח של אוכלין אבל של כלים לא ופרכינן והא ר' אבהו יתיב ומתני ומפתח של פלטרין בידיה ומשני פלפלין ה"ל בהדיה וכתב הרא"ש בשם תשובת כגאונים דמשום כבוד י"ט מותר אם הוא תכשיט אבל הוצאה שלא לצורך אכילה ושלא לצורך תכשיט כגון אותם שיוצאים עם המפתחות קשורות באיזוריהן שלא כדין הם עושים וכ"כ הטור וז"ל וכן מפתח שסוגר בו האוכלין שירא להניחו בביתו יכול להוליכו עמו וכתב ר"ש דוקא להוליכו בידו אבל ברצועה כדרך חול לא ואין נראה לחלק דלא מיחזי כ"כ עובדא דחול מה שהוא תלוי ברצועה עכ"ל ומזה משמע דמפתח של כלים שאין בהם צורך אוכל נפש אלא דמתיירא שלא יגנובו אסור להוליך עמו וכ"ה במהרי"ל שהוציא עמו מפתח מן הכיפה שלו בי"ט כי אמר שהדגים שלו שהכניסן לי"ט הם בכיפה שם וכ"פ רש"ל. אבל ב"י מביא הגה' סמ"ק סי' רפ"ב דמפתח תיבה שאין בה אלא מעות כיון שלבו דואג משום פסיד' חשוב צורך קצת וזה פסק רמ"א להלכה ואיני יודע למה כיון דרוב ראשונים ואחרונים לא פסקו אלא דוק' מפתח צורך אוכל נפש או תכשיט ובמרדכי כתוב ג"כ דמחזורים מותר להוליך עמו ולהחזירם דהתירו סופן משום תחילתן דאל"כ לא יוליכם עמו משמע דאם היו כבר שם קודם י"ט אסור להוציאן מבה"כ וע"כ נראה דאף על גב דכבר הורה זקן רמ"א להקל בזה כל בעל נפש יחמיר לעצמו במקום שאין שם עירוב שלא להוליך עמו רק מה דצריך קצת לאוכל נפש בו ביום אבל משום חשש גניבה לא בפרט במקום שיוכל לתת המפתח למי שהוא נאמן לו בביתו דאז אסור לד"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That are needed by him somewhat.</b> For we say: since carrying out was permitted for the need of ochel nefesh, it was also permitted without need; and that means that at least there is some need of the day in it, such as a child and a lulav, but not stones. And in the Yerushalmi we say it is permitted to move the key of foods, but not of vessels; and they challenged: yet R' Abahu sat and taught with a key of the palace in his hand; and they answered: he had peppers with him. And the Rosh wrote in the name of a responsum of the Geonim that for the honor of Yom Tov it is permitted if it is an ornament; but carrying out not for the need of eating and not for the need of an ornament, such as those who go out with keys tied in their belts, they act improperly. And so the Tur wrote, and this is his language: and likewise a key with which he locks the foods, if he is afraid to leave it in his house, he may take it with him; and R' Shimshon wrote: specifically to take it in his hand, but not with a strap in weekday fashion; and it does not appear [correct] to distinguish, for it does not so appear like a weekday act that it hangs on a strap; end quote. And from this it is implied that a key of vessels that have no need of ochel nefesh, except that he fears lest they be stolen, it is forbidden to take with him. And so it is in Maharil who took out with him a key from his cupola on Yom Tov, for he said that his fish that he brought in for Yom Tov are in the cupola there; and so Maharshal ruled. But Beis Yosef brings Hagahos Sefer Mitzvos Katan, siman 282: that a key of a chest that has only money in it, since his heart is troubled because of loss, it is considered some need; and this the Rama ruled as law. And I do not know why, since most Rishonim and Acharonim only ruled specifically for a key needed for ochel nefesh or an ornament. And in the Mordechai it is also written that machzorim it is permitted to take with him and to return them, for they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning, for otherwise he would not take them with him; implying that if they were already there before Yom Tov it is forbidden to take them out of the synagogue. And therefore it appears that even though the elder Rama already ruled to be lenient in this, every conscientious person should be stringent for himself where there is no eruv, not to take with him anything but what is needed somewhat for ochel nefesh on that very day; but not on account of fear of theft, especially where he can give the key to someone trustworthy in his house, for then it is forbidden according to everyone.

<b>ומותר לשחוק בכדור כו'. </b> כתב רש"ל דבר תימה הוא להתיר שא"צ ביום טוב כלל אלא שחוק של ילדים אבל לגדולים נראה בעיני מנהג רע הוא וחף שכ"כ בשם ר"ת לא ניחא לי כי אפשר שלא בא אלא ליישב מה שנהגו כן ואם איישר חיילי אבטליני' שהרא"ש לא העתיק זה: כתב רש"ל ולענין הוצאות סכינים בימי חורפי ראיתי שנזהרים כשהולכים לטייל חוץ לעיר בי"ט שלא היו נושאים הסכינים עמהם מטעם שהסכינים אחר אכילה אין בהם צורך היום כי אין דרך לאכול עוד היום אבל מ"מ חוץ לעירוב היו נושאים כי לא תקנו חכמים ע"ח אלא בשבת שאסור להוצי' כל דבר אבל ביום טוב שמותר להוצי' כל דבר חוץ ממה שאין בו צורך היום כלל בעבור זה לא תיקנו ע"ח וכ"ד הרי"ף והתוס' והר"ן חילק ביום טוב שאין שם ע"ח אלא שהרשב"א כו' ויש הוכחה בגמ' לסברתו כו' וראוי לכל ירא שמים להניח ע"ח בי"ט כמו בשבת אלא שאין לשנות המנהג אבל מכל מקום לפי מה שנהגו להניח עירוב בע"פ על' כל השנה יכלול בו גם י"ט ויאמר לכל שבתות וי"ט של שנה זו להוצי' נפשיה מפלוגת' במידי דלית ביה חסרון כיס ולא נקר' שינוי מאחר שאינו עושם מעשה חדש עכ"ל בזה שכתב להוסיף בברכה נראה נכון דגם רמ"א כתב כאן דיש תועלת בעירוב להוציא כל מה שיש לו תורת כלי כו' אבל מ"ש רש"ל להחמיר בסכינים לא נלע"ד כן דודאי סכינים כל היום מקרי צורך אוכל נפש דאי מזדמן לו איזה פרי יאכל עמהם וזה הוכחתי גם לענין שבת בסי' ש"ח ס"ד דאסור לטלטל אפילו בבית דבר שאין בו צורך היום כלל וא"כ היאך נושאים סכינים כל היום קשורים בחגור' בשבת אלא ודאי דהוי לצורך כל היום ג"כ איני יודע לחלק בין חוץ לעיר או חוץ לעירוב דגם חוץ לעיר אין בו אלא איסור דרבנן דכרמלית הוא אלא דהתוס' בפ"ק דכתובות כתבו דאין ללמוד מחצר שאינה מעורבת קולא לכרמלית דחמיר טפי הביאם ב"י. ע"כ יש ליזהר טפי בכרמלית במידי דאין בו צורך אוכל נפש ואנן כל מבואות שלנו הם כרמלית כתב הרמב"ם בפ"ב כל שאסור לאכלו או להשתמש בו בי"ט מפני שהוא מוקצה אסור לטלטלו ע"כ פי' אבל לא שאסור משום חוץ לתחום דמותר בטלטול כמ"ש סימן תקט"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is permitted to play with a ball, etc.</b> Maharshal wrote that it is astonishing to permit, for there is no need on Yom Tov at all except children's play; but for adults it appears in my eyes a bad custom; and even though he wrote thus in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, it is not comfortable to me, for it is possible he only came to resolve what they practiced thus; and if my strength were sufficient I would abolish it, for the Rosh did not copy this. Maharshal wrote: and regarding carrying knives, in my youthful days I saw that they were careful when going to stroll outside the city on Yom Tov not to carry the knives with them, for the reason that knives after eating have no need of the day, for it is not the way to eat further that day; but nevertheless outside the eruv they would carry, for the Sages only instituted eruv chatzeros on Shabbos when it is forbidden to carry out any thing; but on Yom Tov when it is permitted to carry out any thing except what has no need of the day at all, for this reason they did not institute eruv chatzeros; and so is the view of the Rif and Tosafos; and the Ran distinguished on Yom Tov that there is no eruv chatzeros there, except that the Rashba, etc.; and there is proof in the Gemara for his reasoning, etc. And it is fitting for every God-fearing person to place eruv chatzeros on Yom Tov as on Shabbos, except that one should not change the custom; but nevertheless according to what they practiced to place an eruv on Erev Pesach for the whole year, he should include in it also Yom Tov and say "for all the Shabbosos and Yom Tov of this year," to take himself out of the dispute in a matter that has no monetary loss, and it is not called a change since he is not performing a new act; end quote. In this that he wrote to add in the blessing it appears correct, for the Rama also wrote here that there is benefit in an eruv to carry out whatever has the status of a vessel, etc. But what Maharshal wrote to be stringent regarding knives does not appear correct to my humble opinion, for certainly knives throughout the day are called a need of ochel nefesh, for if some fruit presents itself to him he will eat with them; and this I also proved regarding Shabbos in siman 308, se'if 4, that it is forbidden to move even in the house something that has no need of the day at all; and if so how do they carry knives all day tied in the belt on Shabbos? Rather certainly it is also for need throughout the day. Also I do not know how to distinguish between outside the city or outside the eruv, for even outside the city there is only a rabbinic prohibition, for it is karmelit; rather Tosafos in the first chapter of Kesubos wrote that one may not learn from a courtyard that is not joined by an eruv a leniency for karmelit, for it is more stringent; Beis Yosef brought them. Therefore one should be more careful in karmelit regarding something that has no need of ochel nefesh; and all our alleyways are karmelit. The Rambam wrote in chapter 2: whatever is forbidden to eat or to make use of on Yom Tov because it is muktzeh is forbidden to move; until here. Meaning: but not that it is forbidden because of outside the techum, for it is permitted by moving, as written in siman 515.

<b>אלא שומטו כו'. </b> בגמ' ס"ל כן לרב מלכיו א"ר חייא בר אשי והוא שיש עליו כזית בשר רבינ' אמר אע"פ שאין עליו כזית בשר מותר לטלטלו מידי דהוה אקוץ בר"ה פירש"י טלטול גמור כמו דאמרי' בפ' כירה בקוץ בר"ה מוליכו פחות פחות מד"א וקי"ל כרבינ'  אלא דק' למה פסקו טור וש"ע דבעי' שומטו דהיינו טלטול מן הצד דהא בקוץ קי"ל אפילו בטלטול גמור ואפילו לגי' הרא"ש דל"ג בדברי רבינא מותר לטלטלו אלא סתם שרי וא"כ י"ל דקאי ארבי חייא לחוד לחלוק עליו דלא צריך כזית בשר אבל אדרב מלכיו אינו חולק ובעי' שומטו מ"מ ק' דהא רבינ' מדמיהו לקוץ בר"ה וק"ל בסי' ש"ח דאפילו טלטול גמור מותר צ"ל דכאן שמטלטלו טלטול גמור בשעה שרוצה לצלות בו ואתי למטעי לטלטלו גם אח"כ אפי' שלא לצורך ע"כ עשו היכר' שדוק' דרך השמטה יניחנו בקרן זוית כנ"ל ופשוט הוא שאם רוצה לצלות בו שנית בשר דמותר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Rather he removes it, etc.</b> In the Gemara they hold thus according to Rav Malkiyo; R' Chiya bar Ashi said: and that is when there is a ke-zayis of meat on it; Ravina said: even though there is not a ke-zayis of meat on it, it is permitted to move it, just as [one may move] a thorn in a public domain. Rashi explained: full moving, as we say in chapter "Kirah" regarding a thorn in a public domain: he carries it less than less than four amos; and we hold like Ravina. However it is difficult: why did the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule that we require "he removes it," that is moving from the side? For regarding a thorn we hold even full moving. And even according to the Rosh's version that does not have in Ravina's words "it is permitted to move it," but rather simply "it is permitted": if so one can say it refers only to R' Chiya to disagree with him that a ke-zayis of meat is not required; but regarding Rav Malkiyo he does not disagree and we require "he removes it." Nevertheless it is difficult: for Ravina compared it to a thorn in a public domain, and we hold in siman 308 that even full moving is permitted. One must say that here, since he moves it with full moving at the time he wishes to roast on it, and he may come to err to move it also afterward even without need: therefore they made a distinguishing mark that only by way of removal he should set it down in a corner, as appears to me. And it is obvious that if he wishes to roast meat on it a second time it is permitted.

<b>It is permitted to move a ladder, etc.</b> I must briefly clarify the sugya with Rashi's explanation and explain it with God's help, for I saw explanations in it that were not clear to my humble opinion. In the mishnah: Beis Shammai say one may not take the ladder from dovecote to dovecote, and Beis Hillel permit; R' Chanan bar Ami said: the dispute is in a public domain, for Beis Shammai hold the onlooker will say he needs to plaster his roof and do melacha on Yom Tov, and Beis Hillel hold its dovecote proves concerning it; and there is another version in the Gemara, but the practical law is like this version; and in a private domain it is permitted according to both. And we say in a beraisa after the dispute of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel: R' Yehuda said, when was this said? Regarding a dovecote ladder; but an attic ladder, all agree it is forbidden. Rashi explained: for regarding that one certainly says he needs to plaster his roof. R' Dosa says: he tilts it from window to window. And we say in the Gemara: R' Chiya forbade to move even a dovecote one; for what the first Tanna permitted, that is a dovecote one for carrying, R' Dosa forbade, and came to say carrying is forbidden and tilting is permitted; and regarding an attic one he did not speak at all. And this is the language of the Rosh: and we conclude that an attic ladder is forbidden to move; and it is difficult from that of chapter "Window," where we say that an Egyptian ladder is something movable on Shabbos; and one cannot say that that sugya is like the version that said in a private domain it is permitted, for even according to that version that is specifically a dovecote ladder; or perhaps since Beis Shammai forbade a dovecote ladder in a private domain, the same applies that an attic ladder is permitted according to everyone in a private domain (it appears simple that there is a scribal error here, and one must say: or perhaps since Beis Shammai permit a ladder, etc.). However it is difficult that R' Chiya himself who forbids here: it is implied there that he holds Babylonian ladders have no fixed place and are taken on Shabbos. And Rabbeinu Tam answered that there it deals with house ladders that it is the way to move from corner to corner, and not similar to attic ladders that were large and fit to plaster a roof. And R' Eliezer of Metz answered that here on Yom Tov it is permitted to take out a ladder to a public domain, and the onlooker says he needs to plaster his roof; and since it is forbidden in a public domain, even in innermost rooms it is forbidden; but on Shabbos there is no way to take it out through a public domain, therefore in the house it is permitted. And according to this answer it is forbidden to move a ladder on Yom Tov; end quote. And this is the language of the Tur: it is permitted to move the ladder from dovecote to dovecote even in a public domain; and when was this said? A small ladder such as of a dovecote; but a large one such as of an attic is forbidden to move even in the house; end quote. And Beis Yosef challenged: here it is implied that he rules like Rabbeinu Tam's answer, for he wrote "but a large one such as of an attic is forbidden," and that is like Rabbeinu Tam's explanation that attic ladders are forbidden because they are large; and it is difficult that in siman 308 it appears he holds like R' Eliezer of Metz's answer, for he wrote even an attic ladder, though large, it is permitted to move in a courtyard on Shabbos, and that is like R' Eliezer, for according to Rabbeinu Tam both on Shabbos and on Yom Tov it is forbidden; end quote. And upon this he elaborated in answers and to distinguish between house and attic and dovecote, and in the end he left it requiring further study. It appears that his memory of blessed memory explained Tosafos and the Rosh who wrote according to this answer that our ladders are forbidden to move: that is even though they are small; and this is the novelty that R' Eliezer was stringent; and so Maharshal and my master and father-in-law of blessed memory understood. And to my humble opinion this is not the way at all regarding the Rosh's intent; rather this is the explanation: at first the Rosh challenged that here it is implied an attic one is forbidden, and in chapter "Window" we say an Egyptian one is permitted and it too is large like an attic one; and he answered that there it deals with a private domain, and in this even Beis Shammai permit; and afterward he challenged Rabbeinu Tam who forbids like R' Dosa, that is even regarding a dovecote one he permits there Babylonian ladders, and that is even in a public domain; and Rabbeinu Tam answered that those ladders a person always keeps in the house, which it is the way always to move from corner to corner, have no prohibition at all even in a public domain, and of this chapter "Window" deals; but those ladders that are always on the street standing for roof repair, those of attics we forbid here; and the matter does not depend on small and large, for certainly even those kept in the house are larger than a dovecote one mentioned in the Gemara and fit to plaster a roof; rather the permit is because it is their way to take them from corner to corner, and the onlooker will not say he needs to plaster his roof; which is not so of those always outside; and small and large are only a mere distinguishing mark that those outside make them larger than those kept in the house; and if so those in the house even if very large may be moved in the house, for there is no suspicion since they have a distinction from attic ones and all see that such a ladder belongs to the house. But R' Eliezer did not distinguish in this but between Shabbos and Yom Tov regarding moving in the house: on Shabbos there is no concern even if it is large, since he cannot plaster his roof, for it is forbidden to take out on Shabbos from private to public domain; but on Yom Tov there is concern of taking it out to plaster, then there is a distinction between a small one like a dovecote, regarding which they did not decree because of plastering, and a large one that is certainly always for plastering, therefore forbidden even in the house lest they err and say he moves it in order to take it out. And it emerges that there is leniency and stringency according to R' Eliezer's explanation: leniency regarding a very large attic ladder, permitted [on Shabbos] in the house, which is not so according to Rabbeinu Tam who only permits one that belongs and is recognizable as of a house; and this the Tur mentioned in siman 308, for he holds like R' Eliezer's explanation and that is essential; and stringency regarding one that always belongs to the house: Rabbeinu Tam permits since it is its way for house use, and according to R' Eliezer it is forbidden since one can take it out on Yom Tov and they will say [he needs] to plaster, etc. And the Rosh only mentioned the stringency alone, and therefore wrote: and according to this answer it is forbidden to move our ladder on Yom Tov, that is our ladders that are always inside the house; for in Talmudic times their roofs were flat and they plastered clay there as Rashi explained in the Gemara, therefore they kept special ladders outside for plastering; which is not so of ours, for we have no outside ladders for this but keep the ladder inside the house, and in this it would be permitted according to Rabbeinu Tam since it is always its way in the house, but according to R' Eliezer forbidden even in this since it is permitted to take it out. And in Tosafos they also wrote this matter that the Rosh wrote, except that they concluded in this language after R' Eliezer's answer: and according to this it is forbidden to move our ladder on Yom Tov even in his house, for perhaps the law is like R' Dosa; and their intent is even if we have small ladders it is forbidden, for perhaps we hold like R' Dosa who forbids even a dovecote one, and that is because in any case they are also fit to plaster a roof, for for this reason Beis Shammai forbade in the Gemara regarding a dovecote one. It emerges there are two stringencies: (1) even in the house; (2) even small ones like a dovecote, for according to Rabbeinu Tam a large one of a house is permitted; but the Rosh did not copy the second stringency, for he did not rule like R' Dosa but like the first Tanna that Beis Hillel permit a dovecote one like R' Yehuda, and an attic one is specifically forbidden, and that is on Yom Tov; therefore the Rosh only mentioned the stringency according to R' Eliezer's explanation regarding even one designated for the house being forbidden, against Rabbeinu Tam, and that is regarding a large one; and this distinction the Tur also mentioned, and it is according to R' Eliezer's explanation. And the Tur need not mention the stringency of our ladders always in the house, for it is obvious one should not be lenient for that reason; and the Rosh only wrote this because he first wrote Rabbeinu Tam's explanation who is lenient for that reason, so he needed to mention that according to R' Eliezer it is forbidden in this; but the Tur did not mention Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at all, and therefore need not mention that we are not lenient because it belongs to the house, for whence would one say so? And Maharshal also distinguished three distinctions among ladders and emended Tosafos's words: and further, perhaps the law is like R' Dosa; and according to what I wrote everything is comfortable and there is no forced stretch, with Heaven's help. But the Shulchan Aruch's words here who hold that an attic one is forbidden because it has no status of a vessel, and also on Shabbos he does not hold like R' Eliezer's explanation, and that is the Rambam's view; except that the Rambam forbade even a dovecote one to carry from dovecote to dovecote, only tilting alone. And it appears that for the practical law: on Shabbos it is permitted to move the ladder in our houses to take out or carry some thing from the attic downward, for this is permitted both according to Rabbeinu Tam and according to R' Eliezer; but on Yom Tov one should forbid the carrying, for perhaps R' Eliezer's explanation is essential and there is a prohibition lest they say [he needs] to plaster, etc. And even though nowadays we need not plaster our roofs because they are sloping, nevertheless a decree of the Sages is not nullified, for nevertheless one can err and say he wishes to take it out for some need of repairing his roof; rather only tilting should be permitted, as appears to my humble opinion.

<b>One may begin with piles, etc.</b> In the Tur it is written that it is prepared and standing for kindling, etc. Beis Yosef challenged: for afterward he wrote that we come according to R' Shimon per one version, and R' Shimon permits muktzeh and it is not required that it stand for kindling; and he strained to resolve this. And there is no difficulty: for this "prepared and standing for kindling" he did not mention for the reason of muktzeh, for in this "prepared" alone does not suffice, but specifically that it is rotten straw, as he wrote afterward; rather he needed this because whoever wishes to take straw for kindling must first make it into small bundles and tie them and afterward kindle with them, and this is forbidden to do even according to R' Shimon, for it is making a vessel; therefore he said it is prepared and standing for kindling, meaning there are already small bundles that need not be made on Yom Tov; nevertheless they do not stand specifically for kindling but also for animal food, and that is the essence; therefore it is only permitted according to R' Yehuda regarding rotten straw which is specifically for kindling, and in this specifically it is permitted according to R' Yehuda. And even though here he ruled like R' Yehuda and deals with rotten straw, nevertheless one must say it is already tied so that he need not tie it for kindling, for this is required according to everyone, as appears to my humble opinion. Beis Yosef further challenged: what does the Tur teach us, for it is obvious that according to R' Shimon muktzeh is permitted and according to R' Yehuda forbidden? And there is no difficulty: for there is a great novelty here, that one would have said even rotten straw is forbidden according to R' Yehuda, as Tosafos challenged, and this is their language: and if you say, what is different from wood in a storehouse that R' Yehuda forbade even though it appears fit for kindling? One can say: specifically regarding a straw storehouse he did not designate his mind away from what is fit now, for he only designated his mind away from animal food, and now it is fit for kindling and from this he did not designate; but a wood storehouse he designated his mind away from what is fit now; end quote. And it requires study of Tosafos's words who wrote he designated his mind away from what is fit now; and if so you have no greater muktzeh than this, for he designated from his mind that now it should be rotten straw and specifically for kindling. And one cannot say he is accustomed that there be rotten straw among him; nevertheless spikes are needed there and this is not common. And one can say: in any case the spikes did not fall there on Yom Tov but already came there at the time the straw was gathered; only he did not know of this until today on Yom Tov: it is permitted, for there is no muktzeh here but lack of knowledge; and in truth it was prepared for this from Bein HaShemashos, as appears to my humble opinion.

<b>One may take wood, etc.</b> In the mishnah we say: one may not take wood from the sukkah except from what is adjacent to it (Rashi explained: for example on Pesach or Shavuos when one sits in it for shade; rather from what is adjacent now; implying that if the schach is thick it is permitted to take from them). And this demolishes a tent (meaning: since he nullified it to the schach, any little bit he takes demolishes a tent). And Shmuel answered: what is "adjacent"? Adjacent to the walls (reeds stood up adjacent to the wall and not woven with them are not nullified, and it is not similar to schach that is not woven; therefore all are equal). Rav Menashe said: you may even say when it is not adjacent to the walls; Rashi explained: rather adjacent to the schach; for it was taught regarding Issurayasa, bundles of reeds: since he did not untie their binding and placed them on the schach, he did not nullify them but [intended] to store them there. And Hagahos Asheri wrote: and he did not nullify them to the wall; and when he did not intend to make the wall thick; for if he intended to thicken it, it is forbidden; and so we say in the Yerushalmi; end quote. And this is the language of the Tur: one who makes a sukkah on Shavuos for shade may not take wood from it on Yom Tov (it appears the explanation is: certainly even if he makes it before Shavuos even for the festival of Sukkos its law is thus; rather if he wrote this law regarding one made for the festival, one would say the prohibition is because of the festival sanctity that rests on the sukkah; it teaches that even a shade sukkah is forbidden; and Beis Yosef strained over this for nothing); even if it was rickety and fell; and R' Shimon permits a rickety one that fell (for if it was not rickety before Yom Tov even R' Shimon concedes it is forbidden, for he certainly removed his mind from it; rather regarding a rickety one, for he puts in mind that it will fall: according to R' Shimon there is no muktzeh, for we say his mind was on it from yesterday; and according to the first Tanna we do not say his mind [applies] to anything not fit from Bein HaShemashos); and if after he finished it with its walls and its schach he leaned additional bundles to it and did not attach them there, or threw still more on the schach, it is permitted to take according to everyone; end quote of the Tur. And Maharshal wrote: that is why the Tur wrote "after he finished," etc.: that then it is permitted even if he intended to thicken; for Hagahos Asheri only forbade intending to thicken before finishing in the process of making it. And this does not appear correct to my humble opinion: for once he intends to thicken, what does the finishing that already was help, since he intends to thicken the wall and it will remain there, of itself it is nullified to the wall, and certainly forbidden to take from there; rather on the contrary we must say the Tur's intent is specifically when he does not intend to thicken at all, that is when everything needed was already finished and there is no need to thicken, then specifically it is permitted; and therefore he said "after he finished it with its walls," meaning it needs no repair for schach or wall; this appears to me simple and clear. Beis Yosef further challenged the Tur regarding what he wrote "he leaned additional bundles to it," etc., implying that also regarding walls there is no permit by leaning except with bundles; and this is not so, for it is explained in Rashi that by the wall even if they are not bundles it is permitted with reeds, that is stood up adjacent to them. And my master and father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that according to Rav Menashe who said regarding Issurayasa he holds that also regarding walls specifically bundles are required, and the Tur ruled like him; and the Gemara's language does not imply thus, for Rav Menashe said "you may even say when not adjacent to the walls," etc., implying that regarding the schach he said we can find a permit through bundles, but regarding walls bundles are not needed, but as Shmuel; and so is explained by the language "you may even say," meaning one need not force and explain "adjacent" as specifically to the walls; but in the law he does not disagree with Shmuel, for otherwise he should have said the mishnah whether walls or schach [requires] bundles; and both are law, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rif and the Rosh. And Maharshal also wrote that the Tur holds Rav Menashe disagrees with Shmuel, for he did not write a permit above except that he threw after making the sukkah, but did not write that if he placed tied bundles it is kosher in every manner; rather he was not concerned about it because he ruled like Shmuel who only permits adjacent to the walls, as he wrote "he leaned after making the sukkah," and the same applies above; but I do not know why we should not say they do not disagree and both are true; end quote. And his words are very puzzling in his understanding of the Tur as permitting to throw there above without a bundle since he already made the schach; and this is certainly forbidden according to everyone, for otherwise one would explain the mishnah as permitting adjacent to the schach because the schach was already finished; rather certainly regarding schach it is forbidden in every manner except with tied bundles; and the Tur who wrote "or that he threw still more on the schach" refers to bundles mentioned before this. And the explanation of his words: certainly Shmuel does not disagree with Rav Menashe, and the Tur rules like both: that is, regarding schach there is a side of elevation to permit if he places there a tied bundle, since he did not untie it, for anyone who wishes to cover scatters the bundle; rather certainly he intends to take them later from there; and this is Rav Menashe's answer. But regarding walls there is more of a permit without bundles, for one who wishes to strengthen the wall places there a tied bundle so that it strengthen the wall by its weight; and if so the Tur well mentioned bundles, that is tied ones, in the opening clause regarding walls for a novelty: that even with bundles that strengthen the wall it is permitted since they are not attached to them; and all the more so when they are not bundles it is permitted regarding walls since they are no strengthening to the wall at all. And in the concluding clause regarding walls (schach) it is specifically with bundles that it is permitted, since he did not untie them as is people's way; but when they are not bundles it is forbidden above. And all this is after he finished it, when his intent is not for benefit by adding: that is when everything belonging to schach and walls was already finished, then there are these permits; but if he intended an addition that it remain there, this is forbidden both regarding walls even without a bundle and regarding schach even with a bundle; all this appears clear in the Tur's explanation with Heaven's help. And Beis Yosef wrote: and it appears to me where he leaned to the wall or on the schach tied bundles, since they are not nullified to them it is permitted to take from there; the same applies that on the festival Sukkos it is permitted to take, for the name of the festival did not rest upon them; however it is possible that by rabbinic law they are still forbidden because of muktzeh, as they forbade what is beyond the measure needed for a sukkah for this reason, as written in siman 638; end quote. And to my humble opinion one should distinguish: adjacent to the wall, where we have no proof to say he intended not to add, one should forbid on the festival Sukkos; but regarding schach with a tied bundle where there is proof he did not intend to add, and if so he should have permitted this, one should permit even regarding a mitzvah sukkah, as appears to my humble opinion.

<b>A house that is full, etc.</b> In the mishnah we say: a house that is full of produce and was breached: he takes from the place of the breach. The Rama: he may even demolish l'chatchila and take. Why? Does he not demolish a tent? Shmuel said: with a course of bricks (Rashi explained: an arrangement of stones without mortar). And the reason it is permitted to take from the place of the breach: we do not say they are muktzeh on account of a prohibition, that one cannot breach on Yom Tov, since breaching is only forbidden by rabbinic law, for there is only a course of bricks there: they are not muktzeh. And therefore the Rosh and the Tur wrote: but if it was a complete building, even R' Shimon concedes that it is forbidden to take from the place of the breach. And the Ran wrote that the Ramban challenged this according to the first Tanna who does not permit to breach: why were the produce not forbidden because of muktzeh, even though there is only a rabbinic prohibition? For a rickety sukkah is muktzeh on account of a rabbinic prohibition; even regarding a sound one one is not liable for demolishing except when demolishing in order to build; and likewise regarding coins on the bed we say that by placing he becomes a base for something forbidden, and even after the coins were taken they are forbidden, even though there is only a rabbinic prohibition. Therefore the Ramban explained that even though it is forbidden to breach, the produce are not muktzeh on account of the prohibition of demolishing, for we only forbid when the prohibition is in their body or they became a base for a prohibition, similar to a lamp and a bed; but here the produce are fit and did not become a base, except that something else causes that he cannot benefit from them, and it is only as if his produce were in a place he cannot go; until here. Therefore the Rambam ruled without specification to permit, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch, implying even if it was a building wall it is permitted even if it was breached of itself on Yom Tov. And Beis Yosef wrote: and it appears that his reason is that we only established it as a course of bricks because of the difficulty according to R' Meir why he permits to breach; we must say the first Tanna deals in every manner; therefore since we hold like the first Tanna, even regarding a complete building it is permitted; end quote. The explanation of his words: certainly the first Tanna also deals with a course of bricks, for he certainly deals in the manner of which R' Meir deals and disagrees with him that it is forbidden to breach l'chatchila even with a course of bricks, and there is no permit except when it was breached of itself; and then it is permitted even regarding a complete building. And Maharshal also rules like the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. And according to this it appears to me that one who has produce outside the techum and a non-Jew brought them on Shabbos or on Yom Tov: they are permitted, for there is no muktzeh here, for the produce themselves today are not muktzeh, except that he cannot take them because of the techum prohibition. And since here it is permitted when breached of itself even with a Torah prohibition, all the more so here regarding what a non-Jew brought from outside the techum even for a Jew: nevertheless afterward it is like coming of itself, permitted to another Jew. And even according to Rashi and the Rosh and Tur who did not permit here regarding a complete building, nevertheless they permit with a course of bricks since it is only a rabbinic prohibition; the same applies regarding outside the techum, for techumim are rabbinic. And if so there is a great astonishment at the Rashba in a responsum that Beis Yosef brought in siman 515, and the Rama ruled it as law there at the end of that siman, regarding a Jew's produce that were outside the techum: that they are forbidden to everyone for the reason of muktzeh, since they were outside the techum the owners designated them away; and here it is proven according to everyone that it is permitted, for in any case muktzeh there is no more stringent than from a course of bricks here. Therefore it appears to my humble opinion that we do not hold like that responsum of the Rashba that the Rama mentioned at the end of siman 515. The Mordechai wrote in chapter "One may make an eruv": and if you say, even though we established it as a course of bricks where there is no demolishing and building, nevertheless how is it permitted to open it (meaning according to R' Meir's words)? Does he not move earth and stones that are muktzeh? And the Riva says that moving of muktzeh that is for the need of ochel nefesh is permitted to move in order to take the food; and a proof from our custom that we kindle upon ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov which are muktzeh, and we move them for the need of ochel nefesh to cover the pastry; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote: one should be astonished, for if so why did they not permit to slaughter l'chatchila in order to cover with ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov? And this requires further study; end quote. And his astonishment is itself astonishing: for Tosafos in the first chapter of Beitzah folio 8 bring this of the Riva that moving muktzeh for the sake of ochel nefesh is permitted, and there itself they bring the Yerushalmi that it is forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila in order to cover with ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov. And it is simple that the reason is that covering the blood is not considered ochel nefesh, for the meat is not forbidden if he did not cover; rather covering is a mitzvah by itself upon him, and he should not cause the mitzvah to be nullified l'chatchila.
