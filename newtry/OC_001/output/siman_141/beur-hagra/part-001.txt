<b>או כו'. </b>כן הנהיגו עכשיו שלא לבייש את מי שאינו יודע וכמ"ש בפ"ג דביכורים תוס' מגילה שם בפ"ג דר"ה כ"ז א' ובפ"ג דמנחות כ"א ופ"א דב"ב והרא"ש כ' טעם אחר משום אותן שא"י ורוצין לקרות בע"פ והציבור אינן יוצאין בקריאתן ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or, etc.</b> This is the present enactment, so as not to embarrass one who does not know how to read, as stated in chapter 3 of Bikkurim; Tosafos in Megillah there; chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah 27a; chapter 3 of Menachos 21a; and chapter 1 of Bava Basra. The Rosh gives another reason: because there are those who do not know how to read and wish to read by heart, and the congregation does not fulfill its obligation through their reading. See there.

<b>שלא כו'. </b>כמ"ש בזוהר ח"ג כ"ב א' ואי ההיא צלותא אישתמע לאודנין דב"נ כו' בג"ד מאן דקרי באורייתא חד קרי וחד לשתוק כו' ומ' דומיא דתפלה שלא ישמיע לאזניו וס"ל כתוספתא אבל בסי' ק"א פ' דגם בתפלה משמיע לאזניו וז"ש בהג"ה ואפי' כו'. ואין ראיה מהזוהר דשם אמר שלא ישתמע לאחר וכמ"ש מ"א שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that, etc.</b> As stated in Zohar, volume 3, 22a: “If that prayer is audible to a person’s ears,” etc.; “therefore, when one reads from the Torah, one reads and the other remains silent,” etc. This implies that, as with prayer, he should not make it audible even to himself, following the Tosefta. Yet in siman 101 it was ruled that even in prayer one makes it audible to his own ears. This is the meaning of the gloss, “and even,” etc. There is no proof from the Zohar, because there it says only that it should not be audible to another person, as Magen Avraham writes there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 6: They may, etc.</b> This is unlike Orchos Chayim, who writes that they may not read because it is stated, “The testimony of Hashem is faithful,” while they are disqualified from testimony. Yet women and minors are disqualified from testimony and may nevertheless receive an aliyah, as stated in chapter 3 of Megillah. The same applies to a slave, as stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 2 of Kesubos, from the Baraisa cited in the Gemara there: “He read three verses,” etc.
