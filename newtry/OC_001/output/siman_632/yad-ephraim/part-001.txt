<b>There.</b> "But it appears to me that Rashi's intent is that if there were a wall there, we would say bent wall, etc." Many struggled with the words of the Magen Avraham in this matter, and some explanations given for him are not at all clear. In my humble opinion, however, the thoughts of the Magen Avraham are deep, and their ways are not his ways. It appears that his intent is as follows. It is explained that even though he did not insert all of it, it is invalid, because that handbreadth outside is also considered sukkah, for if it were valid s'chach, etc. He wrote this so that you should not say that invalid s'chach means specifically four handbreadths placed inside the sukkah, whereas this handbreadth that extends outward, in a place where it has already ended and goes beyond the mouth of the sukkah, does not concern us, and is like something severed, so that we should consider only the three handbreadths inside the interior of the sukkah. Therefore he said that certainly, if this place where the handbreadth is outside were considered a place that is valid for the sukkah, we would say so, that since it is outside it does not join to invalidate the three handbreadths inside. But in truth, that place where the handbreadth is also would validate it if it were valid s'chach, for it is s'chach extending from the sukkah. If so, it is not outside the sukkah, but also inside the sukkah; and since all together it is four wide, it is invalid in the sukkah, and it does not help that it is outside. Rashi learned this from the Talmud, which gives the reason that it is s'chach extending outward, which is judged as a sukkah; it is clear that its repair is its ruin, for since outside too it would still be valid for sukkah if it were valid s'chach, therefore it is like a board of four inside the sukkah, which is invalid. But all this is when the board is at the mouth of the sukkah. If it is placed where there is a wall, we properly say that this handbreadth outside stands alone and is not considered one with the three handbreadths inside, for there one cannot say that it too is inside the sukkah, since it is outside the sukkah and one cannot consider it part of the sukkah's s'chach; rather, one would only say that it belongs to the wall by the law of bent wall. However you formulate it, in any case this handbreadth has no entrance into the sukkah at all. Since one cannot say that the handbreadth is also in a place valid for the sukkah, that handbreadth therefore stands alone, and automatically those three handbreadths that are in the interior of the sukkah are considered s'chach and join to complete the s'chach measure even in a small sukkah. Now you will understand the intent of the Magen Avraham in what he wrote: "But it appears to me that Rashi's intent is that if there were a wall there, we would say bent wall, and that handbreadth projecting outward would not be considered part of the sukkah at all, etc." He means that Rashi's intent in saying "where there is no wall, so that it can be excluded through bent wall" is not as Maharsha, of blessed memory, understood, that if there is a wall and we say bent wall, this helps to validate the three handbreadths of the board. That is obviously not so, as Maharsha wrote: if you judge it by the law of bent wall, you cannot judge it as s'chach that joins in a small sukkah; so too the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 633, small paragraph 6, regarding a platform; see there. Rather, Rashi's intent is not to create a permit through bent wall, but to say that its ruin is its repair: if there were a wall here, we would say bent wall, and through that the handbreadth would remain outside and would not be considered part of the sukkah at all, as though it too were in its interior; it would dwell alone and would not be considered together with those three handbreadths inside. If so, it would be as though the board did not contain four, and it would be as though all of it were valid s'chach. The Magen Avraham does not mean that the interruption of the wall alone helps to separate them from the board; rather, since your whole basis for wanting to consider the board in its entirety as though it is in the interior is that it is also placed in a place valid for a sukkah, so that it is as though it is inside the interior of the sukkah, this cannot be said in the place of a wall, for even if it were inside the interior we would remove it from the sukkah through the law of bent wall. Since the handbreadth is outside, the three handbreadths inside join to validate the sukkah in a small sukkah. This is what he wrote: "for now the initial assumption was that if he turned them on their sides it is valid." Meaning: according to what we rule, that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid because they are like metal spits, and since it is a small sukkah and those three handbreadths are invalid s'chach and are not fit to join, it is invalid, for there is no sukkah measure here. About this he wrote: "If so, although we rule that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid" [in the Magen Avraham it says "and if you say, although, etc." In the precious book and in Mekor Chayim they wrote that it is an abbreviation and perhaps means "and one can say, and it is not so." But it is a printing error, and it should read "and if so, although"; according to their words it should have written "for although"]. Nevertheless, it is invalid s'chach of less than four, since the wall is between them, etc. Meaning downward: according to Maharsha's words, even in a large sukkah where part of the board is inside in a place of a wall, it is forbidden to sit beneath it, since we say bent wall. About this he wrote that according to his explanation, which upholds Rashi's words, in the place of a wall the handbreadth does not join because it is outside; the wall interrupts, and one should not join it, since if it were inside the sukkah's interior, were you to judge bent wall, it too would leave the category of s'chach and take the name of wall. Since it does not join, one no longer needs to say bent wall regarding the three handbreadths in the interior, and the name s'chach remains upon them. If so, even according to what we rule, that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid and the three handbreadths too are called invalid s'chach and do not join in a small sukkah, so there is no valid sukkah measure, nevertheless in a large sukkah, where there is a sukkah measure without them and there is merely a name of invalid s'chach upon the three handbreadths, since it is only three handbreadths it is permitted to sit beneath it. One who examines well will see that this is the intent of the Magen Avraham. I expanded the discussion because the words of the Magen Avraham are brief and people hesitated over them; therefore I saw fit to elaborate where he was brief, and the matters are clear, with Heaven's help.

<b>ט"ז ס"ק ב' </b>כיון דזה מקום חשוב לעצמו כו' תמוה דהא הך טעמא כ' רש"י אהא דבאמצע פוסל בד"ט. אבל כאן הטעם כיון דאין הכשרו רק ע"י דופן עקומה ולכן אע"פ שהסוכה כשרה לא יישן תחתיו וכ"כ הטעם בלבוש והוא מבואר מדברי רש"י ושאר פוסקים באמת שלשון זה שכתב הט"ז כאן היא ל' רש"י בסוכה דף י"ז ע"ב ד"ה אלא לדידכו כו' שזה מקום חשוב הוא לעצמו וחוצץ בסוכה זו כו' ע"ש (ומ"ש בט"ז ע"י חוצץ מלת על ידי ט"ס וצ"ל וחוצץ) והתם לענין סכך פסול באמצע מיירי ולכן נראה שהוא ט"ס וצריך להקדים ציון זה בריש הסי' על מ"ש באמצע בד"ט וכ"כ בהגהות מק"ח ואפשר שכוונת הט"ז לפרש הא דקאמר כ"ז שיש בו ד"ט אבל פחות מד"ט ישנים תחתיו דלכאורה י"ל דדוקא באמצע הדין כן דפחות מד' סכך כשר דהאי גיסא והאי גיסא מבטלים ליה ולכן כ' לעיל דפחות מד"ט ישנים תחתיו משא"כ כאן בפחות מד' מן הצד אע"ג דהסוכה כשירה היינו משום דופן עקומה ומ"מ אין ישינים תחתיו כיון שאין סכך כשר רק מצד א'. וע' בר"ן שהראב"ד סובר דאפי' באמצע בפחות מד' אין ישינים תחתיו רק בפחות מג' אלא שאין כן דעת הרז"ה וכן דעת הטור ולכן כ' הט"ז כאן דעיקר טעם ד"ט הוא משום שהוא חשוב מקום לעצמו. וממילא דבפחות מד' דלא חשיב בטל הוא לגבי שאר הסוכה הכשרה בין באמצע ובין מן הצד וא"צ לדופן עקומה כלל בפחות מד"ט. ואפשר שחסר סיום הלשון וצ"ל זהו טעם פסול ד"ט באמצע וה"ה כאן דוקא כשיש ד"ט כנלע"ד:

<b>Turei Zahav, small paragraph 2.</b> "Since this is a significant place of its own, etc." This is astonishing, for Rashi wrote this reason about the case where invalid s'chach in the middle invalidates with four handbreadths. But here the reason is that its validation is only through bent wall, and therefore although the sukkah is valid, one should not sleep beneath it. So too the Levush wrote the reason, and it is clear from the words of Rashi and the other poskim. In truth, this language that the Turei Zahav wrote here is Rashi's language in Sukkah 17b, s.v. "rather, according to you, etc.": "for this is a significant place of its own and it separates in this sukkah, etc." See there. [What is written in the Turei Zahav, "through separating," the words "through" are a scribal error, and it should read "and it separates."] There it is speaking about invalid s'chach in the middle. Therefore it appears to be a scribal error, and this notation should be moved earlier, at the beginning of the siman, on what he wrote "in the middle, with four handbreadths." So wrote the glosses of Mekor Chayim. It is also possible that the Turei Zahav intended to explain that which he says, "as long as it has four handbreadths," but with less than four one sleeps beneath it. Seemingly, one might say that this law applies specifically in the middle, where less than four is nullified by the valid s'chach on this side and that side; therefore he wrote above that with less than four handbreadths one sleeps beneath it. But here, with less than four from the side, although the sukkah is valid because of bent wall, nevertheless one should not sleep beneath it, since valid s'chach is only on one side. See the Ran, who says that the Ra'avad holds that even in the middle, with less than four, one does not sleep beneath it, only with less than three; but this is not the view of the Razah, and so is the view of the Tur. Therefore the Turei Zahav wrote here that the primary reason for four handbreadths is that it is considered a significant place of its own. Automatically, with less than four, since it is not significant, it is nullified to the rest of the valid sukkah, whether in the middle or from the side, and one does not need bent wall at all with less than four handbreadths. Perhaps the end of the language is missing, and it should read: this is the reason for the invalidity of four handbreadths in the middle, and the same applies here, specifically when there are four handbreadths. So it appears in my humble opinion.
