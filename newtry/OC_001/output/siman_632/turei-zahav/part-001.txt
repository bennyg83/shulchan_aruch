<b>For we say bent wall.</b> In the first chapter, in the Mishnah, it says: if he distanced the roofing from the walls three handbreadths, it is invalid. Rashi explained: it is not speaking of height, but of its horizontal extension; it is invalid because open air invalidates with three even from the side. From here I say and explain that which we say, that with invalid s'chach from the side we say bent wall to validate up to four amos: this means that we consider the s'chach as though it is from the wall, and it bent and folded upward. But regarding open air one cannot say this. I do not explain it as: we view the wall as though it is bent and goes under the invalid s'chach and reaches the valid s'chach, thereby taking the invalid material outside the sukkah, for if so, regarding open air too let us say this. These are Rashi's words. The explanation of his explanation appears to be that "bent wall" means that with the invalid s'chach at the side, we say that the s'chach of the sukkah gives part of itself to the wall; that is, the small part of the s'chach that is invalid, we take from the s'chach and attach it to the wall, and we say that it bent upward. Therefore, if there are three handbreadths of open air there, the s'chach has no part to give to the wall, for there is nothing there to give, since there is open air there. But if you explain that the wall bends and goes under the valid s'chach, etc., for now you cannot say that the wall gives part to the s'chach, as I wrote just before, because the wall ends above when it reaches the s'chach, and what does it have to give? Rather, according to this you must say not that it gives a part of itself, but that we view the wall as though it goes by bending and places itself at its top under the valid s'chach. If so, regarding open air too let us say this, that we view it as though it goes by bending. Why should it be different? Rather, certainly the first explanation is primary. Now according to this, if there is a wall much lower than the s'chach, but higher than the ground by ten handbreadths, and in part of the s'chach there is invalid s'chach, we properly say bent wall, for we can well say here that the s'chach gives part of itself to the wall. Even though the wall does not reach there, nevertheless we say in siman 630 that it is valid when it is ten handbreadths high and much lower than the s'chach, and we consider it as though the wall reaches the s'chach. So too here regarding bent wall. The matter appears even by an a fortiori argument: if when the wall reaches the s'chach, and if you say that the s'chach gives it part of itself, there is no fitting place for that part to attach to the wall, for there is already a wall there, and nevertheless we say that it is as though it receives part from it, all the more so here, where there is empty space that can receive the part taken from the s'chach, namely the invalid part. For this reason our master the Tur wrote properly: even if the wall is only ten handbreadths and the roof is much higher than it, we view the wall as though it rises upward, and then we say bent wall. These are his words. The Beis Yosef wrote that he learned it from Rashi's words: for if so, regarding open air too let us say this. And if it is so that wherever open air interrupts in height we do not say bent wall, all the more so we should not say bent wall where open air runs horizontally. His words are very astonishing, for he makes something taught dependent on something not taught. Indeed, wherever there is bent wall in height, there is also bent wall in horizontal open air; and if there is no bent wall in horizontal open air, there is likewise no bent wall in height. According to what I wrote, the view of the Tur appears correct. However, the Ran wrote that we do not say bent wall when the walls do not reach the s'chach, and I do not know the reason for his words. Furthermore, why is this different from what we say in siman 630:9 regarding walls seven and a bit high, etc., even if the roof is much higher, and even if it is not exactly aligned but is within three opposite it, it is valid? You see clearly that first he says it is as though it is high until the s'chach, and afterward he says <i>lavud</i> upward to the s'chach. The same should apply regarding bent wall, for both <i>lavud</i> and bent wall are laws given to Moshe at Sinai; why should we not equate them in this?
