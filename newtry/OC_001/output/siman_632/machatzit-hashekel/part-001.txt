<b>(ס"ק א) הכותל כו'</b> מגיעות לסכך. דאם יש אויר מפסיק בין הכותל לסכך איך א"ל דופן עקומ' דהיינו שיחשב הסכך לדופן ויצטרך להדופן. הא אי אפשר לצרפו דהא אויר מפסיק:

<b>ודע כו'</b> ונידון כסכה. וז"ל רש"י שם אהאי ענינא דף י"ד סוף ע"ב דהאי טפח דאבראי מיחשב נמי כסכה דאלו סכך כשר הוי מותר לישן שם ויוצא ידי סכה דאמרינן לקמן פסל היוצא מן הסכ' כה"ג נידון כסכ' עכ"ל רש"י. והוא וזה שנזכר בסי' תרל"א סעיף ו'. ור"ל כמו לענין הכשר אף על גב שדופן האמצעי לא נעשה בשביל מחיצות החיצונות אפ"ה כשר דע"כ (חד) סככה הוא הפנימי והחיצון. ה"ה לענין פסול חשבינן כאלו גם טפח הד' הוא בפנים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And know, etc.</b> And it is judged as a sukkah. This is Rashi's language there on this matter, page 14b at the end: "That handbreadth outside is also considered like a sukkah, for if it were valid s'chach it would be permitted to sleep there and he would fulfill the obligation of sukkah, for we say later that s'chach extending out from the sukkah in such a case is judged as a sukkah." These are Rashi's words. This is what is mentioned in siman 631:6. Meaning: just as regarding validation, even though the middle wall was not made for the outer partitions, nevertheless it is valid, for perforce the inner and outer parts are one s'chach; the same applies regarding disqualification: we consider it as though the fourth handbreadth too is inside.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If so, according to him.</b> Meaning, according to Maharsha, for it implies that he holds that even if there is a wall there, nevertheless we consider that handbreadth outside as though it is inside, and it is a board of four handbreadths inside, and there is invalid s'chach of four handbreadths, which invalidates a sukkah. It is only that we would validate it because of bent wall, as in every place where there is invalid s'chach of less than four amos. About this Maharsha asked: what does bent wall help? In any event the sukkah is invalid, for these three handbreadths of the board are lacking from the measure of the sukkah, since we are dealing with a small sukkah. If so, according to Maharsha's view, even in a large sukkah, although if we say bent wall a sukkah measure remains and it is valid, nevertheless it is forbidden to sit and sleep beneath those three handbreadths, like the law of bent wall.

<b>ה"ל כלו סכך כשר.</b> דהא השתא כו' ר"ל דשם פליגי אמוראי בנסרים שרחבן ד' טפחים דפסולים לסכך. אם הפכן על צדיהן והצדדים אין רחבים ד' טפחים אי רשאים לסכך בהן. אי נאמר כיון דאין מן הנסר דסכה רחב ד' טפחים שרי או דלמא נסר שרחב ד' טפחים יש עליו שם סכך פסול. ואפילו אין ממנו בסכה ד' טפחים מכל מקום אסור. מידי דהוי אשפודין של מתכת דאפילו אין רחבים ד' טפחים פסולים. ובעי הש"ס לאותבי' דמאן דאמר דמותר לסכך אם הפכן על צדיהן. מדתניא נתן עליה נסר רחב ד' טפחים אף על פי שלא הכניס לתוכה אלא שלשה טפחים פסולה. והיכי דמי לאו כה"ג שהפכן על צדיהן והצדדים אין רחבים כ"א ג"ט. אלמא כה"ג פסול. ומשני לעולם הפכן על צדיהן כשר. והב"ע דאנח נסר רחב ד' אפומא דמטללתא והכני' ג"ט לתוך הסכ'. וטפח א' יוצא חוץ לסכה. וע"י דופן עקומה. ואותן ג"ט שם דופן עליהם ולא מצטרפים עם הטפח שיוצא לחוץ לדעת מ"א. א"כ גם אותן ג"ט דין סכך כשר יש להן כיון דאין מן הנסר בסכה כ"א ג"ט. אע"ג דשם דופן עליהם מ"מ סכך כשר הוא. דלא אמרינן דופן עקומה כ"א שלא יצטרף עמו הטפח שיוצא לחוץ וא"כ מצטרף לשיעור הכשר סכה כיון דאין בסכה כ"א ג"ט דהא מותר לסכך כל הסכ' בנסרים רחביס ד"ט אם אינו מכני' מהן לתוך הסכ' כ"א ג"ט מכל נסר דהיינו הפכן על צדיהן ונתיישב בזה קושית מהרש"א דהוצרך לו' דאין שם דופן דאי הוי דופן הית' כשרה אע"ג דמיירי בסכה קטנה כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is all valid s'chach.</b> "For now, etc." Meaning: there the amoraim disagree about boards four handbreadths wide, which are invalid for s'chach. If he turned them on their sides, and the sides are not four handbreadths wide, may one use them for s'chach? Do we say that since none of the board within the sukkah is four handbreadths wide it is permitted, or perhaps a board that is four handbreadths wide has the name of invalid s'chach upon it, and even though no four handbreadths of it are in the sukkah, nevertheless it is forbidden, just like metal spits, which are invalid even though they are not four handbreadths wide. The Talmud sought to challenge the one who says that one may use them for s'chach if he turned them on their sides, from the Baraisa: if he placed on it a board four handbreadths wide, even though he inserted only three handbreadths into it, it is invalid. What are the circumstances? Is it not such a case, where he turned them on their sides and the sides are only three handbreadths wide? Evidently in such a case it is invalid. The Talmud answers: really, if he turned them on their sides it is valid; here we are dealing with a case where he placed a board four handbreadths wide at the mouth of the sukkah and inserted three handbreadths into the sukkah, while one handbreadth projects outside the sukkah. Through bent wall, those three handbreadths have the status of wall upon them and do not join with the handbreadth that projects outside, according to the view of the Magen Avraham. If so, those three handbreadths also have the law of valid s'chach, since only three handbreadths of the board are in the sukkah. Although the status of wall is upon them, nevertheless it is valid s'chach, for we say bent wall only so that the handbreadth projecting outside should not join with it. If so, it joins to the measure of a valid sukkah, since there are only three handbreadths in the sukkah; for one may cover the whole sukkah with boards four handbreadths wide if he inserts into the sukkah only three handbreadths from each board, namely where he turned them on their sides. With this Maharsha's question is resolved, for he had to say that there is no wall there, since if there were a wall it would be valid, even though we are speaking of a small sukkah, as above.

<b>ואע"ג דקי"ל כו'</b> ר"ל מה בכך ניהו דקושית מהרש"א מיושבת לפי הס"ד כמ"ד הפכן ע"צ כשר א"כ אי הוי שם דופן גם השלש' טפחים יש עליהן שם סכך כשר. אבל לדינא אין נ"מ כיון דקי"ל הפכן ע"צ פסול. וא"כ ניהו אם יש שם דופן אמרי' ע"י דופן עקומה אין טפח היוצא לחוץ מצטרף עם הג"ט שבפנים מ"מ יש על ג"ט שבפנים דין סכך פסול כמו בהפן ע"צ. וא"כ ה"ה בשינדלי"ן כה"ג. מ"מ ה"ל סכך פסול פחות מד"ט. גם למ"ד הפכן ע"צ פסול. אינו פוסל אלא בסיכך כל הסכ' בצדי נסרים זה אצל זה. אבל משום נסר א' שהופך על צדו לא נפסלת הסכ'. דאפי' נאמר שי"ל דין שפודים של ברזל. הא גם השפודים ובכל סכך פסול פחות מד' טפחים בסכה גדולה ישנים תחתיו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And although we rule, etc.</b> Meaning: what of it? Granted, Maharsha's question is resolved according to the initial assumption, like the one who says that if he turned them on their sides it is valid, for if there were a wall there, the three handbreadths too would have the name of valid s'chach. But in practice there is no practical difference, since we rule that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid. If so, although when there is a wall we say through bent wall that the handbreadth projecting outside does not join with the three handbreadths inside, nevertheless the three handbreadths inside have the law of invalid s'chach, as when they are turned on their sides. If so, the same would apply to shingles in such a case. Nevertheless, it is invalid s'chach of less than four handbreadths. Even according to the one who says that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid, it invalidates only when he covered the entire sukkah with the sides of boards next to one another. But because of one board that he turned on its side, the sukkah does not become invalid. For even if we say that it has the law of iron spits, even with spits and with all invalid s'chach, less than four handbreadths in a large sukkah may be slept beneath.

כאלו הדפנות מגיעות עד הסכך ואח"ז נאמר דופן עקומה. והם צריכים זה לזה דא"א לומר דופן עקומה אם לא נאמר תחלה לבוד. ומה לי אם צ"ל תחלה לבוד ואח"כ דופן עקומה או להיפך שצ"ל תחלה דופן עקומה ואח"כ לבוד. וכן משכחת כה"ג אם הדפנות מרוחקים מן הסכך פחות מג"ט ויש שם אויר. ג"כ צ"ל תחלה לבוד ואח"כ דופן עקומה א"כ גם לדידן נ"מ בדברי הר"ן דכה"ג לא אמרי' וה"ל להרב"י להביא דברי הר"ן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Since the height, etc.</b> We do not say <i>chavot</i> and <i>lavud</i> to be stringent. Meaning: first we would have to say <i>chavot rami</i>, as above in siman 631:5, and through this it would be as though the roof were placed below. Afterward we would have to say <i>lavud</i> of the roof with the beam. To be stringent, we do not say both. Granted, the Magen Avraham holds in siman 502, and likewise at the end of this siman, that we say <i>lavud</i> to be stringent, unlike the Rema; but he agrees that we do not say <i>chavot</i> and <i>lavud</i> together to be stringent. See what the Ran wrote about the dispute on page 11a, where Rabbi Eliezer and Ravina disagree, and everyone holds that if there was open air of three handbreadths in the sukkah and he reduced it with invalid s'chach, it is valid, for the invalid s'chach has no measure to invalidate and the open air too has no measure to invalidate, since three handbreadths of open air did not remain, and we say <i>lavud</i>. All this is when such a case is from the side. But if such a case is in the middle of the s'chach, they disagree whether we say <i>lavud</i> in the middle or not. The Ran asked: why did the dispute take this case, where the open air became less than three handbreadths because he reduced it with spits? It should have taken the simple case: there was originally open air of less than three handbreadths in the middle of the s'chach, whether we say <i>lavud</i>. He answered that it comes to teach a novelty according to the one who says there is no <i>lavud</i> in the middle: even when there had been three handbreadths of open air from the side and he reduced it with invalid s'chach, he agrees that we say <i>lavud</i> only if he placed the invalid s'chach next to the valid s'chach and the open air remained next to the wall; in such a case we say <i>lavud</i> from the side. But if he placed the invalid s'chach next to the wall, we do not say <i>lavud</i>, for this is <i>lavud</i> in the middle. Although one could say that the invalid s'chach placed next to the wall is bent wall, and if so the open air is from the side and one can say <i>lavud</i>, nevertheless we do not say two principles, bent wall and <i>lavud</i>. These are the contents of his words. Certainly the amoraim do not disagree about this; even according to the one who says that we say <i>lavud</i> in the middle, and that is how we rule, presumably he agrees that we do not say <i>lavud</i> and bent wall together. Although it appears from the Magen Avraham's words that we would say <i>chavot</i> and <i>lavud</i> together were it not that this is to be stringent, one must distinguish between bent wall with <i>lavud</i> and <i>chavot</i> with <i>lavud</i>. Nevertheless, it is surprising to me that the Beis Yosef did not bring the above words of the Ran as practical law, for we do not find poskim who disagree with the Ran, and one can also bring proof from the Talmud as above. Even if the Beis Yosef found someone who disagreed with the Ran in this, in any event it should at least be a dispute. Perhaps one can say that the Ran's intent is specifically according to the one who says there is no <i>lavud</i> in the middle; since one cannot say <i>lavud</i> unless one first says bent wall, and through this it becomes <i>lavud</i> from the side, in such a case we do not say it because the two principles need each other. But according to us, who say <i>lavud</i> in the middle, we say in one sukkah both bent wall and <i>lavud</i> if that sukkah needs bent wall and also <i>lavud</i>, since they do not need each other. Therefore the Beis Yosef did not bring the Ran's words for us. However, this is not so. Even if we say this reasoning, nevertheless there is a practical difference for us too, for sometimes one must say bent wall and also <i>lavud</i>, and they do need each other. According to what the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of this siman in the name of the Ran, that we say bent wall only when the walls reach the s'chach, if so the same applies where the walls are high until the s'chach, but the s'chach is distant across the width of the sukkah and there is open air there; according to the Ran one cannot say bent wall. But if that open air is not three handbreadths wide, one could first say <i>lavud</i>, so it is as though the walls reach the s'chach, and afterward say bent wall. They need each other, for one cannot say bent wall unless one first says <i>lavud</i>. What difference is there whether one must first say <i>lavud</i> and afterward bent wall, or the reverse, first bent wall and afterward <i>lavud</i>? Likewise such a case is found when the walls are distant from the s'chach less than three handbreadths and there is open air there; one must also first say <i>lavud</i> and afterward bent wall. If so, even according to us there is a practical difference in the Ran's words, that in such a case we do not say both, and the Beis Yosef should have brought the Ran's words.
