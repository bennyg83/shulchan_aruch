Whether a non-Jew's residence invalidates an eruv. Contains 20 sections One who lives with a non-Jew in a courtyard — the non-Jew does not restrict him until there are two Jews living in two houses who restrict each other, at which point the non-Jew restricts them. It does not help for the non-Jew to renounce his domain; rather they must rent from him. {Rama: Rema: A Jew who rented or lent his house to a non-Jew does not restrict him, since he did not rent or lend it to him in order to restrict him. A Jew who lent or rented a house to his fellow in a place where non-Jews live — even if he has a hold in the house, it does not help and they must rent from the non-Jew.}

**** ENGLISH ****
A courtyard in which a Jew and non-Jew live, where one Jew's house is adjacent to the other's and does not open to the courtyard but has windows between them — he cannot make an eruv with him through the window between them in order to carry his vessels through his neighbor's house which opens to the courtyard. But if there is an opening between them, it is permitted. Some say that even if there is an opening between them, it is forbidden.

A courtyard in which a Jew and non-Jew reside, where windows open from one Jew's house to another Jew's house and they made an eruv through the windows — even though they are permitted to carry from house to house through the windows, they are forbidden to carry from house to house through the doors on account of the non-Jew, until they rent from him. {Rama: Rema: A ship with many Jews on it who have designated quarters must make an eruv and rent domain from the non-Jewish ship owner, or have it included in the ship fare.
