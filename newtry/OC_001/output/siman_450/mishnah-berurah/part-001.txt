(א) קודם הפסח צריך לפורעו - ר"ל אפילו לוה ממנו ככר זה בע"פ סמוך לזמן הביעור שאם לא הלוהו בלא"ה היה צריך לבערו ואם לא ביערו היה נאסר בהנאה אעפ"כ צריך להחזיר לו ככר אחר אחה"פ שהרי עכ"פ לוה ממנו בשעת היתר ואז היה החמץ ממון גמור שהיה עדיין קודם זמן הביעור וכתבו האחרונים דאפילו אם עבר ישראל הלוה והשהה החמץ עד לאחר פסח ונאסר מ"מ צריך לשלם ולא אמרינן שלא יכול המלוה לקבל דמיו משום חליפי חמץ כדלעיל סימן תמ"ג דאין זה חליפי חמץ שכיון שלוה הככר הרי הוא שלו ואין לו למלוה עליו אלא דמים בעלמא. ישראל שהלוה חמץ לעכו"ם לפני הפסח ושילם לו חמץ כיוצא בו לאחר הפסח הואיל ואינו מחזיר לו חמץ הראשון בעיניה מותר [כ"כ בשלטי הגבורים בשם הריא"ז] ומוכח מדבריו דאם מחזיר לו אותו חמץ אסור ופר"ח חולק על זה דלא מיקרי חמץ שעבר עליו הפסח שהרי עד עתה היה שייך להעכו"ם דכיון שהלוהו קם ליה ברשותיה והו"ל חמץ של נכרי שעבר עליו הפסח דשרי אף באכילה ולמעשה יש להחמיר כדעה קמייתא דמחזי כחמץ של ישראל שעבר עליו הפסח כיון שהחזיר לו אותו החמץ גופא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(1) Before Pesach he must repay him.</b> This applies even if he borrowed the loaf on erev Pesach shortly before the time for destroying chametz. Had he not borrowed it, the lender would have had to destroy it, and had he failed to do so it would have become forbidden for benefit. Nevertheless, the borrower must return another loaf after Pesach, because he borrowed it while it was permitted and still constituted full monetary property. The later authorities write that even if the Jewish borrower transgressed by retaining the chametz until after Pesach, when it became prohibited, he must still pay. We do not say that the lender cannot receive its value because it would be proceeds of chametz, as discussed above in siman 443. This is not considered proceeds of chametz, because once the borrower received the loaf it became his property, and the lender retained only an ordinary monetary claim. If a Jew lent chametz to a non Jew before Pesach and the non Jew repaid him with equivalent chametz after Pesach, it is permitted, since he is not returning the original chametz itself. Shiltei HaGibborim in the name of Riaz. His words imply that if the non Jew returns the same chametz, it is prohibited. Peri Chadash disagrees, arguing that it is not chametz owned by a Jew through Pesach, since until now it belonged to the non Jew. Once it was lent to him it entered his possession, and is therefore chametz of a non Jew that remained through Pesach, which is permitted even for eating. In practice, one should be stringent like the first opinion, because returning the identical chametz makes it appear like chametz owned by a Jew through Pesach.

(ג) יאמר לו קודם - הטעם דכיון שנתחייב ליתן לו בכל שבוע ככר א"כ כשמיחד העכו"ם הככר בפסח בעבור הישראל הוי כאלו כבר זכה בו והוי כאלו עבר הפסח על חמץ של ישראל להכי צריך להתנות עמו שבעבור שבוע של פסח אינו רוצה ככרות רק הקמח שלהם או דמי שוין [או שיתן לו ככרות שיאפה אחר הפסח] ואם כן אף אם העכו"ם הכין לו ככרות בפסח לא איכפת לן שהרי אינם משועבדים כלל לישראל והם לגמרי ברשות העכו"ם לע"ע:

<b>(3) He should tell him beforehand.</b> Since the non Jew is obligated to give him a loaf every week, if the non Jew designates a loaf during Pesach for the Jew, it is as though the Jew has already acquired it and as though his chametz remained through Pesach. He must therefore stipulate that for the week of Pesach he does not want loaves, but rather their flour or their monetary value, or loaves that will be baked after Pesach. Then, even if the non Jew prepares loaves for him on Pesach, there is no concern, since they are not encumbered to the Jew at all and presently remain entirely in the non Jew’s possession.

(ד) ככרות חמץ - ר"ל אף אותן שהכין עבורו בפסח והסכימו האחרונים דכ"ז לכתחלה אבל בדיעבד אפילו אם לא התנה כלל נמי שרי לו לקבל הככרות אחר הפסח בשביל שבוע של פסח ואע"ג דיחדם לו לישראל בפסח דכ"ז שלא באו לרשות ישראל לא זכה בהם ואין לו עליו אלא חוב בעלמא של ככרות ולא אותן גופא והרי הן של עכו"ם עדיין כל ימי הפסח:

<b>(4) Chametz loaves.</b> This includes even the loaves the non Jew prepared for him during Pesach. The later authorities agree that all this applies initially. After the fact, even if he made no stipulation, he may receive loaves after Pesach in payment for the week of Pesach. Although the non Jew designated them for the Jew during Pesach, the Jew did not acquire them before they entered his possession. The non Jew merely owed him an ordinary debt of loaves, not those specific loaves, and they remained the non Jew’s property throughout Pesach.

**** ENGLISH ****
(ב) And I will take afterwards — some Acharonim wrote: specifically when he says "and I will take afterwards money against the loaf you took" — that is considered saying he does not want to rent his oven for work on Pesach except for money; or it deals when he says "take for yourself this week of Pesach the oven whether there is [bread] in it or not and I will take the week before or after what happens to me" — that is permitted, for they divide time and have no claim on each other. But when he says "take the loaves that will be on Pesach and I will take this amount afterwards" — then he wants the loaves in the oven [for if nothing is baked on Pesach in his oven the non-Jew will not give him after Pesach] and he acquires the oven for the Jew, only exchanging with the non-Jew partner; if so he benefits from his chametz on Pesach under his share in those loaves — the non-Jew gives him his share of loaves in the week after Pesach and it is forbidden. Some forbid even when he says "and I will take afterwards money against the loaves you took" — nevertheless he benefits from the value of his chametz on Pesach. Acharonim agreed that in practice one should practice selling the oven to the non-Jew for the week of Pesach so it is entirely in his possession, set a price for this, and take money from him first before Pesach — with this there is no prohibition from what occurs that Shabbat and Yom Tov week while it is absorbed.

<b>(15) Chametz.</b> He may not say to him, “Take this dinar and buy chametz from the shopkeeper and eat,” even if he is an unrelated non-Jew whose food is not his responsibility. Since the Jew himself may not purchase chametz on Pesach, he may not tell a non-Jew to purchase it. Although the non-Jew buys it for himself and not for the Jew, since the Jew gives him a dinar and tells him to buy chametz with it, and the non-Jew buys it at his instruction, he appears to be the Jew's agent. It is permitted, however, to say, “Buy chametz with your own dinar and eat.”

<b>(18) In this case as well.</b> According to this view, it is also permitted to tell the shopkeeper, “Give chametz to my workers and I will pay,” for the reason stated above. Regarding a child who urgently needs to eat or drink chametz, one should bring him to a non-Jew's house and ask the non-Jew to feed him chametz. One must be careful not to take it into his own hand, because he would thereby acquire it. If the non-Jew refuses to give it free of charge, one may promise to pay him afterward, since this is permitted according to the basic law. One may not, however, pay before or at the time the food is given, because he would then acquire the chametz, which is forbidden according to all opinions. If the child cannot be taken outside and the non-Jew must bring the chametz into the Jew's house and feed him there, the Jew should tell the non-Jew to gather the remainder and take it home. When the child needs to eat again, the non-Jew should bring it back and feed him. Otherwise, a partition ten tefachim high should be erected before it, or at least a vessel should be inverted over it. All this applies only when the child is not endangered. When there is danger, one need not be exacting in these matters, so that the feeding will not be delayed, as explained in siman 328.

<b>(28) And this is the custom.</b> Regarding selling chametz vessels to a non-Jew during Pesach, some permit it for the reason stated above, since he benefits only from the vessel itself and not from the absorbed chametz. This does not apply when the non-Jew pays more because of the useful absorbed substance, for then the Jew benefits from chametz. Others forbid the sale. The acharonim discuss a Jew who leases from a nobleman a brewery known as a malt house. Anyone wishing to brew beer uses that building and pays rent to the Jew. If non-Jews are allowed to brew there during Pesach, the Jew desires the chametz to continue to exist because of the brewery vessels, since if the malt were to spill while the kettle stands on the fire, the kettle could crack. By accepting rent he also profits from an item from which benefit is forbidden. Moreover, the established practice was to give the Jew a measure of malt as payment rather than money. Consequently, as soon as the non-Jew brews, the Jew acquires his measure of malt. Even if it is delivered only after Pesach, it is chametz belonging to a Jew that remained over Pesach and is forbidden. The acharonim advise that he sell his rights in the malt house to a non-Jew, or rent them out for several weeks so that the days of Pesach are absorbed within a broader rental period. They should merely record in their ledger what they ground during Pesach, and he may collect payment after Pesach. The same applies to one who leases a mill.
