<b>יאמר לו קודם פסח. </b> כ"כ הטור בשם אבי עזרי וכתב ב"י דבהג"מ ובמרדכי בדין ישראל שקנה חוק הכומרים יקח לאחר פסח מה שחסר בפסח משמע שא"צ להתנות כן תחלה וטעם לפי שאין כאן איסור אלא קנס דעבר על בל ירא' וכאן לא שייך למיקנסי' ופסק כן להחמיר ונ"ל דיש חילוק בזמן התנאי ביניה' דבדין זה שמגיע לו ריבית בכל שבוע הוי כל שבוע חוב בפ"ע דהא אי בעי מצי מסלק לי' כל שבוע נמצא כשמתנ' ישראל תחלת השבוע ה"ל כמתנה מעיקרא אא"כ זקף עליו הריבית בפעם א' ולא יסלק לו עד זמן שביניהם לא מועיל התנאי קודם הפסח ודבר זה יש ללמוד ממ"ש סי' רמ"ה לענין שותפות עם העכו"ם לענין שבת דבעי' התנה ה"נ כן הוא דכיון שכבר זכה ישראל בככר של שבוע של פסח מה מועיל התנאי לסלק עצמו ממנו וה"ל כמוכר קודם פסח חמצו שהיה לו בפסח משא"כ אם התנה תחלה לא זכה בו הישראל כלל באותו הככר ומ"ה בההוא שקונה חוק הכומרי' בפעם א' מה שמגיע לו על זמן קצוב צריך להתנות בשעת הקנין מטעם שזכרנו ולא אחר כך קודם פסח ואם לא יתנה תחלה תקנתו לבטל המקח אחר כך ולהתנות אז כמ"ש לענין שבת שם. ובתנור של שותפות עם העכו"ם שכתב אח"ז בסמוך דמועיל התנאי קודם הפסח התם נמי כיון שחצי התנור של ישראל יכול לומר לעכו"ם כל זמן שירצה איני רוצה להשתתף עמך בפסח אלא ע"מ שתתן לי דמים בעד חלק שלי וזה מהני שפיר כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should say to him before Pesach.</b> So wrote the Tur in the name of Avi HaEzri. The Beis Yosef wrote that in Hagahos Maimoniyos and Mordechai, regarding the law of a Jew who bought the priests' allotment, it says that after Pesach he may take what was missing on Pesach, implying that he need not stipulate this initially. The reason is that here there is no prohibition except a fine for having transgressed bal yera'eh, and here there is no place to fine him; nevertheless he ruled stringently. It seems to me that there is a difference between them in the time of the condition. In this law, where interest reaches him each week, each week is a separate debt, for if he wants he can remove him each week. It follows that when the Jew stipulates at the beginning of the week, it is as though he stipulated from the outset. But if he fixed the interest upon him at one time and cannot remove it from him until the time between them, the condition before Pesach does not help. This can be learned from what is written in siman 245 regarding partnership with a non-Jew for Shabbos, which requires that he stipulated. So too here: since the Jew already acquired the loaf of the week of Pesach, what does the condition help to remove himself from it? It is like selling before Pesach the chametz that he had on Pesach. This is unlike where he stipulated from the beginning, for then the Jew never acquired that loaf at all. Therefore, one who buys the priests' allotment at one time, what is due to him for a fixed period, must stipulate at the time of acquisition for the reason we mentioned, and not afterward before Pesach. If he did not stipulate at the beginning, his remedy is to nullify the transaction afterward and then stipulate, as written there regarding Shabbos. Regarding an oven in partnership with a non-Jew, which he writes shortly afterward, that a condition before Pesach helps, there too, since half the oven belongs to the Jew, he can say to the non-Jew whenever he wants: I do not want to be in partnership with you on Pesach except on condition that you give me money for my share. This works well, as it seems to me.

<b>א"ל קודם הפסח כו'. </b> נעלם ממני הבנת דברי הרב בזה טול אתה של פסח כו' דס"ס נהנה הוא מחמץ של פסח במאי דשקיל בעד שבוע שאחר הפסח כל השכר אפי' חלק העכו"ם מחמת שהעכו"ם שקיל חלק שלו בשבוע של פסח בשלמא גבי שבת בסי' רמ"ה מועיל התנאי בזה תחלה דהיינו בשעת עשיית השותפות דאז לא הוי לישראל מעולם חלק בזה השבת כדפירש"י התם הביאו ב"י מה שאין כן כאן דהיינו דהתנו קודם פסח וכבר יש לעכו"ם חלק בכל שבוע ולישראל חלק בכל שבוע נמצא שיש דמים לחמצו של ישראל בפסח ואע"ג שכתבו בסמוך שמועיל אפי' קודם הפסח היינו לענין שיתן לו דמים שיוכל הישראל לומ' לא אתן חלקי לעשו' בו מלאכ' בפסח אם לא שאקבל מעות לא חמץ ואז אף אם יתן לו חמץ אח"כ הוה חליפי מעותיו אבל כאן אין שייך כן דהא מתנה שיהא נוטל עכ"פ חמץ אחר הפסח בעד חלקו בפסח במה שכבר זכה בו בתחלת השותפו' מה מועיל התנאי בזה ובטור בשם רש"י כ' שאלה מה לו' לעכו"ם טול אתה של פסח ואני אטול אח"כ והשיב שיתנה קודם הפסח ויטול דמים מאותו שבוע ונראה דהך ויטול הדמים אין פירושו שאסור לו לקבל חמץ אח"כ רק דמים אלא ה"ק שיתנה קודם הפסח שיטול דמים ועמ"ש בסעיף שקודם לזה ואח"כ יכול ליקח אפי' חמץ אבל בנדון שכתב הש"ע הוא תמוה לי ששינ' מדברי רש"י ומדברי הג"מ שמביא ב"י בדבר שאין מותר לפי הנראה ובהג"מ שמביא ב"י כתוב אבל לענין פסח מותר להתנות קודם הפסח ונוטל הימנו דמים של אותו השבוע ול"ד לישראל כו' אבל הכא כיון שנוטל את הדמים מתחלה מותר עכ"ל וכ' ב"י אבל המרדכי לא הזכיר שצריך ליטול דמים אותו שבוע קודם פסח כו' ובאמת נראה דגם הג"מ לא נתכוין ליקח המעות תחלה דוקא דהא לא זכרו תחילה שנטילת המעות יהיה קודם פסח וכן כתו' בתשו' רש"י ותו' דאף חמץ יכול ליקח אחר הפסח כמ"ש בסעיף שקודם לזה והוא דברי אבי עזרי בטור אלא נ"ל דמ"ש מתחיל' פי' לכתחלה וקאי אדבתרי' דאמר מותר כלומר שכיון שנוטל דמים אפי' לכתחלה מותר דבר זה ולא כמ"ש בסעיף שאח"ז דדוקא קיבל כבר מותר כנלע"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He said to him before Pesach, etc.</b> I do not understand the Rav's words here, 'you take that of Pesach,' etc. In the end, he benefits from chametz of Pesach by taking, for the week after Pesach, all the wages, even the non-Jew's share, because the non-Jew took his share during the week of Pesach. Granted, regarding Shabbos in siman 245, the condition helps if made at the beginning, meaning at the time the partnership is formed, because then the Jew never had a share in that Shabbos, as Rashi explained there and the Beis Yosef brought. This is unlike here, where they stipulated before Pesach, and the non-Jew already has a share in every week and the Jew has a share in every week; it turns out that there is money for the Jew's chametz on Pesach. Although they wrote nearby that it helps even before Pesach, that is regarding where the non-Jew gives him money, so that the Jew can say, 'I will not give my share to do work with it on Pesach unless I receive money, not chametz'; then even if he later gives him chametz, it is an exchange for his money. But here this does not apply, for he stipulates that in any event he will take chametz after Pesach for his share on Pesach, which he already acquired at the beginning of the partnership. What does the condition help for this? In the Tur in the name of Rashi it is written as a question: what should he say to the non-Jew, 'you take that of Pesach and I will take afterward'? He answered that he should stipulate before Pesach and take money from that week. It seems that 'and take the money' does not mean that it is forbidden for him to receive chametz afterward and only money is allowed. Rather, it means that he should stipulate before Pesach that he will take money; and as I wrote in the preceding seif, afterward he may take even chametz. But in the case written by the Shulchan Aruch, it is astonishing to me that he changed from the words of Rashi and the words of Hagahos Maimoniyos brought by the Beis Yosef, in a matter that appears not to be permitted. In the Hagahos Maimoniyos brought by the Beis Yosef it is written: but regarding Pesach, it is permitted to stipulate before Pesach and take from him the money of that week; it is not comparable to a Jew, etc., but here, since he takes the money initially, it is permitted, end quote. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Mordechai did not mention that he must take the money of that week before Pesach, etc. In truth, it seems that Hagahos Maimoniyos also did not mean that he must specifically take the money beforehand, for they did not initially mention that taking the money must be before Pesach. So too it is written in the responsum of Rashi, and furthermore, that he can take even chametz after Pesach, as written in the preceding seif, and this is the words of Avi HaEzri in the Tur. Rather, it seems to me that what he wrote 'initially' means ab initio, and refers to what follows, where he said it is permitted; that is, since he takes money, even ab initio this is permitted, unlike what is written in the following seif, where it is permitted specifically only if he already received it. So it seems to me.

<b>אפי' מעות אסור כו'. </b> בטור כתיב אחר תשו' רס"י דהוא ס"ג וז"ל הרשב"א כתב מעשה בא לידי בישראל שהיה לו תנור ואפו בו עכו"ם חמץ בפסח ובא האופה גזבר של ישראל והביא לו ככרות לאחר הפסח משבח התנור ואסרתי לו לקבלם לא מבעי' חמץ שאסור שזכה בו ישראל בשבח תנורו וה"ל חמצו של ישראל אלא אפי' מעות אסור לקבל בשכרו דה"ל משתכר באיסורי הנאה כיון דחמץ בפסח אסור בהנאה אפי' חמצו של עכו"ם אסור ליהנות ממנו בפסח עכ"ל. ולפי הנראה לכאורה דל' הטור שכתב והרשב"א כתב משמע דיש פלוגתא בינו לרש"י היינו דהרשב"א אוסר ליטול הדמים שהתיר רש"י וזה אינו דרש"י התיר משום התנה קודם פסח ולרשב"א לא שייך תנאי ממילא הככרות שנאפו בפסח היו ממש של ישראל ע"כ אסור לכתחלה ליטול אפי' המעות בעדם אלא ע"כ דעיקר הפלוגתא ע"ד שכ' הב"י בשם ריב"ש דאיכא למ"ד לא ישכיר בהמתו לעכו"ם להביא עלי' חמץ דסבר חמצו של עכו"ם אסור בהנאה ואיכא למ"ד משכיר לעכו"ם וחמצו ש"ג מותר בהנאה מ"ה כיון דרש"י התיר ליטול דמים אע"ג דהדמים יבואו מחמת חמצו דעכו"ם א"כ ס"ל כמ"ד חמצו ש"ג מותר בהנאה אבל הרשב"א כתב בהדיא כמ"ד אפילו בשל עכו"ם אסור וא"כ יש תימה רבתי על הש"ע שהביא בסעיף שקודם לזה דברי רש"י וכאן מביא דברי רשב"א דלרשב"א אין תקנה לתנור של ישראל אפי' בשותפו' עם העכו"ם אא"כ התנו בשעת קניית התנור שבשבוע של פסח יהיה של עכו"ם וכההיא דסי' רמ"ה וא"ל על מ"ש הרשב"א דהישראל זכה בככרות  שהביא לו הגזבר אחר פסח ואסור ולמה לא נימא גם כאן דהתירא ניחא ליה דלקני ולא דאיסורא כמ"ש סי' תמ"ח לענין עכו"ם שמביא חמץ בי"ט דיכול הישראל אח"כ לו' כן ושרי דשאני התם דאין לנו הוכחה דניחא לי' שיקנה לו ביו"ט אמרי'. ודאי לא ניחא ליה באיסוריה משא"כ כאן דהחמץ באלו מחמת מעשה היום שאפה בתנורו היום שהוא נותן התנור לאפות בו היום ודאי ניחא ליה וזה פשוט לפע"ד ועמ"ש בסי' זה בסופו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even money is forbidden, etc.</b> In the Tur, after Responsum 260, which is seif 3, it is written as follows: the Rashba wrote: an incident came before me involving a Jew who had an oven, and non-Jews baked chametz in it on Pesach. The baker, the Jew's treasurer, came and brought him loaves after Pesach from the profit of the oven, and I forbade him to receive them. It is not necessary to say that chametz is forbidden, for the Jew acquired it through the profit of his oven and it is a Jew's chametz; even money is forbidden to receive as his wage, for he profits from items forbidden in benefit, since chametz on Pesach is forbidden in benefit, and even a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden for him to benefit from on Pesach, end quote. At first glance, from the Tur's language, 'and the Rashba wrote,' it implies that there is a dispute between him and Rashi, namely that the Rashba forbids taking the money that Rashi permitted. This is not so, for Rashi permitted because he stipulated before Pesach. According to the Rashba, a condition does not apply, and the loaves baked on Pesach were automatically actually the Jew's; therefore it is forbidden ab initio to take even money for them. Rather, the main dispute is in the manner that the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash: there is one who says one may not rent his animal to a non-Jew to bring chametz on it, because he holds a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden in benefit; and there is one who says one may rent to a non-Jew, because a non-Jew's chametz is permitted in benefit. Therefore, since Rashi permitted taking money although the money comes because of the non-Jew's chametz, he must hold like the one who says a non-Jew's chametz is permitted in benefit. But the Rashba wrote explicitly like the one who says even a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden. If so, there is a great wonder on the Shulchan Aruch, who brought the words of Rashi in the previous seif and here brings the words of the Rashba. According to the Rashba, there is no remedy for a Jew's oven, even in partnership with a non-Jew, unless they stipulated at the time of buying the oven that during the week of Pesach it belongs to the non-Jew, like the case of siman 245. Do not ask on what the Rashba wrote, that the Jew acquired the loaves that the treasurer brought him after Pesach and they are forbidden: why do we not say here too that he is pleased to acquire permitted things and not forbidden things, as written in siman 448 regarding a non-Jew who brings chametz on Yom Tov, where the Jew can afterward say so and it is permitted? There it is different, because we have no proof that he wants it acquired for him on Yom Tov, so we say he certainly does not want the prohibition. This is unlike here, where the chametz comes because of today's act, that he baked in his oven today, when he gives the oven to bake in today; he certainly wants this. This is simple in my humble opinion; see what I wrote at the end of this siman.

<b>יש מי שמתיר. </b> הוא דעת הטו' שמביא ראייה לזה מדתנן שכרו לו לעשות מלאכה אחרת אע"פ שא"ל העבר לי יי"נ ממקום למקום מותר וי"ל דשאני התם דאינו מוכח מלתא כלל שירצה ממנו העברת יי"נ דיש הרבה מלאכות אחרות משא"כ בתנור דודאי לא יעשה בו העכו"ם רק חמץ ה"ל כאלו השכירו בפי' לחמץ ותו ק"ל טובא דהא כ' בסי' רט"ו ס"ג. אבל לא מהני אם מתנה עם העכו"ם שתנוח הבהמה בשבת שאין העכו"ם נאמן על כך וכ"ש כאן שוודאי יאפה בו חמץ וצ"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is one who permits.</b> This is the view of the Tur, who brings proof from what we learned: if he hired him to do another labor, even though he said to him, 'Transfer yayin nesech for me from place to place,' it is permitted. One can answer that there it is different, for it is not at all evident that he wanted him to transfer yayin nesech, since there are many other labors. This is unlike an oven, where the non-Jew will certainly use it only for chametz, so it is as though he rented it explicitly for chametz. Furthermore, this is very difficult to me, for it is written in siman 215, seif 3, that it does not help if he stipulates with the non-Jew that the animal rest on Shabbos, because the non-Jew is not trusted about that. All the more so here, where he will certainly bake chametz in it. This requires analysis.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to rent, etc.</b> The reason in the Tur is that he wants the chametz to continue existing so that the utensil not crack. Furthermore, since the utensil itself is forbidden in benefit, it is forbidden to rent it ab initio. This is not comparable to what is taught in the Tosefta, that a person may rent his donkey to a non-Jew for chametz, because there the donkey is not forbidden, whereas here the utensil itself is forbidden. It seems that this follows the view of the Rashba nearby. But according to Rashi's view that we mentioned, that with a partnership oven it is permitted to rent it when he takes money, we should permit this as well; but it seems that the Tur's conclusion follows the Rashba. <b>After</b> the principles stated in this siman, it is fitting to examine the practice in Russia and Volhynia, where Jews rent orendas from the authorities and take measures of malt from everyone who grinds malt, and the malt is complete chametz, punishable by kareis. What should be done during the week of Pesach? <b>My father-in-law</b> of blessed memory wrote about this that it should be done by way of sale or gift to a non-Jew, as written in siman 441. In my humble opinion there is a difficulty with this. First, a sale does not apply to something that has not yet come into existence, as stated in the first chapter of Bava Metzia: if one says, 'what my trap will catch is sold to you,' he has said nothing. Furthermore, they permitted sale only at a time when it is permitted to eat, because sale is like eating in this respect, as the Mishnah says: whenever it is permitted to eat, it is permitted to sell. This refers to chametz that he has at the time of the sale. If so, with chametz that will come to him at a time when it is forbidden to eat, you have no proof that it is permitted to sell, since the sale does not take effect until the chametz exists, and then it is forbidden to eat. There is another difficulty: do we have only one Pesach in the year? There are more than fifty Pesachs in the year, namely all the Shabbosos, as Hillel the Elder said at the beginning of Perek Eilu Devarim regarding slaughtering the Pesach offering on Shabbos; and we have more than two hundred Pesachs that override Shabbos. We too should say so in this matter: how is it permitted to take Shabbos wages, meaning that what the mills grind on Shabbos, he takes a measure for it? Shabbos wages are obviously forbidden, as explained in several places. If so, you have left no allowance for all holders of orendas, and we have never heard that the Sages protested against them. In my humble opinion there is an allowance similar to what we mentioned in Yoreh Deah, siman 117, regarding raising pigs in orendas. So too here: everything belongs to the ruler, except that the Jew takes from him. This is similar to what was written regarding one who buys the priests' allotment, which we mentioned at the beginning of this siman; according to those views that hold it is permitted even without stipulating, here too it is permitted to take the allotment due to the authority, even though he stipulated with no person. And according to what we hold there, that a condition is required, it seems that they would agree here that it is permitted. There, with the priests, it is a known and fixed thing, that they give them one loaf each week; it follows that when he bought from them the allotment for the whole year, it was as though he bought from them fifty loaves, including that of Pesach. Likewise with the weekly interest mentioned above, a condition is therefore required. But with the mill there is no fixed amount for how much they will grind. From the beginning, when he bought it, he entered into uncertainty whether they would grind, for only then would he take automatically each time they grind and a measure is due from the grinder. It is a new acquisition, as though he buys it at that time from the authority, for the acquisition at the beginning did not take effect unless the purchase came into existence, since it was not clear at the time of acquisition. Therefore it is properly called a non-Jew's chametz over which Pesach passed when the Jew takes it after Pesach, and likewise after each Shabbos, for this was the intent from the beginning. The fine that Rabbi Shimon imposes because one transgressed bal yera'eh does not apply here, because it did not belong to the Jew during Pesach or during the Shabbosos. Do not say that on Pesach itself the Jew acquired the chametz after it came into existence. This is not so, for the Jew can say, 'I do not want to acquire it at that time,' as above in siman 448. Although we wrote in seif 4, in the case of the Rashba regarding the profit of the oven, that this is not comparable to siman 448, there it is different: the oven is his, and he willingly gives it to bake in on that day, so he certainly wants it. This is unlike a mill that belongs to the whole town, where the holder of the orenda cannot protest that the non-Jew not grind on that day. Therefore he can properly say that he does not want to acquire it at the time of prohibition, since it is possible for it to come after Pesach, for there are certainly times when the mill is free from grinding malt, and anyone who has grain to grind can grind even after Pesach. All this applies to the ruler's mill that the Jew rents from him. But a Jew who has a mill fully his own certainly has no basis to allow grinding in it on Shabbosos and Pesach; it is comparable to the case of the Rashba that we mentioned, since he can protest and do as he wants. He has no remedy except to sell it to a non-Jew for the entire year or half-year, so that Shabbos and Pesach are included by havla'ah among them. This is unlike the ruler's mill: although the Jew already gave him the money, nevertheless the sale does not take effect until it comes, since at the time of the sale there was uncertainty what would come afterward. So it seems to me, to derive a full allowance in this. However, one who wants to be stringent should stipulate on Pesach that anyone who wants to grind malt should give him a measure from other grain that contains no chametz; then, if he gives him malt after Pesach, it is an exchange for other grain and is permitted, as above.
