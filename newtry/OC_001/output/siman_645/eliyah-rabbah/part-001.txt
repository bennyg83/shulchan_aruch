[6] <b>Levush: In pressing circumstances, etc.</b> At the end of siman 649 it says that the custom is to recite a berachah over dry ones even when other moist ones are available. One must say that since he himself has no moist one, even though others have moist ones, it is considered a pressing circumstance, and he recites a berachah over his own. See what I wrote there in the name of Masas Binyamin.

**** ENGLISH ****
[7] <b>Levush: And some validate, etc.</b> So ruled the Rema based on the Minhagim. In Eliyahu Zuta I asked about this, for the Mordechai said this specifically about a myrtle, as the Bayis Chadash wrote, and so wrote the Magen Avraham. However, the Turei Zahav wrote that the Rema ruled well, because he holds that a cut-off top is comparable to a dry one, which is valid, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of the Geonim; see there at length. This is not clear, for the Maggid himself concludes that this applies specifically to dry, but not to cut off, and this is also the view of the Rambam and the Tur at the end of siman 649. The Tur disagreed only by validating dry even with the other species, but regarding cut off he concedes to the Rambam. From this there is a major difficulty with the Turei Zahav there, and I have been brief. Moreover, the Turei Zahav himself at the end of siman 649 ruled that one may permit only dry. It appears to me that even the Rema validates only where the upper middle leaf was cut off, because many poskim validate it, and therefore he relied on them when no other is available. But if most of the leaves were cut off, he agrees that pressing circumstances do not help. So I explained the view of the Minhagim in Eliyahu Zuta.
