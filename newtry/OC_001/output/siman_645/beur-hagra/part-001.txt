<b>And the lulav still remains, etc.</b> For otherwise, even if only a minority did so, it is invalid, as written regarding palms of Har HaBarzel: this was taught only, etc. Anything not covered is a mere stick. 'And the lulav still, etc.' means the required measure of the lulav. See the Rashba and Ran, who wrote that likewise with a myrtle, we require its entire required measure to be avos, and if some of its leaves fell it is invalid, for its name is no longer upon it and it is not called avos. They wrote that one should not learn these laws from willow.

<b>ס"ג בריית כו' נחלקה כו'. </b>כן פי' הרי"ף והרמב"ם מ"ש נחלקה התיומת וברוב העלין כמ"ש הרא"ש והג"ה והירושלמי שם מסייע להם ר' מלוך בשם ריב"ל נחלק המתאים כמו שנפרצו העלין אלמא דבכל העלין מיירי וברוב כמו נפרצו וז"ש בה"ג והביאו הרא"ש בעי ר"פ נחלקו התיומת ההוא גבא דהוצא כו' ההוא דמתאים כו' אי כנפרצו עליו דמי או כו' וכ"כ הרי"ף ה"ה כאילו נפרצו העלין ופסול והוא לשון הירושלמי וכן מ' בגמ' מדקבעינהו אחר נפרצו ונפרדו וכמו נפרצו ברוב ה"ה נחלקו שפיסולן משום נפרצו כנ"ל וכמ"ש הרשב"א שכל הפסולין בעלין הוא ברובן דדייני' כאילו נפרצו וכמ"ש בערבה נפרצה כו' ושנשרו מקצת עליה כשרה וה"ה לכל הד' מינים שכולן שוו בפסולן אלא שמוסיף בכ"א שאין לו ענין בחבירו כגון באתרוג חזזית וכן בכולם ונקיט נשירת מקצת העלין בערבה לפי שהוא שכיח בה יותר וכן כמושה דתני בה ובברייתא תני בכל הד' מינים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 3: The formation, etc.; if it split, etc.</b> So the Rif and Rambam explain what is meant by 'the tiyomes split,' and that it is in the majority of the leaves, as the Rosh and the gloss wrote. The Yerushalmi there supports them: Rabbi Maloch said in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, if the matched leaves split, it is like the leaves being split apart. This shows that it speaks of all the leaves, and of a majority, like 'split apart.' This is what Baal Halachos Gedolos wrote, brought by the Rosh: Rav Papa asked, if the tiyomes split, that back of the leaf, etc., that matched part, etc., is it like its leaves were split apart, or etc. So too the Rif wrote that it is as if the leaves were split apart, and it is invalid. This is the language of the Yerushalmi. It also appears so from the Gemara, since it placed this after split apart and separated; just as split apart is in the majority, so too split tiyomes, for its invalidation is because of split leaves, as it appears to me. This accords with the Rashba, that all invalidations in leaves are by their majority, for we judge them as if split apart, as written regarding willow: if it was split, etc., but if some of its leaves fell it is valid. The same applies to all four species, for they are all equal in their invalidations, except that each has an added defect that does not apply to the other, such as chazazis on an esrog, and similarly all of them. The Mishnah mentions some leaves falling regarding willow because it is more common there, and likewise withered, which it teaches there; but the baraisa teaches these laws about all four species.

<b>וי"מ לומר כו'. </b>הוא דעת רש"י שהביאו הגהת סמ"ק ואינו ברש"י שלפנינו וז"ל סמ"ק שם נחלקה התיומת כו' פירש"י שתי עלין העליונים ששם השדרה כלה וכ' בהג"ה שם האמצעיות אע"פ שכל עלה ועלה שלם בעצמו כמו שאר עלי הלולב שכאו"א כפולה לשתים כו' לפיכך לולבין המצויין בינינו רובן פסולים שרובם אין שתי עלין האמצעיות מחוברין יחד והוא פירש"' שלפנינו וכ' ועוד פי' הקו' פי' אחר נחלקה התיומת דהיינו עלה האמצעית שדרכו להיות כפול כשאר עלין נחלק לשתים לפ"ז לולבין המצויין בינינו רובן כשרים כיון שעלה האמצעי שלם ומיהו יש מקצתן שעלה האמצעי נחלק בראשו ומ"מ יש להכשירן לפי' פי' הקו' דפי' דוקא כשנחלקה עד למטה מן העלין כדפי' בתוס' ר' יהודה עכ"ל והוא כמ"ש ברש"י שלפנינו על פי' הראשון נחלקו זה מזה כו' שלמטה מהם וע"ש ד"ה נחלקה כו'. וכ' בת"ה שאלה נחלקה התיומת רבו בו פתרונים האיך אנו נוהגים בו. תשובה דיש לנהוג כפי' אחר שברש"י כמ"ש בהגהת סמ"ק דעלה האמצעית כו' עד למטה מן העלין ובא"ז הוסיף להקל דבעי נחלק גם השדרה עד העלין שלמטה הימנה וכ' בא"ז דעל פרש"י אני סומך הלכה למעשה. וכ"ה ברש"י שלנו כפי' הראשון ונסדקה השדרה עד כו' ובהג"ה כאן לא העתיק אלא כמ"ש בהגהת סמ"ק:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some explain, etc.</b> This is Rashi's view, brought in the glosses to Semak, though it is not in the Rashi text before us. This is the language of Semak there: 'the tiyomes split, etc.' Rashi explained: the two upper leaves, where the spine ends. The gloss there wrote: the middle leaves, even though each leaf is whole by itself like the other leaves of the lulav, each of which is doubled into two, etc. Therefore most lulavim found among us are invalid, for most do not have the two middle leaves joined together. That is the explanation before us. He further wrote: the early authorities explained another explanation of 'the tiyomes split,' namely that the middle leaf, which is normally doubled like the other leaves, split into two. According to this, most lulavim found among us are valid, since the middle leaf is whole. However, some have the middle leaf split at its top, and nevertheless they should be validated according to the explanation of the early authorities, who explain that it is only when it split down below the leaves, as explained in Tosafos Rabbi Yehudah. These are his words. This is as Rashi before us wrote on the first explanation, that they split from each other, etc., and below them; see there, s.v. 'split.' Terumas HaDeshen wrote in a responsum: the matter of a split tiyomes has many interpretations; how should we practice? Answer: one should practice according to the other explanation in Rashi, as written in the glosses to Semak, that the middle leaf, etc., split down below the leaves. The Or Zarua added to be lenient, requiring even the spine to split down to the leaves below it. The Or Zarua wrote: I rely on Rashi's explanation for practical halachah. So it is in our Rashi, according to the first explanation, that the spine split until, etc. In the gloss here, the Rema copied only what is written in the glosses to Semak.

<b>מיהו כו'. כי יש כו'. </b>הוא הרשב"א ור"ן ומ"מ שפי' ג"כ כל' הב' שברש"י והקשה הרשב"א והא אמרי' דוקא דעביד כי המניק וי"ל דהתם שנסדק מיעוטו דהיינו בראשו וכאן דנחלק רובה ועוד י"ל דאפי' מיעוטה פסול דומיא דנקטם וכי אמרי' שם בשנסדק לרחבו דשם כשר במיעוטו עד דעביד כי המניק ולפ"ז ראוי לחוש לארכו אפי' במיעוטו וטעם לפי שמתרבה הסדק בניענועו וכן חוששין בעלי הנפש וכ"כ הר"ן ולעיקר דינא תופסין דוקא שנחלקה רובו וכמ"ש הר"ן על תי' הב' ואין זה במשמע אלא שראוי לחוש ולהחמיר ומ"מ כ' כתי' הראשון ויש מי שפי' נחלקה התיומת העלה האמצעי עליון שבכולם כו' ואין פסול אלא ברוב עלה ולא במיעוטו אלא שהרשב"א והר"ן ומ"מ תופסי' לדינא כפי' הגאונים וש"ע בכל העלין כנ"ל ולכן השמיט בהג"ה דעה זו ברוב עלה לפי שלדעתם אף שתופסים לפירש"י ברוב מ"מ אין סוברין כמותו ולדעת סמ"ק וא"ז דוקא שנחלק כולו אלא שכ' דעת המחמירין לחומרא בעלמא אבל לדינא יש להחמיר כפי' הרשב"א דברובו דבכל פסול רובו ככולו והוא פשוט לדעת הרשב"א ור"ן ומ"מ ועל מקצתו אין להחמיר כלל כמ"ש הר"ן שאין זה במשמע' שפי' סדוק כהימנק על רחבו דאין דרכו ליעבד כהמניק לרחבו וכמ"ש המ"מ כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>However, etc.; because there are, etc.</b> This refers to the Rashba, the Ran, and the Maggid Mishneh, who also explained according to the second language in Rashi. The Rashba asked: but we say it is invalid only when it is made like a hemnek. One can answer that there it split only a minority, namely at its top, while here most of it split. Another answer is that even a minority is invalid, similar to where its top was cut off, and when they said there that if it split it is valid unless it is made like a hemnek, that refers to where it split across its width, where it is valid if only a minority split until it is made like a hemnek. According to this, one should be concerned for a lengthwise split even in a minority, because the split widens through shaking. Men of piety are concerned for this, and so wrote the Ran. As the principal law, however, they hold that it is only when most of it split, as the Ran wrote regarding the second answer; and this is not the plain implication, only that it is proper to be concerned and stringent. Nevertheless he also wrote like the first answer. There is one who explained that 'the tiyomes split' means the middle leaf, the uppermost of all, etc., and it is invalid only when most of the leaf split, not a minority. But the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh hold in practice like the Geonim and the Shulchan Aruch, that it concerns all the leaves, as it appears to me. Therefore the gloss omitted this opinion regarding most of the leaf, because in their view, although they accept Rashi's explanation regarding the majority, they do not agree with his whole approach. According to Semak and Or Zarua, it is invalid only when it split entirely; the Rema mentioned the view of those who are stringent only as a stringency. But as a matter of law, one should be stringent according to the Rashba's explanation, that most is required, for with every invalidation a majority is like the whole. This is obvious according to the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh. Regarding a small minority, one should not be stringent at all, as the Ran wrote that this is not implied; he explains 'split like a hemnek' as across its width, for it is not usual to become like a hemnek across its width, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote. This appears to me.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 5: A lulav that dried, etc.</b> So wrote the Rashba: dry means that most of its leaves dried; even if its spine is moist, it is not hadar. The same applies in reverse. Some wrote the opposite, and some wrote that both must have dried. The first view appears correct. It is not common that the spine is dry while the leaves are moist. So wrote the Ran as well. The second 'some explain' is the view of the Ra'avad brought by the Rosh, according to the reading in the Geonic text: what is the law if the leaves dried, etc. It appears that the question there was resolved leniently. His view is that all invalidations, such as its top was cut off, are in the body of the lulav, meaning in its spine, just as with a myrtle. The Rashba, Rosh, and Ran disagreed with him from what is said there, 32b: it said, etc., and a lulav four, etc., the spine of a lulav, etc. This proves that the body of the lulav is not its spine and is not comparable to myrtle. One must read there in the Yerushalmi: its blossom dried, etc., meaning the upper leaf, which is the upper tiyomes according to Rashi; and what difference is there between this and cut off? See se'if 6. The Rosh wrote: since if most of its leaves were split apart it is invalid, all the more so if most of its leaves dried. It appears that the primary dry case of the Mishnah refers to the spine. This is what the Tur wrote: a lulav whose spine dried or, etc., and here in the gloss. But the Shulchan Aruch takes the Rashba's explanation, that all the invalidations in the Mishnah concern the leaves, and that is called the lulav, as it appears to me. Also, since a dry spine without dry leaves is uncommon, he omitted it.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 6: Its top was cut off, meaning, etc.; and if, etc.</b> Tosafos and the Rosh wrote that at first glance this seems to refer to the two upper leaves. They asked: if so, why was it necessary to say that if the tiyomes was removed it is invalid? They then explained that it refers to the majority of the upper leaves. They wrote two further answers to uphold the first explanation: one can say that cut off is worse, because it is not hadar; and further, cut off requires specifically that both leaves be cut. The Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh wrote that it refers to the upper middle leaf that was cut. The Beis Yosef wrote that perhaps they do not disagree: Tosafos and the Rosh speak of lulavim that end in two leaves, while some lulavim end in three leaves, and the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh speak of the middle one among them. Since the Beis Yosef viewed the latter answers of Tosafos and the Rosh as forced, namely that cut off should be worse than removed, and likewise the second answer that it is only if both were cut, for that is not so, since some are invalidated by one leaf as mentioned above, he therefore wrote in the Shulchan Aruch according to the first answer. But the Rosh wrote that the first answer is forced. The Rashba and the Ran also wrote both explanations and agreed to this explanation, for they wrote that since it does not say 'its leaves were cut off,' as it says 'were split apart,' but says 'its top,' it implies the top of the lulav, the upper leaf. The Maggid Mishneh mentioned only this explanation, writing that there are many interpretations, and the correct one is that the top of the upper leaf, where the lulav ends, was cut off. They wrote that a cut of the smallest amount invalidates it. Some say this is only when most of the upper leaf was cut, as with other invalidations, but they agreed that when it is cut, even the smallest amount invalidates, as with the nose of an esrog. See what I wrote in se'if 9.
