<small>ט"ז סק"א</small> בגמרא מספקא ליה לר' אבהו כך וקשה מהי תיתי לומר כן דהא במתניתין תנן שיעור תרועה כג' יבבות וברייתא אמרינן כג' שברים דהיינו גניחות שהם ארוכים מיבבות ורמינן להו אהדדי כצ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Taz s.k. 1</small>. In the Gemara, Rabbi Avahu was uncertain about this. This is difficult: from where would one derive to say so? In the Mishnah we learned that the measure of a teruah is like three yevavos, while the baraisa says it is like three shevarim, meaning groans, which are longer than yevavos; and we set them against one another. This is how the text should read.

<small>End of s.k. 2</small>. This is the view of Hagahos Ashri. Hagahos Ashri does not accept the reasoning of the Beis Yosef that I brought above. This is what the Rema wrote, 'provided that he does not extend too much,' meaning that in tashat he should not extend as long as three groans, and in tashrat he should not extend as long as three groans and three yevavos; and this is easy to understand.

<small>שם</small> אבל יותר מט' הוי. לאו דוקא אלא צריך לעשות כחות מעט מן ט' טרומיטין ויותר נראה דהט"ז לא כ' זה דרך פי' אהמחבר אלא כ' כן להלכה דס"ל כהג"א כמ"ש הט"ז אחר כך ממילא מותר להאריך ט' טרומיטין דהא כל גניחה הוא משהו יותר מיללה שהוא ג' טרומיטין א"כ שברים יש בו ט' טרומיטין וג' משהו וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There</small>, but more than nine it is. This is not precise; rather, one must make the forces slightly less than nine tromitin. It seems more that the Taz did not write this as an explanation of the Mechaber, but wrote it as practical halacha, for he holds like Hagahos Ashri, as the Taz wrote afterward. Consequently, it is permitted to extend nine tromitin, for each groan is a bit more than a yevavah, which is three tromitin; if so, shevarim contain nine tromitin and three slight additions. This is easy to understand.
