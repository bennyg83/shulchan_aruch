**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 2) More, etc.</b> And if so, the tekiah too must be like both of them together, etc. I am compelled to present the sugya of the Talmud briefly, together with the three views needed for the Shulchan Aruch; the other views that are not needed here I will omit. We learned in the Mishnah, folio 33b: the measure of a tekiah is like three teruos; the measure of a teruah is like three yevavos. In a baraisa it was taught: the measure of a tekiah is like a teruah; the measure of a teruah is like three shevarim. The Gemara first challenges the first clause of the Mishnah, which teaches that the measure of a tekiah is like three teruos, while the baraisa teaches that it is like one teruah. Abaye answers: the tanna of the Mishnah counts the tekios of all the units. Rashi explains that this is what it means: the measure of the three tekios, meaning those before the teruos or after the teruos, is like the three teruos; if so, the measure of one tekiah is like the measure of one teruah. But the tanna of the baraisa counts the tekiah of one unit.

Then the Gemara asks from the end of the Mishnah, which teaches that the measure of a teruah is like three yevavos, which are short sounds and are the teruah, whereas the baraisa teaches that it is like three shevarim, which are longer. **** ENGLISH ****
If so, each shever must be the measure of three forces. According to Rashi, one must extend the tekiah of tashrat the measure of shevarim and teruah, because we do it out of uncertainty, perhaps the teruah of the verse is both of them together, and they had a tradition that the measure of a tekiah is like the measure of a teruah. If so, one must extend the tekiah the measure of both of them. However, in any event, one need not extend the tekiah more than nine forces, because according to Rashi a teruah is three forces, and one shever is necessarily not as long as three forces, as above. If you will say that the length of one shever is like two forces, then three shevarim are six forces, and three yevavos of the teruah are three forces, which together are nine forces; one need only extend the tekiah of tashrat the measure of nine forces. But according to Riva and Rivam, where the measure of a teruah is nine forces and three shevarim are likewise nine forces, one must extend the tekiah of tashrat the measure of eighteen forces. The Raavad is a third view. He holds like Rashi, that the measure of three yevavos is three minimal forces, but he holds that after Abaye concludes at the end that in this they certainly disagree, namely that the tanna of the Mishnah holds teruah means wailing and the baraisa holds groaning, he retracted from the first answer and holds that the tanna of the Mishnah also counts the tekiah of one unit. As for the Mishnah teaching that the measure of a tekiah is like three teruos, while the baraisa teaches that it is like one teruah, each follows its own view. The Raavad holds that there was no tradition that the measure of a tekiah is like a teruah, but rather that the measure of a tekiah is nine forces. If so, the tanna of the Mishnah, who holds that the measure of a teruah is like three yevavos, meaning three forces, teaches that since a tekiah must be nine forces, it is like three teruos, which are nine forces. But the tanna of the baraisa holds that a teruah is three shevarim, each shever being three forces, unlike Rashi, and three shevarim are nine forces; therefore he said that the measure of a tekiah is like one teruah, meaning nine forces. If so, regarding the measure of a tekiah the Mishnah does not disagree with the baraisa, and according to both of them the measure of a tekiah is nine forces. According to this, in tashrat as well one need not extend the tekiah more than nine forces. Correspondingly, in tarat too one must extend the tekiah the measure of nine forces. The first opinion brought in the Shulchan Aruch is Rashi's view. The view of the 'some say' is Riva and Rivam, and the view of the Raavad is not brought in the Shulchan Aruch.

**** ENGLISH ****
If so, even if he extended somewhat in the shevarim, it is nothing so long as he did not extend to the measure of eighteen forces, for it does not become a tekiah, since in tashrat the measure of a tekiah is eighteen forces. This is the view of Hagahos Ashri that the Rema brought. But the view of the Beis Yosef, which is the view of Tosafos and the Rosh, is that even in tashrat one should not extend more than the measure of the tekiah of tarat. Although regarding its own set it is not like a tekiah, nevertheless it leaves the category of shever; so wrote the Beis Yosef. The Bach wrote the reason: Hagahos Ashri and the Rema hold like the Rambam and Semag, that what we do - tashrat, tashat, and tarat - is because of a doubt, for this is what Rabbi Avahu instituted, as we say there. We say that Rabbi Avahu's reason was that he was uncertain, meaning a genuine uncertainty. Therefore, in the tashrat set, one does not fulfill at all if he made tarat, and the name tekiah is not upon it unless he extended it like eighteen forces. Therefore it is permitted to extend in the shevarim up to eighteen forces, as Magen Avraham wrote. But the view of Tosafos and the Rosh is like Rav Hai Gaon, that the Talmud's statement that he was uncertain is not precise. Rather, in truth, whether wailing, groaning, or both groaning and wailing are done, one fulfills the obligation of teruah, for all are called teruah. But in the days of Rabbi Avahu there were differing customs among Yisrael: some made the teruah as wailing, some as groaning, and some as both groaning and wailing. To ordinary people this looked like two Torahs; see Ramban in Milchamos. Therefore Rabbi Avahu gathered all the customs together, and through this there would be one Torah for all Yisrael, with no difference among them.
