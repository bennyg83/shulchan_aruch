[5] <b>[Levush] So that it be distanced, etc., and go out, etc.</b> Meaning: that it remain so; but if he draws the oil a bit away from the wick so that they can grasp the wick and draw it out a bit more than it was at first outside the oil, and he again tilts the oil toward it, it is permitted so that it burn well (Taz). And it seems he did not see Maharil, who wrote that the synagogue sexton cannot fix the synagogue candlesticks while they are burning, for by his touching, oil will diminish from them; specifically after they have gone out he should again fix and light them; until here. It is permitted to walk with a burning oil lamp in his hand even if he moves it about and perhaps he will draw oil from the wick, for it is not a psik reisha; however, to tilt the lamp to one side to draw [oil] is a psik reisha and forbidden (Maharil there). And according to this it would be possible to permit through cutheans, for a psik reisha is permitted with him, as written at the end of siman 253 and siman 277, seif 3; and see siman 276, s.k., and siman 680.

[7] <b>[Levush] Rather he does outside, etc.</b> His words are not precise in this, for at the beginning of seif 3 he wrote Tosafos' reason on Beitzah 22 that he immediately extinguishes; and Rosh challenged them from a tube at the beginning of siman 265 that constantly drips to the lamp's light: why is it extinguishing when taking a little? Therefore he wrote another reason, namely specifically when he does outside, etc. And if so, he should not have written in the gloss what he wrote in the body, or he should have written them as a dispute. And behold, Bach at the beginning of siman 265 challenged why it is forbidden with a tube and answered like Tosafos' reason mentioned above that he immediately extinguishes, etc.; and I wonder: behold Rosh challenged Tosafos' reason from a tube. And perhaps he holds like what Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 21, wrote to answer Rosh's difficulty: we are concerned with a tube that he take all the oil so that the drip by drip cease, and that is like extinguishing; or like Taz's answer that we decree: when he takes from a tube he will also take from the oil in the lamp itself. And in Olas Shabbos he wrote: I do not understand Maharshal's words: what of it if he takes everything? Behold the oil in the tube is not attached to the oil in the lamp, and when he takes the tube he does not extinguish the light; until here. And in my humble opinion Maharshal's words are plain: since it constantly drips drop by drop, the lamp constantly burns with the drip, and so long as he does not take everything it still drips and the light remains clear as when he does not take; but when he takes it all, the drip stops and he extinguishes the light. But I have a difficulty on Maharshal's answer from Pesachim 11, where they challenge R' Yehudah who permits with a tube from R' Yehudah who decreed lest he eat from chametz when checking during Pesach: perhaps a tube is different, for they did not decree so far that he eat it all, which is not so with chametz, where even a part is forbidden; and according to Taz's answer mentioned above it is obviously difficult; and examine well.

[8] <b>In another lamp, etc.</b> And the same law applies to this lamp itself. Taz challenged what is written in siman 502, seif 2, that it is permitted to take wood from a bonfire, etc.; and it seems that whoever ruled there leniently, here too with a wick, requires further study regarding Rama and Maharshal, who ruled for both of them, one lenient and one stringent; until here. And in truth Tosafos on folio 22 wrote only not to take a wick and place it on the ground, implying that for another lamp it is permitted; and therefore I wonder at Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 29, who wrote as follows: Tosafos and Rosh after them wrote that it is forbidden to take, etc., and place it in another lamp; until here: for that is not so, for only Rosh wrote thus, but from Tosafos the opposite is implied. Later I saw that Rabbeinu Yeruham, folio 29, also wrote likewise in the name of Tosafos, and perhaps before him there was a different Tosafos. And regarding Taz's wonder at Rama and Maharshal, I learned a proper reconciliation from what Maharil wrote as follows: it is forbidden to take a wick from this lamp and place it in its fellow, for since he diminishes a drop of oil from it he is liable because of extinguishing; until here. And it appears his meaning is that in taking the wick it is impossible that a drop not drip to waste or that [oil] stick to his hands, therefore it is forbidden; and if so, since with a bonfire this does not apply, therefore they permitted. And what Olas Shabbos wrote is forced.

**** ENGLISH ****
[9] <b>To add wicks, etc.</b> And likewise it is permitted to fold a wax candle so that it burn quickly; see seif 9 (Magen Avraham). And in truth this is included in "some permit to cut a wax candle, etc." that Rama and Levush wrote in seif 3. And behold Maharshal's view there: according to Rosh's reason above, it is forbidden to cut even by fire; and so Bach wrote; and so it implies in Magen Avraham that I cited. But Taz's view in s.k. 7 is that Rosh agrees in this that it is permitted, for it is like increasing wicks so that it kindle quickly; see there where he elaborated; and likewise the implication of Levush in seif 3, who wrote that this is not mere cause, etc.; and Rama wrote that such is the custom. Taz wrote: it is permitted to tilt a wax or tallow candle a bit downward so that the wax melt and return it upward, and so he does many times so that the whole candle burn quickly, for through this tilting he kindles more and it resembles increasing wicks; until here. And my mind is not settled with this permission, since drops of wax or tallow go from it to waste; and increasing wicks is different, for everything is burned; and see the s.k. after this. And further, see this that I found in Sefer Avodat HaKodesh, folio 43: to increase wicks, if to increase light it is permitted, and if to hasten its extinguishing it is forbidden, for this causes extinguishing not for the need of ochel nefesh; until here. And so it implies in Sefer HaManhig, folio 51. And according to this, the Ran whom Beis Yosef brought also deals with increasing light; and so Ran's words are proven clearly to one who examines, for he brought a proof from meat on coals, and there he does not intend. And Rama, Levush, and the Acharonim who wrote in Ran's name that even so that it go out is permitted did not carefully examine the source of the law. And now it is automatically clear that it is forbidden according to Rosh's reason; whereas according to Tosafos it is permitted, since he does not extinguish immediately. Therefore it appears to me to forbid cutting by fire and increasing wicks ideally. And further, behold Hagahos Maimoni, Mordechai, Hagahos Ashiri, and Or Zaru'a forbid.
