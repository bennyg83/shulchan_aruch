<b>שאין לה ד' כנפות. </b> דתניא על ד' כנפות כסותך ד' ולא ג' או אינו אלא ד' ולא ה' כשהוא אומר אשר תכסה בה לרבות בעלת ה' ומה ראית מרבה אני בעלת ה' שיש בכלל ה' ד' ומוציא אני בעלת ג' שאין בכלל ג' ד'. וקשה דלמא כי מרבה בעלת ה' מרבה נמי לה' ציצית. וע"ק ל"ל אשר תכסה ת"ל מהא שיש בכלל ה' ד' ונראה דחדא מיתרצ' באידך דלהכי כתב רחמנא אשר תכסה  דל"ת כיון שאין לעשות יותר מד' ציצית ה"נ אין לעשות יותר מד' כנפות דציצית וכנפות שוין קמ"ל אשר תכס' ליתן רבוי לבגד יותר ממה שיש לציצית א"כ ש"מ דציצית פסולי' יותר מד' כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“That it does not have four corners.”</b> It was taught: “on your four-cornered garment” — four and not three; or only four and not five? When it says “with which you cover yourself” — to include a five-cornered [garment]; “what did you see to include a five-cornered? I include a five-cornered because five includes four; I exclude a three-cornered because three does not include four.” One could ask: perhaps when you include five-cornered you include five <i>tzitzis</i> — and why “with which you cover yourself”? It teaches from this that five includes four — one resolves the other: therefore Scripture wrote “with which you cover yourself” — do not [make more than four <i>tzitzis</i>] — since one may not make more than four <i>tzitzis</i>, so too one may not make more than four corners — <i>tzitzis</i> and corners are equal — “with which you cover” teaches extension for the garment beyond what <i>tzitzis</i> has — hence invalid <i>tzitzis</i> beyond four — as above.

<b>כמו שהיא כפולה. </b> בברייתא אמרינן טלית שהיא כפולה חייב' בציצית ור"ש פוטר ושוין שאם תפרה שחייבת וק"ל כר"ש ופירש"י טעם דר"ש שאם תפשיט נמצאו ציצית באמצעיתא ובנ"י פי' דהברייתא מיירי בבגד ארוך מאוד ואין ראוי ללבשו אא"כ כופלו ור"ש פוטר דה"ל כבגד שאין ראוי ללובשה ונ"ל דלפ"ז אפי' בארבע כנפות הפשוטים נמי פטור כיון שאין ראוי ללבישה אין עליו שם בגד. ולפ"ז יש לתמוה על הרב"י וכאן בש"ע דכתב דעושה בד' כנפיה הפשוטין אלא דנ"ל דיש חילוק בדבר דהנ"י מיירי שכופל הטלית ע"פ ההכרח שהוא ארוך מאוד ומ"ה אין שם בגד עליה ופטור אבל ל רש"י דהטעם משום דיהיו הציצית באמצע מיירי שכופלו מרצון אבל עכ"פ ראוי להשתמש אפי' בלא כפל נמצא דשם בגד עלי' ע"כ חייב להטיל עכ"פ בפשוטים כנ"ל לקיים ב' הפרושים אליבא דהלכתא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“As it is folded.”</b> In a <i>beraisa</i> we say a folded <i>tallis</i> is obligated in <i>tzitzis</i> and R. Shimon exempts — they agree if she sewed it it is obligated — one could ask [we rule] like R. Shimon; Rashi’s reason for R. Shimon: if she unfolds the <i>tzitzis</i> are in the middle; Beit Yosef explained the <i>beraisa</i> speaks of a very long garment unfit to wear unless folded — R. Shimon exempts — it is like a garment unfit for wearing — it appears to me therefore even on four plain corners exempt if unfit for wearing there is no “garment” name. Accordingly one could wonder on Rabbeinu Yerucham here in Shulchan Aruch who wrote one ties on four plain corners — it appears to me there is a distinction: Beit Yosei speaks of folding the <i>tallis</i> by necessity being very long — therefore no garment upon it — exempt; but for Rashi whose reason is <i>tzitzis</i> would be in the middle — speaks of folding willingly — still fit for use even unfolded — therefore garment name applies — obligated to tie at least on plain [corners] — as above — to satisfy both explanations according to halacha.

ראוי לפסוק הלכה כמותו וכמו שהכריע ב"י וש"ע אלא דאין לנו אלא דברי רמ"א שהכריע כרש"י והרא"ש והטור וכפי מ"ש בפירוש דבריהם:

<b>“He tied <i>tzitzis</i> on <i>tzitzis</i>.”</b> To clarify their dispute I abbreviate. Rava said: one who tied on a three-cornered [garment] and completed it to four — invalid because of “you shall make” and not “from what is already made.” They object: do we say “you shall make” and not “from what is already made”? Aruch Zuta: tying on [strings] already tied is valid — meaning she already had valid <i>tzitzis</i> on the latter [corners] — even though afterward we remove the first — even though not for need at the time of doing they validate — Rava said: now he violates “do not add” — there is no [valid] act. Rashi: whenever not for need we call it “from what is already made” — here therefore the latter qualify because he violates “do not add” when doing them — no [mitzvah] act — since transgressing a Torah law it is not called an act — hence when he cuts the first the latter’s tying is the act; but “not for need” on three-cornered where he does not transgress on them — it is an act — called <i>tzitzis</i> made invalid — there is “you shall make” when completing. R. Pinchas objected: perhaps he intends to add — perhaps to annul — and “do not add” — no act? Rashi’s other explanation: main act exists — difficulty returns. Beit Yosef cited Rosh two wordings in Rashi’s name — Mahari Ashkenazi’s explanation — Beit Yosef: no disagreement between them — it appears to me the difference: first wording validates latter only if first cut off; second validates latter even if first still exist since annulled — therefore second wording did not say valid in latter if cut first like first wording — rather plainly valid in latter — Beit Yosef wrote later implies from Rashi — clear — see there — nevertheless Tur and Rivash ruled first wording — specifically if cuts first valid in latter — Rema here before cutting invalid in every way — first wording we cited — Rosh ruled like Rava: we do not care if intent add or annul — in every case valid in latter after cutting first — cutting is main mitzvah performance — even if intent annul — since in that act enough to obligate “do not add” — until he cuts first — improper to fulfill thus — no act yet until after cutting — Beit Yosef concludes: specifically cutting first valid in latter — not reverse — reason: first annulled by making latter — how return to validity by cutting latter — this is not invalid that returns to validity — this is view Rema cites “some validate”; Rambam — view Shulchan Aruch prefers — specifically intent annul first unlike Rashi we cited — it appears to me he explains sugya Rava truly argues R. Zeira who permitted tying on tied. From this they challenged Rava who permitted tying on tied — Rava said R. Zeira’s words cannot stand — he violates “do not add” on adding to first — how no act in that addition — each addition separately — when ties fourth of latter certainly “you shall make” and not “from what is already made” — since “do not add” prohibition it is invalid act — R. Pinchas came to strengthen R. Zeira: surely he spoke well — valid tying on tied — this man presumably not wicked to intend add — surely intends annul — as soon as he began latter he intended annul first — as if they are not — hence valid in latter after cutting first — no “from what is already made” invalid — act is to validate — unlike tying on three-cornered Rava invalidates — there act in invalid no repair relation — here repair in annulment — hence properly invalid tying on three-cornered like Rava’s dictum — here tying on tied valid since repair in annulment — halacha like R. Pinchas — Gemara conclusion — so Rambam — not Beit Yosef’s other explanation of Rambam that R. Pinchas does not argue Rava — strained. Our explanation is very broad — fit to rule halacha like it as Beit Yoseh and Shulchan Aruch decided — but we have only Rema who decided like Rashi, Rosh, and Tur as explained in their words.

<b>ב' לפניהם כו'. </b> בד"מ הביא ע"ז מדרש בפ' והמים להם חומה שהכריז גבריאל למים שאחוריהם הזהרו באותם שמשליכים כנף של ציצית לאחוריהם כו' ונ"ל ראיה מזה שיש להלביש הטלית באופן שישליכו הכנף עם ב' ציצית לאחוריהם ואפשר שמזה נמשך מאיזה  אנשים שמקפילים חלק א' מן הטלית מאד עד שאחר הקיפול הוא דק מאוד ואינו מכסה כלל אל הצואר וכוונתם שיהיו ב' ציצית לאחוריהם. ונ"ל שאין זה מן המובחר דא"כ אין כאן כיסוי גוף אלא כיסוי הראש ויותר טוב כמנהג התוגרמים שמעטפים הטלית כמו שהוא סביב הראש והגוף והיד מפסקת בחצי הטלית מזה ומזה באופן שמב' הצדדים יהיה א' מהציצית לפניו וחלק לאחריו אח"ז מ"כ כן בתו' סוף ערכין וז"ל שם ומצאתי בסילוק שעשה הר"י טעם שנותן ציצית אחד מלפניו ואחד לאחריו מימין וכן לשמאל עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>“Two before him,” etc.</b> Darkhei Moshe cited on this a midrash “The waters were a wall” — Gabriel proclaimed to the waters behind “beware those who throw a <i>tzitzis</i> corner behind them,” etc. It appears to me proof from this one should wear the <i>tallis</i> so one throws the corner with two <i>tzitzis</i> behind — perhaps from this some people fold part of the <i>tallis</i> greatly until after folding it is very thin and does not cover to the neck — their intent two <i>tzitzis</i> behind. It appears to me this is not best — then there is no body covering only head covering — better like Turkish custom wrapping the <i>tallis</i> as is around head and body — hand divides middle of <i>tallis</i> this side and that — so from both sides one <i>tzitzis</i> before him and part behind — likewise Tosafos end <i>Arakhin</i>: “I found in a <i>seder</i> R. Yosei made reason he places one <i>tzitzis</i> before and one behind, right and left.”
