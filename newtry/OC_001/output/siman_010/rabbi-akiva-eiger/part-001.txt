**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5: 'For we follow the majority.'</b> I was uncertain about a garment containing a minority of leather when the entire garment is only the minimum size capable of covering the head and majority of a small child. Do we follow the majority, so that since the garment as a whole has the required size and most of its material is obligated, the whole is treated as obligated material? Or do we say that since the wool alone lacks the minimum obligated size, the garment is exempt from tzitzis? I asked this in the presence of several sages; some answered one way and some the other. At first glance this law can be learned from Magen Avraham above, siman 9, se'if katan 4, concerning a tallis whose warp is wool and whose woof is silk. According to Maharam of Rothenburg it has no remedy. His intent follows Maharam's view, cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot, that wool does not exempt silk; only silk threads do, as Magen Avraham cites in se'if katan 3. The tallis therefore has no remedy: silk tzitzis do not exempt its wool portion. At first glance, according to the poskim who hold that silk is exempt by Torah law, we should say that such a tallis is likewise exempt by Torah law because neither the woof nor the warp alone constitutes an obligated garment. If so, silk tzitzis should exempt the silk portion; and since the wool portion alone is not obligated in tzitzis, it too should be exempt. What difference is there whether the silk is not an obligated material or its obligation has been discharged? We must conclude that since it is a complete garment, the entire garment is treated as obligated. The same should apply here. Nevertheless, an obvious distinction can be made. Examine this carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 6: He placed tzitzis upon existing tzitzis.</b> At first glance, Tosafos' question at the beginning of the chapter HaTecheiles from the discussion in Sanhedrin is difficult: a rebellious elder can be found in a case where one added tzitzis to existing tzitzis. Ra'avad's objection in his gloss is also difficult, for the principle 'each stands independently' should apply here. Tosafos' question, however, is not so strong. One can say that adding tzitzis to existing tzitzis is not called a matter whose interpretation derives from the Sages, because its interpretation is explicit in Scripture: 'tzitzis upon the corners of their garments' explicitly means one set of tzitzis upon each corner. Adding threads to one set of tzitzis is different: the four threads are derived from 'a cord' (two) and 'cords'; four. The same applies to the four passages of tefillin derived from the word totafot, and to the two willow branches of the lulav derived from 'willows of the brook.' These are called matters interpreted by the Sages. Two sets of tzitzis and two pairs of tefillin, however, can be considered explicitly defined by Scripture. At first glance, both aforementioned questions can be resolved as follows. Rambam was troubled by Tosafos' question from Sanhedrin in light of Sukkah, which holds that bal tosif applies even when the added species is outside the binding. Rambam therefore follows Zevachim 18, whose conclusion is that all agree an extra item is considered present. The principle 'each stands independently' may apply only if an extra item is considered absent, so that the addition does not damage the mitzvah itself. If an extra item is considered present, it diminishes the mitzvah. R' Eliezer in Sanhedrin may follow the opinion in Zevachim that an extra item is considered absent; according to Zevachim's conclusion, however, every addition diminishes the mitzvah. The discussion in Sukkah would then follow Sanhedrin's conclusion. This is insufficient, however, because Rambam writes in Hilchot Mamrim that a rebellious elder is liable only regarding the mitzvah of tefillin. If Rambam held that Sanhedrin's discussion is rejected in the final halacha, then as a practical matter a rebellious elder could likewise be liable regarding tzitzis and lulav.

<small>ונלע"ד </small>למה דמבואר בתוספת סנהדרין מדנדחקו בסוגי' דסוכה דמדרבנן אין מוסיפי' מבואר דס"ל דאין לחלק לענין האי לחודי' קאי בין האיסור ב"ת לגרעון המצוה. דלא כדס"ל בסוכה. אלא דס"ל דמישך שייכי אהדדי גירוע המצוה לעבירת ב"ת. ומש"ה הוכרחי לומר דמדרבנן הוא. וא"כ י"ל דגם הרמב"ם ס"ל כן. וס"ל עוד דמכם דהוי ב"ת דרבנן מגרע גם המצוה מדרבנן דכל דתיקון כעין דאורייתא תיקון. ויצא זה להרמב"ם מדקיי"ל הטיל למוטלת בכלל תולמ"ת. ע"כ דמעיק' קודם שחתכן פסולי' והיינו מדרבנן. וכ"מ בהר"ן סוכה פ"ג דף רמ"ח דכ' בדעת הרמב"ם כיון דהאי לחודי' קאי ליכא ב"ת אלא מדרבנן מש"ה כל לנאותו ל"ג בהו רבנן עיי"ש. והרי הרמב"ם כפי גרסתינו כ' שם ואם להוסיף או גרע פסול. ע"כ דמדרבנן אפי' דיעבד פסול. וביותר נראה כמעט דמוכרחי' לומר כן בדברי תוס'. דאם נדון כיון דמדרבנן עובר לא מגרע המצוה. א"כ הא דפריך במנחות מהטיל למוטלת דהוי תולמ"ה. ע"כ היינו אי פסיק להו לראשונות. כיון דבשעת עשייתן להשניי' לא הוי צריך להו. מש"ה הוי אח"כ מן העשוי. וכ' דס"ל להב"י מתחלה. א"כ יקשה הא דאמר רבא להם השתא האי גברא בב"ת קאי מעשה לא הוי. בין לפירש"י דמשום ב"ת לא מקרי עשיי' כלל. וקציצתן היא עשייתן. בין לפירוש הב"י אליבא דהרמב"ם דמשום ב"ת הוי מעשה מעליא יקשה הא מדאורייתא ליכא עבירת ב"ת דהאי לחודי' קאי. וממילא הוי השניות עשיי' בפסול וכשחתך הראשונות השניי' פסולי' משום תולמ"ה. ואיך נאמר משום ב"ת דרבנן אזלא פסול' דאורייתא דידהו. אע"כ דכללא הוא. דכל דאיכא ב"ת מגרע להמצוה. ובמקום ב"ת דרבנן מגרע להמצוה דרבנן. א"כ י"ל מדאורייתא בהטיל למוטלת אף חתך הראשונות ליכא משום תולמ"ה. דמ"מ על שניהם יש שם עשיי' בהכשר. ועיקר פרכת הש"ס רק כיון דמדרבנן קודם שחתכן פסולי'. וע"י קציצתן נתכשרו. הוי מדרבנן תולמ"ה. ולזה שפיר אמר רבא השתא בב"ת קאי מעשה לא הוי ממנ"פ. לגבי דאורייתא בלא"ה לא הוי תולמ"ה. ולגבי דרבנן משום עבירת ב"ת לא הוי תולמ"ה. וכפירש"י או כהרמב"ם א"כ דברי הרמב"ם מיוסדים על אדני פז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In my humble opinion,</b> Tosafos in Sanhedrin indicate the following. Since they were forced by the discussion in Sukkah to say that adding is rabbinically prohibited, they evidently hold that regarding the principle 'each stands independently,' one cannot distinguish between the prohibition of bal tosif and diminishing the mitzvah, unlike the view in Sukkah. Rather, these concepts are linked: diminishing the mitzvah follows from violating bal tosif. They were therefore forced to say that the prohibition is rabbinic. One can say that Rambam agrees and additionally holds that since the bal tosif prohibition is rabbinic, the addition also diminishes the mitzvah rabbinically, for every rabbinic enactment was fashioned in the pattern of Torah law. Rambam derived this from our ruling that adding tzitzis to existing tzitzis falls under ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui. It follows necessarily that before the first tzitzis were cut away, the new set was invalid, and that invalidity was rabbinic. This also appears from Ran, Sukkah, chapter 3, page 248, who writes in Rambam's view that since each component stands independently, there is no Torah-level bal tosif but only a rabbinic prohibition. Therefore, when the addition is for the mitzvah's beautification, the Sages did not decree; see there. Yet according to our text Rambam writes there, 'If his intent was to add or subtract, it is invalid.' It follows that even after the fact it is rabbinically invalid. Indeed, it appears almost necessary to say this in Tosafos' view. If a rabbinic transgression did not diminish the mitzvah, then Menachos' objection from adding tzitzis to existing tzitzis, which constitutes ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui, could only concern a case in which he later cuts off the first set. Since the second set was unnecessary when made, it afterward becomes valid through that which was already made. Beit Yosef initially understands it this way. If so, Rava's statement is difficult: 'At present this person violates bal tosif; his act is not an act.' According to Rashi, because of bal tosif it is not called an act at all, and cutting the first set becomes the act of making. According to Beit Yosef's explanation of Rambam, because of bal tosif it is a fully effective act. Either way, the difficulty remains that by Torah law there is no bal tosif, because each stands independently. The second set was therefore made while invalid, and after the first set is cut the second should be invalid by Torah law because of ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui. How could a rabbinic bal tosif prohibition remove its Torah-level invalidity? We must conclude that whenever bal tosif exists, it diminishes the mitzvah; rabbinic bal tosif diminishes it rabbinically. By Torah law, then, when one adds tzitzis to existing tzitzis, even if he later cuts away the first set there is no ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui, because both sets were made validly. The Gemara's principal challenge concerns only the rabbinic level: before the first set was cut away, the second was rabbinically invalid, and cutting the first set made it valid; this should constitute rabbinic ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui. Rava's answer is therefore sound from either direction: 'At present he violates bal tosif; his act is not an act.' At the Torah level, there is no ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui in any event. At the rabbinic level, because the bal tosif transgression prevents it from being an act, there is no ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui, following either Rashi's or Rambam's explanation. Rambam's words are thus founded upon golden bases.

<small>שם בא"ד </small>דמ"מ אסור משום מראית העין. ק' לי א"כ גם ביו"ד סי' ש"ג ליתסר משום מ"ע וביותר הא הלבוש כ' אין לעשות קרסי' למטה מהחגור אף שהטלית ארוך. ונשאר רובו פתוח מפני מ"ע שיאמרו הרוב הוא סתום כיון שהוא למטה מהחגור. וא"כ חיישי' אולי האי שיטעו בתרתי שיסברו דהקרסים נכפפין יסברו שהרוב סתום בהם. א"כ מכ"ש דראוי לאסור קרסים לענין כלאים דהוי רק חד מ"ע שיאמרו דהקרסי' נכפפי'. וצל"ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There, in the continuation: 'Nevertheless it is forbidden because of marit ayin.'</b> This is difficult for me. If so, it should likewise be forbidden because of marit ayin in Yoreh De'ah, siman 303. This is especially so because Levush writes that one should not place hooks below the belt even when the tallis is long and most of it remains open, because people may think most of it is closed merely because the hooks are below the belt. Thus we are concerned that a person might make two mistakes: he will think the hooks are bent closed and will think that most of the garment is closed by them. All the more so should hooks be forbidden regarding kilayim, where there is only one marit-ayin concern; that people will think the hooks are bent closed. This requires great further study.
