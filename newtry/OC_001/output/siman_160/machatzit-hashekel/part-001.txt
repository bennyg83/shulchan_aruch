**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 7: Which licked, etc.</b> Bach wrote, etc. Or Zarua states that if a dog or pig drank from the water, it is invalid. Beit Yosef asks: Either way, if he follows Rashi, who holds that drinking by animals and birds invalidates the water, why mention specifically a dog or pig? Bach answers that he certainly holds that drinking by an animal or bird is not considered work. Nevertheless, regarding a pig or dog, he holds that the water is invalid because their drinking makes it repugnant. Accordingly, even according to our ruling that drinking by an animal or bird does not invalidate the water, one should nevertheless be stringent regarding a dog or pig.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Rambam wrote, etc.</b> This is derived from the statement in the second chapter of Zevachim, page 22: Everything that completes a mikveh may complete the water of the kiyor, but not a reviis. The Gemara asks what this excludes and concludes that it excludes the case of adding a seah and removing a seah. Meaning, if a mikveh contains forty seah of valid water and drawn water falls into it, the drawn water does not invalidate it. Thus, if one seah of drawn water fell into the forty valid seah, there are now forty-one seah. If one then removed one seah from those forty-one, only forty seah remain. These contain slightly less than one seah of drawn water, while the amount of valid water is only thirty-nine seah and slightly more. One might therefore think that the drawn water should invalidate the valid water. Nevertheless, the mikveh remains valid. If another seah of drawn water then falls into the forty seah and one seah is again removed from the forty-one, the mikveh still remains valid. This continues until most of the original forty seah has been replaced, meaning that twenty-one seah of drawn water fell into it and an equal amount was removed each time; the mikveh nevertheless remains valid. The same law applies to the water of the kiyor. If, however, a small amount of invalid water fell into a reviis of valid water, it remains valid; but if one then removed from the mixture the same amount of water that had been added, leaving only a reviis, it is invalid, because a complete reviis of valid water no longer remains. These are the words of Rambam.

<b>And so wrote Terumas HaDeshen, that they are nullified in the minority.</b> This refers to birds that drank from the water.
