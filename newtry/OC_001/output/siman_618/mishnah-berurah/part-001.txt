(א) רופא בקי - פי' בקי באותו מקום ועיין לעיל סימן שכ"ח סעיף יו"ד דהביא שם המחבר דעת יש מי שאומר דלא בעינן בקי אלא כל אדם בחזקת בקי קצת לענין ספק נפשות (והיינו באומר עכ"פ שמבין בחולי זה) ומסיים הרמ"א שם די"א זה דוקא ברופא ישראל אבל בעכו"ם אינו נאמן עד שיהיה בקי וה"ה הכא ודע דביולדת לכו"ע מהימנינן לנשים דאינהו בקיאי בהו [ד"מ]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) An expert physician - Meaning, one who is expert in that place. See above, siman 328, se'if 10, where the Mechaber brought the view of one who says that we do not require an expert, but every person has a presumption of some expertise regarding a doubt of danger to life, meaning at least when he says that he understands this illness. The Rema concludes there that some say this applies specifically to a Jewish physician, but a non-Jew is not believed unless he is expert. The same applies here. Know that regarding a woman after childbirth, according to everyone we believe women, for they are expert in these matters [Darkei Moshe].

(ה) שומעים לחולה - היינו כשהחולה אומר שמרגיש בנפשו שצריך לאכול שאם לא יאכל שמא יכבד עליו החולי ובלבד שמזכירין לו תחלה שהיום הוא יו"כ דשמא שכח אבל אחר שהודיעוהו שהיום יו"כ והוא שואל לאכול א"צ לדקדק עליו יותר דלב יודע מרת נפשו ואחזוקי אינשי ברשיעא לא מחזקינן. כתבו הפוסקים אם החולה רוצה להחמיר אחר שצריך לכך עליו נאמר אך את דמכם לנפשותיכם אדרוש. עוד כתבו דבמקום שמאכילין אותו אין צריך כפרה ע"ז דאונס רחמנא פטריה כ"ש שלא היה רק כחצי שיעור וכפרה לא נאמר רק על השוגג:
**** ENGLISH ****
(5) We listen to the sick person - This is when the sick person says that he feels in himself that he needs to eat, for if he does not eat perhaps the illness will become more severe. But first they must remind him that today is Yom Kippur, for perhaps he forgot. After they inform him that today is Yom Kippur and he asks to eat, one need not examine him further, for the heart knows the bitterness of its soul, and we do not presume people to be wicked. The authorities wrote that if a sick person wants to be stringent after he needs to eat, about him it is said, 'But your blood of your souls I will demand.' They further wrote that in a place where we feed him, he does not need atonement for this, for the Torah exempted one under compulsion, and all the more so where it was only about half a measure; atonement was said only regarding one who sins inadvertently.

(8) Even if some are more expert than others - Meaning that the two who say he does not need are more expert and more outstanding in this wisdom; even so, we do not follow them to be stringent in a doubt involving life, since the others who say he needs to eat are also expert in this wisdom. See the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah, whose view is to rule like the authorities who hold that when the numbers are equal, we follow those who are more expert and more outstanding in this wisdom, unlike the Rema. **** ENGLISH ****
(15) We do not feed him - For the sick person's 'I do not know' does not come from expertise like the words of a physician, because most sick people do not know and are not expert in their illness. Where the physician is uncertain and says, 'I do not know,' even if another physician says he does not need, we feed him. The Taz holds that if the sick person himself is a physician and is uncertain and says, 'I do not know,' we also feed him, even if another physician says he does not need. Eliyah Rabbah questions this, for perhaps he does not understand regarding himself at the time of his illness, even though he is a physician; see there. It appears that this depends on the nature of the illness. If it is an illness of fever, where his mind is not clear, there is certainly room for the words of the Eliyah Rabbah.

(21) They should check in the sick person himself - Meaning that regarding drinking a cheekful, one must measure in him and not according to the average person, as above in siman 612. The later authorities wrote that the sick person should check this before Yom Kippur, meaning that he should put liquid into his mouth and spit it into a vessel. Likewise he should measure before Yom Kippur and look at a clock to see how many minutes equal the time of eating four eggs; this is the time he should wait on Yom Kippur between one eating and another, and likewise between one drinking and another, as below. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, part 6, siman 16, who wrote that the interruption between one eating and another should be nine minutes, which is the measure of kedei achilas pras. Know that between eating and drinking one need not wait at all, as above in siman 612, because eating and drinking do not combine.

(24) Or the sick person says so - The later authorities wrote that the custom is to place food before him and say to him: Today is Yom Kippur; if you are concerned that there will be danger to you if you do not eat a full measure at once, eat at once, and if not, eat little by little, less than the measure. The later authorities wrote that every halachic decisor must have these laws fluent in his mouth before Yom Kippur, because it may be that even a small delay involves danger to life. If it is a question that requires the decisor to examine the law, and there is another expert present who knows how to answer immediately, we do not give honor to the rabbi in this matter. See more of these laws in Hilchos Shabbos, siman 328 and siman 330, for the only difference between Yom Kippur and Shabbos is that deliberate violation of this is punishable by stoning and deliberate violation of that is punishable by kares.
