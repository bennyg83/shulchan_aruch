<b>(ס"ק א) אפי' כו'</b> הרא"ש בשם רש"י כצ"ל. והוא ברש"י דף פ"ד ע"ב אהא דתניא אין עושים דברים הללו לחולה עפ"י נשים או גוים. אבל מצטרפים לדעת אחרת פירש"י וז"ל אבל מצטרפים כו' כגון שנים אומרים צריך. ושלשה אומרים א"צ. ואשה או גוי אומר צריך מצטרפים למהוי פלגא ופלגא וספק נפשות להקל עכ"ל. ועכ"פ אותה אשה או גוי צ"ל רופא. ואשה לענין יולדת עיין במ"א ס"ק ח'. ומ"מ איכא חילוק בין ישראל לגוי. לפי מ"ש לעיל סי' שך"ח סעיף יו"ד די"א דא"צ מומחה דכל בני אדם חשובים מומחים קצת וספק נפשות להקל. (ומ"מ כתב מ"א שם דעכ"פ צ"ל שמכיר באותו חולי. ועכ"פ אינו נאמן להכחיש המומחה אפי' להקל) וכתב עליו רמ"א וי"א דוקא ישראלים אבל סתם גוים שאין רופאים לא מחזיקים אותם כבקיאים עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if katan 1) Even, etc.</b> The text should read: the Rosh in the name of Rashi. This is in Rashi, page 84b, on that which was taught, that these things are not done for a sick person on the basis of women or non-Jews, but they join with another opinion. Rashi explained: for example, two say he needs, and three say he does not need, and a woman or non-Jew says he needs; they join to make it half and half, and in a doubt involving life we rule leniently. End quote. In any case, that woman or non-Jew must be a physician. Regarding a woman and a woman after childbirth, see Magen Avraham, se'if katan 8. Nevertheless, there is a distinction between a Jew and a non-Jew. According to what was written above in siman 328, se'if 10, some say that an expert is not required, for all people are considered somewhat expert and in a doubt involving life we rule leniently. The Magen Avraham there wrote that he must at least recognize that illness, and in any case he is not believed to contradict an expert even to be lenient. The Rema wrote about this: some say this applies specifically to Jews, but ordinary non-Jews who are not physicians are not presumed expert.

<b>(Se'if katan 3) The text should read: even if the physicians say that the food will harm him, etc.</b> See what he wrote in his name in siman 330. I did not find the matter in siman 330. However, in siman 328, Magen Avraham, se'if katan 6, he wrote in the name of Radbaz: if the sick person says, 'I need this particular remedy,' and the physician says, 'he does not need it,' we listen to the sick person; but if the physician says that this remedy will harm him, we listen to the physician. End quote. One must distinguish between food and medicine. Regarding food, we listen to the sick person, for the heart knows the bitterness of its soul. But regarding medicine, if the physician says it will harm him, we listen to the physician, because there is reason to say that regarding medicine the physician knows more than the sick person; since he says it will harm him, we listen to the physician.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Se'if katan 10) A sick person, etc.</b> For they did not institute this on Yom Kippur, since it is only a rabbinic enactment and is not such an absolute obligation, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 254, se'if katan 23, unlike the Taz at the end of siman 677, who seems to hold that it is from the Torah. Since it is not such an obligation, one can say that on Yom Kippur they did not institute it. There is a reason for this: the reason for two loaves is as a remembrance of the manna, as stated in Shabbos 117b, from the verse 'they gathered double bread.' On Yom Kippur, although no manna fell on it, as Tosafos wrote at the beginning of Beitzah, nevertheless by reasoning, on Erev Yom Kippur a double portion did not fall as on Erev Shabbos, since they did not need manna on Yom Kippur, for they had to fast. Perhaps because of children and sick people, double bread did fall on Erev Yom Kippur. Nevertheless the Sages did not institute two loaves for Yom Kippur, because eating on Yom Kippur is only incidental.
