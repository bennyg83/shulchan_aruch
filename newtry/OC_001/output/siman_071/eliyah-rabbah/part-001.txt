[2] We do not protest, etc. Levush wondered: if even one not obligated to bury — e.g. married sister — may not read, all the more one obligated but with another. Lechem Chamudot 16a: Levush missed Shulchan Arukh's contradiction — Yoreh De'ah 341 wrote some say even if he wishes to be stringent he may not, without dissent. In my humble opinion Levush has no contradiction: first reasoning there wrote exempt from Keriat Shema without concluding 'even if stringent he may not' — exempt means not obligated but if he wishes, permission is his; therefore Shulchan Arukh wrote dissenting view. Shulchan Arukh stated both views in Yoreh De'ah; first anonymous reasoning is primary — as ruled here. Lechem Chamudot's resolution is strained; Magen Avraham here is strained. My father the rabbi resolved Levush and Lechem Chamudot together: 'if he has one who will exert for him' refers above to one not obligated to bury — two advantages permit stringency; one advantage — obligated to bury even with help, or not obligated without help — Shulchan Arukh holds he may not be stringent as in Yoreh De'ah. Correct, though this distinction is not in Beit Yosef; married sister presumably has burials yet they wrote he may not be stringent. Olat Tamid wondered on Shulchan Arukh — Jerusalem Talmud doubt should rule leniently in doubt of blessings like Levush. Per my father's explanation no difficulty — we do not find Jerusalem Talmud doubt in two advantages. Further Olat Tamid's words are wonder — no blessing in vain here; he is obligated in blessings; concern is honor of dead lest he neglect burial (Beit Yosef) — no flaw in blessing itself. Many decisors on the doubt: since unresolved — bless and pray (Beit Yosef); rabbinic blessings we rule leniently but Keriat Shema is Torah — siman 67 doubt is stringent. For law Bach, Nachalat Tzvi, Taz like Levush — may not be stringent unlike Shulchan Arukh; I saw this in practice with my grandfather the Gaon. Shulchan Arukh may not protest one who wishes to be stringent — no court can object since he relies on authorities who permit; but one who asks is told he may not — Beit Yosef, responsa Avodat HaGershuni 42. Taz: son who is onen while others handle burial may go to synagogue for Kaddish for all — see Taz Yoreh De'ah 376; see above siman 133 note 3:

[3] And if he was on Shabbat, etc. — Beit Yosef wrote in name of Rabbenu Yerucham: if he does not wait at the boundary for burial needs he is obligated also at evening; and if he waits he is exempt even at evening. And on second Yom Tov its law is like weekday. And on first Yom Tov if he wishes to bury that day through gentiles its law is like weekday; if not like Shabbat — until here.
