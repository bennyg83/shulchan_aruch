ואפשר דבזה חייב ג"כ לברך ברכת השחר אם לא בירכן בבוקר כשקם ממטתו דהא בשעת חיובן היה חייב ועיין לעיל בסימן נ"ב בבה"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>4. And from prayer. </b>He is likewise exempt from all blessings, including blessings over enjoyment, and from all mitzvos stated in the Torah. If he wishes to eat bread, although he need not recite Hamotzi, he must nevertheless wash his hands without reciting the blessing over washing. [Birkei Yosef, Yoreh Deah 341.] If there is a burial society in the city whose designated bearers remove the deceased, and after the relatives have attended to the deceased's needs they entrust the deceased to them for burial, the relatives become obligated in all mitzvos immediately upon entrusting the deceased to them, even before the deceased is removed from the house, because the burden no longer rests upon them. Accordingly, onenim do not act properly when they wait to pray until after the burial. As soon as they have reached an agreement with the chevrah kadisha concerning the burial place and have paid all expenses, including the shrouds, they are obligated to pray. Sometimes, however, they have not yet agreed where the deceased should be buried, which pertains to the deceased's honor, or the eulogies are still being delivered. During that time they remain exempt, but immediately afterward prayer becomes permitted and obligatory. In a place without a burial society designated for this purpose, however, even if another person will attend to the burial on his behalf, aninus continues until the deceased is buried and earth is cast upon him. As soon as they begin casting earth upon the deceased, the mourner should leave for another area of the cemetery and recite Krias Shema and pray there. If aninus continues until close to the end of the time for Krias Shema, which extends until one quarter of the day according to the proportional hours of that day, and praying in the regular order would cause the time for Krias Shema to pass, he should recite Krias Shema without its blessings. He should then pray in the regular order and recite Krias Shema again with its blessings. If aninus continues beyond four hours, the Magen Avraham holds that he should not recite Bircas HaShachar, meaning the blessings before Baruch She'amar, even if he then has time, because he was exempt during the principal time of their obligation, which is the morning. Others disagree, and because of doubt concerning blessings, one should be lenient and not recite them, except for Bircas HaTorah and the blessings, 'Who has not made me a non-Jew,' 'Who has not made me a slave,' and 'Who has not made me a woman.' According to the Magen Avraham as well, he may recite these blessings. He should not put on tefillin; see above, siman 38, seif 5. He should pray only the eighteen blessings of the Amidah. Regarding the blessings of Krias Shema, the Shulchan Aruch above, siman 58, seif 6, implies that their time has already passed. According to what I wrote above at the end of siman 58 in the Mishnah Berurah, however, he may recite even the blessings of Krias Shema, since the time passed because of circumstances beyond his control. According to all opinions, it is possible that he may recite Pesukei DeZimrah; see above, siman 52, Novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. If four hours, which constitute one third of the day, have not yet elapsed, but the end of that period is near, and praying in the regular order would cause the proper time for prayer to pass so that he would have to pray the Amidah after one third of the day, he should omit portions of Pesukei DeZimrah in the manner explained above in siman 52 regarding one who arrived late at the synagogue. He should not omit any part of Krias Shema or its blessings, even though this will force him to pray the Amidah after one third of the day. [Derech HaChaim; see there for his reason. This requires consideration in light of what I wrote initially.] Derech HaChaim further writes that the blessings, 'Who has not made me a non-Jew,' 'a slave,' or 'a woman,' and Bircas HaTorah may be recited throughout the day. Even if the burial occurs after midday, he should recite them. The eighteen blessings of the Amidah, however, may be prayed only until midday and no later. He also need not make up that prayer during the next prayer period. Thus, if he did not pray Shacharis, he does not make it up at Minchah. This differs from one who forgot or was prevented from praying Shacharis, who makes it up at Minchah. In that case, he was genuinely obligated but failed to pray because of some circumstance, whereas here he was exempt by law. The same applies to Minchah and Maariv. This applies only if the deceased lay before him from the beginning of the prayer's obligatory period until its time ended, such as where the deceased lay before him from dawn, when the entire eastern horizon became illuminated, until after midday. If the death occurred only after the obligation had already begun, such as after the entire eastern horizon became illuminated, then even if the burial did not occur until after midday, he makes up Shacharis at Minchah. Since the obligation had taken effect when the time for prayer began, even though there was not enough time to complete the prayer, he must later make it up at Minchah. It is possible that in this case he is also obligated to recite Bircas HaShachar if he did not recite them upon arising in the morning, since he was obligated when their time began. See above, siman 52, Biur Halachah.

(י) אינו מחשיך וכו' - כתב הט"ז כיון שיש עליו חיוב בעת ההיא יש לו לקרות ק"ש של ערבית בעוד יום אע"פ שאין הקהל קורין ק"ש אלא בלילה מ"מ זה שיהיה פטור בלילה לא יבטל ממנו עול מלכות שמים אבל ממ"א סק"ג משמע דאין סובר כן וכ"כ א"ר דלא יקרא ק"ש וברכות מבעוד יום וכ"ש שלא יתפלל תפלת י"ח. ונראה דיקרא ק"ש בלא ברכות ובמוצאי שבת יאכל בלא הבדלה ולא יתפלל ולאחר שיקבר המת יבדיל אפילו עד סוף יום ג':

<b>10. He does not go beyond the boundary, etc. </b>The Taz writes that since he is obligated at that time, he should recite the evening Krias Shema while it is still daytime. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>11. Has the same law as a weekday. </b>Even if he does not wish to bury the deceased, all the laws of aninus apply to him. This applies specifically where burial is possible but he does not wish to arrange it. If burial is impossible because of a non-Jewish holiday or another unavoidable circumstance, aninus does not apply. Chochmas Adam writes that if a relative died on Yom Tov morning during the time of prayer, in a place where the custom is to convene communal officials, and he cannot gather them until after prayer, aninus does not yet apply and he is obligated to pray, because this is considered an unavoidable circumstance. Likewise, on the night of Yom Tov, even the second day of Yom Tov, most later authorities agree that he should recite Kiddush and pray in full, and the laws of an onen do not apply, because burial is not normally performed at night. He is permitted in all matters except Torah study, because it brings joy. All this concerns Yom Tov. On a weekday, however, whether at night or during the day, even if there is some impediment because the communal officials have not gathered, aninus nevertheless applies. If one began praying or reciting Krias Shema and suddenly became an onen, the later authorities disagree whether he should complete it or stop. See Chochmas Adam, klal 139, seif 5, who follows Shevus Yaakov, while Shaarei Teshuvah cites many later authorities who require him to stop.

(יז) לא יצא - קנסוהו חכמים הואיל ועבר על דבריהם אפילו היה שוגג והראב"ד חולק וס"ל דא"צ לחזור ולקרות כיון דהמקום ההוא היה ראוי לקרות בו ורק משום לועג לרש יצא בדיעבד ולדינא לענין ק"ש הסכימו האחרונים לחוש לדברי הרמב"ם לחזור ולקרות אבל אם בירך או התפלל אינו חוזר. ולענין ברכות ק"ש מסתפק הפמ"ג עי"ש טעמו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>17. He has not fulfilled his obligation. </b>The Sages penalized him because he transgressed their words, even if he acted inadvertently. The Raavad disagrees and holds that he need not recite Krias Shema again, since the location was intrinsically fit for recitation and the prohibition applied only because it would mock the helpless deceased. He therefore fulfilled his obligation after the fact. As a matter of practical law regarding Krias Shema, the later authorities agree that one should take account of the Rambam's view and recite it again. If he recited a blessing or prayed, however, he does not repeat it. Regarding the blessings of Krias Shema, the Peri Megadim remains uncertain; see there for his reasoning.
