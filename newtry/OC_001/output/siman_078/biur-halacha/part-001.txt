<b> היה קורא</b> -  עיין מ"ב מש"כ לענין תפלה והוא מהפמ"ג וכתב עוד דאפשר דאפילו יעבור עי"ז זמן תפלה אפ"ה ממתין וכ"ש לפי מה שכתבנו לעיל דאפשר דבעת השתיתה הוא מדאורייתא יש להחמיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
"He was reading" — see Mishnah Berurah what he wrote regarding prayer; and it is from Peri Megadim; and he wrote further that it is possible that even if through this the time for prayer will pass, nevertheless one waits; and all the more so according to what we wrote above that possibly at the time of drinking it is Torah law — one should be stringent.

<b> מרחיק מהם</b> -  ולענין תפלה עיין בב"י דפוסק כדעת הרשב"א דכיון שכבר הוא עומד בתפלה א"צ להרחיק כלל במי רגלים שהוא מדרבנן אבל לקמן בסימן צ' סכ"ז בהשתין תינוק בבהכ"נ משמע דפוסק כאידך פוסקים דצריך להרחיק גם בתפלה ד"א או שישתוק עד שיביאו לו מים להטיל בתוכם ולא דמי להפשטת בגדים דהקילו רבנן בתפלה אף דהם טופח ע"מ להטפיח וכמו שכתבנו במ"ב משום דהוא הפסק גדול לילך ולפשטם וללבוש אחרים תחתם משא"כ הליכה לא מיקרי הפסק כ"כ וכן שתיקה ובלבוש מתרץ דהכא שבלא"ה בגדיו ג"כ מלוכלכין ממי רגלים וא"א לו לפושטם באמצע תפלתו ע"כ הקילו בו ג"כ שלא ירחיק ג"כ ממי רגלים שנטפו מהם על הארץ ונכון להחמיר כי שיטה השניה והשלישית שהובא בהפמ"ג שניהם דלא כלבוש וגם בתשובת הרשב"א גופא משמע דהוא מיקל אפילו באין בגדיו מלוכלכין וכ"כ בספר מטה יהודא וכן מוכח בתוס' ועכ"פ לענין השתנת תינוק משמע מהפמ"ג דאין לזוז מפסק השו"ע דשם וכן משמע בש"ת בסי' צ' עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
He distances from them — and regarding prayer see Beit Yosef who rules like Rashba's view: since he is already standing in prayer he need not distance at all from urine, which is rabbinic; but below in siman 90 note 27 regarding a child urinating in synagogue it is implied he rules like the other authorities that one must distance also during prayer — or that he should be silent until they bring him water to urinate in. And this is not comparable to removing clothing, for they were lenient regarding prayer even though they are "touching in order to spread" — as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah because it is a great interruption to go and remove them and don other clothing under them; unlike walking, which is not called such an interruption. And silence and remaining clothed he answers: here in any case his clothes are also soiled from urine and he cannot remove them in the middle of his prayer; therefore they were lenient also that he need not distance from urine that dripped from him onto the ground. And it is appropriate to be stringent, for the second and third approaches brought in Peri Megadim are both unlike remaining clothed; and also in Rashba's responsum itself it implies he is lenient even when his clothes are not soiled — so too in Sefer Mateh Yehudah and as proven in Tosafot. At any rate regarding a child urinating it is implied from Peri Megadim that one should not deviate from the Shulchan Aruch's ruling there; and so it seems in Shulchan Aruch siman 90 — see there.

**** ENGLISH ****
And so is the essence, etc. — see in Mishnah Berurah what I wrote after the fact regarding prayer; and although from the words of the gloss in siman 90 note 27 it is implied that after the fact one returns to the beginning of prayer through waiting — see Peri Chadash there who disagrees on the essence of the Mechaber's ruling; and although one should not rely on him as we wrote previously — nevertheless after the fact regarding returning to the beginning there is uncertainty; after the view of Rashba and Tosafot to be lenient ab initio not to distance in the middle of prayer — if so he was not then compelled; and it is possible that nevertheless one should return because otherwise the view of Rif and Rambam is that in prayer one returns even without compulsion, as Magen Avraham wrote similarly in siman 65 note 2 — see there:
