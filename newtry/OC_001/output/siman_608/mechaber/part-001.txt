**** ENGLISH ****
“The order of the meal prior to the beginning of the fast” - Containing four paragraphs. One eats and stops eating before dusk 60 Bein ha-Shemashot , bein hashmashos — twilight (period of Shabbos entry) , is a time which is called twilight or dusk. It is that short period of time from when the sun just begins to set on the horizon until the stars (at least three) begin to appear in the sky, which marks nightime and signifies a new day according to the Hebrew calendar. It is defined as "Not quite (doubtful) day and not quite night" in the Talmud tractate Shabbat 34b. because one must add “from the profane to the holy 61 "From the profane to the holy." It is a talmudic principle which operates on the Sabbath, festivals, therefore on Yom Kippur, that one starts the holiday a bit early, (and prolongs it a bit longer the following day) making it more than exactly twenty-four hours, so that one can increase the holiness that the holiday brings to one's life. Therefore one begins one's fast before the twilight so that one can add to the holiness of the Day of Atonement. ”, (i.e., from the weekday to Yom Kippur). And this addition has no specific time limit. But before dusk, the length of time which is one thousand, five hundred “seconds” prior to nightfall, one has to add from the profane (i.e., from the weekday) to the holy (i.e., Yom Kippur) either a little time or more time.

Women who eat and drink until it becomes dark, and do not know that it is a mitzvah to add from the weekday to the holy — one does not protest against them, so that they not come to act deliberately. {Rama: And the same applies to any prohibited matter: we say it is better that they be unwitting than deliberate — but this is specifically [regarding] that which is not explicit in the Torah, even though it is of Torah [origin]; but if it is explicit in the Torah, one does protest against him (Ran, chapter 4 of Beitzah; Rosh in the name of the Ittur). And if one knows that his words will not be heeded, he should not say [it] publicly to rebuke them more than once; but he should not multiply rebukes, since he knows they will not heed him. But [in private,] with an individual, he is obligated to rebuke him until [the other] strikes him or curses him (Ran, end of chapter "HaBa al Yevimto").}
