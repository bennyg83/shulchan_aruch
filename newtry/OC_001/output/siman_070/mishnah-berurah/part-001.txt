(ב) מק"ש - ופטורות ג"כ מברכות ק"ש דיש להם ג"כ זמן קבוע כדלעיל בסימן נ"ח ס"ו אבל ברכת אמת ויציב דניתקן על ענין זכירת יציאת מצרים וכן הברכות שלאחריה דערבית מחוייבות לאמרם דמצות זכירת יציאת מצרים נוהגת ביום ובלילה וא"כ ממילא צריכים לסמוך גאולה לתפלה דבתפלה חייבות כדלקמן סימן ק"ו ס"ב כן כתב המ"א ועיין בפמ"ג שכתב דלמאן דסובר זכירת י"מ בלילה הוא רק מדרבנן ואסמכתא אקרא א"כ ממילא הוא מ"ע שהזמן גרמא ופטורות מן התורה רק מדרבנן חייבות וכ"כ בספר ישועות יעקב עי"ש ואפילו אם נימא דהוא מצוה מה"ת ביום ובלילה מצדד בספר שאגת ארי' סי' י"ג דהנשים פטורות מטעם דהזכרה דיום היא מצוה בפני עצמה ואם לא הזכיר ביום א"צ להזכיר הזכרה זו בלילה ומה שמזכיר בלילה היא מצוה בפני עצמה עי"ש. ופסוקי דזמרה עיין לעיל בסימן נ"ב בחידושי רע"א דמוכח שם מדבריו דהעיקר ניתקנו בשביל התפלה א"כ ממילא חייבות. ולענין ברכת השחר לכאורה תלוי זה אם נימא דהברכות האלו יש להם זמן עיין לעיל בסוף סימן נ"ב במשנה ברורה ובבה"ל וצ"ע ומסתימת לשון הטוש"ע בסימן מ"ו ס"ד ובפרט מהלבוש שם עי"ש משמע דמברכות ברכות השחר כמו אנשים. ואולם כ"ז כתבנו לענין חיוב אבל פשיטא דיכולות להמשיך חיוב על עצמן ולברך אפילו ברכות ק"ש וכדלעיל בסימן י"ז ס"ב בהג"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(2) From Krias Shema. They are also exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema, since those blessings likewise have a fixed time, as stated above in siman 58, seif 6. They are, however, obligated to recite Emes VeYatziv, which was instituted concerning the remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt, and the blessings following the evening Krias Shema, because the mitzvah to remember the Exodus applies by day and by night. Consequently, they must juxtapose redemption to prayer, since they are obligated in prayer, as stated below in siman 106, seif 2. This is the Magen Avraham's ruling. See Peri Megadim, who writes that according to the view that remembering the Exodus at night is only D'Rabbanan and the verse is merely an asmachta, it is a time-bound positive mitzvah. Women are then exempt on a Torah level and obligated only D'Rabbanan. Yeshuos Yaakov writes similarly. Even if we say it is a Torah-level mitzvah by day and by night, the Shaagas Aryeh, siman 13, suggests that women are exempt because the daytime remembrance is a separate mitzvah. If one failed to remember it by day, one need not compensate for that remembrance at night; the nighttime remembrance is an independent mitzvah. Regarding Pesukei DeZimrah, see Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae above in siman 52, from which it appears that they were principally instituted for prayer, and women are consequently obligated. Regarding Bircos HaShachar, this would apparently depend on whether these blessings have a fixed time. See the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah at the end of siman 52. This requires study. The unqualified language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 46, seif 4, and especially that of the Levush there, implies that women recite Bircos HaShachar like men. All this concerns obligation. It is obvious, however, that women may voluntarily assume the obligation and recite even the blessings of Krias Shema, as stated above in siman 17, seif 2, in the gloss.

(23) According to the Raavad, he interrupts, etc. Most Acharonim, indeed nearly all of them, agree that regarding a Torah-level obligation the decisive issue is whether he began permissibly or impermissibly. Accordingly, regarding work or any of these activities other than eating, if he began before dawn he need not interrupt, provided sufficient time will remain after completing it to recite Krias Shema and pray. We rule below in siman 89, seif 7, that the Sages did not impose a restriction shortly before Shacharis, so he began permissibly. If he began after dawn, however, he began impermissibly and must interrupt for Krias Shema, which is D'Oraisa, but not for its blessings or for prayer. After completing the work, he recites Krias Shema with its blessings and prays, provided sufficient time will still remain. If he sees that the time is passing, he must interrupt immediately. Wherever he is not required to interrupt, he need not do so even if he will thereby miss communal prayer. Regarding eating, the Mechaber in siman 89, seif 5, appears to rule that one must interrupt immediately once dawn has risen, even if he began permissibly. No alternative is effective so long as he has not prayed.

(24) And recites. This implies that after reciting Krias Shema, even though he has not yet prayed, he may complete any of the activities he began, as explained in the preceding seif katan. This wording is not precise regarding one who was eating, since we have already explained that in that case he must also pray first. The Mechaber used this language because of the other activities, or alternatively because of the evening Krias Shema, to which all these laws likewise apply. Regarding the evening Krias Shema, if one began any of these five activities within the half-hour before nightfall, he certainly interrupts immediately. When the stars emerge, he recites Krias Shema without its blessings, then completes his meal or other activity, and afterward recites Krias Shema with its blessings and prays, as stated below in siman 235, seif 2. See Peri Megadim there.

(25) Enough time to recite. This is a decree lest he be drawn after these activities and the time for Krias Shema pass. It differs from Minchah, regarding which we rule in siman 232 that one who began any of these activities before praying need not interrupt, because Krias Shema is D'Oraisa and the Sages were more stringent concerning it.
