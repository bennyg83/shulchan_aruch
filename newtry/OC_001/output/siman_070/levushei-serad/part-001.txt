<small>(מ"א סק"ב)</small> שחרית. ב"י כתב ב' תירוצים על מ"ש הטור ד' ימים תירוץ א' הובא בט"ז וזה שכ' המג"א הוא תירוץ הב' ואף שהב"י בתירוצו השני כ' כמו שהמנהג במצרים שכונסים הבתול' בליל ד' ע"ש כבר השיגו הד"מ דהא דוקא ביום הרביעי תנן כדי שישכים לב"ד לכן כתב המ"א שיכנס לחופה ביום ד' קודם שקר':
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 2.</small> Morning. The Beit Yosef gives two answers concerning the Tur's statement that the exemption lasts four days. The first answer is cited by the Taz, while the answer cited by Magen Avraham is the second. Although in his second answer the Beit Yosef writes that this accords with the custom in Egypt, where a virgin is brought under the chuppah on Wednesday night, see there, the Darchei Moshe already objects that the Mishnah specifically says Wednesday by day, so that the husband may rise early and go to beis din. Magen Avraham therefore writes that he should enter the chuppah on Wednesday before dawn.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Ibid.</small> The halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Meaning, wherever Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel appears in the Mishnah, the halachah follows him. Here, although Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the Mishnah is concerned for arrogance, the ruling differs for the reason the Beit Yosef explains there at length.

The novelty is that today he prays even though prayer requires greater concentration than Krias Shema, because in any event people nowadays do not concentrate properly.

<small>(סק"ה)</small> ועסי' ס"ג ס"ד דשם מבואר דיש מ"ד מצוה א"צ כוונה לצאת לכן אם מתכוון כקור' בתורה יוכל לקרות ויועיל אליב' דהאי מ"ד דאלו למ"ד מצות צריכות כוונה א"כ כיון שמתכוין כקור' בתורה מה בצע בקריאתו כן נ"ל כוונתו. ועדיין צ"ע דאפי' למ"ד מצות א"צ כוונה מ"מ כיון שמתכוין כקורא בתורה ה"ל כמתכוין שלא לצאת דאינו יוצא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif katan 5.</small> See siman 63, seif 4, where it is explained that some authorities hold that mitzvos do not require intent to discharge one's obligation. Therefore, if he intends merely to read as one reads from the Torah, he may recite it and it will be effective according to that opinion. According to the opinion that mitzvos require intent, however, since he intends merely to read from the Torah, what benefit is there in his recitation? This appears to be Magen Avraham's intent. It nevertheless requires study, for even according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent, if he specifically intends to read merely from the Torah, this is like intending not to discharge his obligation, and he does not fulfill it.
