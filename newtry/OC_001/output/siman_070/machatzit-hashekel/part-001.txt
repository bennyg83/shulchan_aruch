<b>(ס"ק א) נשים כו'</b> ג"כ מברכות ק"ש. ר"ל ניהו דברכת ק"ש אין להם שייכות לק"ש וכמ"ש מ"א לעיל סי' נ"ח ס"ק ז'. ובסי' סמ"ך וכ"כ הרב"י בסי' מ"ו בשם תשו' רשב"א והבי' ראיה מדלא מברכים אקב"ו לקרות את שמע. והביאו גם בסי' סמ"ך בב"י. וא"כ ס"ד ניהו דפטורי' מק"ש מ"מ בברכותיה חייבי' קמ"ל דמה"ט דפטורים מק"ש דהוי מ"ע שהז"ג מה"ט גופי' פטורים מברכותי' דגם הברכות הוי מ"ע שהז"ג דהיינו עד ד' שעות כמ"ש ס"ס נ"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 1) Women, etc.</b> They are likewise exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema. Meaning, although the blessings of Krias Shema are not intrinsically connected to Krias Shema, as the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 58, seif katan 7, and in siman 60, and as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 46 in the name of a responsum of the Rashba, bringing proof from the fact that we do not recite the blessing, "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to recite the Shema," and he also cited this in the Beit Yosef in siman 60, one might therefore have thought that although women are exempt from Krias Shema, they are nevertheless obligated in its blessings. The ruling teaches that for the very reason they are exempt from Krias Shema, namely because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah, they are likewise exempt from its blessings, since the blessings too are time-bound, namely until the end of the fourth hour, as stated at the end of siman 58.

**** ENGLISH ****
We treat it as such, or alternatively, because it is a plea for mercy. This is the correct reading. Meaning, since prayer is a request for one's needs, a woman too should appropriately ask these things from Hashem. Do women not also require life and their other needs? It follows that Rabbeinu Yonah maintains that women are exempt even from a Rabbinic time-bound obligation. One cannot say that he holds prayer to be a Torah-level positive mitzvah, as discussed below in siman 106, seif 2, because the Torah-level mitzvah of prayer is not time-bound, as explained there. His question must therefore concern the Rabbinic obligation of prayer, which the Sages fixed at particular times. The Magen Avraham's proof is consequently sound. This also appears in the Gemara on page 20b according to our text. The Gemara asks regarding the Mishnah's ruling that women are obligated in prayer, "Is this not obvious?" It answers that one might have thought that, since it is written, "Evening, morning, and noon I shall speak," prayer resembles a time-bound positive mitzvah. The Mishnah therefore teaches that prayer is a plea for mercy. Rabbeinu Yonah evidently did not have this reading. Tosafos had this reading and wrote: "Rashi does not include this text, since prayer is D'Rabbanan, and what relevance does a positive mitzvah have to it? Nevertheless, one may answer that Hallel too is D'Rabbanan, yet women are exempt because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah."

**** ENGLISH ****
According to everyone, the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as Rabbah bar bar Chanah ruled in the name of Rabbi Yochanan in Gittin 75a and elsewhere in the Gemara. The dispute among the Rosh, Rif, and Rambam depends on the Gemara's two resolutions. In Berachos 17b, the Gemara discusses the Mishnah: "A groom who wishes to recite Krias Shema may do so. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: Not everyone who wishes to assume the reputation may do so." The Gemara asks whether Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is concerned about arrogance while the Sages are not, although elsewhere they apparently hold the reverse. The Mishnah states that in a place where people customarily perform labor on Tisha B'Av they may do so, and where they customarily refrain they may not; everywhere, Torah scholars remain idle. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that every person may always conduct himself as a Torah scholar. Rabbi Yochanan answers that the positions must be reversed. Thus, regarding Krias Shema, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits him to recite it, while the Sages say that not everyone who wishes to assume the reputation may do so. Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi answers that the positions need not be reversed. The Sages' positions are consistent, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's positions are also consistent. Krias Shema depends on concentration, and we know that the groom cannot concentrate; if he recites it, this is arrogance. Regarding Tisha B'Av, however, an observer can say that he simply has no work to do, for there are many idle people. The Rosh therefore ruled according to Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi, who said not to reverse the positions, so Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that not everyone may assume the reputation. The Rosh consequently ruled that the groom may not recite Krias Shema. The Rif and Rambam, however, ruled according to Rabbi Yochanan, who reversed the positions. According to that resolution, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits the groom to recite Krias Shema and is not concerned about arrogance, and they therefore likewise ruled that he may recite it.

<b>See Tosafos Yom Tov, etc.</b> He asks from this very passage that one cannot say the Rambam ruled according to the resolution that reverses the positions, under which Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is not concerned about arrogance. Yet in the Laws of Fasts the Rambam records only that Torah scholars everywhere refrain from work, and does not state that every person may conduct himself as a Torah scholar. The Rif likewise writes this explicitly at the end of the chapter Get Pashut. It is therefore possible that the Rif and Rambam agree with Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi and nevertheless did not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel here because his reasoning did not appear persuasive to them. The Shach writes similarly in Choshen Mishpat, siman 171, seif katan 9.

<b>And in Beit Yosef, Choshen Mishpat, siman 231.</b> The first Tanna holds that a wholesale merchant must clean his measuring vessels once every thirty days. Since he sells and measures frequently, much residue adheres to the vessels, reducing their capacity, so they require cleaning. A private householder, who measures only occasionally and therefore accumulates less residue, need clean them only once every twelve months. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel reverses the rule. The Beit Yosef asks that we ordinarily rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel whenever he appears in our Mishnah. The Beit Yosef himself answers that we follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel only where his reasoning is persuasive.

<b>(ס"ק ה) לא יפסיק כו'</b> ובשכמל"ו דבשכמל"ו יש בו קבלת מ"ש כמו בפסוק א'. וע"ל סי' ס"א ס"ק י"א מ"ש בשם הלבוש ומה"ט כ' גם הלבוש כאן בסעי' א' שכ' ונכון ללמדם שיקבלו עליהם עול מ"ש ויקראו לפחות פסוק א'. שגם בשכמל"ו ילמדם לו'. כקור' בתורה רק לא יאמר ברכת ק"ש והם דברי מגדל עוז פ"ד מהל' ק"ש על מה שכ' הרמב"ם כ"מ שהוא פטור מק"ש אם רצה להחמיר קור'. אבל אם היה זה הפטו' מבוהל אינו רשאי לקרות עד שתתיישב דעתו והשיגו הראב"ד וז"ל ומה בכך יקר' ויהיה כקור' בתורה ולא יהי' כפורק ש"ש מעליו כו' עכ"ל וע"ז כ' המ"ע דלזה גם הרמב"ם מודה רק שלא יאמר ברכת ק"ש. ובב"י בשם הרמב"ם. ר"ל דלמד מדברי הטור דס"ל דאפי' בזמן הזה שגם שאר בני אדם אין מכוונים. מ"מ אם אותו הפטור הוא מבוהל ותמה ואינו יכול לכוין דעתו כשאר בני אדם אינו רשאי לקרו' עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 5: He should not interrupt, etc.</b> And also for 'Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever,' because that passage likewise contains acceptance of the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, as does the first verse. See above, siman 61, seif katan 11, what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Levush. For this reason Levush also wrote here in seif 1 that it is proper to teach them to accept upon themselves the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven and to recite at least the first verse, and that one should also teach them to say 'Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.' He may recite it as one reading Torah, but should not say the blessings of Krias Shema. These are the words of Migdal Oz on chapter 4 of Hilchos Krias Shema, concerning Rambam's ruling that wherever one is exempt from Krias Shema, if he wishes to be stringent he may recite it. But if the exempt person is agitated, he may not recite until his mind has settled. Raavad objected: What is wrong with this? Let him recite and be as one reading Torah, and let him not appear to cast off the yoke of Heaven, etc. Migdal Oz wrote that Rambam also agrees to this, except that he should not say the blessings of Krias Shema. The phrase 'in Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam' means that he inferred from the Tur that even nowadays, when other people also do not concentrate properly, if the exempt person is so agitated and bewildered that he cannot concentrate as other people do, he may not recite.
