<b>ובא לציון. </b> כשמכריזין דבר יש להכריז קודם שמתחיל החזן אשרי ולא בין אשרי ללמנצח וכ"ש בשעה שאומרים אשרי שתתבטל כוונתם סמ"ע בח"מ סי' <small>(רס"ב)</small> [רס"ז]. אם בא יחיד לבה"כ בשעה שאומרים סדר הקדושה יאמרה עמהם אף קודם תפלתו דהא י"א דאין היחיד אומרה וה"ה דיכול לאומרה קודם שיאמר השני פסוקים דהיינו ובא לציון וגו' ואני זאת וגו' ואח"כ יאמר השני פסוקים. ומכ"ש שיכול לומר אשרי ולמנצח אח"כ כדי לאומרה עם הציבור. מ"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>U'Va LeTzion.</b> When an announcement is to be made, it should be announced before the chazzan begins Ashrei, not between Ashrei and Lamnatzeiach, and certainly not while Ashrei is being recited, lest the congregation's concentration be disturbed. Sema, Choshen Mishpat, siman <small>(262)</small> [267]. If an individual enters the synagogue while the congregation is reciting Seder Kedushah, he should recite it with them even before his own prayer, since some authorities hold that an individual may not recite it alone. Likewise, he may recite it before saying the two verses, namely, "U'Va LeTzion," etc., and "And as for Me, this is My covenant," etc., and afterward recite those two verses. Certainly, he may recite Ashrei and Lamnatzeiach afterward in order to recite Seder Kedushah with the congregation. Magen Avraham.

<b>It is incumbent upon us to praise.</b> The writings state that Aleinu should be recited after each of the three daily prayers, and Knesses HaGedolah, siman 234, writes likewise. Sefer Shtei Yados, however, citing Tolaas Yaakov, writes that it should not be recited after Minchah. At the conclusion of Aleinu one should recite the verse, "Hashem shall be King," etc. One should say, "and the abode of His glory." One should say, "He is our God; there is none other." Tashbetz. <b>Mourner's Kaddish.</b> One who is uncertain whether his father or mother died should recite Kaddish when no other mourner is present. Maharil. Grandsons may recite Kaddish, but the other mourners should recite two Kaddishim for every one recited by the grandsons. There is no distinction whether they are grandsons through a son or through a daughter. Responsa Knesses Yechezkel, however, writes that this applies specifically to a son's son, whereas a daughter's son should not recite Kaddish. Certainly a daughter has no right to recite Kaddish in the synagogue at all, although if a separate minyan is made for her they may permit it. See there, at the end of part 14. A person observing a yahrzeit receives all the Kaddishim of that day, even the Kaddish after the chapters, Ruth, and Shir HaShirim. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 80; so rules Magen Avraham. Knesses Yechezkel writes there that every obligatory Kaddish, such as those connected to the prayer, the Kaddish after Shir HaYichud, and likewise those after Barchi Nafshi and the chapters, belongs to the yahrzeit. Regarding Koheles, Ruth, and Shir HaShirim, however, lots should be drawn, since their recitation is only a custom; they are comparable to the Kaddish after Tehillim and the Tehillim of the Day, for which lots are drawn. The Kaddish recited on Friday night after Mizmor Shir LeYom HaShabbos belongs to the mourners, unless the yahrzeit is a member of the group responsible for Kabbalas Shabbos; everything follows local custom. Knesses Yechezkel writes that a yahrzeit whose anniversary falls on Friday has no claim to that Kaddish; rather, the mourners and the one whose yahrzeit falls on Shabbos draw lots. See there. When four or five people draw lots, and two receive equal letters, such as tav, while the others receive lower numbers, the entire drawing is void and all must draw again. The two may not say that the others already lost and only they should redraw. If, however, one person receives tav and the others receive equal but lower numbers, the first person certainly wins. If two or three people drew lots and another claimant arrived the following day, the division is canceled and they must draw again. The one who recited Kaddish at Maariv is excluded from the new drawing. If the newcomer was in the city but failed to attend, he forfeited his right, for his absence indicates that he waived it and accepted being last. If one person recited Kaddish and another arrived the next day and said, "I will recite today in exchange for the Kaddish you recited yesterday, and we will draw lots for the third Kaddish," while the first argues, "Since you did not come yesterday, I acquired that Kaddish, and today we divide the two between us," the second is correct. He recites the first Kaddish and they draw lots for the second. If there are four yahrzeits and three attended the previous evening, the three recite all the Kaddishim connected to the prayer, while the fourth recites the Kaddish after the Tehillim of the Day. If there are so many yahrzeits that even all the Kaddishim cannot be divided among them, one who failed to attend Maariv forfeits his share. All the poskim agree that even if one mourner is able to lead the prayer and another is not, the one who leads does not thereby lose his right to Kaddish. If both are able to lead, they draw lots to determine who is first and who is second. If two people have yahrzeit and one must depart early the next morning and says, "Give me the Kaddish now and you may take mine tomorrow," while the other says that they should draw lots, the latter is correct, for he may say, "I wish to be first in performing the mitzvah." If two people died under duress, one having been killed and the other having died naturally, the son of the murdered person takes precedence over the son of the one who died naturally, because being killed atones and he certainly repented before death, unlike one who died naturally. When there is a mourner within thirty days, he receives all the Kaddishim connected to the prayer, while the Kaddish after a psalm belongs to the other mourners. In places where a resident takes precedence over a visitor, the visitor nevertheless has the right on one occasion to lead the prayer and recite Kaddish. Shach writes that if a mourner within thirty days is present, a visitor has no Kaddish at all. A student studying in a yeshivah has the status of a resident. A teacher whose wife does not live elsewhere is considered a resident. See what Knesses Yechezkel writes concerning this. Regarding Kaddish, we do not say that part of a day is considered an entire day. Thus, although one person's relative died during the week and another's died on Sunday, they are equal on Shabbos. If one is mourning his father and his mother then dies, he receives all the Kaddishim by virtue of being within thirty days, yet still shares the other Kaddishim with the mourners on account of his father. Magen Avraham in the name of Maharan. Knesses Yechezkel objects that, according to this, one mourning both father and mother would recite twice as many Kaddishim as another mourner, which is not so, since one remembrance serves for both. He resolves that the case is one within thirty days, when the Kaddishim connected to the prayer are his and the mourners within twelve months recite Kaddish after Tehillim and similar passages. If several yahrzeits occur on a particular day and the mourner within thirty days is entirely displaced from the Kaddishim connected to the prayer, he may share the other Kaddishim with the mourners on account of his father. Otherwise he does not recite twice as many Kaddishim, for one remembrance serves for both. An adult mourner within shivah who comes to the synagogue on Shabbos takes precedence over a yahrzeit, and certainly over one within thirty days. A minor within shivah, however, has the status of one within thirty days, and the yahrzeit retains one Kaddish. Mishmeres Shalom, siman <small>(72)</small> [73]. Shach, siman 376, writes that even one within thirty days retains one Kaddish against a minor within shivah, whereas Magen Avraham rules that the minor completely displaces the mourner within thirty days, though the yahrzeit retains one Kaddish. One whose shivah was canceled by a festival, or who buried his relative during a festival, has the status of a mourner within shivah. A minor within shivah likewise has that status, since he attends the synagogue throughout the seven days, while the yahrzeit retains one Kaddish. If on Shabbos there are a minor within shivah, an adult within shivah, and a yahrzeit, the adult recites two Kaddishim and the minor one. The adult may tell the minor, "I am not taking the third Kaddish from you, but from the yahrzeit." Eliyah Zuta is uncertain whether an adult within shivah who makes a minyan in his home perhaps has the same status on Shabbos as a minor within shivah, so that the yahrzeit retains one Kaddish. We do not make this distinction, and the practice follows what was stated above. A mourner within thirty days receives all the Kaddishim connected to the prayer, while the remaining mourners within twelve months recite the Kaddishim after Tehillim and similar passages. If a resident has yahrzeit and another resident is within thirty days, the yahrzeit receives one Kaddish at Maariv and the mourner within thirty days receives two at Shacharis. If a visitor has yahrzeit and a resident is within thirty days or within twelve months, the visitor receives one Kaddish. If a visitor is within thirty days and a resident within twelve months, they divide all the Kaddishim connected to the prayer. If both a resident and a visitor have yahrzeit, the resident displaces the visitor. If there are a resident yahrzeit, a visitor yahrzeit, and a resident within thirty days, the visitor recites one of the two Kaddishim at Shacharis that would otherwise belong to the mourner within thirty days. The resident yahrzeit cannot tell the visitor, "I precede you," because the visitor may reply, "I am taking nothing from you, but from the mourner within thirty days." When a resident and a visitor both have yahrzeit, and regarding a Kaddish after Tehillim the yahrzeit participants draw lots with the mourners, if the resident yahrzeit waives his place in the drawing so that the visitor may recite one Kaddish, the mourners cannot object, because relative to them the visiting yahrzeit takes precedence. If, however, lots were drawn and the mourners won, the visiting yahrzeit may be told, "Your own fortune caused your loss." The visiting yahrzeit participates in the drawing with them, because relative to them he has the same standing as one of them. In a minyan formed in the entrance hall outside the synagogue for Pores Al Shema, visitor and resident are equal. A visitor, however, has no share in the Kaddish after Tehillim, the Tehillim of the Day, other psalms, or after Shir HaShirim, Ruth, and Koheles, except when he has yahrzeit, as explained above. The universal practice is that on the day one stops reciting Kaddish at the completion of eleven months, he recites all the Kaddishim. He does not, however, displace a yahrzeit or a mourner within thirty days. He should stop one day or more before the date corresponding to the death, rather than on that date itself. For example, if the death occurred on the third of Shevat, he should stop on the second of Teves. The reason for stopping is to avoid treating his father as wicked. If he recites Kaddish on the third of Teves, that would be the first day of the twelfth month and would count as a month, as though he had recited Kaddish for twelve months. He should therefore recite on the second of Teves and stop that day; the third of Shevat is the yahrzeit. The yahrzeit always follows the day of death, not the day of burial, even if burial was delayed for many days, and even during the first year. If one's mother died and his father objects to his reciting Kaddish, the father has no authority to prevent him. A visitor who recited one Kaddish in any synagogue has no further right to Kaddish in any synagogue of that community for thirty days. I derived all these rulings from the early and later poskim. See Knesses Yechezkel, end of part 14, and Eliyah Rabbah, which cites several additional rulings and responsa concerning these matters.

<b>The psalm that the Leviim recited.</b> I found it written that the practice of reciting the Mishnah, "The psalm that the Leviim would recite," etc., is mistaken. The Tur's intention was that on each day one should recite the psalm belonging to that day. Rambam writes likewise in his order of prayer, as does Knesses HaGedolah. Magen Avraham writes that, in his opinion, the Tur intended that the Mishnah should also be recited, in order to conclude with words of Torah. He concludes that on Shabbos the practice is to recite the Mishnah as printed in all siddurim. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes: "I have seen the practice everywhere that on Rosh Chodesh, Chol HaMoed, Chanukah, and Purim, instead of the Tehillim of the Day they recite another psalm related to the occasion: on Rosh Chodesh, Barchi Nafshi; on Chol HaMoed Pesach, Hodu LaShem; on Chol HaMoed Sukkos, Ke'ayal Ta'arog; on Chanukah, Mizmor Shir Chanukas HaBayis LeDavid; and on Purim, Al Ayelet HaShachar. On Chanukah, however, Mizmor Shir Chanukas HaBayis precedes the daily psalm because of publicizing the miracle. See there and Magen Avraham.
