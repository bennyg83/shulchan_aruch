ולכך אומרו דוד לאחר שמונה עשרה מזמורים כי אשרי האיש ולמה רגשו חדא הוא, עד כאן. כתב הכלבו סימן י"ג שאומרים אותו בלחש חוץ מפסוק ראשון לפי שדברים שבכתב אי אתה רשאי לאומרן בעל פה ופסוק ראשון שגור ביותר. למה תיקנו בקדושה זה ה' ימלוך שאמר משה, ובקדושה מעומד אומרים ימלוך ה' לעולם ועד שאמר דוד. ויש לומר לפי שמתרגמינן זה הסדר ואין תרגום לכתובין, והרא"ש כתב שקבלה בידו שעל תרגום פסוק ה' ימלוך לעולם יצאה בת [1] <b>Kedushah, etc.</b> Levush writes that Ya'ancha BeYom Tzarah was instituted before Seder Kedushah because Kedushah itself was instituted as a result of distress. Rabbi David Abudraham writes that it was placed at the end of the prayer because Midrash Tehillim states that after a person prays, he is told, "May your prayer be answered." David therefore placed it after eighteen psalms, since Ashrei HaIsh and Lamah Ragshu constitute one psalm. Kol Bo, siman 13, writes that it is recited quietly except for the first verse, because one may not recite passages from Scripture by heart, while the first verse is exceptionally familiar. Rabbi David Abudraham explains that "You are holy, enthroned" means that Hashem waits for and arranges the prayers of Israel before those of the angels. Alternatively, the Holy One, blessed is He, waits until the last congregation in Israel has prayed and then accepts all their prayers together. Rabbi David Abudraham writes that the common practice of saying in Kaddish, "le'alam u'le'almei almaya," is mistaken; rather, one should say, "be'alam u'le'almaya," meaning that in the world of the angels He is separate and distinct from them. Lest one argue that because He is so exalted He certainly does not direct His attention toward human affairs, the verse states, "Hashem, Master of legions, the whole earth is filled," etc. Why did they institute in this Kedushah the verse "Hashem shall reign forever," which Moshe said, while in the standing Kedushah we say "Hashem shall reign forever and ever," which David said? Since this order is translated into Aramaic, and there is no authorized Targum of Kesuvim. Rosh writes that he received a tradition that when Yonasan ben Uziel translated the verse "Hashem shall reign forever," a Heavenly voice emerged and said, "Who is revealing My secrets to human beings?" because this verse contains the end of days. Kol Bo writes that we say "Hashem shall reign" in order to use the Targum of Onkelos, which is superior to that of Aquila. Levush writes that Seder Kedushah is not recited at Shacharis on Shabbos and Yom Tov because it is phrased as a request. His words are difficult, for it is recited at Minchah in the same form used every day. The primary reason appears to me to be that of Mateh Moshe: "I found it written that it was instituted at the end of the prayer for the sake of the unlearned who arrive late, so that they would not lose the opportunity to hear Kedushah. They therefore established for them a miniature form of prayer containing Ashrei, followed by Kedushah, mention of Zion, and Torah study. Accordingly, this Seder Kedushah contains eighteen mentions of Hashem, corresponding to the eighteen blessings. It is not recited at Shacharis on Shabbos and Yom Tov because everyone comes to the synagogue before Kedushah. The reason it is recited at Minchah is that they would deliver a discourse before Minchah and customarily conclude the discourse with verses of redemption and Kedushah; the unlearned also came to hear the discourse." I saw the same in Abudraham. It is therefore recited in Aramaic so the unlearned can understand it. According to this, latecomers should concentrate on this Kedushah even while occupied with another part of the service. Magen Avraham writes that if one enters the synagogue while the congregation is reciting Seder Kedushah, he should recite it with them even before his own prayer. Likewise, he may recite it before the two verses, "U'Va LeTzion," etc., and "And as for Me, this is My covenant," etc., and then recite those two verses afterward. Certainly, he may recite Ashrei and Lamnatzeiach afterward. See the beginning of siman 292. When saying, "Blessed is our God Who created us," etc., one should do so with concentration and heartfelt joy. One should pause between "those who seek You, Hashem" and "Hashem desires." Abudraham writes in the laws of Tisha B'Av that in a mourner's house they do not say, "And as for Me, this is My covenant," because a mourner is forbidden to study Torah.

[ב] <b>היחיד אומרה וכו'.</b> ורמז למה שכתב בית יוסף בסימן נ"ט בשם הזוהר דלשון תרגום אין לאומרה ברבים ונתן סימן שנים מקרא ואחד תרגום, וכתב הפרישה רצה לומר בלשון הקודש שהוא מקרא יאמר דוקא שנים ולא דוקא שנים למעלה אלא רצה לומר ברבים ולפחות בעשרה אבל בתרגום יאמר יחיד. אלא צריך עיון דאנן לא קיימא לן כזוהר דהא אמרינן אפילו הלשון הקודש ביחיד, ואם כן מדהא ליתא הא נמי ליתא עד כאן לשונו. ואולי דמשום הכי כתב הלבוש טעם אחר ומבואר בדבריו דבקדושה ביוצר לא מהני שנים אלא עשרה בעינן לזוהר, וכן משמע בבית יוסף סימן נ"ט שהסכים דברי רשב"א דבעינן עשרה לדברי זוהר עיין שם, ולאפוקי מעולת תמיד שכתב דלזוהר אומרים שנים ויותר. ועיין סימן נ"ט דכתב בלבוש דבזוהר ראיתי דתרגום בלחש, וליתא אלא בבית יוסף מדהוא ביחיד יאמרנה בלחש, ובאבודרהם מצאתי מה שנהגו לומר זה הסדר בלחש בעבור פסוק ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד שאמר משה והיה ראוי לאומרו ראשון קודם קדוש ושמו אחרון כדרך סדר הקדושות שמלכות בסוף, ותדע שכן הוא שהפסוקים הראשונים אומר שליח ציבור בקול רם עם תרגומים ופסוק ה' ימלוך ותרגומו אומר בלחש:

[2] <b>An individual recites it, etc.</b> This alludes to what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 59 in the name of the Zohar, that a passage in the language of Targum should not be recited communally. He gave the mnemonic, "Two readings of Scripture and one of Targum." Perishah explains that Scripture, which is in Lashon HaKodesh, is recited specifically by "two," meaning communally, and not literally only two people, but at least ten; Targum, however, is recited by an individual. This requires study, for we do not rule like the Zohar, since even the Lashon HaKodesh portions may be recited by an individual. If that element is rejected, this one should be rejected as well. Perhaps for this reason Levush gave another explanation. His words make clear that according to the Zohar, two do not suffice for the Kedushah in Yotzer; ten are required. Beit Yosef in siman 59 likewise appears to accept Rashba's view that the Zohar requires ten. See there. This excludes Olas Tamid, who writes that according to the Zohar two or more may recite it. See siman 59, where Levush writes that he saw in the Zohar that the Targum should be recited quietly. This is inaccurate. Rather, Beit Yosef writes that since it is recited by an individual, it should be said quietly. I found in Abudraham that the custom to recite this order quietly is because of the verse "Hashem shall reign forever," which Moshe said. It properly should have been placed first, before "Holy," yet they placed it last, following the pattern of the Kedushos in which kingship comes at the end. Proof of this is that the chazzan recites the first verses aloud with their translations, whereas the verse "Hashem shall reign" and its translation are recited quietly.
