**** ENGLISH ****
In Magen Avraham, seif katan 5: "And one to whom it happened." Note: In my humble opinion, in the first chapter of Megillah they said "we do not pass over mitzvos" only regarding the Men of the Great Assembly who enacted Purim. They should have made the first Adar primary because we do not pass over mitzvos, and it says that because of joining redemption to redemption they made the second primary. Automatically, in the language of the Torah, the second Adar is Adar, not the first, as Tosafos wrote this reasoning at the end of the chapter Konam Yayin; see there. If so, automatically one should make the day of his miracle only in the second, which is Adar, and not the first; this is easy to understand. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 163 and siman 191. After he elaborated in siman 163 to explain the entire matter, he concluded with this language: The laws that emerge from these words: 1) A community or individual may properly establish a festival day on the day when a miracle happened for them, and they also perform a mitzvah; see Magen Avraham, unlike Pri Chadash in Minhagei Issur, os 10. 2) If it is in Adar and it involves a positive act, feasting, joy, and a festival day, one need not trouble himself except once, namely in the second Adar, in order to join redemption to redemption. 3) If it is a positive act of mitzvah, such as distributing charity, he should do it in the first Adar because the zealous advance and we do not pass over mitzvos. Nevertheless, no boy becomes bar mitzvah until the second Adar, for the reason Magen Avraham wrote in siman 55, seif katan 10. 4) If it is a fast day of distress, he should do it in the second Adar, for we do not advance calamity; see Milchamos at the end of the first chapter of Megillah. 5) If it is something involving no burden, such as refraining from action, one is obligated to do it in both Adars, the first and the second. 6) Regarding reciting Kaddish, the second is always primary, for the judgment above upon the soul is in the second, like the Torah language in Megillas Esther, where it is written "the second." So too the seventh of Adar, the yahrzeit of Moshe Rabbeinu, etc. 7) In documents, in the first Adar one writes simply Adar, and in the second Adar, second Adar. See Tumim at the end of siman 43. 8) One who vows until Adar means until the first, for ordinary speech follows the language of documents. Nevertheless, one who is stringent until the second Adar, blessing will come upon him. All this appears to me in my humble opinion as halachah and practice.
