**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Standing for eating.</b> For it is prepared incidentally to its mother, since if he wished he could have slaughtered it and eaten it together with the fetus. Even if the mother is a tereifah, what is prepared for dogs is considered prepared for people. According to Tosafot's explanation, one specifically requires that it be prepared and standing for dogs, and nowadays ordinary tereifos do not stand for dogs; if so, the offspring of a tereifah is forbidden, and this requires study. See what I wrote in siman 308, seif 31. Yam Shel Shlomo wrote: according to what we rule like Rabbi Shimon, it is permitted in every case, even if it stands to raise offspring, first, because the Rosh does not distinguish between muktzeh and nolad, and furthermore, because with living creatures there is no prohibition of nolad, as Tosafot wrote. The reason is that it is not fit to eat immediately after its birth while alive, and furthermore, even before its birth it was fit to eat, unlike a chick. These are his words.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For we are concerned.</b> Meaning, although if a calf was born on a weekday it is permitted that same day and we are not concerned for crushed limbs, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 15, seif 2, nevertheless on Yom Tov we are concerned. This is the language of Darchei Moshe: certainly, if we see a change in the inner limbs, we are concerned, and it was explained in siman 497 that it is forbidden to slaughter on Yom Tov in a place where tereifos are common. For this reason they were uncertain in the Gemara regarding a trampled bird, whether it is permitted to slaughter it. One should not say that the Tur should have explained in Yoreh Deah that if a change was found it is a tereifah. One can say that he relied on what he wrote regarding the law of a fallen animal that remained alive twenty four hours: although it left the category of crushed limbs, it requires inspection of the inner limbs. Therefore a calf born that day, although it does not require inspection, nevertheless when a change is seen in it, it is a tereifah. These are his words.
