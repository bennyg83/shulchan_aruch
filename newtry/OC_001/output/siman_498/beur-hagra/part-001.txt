**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if it is doubtful, etc.</b> According to Rashi's explanation at the end of Perek Sho'el, that we are concerned, etc., and as written in the fourth chapter of Eiruvin 45b: and if you wish, say, etc. This is what he wrote, that muktzeh, etc., meaning in such a case because of outside the techum. But a doubtful case of prepared status we hold is forbidden, as written in siman 497, seif 3. The Rashba follows his own view, for he rules like Rashi, but we do not hold so. This is the language of the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, halacha 6: Our teacher wrote that every doubt of prepared status is forbidden; know that every law of muktzeh and nolad is included in this. The reason is because it is an item that has a permitted status later, or because the Sages wished to be stringent in this doubt. The Rashba disagreed and said that since muktzeh and nolad are from their words, we follow leniency, and they were stringent only in a matter that is close to touching a Torah prohibition, such as a doubt whether it was trapped; but other doubts are permitted. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that his words are astonishing, for why is a doubtful egg forbidden, and our entire sugya in the first chapter treats doubtful nolad stringently. Furthermore, the baraisa teaches plainly: doubtful prepared status, Rabban Gamliel, etc. The words of our teacher, who did not distinguish, appear correct. See the beginning of siman 517, and what I wrote in siman 325, seif 7, and there seif 9, and siman 515, seif 7, and siman 325, seif 16; this requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>With muktzeh we follow, etc.</b> It appears to me that he learned this from what is stated in the fourth chapter of Eiruvin 46b: and if you wish, say, it is, etc. Although we hold that a doubt of prepared status is forbidden, the Rashba already wrote in Avodas HaKodesh, 45b: That which we said, that muktzeh and nolad are forbidden on Yom Tov, is not according to the Torah, but from the words of the Sages. Therefore, in their doubts we follow leniency. In what case? In a matter that is not close to coming to a Torah prohibition. But if it is close to touching a Torah prohibition, we follow stringency even in its doubt, as with a Torah matter. How so? If one spread traps or nets for beasts or birds before Yom Tov, and the next day he rose early and found in them a beast or bird, etc., they are forbidden even though they are of doubtful prepared status, because if you permit this, it is close to permitting trapping, which is forbidden according to the Torah. Therefore they were stringent in its doubts. This is the Rashba's approach, and likewise Rashi's approach in Perek Sho'el. But in siman 325 and siman 515 he ruled like the Rif and Rambam; see what I wrote above.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, it is preferable, etc.</b> From what is stated there, 'and not dig,' etc., it implies specifically where he has no spade inserted. This is the language of the Beis Yosef: The Kol Bo wrote in the name of Rabbi Meshulam that if he slaughtered, it is permitted to cover with ash of an oven heated on Yom Tov, and he should not cover with a spade. End quote. It appears to me that this means: he should not dig with a spade and cover, but if he had a spade inserted, it is certainly preferable. This is what we say in the Yerushalmi: if he slaughtered, it is better, etc., and not dig with a spade and cover; evidently it speaks where he does not have a spade inserted. But if he has a spade inserted, it is preferable. So too it appears from the words of the Rosh and Tur. End quote. His words are astonishing, for the Kol Bo wrote, and he should not cover with a spade, which implies even a spade inserted. Likewise, the Yerushalmi speaks of a spade inserted, for it refers to the Mishnah, which says: and they agree that if he slaughtered, he should dig, etc. Furthermore, if it speaks where it is not inserted, what is the meaning of 'it is better,' etc.? It means that if he has oven ash, even without this it is forbidden. The reasoning is also simple, for the prohibition of moving is lighter than labor that is not needed for its own sake, or destructive labor. For the Mishnah says that if a non-Jew comes to extinguish, etc., even in a loss through fire, whereas regarding moving muktzeh all the poskim permitted telling a non-Jew, as written above in siman 276, seif 3 in the gloss. All the more so on Yom Tov, where moving muktzeh for the need of food preparation is permitted, as written above in siman 518. There is no implication in the words of the Rosh and Tur. So wrote the Rashba explicitly, and this is his language in Avodas HaKodesh, section 40a: If he slaughtered and has no oven ash heated on Yom Tov with which it is permitted to cover because of the mitzvah of covering, not only that, but even if he has an inserted spade and oven ash heated on Yom Tov, he covers with the ash and does not cover with the inserted spade. End quote. So too the Bach and Magen Avraham challenged him.
