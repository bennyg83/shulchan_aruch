<b>(ס"ק ז) ביום כו'</b> ודבריו אמת לדעת רשב"א כו' דאיתא במסכת שבת דף קנ"א גוי שהביא חלילין בשבת לא יספוד בהן ישראל אא"כ באו ממקום קרוב (פי' רש"י שבאו מתוך התחום) ושאיו בגמרא מאי ממקום קרוב ואמר שמואל (וכוותיה קי"ל) חיישינן שמא חוץ לחומה לנו ופירש"י דחיישינן ר"ל להקל ואייתי ראיה דמצינו לפעמים דחיישינן הוא להקל ושמואל ס"ל אפי' הובא מחוץ לעיר אנו תולין להקל שכבר אתמול בלילה היה תוך התחום ולנו חוץ לחומה ולכן מותרים מיד במו"ש וה"ק מתני' אלא א"כ באו ממקום קרוב אלא א"כ יש לתלו' שבאו ממקום קרוב לאפוקי היכי שידוע שבאו מח"ל שאסורי' ולפ"ז כל מידי דהוא ספק אם הובא מח"ל הולכים בספקו להקל. אבל הרי"ף פי' דברי שמואל חיישי' שמא חוץ לחומה לנו לחומרא ור"ל אפי' באו ביום השבת בבקר לתוך העיר לא אמרינן דוודאי מתוך התחום באו דאי מח"ל לא היה אפשר שיבואו מיד בבקר לתוך העיר אפ"ה חיישינן שבאו מח"ל אלא שהלכו ליל שבת עד שהגיעו לחומה ולנו שם עד הבקר ולפ"ז מה דקתני במתני' אא"כ באו ממקום קרוב ר"ל שודאי ידעי' שבאו ממקום קרוב. אבל ספק אם בא מח"ל להרי"ף אסור. ופסק הרשב"א כשיטות רש"י דבספק ח"ל אזלינן לקולא ולכן כתב מ"א דדברי הש"ך דאפי' בי"ט א' מותר. אמת לדעת רשב"א דס"ל ספק הובא מח"ל מותר. אבל הש"ע שפסק בסימן תקט"ו כהרי"ף להחמיר. לכן א"ש מ"ש בש"ע ביום שני. דביום א' אסור כיון דספק' הוא. ומ"מ ביום שני מותר גם לדידן משום דהוי ספק ספיקא כמ"ש סי' תצ"ו סעיף ד'. וע"ש במ"א שחולק. ועמ"ש בע"ה לקמן סי' תקט"ו. ודע שכתב רשב"א דאף להרי"ף דמחמיר בספק בא מח"ל. מ"מ אם הגוי שרוי עמו בעיר ויש לו אותו דבר שהובא בעירו אזלינן להקל. ואמרינן שהביא ממה שי"ל תוך העיר ולא ממה שי"ל חוץ לתחום. והכי קי"ל לקמן סימן תקט"ו סעיף ז':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 7) On the day, etc.</b> His words are true according to the Rashba, etc. It states in Shabbos 151 that if a non-Jew brought flutes on Shabbos, a Jew may not eulogize with them unless they came from a nearby place, which Rashi explains as within the techum. The Gemara asks what nearby place means, and Shmuel says, and we rule like him, that we are concerned lest they lodged outside the wall. Rashi explains that concerned here means leniently, and he brings proof that sometimes concern is for leniency. Shmuel holds that even if they were brought from outside the city, we attribute it leniently and say that already the previous night they were within the techum and lodged outside the wall, and therefore they are permitted immediately after Shabbos. The Mishnah means: unless they came from a nearby place, meaning unless one can attribute them to having come from a nearby place, excluding where it is known that they came from outside the techum, in which case they are forbidden. According to this, whenever there is a doubt whether something was brought from outside the techum, we follow the doubt leniently. But the Rif explained Shmuel's words, that we are concerned lest they lodged outside the wall, stringently. Meaning, even if they came into the city on Shabbos morning, we do not say that they certainly came from within the techum, for if they were from outside the techum it would have been impossible for them to arrive in the city immediately in the morning. Even so, we are concerned that they came from outside the techum, except that they walked Friday night until they reached the wall and lodged there until morning. According to this, when the Mishnah teaches, unless they came from a nearby place, it means that we certainly know they came from a nearby place. But a doubt whether they came from outside the techum is forbidden according to the Rif. The Rashba ruled like Rashi's approach, that in a doubt of outside the techum we rule leniently. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote that the Shach's words, that even on the first day of Yom Tov it is permitted, are true according to the Rashba, who holds that a doubt whether it was brought from outside the techum is permitted. But the Shulchan Aruch, which ruled in siman 515 like the Rif to be stringent, properly wrote here, on the second day, for on the first day it is forbidden since it is a doubt. Nevertheless, on the second day it is permitted even according to us, because it is a double doubt, as written in siman 496, seif 4. See there in Magen Avraham, who disagrees, and see what I will write, with Hashem's help, below in siman 515. Know that the Rashba wrote that even according to the Rif, who is stringent regarding a doubt whether it came from outside the techum, nevertheless if the non-Jew lives with him in the city and has that item in his city, we rule leniently and say that he brought it from what he has within the city, not from what he has outside the techum. So we rule below in siman 515, seif 7.

<b>(ס"ק ח) עומדת כו'</b> ולפי' התוס' בעי' כו' דהתו' דף וי"ו ע"ב ד"ה מוכן כו' הקשו למה עגל שנולד מן הטרפה מותר דמוכן אגב אמו לכלבים ומוכן לכלבים הוו מוכן לאדם. ואפרוח שנולד בי"ט אסור. וכמ"ש לקמן סי' תקי"ג סעיף ח' משום מוקצה הא כשהיה אפרוח תוך הביצה עכ"פ היה מוכן לכלבים והוי מוכן לאדם ותי' דבעינן דוקא מוכן ועומר לכלבים ורוקא טרפה דא"א לו כלל שתהיה ראויה לאדם ה"ה מוכן ועומד לכלבים מה שאין כן ביצה שי"ב אפרוח ניהו דעכשיו אינה ראויה אלא לכלבים מכל מקום אינו מוכן ועומד לכלבים דאין דעתו להשליכו אלא להמתין עד שיצא האפרוח ותהיה ראויה לאדם ולעצמו עכת"ד. ובזמן הזה סתם טרפות א"ע לכלבים כ"א למכור לגוי. ודוקא בזמן הש"ס שהיו גוים מועטי' דרים בין הישראלי'. ואפילו היה מוכרו לגוים לא היה הגוי נותן בעדו כ"א דבר מועט וכמ"ש התוספות במסכת ע"ז דף כ' ע"א ד"ה ור"מ כו' היה מוכן ועומד לכלבים מה שאין כן בזמן הזה דגוים הרבה מצוים בינינו. ומוכר נבלות וטרפות לגוי בדמי שווין או מזלזל קצת במכירתן. לא מיקרי מוכן עומד לכלבים עמ"ש סימן ש"ח סל"א דמביא מ"א שם הרבה פוסקים סבירא ליה כסברת התוספות דבעינן מוכן ועומד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 8) Standing, etc.</b> According to Tosafot's explanation, we require, etc. Tosafot on 6b, s.v. muchan, etc., asked why a calf born from a tereifah is permitted, since it is prepared incidentally to its mother for dogs, and that which is prepared for dogs is considered prepared for people, yet a chick born on Yom Tov is forbidden, as written below in siman 513, seif 8, because of muktzeh. When the chick was inside the egg, it was at least prepared for dogs, which should be considered prepared for people. They answered that we require specifically something prepared and standing for dogs. Only a tereifah, which cannot at all become fit for people, is prepared and standing for dogs. But an egg that contains a chick, although now it is fit only for dogs, is not prepared and standing for dogs, since his intent is not to throw it away but to wait until the chick emerges and becomes fit for a person and for himself. These are their words. Nowadays, ordinary tereifos are not standing for dogs, but rather to be sold to a non-Jew. Specifically in the time of the Gemara, when few non-Jews lived among Jews, and even if he sold it to non-Jews the non-Jew would give only a small amount for it, as Tosafot writes in Avodah Zarah 20a, s.v. and Rabbi Meir, it was prepared and standing for dogs. This is not so nowadays, when many non-Jews are found among us and one sells neveilos and tereifos to a non-Jew for their value, or lowers the price somewhat; it is not called prepared and standing for dogs. See what is written in siman 308, seif 31, where Magen Avraham brings many poskim who hold like Tosafot's reasoning, that we require prepared and standing.

See Magen Avraham in siman 323, seif katan 14, who wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 54, that it is permitted to estimate sixty, even if it became mixed before Shabbos and he did not know until Shabbos. See siman 498, seif 9. These are Magen Avraham's words. Magen Avraham alluded to what Terumas HaDeshen wrote there, for he wished to permit estimating on Shabbos whether the permitted matter is sixty, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name. Terumas HaDeshen wrote: as for the case of inspecting a firstborn, it can be resolved as Rashi explained in the chapter Ein Tzadin, where he distinguishes between inspecting blemishes of a firstborn and seeing a slaughtered animal to inspect it from tereifah status, which is permitted on Yom Tov. These are his words. Rashi's words are on 27, where the Gemara thought that Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya proved that Rabbi Meir holds that one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov from the Mishnah: one who slaughtered the firstborn and afterward showed its blemish to a sage, Rabbi Yehudah permits; Rabbi Meir says that since it was slaughtered not by instruction of an expert, it is forbidden. This shows that Rabbi Meir holds that inspecting a firstborn is not like inspecting a tereifah: inspecting a firstborn is before life, while inspecting a tereifah is after slaughter. From this, inspecting a tereifah may be even on Yom Tov, while inspecting a firstborn must be before Yom Tov. Rashi wrote: this shows that Rabbi Meir holds that inspecting a firstborn is not as light as inspecting a tereifah, for the Sages gave it importance, and the entire permissibility of the firstborn depends on it, since even its slaughter is forbidden before he shows it. It is not comparable to inspecting a tereifah, which they permitted to inspect after slaughter. From it Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya learned and said that since it is so significant, it is like repairing and judging a case, and it is forbidden to inspect it on Yom Tov, even though a tereifah may be inspected on Yom Tov. These are Rashi's words. It appears from this that although inspecting blemishes of a firstborn is forbidden, nevertheless inspecting tereifos is lighter and is permitted on Shabbos and Yom Tov. One must say that although Abaye rejected this proof and said that Rabbi Meir's law does not depend at all on the law of inspecting blemishes of a firstborn, but Rabbi Meir's reason is because of a penalty, decreeing blemishes that are not liable to change after slaughter on account of blemishes that are liable to change after slaughter, and were it not for the penalty Rabbi Meir would agree that after the fact, if it was slaughtered not by instruction of an expert, it is permitted, and if so inspecting a firstborn is not so significant, and perhaps he holds that one may inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, nevertheless Terumas HaDeshen holds that although Abaye rejected that there is no proof from here that Rabbi Meir holds one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, the reasoning remains that according to us, who rule that one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, nevertheless inspecting a tereifah is permitted on Yom Tov. For in any case, inspecting a firstborn is more significant than inspecting tereifos, since initially everyone agrees that even with blemishes that do not change after slaughter, one may not slaughter initially except by instruction of an expert who saw the blemish before slaughter, unlike by tereifah. If so, at least inspecting a firstborn is more significant than inspecting a tereifah. Therefore, although we do not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, inspecting a tereifah is permitted on Yom Tov. So one must say according to Terumas HaDeshen. In any case, according to Terumas HaDeshen, the above law of the Taz is not correct, so it appears to me.
