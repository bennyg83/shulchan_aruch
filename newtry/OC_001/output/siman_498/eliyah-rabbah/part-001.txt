[א] <b>[לבוש] לעצמו מערב יום טוב.</b> צריך עיון דבש"ס [ביצה] דף כ"ח מבואר דאף ביום טוב מותר לראות לעצמו כיון שהוא חכם ידע שלא ישחיזנה וכן מוכח בבית יוסף לכל הטעמים, ומה שכתב אחר כך דבזמן הזה יבדוק מערב יום טוב כרמ"א, והיינו משום דאין בזמנו דין תלמיד חכם, ומה שהקשה מגן אברהם דהא לכמה דברים דנין דין תלמיד חכם נראה לי דשאני בדבר שנמסר לעצמו שטועה בעצמו שהוא חכם, וכהאי גוונא כתבתי בספרי ליו"ד סימן א' גבי מומחה בית השחיטה, וראיתי בבאר שבע שמיקל למאי דנוהגין ליטול קבלה דדמי לחכם ולא נהירא שהרי גם בימי מהרי"ל ורמ"א היה מנהג זה ואפילו הכי אסרו ועוד אדרבה גרע דהא נותנין קבלה אף לאינו חכם ועיין בים של שלמה פרק אין צדין סימן כ':
**** ENGLISH ****
[1] <b>[Levush] For himself from before Yom Tov.</b> This requires study, for in the Talmud, Beitzah 28, it is explained that even on Yom Tov it is permitted to inspect for himself, since, being a sage, he knows not to sharpen it. So too it is proven from the Beis Yosef according to all the explanations. What he wrote afterward, that nowadays one should check before Yom Tov like the Rama, is because in his time the law of a Torah scholar does not apply. What the Magen Avraham asked, that for several matters we judge the law of a Torah scholar, appears to me not comparable: here it is different because the matter is entrusted to himself, and one may err about himself, thinking that he is a sage. Similar to this I wrote in my work on Yoreh Deah, siman 1, regarding an expert in the slaughterhouse. I saw in Be'er Sheva that he is lenient because our practice is to take authorization, which is similar to a sage, but this is not clear, for even in the days of the Maharil and Rama this custom existed, and nevertheless they prohibited it. Furthermore, on the contrary, it is worse, for authorization is given even to one who is not a sage. See Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek Ein Tzadin, siman 20.

[6] <b>And if it is doubtful, etc.</b> This requires study, for in siman 515, seif 6, the Geonim ruled that its doubt is stringent, and there is permission only where fruits and non-Jews are common in the city. Although the Beis Yosef wrote this in the name of the Rashba, one can say that he holds like those who are lenient. What the Rashba himself wrote, distinguishing where fruits are common in the city, is according to the Geonim, as the Tur wrote at the end of siman 515, and so it implies in Avodas HaKodesh, page 37. Afterward the Magen Avraham was printed, and he sensed this somewhat. It is also difficult to me regarding the reasoning of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, that with muktzeh we follow, etc.; for regarding outside the techum and items of non-Jews there is no muktzeh, but the reason for the prohibition is the decree lest they tell him to bring it. **** ENGLISH ****
[12] <b>In order to eat, etc.</b> Meaning after its soul departs. Even if he does not eat, and even thought to eat is not required. Yam Shel Shlomo. The Taz wrote that they permitted specifically an animal in mortal danger, because of loss, but a healthy animal is forbidden unless he actually eats an olive-bulk from it. What the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba, 'even if he eats,' is a scribal error, and it must read, 'unless he eats,' etc. End quote. This is astonishing from an author of his stature, for he fell into a great error by emending for no reason. Certainly eating does not help, since he does not need it, and he does it only in order to permit it; this is a device and is forbidden. So it is explained in the Maggid Mishneh cited by the Beis Yosef there. It is even more astonishing that he did not look at the source of the law in Responsa Rashba, siman 680, where it is explicit that his eating does not help. So too it is written explicitly in Responsa Rivash, simanim 16 and 254, and Sefer Tanya, siman 55. See Responsa Be'er Esek, siman 56; his words in this are not correct, see there.

[יג] <b>כזית צלי וכו'.</b> היינו כתנא קמא דר' עקיבא [ביצה] בדף כ"ה וכן פסקו הרמב"ם וסמ"ג הרשב"א וסמ"ק, והט"ז תמה הא קיימא לן הלכה כר' עקיבא אפילו מחביריו פירוש אפילו אם רבים חולקים עמו עד כאן לשונו, ולא ירדתי לסוף דעתו דבעירובין דף מ"ו ע"ב מפורש דוקא נגד יחיד הלכה כר' עקיבא ולא נגד רבים וכן כתבו בספר כריתות ובכנסת הגדולה בכללי הגמרא סימן ח', גם נגד סתם משנה אין לפסוק הלכה כר' עקיבא כמו שכתבו תוספות ביצה דף כ"ה ד"ה לכם וכו', מיהו בזה יש לומר כיון דחולק ר' עקיבא באותו משנה לא הוי דברי תנא קמא כסתם:
**** ENGLISH ****
[13] <b>An olive-bulk roasted, etc.</b> This follows the first Tanna of Rabbi Akiva in Beitzah, page 25, and so ruled the Rambam, Semag, Rashba, and Semak. The Taz wondered: do we not hold that the halacha follows Rabbi Akiva even against his colleagues, meaning even if many disagree with him? End quote. I did not understand the end of his reasoning, for in Eiruvin 46b it is explicit that the halacha follows Rabbi Akiva specifically against an individual, but not against many. So too it is written in Sefer Kerisus and in Knesses HaGedolah in the rules of the Gemara, siman 8. Also, against an anonymous Mishnah one should not rule like Rabbi Akiva, as Tosafot wrote in Beitzah 25, s.v. lachem, etc. However, in this case one can say that since Rabbi Akiva disputes in that very Mishnah, the words of the first Tanna are not considered an anonymous ruling.

[טו] <b>ויש אומרים שאין להפשיטה וכו'.</b> וכן ראוי להורות (רמ"א) והלבוש השמיטו נראה משום דסברא הראשונה הוא הרשב"א ור"ן ובית יוסף ויש אומרים הוא ריא"ז בשלטי גיבורים והוי יחיד ועוד דהוא נגד המשנה מביאה איברים וכן הקשה מגן אברהם מירושלמי ותירוצו דחוק למעיין. והנראה לעניות דעתי שגם ריא"ז לא קאמר אלא לר' עקיבא דמתיר אף אין שהות לנתחה אף שיש קצת איסור מכל מקום סבירא ליה דיש נמי איסור בהפשטת יום טוב לכך התיר מה שאין כן לתנא קמא דסבירא ליה דבעינן שהות סבירא ליה להתיר והמשנה וירושלמי הנזכר לעיל קאי לתנא קמא ורמ"א וכל אחרונים לא ירדו לזה והניחו לי מקום:
**** ENGLISH ****
[15] <b>And some say that one may not skin it, etc.</b> So it is proper to rule. Rama. The Levush omitted it, apparently because the first opinion is the Rashba, Ran, and Beis Yosef, while the 'some say' is Riaz in Shiltei Giborim and is an individual view. Furthermore, it is against the Mishnah, which says he may bring it limb by limb. So the Magen Avraham also challenged from the Yerushalmi, and his answer is forced to one who examines it. In my humble opinion, it appears that even the Riaz said this only according to Rabbi Akiva, who permits even when there is not time to cut it up. Although there is some prohibition, nevertheless he holds that there is also a prohibition in skinning on Yom Tov, and therefore he permitted only that. But according to the first Tanna, who holds that there must be time, he holds that it is permitted. The Mishnah and Yerushalmi mentioned above follow the first Tanna. The Rama and all later authorities did not arrive at this, and they left me room.

**** ENGLISH ****
[17] <b>It is permitted to slaughter it, etc.</b> The Taz asked: how is this different from dovecote birds in siman 497, seif 10, where designation is required lest he find them lean? This is no difficulty, for here it had a presumption of being kosher, unlike there, where it has no presumption of being fat. However, the Rashal, chapter 4, siman 19, and the later authorities prohibit. Likewise, in a place where treifos are as common as kosher animals, it is forbidden to slaughter on Yom Tov. So explained Raavan, page 77.

[יח] <b>אין חכם וכו'.</b> אבל כשרואה המום ושותק אף שגמר בלבו שהוא מום שרי (ים של שלמה פרק אין צדין ט"ו). כתב הט"ז נפל איסור לח בהיתר לח בפחות מששים או יבש ביבש ואין רוב בהיתר ואחר כך נתוסף היתר אסור להורות ביום טוב דהוא ממש כבכור שיש לו חזקת איסור:
**** ENGLISH ****
[18] <b>A sage may not, etc.</b> But when he sees the blemish and remains silent, even if he concluded in his heart that it is a blemish, it is permitted. Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek Ein Tzadin 15. The Taz wrote: if liquid prohibition fell into liquid permission in less than sixty, or dry into dry and there is no majority of permitted matter, and afterward more permitted matter was added, it is forbidden to rule on Yom Tov, for it is exactly like a firstborn that has a presumption of prohibition.
