אפשיטא. אי ד"ס אכשר רחמנא או לא וממילא גם לר"י צריכים לטעמא דמוכן אגב אמו. דהא ליכא היתירא דמתיר עצמו דדלמא ד"ס אכשר רחמנא והיה לו היתר בשחיטה קודם הלידה. ואי דממנ"פ מותר דלאותו צד דד"ס מכשר. הוי מוכן. ז"א. דהא מ"מ לא הוי מוכן כיון דלכתחילה א"י לשחטו במעי אמו דהוי כמו לילה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif 5.</small> A calf born on Yom Tov may be slaughtered if the mother stands for eating. At first glance the plain reading of the sugya is difficult. It says according to Rav: what difference is there between this and a calf, etc. This implies that only according to Rav do we need the reason that it is prepared incidentally to its mother. But according to the one who permits a chick, this reason is not needed at all. If so, since the Tur in siman 513 brought that regarding a chick born on Yom Tov some permit and the Geonim forbid, he should here also have brought the view of those who permit with a calf born where the mother stands for raising, for those who permit a chick and do not require the reason of preparation incidentally to its mother would permit even where she stands for raising. The Tur should not have ruled here plainly that if it stands for raising it is forbidden. Shaar HaMelech asked this as well. It appears to me by first considering Tosafot in the sugya, who ask why it does not answer that it was prepared because he could have inserted his hand into the animal's womb and slaughtered it. They answer that Rabbi Oshaya was uncertain in Chullin whether we say that the Torah made four signs valid, or not. They further answered that initially one certainly may not slaughter it in its mother's womb, for it is no better than slaughtering at night; see there. At first glance, even without their question they need their answer because of another question. Even according to Rabbi Yehudah it is difficult from a calf, for the reasoning that it permits itself through slaughter applies only to a chick, where birth causes the signs to become fit for slaughter, and therefore also causes the permission from muktzeh. But with a calf that was born, how does permitting itself through slaughter apply, since even before birth it was fit in its mother? This requires the explanation above.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>It appears in my humble opinion.</small> A practical difference between the reason of the Rosh and the reason of the Ran is with one born from an animal that stands for raising offspring. According to the Ran, there is no preparation, for in any event he prepared it for himself: if he slaughters for a sick person, it is permitted for a healthy person raw, since on Shabbos we rule like Rabbi Shimon that muktzeh is permitted, and even nolad is permitted according to many poskim. But according to the Rosh it is forbidden. The root of the Ran and Rosh depends on the two explanations the Ramban wrote in Milchamos regarding an egg born on Yom Tov after Shabbos being forbidden because of preparation: even if it had not been completed and could not have been laid today, he could have eaten it by slaughtering the hen; nevertheless, now without slaughtering, he needs yesterday's completion so that the egg come to him through today's laying. Or, it is better and improved in taste through laying. It seems that the main reasoning of the Rosh is that with a calf, which is not improved more through birth, it does not need birth at all, for even without birth he could eat it the next day on Yom Tov by slaughtering the mother, and the birth does not function more to be called preparation. This is only where she stands for eating. But where she stands for raising, even according to the poskim that muktzeh is permitted on Yom Tov and if he slaughtered her she would be permitted, since she stands for raising and he does not want to slaughter her, he needs the birth, and it is preparation. The Ran follows his view, as is clear from his words regarding an egg born on one day being forbidden on the other, that he holds like the first reason of the Ramban, for now he did not slaughter her. This also applies to a calf; therefore he needed the reason of preparation incidentally to its mother.
