<b>א) [סעיף א']  אין מראין סכין לחכם וכו'  </b>כמו שהיו נוהגין בזמן חכמי הגמ' שלא לשחוט בהמה בסכין עד שהראו הסכין לחכם לראות אם ראוי לשחוט בו שאינו פגום לבוש. אמנם האידנא נוהגין בכל גלות ישראל שאין מאמינים לקצבים וממנים אנשים ידועים על השחיטה ועל הבדיקה ולהם מחלו חכמים כבודם כי הם זריזים וזהירים ומתוך כך נתבטל בדיקת החכם לגמרי גם למי ששוחט לביתו כמ"ש הטור ביו"ד סי' ח"י בשם אביו הרא"ש ז"ל ופסקו הש"ע שם סעי' י"ז יעו"ש. וזהו שכתב מור"ם ז"ל כאן בהגה ועכשיו בזה"ז שכל שוחט וכו' והוא לפי דברי הרא"ש הנ"ז ומיהו צריך לבדוק בישוב הדעת הרבה ויראת שמים על פניו וכמ"ש האחרונים שם תוכחת מגולה ע"ז ואכמ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] A knife is not shown to a scholar, etc. </b>This follows the practice in the era of the Sages of the Talmud, when an animal was not slaughtered with a knife until the knife was shown to a scholar to determine whether it was fit for slaughter and free of a nick. Levush. Nowadays, however, the custom throughout the Jewish Diaspora is not to trust butchers. Designated people are appointed over slaughter and inspection, and the Sages waived their honor for them because these appointees are diligent and careful. Consequently, inspection by a scholar was entirely discontinued, even for one who slaughters for his own household, as the Tur writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 18, in the name of his father, the Rosh. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly there, se'if 17. See there. This is the meaning of Rama's statement here in the gloss, 'Nowadays, when every shochet, etc.' It follows the aforementioned position of the Rosh. Nevertheless, the inspection must be performed with great deliberation and with fear of Heaven upon one's face, as the later authorities there wrote in their explicit admonitions regarding this matter. This is not the place to elaborate.

<b>ה) וכתב  </b>עו"ש השו"ג דלפעמים נראה בסכין כעין פגימה ומחדד אותו על כף ידו ברוק כמעשהו בחול ע"ג המשחזת וזה בא לסכין מחמת ששחט בו בעיו"ט ונשאר לחלוחית הדם או הרוק על גביו ובעבור עליו הלילה נתייבש הדם או הרוק ומראה כעין פגימה וכשמחדד הסכין ברוק על כף היד נתרכך אותו דם ועובר משם ואז יוכל להבחין אם הוא פגימה או לא וחידוד כה"ג שרי דלא אסרו לחדדה אלא במשחזת שלה אבל לחדדה ע"ג העץ או חרס או אבן שרי כדלקמן סי' תק"ט סעי' ב' וכ"ש בכה"ג עכ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) Shulchan Gavoah further writes there </b>that at times something resembling a nick appears on a knife, and the shochet smooths it against the palm of his hand with saliva, as he would ordinarily do against a sharpening stone. This appearance comes upon the knife because he slaughtered with it on erev Yom Tov and some moisture from the blood or saliva remained upon it. When the night passes, the blood or saliva dries and appears like a nick. When he smooths the knife with saliva against the palm of his hand, that blood softens and comes off, after which he can determine whether it is truly a nick. Smoothing in this manner is permitted. The prohibition against sharpening applies only to its sharpening stone, but sharpening against wood, earthenware, or a stone is permitted, as stated below, siman 509, se'if 2. It is certainly permitted in this case. These are his words.

<b>ו) ובענין  </b>השפשוף שנהגו השוחטים לשפשף הסכין ע"ג היד או הזרוע אם נמצא בו פגימה אחר שחיטה ואם הלך הפגם אז מכשירים עיין להרב חכ"א כלל ג' או' י"א שהחמיר מאד לאסור בזה וכתב שכן הסכים הגאון בית מאיר ז"ל ואנשי עירו יעו"ש וכ"כ הגאון מהרד"ח בתו"ז סי' פ"ט או' מ"ג משם ברית אברהם סי' י"ב שדעתו לאסור אלא שהוא ז"ל כתב דנהגו להכשיר אלא שצריך להיות בנחת ולא בכח יעו"ש והב"ד הרב זב"צ על יו"ד סי' ח"י או' י"ט וכתב שכן נוהגין בעיר בגדאד יע"א להתיר יעו"ש ונ"מ למקום שנהגו היתר בזה ואירע כזה ביו"ט דמותר לשפשף מטעם הנז' שכתב השו"ג כיון שאינו כ"א ע"ג היד ועוד כיון שהוא לצורך אוכל נפש כדי להכשיר הבהמה או העוף לצורך יו"ט.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>6) Regarding </b>the rubbing practiced by shochtim, in which they rub the knife against the hand or arm when a nick is found after slaughter and declare the animal valid if the nick disappears, see Chochmas Adam, rule 3, subsection 11. He rules very stringently that this is forbidden and writes that the gaon Beis Meir and the people of his city agreed. See there. The gaon Rabbi David Chazan likewise writes in Toras Zevach, siman 89, subsection 43, in the name of Bris Avraham, siman 12, who rules that it is forbidden. Rabbi David Chazan himself writes, however, that the custom is to declare it valid, provided the rubbing is done gently and not forcefully. See there. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh Deah, siman 18, subsection 19, cites this and writes that the custom in the city of Baghdad, may God protect it, is to permit it. See there. This has practical application in a place where the permissive custom is followed and such a case occurs on Yom Tov. It is permitted to rub the knife for the reason given by Shulchan Gavoah, since this is done only against the hand. Moreover, it is needed for food preparation, to render the animal or bird valid for Yom Tov.

<b>ז) שם  </b>שמא תהיה פגומא וכו' כ"כ הרמב"ם פ"ד. מיהו יש עוד פירושים והביאם ב"י דמפירש"י משמע דוקא טבח העשוי למכור בשר באטליז אסור להראות סכינו ביו"ט דאוושא מילתא אבל לשאר כל אדם מותר ולבה"ג משום דחיישינן דלמא אזיל חוץ לתחום ולהרז"ה משום דהו"ל כעין ראיית מומין של בכור ולעצמו מיהא שרי יעו"ש. וכתב שם המ"מ וטעמו של הרמב"ם נראה עיקר יעו"ש והב"ד ג"כ בב"י ולכן פסק כן בש"ע. וגם הרמב"ן במלחמו' הזכיר פי' זה של הרמב"ם ואמר שהוא נכון מכל הפירושים והב"ד הב"ח. מיהו השכנה"ג בהגב"י או' א' כתב דלענין מעשה יש לחוש לכל הטעמים ואפי' אינו טבח ואפי' אינו ע"ה אין מראין סכין לחכם יעו"ש וכ"ה דעת הער"ה או' א' אבל הפר"ח הסכים לטעם הרמב"ם שכתב הש"ע. וכ"כ הלבוש כדברי הש"ע. וכ"כ החמ"מ או' א' ר"ז או' א' מק"ק סי' מ"ג או' א' ועיין באו' שאח"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>7) There. Lest it be nicked, etc. </b>Rambam, chapter 4, writes this. There are, however, other explanations, which Beis Yosef cites. Rashi's explanation implies that only a butcher who sells meat in a shop may not show his knife on Yom Tov, because the matter becomes public, but any other person may do so. According to Halachos Gedolos, the concern is that he might go beyond the techum. According to Razah, this resembles inspecting a firstborn animal for a blemish, but inspecting the knife for oneself is permitted. See there. Maggid Mishneh writes there that Rambam's explanation appears primary. Beis Yosef also cites him and therefore rules accordingly in Shulchan Aruch. Ramban, in Milchamos, likewise mentions Rambam's explanation and says that it is the most correct of all the explanations. Bach cites him. Nevertheless, Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 1, writes that in practice one should be concerned for all the reasons. A knife is therefore not shown to a scholar even by one who is not a butcher and not an am haaretz. See there. This is also the view of Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1. Pri Chadash, however, agrees with Rambam's reason as written by Shulchan Aruch. Levush likewise follows Shulchan Aruch, as do Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; and Mikraei Kodesh, siman 43, subsection 1. See the following subsection.

<b>18) There, in the gloss. He should inspect his knife on erev Yom Tov, etc. </b>He should conceal it in a place where it will not become nicked, and slaughter with it on Yom Tov or lend it to others. Levush. This implies that one may slaughter with it in reliance upon the inspection performed on erev Yom Tov. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, writes likewise. Although on a weekday it is forbidden to slaughter without first inspecting the knife in reliance upon inspecting it after slaughter, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 18, nevertheless, on Yom Tov it is permitted because no other course is possible. These are his words. Chayei Adam, rule 89, subsection 2, likewise writes that one relies upon this presumption and slaughters without inspection, specifically when it is a knife whose established nature is not to become nicked by itself. These are his words. Mateh Ephraim, siman 685, se'if 23, however, writes that when one comes to slaughter on Yom Tov he should nevertheless inspect the knife immediately before slaughter, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 18. There is then no concern that he might find it unsatisfactory and sharpen it. Since he inspected it on erev Yom Tov and concealed it, it will presumably be found satisfactory. His inspection is merely to address the remote possibility that it became nicked. We do not have so great a concern that he might find it unsatisfactory and also forget that it is Yom Tov and sharpen it. When he did not inspect it on erev Yom Tov, however, this concern does apply. See there. Zivchei Ratzon writes likewise, as do Likutei Rima there and Tosafos Chaim on Chayei Adam there, subsection 4. If he did not designate it on erev Yom Tov and inspect it thoroughly, he should not perform its initial inspection on Yom Tov except in pressing circumstances, as stated afterward.

<b>19) There, in the gloss. And not on Yom Tov, etc. </b>If the shochet appointed for the community did not inspect the knife on erev Yom Tov, he may inspect it on Yom Tov. There is no concern that he might find it nicked and sharpen it. This concern was stated only in earlier times, when the butchers themselves performed the slaughter. Now that designated people are appointed, they themselves stand in the place of the scholar, and regarding a scholar himself we do not suspect that he might sharpen it. Knesses HaGedolah on Yoreh Deah, siman 18. See above, subsection 1.
