<b>אע"ג דאיתליד בה ריעותא. </b> פי' ואתרע לה רובא דאמרינן בעלמא רוב בהמות בחזקת כשרות הם כן פרש"י בפ' המביא וא"כ כ"ש היכא ששכיח בהמות טריפות כמו כשירות דמות' לשחוט בי"ט כ"כ הב"י וק"ל למה התיר כאן בדאיכא ספיקא שתטרף ולא חיישי' שמא יבוא לידי שחיטה בחנם שהיא ודאי אסורה בי"ט ומ"ש מסימן תצ"ז ס"ט דיוני שובך כו' דבעי' זימון דוקא משום שמא ימצאו כחושים ויטלטל בחנם וכ"ש כאן דישחוט בחנם וכ"ש במקום דאתילד ריעותא. ונ"ל דשם ביונים כיון דאפשר בזימון מאתמול ואז לא יהיה ספק ביו"ט בזה הצריכו שיעשה כן. ודוגמא לזה אי' בתוס' פ' המביא דף ל"ג לר"א דדוקא גבי אכילה דאדם קובע לו מקום לאכילתו והיה לו להכין והכא נמי כן הוא כיון שידע אתמול ה"ל להכין משא"כ בבהמה שטובה יותר בשחיטה היום משחוטה בעי"ט ע"כ התירו לו אפילו במקום שיש ספק. אבל רש"ל כתב דבעי' לא אפשיטא וכתב הר"ן דאזלינן לחומרא וכ"כ א"ז במקום ששכיחי טריפות כמו כשירות וראוי לחוש לזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even though a defect has arisen in it.</b> Meaning: and its majority has been impaired. For we say in general that most animals are in a presumption of kashrus; so Rashi explained in chapter HaMevi. And if so, all the more so where treifah animals are as common as kosher ones, it is permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov; so wrote Beis Yosef. And it is difficult for me: why did he permit here where there is a doubt that it will be treifah, and we are not concerned that he may come to a slaughter for nothing, which is certainly forbidden on Yom Tov? And how does this differ from siman 497, se'if 9, regarding dovecote pigeons, etc., where we specifically require designation, lest they be found emaciated and he move them for nothing; and all the more so here that he would slaughter for nothing; and all the more so in a place where a defect has arisen. And it appears to me that there regarding the pigeons, since it is possible to designate from yesterday, and then there will be no doubt on Yom Tov itself, they required that he do so. And there is a parallel in Tosafos, chapter HaMevi, folio 33, according to R' Eliezer: specifically regarding eating, a person fixes a place for his eating and he should have prepared; and here too it is so, since he knew yesterday he should have prepared; which is not so regarding an animal, which is better when slaughtered today than when slaughtered on erev Yom Tov. Therefore they permitted him even where there is a doubt. But Maharshal wrote that we require that the matter not be unresolved, and the Ran wrote that we follow the stringency; and so wrote Or Zarua in a place where treifos are as common as kosher ones; and it is proper to be concerned for this.

<b>בכור בזמן הזה כו'. </b> טעם איסור ראיית המומין בי"ט כ' הרמב"ם פ"ב שהוא מפני המומין שנולדו בי"ט שאין שוחטין עליהן מדין מוקצה שהרי לא היה דעתו עליהן מעי"ט ומפני כך אסרו אף בנולדו מעי"ט כל שלא ראה אותם חכם מבערב וה' המגיד כ' שיש מי שנותן טעם שיבוא לטלטל לפעמים מה שאין ראוי לו כלומר שאם ימצא שאין המום מום נמצא שאין הבהמה ראוים ואי אפשר שלא יטלטלנה ויש מי שכתב שמחזי כמתקן ע"כ. וא"ל על הטעם הא' של ה' המגיד דאם כן יהיו כל ההוראות אסורים לחכם לי"ט דשמא יהיה אסור ויטלטל באיסור הא ל"ק דדוקא גבי בכור אמרינן כן שלא היה לו חזקת היתר מעולם והא ראיה שאם נולד ומומו עמו מותר בדיעבד אם רואה החכם בי"ט מטעם שלא היה לו חזקת איסור והא דלא שרי לראות לכתחלה בזה משום דלא היה לו חזקת היתר בבירור כ"ז שלא ראהו החכם משא"כ בשאר הוראות שהיה מעיקרא חזקת היתר ונראה לכאור' דלפעמים יש איסור הורא' בענין שהי' אסו' מתחלתו כגון יתרת אבר שמראין לחכם אם הוא איסור או לאו אלא נרא' דג"ז אינו דומה לבכור דבכור הי' האיסו' מבורר קודם הולדת המום משא"כ בזה שמעולם לא היה האיסור ברור אלא ספק  אלא דבר זה יש ללמוד מבכור באם היה איסור מבורר כגון שנפל איסור לח בהיתר לח בפחות מס' או ביבש ביבש ואין רוב בהיתר ואח"כ נתוסף היתר ונתבטל האיסור דבר זה אסור להורות בי"ט דהוא ממש כבכור ואסור לחכם להורות בזה בי"ט כנלע"ד. וכתב רש"ל בפ' אין צדין דשרי לחכם לראות הבכור ולשתוק ולא להתירו אפי' גמר בלבו שהוא מום דדברים שבלב אינם דברים לענין איסורי שבת וי"ט ע"כ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A firstborn nowadays, etc.</b> The reason for the prohibition of examining blemishes on Yom Tov: the Rambam wrote in chapter 2 that it is because of blemishes that arose on Yom Tov, upon which one may not slaughter by the law of muktzeh, for he had no intention upon them from erev Yom Tov; and for that reason they forbade even regarding those that arose before Yom Tov, whenever a scholar had not seen them from the evening. And the Maggid Mishneh wrote that there is one who gives the reason that he may sometimes come to move what is not fit for him, that is to say, if he finds that the blemish is not a [valid] blemish, the animal is not fit, and it is impossible that he will not move it; and there is one who wrote that it appears like repairing; end quote. And do not object to the Maggid Mishneh's first reason that if so all rulings by a scholar on Yom Tov would be forbidden, lest it be forbidden and he move in a forbidden way: that is not difficult, for specifically regarding a firstborn we say so, since it never had a presumption of permissibility; and the proof is that if it was born with its blemish, it is permitted after the fact if the scholar sees it on Yom Tov, because it never had a presumption of prohibition; and that we do not permit examining it l'chatchila in that case is because it did not clearly have a presumption of permissibility so long as the scholar had not seen it; which is not so regarding other rulings, where from the outset there was a presumption of permissibility. And it appears at first glance that sometimes there is a prohibition of ruling in a matter that was forbidden from the start, for example an extra limb that they show to a scholar whether it is a prohibition or not; but it seems that this too is not comparable to a firstborn, for regarding a firstborn the prohibition was clear before the blemish arose, which is not so here, where the prohibition was never clear, only a doubt. But one may learn this from a firstborn: if there was a clear prohibition, for example a moist prohibition that fell into moist permissible [food] in less than sixty, or dry into dry without a majority of the permissible, and afterward more permissible was added and the prohibition was nullified: this one may not rule on Yom Tov, for it is exactly like a firstborn, and a scholar may not rule on this on Yom Tov, as it appears to me. And Maharshal wrote in chapter Ein Tzadin that it is permitted for a scholar to examine the firstborn and be silent and not permit it, even if he has concluded in his heart that it is a [valid] blemish, for matters of the heart are not matters regarding the prohibitions of Shabbos and Yom Tov; end quote.

<b>יכולים לשחטה. </b> משום דאי אפשר לכזית בשר בלא שחיטה ואפי' יש להם שתים כתב הרא"ש הטעם לפי שאינם שוות. וכ' ב"י  אע"פ שהם שוות בשום דבר אם אינם שוות בכל ענין א"א למיפלגינהו ומ"מ יש לתמוה דמשמע שאם הם שוות לגמרי אסור. ואמאי הא אף ע"ג דאפשר למפלגינהו מחיים שמא אינו רוצה לשחוט לצורכו בהמה שלימה שא"צ אלא לחציה ואם תהיה כולה שלו יפסיד הבשר ושמא י"ל שלא כ"כ הרא"ש אלא בשוחט את שתיהן בי"ט דאילו בשוחט א' מהן אע"פ שהן שוות לגמרי שרי מטעמא דפרישי' ע"כ ולע"ד ל"נ היתר זה דאי' בגמ' שהעיס' חצי' של עכו"ם וחצי' של ישראל אסור לאפות' בי"ט דהא אפשר למיפלג' בליש' וכתבו התו' וא"ת הא אמרי' לעיל ממלאה אשה תנור פת ואע"פי שא"צ אלא לפת א' מפני שהפת נאפה יפה בזמן שהתנור מלא ושרי לאפות בי"ט אפילו לכתחלה. וי"ל דשאני התם דכל הפת של ישראל שהרשות בידו לאכול כל א' וא' או זה או זה אבל הכא חציה של עכו"ם אסור לאפות בי"ט עכ"ל. הרי מפורש דכל שהוא ראוי לחלוקה דאין היתר בשביל התועלת המגיע לו מחלקו של עכו"ם כיון שאין לו רשות לאכול חלק העכו"ם אם ירצם ה"נ אין מועיל מה שמגיע לו הנאה מחלק העכו"ם במה שלא יפסיד בשר שלו כיון שעכ"פ אין לו יכולת לאכול חלק העכו"ם אם ירצם נמצא ששוחט באיסור חלק העכו"ם כיון שאפשר בחלוקה כיון שהם שוות לגמרי זה נ"ל ברור בס"ד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They may slaughter it.</b> Because it is impossible to have a kezayis of meat without slaughter; and even if they have two [animals], the Rosh wrote the reason is that they are not equal. And Beis Yosef wrote: even though they are equal in some matter, if they are not equal in every respect it is impossible to divide them. Nevertheless one may wonder, for it implies that if they are completely equal it is forbidden. And why? Even though it is possible to divide them while alive, perhaps he does not want to slaughter for his need a whole animal, since he needs only half of it, and if the whole becomes his he will lose the meat. And perhaps one may say that the Rosh wrote so only when he slaughters both of them on Yom Tov; but if he slaughters one of them, even though they are completely equal, it is permitted for the reason I explained; end quote. And in my humble opinion this leniency does not appear [correct], for it is stated in the Gemara that dough half of which belongs to a non-Jew and half to a Jew is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov, for it is possible to divide it in the kneading; and Tosafos wrote: And if you say, behold we said above that a woman fills an oven with bread even though she needs only one loaf, because bread bakes well when the oven is full, and it is permitted to bake on Yom Tov even l'chatchila. One may answer that that case is different, for all the bread belongs to the Jew, and he has authority to eat each one, this or that; but here half belongs to the non-Jew, and it is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov; end quote. Thus it is explicit that wherever it is fit for division, there is no leniency on account of the benefit that comes to him from the non-Jew's share, since he has no right to eat the non-Jew's share if he wishes. Likewise here, what he gains from the non-Jew's share in that he will not lose his own meat does not avail, since in any case he has no ability to eat the non-Jew's share if he wishes; so he is slaughtering the non-Jew's share in a forbidden way, since division is possible when they are completely equal. This appears clear to me, with Heaven's help.

<b>He should not slaughter a wild animal, etc.</b> We will briefly copy the sugya. In the Mishnah it is taught: One who slaughters a wild animal or a bird: Beis Shammai say he may dig with a digging-stick and cover; and Beis Hillel say he should not slaughter unless he had prepared earth ready; and they agree that if he [already] slaughtered, he should dig with a digging-stick and cover. And we say in the Gemara on "and they agree that if he slaughtered," etc.: Rav Yehudah said: and that is provided he had a digging-stick stuck in from before Yom Tov. But he is performing crushing! (The Gemara objects: he said "provided he had a digging-stick stuck in," so the Mishnah's reason is not that the positive commandment of "and he shall cover it with earth" overrides the negative commandment against any melacha, but because it is already dug and standing; yet he is performing crushing, for they are still clods and unfit for covering unless he crushes them.) Rav said: with soft earth. But he is making a depression! And it answers: since he needs only its earth, he is exempt for it. And it is further stated there in the first chapter, objecting to this Mishnah that Beis Shammai hold to leniency and Beis Hillel to stringency, yet we hear of them the opposite: R' Yochanan said the attribution of the views is reversed. Rashi explained that that other Mishnah in which Beis Shammai are lenient is reversed, and this one of "one who slaughters" stands; and so the Rif holds for practical law like Beis Hillel toward stringency: one may not slaughter a bird unless there is prepared earth, and after the fact a stuck digging-stick and soft earth avail; and so the Shulchan Aruch ruled here. But Rabbeinu Tam explained that the reversed attribution is of this "one who slaughters": Beis Shammai to stringency and Beis Hillel to leniency, and prepared earth is not required. And therefore Rabbeinu Tam wrote: it is astonishing to me why the world required preparation. And I remember that in my youth they did not require it at all. And Tosafos wrote on this in the name of R' Yitzchak: However this is not so, for according to his explanation as well, at the least Beis Hillel hold what Beis Shammai originally held, and Beis Shammai require a stuck digging-stick, and even with soft earth they require preparation, from what is said here: Beis Shammai say there only where there is a stuck digging-stick, etc.; end quote. And Semag wrote: My teacher wrote that where he has already slaughtered and has no prepared earth, he may cover with what is not prepared, as we say "and they agree that if he slaughtered he should dig with a digging-stick and cover"; and Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that this is difficult, for the Gemara establishes "and that is provided he had a digging-stick stuck in from before Yom Tov"; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote on this: And it appears to me that the reason of Semag's teacher was that he relied on Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at least after the fact. And Maharshal wrote in the first chapter against Beis Yosef: and he erred a great error from either side: if he holds Rabbeinu Tam's explanation that Beis Hillel permit l'chatchila even without a stuck digging-stick, and the Gemara required a stuck digging-stick only for the view that permits after the fact, then even l'chatchila, etc.; end quote. And I wondered that he attributed error to our master Beis Yosef in such upright words: for certainly according to Rabbeinu Tam Beis Hillel permit even l'chatchila without a stuck digging-stick; but we are in doubt whether the halacha follows Rabbeinu Tam or Rashi and the Rif. Therefore Semag's teacher held that l'chatchila one should rule like Rashi toward stringency and specifically require prepared earth; but after the fact we rely on Rabbeinu Tam. And what R' Yitzchak wrote on Rabbeinu Tam, that in any case we require a stuck digging-stick as Rav said: it appears that Rabbeinu Tam does not hold so; rather, since Beis Hillel hold to leniency, that even l'chatchila one may slaughter and dig and cover, they hold that the positive commandment of "and he shall cover it with earth" overrides the negative commandment "you shall not do any melacha." For Rashi wrote that specifically according to Rav Yehudah we do not hold this override, since he required a stuck digging-stick. And now R' Yitzchak's words apply to Beis Shammai according to Rabbeinu Tam, who permit only after the fact; then automatically there is no positive overriding a negative, and a stuck digging-stick is needed; but for Beis Hillel according to Rabbeinu Tam, certainly a positive overrides a negative, and it is permitted l'chatchila in every way. And even though on folio 8 we say that it does not override a negative and a positive, Tosafos wrote that Rava does not hold that there is a positive and a negative on Yom Tov, and R' Yitzchak holds like Rava. And what we say, "Beis Shammai say so only where there is a stuck digging-stick," that was before we said the attribution is reversed; but according to the conclusion, whoever is lenient is lenient even in every way for the reason we said. Thus Semag's teacher properly holds like Rabbeinu Tam after the fact, that one may cover even without preparation and needs no stuck digging-stick from before Yom Tov at all, but only as the Mishnah implies: if he slaughtered he should dig with a digging-stick and cover, meaning even without a stick stuck from before Yom Tov. And R' Yitzchak who establishes it as a stuck digging-stick is according to the view that a positive does not override a negative, since he permitted only after the fact; but Beis Hillel truly hold that he digs and covers, as we say in the Gemara according to Beis Shammai before the reversal: one who comes to ask is told, l'chatchila slaughter and dig and cover. But we for practical law do not rule as lenient as Rabbeinu Tam except after the fact, relying on him, as we also find in the Gemara a distinction between l'chatchila and after the fact, as taught "and they agree," etc. So it appears to me to explain Semag's teacher's view according to Rabbeinu Tam, not like the Rosh and Tur according to Rabbeinu Tam who also require a stuck digging-stick and soft earth. I saw other resolutions on this, and for hatred of length I wrote only what appears correct to me. And so it is somewhat implied from Semak, who wrote: if he slaughtered and did not find fine earth that is not prepared, he covers it, etc.; end quote, implying without any stuck digging-stick. And since Semag with his teacher and Semak agreed to this, it appears in a pressing need to rely on them so as not to nullify the joy of Yom Tov, and to take earth that comes to hand even if it is not prepared at all, to cover with it, if he has no stove ash that was heated from erev Yom Tov.

The meaning of his words is that there are two clauses here, and so it appears to me: namely at first it is permitted even to cover, and at the end regarding his roof he wrote that it is permitted specifically after the fact because it is muktzeh, and even so it is permitted after the fact, for we rely on Rabbeinu Tam according to what I wrote in our words.

And if so, all is well: at first he wrote at the end of chapter 2 that if he pulled it out on Yom Tov, that is after the fact, and the depression was already made, it is permitted to cover with it, meaning even l'chatchila he may slaughter after the pulling out and may move it for all his needs; but if it has not yet been pulled out, he should not l'chatchila slaughter with intent to pull it out and cover, for he will make a depression, and that is forbidden l'chatchila even though he needs only its earth; but if he already slaughtered he may even pull it out, and we are not concerned for the depression since he is exempt for it. And now he properly said at the beginning of chapter 3: one who had prepared earth and ash fit to move; included in this is also a stuck digging-stick already mentioned that was pulled out on Yom Tov; then l'chatchila he slaughters and relies on it. But if he has no earth and ash fit to move, meaning he also has no digging-stick at all (for if he had one it would be fit to move so long as he is not making a depression), then certainly he has no stuck digging-stick at all; in that case he should not cover until evening even if he transgressed and slaughtered. <b>And if so,</b> what is written here regarding the law of mixed bloods, and he has prepared earth: included in this is also a stuck digging-stick, namely if it was already pulled out and he brought up the earth; in that case it is permitted with one digging, implying that if it was not yet pulled out and he transgressed and slaughtered a wild animal and a bird and the bloods mixed, even one digging is forbidden, since he must make a depression, and making a depression after an after-the-fact slaughter was permitted only when he slaughtered a wild animal and a bird alone, not with a mixture. This is the Rambam's view correctly, with Heaven's help. <b>And the Tur</b> wrote on these words of the Rambam: And it appears that if he can cover it with one digging, since he has already slaughtered it is permitted, even if he has no prepared earth, provided he has a stuck digging-stick and soft earth; and if he has prepared earth he may cover it even with many diggings; end quote. He disagrees with the Rambam on two points: one, that after the fact he may cover also with a mixture even if it was not yet pulled out to earth; and where he has prepared earth it is permitted even with many diggings; whereas according to the Rambam that is forbidden here because it is toil for him, and he holds like Rashi as written, that from the aspect of toil it is forbidden with a mixture. And accordingly it is difficult at first glance: if so, why do we need the reason of a decree because of its chelev; behold it is forbidden because of toil as with a mixture? One may answer that regarding a koi specifically we are not concerned about toil, since there is no clear side of prohibition, only a doubt; which is not so with a mixture, where there is a clear side of prohibition, for in any case there is certainly blood of a domestic animal there; therefore the toil is forbidden. And accordingly, in what was said "not only regarding a koi did they say," meaning there the prohibition is from the decree of chelev, which implies that if not for this decree there would be no prohibition: that is not so, for even with a mixture there is in any case a prohibition because of toil. But the Tur is not satisfied with this, for one must equate the reason of mixtures with the reason of a koi, as the language implies: "not only regarding a koi did they say," etc.; and if so toil cannot apply in both, for regarding a koi it is proven that we are not concerned for toil; rather certainly from the aspect of prohibition, and likewise with mixtures from the aspect of prohibition, namely the prohibition of what is not prepared; if so, what is prepared is permitted even with a mixture. And now it is well that there is no dispute in the Gemara as one must say according to Rashi as we mentioned. <b>And still</b> clarification is needed regarding what is stated in the Gemara on this and in the poskim, that a mixture is forbidden with two diggings, implying that without a mixture many diggings are permitted; and so it is written in a manuscript; and the Rama already mentioned it in siman 14. And it is astonishing: why should this be permitted; how does it differ from where there was no stuck digging-stick, which was forbidden; and how does it differ if there is a stuck digging-stick but the earth in it does not suffice even for a bird alone without a mixture, and now he comes to dig with the stick anew: this is as if there was none at all, both from the aspect of preparation and from the aspect of digging or making a depression. And it appears to me that certainly in every place an additional digging on Yom Tov is forbidden; but here regarding a mixture we say that even if he had many sticks stuck from erev Yom Tov, which would permit covering a bird alone with many diggings, with a mixture only one digging was permitted; since regarding a bird alone it is permitted only after the fact even with one digging, automatically with a mixture we do not rely to be lenient with many diggings, since he is making an additional digging for the domestic animal; as appears correct to me. <b>And I saw</b> Maharshal on this, and it appears to me that where one digging will not suffice for him, then he should not cover at all, even with one digging, lest he not cover the blood that is obligated in covering, and he turns out to toil on Yom Tov unnecessarily; end quote. And so wrote my master, my father-in-law of blessed memory. And in my humble opinion this prohibition is not compelling at all, for we hold in Yoreh De'ah, siman 28, that one need not cover all the blood; rather we expound "its blood," even part of its blood. And if so here, where the bloods were mixed together, there is no room here for a split to say that perhaps in one place blood of a wild animal or bird alone remained without blood of a domestic animal, for everything was mixed and there is no portion of one without the other. And know this, for we hold regarding prohibitions in mixtures not wet that it is nullified in sixty; and why should we not be concerned that the prohibition remained in one place by itself? And if so, likewise here in every portion there is in any case blood of a wild animal or bird, and it is proper to cover part of its blood. And also one who slaughters a wild animal alone, and one digging does not suffice to cover all the blood, and he had only one stuck digging-stick: would it enter your mind that he should not cover at all? Certainly whatever he can cover with one digging he must at least cover; likewise so it is with a mixture. This appears clear and correct to me, with Heaven's help.
