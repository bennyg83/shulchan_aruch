<b>א) [סעיף א'] ביום שני וכו'  </b>ויש לעשות ביום שני בסעודה איזה דבר זכר לסעודת אסתר שביום ההוא נתלה המן. של"ה. מ"א ריש הסי' ח"י או' א' וסי' וישבות המן ממחרת הפסח. א"א. ומ"ש שביום ההוא נתלה המן כ"ה בפי' רש"י למגילה דף ט"ו וט"ז ע"א יעו"ש וכ"ה בפרקי דר"א פ"ג ובמדרש אסתר אלא דמ"ש רש"י שם דהתענית היה י"ד ט"ו ע"ז מבואר שם בפדר"א ומד"ר דהתענית היה י"ג י"ד ט"ו יעו"ש. ולפ"ז ויהי ביום השלישי יבא כפשוטו שלישי לתענית וא"צ לדחוק ולפרש ג' למשלוח הרצים כמ"ש רש"י שם יעו"ש ודוק.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] On the second day, etc. </b>At the meal on the second day, it is proper to do something in remembrance of Esther's feast, because Haman was hanged on that day. Shelah; Magen Avraham, at the beginning of the siman; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1. The allusion is in the verse, 'The manna ceased on the day after Pesach.' Eshel Avraham. The statement that Haman was hanged on that day appears in Rashi's commentary to Megillah 15a and 16a, and in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer, chapter 3, and Midrash Esther. Rashi writes there that the fast occurred on the fourteenth and fifteenth, whereas Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer and Midrash Rabbah state that it occurred on the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth. Accordingly, the phrase, 'It happened on the third day,' has its straightforward meaning, the third day of the fast, and need not be interpreted as the third day after the couriers were sent, as Rashi explains there. See there and analyze carefully.

<b>5) It is very proper </b>to study the passage of the omer on the sixteenth of Nissan. It appears in parashas Emor, from, 'Havayah spoke,' and, 'Speak to the Children of Israel,' through, 'And bread,' and so forth; and in parashas Vayikra, from, 'If you bring a meal-offering of first produce,' through, 'A fire-offering to Havayah.' One should afterward say the prayer beginning, 'Master,' and so forth, asking that it be regarded as though the omer was offered at its proper time. Hagahos Yafeh Nof; Pis'chei Olam, subsection 1. Before saying, 'Master of the worlds,' one may also recite chapter 10 of Menachos, beginning, 'Rabbi Yishmael says: The omer,' and so forth. See above, siman 429, subsection 6.

<b>9) Regarding </b>Mizmor LeSodah, see the gloss above at the end of siman 51 and our words there, with the help of Heaven. Regarding wearing tefillin on Chol HaMoed, see above, siman 31, se'if 2, and our words there. Regarding when those who wear tefillin on Chol HaMoed remove them, see the gloss above at the end of siman 25. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2, writes that in accordance with Rema, the established custom is to remove them only before Mussaf, and this should not be changed. See there. Chok Yosef, subsection 7, writes likewise. Although on Chol HaMoed Sukkos the custom is to remove tefillin before Hallel, on Pesach the custom follows Rema in siman 25 and they are removed before Mussaf. Sukkos differs because the esrog and lulav are taken, and the tefillin should not form an interruption. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, however, writes that people customarily remove tefillin before Hallel on both Pesach and Sukkos. See there. Yeshuos Yaakov, subsection 2, writes likewise, but states that his own custom on the first day of Chol HaMoed Pesach is to wait until after reading the passage of Kadesh, which contains the verse, 'You shall observe this statute,' and so forth. See there.
