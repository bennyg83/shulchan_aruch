**** ENGLISH ****
Chol HaMoed (Intermediate Days): The Ma’ariv, Shacharit, and Mincha prayers follow the usual weekday format. Ya’aleh V’Yavo is added in the Avodah section of the Amidah. If one forgets to say Ya’aleh V’Yavo, they must repeat the Amidah. It is also recited in Birkat HaMazon, but if omitted, one does not need to repeat it. Mussaf follows the format of Yom Tov Mussaf. {Rama: Gloss: When reaching "על ידי משה עבדך כאמור" (Through Moses, Your servant, as it is stated...), one recites "והקרבתם עולה וגו’" (And you shall offer a burnt offering...) until "ושני תמידים כהלכתם" (And two daily offerings as prescribed) (Tur).}

**** ENGLISH ****
A mnemonic to remember the Torah readings for the eight days of Pesach: "משך תורא קדש בכספא פסל במדברא שלח בוכרא" This order only changes when Pesach begins on Thursday, in which case the third day (which falls on Shabbat) has a special reading: "ראה אתה אומר אלי", corresponding to פסל. On the first three days, the readings remain קדש בכספא במדברא.

**** ENGLISH ****
Shabbat that Falls on Chol HaMoed: The Ma’ariv, Shacharit, and Mincha prayers follow the usual Shabbat format, with Ya’aleh V’Yavo added in the Avodah section. In Mussaf, one follows the order for Chol HaMoed but includes Shabbat: "ותתן לנו את יום המנוח הזה ויום חג המצות הזה" (And You have given us this day of rest and this festival of Matzot). The conclusion is: "מקדש השבת וישראל והזמנים" (Who sanctifies the Shabbat, Israel, and the festivals). The Haftarah is "היתה עלי". {Rama: Gloss: The blessing on the Haftarah does not mention Pesach, neither in the middle nor at the end. It is customary to read Shir HaShirim on Shabbat Chol HaMoed. If Shabbat falls on the last day of Yom Tov, Shir HaShirim is read then instead. Similarly, on Sukkot, Kohelet is read, and on Shavuot, Ruth is read (Abudraham). The common custom is not to recite a blessing on the reading of these Megillot, neither "על מקרא Megillah" nor "על מקרא כתובים".}
