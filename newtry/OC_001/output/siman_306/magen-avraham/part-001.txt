**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is called day labor.</b> So too the Bedek HaBayis wrote in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, and he wrote concerning it that this does not appear from the words of the poskim. Perhaps he did not see the words of the Ran, who wrote that the reason is that if he dismisses him in the middle of the week, he need not pay him for the whole week, but only for the days that passed. Therefore he is called a day laborer, and it constitutes wages for Shabbos. This is also evident from the Gemara. The same applies to one who rents a room to his fellow. It appears to me that the same applies to those who lend with interest, that they must lend in such a manner that if he repays in the middle of the week, he pays for the whole week, or pays nothing at all, and they should not calculate by the days. For even regarding a rebellious spouse, whom they penalized that as long as he remains rebellious he gives such-and-such per day, nevertheless it is forbidden to give the portion for Shabbos, for that is wages for Shabbos. The same applies to every comparable penalty. But if they reduce from the debt such-and-such each day, that is not called wages for Shabbos in such a case. So it appears from the Gemara in the fifth chapter of Kesubos.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And to pledge charity.</b> Although one may not consecrate, as written in siman 339, seif 4, that refers to a specific object, which appears like buying and selling. But they did not forbid obligating oneself by speech to the sacred. However, the Ran writes that this is difficult, for when one vows his own valuation he also obligates himself, and that is forbidden. See the Beit Yosef, who answers this. According to him, it is forbidden to consecrate a plate on Shabbos for the synagogue. But the Kol Bo wrote that only consecration for the altar or for Temple maintenance is forbidden, since it leaves his domain and enters the domain of the Most High; however, one who vows a Torah scroll or a crown is permitted, because they have not yet designated any particular item that leaves their possession. It appears to me that his intent is that our consecrations are non-sacred property, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 95, and all Israel has a share in them. If so, it has not left his domain, since the one who consecrates also has a share in it. This appears correct, unlike the Beit Yosef.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To pledge charity.</b> Nevertheless, it requires study: if the chazzan is wealthy, this is not considered charity. One must say that since they give it to him so that he will pray, it is a mitzvah need, similar to pledging money to build a synagogue. The Yam Shel Shlomo, fifth chapter of Beitzah, wrote that announcing mitzvos, that whoever gives more will merit them, resembles buying and selling more strongly, and it would have seemed forbidden, unless we say that buying and selling does not apply to a mitzvah matter, but only to an object being acquired. All this is only to justify the custom with difficulty. But purchasing seats in the synagogue, and likewise purchasing the esrogim from the congregation after their mitzvah is completed, is forbidden. During the time of their mitzvah he does not acquire the esrog itself, but only the right to fulfill the mitzvah with it, even on the first day, for he must return it. But to buy an esrog or a shofar on Yom Tov appears to me forbidden; see siman 586. The clearest practice in my eyes is like the custom of the pious men of Austria, that whatever one verbally undertook to give, he gives to charity even if his fellow acquired the mitzvah. If so, it is like fixing sums for the sake of a mitzvah and is not comparable to buying and selling at all. It is therefore permitted even to purchase seats and houses from charity funds, but every G-d-fearing person should be careful about this. The Shiltei Gibborim, in Avodah Zarah, also questions the purchase of mitzvos, and in tractate Bava Kamma he wrote that a person may sell his mitzvos to another. Buying and selling, whether verbally or through transfer, is forbidden as a decree lest one write, as the Rambam states in chapter 23. The Beit Yosef wrote in siman 527 that the Mordechai holds that it is forbidden to give an item as a gift to his fellow on Yom Tov or Shabbos unless it is for a mitzvah need. It is obvious that for Shabbos needs it is permitted, as written in simanim 323 and 516. So too it appears from Eruvin 71a, that acquiring rights on Shabbos is forbidden even without a kerchief acquisition. See Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 254. So too it appears from Gittin 77a, that even acquiring through taking possession is forbidden; see the Rosh there. The Ritva likewise wrote in Kesubos 4a concerning giving movable property as security for her kesubah, meaning that he gave it to her on Shabbos.

<b>כגון למדוד</b>. וה"ה למדוד חור אם יש בו פותח טפח והטומאה נכנסת שם אף על גב דיכול לצאת מהבית מ"מ כיון שמודד שיעורי תורה לא הוי עובדא דחול וכתבו התוס' ספי"ז דדוק' מדידה דלא הוי איסור כ"כ אלא משום דהוי כעובד' דחול ולהכי שרי במקום מצוה אבל מילי אחרינא לא כתב טח"מ סי' ע"ג בשם הרא"ש ז"ל ראובן נשבע לפרוע לשמעון מנה בז' בניסן וכו' אם לא פרעו ואירע אותו יום בשבת צריך ליתן לו משכון שוה מנה ביום השבת לקיים שבועתו דשוה כסף ככסף וחפצי שמים הוא שיקיים שבועתו וכ' הרב"י דאפשר לומר שאף הרא"ש לא אמר שיוצא ידי שבועתו בנתינת המשכון אלא כששמו אותה ונתנה לו בתורת שומא לפיכך הוצרך לבקש היתר משום דחפצי שמים הוא וכו' דאילו אינו נותנה בתורת פרעון משנה שלימה הוא ואם אינו מאמינו מניח טליתו וכו' אבל במ' נ"י ח"א כתב דאפי' נשבע לפורעו במעות ולא במשכון יתן לו משכון של כסף דשוה כסף ככסף עכ"ל הב"י ועפ"ז פסק בש"ע שם להתיר השומא והסמ"ע מתיר אף המדידה וצ"ע דהא הרא"ש מבי' ראיה מדשרי לשדך תינוק וללמדו אומנות ק"ו שמותר לפרוע בשבת לקיים שבועתו עכ"ל כלל ח' סי"ד וא"כ דיו לבא מן הדין להיות כנדון דהתם אסור להזכיר כמ"ש ס"ו גם בס"ג אסור להזכיר סכום והטעם כתב המ"מ לפי שאין בקציצה מצוה אלא הצלת ממונו עכ"ל, וא"כ ה"נ יתן לו מטלטלין שיודע בודאי ששוים יותר מחובו ולא יצטרך שומא ומדידה וגם הרא"ש כתב אח"כ וז"ל שוב אמרו שנשבע לתת מעות ולא משכון וכו' משמע דבתחלה אמר שיתן לו בתורת משכון והא דהוצרך לומר דחפצי שמים הוא היינו משום דאין פורעין בשבת ואפי' ליתן משכון אסור אם לא לצורך שבת כמ"ש ס"ו וסימן ש"ז סי"א ע"ש לכן נ"ל פשוט דשומא ומדידה אסור ועבי"ד סי' רל"ט ס"ו ונ"ל דאם יש לו מעות יתננה לו ע"י עכו"ם כמ"ש סי' ש"ז ס"ה ואם אין לו מעות יאמר לעכו"ם לשום כדאיתא בסנהדרין דף י"ד או יעשה כמ"ש סי' ת"ק שיאמר זה הכלי שוה כמו זה ולמחרת ישימו אותו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Such as to measure.</b> The same applies to measuring a hole to determine whether it has an opening of a handbreadth and impurity enters there. Although one could leave the house, nevertheless, since he is measuring Torah measurements, it is not considered weekday activity. Tosafot at the end of chapter 17 wrote that specifically measuring is permitted for a mitzvah need, because it is not much of a prohibition and is only forbidden because it resembles weekday activity; but other matters are not permitted. The Tur in Choshen Mishpat siman 73 wrote in the name of the Rosh that if Reuven swore to pay Shimon one hundred zuz on the seventh of Nissan, and if he did not pay him, and that day occurred on Shabbos, he must give him collateral worth one hundred zuz on Shabbos in order to fulfill his oath, for an item worth money is like money, and it is a matter of Heaven that he fulfill his oath. The Beit Yosef wrote that it is possible that even the Rosh said he fulfills his oath by giving collateral only when they assessed it and gave it as an assessed payment; therefore he needed to seek permission because it is a matter of Heaven. For if he does not give it as payment, this is an explicit Mishnah: if he does not trust him, he leaves his tallis, etc. But in the responsa of Mahari N. Y., part 1, he wrote that even if he swore to pay him in money and not with collateral, he should give him silver collateral, for an item worth money is like money. These are the words of the Beit Yosef. Based on this the Shulchan Aruch ruled there to permit the assessment, and the Sema permits even the measuring. This requires study, for the Rosh brings proof from the fact that one may arrange a match for a child and teach him a trade, saying that certainly it is permitted to pay on Shabbos in order to fulfill his oath. If so, it is enough for the derived case to be like the source, and there it is forbidden to mention the sum, as written in seif 6; also in seif 3 it is forbidden to mention an amount. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that the reason is that fixing a price is not itself a mitzvah, but only preserving his money. If so, here too he should give him movable items that he certainly knows are worth more than the debt, and he will not need assessment or measuring. The Rosh also wrote afterward that they again said that he had sworn to give money and not collateral, which implies that at first he said he should give it as collateral. The reason he needed to say that it is a matter of Heaven is because one may not pay debts on Shabbos, and even giving collateral is forbidden unless it is for a Shabbos need, as written in seif 6 and siman 307, seif 11; see there. Therefore it appears obvious to me that assessment and measuring are forbidden. See Yoreh Deah siman 239, seif 6. It appears to me that if he has money, he should give it to him through a non-Jew, as written in siman 307, seif 5; and if he has no money, he should tell a non-Jew to assess it, as stated in Sanhedrin 14a, or he should do as written in siman 500, saying, ‘This utensil is worth like this one,’ and tomorrow they will assess it.
