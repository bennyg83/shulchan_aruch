<b>One may not.</b> See the Taz, who wrote at length and brought the Mishnah in Pesachim 55b and the words of Beis Yosef, that according to the Tur, if she died one may not set another on Chol HaMoed; and according to him there are three levels: regarding one that fled, returning her within three days of her rebelling is the lightest of all, for it is easy to return her, and the eggs are damaged in part in any case even if she sat upon them only a day or two; and to set ab initio is the most stringent of all, for there is exertion in setting her and the eggs are not damaged; and if she died, to set another is intermediate: there is exertion like setting initially, for what is the difference, and some leniency in that there is loss of eggs. And accordingly he ruled here like the Tur, that on Chol HaMoed to set is forbidden, and to return her is permitted within three days of her rebelling, like Rav Huna there, for R' Ammi does not disagree about that, even if she sat only a day or two, for they are not entirely lost, and even for a minor loss they were concerned, like Rav Ammi in this (and below, if Hashem wills, it will be clarified whether because Chol HaMoed is rabbinic they were lenient in this, or for another reason); and if she died it is forbidden to set another. And ostensibly, according to Rav Huna, that after three days of her rebelling it is forbidden on Chol HaMoed, that is because if she died it is forbidden on Chol HaMoed with another: since the Mishnah did not teach the novelty on Chol HaMoed regarding one that died, it teaches that only returning is [permitted], but another is not; and the end of the latter clause, "if she died, etc.," applies to the fourteenth of Nisan; namely, even if she sat three days, for without having sat three days, even if she fled it is also forbidden to return her; and from this, three days of rebelling is like setting ab initio, which is forbidden. But according to R' Ammi, who disagrees and said that within three days of sitting we also return, and regarding this if she died another is forbidden because of a minor loss and great exertion, and likewise three days of rebelling in this is forbidden: yet where she sat three days and they are entirely lost, one may say that according to R' Ammi it is permitted when she died, and likewise to return in such a case one may say so; yet presumably R' Ammi does not disagree with Rav Huna at all. And further, dying is more stringent than fleeing, as Beis Yosef wrote, "all the more so when she died, etc.," for three days of rebelling do not involve as much exertion as dying and setting another. And the Taz raised three difficulties against this. And the Taz of blessed memory holds that when she fled within three days of rebelling it is more stringent than when she died, not like Beis Yosef who holds that dying is more stringent than fleeing within three days; and the Gemara that asks, "now that we permit setting, need one ask about fleeing," means that in that comparison fleeing is lighter than setting ab initio, etc. And the Rama in the gloss [rules] like the Rambam of blessed memory, chapter 8 of Hilchos Yom Tov, halacha 21, that if she died it is forbidden to set another on Chol HaMoed, and only on the fourteenth of Nisan is it permitted; see there; and see Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishnah there, and Beis Yosef here. And the three difficulties that the Taz raised against the Rambam of blessed memory: the first two are not difficulties. And the third difficulty one may also answer: for in the first clause it said one may set dovecotes to prepare a place for them to stand there, but to set them on eggs is forbidden on the fourteenth of Nisan; and on Chol HaMoed it is permitted when she fled after she sat three days, like Rav Huna, specifically to return her because of loss and light exertion; but if she died, even though they are entirely lost, it is forbidden to set another ab initio, for great exertion is forbidden even with total loss, as in siman 137, se'if 1, regarding liquids it is permitted, and as the Levush wrote there that with great exertion even with total loss it is forbidden; and if Hashem wills it will be clarified there. And the end of the latter clause, "if she died it is permitted," is specifically on the fourteenth of Nisan; and there is no difficulty why the Mishnah did not teach in the first clause "one may set dovecotes on the fourteenth of Nisan, and if she died it is permitted": first, lest we err as the plain reading that dovecotes on eggs are like when she died, etc.; and further, to teach us regarding the fourteenth of Nisan that if she died it is permitted only where she sat three days upon them, similar to when she fled; and according to Rav Huna, if she did not sit three days, even if she died, on the fourteenth of Nisan one may not set another with a minor loss; whereas the Tur ruled like Rav Ammi that with a minor loss it is permitted; and "dovecotes" means on the eggs, as written in siman 468. And the Gemara that asks "now that we permit setting" asks from when she died and when she fled, and answers that the first clause is the middle, or the latter is the middle; see Maggid Mishneh there. And what the Tur wrote, that according to the view that Chol HaMoed is rabbinic the halacha follows R' Ammi: even though the Rambam of blessed memory ruled in chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov that Chol HaMoed is rabbinic, and nevertheless ruled like Rav Huna, because one may offer another explanation like the Rambam of blessed memory; see there; and this requires further study. This is what appears to me for now regarding this. And see Magen Avraham, letter 3; and in Rashi, after three days of rebelling it is forbidden because of great exertion, perhaps more so; and regarding dying and setting another, one may say it is a doubt; see there; or perhaps that too applies to fleeing after three days of her rebelling; see there. And if Hashem wills, in siman 537 in the Taz and Magen Avraham I will explain further the laws of a matter of impending loss. And see the Bach regarding what he wrote about a minor loss, that we hold like Abaye in Pesachim 20b that they were not concerned about a minor loss; and so ruled the Rambam of blessed memory in chapter 12 of Hilchos Terumos; and this requires further study, to know what is called a major loss: for wood is called minor, so then even one egg and the like, even if entirely lost, would be a minor loss. And however, from this one may say there is no distinction between major and minor according to the view that Chol HaMoed is from the Torah; and if it is rabbinic, regarding exertion and the like one may say so as well. Also setting eggs involves no melacha so as to say whether from the Torah or rabbinic; ostensibly it is rabbinic, and one may say so.

<b>עיין</b> ירושלמי פסחים משמע שם ר׳ מנא אמר מתני׳ אמרה כן י״ד ט״ו ט״ז מקצת היום ככולו כרב הונא יע״ש בהגאון המפרש וציין שם אם מתה מושיבין אחרת לומר דקאי ג״כ אחה״מ לא אי״ד יע״ש והנה ג״י למרידה צ״ע אם מעל״ע או מקצת היום ג״כ ככולו ועמ״ש עוד אי״ה באות ב׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See</b> the Yerushalmi in Pesachim; it is implied there that R' Mana said the Mishnah itself said so: the fourteenth, fifteenth, and sixteenth, part of the day is like the whole, like Rav Huna; see there in the Gaon's commentary, and he noted there whether if she died one sets another, to say that it also applies to Chol HaMoed, not [only] to the fourteenth of Nisan; see there. And regarding three days of rebelling, this requires further study whether they are full day-and-night periods, or whether part of the day is also like the whole; and see further what I will write, if Hashem wills, in letter 2.
