(א) ואפילו וכו' - מפני שכשהוא עומד עליהם הוא טרוד וירא שמא יפול ולא יוכל לכוין בתפלתו ולפ"ז מה שהתירו בסמוך בזקן וחולה אף דאין בו משום גבהות מ"מ יאסר לעמוד מחמת ביעתותא צ"ל הטעם דלא הטריחוהו חכמים והתירוהו להתפלל עליהם במה שייטב לו בישיבה או בעמידה או י"ל דמיירי שם בגווני דליכא ביעתותא:
**** ENGLISH ****
Even so, etc. Because when he stands on them he is distracted and fears lest he fall, and he will not be able to concentrate in his prayer. According to this, what is permitted nearby for an elderly or sick person, although there is no haughtiness there, should nevertheless be forbidden because of fear. One must say that the Sages did not burden him, and permitted him to pray on them in whatever way is better for him, sitting or standing. Alternatively, it speaks there of cases where there is no fear.

(יד) מפני חשד - שיחשדוהו שיש לו זונה מוכנת שם ואם החורבה עומדת בשדה אין לחוש בה לחשד שאין זונה מצויה בשדה וה"ה אם אשתו עמו ג"כ ליכא למיחש לחשד ואם היא חורבה בריאה וחזקה ליכא למיחש בה למפולת ואם שנים נכנסין בה אין לחוש למזיקין שלאחד נראה ומזיק ולשנים נראה ואינו מזיק ולג' אין נראה כלל אם לא במקום שידוע שרגילין שם מזיקין ואבוקה כשנים וירח כשלשה לפיכך אם הם שנים מותר ליכנס בה אם היא בריאה וחזקה ועומדת בשדה [פמ"ג]:
**** ENGLISH ****
Because of suspicion. They may suspect that he has a woman of immorality prepared there. If the ruin stands in a field, there is no concern for suspicion, because such a woman is not usually found in a field. The same applies if his wife is with him, there is no concern for suspicion. If it is a sound and strong ruin, there is no concern for collapse. If two enter it, there is no concern for destructive forces, for to one they appear and harm, to two they appear but do not harm, and to three they do not appear at all, unless it is a place known that destructive forces are common there. A torch is like two, and the moon is like three. Therefore if there are two, it is permitted to enter if it is sound and strong and stands in a field. Peri Megadim.

**** ENGLISH ****
The congregation. Meaning, it speaks of one praying on the side where the entrance is open, namely the west. Therefore, if he turns his face toward the synagogue, he is praying in the direction in which the congregation prays, and is not called wicked. But if he turns his face away from the synagogue, he is praying opposite the congregation, who pray eastward while he prays westward, and therefore he is called wicked, for it appears like two authorities, Heaven forbid. According to this explanation, if he stands behind the synagogue on the eastern side and turns away from the synagogue while praying eastward, as the congregation also prays, he too is not called wicked.

But if he turns his face toward the synagogue, even though he is found praying westward while the whole congregation faces east, we have no concern, because the whole congregation prays toward the eastern wall where the ark is, and his face is also toward that wall.

Between praying alone in the synagogue and praying at home with ten, prayer with the congregation is preferable, and even if he can hear Kaddish and Barchu, nevertheless prayer with the congregation is preferable. Responsa Radbaz writes that a prisoner whom the officer allowed one day to pray with the congregation in a minyan, whichever day he chooses, should pray that day immediately and not delay the mitzvah to wait for Yom Kippur or Purim. **** ENGLISH ****
He should be silent, etc. All this is not called an interruption. But it is forbidden to interrupt verbally to bring water for it, since the prohibition of urine is only D'Rabbanan, and some say that one need not distance from it at all. Therefore even if others are also standing within four amos of the urine and praying, he should not interrupt verbally to inform them. If a child soiled the synagogue with excrement in the middle of prayer and others are standing there praying and do not know, if there is no way to inform them except by speaking, it is permitted to interrupt verbally to save them from a prohibition according to the Torah, for it is better that he perform a minor prohibition, namely a D'Rabbanan interruption in prayer, than that others transgress a prohibition according to the Torah by praying in a filthy place. The cases that are according to the Torah were already explained above in siman 79 in the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha. This applies specifically if he has nothing with which to cover it. But if he has something with which to cover it, whether excrement or urine, it is forbidden to interrupt, and even to move from his place if the bad smell does not reach him.
