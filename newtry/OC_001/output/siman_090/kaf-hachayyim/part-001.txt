<b>א) [סעיף א'] המתפלל לא יעמור וכו'  </b>מפורש בברייתא לפי שאין גבהות לפני המקום כלומר שצריך להראות עצמו שפל ברך ומוכנע וכדתנן אין עומדין להתפלל אלא מתוך כובד ראש, פר"ח אות א' וכתב הלבוש דהמשפיל עצמו ומכניעה תפילתו מקובלת יותר, וכתב בס"ח סי' תשע"ג דאדם המתפלל ונפלה בלבו שמחת אהבת ה' פתאום רע כי ה' חפץ לעשות רצונו וזהו שנאמר מה יפית ומה נעמת אהבה בתענוגים וכתיב אז תתענג על ה' וכו' יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] One who prays should not stand, etc.</b> It is explicit in a baraisa that there is no haughtiness before the Omnipresent; that is, one must present himself as lowly, submissive, and humbled, as we learned: One may stand to pray only in a state of solemnity. Pri Chadash 1. Levush writes that the prayer of one who lowers and humbles himself is more readily accepted. Sefer Chassidim 773 writes that if, while a person is praying, the joy of loving Hashem suddenly enters his heart because Hashem desires to fulfill his will, this is the meaning of, 'How beautiful and how pleasant is love amid delights,' and it is written, 'Then you shall delight in Hashem,' etc.; see there.

<b>ב) שם, הגהה ואפי' אינן גבוהין ג'  </b>ב"י בשם מהרי"א והטעם כתב ב"י בשם מהרי"א לפי שיש לחוש לטירדא שהוא טרוד שמא יפול ולא יתכוין בתפילתו אבל הפרישה אות א' כתב דבפחות מג' טפחים לא שייך בעיתותא בתפלה אלא ה"ק לא ע"ג מטה או שום כלי אפי' בפחות מג' משום חציצה דבעינן תפלה דומיא דעבודה ובעבודה אם היה כלי בינו ובין הקרקע היה חציצה וכמ"ש הטור לקמן סי' צ"ח ואח"כ אמר דאפי' על מקום גבוה אפי' הוא קרקע עולם דלא שייך חציצה אסור וזה אינו אסור אלא גבוה ג' אבל בפחות מג' ארעא סמיכתא היא ולא שייך ביה גבהות יעו"ש, ועיין באות שאח"ז ולקמן סי' צ"ח בס"ד אות כ"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) There, in the gloss: Even if they are not three handbreadths high.</b> Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav. Beis Yosef explains in his name that the concern is distraction: he worries that he may fall and will not concentrate on his prayer. Perishah 1, however, writes that below three handbreadths there is no fear during prayer. Rather, the meaning is that one may not pray upon a bed or any utensil, even below three handbreadths, because it constitutes an interposition, since prayer must resemble the Temple service; in the service, a utensil between the person and the ground would constitute an interposition, as Tur writes below in siman 98. It then says that even an elevated place consisting of natural ground, where interposition is inapplicable, is forbidden. This prohibition applies only when it is three handbreadths high; below three handbreadths it is considered attached to the ground and there is no elevation. See there. See the next paragraph and below, siman 98:26, with God's help.

<b>ג) שם בהגהה, ואפי' אינן גבוהין ג' וכו'  </b>וכ"כ הלבוש סו"ב במק"ח אות א' אבל הב"ח כתב דע"ג מטה וכסא וספסל נמי אינו אסור בגבוה אלא ג' טפחים אבל פחות מג"ט שרי וכתב והכי נקטינן וכ"פ בש"ע ודלא כהרב בהגהה יעו"ש, וכ"נ דעת הט"ז סק"א, אמנם המאמ"ר אות א' חלק על הב"ח וכתב דאין להקל כלל ומ"ש הב"ח וכ"פ בש"ע כתב עליו דאין שום גילוי לזה ושפיר י"ל דמרן ז"ל מודה לדברי מהרי"א דבב"י לא נחלק עליו ובש"ע סעי' א' לא העתיק רק לשון התוספתא ובסעי' ב' כתב שיעור מקום גבוה שאמרו ג"ט וכו' ומשמע דלא קאי הך שיעורא דג"ט אלא ממקום גבוה ובדברי מהרי"א ז"ל וכו' יעו"ש ועי"ש עיד שכתב דגם דברי הרמב"ם יש לפרשם כדברי מהרי"א ודברי הט"ז אינם מוכרחים יעו"ש, וכ"נ דעת הל"ח בפ"ק דברכות אות נ"ז לאסור במטה וכסא וספסל אפי' בפחות מג"ט יעו"ש וכן דעת א"ר או' א' וכתב שכ"מ במהרי"ל ה' תפלה יעו"ש, וכן דעת ח"א כלל כ"ב או' ד' ש"ץ דק"ט ע"א, סידור בי"ע בדיני תפלת י"ח או' ך' ועיין עוד לקמן סי' צ"ח או' כ"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) There, in the gloss: Even if they are not three handbreadths high, etc.</b> Levush and Solel Boneh in Makor Chaim 1 write likewise. Bach, however, writes that a bed, chair, or bench is forbidden only when three handbreadths high, but below three handbreadths is permitted. He concludes that this is the ruling we adopt, and that Shulchan Aruch rules so, unlike Rama's gloss; see there. Taz 1 also appears to hold this view. Maamar Mordechai 1, however, disputes Bach and writes that one should not be lenient at all. Regarding Bach's assertion that Shulchan Aruch rules this way, he writes that there is no indication of this. Maran may well agree with Mahari Abuhav, whom he did not dispute in Beis Yosef. In Shulchan Aruch 1 he copied only the language of the Tosefta, while in se'if 2 he states that the prescribed height of an elevated place is three handbreadths, etc. This implies that the three-handbreadth measure applies only to an elevated place. See there regarding Mahari Abuhav's words. He further writes that Rambam can also be understood in accordance with Mahari Abuhav and that Taz's words are not conclusive; see there. Lechem Chamudos, Berachos 1:57, likewise forbids a bed, chair, or bench even below three handbreadths, as do Eliyah Rabbah 1, who writes that this is implied by Maharil, Hilchos Tefillah; Erekh HaShulchan 1; Chayei Adam 22:4; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 109a; and Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the Amidah 20. See also below, siman 98:26.

<b>ג) שם, ואפי' אינם גבוהין ג',  </b>מי שאינו רגיל בשואיקיס אסור להתפלל בהם דמטריד שמא יפול, פר"ח או' א', י"א בהגב"י וכן יש ליזהר שלא לעמוד על דבר שיחליק משום דמטריד שבא יפול ואפי' על לוח שמתנדנד באופן דמטריד ביה וכן אם לובש, קבקא"ב דמטריד למי שאינו רגיל בו וכן כיוצא בזה בן א"ח ז"ל פ' יתרו או' ג':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) There: Even if they are not three handbreadths high.</b> One unaccustomed to wearing shuaikis may not pray in them, because he is distracted by the possibility of falling. Pri Chadash 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. One should likewise take care not to stand upon anything slippery, lest concern that he may fall distract him. This applies even to an unsteady board if it distracts him. The same applies if he wears kabkab footwear that distracts one who is unaccustomed to it, and to similar cases. Ben Ish Chai, Yisro 3.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) Regarding</b> the practice of praying upon a sofa - beds made of wood or stone that divide half the house like a platform and are fixed to the floor and wall - it appears that they are considered like the ground. They do not have the status of a bed with respect to placing food beneath them, nor with respect to a mourner's obligation to sit only upon the ground. When people assemble in a house to pray, however, and all or most are below, it appears improper for individuals to stand above unless circumstances leave no alternative. Halachos Ketanos 2:122. Yad Aharon cites this in Hagahos HaTur and writes: 'I see no concern in this, and I have seen several great authorities who were not concerned.' Siddur Beis Yaakov, ibid., paragraph 21, cites this and concludes that this is the primary ruling.

<b>26) [Se'if 5] One should not pray in an open place, etc.</b> Rav Kahana said: One who prays in a valley is brazen in my eyes. Rashi explains that when one is in a private place, awe of the King rests upon him and his heart is broken. Tosafos ask that it is written, 'Yitzchak went out to meditate in the field.' They answer that this concerned Mount Moriah, as Pesachim 88a states, 'Not like Yitzchak, who called it a field,' etc. Alternatively, the valley here means a valley where people and travelers regularly pass. Their intent is that one is called brazen only if he prays in a valley through which people and travelers pass, for this prevents awe of the King from resting upon him. If he prays in a valley where people do not pass, awe of the King rests upon him there too, and he is not called brazen. See Beis Yosef's question on this answer of Tosafos; according to our explanation it is resolved. Taz 2 also inclines this way; see there. Eliyah Rabbah 4 likewise writes that Agudah rules in accordance with Tosafos' second answer, permitting a field without passersby, and that one may rely upon it. Yafeh LaLev 6 argues from Tanna Devei Eliyahu that one should not pray in a valley even without passersby because this follows the ways of idolaters, unlike Tosafos. This is not conclusive. One may say that this applies specifically if he initially goes there to pray even though he has another, more private place available, or if he regularly goes there to pray. An isolated occasion, however, or a case where no more private place is available, is permitted when there are no passersby, in accordance with Tosafos.
