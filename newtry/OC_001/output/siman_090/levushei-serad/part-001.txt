**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Shulchan Aruch, seif 7.</small> The side where the entrance is. Their synagogues were like ours, with the entrance on the west. This opinion holds that 'behind the synagogue' means the west, and therefore he must turn his face toward the synagogue, praying eastward, the direction in which the congregation prays. Thus the west is called behind the synagogue, even though the entrance is there, only because the congregation prays eastward, where the ark is. The second opinion holds that behind the synagogue means the east, and the concern is that one's back should not be toward the synagogue.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 15.</small> With a multitude of people. The Peri Megadim asks: even without this, the synagogue is preferable to his house, for even when praying alone the Mechaber wrote that one should pray in the synagogue. It seems to me that the Magen Avraham was bothered by the extra phrase 'with the congregation,' for it would have sufficed to say that he should pray in the synagogue, and we would know that the intent is with the congregation, since it is forbidden to pray before the congregation. He therefore answers that this teaches two advantages: prayer in the synagogue itself, and prayer with the congregation because 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory.'
