<b> אם קנה וכו'</b> -  עיין מ"ב במש"כ שאינו ר"ל שיש לפניו שני אתרוגים וכו' ואין סוף לדבר. ומ"מ באתרוג או לולב שיש בו חשש פסול [וה"ה בכל המצות כה"ג] שתלוי בפלוגתא דרבוותא אף שהלכה דכשר מכ"מ י"ל דבזה לכו"ע צריך לקיים הידור מצוה עד שליש לקנות אחר לצאת כל הדיעות [כ"מ מפמ"ג ועיין בבגדי ישע שהחמיר עוד ביותר מזה]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he bought, etc.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that this does not mean where he has two esrogim before him, etc., for there would be no end to the matter. Nevertheless, regarding an esrog or lulav that has a possible invalidation, and the same applies to all mitzvos in a similar case, where the matter depends on a dispute of the great authorities, even though the halachah is that it is valid, nevertheless one may say that in this case everyone agrees that he must fulfill beautification of the mitzvah up to one third, to buy another in order to satisfy all opinions. So it is implied from the Peri Megadim. See Bigdei Yesha, who was even more stringent than this.

<b> בצמצום</b> -  לפי שיטה זו אם גדול יותר מכשיעור אע"פ ששניהם באים לפניו כאחד לקנות מ"מ א"צ לקנות ההדר ומדסתם השו"ע כסברא זו משמע דהכי ס"ל להלכה שהיא שיטת הרא"ש והגר"א בביאורו משמע דנוטה להיש אומרים דהוא שיטת רש"י משום דבירושלמי מפורש כן וכן הא"ר מחמיר בזה כהיש אומרים אח"כ מצאתי בפני משה על הירושלמי שכתב ג"כ דמהירושלמי מוכח להדיא כרש"י וכתב עוד ובענין הקושיא שהקשו שהרי לעולם ימצא נאה ונאה יש לומר דלא הטריחו עליו אלא בפעם ראשונה בלבד דאל"כ אין לדבר סוף עכ"ל ובזה כתבו ג"כ עוד איזה אחרונים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Barely.</b> According to this approach, if it is larger than the required measure, then although both come before him together to buy, nevertheless he need not buy the beautiful one. Since the Shulchan Aruch states this reasoning without qualification, it implies that this is his halachic view, which is the approach of the Rosh. The Gra in his commentary implies that he inclines toward the 'some say,' which is Rashi's approach, because this is explicit in the Yerushalmi. So too the Eliyah Rabbah is stringent in this like the 'some say.' Afterward I found in Pnei Moshe on the Yerushalmi that he also wrote that from the Yerushalmi it is explicitly proven like Rashi. He further wrote regarding the difficulty they raised, that one will always find something more beautiful and more beautiful, that one may say they burdened him only the first time, for otherwise there is no end to the matter. End quote. Several other Acharonim also wrote this.

<b> יותר משליש</b> -  עיין במ"ב במש"כ דאפילו מי שאין חייו נדחקין א"צ לבזבז יותר משליש ולהיפך מי שמזונותיו מצומצמין אם צריך לדחוק חייו להוסיף שליש בשביל הידור יש ג"כ דיעות בפוסקים דמן נ"י והרמ"ה משמע דגם בזה צריך להוסיף עד שליש והמהרש"ל ביש"ש בב"ק חולק ע"ז ועיין בשמ"ק בשם הראב"ד וז"ל עד שליש משלו. פי' בהדור מצוה עד שליש למצוה לקנות לו לולב נאה אם יש לו מעט או הרבה וכו' ומשמע לכאורה דס"ל כשיטת הנ"י והרמ"ה ואפשר דמה שכתב אם מעט היינו באדם שנכסיו מועטין אבל אין חייו נדחקין והנה מפירש"י והרא"ש ותוס' והריא"ז והרשב"א בביאור דברי הגמרא עד כאן משלו לא הוזכר כלל חילוק לענין מצות הידור עד שליש בין איש אמיד או לא ומ"מ י"ל דלדחוק חייב בשביל הידור גם הם מודים דא"צ והגמרא לא איירי באיש כזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>More than one third.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that even one whose livelihood is not strained need not spend more than one third. Conversely, regarding one whose food is limited, whether he must strain his life to add one third for beautification, there are also opinions among the poskim. From the Nimukei Yosef and the Ramah it implies that even in this case he must add up to one third. Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo on Bava Kamma disagrees. See Shitah Mekubetzes in the name of the Raavad, whose wording is: 'up to one third from his own.' Meaning, for beautification of a mitzvah, up to one third for the mitzvah, to buy for himself a beautiful lulav, whether he has little or much, etc. It seemingly implies that he holds like the Nimukei Yosef and the Ramah. Perhaps what he wrote 'if little' means a person whose assets are few, but whose life is not strained. Now, from Rashi, the Rosh, Tosafos, the Riaz, and the Rashba, in their explanation of the Gemara's words 'up to here from his own,' no distinction is mentioned at all regarding beautification of a mitzvah up to one third between a wealthy person and one who is not. Nevertheless, one may say that even they agree that if he must strain himself for beautification, he need not do so, and the Gemara was not discussing such a person.

<b> יותר מחומש</b> -  עיין במגן אברהם שכתב בשם רבינו ירוחם דמ"מ חייב להוציא עישור נכסיו לזה וכתב הב"י ע"ז ולא ידעתי מנין לו וכתב הא"ר ע"ז דלא סיימיה קמיה דהב"י דהא סיים רי"ו כדפירשתי גבי צדקה ולענ"ד דסברת הב"י דאין אנו יכולין ללמוד משם דאף דהון רב מסתברא דאין אנו חייבין מהא דר"ג דאמר ע"ז בגמרא דכמה חביבין מצות על ישראל ומשמע לא מדינא וגם דשליש בית אין חייבין מהא דב"ק דקמקשה אלו איתרמי תלתא מצותא וכו' עכ"פ אולי ביותר מזה יש חיוב חמישית או ששית וכדומה ואין אנו יכולין ללמוד מצדקה [דשם השיעור חומש לא מדינא רק המבזבז וכו'] דשם הוא מצוה שאינה עוברת ואפילו אם איכא עניים דקיימי הלא יכול להתקיים ע"י אחרים משא"כ באתרוג וכדומה שהיא מצוה עוברת וחל על גוף האדם ותדע דקמקשה הגמרא אלא מעתה אי איתרמי ליה תלתא מצותא וכו' ולא קמקשה בפשיטות והא אמרו אל יבזבז יותר מחומש אלא ע"כ דהכא בענינינו דהוא מצוה עוברת חמור טפי ומה דסמכו הפוסקים לענינינו מהא דהמבזבז היא רק להשמעינו כמו שם חששו שמא יעני ויצטרך לבריות וע"כ אין נכון יותר מחומש כן יש לחוש בענינינו. ובדוחק יש ליישב דבר הרי"ו דכונתו דמעשר בודאי חייב להוציא על מצוה דעכ"פ לא גרע משם אבל אין כונתו להחליט דזהו שיעורו. וע"כ מה שסיים רי"ו והעישור כדפירשתי גבי צדקה ור"ל דשנה ראשונה מן הקרן ואח"כ מן הריוח כמו שפירש הא"ר ר"ל ג"כ דשיעור זה בודאי חייב בענינינו דעכ"פ לא גרע משם ואפשר דיותר מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>More than one fifth.</b> See Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that nevertheless he is obligated to spend one tenth of his assets for this. The Beis Yosef wrote about this: I do not know his source. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote about this that the full text was not before the Beis Yosef, for Rabbeinu Yerucham concludes: as I explained regarding charity. In my humble opinion, the Beis Yosef's reasoning is that we cannot learn from there. Although with great wealth it is reasonable that we are not obligated, from the case of Rabban Gamliel, about which the Gemara said how beloved the mitzvos are to Israel, it implies that this was not by law. Also, that one need not spend one third of his house follows from Bava Kamma, which asks: if three mitzvos happened to come before him, etc. In any event, perhaps more than this does carry an obligation of a fifth, or a sixth, and the like, and we cannot learn from charity, where the measure of a fifth is not by law, but only 'one who spends,' etc. There, it is a mitzvah that is not passing; and even if poor people are present, it can be fulfilled through others. This is not so with an esrog and the like, which is a passing mitzvah and rests on the person's own body. Know this, for the Gemara asks: if so, if three mitzvos happen to come before him, etc., and it does not ask simply: did they not say, 'one should not spend more than one fifth'? Rather, it must be that here in our matter, where it is a passing mitzvah, it is more stringent. That which the poskim relied on regarding our matter from the rule of 'one who spends' is only to teach us that just as there they were concerned lest he become poor and need others, and therefore more than one fifth is not proper, so too there is such a concern in our matter. With difficulty, one can resolve the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham: his intent is that he is certainly obligated to spend a tithe on a mitzvah, for at least it is no worse than there; but he did not mean to decide that this is its measure. Therefore, what Rabbeinu Yerucham concluded, that the tithe is as I explained regarding charity, meaning the first year from the principal and afterward from the profit, as the Eliyah Rabbah explained, also means that this measure is certainly obligatory in our matter, for it is at least no worse than there, and perhaps more than this.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even a passing mitzvah.</b> This is how the text should read, and so it is in the old print. Meaning, although the rule of 'one who spends' does not discuss a passing mitzvah, nevertheless the law is that even for a passing mitzvah one need not spend great wealth, as stated in the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham. Now, regarding the basic law explained here in Shulchan Aruch, that one need not spend great wealth on a mitzvah, which derives from the sugya in Bava Kamma, 'if so, if three mitzvos happened to come before him, should he give his whole house,' and which is connected to the law of charity that one who spends, etc., seemingly this requires investigation from Kiddushin 29, where it is explained regarding redeeming one's son that even if he has only five selaim he must redeem himself, and likewise regarding going up for the festival. There it speaks where he has no assets at all except these five selaim; see there. Seemingly, according to the sugya in Bava Kamma, from which the law of the Shulchan Aruch here derives, one is not obligated to give all that he has for a mitzvah, and even one third of his house he is also not obligated. Also, the incident of Rabban Gamliel is seemingly difficult according to this: what is the novelty that he bought an esrog for one thousand zuz because of love of the mitzvah? Even one who has only five selaim must give them to the kohen to redeem himself or to go up for the festival; all the more so Rabban Gamliel, who was wealthy, and this amount was certainly only a portion of his wealth. In order to resolve the sugyos so they not contradict, one could seemingly say regarding the incident of Rabban Gamliel that the novelty there was that he spent great wealth beyond its value, which is not the price of the mitzvah, but only because there on the ship it was impossible to obtain an esrog, and he gave someone great wealth so that he would agree to sell it to him. For this we are not obligated by law, since if he cannot find it at its usual value, he is coerced and not obligated; he did this only because of love of the mitzvah. One could also explain the sugya in Bava Kamma as speaking in such a case, where the price of the mitzvah rose far beyond its value, and the Talmud's initial thought was that up to one third of his house he is obligated; it rejects this, that he is not obligated. But any mitzvah that he can obtain at its value, the Torah obligated every Jew if he has even a little, like that case in Kiddushin above, and only if he has nothing is he exempt, like that Yerushalmi in the first chapter of Peah. This is its wording there: 'Honor Hashem from your wealth,' from what He has graced you. He separates leket, shikchah, and peah, separates terumah, etc., and makes sukkah and lulav, shofar, tefillin, and tzitzis, and feeds the poor, etc. If you have, you are obligated in all of them; and if you do not have, you are not obligated in any of them. But when it comes to honoring father and mother, whether you have wealth or do not have, honor your father and your mother, even if you go begging at the doors. However, this is against the plain implication of all the poskim, who appear to have understood simply from the sugya in Bava Kamma and the incident of Rabban Gamliel that one is not obligated to spend great wealth on any mitzvah in any circumstance, even if he still has wealth, and only up to one fifth at most, as with charity. One must say that they distinguish the sugya in Kiddushin from Bava Kamma and Sukkah in this way: to spend great wealth, he is indeed not obligated, because of the reason that perhaps he will become poor and need others, as the Raavad wrote. This does not apply to one who has only five selaim and the like, and supports himself from his labor; his livelihood will not be diminished by spending this on the mitzvah, and conversely his condition will not be strengthened by not spending it. From the words of the Nimukei Yosef and the Ramah it is also proven this way, that even one whose life is strained must spend even for beautification, and all the more so for the mitzvah itself. This is unlike the view of Rashal, who disagrees with the Nimukei Yosef and Ramah in this and wrote that perhaps even for the body of the mitzvah he is not obligated, since his life is strained and for him it is like great wealth. But from the above sugya in Kiddushin it does not imply so, and from the above Yerushalmi it is also proven so, even according to how the Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah copied the words of the Yerushalmi, and all the more so according to what we will explain below. It only means that he is not obligated to go begging at the doors for the mitzvos of tefillin and tzitzis, etc.; but if he has enough to buy tefillin and tzitzis and the like, he is certainly obligated, and this is not considered great wealth. Although above we leaned halachically toward Rashal, that was only regarding beautification, not regarding the mitzvah itself. See my book Ahavas Chesed, part 2, chapter 20, letter 3. Incidentally, I will raise here what was difficult for me regarding what the Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah copied in siman 25 as a proof from the Yerushalmi that one is not obligated to go begging at the doors in order to buy tefillin and similar mitzvos. But we hold that for Chanukah lights and the four cups one must beg at the doors in order to fulfill them, although they are only rabbinic mitzvos, and all the more so in our matter. Even if you say that because of publicizing the miracle the Sages enacted so, what will you answer regarding that which we hold above in siman 263:2, that for Shabbos lights one begs at the doors, takes oil, and lights the lamp, for this is included in delight of Shabbos. Yet delight of Shabbos is only a positive commandment from the words of the Prophets, as it says, 'and you shall call Shabbos a delight,' while tefillin and shofar and the like are all positive commandments from the Torah. I searched and found this difficulty in the book Mor Uketziah; see there, where he discusses it at length and remains with the difficulty. The proof they brought from the above Yerushalmi is seemingly no proof, when we examine the language of the Yerushalmi, which says, 'and makes sukkah and lulav, shofar, tefillin, and tzitzis.' What is this language, 'and makes,' that it says? It should have said, 'and sits in the sukkah, and takes lulav, and blows shofar, and wears tefillin and tzitzis.' Therefore it appears to my humble mind that the Yerushalmi follows its own approach, for it is stated there in Berachos, chapter 9, halachah 3: one who makes a sukkah for himself says, 'Blessed are You... Who sanctified us with His mitzvos and commanded us to make a sukkah'; when he enters to sit in it, he says, 'Blessed... to sit in the sukkah.' One who makes a lulav for himself says, 'Blessed... to make a lulav'; when he takes it, he says, 'over taking lulav.' One who makes tefillin for himself says, 'Blessed... to make tefillin'; when he wears them, he says, 'over the mitzvah of tefillin.' It likewise counts there mezuzah and several mitzvos. Thus the Yerushalmi holds that making the mitzvos is a mitzvah in itself, since he recites a berachah over it. See Menachos 42b in Tosafos, that the Yerushalmi disagrees in this with our Gemara. One cannot exempt himself by buying tefillin or a sukkah from his fellow with money. Regarding this the Yerushalmi says that the Torah did not obligate him to make the sukkah himself, and likewise to take parchment and make a mezuzah, houses, and passages, except when he has the means. But if he does not have, the Torah did not obligate him in this to seek wood for a sukkah and parchment for tefillin and the like; rather, he buys with his money tefillin and mezuzah that others made, and likewise with sukkah and all mitzvos, for the making is only a preparation. But regarding the essential positive commandment, to wear tefillin, and likewise regarding fulfillment of the other mitzvos, the Torah did not exempt him at all, for it is an obligation on his own body, and it is no worse than Shabbos lights, Chanukah lights, and the four cups, for which one sells his garment in order to obtain them. Even if you say that the law is not that he must go begging at the doors to obtain money to buy tefillin, nevertheless he is at least obligated to go and seek from a Jew who will lend him tefillin to fulfill the positive commandment.

<b>And see below, etc.</b> Meaning, there it says that it is a mitzvah for each person to buy an esrog for himself. The Magen Avraham wrote: if he has only five selaim and must redeem his son and buy an esrog, he should buy the esrog, for this is a passing mitzvah and that is a mitzvah that is not passing. However, if he can fulfill the obligation with another's esrog, then although it is a mitzvah to buy from his own, nevertheless redemption takes precedence in this case [Bikkurei Yaakov]. If he bought an esrog and it was found to be rotten, and likewise a lulav, the Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Shiltei Giborim that if he can attribute the rotting to having occurred in the seller's hand, this is a mistaken transaction; and if the matter is doubtful, the burden of proof is on the claimant. The Peri Megadim wrote that according to the 'some say' in Choshen Mishpat, siman 199, that if one gave money for wine for Kiddush, he acquires it, because by Torah law money acquires and they upheld Torah law for acquiring a mitzvah object, if so one may say that the same applies to an esrog, and neither of them can retract.
