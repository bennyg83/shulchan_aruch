**** ENGLISH ****
(5) <b>And from the house to the sukkah, etc.</b> If rain forced him to recite Kiddush indoors and then stopped, he may eat in the sukkah without repeating; likewise if he recited in the sukkah and rain forced him indoors. This concerns a sukkah inside the house beneath a removed roof, with no partition besides the sukkah wall. Since that wall serves the mitzvah, not ordinary use, moving is like corner to corner without a partition and requires no repetition even absent prior intent. If the sukkah is outside and the house walls intervene, it is like room to room: absent initial intent, Kiddush must be repeated unless he can see the first place, as below. Some poskim dispute this entire analysis, as explained in Biur Halachah. Therefore, one planning to relocate after Kiddush should first drink a revi'is of wine there, making it a meal-place as below; everyone then agrees no new Kiddush is needed.

**** ENGLISH ****
(26) <b>Fruit does not.</b> The five grains are called food and wine sustains the heart; other foods, however abundant, are not a meal. Thus, one visiting a groom's celebration or bris where after Kiddush only delicacies, not pastries, are served should taste nothing. It does not suffice that the one reciting Kiddush drinks a full revi'is; that helps only him, not others [later authorities]. Shiltei HaGibborim says fruit suffices because every Shabbos meal is fixed, but Tur and Shulchan Aruch are primary [Magen Avraham]. If somewhat weak and without grain food after the cup, some later authorities allow reliance on Shiltei HaGibborim by day, never at night, since the other poskim disagree.
