<b>א) [סעיף א'] חבילי פיאה אזוב וקורנית וכו'  </b>מיני עשבים הם ראוים להסקה וראוים למאכל בהמה לפיכך הכל הולך אחר מחשבתו וכן בשאר מיני תבלין שהן כיוצא באלו. מ"מ פכ"ו דין ח"י:

<b>6) There: With his fingertips.</b> This is provided that he does not rub a large quantity with his hand as he does during the week. Gemara. Rashi writes that using his entire hand is forbidden. See above, siman 319, seif 7, where Rema writes in the gloss that some are stringent. Bach writes there that Rema certainly rules in accordance with those who permit when it is done unusually, but he holds that it is proper to be stringent. This is our accepted ruling, and he writes the same in this siman; see there and our words there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3, however, writes here that Baal HaMaor distinguishes between rubbing grain and rubbing pe'ah and thyme, holding that with pe'ah and thyme one may even crumble the food. He writes that this appears to be Rema's view, since here he states the Shulchan Aruch's ruling without qualification, and therefore one may be lenient; see there. This also appears to be the view of the later authorities, who do not cite here the stringent opinion stated there.

<b>ח) שם. אין עושין מי מלח הרבה וכו'  </b>משמע דוקא לתת לתוך הכבשים אסור אבל אם ירצה לעשות מי מלח הרבה לאכול מיד ולתת בתוך התבשיל מותר אבל ממ"ש בסופה אבל יכול לעשות וכו' יש לדייק אפכא דמרובה אפי' ליתן לתוך התבשיל אסור. ונ"ל דהש"ע אורחא דמלתא נקט דדרך העולם הוא שעושין מרובה לכבשים ומועט לתוך התבשיל ובאמת אסור מרובה אפי' עושה ליתן לתוך התבשיל דלא פליג רבנן. עו"ש או' א' וכ"כ א"ר או' ג' דבעושה הרבה אסור לתת אפי' לתוך התבשיל ואפי' לסעודה זו דנראה כעושה מלאכה ממלאכת התבשיל עכ"ד. וכ"כ השש"א וכתב ודלא כהתו"ש יעו"ש. וכ"כ הר"ז או' ג' בן א"ח פ' בא או' י"ט:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>8) There: One may not prepare a large quantity of brine, etc.</b> The wording implies that it is forbidden specifically for placing into foods to be pickled, but that one may make a large quantity for immediate consumption or to add to a cooked dish. The end of the ruling, however, which states 'but one may prepare,' implies the opposite, that a large quantity is forbidden even for a cooked dish. It appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch describes the usual case: people ordinarily make a large quantity for pickling and a small quantity for a cooked dish. In truth, a large quantity is forbidden even when made for a cooked dish, because the Sages did not distinguish. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, likewise writes that when one prepares a large quantity, it is forbidden even for a cooked dish and even for that meal, because it appears that he is performing the labor of preparing food. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes the same, contrary to Tosefes Shabbos; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, and Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 19, write likewise.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>11) There: But he may prepare a small amount, etc.</b> This means the amount needed for dipping during that meal. Ran. This implies that even a small amount is forbidden for another meal. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Olas Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Ben Ish Chai there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that one may prepare everything needed for that Shabbos even if not for that meal. Ran mentions that meal only because this is the usual case: since one prepares it only for that meal, he makes a small amount. Eliyah Rabbah writes that Rambam, chapter 22, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, page 77, also imply this, and that it is explicit in Shibolei HaLeket, siman 4, as well as implied by Sefer HaTanya, contrary to Olas Shabbos and Magen Avraham; see there. Nehar Shalom also inclines this way. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 2, likewise writes that a small amount does not fall within the category of labor and is therefore permitted even for another meal that day. This differs from selecting, which remains forbidden even for later that day; see there. Nevertheless, since the matter is disputed, it is proper to take care to prepare it shortly before the meal.

<b>טוב) [סעיף ג'] אסור למלוח חתיכות צנון וכו'.  </b>ובהרמב"ם פכ"ב דין יו"ד כתב צנון וכיוצא בו מפני שנראה ככובש כבשים וכו' ור"ל דכל דבר שדרך לעשות ממנו כבשים אסור למלחו בשבת מפני שנראה ככובש כבשים ולא דוקא דבר שרגילין לעשות ממנו כבשים אלא ה"ה כל דבר שהמלח מועיל בו שממתקו ממרותו אסור. וכ"כ הט"ז סק"ב דלאו דוקא צנון דה"ה כל דבר שצריך מליחה (ר"ל שמועלת בו המליחה לרככו או להקשותו או להפיג מרירתו. ר"ז או' ד') כגון בצלים ושומים. וכ"כ א"ר או' ה' ר"ז או' ד' אבל דבר שאין דרכו לכבוש וגם אין המליחה מועלת בו שרי כמ"ש לקמן או' כ"ד. וה"ד לאותה סעודה כמ"ש לקמן או' כ"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>17) [Seif 3] One may not salt slices of radish, etc.</b> Rambam, chapter 22, halachah 10, writes 'radish and the like, because it appears as though one is pickling.' This means that anything ordinarily pickled may not be salted on Shabbos because it appears that one is pickling. The rule is not limited to foods ordinarily pickled; it also includes anything upon which salt has an effect by removing its bitterness. Taz, paragraph 2, likewise writes that radish is not exclusive and that the same law applies to anything requiring salting, meaning that salting softens or hardens it or removes its bitterness, such as onions and garlic. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4, write the same. An item that is not ordinarily pickled and upon which salt has no effect is permitted, as stated below in paragraph 24. This applies specifically for that meal, as stated below in paragraph 25.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>18) There: Four or five together, etc.</b> This is Tur's wording. The statement in the following seif that some write that one may dip several pieces is from Terumas HaDeshen, cited in Beis Yosef. Beis Yosef implies that Terumas HaDeshen does not disagree with Tur but explains him. Why, then, does Shulchan Aruch introduce Terumas HaDeshen's ruling as 'some write'? Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, explains that the author was uncertain how to understand Tur. Tur may mean, as Terumas HaDeshen explains, that only salting them together is forbidden, while salting each piece separately is permitted even for four or five pieces. Alternatively, Tur may hold that once several pieces are salted, it is forbidden even when each was salted separately. The author therefore cites Tur's exact words, which can bear both interpretations, and afterward introduces Terumas HaDeshen's interpretation as 'some write' to indicate that Terumas HaDeshen chose this explanation; see there. It appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch says 'some write' because Terumas HaDeshen himself presents the ruling tentatively, as one can see from his words in Beis Yosef. Examine carefully.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>19) There: Four or five together, etc.</b> Our custom requires examination. On Shabbos, people finely slice radish, place it in a bowl, salt it, pour vinegar upon it, and eat it. This resembles salting many pieces, which is forbidden even if eaten immediately, whereas the permission applies only to dipping each piece separately. It appears that since they do not leave it at all to release moisture but immediately pour vinegar and other ingredients upon it, it does not resemble processing. I have nevertheless seen some people prepare a vegetable called salad by first salting the vegetable alone, leaving it that way, straining off the water that emerges, and only afterward mixing it with olive oil and vinegar. This is absolutely forbidden and resembles processing more closely, since they wait until it absorbs the salt well. Taz at the end of paragraph 1. Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 8 of Sheratzim, writes that one may prepare salted vegetables on Shabbos by placing salt upon the vegetables, then oil, and then vinegar, and the same applies to similar cases. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, and Magen Avraham, paragraph 6, cite him. This must refer to adding oil and vinegar immediately after the salt. If one leaves the vegetables in salt until they release moisture and only then adds oil and vinegar, it is forbidden, as stated above in the name of Taz. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Chayei Adam, klal 32, paragraph 11; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 17. See below, paragraph 67.

<b>יט) ועל  </b>כן העושין סלאט"ה ביום שבת מקשואין או ירקות וכיוצא שחותכין אותם לחתיכות קטנות ונותנין עליהם מלח צריך שתיכף שנתן עליהם המלח יתן החומץ מיד וגם אסור לסחטן במלח כדי לרככן והגם דלעיל בסי' ש"ך סעי' ז' התיר לסחוט כבשיה כדי לרככם שאני התם דכיון שהיו מלוחים מקודם שבת אין הסחיטה מועלת בהם אבל הכא שעתה בא למלחם אם סוחט אותם במלח המלח פועל בהם בשבת ע"י הסחיטה והוי ככובש כבשים אלא אם רוצה לסחטם כדי לרככם יסחוט אותם בלא מלח עד שיתרככו ואח"כ יתן עליהם המלח ותיכף יתן החומץ. והיותר טוב שיעשה אותה מע"ש אם אפשר.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>19) Therefore,</b> when preparing salad on Shabbos from cucumbers, vegetables, or similar produce by cutting them into small pieces and salting them, one must add the vinegar immediately after adding the salt. One may also not squeeze them together with salt to soften them. Although siman 320, seif 7, permits squeezing pickled foods to soften them, that case differs because they were salted before Shabbos and squeezing has no further effect upon them. Here, since one is now salting them, squeezing them with the salt causes the salt to act upon them on Shabbos and resembles pickling. If one wishes to squeeze them to soften them, he should squeeze them without salt until they soften, then add salt and immediately add vinegar. It is preferable to prepare the salad before Shabbos whenever possible.
