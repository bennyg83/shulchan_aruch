(21) Forbidden to salt, etc. — for that which they permit above regarding an egg to salt it is for the need of that meal; but to salt meat and an egg in order to leave for later time is similar to tanning and pickling. And behold Magen Avraham's and Taz's view is that even if his intent is to eat on that day, if it is for the need of another meal one should be careful in this [meaning when the other meal extends a long time after the first meal]; but R' Avraham sides that one forbids only if his intent is to leave until after Shabbat, and so is implied from Be'ur HaGra; and especially if the time is hot and he does so so it not spoil — certainly one may be lenient for another meal, for Taz also permits in this. Raw meat or fish — forbidden to salt it on Shabbat so it not spoil; and even in a place of loss it is forbidden; and even if he wants to salt it in order to eat it after salting while still raw — forbidden, for even though there is no tanning of foods d'oraisa, nevertheless forbidden because it appears like tanning, for salt makes the food fit and corrects it; but permitted to rinse the meat in order to eat it afterward raw, and this is not called correction, for the correction is not in the body of the meat but he rinses it because of visible blood upon it. Nevertheless it appears forbidden to rinse meat that was not salted when the third day falls on Shabbat and so it will not be forbidden afterward for cooking, since he does not want to eat it today and also there is no manner to eat raw — it is recognizable he does for weekday need; and even through a non-Jew it is forbidden to rinse, for there is not so much loss here if he does not rinse it so he can eat roasted — so wrote Magen Avraham; and it deals with an individual in his house; but a meat vendor who sells to others — certainly there is loss here and he may do through a non-Jew; and likewise in fattened geese that he will lose the fat if he roasts them — also according to Magen Avraham permitted through a non-Jew even an individual in his house. And even in animal meat many Acharonim agreed one may be lenient to rinse through a non-Jew, unlike Magen Avraham; and see in R' Avraham and in responsa of Noda B'Yehuda who wrote that if impossible through a non-Jew — permitted also through an Israelite; but if the meat rests in a good vessel he should wash his hands on it until the meat is soaked in water:
