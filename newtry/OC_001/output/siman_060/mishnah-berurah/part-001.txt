**** ENGLISH ****
(7) <b>Some say.</b> Know that, as emerges from the poskim, a mitzvah involves two forms of intent. The first is concentration on the mitzvah itself, meaning that he should direct his heart to what he is uttering and not think about something else, as in Krias Shema, the Amidah, Bircas HaMazon, Kiddush, and the like. Regarding this, all agree that initially it is a mitzvah to concentrate, but after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation without such concentration, except for the first verse of Krias Shema and the first blessing of the Amidah, as explained below. The second is intent to fulfill the obligation, meaning that he intends through this act to fulfill what Hashem commanded, as the Bach writes in siman 8. The intent discussed in this seif concerns this second form. For the preferred fulfillment of a mitzvah, all agree that intent is required, as stated in Nedarim: Rabbi Eliezer son of Tzadok says, perform deeds for the sake of the One who made them. It is also stated, ‘Their fear of Me has become a commandment of people learned by rote,’ as the Gra writes regarding siman 8. See there.

(8) <b>That they require intent.</b> If he did not intend to fulfill his obligation when performing the mitzvah, he has not fulfilled it on a Torah level and must perform it again. Even when he is uncertain whether he intended, if it is a Torah-level mitzvah the doubt is treated stringently, as the Peri Megadim writes in siman 589. It appears to me that he should not recite the blessing when repeating the mitzvah, because there are several opinions concerning the blessing even where he certainly lacked intent the first time.

(9) <b>To fulfill the obligation.</b> Therefore, one who sounds the shofar for practice, or one who recites Bircas HaMazon with children in order to train them in mitzvos while he himself is also obligated in Bircas HaMazon, but forgets to intend to fulfill his own obligation, does not fulfill it. The same applies to all comparable mitzvos performed for some other purpose. See the Taz in siman 489, whose words indicate that one who recites Bircas HaMazon with children as described does not fulfill his obligation even according to the view that mitzvos do not require intent, because it is as though he expressly intended not to fulfill it. If his intent in performing the mitzvah is both for another purpose and to fulfill the mitzvah, he does fulfill his obligation.

(י) וכן הלכה - כתב המ"א בשם הרדב"ז דזה דוקא במצוה דאורייתא אבל במצוה דרבנן א"צ כונה. ולפ"ז כל הברכות שהם ג"כ דרבנן לבד מבהמ"ז אם לא כיון בהם לצאת יצא בדיעבד אך מכמה מקומות בשו"ע משמע שהוא חולק ע"ז וכן מבאור הגר"א בסימן תפ"ט משמע ג"כ שאין לחלק בין מצוה דאורייתא למצוה דרבנן. ודע דכתב המ"א לקמן בסימן תפ"ט סק"ח דאף דהשו"ע פסק להלכה דמצות צריכות כונה וא"כ היכא שלא כיון בפעם ראשונה צריך לחזור ולעשות המצוה אעפ"כ לא יברך עוד עליה שלענין ברכה צריך לחוש לדעת הי"א שאין צריך כונה ועי' בבה"ל. ודע עוד דכתב הח"א בכלל ס"ח דמה דמצרכינן ליה לחזור ולעשות המצוה היינו במקום שיש לתלות שעשייה הראשונה לא היתה לשם מצוה כגון בתקיעה שהיתה להתלמד או בק"ש שהיתה דרך לימודו וכדומה אבל אם קורא ק"ש כדרך שאנו קורין בסדר תפילה וכן שאכל מצה או תקע ונטל לולב אע"פ שלא כיון לצאת יצא שהרי משום זה עושה כדי לצאת אע"פ שאינו מכוין עכ"ל ור"ל היכא שמוכח לפי הענין שעשייתו הוא כדי לצאת אע"פ שלא כיון בפירוש יצא אבל בסתמא בודאי לא יצא כדאיתא בתוס' סוכה [דף ל"ט ע"א ד"ה עובר עי"ש] וכ"ז לענין בדיעבד אבל לכתחילה ודאי צריך ליזהר לכוין קודם כל מצוה לצאת ידי חובת המצוה וכן העתיקו כל האחרונים בספריהם עיין בח"א בכלל כ"א ובדה"ח הלכות ק"ש ובמעשה רב:
**** ENGLISH ****
(10) <b>And such is the halachah.</b> The Magen Avraham writes in the name of the Radbaz that this applies only to a Torah-level mitzvah, but a Rabbinic mitzvah does not require intent. Accordingly, regarding all blessings, which are Rabbinic except for Bircas HaMazon, one who failed to intend to fulfill the obligation has nevertheless fulfilled it after the fact. However, several rulings in the Shulchan Aruch indicate that it disagrees with this distinction, and the explanation of the Gra in siman 489 likewise indicates that no distinction is made between a Torah-level and a Rabbinic mitzvah. Know also that the Magen Avraham writes below, siman 489, seif katan 8, that although the Shulchan Aruch rules that mitzvos require intent, so that one who lacked intent the first time must perform the mitzvah again, he nevertheless should not recite another blessing, because regarding the blessing one must take into account the opinion that intent is not required. See Biur Halachah. The Chayei Adam writes in klal 68 that the requirement to repeat the mitzvah applies where there is reason to attribute the first act to a purpose other than the mitzvah, such as sounding the shofar for practice or reciting Krias Shema as part of one's studies. But where one recites Krias Shema in the normal order of prayer, or eats matzah, sounds the shofar, or takes the lulav, he has fulfilled his obligation even without explicit intent, because this is plainly why he is performing the act. His meaning is that where the circumstances demonstrate that the act is being done to fulfill the obligation, explicit intent is unnecessary after the fact. In an unqualified case, however, he certainly has not fulfilled his obligation, as stated in Tosafos, Sukkah 39a, s.v. over. All this concerns the law after the fact. Initially, one must certainly be careful before every mitzvah to intend to fulfill the obligation, as all the later authorities record in their works. See Chayei Adam, klal 21; Derech HaChaim, laws of Krias Shema; and Maaseh Rav.

(11) <b>Directed his heart.</b> The intent discussed here is not the intent discussed in seif 4. There it means intent to fulfill the obligation of the mitzvah, which is required for all the sections of Krias Shema, as the Ritva in Rosh Hashanah and the Shitah Mekubetzes in Berachos write. Here, however, it means contemplating and paying attention to what he is saying. This is indispensable only in the first verse, which contains the essential acceptance of the yoke of Heaven and the declaration of Hashem's unity. Some say that even intent to fulfill the obligation is indispensable only in the first verse.
