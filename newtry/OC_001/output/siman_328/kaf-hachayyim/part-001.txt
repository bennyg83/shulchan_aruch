<b>א) [סעיף א'] מי שיש לו מיחוש בעלמא וכו'  </b>דאם נפל למשכב או דכאיב ליה טובא עד שנחלה כל גופו אעפ"י שאין בו סכנה מותר לעשות לו רפואה ע"י עכו"ם אפי' במלאכה גמורה כדלקמן סעי' י"ז יעו"ש:

<b>ה) שם. גזירה משום שחיקת סמנים.  </b>מי שיש לו נפח על אחת מפניו שזה יקרה מחמת קרירות ותרופתו שמטבילין מטלית בתוך השכר ונותנין אותו על הנפח ויתחמם ויתרפא אם מותר לעשות כן בשבת באופן שלא יבא לידי סחיטה דהיינו שיהיו נזהרין שלא לשרות המטלית בשכר רק ישפשפו הנפח בשכר ביד היטב ואח"כ ישימו עליו מטלית יבש לשמור חומו. מי נימא דשרי דכיון דליכא סם לא שייכא גזירת שחיקת סמנים או דילמא כל רפואה נאסרה הגם דלא שייך בה שחיקה שמא יעשה רפואה אחרת דשייך בה שחיקה. עיין פתה"ד או' או' מה דשקיל וטרי בהאי ענינא ודעתו להתיר דוקא בדאיכא כ"כ צערא דבמקום צערא לא גזור יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) There: As a decree lest one grind medicinal ingredients.</b> If a person has swelling on one side of his face caused by cold, the treatment is to immerse a cloth in beer, place it upon the swelling, warm it, and thereby cure it. May this be done on Shabbos in a manner that avoids squeezing, by not soaking the cloth in beer but thoroughly rubbing the swelling with beer by hand and then placing a dry cloth upon it to preserve the warmth? One might say that it is permitted because no medicine is involved, so the decree against grinding ingredients does not apply. Alternatively, all treatment may have been forbidden even when grinding does not apply, lest one perform another treatment that does require grinding. See Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, who discusses this matter and permits it specifically when the suffering is sufficiently severe, because the Sages did not impose their decree in a case of suffering; see there.

<b>12) There: Meaning from the teeth inward, etc.</b> Even if it is not tzafidna, a disease of the teeth and gums that begins in the mouth and ends in the intestines, whose sign is that blood flows from the row of teeth when something is placed upon them and that develops from eating excessively hot bread or very cold wheat food in winter, as stated in Yoma 84a and Avodah Zarah 28a, Shabbos is desecrated whenever there is an actual wound. Similarly, when the pain is severe enough to be called a wound, Shabbos is desecrated, as stated in the preceding paragraph in the name of Bach. Tosefes Shabbos there writes the same.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>19) There, gloss: He may tell a non-Jew to extract it.</b> This follows the rule that for any non-dangerous illness one may instruct a non-Jew to act. Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. Maharshal writes that there is no proof that a tooth may be extracted on Shabbos through a non-Jew because of danger to life. Only applying a substance, which is a definite treatment, permits desecrating Shabbos even through Torah-prohibited labor such as grinding medicinal ingredients, whereas extracting the tooth itself also presents danger to life. These are his words, cited by Bach. Taz, paragraph 1, forbids it for another reason: the Jew assists the non-Jew in extracting the tooth; see there. Shach in Nekudos HaKesef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, however, writes that specifically one receiving a haircut is liable for assisting by positioning his head, because Scripture expressly includes him. All the needs of one with common fever may nevertheless be performed through a non-Jew. Chayei Adam there. Common tertian and quotidian fevers are not dangerous, but quartan fever is dangerous. Mor U'Ketziah states this based on the conditions in his locale. In truth, if a physician says danger exists and the case differs from ordinary common fevers, Shabbos is desecrated. This is not an absolute rule and is obvious. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2. An expert is required to determine whether a fever is extremely hot before Shabbos may be desecrated. Rabbi Yosef Molcho, responsa manuscript, siman 119; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.

<b>42) There: And the beginning of inflammation, etc.</b> This excludes the end of inflammation or applying eye salve merely to brighten one's sight, for which Shabbos is not desecrated. Gemara there. A treatment for eye illness uses egg white cooked with specified medicinal ingredients, whose liquid is squeezed into the eye. At the beginning of inflammation, when danger to both limb and life exists, even a Jew may perform it, including to relieve pain, and even when medicinal ingredients must be ground. Certainly this is permitted when the ingredients were ground before Shabbos. At the end of inflammation, if used as treatment, a Jew may squeeze the egg unusually, meaning into his hand or a utensil, and place it in the eye. The egg itself, however, may be placed upon the eye only through a non-Jew. If at the end of inflammation this treatment serves only to brighten the eye and not to heal it, it is forbidden even through a non-Jew.
