<b>אפי' אין בו</b>. משמע דכ"ש כשיש בו סכנת אבר דמותר אפי' אינו חולה שכולל כל הגוף וזו דעת המ"מ מה שסיים אבל באבר אחד כיון שאין בו סכנה אסור וזה שלא התירו לכחול אלא מן העכו"ם עכ"ל פי' שאין באבר סכנה דלא כב"י וכ"כ המ"מ אח"כ אבל מלאכה גמורה בישראל לא הותרה אפילו בסכנת אבר ע"כ ומשמע דשבות מותר בסכנת אבר ואם אין בו סכנת אבר מותר ע"י עכו"ם והיינו עובדא דאמימר וכמ"ש סימן ש"ז ס"ה שבות דשבות שרי במקצת חולי ומיחוש בעלמא אפילו ע"י עכו"ם אסור וכמ"ש ס"א ועמ"ש סי' תצ"ו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even where there is no.</b> This implies that where there is danger to a limb, it is certainly permitted even if the illness does not affect the entire body. This is the view of the Maggid Mishneh. His concluding statement, “But regarding one limb, since there is no danger, it is forbidden,” means that there is no danger to the limb, contrary to Beit Yosef. The Maggid Mishneh subsequently writes likewise that a complete melachah performed by a Jew was not permitted even where there is danger to a limb. This implies that a shevus is permitted where there is danger to a limb. Where there is no danger to the limb, it may be done through a non-Jew. This is the incident involving Ameimar, and accords with siman 307, seif 5, where a double shevus is permitted for a partial illness. For a mere discomfort, even through a non-Jew it is forbidden, as stated in seif 1. See also what I wrote in siman 496.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To assist him slightly.</b> This means that the act would be accomplished without the Jew, and the Jew merely assists slightly. For example, a non-Jew applies eye paint and the Jew closes and opens the eye so that the paint enters. If the non-Jew cannot perform the act without the Jew, it is forbidden. See Rashi and Tosafos, Shabbos 93a. It is implied there that even regarding a melachah according to the Torah, mere assistance has no halachic substance.
