נוהגים לקום באשמורת - דסוף הלילה הקב"ה שט בעוה"ז והוא עת רצון ומה שנהגו מר"ח שאז עלה משה בהר סיני לקבל לוחות אחרונות והעבירו שופר במחנה משה עלה להר שלא יטעו עוד אחר ע"ז והוי עת רצון. ואיכא אסמכתא מקרא אני לדודי ודודי לי ר"ת אלול וס"ת עולה מ' כנגד ארבעים יום מר"ח אלול עד יוה"כ כי באלו ארבעים יום התשובה מקובלת להיות לבו קרוב אל דודו בתשובה ואז דודו קרוב לו לקבל תשובתו מאהבה ועוד סמך מקרא ומל ד' אלקיך את לבבך ואת לבב זרעך ר"ת אלול:<br>(א) אלול ואילך - אבל בר"ח גופא א"א סליחות ותחנונים וכן הנוהגים לומר תהלים כל אלול עם תחנונים יה"ר אין להתחיל בר"ח [פמ"ג]:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The custom is to rise before dawn.</b> At the end of the night the Holy One, blessed is He, moves through this world, and it is an auspicious time. The custom begins from Rosh Chodesh Elul because Moshe then ascended Mount Sinai to receive the second Tablets, and a shofar was sounded throughout the camp announcing that Moshe had ascended the mountain, so that the people would not again be led astray after idolatry. It was therefore an auspicious time. There is also an allusion in the verse, “I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine.” The initial letters spell Elul, and the numerical value of the final letters is forty, corresponding to the forty days from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur. During these forty days teshuvah is accepted, so that one's heart draws near to his Beloved through teshuvah, and his Beloved draws near to him to accept his teshuvah with love. Another allusion is found in the verse, “And Hashem your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your offspring,” whose initial letters spell Elul.

<b>(2) Until Yom Kippur.</b> In our lands the custom is to recite the psalm “L'David Hashem Ori,” morning and evening, every day from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur, after the conclusion of the prayer, followed by Kaddish. Our custom is to continue through Shemini Atzeres, inclusive. On a day with Musaf, it is recited after Shacharis before Ein Kamocha, and in the evening after Minchah. In places where it is recited after the conclusion of the prayer on Rosh Chodesh, the psalm Barchi Nafshi should be said first. Likewise, in places where it is recited after Shacharis and the Shir Shel Yom is also recited after Shacharis, the Shir Shel Yom should come first, as stated by the acharonim.

**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(3) From Rosh Chodesh onward, etc.</b> Some begin on the first day of Rosh Chodesh, and some begin on the second day, as stated in Derech HaChaim. A shofar should be sounded every morning after the prayer. The custom is also to recite ten chapters of Tehillim publicly on every weekday from Rosh Chodesh Elul onward. In the Yehi Ratzon recited before them, the words “which contain seventy years” should be omitted. Since a complete book of Tehillim is not being recited, the prayer afterward should say, “In the merit of the chapters of Tehillim that we have recited before You,” etc. Kaddish is recited after the Yehi Ratzon. If ten people were not present when the Tehillim were recited and they wait for another person to complete the minyan, they should leave one chapter unrecited, recite it after the minyan is complete, and then recite Kaddish. During the days of teshuvah between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, more than ten chapters are recited each day so that Tehillim will be completed for the third time before Yom Kippur, as stated by Mateh Ephraim.

<b>(4) To recite Selichos.</b> An individual may recite those passages that do not contain the Thirteen Attributes. In a Selichah that mentions the Thirteen Attributes, he should omit those words. Likewise, requests written in Aramaic, such as “Machei u'Masei,” etc., and “Maran di biShmaya,” etc., should not be recited unless ten people are present, as stated by Eliyah Rabbah. When beginning Selichos, one recites Elokeinu v'Elokei Avoseinu, except before a Selichah that begins with one of the names of the Holy One, blessed is He. Abudraham writes that in the phrase “Vayikra v'shem Hashem,” one should pause slightly between “v'shem” and “Hashem.” If no minyan is present when Ashrei is recited, they should begin Selichos, and when a minyan arrives they should recite Kaddish in the middle of Selichos. Eliyah Rabbah writes that they should first recite three verses once a minyan is present. If a minyan was present at the beginning of Selichos and some people later left, it appears that Kaddish may nevertheless be recited after Selichos, similar to the law explained in siman 55, as stated by Peri Megadim.

**** ENGLISH ****

<b>(6) From Sunday of the preceding week.</b> Many people have the custom to fast ten days together with Yom Kippur. Four days between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur are always unavailable for fasting, namely the two days of Rosh Hashana, Shabbos Shuvah, and erev Yom Kippur. They must therefore make up four days before Rosh Hashana. In order to establish a fixed starting day, the custom was always set for Sunday. Another reason for establishing four days is that offerings require examination for blemishes during the four days before they are brought. Regarding all the offerings in Parashas Pinchas, the Torah says, “You shall offer an olah,” while regarding Rosh Hashana it says, “You shall make an olah.” This teaches that on Rosh Hashana a person should regard himself as though he were offering himself. Four days were therefore established so that he may inspect all the blemishes of his sins and repent for them. The shaliach tzibbur wraps himself in a tallis when the Thirteen Attributes are recited, even before daylight. He does not recite a blessing over it, although it is a garment designated for daytime use, because he wears it at night and one opinion exempts such a garment from tzitzis at that time, as explained above in siman 18, according to the Levush. The Taz objects that there is no reason for him to enter a situation of doubt. He should therefore not use his own tallis or a communal tallis, which is likewise treated as his own, but should borrow another person's tallis. All opinions agree that he is exempt from tzitzis in that case, provided that he intends to wear it only for honor and not to acquire it.

<b>(7) And a mourner is forbidden, etc.</b> During the twelve months after the death of a father or mother, or during the thirty days after the death of another relative, a mourner may not lead the prayers on Rosh Hashana or Yom Kippur, since those days are treated like festivals in all respects. During the days of Selichos, however, even on erev Rosh Hashana and during the Ten Days of Teshuvah, he may lead. When no other shaliach tzibbur is available, he may lead even on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur. Likewise, on days when Tachanun is not recited, he may lead Shacharis if another person will recite Hallel, but he should not lead Musaf. Chayei Adam writes that it appears the Gra did not permit a mourner to lead even Shacharis on Rosh Chodesh. When no one else is available, however, all agree that he may lead even Musaf.

<b>(11) In Torah and in deeds.</b> It is proper that the shaliach tzibbur and the shofar blower be complete penitents. It is also proper that they study the intentions associated with the prayers and the shofar blasts, as stated in the Zohar. The custom is that one who began to lead the prayers or sound the shofar, even once, should not have the mitzvah given to another person. If he becomes ill, the congregation may appoint someone else, but when he recovers the mitzvah returns to the first person. One who committed sexual immorality is not fit to serve as shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur unless he has repented. If he sees that the prayer assignment is causing a dispute, he should not lead, even though an unsuitable person will lead instead.

(26) They are accustomed to immerse, etc.: and if he cannot immerse because of cold, he should see to pouring nine kavim of water upon his body. And he should not advance to immerse until an hour before midday (Chayei Adam). Some later authorities wrote that it is proper to refrain from marital relations on both nights of Rosh Hashana unless it was a night of immersion, for then he is obligated to fulfill his marital duty, and he should immerse again in the morning. However, those people who eat to satiety of their belly, or Heaven forfend if thought of a woman enters his mind, it is better that he have marital relations rather than, Heaven forfend, come to sin, and he should immerse again in the morning (Chayei Adam).
