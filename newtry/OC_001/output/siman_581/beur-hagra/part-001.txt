**** ENGLISH ****
<b>On Sunday.</b> These are the words of the Ran in the first chapter of Rosh Hashana: And one may ask why a person is judged on Rosh Hashana; for other matters, each is judged in its time: Pesach is the time of grain, etc., as is written in the Gemara. And he answered: this matter we learned from what is written in the Pesikta in the name of Rabbi Eliezer, on the verse "Seek Hashem when He is to be found": on the twenty-fifth of Elul the world was created; and this is what is written in the Gemara: Rabbi Eliezer says, in Tishrei the world was created, referring to the creation of Adam, in which the creation of the world was completed. And we further learn there: you find it said that on Rosh Hashana Adam was created: in the first hour it arose in thought; in the second He consulted with the ministering angels; in the third He gathered his dust; in the fourth He kneaded it; in the fifth He shaped him; in the sixth He made him a golem; in the seventh He cast a soul into him; in the eighth He brought him into Gan Eden; in the ninth he was commanded; in the tenth he sinned; in the eleventh he was judged; in the twelfth he went out acquitted. The Holy One, Blessed be He, said to him: You are a sign for your children: just as you stood before Me in judgment on this day and went out acquitted, so your children are destined to stand before Me in judgment on this day and go out acquitted. And all this: when? In the seventh month, on the first of the month. And according to this, what is written in the third chapter, "According to whom do we pray nowadays 'This day,'" etc.: we say it regarding the formation of Adam; but on the twenty-fifth of Elul is the beginning of the creation of the world; and therefore they were accustomed in Barcelona and its surroundings to rise in the morning watch on the twenty-fifth of Elul, etc.; end of his words, in brief. According to this, in our countries they were accustomed on Sunday, the day of the creation of the world, for since the twenty-fifth of Elul is not a fixed calendar day, they chose the day of the week. And the Ran further wrote there: however, this is according to Rabbi Eliezer that we have come; but according to Rabbi Yehoshua the original difficulty remains. And it appears to me that the Omnipresent wished to merit Israel and to judge them on days of favor, etc. And perhaps from Rosh Hashana the Holy One, Blessed be He, began to be reconciled with Moshe, and on Yom Kippur He was completely reconciled; and thereby it is fine that we hold like Rabbi Yehoshua; and according to this there is no place to rise on the twenty-fifth of Elul, etc. And there are places that advance to the first of Elul, and there is support for their custom, because in it the forty days began, etc., as above. And in this the places disagree: our places hold like the first reasoning and like Rabbi Eliezer, and the places written in the Shulchan Aruch hold like the second reasoning and like Rabbi Yehoshua. And the sugya of the Gemara 27a is like Rabbi Eliezer; but Tosafos there, s.v. "According to whom," etc., hold that the halachah is like Rabbi Yehoshua; see there. And the Ran answered their difficulty and wrote: one may say that when we count the tekufah like Rabbi Yehoshua, it is not because we hold like him, but because in it begins the sprouting of trees and fruits that follow the sun, they began to count from it. And to me it appears that because the tekufah of Nissan begins at the start of the night, for in truth there is no practical difference between the counts; and so one must say according to the Ran's answer, unlike Tosafos 8a, s.v. "for tekufos," etc., who wrote "and it is a wonder," etc., and left it as a wonder; and this is what is written 12a, "the Sages of Israel," etc.
