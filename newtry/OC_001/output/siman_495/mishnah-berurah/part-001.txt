**** ENGLISH ****
{8} Anything that does not lose [flavor]: for kneading and baking and slaughtering and cooking, if one did them on erev Yom Tov there is a deficiency of taste in them: bread kneaded and baked today is not like bread kneaded and baked from yesterday, nor a dish cooked today like a dish cooked from yesterday, nor meat slaughtered today like meat slaughtered from yesterday; therefore they did not decree regarding them. But something that has no spoiling of taste and no loss if done from yesterday: why should he do them on Yom Tov? And the later authorities wrote that we hold in practice like this view. And they further wrote that according to this view it is forbidden to knead on Yom Tov vermicelli (which they call lokshen), for possible that those kneaded from before [Yom Tov] are better; and likewise it is forbidden to cook dried fruits on Yom Tov, for it is known that those cooked from erev Yom Tov are better for eating afterward on Yom Tov. However, if he did not previously have time to cook, and all the more so if he did not previously have the fruits, certainly it is permitted to cook them on Yom Tov; and likewise regarding the kneading mentioned above.

{15} Muktzeh, even though it is permitted, etc.: meaning that above in siman 310 it is explained that there are several kinds of muktzeh that are permitted on Shabbos, such as muktzeh because it stands for commerce, or where he put it into a storehouse and his intent is not to take from it until after some time, or muktzeh because of disgust, and several other kinds; but all this they were lenient specifically on Shabbos, which is severe in people's [eyes], and we are not concerned that if we are lenient with him to carry a muktzeh item he will come to be lenient in other Shabbos prohibitions; but on Yom Tov, which is light for him, for many melachos are permitted to him for food-preparation need, if we are lenient with him regarding muktzeh he will come to be lenient also in other Yom Tov prohibitions; therefore the Sages forbade him to carry it and to eat it. And according to this view, an animal standing for its milk, and a hen for its eggs, and an ox for plowing, it is forbidden to slaughter them on Yom Tov until he prepares them for that from erev Yom Tov; and likewise anything of this sort requires preparation from beforehand; and something that is muktzeh because of nolad is obviously forbidden according to the Mechaber on Yom Tov.

And for practical law one should be stringent like the latter view, at least regarding dried figs and raisins that he put on the roof; and regarding fruits that one puts into a storehouse, it is possible to rely on the first view; and see Biur Halacha.

{23} And it is permitted to prepare, etc.: that is to say, in such a case of fruits and a storehouse where one need not do an act, and by mere speech there is preparation, it is permitted to prepare even on Yom Tov itself from one day for its fellow; for in any case: if the second day is holy, behold today is weekday and it is permitted to prepare; and if today is holy and tomorrow is weekday, behold one needs no preparation at all, and it turns out that he fixes nothing by his preparation so that it should be forbidden for him. And where do we forbid preparation from one Yom Tov for its fellow? Where he does an act, for we are concerned lest tomorrow be weekday, and it turns out that he did an act on Yom Tov for weekday need, like that of siman 503; see there. [And likewise regarding muktzeh of a living creature, such as a hen standing to raise eggs: he cannot designate from one Yom Tov for its fellow unless he designated from erev Yom Tov.] And according to this, per what we wrote to be stringent regarding muktzeh that he designated by hand, such as dried figs and raisins that require marking: if he forgot to mark on erev Yom Tov and remembered on Yom Tov, it is forbidden to make any mark to prepare fruits for the second day of Yom Tov, lest tomorrow be weekday and it turns out that he made a mark on Yom Tov for weekday need.
