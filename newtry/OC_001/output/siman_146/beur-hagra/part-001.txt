<b>ס"ב כיון שהתחיל כו'. </b>רש"י ורמב"ם ומפ' דל"ד שנפתח ובפתחו דקרא לשון התחלה אבל בירושלמי ובמ"ס דמפ' טעמא דפותח ואח"כ מברך שנא' ובפתחו ואח"כ ויברך כו' מ' פתיחה ממש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 2: Once he began, etc.</b> Rashi and Rambam explain that this does not mean specifically when the Sefer Torah was opened, for the word “when he opened” in the verse means the beginning of the reading. However, the Yerushalmi and Maseches Soferim explain that one opens it and afterward recites the blessing, as it is stated, “When he opened it,” and afterward, “Ezra blessed,” etc., which implies actual opening.

<b>ס"ד ויש מחמירין כו'. </b>שנא' ובפתחו עמדו כו' וס' הראשונה כ' דהא אמרי' אין עמידה אלא שתיקה וכן בירושל' אסר לקורא ולמתרגם בישיבה וכן בגמ' רפ"ג מכלל דלכ"ע מותר וכן עיקר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 4: And some are stringent, etc.</b> As it is stated, “And when he opened it, all the people stood,” etc. The first opinion maintains that “standing” means silence, as we say, “There is no standing except silence.” Likewise, the Yerushalmi forbids the reader and translator to sit, and so too the Gemara at the beginning of the third chapter of Megillah. This implies that according to everyone else it is permitted, and that is the primary ruling.
