<b>א) [סעיף א'] כל הדברים כשרים לדפנות.  </b>דכל סוכה הכתוב סכך משמע דדופן לא איקרי סוכה ודנפקא לן דפנות מבסכת בסכת בסכות (סוכה דף ו' ע"ב) מייתורא דקראי ילפינן ולא ממשמעותא הלכך סוכות תעשה באספך מגרנך אסככה הוא קאי. רש"י סוכה י"ב ע"א. ב"י. ט"ז סק"א:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Seif 1] All materials are valid for the walls. </b>For whenever Scripture writes sukkah, it implies the s'chach, since a wall is not called a sukkah. Although we derive the walls from the words basukkos, basukkos, basukkos, in Sukkah 6b, we learn this from the superfluity of the verses and not from their plain implication. Therefore, the verse, "You shall make the festival of Sukkos for yourself when you gather in from your threshing floor," refers to the s'chach. Rashi, Sukkah 12a; Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 1.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) Maharil said </b>that he saw his teacher, Maharam Shalom, mark the boards for the walls of the sukkah with alef, beis, gimel, in order to know the order in which they stood and not change them from year to year. Hagahos Derashos Maharil, Hilchos Sukkah; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 3. So too is it stated in the Yerushalmi concerning the boards of the Mishkan: R' Ami said, "You shall erect the Mishkan according to its rule." Is there a rule for pieces of wood? Rather, a board that merited being placed in the north shall always be placed in the north, and similarly, one placed in the south shall be placed in the south. Shelah, 245a; Eliyah Rabbah, os 17. Certainly, however, this does not invalidate the sukkah and is only an act of piety. See above, siman 8, os 17.

<b>יב) [סעיף ב'] דפנות הסוכה אם היו שתים וכו'  </b>דתנן (סוכה ו' ע"ב) שתים כהלכתן ושלישית אפילו טפח וצריך צורת הפתח הלכך אם יש לה ב' דפנות זו אצל זו עושה טפח מרווח ומעמידו בפחות מג' לאחד מן הכתלים (ר"ל מן הדפנות) ויעמיד קנה כנגד סוף הכותל (ר"ל סוף הדופן) וישים קנה עליו ועל הטפח וכשירה. טור. ור"ל שיעמיד קנה בסוף אותו צד שיש בו הטפח כנגד סוף הדופן שכנגדו וישים קנה על זה הקנה שבסוף אותו צד ועל הטפח כדי שיהיה צורת פתח. ודבר זה שצריך ג' דפנות ושלישי אפי' טפח למדו שם בגמ' מקראי דכתיב בסכת בסכת בסכות ר"ל שנים חסרים ואחד מלא בוא"ו חשיב שנים הרי ארבעה חד לגופיה וג' לדפנות ואתאי הלכתא הלכה למשה מסיני וגרעתה לשלישית ואוקמה אטפח יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>12) [Seif 2] If the walls of the sukkah were two, etc. </b>For we learned in the Mishnah, Sukkah 6b: Two walls according to their full halachic requirements, and a third wall of even one tefach, are sufficient, and the form of a doorway is required. Therefore, if it has two walls adjacent to one another, he makes a slightly wider than one-tefach strip and positions it within three tefachim of one of the walls, meaning one of the existing walls. He then positions a pole opposite the end of the wall, meaning the end of the opposite existing wall, and places a pole across it and the one-tefach strip, and it is valid. Tur. This means that he should position a pole at the end of the side containing the one-tefach strip, opposite the end of the wall facing it, and place another pole over this pole at the end of that side and over the one-tefach strip, thereby forming the shape of a doorway. The requirement of three walls, with the third being even one tefach, is derived there in the Gemara from the verses that write basukkas, basukkas, basukkos. Two are written defectively and one is written fully with a vav, which counts as two, totaling four. One is needed for the subject itself, leaving three for the walls. The received law, a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, then reduces the third wall and establishes it at one tefach; see there.

<b>13) Ibid. If the walls of the sukkah were two, etc. </b>It is permitted to carry within it even on Shabbos, because just as it is considered a partition regarding sukkah, it is considered a partition regarding Shabbos. Tur. This is from the Gemara there, 7a. Rashi explains that although three partitions are required on Shabbos to create a private domain, on the Shabbos of Sukkos one may rely upon the walls of the sukkah. Thus, if one made a sukkah in the public domain adjacent to the entrance of his house, and it has only two full walls and a third of even one tefach, he may carry from it into his house and from his house into it; see there. The Levush writes that although for Shabbos purposes outside the context of sukkah one requires a four-tefach board, a crossbeam, or a sidepost at the fourth wall, in a sukkah it is permitted without any additional correction. This is because, since it is considered a partition by virtue of the received law regarding sukkah, it is likewise considered a partition regarding Shabbos. Nevertheless, the Ran writes that in practice it is proper to be stringent, as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there. So too wrote Chayei Adam, klal 146, os 3, that this leniency applies only in pressing circumstances, but otherwise it is proper to be stringent. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, os 11.
