**** ENGLISH ****
<b>At the beginning of the siman, in Magen Avraham, page 6:</b> He makes a strip of four tefachim and a bit and places it, etc. The text should read: for we know the reasoning that a partition of vertical elements is not a partition. This means that according to the initial assumption, that whatever is considered a partition for Shabbos in the case of a caravan helps for sukkah, there is no reason to distinguish between making four walls and making three. Therefore Tosafot answered that a partition made of vertical elements alone, which helps for Shabbos only for a caravan, does not help here. It is then reasonable to distinguish: if he makes four walls for the sukkah, which is an added strength, then partitions of vertical elements alone can help, since they help for Shabbos at least for a caravan. But when he makes only three walls, the baraita too agrees that partitions of vertical elements alone do not help, since they help only for a caravan.

<b>In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3:</b> But in a large sukkah, etc. His words are puzzling. What appears to me is that the Magen Avraham is uncertain whether we require that the third wall also be connected, at least by lavud, or not. Therefore, if it is more than three tefachim away, where there is no lavud, it is invalid. Since by Torah law we have a received tradition requiring three walls, only two according to their law and a third even of a tefach, all three must perhaps be joined. Although the Gemara there says that there are two joined walls, implying that we do not require the third wall to be connected even by lavud, one can say that this was said only regarding the generous tefach: since there are two walls joined like a Greek gamma, a generous tefach is enough for the third even though it is not a standing measure fit in itself. Nevertheless, the third wall still must be near the middle wall within less than three tefachim. This doubt applies even if he made the middle wall a full partition of seven tefachim and extended the third from there more than three tefachim away, and the same applies to exactly three, since lavud applies only with less than three. For this reason the Magen Avraham cited the Ran, who wrote that this is called two joined walls, etc. That is, the Ran wrote that one must be careful that there not be openings at all the corners of the sukkah, for we require two joined walls; and in a sukkah made like an alleyway, by means of a strip of four tefachim, there are also two joined walls, etc. From this it seems that two joined walls are sufficient even though the third is distant and there is an empty space between the strip and the wall of four tefachim; it is valid, for since there are two joined walls, this empty space is considered an entrance. On this he wrote: and perhaps, etc. Meaning, this is not necessarily proven from the Talmud. In truth one can say that the case where it is validated by a strip of four tefachim is specifically in a small sukkah, meaning one smaller than ten tefachim across in such a way that there is not three tefachim between the strip and the wall, and we say lavud on this side and on that side. What the Shulchan Aruch wrote, that he places it within less than three tefachim adjacent to one of the two walls, does not mean that it is far from the other wall. Rather, it means that on one side he must ensure that it not be less than three tefachim, for with less than that there is no measure of seven tefachim; but on the second side he need not be particular, for even if only two tefachim or less remain it is valid, since there is lavud. But if it is three tefachim or more away, where there is no lavud and the third wall is not connected to the two walls at all, it is invalid, for the third wall stands by itself and does not join to validate the sukkah. This is the intent of the Magen Avraham. What the Chemed Moshe, the Peri Megadim, and the Machatzit HaShekel objected, that if it is a small sukkah and is not three tefachim away, the dispute about tzurat hapesach is not relevant, since on the second side too there is lavud, with respect, they did not understand this well. According to the view that requires a tzurat hapesach with a strip of four tefachim, it does not help even if there is lavud on both sides. In the end, the middle wall exists only because of lavud, and lavud alone does not help to give it the status of a wall. This is compelled, for when there is a complete partition of seven tefachim, no tzurat hapesach is required according to anyone, as the Ran wrote and the Rema cited in the gloss. Nevertheless, that view holds that if the permission is through a strip, then even though by means of lavud there are three tefachim, since it exists only by means of lavud it does not help unless a tzurat hapesach is joined to it. This strengthens it and gives it the status of a wall. What help is there in its being close to the two walls and having the law of lavud? Granted, if the purpose of the tzurat hapesach were to connect the sukkah so that it should be one unit, then lavud on both sides would also help. But in truth the tzurat hapesach does not come for this; it comes only to give the strip the significance of a wall, and specifically the joining of a tzurat hapesach is required even if it is lavud on all sides. This also appears from the Ran's words, which the Magen Avraham brought in the previous se'if katan, where the Ran wrote to distinguish that with a generous tefach, since there is no standing portion of a proper measure, it does not help if he places it in the middle and there is lavud on this side and that side. It implies from his words that with a strip of four tefachim, if he places it in the middle, it does help by the law of lavud, unlike a tefach. At first glance there is no reason for the matter, for the law of lavud should apply just as well to a generous tefach. Rather, the reason is that the Ran follows his own view: with a strip of four tefachim, no tzurat hapesach is required, for even without a tzurat hapesach it is considered a wall by virtue of the strip, which is a proper measure. Therefore if he places it within less than three tefachim on this side and that side, it helps. But with a generous tefach, even if he places it in the middle it does not help, since in the end the wall is made only by lavud, and lavud is not effective without a tzurat hapesach. According to this, for the Rambam, who holds that even a strip of four tefachim is not effective to be considered a significant wall until he also makes a tzurat hapesach, even if he places the strip in such a way that it is lavud on both sides it does not help, since there is no tzurat hapesach. This is clear. Now the Machatzit HaShekel and the Mekor Chayim wrote to emend the text to: more than ten; and according to this one must also add the word amot, and say that it speaks where he made a tzurat hapesach and stated the law simply according to the Rambam. But the Magen Avraham did not mention tzurat hapesach at all in his words. Also, why would he enter unnecessarily into this dispute of the Rambam, when the Shulchan Aruch implies like the first opinion? It is also difficult why he left the matter requiring examination, for if there is a breach of more than ten the matter is clear; it depends on the dispute below. It appears instead that the Magen Avraham means that anything more than three interrupts between it and the wall. Even according to the Rambam, who holds that one must make a tzurat hapesach, nevertheless if it is more than three, because the sukkah is ten tefachim wide so that after making the strip another three remain, a tzurat hapesach does not help, since a tzurat hapesach is not effective when it is not four tefachim wide, as written in the Hagahot Maimoniyot at the beginning of Hilchot Sukkah, cited by the Peri Megadim. This is also explicit in the previous se'if katan, where he copied from the Ran that the tzurat hapesach is four, unlike the emendation of Maharam of Tiktin there. If so, the Magen Avraham's statement that more than three is invalid is understandable, because one cannot permit it on account of tzurat hapesach, since a tzurat hapesach is not effective with less than four. However, it seems that even where there are four tefachim, so that there is a measure fit for making a tzurat hapesach, there is still room for the Magen Avraham's doubt. For example, in a sukkah fourteen tefachim wide, he inserted a reed in se'if 4 from the strip and made a tzurat hapesach there. What the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, that he should place a reed from the strip to the other wall, is not precise; even if a tzurat hapesach of only four tefachim is made by means of an intermediate reed, it helps to strengthen it so that the name of wall should rest upon it. Through the slightly-less-than-four strip, the strip itself, and the tzurat hapesach, there are eleven tefachim, and through this it has the significance of a wall. Nevertheless, there still remains an empty space of three tefachim between the tzurat hapesach and the wall. According to what the Magen Avraham wrote, and perhaps, etc., it is invalid in such a case, since the third wall is not joined to the two walls. Even if there are four tefachim, where it appears from the Ran that since there are two joined walls, even if there is an entrance at the other corner it is valid, the Magen Avraham is uncertain about this, for perhaps the third wall too must be joined. If so, it does not help if there is an entrance there, and even a tzurat hapesach does not help to make it called joined, as the Ran wrote there. Yet this requires examination, for below in se'if katan 7 the Magen Avraham cited the Ran's words simply, that if two partitions are connected, even if there is an entrance at the other corner it is permitted. And the Ran there is speaking even where the partitions are connected by lavud and a strip. If I were not afraid to say so, I would say that a scribal error fell into the Magen Avraham, and the text should read the opposite: and perhaps specifically in a large sukkah it is valid, but in a small sukkah, where the third is more than three tefachim away from it, it is invalid; and this requires examination. This is somewhat reasonable from the fact that he began by saying: and it implies that even if the sukkah is very wide, etc.; on this he wrote: and perhaps, etc. That is, the implication there in the Ran is only in a large sukkah, where in the empty space between the strip there are four tefachim or more, so it can be called an entrance. But in a small sukkah, when it is more than three away, there is no lavud and it also does not have the status of an entrance, since it is less than four; it is invalid, because it does not join to become a wall for the sukkah, as I wrote. See Taz se'if katan 6, which implies that even with more than three but not reaching four, it is possible to consider it an entrance, unlike the Hagahot Maimoniyot. So too it implies in siman 363; see there. It is also difficult, in my humble opinion, to establish the law of a strip of four tefachim and a bit, according to the one who requires a tzurat hapesach, only in a sukkah ten tefachim wide, where it is possible to make a tzurat hapesach of four. But in less than that it would not apply. Perhaps the Ran required a tzurat hapesach of four only in the matter he discusses there, with a generous tefach, where the tzurat hapesach comes to complete a partition of seven; in that case we require a tzurat hapesach according to its proper law, with four tefachim, the measure of an entrance. But with a strip of four, where a partition of seven is already made, even according to the Rambam and the Geonim cited by the Rosh, who nevertheless require a tzurat hapesach, it is a stringency so that it should appear closed along the whole length of the wall and serve as a public recognition, lest people err and think that a strip of four alone is a wall and say that any empty space, even more than three, joins. Therefore they required a tzurat hapesach as a recognition that this empty space, which is more than three, must be fenced by means of a tzurat hapesach so as to appear closed. This accords with the Ran's statement that regarding walls according to their law we are more stringent in several ways for sukkah walls than for Shabbos walls; see there. Therefore we are not particular in this tzurat hapesach if it does not contain four, and this tzurat hapesach suffices when it fences the empty space as it is, whether three or more. Regarding the main partition, we rely on lavud, so that together with the strip there are seven. So it appears from the Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Riaz, who wrote: if there were two walls opposite one another and it resembled an open alleyway, he makes a third wall between them, a strip of four tefachim and a bit, and places it within less than three tefachim of one of the walls, and it is considered closed. The empty space from that strip until the other wall, which is three or more than three, must be permitted with a tzurat hapesach; and from this he requires, etc. See there. It is explicit that even with more than three, a tzurat hapesach helps in such a case. But the Magen Avraham was uncertain about this: perhaps, although it is not considered a breach to forbid it, nevertheless it cannot be considered an actual entrance, and it is an interruption between one wall and the other. One may also say that here, where the breached area exceeds the standing area, for standing area that exists through lavud is not considered standing, as Tosafot wrote, cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 363; and in such a case we do not say that it is like an entrance. See below in Magen Avraham se'if katan 6 and the Taz there. Since there are arguments in both directions, he left it requiring examination.
