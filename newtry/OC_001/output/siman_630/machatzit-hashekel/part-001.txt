<b>ור"ל דמותר לעשות כל מחיצות הסכה ע"י קנים ושלא יהי' בין קנה לחברו ג' טפחים.</b> דמחיצ' של שתי לחוד מהני ועז"כ מהרש"א והיינו לר"י בר"י. דשם בערובין דף ט"ז ע"ב פליגי ר' יודא ור"י בר"י. ביחיד או שיירא דהיינו ג' בני אדם ששבתו בבקעה והקיפו מחיצות לטלטל ע"י ועשו המחיצות ע"י קנים שאין בין אחד לחברו ג' טפחים דהיינו שתי. או ע"י חבלים לרחבו ואין בין חבל לחברו ג' טפחים דהיינו ערב. ס"ל לר' יודא בין לאדם א' ובין לשנים לא הותר לטלטל ע"י מחיצות גרועות הללו כ"א סאתים ר"ל שלא יהי' ההיקף מחזיק יותר מבית סאתים. אבל אם היו ג' בני אדם שאז נקראים שיירא הותר להם להקיף ע"י מחיצות גרועות הנ"ל כל צורכן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Meaning that one may make all the partitions of the sukkah with reeds, provided that there is not three tefachim between one reed and the next.</b> For a partition made of vertical elements alone is effective. On this Maharsha wrote: this is according to Rabbi Yosei bar Yehudah. For there in Eruvin 16b, Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Yosei bar Yehudah dispute regarding an individual or a caravan, meaning three people, who rested in an open valley and surrounded themselves with partitions in order to carry by means of them. They made the partitions with reeds, where there was not three tefachim between one and the next, which is vertical elements, or with ropes across the width, where there was not three tefachim between one rope and the next, which is horizontal elements. Rabbi Yehudah holds that for one person and for two people, carrying is permitted by means of these weak partitions only up to two se'ah, meaning that the enclosure may not contain more than a beit se'atayim. But if there are three people, who are then called a caravan, they are permitted to enclose by means of these weak partitions as much as they need.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But a partition of horizontal elements, etc.</b> That is, he comes to resolve what Tosafot wrote there earlier, s.v. man de'amar, etc., concerning Rav Chisda's statement that a mat of four tefachim and a bit is permitted in a sukkah as a wall. How is it done? He hangs it in the middle, less than three tefachim below near the ground and less than three above near the s'chach. This is the law mentioned in se'if 9. Tosafot asked: why not suffice with two ropes, each a half-tefach and a bit wide, placing one near the s'chach within less than three tefachim, and the second within less than three tefachim of the first and also within less than three tefachim of the ground; it would be a partition ten tefachim high, as stated in Eruvin. Tosafot answered: there in Eruvin we say that they permitted this only for a caravan. These are Tosafot's words, referring to the Gemara in Eruvin 16 mentioned above. Maharsha found this difficult, for on the contrary, what is permitted for a caravan is also permitted for sukkah, as we say regarding reed by reed with less than three tefachim between them, as above. One cannot say that Tosafot is following Rabbi Yosei bar Yehudah, for Tosafot wrote that it was permitted only for a caravan, whereas according to Rabbi Yosei bar Yehudah even for a caravan it was not permitted in that way. Tosafot must therefore be following Rabbi Yehudah. On this Maharsha wrote that specifically partitions of vertical elements help for sukkah, meaning reed by reed with less than three tefachim between them. But horizontal elements, meaning by ropes, even according to Rabbi Yehudah, who permits such a case for a caravan, are nevertheless worse than vertical elements and were not permitted for sukkah. He brings some proof from Eruvin that vertical elements are preferable to horizontal elements. The same Tosafot, s.v. man de'amar, also mentioned that one might have thought to distinguish, with regard to two applications of lavud, between vertical elements and horizontal elements.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But it does not help where there are only three walls.</b> From the plain understanding of the Magen Avraham's words, it sounds that he comes to explain that according to his interpretation we do not need the great forced explanation written above according to Maharsha, to distinguish between vertical and horizontal elements. Rather, there is only a distinction between making three walls and making four walls. However, this is not so, for the Magen Avraham too must accept that forced distinction in Tosafot, s.v. mahu deteima, etc. Tosafot there asked regarding Rav Chisda's statement that a mat of four tefachim, etc., why two ropes, each half a tefach and a bit wide, would not suffice, and they answered that in Eruvin we say they permitted it only for a caravan, as above. Maharsha's question then remains: the baraita permitted reed by reed for sukkah, and that too was permitted only for a caravan. We must therefore distinguish between vertical and horizontal elements, as above. In that passage one cannot distinguish and explain as the Magen Avraham did, between four walls and three walls. First, regarding Tosafot s.v. bepachot mishloshah, where they asked on Rabbi Eliezer's statement about a strip of four tefachim that two reeds should suffice, one can reasonably read their intent as the Magen Avraham did, since this distinction is mentioned in their words when they wrote that there is no basis to distinguish between a sukkah of three walls, etc.; only at first, before they knew the reasoning, the distinction did not appear valid to them, as the Magen Avraham wrote. But in Tosafot s.v. mahu deteima, where there is no mention at all of a distinction between three or four walls, how could Tosafot leave their words unexplained? Rather, one must say as Maharsha did, to distinguish between vertical and horizontal elements. This distinction is mentioned in Tosafot s.v. man de'amar concerning another matter; see there.
