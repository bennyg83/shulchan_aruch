**** ENGLISH ****
We say in Sukkah, end of page 16, that he makes a board of four and a bit and places it within less than three of the wall, and it permits because of a wall. Tosafos wrote that they hold that we say two walls according to their law. This is difficult: let him make two reeds, each one a half-handbreadth and a bit, place one within less than three near the wall and the second within less than three of the first. One can answer because we say at the end of the first chapter of Eruvin: any partition that is not of both vertical and horizontal elements is not a partition. These are their words. The Maharsha wrote that this is according to Rabbi Yosei bar Yehudah, but we rule that reed by reed within less than three is a partition for a sukkah, as stated in the Gemara, page 7. But a horizontal partition with gaps less than three does not help. So wrote the Rosh, chapter 2, siman 40. This is a great pressure: that horizontal does not help because it helps only for a caravan, but vertical helps even though it too helps only for a caravan. The Ran wrote regarding a board of four and a bit, etc., that it is speaking of a sukkah made like an alleyway, and specifically with a board of four, where the standing material has a fitting measure; but with a handbreadth placed in the middle, less than three to this side and less than three to that side, it is not enough, since the standing material does not have a fitting measure. These are his words. So too he wrote regarding the need for a form of a doorway, that we require a broad handbreadth within less than three and a form of a doorway of three. So the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote, that we require seven handbreadths, the valid measure of a sukkah. If so, one must say that the Baraisa which teaches that reed by reed within less than three helps is when he makes four walls; but when he makes three walls, he needs two complete adjacent walls, and the third with a handbreadth and a form of a doorway. So the Ran wrote explicitly regarding "two according to their law"; see there. If so, we need not press the words of Tosafos to say that they are not according to the halachah, as the Rashal wrote. Rather, they too hold that since it is a partition only for a caravan, as written in siman 360, it is not a complete partition and does not help unless there are four walls of reed by reed within less than three. So too it is implied in the Tur, se'if 7, and this is the intent of the Rosh and the Tur. What Tosafos wrote, that there is no distinction, was before we knew this reasoning. <b>Opposite the wall.</b> This means opposite the end of the second corner, like this [a diagram appears in the source], a handbreadth, and as I wrote above in the name of the Ran and Hagahos Maimoniyos.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the wall reaches.</b> This requires analysis: what is the wall doing here? The reed on top rests upon the handbreadth and the reed opposite it, as I wrote above; and so it is explicit in the Mordechai: "and if the two reeds reach," etc. Nevertheless, this requires analysis, for in the Yerushalmi it states: a corner [meaning a woven vine stretched from tree to tree] shelters in a sukkah, but the end of the s'chach does not shelter. What is the difference between this and that? This was made for that, and this was not made for that. Until here. If so, how did the Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos permit it even though it was not made for that? This requires analysis. Therefore one should be stringent. See the Tur, se'if 7, and siman 363:26.

<b>בד"א כו</b>. דברי הרב"י צ"ע דז"ל הר"ן סיכך ע"ג מבוי שיש לו לחי כשרה מיירי במבוי שאינו מפולש וסוכה כלפי חוץ רחוק מדופן האמצעי וכו' ומוכח בגמ' שהעוש' סוכתו בכה"ג כיון שאינ' מפולשת לגמרי כמו העושה סוכתו במבוי באמצע החצר רחוק מדפנות החצר נתרת בטפח שוחק פחות מג' סמוך לדופן כשם שנתרית בו סוכה דאית לה ב' דפנות דעריבן ולא הצריכו פס ד' אלא בסוכה שהיא מפולשת ואין דופן אמצעי כנגד' עכ"ל, וכוונתו כמ"ש התוס' ע"ש והרב"י הבין מ"ש כמו העושה סוכתו וכו' ר"ל דאף זה כשר בטפח וקשה דא"כ תלי תניא בדלא תניא אבל כוונתו דר"ל דהעושה סוכתו בכהאי גוונא כלומר שיש ג' דפנות אף על פי שהיא מפולש' שסיכך רחוק מדופן ג' מ"מ אינה מפולשת לגמרי כמו העושה סוכתו כמבוי באמצע החצר וכו' דהתם ליכא ב' דפנות דעריבן [אבל הכא דאיכא שני דפנו' דעריבן] סגי בטפח שוחק כנ"ל ברור בדברי הר"ן דאטו בגמ' סוכ' במבוי מיירי ע"פ בשד' במקו' שאין מחיצה כנגדה אלא ע"כ לא מהני אא"כ איכא ב' דפנות דעריבן וע' בתו' ורש"י דף י"ח ע"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In what case, etc.</b> The words of Rabbi Yosef Karo require analysis. This is the language of the Ran: "If one roofed over an alleyway that has a side-post, it is valid" refers to a closed alleyway, and the sukkah is toward the outside, distant from the middle wall, etc. It is proven in the Gemara that one who makes his sukkah in such a case, since it is not completely open through like one who makes his sukkah in an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, distant from the courtyard walls, is permitted with a broad handbreadth within less than three near the wall, just as a sukkah that has two adjacent walls is permitted with it. They required a board of four only in a sukkah that is open through-and-through and has no middle wall opposite it. These are his words. His intent is as Tosafos wrote; see there. Rabbi Yosef Karo understood what he wrote, "like one who makes his sukkah, etc.," to mean that this too is valid with a handbreadth. This is difficult, for if so he makes something taught dependent on something not taught. Rather, his intent is that one who makes his sukkah in such a manner - meaning that there are three walls - although it is open because he placed s'chach distant from the third wall, nevertheless it is not completely open like one who makes his sukkah like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, etc.; for there, there are not two adjacent walls. But here, where there are two adjacent walls, a broad handbreadth suffices. This is clear in the words of the Ran. For was the Gemara speaking of a sukkah in an alleyway specifically where there is no partition opposite it? Rather, perforce it does not help unless there are two adjacent walls. See Tosafos and Rashi, page 18b.

<b>סיכך ע"ג מבוי</b>. המחבר סתם כלשון הרמב"ם ורש"י והרא"ש דס"ל דאפי במבוי מפולש מכשירי' דמדאורייתא לחי מחיצה מעלויתא היא וה"ל ג' מחיצות ואף על גב דמדרבנן אסור לטלטל בג' מחיצות בשבת דסוכה מותר משום מצות סוכה ודעת הר"ן ותוס' דדוק' במבוי סתו' כמ"ש ובפסין אף על גב דלא התירו אלא לעולי רגלי' מ"מ כיון דמדאורייתא מחיצה מעלייתא הוא ורבנן הוא דגזרו וגבי סוכה לא גזרו כ"כ התו', וק' דא"כ למה לא התירו במבוי מפולש מה"ט וי"ל דשאני התם דאין לו היתר כלל לעולי רגלים משא"כ כאן דיש היתר לעולי רגלים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He roofed over an alleyway.</b> The Mechaber wrote plainly like the language of the Rambam, Rashi, and the Rosh, who hold that even in an open alleyway we validate it, for by Torah law a side-post is a full partition and it therefore has three partitions. Although rabbinically it is forbidden to carry with three partitions on Shabbos, regarding sukkah it is permitted because of the mitzvah of sukkah. The view of the Ran and Tosafos is that this applies specifically in a closed alleyway, as I wrote. Regarding boards around wells, although they permitted them only for those going up for the festivals, nevertheless since by Torah law they are a full partition and the Sages decreed, regarding sukkah they did not decree; so wrote Tosafos. This is difficult: if so, why did they not permit an open alleyway for this reason? One can say that there it is different, since it has no permission at all for those going up for the festivals, whereas here there is permission for those going up for the festivals.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Others disagree.</b> They hold that even through posts we do not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, since the two walls are not joined like a gamma-shape, as written at the end of siman 361. It also implies that the beam must be four wide, as written there. The Bach inferred from the language of the Tur that a width of one handbreadth is enough; see there, but one should not be lenient. According to what is written in Chochmas Shlomo, it is invalid because the roof was made only for the needs of the portico and not for the needs of the sukkah. Maharsha disagrees with him, and so wrote the Bach, that specifically when one makes the sukkah adjacent to the portico it is invalid, since the roof was made inward and not outward. But if two walls are like a gamma-shape and on the third side posts project one handbreadth, it helps. However, it is implied in the Rosh that a form of a doorway is also required. The Bach wrote that this is specifically when it is visible from outside and flush from inside; but if it is visible inside and flush outside, it does not require a form of a doorway. This is not decisive; see there, for I have abbreviated.

<b>שיקשור</b>. וצריך שתהא מתוחה למעלה או שתהא גבוה כ"כ שאף שתמות ותפול תהיה גבוה י', ואם יש חלל בין רגליה גבוה מג' צריך לגודרו [גמ'] והמאור ובס' יראים כ' דלא חיישי' למיתה כסתם משנה פ"ג דגיטין וע' בגמ' ותוס':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>That he tie.</b> It must be stretched above, or it must be so high that even if it dies and falls it will still be ten high. If there is an open space between its legs higher than three, he must fence it off [Gemara]. The Ba'al HaMa'or and Sefer Yere'im wrote that we are not concerned for death, like the plain Mishnah in the third chapter of Gittin. See the Gemara and Tosafos.

<b>שלא ידע</b>. דאז הוי כמחיצ' העשויה בשוגג אבל בהמה הוי כעץ בעלמא ואסור להעמידה בי"ט לדופן ג' וכ"כ רי"ו:
