**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And also in this, etc.</b> There is a scribal error here in the Shulchan Aruch and the gloss, and this is how it should read: "that he place a reed from the board to the other wall" [gloss]. But if there is a wall of seven without <i>lavud</i>, one does not need a form of a doorway here until the end of the wall, etc. After this gloss belongs: "and some say that this form of a doorway is not needed." This is the view that the Beis Yosef brings in the name of the Rosh, that here, where there is a repair with a board of four, it suffices without a form of a doorway, and a form of a doorway is needed only in se'if 2, where the repair is with a board of one handbreadth, which is a lesser repair. This is unlike here, where there is a good repair with a board of four. The first view holds that here, since it is open through-and-through, it is worse than above and requires a good repair; the good repair here is no better than the lesser repair there, and therefore here too a form of a doorway is required.

<b>כל הפרוץ. </b> פי' חשבי' הפרוץ משני רוחות הפרוצי' לגמרי עם הפירצה שבחלל הפתחים והוא בכלל מרובה על העומד שיש בשני המחיצות והטפח ואז מותר שכ"ה מבואר ברש"י וז"ל דכי מצרפת להו בהדי ב' הפרוצות ה"ל פרוץ מרובה ושרי עכ"ל. משמע אבל עכ"פ יהיה במחיצות עצמן עומד מרובה על הפרוץ. וא"כ קשה הא כ' רש"י לפני זה דדופן סוכה כשר אם יש מחיצה שתי בלא ערב אע"פ שיש ביניהם אויר פחות מג' והקנים של המחיצה כמלא אצבע אפ"ה כשר מטעם לבוד ובזה ודאי יש במחיצה עצמה פרוץ מרובה על העומד בלי צירוף מחיצות. וצ"ל דיש חילוק בדבר כשאין פירצה גדולה רק כל הפירצות כל א' בפ"ע פחות מג' אז מותר אפי' בפרוץ מרובה באותה מחיצה כיון דאמרי' לבוד הוה כל העומד כסתום משא"כ אם יש פירצה א' גדולה יותר מג"ט וצריך אתה להתיר מטעם דהוה כפתח אז צריך שיהי' עכ"פ באותה מחיצה עומד מרובה דאל"כ אין שם פתח על הפרוץ אלא הקולא בזה דאע"פ שבכלל הפירצה הוה פרוץ מרובה וזה מבואר בפשיטות להמעיין ברש"י ותמהני שלא נתבאר חילוק זה בפוסקים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All the breached area.</b> Meaning: we calculate the breached area of the two sides that are completely breached together with the breach in the open space of the openings, and it is in total greater than the standing material that exists in the two partitions and the handbreadth; then it is permitted. This is explicit in Rashi, and this is his language: "For when you combine them with the two breached sides, it is breached more than standing, and it is permitted." These are his words. This implies that in any event, in the partitions themselves, the standing material must be greater than the breached area. If so, this is difficult, for Rashi wrote before this that a sukkah wall is valid if there is a vertical partition without horizontal pieces, even though there is open air of less than three between them, and the reeds of the partition are only the width of a finger; nevertheless it is valid because of <i>lavud</i>. In that case, the partition itself certainly has more breached than standing, without combining the other partitions. One must say that there is a distinction. When there is no large breach, but all the breaches are each individually less than three, then it is permitted even where the breached area is greater than the standing material in that partition, since we say <i>lavud</i> and all the standing material is as though closed. But if there is one breach larger than three handbreadths, and you must permit it because it is like an opening, then there must in any event be more standing than breached in that partition; otherwise the breached area does not have the name of an opening. The leniency here is only that even though, in the total breach, the breached area is greater. This is clear and simple to one who examines Rashi, and I am astonished that this distinction was not explained in the poskim.

מחיצה ג' כיון שיש שם גם צ"ה כמ"ש. ע' במקור הדין בשיטת הטור ותמצא שאין פקפוק כמ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He roofed over a portico.</b> Meaning: this portico has two walls opposite one another, part of it is roofed, and near the roofing until the other end he placed the s'chach. It emerges that this sukkah has only two walls. If there are posts, meaning projecting pillars on the third side, then although in another sukkah that has only two walls and is made like an alleyway we say in se'if 3 that it requires a board of four and a bit, here posts suffice. Since there are posts, we say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, meaning that the edge of the portico roof, the roofing that the portico has, descends and seals; it therefore gives the sukkah a third wall. But without posts, we do not say that it descends and seals. So the Maggid Mishneh explained these words of the Rambam. These posts, although they are not visible inside the sukkah but only to one standing outside, nevertheless help, for the posts are not the sukkah's partition; rather, through them we say that the edge of the portico roof descends and seals. About this the Rema wrote that others disagree, namely Rashi, Tosafos, and the Rosh. They explain that the portico has no walls on the side of the sukkah; rather, one wants to join the walls of the house to the sukkah. See the Tur for their approach. According to them, there are many posts for the portico, with less than three between each one, and the posts become partitions for the sukkah. Then one need not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals. About this the Rema wrote that one should not make the sukkah in such a manner and say that the edge of the roof descends and seals is effective in place of the third wall. The Levush wrote that after the fact it is valid in this case. <b>And I</b> did not want to elaborate here in explaining the words of the Tur according to the approach of Tosafos, but it appears to me to learn one law from their explanation. This is regarding one who wants to make a sukkah in his house and break open the roof above, as people do, and the walls of the house will be the walls of the sukkah. That is, he wants to make the sukkah in the corner of the east wall and the south wall, and there is one beam lying all year above opposite it along the length of the house near those corners, and the sukkah will be in that corner section until the beam. It emerges that the sukkah has only two walls of the house, and for the third it has only the beam above, regarding which we say that it descends and seals downward, so that it will have three walls, which is enough. He need only place two reeds beneath the beam, one near the wall of the house and one at the place where the third wall ends, meaning at the place where the sukkah's s'chach ends. The reason for these reeds is so that there will be a form of a doorway in the third wall because of the ceiling over the two reeds, for here there is no actual third partition, only through the edge of the roof descending and sealing; therefore a form of a doorway is required. The Tur's approach required posts only in a sukkah outside a portico, because we do not say that the portico roof descends and seals in place of the third partition, since the portico partitions are made to serve inside it and not outside them, as the Gemara says in this matter. This is unlike here, with the walls of the house and the sukkah inside the house, where we certainly say that the edge of the roof descends and seals in place of the third partition. As for why we do not say regarding an ordinary sukkah that the edge of the roof descends and seals, and why two walls alone are not enough: there, the roof belongs only to the sukkah and lacks permanence, so one cannot say that it descends and seals, as the Ran wrote about this. But here the beam is permanent, since it is made for the needs of the house and lies there; therefore one can properly say that the edge of the roof descends and seals in place of the third partition, since there is also a form of a doorway there, as I wrote. Examine the source of the law in the Tur's approach and you will find that there is no uncertainty as to what I wrote.
