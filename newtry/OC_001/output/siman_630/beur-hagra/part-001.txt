<b>ס"א  כל הדברים כו'. </b>היינו לדעת הרא"ש שכ' דטעם דקבע עיקר אבל לפסק הרשב"א והר"ן לדעת האלפס כולן פסולין לדפנות בשסומך עליהן אלא באותן שאין שכיח לסכך בהן או אותן דאין פסולין אלא מדרבנן ופי' הרשב"א שם וכולן ר"ל אפי' לא התירן כיון שאינן פסולות אלא מדרבנן וכ"כ ד"מ בשם א"ז וכ' וכן מייתי לה התם מן הירושלמי ובירושל' פ"א סוף הלכה ו' כ' להיפוך ר"י בשם ר"ח בר חנינא וסכות' על הארון את הפרוכת מיכן שהדופן קרוי סכך מכאן שעושין דפנות בדבר שהוא מקבל טומאה ונראה שגי' בירושלמי מכאן שאין עושין כו' וכן נראה כיון שקרוי סכך אבל אין ראיה משם דשם הוא בשיטת ר' יאשיה בגמ' ז' ב':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1, all things, etc.</b> This is according to the Rosh's view, who wrote that permanence is the main reason. But according to the ruling of the Rashba and the Ran in the view of the Rif, all of them are invalid for walls when one relies upon them, except those that are not commonly used for s'chach, or those that are invalid only rabbinically. The Rashba explained there that "all of them" means even if he did not permit them, since they are invalid only rabbinically. So the Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of the Or Zarua, and he wrote that this is also brought there from the Yerushalmi. But in the Yerushalmi, first chapter, end of halachah 6, it is written the reverse: Rabbi Yudan in the name of Rabbi Chama bar Chanina: "And you shall screen the ark with the curtain" - from here that the wall is called s'chach; from here that one may make walls from something that receives impurity. It seems that the text in the Yerushalmi should read: from here that one may not make, etc.; and so it seems, since it is called s'chach. But there is no proof from there, for there it follows the approach of Rabbi Yoshiyah in the Gemara, 7b.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And some say, etc.</b> Since there is the measure of a wall by means of <i>lavud</i>, a form of a doorway is not needed, for a form of a doorway is needed only to complete the measure of a wall. The reason he interrupted it is because he wanted to conclude the matter and distinguish between two adjacent walls. The first view holds that since there are not seven except through <i>lavud</i>, it is not a wall without a form of a doorway, for throughout the sukkah <i>lavud</i> does not help for "two according to their law," etc. For if not, why did it say that the third may be even a handbreadth? It should need four handbreadths, since <i>lavud</i> is a complete partition. Furthermore, two reeds would suffice, as Tosafos wrote: place one within less than three and the second within less than three, and each should be half a handbreadth and a bit. Why is a form of a doorway needed? See Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman and se'if 5. Therefore a form of a doorway is needed. This is what he wrote: "but if, etc."

<b>ס"ח  מפני שהיא כו'. </b>פי' שהאכסדרא היא ב' כותלים זה נגד זה ומקצתן מקורה וסמוך לקירוי על קצה האחד סיכך פצימין לאו בפחות מג' כפירש"י אלא בשיש פצימין שנים לרוח ג' א' מכאן וא' מכאן ע"י אותו הפצימין אמרינן פ"ת יורד וסותם ובזה מתורץ קושית תוספ' שם ד"ה אחוי ליה וא"צ לדחוקי כמ"ש הרא"ש שם דעביד ליה צ"ה ג"כ ורב כהנא לטעמי דבעי נמי צ"ה ומדאורייתא סגי בהכי דצ"ה חשיב מחיצה בכ"מ ולא בעי טפח שוחק אלא מדרבנן וכיון דנראה מבחוץ מהני ע"ש וכן מש"ש א"ל לא סבר מר כו' מ"ש יש לה פצימין כשירה לא שייכא לשאלתו דיש להפצימין הוא מטעם לבוד ואין לה פצימין הוא מטעם פ"ת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 8, because it is, etc.</b> Meaning: the portico consists of two walls opposite one another, and part of it is roofed. Near the roofing, at one edge, he placed s'chach. "Posts" does not mean within less than three, as Rashi explained, but that there are two posts on the third side, one here and one there. By those posts we say that the edge of the roof descends and seals. With this the difficulty of Tosafos there, s.v. <i>achvi lei</i>, is answered, and one need not press as the Rosh wrote there, that he also made a form of a doorway, and that Rav Kahana follows his own reasoning that he also requires a form of a doorway. By Torah law this suffices, for a form of a doorway is considered a partition everywhere, and a broad handbreadth is needed only rabbinically; and since it is visible from outside, it helps. See there. Likewise, what it says there, "He said to him: does the master not hold, etc.," and the statement that if it has posts it is valid, do not pertain to his question, for having posts is because of <i>lavud</i>, while not having posts is because of the edge of the roof.

<b>But others, etc.</b> According to Tosafos' explanation there, a portico has two attached partitions, but if they are opposite one another we do not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, as written there: he said to him, I concede to you, etc. On the third side he relies in such a way that the s'chach touches the portico; and the posts, according to Rashi's explanation, are adjacent within less than three of one another, because of <i>lavud</i>. Without posts, although in general this helps, here it does not help, as written in the Gemara, and Rava said to him: perforce, etc. When it is visible from the outside, etc., a form of a doorway is also needed, like the first answer in the Rosh there. See the Tur. It appears that the Rambam explains like the Rif at the end of the ninth chapter of Eruvin, that a portico has three partitions, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam. Here, even though he did not place the s'chach adjacent to the middle wall and the sukkah has only two walls, the edge of the roof helps because it has posts; and according to Abaye in the first version, even without posts. To this Rava challenged him: according to you, who say, etc., even though it has only two partitions. If so, even if the middle wall was breached, he said to him: I concede to you in that case, since it is open from side to side. This is unlike here, where the wall helps, for we say through the edge of the roof; but according to Rava it does not help and it is like an open alleyway. This is what he wrote: "because it is, etc., for behold, etc., and the middle, etc."
