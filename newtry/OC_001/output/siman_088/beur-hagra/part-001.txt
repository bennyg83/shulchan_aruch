**** HEBREW ****
<b>ובק"ש כו' ובלא כו'. </b>דלא כמ"ש הרי"ף בשם א"ד דוקא לד"ת אבל לק"ש בעי וכ' בשם ר"ה גאון בט' קבין אלא כס' ראשונה שם וראיה ממ"ש בירושלמי פ' בתרא דיומא השתא ברחיצה אסור בתשמיש המטה לא כ"ש ר"ל דאסור להתפלל ומשני במקום שאין טובלין אלמא אף רחיצה א"צ שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And for Krias Shema, etc., and without, etc.</b> This is unlike the Rif's citation in the name of an alternative version, according to which this applies specifically to words of Torah, whereas Krias Shema requires purification, and unlike his citation in the name of Rav Hai Gaon requiring nine kav. Rather, the ruling follows the first opinion there. The proof is from the Yerushalmi at the end of Yoma: “Now that washing is forbidden, is marital relations not certainly forbidden?” Meaning, is prayer not certainly forbidden? It answers that the case is in a place where people do not immerse. This shows that even washing is unnecessary there.
