**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A baal keri is permitted in Keriat Shema. And in it one seif:</b><br>All impure persons read Torah and recite Keriat Shema and pray, except a baal keri — Ezra removed him from all impure persons and forbade him, whether in words of Torah, Keriat Shema, or prayer, until he immerses, so that Torah scholars would not be found constantly with their wives like roosters. And afterward they annulled that enactment and restored the matter to the law — that even a baal keri is permitted in words of Torah, Keriat Shema, and prayer without immersion and without washing of nine kav. And such is the custom's plain meaning. <small>{Rama: Some wrote that a niddah woman on the days of her seeing should not enter the synagogue, or pray, or mention the Name, or touch a book (Hagahot Maimoni chapter 4). And some say she is permitted in all — and such is the main view (Rashi, laws of niddah). But the custom in these lands is like the first view. And on the days of white [flow] they practiced leniency, and even in a place where they practiced to be stringent on the Days of Awe and similar occasions when many gather to go to the synagogue — they are permitted to go to the synagogue like other women, for it is great sorrow for them that everyone gathers and they stand outside (Piskei Mahari'a siman 132).}</small>
