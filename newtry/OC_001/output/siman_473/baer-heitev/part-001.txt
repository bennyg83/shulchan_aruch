<b>שהחיינו. </b> ואם לא אמר אותו ביום א' מברך ביום ב' או בא' משאר ימים כשיזכור ואם אין לו כוס של יין בשעת הקידוש מברך זמן בלא כוס ונ"ל דאפי' בירך בראשונה ולא בירך בי"ט שני של גליות מברך כל שבעה מ"א. וכתב הח"י ונראה דה"ה בעצרת אע"ג דהוא רק שני ימים מ"מ יש לו תשלומין כל שבעה. ועוד נ"ל דאם שכח לומר זמן ליל ראשון ובליל ב' אמר זמן דא"צ שוב לומר זמן מכח לילה ראשונה להשלמה דאם יום ראשון קודש א"כ השלים הזמן ביום ב' ואם יום שני קודש הא אמר זמן ח"י ע"ש. ואם שכח לו' קידוש בליל' דינו כדלעיל סי' רע"א ס"ח וכ"כ הע"ש ס"ס תפ"ה. ואם שכח לומר ההגדה אין לו תשלומין כלל. דכתיב בעבור זה בשעה שמצה ומרור מונחים לפניך וכ"כ המ"א לקמן סי' תפ"ה ע"ש. ואין אומרים שעשה ניסים לפי שעתיד לאומרו בהגדה. טור ועח"י:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Shehecheyanu.</b> And if he did not say it on the first day, he recites the blessing on the second day or on one of the other days when he remembers; and if he has no cup of wine at the time of Kiddush, he recites Shehecheyanu without a cup. And it appears to me that even if he recited [it] on the first [day] and did not recite it on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora, he recites [it] all seven days (Magen Avraham). And Chok Yaakov wrote: and it appears that the same applies on Shavuos, even though it is only two days; nevertheless he has a make-up all seven days. And further it appears to me that if he forgot to say Shehecheyanu on the first night and on the second night said Shehecheyanu, he need not again say Shehecheyanu on account of the first night for make-up: for if the first day is holy, then he completed Shehecheyanu on the second day; and if the second day is holy, behold he already said Shehecheyanu (Chok Yaakov); see there. And if he forgot to say Kiddush at night, its law is as above in siman 271, se'if 8; and so wrote Elya Rabbah at the end of siman 485. And if he forgot to say the Haggadah, he has no make-up at all, for it is written "for the sake of this," at a time when matzah and maror are set before you; and so wrote the Magen Avraham below in siman 485; see there. And one does not say "Who performed miracles," because he is going to say it in the Haggadah (Tur and Chok Yaakov).

<b>He should make Havdalah.</b> And if he remembered in the middle of the meal that he did not make Havdalah, he makes Havdalah in the middle of his meal over the cup; and if his intention was to drink wine during the meal, he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen; and if that was not his intention, he must recite a blessing. <b>And he should arrange.</b> The order of the Ari's plate is copied from Sefer Etz Chaim, and this is its language: the order of the plate of the shemurah [matzos] shall be in this order: that you take three shemurah matzos and place the Kohen above, and after it Levi, and below it Yisrael, which correspond to the three brains of the Supernal Father; and afterwards take maror and karpas and charoses and two kinds of cooked food, a roasted shank of a lamb and a cooked egg, and place these five things above upon the three matzos: namely, the zeroa, which is Chesed, at your right; and the egg, which is Gevurah, at your left; maror, which alludes to Tiferes, in the middle between the zeroa and the egg, for Tiferes decides between Chesed and Gevurah; and afterwards the charoses on the right line under the zeroa, because it alludes to Netzach; and afterwards the karpas, which alludes to Hod, place under the egg on your left line; and afterwards take chazeres and place it below the maror on the middle line, which corresponds to Yesod, and it is in order afterwards to make a wrap with chazeres. And so I heard from a foreigner, that in foreign lands whose countries are warm, chazeres is found among them in the days of Nisan, and it is doubled and thick like what we call in these countries "Hayf Saltin," and they eat that species for maror; and see Chok Yaakov.
