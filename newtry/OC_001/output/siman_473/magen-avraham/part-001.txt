<b>ואח"כ יבדיל</b>. ואם נזכר באמצע סעודתו שלא הבדיל מבדיל באמצע סעודתו על הכוס ואם היה דעתו לשתות יין תוך הסעודה אינו מברך בפה"ג כיון דאיכא פלוגתא ספק ברכות להקל עסי' רצ"ט ס"ג ואם לא היה דעתו צריך לברך ולא ימתין עד אחר הסעוד' דא"כ יהיה כמוסיף על הכוסות ואין לו' שיאמר הבדלה והלל על כוס א' דא"א ב' קדושות על כוס אחד דדוקא קידוש והבדלה שרי דשניהם ענין א' היא כדאי' בפסחים דגם בהבדלה אמרינן המבדיל בין קודש לקודש: ואם שכח להבדיל עד אחר ב"ה או' הלל והבדלה על הכוס א' דהא א"א בע"א כמ"ש סי' רצ"ד ס"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And afterwards he should make Havdalah.</b> And if he remembered in the middle of his meal that he did not make Havdalah, he makes Havdalah in the middle of his meal over the cup; and if his intention was to drink wine during the meal, he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen, since there is a dispute, and a doubt regarding blessings is resolved leniently; see siman 299, se'if 3. And if that was not his intention, he must recite a blessing. And he should not wait until after the meal, for if so it would be like adding to the cups; and one should not say that he say Havdalah and Hallel over one cup, for one cannot [place] two sanctities on one cup; specifically Kiddush and Havdalah are permitted, for both are one matter, as is stated in Pesachim, for also regarding Havdalah we say "Who distinguishes between holy and holy." And if he forgot to make Havdalah until after Birkas HaMazon, he says Hallel and Havdalah over one cup, for there is no other way, as is written in siman 294, se'if 3.

<b>ואין ליטול ידיו כו'</b>. דאי הוי נוטלים קודם קידוש הוי משמע שעושין כב"ש דס"ל קידוש צריך נטילה אבל בכל השנ' נראה כנוטל לסעודה ואף על גב דהכא ג"כ טבילה שאחר הקידוש צריך נטילה מ"מ לא יעל' ע"ד הבריות שנוטל לטיבול ויסברו שנוטל לקידוש עכ"ל ד"מ בשם מהרי"ל משמע דס"ל דאסור ליטול עצמו קודם קידוש (ועסי' ער"א סי"ב), ומ"מ לא ידעתי היכן מצינו שב"ש ס"ל קידוש צריך נטילה רק בברכות פ"ח פליגי ב"ש ס"ל נוטלין לידים ואח"כ מוזגין את הכוס וב"ה ס"ל מוזגים את הכוס ואח"כ נוטלין לידים והטעם מפרש בגמ' משום טומאה וטהרה לב"ה משום הפסק בין נטיל' להמוצי' לכן נ"ל דס"ל כב"ה ודוקא מזיגה הוי הפסק כמ"ש התוס' פ' ע"פ אבל קידוש לא הוי הפסק אבל בפסח דהוי הפסק גדול לכן נוטלין סמוך לסעודה, וכ"ע מודו בזה: ורש"ל כ' דיטול מים קוד' קידוש ויטול ידיו ולא יטול אח"כ לטיבול והב"ח כ' דמ"מ יטול שנית לטיבול ול"נ כמ"ש רש"ל דהא בכל השנה אין אנו נוטלין ידים לטיבול א"כ מיחזי כסתרי אהדדי ועבב"י סי' קנ"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one should not wash his hands, etc.</b> For if they washed before Kiddush, it would imply that they act like Beis Shammai, who hold that Kiddush requires washing; but all year it appears that he is washing for the meal; and even though here too the dipping after Kiddush requires washing, nevertheless it would not enter people's minds that he is washing for the dipping, and they would think he is washing for Kiddush; end quote (Darkei Moshe in the name of Maharil). It is implied that he holds it is forbidden to wash himself before Kiddush (and see siman 271, se'if 12). And nevertheless I do not know where we find that Beis Shammai hold that Kiddush requires washing; only in Berachos chapter 8 they disagree: Beis Shammai hold one washes the hands and afterwards they mix the cup, and Beis Hillel hold they mix the cup and afterwards wash the hands; and the reason is explained in the Gemara: because of impurity and purity according to Beis Hillel, because of an interruption between washing and HaMotzi. Therefore it appears to me that he holds like Beis Hillel, and specifically mixing is an interruption, as Tosafos wrote in the chapter "Arvei Pesachim," but Kiddush is not an interruption; but on Pesach, where there is a major interruption, therefore they wash adjacent to the meal; and all agree about this. And Maharshal wrote that one should take water before Kiddush and wash his hands, and not wash afterwards for the dipping; and the Bach wrote that nevertheless he should wash a second time for the dipping; and it appears to me as Maharshal wrote, for all year we do not wash hands for dipping, so [doing both] appears as contradicting one another; and see Beis Yosef, siman 158.

<b>וכרפס</b>. כ' מהרי"ל נוהגין ליקח כרפס שהוא נוטריקון ס' פרך כלומר ס' ריבוא עבדו עבודת פרך, ואביו הי' מסופק אם מותר ליקח פעטרזל"ן מפני שהוא טוב יותר מבושל מחי וא"כ אין מברכין עליו בפה"א כמ"ש סי' ר"ה ע"כ, ואני תמה למה נהיגי במדינתינו ליקח פעטערזיל"ן ואפשר דס"ל שע"י הבשר הם משביחין כמש"ש ולפי מש"ש בשם תר"י דוקא כשאין נאכלין כלל חיין מברך שהכל אבל כשנאכלין חיין אף ע"פ שטובים יותר מבושלים מ"מ מברך כשהן חיין בפה"א א"כ א"ש, ומ"מ דעתי חוכך בזה למה בחרו בדבר שיש בו פלוגתא ה"ל ליקח ירק אחר ואפשר ששאר ירקות אין מצוים כ"כ בימי הפסח א"נ משום דבגמרא נזכר ירק ושאר דברים נקראו לפת והירק שלנו אין נאכל כמו שהוא חי אא"כ כבוש וזה אינו מצוי בפסח ובטור וגמ' אי' שגם על מרור מברך בפה"א וצ"ל דמרור שלהם הי' ראוי לאכול חי כמו לטי"ך עס"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And karpas.</b> Maharil wrote: they customarily take karpas, which is a notarikon of "samech perekh," that is, six hundred thousand did hard labor (avodat perekh); and his father was in doubt whether it is permitted to take parsley, because it is better cooked than raw, and if so one does not recite Borei Pri HaAdamah over it, as is written in siman 205; thus far. And I am astonished why in our country they are accustomed to take parsley; and perhaps they hold that by means of the meat they improve, as is written there; and according to what is written there in the name of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, specifically when they are not eaten raw at all does one recite Shehakol, but when they are eaten raw, even though they are better cooked, nevertheless when they are raw one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah; if so it is fine. And nevertheless my mind hesitates about this: why did they choose something about which there is a dispute? They ought to take another vegetable; and perhaps other vegetables are not so common in the days of Pesach; or alternatively, because in the Gemara a vegetable is mentioned, and other things are called "lefet," and our vegetable is not eaten as it is raw unless pickled, and this is not common on Pesach. And in the Tur and Gemara it is stated that also over maror one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah; and one must say that their maror was fit to eat raw, like lettuce; see se'if 5.

<b>לא בשורש</b>. ז"ל מהרי"ו אם יקח מערטי"ק יקח מה שהוא למעלה מן הקרקע כגון העלין והסמוך לו אבל לא מה שלמט' מן הקרקע דהוי שורש עכ"ל, וכ"פ מ"ב ושל"ה מיהו לפני זה כ' מהרי"ו וז"ל בפ"ק דסוכ' אמרי' אזוב ג' קלחים ובהן ג' גבעולים ופרש"י קלחים שרשים, ומטע' זה נרא' הא דכ' ר"ת ולא שרשים היינו שרשים קטנים המתפצלים לכאן ולכאן אבל השורש הגדול שבו עומד הירק הוי בכלל קלח עכ"ל וכ"כ בהגמ"נ ז"ל שמעתי שקרוי קלח עד למטה מזנבו ושבלים קטנים יוצאין מהם עכ"ל וכנ"ל עיקר: והעולם נוהגין לברך על העלים ולכריכה נוטלים הקלח ונ"ל הטעם מדקאמר ויוצאין בקלח שלהם אלמא דעלין עדיפא לכן נוטלין העלין לברכה ומיהו צריך ליטול כזית כמ"ש סי' תפ"ו ומפני שאין העלין הרב' כ"כ לכן נוטלין הקלח לכריכ' מיהו אם רוצה ליטול העלין לכריכה הרשות בידו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Not from the root.</b> This is the language of Mahari Weil: if he takes horseradish ("M'retik"), he should take what is above the ground, such as the leaves and what is adjacent to it, but not what is below the ground, for that is root; end quote. And so ruled Mateh Moshe and the Shelah. However, before this Mahari Weil wrote, and this is his language: in the first chapter of Sukkah we say hyssop [has] three stalks and on them three stems, and Rashi explained: stalks [means] roots; and for this reason it appears that what Rabbeinu Tam wrote "and not roots" means small roots that split here and there, but the large root on which the vegetable stands is included in the stalk; end quote. And so wrote Hagahos Mordechai, and this is his language: I heard that it is called stalk down to below its tail, and small spikes come out from them; end quote; and as above is the main [ruling]. And the world customarily recites the blessing over the leaves, and for korech they take the stalk; and it appears to me the reason is from what it says "and one fulfills with their stalk," which implies that leaves are preferable; therefore they take the leaves for the blessing. However, one must take a kezayit, as is written in siman 486; and because there are not so many leaves, therefore they take the stalk for korech; however, if he wishes to take the leaves for korech, the choice is in his hand.

<b>לא כבושים</b>. פירש"י בחומץ ונ"ל דאזיל לשיטתיה כמ"ש הב"י בי"ד רס"י ק"ה אבל אנן ק"ל דדבר השרוי במים מעל"ע הוי כבוש והוי כמבושל א"כ אם שרה המרור במים מע"ל אין יוצאין בו דנתבטל מרירתו ועיין בב"י סי' תקכ"ז כ' מהרי"ו וז"ל מהרר"א נסתפק כיון דמרור איתקש למצה וגזול פסול א"כ לא יעקור ישראל המרור מן הקרקע כמ"ש סי' תרמ"ט ונ"ל דלא דמי דהתם חסא מתקיים בקרקע מש"ה הוי כקרקע עכ"ל נ"ל דה"פ כיון דמרור אינו מתקיים בקרקע הוי כמטלטלין וקניית הנכרי ביאוש ושנוי רשות והוי שינוי רשות ביד הישראל ומ"מ צ"ע דהא קי"ל כל המחובר לקרקע הוי כקרקע כמ"ש סי' תרל"ז ס"ג, וכ"ש כשנשרש בקרקע כמ"ש בי"ד סי' ו' ס"ב ועבח"מ סי' צ"ה וס"ס קצ"ג, ואפשר דמיירי שהמרור כבר נתבשל כל צרכו ועומד ליתלש וסבירא ליה כל העומד ליתלש כתלוש דמי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Not pickled.</b> Rashi explained: in vinegar; and it appears to me that he goes according to his view, as Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 105; but we hold that something soaked in water overnight is pickled and is like cooked; if so, if he soaked the maror in water overnight, one does not fulfill with it, for its bitterness was nullified. And see in Beis Yosef, siman 527: Mahari Weil wrote, and this is his language: Mahari of Rothenburg was in doubt: since maror is compared to matzah, and stolen [produce] is invalid, if so a Jew should not uproot the maror from the ground, as is written in siman 649; and it appears to me that it is not comparable, for there lettuce survives in the ground, therefore it is like ground; end quote. It appears to me that this is the meaning: since maror does not survive in the ground, it is like movables, and the gentile's acquisition [works] by despair and change of domain, and there is a change of domain in the Jew's hand. And nevertheless this requires further study, for we hold that whatever is attached to the ground is like ground, as is written in siman 637, se'if 3, and all the more so when it is rooted in the ground, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 6, se'if 2; and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 95, and the end of siman 193. And perhaps it refers to where the maror has already fully ripened and stands ready to be pulled, and he holds that whatever stands ready to be pulled is like detached.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or another bitter vegetable.</b> Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote, and this is its language: in the Gemara it is evident that one fulfills only with those enumerated in the Mishnah; and what the Agur wrote that one should take wormwood, it appears that he explains that "maror" is wormwood; and according to this, with another bitter vegetable one eats without a blessing; and the Rav's words require further study; end quote. And it appears to me that he hit upon the truth in the Agur's intent, for the Agur wrote so in the name of Maharil, and this is Maharil's language in a responsum: one who does not have lettuce, some say that what is earlier in the Mishnah is earlier for the mitzvah, and there the four species are mentioned in the gloss in Ashkenazic (as above); and Tashbetz wrote to take "Wirmot" in Ashkenazic, which is the maror of the Mishnah; and the reason is that there is one who seeks after bitterness, therefore if there is no chazeres, according to all bitterness is preferable; and Agudah wrote to take "M'retich," which is tamcha; and perhaps because it was not clarified that olshin is "Kirvel," for in the siddurim it is written to take "Korvel" for the first dipping, which implies that one does not fulfill with it the obligation of maror; thus far Maharil's language. And Agudah also wrote that maror is "Wirmot," and it is known that wormwood is "Wirmot" in the Ashkenazic language; and in the customs of Maharac he wrote: a place where there is no lettuce, they take wormwood that is called "Yerimda," or another thing that is bitter (Tashbetz); end quote. If so, he holds that the maror of the Mishnah includes all bitter vegetables; and so Rashi wrote on Chumash: every bitter herb is called maror. But in the Gemara it does not imply so; and also in Sukkah folio 13 it is stated that even the bitterness of a marsh one does not fulfill with, because it has an accompanying name, for it must be that its name is maror without an accompanying name. And also in Rashi's intent it appears to say that everything is called maror, but nevertheless one fulfills only with those that at the time of the Giving of the Torah were called maror; therefore one should not recite a blessing over other vegetables.

**** ENGLISH ****
