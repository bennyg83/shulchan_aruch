<b>ואם</b> עט״ז הנה האדון ז״ל עשה פליליות עם רבינו המחבר במ״ש בס״א כאן אוחזן בידו משמע בלא בגד ובס״ה פסק בבגדו וידו כר׳ עקיבא (ברכות כ״ג א׳) ובהכרח לחלק בין נפנה צריך ג״כ בגד אבל להשתין בקבוע ל״ג שמא יפנה דא״נ יפנה אין גנאי כ״כ ודוקא בראשו גזור דגנאי גדול הוא וכן דעת הד״מ שהביא דברי רבינו יונה דבקבוע בידו לא חיישינן שיפנה כשהם בידו והביא זה דא״צ בגדו ובאמת המעיין ברבינו יונה מפורש יוצא שם דבגדו בעינן שמא יפנה וכ״כ מהר״מ מטיקטין בהג״ה שם ולפ״ז מ״ש הר״ב בהג״ה דאין חילוק בין קבוע לעראי כלל רק אורחא דמילתא שזה במיושב וזה במעומד וזה לשיטתיה בד״מ משא״כ כפי הנראה מרבינו יונה ורא״ש בברכות כתב בפירוש דבקבוע בידו ובגד״ו שרו להשתין (דלא כב״י שכתב ואהא דאמרינן ביד״ו מותר כתב הרא״ש בו דליתא כי הלכה כרבי עקיבא וגם לשמא יפנה נמי צריך בגד״ו וידו) א״כ הפרש יש בין קבע לעראי כי בעראי במיושב (או תיחוח ומדרון) ביד״ו לחוד שרי וקבוע בגדו נמי בעינן שמא יפנה בהן זהו תוכן כוונת האדון ז״ל ועיין נ״ץ ס״ה כ״כ והביא גם דברי הר״מ ז״ל בפ״ד מה״ת הי״ז להשתין בקבוע צריך בגד״ו ג״כ יע״ש: <b>והוי</b> יודע שאף לשיטת הר״ב בהג״ה וד״מ וא״כ אין הפרש בין עראי לקבוע וכדאמרן מ״מ הא איכא הפרש דאלו בקבוע ביד״ו צריך בימינו דוקא כמו בס״ה דאוחזן בימינו דוקא משום קינוח ובעראי במיושב ליכא ניצוצות ושמא יפנה ג״כ ליכא ומותר אפילו ביד שמאל כו׳ וכ״ת גזירה רחוקה משום קינוח לחוד לא גזור כמו דלא גזרינן ביד״ו לחוד (לשיטת הר״ב בד״מ) הא בורכא דיקנח ביד שהתפילין בו ודאי יותר גנאי מכשהם בראשו (עיין רבינו יונה ניצוצות ויגיעו לתפילין פשיטא בקינוח גנאי גדול) אמנם הב״ח ז״ל נזהר מזה וכתב כל מה שבידו מדכר דכיר ולא חיישינן שישכח ויפנה ויקנח בשמאל דאם יפנה ידכר ויקחם בימין משא״כ בראשו שוכח הוא אם יש לו בראשו יע״ש. <b>And if.</b> See Taz. The master, of blessed memory, took issue with the Mechaber. Here in seif 1, the words 'he holds them in his hand' imply without a garment, whereas in seif 5 he rules that they must be in his garment and his hand, in accordance with R' Akiva (Berachos 23a). One must therefore distinguish: when defecating, a garment is also required; but when urinating in a permanent privy, we do not decree lest he defecate, because even if he does defecate the degradation is not so great. They decreed specifically when the tefillin are on his head, because that is a great degradation. This is also the view of Darkei Moshe, who cites Rabbeinu Yonah that in a permanent privy, when the tefillin are in his hand, we are not concerned that he will defecate, and cites this to show that no garment is required. In truth, however, one who examines Rabbeinu Yonah sees explicitly that a garment is required lest he defecate. Maharam of Tiktin likewise writes this in his gloss there. Accordingly, the Rama's statement in the gloss that there is no distinction at all between a permanent and a temporary place, and that it is merely the ordinary manner for one to be seated and the other standing, follows his own approach in Darkei Moshe; this is unlike what appears from Rabbeinu Yonah. The Rosh in Berachos explicitly writes that in a permanent privy one may urinate while holding the tefillin in his hand and garment. This is unlike Beit Yosef's statement that regarding the permission to hold them 'in his hand,' the Rosh writes that this is incorrect because the halacha follows R' Akiva. Even because of the concern that he may defecate, both his garment and his hand are required. There is consequently a distinction between a permanent and temporary place: in a temporary place, when seated (or on loose earth or a slope) his hand alone suffices; in a permanent place, his garment is also required lest he defecate while holding them. This is the substance of the master's intent. See Nechalat Tzvi, seif 5, who writes likewise and also cites Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchot Tefillin, halacha 17, that to urinate in a permanent privy he also needs his garment; see there. <b>Know</b> that even according to the Rama's approach in the gloss and Darkei Moshe, under which there is no distinction between a temporary and permanent place as stated, there is nevertheless a distinction. In a permanent place, when holding them in his hand, he must specifically use his right hand, as in seif 5, because of wiping. In a temporary place, when seated, there are no droplets and no concern that he will defecate, and he may therefore hold them even in his left hand. If you argue that they did not enact such a remote decree solely because of wiping, just as they did not decree against holding them merely in his hand according to the Rama's approach in Darkei Moshe, this is mistaken. Wiping with the hand that holds the tefillin is certainly a greater degradation than defecating while they are on his head. See Rabbeinu Yonah: it is obvious that droplets reaching the tefillin, and certainly wiping, constitute a great degradation. Bach, of blessed memory, guarded against this difficulty. He writes that one remembers everything held in his hand, and therefore we are not concerned that he will forget, defecate, and wipe with his left hand. If he defecates, he will remember and transfer them to his right hand. When they are upon his head, however, he may forget that he is wearing them; see there. Taz's words 'and so Beit Yosef writes' are a scribal error and should read 'Rabbeinu Yonah.' What he cites in the name of Darkei Moshe does not appear alongside the Tur editions except in the Fürth printing; see there. This is what appears correct to me here.

<b>Rather, he removes them.</b> See Taz. The Tur (and the Mechaber here) explains this as referring back to the first case: at a permanent privy he removes them four cubits away, even if he intends only to urinate. Since he must remove them, he removes them four cubits away. He may, however, enter a permanent privy when he does not intend to urinate, as the Mechaber rules in seif 5, unlike Rav Hai. The words 'and gives them to another person' appear at first glance to refer to the latter clause, involving a nonpermanent place, where four cubits are unnecessary. Yet there he need not remove them at all and may keep them upon his head. If he nevertheless wishes to remove them, he must give them to another person, because even holding them in his hand and garment is forbidden. He may, however, tie them to his belt or the like; see Magen Avraham, ot 5, and Taz, ot 4. This explanation is strained. One can instead explain as Magen Avraham, ot 4, does: it concerns urinating while standing in a permanent privy, in which case he must remove them four cubits away and necessarily give them to another person. Taz's comment upon Beit Yosef requires study. Apparently he understood Beit Yosef as saying that the Tur makes no distinction and requires one who urinates even at a nonpermanent place to remove the tefillin four cubits away. Taz therefore objected: according to Rambam it is reasonable to remove them four cubits away at a nonpermanent place, because he holds that the reason is lest the person pass gas and therefore requires their removal; but according to the Tur, who holds that the reason is lest he defecate, he need not remove them at all to urinate at a nonpermanent place. How, then, could the Tur say that he removes them four cubits away? Yet it requires study why Taz suspected the luminary of Israel, Maran Beit Yosef, of such an understanding. Rather, Beit Yosef understood that the Tur does not distinguish even when the place was not previously permanent but the person now intends to use it as a permanent place: he must remove them four cubits away. Yet Rav Acha says in the Gemara, 'They taught this only regarding a permanent place, etc.' Beit Yosef therefore says that Rambam likewise omits Rav Acha's distinction, and even when the place was not previously permanent but one now intends to use it as such, he removes them four cubits away. The reason requires explanation; see Bach and Derishah. One can explain why they omitted Rav Acha's statement. Rav Acha holds that one may not enter a permanent privy while wearing tefillin, following Rav Hai's approach as in the gloss to seif 5. He therefore distinguishes between a place that was originally permanent and one the person now intends to use as permanent. We, however, follow the unresolved question in Berachos 23a whether one may enter a permanent privy to urinate, which shows that the authorities there hold that one may enter a permanent privy wearing tefillin, unlike R' Hai's reading; see Beit Yosef. Why, then, must he remove them four cubits away at a permanent place? The answer is that once he removes them, the Sages forbade him to remove them and immediately defecate; or urinate while holding them. There is consequently no distinction between a place that was permanent originally and one he now makes permanent. Whenever he must remove them, he must move four cubits away; even merely to urinate in such a case requires a distance of four cubits. With God's help, I will explain this further in Magen Avraham. See also what I will write, with God's help, regarding Taz, ot 5. This is not the place to elaborate.

<b>חולצן</b> עט״ז כתב הטור כתב כלשון המחבר דוקא קבוע וכרב אחא ורי״ף ורא״ש שלא גורסין קבוע בב׳ ברייתות מ״מ סוברים דב״ה ור״ע חולקין על ב״ש וסברי דא״צ ד״א ורבי מייאשא הלכה כו׳ כב״ה ור״ע וא״כ א״צ ד״א אפילו בקבוע והטור חשש לאידך ברייתא בסתם כו׳. והנה יש תבלין לדברי ט״ז דהרי״ף גורס ת״ר (הוא התניא אידך) חולץ ברחוק ד״א ונכנס אלו דברי ב״ש כו׳ והשמיט הא דיניחם בחלון הסמוך לרשות הרבים ובגמרא י״ל דפליגי אי במניחם בחלון ברה״ר ולא יכניסם ב״ה אמר מכניסן ולא יניחם ברה״ר משא״כ לרי״ף לכאורה ע״כ הפלוגתא בין ב״ש וב״ה אי חולצן ברחוק ד״א ואי קשיא כיון דרי״ף ורא״ש לא גרסי קבוע (בתניא אידך) א״כ מאן נימא דברייתא דלעיל לאו הלכתא ניהו ודאמר לך מני ב״ש הוא דלמא ב״ה ור״ע בקבוע איירי וכרב אחא לעיל ותניא אידך באין קבוע ולכך א״צ לחלוץ ברחוק ד״א וכרב אחא י״ל דסובר דא״כ מ״ט דב״ש באין קבוע ודאי סברא דא״צ ד״א לחלוץ ולא יחלוק שום אדם בזה. ועל המחבר קשה בתלת. א׳ מאי איריא קבוע אפי׳ עראי ועתה קבוע חולץ. ב׳ מאי איריא צרכיו אפי׳ להשתין נמי חולצן ברחוק ד״א כמ״ש בס״א ואקבוע קאי כמ״ש המ״א וט״ז ג״ד. ג׳ במ״ש גוללן ברצועו״ת שזה פירש׳י שם ד״ה גוללן וסובר כב״ה כמ״ש ד״ה ואוחזן בימינו שלא בבגדו אבל לדידן דקי״ל כר״ע י״ל גוללן בבגד ועיין נ״ץ ס״ד. גם מ״ש שיש תבלין לדברי ט״ז מרי״ף דב״ש וב״ה פליגי רק אי צריך לחלוץ ברחוק ד״א דמשמע דלב״ש נמי רשאי להכניסן בידו א״כ מאי האי דאמר ע״כ דאמר לך מני ב״ש הוא הרי בהדיא דלב״ש בהכרח אסור להכניסן לבה״כ א״כ מנ״ל דפליגי בתרתי ואימא ב״ה ור״ע נמי סוברין חולצין ברחוק ד״א וצ״ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He removes them.</b> See Taz. The Tur writes in the Mechaber's language, specifically referring to a permanent place, in accordance with Rav Acha. Rif and Rosh do not have the word 'permanent' in either baraita. Nevertheless, they hold that Beit Hillel and R' Akiva disagree with Beit Shammai and maintain that a distance of four cubits is unnecessary; R' Meyasha's ruling follows Beit Hillel and R' Akiva. If so, four cubits are unnecessary even at a permanent place. The Tur, however, was concerned for the other anonymous baraita, and so forth. Support can be given to Taz's words. Rif's text reads: 'The Sages taught' (this is the other baraita) 'He removes them four cubits away and enters; these are the words of Beit Shammai, etc.' Rif omits the instruction to place them in a window adjacent to the public domain. In the Gemara one can say that they disagree over whether he should place them in a window in the public domain and not bring them inside: Beit Hillel says that he brings them in and does not leave them in the public domain. According to Rif, however, it apparently follows necessarily that the dispute between Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel is whether he must remove them four cubits away. If one objects that since Rif and Rosh do not read 'permanent' in the other baraita, how do we know that the preceding baraita is not authoritative and that the response 'Who is its author? Beit Shammai' is decisive? Perhaps Beit Hillel and R' Akiva discuss a permanent place, in accordance with Rav Acha above, whereas the other baraita concerns a nonpermanent place, where removing them four cubits away is unnecessary, also in accordance with Rav Acha. One can answer that otherwise, what would be Beit Shammai's reason in a nonpermanent place? It is certainly logical that no four-cubit distance is necessary for removing them, and no one would disagree with this. There are three difficulties with the Mechaber. First, why specify a permanent place? The same applies to a nonpermanent place that he now intends to use as permanent. Second, why specify defecating? Even to urinate he must remove them four cubits away, as stated in seif 1, which refers to a permanent place, as Magen Avraham and Taz, se'if katan 3, write. Third, he writes that one rolls them in their straps. This is Rashi's explanation there, s.v. 'he rolls them,' and follows Beit Hillel, as Rashi writes s.v. 'and holds them in his right hand'; not in his garment. According to us, however, who rule like R' Akiva, one can say that he rolls them in a garment. See Nechalat Tzvi, seif 4. Moreover, regarding the support I suggested for Taz from Rif; that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree only over whether removal must take place four cubits away; which would imply that even Beit Shammai permits bringing them into the privy in his hand: how can the Gemara then say, 'It must be that the one who said this follows Beit Shammai'? It is explicit that according to Beit Shammai it is necessarily forbidden to bring them into the privy. If so, how do we know that they disagree about two matters? Perhaps Beit Hillel and R' Akiva also hold that they must be removed four cubits away. This requires further study.

<b>That it not.</b> See Taz, who cites Rashi, Berachos 23a, s.v. 'that it not,' and Tosafos, Shabbos 61a, s.v. 'and perhaps,' and 63a, s.v. 'shin.'
