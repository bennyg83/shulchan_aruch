<b>א) [סעיף א'] חמץ בפסח אוסר וכו'  </b>מדאו' הוי חמץ כשאר איסורים אלא שחכמים החמירו בו כיון דאית ביה כרת ולא בדילי מיניה כולי שתא. מ"א ריש הסי' והרמב"ם פט"ו מהלכות מ"א דין ט' כתב הטעם משום דהוי דבר שיש לו מתירין יעו"ש והביאו ב"י. וכתב הרמ"א בתשו' סי' כ"ח היכא דאיכא עוד צד להקל אפשר לסמוך על מ"ש המרדכי דיש לסמוך אשאלתות ויש להקל ולבטל חמץ בששים כמו שאר איסורים. וכ"כ בתשו' מ"ב סי' ס"ט והובא ג"כ במ"א לקמן סי' תס"ז ס"ק ל"ג ומ"מ למעשה נראה מדבריהם שאין סומכין ע"ז אלא א"כ דאיכא עוד הרבה צדדים להקל. ח"י או' א' חק יוסף או' א' מק"ח או' א' ח"א כלל קכ"א או' ח'.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] Chametz during Pesach renders a mixture prohibited, etc. </b>Under Torah law, chametz is like other prohibited substances. The Sages were stringent regarding it because its consumption carries the penalty of kares and people are not accustomed to avoiding it throughout the year. Magen Avraham, at the beginning of the siman. Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchos Ma'achalos Asuros, halachah 9, explains that chametz is a substance that will later become permitted. See there. Beis Yosef cites his words. Rema writes in Responsa Rema, siman 28, that when another basis for leniency exists, it may be possible to rely upon Mordechai, who writes that one may rely upon She'iltos and treat chametz as nullified in sixty like other prohibitions. Responsa Mas'as Binyamin, siman 69, writes likewise. This is also cited by Magen Avraham below in siman 467, subsection 33. In practice, however, their words indicate that this position is relied upon only when many additional grounds for leniency exist. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 121, subsection 8.

<b>2) The sacred Zohar </b>states in parashas Ki Seitzei, 282b: 'Those who observe Pesach, when the night of guarding holds sway over them, must all be guarded and protected from even the slightest amount of chametz and leaven, and all their foods and drinks must be guarded. One who guards them from chametz and leaven has the body guarded from the evil inclination below and the soul guarded above. Of such a person it is stated, "Evil shall not dwell with You," for the person's body becomes holy and the person's soul becomes holy of holies. Of the evil inclination it is stated, "No stranger may eat of the holy," and "The stranger who approaches shall die."' These are its words. Ba'er Hetev writes this in the name of the Arizal. At present I have not found it written in the works of the Arizal available to us, except in Mishnas Chassidim, tractate Nissan, chapter 3, subsection 4.

<b>7) There. Even the slightest amount, etc. </b>This applies even to the slightest amount of hardened, inedible chametz, which is rabbinically prohibited. Peri Chadash. Taz, subsection 19, likewise writes that if hardened chametz falls into food, the food may not be eaten during Pesach, but may be retained until after Pesach. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 5, however, writes in the name of Likkutei Maharil that hardened chametz is nullified in sixty even during Pesach, since it does not carry the penalty of kares. Fruit juice mixed with water, however, prohibits even in the slightest amount although the resulting chametz is hardened chametz. Mekor Chaim, there. See below, subsection 268.

<b>8) There. Even the slightest amount, etc. </b>If some of a food that was itself prohibited by the slightest admixture becomes mixed into another dish, creating two successive minute admixtures, Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 92, subsection 16, concludes as follows: if a solid piece became prohibited by the slightest admixture, fell into another pot, and was removed, what it exudes does not prohibit the second pot in the slightest amount, because we do not apply the rule of the slightest amount twice. If, however, one liquid became mixed with another in this manner, it remains prohibited forever by the slightest amount. See there. Peri Chadash agrees with Taz. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, likewise rules leniently where a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture fell into another dish, there is a majority against it, the piece is identifiable, and it was removed. If it cannot be identified, everything is prohibited. See there. Shach in Nekudos HaKesef there, however, challenges Taz's permission of a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture that fell into another pot. He writes that all halachic authorities customarily prohibit all the mixtures. See there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1 in this siman, agrees and writes that the ruling is contrary to Taz. Magen Avraham, siman 467, subsection 13; Chok Yaakov, subsection 5 in this siman and subsection 27 in siman 467; Chok Yosef, subsection 6; and Mateh Yehudah write likewise. Mateh Yehudah writes that this is evident from the words of Maran and Rema, and that this is the custom in Algiers. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 16; Chayei Adam, rule 121, subsection 8. Regarding a doubtful prohibition involving the slightest amount, however, Beis Yehudah, page 109, writes that the second mixture is permitted and agrees with this ruling. See there. Beis David, siman 163, likewise writes that if it is uncertain whether any chametz entered the second mixture, since perhaps all the chametz remained in the first mixture, all authorities permit it. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 4, cites this, but writes that Magen Avraham, subsection 26, implies that it is prohibited even where the chametz may have remained in the first mixture. See there.

<b>ט) חטה  </b>שנמצאת בקדרה נאסרה הכף שהגיסו בה הקדרה. שמוה בקדרות אחרות (ר"ל הגיסו בה קדרות אחרות) כולן אסורות. טור סי' תס"ז. ח"א שם. וכ"כ א"ר בסי' זה סוף או' א' דכף הבלוע ממשהו אוסר במשהו. והגם דבחתיכה שנאסרה במשהו ס"ל להא"ר דאינה חוזרת ואוסרת במשהו כמ"ש באו' הקודם כבר כתב הא"ר שם הטעם דדוקא מחתיכה לא יכול משהו להתפשט אבל מכף שאין לו טעם מעצמו אפשר להתפשט יעו"ש ור"ל שהחתיכה שיש בה טעם כשפולטת פולטת מעצמה ג"כ והו"ל משהו דמשהו אבל הכף שאין בו טעם כשפולט פולט כל המשהו שבלע ולפיכך אוסר הקדרות שהגיסו בו אח"כ.

<b>9) A wheat kernel </b>found in a pot renders prohibited the spoon used to stir the pot. If that spoon was used to stir other pots, all of them are prohibited. Tur, siman 467; Chayei Adam, there. Eliyah Rabbah, at the end of subsection 1 in this siman, likewise writes that a spoon that absorbed the slightest amount renders another mixture prohibited by the slightest amount. Although Eliyah Rabbah maintains that a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture does not subsequently prohibit another mixture by the slightest amount, as stated in the preceding subsection, he explains there that the slightest amount cannot spread from a solid piece, whereas it can spread from a spoon, which has no flavor of its own. See there. In other words, when a solid piece that has flavor exudes, it also exudes some of its own substance, producing only the slightest amount of the slightest amount. A spoon, however, has no flavor of its own; when it exudes, it exudes all the minute amount that it absorbed. It therefore renders prohibited the pots subsequently stirred with it.

<b>10) A person </b>cooked fish in a chametz frying pan that had not been used for many days. The spoon used to stir the fish was subsequently used to stir another dish, and some of that second dish was added to a third dish. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, volume 1, siman 21, writes that one may not rule leniently in this case, following Rema below in se'if 10, who prohibits a detrimental absorbed flavor during Pesach. See there. An exception applies when another basis for leniency exists. Here there is an additional basis to permit a detrimental absorbed flavor during Pesach after two months have passed. This implies that even the food cooked directly in the frying pan is permitted after the fact. This is subject to the strict condition that the cooked food not be sharp. See the glosses to Yoreh De'ah, siman 93, and siman 122, se'if 3. From this one may infer that even one who is stringent regarding a pot lid that was not used that day may permit it after two months. Initially, however, with all prohibitions it is forbidden to use utensils that were not used that day without kashering them, even if they were not used for many years, and this is certainly true of chametz. See there. Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 2, cites this ruling. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 75, however, permits it only when twelve months have passed since chametz was cooked in the utensil. Rabbi Naftali Kohen agrees, as stated there in siman 76, and rules likewise in siman 80. See there. This will be further explained below in siman 451, subsection 4. See there.

<b>יא) ככר  </b>של בעה"ב של חמץ שנתערב בתוך ככרות של מצה אפי' נתערב באלף כולן אסורות באכילה ובהנאה מפני שהוא דבר שבמנין (כמ"ש ביו"ד רסי' ק"י) ויראה לי שאפי' לאחר הפסח אסורין ואם נחתך הפת או נבצע מותר לאחר הפסח. ויש מי שאומר לאחר הפסח מותרת (היינו אף אם עודנו שלם) שהרי התירו כל תערובת חמץ אחר הפסח אף שאיסורו במשהו ולא יהא דבר שבמנין חמור מהם שאיסורו במשהו. ובזה אני מחמיר שהרי עשו תערובת דבר שבמנין כגוף האיסור בעצמו. ס' בית מועד. ברכ"י או' ב' וכ"פ הפר"ח בסי' תמ"ח סעי' ג' לאסור גם לאחר הפסח יעו"ש. ועיין לקמן סעי' ט' ובדברינו לשם בס"ד.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>11) A householder's loaf </b>of chametz that became mixed with loaves of matzah renders all of them prohibited for consumption and benefit, even if it became mixed with one thousand loaves, because it is an item sold by number, as stated at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah, siman 110. It appears to me that they remain prohibited even after Pesach. If the loaf was cut or broken, it is permitted after Pesach. Some maintain that it is permitted after Pesach even while still whole, because every chametz mixture was permitted after Pesach despite having been prohibited by the slightest amount; an item sold by number, which is likewise prohibited by the slightest amount, should be no more stringent. I am stringent in this case, because the Sages treated a mixture containing an item sold by number like the prohibited item itself. Sefer Beis Moed; Birkei Yosef, subsection 2. Peri Chadash in siman 448, se'if 3, likewise rules that it is prohibited even after Pesach. See there. See below, se'if 9, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
