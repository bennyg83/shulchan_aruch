<b>במ"א שם סק"ב </b>וכן משמע בסי' תמ"ז כו' ולא חילק בין אם התרנגולת שמן כו' וצ"ל דאע"ג דהמג"א בסי' תס"ז מוקי לההיא בכחושה דאל"כ קשה על דיעה הראשונה דלא ס"ל לאסור כולו ע"ש מ"מ אין כוונתו דס"ל דמיירי בכחושה לחוד שזה דוחק גדול לסתום כ"כ אך לפי דבדיעה        קמייתא נקט חותך מקום פעפוע לפי אומד הדעת ואם כן נתן כלל שהכל לפי ענין הפעפוע ובתרנגולת כחושה שענין הפעפוע הוא מעט סגי בנטילה ובשמנה שמפעפע בכולו ע"י פיטום באמת אוסר בכולו כיון שמקום פעפוע הוא בכולו וע"ז חלוקות הדיעות שאח"כ דבכל גווני אף בכחושה אסור מטעם היפך השפוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
[2] <b>In Magen Avraham there, se'if katan 2.</b> "And so it sounds in siman 447, etc., and he did not distinguish whether the chicken was fat, etc." One must say that although Magen Avraham in siman 467 establishes that case as dealing with a lean chicken, for otherwise it is difficult according to the first view, which does not hold that all of it is forbidden; see there. Nevertheless, his intent is not that he holds it speaks only of a lean chicken, for that is a great strain, to write so without qualification. Rather, because in the first view he stated "cut off the place of spreading according to estimation," he thereby gave a rule that everything depends on the nature of the spreading. With a lean chicken, where the spreading is small, removal suffices; but with a fat one, where through fattening it spreads throughout all of it, it truly forbids all of it, since the place of spreading is the whole thing. The later views disagree with this and hold that in every case, even with a lean one, it is forbidden because of turning the spit.

**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>There.</b> "Therefore he asks, does it enter your mind? See there, etc." Perhaps it is permitted. From the fact that he asks, "does it enter your mind," there is proof that this is how he explained the words of the Talmud. If we explain it simply, that the questioner understood "they roasted them in two pots" to mean pot-roast, then why does he ask simply, "does it enter your mind?" According to the first tanna of Rebbi on page 41, pot-roast is permitted. Rather, perforce the questioner held that when it answers here that they roasted them in two pots, it means cooking; for otherwise, it should still be forbidden because the smell exits upward. Therefore he asks, "does it enter your mind," because cooking is certainly impossible for the Pesach offering. However, according to what Tosafos wrote on page 41, that even according to the first tanna, pot-roast is invalid, and there is also a positive commandment of "only roasted by fire," then even if it speaks of roasting them in two pots, the question "does it enter your mind" is properly asked simply.

**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>There, se'if katan 29.</b> "And so the language in Hagahos Ashri implies; if so, there is no proof to forbid a second vessel, etc., for he wrote in Darchei Moshe in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, etc." This is the correct reading. It appears to explain as follows: if we say that cold is completely permitted, there is a proper proof from Mordechai regarding a second vessel. Although it refers to where they poured the water into the utensil, nevertheless, since he wrote that it is a second vessel, it must speak of where they placed the chicken into it afterward. Or even if you say that the chicken was there at the time of scalding, nevertheless, although the bowl emits taste because of the pouring, this emitted taste does not have the power to go back and be absorbed into the chicken except through the hot water inside it, and this is only a second vessel. <small>It appears to me that this is the view of the "some who permit" mentioned in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, end of siman 68, regarding if they poured in order to scald a chicken in a dairy pan. See there in Shach in the name of Levush, who wrote the reason because the stream was interrupted. In my humble opinion, this is not needed; rather, the reason is that the return of the emitted taste into the chicken is no longer because of pouring, but is like a second vessel. The Beis Yosef ruled there to permit in a second vessel.</small> If so, there is a proper proof from Mordechai regarding a second vessel: although the pouring acts to make the utensil emit taste, nevertheless regarding forbidding the chicken there is only a second vessel, and it is proven that a second vessel forbids. This is not so if we say that cold is forbidden. Then there is no proof regarding a second vessel, because once the utensil emitted taste because of the pouring, the chicken automatically became forbidden even though it is only a second vessel, for even if it were merely cold it would also be forbidden. If so, from where did Rema know to forbid a second vessel when there is no liquid there? Afterward he wrote: and furthermore, for he wrote in Darchei Moshe, etc. One must say that this is another gloss, meaning that Darchei Moshe held that it is another gloss, and therefore he wrote here in the gloss two views. According to what I wrote, this is not difficult at all, because one can say that there the reason is that the utensil emits through pouring, and automatically the chicken becomes forbidden, since it is no worse than cold. What he wrote in the second gloss, that one need not be concerned for a second vessel, refers to where there is no liquid. About this he wrote: therefore it appears to me that what Mordechai wrote to forbid with cold is because of cracks, etc. Without that, there is no prohibition with cold. If so, Mordechai properly proves that even a second vessel forbids, for he forbids scalding the chicken even though the chicken is in its feathers and has no cracks, and pouring helps only to cause emission from the utensil, as I wrote. See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, Orach Chayim, part 1, siman 47; there you will find this matter explained more fully. Many were confused about this and amended and switched the approaches, but one who examines will see that this explanation is correct and illuminates the eyes, with Hashem's help.

[7] <b>There.</b> Se'if katan 15: "As he wrote in siman 467, se'if 14, etc." This means that there in se'if katan 33 he wrote that if the brine does not rise over it, it has the law of salting, and its law is as written in Responsa Rema; see there, where Magen Avraham alluded to here. With salting, the other pieces are permitted. Regarding that piece itself, what is outside the brine can also be said to be permitted, as Taz wrote in siman 467, se'if katan 20. Although with other prohibitions we say that perhaps it spreads upward, nevertheless with chametz, which is a lean prohibition, one should not be stringent. See Shach, siman 105. What Magen Avraham wrote, "and in Derishah, siman 69, and in Be'er Sheva, etc." In my humble opinion a word is missing, and it should read: "And in Derishah, siman 69, he wrote that even to cause emission from a utensil, the law of soaking applies; but in Be'er Sheva, chapter 25, he wrote, etc." Meaning that Be'er Sheva holds that soaking is ineffective to cause emission from a utensil; see Minchas Yaakov, klal 12, se'if katan 14.

[12] <b>There.</b> "For if it does not have sixty, he transgresses regarding it, and so ruled the Tur, etc." This means that the Tur wrote there that if it has flavor, one is biblically liable for it; see Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1. Therefore he wrote that with the same type mixed with the same type, where there is no taste, if so one does not transgress regarding it, as is also explained in the above siman, and it is permitted after Pesach if he kept it. But to mix it and keep it may be forbidden in such a case. For although anything over which one does not transgress biblically may be mixed and kept, that is specifically where it is made for souring and does not contain enough to eat a kezayis within the time of eating a peras. This is not so here, where it is less than sixty, and against it, if it were not the same type, it would impart flavor. Therefore, even though it is the same type, it is forbidden to mix intentionally and keep, as a decree lest he do so with a different type. He wrote that perhaps this is what R. Yerucham wrote, that if one nullifies it, etc., about which the Tur asked: either way, if it is something over which one transgresses biblically, nullification does not help at all; and if one does not transgress, even mixing it ab initio to keep should be permitted. About this Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps it refers to such a case, where there is enough to impart flavor, but it is the same type; in such a case it is forbidden ab initio, as appears to me. See what I wrote in siman 442 until "and if less than a kezayis became mixed, etc." That is not from the Bach, but Magen Avraham's own words. What he wrote, "and if it became mixed, etc., this is what he intended in the gloss," is from the Bach, who wrote that from Rema's language it sounds that it refers even if he knew before Pesach and transgressed and kept it, redemption helps. This means it refers to where there is not a kezayis of chametz there. This is unlike Hagahos Maimoniyos, who wrote that it must not have been known; that refers to where there is a kezayis of chametz there.
