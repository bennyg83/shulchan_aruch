The Maharit, siman 1, wrote that Hagahot Maimoniyot was stringent specifically with a pan, for such is its use all year, but with other utensils we say even by chametz that ordinary utensils are not ben yomo. So wrote Knesset HaGedolah. It does not appear so to me, for he wrote in Tur Yoreh Deah, siman 122, that the reason we say ordinary vessels are not ben yomo is because it is a double doubt: perhaps they were used today or not, and if you say they were used today, perhaps they were used with something that impairs the taste in its substance. This applies to other prohibitions, but with chametz, where people commonly use pots, most do not impair taste in fat, and it is a single doubt, and therefore it is forbidden. But the Rashba wrote the double doubt differently: perhaps it was not used, and if you say it was used, perhaps it was used for something permitted. If so, by chametz too one can say this, perhaps it was not used for chametz but for water or meat and the like.

<b>It is not nullified.</b> This is also implied by Toras Chatas, klal 39. Mordechai writes that the reason is that since in a liquid mixture the prohibition applies in any amount, the same is true in a dry mixture. Nekudas HaKesef writes that the reason is that it is an item that will become permitted, and therefore rules leniently when the mixture is not of the same type. See there. He overlooked the words of Mordechai, although Shach himself cites them in Yoreh Deah 102:13. Accordingly, on Erev Pesach it is nullified by a majority, as stated in seif 2. See Yoreh Deah, siman 109, regarding the proper procedure. Nevertheless, all the pieces must be eaten before Pesach, as stated in the gloss to seif 4. All this concerns a piece of bread, but a whole loaf of chametz mixed with matzah is not nullified according to any opinion, because it is an item counted individually, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 110. See Bach, and what I wrote in siman 451:19. A matzah in which a kernel of wheat was found, or which folded over on one side and the folded section was cut away and then became mixed with another matzah, is not considered an item counted individually, because its prohibition is due to absorbed taste. The Bach writes that a chametz utensil mixed with other utensils is nullified by a majority. Nevertheless, since some hold that chametz is considered an item that will become permitted, it is not nullified. The same is written in Nachalas Tzvi. Toras Chatas, however, rules that chametz is not considered an item that will become permitted; the Tur and Beit Yosef likewise write this in the name of the poskim. This is the accepted practice, for otherwise it would not be nullified even on Erev Pesach, contrary to seif 2. Regarding utensils, however, one should be stringent, because some authorities maintain that even utensils containing other prohibitions are not nullified, as Shach writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 102. During Pesach itself it is certainly forbidden, for it is no better than any other dry mixture.
