<b>ודוקא במקום כו'. </b> פי' כגון דהתנור קטן ואין מחזיק י"ב עשרונים והוא סתום והחמץ והמצה שניהם מגולים בתוך התנור בזה אנו מתירים בדיעבד בשאר איסורים כמ"ש בי"ד סי' ק"ח אלא דבמצה אסור בזה אבל אם התנור גדול אפי' הוא סתום או שאחד מן התבשילין הוא מכוסה הן של חמץ הן של מצה מותר דבזה אין שייך כלל ריחא וביו"ד שם הכריע רמ"א דבמקום הפסד יש לסמוך על המקילים והוי כמו שאר אסורים והפוסקים כתבו בשם ר"ת דבפת אין שייך בו ריח כלל מ"ה אין אוסר פת חמץ שנאפה עם פת מצה אף דאית ביה הני תלת מילי וכן הביא בש"ע סי' תס"א ס"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
[1] <b>Specifically in a place, etc.</b> This means, for example, where the oven is small and does not hold twelve esronim, and it is closed, and the chametz and matzah are both uncovered inside the oven. In this case we permit after the fact with other prohibitions, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, except that with matzah it is forbidden in this case. But if the oven is large, even if it is closed, or one of the cooked foods is covered, whether the chametz or the matzah, it is permitted, for in this case smell is not relevant at all. There in Yoreh De'ah, Rema decided that in a case of loss, one may rely on those who are lenient, and it is like other prohibitions. The poskim wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that smell is not relevant to bread at all. Therefore chametz bread baked together with matzah bread does not forbid, even if these three conditions are present. So too he brought in Shulchan Aruch, siman 461, se'if 5.

<b>מותר. </b> הטעם דחשש חימוץ שיש במלח שנמלח הוא בטל בס' קוד' הפסח ואינו חוזר וניעו' וא"כ לא ה"ל לסתו' להתיר כיון שקודם לזה כתב שיש חולקין וס"ל דאמרי' חוזר וניעור נר' דהכריע הש"ע בזה כדברי המקילין דכאן היה תרתי לטיבותא חדא שמא לא היה חמץ במלח כלל ואת"ל היה אימ' נתבטל קודם פסח וכדעת המקילים והיש חולקים שמביא רמ"א הוא הרי"ץ גאות שהביא הטור בשמו שאוסר חלב שחלבו ישראל קודם הפסח אם לא ידוע שנזהר בחימוץ וטעמו דהרי"ץ גיאות דס"ל בפסח חוזר וניעור וכיש חולקין דסעיף ד' ובהדיא הביא ב"י בשם ה' המגיד שהרי"ץ גיאות ס"ל כאותן גאונים דס"ל דחוזר וניעו' וא"כ למה כ' רמ"א המנהג להחמיר מאחר שכ' בסעיף ד' המנהג כסברא ראשונה וצ"ל הסברא להיפך דטעם המנהג להחמיר בזה דהמלח הוא בעין על דברים אלו ואפשר שיש חמץ בעין בין המלח ולכך לא מועיל הדחה בהם:
**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>Permitted.</b> The reason is that the concern of chametz in the salt with which it was salted is nullified in sixty before Pesach and does not reawaken. If so, he should not have written simply to permit, since before this he wrote that some disagree and hold that we say it reawakens. It appears that Shulchan Aruch decided here according to the lenient view because here there are two favorable factors: first, perhaps there was no chametz in the salt at all; and if you say there was, perhaps it was nullified before Pesach according to the lenient authorities. The dissenting view that Rema brings is the Ritz Giyyas, whom the Tur cites, who forbids milk milked by a Jew before Pesach if it is not known that he was careful about chametz. The reason of the Ritz Giyyas is that he holds it reawakens on Pesach, like the dissenting view in se'if 4. The Beis Yosef explicitly brought in the name of the Maggid Mishneh that the Ritz Giyyas holds like those Geonim who say that it reawakens. If so, why did Rema write that the custom is to be stringent, after writing in se'if 4 that the custom follows the first view? One must say the reasoning is the reverse: the reason of the custom to be stringent here is that the salt is visibly on these items, and perhaps actual chametz is present among the salt. Therefore rinsing does not help them.

<b>ג"פ קודם הפסח. </b> היינו בכלי שאינו של חמץ לכתחלה ודיעבד מועיל אפי' בכלי חמץ דצונן אינו מפליט ולא זכר רמ"א רק בשר ונראה דה"ה דגים יש להתיר ע"י הדחה כבשר רק שלא ראיתי לנהוג היתר בדגים ע"י הדחה. ויש לתת טעם לזה ממ"ש בסמ"ג מיהו נהגו העולם שלא לאכול גבינה ישינה ולא דגים מלוחים ושמא היינו משום דרגילות לאכלן בלא הדחה אבל בשר מלוח בעי הדחה עכ"ל נראה דה"ק כיון שרגילין לאכול דגים בלא הדחה יש לחוש שיאכלם בלא הדחה משא"כ בבשר: <b>ומ"מ </b> פעם א' הית' דוחק למצוא בשר והתרתי להדיח דגים בפסח דנ"ל דלא אסרו בדגים אלא שמזמן קרוב נמלחו משא"כ בדגים מלוחים בחבי' שא"א בלא הדחה זה עדיף מבשר אבל גבינה פשיטא דלא מהני הדחה והוי ככרכשתא וכתב מו"ח ז"ל בסי' תנ"א דאם ניתן הבשר אחר שהכשירו בכלי שנשתמשו בו חמץ תוך מע"ל דאסור הבשר לפסח דהוי כבוש כמבושל ואני כתבתי בי"ד סי' ק"ה דאין לאסור מחמת כבוש כמבושל בכלי של איסור דה"ל נט"ל אחר שיעור מע"ל שהוא שיעור כבישה ונט"ל מותר קוד' הפסח ונ"ל בשר יבש שהיה תלוי בחדר שמנהלים שם קמח ה"ל חמץ בעין ולא מהני הדחה:
**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>Three times before Pesach.</b> This means ab initio in a utensil that is not chametz. After the fact, it helps even in a chametz utensil, because cold does not cause release of absorbed taste. Rema mentioned only meat, and it appears that the same applies to fish, that it can be permitted through rinsing like meat, except that I have not seen the practice to permit fish through rinsing. One can give a reason for this from what Semag wrote: nevertheless, the world has the custom not to eat aged cheese or salted fish, perhaps because they are normally eaten without rinsing. But salted meat requires rinsing. This is the end of his language. It appears that this is what he means: since people normally eat fish without rinsing, there is concern that they will eat it without rinsing, unlike meat. <b>Nevertheless,</b> once there was difficulty finding meat, and I permitted rinsing fish on Pesach, because it appears to me that they forbade fish only when they were salted recently. This is not so with salted fish in a barrel, which cannot be eaten without rinsing; this is better than meat. But regarding cheese, it is obvious that rinsing does not help, and it is like intestines. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote in siman 451 that if after the meat was kashered it was placed in a utensil used for chametz within twenty-four hours, the meat is forbidden for Pesach, because soaking is like cooking. I wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 105, that one should not forbid because of soaking like cooking in a prohibited utensil, since after the measure of twenty-four hours, which is the measure of soaking, it is impaired flavor, and impaired flavor is permitted before Pesach. It appears to me that dry meat that was hanging in a room where they sift flour is like actual chametz, and rinsing does not help.

<b>אבל בשומן מהותך. </b> הקשה ב"י הא קי"ל סתם כלים אינן ב"י והאריך בזה ונלע"ד דבזה לא אמרי' כן דכבר כתבתי בי"ד סי' קכ"ב בשם ד"מ בשם הג"ם ש"ד דבישראל יש תקנה בשאלה ואם אינו יודע נקנסיה ונ"ל דאין שייך קנס אלא באותו ישראל דהקדיר' שלו דה"ל לידע ולא ידע אבל באחר לא עכ"ל ד"מ ונ"ל דנהי דאין סברא זו מוסכמת להלכה כמ"ש הש"ע בס"ח מ"מ נ"ל דלענין פסח כ"ע ס"ל כן דכל אדם רגיל ליזהר בכל ענין הצורך לפסח שמכין עצמו להשמר מחימוץ ע"כ זה שהתיך השומן בכל דבר המבשל ה"ל להזהיר שתהא המחבת ודאי אינו ב"י ע"כ קנסי' לי' לאסור תבשילו לאכלו בפסח כנ"ל נכון בזה: <b>ונ"ל </b> דבמרקחת שהוא דבר קיוהא וחריף זה אסור אפי' בשאר איסורים אפילו אינו ב"י ע"כ יש להחמיר בו אפי' בי"ט האחרון:
**** ENGLISH ****
[5] <b>But with melted fat.</b> The Beis Yosef asked: do we not hold that ordinary utensils were not used that day? He wrote at length about this. In my humble opinion, here we do not say so. I already wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 122, in the name of Darchei Moshe in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos Sha'arei Dura, that with a Jew there is a remedy by asking him, and if he does not know, we penalize him. It seems to me that a penalty applies only to that Jew whose pot it is, for he should have known and did not know; but not to another person. This is the end of Darchei Moshe's language. It appears to me that although this reasoning is not agreed upon as halachah, as written in Shulchan Aruch, se'if 8, nevertheless regarding Pesach everyone agrees to this. Every person is accustomed to be careful in every matter needed for Pesach, preparing himself to guard against chametz. Therefore, one who melted fat in any cooking vessel should have warned that the pan certainly not be used that day. Therefore we penalize him by forbidding his cooked food to be eaten on Pesach. This appears correct to me. <b>It appears to me</b> that with preserves, which are sour and sharp, this is forbidden even with other prohibitions even if the utensil was not used that day. Therefore one must be stringent with it even on the last Yom Tov.

<b>בוסר שדכין כו'. </b> כ' סמ"ק דהוי צונן ואין לו כח להפליט ואפי' אי פלט משהו בעלמא הוא דפליט ונתבטל קודם הפסח מידי דהוי אכרישי' ובצלים שחתכן בסכין חולבת ונתנם עם הבשר כדפירש ר"ב עכ"ל ונראה פירושו דאמרי' ביו"ד סי' צ"ו דבדבר חריף ויש דוחקא דסכין יש איסור כמ"ש שם בס"א ע"כ אמר כאן אפי' אי פליט דהיינו ע"י דוחק' דמדוכה ויש כאן דבר חריף דהבוסר יש בו קיוהא מ"מ א"צ לבטלו אלא הפליטה לחוד ואין הבוסר כולו נעשה איסור ויליף לה מבצלי' שחתכן בסכין חולבת ונתנום עם בשר דאין כל הבצל נעשה איסור אלא משערי' נגד הבלוע בהם משא"כ בחתך בסכין ש"ג דאז כל הבצל נעשה נבילה ה"נ כיון שנעשה קודם הפסח והיה אז היתר ע"כ אח"כ בשעת פסח מותר כיון שכבר נתבטל הפליט' המועטת מ"ה אם נעש' בפסח ודאי אסור אם חתכו בסכין. חמץ אפי' אם הוא נקי מטעם דוחקא דסכינא והוא דבר חריף וכמ"ש בי"ד לענין צנון סי' צ"ו דיש אוסר אפי' בצונן וע"כ צ"ע למה כ' רמ"א דסת' סכין אינו נקי דלפי הנראה אפי' בודאי נקי יש לאסור אם חתכו תוך הפסח בסכין חמץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
[7] <b>Unripe grapes that are pounded, etc.</b> Semak wrote that this is cold and does not have the power to cause release of absorbed taste; and even if it releases, it releases only a minimal amount, which was nullified before Pesach, like leeks and onions cut with a dairy knife and placed with meat, as R. Baruch explained. This is the end of his language. It appears that his explanation is as follows: we say in Yoreh De'ah, siman 96, that with a sharp item and pressure of a knife there is prohibition, as written there in se'if 1. Therefore he says here that even if it releases taste, meaning through the pressure of the mortar, and there is a sharp item here, since the unripe grapes have sourness, nevertheless only the released taste itself needs nullification, and the entire unripe grape does not become forbidden. He learns this from onions cut with a dairy knife and placed with meat, where the entire onion does not become forbidden; rather, we measure against what is absorbed in them. This is unlike cutting with a knife of idolatrous use, where the entire onion becomes neveilah. Here too, since it was done before Pesach and was then permitted, afterward on Pesach it is permitted, because the small released taste had already been nullified. Therefore, if it was done on Pesach it is certainly forbidden if he cut with a chametz knife, even if it was clean, because of the pressure of the knife and because it is a sharp item. As is written in Yoreh De'ah regarding radish, siman 96, some forbid even with cold. Therefore it requires examination why Rema wrote that an ordinary knife is not clean; as it appears, even if it is certainly clean one should forbid if he cut them during Pesach with a chametz knife.

[8] <b>Olives with which they were careful, etc.</b> The Tur wrote: olives that were pickled during the rest of the year, according to Baal HaTerumah, who forbids radish and onion cut with a knife of idolatrous use, comparing them to asafoetida, etc., so that we measure against the whole knife, if so all the olives become chametz, etc. This is difficult for me: why should we not measure sixty against the knife? Certainly among all the olives there is sixty against it, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 96, regarding onions, se'if 2. Here too, when the olives absorbed from the knife, it was still permitted, and it is not comparable to a knife of idolatrous use in siman 96. Also at the end of his words the Tur wrote: it appears to me that even according to Baal HaTerumah, if they were careful with cutting by knife, etc. This implies that if they were not careful with the knife, there is no permission through nullification against the knife. It appears that this case of cutting olives must refer to before they were pickled, for then they are sharp, since the Tur wrote that through the pickling water their sharpness is nullified. Therefore the Tur compares them to onions. In Issur VeHeter HaAruch, klal 38, law 15, he wrote: furthermore, even regarding the prohibition of chametz, Orach Chayim does not forbid olives cut with a chametz knife unless they were cut after they were pickled; but if they were cut before they were pickled, he permits there eating them on Pesach. This is not found in our Tur; on the contrary, it is the opposite, as I wrote. Perhaps he cites Sefer Orchos Chayim, not Orach Chayim. Therefore it is understood here why sixty does not help to nullify, although in total there is sixty against the knife: since he takes each one separately before its pickling, and each individual one does not have sixty against the knife, it remains with what it received, and joining them does not help, for there is nothing here to join them and carry the taste from one to another. This is unlike there with onions, where the cooking liquid carries taste from one to another. But here, with pickling, each one is pickled separately through its placement in water; therefore joining does not help. It is not comparable to unripe grapes mentioned in the previous se'if, where all of them help to nullify the released taste of the mortar, as I wrote nearby, because there they were all pounded and mixed well; just as they received together, so too joining them helps to nullify the released taste. This appears correct to me. What he wrote here to permit even though they were not careful to pickle them in a new pot is because of the reason the Tur wrote, that their sharpness is nullified at the time of pickling, since at the time of pickling they put water in them, and one can no longer say that it strengthens them. Still, one must be careful about inserting the knife, for the reason we mentioned. <b>In the Tur</b> it is written: "and so it appears, etc." At first glance it is difficult: how does he bring proof from something cut on Pesach itself for what he wrote before regarding olives of the whole year? One can say that he brings proof only for this: that olives, because of their sourness, have the same law as asafoetida mentioned in the Gemara. For onion too resembles asafoetida, and the Rosh forbids if it was cut with an ordinary knife even though it is impaired flavor, while according to the Rosh impaired flavor is permitted, as the Tur wrote in his name. Rather, certainly it is forbidden because it is a sharp item.
