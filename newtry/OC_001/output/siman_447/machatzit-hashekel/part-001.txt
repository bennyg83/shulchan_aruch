**** ENGLISH ****
See Yoreh Deah, siman 111, and Shach, seif katan 3, who writes in the name of Minchas Yaakov as follows. Although we rule there that we may say, “I presume,” such as where two pots stand before us, one containing permitted food and one containing prohibited food, and two pieces likewise stand before us, one prohibited and one permitted, and the two pieces fell into the two pots: if the pieces are prohibited only D'Rabbanan, both pots are permitted, because we say that the permitted piece fell into the permitted pot and the prohibited piece into the prohibited pot. If, however, the prohibition is according to the Torah, the permitted pot must contain a majority against the prohibited piece, so that even if the prohibited piece fell into it, it remains permitted according to the Torah. Only then do we say that the permitted piece fell into the permitted pot, etc.; without a majority, we do not. Nekudas HaKesef writes regarding this that perhaps, for severe prohibitions that apply in any amount, we do not say “I presume,” even when the prohibition is D'Rabbanan and there is less than a majority. Accordingly, chametz nuksheh, which according to our ruling is prohibited only D'Rabbanan, would nevertheless not qualify for this presumption according to the opinion that even chametz nuksheh prohibits in any amount, even though its prohibition is only D'Rabbanan. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. According to Nekudas HaKesef, therefore, in the case described above we do not say “I presume” unless there is a majority. Pri Chadash in Yoreh Deah, however, disputes Nekudas HaKesef; see there.

<b>(Seif katan 5) It is not, etc.</b> “And it is obvious,” etc. His intent is as follows. Pesachim 6b states that Rav Yehudah said in the name of Rav: one who searches for chametz must nullify it. The Gemara asks why he must nullify it at the time of the search; let him nullify it in the fourth or fifth hour. It answers that since this is neither the time when chametz is forbidden nor the time of its elimination, he may forget. The Gemara then asks that he should nullify it in the sixth hour, when he is occupied with burning it. It answers that once chametz is prohibited D’Rabbanan it is no longer in his possession and he cannot nullify it. Rav Giddel said in the name of Rav Chiya bar Yosef that one who betroths a woman from the sixth hour onward, even with hard wheat from Kurdistan, which is chametz nuksheh, need not be concerned for the kiddushin. Rashi explains “from the sixth hour onward” as the beginning of the sixth hour, and the Gemara proves that even then the chametz is not in his possession, for a woman betrothed with it is not betrothed. Rabbeinu Tam proves that Rav Giddel’s statement means from the end of the sixth hour, meaning the beginning of the seventh, when chametz is forbidden according to the Torah. The proof nevertheless stands because although Rav Giddel discusses the beginning of the seventh hour, when ordinary chametz is forbidden according to the Torah, the Kurdistan wheat is chametz nuksheh, which in his view is forbidden only D’Rabbanan. Thus ordinary chametz in the sixth hour, whose leniency arises from the time, is learned from chametz nuksheh at the beginning of the seventh hour, whose leniency arises from its own nature. Maharil follows Rabbeinu Tam. We therefore see that the Gemara compares an item lenient by its nature with another item bearing the same leniency because of the time. Maharil consequently holds that chametz nuksheh on Pesach, although forbidden according to the Torah in accordance with those authorities, carries no kares and is comparable to ordinary chametz from the sixth hour onward on Erev Pesach, which likewise carries no kares because Pesach has not yet begun. Just as ordinary chametz from the sixth hour onward is nullified in sixty because the time does not carry kares, chametz nuksheh on Pesach, which inherently carries no kares, is also nullified in sixty. Maharil’s description of chametz from the sixth hour onward as D’Rabbanan is therefore borrowed language. Since he follows Rabbeinu Tam, “from the sixth hour onward” in Rav Giddel’s statement means the beginning of the seventh hour, when the prohibition is according to the Torah. Maharil’s phrase “from the sixth hour onward, which is D’Rabbanan,” instead refers to the beginning of the sixth hour, because he is commenting on the Gemara’s question, “Let him nullify it in the sixth hour,” which means the beginning of that hour. The answer is that once it is forbidden D’Rabbanan it is no longer in his possession.
