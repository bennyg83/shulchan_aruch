ועבי"ד סי' קי"א ובש"ך ס"ק ג' שכתב בשם מ"י אע"ג דקיי"ל שם דאמרינן שאני אומר כגון היה לפניו שתי קדרות א' של היתר וא' של איסור. ולפניו שתי חתיכות א' של איסור וא' של היתר אם החתיכות איסור הוא רק איסור דרבנן ונפלו אלו לתוך אלו מותרים שאני אומר היתר לתוך היתר נפל ואיסור לתוך איסור נפל אבל אם היה איסור דאורייתא אז בעי' דוקא שיהיה קדירות היתר רוב נגד איסור דאפי' נפל לתוכו האיסור מותר מן התורה אז דוקא אמרינן אני אומר היתר לתוך היתר נפל כו' אבל אי ליכא רובא לא. וע"ז כ' הנ"י דאפשר דלענין איסורי משהו החמורים ל"א שאני אומר אפי' באיסורי דרבנן בפחות מרוב עכ"ל וא"כ חמץ נוקשה לדידן דקיי"ל דאיסורו מדרבנן ולמ"ד דגם נוקשה במשהו אפילו למ"ד דאין אסורו רק מדרבנן ועמ"ש מ"א סק"ה לדעת נ"י ל"א שאני אומר בנדון כנ"ל אלא אי איכא רוב אך הפר"ח שם בי"ד חולק על נ"י ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
See Yoreh Deah, siman 111, and Shach, seif katan 3, who writes in the name of Minchas Yaakov as follows. Although we rule there that we may say, “I presume,” such as where two pots stand before us, one containing permitted food and one containing prohibited food, and two pieces likewise stand before us, one prohibited and one permitted, and the two pieces fell into the two pots: if the pieces are prohibited only D'Rabbanan, both pots are permitted, because we say that the permitted piece fell into the permitted pot and the prohibited piece into the prohibited pot. If, however, the prohibition is according to the Torah, the permitted pot must contain a majority against the prohibited piece, so that even if the prohibited piece fell into it, it remains permitted according to the Torah. Only then do we say that the permitted piece fell into the permitted pot, etc.; without a majority, we do not. Nekudas HaKesef writes regarding this that perhaps, for severe prohibitions that apply in any amount, we do not say “I presume,” even when the prohibition is D'Rabbanan and there is less than a majority. Accordingly, chametz nuksheh, which according to our ruling is prohibited only D'Rabbanan, would nevertheless not qualify for this presumption according to the opinion that even chametz nuksheh prohibits in any amount, even though its prohibition is only D'Rabbanan. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. According to Nekudas HaKesef, therefore, in the case described above we do not say “I presume” unless there is a majority. Pri Chadash in Yoreh Deah, however, disputes Nekudas HaKesef; see there.

<b>(ס"ק ב) ואין שם ט' בי"ד לענין צלי.</b> דס"ל להרב"י דאינו אוסר אלא כדי נטילה אבל א' מהן שמן מפעפע בכולו ול"מ אם האיסור שמן אלא אפי' האיסור כחוש והיתר שמן מ"מ אוסר כולו דאזיל היתר ומפטם כו' וצריך ששים ה"ה בחמץ כה"ג אוסר במשהו כיון דמפעפע בכולו אע"ג דחמץ מצד עצמו לא משכח' שיהיה שמן מ"מ ההיתר מפטם ליה. במקום שלא נכפטלה. דאותו מקום אינו אוסר כ"א משום שבלע ממקום הכפל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if katan 2: And there is no sixty in Yoreh Deah regarding roasting.</b> For the Beis Yosef holds that it forbids only the amount requiring removal by peeling; but if one of them is fatty, it spreads through all of it. Not only when the forbidden item is fatty, but even when the forbidden item is lean and the permitted item is fatty, it nevertheless forbids all of it, for the permitted item goes and strengthens the forbidden item, etc., and sixty is required. The same is true with chametz: in such a case it forbids by a mashehu, since it spreads through all of it. Although chametz in itself is not found to be fatty, nevertheless the permitted food strengthens it. In a place that was not folded: that place forbids only because it absorbed from the folded place.

<b>אפי' ההיתר שמן כו'</b> דודאי איסור שמן הבלוע יוצא מחתיכה לחתיכה בלא רוטב אבל לא איסור כחוש. ומאי דלא אמרינן כה"ג אזיל היתר ומפט' כו' כתב ש"ך בי"ד סי' ק"ה ס"ק י"ט דדוקא באיסור מצד עצמו דעכ"פ אוסר מצד עצמו אפי' כחוש כדי נטילה גורם פיטום ההיתר שמוליכו עד שמפעפע בכולו משא"כ איסור כחוש הבלוע דמצד עצמו אינו מפעפע כלל ל"א דאזיל היתר ומפטם כו' ע"ש באחרונים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even if the permitted item is fatty, etc.</b> For certainly an absorbed fatty prohibition emerges from one piece to another even without gravy, but a lean prohibition does not. As for why we do not say in such a case that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 105, s.k. 19, that this applies specifically to a prohibition in its own right, which at least forbids in its own right, even when lean, to the amount requiring peeling; the fattening effect of the permitted item then causes it to travel until it spreads throughout all of it. This is unlike an absorbed lean prohibition, which in itself does not spread at all; we do not say that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc. See the later authorities there.

<b>And so wrote the Mishnah Berurah, etc.</b> He had to bring proof, lest you say: granted, with other prohibitions we say that an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge, etc.; that is because with other prohibitions it does not forbid until it imparts flavor, and an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge in so great an amount as to impart flavor. דאזיל היתר ומפטם כו' לא הוזכר בשום מקום. ואדרבא הא לפי האמת ס"ל למ"א דחמץ שוה בזה לשאר איסורים וא"א דבחמץ אמרי' דאזיל היתר ומפטם כו' לא ה"ל לסתום בסי' תס"ז אלא לפרש דאם היא שמנה אפילו אם לא היפך כו' אסורים א"ו דבאמת חמץ שוה לשאר אסורים בזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To permit the other pieces that touched the piece on which the wheat kernel was found.</b> We do not say that the other pieces become forbidden because they absorb from the piece on which the wheat kernel lies, since that piece on which the wheat kernel lies is forbidden only because it absorbed the flavor of the wheat kernel, and an absorbed prohibition does not emerge, etc. So too it appears in siman 467 se'if 9, that specifically, etc. That is to say, he brought there three views regarding a roasted chicken in which a wheat kernel was found, etc. One view says to cut away the place to which it spread, etc. A second view says the whole chicken is forbidden, because by turning the spit the flavor spreads through the whole chicken. A third view says that all the chickens roasted with it on the spit and touching one another are forbidden, because by turning the spit the flavor spreads from one to another. Magen Avraham inferred from the third view that only through turning the spit are they all forbidden, which implies that if he did not turn it, even though they touch one another, the other chickens are permitted. Now, if all the chickens are lean, this is understandable, for an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge, etc., even with chametz. But since the ruling is stated without qualification, it implies that even fat chickens are permitted if he did not turn the spit; for if not, he should have explained this explicitly. Why should we not say that since a fat chicken strengthens the lean chametz prohibition, it must be that even with chametz we do not say that an absorbed lean prohibition emerges? Nor do we say that the permitted fat goes and strengthens it so that at least a mashehu emerges and forbids as chametz. People wonder about this proof from the unqualified language, that it must therefore speak even of a fat chicken: for otherwise, how did Magen Avraham himself prove below in siman 467, from two proofs, that it necessarily speaks there specifically of a lean chicken? The first proof is in s.k. 34, from the first view, which holds that it is enough to cut away the place to which it spread. His language is: it is difficult, for he wrote in siman 447 se'if 1, and there is no substance there that causes spreading, etc.; this implies that if there is a substance that causes spreading, it forbids all of it. One must say that here too it speaks of a lean chicken. End quote. The second proof is in s.k. 30, from the third view, which requires specifically, in order to forbid all the chickens, that all the chickens touch one another. Magen Avraham wrote as follows: but if they are not touching, they do not forbid one another, and we do not say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 32 se'if 5. There, because of doubt, we say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil; if so, even if the chickens do not touch one another, they nevertheless touch the spit on which they were roasted, and the absorbed matter would spread from the place where the chicken with the wheat kernel lay until the end of the spit, and the others would be forbidden by the spit. For there it speaks of cooking; that is to say, with roasting it is obvious to us that the absorbed matter does not travel through the whole utensil. If so, if the chicken on which the wheat kernel lies is fat, even if it is not touching, it forbids everything. End quote. Thus he proved from two proofs that it speaks there of a lean chicken, even though it is stated there without qualification. In my humble opinion this is not difficult, and the words of Magen Avraham are simple. Certainly it is not the way of the halachic decisors, and especially of the Shulchan Aruch, to state matters without qualification. But according to the first view, which holds that removing the place to which it spread is enough, and which necessarily speaks of a lean chicken from what the Beis Yosef wrote here, and there is no substance there that causes spreading, etc., one may say that for this reason he stated it there without qualification and did not explain that it speaks specifically of a lean chicken, because he relied on what he wrote here in siman 447, and there is no substance there that causes spreading. So too, according to the third view, which wrote that it is specifically when they are touching, and which necessarily speaks of a lean chicken, for otherwise we would say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil, even though it is stated without qualification there is no difficulty, for one may say that he relied on what he wrote in Yoreh Deah, that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil. But if it would enter your mind that with chametz we say that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., then necessarily the reason he needed there, through turning the spit, etc., implying that if he did not turn it they are permitted, would speak specifically of a lean chicken; this he should have explained and not left unqualified, for this law, that chametz differs from other prohibitions and we say regarding it that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., is not mentioned anywhere. On the contrary, in truth Magen Avraham holds that chametz is equal to other prohibitions in this regard. If with chametz we said that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., he should not have stated the ruling in siman 467 without qualification, but should have explained that if it is fat, even if he did not turn it, etc., they are forbidden. Rather, it must be that in truth chametz is equal to other prohibitions in this regard.

Aroma is a halachic factor. Levi said it is permitted; aroma is not a halachic factor. They raised an objection: one may not roast two Pesach offerings together, because of mixture. What, is this not mixture of flavors, and it is difficult for Levi? No, it is because of mixture of the bodies. The reason for mixture of their bodies or of their flavors is that through this the Pesach is eaten not by those appointed for it. The Gemara derives this from what the latter clause teaches, even a kid and a lamb. Granted, if it is because of their bodies, that is why it teaches even kid and lamb; but if it is because of mixture of flavors, what difference is there between kid and lamb and kid and kid? At first glance one could answer Maharil's proof: even if we say Levi concedes that at least a mashehu exists, nevertheless the Gemara properly asks what difference there is between kid, etc.; although kid and kid is one type with its own type, the baraisa is speaking initially, for it teaches, one may not roast, and regarding the initial law there is no distinction between its own type and another type. If so, there is no challenge to Levi at all from the baraisa of one may not roast two Pesach offerings. According to this, Maharil's proof is sound: if there is certainly a mashehu, kid and lamb would properly be a novelty, since they are not the same type, and one might have thought that aroma is only a mashehu and that even initially, with another type, it is permitted because his intent is not to nullify; therefore it teaches us that it is a full flavor. But according to the Rif, even Rav himself holds there is only a mashehu of flavor; if so, certainly according to Levi there is not even a mashehu. For if not, what is Levi's reason? Do not say it is because Levi holds that one type with its own type is nullified, for then their dispute would not depend at all on the law of whether aroma is a halachic factor, but on whether one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu or not, and they should have disputed generally about one type with its own type. Rather, perforce Levi holds that even according to the one who says one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, aroma is nevertheless not a halachic factor, for there is not even a mashehu of flavor. The Rif, however, holds that we rule like Rav against Levi for the reasons the Rif wrote there; yet one cannot rule like Rav in this case, since Rav concedes it is only a mashehu of flavor. Rather, Rav follows his own view that one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, and kosher-slaughtered meat with neveilah is one type with its own type. If so, for us, who hold that one type with its own type is nullified, one cannot rule like Rav in that case; but regarding chametz, where we hold that it forbids by a mashehu, we rule like Rav that aroma is a halachic factor. According to this, Maharil's proof is rejected. For according to the Rif one must say that when the Talmud asks Rav, why even kid and lamb, it should have said: if the reason is mixture of flavors, why is kid and lamb, which is one type with another type, forbidden, since even according to Rav aroma is only a mashehu? Perforce one must say that nevertheless, since the baraisa speaks regarding the initial law, as it teaches, one may not roast, etc., regarding the initial law even another type is forbidden according to Rav, because one may not nullify a prohibition initially; perforce one must say that this is called intending to nullify. Even so, it properly asks against Levi, because according to Levi it must be permitted even initially, for according to Levi there is not even a mashehu of flavor, as above. According to this, Maharil's proof is gone, for the Gemara properly asks what difference there is between kid, etc.; although one is one type with its own type and the other is another type, since the baraisa speaks initially, and regarding the initial law there is no distinction between its own type and another type, for one may not nullify a prohibition initially, as above.

What Rabbeinu Tam wrote, that we have not found the aroma of bread, etc., implying that a complete aroma does forbid, is not because of the stringency of chametz, but because Rabbeinu Tam holds that even with other prohibitions aroma is a halachic factor, like Rav, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 3. The Gemara asks why he must nullify it at the time of the search; let him nullify it in the fourth or fifth hour. It answers that since this is neither the time when chametz is forbidden nor the time of its elimination, he may forget. The Gemara then asks that he should nullify it in the sixth hour, when he is occupied with burning it. It answers that once chametz is prohibited D’Rabbanan it is no longer in his possession and he cannot nullify it. Rav Giddel said in the name of Rav Chiya bar Yosef that one who betroths a woman from the sixth hour onward, even with hard wheat from Kurdistan, which is chametz nuksheh, need not be concerned for the kiddushin. Rashi explains “from the sixth hour onward” as the beginning of the sixth hour, and the Gemara proves that even then the chametz is not in his possession, for a woman betrothed with it is not betrothed. Why compare detrimental taste with a minute amount, which contributes favorable taste? See Pri Chadash there. Detrimental taste is indeed less severe than a minute amount because it impairs the food, but since even non-taste, meaning a minute amount, was prohibited, the rule was not divided where the taste is detrimental. He therefore writes that one may distinguish and permit detrimental taste in chametz nuksheh. Although he regards a minute amount of chametz nuksheh as plainly forbidden, contrary to Maharil, that minute amount is the substance of the decree, while the prohibition of detrimental taste in ordinary chametz exists only because of lo plug and was not extended to chametz nuksheh. Tosafot explains that the Sages required searching even though bittul alone suffices because one might come to eat the chametz. Why were they more concerned with chametz than with other prohibited foods that may remain in one’s home? One answer is that people are not accustomed to separating themselves from chametz. Another answer is that the Torah was more stringent with chametz than with other prohibitions by imposing bal yera’eh upon one who retains it, and the Sages therefore also prohibited retaining it after bittul lest one eat it.
