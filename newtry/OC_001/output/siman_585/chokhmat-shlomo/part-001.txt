<b>Se'if 2.</b> Before he blows he should recite the blessing “to hear the sound of the shofar,” and he should recite Shehecheyanu. Note: see in the Magen Avraham who wrote in the name of the Rivash that if he is in doubt whether he blew or took the lulav on the first day, he should blow and take without a blessing, and on the second day he is exempt, etc. (see there). And I do not know why he wrote so in the name of the Rivash; for it is stated explicitly so in the Ran in chapter 4 of Sukkah in the sugya that an esrog is forbidden all seven [days] and a sukkah is forbidden even on the eighth (see there, where it is stated explicitly so in the Ran and in the novellae of the Ritva there, that on the second day he is exempt from blowing and from taking the lulav). And from this it is proven explicitly that a doubt of rabbinic [law], even where a prohibition has been established, [is treated] leniently; for already it has been established that he is obligated to blow and to take the lulav, and [there is] a doubt whether he took or not, and why should we not go stringently? Necessarily even in such a case we go leniently, and not like the view of the Shach in Yoreh De’ah; and it is astonishing regarding the Shach that he did not see all this, and examine [it].

(There, se'if 3) In the gloss: They are accustomed to call out before the baal tokea the order of the blasts word by word, etc. Note: see in the Magen Avraham, s.k. 11, who wrote “once,” etc. And in my humble opinion one may say [that] the baal tokea [recites] chapter 144 in Tehillim before the blasts, because it is said, “Who trains my hands for battle, my fingers for war” for this is the great battle that we fight on this day, and he should pray about this beforehand; and in my humble opinion it is efficacious if he says it with intention (and understand. And what the Rama wrote, that they are accustomed to call out before the baal tokea) behold, there is nothing that is not hinted in the Torah. And it appears that the Torah hinted this to us (that there be one who reads after the reading) from what is written in Parashas Yisro: at first it said, “And there were thunderings and lightnings and a heavy cloud upon the mountain, and the sound of a shofar going and very strong, and all the people who were in the camp trembled”; and afterwards it said, “And the sound of the shofar was going and very strong; Moshe would speak and God would answer him with a voice.” And behold, what is written “Moshe would speak” has no [simple] understanding, and also why at first it said only simply “and a strong sound of a shofar,” and afterwards it said “and the sound of a shofar going and very strong.” But behold, Rabbeinu Saadiah Gaon wrote that the shofar of Rosh Hashanah is a hint to the shofar of the day of the Giving of the Torah; and in my opinion one may say: certainly, what connection is there on Rosh Hashanah to mention the shofar of the Giving of the Torah? But one may say the intent is the reverse: for behold, the essence of the shofar hints to repentance, as the Rambam wrote in Hilchos Teshuvah, “Awake, sleepers, from your sleep,” etc. and therefore, since the Holy One, Blessed be He, wished to give the Torah to Israel and recognized that Israel would fear lest they be unable to fulfill the Torah and be punished, therefore the Holy One, Blessed be He, showed them that even if they transgress, there is arranged for them the path of Rosh Hashanah (through the shofar they will be aroused to repentance and become like a new creature; and therefore He showed them the matter of trembling) how it would fall upon them through the shofar; and therefore the Torah is close to reward and far from loss, for if they transgress they have a remedy through the shofar and repentance; and therefore the Holy One, Blessed be He, further showed them how they should act regarding the blasts; that there be two: one who reads, and afterwards one who blows. And thereby the shofar is elevated more than if the baal tokea blew alone. And therefore at first it said, “And the sound of the shofar was going and strong,” but not “very”; and even so, “and all the people who were in the camp trembled”; but afterwards it said, “And the sound of the shofar was going and very strong,” more than it was at first, namely, “Moshe would speak,” and he is the one who reads and arranges the blasts, “and God would answer him with a voice,” and the Holy One, Blessed be He, blows according to his speech; and therefore through both of them (Moshe’s reading and the blasts) the shofar became stronger than it was at first before Moshe was the reader, for then it was only a strong sound, and afterwards it became very strong because Moshe was the speaker and the reader. And also what the Magen Avraham wrote, that in any case the one who calls out should intend the secrets; it appears to hint this in that once it said, “A day of teruah it shall be for you,” and once it is written, “a remembrance of teruah, a holy convocation (mikra kodesh)”; and the intent is: for behold, our Sages, of blessed memory, expounded “A day of teruah it shall be for you”; and therefore the Torah hinted to us thus: that the baal tokea himself (“a day of teruah it shall be for you”) nevertheless, whether he can intend the secrets or not; but the remembrance of the reader who mentions the teruah with his mouth; this shall be a “holy convocation,” and he should intend with the holiness within them. But through our many sins there is none among us who knows until what [point], and we have none to rely upon except our Father in Heaven.
