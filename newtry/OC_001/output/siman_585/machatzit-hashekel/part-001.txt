**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(S.k. 3) And there is no, etc.</b> And it is better that the hearer recite the blessing; and his reason, [as] written in Terumat HaDeshen: for the mitzvah is the hearing, and for that reason we recite “to hear the sound of the shofar”; and since the baal tokea now is not subject to the obligation, for he has already fulfilled [his], and the mitzvah of hearing rests upon the hearer; why should the hearer who is obligated in the matter not recite the blessing? And it is the same as one who ties tefillin for his fellow on his arm and on his head: presumably the one for whom they are tied recites the blessing, and not the one who ties. Except that people do not practice so (end of the words of Terumat HaDeshen. “And regarding Shehecheyanu he wrote nothing”) that is to say, Terumat HaDeshen did not reveal his view as to who is fitting to recite Shehecheyanu, and also what the custom is. And the Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote in chapter 11 of Hilchos Berachos: whether one who performs a mitzvah for himself or for others, he recites a blessing before performing them, “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to do”; but he does not say Shehecheyanu except upon a mitzvah that he performed for himself.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should recite for himself and fulfill [his obligation] according to all opinions.</b> And certainly regarding the blessings of Shehecheyanu it is fine: if he recites for himself he fulfills [his obligation] according to all opinions; for in the Rambam it is stated explicitly that such is the law; and Terumat HaDeshen did not reveal his view regarding Shehecheyanu, as above. But regarding the blessing of the shofar, if he recites for himself (granted, for Terumat HaDeshen it is fine, for he wrote that so it is according to the law); but according to the Rambam the law is that the baal tokea should recite [it]. And for this the Magen Avraham wrote, “and so wrote Kenesses HaGedolah.” And that is to say: according to Kenesses HaGedolah’s words it is fine, for also according to the Rambam the hearer should recite also the blessing of the shofar; for he wrote that the Rambam is dealing specifically with one who circumcises another’s son, etc. For a reason is needed: why according to the Rambam should the one who performs recite the blessing of the mitzvah, and Shehecheyanu should be recited by the one for whom it was done? Necessarily he is dealing specifically with one who circumcises another’s son, or who makes a sukkah for his fellow, and the like (for the mitzvah being performed now is the performance; if so, the one who performs) namely the mohel or the one making the sukkah; is fitting to recite the blessing; but how should the father of the son or the owner of the sukkah recite [it], when he does nothing in the mitzvah? But Shehecheyanu the performer should not recite; for in any case he does not fulfill the main mitzvah, for he is not subject to this obligation, and it is not apt to say “Who has kept us alive and sustained us and brought us to this time” that he merited to fulfill the mitzvah; and it is more fitting that the father of the son or the owner of the sukkah recite Shehecheyanu. And even if the performer and the mohel wish to recite Shehecheyanu under the law of agency, having been made the agent of the father of the son and the owner of the sukkah; even that he is not permitted; for granted that the father of the son and the owner of the sukkah recite Shehecheyanu, nevertheless they are not called complete owners since they do nothing in the mitzvah; therefore their power is not strong enough to make him an agent. But regarding blowing the shofar, where the mitzvah is in the hearing, if so, if another blows and the hearer heard, then the hearer fulfills the mitzvah; the Rambam admits that the hearer recites both blessings, both the blessing of the shofar and Shehecheyanu; and he can also make an agent of the performer, that the performer recite both blessings, since they are complete owners and their power is strong enough to make an agent. End of the words of Kenesses HaGedolah, as I saw when the book Kenesses HaGedolah happened by chance to be in my hand for a short time. And if so, the Magen Avraham’s words are fine: it is preferable that the hearer recite both blessings and fulfill [his obligation] according to all opinions; for according to Terumat HaDeshen, from the essence of the law the hearer should recite the blessing of the shofar, and regarding Shehecheyanu he said nothing, and presumably he admits to the Rambam that from the essence of the law the hearer should recite Shehecheyanu; and according to the Rambam, by law he admits regarding shofar that the hearer may recite both blessings, as Kenesses HaGedolah wrote as above. And even though if the baal tokea recites both of them they also fulfill [their obligation], as Kenesses HaGedolah wrote; nevertheless, a mitzvah [done] by oneself is better than [done] by one’s agent.

But on the second day, when even the blasting is rabbinic (for they are expert and know that the first day is primary) therefore its doubt [is treated] leniently and he need not blow.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>See Yoreh De’ah siman 28 and above siman 67.</b> That is to say: in Yoreh De’ah siman 28 it is explained as I wrote, that on day one, granted he is obligated to blow out of doubt, but he should not recite a blessing; for it wrote there regarding a koi [uncertain animal]: since it is a doubt whether it is a wild animal, one must cover [the blood] out of doubt, for it is a Torah-law doubt; and nevertheless one should not recite a blessing on the covering. But above in siman 67 it implies apparently the opposite: for it wrote [regarding] a doubt whether he recited Shema (which is a Torah-law doubt) he must recite Shema out of doubt, and also he should recite the blessing out of doubt; even though the blessings are rabbinic. Already the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 67 in the name of the Rashba that the blessings of Shema are different, for originally so they instituted: whenever he recites Shema, even out of doubt, he should also recite the blessings. But the Peri Chadash disagrees; and he distinguished: specifically if the doubt is whether the person is subject to the obligation (like regarding a koi, where it is possible that it is a domestic animal and the person is not subject to covering its blood) one should not recite a blessing out of doubt; which is not so regarding Shema, where certainly the person is subject to the obligation, only the doubt is maybe he was already exempt because he already recited; in such a case he must also recite a blessing out of doubt. And according to this he wrote that also regarding shofar he should recite a blessing on the first day out of doubt (see there).
