**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He ate prohibited food.</b> Rambam rules this in chapter 1 of Hilchos Berachos. Raavad objects that the Mishnah said only that they do not include such people in zimun, because their eating lacks the significance of a fixed meal, like eating fruit, but why should they not recite initial and concluding blessings after deriving benefit? Raavad understands the Mishnah at the beginning of the third chapter, which says that one who ate tevel, even D'Rabbanan tevel, is not included in zimun, as applying only to zimun. Rambam must understand that the Mishnah teaches the greater novelty regarding zimun, although zimun is otherwise easier than Bircas HaMazon, since even one who ate a vegetable leaf may join. Prohibited food is worse, and certainly no blessing is recited. Rosh agrees with Raavad from the statement in Bava Kamma that one who stole wheat, ground it, kneaded it, baked it, and separated challah from it does not bless but blasphemes. This implies that he is obligated to bless, though his blessing is blasphemy. Beit Yosef supports Rambam from the Yerushalmi concerning stolen matzah and from a Tosefta stating that no blessing or zimun is recited over prohibited foods, and explains that “he does not bless but blasphemes” means that he therefore should not bless. In my opinion, Rosh certainly did not intend to prove that one should bless and thereby blaspheme Hashem, for silence is preferable. Rather, Raavad disputes Rambam only where the prohibited food was eaten inadvertently. Rambam explicitly writes that whether one ate deliberately or inadvertently, he recites no blessing. All agree that the Mishnah’s case is inadvertent, for one who ate deliberately is plainly wicked and would not be included. Rambam holds that such consumption is not regarded as eating at all, comparable to Yoreh Deah 119, where one who sold another a Torah-prohibited food that was eaten must refund the money because it is as though the purchaser did not eat. For blessings even a Rabbinic prohibition is treated as though no eating occurred. For the same reason, according to Rambam one who eats prohibited food because of danger should not bless, since inadvertence and compulsion are alike. Beit Yosef, however, writes that Rambam would agree that in danger one blesses. This is very difficult, for why should inadvertence be worse than danger? Inadvertence is also a form of compulsion. Responsa Rashba 794 concerning a sick person who eats on Yom Kippur equates the two cases and writes that although Rambam ruled that one who ate tevel does not bless, Raavad disputed him. This shows that according to Rambam even in danger no blessing is recited, while Raavad holds that after inadvertent consumption one blesses because he benefited. Initial blessing can also arise in a case of compulsion, which is considered inadvertent even though he knowingly eats. Rosh can therefore agree with Raavad: the Gemara’s description of a blessing as blasphemy applies to deliberate sin, but not to inadvertence, so the obligation remains. Beit Yosef’s proofs from stolen matzah and the Tosefta concern deliberate sin and do not disprove this. Even if Raavad and Rosh are not interpreted this way, practical halachah should distinguish: one who ate prohibited food inadvertently should recite the concluding blessing, since we already rule that one who eats because of danger must bless, and the two are equivalent. See Responsa Rashba 797.

<b>והזר אוכל פת כו'. </b> ב"י הביא דברי האגור בזה דמצטרפין כיון שאם יזדמן להם בסעודתן פת חולין של ישראל יכולים לאכול יחד וכת' ע"ז שהם דברים בטלים מההיא דעירובין עכ"ל והיינו מדאמרינן בתרומה שאינם מצטרפים ואע"פ דאפשר בשאלה מ"מ הרי עדיין לא נשאל ונלע"ד דאם מזומן ומצוי פת של ישראל אע"פ שלא אכלו ממנה מ"מ אפשר להם לאכול ביחד ממנו ומצטרפין ובזה מודה ב"י  אלא דק"ל ממה דאי' בסי' שאח"ז בש"ע דהיינו דאי' בגמ' רב ושמואל הוו יתבי בסעודה אתא רב שימי קא ממהר לאכול עמהם כדי להצטרף עמהם לזימון א"ל מאי דעתך לצרופי בהדן כבר אכלית אמר שמואל אלו מייתי גוזליא לאבא מי לא הוה אכיל. וכ' הרי"ף אלמ' כל היכי דאי מייתי להו מידי דמצי למיכל מיניה מצטרף בהדייהו עכ"ל. וא"כ ק"ו דמה התם שלא נתחייבו כלל בזימון אפ"ה כיון דאפשר לו' דאי מייתי להו מידי היו אוכלים מהני סברא זו לחייב זימון ק"ו הכא דכולהו אכלי אלא דא' אינו יכול לאכול עם חבירו פשיט' דמהני הך סברא דאי מתרמי להו לחם ישראל דהוי מצי לאכול א' עם חבירו ויצטרפו לזימון:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the non-kohen eats bread, etc.</b> Beit Yosef cites Agur that they combine because if ordinary Jewish bread became available during their meal, they could eat together. He dismisses this based on Eruvin, where kohanim eating terumah do not combine with a non-kohen, although the terumah could theoretically be undone. In my view, if ordinary Jewish bread is actually available, even if they have not eaten from it, they can eat it together and therefore combine. Beit Yosef would agree to this. Proof comes from the next siman: Rav and Shmuel were eating when Rav Shimi arrived and hurried to eat with them so he could join their zimun. They told him they had already eaten. Shmuel responded that if young pigeons were brought, would they not eat? Rif concludes that whenever additional food could be brought that they could eat, he combines with them. If this possibility creates an obligation of zimun where no obligation previously existed, all the more so here, where all ate but one could not eat the other’s food, the possibility that ordinary Jewish bread may become available allows them to combine.

<b>אם אוכלין מככר בע"ה. </b> בי"ד סי' רכ"א מבואר דאסור להם לאכול בקערה א' שמא יאכל מחלקו אא"כ שדרך בע"ה שחוזר וממלא הקערה א' לאחר שאכלו או מותר א"כ ה"נ ע"כ מיירי מדרך זה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If they eat from the householder’s loaf.</b> Yoreh Deah 221 explains that people forbidden by vows from benefiting from one another may not eat from one bowl, lest one eat the other’s portion, unless the householder ordinarily refills the common bowl after they eat or otherwise permits it. The case here must likewise involve such a circumstance.

<b>אין מזמנין כו'. </b> במשנה אמרי' עד כמה מזמנין עד כזית פירש"י כמה יאכל עמה' ויתחייב עמהם בזימון  ונ"מ להוציאן י"ח אם יתנו לו הכוס לברך עכ"ל. וא"כ היינו מ"ש אח"כ סי' קצ"ז שיהיה המברך א' מאוכלי הפת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They do not recite zimun, etc.</b> The Mishnah asks: How much must one eat to join zimun? An olive-sized portion. Rashi explains: how much must he eat with them to become obligated in zimun with them? A practical consequence is whether he can discharge them if they give him the cup to lead. This is the law stated below in siman 197, that the one leading must be among those who ate bread.
