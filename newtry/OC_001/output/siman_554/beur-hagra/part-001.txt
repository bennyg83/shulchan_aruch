<b>ס"ח הלכך כו'. </b>ואע"ג דתוס' שם הקשו דבסוף יומא מ' דאף למ"ד טבילה בזמנה לאו מצוה מותר לטבול כמ"ש מ"ט דרבנן קסברי רבנן כו' מכלל דת"ק דברייתא קמא ס"ל דלאו מצוה ואפ"ה מותר לטבול תוס' ביומא שם תי' דה"ק בשביל התפלה א"צ לטבול אבל בשביל טבילה בזמנה יש לה לטבול וע"ש ד"ה קסברי והרמב"ן תירץ דבעל קרי דשם דראה לפני יום הכיפורים והוי שלא בזמנה ואינו מותר אלא משום תפלה וכן הלכה דטבילה בזמנה מצוה כת"ק דשם וב"ש וב"ה דפליגי אר"י בר יהודה וז"ש טבילה כו' ודלא כר"ח בן אנטינגונוס וכן דלא כתי' אחר דתוס' מפני טהרות דמכל מקום קשה מבעל קרי וצ"ל כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 8: Therefore, etc.</b> Although Tosafos there asked from the end of Yoma 88a that even according to the opinion that immersion at its proper time is not a mitzvah, immersion is permitted, as it states: ‘What is the reasoning of the Sages? The Sages hold,’ etc., implying that the first Tanna of the first baraisa holds that it is not a mitzvah and nevertheless permits immersion. Tosafos in Yoma there answers that the meaning is: immersion is unnecessary for the sake of prayer, but she should immerse for the sake of immersion at its proper time. See there, s.v. ‘The Sages hold.’ Ramban answers that the baal keri there had an emission before Yom Kippur, so the immersion was not at its proper time, and it was permitted only for prayer. The halachah follows the view that immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah, like the first Tanna there and Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel, who disagree with Rabbi Yose bar Yehudah. This is the meaning of, ‘Immersion, etc.,’ unlike Rabbi Chanina ben Antigonus. It is also unlike Tosafos’ alternative answer concerning sacred foods, for the difficulty from the baal keri remains. It must therefore be explained as above.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 10: He washes, etc.</b> They prohibited only washing for pleasure, as is evident from the ruling concerning one who has scabs, etc., and from the statement that all those obligated to immerse, etc., and that one going to greet his teacher may cross through water, etc. All the more so is this permitted for prayer. There is no greater case of soiled hands than this, concerning which they said, ‘A hand to the eye,’ etc.; and there is no greater discomfort than being unable to touch. There is also proof from the Yerushalmi, as written in seif 11.

<b>סי"א לאחר שניגב כו'. </b>ירושלמי פ"א דתענית הלכה ו' בת"ץ רוחץ פניו ידיו ורגליו כדרכו בט"ב רוחץ ידיו ומעביר על פניו וגי' הטור על גבי עיניו ופי' כה"ג ואמרו שם בי"ה רוחץ ידיו ומקנחה במפה ומעביר את המפה על פניו ר"ל כמין כלים נגובים ומזה ראיה דמותר רחיצת ידים בט"ב ויום הכפורים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 11: After he dried, etc.</b> Yerushalmi, chapter 1 of Taanis, halachah 6: On a communal fast one washes his face, hands, and feet in the usual manner. On Tishah B’Av he washes his hands and passes them over his face. The Tur’s reading is, ‘over his eyes,’ and he explains it in this manner. They also state there that on Yom Kippur one washes his hands, dries them with a cloth, and passes the cloth over his face, meaning with a cloth that is merely damp after drying. This proves that washing one’s hands is permitted on Tishah B’Av and Yom Kippur.
