<b>ונעילת הסנדל</b>. ואסור לנעול סנדל כל היום (הגאונים) ור"ח כ' יהא בנדוי מי שעושה א' מכל אלו כל היום (תניא וש"ל ומהרי"ל וב"ח ושל"ה) אך אותם ההולכים בין העכו"ם ועוסקין במשא ומתן אחר חצות אותן מותרים לנעול ומהם למדו כולם להקל ויש למחות בידם:

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Through thought.</b> Although thought is not like speech, as written in siman 62, here it is different, for the main reason is joy, and thought also contains joy; Maharil. For this reason it appears to me that it is forbidden to study any derashah, question, or answer, even in sad matters, because it gives him joy. It is also forbidden to issue a halachic ruling unless for a sick person who needs it now, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 384, seif 4, for ruling is like thought in Torah, as written in siman 85. It is forbidden to judge monetary cases, for judgment is Torah. However, if the litigants cannot wait until tomorrow because they are pressed to travel, perhaps it is permitted, since the public needs him.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A woman after childbirth all thirty days.</b> Maharshal wrote in a responsum that a woman who gave birth on Shabbos, the second of Av, must fast on the postponed Tisha B'Av, for only within seven days is she exempt, but after seven, even if she says she needs to eat, she must fast unless she is somewhat ill. The Darkei Moshe notes that the Beit Yosef in siman 559 implies that after seven she should fast, while the Shulchan Aruch there implies she should not. The Levush in siman 602 wrote that she should fast, and so did Hagahos Maimoniyos. The Bach wrote that by law she may eat, but the custom has already become to fast as long as they are not weak and have recovered from childbirth, and even on the other three fasts healthy women have been stringent. Where this is the custom, it remains the custom; but on a postponed Tisha B'Av one may be lenient. Some women after childbirth have the custom to refrain from meat and wine from the seventh of Av and onward, which is proper, for on that day the non-Jews entered the Sanctuary, as stated in the Tur.

<b>שלא לעשות</b>. כדי שלא יסיחו דעתן מהאבילו' (הרע"ב פ"ד דפסחי') ובת"ה פלפל למצו' טעם כמ"ש בסמוך ונ"ל דמטעם זה גם בלילה אסור במלאכה (דהא גם בלילה מחויב להתאבל) וכ"כ רש"י בפסחים דף נ"ד ע"ב בין השמשות שלו אסור באכילה ובמלאכה וצ"ע דבתעני' דף י"ג פרש"י מהכא משמע דבליל ט' באב מותרים במלאכה ואין איסור אלא ביום ואין מפרסמין הדבר עכ"ל ומסתימת כל הפוסקים משמע דאסור גם בלילה ול"ד לת"צ דהתם הטעם שיהיו פנוים ויתאספו להתפלל (ע' סימן תקע"ה ס"ג) ולכן מותרים בלילה דלא היו מתאספים בלילה כדאיתא בגמרא משא"כ בט' באב שאין אסור במלאכה ותליא במנהגם שנוהגים איסור טעמם שלא יסיחו דעתם מאבילות או משום עינוי ולכן אף בלילה אסור ולכן כל מלאכה שאין בה שיהוי שרי דאין מסיח דעתו בכך כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Not to do.</b> This is so they not divert their attention from mourning; Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura on Pesachim chapter 4. Terumas HaDeshen discussed finding a reason, as below. It appears to me that for this reason work is also forbidden at night, since at night too one is obligated to mourn. Rashi in Pesachim 54b writes that twilight of Tisha B'Av is forbidden in eating and work. This requires study, for in Taanis 13 Rashi implies that work is permitted on the night of Tisha B'Av and forbidden only by day, and the matter is not publicized. From the silence of all the poskim it appears that it is forbidden at night too. It is not comparable to other public fasts, where the reason is that people be free and gather to pray, and therefore they are permitted at night, when they do not gather. Tisha B'Av depends on custom, and the reason is so that one not divert his attention from mourning or because of affliction, so it is forbidden even at night. Therefore any work that does not take time is permitted, since it does not divert his attention.

<b>If possible through a non-Jew.</b> Although it is a matter of loss, as written in siman 533, that case is different, for the prohibition of work there is because of rest, and the Sages permitted any amateur work that is not burdensome. But Tisha B'Av is because of affliction, as Rashi wrote, and therefore even work that is not burdensome and is amateur is forbidden. Yet some poskim imply that here too the reason is the prohibition of work, and therefore if it cannot be done through a non-Jew, one may do it himself as on Chol HaMoed; Terumas HaDeshen.

<b>דבר האבד</b>. אם יריד או שיירות באים מותר לקנות מהם ע"י עכו"ם וכ"ש למכור דהוי דבר האבד כמ"ש בי"ד סי' ש"פ ס"ז לענין אבל (הגמ"נ ומט"מ), והרא"ש כתב במס' תענית במשא ומתן דאיכ' פרסום החמירו טפי בט"ב מבאבל (ועמ"ש ס"ב) ומ"מ יש להקל כיון דאינו אלא מנהג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A matter of loss.</b> If a market day or caravans arrive, it is permitted to buy from them through a non-Jew, and certainly to sell, for it is a matter of loss, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 380, seif 7, regarding a mourner; Hagahos Maimoniyos and Matteh Moshe. The Rosh wrote in tractate Taanis that regarding business, which is public, they were more stringent on Tisha B'Av than by a mourner. Nevertheless one may be lenient, since it is only a custom.

<b>כל העושה מלאכה</b>. משמע אפי' במקום שנהגו לעשות וכ"מ ברי"ף ורא"ש וטור ובקיצור פסקי הרא"ש דילג הא דמקום שנהגו וכתב רק הא דאינו רואה סימן ברכה וא"כ צ"ע למה אין נזהרין בזה ומ"מ מגמרא משמע דוקא כשקובע עצמו למלאכה ומסיח דעתו מהאבילות ע"ש, וא"כ אף שמותר לישא וליתן אחר חצות לא ימשוך בעסקיו כדי שלא יסיח דעתו מהאבילות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Whoever does work.</b> This implies even in a place where the custom is to do work, and so it appears from the Rif, Rosh, and Tur. In the abbreviated rulings of the Rosh, he omitted the matter of a place where the custom is to do work and wrote only that one sees no sign of blessing. If so, it requires study why people are not careful about this. Nevertheless, from the Gemara it implies that this is specifically when one sets himself to work and diverts his attention from mourning. Therefore even though it is permitted to engage in business after midday, he should not prolong his affairs, so as not to divert his attention from mourning.
