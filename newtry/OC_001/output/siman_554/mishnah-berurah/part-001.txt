**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A woman after childbirth all thirty days.</b> Presumably she is considered weak and like a sick person without danger. Therefore, although regarding Yom Kippur after seven days she is like anyone else, as written in siman 617, here regarding Tisha B'Av, which is D'Rabbanan, they did not decree in a case of illness. See the Taz and the other later authorities, who write that even where there is certainly no danger, and likewise the sick person mentioned next, even if they did not say, we need to eat, they may eat on Tisha B'Av. Although Maharshal disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch's ruling and holds that regarding this Tisha B'Av is like Yom Kippur, many later authorities agree that by law she may eat. However, the custom has already become to fast, as the Rama wrote, so long as they have no weakness and have recovered from childbirth. But if she has not fully recovered, or is somewhat ill, or is naturally weak, she should not fast within thirty days. If a healthy woman after childbirth fasts and feels unusual weakness in the middle of the day, one should rule that she not fast for the rest of the day; later authorities.
