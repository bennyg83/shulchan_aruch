**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2, and children, etc.</b> Even though the child does not understand, good and bad subjects are the same for him, for he rejoices in knowing how to read and say the meaning of the words even without grasping the matter. Therefore the Magen Avraham writes that it is forbidden to study with others, as in Yoreh Deah. Accordingly, with a child, whether he understands or not, it is forbidden, even sad matters, because of the teacher's prohibition. However, since the Shach writes that where the public needs him it is permitted, and a teacher of children is considered needed by the public, one could have said this is permitted. Still, on Tisha B'Av the mourning applies also to a minor who has reached education, so it is more stringent. The Taz held that the prohibition of studying with the child is not because of the child but because of the teacher, and this applies specifically to subjects that are not sad matters.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 10, immersion, etc.</b> Since we require closeness, etc. Rashi and the Sheiltos disagree. According to Rashi, initially the combing should be on the day before the immersion night, lest because she is hurried and rushing home she not comb properly. According to the Sheiltos, it is preferable to comb at night before immersion, so that the combing is as close to the immersion as possible. Therefore the Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 199, that the custom is for her to begin combing while it is still day and continue until night. If her immersion falls on Motzaei Shabbos, when she cannot comb while it is still day, the Rama writes there that she combs on Friday and then repeats some combing on Motzaei Shabbos. The same applies here on Tisha B'Av. See siman 551, seif 16, in the gloss, which states that even if she immerses on the night of the tenth of Av, she may wash on Erev Tisha B'Av if she cannot wash on the night of the tenth. This implies that if she can wash on the night of the tenth, she may not wash on Erev Tisha B'Av, and we rely on the view of the Sheiltos.

And so it is implied in Chagigah 5b, where Rabbi Yaakov bar Chatiya would regularly greet his rebbe every day. When he grew old, his rebbe told him not to trouble himself daily because it was difficult for him due to age, and he answered from the reward of seeing sages. Eliyah Rabbah asked why the Shunammite's husband asked, why are you going today, if there is a mitzvah every day. This is no difficulty, for it was not her way to go every day, for whatever reason, and since he saw that she changed her custom he wanted to know why. In truth she went because of her son, not for greeting the rebbe. However, in siman 301, seif katan 7, it seems that the Magen Avraham accepts the first answer, that on a weekday there is not even a mitzvah. There he writes that the obligation is on the festival, while on Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh there is a mitzvah; see siman 554, seif 12. Thus on an ordinary weekday there is not even a mitzvah. He must explain Rabbi Yitzchak as deriving that on Rosh Chodesh and Shabbos there is at least a mitzvah, and therefore on a festival the level is higher and there is an obligation.

It must be that he explained it as stated earlier in the name of the Rav, that one should not divert his attention from the affliction, since by doing work he diverts his attention from mourning and affliction.
