**** ENGLISH ****
<b>In the Taz, s.k. 4.</b> And it is difficult for me: from that he said 'assessment is not required,' it is implied that there is doubt, etc. And apparently his words are hard to understand; for in his answer he concludes that it means assessment is not required, even if certainly there is no danger in it, etc. Also this requires further study: what compelled him in his answer to explain even if certainly there is no danger, etc., and not explain this 'assessment is not required' simply, that it deals with doubt, except that she did not say 'I need'? And it appears that his intent is as follows: the Taz thus found difficult: from that he said she does not require assessment, it is implied that it deals with a sick person regarding whom we are in doubt whether there is danger in the matter; and he says assessment is not required, lest according to my assessment she be endangered because of the fast; rather they feed her immediately. And on this the Taz found difficult: for if she says 'I need,' if so what does it teach us that assessment is not required? For behold, even when physicians assess her and say there is no danger, nevertheless if a physician says she does not need and the sick person says she needs, we heed the sick person. And what the Taz wrote, 'there is no danger; assessment does not apply': one can say, if it deals with a sick person to whom danger does not apply, and there is no doubt in the matter that certainly there is no place for danger, then assessment does not apply; and therefore it must deal where there is doubt whether she will be endangered; if so it is obvious that assessment is not required, for even if they assessed her we pay no attention at all, since she needs [food] by assessment as well. And also one should not explain 'assessment is not required' simply, and she did not say 'I need'; for then it would truly be difficult: since then they would assess her that there is no danger in her and it would be forbidden; if so, what difference to me Yom Kippur, whose prohibition is according to the Torah, and what difference to me Tisha B'Av, which is rabbinic: why should we not assess her, and if they say she will not be endangered we should not feed her, since she does not say 'I need'? And likewise regarding a sick person of this kind; for if we knew there is no danger we would not feed her: one should not be lenient without assessment. Therefore the Taz explained that it means assessment is not required, that is, because even if they assess that she will not be endangered, nevertheless they feed her even when she does not say 'I need,' since this sick person in any event has a doubt lest she be endangered through it; we need not be stringent according to the assessment that she will not be endangered, since Tisha B'Av is rabbinic. And regarding this it fits well to distinguish between rabbinic and according to the Torah, that is, between Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur; which is not so if we say that when they assess her that she will not be endangered it is forbidden even on Tisha B'Av: we need not be lenient without assessment, since it can be clarified even on Tisha B'Av to be stringent; such appears to my humble opinion to be the intent of the Taz.

<b>שם </b>אותו חולה א"צ אומד שמא יש בו סכנה דאפי' כו' זה צ"ע ונראה כוונתו דמתחלה פי' דע"כ מיירי דיש ספק אם יש סכנה ר"ל שאנו נכנסין לגדר הספק בזה דאם אין נופל לנו שום ספק בדבר לא שייך אומד אלא ודאי שאנו מסופקים ולזה אמר שא"צ אימוד לשאול לרופאים בזה אלא מאכילין אותו עפ"י הספק שלנו ולכך קשיא ליה על רש"ל דמפרש בצריך אני דבזה פשיטא דאפי' ביוה"כ מאכילין אותו בלא אומד הרופאים כמבואר שם דאפי' ק' אומרים אינו צריך שומעין לחולה ולכך כ' דמיירי באין אומרת צריכה אני ובזה שפיר קאמר דבחי' או בחולה שאנו מסופקים בו אם יש בו איזה חשש סכנה דבכה"ג ביוה"כ אם החולה אומר א"צ אז צריך אומד מבקיאים אבל בט"ב א"צ אומד שיאמר הרופא שיש לחוש בו לסכנה שאפי' אם יאמר הרופא שבודאי אין בו סכנה מ"מ בט"ב הקילו כיון שעכ"פ אנו רואין שהוא חולה עד שהיינו מסופקים בו מחשש סכנה לולי מאמר הבקיאים בחולי כה"ג לא גזרו ביה וזה כוונת הט"ז א"צ אומד לומר שיש בו סכנה ר"ל אין צריך שאומר הרופא שיש חשש אם יתענה לסכנה דאפי' אם יאמר בוודאי אין בו סכנה והחולה ג"כ אין אומר צריך אני דבכה"ג ביוה"כ מאכילין אותו מ"מ בט"ב שפיר מאכילין אותו שאנו נכנסין לספק בו שצריך לשאלת הבקיאין לא גזרו בי' רבנן לדינא צ"ע אם יש להקל בכה"ג ונראה שעכ"פ צריך שסתם בני אדם הבקיאין קצת יהיו מסופקים בחשש סכנה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There.</b> 'That sick person does not require assessment lest there be danger in him; for even...' This requires further study. And it appears that his intent is: at first he explained that necessarily it deals where there is doubt whether there is danger, that is to say, that we enter the category of doubt in this; for if no doubt at all falls for us in the matter, assessment does not apply; rather, certainly we are in doubt; and for this he said that assessment is not required, to ask the physicians about this; rather they feed him according to our doubt. And therefore it was difficult for him against Maharshal, who explains it as where she says 'I need'; for in that case it is obvious that even on Yom Kippur they feed him without physicians' assessment, as clarified there, that even if a hundred say he does not need, we heed the sick person. Therefore he wrote that it deals where she does not say 'I need'; and in this it fits well to say that regarding a woman after childbirth or a sick person about whom we are in doubt whether there is any concern of danger: in such a case on Yom Kippur, if the sick person says 'I do not need,' then assessment from experts is required; but on Tisha B'Av assessment is not required for a physician to say there is concern of danger if he fasts; for even if the physician says that certainly there is no danger, and the sick person also does not say 'I need' (in which case on Yom Kippur they would not feed him), nevertheless on Tisha B'Av they properly feed him; for since we enter doubt regarding him such that one needs to ask the experts, the Rabbis did not decree upon him. And this is the intent of the Taz: 'assessment is not required to say there is danger in him,' that is to say, it is not required that the physician say there is concern of danger if he fasts; for even if he says that certainly there is no danger, and the sick person also does not say 'I need' (in which case on Yom Kippur they do not feed him), nevertheless on Tisha B'Av they properly feed him; for since we enter doubt regarding him such that one needs the experts' inquiry, the Rabbis did not decree upon him. As a matter of law this requires further study whether one should be lenient in such a case; and it appears that in any event it is required that ordinary people who are somewhat expert be in doubt with concern of danger.

And it appears to my humble opinion that perhaps he was stringent in this only on Yom Kippur, which is according to the Torah and a doubt of karet; but Tisha B'Av is rabbinic, and certainly one may rely on the Rosh who holds that we require a full twenty-four hours; and so it is implied from Maharshal's words, who took [the case of] one who gave birth on the second of Av, implying that if she gave birth on the third of Av she is exempt from fasting, even though Sunday when it was postponed is after seven; and it is strained to say that the incident happened so, and also not to say the reason she is exempt in such a case is because on Shabbos which is Tisha B'Av it is after seven, and if it had been a weekday she would have been exempt, and if so even on Sunday when it was postponed she is exempt, because once the fast of the whole world was already fixed on Sunday and then she is after seven we have no reason to exempt her on that ground; rather it appears that he holds we require a full twenty-four hours; and if so, if she gave birth on the third of Av, then before the tenth of Av seven full twenty-four-hour days were not completed, and when the tenth of Av entered she was within seven and is exempt; and perhaps even when it was not postponed one should be lenient in such a case. And accordingly there is no proof from what Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 559, which implies she should not fast, namely from his writing that the woman after childbirth should drink the cup; and according to what I wrote, one may say that there it deals with one who gave birth on the third of Av, when now the seven days were not completed before Tisha B'Av, and in such a case one should be lenient; and with this it fits well what is written in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 265, that on Tisha B'Av, when the woman after childbirth cannot drink from the cup, he should not recite the blessing, see there; for there it deals with a case such as where [the child] was born at the start of night Monday night. And Be'er HaGolah distinguishes that there it deals where she does not need to eat, and here it deals where she needs; however, even according to Maharshal's view one may say in siman 559 that she is somewhat sick. And it appears to me that in any event when postponed one should be lenient if she gave birth on the third of Av, for then seven days are completed on the tenth of Av as full twenty-four-hour periods, and to be lenient per the Rosh's answer that we require a full twenty-four hours; and see what I wrote in my work Beis Ephraim, Yoreh De'ah section, on the matter that there is no sircha less than three days, what I wrote on this.

<b>S.k. 23.</b> For he does not divert his mind thereby; and also because of nullifying the affliction it applies only to work that has some lingering, so that he amuses himself with it; so wrote Terumat HaDeshen, see there.
