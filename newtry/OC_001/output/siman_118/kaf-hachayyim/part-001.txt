<b>1) [Seif 1] And from Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur he concludes 'HaMelech HaMishpat.' Gloss: However, if he said 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat' he need not repeat, etc.</b> Behold, in this law the opinions are many, and therefore we shall first bring the language of the Gemara, Berachos folio 12b: Rabbah bar Chinnana Sava said in the name of Rav: The entire year a person prays 'HaKel HaKadosh' and 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat,' except for the ten days between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, when he prays 'HaMelech HaKadosh' and 'HaMelech HaMishpat.' And Rabbi Elazar said: Even if he said 'HaMelech HaKadosh' he has fulfilled his obligation, etc. What was concluded about it? Rav Yosef said: 'HaKel HaKadosh' and 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat'; Rabbah said: 'HaMelech HaKadosh' and 'HaMelech HaMishpat,' and the halachah follows Rabbah. Until here. And the Tosafos wrote there that one must conclude 'HaMelech HaKadosh' and 'HaMelech HaMishpat,' and if he did not say so, we make him repeat. So too wrote the Tur in siman 582 in the name of the Rif, the Rambam, and the Rosh; only in the name of the Ravyah did he write that after the fact he does not repeat. And the Beis Yosef wrote there that as for the halachah, since the Rif, the Rambam, and the Rosh, of blessed memory, agree that after the fact as well he has not fulfilled his obligation, so do we hold; and so he ruled there in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1; see there. However, R' Yechiel, the brother of Rabbeinu Yaakov the Baal HaTurim, wrote that he does not know what difference there is between 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat' and 'HaMelech HaMishpat' with respect to having to return to the beginning if he forgot, and the Tur cited him in this siman. So too wrote the Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah at the end of the first chapter of Berachos, that if he said 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat' he need not repeat, since he mentions kingship in it; see there. So too wrote Moreinu HaRav Moshe [Isserles], of blessed memory, in Darkei Moshe, siman 582, ot 3, in the name of the Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Rashba, and this is why he ruled here that he need not repeat if he said 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat.' So too ruled the Bach in this siman, and so wrote the Magen Avraham in s.k. 1 in the name of the Mateh Moshe and the Shelah, and wrote that it is not like the Levush, who wrote that the custom is to repeat, for behold, the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that one need not, and he was expert in customs; see there. So too wrote the Gan HaMelech, siman 150, in the name of a responsum of the Rambam; see there. However, the Pri Chadash, siman 582, ot 1, ruled that even if he said 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat' he must repeat; see there. So too wrote the Mateh Yehudah there, and so ruled the Birkei Yosef in this siman, ot 2, and in siman 582, ot 1; Maamar Mordechai, ot 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 312c. The Taz, however, in this siman, s.k. 2, wrote that he repeats and prays as a voluntary prayer (nedavah); see there. Soles Belulah, ot 1. And so wrote the Shulchan Aruch HaRav in siman 582, ot 2. And the Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 582, in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 2, wrote that most of the poskim hold that he does not repeat, and wrote that also in the view of Maran, of blessed memory, one can say so, that if he said 'Melech oheiv tzedakah umishpat' he does not repeat; and he concluded that since there is a dispute among the poskim in this, array facing array, [we say] safeik berachos l'hakel [in a doubt concerning berachos we are lenient], and he does not repeat; see there. So too appears to be the view of the Eliyah Rabbah in siman 582, ot 5, and so ruled the Yad Aharon there in the Hagahos Beis Yosef; Chayei Adam, klal 24, ot 16; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 129, ot 3; the Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, parashas Nitzavim, ot 19, who wrote that although Maran, of blessed memory, holds that he repeats, we hold that safeik berachos l'hakel applies even against the view of Maran, of blessed memory; see there. Derech HaChaim, ot 2; Mishnah Berurah, ot 3. And so is the essential ruling, since we hold safeik berachos l'hakel even against Maran, of blessed memory, as the Rav Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, wrote, and as we in our humble way wrote above in siman 4, ot 10, and siman 18, ot 7, and in many places; see there. And one should not say that he should repeat and pray as a voluntary prayer, because a voluntary prayer requires great concentration, as stated at the end of siman 107; therefore, in such a case, where most of the poskim hold that he need not repeat, one may rely upon them.
