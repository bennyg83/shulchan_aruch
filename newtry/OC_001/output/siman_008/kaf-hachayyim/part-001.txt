<b>א) [סעיף א'] יתעטף בציצית וכו'  </b>מיד "אחר נט"י, טור. מ"א סק"א, ח"א כלל י"א אות א' ר"ז אות א', והטעם כתב המחה"ש כדי שיתעטף מיד במצוה. והפרמ"ג בא"א כתב כדי שלא ילך ד"א בלא ציצית, וכ"כ ח"א שם, אבל קודם נט"י אין ללבוש ציצית כי ראוי לברך עובר לעשיותן עיין סעי' יו"ד, ומיהו לדידן דמברכין על טלית גדול להתעטף ומכוין לפטור זה אין צורך אלא ילבש תחילה קודם נטילת ידים, אכן לפי מ"ש מג"א סי' ד' סעק"א בשם סד"ה דלא יגע במלבושין קודם נט"י א"כ לא ילבש הטלית עד אחר נטילת ידים, פרמ"ג א"א ס"ק א' (עיין לב"ש ובדברינו לעיל סי' ד' אות ו') ואפילו כשמשכים קודם אור היום יתעטף מיד וכשיאיר היום ימשמש בו ויברך וכמ"ש סעיף ט"ז. מחה"ש. ולדידן כשמברך על טלית גדול להתעטף יכוין לפטור את זה וכאשר יבואר שם בס"ד, והגם שמנהגינו לשכב בטלית קטן כדברי האר"י ז"ל כאשר יבואר סי' כ"א סעי' ג' בס"ד מ"מ נפקא מנה לענין אם אירע מקרה שלא ישן בטליתו או במקומות שאין יכולים לשכב במלבושיהם מפני הפרעושים וכדומה, ועיין שע"ת אות א':

<b>1) [Se'if 1] He should wrap himself in tzitzis, etc.</b> This should be done immediately after netilas yadayim. Tur; Magen Avraham 1; Chayei Adam 11:1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1. Machatzis HaShekel explains that the reason is so that he immediately wraps himself in a mitzvah. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, writes that it is so he does not walk four amos without tzitzis; Chayei Adam there writes likewise. One should not, however, don tzitzis before netilas yadayim, because it is proper to recite the berachah immediately before performing the mitzvah; see se'if 10. According to our practice of reciting 'to wrap oneself' over the tallis gadol with intent to exempt the tallis katan, this is not necessary, and he could first don it before washing his hands. Yet Magen Avraham, siman 4:1, writes in the name of Seder HaYom that one should not touch his garments before netilas yadayim. Accordingly, he should not don the tallis until after washing. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1. See Levushei Serad and our comments above in siman 4:6. Even if one rises before daylight, he should wrap himself immediately; when daylight comes, he should touch the tzitzis and recite the berachah, as stated in se'if 16. Machatzis HaShekel. According to our practice, when reciting 'to wrap oneself' over the tallis gadol, he should intend to exempt this one, as will be explained there with God's help. Although our custom, following the Arizal, is to sleep in a tallis katan, as will be explained in siman 21:3 with God's help, this ruling remains relevant if one happened not to sleep in his tallis, or in places where people cannot sleep in their clothing because of fleas or the like. See Shaarei Teshuvah 1.

<b>ב) שם  </b>ויברך וכו' הא דאין מברכין על כל המצות תי' בת' הרמב"ן סי' קפ"ט דכל מצוה שאין בה מעשה כגון שמיטת כספים וכ"ד שנמסרו לב"ד וכן דברים התלויים בדעת אחרים כגון צדקה וכדומה, וכן מצוה הבאה ע"י עבירה כנון השבת גזל ורבית, ובמתנות כהונה נמי לא דמשלחן גבוה זכו משא"כ פדיון הבן והפרשת תרומה שאין מברכין אנתינה אלא אהפרשה. וכן א"מ ע"כ מצוה שאין עיקר מצוה כחליצה ויבים דמצוה היא פו"ר אלא דהמצות אלו מסתעפים ממנה, וכן מיתות ב"ד נמי לא לפי שאין הקב"ה חפץ במיתת רשעים ומה"ט נמי א"מ על מים אחרונים הבאה משום מלח סדומית ואין לברך על הפורעניות ועי"ש באורך, א"א אות א' ביאורי הגר"א אות ב' כר"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) There: And he should recite the berachah, etc.</b> Responsa Ramban 189 explains why a berachah is not recited over every mitzvah. No berachah is recited over a mitzvah that involves no action, such as release of debts, or over matters entrusted to beis din. The same applies to matters dependent upon another person's decision, such as tzedakah; to a mitzvah occasioned by a transgression, such as returning stolen property or interest; and to gifts to kohanim, because they receive them from the table of the Most High. Pidyon HaBen and separating terumah differ: the berachah is not recited over giving them, but over the act of separation. Nor is a berachah recited over a mitzvah that is not itself the primary mitzvah, such as chalitzah and yibbum, for the primary mitzvah is procreation and these mitzvos branch from it. No berachah is recited over judicial executions either, because the Holy One, blessed is He, does not desire the death of the wicked. For this reason, no berachah is recited over mayim acharonim, which are required because of Sodomite salt; one does not recite a berachah over calamity. See the responsum at length. Eshel Avraham 1; Gra's commentary 2; Kerem Shlomo.

<b>3) There: He should wrap himself in tzitzis and recite the berachah, etc.</b> This implies that one does not recite the berachah before beginning to wrap himself, but while wrapping, before he is fully wrapped, as with tefillin. Eliyah Rabbah 2 writes that Responsa Beis Yaakov 88 implies this; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, writes that after placing the tallis upon himself, but before taking the tzitzis in his hand (having merely thrown them behind him) he may recite the berachah; see there. Chida, Machazik Berachah 1, however, writes in the name of Shaagas Aryeh 32 that it is proper to recite the berachah before wrapping, when one holds the tallis and is about to wrap himself, because this is immediately before performance. He writes that this is the custom, unlike Responsa Beis Yaakov. Kesher Gudal 2:5; Maamar Mordechai 6; Ikrei HaDat 2:34 in the name of Shaagas Aryeh; and Tefillah LeDavid, page 5b, rule likewise, unlike Beis Yaakov. See also Zechirah LeChaim, Hilchos Berachos 9; Beis Yaakov 21 in the names of Shaagas Aryeh and Machazik Berachah, noting that Rokeach at the beginning of siman 361 agrees; Kerem Shlomo in the name of Shaagas Aryeh; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 9:8; and Chayei Olam on Shulchan Aruch, se'if 1, who writes that this is implied by Magen Avraham. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 2, likewise writes that Magen Avraham implies this. See Pnei Yitzchak, maareches tes, paragraph 118, and Yafeh LaLev 1:3, who agrees and cites several additional later authorities. Shalmei Tzibbur discusses this at length and writes on page 32a that there is a straight path by which one can satisfy Taz and Beis Yaakov even while reciting the berachah beforehand with the tallis still in his hands: immediately after the berachah, he should place the entire tallis backward over his head and body at once. The berachah then unquestionably precedes performance. After putting it on this way, he should raise the corners over his neck and wrap himself in the manner of the Yishmaelim for the time required to walk four amos. He concludes: 'I have seen people of exemplary conduct among the prominent residents of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, follow this practice.' Ben Ish Chai, first year, Bereishis 5, however, writes: 'After completing the berachah, wrap only your head in it, unlike those who consider themselves clever and first wrap their entire body. We have only the Arizal's words.' Nevertheless, the chassidim of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, who follow the Arizal, practice as Shalmei Tzibbur writes, because this matter is not stated explicitly in the Arizal's words; see Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis. I, the servant, provided support for their practice in Responsa Be'er Mayim Chaim; see there.

<b>ה) שם  </b>ויברך מעומד, כתב הרב שמן המאור וז"ל הנה המחבר בש"ע לא ביאר לנו הדין לענין דיעבד אם התעטף מיושב בדיעבד אם צריך לחזור ולהתעטף מעומד א"ל רק המ"א בסק"ב שכתב עיין מ"ש סי' תקפ"ה כתב הפרמ"ג דכיונתו דשה סעי' א' גבי שופר כתב אם תקע מיושב יצא וה"ה הכא אם התעטף מיושב דיצא ונכון הוא עכ"ל, וכ"כ ח"א כלל י"ב אות ג' דאם בירך מיושב יצא, וכ"כ עמודי השלחן על קי' הש"ע סי' ט' אות י"ג. וכ"כ תפלה לדוד אות י"ח דאם נתעטף מיושב ובירך מיושב יצא, ומ"ש א"ר סק"ב עיין להשמן המאור שם שדחה דבריו יעו"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>5) There: And he should recite the berachah while standing.</b> Shemen HaMaor writes: Shulchan Aruch did not explain the after-the-fact law; if one wrapped himself while seated, must he wrap himself again while standing? Magen Avraham 2 merely refers to what he wrote in siman 585. Pri Megadim explains that there, se'if 1, regarding shofar, Shulchan Aruch rules that one who sounded it while seated fulfilled his obligation; the same applies here, and one who wrapped himself while seated has fulfilled his obligation. This is correct. Chayei Adam 12:3 likewise writes that one who recited the berachah while seated fulfilled his obligation. See also Amudei HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 9:13 and Tefillah LeDavid 18, who writes that one who wrapped himself and recited the berachah while seated fulfilled his obligation. Regarding Eliyah Rabbah 2, see Shemen HaMaor there, who rejects his words.

לאחיריהם לצד שמאל מכים בד' ציציות ב"פ כנגד העורף לצד שמאל כנגד ב' הכאות הנזכרים בשער הכוו' דרוש ב' של הציצית ובסי' של הרש"ש ז"ל:

<b>7) [Se'if 2] The manner of wrapping is like people who wrap themselves in their garments, etc.</b> This is the ruling of Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur, unlike the Geonim, who require the wrapping of the Yishmaelim, which is a complete wrapping. Beis Yosef explains that the Geonim infer this from the berachah 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis,' whereas Baal HaIttur holds that 'to wrap oneself' means covering; see there. Nevertheless, to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis in the most choice manner, it is proper at the time of the berachah to wrap oneself like the Yishmaelim and remain so for the time required to walk four amos, as stated in the next se'if. This satisfies even the Geonim, because the essential requirement concerns the initial donning; afterward, he could remove it if he wished. Magen Avraham 5 in the name of Terumas HaDeshen; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, ibid. Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, chapter 1, states: 'The Geonim required wrapping in the manner of the Yishmaelim. One brings the two tzitzis on the right side over to the left, so that all four tzitzis join behind him on his left side and hang behind him there. Rabbeinu Saadiah also explained this wrapping in his Arabic commentary to Parashas Bo. It is good and proper to do so, and one should remain wrapped this way at least for the time required to walk four amos when wrapping himself in tzitzis. He may then remove the wrapping from his neck. This was my master's custom.' Solel Boneh 6 writes likewise in the name of the Arizal's writings, as do Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Machazik Berachah 4; Siddur Beis Yaakov 25; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32. Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, however, writes that when a person wraps himself in a tallis, he should grasp the tallis's right side with its two tzitzis and throw them leftward around his throat, over his left shoulder toward the back. The other two tzitzis, on the left, should remain hanging in front of him, not behind. Matzas Shimurim, page 24; Chesed LaAlafim; Midrash Talpiyos, page 170c; and Yaavetz's Amudei Shamayim, page 59b, write likewise; Yafeh LaLev 1:4 cites them. Imrei Lev already posed this difficulty in Kuntres Sefas Emes 45 and writes: Both teachings are correct. The order of wrapping should be as follows. First, he should throw the two right-hand tzitzis to the left and leave the two left-hand tzitzis in front of him, intending to give the chasadim and gevuros to Rachel and the chochmah and binah to Yaakov. After this intention, he should also throw the two left-hand tzitzis to the left, so that all four join there, as Rabbeinu Saadiah writes; this nullifies the force of the evil inclination. Machazik Berachah 4 writes likewise. Ben Ish Chai cites this in the aforementioned paragraph and writes that it is stated explicitly in the siddur of Rashash. Ben Ish Chai adds that even though we do not have these intentions, after throwing the two right-hand tzitzis behind him to the left, one should wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then throw the two left-hand tzitzis behind him to the left and again wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then lower the tallis wrapped around his neck over his entire body, thereby carrying out the instruction expressed by this act; see there. This satisfies all opinions, including the works that cite only Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, because he waits the time required to walk four amos before throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind him. Nothing further can be questioned. The custom of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, is that after also throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind themselves to the left, they strike all four tzitzis twice against the left side of the nape, corresponding to the two strikes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 2, and in Rashash's siddur.

<b>12) Magen Avraham further writes there</b> that Kiddushin 29b implies that an unmarried man did not cover his head with a tallis, even if he was a talmid chacham. It is particularly difficult in light of our Sages' teaching in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillas HaShachar, derushim 2–3, that wrapping in tzitzis and reciting the berachah effects a rectification in the supernal worlds. How could a person exempt himself from this? Even apart from that, Magen Avraham's words are not conclusive. The passage there does not discuss a tallis used for a mitzvah, but a rabbinic headcloth, as Be'er Heitev writes at the end of siman 17. Bach in this siman and Mor U'Ketziah likewise imply this.

<b>13) [Se'if 3] Small talleisim, etc.</b> Ateres Zekeinim writes: The shoulder sections should be somewhat wide, and the garment somewhat large, so that it can cover one's head and the majority of his body from behind; otherwise it is not called a garment. Terumas HaDeshen, cited by Beis Yosef, writes: One who wears a tallis with a neck opening should make it large enough to cover his head and the majority of his body from behind. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>16) There: He should remain wrapped this way, etc.</b> This applies specifically to a garment made as a single square piece with the neck opening in the center. When he wraps its length around his head and its width around his body, two tzitzis are on one side and two on the other. If the garment is sewn, however, then when he wraps its length around his head and its width around his body, all four tzitzis will come to one side, which is improper. He should therefore put it on in its ordinary manner, not wrap it over his head at all, and not recite 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis,' but rather 'concerning the mitzvah of tzitzis.' Shulchan Aruch HaRav 7.

<b>17) There: Afterward he should draw it forward over his head and wear it.</b> The custom developed to make an atarah from a piece of silk as a marker, so that the tzitzis that were in front remain forever in front, just as a board that merited being placed on the north side of the Mishkan remained forever on the north.
