<b>(ס"ק ב) מעומד.</b> פי' הברכה והעטיפה דבטור כ' וז"ל ומיד כו' יתעטף בציצית מעומד. הרי שכתב שהעטיפה יהיה מעומד. וגם בירושלמי יליף העטיפה בגז"ש דבעי מעומד דציצי' כתיב לכם ובעומר כתיב לכם ובעומר כתיב בקמה ודרשינן בקומה ומיניה יליף הירושלמי דגם ברכת כל המצו' בעי בעמידה. ולכם גבי עטיפ' כתיב דהא ברכה דרבנן א"כ ע"כ ילפי' דהעטיפה צ"ל מעומד. וכיון דהירושלמי יליף מיניה כל ברכת המצות. ע"כ הברכה דין המצוה יש לה ולכן צריך שיהיה העטיפה וגם הברכה מעומד. ועיין ב"ח וס' א"ר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 2, standing.</b> Meaning, the blessing and the wrapping. For the Tur wrote: immediately, etc., he should wrap himself in tzitzis while standing. Thus he wrote that the wrapping should be standing. The Yerushalmi also derives the wrapping by a gezeirah shavah that it must be standing, for by tzitzis it says “for you,” and by the Omer it says “for you,” and by the Omer it says “standing grain,” which we expound as standing upright. From this the Yerushalmi learns that all blessings over mitzvos also require standing. Since “for you” is written regarding the wrapping, and the blessing is D'Rabbanan, necessarily we learn that the wrapping must be standing. Since the Yerushalmi learns from it all blessings over mitzvos, the blessing has the law of the mitzvah, and therefore both the wrapping and the blessing must be standing. See Bach and Eliyah Rabbah.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 4, and he should stand, etc.</b> Specifically wrapping. For we bless “to wrap,” and regarding a mourner who needs wrapping, the Shas concludes that it is like the wrapping of Yishmaelites, namely below the mouth. But the Bach wrote that this applies specifically to a mourner, for we learn from Yechezkel, where the Holy One, blessed is He, told him when his wife died, “do not cover over the lip,” and we learn from this that another mourner does require covering of the lip. But regarding tzitzis, necessarily what we bless “to wrap” does not mean Yishmaelite wrapping. For how can we say “Who commanded us to wrap”? Where were we commanded regarding wrapping? It is written only “with which you cover yourself.” Rather, necessarily, what we bless “to wrap” means to cover oneself. In order to satisfy all opinions, he should stand wrapped for the time it takes to walk four cubits, and this suffices, as written in seif katan 5.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 7, with tzitzis, etc.</b> According to grammar, saying it with a patach is not explained why it is so grammatically. The Levush wrote that some say it with a patach because it is the end of a matter, and one may say it with a patach as definite and separate from what precedes, unlike a sheva. He rejected this, because we find many times at the end of a verse vowelled with a sheva. On the contrary, one may say it with a sheva, for with a patach it implies the known tzitzis, namely techeles, and because of our sins we do not have techeles. Therefore it is better to say it with a sheva, meaning any tzitzis. But the Bach wrote the opposite, to say it with a patach in order to indicate the known one, namely techeles, according to what is written that techeles resembles the sea, the sea resembles the sky, and the sky resembles the Throne of Glory. He brought in the name of the Zohar that through remembrance of the Throne of Glory, where the place of judgment is, fear of Hashem will be upon his face. Since it is written “and you shall see it and remember,” it is proper to remember. Therefore, although we do not have techeles, nevertheless he should remember it in his blessing, and through this he will place the awe of judgment upon his heart. See there.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 16) If he removed, etc.</b> In the responsum of Maharch it does not imply this. These are the words of Maharch: if he spoke between the small tallis and the large one, it is an interruption and he must bless again. As for Maharam, who did not go with a small tallis to the synagogue, though afterward Maharam retracted, as explained in the Beit Yosef, but rather when he returned home from the synagogue he put on the small tallis and blessed over it, his reason appears to be that sometimes he did not need to speak between the small tallis and the large one, and he also was not permitted to speak without need in order to obligate himself in a blessing, since he could exempt himself with one blessing. He would then be uncertain whether to bless over the large tallis, if he had first blessed over the small tallis. Therefore he would wait until after the prayer, until he needed to speak in any case. The Terumas HaDeshen wrote about this: It would have seemed to say that specifically while he is still wearing one tallis, he is not obligated to bless over the second unless he diverted his attention between this one and that one. But if he removed this one and wants to wear another, he becomes obligated even if he did not divert his attention. However, the language of Maharch above does not imply this, for he wrote that he would wait until after the prayer, until he needed to divert his attention. This implies that if he had not diverted his attention, even though presumably he had already removed the large tallis when leaving the synagogue, nevertheless, if he did not divert his attention, we would not say that since he removed one tallis, its blessing has departed and the small tallis is not exempted through it. Thus, the matter depends on diversion of attention. This is not comparable to what is written in seif 14, that if he removed his tallis, even if he intended to return and wrap himself in it immediately, nevertheless according to the Mechaber there he must bless, even though he is wearing the first tallis. All the more so here, where he now wears another tallis after removing the first; this is how the text should read. And even though the Rama disagrees in seif 14 and holds there that he need not bless, nevertheless here, where he wears another tallis, he agrees that he should bless. So too wrote the Olas Tamid. About this the Magen Avraham wrote that it is not comparable, for there he did not have intent, etc.
