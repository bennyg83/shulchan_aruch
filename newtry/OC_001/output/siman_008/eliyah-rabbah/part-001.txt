[א] <b>יתעטף וכו'.</b> משמע שאין מברך קודם עטיפה אלא בשעת עטיפה קודם שיתעטף יפה כמו בתפילין סימן כ"ה סעיף ח'. ובתשובת בית יעקב סימן פ"ח כתב דהעולם נוהגין לברך קודם שיתעטף, ומכל מקום משמע שם דאין לעשות כן אלא כדאמרן. גם בתפילין כתב הב"ח דלא כמו שנוהגין העולם. כתב הלבוש מברכין בציצית בשוא תחת הבי"ת אבל הב"ח בסוף סימן כ"ד כתב בפת"ח תחת הבי"ת. וכן בספר ארחות חיים כתב מורי ורבי רש"ם ז"ל האריך בדברים של טעם שיש לקרות בפת"ח. וכן נראה כי רוב ברכות מצינו בלשון ידוע כמו על המילה. נותן התורה, עד כאן. ולא הרגיש דהכא לא שייך לשון ידוע כיון שאין לנו תכלת כמו שכתב הלבוש. גם שיירי כנסת הגדולה כתב מנהג מדקדקים כלבוש. וכן מנהגי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should wrap himself, etc.</b> This implies that one does not bless before wrapping, but at the time of wrapping, before he has wrapped himself fully, as with tefillin in siman 25, seif 8. In Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 88, he wrote that the common practice is to bless before wrapping; nevertheless, it implies there that one should not do so, but rather as we have said. Also regarding tefillin, the Bach wrote not like the common practice. The Levush wrote that one recites the blessing ‘betzitzis’ with a sheva under the beis, but the Bach at the end of siman 24 wrote with a patach under the beis. So too, in Sefer Orchos Chaim my teacher and master Rasham, of blessed memory, wrote at length with reasoned arguments that it should be read with a patach. This also seems correct, for we find most blessings phrased in the definite form, such as ‘upon the milah,’ and ‘Who gives the Torah,’ end quote. But he did not note that here the definite form is not applicable, since we do not have techeiles, as the Levush wrote. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah also wrote that the practice of precise grammarians is like the Levush, and so is my practice.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And he should bless while standing.</b> Meaning, both the blessing and the wrapping. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov as follows: if he sat, he does not go back, and it is not comparable to keriah in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 340; so wrote Maharash in the Derishah, end quote. This requires study, for if by keriah, which is D’Rabbanan, he does not fulfill it, all the more so by tzitzis, which is according to the Torah. Furthermore, according to him, also by counting the Omer, lulav, shofar, and milah he would fulfill it after the fact, since all are derived from one gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ from the Omer. Yet there we say that one must bless standing, as by keriah in Yoreh Deah, as the Beis Yosef wrote there. Furthermore, Abudraham, folio 19, wrote as follows: there are other mitzvos over which one blesses while standing for the preferred fulfillment, but if he did not bless while standing there is no concern, such as Hallel, Megillah, and sukkah, end quote. This implies that with those other mitzvos mentioned, even after the fact he did not fulfill it. It is difficult to say that ‘there is no concern’ means that even so he fulfilled the mitzvah properly, but in truth after the fact he fulfills all of them. It is also possible that Maharash’s view is that only by keriah, if he is not standing at the time of tearing, the mitzvah has passed, and the same applies to counting, shofar, lulav, and milah. But by tzitzis, even if he is not standing at the time of wrapping, nevertheless as long as he is wearing it the mitzvah is upon him; if he then stands a little afterward he fulfills it. This requires study. Olas Tamid wrote that it is proper to be stringent and stand for the time needed to walk four amos, as in seif 3 regarding wrapping in a tallis katan, end quote. According to what I wrote, one need not stand specifically that long at the time of wrapping. However, in Shulchan Aruch of Maharai Luria he wrote that one’s head must be covered with the tallis for the time needed to walk four amos after the blessing, like the wrapping of the Yishmaelim, end quote. This is what people practice, wrapping their heads in the tallis, winding the other end of the tallis around their throat and neck, and bringing all the tzitzis to one side. So wrote Sefer Divrei Chachamim. See below, siman 10, s.k. 12. He further wrote that when one wishes to wrap himself, he should take the sides of the tallis in his right hand and put on the right side first and afterward the left side. The reason for standing is derived through ‘lachem, lachem’ from counting the Omer. This is the language of the Levush: from here they learned that the same applies to all blessings over mitzvos, that they should be recited standing. It appears that with this he answers the Bach’s difficulty, for the Bach wondered why the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ is needed, since we learn in the Yerushalmi that all blessings are said standing. That is, the Bach’s difficulty is that since the Yerushalmi says so about all mitzvah blessings, it must not have learned it from the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem,’ for by other mitzvos ‘lachem’ is not written. Rather, the Yerushalmi holds by reasoning that all mitzvos are like avodah, which is performed standing, and the law of the blessing follows the law of the mitzvah. If so, the Bach properly asks why the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ is needed for tzitzis, as above. **** ENGLISH ****

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And the others should answer, etc.</b> Hagahos Ashri at the end of chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah wrote that if one person wraps himself in tzitzis, his fellow may bless for him. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 140, he wrote that one who ties tefillin onto his fellow’s head presumably should not bless, but rather the wearer should bless, end quote. One must say that Hagahos Ashri speaks where the one wrapping himself is not expert in the blessing, end quote, language of the Magen Avraham. This is not correct, for Hagahos Ashri wrote afterward, ‘and if he himself does not know, etc.,’ implying that the opening clause speaks even where he does know. There is no difficulty at all from Terumas HaDeshen, for in truth, presumably the one performing the mitzvah blesses; Hagahos Ashri merely says that his fellow can bless, and this is clear.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He should examine the strings, etc.</b> One must be careful to check also above at the corner, where it is invalid even if one string snapped, and likewise in the braided section. So wrote the Taz from the Shelah, unlike Magen Avraham. The Bach wrote: if one checked his tallis, left it in a known place, and found it as he left it, he need not check it, as the Rosh ruled regarding a slaughtering knife in Yoreh Deah siman 18, end quote. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote about this that he saw meticulous people who, even though they place the tallis in their pouch, check it every time they put it on, perhaps because some incident occurred and it was taken from the pouch without their knowledge, end quote. This reason is remote. It appears to me rather that one can reject the Bach’s proof, for a knife is checked after slaughter, and therefore we rely on it when he left it in a known place. Furthermore, the Rama rules there in seif 14 that even by a knife, lechatchilah it is forbidden without checking. However, in my Eliyah Rabbah I raised difficulty against the Rama. It is also possible to distinguish. Nevertheless, one may rely on this when checking it would prevent him from praying with the congregation. One may also say that one who left the synagogue, removed his tallis, and returns and puts it on with a blessing need not check it, since he already checked it that day and left it with the status of checked; this is also implied in Magen Avraham, siman 13. The Taz wrote in siman 13 that if one was called to the Torah and wants to put on a tallis, one should not check at that time because of burdening the congregation, as we find in Yoma that he reads by heart because of the honor of the congregation, end quote. But I saw in Mateh Moshe, siman 16, that he wrote that he saw Maharash check each time he was called to the Torah before wrapping himself. His proof from Yoma can also be rejected, for this does not burden the congregation as much as rolling the scroll in public, and the Beis Yosef wrote similarly in siman 144, especially according to the Bach, who wrote that nowadays in any case the congregation is delayed. The Taz further wrote in this siman that they were stringent to check specifically because of a blessing in vain, so that he not transgress ‘You shall not bear,’ but not because he is wearing it without tzitzis. According to this, one who wears many tzitzis garments need check only the first one over which he blesses. But from the Bach it is clear that the reason is also because he wears it without tzitzis, and this is likewise implied in the Levush, siman 13, seif 2. There is a little proof from Maharash, for one may say that Mateh Moshe testified that he checked for the Torah reading every time, even though Maharash holds that one need not bless, as I will write below in siman 14 regarding a borrowed tallis. It is strained to say that it speaks where the tallis was not borrowed, for from his plain language and the phrase ‘every time’ it implies that he always checked. Furthermore, he was presumably always wearing tzitzis. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 84, wrote that if one is on the road and has no other tallis, he may avert his eyes and bless without checking.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He must bless, etc.</b> The Bach’s view is that even if other garments were before him and he had in mind at the time of the blessing to wear them, he must bless, since his mind was not on this one that was brought to him. This is not comparable to fruits in siman 206 or slaughter in Yoreh Deah siman 19, for eating and slaughter have a fixed framework, unlike dressing, which has no fixed framework. But the Taz and Olas Tamid challenged him, saying that slaughter and dressing are the same, and he need not bless. This is difficult, for if so, even where it was not before him, if they bring him more of the same kind he should also not need to bless, as the Rama ruled in Yoreh Deah there. Although the Taz wrote there about the Rama that the Beis Yosef wrote that the Ran, Raavad, and Rambam hold like the Rosh, so that the Rama’s words are a lone view against them, end quote, I already wrote in Eliyah Rabbah that with respect, he erred in this, for the Beis Yosef did not write so. Rather, in slaughter there is a law of changed intention, as he brought proof there that it is not comparable to withdrawal from a meal, where one does not bless again; see there. But regarding the law of siman 1 he did not write at all. See there, where I concluded in accordance with the Rama. Even aside from this it is difficult, for the Rama should have ruled so here as well. Rather, the main ruling is like the Bach, and his reason may be that in slaughter, as long as something is before him, he fixes his intention that more may be brought for him to slaughter; this is not so by tzitzis, where with one garment he has fulfilled the mitzvah. This is further implied by the Bach and Perishah to Yoreh Deah siman 13. Sefer Magen Avraham wrote that lechatchilah one should not interrupt, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He must go back, etc.</b> Some disagree with this, and that is reasonable if at the time of the blessing his intent was also on this one and it was lying before him at the time of the blessing, as the Levush wrote. In Sefer Migdal David he wrote at great length to challenge the Levush, asking how he ruled against Terumas HaDeshen, siman 45, who ruled that one must bless, end quote. I am astonished, for on the contrary, one who examines Terumas HaDeshen there will see that he rules like the Levush; he only wrote that by reasoning it would seem that one blesses, but since we find that the Or Zarua ruled that one does not bless, we hold that one does not bless. He also wrote at length to wonder that the Levush contradicts himself, for he ruled in seif 14 that if one wore a tallis and removed it, even if he did not divert his mind, he must bless, end quote. Nachalas Tzvi likewise compares the two cases. In my humble opinion there is room to distinguish: there he already fulfilled the mitzvah with this tallis, and once he removed it, the mitzvah left him. Here, however, his intent at the time of the blessing was also on this one, and he has not yet worn it and has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Even though he removed one tallis, nevertheless with this second one his intention on which he blessed has not yet been fulfilled. Furthermore, it is explained in the Levush in seif 14, when he writes, ‘all the more so we should say that by removing this very garment its mitzvah has left him, etc.,’ that he holds there is a distinction, and removing the same garment is a greater diversion of mind than diversion from another garment. In Eliyahu Zuta I brought further proofs for the Levush that he does not go back and bless again, and so ruled Sefer Magen Avraham.

<b>For walking from his house, etc.</b> So the Beis Yosef explained the Tur’s words, where he wrote that one who usually wraps himself in a tallis gadol in the synagogue must bless over it even though he already blessed on the tallis katan he wore, end quote, because one may say that walking from his house to the synagogue is considered an interruption. The Taz wrote at length to disagree with this ruling, as did Olas Tamid, but in my humble opinion the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush are primary; therefore I must elaborate somewhat. The Taz challenged the Beis Yosef, saying that the reason the Tur requires a blessing is that he holds, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 25, seif 2, that one places tefillin between the tallis katan and the tallis gadol, and that is the interruption, not the walking. This is not convincing, for if so the Tur should have distinguished between Shabbos and Yom Tov, when tefillin are not placed, and a weekday, when they are placed; since he wrote without qualification, it implies that one must always bless. Furthermore, from the Tur’s language at the beginning of siman 25, ‘after he has wrapped himself in tzitzis he should immediately put on tefillin so that they be present for him at the time of Shema and prayer,’ it implies that he holds that one wraps himself even in the tallis gadol before placing tefillin. Otherwise he should not have written generally, but should have explained. This also follows from the reason he wrote, ‘so that they be present for him,’ etc.; and the phrase ‘wraps himself’ implies a tallis gadol. So too it is implied in the language of the Rosh that the Beis Yosef brought in siman 28: one who is wearing a tallis katan and, when he prays, wraps himself, must bless, end quote. This implies that the phrase ‘wraps himself’ refers to a tallis gadol. The Taz and Olas Tamid also wrote that this is explicit from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 105, that walking is not an interruption at all, but only speech about another matter, end quote; Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 16, likewise wrote at length. In my humble opinion this can be rejected, for Terumas HaDeshen does not mention that it speaks of one who usually wraps in the synagogue; rather, it speaks of one who wraps at home. Possibly the same applies where he removes it at home when leaving the synagogue; see there and examine carefully. It also appears to me to be a proof from what we say below, siman 178, that a change of place requires a blessing according to all opinions for matters that do not require a blessing in their place, and the law of a tallis is comparable to them. I also found that Sefer Birkat Avraham, part 4, ruled explicitly and simply that walking to the synagogue is an interruption; it implies there that the reason is that walking and change of domain are an interruption. With this, the Taz’s difficulty from siman 104, seif 13, that walking is not an interruption, is answered, for there the case is different since there is no change of place. His difficulty from Tosafos, Sotah folio 39, can also be answered. Tosafos derive the measure of immediacy between washing the hands and the blessing from the immediacy of leaning and slaughter, which was not possible by a metzora because he could not enter Shaar Nikanor, and from there to the altar was twenty-two amos. The Taz asked how they know that delay is an interruption; perhaps walking is the interruption. One can answer that it follows the view that partial entry is not considered entry, and he inserts his hands within the gate and leans; from the gate to the altar there is no change of domain. Although now it may be that the place of the hand, where the animal is, is less than twenty-two amos, for the twenty-two amos begin from the gate, one can say that they were not precise about such a small amount. See below. With this I am satisfied regarding the Magen Avraham’s difficulty in siman 166 on Tosafos, from Zevachim folio 33, which proves that going from place to place is considered an interruption even with a small amount of walking. It is not difficult at all, for there it is a change of place, while Tosafos speak of walking without a change of place, such as when he inserts his hands; therefore twenty-two amos are required. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 16, pressed to answer the Magen Avraham’s difficulty, but it does not stand for one who examines it. He also wrote at length to answer the Taz’s difficulty by saying that delay can be worse than walking, as the Rama wrote in siman 90 regarding one standing in prayer when a child urinated, that it is better for him to go elsewhere and not be silent, lest he pause long enough to complete the entire prayer and have to return to the beginning. If so, Tosafos derived delay from walking by a kal vachomer. But this is not precise, for if so, delay alone should also be an interruption by tzitzis, and the Taz already proved that delay does not invalidate by tzitzis or by any mitzvah blessing. There, by prayer, the law of pausing long enough to finish the whole prayer is different. Furthermore, there it refers to walking four amos to distance himself from the urine. According to this, if one goes to another room and returns to the first room and wraps himself, he does not go back and bless if he did not interrupt. Sefer Magen Avraham does not imply this, but what appears in my humble opinion I have written. One can also answer Olas Tamid’s difficulty from walking during Shema, which is not an interruption, because that is the law there, as it is written, ‘and when you walk on the way.’ Similarly, Sefer Birkat Avraham wrote there, folio 155. Furthermore, one who eats while walking on the road, according to all opinions, is not interrupted, because that is not a fixed setting, as below in siman 178, seif 4. There are many other distinctions between our law here and their proofs, and one should not elaborate further.

<b>And so is the practice, etc.</b> The Levush wrote: I am astonished, for nearby he wrote that if one removed the first garment before he wore the second, he must go back and bless; this shows that by removing the garment the mitzvah leaves him, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that this is not difficult at all, for there is different because no tallis katan remained upon him and the mitzvah left him entirely. Here, however, since something of the mitzvah still remains upon him, he does not go back and bless, end quote. In my humble opinion this is rejected by what the Levush wrote: if so, also when he blessed on one, etc.; it is easy to understand. But there is a difficulty on the Levush, why he omitted the ‘some say’ brought by the Rama, that one does not bless at all even if he has no tallis katan on him. It is even more surprising that below in siman 25, seif 12, by tefillin, he ruled that one need not go back and bless. A similar difficulty applies to the Rama, who distinguishes here between whether he has a tallis katan on him or not, while below he ruled that he need not bless. Because of this difficulty the Taz ruled that one should not bless at all, even if he has no tallis katan on him. Lechem Chamudos likewise emends the Rama’s words, that the phrase ‘and so is the practice’ should refer to not blessing at all. But Nachalas Tzvi and Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah ruled like the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, that one always goes back and blesses. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that it appears that if his intent is to return and wear it immediately, he need not bless even if he has no tallis katan on him; but if he does not intend to return and wear it, he always must bless. When he removed it without specification, he holds that there is a distinction between whether he has a tallis katan on him or not. This also appears to be the view of the Bach and is implied by Magen Avraham. The same law applies to tefillin.
