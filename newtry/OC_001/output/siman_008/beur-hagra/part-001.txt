<b>סעי' א יתעטף. </b>ר"ל דהעיטוף ג"כ מעומד כמ"ש בסמ"ק סי' צ"ב דתקיעה וציצית ילפינן מג"ש לכם לכם מעומר דכתיב בקמה ואמרינן אל תקרי בקמה כו' כמ"ש ברא"ש בסוף ע"פ וסימן עצ"ת ה' לעולם תעמוד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 1, "he should wrap."</b> Meaning that the wrapping too is done standing, as written in Semak, siman 92, that shofar and tzitzis are derived by the verbal analogy "for you" and "for you" from the Omer, where it is written "standing grain," and we say: read not "standing grain" but "standing," etc., as written in the Rosh at the end of Arvei Pesachim; and the mnemonic is: for counsel, always stand.

<b>ויברך. </b>נראה ליתן טעם למה בקצת מצות אין מברכין. א' כל מצוה שאין בה מעשה כגון השמטת כספים. ב' שאינה תלויה כולה ביד עושה כגון צדקה שמא לא יקבלנו וכן משפט שמא לא יקבלו הבעלי דינים וכיוצא. ג' מצוה הבאה ע"י עבירה כגון השבת גזילה שלא צוונו לגזול כדי להשיב. וכן על מיתות ב"ד שה' חס על בריותיו ואינו חפץ כו' וכמ"ש אין מברכין על הפורעניות ומה"ט אין מברכין על מים אחרונים ומתנות כהונה דלאו איהו קיהיב דמשולחן גבוה קזכו ועל חלה וכיוצא מברכין על הפרשתה ובמה שכתבתי לעיל שמא לא יקבלו כולל מצות הרבה כגון כיבוד אב ואם דאם מחלו מחול ומיעקרא וכמ"ש בכתובות מ' ע"א היכא אמרינן כו' וכן מצוה הבאה בעבירה כמ"ש ברפ"ט דב"ק וע' באבודרהם ה' ברכות ובתשובת הרמב"ן סי' קפ"ט בשם ראב"ד ורי"פ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"And recite the blessing."</b> It seems proper to give the reason why no blessing is recited on some mitzvos: 1. any mitzvah that has no act, such as remission of debts; 2. one not entirely dependent on the doer, such as charity, lest the recipient not accept it, and judgment, lest the litigants not accept, and the like; 3. a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, such as returning stolen property, for we were not commanded to steal in order to return it; and similarly court-imposed death penalties, for God has pity on His creatures and does not desire, etc., as they said: one does not bless over misfortunes. For this reason one does not bless over mayim acharonim or priestly gifts, for he is not the giver; they receive from the table of the Most High. But on challah and the like one blesses over its separation. What I wrote above, "lest they not accept," includes many mitzvos, such as honoring father and mother, for if they waive it, it is waived and uprooted, as stated in Kesubos 40a: where do we say, etc.; and likewise a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as stated at the beginning of Bava Kamma chapter 9. See Abudarham, Laws of Blessings, and the responsum of the Ramban, siman 189, in the name of the Raavad and RIF.

<b>מעומד. </b>כ' בא"ח בשם הירושלמי כל הברכות מעומד ופי' הוא דבברכח המצות קאמר דא"א לפרש על ברכת הנהנין דהא אמרינן ישבו כ"א מברך לעצמו הסיבו כו' וכן דאמרינן כיצד סדר הסיבה כו' וכן בבהמ"ז וכהנה רבות. ועמ"א שהקשה ממתניתין דחלה האשה היושבת כו' והתם משום הברכה ועיי' רא"ש פ"ד דסוכה סי' ג' אבל הר"י לא כתב אלא עטיפתו מעומד וקודם לכן בעניין הברכה לא כ' מעומד וכן הטור ודברי מ"א שכתב שחלה אינה מצוה דבריו אין להם שחר והלא אסור לעשות עיסתו קבין ואמרינן בפ"ג דפסחים מאי דעתך לחומרא חומרא דאתי לידי קולא כו':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"Standing."</b> Orchos Chaim wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: all blessings are recited standing. He explained that this refers to blessings over mitzvos, for it cannot refer to blessings over enjoyment, since we say: if they sat, each blesses for himself; if they reclined, etc., and likewise: what is the order of reclining, etc.; and likewise Grace after Meals and many similar cases. See Magen Avraham, who objected from the Mishnah in Challah: a woman who is sitting, etc.; there it is because of the blessing. See Rosh, Sukkah chapter 4, siman 3. But Rabbenu Yonah wrote only that the wrapping is standing; earlier, regarding the blessing, he did not write "standing," and neither did the Tur. The Magen Avraham's statement that challah is not a mitzvah has no basis, for it is forbidden to make one's dough in small kav measures, and we say in Pesachim chapter 3: what are you thinking, to be stringent? It is a stringency that leads to a leniency, etc.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>"And it is proper," etc.</b> This is the Tur's language: he covers his head so that he not be bareheaded. This is from the Baal HaIttur. The Beis Yosef wrote that it apparently does not mean completely bareheaded, for that is unrelated to tzitzis; moreover, how did he go until now to relieve himself and do other things? Rather, even though his head is covered, modest people place a scarf or tallis over their heads, etc.; perhaps this means that his head should not be uncovered by the tzitzis, etc. This is what he wrote: "and it is proper," etc. But his explanation is forced, for that is not called bareheaded. We find many places in the Talmud where it was their way to cover the head with a scarf: Kiddushin 8a, Berakhot 60b, Arvei Pesachim, Shabbos chapters 16 and 8, Moed Katan chapter 3, not with a hat and scarf; likewise Eruvin chapter 8. According to the Rif's reading there in Berakhot, the tzitzis blessing comes after "Who clothes the naked," and finally, "when he spreads a scarf over his head," etc. Also what he wrote, "how did he go," etc., implies that in his view it is legally forbidden to walk bareheaded. This is not so, since we say in Shabbos chapter 16, "may it come to me," etc.; and in Kiddushin chapter 1, Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua did not, etc.; and there, "how brazen is this man," etc.; perhaps he is from Mata Mechasya, etc. This shows that it is permitted, except before Torah scholars. So too in Maseches Derech Eretz with the two children, and there in Kiddushin regarding Rav Hamnuna. In Kiddushin one can perhaps explain like the Beis Yosef, because of modesty; also see Nedarim 30b. Likewise the end of Shabbos, "cover your head," is literal, as in Kiddushin 31a: Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua would not, etc., and like before Torah scholars. In Megillah chapter 3, "one who uncovers," etc., is explained in Maseches Sofrim as bareheaded. Likewise Kiddushin 8a is literal: another person need not have a scarf at all, as written in Shabbos chapter 8, Arvei Pesachim, and the Midrash Rabbah regarding Shema, cited by the Beis Yosef in siman 61: "I did not burden you," etc. Nevertheless the intent of the Baal HaIttur and Tur is that he should cover his head with the tallis, as written: the order of wrapping, etc., and the way, etc., and he returns, etc., and covers, etc., and blesses, etc. Because his wrapping is for prayer, he said to cover his head, as in siman 91, seif 3; see Magen Avraham there, small paragraph 2, and see there seif 5. Although the Tur already wrote in siman 2 to cover the head, there it is occasional and a measure of piety until he wraps; here he wrote it as law. Yet it is not strict law, for legally even for prayer and entering a synagogue everything is permitted, as written below; rather here everyone should be stringent, unlike siman 2, which is only for the modest, as in Kiddushin 8a. There any covering suffices until he covers his head; here, with a tallis or scarf. But see Zohar 3:122b: because she, etc., due to prayer and synagogue, and for ordinary people who do not have scarves. So it also appears from Shabbos 147a regarding a folded tallis; see Rashi there, and as written below, siman 301, seif 29, meaning after, etc.; and likewise Menachos, chapter HaTecheles: a tallis with which a minor covers, etc. See Zohar 3:122b. Also what Maseches Sofrim says, that some say it is forbidden to utter the Divine Name bareheaded, is also a measure of piety, for the Gemara in Berakhot 60b does not imply so, as written below siman 47, seif 1, and likewise from the above Midrash Rabbah, "I did not burden you," etc. In sum: there is no prohibition at all in being bareheaded, ever, except before important people; and at the time of prayer it is proper as a matter of conduct. For the rest of the day, it is for holy people who stand before God always.

<b>סעי' טז הלן כו'. </b>כן למד הרא"ש מעובדא דר' יהודה במנחות דמשני כר' כו' עט"ז שהקשה עליו וכן ב"ח. ולי נראה דראייתו מהא דס"ד דס"ל כרבנן ולרבנן לעולם אינו מברך אלא אחת כמ"ש בסוכה כל שבעה אינו מברך אלא יום ראשון ואעפ"כ בצפרא מברך אי לאו דס"ל דלילה זמן ציצית וכן בלולב אמר שם כיון דמפסקי לילות מימים מברך כל שבעה אף לרבנן ואף מאן דפליג אינו אלא משום שהוא מצות זקני' וה"ה לרבי אף בלא פשט דהא לרבנן הפשיטה אינה מעלה כלום:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 16, "one who slept," etc.</b> So the Rosh learned from the incident of Rabbi Yehudah in Menachos, where it answers that he followed Rabbi, etc. See Taz and Bach, who challenged him. To me it appears that his proof is from the initial assumption that he held like the Rabbis; according to the Rabbis, he never blesses more than once, as stated in Sukkah: all seven days he blesses only on the first day. Even so, in the morning he blesses, unless he holds that night is a time for tzitzis. Likewise regarding lulav it says there: since nights separate days, he blesses all seven even according to the Rabbis; and the one who disagrees does so only because it is a mitzvah of the elders. The same applies according to Rabbi even without removal, for according to the Rabbis removal accomplishes nothing.
