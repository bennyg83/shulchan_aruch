<b>ויש ליזהר וכו'. </b> ואם עבר והוציא' קודם שנאפית עד שאין חוטין נמשכין נראה דאפי' דיעבד אסור שנתחמצ' בההיא שעתא דהיתה חוץ לתנור דע"י חמימות נתחמצ' מיד וכ"כ במרדכי פ' א"ע דתשרף והמרדה ג"כ נתחמצה ואין לה תיקון כדלעיל סי' תנ"א סי"ט וכ' המרדכי פ' א"ע בשם ריב"א דבקרימת פנים הוי שיעורא דאין חוטין נמשכין אחריה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one should be careful, etc.</b> And if he transgressed and removed it before it was baked to the point that threads no longer trail, it appears that even after the fact it is forbidden, for it became chametz in that time that it was outside the oven, for through the heat it becomes chametz at once; and so wrote Mordechai, chapter "These are the ones who pass," that it should be burned, and the oven peel also became chametz and has no remedy, as above in siman 451 se'if 19; and Mordechai wrote in chapter "These are the ones who pass" in the name of Riva that crusting of the surface is the measure that threads no longer trail after it.

<b>אם בישלה כו' </b> לפי שנתבטל טעם מצה והא דאמרי' בסי' תע"ה ס"ב בלע מצה יצא דלא בעינן שיטעום טעם מצה היינו שאין צריך שיהי' טעם מצה בפה כמו מרור אבל היא עצמה צריכ' שיהא בה טעם מצה וע' סי' תע"ה. וכתב הרוקח דמצ' שרוי' דמותר היינו דוקא במים או יין הקרים ולא ברותחים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he cooked it, etc.</b> Because the taste of matzah was nullified; and as for what we say in siman 475 se'if 2, if he swallowed matzah he fulfilled, for we do not require that he taste the taste of matzah: that means he need not have the taste of matzah in the mouth as with maror, but the matzah itself must have the taste of matzah; and see siman 475. And Rokeach wrote that soaked matzah that is permitted means specifically in cold water or wine, and not in boiling liquids.

<b>אוסרים אותה תוך הפסח. </b> וכ' רש"ל בתשו' סי' נ"ז דשאר מצות שנגעו בזו אפי' בנשיכ' אינם נאסרות דאין איסור יוצא מחתיכ' לחתיכ' בלא רוטב וכי היכי דבשאר איסורים סגי בקליפה ה"נ לענין חמץ רק אם נגע במקום הכפול ממש אז אוסר בחם אפי' בלא רוטב:

<b>רק במקום הדבוק. </b> בת"ה כתוב דהשאר מותר אם יש במצה ס' נגד מקום הדיבוק אבל מדברי רמ"א משמע כאן אפי' בלא ס' מותר וכ"נ עיקר דהא בצלייה במידי דאין פעפוע סגי בנטילת מקום כמ"ש בי"ד סי' ק"ה בהג"ה דמידי דודאי אין מפעפע אוסר כדי נטילה ואפייה דינה כצלי כמבואר שם ע"כ סגי בנטילת מקום וכ"כ בת"ה סי' ל"ח דלשון כל המחברים משמע דוקא בדאיכא סרך משמנונית בעי' ס' ע"כ הכא ודאי לא צריך ס' אלא דבפסח שאוסרין כל המצה אינה אלא חומרא וכן נוהגין בכל המקומות שראיתי שאין מצריכין ס' נגד הדיבוק וכן ראיתי בתשו' ר"מ לובלין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only at the place of adhesion.</b> In Terumas HaDeshen it is written that the rest is permitted if there is in the matzah sixty opposite the place of adhesion; but from the words of Rama here it implies that even without sixty it is permitted; and so appears primary, for with roasting of something that has no seepage, removing the place suffices, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 in the gloss, that something that certainly does not seep forbids only the measure of removing the place; and baking has the law of roasting, as explained there; therefore removing the place suffices; and so wrote in Terumas HaDeshen siman 38 that the language of all the authors implies that only where there is a tinge of greasiness do we require sixty; therefore here we certainly do not need sixty; rather, that during Pesach they forbid the whole matzah is only a stringency; and so they practice in all the places I have seen, that they do not require sixty opposite the adhesion; and so I saw in Responsa R' Meir of Lublin.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>A puffed matzah, etc.</b> This is the language of Maharil: he differed with Maharash over Maharam of Augsburg, how he forbade a matzah that rose in baking; and Maharash said that he forbade improperly, for what is there to be concerned about if it puffed because of baking; and he showed Mahari Segal a baked matzah on which a crust had risen like a fistful, and he permitted it, and said: specifically if it had puffed in its middle because of baking, then it would be forbidden; but this crust is considered like all the crusts that rise on every thin [matzah]; end quote. And at first glance the meaning is that the thickness split in two and the upper side rose upward. And indeed the words of Maharash are correct: what chametz is there in baking? For if so we should be concerned in every baking that perhaps it became chametz before the baking finished. Therefore Maharil's reason must be that the fire does not reach that cavity; but it is difficult, for we see that it does reach there and lifted one side upward; and further, it is not the way of chametz to have a cavity beneath it; and further, if so even with a crust we should say the same, that the fire did not reach there, for who can decide this for us; and further, what distinguishes between its middle and a crust: we do not know the measure until when it is called a crust, and what measure a crust has. And the language implies that anything less than the measure of the middle would be permitted; and this is astonishing; and it would have been proper for Rama to rule like Maharash, especially in such a rabbinic matter; and further, he was Maharil's teacher. And what appears to my humble opinion is that Maharash and Maharil do not disagree: if we examine Maharil's language carefully, this puffing that Maharil mentioned means that the dough inside did not split in two with a cavity there, but rather became like a mountain through baking, in the way of chametz that leavens and rises upward, and this is visibly leavened; and in this Maharash agrees, for he permitted only what rises upward through baking with a cavity beneath it, and that is not the way of leavening, but rather the power of the fire does so; and now he properly distinguishes: if there is puffing up to the top, and that is the language "in its middle" that Maharil used, it is forbidden, for there is no cavity beneath it and it is certainly chametz; which is not so when there is a cavity there, whatever it may be: it is called a crust and is permitted. And this explanation is more comfortable: for according to the first explanation that the dough split, he should not have said "in its middle" but "puffed inside it," meaning there is a cavity inside it, and he should not have mentioned "puffed" at all, whose implication is that it rises upward; rather he should have forbidden even if above it is even, for the fire does not reach the cavity. And according to what I wrote it fits well: that what chametz rises upward is called "its middle," for so is the way of things that leaven, that they rise upward opposite their lower middle, like something round. <b>And I saw</b> that my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote the two explanations we mentioned, and he wrote that one should forbid in both; and to my humble opinion there is no prohibition except according to the second explanation we mentioned, but when there is a cavity there there is no reason to forbid. And in Responsa R' Meir of Lublin I saw that he gave a reason for a puffed [matzah] because it already became chametz before baking and afterward was revealed through the baking; and that is a remote concern and not at all plausible, and there is no proof for it; and the matter is simple that whenever a cavity formed, it came because at the time of rolling, which they call beren, he did not roll it properly until it would be truly one body; therefore they split by the power of the fire; and it is not the way of chametz to lift one part upward, only crusts; and in this Maharil explicitly permitted. And our eyes see every day in baking bread that when it sits in the oven more than the measure it needs, a split forms in it that they call Abend-Rinde, and this is not from leavening, for it is already baked; rather, remaining longer causes this; so too with this matzah that became thus because it remained more than its measure after its baking; and this matter is true and clear. And if it should enter the mind to explain according to the first explanation and forbid when there is a cavity beneath it from force of the language's meaning, I would say to you: according to our words no stumbling-block of issur will come from this, for Maharash explicitly permitted it, and he was the teacher of Mahari Segal; and in Responsa Rashal siman 79 he praises Maharash and brought that he was important and had the power to disagree with the Tur; and certainly in such a matter we can rely on him to permit it. And in truth I never saw my father-in-law, of blessed memory, in all the days I was with him, forbid when the matzah split and there is a cavity there; rather he always said this comes because it was not rolled properly; and one who wishes to be stringent can at least be lenient with what was made before Pesach, as appears to my humble opinion.
