**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One fulfills with it.</b> But when threads trail from it one does not fulfill with it the obligation of matzah; it implies that everywhere it is permitted to eat and there is no concern of leavening; and so is the implication of the language of the Tur and Rambam and other poskim; however from the words of the Rav in the gloss it implies there is a concern of leavening so long as it was not baked until threads no longer trail from it. (Even though from the words of Maharil himself there is no decisive proof to explain thus, as Bach wrote.) And so wrote Mordechai, chapter "These are the ones who pass," that one should burn them at once; and so the world practiced to forbid the matzah when threads trail from it; and if one cannot determine whether threads trail from it or not, he wrote in Responsa She'eris Yosef siman 48 that one should forbid out of doubt; see there. And even though by majority our matzos that come before us have crusted surfaces on every side, and then one need no longer be concerned for leavening, as Tosafos wrote in Menachos folio 57b, and so it implies in the Gemara there folio 78b: nevertheless one can say that "its surface crusted" is only called thus when it was baked so much that threads no longer trail from it, as Mordechai wrote in chapter "These are the ones who pass," and so it implies from Rashi's language in chapter "Every hour"; and see above at the end of siman 454. And nevertheless if one inserts a finger into the matzah and dough does not stick to it, it appears to me that we are no longer stringent, for in such a case there is certainly no room to doubt threads trailing from it.
