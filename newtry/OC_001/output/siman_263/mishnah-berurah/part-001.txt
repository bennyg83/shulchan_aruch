**** ENGLISH ****
<b>When she kindles.</b> Some authorities write that when there is a wedding on erev Shabbos and they delay there until after sunset, and the woman does not wish to accept Shabbos before the canopy lest she need do something else forbidden on Shabbos; she should therefore kindle the candle without a blessing before the canopy, and afterward in darkness she spreads her hands over the candles and blesses; or while still in bein hashmashos after the canopy she says to a non-Jew to kindle, for they did not decree shevut in bein hashmashos for mitzvah need, as explained above in siman 261, and she blesses. The later authorities questioned on this, for a blessing cannot be recited over a light that is already burning; and especially at that time kindling is already forbidden; how can she say “Who commanded us to kindle”; and regarding the alternative proposal that she say to a non-Jew to kindle; also difficult, for there is no agency for a non-Jew, and thus she is not kindling; how can she bless; and even even when one Jew instructs another Jew to kindle for him; per Derech HaChaim the one who gave the instruction does not recite the blessing; only the person who kindles does; all the more so here, the woman should not recite the blessing. The later authorities therefore propose a different solution: according to our ruling she can bless and stipulate in any case for need that she not accept Shabbos at kindling; here too, this is considered necessary: she kindles and blesses before going to the canopy and stipulates; and likewise a woman whose night of immersion falls on erev Shabbos; she kindles and blesses before going to the mikveh and stipulates she does not accept Shabbos until after washing and combing her hair.

And Magen Avraham wrote the reason: he follows his own view that an erroneous communal acceptance is considered acceptance; and thus the candle was designated for its mitzvah; and since it was designated for the owners it is designated for all; and also forbidden to touch, lest perhaps he use it. For the sake of a mitzvah one may be lenient [Derech HaChaim]. Peri Megadim wrote: one who kindled a Shabbos candle while it was still broad daylight and stipulated not to accept Shabbos; nevertheless the candle was designated for its mitzvah and forbidden to use it for weekday use; and even others are forbidden, because an item designated as muktzeh for its owners is forbidden to everyone.
