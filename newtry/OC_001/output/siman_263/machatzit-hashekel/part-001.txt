(1) (s.k. 1) <b>One should be careful, etc., that he goes there.</b> See se’if 6 whether he blesses on them; for it is like a bonfire. And he brought proof from what Rama wrote in siman 671 se’if 4, and these are his words there in siman 671: "And they should be careful when making the lights, even of wax, not to attach them together and light them, for it becomes like a bonfire; even for Shabbos and Yom Tov lights they should be careful not to do so"; end Rama's words; and Peri Megadim understood that Rama forbids even braided ones like chains. And on this Magen Avraham wrote that he did not look at Darkei Moshe there in siman 671, for from the books of Darkei Moshe his Hagahot in Shulchan Aruch flow, and from Sefer Darkei Moshe it is proven that Rama's intent is not so; Magen Avraham brought there, and these are Darkei Moshe's words: "When one lights a Shabbos light or Chanukah light or Yom Tov light and attaches the lights adjacent to one another until they warm each other and wax drips from the warmth and they also double and fall; they have not fulfilled the mitzvah"; until here Darkei Moshe's language; and Magen Avraham wrote there that from this it implies it is permitted to make twisted ones; and Magen Avraham brought proof, for on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is a mitzvah to make twisted ones. Afterwards he brought in the name of Mahariv that he forbids on Chanukah even twisted ones, for it is like a bonfire; see there; if so, even though on Chanukah it is forbidden even twisted ones; meaning specifically on Chanukah where a light is required similar to the miracle; but twisted ones are a bonfire and called an avukah; but regarding Shabbos where we require for light and joy; on the contrary twisted ones are preferable; but from Rama's language in Hagahah siman 671 where he concluded the reason that it becomes like a bonfire; it implies he retracted in Shulchan Aruch and ruled like Mahariv that twisted ones are also forbidden, and this is a general rule including Shabbos and Yom Tov with them there; it implies that on Shabbos and Yom Tov too he forbids twisted ones; and so it appears from Peri Megadim's reference to Rama's Hagahah siman 671 Mahariv. However I saw in Darkei Moshe siman 671 he first brought Mahariv's words that he forbids for it is like a bonfire, and wrote on this "and so I found chapter Bameh Madlikin" etc. "and attaches the lights adjacent" etc.; it implies he does not disagree with Mahariv:

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>As written in siman 158, regarding performing the blessing before the act, the Sages did not distinguish between cases.</b> In siman 158, se’if 1, it is written that one does not recite the blessing before washing, but after washing and before drying. This is because at times one’s hands are unclean, such as after leaving the privy, and one cannot recite the blessing before washing. Therefore, the Sages did not distinguish, and even when one’s hands are clean, one should not recite the blessing before washing. Tosafos similarly write in Pesachim that for this reason one does not recite the blessing over immersion before immersing, even though that would place the blessing before the act. This is because of the immersion of a convert: we rule that he does not become a Jew until he has undergone circumcision and immersion. He therefore cannot recite the blessing before immersing, since before immersion he is not yet a Jew, and how could he say, “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us,” when he is not yet commanded? Therefore, even a woman immersing from niddah recites the blessing after immersion, because the Sages did not distinguish between cases.
