<b>ומיהו בדיעבד כשר. </b> כי מה שיהיו הבתים שחורים אינו הל"מ א"כ לא הוה השחרות רק תיקון בעלמא וכשר ע"י עכו"ם כמו שאר תיקוני עור משא"כ ברצועות דהל"מ שיהיו שחורות הוה זה כמו טויה דציצית דפסול בעכו"ם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, after the fact they are valid.</b> The blackness of the batim is not a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. It is therefore merely an external improvement, and may be performed by a non-Jew like other leather finishing. The straps are different: their blackness is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai and is comparable to spinning tzitzis, which is invalid when performed by a non-Jew.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The section that encircles, etc.</b> This matter requires analysis.

Menachos relates that Abaye was standing before Rabbi Yosef when his tefillin strap tore. He asked whether he could tie it. Rabbi Yosef answered from “You shall bind them,” requiring a complete and attractive binding, or, according to the second explanation of Rashi, a complete strap rather than one joined by a knot.

Rav Acha asked Rav Ashi whether it could be sewn with the stitching turned inward. Rav Ashi answered: go see what the people do. Rashi explains that since the practice is not to sew it, one should not do so. Rabbeinu Tam permits it.

Rav Pappa initially said remnants of straps are valid, meaning that if the hanging portion below the knot tore and only a remnant remained, it was valid. The Gemara rejects this: remnants of techeiles are valid because tzitzis are appurtenances of a mitzvah, but tefillin straps are appurtenances of sanctity. The Gemara then asks what minimum measure the straps require and answers that they must extend to the middle finger.

Rashi understands this as referring to the head-tefillin straps: beyond the knot they must extend enough to reach from the thumb to the middle finger. Rosh rejects this and follows Aruch, who explains it as referring to the arm-tefillin strap, which must reach the middle finger. The Gemara does not explicitly state the measure for the head straps. Shimusha Rabba gives their measure: the right strap to the navel and the left to the chest.

Rosh writes concerning Rabbi Yosef's prohibition of tying that it appears to apply specifically where the strap encircling the head or upper arm tore, because there a complete binding is required and there may not be two knots. The straps hanging below are not part of the binding, and tying them is permitted.

Terumas HaDeshen cites this view, but then quotes Sefer HaTerumah, who writes that one must be stringent and have the right head strap reach the navel, the left reach the chest, and the arm strap reach the middle finger and be wound three times. If the strap tore and the stated measure does not remain, neither tying nor sewing helps.

This explicitly indicates that the prohibition applies not only within the knot itself, but throughout the required length established by the Geonim, for both the head and arm tefillin, unlike Rosh.

Terumas HaDeshen then writes that regarding the arm tefillin, the required length should mean the strap stretched straight to the finger without winding. The extra length needed merely for multiple windings around the arm need not be free of knot or sewing. He had ruled leniently several times regarding that extra length.

He did not permit even within the required length, but did permit beyond the binding itself in accordance with Rosh, because Rabbeinu Tam permits sewing even within the binding. The conclusion of Terumas HaDeshen contains an obvious scribal error. It should read that he permitted sewing beyond the knot in accordance with Rosh because Rabbeinu Tam permits even within the knot, but that it is difficult to ensure the stitching is properly hidden according to the various explanations, and therefore one should not be lenient within the required length.

According to this, even for the head tefillin one should not permit tying, or even sewing, anywhere within the required length, namely the right strap to the navel and the left to the chest.

Sefer HaTerumah evidently interprets “a complete binding” according to Rashi's second explanation, requiring an entire and unjoined strap.

The Shulchan Aruch therefore requires study. Regarding the arm tefillin, it invalidates tying and sewing until the strap reaches the finger, while regarding the head tefillin it mentions only the portion encircling the head. This follows neither Rosh, according to whom the encircling portion alone is critical for the arm as well, nor Sefer HaTerumah, according to whom the head straps must remain whole to the navel and chest.

Terumas HaDeshen's conclusion likewise appears to require the longer measure for the head tefillin, and Rambam at the end of chapter 2 makes no distinction between head and arm tefillin: within the required measure, tying and sewing do not help.

I am further surprised because Beit Yosef concludes that one should follow the stringent view where possible, and rely upon the lenient view only where otherwise the mitzvah would be entirely lost. This indicates that where possible one should be stringent even regarding the full required lengths of the head straps. Yet Shulchan Aruch here records no stringent opinion regarding the head straps and merely concludes that where no alternative exists one may rely upon Rabbeinu Tam concerning sewing anywhere. This requires study.
