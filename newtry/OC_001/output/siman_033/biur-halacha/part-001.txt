**** ENGLISH ****
<b>According to Rambam, etc.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote, “But the other poskim, etc.” See above, at the end of siman 32, in Beit Yosef, s.v. “And if the stitching was severed, etc.” At the end of his words he writes: “As a matter of practical halachah, where no alternative is available, one may rely upon the lenient opinion so as not to be deprived of the mitzvah of tefillin.”

This implies that in his view all the poskim he cited initially, namely Rosh, Sefer HaTerumah, and Semag, are lenient and disagree with Rambam, maintaining that where two stitches were severed the tefillin are valid in every case.

In truth, I did not initially understand this. Perhaps they too are stringent, and they mentioned three stitches because when three were severed, even repairing the stitching in that place does not help, and the section must be sewn anew. I later found in Maharshal's commentary to Semag that he indeed writes that their view is lenient.

It is possible that Beit Yosef reasoned that if they agreed with Rambam they should have mentioned some practical distinction between new and old tefillin, and between corresponding and noncorresponding tears. Furthermore, since Rosh, Aruch, and those who follow them explain the statements of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda in Menachos as not referring to stitching at all, from where would they derive stringency regarding two stitches?

Eliyah Rabbah likewise understands Beit Yosef this way.

At times Rambam is actually more lenient than they are. If three stitches were severed in new tefillin, Rambam validates them even without repair, whereas according to them they require restitching, whether new or old. It is clear that all of this is included in Beit Yosef's statement that where no alternative is available one may rely upon the lenient view.

Rama then writes: “Some say that with new tefillin they are invalid, etc.,” and the references cite Rashi, Tur, and Rosh. This does not mean that they themselves hold this view, for according to them there is no distinction in stitching between new and old tefillin, as explained above, since the Gemara's discussion did not concern stitching. Rather, Rama means that according to their earlier reading of the Gemara, which reverses Rambam's distinction between new and old, if one nevertheless interprets the statements of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda as referring to stitching, the practical ruling would be the reverse of Rambam's. I found this explanation in the later authorities, and it is straightforward.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To be concerned.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that one may rely upon the lenient view, etc. This follows Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Beit Yosef, cited above, who records it as practical halachah.

It is clear that even where three stitches were severed one may rely upon the lenient view in pressing circumstances, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, whether the tefillin are new or old. Since in any event no blessing will be recited over them, why should he entirely forgo the mitzvah of tefillin when there is a valid opinion that permits them?

I later found that Derech HaChayim likewise appears lenient in every case in pressing circumstances, although he writes that no blessing should be recited. It appears to me that where only two stitches were severed, one may rely in pressing circumstances upon the majority of poskim who disagree with Rambam and are lenient. On this basis I wrote the ruling inside.

However, the basic law concerning severed stitching requires great study. We rule that stitching with sinews is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. Rambam also implies that the squareness of the stitching is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, and some are even more stringent concerning this than concerning the squareness of the batim. See above, siman 32, seif 40, and Biur HaGra there.

Accordingly, since Shulchan Aruch rules above in siman 32 that if the tefillin were originally square and their squareness later became damaged they must be repaired and remain invalid until repaired, as implied there in seif 39 and Taz, why does Rambam validate new tefillin when three stitches were severed? Ordinarily, severing stitches also damages the squareness, since there are generally stitches on every side.

Perhaps the case is specifically where the three severed stitches were not all on one side, and Rambam maintains that the stitching is still considered square. If, however, the squareness of the stitching itself was damaged through severed sinews, such as where a corner of the stitching broke, perhaps even fewer than three severed stitches would invalidate it.

Yet if so, Rambam and the poskim should have distinguished this explicitly. Since they give no indication of such a distinction, it appears that they validate it in every case. This would imply that they do not accept the ruling above that tefillin whose squareness became damaged after being made square are invalid.

Perhaps this is also Rambam's intent when he writes, “So long as the face of the tablet remains intact.” The “tablet” means a square tablet, as Aruch explains and Rosh cites in Hilchos Tefillin Ketanos. The meaning would then be: so long as the squareness of the stitching remains intact, as we first suggested.

This explanation appears more precise than the interpretation that the phrase means only that the titura remains intact. What connection does the existence of the titura have to damage in the stitching? The entire matter requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it must be processed for its sake.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that if the leather was not processed for the sake of the mitzvah, subsequently blackening it for the sake of the mitzvah does not help, unlike Be'er Eisek.

I saw in Damesek Eliezer, seif katan 11, that he wishes to say that the Gra agrees with Be'er Eisek. In truth, it appears clear to me that this is not so. The Gra's intent is that since we see that the Torah requires the leather to be fully prepared, even to the extent of blackening it, then all the more so the essential preparation of the leather itself, namely its processing, must be done properly. This is not a formal a fortiori argument from a received law, but merely a clarification of the matter.

This is exceedingly precise in the Gra's wording, for he then writes, “This is what Magen Avraham wrote: ‘It appears to me, etc.’” His intent is to answer Peri Megadim's question upon Magen Avraham. According to Damesek Eliezer's interpretation, Magen Avraham's wording “processing and blackening” would be very difficult, because blackening alone would suffice.

I learned the reasoning I have written from Sefer HaTerumah, cited by Beit Yosef regarding blackening for the sake of the mitzvah. He writes that blackening is like the other finishing processes of the leather that take place after processing; see there.

Know further that in my humble opinion, according to Rambam, who maintains that processing for the sake of the mitzvah is only D'Rabbanan, even if he also agrees with Sefer HaTerumah that blackening must be for the sake of the mitzvah, that requirement too would be only D'Rabbanan.

Sefer HaTerumah's reason is that since the essential requirement of blackening is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, the blackening must consequently be done for the sake of the mitzvah, as Beit Yosef explains. Yet the essential requirement of processing is likewise a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, as Peri Megadim explains at the beginning of siman 32, and nevertheless processing for the sake of the mitzvah is only D'Rabbanan according to Rambam, as Peri Megadim discusses at length there. This appears correct.

<b>The straps must be black.</b> This applies both to the tefillin of the head and to those of the arm. Rambam there.

I am uncertain whether according to the Torah it is sufficient for each strap to be black only for its minimum required length, while the remainder is merely for preferred fulfillment and beauty, or whether, since the entire strap is one connected object, all of it must be black.

There is some proof for the latter from Menachos 35a. The Gemara cites the baraisa that permits straps of green and other colors and does not answer that the permission applies to the portion beyond the required length. Nor would the Gemara then have needed to ask whether the color was on the inner side. This proof can be rejected, because the Gemara may assume that the baraisa would not permit even D'Rabbanan coloring one side in two different colors, because it appears mottled. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 7. The matter requires study.

I also do not know whether the received law of blackness applies to the segment of strap inserted through the ma'abarta. Initially there is no question that it should be blackened, because the bayis may turn over from that point, as mentioned in the Gemara. After the fact, however, perhaps it is comparable to the inner side. See Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 14.

For the degree of blackness, see Baruch She'amar, who writes that the strap should be blackened once, then when it dries somewhat it should be blackened a second time, and then a third, until the leather is fully black like a raven.

It appears to me that this is only for preferred fulfillment. According to strict law, once the color is properly called black, it suffices. Accordingly, even if its appearance resembles dark blue, which is called “blue” in the vernacular, it is valid.

A proof is from Niddah 19a concerning the colors of blood. If it is black like ink it is impure, while if it is lighter, even black like blue, it is pure, as Rashi explains. This shows that dark blue is included within black. The same appears in Chullin 47b concerning a lung, and Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Shechitah, halachah 19, who says “black like blue.” Thus a blue appearance is included in the category of black.

Nevertheless, initially there is certainly a mitzvah to blacken it fully, as Baruch She'amar writes. See what I wrote above in siman 32, seif 3, concerning the blackness of ink, where actual black is required.

Know that what we wrote there resolves a question I saw raised by a great authority: why did Rambam not formulate the received law simply as requiring writing with something black? According to our explanation, this is clear. A general statement of “black” would have included a color merely dark blue. Examine this carefully.

<b>Invalid.</b> See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the later authorities reject Magen Avraham's proposed remedy.

Nevertheless, it appears to me that if a Jew blackened the straps the first time without thinking explicitly for the sake of the mitzvah, but also without contrary intent, we may possibly rely upon Magen Avraham and have him blacken them a second time explicitly for the sake of the mitzvah. This is a type of double doubt.

At first glance I thought of another remedy that would work according to all opinions even if a non-Jew blackened them initially. Their entire objection to Magen Avraham is that this would be ink placed over ink. One could first paint over the black with red, green, white, or a similar color, and that color would nullify the black, as Gittin 19a says that red dye over ink constitutes erasure. He could then paint black over the new color for the sake of the mitzvah. Ink over red is considered writing according to all opinions.

Although the Gemara there says, “Is it because we compare that we should act upon it?” this case is different, because it would again be a form of double doubt, since Sefer HaTerumah himself was not certain of the law, as explained above.

However, the matter still requires study. Perhaps only writing ink over written red nullifies the lower writing, because black writing is superior according to all opinions. In the case of black paint over red paint, perhaps the lower color remains primary and one who does this on Shabbos would not be liable for coloring. In our case as well, the upper black color might not be significant enough to nullify fully the color that was previously beneath it, leaving only the name “black” upon it. This resembles the discussion there concerning red over ink. The matter requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If it was severed, etc., and for the tefillin of the arm enough to encircle, etc.</b> Machatzis HaShekel writes that after sewing, one must first stretch the repaired strap straight from the upper arm to the finger, and only afterward return with the strap from below and wind it around the arm according to custom. In his view, the length reaching the finger must be free of any knot or repair.

In Shenos Chayim, Rav Shlomo Kluger writes that the world does not follow this practice and is correct not to do so; see there. Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, later authorities, siman 4, likewise disputes Machatzis HaShekel. The main concern is only that a sufficient total length remain for stretching and winding. Although after winding around the arm the repaired point remains above the finger, we are not concerned.

He is nevertheless uncertain whether the repair itself may lie directly upon the finger, since binding applies there as well. See his lengthy discussion.

In my humble opinion, initially it is good to sew that place with sinews and on the inner side so that it is not noticeable. Even if we take Machatzis HaShekel's stringency into account and regard that place as within the required length, it appears clear that we may rely here upon Rabbeinu Tam and the other early authorities who permit sewing.

Know that when some people reconnect the strap to itself without actual stitching, this is no better than tying and may perhaps be worse.

<b> אין התפירה והקשירה וכו'</b> -  ומ"מ יש נ"מ בין השל יד לשל ראש דבשל ראש אם ישאר כך ולא יחברנה לה הרצועה שנפסקה לא בקשירה ולא בתפירה בודאי אסור לו לברך על התפילין ולא נוכל להכשיר במה שנשאר גרדומין דהיינו הרצועה השנייה וכמו בציצית לעיל בסימן י"ב דהרי מסקינן במנחות ל"ה ע"ב דברצועות פסול גרדומין [ומ"מ מחויב להניח התפילין אם נאבד הרצועה ואין לו שום עצה דשמא הלכה כהשיטות שסוברים דהאי וכמה שיעורייהו אשל יד קאי עיין ב"י לעיל בסימן כ"ז אך באמת בזה יש לו עצה אחרת דיכול להתיר הקשר ולמשוך הרצועה לשני הצדדים ויוכל בשעת הדחק לצאת בזה אם ישאר שני טפחים לכאן ולכאן עי"ש בסימן כ"ז במ"ב ומ"מ נ"מ מכל זה לענין אם נפסקו שני הרצועות משני הצדדים ולא נשאר כ"א מה שמקיף הראש] ובשל יד אפילו אם לא יחבר כלל מותר לברך על התפילין וכדלעיל בסימן כ"ז ס"ח ולפ"ז בודאי מהני תפירה אפילו בחוטים ומצד חוץ או קשר אך כ"ז אם לא היה עושה הז' כריכות ולדידן דמנהגינו לעשות כריכות כבר כתבנו שטוב שיתפרנו בגידין ומצד פנים כדי לצאת בזה חשש של המחה"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The sewing and tying do not help, etc.</b> Nevertheless, there is a practical difference between the tefillin of the arm and those of the head.

Regarding the tefillin of the head, if the strap remains severed and he does not reconnect it by tying or sewing, he certainly may not recite a blessing over the tefillin. We cannot validate it on the basis of a remaining remnant, meaning the second strap, as we do for tzitzis above in siman 12, because Menachos 35b concludes that remnants of tefillin straps are invalid.

Nevertheless, he is obligated to put on the tefillin if the strap was lost and he has no alternative, for perhaps the halachah follows those who maintain that the phrase “and how much is their measure?” refers only to the tefillin of the arm. See Beit Yosef above in siman 27.

In truth, he has another remedy. He may untie the knot and draw the remaining strap equally to both sides. In pressing circumstances he may fulfill the mitzvah if two handbreadths remain on each side. See siman 27 and Mishnah Berurah there.

The practical difference would therefore be where both straps of the head tefillin were severed on both sides and only the portion encircling the head remains.

Regarding the tefillin of the arm, even if he does not reconnect the strap at all, he may still recite the blessing over the tefillin, as explained above in siman 27, seif 8. Accordingly, sewing with ordinary thread and on the outer side, or even tying, certainly helps.

All this is where he does not make the seven windings. According to our custom to make those windings, we have already written that it is preferable to sew the strap with sinews and on the inner side so as to satisfy Machatzis HaShekel's concern.
