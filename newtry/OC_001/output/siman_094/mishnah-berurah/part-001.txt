**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Opposite midday.</b> This wording is not precise; rather, it means approximately half an hour or an hour afterward, for then the sun has already shifted considerably from its place. All this is estimated at the time of the tekufah of Nisan or Tishrei, or one week before it. See the Biur Halacha, where I copied the wording of the Levush on this. See the later authorities, who all agreed to the words of the Levush, that in our countries, since we do not sit exactly west of Eretz Yisrael but also incline to its north, they should position the synagogue wall so that it inclines slightly toward the southeastern side of the world, for then it will stand directly opposite Eretz Yisrael, the Beis HaMikdash, and the Holy of Holies. So too, every country according to its location should position the synagogue according to its direction. In the sefer Lechem Chamudos he justified the common practice, but nevertheless it also appears from him that it is better to do as the Levush says. If one happens to be in a place where the wall is exactly opposite the east, he should turn his face toward the southeast; see there. However, if it happens that he is standing and praying on the southern side of the Aron Kodesh, he should not do so, for then he will appear as turning his back and not his face toward the Aron Kodesh; see there in Lechem Chamudos. The Peri Megadim wrote that in places where they make the wall exactly toward the east, it is good to make the rabbi’s place north of the Aron Kodesh, so that he can turn southward and become wise, for from the south he cannot turn further south, as above. In places where they make it as the Levush says, it is proper to seat the rabbi to the right of the ark, unless this will remove a dispute; then he should not be particular.

Nevertheless, if he wishes, he may pray a second time as a voluntary prayer if he is certain that he will direct his heart in his prayer, as written at the end of siman 107; and he need not introduce something new into it, for the fact that he is now praying standing is itself considered an innovation. So wrote the sefer Magen Gibborim. From the words of the Peri Megadim it seems that nowadays it is not worthwhile to pray a voluntary prayer.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that the members of the household will not disturb him.</b> This should be placed at the end, after the words "a corner," meaning that he should be concerned only to find a corner where they will not disturb him, but should not be concerned for the idols, since all our prayers are in towns full of idols. If idols are hanging on the eastern wall, he should not pray toward that side, but toward another side, even though it is not east. If he cannot leave the road and must pray while walking or sitting, and he also knows that in the inn he will not find a corner where they will not disturb him, it is better to pray on the road sitting or walking, since then he can have more intent. Nevertheless, if he can pray at the inn in an open field, courtyard, or garden, that is better, and he fulfills both: he prays standing and with intent.
