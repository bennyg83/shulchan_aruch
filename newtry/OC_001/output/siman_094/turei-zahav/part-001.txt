<b>לרוח משאר רוחות. </b> בב"י מביא בשם אבוהב וז"ל ותימה על מנהג שאנו נוהגין לצפון ולדרום ולא הי' לנו להתפלל אלא למזרח ונראה שיש לנו להחזיר הפנים בצד המזרח כשאנו מתפללים לצפון ולדרום כדאמרינן בפרק לא יחפור דמצדד אצדודי פי' מי שמתפלל לצפון ולדרום יש לו להטות פניו לצד א"י עכ"ל ונ"ל שזה התיקון של חזרת פנים לא מהני אלא במתפלל לצפון או דרום דאפשר בחזרת פנים למזרח אבל מי שמתפלל למערב לא מהני ליה חזרת פנים למזרח כי א"א לו להפוך פניו ממש לאחוריו לצד מזרח ולא אמרו בגמ' אלא מצדד אצדודי שנוטה לצד אבל לא לאחוריו וע"כ לא נקט מהרי"א אלא מתפלל לצפון ודרום אבל לא למערב ואע"פ שהש"ע כאן כ' סתם לרוח משאר רוחות איהו  לא מיירי דוקא מאותן שיושבים במערב של ארץ ישראל שצריכים להתפלל למזרח כמו אנו אלא כ' דרך כלל שצריך להפוך פניו כנגד א"י לכל צד שיושבי' נגד א"י וע"כ כ' גם בזה דרך סתם לכל רוח דמהני ביה נטיית פניו לצד של א"י בדרך האפשרי וזה דבר שהשכל מחייב ונפק' לן במי ששכח ומתפלל לרוח אחרת אם הוא בצפון או בדרום יהפוך פניו לצד המזרח אבל אם שכח והתפלל למערב נראה דלא יהפוך פניו לצד דא"כ יהיה פניו נגד צפון או דרום ומ"מ נרא' דא"צ להפסיק ולעקור רגליו כדי להתפלל למזרח דהרי אמרינן בגמ' בפ' לא יחפור דאיכא מאן דס"ל דשכינה בכל מקום ובדיעבד יש לו לסמוך ע"ז ואין שייך כאן לומר כדו בר קיימא כו' כיון שאין אחרים מתפללים באותה שעה למזרח כנ"ל:

<b>Toward any other direction.</b> Beit Yosef cites Rabbi Avraham ben Chaim as follows: It is difficult regarding our practice of praying toward the north or south, for we should pray only eastward. It appears that when praying toward the north or south we should turn our faces eastward, as stated in Bava Basra, chapter Lo Yachpor, that one should angle himself. Meaning, one who prays toward the north or south should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael. It appears to me that this correction of turning the face is effective only when praying northward or southward, because one can turn his face eastward. If one prays westward, however, turning his face eastward is ineffective, since he cannot turn his face completely behind him toward the east. The Gemara speaks only of angling sideways, not backward. Rabbi Yosef Abuhav therefore mentioned only one praying northward or southward, not westward. Although the Shulchan Aruch writes generally, “toward any other direction,” he does not speak specifically of those who dwell west of Eretz Yisrael and must pray eastward, as we do. Rather, he writes generally that one should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael from every side on which people dwell relative to it. He therefore also writes generally that one may angle his face toward Eretz Yisrael in whatever manner is possible. Reason itself requires this. The practical consequence is that one who forgot and is praying in another direction should, if facing north or south, turn his face eastward. If he forgot and prayed westward, he should not turn his face sideways, for then he would face north or south. Nevertheless, it appears that he need not interrupt and move his feet in order to pray eastward, since the Gemara in Bava Basra records an opinion that the Shechinah is everywhere, and after the fact he may rely upon it. Nor is there reason here to call him wicked, because others are not praying eastward at that time.

<b>נגד זריחת השמש </b> כתוב בלבוש דארצות הללו הם נגד מערבי' צפוני' של א"י ע"כ צריך ליזהר כשבונין בה"כ שיעמדו כותל מזרחית שבו הארון נוטה לצד מזרחית דרומית דהשתא מחזיר פניו כנגד א"י וב"ה וקדשי קדשים אבל אם יעמידו כותל מזרחית כנגד זריחת השמש ממש יש בה תרתי לריעותא חדא דאינו מחזיר פניו לארץ ישראל כלל אידך דזה חוק המינים שמשתחוים לשמש ע"כ יש להעמיד כותל מזרחית כנגד מזרחית דרומית כזה והאריך בזה:

<b>Opposite sunrise.</b> The Levush writes that these lands lie northwest of Eretz Yisrael. Therefore, when constructing a synagogue, care must be taken that the eastern wall containing the Aron should incline southeastward. One then turns his face toward Eretz Yisrael, the Beis HaMikdash, and the Holy of Holies. If the eastern wall is positioned directly toward sunrise, there are two disadvantages: first, one does not face Eretz Yisrael at all; second, this is the practice of the sectarians, who bow to the sun. The eastern wall should therefore be positioned toward the southeast. He discusses this at length.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless, he should angle, etc.</b> One may also do the reverse, praying eastward while angling his face northward or southward. This is mentioned in Rashi’s explanation cited by Beit Yosef. It appears more correct to do so, for if he prays northward or southward he appears somewhat separate from the congregation, whereas he does not appear so when acting as described.

<b>וכן אם היה בספינה כו'. </b> פי' בקרון או ספינה יכול לעמוד דאין העגלה מתנועע כ"כ במהירות והספינה היא רחבה הרבה דליכא ביעתות' דמיא כשהוא עומד בתוכה וי"ל מאי מהני ליה מה שעומד בקרון או בספינם כיון שהקרון או הספינה מהלכת ה"ל כמהלך בעצמו כמ"ש רכוב כמהלך דמי וא"ל דשאני יושב בקרון או בספינה מרכוב דהא בפ"ק דב"מ אמרינן לענין קנין של חצר מהלכת דספינה לא חשיבה חצר מהלכת דספינה קא נייח' ומייא הם דקא מסגי מתות' והקשו התוס' מ"ש מרכוב דהוי כמהלך ותי' דבאמת גם ספינה חשיבה כמהלכת לשאר מילי רק לענין קנין לא מיקרי מהלכת דה"ל כידו דנייח' והגוף הוא דקא ממט' ליה א"כ קשה כאן דלהוי קרון וספינה כמהלך ומה מועיל מה שעומד בתוכה וצ"ל דכאן לענין תפל' לא הצריכו להתפלל מעומד אלא כדי שתהיה אימת שכינה עליו ובזה סגי גם עומד בקרון וספינה אף שלא מיקרי עומד מצד תנועת הקרון והספינה כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Likewise, if he was in a ship, etc.</b> Meaning, in a wagon or ship he can stand, because a wagon does not move so rapidly and a ship is broad, so there is no fear of the water while standing inside it. One might ask what standing in a wagon or ship accomplishes, since the wagon or ship moves and he should be considered as walking, just as one riding is considered walking. It cannot be answered that sitting in a wagon or ship differs from riding, for Bava Metzia, chapter 1, states regarding acquisition by a moving courtyard that a ship is not considered a moving courtyard because the ship rests while the water moves beneath it. Tosafos ask why this differs from riding, which is considered movement, and answer that for all other matters a ship is indeed considered moving; only regarding acquisition is it not called moving, because it is like his hand, which rests while the body carries it. It therefore remains difficult why standing in a wagon or ship helps. One must answer that regarding prayer the Sages required standing only so that awe of the Shechinah should be upon him. For this purpose, standing in a wagon or ship suffices, even if because of their movement it is not technically considered standing.

<b>He must repeat the prayer, etc.</b> Beit Yosef derived this from Tosafos in Berachos, chapter Tefillas HaShachar, in the name of Maharam, regarding the dispute whether prayer while standing is preferable or juxtaposing redemption to prayer is preferable. Tosafos write that one may pray the Eighteen Blessings while walking on the road and need not stand. They ask why this differs from Havineinu, which must be recited while standing, and answer that Havineinu is short and therefore causes less delay to the journey. Maharam further explains that here, upon returning home, one prays again while standing, as stated below that Rav Ashi prayed with the congregation while seated and upon returning home prayed while standing. Rabbeinu Tam ruled like the father of Shmuel and Levi. Yet since Rav Ashi said that he had not seen the elder Sages act that way, it implies that the halachah does not follow them. I am greatly perplexed by Maharam’s explanation. According to him, Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, who holds that juxtaposing redemption to prayer is preferable, must also hold that one later repeats the prayer while standing, as Rav Ashi did. If so, why did the Gemara challenge Rav Ashi to act like the father of Shmuel and Levi? Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar supported his practice, and the Tannaim had already disputed the matter, each with his own reason. Rather, Rav Ashi did not act in accordance with Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar either, for according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar there is no need to pray again while standing. The Gemara challenged him that since he required two prayers out of concern for standing prayer, why did he not act like the father of Shmuel and Levi, who likewise valued standing prayer and therefore prayed earlier in the morning? Rashi explicitly explains the question this way, asking why Rav Ashi needed two prayers. It follows that Rav Ashi innovated the practice of repeating the prayer while standing, unlike Maharam’s explanation that Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar agreed. The words of Rabbeinu Chananel also plainly indicate that he did not accept Maharam’s explanation. Had he accepted it, how could he rule like the father of Shmuel and Levi, who followed the first Tanna, when Rav Ashi, the later authority, followed Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar? Even Tosafos, who challenge Rabbeinu Chananel, argue only from Rav Ashi’s statement that he had not seen the elder Sages act that way, implying that the halachah does not follow them. Why did they not argue from Rav Ashi’s actual conduct of praying twice, which according to Maharam would follow Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar? Necessarily, they did not accept Maharam’s explanation. Perhaps the answer cited in Maharam’s name was a later marginal gloss rather than the words of Tosafos themselves. The Rosh likewise clearly disagrees with Maharam, for he writes that the halachah follows the father of Shmuel and Levi, and that even Rav Ashi did not disagree with them but merely said that he had not seen the elder Sages act so. According to Maharam, however, Rav Ashi certainly disagreed with them, since he acted exactly like Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, while they followed the first Tanna. Rather, Rav Ashi introduced a new practice of praying twice. We do not derive the halachah from his innovation, for he did so as a voluntary prayer, which was appropriate for him because he could concentrate during a voluntary prayer. This does not apply to us, as the Tur writes at the end of siman 107 and Beit Yosef also writes here. Beit Yosef later cites Maharam’s explanation and on that basis rules that anyone who prayed while seated must pray again while standing when able. It has already been shown, however, that Rashi, Tosafos, Rabbeinu Chananel, and the Rosh all disagree. Rambam and Tur also make no mention of this. Had they held that a second prayer was required, they would not have remained silent. We must therefore be concerned that repeating the prayer may constitute a blessing in vain. We have never seen great authorities who prayed while seated in a wagon on the road pray again upon reaching an inn or home. It therefore appears that here too one should not pray again unless he is certain that he can concentrate during the prayer, as written at the end of siman 107.
