<b>The writer said.</b> In my novellae to Megillah, chapter Bnei Ha'ir, I wrote at length, and here I will write briefly. Know that the sanctity of a synagogue and of a beis midrash is D'Rabbanan and not according to the Torah. See the Ran there regarding a gift, with what is its sanctity removed, 26b, where he concludes that the sanctity of a synagogue is from the words of the Sages, which implies that it is not according to the Torah. Similarly, in Sukkah, siman 638, Taz, seif katan 1, the schach is according to the Torah from the comparison to the festival, and even this is not by mere designation. But regarding the festival offering, designation is effective, as explained where one says, this animal is an olah, even in his heart. Partitions are only D'Rabbanan. See Shabbos, chapter Bameh Madlikin, the root of them all is blood; there he performs the mitzvah in a degrading manner. See there at length. However, the sanctity of a Torah scroll, that one may not treat it disgracefully, what is its status? It is derived by a kal vachomer: if one stands before those who study it, all the more so before it. See Yoreh Deah 282. This is a full kal vachomer, and honoring the face of an elder is according to the Torah. Also, the rule that one may not lower from a stricter sanctity to a lighter one, even if you say that these things have sanctity according to the Torah, it is possible that the prohibition to lower them is D'Rabbanan. Although Rashi and the Levush cited Tosafot regarding coating for the altar, etc., one can say that it is D'Rabbanan and an asmachta, for in Pesachim 26a Rabbi Dosa permits it for an ordinary kohen. And the Rambam, Laws of Temple Vessels 8:5, ruled like the first Tanna that the garments of the kohen gadol are entirely forbidden from the phrase that he leaves them there entirely, implying that otherwise, even though he lowers them from a stricter sanctity to a lighter one, nevertheless it is permitted.

<b>Sanctity.</b> See Taz. There are two initial questions here. First, may one sell one item of sanctity in order to buy another like it? Second, if he already sold it, may he buy something like it? From the Ran in the Mishnah of Bnei Ha'ir it is explained that even partial initial action is like initial action itself, for in his explanation he says so: since he began with inferior items, he used that formulation for all of them. Thus, in truth, for equal sanctity it is forbidden, and the Mishnah taught, if they sold, which shows that partial initial action is exactly like initial action. Certainly, from the question later on 27a there is no proof except that actual initial sale is forbidden for equal sanctity in the other items, since the question was asked about a Torah scroll, where there is no higher elevation. But the Ran holds that partial initial action is exactly like initial action. Mahari disagrees with the Ran and permits even actual initial sale, for it is all one matter. The Beit Yosef brought the beginning of the Ran's words but not the end, and wrote that Mahari disagrees. One might have said to distinguish between after the fact and initially, as the Bach wrote, but as we said, there is no distinction, for this too is initial action. This also seems to be Rashi's view there, 27a, s.v. after the fact, for otherwise what should they buy with it, since partial initial action is like initial action. So too it appears to be the view of the Rambam, Laws of Tefillah 11:14, who concludes that if they sold a Torah scroll, they may buy with its proceeds only another Torah scroll, for there is no sanctity above a Torah scroll. Since he did not use the language of the Mishnah, that they may not buy scrolls, and since there is no higher sanctity, this is the meaning: a Torah scroll is permitted, for what else should be done with its proceeds, but with other items, equal sanctity is forbidden, because partial initial action is like initial action. What he wrote in halacha 17, that villagers who wanted to sell their synagogue to build another or the like, seems to imply that equal sanctity is permitted even initially. See Tosafot Yom Tov and Lechem Yehudah of Mahari Ayash. One can say that he is speaking of a case where it is permitted, such as where there is a flaw in it or the like, and in my novellae I wrote at length about this. In Shulchan Aruch, one opinion says this, and there are those who explain that there is no distinction between partial or actual initial action. Since he brought the latter explanation last, and especially since it is a D'Rabbanan matter, one can say that his view is to be lenient. However, regarding a Torah scroll he ruled in Yoreh Deah 270, and so too the Tur, that it is forbidden to sell an old one to buy a new one. Regarding a Torah scroll there is only actual initial sale, for otherwise what will he do with the proceeds. One can say that there it is because of negligence, but the Taz and Shach there, note 3, not like the Levush there, explain it because of negligence; see there. The Taz raised two difficulties against Mahari. First, why did he infer from the Tur and not from the Mishnah, scrolls and a wrapper, that equal sanctity is permitted? One can answer that this is because the Tur wrote, but the reverse, to lower them, is not permitted, and not merely, but the reverse is not permitted. He answered that certainly the Tanna mentioned two matters incidentally with one, but the rule of since does not apply, as explained in many sugyos of the Gemara. In a Mishnah, however, he mentioned each item separately and did not include them all together. One can say as the Ran wrote, that since he began with what is lower than a Torah scroll, that they may not buy scrolls, but may buy a Torah scroll because what else should be done with its proceeds, he used the same formulation for the others and did not want to make a separate clause for equal sanctity. But the Tur included them together, saying the reverse, to lower them, and the difficulty remains: how could he teach a law that is not true, since even equal sanctity is forbidden? We do not say that he included so much incidentally because of one item. This proves that in truth equal sanctity is also permitted with the other items. If you ask that if so one might mistakenly think that even by a Torah scroll initial sale is permitted, for partial initial action is like actual initial action, he relied on Yoreh Deah 270, where he rules without qualification and forbids a Torah scroll, which certainly implies that even where there is no concern of negligence it is forbidden, as we said. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further elsewhere. See what I write below on this.

<b>It seems to me.</b> In my poverty, there is a difficulty here. In seif 4 he did not decide, but brought those who forbid and those who permit, implying that in a D'Rabbanan matter we are lenient. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Was sold.</b> See Taz. At first glance, the meaning of the Shulchan Aruch, that it is permitted to change it, refers to a synagogue and the like, which becomes profane as in seif 9, and therefore requires no announcement. But something that may not be changed to a lighter sanctity, such as accessories of sanctity, and certainly scrolls, requires an announcement. The Magen Avraham, note 20, emended it as a textual error, etc. This is also the view of the Taz here, that permitted to change it means to give it as a gift, as in seif 11, meaning that one may change it from what it originally stood for, like a house for the poor, as the Magen Avraham wrote. In this, the Taz and Magen Avraham agree. But regarding where there is no law of overreaching, they disagree. According to the Taz, the Rama states two matters; according to the Magen Avraham, note 19, it speaks of land. According to the Magen Avraham this is fine: he began with two matters, that it needs no announcement because it is like his own, and therefore there is no overreaching in land, like an ordinary agent, for even by land there is overreaching when one can say, I sent you to improve, etc.; and he ended with one matter, that it requires announcement, and automatically there is overreaching. But according to the Taz, who says it refers to movable items, what difference and reason is there between requiring an announcement or not? For even one's own movable items are subject to overreaching. Why did he begin with two matters and end with one? See Sema, Choshen Mishpat 227, note 48, that nowadays a synagogue and other charity endowments do not have the law of Temple property; apparently he alluded to that here. This implies even accessories of sanctity.

<b>הפרש</b> יש בין תשמישי קדושה לתשמישי מצוה שאלו ר״ל מצוה נזרקין לאחר מצותן עסי׳ כ״א. ותשמישי קדושה צריך גניזה במקום המשומר (ובקנ״ד סעיף ד׳ יכולים כו׳ לא חובה דוקא יע״ש) גם הא דאין מורידן מחמורה לקלה בקדושה הא במצוה מורידן עש״ך י״ד רנ״ט אות י״א בשם המרדכי יע״ש ולכאורה במחלוקת שנוי דלר״ן ר״פ בני העיר דביה״כ קדושה יש בה שפיר משא״כ לרמב״ן דמצוה הוי ואפ״ה ביה״כ יקחו תיבה ואסור להוריד ואנן קיי״ל כר״ן דביה״כ וכל שבתוכה קדושה יש בה עמ״א אות כ״ח ואפ״ה אסור לעשות מכסא ספסלים לישב בהן דזה תשמיש קדושה דס״ת יש בה ואסור להוריד אפילו בקדושה חדא מקבוע לעראי כבסי׳ קנ״ד ס״ו מתיבה כסא. אבל מעראי קצת לעראי יותר שרי עיין תשובת מהר״מ פדוואה סימן פ״ב עמ״א ק״ד אות י״ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There is a difference.</b> There is a difference between accessories of sanctity and accessories of mitzvah. The latter, meaning mitzvah items, may be discarded after their mitzvah; see siman 21. Accessories of sanctity require genizah in a guarded place, and in siman 154, seif 4, the word they may does not mean it is an obligation specifically; see there. Also, the rule that one may not lower from a stricter sanctity to a lighter one applies to sanctity, but with a mitzvah one may lower it. See Shach, Yoreh Deah 259, note 11, in the name of the Mordechai; see there. At first glance, this is subject to dispute: according to the Ran at the beginning of chapter Bnei Ha'ir, that a synagogue has sanctity, it is well understood. But according to the Ramban, that it is a mitzvah, and even so a synagogue may be used to buy an ark and may not be lowered, what can be said? We hold like the Ran, that a synagogue and all that is within it have sanctity; see Magen Avraham, note 28. Even so, it is forbidden to make from a chair benches to sit on, for this has the status of an accessory of sanctity of a Torah scroll, and it is forbidden to lower even within one sanctity from fixed to temporary, as in siman 154, seif 6, from an ark to a chair. But from somewhat temporary to more temporary it is permitted. See responsa Maharam Padua, siman 82, and Magen Avraham 104, note 13.

<b>והוי</b> יודע דתשמיש קדושה אסור להשתמש תשמיש חול אפילו לאחר שבלה וטעון גניזה ותשמיש דתשמיש (כל שאין משמש לגוף הקדושה ומיהו ארון ותיבה שס״ת מונח בו אע״פ שמונח במטפחת הוי ארון ותיבה תשמיש קדושה עי״ד רפ״ב סי״ב ור״מ פ״י מס״ת ה״ד יע״ש) ל״מ שא״צ גניזה אפילו בעודו ראוי לית ביה קדושה כלל ושרי לתשמיש הדיוט ומהא אמינא לה ממ״ש הב״י בסימן קנ״ד שולחן שלנו רשאי להשען עליו דמפה פרוסה עליו וכ״נ מלבוש קנ״ד ס״ג תשמיש דתשמיש מותר להשתמש בו ואל תשיבני מקנ״ד ס״ו בהג״ה דמכלונס אסור עצים שמסמנין אע״ג דתשמיש דתשמיש הוי אלמא יש ביה קדושה אדרבה משם ראיה שהרי בפסקי מהרא״י ז״ל סימן רכ״ה כתב דמ״מ כלונס קדושת ביה״כ יש בו כמו ספסלים כו׳ אלמא דאל״ה לית ביה קדושה כלל ומש״ה אותן שעושין מכסה כסף או בלע״ך לתפילין של יד הוי תשמיש דתשמיש ולית ביה קדושה כלל אבל של ראש משמע דהוי תשמיש קדושה מפני השי״ן עמ״ש בקנ״ד בט״ז אות ב׳:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And know.</b> It is forbidden to use an accessory of sanctity for mundane use even after it has worn out, and it requires genizah. An accessory of an accessory, meaning anything that does not serve the body of the sacred item, though an aron or ark in which a Torah scroll rests, even if it rests in a wrapper, is considered an accessory of sanctity; see Yoreh Deah 282:12 and Rambam, Laws of a Torah Scroll 10:4. Not only does an accessory of an accessory not require genizah, but even while it is fit for use it has no sanctity at all and may be used for ordinary purposes. I derive this from what the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 154, that one may lean on our table because a cloth is spread over it. So it appears from the Levush, siman 154, seif 3, that an accessory of an accessory may be used. Do not challenge me from siman 154, seif 6 in the Rama, where poles are forbidden, the wood used as markers, even though they are an accessory of an accessory, which would show that they have sanctity. On the contrary, from there is a proof, for in the rulings of Mahari, siman 225, he wrote that nevertheless a pole has the sanctity of the synagogue, like benches, etc.; this shows that otherwise it would have no sanctity at all. Therefore those who make a silver or tin cover for the hand tefillin have made only an accessory of an accessory, and it has no sanctity at all. But for the head tefillin it seems to be an accessory of sanctity because of the shin. See what I wrote in siman 154, Taz note 2.

<b>ותשמישי</b> מצוה דנזרקין לאחר מצותן לא בעודם ראויין למצותן ועסי׳ כ״א בב״ח דשופר ולולב אחר ר״ה וסוכה אפ״ה קיימי למצותן לשנה הבאה אסור להשתמש בהן ועמ״א ש״ח אות י״ב ושבת ל״ו א׳ שופר ראוי לגמוע מים משמע דשרי לאחר מצותן וצ״ע. ועסי׳ כ״א ותרס״ו ס״ח תשמיש מגונה אסור אף בתשמישי מצוה ועיין ר״ן ורמב״ן ובעל המאור ובלולב הגזול בהא דהיו מורישין לולביהן לבני בניהם הקשו לעשות מכבדות יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And accessories.</b> Accessories of mitzvah that may be discarded after their mitzvah refers only after they are no longer fit for their mitzvah, not while they are still fit. See siman 21 in the Bach, that a shofar and lulav after Rosh Hashanah, and a sukkah, nevertheless stand for their mitzvah for the next year, and it is forbidden to use them. See Magen Avraham 308, note 12, and Shabbos 36a, where it says a shofar is fit to draw water, which implies that after its mitzvah it is permitted. This requires study. See siman 21 and 666:8, that a degrading use is forbidden even with accessories of mitzvah. See the Ran, Ramban, and Baal HaMaor in Lulav HaGazul regarding that which they would bequeath their lulavim to their children; they asked why they did not make brooms from them. See there.
