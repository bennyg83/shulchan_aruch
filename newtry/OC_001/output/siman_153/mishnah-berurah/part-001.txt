See below in seif 13, in the Magen Avraham there, where it is proven that it is not proper to sell even a synagogue, even for redeeming captives, unless it is impossible to obtain other money for this from the public. In this manner it is certainly permitted even where they have only one synagogue. Since seif 7 has many details, I will first introduce a brief preface: what was mentioned above regarding selling a synagogue applies if it is of a village, for presumably they built it only with themselves in mind, and therefore the majority of the townspeople have the ability to sell it, provided that afterward they do not lower the money from its sanctity. When it is sold also with the consent of their seven leaders, then they may do with the money whatever they wish, meaning to remove it to non-sacred status, as stated below. But if it is a synagogue of a large city, we assume that at the time of building they built it also with the entire world in mind. Even if they gave all the building money from their own funds, nevertheless we assume that they built it for everyone. Therefore, even if afterward the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople agreed to sell it, their sale is not a sale. Now we will explain the words of the Shulchan Aruch.

<b>The seven leaders of the city.</b> The Rashba, part 1, siman 617, wrote, and the Rashdam, siman 175, brought it, that the seven leaders of the city are not the seven people most distinguished in wisdom, wealth, or honor, but seven people whom the public appointed as communal leaders over the affairs of the city, and they are like guardians over them. (צט) אין הצבור יכולים - ולא אמרינן דבודאי החליטה להקדש ולא דמי לכלי הקודש של כסף שנהגו להביאם בביהכ"נ המובא בסעיף י"ח דאין הבעלים יכולים להוציאם לחולין דשאני הכא בס"ת שכן הדרך לעשות ס"ת ע"מ כן להניחה בביהכ"נ שיקראו בה רבים ושתהיה מונחת שם עד יום פקוד הבעלים אותה ליקח לביתו ואפילו היתה לבושה מעילין של צבור ולא הקפיד לא אמרינן בשביל זה מסתמא כבר סילק ידו ממנה משום דכן המנהג תדיר להחליף המעילין מס"ת אל ס"ת הן של יחידים הן של הקהל מיהו כמה אחרונים כתבו דגם הכא בס"ת מיירי שאנו יודעין בבירור שבתחלה לא הניחה לחלוטין רק שהשאילה לבהכ"נ שיקראו בה רבים ואשמועינן דלא אמרינן כיון שהניחה זמן רב מסתמא כבר החליטה להקדש אבל בהניחה סתם אמרינן דמסתמא אקדשה לעולם וכההיא דכלי הקודש בסעיף י"ח הנ"ל. ועיין במ"א סקכ"ב שכתב דמנהג העולם כהיום למכור ס"ת אפילו נתנו מתחלה לביהכ"נ לקרות בו וכתב דכיון שנהגו כן הו"ל כאלו התנו מתחלה בהדיא שלא תחול קדושת רבים עליהן ולפ"ז נראה דלכו"ע אין להחמיר בזה בדיעבד ומ"מ לכתחלה בודאי טוב להתנות בהדיא שאין נותנה לחלוטין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The public cannot.</b> We do not say that he certainly transferred it absolutely to hekdesh. This is not comparable to the silver sacred vessels customarily brought to the synagogue, mentioned in seif 18, where the owners cannot remove them for non-sacred use. Here, by a Torah scroll, it is different, for this is the way people make a Torah scroll, on this condition, to place it in the synagogue so that the public will read from it, and so that it remain there until the day the owners decide to take it to their home. Even if it was dressed in covers belonging to the public and he did not object, we do not say that because of this he presumably already removed his hand from it, for the constant custom is to switch covers from one Torah scroll to another, whether privately owned or communal. However, several later authorities wrote that here too, regarding a Torah scroll, it speaks where we know clearly that at first he did not place it there absolutely, but only lent it to the synagogue for the public to read from it. It teaches that we do not say that since he left it there a long time, he presumably already transferred it absolutely to hekdesh. But if he placed it there without specification, we say that he presumably consecrated it forever, like the law of sacred vessels in seif 18. See the Magen Avraham, seif katan 22, who wrote that the common custom today is to sell a Torah scroll even if it was initially given to the synagogue to read from it, and he wrote that since they practiced this way, it is as though they stipulated explicitly from the beginning that the sanctity of the public should not take effect upon them. According to this, it appears that all agree that one should not be stringent in this after the fact, but initially it is certainly good to stipulate explicitly that he is not giving it absolutely.

(ק) להחזיק בו - ואפילו יש עדים מבני אותה העיר דנתנה לצבור לא מהני דנוגעין בעדות הם ואפילו אם ירצו לסלק עצמם ממנה לא מהני אא"כ יש להם ס"ת אחרת מדוייקת וכשרה כמותה [אחרונים]. ישוב שלא היה לצבור ס"ת וקראו בס"ת של יחיד ואח"כ קנו הצבור ס"ת וטוען היחיד שיש לו חזקה שיקראו דוקא בס"ת שלו אין זו חזקה דאין עמה טענה [וכמבואר בחו"מ סוף סימן קמ"ט בהג"ה ע"ש] ומ"מ אין למעט כבוד ס"ת של היחיד אלא יקראו בכל שבת בס"ת אחרת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To retain it.</b> Even if there are witnesses from that city that it was given to the public, they are not effective, for they are interested parties in their testimony. Even if they want to remove themselves from it, it does not help unless they have another Torah scroll that is as precise and valid as this one. A settlement had no communal Torah scroll and read from an individual's Torah scroll. Afterward the public bought a Torah scroll, and the individual claimed that he has a chazakah that they should read specifically from his Torah scroll. This is not a chazakah, for it has no claim with it, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 149, in the gloss, see there. Nevertheless, one should not diminish the honor of the individual's Torah scroll; rather, they should read each Shabbos from a different Torah scroll.
