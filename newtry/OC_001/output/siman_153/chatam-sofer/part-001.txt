<small>שם במג"א ס"ק ב' </small>וצ"ע. נ"ב ובמתני' נמי קשיא שלא הזכיר החילוק שבין חומשין לספרים וכן תמה בטורי אבן שם. ולפענ"ד נראה דשלשה חילוקים יש. א' לענין אמירת הפסוקים דברי נביאים קדושים טפי כמ"ש הר"ן בר"ה. אמנם מכל מקום לענין קדושת הספר אין שום חילוק דאותה קדושה שמתקדש הגויל ע"י דברי נביאים מתקדש נמי ע"י כתובים דמ"ש ומשום הכי כתב הר"ן בפרק בני העיר דמטפחת נביאים וכתובים אחת היא. אמנם חומשין שגם הגויל קדוש טפי שהרי ראויה להשלימו לתורה שלימה ומכ"ש ס"ת שלם רק חסר בו אות אחת שאפשר לתקנו ומש"ה אוסר בברייתא לכרוך במטפחת חומשין וכ"ז לענין מטפחת אמנם למכור ולקנות דא ודא אחת היא דאין שום חילוק בין העוסק בתורת מרע"ה או בספרי נביאים וכתובים ואפי' רק במפרשי הש"ס משום הכי לא חילק במתני' ר"פ בני העיך כלל בענין מכירה בין חומשין לספרי' ומ"מ בס"ת שלם שיש בו קדושה אחרת לקרות בציבור ועוד מצות עשה של ועתה כתבו לכם בודאי מתעלה בקריאתו מחומשים וספרים לס"ת שלם וזה ברור ומיושבים כל הקושיות. וחילוק כזה צריכין לומר ג"כ במכירת בהכנ"ס דקיל היינו מכירת הבית כשאין בו ביטול תפלה אבל בלי ספק שמותר למכור מטפחת לצורך תפלה בעשרה לקדש שם שמים בעשרה ומשום הכי כשאין בהכנ"ס אחר אסור למכור כמ"ש הרמב"ם שמביאו המג"א ס"ס ק"א ודו"ק בכ"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2.</small> <b>And it requires study.</b> Note: in the Mishnah as well it is difficult that it did not mention the distinction between Chumashim and other books, and the Turei Even there also wondered about this. In my humble opinion, it appears that there are three distinctions. First, regarding reciting verses, the words of the Prophets are more sacred, as the Ran wrote in Rosh Hashanah. Nevertheless, with respect to the sanctity of the book, there is no distinction, for the same sanctity with which the parchment becomes sanctified through words of the Prophets also becomes sanctified through Writings, for what difference is there? Therefore the Ran wrote in chapter Bnei HaIr that a covering for Prophets and Writings has one status. Chumashim, however, have greater sanctity even in the parchment, since it is fit to complete them into a whole Torah, and certainly a complete Torah scroll that is missing only one letter, which can be repaired. Therefore the baraisa forbids wrapping with a covering of Chumashim. All this is regarding a covering. But regarding selling and buying, this and that are one, for there is no distinction between one who occupies himself with the Torah of Moshe Rabbeinu or with books of the Prophets and Writings, and even only with commentaries on the Talmud. Therefore the Mishnah at the beginning of Bnei HaIr did not distinguish at all regarding sale between Chumashim and other books. Nevertheless, a complete Torah scroll, which has an additional sanctity for public reading and also the positive commandment of “and now write for yourselves,” is certainly elevated beyond Chumashim and books by reading from a complete Torah scroll. This is clear, and all the difficulties are resolved. A similar distinction must also be said regarding the sale of a synagogue, that when it is considered lighter, that means selling the building where there is no cancellation of prayer. But without doubt it is permitted to sell a covering for the sake of prayer with ten, to sanctify the Name of Heaven with ten. Therefore, when there is no other synagogue, it is forbidden to sell, as the Rambam wrote, cited by the Magen Avraham at the end of seif katan 101. Examine all this carefully.

It is difficult for me, for it is proven that they hold that regarding a woman there is only the matter of sheves, which is from Divrei Kabbalah, and for that we do not sell, whereas regarding a man there is also the mitzvah of peru u’rvu, which is according to the Torah, so sheves is a great mitzvah. As for what the Eliyah Rabbah wrote from Kesubos that marrying off an orphan girl comes first because of her shame, it appears to me that this is only to give precedence, not to override. Know that all this is when he cannot find a way to learn Torah or marry otherwise. But otherwise, although it is still a mitzvah, nevertheless it is lighter than other positive commandments, as Tosafot wrote in the name of the She’iltos in Parshas Emor, Avodah Zarah 13a. But when it is impossible in another way, the She’iltos rules as is proven from what is written in his name at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma. Examine all this well.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There, seif katan 46.</small> <b>And he gave her much.</b> Note: this is like “exchange price” in Bechoros 54a, and below in seif katan 47, where he gave ten lambs and the buyer will choose one in exchange for the dog. Then, according to the opinion that there is no retroactive clarification, all are forbidden. But if the seller clarifies and says, “One of ten lambs that I have I will give you as the hire,” then when he later clarifies one, the hire does not take effect retroactively, as Tosafot wrote in Avodah Zarah 63a, s.v. kegon. It is possible that the same applies to an exchange price: if he said, “One of ten that I will choose from my lambs shall be the exchange price of your dog,” it is not forbidden as an exchange price. But perhaps one can say that an exchange price is different, because the dog is still in existence; if it did not take effect before it was clarified, it takes effect now when he clarifies and becomes the exchange price of the dog that still exists. This is unlike hire, for since it did not take effect before it was clarified, it no longer takes effect, because the benefit of relations has already passed. This requires study for practical halacha.

**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There, Magen Avraham, seif katan 49.</small> <b>And their words require study.</b> Note: in my humble opinion their words may be resolved as follows. They hold that according to Rabbi Meir, who is not concerned for enmity, the reason is that in other appointments the first does not return to his authority at all. If so, granted that by a Kohen Gadol the first returns to his service, nevertheless he will not have enmity if the second also serves with him, for in other appointments the first has nothing at all; thus it is enough for him that he returns to his service. But Rabbi Yosei holds that in all appointments the law is that the first returns to his service. If so, we are concerned for enmity. Therefore it was necessary to rule that the halacha follows Rabbi Yosei, and this is easy to understand. See regarding this for practical halacha in my responsa, Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, simanim 12, 13, and 41, where I wrote about appointment to a rabbinic position, whether the son inherits his father’s seat, and elaborated to clarify this matter. See also Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, simanim 21 and 22.
