<b>וכן נביאים וכתובים</b>. משמע דס"ל דחומשין ונביאים וכתובים כי הדדי נינהו וצ"ע דבי"ד סי' רפ"ב סי"ט אי' דחומשין קדושתן חמורה מנביאים וכתובים וכ"ה בהדיא בגמ' דף כ"ז, והע"ת לא ע"ש לכן נ"ל דאסור ליקח בדמי חומשין נביאים וכתובים ונביאים וכתובים כהדדי נינהו כמ"ש בי"ד שם בהג"ה בשם התו' והר"ן וצ"ע דהר"ן גופא כתב דקדושת נביאים חמורה מקדושת כתובים הביאו הב"י סי' תקצ"א ע"ש ואפשר דמ"מ אינו חמור כ"כ שיהיה אסור להניח א' ע"ג חבירו או ליקח א' בדמי חבירו וצ"ע דהא אפי' בדבר שקדושתו שוה בעי למימר בגמ' דאסור להניח א' ע"ג חבירו אלא דשרי משום דלא אפשר א"כ בדבר שיש בו צד חומר למה יהיה מותר עמ"ש סק"ח, ומשמע דר"ן במגילה הדר ביה ממ"ש במס' ר"ה וס"ל דכי הדדי נינהו רק דמקדמי כתובים משום שדוד הי' קודם לנביאים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And likewise Prophets and Writings.</b> It implies that he holds that Chumashim, Prophets, and Writings are all equivalent. This requires study, for in Yoreh Deah, siman 282, seif 19, it is stated that the sanctity of Chumashim is more severe than that of Prophets and Writings, and so it is explicit in the Gemara, page 27. The Olas Tamid did not look there. Therefore it appears to me that it is forbidden to use the proceeds of Chumashim to purchase Prophets and Writings, while Prophets and Writings are equivalent to one another, as written there in Yoreh Deah in the gloss, in the name of Tosafot and the Ran. It requires study, for the Ran himself wrote that the sanctity of Prophets is more severe than the sanctity of Writings, as the Beis Yosef brought in siman 591, see there. Perhaps nevertheless it is not so severe that it would be forbidden to place one upon the other or to buy one with the proceeds of the other. Yet this requires study, for even regarding an item whose sanctity is equal, the Gemara wanted to say that it is forbidden to place one upon the other, and it is permitted only because it is impossible. If so, regarding something that has an aspect of greater severity, why should it be permitted? See what I wrote in seif katan 8. It implies that the Ran in Megillah retracted from what he wrote in tractate Rosh Hashanah and holds that they are equivalent; only Writings are given precedence because David preceded the Prophets.

<b>יש אוסרין</b>. והב"ח כתב דליכ' פלוגתא דלכתחלה ודאי אסור למכור כמ"ש הר"ן מדאיבעי' לן אי שרי למכור ס"ת ישן כדי לקנות חדש כיון דליכא לעלויי משמע דבשאר מילי ודאי אסור והא דכתב הטור להורידן מקדושתן אסור משמע אבל לקדוש' כיוצ' בה שרי היינו בדיעבד אם מכרם כבר וכ"ד מהרי"א אך בס"ת נקטי' לחומר' דאסור למכור ליקח בדמיה אחרת אפילו כי ליכא פשיעות' וכ"ד הרי"ף והרא"ש והטי"ד והש"כ שם בסי' ע"ר אבל לשון הרמב"ם משמע דאף לכתחילה מותר למכור בה"כ לקנות בדמיה אחרת שכתב פי"א מה"ת וז"ל בני הכפר שרצו למכור בה"כ שלהן או לבנות בדמיה בה"כ אחרת וכו' ע"ש ולהכ' כתב הרב"י כאן ויש מתירין ונ"ל דעתו דדוקא ס"ת שהמכירה בעצמו אסור דהא אין יכולין להפקיעה מקדושתה אבל בה"כ יכולין להפקיע קדושתן כמ"ש ס"ט א"כ אף על גב שלא מכרו זט"ה במא"ה דאין יכולין להפקיע קדושתן שמא יש איש א' שאינו מסכים בזה מ"מ רשאין למכרו כדי לבנות בדמיה אחרת דבהא לא מפקע קדושתה ועמ"ש סי' קנ"ב ומ"ש הרב"י דלהרמב"ם לכתחלה אין מוכרין בה"כ אפי' ליקח בדמיו קדושה חמורה היינו כשאין להם בה"כ אחר והיינו מ"ש סי"ג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Some forbid.</b> The Bach wrote that there is no dispute; initially it is certainly forbidden to sell, as the Ran wrote from the fact that they asked whether it is permitted to sell an old Torah scroll in order to buy a new one, since there is no way to elevate it, which implies that with other matters it is certainly forbidden. What the Tur wrote, that it is forbidden to lower them from their sanctity, which implies that to a sanctity like it is permitted, refers after the fact, if they already sold them, and this is also the view of Mahari Abuhav. But regarding a Torah scroll we adopt stringency, that it is forbidden to sell it in order to buy another with its proceeds even when there is no concern for negligence. This is also the view of the Rif, Rosh, Tur Yoreh Deah, and the Shach there in siman 270. However, the language of the Rambam implies that even initially it is permitted to sell a synagogue in order to buy another with its proceeds, for he wrote in chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah: the villagers who wished to sell their synagogue or to build another synagogue with its proceeds, etc.; see there. Therefore the Beis Yosef wrote here, and some permit. It appears to me that his view is that specifically regarding a Torah scroll, where the sale itself is forbidden because they cannot remove its sanctity, this applies; but with a synagogue, they can remove its sanctity, as written in seif 9. Therefore, even though they did not sell it through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, so they cannot remove its sanctity, for perhaps there is one person who does not agree to this, nevertheless they may sell it in order to build another with its proceeds, for in this case they do not remove its sanctity. See what I wrote in siman 152. And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that according to the Rambam initially one may not sell a synagogue even in order to buy a stricter sanctity with its proceeds, refers to where they have no other synagogue, and that is what he wrote in seif 13.

<b>אין משנין</b>. הקשה הכ"מ בשם הרמ"ך הא קי"ל דהזמנה לאו מלתא' היא וכמ"ש ס"ח וכ"ש מעות דכטווי לאריג דמי והניחו בצ"ע ול"נ דבאמת במעו' אין בו קדושה דאפילו בבה"כ עצמה אין בה קדושה דהוי כתשמישי מצוה כמ"ש הרב"י והר"ן בשם הרמב"ם וע"ש אלא דאיכא משום אכחושי מצוה ולכן אסור לשנות המעות אבל במעות פשיטא דליכא קדושה וכ"כ הרא"ש בתשו' כלל י"ג סי"ד והא דאסור לשנות המעות היינו משום דבני העיר לא נתנו המעות אלא אדעתא שיבנו בה"כ ואסור לשנותה שלא מדעתן וכ"כ הרא"ש פ"ק דב"ב ובי"ד סי' רנ"ו אלא שהצבור רשאי לשנותן דניתן על דעתם והש"כ בי"ד סי' רנ"ט כתב בשם הרא"ם ח"א סי' נ"ג הטעם כיון שגבו המעות הוי מעשה ולא הזמנה וכ"כ הב"ח וקשה הלא אין לך מעשה גדול מבנין בה"כ ואפ"ה אינה קדושה כמ"ש ס"ח וע"ק דבסנהדרין דף מ"ח אי' דבשגבו מעות למת מקרי הזמנה ולא מעשה לכן נ"ל דהטעם ג"כ כאן כמו במת כמ"ש דכיון שגבו לצרכו ה"ל בזיון כשמשנה אותן משא"כ ביחיד המנדב או שקנו ממעות צבור שגבו כבר ועבי"ד סי' שנ"ו, ומ"ש ס"ח דהזמנה לאו מילתא היא איירי שקנאוה הקהל ממעות חולין שגבו סתם וה"ה לבנים חדשים שרי לשנותן כה"ג ופשוט דשבעה טובי העיר במעמד אנשי העיר שרי לשנות המעות כמ"ש סעי' ז':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One may not change.</b> The Kesef Mishneh asked in the name of the Ramach: we hold that designation is not effective, as written in seif 8, and certainly money is like spun thread for a garment. He left it requiring study. It does not appear so to me, for in truth money has no sanctity; even the synagogue itself has no sanctity, for it is like accessories of a mitzvah, as the Beis Yosef and the Ran wrote in the name of the Rambam, see there. Rather, there is a concern of weakening the mitzvah, and therefore it is forbidden to change the money. But it is obvious that money has no sanctity. So too the Rosh wrote in responsa, klal 13, siman 14. What is forbidden in changing the money is because the townspeople gave the money only with the intention that they build a synagogue, and it is forbidden to change it without their consent. So too the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Bava Basra and in Yoreh Deah, siman 256. However, the public may change it, for it was given according to their intent. The Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, wrote in the name of the Re'em, part 1, siman 53, that the reason is that once they collected the money, it is an act and not mere designation. So too the Bach wrote. This is difficult, for there is no greater act than building a synagogue, and nevertheless it is not sanctified, as written in seif 8. Further, in Sanhedrin 48 it is stated that when they collected money for a dead person it is called designation and not an act. Therefore it appears to me that here too the reason is like by a dead person, as I wrote, that once they collected for his need, it is a disgrace if they change it; unlike an individual who donates, or where they bought with public money that had already been collected. See Yoreh Deah, siman 356. And what is written in seif 8, that designation is not effective, speaks where the congregation bought it with non-sacred money that they had collected without specification. The same applies to new bricks; it is permitted to change them in such a case. It is obvious that the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople may change the money, as written in seif 7.

Nevertheless, it is not more stringent than a synagogue.

<b>וכל זה</b>. פי' הא דכפרים נמכר דוקא כשיש להם אחרת פי' שבנאו עכשיו אחרת אבל בשיש להם שני בתי הכנס' מקודם אסור למכור א' דהא אפי' לסתור אחד אסור כמ"ש ריש סי' קנ"ב: ומשמע הכי בהדיא דשל כרכים אפי' בנאו אחרת אסורים למכור הראשונ' דהא עלה קאי והטעם משום דאין בידן ליפקע קדושתן אבל לסתרו שרי דהא בקדושתן קיימי ומ"מ העצים והאבנים והקרקע בקדושתייהו קיימי ואסור להשתמש בהם וגם למכרן דשמא יש אחד בסוף העולם דלא ניחא ליה שתפקע קדושתן, וכ"מ ברי"ו נ"ג ח"ה וז"ל לבנים או סיד של ב"ה מוכרין אותם כמו שמוכרים בית הכנסת ודינן ממש לצאת לחולין כמו דין בה"כ כמש"ל עכ"ל, וכ"מ ברמב"ם שדומין ממש ע"ש ובמ"ב התיר להשתמש באבנים אפי' בשל כרכים דמאי איכפת להו לרחוקים באבנים כיון שיש להם בה"כ אחרת להתפלל בתוכה וכ"כ בס' מ"ש סי"ז ול"נ כמ"ש דמ"מ א"א למכור של אחרים בלא דעתן כמ"ש בח"מ סימן שנ"ט ס"ב, ומכ"ש בדבר שבקדושה א"א ליפקע קדושתן דאל"כ ל"ל במעמד אנשי העיר לימא מאי איכפת להו בלבני' אלא ע"כ כמ"ש ועמ"ש סי"א וסי"ד בשם ר"ם לובלין דמשמע כמ"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>All this.</b> Meaning, that a village synagogue may be sold specifically when they have another, meaning that they have now built another. But if they had two synagogues previously, it is forbidden to sell one, for even demolishing one is forbidden, as written at the beginning of siman 152. This also clearly implies regarding a synagogue of a large city, that even if they built another, it is forbidden to sell the first, for that is what the discussion refers to. The reason is that they do not have power to remove its sanctity. But demolishing it is permitted, for it remains in its sanctity. Nevertheless, the wood, stones, and ground remain in their sanctity, and it is forbidden to use them and also to sell them, for perhaps there is one person at the end of the world who is not agreeable that their sanctity be removed. So it also implies in Rabbeinu Yerucham, nesiv 3, part 5: bricks or plaster of a synagogue may be sold as one sells a synagogue, and their law is exactly to go out to non-sacred status like the law of a synagogue, as written above. So too it implies in the Rambam, that they are exactly similar; see there. In the Mishnah Berurah he permitted using the stones even of a large city synagogue, for what do distant people care about the stones once they have another synagogue in which to pray? So too it is written in the Sefer M. Sh. seif 17. But it appears to me as I wrote, for nevertheless one cannot sell what belongs to others without their consent, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 359, seif 2, and certainly in a matter of sanctity one cannot remove its sanctity. Otherwise, why would one need the presence of the townspeople? Let it say: what do they care about the bricks? Rather, it must be as I wrote. See what I wrote in seif 11 and seif 14 in the name of R. Meir of Lublin, which implies as I wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>It is forbidden to sell it.</b> Unless they wish to buy another with its proceeds in a manner where there is no concern for negligence, or they wish to buy a stricter sanctity. So it implies in the Ran in the name of the Ramban. See what I wrote in seif 4 in the name of the Rambam, and likewise seif 13, see there, and in the Gemara there. This speaks where they have no other synagogue. See Yoreh Deah, siman 252, seif 1. The Mishnah Berurah wrote in siman 33 that in a place where many people from elsewhere are not commonly found, it is called a village, even though many passersby are commonly found from time to time, since they are not fixed in the city but merely pass through casually. It is called a village. However, any place where many people from elsewhere are commonly found and come to pray there, such as a place where merchants gather regularly for commerce, or a place where there is a great scholar to whom many who need him and his Torah gather, is called a large city, for presumably they built it with the entire world in mind. But if it is clear to us that they built it only with the townspeople in mind, and others did not assist them, it is called a village. Although from the language of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham it implies that whenever many come to pray there, presumably the townspeople consecrated it with them in mind and we do not require that the townspeople’s intent at the time of construction be for the many, nevertheless from the language of Tosafot, the Ramban, and the Ran, it is explicit that the matter depends on the time of construction. Perhaps the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham also hold this way, only they were brief in their language. End quote. I did not understand this, for according to everyone, even if they built without specification, we say that they built it with the whole world in mind.

For example, one instructed that this house should belong to the poor so that they eat its fruits, and instructed that the community not evade this. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>From the hire of a harlot.</b> It is clear in the fifth chapter of Avodah Zarah that if he cohabited with her and afterward gave her the hire, it is permitted, for at the time of cohabitation it was not acquired by her, and what he gave her afterward was merely a gift. But if he gave it to her and afterward cohabited with her, it is forbidden. If the woman gave him hire, the hire is permitted. If he gave hire to a male to lie with him, the hire is forbidden. If she made a change, such as wheat that she made into fine flour, it is permitted, and their offspring are also permitted. One who says to a harlot, take this lamb and cohabit with so-and-so, it is forbidden. Likewise, if he agreed with her for one lamb and gave her many, all are forbidden. All this is in Rabbeinu Yerucham. If the lamb was in her courtyard at the time of cohabitation, it is forbidden, for she acquired it at the time of cohabitation. Even if he made it an apotiki for her, saying: if I bring you money from now until such-and-such day, good, and if not, take the lamb as your hire, it is forbidden, since it stands in her courtyard. The Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Issurei Mizbeach: if he gave it to her and did not cohabit with her, but said to her, let it be with you until I come to you, when he cohabits with her it is forbidden. For if he gave it to her without specification from now, he merely gave her a gift. Tosafot. If she preceded and consecrated it before he cohabited with her, and afterward he cohabited with her, this is a doubt whether it is the hire of a harlot; therefore it may not be offered, but if it was offered, it is accepted. This is where he said to her at the time he gave it: whenever you wish, acquire it from now. But if he did not say this to her, she cannot offer something that is not hers. End quote. So it is in the Gemara. It appears to me that regarding a synagogue we adopt leniency, for it is only D'Rabbanan; the verse speaks specifically of the Beis HaMikdash, as it is written, the house of Hashem your G-d, and so it implies in the Gemara. Also, all the poskim did not mention this matter except Rabbeinu Yerucham. Therefore we adopt leniency in its doubt. Furthermore, the Rambam ruled that if it was offered, it is accepted. If so, for us regarding a synagogue, even initially we may bring it. Similar to this appears in siman 272 regarding wine for kiddush; see there.

If among the ten there is one whose value is like the value of the dog or more than its value, he removes it from the ten and the rest are permitted. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>A prohibition of ervah.</b> A non-Jewish harlot, a maidservant, or those forbidden by negative commandments. Rambam and Rabbeinu Yerucham. It appears to me that since he used the phrase a non-Jewish harlot and did not say simply a non-Jewish woman, it implies specifically where someone had already cohabited with her, making her a harlot. If so, when another afterward cohabited with her and gave her hire, it is the hire of a harlot. So Rashi wrote on page 76a. So too Tosafot wrote in the sixth chapter of Temurah, beginning of s.v. the first version, and so too in Yevamos 61a, end of the page. Their language is: Rava disagrees only where she committed harlotry, for he derives it from a Jewish harlot; but if she did not commit harlotry, he agrees that she is not forbidden to a kohen as a harlot according to the Torah, for desecration does not apply to her. But where she converted, where marriage acquisition applies to her, she is forbidden according to the Torah even if she did not commit harlotry. End quote. So too they wrote in Avodah Zarah 36b and in Bava Kama 70b in Tosafot. In the Gemara it implies that kehuna and hire are equivalent, for in Temurah Rava derives them from one another through a common inference. The Rambam follows his own view, for he holds that those forbidden by negative commandments are also called a harlot, as written in Even HaEzer, siman 7, and therefore he wrote here as well that it is hire. But a woman who is promiscuous is permitted to a kohen, as he wrote there, and so it is in the Gemara in Yevamos 61. Therefore it is not hire, as he wrote here in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. But Tosafot and the Rosh hold that specifically those liable to kareis make her a harlot, but those forbidden by negative commandments are not called a harlot; rather, they are forbidden to a kohen from another verse, as Tosafot wrote in Hacholtz, page 44b, see there, and the questions of the Beis Yosef in Avodah Zarah will be resolved. Therefore they wrote in Temurah that if he cohabited with those liable to kareis, it is hire even without specification, since through that cohabitation he made her a harlot. But where she had already become a harlot and another cohabited with her, it is not hire unless he said to her explicitly: take this as your hire. Now the Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe: see in Avodah Zarah which one is called a harlot. End quote. His intent is that although Tosafot wrote here that with a promiscuous woman it is hire, we do not rule like them, but like Rabbeinu Yerucham, for the Gemara explicitly implies that hire and kehuna are equivalent, and the words of Tosafot require resolution. He wrote here a prohibition of ervah, meaning specifically those liable to kareis. Since there is a dispute regarding those forbidden by negative commandments, we adopt leniency here, for the prohibition is D'Rabbanan, as I wrote above. He does not discuss a non-Jewish woman here, for it is not the way of a non-Jewish woman to consecrate something. This is clear to me. See the book Be'er HaZevach, and see Bechoros 56b.
