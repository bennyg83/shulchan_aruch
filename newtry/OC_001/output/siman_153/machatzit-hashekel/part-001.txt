**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 4, some say, and the Bach wrote, etc.</b> At first glance, the “some say” written in the Shulchan Aruch are the Ran, who forbids, and Mahari Abuhav is the one who permits. For the Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Ran that from the question in Megillah 27a, “May one sell an old Torah scroll in order to buy a new one? Since he does not elevate it, is it forbidden, or perhaps since there is nothing higher to which to elevate it, etc., it is proper?” and it concludes that where there is concern for negligence it is certainly forbidden. The question is only where it is already written and lying in the scribe’s house and nothing is lacking except payment of the money; what is the law in such a case? It was not resolved. On this the Ran wrote that therefore the Talmud was uncertain whether to permit buying an equal sanctity only with respect to a Torah scroll, since there is nothing higher in sanctity than a Torah scroll. But regarding other things, where one can elevate them to a stricter sanctity, it was never uncertain, for it is certainly forbidden. The Beis Yosef then brought as follows: Mahari Abuhav wrote that the words of the Tur, who wrote like the language of the Shulchan Aruch in seif 2, “but the opposite, to lower them from their sanctity, no,” imply that we are particular only that they not lower them from their sanctity, but to buy something equal to them is permitted. End quote. From this it implies that the Beis Yosef held that the Ran and Mahari Abuhav disagree, and these are the two opinions brought in the Shulchan Aruch. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that the Bach wrote not like the Beis Yosef, but that the Ran and Mahari Abuhav do not disagree. Initially it is certainly forbidden, and Mahari Abuhav agrees; the Ran speaks about this, for he brings proof from the above question, which speaks initially.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif katan 12, the great city, etc.</b> To explain the matter: in Megillah they say that a village synagogue may be sold, but a city synagogue may not be sold, because it belongs to many people. The Rishonim disagree whether the reason is that outsiders contributed money to its building, or that the residents built it with the intention of all who come to pray there. According to the first reason, if they built it entirely from their own money, they may sell it. According to the second reason, even if they built it from their own money, they may not sell it, because they built it for the sake of all who come from elsewhere to pray there. The halacha follows the stringent view. The Talmud also says that Rav Ashi said of the synagogue of Mata Mechasya that although people came to it from elsewhere, since they came relying on him, if he wished he could sell it. From this the Tur wrote the law cited in the Shulchan Aruch, that a city synagogue may not be sold unless they made it dependent on the opinion of an individual. The Beis Yosef brought the words of Mahari Chaviv, who asked: if this condition was at the time of building, why say that they made it dependent on the opinion of an individual? Even if it was dependent on their own opinion they could sell it. Perhaps because it was a large city, one must be concerned that one person might be absent at the time of sale. He wrote that this is not sufficient, for there is a difficulty, and from the words of the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef we learn the true law, that this applies specifically to a unique person of the generation like Rav Ashi, whose opinion all Israel accept, but there is no substance when they make it dependent on one ordinary individual, and even if they make it dependent on the opinion of all of them it does not help. The Beis Yosef wrote that this explanation cannot be placed into the words of the Tur, and therefore he explained that when the Rosh wrote specifically Rav Ashi, a unique person of the generation, that is because then even without explicit condition we assume that all agree to his opinion. But if they explicitly made it dependent on the opinion of an individual, even the least of people, it helps; and the same applies if they made it dependent on their own opinion. As to why the Tur stated “dependent on the opinion of an individual,” as the manuscript asked, the Beis Yosef gave one answer: he stated the usual case, for usually many people do not agree to one opinion. Now we can come to the words of the Magen Avraham. Since the simple implication of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which wrote “but of cities, even if they built it from their own funds,” from the word “even” implies all the more so if they built it from others’ funds, and on this it continues, “unless they made it dependent on the opinion of the individual,” implying that this applies to both cases, even if it was built from others’ funds. Therefore the Magen Avraham needed to say that this is not so.

<b>אבל אם עשה מטפחת כו'</b> יכול לחזור בו. דכה"ג שלא אמר ליתן לאחרים ליכא משום נדר ומ"מ צ"ע דאית ביה משום אמירתו לגבוה אף דלית ביה משום צדקה מ"מ לא גרע משאר מצוה וכדקי"ל ביורה דעה סי' רי"ג סעיף ב' האומר אשנה פרק זה הוי כאלו נדר לעשות צדקה או לעשות שאר מצות ונדרו קיים ופי' הש"ך מ"ש לעשות שאר מצות כשאומר לעשות שאר מצות דינו ג"כ כאומר אשנה פרק זה והוי נדר עכ"ל. ומרדכי דאמר הטעם משום צדקה עדיפ' מיניה נקט. ובאמת ראיתי בספר א"ר הניח דין זה בצריך עיון ולא ביאר טעמו ואי איירי שלא אמר כלום בשעת עשייתו רק מחשבתו היתה לכך א"כ כה"ג בבית הכנסת אמאי אינו יכול לחזור ועיין בא"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if he made a cloth, etc.</b> He can retract. For in such a case, where he did not say he would give it to others, there is no vow. Nevertheless, this requires study, for it involves “his statement to the Most High” even though it does not involve tzedakah. In any event, it is no worse than another mitzvah, as we rule in Yoreh Deah, siman 213, seif 2: one who says, “I will study this chapter,” is as though he vowed to give tzedakah or to do other mitzvos, and his vow stands. The Shach explained that what it says, “to do other mitzvos,” means that when he says he will do other mitzvos, his law is also like one who says “I will study this chapter,” and it is a vow. The Mordechai, who gave the reason of tzedakah, mentioned the stronger reason. In truth, I saw that the Eliyah Rabbah left this law requiring study and did not explain his reason. If the case is where he said nothing at the time he made it, and only his thought was for this, then in such a case why can he not retract with a synagogue? See Orach Chaim.
