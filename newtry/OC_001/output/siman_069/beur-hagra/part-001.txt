**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And nowadays, etc.</b> The reason they would recite the first blessing is, as stated in siman 59, because of the Kedushah contained in it, according to the second opinion there in seif 3. According to the first opinion there, however, which the gloss there rules in accordance with, the Beit Yosef was pressed here and agreed in Darkei Moshe with Rabbeinu Yonah's explanation, that it was to discharge the obligation of one who was not proficient, as stated in seif 4. Nowadays, however, we are all proficient, and it is needed only in order to answer Kaddish and Barechu. This is certainly so when they have already recited the blessings of Krias Shema. See there. The reference to one who is not proficient is not exact. Rather, it means one who wishes to discharge his obligation through another, whereas nowadays people cannot concentrate properly while listening, as stated there in seif 4.

<b>וצריך כו'. </b>כמ"ש בספ"י דמ"ס וא"א קדיש וברכו בפחות מעשרה רבותינו שבמערב אומרים בשבעה ונ"ט לדבריהם בפרוע פרעות בישראל בהתנדב עם ברכו ה' וי"א אפי' בששה עד ברכו ופי' תוספת דל"פ את"ק ואמתניתין אלא דמיירי כאן כמה יהיו אותן שלא שמעו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And it is necessary, etc.</b> As stated at the end of chapter 10 of Maseches Soferim: 'Kaddish and Barechu may not be recited with fewer than ten. Our rabbis in the West say with seven,' and they give as support for their view, 'When breaches are repaired in Israel, when the people volunteer, bless Hashem.' Some say even with six, through Barechu. Tosafos explain that they do not disagree with the first Tanna or with our Mishnah. Rather, the discussion here concerns how many of those present have not yet heard these prayers.

**** HEBREW ****
<b>ואם אינם כו' ואפי'. </b>כ"כ התוס' וש"פ בשם תלמידי רש"י שכ' בשם רש"י וכן מסיים שם במ"ס ובמקום שיש שם תשעה או עשרה ששמעו בין ברכו ובין קדיש ולאחר התפלה עומד א' מאלו ויאמר ברכו או קדיש וענו אחריו יצא ור"ל תשעה שאפילו בשביל א' ואמר עשרה שיש עוד א' שלא שמע יכול לפרוס א' מאלו עשרה והביאו ראיה מש"ץ וירד לפני התיבה ואמרינן בסוף ר"ה לר"ג להוציא אפילו הבקי ואף שהש"ץ כבר התפלל ומ"ש במ"ס בתחלה רבותינו שבמערב כו' היינו אפילו אין בכולם רק שבעה וחולק על הת"ק וכ"מ הלשון שם שאמר וא"א כו' רבותינו כו' מ' דפליגי וכן הסכים הרא"ש אלא שר"ת לא רצה לסמוך ע"ז כפי פי' במ"ס כנ"ל לכן כ' בש"ע וצריך כו' ואם אינם כו' דדברי רש"י הם עיקר וכן הסכימו הפוסקים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if they are not, etc., and even.</b> Tosafos and the other poskim write this in the name of Rashi's disciples, who wrote it in Rashi's name. Maseches Soferim likewise concludes there: 'In a place where there are nine or ten who have heard both Barechu and Kaddish, after the prayer one of them stands and recites Barechu or Kaddish, and they answer him, and he has fulfilled the requirement.' The meaning of 'nine' is that this may be done even for the sake of one person. It then says 'ten,' meaning that if there is one additional person who did not hear, one of those ten may lead the responsive recitation. They brought proof from the shaliach tzibbur who passes before the ark, since at the end of Rosh Hashanah we say that Rabban Gamliel's enactment was to discharge even one who is proficient, although the shaliach tzibbur has already prayed. The earlier statement in Maseches Soferim, 'Our rabbis in the West,' etc., means that this may be done even when there are only seven people altogether, and it disputes the first Tanna. This is also implied by the wording there, which first says, 'Kaddish may not be recited,' etc., and then, 'Our rabbis,' etc., implying a dispute. The Rosh likewise agrees. Rabbeinu Tam, however, did not wish to rely on this according to the above explanation of Maseches Soferim. The Shulchan Aruch therefore writes, 'And it is necessary,' etc., followed by, 'And if they are not,' etc., because Rashi's view is primary, and the poskim agreed with it.
