<b>According to Rashi.</b> And Rambam. See Ateres Zekeinim and Menachos 34b. Three views exist, though the poskim mention only two.

Rashi and Rambam maintain that Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are on the wearer's left, while Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa follow them in Torah order. Rabbeinu Tam also places Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha on the wearer's left, but places Shema in the outer compartment on the wearer's right and Vehayah Im Shamoa beside it. The reader then reads them in the order of their compartments.

A third view is Raavad's. He explains that Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are on the wearer's right, while Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa are on his left, so that the reader standing opposite reads Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa in their Torah order. This also gives special meaning to the baraisa's phrase “one who reads reads them in order.”

A support is the verse, “The Name of Hashem is called upon you, and they shall fear you,” since Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa contain acceptance of the yoke of Heaven.

Nevertheless, we follow only Rabbeinu Tam's alternative to Rashi. It is surprising that Beit Yosef did not mention Raavad, perhaps because he regarded the view as rejected. Therefore one should make only two pairs, as Shulchan Aruch states, not three.

<b>And he should put on.</b> Taz discusses the matter at length. Bach and Tosafos Yom Tov maintain that two pairs involve bal tosif. Taz holds that two pairs do not involve Torah-level bal tosif, though there is a D'Rabbanan concern. Where Torah-level bal tosif applies, it is forbidden even without intent; where it is only D'Rabbanan, lack of intent permits it when needed.

Eruvin 96b appears to say that the first Tanna permits only one pair because bal tosif does not require intent. Taz explains that we do not follow that explanation, but the one that makes their dispute depend upon whether Shabbos is a time for tefillin.

Even there the concern is only D'Rabbanan and without intent. Taz answers that the second pair is also a burden on Shabbos; although neither factor alone would suffice, the appearance of bal tosif joins the carrying concern.

According to the later explanation that the first Tanna denies there is room on the head for two pairs, the prohibition follows simply from carrying and not bal tosif.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nevertheless.</b> Taz's assumption that Torah-level bal tosif applies even without intent requires study. That is true where one acts ordinarily during the proper time. If he explicitly states that he acts only because of doubt, there is no bal tosif, as many sources show.

For example, when blood requiring four applications is mixed with blood requiring one application, the additional applications are made as water, without violating bal tosif.

The Sanhedrin passage may likewise mean that a separate fifth compartment involves bal tosif upon the person but does not invalidate the original four compartments, whereas the law of a rebellious elder requires that the addition also detract and invalidate the mitzvah. When the fifth compartment is attached, it causes the outer compartment not to face the air and invalidates the entire tefillin. The matter requires study. See Tosafos there and what I wrote in the general introduction concerning bal tosif.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>What is written.</b> See the responsum cited in Ateres Zekeinim, letter 54, which resolves the difficulty and that of Tosafos Yom Tov.

Our practice is to put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin only after removing Rashi tefillin, as explained in siman 25, seif 13, and to recite with them Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa. The tzitzis passage is unnecessary, because the concern of reciting Krias Shema without tefillin is remedied by the two passages.

Some recent practitioners remove Rashi tefillin and put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin after Kedushah in the repetition of the Amidah. Besides acting contrary to siman 25, seif 13, they must listen to the chazzan's repetition and may not occupy themselves with another matter. I did not wish to ask them for their explanation, nor do I desire their answer.
