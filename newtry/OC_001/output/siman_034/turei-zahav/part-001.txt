<b>לרש"י והרמב"ם וכו'. </b> בהא פליגי רש"י ור"ת דת"ר כיצד סודרן קדש והיה כי יביאך מימין שמע והיה אם שמוע משמאל ומפרשי' לה אחר כך דהך ימין הוא ימין הקורא שהוא עומד נגד המניח ונמצא שהוא שמאל המניח וס"ל לרש"י דכמו שקדש והיה כ"י וגו' הוא סדר הנחתן זא"ז דהיינו תחלה קדש לשמאל המניח ואחריו והיה כ"י ה"נ שמע אחר והיה כ"י ופ' והיה אם שמוע אחר שמע נמצא של ימין המניח הוא והיה א"ש אבל ר"ת פי' דשמע והיה אם שמוע קאי אצד השני פי' מצד שמאל המניח קדש והי' כ"י ומצד ימין הוא שמע והי' א"ש פי' שמצד החיצון השני שהוא ימין המניח הוה שמע וסמוך לו והיה א"ש סי' לדבר הוויות להדדי ושיני"ן דהיינו קדש ושמע אצל השיני"ן שבצדדי בתים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>According to Rashi and Rambam, etc.</b> Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam dispute the baraisa: “How are they arranged? Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha on the right; Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa on the left.” The Gemara explains that “right” means the reader's right, as he stands facing the wearer, which is the wearer's left.

Rashi understands that just as Kadesh is followed by Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, so Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is followed by Shema, and Shema by Vehayah Im Shamoa. Thus Vehayah Im Shamoa is on the wearer's right.

Rabbeinu Tam understands that Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa refer to the opposite side: Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are on the wearer's left, while on the wearer's right the outer compartment contains Shema and beside it Vehayah Im Shamoa. A mnemonic is that the two passages beginning Vehayah are adjacent, and Kadesh and Shema, whose initial letters are shin, are beside the shins on the outer sides of the batim.

<b>And he should put on both and intend, etc.</b> Tur cites Rosh that there is no bal tosif here unless one makes five compartments. This initially suggests that wearing two pairs does not involve bal tosif.

The chapter HaMotzei Tefillin, however, states that on Shabbos one may bring in only one pair at a time, not two, because bal tosif would make the second pair a burden. Me'adanei Melech reconciles this with Sanhedrin: a separate fifth compartment stands independently and does not create Torah-level bal tosif, whereas there may still be a D'Rabbanan prohibition. Taz accepts this distinction.

Thus, with two pairs there is no Torah-level bal tosif because each stands independently, but there is a D'Rabbanan prohibition when both are intended as mitzvah tefillin. Rosh therefore requires intent that one pair fulfill the mitzvah and the other be like mere straps.

Taz was asked which pair should be placed lower toward the face. He rules that Rashi tefillin must occupy the primary lower position and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin the higher position.

The statement that there is room on the head for two pairs is not entirely unquestioned. The dispute in Eruvin can be explained either as concerning whether there is room for two pairs or whether Shabbos is a time for tefillin. Since the halachic rulings appear to follow different sides of these explanations, one should act safely: Rashi tefillin should be in the primary valid place, and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin above them.

The same applies to the arm: Rashi tefillin should occupy the primary place and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin the additional area. This also teaches that one who wears only one pair should place it toward the face, not high above, because perhaps there is only one valid tefillin position.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he does not know how to position them.</b> This is Semag's view concerning one who regards Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin as equally doubtful. He puts on one pair first, even if Rabbeinu Tam tefillin happen to come first, despite the resulting slight interruption.

The later clause, “and if he cannot put them on,” comes from the German responsum cited by Beit Yosef, which regards Rashi tefillin as primary. According to it, he must recite the blessing specifically over Rashi tefillin and afterward put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin without a blessing. Semag himself likewise concludes that the lands of Edom and Ishmael follow Rashi.

It is difficult why Shulchan Aruch first cites the earlier procedure, which conflicts with the later one. The text should read: “Some say that if he cannot put them on, etc.” The two approaches are disputed opinions, not one explanation of the other.
