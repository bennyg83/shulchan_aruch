<b>א) [סעיף א'] דגן שנטבע בנהר וכו'  </b>ויש בהם חשש חימוץ ואינו ניכר בהם. טור. לבוש. משמע דאם החמץ ניכר מותר (למכור אפי' הרבה לגוי א' ר"ז או' ד') ולא חיישינן שמא יטחנם וימכרם כיון שאין לוקחים קמח מן השוק אלא בשעת הדחק כמ"ש סי' תנ"ג ודלא כעו"ש. מ"א ריש הסי' מיהו הר"ן בפ' כ"ש כתב דאפי' נתבקעו שהחימוץ ניכר בהם אפ"ה אסור למכור לעכו"ם דחיישינן שמא יטחן אותם ואח"כ ימכור לישראל עכ"ל וכ"כ העו"ש או' א' ותמה על ל' הטור דמשמע דאם החמץ ניכר מותר יעו"ש וזהו שכתב מ"א ודלא כעו"ש. ודברי הר"ן הנז' הביאו ח"י או' א' והקשה על דברי המ"א הנז' דמ"מ חיישינן שמא יקח הישראל אותו קמח קו"פ ויקיימו בביתו וקעבר על ב"י וכתב דכוונת הטור דאע"ג שאין החמץ ניכר ויודע בו רק שיש חשש חמץ אפ"ה חיישינן לתקלה בעניין המכירה ומכ"ש בחמץ ידוע וניכר דחיישינן לכתחלה יעו"ש אמנם הא"ר או' א' כתב לתרץ קושיית ח"י דס"ל למ"א דאי משום זה לא גזרו דהא מבטלו במה שאומר דלא חזיתיה יעו"ש וגם המק"ח בביאורים הסכים לדברי מ"א יעו"ש. והער"ה או' א' כתב להתיר למכור לגוי מכח ס"ס שמא ימכור לגוי ואת"ל ימכור לישראל שמא ימכרנו חטים שהוא ניכר יעו"ש אבל הרב ב"ד סי' רל"ב הסכים לדברי הר"ן יעו"ש. נמצא דדבר זה בפלוגתא שניא וע"כ לכתחלה אם אפשר יש למכור מעט לכל אחד ואחד או למכרו בענין שיחזירו לו אחר הפסח כמ"ש לעיל סי' תמ"ח סעי' ג' ואם א"א ויש הפ"מ אז יש לסמוך על המתירין. וכ"כ הר"ז או' ד' דבהפ"מ יש לסמוך על המתירין.
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>1) [Se'if 1] Grain that was submerged in a river, etc. </b>There is concern that some kernels became chametz, although this cannot be identified. Tur; Levush. This implies that if the chametz is identifiable, it may be sold, even in a large quantity to one non-Jew according to Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4. We are not concerned that the non-Jew might mill and resell it, since flour is purchased in the marketplace only in pressing circumstances, as stated in siman 453. This is contrary to Olas Shabbos. Magen Avraham, at the beginning of this siman. Ran in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, however, writes that even when kernels split and the chametz is identifiable, they may not be sold to a non-Jew, lest the non-Jew mill and then sell them to a Jew. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes likewise and questions Tur's implication that identifiable chametz may be sold. Magen Avraham therefore writes that his ruling is contrary to Olas Shabbos. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, cites Ran and questions Magen Avraham: we should still be concerned that a Jew might purchase the flour before Pesach, retain it at home, and violate the prohibition against chametz being seen. He explains that Tur means that even though the chametz cannot be identified and one knows only that there is a concern of chametz, we nevertheless fear a mishap when it is sold. This concern certainly applies initially when the chametz is known and identifiable. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, resolves Chok Yaakov's objection by explaining that Magen Avraham holds that the Sages did not issue a decree on this account, since the Jew nullifies it by declaring all unseen chametz ownerless. See there. Mekor Chaim in the explanations likewise agrees with Magen Avraham. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, permits selling it to a non-Jew because of a compound doubt: perhaps the non-Jew will sell it to another non-Jew, and even if it is sold to a Jew, perhaps it will be sold as visibly affected wheat. See there. Beis David, siman 232, however, agrees with Ran. See there. The matter is therefore disputed. Initially, if possible, one should sell a small amount to each buyer or sell it under an arrangement requiring its return after Pesach, as stated above in siman 448, se'if 3. If this is impossible and a substantial loss is involved, one may rely upon the permitting authorities. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, likewise writes that one may rely upon them where a substantial loss is involved.

<b>4) There. And inform the buyer, etc. </b>This is Rambam's language in chapter 5. Beis Yosef writes that according to Rambam, selling one kav at a time applies only when selling to a non-Jew. When selling to a Jew without informing the buyer, even one kav at a time is prohibited, because the Jew might retain it to eat during Pesach. If the buyer is informed, even a large quantity may be sold. Maharshal, however, writes that even after informing the Jewish buyer, selling a large quantity is prohibited. Bach, Taz, subsection 2, Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, agree. Peri Chadash, however, agrees with Beis Yosef: without notification, even one kav at a time is prohibited lest the buyer retain it for Pesach; with notification, even a large quantity is permitted. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1; Chok Yaakov, subsection 3; Chok Yosef, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Chayei Adam, rule 126, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2, agree.

<b>8) There. So that it will be consumed before Pesach, etc. </b>Even if the non-Jew sells that small amount to a Jew, the Jew will consume it before Pesach. This implies that during the fifth hour on the eve of Pesach, when it cannot be consumed before Pesach, it is forbidden to sell even small amounts to a non-Jew lest they be sold to a Jew. This also appears from the Talmud, Rambam, and the other authorities. Nevertheless, if a large amount of a Jew's grain became chametz on the eve of Pesach, it cannot be sold to non-Jews in quantities that will be consumed before Pesach, and a substantial loss is involved, one may sell it to a known non-Jew who will not touch it but will guard it until after Pesach. One may tell the buyer, 'Instead of buying for one maneh,' and so forth, as explained above in siman 448, se'ifim 3 and 4. There is then no concern. Chok Yaakov, there; Chok Yosef, subsection 3; Mekor Chaim, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3.

<b>יא) שם. וימכרנו לישראל.  </b>מעשה בישראל שלקח חטים מעכו"ם ובא אח"כ ישראל אחר וביקש מהעכו"ם שימכור לו ג"כ חטים ואמר אין לי חטים כי היהודי שמכרתי לו הוא אוהבי ומכרתי לו החטים שרחצתי ובקשתי לטוחנן לצורך ביתי ומכרתי אותם לאהבתי אותו. נ"ל דדמי למ"ש ביו"ד סי' ט"ז סעי' י"א דאין העכו"ם נאמן במה שאמר לאחר שמכרם ויצאו מתחת ידו מיהו אי מהימן ליה אסור עכ"ל. מ"א סק"ד ח"י או' ה' א"ר או' ג' י"א בהגה"ט. חק יוסף או' ד' מק"ח או' ה' וכתב שם ח"י משמע דקודם שמכרם כיון שהוא בידו נאמן וזה אינו דכיון די"ל שנתכוין להשביח מקחו אינו נאמן יעו"ש. אבל חק יוסף שם השיג על דברי ח"י הנז' וכתב בשם כמה פו' דבעודם בידו נאמן לאסור אפי' היכא דאיכא למימר דלהשביח מקחו אומר יעו"ש. וכ"כ בס' זבחי צדק על יו"ד סי' ט"ז או' ל"ד בשם כמה פו' יעו"ש.

<b>11) There. And sell it to a Jew. </b>An incident occurred in which a Jew purchased wheat from a non-Jew. Another Jew later asked the non-Jew to sell wheat to him as well. The non-Jew said, 'I have no wheat. The Jew to whom I sold it is my friend, and I sold him the wheat that I had washed and intended to mill for my household, because of my affection for him.' It appears to me that this resembles the law in Yoreh De'ah, siman 16, se'if 11: after the non-Jew sold the wheat and it left the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is not believed. If the Jew personally considers the non-Jew trustworthy, however, it is prohibited. Magen Avraham, subsection 4; Chok Yaakov, subsection 5; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Chok Yosef, subsection 4; Mekor Chaim, subsection 5. Chok Yaakov writes there that this implies that before the wheat is sold, while still in the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is believed. He rejects this, because the non-Jew may be speaking to improve the sale. See there. Chok Yosef there, however, challenges Chok Yaakov and writes in the name of several authorities that while the product remains in the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is believed to prohibit it even where the statement might have been made to improve the sale. See there. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh De'ah, siman 16, subsection 34, writes likewise in the name of several authorities. See there.

<b>12) [Se'if 2] This grain that was submerged in a river, etc. </b>Beis Yosef cites authorities who distinguish between wheat and barley. Barley must split, whereas wheat, which naturally has a crease, is considered split. He concludes, however, that Rif and Rambam make no distinction between wheat and barley, and this is the ruling to follow. This also appears from the unqualified language of the Shulchan Aruch here, which prohibits every type of grain even when it did not split. Olas Shabbos, subsection 2, writes likewise. This also follows from the later authorities' unqualified use of the word grain, implying that every type of grain is prohibited even when it did not split.

<b>15) There. It is prohibited even if it did not split, etc. </b>Although regarding soaking grain it is prohibited only if the kernels split, that case differs because one is actively handling them. Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim; Magen Avraham, subsection 3. Below in se'if 9, the Shulchan Aruch permits a cooked dish if a kernel did not actually split, even though the kernel rests undisturbed in the food. This may be because the cooked dish is hot and therefore resembles scalding, which does not cause leavening according to Tur, simanim 461 and 467. See there. We are concerned only that the kernel may have been in the water before it boiled, and therefore are not so stringent. Another and primary explanation is that a minute admixture in a cooked dish is only rabbinically prohibited, and we are not so stringent, as Magen Avraham writes in subsection 12. See there. Based upon Rambam, chapter 5, the reason appears straightforward: grain that was mixed with water or cooked but did not actually split is not chametz under Torah law. The prohibition of a minute admixture is also rabbinic, and we do not impose two rabbinic stringencies. Chok Yaakov, subsection 7. He writes there that all this explains the authorities and the Shulchan Aruch, but in practice Rema concludes in the gloss to se'if 9 that the custom is to prohibit under all circumstances. See there. See our words there, with the help of Heaven.

<b>16) There. It is prohibited even if it did not split, etc. </b>This means that even when we see no defect and the kernel is not at all close to splitting, it is nevertheless prohibited. It is unlike soaked grain, which is actively handled. Therefore, placing it upon the opening of a barrel or using a similar test to determine whether it became chametz does not help. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 4.
