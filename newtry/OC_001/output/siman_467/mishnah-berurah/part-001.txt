**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(15) We are not concerned, etc.</b> So long as we do not see in them a sign of chametzing, because we follow the majority and the majority do not become chametz; and even those heaps on which we saw that rains fell, we attribute for leniency: perhaps the rains entered only the upper sheaves but not the belly of the heap; and even if they entered, perhaps they did not manage to become chametz. And nevertheless if one sees in the wheat brought before him that there are sprouted ones among them, even the Mechaber agrees that one must check if there is sixty against them; and it is plain that when they take the wheat to sell and rains fall on the sacks until the sack is almost entirely soaked in water and the wheat too became moist, they are forbidden by law on Pesach, for the defect is revealed to the eye; and one must warn about this, for the sellers are not careful about this since most are not learned in Torah; and one must also warn grain sellers in a year when rains were abundant or when water came down on the sacks: then they are obligated to sell the grain as the law of selling chametz, and they are forbidden to sell of them to a non-Jew after the time of its prohibition (Chayei Adam).

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(25) Not to eat honey except, etc.</b> Meaning that regarding this honey that they bring in a barrel from the hive with wax still mixed in it they practice to eat it even plain, and likewise to make from it a mead drink, because it is not the way to forge; and even to make from it a mead drink on Pesach is permitted for Yom Tov joy if he has no other drink; but what they bring in small vessels cleaned of wax: the non-Jew's way is to mix flour into them, and therefore it is forbidden to eat it and even to make a drink from it on Pesach. And some later authorities are stringent in this even before Pesach because it is common that they mix much flour into them until even in the water mixture there will not be sixty against the flour to nullify them; and likewise they are stringent not to eat honey plain on Pesach even from those that come from large barrels, lest they come to confuse and be lenient with all honey (except those they bring with the small combs whole as they rest in the hive that have not yet been crushed). And for practice regarding making a mead drink before Pesach it appears from the later authorities that now they practice to be lenient even from those that come in small vessels, for we attribute that the flour will be nullified in sixty (unless it is known that in those places the way is to mix much flour); and nevertheless one who is stringent in this not to make a mead drink except from honey that comes in a barrel with the wax, may blessing come upon him. And regarding eating honey plain on Pesach that comes from barrels it depends on the custom of the places; and in our land the custom is to be lenient. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>(49) In which the wheat kernel was found.</b> In which it was found when baked: so the text should read. And if the wheat kernel is full of water and moist through and through, then it is recognizable that it was in water and in dough: one should forbid the whole dough; and nevertheless if one can attribute that it was absorbed from elsewhere from water and fell into the matzah, all according to the circumstances, for we attribute to what is common whether for leniency or stringency, and it is not called a doubt at all. And one who had dough in hand and cut several matzos from it and gave to others and one matzah remained in his hand and a wheat kernel was found on it: all the matzos from that dough are forbidden, for we say perhaps it was in that dough before it was cut into pieces; for even though we do not establish a presumption of prohibition from place to place and we say "here it was found," etc., and not from piece to piece: from time to time we do establish a presumption of prohibition and say perhaps it was in this dough before it was cut (Magen Avraham and other later authorities). And some later authorities hold that even here one may be lenient for the other matzos because of a double doubt: perhaps it fell now, and even if beforehand, perhaps it did not become chametz until now; and in a place of major financial loss one may rely to be lenient. And nevertheless in a place of major financial loss one may be lenient even if found in the barrel for those householders who took beforehand, for there is a double doubt: perhaps it fell now in the barrel, and even if beforehand, perhaps the wheat kernel did not become chametz until now: unless when they took it immediately it was found in the barrel and there was no time to become chametz in that time. And some later authorities wrote that all this is when the wheat kernel is cracked open; but if it did not crack open but only softened one may permit even the cabbage in the barrel if there is major financial loss by force of a double doubt. And if the brine does not rise over the cabbage, anyway one may permit the cabbage in the barrel, for it does not have the status of pickled but only the law of salted, and only the place where it was found on it is forbidden and the rest is permitted as the Rama wrote in the gloss in se'if 14. And know that all this, that the Rama is lenient for the other pieces through peeling or through sale to a non-Jew, is specifically when the wheat kernel is found on salted meat at the time of salting or after salting and rinsing and they salted a second time for the pot and found the wheat kernel on the meat without brine; but if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine then everything is considered like cooked, for in a small measure it is pickled through brine, and then all that rests in the brine one must forbid and it requires destruction; but what rests above the brine one should do as the Rama wrote, for in brine we do not say it spreads upward, or keep it until after Pesach as we wrote above according to Taz's view. And likewise if a wheat kernel is found lying in a barrel of salted cabbage the whole barrel is forbidden, for pickled is like cooked; and specifically when the brine rises over it; for if the brine does not rise over it it has merely the law of salting, and its law is as above in s.k. 76 and 77. If a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water in it and rinsed meat in it after its salting on Pesach: Chok Yaakov's view is that one may permit the meat even without peeling according to the basic law, for even though the water was forbidden because of the beer mixed into it, nevertheless the meat absorbed nothing from this water since it is cold; and even though the meat was still salted when they rinsed and salted is like hot: nevertheless it absorbed nothing from the water because the water they rinsed with nullifies the power of the salt and it has no power to cause the water to be absorbed into the meat; but because of the stringency of chametz on Pesach one should peel a little of the meat where this water touched it; and Chayei Adam's view is that by law the meat needs peeling since there are cracks in it, and even if the meat was not salted at all.
