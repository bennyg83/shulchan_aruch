This appears correct to me, unlike Olas Shabbos. <b>And he informs him.</b> But one may not rely upon a public announcement. See Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah.

<b>כדי שיכלה</b>. כלו' אף את"ל שימכרנו לישראל יאכלנו הישראל קודם פסח והכ"מ כ' דה"פ דכשאינו קונה אלא קבא קבא לביתו צריך אותו ויכלה קודם הפסח ליכא למיחש למידי עכ"ל וצ"ע דא"כ למה קאמר ויכלה קודם פסח דמשמע דאי ידעי' שלא יכלה קודם פסח כגון שמוכרו בע"פ בשעה ה' אסור ולהכ"מ לישתרי דלצורך ביתו הוא לוקחו ועוד דבע"א ד' ס"ה אי' ג"כ בחטי' שנפל עליהם יי"נ והתירו רבא למכור לעכו"ם ואותבי' רבה בר לואי והדר ביה ואורי למיפינהו וליזבנינהו לעכו"ם שלא בפני ישראל (דאין לוקחים פת מעכו"ם) והקשו התוס' מאחר דכבר אותביה רבה בעובדא דפסחים היאך טעה כאן שני', ותי' התו' דשני המעשים באו לפניו בפעם א' והורה בשניהם למכור לעכו"ם ואותבי' רבה וחזר בו ע"כ וא"כ קשה למה הטריחם שם למפינהו ולזבנינהו ה"ל ג"כ להורות למכור מעט מעט לעכו"ם אלא ע"כ דוקא בחמץ מועיל מעט מעט דאף את"ל שימכור לישראל יאכלנו קודם פסח אבל בשאר איסורים לא מהני מעט מעט דמ"מ חיישינן שימכרם לישראל או יתן במתנה לאוהבו ישראל ועוד דאין לך דבר מועט יותר מביצה ואפ"ה אין מוכרין ביצת נבילה וטריפה לעכו"ם ומשמע מלשון הגמרא והפוסקים אפי' ביצה א' עבי"ד ססי' פ"ו ובסי' פ"ד מביא הרב"י קמח שהתליעה להשליכו לנהר משמע דאין לו תקנה: ומ"ש רמ"א בסי' קנ"ד ס"ה ביי"נ שנתערב מיהו לא ימכור הרבה ביחד שמא יחזור וימכרנו לישראל משמע דמעט מעט ליכא למיחש הנה דברים אלו לקוחים מדברי רי"ו ואני שמעתי ולא אבין דמעולם לא כיון רי"ו לכך דהא התם מרגישין טעם היין ואיך יקח הישראל מעכו"ם אלא כוונתו דלא ימכור הכל ביחד לעכו"ם חוץ מדמי יי"נ שבו ע"ש ברי"ו ותמצא כדברי אבל גם דבריו צ"ע דהתו' ספ"ג דע"א לא כתבו אלא שא"צ למוכרו ביחד חוץ מדמי יי"נ ואם רצה מוכרו ביחד וכ"כ ביבמות ד' פ"א ע"ב ובסוף ע"א ובזבחים דף ע"ב ואין כאן מקומו אבל בדבר דאיכא למיחש שיחזור וימכרנו לישראל אין היתר במעט מעט אלא בחמץ כנ"ל ועבי"ד סי' קי"ב סי"א ובכ"ה כ' בשם רשד"מ בי"ד סי' מ"ה ורי"ט בי"ד סי' ב' וז"ל ואף על גב דהוי ס"ס דשמא יאכלנו הישראל קודם פסח כיון דהוי ס"ס הנעשה בידי' אסור עכ"ל וצ"ע דהא מיירי בחמץ הרבה וי"ל דה"ק דשמא העכו"ם ימכור להרבה ישראלים לכל א' מעט משמע לכאורה דס"ל כפירוש הכ"מ דבמעט תלי' שיאכלנו העכו"ם בעצמו ועבי"ד ס"ס נ"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that it will be consumed.</b> Meaning, even if you say that the non-Jew will sell it to a Jew, that Jew will eat it before Pesach. Kesef Mishneh explains that when he purchases only one kav at a time for his household, he needs it and will consume it before Pesach, and there is therefore no concern. This requires study, for if so, why does the Gemara say that it will be consumed before Pesach, implying that if we know it will not be consumed, such as where it is sold during the fifth hour on Erev Pesach, it is forbidden? According to Kesef Mishneh, it should be permitted because he purchases it for household use. Moreover, Avodah Zarah 65a likewise discusses wheat upon which yayin nesech fell. Rava permitted selling it to a non-Jew, but Rabbah bar Livai objected, so Rava retracted and instructed that it be sifted and sold to a non-Jew not in the presence of a Jew, since Jews do not purchase bread from non-Jews. Tosafot ask how he could have erred there after Rabbah had already objected in the incident in Pesachim. They answer that both incidents came before him at one time, he ruled in both that it might be sold to a non-Jew, Rabbah objected, and he retracted. If so, why did he trouble them there to sift it and sell it? He should likewise have instructed them to sell it little by little. It follows that selling little by little helps specifically with chametz, because even if the non-Jew sells it to a Jew, the Jew will eat it before Pesach. For other prohibitions, selling little by little does not help, since we remain concerned that he may sell it to a Jew or give it as a gift to a Jewish friend. Indeed, nothing is smaller than an egg, yet one may not sell an egg from a neveilah or tereifah to a non-Jew, even a single egg, as implied by the Gemara and poskim. See Yoreh Deah, end of siman 86. In siman 84, Beit Yosef cites a ruling that worm-infested flour must be cast into a river, implying that no remedy exists. Rama writes in siman 154:5 regarding yayin nesech that became mixed in, ‘Nevertheless, he should not sell a large quantity together, lest the non-Jew resell it to a Jew,’ implying that selling a little at a time raises no concern. These words are taken from Rabbeinu Yerucham, but I have heard and do not understand, for Rabbeinu Yerucham could not have intended this. There, the taste of wine is detectable, so how would a Jew purchase it from a non-Jew? Rather, his intent is that he should not sell the entire mixture together except for the value of the yayin nesech. See Rabbeinu Yerucham and you will find this to be so. Even his words require study, for Tosafot at the end of chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah write only that he need not sell it together except for the value of the yayin nesech, but if he wishes, he may sell it together. They write similarly in Yevamos 81b, at the end of Avodah Zarah, and in Zevachim 72. This is not the place to elaborate. In a matter where there is concern that it may be resold to a Jew, however, there is no permission to sell it little by little except with chametz, as explained. See Yoreh Deah 112:11. Knesses HaGedolah cites Rashdam, Yoreh Deah 45, and Maharit, Yoreh Deah 2, as writing: although there is a double doubt, perhaps a Jew will eat it before Pesach, a double doubt created by one's own act is forbidden. This requires study, for the case concerns a large quantity of chametz. Perhaps their meaning is that the non-Jew may sell it to many Jews, a little to each. This apparently follows Kesef Mishneh, that the permission depends upon the small amount being consumed by the non-Jew himself. See also the end of Yoreh Deah siman 57.

<b>לידי חימוץ</b>. ואם לא שהה עדיין שיעור מיל עדיין לא החמיץ (וכ"מ בת"ה סי' קכ"ג ובאגור) וא"כ אפילו נעשה בפסח למוכרו לעכו"ם קודם ששהה שיעור מיל: מעשה בישראל שלקח חטים מעכו"ם ובא אח"כ ישראל אחר וביקש מהעכו"ם שימכור לו ג"כ חטים ואמר אין לי חטים כי היהודי שמכרתי לו הוא אוהבי ומכרתי לו החטים שרחצתי ובקשתי לטוחנן לצורך ביתי ומכרתי אותם לאהבתי אותו נ"ל דדמי למ"ש בי"ד ססי' י"ו דאין העכו"ם נאמן במה שאמר לאחר שמכרם ויצאו מתחת ידו מיהו אי מהימן ליה אסור עכ"ל וע"ש סי' קכ"ז: כתב הב"ח כתו' במרדכי הארוך בשם ראבי"ה חטין שנתבקעו ואין ביקוען ניכר שנתערבו בכרי אסור להשהותן ואם עבר והשה' אותן אסורים אחר הפסח ואסור למכרן לעכו"ם אבל אם אכלו מן התבואה קודם הפסח מותר לאכלם בפסח כמ"ש בי"ד סי' ק"י ס"ז דמותר לאכול הנשארות שנים שנים ואפי' לא אכלו מנייהו מצי למישקל מנייהו ולמשדי לים ואינך משתרי ואין כאן משום מבטל איסור לכתחלה דאכתי לא חל פסח (ועמ"ש ססי' תמ"ז בשם הרשב"א) ואף על  גב דאין מותר לאכול אלא ב' ב' אבל א' א' אסור ה"נ אין לך כל מצה שלא יהא יותר מן השיעור של איסור וה"ל תרתי תרתי ע"כ והכל בו כ' שמותר להשהות החטים שנתערבו דיבש ביבש בטל וכ"פ הב"ח לדינא ומ"ש הראבי"ה כשנאכלו קצתם שמותר לאכול השאר בפסח משום דס"ל דאפי' אינו אוסר יותר מכדי נטיל' אבל לדידן דס"ל דאוסר כולו א"כ משהו חמץ שנשאר בו אוסר כל המצה דיהיב טעמא במשהו (ב"ח) ועססי' תס"א ולפי מ"ש סי' תמ"ז ס"ט דקודם פסח בטל ברוב א"כ מותר לטחנן קודם פסח ולבטלן בס' עסי' תנ"ג ס"ג וכ"ה בי"ד סי' ק"ט ויש כאן מקום עיון בב"ח ע"ש: ויש ללמוד דאם נתערב יבש ביבש בפסח ונאכל א' מהן דשרי וכמ"ש בי"ד סי' ק"י ואפשר ג"כ דהתערובות השלישי שרי כמ"ש שם אבל בלח לא שייכי היתרים הללו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To the point of becoming chametz.</b> If it has not yet remained for the time needed to walk a mil, it has not yet become chametz. This is also implied by Terumas HaDeshen, siman 123, and by Agur. Accordingly, even if this occurred on Pesach, it may be sold to a non-Jew before the time of a mil has elapsed. An incident occurred in which a Jew purchased wheat from a non-Jew. Another Jew later asked the non-Jew to sell him wheat as well. The non-Jew said: I have no wheat, for the Jew to whom I sold is my friend, and I sold him wheat that I had washed and intended to grind for my household, because I loved him. It appears to me that this resembles the ruling at the end of Yoreh Deah siman 16, that the non-Jew is not believed in what he says after he has sold the item and it has left his possession. Nevertheless, if the Jew personally trusts him, it is forbidden. See there and siman 127. Bach cites Mordechai HaAruch in the name of Raavyah: wheat kernels that split, though the splitting is not visible, and became mixed into a pile, may not be retained. If one transgressed and retained them, they are forbidden after Pesach and may not be sold to a non-Jew. But if some of the grain was eaten before Pesach, the remainder may be eaten on Pesach, as stated in Yoreh Deah 110:7, where the remaining pieces may be eaten two at a time. Even if none were eaten, one may take one and cast it into the sea, and the others become permitted. This is not considered intentionally nullifying a prohibition, since Pesach has not yet begun. See what I wrote at the end of siman 447 in the name of Rashba. Although they may be eaten only two at a time and not singly, here every matzah contains more than the measure of the forbidden substance, so each constitutes two against one. Kol Bo writes that the mixed wheat may be retained, because dry matter mixed with dry matter is nullified, and Bach rules accordingly. Raavyah's statement that after some were eaten the rest may be eaten on Pesach follows his view that the prohibition extends only to the amount requiring removal. According to our view that the entire item becomes forbidden, however, any minute amount of chametz remaining prohibits the entire matzah, because chametz imparts prohibition even in the smallest quantity. See the end of siman 461. According to what is written in siman 447:9, that before Pesach it is nullified by a majority, it may be ground before Pesach and nullified in sixty. See siman 453:3 and Yoreh Deah 109. Bach's treatment requires study. It may also be learned that if dry matter became mixed with dry matter on Pesach and one of them was eaten, the others are permitted, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 110. Perhaps the third mixture is also permitted, as written there. These leniencies do not apply to a liquid mixture.

<b>רק אותו כו' שעושין כו'</b>. פי' תרתי בעינן דדוקא מאותו שבחבית מותר לעשות משקה דאף את"ל שיש בו מעט קמח בטל בס' בשעת בישול קודם פסח אבל אותן שמביאין בכלים קטנים אסור אפי' לעשות ממנו משקה וכמה פעמים נתברר לי שמערבין בהן קמח הרבה: ולאכול הדבש בעין בכל ענין אסור דאתי לאיחלופי וכ"כ מהרי"ל ורש"ל ואסור לעשות המשק' בפסח (ב"ח וכ"מ בד"מ) והלבוש כ' וז"ל י"א דוקא המשק' שקורין מע"ד מותר מפני שיש בו הרבה מים ומסתמא נתבטל הקמח קודם פסח אבל דבש בפ"ע שבשלו קודם פסח אף על פי שנזהר בו מחמץ וסיננו אותו מהדבורים שבהם אסור לאכלו וליתנו לתבשיל ונ"ל שהמקיל לא הפסיד והמחמיר יחמיר ותע"ב: מיהו דבש שמביאין מן הכוור' נוהגין בו היתר לכ"ע שאין דרך לערב בו כלום כשרודין אותו מן הכוורת עכ"ל דקדק וכ' שמביאין מן הכוורת ולא כ' שמביאין בחבית מן הכוורת כמ"ש משום דאותו אסור לאכלו בעין ולא שרי אלא אותו שמביאין עם החררים קטני' שלם כמו שהו' מונח בכוור' שעדיין לא נתרסק, ויש נוהגין לבשל מי דבש עד שחוזר ונעש' עב כמו דבש ואוכלין אותו ולדברי הב"ח אסור כמש"ל והכל לפי המנהג:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Only that, etc., which they make, etc.</b> Two conditions are required. A beverage may be made only from the honey kept in the barrel, because even if it contains a small amount of flour, it was nullified in sixty when cooked before Pesach. Honey brought in small vessels, however, may not even be used to make a beverage, and I have many times established that a large quantity of flour is mixed into it. Eating the honey itself is forbidden in every case, lest one confuse the cases. Mahari Weil and Maharshal write likewise. The beverage may not be prepared on Pesach. Bach writes this, and it is also implied by Darchei Moshe. Levush writes: Some say that only the beverage called mead is permitted, because it contains much water and the flour was presumably nullified before Pesach. Honey itself that was cooked before Pesach, however, even if care was taken to protect it from chametz and it was strained from the bees within it, may not be eaten or added to cooked food. It appears to me that one who is lenient has not lost, while one who is stringent may be stringent and will receive blessing. Nevertheless, honey brought directly from the hive is customarily permitted according to all opinions, since nothing is normally mixed into it when it is removed from the hive. Levush carefully writes ‘brought from the hive,’ and not ‘brought in a barrel from the hive,’ because the latter may not be eaten by itself and is permitted only when it arrives together with intact small pieces of honeycomb as it lay in the hive and has not yet been crushed. Some customarily cook honey-water until it becomes thick like honey and eat it. According to Bach this is forbidden, as explained above. Everything follows local custom.

<b>וענבים יבשים</b>. כתו' בתשו' מהרי"ל סי' ק"ה שלא לאכול ענבים יבישים ואם יחפוץ אדם לקיים מ"ש בשיר השירים פתח הסמדר יכול לקיים בענבים דידן עכ"ל ובמהרי"ל כתו' דיש ליזהר בענבים דקים לפי שמערבים אותן עם הגרוגרות: פה ק"ק קאליש נוהגין היתר מקדמונים ליקח אגסים יבשים קטנים מן העכו"ם ולעשות מהן משקה דהיינו ששופכין עליהם מים קרים ושורה בתוכה עד שנעש' משקה אבל לבשלו עם האגסים נוהגים איסור וגם האגסים עצמן אין אוכלים אותם והכלים ששואבים בהם המשקה נוהגין שלא להשתמש בהם דבר אחר: נ"ל הטעם משום דחוששין שמא נדבק בהם קצת חמץ לכן אין אוכלין אות' אבל המשקה שותים דאותו המעט בטל בס' ואף על גב ששורה גם בפסח מ"מ לא החמירו מספק, ומה שנוהגין איסור  לבשלן משום דע"י בישול יהיב טעמא ביותר ואף על גב שיש עכו"ם שמייבשין האגסים בתנור אחר  שאפו בו לחם מ"מ הוי נ"ט בנ"ט דהתירא ואף על גב שרמ"א כתב בסי' תמ"ז להחמיר בזה הכא  דספק הוא שהרבה בחמה נתייבשו או אחר שהוסק התנור לכן נהגו להקל כנ"ל ליישב מנהגם, אבל במקומות אחרים נהגו להחמיר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And dried grapes.</b> Mahari Weil writes in responsum 105 that dried grapes should not be eaten. If a person wishes to fulfill the phrase in Shir HaShirim, ‘the blossom has opened,’ he may do so with our grapes. In Maharil it is written that care must be taken with thin grapes because they are mixed with dried figs. Here in the holy community of Kalisz there is an ancient custom to permit purchasing small dried pears from non-Jews and making a beverage from them by pouring cold water over them and soaking them until they become a drink. They customarily forbid cooking the drink together with the pears, and they do not eat the pears themselves. They also customarily avoid using the vessels with which the drink is drawn for any other purpose. It appears to me that the reason is concern that a small amount of chametz may have adhered to the pears. They therefore do not eat them, but drink the liquid because the small amount is nullified in sixty. Although they continue soaking during Pesach, they did not act stringently in a case of doubt. They forbid cooking them because cooking extracts more flavor. Although some non-Jews dry the pears in an oven after bread was baked in it, this is a secondary imparting of permitted flavor. Rama in siman 447 is stringent regarding this, but here there is a doubt, since many were dried in the sun or after the oven was fired. They therefore customarily acted leniently. This appears to explain their practice, but in other places the custom is stringent.

<b>לא נתבקעו</b>. דכיון דאיכא פלוגתא בגמ' די"א דשרי בלא נתבקעו ואיסור משהו דרבנן אזלינן בספיקן לקולא (רי"ף) משמע דאם נתערב בפחות מס' אסור ע' בי"ד סי' צ"ח והקשה בהג"מ דהא בס"ב פסק אפי' לא נתבקעו אסור ותי' דדוקא למיכלה חטים בעינייהו אבל לאסור התבשיל לא החמירו וקשה כיון דלא מקלי' אלא משום דהוי פלוגתא וע"כ לא פליגי אלא בלתיתה אבל היכא דמינח נייח' כ"ע מודו שאסור א"כ למה נקל בתבשיל וי"ל דכתב לעיל אתי לידי חימוץ משמע שיבואו לידי חימוץ אבל עדיין אינן חמץ ודאי אלא ספק לכן מתירין התבשיל וע' בטור, כתב ב"י בשם הכל בו אם נמצאו ג' שעורים בתבשיל חזקה דאיכא טובא ושמא נתבקעו (ד"מ) ונ"ל דבכוונה השמיטוהו בש"ע דלא קי"ל הכי דהא כ' הט"ז בי"ד סי' פ"ד ס"ט בשם רש"ל דלא אמרי' כן אלא בתולעים דמיני' קא רבו אבל במלתא דאתי מעלמא לא ועוד דהא יכולין לבדקו אם יש בו שעורים מיהו בכרי של חטים שנפל עליו גשמים י"ל האי סברא עס"ב מ"ש שם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They did not split.</b> Since the Gemara records a dispute, with some permitting kernels that did not split, and the prohibition of even the smallest amount is D'Rabbanan, we rule leniently in a doubt, as Rif writes. This implies that if it became mixed in less than sixty, it is forbidden. See Yoreh Deah siman 98. Hagahos Maimoniyos asks that in seif 2 it was ruled forbidden even if they did not split. It answers that this stringency applies only to eating the wheat itself, but they were not stringent enough to prohibit a cooked dish. This is difficult, for the leniency rests only on the dispute, and the dispute appears to concern soaked grain. Where the kernels lie at rest, all agree that they are forbidden. Why, then, are we lenient regarding the cooked dish? One may answer that the earlier wording, ‘they may come to become chametz,’ implies that they will become chametz but are not certainly chametz yet. The cooked dish is therefore permitted. See Tur. Beit Yosef cites Kol Bo that if three barley kernels are found in a cooked dish, it is presumed that many are present and perhaps some split. Darchei Moshe cites this. It appears to me that Shulchan Aruch intentionally omitted it because we do not rule so. Taz in Yoreh Deah 84:9 cites Maharshal that such a presumption applies only to worms, which reproduce from within, but not to something that comes from outside. Moreover, the dish can be examined for barley. In a pile of wheat upon which rain fell, however, this reasoning may apply. See seif 2 and what is written there.

<b>אוסרים הכל</b>. הכיסוי והכף וה"ה שאר תבשילין שנתערב בהן מאותו תבשיל וכן נוהגין (ב"ח) וכ"כ הטור בשם הרוקח חטה שנמצאת בקדר' נאסרה הכף שהגיסו בו שמוהו בקדירות אחרות כולן אסורות עכ"ל וכ"מ ססי"ב ובט"ז בי"ד בסי' צ"ב כתב דאם נאסרה חתיכה מחמץ משהו ונפלה לקדירה אחרת אינה אוסרת בפליטתה ע"ש והפריז על מדותיו שבכל מקום נוהגין לאסור וכן מ"כ בהגהת או"ה מרמ"א ז"ל ואף על גב דאין החתיכה נ"נ מ"מ לענין האיסור  אוסר במשהו ויש נפקות' כגון דבר דעביד לטעמיה לא בטיל ובמקום שאין עביד לטעמי' בטיל חמץ תוך זמנו במשהו ולאחר זמנו בס' או לענין אם נתערב במינו ושלא במינו דאמרי' סליק מינו כמי שאינו עכ"ל וכ"כ הב"ח וע"ש בדריש' מ"מ מ"ש דחמץ תוך זמנו וכו' פי' אם נתערב משהו חמץ בתבשיל תוך הפסח ושהה אותו עד אחר הפסח ונתערב מאותו תבשיל בתבשיל אחר א"צ ס' אלא נגד המשהו וקשה דהא הב"ח עצמו כתב בסי' תמ"ז דדבר הנאסר במשהו ועבר עליו הפסח מותר בלא תערובות ובספר נקודת הכסף כתוב ושהיה במזיד ר"ל דכשעבר במזיד אסור אבל לפי מ"ש ססי' תמ"ז אפי' שהיה במזיד מותר ע"ש גם מ"ש ססי' נ"ה דחמץ בע"פ אוסר במינו במשהו משום דהוי דשיל"מ כבר הכריע בת"ח דלא הוי דשיל"מ וכ"מ סי' שס"א גבי מצה כפולה גם מ"ש דיבש ביבש לא בטיל בפסח משום דשיל"מ ושלא במינה בטל בס' לא עיין במרדכי שכתב בהדיא שהטעם כי היכי דלח במשהו ה"ה ביבש וכ"ה בי"ד וכ"ה בת"ח וא"כ אפי' שלא במינה לא בטיל ואפי' חתיכה הנאסרת מחמת משהו ונתערבו באחרות ביבש כולן אסורות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>They prohibit everything.</b> This includes the lid and spoon, and likewise other cooked dishes into which some of this dish was mixed. This is the custom, as Bach writes. Tur likewise cites Rokeach: If a wheat kernel is found in a pot, the spoon used to stir it becomes forbidden; if the spoon was then placed in other pots, they all become forbidden. The same is implied at the end of seif 12. Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 92 writes that if a piece became forbidden by the smallest amount of chametz and fell into another pot, it does not prohibit that pot through the flavor it emits. He has gone too far, for everywhere the custom is to forbid it. I likewise found this written in Rama's gloss to Issur VeHeter. Although the piece itself does not become intrinsically forbidden, the absorbed prohibition nevertheless prohibits even in the smallest amount. This has practical consequences, such as where an ingredient added for flavor is never nullified, while where it is not added for flavor, chametz during its prohibited period prohibits in any amount and after that period requires sixty; or where it became mixed with its own kind and another kind, in which case we regard its own kind as though absent. Bach writes likewise. What Rama writes that chametz during its prohibited period, etc., means that if a minute amount of chametz became mixed into a cooked dish during Pesach, and he retained it until after Pesach, and some of that dish then became mixed into another dish, one requires sixty only against the original minute amount. This is difficult, for Bach himself writes in siman 447 that something prohibited by the smallest amount and retained over Pesach is permitted after Pesach without any mixture. Nekudas HaKesef explains that it was retained intentionally, meaning that intentional retention makes it forbidden. Yet according to what is written at the end of siman 447, even intentional retention is permitted. See there. Also, what he writes at the end of siman 55, that chametz on Erev Pesach prohibits its own kind in any amount because it is an item that will become permitted, was already rejected in Toras Chatas, which rules that it is not treated as such an item. The same is implied in siman 461 regarding a folded matzah. He also writes that dry matter mixed with dry matter is not nullified on Pesach because it will become permitted, while unlike kinds are nullified in sixty. He did not see Mordechai, who writes explicitly that just as a liquid mixture is prohibited in any amount, so too is a dry mixture. This is also ruled in Yoreh Deah and Toras Chatas. Accordingly, even unlike kinds are not nullified, and even if a piece became forbidden by the smallest amount and was then mixed dry with other pieces, they are all forbidden.

<b>כל העיסה</b>. ז"ל הרשב"א סי' קע"ו שלא במקום א' בלבד נגעה החט' אלא כאן וכאן דרך גלגולה של עיסה וא"ל בכי הא כאן נמצא כאן היתה (עסי"ב) ועוד שהמים שבעיסה מוליכין את היוצא מאותו גרגיר בכל העיסה שלא אמרו קליפה אלא יבש ביבש וכן אפילו במקום לחות רב כלחת מוהל בשעת מליח' אבל במקום רוטב אמרו שהרוטב מוליך אפילו דבר שאינו מפעפע וכן לחם הנמצא בעיסה הלחם עשוי להתפרר ונתערבו פירורין בעיסה מלבד שהוא בעצמו אפשר שמחמץ אחרים אף על פי שהוא אפוי אלא שאין דעתי נוטה לכך שיהא דבר מועט מחמיץ כל העיסה כו' ע"ש מוכח דס"ל דאוסר אפי' בצונן ע' סי"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The entire dough.</b> Rashba writes in responsum 176 that the wheat kernel did not touch only one place. Rather, as the dough was rolled it touched here and there. One cannot say in such a case, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ as discussed in seif 12. Moreover, the water in the dough carries what emerges from that kernel throughout the entire dough. The law of removing only the outer layer was stated only regarding dry contact with dry matter. Even where there is moisture without liquid, the Gemara in Chullin 112a states that roasting diffuses flavor to the extent of a fingerbreadth. All the more so does this apply to dough, where the moisture is greater. Therefore, if the dough was divided into several matzos and the kernel was found in one, all are forbidden. If, however, it was found only after the matzah had been removed to another place, one may say, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ as explained below. Bach writes that the entire dough is forbidden only when the wheat was softened, but if it was not softened all is permitted. See seif 2. Maharam Lublin writes in a responsum that one person took cabbage from a barrel into a bowl, carried it to another house, and a wheat kernel was found upon it there. He permitted the cabbage remaining in the barrel, because we say, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been.’ If, however, a wheat kernel is found in the barrel, even the cabbage in the bowl is forbidden, for perhaps the kernel had previously been in the barrel, as written in seif 12 regarding a chicken. The same applies where one has dough, cuts matzos from it and gives them to others, retains one matzah, and later finds a wheat kernel upon it. All are forbidden, because perhaps it had been there previously. Only from place to place do we not establish a prohibition retroactively. According to Bach, who treats the matter as a double doubt, it would always be permitted for this reason. This does not appear correct to me, for the doubt whether it became chametz is a lack of knowledge, which the poskim in Yoreh Deah do not treat as a halachic doubt.

<b>אסור' התרנגולת</b>. דאמרי' שמא היתה החטה במים בשעת מליגא דלא אמרינן כאן נמצא כאן היתה אלא ממקום למקום או מחתיכה לחתיכה כמ"ש סי"א אבל במקום א' כגון במליג' דיש לספק בזמן ולו' שמא היתה החטה במים קודם לכן לא אמרינן השתא נפלה דהכי אשכחן פ"ק דחולין ופ"ק דנדה דלא אמרי' השתא הוא דאיתרע היכא דליכא חזקה, אך מ"ש הרב"י בשם המרדכי דאם נמצא גרגיר חטה בקערה אצל התרנגולת דשרי מטעם ס"ס (כמ"ש רמ"א בהג"ה) ולא שרי מטעם כאן נמצאת כאן הית' וי"ל כיון דשכיח למצוא חטים בתרנגולים שאוכלים חטים אמרינן שמא הי' בתרנגולת כמ"ש בי"ד סי' נ"ז שתולין במצוי לכן לא שרי' אלא מטעם ס"ס עכ"ל מ"ב סי' ס"ט וקשה דא"כ אפי' ס"ס לא הוי דדבר המצוי לא הוי ספק כלל דהא אפי' לקולא תולין בו כמ"ש בי"ד סי' נ"ז וכ"כ האו"ה, אלא צ"ל דדוקא התם דשכיח טובא אבל דבר המצוי קצת נחשבינהו לספק ספיקא כמ"ש סי' פ"ד וא"כ לא ה"ל למ"ב להביא מסי' נ"ז ועיין בא"ע סי' ו' בב"י דדבר שאינו מצוי לא חשבי' לס"ס ול"נ דאע"ג דשכיח למצוא חטים בתרנגולים מ"מ כשמדיחים אותם לא נשאר בתוכן כלום ובפרט לצורך פסח וא"כ לא מקרי ספק הרגיל והא דלא התיר במרדכי מטעם כאן נמצאת כאן היתה ובקערה הוא דנפלה משום דהמרדכי ס"ל דכלי שני אסור בפסח כמ"ש סי' תמ"ז ס"ג ומזמן לזמן מחזקינן איסור וא"כ את"ל שהיתה בקערה כשעירו לתוכ' התרנגולת היתה אסורה לכן התיר מטעם ספק ספיקא דעכשיו היא צוננת ואינה אוסר' דמיירי שלא היה שם רוטב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The chicken is forbidden.</b> We say that perhaps the wheat kernel was in the water when the chicken was scalded. The principle, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ applies only from one place to another or from one piece to another, as written in seif 11. But where the question is within one location, such as scalding, and the doubt concerns time, perhaps the kernel had already been in the water, we do not say that it fell only now. This is found in the first chapter of Chullin and the first chapter of Niddah, where we do not say that the defect arose only now in the absence of a prior presumption. Beit Yosef cites Mordechai that if a wheat kernel is found in a bowl beside a chicken, it is permitted because of a double doubt, as Rama writes in his gloss, rather than because it was found there. One may answer that since wheat kernels are commonly found in chickens, which eat wheat, we say that perhaps it came from the chicken, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 57, where we attribute matters to the common cause. It is therefore permitted only by way of a double doubt. Masas Binyamin writes this in siman 69. This is difficult, for if the matter is common, it should not count even as one doubt, since we attribute to the common cause even leniently, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 57 and in Issur VeHeter. Rather, one must say that only something extremely common is not considered a doubt, but something somewhat common may form part of a double doubt, as written in siman 84. Masas Binyamin should therefore not have cited siman 57. See Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer siman 6, that an uncommon possibility is not counted toward a double doubt. It appears to me that although wheat is commonly found in chickens, nothing ordinarily remains in them after they are washed, especially when prepared for Pesach, so this is not called a common doubt. Mordechai did not permit it because ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ and say that it fell only in the bowl, because Mordechai rules that a second vessel prohibits on Pesach, as written in siman 447:3. Regarding a doubt over time, we establish the prohibition retroactively. Thus, even if it was in the bowl when the chicken was poured into it, the chicken would be forbidden. He therefore permitted it through the double doubt that the food is now cold and does not prohibit, since the case contains no liquid.

<b>And it does not contain enough to impart flavor.</b> It appears to me that the meaning is that the chametz is small in quantity and does not impart flavor to the water because the water contains sixty against it. It is therefore permitted, since the prohibition of the smallest amount is D'Rabbanan and we are not stringent in cold food. Nevertheless, some forbid it. See what I wrote in seif 11, that one should be stringent regarding bread, and the same applies to dough, because the wheat causes the dough to ferment and imparts flavor throughout.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For we say that perhaps afterward.</b> The wording is imprecise, for how can we permit merely because of a doubt? Rather, the reason is that we maintain the prior presumption and say that it fell only now. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes so. Beit Yosef states: Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in the name of Rashba that if a wheat kernel is found in the vessel in which a chicken was washed, we say, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ as with physical defects, and we do not say that it came from the chicken. He ruled this as practical halachah. This is difficult, for Rashba rules that if wheat is found in a matzah or dough, it prohibits the matzah or dough, as written in seif 11, and we do not say that it fell only now. Moreover, according to Rama's explanation, why does he write, ‘and we do not say that it came from the chicken’? He should have written, ‘and we do not say that it had previously been in the water.’ It is also unlike the law of physical defects. It therefore appears to me that the case concerns a chicken that was salted and then washed in cold water in a vessel, after which a wheat kernel was found in the vessel. There is concern that the kernel had been in the chicken and was salted together with it. Rashba therefore rules that we do not suspect this. His proof is from physical defects, concerning which Kesubos chapter 6 rules that when defects are found while a woman is in her father's domain, we presume that they were present before betrothal, while if they are found in her husband's domain, we say that they arose only now. Here too, since the wheat was found in the vessel, we do not say that it had been in the chicken, which would be moving the prohibition from place to place. A matzah or dough, however, presents a doubt from one time to another, as explained above. There is therefore no dispute at all. This appears to be the primary explanation.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Or was scalded.</b> Meaning, the wheat was found afterward, so one may say that it fell only now. What was ruled above at the end of seif 2, that it may not be eaten despite a double doubt, refers to eating the wheat kernels themselves. The mixture, however, is permitted. Accordingly, if a matzah presents a double doubt, the matzah itself remains forbidden to eat. Bach writes that with a single doubt it is forbidden even to retain the item, even in a case of substantial loss. With a double doubt it may be retained, and certainly sold to a non-Jew, but may not be eaten, because the possibility that it was digested is not a complete doubt, since some authorities hold that digestion does not help regarding prohibition. This applies specifically to complete chametz, such as bread or dough. If a wheat kernel is found in water, however, a cooked dish prepared from that water beforehand is permitted because of a double doubt: perhaps the wheat fell only now, and if it fell earlier, perhaps it did not soften until now. If it did not soften, it does not prohibit at all, even according to our custom. His words imply that even in hot water one need not prohibit it unless it softened. See there. An incident occurred in which a person placed a piece of matzah and a piece of chicken in his mouth and ate them together. He felt a wheat kernel, removed the food, and saw a kernel, but could not determine whether it had been in the matzah or in the chicken. The chicken may be permitted through a double doubt: perhaps the kernel fell onto the chicken only now while cold, and if it had been there earlier, perhaps it was digested. If it had been in the matzah, however, it necessarily was there during baking, for had it fallen upon the cold matzah, it would have rolled off. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the matzah is also permitted, since the prohibition of the smallest amount is D'Rabbanan and we attribute the kernel leniently to the chicken, as in Yoreh Deah siman 111 regarding two pots, one of which contains sixty. We attribute the prohibition to the one containing sixty, and both are permitted. The amount requiring removal from the matzah at the place from which the piece was taken is nevertheless forbidden by law. It further appears to me that even if the other item was a piece of meat, both are permitted, because we do not establish a prohibition from one place to another and say that the kernel fell upon the meat only now while cold. The matzah is then also permitted. If, however, the meat lay in a bowl and he ate from it, the meat is forbidden, as explained above, because with a doubt from one time to another we establish the prohibition retroactively, perhaps the kernel was in the bowl when the meat was poured into it, and a second vessel prohibits on Pesach. One might suggest a double doubt: perhaps it was in the matzah, and if in the meat, perhaps it fell only now. This is not a valid doubt, because to say that it was in the matzah requires forbidding the matzah, and we do not create such a doubt even regarding a D'Rabbanan prohibition or even as part of a double doubt. Darchei Moshe writes so in siman 439. Accordingly, both the meat and matzah are forbidden. In most cases, however, one can recognize whether the wheat was cooked or baked. See Niddah 3b regarding a basket in which a creeping creature was found. This implies that if food was cooked in a pot, the pot was removed from the stove to another place and cooled, and a wheat kernel was then found upon it, we say that it fell only now, because we do not establish a prohibition from place to place.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But the primary ruling, etc.</b> This opinion holds that with a D'Rabbanan prohibition we do not say that a prohibition passes from one piece to another through salting. The requirement of sixty for other prohibitions was adopted as a fixed rule so that we need not distinguish between lean and fatty substances, and we apply that rule even leniently, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 105. Accordingly, even with chametz, sixty is sufficient. Moreover, only prohibitions involving fat are measured in sixty, but not chametz, to which diffusion of fat does not apply. Further, where another basis for leniency exists, we rely upon Sheiltos, who holds that chametz is nullified in sixty. No greater basis for leniency exists than the fact that many Geonim hold that flavor does not spread through salting. A compromise should therefore be made to forbid only that piece, just as in seif 11 only that matzah is forbidden. One who wishes to be stringent should rule that the piece be burned while the other pieces lying with it in the barrel may be retained. The other pieces may also be permitted for benefit by redeeming the value of the chametz, as written in siman 447:1. Rama writes this in responsum 28, and Masas Binyamin rules likewise in siman 69. He further rules that if the other pieces were cooked without peeling, they remain permitted, because peeling them is only a stringency, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 91. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the piece itself requires peeling, but that the peel does not prohibit the piece even if it was cooked together with it. This is also his intent in Yoreh Deah there. Examine the matter carefully, unlike the later authorities. Masas Binyamin's statement that here the others are permitted even without peeling applies, in my opinion, only where there is no doubt that the wheat touched the other pieces. If there is such a doubt, all are forbidden by law, as written in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah siman 105. Even if it is certain that the wheat touched only one but we do not know which one, all are forbidden by law and require peeling, because chametz on Pesach is not nullified by a majority, as written in siman 447:9. If they were then cooked without peeling, they prohibit the mixture, as written in Yoreh Deah simanim 69 and 98. Only regarding the rule that the lower item prevails is peeling treated as a mere stringency, since the Gemara initially says that peeling is unnecessary and we are concerned only that a small amount may have been absorbed. The peel therefore does not prohibit the entire piece. Where peeling is required by law, however, if the piece was cooked without peeling, it prohibits the entire piece. This resolves all the objections of the later authorities to Rama and is likewise implied by Derishah. Maharam Lublin writes in a responsum that if a wheat kernel lies in a barrel of salted cabbage, the entire barrel is forbidden because soaking is like cooking. One must say that the brine rose above the cabbage. If the brine did not cover it, the case follows the law of salting, as explained above from Rama's responsum. See what I wrote in siman 447:5. I likewise heard of a wheat kernel found in a barrel of fish salted for Pesach, and the Gaon Rabbi Binyamin, head of the beis din of Poznan, ordered that the row containing it be cast into the river and the remainder sold to a non-Jew. Maharam further writes that before Pesach a crop filled with wheat was found among salted chickens. Although Rama rules in Yoreh Deah siman 105 that chametz on Pesach prohibits through salting only the amount requiring peeling, I did not have the courage to permit that chicken because a large quantity of chametz was present. Bach follows the stringent opinion regarding salting on Pesach, and Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah rules likewise. Darchei Moshe writes that he heard that some in Krakow were stringent and therefore did not wish to rule either prohibition or permission. Masas Binyamin writes that he was in Krakow and never heard that they were stringent. Perhaps Rama referred to the elderly author of Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah.
