**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Rather his intent, etc.</b> That there are two remedies in the matter: one is the remedy of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel mentioned in Avodah Zarah folio 74: sell all of it to a non-Jew except for the value of the yayin nesech in it. And the second is the remedy of R' Eliezer explained on folio 49b: take the benefit to the Dead Sea; and see there in Yoreh De'ah siman 134 in the Tur and Beis Yosef and the distinction between these two remedies. For if he sells them except for the value of the yayin nesech in it he must sell everything at one time; but through the remedy of taking the benefit to the Dead Sea he may sell them little by little; and to this Rabbeinu Yeruham intended: however he should not sell together. And what the Rama concluded there, "lest he sell it back to a Jew," is not from Rabbeinu Yeruham's language, but the Rama added so from his own understanding according to the Rama's grasp; but Magen Avraham holds that what Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote "however he should not sell together" means it was permitted only through taking the benefit to the Dead Sea, and through this it is permitted to sell little by little; but a remedy to sell specifically everything together, namely to sell it except for the value of the yayin nesech in it, we do not hold that permission. (And according to Rabbeinu Yeruham's language that Beis Yosef brought there in siman 134 one cannot intend thus, and Sefer Rabbeinu Yeruham is not before me.) But his words too require study, for Tosafos at the end of the third chapter of Avodah Zarah, etc.: meaning Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote so in the name of Tosafos, and Tosafos's intent is not as Rabbeinu Yeruham understood; for in Avodah Zarah 49b: A certain man whose barrel of yayin nesech mixed into his wines came before R' Chisda, who said to him: Take four zuz and cast them into the river and it will be permitted for you. And Tosafos wrote, and this is their language: "Take," etc., and to sell them each by itself he told him so, as I explained (end quote). And Rabbeinu Yeruham understood that Tosafos's intent is specifically to sell them each by itself, namely through the remedy of casting the value of the yayin nesech, that is four zuz, it was permitted for him, but it was not permitted for him to sell them at one time through the remedy of except for the value of yayin nesech in it; and on this Magen Avraham wrote that on the contrary Tosafos's intent is the opposite: certainly if he wishes to sell them at one time except for yayin nesech it is permitted; only that R' Chisda came to lighten for him and told him to cast four zuz into the river and through this he may sell them little by little each by itself. But regarding a matter, etc.: meaning according to how Magen Avraham explained Rabbeinu Yeruham's words unlike the Rama: if so there is no proof from Rabbeinu Yeruham to permit regarding other prohibitions to sell little by little lest he sell it to a Jew; rather yayin nesech is different, for no Jew will buy wine from a non-Jew; but regarding chametz [it differs]; and not like Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 84 s.k. 17 who permitted also regarding other prohibitions to sell to a non-Jew little by little; and see Peri Chadash. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 112 se'if 11, who wrote: Bread of a Jew that a non-Jew baked without a Jew's assistance is forbidden, and even to sell it to a non-Jew is forbidden in a place where they practice eating non-Jews' bread, lest he sell it back to a Jew, unless he cuts it into pieces, for pieces of bread are forbidden to buy from a non-Jew. Implying that even little by little it is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew regarding other prohibitions, since he needed to give a remedy to cut the bread into pieces and did not write to sell little by little.

<b>Chametz within its time, etc.</b> Magen Avraham explained below his intent herein. If it mixed with its species and not with its species, where we say "remove," etc.: therefore the intent is not as simple that it fell immediately into its species and not its species: for even though according to R' Yehuda same-species is not nullified, nevertheless we say remove its species as if it is not, and what is not its species outweighs it and nullifies it, as we say in Chullin folio 100; and this law has no connection here to a practical difference; therefore one must say it first fell into its species alone and forbade in any amount, and afterward the piece forbidden in any amount fell into not its species: if the piece had become nevelah one would need sixty against the whole piece, and now that through any amount it did not become nevelah one needs sixty only against the any-amount. And what he concluded "remove its species as if it is not" is Gemara rhetorical polish; and his intent is that that piece forbidden in any amount because it was its species: regarding that we say "remove," for it does not forbid through its release since it did not become nevelah; therefore he did not conclude in the Gemara's language "and what is not its species outweighs it and nullifies it." Only that it is still difficult: why do I need that it afterward fell into not its species? Even if it fell again into its species, nevertheless since the Rama necessarily holds as Nekudos HaKesef wrote, since another force is mixed into it, it is not [simply] its species as above: if so even if it fell into its species it is called not its species; and it is a great stretch to say that this is truly his intent in writing "and into not its species," meaning even its species is not its species. Rather, even without this it is a stretch in my eyes that the practical difference be as simple according to R' Yehuda who holds same-species is not nullified, for we do not hold like him; therefore it appears to my humble opinion that it refers regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin, where we hold same-species is not nullified and not-its-species is nullified; and accordingly it fits that the Rama follows his own view, for he wrote in Toras Chatas the reason we distinguish regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin between its species and not its species: Shach brought it in siman 102 s.k. 4, and this is his language: that the importance is not from the prohibition itself, but since it will have permission afterward, etc.; and whenever it mixed with not its species the permission is not called by the name of the prohibited thing but by the name of the thing mixed into it, and it is like something that has no permitters (end quote). And if so regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin even not its species would not be fit to be nullified except because the prohibition is called by the name of the permitted; if so in such a case even the absorbed any-amount with another force mixed into it: granted according to R' Yehuda that same-species is not nullified by scriptural decree, in such a case it is called not its species as Nekudos HaKesef wrote; but regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin, since in any case it is called by the name of the prohibition even if it is not its species it is not nullified; therefore the Rama needed to say it afterward mixed into truly not its species; and this is a straight reasoning in my humble opinion. But Nekudos HaKesef necessarily does not hold my reasoning, for he too holds like the Rama that that absorbed any-amount forbids, and wrote that there is a practical difference regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin if it first fell into its species and forbade the piece in any amount, and afterward the piece fell even into its species it is nullified, for the second time it is not its species since another force is mixed into it: behold even regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin he holds it is not called its species and is nullified. And perhaps in Nekudos HaKesef he holds like the Ran's reason in Nedarim brought in Taz there siman 112 s.k. 5: that the Rabbis disagree with R' Yehuda and hold same-species is nullified only where the prohibition has no permission: then it is not [truly] same-species, for even though they are one species they differ in that this is forbidden and this permitted; therefore regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin the Rabbis agree that where it is same-species it is not nullified, for they are exactly equal: both one species, and also regarding forbidden and permitted they are equal, for the prohibition too will have permission; and this is the reason a davar sheyesh lo matirin is not nullified; therefore in not its species it is nullified, for even though equal regarding forbidden and permitted they differ in their species (end of his words). And according to this reason one may well distinguish if another force is mixed into it also regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin it is called not its species; and even though Shach copied the Rama's reason in Toras Chatas as above, nevertheless in his book Nekudos HaKesef one can say according to himself the Ran's reason was more reasonable to him. For the Bach himself, etc.: meaning the Bach also wrote this practical difference that is written in the gloss Orchos Chaim from the Rama: chametz within its time, etc.

<b>ומור"ם כו'</b> והוליכו לבית אחר כו' דהוי ממקום למקום. אבל אם נמצא חטה בחבית אז הוי מזמן לזמן. ולפ"ד הב"ח כו' וזהו נ"מ בין טעם רמ"א לטעם הב"ח דהוי חסרון ידיעה צ"ב ניהו דהספק שכתב הב"ח שמא לא נתחמצה הוי חסרון ידיעה דהמבין ומכיר ידע אם נתחמץ או לא מ"מ אפי' ודאי נתחמצ' הא איכא ס' שמא עתה נתחמצ'. וכמ"ש מ"א בשם ב"ח ס"ק כ"ה דאמרי' שמא עתה נתרככה וכ"מ בס' א"ר מ"מ איכא נ"מ אי נמצאת החט' במצה שנשאר' בידו לאחר אפיה ל"ל עתה נתחמצה אבל שמא לא נתחמצ' איכא למימר. אבל זה הוי ס' חסרון ידיעה דל"מ ספק. אולם כתב הש"ך בסי' נ"ג ס"ק ט"ו בשם תשו' מ"ב דבס"ס אפי' ב' הספקות הם מחמת חסרון ידיעה מ"מ בהפס"מ סמכי' על האי ס"ס. והטעם דהש"ך כתב בכללי ס"ס בסי' ק"י דבהפס"מ ואיכא ס"ס אפי' איכא לברורי א"צ לברר וא"כ אינו מעכב במה שיש בס"ס חסרון ידיעה דהא אפי' היה לו ידיעה והיה יכול לברר ע"י טוב הבחינה א"צ לברר. אלא שהפר"ח כ' שם שהש"ך סותר דברי עצמו בסי' נ"ה ס"ק ד' ויש ליישב והפר"ח גופו' ס"ל דלא מהני ס"ס היכא דאיכא חסרון ידיעה אזיל לטעמיה דס"ל דהיכי דאיכא לברורי בס"ס צריך לברר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And Moreinu HaRav, etc.</b> And they brought it to another house, etc.: for it is from place to place. But if a wheat kernel is found in a barrel then it is from time to time. And according to the Bach's words, etc.: and this is the practical difference between the Rama's reason and the Bach's reason that it is lack of knowledge: it requires understanding. Granted that the doubt the Bach wrote, "perhaps it did not become chametz," is lack of knowledge, for one who understands and recognizes knows whether it became chametz or not: nevertheless even if it certainly became chametz there is still a doubt: perhaps it became chametz now. And as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Bach in s.k. 25: we say perhaps it softened now; and so too in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah. Nevertheless there is a practical difference if the wheat kernel is found in matzah that remained in his hand after baking: one cannot say "it became chametz now," but "perhaps it did not become chametz" one can say. But this is a doubt of lack of knowledge that does not help [as] a doubt. However Shach wrote in siman 53 s.k. 15 in the name of a responsum of Mab that regarding a double doubt, even if both doubts are from lack of knowledge, nevertheless in major financial loss we rely on this double doubt. And the reason is that Shach wrote in the rules of double doubt in siman 110 that in major financial loss when there is a double doubt, even if one can clarify one need not clarify; and if so it does not impede that there is lack of knowledge in the double doubt, for even if he had knowledge and could clarify through good examination he need not clarify. Only that Peri Chadash wrote there that Shach contradicts himself in siman 55 s.k. 4, and there is room to resolve; and Peri Chadash himself holds that a double doubt does not help where there is lack of knowledge: he follows his own reason that wherever one can clarify in a double doubt one must clarify.
