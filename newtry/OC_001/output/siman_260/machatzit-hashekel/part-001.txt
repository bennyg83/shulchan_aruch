<b>כ' מ"א וכ' הב"ח כו'</b> שיש טעם יפה. והיינו מ"ש מ"א בסס"ק זה בשם ס' הגן. וצ"ע כו' בפ"ב ובפ"ד כו' פ"ב הוא מה דתנן דף ט"ו ע"ב אנשי מעמד (והם מה דתנן דף ך"ו ע"א אחרי שהתמידים שהיו קריבים בכל יום יש לכל ישראל חלק בהם והאיך קרבנו של אדם קרב והוא אינו עומד ע"ג התקינו נביאים ראשונים וחלקו לכהנים ולוים וישראלים לכ"ד חלקים וכהנים ולוים נקראים אנשי משמר והישראלים נקראים אנשי מעמד וכל חלק מכ"ד חלקים הנ"ל היה משמש שבוע א' וחוזר חלילה וכשהגיע זמן כל חלק מהחלקים היו הכהנים והלוים עולין לירושלים וכן הישראלים שהיו דרים בירושלים או הסמוכים לירושלים היו ג"כ עולין לירושלים והרחוקים מירושלים היו מתכנסים בעריהם כל יום לבתי כנסיות והיו קורי' בתורה כל יום במעשה בראשית וגם מתעכים כל ימי השבוע חוץ מיום א' ויום ו' והיו מתפללים כל יום שהקרבן תקובל ברצון לפניו יתברך ולכן נקראים אנשי מעמד שהם שלוחים מכל ישראל להיות עומדים על הקרבן) אסורים מלספר ולכבס (כדי שלא יכנסו למשמרתן כשהן מנוולים בלי סיפור וכיבוס לכן אסרו להם הסיפור וכיבוס ימי משמרתן כדי שיסתפרו ויכבסו קודם שיכנסו למשמרתן) ובחמישי מותרין מפני כבוד השבת ופירש"י דרך רוב ב"א כו' הרי דאנשי מעמד היו מסתפרים בחמישי ולא היו ממתינים עד ע"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The Magen Avraham wrote, and the Bach wrote, etc.</b> That there is a sound reason. This refers to what the Magen Avraham writes at the end of this se'if katan in the name of Sefer HaGan. The Magen Avraham's difficulty from the second and fourth chapters of Taanis is as follows. The second chapter refers to the Mishnah on folio 15b concerning the men of the ma'amad. The daily offerings brought each day belonged to all Israel, and a person's offering cannot be brought while he is not standing over it. The early prophets therefore divided the kohanim, leviim, and Israelites into twenty-four groups. The kohanim and leviim were called the men of the watch, and the Israelites were called the men of the ma'amad. Each group served for one week in rotation. When the time of a group arrived, the kohanim and leviim went up to Jerusalem. The Israelites who lived in or near Jerusalem also went up, while those far from Jerusalem gathered daily in the synagogues of their towns, read the account of Creation each day, fasted throughout the week except Sunday and Friday, and prayed that the offering be accepted favorably before Hashem. They were called men of the ma'amad because they served as the agents of all Israel to stand over the offering. They were forbidden to take haircuts or launder clothing during their week, so that they would prepare beforehand and not enter their watch unkempt. On Thursday they were permitted because of the honor of Shabbos. Rashi explains that most people take haircuts on Thursday, etc. Thus the men of the ma'amad took haircuts on Thursday and did not wait until erev Shabbos.

**** ENGLISH ****
The fourth chapter of Taanis begins on folio 26b. The Mishnah states that during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, it is forbidden to take a haircut or launder clothing, but on Thursday it is permitted because of the honor of Shabbos. Rashi explains that if Tisha B'Av falls on erev Shabbos, laundering is permitted on Thursday. This applies when the calendar was fixed by observation, since in our calendar Tisha B'Av cannot fall on erev Shabbos. This is not a proof against the Bach and Maharshal, who forbid taking a haircut on Thursday. That case is different because one cannot wait until Friday, which is Tisha B'Av, and therefore Thursday was permitted. Ordinarily, however, it may be preferable to wait until erev Shabbos. The proof is rather from Rashi's concluding words on folio 26: when Tisha B'Av falls on Wednesday, the Mishnah need not teach that laundering is permitted on Thursday, because the Gemara states that the restriction applies only before Tisha B'Av, while afterward it is permitted. Rashi thereby explains why he establishes the Mishnah when Tisha B'Av falls on Friday rather than on Wednesday. From this the Magen Avraham can indeed prove that taking a haircut on Thursday is ordinarily permitted. Otherwise, why was Rashi troubled? Perhaps the Mishnah concerns Tisha B'Av on Wednesday, and it was necessary to permit a haircut on Thursday, since one might otherwise have required waiting until Friday. If Tisha B'Av fell on Friday, however, Thursday had to be permitted. It follows that Thursday is ordinarily permitted. There is also a proof from the Rambam. The Rambam omitted the law that Thursday is permitted. Lechem Mishneh explains that he follows his commentary to the Mishnah, where he writes that before Tisha B'Av no leniency was granted even for the honor of Shabbos. The Mishnah therefore concerns Tisha B'Av on Wednesday and follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who forbids even afterward. Thursday was permitted only for the honor of Shabbos. Since we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, the Rambam omitted this law. According to us, a special permission for Thursday would arise only if Tisha B'Av fell on Friday. If it fell on Wednesday, permission on Thursday would be obvious because we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Yet if it fell on Friday, Thursday would actually be forbidden because it precedes Tisha B'Av. Thus, according to the Rambam, the Mishnah concerns Tisha B'Av on Wednesday, when one could wait until Friday, and nevertheless the Sages permitted Thursday, apparently against the Bach in the name of Maharshal. In my humble opinion, however, no proof may be brought from the fourth chapter according to either Rashi or the Rambam. The permission on Thursday may refer only to laundering and not to taking a haircut. Rashi on folio 26b explicitly says that if Tisha B'Av falls on erev Shabbos, laundering is permitted on Thursday. He mentions only laundering and omits haircutting. This actually supports the Bach and Maharshal, that haircutting is forbidden on Thursday, whereas laundering was permitted in accordance with Ezra's enactment that clothing be laundered on Thursday. See the Taz here for the reason. The proof from the second chapter, however, is sound, for Rashi writes there that on Thursday of their watch they were permitted, since most people take haircuts on Thursday rather than erev Shabbos because of the burden. Rashi explicitly applies the permission to haircutting and says this is common practice. Perhaps, since Rashi explained the permission in the second chapter as including haircutting, the same wording in the fourth chapter also includes it, and his mention of laundering there is not exclusive. This is somewhat forced, and the Magen Avraham's resolution does not fully address it.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who burns his nails, etc.</b> The Gemara in Niddah, in the chapter Kol HaYad, explains that a pregnant woman might step over them and miscarry, for this is a property of cut nails. In the chapter Elu Megalchin it is stated that in a study hall, where women are uncommon, they may be thrown away, because there is no concern. Although the study hall might later be swept and the nails carried outside where a pregnant woman could step over them, the Sages were not concerned, because once the nails have been moved from the place where they first fell at the time of cutting, they no longer cause harm. Therefore, if nails accidentally fall to the floor while being cut, one should sweep that place. However, Pri Chadash in Yoreh De'ah siman 115 writes that if, while cutting them, one placed them in one place with the immediate intention of moving them elsewhere, then the second place assumes the property of the first and they can cause harm. He brings a proof; see there. Tosafos explain why burning them is more pious than burying them: burning eliminates the possibility that they will later be uncovered. The Aruch explains that burning a nail harms the person, as does burning anything that comes from the human body. He is therefore considered pious because he is stringent to burn them even though it harms him. Regarding bathing, Tevuos Shor writes that Pesachim 111b states that one should not put on his shoes while his feet are still wet from bathing, because this endangers the eyesight. People are not particular about this, and 'Hashem protects the simple.'

<b>Paragraph 2: In a gentle tone; because a person is forbidden to impose excessive fear, etc.</b> Tosefes Shabbos writes: At first glance these words are astonishing. Certainly one may impose some fear upon members of his household, as the chapter HaOreg relates concerning several Amoraim. Here the requirement to speak in an actually gentle tone does not arise from excessive fear, but so that they will accept his words, as the Gemara explicitly says and Beis Yosef writes. Magen Avraham must mean that if one were permitted to impose fear, he would not need to speak calmly so that they accept his words; he could speak harshly with excessive fear and they would be compelled to obey. Since excessive fear is forbidden, he must speak calmly. This is obvious.

אמר רבב"ח הא דאמרי רבנן ג' דברים צ"ל תוך ביתו ערב שבת עם חשיכה צריך לממרינהו בניחותא כ"ה דלקבלו מיניה כו' אר"א לעולם אל יטיל אדם אימה יתירה תוך ביתו כו' והוי קשיא למ"א מאי שייכות יש לדברי רבב"ח שאמר שצ"ל הני ג"ד בניחותא כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה שם בגיטין ובאמצע דברי שאר אמוראי' שמדברי' מהטלת אימה יתירה שלא יבא לידי איסור ומכשול אע"כ דגם רבב"ח במה שאמר כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה נתכוין לדברי שאר אמוראים שאסרו אימה יתירה שלא יבא לידי מכשול ואיסור לכן מפרש מ"א ומ"ש כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיני' אין הכוונ' שיצייתו לו דמה"ת לא ישמעו לדבריו הלא כל איש שורר בביתו וכדאמרי' פ"ק דמגיל' דאפי' קרח' בביתא פרדשכי ליהוי אלא ישאל להם בנחת עשרתם כו' אם עדיין לא עשרו כו' יאמרו עדיין לא עשרנו אבל עתה נעשר וכן יעשו באמת אבל אם לא ישאל להם בנחת כיון דסתם אימת בעה"ב על אנשי ביתו כיון דהוא סמוך לחשכה הי' ראוי לעשות הדברים אשר שואל עליהן קודם שישאל ולא להתרשל ולהמתין עד סוף הזמן ומחמת אימה יכחשו ויאמרו שכבר עשרו כו' ואפשר שוב לא יעשרו אם מצד שכחה או מצד אימה פן יודע לו או בעיניו יראה שעשרו אחר השאלה ויראה שכחשו במה שאמרו תחלה שכבר עשרו אח"כ מצאתי כן בדריש' שכתב על מה שכתב הטור שישאל בנחת וז"ל בנחת כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה פירוש שאם לא עשו יעשו עוד אבל אם אומר בלשון אימה יתירה דלמא מכח אימה יאמרו עשינו ולא עשו גמרא עכ"ל הדרישה. וכן הרא"ש בפ' במה מדליקין הביא סמוך למימרא דרבב"ח הנ"ל שישאל בנחת מימרא דגיטין דאסור להטיל אימה יתיר':
**** ENGLISH ****
Rabbah bar bar Chanah says: The three matters that the Sages said one must state in his home as Shabbos approaches should be said calmly so that the household will accept them. Rabbi Avahu then says that one should never impose excessive fear in his household, etc. Magen Avraham found it difficult why Rabbah bar bar Chanah's statement (that these three questions must be asked calmly so they will be accepted) appears there in Gittin amid the statements of other Amoraim concerning excessive fear that can cause prohibitions and calamity. It must be that Rabbah bar bar Chanah too intended the other Amoraim's prohibition against excessive fear because it can cause a violation. Magen Avraham therefore explains that "so they accept it" does not mean merely that they obey, for why would they not? Every man has authority in his home, as Megillah says that even the lowliest man should be an officer in his house. Rather, he should gently ask whether they tithed, etc. If they have not yet tithed, they will say so and then do it. If he does not ask gently, the household naturally fears the head of the home. Since darkness is approaching, they should already have done what he asks about rather than delay until the last moment. Out of fear they may lie and say they already tithed, and then perhaps never tithe; whether from forgetfulness or fear that he will discover, or see them tithing after the question and realize their initial claim was false. I later found this in Prishah on Tur's instruction to ask calmly: "Calmly, so they will accept it" means that if they have not done it, they will still do so; but if he speaks with excessive fear, they may say from fear that they did it when they did not. This is the Gemara's meaning. The Rosh in the chapter Bameh Madlikin likewise places immediately after Rabbah bar bar Chanah's statement to ask calmly the teaching in Gittin forbidding excessive fear.
