**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Magen Avraham writes: Bach writes, etc.; that there is a fine reason.</b> This is what Magen Avraham writes at the end of this paragraph in the name of Sefer HaGan. "This requires study, etc., from chapter 2 and chapter 4." Chapter 2 refers to the Mishnah on Taanis 15b concerning the members of the maamad. The Mishnah on 26a explains that all Israel has a share in the daily offerings, and asks how a person's offering can be brought when he is not standing over it. The early prophets therefore divided the Kohanim, Leviim, and Israelites into twenty-four divisions. The Kohanim and Leviim were called members of the mishmar, and the Israelites members of the maamad. Each division served for one week in rotation. When a division's time arrived, its Kohanim and Leviim ascended to Yerushalayim. Israelites living in or near Yerushalayim also went there, while those distant from Yerushalayim gathered daily in their towns' synagogues, read the account of Creation from the Torah each day, fasted throughout the week except Sunday and Friday, and prayed daily that the offering be favorably accepted before Hashem. They were called members of the maamad because they served as all Israel's emissaries to stand over the offering. They were forbidden to cut their hair or wash their clothes during their week, lest they enter their service unkempt; the prohibition induced them to cut their hair and wash before entering. On Thursday they were permitted because of the honor of Shabbos. Rashi explains that most people customarily, etc. Thus, the members of the maamad would cut their hair on Thursday and did not wait until Erev Shabbos.

שכתב שם וז"ל ובה' של משמרתו מותרים דרך רוב ב"א להסתפר בה' ולא בע"ש מפני הטורח הרי דרש"י העתיק להסתפר מותר בה' וגם כ' דכן דרך העולם ואולי כיון דרש"י פי' בדף ט"ו ובה' מותר על תספורת ה"ה בדף כ"ו דקתני גם **** ENGLISH ****
Chapter 4 of Taanis means the beginning of chapter 4, folio 26b: During the week in which Tishah BeAv occurs, haircutting and laundering are forbidden, but on Thursday they are permitted because of the honor of Shabbos. Rashi explains that if Tishah BeAv occurs on Erev Shabbos, laundering is permitted Thursday. This occurred when months were sanctified through eyewitness testimony; as Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura writes, under our calendar Tishah BeAv does not occur on Erev Shabbos. This does not refute Bach and Maharshal, who forbid haircutting on Thursday. The week of Tishah BeAv differs because one cannot wait until Friday when Friday itself is Tishah BeAv; Thursday was therefore permitted. Ordinarily, however, Erev Shabbos may be preferable. Magen Avraham's proof comes instead from Rashi's continuation on folio 26: When Tishah BeAv occurs Wednesday, the Mishnah need not teach that these activities are permitted, because the Gemara says that the restriction applies only before Tishah BeAv, not afterward. Rashi thereby explains why he establishes the Mishnah when Tishah BeAv is Friday, a case possible only when months were sanctified by testimony, rather than the simpler case of Wednesday: Wednesday is obvious and need not be taught. This does support Magen Avraham that Thursday is ordinarily permitted. Otherwise, why did Rashi ask? Perhaps the Mishnah concerns a Wednesday Tishah BeAv; haircutting on Thursday then had to be permitted because one could otherwise wait until Friday. The fact that this is obvious shows that Thursday is generally permitted. Rambam also provides evidence. He omits the permission on Thursday. Lechem Mishneh explains that Rambam follows his commentary to the Mishnah, where he writes that even for the honor of Shabbos the prohibition was not relaxed before Tishah BeAv. The Mishnah therefore concerns a Wednesday Tishah BeAv and follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who forbids even afterward; Thursday was permitted only for the honor of Shabbos. Since we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, Rambam omits the Thursday permission: for us it occurs only if Tishah BeAv is Friday. If it is Wednesday, permission is obvious because we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as Rashi writes; whereas if it is Friday, Thursday remains before the fast and is forbidden. Thus, Rambam interprets the Mishnah as Wednesday, when one could cut hair and launder Friday, yet Chazal permitted Thursday because most people cut hair then. This disproves Bach in Maharshal's name. In my humble opinion, however, chapter 4 supplies no proof under either Rashi or Rambam. One can say that "on Thursday they are permitted" refers only to laundering, not haircutting. Rashi's wording on 26b is: "On Thursday they are permitted; if Tishah BeAv is Erev Shabbos, they may launder Thursday." He mentions laundering and omits haircutting, which if anything supports Bach and Maharshal: haircutting is forbidden Thursday, while laundering is permitted because Ezra instituted in Bava Kamma 82b that people launder Thursday. See Taz here for the reason. Chapter 2, however, supplies a sound proof for Magen Avraham. Rashi states there: "On Thursday of their mishmar they are permitted; most people customarily cut their hair Thursday rather than Erev Shabbos because of the burden." He explicitly permits haircutting Thursday and says this is common practice. Perhaps because Rashi interpreted the Thursday permission on folio 15 as including haircutting, the same phrase on folio 26 likewise includes haircutting, and Rashi's mention there of laundering alone is imprecise. This is strained, and Magen Avraham's answer does not resolve it.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>One who burns his fingernails, etc.</b> The reason stated in Niddah, chapter Kol HaYad, is that a pregnant woman might pass over them and miscarry, for cut nails possess this property. Moed Katan, chapter Eilu Megalchin, therefore says that in a beis midrash, where women are uncommon, they may be thrown away because there is no concern. Although the beis midrash may later be swept and the nails swept outside, where a pregnant woman might pass over them, Chazal did not consider this, because once the nails have been moved from the original place where they fell when cut, they no longer harm. For this reason, if nails accidentally fall to the floor while being cut, one should sweep that place. Pri Chadash in Yoreh Deah, siman 115, writes, however, that if while cutting them one placed them in one location with immediate intent to transfer them elsewhere, and then placed them in that other location, the second location has the property of the first and they can cause harm. He brings proof; see there. Tosafos explains why burning them is more pious than burying them: a pious person burns them, because then they cannot become uncovered. Aruch explains that burning a fingernail harms a person, as does burning anything that comes from the person; he is therefore pious for burning them despite the harm to himself. Regarding bathing, Sefer Tosefes Shabbos writes that Pesachim 111b states that one should not put on shoes while his feet are still wet from bathing water, because this endangers the eyesight. People are not particular, and "Hashem protects the simple."

אמר רבב"ח הא דאמרי רבנן ג' דברים צ"ל תוך ביתו ערב שבת עם חשיכה צריך לממרינהו בניחותא כ"ה דלקבלו מיניה כו' אר"א לעולם אל יטיל אדם אימה יתירה תוך ביתו כו' והוי קשיא למ"א מאי שייכות יש לדברי רבב"ח שאמר שצ"ל הני ג"ד בניחותא כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה שם בגיטין ובאמצע דברי שאר אמוראי' שמדברי' מהטלת אימה יתירה שלא יבא לידי איסור ומכשול אע"כ דגם רבב"ח במה שאמר כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה נתכוין לדברי שאר אמוראים שאסרו אימה יתירה שלא יבא לידי מכשול ואיסור לכן מפרש מ"א ומ"ש כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיני' אין הכוונ' שיצייתו לו דמה"ת לא ישמעו לדבריו הלא כל איש שורר בביתו וכדאמרי' פ"ק דמגיל' דאפי' קרח' בביתא פרדשכי ליהוי אלא ישאל להם בנחת עשרתם כו' אם עדיין לא עשרו כו' יאמרו עדיין לא עשרנו אבל עתה נעשר וכן יעשו באמת אבל אם לא ישאל להם בנחת כיון דסתם אימת בעה"ב על אנשי ביתו כיון דהוא סמוך לחשכה הי' ראוי לעשות הדברים אשר שואל עליהן קודם שישאל ולא להתרשל ולהמתין עד סוף הזמן ומחמת אימה יכחשו ויאמרו שכבר עשרו כו' ואפשר שוב לא יעשרו אם מצד שכחה או מצד אימה פן יודע לו או בעיניו יראה שעשרו אחר השאלה ויראה שכחשו במה שאמרו תחלה שכבר עשרו אח"כ מצאתי כן בדריש' שכתב על מה שכתב הטור שישאל בנחת וז"ל בנחת כ"ה דלקבלינהו מיניה פירוש שאם לא עשו יעשו עוד אבל אם אומר בלשון אימה יתירה דלמא מכח אימה יאמרו עשינו ולא עשו גמרא עכ"ל הדרישה. וכן הרא"ש בפ' במה מדליקין הביא סמוך למימרא דרבב"ח הנ"ל שישאל בנחת מימרא דגיטין דאסור להטיל אימה יתיר':
**** ENGLISH ****
Rabbah bar bar Chanah says: The three matters that the Sages said one must state in his home as Shabbos approaches should be said calmly so that the household will accept them. Rabbi Avahu then says that one should never impose excessive fear in his household, etc. Magen Avraham found it difficult why Rabbah bar bar Chanah's statement (that these three questions must be asked calmly so they will be accepted) appears there in Gittin amid the statements of other Amoraim concerning excessive fear that can cause prohibitions and calamity. It must be that Rabbah bar bar Chanah too intended the other Amoraim's prohibition against excessive fear because it can cause a violation. Magen Avraham therefore explains that "so they accept it" does not mean merely that they obey, for why would they not? Every man has authority in his home, as Megillah says that even the lowliest man should be an officer in his house. Rather, he should gently ask whether they tithed, etc. If they have not yet tithed, they will say so and then do it. If he does not ask gently, the household naturally fears the head of the home. Since darkness is approaching, they should already have done what he asks about rather than delay until the last moment. Out of fear they may lie and say they already tithed, and then perhaps never tithe; whether from forgetfulness or fear that he will discover, or see them tithing after the question and realize their initial claim was false. I later found this in Prishah on Tur's instruction to ask calmly: "Calmly, so they will accept it" means that if they have not done it, they will still do so; but if he speaks with excessive fear, they may say from fear that they did it when they did not. This is the Gemara's meaning. The Rosh in the chapter Bameh Madlikin likewise places immediately after Rabbah bar bar Chanah's statement to ask calmly the teaching in Gittin forbidding excessive fear.
