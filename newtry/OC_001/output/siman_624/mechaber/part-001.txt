<i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ד" data-order="4"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ו"></i>מברכין על האור <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ה" data-order="5"></i><i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="2"></i>ואין מברכים <i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ז"></i>במוצאי יום הכפורים על האור <i data-commentator="Turei Zahav" data-order="3"></i>שהוציאו <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="3"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ח"></i>עתה מן האבנים <i data-commentator="Be'er HaGolah" data-label="ו" data-order="6"></i>וי"א שמברכים עליו <i data-commentator="Magen Avraham" data-order="3"></i><i data-commentator="Mishnah Berurah" data-label="ט"></i>מעמוד <i data-commentator="Ba'er Hetev" data-order="4"></i>ראשון ואילך:
**** ENGLISH ****
We (say a) blessing over the light, but we do not say a blessing after Yom Kippur on the light which they brought forth now from stones (i.e., sparks). Some say that we do say the benediction over the first fire which was ignited by this spark and onward. 232 To say the blessing over the lights in the Havdalah ceremony one may not use a fire that one sees from the first spark that results when two stones are struck together. The fire that can be used for the prayer has to be the result of at least the second fire that was ignited by those first sparks. The first sparks do not burn long enough to say a blessing over them. According to the commentator Magen Avraham , a commentary on the Shulḥan Arukh, Oraḥ Ḥayyim by Abraham Abele Gombiner (see footnote 33), the first sparks are not a real fire. Only the second fire ignited from the first sparks is considered a real fire that continues to burn long enough for a benediction to be recited over it.

A Jew who kindled [a flame] from a non-Jew — we do not recite a blessing over it on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, even though on Motza'ei Shabbos we do recite a blessing over it; for on Motza'ei Yom Kippur we recite a blessing only over a flame that rested [i.e., was burning] while it was actually still day, or over a flame that was kindled from it. And so is the custom, to kindle from the lamps of the synagogue. However, even if it was kindled on Yom Kippur, if it was kindled permissibly — such as for a sick person — one may recite a blessing over it. {Rama: Some say to make Havdalah over a synagogue lamp (Maggid; Avudraham; Or Zarua); and some say not to make Havdalah over it, but rather to kindle another lamp from it; and the correct [practice] is to make Havdalah over both together — that is, to kindle one lamp from the synagogue lamp — and not to make Havdalah over the synagogue lamp itself alone [Maharil]. We do not say supplications nor "Tzidkascha" from Yom Kippur until Sukkos; and the meticulous begin immediately on Motza'ei Yom Kippur with the building of the sukkah, in order to go from mitzvah to mitzvah [Maharil; Minhagim; Hagahos Maimoni, chapter 2 of Berachos; Mahari Weil].}
