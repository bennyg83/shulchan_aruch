**** ENGLISH ****
(23) Shehakol. Since the water is so abundant that the mixture is unfit for eating and is suitable only for drinking, it is not considered food at all, and one recites Shehakol as one does over water. This applies specifically to flour whose substance is no longer discernible and is nullified in the abundant water. If, however, one prepares a dish from groats made from the five species of grain, such as those made in our countries from barley or oats, where each kernel is split in two, and adds so much water that it is suitable only to be drunk as soup, the groats are not nullified to the water, since they remain discernible and the five species of grain are not nullified. One must therefore recite Borei Minei Mezonos over the groats. It is nevertheless possible that the water is likewise not nullified to the groats, since the dish was prepared primarily for drinking rather than eating, and one must also recite Shehakol over the water. He should therefore first recite Shehakol over the water and afterward Borei Minei Mezonos over the groats. This follows from Magen Avraham here and in siman 205. Chayei Adam writes that it is preferable to recite Shehakol over another food and thereby exempt the liquid. All this applies specifically to the five species of grain, which are not nullified. With other species, however, such as buckwheat groats prepared with so much water that they are unsuitable for eating and serve only as soup, one recites only Shehakol, because the groats are nullified to the water.

(25) Rice. Rice is reis, and millet is hirz in the vernacular. Beit Yosef and Levush. Some reverse these identifications. Therefore, some are stringent not to eat either of them after it has been crushed except during a meal, or else to recite Shehakol because of the uncertainty. Lechem Chamudos writes that the generally accepted identification is that rice is reis and millet is hirz. This is also evident from Birkei Yosef and Mateh Yehudah. I likewise found in Maaseh Rav, recording the practices of the Gra, that rice is reis and that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos over it.

(33) Over millet bread, etc. Although millet grows from the ground and improves when made into bread, once it has left its original form it can no longer be described as “the fruit of the ground,” while HaMotzi is recited only over bread from the five species of grain. According to this reason, in countries where corn is regularly made into bread, one would apparently recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, since that is its normal use. Peri Megadim. Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 50, however, concludes that one never recites Borei Peri HaAdamah over such bread. These crops are not planted for this purpose, but chiefly for birds and for fattening geese, and only under pressure do people make bread from them. Bread is therefore not their primary produce, and Borei Peri HaAdamah should not be recited. See there. He remains uncertain, however, whether perhaps they are included in the category of rice, whose bread and cooked dishes require Borei Minei Mezonos.
