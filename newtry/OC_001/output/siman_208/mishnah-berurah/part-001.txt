(15) <b>And the kernels remain whole.</b> They were neither initially divided nor mashed through cooking, so they are no more nourishingly significant than other ground produce. If pounding removed the hull, some say cooking makes their blessing Borei Minei Mezonos as a cooked grain dish; others say Borei Pri HaAdamah because the kernels remain whole. It is best to eat them only during a meal, apart from Tosafos's view below. If cooking made them adhere, one may initially bless Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward Al HaMichyah, as paragraph 6 states. For pearl barley whose milling removes both hull and some kernel, making it smaller, those who initially recite Mezonos and Al HaMichyah even without mashing should not be protested.

(כג) שהכל - דכיון שהמים רבים עליו כ"כ עד שאינו ראוי לאכילה ורק לשתיה אינו בכלל מאכל כלל ומברכין שהכל כברכת המים. וכ"ז הוא דוקא בקמח שממשו אינו בעין ומתבטל בריבוי המים אבל העושה תבשיל ממיני גרויפי"ן שנעשה מה' מיני דגן [כגון הנעשים במדינתנו משעורים ושבולת שועל שנחלק כל גרעין לשנים] ונתן בהם מים הרבה עד שאינו ראוי רק לשרפו שקורין זופ"א אין הגרויפ"ן בטילין לגבי המים כיון שהם בעין ומיני דגן לא בטלי וצריך לברך על הגרויפי"ן במ"מ ומ"מ אפשר שגם המים לא בטלי לגבייהו כיון שעיקרן נעשה רק לשתיה ולא לאכילה וצריך לברך גם על המים שהכל וע"כ יברך תחלה על המים ואח"כ על הגרויפי"ן [כן מתבאר מדברי המ"א בסימן זה ובסימן ר"ה] ובח"א כתב שיותר טוב בזה לברך שהכל על דבר אחר ויוציא את הרוטב. וכ"ז דוקא בה' מיני דגן דלא בטלי אבל בשאר מינים כגון רעצק"ע גרויפי"ן שנעשים בריבוי מים שאינם ראוין לאכילה ורק לזופ"א מברך ברכה אחת שהכל דהגרויפי"ן נתבטלו לגבי המים:
**** ENGLISH ****
(23) Shehakol. Since the water is so abundant that the mixture is unfit for eating and is suitable only for drinking, it is not considered food at all, and one recites Shehakol as one does over water. This applies specifically to flour whose substance is no longer discernible and is nullified in the abundant water. If, however, one prepares a dish from groats made from the five species of grain, such as those made in our countries from barley or oats, where each kernel is split in two, and adds so much water that it is suitable only to be drunk as soup, the groats are not nullified to the water, since they remain discernible and the five species of grain are not nullified. One must therefore recite Borei Minei Mezonos over the groats. It is nevertheless possible that the water is likewise not nullified to the groats, since the dish was prepared primarily for drinking rather than eating, and one must also recite Shehakol over the water. He should therefore first recite Shehakol over the water and afterward Borei Minei Mezonos over the groats. This follows from Magen Avraham here and in siman 205. Chayei Adam writes that it is preferable to recite Shehakol over another food and thereby exempt the liquid. All this applies specifically to the five species of grain, which are not nullified. With other species, however, such as buckwheat groats prepared with so much water that they are unsuitable for eating and serve only as soup, one recites only Shehakol, because the groats are nullified to the water.

(25) Rice. Rice is reis, and millet is hirz in the vernacular. Beit Yosef and Levush. Some reverse these identifications. Therefore, some are stringent not to eat either of them after it has been crushed except during a meal, or else to recite Shehakol because of the uncertainty. Lechem Chamudos writes that the generally accepted identification is that rice is reis and millet is hirz. This is also evident from Birkei Yosef and Mateh Yehudah. I likewise found in Maaseh Rav, recording the practices of the Gra, that rice is reis and that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos over it.

**** ENGLISH ****
(81) <b>And include with it, etc.</b> If one ate tree fruit but does not know whether it is among the seven species, and lacks another seven-species fruit, Magen Avraham permits taking wine or a food from the five grains, whose blessing is also three-faceted, and adding 'for the tree and its fruit.' Either way this wording applies, even if the doubtful fruit is not one of the seven, as in se'if 13.

**** ENGLISH ****
(82) <b>Because of doubt.</b> Taz and several later authorities hold this rationale applies only initially: one should not drink the doubtful beverage planning to rely on a later addition to another blessing. After the fact, once he drank it, it is better to include the doubtful phrase than leave it with no after-blessing at all.
