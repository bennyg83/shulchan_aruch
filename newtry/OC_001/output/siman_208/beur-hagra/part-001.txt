**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And afterward Borei Nefashos.</b> This applies even according to Tosafos’ view in seif 4, as stated below regarding “if he cooked it,” etc., and “afterward,” etc. See Rashi there, s.v. velo klum, and likewise the other poskim in siman 207. The Rif and Rosh write in chapter 6 of Berachos, seif 8: “We hold that whenever the Baraisa says that afterward he recites nothing, he recites Borei Nefashos,” etc. At first glance, it is difficult to understand how “nothing” can mean Borei Nefashos, and what their source is. It appears that the explanation is as follows. At the end of chapter 6, 44b, Rabbi Yehudah bar Abba said in the name of our master: “After an egg,” etc., “and meat,” etc., “one recites Borei Nefashos,” etc., but not after other foods. Later Amoraim added vegetables and water, and Rav Ashi said, “I,” etc., “follow all of them.” Tosafos and all the poskim ruled like Rav Ashi, since he was later, that one recites Borei Nefashos after vegetables, water, and other foods whose first blessing is Shehakol. See Tosafos there, s.v. avidna, etc.: “And so we practice,” etc. The earlier practice had been to recite it only after an egg and meat. The reason was that they interpreted nefashos as referring to foods derived from living creatures, as stated in the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 6: when Rabbi ate meat or an egg, etc., he would recite “Who created living beings,” etc. Therefore, the Baraisa concerning rice and millet taught that afterward one recites “nothing,” because according to their practice they in fact recited no after-blessing at all after rice bread or millet bread. According to our practice, however, which follows Rav Ashi, every case in which the Baraisa says “nothing” means Borei Nefashos. This is the meaning of “and we hold that whenever afterward,” etc., one recites Borei Nefashos.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 8: On, etc.</b> Rif and Rambam. Although the Baraisa there teaches both rice bread and millet bread, nevertheless, since the Baraisa concerning one who chews grain mentions only rice, and likewise the passage, “These are the cooked dishes,” etc., mentions rice, it follows that on millet one recites only Shehakol. The Rosh rejected this proof and wrote that rice was singled out to exclude the opinion of Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, but the same law applies to millet. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that rice is mentioned because even when whole its blessing is Mezonos, whereas millet requires that it be split, since it is not ordinarily eaten whole, as stated above. Both explanations are needed, for according to Rabbeinu Yonah one may ask why millet was not mentioned in the Baraisa concerning one who chews grain, while according to the Rosh one may ask why millet was not mentioned in the Baraisa concerning cooked dishes. Rabbeinu Yonah and the Tur write in the name of a Gaon that the same law applies to panicum, which is likewise sustaining like millet and, according to Rashi there, is itself millet. They, however, explain it in accordance with Tosafos, s.v. Rashi, etc., “and some explain,” etc. Beit Yosef writes that the common understanding follows this explanation. Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification like Rambam, who wrote, “millet bread,” etc., “or of,” etc., and Shulchan Aruch added “panizo.” It appears that this is panicum, except that in their language they call it panizo.

<b>And if he made, etc.</b> As stated in Challah, chapter 3, mishnah 7: “One who makes dough from wheat,” etc. Raavad writes that this applies only when there is a kezayis of grain within the quantity eaten in kedei achilas peras. Even according to Ramban and Rashba, who disagree and hold that this is unnecessary because the rice is drawn after the grain, that rule applies specifically to rice. See siman 453, seif 2, and what is stated there.
