**** ENGLISH ****
<b>But if he cooked, etc.</b> Meaning that he did not remove the husks. If he removed their husks, then even when the kernels remain whole they may become mashed and adhere together. It must therefore be that both the opening and concluding cases state a definite situation. In the opening case he speaks of grain that was crushed, because such grain ordinarily becomes mashed. In the concluding case he speaks of whole wheat, meaning that its husks were not removed, in which case it cannot adhere together. When the kernels are whole but their husks were removed, prolonged boiling sometimes causes them to become mashed and adhere together, and sometimes it does not.

<b>(Seif katan 5) Whole, etc.</b> This differs from rice, which is ordinarily cooked whole. Magen Avraham, seif 6, seif katan 10, explains that according to Beit Yosef one recites Borei Minei Mezonos on rice even when it did not become mashed. On that view, rice and wheat have the same law, and the Kesef Mishneh’s distinction has no place.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>(Seif katan 8) For eating and chewing, etc.</b> This is also implied by Berachos 38a, which discusses patisa, flour boiled with water or another liquid. Rav says Shehakol, while Shmuel says Borei Minei Mezonos. Rav Chisda explains that they do not disagree: on a thick mixture one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, and on a thin mixture one recites Shehakol. The unqualified term “thick” implies even if it is not fit to chew. Accordingly, “thin” must mean thin enough to drink. Although the Gemara says merely “thin,” it relies on its preceding unqualified statement concerning a thick mixture. The latter must include even a mixture that cannot be chewed, provided that it is thick, so the contrasting thin mixture must be one fit to drink. This interpretation is more reasonable than the reverse, because it is more natural for the later phrase to rely on the earlier one than for the earlier phrase to rely on a qualification stated only afterward.
