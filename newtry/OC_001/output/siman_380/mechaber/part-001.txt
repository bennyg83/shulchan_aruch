**** ENGLISH ****
Laws of renouncing domain. Contains 4 paragraphs: One of the courtyard residents who forgot and did not make an eruv with the others restricts them. What is their remedy? He renounces his domain to them by saying "my domain is renounced to you" or "transferred to you," and no formal acquisition is required. He may renounce even after nightfall. If he lives with four or five others he must renounce to each one individually, saying "my domain is renounced to you and to you." Some say it suffices to say "my domain is renounced to all of you." If he renounced his domain without specification, he has only renounced his share in the courtyard — therefore they are permitted to carry from their houses to the courtyard, and he too is like a mere guest. But they are forbidden to carry from his house to the courtyard, and so is he. Some say he must lock his house so that he not come to carry out in a forbidden manner, and he should only open it when he wishes to enter or exit, locking it immediately after.

If the courtyard residents who made an eruv renounce their domain to one who did not make an eruv — he is permitted to carry from his house to the courtyard but not from their houses They are forbidden even from his house to the courtyard, and we do not say they become like guests, since many cannot become guests of an individual. The same applies if there were only two and one renounced to the other — the one who renounced is forbidden even in the other's domain, and the other is permitted even in his own. Similarly, many who did not make an eruv who give their domain to many who did make an eruv — since an individual becomes a guest of the many, but many do not become guests even of many, nor does an individual become a guest of an individual. If two forgot and did not make an eruv, they may renounce their domain — each one renouncing his domain to all the courtyard residents — whether renouncing to the many who made an eruv, provided each one renounces to each individual, or whether renouncing to one who did not make an eruv. But they cannot renounce to two who did not make an eruv — whether the many who made an eruv are renouncing or a single individual who did not make an eruv — since even after they renounce to them, there are still two who restrict one another. Even if one says to the other "I renounce to you on condition that you in turn renounce to your fellow" — this is not effective. {Rama: (unless they explicitly renounced the domain of their houses as well).}
