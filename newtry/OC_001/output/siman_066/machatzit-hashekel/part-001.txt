Kesef Mishneh there. For this reason the Shulchan Aruch used the case of a Kohen who was reading, etc., as a stronger case for the view that says he should not go up. **** ENGLISH ****
<b>On the plain meaning of the Zohar.</b> Meaning, in siman 141 the Beis Yosef wrote that from the language of the poskim it is clear that the one called to the Torah must also read with the shaliach tzibbur who reads, for if he does not read with him his blessing is in vain. But in the Zohar it is written that only one should read. If so, it implies that the one called up should not read with the reader. Although wherever the Gemara disagrees with the Zohar we rule like our Gemara, nevertheless, since this law is not explicit in the Gemara but only in the words of the poskim, one should rule like the Zohar, which disagrees with them. There is also no issue of a blessing in vain, for one can say that since the one called up listens and directs his heart to the words of the reader, hearing is like answering. Afterward the Beis Yosef wrote that since the Zohar compares Torah reading to silent prayer, and regarding prayer the Zohar wrote that one should pray quietly until his voice is not heard, yet in any event speech is required in prayer, if so the same applies to the one called to the Torah: certainly the Zohar was particular only that the voice heard should be only the reader's voice and not the voice of the one called up, but quietly the one called up should also read, like the view of the poskim. Only regarding whether he may make it audible to his own ears is there a dispute between the Beis Yosef and Rama below. In any event, the simple meaning of the Zohar is that the one called up should not read at all but should only direct his heart to the words of the reader, and this was also the Beis Yosef's initial understanding, as above. Therefore in such a case one may rely on this not to read, but only to intend to the reader's words.
