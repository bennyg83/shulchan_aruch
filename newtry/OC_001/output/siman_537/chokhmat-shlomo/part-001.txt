ודוק: הנה ראיתי לחכם אחד בספרו שנשאל על בעה"ב שהקדים לאומנים שכרן שיעשו אצלו מלאכה בבנין ביתו ובתוך כך נלקחו האומנים לעבודת המלך ואין להם פנאי לעשות מלאכת הישראל רק בחוה"מ ואם לא יפסיד שכרו שאין להם חוב מותר בחוה"מ מחלק שם חילוקי דינים. אך לדעתי אין זה נכון דודאי הוי דבר האבד ואין דמיון חוה"מ לחזרת פועל דבחוה"מ אף ספק דבר האבד מותר וכדמשמע מדברי מוהריב"ל הובא במג"א סימן תקל"ט ס"ק ז' דדוקא אם יודע חוה"מ דקיל מעשה דלי בני ישראל עבדים. ויומתק בזה מה שהש"ע בחו"מ סימן של"ג סיים ואפי' קיבל כבר דמי שכירתו ואין לו לשלם לבעה"ב יכול לחזור בו והמעות חוב עליו שנאמר כי לי בני ישראל עבדים וכו'. ולכאורה אין **** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 1.</b> A davar ha'aved is permitted to do on Chol HaMoed, etc. Marginal note: See Magen Avraham s.k. 1, what he wrote, that a doubtful davar ha'aved is permitted; and see Magen Avraham in the previous siman, what he wrote at the end, "And therefore we do not toil on a doubt," implying that he holds a doubtful davar ha'aved is forbidden; and this contradicts that; and this requires further study; and examine carefully. Behold, I saw a certain scholar in his book who was asked about a homeowner who paid craftsmen their wages in advance that they would do work for him in building his house, and meanwhile the craftsmen were taken for the king's service and have no free time to do the Jew's work except on Chol HaMoed; and if not, he will lose his wages, for they have nothing with which to repay him: is this a davar ha'aved? And he replied that this is not a davar ha'aved, since it is possible that they will become wealthy; and he brought proof from the words of Beis Yosef in Choshen Mishpat siman 333, who wrote that the workers may retract even though they have nothing with which to return their wages, by force of "perhaps they will become wealthy"; see there. And as to what is explained in the Shulchan Aruch that claiming a debt is permitted on Chol HaMoed, he distinguishes there various laws. But in my opinion this is not correct, for certainly it is a davar ha'aved, and there is no comparison between Chol HaMoed and a worker's retraction; for on Chol HaMoed even a doubtful davar ha'aved is permitted, as is implied from the words of Mahari ibn Lev brought in Magen Avraham siman 539 s.k. 7, that only if he knows with certainty that he will not lose from the principal; but if it is doubtful to him, then it is permitted and is considered a davar ha'aved. And therefore all the more so a doubt of "perhaps he will become wealthy" is at least a doubtful davar ha'aved. And know that even regarding testimony it is not a [disqualifying] interest by force of "perhaps he will become wealthy," and it is proven that even a doubtful interest of his [matters]; all the more so regarding Chol HaMoed. However, since in any case it is a doubt, in the end a doubtful davar ha'aved is permitted on Chol HaMoed; but regarding a worker, he cannot compel him to work except for a certain davar ha'aved, but for a doubtful davar ha'aved he cannot compel him to work. And therefore where there is a doubt of "perhaps he will become wealthy," he may also retract; and Chol HaMoed labor is different, for it is lighter than the matter of "for unto Me the children of Israel are servants." And this sweetens what the Shulchan Aruch in Choshen Mishpat siman 333 concluded: And even if he already received the money of his hire and has nothing to pay the employer, he may retract, and the money is a debt upon him, as it is said "for unto Me the children of Israel are servants," etc. And prima facie it is not the way to write in his rulings a reason from the verses; and why here did he write a reason from the verse? And also here, regarding [the case where] he does not have [means] to pay, at first he should have written in the body of the law, where the Shulchan Aruch concluded that he retracts, "as it is said, for unto Me the children of Israel are servants," and afterwards he should have written "And even if he already received," etc. But this is the matter of which we spoke: that he came to resolve so that one not ask why regarding Chol HaMoed and testimony "perhaps he will become wealthy" is not considered a claim, and here a doubt by force of "perhaps he will become wealthy" is considered [valid]; and therefore he concluded, since it is written "for unto Me the children of Israel are servants" and not servants to servants, and therefore this positive commandment is strong, for it touches upon His honor, blessed be He; and just as the Torah penalized the [Hebrew] slave to be pleased [to serve] for that reason, so the Torah penalized the employer who hired him that the worker may retract whenever there is any side to say in any way that he has no loss. And one may further say that certainly by Torah law the worker could retract even regarding a davar ha'aved, for it is written without qualification "for unto Me the children of Israel are servants," and therefore it would be fitting that he be able to retract even for a certain davar ha'aved; only that his inability to retract regarding a davar ha'aved is only by rabbinic decree, for the Rabbis enacted that he cannot retract regarding a davar ha'aved, for Chazal were concerned for the employer's benefit and said that regarding a davar ha'aved he cannot retract. Therefore, since it is only rabbinic, they enacted only if it is a certain davar ha'aved, but regarding a doubt they did not enact. But on Chol HaMoed, according to the Torah they handed the matter to the Sages to permit as they wish; and if so, according to the Torah a davar ha'aved is permitted in this; and likewise regarding testimony, if he has no interest according to the Torah he is fit to testify; therefore whenever there is no interest here except in a remote way of "perhaps he will become wealthy," he is not an interested party. And even if we say that [the block on retracting for] a davar ha'aved regarding a worker is according to the Torah, it also fits well: for on Chol HaMoed, since no explicit positive commandment was stated regarding it, only that the Torah handed the matter to the Sages to say what is forbidden and what is permitted, therefore they said that even a doubtful davar ha'aved is also permitted; but regarding a worker, where there is an explicit positive commandment "for unto Me the children of Israel are servants," if so, however [true] that regarding a davar ha'aved he cannot retract, that is regarding a certain davar ha'aved; but regarding a doubt it is a Torah-level doubt [resolved] stringently, and the worker is in possession of himself. Therefore whenever there is any side to say that it will not be a davar ha'aved, he may retract; and especially since the worker, being in possession of himself, is like a presumption of ownership of money, regarding which we are concerned even for a minority, and we do not follow the majority. And with this it is comfortable what [explains] why it is unlike testimony: there regarding testimony, which depends on the witnesses' mind [such that we] are concerned that they are testifying because of benefit, in this we say that regarding a person's mind we certainly follow the majority and he is not concerned for the minority, and whatever is [only] by way of the minority is not considered benefit for him such that one should be concerned that because of it he is testifying; which is not so here, where it does not depend on a person's mind, but rather on the law that the Torah said that regarding a davar ha'aved he cannot retract; and therefore fine for a certain davar ha'aved, but whenever there is a side that it will not be a davar ha'aved, the worker may say that he is concerned for the minority and it is possible that he will become wealthy; and regarding money, where he is in possession, we are concerned for the minority; and therefore here too the worker is in possession of himself and we are concerned for the minority of "perhaps he will become wealthy." But on Chol HaMoed, which is only a prohibition, certainly we follow the majority; and since wealth is uncommon, and as Chazal said, "Your fellow became wealthy; do not ascribe [it to yourself]," therefore we follow the majority and there is a law of a davar ha'aved. So it appears correct and clear for practical law without hesitation; and in my opinion the matter is exceedingly obvious, and I wrote it only to remove [error] from the heart of one who reads in the book of the scholar that was newly printed. And in my opinion everything is clear as I wrote; and examine carefully well.
