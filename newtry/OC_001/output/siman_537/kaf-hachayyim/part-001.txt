<b>ב) שם.  </b>דבר האבד מותר וכו' אפי' ספק דבר האבד מותר. הרש"ך ח"א סי' קי"ג. כנה"ג בהגה"ט עו"ש או' א' מ"א ריש הסי' א"ר או' א' והטעם כתב א"א משום דחו"ה מדרבנן וספק לקולא. וכ"כ המחה"ש והלב"ש. וא"כ משמע דלמ"ד דאו' יש להחמיר. מיהו בב"י סי' תקל"ט ד"ה ומ"ש הרא"ש כתב וכיון דלאו דבר האבד בודאי הוא אסור וכו' יעו"ש. וזהו שכתב שם לדעת הרא"ש שהוא מכת הסוברים דמלאכת חו"ה איסורא מדרבנן כמ"ש בהרא"ש ריש מו"ק והובא בב"י סי' תק"ל יעו"ש. וא"כ מזה משמע דגם למ"ד מדרבנן ספק דבר האבד אסור. אמנם לכאורה נראה דיש סתירה לזה ממשנה מ"ק י"ח ע"ב ואלו כותבין במועד וכו' וכתבו שם התו' נראה דכל הני הוו דבר האבד פן ימות הנותן וכו' וכ"כ שם בחי' הריטב"א ומשמע מזה דמקילין מספק וכ"מ מתשו' הרשב"א שהביא בב"י סי' תקמ"ה שכתב הטעם דמתיר התוספתא לכתוב חשבונותיו במועד שמא לא יזכור ויאבד ממונו עכ"ל והרשב"א הוא מכת הסוברים דמלאכת חו"ה אסורה מדאו' כמ"ש בב"י סי' תק"ל ואפ"ה סובר ספק דבר האבד מותר. וכ"כ המ"מ פ"ז מה' יו"ט דין ג' בשם הרמב"ן דמה שהצריכו בהכנסת פירות בצנעה (עיין לקמן סי' תקל"ח סעי' ב') לפי שאינן ודאי הפסד אלא ספק וכו' יעו"ש הרי מתיר בספק דבר האבד והוא ג"כ מכת הסוברים דאו' כמ"ש בב"י סי' תק"ל יעו"ש. וע"כ נראה דאין זה תלוי בדאו' או בדרבנן אלא כל שהוא קרוב לודאי שיבא לידי הפסד לפי הראות יש להתיר אפי' בספק וכל שהוא חששא רחוקה אין להתיר מספק. ועיין ח"א כלל ק"ו או' ה' שכתב דספק דבר האבד יעשה ע"י עכו"ם יעו"ש. וע"כ אם אפשר יש להחמיר ולעשות ע"י עכו"ם וכן בחששא רחוקה אם יבא לידי הפסד יעשה ע"י עכו"ם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>2) There. A matter subject to loss is permitted, etc. </b>Even a doubtful matter subject to loss is permitted. Maharshach, volume 1, siman 113; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, at the beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. Eshel Avraham explains that the reason is that the laws of Chol HaMoed are rabbinic, so a doubt is treated leniently. Machatzis HaShekel and Levushei Serad write likewise. This implies that according to the opinion that the prohibition is under Torah law, one should be stringent. Nevertheless, Beis Yosef, siman 539, s.v. u'mah shekasav haRosh, writes: 'Since it is not certainly subject to loss, it is forbidden, etc.' See there. He writes this according to Rosh, who is among those who maintain that melachah on Chol HaMoed is prohibited rabbinically, as Rosh writes at the beginning of Moed Katan and Beis Yosef cites in siman 530. See there. This implies that even according to the rabbinic opinion, a doubtful matter subject to loss is forbidden. At first glance, however, this appears to contradict the mishnah in Moed Katan 18b, 'The following documents may be written during the festival, etc.' Tosafos there write that all these are matters subject to loss, lest the giver die, etc. Ritva writes likewise there in his novellae. This implies that we rule leniently in a doubtful case. The same appears from Responsa Rashba cited by Beis Yosef in siman 545. Rashba explains that the Tosefta permits one to write his accounts during the festival lest he forget and lose his money. Rashba is among those who maintain that melachah on Chol HaMoed is forbidden under Torah law, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 530, yet he permits a doubtful matter subject to loss. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 3, likewise writes in the name of Ramban that produce must be brought in privately because the loss is uncertain rather than definite. See below, siman 538, se'if 2. This shows that he permits a doubtful matter subject to loss, and Ramban too is among those who hold that the prohibition is under Torah law, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 530. See there. It therefore appears that this does not depend upon whether the prohibition is under Torah or rabbinic law. Whenever it appears nearly certain that a loss will result, one may permit the work despite some doubt. When the concern is remote, one may not permit it because of the doubt. Chayei Adam, rule 106, subsection 5, writes that a doubtful matter subject to loss should be performed through a non-Jew. See there. Therefore, when possible, one should be stringent and have a non-Jew perform it. Likewise, when the concern is remote, if a loss may result, it should be performed through a non-Jew.

<b>ג) שם.  </b>דבר האבד מותר וכו' להדפיס ספרים בחו"ה אף שיש לו פועלים שפסק להם שכרן קבוע מידי חודש בחדשו בין אם יהיה לו פעולה בין אם לא יהיה לו לא מיקרי דבר האבד בשביל שמאבד שכר פועלים מאחר שמלאכת הדפוס בעצמה אינה דבר האבד. וגם אם יקח פועלים שאין להם מה לאכול אסור דאין אדם יכול לעשות מלאכה בחו"ה כדי לתת שכר לפועל שאין לו מה יאכל. מהר"ם פרובינצא"ל בתשו' כ"י סי' נ"ח והב"ד ברכ"י או' א' וכתב עליו ועיין לקמן סי' תקמ"ב ובמ"ש האחרונים ודוק עכ"ל ור"ל דשם בסי' תקמ"ב סעי' ב' כתב דמותר ליתן מלאכה לפועל שאין לו מה יאכל. ואיך הוא אוסר. וכ"כ הברכ"י שם או' ב' על דברי מהר"ם הנז' דדבריו צריכים ישוב. והב"ד השע"ת בסי' זה וכתב ליישב דברי מהר"ם הנז' דס"ל דדוקא שהמלאכה יש בה קצת ליו"ט התירו משא"כ מלאכת הדפוס יעו"ש. ולע"ד נראה דוחק חילוק זה דבסתמא התירו לפועל שאין לו וכו' דמשמע כל מלאכה אפי' שאינה צורך המועד אבל הנלע"ד ליישב דמה שהתירו לתת מלאכה לפועל וכו' הוא דוקא שיקח המלאכה ברשותו ויתעסק בה דאף אם יראו אותו ידעו שזהו בשביל שאין לו וכו' אבל לא יעשה המלאכה ברשות הנותן מפני שיחשדוהו שעושה מלאכתו במועד ולכן בדפוס שמוכרח הוא לעשות המלאכה בבית הדפוס שהוא רשות הנותן דאיכא חשדא ע"כ כתב לאסור. וית' עוד מזה לקמן סי' תקמ"ב סעי' ב' וסי' תקמ"ה סעי' ד' קחנו משם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>3) There. A matter subject to loss is permitted, etc. </b>Printing books on Chol HaMoed is not considered a matter subject to loss, even if one employs workers whose wages were fixed monthly, whether or not work is available, and therefore loses the wages paid to them. The printing itself is not subject to loss. It is also forbidden even if he hires workers who lack food, because a person may not perform melachah on Chol HaMoed merely to provide wages to a worker who lacks food. Maharam of Provence, handwritten responsum, siman 58. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, cites this and comments: 'See below, siman 542, and what the later authorities write, and examine the matter carefully.' He means that siman 542, se'if 2, states that one may give work to a laborer who lacks food, so how can Maharam forbid it? Birkei Yosef there, subsection 2, likewise writes that Maharam's words require resolution. Shaarei Teshuvah in this siman cites this and resolves Maharam's ruling by saying that permission was granted only when the melachah provides some benefit for Yom Tov, unlike printing. See there. In my humble opinion, this distinction is strained. Permission was stated without qualification for a laborer who lacks food, implying any melachah, even one not needed for the festival. It appears to me that the resolution is as follows. Permission to give melachah to such a worker applies specifically when he takes the work into his own domain and performs it there. Anyone who sees him will know that he does so because he lacks food. He may not, however, perform the melachah in the employer's domain, because observers will suspect the employer of having his work performed during the festival. Printing must be performed in the printing house, which is the employer's domain, and therefore arouses suspicion. Maharam consequently forbids it. This will be explained further below, siman 542, se'if 2, and siman 545, se'if 4; take the ruling from there.

Although one loses the benefit of the saturation performed before the festival, since there are no seeds in the field, this is not considered a loss. After the festival he can saturate it again as much as he wishes. He merely delays his produce and forgoes the profit and improvement he could have obtained. Riaz. <b>15) [Se'if 3] Provided that they do not cease flowing. </b>Some explain this as referring to ponds that do not dry up because they have an abundant supply of water. Others explain it as referring to rivers, meaning that the river flowing from the pond does not cease. If it does cease, then although the river presently contains so much water that there will be no need to draw during the festival, it is nevertheless forbidden to water from it. We decree lest one think that the waters are so abundant when perhaps they are not, causing him to draw water and engage in excessive exertion, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 9, writes that it appears proper to be stringent according to all the explanations, since the phrase 'they do not cease' can bear both meanings. Therefore, whenever the pond water or the rivers flowing from it cease, watering from them is forbidden lest one come to finish watering with his pitcher. These are his words. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 9, writes likewise.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>16) There. When an irrigation channel, etc. </b>If a channel from a flowing spring constantly passes among them, one may water from it during the festival because its waters do not cease. Nimukei Yosef on Rif. There is no distinction between a channel that enters the trenches without flowing out beyond them and one that flows out the other side. In every case, if its waters ordinarily cease, watering is forbidden; if they do not cease, it is permitted. This is unlike Beis Yosef, who makes this distinction. Darkei Moshe, subsection 3; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5.
