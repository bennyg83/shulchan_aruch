And why did the Mechaber repeat and teach it? The later authorities answered: this seif is only a detail and explanation of the first law — that whenever a post of four remains at its head, it is considered breached not toward its head. And because of the latter part he cited it — and it is as if he said in seif 2: that which we say in seif 1 that if a post of four remains it is permitted up to ten amos — this applies even if nothing remains of the wall at its base. However, it is only permitted where many people do not pass through.

(7) Not toward its head — and the same applies when breached at its head itself — for example, when the alleyway is closed on three sides and partly on the fourth side, and the alleyway's rectification was by lechi or beam (that was placed from the place of closure to the opposite wall), and a breach was made at the place of closure — it is permitted up to ten amos, provided the standing portion exceeds the breach. And some say: here, when there is a breach on the fourth side, we require that the beam be placed specifically on the alleyway walls from end to end. But most later authorities agreed that what is placed on the standing portion is as if placed on the alleyway wall — this too applies to the alleyway. And what is breached in between is nullified relative to the standing portion. And the same applies if a lechi is made there — it suffices and a lechi adjacent to the house is not required.

**** ENGLISH ****
(12) Alleyway, etc. — because this seif has many details, I will first give a brief introduction. It is known from siman 364 that an alleyway open to a public domain is more stringent than one not open — it requires tzurat hapetach on one side. And it will be explained in this seif: if a courtyard separates between the end of the alleyway and the public domain, and the courtyard is breached on both sides about ten amos (and side-sections remain on this side and that side) — so that one standing in the alleyway sees the public domain opposite him, as the courtyard had no other wall except the alleyway wall entering into it — what is the law of the alleyway and courtyard regarding carrying? Perhaps the alleyway will be judged because of this as open to the public domain (and it will be explained whether there is a distinction if the courtyard's breach toward the public domain faces the courtyard's breach toward the alleyway — then it is judged as open; if not, not). And also the courtyard residents can restrict it if they did not make an eruv together. And how is the law regarding the courtyard — behold, it is breached to the public domain?
