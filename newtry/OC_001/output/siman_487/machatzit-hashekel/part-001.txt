<b>Specifically when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov etc.</b> For he already wrote in siman 268, se'if 4, even if he prayed a weekday Amidah etc., and all the more so here etc., for Yom Tov too is called Shabbos. Eliyah Rabbah and likewise Tosefes Shabbos understood that the Magen Avraham's view for the law is: if he mentioned [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, even if he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos, he has fulfilled. Therefore in Eliyah Rabbah in siman 268 he wrote that the Magen Avraham's words here contradict what he wrote in siman 268; and in Tosefes Shabbos, siman 268, on what the Magen Avraham wrote there s.k. 5, and this is his language on what the Shulchan Aruch wrote there, that if he prayed an Amidah of eighteen on Shabbos and mentioned [the formula] of Shabbos in the middle he has fulfilled, and this is his language: this law requires further study, for in siman 487, se'if 1, he wrote that if on Yom Tov he concluded "Who sanctifies the Shabbos," he has not fulfilled even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, since he concluded improperly etc.; and all the more so where he fixed no blessing at all for Shabbos. And one must say that it is worse when he concluded with [the formula] of another day; and this requires further study; end quote. And Tosefes Shabbos wrote, and this is his language: and his resolution is forced, for we hold in siman 108 that one who errs and mentions an occasion of other days in a prayer not in its time, it is not an interruption, and the same applies here too etc.; but in siman 487 the Magen Avraham resolved that there it deals with when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle; end quote of Tosefes Shabbos. What he asked from siman 108, in my humble opinion is not a difficulty, for what connection is there between mention in the middle and conclusion? Granted that interruption it is not, nevertheless in the conclusion it is worse, for he changed from the coinage of the Sages, for everything follows the conclusion, as is written here. But regarding the essence of the law, in my humble opinion the intentions of the Magen Avraham in siman 268 and here are twins together and have one intention. And here too he holds that even if he mentioned [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, since he concluded improperly [he has not fulfilled]. For granted that here he first wrote "specifically when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle," nevertheless in his conclusion here he concludes as he wrote in siman 268, for afterward he also asked on the law of 268 that everything follows the conclusion, and he resolved: since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos, only that they did not impose the burden on him. And if so, when he prays weekday [Amidah] on Shabbos, it is not a change in the conclusion from what the Sages coined, for on the contrary even the Sages' intent is to pray weekday [Amidah], except that they were lenient for him that a short prayer of seven blessings of Shabbos suffices. And if so, if he is stringent upon himself to pray eighteen blessings, there is no change from the coinage of the Sages; and if so this works well for that of siman 268. But here, where he prayed and concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, what of it? Behold everything follows the conclusion, and he changed in the coinage of the conclusion. And even if he prayed the entire Yom Tov Amidah, only that the conclusion was of Shabbos, and the conclusion was improper, he has not fulfilled, for he did not conclude as the Sages fixed.

<b>And even though he first wrote "and all the more so here that he concluded Who sanctifies the Shabbos, for Yom Tov too is called Shabbos."</b> It is implied that here is better than if he prayed an Amidah of eighteen; he wrote this before he concluded this reasoning, since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos, and if so certainly here that he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos is better; but after he concluded this, "since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos etc.," on the contrary that of siman 268 is better. And automatically we know that each must finish according to its law: on Shabbos he must add the word "the Shabbos" in the conclusion, and on Yom Tov he must add the word "and the seasons." And perhaps the Magen Avraham's intent is that it is impossible that Rava disagreed only that also on Shabbos one must mention Yisrael in the conclusion; if so, why lengthen "prayer, whether on Shabbos or on Yom Tov, Who sanctifies Yisrael," why mention Yom Tov, for on Yom Tov he agrees, and he should have said only that prayer even on Shabbos is "Who sanctifies Yisrael"; rather, necessarily he also disagrees on Yom Tov that one need not finish "and the seasons," and on Shabbos one need not mention Shabbos.
