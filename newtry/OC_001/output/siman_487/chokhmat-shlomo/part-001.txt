<b>Se'if 1.</b> And if he said Who sanctifies the Shabbos and retracted within toch kedei dibbur, he has fulfilled etc. Marginal note: see in Magen Avraham here who wrote that if he finished with [the formula] of Shabbos, only that he mentioned [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, he need not return and pray; and see in Eliyah Rabbah who disagrees on this and holds that he returns; and so ruled the Gaon, author of Chavos Daas, in his siddur. And even according to the Magen Avraham it appears that that is specifically if he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle within the middle blessing; then, since in this he at any rate did according to law and also prayed with [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, therefore he holds that he does not return. But if he prayed with [the formula] of Shabbos alone, only that in the Avodah he mentioned Ya'aleh Veyavo, since he did not mention Yom Tov in the middle [blessing] at all, he certainly is obligated to return and pray; even though if he prayed weekday [Amidah] and mentioned Yom Tov in the Avodah he does not return, that is because there by law even on Yom Tov one should pray eighteen; and for this reason in truth Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with the Magen Avraham and holds that if he did not pray eighteen, even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, he returns etc. And therefore even according to the Magen Avraham that is only [regarding] Yom Tov; but if he mentioned in the Avodah, if he prayed eighteen he did as Chazal fixed at any rate in this that he prayed eighteen, and by law it is so; but if he did not pray eighteen and did not mention in the middle, he did not at all as the Sages fixed, and therefore he certainly returns; and so is the implication of the Magen Avraham's language. And therefore in such a case he returns without doubt. And if he mentioned in the middle too, he should still return, only that in this he should condition: if he is obligated to pray it will be as his obligation, and if he is exempt it will be as a freewill offering; and even though we do not pray a freewill offering on Shabbos and Yom Tov, nevertheless since there are opinions that we do pray a freewill offering on Shabbos, see in Beis Yosef, siman 108, therefore there is a double doubt: perhaps like the view of Eliyah Rabbah, and even if you say like the Magen Avraham, perhaps we do pray a freewill offering on Shabbos; therefore he should pray with a condition as above; and examine carefully; so it appears to me according to my humble opinion, correct for the law.

(There) Se'if 4: On the first night of Pesach they finish the Hallel in public with pleasantness, with a blessing at the beginning and the end. Marginal note: indeed so it is implied in Masseches Sofrim. But in my opinion one can bring a clear proof to the contrary from our Talmud that they do not bless at all on the Hallel of Pesach nights, from what we read in Masseches Megillah, folio 21: anything whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night; to include what? To include eating of the Pesachim, and not like R' Eliezer ben Azariah. And behold in Tosafos there it is proven that even according to R' Eliezer ben Azariah, regarding Hallel after Afikoman it certainly helps all night; see there. And if so it is difficult: whence does the Talmud know that that latter clause, that something whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night, excludes eating of the Pesachim and not like R' Eliezer ben Azariah? Perhaps it accords even with R' Eliezer ben Azariah and includes reading of Hallel that is valid all night, for in this R' Eliezer ben Azariah admits; and behold in the first clause the Tanna stated Hallel among things whose mitzvah is by day and are valid all day, and he also took reading of Hallel; if so let us say that also at night he similarly includes reading of Hallel. And [as for the claim] that something D'Rabbanan is not taught, in the manner of what Turei Even wrote according to his approach, nevertheless this is not so, for behold Hallel is counted in the Mishnah necessarily, for he holds that since the prophets instituted it, it is not considered D'Rabbanan, as he wrote there; and if so, since he wrote there that from the Talmud saying that Hallel of Rosh Chodesh is only D'Rabbanan it is proven that the other days are midivrei kabbalah, if so also on Pesach night it is midivrei kabbalah, for the Talmud excluded only Rosh Chodesh alone; and if so also on Pesach night it is midivrei kabbalah. And so reason dictates: if there is an obligation to say Hallel on it, what difference between night and day? And if so it is also midivrei kabbalah at night; and if so why does it not answer that this is what it teaches us, and it accords even with R' Eliezer ben Azariah? And necessarily the reason is: behold, what it teaches us that it is valid all night is only after the fact, for ideally it is obvious that one should hurry as much as possible, for the zealous advance mitzvos; and necessarily it teaches that it is valid all night only after the fact if they did not read earlier. And if so, that works well for eating of the Pesachim, for then if its time has passed it is leftover and forbidden to eat; and likewise regarding those where a blessing applies and there is concern of a wasted blessing, therefore it needed to inform us that its time still stands and it is valid all day and all night. But regarding Hallel of Pesach night, since they do not bless upon it, it is obvious that if they did not read earlier it is valid all night, for even if you say he is like one reading in the Torah. But that works only if they do not bless on the Hallel at night; but if we say they do bless on Hallel at night, it is very difficult to resolve that at night it teaches us regarding Hallel of the night and it accords even with R' Eliezer ben Azariah; and necessarily at night there is no Hallel with a blessing. And this is a clear proof in my humble opinion for the custom of Rama and for our custom; and it will in truth be compelled like the view of Rama in the name of Tosafos and the Rashba that ideally one must also read Hallel before midnight, as Chok Yaakov wrote there, for if ideally it is permitted after midnight, let it say this practical difference; and examine very carefully.

And behold I was asked by a certain maskil on these words of the Shulchan Aruch from what is written in the Yerushalmi Shekalim at the beginning of chapter 3, and this is its language: May one drink them in one order? From what R' Mani said that R' Yochanan said: Hallel, if they hear it in the synagogue, they have fulfilled; this says that if he drank them in one order he has fulfilled. And I was asked: since according to Beis Yosef's view they say Hallel in the synagogue, necessarily it is from the law and they fulfill with this, from that they bless upon it, for upon a custom they do not bless; and if so, if they fulfill with this, why did the Shulchan Aruch rule that if he drank them not in order he has not fulfilled; end quote of the aforementioned maskil. And I answered that there is nothing here, for certainly the Shulchan Aruch's view is that even though he wrote that they say Hallel on Pesach with a blessing, nevertheless he does not fulfill with this, from that he did not write that he fulfills with this the obligation of Hallel; necessarily he holds that he has not fulfilled with this; and as for that they bless upon it even though upon a custom they do not bless: this is different, for upon a custom that has no connection at all to this day, and the essence of the matter is only a custom, for example Hoshanos, upon that they do not bless; but Hallel on this night, its essential obligation is from the law, and since an obligation of Hallel applies on this night, they properly bless upon it even at a time when it is only a custom, and also to exempt the Hallel that he will say afterward, which is from the law. And so I afterward found in the language of the Tur, siman 473, that this custom to say Hallel on Pesach night in the synagogue is a custom; see there; and even so he wrote that they bless upon it. And according to this the aforementioned maskil's difficulty is resolved: from the words of R' Yochanan who said if he said Hallel in the synagogue he has fulfilled, from which it is proven that he holds one need not say Hallel afterward at all, from this it is properly proven that he holds that if he drank them not in order he has fulfilled; but we do not hold so, rather even if he said [it] in the synagogue he is obligated to say also afterward in order, and therefore if he drank them not in order he has not fulfilled; and it works well, and this is clear in my humble opinion; and examine.

And behold Sha'agas Aryeh, laws of Yom Tov, siman 69, elaborated to investigate whether Hallel on Yom Tov is midivrei Torah or D'Rabbanan; see there. And I will show, with God's help, that the matter is compelled in the Talmud that Hallel is entirely D'Rabbanan and has no root from the Torah; and that is from the Talmud of Taanis, chapter On three festivals etc., folio 28b, where it asks there: what is different about Hallel that pushes aside its own etc.; I meant to say let it only push aside its own; he said to him: my son, Yose stands like you etc.; see there. And behold I was asked by a certain maskil why he mentioned R' Yose, for Ben Azzai holds so in the Mishnah, that Hallel pushes aside only the ma'amad of Minchah, and he should have said Ben Azzai stands like you. And behold at first glance I thought that from the Mishnah there is no proof, for one can say that Ben Azzai does not disagree for the law with the first Tanna regarding the Musaf korban, only regarding the wood-offering; and also the implication of the derashos will be between them: for according to the first Tanna it is more reasonable to push aside the ma'amad of Minchah than the ma'amad of Ne'ilah, for it is pressed from the time of Musaf, therefore he stated for a novelty that even the ma'amad of Ne'ilah it does not push aside; but Ben Azzai holds the opposite, that the ma'amad of Ne'ilah is more reasonable to push aside, since it is already near night and there is no leisure, but the ma'amad of Minchah has leisure; and therefore he stated the opposite, that a Musaf korban has no ma'amad of Minchah, and all the more so of Ne'ilah, but a wood-offering has no ma'amad of Ne'ilah, but it has a ma'amad of Minchah; and if so Ben Azzai is not like him. And thereby the Mishnah's language is more precise, that it says so Ben Azzai would teach; and if he disagreed for the law it should have said so Ben Azzai says; and necessarily Ben Azzai does not disagree, only teaches in another language the opposite. And even though the Rav [Bartenura] explained the opposite, that according to Ben Azzai only Musaf pushes aside [the ma'amad] of Minchah, and a wood-offering pushes aside both: one can say that that is only for us, since we already know from R' Yose that he holds Musaf pushes aside only [the ma'amad] of Minchah, therefore we do not multiply disputes to say that Ben Azzai is a third opinion, therefore we explain that Ben Azzai too holds so; but according to the initial assumption that we did not know from R' Yose, there is no proof from Ben Azzai that he holds so, for perhaps the opposite, and between them there is only that for this Minchah is more reasonable and for that Ne'ilah is more reasonable. And one should not ask: how could the initial assumption interpret Ben Azzai's words so, for if so why did the Mishnah afterward need to say that the first of Teves had no ma'amad because there was Hallel and a Musaf korban and a wood-offering; let it derive that because of Hallel and Musaf too there is no ma'amad, and you find this also on other days, for example on Chol HaMoed; and necessarily for Ben Azzai it was needed, for he holds Musaf pushes aside only [the ma'amad] of Minchah: this is not difficult, for one can say that the first Tanna only holds that Musaf pushes aside both where there is no Hallel at Shacharis, for example on Rosh Chodesh, for then since the institution of the ma'amad is not uprooted entirely, for it exists at Shacharis, therefore they instituted that a Musaf korban pushes aside both; but where there is Hallel, then since there is none at Shacharis because of Hallel, then because of Musaf only [the ma'amad] of Minchah is uprooted and not of Ne'ilah, in order to uphold at least one ma'amad in the day; and therefore the Tanna stated the first of Teves, for then since there are three, each pushes aside only its own, and we do not attend to uprooting the ma'amad. So one could say according to the initial assumption that he did not know from R' Yose, therefore he did not say Ben Azzai [stands like him], as above. But in truth this is forced. And the essence appears that it works well simply according to what the Rav [Bartenura] wrote: that therefore Ben Azzai holds that a wood-offering pushes aside more because it is D'Rabbanan and needs strengthening, which is not so of Musaf; see there. And according to this, how can one say that Ben Azzai holds like him? If so his own difficulty will apply against the wood-offering: what is different about a wood-offering that pushes aside even [the ma'amad] of Ne'ilah, and what is different about Hallel that pushes aside only its own? And one cannot say that a wood-offering is only D'Rabbanan, for behold Hallel too is D'Rabbanan; and if so his difficulty that he asked against the Rabbis, what is different about Hallel and what is different about Musaf, let it only push aside its own, itself is also difficult for Ben Azzai against the wood-offering, what is different about Hallel and what is different about a wood-offering; therefore he is compelled to resolve that it is R' Yose, and R' Yose, since he disagrees with the first Tanna only regarding Musaf and said a day that has Musaf has a ma'amad, and he does not disagree regarding a wood-offering and does not mention a wood-offering in his language, necessarily regarding a wood-offering he admits to the first Tanna that it pushes aside only [the ma'amad] of Minchah, and he disagrees only regarding Musaf; and if so according to R' Yose all are equal, that each pushes aside only its own, whether Hallel or Musaf or a wood-offering; and therefore he said R' Yose stands like you. And this is clear and true in the intent of the sugya. And according to this it is explicitly compelled that Hallel is D'Rabbanan, for if it were from the Torah it is difficult: let him say Ben Azzai stands like you. And one cannot say that it is still difficult against a wood-offering, for one can say a wood-offering is different because it is D'Rabbanan and Hallel is midivrei Torah, and he only asks against Musaf which is also midivrei Torah; and why did he not say Ben Azzai stands like you? And one cannot say that Hallel is forever midivrei Torah, only that it is still difficult from Hallel of Chanukah which is D'Rabbanan, and what is different about a wood-offering: for one can say that there Hallel is midivrei Torah that has a root in the Torah, therefore it needs no strengthening, which is not so of a wood-offering that has no root from the Torah, therefore it needs strengthening, as this distinction is proven in several places in the Talmud; and from that he did not answer so it is proven that Hallel has no root in the Torah and all Hallel is D'Rabbanan and similar to a wood-offering. And this is a clear proof in my humble opinion; and see in Rashi's commentary in Masseches Taanis, folio 28b, there it is explicit that Rashi holds that Hallel is D'Rabbanan, only that it resembles D'Oraisa; see there; and examine carefully on this.
