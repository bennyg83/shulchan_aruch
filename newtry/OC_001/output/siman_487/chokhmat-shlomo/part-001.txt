<b>Seif 1.</b> And if he said "Who sanctifies the Shabbos" and corrected himself within the time of an utterance, he fulfilled, etc. Note: See the Magen Avraham here, who wrote that if he concluded with that of Shabbos, only that he mentioned that of Yom Tov in the middle, he need not return and pray again; and see the Eliyah Rabbah, who disagrees in this and holds that he repeats; and so ruled the Gaon, author of the Chavos Daas, in his siddur. And even according to the Magen Avraham it appears that this is specifically if he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, within the middle berachah; then, since in this he at any rate acted according to the law and prayed also that of Yom Tov in the middle, he therefore holds that he does not repeat. But if he prayed that of Shabbos alone, and only in the berachah of Avodah mentioned Yaaleh VeYavo, then since he did not mention Yom Tov in the middle berachah at all, he is certainly obligated to return and pray again. And although one who prayed a weekday prayer and mentioned Yom Tov in Avodah does not repeat, that is because there it is by law that even on Yom Tov one should by right pray eighteen berachos; and for this reason the Eliyah Rabbah indeed disagrees with the Magen Avraham and holds that if he did not pray eighteen, even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, he repeats, etc. Therefore, even according to the Magen Avraham, this is only regarding Yom Tov; but if he mentioned it in Avodah: if he prayed eighteen, he acted according to the enactment of the Sages at least in that he prayed eighteen, and by law it is so; but if he did not pray eighteen and did not mention it in the middle, he did not act according to the enactment of the Sages at all, and therefore he certainly repeats; and so is the implication of the language of the Magen Avraham; and therefore in such a case he repeats without any doubt. And if he mentioned it in the middle, he should likewise repeat, only that in this case he should stipulate: if he is obligated to pray, let it count toward his obligation, and if he is exempt, let it be a voluntary prayer. And although one does not pray a voluntary prayer on Shabbos and Yom Tov, nevertheless, since there are opinions that one may pray a voluntary prayer on Shabbos (see the Beis Yosef, siman 108), it is therefore a double doubt: perhaps the law is like the view of the Eliyah Rabbah; and if you will say it is like the Magen Avraham, perhaps one may pray a voluntary prayer on Shabbos; therefore he should pray with a stipulation, as above. Examine this well; so it appears in my humble opinion, correct for the halachah. (There,) seif 4: On the first night of Pesach they complete the Hallel with the congregation, pleasantly, with a berachah before and after. Note: It is indeed so implied in Maseches Sofrim. But in my opinion a clear proof can be brought to the contrary from our Gemara, that one does not bless at all upon the Hallel of the nights of Pesach, from that which we learn in Maseches Megillah, folio 21: "Anything whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night" comes to include what? To include the eating of the Pesach offerings, and not like Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah. Now, in Tosafos there it is proven that even according to Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah, for the Hallel that is after the afikoman, all night certainly avails; see there. If so, it is difficult: from where does the Gemara know that this latter clause, "a matter whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night," comes to include the eating of the Pesach offerings and is not like Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah? Perhaps it accords even with Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah, and comes to include the reading of Hallel, that it is valid all night, in which Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah concedes. For in the first clause, regarding "a matter whose mitzvah is by day is valid all day," the Tanna included the reading of Hallel as well; if so, let us say that at night too it likewise includes the reading of Hallel. And to say that it does not teach rabbinic matters, along the lines of what the Turei Even wrote according to his approach, nevertheless this is not so, for Hallel is reckoned in the Mishnah; perforce it holds that Hallel, since the Prophets instituted it, is not considered rabbinic, as he himself wrote there. And so, since he wrote there that from the Gemara's statement that the Hallel of Rosh Chodesh is only rabbinic it is proven that that of the other days is divrei kabbalah, if so, on the night of Pesach too it is divrei kabbalah, for the Gemara excluded only Rosh Chodesh alone; and if so, on the night of Pesach as well it is divrei kabbalah. And logic dictates so: if there is an obligation to say Hallel on it, what difference is there between night and day? If so, at night too it is divrei kabbalah; and if so, why does the Gemara not answer that this is what it teaches us, and it would accord even with Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah? Perforce the reason is as follows: perforce, that which it teaches us, that it is valid all night, is only after the fact, for at the outset it is obvious that one should hasten as much as possible, for the zealous perform mitzvos early; perforce it teaches us that it is valid all night only after the fact, if they did not read it earlier. If so, this is well regarding the eating of the Pesach offerings, for once its time passes it becomes nosar and is forbidden to eat, and likewise regarding those things to which a berachah applies and there is a concern of a berachah in vain; therefore it needed to inform us that it is still its time, and it is valid all day or all night. But regarding the Hallel of Pesach night, since one does not bless upon it, it is obvious that if they did not read it earlier it is valid all night, for let it be no worse than one reading Scripture. But this is well only if one does not bless upon the Hallel at night; if we say that one does bless upon the Hallel at night, it is very difficult to answer why it did not teach us this regarding the night, concerning the Hallel of the night, and it would accord even with Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah. Perforce, at night there is no Hallel with a berachah; and this is a clear proof, in my humble opinion, for the custom of the Rama and for our custom. And it will indeed be compelled, like the view of the Rama in the name of Tosafos and the Rashba, that at the outset one must read the Hallel too before midnight, as the Chok Yaakov wrote there; for if at the outset it were permitted after midnight, let it state this practical difference; and examine this very well. Now, I was asked by a certain scholar concerning these words of the Shulchan Aruch, from what is stated in the Yerushalmi, Shekalim, end of chapter 3, and this is its language: "What is the law as to drinking them in one draught? From that which Rabbi Mani said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, that regarding Hallel, if they hear it in the synagogue they have fulfilled, this tells us that if he drank them in one draught he has fulfilled." And I was asked: since according to the view of the Beis Yosef they say Hallel in the synagogue, perforce it is by law, and one fulfills with it, from the fact that they bless upon it, for upon a custom one does not bless; and if so, if one fulfills with it, why did the Shulchan Aruch rule that if he drank them not in their order he did not fulfill? Thus far the words of the aforementioned scholar. And I answered that this is no difficulty at all: certainly the view of the Shulchan Aruch is that although he wrote that they say Hallel on Pesach with a berachah, nevertheless one does not fulfill with it; from the fact that he did not write that one fulfills the obligation of Hallel with it, perforce he holds that one does not fulfill with it. And as for their blessing upon it even though one does not bless upon a custom, this is different: upon a custom that has no connection at all to that day, where the entire matter is only a custom, such as the hoshana, upon this one does not bless; but Hallel on this night, its essential obligation is by law, and since an obligation of Hallel applies on this night, one may properly bless upon it even at a time when it is only a custom, and also so as to exempt the Hallel that he will say afterward, which is by law. And so I found afterward in the language of the Tur, siman 473, that this practice of saying Hallel on the night of Pesach in the synagogue is a custom, see there, and even so he wrote that they bless upon it. And according to this the aforementioned scholar's difficulty is settled: from the words of Rabbi Yochanan, who said that if he said Hallel in the synagogue he fulfilled, whence it is proven that he holds one need not say Hallel afterward at all, it is properly proven that he holds that if he drank them not in their order he fulfilled; but we do not rule so; rather, even though he said it in the synagogue, he is obligated to say it also afterward in its order; and therefore, if he drank them not in their order, he did not fulfill. And it comes out well, and this is clear in my humble opinion; examine it. Now, the Shaagas Aryeh, Hilchos Yom Tov, siman 69, discussed at length whether the Hallel of Yom Tov is from the Torah or rabbinic; see there. And I shall show, with the help of Heaven, that the matter is compelled in the Gemara that Hallel is entirely rabbinic and has no root in the Torah; and it is from the Gemara of Taanis, the chapter "BiShloshah Perakim," folio 28b, where it asks there: what is different about Hallel, that it overrides its own, etc.; "I say thus: let it override only its own"; he said to him, Rabbi Yosi holds like you, etc.; see there. Now, I was asked by a certain scholar: why did it mention Rabbi Yosi? Behold, Ben Azzai holds so in the Mishnah, that Hallel overrides only the ma'amad of Minchah, and it should have said "Ben Azzai holds like you." Now, at first view I thought that from the Mishnah there is no proof, for one can say that Ben Azzai does not disagree with the first Tanna regarding the law in the case of the korban musaf, only in the case of the wood offering, and there would be between them only the implication of their expounding: for the first Tanna holds that it is more reasonable to override the ma'amad of Minchah than the ma'amad of Neilah, which is already pressed for time from the time of the musaf, and he therefore stated the greater novelty, that it does not override even the ma'amad of Neilah; while Ben Azzai holds the reverse, that the ma'amad of Neilah is more reasonable to override, since it is already close to night and there is no leisure, while for the ma'amad of Minchah there is leisure; and he therefore stated the reverse, that on a day of korban musaf there is no ma'amad of Minchah, and all the more so of Neilah, while on a day of the wood offering there is no ma'amad of Neilah, but there is a ma'amad of Minchah. And if so, Ben Azzai would not be in accordance with him. And with this the language of the Mishnah is the more precise, for it says "thus would Ben Azzai teach"; and if he disagreed regarding the law, it should have said "thus would Ben Azzai say"; perforce Ben Azzai does not disagree, but only teaches in a different, reversed formulation. And although the Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura explained the reverse, that according to Ben Azzai only musaf overrides that of Minchah, while the wood offering overrides both, one can say that this is only for us, since we already know from Rabbi Yosi that he holds so, that musaf overrides only that of Minchah; therefore we do not multiply dispute so as to say that Ben Azzai is a third opinion, and we therefore explain that Ben Azzai too holds so; but at the initial assumption, when we did not yet know of Rabbi Yosi, there would be no proof from Ben Azzai that he holds so, for perhaps it is the reverse, and there is between them only that to this one, that of Minchah is the more reasonable, and to that one, that of Neilah is the more. And one should not object: how could one entertain explaining Ben Azzai's words so? For if so, why did the Mishnah afterward need to say that the first of Teves had no ma'amad, since it had Hallel and korban musaf and the wood offering; derive it from the fact that on account of Hallel and musaf as well there is no ma'amad, and you find it so on the other days as well, such as on Chol HaMoed; perforce it is needed for Ben Azzai, who holds that musaf overrides only that of Minchah. This is not difficult, for one can say that the first Tanna holds that musaf overrides both only where there is no Hallel at Shacharis, such as on Rosh Chodesh, for then, since the enactment of the ma'amad is not entirely uprooted, for it exists at Shacharis, they therefore enacted that the korban musaf overrides both; but where there is Hallel, then, since there is none at Shacharis on account of Hallel, it is not uprooted on account of musaf except that of Minchah and not that of Neilah, in order to preserve at least one ma'amad in the day; and the Tanna therefore stated the first of Teves, for then, since it has all three, each one overrides only its own, and we pay no regard to the uprooting of the ma'amad. So it would have been possible to say at the initial assumption, which did not know of Rabbi Yosi; therefore it did not say "it is Ben Azzai," as explained above. But in truth this is forced. And the essential answer appears to come out well simply, according to what the Bartenura wrote, that Ben Azzai holds that the wood offering overrides more by force of its being rabbinic and requiring reinforcement, which is not the case with musaf; see there. And according to this, how is it possible to say that Ben Azzai holds in accordance with him? If so, his own difficulty would apply to the wood offering: what is different about the wood offering, that it overrides even that of Neilah, and what is different about Hallel, that it overrides only its own? And one cannot say that the wood offering is only rabbinic, for Hallel too is rabbinic; and if so, the difficulty that he raised against the Rabbis, what is different about Hallel and what about musaf, that it too should override only its own, is itself equally difficult for Ben Azzai regarding the wood offering: what is different about Hallel and what about the wood offering? It is therefore compelled to answer that it is Rabbi Yosi; and Rabbi Yosi, since he disagrees with the first Tanna only about musaf, saying "a day that has a musaf has a ma'amad," and does not disagree about the wood offering and does not mention the wood offering in his statement, perforce concedes to the first Tanna regarding the wood offering that it overrides only that of Minchah, and disagrees only about musaf; and if so, for Rabbi Yosi all are equal, each one overriding only its own, whether Hallel, whether musaf, whether the wood offering; and it therefore said that Rabbi Yosi holds like you. And this is clear and true in the intent of the passage. And according to this it is explicitly compelled that Hallel is rabbinic, for if it were from the Torah it would be difficult: let it say "Ben Azzai holds like you." And one cannot say that it would still be difficult regarding the wood offering, for one can say the wood offering is different, being rabbinic, while Hallel is from the Torah, and it asks only about musaf, which is likewise from the Torah; and why then did it not say "Ben Azzai holds like you"? And one cannot say that in fact Hallel is from the Torah, only that it would still be difficult from the Hallel of Chanukah, which is rabbinic, and what then is different about the wood offering; for one can say that there Hallel is still of the Torah in that Hallel has a root in the Torah, and it therefore needs no reinforcement, unlike the wood offering, which has no root from the Torah and therefore requires reinforcement, as this distinction is evident in several places in the Gemara; and since it does not answer so, it is proven that Hallel has no root in the Torah, and all of Hallel is rabbinic and comparable to the wood offering. And this is a clear proof in my humble opinion. And see Rashi's commentary in Maseches Taanis, folio 28b, where it is explicit that Rashi holds that Hallel is rabbinic, only that it resembles that which is from the Torah; see there, and examine this very well.
