<br>We shall copy here the introduction of the Peri Megadim to simanim 74 and 75, because it is greatly needed.<br>1. Know that the laws concerning the prohibition of ervah explained in this siman and in siman 75 divide into five categories, as will be explained with Hashem's help.<br>[1] His eyes see his own ervah.
[2] His heart sees his own ervah, such as when he is walking in a shirt without trousers and without a belt, or when he is lying naked beneath a covering, has put his head outside it, while his heart and ervah remain beneath the covering.
[3] Exposure of ervah, such as when he walks naked in his house, even though he has put his head outside the house and his heart is also covered by a garment. Although neither his eyes nor his heart sees the ervah, there is nevertheless a prohibition because the ervah is exposed.
[4] His eyes see another person's ervah. There is no distinction between the ervah of a Jew and that of a non-Jew, even a boy of nine or a girl of three, and certainly a woman, whether married or unmarried. See Chayei Adam.
[5] His heart sees another person's ervah.<br>When his eyes see his own ervah, category 1, or another person's ervah, category 4, the prohibition is D'Oraisa, as it is written, “No nakedness shall be seen among you,” with regard to reciting Krias Shema, engaging in Torah study, or any sacred matter. Thus, one who recites Krias Shema while seeing ervah violates a Torah prohibition, and the same applies to every sacred matter. Thinking Torah thoughts is permitted in the presence of ervah, but listening under the principle that one who listens is as one who responds is prohibited, since it is considered speech. See the Peri Megadim there, who further inclines to say that although one does not receive lashes for this prohibition, because it involves no action, he is nevertheless liable to Rabbinic lashes and is Rabbinically disqualified from testimony on its account. If there is doubt whether he saw ervah, he must repeat Krias Shema, because a doubt concerning a Torah law is treated stringently. He must therefore repeat it out of doubt. Whether he must repeat it together with its blessings may depend on whether the halachah follows Rava in Temurah, that whenever the Torah says not to do something, if one did it the act is ineffective. On that view, the doubt concerning Krias Shema is a Torah-level doubt and he should recite it with its blessings. If, however, his obligation to repeat it is Rabbinic, one might say that a doubt concerning a Rabbinic law is treated leniently. There is a distinction between seeing one's own ervah and seeing another person's ervah. Regarding one's own ervah, there are opinions in siman 75 that closing one's eyes does not help, and turning away cannot readily apply, because he must turn his body as well, as the Taz writes there. Regarding another person's ervah, however, all agree that turning one's face and body away is effective. There is also a distinction between one's eyes seeing ervah and the ervah merely being exposed. Although both are Torah prohibitions, covering by water does not help when one's eyes see it, just as ervah behind glass remains prohibited, as explained in the Shulchan Aruch. For the prohibition of exposed ervah, however, covering by water is effective, as explained there.<br>2. The prohibition when his heart sees his own ervah or another person's ervah is only Rabbinic.<br>3. All this applies when he is wearing a shirt and the ervah is covered. If the ervah itself is exposed, then even though his heart is covered and his eyes do not see it, because he closes them or puts his head outside a window, it is nevertheless forbidden to recite Krias Shema or engage in Torah study. This is contrary to the Bach in Yoreh Deah siman 200, who maintains that exposed ervah entails no independent prohibition and is prohibited only because one's heart sees the ervah. That is incorrect. Exposure of ervah is a Torah prohibition, as the Rashba writes in Berachos 24, deriving it from the verse, “No nakedness shall be seen among you.” The verse does not say, “You shall not see,” but rather, “It shall not be seen,” meaning by others. It is certainly prohibited by Torah law when another person stands opposite him, but even if no one stands opposite him it is prohibited, as Rashi explains in his commentary on the Torah that “shall not be seen among you” refers to Hashem. If he recited Krias Shema while his ervah was exposed, he must repeat it. The prohibition of one's heart seeing ervah is only Rabbinic. Therefore, if his heart is outside the water, or the ervah is behind glass and his heart sees it, he may recite Krias Shema and engage in Torah study. This differs from his eyes seeing it, which is a Torah prohibition and remains forbidden in such circumstances.<br>There is also a distinction between his heart seeing his own ervah and seeing another person's ervah. For his own ervah, an interruption formed by his arms is effective, and certainly a belt is effective. For another person's ervah, an interruption does not help, because his heart still sees that person's ervah. Covering his heart with his hand is ineffective even regarding another person's ervah, because one part of the body does not cover another part. If he is wearing only a shirt, so that the prohibition is that his heart sees the ervah, as where he wears no belt, he may correct this by embracing himself with his arms. Even his hand can help if he uses it to draw the shirt over his heart. If the shirt is so thin that the ervah can be seen through it, its law is like the water discussed in this siman, and it does not help for his eyes, just as ervah behind glass does not. This is the substance of the Peri Megadim's words, with some explanation.

<b> היה ישן ערום וכו'</b> -  ר"ל אפילו אם הוציא ראשו חוץ לטליתו שלא יהיה עם ערותו תחת מכסה אחד מ"מ אסור דלבו רואה ערוה עד שיחוץ על לבו להפסיק ואז אפילו אינו מוציא ראשו חוץ להטלית שרי ע"י זה כן מוכח בברייתא ברכות כ"ג ע"ב עי"ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If he was sleeping naked, etc.</b> Meaning, even if he put his head outside his tallis so that his head would not be beneath the same covering as his ervah, it is nevertheless forbidden because his heart sees the ervah. He must press the covering against his heart to form an interruption. Once he does so, it is permitted even if he does not put his head outside the tallis. This is evident from the baraisa in Berachos 23b. See there.

<b> משום דלבו</b> -  עיין במ"ב בדין כילה ואם הוציא ראשו ולבו חוץ לכילה אסור דהוי כאלו הוא כולו לחוץ. ואם הוא עומד ערום במגדל של עצים ומוציא ראשו לחוץ מסתפק הירושלמי אם דינו כבית ואוהל ואסור או דינו ככלי והוי כמו מלבוש והרבינו ירוחם פוסק בזה לקולא אך שלא יהא לבו רואה את הערוה וכנ"ל גבי כילה כ"ז מתבאר מדברי המגן אברהם ולפ"ז משמע בפשיטות דאם אחד עומד ערום בתוך כלי נחשב הכלי כמו מלבוש ומהני כשמסתכל בעיניו כלפי חוץ וכ"כ הדרישה ביו"ד סימן ר' והט"ז מחמיר בזה שם ותמה הפמ"ג עליו ומ"מ צריך להפסיק בין לבו לערוה דלא עדיף מכתונת:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Because his heart.</b> See Mishnah Berurah concerning a canopy. If he put his head and heart outside the canopy, it is forbidden, because it is considered as though his entire body were outside. If he is standing naked inside a wooden cabinet and puts his head outside it, the Yerushalmi is uncertain whether it is treated like a house or tent, in which case it is forbidden, or like a utensil, in which case it is comparable to clothing. Rabbeinu Yerucham rules leniently, provided that his heart does not see the ervah, as explained above regarding a canopy. All this is derived from the Magen Avraham. It therefore appears straightforward that if one stands naked inside a utensil, the utensil is considered like clothing, and it is effective when he directs his eyes outward. The Derishah in Yoreh Deah siman 200 writes likewise. The Taz there is stringent, and the Peri Megadim questions his ruling. In any event, he must form an interruption between his heart and the ervah, because it is no better than a shirt.

<b> ואם היו המים עכורים שאין אבריו וכו'</b> -  ועדיף זה יותר משאם היה לבוש חלוק בלי מכנסים אף דשם ג"כ אין אבריו נראין בהם מלמעלה משום דהם דבוקים לגופו ודמו כארעא סמיכתא כדאיתא בגמ' ומקרי עי"ז שאין לבו רואה לערוה ועיין בסימן צ"א מ"א סק"ב:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If the water was murky, so that his limbs, etc.</b> This is more effective than wearing a shirt without trousers, although in that case as well his limbs cannot be seen from above. The reason is that the water adheres to his body and is comparable to solid ground, as stated in the Gemara. It is therefore considered that his heart does not see the ervah. See siman 91, Magen Avraham seif katan 2.
