(א) היה ישן ערום - המחבר תפס לשון הגמרא [ברכות כ"ג ע"ב] דשם שייך האי לישנא אבל בענינינו ה"ה כשהיה ישן בחלוקו בלא מכנסים ג"כ דינא הכי דאסור בד"ת או לברך שום ברכה עד שידביק בידיו הכתונת במקום הלב או למטה מלבו כדי שיהא הפסק בין לבו לערוה:

For our law, the same applies if he slept in a shirt without trousers. He may not speak words of Torah or recite any blessing until he presses the shirt with his hands at the heart, or below the heart, so that there is an interruption between his heart and the ervah.

(ד) משום דלבו וכו' - דערוה בראיה תלי רחמנא כדכתיב לא יראה בך ערות דבר וגו' ומצינו לשון ראיה בלב כדכתיב ולבי ראה הרבה חכמה לפיכך החמירו חכמים בזה. ודע דאפילו אם לבו מכוסה בבגד להפסיק בינו לערוה צריך שתהא ערותו ג"כ מכוסה ואם הוא מגולה הסכימו הרבה אחרונים דאסור לקרות מן התורה אפילו אם מכוסה בהטלית גם על ראשו ועיניו שלא יוכל לראות את הערוה שהרי לא נאמר לא תראה ערות דבר אלא לא יראה בך כלומר שתהא מכוסה בענין שלא יוכל להראות אפילו לאחר. לפיכך העומד בבית ערום והוציא ראשו חוץ לחלון שלא יראה ערותו אע"פ שחצץ בבגד על לבו ה"ז לא יקרא כיון שערותו מגולה ונראית בבית אבל הישן ערום בכילה שאינה גבוה י' טפחים והוציא ראשו חוצה לה וגם חצץ באיזה דבר להפסיק בין לבו לערוה אע"פ שערותו מגולה בכילה ה"ז כמכוסה בטלית שכיון שאין הכילה גבוה י"ט שם כיסוי עליה כמו מלבוש ואינו דומה לבית שהוא חלוק רשות לעצמו ולא נוכל לכנות עליה שם כיסוי ע"כ אסור. וכן אם צואה בבית בתוך ד"א והוציא ראשו חוץ לחלון אסור לקרות ק"ש דשדינן ראשו בתר רובו [מ"א ור"ל דאסור אפילו לדעת המתירין לקמן בסימן ע"ו ס"ד עי"ש ועיין בפמ"ג] ואם הכניס ראשו לחדר שיש שם צואה אסור ג"כ לכו"ע לקרות דעכ"פ הצואה נגד עיניו ולדעת הרשב"א המובא בסימן ע"ט ס"ב אסור אפילו הוא עומד מבחוץ נגד הפתח כיון שהצואה כנגדו עי"ש [פמ"ג]:
**** ENGLISH ****
(4) Because his heart, etc. The Torah makes the prohibition of ervah dependent upon sight, as it is written, “No nakedness shall be seen among you,” and we find the language of sight applied to the heart, as it is written, “My heart saw much wisdom.” The Sages therefore ruled stringently in this matter. Know that even if his heart is covered with a garment separating it from the ervah, the ervah itself must also be covered. If it is exposed, many Acharonim agree that it is forbidden by Torah law to recite, even if the tallis also covers his head and eyes so that he cannot see the ervah. The verse does not say, “You shall not see nakedness,” but, “No nakedness shall be seen among you,” meaning that it must be covered so that it cannot be seen even by another person. Therefore, one who stands naked inside a house and puts his head outside the window so that he cannot see his ervah may not recite, even if a garment interrupts at his heart, because his ervah is exposed and visible inside the house. If, however, one sleeps naked beneath a canopy lower than ten handbreadths, puts his head outside it, and also places an interruption between his heart and the ervah, he is considered covered by a tallis even though his ervah is exposed within the canopy. Since the canopy is not ten handbreadths high, it has the status of a covering like clothing. It differs from a house, which is a separate domain and cannot be called a covering. Similarly, if excrement is in a house within four cubits and he puts his head outside the window, he may not recite Krias Shema, because we associate his head with the greater part of his body. The Magen Avraham means that it is forbidden even according to the authorities who permit the case discussed below in siman 76 seif 4. See there and Peri Megadim. If he puts his head into a room containing excrement, all agree that he may not recite, because the excrement is at least opposite his eyes. According to the Rashba cited in siman 79 seif 2, it is forbidden even if he stands outside opposite the doorway, since the excrement is before him. See there. This is from Peri Megadim.

**** ENGLISH ****
(5) Sees the ervah, etc. See Derech HaChaim, who rules that even after the fact, if one recited Krias Shema or prayed in this condition, he must repeat it. This is also evident from Peri Megadim in siman 71, Eshel Avraham, letter 5. It is further evident there that if he recited Krias Shema and its blessings while his heart saw the ervah, he repeats Krias Shema together with its blessings, because the Sages instituted them in that manner. Regarding other blessings, however, it is uncertain whether he should repeat them after the fact, and a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. Chayei Adam, klal 3, law 33, and Nishmas Adam, klal 4, imply that after the fact one may be lenient even regarding prayer when the prohibition is only Rabbinic. See Biur Halachah to siman 185. It may be appropriate to follow his view here and combine it with the opinion of Rashi and his disciple Rabbi Shimon cited by Tosafos in Berachos 25b.

(6) Sees another person's ervah. For example, both are lying naked under one tallis and are far enough apart that their bodies do not touch, but they face one another. Although he has an interruption between his own heart and ervah, it is nevertheless forbidden because his heart sees the other person's ervah. Another example is where both stand in water. His heart does not see his own ervah because he is covered by a garment from below his heart, but he sees the other person's uncovered ervah. This is forbidden even though his eyes are above the water, because his heart nevertheless sees it.

(14) As an interruption. Meaning, if he stands in clear water and his heart is also within the water, so that he must interrupt between his heart and ervah, embracing himself is sufficient. (15) They may recite blessings, etc. The reason is that their ervah is positioned very low and their heart cannot see it. Nevertheless, if they stand naked, another prohibition applies, namely exposed ervah. This is not because of the heart, but because it is written, “No nakedness shall be seen among you.” A woman may therefore not recite a blessing unless she sits, as the Rama writes, because by sitting her lower front is covered and her ervah is not visible. The Rama's words, “then their hearts do not see their ervah,” are not precise, because their hearts do not see it in any case. He means that their ervah is then not visible. The text should read, “then their ervah is not visible.” This is the conclusion of the Acharonim.

(16) From the heart. The Bach disagrees and maintains that a woman also requires an interruption between her heart and ervah even when she is clothed. The other Acharonim, however, agree with the Shulchan Aruch. See Yoreh Deah siman 200, where Taz and Shach conclude that according to the Shulchan Aruch here, which holds that the law of one's heart seeing ervah does not apply to a woman, she may recite the blessing over immersion while standing in the water even though she is still naked. There is no prohibition of exposed ervah, because the water is considered a covering. It is better for her heart to be above the water, because this is permitted even for a man, as the Rama writes in the gloss to seif 2, or for her to embrace herself with her arms to interrupt between her heart and ervah. Chochmas Adam rules likewise. The same applies if she makes the water murky with her feet so that her limbs cannot be seen through it, which is effective according to all opinions. If other naked women are standing in the immersion room, she plainly must turn her face and body away from them while reciting the blessing. Even if we say that the mikveh area is a separate domain, this does not help, as is evident below in siman 79 seif 2. See there.
