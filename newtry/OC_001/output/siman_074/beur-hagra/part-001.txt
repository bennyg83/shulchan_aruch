<b>ס"א היה כו'. </b>כי"א דסתמא דגמ' פריך שם כ"ה ב' והרי לבו כו' ועוד מדפליגי בעקבו רואה כו' ש"מ דבלבו ס"ל לאביי ורבא דאסור ואף דשמואל בסוכה י' ב' ס"ל דמותר הלכה כאביי ורבא. רא"ש ונ"ל שרש"י שפ' כמ"ד מותר אזיל לשיטתו שפי' שם ד"ה והרי עקבו כו' קס"ד כו' ר"ל דק"ל דכיון דמשני קסבר לבו כו' מאי פריך מעקבו לכן פי' דעקבו חמי' מלבו כיון שאין דרכו לראות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Seif 1: If one was, etc.</b> This follows the first opinion, for the anonymous Gemara there, 25b, challenges: “But his heart sees, etc.” Furthermore, since Abaye and Rava disagree concerning whether his heel sees, etc., it follows that regarding his heart they both hold that it is prohibited. Although Shmuel in Sukkah 10b holds that it is permitted, the halachah follows Abaye and Rava. This is the Rosh's view. It appears to me that Rashi, who explains the passage according to the opinion that permits it, follows his own approach. There, s.v. “But his heel, etc.,” he explains: “The initial assumption was, etc.” Meaning, he was troubled that once the Gemara answers that he holds his heart, etc., why does it then challenge from his heel? Rashi therefore explains that one's heel is more severe than one's heart, since it is not its normal manner to see.

<b>ואם אין כו'. </b>דמ"ש שם ישב עד צוארו דל"ל עד צוארו אלא משום וי"א שבכה"ג דוקא עוכרן ברגלו וזהו דפריך שם והרי עקבו כו' ור"ל הא בלא"ה אסור לי"א משום עקבו והא דפריך שם על מתני' וא"ל תכסה כו' והרי כו' אחר דמשני כותיקין פריך דע"כ גם מתפלל שם דטעמא דותיקין הוא כדי שיסמוך ובתפלה קאמר שם א' עד שיכסה את לבו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if it is not, etc.</b> The statement there that he should sit in the water up to his neck would not need to specify “up to his neck” except because of the first opinion. According to that opinion, specifically in such a case he muddies the water with his feet. This is the Gemara's challenge there: “But his heel, etc.” Meaning, even without that concern it would be prohibited according to the first opinion because of his heel. As for the Gemara's challenge to the Mishnah, “Let him cover, etc., but surely, etc.,” after answering that it follows the practice of the early pious men, it challenges because he must necessarily also pray there. The reason for their practice was to juxtapose redemption to prayer, and regarding prayer the Gemara states on the first side of the page that one must cover his heart.
