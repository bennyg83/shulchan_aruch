**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Magen Avraham, seif katan 2</small> It appears to me that in any event. Meaning, even Maharshal, who devised this solution, referred only to two householders or two guests. But in the case of a householder and a guest, since the mitzvah rests upon the householder, Maharshal agrees that he should recite the blessing over both. The proof is from seif 5, where we do not say that the guest should recite the blessing over the non-Jew’s bread. This is because the mitzvah rests upon the householder.

<small>סק"ה</small> כמ"ש ר"ס קס"ז מלמעלה ומלמטה שהיא ג"כ קרים ואין חיוב לבצוע ממקום שכל סביבותיו קרים א"כ ה"נ יכול לבצוע ממקום שהיה דבוקה מש"ה יהיב מהרי"ל הטעם שנראה כפרוסה שאפילו מן הצד לא יבצע ממילא בפרוסה ממש שפיר חותך מן הצד:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>Seif katan 5</small> As written at the beginning of siman 167, from the top and the bottom, which are also crust. There is no obligation to break bread specifically from a place surrounded entirely by crust. Accordingly, here too he could break it from the place where it had been attached. Maharil therefore gives the reason that it appears like a broken loaf, teaching that one should not break it even from the side. It follows that from an actually broken loaf, one may properly cut from the side.
