<b>דבש. </b> פי' שרש"י מפרש פת הבאה בכיסנין היינו מצד המילוי והעיסה עצמה נילושה במים אלא שמלאוהו בצוק"ר או דבש או אגוזים. ובכלל זה מה שעושין בפורים עיסה רחבה וכפולה וממלאין אותה בשומשמין. והי"א הוא הרמב"ם דס"ל דכיסנין הוא מצד העיסה עצמה שנילושה בדבש או תבלין. ונסתפק הב"י אי נימא דוקא שנילושה בהרבה דבש והמיעוט מים אבל אי הוי מיעוט דבש בטל לגבי רוב מים והוי פת גמור. או דלמא אפי' ע"י תערובות מעט דבש כל שניכר הטעם בעיסה מיקרי כיסנין. וכתב דלענין הלכה כיון דמידי דרבנן הוא יש להקל כדברי כולם ועי' מ"ש המ"א על הרמב"ם ועי' בתשובת פנים מאירות סי' ס"ח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Honey.</b> Meaning, Rashi explains that pas haba'ah b'kisnin is defined by its filling, while the dough itself was kneaded with water, but it was filled with sugar, honey, or nuts. Included in this is what they make on Purim, a broad folded dough filled with sesame. The opinion cited as 'some say' is the Rambam, who holds that kisnin is defined by the dough itself, which was kneaded with honey or spices. The Beit Yosef was uncertain whether this means specifically that it was kneaded with much honey and little water, but if there is only a little honey it is nullified to the majority water and is complete bread, or perhaps even through a small admixture of honey, as long as its taste is recognizable in the dough, it is called kisnin. He wrote that for practical halacha, since it is a D'Rabbanan matter, one should be lenient like all the opinions. See what the Magen Avraham wrote regarding the Rambam, and see Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 68.

<b>לפניהם. </b> וא"כ כל מיני פת הנזכרים בסעיף ז' טעונים ברכה לפניהם תוך הסעודה כשאוכלין לקינוח והא דקי"ל בירך על הפת פטר את הפרפרת היינו כשבאין להמשיך תאות המאכל. כללא דמילתא כל שאוכל למזון ולתבשיל כגון עפי"ל פלאדין ופאווידלי פלאדין תמיד מחמת הסעודה הם באים וא"צ לברך עליהם תוך הסעודה כי הפרי נעשה תבשיל תוך המולייתא וקימחא עיקר ואין להחמיר וליקח קצת תפוחים מבפנים ולברך עליהם בפה"ע דהוי ברכה לבטלה דקימחא עיקר עיין סי' קע"ז. ודבר שבא לקינוח או לתענוג כגון עוגות דקות או מיני מתיקה שכתב בס"ז צריכה ברכה ואין הפת פוטרתן אא"כ שבאין להמשיך תאות המאכל מ"א. ואפלטק"ס והויז"ן בלאזי"ן שקורין פריטל"ך צריך לברך במ"מ בתוך הסעודה. ט"ז:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Before them.</b> Accordingly, all types of bread mentioned in seif 7 require a blessing before them within the meal when eaten as dessert. What we rule, that if one blessed on bread he exempted the parperes, refers to when they come to stimulate the appetite. The general principle is: anything eaten for nourishment and as a cooked dish, such as apple fladen and povidel fladen, always comes because of the meal and does not require a blessing within the meal, for the fruit has become a cooked dish inside the filling and the flour is primary. One should not be stringent to take some apples from inside and bless Borei Pri HaEtz on them, for that would be a blessing in vain, since the flour is primary; see siman 177. But something that comes for dessert or pleasure, such as thin cakes or sweets mentioned in seif 7, requires a blessing and the bread does not exempt it, unless they come to stimulate the appetite. Magen Avraham. On aflatkes and hoizen blazen called fritlach, one must recite Borei Minei Mezonos within the meal. Taz.

The Shulchan Aruch here discusses where he fixed his meal on it, and teaches that one might have thought this pastida would not require HaMotzi even if he fixed his meal on it, because the meat is primary; therefore it teaches that this is not so. See there. The Magen Avraham distinguishes between a meat filling and a filling of fruit and spices, for a filling of fruit and spices is dessert and is kisnin. See there. Know that pas haba'ah b'kisnin, on which one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, is so even if it was baked in an oven. Principles of this siman: <b>1</b> dough kneaded with fruit juice and afterward baked, in seif 7; <b>2</b> if it was fried in fruit juice or in water, in seif 13; <b>3</b> if it was filled with fruit, in seif 7; <b>4</b> if it was filled with meat and fish, in seif 16, and all this is when its kneading is thick; <b>5</b> if its batter is soft, in seif 8, seif 14, and seif 15; <b>6</b> and complete bread that one soaked and afterward cooked, in seif 10. This is according to the Mechaber and the Magen Avraham, who distinguish between a meat filling and a fruit filling. But according to the Mechaber as understood by the Taz, there is no distinction between a meat filling and other things, and it is always kisnin. Seif 7 discusses both a meat filling and other fillings, while seif <small>(16)</small> [17] discusses where he fixed a meal on it. Analyze carefully, and see Bnei Chiya, Perach Shushan klal 1 siman 5 and siman 3, and Yad Aharon.
