<b>פת קיבר וקטן</b>. לכאור' משמע בטור דכשיש לחתיכות ב' מעלות לא מברכי' אשלם ע"ש אבל בגמ' משמע כמ"ש בש"ע דהא מסיים אבל פרוסות וכו', ומיירי בפרוסות גדולות מן השלם דבהכי פליגי ודוקא חטים משום דאקדמינהו קרא אבל הכא מין א' והחתיכו' נקיות גדולות אפי' הכי שלם עדיף:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Coarse and small bread.</b> At first glance, the Tur implies that when the pieces have two advantages one does not bless on the whole loaf; see there. But from the Gemara it appears as written in the Shulchan Aruch, for it concludes, 'but slices,' etc., and it is speaking of slices larger than the whole loaf, since that is the case of the dispute. Only wheat is different, because the verse gives it precedence. But here, where they are of one type and the pieces are clean and large, even so the whole loaf is preferable.

<b>מניח הפרוסה</b>. ואם אחר מיסב אצלו יברך אחר על השלימה וא' על הפרוסה (מט"מ וב"ח ושל"ה בשם רש"ל) וצ"ע דהאיך נעשה פשרה שלא נזכ' בגמ' דהא אפי' בית המדרש מבטלין שיברך א' לכלם משום ברוב עם הדרת מלך כמ"ש סי' רצ"ח סי"ד ועוד דהא ליכא פלוגתא בזה דכ"ע ס"ל דפרוס' חטים עדיפא אלא דאמרי' י"ש יצא ידי שניהם הלחמי' וא"כ היאך יברך האחד על השעורים ויניח החטים וצ"ע. ונ"ל דעכ"פ אם האחד אורח יבצע בע"ה על שתיהן דהא שריא ליה לאכול פת של עכו"ם משום זה כמ"ש ס"ה, ועוד נ"ל דאם א' שיפון א"צ להניחו בתוך הפרוסה כמ"ש ס"ד דאינו ממין ז' וכ"ש אם החטים פת ישראל והשיפון פת של עכו"ם דהא לרש"י וסייעתו א"צ כלל להניח פרוסה תוך השלימה והבו דלא לוסיף עלה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He places the slice.</b> If another person is reclining with him, one should bless on the whole loaf and one on the slice. Mateh Moshe, Bach, and Shelah in the name of Maharshal. This requires study, for how can we make a compromise not mentioned in the Gemara? Even the beis midrash is suspended so that one should bless for everyone because 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory,' as written in siman 298, seif 14. Moreover, there is no dispute here, for all agree that the wheat slice is preferable, only that we say a G-d-fearing person satisfies both breads. If so, how can one bless on the barley and leave the wheat? This requires study. It appears to me that at least if one of them is a guest, the host should break bread on both, for he is permitted to eat bread of a non-Jew because of this, as written in seif 5. It further appears to me that if one is rye, he need not place it inside the slice, as written in seif 4, since it is not of the seven species, and certainly if the wheat is Jewish bread and the rye is bread of a non-Jew, for according to Rashi and those who agree with him one need not place a slice inside the whole loaf at all, and we should not add further.

<b>לקבוע עליו</b>. נ"ל דאם הוא קבע סעודתו עליו אף על פי שאכל עמו בשר ודברים אחרים ואלו אכלו לבדו לא היה שבע ממנו אפ"ה מברך המוציא וג' ברכות וכ"מ הל' בגמ' כל שאחרים קובעין עליו וכ"מ קצת בתו' שכתבו גבי לחמניות אם קבע עלייהו כמו בפורים עכ"ל, ובסמ"ק כתוב בליל פורים ובליל ברית מילה, ומסתמא בפורים אין קובעין לבדם ואם אכלו לבדו בעינן שיאכלו כל כך שאחרים רגילים לשבוע ממנו לבדו דאם נשער שיעור שאוכלים עם ד"א נתת דבריך לשיעורין ועפ"ח דערובין דאפשר לשער וצ"ע, ואם הוא שבע ממנו וגם אחרים רגילין לשבוע ממנו כשמלפתין עמו דבר אפילו אכלו לבדו מברך המוציא כיון ששבע ממנו כנ"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>To fix on it.</b> It appears to me that if he fixed his meal on it, even though he ate meat and other foods with it, and had he eaten it alone he would not have been satiated from it, nevertheless he recites HaMotzi and the three blessings. This also appears from the language of the Gemara, 'anything on which others fix a meal,' and somewhat from Tosafot, who wrote regarding small loaves: if he fixed a meal on them, as on Purim. In Semak it is written: on the night of Purim and on the night of a bris milah. Presumably on Purim they do not fix the meal on them alone. If he ate it alone, we require that he eat so much that others are accustomed to be satiated from it alone, for if we measure the amount people eat with other foods, you have made the matter variable. See the first chapter of Eruvin, where it is possible to measure, and this requires study. If he is satiated from it, and others are also accustomed to be satiated from it when they eat something with it, then even if he ate it alone he recites HaMotzi, since he was satiated from it, as appears to me.

<b>שעירב בה דבש</b>. ק' דהרמב"ם בעצמו פסק פ"ו מחמץ מצה שלשה במי פירות יוצא בה י"ח בפסח ובגמרא משמע קצת דאם מקרי לחם לענין מצה ה"ה לענין המוציא וכ"מ ברמב"ם פ"ו דין ז' ע"ש ובברכות דף ל"ז עמ"ש סי' קצ"ו, וע"ק דאם כן יאכל בפסח ולא יצטרך לברך ב"ה ולא משמע כן בסי' קפ"ח ואפשר לומר דהרמב"ם דוקא נקט כאן דבש שמן וחלב דמברך במ"מ דגם להרמב"ם אין יוצא בו י"ח בפסח דהוי מצה עשירה אבל בשאר מי פירות אה"נ דמברך המוציא וב"ה דלא מפקי להו מידי פת ולפ"ז יין הוי כדבש כמ"ש שם ועי"ל דהכא מיירי שאין בה מים כלל שכתב שלשה בדבש או שעירב בה תבלין ואפאה שלשה כולה בדבש אבל התם מיירי שיש בה מעט מים כמ"ש המ"מ שם וצ"ע על הרב"י ששינה ל' הרמב"ם פ"ה וכתב שעירב וכו' ודברי רמ"א עיקר:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Into which he mixed honey.</b> This is difficult, for the Rambam himself ruled in the sixth chapter of Chametz U'Matzah that matzah kneaded with fruit juice fulfills one's obligation on Pesach, and the Gemara implies somewhat that if it is called bread regarding matzah, the same applies regarding HaMotzi. So too it appears from the Rambam, chapter 6, law 7; see there, and in Berachos 37, and what I wrote in siman 196. It is also difficult, for if so, one could eat this on Pesach and would not need to recite Birkas HaMazon, which does not seem so from siman 188. Perhaps the Rambam specifically mentioned here honey, oil, and milk, over which one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, because even according to the Rambam one does not fulfill his Pesach obligation with them, since it is rich matzah. But with other fruit juices, indeed one recites HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon, because they do not remove it from the category of bread. According to this, wine is like honey, as written there. Alternatively, here it is speaking where there is no water at all, for he wrote 'kneaded with honey' or 'mixed with spices and baked,' meaning that it was kneaded entirely with honey, whereas there it is speaking where there is a little water, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote there. This requires study regarding the Beit Yosef, who changed the Rambam's language in chapter 5 and wrote 'mixed,' etc. The words of the Rama are primary.

<b>טעונין ברכה</b>. כ"כ רי"ו והטור והרא"ש, ונ"ל שיצא להם זה ממ"ש בגמ' אין לך דבר שטעון ברכה לפניו ולא לאחריו אלא פת הבאה בכסנין בלבד וס"ל דמיירי תוך הסעוד' לקנוח ולחמניות בכלל (וכ"כ הג"מ ספ"ד) וא"כ כל מיני פת הנזכרים בס"ז טעונים ברכה תוך הסעודה כשאוכלין לקינוח וכ"כ תר"י והא דכתב תר"י במשנה בירך על הפת פטר את הפרפרת פי' ניבלא"ש ופירות וכיוצא בהם כו' ולחמניו' הם ניבלא"ש וא"כ ש"מ דפטר להו פת היינו כשבאין להמשיך תאות המאכל דהא פירות ע"כ מיירי בהכי דאל"כ לא פטר להו פת כמ"ש סי' קע"ז: כללא דמלתא כל שאוכל למזון ולתבשיל כמעשה קדירה שקורין עפי"ל פלאדי"ן ופאויד"לי פלאדין תמיד מחמת הסעודה הם באים וא"צ לברך עליהם תוך הסעודה כי הפירי נעשה תבשיל תוך המולייתא וקימחא עיקר ואין להחמיר וליקח קצת תפוחים מבפנים ולברך עליה בפה"ע דהוי ברכה לבטלה דקימח' עיקר [עמ"ש סי' קע"ז] [של"ה] ודבר שבא לקינוח כגון עוגות דקות כעבין יבשין או מיני מתיקה שכתב ס"ז צריכה ברכה ואין הפת פוטרתן דומי' דלחמניו' ועסי"ג בהג"ה מ"ש ועב"י סימן קע"ז שיש להרשב"א שיטה אחרת אך לא קי"ל כוותי':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Require a blessing.</b> So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, the Tur, and the Rosh. It appears to me they derived this from what the Gemara states: there is nothing that requires a blessing before it and not after it except pas haba'ah b'kisnin alone. They hold that this refers to within a meal for dessert, and small loaves are included. Accordingly, all types of bread mentioned in seif 7 require a blessing within the meal when eaten as dessert. What Rabbeinu Yonah wrote on the Mishnah, that one who blessed on bread exempted parperes, meaning niblash and fruit and the like, and small loaves are niblash, showing that bread exempts them, refers to when they come to stimulate the appetite, for fruit must be in that case, since otherwise bread does not exempt them, as written in siman 177. The general rule is: anything eaten for nourishment and as a cooked dish, like cooked foods called apple fladen and povidel fladen, always comes because of the meal and one need not bless on them within the meal, for the fruit has become a cooked dish inside the filling and the flour is primary. One should not be stringent to take some apples from inside and bless Borei Pri HaEtz, for that is a blessing in vain, since the flour is primary. See what I wrote in siman 177. Shelah. But something that comes as dessert, such as thin cakes, dry cakes, or sweets mentioned in seif 7, requires a blessing and the bread does not exempt it, similar to small loaves. See the gloss in seif 13 and see Beit Yosef, siman 177, that the Rashba has another approach, but we do not rule like him.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Recognizable and known, etc.</b> The Mordechai concludes: that it was not completely dissolved. The Beit Yosef intentionally omitted this, for the Mordechai follows Rashi, who holds that even if it was cooked it is called the form of bread unless it was completely dissolved. But according to us, who hold that even if it was not dissolved it does not have the form of bread once cooked, then likewise, when it was not cooked, if it is not recognizable as bread, it is not called the form of bread. See seif 12. Know that in all these cases, even if he fixed his meal on it, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, since it is not bread at all; see seif 6. The Gemara says that if one crumbled bread until it became like fine meal, then kneaded it together again and baked it again, he must recite HaMotzi, even if it lacks the form of bread, for according to Rabbi Yehudah it is called crumbs that contain an olive-bulk, and with an olive-bulk the form of bread is not required. It implies there even if they were first fried in oil, similar to menachos, as Tosafot wrote in Berachos 37. Accordingly, those who make frimzelach from crumbled bread or from bread soaked in water must recite HaMotzi, since its beginning was bread and it does not leave the category of bread until each piece lacks an olive-bulk. Although according to what the Beit Yosef wrote for the Rambam, if the form of bread passed away completely through cooking, even if they contain an olive-bulk one does not recite HaMotzi, nevertheless we do not rule so, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. Still, if he mixed flour into them and fried them in fat, or kneaded them, it requires study, see seif 13, whether we follow the majority. However, those who fry whole slices plainly recite HaMotzi, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. In Kenesses HaGedolah, in the name of Rashach, part 1, siman 162, he wrote that the fritters made from crushed matzah, mixed with eggs and fried in oil, even though while it is crushed one must recite HaMotzi, nevertheless when he joined it with water and eggs it changed and he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, and certainly after frying. This requires study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And one does not bless.</b> This requires study, for in siman 461, seif 4, it is written that one fulfills the obligation with soaked matzah that was not dissolved, implying even if it was soaked a great deal; if so, he should not need to recite Birkas HaMazon, as written in seif 7. One can answer that he must soak the whole olive-bulk together and eat it, as written at the beginning of siman 475, and then he recites HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon, as written in seif 12. The Maharshal wrote: one who soaks his bread in wine or in some beverage called zupa, Rabbeinu Shlomo wrote that the bread exempts it, and he is never obligated to recite Borei Pri HaGafen or Shehakol unless he drinks the liquid in the manner of drinking. So too it appears from Shiltei Gibborim, page 29, and so wrote the Tanya in his name, concluding: therefore it is good to bless first on the liquid and afterward soak his bread in it. Likewise, when they put wine in a bowl with salt, called seitza, in order to dip meat into it, one need not bless, for the meat is primary and the meat has already been exempted by the blessing on HaMotzi. This follows his own view that liquids require a blessing within a meal. The practical difference for us is if it lacks the form of bread and one recites Borei Minei Mezonos on it; then the bread does not exempt the liquid, so one must bless on the liquid, and so one should practice. Afterward I found in Tosafot, chapter 4, that with rice and wine one blesses on the rice and exempts the wine, for the rice is primary. However, if his main intention is for the soup, the wine is primary, and if his intention is for both, he blesses on both; see what I wrote in siman 177. Maharil wrote in the name of Maharam Shalom that bread that fell into wine during the meal requires Borei Pri HaGafen. It appears to me that he derived this from Tosafot in Berachos 41, that according to Rashi, when one soaks his bread in his wine, bread does not exempt it; see there. However, we hold that the bread exempts it when he soaks into it and eats the bread, as Tosafot explain; see there and siman 212. It appears to me that if one crumbles a little bread very finely into hot beer to give it flavor, he recites only Shehakol on the beer; see siman 208, seif 6. The Rambam writes in chapter 3: any cooked dish into which one mixed one of the five species, whether flour or bread, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, provided that it is not subordinate, etc. See the end of siman 204. Here the bread is subordinate, for his primary intention is to drink the beer, and the bread has no substance or importance.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And even if it became obligated.</b> Meaning, even according to the opinion that it is obligated in challah, but we rule that it is exempt, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 329. The Bach wrote that if his intention was to bake it and he reconsidered and cooked it, Rabbeinu Shimshon agrees that one recites HaMotzi, etc.; see there. In my humble opinion this is not so, for regarding challah, the obligation arises at the time of kneading, and then he intended to bake. But HaMotzi comes at the time of baking. Furthermore, if he baked a little of it and cooked the rest, one does not recite HaMotzi on the cooked portion. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, that Maharam baked a little of it to obligate it in challah and ate it during the meal because of doubt regarding the blessing; see there. So too is the view of Maharam Shalom, who brings proof from a cooked wafer, that although it had been complete bread, once he cooked it, it left the category of bread, and certainly when he only intended to make bread from it. It appears to me that the language of the Beit Yosef misled the Bach, for at the end of this siman he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Shimshon that if one made it like cakes, it is not obligated in challah unless he takes from it a cake and bakes it, for we decree lest he reconsider regarding the whole dough, and the Beit Yosef wrote that the same applies regarding HaMotzi. The Bach understood this to mean that regarding HaMotzi too, if he baked a little, he blesses HaMotzi on the rest. This has no reason, for how does a decree apply at the time of HaMotzi? Also, if so, why did he write plainly in seif 16 that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and not distinguish? Rather, the Beit Yosef's intent must be that the same applies regarding HaMotzi, that it is exempt even if made like cakes, just as it is exempt regarding challah; and that which obligates in challah if he baked a little is because of a decree, which does not apply to HaMotzi. See there.
