**** ENGLISH ****
<b>If</b> one is careful, etc. See Taz and Tosafos, Berachos 39b, s.v. aval. Regarding the doubt of the Sar of Coucy, it is possible that the distinction applies specifically to ritually impure and pure bread, where there is a clear preference because one may not eat the impure bread in purity, and so forth. Bread of a non-Jew, however, may perhaps be displaced only in favor of Jewish bread, so that an observer should not establish this as the halachah. He therefore instructed that it be removed, similar to what Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 9. Alternatively, he may have been uncertain whether one should prefer specifically Jewish bread or specifically the non-Jewish bread, since the prohibition of non-Jewish bread is more lenient, as stated in Yoreh Deah 112:15 in the gloss, and therefore it was necessary to remove one of them. It is also possible that the Mechaber's words, that one who is careful should remove the non-Jewish bread, refer back to the first case as well. Thus, whether he is generally careful or not, if he is eating Jewish bread he should remove the non-Jewish bread, lest it appear disgraced when he chooses the cleaner bread and leaves it aside. But if he is not careful and eats the non-Jewish bread, he need not remove the Jewish bread, for there is no disgrace in choosing the cleaner bread, and the superiority of Jewish bread is not evident from that choice. See Eliyah Rabbah on this. See also what I wrote in Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, and Taz here. See Yoreh Deah, Shach seif katan 21 and Taz seif katan 8, that when a guest reclines with him there may be hostility if he does not break bread over the non-Jewish loaf. It was therefore permitted to break bread over it and use it for the entire meal. This appears to be merely optional; see there. However, in seif 16 in the gloss and in Magen Avraham seif katan 12, we wrote that Terumas HaDeshen and Beit Yosef imply that it is a mitzvah to give it precedence.

<b>והנה</b> באין נזהר ושל עכו״ם שלם אפילו ושל ישראל נקי וגדול פתיתין שלם עדיף מעשר מעלות כמבואר ס״א מ״א א׳ ואם של עכו״ם חטים וישראל שעורים וכדומה י״ל מניח שתיהם יחד כבס״א ומשמע שם אפי׳ נגד כמה מעלות מעלה דמוקדם בקרא שקולים היה וירא שמים יי״ש עיין לבוש ס״א (מ״ש שקולים) ובנזהר מפת של עכו״ם והוא שלם ושל ישראל פרוסה צ״ע ויראה י״ש יי״ש יניח זה אצל זה ויבצע רק מכשר אע״ג די״א יבצע משתיהן כאן א״א ושם ג״כ י״א יבצע מפרוסה וענ״ץ וא״ר והנלע״ד כתבתי ואין להאריך:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Now</b>, where one is not careful and the non-Jewish loaf is whole, even if the Jewish bread is cleaner and larger but consists of pieces, the whole loaf takes precedence over ten other advantages, as explained in seif 1 and Magen Avraham seif katan 1. If the non-Jewish loaf is wheat bread and the Jewish bread is barley bread, or the like, it appears that he should place both together, as in seif 1. It is implied there that even against several advantages, the advantage of the species mentioned earlier in Scripture is weighed equally. A God-fearing person should satisfy both views. See Levush, seif 1, regarding what he writes about equal considerations. If one is careful regarding non-Jewish bread, but that loaf is whole while the Jewish loaf is a piece, the matter requires study. It appears that a God-fearing person who wishes to satisfy both views should place them together but break bread only from the kosher loaf. Although some say he should break from both, that is impossible here, and there too some say he should break only from the piece. See Nechalas Tzvi and Eliyah Rabbah. I have written what appears correct to me, and there is no need to elaborate.

<b>אמר</b> ההדיוט הכותב יש לי בכאן הרהורי דברים והנני פורטם בעזה״י. דע דשלש ברכות בלחם גמור מ״ה הוא ויש להסתפק פת בכיסנין אם אכל כדי שביעה אי הוי מ״ה או מדרבנן ונ״מ באם מסופק אם בירך או לאו והנה פת לעקוכין עי״ד שכ״ט בש״ך אות ט׳ די״א עיסה במי פירות לאו לחם לחלה ה״ה לבהמ״ז לאו להם מיקרי ואין בשבע רק מדרבנן אטו לחם ד״ת ובלא שבע לא גזרו ולהמחבר שם הוי ד״ת וטרוקנין להטור סובר דמר בר רב אשי חולק ברכות ל״א א׳ עב״י כאן וכעכין הם פת גמור רק שרגילין לכוס אותם לתענוג ואין רגילין לאכול כדי שביעה מש״ה כל שלא קבע סעודה לא גזור הא כדי שביעה הוי מ״ה וכן פת שממלאין צוקר ושקדים פת גמור הוי באופן שכעכין ופת שממלאין כיסים אם כדי שביעה מ״ה חייב ובספק חייב לברך כבסי׳ קפ״ה ופת הנילוש במי פירות לרא״ש ספק הוא הואיל ואין חייב בחלה לאו לחם הוי ואם מסופק הוי ס״ס שמא בירך ושמא הלכה כרא״ש וס״ס בד״ת וספק א׳ בדרבנן די כמבואר בי״ד ק״י כה״ג וכובא דארעא ג״כ ספק הוא ובחידושינו ברכות ל״ה א׳ ומ״ב א׳ הערנו בזה. ובמ״א אות י״ו כתבנו דהוי מ״ה ושאני חלה יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>The humble writer says:</b> I have several thoughts here, and with Hashem's help I shall set them forth. Know that the three blessings of Bircas HaMazon after full-fledged bread are Torah-level. There is room to question whether, after one eats enough pas haba'ah b'kisnin to be satiated, his obligation is Torah-level or Rabbinic. A practical difference arises if he is uncertain whether he recited Bircas HaMazon. Regarding pretzel-like bread, see Yoreh Deah 329, Shach seif katan 9, where some hold that dough kneaded with fruit juice is not considered bread for challah. The same would apply to Bircas HaMazon: it would not be considered bread, and even when one is satiated the obligation would be only Rabbinic, as a decree because of true bread. If one is not satiated, the Sages did not decree. According to the Mechaber there, however, the obligation would be Torah-level. Regarding teruknin, the Tur holds that Mar bar Rav Ashi disagrees, as explained in Berachos 37a and Beit Yosef here. Ring-shaped crackers are full-fledged bread, but since they are ordinarily nibbled for pleasure and not eaten to satiation, the Sages did not impose Bircas HaMazon unless one establishes a meal upon them. If one eats enough to be satiated, however, the obligation is Torah-level. Likewise, bread filled with sugar and almonds is full-fledged bread in the manner of those crackers, and bread formed as filled pockets is Torah-level when one eats enough to be satiated. In a case of doubt he must recite Bircas HaMazon, as in siman 185. According to the Rosh, bread kneaded with fruit juice remains doubtful, since if it is not obligated in challah it may not be considered bread. If one is uncertain whether he recited Bircas HaMazon, there is a double doubt: perhaps he did recite it, and perhaps the halachah follows the Rosh. A double doubt suffices regarding a Torah obligation, and a single doubt suffices regarding a Rabbinic obligation, as explained in Yoreh Deah 110 in similar cases. Kova d'ara is likewise doubtful. We discussed this in our novellae to Berachos 35a and 42a. In Magen Avraham seif katan 16, however, we wrote that the obligation is Torah-level and that challah is different; see there.

<b>נסתפקתי</b> באם שנים אכלו פת גמור וא׳ אכל פת כסנין כזית דא״צ לברך בהמ״ז אם יכול להוציא אותם ומה שהביאני לזה הוא מ״ש בקצ״ז במ״א וט״ז י״א ג׳ דכזית יכול להוציא אותם שאכל כ״ש דמ״ה ערבים רק שא״י לומר שאכלנו וכאן יכול לומר שאכלנו אלא דחז״ל לא החמירו עליו כזית לברך דליכא למיגזר אטו כ״ש דאין רגילין לקבוע עליו כו׳ ומ״מ שאכלנו יכול לומר א״ד כל דלא מחויב מדרבנן לא תקנו שיוציא אחרים דבב׳ שאכלו א״צ שאכלנו אפ״ה אין אחד מוציא חבירו אא״כ אכל כזית כמ״ש המ״א קצ״ז י״א כבסי׳ קצ״ג ס״א וטעמא נראה כי מדרבנן החמירו דלא הוי כ״כ ערבות הואיל ואי לא אכל לא מחויב הלכך בעינן שיהא חייב מדרבנן ה״ה כאן. ואי״ה יבואר עוד בקצ״ז י״א מזה ועמ״א קס״ז מ׳ ובמ״ש שם ובפתיחה כוללת מזה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>I was uncertain</b> about the following case: two people ate full-fledged bread, and a third ate only a kezayis of pas haba'ah b'kisnin, for which he is not required to recite Bircas HaMazon. May he discharge their obligation? What led me to this question is what is written in siman 197, Magen Avraham and Taz, seif katan 11: one who ate a kezayis may discharge those who ate any amount, because by Torah law all Jews are responsible for one another, except that he cannot say, “of which we have eaten.” Here, however, he can truthfully say, “of which we have eaten.” The Sages merely did not obligate him to recite Bircas HaMazon after a kezayis, since there was no need to decree because of a smaller amount, as people do not ordinarily establish a meal upon this food. Nevertheless, he can say, “of which we have eaten.” Or perhaps, whenever one is not Rabbinically obligated, the Sages did not institute that he should discharge others. Two people who ate do not need to say, “of which we have eaten,” yet one cannot discharge the other unless he ate a kezayis, as Magen Avraham 197:11 writes, following siman 193:1. The reason appears to be that the Sages were stringent because the principle of mutual responsibility is weaker here, since had he not eaten he would not be obligated. They therefore required him to be Rabbinically obligated. The same may apply here. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in siman 197, seif katan 11. See also Magen Avraham 167:40, what I wrote there, and the general introduction on this subject.

Beit Yosef understands that Rambam rules that regarding broth one favorable factor suffices, following Rav Yosef and Rav Sheshes, whereas regarding cooked bread two conditions are required: a kezayis and the appearance of bread. Taz understands that according to Rambam both conditions are required, whether the bread is cooked or soaked in broth. In cooked bread, even if there was not initially a kezayis in a single crumb, once cooking in a primary vessel causes the pieces to adhere firmly and form a single piece containing a kezayis, it is properly considered a kezayis. <b>And even.</b> See Taz. Rabbeinu Tam's view is stated in Tosafos, Pesachim 37b, s.v. dekulei alma. Thick dough is obligated in challah immediately upon kneading even if one intends to cook it in a pot, because Scripture says, “your doughs.” Likewise, thin batter that is baked in an oven is obligated because Scripture calls it “bread.” Regarding HaMotzi, thick dough prepared with the intention of cooking in a pan, whether with liquid or much oil, is considered bread so long as it retains the appearance of bread. This differs from seif 10, where cooking in a pot is not considered to retain the appearance of bread, unlike frying in a pan; see Magen Avraham seifim katanim 31 and 36. One therefore recites HaMotzi. Rabbeinu Tam proves this from Berachos 37b, which speaks of one standing and offering meal-offerings, implying that even a pan-offering or deep-pan offering, which one intended to fry in much oil, requires HaMotzi. The Rosh, however, in the first chapter of Challah, mishnah 5, holds that dough is obligated in challah only if it was prepared with the intention of baking, and one later changed his mind. Even then, regarding HaMotzi, if one intended to bake it but later fried it in a pan with much oil or liquid, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos. The passage concerning meal-offerings refers to an oven-baked offering. The statement that one ground, baked, and cooked it and still recites HaMotzi when an intact slice remains, in Berachos 37a, follows Rabbi Meir, who holds that cooking does not nullify baking, whereas we rule like Rabbi Yose that it does. So writes the Rosh, and he writes similarly in Pesachim. It is explicit that even if one intended to bake the dough and actually baked it, subsequent frying in a pan nullifies the baking. The statement that food prepared in a pan is obligated applies where there is only a small amount of oil, as stated in seif 14 in the gloss. The Tur writes here that thick dough is obligated in challah, and that one recites HaMotzi where it retains the appearance of bread. Beit Yosef objects that in Yoreh Deah 329 the Tur rules like Rabbeinu Shimshon that the dough is obligated only when one intends to bake it. Bach answers that here too the Tur speaks of dough intended for baking, where Rabbeinu Shimshon agrees that it is obligated in challah and requires HaMotzi. This cannot be correct, as explained above. Perishah likewise writes that according to the Rosh, even dough intended for baking is obligated in challah but does not require HaMotzi, as is evident from the Rosh's statement concerning one who ground, baked, and then cooked it. Taz writes that the Mechaber's ruling here is understandable: although it is obligated in challah, that is because it was intended for baking, as in Yoreh Deah 329:13. In regard to HaMotzi, however, one does not recite it, following Rabbeinu Shimshon in the first chapter of Challah. This remains true even though according to our ruling in seif 10, where a kezayis remains one recites HaMotzi because cooking does not nullify baking. We distinguish the case of one who roasted the korban and then cooked it, and the matzah of siman 461, because the taste of matzah is required, as I wrote in seif katan 12 and in Magen Avraham seif katan 25. Nevertheless, we rule that one does not recite HaMotzi over dough fried in a pan. Seif 10 is different because it was initially full-fledged bread. See Magen Avraham seif katan 31, where with Hashem's help this will be explained. Beit Yosef's objection to the Tur remains, and one cannot answer as Bach does that the case is one in which the dough was intended for baking, for the Tur nevertheless rules unlike Rabbeinu Shimshon, as explained. Yet I cannot interpret Taz's words accordingly, since he connects the law of challah with HaMotzi. One might suggest that according to Rabbeinu Shimshon one indeed does not recite HaMotzi, while the Tur disagrees with him on that point. But then what is the difficulty in connecting challah with HaMotzi? According to Rabbeinu Tam too the laws are not fully identical, since challah is required from the time of kneading even without the appearance of bread, while HaMotzi requires the appearance of bread. One could then answer like Bach. In conclusion, I, the humble writer, have not fully understood Taz's intention here. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham seif katan 31; there is no need to elaborate further here.
