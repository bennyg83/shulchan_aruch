[א] <b>אפילו פת קיבר וכו'.</b> לשון הסמ"ק סימן קנ"א ואפילו הוא פת של שאור, ועיין בנחלת צבי שהאריך לפסוק דאם הפרוסה יפה וגדולה מהשלם יניח הפרוסה תחת השלימה כמו גבי פרוסה של חיטים, וכן נראה לעניות דעתי מתוס' ברכות דף ל"ט ד"ה כל וכו' שכתב זה לשונו, דלמה יברך בגדול הואיל ואינן נקי מזה עד כאן לשונו, משמע דאם הוא נקי יברך בפרוסה הגדולה. אלא שצריך עיון דאם כן יהיה דברי תוס' סותרים דהא בד"ה אבל וכו' משמע כפסק השולחן ערוך ולבוש. שוב מצאתי להדיא בפסקי' תוס' זה לשונו, של חיטים ושל שעורים שלימים או שניהם ממין אחד ואחד יפה מחבירו, עד כאן. הרי דמניח פרוסה תחת השלימה כמו בפרוסה של חיטים וצריך עיון, מיהו לדינא ראוי לעשות כן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Even coarse bread, etc.</b> This is the language of the Semak, siman 151: even if it is sourdough bread. See Nachalas Tzvi, who wrote at length to rule that if the piece is better and larger than the whole loaf, he should place the piece beneath the whole loaf, as with a piece of wheat bread. So it also seems in my humble opinion from Tosafot, Berachos, folio 39, s.v. kol, etc., who wrote as follows: why should he bless on the large one, since they are not as clean as this one, end quote. This implies that if it is clean, he blesses on the larger piece. However, this requires study, for if so the words of Tosafot would be contradictory, since in s.v. aval, etc., it implies like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush. I then found it explicitly in Piskei Tosafot, as follows: if wheat bread and barley bread are whole, or both are of one species and one is better than the other, end quote. Thus, he places the piece beneath the whole loaf, as with a piece of wheat bread, and this requires study. Nevertheless, as a matter of law it is proper to do so.

[ב] <b>חיטין וכו'.</b> ועיין בסעיף ד' דכוסמין לא (מלבושי יום טוב). כתב הפרישה נראה דפת שיפון כרוב פת שלנו ופת כוסמין פת כוסמין קודם דכוסמין מין חיטין ושיפון מין שעורים והוא הדין לאינך, עד כאן, ועיין ביו"ד סימן שכ"ד. כתב מגן אברהם נראה לי דאם השיפון שלם אין צריך להניחו בתוך הפרוסה, עד כאן. ולי נראה דאף שהוא של כוסמין דינא הכי דדוקא בשלם שעורים מניח, ועיין לקמן סעיף ד':
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Wheat, etc.</b> See seif 4, that spelt is not included, according to Malbushei Yom Tov. The Perishah wrote: it seems that rye bread, like most of our bread, and spelt bread, the spelt bread takes precedence, for spelt is a type of wheat and rye is a type of barley; the same applies to the others, end quote. See Yoreh Deah, siman 324. The Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me that if the rye is whole, he need not place it inside the piece, end quote. To me it seems that even if it is spelt, the law is the same, for only with whole barley does he place it; see below, seif 4.

[ג] <b>מניח הפרוסה וכו'.</b> כתב מגן אברהם ושל"ה וב"ח וכל אחרונים בשם רש"ל דדוקא כשסועד יחידי אבל אם סועדים שנים יברך אחד על הפרוסה של חיטין ואחד על שלימה של שעורים, עד כאן. ומגן אברהם תמה הא כולי עלמא סבירא להו דפרוסת חיטים עדיפא אלא דאמרינן ירא שמים יצא ידי שניהם, ואם כן היאך יברך אחד על השעורין ויניח החיטים, עד כאן. ולעניות דעתי משמע מאשרי דלר' שלמן איכא פלוגתא והיינו שכתב הלבוש שקולין הן וכן משמע בב"ח. ומה שהקשה מסימן רצ"ח סעיף ד' דהא אפילו בית המדרש מבטלין שיברך אחד לכולם לא קשה מידי דשאני במוציא דבעי צרופי כדלעיל סימן קס"ו ואפילו באורח נראה לי דדינא הכי, וכן משמע בב"ח ודלא כמגן אברהם. מיהו כששניהם ממין אחד כתב הב"ח דאין לעשות כן כיון דקיימא לן דשלם עדיף, ועיין מה שכתבתי לעיל באם הפרוסה נקיה משלימה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He places the piece, etc.</b> The Magen Avraham, the Shelah, the Bach, and all the later authorities wrote in the name of the Maharshal that this is specifically when one eats alone; but if two are eating, one should bless on the piece of wheat bread and one on the whole barley bread, end quote. The Magen Avraham wondered: all agree that the piece of wheat bread is preferable, but we say that a God-fearing person should fulfill both views. If so, how can one bless on the barley and leave the wheat? end quote. In my humble opinion, it is implied from the Ashri that according to R. Zalman there is a dispute, and this is what the Levush wrote, that they are equivalent; so it also implies in the Bach. What he challenged from siman 298, seif 4, that even a beis midrash is cancelled so that one person should bless for all, is not difficult at all, for HaMotzi is different, since it requires joining together, as above in siman 166. Even regarding a guest, it seems to me that the law is the same, and so it implies in the Bach, unlike the Magen Avraham. However, when both are of one species, the Bach wrote that one should not do this, since we hold that whole takes precedence. See what I wrote above regarding a case where the piece is cleaner than the whole loaf.

[ד] <b>מברך על הגדולה.</b> כתב נחלת צבי דוקא אם שניהם שוים ביופי, אבל אם הקטן פת ונקי והגדול פת קיבר מברך על הקטן, עד כאן. ונראה לי ראיה לזה מתוספתא שהביא תוס' שם שלימה דגלוסקאות ושלימה דבעל הבית מברך על שלימה דגלוסקאות פרוסה דגלוסקאות ושלימה דב"ה מברך על השלימות דבעל הבית, ואם איתא דמברך על שלם היותר גדול הוה ליה למינקט הכל בשלימה ויותר רבותא כגון שלימה קטן דגלוסקאות וגדול דבעל הבית מברך על בעל הבית, אלא מוכח דאז מברך על שלם קטן דגלוסקאות, ואפשר דבהכי מיירי רישא דתוספתא והיינו דהוצרך למיתני דמברך על שלימה דגלוסקאות:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He blesses on the larger one.</b> The Nachalas Tzvi wrote that this is specifically when both are equal in quality; but if the smaller one is fine bread and the larger one is coarse bread, he blesses on the smaller one, end quote. It seems to me that there is proof to this from the Tosefta cited by Tosafot there: if there is a whole loaf of fine bread and a whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the whole loaf of fine bread; if there is a piece of fine bread and a whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the whole loaf of the householder's bread. If it were so that he blesses on the larger whole loaf, it should have stated everything with whole loaves, with a greater novelty, for example: a small whole loaf of fine bread and a large whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the householder's bread. Rather, it is proven that in that case he blesses on the small whole loaf of fine bread. It is possible that this is what the first clause of the Tosefta discusses, and that is why it needed to teach that he blesses on the whole loaf of fine bread.

שניהם נקיים אין מברכין על השלם של כותים דאם לא כן לאשמועינן רבותא אפילו בשניהם נקיים, ואפשר לדחוק ולדחות ודו"ק וכן משמע יותר בשל"ה שהבאתי. ולדינא נראה לי דאף בשניהן נקיים ופת כותים שלם יש להניח הפרוסה תחת שלימה. עוד יש לומר מדלא הזכיר בתוספתא וכל הפוסקים אלא פת כותים נקיים ופת קיבר של ישראל משמע דאם של כותים מחיטין וישראל שעורים יברך על של כותים, ומי שלבו נקפו בדבר זה יבצע לשתיהן ויניח של ישראל תחת של כותים:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He blesses on whichever, etc.</b> The Bach here and in Yoreh Deah, siman 112, challenged the Shulchan Aruch and Levush in this, for it is explained in the Mordechai, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, that regarding one who is not careful, the minister of Coucy instructed that the white bread be removed; but regarding one who is careful, it need not be removed. Nachalas Tzvi added to the difficulty, for he saw this explicitly in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 32. In my humble opinion, the words of the Mordechai should be explained that the minister of Coucy is indeed always discussing one who is careful. This also seems so, for it implies that the minister of Coucy instructed this for himself, and presumably he was careful about bread of non-Jews. The reason the Mordechai brought this regarding one who is not careful is that he brings proof from one who is careful to one who is not careful: since regarding one who is careful he instructed to remove it, the same applies to one who is not careful, that he blesses on whichever he wishes; if he wants to bless on the kosher bread, he removes the white bread from the table. But in truth he has permission to bless on the white bread, since he is not careful. So it also seems to me to explain the words of Tosafot there, folio 39, s.v. aval, etc. A clear proof to this is that Tosafot and all the poskim bring proof for this law from the Tosefta, that he blesses on whichever he wishes. If so, how could the minister disagree with a Tosefta? And if he disagreed with the proof, Tosafot or one of the poskim should have explained his reason. Rather, perforce, they do not disagree, and according to everyone, one who is not careful blesses on whichever he wishes. So too in the Agudah it implies that the minister refers to one who is careful; see there, and see what I wrote at the end of s.k. 13. As for the halacha: one who is careful should always remove the white bread and bless on kosher bread, and one who is not careful blesses on whichever he wishes; if he wants to bless on the kosher bread, he should remove the white bread. The Magen Avraham in siman 284 wrote in the name of the Maharshal that if another person is with him, he should give him the kosher bread to bless on, and he should bless on the white bread, for in such a case everyone agrees that it is a mitzvah to divide them, end quote. So too the Perishah wrote in his name, that he should give kosher bread to one of the members of his household. In my humble opinion, this does not seem so from the Mordechai and Terumas HaDeshen, siman 32, and it also implies otherwise from the Shulchan Aruch and Levush in the law of a householder who is not careful while the members of his household are careful, and in the law of a householder who is careful while a Jew reclines with him; examine it carefully. It is strained to distinguish between them, and so it also implies explicitly from Issur VeHeter HaAruch, klal 44, and the Bach in Yoreh Deah there. It seems that for this reason the later authorities did not write this, as they wrote the words of the Maharshal above regarding a piece of wheat bread. The Shelah, folio 89, wrote that it is reasonable that the rule that he blesses on whichever he wishes applies when both are whole or both are not whole; but if one is whole, the whole one takes precedence, because it has an additional advantage over the other, end quote. So Nachalas Tzvi also inferred, and I also found in a small Mordechai on parchment as follows: fine impure bread and coarse pure bread, he blesses on whichever he wishes, end quote. Tosafot and the poskim compare bread of non-Jews to impure bread. Nachalas Tzvi further wrote that if the Jewish bread is a fine piece and the non-Jewish bread is a whole coarse loaf, since they said that it is a mitzvah to bless on a whole loaf even if it is coarse, as above in seif 1, it seems that he should place the piece beneath the whole loaf and break from both. But if both are fine, or both are coarse, and the Jewish bread is a piece while the non-Jewish bread is whole, he blesses on whichever he wishes, for they are equal in their advantages, as mentioned above. I am astonished, for in the first case, where the Jewish piece has two advantages, he nevertheless ruled that one should not set aside the whole loaf; all the more so in the latter case, where the piece has only one advantage, one should not set aside the whole loaf because of the piece. If so, at any rate he must break also from the whole loaf; why then did he rule that he blesses on whichever he wishes, which implies that if he wishes he blesses on the piece and leaves the whole loaf entirely? Furthermore, it is difficult to me: since we hold that with coarse Jewish bread and fine non-Jewish bread, he blesses on whichever he wishes, perforce the advantage of Jewish bread is not greater than the advantage of fine bread, but they are completely equal. If so, since we hold in seif 1 that the advantage of whole bread is greater than the advantage of a fine piece, for we bless on the whole loaf even when it is coarse bread, the same should be true that the advantage of whole bread is greater than the advantage of Jewish bread. If so, it would be proper to bless on the whole loaf of a non-Jew even when both are fine; examine this carefully. However, from the small Mordechai cited above it also implies that if both are fine, one does not bless on the whole loaf of a non-Jew, for otherwise it should have taught a greater novelty, even when both are fine. It is possible to force a distinction and reject this, and examine it carefully; so it also seems more from the Shelah that I cited. As a matter of law, it seems to me that even when both are fine and the non-Jewish bread is whole, he should place the piece beneath the whole loaf. One can further say that since the Tosefta and all the poskim mentioned only fine bread of non-Jews and coarse bread of a Jew, it implies that if the non-Jewish bread is wheat and the Jewish bread is barley, he should bless on that of the non-Jew. One whose heart troubles him in this matter should break from both and place the Jewish bread beneath that of the non-Jew.

<b>[Levush:] We rule, etc.</b> So wrote the Beis Yosef, as follows: with respect to the halacha, since it is a doubt in a D'Rabbanan matter, we rule like all of them leniently, that one does not bless HaMotzi or the three blessings over them unless he established his meal upon it or ate the amount that people normally establish as a meal, end quote. The Bach wrote: this is astonishing. Why did he call it a leniency? If he does not recite the blessing that is proper for it, either way it is a stringency, for he transgresses You shall not take. Therefore he disagrees with this ruling, that even our kichlin should not be eaten except within a meal, as above. But I found in the Derishah that he wrote as follows: perhaps this is because the blessing Borei Minei Mezonos is a general blessing like Shehakol, and therefore one should bless Borei Minei Mezonos on all of them. For the final blessing we are lenient and shorten, blessing Me'ein Shalosh, even though Me'ein Shalosh does not stand in place of the three blessings, end quote. Similar to this I now saw printed in the Taz, as follows: certainly Borei Minei Mezonos exempts everything, even dough that was mixed. However, because of the importance of bread, HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon were fixed upon it; therefore, whenever it is not clear to us that it has that importance and is not included in kisnin, we rule leniently, end quote. See below, siman 268, s.k. 18, and see below, s.k. 26; this requires study. I am also astonished at the Beis Yosef, for he contradicts himself, since he already wrote that the matter does not depend on his own establishment of a meal, but on most people, and so he ruled in the Shulchan Aruch. How then did he write here, unless he established a meal or ate the amount, etc.? Perhaps because with all these cases there is a dispute among the poskim, he holds that one may rely on the Raavad cited in the Beis Yosef, that the matter also depends on his own establishment. Although Purim kreplach are also included in this, and I have already written in the name of the Bach and Shelah that everyone agrees in this case, perhaps the Beis Yosef holds that the one who explains kakin as dry cakes holds that regarding kreplach their law is like bread, while the Bach does not hold so. Alternatively, the Bach and Shelah discuss our kreplach, which are kneaded with honey, and in this case everyone agrees that it is permitted. But the Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch refer to kreplach that are kneaded with water, only filled with honey and fat; examine this carefully, and it requires study. Also, according to this, why did they not write it in the Shulchan Aruch? Perhaps he retracted.
