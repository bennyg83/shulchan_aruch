<b>Every vessel.</b> Pesachim 30b: all vessels used with chametz cold may be used with matzah cold, except a yeast-house, etc.; so is the reading of the early authorities. And the Mordechai wrote: not that with hot [use] it is forbidden, but because of the latter clause of a yeast-house: even if one used cold, since its leavening is hard, it is as if one used hot, and it is forbidden to use even cold; and the same applies to every thing whose use is hot, lest one come to use it hot; and so too the Ran wrote from that of a yeast-house, as appears to me; only that he explained that in the first clause it is forbidden to use hot; and so wrote the Rambam; only that the Ran wrote specifically outside [a yeast-house] because it is sharp like wine of libation; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that "one uses cold" was stated not that hot is forbidden, but to exclude that if one used hot, even cold use is forbidden; and the Ran wrote that therefore they said regarding pots on Pesach they should be broken; and in Chullin 111b, that of that plate, etc.; only that the Ran and Maggid Mishneh wrote specifically an earthenware vessel, like the reading of the Rif and Rambam and the Geonim: every earthenware vessel, etc.; and the reason is that since it has no remedy by kashering, one comes to keep it, and thereby comes to use it hot. And the Rosh does not have the reading "cold," and permits even hot; and see above se'if 22. And here he ruled according to the Mordechai's reading, who reads "every vessel," etc., and according to his explanation; and so is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 5 in the gloss; only that he wrote specifically for fixed [use], but temporary use is permitted, as is written at the end of Avodah Zarah: and thrust, etc., and to cut cold with it; but if one used cold, it is permitted even hot according to all readings, and even according to the Rambam and Ran; and see Milchamot. And even regarding earthenware, of which the Ran wrote specifically regarding chametz which is sharp like wine of libation: if one puts it away for keeping, and even if one does not put it away for keeping, only that it is earthenware and its first use was with wine of libation, it is forbidden, as is written in Avodah Zarah 33; see there. But regarding other prohibitions, no; and so he ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 5 in the gloss; only that he was stringent regarding other vessels if there is concern that sometimes they were used hot, since kashering is possible. And all this is with rinsing; but without rinsing it is certainly forbidden, as is written at the end of Avodah Zarah 73: things used cold, rinse them, etc.; and even to use them cold, as is written in Chullin: cold with cold it is; granted, etc.; and there 111b: a knife that slaughtered, etc.; and we hold like the view that rinsing is required, and it refers to a knife, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of Chullin, se'if 10; see there. And even though it did not absorb at all, as is written there 8b: permitted [food] too, etc.; and likewise with a treifah knife to slaughter with it, even according to the view that a yeast-house cold needs rinsing; and specifically a kosher knife to slaughter is permitted; and see Tosafos there s.v. "a knife." And all this is regarding something moist; but regarding something dry it is permitted, as is written there siman 91, se'if 2 in the gloss; and see Tosafos of Avodah Zarah 32a, s.v. "and this," and the like.

<b>Se'if 3. Knives: one hagals them.</b> The Gemara there. And in the first chapter of Chullin 8b it requires libun for a knife; and likewise in the Tosefta of Avodah Zarah: the spits and the grids and the knives, one libuns them in the fire. Rabbeinu Tam answered in Tosafos of Chullin there s.v. "that he libuned it," and at the end of Avodah Zarah 76b s.v. "he said," to distinguish between chametz, which absorbed permitted [food], and other prohibitions, which absorbed a prohibition; and as he distinguished there in the Gemara regarding spits and grids; see there. And the Ran wrote that so is the view of the Ra'avad; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that so is the view of the Rambam, who in the laws of chametz wrote the knives by hagalah, and in chapter 16 of the laws of forbidden foods wrote by libun; and therefore in the laws of chametz he omitted that of spits and grids that need libun, for according to his view they too [are] by hagalah, and it is not his way to write what is not explicit in the Gemara. And Rabbeinu Tam further answered there and there to distinguish between a small knife and a large knife on which one roasts meat, whose use is by means of fire; and he brings proof from the Yerushalmi at the end of Avodah Zarah: a knife, one thrusts it three times into the ground and that suffices; R' Bun in the name of R' Yehuda: this was said regarding a small knife, but a large knife needs libun. And Tosafos rejected his proof, that the Yerushalmi deals with cutting cold, and regarding cold there is no distinction whether use is by fire or by hot water; rather this is the meaning: a large knife tends to have grooves, and therefore neither polishing nor hagalah helps. And R' Baruch resolves the proof; see there. And accordingly it is possible to say there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions; and so is the view of the Rif and Rosh in the chapter "Kol Shaah," who brought the baraita of spits and grids regarding chametz. And the Ran wrote that so is the view of the Ramban, that chametz is not called "permitted absorption," since the name chametz is upon it; which is not so regarding consecrated foods, which are not called leftover before the time of their prohibition; and one must distinguish between large and small; and this even according to the first answer regarding a small knife: since its use is not by fire, it does not need libun, as is written there in the baraita; and so wrote the Rashba, that in every case a knife needs only hagalah, and they erased from the reading of Avodah Zarah "the knives," for so they are not in the Tosefta; and see the Ran and Terumat HaDeshen. But Rivam wrote that even a small one needs libun, for sometimes one fixes a tallow wick and turns meat on coals; and Rabbeinu Tam rejected his reasoning, since its primary use is not by fire; and so wrote the Ran; and a proof: that for every vessel we follow its majority use, like cups and flasks: even though sometimes one may use them hot, nevertheless we follow the primary use and rinse them. And Beis Yosef wrote that according to the conclusion of Tosafos one should distinguish between that of Pesachim and that of Chullin and the Tosefta: between a small one that has no grooves, etc. And the words of the Shulchan Aruch at first glance require study: for in Yoreh De'ah siman 121 he wrote between large and small, and even if it has no grooves it needs libun to cut boiling with it; and here he wrote in general "knives," etc., and afterward in se'if 7: vessels, etc., such as spits and grids, need libun. And the main point is that he ruled like the Rif and Rosh, that there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions; for otherwise the whole siman collapses, and libun would not apply at all regarding chametz. And even though Rabbeinu Tam distinguishes between small and large, there in Yoreh De'ah he is concerned for Rivam's ruling as a mere stringency; for here he ruled like Rabbeinu Tam and the Ran, as is written in se'if 6: every vessel, we follow, etc. And further, even according to Rivam, only the edge of the knife needs libun, as the Rosh wrote in Avodah Zarah; only as a stringency he wrote thus; which is not so for a chametz knife of a Jew, whose way of use is not by fire even with a large knife; and therefore he wrote generally here.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Se'if 26. Glass vessels even, etc.</b> For in such a case kashering is required even cold, as is written in Avodah Zarah 33a and 74b. And even though the poskim disagree there whether it deals specifically with earthenware or even other vessels, nevertheless regarding earthenware there is no dispute; and glass vessels are like earthenware, as is written below. Nevertheless they do not need kashering, for they do not absorb, as is written in Pesachim 74b: different is a heart, for it is smooth; and see Rashi in Shabbos 15b s.v. "with a glass vessel," etc.; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 135, se'if 8. And therefore even if one used hot, it does not need kashering, as is written regarding a heart there. And it is taught explicitly in Avot deRabbi Natan, chapter 41: an earthenware vessel absorbs and does not expel, etc.; a glass vessel does not absorb and does not expel, etc.; in a hot place, hot; in a cold place, cold. And even though they asked regarding smooth konya whether it absorbed, and resolved: we see that they bring there because it is a slight lining; but a whole glass vessel does not absorb. Tosafos in Avodah Zarah there s.v. the aforementioned "konya," etc.; and see there.
