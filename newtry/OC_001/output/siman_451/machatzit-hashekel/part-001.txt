**** ENGLISH ****
And so is Beis Yosef's view, who omitted the law of a kli sheni; thus he retracted and holds specifically use through pouring. From this there is no proof from what the Rama wrote, "and some forbid," which implies that the Mechaber permits it. For it is known that such is the Rama's way: sometimes if it is not explicit in the Mechaber's words he writes upon it the language "and some say"; rather, the proof is that Beis Yosef did not bring the Rambam's view that forbids even a kli sheni. And even though in his book Beis Yosef he wrote on the Tur's language, which is the language of the Shulchan Aruch, that the Tur too holds that a kli sheni is forbidden: that is because according to Beis Yosef the Rambam forbids a kli sheni; if so, in order not to create a dispute between the Rambam and the Tur, we say that what the Tur wrote is not precise, and the same applies to a kli sheni. But in the Shulchan Aruch Beis Yosef should not have left it obscure and given room to err that pouring specifically is meant; rather, in truth he retracted and holds that the Tur mentioned pouring precisely, and the Rambam too holds so, as Magen Avraham wrote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For behold the Rashba, etc.</b> That is to say: even though we have not found anyone who is more stringent on Pesach than regarding other prohibitions to forbid a kli sheni, nevertheless behold the Rashba, etc. And what Chok Yaakov wrote: how can one say that Beis Yosef holds that a kli sheni does not forbid: behold he wrote in se'if 5: a vessel that was used, etc., and if their use is in a kli sheni, their kashering is in a kli sheni. And necessarily that means an actual kli sheni; where use is through pouring, he wrote its law afterward: a vessel that one uses through pouring, etc. Magen Avraham himself already resolved this at the end of s.k. 13, where he wrote on this: and all this is l'chatchila. And see what I wrote in se'if 1. That is to say: Beis Yosef holds that l'chatchila one should also hagal a vessel used through a kli sheni; but here, dealing with earthenware that has no remedy by hagalah, it is like after the fact; and therefore Magen Avraham holds that Beis Yosef relies on the main law that a kli sheni needs no kashering. And nevertheless Magen Avraham holds that one must reconsider the matter and be stringent according to the Rashba's words; and see Peri Chadash of Yoreh De'ah siman 68, who is stringent even all year regarding a kli sheni.

<b>וכ"מ באו"ה כו'</b> הבין בס' חמד משה דמייתי ראיה מאו"ה דבקדם טלאי צריך ליבון. וע"ז האריך וכתב שהב"ח ומ"א שגגו בכוונת או"ה ע"ש. ולענ"ד כוונתם להביא ראיי' להיפך דעכ"פ ליבון מהני דס"ד דבעי' דוק' כתשמישו כך הכשירו וכיון דנשתמש ע"י רותחין לא מהני הליבון אע"ג דבאמת עדיף מיניה הליבון מ"מ יש מקום לטעות וע"ז הביאו ראי' מאו"ה דעכ"פ ליבון מהני וזה מוכח שם מאו"ה ע"ש וה"ה דה"מ להביא ראי' לזה מדברי הטור שכ' וכן קדיר' של מתכות אם רוצה להסיקו ולמלאה גחלים שרי עכ"ל. ואולי אפשר דבאו"ה כ"כ על דין טלאי דאפשר דגרע לכן הביאו ראיה מאו"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And so it is implied in Issur VeHeter, etc.</b> Chemdat Moshe understood that he brings proof from Issur VeHeter that where the patch preceded, libun is required. And on this he lengthened and wrote that the Bach and Magen Avraham erred in Issur VeHeter's intent; see there. And in my humble opinion their intent is to bring proof to the contrary: that in any case libun helps; for one might have thought we specifically need as its use so its kashering; and since one used by means of boiling water, libun does not help; even though in truth libun is better than it, nevertheless there is room to err. And on this they brought proof from Issur VeHeter that in any case libun helps; and this is proven there from Issur VeHeter; see there. And the same applies that they could have brought proof for this from the Tur's words, who wrote: and likewise a metal pot, if one wishes to heat it and fill it with coals, it is permitted; end quote. And perhaps it is possible that in Issur VeHeter he wrote thus specifically regarding a patch law, which may be worse; therefore they brought proof from Issur VeHeter.

<b>וז"ש עד שישרף גוף האיסור ואם קדם כו'</b> נרא' שנדפס בטעות וצריך להפך הדברים וכצ"ל ממנו ואם קדם טלאי לבליעה די בליבון קל וז"ש עד שישרף גוף האיסור כו' ר"ל דלשיטות מ"א דהרשב"א מיירי כה"ג בקדים טלאי לבליעה ואפ"ה מצריך גחלים והיינו ליבון קל. ולכן כ' רשב"א ל' עד שישרוף גוף האיסור דלא מהני במקום ליבון אלא לשרוף מה שהוא בעין תחת סדקי הטלאי:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And that which he wrote, until the body of the prohibition is burned; and if it preceded, etc.</b> It appears that it was printed in error, and one must reverse the words, and so one should read from it: and if the patch preceded absorption, light libun suffices; and that which he wrote, until the body of the prohibition is burned, etc.: that is to say, according to Magen Avraham's approach that the Rashba deals with such a case where the patch preceded absorption, and even so he requires coals, namely light libun. And therefore the Rashba wrote the language "until it burns the body of the prohibition": that it does not help in place of libun except to burn what is in kind under the cracks of the patch.

**** ENGLISH ****
And see Yoreh De'ah siman 120 regarding immersion: that is to say, that he wrote: vessels coated with lead inside or outside: immerse without a blessing (but if coated outside and inside, Peri Chadash ruled there to immerse and bless). If so we learn: even though the vessels themselves need no immersion, for they are earthenware, nevertheless through a lead coating they need immersion; if so the same applies to old vessels: even though they were already immersed, nevertheless if one coated them with lead or tin they need immersion again because of the coating.
