[ה] <b>ואם מלאו וכו'.</b> זה לשון בית יוסף הרשב"א בתורת הבית נראה שלא היה גורס בש"ס ואי מליא גומרא שפיר דמי שכתב ואם בכלי שאין דרכו להסיק מבפנים כקדירה אף שהסיקו מבפנים אסור שמא יחוס ואם החזיר לכבשן מותר, עד כאן. והוא הדין בוכיא שאין דרכו להסיק מבפנים, ואפשר דמודה הרשב"א בבוכיא כיון דעשוי מלבנים ועפר עד כאן לשון בית יוסף. ובחק יעקב האריך לתמוה מאי תמה בית יוסף על הרשב"א הא חילוק זה דבוכיא מוכח בסוף סימן דקאמר אי מליא גומרא שרי עיין שם. ולא קשה מידי דמכל מקום כיון דרשב"א לא הביא שם דין דמליא גומרי סלקא אדעתיה דלא גריס להו, ועוד הא בש"ס יש לומר דגם בקדרות מותר במליא גומרי כמו שכתב, ובמחילות כבודו לא ראה רק בתורת הבית דף מ"ו אבל אשתמיטתיה בדף ל"ג שפסק להדיא דמותר במלאו גחלים, גם בתורת הבית הארוך לרשב"א דף פ"ג לא ראה, שהעתיק הש"ס ואי מליא גומרי שפיר וגריס כמו בש"ס דילן, גם תימא עליו דמבואר להדיא לרשב"א שם דמתיר אף בבוכיא של חרס במליא גומרי וכן משמע בשולחן ערוך ולבוש שכתבו וכן תנור קטן שקורין פדילא וכו' והוא כלי חרס כמו שכתב בית יוסף ריש סימן תס"א, וכן הוכחתי בספרי ליו"ד ריש סימן צ"ז מתוס' פסחים דף ל' דאם לא כן מאי מקשים על הא דבוכיא דשרי במליא גומרי הא התורה העידה על כלי חרס שאינו יוצא מידי דופיו לעולם עיין שם הא בוכיא מיירי מלבנים ועפרא, אלא על כרחך סבירא ליה דמיירי בחרס וכן פירש הרמב"ם פרק ה' מהלכות חמץ. גם תמהתי מזה על הב"ח כאן שכתב בשם התוס' להיפך דמיירי מלבנים ועפר. והא דקיימא לן דבר דהסיקו מחוץ לא מהני היסק יש לומר דוקא קדרות דחייס מה שאין כן בבוכיא דלא חייס אף בשל חרס וכן מבואר ברוקח סוף סימן תפ"ד וכל זה כתבתי בקצרה באליהו זוטא. עוד יש לומר דמליא גומרא דמתיר בש"ס קאי נמי אקדרות דדוקא היסק בעלמא אמרינן דחייס מה שאין כן במליא גומרי אנו רואין דלא חייס ודמי לחוזר בכבשן וכן נראה דעת מגן אברהם וכן משמע בתוס', שם וכן מבואר להדיא בפסקי תוס' פרק כל שעה סימן פ"ח, וכל זה דלא כשבלי לקט ותניא שפירשו דמליא גומרי שרי מיירי דוקא בתנור שהסיקו מבפנים:

[5] <b>And if one filled it, etc.</b> This is the language of Beis Yosef: the Rashba in Torat HaBayit appears not to have had the reading in the Gemara, "and if it is filled with coals, it is fine," for he wrote: and if it is a vessel that is not normally heated from inside, like a pot, even if one heated it from inside it is forbidden, lest one be concerned; and if one returned it to the kiln, it is permitted; end quote. And the same applies to a bukhya that is not normally heated from inside; and it is possible that the Rashba agrees regarding a bukhya, since it is made of bricks and earth; end of the language of Beis Yosef. And in Chok Yaakov he lengthened to wonder: what does Beis Yosef wonder at the Rashba? Behold this distinction of a bukhya is proven at the end of the siman, where it says if it is filled with coals it is permitted; see there. And it is no difficulty at all: for nevertheless, since the Rashba did not bring there the law of filled with coals, it came to his mind that he does not have that reading. And further, in the Gemara one may say that even regarding pots it is permitted when filled with coals, as he wrote. And with apologies for his honor, he saw only Torat HaBayit folio 46, but folio 33 escaped him, where he rules explicitly that it is permitted when filled with coals; also in the long Torat HaBayit of the Rashba, folio 83, he did not see, where he copied the Gemara "and if filled with coals, fine," and has the reading like our Gemara. Also it is puzzling upon him, for it is explicit in the Rashba there that he permits even an earthenware bukhya when filled with coals; and so it is implied in the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who wrote "and likewise a small oven that they call a padila," etc., and that is an earthenware vessel, as Beis Yosef wrote at the beginning of siman 461; and so I proved in my book on Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 97 from Tosafos of Pesachim folio 30, for if not so, what do they ask on that of a bukhya that is permitted when filled with coals: behold the Torah testified regarding an earthenware vessel that it never leaves the status of its wall; see there: for if a bukhya meant bricks and earth, then necessarily he holds that it means earthenware; and so the Rambam explained in chapter 5 of the laws of chametz. I also wondered from this at the Bach here, who wrote in the name of Tosafos to the contrary, that it means bricks and earth. And as for what we hold, that a thing heated from outside: heating does not help: one may say that is specifically pots, for which one is concerned; which is not so for a bukhya, for which one is not concerned even if of earthenware; and so it is explained in the Rokeach at the end of siman 484; and all this I wrote briefly in Eliyahu Zuta. One may further say that "filled with coals" which the Gemara permits also refers to pots: for specifically mere heating we say one is concerned; which is not so when filled with coals, for we see that one is not concerned, and it is similar to returning to a kiln; and so appears the view of Magen Avraham; and so it is implied in Tosafos there; and so it is explicit in Piskei Tosafos, chapter "Kol Shaah," siman 88; and all this is not like Shibbolei HaLeket and Tanya, who explained that "filled with coals is permitted" deals specifically with an oven heated from inside.

**** ENGLISH ****
[6] <b>One hagals, etc.</b> Since it absorbed permitted [food]; but if it absorbed a prohibition, I explained in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 121 that libun is required. And Mateh Moshe wrote that even on Pesach, one who is stringent to libun it, may blessing come upon him; and so it is implied in Magen Avraham, because sometimes they stick in a knife bread called "penitz" by the fire, or stick in it a pastry or pancakes by the fire; and in the piyut: and if new is possible, it is praiseworthy. And where the handle is of wood, Mahariv siman 153 wrote that by law hagalah does not help, for one is concerned for it; and likewise regarding wood, since it is attached with glue, one is concerned for it. And the Bach wrote that the same applies if the handle is fixed with nails, for presumably there are grooves in them. And according to this we do not find a law of hagalah for knives unless the handle too is of the same material as the knife, for example if it is entirely metal. And this requires study: for in Pesachim folio 30 it says regarding their handles, [put them] in clay, and the iron in the fire; and the halacha is both this and that in boiling water; and Rashi explained: their handles in clay to protect them so that the fire not burn [them]; end quote. This implies that it deals with a wooden handle, and even so boiling water helps.

[9] <b>All rust, etc.</b> The Taz explained: specifically what is called Rost, and there is substance there, and when one scrapes from there there is like dust; but when there is blackness or redness and one cannot remove it entirely, even after scraping there is no substance, only appearance alone: one hagals it after scraping all that is possible; only that in any case one should libun that place for a better measure; end of his words in brief. And this requires study: for he afterward wrote that with libun one need not remove the rust; if so, why scraping? And it is possible that that is specifically when one libuns the entire vessel, which is not so when one libuns one place. But it is difficult: for even with actual rust, libun helps (in a place of libun) in one place, as is written in se'if 5; and what difference is there between [cases with] no substance? And one may say that the Taz said for a better measure to libun, and not by law. But this requires study: for Darkhei Moshe wrote on Yoreh De'ah siman 121, this is his language: and if one cannot remove the rust, Issur VeHeter wrote that even if only a little remains, it has no remedy by hagalah; end quote; and so wrote Maharil. And it is possible that he deals with rust that has substance; and in my book on Yoreh De'ah there I raised a difficulty from this against the Bach there and the Perishah in the name of Maharshal in a gloss, who permit; and they wondered from their own reasoning that it is fitting to forbid, and they did not mention the aforementioned poskim who forbade explicitly. However, Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 14 of the laws of forbidden foods wrote to permit, and to this Maharshal intended; and so it appears to me, for it deals with the sort of case the Taz deals with, where the rust has no substance; and with this everything fits, with Heaven's help.
