**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Taz s.k. 8.</b> And it is difficult to me: for that which we hold in siman 447, se'if 4, is not understandable; regarding nullification, what does it matter that its name remains upon it and it is called by the name of a prohibition? Nevertheless put it on the law that it is nullified like all other prohibitions. For even when it was mixed on eve of Pesach after midday, when certainly the name of a prohibition is upon it, it is nullified; and since it was nullified and its prohibition departed, it was permitted entirely. But regarding "it absorbed permitted [food]," one may say that since its name remains upon it, the name of a prohibition is upon it. And likewise regarding nat bar nat, where the Rashba holds that it is not called nat bar nat of permitted [food], for chametz still has its name upon it. And that is all that it is not called "permitted absorption," but it is not more stringent than the absorption of other prohibitions. Also what the Taz wrote, and further "I am puzzled by a frying pan": behold the Taz himself in se'if 16 wrote that there it is by means of liquid. Also what he wrote, "and one must say that this spit is dealing with," etc.: the difficulty from siman 452 is still not resolved, for regarding nat bar nat it is called "it absorbed permitted [food]," and if so, even if one roasted chametz in kind, hagalah should suffice. And all of this requires further study.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>There, in the same discussion.</b> And as is written in siman 94. The explanation of the Magen Avraham's words is that the distinction between that of siman 92 and that of siman 94 is: in siman 92 we say that it absorbed throughout, and in siman 94 we say that it does not expel its entirety. But in truth it would seem that the distinction is only that in siman 92 the whole pot is hot, whereas in siman 94 the spoon is not hot. And a proof for this: for it is stated there in siman 94, and some say if the spoon is of metal we estimate against its entirety, for if part of it is hot, its entirety is hot; and that is the view of Rabbeinu Peretz. Thus if it is hot throughout, one needs sixty against its entirety. But with a wooden spoon, and likewise for us with metal, that we need sixty only against what was stuck into the pot, because the spoon is cold: if so, there is no contradiction from that of siman 92; and it is clear.
