<b>של חרס שנשתמשו. </b> וכת' האו"ה בכלל כ"ח דין מ"ג דכלים חדשים שקונין מהשוק אפי' בכלי חרס אין צריך לספק בהן לשמא נשתמשו בו דאין דרך כלל להשתמש בכלים חדשים אבל כלי מתכות שנקנים מן העכו"ם בביתו ואינו מכיר בהן ודאי שהם חדשים צריכים מספק להגעילם או ללבנם כפי תשמישן:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Earthenware that were used.</b> The Issur VeHeter writes in klal 28, law 43, that new utensils bought in the market, even earthenware utensils, need not be treated doubtfully lest they were used, for it is not at all usual to use new utensils. But metal utensils bought from a non-Jew in his home, when one does not recognize with certainty that they are new, require, out of doubt, hagalah or libun according to the manner of their use.

<b>משפשן היטב. </b> ואם לא שפשפן קודם פסח משפשפן תוך הפסח או לאחר פסח כדין לא ביער קודם פסח דמבואר לעיל סי' תל"ה וה"ה בשאר כלים שאינם של חרס אם אינו רוצה להגעילן או ללבנם ג"כ אפשר בהאי תקנתא לשפשפן ולקיימו עד אחר פסח אלא שדבר בהוה דבשאר כלים כיון שיש להם תקנה אינו עושה כן רק מגעילן או מלבנן כאשר יתבאר לפנינו:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He scrubs them well.</b> If he did not scrub them before Pesach, he scrubs them during Pesach or after Pesach, like the law of one who did not remove chametz before Pesach, which was explained above in siman 435. The same applies to other utensils that are not earthenware: if he does not want to kasher them by hagalah or libun, he may also use this remedy, to scrub them and keep them until after Pesach. The text merely speaks in the usual case, since with other utensils, because they have a remedy, one does not usually do this, but rather performs hagalah or libun, as will be explained before us.

<b>ומצניען בפסח </b> כ' בתשובת מהר"מ מרוטנבורג סי' תמ"א מה שיסד הפייטן קדרות חרס בשברונ' ואחר כך והתירו במחיצה מעין לטמנם לא קשה כי כן דרך הפייטנים ליסד דברי רב ודברי שמואל עכ"ל ובאמת בפיוט שלנו לא מצאתי בן רק בפיוט שבמוסף שבת הגדול של בני אשכנז מצאתי כן ולפמ"ש לעיל בסי' תמ"ז ס"ק ד' בשם הרוקח אפשר ליישב פיוט זו בדרך אחר ועי"ל דה"ק קדרת חרס בשברונם פירוש אע"פ דאין להם תקנה להפליט שום דבר אלא שבירה מכל מקום התירו בפסח על ודי מחיצות לטמנם מעין וצ"ע בפירושיהם של פיוטיהם כי אין בידי לעיין בהם:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And puts them away on Pesach.</b> Maharam of Rothenburg wrote in a responsum, siman 441, that what the liturgical poet composed, regarding earthenware pots by breaking them, and afterward, that they permitted them with a partition in order to hide them away, is not difficult, for such is the way of poets, to arrange both the words of Rav and the words of Shmuel; end quote. In truth, in our piyyut I did not find this, but in the piyyut in the Musaf of Shabbos HaGadol of the communities of Ashkenaz I did find it. According to what I wrote above in siman 447, s.k. 4, in the name of the Rokeach, this piyyut may be resolved another way. One may also say that this is its meaning: earthenware pots by breaking them, meaning that although they have no remedy to expel anything except breaking, nevertheless on Pesach they permitted them by means of partitions, to hide them away. The explanations of their piyyutim require study, for I do not have them available to examine.

<b>כדי שלא יבא להשתמש בהם בפסח. </b> משמע דאף בכלים שאינם ב"י ג"כ חיישינן כן וק"ק דהא לדעת המחבר לעיל סי' תמ"ז סעיף יו"ד נטל"פ מותר בפסח וכבר נתבאר בדברי פוסקים כמה פעמים דכל מילי דמותר בדעבד לא גזרינן לכתחלה שמא ישכח וכ"ש באותן כלים דליכא רק חשש דחמץ משהו ושמא י"ל דחמץ כיון דלא בדילי מיני' כולי שתא גזרינן טפי ולדינא אין נפקותא כיון דקי"ל בהגהות הרב דלעיל סימן תמ"ז לאסור אף במשהו ונטל"פ בפסח:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>So that he not come to use them on Pesach.</b> This implies that even with utensils that are not ben yomo we are also concerned about this. This is somewhat difficult, for according to the Mechaber above, siman 447, seif 10, a spoiled taste is permitted on Pesach, and it has already been explained many times in the words of the poskim that whatever is permitted after the fact, we do not decree against ab initio lest he forget. All the more so with those utensils, where there is only a concern for a minute amount of chametz. Perhaps one may say that regarding chametz, since people are not separate from it all year, we decree more strictly. In practice there is no difference, since we rule in accordance with the glosses of the Rav above in siman 447, to forbid even a minute amount and even a spoiled taste on Pesach.

<b>ולא לשום כלי חרס שנשתמשו בו חמין. </b> כן הוא לשון הטור ופירש הב"י כוונתו אפי' לא נשתמש בו רק בכלי שני לא מהני בו היסק וכן הרמב"ם בפ"ה מה' חמץ השוה כלי חרס שנשתמש בו בכלי שני להשתמש בו בכלי ראשון שאין לו תקנה עכ"ל ואם כן המחבר נראה לי שהעתיק כאן כלשון הטור כוונתו לאסור אף בכלי שני כדעת הרב בהג"ה אך הר"ב בד"מ כ' דלדעת הטור אינו בולע אלא על ידי כלי ראשון או בעירוי אבל כלי שני לא דלא כב"י עכ"ל וכן הוצרך להגי' כאן על לשון המחבר שהוא כלשון הטור וי"א אפילו בכלי שני וק"ל אבל ודאי דעת המחבר עצמו ג"כ דבולע ונאסר וכן מבואר להדיא מדבריו בסמוך סעיף ה' ואם תשמישן בכלי שני הכשרן בכלי שני הרי מבואר מדבריו שיש בליעה על ידי כלי שני וכן לשון הטי"ד סי' קכ"א וכל זה פשוט דלא כמ"א ס"ק ג' שכתב שהב"י השמיט דין כלי שני אלמא הדר ביה ושא"צ להגעיל וזה אינו אלא ברור כמ"ש ומ"מ לדינא המעיין בהרי"ף והרמב"ם והרא"ש פ' כל שעה ושאר כל הפוסקים אינו מבואר כלל בדבריהם שנאסר הכלי על ידי כלי שני רק בקערות וכה"ג שתשמישן על ידי עירוי להצריך הגעלה על ידי עירוי ולזה קראוהו בדבריהם כלי שני כמ"ש המ"א וכן מבואר בכל בו להדיא בהל' פסח כ"פ האו"ה ועיין סי' תס"ז ס"ק ל"ג ועל פי זה יהיה מיושב היטב מה שהקשתי בספרי מנחת יעקב כלל ל"ג סק"ג: אכן בטי"ד סי' קך"א ובש"ל כלל ז' סי' נ"ח כ' בשם בעל הדיברות כמ"ש המחבר בסמוך סעיף ה' שאם תשמישן בכלי שני הכשרן בכלי שני וכלי שמשתמשין בו בעירוי וכו' משמע דמיירי אפי' מכלי שני וכמ"ש הש"כ בסי' ק"ה גם הט"ז מחמיר שם בכ"ש וכמ"ש גם כן בספרי מ"י כלל ל"ג וכן בספר פר"ח בי"ד סי' ס"ח הפליג להחמיר בכ"ש מ"מ במקים הפסד מרובה ומניעת שמחת י"ט בפרט בכלי שאינו ב"י נ"ל להקל בדעבד אם נשתמש בכלי שנתמש בכלי שני אף בכלי חרס כיון שהסכמת רוב ראשונים ואחרונים שכלי שני אינו מבליע כלל וכמבואר בב"י בי"ד סי' ס"ח ועיין בספרי מ"י כלל ל"ג ומ"ש שם וגם נ"ט לפגם רבים מקילין גבי חמץ:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Nor for any earthenware vessel that was used with hot food.</b> This is the wording of the Tur. The Beis Yosef explained that his intent is that even if it was used only as a second vessel, heating it does not help. Similarly, the Rambam in chapter 5 of the laws of chametz equated an earthenware vessel used as a second vessel with one used as a first vessel, that it has no remedy; end quote. If so, it appears to me that when the Mechaber copied here the wording of the Tur, his intent was to forbid even a second vessel, in accordance with the opinion of the Rav in the gloss. However, the Rav in Darkei Moshe wrote that according to the Tur it absorbs only through a first vessel or by pouring, but not through a second vessel, unlike the Beis Yosef; end quote. Therefore he needed to gloss here upon the wording of the Mechaber, which is the wording of the Tur, that some say even a second vessel. This is clear. But certainly the Mechaber himself also holds that it absorbs and becomes forbidden, as is explicit from his words nearby, seif 5: if their use is in a second vessel, their kashering is in a second vessel. It is explicit from his words that there is absorption through a second vessel, and so too is the wording of the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 121. All this is simple, not like the Magen Avraham, s.k. 3, who wrote that the Beis Yosef omitted the law of a second vessel, which shows that he retracted and that one need not perform hagalah. This is not so; rather, the matter is clear as I wrote. Nevertheless, in practice, one who examines the Rif, the Rambam, the Rosh in chapter Kol Shaah, and all the other poskim, will not find it at all explicit in their words that a vessel becomes forbidden through a second vessel, except for bowls and similar items whose use is by pouring, which require hagalah by pouring. This they called in their words a second vessel, as the Magen Avraham wrote. This is also explicit in Kol Bo in the laws of Pesach, and so the Issur VeHeter ruled. See siman 467, s.k. 33. On this basis what I asked in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, s.k. 3, is well resolved. However, in the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 121, and in Shibbolei HaLeket, klal 7, siman 58, they wrote in the name of Baal HaDibros as the Mechaber wrote nearby in seif 5, that if their use is in a second vessel, their kashering is in a second vessel, and a vessel used by pouring, etc. This implies that it speaks even of an actual second vessel, as the Shach wrote in siman 105. The Taz also rules stringently there regarding a second vessel, as I also wrote in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, and in Pri Chadash, Yoreh Deah siman 68, he was very stringent regarding a second vessel. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss and prevention of Yom Tov joy, particularly with a utensil that is not ben yomo, it appears to me to be lenient after the fact if one used a utensil that had been used as a second vessel, even if it was earthenware, since most early and later authorities agree that a second vessel does not cause absorption at all, as explained in Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah siman 68. See my Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, and what I wrote there. In addition, many are lenient regarding spoiled taste in the case of chametz.

חיישינן דלמא פקעי וכאשר כ' הב"י לתרץ תמיהתו בדברי הרשב"א אך צ"ע על הב"י בתמיהתו על הרשב"א הלא כדבריו צ"ל בסוגיא דש"ס שהיא כלשון הרשב"א וכה"ג צ"ל לדעת הטור בי"ד סי' קך"א וכאן וק"ל: אכן בשיבולי לקט כלל ז' סי' נ"ח ובס' תניא כתבו וז"ל ובוכיא והוא כעין מחבת דקה אסור למיפ' בה בפסחא מ"ט היסקו מבחוץ הוא. ואי מליא גומרי והסיקו מבפנים שרי כגון מחבת של חרס שדרך להסיקה מבפנים ואופין תחתיה עוגת כיון דהיסקה מבפנים ה"ל כעין תנור עכ"ל וכ"כ הכל בו דף נ' ע"ד וז"ל דלמא חייס עלייהו דלמא פקען ולא דמי לביב של חרס (וקרי לבוכיא שבש"ס ביב נמשך אחר לשון הרמב"ם פ"ה דין ך"ב שכ' כן ע"ש) שכתבנו שאם מלאו גחלים והיסקן שמשתמש בו מנה דהתם היסקן מבפנים ואלו היסקן מבחוץ אם מסיקו מבפנים לא מסיק בהו שפיר דחייס עלייהו דשכיחין דפקעי עכ"ל. הרי להדיא מבואר מדבריהם דגם בבוכיא לא מהני הסקה מבפנים אא"כ באותו בוכיא שדרכן להסיק מבפנים (ואף שלשון הכלבו אפשר לדחוק ולפרש קצת בדרך אחר מ"מ. לשון הש"ל ותניא א"א לפרש אלא כמ"ש) ונראה שנמשכו בזה אחר גירסות ונוסחת הרי"ף והרא"ש פ' כל שעה דגרסינן בש"ס ואי מלייא גומרי והיסקו מבפנים שפיר דמי ע"כ ולפי גרסא זה קשה לכאורה דהוא כפל לשון היינו מלייא גומרי היינו היסקו מבפנים לכן מפרשים דה"ק ואי מליא גומרי היינו באותו בוכיא שהסיקו מבפנים שפיר דמי ולפי דבריהם יתיישב היטב סוגיא דש"ס ואין צריכין לדחוק ולומר דבוכיא שאני כמ"ש הב"י. והארכתי בכל זה אף דלענין דינא אפשר דלא פליגי דאינהו לא מיירי מבוכיא שנעשה מלבנים ועפר דאפשר דכ"ע מודו שהנעשים מלבנים ועפר לא חיישינן דפקעי ופלוגתייהו הוא בפי' הבוכיא שבש"ס מ"מ הארכתי לבאר הדברים לפי שראיתי שהב"י טרח לדחוק ולתרץ דברי הרשב"א ואחריו נמשכו האחרונים להתקשות בדבריו ודברי הטי"ד ובאמת אין כאן אפילו קצת קושיא כי כל הדברים לקוחים מסוגיא דש"ס להדיא וצ"ע בדברי הרשב"א גופי' ובדברי טי"ד שם וכאן בטא"ח נראה כדעת המחבר והיה מפרש דברי הרא"ש כפשוטו ולא רצה לדקדק בלשון והיסיקו מבפנים וק"ל:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And if he filled it with coals from inside, it is permitted.</b> This is the reading in the Gemara before us, chapter Kol Shaah, Pesachim folio 30: if he filled it with coals, it is valid. Yet this requires some study there in the Gemara, for we read there regarding the statement that heating is effective for an oven: if so, why did Rav say that pots on Pesach must be broken, let one heat them? The Gemara answers: this oven is heated from inside, while these pots are heated from outside. And if you would say, let him heat them from inside, he will have pity on them because they may crack. Therefore this buchya is heated from outside and is forbidden, but if he filled it with coals it is valid; end quote. This is difficult, because once the Gemara first concludes regarding pots that since they are heated from the outside, even if he wants to heat them from inside it is not effective because he will have pity on them lest they crack, how does it conclude that with a buchya, whose heating is from outside, nevertheless, if he fills it with coals it is valid? One must say that a buchya is different, for we are not concerned lest it crack, as the Beis Yosef wrote to answer his difficulty with the Rashba. But the Beis Yosef himself requires study, for regarding his question against the Rashba, according to his own words this must also be said in the sugya of the Gemara, which is like the wording of the Rashba. Similarly, one must say this according to the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 121 and here. This is clear. However, Shibbolei HaLeket, klal 7, siman 58, and Sefer Tanya wrote as follows: a buchya, which is like a thin pan, is forbidden for baking on Pesach. What is the reason? Its heating is from the outside. But if he filled it with coals and heated it from inside, it is permitted, such as an earthenware pan whose way is to be heated from inside and under which cakes are baked, since its heating is from inside it is like an oven; end quote. So too the Kol Bo, folio 50b, wrote that he may have pity on them lest they crack, and it is not comparable to an earthenware pipe, which he calls the buchya of the Gemara by the term pipe, following the wording of the Rambam in chapter 5, law 22, who wrote so; see there. We wrote that if he filled it with coals and heated it, the utensil he uses is permitted, for there its heating is from inside, whereas these are heated from outside. If one heats them from inside, he does not heat them well, for he has pity on them, since they commonly crack; end quote. It is explicit from their words that even for a buchya, heating from inside is not effective unless it is the type of buchya whose way is to be heated from inside. Although the wording of the Kol Bo could perhaps be pressed and explained somewhat differently, the wording of Shibbolei HaLeket and Tanya cannot be explained except as I wrote. It appears that in this they followed the reading and text of the Rif and the Rosh in chapter Kol Shaah, where we read in the Gemara: if he filled it with coals and heated it from inside, it is valid. According to this reading, at first glance it is repetitive, for filling it with coals and heating it from inside are the same. Therefore they explain that this is what it means: if he filled it with coals, meaning in that buchya whose heating is from inside, it is valid. According to their words the sugya of the Gemara is very well resolved, and one need not press and say that a buchya is different, as the Beis Yosef wrote. I have written at length about all this, even though in terms of practical law perhaps they do not disagree, for they are not speaking of a buchya made of bricks and earth, where perhaps all agree that those made of bricks and earth are not subject to the concern that they may crack, and their dispute is only in explaining the buchya of the Gemara. Nevertheless, I have written at length to clarify the matter, because I saw that the Beis Yosef labored to press and resolve the words of the Rashba, and the later authorities after him were drawn into difficulties with his words and with the words of the Tur in Yoreh Deah. In truth there is not even a slight difficulty here, for all the matters are taken explicitly from the sugya of the Gemara. The words of the Rashba himself and the words of the Tur in Yoreh Deah require study; here in Tur Orach Chaim the view appears to be like that of the Mechaber, and he interpreted the words of the Rosh simply and did not wish to be exacting about the phrase, and heated it from inside. This is clear.

All this happened because when they copied the words of the Bach, they also copied what the Bach concluded: and so wrote the Issur VeHeter, klal 58, law 19. They understood that this referred to this law, and therefore took his words as practical halachah. In truth, I examined the Issur VeHeter there well and saw no proof from his words for this law. The Bach wrote, and so wrote the Issur VeHeter, only regarding the end of his wording, where he concluded that the fire burns everything, and what now remains in it will never discharge; this is what the Issur VeHeter wrote explicitly there, see there. If it was subjected to libun in this way, or if he removed the patches one time until after he performed hagalah, then afterward it is again permitted to perform hagalah in this way with the patch each time, since it is no longer called a case where the absorption of prohibition preceded the patch. All this is where the patch is well attached, in a manner that there is no crack or indentation between the body of the utensil and the patch. If not, even where the patch preceded the absorption, it requires libun, or removal of the patch, or widening of the crack so that one can clean it. This is simple, and so is the agreement of the later authorities.

<b>That hagalah is not effective for it.</b> Because its use is by fire. The Taz wrote that a Pesach peel, if one keeps it for the next year, needs no kashering at all for the next year, and one who is stringent to perform hagalah on it has not acted well, for this may lead people to err and perform hagalah on a chametz peel. Rather, one should clean it well; end quote. His words appear correct, unlike what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahari Bruna, that it requires hagalah. The Magen Avraham also wrote that it is not effective if he peels the peel and performs hagalah on it. <b>And if</b> one removed matzah with a peel that is used all year, if it is clean, peeling off the surface of the matzah suffices. If it is not clean, since cakes with fat are used on it all year, the whole matzah becomes forbidden. If they removed a leavened matzah with it and afterward removed other matzos with it, all require peeling. The work Ape Ravrevei wrote in Shaar 70 that if one removed a leavened matzah with a peel and afterward removed others with it, and the first ones became mixed with the later ones, they are nullified; end quote. This is a rejected reasoning, for he holds that absorbed prohibition does not leave a utensil without liquid, and the only prohibition is the first loaf, because there was a little substance present; if it became mixed, it is nullified. But we rule that a utensil discharges even without liquid; end quote, these are the words of the Magen Avraham. My brother-in-law, Rabbi Aharon, may his light shine, answered that Ape Ravrevei did not intend those matzos that were removed afterward, that the first ones removed immediately after the leavened matzah should be nullified, as the Magen Avraham understood. Rather, by first ones he meant those removed before the leavened matzah was removed, which then became mixed with what was removed afterward. He understood this well. Although it appears to me that the wording of Ape Ravrevei pressed the Magen Avraham in that direction, since he wrote that the first ones became mixed with the later ones, implying that the later ones were the majority of permitted food, as the Gemara in chapter Elu HaNisrafin infers regarding those sentenced to stoning among those sentenced to burning, nevertheless this is not such a decisive inference in the words of poskim as to reject their words on that basis. See further in the Magen Avraham, who wrote at length about this nullification. In my humble opinion, all these matters were said only according to the one who holds that dry matter mixed with dry matter is nullified one in two regarding Pesach. But according to what was explained above in siman 447, that dry with dry is not nullified on Pesach, and even from six hours onward it reawakens during Pesach so long as it was not mixed wet with wet, and it is forbidden to eat them during Pesach because the prohibition is present in them, as explained in the words of the Magen Avraham himself in siman 458; see there. If so, the law of nullification is not relevant here at all, except to feed them on erev Pesach to a child or the like. The Magen Avraham further concluded: it appears to me that all this speaks of matzah that became full chametz, but a folded or swollen matzah, since it itself is only a stringency, as written in siman 461, is sufficient to forbid the peel ab initio, but not to forbid the other matzos; end quote. I saw an even greater ruling by my grandfather, the gaon Rabbi Shimon, that he did not forbid the peel when he was asked about swollen or folded matzah. The reason, it appears to me, is that this is considered like after the fact, for in most bakings it is almost impossible that one of them not be forbidden because of swelling or folding, and each time one would need a new peel, and the oven too would be forbidden and would need heating again. In such cases the Torah has pity on the money of Israel. Moreover, the Kol Bo, brought in the Beis Yosef in this siman, ruled that if one placed dry matzah, even hot, on a chametz utensil, or even on hot chametz bread, it does not forbid any part of it at all, and he brought this as practical halachah. He wrote further there regarding a chametz peel: perhaps if he scours it very much and heats it inside the oven, it is permitted, for as it absorbed, so it discharges. Although we do not rule like him, nevertheless regarding swollen and folded matzah, which itself is only a stringency, let us not add to it, and we do not forbid the peel even ab initio. So practiced after him my father-in-law, the gaon Rabbi Wolf, may his light shine. Regarding the statement that one must buy a new one, the author of Imrei Noam wrote in a responsum, siman 5, of an incident where matzos were baked before Pesach with a peel and they were uncertain whether it was new or old, on which chametz had been used; all faces are equal that there is no concern of chametz prohibition in those matzos; end quote.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>And a winter-house oven.</b> The later authorities agree that after the fact the dish cooked inside it is permitted, and so it appears to me to be the main ruling, for it is no worse than two pots, which do not absorb from one another, as explained in the Taz, Yoreh Deah siman 92, s.k. 29. See my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 55, s.k. 22. Likewise, if it is a pressing circumstance and it is impossible in another way, it is treated like after the fact. Nevertheless, in any case one should clean the oven well on all sides, heat it well, and afterward coat with clay the place where the pot rests, to the thickness of a finger. But if one baked matzah inside it without an intervening barrier, one should forbid even after the fact. However, in the Levush it implies that one may permit through thorough heating even ab initio, since it was heated from inside. One need not heat the entire oven full of wood, only the place where he usually heats all year and the place where the pot rests, for as it absorbed, so it discharges. This is unlike an oven in which one bakes, where we require coals to pass over all of it, since its way is to heat all of it. The same applies if one bakes in this oven. This is clear. So too I concluded in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 60, that this appears to me to be the main ruling, and see what I wrote above in s.k. 9. Yet who would act lightly in a matter where the later authorities agreed to be stringent? My grandfather was also stringent in the matter, as my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Sefer Eshel Zahav. Therefore one should not be lenient if matzah was baked in it, in any case. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss and prevention of Yom Tov joy, one may permit after the fact, and in pressing circumstances even ab initio. Regarding the outside of the oven, one may permit simply by coating it with clay to the thickness of a finger.
