**** ENGLISH ****
(102) That they call cast iron: for sometimes one crumbles chametz bread upon it, and it is impossible that a mashehu crumb not remain in its holes; and also horseradish (called chrein) that one grated upon it all year and was cut with a chametz knife: the horseradish is a sharp thing and absorbs the taste of chametz, and afterward when one grates upon it, the taste of chametz is found in it. And in this even after the fact one should forbid if one used it on Pesach, even if one used it cold, for example if one crumbled matzah upon it [and all the more so horseradish, which is a sharp thing]; and even if one scraped it well first, because it is impossible that a crumb of chametz or a crumb of horseradish that absorbed from chametz not stick to it. But when one used it on eve of Pesach there are distinctions: if one used upon it only matzah on eve of Pesach, even if a crumb of chametz mixed into it, behold they were nullified in it in sixty, and it is permitted to eat it until nightfall [but when night arrives, the crumb reawakens and forbids in a mashehu, as is explained in siman 467]. And if one grated horseradish upon it on eve of Pesach, which is a sharp thing, one needs sixty against the entire cast-iron grater, which became entirely chametz through the absorption it absorbed from the horseradish grated upon it and cut with a chametz knife, as above. And if not its entirety, it is forbidden.

**** ENGLISH ****
(111) Hagalah: and even prior peeling with a craftsman's tool also does not help. And after the fact, if one removed a hot matzah from the oven with a chametz paddle: if the paddle was not clean, but had dirt of chametz in kind upon it, it forbids the underside of the matzah enough for a netilah, which is the width of a thumb (and according to our practice of baking thin matzos, its entirety is forbidden). And if one baked with this peel cakes of chametz with fat, the whole matzah is forbidden, for by means of the fat the chametz permeates throughout [and specifically during Pesach; but before Pesach until nightfall, that slight amount of substance on the peel is nullified in sixty]. But if one rinsed the peel beforehand and there is no chametz in kind upon it, only what is absorbed in it: it forbids only enough for a peeling, whether one previously used it with fat or without fat; for a prohibition absorbed in a vessel cannot forbid more than enough for a peeling. If one removed a leavened matzah with a Pesach peel and afterward removed other matzos with it and they were forbidden through the peel, and afterward these were mixed with many other kosher ones: all are forbidden; for it has already been explained in siman 447 that on Pesach dry with dry is not nullified; and even if mixed on eve of Pesach, nevertheless it reawakens during Pesach and it is forbidden to eat them on Pesach [and only on eve of Pesach is it permitted to feed them to a child]. And some say that since it was forbidden only from the absorbed side, it does not reawaken on Pesach. And all this is regarding full chametz; but regarding a paddle with which one removed a doubled or swollen matzah: it is enough to forbid the paddle l'chatchila, but after the fact it does not forbid. And more than this Chok Yaakov wrote: that if one cannot easily find another paddle, it is permitted to remove the matzos with it l'chatchila; for if so one would always need a new paddle, and it is like after the fact. And if they were in doubt about a paddle whether it was of chametz or new: all the matzos are permitted.

If both are cold, regular use is forbidden, while occasional use is permitted after sweeping and cleaning the oven surface so that no actual chametz remains, as stated above at the end of seif 1, seif katan 16. After the fact, if matzah was placed there while the oven was hot, while the matzah was hot, or while both were hot, the underside of the matzah is forbidden to the depth of peeling. This applies when the matzah was placed directly upon the oven. If pots containing food were placed there, even when both surfaces were hot, the food is not forbidden after the fact, because absorbed prohibition does not pass from the oven wall into the wall of the pot. Initially, however, pots may not be placed there under any circumstances unless both are cold. If an intervening sheet of metal or similar object is placed beneath, or the surface is coated with clay one fingerbreadth thick, everything may be used above it. These laws also apply to the tiles built into the sides of ovens, except that there an iron barrier or clay coating only beneath does not suffice unless clay is also applied to the sides and above. The upper clay coating does not prevent permission after the fact. When the pots placed there are covered, initial use is permitted even without clay above. Regarding the inside of an oven that was not kashered, if matzah was placed there, its law is the same as upon the oven, as explained. Baking matzos there is governed by siman 461. Cooking there initially requires kashering as for baking, or coating the floor with clay one fingerbreadth thick. After the fact, however, the cooked food is not forbidden even if the oven was not kashered.
