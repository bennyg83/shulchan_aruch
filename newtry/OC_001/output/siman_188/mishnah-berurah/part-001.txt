**** ENGLISH ****
(2) And it seems specifically etc.—and some reconcile the custom for another reason: they were not concerned about treating the fourth berachah lightly except in their days when the workers were going to their melacha when they hear Amen after Boneh Yerushalayim (for the Sages did not obligate them in the fourth berachah because of the loss of the employer's melacha)—therefore he had to answer Amen quietly so the workers would not hear and recognize that the fourth berachah is not from the Torah and come to treat it lightly even when not in a place of loss of the employer's melacha; but now that the workers also bless four berachot as written in siman 191 there is no concern for this; and per this reason whether when blessing with zimun or when blessing alone he may answer Amen in a loud voice, and it seems the custom is so. He should not say Boneh Yerushalayim Amen in one breath—only wait a bit after the word Yerushalayim and then say Amen so it does not imply that Amen is also from the conclusion of the berachah.

(19) L'sason ul'simcha—and on Rosh Hashanah one does not say l'sason ul'simcha; also one does not say uDevarekha Malkeinu etc.; and this is its formula: Baruch Atah Heaven Elokeinu Melech HaOlam asher natan yamim tovim l'amo Yisrael et yom hazikaron hazeh, and he does not conclude in it as on Rosh Chodesh—and this is Magen Avraham's opinion who disputes Kol Bo that compares Rosh Hashanah to other Yom Tov; but many Acharonim agreed with Kol Bo—and therefore one must conclude Baruch Atah Heaven mekadesh Yisrael veyom hazikaron; and if he forgot and began haTov vehaMetiv—also Magen Avraham's opinion is he again does not return as on Rosh Chodesh, and Ateret Zekenim disputes him and holds he returns. (33) After the beginning of the meal—and therefore even if Rosh Chodesh fell on Maariv he mentions in birkat hamazon that of Shabbat alone and not that of Rosh Chodesh—and specifically when he finished his meal while still day; but if he ate bread also at night and has upon him obligation to mention also that of Rosh Chodesh—and both cannot be mentioned for it is two that contradict—how will he say on this Shabbat day and afterward say on this Rosh Chodesh day—for Rosh Chodesh is on one day?
