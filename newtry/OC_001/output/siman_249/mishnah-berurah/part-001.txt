(א) יותר מג' פרסאות - מתחלת היום והוא קרוב לשליש היום כפי מהלך אדם בינוני י' פרסאות ביום ובנוסע בעגלה או רוכב על סוס דנוסע במהרה יוכל ליסע הרבה יותר מג' פרסאות עד שליש היום ומהב"ח משמע להקל בנוסע בעגלה ליסע אפילו אחר חצות היום ובלבד שיעמוד לשבות בעוד היום גדול בכדי שיוכל להכין צרכי שבת:
**** ENGLISH ****
(1) More than three parasangs: measured from the beginning of the day. This is approximately one third of the day, since an average person walks ten parasangs in a day. One traveling by wagon or riding a horse, who moves more quickly, may travel much more than three parasangs until one third of the day. Bach appears to permit one traveling by wagon to continue even after midday, provided that he stops for Shabbos while much of the day remains, enough to prepare Shabbos needs.

(ג) שהולך לביתו - ואף שהוא מכוין שבביתו יהיה לו יותר עונג שבת מ"מ אסור שמא לא ידעו כלל מביאתו ולא הכינו בשבילו. ובמדינות אלו רוב בני אדם מכינים צרכי שבת בריוח ולכן אין נזהרין בזה כלל בין כשהולך לביתו או להתארח בבית אחרים [ממ"א בשם אגודה] ובהרבה אחרונים ראיתי שכתבו דמ"מ צריך ליזהר לכתחלה שלא ילך או יסע עד סמוך לערב מפני שכמה פעמים נכשלים עי"ז ובאים לידי חילול שבת כי בעל אושפיזא או אפילו בביתו כשבא סמוך לשבת מוסיפין לבשל בשבילו ומחללין שבת וגם כמה פעמים יארע דלא יגיע למלון ולביתו מבעוד יום עד שחשכה ממש וכמה חילול שבת יש בהוצאה והכנסה ויציאה מחוץ לתחום ושביתת בהמתו ולכן כל זה ישים האדם ללבו וימהר לשבות אפילו בכפר ולא יסיתנו היצר לומר עוד היום גדול והדרך טוב אך אם אירע שהלך עד חשיכה והיה בבה"ש בתוך תחום עיר מותר להלוך בתוכה כבסימן תי"ו וצריך לירד מעגלה וסוס עיין סימן רס"ו ושם יבואר אי"ה:
**** ENGLISH ****
(3) When he is traveling home: Even if he intends to enjoy Shabbos more at home, it is forbidden, lest they not know of his arrival and have prepared nothing for him. In these countries most people prepare Shabbos provisions generously, and therefore people are generally not careful about this, whether traveling home or staying as a guest. Magen Avraham in the name of Agudah. Nevertheless, many later authorities write that one should initially take care not to walk or travel until close to evening, since this frequently leads to Shabbos desecration. A host, or even members of his own household, may add cooking for him when he arrives close to Shabbos. He may also fail to reach his lodging or home before nightfall, resulting in carrying, entering or leaving Shabbos boundary, and violating the obligation that his animal rest. A person should therefore take all this to heart and hasten to stop even in a village. He should not allow the evil inclination to tell him that much of the day remains and the road is good. If he nevertheless traveled until nightfall and was within a city’s boundary during twilight, he may walk throughout the city, as stated in siman 416. He must descend from the wagon or horse; see siman 266, where this will be explained, with God’s help.

**** ENGLISH ****
(9) An engagement feast: If he became engaged previously, he may not hold the feast on Friday. Although an engagement feast is a mitzvah feast, he should have held it earlier. If he became engaged on Friday, however, he may hold the feast, since becoming engaged on Friday is permitted lest another precede him; the feast is consequently permitted as well and is considered like a circumcision feast or redemption feast whose time is fixed. Likewise, if the wedding took place on Friday, the feast may be held. Nevertheless, initially, where possible, it is proper to postpone the feast until the next day or another day, even if the engagement occurred on Friday. All this concerns an engagement feast. A feast held for our customary matchmaking agreement is not a mitzvah feast, as Peri Megadim writes in siman 444. It should therefore not be held on Friday even if the match was concluded that day. Eating sweets when the conditions are written is not considered a meal.

(22) In an individual fast, etc. — an individual fast is called one who accepted upon himself to fast on this erev Shabbat, or whose custom is to fast every erev Shabbat, or erev Rosh Chodesh falling on erev Shabbat [meaning those people who do not observe Yom Kippur Katan on the Thursday before it, for those who observe it also fast on Thursday — Rama in siman 418]; or who fasts on the day his father and mother died, or the 20th of Sivan, or who fasts the fasts explained in siman 580. Magen Avraham wrote that if one fasts every erev Shabbat and completed the first time, he must conduct thus always, and the same applies to all the above, for presumably all this is without explicit condition that he does not intend always to complete — we say his intention is to conduct thus always. And l'chatchila it is better not to complete the first time when it falls on erev Shabbat, as Rama wrote, so he need not always complete. And if at first when he began to fast — a day his father and mother died, or erev Rosh Chodesh and the other fasts above falling on a weekday — and on weekdays they do not complete, therefore some poskim hold that even when it later occurs on erev Shabbat he must also complete until nightfall, for presumably his intention is always to conduct thus, unless he explicitly conditioned that he does not intend to conduct thus always; and some dispute this and hold that presumably he did not accept upon himself to complete even on erev Shabbat, and therefore one who suffers may rely on those who are lenient and eat immediately after leaving the beit knesset even though the day is still great:

(23) Until nightfall — and even the above view agrees to this, for behold even on Shabbat itself one can begin and fast a dream fast, as below in siman 288, and certainly he completes until night. And behold some poskim dispute and hold that even for a dream fast it suffices to wait until they leave the beit knesset for maariv, for this is called completion; but the later authorities agreed with Shulchan Aruch not to be lenient in this, for since it is because of danger — to nullify and regret a bad dream — perhaps it will not help to nullify it if he does not complete until night:
