**** ENGLISH ****
The Magen Avraham states that an engagement feast may not be held during Chol HaMoed, as set forth in siman 546. People generally understand him to mean that one should not say that an engagement feast is also forbidden on Pesach eve and that the case in siman 444, of one traveling to eat an engagement feast, means that he travels on Pesach eve to eat such a feast during Chol HaMoed because his father-in-law lives far away and he must depart early. They therefore objected that one could still say that the feast itself is forbidden on Pesach eve, but that he must leave then in order to attend an engagement feast after Pesach because of the great distance to his father-in-law’s home. In my humble opinion, this is not the Magen Avraham’s intent. Certainly one may not rely upon nullification, even where he cannot return, unless the engagement feast itself is taking place that day. Only then is the mitzvah of removing chametz deferred before the mitzvah of the engagement feast, since both obligations rest upon him that day. But in order to eat an engagement feast during Chol HaMoed, he may not nullify the mitzvah of removal, whose time is today, on account of an engagement feast that is not incumbent upon him today. I later found this stated plainly in Olas Shabbos. Rather, the Magen Avraham’s intent is that the Mishnah in Pesachim 49a states: “One who travels to slaughter his Paschal offering or to eat an engagement feast and remembers that he has chametz,” and so forth. One might have said that an engagement feast is forbidden on Pesach eve and that the Mishnah concerns a feast during Chol HaMoed, meaning that on the very day of his travel he will eat the engagement feast during Chol HaMoed. One cannot object that the Mishnah then says, “If not, he should nullify it,” although after the time when chametz becomes forbidden it is no longer in his possession and cannot be nullified. This may be answered as the Gemara answers in Pesachim 7a: the case concerns rolled dough in his home that he fears may become leavened, and he nullifies it before it leavens. Alternatively, the Mishnah may follow Rabbi Yose HaGelili, who holds that chametz remains permitted for benefit, so that even after the time of prohibition he can nullify it because according to him it remains in his possession. Why the Gemara on 7a did not offer this answer is addressed in my novellae to Pesachim. The Magen Avraham therefore writes that an engagement feast may not be held during Chol HaMoed. This also removes the popular objection that perhaps the person is traveling to eat an engagement feast after Pesach. For either way, if he travels on Pesach eve, he may not rely on nullification when the time of the feast’s mitzvah is not that day, as explained above; and if he travels after Pesach, what benefit would nullification provide, since his only obligation is to remove the chametz? One should not object that there was no need to suggest that he travels during Chol HaMoed, since below in siman 444 the text expressly says, “One who travels on the fourteenth.” The Magen Avraham intends to challenge the Jerusalem Talmud as well, from which the Bach’s ruling derives, as he writes below: “It is an explicit Mishnah,” meaning: how could the Jerusalem Talmud disagree with the Mishnah? Had it been possible to answer that the Mishnah concerns one traveling during Chol HaMoed, then although our version states “one who travels on the fourteenth,” the Mishnah itself does not, and the Magen Avraham’s challenge to the Jerusalem Talmud would at least have been resolved. All the more so is Friday more lenient than Pesach eve. At the beginning of the final chapter of Pesachim, the Mishnah states that close to Minchah on Pesach eve one may not eat until dark. The Gemara asks why this is stated specifically regarding Pesach eve, since the same applies on Friday and on the eve of a Festival. It answers that the ruling is needed according to Rabbi Yose, who permits eating on Friday but agrees that it is forbidden on Pesach eve. Alternatively, even according to Rabbi Yehudah, who also forbids it on Friday, his prohibition begins only from Minchah onward, whereas on Pesach eve it begins shortly before Minchah. According to either answer, Pesach eve is stricter than Friday regarding a meal. Therefore, since an engagement feast is permitted on Pesach eve, it is certainly permitted on Friday.
