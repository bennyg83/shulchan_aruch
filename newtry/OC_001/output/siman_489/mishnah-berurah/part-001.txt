(32) He does not go back and bless: The later authorities wondered about this law. Granted, in the first clause, that he concluded with five and it is five, it is fine that he fulfilled, for he counted properly; even though at the time of his blessing he thought it was four, that is nothing, for even without blessing at all he would fulfill. But in the latter clause, what does it help us that at the time of the blessing he knew which day it is; in any case with his mouth he counted a day that is not, and they agreed that in this he must count anew and bless, for his first blessing was in vain since after it he concluded a different day, unless he remembered within the span of speech of his counting, for then he goes back and counts properly and the blessing counts for him. And know further that although in Shulchan Aruch it speaks where at the time of the blessing he knew the day of the counting and blessed with intent to count properly, only that afterward he erred and concluded improperly; nevertheless several later authorities agreed that even if at the time of the blessing his intent was also on a different day and he likewise counted in error, nevertheless if within the span of speech he remembered that he erred, he should immediately conclude properly and fulfills with this. They further wrote that if he erred and said "today is the fourth day of the omer," and within the span of speech he remembered that it is the fifth day, it is enough that he conclude "fifth of the omer" and he fulfills with this, even though he did not say "today is the fifth day," since he is still within the span of speech.

(45) Even nowadays: from what is written, "And bread and roasted grain and fresh ears you shall not eat until this very day," etc., "in all your dwellings," it implies from this that even outside the Land the prohibition applies until this very day, namely the day of bringing the offering, which is the sixteenth of Nisan; and because of doubt of the day we are additionally stringent one more day. Now in Yoreh De'ah, siman 293, the Rama wrote a leniency for new grain, for it is a double doubt: one, perhaps the produce is from the previous year; and even if you say it is from this year, perhaps it took root before the omer. And the same applies if it is a place that brings produce from other places whose produce takes root before the omer. And the later authorities wrote that in the land of Poland one should not be lenient except with wheat and rye, for the vast majority are sown in the month of Cheshvan and new grain does not apply to them [unless those known to have been sown in summer]; but barley, oats, and spelt, most and almost all are sown after Pesach, and it is also not usual to bring produce there from other lands, so one should not be lenient with them. Nevertheless, most of the world are not careful at all about the prohibition of new grain, and some found a justification for them, because it is a hard thing to be careful about this, and therefore under pressure they rely on some of the early authorities who hold that new grain outside the Land is only rabbinic, which they decreed because of the Land of Israel, and they decreed only in places adjacent to the Land of Israel, such as Egypt and Babylonia. And some found a justification for them who hold that new grain applies only to produce of a Jew, but not to that of a non-Jew; and according to this one must warn Jews who have produce sown in their own fields to practice the prohibition of new grain with it. Now although we do not have the power to protest against those who are lenient, nevertheless every conscientious person should not rely on these leniencies and should be stringent for himself in everything he can, for according to many great early authorities it is a Torah prohibition in all cases. And know further that although we wrote above in the name of the later authorities that regarding wheat one need not be concerned at all that it is new grain, because the majority were certainly sown in the month of Cheshvan, nowadays when the way is to bring by railroad wheat flour from distant places, and it is known that in the interior of Russia there are many places where wheat is sown in summer, and new grain is found there almost more than the old: if he knows that the flour came from there, he must be careful about this in the winter months, for then flours are already made from new produce. And regarding beer and the rest of the laws of new grain, see Yoreh De'ah, siman 293, and the later authorities there.
