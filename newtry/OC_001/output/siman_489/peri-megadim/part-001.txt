<b>And a mitzvah.</b> See Taz; Menachos 65b; see Magen Avraham; and God willing there it will be explained. See Eliyah Rabbah [1]. And one may say: "and you shall count" implies all Israel; and "for you," that one who is not able [to count himself] by hearing from the chazzan; which is not so for Yovel, where it is written "and you shall count for yourself" to the court. Also regarding Yovel we hold that even the court has no mitzvah to count. Peri Chadash, se'if 8, brought a question: one who forgot one day to count, may he be chazzan and count with a blessing to discharge others, and he too fulfills? He concluded that he cannot, for he is not obligated in the matter; and proof from Megillah: a city person does not discharge a metropolis person; and so he wrote in siman 688 and 689; see there. But he may hear the blessing from another and intend to fulfill through his blessing; see there. Now this is certainly so: although it is a matter of mitzvah, whoever is not obligated in that mitzvah does not discharge others of their obligation; and one should not say from the reason of arevus he discharges; and specifically a deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor: arevus does not apply to them. For in siman 589, se'if 2, a deaf person who speaks and does not hear, although it is a matter of mitzvah, does not discharge others with shofar of their obligation; see Beis Yosef there; and see Rosh Hashana 29a. And see the Rosh on Berachos 20b, who asked: if women's Birkas HaMazon is rabbinic, she does not discharge men of their obligation; and what differs from one who ate an olive-bulk who discharges others? And he answered: there it is from the reason of arevus, and a woman is not included in arevus. And resolve: even if a woman is included in arevus, she is not obligated in Birkas HaMazon of the Torah, therefore arevus does not apply to her. And one may say that here too he says arevus does not apply to her, for she is not obligated in this mitzvah. And indeed women regarding kiddush of the day according to the Torah discharge men with kiddush, for arevus applies; and Parashas Nitzavim, see Rashi there. And so too a man for a woman: even though he has fulfilled, he discharges, for they are included in arevus; and see what I wrote in siman 271, se'if 2, and 273, and in other places we wrote about this. And in the general introduction: also an adult for a minor who is of education age, one may say that if he has fulfilled he does not discharge them, for arevus does not apply; and kiddush and Birkas HaMazon are different, for the Sages enacted to discharge his sons and household even though he has fulfilled, for they rely on him, or he is called obligated since upon him is to educate them. And so too a woman in such a case, who is not obligated to educate her son; see siman 343, Magen Avraham [1].

<b>However.</b> One may say it is not similar to a city person who does not discharge a metropolis person, who is called not obligated; which is not so for this one, who was obligated but did not count on the first night: nevertheless he is called obligated in the mitzvah. Also it is a double doubt: perhaps the law is that if he skipped the first night he counts and is obligated; and even if you say he is not obligated, perhaps the law follows Rashi that a city person discharges a metropolis person; and see Magen Avraham, siman 493, letter vav, that the Tur in his hints took as main the first answer of the Rosh, and like Rashi of blessed memory that a city person discharges a metropolis person; and with a double doubt he blesses, as is fitting, as Peri Chadash himself wrote here: if it is doubtful whether he skipped, he counts with a blessing. And one may say there that it is a dispute of the authorities from one source; and also perhaps Rashi would agree that he does not discharge, and it was a leniency they were lenient in the villages; see there. And see Seven Views of the Rashba of blessed memory, Yevamos 14a. Now certainly if there is someone from whom to hear the blessing, he should certainly do as Peri Chadash wrote. And if not, etc., and he has a minor son who has reached education age and can bless, etc., see siman 186 in Magen Avraham: whether one rabbinic [obligation] discharges another rabbinic. And if his father recites to his minor son who has reached education age word by word, in such a case one may say it is fine, like a minor and a woman who recite to an adult word by word, and the son.

<b>One week.</b> See Taz, Parashas Vayeitzei; and in masculine count the letter hei is a mnemonic of inverted other words; see Beis Shmuel, Even HaEzer 126, letter 9. Therefore "seven weeks" (Devarim 16) is masculine; which is not so for "seven complete sabbaths" (Vayikra 23): Shabbos is feminine language, like "those who desecrate it," etc., "it is holy" (Shemos 31); and "sabbaths" refers there to the day of Shabbos, calling the week thus. And that which is written "one who does," etc., melachah: see Tosafos Kesubos 5a; and siman 292 [2] in Taz. And one may say it refers to "oyam" (Shemos 31); and see Rashi on the Chumash: "a week of this," and it refers to her; and so wrote Rashbam of blessed memory and Ibn Ezra and in the Targumim. Nevertheless "week" with a sheva mobile, not "week" with a kamatz; and he said two things; see there: namely, a week of this, and also the word "week" here means sevenths of days, etc. And I built per the rule of the letter hei in masculine count: a kamatz is a mnemonic, for the hei does not rest in the middle, etc.: "eight" for masculine, the nun with a kamatz; and for feminine, the nun with a segol; "eight" amos. And in other matters the opposite: "sister fair" with a kamatz, "a fair man" with a segol. And what the Mechaber wrote "four days": see Even HaEzer until ten days, and from there onward "day," in siman 126; and see Eliyah Rabbah [6]; and so is the custom to say "eleven days."

<b>עד</b> עט״ז עב״י ור״ן וחיסור לשון יש כאן וצ״ל רוב המפרשים כתבו דבזה״ז דרבנן הלכך יותר טוב לספור ביה״ש שיהא תמימות ודאי וספק דרבנן לקולא מה שאין כן בזמן המקדש היה צריך צ״ה ספק תורה וכתב הר״ן דאין נכון לעשות ספק דרבנן לכתחלה (ועסי׳ תס״ו במ״א ב׳) ואי תמימות אין להחמיר בזה״ז יותר מד״ת דבהכרח מונה בצ״ה. ועתוס׳ מנחות ס״ז א׳ ד״ה זכר ובתוס׳ כריתות י״ז ב׳ ד״ה מדסיפא דביה״ש אתחזק מקרי קצת ובלח״מ פ״ח משגגות ה״ב. ויש לחלק ביניהם ולהלכה לכתחלה לא יספור עד צ״ה ואם סופר ביה״ש יספור אח״כ בלא ברכה עכ״מ פ״ז ממצה ה״ח בד׳ כוסות ל״ש ספק דרבנן הואיל ולא טורח ה״ה כאן ובר״פ נישיילי׳ כו׳ ואי״ה במ״א ו׳ ז׳ אבאר עוד. ובערב שבת בציבור המאחרין יותר טוב ולא כאותן המקדימים ואומרים כבר לילה ורגע מקודם אומרים יום גדול לקבל שבת וקורא אני שמים חשך לאור ואותו האור במעט רגע לחשך ע״כ ראוי למנוע זה וישראל קדושים שומעים ומקבלים תוכחה ומוסר ע״כ לקבלת שבת מקדימין ולספירה מאחרין:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Until.</b> See Taz; see Beis Yosef and Ran. And there is missing language here, and it should read: most commentators wrote that nowadays it is rabbinic; therefore it is better to count at twilight so that completeness be certain, and a rabbinic doubt is to leniency; which was not so in Temple times, when one needed the appearance of the stars for a Torah doubt. And the Ran wrote that it is not proper to create a rabbinic doubt l'chatchila (and see siman 466 in Magen Avraham [2]). And if "complete," one should not be more stringent nowadays than according to the Torah, where of necessity one counts at the appearance of the stars. And see Tosafos Menachos 66a, s.v. And on erev Shabbos in a congregation that delays, it is better, and not like those who advance and say it is already night, and a moment earlier they say it is a long day to accept Shabbos; and I call upon them "darkness for light," and that light in a small moment becomes darkness. Therefore it is proper to refrain from this; and holy Israel hear and accept rebuke and discipline. Therefore for Kabbalas Shabbos they advance, and for counting they delay.

<b>מונה</b> עט״ז ועמ״א ז׳. והנה סובר הט״ז דאבודרהם כתב די״ל אם מונה בלא ברכה יצא מ״ה ומדרבנן לא יצא בספירה אא״כ בירך תחלה שכך שקנו לברך על מצות וא״ש דקורא עם הציבור ביה״ש בלא ברכה ושמא ישכח בלילה יצא עכ״פ מ״ה ואם יזכור יספור בלילה בברכה ולמ״ש אח״כ דלהרמב״ם יצא אף מדרבנן בלא ברכה וכן הלכה בכל המצות דברכות אין מעכבות בהכרח שסופר בציבור ומתכוין שלא לצאת כלל בספירה זו ומה תועלת יש בספירה עם הציבור ולא ניחא ליה שמונה על תנאי אם אזכיר לא יהיה זו למצוה כו׳ דכה״ג סובר דאפשר דלא יצא כלל ועמ״א ומ״ש הט״ז דהציבור קורין בספק חשיכה ביה״ש עיין אות ה׳ ולגבייהו דיעבד הוי משום עמי הארץ דיש בבית הכנסת ג״כ וישכחו בלילה לספור אבל בלא״ה אין לעשות ספק דרבנן לקולא כמ״ש באות ה׳ ואי״ה במ״א ז׳ יבואר עוד בזה יע״ש:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He counts.</b> See Taz; and see Magen Avraham [7]. Now the Taz holds that Avudraham wrote that one may say: if he counts without a blessing he has fulfilled according to the Torah, and therefore from the rabbis he has not fulfilled in counting unless he blessed first, for so they enacted to bless over mitzvos. And it fits that he reads with the congregation at twilight without a blessing, lest he forget at night: at least he has fulfilled according to the Torah; and if he remembers he counts at night with a blessing. And according to what he wrote afterward, that according to the Rambam he has fulfilled even rabbinically without a blessing, and so is the law in all mitzvos that blessings do not impede: necessarily he counts in the congregation and intends not to fulfill at all with this counting; and what benefit is there in counting with the congregation? And it does not please him that he counts conditionally: if I remember, this will not be for the mitzvah, etc.; for in such a case he holds it is possible he has not fulfilled at all; and see Magen Avraham. And what the Taz wrote, that the congregation reads at doubtful darkness at twilight: see letter 5. And regarding them after the fact it is because of the unlearned who are also in the synagogue and may forget at night to count; but otherwise one should not create a rabbinic doubt for leniency, as I wrote in letter 5. And God willing in Magen Avraham [7] this will be further explained; see there.

<b>שאם</b> עט״ז הנה המחבר בסי׳ סמ״ך ס״ד פסק דמצות צריכות כוונה בד״ת ודרבנן י״ל דא״צ כוונה עמ״א שם ג׳ וספירה בזה״ז הוי דרבנן לרוב פוסקים עיין אות ט׳ א״כ י״ל דא״צ כוונה וז״ש הר״ב בס״ג אם היה דעתו שלא לצאת הא בסתם יצא דא״צ כוונה בדרבנן מ״מ זה שמשיב היום כך וכך מסתמא רק להשיב לשואלו דבר אבל כוונתו שלא לצאת ומ״מ מה שמביא ראיה מברכת המזון בסי׳ רפ״ד קשה דהוי ד״ת ומלולב בתרנ״ח שפיר דהוי בשאר הימים דרבנן ועא״ר כיון שאין הגדול אוחז הלולב יע״ש ועמ״א אות ח׳ וט׳ ומשמע להלכה דיעבד אם אמר היום כך וכך סופר אח״כ בברכה דסתמא כפירושו שמכוין שלא לצאת ועפר״ח ועח״י ט״ו דל׳ המחבר אינו יכול. לחזור ולמנות בברכה משמע דאסור לברך וכמ״ש המ״א באות ה׳ דאף דהלכה דמצות צריכות כוונה ברכה ספיקו לקולא וחומר לא תשא כבסי׳ רט״ז יע״ש. ואם אמר הימים ולא השבועות לכאורה ס״ס לברך שמא מצות צריכות כוונה ושמא לא יצא בימים לחוד עמ״א ד׳ ופר״ח כאן וצ״ע:
**** ENGLISH ****
<b>For if.</b> See Taz. Now the Mechaber in siman 60, se'if 4, ruled that mitzvos require intent according to the Torah; and regarding rabbinic, one may say intent is not required; see Magen Avraham there [3]. And counting nowadays is rabbinic according to most poskim; see letter 9; if so, one may say intent is not required. And this is what the Rama wrote in se'if 3: "if his intent was not to fulfill," implying that with unspecified [intent] he has fulfilled, for intent is not required for rabbinic. Nevertheless, this one who answers "today such-and-such" presumably only answers the asker something, but his intent is not to fulfill. Nevertheless, the proof he brings from Birkas HaMazon in siman 284 is difficult, for that is according to the Torah; and from lulav in siman 658 it is fine, for on the other days it is rabbinic. And see Eliyah Rabbah: since the adult does not hold the lulav; see there. And see Magen Avraham, letters 8 and 9. And it implies for law that after the fact, if he said "today such-and-such," he afterward counts with a blessing, for unspecified is as his interpretation that he intends not to fulfill. And see Peri Chadash and Chok Yaakov [15]: the Mechaber's language "he cannot go back and count with a blessing" implies that it is forbidden to bless, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 5: even though the law is that mitzvos require intent, a doubtful blessing is to leniency, and the stringency of not taking [Hashem's Name in vain], as in siman 216; see there. And if he said the days and not the weeks, ostensibly there is a double doubt to bless: perhaps mitzvos require intent, and perhaps he has not fulfilled with days alone; see Magen Avraham [4] and Peri Chadash here; and this requires further study.
