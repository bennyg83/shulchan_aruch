It seems to me that there a reason is needed that it is according to the Torah because the Gemara was in doubt, perhaps we follow the conclusion. But here we certainly follow the opening, for if he began with Atah Chonen he completes that blessing.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>[Levush] Even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc.</b> The Magen Avraham objected, for we hold in siman 209 that a blessing is not ruined merely by lack of intent, and therefore the Rav Beit Yosef omitted what is cited in Terumas HaDeshen, even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc., end quote. In my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for in siman 209 we do not approach the matter from that reason, but because perhaps we follow the conclusion, as I explained. So too in Sefer Mishpat Tzedek he wrote in the name of the Shulchan Aruch that even in Atah Kidashta the law is this way, and so is the primary ruling.

<b>[Levush] The beginning of the Shabbos prayer, etc.</b> It seems that nevertheless he does not complete either Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad, for which of them should he complete? In my humble opinion this is not clear; rather, he must stop and begin the appropriate blessing. In seif 15 it says only that he does not go back, meaning if he already completed the blessing. But as long as he has not completed it, he stops. So too it is proven in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 14, in the question there; see there. This also appears from the Magen Avraham.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>He mentioned Shabbos, etc.</b> This requires study, for in siman 487, if on Yom Tov he concluded 'Who sanctifies Shabbos,' he has not fulfilled his obligation, even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle. One must say that it is worse when he concluded with the formula of another day, and this requires study. The Rosh and Rambam imply that he fulfilled the obligation specifically in Mussaf (Magen Avraham). But later he wrote that it refers to where he also did not mention Yom Tov in the middle, and his words contradict one another. Also what he wrote, 'and in the Rosh and Rambam,' etc., is not so, for the Rambam in chapter 10 discusses where he did not mention Shabbos, and the Rosh mentioned Mussaf as a novelty, and also in order to bring proof from it; see there. It also seems to me that later as well it refers to where he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, and nevertheless he did not fulfill the obligation, since he did not pray a proper Shemoneh Esrei. For one fulfills with Shemoneh Esrei provided that he mentions the day, or that he prays the actual blessing of the day.

**** ENGLISH ****
<b>Some say, etc.</b> This is surprising, for in the Beit Yosef he wrote this entire seif in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket without any dispute. Even the first opinion in seif 2 appears to agree here, since he mentioned nothing of the Mussaf prayer. I later saw that in Tanya, page 30, he cited the law of one who switched in general terms and did not distinguish between Mussaf and the others; perhaps for this reason the Beit Yosef wrote it in the name of 'some say.' Nevertheless, as practical law it seems to me that he must go back, and so wrote the Magen Avraham. The Magen Avraham wrote: It seems to me that if he fulfilled the obligation of Mussaf, he afterwards prays Shacharis, end quote. This requires study, for he wrote in seif katan 9 that if he said Mussaf in place of Shacharis, he must go back. It appears that if he already prayed Mussaf for Mussaf, he must go back and pray Shacharis. In the Magen Avraham one must read: if he did not fulfill, etc.; it seems to me to be a scribal error, and he meant that when he has not yet prayed Mussaf as Mussaf, he prays Shacharis. It is also possible that since he spoke falsely before the Omnipresent, he must go back; but it is strained to say that since he later prays Shacharis in place of Mussaf, this is called going back. Perhaps this is what he means: if he intends that the Shacharis prayer should serve as the Mussaf prayer, he has fulfilled Mussaf, and he later prays Shacharis, for since he initially intends it to be Mussaf, he fulfills the obligation of Mussaf. But this is difficult, for it is explicit at the beginning of siman 286.

Rabbi David Abudraham wrote that 'and your sin is removed' means that he is not included in 'if he does not tell, he shall bear his iniquity,' for they are giving testimony. In the Midrash it says that the word 'asher' appears three times in Vayechulu, as in the passage of the red heifer, to teach that one who says Vayechulu three times is atoned. Rabbeinu Yerucham, page 102, writes that because there is strength in three times, one says it three times: in the prayer, after the prayer, and over Kiddush. <b>[Levush] To testify together, etc.</b> This requires study, for in Choshen Mishpat, siman 28, he wrote that each witness says his testimony separately. One must say as the Sema wrote in seif katan 37, that after each one says it separately, they say it together. Nevertheless, according to the conclusion, this does not remain so (Magen Avraham). To me it seems that from the Shulchan Aruch in Choshen Mishpat, siman 30, seif 9, it is implied that specifically in monetary cases each one says his testimony separately. Moreover, this is comparable to testimony in capital cases, for through their testimony they render Shabbos desecrators liable to death. This requires study for me, whether in capital testimony we require that they testify together. See Rambam, chapter 2 of Kesubos, Mishnah 8.
