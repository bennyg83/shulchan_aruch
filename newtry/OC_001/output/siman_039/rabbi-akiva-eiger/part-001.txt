<small>סעיף א' </small>או אשה. טומטום אף דמחויב להניח תפלין דספק דאורייתא מה"ת לחומרא. ס"ס פסול לכתוב תפלין דשמא הוא נקבה ולא הוי בר חיובא כנלע"ד והמשבצות זהב לעיל סי' י"ד ס"ק א' לא משמע כן. אח"כ ראיתי שהמחבר בעצמו כאן באשל אברהם כ' בפשיטות דפסול לכתוב:
**** ENGLISH ****
Se'if 1: “Or a woman.” A tumtum is obligated to put on tefillin because, where a Torah-law doubt exists, Torah law itself requires stringency. Nevertheless, it appears to me that a tumtum is invalid to write tefillin, because perhaps the person is female and not obligated in the mitzvah. Mishbetzot Zahav above, siman 14, se'if katan 1, does not appear to hold this way. I later saw that the author himself writes here in Eshel Avraham as a straightforward matter that a tumtum is invalid to write them.

<small>ט"ז ס"ק א' </small>עשאוהו חז"ל כמי. משמע דרק מדרבנן פסול. וזה דעת האחרים הובאו בנ"י. אבל כ' שם הנ"י דמדברי רש"י והרמב"ם נראה דמדאוריי' פסול ע"ש ונ"מ באין לו תפליו אחרים:
**** ENGLISH ****
Turei Zahav, se'if katan 1: “The Sages treated him as one who,” etc. This implies that the writing is invalid only rabbinically. This is the view of the other authorities cited by Nimukei Yosef. Nimukei Yosef writes there, however, that Rashi and Rambam appear to hold that it is invalid by Torah law; see there. A practical difference arises when he has no other tefillin.

<small>מג"א ס"ק א' </small>אבל מספיקא דבדאוריי' לא סמכינן אחזקה שהביא ב' שערות. ונלע"ד אם כתב הוא עצמו מותר להניחן. דלגבי דאורייתא הוי ס"ס. ס' דגדול היה בשעת הכתיבה. את"ל קטן דלמא גם עתה קטן הוא ופטור מתפלין. ולגבי חינוך דרבנן סמכי' על חזקה דהביא ב"ש כשהגיע לי"ג שנים:
**** ENGLISH ****
Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: But because this is a Torah-law doubt, we do not rely upon the presumption that he developed two pubic hairs. In my humble opinion, if he himself wrote the tefillin, he may put them on. Regarding the Torah obligation there is a double doubt: perhaps he was already an adult when he wrote them; and even if he was then a minor, perhaps he is still a minor now and is exempt from tefillin. Regarding the rabbinic obligation of education, we rely upon the presumption that he developed two pubic hairs upon reaching thirteen years of age.

<small>מג"א ס"ק ב' </small>דעכו"ם בלא"ה. לענ"ד י"ל דנ"מ בנמצאו ביד נכרי ואמר שכתבן לשמה דיש להאמינו במגו דהי' אומר דישראל היה כתבן שהיה נאמן כדאיתא סעיף ו'. וכן נכרי שכתבן ונתגייר ויודע בעצמן דכתבן לשמן דהיה רשאי להניחם ואפשר דהיה נאמן אף לאחרים מדין ע"א נאמן באיסורין. גם היכי דאין לו תפלין אחרים דהיה צריך להניחן מספק שמא כתבן לשמן. ע' אה"ע סי' קכ"ג. לזה צריך לטעם דאינו בקשירה ופסולים וודאי. וכבר כתבתי בזה בתשו' סי' ג' על ראיית תוס' דתליית ציצית לא בעי לשמה מדצריך בני ישראל ועשו למעט נכרי. דיש לדחות דצריך קרא ליתגייר אח"כ ואומר שתלאן לשמן:
**** ENGLISH ****
Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2: “For a non-Jew in any event,” etc. In my humble opinion, there is a practical difference where tefillin were found in a non-Jew's possession and he says that he wrote them for the sake of the mitzvah. He should be believed through a migo: he could have said that a Jew wrote them, in which case he would be believed, as stated in se'if 6. Likewise, if a non-Jew wrote them and subsequently converted, and knows himself that he wrote them for the sake of the mitzvah, he would be permitted to put them on. It is possible that he would even be believed concerning others under the rule that one witness is trusted in matters of prohibition. Another case is where one has no other tefillin; he would have to put them on because of the doubt that perhaps they were written for the sake of the mitzvah. See Even HaEzer, siman 123. For these cases we need the reason that the writer is not included in the mitzvah of tying; the tefillin are therefore certainly invalid. I already wrote about this in Responsa, siman 3, concerning Tosafos' proof that attaching tzitzit does not require intent for the sake of the mitzvah because the verse needs the words “the children of Israel shall make” to exclude a non-Jew. The proof can be rejected: the verse is needed for a non-Jew who later converts and says that he attached them for the sake of the mitzvah.
