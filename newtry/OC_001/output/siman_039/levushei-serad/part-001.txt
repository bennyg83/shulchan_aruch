**** HEBREW ****
<small>(שם)</small> וכתב הר"ן דאינו מלת' באפי נפשי' הוא ולא קאי במומר שלא להניח תפילין אלא קאי אדברי המחבר שהם מש"ס דגיטין דמומר לדבר אחד פסול לכתיבת תפילין ע"ז כתב הר"ן שם דכיון דאינו מומר לכל התורה אין פסולו רק מדרבנן וע"ז הקשה דהא ע"כ בלהכעיס מיירי דאלו לתיאבון כשר וא"כ הוי דאורייתא:
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>(There.)</small> 'The Ran writes that it is not, etc.' This is not an independent statement and does not refer to an apostate who refuses to put on tefillin. Rather, it refers to the author's words, which derive from the Talmud in Gittin, that an apostate regarding one matter is disqualified from writing tefillin. Concerning this, the Ran writes there that since he is not an apostate regarding the entire Torah, his disqualification is only rabbinic. The objection to this is that the case must concern one who acts in order to provoke, since one who sins to satisfy an appetite remains qualified. If so, the disqualification is by Torah law.
**** END BLOCK ****

<small>(שם)</small> ואין לומר דהכא מיירי. ולחומר' קאמר הר"ן וס"ל דבה"ג נמי אפי' מומר לדבר אחר שלא לאותו דבר עצמו מיפסל מדרבנן עכ"פ וסתר זאת דהא להר"ן בה"ג נמי מיפסל מדאורייתא אפי' לדבר אחר דאיהו ס"ל דזה הוי להכעיס:

<small>(There.)</small> One cannot say that the case here is, etc., and that the Ran states the rule as a stringency, maintaining that in such a case even an apostate regarding a different matter, not that particular matter itself, is at least disqualified rabbinically. This proposal is contradicted by the fact that according to the Ran, in such a case he is disqualified by Torah law even when his apostasy concerns another matter, because the Ran considers this conduct to be acting in order to provoke.

<small>שם</small> וצ"ל דבגיטין מייריי ר"ל מ"ש הר"ן בגיטין דאף דשביק היתר' אינ' רק מדרבנן היינו שאומר בהדיא שאינו להכעיס רק למיטעם כו' אבל בסתמ' לא תלינן דעשה למיטעם כו' ומיפסל מדאורייתא והוי נמי מין ולתירוץ ב' בסתמ' נמי תלינן למיטעם ואינו רק מדרבנן ודוקא בהרבה פעמים ה"ל מין מדאוריית':
**** ENGLISH ****
<small>There.</small> It must be said that the case in Gittin is as follows: When the Ran writes in Gittin that even one who leaves permitted food available and eats prohibited food is disqualified only rabbinically, he means that the person explicitly says that he is not acting in order to provoke but only to taste the prohibited food, etc. In an unspecified case, however, we do not assume that he acted merely to taste it, and he is disqualified by Torah law and is also deemed a sectarian. According to the second resolution, even in an unspecified case we assume that he acted merely to taste it, and his disqualification is only rabbinic. Only when he acts this way many times does he become a sectarian by Torah law.
