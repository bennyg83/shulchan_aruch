And all the more so in what he wrote, etc. I already wrote a responsum on this law, and I am copying it. I turned my heart to understand and contain a little in my understanding how to conduct this ruling over which the later great Rabbi Rama in Orach Chayim siman 263 wrote: if one continued his first meal on Motza'ei Shabbat until night, he is permitted to tell his fellow who already made havdalah to do his work and he is permitted to benefit from his cooking then; and afterward Rabbi Mohar Ram Yafe z\"l rose and ruled to forbid as he wrote nearby his words. First we will explain the Rishonim's dispute — namely Rashba with Ran on the source of this law regarding entering Shabbat: it is stated in chapter Shoel (Shabbat 151): R' Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel — a person is permitted to tell his fellow: guard my fruits in your boundary and I will guard your fruits in my boundary; and Beit Yosef in Orach Chayim siman 263 in the name of Rashba: from this we learn that an Israelite who accepted Shabbat before dark is permitted to tell his fellow Israelite to do a certain work for him. And Ran wrote on this: and to me it appears the case is not comparable to the proof, for if there is a watchman there he himself guards — end of his words; and Beit Yosef on Ran's words: and I do not understand his words, for likewise one could say if he did not accept Shabbat he himself would be permitted to do work for him — end of his words; and it appears to me Beit Yosef's claim is not a claim here, for perforce this reasoning that there is permission now does not help unless there is permission now; but it does not help to say so retroactively that it could have been initially in a matter where there would be permission now — nevertheless now when he has no permission you know it is so; for if we say retroactively also helps — why did R' Yochanan there permit to tell the hippei: I go tomorrow, since he has permission via watchman he could have said that if he wanted he would have gone there or near it before Shabbat; rather it is simple it cannot be so except where there is a side now to permit even though it is Shabbat; and since on Shabbat itself we find permission in this — all who have watchman — perforce permitted even without watchman, for the prohibition of amirah does not apply here since it is not equal for all now on Shabbat day; unlike here where he accepted Shabbat upon himself — he accepted all its holiness, for otherwise he would need to stipulate which accepts and which does not accept; and since he did not stipulate, Shabbat applies immediately to all matters — and this is obvious in my eyes. However regarding Ran's challenge on Rashba in this matter — although I am not fit to decide — nevertheless it appears to me there is room to answer Rashba: what Ran wrote is different in "guard my fruits," etc., for it is possible via watchman — it is proven in the Gemara the reason is not because of watchman, for R' Yehudah said Shmuel innovated this matter of watchman on the law that it is permitted to tell the hippei: I go tomorrow — that if there is watchman, etc.; and we challenge him from the mishnah: we taught one may not set out on the boundary at night to bring fruits — for there is permission if there were partitions, etc.; it appears that without Shmuel's words the mishnah itself would not be difficult — rather the mishnah itself is resolved per Rashi in the mishnah: bringing fruits is something forbidden to do on Shabbat — therefore one may not set out at night for it to leave after Shabbat for this; unlike guarding fruits which itself is permitted on Shabbat if it were in his boundary, only walking outside the boundary prevents him — therefore permitted to set out at night for this, only because of Shmuel's words who innovated this permission and said: all who have a side of permission now on Shabbat for some person — namely one who has watchman — they permitted amirah for every person; likewise in bringing fruits we could say: from the fact there is permission for one who has partitions, it would be permitted to set out at night. We find to learn that the reason for permission in the mishnah to set out at night to guard his fruits does not depend on watchman permission, but because guarding itself has no forbidden act; and if so, after Shmuel's words were stated the reason of the mishnah truly changed and the reason to set out at night to guard fruits would be because of watchman — this is not so, for then Shmuel would not need to innovate a new law permitted to tell the hippei: I go, etc.; rather he should have said: what is the mishnah's reason permitting to set out at night to guard fruits — because if there is watchman, nevertheless the mishnah's reason is not because of watchman but because there is no Shabbat prohibition from the act itself as stated; and this itself is Shmuel's reason in that other statement that he said: a person tells his fellow — guard my fruits in your boundary, etc. — for there is no prohibition at all; this is exactly like the mishnah's reason for setting out at night to guard fruits as stated there — this depends on that; and further proof: if you say the reason for permission of "your boundary fruits" is because of watchman like Shmuel's other statement permitted to tell the hippei: I go — two difficulties on Shmuel: why are two needed since one reason for both; and if you say the reason is not because of watchman — all the more Shmuel's statement about boundary fruits is unnecessary, for we find permission even where there is Shabbat prohibition such as amirah to tell the hippei: I go tomorrow, and nevertheless permitted since there is permission via watchman — all the more where watchman is not needed. And further: if you say the reason for permission of boundary fruits is watchman like the other Shmuel statement about the hippei — two Shmuel statements are unnecessary; and if the reason is not watchman — all the more the boundary fruits statement is unnecessary. We find to learn that the mishnah's reason to set out at night to guard fruits does not depend on watchman permission. And Abba Shaul who gave a general rule: all that I am entitled to say amirah for, I am permitted to set out at night for it — is from Shmuel's statement about boundary fruits; and he said to the tanna kamma: since permission to set out at night depends on permission to say boundary fruits, you should have given a general rule in this that amirah and setting out at night are equal; practical difference regarding attending to matters of a dead person and a bride where there too there is permission to set out at night since there is permission in amirah; and there regarding the dead there is no permission otherwise as we say there in Shmuel's first statement — we challenge: but a dead person, what is it — to bring him a coffin and shrouds, etc.; likewise afterward, since if there were partitions there would be permission; and we answer: you find to cut a cloak for him — in this partitions do not help, like watchman permission; and if so Shmuel's words are properly resolved, who needed to state the boundary fruits statement to reveal to us that here we do not speak of permission via watchman; and from this we derive permission for dead and bride matters that we do not speak of a matter where there is permission otherwise. And per this Rashba properly derives: just as here in boundary fruits we do not need watchman permission but it is permitted because there is no prohibition at all since for his fellow it is permitted and no Shabbat prohibition is upon him — therefore permitted for this one amirah that he guard his fruits; likewise here one who accepted Shabbat before dark — there is no prohibition of amirah to his fellow who is entirely permitted, for amirah was forbidden only in a matter equal for every Israelite; and Rashba's words stand properly; therefore Beit Yosef brought his words for halacha; and further Ran did not disagree in law from him, only he wrote the case is not comparable to the proof — and likewise seif 4 is very similar. And now we discuss the dispute of the Acharonim — Rama derives from Rashba above that likewise on Motza'ei Shabbat if one continued his meal until night he is permitted to tell his fellow to do work for him and benefit from him; and Maharam Yafe disagrees: but in my humble opinion it is not comparable — entering Shabbat is not like leaving Shabbat, for in entering Shabbat the matter depends on his vow and they accepted upon themselves kedushat Shabbat, and we can say this one accepted and this one did not; but in leaving Shabbat, once it was forbidden to him on Shabbat day and he continues in its holiness at night, all prohibitions continue with him, etc.; and all the time he did not bless havdalah, behold kedushat Shabbat is upon him. And it appears to me the essence is per Rama and Maharam Yafe — he did not examine sufficiently here, for he thinks Rashba's permission on Erev Shabbat before dark is because this one accepted did not accept to forbid upon himself his fellow's work; and such is stated explicitly at the beginning of seif 17: although he accepted Shabbat to add from profane to holy, nevertheless he forbade only his own melacha but not his fellow's melacha — end of his words; behold he tied the permission because he did not accept his fellow's melacha — and this is not so, for then it has no connection to Rashba's derivation from the Gemara of boundary fruits, where it is full Shabbat and even so permitted because the prohibition is not equal for all; and likewise here. And I saw in a new edition of Levush HaChur that Maharam Yafe explained in a responsum Rashba's words in this manner — that just as boundary fruits are permitted since there is permission via watchman, likewise here there is permission in accepting Shabbat — for if he wanted he would not have accepted Shabbat; and this is Beit Yosef's explanation that I brought at the beginning of this passage — and we already rejected it there; and I add now: if he derives from watchman reasoning, why does Rashba derive from Shmuel's second statement about boundary fruits and not from the earlier statement much more — namely what they said: permitted to tell the hippei: I go, etc., where the reason is explained because of watchman; and why did Rashba omit it and derive from the later statement where watchman reason was not mentioned at all; on the contrary it is proven the reason was not so, as I wrote at first with clear proof. If so, all the more on Motza'ei Shabbat if he wanted he would have made havdalah — this is preferable to accepting Shabbat, for there he cannot fix now, and here he can still fix now and tell the one who made havdalah; rather because he initially ate on Shabbat he should bless now on the beginning of the meal; but not on what he eats now at night — know for yourself: if one is called one who began eating at night before he prayed — simply he should not say retzeh since there is no beginning of a meal by day; perforce no room to say since he did not bless havdalah Shabbat is still upon him; and what Maharam Yafe brought as proof from one who observes two days Yom Kippur — likewise forbidden on the second even for one who does not observe two days — because there he is stringent wanting to exit safek lest the main Yom Kippur is tomorrow and the second would be observing in prohibition per his view; unlike here where the other acts in full permission since night is permitted even for this one who has not yet made havdalah; and this was already challenged on Maharam Yafe as written there in his responsum — except his answer there is not because of safek yoma since he releases his vow on this; rather certainly because of a fence of piety it is — for we are expert in the fixed calendar; and his words are very puzzling, for on both Yom Tov of Galut we do not rely on that expertise lest the expert forget, as they sent from there at the beginning of Beitzah; except that on Yom Kippur because the public cannot stand otherwise we rely on the essence of the law as Maharam Yafe himself wrote siman 624; except some pious ones are stringent and observe Yom Kippur like other Yom Tov — and for them it is two days of Yom Kippur like other Yom Tov literally.
