One may not knead, etc. Thus the Rosh: because in Nissan days the springs boil and it is still the rainy season and the sun travels in the lower sky — therefore one fills from evening and they cool until morning. R' Avigdor wrote: according to this reason, one who draws in the morning and leaves until evening is fine; but R' Eliyahu Mizrachi explained: because at night the sun travels under the earth, therefore they have no remedy except not to be attached at night; therefore those who draw at the beginning of the night are permitted immediately and forbidden to draw after the beginning of the night — end quote. Thus Semag: R' Eliezer of Metz says even l'chatchila one may knead with them when impossible, as said at the end of chapter Kol Sha'ah: Rav Matna expounded in Papunia — a woman should knead only in mayim shelanu; tomorrow they all brought their jugs to him, thinking shelanu meant Rav Matna's water; he told them: I mean water from my house — meaning they left overnight; and we do not find they fasted from eating. Rashi explained the reason for mayim shelanu: because the sun travels in Nissan days which is still rainy season, etc.; according to his reason, drawing in the morning and leaving until evening is permitted; and from Papunia there is no proof — but the reason must be because at night the sun travels under the ground; therefore one who draws at the beginning of the night is permitted immediately; therefore forbidden after drawing after the beginning of the night — end quote. Rambam and R' Yehuda bar Natan ruled in commentary on Pesachim: forbidden to bake before dawn; if they baked, the baked product should be forbidden lest the end of the night causes cooling — however this prohibition applies only to Rashi, not R' Eliyahu Mizrachi — end quote. R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote on what Semag wrote \"we do not find they fasted from eating\": it appears it was during Pesach when they kneaded in water not left overnight; when he said \"I mean from my house,\" he presumably permitted kneading in water not left overnight — you will ask: on that day they ate no matzah but fruits — why did the Talmud not mention this to teach us? One cannot say he permitted the baked matzot that were shogeg in kneading, for only \"if one transgressed and kneaded\" did Rav Ashi forbid, but shogeg not, and he would not permit l'chatchila, for presumably they destroyed all their matzot before coming to him because of chametz prohibition (and per this reasoning the Talmud need not mention shogeg is permitted); if it was the Shabbos before Pesach, what of \"we do not find they fasted\" — they already ate chametz four hours on the first night, and in the evening they could already knead with water drawn in the morning after 12 hours per Rashi, or draw at beginning of night permitted immediately per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, etc.; but the question is: whence that exposition was during Pesach — perhaps on Shabbos before Pesach, and he permitted on the first night for fulfillment of eating matzah, not during Pesach where there is no mitzvah fulfillment — end quote Mizrachi's words. Objection on him per his reasoning: he asked perhaps it was Shabbos before Pesach — why did he need to ask because of matzah fulfillment; he should have asked perhaps he expounded Shabbos before Pesach not to draw except mayim shelanu and they thought Rav Matna's water, and on the first night he said draw on the first night per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi who need not distinguish first night from rest of Pesach — forced distinction as Beit Yosef wrote on him: wherever chametz prohibition applies there is no distinction; or he should have asked from what he himself said to prove R' Eliyahu Mizrachi held the exposition was during Pesach not Shabbos before, and therefore asked whence for R' Eliyahu Mizrachi it was not Shabbos before — Beit Yosef already sensed this. It appears to me on the contrary R' Eliyahu Mizrachi holds the exposition was Shabbos before, as all who want to learn Pesach laws before Pesach; what Mizrachi wrote they already ate chametz four hours on the first night — I wonder why Mizrachi did not analyze R' Eliyahu Mizrachi's words \"we do not find they fasted from eating\" — why not say they fasted alone and this \"from eating\" is extra language; rather it appears not a full fast all day but on mealtime which is beginning of the night itself, for they hurry to eat lest children sleep, as siman 472; and he holds as Tur siman 458 in the name of R' Yehuda HaKohen: on Yom Tov night one should not trouble on Yom Tov to bake matzah — therefore it fits here: you will ask they drew beginning of Pesach night and delayed in drawing, kneading, arranging, baking — mealtime which is on the first night by law would be lost; therefore he said we do not find they fasted from eating — meaning the proper mealtime; rather certainly he permitted kneading immediately in water not left overnight. Granted for Rashi no proof — one could say they had other water at home and waited until after chatzot and baked matzah after 12 hours and ate in evening; but per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi there is proof as written; thus other difficulties of Mizrachi that Beit Yosef brought are settled. Mizrachi wrote further: per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi he must hold if drawn before beginning of night while sun still on earth, forbidden to knead, because they must be attached in earth by day when earth is cold and cools them — otherwise no proof from Papunia even per his words that we can say they left what was drawn by day; and from \"one who draws at beginning of night\" it implies if drawn before beginning or after, no — end quote. Beit Yosef disagrees: if so how does it say one may knead only in mayim shelanu — what is night's work: if drawn while sun on earth or after beginning of night, even ours forbidden; if at beginning of night, even not ours permitted; rather should have written one may knead only in what was drawn at bein hashemashot; therefore he explained per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi three divisions: drawn at bein hashemashot — permitted immediately; drawn after beginning of night — wait another day and night; drawn by day — wait that day and following night; and when he said mayim shelanu until they stood one full night and cooled in coldness — when drawn after beginning of night the reason is heat of sun under ground; when drawn by day, although earth is cold, they do not cool from boiling until 12 hours pass; and since the world is hot by day and water heats when drawn by movement as Rif and Ran wrote, they do not cool until one night; but drawn at bein hashemashot, although they heat when drawn by movement, R' Eliyahu Mizrachi holds since the world is cold then they cool immediately. Therefore \"one who draws at beginning of night permitted immediately\" means then permitted immediately; but if drawn before beginning while sun on earth or after beginning, forbidden until full night passes. And \"they have no remedy except not to be at night attached\" — not that no remedy forever, rather remedy of 12 hours detached does not help when drawn by day or after beginning of night, only one full night — end Beit Yosef; many questions on Rabbeinu Yehuda's explanation, for night's stringency is from one of two ways: either because in measure of night the boiling in them cools as Rashi, or at night itself there is boiling per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi; per Beit Yosef's words of R' Eliyahu Mizrachi when drawn by day they heated from movement and need one night to cool; when drawn at night they boil from the night per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi — he should hold two powers in that night: cooling and fixing boiling drawn by day, and second boiling of water attached at night — why did R' Eliyahu Mizrachi write drawn at bein hashemashot permitted immediately, whence — since anyway there is boiling from drawing movement, certainly here too needs full night to cool like by day. What Beit Yosef wrote: since bein hashemashot is cold they cool immediately is very astonishing — whence for Rambam boiling cools in one moment as soon as they draw; and since day's boiling by movement and bein hashemashot's boiling are equal, why does drawing by day not suffice to wait until bein hashemashot and cool as drawn at bein hashemashot; a fortiori drawing by day whose time is longer should help bein hashemashot's cooling for day's drawing boiling, since bein hashemashot's cooling helps what was drawn at bein hashemashot when boilings are equal — I do not know place to settle our rabbi Beit Yosef's words and why he troubled to explain \"they have no remedy\" that R' Eliyahu Mizrachi mentioned and why he asked what does Semag bring proof from Papunia per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi and not Rashi — granted for R' Eliyahu Mizrachi no proof, perhaps drawn at beginning of night; Beit Yosef explained Semag's proof: Gemara needed this incident of Papunia which is not for need — implying it teaches permission to knead in water not left overnight; granted for Rashi no proof — one could say it teaches leniency when drawn morning and wait until evening, unlike R' Eliyahu Mizrachi who is not satisfied with that — if so certainly Gemara wrote this incident to inform us water not left overnight is permitted when impossible — these are Beit Yosef's words though not his language. I wonder why the rabbi here deviated from his path of truth everywhere and inclined to sharp pepper and explained what did not occur to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi — he had proof from not finding they fasted from eating, and Rabbeinu Yehuda discarded that proof and wrote another proof: why Gemara needed this incident not mentioned in R' Eliyahu Mizrachi's words; and per Beit Yosef's pilpul we too say for R' Eliyahu Mizrachi no proof — Gemara teaches mayim shelanu means water from his house — meaning night itself requires, not measure of overnight like Rashi where even by day helps; this certainly should be informed in Talmud; Mahari Weil also explained for R' Eliyahu Mizrachi overnight of night helps for drawn by day and washed his hands to explain Mizrachi who holds so; he raged at Beit Yosef who explained Mizrachi's words as mistaken — rather Mizrachi also holds so, except drawing at night he explains they have no remedy forever; astonishing, for Mizrachi explicitly wrote if drawn before beginning or after, no — clear before is like after with no distinction. And no reason ever to distinguish invalidity from day's boiling and night's boiling — who is the prophet to tell us there is distinction — Gemara did not mention this at all, only we require overnight without condition. It appears to me Mizrachi's explanation per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi is clear truth: for Rashi there is no boiling for night because it is night, but because rainy season and springs always boil day and night; night came only to fix boiling — cool at night time; thus also waiting by day measure of night suffices.
