(א) That he too transgresses, etc. Since this man does not desire children, she has not lost her ketubah. If she fed him forbidden food and he himself also eats forbidden food, she has not lost it. But if she fed him forbidden food while he transgresses vows, Mordechai wrote in the name of Maharam that she has lost her ketubah, for we hold that an apostate regarding one matter is not an apostate regarding another. One can say that although he transgresses vows, nevertheless he does not eat forbidden food, and likewise the reverse. Piskei Mahara'i, siman 68, wrote that in our time one must examine very carefully before removing a woman without a ketubah because she transgresses vows, for many people are lenient with vows. If she swore regarding something she had done that if she does it again she must accept a get from him, and she violated the oath, it appears that the oath takes effect. The cherem of Rabbeinu Gershom was for her benefit, that he not divorce her against her will, and she can waive it; all the more in our time, when one can say that the time of the cherem has already ended. Since the oath took effect upon her and she violated it, she is found to be one who transgresses dat, and he may divorce her against her will. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that the same applies if he transgresses other sins because of which children die: if she transgresses vows, she has not lost her ketubah, for the above reason.
(ב) A woman who apostatized, etc. In Responsa Rosh he wrote that if she apostatized under coercion and did not sanctify Hashem's name, she is not considered one who violates Dat, for she did not cause her husband to stumble. However, one can say that even here there is a stumbling block for her husband and for the children she will bear from him, and it is also possible that there is danger for her husband to live with her in the place where she apostatized. Therefore it is possible that she forfeited the ketubah, specifically with warning. But if she apostatized willingly, this depends on the dispute brought in siman 7. According to those poskim who hold that she is forbidden to her husband because her law is like one who committed zenut, she does not need warning, as written in Rambam: even with witnesses of ugly conduct, warning is not required. The Rav Rama follows his own reasoning, for there in siman 7 he brought the lenient view that even if she apostatized willingly we do not say she committed zenut. If so, even regarding one who violates Dat Moshe, voluntary apostasy is equivalent to coerced apostasy wherever there is no concern for zenut. However, the primary view appears to be that if she apostatized willingly she is forbidden to her husband, for the Rosh of Sens holds so, as written in Responsa Maharik at the end of shoresh 160, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot and Terumat HaDeshen, siman 241; so too it appears in Responsa Rosh. Even if there is a dispute, he cannot be required to pay the ketubah even without warning. Although according to those who forbid her it is only a doubt, lest she committed zenut, this is not like a case where he says "perhaps I paid you," while the debt is certain and payment doubtful, in which case one who says "I know I borrowed but do not know whether I repaid" is liable. There it is different: here she is forbidden to him and she caused the prohibition, and therefore she has no ketubah, as in the case of warning and seclusion, as I wrote at the end of the previous s.k.
