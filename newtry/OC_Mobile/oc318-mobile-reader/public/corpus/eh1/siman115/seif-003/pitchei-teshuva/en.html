That she served him while she was a niddah. See Beit Meir above, siman 96, seif 12, who cites Responsa Panim Me'irot, part 2, siman 12, regarding a husband who claimed that his wife served him while she was a niddah. She admitted it, but claimed it was done with his knowledge. The ruling there was that the husband must swear that he did not know and that she caused him to stumble, and he divorces her without a ketubah. Beit Meir writes that this ruling is correct based on the Shulchan Aruch there, that where she is suspect, the opposing party swears and is exempt. Although Choshen Mishpat, siman 92, seif 9, brings two opinions about such a case, and the Shach concludes primarily that she collects without an oath, this is not decisive. The main law appears to follow the Responsa Rashach cited by the Shach, that the possessor can say kim li like the first view. If so, here too the husband can say kim li. According to the Shach’s conclusion, however, a certain scholar answered well that she should take without an oath, and Beit Meir’s response from Choshen Mishpat does not appear comparable. From Panim Me'irot it is at least clear that if she claims the husband also knew of the transgression, her claim has substance and she can make him swear on it. At first glance, Responsa Mahari HaLevi, brother of the Taz, siman 39, printed also in Teshuvot Geonei Batra'ei, siman 26, implies the opposite, that she cannot make such a claim unless she brings witnesses. He proves this from the Mordechai, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Hagahot Ashri, and Rashba, all of whom write that if she violated an oath and brings witnesses that he too violates it, he cannot remove her without ketubah; they all specify that she must bring witnesses. But one may distinguish: Mahari HaLevi speaks where witnesses testify that she transgressed; in that case she cannot claim he also transgressed unless she brings witnesses. Panim Me'irot speaks where there were no witnesses that she served him as a niddah, only her own admission; perhaps even Mahari HaLevi agrees that she can immediately claim he also knew, with a migo that she could have said she did not serve him as a niddah, and she can make him swear. This requires study. Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 26, in a similar case, also makes this distinction.
