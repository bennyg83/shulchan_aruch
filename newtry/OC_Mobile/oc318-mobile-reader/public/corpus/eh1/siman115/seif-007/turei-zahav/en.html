(א) And his mind relies on him, etc. The Tur wrote: if there is one witness that she committed adultery, he is not obligated to divorce her by human law, but because of slanderous talk it is forbidden for him to remain with her if he relies on that witness and the witness is trusted by him like two. This is based on the Gemara's case brought by Beit Yosef, where Shmuel said that if one witness is trusted by you that your wife committed adultery, go divorce her. Rif and Rosh wrote that although the halachah follows Rava that one witness in a matter of ervah is insufficient, this is only in human courts; to fulfill the duty of Heaven, if the witness is trusted like two he must divorce her. The Tur wrote similarly in siman 178. Beit Yosef asked why here the Tur wrote "because of slanderous talk" while in siman 178 he wrote in terms of duty before Heaven. Taz explains that these are not contradictory. Where the husband truly trusts the witness like two, he must separate because of the heavenly obligation. Where the matter is not fully trusted but creates public suspicion, the language of slanderous talk applies. The distinction also affects whether coercion is possible, whether the witness creates a prohibition only for the husband, and whether the woman's ketubah is lost. Since a matter of ervah requires two witnesses for court action, one witness alone cannot remove money or compel divorce in beit din; the husband's personal certainty governs only his own conduct.
