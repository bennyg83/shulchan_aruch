(א) One who violates Dat Moshe, etc. The rule is that she does not leave without a ketubah unless she caused him to stumble in a matter of prohibition, or she transgressed a sin for which children die, or she acted immodestly, in which case there is concern that she may commit zenut under him. But because of some other prohibition she committed, she does not leave without a ketubah. See Responsa Rosh, kelal 32 siman 3, where he wrote that a woman who apostatized is not considered one who violates Dat Moshe and Yehudit, since it is not among the above concerns. Rather, one can say that there is a stumbling block for her husband and for the children she will bear from him, for they will have great disgrace that their mother was among the uncircumcised; and it is also possible that there is danger for her husband to live with her in the place where she apostatized. Shiltei HaGibborim wrote that if he causes her to stumble in a matter of prohibition, or if he vows and does not fulfill, we compel him to divorce and give a get. This is when she comes with the claim "I need a staff for my hand"; where he vows and does not fulfill, since the children die for that sin, she must claim "I need a staff for my hand." If she vows and does not fulfill, and she is barren and has no child, Piskei Mahara'i, siman 68, was uncertain whether she leaves without a ketubah, since she has no children; even though perhaps if she had not violated vows a miracle would have occurred for her and she would have had children.
