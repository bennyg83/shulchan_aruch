Who fed her husband. See Baer Heitev, who wrote that if she vows and does not fulfill her vows and she is barren, etc. At first glance, according to what the Rash and the Baer Heitev write below in seif katan 7 in the name of Mahari himself, that in our time the matter requires very careful scrutiny, there is no practical difference in this doubt if she is barren, for even when she is not barren we do not rule in our time to remove her without a ketubah because of vows. This is also explicit in the rulings of Mahari there, where he wrote: at first glance the law is with her, for so Maharam inferred in Mordekhai where he violates the cherem, as Rama wrote in seif 3. However, one may distinguish, for here he can claim: had you not been negligent, a miracle might have occurred for me, as happened for Sarah our mother, peace be upon her; and so we find in Perek HaMekabel that one may say a miracle might have occurred for me. Still, it is not clear to me to distinguish this way. In any event, nowadays the matter requires very careful scrutiny, etc.; see there. One must say that Beit Shmuel copied this because it is relevant where it is known that the generation has become fit. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer II, siman 171.
