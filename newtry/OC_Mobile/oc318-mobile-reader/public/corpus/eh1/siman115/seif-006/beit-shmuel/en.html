(א) Specifically where there are no indications. See Tosafot, Yevamot 104, who wrote regarding a woman who says "I am impure," where there are indications and there are also reasons to permit, as in the case at the end of Nedarim: if the husband and the suspected man came and the suspected man did not hide himself, she is permitted to her husband, for if he had committed a prohibition he would have hidden. So wrote the Rosh as well, and Chelkat Mechokek brought their words simply. He overlooked Tosafot in chapter Af Al Pi, Ketubot 63, who disagree with this and do not permit her on that basis when she says "I am impure"; see there. Piskei Mahara'i, siman 222, asked that these words of Tosafot contradict one another, and answered that what they wrote in chapter Af Al Pi is from the words of Rashba of Sens, who composed Tosafot. So too it appears from this gloss: where there are indications, she is forbidden, with no distinction. If a widow married a kohen and after the marriage it became clear that she had committed zenut before the marriage, and she said that she committed zenut with her sister's husband, she thereby became a zonah and is forbidden to her husband, who is a kohen. See Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 82, whether she is believed. This should not be compared to what is written at the end of siman 46, that according to Rama, if a rumor emerged before the marriage she is believed to forbid herself to her husband; here too it became clear that she committed zenut before the marriage. There the case is worse, because she says the rumor was true, that she had been betrothed; but here, even if she committed zenut, one can say she did so with someone else. Also, according to what I wrote there, that law is not so. If she committed zenut and does not know with whom, see siman 6 and what I wrote there. If one witness says she committed zenut and she is silent, this is like admission, and we do not say perhaps she set her eyes on another; he must divorce her. Even if the witness is not believed by him like two witnesses, he is obligated by law to divorce her. So wrote Maharshal. But according to Beit Yosef's answer in this siman, he is not obligated by law to divorce her; only by the laws of Heaven must he divorce her. See Perishah, siman 178. If one witness says she committed zenut and says that the husband knows she committed zenut, and the husband is silent and the witness is trusted by him like two witnesses, Maharshal wrote that he must divorce her to satisfy the laws of Heaven, but by law he is not obligated to divorce her. This is what Rif, Rosh in chapter HaOmer, and the Tur in siman 178 wrote: he is not obligated by law to divorce her, even though he was silent; we do not say silence is like admission, as written in Tosafot in chapter HaOmer. These laws should not be compared to the other cases.
