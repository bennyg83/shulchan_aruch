This is a doubt. Although what he said to her, be betrothed with four zuz, was after the giving of the money, nevertheless it is a doubt, because we say: if she was not pleased, why did she not throw away the kiddushin? But there is no concern because of the doubt that perhaps the object is worth a perutah elsewhere, for he retracted from what he first wanted, namely to betroth with less than a perutah through the object. So wrote the Rosh. The Ran wrote that he did not want to betroth her with the mat, but from the beginning his intent was to betroth her with the money in it, only she did not understand. A practical difference follows from what is written nearby in the name of Shiltei Gibborim: if he did not repeat and say, be betrothed, etc., according to the Rosh's explanation it does not help, for what he now wants is new kiddushin with the money. The Taz challenged the Rama for bringing the words of Shiltei Gibborim. According to the Rambam we are concerned that perhaps his intent was on the object. If it came into her hand as a deposit and afterward he said, be betrothed with it, and she was silent, we do not say that if she was not pleased she would have thrown away the kiddushin, for one may say that she thought she would become liable to pay, since it came into her hand as a deposit. According to the above poskim, in such a case there is no concern for kiddushin. If she had a deposit in her hand and they stipulated that she was not liable for responsibility, then even with a deposit we say she should have thrown it away; so wrote responsum Maharam Padua siman 24. However, responsum Rivash does not imply so.
Same if did not return etc. Per Rosh explanation not help if did not return and say be betrothed for he returned betrothing anew with money; Taz wrote even to her.
And she was silent, etc. What difference is there whether it came into her hand as a deposit or came into her hand as theft? In any case, silence after the giving of money does not help. Although responsum Maharam Padua did not decide leniently, nevertheless responsum Shevut Yaakov siman 28 ruled leniently. At first glance it appears that even if she said yes it does not help, since it is not in his possession and he cannot betroth her with it, just as one cannot consecrate something that is not in his possession. So too it implies in responsum Shevut Yaakov. However, from the fact that the gloss wrote, she was silent, it follows that he holds that if she said yes, it immediately becomes a deposit in her possession and she is betrothed, and it becomes a deposit and kiddushin at once. According to this, what Rabbi Yochanan said, that if one stole and the owner did not despair, neither can consecrate it, means that each one alone cannot consecrate it; but with the consent of both, it becomes consecrated. See the above responsum: even with a deposit, if he demands it from her and she does not want to return it to him, he cannot betroth her with it. According to what I wrote, if she said yes she is betrothed, and nothing follows from this.
