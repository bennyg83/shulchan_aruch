Safek betrothed — can say since initially accepted for kiddushin though object not perutah since returned said betroth with zuz in it; if not please would throw — not obligated guard since not deposit in her hand; from not throwing implies consented to kiddushin.
And if did not return etc. — wonder Rabba appears here for Rava said in this silence after giving money implies if said yes certainly kiddushin for language of kiddushin he said then and she consented; if so here since did not return and said behold inside perutah — not like tzipita case.
A woman who snatched, etc. Maharam Padua siman 24 wrote that silence after the giving of money does not help. He wrote that according to the Rif, who wrote like the view that we are concerned for it and it is a doubt requiring a get, that is because they had arranged shidduchim beforehand, meaning that he first used language of kiddushin, unlike here, where it was not initially for the sake of kiddushin before she snatched it. His words are very surprising to me. If you consider the first kiddushin as shidduchim, why is it doubtful kiddushin? We hold that where he arranged shidduchim beforehand, even if he says nothing afterward, she is certainly betrothed. If so, even if he said nothing after the first kiddushin she would be certainly betrothed. He further brought proof from the Tur's words: in what case is this said? Where it came into her hand as a deposit. But if it came into her hand as kiddushin, etc. He asks: the Tur should have shortened and written, but if it came into her hand not as a deposit, so that she is not liable for guarding it, and he said to her, be betrothed, and she was silent, it is a doubt. Rather, certainly this teaches that if there were no shidduchim beforehand it is not kiddushin at all, and the first kiddushin are like shidduchim. End quote. I see no inference here at all. One cannot infer from here that the first kiddushin are like shidduchim from the fact that the Tur did not write, but if it came into her hand not as a deposit, etc., for it is impossible to say so. How could the Tur say that if she received it not as a deposit it is doubtful? Anything not as a deposit is by way of snatching, like the incident mentioned there, and in such a case there is certainly no kiddushin at all. If her intent was to hold it for herself by way of theft, it is obviously worse than a debt she owes him from a loan, and there is no kiddushin here, as appears to me. The rabbi's words in this matter are very surprising to me, as are his proofs there. He concludes that in any case she requires a get, but this is a stringency without reason. The Rama also wrote here that it is not kiddushin at all.
