At the host's. Although in Orach Chayim 170 it is stated that guests are not permitted to take from what is before them and give it to the host's son, one must say that specifically to his son they may not give, but guests may give to one another. Since he invited them, he is not particular about their giving to one another; if so, here too the host is not particular that he betrothed a woman with it, since he invited him. Beit Hillel, Yoreh De'ah. Taz wrote that because of this difficulty it is only doubtful kiddushin. Kenesset HaGedolah 58a wrote: I am uncertain whether this is specifically when he betrothed another woman, but if he betrothed the daughter of the host, whether she is betrothed; from Sefer Mekor Baruch, siman 25, it appears to be doubtful. I found in Maharshal, chapter Gid HaNasheh, cited in Orach Chayim 170, that after he has given his portion to the guest, the guest may give it to the host's son; see there. If so, one may say that for this reason he wrote here, "and he takes his portion," to teach that after his portion had been given to him, he betrothed the woman with it. According to this, the questions of Beit Hillel, Yoreh De'ah, and Taz from Orach Chayim 170 are not difficult. Guests reclining at a host's table: even if they separated their portions and placed them before themselves, and the host took from what they placed before themselves and betrothed the woman, she is betrothed so long as they had not placed it into their mouths. Maharit, part 1, siman 120.
