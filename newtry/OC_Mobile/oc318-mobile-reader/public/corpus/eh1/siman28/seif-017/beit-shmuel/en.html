So as not to be embarrassed. The Ritva wrote that even if he spoke in a pleasant tone, we say he said it because of embarrassment. But if there is a disclosure of intent that he is pleased, and the money has not been consumed, the other acquires it now and betroths with it. This implies that from now the kiddushin take effect if the money still exists; but if there is concern that he said it because of embarrassment, it is not kiddushin even from now, unlike the Bach. Rashi and the Ran wrote in this sugya that he said it because of embarrassment, so that he not be embarrassed, but retroactively he was not pleased. One may say their intent is that, so he not be embarrassed, he waives it from now, and the kiddushin take effect from now, but not retroactively. But one cannot say this, for in the Gemara, chapter Elu Metziot, Bava Metzia 22, Mar Zutra did not eat from those fruits when the owner said to the sharecropper: why did you not bring the rabbis from these fine fruits? because he held that the owner said so because of embarrassment. This proves that he does not waive at all, even from now, for otherwise why did he not eat? Rather, one must say that their intent is to establish the sugya with a broker, who has a share; and this is also the Rif's text, that it speaks of a broker. If so, he can betroth from now when the owner sees with what he is betrothing, but not retroactively. So too wrote the Bach. Tosafot holds that it does not speak of a broker, and therefore they wrote that it speaks where the owner gave him permission. It is also proven that the sugya concerns kiddushin, whether they take effect from now, and does not concern kiddushin taking effect retroactively. For we hold that despair without knowledge is not despair; if so, why do I need the reason that he said it because of embarrassment? Let it be that retroactively it is not kiddushin because we do not say that since he waives now he waived retroactively, just as we do not say this regarding despair without knowledge. Tosafot wrote similarly there in chapter Elu Metziot. Rather, certainly the sugya concerns whether the kiddushin take effect from now.
