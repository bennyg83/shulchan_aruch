In her hand. We hold loan for spending given — as if already spent — not giving now anything; see Chadashim Mahari"t.
The document. She is not betrothed, even if it is worth a perutah, since he did not mention at the time of kiddushin that he was betrothing her with it; by default it is like one who betroths with a loan alone. But if he says, "Be betrothed to me with this debt document," and gives it to her, they evaluate the paper: if it is worth a perutah, she is betrothed; if not, it is doubtful kiddushin. So wrote the Tur in the name of the Rambam, and so wrote Beit Yosef and Lechem Mishneh. Bach and R. Shlomo Yonah wrote that this is a scribal error and should read "the Ramah." In truth, I did not find this in the Rambam; the Maggid wrote likewise there in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Ishut. In my humble opinion, it appears that the Ramah learned this law from what the Gemara says in Kiddushin 47b-48a: "Rather, is it not with her debt document, and they disagree about one who betroths with a loan?" Why are both clauses necessary, that it says "with her debt document" and "one who betroths with a loan"? It is to teach that specifically where he betroths with the loan and returns the document to her; but if he betroths her with the document, according to everyone she is betrothed if it is worth a perutah. Examine this carefully. But in truth this requires analysis: how did the Gemara know to ask, "Shall we say it is a dispute of tannaim," when the baraita speaks of his saying, "Be betrothed to me with a debt document"? Perhaps it speaks precisely of his saying, "Be betrothed to me with a debt document," while Rav speaks of one who betroths with a loan and not of a document. See there and examine it carefully; see Nachalat Tzvi and Beit Shmuel.
