One who borrowed an object from his fellow. The Tur first brought that Baal HaIttur raised the question, for there is a dispute in this matter. There is one opinion that holds it is like a gift on condition to return, meaning as though he gives her a gift on condition that she return it, where we hold in siman 29 that she is not betrothed, because it resembles chalipin, and a woman is not acquired through chalipin. So too here: with what is he betrothing her? With an item borrowed in his hand. This is so even though he informs her that it is borrowed in his hand, for otherwise she is certainly not betrothed, since we hold that a borrower may not lend. Since she must return it, even a loan resembles a gift on condition to return it. Another opinion holds that since an ordinary borrowing is for thirty days, it will automatically also be in her hand for thirty days, and it is as though he says: with this benefit, that it will be in your hand for your needs for thirty days, I betroth you. This is not comparable to a gift on condition to return, for there is no fixed time there for returning it. Although it is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 111 that one who receives a gift on condition to return may delay returning the gift even for a long time, because he can say, I will return it after a long time, nevertheless, since there is no fixed time, at every moment he has an obligation to return it. This is unlike the thirty days of borrowing, for during that time it is like a sale, as we say regarding rental, that throughout the rental days it is like a sale. So too with borrowing, even when unspecified it is like specifying thirty days; and where he specifies, all agree that she is betrothed. This is as the Rama wrote, for he holds that if he says, with this benefit that you derive, etc. Afterward the Tur brought the view of the Rosh, as written here in Shulchan Aruch, that if he informed the lender that he would betroth with it, and informed the woman that it is borrowed until such-and-such time, though this is not in the Tur and in truth it is not needed when the lender knows he will betroth with it, then she is betrothed in every case, for we are witnesses that the lender resolved in his mind to give it to him in whatever manner is effective for kiddushin, etc. See what I wrote about this in Orach Chayyim siman 14, for there is reason to wonder about it. Darkei Moshe wrote: since the Geonim disagree about the law of one who betroths with a borrowed ring, it is good to be careful not to betroth with it. So too it implies in Hagahot Mordechai to Kiddushin. But the custom has already spread to betroth with a borrowed ring for the purpose of betrothing with it.
And informed wants betroth etc. — Terumat Hadeshen 210: need inform so not think hers and betroth only ring itself; but explicitly betroths with ornament benefit though not informed — kiddushin; also Rosh needs give him ring or money.
And betrothed in it — Darkhei Moshe: if betrothed ornament benefit alone all betrothed; but in thing itself per Tosafot chapter HaZahav can say rental for day sale only for onaah not kiddushin even if knew rented.
