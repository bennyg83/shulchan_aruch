And he informed him that he wishes to betroth with it. Then we say that he resolved in his heart to give it to him in a manner that will be effective for kiddushin; and if it will not help as a borrowing, it should be as a gift, and at least as a gift on condition to return. She will be betrothed, and he will acquire it from the woman and return it, or return its value. For one who gives a gift to his fellow on condition to return, if he does not return the utensil but only its value, that is considered return. Then he does not need to tell her that he borrowed the utensil, but may betroth her with the utensil, because he can return the utensil's value to the lender. It appears that likewise, if one borrows an etrog from another to fulfill the mitzvah with it and tells him that he wishes to fulfill the mitzvah with it, we also say that he gives it to him in a manner by which he can fulfill it; see Orach Chayyim siman 658. If he did not return its value, it requires study whether this is definite kiddushin, because his intent was for a gift, or whether it is doubtful kiddushin; see Chelkat Mechokek. If he did not inform him that he wished to betroth with it, the Rosh's view that she is betrothed applies only in these four conditions. First, he borrowed it for a known time; otherwise the lender can demand it immediately and there is no perutah's worth of benefit. Second, the lender must give him permission to lend it to another; otherwise he has no permission to give it to another, and it is as though he betrothed her with stolen property. Nevertheless, after the fact it is not actual theft and she is betrothed; see Terumat HaDeshen there. Third, he must inform her that he borrowed the utensil until such-and-such time; otherwise she thinks he gives it to her permanently. Fourth, the benefit of adornment for that period must be worth a perutah. Then, when these four conditions exist, she is betrothed, even though she must return the utensil afterward and it resembles a gift on condition to return. Nevertheless she is betrothed, because he gives her what is his, and he gives that thing to her as an absolute gift. See Perishah. So too it is proven in Terumat HaDeshen siman 210, in his incident where one betrothed a woman with a utensil that he had rented for her to adorn herself with two or three times, and he ruled that she is betrothed. Although it was like a gift on condition to return, because she had to return it to him after the adornment, he did not object on account of its return after the rental period; rather, certainly when he betroths her with what he has in the utensil, it is kiddushin and is not like a gift on condition to return. Terumat HaDeshen wrote there that if he betrothed her with the benefit of adornment for several days, he need not tell her that he borrowed the utensil, for he does not betroth her with the utensil but with the benefit of adornment for a certain time. But if he said, be betrothed with the body of a borrowed utensil, it is not kiddushin, for it is clear that his intent was to betroth her with the body of the utensil, and the utensil is not his. So wrote responsum Rosh there. According to this, one must say that the Rosh in the first chapter of Kiddushin speaks where he said plainly, be betrothed, and did not say, with the body of the utensil. One must also say that there he did not say, with the benefit of adornment; otherwise he would not need to inform her that it is borrowed. The Mechaber wrote plainly that if he did not inform her, she is doubtfully betrothed, implying that even if these four conditions exist it is only a doubt. At first glance this is difficult: from where does he know this, since we have not found such a distinction? One should not infer this from the Itur brought by the Tur, who wrote that because of doubt we do not act, for one may say the Itur speaks where he borrowed it without specifying a term; therefore it is doubtful, because he holds the lender can demand it immediately, as the Perishah and Bach wrote. But if he borrowed it for a fixed time, he agrees. Although the Rosh there in the first chapter of Kiddushin wrote at the end of his words that if he did not inform him that he borrows the utensil in order to betroth with it, it is not kiddushin, nevertheless the Rosh's words certainly do not contradict one another. Necessarily one must say that there he speaks where the above four conditions are absent. Even if you say that the Mechaber understood from the above Itur that if he did not inform her it is like a gift on condition to return, since she must return it at the end of the borrowing period, as the first explanation in the Perishah, nevertheless it appears that if he said explicitly, be betrothed to me with the benefit of adornment, it is kiddushin, for this benefit is not returned. The proof is from what is written in seif 9, he lent her 200 zuz, etc.; there too she has no benefit except that the money is in her hand for a certain time. With this, what is written in the gloss at the beginning of siman 29 in the name of Ri"u is also clear: if he said, with the benefit of adornment, it is kiddushin. So too Terumat HaDeshen ruled there plainly, unlike the Chelkat Mechokek.
