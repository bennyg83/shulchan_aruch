It is known what Choshen Mishpat wrote; but if at time of kiddushin etc. even if afterward known there was already despair — it is like despair without knowledge. What relevance does despair without knowledge have here? This applies only to lost objects: if it came to his hand before owners knew it was lost, even though when he learned it was lost they presumably despair — nevertheless considered despair without knowledge since it came to his domain before the loser despaired, as explained siman 262 Semag s.k. 9; there too implies even in lost objects if found thing though presumably at finding did not despair and later witnesses that at finding already despaired — he acquires. And Yeshuot Shlomo what is said on this does not refer at all to without knowledge of the taker but as Semag explains without knowledge of the loser; the essence depends on whether it came to his hand permissibly after despair or forbidden after despair. Therefore in my view Rambam's precision is only in this: if there was despair after kiddushin — change of domain and afterward despair; but if known afterward there was despair before kiddushin — what difference her knowing? He left Rambam's and Shulchan Aruch words in doubt. This is Tosafot R. Yehuda's language there: R. Shimon's view is not concerned — seems same for theft, for one view plain despair of owners; for braitot teach robbery and theft together implying equal law; in Sefer Ayelet wrote on them and I did not reach their intent. In my view perhaps they intended: since braitot teach robbery and theft and explain how regarding hers specifically — implies in others robbery and theft law equal — not betrothed plainly; since in robbery even stringently we do not concern R. Shimon — likewise plain theft not betrothed even stringently. This too may be Rambam's intent though he rules chapter 6 of Hagahot plain theft is despair — perhaps holds for kiddushin because whoever betroths etc. they annulled kiddushin so as not to come to doubts of plain cases and established law on its basis: with clear despair certainly betrothed and plainly not betrothed. Thus can rescue Tur Choshen Mishpat 368 who understood his father's view that even plain Israel robbery is despair; they challenged from Rava who said R. Shimon not concerned even Israel.
