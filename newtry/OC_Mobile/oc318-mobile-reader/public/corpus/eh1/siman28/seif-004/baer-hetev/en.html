To me with it etc. See Rashakh 1:9; Mahari"t 1:57 — two witnesses one says said behold betrothed with it other says not — see there.
The kiddushin, etc. At first glance it implies that this law applies specifically where he first said to her, "Take this deposit," and afterward threw it into her lap. But from the words of the Ramah it appears that it applies to anyone who throws kiddushin into her lap without prior matchmaking and without disclosure on her part that she wants kiddushin: she is not betrothed. Chelkat Mechokek, Beit Shmuel; so too Kenesset HaGedolah 57a and Derishah. What Bach wrote about this was disputed by Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel. The author of Nachalat Tzvi wrote things on this that are difficult for one who examines the sugya to accept; see there. Reuven entered Leah's house, threw a ring onto the hem of her garment, and said to her, "Take this ring and see that I give it to you for kiddushin," and she took the ring from the hem of her garment into her hand: it is as though she said yes, and she is betrothed. Rashdam, Even HaEzer, siman 77. Mahar"י Adarbi, siman 289, disagrees.
Betrothed. But engaged does not help here; see Beit Shmuel; all who did not say yes explicitly but answered words not implying yes — certainly not betrothed.
