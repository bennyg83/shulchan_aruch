Specifically where she does not know. This is the Tur's language in the name of the Rosh. The Perishah writes that although the Tur began with both a deposit and a borrowed object, this final rule applies specifically to a deposit. With a borrowed object, he says, even if she knew the amount at first and at the time of kiddushin did not know it had been stolen, she is nevertheless betrothed if a perutah remains, because as a borrower she is obligated to pay for the stolen amount. The amount she owes is a loan, and the remaining perutah is a perutah, so this is a case of kiddushin with a loan and a perutah, where she is betrothed. My father-in-law wrote similarly. I am astonished that these great scholars did not attend to the plain sense. The Gemara itself speaks of one who betroths with a deposit in her hand, and it is speaking of a deposit for which she bears responsibility, as Rashi explicitly says: even though she accepted liability, if nothing remains she is not betrothed. On that very case the Rosh writes that if she knows the amount of the deposit, the remaining perutah does not help. This is therefore just like a borrowed object. It seems clear that the Tur mentions borrowing precisely to teach that the deposit in the Gemara is one for which she is liable, like a borrowed object. There is no ordinary rule of "loan and perutah" here. Since, according to the Rosh, his intent was specifically for the entire amount of the deposit, the whole amount is included in the kiddushin. Therefore, if part of it is a loan, the remaining part does not help. This is also stated in the gloss later in this siman. If at the time of kiddushin he explicitly says, "Be betrothed to me with this loan and this perutah," we say she is betrothed with the perutah, because the ineffective loan language is disregarded and the kiddushin rests on the perutah. Here, by contrast, she thought the whole deposit was still intact, and the entire sum was the object of the kiddushin. Once part of that sum is only a debt, it is as though the whole matter is a loan. Therefore, if part was lost, even though a perutah remains, it does not help in a deposit for which she is liable, just as with a borrowed object, because the lost portion is included in the kiddushin as a loan. The reason the final clause mentions only a deposit is that the Gemara itself mentions only a deposit; the Rosh revealed that borrowing is the same because the deposit in the Gemara bears liability. In both cases there is a distinction between whether she knew the amount of the deposit or did not know it.
