Some say that where we would have had to suspect that he committed a prohibition, etc., such as where he sent sivlonot on Shabbat. See Magen Avraham siman 306 s.k. 15, who concludes: if so, what is practiced, giving utensils as a gift to a groom who lectures, requires study, etc. According to his words, the sending, aside from kiddushin, involves a Shabbat prohibition; if so, here the question returns: what proof is there that he did not betroth? This is the language of the Magen Avraham there: the Beit Yosef in siman 527 wrote that the Mordekhai holds it is forbidden to give something as a gift to one's fellow on Yom Tov or Shabbat unless it is for the need of a mitzvah, etc. So too it appears in Eruvin 71, and so wrote the Ritva; and it is written regarding, they seized movable items, etc. He concludes that even a pledge is apparently forbidden, and further, hekdesh is like a gift and is forbidden. So too it appears in the Rif, chapter 5 of Yom Tov, and in the Rambam at the end of Hilkhot Mekhirah. If so, the above requires study. I am astonished at his view, for the Ran contradicts himself. At the beginning of Ketubot he also asks the Ritva's question, yet in Beitzah chapter 2, brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 527 before the above Mordekhai, the Ran explained that it means to transfer ownership through the language of gift without a sudar, but like other movable items acquired by meshikhah. Because of this the Beit Yosef wrote about the Mordekhai: I do not understand his words, for if it is by language of gift without a sudar, why specifically because of a mitzvah? Even without a mitzvah, why should it be forbidden? In the end he wrote, perhaps, etc.; see there. Thus the Ran necessarily distinguishes between giving a pledge, which is included in the decrees of commerce, and a gift through meshikhah without kinyan sudar, which is certainly permitted. A pledge is different, because he gives it for a loan and it more closely resembles commerce. If so, what are all these bundles of proofs that he brings for the Mordekhai from the Ritva and Eruvin? The Ran himself also wrote the words of the Ritva and the proof from Eruvin, and necessarily he distinguishes between transferring land, which requires some kinyan, even chazakah, and meshikhah of movable items, where it does not appear at all that he acts for the sake of acquisition, and is permitted. In my humble opinion, it is explained like the Ran in Orach Chayyim siman 516, where we rule that anything one benefits from on a weekday may be sent on Yom Tov, provided it does not need repair; even tefillin, which according to most poskim are forbidden on Yom Tov, and all the more utensils and jewelry, are permitted, as explained in Hagahot HaRosh. But according to the Mordekhai and Magen Avraham it should be forbidden to give any gift on Yom Tov except food needs. The clear point is that what is permitted on Yom Tov is likewise permitted on Shabbat, for the decrees of commerce are equal on Shabbat and Yom Tov, as explained in Perek HaMashilin in the Rif cited by the Magen Avraham. There the law of sending refers to a gift. Even wheat and grain that are forbidden: the Rambam in his commentary to the Mishnah wrote the reason, that it appears like weekday conduct, and in the halakhot too he gave this reason, as is clear from his language at the beginning of the chapter. We learn that giving movable-item gifts through meshikhah is not included in the decree of commerce. This is compelled by the law of tefillin and also by the language, one may not send, which means through an agent; with grain and unfinished utensils this appears like weekday conduct, but to give hand to hand implies there is no prohibition. The Rambam's language in chapter 23 of Hilkhot Shabbat, on the contrary, indicates that he forbids only sale. From his language at the end of Hilkhot Mekhirah, and likewise the Rif's language in Perek Mashilin, there is no proof, although they wrote, or one who gives, for one may say that there they speak of giving land, which is impossible without kinyan, or movable items by kinyan sudar. Even the Mordekhai, in my humble opinion, agrees with the Ran and wrote only for completeness; and even if he disagrees, in my humble opinion the Ran's words are compelled. Be that as it may, what the Magen Avraham wrote, that the custom requires study, is in my humble opinion an explicit Mishnah permitting it, as explained in the above Hagahot HaRosh, that it is permitted on Yom Tov, and the same applies on Shabbat. This is the main view, and so it implies here. Incidentally I will mention what is difficult to me at first glance on the above matter regarding the Taz in siman 516, who wrote that even things designated for animals, if one does not take them from private domain to public domain, are permitted; so too wrote Magen Avraham s.k. 1. According to this, it is permitted to send them in a place that is one domain for Shabbat. I do not know from where they derived this. Granted, the Beit Yosef properly ruled that what is permitted is permitted even from private to public domain, as stated in Yam Shel Shlomo siman 30. But regarding what is forbidden, where the reason is as above, that reason applies even in a place that is one domain for Shabbat, and this requires study. All the more according to the Magen Avraham there is a prohibition in giving a gift because of commerce, but in my humble opinion this is not so. The Beit Shmuel wrote: the same applies if he married a woman, etc. Although above the Rama wrote that after the act they are not believed, even with an oath, he wrote this because it is practically relevant for us according to the Rama z"l's conclusion at the end of seif 2.
