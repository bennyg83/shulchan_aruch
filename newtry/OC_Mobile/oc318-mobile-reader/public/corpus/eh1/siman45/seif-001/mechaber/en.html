Law: we are concerned for gifts (savlonot) and for ketubah. It contains 3 seifim. In a place where the custom is to send gifts to the fiancée after she becomes engaged, and witnesses came who saw gifts brought to her — we are concerned perhaps she was betrothed and needs a get out of doubt — even though most people of the city do not send gifts except before kiddushin; even though he did not send the gifts before witnesses (Tur and so Ran chapter 2 of Kiddushin). And in a place where all customarily send gifts first and afterward betroth (even though sometimes by chance they send after kiddushin since they are not particular about this) (there in Ran) — and they saw gifts — we are not concerned for her. And Rashi explained the concern for gifts is perhaps the gifts themselves were given as kiddushin. {Rama: Therefore even if witnesses say he did not yet betroth her we are concerned perhaps the gifts were for kiddushin; and one should be concerned for both explanations to be stringent (Tur). And all this when he sends plainly; but if he explicitly states he sends for gifts — there is no concern per Rashi (Ran chapter 2 of Kiddushin). And all the more if he said he sends as a mere present; even if he said the groom sent to the bride (Maharik root 171). And all this discusses when matchmaking preceded; but without matchmaking — no concern per all (Rashba 1023). And wherever we are concerned, even if both say they did not intend for kiddushin (Ran and Maharik root 25) and swore to this — they are not believed (Terumat HaDeshen siman 207). Some say any gifts sent immediately after matchmaking — a day or two — we are not concerned at all; and even if he sent again afterward, since he sent first we see he gives gifts and afterward betroths and we are not concerned for gifts (Maharik root 171). And all concern for gifts is only to require a get ab initio; but if she already married she does not leave and we follow doubt leniently (there). Some say if he did not send gifts before witnesses we are not concerned per Rashi that the gifts themselves are kiddushin; and even per one who is concerned when betrothing before one witness — for gifts there is no concern (Maharik roots 28, 171). And some are stringent if he sent before witnesses even though witnesses were not present at giving to the woman (Terumat HaDeshen siman 207). Some say all this discusses when the bride herself received the gifts; but where brides customarily do not receive gifts themselves but another receives for them — we are not concerned (Maharik root 171). And some say we do not distinguish whether he sent gifts or the groom gives to her himself — and this is primary (responsum Rashba 180, 189). And not like one who was stringent that where the groom himself gives to her we are concerned in every matter (responsum Maharash). Some say where we would be concerned — if his intent was to betroth through sending gifts — such as he sent on Shabbat (Maharik root 171) or there is a ban in the city not to betroth except with ten men (Beit Yosef in name of Ritva) — we are not concerned the gifts are kiddushin. The groom did not send to her himself — only the groom's father sent — we are not concerned per Rashi perhaps the gifts themselves are kiddushin even if the agent said the groom sent to you. And all this when we know certainly about sending gifts; but for rumor of gifts we are not concerned so much (Piskei Maharai siman 226).}
