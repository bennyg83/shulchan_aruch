Some say that every, etc. From what is stated in Ketubot 23a: one might have said that if it were true that he betrothed her, etc.; therefore it teaches, etc. The reason is that he fears they will not leave them alone, as stated in Kiddushin 50a, "and it was necessary, etc., in that place, etc." This is unlike such a case, where he arranged the match publicly; why would he betroth her secretly? All the more we do not suspect that he may have betrothed her before the matchmaking, perhaps arranging the match afterward. This follows the explanation of the Tosafot cited above and of the Geonim; see Maharik there, section 170.
A concern, etc. From what is stated there: one master said, "shall we not be concerned for the minority?" and the other said, "follow the majority," as in every place. Certainly we follow the majority, and all the more here, where there is also a presumption with it, the presumption that she is unmarried. Even Rabbi Meir does not suspect in such a case, as stated in Yevamot 119b. Rather, it is only a mere stringency because of the severity of a married woman, like waters with no end. As stated there, if she married she need not leave; so too here. We are also lenient in doubt, since it is only a mere concern. See the Rosh on Kiddushin there and Tosafot Bekhorot 20b, s.v. "milk." But the Rashba and Rabbeinu Tam wrote that here the reason is that in a matter dependent on custom we do not follow the majority, lest he be from the minority. Nevertheless, his words are true, etc.
