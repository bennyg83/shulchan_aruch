Although the majority, etc. The Beit Shmuel wrote: the reason we are concerned here for the minority, the Rosh wrote, is because of the severity of a married woman, like one who drowned in water that has no end. This requires study. It is not similar. There, the minority joins the presumption that she is a married woman; here, the presumption that she is unmarried supports the majority, and it is a minority of a minority, for which even Rabbi Meir is not concerned. Maharik also wonders about this. Were it not for his holy words, I would say that this is the reason: because the majority does not clarify the lenient side in the same way that the minority clarifies the stringent side. Meaning: if he is from the minority, it is necessary that what he sent was either for kiddushin or because he had already betrothed her. But regarding the majority who send sivlonot and afterward betroth, this works only if they are particular to send sivlonot specifically first. But this is not so; rather, they are not particular and may also send first. Therefore a doubt still remains: perhaps he nevertheless sent it for kiddushin, or perhaps he had already betrothed her. Therefore we are stringent. See something similar in Tosafot, Ketubot 27b, s.v. kan. Gloss: although he did not send the sivlonot with witnesses. See Chelkat Mechokek. In my humble opinion, the Rambam, whose language is the language of the Shulchan Aruch, also holds the explanation of Rabbenu Chananel, and nevertheless is precise and writes, witnesses came who saw the sivlonot. We learn that he holds that where the parties themselves admit, they are believed by migo. The Rama z"l did not write this in language of dispute, which shows not as the Chelkat Mechokek z"l wrote. Rather, he teaches us that although he did not send with witnesses, meaning that he did not appoint witnesses for the agent, it is also possible that the witnesses themselves saw the agent on their own and he did not see them. In such a case, according to Rashi's view, there is certainly no concern, as explained in siman 42 seif 3. This is exactly the Rambam's intent: witnesses came, etc., while the sender did not see them. There is no need for the Chelkat Mechokek's words, that it speaks where there were no witnesses at the time of giving, but there were witnesses at the time of sending. Apart from his difficulty, the language, although he did not send, does not indicate this. What Terumat HaDeshen brings, that one who claims contrary to the custom is not believed even with a migo: it is true that he initially wrote this, but afterward he brings a responsum of Or Zarua, who wrote explicitly that even according to Rabbenu Chananel's explanation he is believed. In my humble opinion this is compelled from the Talmud. For Rabbenu Chananel's version is that Rav Pappa teaches that even in a place where the majority send sivlonot and afterward betroth, we are concerned. Thus Rav Pappa's words are explained as follows: when he says, in a place where they betroth and afterward send sivlonot, we are concerned, this refers to the minority and certainly to the majority; and when he says, they send sivlonot and afterward betroth, we are not concerned, this means all of them. But where the minority betroth first, etc., it is obvious that he is believed with a migo, for one cannot say that he claims contrary to the custom. Necessarily, this case involves witnesses. Consequently, everything included in his statement, they betroth, etc., we are concerned, even where the majority betroth first, is also specifically with witnesses. It is not similar to claiming contrary to a custom, which is a custom of everyone, unlike here where it is only a custom of the majority. See also the conclusion of Terumat HaDeshen, that even according to Rabbenu Chananel's explanation it is only a concern. Therefore it appears to me that he retracted from his opinion in this explanation. The Rama z"l too intended only what I wrote. The Beit Shmuel z"l pressed himself to explain it by means of a retroactive migo, but this is not correct. Also what he brings in the name of the Taz, to distinguish between one place and another place, in my humble opinion there is no such distinction in Rav Pappa's words. In the responsum of the Rama z"l too it implies that Terumat HaDeshen ultimately retracted. The main point in my humble opinion is what the Rama wrote nearby: wherever we are concerned, meaning all cases where they have no migo. He relied on the beginning of the words of the Shulchan Aruch, witnesses came, etc. The words of the Tashbetz, which the Darkhei Moshe found astonishing, are properly settled by the Taz according to the above. I will copy them. It is in part 1 siman 42: A question concerning one who sent sivlonot after shiddukhin and did not bring witnesses to the matter, and claimed that he sent them as kiddushin, while she said that she did not receive them as kiddushin but according to the custom of the city. About this he ruled that according to Rashi's explanation there is no concern for two reasons: one, it is a place where they send sivlonot first; two, it was without witnesses, etc. And there is no concern that before he sent the sivlonot he betrothed her, according to Rabbenu Chananel's explanation, because this is not the custom of the place, and he does not claim so. Rather, he says that he sent the sivlonot as kiddushin, and does not claim that he betrothed her and afterward sent them. If so, whether according to Rashi or according to Rabbenu Chananel, this woman is not betrothed at all. End quote. Without doubt the Beit Yosef intended this, only he abbreviated in copying it. According to this, the Darkhei Moshe's question is not difficult at all, and this is clear. One must be concerned for both explanations. The Beit Shmuel brings in the name of Rivash that according to Rashi z"l we are not stringent to be concerned for the minority, and the Darkhei Moshe disagrees. In my humble opinion, this is the Rivash's reason: according to Rashi, there is necessarily no place for Rabbenu Chananel's version, for according to his explanation the concern is that he betrothed her before witnesses, and according to him it must be that even if he and she say that they did not intend kiddushin, they are not believed. For if they were believed, even their admission would be nothing, since it would be like kiddushin without witnesses; and immediately when she received the sivlonot she became betrothed by force of the proof. This works where the majority betroth first, but where the majority send sivlonot first, how could it enter one's mind that because of the stringency of a married woman, where we are concerned that she is from the minority, her receiving should be considered kiddushin? By what Torah law should the witnesses think of kiddushin where the majority send sivlonot first? According to Rabbenu Chananel this is understandable; according to the Darkhei Moshe it requires study. All this is where he sent to her plainly. See Beit Shmuel. It is true that this is the language of Mahari Levi. Also, one who sends a chicken for kapparah is not using language of sivlonot and not language of affection, and in this there is no manner of concern for kiddushin, as Maharik wrote in root 170, that we are concerned only where he mentioned some language of affection. He brings proof from the words of Maharam. But one who studies Maharik will find explicitly the opposite. This is his language: moreover, it appears to me that Rashi disagrees only where he gives plainly, or where he says it is out of love and affection or the like. Granted, when he gives plainly, one may say that he relied on the shiddukhin, that he had already appeased her to become betrothed to him, and therefore he did not explain. All the more so where he used language of love and affection. But when he gives it merely as a gift, he reveals his intent that it is not for kiddushin but as a gift. Thus explicitly, where it is plain we are concerned. What he later brings as proof from Maharam is regarding a case where there was speech; not every expression is fit for concern, and then we require speech that relates to affection. So he concludes: rather, it is obvious that Rabbenu Meir holds that one must say language implying some closeness, and ordinary language of gift does not suffice. Thus he excluded from Maharam's proof only language of gift, but with plain giving we certainly are concerned. This is as I proved in siman 27 from the end of Maharam's words, that what he was stringent about in silence was also because of sivlonot; see there. The Beit Shmuel should not have copied these words of Mahari Levi z"l, which came only incidentally and without all the necessary study. Accordingly, what he wrote, according to this, is not compelled.
