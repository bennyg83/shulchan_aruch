(ב) And likewise she should not strengthen its repair. In the Gemara it says: what if she repaired it? Teiku. Tosafot explain that "repaired" is a noun, meaning the act of repair. They hold this is a leniency for the defendant, namely the heirs. At first glance this is difficult according to the Rosh, who holds that a widow who delayed and did not claim sustenance for two years has not lost it unless she is poor and promiscuous, as written in the previous siman, because he holds that the assets are in her possession. If so, here too, with an unresolved question, she should not lose. It is forced to distinguish that regarding sustenance the assets are in her possession but not regarding residence; yet this is strained. Rather, one must say that here, in any event, they give her another residence, and therefore we rule leniently for the heirs. However, the Tur is difficult, for he wrote that even according to the first view, that they are not obligated to give her a residence, nevertheless she should not strengthen its repair. Shiltei Gibborim ruled that if she took possession, the heirs cannot prevent her. If she took possession, it implies from Tosafot and Rambam that she is not removed from the house, for the Gemara's question concerns ab initio whether she may waive, unlike Rashi.
