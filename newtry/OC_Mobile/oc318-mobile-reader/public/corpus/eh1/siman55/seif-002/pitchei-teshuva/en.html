The groom to his house. See Ba'er Heitev. What he brought first as the view of the Beit Shmuel and afterward as the view of the Chelkat Mechokek was not proper, because the Beit Shmuel brings the words of the Chelkat Mechokek and disagrees with him here, s.k. 4, and below, siman 64, s.k. 6. So too Magen Avraham, Orach Chaim, siman 339, s.k. 11, disagrees with the Chelkat Mechokek. However, see Beit Meir, who lengthens to uphold the Chelkat Mechokek and concludes that one who relies on him has not lost. So too Tosafot Shabbat cites Solet LaMinchah that he upheld the Chelkat Mechokek; the Taz in simanim 57 and 64 also writes exactly like the Chelkat Mechokek and adds that even if the wedding is in the bride’s father’s house, it is not considered her house, since the father has already given that room to the groom, even by loan, and it is like a sale. Since the groom goes with the bride from the chuppah to the place of nisuin, he acquires by bringing her into his domain. The Beit Shmuel cites this briefly and concludes that it does not appear so. The annotator on the Taz rejects the Beit Shmuel and upholds the Taz and Chelkat Mechokek. See also Biur HaGra, siman 64, s.k. 11. Nevertheless it is proper to be stringent like Beit Shmuel, Magen Avraham, and other great later authorities who forbid even in such a case, especially since there is a danger concern, as Magen Avraham writes in the name of Maharil. It implies from Beit Shmuel that the primary law follows those who say that the chuppah of a virgin does not require seclusion fit for relations, whereas for a widow seclusion fit for relations is sufficient. This is clear in siman 64 and unlike Ba'er Heitev there, who copied in his name that actual relations are required. Therefore, when Beit Shmuel later disputes the Bach about the meal after the chuppah and says there should be complete seclusion, his intent is only that since the Bach wrote that our custom is to satisfy all opinions, it is proper to be concerned for the opinion that even a virgin’s chuppah requires seclusion fit for relations. But Beit Shmuel agrees this is not indispensable. So too the Siddur of the Gaon of Lissa writes that the main chuppah is not standing under the cloth on poles, but the later seclusion in a room. Even if she is not fit for relations, such as if she is a niddah, or people enter and leave the room, it is nevertheless a complete chuppah for a virgin if he designated that room for the groom. But for a widow, chuppah acquires only through seclusion fit for relations, meaning she is tahor and they enter a special room with no people there. Therefore great care is needed when making a chuppah on Friday afternoon that it be done well before day. See Sha'ar HaMelech, Hilchot Ishut, and Biur HaGra.
