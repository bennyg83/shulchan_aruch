Section 1 in the gloss, "that the groom brought her into his house." The Ran wrote that from the verse "if she vowed in her husband's house" it is clear that chuppah means bringing her into his house. Perhaps, just as the decisors wrote that a rented courtyard does not acquire for him, see the Shakh in Choshen Mishpat 313, and it acquires only for the lessor, so too regarding chuppah, which is "her husband's house," it must be his house permanently, not merely by rental or borrowing. Or perhaps the form of chuppah is not like courtyard acquisition, and rental is effective like a full sale. This requires analysis for now. With a time-limited sale, as in Choshen Mishpat 212, perhaps all agree that the buyer acquires courtyard rights, and the same would apply to chuppah; all the more with a gift on condition to return, which is almost stronger than a time-limited sale. This still requires careful analysis.
