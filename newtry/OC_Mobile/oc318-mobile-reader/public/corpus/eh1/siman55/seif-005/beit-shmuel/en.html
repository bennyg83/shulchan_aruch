He is not, etc. Although she is acquired to him, nevertheless regarding becoming tamei, Scripture says "except for his close relative," and an arusah is not called his close relative.
She is not obligated to become tamei. But there is no prohibition, for we hold that the sons of Aharon are warned against tumah to the dead, not the daughters of Aharon. See Yoreh Deah, siman 373. Rashi in Yevamot 29 wrote that on a festival she is forbidden to become tamei, because a person is obligated to purify himself on a festival.
In a place where he did not acquire the dowry. This means where he inherits nothing, even nichsei melog. See Responsa Maharil, siman 80. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that in the second year after marriage, where half is returned, burial expenses fall on both parties. This does not appear correct, for with a shomeret yavam the yavam also divides with the heirs, and nevertheless the yavam is obligated in burial, as written in siman 160. All the more so the husband, where he inherits even half of the nichsei melog, is alone obligated in burial expenses. So it also implies in Responsa Maharil, siman 88, as I wrote in siman 118.
