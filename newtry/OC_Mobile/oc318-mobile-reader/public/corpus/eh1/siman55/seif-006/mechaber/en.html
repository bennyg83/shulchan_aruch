One who betroths a woman and wrote her a ketubah and did not enter her to the chuppah — she is still betrothed and not married, for the ketubah does not effect marriage; and if he died or divorced her she collects the principal of the ketubah from free property and does not collect additions at all since he did not enter. But if he betrothed a woman and did not write her a ketubah and died or divorced her while she was betrothed — she has nothing, not even the principal, for they did not institute for her principal ketubah until she marries or until he writes. {Rama: And some say a betrothed woman has a ketubah — Rosh and Ran and Tur (but the custom follows the first view).}
