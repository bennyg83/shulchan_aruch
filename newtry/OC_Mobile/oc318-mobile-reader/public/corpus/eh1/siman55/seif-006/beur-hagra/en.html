And some say, etc. See the Rosh on Ketubot there, Rashi there s.v. "and collects," and Tosafot Bava Metzia 17b s.v. "from." This is the principal halakhah, for their proof is very compelling. It seems to me there is another proof from what is stated in the fifteenth chapter of Yevamot, "one betrothed, etc." Even the Rambam's explanation that it speaks where he wrote a ketubah for one woman and it was lost is not correct. First, the Mishnah does not mention a ketubah claim at all; and when Rabbi Akiva says "a bill of divorce and a ketubah," the mention of the bill of divorce is otherwise superfluous, unless the bill of divorce removes the ketubah claim. Further, the Gemara says "it taught betrothed," etc., and if the prohibition of betrothal is unrelated to ketubah it is not comparable to theft. Nor is there need to speak of a rabbinic prohibition when a Torah prohibition is already present. Certainly all the sugyot prove that she has a ketubah, as stated in Kiddushin 66a and many other places. In the first chapter of Bava Metzia they ask from where we know that she has a ketubah, and although it is unresolved how we know it, certainly she has a ketubah. Still, the custom recorded by the Rav is also correct, as the Ra'avad wrote: specifically in their times they relied on the court's condition, and even in places where they wrote ketubot, some wrote them for an arusah too; this is unlike the present. See the Mordechai at the beginning of chapter 2 of Ketubot.
