She is not obligated to become tamei for him. The Beit Shmuel wrote that there is no prohibition, because we hold that the sons of Aharon are warned, not the daughters of Aharon; but Rashi in Yevamot 29 wrote that on a festival she is forbidden to become tamei because one is obligated to purify himself on the festival. This implies he understood Rashi to apply even nowadays. But the Tur and Shulchan Aruch say only "she is not obligated," implying that Rashi’s words apply only in Temple times. The Ba'er Heitev in Yoreh Deah, siman 373, brought in the name of Beit Hillel that the decisors omitted this because it applies only in Temple times. So too Responsa Sha'agat Aryeh, siman 65, and Yad Eliyahu, siman 68, write that the obligation to purify oneself on a festival does not apply nowadays. It follows that the Beit Shmuel’s words in the name of Rashi do not apply in practice nowadays. See also Korban Netanel, end of Yoma, who disagrees and writes that the obligation continued after the destruction while they still had ashes of the parah.
