But if there are witnesses that he secluded himself with her after he became an adult, she requires a get. In Sefer Torat Yekutiel, may his lamp shine, there is a question: one who married a woman while he was a minor, and after some time she left him without a get because they thought he was still a minor, and she married another man and already bore children from the second husband. Afterward it became known that when the first husband became an adult he lived with her in one house for about half a year. We do not have actual witnesses to seclusion before us, and she says that she did not seclude herself with him at all. What is her law? I am astonished if such a case arose in our day. He discussed it at length, and on a partial review I saw that the core of his words is built on his forcing from Responsa Rosh, klal 35, according to the second explanation, that when we say these are the witnesses to seclusion and these are the witnesses to intercourse, it is not because she certainly had intercourse in that seclusion. Rather, in kiddushin by intercourse the witnesses need not see the intercourse itself; once they saw that they secluded themselves, that is enough, and if she had intercourse in that seclusion it is as though she had intercourse in the presence of witnesses, even though it is not certain that she had intercourse in that seclusion. One may say the reason is like what we rule regarding adulterers, that the witnesses need not see like a brush in a tube, but only that they saw them acting in the manner of adulterers. Therefore one may say that in kiddushin by intercourse the witnesses need not see the intercourse itself; witnesses to seclusion suffice, as written in one responsum of Hagahot Maimoniyot to Hilkhot Ishut and in Mordechai at the end of HaMegareish. Therefore Beit Hillel holds that we say these are they, meaning the witnesses need not see that she had intercourse; once they saw that the couple secluded themselves, that suffices, and if she had intercourse in that seclusion, it is as though she had intercourse before witnesses. According to this, the reason that from betrothal alone she does not require a get is that since they are not familiar with one another we do not suspect that she had intercourse; but if we know she had intercourse, such as where witnesses saw in the above manner, or where she admits as above, then even from betrothal she requires a get, because through them it is like intercourse before witnesses even if it is not certain that she had intercourse. The reason is as above, that witnesses to kiddushin by intercourse need not see the intercourse but witnesses to seclusion suffice. End quote. In my humble opinion this explanation does not accord with the principle taken by the poskim many times, especially Responsa Rashba siman 1193 and Ritva, whom I brought in siman 42: witnesses to kiddushin are a Scriptural decree and must be witnesses whose testimony is effective even against denial. But witnesses whom she is believed to contradict are nothing even with her admission, and it is like kiddushin without witnesses. At minimum it must be close to certain in such a way that even if she contradicted them she would still be at least doubtfully betrothed. From this it follows that according to Responsa Maimoniyot and the Ra'ah whom he cited, and they are also in Beit Shmuel siman 31, where a man and woman lived together in the manner of husband and wife she is doubtfully betrothed, it must be that her denial is nothing and she is presumed at least possibly to have had intercourse; and this is the truth. As for the proof he brings for this explanation from Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai, I did not find it in their words. On the contrary, they wrote that it is because they are like fire in tow, meaning that there is a presumption of intercourse through this. What he wrote further, that one cannot see like a brush in a tube, I already wrote in siman 42 that this proves that the essence of kiddushin by intercourse is through sight close to certainty, not literal sight, which is impossible. One who examines will see that his main reliance for this explanation is from Responsa Rosh as cited in Beit Yosef in this siman. He asks: how can the Rosh bring proof that where a minor betrothed and, after he became an adult, secluded himself with her, she needs a get, from what is stated in Perek Na'arah Shenitpatet, that if he had a courtyard on the road and she entered with him, since we hold that an unspecified courtyard of his is for marriage, her husband inherits her. This shows that when they travel on the road and enter a courtyard together, the seclusion is considered like intercourse and she is his wife. All the more here, when they entered a hidden place for the sake of seclusion, the seclusion should be considered like intercourse, she should be like his wife, and she should need a get. These words are difficult to understand. If his intent is that seclusion alone makes her like his wife even if she did not have intercourse, as his language seems to imply when he wrote that the seclusion is considered like intercourse, then there, in truth, we do not require that she had intercourse; since she secluded herself for marriage she is like a married woman, as stated there and as Rashi explicitly wrote, and this is obvious. But here this has no sense: how can seclusion alone suffice when the kiddushin performed while he was a minor was nothing? What does seclusion help? There, the case is different because she had been betrothed, but her status was like an arusah until she entered the chuppah; the Mishnah teaches that if she secluded herself she is like a nesu'ah, and we do not require that he had intercourse. Here, however, where she was not betrothed, what does seclusion help? If he means that because of seclusion she is like his wife because we say he had intercourse, what proof is there from there? There we do not require intercourse; through that seclusion she becomes a nesu'ah, as above. He therefore explains in a distant manner, as he himself admits, based on the second explanation, which in my humble opinion is not correct. Still, the question requires study. It appears to me to resolve it based on what the Rosh and Tur concluded in siman 27, that one should rule like Rav Huna that chuppah effects acquisition and she requires a get. Further, in siman 61 the Tur writes, what is chuppah, etc., and these are the Rambam's words; in siman 62 he writes in the name of the Rosh that one must be precise about what is called chuppah, etc., but the main residence of bridegroom and bride is called chuppah, etc. See Beit Yosef there, for the Rosh is somewhat uncertain what the name chuppah means. If so, the plain explanation is that this woman requires a get whichever way you look at it. Since there is some doubt about the meaning of chuppah, and especially according to his premise that a place made for temporary use, like his courtyard on the road, is not a full chuppah but only like chuppah, as also appears from Tosafot Yevamot 89b, s.v. mishetagdil, the Rosh states the matter as whichever way you look at it: if it works as chuppah, and seclusion is chuppah, then she requires a get according to Rav Huna who holds that chuppah effects acquisition. And if it is not called chuppah, then necessarily after kiddushin we say these are the witnesses to seclusion and these are the witnesses to intercourse, as the Ran wrote based on the Rif; from this it is clear that intercourse for the sake of marriage after kiddushin certainly makes her a nesu'ah. So too here, for this purpose it is like seclusion after kiddushin: we say these are they, and she became betrothed after he became an adult, so she requires a get. See, however, siman 55. Regarding the actual case, he concludes there: provided that they agree with him, etc. It seems likely to me that they did not agree and required a get, for otherwise he would not have remained silent from telling us such a novelty. And rightly so, for in our poverty she requires a get. However, regarding the children from the second husband, the opposing authorities are certainly sufficient to say that since there were no definite witnesses to seclusion, the offspring are valid. Moreover, without doubt there was another uncertainty there: whether at the time she left the first husband's house he was already an adult with signs, which is the essential point for kiddushin to take hold, as stated in Bava Batra 156. See Beit Shmuel siman 2 s.k. 16 and siman 4 at the beginning of seif 26. In my humble opinion, this law regarding the get should not be compared to the rule in Yevamot that if she has children she does not leave, for several reasons. See siman 11 and examine carefully. The Beit Shmuel, s.k. 1, wrote that the reason it does not help when he grew up and did not seclude himself, unlike with a minor girl, is that with a minor girl there were rabbinic nisu'in, unlike with a minor boy. See siman 37 seif 14 in the gloss, where it says that he may detain her until she grows up and the kiddushin is completed. There it refers to the second opinion, according to which the first kiddushin had no kiddushin at all. The Beit Shmuel there wrote that this is when he had intercourse only because the father objected before she grew up; but if he did not come and object, and she grew up with him, the kiddushin is completed. It is implied there in the Tur that even with a minor girl for whom nisu'in were not instituted, the law is so even if he did not have intercourse after she grew up. The main distinction is explained in Maharik, shoresh 30: with regard to the man, who performs the act of kiddushin and acquires her, one cannot say that by consent and silence upon adulthood she becomes betrothed to him, since the kiddushin did not take effect at the time, for he must perform an act of kiddushin as it says, when a man takes, and as Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of Kiddushin. Therefore his silence after adulthood does not help, for in the essential kiddushin what does his consent and silence accomplish? If she gave the money and she made the declaration, even if he is silent and accepts the kiddushin, it does not help; even if he gave and she made the declaration, she is betrothed only rabbinically. But a woman, who transfers herself by consent without any statement, when she grows up the kiddushin becomes kiddushin through consent and silence. End quote. Regarding a minor boy who betrothed and stipulated that the kiddushin should not take effect until he becomes an adult, see Mishneh LaMelekh, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Ishut, halakhah 7. See Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 1, who wrote about the Bach that he found no hint of proof for his view; see siman 1, where I too briefly challenged it from Mordechai. However, in this siman in the Beit Yosef, in the name of Maharik and the poskim, there is something almost like his view, for he wrote that a minor of eleven years whom his father betrothed to a woman was required to give a get. In Maharik there is one reason: we find everywhere that one may acquire merit for a person not in his presence, but the poskim wondered at it. The Beit Shmuel in siman 1 proved against the Bach from Tosafot in Perek HaNisrafin: according to the Bach, what is the difficulty, perhaps his father married him off to two? According to the poskim cited above who required a get, one could say that they truly answer Tosafot's question this way, and she requires a get either like the Bach rabbinically, or for their reason. If so, at first glance it would be proper to be stringent like them. But see Responsa Maharit, Even HaEzer part 2 siman 41, where he discusses this at length; there you will find a refutation of the Bach's proofs from siman 141 from the law of R. Peretz, for he explains that it speaks of rabbinic marriage by his father. In that case, just as he can divorce by himself while he has not yet been checked because in rabbinic matters we rely on Rava's presumption, so too regarding agency. Also what was established in siman 141 as a minor who had intercourse with his yevamah is refuted there. See there, for nevertheless from the conclusion in Bava Batra 156 it is clearly proven that there is certainly no concern for Torah-level kiddushin, since it did not answer that it was kiddushin by his father; and from Gittin 55 it is explicit that he has no rabbinic marriage in any way. See Rashi there, s.v. pikachat. Parenthetically, I will explain here that the words of Tosafot in Bava Batra there, s.v. vechi me'achar, in the middle where they write and if it was doubtful, in my humble opinion do not follow the context and are a scribal error; they belong in the middle of the comment s.v. bodekin, after they conclude as stated in Perek Ben Sorer. One should read there: it is forced to say that we check for kiddushin, etc., until the end, that this is not according to halakhah. In the comment vechi me'achar, the end of the comment is he was an adult at the time of divorce, and all the words from and if it was doubtful until it is forced are extra. If you do not wish to erase them, one must press and say that they are joined to the question: if it was doubtful, then if the doubt were leniently oriented, one could say that after they had to be stringent rabbinically they were not stringent because of doubt but decreed about the doubtful case even if that doubtful person is certainly a minor, so that he should have rabbinic kiddushin, and then it would not be a whichever-way argument, because one could say that even for rabbinic kiddushin he needs divorce in adulthood. Therefore they ask that this is not so, for if it was doubtful it is a Torah doubt and we are stringent; why would they need to come to their decree about him? It remains that he is betrothed out of doubt, and they ask that it should be asked simply as a whichever-way argument.
