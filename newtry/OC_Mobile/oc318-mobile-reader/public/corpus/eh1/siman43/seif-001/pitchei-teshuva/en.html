For the Rabbis did not institute marriage for a minor boy. See Chelkat Mechokek, s.k. 1, who cites the Bach above in siman 1 that if a father marries a woman to his minor son of twelve years, it is rabbinic kiddushin. He writes that he knows no hint or proof for this new law, and one who innovates such a law must bring clear proof. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 62, regarding a minor under twelve whose father arranged a match for him, and the wedding too was before he reached twelve. The minor betrothed the virgin with chuppah and kiddushin, but they were not secluded at all, because the teachers ruled to be concerned for the decisors who consider relations before the thirteenth year to be zenut. Later disputes arose; the boy reached thirteen, and the girl did not want him. The question was whether she needed a get because of the view of Ri bar Barzilai, brought by Mishneh LaMelech in chapter 6 of Hilchot Gerushin, that when a father betroths a wife for his minor son one should be concerned. He discusses this at length. The questioners wrote that Ri bar Barzilai meant only the thirteenth year, when it is a benefit for him, because we are not concerned she will commit zenut, as Rashi wrote in Perek HaNisrafin, that for a year or half-year she will not commit zenut against him. But before that, in the twelfth year, it is a detriment. Noda BiYehudah rejects this, because Maharik, shoresh 31, cites the case explicitly regarding an eleven-year-old boy whose father betrothed a woman for him, and Rav Yitzchak required a get. It is therefore clear that Rav Yitzchak was stringent even for an eleven-year-old. However, even Ri bar Barzilai was concerned only for rabbinic kiddushin, not biblical kiddushin, unlike the implication of Mishneh LaMelech. He then questions the first reason, that kiddushin are a benefit to the minor, since they also create obligations and prohibit him to her relatives. One might answer that Rav Yitzchak’s case involved only kiddushin without chuppah and nisuin, as was the practice then, so there were no obligations of support and clothing at the time of erusin. But in our time, where kiddushin are under the chuppah and erusin and nisuin occur together, the husband becomes obligated in all marital obligations, and this is not a simple benefit. Since in the case at hand they did not have seclusion and the marriage was never consummated, and there are many grounds to say that the father’s act did not create a full marriage for the minor, he inclines that there is no biblical kiddushin; the details depend on the circumstances discussed there. See the responsum at length.
