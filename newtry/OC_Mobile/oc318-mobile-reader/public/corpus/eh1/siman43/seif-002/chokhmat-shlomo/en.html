Section 1. "And it is forbidden to marry him to a woman while he is still a minor, etc." Note: If a father betrothed a woman to his minor son, the view of Rabbi Yehudah bar Barzilai is known among the authors, that one must be concerned for the betrothal, and the Mishneh la-Melekh in chapter 6 of Divorce holds that it is a Torah-level concern. I have already written about this in several places. When I was studying Gittin 85a, concerning the question there, "except for the betrothal of a minor, what is the law?" I asked: what is the question, since it can occur through his father's betrothal? This would seem to prove against Rabbi Yehudah bar Barzilai. Later, however, I saw in Noda bi-Yehudah, first edition, section 63, that his learned son-in-law had already noticed this. After further analysis, I saw that on the contrary this sugya is a proof for Rabbi Yehudah bar Barzilai. For why did the Talmud use the phrase "except for the betrothal of a minor"? It should have said, "except for a minor, what is the law?" as Rashi in fact explains, and as it later says, "except for those yet to be born, etc." Why did it say "the betrothal of a minor"? It must be according to Rabbi Yehudah bar Barzilai. Therefore, if he had said "except for a minor," it certainly would not be a valid bill of divorce, since the case can occur through his father's betrothal. The question is only where he says "except for the betrothal of a minor," which implies that the minor himself betroths her; that certainly has no betrothal, and so the question arises because he will later enter the category of legal marital capacity. This is clear in my humble opinion as a proof, with God's help.
