One should not ask why we should not say that a Torah doubt is treated stringently, since he has the presumption of a minor and she has the presumption of an unmarried woman. One can answer because of Rava's presumption, but this is forced. Also, according to what is explained in the above Ritva, one cannot say that for rabbinic kiddushin he requires divorce after reaching adulthood. It appears more correct to me that there is a scribal error in the Shulchan Aruch. And it is forbidden to marry him off while he is still a minor. In siman 1 the Shulchan Aruch wrote: but before thirteen he should not marry, for it is like promiscuous intercourse. Since that implies only an ab initio warning, the Rama z"l did not need to gloss there as he did here, where he wrote forbidden. This is from responsum Rashba 803, and one could err to think that separation is required, so he glossed that it is not. The Rashba z"l gave two reasons: one, that these are acts of promiscuity; and further, that there is a decree lest the world say that since he married he is an adult, and if she accepts kiddushin from another they do not take effect. He explains the Mishnah in Perek HaKotev, a minor whom they married off, as teaching the law if one transgressed and married him off. From it is proven in any case that he agrees that he is not obligated to divorce her, unlike what was discussed at length in Sefer Torat Yekutiel Nerot in a responsum; the challenge against the Rama z"l is for nothing. The first reason of the Rashba z"l can be reconciled according to the words of Tosafot in Perek HaBa al Yevimto: it refers to before the time near maturity, for then, since there is no mitzvah, they did not institute marriage for him, and it is promiscuous intercourse. This is unlike near maturity, where because of the mitzvah they instituted marriage for this purpose, that it not be promiscuous intercourse, as I explained in siman 1. The language of the Mishnah may be more comfortable to explain as referring to forbidden marriage than specifically near maturity. But the second reason is surprising to me, for if so, all the more so near maturity, where the decree applies and there is even a mitzvah. In practice, I already concluded in siman 1 that it is forbidden to marry him off at least before near maturity, because of the verse, in order to add, etc. The words of the Rashba z"l require study, especially since he does not mention the sugya in Sanhedrin. Further requires study: the Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilkhot Ishut halachah 6, wrote, we do not marry off a minor until we examine him and know that he has brought signs. At first glance this contradicts what he wrote at the end of Hilkhot Issurei Biah, that there is a mitzvah to marry off one's sons near maturity. Necessarily, there in Hilkhot Ishut he speaks of an orphan, warning bet din not to marry him off until they examine him; whereas regarding the father, it is a rabbinic mitzvah. This gives some support to the Bach's distinction, but it requires study from where he derived it, and the Maggid Mishneh z"l did not illuminate the source for us.
