And specifically one maneh or two hundred. This is because of a condition of beit din; from the document itself she would not collect, for it is like a mere shard, as Rashi explains. So wrote the Tur in siman 67: even the principal is not collected by force of the ketubah document, but only by the condition of beit din. I therefore wonder about Rama's later statement that if he did not write her a ketubah, etc.; what do we gain from his writing of a ketubah, since it neither adds nor subtracts, for it is like a mere shard? This also appears from Tosafot at the end of HaKotev, who ask what the Mishnah teaches by saying "on that condition he maintained her"; even if he never wrote her a ketubah she has a ketubah. They answer that it teaches that where he married her as a virgin, even though when she matured or converted she was already a non-virgin, she nevertheless has a ketubah of two hundred, because on that condition he maintained her, that now it should be like the beginning of the marriage. Thus they treated her in every respect as she was at first, as though she is now a virgin. If so she has a ketubah even if one was not yet written for her. Moreover, the distinction between maneh/two hundred and the addition is that the addition comes by force of the writing, while the maneh/two hundred exists without the document; therefore not written is like written, since she would have a ketubah by condition of beit din if she were an adult. This is also proven from the end of the Mishnah concerning a convert whose wife converted with him: her ketubah remains, because on that condition he maintained her. The writing from their gentile period certainly has no force; rather, now that she converted, the original condition is applied.
Words of the glossator: Beit Shmuel s.k. 4 wrote, "he did not write," and brought a proof from Tosafot at the end of HaKotev. They asked what the Mishnah teaches, since even if he did not write her a ketubah she has one, and answered that it teaches that if he married her as a virgin, even though when she matured or converted she was already a non-virgin, she nevertheless has two hundred because he maintained her on that condition, that now it should be like the beginning of the marriage. Beit Shmuel infers that if he did not write for her, she has no ketubah; otherwise the question would remain. In siman 67 Beit Shmuel wrote that although the document is like a shard, he should have stipulated that he would not give this amount. This does not appear correct to me. Since it is a mere shard, why should he need to stipulate? It is as though she has no document at all. His proof from Tosafot is no proof. Tosafot's question is on the Mishnah's phrase "on that condition he maintained her," which sounds as though it refers to her first ketubah that he had written. They ask that this is unnecessary, since even if he never wrote her a ketubah she has one by condition of beit din. They answer that "on that condition he maintained her" does not mean because of what he had written for her, for even without writing she has a ketubah; rather, it means he maintained her on the condition that now it is treated like the beginning of the marriage, so that her ketubah is two hundred like a virgin. It speaks even where he did not write for her, and there is no distinction between written and not written, for the document is a mere shard.
