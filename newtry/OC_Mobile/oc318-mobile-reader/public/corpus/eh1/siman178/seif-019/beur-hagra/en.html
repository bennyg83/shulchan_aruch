See Tosafot, Ketubot 3b, s.v. "and let them expound," etc. The Rosh there wrote: it appears to me to uphold Rabbenu Tam's ruling, but not for his reason, for we say that she is forbidden to the adulterer only where she was not otherwise forbidden, etc. This is what is written in the gloss, "and nevertheless," etc.; do not say that here too the Rosh's reason applies, that a gentile is entirely outside the realm of kiddushin, unlike forbidden relatives, where kiddushin may apply elsewhere. Mahari explained this. He further wrote there to distinguish that with a wife's sister, being forbidden to the adulterer applies if his wife dies, unlike with a gentile, for when he converts he is a different body, since a convert who converted, etc. Mordechai, chapter Arba Mitot, wrote: nevertheless R.
