A husband who sold his property and afterward his wife wrote to the buyer "I have no claim against you" and agreed to his act — even though they acquired from her — behold she repossesses; for she wrote to him only so there not be quarrel between her and her husband, and she can say "I did it for my husband's pleasure." And even if she wrote to him that she cannot say "I did it for my husband's pleasure" — it is nothing. But if they first acquired from the woman's hand that she has no lien on this place and afterward the husband sold it — she does not repossess it. And likewise if the husband sold and told his wife to write to the buyer "I have no claim against you" and she did not agree to his act, and the husband returned and sold to another person whether that field or another field, and after the husband sold she agreed to his act and they acquired from her hand that she has no lien on this field — she cannot protest, for she cannot say "I did it for my husband's pleasure." And likewise if she accepted liability upon herself explicitly that if a creditor of her husband repossesses from him she will pay him — she cannot claim "I did it for my husband's pleasure." {Rama: And some say if the woman received the money she can no longer say "I did it for my husband's pleasure" (Nimmukei Yosef chapter Chazakah and Meisharim siman 123 ch. 4). A woman who admitted she is liable with her husband must pay and cannot claim in this "I did it for my husband's pleasure," for we do not say so except regarding property he sells (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Ramban). A woman who removed her power from her husband's property before the husband acquired them — he can sell them afterward and she does not repossess from them, and it is not relevant to say in this "I did it for my husband's pleasure" (Mordechai start of HaKotev and Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 23).}
