If the husband wishes to build. See Responsa Radbaz, part 1 siman 118, who wrote: nevertheless, if he wishes to build in order to increase the produce, and he stipulates that if the buyer prevails the builder's hand will be on the lower side, it seems to me that the buyer cannot prevent him. Although one who wishes to enter another's field even on this condition can be stopped by the field owner, who can say, "I do not want you to build in mine even on condition that your hand be on the lower side," here it is different, because the husband has a connection to this land: first, he eats the produce, and there is an opinion that ownership of produce is like ownership of the body; second, perhaps the buyer will never acquire this land at all, since we rule according to the enactment of Usha, etc. See there.
(11) The buyer can object. See Chelkat Mechokek, who wrote that even if she did not sell it herself she can object, etc. In the glosses of R. Akiva Eiger on Shulchan Aruch he wrote: Sema, siman 212 s.k. 13, wrote that she cannot object. End quote.
