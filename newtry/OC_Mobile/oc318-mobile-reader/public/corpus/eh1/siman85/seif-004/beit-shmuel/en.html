And if there is produce on the land. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 312, where it is explained that Rabbenu Hai, the Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchot Sheluchin, and the Semag hold that land that has no produce requires authorization. It implies that even if it stands to grow produce, authorization is required, unlike the Sema in siman 122, who wrote that when it stands to grow produce, authorization is not required even according to Rabbenu Hai. According to the Rosh, authorization is not required, and regarding money, according to all opinions authorization is not required. So too with land that has no produce, if he wants to sell it and buy something that produces fruit, authorization is not required, since he would incur a loss. All this discusses nichsei melog, but nichsei tzon barzel do not require authorization, even after her death. See Tur Choshen Mishpat there, and so wrote the Shach there. See the Ran at the end of Perek HaSholeach. The general rule is: nichsei melog that have produce are treated, between husband and wife, like a partnership. Therefore, when she is in the town and he entered litigation and was found liable because of witnesses who testified, this is not considered accepting testimony outside the presence of the litigant. But if she is not in the town, one cannot accept testimony outside her presence. Also regarding produce, his own admission is not effective, even regarding his own share, since he cannot sell because of household profit. Regarding nichsei tzon barzel, witnesses may be accepted outside her presence even if she is not in the town, but his admission is not effective, since he can say, "I take my utensils." So wrote the Rashba in his novellae. From this we also learn that when there is no produce on nichsei melog land, he cannot litigate without authorization, for the reason is that they are like partners. So too, it appears at first glance that even with money he cannot enter litigation without authorization. So Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 67, infers that the Rashba disagrees with the law of Terumat HaDeshen, but one can reject this.
