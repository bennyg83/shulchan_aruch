(א) They were found, etc. This too is the language of the Rambam there, from what is written: she is believed. So it also appears in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 6 of Ketubot, brought by Hagahot Ashri there, s.v. "Yerushalmi," etc., and Tosafot in Kiddushin 25a, s.v. "and Rabbi Eliezer says," etc. The Rosh there wrote: consider this yourself, etc.; a rumor, etc. He explains that therefore they did not enact this where she claims it was a gift, because she is not brazen enough to claim this unless it is true. Sefer HaTerumah, gate 6, part 4, and the Maggid Mishneh there. From what is written in Bava Batra 52b, "and likewise the woman," etc., this applies specifically when she handles business transactions; otherwise she is believed. See Tosafot there 51b, end of s.v. "he accepted," and Choshen Mishpat, siman 62, in the gloss. Sefer HaTerumah there.
(ב) And it shall be bought, etc. As written in Be'er HaGolah, seif 7.
(ג) And he has, etc. Like the law of every doubtful claim.
(ד) And if she said, etc. See above, siman 86, seif 1. One should not say: let him give it to the heirs, and if so, let her be believed through a miggo that she could have said, "he gave it to me on such-and-such condition," etc., which would remove it from produce and inheritance. See Maggid Mishneh and Sefer HaTerumah there, where he discusses it at length. The Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah disagree with this.
(ה) And some say that this, etc. See Chelkat Mechokek, and see the above Tosafot, s.v. "he accepted," etc.
(ו) And some say, etc. Like Choshen Mishpat, siman 56, seif 7.
(ז) And if she said: you, etc. See Be'er HaGolah.
