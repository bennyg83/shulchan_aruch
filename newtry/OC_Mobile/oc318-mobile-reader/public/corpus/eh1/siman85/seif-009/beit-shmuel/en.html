(א) One who sells land. See Hagahot Ashri: in a place where acquisition is not made by money alone, if she gave money and afterward he gave a shtar, she acquires.
(ב) Open and known. The Rambam at the end of chapter 2 of Hilchot Malveh does not distinguish, when he borrows from his wife, between open money and hidden money. Rather, he holds that when he borrows, the money is always his. The Maggid Mishneh wrote the reason: when he sells her land, the money still remains in her presumptive possession, since she gave the money for the purpose of a sale; therefore the money remains in the status of nichsei melog. But in a loan, the money left her possession, and we presume that the money is fully his. It implies from the Maggid there that this is even if he does not make a definite claim. So wrote the Beit Yosef and Sema in Choshen Mishpat, siman 127. See siman 86.
(ג) And some say that if he claims with certainty, etc. This is the approach of Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Rashba, that sale, loan, and seizing from her hand are equal: with hidden money he is believed to say that it is his. Ri and the Rashba wrote that this is specifically when he claims with certainty, but if he claims perhaps and she claims with certainty, we retain the money as nichsei melog. See the Tur and the Maggid. She is not believed to say that it was given to her on condition that the husband has no right in it. Therefore, even with a doubtful claim, in any case the sale is void. For if the money were in her hand, she would be believed to say that it was given to her on condition that he has no right. If so, one can say that his intent is to retain the money so that she will no longer be believed to say that he has no right in it. Even according to what is written in the gloss, seif 13, that regarding hidden money she is not believed to say that he has no right in it, nevertheless he had no benefit from the money, and if so one can say that he only wanted to reveal the money. If she too did not state a definite claim, we do not remove it from him. Chiddushei HaRamban. It implies from Tosafot that if it is known that she has money, even though it is not known where the money came from, its law is like open money. Otherwise, how could they challenge Rabbi Shimon regarding open money? Let him say that his intent was to remove the money. How would he want to remove it if it is known that others gave it to her on condition that he has no right in it? The Nimukei Yosef wrote that open money means that it is known that they gave her money on condition that he has no right in it.
