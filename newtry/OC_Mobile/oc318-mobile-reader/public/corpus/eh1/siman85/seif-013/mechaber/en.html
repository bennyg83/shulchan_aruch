Melog property — the husband eats fruits; and if they are something that does not produce fruit they are sold and something that produces fruit is acquired; and if he says such and such shall be acquired with them and she says I am not acquiring with them except such and such — they acquire the thing whose fruits are many and whose depreciation is little, whether the thing was per his will or per her will. And they do not acquire something whose species does not regenerate. And if something whose species does not regenerate fell to her — even though none of the principal remains — he need not sell it, such as if she brought him a goat for its milk, a sheep for its shearing, a palm for its fruit — even though she has only these fruits alone behold he eats and goes until the principal is consumed. And likewise if she brought vessels as melog property — he uses them and wears and spreads and covers until the principal is consumed; and when he divorces he is not obligated to pay depreciation of melog property.
