(א) Or that they were given to her as a gift. So it is in the Yerushalmi. Although a found object belongs entirely to him, there we are concerned lest a woman conceal property and say, "I found a found object." This is unlike a gift, since a gift becomes known. See Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Perek Metziat HaIsha. This is according to the one who says there in the Yerushalmi that her found object belongs to the husband for the above reason. According to the first Tanna there, the reason is because of quarrel, namely enmity. One must say that with a gift we are not concerned for enmity, since the giver does not want to give it to him. The produce of this gift cannot be collected by his creditor because of household profit. Ba'al HaTerumot, gate 6.
(ב) That they injured her. This means others. If she did not collect the damages in her lifetime, the husband does not inherit it, for it is property fit to come to her, as Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in Perek Yesh Nochalin, page 125. See Shor Shenagach, page 42. If her husband injured her, see siman 83.
(ג) Those monies. For they are produce of produce that came from the outside. Talmud, Perek HaChovel.
(ד) He cannot sell. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote: it appears that in the first division, where she sold the ketubah at a discount, she has permission to give the money to whomever she wishes. To me it appears that this is proven from the sugya in Perek HaChovel, as I wrote in the following seif. If she sold it, the sale is void. So wrote Tosafot and the Rosh, and this implies that the sale is void immediately. It is not comparable to money from an injury that her husband caused her, where he has no entitlement at all, for there it is a penalty that the Sages imposed on him, that he has no entitlement in the money. The Rashba wrote that the sale is valid all her life, and after her death he removes it from the purchasers, and with nichsei melog he removes it immediately. From the Ramah cited by the Tur it implies that he holds that even after her death he does not remove it from the purchasers, and he also holds that he does not have produce of produce. So wrote the Bach.
(ה) And the produce, etc. Tosafot in Perek HaChovel wrote that if she sold the ketubah at a discount, he has no produce from the discounted value, and she does not need to buy land with the proceeds. So too it is proven there, for otherwise what is the challenge: let her sell the ketubah at a discount and give it to the injured party? If he has a share in the produce, how could she give it to the injured party? Rather, certainly he has no right in it at all. From this it is also proven that she can give that money to another. Also, he does not inherit that money, for otherwise how could they have thought that she should sell and give to the injured party? See the end of siman 266 and 290. In siman 118 it is explained in Bach that one who lent her, even after marriage, collects from this gift.
