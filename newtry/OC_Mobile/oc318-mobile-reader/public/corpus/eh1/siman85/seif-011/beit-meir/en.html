{Rama: And some say, etc.} Beit Shmuel wrote that this is specifically with hidden money, but what he distinguishes, etc. In my humble opinion, the Or Zarua is proven from Rashba's responsum, siman 906, which is cited in Beit Yosef chapter 6. His language is: a woman who bought objects from her husband with hidden money did not acquire, because he acted only in order to reveal the money; therefore what she acquired should be sold, land should be bought with the proceeds, and he eats the produce. But the husband may not seize all of it, for he has only the produce. This implies that even if he claims with certainty that the money was his, he is not believed. This is difficult, for Rashba follows the approach of Ri, who equates purchase of land and a loan, that with hidden money he is believed when he claims with certainty. It must therefore be that only where he sold land for hidden money, since she did not acquire it, the land remains in his possession and he is considered in possession of the money, so he can claim; and likewise with a loan, she holds the document and he holds the money, because a document in his favor is like collected property and he is properly considered in possession. See Tumim, siman 130, and his words do not harm what I have written, for here with respect to the actual money he seized from the woman he is indeed considered in possession, like the claimant against the ketubah whom he cites there. But with objects, even though she did not acquire them, she nevertheless holds the objects in exchange for the money and is considered established in possession. If so, the difficulty remains: here too it inclines to Tosafot's approach that what is in her hand is believed to be entirely hers, so why should he eat the produce? Rather, one must say that he distinguishes: only with revealed money is she believed regarding everything, but not with hidden money. Just as if the husband seized them, because of the weakness created by concealment he is believed to say they were stolen, so even when they are in her hand we suspect that she wanted them not to be known to the husband so that he would not eat the produce; therefore she is not believed to say they are entirely mine. Darkei Moshe seif katan 3 alludes to this responsum; see there. See Shach, Choshen Mishpat siman 127. Know that according to the gloss, if they dispute this, she saying they were revealed to him and he saying they were hidden, she is certainly believed. Only where she borrowed and he is in possession of the money is he believed, because the document in her hand is considered unverified; but if she is in possession, she is believed. In the responsum above, it was known that they were hidden. In this, in my humble opinion, even the position of Tosafot and the Rosh fits this approach.
