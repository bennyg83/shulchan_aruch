Someone gave her. In Nedarim, Rav and Shmuel disagree, and there are three approaches. The Rash in the name of Rav Amram Gaon wrote that the halacha follows Shmuel, so even if he said plainly, "on condition that the husband has no right," it is effective. This is also the view of the Ramban. So too, if he only said "do with it what you want," it is also effective. The Ran wrote this according to this approach. Tosafot in the first chapter of Kiddushin, page 24, in the name of Rabbenu Tam, ruled that the halacha follows Rav, that he must specifically say, "on condition that the husband has no right except what you put in your mouth," or something similar, where he designates a particular matter. But if he said plainly, "on condition," etc., or "do what you want," it is not effective. So too is the view of the Ra'avad, the Rosh, and the Maggid. The Rambam, chapter 3, halacha 7, ruled like Shmuel, but holds that even according to Shmuel he must say: "on condition that her husband has no right, and do what you want." Then it is as though he said to her: it shall be yours only for that thing that you choose to do. Here he wrote plainly like the Rambam because most decisors hold that when he says "on condition" and "what you want," it is effective. So too wrote Responsa Rivash, siman 215. However, if the husband seized it, it appears that he can say: I hold like the approach of Rabbenu Tam, the Ra'avad, and the Maggid. In Yoreh Deah, siman 222, he brought the view of Tosafot, because there regarding prohibition he was concerned for the view of Tosafot too. It is explained in Ba'al HaTerumot, gate 6, that if he stipulated "on condition that you do what you want" without the husband’s permission, he does not inherit her. This is also brought in Kesef Mishneh. There is some basis to infer from Rashi and the Rosh in the above sugya that they do not hold this way, for they wrote regarding what is stated, if one accepted from the woman he returns it to the woman, and if she died he returns it to her husband. We are not concerned that perhaps they gave it to her on condition that the husband has no right, because even if they gave it to her that way, he inherits her. According to what Ba'al HaTerumot wrote, the difficulty still remains: perhaps they gave it to her with a condition that he has no right except what she wants, in which case he does not inherit her. So too I saw in the gloss of the Shach there.
