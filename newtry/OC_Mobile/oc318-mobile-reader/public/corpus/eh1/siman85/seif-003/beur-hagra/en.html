And there is one who, etc. So is the view of Rashi in Yevamot there, in the above s.v.: "and there is one who brings in much money and does not write," etc. The Rashba there, however, agreed with the first opinion, and inferred from what is written, "she brought in to him nichsei melog slaves," and "she brought in," which implies what is written in the ketubah. He further proved this from the Tosefta, where it is taught there: If she brought in slaves to him with an appraisal, she takes them with an appraisal; if they were children, she takes them as young men; if they were young men, she takes them as old men. He explains that the latter clause refers to where she brought them in without an appraisal. This is also the view of the Rambam, chapter 16, halacha 2. See the Maggid Mishneh there.
