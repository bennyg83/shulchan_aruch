(א) Because he is like a purchaser. So wrote the Rambam at the end of chapter 2 of Hilchot Sheluchin. It implies that he holds he is exempt because he is like a purchaser, without the reason of guarding while the owner is with him. He disagrees with Ri, who holds that one who purchases for thirty days is liable for negligence. So wrote the Chelkat Mechokek, and so it is in Nimukei Yosef. However, from Tosafot in Perek HaShoel, in what they wrote in the name of Ri, it implies that he holds he is liable for negligence, for even though he does not accept guarding and guarding while the owner is with him does not apply to utensils whose body is not hers. See the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot. The Rosh holds that the husband is exempt because of guarding while the owner is with him, even though the body is not hers. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 346.
(ב) Like a purchaser. But if his law were like that of an heir, he would be liable, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 341. Although at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin it is found that sometimes his law is like that of an heir where there is loss to another, here we are not concerned for the loss of the lender, for who forced him to lend? Tosafot, Perek HaShoel. However, this still needs explanation: why do we not say regarding a lender who lent her orally also, who forced him to lend? There, regarding a lender, the question was not resolved whether his law is like an heir or like a purchaser. See Tosafot at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin.
(ג) And if she informed him, etc. So wrote the Rambam. According to the Rosh, who holds that this is guarding while the owner is with him, one can say that he is exempt even if she informed him, unless he explicitly stipulated that he is obligated to pay. It is possible that if she informed him, it is as if she told him that he is the litigant of the lender. See the Maggid Mishneh, who wrote that even according to the Rambam, this is specifically if he used it after being informed. According to the Ra'avad, it is specifically where he accepted it upon himself as a borrower, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 346.
