(א) She sells from her husband's property. The reason is explained in the Shas. One reason is favor, meaning that she should have favor, that is, money with which to marry a husband. The second is so that she not be disgraced in beit din. The practical difference from the first reason is a divorcee: he is not particular about her disgrace, but the reason of favor applies. According to the Tur, if she already married another, he is not particular about her disgrace, and favor also does not apply unless she married relying on this money, as written in Shiltei Gibborim. Perhaps if she became engaged to another he is also not particular about her disgrace. Rambam and the Rosh ruled because she should not be disgraced, even though regarding "we do not attend to orphans' property" we hold it is because of favor, as written in siman 96. Here she has no loss, so they ruled by the reason that she not be disgraced. Rashba ruled that the reason is favor.

(ג) Specifically the principal ketubah and the addition. Ritva holds that for the principal ketubah she sells without beit din, but not for the additional ketubah or the dowry. Maggid Mishneh disagrees and holds that if they were lenient in a matter where she has no loss, all the more with the dowry. Rama ruled like Ritva and ruled that even for the additional ketubah she sells without beit din. This may be because Ritva speaks of a woman who has no sustenance, while Rama speaks of a woman who has sustenance. One can also say that Ritva did not decide this reasoning, but only wrote: even if you say that the additional ketubah has neither disgrace nor favor, nevertheless there is sustenance; therefore he rules in this like Maggid Mishneh's approach.
