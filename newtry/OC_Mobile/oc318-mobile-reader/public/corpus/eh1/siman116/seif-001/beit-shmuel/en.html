(ה) And everything that was lost, etc. This follows Rambam's approach according to his explanation in the Commentary to the Mishnah, used to settle the words of Rif: the sugya speaks where property was lost or stolen. With other women the husband is exempt from paying for what was lost or stolen from nichsei melog; but here, because the marriage was a mistaken purchase, he must pay. All the more so, if he sold nichsei melog he must pay; even what wore out through his use of them he must pay. But according to Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh, the entire sugya speaks of what wore out unlawfully. Women prohibited by negative commandments and an aylonit have the same law as other women; therefore nichsei tzon barzel, which are in his domain, and what he used lawfully, he need not pay. Nichsei melog that he sold he did unlawfully and must pay; but if they were lost or stolen, he need not pay, and likewise he is exempt from paying for what wore out through use. However, the Tur does not imply so, as I wrote nearby. With sheniyot, the matter likewise depends on this dispute. According to Rambam, when a sheniyah has no nichsei melog, this means what was lost or stolen, as with any other woman; the penalty is that although by right he should have had to pay her, as with women prohibited by negative commandments, nevertheless he does not. But what he sold he must pay her. According to Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh, a sheniyah has no nichsei melog even regarding what he sold, and he need not pay; all the more so regarding what was stolen or lost. With a girl who performed mi'un, according to Rif's approach, when the property is not present, whether nichsei melog or nichsei tzon barzel, she does not have it; that means nichsei melog that were lost. But what he sold he must pay. According to Rashi, even what he sold and did unlawfully he need not pay. The reason is that he can say: had she not performed mi'un, perhaps she would have died and he would have inherited her. Regarding one who committed adultery and one who violates dat, where we learned that she takes what is present, this implies that what is not present, even if he sold it, he need not pay; we penalize her more, just as we penalize her regarding nichsei tzon barzel, that he need not pay, as written in Rambam. But with sheniyot he must pay nichsei tzon barzel. According to Tosafot, who explain the entire sugya as dealing with property worn out unlawfully, and nevertheless he need not pay for sheniyot, then all the more so for one who violates dat: even if he sold, he need not pay. See Chelkat Mechokek in the preceding siman, seif katan 2, regarding what he wrote. It is not exact: according to Tosafot it is obvious that he need not pay, and even according to Rambam's approach, nevertheless for one who violates dat he need not pay. Necessarily, he infers from the wording she takes what is present that what is not present he need not pay; from this it follows also that what he sold from nichsei melog he need not pay. When defects are found in her, or if she is a vow-maker, where the Talmud said nothing about them, it appears according to Rambam's approach, who wrote regarding an aylonit and women prohibited by negative commandments where he did not recognize the prohibition that he must pay what was lost from nichsei melog, and whose reason is that there is no complete marriage here, that all the more so with defects and vows he must pay. One should not give the Ran's reason, that she lost because she did not reveal it to him, for that applies specifically to gifts he gives her, where we say that if he had known of the defects he would not have given her gifts. But certainly she did not lose what is hers because she did not reveal it to him. According to Tosafot's approach, he need pay only what he sold unlawfully from nichsei melog, as above. However, the Tur implies that although according to the Rosh a sheniyah and a girl who performed mi'un do not receive payment even for what he sold unlawfully, nevertheless with an aylonit and women prohibited by negative commandments where he did not know, he must pay for nichsei melog even if he used them lawfully, if they were entirely consumed, as Raavad wrote. This is what he wrote regarding those prohibited by negative commandments and an aylonit: such as where he used them and wore them out. Bach's explanation of the Tur's words, that wore them out means they were stolen through use, is forced. What Bach wrote, that the Rosh wrote regarding an aylonit specifically where they wore them out all at once and sold them, is not in the Rosh. Likewise what he wrote to explain Rambam's words not like Beit Yosef does not appear to me. Nevertheless, the Tur's reasoning is not compelled, and Tosafot states explicitly that regarding an aylonit it speaks of what wore out unlawfully. Even according to the Tur's approach, however, it follows that if it was stolen or lost by itself, he is exempt; so wrote Beit Yosef and Perishah. But there he wrote the reason according to the Tur as being that there is no complete marriage, and that does not appear to me; rather, his reason is as I wrote in the Rosh in the name of Raavad. According to Rashi in Bava Metzia 67 and in Yevamot there, and according to Raavad, a sheniyah has no worn-out property even if it is still present. The Ran also has another approach, and holds that an aylonit is equal to a girl who performed mi'un; see there. See Mordekhai at the end of Ketubot regarding what he asked from this sugya against the law of the academy, and see siman 75 and siman 77 regarding what I wrote.
