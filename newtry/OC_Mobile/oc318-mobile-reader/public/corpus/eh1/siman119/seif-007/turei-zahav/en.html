(א) One who divorced his wife, etc. In Ketubot chapter 2 it is taught: one who divorces his wife may not marry in his neighborhood, because she recognizes his gestures and they may come to transgression. If he was a kohen, she may not live with him in the same alley. Such a case occurred, and they said that a small village is treated like a neighborhood. Rashi explains: if he was a kohen, even if she did not remarry, she may not live with him in the same alley, lest he have relations with her, for a kohen is forbidden to a divorcee. But with an Israelite, as long as she has not remarried she may live in his neighborhood. It appears clear that a neighborhood is larger than an alley, and therefore the baraita says she may not marry in his neighborhood: if she remarries, there is a capital prohibition, and they were stringent to distance even from the neighborhood. All the more so with the divorced wife of a kohen who remarried. But as long as she has not remarried there is a distinction: with an Israelite she may live in his neighborhood, and not specifically the neighborhood; even in the same alley she may live, for the reason is the same. Since there is not even a negative commandment, there is no concern he will have relations with her, for if they desire one another he can remarry her permissibly. Rashi mentioned that she may live in his neighborhood only to teach that the prohibition stated by the tanna, "in his neighborhood," is when she remarried. But as long as she has not remarried, they were not stringent about the neighborhood, and the same applies to an alley. This was not included in the tanna's prohibition of the neighborhood. But with the divorcee of a kohen, where there is a negative commandment even if she did not remarry, he must distance himself; yet he need not make a large distancing, only to the end of the alley. Tosafot wrote that if he was a kohen she may not live in his alley, for since she is unmarried the matter is lighter for him; but if she remarried, he need distance only the measure of a neighborhood, namely three houses, because once she remarried the matter is more severe for him even if he is a kohen. It is clear that they hold a neighborhood is smaller than an alley. Therefore, where she remarried, whether she is the divorcee of an Israelite or of a kohen, distancing of a neighborhood suffices. But if she did not remarry, where the prohibition for a kohen is only a negative commandment and is lighter to him, he must distance to an alley.
