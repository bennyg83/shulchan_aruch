(ד) And if he divorced her, etc. This is Rambam's language in chapter 10 of Hilchot Gerushin. We will explain it from the source, for it is not found in Maggid Mishneh or Beit Yosef. In the Gemara, chapter Cheresh, regarding one who knows how to guard her get but not herself, R. Yitzchak said: by Torah law she is subject to divorce, just as a competent woman may be divorced against her will; but the Sages enacted concerning her that people not treat her as ownerless. In the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot there: "If she became deranged he may not divorce her." The house of R. Yannai said: because of being dragged after everyone, meaning she would be treated as ownerless. R. Zeira and R. Mani said: because she cannot guard her get. The Yerushalmi says there are three practical differences between these reasons: first, if he transgressed and divorced her; second, if she has a father; third, if she is at times deranged and at times lucid. Tosafot explain that the case of transgressing and divorcing means: according to the reason of being dragged, since there is only a rabbinic prohibition lest people treat her as ownerless, if he transgressed and divorced her it is a get. According to the reason that she cannot guard her get, it is a Torah prohibition, for she has no legal hand; therefore even after the fact it is not a get. The second case, where she has a father, and the third, where she is at times deranged, are stringencies according to the reason of being dragged, for the father cannot guard her from ownerlessness, and when she is at times deranged one must be concerned for the time when she is deranged and will be ownerless. According to the reason that she cannot guard her get, it is a get whether she has a father, who can guard the get, and likewise when she is at times deranged, for at least when lucid she is fit to guard herself and is subject to divorce. It appears from this Yerushalmi that the one who holds the reason is guarding the get is not concerned at all for ownerless conduct; if she has understanding to guard her get, even if she cannot guard herself from ownerlessness, he may divorce her. But our Gemara does not hold so. Rather, even where it is a get by Torah law because she guards her get, nevertheless he may not divorce her because of the rabbinic concern that people will treat her as ownerless.
