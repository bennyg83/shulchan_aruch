(ה) And she cannot guard herself. This is the rule: if she cannot guard her get, then by Torah law she is not divorced, for it is written, "and he shall place it in her hand," and then according to all opinions he remains liable for every monetary obligation. If she can guard her get but cannot guard herself, then by Torah law she is divorced, but Chazal enacted that he should not divorce her, so that people should not treat her as ownerless. In this case Rambam holds that Chazal did not enact monetary obligations, since he is prepared to settle what is hers and is prevented only by the enactment of Chazal; therefore he is exempt from all monetary obligations. With this one can answer the difficulty raised by Rama in siman 70: there one may say it speaks where she became deranged and cannot guard her get, so he remains liable for every monetary obligation. As for medical expenses, which Rambam says he does not owe, it appears he refers to other medical expenses; but if it is possible to cure her of this illness, he is obligated in her treatment. Raavad challenged him without need. Tosafot and the Rosh in Perek HaCheresh cite the Yerushalmi that when her father is alive and she is betrothed, then even if she cannot guard the get, she is divorced by Torah law, because her father can guard it. Likewise, if she is sometimes sane and sometimes insane, it is as though she can guard her get; nevertheless, ab initio it is forbidden to divorce her, because we are concerned she may come to promiscuity, since her father cannot guard her at every moment, and when she is insane she may come to promiscuity. After the fact, however, she is divorced in such a case even if she cannot guard her get.
(ו) And some say that he is obligated in her sustenance. The Tur wrote in the name of Ramah that even if he already divorced her, he is obligated in sustenance and other obligations except marital relations. He wrote two reasons. First, since Chazal enacted that he not divorce her, even if he transgressed and divorced her he remains obligated in all obligations, for otherwise what did Chazal accomplish with their enactment? Second, she is no worse than one who is divorced and not divorced, where he is obligated in her sustenance. Since Chazal enacted that he not divorce her, the practical difference between these two reasons is nowadays after the ban of Rabbenu Gershom: if he divorced her against her will, according to the first reason one may say that here it is not relevant to ask what Chazal accomplished, since there is a ban in the matter and presumably he will not transgress; but according to the second reason one may say that he is obligated in all obligations.
