He placed a ban not to divorce. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 75, regarding one who divorced his wife against her will through an agent: soldiers seized the woman in the street, the agent threw the get into her lap, and she cried out with no one to save her, did not consent, and threw the get away. He ruled that since there is a grave transgression of violating the cherem of Rabbenu Gershom by divorcing against her will, there is no agency for a transgression, and the agency act is entirely void. She was not divorced through the husband’s agency, there is no divorce, she may not marry, and the husband remains her husband and is obligated in all marital obligations. He is also forbidden to marry another woman because of the cherem against marrying two wives. Many sages challenged him, saying that "there is no agency for a transgression" means only that the sender is not liable, but the act itself is not void. Noda BiYehudah defended his ruling at length, from siman 75 through siman 82. The note then discusses whether this rule that the agency itself is void is found in Mishneh LaMelech. It points out that Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 3 of Hilchot Geneivah, law 6, implies that even where the Torah included agency for slaughter and sale, if he made an agent to slaughter on Shabbat the sender is exempt from four- and fivefold payment, because the agency for the slaughter is not effective. This supports the position that the agency is void. Maharit and Machaneh Ephraim also were uncertain whether in cases of transgressive agency the act itself is void. See Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, simanim 111, 112, and 135, and what I wrote below, siman 141, seif 36, s.k. 38. Noda BiYehudah also proposed a new law based on the Rosh in Gittin 11 regarding one who seizes for a creditor: even though generally a person’s agent is like himself, he cannot appoint an agent in a way that harms others. Therefore, even before Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, and under Talmudic law where a man could divorce against his wife’s will, he cannot divorce her against her will through an agent when the get is a detriment to her. Several scholars objected from explicit passages about agency for divorce against her will. He answered that he agrees agency for delivery can exist against her will where the get is not a detriment, such as an arusah who has not yet acquired benefits from the husband, or a woman already forbidden to kehunah, or a woman who committed zenut and thereby lost her ketubah. But where the get is a detriment, he cannot appoint an agent to harm her.
