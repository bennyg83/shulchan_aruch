The ketubah. See Ba'er Heitev, who wrote that Pri Chadash challenged this from the Talmud in Yevamot, etc., and also from Perek HaNizakin, etc. He was already preceded in bringing these proofs by Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 253, and this is also brought in Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 91, letter 5; see there further. As for what Ba'er Heitev wrote, "I say that we do not derive law from aggadot," and that Tosefot Yom Tov wrote similarly, etc., this is not a difficulty against Pri Chadash. Pri Chadash himself, in his work Mayim Chayim, objected to the Tosefot Yom Tov there and wrote that this principle, that one does not derive law from midrashim, applies only regarding deciding the halacha according to one view where the Talmud does not explicitly state the halacha. But with a law not explained in the Talmud, we find many times that we do derive from midrashim. His words are brought in the margin of the Mishnayot, Berachot, chapter 5, in the additions of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, letter 36. Sefer Ba'er VeNekev also rejected the words of Ba'er Heitev in this matter, because the rule that we do not derive applies when it contradicts the words of the Talmud. One can also distinguish that if the matter was established in our Talmud, we certainly derive from it. See there. See also Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh Deah, siman 161, on this matter.
