And if he informed her, etc. See Get Pashut, s.k. 3, who resolves why this is not difficult from the statement at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim, "and I will purge from you the rebels and sinners against Me," which Reish Lakish explains as referring to children born from nine improper traits. See there at length. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 21 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, end of law 28, who writes that what Get Pashut says, that children of a woman divorced in the heart are so called only when he does not inform her, but if he informs her it is permitted, is astonishing. The Rosh at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim writes that the reason for the prohibition is that he is thinking about another woman; if so, even if he informs her, the reason applies. Mishneh LaMelech therefore explains that when one has decided in his heart to divorce her, it is forbidden to have relations with her only where he wants to take another woman, for then the reason of thinking about another applies. But where he does not want to take another woman, it is permitted. Thus Rav and Rav Nachman, who already had wives in their own cities and when they traveled wanted to take a woman for the days they would be delayed there, even though they had decided to divorce after that time, were not thinking about their wives while with another. The Mechaber, who wrote generally that if he informed her it is permitted, speaks of such a case. He further writes that what appears from Get Pashut, that the prohibition of children of a woman divorced in the heart applies only if she becomes pregnant, is not correct according to the Ra'avad’s view brought by Beit Yosef, Orach Chaim, siman 240, that the verse refers to those who have relations with one of the nine traits; according to that, even if she does not become pregnant it is forbidden.
