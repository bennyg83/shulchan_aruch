She should not remain under him, etc. For it is written, "Do not devise evil against your fellow." Chelkat Mechokek wrote that perhaps in such a case, where she is already married to him, it does not help if he informs her, for what can she do? He also infers from the language "and she serves him" that the prohibition is specifically when he has relations with her while intending to divorce her. In Perishah, siman 25, he wrote that after marriage it does not help if he informs her, and it is forbidden because of the category of children of the nine defective unions, namely children of one whose husband intended divorce in his heart. At first glance his explanation is difficult: if she is forbidden to him because of "divorce in the heart," why did Rav Mesharshya ask Rava what the law is if his heart is to divorce her while she remains under him? Did he not know the teaching of the nine defective unions? And Rava answered that it is forbidden because of "Do not devise evil." If so, how does the teaching of the nine defective unions apply? From this it appears that the nine defective unions speak when he has relations with her, whereas here, where he has not had relations, it is forbidden only because of "Do not devise evil," unlike Chelkat Mechokek, who wrote that here it speaks specifically where he has relations with her. However, Perishah's words are not forced. One may say that the nine defective unions, including divorce in the heart, speak when at the time he has relations with her his intent is to divorce her; here it speaks where his intent is to divorce her at a fixed later time, and at the time he has relations with her he does not intend to divorce her, yet it is still forbidden because of "Do not devise evil." If he has not had relations with her, one can say there is no prohibition. One may also say that the nine defective unions refer to the children being blemished, while here he transgresses "Do not devise evil." Chelkat Mechokek's inference from the language "and she serves him" is not compelling, for the Tur did not write "and she serves him." At first glance one could distinguish further: the child of a woman divorced in the heart refers to those whom he is compelled to divorce, as implied by the Tur's language there. However, we have not found such a distinction, and the Tur's words there are explaining the Rosh in Nedarim, who wrote that he does not hate her and divorce her; the Tur wrote that this refers to those whom he is compelled to divorce, for otherwise how could the case occur. But regarding children of one divorced in the heart there is no distinction. I later found that Baal HaTurim on Parashat Va'etchanan explained likewise: children of a woman divorced in the heart means that he does not hate her, but he is among those compelled to divorce.
