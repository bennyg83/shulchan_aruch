A woman without her consent. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 84, who was uncertain whether Rabbenu Gershom’s decree also applies to a deaf-mute woman. One could say that just as Chazal did not institute a ketubah for a deaf-mute woman lest men refrain from marrying her, perhaps for the same reason Rabbenu Gershom did not decree regarding a deaf-mute woman; if he could divorce her only with her consent, this would be worse than a ketubah obligation and men would refrain from marrying her. Nevertheless, he writes that we cannot rule in practice on something not explicit in the early authorities, for doubtful cherem is a biblical prohibition. He also notes that regarding divorcing against her will, the time of the cherem may not yet have ended. This is not clear; Get Pashut is uncertain, and from Beit Shmuel above, siman 115, it appears he holds that in our time perhaps the time of the cherem not to divorce against her will has already ended.
