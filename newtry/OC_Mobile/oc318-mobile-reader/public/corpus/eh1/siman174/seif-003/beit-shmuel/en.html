He acquired her, and her rival is exempt. The reason is as written in seif katan 1. At first glance this is surprising in light of the sugya there: because a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment, she is fit for intercourse, he acquires her through intercourse, and her rival is exempt. Rava's answer there was rejected, where he said that a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment when one can fulfill both the positive commandment and the negative commandment by giving chalitzah; that was rejected there because we hold that chalitzah in the place of yibbum is not a mitzvah. If so, according to those poskim who rule that the mitzvah of chalitzah takes precedence, as written in siman 165, the positive commandment should not override the negative commandment, just as in Rava's statement, for the mitzvah of chalitzah is now the greater mitzvah. If so, Rava's answer would be the main one according to this view: the positive commandment would not override the negative commandment, and the rival would not be exempt, because she is not fit for intercourse, as in the Gemara's difficulty there. One must say that the rule that the mitzvah of chalitzah takes precedence is rabbinic, but on a Torah level the mitzvah of yibbum takes precedence according to all opinions, and it overrides the negative commandment. So too it is in Nimukei Yosef, as I cited above. But the sugya on folio 39 still requires explanation, for it implies that they disagree whether there is a prohibition of ervah, since the Sages who disagree with Abba Shaul derive from the verse her yavam shall come upon her. If Abba Shaul's rule is only rabbinic, how can the Rabbis bring a Torah verse? One may say that the Rabbis hold that even if he marries her for some other motive there is no Torah prohibition; therefore, if he marries her without specifying and we do not know whether he marries for another motive, the Sages did not decree. Therefore they cited the verse her yavam shall come upon her, meaning: since on a Torah level there is no prohibition, the Sages did not decree in an unspecified marriage. Abba Shaul holds that if he marries for another motive there is a prohibition of ervah, and therefore he holds that if he marries without specifying it is forbidden. When he says it is as though he violated ervah, this means that since it resembles ervah, the Sages decreed that if we do not know whether he marries for another motive, he should not marry her. This appears to me according to what Nimukei Yosef wrote. It is further proven from this sugya that anyone who is not fit for yibbum on a Torah level does not exempt her rival through her intercourse, and she herself is also not exempt through that intercourse, for there the Gemara asked from the baraita: if a man with crushed genitals had intercourse with her, he acquired her, and there it does not speak of her having a rival. At first glance, why did Rambam write in chapter 6 that if he had intercourse with a widow from nisuin, the rival is not exempt? Let him say that she herself is also not exempt. This can be answered.
