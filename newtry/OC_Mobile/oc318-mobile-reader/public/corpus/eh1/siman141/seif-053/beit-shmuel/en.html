Etc. The get is invalid if the agent lacked authority to perform the act, even if the husband might have been willing. Later in the siman the same principles are applied to cases where the agent appoints another agent or where the husband’s words can be read narrowly or broadly. Where there is room for doubt, the woman may be treated as doubtfully divorced.
But there is no, etc. Where there is no clear agency, one cannot validate the get merely because the outcome accords with the husband’s presumed desire. The Bach and Rosh discuss whether a get can be upheld based on the agent’s claim, but without proper appointment or witnesses the case remains deficient.

The child is a mamzer, etc. If the get is certainly void, a child from a later union can be a mamzer. If the get is only doubtful, the child has the status of a safek mamzer. The Rambam and other decisors distinguish carefully between a woman who is not divorced at all and one who is doubtfully divorced. In these agency cases the child’s status follows the strength of the get’s validity.
