Doubtful, etc. The Tosefta and Yerushalmi, which the Rosh brought there. See there. The Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote, etc., and it was emended against him, etc. Our version in the Rambam is: this is a get. So too is the Rashba’s version in the Rambam, and it accords with the Ra'avad. The explanation of the Beit Yosef and Knesset HaGedolah, that the Rambam speaks where he fell immediately, and that his words are not precise, is not correct. Rather, the Rambam stated his words plainly based on the Yerushalmi. The explanation of the Yerushalmi is as follows: "doubtful," etc., meaning that he does not know whether he fell immediately. It is clear that what he wrote, "if he fell immediately, this is a get," means that immediately it is not known whether he fell on his own or not; rather, I say, and because of doubt we attribute it this way. In the latter clause, "after time," the Tosefta does not read "perhaps," but rather "the wind pushed him," for that is certainly so. This is what the Rambam wrote: this is a get until it is known, etc.
