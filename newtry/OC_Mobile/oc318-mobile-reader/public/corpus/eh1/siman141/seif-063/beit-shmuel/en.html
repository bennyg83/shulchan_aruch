(א) He said: it is void. The general rule is that he must use language that he is now voiding the get, and not language implying that the get is inherently void or invalid, for we see that the get itself is valid. According to what I wrote above in the name of Chiddushei Rashakh, if the husband said that he had already voided this get, he is believed by migo, since he can still void it. To reconcile the sugya, see Chiddushei Rashakh. However, regarding the question there if he said "void" and did not say "it is void," one must say that this is its explanation: if he did not say "it is," it implies that the get is inherently void, namely past language, that it was void by itself, not that he voided it. For if the meaning were that he had already voided it, he would be believed by the migo above.
