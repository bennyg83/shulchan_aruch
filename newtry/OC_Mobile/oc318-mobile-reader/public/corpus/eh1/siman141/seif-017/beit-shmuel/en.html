(א) Immediately, etc. At first glance it implies that just as we do not require immediacy when it is known that he killed himself, and so too it appears from the Tosefta, which states regarding a doubt whether he killed himself: if he fell immediately, I say he fell of his own accord; if he fell after some time, we do not write, lest the wind pushed him. This implies that if it is known that he fell of his own accord, we are not concerned that at that time he did not intend to kill himself. One can say that the Tosefta teaches that even in a doubtful case, if he fell immediately, it is a get. It also follows from the Tosefta that even in a doubtful case where he did not fall immediately, it is nevertheless a doubtful get, as it says there that we do not write because perhaps the wind pushed him; this implies it has not left the realm of doubt. We do not say there is a double doubt: perhaps the wind pushed him, and even if he killed himself, perhaps at that time he did not intend to kill himself. Therefore the Mechaber ruled in the next seif that where there is doubt and he did not fall immediately, it is a safek get.
