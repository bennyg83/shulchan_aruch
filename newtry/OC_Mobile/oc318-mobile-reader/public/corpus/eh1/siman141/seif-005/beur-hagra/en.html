She said, etc. But, etc. Rav and Rabbi Chanina disagree only where he said, "Bring a get to my wife." But if he said, "to my wife’s hand," that is an insistence, as they said there, 65a: "in such-and-such place," etc. So too regarding the woman, etc. He wrote that even "from my husband" is specifically according to Rabbi Chanina. According to Rav, by the language that the reason is because it is her courtyard, etc., but according to the language that the reason is disgrace, even "from my husband" she cannot receive it from his agent, because here too there is her disgrace, as if the husband is disgracing her, etc. This is what the Rav wrote after the first opinion. Nevertheless, there is a practical difference even for the latter opinion where she is not particular, as above.
