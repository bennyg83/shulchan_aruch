A get. The validity depends on whether the appointment was made in a way that can operate according to halacha. Responsa Rashba, Rivash, and other authorities discuss cases in which an agent appoints another agent and whether that second appointment stands. If the first appointment was limited, the second agency may fail; if the wording empowered him broadly, it can stand.
It is valid. Even if the matter is not ideal, if the required agency can be established and the get was ultimately delivered properly, the get may stand bedieved. The Bach, Tur, and Rosh discuss which defects invalidate even bedieved and which are only lechatchilah concerns.
The Rambam wrote. The Rambam’s view is that where the get is not a get, the woman is not divorced and the related kiddushin consequences do not follow. Other decisors treat some cases as a doubtful get. Therefore beit din must distinguish between a true void get, a rabbinically invalid get, and a doubtful get.
(ד) And the law of an apostate, etc. So wrote Shiltei HaGibborim. His reason is that a gentile is excluded either because he is not a member of the covenant or because he is not subject to the laws of gittin, whereas an apostate is a member of the covenant and is subject to the laws of gittin and kiddushin. Therefore he holds that an apostate is valid for agency. The reason he is disqualified from writing a get is that he does not write it for her sake. However, Tosafot in Sanhedrin 72 imply that the law of an apostate is like the law of a gentile, for they wrote there that an apostate is not a member of the covenant. So too it appears from Rambam, chapter 3. Therefore he wrote that the law of an apostate is like the law of a gentile, and from the day he became an apostate he is considered as though he is not subject to the laws of gittin and kiddushin. See siman 123.
