(א) Whoever hears my voice. In that case he designates that whoever hears his voice becomes his agent. But if he said, "whoever wishes to write may write," Tosafot wrote, as cited on folio 66 in their first answer, that this is ineffective. So too wrote the Maggid Mishneh and Ran in the name of Ramban. In another answer Tosafot wrote that it is effective, even though if one is prohibited by vow from deriving benefit from another, he may say, "whoever wishes to separate terumah may separate," and this is not considered making him his agent for purposes of benefit. Benefit is different; it is not considered that he benefits from him, since he did not command him to act. Ran agreed to this. Even according to the poskim who hold that agency is not required for writing the get, as written in siman 123, nevertheless he must command him to write. Tosafot's distinction is difficult, for at the beginning of chapter HaMadir the Gemara challenges from the case "whoever hears my voice should write," where he becomes his agent: why then, in the case of one who forbids benefit, may he say "whoever feeds will not lose"? According to the above distinction there is no difficulty, for although he becomes his agent, it is still not considered benefit since he did not tell him explicitly to be his agent. A practical difference between these two answers appears in what is written in siman 70 in the name of Ba'al HaTerumot; see there.
(ב) Even though they raised him and did not recognize him. So wrote Rambam. What Rambam wrote, "provided that they know him," means that he specified his name and the name of his city. In Chiddushei Rashach he brought some who explain that "provided that they know him" means that they know he is a human being and not a demon.
