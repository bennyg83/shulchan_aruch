He joins with one witness. Regarding receipt, the agent himself joins with one witness, and it is enough if he received the get before one witness; it is as though he received it before two. Likewise, to testify about his agency as an agent for receipt, one witness is enough because the agent joins with him. So wrote the Derishah, Bach, and Shiltei HaGibborim. The dissenting view is the Rosh, who holds that the agent stands in place of the husband and the wife; therefore he does not join at all, neither to testify about his appointment nor at the time he delivers the get to her. According to that view two witnesses are required both when she appoints him and when he gives her the get. Nevertheless, he is believed to say that she appointed him as agent before witnesses, as written nearby. If there are two agents, according to everyone they become witnesses, and this is the meaning of the baraita: they themselves are the agents and they themselves are the witnesses.
