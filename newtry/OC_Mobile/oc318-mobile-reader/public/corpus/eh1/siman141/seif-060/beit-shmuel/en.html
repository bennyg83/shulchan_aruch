(ג) Or the woman admits. Certainly, if the Get is already in her hand and another man betrothed her, she is not believed to say that he canceled this agency so as to forbid herself to the second man. Also, when the divorcing husband is a kohen, she is not believed to permit herself to him. This speaks of a case where the Get came into her hand, for if it did not come into her hand, the earlier difficulty returns: why is he not believed without her statement? When one cancels the agent through another agent, Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that the agent counts as one of the two; see the Rosh in Perek HaSholeach. Ri"v wrote that he does not count even as one, because he stands in place of the husband. Beit Yosef wrote that the Yerushalmi implies that even when he cancels in the agent's presence, two are required, unlike Rambam, who rules that if he cancels in the agent's presence, two are not required. According to Beit Yosef's explanation, one can also answer that Ri"v does not disagree with the Yerushalmi: Ri"v speaks where he cancels not in the presence of the first agent, and then two are needed apart from the second agent, because the second agent stands in place of the husband. But the first agent does not stand in place of the husband, since he is canceling his agency; therefore the first agent joins when he cancels before him. Do not object from what Beit Yosef wrote, that the Yerushalmi holds that the agent does not join, as written at the beginning of the siman, for cancellation is different, as stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi.
