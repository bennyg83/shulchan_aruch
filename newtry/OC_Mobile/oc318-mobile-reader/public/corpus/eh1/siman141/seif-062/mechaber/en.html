In what circumstances is a get nullified? Only when he nullified it explicitly. But if he did not nullify it explicitly, even if he demonstrated that he wanted it to be nullified, such as if the agent told him "I have not yet given it to her" and he replied "Blessed is the Good One who does good!" or something similar, it is not nullified. And in addition, even if he had attempted to run after the agent in order to nullify it, but he did not reach him until the get was in her hand, it cannot be nullified. Rem"a: Similarly, if he said to the agent, "bring me back this get and I will give you a different one," that is only demonstrated desire and does not nullify the get. If he sent a letter after the agent telling him not to deliver the get, that constitutes nullification because a get can be nullified in writing. If he sent a get to his wife and told the agent: "if my wife wants to come to me, I will honor the marriage, but if not, release her with the get" and then left the city, the agent may deliver the get, because the wife did not come to her husband because he had left the city. And even if the husband was in the city, if he could not make up with her, the agent may deliver the get, because what he said originally, "if she comes to me, I will honor it" is nothing but idle words, and is only demonstration of desire, which is nothing.
