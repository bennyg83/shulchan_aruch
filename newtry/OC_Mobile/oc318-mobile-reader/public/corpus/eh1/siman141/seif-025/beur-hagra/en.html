And if the sender, etc. The Hagahot Maimoniyot, on the words of the Rambam that witnesses are not needed in kiddushin for witnesses of delivery-agency, wrote: so too in Perek HaTekabel, "husband," etc. He means that we rule like Rabbi Chanina, and he explains like the Rif, whom the Ran wrote is speaking of an agent for delivery. But according to Rashi, Tosafot, and other decisors, who explain it regarding an agent for receiving, one may say that even Rav Huna agrees, as above. One can also say that he holds like the view of Tosafot, etc., who compare them to one another, as above. He wrote there: if the sender denies after the giving of the get and kiddushin, it is a safek and we follow stringency, for his third-party object came out from under his hand. He means that it is because of the law of a third party, as written there, and specifically where his third-party object comes out from under his hand, as written in the Tosefta. Otherwise, he is like one witness, etc. He wrote: according to Ra'avyah it appears that there is no kiddushin at all, as written there: "Is the get coming out," etc. It appears to me that this is what he means: according to Ra'avyah, etc., even if he does not deny, as the Rosh wrote there that the Gemara’s question is even according to Rav Huna, where the husband does not contradict him. Nevertheless, that is according to his view, even regarding an agent for delivery. But according to the Rambam, that our sugya is specifically regarding an agent for receiving, the words of Ra'avyah do not apply. But the Rambam agrees to the first opinion, as written there: if the agent and sender admit. Nevertheless, the words of the author are unclear, for he did not explain that it is specifically after giving, since it is no worse than witnesses of receiving. Still, his words are correct, for the Hagahot Maimoniyot holds like Rashi there, that witnesses to the statement are also not needed. But according to Tosafot and the Rambam, witnesses to the statement are always needed, and the fact that the get is in his hand does not help. Here it helps only as the Rambam wrote there, that witnesses are not needed for the shlichut except to clarify the truth of the matter. Therefore he wrote plainly: if they admit, etc. However, the core of his words is astonishing, for one cannot learn from kiddushin, since here, even if the husband denies, the agent is believed, as the Rosh and Ran wrote from what is stated in the first chapter: if the husband comes and objects, etc., and even after giving. Even according to Tosafot in the view of Rav Huna, this is specifically where they are in another city, as above. According to one answer of the Ran there, it is specifically while it is still in the hands of the third party, but once it is in the woman’s hand, Rav Huna agrees. See there. Therefore the Rambam did not write here, "if they admit," etc. So too the words of the Tur are not precise, for he copied here the language of the Rambam regarding kiddushin.
