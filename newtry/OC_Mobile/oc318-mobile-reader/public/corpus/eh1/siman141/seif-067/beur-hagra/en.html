Whether, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. "to receive," etc., and s.v. "by implication," etc. The Rosh wrote there that we hold like Rabbi Yochanan, and it is invalid both for delivery and for bringing. But Ri Migash explains that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees with Rabbi Ami, etc., because one cannot learn receiving from bringing. But regarding bringing he agrees, according to Rabbi Elazar’s inference. See Tosafot there, s.v. "one does not," etc. The Ran wrote that in the Yerushalmi it is explicitly implied like their words, for we read there: a case came before Rabbi Ami regarding a slave who brought the get, and he validated it. Rabbi Abba said to him: But Rabbi Chiyya taught that a slave who brought the get is not valid. Rabbi Ami said: If not for Rabbi Abba, we would have permitted a married woman. And if you say Rabbi Ami did not hear this that Rabbi Chiyya taught, did he not hear the words of the Rabbis? Let us make him hear it from this: Rabbi Zeira said, Rabbi Chiyya in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: the words appear that a slave may receive his own get of emancipation, but may not receive, etc. The Rashba wrote that for practical halacha we act like Rashi, stringently. It sounds that he was uncertain. Beit Yosef.
