He does not need witnesses of receipt. Meaning, he does not need witnesses who saw him receive the get, but he does need witnesses to her statement appointing him as her agent for receipt. So wrote the Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh. Although he is a third party, since the get is in his hand, nevertheless for her to be divorced immediately upon his receiving it we require witnesses that he is her agent for receipt. If there are witnesses that he is her agent for receipt, then even if the husband did not know he was such an agent, we say that the husband presumably delivered it to him so that she should be divorced immediately if he is indeed her agent. When there are no witnesses that he is an agent for receipt, we do not say that she should at least be divorced from the day he arrived with the get by a migo that he could have given it to her, because perhaps he does not want to receive the get when he does not know that the husband gave it to him to give to her. This is implied by the Rosh. The Beit Shmuel then discusses the apparent contradiction between this law and later laws about whether the husband can contradict the agent, and concludes that this case must be speaking of an agent bringing a get from place to place, where the agent is believed; but in one city, even if the husband is not present, we are concerned that he may come and contradict him.
