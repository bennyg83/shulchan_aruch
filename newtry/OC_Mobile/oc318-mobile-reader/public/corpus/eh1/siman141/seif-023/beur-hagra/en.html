When does this apply, etc. From what they said there: we learned, the woman, etc. According to Rashi there, even witnesses to the statement are not needed in this kind of case. But Tosafot there, s.v. "and let him be believed," etc., and s.v. "it is understandable," etc., explain that witnesses to the statement are always needed. This is the view of the Rambam. Although the Gemara answers there that this is Rabbi Elazar, etc., and if so even if it went out, etc., so that now we do not need the first answer of "who said," etc., Tosafot already explained there, s.v. "Rabbi Elazar," that it speaks where there are no witnesses signed on it, etc. All the more so according to the Rambam’s approach, as written above, siman 133. Although all this follows Rabbi Chanina, he follows his own view, for he ruled like Rabbenu Tam, as written above, seif 55. Nevertheless, according to the approach of the Rif, who explains that the question "let him be believed" is specifically according to Rabbi Chanina, the Rosh there wrote that it is according to all opinions, since the husband does not contradict him.
