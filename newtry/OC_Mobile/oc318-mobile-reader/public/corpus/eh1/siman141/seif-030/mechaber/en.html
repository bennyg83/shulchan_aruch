In the text of the authorization of an agent for delivery, one must add to the formula written in the Tur, Even HaEzer: "Before us, the undersigned witnesses, so-and-so son of so-and-so delivered a bill of divorce into the hand of so-and-so son of so-and-so, to bring it to his wife so-and-so daughter of so-and-so and to place it in her hand. Thus did so-and-so son of so-and-so, the husband, say before us to his agent so-and-so son of so-and-so: 'Bring this bill of divorce to my wife so-and-so daughter of so-and-so wherever you find her, and place it in her hand, or in the hand of her agent, or in the hand of the agent of her agent; and your hand shall be as my hand, your mouth as my mouth, your speech as my speech, and your act as my act. I give you permission to appoint an agent, and that agent another agent, up to one hundred agents, even without coercing circumstances. Immediately when this bill of divorce reaches the hand of my aforementioned wife, or the hand of her agent, or the hand of the agent of her agent, from your hand or from the hand of your agent or from the hand of the agent of your agent, she shall be divorced from me and permitted to every man.'" If there is a condition in the bill of divorce, it is mentioned here, and they write, "on such-and-such condition," and they mention it again at the end after writing that she is permitted to every man. They also write in the authorization the name of the city and its rivers as in the bill of divorce, and all bynames of the man and the woman as in the bill of divorce. Some say that they also write the byname of the agent, while others say not to lengthen the authorization with unnecessary matters. Others say that one need not be concerned about lengthening in a matter on which the essence of the agency depends, since anyone may write according to the clarity of his language and it is not a fixed rabbinic formula. See below in the order of the bill of divorce, section 26, how one writes the authorization of an apostate. If a delivery agent brings an authorization in which it is written that he is an agent for receipt: if the agent does not contradict the authorization, but says that he was appointed both as delivery agent and as receipt agent, then the agency is valid, and he acts as a delivery agent and gives the bill of divorce to the woman. But if the agent says that he was never appointed as an agent for receipt, the authorization contradicts him and the entire agency is void. The bill of divorce itself is also invalid, since it is evident that those who arranged it were ignorant and did not know that the husband does not appoint an agent for receipt; one must therefore be concerned for several kinds of invalidity. The same applies in any comparable case where it is recognizable that those who arranged the divorce were laymen.
