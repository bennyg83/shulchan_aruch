(א) In the text of the authorization. Darkei Moshe wrote that at first he appoints the agent before commanding that the get be written, and after the get is given into the agent's hand they write the authorization; for if it were written beforehand it would look false. They write "your mouth as my mouth"; if this was not written, he should not give it to him, because perhaps he is an agent only for the giving. Although the agent is believed to say that he appointed him as agent, nevertheless here the document contradicts him. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 114. They write "and an agent of an agent, up to one hundred." But if he writes generally, "I give you permission to appoint my agents up to one hundred," one can say that everything refers only to the first agent, that he may appoint agents up to one hundred, but the second cannot appoint another agent. Therefore they write "and an agent of an agent," etc. They also write that this may be done even without coercing circumstances. If this was not written explicitly, nevertheless he may appoint an agent without coercing circumstances, for authorization is needed only because of the making of agents without coercing circumstances, as written in Responsa Rivash. They do not write the agent's nickname, because even if one were to say that he is another man, he is nevertheless fit for agency; so ruled Piskei Mahara'i. One should not ask why we are not concerned that this is not his agent but another person, so that the one giving the get is not his agent. We can answer that we are not concerned, since the get is in this man's hand; we say he is certainly his agent. If he divorces his betrothed woman, he should write in the authorization that the husband sends the get. When the arranging rabbi asks the agent, he should ask him in the name of the betrothed man, as stated in Tikkun Yashan.
The agent, etc. See Beit Yosef. Even where there is a concern of invalidity, if it is proven that the get was given according to the husband’s instruction and the agency was properly transferred, the get can be valid. The dispute concerns whether we treat the agent’s act as within the original agency or as a new agency requiring clearer authorization.
