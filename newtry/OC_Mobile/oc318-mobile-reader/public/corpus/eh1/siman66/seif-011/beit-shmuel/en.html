(ג) And there are places where the groom adds. Where there is no custom, we judge according to the law, as written in the Talmud: with money and merchandise one adds a third, since he can profit with them. Bach wrote that our custom is to add a third from the outside, and included in this are the two hundred zekukim written in the ketubah. In the holy community of Krakow this addition is customary apart from garments and ornaments that belong to her person; so they write explicitly. If it was not written, the garments are appraised for her. If one comes from another city and does not wish to add, he is not permitted to change, for anyone who marries and obligates himself without specification intends according to the local custom. However, Bach implies that he may object regarding garments. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, siman 45. In this there is no distinction between a widow and a virgin. The dowry is a full debt. Perishah wrote concerning Darkei Moshe's statement that one does not write "agav" in a ketubah, because then the husband would be unable to sell any movable property: one must say he wrote this only regarding the principal ketubah, and all the more the ketubah additions; but as to the dowry, why should it be worse than any other creditor? The additional third appears to have the same law as the dowry, and I will write more about this below, with Hashem's help.
