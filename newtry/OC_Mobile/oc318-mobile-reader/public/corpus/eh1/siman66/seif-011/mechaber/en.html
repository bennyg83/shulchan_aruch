One who marries a woman without specification writes according to custom; and likewise she who undertook to bring a dowry gives according to provincial custom. {Rama: And what the woman brings to her husband — whether money or garments — and he accepts them upon himself and they are under his responsibility — is called nedunya everywhere, and it is collected only with the ketubah; but for other matters its law is not like the ketubah, as will be explained below. And there are places where the groom adds to her in the nedunya and writes more than he received, and they follow custom in this (all the above in Tur). And by default responsibility for the nedunya is upon him like iron-flock property; but if he wishes to leave them in her possession and not accept responsibility for them — the choice is his. And see above beginning of siman 93 and 100 for more of these laws. When the father is alive and marries off his daughter they write "and the nedunya that you brought him from her father's house"; and when the father is not alive they write "from the house of the groom" (Mordechai ch. Bava Metzia in name of Rabbeinu Tam). However, if he changed the wording — we pay no attention (responsum of R' Shmuel of Nantes). For a divorcee's ketubah they write "the divorced woman" so they know she is a divorcee and forbidden to kohanim (Piskei Maharai siman 50). And when she comes to collect her ketubah — they collect for her what is in the ketubah according to provincial custom. {Rama: And if she says he added more than the custom — she must bring proof for her claim. But if she has a ketubah document that is not validated, or one of the witnesses is disqualified — even though she shows the conditions between them that he had to add for her — she collects only according to custom (Piskei Maharai siman 89). And see above siman 100 seif 30.} And in all these matters and the like — provincial custom is paramount and they judge accordingly, provided that custom is widespread throughout the province. {Rama: However, if he wishes to stipulate and reduce for his wife from the custom — the choice is his (Maharil siman 76), provided he does not reduce below what the Sages enacted. And it seems to me specifically when he so stipulated at the time he made the match; but if they made a match without specification and obligated themselves with penalties — he must write according to custom and cannot change (the master's words, and so implied in Maharil). And there are places accustomed to write all ketubot equally even if she brought him nothing; and if he wishes he adds for her; and if he wishes to reduce for her — she writes him "such-and-such I accepted upon my ketubah" — and so is practiced in these countries.}
