(ג) And some say that one may not rely on witnesses, etc. This is the Tur's view, which he wrote in the name of Rambam. Beit Yosef disputes what the Tur wrote in understanding Rambam's words. Rambam wrote: whether one writes for a virgin, etc., or witnesses testified about him and made a kinyan from him that he owes her one hundred or two hundred, this is permitted; and likewise if he gave her movable property corresponding to her ketubah, it is permitted for him to have relations until he has time to write. End quote. The Tur wrote: if he forgot to designate movable property for her, he may have relations and afterward write for her immediately. Afterward he wrote in Rambam's name in this language: likewise if witnesses testified about him and made a kinyan from him that he owes her, this is permitted until he has time to write. Beit Yosef wrote that our teacher was not exact in writing that according to Rambam, where they made a kinyan from him, we require only until he has time to write; this is not so, for Rambam equated making a kinyan from him with writing her a ketubah. Rather, specifically when he designates movable property, as soon as he has time he must write; but with a kinyan, it is permitted even if he has time, and he may leave it until tomorrow or another day. Therefore the Mechaber wrote here, "or witnesses testify about him," etc. I am astonished at this, for we see that the Tur intended to teach that he did not understand Rambam like Beit Yosef did, and this is correct. It is certainly difficult to say that with witnesses and a kinyan he need not write as soon as he has time. Why distinguish? In any case writing is required. Although an ordinary kinyan stands for writing, nevertheless he must write, for her mind does not rely on it, lest the witnesses die or travel far away, and by what will it be known that she had a ketubah? For this reason Beit Yosef also required writing the next day. Since there is a need for later writing, certainly its law is that he must write when he has time, as with movable property. All the more so from movable property: with movable property at least she has her sum in hand, and the concern is only lest it be lost, so he must write and encumber his property; here she has nothing in hand and relies only on the kinyan of witnesses. Are witnesses always with her? Ultimately people die. Certainly she needs a written ketubah more, and therefore it must be written as soon as he has time. It is therefore clear that Rambam's final phrase "until he has time" applies also to witnesses and kinyan.
