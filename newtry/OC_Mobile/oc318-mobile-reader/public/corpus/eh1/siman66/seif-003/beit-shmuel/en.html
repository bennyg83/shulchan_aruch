(א) Specifically where she wrote to him, "I have received." This implies that if she waives orally, he need not write her another ketubah, and there is no prohibition in living with her without a ketubah. At first glance this is astonishing, for there is still concern that she does not rely on the waiver and thinks the waiver is effective; if so, the relations would be like promiscuous relations, as written by the Rosh and Tosafot. One must say that when she waives orally we are not concerned for this. Tosafot and the Rosh speak of a stipulation at the time of kiddushin, where she thinks the condition stands; but with an oral waiver afterward, she does not think the waiver stands. So wrote Bach. However, one can also say that even orally she does not rely on it; rather, orally there is a remedy without another ketubah, namely to tell her that the waiver is ineffective. But when she wrote "I have received," the lien of the ketubah is cancelled and he must write her another ketubah. Yet below it is proven that the Mechaber holds primarily like Rambam, that even writing is ineffective and she does not lose the ketubah. If so, the requirement to write her a ketubah must be because her mind does not rely on it; and when she waives orally, he need not write another ketubah. Therefore one must say that her mind does rely on it when she waives orally. The Rav Rama's word "specifically" is learned from Mordekhai in chapter HaChovel, who wrote on the statement "with respect to a husband she certainly waives" that this is specifically where she wrote "I have received"; but mere waiver is not a waiver once she is already married. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that although Maggid Mishneh ruled regarding a debt document that waiver is effective even if the document is still held, one can distinguish: with a ketubah, for a woman sitting under her husband, as long as she has not written "I have received," she can say, "I was joking with you and spoke empty words." After her husband's death, however, Maggid Mishneh's words imply that even with a ketubah, mere waiver is effective. Thus from Mordekhai we hear that when she writes "I have received," she has no ketubah, unlike Rambam, who holds that even if she wrote "I have received" she still has a ketubah. The Mechaber follows Rambam.
