Sefer HaPela'ah wrote in Kuntres Acharon letter 13: Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote this is not interest because even if he divorces or dies immediately he must give this addition. But per our enactments, in the first and second year he does not give the addition in full until after three years — thus this answer does not apply. See there where he elaborated; see also Hagahot Yad Efraim and in my view no need to elaborate per what is explained above siman 53 in Beit Shmuel s.k. 16 that returning within three years does not apply to nedunya law or one-third addition which is like a nedunya debt and she loses only regarding maneh and two hundred or voluntary addition. In my view these do not suffice to answer the aforementioned Gaon: first, Beit Shmuel's law that one-third addition is like nedunya is not agreed, for in our case it is clear she takes only nedunya not one-third addition as I brought there in his name. Further, Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 12 wrote if he did not profit on the principal sum Beit Shmuel also agrees — brought there too. Further, the Gaon did not refer to ShUM enactment but another enactment, since he says "only after three years" while ShUM is only the second year; Yad Efraim there also cites "only after three years." Even if "after three" is a mistake for "after two," his language implies an enactment not to give the addition in full, like the custom now in the country I mentioned siman 53 s.k. 14 or Prague custom in responsum Eliyahu Zuta part 2 siman 147 giving half addition — the above answer does not apply. Nevertheless in my view I do not understand Sefer HaPela'ah's difficulty, for all these enactments whether ShUM or ours were enacted lest she remain empty — which applies only to death not divorce, for he divorces willingly; thus Hagahot Maimoniyot properly said it is not interest since even if he divorced immediately he must give this addition — it is not "waiting for payment," and "or if he dies" is incidental, but the main answer is if he divorces her. In Hagahot Ashri ch. Found Object of a Woman siman 4, R' Shmuel explained it is not interest since if he divorced immediately he would have to add; also Turei Zahav on that chapter brought this answer in Mordechai's name; also Beit Shmuel s.k. 16 brought this answer because if he divorces even immediately, etc. — and from omitting "or dies immediately" it appears they intended this; uncertain. See Brit Avraham siman 75 letter 1 who elaborated on Hagahot Ashri's main reason that even if he divorced immediately he must add — he proved in his work Hovat Chen siman 177 that even then it is interest, but concluded per Sefer Halakhot Ketanot it is not interest since perhaps collection will never come, like acquiring her property on doubt that if she dies he inherits, etc. See there. And what Ba'er Hetev further wrote about Krakow custom excluding garments, etc. — see Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 100 questioned a ketubah stating the groom added one-third of the principal beyond garments and jewelry, doubting whether "belonging to her person" means the husband acquires for her or only what she brought; also that extra language was not used for jewelry as gift but only that she takes jewelry; he answered the necessity that it refers to what she brought is no proof, for there is relevance to two returns siman 53 and moredet siman 77 and taking without oath end of siman 88. On the contrary, it proves the intention is on what the husband does; if on what she brought, why write she takes all this besides ketubah — she would take nedunya besides ketubah even without writing. Beit Yosef Choshen Mishpat end of siman 61 in Rashba's name: we do not interpret extra language for benefit except where they said we have no power to judge imaginings; but siman 42 Beit Yosef brought Maharik who does interpret extra language; and Rashba did not close us off except where the matter itself must be written with only extra language, but where it need not be written at all we say from explicit writing it comes for benefit she would not otherwise deserve (he elaborated; apparently disputed between Tanna Kamma and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel Bava Metzia 83 — per our ruling like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, see Tur Choshen Mishpat siman 231 and Sma there; uncertain. See Bava Batra 67 and 138 and Perishah Choshen Mishpat siman 42 letter 10 where I elaborated). Thus also here the condition she takes applies to her person if the intention is nedunya she brought — entirely superfluous; though one may doubt why he wrote one-third addition for belongings she brought — it is strained; Beit Yosef Choshen Mishpat siman 42: whatever meaning is clear to beit din from the writing, they judge even for the husband's benefit, etc. Regarding the second doubt since he did not mention he added, etc. — in my youth I wondered why they give a widow per ketubah all belonging to her person even what the husband did not have at marriage — by what right, even if written as gift he did not obligate himself to buy jewelry, and post-wedding jewelry is not in his power to give as gift per Choshen Mishpat siman 259. I then justified the custom: since for a widow they assess what is upon her below siman 99 because she did not intend gift when leaving — we can say this is plainly, but where he wrote in the ketubah she takes all belonging to her person, he revealed he is pleased even when leaving she should take; thus when he gives after the wedding he acquires for her and she takes, not by kinyan at marriage. Therefore even inferior language not of gift, he revealed he wants her to take when leaving. Another reason for the custom: per Choshen Mishpat siman 259 s.k. 4 if he seized after it came into existence, etc. — specifically with effective language; thus one may doubt this ketubah. But we need my youth's reason, for if she acquires when it comes into existence — fine per Sma siman 66 s.k. 46 even seizure not before seller; but per Shach only when seller knows, and waiver applies to selling to a stranger, etc. — here if this woman acquires belongings it depends on Sma-Shach dispute; even per Sma my reason alone is straight; therefore hard to completely deny this woman belongings, better to favor the widow. See there siman 101 for further merit that jewelry specifics were listed initially but silver and gold jewelry were added finally — intention is on belongings he will acquire after the wedding; nevertheless he concludes compromise is good.
