(א) Provincial silver. The Sages relied on the verse "according to the mohar of virgins" to give her fifty provincial selaim corresponding to the fifty selaim of the virgin's mohar. Fixed Torah silver is Tyrian silver, while rabbinic silver is provincial silver, meaning one-eighth of Tyrian silver; this totals six and a quarter Tyrian selaim according to those poskim who hold that she collects provincial silver. See Yoreh Deah there, where Perishah and Taz explain the measure of five selaim for pidyon haben, and from there you can learn to here.

(ג) In Torah zuzim. This implies that the ketubah is rabbinic, but we assess it in Torah zuzim. So it is according to the approach of all the poskim who hold that the ketubah of a virgin is rabbinic, and so ruled Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. Only Rabbenu Tam and Ri hold that a virgin's ketubah is Torah law. As for the formula "that is fit for you from the Torah," the Rosh wrote that it refers to Tyrian coinage, which is Torah coinage; the Sages enacted for her Torah-level coinage. At first glance, one must say so, for the Talmud states that according to the view that ketubah is Torah law, he is not believed to say "I found an open opening," as Mordekhai and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote; yet we rule that he is believed to say "I found an open opening." Necessarily, then, the formula "fit for you from the Torah" refers only to the coinage, unlike Perishah, who wrote that the ketubah is Torah law. His question, that if it refers to coinage why do we not write so for a widow, is not difficult. For a widow the Sages did not enact Tyrian silver; even according to Rabbenu Tam and Ri, who hold that a virgin's ketubah is Torah law, a widow's ketubah is rabbinic, as stated on Ketubot 10. According to the Rosh as well, the Sages did not enact Torah coinage for a widow. Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of Ketubot, who holds that for a widow too they enacted Tyrian coinage, is a lone view. The gloss's statement that for a widow we assess in Torah zuzim follows Hagahot Maimoniyot, but the custom is not like him. Rather, the main law follows the general body of poskim: for a widow we do not assess in Torah zuzim. Even according to the custom that a widow collects half the amount of a virgin, it is still not appropriate to write "fit for you from the Torah," for the Sages did not enact that. Bach wrote that according to the view that ketubah is Torah law, one collects from intermediate-quality property. This does not appear to me, for Tosafot wrote that even according to R. Meir, who holds ketubah is Torah law, his view that it is collected from intermediate property is not because ketubah is Torah law.
