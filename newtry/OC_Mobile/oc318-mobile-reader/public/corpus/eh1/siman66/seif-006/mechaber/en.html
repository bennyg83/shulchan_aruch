What is the measure of the ketubah? For a virgin — two hundred; and for a widow — a maneh. And for both, provincial silver — thus a virgin's ketubah is 37.5 drachma of pure silver, and a widow's ketubah is half of that, which is 18.75 drachma of pure silver. {Rama: See Yoreh Deah siman 294 and siman 305 for the value of the coin called drachma. And all this is per the view of some poskim who hold that a virgin's ketubah is only rabbinic zuzim (Rif, Rambam, Ran, and R' Meir of Rothenburg) — that two hundred for a virgin equals six and a quarter selaim. And some estimate a virgin's ketubah at ten gold coins and a widow's at five gold coins (Maharil in name of Agudah). But per the view of poskim, two hundred for a virgin and a maneh for a widow are estimated in Torah zuzim, which is eight times more; and they wrote that therefore the custom is to write in the ketubah "that which is fitting for you from the Torah" (Rabbeinu Tam). And some say they write "that which is fitting for you" plainly (Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 10 of Ishut). The custom is to write for a virgin "that which is fitting for you from the Torah," but not for a widow — all this where there is no custom; but where there is custom, they follow what is collected according to custom, as will be explained below.}
