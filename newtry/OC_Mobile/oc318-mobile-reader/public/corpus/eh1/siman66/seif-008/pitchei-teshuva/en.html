They write "the divorced woman." Ba'er Hetev. See Chasam Sofer siman 130 regarding a divorcee's ketubah without "divorced woman": a rabbi went abroad for a month and it was hidden from everyone that Tashbetz part 2 siman 173 wrote they did not practice like Rambam (ch. 4 Laws of Yibum halakhah 33) to write in a divorcee's ketubah "so-and-so the divorcee," etc. He answered this is incorrect — if Tashbetz were before us he would require another ketubah or seizing movable property and writing another ketubah after chuppah, for one examining Tashbetz sees his main reason that one marrying her (if he checks this ketubah and sees she has only a maneh) knows she is a divorcee from the first, for if a widow she would be a killer and forbidden to all, etc. — meaning Rambam per his view is not concerned for killer-wife suspicion but only a mere concern, therefore concerned for priesthood; but per all poskim who are concerned for killer-wife, she has both priesthood and killer concerns, so how blame the woman on her husband. Therefore for us who are very lenient in the manners mentioned siman 9 in the gloss, the concern returns — thus great necessity to mention "divorced woman." And reciprocally, since they already practice as Rama wrote to write "divorced woman," even if Tashbetz himself were before us he would fear if this ketubah comes before a third party he would judge she is certainly not a divorcee since they practice writing "divorced woman" — thus what was done in the case before us is improper, etc. It would seem right to say the ketubah is invalid, not written for this divorcee woman but for a widow — she is another. But Maharam siman 109 wrote nevertheless it is not invalid, for a divorcee is also called a widow, etc., and I say because the Sages called widow after maneh — whoever collects a maneh can be called widow unless "widow" is written (and what he wrote above requiring another ketubah and even without writing widow — when he sensed the mistake before chuppah, as ab initio). See there. And what he wrote "if she had intercourse they write 'had intercourse.'" See Nishmat Adam not to write "had intercourse," so Beit Meir; write only "so-and-so betrothed" and that suffices as a sign. See responsum Radbaz part 1 siman 180: a convert married in ancient days, divorced, now remarries — do they write "the convert" or "the divorcee"? He answered apparently no concern since both are forbidden to priesthood; but on examination it is proper to write "the convert" so conversion is not forgotten, lest tomorrow this one divorces her and she marries a convert and his daughter is unfit for high priesthood per Ra'avad (ab initio when consulting they so instruct, see Kiddushin 48b and Maggid Mishneh siman 7 s.k. 21). Further relevance for marrying her father's relatives — they did not decree on a convert regarding father's relatives at all, and even mother's relatives there is relevance since prohibition is only rabbinic; therefore always write "the convert" so her lineage is not forgotten. Need not write "divorcee" for divorcee and widow are included in convert forbidden to priesthood. And for divorcee and widow write "the divorcee," etc. See there.
