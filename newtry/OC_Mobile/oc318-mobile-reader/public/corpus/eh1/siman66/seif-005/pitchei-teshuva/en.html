And for a widow a maneh. Dagul MeRevavah wrote: the questioner asks how much is a seduced woman's ketubah — see Tur siman 177, Rambam ch. 1 Laws of Na'arah halakhah 3, and Maggid Mishneh there; and it seems all this when she was a minor, but an adult who was seduced — certainly even per Rambam and Tur she has only a maneh even if the seducer himself marries her. These matters are stated at length in Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 33; see there and below siman 177 s.k. 3. See also Chasam Sofer end of siman 133: regarding one who marries his own seduced woman I would write two hundred per Tur siman 177 until I found in Dagul MeRevavah that this applies only to a minor, not an adult who has only a maneh — the elder so ruled and so is proper. And there siman 125 regarding one who conceived soon after the wedding and they admit the pregnancy is from him without doubt of another man's nursing — a sage challenged that two hundred was written as for a virgin, and Dagul MeRevavah wrote that if she is an adult she has only a maneh (and thus the ketubah is invalid); he answered it is correct, even though we learn "if he wishes to add he may add" and Ran holds additions may be included in one sum with the ketubah plainly, not like Mordechai — nevertheless Ran agrees one cannot write "that which is fitting for you from the Torah," and Dagul MeRevavah's words are very correct for an adult who has no fine and whose first intercourse was with consent — Rambam also agrees she has only a maneh. Nevertheless in our case there is no concern, for all this is a generally seduced adult who later marries; but for his betrothed with conditions and penalties as customary, her heart was confident she would marry him and knowingly gave herself to him that he would marry her with two hundred like virgin bride-price — certainly she waived only with this understanding. And even if one stubbornly says if this seducer will not write two hundred we cannot compel him — at minimum if he wrote two hundred "that which is fitting for you," how can we say it is not fitting from him, for certainly it is fitting and he is obligated to write so.
