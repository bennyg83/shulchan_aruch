Seif 17, one who has two sets of daughters etc. — nb: the reason for this law is explained in the Talmud and in Beit Yosef and Beit Shmuel from the principle we hold like R' Yosi that a person does not put himself in doubt. When I was here, with God's help, I was asked on this matter and I write here what appeared to me in Rambam's words Laws of Sacrificial Procedures ch. 15 halakhah 8: if he had a pregnant animal and said if it bears a male it is an olah, if female shelamim, etc., and it bore two males — one is olah and the second is sold for olah needs and its value is non-sacred, etc.; if it bore a tumtum or androgynous they are not consecrated and are non-sacred as we explained. See in Lechem Mishneh what he challenged on this; and it appears to me Rambam's intent is not as they understood — rather the intent is it bore tumtum and androgynous, first tumtum and then androgynous; since we hold a person does not put himself in doubt, even though he said if male olah if female shelamim, nevertheless for tumtum he did not intend to consecrate, for one does not put oneself in doubt; therefore the first is not consecrated and the second, being androgynous, since even if fit for sacrifice, being the second its money would be non-sacred only sold for olah needs, but being androgynous it itself is unfit and the money is always the second's non-sacred money; therefore he wrote as we explained above regarding the second's money being non-sacred. The law if it bore only tumtum he relied on what is written in Laws of Disqualifications for the Altar; and here he teaches a new law that when it bore both tumtum and androgynous they are not consecrated at all. Rambam teaches a new law per my view; and although Rambam's intent may not be this, nevertheless for the law it appears correct as I wrote, and examine. Incidentally I note a difficulty on Rambam in ch. 9 of Ma'aseh HaKorbanot on one who vows unspecifiedly brings from the largest of that species — how if he vowed olah from cattle he brings an ox, if he said olah unspecified he brings a cow, etc. — why when he said from cattle, though it includes cow etc., must he bring the largest; and why for unspecified vow even a bird may come from the largest. Lechem Mishneh sensed this and answered that in that species one must bring the best, but it is difficult for Rambam later wrote one who vowed an ox brings medium, etc. — so one is not required to bring the best in that species, yet for "from cattle" he brings the largest ox in that species while in the species itself he brings medium — very difficult; I searched all sides to answer and found none, and examine.
