And even if he corrected himself within kedei dibbur — for we hold in ch. 13 folio 129 regarding four matters that within kedei dibbur is not as speech, and this is one of them as I mentioned siman 29; and see in seif 20 from this — Tur wrote in name of She'elot uTeshuvot: Reuven had four daughters and Shimon gave him kiddushin unspecified for one of his daughters and did not specify to whom — to his eldest daughter she is not in his authority and is not betrothed; those who are minors in his authority to betroth — he is believed to say I intended this one and on her I accepted kiddushin; and if he does not know, all forgot — all need a get — end quote. Beit Yosef challenged: why is he believed saying I intended this one (for we hold kiddushin not consummated are kiddushin) and all need a get; and why is the betrother not also believed to say he intended this one (like the father); therefore he did not bring this here for halakhah. And in my humble opinion the law is clear — one can deduce from what he wrote "and did not specify to whom he was giving" — why not say to whom he was giving; and this extra language — he should not have said this and not specified at all; rather here it discusses the betrother giving the choice to the father which daughter he intended at time of kiddushin he will give him — it is as if he specified "your daughter so-and-so" and the rest he did not intend are not included at all; in this the father is believed which one he intended, for the betrother trusted him first; and perforce if he forgot afterward all need a get, for the choice he gave him was at time of kiddushin not afterward. I again saw in Darkei Moshe who also wrote here it discusses relying on the father's mind and did not mention it is specifically at time of kiddushin — when then it is specifically consummated; this is the main point. Further in Tur's question to Or Zarua: Reuven had two minor daughters Rachel and Leah; he betrothed Rachel to his brother's son and matched Leah with a certain kohen; the kohen later did not find favor and he agreed in his mind to betroth Leah to another to disqualify her to the kohen; his brothers came and took out a coin and said to Reuven "take your kiddushin for your daughter" and did not specify which of his daughters nor to whom he betrothed her, nor spoke of kiddushin at all before witnesses before the act; but after they left one witness asked to whom he betrothed and he answered "I betrothed her to myself" and answered there is no concern and no get needed — end quote. The reason because he did not say as Beit Yosef wrote; but Beit Yosef challenged this teshuvah has no place in this siman etc.; and plainly he intended only this unmarried one etc.; and further in teshuvah of Rosh we have that the nephew who betrothed Rachel was a minor and then the kiddushin are nothing and perforce both need a get unless he said "to me" etc. And in my humble opinion nine points it teaches: the main abundance in this manner — that even though it is not known which daughter and to which man, nevertheless his actions prove that the one matched to the kohen who did not find favor — he agreed in his mind that whoever comes to betroth her he will consent, and he did this to disqualify her to the kohen — per what we find in ch. Merubah regarding a woman whose son troubled her and she swore whoever comes to me I will not return him, and men jumped who were not proper and the sages said she intended only one proper for her — likewise here he said in his mind he will give her to another to betroth, meaning whoever comes who is proper for him, and he did this to disqualify from the kohen — if so perhaps there are kiddushin, for there is clarification who is betrothed, namely the unmarried daughter specifically, and also clarification who betrothes — for he said whoever is proper for him who comes will betroth her to him, and this is his brother who is certainly proper for him — it is as if he said explicitly he will betroth her to his brother; perforce need not say "to me"; this is better than one who speaks first about kiddushin matters — it teaches this is not so since Reuven did not say explicitly before witnesses first; and if his nephew was a minor as mentioned in Rosh's teshuvah and his kiddushin are nothing, nevertheless this helps — certainly he did not intend Rachel since she already accepted kiddushin from his nephew; no worse than matching a bogeret mentioned seif 15; and Tur did not copy this except because we explained the abundance is that one could say he specified Leah even though Rachel was already fully betrothed — perforce he intended afterward only Leah with the kohen; nevertheless there are no kiddushin at all since he did not say "to me" as above — very correct in my humble opinion; and I saw many strains in this and what we recorded is corrected and accepted — examine.
