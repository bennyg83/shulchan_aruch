A minor whose father went to a distant land and her brothers and mother married her off — these are kiddushin and she needs mi'un; and when her father comes she does not need other kiddushin. And some say they are not kiddushin and even mi'un is not needed. Nevertheless we do not forbid her to him, for she is considered as unmarried and standing with him in promiscuity since the way of kiddushin and marriage is with him. {Rama: And even if the father comes and objects we do not forbid her to him, and his relations are not relations of promiscuity; and he may keep her with him until she grows and her kiddushin are completed. But if the father betrothed her to another before she grows she needs a get from both (Rivash siman 193). And there it is written she needs a get from the second, but the Rav ruled she needs from both; one should be stringent like the first view. And some are even more stringent to say that whenever her father is in a distant land, even if a minor betrothed herself we are concerned that if the father heard he would consent, and it is safek betrothed (Mordekhai chapter HaIsh Mekadesh). There was an incident of one who divorced his wife and arranged with her that she care for his daughters, feed them, raise them, and marry them off, and she did so; the father came and objected to the marriage of his minor daughters and they ruled that if he ordered to marry them off and raise them, what she did is valid and he cannot object, provided he commanded thus before witnesses, for an agent of acceptance requires witnesses as explained above siman 35 seif 3. But if he said she should have authority to marry them off when they grow and she married them in their minority, her act is void and he can object for she deviated from what he commanded (Rivash siman 493).}
