To forbid her to him. See Ba'er Heitev who wrote: but if he brought two witnesses etc. until and she is forbidden to the husband and the paramour. And see in Yad HaMelekh ch. 2 of Laws of Sotah — he wondered on this: first, even if we judge the daughter as intentional, behold all laws of the father's credibility discuss a minor still, for regarding an adult the father is no longer believed per most poskim; if so, per Rashi and Tosafot and Raavad that licentiousness of a minor is not forbidden — seduction of a minor is rape (below siman 178 seif 3); furthermore even if we strain to say it discusses a father who said about his minor daughter he betrothed her to so-and-so and afterward when she became adult witnesses came and contradicted the father and testified he betrothed her to another — she is forbidden in relations of her adulthood — why is she forbidden, behold she is fully raped; still difficult — Torah believed her to the father and what practical difference does it make after the fact, for before witnesses came to contradict the father the father was biblically believed; and he wrote he saw in R' Yerucham from whom Choshen Mishpat copied this law and the language "forbidden to husband and paramour" is not mentioned at all — there it only says "she leaves from this one and that one" which is not the matter of forbidden to husband and paramour at all — there she is biblically forbidden but here "she leaves from this one and that one" is only rabbinically, like a woman whose husband went to a distant land and she married on one witness and the husband came etc. — see there further; and see below end of siman 17 and what is written there.
