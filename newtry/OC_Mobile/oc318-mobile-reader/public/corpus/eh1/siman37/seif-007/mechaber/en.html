Just as the father can betroth her himself, so too he can betroth her through his agent or through herself, that he says to her: Go out and accept your kiddushin. {Rama: And he must say this to her before witnesses, for an agent of acceptance requires witnesses (Piskei Maharai siman 50), as explained above siman 35 seif 3. And if it is visible to all that he is preparing her for chuppah and to accept kiddushin it is as if he spoke before witnesses (Rivash siman 479). And there is no distinction between na'arah and minor (Ran first chapter Kiddushin), and such is the essential ruling. But some dispute and say a minor daughter cannot accept her kiddushin; rather the father himself must accept them (Rif and Mordekhai in the name of RaM). In order to remove himself from the dispute the father should hold the minor's hand when she accepts kiddushin, or stand next to her when she accepts, so it is as if he accepted himself (Kol Bo and Terumat HaDeshen siman 43). And this is preferable to his accepting alone, for some say it is forbidden to betroth one's minor daughter (there in Terumat HaDeshen), as will be explained shortly. And when betrothing the minor he says to her: Behold you are betrothed to me (Ran first chapter Kiddushin). And if he said: Your daughter is betrothed to me — it is kiddushin, for she stands in place of her father even though he gives her the kiddushin (Rivash siman 479). And if he betrothes her with a document he writes in the document: Your daughter is betrothed to me, and when he gives it to her he says: Behold you are betrothed to me (there in Ran). And if he changed these words, nevertheless she is betrothed (there). And when they send minor daughters elsewhere and the father is not with them, when they accept their kiddushin — since he prepared her to enter chuppah and kiddushin it is as if he holds her hand at acceptance (Piskei Maharai siman 33).}
