This is difficult, for the Tur wrote before this in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that if he said, your eldest daughter is betrothed to me, we say that his intent is the eldest even if she is a bogeret. One cannot distinguish between where the betrother said, your eldest daughter is betrothed to me, and where the father said so, for in any case we also require the father's intent, and his intent was on the daughter who is incumbent upon him. One may say that specifically if he has daughters from one wife, then if he says eldest, we say he means the eldest of all, even if she is a bogeret, for the name eldest applies only to the eldest of all, and the middle daughter is called by her own name, as written in the Gemara. But if he has two sets of daughters, then the name eldest applies even to the one after the bogeret, because she is called eldest relative to the second set; then we say that the bogeret is not included. This is also precise in the Tur, for in the name of Rabbeinu Tam he wrote where he has daughters only from one wife, while in this law he wrote of one who has two sets of daughters. See Bach and Derishah. As halachah, it appears that since we find no one who disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam in this, where he said eldest and has only one set, the bogeret is betrothed. Although regarding shidduch most poskim disagree with Rabbeinu Tam and hold that if afterward he betroths without specification all are included, that is because afterward he spoke without specification. But if he said eldest, it is as though he explicitly said the eldest of all. Although we hold that even with two sets he means the eldest of all, nevertheless that is specifically when she is not a bogeret. Two sets are different, because the second is also called eldest, as I wrote; only because a person does not enter into uncertainty, as written in the Gemara, do we say he meant the eldest of all. If she is a bogeret, one may say that he did not enter into uncertainty because the bogeret is not included among his daughters, since she is not incumbent upon him. But with one set, no one is called eldest except the eldest of all, and therefore the bogeret is betrothed. If the bogeret appointed him as agent to betroth her to so-and-so, and he said the eldest shall be betrothed, then we say he betrothed the bogeret, since we generally hold that if she made him her agent to betroth her to so-and-so, she is included, as written in seif 15. Therefore, if he said the eldest, she is betrothed according to all opinions; so wrote Chelkat Mechokek. If all of them are bogerot, then he betrothed the eldest of all. See Yoreh Deah siman 220.
