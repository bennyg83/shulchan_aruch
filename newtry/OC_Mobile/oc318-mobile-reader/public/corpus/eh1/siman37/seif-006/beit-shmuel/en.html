It is a doubt and she requires a get. It appears that if her father alone betrothed her, the kiddushin take effect. The presumption that she is unmarried alone is not effective except at the end of six months, where there are two favorable factors: the presumption that she is unmarried, and the fact that she is now a bogeret. Still, it is only doubtful kiddushin, because the presumption that she is unmarried contradicts it, as the Chelkat Mechokek wrote. If she betrothed herself and not her father, it appears that according to all we are not concerned, since she has the presumption of na'arut and the presumption of being unmarried, as Rashi wrote: within six months she has the presumption of na'arut and the presumption of being unmarried. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that if she alone became betrothed it is also doubtful kiddushin. It implies that within six months there is no distinction between whether she contradicts him and whether she does not; we are always stringent and she requires two gittin. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that if she was betrothed after six months by her father, and long after the kiddushin signs of bogerut were found on her, she was certainly a bogeret at the time of kiddushin, even if the examination was long after her father's kiddushin, since the kiddushin were after six months. Possibly after six months, even if she lacks signs of bogerut, we say she is a bogeret, and the signs of bogerut were mentioned only to make her a bogeret within six months. All of this follows the commentators who hold that signs of na'arut and signs of bogerut are separate; if she brought signs of bogerut within six months, she is a bogeret. But the Rambam did not mention any sign for a bogeret, implying that in his view she is never a bogeret before six months. This is his language at the beginning of chapter 2, halakhah 1: from the day of her birth until she is twelve full years old she is a minor, even if she produced hairs; but if she produced two lower hairs and is twelve years and one day old, she is called a na'arah. From when she brings the lower sign she is called a na'arah until six full months; from the beginning of the day completing six months and onward she is called a bogeret, and the interval between na'arut and bogerut is only six months. End quote. What the Chelkat Mechokek wrote, that possibly after six months, etc., is indeed the Rambam's view: after six months she is a bogeret without signs of bogerut, as he wrote. What he wrote that if after the kiddushin signs of bogerut were found on her, we say she was a bogeret at the time of kiddushin, applies specifically where it was known that she had signs of na'arut when she was twelve years old, and after six months she was betrothed by her father, and long afterward signs of bogerut were found. We then say that at the time of kiddushin she too was a bogeret. So it is in the Tashbetz cited by the Beit Yosef. It is heard from his words that when signs of na'arut appeared three or four months after age twelve, we say presumably those signs were present when she was twelve. So too is it in Chelkat HaMechokek siman 35. The Beit Yosef in siman 43 wrote that the Rashba disagrees with this. When six months after age twelve are completed, she is certainly a bogeret, even if only two months passed after the signs were seen. We rely on women who say that she had signs of na'arut three or four months after age twelve, and she is now twelve years and six months old, and we say presumably those signs were present immediately when she was twelve. But where signs of na'arut were not seen, even if at the time of kiddushin she was twelve years and six months old and now signs of bogerut are seen, we are concerned that perhaps she only now became a bogeret. It also implies from his words that he holds like the Rambam: after six months from the day she had signs of na'arut she is a bogeret even without signs of bogerut. See Responsa Maharam Lublin, simanim 139 and 140. As for what Rabbi Yerucham wrote, that signs of bogerut help within six months, the Rif, Rashi, and Tosafot dispute this, and from the Rambam it also implies that within six months she does not become a bogeret. The Rambam's phrase, "from the beginning of the day completing six months she is called a bogeret," is difficult to reconcile with the sugya, the dispute of Rav and Shmuel on the day the six months are completed, where she has signs of bogerut. What are they disputing? She is a bogeret immediately at the beginning of that day, as the Rambam wrote. His phrase "six full months" implies that four months and some days do not help, unlike the Chelkat Mechokek, who wrote that six months of na'arut can be four months and two days and that the shofar causes it; this is not so. The difficulty raised by the Chelkat Mechokek in siman 4 can be answered differently. Regarding signs of bogerut, see Niddah 47, where they ruled like all the opinions stringently. The Rif wrote that if she brought one of all the signs and betrothed herself during her father's lifetime, her kiddushin is kiddushin and she requires a get, and her father may not annul her vows; and if her father betrothed her without her knowledge, she requires a get.
