If he said to her. But if he did not say to her, it is nothing. So wrote the Tur in the name of Ramah. I already wrote the dispute among the decisors whether agency is required in writing the get. Ramah holds that agency is required, and he writes that "and he shall write" in the Torah refers to the husband; therefore the husband must write it himself or through his agent. Thus even if the woman writes it, he must command her. There is no difference between his wife and another person. The Bach lengthens about this unnecessarily. This law depends on the dispute between Rabbi Elazar and Rabbi Meir. According to Rabbi Meir, "and he shall write" refers to the signing; therefore even if he found it in the trash it is valid. According to Rabbi Elazar, it refers to the writing. The baraita in Perek Mi SheAchazo, which teaches that the get is void until he says to the scribe "write" and to the witnesses "sign," is difficult, for it follows neither Rabbi Elazar nor Rabbi Meir. Perhaps it follows Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that both writing and signing must be for her sake, as written at the beginning of Gittin. Tosafot hold that agency is not required, and "and he shall write" refers to the scribe. The reason the husband must command the scribe himself is that if the scribe did not hear from the husband, it is like writing generally and not for her sake. According to their explanation, it is difficult why the baraita says the get is void, since this should be only a concern and rabbinic. This requires resolution.

(ד) And lechatchilah he should write with his right hand. At first glance it implies that bedieved it is valid if he wrote with his left hand, even if he does not control both hands, and so it is written in the Chelkat Mechokek. He wrote that although if one wrote on Shabbat with his left hand he is exempt, which implies that it is not considered writing, Shabbat is different because we require melechet machshevet. See Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim, siman 32, where he brought the words of Sefer HaTerumah, who learned from Shabbat regarding tefillin, a Sefer Torah, and mezuzot, for in all of them "writing" is written, and he invalidates even bedieved if one wrote with the left hand. So too is written in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot. What is written in Shulchan Aruch there, that it is valid if others cannot be found, is because he brings there that the Rosh and the Semag did not write that it is invalid bedieved. Therefore he distinguishes there between whether others can be found and whether they cannot be found. Nevertheless it appears that even if others cannot be found, he should put them on without a blessing, because doubtful blessings are treated leniently, unlike the Levush, who wrote there that one recites a blessing on them. We learn that the decisors disagree in this, and we have not found the view of those who validate stated explicitly. According to this, it implies that by get, even bedieved it is invalid. However, one can say that a get is different, for we do not learn to the laws of get, as written in siman 155 regarding ink, and as written in the Rosh, chapter 2 of Gittin, in the sugya of me'urah. Nevertheless, it appears that it is valid only in a case of agunah or bedieved where it was already given, like regarding scoring lines, surrounded by blank parchment, and Ashurit script in siman 126.
