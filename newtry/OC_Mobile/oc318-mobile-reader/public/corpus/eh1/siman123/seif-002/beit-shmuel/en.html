A Jew who converted, etc. The Rambam writes that a deaf-mute, imbecile, minor, gentile, and slave are invalid to write a get: a deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor because they lack da'at; a gentile because he writes for his own intent; and a slave because he is not within the law of severance. He did not write regarding a gentile that he is not within the law of severance because he includes an apostate with a gentile, and an apostate is within the law of severance; his invalidity is only because he writes for his own intent. It appears the Rambam holds agency is not required for a get; therefore he did not say that a deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor are invalid because they cannot be agents. From this it is proven that he holds agency and being within the law of severance are two separate matters. Although agency is not required, one must still be within the law of severance; therefore a slave is invalid. Tosafot in this sugya write that agency and being within the law of severance are one matter, and if agency is unnecessary, being within the law of severance is unnecessary. The Rambam’s view requires resolution against Tosafot’s proof. According to this, if a Samaritan wrote the get, it is certainly void even if a Jew stands over him, since he is not within the law of severance. An apostate, who is within the law of severance, if he wrote it with a Jew standing over him, is a doubt, perhaps he did not write for her sake, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote; perhaps even without a Jew standing over him there is a doubt that he wrote for her sake. See siman 141. The Ramban and Rashba hold that a slave is fit to write a get and was disqualified only for agency; here, where agency is not required, he is fit. But this is difficult from the Ramban’s statement that the husband must command personally because agency is required. Thus if a slave wrote, it may be a doubtful get. According to Tosafot, if agency is not required and being within the law of severance is not required, then a Samaritan writing with a Jew standing over him is only doubtful because perhaps he did not write for her sake. According to Ri, agency is needed, and according to the Rambam, being within the law of severance is needed; therefore where a Samaritan writes with a Jew standing over him, it is a doubtful get. If a deaf-mute, imbecile, or minor write without a Jew standing over them, it seems according to all the get is void, because they do not write for her sake. If a Jew stands over them, it depends on the dispute whether agency is needed and is also a doubtful get.
