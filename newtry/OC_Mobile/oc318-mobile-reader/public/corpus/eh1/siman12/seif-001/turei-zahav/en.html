And likewise the single witness, etc. In chapter Keitzad there is the order of mishnayot: first one accused of [relations with] family, etc. — if he married he need not divorce; and if accused of a married woman he must divorce; and afterward one who brings a get, etc. — he may not marry her; and afterward the testifying witness; and afterward the sage who forbade, etc. And in these it did not teach "and if he married he need not divorce" except after the mishnah of the sage who forbade — there is a question: if he married — nevertheless he must divorce or he need not divorce. Our mishnah: one accused of the maidservant — if he married he need not divorce; behold because of mere suspicion we do not remove; and Rashi explained this question refers to sage who forbade and one who brings a get; and apparently Rashi comes to exclude the testifying witness that even if he married he must divorce because if the libel were true he testified falsely there would be Torah prohibition; and similar to what Tosafot wrote there on what Rav Ashi permits regarding a sage; and if so likewise regarding court; but one should not say so, for Rashi in the Gemara there on what Rav Ashi expounded from one accused of family and "one accused of a married woman" — there since he removed her from her husband before this one and this one went and married — the matter is ugly, end quote; if so regarding a witness who testifies it is not applicable — likewise he need not divorce since there is no companionship as in one accused of a married woman which is removal from her husband; so too the Maggid in name of Rashi: it refers to all of them as Beit Yosef wrote; and it appears clear that also for Tosafot's answer that we mentioned it was so — even the testifying witness if he married he need not divorce; for they wrote the reason in one accused of a married woman because there would be Torah prohibition if the libel were true — meaning if true automatically there would be definite Torah prohibition; unlike the testifying witness — even if he testified falsely nevertheless it is not certain the husband lives — say perhaps he died in any case even if another killed him; and all the more so one who brings a get — even if we say he testified falsely and the get was not written before this witness — nevertheless the get is valid from Torah, for "before it was written" is in place of validating the signature; and from Torah witnesses signed on the document are as if their testimony was examined in court; and the rabbis required validation as our Gemara states beginning of Gittin — per Tosafot and Rashi; and if so what Rashi wrote that this question refers to sage and one who brings a get — he did not intend except that it does not refer only to the adjacent mishnah in the Gemara but also to the mishnah before it, i.e. one who brings a get — meaning all mentioned in that mishnah, and the testifying witness is also included. And the Rosh who brought this question did not limit to say elsewhere even if he married he must divorce — only he added the word "sage who forbade" within the question to explain the matter of the question why it refers; and truly also what is mentioned in the preceding mishnayot the law was thus; and he relies on what he concludes that because of mere suspicion we do not remove — likewise in all those where there is only suspicion except one accused of a married woman who removed her from her husband; and with this Tur siman 141 brought teshuvah of Rosh regarding the testifying witness that if he married he need not divorce; and so Beit Yosef in name of Rambam and as he copied his words here in Shulchan Aruch; and also other rishonim ruled in all of them to permit except Rashba who is stringent regarding the testifying witness and gratuitously; Maharshal wrote there is greater stringency regarding one who brings a get since there is Torah prohibition — it is not so as we mentioned; and there is no stringency in the words of Rosh; and he ruled well here in Shulchan Aruch to be lenient in all of them.
