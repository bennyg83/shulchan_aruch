(א) After he finished writing the essential text. This means that a condition phrased as "on condition" is valid. But a condition that nullifies the get, such as "except," or a condition that she not drink wine all her life, is invalid. A valid condition phrased as "on condition" means when the condition was fulfilled, or he went back and erased it. However, regarding erasure, we hold that it is invalid, as written in siman 125.
(ב) This is a safek divorce. Rambam wrote this regarding a condition phrased "on condition" before the essential text, whether in writing or orally. Even if the condition was later fulfilled, nevertheless at the time of writing it was not known whether it would be fulfilled, so it was not a "scroll of severance," and we do not say bereirah. See Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Merubah; I do not know how he reconciles this law, for Rambam's reason must be bereirah, and we do not say bereirah. However, most poskim disagree with Rambam and hold that a written condition of "on condition" before the essential text invalidates only rabbinically, as a decree because of a condition of "except." According to Ran, Baal HaMaor, and several poskim, an oral condition of "on condition" before the essential text is valid, for they hold that even "except" orally before the essential text is invalid only rabbinically as a decree lest he write "except." Therefore an oral "on condition" is valid. Beit Shmuel then contrasts Rambam, Rashi, Ran, Baal HaMaor, and the other poskim, and explains the practical differences between written and oral conditions, before and after the toref, and conditions that reserve part of the divorce versus conditions that merely suspend it.
