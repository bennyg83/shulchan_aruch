But thirty days from the day of the demand. The Tur explained this — there is a dispute between Rashi, who explains from the time of betrothal, and R' Meir HaKohen, who explains from the time of demand. It seems that for a widow whose measure is thirty days, all hold from the time of demand; and this is proven from the Mishnah's language: they give a virgin twelve months, etc., and to a widow thirty days — implying the law of thirty days for a widow is like the law of twelve months for a virgin, which is from the day of demand. And they dispute only regarding an adult woman — for the Gemara says an adult woman is like the demand of a widow; and Rashi holds that when she became an adult, that is in place of the virgin's demand — meaning as if she was already demanded before this for twelve months regarding preparing most of the jewelry; nevertheless she still needs thirty days from betrothal after maturity to fully prepare all her needs for the chuppah. But for a widow we consider only the time of demand — regarding this one cannot say as if she was already demanded, for there is no fixed time for her marriage; specifically for a virgin adult there is a fixed time, for most virgins marry at the time of maturity — therefore it is as if she was demanded before that time. Not so for a widow — there is no fixed time to say that before this she was as if demanded; therefore we count from the subsequent demand for her thirty days, not from betrothal. We find that when it says an adult woman is like the demand of a widow, the comparison is only regarding thirty days alone, not regarding demand — for they said only regarding demand, implying as is the measure for a widow if she demanded, so is it for an adult without demand but from maturity. Otherwise they should have said only: an adult woman is like a widow. But R' Meir HaKohen explains that they are also similar regarding demand; and in truth the Gemara's language leans more toward Rashi for the reason I recall. And I saw things to say for Rashi — that even for a widow it would be from betrothal, and this is not correct at all for the reason we mentioned.
