Na'arah. But if she is still a minor it will be explained shortly in seif 4 that both he and her father can delay. It is difficult — for in the Gemara we derive that they practice twelve months from when they demanded Rivkah; see Ketubot folio 57b — and behold Rivkah was a minor and married Isaac at age three, as Rashi explained in the Torah. See Be'er Heitev.
The demand. So wrote the Rambam. But the Rosh and R' Meir HaKohen hold from the day of betrothal, and so ruled in Shulchan Gavoah. And if she spent six months before the first and he died and she was betrothed to another — they give her twelve months also for the second, and they do not complete for her the first six months she spent before the first fiancé. Even if she spent twelve months before the first — the first days are lost. However, if the first demanded her during her na'arut days and the twelve months passed — then she is an adult for six months — she does not have from the second except six months; for if the first demanded after she matured, they count also from when she became an adult. And it appears that even for a minor the demand is effective, and they count twelve months from the day of the demand to perform the marriage in her adult years. See Beit Shmuel.
