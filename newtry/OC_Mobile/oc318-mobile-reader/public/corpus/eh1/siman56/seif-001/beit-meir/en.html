When the time arrived, etc. — he is obligated in her sustenance. So wrote Shulchan Gavoah end of Even Though folio 90: a fiancé who rebelled against his betrothed and does not wish to enter her — they do not add to her ketubah, but they obligate him to sustain her from when the time to enter arrived. And in Beit Yosef siman 77 it is stated: in responsa they write a letter of rebellion for a betrothed woman against the fiancé if he does not wish to marry — and specifically when she comes due to a claim, etc. — for it is impossible: if he rebelled, no claim is needed since he is bound to marital duty. And siman 38 seif 36 implies that even without her coming with a claim they write. See chapter Even Though folio 61 Tosafot s.v. for chalitzah, and in Shulchan Aruch siman 161 — and it appears from there that the same applies to a betrothed woman. And from the Mishnah that teaches: when the time arrived she eats from his — there is no proof, for it does not discuss a rebel who says I do not wish to enter, but one who delays with postponement not due to duress.
