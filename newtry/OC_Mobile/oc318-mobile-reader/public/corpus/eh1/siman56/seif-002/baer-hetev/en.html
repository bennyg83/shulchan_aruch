Twelve months, etc. Meaning: if she is a widow and he is a bachelor, they do not give him twelve months but only thirty days per the one who says thirty days — for they do not consider him but her. So is the view of the Rambam and Ran. However, in Yerushalmi it is stated: he is a bachelor and she is a widow — they give him twelve months, as the Rosh and Tur ruled. And what they give a man as time — twelve months — is to hire workers for twelve months in order to support himself for the needs of the feast and chuppah, per Rashi's explanation. And he wrote in Be'er Heitev: since nowadays in these lands the custom is that mostly the bride's father makes the chuppah feast, they do not give him so much time — since the bride's father makes the chuppah feast, he need not trouble for feast needs, only for his garments and other things; and there is not so much trouble here — thirty days or a bit more suffices per the court's discretion.
