Such as he fell ill. So ruled the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh — even though his field was ruined; nevertheless, wherever the delay is due to duress he is not obligated in sustenance. All the more if she became a niddah, even not at her regular time. And after the duress passes he must enter her; and if he does not enter, he is obligated to give sustenance — and it is possible even nowadays when they do not betroth immediately but only make a match, nevertheless since the time of marriage arrived and he delays, he is obligated to give her sustenance. However, they do not practice thus.
Or she became a niddah. Meaning, per the first version in the beginning of Ketubot; and there is no proof from this for what the Rambam wrote — that chuppah of a niddah is not a chuppah — for one can say he does not wish to enter at the time of her niddah.
