A woman, etc. The halacha was decided there, in halacha 5, in accordance with Rabbi; and so too is the anonymous Mishnah there and in the fourth chapter of Kesubos.
And if she married, etc. As stated in the first chapter of Sotah, regarding the verse, "and he did not continue, etc.," since she knew, etc. However, the view of the Rosh in the fourth chapter of Kesubos is that danger is more stringent than prohibition. Just as we compel in a matter of prohibition, as stated at the end of the chapter HaMadir, etc., there is proof from what is stated there regarding one afflicted with boils, that we do not allow him, etc., because she causes him to decay; and in the fourth chapter of Yevamos, regarding a pregnant or nursing woman, even according to the Sages he must divorce her with a get, even if he is a kohen. The Gemara states there that this is because she may crush the fetus, or because her milk may become turbid, and we compel him. Thus, if she married, etc., and even, etc. The early authorities wrote in the name of the Ritva, in the chapter concerning those whom we compel to divorce, that beis din is cautioned regarding this, because danger is more stringent than prohibition. The Rambam, however, derived it from what is stated in Tosafos, etc., where the daughter of Rav Chisda said to Rava, "You killed three and now you come, etc." If this were so, how could she have done so, since we do not permit, etc.? See the lengthy responsum of the Rivash in the collected material.
And even, etc. For betrothal is like marriage, as stated in the Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halacha 10: If he betrothed her, it is as though he married her. They said to him that he should not consummate the marriage; if he did consummate it, we remove her from him. This is the reading in our texts, and it is also the version of the Rashba. The Ramban reads there: If he betrothed her, it is impossible for him to marry her, but he is warned not to marry her; if he did marry her, we do not remove her from him.
And if he knew, etc. The Gemara there, folio 65a, teaches: Our Rabbis taught, if she married, etc., she leaves without a kesubah. This must refer to an immediate marriage, for if it referred to one in which she remained married for ten years, then regarding what the Gemara asks there, whether she may claim from the third husband, etc., on the understanding that, etc., he would in any event be required to divorce her, as stated in the Mishnah, lest he not have merited to be built up through her. Nor can it be said that this is a penalty, for if so, how could the Gemara say that she may claim from the third husband? Rather, it is because the marriage was a mistaken transaction, since he did not know of her status. This is the meaning of his statement, "I entered into it on the understanding, etc." It is also proper that she may claim from the third husband. So too in chapter 8: To what extent may she marry? Up to three times. Beyond this she should not marry, except to one who already has children. If she married one who has no children, she leaves without a kesubah, because the marriage was a mistaken marriage. See Tosafos there, s.v. "she leaves."
But, etc. This follows from the Gemara's statement there, "to the third, etc., she leaves, etc.," and further from the question there whether, if she married a third, etc. The case concerns one who did not know of her status, as explained above.
And some say, etc. This follows the opinion that the source of the danger is her bodily condition. Above, however, he wrote in accordance with the opinion that mazal is the cause, for that is the principal view. He cites this opinion only because he wishes to conclude with the qualification, "and therefore, etc."
Or he died, etc. This applies even according to the opinion that mazal is the cause, as stated in Bava Metzia 105b and in Bava Kamma 1a.
Some say, etc. But, etc. See Rashi and Tosafos at the end of the third chapter. There is proof for the explanation of Tosafos. According to the opinion that her bodily condition is the cause, it must specifically involve death. The Gemara states no practical difference between the opinions except where the matter was prolonged, etc.
