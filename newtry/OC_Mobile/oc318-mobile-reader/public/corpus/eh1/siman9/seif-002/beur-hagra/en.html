A man, etc. When he explains that mazal is the cause, he does not mean that the wives die because of his mazal, for one person's death is not attributed to another person's mazal. Rather, it refers to the mazal of sustenance that comes to her through a husband, as stated at the end of the third chapter of Niddah: "A female is clean, etc." This is unlike a male, regarding whom it is stated, etc.
