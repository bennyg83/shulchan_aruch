And she died. In Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 103, the Mechaber did not write "and she died," but ruled generally like the Rambam, who holds the sugya speaks while she is alive. Therefore the reason there was needed that the husband is like a purchaser: if he were like an heir, he would need to return, since in her lifetime one cannot say he inherits her. They would return it to him if his law were like an heir; only because his law is like a purchaser does he not return it in her lifetime. After her death he would not return even if he were like an heir, because the Rambam holds even an automatic heir does not return, and so ruled the Mechaber there. It is therefore surprising that here he wrote "and she died" and gave the reason that the husband is like a purchaser, since even if he were an heir he would not return it. The Rama there wrote "and she died" based on the Beit Yosef’s explanation of the Tur, but this is not proven, and the Bach already challenged him in this siman. It appears that this law derives from the enactment of Usha, where it is clear that even in her lifetime his law is like a purchaser. The Tur’s "and she died" is not precise. So holds the Shach. All this speaks of nichsei melog, but with nichsei tzon barzel, according to everyone even in her lifetime his law is like a purchaser, as written nearby and in the Shach there.
That the husband is like a purchaser. If his law were like an heir, he would return it, as I wrote. Do not ask why his law is truly like a purchaser, since generally where another would suffer loss his law is like an heir, which is difficult according to those who hold that an automatic heir returns. Tosafot at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin answer that here, by strict law, he need not return it at all; it is only because of "you shall do what is upright and good," and there is not such great loss.
We do not return the assessed property to him. Because his law is like a purchaser. If his law were like an heir, they would return it to him both during her lifetime and after her death, because we hold that assessed property is returned to the borrower’s son. Since his law is like a purchaser, it is not returned even in her lifetime, as though she sold it to him. Why do we not say his law is like an heir because of his loss? Tosafot in Perek HaMafkid write that we are concerned for the lender’s loss. Although at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin Tosafot wrote that this is not such a great loss, that was to remove property from the husband; but to remove from the lender, we are concerned for the loss. So wrote the Bach. Regarding assessed property taken for ketubah payment, whether it is returned, see Responsa Maharshal, siman 20, and Responsa Rama, siman 8.
