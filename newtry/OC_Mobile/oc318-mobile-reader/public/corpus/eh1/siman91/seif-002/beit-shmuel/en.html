He is like a purchaser. Although where another would suffer loss his law is sometimes like an heir, here there is not such great loss, since it was given to her as a gift. Tosafot at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin. The Ran answered in the name of the Rashba that since a woman normally stands to marry, when the giver said "after you," he meant when it was not in the husband’s place; but if she is already married, he should have specified.
And she sold and died. One should not ask according to the Rosh, who generally holds that if she sold, he can remove the body of the property, that perhaps "after you" should acquire and not the buyer, because the buyer did not acquire in the woman’s lifetime. This case is different: since the giver removed the husband and said, "after you shall acquire, the husband shall not acquire," the husband is not like the first purchaser, and the buyer acquired the body even according to the Rosh. The husband merely eats the fruits during her lifetime, and afterward the buyer acquired and precedes the "after you." Chelkat Mechokek.
The property remains in the buyer’s hand. Because the husband can remove it from the buyer, "after you" can remove it from the husband, and the buyer can remove it from "after you." Therefore the property remains with the buyer, and the husband and "after you" cannot compromise between themselves to harm the buyer. Tosafot. The reason the Gemara leaves it with the buyer is that the buyer spent money and would lose. If it was given as a gift, all are equal and the cycle returns, so they should compromise. Although we are concerned for purchasers’ loss and do not say he harmed himself by buying from a woman who had a husband, Tosafot answer that otherwise they could compromise with "after you" and the husband would lose.
