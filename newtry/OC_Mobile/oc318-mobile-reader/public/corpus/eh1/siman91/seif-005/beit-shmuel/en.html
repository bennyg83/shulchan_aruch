Her law is like a loan with a shtar. This implies that it is collected even from nichsei tzon barzel. However, in Responsa Rosh and the Tur it states that it is collected from nichsei melog, and Responsa Maharik writes that specifically from nichsei melog it is collected, not from nichsei tzon barzel. The Perishah and Chelkat Mechokek ask why it is not collected from nichsei tzon barzel if its law is like a loan with a shtar. They answer that it speaks where she brought him movable property; even though it is like a shtar, one does not collect from movable nichsei tzon barzel without agav, because in nichsei tzon barzel he is a true purchaser. But one collects from movable nichsei melog even without agav, because with nichsei melog he is not a true purchaser. The Rama’s gloss at the end of seif 7, that one does not collect from movables without agav, also speaks of movables of nichsei tzon barzel. Later authorities debate whether movables written with agav are collected from the husband, and whether the Rosh’s reasoning is based on publicity or on the fact that the creditor did not cause his own loss. The practical distinction is between nichsei melog, where in a place of lender’s loss his law may be like an heir, and nichsei tzon barzel, where he is like a purchaser. See Sema, Shach, Knesset HaGedolah, Mahari ben Lev, Rashdam, Radbaz, and Maharik cited there.
He is not obligated to pay. Whether the obligation arose before marriage or afterward, and whether from the principal or from the fruits. So writes the Nimukei Yosef there.
She had not yet sworn. And she had not written him da'aknei. One might have thought that at the time of the shidduchim it was as though she had no property, since she had not yet sworn, and it would be like property she acquired afterward and sold when she gave it to her daughter. Therefore he teaches that it is as though the property was in her hand, even though she had not yet sworn. However, the wording "like a loan with a shtar" implies that because of publicity its law is like a shtar; in that responsum one can say that because of the conditions written between them its law was like a shtar.
Agav land. Although the husband’s law is like a purchaser, in this respect his law is like an heir. So wrote the Chelkat Mechokek. From purchasers one does not collect movables even if the shtar wrote movables agav land, because of tikkun ha'olam, as in Choshen Mishpat, simanim 60 and 113. With a husband, tikkun ha'olam does not apply.
Only movable property. I already wrote that it is proven from the Ramban’s responsum, which is the source of this law, that even movables of nichsei melog are not collected. This also appears from the language of the gloss. Nevertheless, if the creditor seized movables, it helps according to us, because if the husband’s law is like an heir, movables of orphans are collected. The Perishah and Shach explain that only movables of nichsei tzon barzel are not collected, but movables of nichsei melog are collected, because regarding movables they did not make him fully like a purchaser. Darkei Moshe brought Responsa Rosh, klal 32, that movables are collected, and wrote that perhaps it speaks where the lender wrote movables agav land and does not disagree with the Ramban. He further answered that the Rosh’s responsum speaks after her death, when his law is like an heir. The Chelkat Mechokek cites this answer, but at first glance it is astonishing, because regarding an oral loan I wrote nearby that after her death his law is like a purchaser, and likewise regarding return of assessed property. From where do we say that regarding movables his law is like an heir after her death? This requires study.
