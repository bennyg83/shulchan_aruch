One unmarried woman. Ba'er Heitev explains this as referring to a widow. Magen Avraham 75:3 finds that reading forced and explains that "uncovered hair" in Even HaEzer means undoing the braids and going in the marketplace, which is forbidden even for an unmarried woman, though not biblically. Dagul MeRevavah, however, writes that Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel were correct: the case is a widow or divorcee, as appears from the Yerushalmi in Ketubbot. Ba'er Heitev's phrase "widow, divorcee, or non-virgin" seems to include a woman who became non-virgin through illicit relations, following Shevut Yaakov. Pitchei Teshuvah objects that Ba'er Heitev did not copy the sources precisely. Bach and Chelkat Mechokek mention only a non-virgin, while Beit Shmuel mentions only a widow or divorcee; Beit Shmuel certainly implies that an unmarried woman who sinned is not prohibited to uncover her hair. Even Bach and Chelkat Mechokek may mean a non-virgin through marriage, namely a widow or divorcee from marriage, excluding one merely betrothed; one who sinned while unmarried may not be included. Responsa Panim Me'irot likewise rules that a woman known to have sinned need not be forced to cover her head. If the concern is her ketubbah, a prospective husband can investigate and decide knowingly; the custom of covering is for her dignity, but if she rejects that benefit, she may do so. Dagul MeRevavah follows Shevut Yaakov and reads the Yerushalmi to prove that any non-virgin, even if never married, may not go with uncovered hair, though Kerem Shelomo questions that proof. Ba'er Heitev also cites Chavot Yair that even a betrothed virgin should cover her hair. Chavot Yair explains that if betrothed women in Talmudic times went uncovered, that was because betrothal then preceded marriage by a long period. In another responsum, Chavot Yair rejects a proposal to betroth a young woman conditionally after a sick wife dies: even aside from broader impropriety and possible takkanat Rabbeinu Gershom issues, such a betrothed woman could not properly go with uncovered hair, and the arrangement would create social and moral dangers. He advises using permitted financial undertakings instead, and says he would not normally reveal such advice because it lies outside ordinary communal practice.
