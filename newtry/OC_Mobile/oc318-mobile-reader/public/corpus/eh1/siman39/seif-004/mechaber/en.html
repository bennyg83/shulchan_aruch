What are the disqualifying blemishes in women? All blemishes disqualifying kohanim disqualify women, and in addition for women: bad odor, sweat, and mouth odor (and some say also nose odor) (Mordechai in name of RaMaH); thick voice; breasts coarse compared to her peers by a handbreadth; and a handbreadth between breast and breast; dog bite where the place became a scar (meaning the wound healed and skin formed over it and only the wound's mark remained); and a mole (meaning a wart in Old French — Rashi) on the forehead, even if very small and even if there is no hair — this is the mole that permits a woman to kohanim, which stands under the cap on her head: sometimes visible, sometimes not. If always visible — "he saw and was appeased." If always not visible — it is not a blemish. {Rama: If there is a bathhouse in the city, even hidden blemishes do not void. When do these words apply? When daughters' way is to go to the bathhouse openly; but where daughters go to bathe only at night and hidden — even exposed blemishes void. Epilepsy: if for a fixed time it is a hidden blemish; if not for a fixed time it is an exposed blemish (and see below siman 116 more laws of blemishes). Some say epilepsy and mouth odor — no distinction whether there is a bathhouse in the city or not (R' Yehudah Netiv 23).}
