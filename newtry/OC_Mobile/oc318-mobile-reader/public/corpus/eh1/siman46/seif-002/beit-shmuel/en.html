(א) And similarly, if two came. This means there is a rumor in a certain city that so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so; when beit din investigates the rumor, we require two people to come and testify that they heard from so-and-so, who heard, etc. But where there is no rumor and public commotion in the city, it is not called a rumor. Bach, in the name of Maharal of Prague. Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that the witnesses must be valid. At first glance, the Talmud implies that they need not be valid, for it says there that one wished to resolve from R. Chisda's statement, "until they hear from valid people," that one may nullify a rumor; and according to R. Shimon, who says that they need not be valid and even women suffice, it follows that one does not nullify a rumor. Since we rule that one does not nullify a rumor, it appears that they need not be valid. This is the reasoning of the Ramah brought by the Tur, who holds that even a woman who heard from two people creates a rumor. However, it seems that the rule that we do not nullify a rumor is based on custom rather than strict law; therefore the witnesses must specifically be valid. The Tur also implies that this testimony, that they heard from so-and-so, must include that she was betrothed today by so-and-so. However, Ran wrote that they need not testify "today," and perhaps they also need not testify that she was betrothed to so-and-so. So too it appears from Rambam's language, and this is the wording of the Mechaber. See Kesef Mishneh, who wrote in the name of Rivash.
