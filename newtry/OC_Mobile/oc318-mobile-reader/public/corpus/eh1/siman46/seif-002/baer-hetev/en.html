(ד) A rumor. So wrote Rambam. The Tur, in the name of Ramban, wrote, and according to Beit Yosef this is the Ramah; see there, that this applies specifically when they speak in the name of two witnesses who are still alive but have gone overseas, for then people are afraid lest they come and contradict them. But if they speak in the name of witnesses who have already died, it is not a rumor. One can say that they do not disagree. Rambam, who holds that if the witnesses died it is still a rumor, speaks because he understands that two people came and testified from the mouth of one who heard from another that so-and-so was betrothed before so-and-so and so-and-so. Since in any case the person on whom the report depends has not died, why should we care whether the witnesses before whom she was betrothed have died? Even though they said that the witnesses before whom she was betrothed had died, they can be believed that they heard from so-and-so, since the matter is attached to him and they are afraid he may come today or tomorrow. But according to the explanation of the Tur and those who support him, where there are only two who heard from two, and the latter two are the witnesses to the kiddushin, it is reasonable to say this applies only when they attach it to two who went overseas, for they are afraid lest they come today or tomorrow. But if they attach it to two who died, it is not a rumor. Keneset HaGedolah, folio 80b, seif 18.
