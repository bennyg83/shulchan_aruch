(א) Today. Four elements are required: first, that they said "she was betrothed," in the past tense; second, that they said the kiddushin took place today; third, that they say she was betrothed to so-and-so; and fourth, that they said she was betrothed in this city. A rumor based on a written and signed document stating that she was betrothed does not constitute kiddushin through the force of the document, for we require testimony "from their mouths" and not from their writing; it is therefore not considered a rumor. Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 68. See also Maharam Alshikh, siman 8.
(ב) We are concerned for it. If one beit din said that we are concerned for kiddushin in a case where the expression "to me" was not said, and another beit din said that we are not concerned, this is not called a rumor that was established in beit din. Radbaz, part 1, siman 129.
(ג) The candles. These two must be valid witnesses. The statement of these two does not require them to testify in beit din on the very day she was betrothed; even after some time they may testify that on a certain day they saw lit candles in a certain house, etc. So wrote Bach; see there. As for what Beit Shmuel wrote, that this does not seem to follow from Rashba's responsum, where R. Abba wrote that he does not disagree with Ulla, for had it been at the time we would require it like Ulla, etc.; see there. He was not precise. The plain meaning is that "had it been at the time" means that they saw the candles lit and were in the city at the time of the celebration. But "after some time, when investigating the rumor" means that they did not see the candles lit and were not actually in the city at the time of the celebration, but came there later and heard, etc. See Beit Yosef and Keneset HaGedolah, folio 80a, seif 11. In any event one can say that if they saw it at the time, they may testify later about what they saw on a certain day. Examine carefully.

(ה) So-and-so. He did not require the women to say "to so-and-so in this city"; rather, the women may say simply that so-and-so was betrothed, and the rumor emerges from their words that she was betrothed to so-and-so in this city. Today it is obvious that they need not say this, since on the day of erusin they make the celebration; see Chelkat Mechokek and Bach. However, the Tur wrote that they must say "so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so." Keneset HaGedolah brought the view of Maharival, who holds that Rambam, Rashi, and the Tur require them to say "she was betrothed to so-and-so" only where there are no supporting indications, such as lit candles and beds spread. But where there are supporting indications, lit candles and beds spread, they need not say "to so-and-so"; see there, folio 80a seif 11 and folio 82a seif 6. This is unlike Beit Shmuel, who wrote, "and so wrote Rivash"; see there. According to everyone, they need not say where the kiddushin took place, for from their words it is understood that she was betrothed in the place where the candles were lit. So wrote Kesef Mishneh and Ba'er Hetev.
