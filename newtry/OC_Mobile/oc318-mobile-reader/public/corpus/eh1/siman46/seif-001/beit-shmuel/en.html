(א) That she was betrothed. Four elements are required: first, that they said "she was betrothed," in the past tense; second, that they said the kiddushin took place today; third, that they say she was betrothed to so-and-so; and fourth, that they said she was betrothed in this city. Bach and Chelkat Mechokek wrote that the women do not need to say "she was betrothed to so-and-so today here"; rather, the rumor emerges that way, while the women simply say that she was betrothed. However, the Tur wrote that they must say "so-and-so was betrothed to so-and-so," and Rivash likewise wrote that this is the correct reading in Rambam. Still, they need not say where the kiddushin occurred, for from their words it is understood that she was betrothed in the place where candles were lit; so wrote Kesef Mishneh. (ד) She was betrothed, plainly. What he wrote in the name of the Tur and Rivash is that even if the women say only that she was betrothed, without specification, this is ineffective. But they do not need to say that she was betrothed here. This also explains well what he wrote: if they said that she was betrothed in another city, it is not called a rumor. We infer that if they did not say "in another city," even though they also did not say "she was betrothed here," it is a proper rumor.
