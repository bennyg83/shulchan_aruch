A rumor spread that she was betrothed to so-and-so, and after some days they gave an excuse — if the matter appears plausible to beit din that it is so, we rely on the excuse and she is not presumed betrothed. If not, since the excuse was not heard at the time the kiddushin were heard — we are not concerned for the excuse. {Rama: If it later became clear the rumor was false — such as the witnesses on whom the kiddushin were hung came and said there never was such a matter — they nullify the rumor (Tur in name of Ramah). Some say that nowadays we do not nullify a rumor (Beit Yosef — so is Rambam's view, and so Rif implies, and Ran wrote likewise in chapter HaMekadesh). If he sent her savlonot (engagement gifts) in a manner where we are not concerned for savlonot as kiddushin (above siman 45), even though a rumor spread from this that she was betrothed — we are not concerned for the rumor, for it is customary to call savlonot "kiddushin" (so implied in responsum Rosh kelal 35). And wherever we need not be stringent we should not be stringent regarding rumor at all, so as not to give wicked men license to spread rumors about daughters of Israel to make them agunot or extort money from them (Beit Yosef in name of Rashba).}
