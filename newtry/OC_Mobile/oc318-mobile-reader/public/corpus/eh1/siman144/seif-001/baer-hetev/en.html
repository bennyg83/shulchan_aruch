(1) With gittin. A condition that he stipulates for his own benefit, requiring something that she must do, is not defeated by claims of ones, because he agreed to give the get only on condition that she do this. The same applies after the enactments of Rabbeinu Gershom, when one may not divorce a woman against her will: if she stipulated something that he must do, claims of ones do not help, even an entirely uncommon ones, for the above reason. Beit Shmuel. Raanach hesitated regarding a get given on condition not to say, "If I do not come within such-and-such time, it shall be a get," because he was concerned that perhaps "within" means the middle. If one stipulates "from today until such-and-such day," it is doubtful whether that day is included. Maharit. One who stipulates from now until twelve months, or from now until thirty days: there is doubt regarding the final day, whether "until" includes the endpoint or not. Maharit. Knesset HaGedolah, folio 145a, wrote that since there is room for uncertainty whether using the language "within," "from today," or "from now until such-and-such day," it is best to formulate the condition in this language: "If I do not appear before two witnesses inside the wall of such-and-such city on such-and-such day." So too wrote Raanach.
(2) At all. There is no distinction between an ones caused by Heaven and an ones caused by human beings; whenever it is an uncommon ones, the get is void. Beit Shmuel.
(3) That is common. Meaning, it is common but not fully common. Therefore with gittin it is not considered ones, but in monetary law it is considered ones. Chelkat Mechokek; Beit Shmuel. See Knesset HaGedolah 177a. Baer Heitev in siman 50 wrote that this is a scribal error and it should read: captivity is an uncommon ones and voids the get, even if he did not stipulate; see there.
