(א) But the communities enacted, etc. This law derives from Mordechai in chapter HaCholetz: the communities enacted this so that she not remain an agunah, for many times he wants to perform yibbum and she does not want, and according to Tosafot we do not have the power to compel her. They also did not want to rule that she is a rebellious woman, for according to Tosafot the mitzvah of chalitzah takes precedence, and she would come to agunah and leave to bad ways. Therefore they enacted that he divide with her. Regarding the division, see Responsa She'erit Yosef siman 65, Responsa Maharam siman 10, and Responsa Mishnat Binyamin siman 110: they divide only the amount of the ketubah, and nichsei tzon barzel are divided, as written nearby regarding the chalitzah-man and the chalutzah. The father and other brothers have no share in this; the remainder, if there is a father, goes to the father, and if there is no father, all the brothers and the one performing chalitzah divide equally. The one performing chalitzah has no special extra right beyond his share. Beit Shmuel continues that this enactment operates only within the communal takkanah and does not change the basic Torah/rabbinic laws of yibbum and chalitzah; it is a settlement to prevent anchoring and coercive misuse of yibbum.
Explained, etc. If the brother or yavam refuses and there is no lawful yibbum, beit din may compel chalitzah. The concern is that perhaps he is delaying for improper reasons or relying on a condition that is not effective. The decisors distinguish between a case where yibbum is theoretically possible and one where the only proper release is chalitzah.
