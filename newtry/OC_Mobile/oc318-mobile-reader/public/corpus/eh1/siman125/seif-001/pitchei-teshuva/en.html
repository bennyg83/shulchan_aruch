That its impression remains. See Ba'er Heitev. See also Ginat Veradim, who writes that it appears to him that the Tur also agrees to this, namely that we require the writing to stand and endure. When the Tur writes that “its impression is recognizable,” he means that both requirements are necessary: it must endure and it must be recognizable. However, if it endures but is not recognizable, such as gallnut water on gallnut-treated parchment, as written below in se'if 3, or if it is recognizable but does not endure, such as fruit juice and the like, it is not valid. This is also written by the Pri Chadash, cited in Yeshuot Yaakov, se'if katan 1. He further writes that there is a type of thick, shiny ink called “papil bruneido,” known in our language as “glanz tint.” Its nature is that after the writing dries nicely, after a day or two, because of the thickness of the ink, the letters fall off and spring away from the paper. It appears that this is considered something that does not endure, and one must be careful not to write a get with this ink. See there. See also Get Mekushar, in Kuntrus Acharon, letter 6, who disagrees with him. He writes that it seems to him that this ink also does not spring off unless caused by something external, such as moving the paper and the like. Since it does not pass away on its own, it is properly called enduring. This is especially so because even after it springs off, an impression remains, and for a get an impression is sufficient. See there. The Torat Gittin here also disagrees with the Ginat Veradim on this matter. See there.
