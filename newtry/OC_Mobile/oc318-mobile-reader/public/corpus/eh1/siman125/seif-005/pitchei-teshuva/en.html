See the Levush below, se'if 22, who writes: I am astonished by this reason, for we explicitly conclude in the second chapter of Gittin, as I wrote above in se'if 4, that engraving can be considered writing. Therefore, it does not appear to me that the reason for those who are careful not to write with these quills is the reason they gave. Rather, it appears that the reason is because the verse says “and he shall write,” which implies one act of writing, and not writing on top of writing. Here, if he writes with one of these quills, the quill engraves and digs the writing into the parchment, which by itself is valid, as we wrote above in se'if 4. Afterwards, the ink in the quill comes and fills the engraved writing made by the quill, and this becomes writing on top of parchment in a way that we hold is invalid, as above in se'if 10 regarding the care not to rule lines with lead. End quote. See Ginat Veradim, end of se'if katan 15, who explains the words of the Rama, “so as not to come to engraving,” to mean that perhaps the paper will be thin and the point of the quill will engrave the paper and puncture the letter with a hole going through it. He then cites the above words of the Levush and writes about them: I do not know from where he derived that writing on top of writing is invalid. According to his words, if one letter was written and there was not enough ink in it, and he passed the quill over it a second time, it would become invalid because of writing on top of writing. We have neither heard nor seen such a thing. On the contrary, we see what Maran writes below in siman 135, se'if 5. Even according to the one who says there that it is not a get, that is because it was originally written not for her sake, and the second writing does not help at all. However, if both writings were for her sake, everyone agrees that it is valid, and there is no invalidation because “the Torah said one writing and not writing on top of writing.” We have not heard such an exposition. Regarding the proof that the Levush brought from se'if 10, see Ginat Veradim below, se'if katan 45. Moreover, Terumat HaDeshen, siman 48, cited in Orach Chaim, siman 32, se'if 27, writes regarding letters and words that were partly erased, and the Levush himself copied this in Orach Chaim there. These are his words. See there. See Yeshuot Yaakov, se'if katan 2, who was asked about this matter, namely the practice of scribes to pass the quill over the get a second time before the signatures. The rabbi who asked wrote that this is improper, since the lower writing is not visible, and the upper writing is considered writing on top of writing according to the Levush. He answered him that there is no concern in this at all. He also mentioned the words of Mahari in Terumat HaDeshen, cited in Orach Chaim, siman 32, se'if 27, which oppose the words of the Levush. He writes that it appears proper to rule that the essential law follows Mahari, for we say that writing on top of writing does not help only where the first writing was written invalidly. He discusses this at length. See there. See Torat Gittin, se'if katan 3, who explains the words of the Rama differently. His explanation is somewhat similar to the reason of the Levush, but in another form: there is concern that he may engrave with the iron because there is no ink in the quill, and this too is considered engraved writing. Afterwards, he passes ink over the engraved place, and it is then like ink over red pigment, regarding which we say in Gittin 19 that one is liable twice, once for erasing and once for writing. Since he erased the first writing and wrote a second writing, we say that the witnesses have already fulfilled their agency. Certainly, if the scribe first wrote with red pigment and afterwards passed a quill with ink over it, the get is void, since the witnesses already fulfilled their agency with the first writing. So too here. These are his words. In my humble opinion, although he has said a wise idea, there remains a difficulty. The Rama should not have needed to warn about this here, for it is explained above, siman 122, se'if 1, that the husband gives permission to write from one get up to one hundred, until one is valid, with no concern. Even though the custom is to be stringent in every matter, nevertheless, since this is not the strict law, it is not appropriate to decree one safeguard upon another. Furthermore, it appears that once the custom is that the husband gives the parchment and ink to the scribe and says to him, “Here is the parchment and ink, write a get,” as explained in Seder HaGet, se'if 15, we see that the husband is particular that it be written specifically with ink. It is therefore not relevant to say that through engraving he fulfilled his agency. Although engraving is also considered writing, that is only where the husband did not instruct him to write with ink. But according to the custom, where he gives him the ink, he certainly has no permission to change. Even if he wrote with red pigment, it would also be nothing, and if so it is not relevant to say that he already fulfilled his agency. This requires further study. See Taz, se'if katan 3, who writes that it appears that if one needs to write with an iron quill, there is no need to be stringent at all.
