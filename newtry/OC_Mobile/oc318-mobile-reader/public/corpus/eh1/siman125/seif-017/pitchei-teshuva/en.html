The letter is recognizable. See Ba'er Heitev in the name of Chelkat Mechokek, who raised a difficulty. See the responsum of Noda BiYehuda, siman 85, who writes, after explaining there that if a drop of ink fell on blank parchment and one wrote over it with darker ink, it is called writing, as cited above, se'if 6, se'if katan 7. He writes as follows: To me, this is not difficult. There, some of the ink itself with which the letter was written touched another letter. If so, what does it help that the letter is recognizable? In any case, it is not surrounded by parchment, and ab initio we are stringent with a get as with tefillin and a Torah scroll. But here, a drop of ink fell, and since the letter is recognizable, the ink of the letter is certainly darker and therefore recognizable. What spread from the drop onto the surrounding parchment does not ruin it, because even the place of the drop is called parchment in relation to the blackness of the letter. If so, there is no basis to be stringent because it is not surrounded by parchment. The Rama therefore teaches us that nevertheless we are stringent ab initio. Even aside from this, I do not know why the Chelkat Mechokek was astonished. According to both answers that the Beit Yosef writes in Orach Chaim, siman 32, regarding why we validate a case of a hole, see there, here, where a drop of ink fell and does not reach another letter, and it happened after the letter had already been written, according to both answers there is no concern of surrounding parchment. The Rama therefore teaches us that nevertheless one should be stringent ab initio. These are his words. See there.
