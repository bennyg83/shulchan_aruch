See Taz, se'if katan 4, who writes that according to this, in the case below in se'if 9 of an open mem that became closed, if he closed the mem before completing it, he may scrape away the closure, since he still needs to add the slanted strip shaped like a yud. Therefore, he should first scrape away the attachment and then complete the letter. End quote. What Ba'er Heitev writes, “and if a little ink fell,” is the language of Beit Shmuel, se'if katan 5. It is obvious that this applies specifically in such a case, where the ink fell first and he then completed the letter through writing, in which case it is valid because the letter was completed validly. But if the drop itself completed the letter, it is certainly invalid, for everything follows the completion. This is also explicitly clear from the words of the Beit Yosef. See there, and see what I wrote on this in my commentary to Seder Gittin, in Seder Get Rishon, letter 87. What Ba'er Heitev writes, “The Beit Shmuel wrote that if an invalid person wrote the letter,” and the Pri Chadash writes that it should simply be invalidated, from this wording it sounds as though the words of the Pri Chadash refer only to the ruling of the Beit Shmuel, namely where an invalid person wrote the letter and a valid person completed it. In truth, however, this is not so, as I saw in Beit Meir, who cites him. The Pri Chadash also disagrees with the main ruling of the Beit Yosef in the case where a little ink fell, and with the ruling of Terumat HaDeshen and the Rama here, where part of the letter was made through chok tochot and still needs correction. See there. See Magen Avraham, Orach Chaim, siman 32, se'if katan 23, where it is also clear that this law is not agreed upon. There he cites the Radach, that even if one completely erased the drop and completed the letter by writing, it is invalid, because the entire letter must be made through writing. He then cites Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 5, that if a drop of ink fell and formed something like half a letter, we do not find anyone who permits completing the letter through writing. However, from the wording of the Magen Avraham there it appears that he agrees with the opinion of the Beit Yosef and Rama here. See there. The Ginat Veradim, end of se'if katan 30, also writes: As to the halacha, since the Kol Bo, Rivash, and Maran agree that one may add to the letter, and similarly may add to a drop of ink to make it into a yud or a vav, this is how we rule. End quote. However, this requires analysis, because from the language of the Ginat Veradim here it sounds that one may do so even ab initio, whereas from his words in se'if katan 39 it does not sound so. There he greatly elaborates on the above words of the Radach, and he mentions there that the root of the Radach’s words concerns the case of an open mem that became closed, whether one must erase all of it or whether it is enough to erase part of it until its form is ruined and then complete it. See Beit Yosef, Orach Chaim, siman 32, on this. At the end of his words he writes: As to the halacha, we have seen one side against the other, and Maran the Beit Yosef’s view is decisive, that where a mem became closed, scraping away the protrusion is certainly enough, and this is the proper ruling after the fact. However, ab initio one should be concerned for the view of the Radach and Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, who hold that one must scrape away the whole thing. End quote. According to this, the same, and all the more so, applies in the case of a drop of ink that fell and one wishes to complete the letter by writing: this is not proper ab initio. If so, his words contradict each other. One must say that what he wrote at the end of se'if katan 38, that ab initio it is proper to be concerned, was only as a matter of extra care. But as to the essential law, throughout that se'if katan his view appears to agree with Mahari ben Chaviv, his son the Ralbach, and Maran the Beit Yosef, that even ab initio it is sufficient to scrape away only the protrusion, as all the later authorities ruled in Orach Chaim, siman 32, se'if 18. I later saw that Taz, se'if katan 10, disagrees with the Beit Yosef regarding the case of a drop of ink falling. He writes that it does not appear proper to rely on this leniently in practice, because the entire body of the letter must certainly be formed through writing. What does the small addition made by writing help? This is only where the drop that fell is still moist, and one moves it and pulls it here and there with a dry quill, or even with a knife, and from it makes an entire letter, such as a vav or a yud. In such a case there is no concern, and it is valid, for it is as if he wrote the whole thing anew with ink. This is the explanation of the words of the Kol Bo cited by the Beit Yosef. See there. From these words it appears at first glance that he also disagrees with the ruling of Terumat HaDeshen and the Rama here, but from his words in se'if katan 4 it is clear that he does not disagree about that. The Beit Meir below, se'if 8, also distinguishes between these cases. In his view, where a drop of ink fell, which is considered actual chok tochot, the main ruling follows the Pri Chadash, who disagrees with the Beit Yosef and holds that the small added part is considered as if it does not exist. But in the case of Terumat HaDeshen, and the Rama here, where one needed to write a bent kaf and wrote a bent nun, and a strip extended from end to end, and after scraping one still needs to draw and make it into a kaf, in that case it appears to him that since everything was made through writing, albeit invalidly, and at least not automatically, it is only rabbinic chok tochot. Certainly one can say that wherever the scraping alone does not complete the form of the letter, and one must add until the form is made properly, everyone agrees that it no longer appears to be chok tochot, even rabbinically. See there. See further Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 34, who writes: However, I am uncertain according to the above-mentioned opinion of Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, where a drop of ink fell and one extended it and made it into a yud or a nun, and in the part that he extended there is enough measure for the letter. What is the law? Perhaps half a letter made through chok tochot is invalid only where, if the legs were removed, the necessary measure would be lacking, and the drop that fell already completes the letter. But where he extended the letter beyond that, the drop that fell is considered as if it does not exist, and it is permitted according to all. This seems reasonable. End quote.
