(א) They have the custom to be stringent, etc. The reason is that the letter does not look proper when the letters touch one another. But by strict law, touching letters in a Get are valid, even though in a Torah scroll it is invalid, because regarding a Torah scroll the verse says, you shall write them, meaning complete writing; and we do not derive the law of a Get from a Torah scroll, just as we do not derive parchment and ink requirements from a Torah scroll. So wrote Semag and the Rosh in Gittin chapter 2. A practical difference follows: if one wrote a letter and it is not surrounded by blank parchment below, according to all opinions it is valid, for the letter then appears proper. However, the Rosh did not give this reason, but wrote only that the custom is to be stringent that it be surrounded by blank parchment. One may say the reason is that initially we make it like a Torah scroll; according to this, even below it must be surrounded by blank parchment. See Responsa Mishnat Binyamin, siman 23, which I cited at the end of seif 7. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 that only when a child can read it is it valid according to those who validate; but when a child cannot read it, all agree it is invalid. According to those who are stringent, even if a child can read it, it is invalid. So too it appears from Semag and from Responsa Rivash, siman 56, and so Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 101, ruled. At first glance this does not appear so from the Rosh and the Tur, since they wrote that if ink fell into a letter and it is no longer recognizable, he may not remove the ink; this implies that if letters touch one another, even if it is not recognizable, he may repair it.
