Inside the letter. Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 32, writes as follows: It appears to me that when it says “inside the letter,” this is to exclude a case where the drop fell on top of the letter but does not touch the body of the letter, in which case he may erase it. But where it fell inside the letter, whether it touches the sides of the letter or does not touch them, the matter depends on whether the letter is recognizable through a child’s reading. If so, he may scrape it away. If it is not recognizable, he may not scrape it away, because that would be chok tochot. End quote. See there.
