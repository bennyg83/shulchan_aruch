And there are those who are stringent. See Beit Shmuel, se'if katan 12, who writes that Seder Maharam, letter 87, is stringent in this matter if it is in the essential text of the get, even in a case of igun. But this does not seem to be the view of Maharik. See Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 37, who also writes that in a case of igun and pressing circumstances, even according to Maharik, one may erase the drop of ink. Even if the drop touches the sides of the letter, if a child reads it properly, he may scrape away the drop and it is not chok tochot. See there further, where he writes that if a drop of ink fell and the form of the letter did not change, and before the ink was absorbed into the parchment or paper the scribe quickly sucked the ink with his tongue and wiped it from the get, and it is not recognizable that there had been an erasure, it is logical to be lenient in this. Since even if the drop had dried there, it would not have changed the form of the letter, and according to the strict law it would be permitted to erase it even with a knife, except that as a stringency we do not erase with a knife because the erasure is noticeable, in this case one should not be stringent because the erasure is not noticeable. All the more so, if gallnut water or coffee water fell on the get and made stains, the get should not be invalidated, since the letter is recognizable. However, if actual ink fell on one word, or even on one letter, whether in the essential text or in the standard formula, and it changed the meaning of the divorce, and if the ink had dried there it would have ruined the form of the letter, but the scribe quickly wiped it with his tongue, it appears to me that it is invalid and is considered chok tochot. See there, and see what I wrote above, se'if katan 7. See the responsum of my revered grandfather, Panim Meirot, part 2, end of siman 47, concerning a get brought by an agent from far away. The woman’s name was שאשה, and there were two dots inside the heh of שאשה. It was recognizable that because the get had been folded, the leg of the alef in שאשה reached into the hollow of the heh, and because of the moisture of the ink, some ink from the leg of the alef attached itself inside the heh. It looked like two yuds inside the hollow of the heh. He wrote: In siman 125, se'if 8, it is explained that if a drop of ink fell inside the letter, and there are those who are stringent even if the letter is recognizable. The Bach explains that this applies not only where it is now read as another letter, such as where ink fell inside a bet and it became a peh, but even where it did not become another letter. However, it appears that there the case is where the form of the letter did not change, but nevertheless the drop is attached to the upper roof or to the leg on the side. This is unlike our case, where it is not attached, but merely an extra dot inside the heh. It is possible that everyone agrees that he may erase the extra part and that this is not chok tochot. It must be that the Chelkat Mechokek, se'if katan 12, also understood this way, for he asks that if the intent is to be stringent ab initio, there would have been no need to write it, as cited in Ba'er Heitev, se'if katan 10. If the view of these stringent authorities were that even if a drop of ink fell in the middle of the hollow of a letter, such as in a bet or heh, and it does not touch the sides, it is still prohibited, then what is his question from letters touching each other? There, they touch and meet; here, the drop does not touch any side. Rather, it is necessarily so that the view of the stringent authorities applies only where the drop touches the roof of the letter or the side, and nevertheless the form of the letter is recognizable. But if it does not touch any side, everyone agrees that it may be scraped away. All the more so in a case of igun, where one may rely on those who are lenient even if it touches the side of the letter but does not cover the letter entirely. If so, in our case it is obvious that one may rely on this in a case of igun. These are his words. See there. See above, se'if katan 14, and also the following se'if katan.
