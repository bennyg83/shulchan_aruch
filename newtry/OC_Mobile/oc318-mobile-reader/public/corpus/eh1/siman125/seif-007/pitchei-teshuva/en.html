See also the responsum of Maharam Lublin, siman 101, concerning a get that had already been given and was found to contain attached letters, namely a dalet attached to a heh in the word דהוית. He ruled that since most decisors validate it after delivery, there is no need to be stringent and be concerned for the view of the Or Zarua and Ra'avyah, cited in Seder Get of Rabbi Yehuda Mintz, siman 88, where it is explained that it is invalid even after delivery. They are a very small minority against the majority of decisors. However, this is on condition that a child who is neither especially wise nor foolish can read it. If not, it is obvious that another get must be required and it is invalid. See there. See Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 21, who questions this, for the word דהוית is part of the standard formula. Even if this word were omitted entirely, it would not change the meaning of the divorce and we would not be concerned after the fact once the get was given, as written in siman 126, se'if 49, and there in se'if 35 in the gloss. All the more so, it should not be invalidated after delivery because of attachment, even if a child cannot read it. See there. See the responsum of Noda BiYehuda, siman 85, concerning a get that was brought from a distant place by an agent. Because it had fallen into water, several letters became blurred, and the impression of the ink spread outside the letters, between the letters and in the spaces between lines, so that even outside the letters the parchment was black. Nevertheless, the letters themselves were recognizable, because the body of each letter was very black, while what was outside the letter was much lighter. The question was whether such a get should be invalidated because the letters were damaged and because they were not surrounded by parchment. He writes that certainly writing on top of writing does not help, even if the second writing is blacker. However, he was uncertain in a case where ink fell on blank parchment and the parchment remained black, and afterwards one wrote in that place with ink that blackens more strongly, so that the letters are clearly recognizable, whether this is called writing or not. It appears that this matter depends on a dispute between Rashi and Rabbenu Tam in Gittin 19a. Rashi explains, under the words “where it was treated with gallnuts,” that the appearance of the parchments is like the appearance of the writing, and the writing is not recognizable. The Shulchan Aruch above, se'if 3, also uses this language. This implies that if it were recognizable, it would properly be considered writing. But Rabbenu Tam there in Tosafot, under the words “ink,” writes that ink is not made from gallnuts, for how could we write a Torah scroll on parchment that was treated with gallnuts? See there. Now certainly ink made from gallnut water with vitriol is recognizable on gallnut-treated parchment, and nevertheless according to Rabbenu Tam it does not help. Since in this law we do not rule like Rabbenu Tam, as is explained in Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 271, and in the Beit Yosef there, it is proven that if it blackens more strongly and is recognizable on the parchment, it is considered writing. Furthermore, it appears that this is clear from Shulchan Aruch, siman 125, se'if 8, in the gloss, where the Rama writes, “There are those who are stringent even if the letter is recognizable,” as I wrote there. Since, according to the questioner, there is great concern of igun in this case, because the husband is in a distant country, caravans are not common, and there is the upheaval of war, the rabbi who asked ruled properly to validate the get. There is no concern because of being surrounded by parchment, nor because of damage to the letters, provided that one examines carefully that at least throughout the essential text of the get, every letter remains in its proper outline. These are his words. See there. See also Noda BiYehuda Tinyana, siman 113, concerning a get where, in the word היום, something like a thin line extended and connected the head of the vav to the closed mem. However, it was recognizable that it was not from the ink, but only from waste in the paper. He wrote: I do not know of any concern in this, since this line is not ink but only from the paper itself. As for the concern that perhaps writing over it does not help because it is black anyway, and it would be like ink on top of ink, see my work Noda BiYehuda, siman 85, and you will see that this is not a concern. These are his words.
