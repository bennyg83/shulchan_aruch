In pressing circumstances, to correct it. See Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel, who question the words of the Rav in the gloss. Part of this is cited in Ba'er Heitev, se'if katan 7. They agree as to the law that in pressing circumstances and a case of igun, or if the get has already been given, it is valid even without correction, although it is better to correct it, even by another person, because of appearances. When the husband, or the scribe who is the husband’s agent, is present, then even without igun it is valid to correct it before the witnesses have signed. See there. See Torat Gittin, who writes that it appears to him that this was also the opinion of the Rama, of blessed memory, but that there is a scribal error in his words and the word “or” is missing. The correct reading should be: “There are those who are lenient in pressing circumstances, or to correct it before they sign.” This means that in pressing circumstances it is valid even if it was not corrected, or that even not in pressing circumstances one may correct it before they sign. See the previous se'if katan. He further writes that where there is a complete invalidation, such as an extra letter in the essential text of the get, it does not help for another person to scrape away the letter, even in pressing circumstances. Since in such a case the get is completely invalid, the scribe did not fulfill his agency at all. For this reason, the scribe is permitted to write another get, as explained in siman 122. It is as if the whole get was written without agency, and when another person erases it, it is as if another person, who is not the husband’s agent, wrote it. But when the scribe himself scrapes it away, there is no invalidation except because there is an erasure in the get, and it is like a document written in a manner that could be forged. It is valid through witnesses of delivery in a case of igun, such as where the husband has abandoned the religion or the like, where there is no time to write another. Even with tefillin, scraping away a letter can help to validate it. One should not say in this case that it is chok tochot, since the word is corrected through scraping, because that concern applies specifically to a letter and not to words. See there. Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 36, also writes this. See there.
