A drop of ink. See Ba'er Heitev, which is the language of the Beit Shmuel. Regarding what he wrote, that it sounds as though biblically it is void, see Beit Meir, who discusses this at length and concludes that the essential ruling appears to follow the Rashba and the implication of most decisors: anything made through writing, even if it was made invalidly, but at least not automatically, is only rabbinic chok tochot. See there. In the middle of se'if katan 38, Ginat Veradim also appears to hold this way, unlike the Radach, siman 1. He mentions there that Rabbi Yehuda ben Chaviv and his son, the Ralbach, in responsum siman 1, also appear to hold this way, namely that every such case is only rabbinic because of a resemblance to chok tochot. This is also implied by the words of Semag and Sefer HaTerumah in the laws of tefillin. See there. What he further wrote, “even if a drop of ink fell after the letter was completed,” means to exclude the view of the Kol Bo and Semak, cited in the Beit Yosef, who hold that if ink fell after the letter had been made properly, there is no concern. See there. See Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 31, who also writes that we follow the view of most decisors who disagree with the Semak. Nevertheless, he writes that if she was divorced with a get in which this occurred, we count her as possibly divorced. There is also a practical consequence in using the view of the Semak and those who agree with him as a supporting factor, such as where the letters themselves became attached to their yuds, for example alef, peh, tzadi, and shin, aside from their proper place of attachment. There is a dispute among the great authorities whether they need correction, as written above and cited in our words above, se'if 6, se'if katan 8. If this attachment occurred after the letter was written properly, and one merely scraped away the attachment, this becomes a double doubt. In a case of igun and pressing circumstances, one may rely on the view of the Semak and those who support him. This can also be learned from the words of the Ralbach, siman 1. There is also a practical consequence from the view of the Semak and those who support him in a case where letters became attached to one another. It was already explained in se'if 6 that according to the strict law, attachment of letters does not invalidate a get, except that the custom is to be stringent that the letters be surrounded by parchment. If so, when the attachment is below, one may be lenient in pressing circumstances. But if it is above, one may be lenient only after the fact in a case of great igun. These are his words. What he further wrote, “and there is no distinction between whether the letter is moist or dry,” means to exclude the view of the Levush, who writes that if the ink fell after the letter had dried, and one removed the ink, it is valid. See there. See also Beit Shmuel, who writes that Seder Maharam explains the words of the Levush as referring to a case where a drop of ink fell on the letter and did not spread at all. See what I wrote about this in my commentary to Seder Gittin, in Seder Get Rishon, letter 87. What he further wrote, “however, in a case of igun, even a complete error may be scraped away,” until “and so ruled the Taz,” see Taz, se'if katan 17. He writes there that according to the strict law it is valid to write over an erasure if one wishes to rely on witnesses of delivery and bring them before the court, except that this is not the practice because we do not differentiate. In a case of igun, one need not be concerned, especially according to our custom to take the get after it is given and tear it, so that the matter will never come to relying on the witnesses of signature. I saw this reasoning in Terumat HaDeshen regarding another matter. These are his words. See there. What he further wrote, “and in the signatures of the witnesses it is permitted even ab initio to erase the entire letter,” makes clear that it is specifically writing over an erasure that is permitted. But chok tochot is invalid even in the signatures of the witnesses. This is also stated explicitly in the Taz there, and so writes the responsum of Mahari Weil, siman 27. See there. See Beit Shmuel below, siman 130, se'if katan 20, where it is explained that if chok tochot occurred in the name of the father of the witnesses, it is valid in a case of difficulty and igun; and if it occurred in the word עד, “witness,” it is valid even without difficulty or igun. See there. In Ginat Veradim above, se'if katan 18, it is explained that his view is that in the name of the father of the witnesses one may also be lenient even without difficulty and igun. See there. What he further wrote, “and if they already signed,” see what I wrote about this in my commentary to Seder Get Rishon, letter 87. What Ba'er Heitev further wrote, “and if a drop of wax fell, he may scrape away the wax,” see Ginat Veradim above, end of siman 124, who inclines toward stringency even if the wax was removed, because perhaps it resembles chok tochot. See there. However, Get Mekushar, in Kuntrus Acharon, letter 9, disagrees with him. His opinion is that even if he did not remove the wax, as long as it is possible to remove it, the get is valid. In practice, this requires further study. But if the wax was removed, it should plainly be validated. See there.
