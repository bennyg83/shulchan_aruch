They protest, and it is in his power. The Beit Shmuel writes that the reason is because of casting aspersions on earlier gittin. Only where the matter is merely a stringency do we worry about such aspersions. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 232, amud 2. See Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 31, who gives several distinctions in this matter, namely when we do not cast aspersions on the earlier ones. See also the responsa of Maharit, part 1, siman 86, regarding what he writes on this. See Tiv Gittin, in Likkutei Shemot, se'if katan 7, which will be cited in part below, siman 129, se'if 14, se'if katan 36. He writes that it appears obvious that the concern of casting aspersions on earlier gittin applies only to something that is practiced in all gittin given in that place, such as the name of the city or the wording of the body of the get. But regarding the names of the man and the woman, where each get is a matter unto itself, this does not involve casting aspersions. See there. See the responsa of Raanach, part 2, siman 11, who writes that they said “you are casting aspersions on the earlier generations” only in matters of marriage, because those who descend from them and are with us would be rendered blemished. But with other prohibitions we do not say this, for what happened has happened. See there. I also brought these words in Pitchei Teshuva, Yoreh Deah, siman 214, se'if katan 4, and there I noted that from Magen Avraham, siman 32, end of se'if katan 48, this does not seem so. See there. See also Be'er Heitev below, siman 128, se'if katan 13.
