That came from a distant land. See the responsum Perach Shoshan, kelal 1, end of siman 2, who cites that the responsa of Maharshach, part 2, siman 5, also writes that wherever caravans are common, it is not called a pressing circumstance. The responsa of the Radach, end of bayit 3, also writes this. However, the responsa of Rivaz, siman 340, writes that one who says that where the place is close and caravans are common there is no concern of igun at all, in his eyes this is close to an error in an explicit Mishnah. For we learned: One who brings a get from one country to another overseas must say, “It was written before me and signed before me.” The Gemara says about this that by right we should require two witnesses, but because of igun the Sages were lenient. This shows that even from a nearby place there is concern of igun, for the Sages did not make their words depend on measures. The reason is logical: who can tell us that while the get is being returned, the husband will not go overseas, or nullify the agency, or die? No one has control over the spirit, and there is no dominion. See there. I later saw in Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 79, that he also cites the above words of the Radbaz, disagrees with him, and concludes that one may not rely on this to be lenient and say that now in every place there is igun. Wherever caravans are common, it is not considered a place of igun. So too concluded Rabbi Maharadach. See there. He also mentions some of this in siman 126, se'if katan 65. See there. See Beit Meir, se'if 6, who writes that what the Ginat Veradim answered against the above proof of the Radbaz is not understandable. I also wrote in Nachalat Tzvi to question the Ginat Veradim on this from another angle. However, as to the law, the Ginat Veradim was correct, for Rashi explicitly explains on page 19 that “pressing circumstances” means that this person went on his way or she remarried. One cannot learn anything from the enactment of believing the agent, because they believed him due to a light concern of igun. Know this, for even when bringing a bill of manumission, he is believed. But for other matters, actual pressing circumstances are required. These are his words. See also what I wrote above, siman 42, se'if katan 6.
