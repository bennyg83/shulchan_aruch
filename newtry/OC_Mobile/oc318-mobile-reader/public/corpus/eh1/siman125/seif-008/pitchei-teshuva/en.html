Attached letters. Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 24, writes as follows: Regarding the attachment of the alef, peh, tzadi, and shin, where their yuds became attached to the letters themselves in another place besides their proper point of attachment, the Bach was asked about this in siman 1 concerning a Torah scroll. He answered that Rabbi Menachem wrote that this applies specifically where it became attached to another letter. But if it is attached to itself, as long as the form of the letter was not ruined, it is valid. This also appears from the words of the Rambam. He himself concludes that it requires correction. He also writes there that there are some attachments, even within the letter itself, that slightly change the form of the letter. Examples include the leg of a kuf becoming attached, the middle head of a shin becoming attached above, the yud of a peh becoming attached below, and the same applies to the heads of an ayin or tzadi. In those cases, the inner space is left closed on all sides. These require correction, and not by scraping alone. Rather, one must scrape away part of the letter in such a way that its name no longer remains upon it, and then write it again, so that it not be chok tochot. End quote.
