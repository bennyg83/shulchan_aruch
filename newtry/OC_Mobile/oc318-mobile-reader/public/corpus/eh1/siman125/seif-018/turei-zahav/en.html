(א) Even in the place of its writing. Beit Yosef concluded so and emended the Tur's text from "its giving" to "its writing." Not only as a matter of halachah did he rule to be concerned even for the place of writing. My teacher and father-in-law, z"l, disagreed and said that one need not be concerned for the place of writing. Beit Yosef's words appear correct, for this is similar to what is written in siman 129 seif 7 regarding one who has another name in the place of writing.

(ג) Some say it is invalid. My teacher and father-in-law, z"l, wondered at this, for in chapter 3, folio 6, Rav Papa asks: what is the law if he stipulated that the paper between the lines should be his? The Gemara leaves it unresolved. Why not derive that the Torah said "one book," not two or three books? It answers: it is needed where the lines are joined. From here one might hear that the reason for joined lines is so that it be one book, or so that it not be possible to forge and write between the lines, as these reasons are found in Seder Gittin. Nevertheless, invalidity is not heard from there, and the language "it is needed where it is joined" implies that ordinary gittin are not joined, only that this case speaks so. In all other kingdoms they do not write joined lines at all; therefore even not in a case of agunah, if one erred and wrote lines that are not joined, another get is not needed, and this one may be given ab initio. In my humble view, one can defend the invalidating view from the sugya. The questioner was uncertain whether even the space between the lines must belong to the woman, and therefore whether that space must be whole, such that it is not enough that the empty part be cut away even if some remains at each edge. The Gemara asks that it should obviously be invalid, for the Torah requires one book, meaning that even the space between the lines should be whole; only then is it one book. It answers that the verse was not needed for the space between the lines, but for the writing of the get, that the lines should be joined into each other. The empty parchment between the lines is not what the verse discusses, and that remains unresolved. Thus the verse indicates the joining of the writing, and this is required. This also answers the difficulty from siman 130, where if he left part of the get and wrote it on a second page, writing two pages is valid; we consider the second column as though placed below the first, like the laws of open and closed sections in tefillin. Therefore Rama ruled well that one should be lenient only in a case of agunah.
