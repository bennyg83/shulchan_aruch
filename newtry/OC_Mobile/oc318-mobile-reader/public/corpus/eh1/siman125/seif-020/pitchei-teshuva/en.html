See the new responsa of Bach, siman 87, who writes that regarding the case where the scribe wrote one line in the get somewhat far from the ruling line and it bent slightly downward, we have not found any concern in this. See there. See Pitchei Teshuva, Yoreh Deah, siman 271, se'if katan 13, where I wrote in the name of the new responsa of Radbaz, siman 156, regarding the practice of scribes to fold the paper on which they write the get in place of ruling lines. He writes that if the paper is firm enough that the folds will remain and be recognizable until the end of writing the get, this is sufficient. What difference is there whether he rules with a reed or with anything else that remains and is recognizable? The reason is so that the writing should be straight, and here that is achieved. See there. Now I have seen in Ginat Veradim, se'if katan 44, that he also writes that the custom became widespread in his place to fold the paper instead of ruling lines. The hollow of the fold’s crease must face outward, and the get should be written on the raised side of the fold. See there.
