(ג) But if she stands in her own domain. These are Ran's words brought by Beit Yosef: the unresolved question whether the airspace of four cubits acquires applies where the place is not her domain. Since the Sages enacted four cubits so that people not come to quarrel, perhaps they enacted the ground but not the airspace. But in a domain acquired to a person, it is obvious that he has its airspace, for it is his from the depths of the earth to the height of the heavens. Taz asks from seif 9, where once he threw the get into the airspace of the outer vessel, etc., it implies that above the airspace of the outer vessel it is not a get, and we do not say that it belongs to him from earth to heaven. He answers that there the walls of the outer vessel are not the walls of the inner owner; rather, the outer walls are merely subordinated for the inner vessel's use. Thus the airspace does not work in the same way as a person's actual domain. Where the domain itself is hers, its airspace acquires; where the right is only borrowed or dependent on another vessel, the airspace may not be treated as hers.
