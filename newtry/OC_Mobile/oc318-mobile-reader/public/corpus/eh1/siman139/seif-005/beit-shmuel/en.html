And there is one who says, etc. This is Rambam's view in chapter 5. According to the Ran's explanation, Rambam holds that since there is here an upward path and a downward path, meaning upward when he throws it upward, as long as it is on the upward path, if it was erased or burned it is not a Get until it began to descend. Therefore the Mishnah did not teach in the first clause that if it was erased or burned, because that is not definite; but in the latter clause it speaks entirely where the Get is descending and there is no upward path. According to the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, whenever it has an upward and downward path and he throws the Get upward contrary to its natural motion, she is not divorced until it comes to rest. If it was erased or burned before it came to rest, even if it was in the airspace of the partitions, she is not divorced. If it was not erased and then came to rest, then once it rests she is retroactively divorced from the time it reached within three tefachim or the airspace of the parapet's partitions. The practical difference is if the husband died, or if another man betrothed her in between; the kiddushin begin from the time it reached within three tefachim of the airspace of the partitions. According to this, also in the latter clause, namely what is written in seif 7, if there are partitions around the roof and he throws the Get upward to pass over the roof's partitions, it is not a Get even after it began to descend, until it rests, since he also threw it contrary to its natural motion. So wrote Rashakh in his novellae. It seems that here in Shulchan Aruch he ruled plainly according to the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, for he wrote if it burned before it came to rest, meaning that before it came to rest she is not divorced, even on the downward path. This follows that explanation. But what he wrote in seif 6, such as after it began to descend, is difficult either way: if the roof has no partitions, then immediately when he threw it, it was certainly on a downward path; and if it has partitions, she is not divorced until it rests. Perhaps he does not hold like the reasoning I wrote in the name of Rashakh, but rather whenever he throws it downward, even if he throws it to pass through partitions, its law is as though he threw it downward, and after it began to descend she is divorced. Rashi wrote: but if it was erased on the upward path she is not divorced, because the path of its removal is not called giving, only the path of its resting as it comes into her domain. Necessarily his intent is that if it was erased it is not giving as long as it was on the upward path; but if it was not erased and came to rest, then she is divorced from the time the Get was on the upward path. Bach inferred from Rashi that even if it was not erased, it is not a Get as long as it was on the upward path, and the reason the Mishnah taught if it was erased on the upward path was only because it taught erased on the downward path. This does not appear correct. All this applies when the Get is not within her four cubits. But if the Get is within her four cubits, she is divorced even if there is no parapet on the roof and it is not within three tefachim of the roof, because whatever is within her four cubits is guarded; so wrote Rashba. If the courtyard and the roof are hers and he throws it to the roof, nevertheless she is not divorced as long as the Get is in the airspace of the courtyard. Even though it is her airspace, since it is not destined to rest in the courtyard she is not divorced. Tosafot wrote that these laws are worse than airspace where it is not destined to rest, for airspace where it is not destined to rest speaks of a roofed house; and on the upward path it is not standing to rest at all.
