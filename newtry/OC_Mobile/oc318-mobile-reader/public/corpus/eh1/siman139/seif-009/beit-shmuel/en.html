This means that he threw it toward the inner partitions, but because the walls of the outer courtyard are high, if a wind comes the get will fall into the space of the outer courtyard. If so, the get is in the airspace of the walls of the outer courtyard and opposite the inner courtyard.
(ב) Into the airspace of the inner vessel. Regarding the airspace of a vessel, Rashi and Tosafot disagree. According to Rashi, she does not acquire, and likewise she is not divorced; according to Tosafot and Ran, she is divorced and acquires. The Mechaber, who wrote in Rambam's language, has no proof from what he wrote that even into the airspace of the inner vessel she is not divorced, because it speaks of a vessel that has no bottom and is not fit for use. Regarding such a vessel one can say that according to everyone its airspace does not acquire.
(ג) When it was tilted on its side, etc. He rules like Rambam, meaning that the inner vessel has no bottom, and then the husband is not particular; therefore her vessel acquires. But when it has a bottom, he is particular and she is not divorced, since it stands within his vessel. All the poskim speak where the outer vessel has no bottom; then the inner one does not stand inside his vessel, but on the ground. It must therefore speak where it stands on her ground, as written by Ran, for otherwise it is difficult: we hold that a vessel does not acquire even in a simta. We learn from here that a buyer's vessel inside a seller's vessel, standing on the buyer's ground, does not acquire; for otherwise why establish it with a basket that has no bottom? According to Maggid Mishneh's explanation that I wrote above, in all these cases if he was forced to throw it, she is not divorced.
