But he prohibits himself. See responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah vol. 1 simanim 149 and 156 — asked by his son-in-law R' Yitzchak Shpetz regarding one who said about himself "I am a kohen." Per strict law he is not believed on his own word; rather he views himself with all priestly disqualifications as one. If it happened that a kohen made this one an agent to sanctify a divorcee — per the sugya in Bava Metzia (10b): if a kohen told a kohen to sanctify a divorcee for him, since he is bar chiyuva there is no agency; the act is also void, as explained in responsa Noda BiYehuda siman 76 (brought below siman 119 s.k. 6). In this case there is room to doubt whether we only say he is believed to prohibit himself, but we are concerned about him lest he be an Israelite and we are concerned for kiddushin; or perhaps after priestly disqualifications are upon him as one — if he married or became impure he receives lashes per the Rambam — he is properly called bar chiyuva, and we are not concerned for his kiddushin. He wrote: per first reason it seems she is sanctified, and we hold him as the majority of the world who are not kohanim — only regarding himself he is believed to prohibit. He concluded that still requires study; per my first reason it tends the opposite.
