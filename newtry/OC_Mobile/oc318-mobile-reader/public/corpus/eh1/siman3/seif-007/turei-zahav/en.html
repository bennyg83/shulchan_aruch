One witness came, etc. — one witness has power to nullify rumor.
Two witnesses came; one insufficient to say rejected witness against witness and rumor remains to disqualify — since rumor already nullified through one witness — as if rumor never went out; remains initial presumption of fitness as above.
These are rejected, etc. Tosafot in the first chapter of Ketubot asked: where there are two witnesses against two witnesses, we rule stringently, as stated in chapter HaOmer in Kiddushin regarding Yannai the king. Ri answered that here it is a rabbinic doubt, for the Sages were stringent with two against two, and here the case concerns rabbinic terumah, so we rule leniently. This seif follows Rambam and Tur, and it seems clear that they too hold that the discussion here concerns only rabbinic terumah, for the law is made dependent on his father having been established as a kohen; Rambam already wrote in chapter 20 of Hilchot Issurei Biah that all kohanim today are presumed only for eating rabbinic terumah. My teacher and father-in-law made this a dispute between Tosafot and Rambam, but it does not appear so to me, as I wrote. There is further proof: the Tur here writes like Rambam, while in siman 47 he writes that we do not say "set two against two," etc.; necessarily there he speaks of a Torah prohibition.
Two like hundred — seems therefore Rambam wrote this Torah law why reject rumor — rumor assists disqualifiers; holds as three witnesses to disqualify — wrote no distinction three and two for everything.
