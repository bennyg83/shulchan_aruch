On the first day of the week. See Beit Shmuel, who wrote that one should not write "to the Shabbat," because then one may err, etc. In Get Pashut he writes that according to this, if the get was written on Wednesday and the get says "on the fourth to the Shabbat," with a lamed, which implies that the count begins from Shabbat, people may understand that it was written on Tuesday, since it is the fourth day from the previous Shabbat. If so, it is an antedated get and invalid, as written in siman 127, seif 2. However, it appears that bedieved one should validate it in such a case, and proof is from what Maran wrote in seif 21: if he wrote "on the fourth, twenty days," etc. Here too one can attribute the error to his thinking that "to the Shabbat" means the same as "in the Shabbat." Even the Rivash, who disagrees there, would appear to validate in this kind of case. End quote. What the Beit Shmuel wrote about the language of "yerach," see there, "geresh yerachim," is from the Levush. In Get Pashut he writes that it appears that if he wrote "to the month" and did not use the expression "in the yerach," one should validate bedieved. He further writes in the name of the Rashbam, siman 46, regarding a get in which instead of "on the first day of the week" it said "on the first in the week," and he responded that it is valid in a pressing circumstance, etc. See there.
