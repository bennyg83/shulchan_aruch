See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 85, regarding a get brought by an agent that fell into water, and in the word muteret the ink impression of the reish spread at the end of the roof toward the left and became like the leg of a tav. Nevertheless, a child read it as a reish, because the reish itself was truly black and what was formed through the accident was faint. He wrote that at first glance this is a major concern, because although that leg is not as black as the body of the reish, because of that it does not leave the category of a tav. If from the beginning one letter was written with one half much blacker than the other half, would it not be called writing for that reason? If so, the word muteret, as it now reads, is muteret, and the Ramban's view is that even for us this is the toref, and if he did not write it, even if she married she must leave, and his reason is explained in the decisors. However, I say that the Ramban was stringent only where the husband wrote it, or the scribe wrote it by the mouth of the husband, for then the husband can object and say that he intentionally did not command it to be written because he wanted to reserve promiscuity. Even in that case, this is only if the husband comes and objects and says explicitly that this was his intent, for otherwise there is a double safek: perhaps he did not intend to reserve promiscuity, and even if he did intend, there is still doubt whether "except your promiscuity" forbids, since that question was not resolved. See below, siman 137, seif 3. Even if we say like the decisors that a double safek does not help against a presumption of prohibition, as the Shach concludes in Yoreh Deah in the rules of double safek, seif 29, and if so even where the husband does not object we are uncertain that perhaps he intended to reserve promiscuity, nevertheless I say that certainly lehitnasba can include even promiscuity, as explained in the Ran in chapter HaMegareish. Rather, because of this we are concerned lest the husband or scribe reserved it. All this is where it was not written originally. But with a get where it was originally written and only afterwards became damaged, we see already that neither the husband nor the scribe reserved anything, and automatically it is included in lehitnasba. If so, "behold, you are permitted" is no longer considered part of the toref at all. All the more so in our case, where it is still recognizable to most, and a child also reads muteret, that one may rely on leniency. These are his words. See there. See the previous s.k. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, end of siman 113, regarding a get brought by an agent from a distant place, where in the word muteret the first tav had its left leg split and looked like a heh. They showed it to a child and he read it as tav, but he already knew how to read. They then showed it to two small children who did not know how to combine letters, and at first glance they said it was a heh, but immediately, within the same time, they said it was a tav. He wrote: The Ramban is the stringent one, that even for us, who write several expressions in the get indicating severance, nevertheless "behold, you are permitted" is the main toref. However, in my view it is obvious that where he did not write "behold, you are permitted" at all, that is where the Ramban was stringent. But if he skipped the word muteret and wrote "behold, you are to every man," he was not stringent. If so, in our case, remove this word entirely and she is permitted by force of the other words that help. This reasoning had not yet occurred to the Gaon, of blessed memory, when he wrote the responsum in the first edition of Noda BiYehudah, siman 86, mentioned above, and therefore there he needed to search for another permission. However, one can say that this error, where the tav became a heh and is read moheret, is worse, etc. Even according to what I explained in my work Noda BiYehudah, etc., he discusses this at length and concludes: Nevertheless, it appears to be lenient. First, if moheret means mohar, namely kiddushin and ketubah, he should have written "behold, you are permitted to be permitted." Furthermore, since the first child read muteret, and the two other children also immediately retracted and said it was a tav, my view inclines to be lenient and to permit the woman through this get, which the agent should give her properly and in the manner I wrote, brought above, siman 125, seif 16, s.k. 32: first he should deliver it as it is, then correct it, and then give it a second time to the woman as agent of the husband, as at first. These are his words. See there. See Responsa Olat HaGer, siman 40, regarding a get brought by an agent where the yud of the alef in the word adam of "behold, you are permitted to every man" was touching the body of the alef. He responded that at first glance it appears that if it were easy, she would need another get, as explained from the words of Hagahot Yad Aharon, brought above, siman 128, seif 10 in the gloss, in what he wrote there: if what should touch is not touching, etc. If it is in the tofes of the get, even without igun one may be lenient, which implies that if it is in the toref not in a place of igun, one should not validate.
