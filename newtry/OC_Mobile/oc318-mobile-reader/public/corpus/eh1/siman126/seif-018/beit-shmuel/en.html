It must not be, etc. So wrote the Rashba and the Tur. But Tosafot and Semag wrote that "and you are hereby permitted to any man" may be written on the last line, because it is only a repetition, since "sefer teruchin" was already written. However, "sefer teruchin" should not be written on the last line, because we do not learn from the last line, and it is as though it was not written. Although in their formula "patarit," "shevakit," and "you are hereby permitted" were already written earlier, nevertheless it was not written that this get releases her; therefore "sefer teruchin" is indispensable. From this it follows that "sefer teruchin" is part of the toref, even though "patarit," "shevakit," and "pashatit" were written earlier. The practical difference is that if it is omitted, the get is invalid. However, this is specifically if "and you are hereby permitted" is also omitted; otherwise, what difference would there be between "sefer teruchin" and "and you are hereby permitted"? The Semag wrote not to put it on the last line because then automatically "and you are hereby permitted" would also be on the last line. According to the Rashba, however, even "sefer teruchin" is valid on the last line, even according to the view requiring "vedein." In the Talmud, Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages dispute: according to the Sages it is enough to write "you are hereby permitted," and so ruled the Rambam. According to the Rambam, even "sefer teruchin" is not indispensable, and all the rest is tofes. According to Rabbi Yehudah, one must write "vedein di yehevei," and the Talmud leaves unresolved whether "vedein" is required. Other decisors rule that it is required, and if not written she is doubtfully divorced. It also implies that "you are hereby permitted" is required according to everyone and is the toref. It is not invalidated thereby. Because the phrase "according to the law of Moshe and Israel" functions like "valid and established" in other documents, and then we do learn from the last line. So wrote the Rosh in a responsum. Although Rabbenu Tam was concerned that "according to the law of Moshe and Israel" may not count as "valid and established," bedieved it is valid. See Beit Yosef, who wrote that if "sefer teruchin" and "you are hereby permitted" were written on the last line, if the delivery witnesses come before us and testify that it was not forged, it helps, just as if it was written on something that can be forged. According to this, the delivery witnesses must specifically come before us. Although in Choshen Mishpat, siman 44, Beit Yosef cites Rashba that if the last line was not repeated, even if the witnesses say "we wrote it," they are not believed to validate it, one must say that there it refers to signing witnesses; delivery witnesses help, as written in siman 124.
