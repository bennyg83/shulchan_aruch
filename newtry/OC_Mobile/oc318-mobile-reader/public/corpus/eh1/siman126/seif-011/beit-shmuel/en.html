He should lengthen the vav of tirukhin. The reason is that if it looks like a yod, it implies that it refers to women in general and is not speaking to his wife. According to Rambam, the reason is that it would be as though she divorced him. The vav of piturin depends on whether one writes a yod after the peh: if a yod is written, there is no concern and one need not lengthen the vav; but if he did not write the yod, then he must also lengthen the vav of piturin. So wrote the Rosh. See the writings of Mahara'i, siman 221, regarding shefat and pashat. In the word mehekh the yod is missing, because with a yod it would imply "to me you shall be." Even when reading it, one should hasten the reading of the mem into the lamed; Nachalat Shiv'ah. In the phrase de-la anisna, one must be careful that the bet not look like a kaf, and it should be written on one line; see Responsa Ben Sasson, siman 30. In the word bir'ut, the resh is read with patach and the ayin with shuruk, and the vav with patach; Levush. The vav of vekhadu must be lengthened so that it not look like a yod, which would imply that he divorces her without anything, without a document. Rambam wrote the reason that it would imply, "with this condition I divorce you." What is written that it need not be lengthened will be explained below, with God's help. The heh of mehekh should not look like a chet, for then it would be language of mockery, "I am mocking you." In lehitnasba, one should not distance the lamed from nasba, for then it would imply "you shall not marry." Yimcha is read with the yod in sheva, the mem in patach, and the chet in tzere, and so it is in Daniel; Nachalat Shiv'ah, folio 23.
