See Get Pashut, who writes that from this one can learn that when the husband writes a get to send to his wife in another city, where they do not write "who stands," etc., as written in siman 128, seif 2, one should not write in it "and every name that you have," and not "and your place," namely the word "and your place," since the woman's place is not mentioned. So too Rika"sh wrote that if he did not write the name of his place, he should not write "and my place." So it also appears from the responsum of Ralbach, page 201. See Responsa Chaim Sha'al, part 1, siman 39, letter 13, regarding an incident of a get through an agent where the scribe erred and wrote the word "and after you," and Rabbi Mahari Zayin ruled in a handwritten responsum, as practical halacha, to validate it, and the rabbis agreed with him. See there. The same applies to a get of the children of anusim, where the father of the divorcing man has no Jewish name but only a non-Jewish name. The Ribal wrote, part 3, siman 6, that they should write two gittin, one with the father's non-Jewish name and one with only the name of the divorcing man. Maran wrote in a responsum, page 65, that one should write only the name of the divorcing man, and for extra clarity write his nickname, which can stand in place of his father's name. When writing "and every name that I have," one should not write the word "and my father's house," since he did not mention his father's name in the get. So I have seen practiced here in Jerusalem. Once it happened that they wrote a get for the son of an anus by his name and nickname, and the scribe forgot and wrote "and my fathers," and they validated it and gave it to the woman, since in the earlier forms we saw that they would write "and the places of your fathers," even though they did not mention the residence of their fathers.
