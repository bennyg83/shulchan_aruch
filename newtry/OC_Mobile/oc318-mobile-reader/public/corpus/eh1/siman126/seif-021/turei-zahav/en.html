(א) If he wrote on Wednesday, etc. The Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh that the get should not be invalidated because it says the twentieth of Shevat but was found to be the twenty-first, which would make it postdated or predated by one day. Since it is known that Rosh Chodesh Shevat was on Thursday, and Wednesday cannot be the twentieth but must be the twenty-first, we attribute this to a scribal error: he did not remember the calendar fixing of the month, and it is not treated as a predated get. Although we hold that people err in the month's fixing only until most of the month has passed, and after most of the month they do not err, here there is no such distinction, because necessarily he erred: his words contradict each other. If it was Wednesday, it was the twenty-first; if it was the twentieth, it was Tuesday. Thus either he erred about the correct day of the month or between Tuesday and Wednesday, and people are not commonly mistaken about the weekday. Therefore we preserve the get by attributing the mistake to the date calculation. The discussion continues that where the contradiction itself identifies the error and no one can reasonably use the get to collect unlawfully from an earlier time, the invalidity of a predated get is not applied in the ordinary way.
