So is the custom. See Get Pashut, who discusses this at length and brings the words of the decisors in their responsa, one side against another. At the end he concludes that for Sephardim, who customarily write piturin with two yuds, if the scribe wrote it missing the first yud and the get has not yet been delivered into the woman's hand, one should write another get with two yuds. But if the time is pressing, such as a get of a deathly ill person, or a get with time pressure where the husband is hurrying to leave, or a get that came from overseas and is missing the first yud, one should validate and deliver it to the woman lechatchilah. It is needless to say that if the get was already delivered into the woman's hand, there is no concern requiring another, even if the husband is before us. The same applies to Ashkenazim, whose custom is to write piturin missing the first yud: if they wrote it full, one should write another get if it is not a pressing circumstance. If it is a pressing circumstance, or if it has already been delivered to the woman, there is no concern requiring another get. See there, and what I wrote below, seif 49, s.k. 50.
