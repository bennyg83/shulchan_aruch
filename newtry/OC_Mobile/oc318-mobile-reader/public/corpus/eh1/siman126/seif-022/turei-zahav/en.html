(ג) And some say that if he did not object, etc. The Tur wrote: Baal HaItur wrote in the name of the great authorities that all these exact spellings apply specifically when the husband writes, or the scribe wrote from his mouth, and the husband comes and says, "I intended to change it in order to ruin her." But if he told the scribe plainly to write and sign his get, and they erred in one of these matters, and the husband does not object, it is valid and she may marry ab initio. So wrote Rav Hai: if the husband gave the scribe permission to write and the scribe erred in them, if the scribe is present he corrects them, and if he did not correct and she married with this get, we do not remove her. If she comes to marry and the husband comes and objects, saying that when it is written "vedein" I said "law," and that is what I intended, she may not marry; and if she married, we do not remove her. End quote. The details are difficult with one another, for at first he said that if the husband does not object it is valid and she may marry ab initio, yet afterward he says only that if she married we do not remove her. One must distinguish: where the error is one that can be corrected, we correct it if possible; if not, and there is no objection, it is valid. But where the husband objects and gives a corrupt intent to the wording, we are stringent before marriage.
