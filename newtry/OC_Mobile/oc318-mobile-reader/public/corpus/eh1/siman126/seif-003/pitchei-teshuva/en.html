Only in a case of igun. In Get Pashut, s.k. 10, he writes that one should investigate a case where no one is found who knows how to write proper Ashurit, but there is someone who can write defective Ashurit, such as the writing called among the Sephardim mashki, which resembles Ashurit, and there is someone who knows how to write Provencal script, which is what is called Rashi script. He wrote beautifully, and the case is one of urgency and igun, such as a deathly ill man who wants to give a get so that his wife not fall to a minor yavam, or one who wants to travel overseas. Which of these is preferable for writing the get? This matter is a dispute among the authorities, according to what Maran the Beit Yosef brought. According to the view of those who hesitate, mashki script is preferable because it resembles Ashurit. According to Maran, Provencal script is preferable, because it is an independent script and is no worse than Samaritan script. This is unlike mashki script, for since it is like Ashurit, it must be written with full precision, and even the point of a yud invalidates. It is logical like Maran, because in mashki script, since the letters are not properly formed, perhaps the reader will read a beit as a kaf or the reverse, and similarly with other letters, one letter may resemble another and the meaning will be changed. This is not so with Provencal script. However, if it is actual Ashurit script and the letters do not resemble one another, even though it is not straight and beautiful like the Ashurit written in a Sefer Torah, nevertheless it is preferable to Provencal script.
