With five yuds. So wrote Tosafot. From this Maharil infers that it is all one word, for otherwise it would not be appropriate to say five yuds. However, Tosafot also wrote that "di," the translation of "asher," and "deyitehevyin" are two words. According to this Tosafot appear contradictory. The Rosh and Tur also imply they are two words. It appears to me that although they are two words, one writes them on one line, because the meaning can also tolerate one word; but if written on two lines it would be specifically two words. So wrote the Chelkat Mechokek. Even on one line one should not distance the word "di" much, but only slightly. Levush. In pressing circumstances, or if it was already given, it is valid, for there is no change in the get’s meaning, and Maharil’s invalidation is not conclusive. So wrote the Taz.
