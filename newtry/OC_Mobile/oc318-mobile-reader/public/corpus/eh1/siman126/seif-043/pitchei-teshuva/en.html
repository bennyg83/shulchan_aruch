One must write "and behold, you are permitted." See Beit Shmuel. See Get Pashut, s.k. 96, who discusses this at length and concludes: Regarding halacha, certainly lechatchilah, even though he wrote lemehach lehitnasba, he must write "behold, you are permitted." If he did not write it, he must write another get. However, if she was already divorced with a get that does not contain "behold, you are permitted," but it does contain lemehach lehitnasba, and it is a place of igun where it is impossible to obtain another get, it appears to me that we can rely on most decisors, who hold that "behold, you are permitted" does not invalidate, since there are other expressions that are considered like "behold, you are permitted." In particular, we find that in the earlier get forms, "behold, you are permitted" was not written. Furthermore, there is a double safek here. Even if he said to her "except your promiscuity," it is an unresolved question whether that is considered a reservation. If so, one can say: perhaps "except your promiscuity" is a reservation or perhaps not, and even if it is a reservation, perhaps that is specifically where he says explicitly "except your promiscuity," but when he says lehitnasba he did not reserve promiscuity, and this is a reversible double safek, etc. So too, if he wrote "behold, you are permitted" but did not write lemehach lehitnasba, etc., and she was already divorced with this get, and it is a place of igun where it is impossible to obtain another get because the husband has gone far away, it appears in my humble opinion that one may permit, because the language "behold, you are permitted to every man" includes promiscuity and marriage according to the view of most decisors, and only the Ran, of blessed memory, is found to be stringent in this, etc. See above, s.k. 26. See there further, where he wonders about Maran and Moram, of blessed memory, why they did not write that one must write "and this shall be for you from me." The word "this" must be written so that there are clear indicators that he divorces her with this get and not with speech, and the word "from me" must also be written so that there are clear indicators that he is divorcing his wife, etc. In truth, Maran the author mentioned this above, seif 9, and below, seif 44, but the intent of Get Pashut is why he did not mention that it is essential. He concludes that if he did not write "and this" or the word "from me," and she was divorced with that get, it appears to him that we judge her as doubtfully divorced. See there.
