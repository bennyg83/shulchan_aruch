If he wrote "Adar" without specification, it is valid. This is the view of most decisors, and so is written in Orach Chaim, siman 422, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 43. There is no distinction whether he knew it was a leap year or did not know. In Yoreh Deah, siman 222, the Mechaber cites the Rambam’s view distinguishing whether he knew or not. The Shach there asks why he was not concerned here for the Rambam’s view, and the Bach also ruled that if he knew it was a leap year, it is a doubtful get and one should not be lenient. Were it not for their words, it would appear that we hold like most decisors, that plain "Adar" means the first Adar and there is no distinction whether he knew or not. Only in vows, which involve a biblical prohibition, did he take the Rambam’s view into account. Here there is no biblical prohibition, for even according to the Rambam it is only postdated, and a postdated get is valid according to most decisors. Therefore he was not concerned to bring the Rambam’s view here. So wrote the Taz.

If he wrote it with one yud. Terumat HaDeshen wrote that if it was discovered immediately, he should give another get; if it was discovered only later, he need not give another get. So wrote Levush. Although with other invalidities there is no such distinction, here there is no real invalidity and it is different. Some are stringent to give two gittin in Iyar, as Darkei Moshe writes in the name of Maharil. The Shulchan Aruch omitted his view because he holds we do not give two gittin except under pressure, and then it is better to write with two yuds, because two gittin are written only with difficulty. "Marcheshvan" is one word. Regarding "Kislev," Terumat HaDeshen wrote that in Scripture it is found lacking, while in Targum it is written full; we follow Scripture. Although most of the get is in Aramaic, the date is written in Hebrew: "two days," not "trei yomin." Therefore the date is written in Hebrew, the language of Scripture. This is not like "yimcha" with an alef, where we follow the Aramaic language, because "yimcha" is written among Aramaic wording, unlike the date. If he wrote "Kislev" full, it is valid, like "shemoneh," and so with all such cases.
