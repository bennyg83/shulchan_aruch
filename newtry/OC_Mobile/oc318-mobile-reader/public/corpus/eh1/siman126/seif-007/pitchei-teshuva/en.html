And he wrote twelve. In Get Pashut, s.k. 21, he writes that it appears this means where he placed dots over the letters yud-bet, etc. See there. He further writes in s.k. 73 that one may be uncertain whether this applies only in such a case as "yud-bet of the month," and similar expressions that people commonly write or say in this way, but if the get was written on the seventeenth of the month and he wrote "on the day tov of the month," perhaps not. My view inclines that even in this one should validate, because people sometimes write this way in shtarot and letters of greeting. Also in numbering years, people commonly write verses that allude to the number of the year, such as for the year tav-lamed-zayin they write the year "tizal katal," and they put two dots over the word. Similarly, they write in each year, and if something like this was written in a get, my view inclines that it is valid. These are his words.
