Only lehitnasba. See Ba'er Heitev, who wrote that the reason is lest he distance, etc. This is the language of Rashi in chapter HaMegareish, page 85b. It implies that bedieved, if he wrote leitnasba and did not distance the alef from tinasba, it is valid. However, in Get Pashut, s.k. 52, he brings the responsum of Ralbach, page 201b, who wrote that although Rashi, of blessed memory, wrote that the reason for this invalidity is lest he distance it and write it as two words, nevertheless even if he wrote it as one word, if he wrote it with an alef the get is invalid. What Rashi wrote, lest he distance, etc., is the reason for the invalidity, but even without distancing it is always invalid. So it appears from the words of all the decisors, etc. See there. What the Ba'er Heitev noted about the language of the Beit Shmuel is simple: it is a scribal error in the Beit Shmuel, where one shin is missing from the word shelo, and it should read "so that he not distance," etc.
