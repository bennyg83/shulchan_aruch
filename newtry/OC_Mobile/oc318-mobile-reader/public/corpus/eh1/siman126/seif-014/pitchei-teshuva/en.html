See Get Pashut, who brings the words of the Radbaz, siman 222, who was asked about a get written in the language of witnesses: "Before us, the witnesses, so-and-so dismissed and sent away so-and-so," and, "Thus he said to her: I, so-and-so," etc., in the language of the get, and the witnesses signed and gave it to her. Does she become divorced with this get or not? Or where he wrote, "I, so-and-so, dismissed so-and-so," etc., and the whole get is written not in direct address, but in the language of an admission before witnesses that he did such-and-such, and the witnesses signed and gave it to her. Is it a get or not? He responded at length, and it is clear from his words that a get written in the language of witnesses or in the language of an admission not in direct address is not a get. He, of blessed memory, wrote about this that not all are equal regarding something written not in direct address, etc. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 191, seif 3. He brought the view of several great authorities in their responsa to validate. See there.
