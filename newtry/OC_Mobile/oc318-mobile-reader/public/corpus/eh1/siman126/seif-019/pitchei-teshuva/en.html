Three consecutive yuds. See Ba'er Heitev, what he wrote according to Rashi's explanation of the reason, etc. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 113, regarding a get in which the second leg of the tav of dehavayit was remembered in the middle and looked like a heh. He wrote that although there is a change in meaning, since dehavayit speaks directly to her, "who was my wife," while with a heh it speaks about a hidden woman, that she was his wife, nevertheless bedieved or in a place of igun it is valid. This is as the Pri Chadash wrote, s.k. 28, regarding another similar matter. Although it is explained in Shulchan Aruch, siman 126, seif 22, that if he did not write the extra yuds, meaning tehevyin and titzviyin, it is invalid, there it is worse, because "you shall be permitted and in control of yourself to go and marry," etc., is the main severance. If this is missing from the get, or its meaning refers to women in general, he did not permit her through the get at all, and "behold, you are permitted" is not a permission for marriage but only a permission for promiscuity, that she remains permitted to every man, as explained by the decisors. The primary permission is "to go and marry." If there is a meaning referring to women in general, he did not permit her. But dehavayit antati, even if he wrote dehavayah and it refers to another woman, this is not the essential part of the get, and afterwards he again speaks to her directly: yatichi, lechi, di tehevyin, etc., benafshechi, etc. If so, he has permitted her. What do we care if in the middle of the get he wrote dehavayah? In any case, in a place of igun it is valid. These are his words. See there.
