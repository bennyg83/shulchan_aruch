Or he skipped the word antati. In Get Pashut, s.k. 89, he wonders that from the flow of the Rama's language it sounds that he discusses the second word antati, where instead of "dehavayit antati from before now" he wrote "dehavayit from before now." The source reference is marked as Radach. In truth, in the responsum of Radach, bayit 4, he discusses one who skipped the first antati, where instead of "and I dismiss you, you, my wife so-and-so," he skipped the word antati. About this he responded that several great authorities had the practice not to write it, because since he writes to her that he is dismissing her, it does not fit well to call her now his wife, etc. If so, from where do we know that he would validate if he skipped the second antati? Nevertheless, regarding halacha he concludes that if he skipped both, the first antati and the second antati, it is an invalid get, because the get must clarify with clear indicators that it is his wife whom he is divorcing. But if he wrote antati once and skipped the second, whether he wrote the first and skipped the later one or wrote the later one and skipped the first, it is valid lechatchilah to divorce with it if it has already been written, even not in a pressing circumstance.
