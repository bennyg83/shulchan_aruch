The Raanach, part 1, siman 14, was asked about a get and a shtar testifying to its delivery, where the shtar said that so-and-so divorced on such-and-such condition. Thus he said: "This is your get if I do not appear," etc.; "it shall be a get from now and you shall be divorced," etc. In a place where they needed to write vetiheyi megureshet, which is feminine second person, they wrote vetiheyeh, which is third person. See what I wrote in my commentary to Seder Get Rishon, letter 231. The witnesses testified that the husband arranged his words properly and said it to her in a foreign language in direct address, but the scribe and witnesses were not skilled in grammar and thought vetiheyeh could also be said to a present female. At first the Rav, of blessed memory, hesitated because of the case of Ralbach, page 207, who invalidated a get that had written "you are my wife wherever you are found," etc. In the end he concluded to validate, because people are prone to err in this, and the witnesses are believed when they explain their words, even though their handwriting is verified from elsewhere. That case of Ralbach is different, because it was in the get itself, where there is greater care, and also the witnesses were not there to explain their words. See there. See the responsum of Radbaz, part 4, siman 85, regarding one who said, "This is your get on condition that you do not marry so-and-so." There was someone who wished to void the condition because he did not say tinase'i with a yud. The Rav responded that it is a complete condition, because he is speaking to the witnesses: I divorce this woman on condition that she not marry so-and-so, etc. See there. The Ra'ah, part 1, siman 85, was asked about a get brought by an agent, where the agency shtar contained expressions in direct address and expressions not in direct address. That get was delivered before Ralbach, of blessed memory, and the husband arose and objected, saying that he intended through the change of language in the agency shtar that no beit din should involve itself in delivering the get because of the changed language. The authorization text was that the agent should say to her: "This is your get on condition that if I do not come to her," etc., "she shall be divorced." Some rabbis of Salonika wanted to invalidate that get since the husband objected. The Ra'am responded: I saw the authorization in the agency shtar and did not see doubts or changed expressions, only a change between direct and indirect address. This is not a reason for a beit din to refrain from working on delivery of this get, because such expressions are already found in the holy tongue, etc. See there.
