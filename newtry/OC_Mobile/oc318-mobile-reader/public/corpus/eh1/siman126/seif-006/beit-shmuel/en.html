On the first of the month. Although in seif 4 he ruled that one writes "on day one of the month," Bach wrote that if Rosh Chodesh is only one day, one writes "on day one," because it is clearer. But when Rosh Chodesh is two days, one should not write "on day one," because that would imply the first day of Rosh Chodesh; rather, one writes "on the first of the month," because he intends only to mention the count of days in the month. The Rama evidently does not accept this distinction, for otherwise why would one not write on the second day, where there is no doubt at all? Rather, according to everyone one does not write "day." The reason a get is not given on the first day of Rosh Chodesh is that several decisors wrote not to give a get then, because one would need to write that it is day thirty of the previous month, and people might say it is predated. This is unlike the Chelkat Mechokek, who gave the reason as the dispute between the Ran and Rashba. In fact they hold one can give a get on the first day of Rosh Chodesh. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that on the first day of Rosh Chodesh one should not give a get except in a case of igun, while on the second day there is only a mere concern, like when Rosh Chodesh is one day.
