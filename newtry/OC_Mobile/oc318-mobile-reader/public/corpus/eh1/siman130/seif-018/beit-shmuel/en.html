That the scribe should not sign as a witness. The Talmud explains the reason: we are concerned that perhaps the husband will tell two people, "Tell the scribe to write, and so-and-so and so-and-so shall sign," and because of the scribe’s embarrassment they will have one of those witnesses and the scribe sign together; or he will tell two people to tell the scribe to write and "you sign," and because of embarrassment they will tell the scribe to sign too. Although we hold that "tell others" is invalid, so the scribe should not write unless he heard from the husband, nevertheless that invalidity is because of doubt, and we are stringent in both directions. It follows that "tell others" and "scribe plus one witness signed" are two contradictory concerns, and we are stringent for both. If someone gave two gittin, one through "tell others" and one signed by the scribe and one witness, one of them is valid whichever way you look at it. Likewise, if he told three people to do as they wished, either write and sign the get themselves or tell others to write and sign, and one of the three died, there is the above remedy, and it is valid whichever way you look at it. However, the Talmud explains that if he said to two, "Tell the scribe to write and so-and-so and so-and-so to sign," there is another concern, that she may hire witnesses who will say this in the husband’s name. According to that, the above whichever-way remedy does not apply in such a case, for perhaps both gittin are invalid. If he said to three to write and sign and one died, in a case of igun and pressure one can say it is valid for them not to sign at all and give it before witnesses, because according to the Rif and Rambam it is valid, and even according to us I wrote at the beginning of the siman that if the get was given without signing witnesses it is valid in a pressing case of igun. If two witnesses signed and also the scribe, according to the Rosh it is valid bedieved; according to the Ran it is valid even lechatchilah, while the Mordechai writes it is invalid. Beit Yosef writes that where no other get is possible, it is valid. Rivash rules that with a scribe and one witness, if she remarried she need not leave. If a get was brought from a place that validates scribe-plus-one-witness to a place that invalidates, see Responsa Rashba. If the witnesses told the scribe to sign their names, see the gloss. The scribe may be one of the delivery witnesses. Perishah, siman 141.
