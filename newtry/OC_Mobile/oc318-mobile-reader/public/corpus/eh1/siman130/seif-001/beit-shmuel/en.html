(א) Two witnesses must sign. The Gemara explains that Rabban Gamliel enacted that witnesses sign. According to R. Elazar, who says delivery witnesses effect the divorce and signing witnesses are unnecessary, Rabban Gamliel enacted a good counsel that witnesses sign, because sometimes the delivery witnesses die or go overseas. According to R. Meir, who says signing witnesses effect the divorce and they must sign by law, Rabban Gamliel enacted that they specify their names, for at first they did not specify them; he enacted this so that the signatures could be validated easily. Although for us the get is torn immediately and these reasons do not apply, nevertheless ab initio one must write the get so that it is valid even according to R. Meir. So wrote Terumat HaDeshen, siman 232. Still, it remains difficult for us: since we tear the get immediately after delivery, why must they specify their names? Even according to R. Meir, they specified their names only because Rabban Gamliel enacted this so the signatures could be validated, and now the get is torn. If so, even if they do not specify their names it should be valid even according to R. Meir. This is the reason of Rashba and Ri"v, who wrote that they need not specify their names. But Rambam, the Tur, and the Mechaber wrote that they should specify their names, and this is difficult as above. For Rambam one can say that he wrote the law as it applied in the Gemara, when they did not tear the get. One can also say that he holds that according to R. Meir they must specify their names by law, while Rabban Gamliel enacted that they specify both their names and their fathers' names so that validation be easy; today, in truth, he holds that they need not specify their fathers' names. But the Mechaber is difficult, for in Seder HaGet he wrote that the get is torn, while here he wrote that one must write his name and his father's name. See Tosafot at the beginning of Gittin and the Rosh in Perek HaSholeach, who wrote that Rabban Gamliel's enactment that they sign does not mean they must sign, but is good counsel. Although Tosafot wrote this according to the first answer, which establishes the sugya according to R. Elazar, who says delivery witnesses effect the divorce, nevertheless even according to R. Meir the law is so. Rashbatz wrote that by strict law the witnesses' signatures need not be in Ashurit script; if a sharp child can read it, it is valid even where a non-sharp child cannot.

It is difficult why the Mechaber ruled in Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, that if they distanced the signatures two lines it is invalid, and did not distinguish whether delivery witnesses are before us. It is even more difficult on the Rambam, for he holds that in gittin signing witnesses alone suffice, yet he rules here that if there are delivery witnesses it is valid, while in Hilchot Malveh he invalidates. Perhaps documents are different because they must stand for many days. If a witness erred and signed Yosef ben Shimon, while his father’s name was Reuven, and then signed again Yosef ben Reuven, Beit Yosef cites Tashbetz that it is invalid, because the signature Yosef ben Shimon is not a real person here and it is like forgery within the document. This should not be compared to Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, where invalid witnesses signed merely to fill space, because with a get we do not say they signed only to fill space, as written in siman 120 in the name of Tosafot. The Taz compares the case here to that law, but this does not appear correct.
