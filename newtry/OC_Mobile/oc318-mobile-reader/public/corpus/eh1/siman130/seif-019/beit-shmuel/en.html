Nevertheless it is invalid. The Rosh and Ran write that the halacha follows the first Tanna, that the get is invalid, because we decree lest one rely on non-distinct gentile names. They do not give the reason that it is as though no witnesses are signed on this get, since the signatures are gentiles and we hold both signing and delivery witnesses are needed, because for that reason alone, if the get was already given, it would be valid as written above. Rather, because of the decree regarding non-distinct names it is like forgery within the get and invalid even if already given. According to Rabbenu Chananel and the Ra'avad, the halacha follows Rabbi Shimon that with distinct gentile names the get is valid, but this is only when it is known there were delivery witnesses, because from these signatures we do not presume there were certainly delivery witnesses. A practical difference in the explanations is a get that comes before us with the witnesses’ names like gentile names. According to the Ra'avad, distinct names mean we say the signers are gentiles and the get is nevertheless valid with delivery witnesses. According to the Geonim, we also say they are gentiles. According to the Rosh, we say the signers are Jews, because one would not have gentiles sign with such names; they must be Jews. Therefore, although the Mechaber ruled stringently where it is known they are gentiles that even distinct names invalidate, nevertheless at the end, where a get comes before us and the witnesses’ names are like gentile names, one should be concerned for the Ra'avad’s view that perhaps they are gentiles. If it was given before delivery witnesses, it is valid whichever way you look at it. However, we do not say there were certainly delivery witnesses according to the Geonim. The Mechaber wrote she may marry with it because they are certainly Jews, implying even if we do not know whether there were delivery witnesses. That follows the Rosh. According to the Ra'avad it is invalid in such a case, because there are no signing witnesses and we are concerned there may not have been delivery witnesses. The Rashba and Ran also appear to explain the sugya like the Ra'avad; therefore it appears one should be stringent.
