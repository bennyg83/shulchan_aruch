Invalid. According to the Rambam’s approach, signing witnesses alone suffice. If there are no delivery witnesses, there is a distinction: if it is proven that the signed witnesses do not refer to this get, the get is void; but if it is not proven, such as where there is an added greeting, or where he left part of the get and wrote it on the second page, it is a doubtful get. Even if he did not write the vav of "vesha'alu," one can still say the signatures may refer to the get, and it is only doubtful. What is written nearby that if they signed at the top of the get and similar cases are invalid is not precise; in such a case it is void, because it is proven they did not sign on this get. According to this, what is written in seif 6 that if there are delivery witnesses it is valid, even when they signed at the top and it is proven they did not sign on the get, must mean bedieved after the get was given; lechatchilah it should not be done, because we require both delivery and signing witnesses. We learn from this that a get with no signing witnesses at all, given before delivery witnesses, is valid even if she has not remarried. Otherwise the term "valid" would not apply. So wrote the Perishah in siman 133, and so it implies in the gloss to seif 8. Darkei Moshe at the end of this siman, however, cites Hagahot Ashri that if the scribe wrote and one witness signed, even if it was delivered before witnesses and given, she should not marry. See the end of the siman. It follows that if the get itself does not prove who is divorcing, such as where there are two Yosef ben Shimon, but the delivery witnesses testify who the divorcing man is, the get is valid even if she has not remarried. Just as according to Rabbi Meir, who holds signing witnesses effect the divorce, the identity must be evident within the get, these two laws depend on each other. For us, if the get was given without signing witnesses it is valid; likewise where the identity is not evident within it. This is unlike Responsa Shevut Yaakov, who ruled she should not marry with it, though if she married she need not leave.
