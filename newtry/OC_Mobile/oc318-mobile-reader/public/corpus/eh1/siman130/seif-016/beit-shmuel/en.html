They read before them. See Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, where he writes that according to the Rif and Rambam, the sugya speaks where two read before them. Regarding a get we are lenient that they may sign because two read before them, but if one reads before them, they may not sign. In other documents, even if two read before them, one should not be lenient. In the novellae of Rashach he writes that according to the Rambam, even one reader before them is enough, and that the correct text in the Rambam is not "they read" but "he reads" before them, meaning one person reads. So wrote the Tur. However, Tosafot and Ri"v wrote that if one reads it does not help even for a get, and so ruled the Bach. This is primary.
Provided they recognize the language of the get. The Tur rules that even if they do not recognize the language, but the reader translates before them, it helps; so wrote the Maggid Mishneh. Beit Yosef, however, explains the Rambam as requiring that they recognize the language of the get, and then, once they understand the language, the reading is only revealing the matter. But if they do not recognize the language, it does not help. He proves this from the Yerushalmi, which teaches that judges may sign even though they cannot read, while witnesses may not sign unless they can read. He assumes the Yerushalmi speaks where others read before them; otherwise how could judges sign? Nevertheless, witnesses must know how to read. One can reject this, because the Yerushalmi speaks of judges validating signatures, who need not read the shtar because they testify only to the signatures. It says witnesses may sign only if they know how to read, meaning where no one reads before them. The Rambam’s statement that they must know the language of the writing, as in seif 14, was explained above as referring to where they read by themselves. But if others read before them, one can say that even if they do not know the language and the reader translates for them, it helps. See the Shach at the beginning of siman 45, who rules that bedieved it helps even if they did not know the language of the get, if it was read and translated before them.
They mark for them. Specifically with a get they were lenient and mark for them, but not with other documents, as written by the Rambam in chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh. In chapter 1 of Hilchot Gerushin the Rambam writes that they were lenient with a get because of agunot, since signing witnesses on a get are rabbinic. This implies specifically where it was delivered before delivery witnesses. According to decisors who require both delivery and signing witnesses, even if it was delivered before delivery witnesses, if they marked with something considered writing, it is as though they did not sign at all, and one should not divorce with it lechatchilah. Bedieved, any marking is valid, for it is no worse than having no signatures at all, where if the get was given it is valid. The Tur cites Ramah that if they mark with lead-water, it is invalid even bedieved. At first glance this is difficult, because according to the Tur delivery witnesses are required and without them the get is void; if so, the case must be where there were delivery witnesses, and why is it worse bedieved than a get with no signing witnesses? Perhaps it is worse because we decree signing because of writing, as Tosafot write at the beginning of Gittin. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 234. In siman 155 it is explained regarding the writing of the get that if the signatures are marked, it is invalid as though they did not sign. The Rambam in chapter 24 implies that "marking" means cutting the signatures into the parchment and the witnesses fill them with ink. This is difficult, because the Rambam holds that engraving on skin is writing, as written in siman 125, so it is writing over writing. One must say they cut only part of the signature, part of each letter, or widen the cut and the witnesses sign in it, as Tosafot write. From here there is some proof to Terumat HaDeshen’s view regarding chak tochot: if the letter was not completed, it is valid. Ink over ink according to the Rambam, if one writes a get that way, is not a get at all and does not disqualify for kehunah. Ramah holds it is not a get but disqualifies for kehunah; therefore if he marked for them with lead-water or gall-water, it is not a get and disqualifies for kehunah, because it is like ink over ink, not like the Beit Yosef who compared it to ink over red paint. According to the Rashba cited in siman 131, it is a doubtful get. The practical difference is for decisors who hold signing witnesses without delivery witnesses are valid: if there are no delivery witnesses and the signing witnesses signed in this manner, the get is void if it is ink over ink. But according to us, who require delivery witnesses, the get is never void if it was delivered before witnesses.
