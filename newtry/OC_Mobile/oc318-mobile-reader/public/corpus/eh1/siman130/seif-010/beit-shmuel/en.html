But if he wrote a date for each one, even though he initially included them all together, since he writes a date for each one it is considered separate. So wrote the Rashba. This is stated in the Talmud according to Reish Lakish. Although one might say that according to Rabbi Yochanan, who is more lenient and holds one date for all counts as general, if he first grouped them and then specified a date for each, it would still be general, we do not find this explicit in the Talmud. It may be that even Rabbi Yochanan considers it separate. The Rashba disagrees with the Rambam and holds that according to Rabbi Yochanan, once he grouped them all together at the beginning, later specification does not help and it is not a get. However, the Ran and Maggid Mishneh write that the main law is like the Rambam. The Rashba’s proof from the Rif’s omission can be rejected: when the Rif wrote that the halacha is like Rabbi Yochanan, who is more lenient than Reish Lakish, this includes that whatever Reish Lakish is more lenient about, Rabbi Yochanan does not dispute; otherwise it would not be correct to say Rabbi Yochanan is more lenient.
