It is not a get. The Mechaber rules like the Rambam, who rules like Rav, that scribe’s writing with one witness signed is worse than the husband’s handwriting. It implies no distinction whether the scribe is expert or not. According to the Rambam, who holds signing witnesses without delivery witnesses help, the case is where there are no delivery witnesses and no two signing witnesses, only the scribe’s writing and one witness signed; it is not a get at all and does not disqualify for kehunah. However, Rivash at the end of siman 413 writes in the name of the Itur and Rabbenu Chananel that even according to Rav, an expert scribe is no worse than the husband’s handwriting, with practical effect for monetary law as in Choshen Mishpat, siman 46. For us, who hold delivery witnesses are required, this law has little practical effect: if there are no delivery witnesses, even two signed witnesses would leave the get void; if there are delivery witnesses, then even without signing witnesses, once the get was given it is valid. But if there are delivery witnesses and the husband wrote the get in his own handwriting, it may be like signing witnesses and valid lechatchilah. Likewise, if a get signed before us comes and we do not know whether there were delivery witnesses, we presume there were delivery witnesses. If a get has only the husband’s handwriting and she remarried with it, she need not leave, because bedieved we presume delivery witnesses were present. But if the scribe wrote the get and one witness signed, she must leave; this is what the Rosh means by the practical difference for us regarding the validity of signing witnesses according to Rabbi Elazar as according to Rabbi Meir. If there were delivery witnesses and only one signing witness, it is as though no witness signed, and it is not valid to give lechatchilah, but if already given it is valid. If it is the writing of an expert scribe, the Itur holds it is like the husband’s handwriting, and for us the practical difference is that she need not leave; according to the Rambam, even bedieved it is not like signing witnesses. The Tur’s words can also be resolved by saying that all the amoraim at the end of Perek HaMitkabel hold that scribe-plus-one-witness is invalid, as the Rosh wrote in Perek HaMegarish in the name of Ri. Therefore they must hold like Shmuel, who establishes the Mishnah as the scribe wrote but did not sign, not like Rav and Rabbi Yirmiyah, who explain it as the scribe signed. Even according to Rav Ashi, who excludes "tell others," one need not say the Mishnah speaks of the scribe signing; he may hold that the scribe’s writing is also valid. Therefore the ruling of "she need not leave" can apply even with a non-expert scribe.
