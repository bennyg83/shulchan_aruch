The language of the script and the writing. Certainly, regarding the language of the document, they must understand the language, as written nearby. But what he wrote that they must recognize the writing is difficult, as the Kesef Mishneh asks: we hold that even if they cannot read, others may read before them and they sign, so why must they recognize the writing here? The Kesef Mishneh did not answer. One can say that if others read before them, they need not recognize the writing. Here it speaks where they read by themselves; then they must recognize both the written language and the script. It is forced to say that they must at least know the letters even if they cannot read, for we find no proof for this. The Rambam’s words are taken from the Yerushalmi, which teaches that witnesses do not sign unless they know how to read. The Yerushalmi asks from a get written in one script and witnesses signed in another, assuming they did not know the get’s script and it is nevertheless valid. It answers that they knew the script but signed in another script. This implies that if they do not know the get’s script, they may not sign. One must say this refers to where they read by themselves, not where others read before them.
