And when he gave the kiddushin, etc. Meaning he gave her two perutot and said "behold so-and-so whom you sent is betrothed to me, and you too" — so he said in first chapter; and Beit HaGadol wrote even if he did not say in first chapter but only said "thus-and-thus is betrothed" the second — even though "thus-and-thus" does not help in kiddushin, meaning specifically regarding retraction it does not help; if one betrothed one and gave the second a perutah and said "and you — superior kiddushin" — even though he did not say "and you too," the reason is because when he gives to her hand to betroth the woman who sent her — meaning he said "and you — what is in your heart"; if she does not wish to betroth, give everything to the one who sent you. Maharshal requires saying "and you too"; but when one betroths and gives to her and says "and you — kiddushin" — even if he did not say "and you" — see responsum Rivash and Maggid there. This is what is written: if he gives to both, both are certainly betrothed. Per this, what is written above siman 27 seif 4 — if he betrothed one woman and said to her friend in her presence "you shall be betrothed" and did not say "to me" — it is only doubtful kiddushin; per what is written here "kiddushin" certainly — if he already betrothed one — because when he says "and you" it refers to what he said "you shall be betrothed to me."
