Some say — Chelkat Mechokek wrote if she married without get — forbidden; this is from Beit Shmuel's difficulty; as will be explained in the following siman. In my humble opinion, since the Ittur includes both in one wording that she requires get — whether he sent gifts and she accepted or did not send — it implies just as accepting gifts per law requires get per "perhaps she agreed" — so too without sending, from concern "perhaps she agreed" is also per law; and the Ittur is per Rif and Rambam that even if she agreed, unless also not effective; therefore even accepting gifts requires get — only from stringency of this; but certainly this is not so, for siman 129 seif 5 I brought in his name he holds even regarding an ordinary person who betrothed another and afterward informed him and he agreed — she is betrothed; and all the more he holds so regarding father with daughter. Or the Ittur itself per view of Geonim that Rif brought — poskim like Rosh that we are concerned; only Chelkat Mechokek wrote Shulchan Arukh wrote only stringently; nevertheless difficult — in siman 37 it implies Shulchan Arukh's view even stringently does not require get; this is Beit Shmuel's reason. In my humble opinion it appears the Ittur and Shulchan Arukh hold one distinguishes between daughter in father's act — both apply "a woman in any case is agreeable," and father's way to strive for daughter's match even adult; unlike regarding son — even father's way, nevertheless not in any case agreeable to him; and likewise father in kiddushin of minor — even agreeable in any case, nevertheless it is not her way — she did brazenness betrothing without his knowledge; therefore here we are more concerned "perhaps she agreed."
