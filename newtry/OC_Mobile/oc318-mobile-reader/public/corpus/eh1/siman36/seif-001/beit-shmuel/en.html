A woman may appoint an agent. It appears if she revealed her mind that she wishes to be betrothed to this one — any man can acquire for her and receive kiddushin on her behalf; for certainly in the Gemara regarding get — if one can acquire a get for her; in get one can say even in quarrel or place of yibum she does not want the get because "a woman in any case is agreeable"; but in kiddushin this reasoning does not apply and one can acquire for her — see siman 35.
And even if silent. Beit Chadash wrote it applies if they were engaged in the matter; but the agent must answer in any case yes — so in Chadashim of Maharshal; and in Derishah he wrote it refers to the agent: if they were engaged in that matter it helps even if the agent was silent and the betrother also said nothing — so Chelkat Mechokek and Taz. Per Beit Chadash, what is written in seif 8 "she was silent and accepted without specification" means she did not say she was an agent; nevertheless she answered yes. One can also say there it is preferable since she is certainly betrothed — then we say her friend too is betrothed. However it is difficult — from where does Rambam have this law that the agent must specifically answer?
Except with agent's knowledge. Meaning her knowledge is not required — agent's knowledge suffices; but if he writes with her knowledge — obviously it helps — so Chelkat Mechokek. In the Gemara one can say that which we require her knowledge is to exclude that he cannot write a receipt/kiddushin document before she agrees to betroth to him — for we require knowledge of the acquirer; but if she agrees to be betrothed, even without stipulating whether kesef or shtar — he can write a document without her knowledge. Here is not so, for she made an agent and agrees to him; nevertheless we require her knowledge to write a kiddushin document.
