That the father cannot, etc. Tur brought these words of Rashba; Rosh disputes and proof from Ittur, etc.; Maharach wondered: the Gemara is arranged in chapter HaIsh Mekadesh; brought below siman 57 "one who betroths his daughter without specification — adult is not included"; and there we say even if he made him agent — we learn he can be agent; if so certainly astounding Rashba did not see the Gemara; also Rosh did not bring from Talmud but from Ittur — therefore Rashba only said lechat'chila and strained to find proof even in Ittur it is permitted lechat'chila. In my humble opinion the correct way: the dispute between Rashba and Rosh is correct and both are words of the living God. First we precede what Beit Yosef wrote: Rashba gave reason because of decree regarding minor daughter, etc.; per Rashba's responsum kelal 35 it appears this is not truly Rashba's reason; Rosh wrote there wondering on Rashba what is his reason; wrote perhaps he decrees because of minor daughter, etc. — new decree not found in Talmud; afterward wrote I found in Sefer HaIttur, etc. Thus one can well say this is not Rashba's reason but another reason as explained in Rosh's responsum: although father has no permission to betroth her since he accepted gifts for her — we are concerned lest she agreed to those kiddushin and requires get from doubt; even though regarding a son we are not concerned for woman's kiddushin — "perhaps son agreed" we do not say where we can say "a woman in any case is agreeable," etc. — requires get from doubt, end quote. Tur wrote above siman 35 regarding betrothing without appointing him agent and not saying match her to him, etc. — she is not betrothed; father's word, another's word — even if he agreed to kiddushin when he heard — it is nothing, end quote — agreement after kiddushin does not help since at time of kiddushin there was no will; afterward agreement does not help lest perhaps this sender did not agree — we do not say he holds he does not agree all this time we did not hear consent; and consent made afterward does not help — it is after giving money and there are no kiddushin here. Here Ittur's words (wrote): since he accepted gifts — concern lest she agreed; therefore one must say here Ittur differs — one can say "a woman in any case is agreeable" as explained in Rosh's words on Ittur I cited; thus it seems this is their dispute; certainly Rashba too admits regarding adult daughter father can explicitly be agent to receive her kiddushin as explained in Talmud we cited; only Rashba discusses where he wants to be agent for daughter without her knowledge since surely father agreed to this — then after kiddushin come to her she is betrothed — Rashba holds we do not say so; just as agreement does not help when father betroths woman to his son — agreement of son afterward does not help, for we testify at time of kiddushin he was not agreeing — a person not in betrother's domain does not agree presumptively with betrother without permission; likewise here adult daughter not in father's domain — therefore Rashba wrote make agent in language implying he himself wants to insert himself as agent — we say it does not help. On this Rosh wrote one can make agent even without her knowledge and kiddushin take effect after she knows and agrees — we say she also presumptively agreed at time of kiddushin and this is not new agreement. Similarly Rosh holds regarding minor betrothed without father's knowledge: if father heard afterward and agreed — kiddushin as written siman 37 seif 11 in name of some; Rashba holds like first opinion there agreement of father afterward does not help; Rosh brings proof I cited there that Rashba and Rosh dispute; proof from Ittur wrote explicitly concern lest she agreed, etc. — implies even though at kiddushin she did not know, only afterward agreed — nevertheless we say she presumptively agreed also at kiddushin from father's act. On this Rosh wrote in responsum as Tur brings: from this responsum it implies father can betroth his adult daughter — meaning he does so of his own mind to be agent and bring her kiddushin and she will agree afterward as we said — certain kiddushin; for there Ittur said lest she agreed — doubt; if so certainly she agreed — certain kiddushin. In my humble opinion correct and clear explanation of these matters.
