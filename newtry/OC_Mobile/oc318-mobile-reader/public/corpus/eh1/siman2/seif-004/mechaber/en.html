Anyone called mamzer and silent, or natin and silent, or chalal and silent, or slave and silent — we are concerned about him and his family and do not marry from them unless they check as explained. {Rama: Some say this is specifically a family in which one of these disqualifications became mixed; but another person whom they call thus and who is silent — there is nothing in that (Tur in the name of R' Yitzchak; Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban and Rashba). Some say further that all this is specifically in early generations when bet din would attend to one who insulted his fellow and punish him properly — therefore silence is as confession; but now one who is silent during a quarrel — that is praiseworthy unless they call him thus not at time of quarrel (Beit Yosef in the name of Hasagot HaRaavad). Some say we do not say silence is as confession unless he shouted about another's disqualification; but if he is always silent — that is not as confession (Rosh end of first chapter of Ketubot). All this speaks of disqualification affecting himself; but if they wish to disqualify his offspring before him and he is silent — there is no confession in that, but we require some concern nevertheless (responsum of Maharam Padua siman 14). One who hears his disgrace in other matters and is silent — it is a sign he is of good lineage.}
