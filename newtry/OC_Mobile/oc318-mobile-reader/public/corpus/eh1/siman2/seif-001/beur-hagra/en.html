One shall not marry, etc. See Beer HaGolah; there Rav Hamnuna stated the principle, etc. — there.
But without this, etc. From what is stated "strange children" and per Rashi there on "children," etc.
One who wants, etc. Because of family blemish — for even regarding one selling his grave they can protest, per Tur as written in Bava Batra and Bekhorot.
And if he is not, etc. As written in the second chapter of Ketubot.
And one who does, etc. As stated: whoever does so is called, etc.; and as stated: these are children who are not fitting; "lest you say," etc.; and this is what is meant: he does not succeed and his match is not, etc.
For money, etc. As stated: and whoever does, etc.; and this is unlike Rivash cited above; but the main point is he holds not like Raavad that all because of money is also "marrying," etc.; rather if he would take her without this — even though he also takes money — he is not called "marrying," etc.; and this is what is meant: rather, etc.
