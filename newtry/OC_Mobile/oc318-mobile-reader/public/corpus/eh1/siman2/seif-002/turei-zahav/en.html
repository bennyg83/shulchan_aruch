**A — That fit women were not warned, etc.** Rashi explained: such as chalal and natin — since the Torah was not strict about them regarding lineage, the Sages too did not enact requiring them to check, even for mamzeret — specifically when prohibition was established in him; but for mere concern and checking they did not require them — end quote. But from the wording of this seif, which is Rambam's language that this law is called an objection raised against him, it implies even if prohibition was not established — women were not warned. And if you ask: what is different about Levites and Israelites from women — for they too were not warned regarding chalalim and converts, yet they were warned to marry disqualified persons and require checking? It is different there, for we find greater stringency in their males: if they are kohanim they are forbidden by law, whereas converts and women — even a kohenet — are permitted in them; unlike Levites and Israelites, where we find no leniency in their kind in any respect. **B — Some say specifically a family in which one of the disqualifications became mixed** — so Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak. Difficult: Tur first cited Rambam — anyone called mamzer, etc., and silent — we are concerned, etc. — as he cited here in Shulchan Arukh; then wrote "and so any family about which objection was raised," etc., as in Shulchan Arukh seif 3 — implies disqualification of silence does not speak of objection. If so, how did he bring Rabbeinu Yitzchak on this mamzer-and-silent whom they called with objection — for with objection checking is needed in any case since objection is before us? I have seen some distinguish: "became mixed" with silence is not with witnesses but merely a rumor — and these are empty words, for "became mixed" implies with clear witnesses. Must say Tur did not bring Rabbeinu Yitzchak to explain Rambam's words but to disagree — proven from Tur's wording "what Rambam wrote, anyone called, etc., Rabbeinu Yitzchak, etc." — why bring Rabbeinu Yitzchak to explain Rambam? He should have brought the Gemara there, or Rabbeinu Yitzchak disagrees with Rambam — Rambam wrote disqualification of mamzer and silence without mixture of disqualification, and R' Yitzchak explained that specifically mixture of disqualification is required. It appears two further disputes depend on this: per Rambam this silent disqualification is safek and checking helps him; per R' Yitzchak we say the one who was silent is definitely disqualified and checking does not help. And further: the rest of his family are fit once doubts are clarified in that silent one; per Rambam the whole family are safek disqualified — so Rama's words imply some disagree with what was first written on Rambam's words. (Editor's note: Beit Shmuel seif 11 — even if they call him and he is silent it does not help render his family fit, unlike Chelkat Mechokek; also seif 12 on Chelkat Mechokek if became mixed and silent we say this is the disqualification and rest of family need not check — I already wrote this does not exist, also from Rama it is proven — for he refers to Mechaber "we are concerned for him and his family" — hence whole family needs checking — end quote. Per Taz's explanation Tur and Rama brought Rabbeinu Yitzchak to disagree with Rambam — silence alone does not disqualify — Chelkat Mechokek also holds thus, for Mechaber is concerned for whole family and speaks without mixture; but "some say" Rama brings — rest of family are fit — Rama wrote rest of family are fit only when this silent one was not checked — implies checking helps him; Taz holds checking does not help at all per R' Yitzchak. It seems to decide like Taz from two reasons: one, Tur wrote when became mixed in his family and one of the family is called mamzer or natin and is silent — then it is proven he is the one — implies he says definitely he is the one, not that one must check after him, or checking does not help. Further, Tur speaks even if safek mamzer became mixed, as Beit Yosef, Maggid Mishneh, Ramban, Rashba, Bach wrote — not only definite mamzer; likewise safek mamzer when called mamzer and silent — proof he is the safek — end quote. If so, per Tosafot Ketubot 14 in the passage beginning almanat Issah — to distinguish between the widow herself and her daughter — widow is permitted through safek because she had presumption of fitness, daughter disqualified because no presumption — if we say whenever this silent one was not checked the whole family are fit, disqualified ones are clarified in this silent one — then whole family had presumption of fitness before his checking, and again safek-safek does not apply after his checking, as with the widow herself, and whole family permitted — then there would be leniency through this one's silence, and certainly no one would say so; or per Taz this silent one — checking does not help and he is always disqualified and his family are fit — end discussion.) **Not at time of quarrel** — this is Raavad's view. I wonder: since he concludes not at time of quarrel disqualifies one who is silent — why did he first distinguish between early generations who punished properly? It is not enough in this distinction alone to say silence does not disqualify except not at quarrel, but at quarrel it is praiseworthy — as the article says, honor to a man of Shabbat quarrel.
