(ב) He does not lie with her, etc. This is astonishing, for it is clear that this law is not comparable to one who lacks male potency, as we explained. The Tur brings a responsum here and writes that the oath did not take effect for this matter, because he was already obligated first to support his wife. Derishah asks from siman 118, where the Rosh discusses Reuven the tailor who swore to give Shimon half of what he earned, and in that half there was not enough for his wife's sustenance; his wife claimed sustenance, and the Rosh answered that the oath took effect and he must fulfill it. One can answer that here, where the oath did not take effect, it did not take effect on all that he would earn, but it did take effect on half; and there too it took effect only on half, not on what is needed for the wife's prior right. Taz continues to distinguish obligations that pre-exist an oath from later monetary commitments, and explains that an oath cannot uproot a prior marital obligation. Where the oath can be fulfilled in part without nullifying the wife's rights, it stands to that extent; where it would entirely prevent his marital obligations, it does not take effect.
