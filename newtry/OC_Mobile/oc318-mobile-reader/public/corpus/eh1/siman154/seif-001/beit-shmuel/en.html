(ב) And if she knew of them. This is the view of Ramah. According to this, with foul breath and other such matters, if she knew of them we do not compel him. If so, according to the tanna in the first Mishnah who taught, "a man in whom blemishes arose," etc., and the Gemara says that if they arose after marriage we do not compel him, all the more if they existed beforehand, because she saw and accepted them. It means: all the more if they existed beforehand, for then even with foul breath, etc., we do not compel him. According to the one who reads the Mishnah as "they existed beforehand," the difficulty is that even foul breath should not compel him. Perhaps according to that view the second Mishnah, "these are those whom we compel," speaks of foul breath even if it existed beforehand, and nevertheless we compel him; R. Meir and the Sages disagree only where he stipulated explicitly. But we rule like the one who reads "they arose after marriage," all the more if they existed beforehand, and then we say the dispute concerns cases that existed beforehand. What the Mishnah teaches, "these we compel, whether they existed before she married," Bach explained to mean that they existed but she did not know. However, in the first Mishnah regarding blemishes, one can say that if they existed and she did not know, we compel him too; the difference between the two Mishnayot is where they arose after marriage: with foul breath we compel, but with other blemishes we do not. If she did not know, it is a mistaken acquisition and we compel him to divorce, as written in siman 39 and 117 regarding blemishes found in her. Do not ask why the Gemara did not establish the dispute this way: one view says "they existed" means existed and she did not know, all the more if they arose after marriage, while the other says that if they existed beforehand it is a mistaken acquisition. One can answer that all agree in such a case that it is a mistaken acquisition. Bach explains differently; according to him, following Ramah, we do not compel for blemishes even if they existed and she did not know, and all the more according to the other poskim. According to their approaches, all cases speak where she knew, and the case where they existed and she did not know is not heard at all; one may say that all agree we compel even for ordinary blemishes. Now, according to several poskim, even if she knew of them she can say, "I thought I could tolerate it, but now I cannot." Therefore it appears that even according to Rama, who wrote that we do not compel him to divorce, nevertheless she does not have the law of a moredet if she does not wish to live with him, for she can say kim li like those poskim. The reason Rama ruled like Ramah and not like those poskim is that Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef wrote that Rambam holds we compel him only if the blemish arose after marriage, but if it was there originally we do not compel him. Yet it is not necessary to say so. One can say that Rambam wrote this only regarding the ketubah: if the blemish arose after marriage, he must give her the ketubah, but if it was there from the beginning, he need not give her the ketubah, for otherwise every woman would scheme and marry such a man, as Tosafot wrote in Yevamot 64, in Perek HaMadir, in Mordechai, and in Agudah. A proof is that regarding one afflicted with boils Rambam also wrote, "he became afflicted with boils," even though according to all opinions, even if he stipulated and it existed originally, we compel him to divorce. Rather, he wrote so because of the ketubah.
