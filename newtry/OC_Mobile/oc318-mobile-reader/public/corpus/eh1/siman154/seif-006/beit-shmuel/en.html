(ב) And she says that he is the cause, etc. According to Tosafot and the Rosh, the reason she must say that he does not shoot like an arrow is that this must speak where she stayed with him ten years; otherwise we would be concerned that she set her eyes on another, since she demands a get. Since it speaks where she stayed ten years, if he has not yet fulfilled peru u-revu, we should compel him to divorce her by the law of the Shas so that he can fulfill peru u-revu. It must therefore speak where he already fulfilled peru u-revu, and we do not compel him except because of her claims. Therefore she must say that he does not shoot like an arrow, for since he already fathered children it is reasonable to assume that she is the cause of the failure to have children, unless she makes that claim. But for us, since we do not compel because of failure to fulfill peru u-revu even after ten years, then even if he does shoot like an arrow, nevertheless when she comes with the claim that she needs a support in old age, we compel according to the relevant opinions. Beit Shmuel then compares Tosafot, Ramban, Rosh, Nimukei Yosef, and the gloss, and distinguishes between monetary ketubah claims, coercion to divorce, and the evidentiary weight of her claim.

(ח) And the same applies if he remained ten years, etc. In the Talmud it states: he says the impediment is from her, and she says it is from him; she is believed. If he says, I will go and test myself, he is permitted. I have already written the various approaches, and according to each approach, just as in the first clause where he says the impediment is from her, so too in the latter clause if he says I will go and marry another woman. According to Rambam's approach that I wrote, every woman must say that he is not capable of fathering children; this is what the Talmud means when it says perhaps he did not merit to be built from her. If he says I will marry another woman, then if she bears a child it becomes clear that the impediment was from the first wife, and we do not say perhaps he did not merit to be built from her. The Tur cited a responsum of the Rosh, who wrote that if he married a second wife and she bore a child, the first wife has not lost, and we say perhaps he did not merit to be built from her, until she is established as not bearing children to three men. Afterward the Tur wrote this law: if he says I will marry another, and if she bears a child the first wife leaves without a ketubah. In Darkei Moshe he distinguishes between these two laws and writes that here the case is where she did not bear children to two men, then married a third and did not bear children, and she says he is not capable of fathering; she is believed and has a ketubah. If he married another and she bore a child, then we no longer say that he did not merit to be built from her, but rather she is established as one who does not bear. This follows the answer of the Rash in Tosafot that I cited above: the sugya of he says the impediment is from her, etc., speaks of the third husband. Accordingly, what the sugya says there, that he says I will go and test myself, necessarily refers to the third husband. But according to Rif's approach, that the sugya speaks even of the first and second husband where he claims with certainty, necessarily the phrase I will go and test myself also speaks where he claims with certainty. Then if the second wife bears a child, the first wife leaves without the ketubah. But where he does not claim with certainty, even if the second wife bears a child we say perhaps he did not merit to be built from the first. If he claims with certainty that the impediment is from her, meaning that she emits semen, and he is believed in his claim, while she claims that he too is not fit to father because he is not capable of erection, then when the second wife bears a child her first claim is contradicted and his certain claim remains. With this the words of the Tur are well resolved, unlike Darkei Moshe, who rejects this distinction; his difficulty there does not appear to me. Maharshal and Bach also explained that the Tur speaks where he claims with certainty. Likewise according to Tosafot's answer, where he already had children she must say that he is not capable of fathering, for otherwise we do not attribute the failure to merit, but attribute the defect to her. What it says, I will go and test myself, also speaks in such a case, to contradict her claim that he is not capable of fathering, meaning that now his strength has weakened; and if the second wife bears a child, it is clarified that his strength has not weakened. However, according to this answer of Tosafot, if he already had children we do not attribute the failure to merit, and therefore she must say he is not capable of fathering. Necessarily, even if he had not previously had children but now fathers with the second wife, the first wife leaves without a ketubah, for she cannot say his strength weakened; and since he fathered, we do not attribute it to his not meriting to be built from her. The Rosh ruled in responsum that even if the second wife bore a child, the first wife does not lose, because in that responsum he rejects this answer, and in his rulings too it implies that he does not accept this answer; he treated as primary the Rash's answer above, that as long as she has not been established with three men, we say he did not merit to be built from her, even though he fathers children from other women. According to this, Rama's words in the gloss are astonishing to me, for nearby he wrote like Tosafot's answer, that when he had children she must say he is not capable of fathering; otherwise we do not attribute the matter to merit, but say she is barren. If so, how can he say that after ten years he cannot say I will go and test myself? If he fathers with another woman, we no longer attribute it to merit, but to her. It does not seem right to say that he holds that even according to Tosafot, if he previously had children we attribute the defect to her, but if he had not previously had children we do not say he may test himself. First, his language implies that even if he went and tested himself and the second wife bore a child, the first wife would not lose; and according to Tosafot, certainly if the second wife bore a child we attribute the defect to the first, as implied in the Rosh's responsum there. Also, from where would he derive such a distinction? This requires study. The Tur's words too are at first glance astonishing. In the law where she comes with the claim I need a staff for my hand and says he is not capable of fathering, it is difficult: it speaks where he claims nothing against her, and it also does not speak of a third husband, for that was not mentioned. If so, according to Rif's approach, and also according to the Rash's answer in Tosafot, she need not say he is not capable of fathering. If he holds like Tosafot's answer that where he has children she must say he is not capable of fathering, that is difficult: first, the Rosh in the responsum above rejects that answer; second, why did the Tur write even if he has children, when the case is specifically where he has children? Also, the earlier difficulty remains: if so, how could he cite the Rosh's responsum that when he fathers from the second wife we attribute it to his not having merited to be built from the first? If he holds like Rambam, as I wrote above in seif katan 9, it is also difficult: if she must claim with certainty that the impediment is from him, then necessarily the sugya of I will go and test myself also speaks in such a case, and if he tested himself and the second wife bore a child, we no longer attribute it to merit. Regarding the Tur, however, one may say that he rules like Tosafot: when she comes to force him to divorce, we say that since he already had children, one may say the defect comes from her and she cannot force him to divorce. In the Rosh's responsum, however, the case is where he comes to divorce her and reduce her ketubah; then one may say the opposite, that even if he fathers from other women, perhaps he did not merit to be built from her. What he wrote in seif 30, that she claims he is not capable of fathering, and there the case is where he wants to divorce her, may be because he claims with certainty that the impediment is from her; therefore she must claim with certainty that he is not capable of fathering. With this one can explain the Tur's words. But Rama remains difficult, for it is impossible to explain him this way, since he wrote these laws on the case where she wants to force him to divorce.
