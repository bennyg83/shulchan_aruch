It is proper to be stringent. See these laws in Tosafot and the Rosh at the end of Perek HaBa Al Yevimto, and in the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of Perek HaMadir. The rule is: one who marries a woman in violation of a prohibition is compelled even with lashes, as written at the end of Perek HaMadir; likewise if he married a sheniyah, and likewise all those listed at the beginning of seif 7. According to all opinions we compel with lashes, because the Talmud states explicitly that we compel them. In all cases where compulsion is because of them, they are not given the added ketubah. But where the beit din compels him to divorce because she did not bear children for ten years, she does have the added ketubah, for it is not because of her, as written in Tosafot at the end of Perek HaBa Al Yevimto. When she comes with a claim, the Talmud there states that we compel him, meaning with lashes, and Tosafot wrote likewise; however, Rashba wrote in responsum 1192 that we do not compel him with lashes, but add to the ketubah like the law of a rebellious husband. One who stayed with his wife ten years: according to Rif we compel with lashes, and Rambam wrote likewise in chapter 15 law 1; see Derishah. In Hagahot Maimoniyot, Perek HaBa Al Yevimto, he first wrote that it requires study in our time whether he can divorce her against her will, since Rabbenu Gershom decreed not to divorce a woman against her will; afterward he cited a responsum of Rabbenu Gershom ruling that he may divorce her against her will. It is forced to say that this responsum was written before the enactment. In Responsa Rivash, siman 15, he wrote that the sages of the generations turned a blind eye in matters of marriage, because otherwise they would have had to compel everyone and that is impossible; therefore, whenever the prohibition is not intrinsic to the marriage, they turned a blind eye. He also gave a reason according to the custom of his country, that he could marry another woman; even though this one does not bear children, he can marry another, and therefore they turned a blind eye. It follows that according to our custom the second reason is not relevant at all. Even according to the first reason, the prohibition nevertheless remains in place, though the sages of the generation do not compel; the prohibition still rests on the person. Therefore it appears that even in our time, when the cherem of Rabbenu Gershom exists, nevertheless if she did not bear children for ten years he may divorce her against her will. In all these cases it appears that even if a sage ruled leniently and she married, we compel her to leave. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 17 seif 34 in the name of a responsum of the Rosh regarding endless waters, that if a sage ruled leniently she does not leave, because there we hold that most people die and therefore we are lenient; but in these prohibitions there is no permission at all.
