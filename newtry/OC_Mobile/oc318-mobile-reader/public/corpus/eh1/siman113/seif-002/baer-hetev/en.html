(ח) His death. This means by the middle valuation. For example, if he married off one daughter with five, one with four, and one with three, and he had two thousand in his lifetime and at his death one thousand gold coins, they give this one two, meaning half of the middle valuation. What he wrote that they give everything to the second speaks where, at the time he gave to the first, he had no more than that amount; see Beit Shmuel. Shimon died after marrying one daughter to Reuven during his lifetime and giving her a half-male-share document as customary; afterward he died without a will, and beit din assessed the father's intent as a fixed sum for his unmarried daughters. Before the marriages, the daughters died. Reuven, his son-in-law, has a right by force of the half-male-share document to that sum that had been set aside for the daughters' portion, since they died before marriage. Shevut Yaakov, part 1, question 110; see there.
