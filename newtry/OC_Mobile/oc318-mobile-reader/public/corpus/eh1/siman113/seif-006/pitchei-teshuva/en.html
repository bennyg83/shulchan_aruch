A share in lands. See Baer Heitev below, s.k. 13, in the name of Shevut Yaakov and Responsa Chavot Yair. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of siman 130, that houses are included in lands, and there is no doubt about this at all, unlike one rabbi's view. What that rabbi further wrote, that this applies only to land he had at the time of writing the half-male-share document, but not what he acquired afterward, is also incorrect; lands acquired afterward are included too. The exception is houses held for profit by renting them out, not for dwelling; regarding these, the geonim wrote in Responsa Chinukh Beit Yehudah that the daughters acquire. Although my heart is very uneasy about this, for that reasoning suffices for one who trades in houses, buying and selling them, but where he holds them to place tenants in them we have no proof, nevertheless the elders have already ruled, and it is certainly proper to compromise as Shevut Yaakov concluded; see there. See this law in Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 93, where a correct reason is given why a half-male-share document excludes books and lands. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 147, also wrote a good reason for this; see there. See there further regarding a place where a decree was issued that a Jew who buys houses may not register them in the government ledger under his own name but only under the name of a non-Jewish citizen, whether those houses have the law of the buyer's land regarding a tenth of the estate or a half-male-share document; see there at length. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer part 2, siman 168, and what I wrote above at the end of siman 108.
