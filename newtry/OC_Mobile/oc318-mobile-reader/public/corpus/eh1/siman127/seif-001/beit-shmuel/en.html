One must write a date in a Get. Perhaps he will marry his sister's daughter, she will commit adultery, he will be fond of her, and he will give her a Get, and she will say that he gave her the Get before she committed adultery. Even nowadays, when capital cases are not judged, there is still a practical difference so that she not become forbidden to him; and there is also a practical difference that no trace of disqualification attach to the children. But now that the Sages enacted a date in a Get, if he did not write a date, even though on a Torah level she is divorced and if she married she need not leave, nevertheless if she commits adultery she is not believed to say that he gave her this Get before she committed adultery unless she brings proof to her statement, since it was not written according to the enactment of the Sages. Do not ask why the sugya there challenges from the Mishnah that teaches: three Gittin are invalid, but if she married, the child is valid; what did the Sages accomplish with their enactment? What is the difficulty, since they accomplished that he cannot cover for her, because it was not written according to the enactment of the Sages? One may answer that the sugya concerns capital punishment, and regarding execution, even though it was not written according to the enactment of the Sages, she is exempt from death when she says that he gave her the Get before the adultery, as Tosafot wrote there. Therefore Rashi explained the answer that it helps initially, that she may not marry, and then witnesses will not sign themselves, so there is no concern that they will do so to save her from death. But because of produce, to know from what time he no longer has produce rights, they did not enact writing a date, because we hold that the husband has produce until the Get comes into her hand; that can be clarified by a written statement from the beit din as to when the Get came into her hand. Beit Yosef cited the view of several poskim who hold that when the Get is delivered before witnesses of delivery, no date is needed, but we do not hold so, as is implied by most poskim. See Piskei Mahari, siman 19.
