Seif 9, "a get whose date is later," etc. NB: See Beit Shmuel s.k. 15 and what he asked against Beit Yosef. At first glance his words are difficult, for Beit Yosef's words show that it helps after the date of the get, but before the date of the get he agrees that it does not help. His intent appears to be like Mishneh LaMelekh's challenge: either way, it does not help. Before the date of the get he can shield her; after the date of the get, if she commits adultery, she is already unmarried, so what concern is there that he may shield her? Pnei Yehoshua also wondered about this. Lechem Mishneh explained Beit Yosef as follows: although if witnesses come before the date of the get that she committed adultery he can shield her, if witnesses come after the date of the get that she committed adultery before the date of the get, she will not be believed to say the get was postdated; we assume the get was written on its date. Pnei Yehoshua did not see these words of Lechem Mishneh. Yet his explanation is also not correct, for it still is not like a get dated by a week. There, the dating helps fully, because she has no stratagem to use against the earlier week and she is certainly executed. Here she can shield herself even after the date of the get: if she fears that witnesses will come after the date saying she sinned before the husband gave her the postdated get, she can first produce witnesses who saw the get in her hand before its written date. Then, when witnesses later say she sinned before the written date, she will bring those witnesses and prove that the get was postdated, and she can say the adultery occurred after the divorce. Since she can do this, the Sages accomplished nothing by their enactment, and Beit Yosef's comparison is difficult.
With this one can explain Beit Shmuel here, where he writes in resolving the words of Raavad: perhaps since it was not done according to the enactment of the Sages, etc. At first glance this is hard to understand: if so, why regarding a get dated by a week does the Gemara ask, "If so, what did the Sages accomplish?" Why not say there too that since it was not done according to the Sages' enactment, she must bring proof? According to the above it is settled. Raavad holds that writing the week is valid even ab initio and is itself included in the Sages' enactment, so the Gemara asks properly. But a postdated get is forbidden ab initio, and therefore it was not done according to the Sages' enactment. This appears to be Beit Shmuel's view, unlike what I wrote above according to the poskim who disagree with Rashi; they must hold that this too is called done according to the enactment, since if it was written she may remarry, otherwise the Gemara's question about dating by a week would not apply according to them. Examine and understand this.
