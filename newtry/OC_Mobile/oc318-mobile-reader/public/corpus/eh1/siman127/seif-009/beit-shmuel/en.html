(א) A get whose date is later. It was written and signed in Cheshvan but its date was written as Kislev. This is disputed. According to Tosafot and Raavad it is valid, but according to Tosafot the get does not take effect until Kislev, while according to Raavad she is divorced from the day of giving. According to Tosafot there is no concern either of promiscuity or of produce, because the get does not take effect until Kislev. According to Raavad there is difficulty: perhaps she will say that she committed adultery after the get, and the date of the get is no proof, since the get was not written at its actual time. Regarding produce there is no concern, because he holds that she waived the produce to the husband until Kislev, since she knew the date was written as Kislev. But regarding promiscuity there is concern, as Beit Yosef asked. Beit Yosef answered that it is like writing a particular week or year, but Beit Shmuel questions whether that fully resolves the concern. The practical distinction is whether a delayed date delays the legal effect of divorce or merely the evidentiary date of the document.
