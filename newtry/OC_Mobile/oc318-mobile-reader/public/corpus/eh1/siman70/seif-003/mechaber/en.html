And how much sustenance do they assess for a woman? Bread — two meals each day; and relish to eat with the bread; and oil for eating and for lighting the lamp; and a little wine to drink if it was local custom that women drink wine. And if she is nursing — they give her wine (Tur). And on Shabbat — three meals, and meat or fish; and he gives her each Shabbat a silver ma'ah for her needs (and they give her wood to cook her food) (Ran ch. Even HaEzer). In another matter — for a poor person in Israel; but if he was rich — everything according to his wealth. They reduced and added from these sustenance amounts — it belongs to the husband (Tur). And if he was exceedingly poor and cannot give her even the bread she needs — they compel him to divorce. {Rama: And her ketubah shall be a debt upon him until he finds and pays (Tur in name of Rambam). And some say they do not compel him to divorce after he has nothing (R' Yehuda in name of Rabbeinu Tam). One who has sustenance only for one day — he is obligated to sustain his wife from it or eat with her (Tur in name of RaMAh). And some say further that he must hire himself out as a laborer and sustain his wife (Tur in name of R' Eliyahu and Maharam in name of the rabbis of France). If he had land — the choice is the woman's to take her sustenance from the produce of the land or from the land itself, or they must sell it (Rashba siman 499).}
