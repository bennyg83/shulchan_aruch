If she stood in judgment and they assessed sustenance for her and beit din sold and gave her, or she sold for herself, and the husband came and said "I left her sustenance" — behold she swears while holding a sacred object that he did not leave her sustenance. And if she did not claim and did not sell but waited until he came and said "I left you sustenance," and she says "you did not leave but I borrowed from so-and-so and was sustained" — he swears a heses oath that he left her sustenance and is released, and the debt remains upon her. {Rama: (But a man is not believed to say he gave her for the future) (Ran beginning of Almanah Nezunah). And if she sold movable property and said she sold for sustenance, and he claims and says "I left your sustenance" — she swears a heses oath that he did not leave. {Rama: If he claimed and said she should have used her handiwork for her sustenance — if she borrowed without beit din and she is a craftswoman whose handiwork suffices for large matters even if not for small matters — his claim is a valid claim. But if beit din assessed for her — her handiwork must suffice for everything (all this in Tur).}
