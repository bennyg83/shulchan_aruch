One who went overseas and his wife came to beit din to claim sustenance — for the first three months from the day he left they do not assess sustenance for her, for the presumption is a man does not leave his house empty. {Rama: But if she was silent longer and claimed afterward — they do not assess retroactively, only from the day of the claim, even if beit din delayed the judgment (Ran ch. 2 of Dayanei Gezeirot). And some say if he parted from her in quarrel and wishes to make her an agunah — they assess for her immediately, for certainly he left his house empty (Mordechai beginning of Dayanei Gezeirot). From here onward — even if her ketubah is not in her possession they assess sustenance for her; but they do not give her for adornment. And if he had property — beit din descends to his property and sells for her sustenance even if they did not hear he died; and they do not account with her for her handiwork until her husband comes. If he finds she worked — behold it is his. And likewise if she did not stand in judgment but sold for herself for sustenance — the sale is valid and she needs no proclamation or oath until her husband comes and claims, or until she comes to collect her ketubah after his death — we presume she sold only for sustenance she needed.}
