A woman who left her husband's house and went to another house — if she came because of a claim that she is in the neighborhood and they cast bad name upon her and the like — he is obligated to sustain her there if she claims sustenance. But if she did not claim — she waived past sustenance. {Rama: And likewise if she quarreled with her husband and he does not dwell with her and the prevention is from him, and she borrowed for sustenance — he must pay. But if the prevention is from her — he need not pay, for a man is not obligated in his wife's sustenance except when she is with him (Mordechai ch. 2 of Dayanei Gezeirot, and so Beit Yosef in name of Ritva). And specifically when her husband is in the city; but if her husband is not in the city — it makes no difference if she went to her father's house. And regarding household work — if she was ill, obviously he is obligated, for an ill person is not fit for work. And if she is not ill — they deduct from her sustenance according to what is needed to hire someone to do the small household tasks; but she does not lose her sustenance because of this, only because of her handiwork if she is fit for work (all this in Beit Yosef siman 77 in name of responsum of Rashba). And in a place where he is obligated to give her sustenance even though her husband proclaimed that no one should lend her, and he is in the city and she did not sue him in court and borrowed — he is obligated to pay, for it is not in his power, provided she did not suffice with her handiwork (responsum of Rashba siman 492).}
