Her husband went and she borrowed and ate — he is obligated to pay. {Rama: The lender sues the woman and she sues her husband; and if the woman is not here — sue the husband (Ran ch. 5 of Dayanei Gezeirot). However, if the woman waived to her husband — the lender has nothing (Mordechai ch. Yesh Nochalin). If beit din assessed sustenance for her and ordered someone to sustain her and collect from the husband's property — if the husband died he has no claim against her. But if they said plainly to lend to her — if the husband died or has nothing to pay and she inherited from elsewhere — she must pay (so implied from responsum of Rashba brought by Beit Yosef). If someone on his own sustained her from his own — the husband is not obligated to pay him, and behold he lost his money. {Rama: And there is no distinction between her father and another who sustained her; however, if they were obligated to the husband and sustained his wife — they deduct from his debt (all this in Hagahot Mordechai on Kesubos). And if her father stood and sustained his daughter along with the groom — nevertheless he need not pay him except for his own sustenance and not his daughter's (Terumat HaDeshen siman 317). If she did not borrow but sold her property and sustained herself — she has no claim against him, and her handiwork is hers (Ran beginning of Dayanei Gezeirot).}
