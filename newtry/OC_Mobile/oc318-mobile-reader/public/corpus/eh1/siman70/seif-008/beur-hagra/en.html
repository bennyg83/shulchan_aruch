(א) One who lends, etc. Rashi, 107b, in the Mishnah, s.v. "Chanan," etc. So too other decisors.
(ב) And if there is no, etc. From Rabbi Natan, as in a case where there are no assets, as written in Choshen Mishpat there and in the Ran here. Specifically if the woman is not here or if the woman has no assets, but otherwise not, as written there, seif 2. So too Yam Shel Shlomo wrote there, that when he is not there, it is like he has no assets.
(ג) However, etc., he is. This is astonishing. See Chelkat Mechokek, siman 86, seif 5, and Chelkat Mechokek.
(ד) They ruled, etc. For his primary lien is on the husband. Greater than this, we say on 57a: a widow who sold, etc.
(ה) But if, etc. For his primary obligation is upon her. It is not because of this reason that he did not place it on the horn of a deer, as Rashi wrote, as above. See Chelkat Mechokek.
(ו) And there is no distinction, etc. In the Yerushalmi, chapter 4 and the beginning of chapter 13: One who went, etc., on the horn of a deer. Rabbi Chaggai said: they said this only regarding the father; but another person does not collect. Rabbi Yosi said: whether the father or another person, he does not collect. The Ran, Mordechai, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and other decisors wrote that since it is a dispute, because of doubt we do not remove money.
(ז) However, if, etc. For specifically with his own funds we say that he placed it, etc. Even regarding a guarantor, we do not say he placed it, as written in Bava Batra 175b.
(ח) And if her father, etc. See Turei Zahav, end of siman 246.
(ט) She did not borrow, etc. As above, seif 5, from what they said there: her labor went out, etc., according to the second explanation.
