(ג) However, if she waived. Chelkat Mechokek challenged this: since the husband is obligated by the lien of Rabbi Natan, how can she waive? Although several poskim hold that she can waive, it is nevertheless difficult, for there in Choshen Mishpat the Mechaber ruled that she cannot waive. It appears that this is not difficult at all, for here it speaks where she had with what to pay and waived; then she can waive. Now that she is not here, the lender cannot claim from the husband. Although Mordekhai, who wrote this law that she can waive, holds that even where the debt is subject to Rabbi Natan's lien she can waive, and we do not hold like him, nevertheless Rama cited his words for us as well: although we generally hold that she cannot waive, nevertheless if she had with what to pay, then she can waive. This is what he wrote: if the woman is not here, he claims from the husband. This implies that if she were here he could collect from her, because she has with what to pay. See what I wrote on this in Choshen Mishpat there in that siman, seif katan 12. In Responsa Shach II he ruled that if the borrower whose debt is subject to Rabbi Natan's lien waived, one may say kim li like those poskim who hold that he can waive.

(ה) The husband is not obligated to pay. This is not comparable to one who descends into another's field without permission, who must pay, because there benefit comes to the other person; here, however, no benefit comes to him, but only removal of harm. See Tosafot, the Rosh, and the other poskim. See also Tosafot in Gittin 66, which I brought in siman 141. At first glance this law depends on the two answers of Tosafot there. According to one answer, with which the Ran agrees, he becomes his agent and must pay, but regarding one who forbids benefit by vow he is not his agent. Baal HaTerumot, gate 65, wrote that if one says, "Whoever sustains her will not lose," and someone arose and supported her, he has lost his money, because he is not his agent, as written in Perek HaMadir: when he forbids her benefit by vow he appoints a provider, meaning he says, "Whoever sustains her will not lose"; necessarily, that person is not his agent. Likewise, if he said to a shopkeeper who regularly deals with him, "So-and-so is forbidden benefit from me by vow, and I do not know what to do for him," and the shopkeeper gave to him, he need not pay the shopkeeper. In Yoreh Deah siman 221 and siman 295 the later authorities overlooked the words of Baal HaTerumot.
