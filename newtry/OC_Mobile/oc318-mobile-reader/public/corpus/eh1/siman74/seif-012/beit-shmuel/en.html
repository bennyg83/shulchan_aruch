More than that, he must annul his vow or divorce; this is how it should read. So is the version in the Chelkat Mechokek and small Shulchan Aruchs, and so it is in the Rambam. The Maggid Mishneh explains that all these vows are where she made them dependent on his benefit, and "he must annul his vow" means he waits only the amount of time needed to annul the vow. It remains difficult what the Mechaber adds in the following seif. The Bach explains it as a case where he says, "Konam, my utensils are forbidden to my neighbors if you go to the bathhouse." At first glance it is difficult why he must divorce immediately and not wait the time she can be without the bathhouse. One must say that just as where he forbids her not to go to her father’s house for more than a month on condition of marital relations, we treat it like a vow only from marital relations and she waits seven days, so too here, since he forbids benefit from utensils if she goes to the bathhouse beyond the bathhouse period, it is as though he forbids benefit from utensils and she leaves immediately. The Chelkat Mechokek writes that if he forbids her something for her own benefit, such as "your marital benefit is forbidden to me if you do not adorn yourself," and she does not want to, he cannot force her; the vow takes effect, he is not a mored, and she is not a moredet. After six months he divorces and gives the ketubah, as written in siman 77.
