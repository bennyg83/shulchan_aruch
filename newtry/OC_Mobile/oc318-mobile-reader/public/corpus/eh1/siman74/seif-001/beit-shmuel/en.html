And he made it dependent on marital relations. If he did not make it dependent on marital relations, he cannot forbid her not to adorn herself or other things he wants to forbid her, unless he makes it dependent on marital relations. If he made it dependent on his benefit and said, "My benefit is forbidden to you if you adorn yourself," this depends on the dispute explained in siman 72 in the Tur. According to several decisors the prohibition does not take effect at all, since he is obligated to provide her food, unless he said, "take your earnings for your food" and they suffice for major needs; then the prohibition takes effect, and she adorns herself immediately, becomes forbidden in benefit, and he maintains her for thirty days. The Rambam wrote at the end of chapter 12 that even if he said generally, "my benefit shall be forbidden to you," it is as though he said, "take your earnings for your food." If he made it dependent on marital relations, then according to several decisors the halacha follows the first Tanna, who holds adornment has no measure, for a woman cannot be without adornment even one day; therefore she adorns herself immediately. The Rambam holds that a poor woman can be without adornment for a year, and this is the "some say" that he brought. According to this, if she vowed for a year and he maintained the vow, we do not say that he hates her, as written nearby: whenever she can be without the thing, we do not say he hates her. But if she vowed for more than a year and he maintained it, we say he hates her and he must divorce her immediately.
