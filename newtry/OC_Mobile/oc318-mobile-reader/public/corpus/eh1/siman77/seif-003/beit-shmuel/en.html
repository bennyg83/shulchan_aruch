If she seized. I already wrote at the beginning of the siman when seizure helps. The husband need not seize; when she has not seized, he acquires. But land is like something she seized. See Rivash, siman 342. I already wrote that according to the Ramban and Rashba, seizure does not help for nichsei tzon barzel; their view is omitted here because most decisors disagree. The Rambam, Ran, and Tur hold seizure helps, and so it implies in the Mordechai in the name of Responsa Maharam and in Shiltei Gibborim. In seif 2 he brought their view regarding "ma'is alai," where seizure is needed, because according to Rashi and Rosh, the sugya of Rav Zevid’s daughter-in-law speaks of "ma'is alai." The Chelkat Mechokek’s question from seif 2 to here is not difficult; the two laws do not depend on one another.
All this is when she gives no reason, etc. This refers above to seif 2, where the law of "ma'is alai" was stated. There it speaks where she says "ma'is alai" without a clear reason. But if she gives a clear reason which beit din recognizes as true and fitting for her to find him repulsive, the Geonim enacted not to apply Talmudic law, and the husband must return the entire dowry. There are three categories: a clear claim, a false claim, and an unclear claim. Their sources are in the Rif and the laws of the Metivta. Where the claim is clear, we remove from him nichsei melog and nichsei tzon barzel and give them to her. Where the claim is not clear, if she seized, we do not remove it. Where it is shown false, everything is given to the husband, even what she brought in, according to the Rama’s explanation. The Bach inferred that the Metivta law does not speak about the dowry money and that she acquires only if she seized, but the Chelkat Mechokek disagrees, and the main law is like him. The Taz writes that we remove from him only at the time of the get; if he does not want to divorce and we do not compel him, we do not remove anything unless she seized. The law of the Metivta applies specifically to "ma'is alai," not to "I want him but I want to pain him." The reason for the enactment is to prevent Jewish women from going astray. I already wrote in siman 75 that if property was consumed because he did not guard it properly, or it was stolen or lost, then for nichsei melog he is exempt and for nichsei tzon barzel he must pay; but if he used it improperly, the reverse applies: for nichsei tzon barzel he is exempt, because it is his, but for nichsei melog he must pay if he did not use it properly and consume the fruits.
With claims that he is repulsive, and she is not, etc. In that case, if she seized money, it helps, because according to the law of the Metivta, if her claim is true, he must return even dowry money. Therefore, if she seized, one can say her claim is true. What I wrote above that seizure of money does not help refers to Talmudic law or to one who says, "I want him," where the Metivta law does not apply. This is unlike the Bach.
She may not give her property to others. So wrote Mahari Weil. Responsa Ralbach wrote that he cannot protest, since he does not eat the fruits. Bedieved this requires study.
Even in a place, etc. See siman 159 for several laws in this matter. The Chelkat Mechokek wrote that if he betrothed her deceitfully and married her deceitfully, we compel him to divorce her; and if she died before the get, he does not inherit her.
If her father seized. I already wrote at the beginning of the siman that the father’s seizure helps only where the dowry had not yet reached the groom; then he need not give it to the groom.
