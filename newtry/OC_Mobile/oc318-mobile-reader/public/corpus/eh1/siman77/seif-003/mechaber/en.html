This rebellious wife — when she leaves after twelve months without ketubah — she returns everything that belongs to the husband; but property she brought to him and her existing tzon barzel — if she seized it they do not remove it from her hand; and if she did not seize it they do not give it to her. {Rama: And land of tzon barzel she takes (Tur). And likewise everything lost from her property for which the husband accepted liability upon himself — he does not pay her anything. All this is regarding tzon barzel property; but melog property of hers is in her possession and she takes it (Tur). And some say all this is when she does not give a valid excuse and reason for her words why she says he is repulsive to me; but when she gives a valid excuse for her words — such as saying he does not walk in a straight path and wastes his money and the like — then they judge her according to the law that the Geonim enacted (Tur in name of Maharam of Rothenburg); and it is called dina demetibta — the husband must return to her everything she brought to him in her dowry, meaning tzon barzel if they still exist and are fit for their work she takes all as is; and if they are not fit for their work — and all the more if stolen or lost — the husband must pay everything; and her melog property if still existent or something that comes from her power she takes; but if completely consumed the husband need not pay (dina demetibta, Tur in name of Rif). But everything he gave her or wrote for her she does not take at all — even if she seized she must return (Mordechai ch. HaIsha). And they do not compel him to divorce nor her to remain with him (this too in Tur in responsum of Maharam). And if he acted improperly and betrothed her with deceit and schemes — they compel him to divorce (Rosh kelal 35). And some say they also impose a cherem (Tur in name of Maharam) if people learned from her to rebel, or he does so out of anger and quarrel, or to extract money from him — then even what she seized from her property they take from her hand and return to the husband (Mordechai ch. HaIsha). And there is no distinction whether she seized or not — except when she claims repulsive and gives no valid excuse clarified for her words; but if she gives a valid excuse and there is no deceit in this — beit din judges according to what their eyes see (Mahari Weil 1:7). And they can administer an oath to her on this if she truly claims he is repulsive to me (there). And so is fit to rule; and all the time he has not given a get he has no compulsion and approach upon her; but nevertheless she may not give from hers to others and he can protest — for if she dies he inherits her (this too there). Even in a place where they compel him to divorce a rebellious wife — if she died before he divorced her he inherits her, for her inheritance is not uprooted except through divorce (Tur in name of a Gaon). If her father seized dowry property she brought to her husband — it is effective as if she seized herself (Mordechai in name of responsum of Maharam ch. HaIsha, and in glosses in name of responsum of Maharam). The garments the bride brought in have no law of tzon barzel; and tzon barzel is only the appraisal that the woman brings to her husband (Tur in name of Rosh).}
