(א) The woman who withheld her husband from marital relations, etc. The Tur records here the Talmudic law, three enactments, and afterward the laws of the metivta. The Talmudic law included, first, diminishing the ketubah by seven dinars each week; second, warnings by beit din that she return and public announcements four times; and third, a waiting period of twelve months. The Tur writes that they later added an enactment to wait twelve months and then he may divorce her if he wishes. Beit Yosef understood from this language that besides the four announcements they enacted that he not give her a get until twelve months, and that this is Rambam's view. The Shulchan Aruch wrote similarly. Beit Yosef also cited Maggid Mishneh that the Gemara's conclusion concerning one who says "he is repulsive to me" is that she loses nothing until twelve months, and during those twelve months we do not announce against her and do not reduce her ketubah; in this respect she differs from an ordinary rebellious wife. Beit Yosef earlier cited the Rosh that the twelve-month enactment was made in the days of the later Amoraim when they saw that Jewish women had become haughty and, when angry with their husbands, would say "I do not want him"; the husband would divorce her immediately because she exempted him from the ketubah, and then they would regret it. They therefore enacted that she remain a year, and if he divorces her within the year, her waiver is not a waiver. The same applies both to a claim of repulsion and to "I want him but wish to pain him." The Tur's expression "they added to enact" is not precise proof of a separate cumulative enactment. Rather, the Tur guarded against error: where the Gemara says "we delay her twelve months," it does not mean that this is in addition to the earlier four-week consultation. It is a later enactment replacing the intermediate enactment. Thus for both claims only the twelve-month waiting period is needed. My teacher and father-in-law followed Beit Yosef's reading and was forced into difficulties, but the truth is that the Tur's view is clear: the two cases are equal. The rest of the discussion clarifies the practical results for ketubah, announcements, and later claims, and compares the Rosh, Rambam, and the Shulchan Aruch.
