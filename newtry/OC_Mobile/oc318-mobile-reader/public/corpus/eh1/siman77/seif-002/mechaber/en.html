A woman who deprives her husband of marital relations — she is called a rebellious wife; and they ask her why she rebelled. If she said: He is repulsive to me and I cannot cohabit with him willingly — {Rama: And specifically when she requests a get without ketubah; but if she says: Give me a get and my ketubah — we are concerned perhaps she set her eyes on another and she has the law of a rebellious wife who says "I want" and "I pain him" (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Ran, and so Rashi explains in the Gemara).} If the husband wishes to divorce her — she has no ketubah at all; and she takes only her guaranteed property (tzon barzel) — whether from property she brought into her husband's possession and he became liable for it, or from melog property for which he did not become liable; and she takes nothing from her husband's property — even a shoe on her foot and a kerchief on her head that he bought for her she removes and gives; and everything he gave her as a gift she returns. {Rama: And some say even from tzon barzel property she takes only what she seized; and some say even melog property she takes only what she seized (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Ran who so wrote in name of Rashba).} And if she rebelled under her husband in order to pain him and said: Behold I pain him thus because he did such-and-such to me, or because he cursed me, or because he quarreled with me and the like — they send to her from beit din and say: Know that if you persist in your rebellion — even your ketubah of a hundred maneh you lose; and afterward they proclaim about her in synagogues and study halls every day for four Sabbaths one after another. {Rama: And some say they need not proclaim every day but four Sabbaths literally (Ran and Hagahot Maimoniyot, and so the language of Tur implies); and so is primary.} And they say: So-and-so rebelled against her husband; and after the proclamation they send to her from beit din a second time: If you persist in your rebellion — you lose your ketubah. If she persisted in rebellion and did not return — they consult about her and she loses her ketubah and has no ketubah at all; and they do not give her a get until twelve months; and she has no sustenance all twelve months. {Rama: And even if she is pregnant (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Rashba); and her handiwork is hers; but he takes the fruits (Tur).} And if she died before the get — her husband inherits her. {Rama: And he is obligated in her redemption and burial (Tur); and after twelve months the husband has no claim upon her and likewise she upon him — since the ketubah is lost she has no ketubah conditions at all, since he can divorce her without ketubah and withhold her at his will (words of the Rav, and so appears in Maharil siman 20).} After twelve months her return does not help — only her ketubah is lost; and if he wishes to keep her he must write her another ketubah; but within twelve months she can retract and has ketubah; and if she died within twelve months she has ketubah from the heirs (all this in Ran ch. HaIsha). If he wishes to divorce her within twelve months — he gives her tzon barzel and her ketubah and everything he wrote for her (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Rashba). {Rama: And some say nowadays when they do not marry two wives — we do not delay her twelve months if he wishes to divorce her; and if she does not wish — they permit him to marry another (Mordechai end of HaIsha in name of Raavan). And some dispute that they should not permit him to marry another (there in the gloss and responsum of Rashba siman 494 and Maharik shoresh 63); and so is primary. And specifically regarding a married woman; but a betrothed woman who rebelled against her husband and does not wish to enter to him — he divorces her against her will or marries another and they permit him (there). And it seems specifically within twelve months; but after twelve months if he wishes to divorce — she must accept from him against her will, or they permit him to marry another — for a woman has no power to make him an agunah forever; and so it appears (to rule). And some say even within twelve months if he went and married another on account of her rebellion — they do not compel him to divorce (Maharik shoresh 29).} And even if she was a niddah or ill and unfit for relations. {Rama: And there is no distinction whether she began to rebel before illness or afterward (Ran ch. HaIsha, and so appears in Mordechai in name of Raaviyah and Hagahot Alfasi) (and some dispute) (there in name of Maharam).} And even if her husband was a sailor whose onah is six months; and even if he has another wife; and likewise a betrothed woman whose time came to marry and she rebelled in order to pain him and was not married — behold she is a rebellious wife from relations. {Rama: And some say also a yevamah who did not wish to perform yibbum in order to pain him — they do to her according to this procedure.}
