An arusah who does not want, etc. If she clarifies her claim, it is obvious that her father is not obligated, for this is no worse than a married woman, where we remove nichsei tzon barzel from the husband; all the more so he need not give it to him. Rather it speaks where she cannot clarify the claim. With a married woman, if she seized we do not remove it from her, but seizure by her father does not help; here, where the dowry is still with her father, he need not give it. The same applies if she is married and the dowry is still with her father. Even if he gave a debt document to the groom, he need not pay, because these laws resemble those in siman 53 regarding a daughter who died. Even if she says, "I want him but I want to pain him," the law is so, as written there. However, if he gave a mamarni note as customary in Poland, where the lender’s name is not stated, it is like money in the groom’s hand and he must pay, as the Taz writes there. If the dowry is held by a third party, I also wrote there. All the more so from these laws we learn regarding our shidduchim that if she refuses, her father need not give what he pledged. A brief summary of the laws of mored and moredet: a husband who rebels against his wife has three dinarim added to her ketubah each week as a debt. According to most decisors, mored applies specifically to marital relations; if he refuses support, he is compelled to support her, though Tosafot, the Rosh, and Tur hold that refusing support also makes him a mored. If she wants, we compel him to divorce. If he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah, he is not a mored even after Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment, though there is dispute regarding support and clothing; we do not compel him to have relations, since according to his claim she is repulsive to him. A moredet who says, "I want him but I want to pain him," or says "ma'is alai" while demanding the ketubah, follows Talmudic law, not the Metivta law. She is warned, announced about for four weeks, warned again, and told by beit din that she will lose the ketubah. If she returns, some reduce her ketubah by seven dinarim per week and some do not. If she persists, according to Rambam, Ramban, Rashba, and Ran she loses the ketubah after the warnings, though he is asked not to divorce for twelve months; according to the Tur she loses only after twelve months. During twelve months she has no support and her earnings are hers; he remains obligated in burial and eats the fruits. After twelve months she has no ketubah or conditions of ketubah, and he is not obligated in burial unless she had seized nichsei tzon barzel, in which case he inherits them and must bury her. All agree he inherits her. Nichsei melog return to her; nichsei tzon barzel depend on seizure, and seizure must be by her herself, not by an agent or father, except where the dowry never reached the groom. Gifts of the husband are returned. Nowadays he may be allowed to divorce her against her will or, where impossible, marry another. A moredet who says true "ma'is alai" and does not demand the ketubah has three cases. If she gives a clear and verified reason, she receives nichsei melog and nichsei tzon barzel under the Metivta enactment. If her claim is visibly false, nichsei tzon barzel are given to him, while nichsei melog remain hers. If the claim is unclear but not fraudulent, he may divorce her immediately without ketubah; nichsei melog are returned, and nichsei tzon barzel depend on seizure. In such a case seizure of dowry money helps, because according to her claim the Metivta law applies. If she dies, burial and fruits depend on the doubts explained above.
