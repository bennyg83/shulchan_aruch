But I will not have relations with her. According to several decisors, one who rebels from marital relations is a mored, but one who rebels from support is not a mored; rather, we compel him to provide support or divorce her. Likewise, when she rebels from work, she is not a moredet; rather, we compel her, as written in siman 80. Tosafot write that this law depends on the dispute whether she may say, "I will not be supported and I will not work." Therefore, since the halacha is that she may say this, she is not a moredet; but he cannot say, "I will not support you," and he is a mored. So wrote the Rosh and Tur. However, he is not a mored if he says, "take your earnings for your food," and it suffices for her. Bach rules like the decisors that mored refers specifically to marital relations.
We add to her ketubah. Meaning that it becomes a debt upon him; it is not given to her immediately. Ran.
The weight of thirty-six barley grains. This means three dinarim taught in the Mishnah, which are rabbinic dinarim, one-eighth of Tzori coinage. See seif 66. This addition is half a dinar for each day. On Shabbat we do not add because it appears like Shabbat wages, unlike with a moredet, where the ketubah is reduced even for Shabbat. Gemara.
He sits and does not have relations. We do not compel him to have relations, because that is prohibited, as it is written, "also without knowledge the soul is not good," and both his will and her will are required. But in truth, even if he had relations through coercion, he remains a mored, as written nearby in the name of the Rosh.
We compel him immediately to divorce. So wrote the Tur, implying that we do not give him seven days, although regarding one who forbids by vow we wait seven days, as written in siman 74, because one who vows has more "bread in his basket" than a mored. So wrote the Perishah. Here, according to everyone, we compel him to divorce her, as written by Tosafot and the Rosh. Do not ask from the Rama below that we do not compel her to accept a get within twelve months because of Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment; perhaps there it is different because there is a set limit of twelve months. The Rosh elsewhere cites the Yerushalmi that we compel only regarding women forbidden to him, and all the more where she withholds herself we do not compel him; but he also writes at the end of HaMadir that we compel one who says, "I will not feed or support." One must say that he cites the Yerushalmi only to show that where she prevents herself, we do not compel him; but in truth the Rosh holds we compel him. See Bach, seif 15.
And some say that if he wants to divorce immediately. At first glance it is puzzling to prove from the Rambam, since he ruled according to Talmudic law where a man could divorce against her will. But nowadays, with Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, perhaps his law is like a mored even if he wants to pay the ketubah. The Chelkat Mechokek asks this. One can answer that the Rambam’s plain ruling that if he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah he is not a mored, without distinguishing even a first marriage where one may not divorce unless he found ervah, shows that even where it is forbidden lechatchilah to divorce, he is not a mored and we do not add to the ketubah. From this it follows that even for us, with Rabbenu Gershom’s ban, he is not a mored. Shiltei Gibborim also implies that even nowadays he is not a mored if he wants to divorce and pay the ketubah. Responsa Maharam Mizrachi, siman 30, ruled that he need not provide support because he is prevented by Rabbenu Gershom’s enactment, comparing it to one whose time to marry arrived but Chazal prevent the marriage. The Chelkat Mechokek objects that there he had not yet married her; Responsa Maharam ben Sasson raised this and still ruled that the husband can say kim li like Maharam Mizrachi, and Bach also rules like him. However, Responsa Rosh, Responsa Maharik, and below at the end of siman 117 imply that he must provide food and clothing, though it appears we do not compel him to have relations with her. Regarding inheritance, the Rif, Rosh, and other decisors write that even those whom we compel to divorce, whether by law or enactment, if she died before the get he inherits her. Shiltei Gibborim and Darkei Moshe write that where he is a mored or forbids his wife by vow he does not inherit, as in Shulchan Aruch below, siman 90. See the Bach’s distinction between one who finds her repulsive and one who wants her but torments her.
