(I also found) Similar to what I wrote elsewhere regarding a custom that functions as an enactment, concerning a fixed town scribe for documents and ketubot where one witness does not know how to read: all the more a communal enactment helps here, that designated people serve as witnesses for all betrothals. It is as if they designated those witnesses alone for each betrothal. All the more the principle applies that whoever betroths does so according to rabbinic intent, and rabbinic intent is that the witnesses be valid and uniquely designated as witnesses. In such an enactment it is also possible that the cantor and sexton are designated as the sole witnesses. If one of them is a relative in a particular betrothal and another signs the ketubah, then the two witnesses signed on the ketubah are designated as the betrothal witnesses; that is the automatic form of the enactment. Nevertheless, one who states explicitly during the betrothal ceremony that the ketubah witnesses alone shall be the betrothal witnesses acts in the best manner explicitly. Even without that, however, it is so as stated above. The Nachalat Shiv'ah cites a custom to have two ketubah witnesses besides the two betrothal witnesses, and apparently there too specific people are designated for this and are always known as designated for that purpose.
