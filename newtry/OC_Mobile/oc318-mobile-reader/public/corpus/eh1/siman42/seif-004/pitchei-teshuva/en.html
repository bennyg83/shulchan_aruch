He took the woman’s hand. See Chelkat Mechokek, s.k. 2, who wondered why the Rama above, siman 28, seif 4, copied the words of the Ramah, while here he copied the words of Hagahot Mordechai. The Beit Shmuel, s.k. 3, answered that in the case of Hagahot Mordechai she initially wanted to become betrothed to him, and only afterward he took her hand by force, so we say she should have thrown the ring away, etc. See the Taz above, siman 30, s.k. 2, in the words of the annotator there. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 84, who writes that one who examines the source will see that the truth is with the Beit Shmuel and not with the annotator in the Taz. In his humble opinion there is another distinction: here, where he inserted the ring on her finger as jewelry, even if we say she did not throw it away because she thought she would be responsible for it, she should have removed it from her finger, guarded it in her hand, and covered it, rather than adorning herself with what was against her will. Therefore even if she had not consented initially, there is still concern. See also Biur HaGra and what he wrote further in siman 101, part of which was brought above, siman 28, s.k. 16. He further wrote about the case there that the witnesses contradicted each other in the essence of the matter: one said he saw him insert it on her finger as jewelry, while the other said he saw them both holding it, then the man released it and left it in her hand, and it was never inserted on her finger. Although in that case there was no practical difference, nevertheless these are parts of the body of the act and sometimes affect the law. They are considered contradiction in the examinations and their testimony is removed completely. This matter was already stated in Maharit, part 2, Even HaEzer, siman 43, and Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 104. In that case, although there were two witnesses disqualifying the first witness, one of them testified only based on his estimate that the witness had stolen his purse, and that is not testimony fit to remove a man from his presumption of validity. Therefore the witness remains valid, the witnesses contradict each other, and the woman is permitted. Since he found no other basis for leniency, he did not want others to act on his ruling unless the matter came before the leading Gaon of the generation. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 59, for several distinctions in this matter.
