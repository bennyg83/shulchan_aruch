He holds like the Rambam that anything not validated is nothing, though many disagree, as stated in the second chapter of Ketubot: the Sages were lenient regarding validation of documents because it is rabbinic. The Rashba, however, wrote that even if it is validated it is nothing, lest they signed without her knowledge, as in Sanhedrin 29b, the case of the deed, etc., where they asked the scribes, etc. In such a case one does not know in the present time; it is not a document and is testimony from writing. See Choshen Mishpat 39:11 and 18. The glosses of Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbi Simchah that even a validated document written properly is nothing, from the Tosefta in the second chapter of Ketubot: the power of witnesses is greater than the power of a document, for if witnesses say that so-and-so died or was killed, his wife may remarry, but if they found written in a document that so-and-so died or was killed, his wife may not remarry. The power of a document is greater than witnesses in that one who lends by document collects from encumbered property, whereas by witnesses he collects only from free property. Thus even a full document is nothing regarding a married woman, as the end regarding loans shows. The reason is that such a document was not given to be written in the case of a woman. The Yerushalmi at the end of Yevamot, cited by the Rosh, also discusses finding it in a document; even the lenient view there is because they were lenient in that matter. His words are correct, for the Yerushalmi can be read as speaking of a full document, and the baraita's reason is that it was not given to be written and is therefore testimony from writing.
