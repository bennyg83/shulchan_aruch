Section 5, "among those disqualified for testimony." One of the betrothal witnesses was the collector of the Sabbath candle payments collected nowadays in these countries. Elsewhere I wrote that one should not collect from those called "hidden," that is, people not listed in the government's population registers, and that it tends toward theft. Nevertheless, there is no concern about this witness, as in Choshen Mishpat 34:24-25, for he does not know of it; and even if informed, it is known that he will not comply. Besides, he thinks he is escaping danger to life from the army, and believes he acts for preservation of life. The holder of that collection originally leased it with the understanding that he would collect from the hidden, and no words would help him. Since this has spread throughout the entire country, no one will comply until it is corrected everywhere. The collector himself does not collect; a gentile goes with him. In any event, he is no worse than tax collectors. Regarding a sexton about whom a rumor emerged that he misappropriated the candles he receives from the community to light in the synagogue and lights them in his house: as written in Responsa Mabit, section 51, there is no concern even by his own admission. So too with an uninterrupted rumor, even initially. Although the Rashba wrote that initially one should not join for testimony or extract money by a witness about whom rumor emerged, the Mabit wrote that this is not according to all. In such a case, even if there were witnesses to the theft, there would be grounds not to disqualify him, whether because it resembles those who bury the dead on the first festival day, or the tax collectors mentioned above, or because he thinks "the ox eats from its threshing," and that they gave with him in mind, and that it is money with no claimant or no owner. Still, this would require great analysis if there were an uninterrupted rumor. Here, however, it was not in the category of rumor; only one woman said so from a slight and inconclusive estimate. Even if she said so before several women, it is not a rumor, for we do not presume that the other women also speak so when we have not heard it. It is no rumor at all, and all agree that we do not establish aspersion against all testimony until now. There is no opening for objection here.
