Against her will she is not betrothed. Although we hold that one who sells under coercion, his sale is a sale, and she is as though she sold herself to her husband, the Gemara states that since the husband acted improperly, the Sages uprooted the kiddushin, for we hold that anyone who betroths does so according to the will of the Sages. If they coerced him to betroth, the Maggid Mishneh wrote that we do not say this is comparable to coercing her, because a woman cannot leave him and release herself from him, whereas a man can divorce her against her will; therefore she is betrothed. At first glance one may say that nowadays, after the enactments of Rabbeinu Gershom, that one may not divorce against her will, the woman's law should equal the man's. Perhaps in such a case, where he betrothed through coercion, Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact, and he can divorce her against her will. So too wrote the Taz. It is proven from the Maggid Mishneh that he holds that if one acquires under coercion, he acquires; for otherwise, from where would one have thought that if they coerced him to betroth, his kiddushin are kiddushin? After all, he acquires against his will. Although he can divorce her against her will, how does he acquire against his will? Rather, one must say that he holds that one acquires under coercion, as with a seller. Still, one might have thought to compare his coercion to her coercion; for this the Maggid Mishneh gave his reason, that they are not comparable because he can divorce her against her will. So too it is necessarily proven from the Semag that one acquires under coercion, for he holds that the sugya speaks where they coerced him, and his text is, she acted improperly; this shows that coerced acquisition is effective. The some-say view he brought is what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rif and the Ittur. So too it appears in Choshen HaMishpat at the end of siman 205 in his name, that they hold coerced acquisition is ineffective. There the Rama ruled like him. This is surprising, for here it is clear according to these poskim that one acquires under coercion, and Chelkat Mechokek also asked this. The Raavad wrote that here it speaks where they coerced him and he said, I want. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that he need not say, I want. See Sema at the beginning of siman 205, and see siman 134.
