There is no, etc.; and one who betroths, etc. Kiddushin 2b and 44a; Yevamot 19; and the dispute in Bava Batra 48b regarding one whom they coerced to accept betrothal, analogous to a coerced sale there. As stated at the end of chapter 6 of Bava Kamma, when he says "I want," or when he accepted the money on his own, it is as though he says "I want." So too in the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 5, and we rule like Mar bar Rav Ashi, as Rabbeinu Tam and the Geonim wrote. Thus "against her will" means even where she accepted the betrothal herself, but only because of coercion.
But, etc.; and some say, etc. They said that if one was coerced and sold, the sale stands; they did not say that if he was coerced and acquired, it stands. But the Rashba and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that if he was coerced to acquire, it is all the more so; and this is also the Ra'avad's view, except that he wrote that he must say "I want." Yet he already wrote that whenever one performs the act with his own hands, he need not say "I want," and the Maggid Mishneh wrote so here regarding the Ra'avad. The Baal Ha'itur's reason is as stated there: specifically coerced sale, where he received money, as there. See Tosafot there, s.v. "if we say," and s.v. "he betrothed," and the Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat 205 in his name. The Rav wrote at the end of section 205, but it escaped them that the Rambam, Ra'avad, Rashba, Maggid Mishneh, Semag, and other decisors disagree; and this is the main view. It is specifically in a sale where he gives and receives no benefit at all. But where he takes money, even if the benefit is less than the loss, since a person does not wish to sell land, nevertheless Amemar says in Kiddushin that she is betrothed even though she receives only a perutah. Maharik wrote similarly, even where the benefit is very small. All the more so one acquires the field, for we say that he jumped and sold. According to the Semag, the passage "they hung him and he betrothed" refers to the man, and all the more to a sale. The Hagahot Maimoniyot wondered at him and explained like Rashbam and Tosafot that it refers to the woman, from "he acted improperly, etc." Still, as stated at the beginning of chapter 14 of Yevamot and chapter 5 of Gittin, the principle differs between him and her.
