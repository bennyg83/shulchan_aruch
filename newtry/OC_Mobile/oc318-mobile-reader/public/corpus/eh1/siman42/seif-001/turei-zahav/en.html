A woman against her will: although regarding a sale we say that because of the coercion he resolves to transfer ownership, here, with respect to kiddushin, the Sages uprooted the betrothal and the money is a gift, since he acted improperly. Everyone who betroths does so subject to the will of the Sages, for he says "according to the law of Moses and Israel"; therefore the Sages have the power to annul the kiddushin.
But the man, etc. The Rabbis did not need to uproot the kiddushin, since he is able to divorce the woman against her will. Therefore it is like a sale: under coercion he resolves and obligates himself to her. Nowadays, when we do not divorce against her will because of the ban of Rabbeinu Gershom, one could compare the man to the woman; but it appears that in such a case the ban of Rabbeinu Gershom does not apply.
