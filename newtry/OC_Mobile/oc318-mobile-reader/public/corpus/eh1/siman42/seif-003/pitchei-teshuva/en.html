See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, beginning of siman 55, who writes that at first glance it is surprising why we should not believe her that she did not intend kiddushin. After all, one witness is believed regarding prohibitions even regarding his own matter, and we require it to be in his power only where a prohibition has already been established. Here no prohibition had been established, for we are discussing the beginning of the kiddushin, and on the contrary she stands in the presumption of being unmarried. In truth, Maharam’s reason is not that she is not believed; rather, she is believed, but we do not consider the intent, because the matter follows the spoken words, and even if the thought was otherwise, unspoken thoughts are not legally effective. He discusses this at length and concludes that regarding credibility in a matter that is not against an established presumption, both the man who betroths and the woman betrothed are believed, even according to Beit Shmuel and Riva, who say above, siman 27, Beit Shmuel s.k. 5, that the woman does not have the power to say she did not understand. Perhaps there that is because the language is clear, so her claim that she did not understand is weak. But where there is no weakness, she is believed, because one cannot contradict the sugya that one witness is believed regarding prohibitions where no prohibition was established. From his words it appears that he assumes simply that where no prohibition was established, one witness is believed even in a matter of ervah, and this also appears to be his view in simanim 38 and 61, as I wrote below in siman 43, seif 1. However, in siman 54, brought below in siman 157, seif 4, s.k. 9, he is uncertain about this. See Binat Adam, sha'ar Beit HaNashim, end of siman 29, who writes that Tosafot in Gittin 2 implies that even in a matter of ervah one witness is effective, and the Rashba’s novellae there imply this even more, and so the Ramban writes explicitly, as brought by the Ran in Perek HaMitkabel regarding one who tells his agent, "Go betroth a woman for me." But from the Ran’s novellae to Gittin it implies that in ervah it is not effective, and he writes so explicitly in Perek HaMitkabel, though his proof requires study. The Ran writes in two places that one witness is effective; see Beit Shmuel, siman 37, s.k. 45, and there seif 24 in the Rama, with Beit Shmuel s.k. 50, and so writes Maharit. The Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Ritva does not prove otherwise, for he speaks where a prohibition had already been established. Maharik, shoresh 72, writes explicitly that one witness in ervah, where no prohibition was established, is believed, and one may rely on him. See also Responsa Bigdei Kehunah, siman 18, and the later responsa cited there, as well as Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 82.
