(ד) And one of them is related. The reason the second witness is not invalidated by joining with him, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 37, where both are invalidated and even if the second later comes to testify anew it does not help, and so Sema wrote in siman 37 and siman 45, is that specifically when the witnesses are signed, it helps for the valid witness to testify again; but with oral testimony it does not help. This is also proven in the Talmud, the beginning of Makkot, in the sugya of what should two brothers do, etc. One must say that here it speaks in a manner where the testimony was not nullified, and there Rif and the Rosh disagree when testimony is nullified; so wrote Chelkat Mechokek. One may also say that he did not intend to testify, and nevertheless what they testify today helps, even though they did not originally intend to testify. I later saw that according to the Rosh's approach this is unnecessary: even if they intended to testify and had already testified, he holds that the testimony is nullified only in the sense that the beit din cannot rule on their testimony; but here, at the time of kiddushin, their testimony had not yet been nullified. If so, the kiddushin were valid kiddushin, and although the beit din cannot rule on the basis of their testimony, nevertheless the man performed kiddushin before valid witnesses. Likewise, if he betrothed only before two people and one of them was related, and they already testified before the beit din, nevertheless at the time of kiddushin the kiddushin was before one witness. In Sefer Bayit Chadash he wrote that where both admit, the testimony of the valid witness helps. This is not so, for we derive from Scripture that a related witness nullifies the testimony, and it is as though he betrothed before invalid witnesses; their admission does not help. Rather, it appears to me as I wrote. If the witness is related to Reuven but unrelated to his daughter who is being betrothed, if her father accepts the kiddushin, he is invalid as a witness because the father is the principal party. Although regarding monetary wills some validate in such a case, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 33, see Responsa Rashakh I, siman 2.
