One who betroths before Torah-disqualified witnesses — they are not kiddushin. But before rabbinically disqualified witnesses or witnesses who are doubtfully Torah-disqualified — if he wishes to marry her he returns and betroths before valid witnesses; and if he does not wish to marry — she needs a get out of doubt; even if the woman denies and says you did not betroth me — he is compelled to give a get. And so is the law of all doubtful kiddushin: if he wishes to marry he returns and betroths certainly; and if he does not wish to marry — she needs a get from him out of doubt. And if another came and betrothed her — behold she is doubtfully betrothed to the second and needs a get from both, or the first divorces and the second marries; but not to divorce the second and marry the first. {Rama: One suspected of forbidden relations even if there are no witnesses that he committed the forbidden act but only a general suspicion (Tosafot and Rosh) — disqualified for a woman's testimony, whether for kiddushin or divorce (Rambam). One who betroths before Torah-disqualified witnesses due to their wickedness — such as before apostates or other wicked men — some are concerned for kiddushin lest they repented (R' Yonah Mintz siman 11 and so Orach Chayyim in name of Rashi). And some are lenient (Beit Yosef and Maharam Padua siman 37). However those apostates who were compelled to convert out of fear and could not escape with their lives and secretly observe the mitzvot — they are valid for testimony (Rivash siman 11). Some say that which we say one who betroths before rabbinically disqualified witnesses we are concerned for kiddushin — refers to Torah kiddushin; but for rabbinic kiddushin with rabbinically disqualified witnesses we are not concerned for kiddushin at all (Beit Yosef in name of responsum of Rashba).}
