But, etc. Since by Torah law they are valid; see above, section 28, paragraph 21, and Kiddushin 24b, "since it is a rabbinic exposition, etc.," all the more for a rabbinic matter. This is the Rambam's view, for he holds that even maternal relatives create betrothal rabbinically, as stated in Choshen Mishpat 33:2 in the gloss. Rav Hai Gaon, Baal Halakhot Gedolot, many Geonim, and the She'iltot brought the above proof. The Rif disagreed and wrote that the proof is nothing, for if so, all relatives should be the same, since all are from rabbinic exposition, while the plain verse "fathers shall not be put to death for children" concerns the sins of children. There is also an explicit verse in Chronicles indicating that no bill of divorce is required, as stated, "whoever betroths does so according to rabbinic intent." What is said there, "a rabbinic exposition," the Rashba explained in responsum 1188, and Tosafot in Gittin 42b wrote similarly. He writes that the concern is lest one err, find him in the marketplace, and say, "you are my slave." Nevertheless, his view follows the Geonim, and he wrote that we do not say "whoever betroths does so according to rabbinic intent" except where it was stated explicitly; otherwise many cases in the Talmud would have used that principle, and they did not.
