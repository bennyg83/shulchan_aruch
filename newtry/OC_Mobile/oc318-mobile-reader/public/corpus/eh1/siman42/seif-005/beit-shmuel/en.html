We compel him. In the Rambam the text reads: we compel her. Kesef Mishneh wrote that the Tur's reading is primary, because compulsion does not apply to her; if she wishes to marry, she must take a get. In Kuntres Acharon, the Bach explains that we compel her not to marry without a get. According to the Tur's reading, that we compel him, the reason is that the witnesses' testimony is as though he said, I betrothed you, and she says, you did not betroth me, in which case we compel him, as written below. However, if he wishes to betroth her anew, one may say that we do not compel him, even though when she says, you did not betroth me, one may say that we compel him even if he wishes to betroth her; that case is different, because she does not want him. Here, however, he certainly betrothed her, so we do not compel him to divorce if he wishes to betroth her; rather, this speaks where he withholds and does not wish to betroth her, and therefore we compel him.

Perhaps they repented. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that one who examines the responsum will see that they disagree only where he repents at the end; then one can say that his end proves his beginning, and perhaps immediately at the time of kiddushin he contemplated repentance. But if he did not repent, we are not concerned that perhaps he contemplated repentance. This is not comparable to a condition, on condition that I am righteous, where we are concerned that perhaps he contemplated repentance, because there there is a basis for the matter, since he stipulated so.
