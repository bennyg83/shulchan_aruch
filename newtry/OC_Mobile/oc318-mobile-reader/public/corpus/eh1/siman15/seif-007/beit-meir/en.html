And permitted with the second one's relatives — Beit Shmuel wrote Derishah wrote specifically after the second's death; and Maharshal wrote I did not see in Derishah hint from these words; and in my opinion I explain: since this is Perishah's language "and permitted with his second relatives" — meaning to marry — for we hold one marries his rape victim and he brings this as proof promiscuity does not forbid his wife to him since permitted with her relatives; and Maharshal wrote I do not know who inserted this strained proof — behold Tur's words are an arranged mishnah and it is a law per se; and it appears Perishah was challenged regarding itself the law — Tur already informed us his rape-victim's daughter is permitted after death and strained in his explanation. And perhaps also the mishnah "and permitted with the second relatives" explains this view (and requires examination in the book itself and it does not come); and if he holds like Maharshal that here even in her lifetime permitted — no need to strain — for we informed much; rather learn as Beit Shmuel understood. Nevertheless in my opinion the law Maharshal who agrees with Taz. {Rama gloss: And permitted with his second relatives; however if the relative is accustomed, etc.} Beit Shmuel challenges this law from what we hold seif 13 if he consummated he need not divorce; and Maharshal already wrote great distinction between what sages forbade lekhatchilah because common versus we see she is accustomed only; but Beit Shmuel wrote whence to distinguish between common and accustomed — meaning whence proof for this distinction from the Talmud; and in my opinion one can learn from betrothal 12: that son-in-law who passed his father-in-law's door — they would say "your mother-in-law from me" — behold if he fornicated with his mother-in-law forbidden to pass her house; all the more if we see she is accustomed in his house through her daughter and impossible to prevent her coming to her daughter through false claims that she goes to visit her — they compel the husband to divorce his daughter lest he transgress the prohibition he is liable for her rebuke; and the truth in Hagahot Alfasi as Darkei Moshe copied there is only regarding his mother-in-law; but Darkei Moshe wrote and it appears in Gemara chapter HaIshah Rabbah no distinction between mother-in-law and other forbidden relations and I did not merit to understand; therefore Maharshal wrote perhaps this was said only regarding mother-in-law who is drafted because of her daughter; and it may be the law is with him since we see even Rambam chapter 2 Laws of Forbidden Relations law 12 though he equates other forbidden relations to mother-in-law — Chelkat Mechokek wrote because of the distinction he does not mention lashes of rebellion on them; and perhaps also distinction regarding removing his wife and requires examination. And what Beit Shmuel wondered — even per his view not wanting to distinguish between common and accustomed — there is no permissibility here unlike seif 13 which is only regarding an available woman who did not come to this custom to be suspected of forbidden relations — understand; and know we see an available woman seduced — even afterward becoming a married woman — nowhere is it forbidden to dwell with her in an alley; forbidden only to marry her relatives; but one merely accused and all the more one who fornicated with the forbidden relation — Rambam forbids dwelling with her in an alley — learn there is distinction. But the main thing compelling distinction between common and accustomed is Rama's own words where he wrote likewise all promiscuity, etc., because of his wife they do not forbid his wife to him; he also wrote Beit Shmuel beginning s.k. 7 — and the scribe erred and it belongs here on what he wrote "and permitted with the second relatives" from s.k. 37 implying even in her lifetime and it is not so; and the law is with him and not as Maharshal who meant here the second since without marriage the promiscuous relative has opportunity to enter his house to visit the relative forbidden because of her — and therefore perhaps here even in her lifetime; and in my opinion one reason: tomorrow his wife may die and she would be forbidden to him and today he would marry her; and also even in his wife's lifetime it is not comparable to one entering once to visit versus entering twice.
