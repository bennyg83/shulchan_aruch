And likewise the other arayot whom he married after presumed death. Necessarily this means that he did not know she was forbidden to him as an ervah. But in this case there is no possible permitted path through error, for no death can permit her to him. This is the Rambam's language, drawn from the baraita on Yevamot 94b: all arayot in the Torah, etc.; see there. However, see Tosafot there, s.v. Rabbi Akiva adds; their words imply that according to Rabbi Akiva, whom we rule like, this applies to all arayot who became forbidden to him through kiddushin without nisu'in, such as his father's brother's wife and the like. Whether he married her intentionally or unintentionally, she requires a get, lest people say the first kiddushin were mistaken and a married woman leaves without a get. See Maharsha there. The language of the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch does not quite imply this here, and the matter requires study.
