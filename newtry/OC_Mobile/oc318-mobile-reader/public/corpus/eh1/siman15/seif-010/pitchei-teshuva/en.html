Who forbids. Ba'er Heitev wrote if already married father-in-law's wife Tzemach Tzedek wrote, etc.; and see Beit Meir who wrote examiner of Tzemach Tzedek responsum sees not for that reason he permitted but other reason — one because disgrace to children and also because his teachers who permit even lekhatchilah and we forbid post factum; and brings proof — see there; and in my opinion proof clear from siman 164 seif 7, etc.; therefore Tzemach Tzedek ruled well and not Yeshuat Shlomo who ruled per Nimukei Yosef to forbid even post factum, etc. — see there. And regarding son-in-law's wife he brought in Beit Meir Yeshuat Shlomo who wrote I desire when it comes to my hand I will permit and if I succeed I will abolish it; and also brought Sefer Kenesset Yisrael chapter 2 Yevamot letter 9; and what Ba'er Heitev wrote in name of Beit Shmuel if married secondary under duress, etc. — see Beit Meir who wrote difficulty in what it discusses if duress forced both to marry each other in fear of death — I wonder if room for kiddushin and marriage as these even to require get; kal va-chomer from siman 42 if raped woman and betrothed no get needed — all the more both under duress, etc.; and if discusses where second was wicked, etc. — plainly ruled there at least betrothed from uncertainty — if so plainly obligated to divorce because of her prohibition for all forbidden relations and secondary equal in prohibition, end quote.
