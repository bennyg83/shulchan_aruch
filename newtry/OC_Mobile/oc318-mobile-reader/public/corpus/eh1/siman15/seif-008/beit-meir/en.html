And permitted with the second one's relatives — Beit Shmuel wrote Derishah wrote specifically after the second's death; and Maharshal wrote I did not see in Derishah hint from these words; and in my opinion I explain: since this is Perishah's language "and permitted with his second relatives" — meaning to marry — for we hold one marries his rape victim and he brings this as proof promiscuity does not forbid his wife to him since permitted with her relatives; and Maharshal wrote I do not know who inserted this strained proof — behold Tur's words are an arranged mishnah and it is a law per se; and it appears Perishah was challenged regarding itself the law — Tur already informed us his rape-victim's daughter is permitted after death and strained in his explanation. And perhaps also the mishnah "and permitted with the second relatives" explains this view (and requires examination in the book itself); and if he holds like Maharshal that here even in her lifetime permitted — no need to strain; rather learn as Beit Shmuel understood. Nevertheless in my opinion the law Maharshal who agrees with Taz. {Rama gloss: And permitted with his second relatives; however if the relative is accustomed, etc.} Beit Shmuel challenges from seif 13 if he consummated he need not divorce; and Maharshal wrote distinction between what sages forbade lekhatchilah because common versus we see she is accustomed only; but Beit Shmuel wrote whence proof for distinction from the Talmud; and in my opinion one can learn from Kiddushin 12: son-in-law passing father-in-law's door — they say "your mother-in-law from me" — behold if he fornicated with mother-in-law forbidden to pass her house; all the more if accustomed in his house through her daughter — they compel him to divorce lest he transgress; and Hagahot Alfasi as Darkei Moshe copied is only regarding mother-in-law; but Darkei Moshe wrote Gemara chapter HaIshah Rabbah no distinction; therefore Maharshal wrote perhaps only mother-in-law drafted because of her daughter; and perhaps Rambam law 12 though he equates other forbidden relations — Chelkat Mechokek wrote he does not mention lashes of rebellion; and perhaps distinction regarding removing his wife. And what Beit Shmuel wondered — even per his view — no permissibility here unlike seif 13 regarding available woman; and know available woman seduced — even becoming married woman — nowhere forbidden to dwell in alley; forbidden only to marry relatives; but one accused and fornicator — Rambam forbids dwelling in alley — learn distinction. But main thing is Rama's words; Beit Shmuel beginning s.k. 7 scribe erred — belongs here on "permitted with second relatives" implying even in lifetime — not so; law with him not Maharshal who meant second without marriage promiscuous relative enters house — perhaps even in lifetime; one reason: tomorrow wife may die; also not comparable one visit versus two.
