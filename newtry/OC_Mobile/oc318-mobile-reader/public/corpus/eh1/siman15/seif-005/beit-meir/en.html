A man is permitted, etc.; and some forbid — Beit Shmuel wrote; and if he already married — Tzemach Tzedek responsum, etc. — see there — for not for that reason he permitted; on the contrary he wrote for that reason he was obligated to remove since the instruction was not from renowned decisors but from one sage who permitted — one reason because of disgrace to children and also plainly because his teachers who permit even lekhatchilah and we forbid post factum; and he brings proof — see there; and in my opinion the proof is clear from siman 164 seif 7 they do not forbid his wife because of rumor of their words — and certainly this is correct Tzemach Tzedek and not Yeshuat Shlomo who ruled per Nimukei Yosef to forbid even post factum, etc. — see there. Regarding his son-in-law's wife he brought in Beit Meir the words of Yeshuat Shlomo who wrote I desire when it comes to my hand I will permit and if I succeed I will abolish it; and also brought in Sefer Kenesset Yisrael chapter 2 Yevamot letter 9; and what Ba'er Heitev wrote in name of Beit Shmuel if he married a secondary relation under duress, etc. — see Beit Meir who wrote difficulty in what it discusses if duress forced both unwillingly to marry each other in fear of death — I wonder if there is room for kiddushin and marriage as these for a layman even to require a get; and kal va-chomer from what we hold siman 42 if he raped a woman and betrothed her no get needed — all the more when both are under duress there is no substance and it is only like forcing promiscuous intercourse — the secondary is plainly forbidden because of duress and no get needed; and if so specifically where he caused and acted improperly; but when he too is under duress they did not nullify — for the essential reason in Rashi Bava Batra 48b because of "whoever betroths" etc. concludes he relied on sages' view and they do not want this kiddushin; if so consider — there one can say he did not rely his kiddushin on sages nevertheless they do not take effect without their will; all the more when he too is under duress certainly he relied on sages and from the Torah they want betrothal of raped woman as if betrothed under duress forever; and likewise proven from Tosafot Gittin chapter 2 2b s.v. for R' Eliezer concludes marriage against her will — learn even when both under duress against her will; otherwise still concern perhaps both will be forced; and if it discusses where the second was wicked and handed him to a rapist until he betrothed and married her by her duress and her will — this we plainly rule siman above she is betrothed from uncertainty — if so plainly obligated to divorce because of her prohibition — for all forbidden relations and secondary relations are equal in prohibition.
