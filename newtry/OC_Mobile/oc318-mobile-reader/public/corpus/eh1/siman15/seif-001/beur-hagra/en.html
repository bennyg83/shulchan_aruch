Some from the Torah. And they are eighteen forbidden relations in the Torah apart from niddah, married woman, male, and animal; and all explicit in Torah except his daughter derived from gezeirah shavah "here here" as in Yevamot and Sanhedrin; and his mother-in-law and her mother-in-law and his father-in-law are included in woman and her daughter, etc., as Rashi wrote beginning Yevamot; and as in beginning chapter HaNisrafin; and all explicit in mishnah beginning Yevamot "fifteen women," etc., and mishnah "six forbidden relations," etc.; and subtract those counting brother's wife three times and sister twice — eighteen forbidden relations remain.
Some rabbinically. And they are sixteen as written there 21b "and Scripture writes sixteen," etc.; and as written wife of mother's brother whether from father or mother counted as one; and subtract two there per Amimar and count two Yerushalmi adds as in seif 5 and 7; and per R' Yehudah chapter 2 unlike Amimar there are eighteen; and though there is another father-in-law's wife per Rabbenu Tam — this is only marit ayin; and Rambam counts twenty counting R' Chiya's ten; and as written siman 14.
Those, etc. Plain in Talmud in several places as written 23a, 94b, and other places; and in Tosefta: forbidden relations they said are not like his wife for all — and no get needed from him; secondary relations need get from him; and likewise Yerushalmi — Rosh brought: what difference forbidden relations and secondary relations — forbidden relations if he transgressed and married her she leaves without get; secondary relations, etc.; and likewise chapter 1 of Devarim: all forbidden to him, etc. — see there.
And likewise, etc. As written chapter One Who Marries regarding yibum that it is safek, etc.; and likewise regarding rival of forbidden relation chapter 4 Brothers; and chapter Widow to High Priest fell, etc.
