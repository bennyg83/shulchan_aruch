And Rambam in one who betroths — both with disqualified witnesses rabbinically — she needs a get from both; and I do not know how to connect the words — do we doubt secondary relations whether sages have power to say that even though Torah kiddushin take effect they do not apply fully and there is room for a second betrothal to take effect — we did not doubt this; for certainly whoever betroths, etc.; rather the book alongside Darkei Moshe has proof they decreed thus from the Torah to make her a married woman — unlike standalone rabbinic prohibition where we find sages disqualified him for all testimony and it is as if they said everything done before them is as if done without witnesses — plainly kiddushin are void; but some say because of "whoever betroths" they are completely void and no get needed; and Rambam is concerned to be stringent perhaps they did not want to establish their words leniently — all follows their view; therefore she needs a get from both. In my humble opinion the law Darkei Moshe brought from Tur's words is almost not in the category of books at all; perhaps he meant what he wrote that they are not fit for kiddushin — meaning they are forbidden to betroth them in any case; and this is clear prohibition of action; and proof from siman 13 they forbade betrothal because they needed to forbid marriage; and likewise secondary relations; and likewise chalal even though Torah prohibition — one can say they do not need strengthening; and though Ritva says betrothal of a chalal and Rambam chapter 17 Laws of Forbidden Relations regarding chalal — kiddushin have no practical difference for lashes but prohibition of action in the act that brings to intercourse; and likewise Gittin 40 implies; and he did an issur — learn even in betrothal there is prohibition for he only married betrothal by document though this prohibition is not stated in "taking" but in "she shall not be a kedeshah"; and for Rambam it is not Torah at all (but from Rashi it appears regarding a slave since kiddushin do not take effect certainly no prohibition in betrothal — therefore he explained he marries his maidservant; per this one can say what they enacted writing a document, etc. — meaning he married her through this betrothal).
