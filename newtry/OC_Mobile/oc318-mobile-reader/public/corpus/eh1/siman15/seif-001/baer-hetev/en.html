Choshen Mishpat was uncertain regarding rabbinic forbidden relation — if one betrothed and afterward another betrothed her — does she need a get from the second; also regarding those bound by negative commandments where we say kiddushin take effect with them — if a second betrothed her does she need a get rabbinically from the second. And Beit Shmuel wrote on this: I did not see there uncertainty in his words but that he holds plainly kiddushin of both take effect. And I say "I did not see" is not proof — it appears to the viewer in Choshen Mishpat printed alongside Tur that he wrote explicitly and requires examination: if one betrothed rabbinic prohibition and another came and betrothed her — does she need a get from the second since they are not kiddushin from the Torah at all — perhaps the rabbis were not stringent and requires examination, end quote — see there. He also wrote Choshen Mishpat: what he was uncertain about must be said specifically when she is not forbidden relation at all to the second; but if she was forbidden to the second as to the first — the second's kiddushin did not take effect; and likewise regarding those bound by negative commandments the law is so — see there. And Beit Shmuel disputes and rules even if she is forbidden relation to the second too — kiddushin take effect and she needs a get from the second too; and he brought proof from siman 42 where he found written that Beit Yosef brought — see there. And I say one does not bring proof from that matter — for there it is truly wondrous that she needs a get from both for in any case the second's kiddushin do not take effect — see there; and Kenesset HaGedolah similarly challenged in siman 42 in Hagahot Beit Yosef and wrote there that he asked his teacher this question; and likewise in responsum of the Rosh it was challenged and left requiring examination — see Kenesset HaGedolah folio 76a.
