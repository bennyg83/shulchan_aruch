Kiddushin take effect with them. Tur wrote "these who are not fit for kiddushin because of forbidden relation — some from Torah and some rabbinically"; and Beit Yosef challenged — those rabbinically are fit for kiddushin since kiddushin take effect with them; and Darkei Moshe answered: our rabbi was precise to say they are not fit for kiddushin — meaning kiddushin do not take effect with them like with others; for with rabbinic forbidden relation if one came and betrothed her she needs a get from him — behold they are not fit for kiddushin like other women as above, end quote. It appears if another came and betrothed rabbinic forbidden relation — even though from Torah betrothed to the first — nevertheless if another betrothed her the second's kiddushin take effect. And Choshen Mishpat wrote in name of Rambam he was uncertain in this law and I did not see uncertainty there — rather plainly kiddushin of both take effect. Also Choshen Mishpat wrote specifically when she is not forbidden relation at all to the second; but if forbidden to second like first — second's kiddushin did not take effect. However in siman 42 Beit Yosef brought: if one betrothed before disqualified testimony rabbinically and another betrothed before disqualified testimony rabbinically — kiddushin of both take effect; and Beit Yosef ruled there — from this it appears likewise if two betrothed rabbinic forbidden relation — kiddushin of both take effect as long as first's kiddushin not completed even rabbinically second's take effect. And Taz brought proof from siman 44: if one betrothed those bound by negative commandments — behold full kiddushin — implies no further kiddushin of another take effect; however certainly another's kiddushin take effect — otherwise contradicts Beit Yosef siman 42 above.
