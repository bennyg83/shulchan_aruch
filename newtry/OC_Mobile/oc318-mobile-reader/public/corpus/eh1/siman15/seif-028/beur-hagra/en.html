His wife went, etc. We are uncertain perhaps per R' Ami; and per R' Ami no distinction his wife betrothed or married — always disqualifies through others disqualifies through himself; and even though married wife no concern he divorced or condition — nevertheless concern lest brother-in-law comes before his wife's coming; and as Rashi there s.v. perhaps per R' Ami; and likewise Rosh there; and no permission for R' Ami except married on witnesses per R' Shimon; but we do not hold like R' Shimon as siman preceding 91b; and always disqualifies through others likewise always through himself; and per R' Yitzchak Nafcha no practical difference except seif preceding we hold like R' Akiva — per R' Ami whoever does not disqualify through others does not disqualify through himself even his betrothed wife; and first tanna R' Akiva here we hold like R' Yehudah per R' Yitzchak Nafcha; but in this siman we rule on all like R' Ami as Ra'avad wrote two witnesses permitted to marry his wife whether per R' Ami or R' Yitzchak Nafcha; and Ramban answered: we do not hold like R' Shimon marrying on court — certainly Ra'avad knew this; rather holds though we are stringent on brother-in-law what could he do — nevertheless not stringent on his wife against him; and likewise Nimukei Yosef; and learned from Rif who wrote since not clarified, etc. — implies we hold both stringently; and if so no stringency in Ri'az's words; but Ramban and Rosh were careful and wrote practical difference per R' Yitzchak Nafcha if wife's sister available as siman preceding; and Ra'avad there therefore concern for divorce, etc., and we are concerned, etc. Likutei.
