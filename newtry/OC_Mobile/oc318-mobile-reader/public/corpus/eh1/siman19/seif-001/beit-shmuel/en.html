We judge based on presumption. Rambam wrote proof for this from what the Torah judged one who curses his father and one who strikes his father — "he shall be put to death" — and we do not know that this is his father except through presumption. And apparently it is difficult: why doesn't the Talmud at the beginning of Hullin derive from this that we follow presumption? And in siman 149 I brought a dispute: if he married her — several poskim hold it is as if we saw him have relations with her. And apparently it implies Rambam does not hold so; for if so, whence does he bring proof from one who curses or strikes his father — perhaps he married her before us? From this we know he is her husband, and then we say majority of cohabitations are from the husband; but we do not say presumption. And Mahariik in a responsum siman 87 divides these laws and wrote: regarding lashes we follow presumption without proof; but regarding capital cases we require proof with presumption — such as that they conduct as man with wife, or a son clings after her. And apparently this does not appear so from Rambam and Tur.
