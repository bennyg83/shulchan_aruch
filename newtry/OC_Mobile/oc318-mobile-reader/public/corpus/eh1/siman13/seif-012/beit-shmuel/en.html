And he removes with a get. I already wrote for the Rambam the fleeing helps and he need not divorce; and what the Mechaber wrote "unless he fled and removes with a get" — meaning if he fled he need not remove; and if he married a pregnant woman and she miscarried — see siman 164.
And he writes her another ketubah. Beit Yosef wrote: even if he did not pay her ketubah — nevertheless he must write her another ketubah because it is a document whose lien was forgiven; and this means we say presumptively when she did not claim the ketubah at the time of divorce — then was the time of collection — presumptively she forgave the ketubah; but if she said of her own will to return/hold the ketubah until he returns her — he need not write another ketubah — and so in Chelkat Mechokek.
He married and fled. In Chelkat Mechokek he wrote: ab initio he did not act properly, for he fled and did not remove her; and that which is written above in the hagahah that we teach him to flee — specifically if he betrothed, then we teach him to flee; but if he married — he did not act properly, for he fled and did not remove.
And fled. And this fleeing should distance wandering until he cannot return within twenty-four months.
And if he betrothed. Rivash wrote there: for the Rambam we place him under nidui as with betrothal within three months of waiting; and from his words it is implied specifically regarding betrothal — for then he does not divorce her, then we place him under nidui; but if he married and divorces we do not place him under nidui since he nullified his act; if so the same if he betrothed within three months — if he divorces we do not place him under nidui, even though I wrote above if bet din did not coerce him until after three months we place him under nidui — that is different, for there his act is not nullified; however for what Tosafot and Rosh wrote that waiting is more severe — there is no proof for Rivash's words; and one may say here we do not place him under nidui even if he married her — he must divorce her; and there even if he married her he need not divorce her — different reason because it is a long time; but regarding prohibition there is more severity.
We do not coerce. So Rambam, Tosafot, and other poskim in the name of Mahari of Orleans and Or Zarua, as written in Terumat HaDeshen siman 216; and uncertain whether one may rely on these poskim regarding a kohen; and in Darkhei Moshe it is implied here too one may rely on those who are lenient; and if he married unintentionally — explained above there is room to permit regarding unintentional — so too here; and see in Terumat HaDeshen there.
