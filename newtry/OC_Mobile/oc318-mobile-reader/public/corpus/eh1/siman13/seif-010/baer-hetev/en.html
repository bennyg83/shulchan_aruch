We place him under nidui. See Beit Shmuel; and if she prostituted we punish him only for the prostitution and not for not having waited ninety days — Rivash siman 111.
Unintentionally. Specifically he did not know her husband died within three months, or that she was pregnant; and so is the law; but if he knew the prohibition and erred in another matter — it is like intentional — Terumat HaDeshen, Beit Shmuel. What was not mentioned — nidui regarding a woman, for she too transgressed the rabbinic decree; because this nidui is light and a woman is closer to being unintentional, and in such nidui any unintentional act is exempt — Ranach siman 10 in ch. 1. And if he is believed to say I was unintentional — it seems he is believed; and the proof from the type of first chapter Bava Metzia: believed to say I was intentional — in his name in Tosafot; and from this that he is believed to say I was unintentional — see Kenesset HaGedolah in hagahah on the Tur seif 12.
Regarding a kohen, etc. In Darkhei Moshe he wrote: even if he betrothed intentionally one may rely on the Rambam that he need not divorce; and regarding an Israelite one may be lenient regarding unintentional; and what is written here one may rely on the words of those who are lenient above — even if he betrothed intentionally; and what is written here regarding an Israelite one should not be lenient — is a scribal error; and it should read: one may rely, etc. — Beit Shmuel; not like Chelkat Mechokek — in his name. And in all this there is no distinction whether he betrothed or married — Taz; and in all these laws there is no distinction between a minor and an adult; even a minor not fit to bear children — the law is likewise — Beit Shmuel.
In his alley. And so is the law in all rabbinic prohibitions except a captive, where the Sages were lenient.
He betrothed and fled. And if he married and fled — per the Rambam, the fleeing is to atone for the prohibition he committed and the fleeing helps. And for other poskim who hold fleeing is because he reveals his intent that he does not wish to marry — if so, if he married her the fleeing does not help; and some say it helps — Beit Shmuel. An incident came before me: one betrothed within the months of waiting and I decreed upon him that he flee; and he said he could not flee for many reasons; and I instructed that the woman flee — and with this it suffices — Kenesset HaGedolah in his name.
