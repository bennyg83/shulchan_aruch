We place him under nidui. So too the Rambam; and he holds he need not divorce, only nidui; and see below what is written: accordingly the same if bet din did not coerce him until three months passed — nevertheless we place him under nidui for the prohibited act — and so in Darkhei Moshe; but per the views of poskim who hold we place him under nidui so he will divorce her — if so after three months nidui is not needed; and it should be read per the Rambam: he need not distance until the measure of his return of three months, for this distance is only to atone for the prohibition he committed, as the Maggid wrote, and exile atones — if so distance is not needed so much; and the Mechaber brought only the Rambam's view that we place him under nidui for the prohibition he committed; he should not have brought the Rosh's view that distance is needed so he not return until after three months; accordingly if he married it is obvious we place him under nidui for the prohibition of marrying, even though it is not explicit in the Rambam — nevertheless we hear this per the explanation that we place him under nidui for the prohibition he committed; and what the Rambam wrote regarding marriage — "we separate them" — comes to teach us not to say that since he already married we do not separate him; it teaches us therefore he wrote "we separate"; and he did not write nidui because we hear it from the law of betrothal; and so is implied in the Maggid and Nekudot Yosef that we place him under nidui per the Rambam's view if he married — and not like Bach; and if she prostituted we do not punish her except for prostitution and not for not having waited ninety days — Rivash siman 111.
If he betrothed unintentionally. Terumat HaDeshen wrote: specifically he did not know her husband died within three months or that she was pregnant; and so is the law; but if he knew the prohibition and erred whether the month of pregnancy is included — it is like intentional.
And it seems regarding a kohen, etc. In Darkhei Moshe he wrote: even if he betrothed intentionally one may rely on the Rambam that he need not divorce; and regarding an Israelite one may be lenient regarding unintentional; and what is written here one may rely on the words of those who are lenient above — even if he betrothed intentionally; accordingly he need not write what Chelkat Mechokek wrote if he is a Torah scholar, for even regarding complete intentional there is room to be lenient; and what is written here regarding an Israelite one should not be lenient, etc. — possibly it is a scribal error; and it should read: one may rely on what Darkhei Moshe wrote; and in all this there is no distinction whether he betrothed or married — Taz; and it is implied in all these laws there is no distinction between minor and adult; even a minor not fit to bear children — the law is likewise; and not like some who brought Nekudot Yosef.
If he divorced her — forbidden to dwell in his alley. And so is the law in all rabbinic prohibitions except a captive where the Sages were lenient; and see siman 119.
He betrothed and fled. And if he married and fled — per the Rambam, fleeing is to atone for the prohibition he committed and the fleeing helps; and so the Maggid; and for other poskim who hold fleeing is because he reveals his intent that he does not wish to marry — if so if he married her the fleeing does not help; and some say it helps, as written in Nekudot Yosef and the Maggid ch. 1.
