And regarding what is written there: when she weaned him in her husband's lifetime — if her husband divorced and weaned him — it seems lighter since there are those who say a divorcee is permitted to marry; and perhaps the reason that weaning in her husband's lifetime helps is because he was silent and waived for her the obligation upon her to nurse; and perhaps this is not relevant regarding a divorcee — what he was silent perhaps from this there is no decision that he waived, for he is distant from her; also we hold a divorcee does not have so much obligation; and in siman 82 nevertheless when he has nothing to sustain his son we hold there per several poskim she is obligated; and through this one may say we hold she is included as another's wet-nurse since in some respect there is obligation upon her; and through this one may say it is reduced from weaning willingly in her husband's lifetime — for while he weaned her he had no power to prevent her, for only when he has nothing to sustain his son is it incumbent upon her to nurse; and I have not examined well. And perhaps the reason in her husband's lifetime is far that she will scheme in order to marry after his death within twenty-four months; and regarding a divorcee in her husband's lifetime scheming also applies; and sometimes even when she was not yet divorced when she knew she would certainly be divorced — perhaps more severe; and when her husband converted perhaps also what he knew and waived for her that she wean — because her obligation to nurse is a right belonging to the child; and when her husband removed the yoke — the child's right belongs to him to inform her in siman 82: when the father has nothing to hire a wet-nurse — the divorcee is obligated to nurse; and perhaps the Sages forbade only another's wet-nurse when she is obligated to the father and not otherwise; and without distinguishing; and I have not examined well.
