To the hagahah: and specifically regarding intentional — this Or Zarua invented; Mahari Weil and Terumat HaDeshen brought it; and nowhere did he mention from where he learned to distinguish, since for the rabbis the reason for the get is not because of a fine but concern that separation is insufficient and lest he have relations with her as explained in the Rosh; and in my humble opinion per what I examined in Terumat HaDeshen's words that Chelkat Mechokek abbreviated: and possibly because of this we are not lenient in the other matter of Or Zarua if he married unintentionally to permit him with separation — and it refers to what he brought before that they do not practice like Or Zarua in what he explained: he betrothed and did not marry a wet-nurse — he does not need a get — he wrote: and possibly, etc., as above; and there is a question: and because in one the law is not like him — so in the other; and it seems he holds Or Zarua all infer to distinguish between unintentional and intentional — from what follows the view of Mahari of Orleans who learns from sotah: where an additional prohibition was added that we did not see from him that he intended to transgress — parallel to marrying a wet-nurse, kinuy, and setirah — he does not need a get; so too betrothal without marriage even though we saw he intended to marry her and he is suspected of this prohibition — nevertheless when marriage was prevented from him it suffices and he need not give a get; and we do not suspect him of the prohibition of relations without blessing; and from this he learned the same even if he married unintentionally — separation suffices and he need not give a get to fence; for even regarding this prohibition he was not made suspected regarding intentional; and if so Terumat HaDeshen properly wrote: and possibly since in this we do not practice like him per She'iltot — so here; and with this it is resolved for me what Maggid Mishneh challenged on Terumat HaDeshen: and he wrote it was hidden from him that the Rosh disputes Or Zarua since he strained to reject proofs of Mahari of Orleans from the chapter even though from arus and arusah Abaye made in his rulings and it was complete unintentional — and per the above it is satisfactory: since our laws these depended on each other — there is a question on the Rosh on Or Zarua: the Rosh since he disputes R' Yosi — plainly he does not distinguish between unintentional and intentional; not so Or Zarua who distinguishes between betrothal and marriage — all the more there is room to distinguish between unintentional and intentional. After I wrote as above an incident came before me responsum of the Gaon — and I found in it good pearls in the name of the Gaon Rabbi Meir son of Rabbi Neriah av beit din and rabbi of the community Frankfurt siman 25 who wrote it was hidden from the later authorities the words of Maharam in responsum Maimon siman 25 from the incident where a kohen married a wet-nurse, etc., that it was complete unintentional per the rulings of R' Yosi; and nevertheless Maharam concludes she needs a get (and it is clear per his words that what was done per a Torah scholar's ruling is considered complete unintentional as Maggid Mishneh also seized in the absolute — and not like Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 8 who equates unintentional per a Torah scholar's ruling with Terumat HaDeshen's unintentional where he erred in reasoning, for Terumat HaDeshen speaks only of a scholar who teaches himself per his reasoning; but per a Torah scholar's ruling there is no greater unintentional as proven in rulings and beginning of Or Zarua); and the Gaon author as a question arose for him to equate the dispute between Maharam the Talmud and R' Yosi Or Zarua his teacher — strained to equate them; and in my humble opinion it cannot be and certainly he disputes; for what he wrote that what is said "and per R' Yosi they did so" — we can explain the return was per the majority after the marriage; only giving to the wet-nurse was before the marriage; and the body of giving and marriage they did without ruling; and after the marriage they informed R' Yosi and he ruled they should vow, etc.; and indeed this is somewhat strained; so I say: in my humble opinion even with strain it cannot be brought into the intent of the statement; and also it cannot be, for per R' Yosi's view there is remedy through return — there is a question on the rabbis who hold Yevamot 37b: if she married she removes specifically with a get; until we coerce to remove we did not give to the wet-nurse with return; and even if you strain it deals with one who does not remove thus — still there is a question: and what is asked regarding a kohen if they made him a remedy; and so here there is remedy with a wet-nurse; and shall we say because it is uncommon to invent a wet-nurse thus they make remedy for a kohen; rather in my humble opinion R' Yosi follows the Raavad's view that I brought seif 12: even the rabbis' reasons are because of a fine if he married in prohibition and did not give to the wet-nurse — not because of child regulations.
