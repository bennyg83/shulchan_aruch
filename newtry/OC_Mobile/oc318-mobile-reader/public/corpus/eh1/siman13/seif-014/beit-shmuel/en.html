For if she wishes she will not nurse, etc. Here in a widow too we find she is not obligated to nurse — when she is wealthy or where she has no sustenance; accordingly one may say a wealthy widow too is permitted to marry, for if she wishes she will not nurse; and so ruled in responsum Emunat Shmuel; and the foundation of his building is because he relies on the elder Rosh who holds a divorcee is permitted to marry because she is not obligated to nurse — so too if she is a wealthy widow; but in my humble opinion to be lenient — for the poskim did not write this permission except regarding a divorcee and not a widow; and therefore the reason is because a divorcee is always exempt from nursing — if so she is not included as nursing; but a widow who is sometimes obligated — then she is included as another's wet-nurse; and so is the intent of Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri who wrote a divorcee is not comparable to a widow who is obligated to nurse her son — her intent is as written that she is included as nursing; also a divorcee is different because her father lives, as poskim wrote — not so a widow; and at first glance one may bring from the Talmud: even a wealthy widow is forbidden, for in Bei Rish Geluta they permitted because they are famous and certainly the head of exile were wealthy — nevertheless because of wealth they are forbidden; and one may say it deals with a widow who is poor and comes to marry Rabban Gamliel — therefore the reason of Rabban Gamliel being famous is needed; for Rashi explained when there was in them at Rabban Gamliel a nursing widow — implies they were Rabban Gamliel's wives; and so Taz ruled to forbid; only in this he ruled to be lenient post factum if she married — one may rely on the elder Rosh.
And some say. Meaning the Rivash simanim 13 and 463; and from this law we do not hear the law of a divorcee; but the Mechaber infers so from Rivash's words; and at first glance it is difficult why he needs to infer so from Rivash's words — it should be heard so from Tosafot and Rosh and Mordechai, for they brought the elder Rosh's words who permits a divorcee; and his proof from the beraita that teaches: if she was divorced she is not obligated to nurse, and if he recognizes her she is obligated to nurse — implies the elder Rosh does not permit when he recognizes; and they challenged him — implies he holds even if he does not recognize she is forbidden; and possibly the elder Rosh's proof is from what the beraita teaches: a divorcee is sometimes obligated and sometimes exempt — from this they learned a divorcee is permitted to marry because she is not included as nursing and permitted to marry even when he recognizes; and Tosafot disagree regarding recognition — but if he did not recognize she is permitted; and so understood Mahari Minz; and in the Maggid it is also implied the elder Rosh permits even when he recognizes, for he brought the elder Rosh's view who permits and the Rashba forbids; and he wrote: if she was divorced — if he does not recognize she is permitted — implies the elder Rosh permits even when he recognizes; and so in Semag; however in Tosafot and Rosh it is implied he holds even if he does not recognize she is forbidden, for their proof is: since we learned "wet-nurse presumptively" — "widow presumptively" — "divorcee and widow forbidden" — even if he does not recognize — if so a divorcee is also forbidden; and possibly the Mechaber infers from Rivash: even if she did not begin to nurse — forbidden; and see Bach; and he ruled: a divorcee is permitted where the child does not recognize her and she does not wish to nurse; and Taz is stringent in every matter.
