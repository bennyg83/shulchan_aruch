perhaps they were concerned: if he enters her house regularly "they examined each other" will be done; and since betrothal and marriage are forbidden to him — it seemed to them to compare to a kohen who divorced from betrothal who is forbidden even to dwell with her in an alley (and see seif 10 in the hagahah and siman 11 seif 7, and Perach ch. 119; and it escaped him the Rosh in responsum klal 32 law 13 which is seif 7 of siman 11) — so too one should be stringent regarding this entering at least so "they examined each other" not be done; but if so there is a question for us, for unmarried women do not immerse and Torah study is forbidden; and this too is forbidden regarding a betrothed woman to enter her house (and perhaps it is different because marriage is permitted); and perhaps therefore Rama omitted the oath, for he holds Beit Yosef was not really stringent with an oath except in places where unmarried women immerse — not so for us who are sworn and stand from the prohibition of niddah on the majority, as is in Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah siman 183: it seems to him that a mere warning suffices; if so plainly the stringency of the oath does not seem to him, for we do not find the like; at any rate in my humble opinion the law is with Chelkat Mechokek that the same applies to pregnant and nursing women, for since marriage is forbidden to him per their words — entering is stringent in this; and not like responsum of the Gaon siman 19 who wondered much at Beit Yosef; and I do not know why he did not wonder at the law in seif 10 in the hagahah that Rivash forbade one who married another's wet-nurse and divorced her to dwell with her in an alley — let him challenge his own words, for regarding prostitution there is no concern to curdle her milk from turning over and prostituting, and all Rivash's reasons he brought — and what stringency is this over other unmarried women; or that Rivash learned to be stringent from a kohen who divorced, whose prohibition is also not in relations without kiddushin as explained in Rambam ch. 7 of Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah, and Chelkat Mechokek agreed in this detail; and Hagahot Maimoniot; and see siman 119 s.k. 15 in Beit Shmuel; and nevertheless since betrothal and marriage are forbidden they were stringent the same regarding a wet-nurse whom the Sages forbade betrothal and marriage — for all that the Sages enacted they enacted as at first; and from this Beit Yosef learned to be stringent regarding a betrothed woman; and his wonder is nothing in my humble opinion; and in the end he raised Beit Yosef's words that in my humble opinion; and in particular what he wrote: and all this applies to a betrothed woman to whom he said as she abandoned herself to him she also prostituted with others because she is designated to him; and this view was never heard in siman 4 except regarding arus and arusah or one designated in his house for prostitution — and that is a dispute; but betrothed man and betrothed woman who do not fear him from the Torah — and from this Chelkat Mechokek raised wonder; and in my humble opinion there is no question on him; and from his words it is implied in Beit Yosef from what he cited his words at the beginning of the siman — and if his intent was to distinguish between prohibition and prohibition he would have been silent; and what Be'er Heitev brings responsum of Shevut Yaakov regarding this stringency from siman 119 where he wrote there: but permitted to enter her house even though she married since he does not dwell there and is not secluded with her — in my humble opinion he did not permit entering except incidentally to carry and give with another who dwells with her or with her husband; and even with her husband — not so here: if entering is not forbidden to him he will regularly go to her house and be alone with her properly — they saw fit to forbid; however the oath — since Rama omitted it — one should not be stringent; and so Shevut Yaakov raised.
