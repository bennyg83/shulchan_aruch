Ninety days. The reason for waiting — from Shmuel we derive from a verse: "to be for you as God and for your seed after you" — to distinguish between the seed of the first and the seed of the second; and Rava said the reason is a decree lest he marry his sister, etc. And it seems the halakhah is like Rav; and one may also say even for Shmuel there is no biblical prohibition, and this derivation is only an asmakhta; and in the Talmud there are three months; and he wrote in Shulchan Gavoah that this means each full month which is thirty days.
Only that he may not cohabit. Beit Yosef wrote that the matchmaker should swear not to enter the betrothed woman's house until the time arrives, so they not come to mishap.
That we count from the day of giving the get. Meaning: if he sent via an agent who went — we count from the day the get reached her hand — there in the Talmud.
