One who betroths within ninety days — we place him under nidui — so Beit Yosef decided to rely here on the Rambam, not like Perush Rashi per responsum of the Ran; and there is a question: behold the Ran did not decide so except regarding pregnant and nursing, and in this many agree as is also in Tosafot; and somewhat implied likewise the Raavad's view since he did not challenge ch. 28; and this is the view of Mahari of Orleans; but within ninety days — from where that the Ran explains like Maharam against all disputants except Chelkat Mechokek; and one may say; and see Maggid Mishneh who challenged the Ran's words against each other even regarding pregnant; and examine. And behold after Beit Shmuel wrote in this law and seif 11 several matters that in my humble opinion I am compelled to elaborate somewhat: in the Gemara in this law we place him under nidui; and if he fled — fleeing helps; and in the law of seif 11 in the Gemara: if he married he removes with a get; and Rashi explained nidui is to divorce; and if he fled — therefore fleeing helps so as not to coerce him to divorce because he revealed his intent he does not wish to marry; and all commentators learned from him that if he married fleeing does not help and he is obligated to divorce specifically; and so in Rivash siman 396 and the Rambam who is the language of Shulchan Aruch explained Chelkat Mechokek who holds his law is not to divorce but nidui for transgressing the rabbinic decree; and if he fled exile atones and he is exempt; and Rivash adds he forced his explanation that if this law were to coerce removal they would not have said "if he fled fleeing helps"; we hear from this: if he married a pregnant woman — for all he is obligated to divorce — fleeing does not help for the Rambam to save him from coercion to divorce; and in this contradiction to Beit Shmuel s.k. 109 in his name; and in this law of seif 10 the Rambam further wrote: if he married within three months we separate them — and teaches us a novelty that this prohibition is astounding; and even if he married he need not give a get — and so in Rivash, not like Beit Shmuel s.k. 15 who wrote: comes to teach us not to say that since he already married we do not separate him; for from the Torah and because he had relations once the waiting was partially confused — these became pregnant he will eat more; and perhaps not yet pregnant; and see responsum Rosh klal 53 siman 7; but many disagree and hold if he married within three months he is obligated to remove with a get; and perhaps specifically regarding betrothal he explains like the Rambam per responsum of the Ran; and regarding marriage he omitted because of the dispute that even per the Rambam nidui is not to coerce divorce — nevertheless one may say even regarding betrothal; but regarding marriage he is obligated to divorce — parallel to pregnant woman; and in the law of seif 11 the Gemara did not mention nidui nor fleeing — only all commentators of the Rambam learned from explaining nidui; but to suffer on his own we hear that even if he married within three months even though he wrote only "we separate" — nevertheless he is liable for nidui for the bad act; and if so we also hear that even if he married a pregnant woman even if obligated to divorce — he is also liable for nidui or fleeing; except Beit Shmuel s.k. 33 wrote from Rivash's words it is implied even for the Rambam if he married and divorces we do not place under nidui since he nullified his act; and in my poverty I examined Rivash and I do not find a place with this implication except in what he answered because he was uncertain if there was only betrothal or also marriage to a wet-nurse; and the answer first: if he married — there is no one who says he need not remove with a get and we coerce with all coercions; and if he betrothed and did not marry — there is dispute for the Rambam we do not coerce; but it seems from his view we place under nidui; and it is implied to him from what he did not challenge the Rambam's "married" — even if he divorces he is liable for nidui; and he proved his reasoning; and this implication in my humble opinion is not certain — if this were his view to rule nidui's explanation like the Rambam he would truly have written so; but behold in the responsum if he betrothed he explains explicitly that in all he is obligated to divorce even regarding betrothal; and per Rashi's explanation it is obvious he need not write "or even if he removes with a get he is liable for nidui"; but for the Rambam he is always liable for nidui even when divorcing; and know that the Rambam — and he is Shulchan Aruch — "married and fled and afterward came and dwelt with his wife — it is of no consequence"; and why was he particular about fleeing — if you say as Beit Shmuel s.k. 28 wrote: the Rambam wrote "and if he fled he need not divorce"; and accordingly he also wrote s.k. 29: I already wrote for the Rambam the fleeing helps and he need not divorce — it seems he understood this fleeing works that he need not again remove with a get; and so is the plain implication of Chelkat Mechokek; but in my humble opinion it cannot be — for one question is very great: first per his explanation that fleeing stated regarding betrothal within three months was not stated to exempt from a get, for regarding betrothal in any case he needs a get; rather for atonement; from where does he learn regarding marriage that is explicit in the Gemara "he removes with a get" that fleeing saves from the get; and is it the Rambam's way to invent his law from his own mind; and further the essence of Beit Shmuel's implication stems from Rivash; and in Rivash it is explained he learned from the Rambam that he forced his explanation that regarding betrothal he need not give a get — otherwise fleeing would not help; and how could he hold that because of this he was particular here about fleeing to save from the get; and further that in this manner since the time already passed from the Torah for the Rambam who has no rabbinic fine at all — the essence of the get is only for child regulations as written nearby in the name of the Rashba that from taste alone in miscarriage even if he did not divorce she is permitted to him; and the same after nursing time; rather the clear matter is that the particularity about this fleeing is in what he concluded "it is of no consequence" — meaning he need not nidui because he was atoned by fleeing; and if he returned within the time he is certainly fit to be coerced for the get; and likewise if he fled and it was in bet din's power — nevertheless to coerce for the get within the time; possibly in the present that he fled and his place was unknown — and in this the matter was delayed until the time passed; and he wrote "it is of no consequence" that he was exempt from nidui; and all the more from the get since the time passed — and how will he divorce and tomorrow take her and it be rabbinic matters as mockery; and though I know Chelkat Mechokek's implication tends to Beit Shmuel's intent — nevertheless it seems Shulchan Aruch teaches my intent when he wrote seif 12: he transgressed and married — we place him under nidui unless he fled and he removes with a get — meaning the get mentioned in the Gemara; and nidui learned from the Rambam's explanation that it is for punishment a fortiori from betrothal unless he fled and is saved from nidui through exile; and nevertheless he removes with a get — and not like Beit Shmuel s.k. 29 who reversed Shulchan Aruch's intent; and per Chelkat Mechokek's explanation on the Rambam's language "married and fled," etc. — but our master holds in every case fleeing helps — this is certainly Beit Shmuel's intent; and all the above questions; and therefore it seems Shulchan Aruch was unwilling in the chapter of Chelkat Mechokek and his explanation as above.
