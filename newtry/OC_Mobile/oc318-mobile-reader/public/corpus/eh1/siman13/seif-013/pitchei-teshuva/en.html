Regarding a kohen, etc. Be'er Heitev until "and what is written here regarding an Israelite one should not be lenient — is a scribal error," etc.; and behold in Beit Shmuel here he wrote: possibly it is a scribal error, etc. — it is implied it is not clear to him; but in Beit Shmuel below s.k. 34 it is implied he accepts plainly; and see in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 34 who wrote: so it is correct for practical law (that regarding an Israelite one may rely on Or Zarua who is lenient regarding unintentional at least) in such a matter whose entire foundation is a rabbinic fine — in his name; and see in responsum Noda BiYehudah s.s. 18 who brought Maharshal's words in Yesh Nohalin ch. HaCholeitz siman 4 who explicitly forbids even regarding unintentional to permit with separation — only she needs a get; and again he brought Maggid Mishneh ch. 1 of Hilchot Gerushin who proved the Rosh in the name of Mahari of Orleans disputes Or Zarua (who is lenient regarding unintentional with separation) — for otherwise how did he prove betrothal need not divorce from what we do not find Abaye in Ketubot 60 requiring his betrothed to divorce; and behold the betrothed was complete unintentional who acted per a Torah scholar's ruling (also in responsum Mishkenot Yaakov siman 6 he wrote this proof and concludes to be stringent to divorce specifically — in his name); and he wrote to reject this proof with good reason (see in Sefer Mar'ot HaTzov'ot siman 17 s.k. 136 in the hagahah that he too wrote to reject this proof). Nevertheless for practical law there is no compulsion from Noda BiYehudah's words there, for even though he was lenient in his case — one may say because the prohibition was not so clear in his eyes as explained there; and also from siman 12 there is no proof; and see there further in siman 25 and siman 150.
