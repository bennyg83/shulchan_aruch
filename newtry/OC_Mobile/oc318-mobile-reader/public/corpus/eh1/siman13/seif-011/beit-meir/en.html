And the truth: I too, poor in understanding, always went with this thought until from it I received in a responsum a question on Shulchan Gavoah ch. HaCholeitz who permits a nursing divorcee even if he recognizes her to marry like a gentile woman; and I wrote that in my humble opinion even a gentile woman who nursed until recognition is forbidden to marry; and proof from Rabbenu Nissim who permitted in the house of Rabban Gamliel; and it is clear as Kenesset Yisrael wrote: for an unmarried woman they hired — and are the house of Rabban Gamliel robbers; and behold we hold in Chelkat Mechokek siman 353 seif 7: but if there are workers to hire, etc.; and all the more a worker who can return even in half a day; or that in this manner where there is another there is no concern except concern there will not be found another or she will retract after recognition — this country is not permitted to retract — there is no greater lost matter; but regarding others nevertheless there is concern within the time of coercion danger — not so in the house of Rabban Gamliel; and if it were that she is permitted to marry still even the house of Rabban Gamliel did not release that she nurse until recognition and again marry; or that even a gentile woman after recognition or without another being found she is delayed from marrying; so there; and however now I found in responsum of the Gaon siman 25 a challenge in the name of the Gaon Rabbi Meir son of Rabbi Neriah av beit din of Frankfurt on responsum of Maharam in responsum Maimon letter siman 25: what helps that she gave to a wet-nurse — behold she was married; and in the Gemara when they said "they gave to a wet-nurse" — meaning unmarried; and behold this challenge on the entire principle emerges against R' Yosi who permitted and all the disputants there; and in particular all Ashkenaz scholars were the disputants as explained there and in responsum of the Rosh; and there is none who would sense such a plain matter; and in particular Maharam who established the foundation of the prohibition because she has a husband; and there is room to permit the oath with strain — in his name; and also R' Yosi who returned and argued because he is a kohen there is room to rely on the beraita "she gave to a wet-nurse — permitted to marry"; and Maharam disagrees also with strain; and per the above there is no leniency from the beraita that deals specifically with unmarried; and see above; and I gave to my heart: behold he spoke only; and certainly the view of the rishonim from me is elevated until I found it should be understood: possibly the law is with them; for in any case uncertain what we learned regarding a divorcee: and if he recognizes her we coerce her and nurse for wages; and so in Shulchan Aruch siman 82; and there is a question: behold we conclude in the sugya the husband may have relations with his nursing wife — if she becomes pregnant he will feed him eggs — implies feeding eggs suffices even if he nursed until recognition; if so what is the necessity to coerce the divorcee — he will feed him eggs; and what does he care about child care; and truth one may say per taste Rambam ch. 1 of Hilchot Gerushin explained feeding eggs are medicines for the mother so her milk not curdle — and it fits; however for all our rabbis except him — certainly there is a question; and perforce they hold specifically a woman with her husband suffices for the child — feeding eggs even if he recognizes because she is in any case with her mother who recognizes and his longing for her; and immediately the feeding stops at the nursing place — not so a recognizing divorcee — how shall we act; the father will give egg-feeding wages to the mother — there is no remedy for the child for sometimes the money will end or be lost immediately or from the third parties — if he gives weekly there is concern a woman is ashamed to come to bet din and in particular with her divorcer; and plainly if he does not recognize even if there is no other found she can say what do I care for your child's care — take him and feed him eggs (and with this it seems in my humble opinion what is said siman 82 in the hagahah: but if he has no — meaning even money for feeding eggs; but if he has — what of it that he does not remove the wet-nurse and feed him eggs unless we are concerned per the Rambam's explanation); not so regarding recognition — presumptively an infant who recognizes upon separation from his wet-nurse has longing for her and there is danger; therefore we coerce her; and further one may infer in the beraita that permitted through a wet-nurse; and so Rabbenu Nissim who permitted in the house of Rabban Gamliel because there is no concern she will retract; and there is a question: perforce we must hold the explanation that a woman is ashamed to come to bet din; and though we are not concerned lest she perhaps retract — that is if she achieves what was fixed in her wages — nevertheless there is concern perhaps the one obligated for nursing wages will not give what was fixed for her; and a woman is ashamed to come to bet din and the breast will slip from her mouth and danger; or specifically if the infant is one she is ashamed to claim sustenance for her son as Rashi explained; but a gentile woman has no shame to claim from the obligator whoever he may be; and if so I do not find Rambam's explanation that feeding eggs are medicines so her milk not curdle — plainly regarding a gentile woman there is no concern if she has a husband that if she needs medicines she will claim from the obligator; but even per all commentators' explanation one may also say there is no concern that if her milk curdles and the child recognizes — this too is included since he recognizes and the infant's longing for her that she is obligated in any case to feed him eggs and eggs suffice for him as with a married woman; and shame — behold she has no claim; and with this the view of the rishonim rises, for certainly per their view is the implication of presumptive wet-nurse "she has a husband," for unmarried it is not common that she stands to nurse.
