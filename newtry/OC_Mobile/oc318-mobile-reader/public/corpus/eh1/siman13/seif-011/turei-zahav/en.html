Another's pregnant woman. The reason in the Gemara regarding nursing: lest she become pregnant and her milk be lost and kill the child through hunger; plainly regarding another woman they say she will eat eggs and milk her husband gives her; not so here where the infant's father died and she will be ashamed to claim from his heirs; and the reason of another's pregnant woman they say in the Gemara: presumptively pregnant will nurse — meaning she will afterward nurse; and there is a question: if so why did the tanna teach wet-nurse; and one may say the reason teaches us: why is pregnant forbidden — because she will nurse; and if so the reverse question: why teach pregnant — certainly everyone knows she will eventually nurse; and one may say: there is a woman who was already nursing and established as nursing — she may not marry; but pregnant — perhaps they did not concern for nursing afterward, for perhaps she will not nurse him at all; it teaches us presumptively pregnant stands to nurse even though not yet established — as above.
And an intercalary month counts toward the number, etc. This is the view of Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 11 of Hilchot Gerushin, brought by Terumat HaDeshen, siman 216. His reason is that the Talmud says twenty-four months for the child and does not say two years, which shows that in a leap year twenty-four months also suffice. The "some say" is the view of Maharam in Mordechai, chapter HaCholetz, and in Hagahot Mordechai there. Terumat HaDeshen records a case where Reuven married another's nursing woman after twenty-four months, counting the intercalary month as one of them. A sage wished to separate him, as the Talmud says one who marries another's pregnant or nursing woman must divorce her. Mordechai wrote in the name of Maharam that if the year is intercalated, it is intercalated for the child. Another great authority permitted after the fact based on Hagahot Maimoniyot, not like Maharam. Terumat HaDeshen notes that Maharam appears inconsistent, for regarding mourning he infers from "twelve months" and not "a year" that in a leap year twelve months suffice, and that is the custom, while here he is stringent. He answers from the Mishnah in Mi SheAchzo, which speaks of nursing for two years, and the baraita in Af Al Pi, which speaks of twenty-four months: both expressions are used stringently, "two years" excluding the intercalary month and "twenty-four months" excluding the possibility that one day in a year counts as a year. In my view, Hagahot Maimoniyot's words are correct and Maharam's words are very difficult. Where the Talmud says twenty-four months, it is precise: do not subtract from it and do not add to it. The Talmud explicitly says she must wait twenty-four months, excluding the day of birth and the day of betrothal, but it does not add "excluding the intercalary month"; this shows that the intercalary month is like any other month. Furthermore, if the intercalary month were added to the nursing prohibition, the same should apply to the child's nursing period in the baraita, which would lead to the strange result that according to R. Eliezer a child may nurse twenty-five months in a leap year, and according to R. Yehoshua one who separated after twenty-four months in a leap year could resume nursing, even though the baraita states simply that after twenty-four months the law changes. Therefore the language of twenty-four months is exact in all cases. Although we cannot, God forbid, dismiss Maharam, I write my understanding because it is Torah and I must learn. Since there is in any event a dispute among early authorities, and the matter is rabbinic, we rule leniently in a dispute. In a practical case where the child completed twenty-four months on Rosh Chodesh of the second Adar, I ruled that even Terumat HaDeshen would agree she may marry, for he was stringent only where the intercalary month was counted within the twenty-four months; where twenty-four months passed before the intercalary month, there is no reason to add another month. In any case where there is some other basis for leniency, even if insufficient by itself, it combines with the view that the intercalary month counts. This is especially so because the lenient view of Hagahot Maimoniyot best fits the sugya.
And he hired a wet-nurse for him. In the Tur the version is "or he hired"; and even with this version it would be "and he hired" like "or he hired" — for if per Beit Yosef's explanation "and he hired" refers to what precedes — we need two matters; why afterward "in her husband's lifetime" since they already wrote so — Maharach; and accordingly if he hired a wet-nurse alone it suffices even near her husband's death; and if so there is a question: what is written afterward — a different division: or she gave to a wet-nurse three months, etc.; and in the Tur truly this is not the division at all; rather there is a question: behold Beit Yosef wrote the Rosh in responsum was astonished at what he saw in the Geonim's words this division of three months before her husband's death; and on this the Rosh wrote: it seems they knew that within three months milk can return to the woman and not afterward; and so is his conclusion for practical law; also Rivash wrote in the name of responsum of the Ran this law of three months — therefore it seems primary as Beit Yosef wrote regarding the law of milk stopping for the child: not sufficient except specifically after three months; and that the Tur did not mention three months — because it is not a fixed matter; for Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rivash that what the Gaon wrote three months — he holds the majority's milk stops after three months; but there are those who stop after one or two months; and if so we explain the Tur's words: what is written "or her milk stopped" — meaning the known measure for her milk stopping; or "she hired a wet-nurse for him in her husband's lifetime" — meaning even though her milk did not stop but of good will she hired him a wet-nurse in her husband's lifetime as the poskim's measure for milk stopping; and per the version "and she hired" it also seems not sufficient if she hired less than three months or measure of milk stopping — only a question what is missing and she is not his wet-nurse entirely even a small amount but she hired him a wet-nurse — and from this the child's life; and this is in any case as the measure of her milk stopping; and one may not challenge this version "and she hired" — for the Tur cited in explanation in bet haba it seems his intent: if he did not write "in her husband's lifetime" only afterward "and she hired" — this means after her husband's death her milk stopped and before her husband's death she hired him a wet-nurse even a short time — it teaches us we require it stopped in her husband's lifetime — i.e., the poskim's measure; and in any case three months suffice for all women.
