Another's pregnant woman. In the Talmud the reason is lest she become pregnant and kill the fetus; and this reason was rejected and the conclusion arose that the reason is: a pregnant woman presumptively will nurse, and lest she become pregnant during nursing days and not have milk to nurse her son; and so wrote the Rif and Rosh; the Rambam wrote the reason is lest she become pregnant — and it is wondrous how he wrote the reason that was rejected; and in many places and in Perush Mahara Shatin they also challenged this; and see nearby; and per the Talmud's view it may be said at first glance: a woman who has dried breasts is permitted even if pregnant, for the reason "presumptively pregnant will nurse" does not apply; but from Tosafot it is heard even in such a case she is forbidden, for they wrote in the name of the elder Rosh that a divorcee nursing is permitted because she is not obligated to nurse; and Tosafot disagree and wrote: like the first reason — because she may become pregnant — a pregnant divorcee is forbidden; if so a nursing divorcee is also forbidden, for it is taught in one Bava: pregnant and nursing like pregnant divorcee forbidden — so too nursing divorcee forbidden; even though this reason was rejected — nevertheless even though he retracted from the reason — nevertheless he did not retract from what is forbidden per the first reason — forbidden also per the second reason; therefore a nursing divorcee is forbidden — so Tosafot; accordingly we also say per the first reason: pregnant with dried breasts is forbidden also per the second reason; and they challenged: if so every woman with dried breasts would be forbidden until twenty-four months, for pregnant and nursing is taught in one Bava; and per the first reason pregnant with dried breasts is forbidden — if so nursing is also forbidden — if so even per the conclusion she is forbidden; and why is a woman with dried breasts permitted? And one may say: since nursing is taught in one Bava with pregnant — if so if she has dried breasts she is not included as nursing; accordingly it may be said this is the Rambam's intent when he wrote the reason "lest she become pregnant" — because what is forbidden per the reason "lest she become pregnant" is forbidden even per the conclusion; and see what the Maggid wrote ch. 11 hagahah 3: a nursing divorcee is forbidden to marry because of pregnant — "presumptively nursing is taught" — his intent is also what Tosafot wrote.
Another's wet-nurse. Lest she become pregnant and her milk curdle; even if he is a saris — forbidden, for we do not distinguish; and so in Chelkat Mechokek; and so too in shidduchim it is forbidden as within three months of waiting.

And he hired a wet-nurse for him. So is it in the Rosh; and Beit Yosef wrote it refers above to dried breasts or milk stopped and she hired a wet-nurse; and so Maharshal; and so in some versions of the Tur; and see Perishah and Bach who agree with the version "or he hired a wet-nurse"; at first glance it is proven that hiring a wet-nurse alone does not help, for in responsum of the Rosh he brought Semak's view that permits if she gave to a wet-nurse three months in her husband's lifetime and did not nurse at all within three months; and the Rosh questions the permission; and how did he rule that hiring a wet-nurse helps without fixing any time; and I saw in responsum Maharam of Padua siman 30 he explains the version "or he hired a wet-nurse" — not to say when her milk stopped because she hired a wet-nurse it is worse; it teaches us; but we require her milk stopped and she hired; and so is Taz's view; and so it is proven: if she has dried breasts or milk stopped per the child with a wet-nurse — if she hired a wet-nurse alone she is permitted; why need he mention dried breasts; and it is strained to distinguish between hiring a wet-nurse for wages and a wet-nurse for free.
Or she gave, etc. So Semag and responsum of the Rosh questions this; and responsum of the Ran and other poskim agreed with Semag; and per Beit Yosef what the Rosh wrote in his rulings — milk stopped she is permitted to marry — meaning milk stopped for some reason even one day and she gave the child to a wet-nurse; and the law of Semag deals when the milk did not stop but she gave to a wet-nurse — then she needs three months, which is the measure by which milk usually stops after three months, as written in responsum of the Ran; and so Lvush and hagahah of Perishah and Chelkat Mechokek; and nearby explained: if she gave birth after her husband's death and does not wish to nurse the child at all — nevertheless forbidden to marry — as responsum of Rivash; and see Shulchan Gavoah ch. Af Al Pi and responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 55.
