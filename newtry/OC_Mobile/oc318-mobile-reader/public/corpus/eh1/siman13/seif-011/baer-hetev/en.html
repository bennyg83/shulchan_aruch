Another's. Even if she has dried breasts; not so regarding another's wet-nurse — if she has dried breasts she is permitted — Beit Shmuel in his name; and see Sefer Devar Shmuel siman 82, Maharash siman 11, responsum Edut Bihosef siman 39, and responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 simanim 95–96. A widow who had three children and was pregnant, and her cousin a widower is a matchmaker and has many who wish to betroth with him — but only her husband before death called his wife and the widower and commanded they marry each other because he arranged his children since they are relatives and he was rich; and now the widower wishes to marry her — but since she must wait twenty-four months until her nursing days end he cannot fulfill the deceased's will to wait so long because he has children and their care is extensive. If there is a remedy to marry her after her birth days and that she not begin to nurse the child at all, only hire one or two wet-nurses and make them swear not to retract at all — in responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 1 siman 95 he elaborated on this law and raised that there is room to permit, specifically after her birth days — in his name; and in responsum Kenesset Yechzekel question 59 he disagrees — see s.k. 18. See responsum Maharam siman 10, responsum Emunat Shmuel siman 4, Tzemach Tzedek siman 55, and Beit Yaakov siman 147.
Another's. Even if he is a saris — we do not distinguish. And so too in shidduchim it is forbidden as within three months of waiting.
The month of pregnancy. Taz wrote: if twenty-four months were completed on Rosh Chodesh Adar Sheni — she need not wait more, since twenty-four months were completed before the month of pregnancy; and in the Gemara they say twenty-four months and do not mention a year unless the month of pregnancy is within the months — then they are stringent. He also wrote: wherever there is room to be lenient from another side, even though it is not sufficient — nevertheless the lenient views combine regarding the month of pregnancy that she need not wait. And post factum if she did not wait the month of pregnancy — we do not place him under nidui nor separate them — Beit Shmuel.
Whether she is a divorcee. See Tosafot Yevamot 42a s.v. stam me'uberet; and see Beit Shmuel s.k. 20, responsum Kenesset Yechzekel question 59, and Kenesset HaGedolah in hagahah on Beit Yosef seif 44; and examine carefully.
Regarding a zona. See responsum Mahari Segal siman 110, Mahari Weil siman 73, Maharam of Trani vol. 1 siman 292, Mahari Karo in responsum Even HaEzer siman 42, Tzemach Tzedek simanim 55 and 104, and responsum Rashdam Even HaEzer siman 137. And if he and she admit she prostituted with him and from him she became pregnant — we are not concerned lest she prostituted with another and there is no concern to curdle her milk — see responsum Avodat HaGershuni siman 1, responsum Maharam Alashkar siman 12, responsum Panim Me'irot siman 70, and responsum Kenesset Yechzekel question 73.
To a wet-nurse. A man died and commanded his pregnant wife that when she gives birth she hire her son to a wet-nurse and take a husband; and some rabbis wished to permit because he waived. In responsum Kenesset Yechzekel question 59 he ruled to forbid — in his name; and see above s.k. 13 from Beit Shmuel in the name of Shevut Yaakov. A woman whose son died and she took another's son to nurse — may marry, since she is not his mother — responsum Mekor Baruch siman 5; and see Perach Mordechai vol. 1 siman 94 and the Rosh siman 11.
Even if she swore. And the same applies to collateral — Beit Shmuel. A pregnant woman who became a widow and took two wet-nurses to nurse the child — nevertheless she may not marry within twenty-four months — Maharam vol. 2 siman 10.
And some say if the wet-nurse swore. And if she gave collateral — uncertain whether it helps post factum if he married — Beit Shmuel.
Dried breasts. A woman whom doctors decreed her milk is poisonous and it was established thus because the first three children she nursed died; and experience proved likewise: since she gave her son to a wet-nurse he lived; and while pregnant she was widowed, gave birth, and gave the child to a wet-nurse per doctors' decree — Chakham Tzvi ruled in his responsum question 64–65 she is permitted to marry within twenty-four months — in his name; and so too in responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 1 siman 96.
Her husband. See Kenesset HaGedolah in hagahah on the Tur seif 20.
Her husband. One matter: her milk stopped and he hired a wet-nurse — Beit Shmuel.
To a wet-nurse three months. Meaning: the milk did not stop but she gave to a wet-nurse — then she needs three months, which is the measure by which milk usually stops after three months; not so if it stopped for some reason — it helps if she gave her son to a wet-nurse even one day — Beit Shmuel, Chelkat Mechokek — in his name. A wet-nurse who did not begin to nurse and gave birth after her husband's death — nevertheless forbidden to marry — Beit Shmuel; see above s.k. 13 and s.k. 18 from Beit Shmuel.
Her husband. And the same applies to three months before divorce — Rama in his responsum siman 121.
