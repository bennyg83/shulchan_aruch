(ב) And it is not proper to do so. Maggid Mishneh and Ran wrote that it is stated in the Sifrei: if she was ill, he must wait for her until she is healed. Raavad and Rashba wrote that the Mishnah here, which teaches that he may say, "Here is your get and your ketubah; heal yourself," speaks when she is not lying on a sickbed, while the Sifrei speaks when she is lying on a sickbed. But Rambam, Rosh, and Tur do not hold of this distinction between lying on a sickbed and not lying on a bed; rather, they hold that the Sifrei's derivation is only an asmachta. Maharshal wrote that nowadays, even with a prolonged illness, even if she is not lying on a sickbed, he cannot divorce her against her will. However, one can infer from the Mishnah and from the poskim, who wrote plainly that he may say, "Here is your get and your ketubah; heal yourself," that even nowadays this is so where he is permitted to divorce her.
