(א) Medical treatment with a fixed cost. Rashi explained: where she is not constantly ill. The same applies if she is constantly sick but the doctor wants to take a fixed sum and treat her at every time and whenever she needs; that too is called medical treatment with a fixed cost. The Rosh wrote: medical treatment without a fixed cost is included in sustenance, and when it has a fixed cost it is included in redemption, because it too comes all at once. Therefore the orphans are exempt from medical treatment with a fixed cost just as they are exempt from redeeming her. According to this, at first glance it appears that the husband cannot say, here is your ketubah, heal yourself, for treatment with a fixed cost. Tosafot wrote that the reason he may say heal yourself is that medical treatment is included in sustenance, which corresponds to her handiwork, and she has already received sustenance in exchange for her handiwork until today. But redemption corresponds to the produce he consumed, and she has not yet received anything in exchange for the produce; therefore he cannot say redeem yourself. If so, treatment with a fixed cost also corresponds to the produce, and since she has not yet received anything, he cannot say heal yourself. According to this, one can explain the Tur's statement that if the husband saw that her illness was long, he may say here is your ketubah and heal yourself. At first glance it is difficult: why did he write that the illness was long? According to what I wrote, it is correct, because when the illness is long, presumably the treatment has no fixed cost and is included in sustenance; therefore he may say heal yourself. But for medical treatment with a fixed cost he cannot say heal yourself.
