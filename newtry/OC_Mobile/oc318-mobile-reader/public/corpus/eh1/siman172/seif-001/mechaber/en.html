Someone "saris chamah" (explanation: he became impotent through a disease of fever, Aruch), and an "ayalonit" (explanation: she has a male nature, which means she cannot become pregnant, and "ayalonit" comes from "ayil" which is the male sheep [i.e. ram]), they do not have to do chalitzah and yibbum. Therefore, someone who is a definite ayalonit who does chalitzah [even though she didn't have to] is not invalid to a kohen. And similarly if someone who is impotent because if fever who does chalitzah, or [his relative when he dies] does chalitzah to his wife, it is nothing. {Rama: If it is [only] a possible saris, he does do chalitzah. And we do not assume for a definite saris, unless they see when he was born that he had one of the signs of impotence, for example they sprinkle water but does not make a covering. Buit without this, we suspect the damage happened after birth, and he must do chalitzah (Rivash Siman 474). A definite ayalonit's rival wife is permitted to her yibbum [despite the fact that the ayalonit is not].}
