(א) And he shall bring it to her. In the Gemara Rava asked Rav Nachman: if one said, "Write it and give it to the agent," and it was lost before they gave it to her, what is the law? Have they removed themselves, because we say they have performed their agency, or was he concerned only for their exertion? Rashi explains that even here, until the get reaches her hand and she is divorced, he made them agents to write, but he was concerned not to trouble them with the delivery. Ravina said to Rav Ashi: if he said, "and he shall bring it to her," what is the law? Rashi explains: if you say that where he said only "write and give it to the agent," and they wrote and gave it to him, then if it was lost they have performed their agency, for they completed all that he commanded, what is the law here? Do we say that since he said, "write and give it to the agent and he shall bring it to her," he made them agents to write again until it reaches her hand, or is there no difference? The question remains unresolved. The Tur writes that if he told the scribe, "write and give it to the agent," and it was lost from the agent, certainly he made them agents only to bring it to the agent. Even if he said "and he shall bring it," we do not say he made them agents until it is brought; once they gave it to the agent they performed their agency, and they may not write another get until they ask him. If they wrote another and she was divorced with it, she is doubtfully divorced. But if they wrote an invalid get and gave it to the agent, they certainly may write another. His words show that regarding Rava's question he rules that it certainly is not a get, while regarding Ravina's question he rules that it is doubtful. This is difficult: why should we not also treat the first case as doubtful? Beit Yosef wrote that the Tur's statement that it is doubtful also refers to the first case.
Words of the glossator: Beit Shmuel, s.k. 7, wrote that according to what he wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 346, that the Rosh did not rule according to the geonim, he would not need this. Taz wrote that the Tur holds that in the first question they certainly performed their agency, and that Ravina's question of "and he shall bring it" means that Rava's question was specifically in a case where that phrase was said. In practice, however, even where he did not say "and he shall bring it," it is also a doubt. The glossator notes that Taz had already anticipated the point about Choshen Mishpat, and he strengthens the words of his grandfather, Taz: the precision is that the Gemara does not say "Ravina asked Rav Ashi," but rather that Ravina said to Rav Ashi, indicating that he was clarifying Rava's original question. Even so, one might explain that Ravina was asking what Rava would hold if "and he shall bring it" had been said. Therefore the issue remains one of analysis, but for halachah there is strong ground to treat even the first case as doubtful.
(ב) And the get is found void, etc. This is Rambam's language, and Beit Yosef explains that if it was lost, they have performed their agency, since it was valid. In my humble opinion this is not so. If the phrase "and he shall bring it" has power to extend the agency until it reaches her hand, then the same should apply where it was lost, since it was still within the time of the agency. Therefore this too should be doubtful where he said "and he shall bring it." Rambam did not specifically use "void" or "invalid" because the Gemara there discusses that case, where the name was written incorrectly; but for the practical law the same principle applies. The dispute with Rambam is only where he said "and he shall bring it" and the get was found invalid: according to the Tur they may write another, and this is the "some say" brought by Rama.
