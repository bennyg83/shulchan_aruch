(א) From today if I die. Because the phrase if I die implies two meanings: it can imply from now, and it can imply after death. Therefore, if he said from today, it is like one who said from now; if he did not say from today, it is like one who said after death. In siman 148 the dispute is explained: whether his intention is that the Get take effect immediately, in which case even if the Get no longer exists at the time of death she is divorced, because the divorce takes effect immediately. This is the opinion of Rif and Rambam, chapter 9 law 19. But according to the Rosh, the Tur, and the Maggid Mishneh, for Rabbi Yose it is uncertain whether his intention is to delay the Get as much as possible, meaning that the condition begins now but the Get takes effect one hour before his death; the Get must therefore exist at that time, and if it does not exist the Maggid Mishneh ruled there that she must leave. Darkei Moshe in siman 267 briefly cited the view of Mordekhai and wrote regarding it: see siman 143, for many poskim disagree and hold that anyone who says from now, even if the Get was lost or burned, the Get is valid. At first glance, it appears that one should not compare them. Certainly, a healthy man who divorces with a condition from now begins the divorce immediately according to all opinions. But with a shekhiv mera, they hold that his intention is to delay the Get as much as possible. Regarding seclusion with her: according to Rif's approach, if he secludes himself before witnesses there is concern for kiddushin, lest he die and the Get take effect retroactively immediately, and perhaps he betrothed her at that time; without witnesses there is a prohibition of seclusion. But a stale Get is not relevant here either way, for the Get takes effect immediately when he dies, and if he does not die she is his wife. According to the Rosh's approach, there is concern for a stale Get, because the Get has not yet taken effect. I already wrote in the preceding siman that we are not concerned here that perhaps both of them will agree to nullify the Get, since the condition is fulfilled against their will and is not in their control; it is not appropriate to say that it is as though it was not fulfilled. See Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9 law 9.
