(ב) From this illness. At first glance it implies that if he said simply, "if I die," no assessment is needed. So wrote Ramban and Ri"v, and they wrote that even if he died from another illness she is divorced, since he did not fully recover to the state he was in before he became ill; even if he walked without a cane, it is a get. But if he fully recovered from the illness as he was before becoming ill, the get is void, even if he later became ill and died. In the following seif the Mechaber brought the words of Rashba and the Ran, which imply that one who says "if I die" means "from this illness"; therefore, if he recovered and afterward became ill and died, it is not a get. It appears that they do not disagree with Ramban except where he recovered as he was before illness; then the get is void even though he later became ill and died, because we say that "if I die" meant from this illness. But if he did not recover as he was before illness, even though he walked in the marketplace without a cane and then became ill from another illness, she is divorced, unless he explicitly stipulated "if I die from this illness." In that case, even if he did not recover as he was before illness, we require assessment whether he died from the first illness. But from Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 8, halakhah 7, it is clear that Rif and Rambam do not hold so; rather, even if he said simply "if I die," we require assessment whether he died from the first illness, and the sugya applies even where he said simply "if I die." At first glance this makes difficult what the Mechaber wrote in seif 9 as good counsel, etc., for the concern still remains, since he holds that even when one says simply "if I die," assessment is required whether he died from that illness.
