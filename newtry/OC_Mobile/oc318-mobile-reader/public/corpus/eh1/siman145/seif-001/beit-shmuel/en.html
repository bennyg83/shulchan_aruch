(א) It is not a get. Do not ask why we do not say that the condition is void and the get takes effect immediately, since he did not double the condition; if so, the act stands and the condition is void. One may say that "if I die" is like saying "after death," and it is as though he fixes a time, as written in the following siman, where no double condition is needed. Shmuel's enactment of a doubled condition for "if I die" is as Maggid Mishneh wrote: when he doubles the condition, it is recognizable that his intent is not to divorce after death, but rather he divorces immediately and it is only conditional on whether he dies. This is unlike one who says plainly "if I die," where we say that his intent is to divorce after his death, and it is fixing a time, so no doubled condition is needed.
