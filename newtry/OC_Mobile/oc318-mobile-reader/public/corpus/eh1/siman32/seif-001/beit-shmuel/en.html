On pottery. It is something that can be forged; they write on it when handing it before witnesses of transfer specifically. And Ran wrote specifically when witnesses do not sign on it; but if witnesses signed on it we are concerned he may rely on the signature — see more on this siman 124.
And he gives it to her before witnesses. Below siman 133 regarding a get — if witnesses signed on the get and handed it between them it is valid, and some say invalid; and this dispute applies here also in kiddushin — Chelkat Mechokek.
For the sake of the woman. See Shakh at beginning of 425 that he must write the name of his city and the name of her city as in a get; and a fortiori "behold you are betrothed to me" — rather he says "this is for your betrothal."
Doubtfully betrothed. Without this she is doubtfully betrothed on account of the paper as written nearby; only the difference is if he stipulated that he does not betroth with paper — so too Chelkat Mechokek; also a difference in this doubt whether the child is a doubtful mamzer; and in the concern that perhaps the paper is worth a perutah — it is only a rabbinic concern.
