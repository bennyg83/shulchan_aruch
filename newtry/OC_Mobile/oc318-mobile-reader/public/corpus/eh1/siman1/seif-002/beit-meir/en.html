But before thirteen he should not marry, for it is like prostitution — Beit Yosef explained: because the Sages did not enact marriage for a minor; therefore Beit Shmuel brings the view of Tosafot and Rosh who wrote that it is not prostitution since through kiddushin she is bound to him. This language — "since through kiddushin she is bound to him" — is said only according to Rosh chapter 2 of Kiddushin; but Tosafot in chapter haNisrafim and chapter haIsha Rabba wrote in this language "nevertheless Rabbi Yose says there is no prohibition, for it is not intercourse of prostitution." And one could say their reason: they follow Ramban in siman 26 in Beit Shmuel seif 2, that for a designated woman there is no prohibition at all even without kiddushin; and marriage of a minor is no worse than a designated woman. (And according to the flow of the argument there is proof that at least for a pilegesh there is no Torah prohibition, from what they enacted marriage for a minor orphan girl; and even according to Rambam that anything without kiddushin has a positive prohibition of "when he takes," etc. — according to his words even for a designated woman, i.e. pilegesh, this prohibition applies — how can they enact for him to transgress a positive commandment, even a positive commandment? We conclude in Yevamot chapter 9 specifically for "sit and do not act"; but for Rambam one must interpret that marriage of a minor is considered a matter in which the Sages have power to uproot even a Torah matter, as Tosafot conclude there in the passage beginning "since," etc. — see their words. And one opinion of Tosafot in the passage beginning "they immerse.") And one could also say the intent of Tosafot is as Beit Shmuel understood: "since nevertheless," etc. However, in chapter haBa al Yevimto 62b they explained their intent is not according to this, for they wrote "nevertheless for this reason they enacted marriage for him, that his intercourse not be prostitution" — it appears from their language that specifically since through kiddushin and marriage she is bound to him, because of the mitzvah to marry near her maturity they needed to enact this rabbinic marriage so that his intercourse not be prostitution. And necessarily they hold there is prohibition at least for pilegesh; and it also appears from their words that before near her maturity, i.e. the year before majority, in this there is no mitzvah and there is also prohibition because of "lest she become pregnant"; and from the Torah they would enact marriage for him — if so, besides the prohibition of "lest she become pregnant," prostitution also applies. And a practical difference: one who marries a minor Kutite — because of "lest she become pregnant" there is no concern since she too is saturated; but because of prostitution there is concern. And even though we establish that a minor who eats nevelah — bet din is not commanded to separate him — nevertheless regarding those who endeavor, the prohibition applies not to marry him off. And behold, according to Tosafot in chapter haNisrafim and chapter haIsha Rabba one could say the default follows their explanation in chapter haBa al Yevimto, and not as Beit Shmuel's wording implies; but in Rosh one cannot explain it this way — "for this reason they enacted marriage for a minor that his intercourse not be prostitution" — for if so, how does he derive from this marriage of a minor during his father's lifetime? Even though the Torah did not give the Sages power over marriage for the matter of refusal, nevertheless he is not obligated to separate them and it is not prostitution. Or Rosh's plain intent is that without the Sages' enactment it would not be prostitution; nevertheless, from his precision "since through kiddushin," etc., we learn he holds that anything without kiddushin is forbidden even for pilegesh. And this first chapter of Kiddushin that Tosafot brought — Beit Shmuel wrote accordingly that it is possible to fulfill peru u-revu without kiddushin through pilegesh; and necessarily one must interpret according to Ramban's words in Beit Shmuel above on the difficulty from the verse "a bride without blessing is forbidden," etc. — see Tosafot there. And one could say similarly that even though he holds pilegesh is permitted, nevertheless regarding a minor and minor girl marrying for acquisition it is inferior and relevant is what the Torah said — in this manner there must be kiddushin and blessing; and kiddushin for a minor and minor girl is prostitution. And necessarily it is not prostitution since through kiddushin, etc. If so, one could say he holds specifically an adult pilegesh fit to bear children whose intent is to fulfill peru u-revu is permitted; but a minor and minor girl who are not fit for peru u-revu except through kiddushin — they are prostitution. And it is clear according to the aforementioned Ramban in Beit Shmuel that even Rambam among those who permit pilegesh, and also according to the later authorities there on the Tur that they too hold thus. Here, according to Beit Yosef's explanation of Rambam and Tur, that before thirteen years and one day it is prostitution because the Torah did not give the Sages power over marriage — therefore one must also interpret in one of the two manners above. And nevertheless Beit Shmuel's difficulty remains, that the Tur would contradict his father's view. And in my view there is a greater difficulty: he should have explained that it is forbidden to marry him off before thirteen because of "lest she become pregnant," regarding which everyone agrees. (Because it is more than a year before majority.) And further difficulty according to Beit Yosef's explanation: the prohibition emerges from the fact that the Sages did not enact marriage for him; and if so, one must explain what they answer "near her maturity" — "this is different," i.e. after thirteen years and one day, and not as one might think while he is still a minor. If so, the questioner — what is the case of kiddushin for him on Rav's statement that applies only to minor and adult? One could answer that the mitzvah is for minor and minor girl. He should rather have asked from the braita that it is prostitution, and answer that "near her maturity" means after thirteen years and one day; and we could say the questioner too understood the braita as after thirteen years and one day; nevertheless he thought "lest she become pregnant" applies — this is strained. And further, in any case there is difficulty on Rav who said because of "lest she become pregnant," and the verse that it is prostitution. And further great difficulty: if so, even after thirteen years and one day he should require examination for marriage by hairs, for kiddushin depends on hairs as explained in Darkei Moshe siman 43 — how is it a mitzvah near her maturity even after thirteen? And necessarily we rely on Rava's presumption in Niddah. However, in Darkei Moshe he asked on Beit Yosef: from where did he see to explain Rambam's language unlike Rashi? In my view, prostitution mentioned in their words is strained — they hold unlike Rashi; and further, since they hold prostitution applies to a minor boy, there cannot be mitzvah in the year before majority — as Tosafot's difficulty; therefore they explained according to the Tur that there is mitzvah only after thirteen years and one day, when we rely on Rava's presumption. However, from Hagahot Maimoniyot it appears he explains Rambam literally from Rav Yehuda's reason "mar bar Rav," for he cited the word "there" upon it. Therefore it seems to me Hagahot Maimoniyot holds that not because marriage does not apply is it prostitution — in this it is possible he holds either like Tosafot or like Rosh or Ramban regarding pilegesh; but rather Rambam disagrees with Rashi who explains the prohibition because she is thirsty and lest she fornicate. And Rambam could ask on this explanation: if so, what is the refutation "perhaps it speaks of a minor girl"? And further, "he shall not marry a woman" implies even a minor girl. And further, if there is concern she will fornicate even near her maturity, which is a year — there is concern; therefore he explains the reason of the prohibition that he accustoms the evil inclination upon his son — i.e. he arouses the thirst that acts so one who is sated becomes thirsty; and even "he shall not give his daughter to an old man" — certainly the reason is as Rashi explained; nevertheless regarding a woman to a minor, which implies even a minor girl, he explains from that reason. And it is also possible for a minor there is no concern "lest she fornicate" because she is designated for a man; and everything is by way of asmakhta on the chapter "lest she become pregnant"; therefore Rambam divided the two halakhot because of the difference in reasons. And according to Ramban's explanation on the Torah in this chapter, and upon this they answer "near her maturity — this is different," for then he reached the age of the evil inclination and there is mitzvah to prevent him from sinful thoughts. And for Rabbenu Tur it is reasonable Rambam's explanation; therefore his language is that it is like intercourse of prostitution in imagination — certainly actual prostitution does not apply, only because of the aforementioned reason; and also that this intercourse does not lead to a mitzvah matter, he calls it like prostitution. And even though Rambam used the Gemara's language, if the Tur's explanation is after thirteen years and one day like Beit Yosef, it is possible he saw Rambam's explanation to be stringent and explain "near her maturity" specifically after thirteen; and it is also possible like the later authorities — see and consider. And in my view this explanation is compelled from the summary of Piskei HaRosh who wrote: forbidden to marry a woman to his minor son; mitzvah to marry him when he is near becoming adult; and the prohibition of a minor was not mentioned in Rosh except because of "lest she become pregnant." And it is astonishing on Rif that he omitted this statement. The conclusion from Rambam and Tur's words: marriage of a minor, even to a minor girl, is prohibition; unlike Rashi's explanation. And according to Tosafot in chapter haBa al Yevimto, also before the year near her maturity it is prostitution; and so in responsum Rashba siman 483 that Beit Yosef brought siman 43 — even for minor boy and minor girl, and not as Beit Shmuel's wording implies; unlike Rosh's view. And it is proven also from Tosafot's language in chapter haBa al Yevimto that specifically for this matter the Sages enacted marriage for a minor near her maturity that his intercourse not be prostitution because of the mitzvah, and not for another matter to require a get; and so it is explained there accordingly in Mordechai (and siman 43). And unlike Bach; and in responsum Kenesset Yisrael Beit Yosef brought Beit HaEfodi decided like Tosafot from early generations, and people of Jerusalem who married off sons at eight — and I am astonished: if so, even for Tosafot there is difficulty, for they transgressed "lest she become pregnant." And if you say in early generations they were children of elders and this prohibition did not apply — we could also say to understand Beit Yosef according to Rambam's reason that then they were children of complete intellect and were like adults even for kiddushin. And see Tosafot Sanhedrin 69 in the passage beginning "it is known," etc. He also decided according to his explanation to distinguish regarding a father who marries off his son — he is permitted, and he seizes movable property for the ketubah; and his words are very well founded. This is clear in the Tur siman 67: even a minor whom his father married off — she has no ketubah until he matures; and from this mishnah is the main proof of Tosafot, because presumably the mishnah does not speak of prohibited marriage. And what he asked from prostitution because of absence of ketubah — he should have looked in Beit Yosef siman above: he brings this difficulty in the name of Nekudot HaKesef on marriage of a deaf-mute; and wrote: one could say there regarding intelligent people who are fit for ketubah — does it not appear to them as prostitution? But for deaf-mutes who are not fit to write, he was not particular; and even if she is intelligent — this applies also to a minor. And in our time see Beit Shmuel who concludes in the name of Mordechai and Agudah — therefore in any case we do not compel if he does not fulfill peru u-revu, even in Eretz Yisrael. And these words are not understood: what does Eretz Yisrael have to do with Tosafot's mitzvah of peru u-revu? And in Mordechai it is said regarding one who stayed with a woman ten years and did not bear — see and consider.
