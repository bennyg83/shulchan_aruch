Nor is it only a Sefer Torah — the same applies to other books, which may also not be sold except for these purposes, as stated in YD siman 270, and so too Beit Yosef. And Choshen Mishpat wrote that only for the mitzvah of peru u-revu may a Sefer Torah be sold; but to marry off an orphan girl, one may not sell — since she is not commanded in peru u-revu; only to marry off a male orphan may one sell on account of peru u-revu. And Beit Shmuel argued: but according to what is written below seif 40 — one sells a Sefer Torah to fulfill "in the evening do not withhold your hand" — similarly one could say to marry off an orphan girl too, selling a Sefer Torah to fulfill shevet yitzrah, since a woman too is commanded in shevet. However, from what is written at the end of siman 13 in the Gloss — "a woman should not remain without a husband on account of suspicion" — which comes from Rambam ch. 15 halacha 16 — it implies that she is not commanded in shevet. See the responsum of Radbaz who wrote that the woman is commanded in shevet. At first glance it is difficult: if one can say we sell a Sefer Torah to marry off an orphan girl in order to fulfill shevet yitzrah, then likewise we sell a Sefer Torah so that he himself should not remain without a wife, even after he has already fulfilled peru u-revu, as held by the "some say" in seif 8 — in that case Tosafot in Chagigah 2b, "this positive commandment is stronger," as said in Megillah "a man may sell a Sefer Torah, etc.," citing "He did not create it to be desolate, but formed it to be inhabited, etc." — proves nothing; and likewise in Gittin 41b, perhaps the Gemara brings the verse "He did not create it to be desolate, etc." to teach that even after fulfilling peru u-revu, and similarly for an orphan girl where peru u-revu is not relevant, one still sells for shevet yitzrah. After careful analysis, the question is not a difficulty. For the Tosafot who wrote "this positive commandment is stronger, etc." truly hold the first opinion in seif 8, as is explicit in Yevamot 61b, Tosafot in the passage "nafka mina, etc." And Beit Shmuel who wrote we sell a Sefer Torah to marry off an orphan girl to fulfill shevet yitzrah — wrote so according to the "some say" in seif 8. See and think carefully. And regarding whether there is a distinction between a privately-owned Sefer Torah and a communally-owned one, see YD siman 270 and OC siman 153.
