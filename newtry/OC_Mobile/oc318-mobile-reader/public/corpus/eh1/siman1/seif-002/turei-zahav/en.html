And included in marrying a woman is marrying off orphans with his money, because a woman is also commanded to fulfill shevet yetzera , so wrote Magen Avraham in Orach Chaim siman 156 seif 3, unlike Chelkas Mechokek. And other books are also forbidden to sell, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 74. And Beis Shmuel wrote: however, what Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 67 in the gloss in the name of Hagahot Asheri , "a woman shall not sit without a husband because of suspicion" , implies she is not commanded regarding shevet yetzera; and so appears in Tosafot beginning of Chagigah; also in Tosafot Gittin 41b and Tosafot Bava Basra 13b they did not write that a woman is commanded regarding shevet yetzera, but for one answer alone , end quote. We do not live by his mouth , he abbreviated where he should have expanded; how does it appear thus in the Tosafot he cited? And when we explain Tosafot's explanation, it will not be as his words; for also for the rest of Tosafot's answers it will not appear at all that a woman is not commanded regarding shevet yetzera. And Tosafot in Gittin there 41b in the passage beginning "He did not create it desolate but for shevet yetzera" , why does the verse of peru u-revu not cite? Because if he could fulfill shevet yetzera in any case we would not compel him because of peru u-revu; and here we compel him as I explained according to the flow of the argument , this is the explanation. On that page 41a Tosafot asked in the passage beginning "to marry a maidservant" , "if he can marry a maidservant, behold he does not fulfill peru u-revu, as proven in Yevamos 62 that a slave has no lineage." And one could say: if he could fulfill shevet yetzera in any case, because of the mitzvah of peru u-revu alone we would not compel his master, for he is not obligated in it since he is compelled , end quote. At first glance this is his explanation: specifically because he cannot fulfill both shevet yetzera and peru u-revu, and from the aspect of freedom in him he is obligated in both , therefore we compel his master. But if he could fulfill shevet yetzera we would not compel him because of peru u-revu, since he is compelled and not obligated in it. And so they answered here 41b: therefore the verse of peru u-revu cites , we do not compel his master for this to acknowledge; but from the aspect of servitude he is exempt from both. And so for their answer in another manner: or they cite this verse because it is a great mitzvah; and so in chapter 2 of Megillah regarding one who sells a Sefer Torah, etc. , its explanation is also from what is obligatory from the aspect of freedom in him; but from the aspect of servitude he is exempt also from sheves. And so understood Maharam of Lublin in tractate Gittin in the passage beginning "He did not create it desolate," etc. , see there. And since a slave is exempt also from sheves, a woman too is exempt, for every mitzvah a woman is obligated in, a slave is also obligated in them; and upon this Beis Shmuel also relied. And Levush wrote: Tosafot , a woman is not commanded regarding shevet yetzera; but accordingly Tosafot's words here contradict Tosafot beginning of Chagigah where they wrote a slave is commanded also regarding peru u-revu, only he does not fulfill peru u-revu because he has no lineage , see there. And here it appears even for shevet yetzera he is not obligated. Also Maharsha sensed this, but he only asked: from this Jerusalem Talmud that Tosafot inferred here that a slave is commanded also regarding peru u-revu; and in chapter haSholeach they inferred from that Jerusalem Talmud that it does not apply to a slave except for shevet yetzera. And according to our explanation there will be difficulty: why did they not raise a great difficulty from this , that in tractate haSholeach it appears even for shevet yetzera he is not commanded? And further, if we do not compel his master for peru u-revu alone because he is not obligated since he is compelled , we should also not compel him for shevet yetzera since he is compelled. Rather it appears his explanation is thus: a slave is commanded also regarding peru u-revu; only if he could fulfill shevet yetzera we would not compel his master because of peru u-revu since he is compelled; and the compulsion now does not come from half-freedom, for even before he could not fulfill peru u-revu since he has no lineage. And if we say here we compel his master so the slave will fulfill peru u-revu , we should compel all slaves to fulfill all mitzvot as Tosafot wrote in Bava Basra 13. But shevet yetzera he could fulfill before when he was a slave; and now because of his half-freedom cannot fulfill also shevet yetzera , therefore we compel his master; and therefore they also did not cite the verse of peru u-revu, because we do not compel his master because of peru u-revu, as written. And also for the second answer that cites this verse because the mitzvah is great , they also hold a slave is commanded regarding peru u-revu; and they did not cite the verse of shevet yetzera except because it is a great mitzvah. And Ribam's answer that Tosafot brought there , he is commanded only regarding shevet yetzera; and what Tosafot wrote "and so it appears somewhat in the Jerusalem Talmud" , they wish to infer from the Jerusalem Talmud according to Ribam's explanation specifically, and not like their prior answers. And in beginning of Chagigah they infer the opposite from this Jerusalem Talmud that he is obligated also regarding peru u-revu , i.e. not from what is explicit in the question is their precision, but from the question itself: the slave asks what he marries on the festival , it appears he establishes that a slave is not commanded regarding peru u-revu; therefore he asks what there is, etc. And according to the two answers above that a slave is also commanded regarding peru u-revu, it would not be a question at all; therefore they strained themselves also after if the question is resolved to say "all the more so he is commanded regarding peru u-revu" , this is because of shevet yetzera; therefore they wrote "and so it appears somewhat," i.e. somewhat it appears thus , according to the question, and not according to how the question is resolved; for according to how it is resolved it appears he is commanded also regarding peru u-revu, only they needed to interpret it refers to shevet yetzera according to Ribam's answer; but according to the prior answers it speaks even of peru u-revu itself , the slave is obligated without any strain at all. And similarly Tosafot inferred also in beginning of Chagigah; and thereby Maharsha's difficulty is resolved. Also what Maharsha asked there on what Tosafot wrote "and furthermore peru u-revu is written regarding all the children of Noah, even Canaan" , this too is astonishing, for the mitzvah of peru u-revu was stated for Israel and not for the children of Noah, as found in the chapter Arba Misos; and he left it undecided. It appears there is a practical difference: what is said there , every mitzvah stated for the children of Noah and not repeated at Sinai for Israel was stated for Israel and not for the children of Noah; and Rashi's explanation: until Sinai they were commanded in it; and since it was not taught again, we learn Israel who entered holiness stood in their prohibitions, but gentiles took from them , if so, a slave who enters holiness like an Israelite woman , whatever a woman is obligated in, a slave is obligated in them , therefore at least what they were commanded before the Torah, more than a woman , they certainly did not take from them since they too entered holiness; therefore slaves are obligated in peru u-revu even though a woman is exempt. And accordingly, also according to the two answers above we can say a woman is commanded regarding shevet yetzera, as Magen Avraham proves in siman 153; and it appears from the Gemara in Megillah where they say one sells a Sefer Torah to marry a woman because it is written "He did not create it desolate," etc. , why not bring the verse of peru u-revu? Rather certainly even for the woman one sells a Sefer Torah to fulfill shevet yetzera. And what Tosafot did not write also according to the two answers , the reason we do not compel his master for a half-maidservant that she too is commanded regarding shevet yetzera: because they were not compelled for this reason, for we can say she is not commanded at all regarding shevet yetzera even though a slave is obligated also regarding peru u-revu, as written. And what they do not bring in Megillah the verse of peru u-revu , because shevet yetzera is a great mitzvah; but according to Ribam's answer it is proven the maidservant too is obligated in shevet yetzera, for according to his view the slave is exempt from peru u-revu and obligated in shevet yetzera; and accordingly the woman too. And we are compelled to say we do not compel because she is not commanded regarding peru u-revu, etc. But in truth, also for the two answers we can say a woman is commanded regarding shevet yetzera; if so, also to marry off an orphan girl one sells a Sefer Torah.
