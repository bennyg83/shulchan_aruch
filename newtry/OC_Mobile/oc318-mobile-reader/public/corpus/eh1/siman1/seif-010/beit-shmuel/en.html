Rabbeinu Gershom imposed the cherem, etc. The reason is explained in the Hagahos Maimoniyos to Kesubos. The enactment was made because of quarrels, not because of any underlying prohibition. The Darkei Moshe wrote that a practical difference arises where there is a dispute concerning the enactment. One who rules leniently has not lost, since there is no concern of prohibition. He also wrote that after the end of the fifth millennium the original period had already expired, and from then onward it remained only a custom that people practiced stringently. One may not permit it in their presence. Therefore, in a case of doubt we establish the matter according to Torah law.
And likewise regarding a betrothed woman, etc. Meaning, just as Rabbeinu Gershom enacted nothing regarding a yevamah, and a man may perform yibbum even though he already has a wife, so too he enacted nothing regarding a betrothed woman. This means that if the man does not wish to marry her, we do not compel him to do so when he wishes to divorce her. If he does not wish to divorce her, however, we compel him either to marry her or to divorce her. The wording of the Rama appears to imply that no enactment was made regarding a betrothed woman and that he may therefore marry another woman. There is no basis for this implication in Maharik, and one must therefore explain it as I have written.
Such as when she became mentally incompetent. The Kol Bo wrote that Cherem Rabbeinu Gershom may be released only with the approval of one hundred men from three communities in three countries. The Darkei Moshe wrote that nowadays one need not obtain permission from one hundred men, since the period of Cherem Rabbeinu Gershom has already ended and it remains only a stringent custom. The Rama therefore grouped these two cases together, when she became mentally incompetent and when by law he is entitled to divorce her. This indicates that they are equivalent. When by law he is entitled to divorce her, Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact his decree, as stated in siman 115. The Bach, however, wrote that there is an accepted tradition from the great authorities that the matter should be presented before the leading sages of that generation, that one hundred rabbis should agree to the permission, and that he should deposit with the beis din the amount of her kesubah, her dowry, and additional kesubah. Only afterward may he marry another woman. The Bach further wrote in siman 119 that the husband should give the get to an agent for delivery, who should hold it until she recovers. He must designate a separate residence for her, protect her from abandonment and abuse, and provide her support. What the Bach wrote there in the name of the Maharshal contradicts what he wrote in this siman. If the husband cannot afford to deposit the kesubah, Responsa Tzemach Tzedek siman 67 wrote that there is a remedy. Her relatives may sell her kesubah rights to her husband for their present discounted value, and he must provide her support. After she recovers, he must divorce her immediately so that he not remain married to two women.
To divorce one of them. This means that he must divorce the second wife, unless the first willingly accepts a get. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 14.
He transfers acquisition of the get to her through another person. This is because of Cherem Rabbeinu Gershom. The Darkei Moshe gave another reason. Even without Cherem Rabbeinu Gershom, he must transfer the get to her, for there is concern that her co wife will enter yibbum while she herself is forbidden to enter yibbum because she is the co wife of a sotah. It is therefore a proper custom in places where yibbum is practiced. See siman 140.
