He enacted it on account of strife, not on account of a concern about a prohibition — and the practical difference is: where there is a dispute about the enactment, one who is lenient has not lost out. Also, now after the fifth millennium the time has already passed and henceforth it is only a custom that people practice stringently; therefore in a case of doubt we revert to Torah law (Darkei Moshe). The Kelalei Katan wrote in siman 38 that Rabbenu Gershom's enactment rests upon men, but a woman is agreeable with anything; therefore it is permitted for a second [Ashkenazic] woman to marry the one Sephardic man, and so ruled the Rav Uri Shraga — see there.
To marry. So too Maharik root 101. And see Maharik root 63 who wrote regarding a betrothed woman who claims "he is repugnant to me," etc., to permit him to divorce against his will or marry another — see there; and that responsum of Maharik the Rama ruled in accordance with him in siman 76 seif 2 in the gloss — see there; from which it appears that even for a betrothed woman he needs permission on grounds of rebellious wife, and he does not say that for a betrothed woman Rabbenu Gershom did not decree at all. And it seems to me that what they say — that for a betrothed woman Rabbenu Gershom did not impose the cherem — means specifically if he does not wish to consummate the marriage but only to release her; but if his intention is to consummate with her eventually, anyone who stands ready to consummate is considered as consummated; immediately the cherem applies to him not to marry another upon his wife even while she is still betrothed. Therefore in root 63, where he wishes to consummate with her when she is rebellious against him, therefore the cherem applies to him and he needs permission on grounds of rebellious wife. And this is the explanation in the gloss that writes on this "and similarly for a betrothed woman if he does not wish to consummate but only to release" — Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 52; and in the responsum of Chacham Tzvi question 124 he wrote that the Rama's explanation is what he wrote "if he does not wish to consummate but only to release" — that she should not err that if he has a betrothed woman he is permitted to marry another upon her even though he intends to consummate his betrothal; and on the grounds that the second is not forbidden since the first is not yet his wife; and also the first would be permitted to marry another after the second's marriage, since with permission she was betrothed to him — he comes to teach that it is forbidden. And in Beit Yosef he explained the Rama differently — see there. And see Beit Shmuel. And in the body of the law Chacham Tzvi in his responsum siman 124 disagrees and rules that for a betrothed woman too Rabbenu Gershom imposed the cherem — see there. And so too in the responsum of Meimonit siman 34 and Mahari Segal seif 1; but in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 112 and in Choshen Mishpat siman 52 he ruled like Maharik that for a betrothed woman he did not impose the cherem, and so too Meiri part 2 siman 209 and Maharshadam EH siman 78. A wealthy son betrothed one virgin — a Hebrew maidservant in his household — before witnesses, and afterward he regretted it; also his father and family protested against bringing her into marriage; and the virgin does not want to accept her get; and he wants to marry another — should he be prevented on account of the cherem? Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 112 ruled that he should not be prevented on account of the cherem, for for a betrothed woman he did not impose the cherem. And according to the words of Chacham Tzvi above who disagrees and rules that for a betrothed woman too Rabbenu Gershom imposed the cherem — he should be prevented. And Kenesset HaGedolah in the Gloss on Beit Yosef seif 59 raised that Maharik speaks when the first is in betrothal, but when the first is in marriage, even though the second is in betrothal, he violates the cherem — unlike Maharshadam EH siman 78 who holds that whenever the second is not in marriage but only in betrothal he does not violate the cherem — see there. And see the responsum of Beit Yosef siman 12 regarding laws of gittin, and Maharit EH siman 8, and Maran HaLevi siman 21: a minor for whom a yevamah fell, and while he was still a minor he became engaged to his match; and now he is thirteen years and one day old and has not brought two hairs; and the time of marriage has arrived; and the girl's father wants to marry his daughter to him as per the engagement conditions — should he be prevented from marrying his match on account of the cherem, even though it is written that regarding a yevamah he did not impose the cherem — that is specifically when he wishes to perform yibbum upon his already-married wife, but not when he wishes to marry upon his bound yevamah? Chacham Tzvi question 116 ruled that he is permitted to marry his match before performing chalitzah for the yevamah, and there is no issue of cherem here; and even according to the Rama who ruled that [the custom] applies in a case of mitzvah, and even in a case of yibbum one must perform chalitzah — that is, where he can perform chalitzah, but not in a case of compulsion such as here — see there. Who went insane. He wrote in the Kol Bo that the cherem should not be permitted except by one hundred men from three communities from three countries. And in Darkei Moshe he wrote that nowadays one need not permit through one hundred men, since the time of the cherem has already passed and it is only a custom practiced stringently. However, Bach wrote: there is a received tradition from the great ones of the world that the matter should be presented before the gedolim of that generation and they should agree upon the permission of one hundred rabbis; and he should also deposit the amount of the ketubbah, dowry, and additional ketubbah with the court — and then marry another. He also wrote in siman 119 that he should give the get through a messenger who carries it, so that the get is in his hand until she recovers; and he is obligated to provide her a separate private dwelling, protect her from licentiousness, and provide her maintenance. And his words there — what he wrote in the name of Rashal — contradict what he wrote in this siman — see there. And if he cannot afford to complete the ketubbah payment, the Tzemach Tzedek siman 66 wrote that there is a remedy: relatives may sell the ketubbah to the husband at a concessionary price, for an amount sufficient to provide her enough income for her maintenance and support — see there. And after she recovers he is obligated to divorce her immediately so that he not have two wives (Beit Shmuel).
To be stringent. For it was explained there that he cannot marry two women — as the cherem is not location-dependent but rests upon the man's person. And Rashakh part 2 siman 37 wrote that he is not obligated to observe the cherem except when he is married to a woman from the place he came from; but if he was not married to a woman from the place he came from, he may marry here in a place where the cherem is not practiced — two women. But Maharival part 3 siman 120 wrote that even if he did not marry in a place where the cherem was practiced, but married a woman in a place where the cherem is not practiced and wished to marry another upon her, it is forbidden since he is from a place where the cherem was practiced — and Kenesset HaGedolah ruled like Maharival as stated in the Gloss on Beit Yosef seif 2. One who goes from a place where the cherem was not practiced to a place where it is practiced cannot marry another woman upon his wife in the place where it is practiced. One who went up to Eretz Yisrael and his wife did not go up with him and wishes to marry another woman there — if he is concerned about the cherem, he sends her a get through a messenger and estimates the time it was possible for the get to reach her hands, and then marries another (Maharit part 1 siman 210). And Kenesset HaGedolah commented: I marvel — if he is concerned about the cherem regarding marrying another woman, why was he not equally concerned about the cherem of divorcing her against her will, and what solution did he find in sending her a get against her will? One could strain to say: regarding a get — at the moment of giving the get he violates the ban and nothing further; but to marry another woman — as long as she is bound to him he continuously violates the ban, and this falls under the principle of "arise and remove" [a continuing prohibition].
