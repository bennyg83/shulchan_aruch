And likewise regarding a betrothed woman. It appears that even if he betrothed a woman and does not wish to marry her, but rather intends to release her, he may marry another woman before giving the get. Maharik writes there that the ban of Rabbeinu Gershom applied only to marrying another woman in addition to one's married wife. If he has not yet married the first woman, who can compel him to write her a kesubah and obligate himself in all the other marital duties if she does not find favor in his eyes? It is obvious that we do not compel him. This proves that through the betrothal alone she is not yet his wife for this enactment, because Rabbeinu Gershom decreed only regarding a married wife. Nevertheless, afterward we compel him to release the betrothed woman, since she remains bound to him through the kiddushin. This is unlike Beis Shmuel, who was forced to explain the ruling differently.
However, there are those who disagree, etc. Darkei Moshe writes that where there is a dispute concerning the scope of an enactment, one who follows the lenient view need not be concerned. As I wrote above, the enactment was not made because of concern for a Torah prohibition; it is merely an enactment.
And where the first wife is incapable of receiving a divorce, etc. Bach writes: I saw that my teachers had received from leading earlier authorities that the matter should be presented before the leading sages of the generation. They should agree to this permission, and one hundred rabbis should sign that agreement. He must also deposit her kesubah, dowry, and the additional sum that he wrote for her in the hands of beis din, and only afterward may he marry another woman. We do not, however, permit him to divorce her against her will. In siman 119 Bach further writes that he should give a get to an agent for delivery, and it should remain in the agent's hands until she recovers. As long as she has not recovered, he is obligated to designate a separate residence for her, where she will be protected from licentious conduct, provide her sustenance, and fulfill all the conditions of her kesubah.
To divorce one of them. This means the second wife, unless the first wife agrees willingly to accept a get, as stated in the responsum of Maharshal.
And where there is no established custom, one need not be stringent. Since Rama elaborates and writes that he may marry another woman, even though this would seemingly be understood automatically, it appears that Rama intends to teach that only with respect to the enactment of Rabbeinu Gershom may he marry another woman in a place where there is no custom to observe it. There is, however, another concern that Rama records in Darkei Moshe. In a place where yibbum is practiced, one must be concerned that the husband may die without children, his wife will fall to yibbum, and she will be forbidden to undergo yibbum because she is the co-wife of an apostate woman. According to this concern, even where there is no established custom concerning Rabbeinu Gershom's enactment, one should be stringent if the community practices yibbum.
