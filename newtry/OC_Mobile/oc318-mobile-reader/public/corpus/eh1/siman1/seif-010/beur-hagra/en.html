But in the case of a yevamah, etc. Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact his decree where a mitzvah is involved.
And similarly regarding a betrothed woman, if, etc. He is not obligated to marry her, as stated in Kesubos 109a: "Either marry, etc." This is the meaning of, "if, etc., but rather, etc." See Be'er HaGolah.
However, some disagree, etc. As stated in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto 65a: he said, "I will go, etc." R. Ami said, etc. This shows that it is forbidden even where a mitzvah is involved. Sefer HaTerumah explains R. Ami's statement accordingly. Even for us, who rule like Rava, Rabbeinu Gershom's decree applies to us just as the earlier law applied to them according to R. Ami. This is the meaning of "even where a mitzvah is involved," referring to what was stated above. The same applies wherever there is, etc. This is R. Ami's teaching.
Even where yibbum is involved. Rabbeinu Gershom enacted the decree because of strife, and it therefore applies even in a case of yibbum, as explained above.
And he must perform chalitzah. This means that we compel him, as stated there on folio 111b regarding women who made vows during their husband's lifetime, etc. Although she caused the situation, since she did not intend this result, he is compelled. All the more so is that true here, where she did not cause it. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 10.
Nevertheless, etc. This is because of permitted matters that people accepted as forbidden, as stated in Darkei Moshe. This is the meaning of "and some say, etc." For that reason, one does not compel him.
And there is no, etc. As explained above in seif 9, and where the custom is, etc. She may object to his doing so, as stated below in siman 46, seif 8.
He grants possession, etc. As stated on folio 118b regarding one who grants a get on behalf of an adulterous wife, etc. In such a case she certainly prefers that she not violate the prohibition against a married woman, as stated in Kesubos 11a regarding a minor convert. One cannot reject this from what is stated there regarding a slave, and the same applies to a kohen, because of the principle of "stolen waters are sweet." We do not apply that principle here, as stated at the end of Nedarim.
