A deaf-mute or mentally incompetent person fulfills the mitzvah. Beit Yosef explains that this is because they are subject to mitzvos, and competent people are warned concerning them. He brings proof from the fact that their shechitah is valid. "They are warned" means that others may not feed them a prohibition directly, although beis din is certainly not commanded to separate them from a prohibition. One should not object that Rambam, in chapter 9 of Hilchos Edus, gives as the reason for disqualifying them from testimony that they are not subject to mitzvos. One may say that his intent is that they are not subject to punishment for transgressing mitzvos, and therefore they are disqualified. See Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh there regarding their difficulties. Perhaps this is his intent, for it is explained there that a deaf-mute and a mentally incompetent person are derived from a minor, and it is also explained there that a minor is excluded by a gezeirah shavah even when he is discerning and wise. Accordingly, if a deaf-mute or mentally incompetent person testified and we see from the orderly presentation of his words that he arranges the matters coherently, one might initially ask how this can be learned from the gezeirah shavah concerning a minor. Rambam therefore explains that the Gemara's repeated statement that these three are not people of understanding means that this is the rationale of the gezeirah shavah regarding a minor, even when he is discerning and wise. That is to say, they do not possess the complete legal understanding required to be subject to punishment, and this rationale applies to all three.
