It is a mitzvah for every man to marry at eighteen. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 15, writes that the mitzvah begins at the start of the eighteenth year, while Bach writes that it means a complete eighteen years. One who marries earlier at thirteen means after thirteen complete years, as Beis Yosef and Bach wrote. Bach further wrote that if the father marries off his son there is a mitzvah, kiddushin takes effect rabbinically, and she requires a rabbinic get. Beis Shmuel wrote that Bach's main proof is from siman 141, but it can be rejected as written there; Tosafot in chapter HaNisrafin also proves there is no such distinction, for Tosafot ask on the statement that close to maturity it is a mitzvah for a father to marry off his son from the sugya in Yevamos that the Rabbis did not institute marriage for a minor. According to Bach's distinction there is no question, since the mitzvah exists only when the father marries him off. I am very surprised at this objection. Beis Shmuel made what is secondary into Bach's main proof. Bach distinguishes between a minor who marries on his own initiative and one whom his father marries off because otherwise the sugya in HaNisrafin would not ask from "close to maturity" against "one who marries a woman to his minor son"; and because otherwise the Tur would rule against Rashi, Tosafot, Responsa Rashba, and Mordechai. Rambam too merely records the Gemara's language: the Sages commanded that a man marry off his sons and daughters close to maturity, and it is forbidden to marry a woman to a minor, for it is like promiscuity. Just as Rashi explained the Gemara, Rambam can be read the same way. Therefore Bach explains that when a minor takes a wife on his own before thirteen, it appears as though done for promiscuity and the Rabbis did not institute marriage for him; but when his father marries him off, the father is training him not to act promiscuously, and there is a mitzvah. When the Tur says that before thirteen one should not marry them off because it is like promiscuity, he does not mean actual promiscuous intercourse, but that it appears to be for promiscuity, as the Rosh wrote in HaIsh Mekadesh: although the Rabbis did not institute marriage for a minor, it is not promiscuity when it is by way of kiddushin and marriage. Thus even during the thirteenth year, if he marries on his own and appears to act for promiscuity, it is forbidden; when his father marries him off, that reason does not apply and there is even a mitzvah. If the intercourse of a minor's marriage were actual promiscuity because the Rabbis did not institute marriage, it would be forbidden even when his father marries him off. Tosafot in HaBa Al Yevimto and at the end of HaIshah Rabbah should be read accordingly: since it is not promiscuity, there can be a mitzvah for the father to marry off his son close to maturity specifically. This resolves Beis Shmuel's question from Tosafot in HaNisrafin. Tosafot asked because if the lack of rabbinic marriage made it promiscuity, it should be forbidden even when the father marries him off; Tosafot therefore explain that it is not truly promiscuous. According to this, there is room for Bach's distinction between the minor acting on his own and the father arranging the marriage. Accordingly, before thirteen complete years, when the minor marries on his own, the union appears like promiscuity. When his father marries him off during his thirteenth year, there is also a mitzvah, and the kiddushin requires a get by rabbinic law. Before the beginning of the thirteenth year, even when his father marries him off, it is considered like promiscuity. A minor yavam who has relations with his yevamah acquires her only by rabbinic law, as Tosafos write in Kiddushin 18 and Yevamos 68; Yevamos 36 likewise implies that the intercourse of a minor has the status of maamar performed by an adult.
It is permitted to remain unmarried. This applies only as long as his inclination does not overpower him to such an extent, as the Beis Yosef writes.
Even if he married a woman and remained with her for ten years. Beis Shmuel writes that if he wishes to divorce her against her will nowadays after remaining with her for ten years, it appears that Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact his decree in such a case; see siman 154. This is likewise implied by the Rivash, siman 15, who writes that the sages of the generations overlooked such marriages so as not to prevent them, and certainly did not separate the couple when both wished to remain together. This implies that when both do not wish it, the sages did not overlook the matter so as to refrain from preventing it. The same applies here.
