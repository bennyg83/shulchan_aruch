It is a mitzvah for every man to marry at eighteen. Although one becomes obligated in all mitzvos immediately upon reaching thirteen, the Sages received that eighteen is the age for marriage, since one must first study Torah before marrying, and the beginning of that course of study is from age fifteen onward. So writes the Chelkas Mechokek. The Maggid Mishneh wrote at the beginning of chapter 15, halacha 2, that the mitzvah is to marry at the beginning of his eighteenth year. One who marries earlier, at thirteen, performs the mitzvah in the choicest manner, meaning after completing thirteen full years. The Beit Yosef explained it this way, and this is also implied by the Rambam in chapter 21, that before becoming an adult it is forbidden to marry. Rashi and Tosafos, however, hold that marrying half a year or a year before adulthood is also a mitzvah. The Bach wrote that the ages eighteen and thirteen stated by the Tur refer to completed years. Before the beginning of the thirteenth year it is forbidden to marry. During the thirteenth year he distinguishes that if the father marries off his son, there is a mitzvah, the kiddushin take effect rabbinically, and she requires a get rabbinically. His principal proof is from siman 141, but it can be rejected as I wrote there. Tosafos in the chapter HaNisrafin, Sanhedrin folio 76, also prove that this distinction is incorrect. They challenge the statement there that shortly before maturity it is a mitzvah for a father to marry off his children from the Gemara in Yevamos, which states that the Sages did not institute marriage for a minor. According to the Bach's distinction, there would be no difficulty, since there is a mitzvah specifically when his father marries off the minor.
For it is like promiscuity. See Tosafos there and at the end of the chapter HaIshah Rabbah. The Rosh in chapter 2 of Kiddushin wrote that it is not considered promiscuity, since she is with him through kiddushin. This is also proven by Tosafos from a minor whom his father married off, for even before age thirteen there is no promiscuity. Tosafos wrote at the end of that chapter that although the Sages did not institute marriage for a minor, Rabbeinu Yitzchak nevertheless said that there is no prohibition, because the intercourse is not considered promiscuous, and there is even a mitzvah, as stated in HaNisrafin. At first glance this might imply that Tosafos discuss specifically one whose father marries him off during his thirteenth year, when there is a mitzvah, whereas before then it would be like promiscuity. The Bach understood it this way, but this is incorrect. Tosafos intend to prove that even where the Sages did not institute marriage, the intercourse is nevertheless not promiscuous. Their proof is that during the thirteenth year the Sages did not institute marriage, yet there is no promiscuity and there is even a mitzvah. It follows that even where the Sages did not institute marriage there is no promiscuity. The Drishah wrote likewise. The Tur's statement that before age thirteen it is like promiscuity is difficult, for it contradicts what the Rosh wrote. See the Perishah. A minor yavam who has relations with his yevamah acquires her only rabbinically, as Tosafos wrote in Kiddushin folio 19.
He may delay. So writes the Rambam, provided that his inclination does not overpower him. The Rosh wrote that we do not know the fixed limit for such study, for it is inconceivable that he should neglect peru u'revu his entire life. We find such conduct only regarding Ben Azzai, whose soul passionately desired Torah. The Rambam appears to hold that there is in fact no fixed limit. Ben Azzai, whose soul passionately desired Torah, was permitted to neglect peru u'revu even though he could have married without bearing the burden of providing support. This is what the Mechaber means in the following seif. Another person must marry when doing so will not impose a burden of support. If supporting a wife would be burdensome and his inclination does not overpower him, he may delay as long as his inclination does not overpower him, without a fixed limit.
In the present era the practice is not to compel. See the Mordechai and Agudah, who wrote that outside Eretz Yisrael we do not compel. It is also logical, for we have effectively decreed upon ourselves not to marry, yet no decree may be imposed upon the community unless most of the community can endure it. Therefore, in any event, we do not compel one who has not fulfilled peru u'revu, even in Eretz Yisrael. See the end of siman 24.
Even if he married a woman and remained with her for ten years. See Rivash siman 15, from which the Rama derived this ruling. See the Chelkas Mechokek, who wrote that the Rivash stated the opposite. The Rivash's intent there is that one who wishes to marry a barren woman is more logically subject to compulsion. According to the Rama, however, it appears more logical to compel even one who already married and remained with his wife for ten years. If, in the present era, he wishes to divorce her against her will after ten years, it appears that Rabbeinu Gershom did not issue his decree in such a case. See siman 154.
