For it is like promiscuity. See Baer Hetev in the name of Kenesset Yisrael who distinguishes whether his father marries him off, etc. And see in the responsum of Kenesset Yisrael there who explains according to this reasoning that Tosafot, Rashi, and Rosh do not disagree at all with Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Arukh; for in precision Tosafot and Rosh wrote that it is not considered intercourse of promiscuity — i.e., from the aspect of the intercourse itself; and there is also a mitzvah to marry off one's minor son — i.e., his father [doing so]. And the mishnah of "one who writes" also speaks of a minor whom his father married off; and so is the language of Rashi and Rosh; and so early generations and people of Jerusalem would marry off their sons. Unlike the language of Tur and Shulchan Arukh: "but before thirteen he should not marry" — meaning, he himself without his father — for it is like promiscuity, because it is impossible to write a ketubah [for a minor]; and Rambam's language can be explained accordingly. And he further wrote there that specifically if the father seizes movable property it is effective; but if the father gives a document on her ketubah it is nothing, lest the father die and not leave enough to cover his sons' ketubbah — see there. And see in Sefer Shaar Naftali in this siman, who rejected Kenesset Yisrael's words above, because one should compare marriage of a minor to the matter of marriage of deaf-mute persons — for whom the Sages enacted kiddushin but did not enact a ketubah (as below siman 67) — see there further.
