One who acts earlier, etc.; but, etc., Tur. As stated in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto, and in the chapter HaNisrafin: "one who marries them off close to their maturity." The Tur explains this as close after they reach maturity, unlike Rashi and Tosafos, who explain it as close before maturity. This is what is stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin: "Had I married at fourteen, etc." It means during his fourteenth year, when he was thirteen years old. See Yoreh Deah, siman 245, seif 8, and the gloss there. Otherwise, it should have said, "Had I married at thirteen," since the closer it is to maturity, the better. Before thirteen, however, he did not say this, which shows that it is forbidden, as stated there regarding one who marries a woman to his minor son. One might have thought that even after thirteen he is still included in that category, as stated in chapters 7, 10, and 14 of Yevamos, that the Sages did not institute marriage for a minor.
And one for whom the years have passed. As stated at the end of the chapter HaMadir regarding Rav Tachlifa, etc. All the more so does this apply to one who acted foolishly and never married at all.
However, if, etc. This is the wording of the Rambam through seif 8. In Kiddushin there our Rabbis taught: "to study, etc." Rav Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel, etc., and R. Yochanan said, etc.; they do not disagree, etc. See Rashi. The Ran writes that others explain that the wives of the people of Bavel engaged in commerce and labor, whereas the wives of the people of Eretz Yisrael were pampered, ate, and did not work. See seif 4.
Such as a barren woman, etc. Even though according to strict law, etc., as stated there, folio 65a: "She may not marry a third, etc." All the more so does this apply to one who is certainly barren, since that woman may yet bear children, as stated there: "If she married a fourth, etc." The same applies to an elderly woman, as stated on folio 61b: "If he has no children, he marries, etc." The same applies to a minor, as stated in the second chapter of Niddah regarding those who have relations with minors, etc. Beis din compels him because of neglect of procreation, as stated at the end of the chapter HaMadir, as explained above. This applies even if he married, etc., as stated there. It is taught in Tosefta Yevamos, chapter 8: It is forbidden to remain without a wife, and it is forbidden to marry a barren woman, a minor, an aylonis, or one who is incapable of bearing children. This is as stated in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto regarding an aylonis, and the same applies to the others.
The custom is not, etc. The Mordechai writes in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto that this is because the halacha follows Rav there, who says that we do not compel, etc. Moreover, Rav and Shmuel disagree only in Eretz Yisrael, in light of what is stated in HaBa Al Yevimto 64a, "to teach you, etc.," and at the end of the third chapter of Bava Basra: "From the day the kingdom spread, etc., it would be proper, etc." We rely upon that passage not to compel, etc. As a matter of law, however, all these arguments are rejected. We rule like Shmuel, as explained below in siman 154. The Rosh already rejected the proof from HaBa Al Yevimto there. Regarding the passage at the end of the third chapter of Bava Basra, Tosafos already wrote there: "This is difficult, for it is written, 'Be fruitful and multiply.' Perhaps this applies to those, etc." See Ein Yaakov there, and the Gemara and Tosafos, for all of this applies specifically under a governmental decree.
