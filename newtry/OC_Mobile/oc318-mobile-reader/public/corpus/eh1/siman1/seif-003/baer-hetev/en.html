Son of eighteen. Even though for all mitzvot one is obligated to fulfill them immediately when the son reaches thirteen, for this mitzvah our Sages of blessed memory received the tradition that at eighteen one goes to the wedding canopy — since one must first learn before marrying a woman, and the study of Gemara begins from age fifteen onward. And the Maggid Mishneh wrote at the beginning of ch. 15 of Hilkhot Ishut that it is a mitzvah to marry at the beginning of one's eighteenth year.
The most propitious [time]. That is, thirteen complete years, as is evident from the Rambam. But Rashi and Tosafot in Sanhedrin 86b hold that half a year or a year before becoming an adult is also a mitzvah.
Like promiscuity. This is the view of Rambam and Tur. But Tosafot and Rosh hold it is not promiscuity since she is betrothed to him. See Choshen Mishpat and Beit Shmuel. And in the responsum of Kenesset Yechezkel question 55 a distinction is drawn: if his father betroths him while he is a minor, or if the father seizes movable property for the ketubbah — then it is a mitzvah; but if the minor himself marries a woman it is like promiscuity, because it is impossible to write a ketubbah (a minor's acts are void) and he would dwell with his wife without a ketubbah, making his intercourse promiscuous — see there. And see the challenge that Beit Shmuel seif 3 posed to the Bach, and see Sanhedrin 15b — think well.
To delay. There is no fixed time for this matter — even all his days — provided his evil inclination does not overpower him (Beit Shmuel).
To divorce her. See Choshen Mishpat. And if he wishes to divorce her nowadays against her will after she has waited ten years [without children], it seems that in such a case Rabbenu Gershom did not decree — Beit Shmuel. See siman 154.
