Beit Shmuel brings in the name of Bach, siman 119, that he should place the get in the hand of an agent for delivery, so that it remain in his hand until she recovers, etc. Chelkat Mechokek, in that siman, s.k. 12, asks two questions: why is it necessary to obligate him both to set aside the ketubah and to undertake the obligations of the ketubah stipulations? He writes that perhaps, since because of the stringency of the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom one must designate a get through an agent, therefore the ketubah must also be prepared. It appears from this that he treats as primary what Bach testified in siman 119, although here he makes no mention of a get. In my humble opinion, his words here are primary, and I do not know what this get accomplishes for fulfilling the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom. In any case, as long as she has not recovered, it is impossible to divorce her, and he has a wife in addition to his wife. When she recovers, either way: if both of them want the get, who will prevent them? And if he refuses, he can likewise annul it now, unless the intent is to make him swear that he will not annul it, or the court will compel him. And if she refuses to receive it, the same is true when it is in the agent's hand. Not only is the benefit remote, but see Mishneh LaMelekh, chapter 6 of Hilchot Gerushin, halachah 3, s.v. "ve-da," where it is explained that there is concern for a prohibition if she remarries with such a get. It is not only regarding a complete shoteh, where by Torah law she is not divorced, that at first glance he is certainly correct because any matter, etc.; rather, even regarding a rabbinic shoteh, where after the fact one might with great difficulty permit such a get, nevertheless why enter into a narrow breach when the benefit is remote? In Tzemach Tzedek no get is mentioned. See siman 119 seif 6, where I discussed this at length. As for Chelkat Mechokek's difficulty about setting aside the ketubah, in my humble opinion it is simple: since they permit him to marry, and when she recovers she will by law claim the ketubah and divorce, perhaps he will then claim, "I have nothing," or in truth he will not have anything, and the poor woman will be harmed. Therefore he is obligated to designate the ketubah. See the beginning of siman 93. Nevertheless, she remains his wife, for he is not able to divorce her, and he remains obligated in all obligations. This requires study.
