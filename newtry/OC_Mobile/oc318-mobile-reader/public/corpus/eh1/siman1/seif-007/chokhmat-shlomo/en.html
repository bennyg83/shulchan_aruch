In that same seif 10 in the Hagah One whose wife apostatized transfers acquisition of a get to her, etc. NB: I saw in Avnei Miluim that he brought proof permitting one to transfer acquisition of a get for an apostate woman from what Rashba wrote in his novellae to the first chapter of Kiddushin, 23, that where it is an absolute benefit, even if he stands and protests it does not help, for his view is nullified against that of all people; see there. Seemingly there is proof for Rashba from what is written at the end of the second chapter of Eruvin, 26b, regarding R. Eliezer's question: if he said "I do not nullify," and according to the Rabbis, if he said "I do nullify," what is the law, etc.? This is difficult both according to the Rabbis and according to R. Eliezer. Granted that the reason of each is that people do not live in a house without a courtyard, nevertheless he stands and cries, "I do not nullify." And conversely according to the Rabbis, granted that their reason is that a person does not usually remove himself entirely from that house, nevertheless he says, "I do nullify," and removes himself entirely. Rashi explained that this is because his view is nullified against that of all people; in truth this is a reason without taste. It is true that we find this reason in the third chapter of Pesachim regarding "leave the Babylonian kutach," etc., but there it is understandable: regarding liability for punishment for eating a forbidden food, it must be significant for all people, for that is why one is liable only for a kezayit and is exempt for less than a kezayit. Even though we see that for this eater even the smallest amount is significant, since he ate it, nevertheless he is exempt because it is not significant for all. So too, if generally that food is not normally eaten in that way, even though he ate it, it has no significance to make him liable. But regarding transfer of monetary ownership, this depends on the owner, and what difference does it make that other people's view is not so? According to the Rabbis, where he says "I do nullify," all is well, for this would be proof to what I wrote in a responsum on the laws of kiddushin, siman 30, which is in my sixth edition on Even HaEzer, siman 21, that in acquisition of money we require his mouth and heart to be equal, and where there is a clear assessment that he did not fully resolve and transfer ownership, his verbal statement to the contrary does not help; see there. I wrote similarly in a responsum on the laws of Pesach to the holy community of Lintovitz, in my work on Orach Chayyim, part 95, siman 448; see there. If so, here too, even though he says "I do nullify," since the assessment is to the contrary, that in his heart he does not resolve and nullify, it does not help. But in the opposite direction it is difficult according to R. Eliezer: if he says "I do not nullify," what does it matter if in his heart we say he resolves and nullifies? With his mouth he says that he does not nullify, and he stands and protests that he is not nullifying and is not transferring his domain to them. Rather, necessarily it is like Rashba's words: where it is an absolute benefit, even if he stands and protests to the contrary, it does not help, even in monetary acquisition. Although Rashba himself weakened his argument there from what the Talmud says that when they grow up they can protest, nevertheless one can say that there it is not an absolute benefit, for one may say that he prefers ownerless status; but where it is an absolute benefit, his protest to the contrary does not help. Examine carefully.
