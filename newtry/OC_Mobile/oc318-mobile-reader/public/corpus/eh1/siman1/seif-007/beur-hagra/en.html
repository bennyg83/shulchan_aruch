And he converted — he and they. For otherwise what does he say, and it is necessary, etc. — behold a gentile inherits his father by Torah law — see there Rashi and Tosafot who strained themselves to explain regarding firstborn status; but according to Rambam's explanation it is fine, for this is what he says, i.e. Rabbi Yose says he did not fulfill peru u-revu — a convert who converted, etc., i.e. the sons who converted and are not his sons; but because of his own conversion — no, since in his gentile state, etc., as Tosafot wrote there in the passage beginning "Rabbi Yose"; and even Rabbi Yose agrees except he argues because of the sons' conversion, as if the sons died — for a minor, etc.; and upon this he says "and it is necessary," etc. — that fundamentally too, etc., i.e. before they converted; even after he converted — unlike inheritance, for a gentile does not inherit the convert, as written in Kiddushin there. And this is what is written there "they converted" — why plural language of life. And the sugya in Bekhorot does not prove Rambam's words, for he asks in Bekhorot 47a "we learned: who," etc.; and if you say — from where do we know the mishnah speaks when they also converted? And so in Choshen Mishpat siman 277 (should read 277 seif 9) he did not write that they converted; and the Gemara there and in Yevamot makes the necessity there; and also it is not mentioned at all in the Gemara except "and he converted"; and also if you say they did not convert — not certainly regarding them; certainly "a convert who converted," etc., as written there "we learned," etc. — "a gentile is different," etc. — it works for Rabbi Yose, etc. — apparently it is straightforward to the Gemara that in this case certainly plural language of lineage is not needed, and this is obvious without any proof; and so is the view of all the poskim, unlike Rambam.
