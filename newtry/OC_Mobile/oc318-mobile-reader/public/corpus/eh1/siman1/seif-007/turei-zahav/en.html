And he and they converted. So write the Rambam, chapter 15, halacha 6, and the Tur. Maharil, siman 223, writes that even if his children did not convert with him, he has fulfilled peru u'revu, and this is somewhat implied by Yevamos 62, which says, 'If he had children while a gentile and then converted,' etc. It appears, however, that the passage refers specifically to a case in which his children converted with him. Rabbi Yochanan says that he fulfilled peru u'revu, whereas Reish Lakish says that he did not, and they follow their respective views regarding a man who had children as a gentile and then converted. Rabbi Yochanan says that a later son is not a firstborn for inheritance, because the earlier son was already the first of his strength; Reish Lakish says that the later son is a firstborn for inheritance. The Gemara explains that both teachings are necessary, because one might have thought that Rabbi Yochanan rules only regarding peru u'revu, since gentiles were originally subject to that mitzvah, but would agree regarding inheritance, since gentiles are not subject to the laws of the firstborn. Rashi and Tosafos explain that this refers to the special inheritance of the firstborn, since among gentiles a daughter is like a son and a firstborn is like an ordinary son; ordinary inheritance, however, does apply, for a gentile inherits his father by Torah law. It would be very difficult to say that Rabbi Yochanan considers children born before conversion his seed for peru u'revu, yet does not consider the first of them the first of his strength. Therefore the case must be one in which the children also converted. If they did not convert, he has not fulfilled peru u'revu, since he must establish Jewish children, as Chelkas Mechokek writes. Consequently, those children are not called the first of his strength after his conversion. Once we find that converted children count for peru u'revu but still do not retain the ordinary laws of inheritance from their father, as stated in Kiddushin 18b, the Gemara teaches that nevertheless there is no later firstborn for inheritance, because they are called the first of his strength. The distinction between whether the children converted with him applies specifically to peru u'revu, where he must establish Jewish children; it does not apply in the same way to inheritance.
