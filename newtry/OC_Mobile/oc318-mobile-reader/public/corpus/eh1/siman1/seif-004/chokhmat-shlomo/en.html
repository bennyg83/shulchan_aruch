Seif 10 **Rabbenu Gershom imposed the cherem, etc.** Note: I saw in Yeshuot Yaakov that he brought Rashba's words in Kiddushin chapter haOmer that from a verse they derive for all kiddushin "an act is effective"; and see in Choshen Mishpat siman 198 in my fourth edition called Mishpat Tzedek what he wrote on this regarding acquisition of land — see there. And it appears proven from Tosafot to the contrary in Temurah (6a) where they asked: why does he not say there is a practical difference between Abaye and Rava regarding one who swore not to divorce, etc.? And according to Rashba's words one could say: since they expound "and she shall go out and be," etc. — if in kiddushin an act is effective, so too in divorce; and what about against his will — he does not hold thus? It is true as their words prove from the Gemara there, for otherwise what does the Gemara ask Rava from one who divorces under duress; and what does it ask — let us say it is different there, for they derive divorce from kiddushin that an act is effective; and necessarily it will be proven that in divorce one should say "an act is not effective," and necessarily proven that also in kiddushin there is what we say "an act is not effective." However, in truth there is no proof from the Gemara there, for it only asks Rava why he is not lashed; but if the get is not void it does not ask at all; and on the contrary, in our words Tosafot's difficulties there are resolved, since divorce does not work to obligate him for married woman, etc.; and according to what is written, it will be a question that certainly regarding the body of divorce we derive from kiddushin that divorce takes effect; only it asks: for Abaye he is not lashed since his act does not work completely, for he must return; but for Rava, even though his act does not work completely, nevertheless he is lashed for transgressing the master's word; and if so, on the contrary from Tosafot's difficulties there will be proof for Rashba. But from Tosafot's words it will be proven he does not hold thus; and if so, this law is in a second dispute, and I have no leisure to expand; perhaps God will grant me at another time to explain more — see and consider well. And behold, in law: if one sends a get to his wife, is he permitted to marry a woman before it is known whether it reached them — the author debated this in a responsum on whether we say the presumption of an agent, etc.; and I too already wrote on this. And behold, now it seemed to me to add a new permission from what I found in responsum Maharam Chaviv laws of agents siman 21: he wishes to say that even according to the one who does not say presumption of an agent, nevertheless if the agent says he will do this thing, then one may rely on him by virtue of "the remnant of Israel will not do iniquity"; and if so, one could say similarly regarding a get: since the custom is and it is explicit in Shulchan Arukh laws of Gittin that we accept kinyan from the agent who resolves in his heart to be an agent — behold there is statement and act by the agent; therefore it works even for one who does not say presumption of agent. And even though for a get the matter is not in the agent's hand lest she not wish to accept it — nevertheless one could say if the woman begins to consent to accept the get, then one could say in her too this presumption that the remnant of Israel will not do iniquity, and she will presumably accept the get. However, in truth Maharam's words are astonishing to me: if according to his view, why does the Gemara ask in Eruvin (32a) for one who does not say presumption of agent — from the braita of one who says to his friend "pick figs for yourself," and the dispute of Rabbi and Rabban Gamliel — what does it ask? Behold there the homeowner begins with him to say "pick figs for yourself"; and if so, one could say there it is different because the remnant of Israel will not do iniquity and will not give him something improper, since then he is the one speaking to him to pick; and what proof is there from this to the law whether the sender speaks to the agent and the agent says nothing? Therefore his words are undecided. And also according to his words there, he himself distinguishes whether it is possible the sender will do it through another or not; and if so, similarly for a get it is possible he will send her a get through another. And what Maharam brought as proof from Pesachim and chapter haZahav, and what he asked from Kiddushin chapter 2 Abaye and Rava — they do not hold this view of remnant of Israel, etc. — it appears a question: the distinction is like what we say in general "perhaps he died — we are not concerned; perhaps he will die — we are concerned"; for regarding the future it is not relevant to establish a presumption; even though from there there is no complete proof, for there he will eventually die — nevertheless as a type of proof, one can distinguish in presumption between whether the doubt is whether it already happened, and whether the doubt is lest it happen afterward. So too here one could say: certainly where the main doubt depends on whether the act already happened or not — since every matter is fit to establish in presumption that the act did not happen, as is known — therefore this is stronger than presumption of remnant of Israel, etc.; therefore we do not rely on it leniently. But where the doubt is lest it happen afterward — here one properly relies on this presumption of remnant of Israel, etc.; and presumably he will do so; therefore a question: there regarding slaughter for one sitting in prison — the doubt is regarding the future whether they will release him afterward — here we rely on remnant of Israel, etc. And similarly in chapter haZahav regarding one who promised to give him a gift that a Levite may make it tithe — behold now he certainly did not give him, only that he relies he will give afterward — here he can rely on this presumption and make it tithe, for he will certainly give him. But for kiddushin where the doubt is whether she already consented or not — here we do not rely on this presumption. And similarly in Eruvin when he said "pick for yourself a basket of figs" at the time he wishes to eat — the doubt is whether he already tithed them — we do not rely on this presumption of remnant of Israel, etc. And this is correct in reasoning, with God's help; and if so, similarly for a get where the doubt is whether he already gave her the get — for as long as he did not give her it is forbidden to marry a woman, even though he will eventually give her — therefore here this presumption of remnant of Israel does not work to permit marrying a woman even though he and the agent explicitly said they will do so. And this is correct in my humble opinion for practical law — see and consider well.
