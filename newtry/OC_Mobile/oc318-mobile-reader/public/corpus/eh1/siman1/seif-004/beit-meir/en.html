And similarly regarding a betrothed woman the gloss: "if he does not wish to consummate but only to release" — Beit HaEfodi explained at the beginning: just as for yevamah he did not enact and one can perform yibbum even though he has a wife, so for betrothed he did not enact what he enacted not to divorce against his will; but if he does not wish to consummate but only to release, he may divorce against his will. And accordingly, in any case it is forbidden to marry before he divorces, unless for the reason mentioned at the end of the gloss — that she does not wish to marry him or to be released — then one may be lenient for him to marry a woman; it appears that whoever she wishes to marry and he refuses — it is forbidden to take a wife. And for this it appears the Rama added that one should not interpret "and similarly for betrothed that it is permitted to marry upon her" — however Chacham Tzvi siman 124 understood "and similarly for betrothed" in Shulchan Arukh that even for betrothed it is permitted to marry upon her if he intends to divorce the betrothed; and the Rama's addition is thus: that she should not err that he is permitted to marry another even though he intends to consummate his betrothal; and on the grounds that the second is not forbidden since the first is not yet his wife; and also the first would be permitted to consummate with another after the second's marriage, since with permission she was betrothed to him — he comes to teach it is forbidden. And its explanation is strained. And if according to Darkhei Moshe he wrote after Maharik's words and Maharam's responsum that Rabbenu Gershom did not decree on betrothed like Maharik above — Maharam's language in siman 14 is: it is further correct to say that Rabbenu Gershom enacted not to marry two wives in his actual language that both are married to him, because they were concerned, etc.; but one of them betrothed — as we say, lest she bear from him, etc. — and we find we can say Rabbenu Gershom did not enact here; and do not add to the enactment except in his clear language that one may not marry two women. From this it appears the Rama learned even from Maharik's words that until here he only enacted not to marry upon his married wife; but where he did not marry her and wishes to retract — who will compel him to write a ketubah and commit to the rest, etc.? And since he wrote he only enacted upon his married wife — behold for betrothed he enacted nothing; i.e. neither the enactment of divorce against his will nor the enactment to marry upon her; for otherwise sometimes through one of these enactments he would be compelled to marry the betrothed — and who will compel us, etc.? And so it appears Beit Yosef understood. If so, Chacham Tzvi's explanation is necessarily compelled; therefore he asked from siman 116 to siman 159 seif 5 in the gloss: why should rabbinic zikah be preferable to Torah betrothal? But in my view, even though in Darkhei Moshe it appears thus, nevertheless it seems the end of the gloss teaches according to Beit HaEfodi above, and not as he returned to explain with strain that if she does not wish to accept the get even though she wishes to marry him and he refuses — it is permitted to marry; on the contrary, from the end of the gloss it appears as above; and Beit Shmuel's explanation is more strained — see and consider.
