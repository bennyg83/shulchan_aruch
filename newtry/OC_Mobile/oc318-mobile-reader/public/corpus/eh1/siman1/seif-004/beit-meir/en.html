And similarly regarding a betrothed woman. The gloss states, "If he does not wish to consummate the marriage but only to release her." Beit Hillel initially explained that just as Rabbenu Gershom did not enact his decree regarding a yevamah, and a man may perform yibbum even though he already has a wife, so too regarding a betrothed woman he did not enact the prohibition against divorcing her against her will. Therefore, if he does not wish to consummate the marriage but only to release her, he may divorce her against her will. According to this, however, he is in any event forbidden to marry another woman before divorcing the betrothed woman, unless the circumstance mentioned at the end of the gloss applies, namely that she wishes neither to marry him nor to be released. In that case one may be lenient and permit him to marry another woman. This implies that whenever she wishes to marry him and he refuses, he may not marry another woman. It therefore appears that the Rama added these words so that one should not interpret "and similarly regarding a betrothed woman" to mean that he may marry another woman while remaining betrothed to her. Chacham Tzvi, siman 124, however, understood the Shulchan Aruch's words "and similarly regarding a betrothed woman" to mean that even regarding a betrothed woman he may marry another if he intends to divorce the betrothed woman. He therefore explained the Rama's addition as follows: one should not mistakenly think that he may marry another woman even while intending subsequently to consummate his betrothal. One might reason that the second woman is not forbidden because the first is not yet his wife, and that the first would likewise remain permitted to him after the second marriage because she had been betrothed to him permissibly. The Rama therefore teaches that this is forbidden. This explanation is forced. Darkei Moshe, after citing Maharik and the responsum of Maharam Padua, wrote that Rabbenu Gershom did not decree regarding a betrothed woman, as Maharik wrote above. Maharam's language in siman 14 is that it is also correct to say that Rabbenu Gershom enacted only that one may not marry two wives, literally meaning that both are married to him, because the concern was, etc. Where one of them is only betrothed, as in our case, and she cannot bear children from him, etc., one may say that Rabbenu Gershom did not enact in such a case. We should not add to the enactment beyond his clear language that one may not marry two women. It appears that the Rama understood even Maharik's words in this manner. Maharik wrote that Rabbenu Gershom enacted only that one may not marry another woman in addition to a wife already married to him. Where he has not married the betrothed woman and wishes to retract, who can compel him to write her a ketubah and obligate himself in the remaining marital duties? Since Maharik wrote that the enactment applies only to a married wife, nothing was enacted regarding a betrothed woman, neither the prohibition against divorcing her against her will nor the prohibition against marrying another woman while betrothed to her. Otherwise, one of these enactments could sometimes compel him to marry the betrothed woman, and who can compel him to do so? Beit Yosef also appears to have understood the matter this way. Chacham Tzvi's interpretation is therefore necessarily required, and for this reason he asked from siman 116 here to siman 159, seif 5, in the gloss: why should rabbinic zikah be stronger than Torah betrothal? In my humble opinion, however, although Darkei Moshe appears to support that interpretation, the end of the gloss nevertheless points to Beit Hillel's original explanation. It does not support the strained alternative that if she refuses to accept the get, even though she wishes to marry him and he refuses, he may marry another woman. On the contrary, the end of the gloss implies as stated above. Beit Shmuel's explanation is even more forced, and this requires further study.
