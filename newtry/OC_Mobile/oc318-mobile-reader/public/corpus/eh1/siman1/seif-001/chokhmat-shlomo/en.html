Seif 1 **Every person is obligated to marry a woman in order to be fruitful and multiply, etc.** Note: See in Beit Shmuel s.k. 1; and if so, perhaps he fulfills "lest you withdraw your hand" in the place he goes, etc. And in my humble opinion it appears there is a practical difference according to what is explained below in Shulchan Arukh (siman 90) that it is forbidden for a man to marry a woman in this country and take a wife in another country — lest a brother marry his sister; and it implies that "country" is only a city called "country" in Rambam's language, as Semag wrote in siman 192; and so too in the Gemara in what Rav challenged, etc. And if we say "country" specifically — the name of a country in its entirety — we would say Rav married a woman in that country in another city; and necessarily that too is forbidden. And if so — how can he fulfill "lest you withdraw your hand" in the place he goes? And the Tanna cited stam — it implies all the students, even though their names were not removed; and Rambam's law is proven well. But PUA he can fulfill thus — it appears plain; question. And behold I was satisfied regarding one who raised an orphan boy and orphan girl in his house — whether this is considered fulfillment of PUA, since Chazal said one who raises an orphan boy and orphan girl in his house is elevated upon him as if he bore them; if so, one can say it is also as if he fulfilled PUA; or at least it is not like a real child and he did not fulfill PUA with this. And at first glance it appears this depends on the dispute of Derishah with Taz in Yoreh De'ah (siman 242): Derishah holds wherever it says "as if" — the subject of the comparison is not equal to what it is compared to; and Taz disagrees — it is literally equal. And therefore for Derishah — since it is not literally equal, therefore though we say it is as if he bore them, nevertheless it is not actual birth and he does not fulfill PUA with it. But for Taz — since it is literally equal, then he fulfills PUA with it. However, even for Derishah — nevertheless it is only if he did not bear at all; but if he bore and they died, and he raised an orphan boy and orphan girl — certainly he fulfilled PUA; for either way: if we require birth — behold he bore literally; and even though he did not raise them — behold he raised orphan boy and orphan girl; and though he did not bear, at least he raised; and he exits either way. So it seems to me correct for practical law — examine well.
