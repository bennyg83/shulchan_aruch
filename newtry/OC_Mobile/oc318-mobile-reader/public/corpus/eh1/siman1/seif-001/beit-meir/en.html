One may not sell a Sefer Torah. Beit Shmuel wrote that, nevertheless, what the Rama wrote at the end of siman 63 in the gloss in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim, etc., does not prove the matter in my humble opinion. One may say that he chose a reason that applies even to an elderly woman or to a woman who already has children. Rambam's view, however, is clearly unlike that of Tosafos. See Baer Heitev, s.k. 27. This also appears to me from the unqualified language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. According to Tosafos, I have difficulty with the sugya at the end of the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto. Why does the Gemara ask, "If she is not commanded, what claim does she have to a ketubah?" Perhaps she nevertheless has a ketubah because she is commanded regarding "He did not create it for emptiness, He formed it to be inhabited." Likewise, what is gained by telling her, "Go, you are not commanded"? She may answer, "I wish to fulfill the mitzvah of inhabiting the world." Although one can answer with difficulty, as Tosafos answered concerning a half-maidservant, that we are concerned that even after he divorces her she will not marry because she is not commanded in procreation, for which we compel him, it remains difficult. From where did the Gemara know to ask its question? It is forced to say that it relied upon what is implied by the case of the half-maidservant. Furthermore, even when she presents her claim, do we know that she will marry? Perhaps she is lying. Rather, depriving her of her ketubah is different. Yet why should her claim based upon the mitzvah of inhabiting the world differ from her claim, "I need a staff for my hand and someone to bury me"? Magen Avraham's proof from the fact that the verse concerning inhabiting the world is cited regarding the sale of a Sefer Torah, implying that this is on account of a woman, may be rejected. The verse is brought to explain why this positive commandment differs from others, to the extent that one sells a Sefer Torah for it. It teaches that this is a great mitzvah. This also appears to be the view of Tosafos in their answer at the beginning of Bava Batra, and likewise in Tosafos, Gittin 41b.
