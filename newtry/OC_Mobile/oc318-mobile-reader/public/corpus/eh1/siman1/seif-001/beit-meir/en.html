One does not sell a Sefer Torah. Beit Shmuel wrote: however, what he said at the end of siman 63 in the gloss in the name of Shiltei Giborim, etc. — in my humble view, if that is the reason, it does not prove anything, for one could say he uses a reason that applies also to an elderly woman or to a woman who has children. But Rambam’s view is clear: unlike Tosafot. And see Baer Hetev seif 27. And so it seems to me the silence of Tur and Shulchan Arukh; and according to Tosafot I have a difficulty from the sugya at the end of chapter haBa al Yevimto: what does it ask — “and if she is not bound by a commandment, what is her ketubah for”? Perhaps she nonetheless has a ketubah since she deposits for shevet; and those who say “go — you are not commanded” — so what? She said “I wish to fulfill shevet.” And even though one can strain as Tosafot strained regarding the half-maidservant — that we fear even after he divorces her she will not marry, since she is not commanded in peru u-revu for which we compel — nevertheless it is difficult: from where does one raise the question? It is strained to rely on what is implied from the half-maidservant case. And further, when she comes with the claim “we know she will marry” — let there also be doubt, perhaps she is lying. Necessarily, to annul her ketubah is different; and what is the difference between her claim regarding the mitzvah of shevet and her claim “I want a staff”? The proofs of Magen Avraham from the fact that regarding sales of Sefer Torah the verse about shevet is cited — implying it is because of a woman — one can refute: he brings it to explain why this positive commandment differs from others to the point where one sells a Sefer Torah for it; he cites that verse to teach it is a great mitzvah. And so it appears the view of Tosafot in their answer at the beginning of Bava Batra; and so too in Tosafot Gittin 41b.
