Every man is obligated to marry a woman. Rambam wrote in chapter 15 of Hilchot Ishut that if a wife permits her husband to withhold her onah, this is permitted only when he has children and has already fulfilled peru u-revu. If he has no children, he must have relations at every onah until he fulfills the mitzvah of peru u-revu. This is also stated below, siman 76 seif 6. Beit Shmuel asked from where Rambam derived this distinction: why should her waiver be effective after fulfillment of peru u-revu but not before, since even after fulfilling peru u-revu he remains obligated by "in the evening do not withhold your hand"? Although that mitzvah is rabbinic, from where do we know that her waiver helps? He discussed this at some length. It escaped him that Maggid Mishneh already wrote on Rambam there that chapter 7 teaches that a man should not marry a barren woman, an aylonit, or a minor unfit to bear children unless he has fulfilled peru u-revu or has another wife through whom he can be fruitful. R. Moshe HaKohen objected that even after fulfilling peru u-revu one may not marry a woman who cannot bear children, for the halachah follows R. Yehoshua, who says that if one had children in youth he should have children in old age. Maggid Mishneh answers that R. Yehoshua's law is rabbinic, while Rambam there speaks by Torah law; Rambam later records R. Yehoshua's view as a rabbinic mitzvah not to refrain from procreation as long as one has strength. In chapter 21 of Hilchot Issurei Biah Rambam writes without qualification that one should not marry a woman incapable of bearing children, and that is rabbinic. Thus Rambam's statement that once he has fulfilled peru u-revu her permission helps not to fulfill onah is likewise from the Torah perspective; rabbinically, because of "in the evening do not withhold," it remains prohibited. The difficulty is really on Shulchan Aruch, which cites this as practical law in siman 76 seif 6, although he is at least obligated rabbinically because of "in the evening do not withhold." But even if Rambam is speaking also rabbinically, there is no difficulty. Rambam later wrote that one should not marry a woman who cannot bear children unless he has children or another wife for peru u-revu. Why did he not write here too, regarding a wife who waived her onah, that if he has another wife for peru u-revu her waiver is effective? It must be that where a wife waives her onah, even if he has another wife, her waiver is ineffective unless he already has children and fulfilled peru u-revu. Only there, where the issue is marrying a woman who cannot bear children, can we say that since he already has a wife he is not obligated to marry another until ten years have passed; what difference is there between not taking another wife at all and taking a wife who cannot bear children? But here, where he already has two wives, he is certainly obligated in onah with both as long as he has no children, for perhaps he will not have children from one but will from the other. Moreover, wherever he can advance the mitzvah, the obligation rests on him to do so. As Maggid Mishneh wrote regarding Rambam's ruling not to marry a minor: although she will eventually become capable of bearing children, he is obligated now and she is not presently capable. Even if a woman he already married has not given birth, he need not divorce her until after ten years, and a minor will become capable before ten years; nevertheless, initially it is forbidden, because whatever he can do earlier to fulfill the mitzvah he must do earlier. The same applies here with two wives. One should not say the concern is only that after becoming capable she may still not give birth and he will need to wait another ten years, for we do not forbid on that basis; we follow the majority who are capable of bearing children. Rather, the reason is that the obligation is immediate. Regarding "in the evening do not withhold," however, all poskim agree that even according to R. Yehoshua, if she remained with him ten years and did not give birth he is not obligated to divorce her. R. Yehoshua spoke only of the initial duty to marry a woman capable of bearing children. Ramban even wrote that we do not compel him over this; if he fears quarrels between children, he may initially marry a woman unable to bear children. The poskim record several leniencies. Therefore all the more where he has another wife for peru u-revu, he certainly satisfies even R. Yehoshua, and he is not obligated to maintain onah with the second wife when she waived it. This seems the correct way to settle Rambam. Shulchan Aruch in siman 76 also speaks where he has another wife. Although Shulchan Aruch did not say so explicitly, it relied on what was written in siman 1: even after fulfilling peru u-revu he should marry a woman capable of bearing children, and certainly he may not remain entirely without a wife, a point with which no one in the Gemara disagrees. If a wife's waiver would leave him without a wife capable of relations or children, why would it help? Necessarily it speaks where he has another wife, and this is understood without spelling it out.
