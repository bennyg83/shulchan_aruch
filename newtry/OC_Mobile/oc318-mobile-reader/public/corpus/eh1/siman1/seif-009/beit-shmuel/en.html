Provided that it is possible. If she waives her rights and wishes to be satisfied with little, he may certainly marry her even if he cannot provide the full support owed to all of them. The Chelkas Mechokek wrote likewise. Regarding a yevamah, however, it appears that even if she waives her rights, the beis din must nevertheless advise her not to marry him. This is proven in the Rosh, chapter HaCholetz, where the Gemara asks, "Do we leave them so? But was it not taught, etc.?" This is also implied by the language of the Shiltei Giborim there.
Conjugal duty once a month. This means where he is a Torah scholar, whose onah is every Shabbos. Even if he has several wives, no greater onah obligation is imposed upon him. He owes only the same onah that he would owe if he had one wife, as stated below in siman 76 and in the Rambam and Tur. This is unlike the Nimukei Yosef in the chapter HaCholetz, who distinguishes between a yevamah and other women.

Since the restriction stems from custom, her consent is effective. With respect to Cherem Rabbeinu Gershom, however, her consent is not effective. Darkei Moshe.
