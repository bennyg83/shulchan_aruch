When does this apply, etc. There it states: "a son for a son, etc.; according to everyone, two, etc." Since it says "a son for a daughter," it implies that this applies specifically when the grandchildren descend from both a male and a female. The plain sense of the passage implies that it applies even when both grandchildren are males, and according to Rava even when both are females. See Tosafos there, s.v. VeKol Shekein. One might press the language and explain: all the more so a son born to a daughter where the elderly man's son remains alive, and similarly a daughter born to a son where his daughter remains alive. It is difficult, however, to distinguish between his own child and that child's child, and the Rambam also does not mention such a distinction. The essential reading is that of the Rambam: Rava said to him, "We require 'He created it to be inhabited,'" and the text does not read, "but there is, etc." Rava disputes Abaye's statement, "all the more so a son for a daughter," because the Mishnah's reason is that a male and female should exist in the world. That is the meaning of inhabitation according to the Tanna of the Mishnah. The correct reading of the Rambam in Abaye is: "But a son for a daughter or a daughter for a son does not suffice." Rava said to him, "We require inhabitation, and there is, etc."

If the son was, etc. This is from the Yerushalmi, chapter 2, on the Mishnah. His son is considered his son for every matter, even for procreation, and all the more so regarding the commandment of inhabiting the world, since such descendants are included in one who has a son in any event. The Gemara did not need to derive this explicitly, for it is not a novel ruling, as it states there: "except for the son of a maidservant, etc."
