When he had a male and a female. The issue is whether descendants can stand in place of children for fulfilling peru u-revu and for the separate concern of "He created it to be inhabited." Tosafot wrote that where a person had children and they died, grandchildren who descend from them can count in their place. The matter requires careful explanation. It is not that any descendant automatically counts exactly like the original child in every respect. Rather, for peru u-revu one needs the equivalent of a male and a female, like the world's original creation. For the broader mitzvah of inhabiting the world there is room to say that one who comes through him can stand in place of the child through whom that descendant came, provided the descendant is not a lesser substitute in the relevant respect. Thus a son's son may stand in place of a daughter only according to the initial assumption, but according to Rava's answer the form must resemble peru u-revu itself, namely male and female. This is why the Tur's text here can be read naturally, as in Yoreh Deah siman 392, without forcing a dispute between Tosafot and the poskim. Tosafot were writing according to Abaye's assumption; after Rava connects the matter to "He created it to be inhabited," the conclusion is that the fulfillment must follow the pattern of male and female. I later saw that Torat Chayim wrote similarly: Tosafot spoke according to what Abaye initially thought, but according to the conclusion, since Rava grounds the matter in inhabiting the world, we require male and female and do not apply Abaye's kal vachomer. Although one might ask that the reason of inhabiting the world should suffice even with a son alone or a daughter alone, according to what I have written the matter is settled.
