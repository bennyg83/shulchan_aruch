When the grandchildren were a male and a female. So writes the Rambam in chapter 15. This implies that two males, and all the more so two females, do not suffice. This is also the view of the Semag, positive commandment 79, and of the Semak. It is likewise cited in Yoreh De'ah siman 322. The Perishah and Bach wrote that this is the correct reading in the Tur in this siman. See Terumas HaDeshen siman 267, who reconciles the sugya, and Responsa Maharam of Lublin. Tosafos, however, hold that two males suffice since they descend from a male and a female. According to our reading of the Tur in this siman, even two females suffice. So writes the Shiltei Giborim in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto.
And one of them left, etc. If he had two sons and they left a grandson and a granddaughter, he has not fulfilled peru u'revu. So writes the Darkei Moshe, for according to the Gemara's conclusion we do not say that grandchildren from separate children combine. If the son remains alive, while the daughter died and left a daughter, he has fulfilled peru u'revu. If the son remains alive but has not yet fathered children, while the daughter died and left a son, it appears that he has not fulfilled peru u'revu. The Chelkas Mechokek wrote that there is room to question whether a woman who conceived in a bathhouse thereby enables the father to fulfill peru u'revu and whether the child is considered his son in every respect. A proof may be brought from the glosses to the Semak, cited by the Bach in Yoreh De'ah siman 195, that a woman is warned not to lie on sheets upon which another man lay, lest she conceive from his semen, due to the concern that the child might later marry his paternal sister. This implies that he is considered the man's son in every respect.
The son was a mamzer. Sefer Chassidim, siman 500, wrote that one does not fulfill peru u'revu by fathering a mamzer, because we rule that a mamzer does not live. It is possible that Sefer Chassidim refers to a case in which his status as a mamzer is unknown, for such a mamzer does not live. If his status is known, however, the father fulfills peru u'revu, because the child lives.
