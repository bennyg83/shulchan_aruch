A woman. The prohibition is rabbinic, and the verse is merely an asmakhta (scriptural support) — Beit Shmuel. And Ramban in Milhamot Hashem has uncertainty about this.
Fit to bear children. The obligation is rabbinic — Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. And Ramban wrote that no one compels him, in any view, for it is not a formal enactment but rather in the manner of civilized behavior (derekh eretz), and they do not apply great stringency upon him.
Children. And if he cannot find any woman without selling, he must sell a Sefer Torah so as not to remain without a wife — and this is why it concludes "he shall not remain without a wife," for otherwise this was already stated earlier that he is forbidden to remain without a wife. And even though the straightforward reading of the Gemara implies that the reason for selling a Sefer Torah to marry is on account of shevet yitzrah, nevertheless sinful thoughts are harder [to bear] than shevet yitzrah (Choshen Mishpat). And Beit Shmuel wrote that according to Nimukei Yosef one does not sell — see there.
Sefer Torah. See above seif 2, what was written there.
This [wife]. Due to concern about strife, he may forgo the mitzvah of "in the evening do not withhold your hand," but he may not commit a transgression — for one must fear lest his evil inclination overpower him (Beit Shmuel). And see Choshen Mishpat.
