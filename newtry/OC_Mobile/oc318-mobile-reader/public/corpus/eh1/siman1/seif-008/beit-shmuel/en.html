It is forbidden for him to remain without a wife. This prohibition is rabbinic. Although it is derived from the verse, "It is not good for man to be alone," the verse serves only as an asmachta. See the Rambam, chapter 15, halacha 1. The Ramban in Milchamos Hashem is uncertain about this.
A woman capable of bearing children. The Rif, Rambam, and Rosh wrote that the obligation is rabbinic. The Ramban wrote there that according to all opinions we do not compel him, for this is not a formal enactment, but rather resembles the proper settlement of society, and we therefore do not treat him so stringently.
Rather, he should marry, etc. Regarding the sale of a Sefer Torah, there are three laws. The first is selling it for peru u'revu. The second is selling it to fulfill "In the evening do not withhold your hand." The third is selling it so that he not remain without a wife and come to sin. A Sefer Torah may be sold for peru u'revu according to all opinions. Regarding "In the evening do not withhold your hand," the dispute is explained in the Shulchan Aruch. The third case, selling it so that he not remain without a wife, is not explained here. We find a dispute concerning it. The Nimukei Yosef holds that it may not be sold. Although the Chelkas Mechokek wished to explain the Nimukei Yosef differently, this does not appear correct to me. The Bach likewise understood that the Nimukei Yosef forbids the sale. The proof brought by the Chelkas Mechokek from Terumas HaDeshen, cited nearby, that remaining without a wife is worse, can be rejected. Selling a Sefer Torah is different, because its proceeds must be used to perform a mitzvah, not merely to avoid a transgression. The Ramban in Milchamos Hashem holds that it may be sold. When one is therefore compelled to sell a Sefer Torah, he must marry a woman capable of bearing children. Only if he cannot marry such a woman may he marry one incapable of bearing children. One cannot simply be stringent in this dispute, since the stringency is itself a leniency regarding selling a Sefer Torah. No proof may be brought from the Rosh, who wrote, according to the authorities who hold that one may not sell a Sefer Torah, that he is not obligated to sell it. This might imply that he may sell it voluntarily, but the Rosh follows his own view that an individual may sell his Sefer Torah, as stated there in Orach Chaim.
And some say that even, etc. The Mechaber appears to consider the first opinion primary, but this is incorrect. The Rosh wrote that a Sefer Torah may be sold even after one has fulfilled peru u'revu. This is also the view of the Baal HaMaor, Ramban, Ritva, and Nimukei Yosef. We do not find anyone who explicitly holds like the first opinion. The Rosh and Baal HaMaor merely understood the Rif this way, but this is not compelled, as stated in Milchamos Hashem. The Bach's assertion that Tosafos on folio 61 hold that it may not be sold is imprecise, for there they wrote so according to the Mishnah's view. According to Rabbi Yehoshua, it is obvious that he must sell it, and this is the primary ruling. Nevertheless, Rav Nachman's statement that one may not remain without a wife is still necessary, lest one think that when he cannot marry a woman capable of bearing children he should remain unmarried. Rav Nachman therefore teaches that it is forbidden to remain without a wife.
To remain without a wife. The reason is that because of concern for quarrels he may refrain from the mitzvah of peru u'revu. Concern for quarrels, however, does not permit him to commit a transgression, for one must be concerned that his evil inclination may overpower him.
