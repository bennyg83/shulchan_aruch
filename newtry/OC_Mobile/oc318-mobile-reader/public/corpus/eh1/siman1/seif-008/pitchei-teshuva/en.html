Saris. See Baer Hetev in the name of Choshen Mishpat: if the children are fit to bear children but the son married an aylonit and she married a saris, their father is exempt from peru u-revu. And Choshen Mishpat further wrote: "or they did not marry at all" — and Baer Hetev did not copy this since it goes without saying. And see Sefer Bnei Ahuvah ch. 15 of Hilkhot Ishut halacha 5, who doubts this from the law we hold: if he had children and they died, he has not fulfilled peru u-revu, since we need "the world shall be inhabited" — and if so, these children who did not marry will ultimately die without offspring. But one could say: specifically if they died in the father's lifetime he has not fulfilled it; but regarding a daughter who married a saris and will certainly not bear children — what can the father do to fulfill peru u-revu, and must one rely on the hope that the husband will divorce her or will die? And Choshen Mishpat decided affirmatively even in this case that he has fulfilled it — but to me it requires examination — see there.
