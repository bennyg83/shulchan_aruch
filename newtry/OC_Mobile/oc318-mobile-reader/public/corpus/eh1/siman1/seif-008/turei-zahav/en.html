Rather one should marry a woman who is not fit to bear children. Ramban in Milchamot Hashem and Nimukei Yosef disagree in chapter haBa al Yevimto on this: if he finds no woman without selling a Sefer Torah — Ramban holds one sells, and it is forbidden to stand without a wife; Nekudot HaKesef holds one does not sell. And Chayei Adam wishes to explain Nekudot HaKesef's words in another manner so he does not disagree at all on this. And Beit Shmuel wrote: Nekudot HaKesef does not hold thus — end quote. And according to what appears, both did not examine Nekudot HaKesef himself at all, only what Beit Yosef quoted in his name; but Nekudot HaKesef himself wrote explicitly regarding Rabbi Meir: "Forbidden to stand without a wife like Rav Nachman, and one does not sell for this a Sefer Torah" — end quote. And Beit Shmuel wrote: and what he brought as proof in Choshen Mishpat from words of Tosafot Chiddushei that Rama brought nearby that it is worse to sit without a wife because of concern for this — one could say regarding selling a Sefer Torah it is different, for one must do a mitzvah with money and not saving from sin — end quote. And his words are astonishing, for certainly saving from sin is more severe, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 156 and Orach Chaim siman 456; and they say in the Gemara Shabbat 4b and in Tosafot there and Eruvin 32b and Bava Batra 12b in Tosefta and in many places: if one does a severe sin even though he did a sin in selling the Sefer Torah — what of it? A person does a light sin, etc. And similarly difficulty on Nekudot HaKesef: he ruled one sells a Sefer Torah because of Rabbi Yehoshua even though it is rabbinic; and for a woman not fit to bear children one does not sell — and the inclination is as written. And further difficulty: why did Nekudot HaKesef double his words and write he does not release from Sefer Torah except for a woman not fit to bear children — behold he already wrote before this that it is forbidden to stand without a wife and one does not sell a Sefer Torah for this? Rather it appears Nekudot HaKesef holds like Rif that one does not sell a Sefer Torah because of Rabbi Yehoshua; and if so, also for a woman not fit to bear children there is no a fortiori argument; now that even what is forbidden to stand without a wife is rabbinic, as Rambam wrote laws of marriage chapter 15 — from what he is obligated by Torah law we do not derive a fortiori from rabbinic to Torah. And what he wrote "and it is reasonable that when fit to bear children one sells a Sefer Torah because of Rabbi Yehoshua" even though it is only a rabbinic mitzvah — this refers to what he wrote first: "even though he fulfilled peru u-revu, forbidden to stand without a wife and one does not sell a Sefer Torah for this" — i.e. even if no woman is available at all, even not fit to bear children, one does not sell a Sefer Torah for this and sits without a wife. And upon this they wrote "it is reasonable when fit to bear children" — and in selling a Sefer Torah he can also fulfill Rabbi Yehoshua; one sells a Sefer Torah because of Rabbi Yehoshua even though rabbinic, since without selling he sits without a wife entirely, and through selling he fulfills also Rabbi Yehoshua. And upon this he wrote the reason he does not release here from Sefer Torah except for a woman not fit to bear children — i.e. he does not release here in the Gemara a Sefer Torah except for a woman not fit to bear children, i.e. that he can take a woman not fit to bear children without selling — he does not sell for children as Rashi and Tosafot explained there; but if he finds no woman at all and through selling a Sefer Torah he can take a woman fit to bear children — the Gemara does not speak of this at all. Therefore he wrote "it is reasonable one sells"; and even though also when he finds no woman not fit to bear children except through selling a Sefer Torah the Gemara also does not speak — nevertheless there is no a fortiori for this alone, and Rabbi Yehoshua alone as written. If so, this law is included in the laws the author brought: if we say one sells a Sefer Torah so one marries a woman even if no woman is available except through selling a Sefer Torah; and if there is no a fortiori in chapter 22 also for this — therefore the author did not bring this law in Shulchan Arukh, for it is heard automatically; unlike Chayei Adam. And thereby also what Beit Shmuel wrote afterward is negated: "it appears according to the author he holds primarily the first view" — no, for Rosh wrote one sells even though he fulfilled peru u-revu; and so is the view of Meor, Ramban, Ritva, and Nekudot HaKesef; and according to what is written, Nekudot HaKesef concluded his words and so ruled Ritva — end quote; and he understood Nekudot HaKesef holds one sells; and if so Ritva too holds thus; and according to what is written, Nekudot HaKesef holds there is no a fortiori; and if so Ritva too holds thus. And what he wrote that Rosh holds one sells — so is Meor's view; Beit Yosef already wrote there is no decision in Rosh's words he brought of Meor's view, but as he wrote lastly. And one could say they are balanced in his view. And what he wrote that Rosh and Meor understood thus from Rif's words is not compelled, as written in Milchamot Hashem — behold they wrote the reason because Rif did not write "sons"; the Gemara's mishnah unlike Rabbi Yehoshua; and if because Ramban wrote therefore Rif did not bring it because it did not help him at all — even so he must bring the mishnah; what is obligatory from Torah and Rabbi Yehoshua who is rabbinic is not worthy in it to reject Meor and Rosh's words. Did not Rif write several statements — Rav Nachman's statement: even though a man has several sons, forbidden, etc.; and it was stated: he had sons in his gentile state, he fulfilled peru u-revu; and further there, etc. — from all of them we hear the obligation is not from Torah when he had sons; and Ramban's difficulty remains in its place: why did Rif write on the mishnah; and what Ramban wrote that one sells a Sefer Torah even though he fulfilled peru u-revu because of shevet yetzera — behold he makes shevet yetzera for him; the Gemara's intent is not so. And in this siman seif 7 I brought the aforementioned Gemara; and what is written on Bach who wrote in the name of Tosafot 61b that he holds there is no a fortiori — he wrote Tosafot wrote thus according to the mishnah, but for Rabbi Yehoshua it is obvious one must sell — end quote. Bach's intent is: since Tosafot wrote "and Rabbi Yehoshua further says" and did not write "Rabbi Yehoshua says" — we learn even what they wrote initially is Rabbi Yehoshua's view; therefore Bach wrote in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua. And in the first reasoning Tosafot wrote — he holds that for Rav Nachman there is not even mitzvah to marry a woman fit to bear children; and if so, Rabbi Yehoshua's dispute speaks only that there is mitzvah to marry fit to bear children; but one does not sell a Sefer Torah for this — a distant dispute we only establish according to Tosafot's second view that Rav Nachman holds there is mitzvah to marry a woman fit to bear children; therefore Rabbi Yehoshua's dispute holds one also sells a Sefer Torah for this. But since Tosafot wrote "and further Rabbi Yehoshua said one could say even if he has sons," etc., and did not write "and Rabbi Yehoshua further said there is mitzvah" — we learn this is not decided among them that there is mitzvah, but one could say thus. And the proof Bach brings from Tosafot's words — and even if Tosafot's initial words were not Rabbi Yehoshua's view, nevertheless we hear from what he says "and further Rabbi Yehoshua said one could say" and did not write "but mitzvah there is" — as written — from this we hear that also for Rabbi Yehoshua the first view of Tosafot is primary.
And he fears that if he marries a woman fit to bear children, quarrels will come, etc. And Maharam Chaviv wrote: the proof that Tosafot Chiddushei brought from this that they marry him off with advice suitable for him and we push aside the mitzvah of yibbum because of quarreling — is a weak proof — end quote. His words are not clear: what he wrote "there is one who says mitzvah of chalitzah precedes" — behold here too there is mishnah exempting completely from peru u-revu when he has sons. And further, even for one who says mitzvah of chalitzah precedes — it is because perhaps he will marry not for the sake of the mitzvah; but otherwise he also holds mitzvah of yibbum precedes. And what he wrote "yibbum and chalitzah depend on him, the Merciful One" — this too is not so; for Tosafot Chiddushei's proofs from marrying him off with advice because of quarreling: even if the Torah gave him permission in what he wishes, nevertheless they do not marry him off with advice to transgress the prior mitzvah; and certainly it does not speak of wicked people that we should say because of this they give him advice lest he not intend for the sake of the mitzvah — he derives it in Yevamot 44 from the verse "and the elders of his city shall call him," etc. Or because of quarreling it is permitted; and similarly here.
