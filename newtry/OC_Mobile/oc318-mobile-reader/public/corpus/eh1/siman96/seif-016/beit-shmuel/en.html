The first swears to the second. I already wrote in the name of Rashi and Ran that this is specifically when the second says, "Swear to me that you took nothing from my husband." Know that all these laws, that we attend to collect the ketubah even from minor orphans, are stated in Arakhin and brought by the Rif and Rosh in Perek Almanah Nizonet. We hold like Rabbi Yochanan, who gives the reason of favor, meaning so that she should have favor and money with which to remarry, as written in Maimonides, chapter 12 of Hilchot Malveh. Therefore he ruled that if she already married, we do not attend to collect for her unless she married in reliance on this money, as Shiltei Gibborim writes there. Rashi explains "favor" as meaning that their husbands should have favor in their eyes; according to this, even if she remarried we attend to collect. But the Rif, Rambam, and Maharam hold like the first explanation, and so ruled the Ran. The Rashba writes that even if she is elderly and unfit to remarry, we attend to collect for her, and it implies this is true even according to the Rif. See Beit Yosef at the beginning of seif 7, the beginning of siman 101, Darkei Moshe there, and siman 103.
And the fourth swears to the orphans. It appears that the earlier wives also swear to the orphans’ children, but the first must also swear to the second, and the second to the third. According to this, since the oath is for the orphans, we plead for them that she must swear before the second even according to Rashi. What Rashi wrote, that it is only when she claims this, refers to the first Tanna, who holds she need not swear for the orphans. The practical difference for us is where there are no orphans present.
