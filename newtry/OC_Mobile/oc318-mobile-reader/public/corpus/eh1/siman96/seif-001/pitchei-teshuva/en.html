Only with an oath. See Beit Shmuel, s.k. 1. Since his words are very brief, I will explain somewhat. What he wrote, that although we hold there is a chazakah that a person does not pay within the time, means that according to this chazakah she collects without an oath even from orphans, as explained in Bava Batra 5b and in Choshen Mishpat siman 78:1 and siman 108:1. What he wrote that Tosafot according to the Rosh gave another answer there means that in Bava Batra there the Rosh brought this other answer in the name of Ritzba. What he wrote that there is only a concern that perhaps she took it herself: this concern is mentioned in Tosafot in Perek Mi Shemet, Bava Batra 158, s.v. "and Beit Hillel"; see there. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Choshen Mishpat, siman 18, who was asked about a woman whose husband died while she had not been in the same city with him for several weeks before his death, having traveled from him while he was healthy to her own city; after several weeks he became ill and died, and afterward the woman died. She had a large ketubah because she had brought in a large amount, and she died before taking an oath on her ketubah. The question was whether the law of heirs from heirs applies. He answered that regarding the assets she brought in, namely her dowry and the added third besides the Torah ketubah, there is certainly no concern for hidden payment: not according to Tosafot's answer, since there is no condition of beit din, and not according to the Rosh's answer, since she was not with him at his death or during his illness. The concern that he may have handed her funds applies only where he could place them directly in her hand, not by giving them to another for her; proof is from Ketubot 107, where regarding a minor there is no concern for such payment. The concern that she may have seized it herself also applies only before his death, as Beit Shmuel wrote. Since she was not with him at his death, this concern does not apply. Although Tosafot in Perek Mi Shemet implies regarding a house that fell on him and his wife that we are concerned she seized even not at the time of death, unlike Beit Shmuel's wording "before his death," it appears that we are concerned for this only where he died suddenly, as in that case. But where he died from illness and had the capacity to command his household, this concern does not apply, for the reason stated there. There is only concern that he transferred funds to her or that she seized them at the time of death; since in the case at hand she was not present at his death or illness, there is no concern for hidden payment regarding the assets she brought in, and she had no obligation of oath in her lifetime. Therefore her heirs inherit her dowry and the added amount, except for the Torah ketubah of a virgin, which they do not inherit, for regarding that there is concern for hidden payment since it is a rabbinic enactment. See Beit Shmuel below, s.k. 8. He further writes that regarding ketubot made in our place, etc., this will be brought below in siman 98 seif 3; according to that reason, the heirs inherit everything, even the Torah ketubah, because it is later included in the added ketubah document, which explicitly contains trust. See his words there carefully, for the language there is somewhat difficult.
