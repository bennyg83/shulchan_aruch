That she did not spend anything. See Beit Meir, who wrote one should avoid imposing this oath, for certainly a woman who was with her husband many years cannot fulfill this oath, for at the time it was permitted to her entirely and afterward it is not placed upon her — if I could I would abolish it; at minimum one who wishes to impose it should specify it does not include what she spent on herself from surplus sustenance or clothing, even small gifts to relatives or charity — only that she did not spend a large gift of a fixed sum, ten rubles per local custom or per husband's wealth. But plain language "she did not spend anything" without husband's permission — certainly we do not have. (6) Lest they waive, etc. Krem Shlomo wrote: a pregnant widow who also has a son — at ketubah collection the son exempted her from oath; she bore a viable child living thirty days who died; the son wishes to make her swear on the fetus's share claiming she exempted only his share — responsum Pri HaAretz siman 5: law is with the son and he can make her swear — see there.
