Provided that he informs the first witness. Otherwise she can say she had two ketubot, and this witness assists her, since he knows only that payment was made toward a ketubah. Therefore he informs the witness before payment, and she is no longer believed to say she had two ketubot, because that is uncommon. So wrote Rashi. In the Derishah, in the name of Maharshal, he wrote that if she claims she had two ketubot, she is believed and swears a biblical oath. At first glance this does not appear correct, since it is uncommon. Also, against one witness who testifies that she was paid, she is not believed to say she had another ketubah. She is believed against one witness only when she directly contradicts him, like the case of the ingot of Rabbi Abba. But where he did not inform the witness, she is believed because the witness assists her.
