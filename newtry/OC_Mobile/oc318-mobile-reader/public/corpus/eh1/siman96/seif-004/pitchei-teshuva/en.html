In Chelkat Mechokek this rule is not mentioned at all; rather, Knesset HaGedolah, in the rules of migo printed in Orach Chayim, brought it in the name of Giddulei Terumah, who derived it from the law here. Tumim there seeks to reject his proof; see there. However, at first glance this rule is compelled by Choshen Mishpat siman 82:10, regarding one who produces a validated document and the defendant claims it was a trust document or involved ribbit: he cannot impose an oath on the holder, even if he says, "swear to me." Yet if he claims "I paid," and says "swear to me," the holder must swear, as explained there in seif 1. Necessarily, we do not say migo to impose an oath on the defendant, since there is also a chazakah that a person does not bring a claim, etc. For this reason, after payment he can impose an oath with a claim of trust document or ribbit, since now he is the claimant. Examine carefully, and see Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 87 in the gloss.
