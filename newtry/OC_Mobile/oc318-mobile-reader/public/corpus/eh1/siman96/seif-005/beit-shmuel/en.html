This applies to movable property. See the Tur and Beit Yosef there, where it is clear that the Rif holds that even if she sold land, the sale stands, provided she ultimately swears. So too the Rashba explains the Rif. The Rosh, however, in one responsum wrote that if she sold movables, the sale stands even without an oath, while in another responsum he ruled that if she sold without an oath the sale is void even if she later swore, because a creditor collects only from now on; at the time of sale she had no right. The Beit Yosef gave two answers to reconcile these responsa. The Chelkat Mechokek explains the Shulchan Aruch as speaking of known movables, but according to the Beit Yosef’s first answer, even if she later swore, the sale of known movables is void. It is more reasonable that the Shulchan Aruch here did not rule based on the Rosh’s responsum, but on the Rashba and the implication of the decisors in the Mordechai, who hold that even movables are ineffective without an oath. Therefore the Rashba wrote that according to the Rif it is specifically if she later swore, and it implies even regarding movables. He therefore ruled that with land the sale is void because land remains in its owner’s possession, but with movables the sale stands if she later swore, even known movables. It is surprising that Beit Yosef did not mention the Rambam, who wrote that even with land the sale stands, even if she sold privately without an oath. It is also difficult from below, siman 103, where it is ruled simply that the sale stands, for there it says she must include in the oath that she did not sell cheaply, implying the sale occurred before she swore.

And similarly if they are in possession. So wrote Maharik. His reason is that since there is a dispute, one cannot remove from the possessor. Although Darkei Moshe initially writes "all the more so where the buyer is in possession," implying possession alone may not help unless it appears to the judge that she did not seize improperly, at the end he cites Maharik plainly and rules that possession alone helps. The Chelkat Mechokek did not see the end of Darkei Moshe. It appears that even if she never swore and died, we do not remove from their hand, for the Mordechai cites several decisors that even if she died without swearing, the buyers acquired, since the reason she cannot sell before the oath is that a person cannot bequeath, sell, or give an oath, and we hold that a judge who acted like Rabbi Elazar is not reversed. So too if the buyer is in possession, we do not remove from him, even where there are witnesses who saw. If she sold movables without beit din assessment, we also do not remove from the buyer, because the buyer can say kim li like the Rambam’s view that the sale stands.
