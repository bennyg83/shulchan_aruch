A widow who sold or gave — it is valid if she swore at the end, and they deduct from her ketubah. This applies to movables. But for land — even if she swore at the end — her sale and gift are not valid unless a court imposed the oath on her, even of laymen. And there are those who say a court must collect for her. {Rama: And some say that even for movables her sale and gift are void even though she swore at the end, since she did not swear at the beginning. And some say one should rule according to what the judge sees (Mordechai ch. HaKotev), for where it appears to the judge she did not seize bundles — such as if he died suddenly or similar — one may rule her sale and gift valid (Rosh kelal 85). And likewise if the recipient or buyer is in possession — so it seems to rule. And all this is when the woman retracted after swearing; but if she did not retract — her sale and gift are valid from now (Maharik root 18). Therefore a widow who did not swear on her ketubah and married a husband and brought him much money from her first husband's money — she still swears. And if the second husband died — her heir takes and holds what is in her hand (Hagahot Mordechai on Ketubot). And see above siman 101.}
