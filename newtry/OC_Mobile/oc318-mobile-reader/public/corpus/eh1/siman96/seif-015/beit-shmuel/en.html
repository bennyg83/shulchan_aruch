A hesset oath. So wrote the Tur in the name of the Rosh. At first glance this is difficult, because the Rosh wrote that he speaks in a place where they do not write a ketubah, where the husband cannot deny the basic one hundred or two hundred; therefore it is not partial admission, and the Rosh holds he swears only hesset. But the Tur wrote the law even in a place where they write a ketubah and the ketubah was lost. How did the Tur know this? Perhaps in such a case he must swear a biblical oath, because he could have said "I paid," making him one who admits partially. The Bach also raised this difficulty. One must say that it speaks where he claims against the divorcee at the time of the divorce, and at that time he has no claim of payment, because we hold a person does not pay before the time. Although generally when the lender lost the document the borrower can say "I paid" even within the time, that is only when there is a weakness before us, for the document is not in his hand; but if it is known that it was lost through ones, he cannot say he paid within the time. Here it speaks where it was lost through ones, and she also has strength because the ketubah is a condition of beit din. According to this, one can say the Maggid Mishneh does not disagree, but speaks where we do not know whether it was lost through ones. The Rama brought the Maggid Mishneh as disagreeing with the first view, which is surprising. I later found that the Taz says the same as I have written. See siman 100, gloss to seif 6.
