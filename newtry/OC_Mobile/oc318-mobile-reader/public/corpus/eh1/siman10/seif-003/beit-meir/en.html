One who divorces, etc. — they tell him. For the first opinion, the three in seif 3 is always the opinion of the RaMaH — they must always say this regarding an ailonit because of degradation; and regarding bad reputation and vows, even though he did not say of himself "because of this I am divorcing you" and there is no degradation — nevertheless beit din tells her in order to warn her, and automatically there is degradation, and they must also say "know," etc. This is the view of the Maggid and RaMaH, and not like Beit Yosef; and Chelkat Mechokek already brought proof for the Maggid from Perush HaMishnayot. But it states in responsum of Mahari Peretz siman 34: if he did not say, she is permitted to return to him; and he wrote this is the agreement of most poskim, like the first version of Rabbeinu Nissim — and then too one need not tell him "know," etc., except one who divorces because he sees blood at every time of relations — for she is always forbidden to him, even if he divorces stam, and they must also say "know." See responsum of the Gaon siman 11 — meaning all this is only when he divorces of his own accord. But if beit din tells him "you are obligated to divorce her" — forbidden to return her according to all, and then they must also tell him "know," etc.; and it requires investigation, for the poskim do not appear so closed, and see siman 117 in Beit Yosef. Nevertheless the Ran's question and his answer in the law of one who divorces a woman who has no regular period seems to imply the opposite.
