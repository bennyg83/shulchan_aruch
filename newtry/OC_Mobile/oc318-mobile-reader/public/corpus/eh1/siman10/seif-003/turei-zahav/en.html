He may not return. In the Gemara there are two versions. In the first version the reason is because of degradation — lest she degrade herself against her husband; Rashi explained: after she marries another and he will say "had I known the matters were false or that the vow has annulment I would not have divorced her," and so I said at the time of the get "because of this I am divorcing you" — and then it was in my mind to return you if they are found false, and the get is found void; therefore they say "know" that you may never return her, and he is no longer believed to say so — since he knew it is forbidden to return her even if she did not marry, and he did not care to wait and investigate after the matters — he revealed his intent that she was not dear to him. But if he did not say so initially at the time of the get he cannot degrade — for he did not say initially that he divorces her because of bad reputation and vow; and since he cannot degrade, if she married they did not forbid him to return her until she married — end quote Rosh. And the latter version holds the reason he may not return is a penalty — lest the daughters of Israel be promiscuous in immorality or vows; therefore he must tell her "know that because of this I am divorcing." It turns out per this statement is not needed except to warn her — but he cannot return her even if he did not say so and did not warn her; nevertheless the penalty of him forbids her return, and this statement which is necessary for the prohibition — in the first version it must be with doubled condition, for Rava established this tanna holds like R' Meir who said we require doubled condition in every condition; and perforce the statement here does not forbid him because of degradation except if he doubled to say "were it not for this I would not be divorcing you" — but without doubling there is no insistence to fulfill his words. The Rosh ruled like the first version — and this first "some say" here in Shulchan Aruch; and the second is the Rambam's view that doubling is not required, for he did not mention it at all, but wrote in ch. 10 of Gerushin: one who divorces his wife because of bad reputation or because she was promiscuous in vows — they tell him: inform her for what reason you divorce her in order to warn her, and know you may never return her; and why may he never return her? A decree lest she marry another and repent and the first will say "had I known it is so I would not have divorced her," and it is found like divorcing conditionally and the condition was not fulfilled, and the get is void retroactively — end quote. And it is questioned how he ruled against Rava who concluded we require doubled condition for the Sages who hold like R' Meir that we require doubled condition; and in Perush HaMishnah the Rambam wrote halakhah is like R' Yehudah who does not require doubled condition — and so Maggid Mishneh brought it in Beit Yosef; except he challenged him — for the Rambam ruled in ch. 6 of Ishut halakhah like R' Meir that we require doubled condition; and previously Beit Yosef explained the sugya conversely — he did not say his establishment except as general sharpness, but in truth of the matter it deals even without doubling his condition; and if so there is wonder here that Beit Yosef's words contradict each other — since per truth it must require doubling, and this is against his words in ch. 6 of Ishut that Beit Yosef himself mentioned; aside from what is strained to say Rava's words are only for sharpness. And in truth it seems Beit Yosef's words here are only general sharpness; and Maharachal strained for this to explain that R' Yehudah in truth does not dispute R' Meir generally, but here from stringency they said even though he did not double doubly — all this is not worth it to me. Rather in my humble opinion certainly the Rambam in his composition retracted from what he wrote in Perush HaMishnah where he wrote halakhah is like R' Yehudah that doubling is required — rather halakhah is like R' Meir as he stated explicitly in Hilchot Ishut; and the closing the Rambam deals with is when he doubled; and that he did not mention this in Perush is because he gave the reason it is like divorcing conditionally, which is explained in his words in the place that is the source of the law of condition that we require doubling — and he did not need to mention it here since he mentioned condition, the whole law of condition applies; and that which is mentioned in the Gemara in four doublings supports our words — for where it deals without doubling they needed to explain, not so where stam was as explicit in doubling. Behold the Rambam brings in his language "and in order to warn her" — which is the latter version; and afterward he mentioned concern for degradation — and this is in the way that the latter version is stricter than the first version which said because of degradation; automatically the prohibition is only when he says at the time of divorce "because of bad reputation I am divorcing you" — meaning with doubling; and if he did not say so there is no prohibition. And in the first version they hold that nevertheless it is forbidden because of promiscuity; therefore one must inform her. And the Rambam ruled like the first that statement is required; and in truth statement suffices even not in her presence but before witnesses — for in this too there is degradation; except it is better that the statement be in her presence in order to warn her per the reason of the latter version — but the prohibition does not depend on this; and however the statement is, in any case doubling is required like every condition, and he need not mention it here. And therefore when the Tur brought the RaMaH's view who wrote doubling is not required — once suffices — the Tur did not mention that this is the Rambam's view for the reason I mentioned. It turns out the second "some say" mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch is (not) the Rambam's view.
And there is one who says even if he did not tell her, etc. He explained at the time of the get; but before that at least he said so — and so Rashi explained on what Rav Yosef said: that he told her "because of bad reputation," etc., at the time of divorce; but the Rif did not bring this of Rav Yosef — automatically he did not say so at the time of the get suffices; and it seems this "there is one who says" is the Rif.
