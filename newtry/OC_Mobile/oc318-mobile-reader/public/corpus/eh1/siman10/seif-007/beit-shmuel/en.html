He may take her back. We are not concerned for later undermining of the get. Bach wrote the reason: everyone knows that a vow can be released. This is strained, for where she vowed we are concerned that he may say, "Had I known that either I or the sage could release it..." In Derishah he wrote the reason: he cannot undermine her, for he vowed only to divorce her, and he could have taken her back immediately after the get, yet did not take her back. According to this, if he vowed to divorce her forever and divorced her because of the vow, then later undermining would apply if a release from his vow were later found. Taz answers that here later undermining does not apply, for he did not say, "Because I think there is no release for my vow, I divorce you." Therefore there is no undermining, since he cannot say, "Had I known there was release for my vow, I would not have divorced you"; even if that were so, there is no undermining, for he divorced her without any condition. This is unlike where he says, "Because of this I divorce you," which is as if he stipulated a condition that if this matter is not so, the get is not a get.
