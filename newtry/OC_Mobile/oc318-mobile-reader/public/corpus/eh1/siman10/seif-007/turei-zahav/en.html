And R' Yossi bar Yehudah: an incident also in Tzaidan — one who said to his wife "Konam all fruits upon me if I do not divorce you" — and the Sages permitted him to return her because of tikkun olam. And we challenge: they permitted — plain — for what do we concern for her? Because of R' Natan — as it is taught: R' Natan says whoever vows is as if he builds a bamah, and one who fulfills it and is not asked to permit it is as if he offered a sacrifice on it — he completes his sin whole; and this one who fulfilled his vow and divorced her — they are penalized regarding return — teaches us. And we challenge: what tikkun olam is here? There is here, said Ravina: this is what it means — there is no here because of tikkun olam; and Rashi explained: they permitted me to return her because of tikkun olam that no prohibition is found here — end quote. And his explanation implies: even though initially when she was divorced because she vowed it is forbidden to return her because of tikkun olam — meaning because of degradation; but here when she vowed there is no concern of tikkun olam such as degradation — no prohibition is found here, and one must understand: if there is no concern of prohibition why did the tanna need to teach us this matter? Perforce one must say there is a side of prohibition because of degradation — meaning she will marry another and find an opening to annul his vow and he would not have needed to divorce her first, and he will say "had I known there is an opening for my vow I would not have divorced her and would have permitted the vow" — teaches us that specifically when she vowed there is this concern of degradation, since there is a defect in her we say he divorced with this intent; not so when he divorces because of his vow to divorce. And accordingly (question why he challenges) initially plain — why not answer that it teaches us this matter that we mentioned that there is a difference between her vow and his vow in this. And I saw in Perishah: he may return her — there is no concern for degradation — so much Rashi; and per his words: behold it was in his power to return her immediately after the get and he fulfilled his vow; and since he did not return her certainly he did not desire her; therefore if he said at the time of the get "had she not vowed I would not have divorced her" — nevertheless there is no concern for degradation. Accordingly if she vowed she is forbidden to him forever, and at the time of the get he also said he divorces because of this — also forbidden to return her because he can degrade saying "had I known there is an opening for my vow I would not have divorced her" — end quote. The author waives the honor of the rabbi — his words are not correct in what he wrote: since he did not return her certainly these matters cannot be said; certainly in concern if she marries another there is no difference whether she was betrothed immediately after or waited some time — and he will degrade saying "I desired to return her but she hurried to marry another and did not wait for me, and had I known this I would not have divorced her." And the matter is clear in the plain explanation that there is no proof at all from what he did not return after the get — neither in divorce because of her vow or his vow; rather the difference between them is that when she vowed — if bad reputation spread about her or she is an ailonit — meaning she did not bring signs — in these a blemish applies; but in his vow annulment is possible, and bad reputation is possible it will be explained it is false, as Rashi explained whom I brought at the beginning of this seif; and for the Rambam: lest she repent. And for an ailonit perhaps she will still bring signs — and these matters he remembered at the time of the get; and especially if he doubled them it was like giving a get on an actual condition, as the Rambam wrote; and it is plain that in every condition if it was not fulfilled the act of the get is nothing and it is as if it was not given at all. So too this matter in the eyes of the husband — since the get was given when he thought she was an hour because of bad reputation or she was an ailonit or she vowed and he does not desire a niddah; and since this is false and the vow has annulment — automatically it is as if she did not vow at all; if so he will say the get has no effect and she is still his wife — all this if she does not marry, the first returns her without new kiddushin. So was my opinion; and with this the words of Rashi are settled who wrote "and when I divorced you it was in my mind to return you" — for seemingly questioned: is it a decree in him lest she not agree to marry him again since he already shamed her? And per the words it is settled that she was compelled to return to him since the get did not take effect; and for this he relied at the time of giving the get. But regarding his vow to divorce her — he thought annulment is impossible and because of this he gave and fulfilled his vow; except that in the end it became known to him annulment is possible — nevertheless it took effect since he did not actually annul and was obligated to do so; and annulment of his act does not apply here, and condition does not apply here — perforce there is no degradation. With this — and in truth if he said at the time of the get "had I known there is annulment for my vow I would not have divorced you" — in this certainly similar to her vow, it was like conditionally; and whenever it became known to him there is annulment the get itself is void — not so here where he did not say at the time of the get but the vow brings him to this; even though he said "had there not been the vow I would not have divorced you" — this is not included that there will not be a get if annulment is possible; for it can be that even though annulment is effective nevertheless he does not want to nullify his words because of annulment; and also if in his heart annulment is impossible — matters of the heart are not matters. With this the plain explanation in the Gemara is settled that stated ma'aseh beTzaidan — one who vowed, etc., and they permitted him, etc. — for in truth also when she vowed or bad reputation and ailonit — if he divorced her she is permitted to return to him before she married another; except the Sages made a prohibition to return her since degradation is possible if she marries — not so when he vowed, the Sages did not make a prohibition since there is no concern for degradation as we said — rather it remains as Torah law. If so what does it teach that they permitted to return her? And it answers there is one opinion they are penalized since he did not seek to permit it; and afterward the tanna himself explained the matters we mentioned that he said there is no here because of tikkun olam; and Rashi explained no prohibition is found here for the Sages because there is no concern at all as we mentioned — and this is the tanna's main intent that stated in another case when she vowed; but if he vowed, etc. — it implies they are seemingly equal and we are from concern of degradation as I mentioned. And in what he concluded in Perishah: if she vowed she shall be forbidden to him forever, etc. — I do not know a place for his words; certainly if she forbade him eternal prohibition — meaning Konam your relations upon me — he cannot return after divorce without concern without degradation except because of his vow in his explanation. And if it deals that afterward he finds an opening on his vow — certainly he may return her, for there is no concern for degradation at all — the get is a complete get as we mentioned. All this seems clear and plain after examination, with God's help.
