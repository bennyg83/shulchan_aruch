(ב) He had a son, etc. Throughout the Shulchan Aruch, Beit Yosef is cited here in the name of Responsa Rav Hai, and this is what the Mechaber wrote nearby in seif 10. In Darkei Moshe in this siman he brought only this responsum of Rav Hai. One can say that he learned from there, for it states there: how can we say that he made her an apotropos over his property, when he gave the two what he wished? And if you say that there is one to whom he gave nothing, since Torah inheritance stands even with one of his sons, we learn that if he had only one son and gave him a little, we say that if he wished to make him an apotropos he would not have given to him. The reason we do not say here that he upheld biblical inheritance, I wrote nearby. However, in Choshen Mishpat, Beit Yosef brought a responsum of Rashba that wrote this law, and one can say that he learned this law from there. So too Chelkat Mechokek wrote. It appears from Rashba's responsum that even if he gave something to his wife, nevertheless she is not treated as apotropos.
