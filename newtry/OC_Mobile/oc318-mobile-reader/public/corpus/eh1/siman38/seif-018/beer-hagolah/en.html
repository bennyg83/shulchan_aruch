This refers to the earlier division, where he said to her, "on condition that I show you." In my humble opinion, what is written below in section 35, regarding one who says, "on condition that I give you, etc.," that a scrupulous person should be concerned for himself, etc., requires analysis; see there. One may distinguish: when he says, "on condition that I give you," even though he has not given it when she demands it, one can say that she still wants to fulfill his condition, and he is merely delaying so as to profit further with the money in her hand. This is unlike the case of "on condition that I show you": once she demands it and he does not show it, then either way, if he has it and does not show it, he is as one who says that he does not intend to fulfill the condition, and if he does not have it, she need not wait and remain chained until he earns it, for she was not betrothed to him with that in mind. So it appears to me.
