On condition I speak for you. Rashal wrote: the mishnah teaches here precision in specifying; but for wage of speech alone she is not betrothed — hiring extends from beginning to end and it is betrothal through a loan. Rosh in name of Tosafot challenged: what does the mishnah teach? They answered: if he spoke to the authorities she is betrothed and she cannot say to those matters I did not want; likewise in labor. Tur brought this and wrote afterward in Tosefta: if he spoke in the manner of speakers she is betrothed — implies the matters are known he must speak about her. His intent: Tosefta does not hold like Tosafot — for it requires known matters specifically; if so one cannot say she cannot say to those matters I did not want for she can certainly say so; if matters are not known there is no kiddushin since it must be established with known matters. Beit Yosef challenged Tosafot's challenge — what does mishnah teach? Rashi comes to answer the simple challenge that she is betrothed with a perutah; Tosafot come to answer what mishnah teaches: if he fulfills the condition she is betrothed — this is obvious; if to teach hiring extends etc. it should teach briefly that betrothal for wage of speech is not kiddushin — rather it teaches this is fulfillment of condition and she cannot say to this matter I did not want. Maggid's words; with this Choshen Mishpat and Beit Shmuel's words fall away that Beit Yosef's words are not compelled — with unknown matters she is betrothed even if she does not need them; per Turei Zahav if so establish mishnah with unknown matters — and why strain to establish with known matters — Beit Yosef's words are compelled: with known matters she is not betrothed since she can say she wanted those matters.
