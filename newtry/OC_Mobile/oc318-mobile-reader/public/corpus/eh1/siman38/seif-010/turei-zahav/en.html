Therefore forever there is doubt until the father dies. Tur wrote: if the son died even if the father said I do not consent — she performs chalitzah or undergoes yibbum; the reason she may marry: if he says yes she is his brother's wife and yibbum is proper; if he will not say yes she is like any other woman without kiddushin from the first. Maharshal challenged: why chalitzah — lest the father die and not say yes, then chalitzah is not chalitzah and she is permitted to a kohen and one may err that chalitzah permits a kohen — see siman 157 regarding a woman who goes out with her husband for this reason; due to this challenge he wrote truly she does not perform chalitzah rabbinically, here he spoke only Torah law. I say there is no challenge: per teshuvah siman 157 and here in any case our sugya is that kiddushin condition is known to all and they know the condition was not fulfilled; therefore chalitzah was valid; likewise Rashi Yevamot 95 s.v. forever on the end — if due to brother's wife the condition was in first kiddushin and not fulfilled; apparently when people see some change and prohibition they assume condition in kiddushin — all the more here where the condition was truly before witnesses and listeners that the father did not say yes and the condition was not fulfilled — no destruction at all.
And some say if he said yes once etc. — question: this "some say" is Tur's words from on condition he say yes — even if he said I consent it helps afterward to say yes; this too is Tur in name of RaMAh. Apparently certainly per reasoning whoever says yes once can no longer protest — this "some say." But for on condition he say yes there is challenge: why need stipulate extra condition — suffices if he not protest or is silent; why need explicit yes — what does he gain? Perhaps intent: as long as he says yes the kiddushin stand even if before he said no; unlike on condition he be silent — if he said no, silence or yes afterward does not help since he protested when he heard — forever doubt if he later says yes. Rashal answered: on condition he not protest is passive condition — father does act of saying yes fulfilling it; but on condition he say yes is active condition — even if he first said no he cannot void the first act. Rashal's answer seems essential as we find below in Tur in name of RaMAh — therefore he said on condition I show you and not on condition I have — likewise one can be precise as I wrote.
