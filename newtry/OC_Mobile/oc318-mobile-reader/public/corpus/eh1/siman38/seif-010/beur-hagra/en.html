One who says on condition etc. Tosefta: on condition so-and-so consent — even if he said I do not consent she is betrothed lest he consent later until he says I consent; and if he said I consent she is betrothed and R' Eliezer says she is not betrothed on condition that the father consent who said the father I do not consent she is betrothed lest the father consent later. And this is why etc. and likewise perhaps etc.
And one who says on condition etc. Gemara there — except on condition he be silent — establish the end etc.
However if etc. As explained s.k. 45 part 2 lest the father come and protest — Rivash.
And if he protested. As explained — on condition he be silent means at time of hearing alone; and as Ran wrote he cannot forever for he cannot cease protesting entirely. And this is what "establish the end teach him" etc. without this it is fine and we are not concerned lest he consent afterward; and one can say that if at first we establish on condition he say yes — also difficult "what teach him" etc. likewise; except the middle section is also difficult — Ran and Rashba.
On condition he not protest etc. and even if etc. Therefore we do not establish the first section also on condition he not protest — impossible "the father consented she is betrothed" and what is "consented" that he said no — nevertheless he can return and protest — Rosh; and likewise Rashba and Ran; and some say. But the view of the poskim is as written in Shulchan Aruch; and the rule Ran wrote: anything without a fixed time he can do forever — whether active or passive condition; he can do the act in active condition to fulfill the condition and in passive to void the condition forever even if he did the opposite at first or end — except on condition he be silent which at time of hearing alone for he cannot be silent forever as explained.
