But its straps do not need to be leather. So wrote the Tur. The Beit Yosef understood that the Tur disagrees with the Nimukei Yosef, who wrote that straps are validated only if they are of hair that comes from leather, and not of other materials. According to the Tur, even other materials are valid. So too the Rama holds. The "some say" he brought, who hold that its straps must also be leather, means something that comes from leather. But it is not necessary to say this. Rather, one can say they do not disagree: the Tur’s intent is that they do not need to be leather itself, but hair is valid since it comes from leather. So wrote the Bach. It appears that this is proven from the Talmud. If one would think that straps of any other material are valid, then the verse "tachash" would apply only to the body of the sandal. If so, what did the Gemara ask there: if so, all of it of hair should also be valid? From where do we know that hair is called "tachash"? Perhaps hair is not called "tachash," but we do not care if the straps are of other materials, while a shoe of hair is invalid because hair is not called a shoe. So too, if the majority of the shoe is of hair it is invalid, as written in Shiltei Gibborim. If the stitching is of hair, one can also say that it is invalid, as written in Shiltei Gibborim there regarding the reason that linen is invalid, because everything follows the thing that holds it together, and it is as if all of it is hair. However, one can say it is valid, because even if all of it were hair it would be like leather, except that it is invalid because it is not called a shoe. But if it is leather and only the stitches are hair, it is called a shoe and valid. If the shoe can be walked in without tying, then it appears that according to all opinions it is valid if the straps are of other materials, as written below in the name of the Rosh. See there.
