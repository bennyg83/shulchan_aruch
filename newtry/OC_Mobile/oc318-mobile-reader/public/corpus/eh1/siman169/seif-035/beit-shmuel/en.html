And to tie it below the knee. But if the knots are above his knee, it is invalid. According to the Yerushalmi’s explanation, which distinguishes between knots below the knee and knots above the knee, this speaks where the foot was cut off. But if the foot is whole, the shoe fits him, and he can walk in it, then even if the knots are above the knee it is valid. According to this, it appears that even if the foot was cut off but enough of the lower leg remains that he can wear a shoe and hold it on his lower leg without tying, then even if he ties it above the knee it is valid, just as if his foot were there. Rather, it speaks where only a small part of his lower leg remains and it cannot stand without tying. See Responsa Mahara ben Sasson, simanim 142 and 143, regarding a yavam who is bent and walks on the side of his feet and sometimes presses his heel into the ground, that he performs chalitzah. See what I wrote in the name of Maharshdam: one who walks on his lower leg performs chalitzah if there is no other brother. See Responsa Rashach, part 2, siman 52, where it is established that removing most of the heel is chalitzah. Therefore, if the shoe tore at the side of the heel, even though he can walk in it, the chalitzah is invalid. See Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah.
