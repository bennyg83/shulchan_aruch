And there is one who says. See Responsa Perach Shoshan, klal 2, siman 2 and 3, who noted that here Maran brought the words of the Rambam plainly, and the view of the Ra'avad in the name of "there is one who says." This proves that his view is to rule like there, that in every case she should not perform chalitzah lechatchilah. But in Seder Chalitzah, seif 17, it sounds that he ruled plainly like the Ra'avad. One should also wonder at the Rama, of blessed memory, who wrote in siman 172, seif 15 in the gloss: "It appears to me that if there is no brother here," etc. He discusses this at length and concludes that regarding practical halacha one should not move from the words of Maran in Seder Chalitzah and the above gloss of Moram, that where there is no brother there except him, or because of some other cause, she may perform chalitzah from him lechatchilah, for anything impossible is considered like bedieved.
