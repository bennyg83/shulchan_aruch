It is valid. Even if she did not spit at all, it is valid. Rather, this teaches that one should not say she must spit again, but that this spitting is enough. It appears that even regarding disqualifying her to the brothers, meaning where she did nothing but spit before the beit din and they did not see it, she is disqualified to the brothers. Tosafot wrote there on the baraita, "one who performs chalitzah from a blind man, it is valid," and asked: from where would one think to exclude a blind man? They answered that since it is written "before the eyes of the elders," one might think the same applies to the eyes of the yavam. Alternatively, since it is written "and she shall spit before him," it is like "before his eyes"; therefore it teaches otherwise. The Bach inferred from Tosafot’s words that they hold that if the judges did not see the spittle, it is invalid, because it is written "before the eyes of the elders." This is not precise, for certainly according to everyone Rava’s derivation that the judges must see the spittle is only an asmachta, since "and she shall spit" refers to "before him" and does not refer back to "before the eyes of the elders." The intent of Tosafot in the first answer is that one might have thought from "before the eyes of the elders" regarding removal of the shoe that the same applies to the eyes of the yavam, and it would be invalid because he did not see the removal of the shoe; therefore it teaches otherwise. According to the second answer, one might have thought that since it is written "before him," it is as if it said "and she shall spit before his eyes." If it had written "and she shall spit before his eyes," it would obviously be indispensable. But regarding the judges, where it is not written that she must spit before their eyes, it is obvious that it is not indispensable. One cannot ask according to Tosafot from where one would think to invalidate when he is blind because he did not see the spitting, since we hold that spitting is not indispensable. One must say that he must be fit for mixing and fit to see the spittle. It implies from these Tosafot that they hold that if the yavam is blind, he should not perform chalitzah lechatchilah. See seif 48, where it is explained that according to the Rambam and Ra'avad, the yavam too must see the spittle, and so it implies from these Tosafot.
