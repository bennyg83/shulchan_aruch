An offering to avodah zarah. The Maggid explains that if the shoe itself is the offering, there is no prohibition, for we hold that an offering to avodah zarah is only something poured or offered like sacrificial service inside. Rather, it speaks where he slaughtered the animal before avodah zarah and made a shoe from its hide. So it is in the Talmud, Perek Rabbi Yishmael, and in all the decisors, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 139. One must force Rashi’s language to align with this as well: what he wrote, "that he offered and handed it over to avodah zarah," refers to the animal and not to the shoe.
