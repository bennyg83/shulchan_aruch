On the left foot. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 156, who wrote that although the law is plain in the Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and all the decisors, that if she performed chalitzah with the left foot, her chalitzah is invalid, and no decisor questioned this, I have room for analysis, as I wrote in my work Tzlach to Berachot, page 20, etc. He discusses this at length. The result of his words is that there is no clear halacha that it is invalid, since according to Rabbi Yochanan her chalitzah is valid, and we have no resolution from the words of the Gemara whether the halacha follows Rabbi Yochanan or Rava. The Rambam too did not mention this law, that if she performed chalitzah with the left foot her chalitzah is invalid, except regarding one whose right foot was cut off, and that is not because the left is invalid for chalitzah, but because we require that he be included in "he shall stand and say." But if his foot is not cut off, perhaps even if she performed chalitzah with the left it is valid. Therefore he joined this doubt of left-foot chalitzah to other doubts to create three doubts to permit, as will be brought below in seif 25, s.k. 24. See there. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 122, the responsum of his son-in-law, the Gaon Rabbi Moshe Sofer of blessed memory, who brought there in the name of the Gaon Rabbi Zalman Margolies that he found in the Ra'avan that if she performed chalitzah with the left foot, bedieved it is valid. Based on this he considered joining left-foot chalitzah to other grounds of leniency and concern of agunah. He, of blessed memory, analyzed his words and also the words of the above Noda BiYehudah, and concluded that this should not be used as any support anywhere to perform chalitzah with the left, even for one whose foot is cut off, and all the more so where it is not cut off. See there. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 71, and further Responsa Radbaz, part 5, siman 2095, in the responsum of the annotating Rav there. There he discusses at length what they said in Niddah, page 7, "Rabbi Eliezer is worthy to rely on in pressing circumstances," and the words of the Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 242, on this. See further there, siman 205, regarding this.
