A blind man does not perform chalitzah. The Rambam and Ra'avad hold that the yavam must see the spittle. Therefore lechatchilah he does not perform chalitzah. But according to the Ra'avad, if there is no brother other than him, it is like bedieved, since there is no remedy, as written in the gloss, seif 12. It requires explanation why regarding one who cannot stand, he rules plainly that he performs chalitzah sitting, while here he brings the Ra'avad’s view in the name of "there is one who says." Perhaps with a sick person he also cannot do yibbum, while here he can do yibbum. What he wrote, "and if he performed chalitzah it is valid," the Maggid wrote the reason is that we hold spitting is not indispensable. This does not appear correct, because we require it to be fit for mixing, as written above regarding reading. Rather, it appears that this derivation, that he must see the spittle, is only an asmachta, for it is written "and she shall spit before him," not "before his eyes." "Before him" means opposite his face, even if he does not see. See the Nimukei Yosef at the end of Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, who wrote that a blind man may perform chalitzah lechatchilah, because he does not need to see the spitting. It appears that regarding seeing the spittle one should be stringent lechatchilah. Therefore, if there is another brother, the other should perform chalitzah; if there is no other brother, this one performs chalitzah according to the Ra'avad.
