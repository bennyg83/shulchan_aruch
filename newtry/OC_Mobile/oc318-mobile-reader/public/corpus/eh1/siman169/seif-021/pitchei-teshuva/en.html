In the shape of a right foot. See Ba'er Heitev in the name of Shevut Yaakov, that it should be angled from the big toe, etc. See Sefer Kerem Shlomo, who brought another sign for the shape of a right foot in the name of Sefer Pachad Yitzchak, who wrote: Know further that when we need to perform chalitzah for one who is left-footed on his left foot, we have here in Ferrara a shoe prepared for the left foot. This is the difference: in a right shoe, the hooks are on the left side when the shoe is on the yavam’s foot, and the loops are on the right side. The long stitching is also on the right side, and on the left side is the smooth part without stitching. In a left shoe, the hooks are on the right side when the shoe is on the yavam’s foot, the loops are on the left side, the long stitching is on the left side, and on the right side is the smooth part without stitching. End quote. This is according to the custom of his place, where they make the entire shoe from one piece, like the view of the Magen Avraham above. But according to our custom through the Rama there, that it is made of two pieces, this sign of the long stitching does not apply. There is only a sign through the stitching on the upper of the shoe, as written in the previous s.k. See Sefer Zachor LeAvraham, letter chet, who wrote in the name of Mahari Abulafia of blessed memory that he was not particular about the view of the above Shevut Yaakov, but practiced recognition of the shoe through the hooks alone: on the right foot he would make the hooks on the left side, and he was not particular that the body of the shoe be angled. He discusses this somewhat at length and concludes that nevertheless it is good and proper to be concerned also for the view of Shevut Yaakov, so there should be no question. See there.
