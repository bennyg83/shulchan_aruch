If it was made of wood, etc. It is valid. This implies that specifically bedieved it is valid, and so it implies in the Talmud and in the Rambam. So too it is explicit in the Nimukei Yosef. The Rosh requires explanation, for he brought like Rashi that a sandal stitched with linen means linen cloth stitched inside, and he asked: why is it worse than a wooden sandal covered with leather? What is the question? According to the Rosh’s explanation, a sandal stitched with linen is bedieved valid. If so, perhaps according to Rashi too, if linen cloth was stitched inside, it is truly bedieved valid and is no worse than a wooden sandal covered with leather. One must say that since Rashi explained that one does not perform chalitzah with a stitched sandal because it is written "shoe," and in the dispute of Rabbi Meir regarding an amputee’s wooden leg it is proven that according to Rashi the derivation from "shoe" is not an asmachta but a full derivation, as written in s.k. 10, it implies that according to Rashi even bedieved it is invalid. Therefore he asked: what is different? Why is it worse than a wooden sandal? I later saw that the Bach, seif 7, resolves it differently. This law of covered with leather is learned from the explanation of the father of Shmuel, as written there, page 103. I do not know why the Maggid wrote that they learned it from Abaye’s explanation.
