That one should cut into the shoe. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 154, who wrote regarding the chalitzah shoe: When I came here, I found that their shoe was cut at its back into three pieces, and I changed it to two pieces, as I saw in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 12 of Yevamot, siman 7. I said that it is enough to minimize the pieces and not add a third one without purpose, because with many pieces there is certainly concern of a torn and loose shoe. See Sefer Korban Netanel, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, letter 8. This is especially so because the third piece is empty work without need. Regarding the wrapping of the straps, the custom is to begin from the front to the back, and the first wrapping begins behind the leg, meaning in the manner of tying in order to tighten the shoe on the foot. End quote. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 87, who wrote to one Rav regarding a chalitzah shoe: I am sending it as requested. Know that two holes were cut behind it through which the strap passes, and it is also cut in front so that the shinbein is exposed in front and also behind the shinbein, as written in the Derishah, etc. Some have the custom to make only one very wide notch behind it, so that the straps meet it twice, as written in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 59. To me it is preferable to make two narrow notches and not one wide one, so that the air on this side and that side not come and nullify the remaining strip at the top of the shoe’s heel, making it like a torn and loose shoe, similar to what Maharil wrote, brought in Magen Avraham, siman 16, etc. So I saw the earliest elders do this, and so the shoe is illustrated in Sefer Chok LeYisrael, from which one can learn that in Prague they practice this way. See there further, that one great Rav innovated to make another two holes like square windows on the two sides of the shoe, two on the right and two on the left, aligned between the notches behind it. He derived this from the words of the Mordechai at the beginning of Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah. He, of blessed memory, discusses this at length in explaining the words of the Mordechai, and also brought the words of the explanation of Seder Chalitzah in the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, seif 59, and the words of Maharshal, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, siman 7. He concludes that one should not make holes or notches on the sides, only two notches behind it. However, they should be somewhat long, so that they extend a little to both sides of the foot, because the fold made in the straps also extends somewhat to the sides of the foot. This appears correct to me. See there.
