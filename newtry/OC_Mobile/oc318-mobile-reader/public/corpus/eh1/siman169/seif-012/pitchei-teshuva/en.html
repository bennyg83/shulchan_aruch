And their examination was explained, etc. See Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 7. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger of blessed memory, Orach Chaim, siman 7, whose view is to be stringent regarding examining the man even where the years are known and there is Rava’s presumption. Nevertheless, he should be examined by two witnesses. Although regarding examination of the yevamah, examination by women is effective, even by one woman, there is no proof from examination of the yevamah. First, one must be concerned for the approach of the Rif and Rambam that the chalitzah of a minor girl is invalid only rabbinically, see Beit Shmuel, s.k. 49, unlike chalitzah of a minor boy, which is certainly biblically invalid. Chalitzah is called a matter of ervah, as the Nimukei Yosef wrote in chapter 12 of Yevamot. Furthermore, even otherwise there is a distinction: specifically examination of a minor girl is a matter of disgrace to examine through men, and therefore even one woman is believed. This is unlike examination of a minor boy, which can easily be performed by two witnesses. See there, and what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 281, s.k. 7. However, one can say that this applies specifically to examination for two hairs. But regarding examination for many hairs, where the reason one witness is believed is because it is a matter that will be revealed, as written in Darkei Moshe and Beit Shmuel, s.k. 7, one can say that he too agrees that there is no need to be stringent, at least where the years are known. See what I wrote in my commentary to Seder Chalitzah there.
