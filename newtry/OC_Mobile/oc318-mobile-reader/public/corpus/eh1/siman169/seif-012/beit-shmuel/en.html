For the mitzvah of chalitzah is standing. Because it resembles judgment. If the yavam is weak or old, they give him a cane in his hand to lean on. According to the Rambam and Nimukei Yosef in the name of many decisors, even lechatchilah the removal of the shoe may be done while sitting. One cannot ask according to those decisors who hold that lechatchilah the mitzvah is standing, and who also hold that what Ameimar said, that he must press his foot down, means lechatchilah but bedieved it is not indispensable: if so, what is the Gemara’s challenge against Ameimar from the baraita, "whether standing or sitting"? The Gemara initially thought that while sitting he does not press his foot down. What was difficult? One could say that the baraita speaks bedieved. So the Nimukei Yosef also asked. One must say that they hold the baraita necessarily speaks lechatchilah, since it teaches "whether standing." If so, what it teaches, "sitting and leaning," is also lechatchilah. The Gemara initially thought that while leaning he does not press his foot down. One should not ask how it could initially think that sitting is lechatchilah, since the mitzvah of chalitzah is lechatchilah standing. One can say that it thought that although lechatchilah they must stand, this is because it is like judgment, and they need to stand because she collects her ketubah, which is like judgment. But because of the mitzvah of chalitzah itself, even lechatchilah sitting would be permitted. So too, if the judges stood, it is valid, for it is one reason.
