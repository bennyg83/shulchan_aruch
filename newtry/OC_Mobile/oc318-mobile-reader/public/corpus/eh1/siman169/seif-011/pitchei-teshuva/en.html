See Ba'er Heitev in the name of Maharival, who wrote that this is specifically not at the time of the act, etc. The root of this matter is from the words of the Rivash, siman 182, brought in Beit Yosef above, beginning of siman 157, who wrote that it appears to him from the language of the Rif in Perek HaCholetz regarding "and they identified them," that a woman and relative are not believed when they testify this at the time of the act, etc. Maharival concludes there that it is proper to be stringent like the Rivash, for so it appears from the precision of the Rif’s language. See there. However, the Beit Yosef already wrote there regarding the Rivash that it does not appear so from the words of the decisors; rather, even if they said it only at the time of the act, we rely on them, etc. See there. Also in Sefer Get Pashut above, siman 120, s.k. 14, he mentioned these words of Maharival and wrote that his inference from the Rif’s language can be rejected, etc. So wrote the Ra'anach, part 1, siman 89. Also in Sefer Bnei Shmuel, siman 49, he inferred from the Tur that even if they said it only at the time of the act, we rely on them. So it also appears from the words of the Rosh, Ra'avad, and Rashba, etc. End quote. See there, and what I wrote above, siman 142, end of s.k. 7. What the Ba'er Heitev wrote in the name of Rashdam, that even from writing it is possible that it is effective, see below in Seder Chalitzah in brief, where Beit Meir, s.k. 25, wrote plainly that it is effective. See Sefer Beit Meir, who wrote: an incident came before me involving a poor woman who came from forty parsaot away to seek out her yavam for chalitzah. Both came before me, and when I investigated who recognized her, there was not a single person who recognized her. Even the yavam had never seen her and did not recognize her. I said, if so, it is impossible for her to perform chalitzah. She answered that she had writings with her; perhaps they would help. I read a letter of recommendation from the judges of her city asking that people assist her to obtain chalitzah from the deceased’s brother for free, and there it was clearly mentioned that this woman, named Gutka, had been the wife of Rabbi Yosef ben Mordechai Shvid, and this Rabbi Yosef was known to us as the yavam’s brother. At first glance this was sufficient. But I said that the above words of Rashdam were said only regarding a woman whose name is at least established, and we only lack knowledge whether she was the deceased’s wife. But in our case, what identification is there in the testimony that a woman named Gutka was the wife of the deceased? We still do not know that this woman’s name is so, etc. Therefore I instructed her to remain in our district until thirty days were completed, so she would be established by this name through being called and answering, as in siman 120, seif 3. Then one could rely on the writing in her hand. Although there was still room to doubt that perhaps the deceased’s wife selected this woman, whose name is like her own, for this purpose, one can say that in such a case we are not concerned for two Yosef ben Shimon even without joining the city name, etc. He discusses this at length there to explain the view of Rav Aharon ben Asmael, brought by the Beit Yosef and in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 37, who holds that one does not need to recognize that this is the deceased’s wife. He brought proofs for his words and concludes: from all this it appears that in a truly pressing circumstance, where without this there are those who are lenient even regarding recognition of the man, perhaps there is room to rely on Rav Aharon at least regarding her recognition. But in our case it is not a pressing circumstance at all, because I found a remedy for her by establishing her name for thirty days. Heaven forbid to be lenient in something in which all the great early authorities were stringent. But to be concerned further that perhaps this woman’s name is the same as hers, one can at least rely in this on joining the view of Rav Aharon, etc. And so the act was done, that after thirty days passed she performed chalitzah before me. End quote. See there, and what I wrote above, siman 120, s.k. 12. See Sefer Beit Hillel, who wrote: The Or Zarua wrote that in a pressing circumstance, such as where the yavam wants to go overseas and there are no witnesses here who recognize him, they should perform chalitzah even though they do not recognize him. This requires study. This is brought in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 35, in the gloss; see there and what I wrote above, siman 142, s.k. 7. I once saw a man and woman come together at the state assembly to perform chalitzah, and no one recognized them. They were given chalitzah at the state assembly, and at that time there were several Geonim there, and they did not protest against the Rav arranging the chalitzah. End quote. The implication of his language is that this incident too was a pressing circumstance. So wrote Responsa Bigdei Yesha, siman 30. See also Responsa Ma'amar Mordechai, simanim 82 and 83, at length.
