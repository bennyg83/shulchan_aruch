They teach her, etc. This teaching applies to the entire verse, so that she will be accustomed to reading it. Afterward he says that she should be able to read "lo avah" in one breath. Even if she did not read it in one breath, we hold that anything fit for mixing is not invalidated by lack of actual mixing. The "there is one who says" adds that he too should be accustomed to read "I do not desire." But in the explanation of chalitzah, s.k. 66, he infers from the Rambam, who wrote "they teach her and the yavam," etc., that it refers to "I do not desire." According to this, I do not know what the "there is one who says" adds.
