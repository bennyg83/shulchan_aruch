In one breath. She should not pause between the word "lo" and the word "avah," so that one not come to say that "lo" refers to the beginning of the verse, where she said "my yavam refused to establish a name for his brother in Israel," meaning: he did not refuse, but rather my yavam desired. According to the "some say," she must say three words in one breath, so that she not say "lo avah" is the refusal and "yevami" is separate. Although the reading is not indispensable, that is when they did not read at all. But in this kind of case, where it may cause error, it is worse. According to the Rif and Rambam, it appears that when they say the reading is not indispensable, that is when she is fit to read according to the law; but if she cannot read in one breath and she did not read, it is indispensable. The Bach infers that one need not leave space between the alefs of "lo avah." If he is tongue-tied and cannot speak, he may perform chalitzah, since we know that this is his manner of speech. Maharik, siman 15.
