(א) It should have, etc. 102a: “we tie a cord to it,” etc. And there, 102b: “its tarsiyyot,” implying two, as stated explicitly in Shabbat 112a. However, Rashi there explained tarsiyyot differently, as the place where the straps are fixed. Nevertheless, it implies that there are two.
(ב) And some say, etc. From Bava Kamma 59b, Sanhedrin 74b, and Ta'anit 22a, and as Tosafot wrote there and there. See what is written there. But the Rif and the Rosh explain in Sanhedrin there that arkatta demesana means that gentiles make them red and Jews black. Even according to Tosafot’s explanation, this stringency has no basis. It is understandable to distinguish between a shoe and a sandal, since a shoe lechatchilah is a decree because of, etc. But regarding the shoe itself, since nowadays everyone walks with black straps, what concern is there? Although here in this siman he wrote several stringencies and compounded stringencies, this stringency has no basis and is actually a leniency.

(ו) And the straps should be, etc. As stated there, that it tightens more, etc., and in this way it is well tightened.
