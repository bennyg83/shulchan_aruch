And also that it is made of two pieces. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 112, regarding a chalitzah shoe in one city made of three pieces, meaning the heel surrounding the back of the foot was sewn to the upper that is over the foot, and the sole under the foot was a third piece. A certain scholar came and said that all chalitzot performed with this shoe were invalid, because the Rama of blessed memory wrote only that it should be made of two pieces, not three. He answered that the words of that scholar have no substance, and the matter is simple that it is valid even lechatchilah according to our practice of using a shoe sewn from two pieces, for there is no distinction between two and three. He brought clear proof from Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, siman 5, etc. He wrote: May it be His will that Jewish women not come to chalitzah, but if, Heaven forbid, they come before me, I will permit lechatchilah in such a case. End quote. See Sefer Zachor LeAvraham, Hilchot Chalitzah, letter 15, who wrote that regarding the leather of the sole, the custom is to make it harder than the leather of the upper. He wrote further that regarding the color, the upper of the shoe, the ears, hooks, and loops are white on the inside, not specifically white, but meaning they are not dyed; the reason is explained in Darkei Moshe, s.k. 14, and black on the outside. However, the sole and straps are completely white both inside and outside. He should sew the entire shoe with white leather. The stitches are on the outside, and likewise the hooks, loops, and straps are all on the outside, meaning they are inserted from the outside toward the inside by sewing. See there further that the stitching on the upper of the shoe is customarily sewn in the middle of the opening for placing the foot, toward the left side, meaning in a right shoe; in a left shoe it is the opposite. He further wrote that the straps should be sewn at the end of the loops and hooks, meaning on the open edge of the shoe through which the foot is inserted, between the end of the ears and the beginning of the body of the shoe. See there.
