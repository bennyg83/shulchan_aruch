See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 16 of Hilchot Edut, where he investigates the law that we hold that if one of them was found to be a relative or disqualified, then regarding testimony their testimony is void. What is the law regarding judges if the beit din had more than three and one was found to be a relative or disqualified? He wrote that from Rashi’s words in Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, Yevamot 104b, on the Mishnah that chalitzah with two or three where one of them was found to be a relative or disqualified is invalid, and Rashi explains that when one is found to be a relative or disqualified it turns out there are only two, it is clear that this is specifically with three, because if you remove the disqualified one, there are only two. But if there were more than three, her chalitzah is valid. However, from the words of the Nimukei Yosef it is clear that even if there are three without him, her chalitzah is invalid. End quote. See there. See Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 3, what he wrote about this, and see Sefer Netivot HaMishpat in Choshen Mishpat, siman 36, regarding this matter. See further in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 6, regarding an incident involving a woman whose husband died without children. He had two brothers, one adult fit to perform chalitzah and one minor of four years. With great difficulty she obtained chalitzah from the adult brother, and after the chalitzah it became known that two of the three judges were second-degree relatives through one husband. They wanted to compel the adult yavam to perform chalitzah again before valid judges, but they were uncertain, since the second chalitzah would be an invalid chalitzah that is not fit for yibbum because of the first chalitzah. If so, she would need to go back to all the brothers and would need to wait until the minor brother grows up, which was impossible for several reasons. He answered this at length and inclines to permit this woman to perform chalitzah a second time from this yavam, without needing to wait until the minor grows up. See there.
