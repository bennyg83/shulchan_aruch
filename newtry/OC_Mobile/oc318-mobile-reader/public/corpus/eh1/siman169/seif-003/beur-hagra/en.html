(א) And even if the two are ignoramuses. For they do not need to read aloud.
(ב) They performed chalitzah, etc. Meaning, regarding writing, this does not prevent validity, as stated there, 105b. See Tosafot there, s.v. lechatchilah.
(ג) And these two, etc. For even converts, who are valid in monetary law, are disqualified for these, as written on 101b, and all the more so relatives or those otherwise disqualified.
(ד) Specifically, etc. For they do not prevent validity, as above, and like three according to R. Shimon and R. Yosi HaSandlar there, 104b. So wrote the Beit Yosef: “And it appears to me,” etc. This is specifically where the three alone were designated. Otherwise, we hold at the end of the first chapter of Makkot that even if there are one hundred and one of them is found to be a relative or disqualified, the entire testimony is void. The same applies to judges, for we derive judges from testimony. So wrote Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Ritva, except that he wrote that in such a case it is valid even for writing, only that it was not respectful to Rav Shmuel to exclude him from the beit din. It appears that the Rav wrote simply because the custom is to designate him separately, as written in Seder Chalitzah, seif 12, and he holds unlike Nimukei Yosef, who validates such a case for writing. See Piskei Seder Chalitzah, seif 1, and so appears to be the main ruling.
