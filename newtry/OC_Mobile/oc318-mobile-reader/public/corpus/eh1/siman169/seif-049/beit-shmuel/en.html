Her chalitzah is invalid. There are variant versions of this Mishnah. According to our version, the Tanna taught: a minor girl who performed chalitzah should perform chalitzah again when she grows up, and if she did not perform chalitzah again, her chalitzah is invalid. Tosafot inferred from this: since it teaches "invalid," why did it teach "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up"? Rather, so that you should not say it is only rabbinically invalid chalitzah, it taught the extra language "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up" to teach that the chalitzah of a minor girl is nothing, like the chalitzah of a minor boy. From this we learn that just as we hold that the chalitzah of a minor boy does not disqualify, so too the chalitzah of a minor girl does not disqualify. Although it is stated in the Talmud that the Sages say "man" is written in the section and we do not compare woman to man, nevertheless for final halacha we do not hold like the Sages according to the conclusion, for Rava said there: the halacha is until she brings two hairs, meaning we do not hold like the Sages in this. So it is in the Semag, page 137a. The Rosh took this version as primary, and therefore it is proven from his words that the chalitzah of a minor girl is nothing and does not disqualify. This is what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh, equating the chalitzah of a minor girl to that of a minor boy. This is also the intent of the Beit Yosef in inferring from the above Tosafot that they hold the chalitzah of a minor girl does not disqualify. Although Tosafot wrote this according to Rabbi Meir, who holds that even the chalitzah of a minor boy disqualifies, nevertheless from Rabbi Meir one learns for the Sages, whom we hold like in this, that since the chalitzah of a minor boy does not disqualify, that of a minor girl also does not disqualify, because we learn from the extra language of the Mishnah that this comparison of a minor girl to a minor boy is biblical in this matter. With this all the astonishments of the Bach and Derishah against the Beit Yosef are settled, and what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh is also clear. The Beit Yosef also understood this way; his only wonder was that we do not find it explicit in the Rosh. According to the Rif, the version is: if she did not perform chalitzah after she grew up, it is valid. According to the Sages, who say we do not compare man to woman, then even lechatchilah one may perform chalitzah with a minor girl. What is concluded there, that the halacha is until she brings two hairs, means lechatchilah. The Rambam, who wrote that her chalitzah is invalid, appears to have taken our version, unlike the Maggid. To answer Tosafot’s question, why did it teach "she should perform chalitzah when she grows up," one can answer differently: although this comparison is rabbinic, nevertheless she requires another chalitzah when she grows up. According to this, there are three views: according to Tosafot’s approach, according to the Sages there is no chalitzah at all and it does not disqualify; according to the Rif, bedieved it is valid; according to the Rambam, it disqualifies and requires another chalitzah. But what the Maggid wrote, that even according to the Rif’s version, the conclusion there "the halacha is until she brings two hairs" means it is invalid even bedieved, appears very forced, since according to Rabbi Meir and according to the Rabbis, chalitzah is valid, so how would Rava rule like no one? At first glance, the Rambam is difficult, because he rules regarding a deaf-mute man and woman that their chalitzah is nothing and does not disqualify. The Maggid wrote that the reason is because we hold that throughout the Torah, a deaf-mute, insane person, and minor are equivalent. Therefore, in chalitzah too they are equal, and it does not disqualify, like the chalitzah of a minor boy. If so, why does the chalitzah of a minor girl disqualify? If the comparison of woman to man is only rabbinic, then even the chalitzah of a deaf-mute woman should disqualify. One must say that it speaks of a minor girl who has understanding, and then the disqualification is because "man" is written in the section and not a minor, even if he has understanding. The comparison of woman to man is only rabbinic, and therefore she disqualifies. This is unlike a deaf-mute man or woman and an insane person, who lack understanding, and therefore their chalitzah does not disqualify, as the Rambam wrote there in law 13 regarding a deaf-mute man and woman, that they are not of understanding. A minor boy, even if he has understanding, does not disqualify, because the Torah excluded him by writing "man." I later saw that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 225, also wrote that the Rambam’s version is like our version.
