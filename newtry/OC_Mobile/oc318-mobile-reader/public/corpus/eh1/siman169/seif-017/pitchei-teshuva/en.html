Not to examine. See Beit Shmuel, s.k. 10, who disagrees with the Bach, who wrote that we are not expert in examining depressions. This is not so: we are not expert regarding depressions without hairs, but if there are hairs and we only need to examine whether there are depressions under the hairs, we certainly are expert. The Taz in siman 155 also wrote this. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 93, who wondered about them, because it is clear from their words that at least with depressions without hairs we are not expert. But the root of this matter, that we are not expert in depressions, is from the Tur in siman 155 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, in Shulchan Aruch there, seif 22, and there it is not mentioned at all that even regarding depressions without hairs we are not expert. There he wrote that we are not expert to distinguish that there should be no depression. This means that the fact that we did not see depressions is no proof, for perhaps there are small depressions over which our eyesight has no control. Therefore he was stringent there that she may not do mi'un. But where we see depressions, even depressions alone without hairs, to say that our eyesight is not effective regarding what we see with our senses, we have not heard such a thing. He discusses this somewhat at length and concludes that without doubt we are expert regarding depressions. See there. In Sefer Kerem Shlomo he brought that in Responsa Beit Ephraim, siman 131, he concluded in the name of the Gaon author of Hafla'ah to be stringent like the Bach, that even to examine whether there are depressions under the hairs, we are not expert. See there. See the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 27, in the gloss of the Beit Shmuel there. See above, s.k. 15, and what I wrote in my commentary to Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 7.
