And some say regarding one whose foot was amputated. This speaks even though with a twisted foot we hold that the chalitzah is invalid, because there it is worse: since the foot is reversed, the lower leg is not called "from upon his foot." This is unlike where the foot was straight, in which case the lower leg is called "from upon his foot," and therefore even if part was cut off, the lower leg is still called "from upon his foot." So wrote Rashi. According to this, if he was born that way, the chalitzah is also invalid. However, according to what the Rosh wrote in the name of the Ra'avad, the reason with a reversed foot is that when he performs chalitzah, he removes from upon the sole of his foot and not from upon his foot. If so, with a stump, even if he was born that way, the chalitzah is valid. The Rashba wrote that when the yavam’s foot was amputated, he must press his lower leg into the ground, and so it is in Tosafot.
