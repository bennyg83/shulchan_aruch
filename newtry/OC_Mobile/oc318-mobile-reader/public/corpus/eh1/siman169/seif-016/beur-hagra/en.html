(א) It must lechatchilah, etc. For lechatchilah one must perform chalitzah with a sandal. Between Rabbah and Rav Yosef, the halacha follows Rabbah, as written in Bava Batra 114 and 143, and in Gittin 74b. Also, the halacha follows R. Yosi even against his colleagues. The plain meaning of the Mishnah also implies bedieved, as stated there. However, since sandals are not common among us, we rely on the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah: If she performed chalitzah with a shoe, her chalitzah is valid. For whom was this needed? For R. Meir, for R. Meir says one may not perform chalitzah with a shoe. It was taught: R. Yosi said: Once I went to Netzivin and saw an elder there, and I said to him: Are you familiar with R. Yehudah ben Beteira from your days? He said to me: I was a moneychanger in my city, and he was regularly at my table. I said to him: Did you ever see someone perform chalitzah? With what did he perform chalitzah, with a sandal or with a shoe? He said to me: My master, is there a sandal in our place? And I said: What did R. Meir see to say that one may not perform chalitzah with a shoe? As the Rosh and other decisors wrote. But the Ramban challenged this: on the contrary, from there the opposite is proven, that even in a place where a sandal is not common, since he said, “What did R. Meir see,” etc. Rather, because the matter cannot be corrected, it is considered bedieved. We also rely on the above decisors. Nevertheless, lechatchilah we make its form similar to a sandal as much as possible.
(ב) That the leather be, etc. Rashi 101 and 102.
(ג) And some say that it should be, etc. From Shabbat 60b, according to the explanation of the Semag and Semak.
(ד) And some say from inside. This is a scribal error, and it should read: “and not from inside,” etc.
(ה) And some say, etc. Like Rashi’s explanation there. What he wrote, “cloth,” is according to Rashi’s first explanation to Yevamot 102b, s.v. hatafur.
(ו) And some say that they make, etc. For this is how he explained the distinction between a shoe and a sandal, and they said that this is the mesana that the Rif mentioned there. It appears that he explains what is written, “a decree because of a torn shoe,” as meaning that the seam was torn. But in Shabbat it implies that it was made of two pieces. See Rashi there, s.v. ela lechazek, etc.
(ז) But, etc. “And also,” etc., from what is written: a sandal sewn with linen, etc.
(ח) And also the straps, etc. From what is written: it is of leather and its tarsiyyot, etc.
