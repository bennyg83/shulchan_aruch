If they have many hairs or they are long. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 33, regarding a minor yavam whose father testified that he is thirteen years old and who has many hairs, but among all of them there is not even one as wide as a finger called an amah; some are only enough to grasp with a fingernail, some enough to take with a scissors, and some enough to bend the head to the root. He wrote: the measure of the length of hairs is written in the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 27, as the width of a finger called an amah. The view of the Bach, brought in the gloss of the Beit Shmuel there, is that if the yevamah is sitting as an agunah for several years, even this measure should not be relied on unless they are very long. In siman 155, seif 18, the Shulchan Aruch ruled that the measure of the length of hairs for chalitzah is enough to bend the head to the root. The Beit Shmuel there wrote in the name of the Taz that this does not contradict what is explained in Seder Chalitzah, that an amah’s width is required, because there it speaks where it is not known that he is thirteen years old, and there too an examination for depressions is not needed. According to this, where it is known that he is thirteen, hairs long enough to bend are sufficient, except that examination for depressions is required. However, all this is where there are not many hairs. But where there are many hairs, Shiltei Gibborim, siman 169, wrote: if they have many hairs or they are long, etc., there is a presumption that they have reached the age of majority, and chalitzah is performed, and we rely on this not to examine whether there are depressions, etc. Thus, many hairs are effective even when they are not long, even without the years being known, and they also do not require examination for depressions. Since the author did not mention a minimum size for many hairs, it implies that his intent is ordinary hairs according to their measure for chalitzah according to the author, in siman 155, meaning enough to bend. See Shulchan Aruch above, siman 141, seif 28. But the Bach in siman 155 ruled that when there are many hairs, there is no need to examine their length at all, for presumably he has reached majority. Nor is there need to examine whether there are depressions at their roots, nor even to check whether among them there are at least two hairs with the measure of bending. But all this can be said where the years are known through witnesses. It appears that he explains the Bach’s words, "presumably he has reached majority," not as referring to years, but rather that through these hairs he is considered an adult after we also know that he is thirteen. The language "presumably" somewhat does not imply this. But in the case of the question, where the years were not known through valid witnesses, and what his father testified about him, that he is thirteen, is subject to the plain law in the Tur and Shulchan Aruch that we do not rely on relatives for the count of years, etc., perhaps the fact that many hairs is effective to rely on this that he reached his years is specifically where all of them can be bent. But if many hairs cannot be bent and only a few hairs can be bent, perhaps not. However, since this matter is not explicit, and I already wrote above, brought above s.k. 13, that it appears to me from the Rif, Rosh, and Rambam that the father is believed regarding chalitzah, therefore in any case, based on the father’s testimony together with many hairs, one may be lenient if some of them have enough to bend, while the rest do not. End quote. Study there carefully. See Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 8, a long responsum on this matter. He also mentioned the above words of the Bach, who wrote that if there are many hairs there is no need at all to examine the length of the hairs, even to see if there are two hairs with the measure of bending. He discusses at length that even the Bach spoke only where the years are known through valid witnesses, like the above Noda BiYehudah. Even where the years are known through witnesses, one should not rely on this in practice. He concludes there: Therefore, in my opinion, Heaven forbid to be lenient in this, to rely on many hairs where there are not even two whose heads can be bent to their roots, especially regarding the prohibition of a yevamah to the public and removing her from the presumption of being bound by zikah, and especially where the years are not known through complete witnesses, for in this everyone agrees and no one ever disputed. Even if the years are known through witnesses, I am afraid to rule leniently in a Torah prohibition, and it is very proper to be stringent in this, especially where there is no concern of agunah. End quote. See there. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 86, regarding a chalitzah performed in beit din after they were informed by many people from the yavam’s city that more than thirteen years had passed for him, although they knew this through the father, and one witness examined him and found many hairs whose heads could be bent to their roots, and also depressions. Later one Rav challenged, saying that the father brought the chalutz before him and he examined him and found no trace of hair. He answered: testimony about the years through the people of his city, who know through the father who had established this not at the time of the act, is complete testimony and biblically valid, etc., as above s.k. 13. According to this, after his honor found many hairs that could be bent and also depressions, it was proper to perform chalitzah, even though not even one was as large as an amah finger, and there is no concern about this. As to this challenger, who says that the father brought the chalutz before him and he found no trace of hair: if there were here two sets of witnesses contradicting one another, although by law one might say to place two against two and leave the woman in her presumption of prohibition, nevertheless here it is more lenient. On the contrary, leave the man in the presumption that he brought two hairs, Rava’s presumption, since there are witnesses that much time passed for him. There is another leniency: the matter already went out in permission before the second testimony came. This is the reasoning of Ri bar Baruch in Tosafot, Ketubot, etc. However, we would need all this only if the second group contradicted the first by saying: we were at that same event and he had no hairs. But now, since they testify about a different time, we hold in Choshen Mishpat, siman 30, that we reconcile their words even in a distant way. Therefore we should say that they fell out in the meantime in order to uphold the words of the first ones, all the more so here, where logic indicates that the father intentionally removed them for monetary benefit or revenge, as explained, etc. Perhaps, since his honor mentioned depressions in his words, even though he did not need them, it is like one who comes to litigate with a shtar and chazakah, Choshen Mishpat, siman 140, seif 4, that since the chazakah comes by force of the shtar, he must clarify. Here too, perhaps regarding depressions, since they come by force of hairs, where it is possible to clarify, one must still clarify, like "send and show you" regarding a blemish, Kiddushin 66b. But in truth this is not so, for it is not comparable to "send and show," because here it is impossible to clarify, as the Tur wrote in siman 155 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, that we are not expert in depressions, etc., as above s.k. 17. The conclusion is that this woman is permitted to any man she wishes, and one certified Rav agreed with him to permit her without hesitation. See there.
