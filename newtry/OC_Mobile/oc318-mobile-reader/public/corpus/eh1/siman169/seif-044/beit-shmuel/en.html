And some say the same applies to chalitzah of a deaf-mute. This is the view of the Rambam, for a deaf-mute has the same law as a minor everywhere. See the Maggid, who wrote at length to resolve the sugya according to the Rambam’s explanation, for the Talmud excludes only left-handed chalitzah and the chalitzah of a minor as nothing, which implies that the chalitzah of a deaf-mute does disqualify. See Tosafot, who wrote that a deaf-mute man and deaf-mute woman are not disqualified because they lack understanding, since the beit din stands over them and directs them. So too we hold by get that if they wrote and an adult stood over them, it is valid. They are disqualified only because they are not included in "he shall say" and "she shall say"; therefore their law is like a mute man and mute woman, and their chalitzah disqualifies. But in a matter where the beit din does not stand over them and direct them, their act is nothing. With a minor, even if they stand over him it is nothing, because "man" is written in the section. So too with an insane person, standing over him is not effective, as written in Tosafot. According to the Rambam, by chalitzah even standing over them is not effective, and their act does not disqualify at all. One should not ask why by get, if others stand over them, it is effective, and therefore he rules there that if a deaf-mute, insane person, or minor wrote the toref and the beit din stood over them, it is biblically valid, as written in siman 123. For otherwise he would have had to decree the tofes because of the toref if the toref were biblically invalid. Rather, certainly by get it is biblically valid. One can say that this is because there are not as many matters involved as in chalitzah. Also, by get it is proven that he writes lishmah, since he writes his name and her name. So wrote Tosafot.
