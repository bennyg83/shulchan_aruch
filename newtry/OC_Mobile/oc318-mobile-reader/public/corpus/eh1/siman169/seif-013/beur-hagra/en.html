(א) That if, etc. As written regarding a get at the end of chapter 5 of Arakhin 21b. See Tosafot there, s.v. hai, etc. In Yevamot there: “chalitzah,” etc., “sometimes,” etc., as taught, “and near,” etc. And in Arakhin there: what means “until he says,” etc.
(ב) And likewise if, etc. The Gemara in Yevamot equated these to a get. In the Tosefta, chapter 12: forced chalitzah in a beit din of Israel is valid; in a beit din of gentiles it is invalid. Among gentiles they strike him and say to him, “Do what Rabbi so-and-so tells you,” etc. We say similarly in Gittin chapter 1 regarding a get.
(ג) And although, etc. Meaning, since in that beit din, an Israelite is valid, as above, as stated there regarding a coerced get, etc.
(ד) And if so, every, etc. Meaning, coercion by Israelites is valid specifically when done lawfully, as stated there at the end.
(ה) Nevertheless, one may not, etc. Meaning, since most decisors agree that one may not coerce because of this movement, as written on 39b: “we do not coerce,” for we hold like Tosafot, s.v. eimar, that we do not coerce, unlike Rashi, as Tosafot proves there. What is written there in the words of Rabbenu Tam, “rather, on the contrary, we coerce,” etc., means with words, as written in Ketubot 64a, end of s.v. vedinei, etc., and as written in siman 165, seif 1. See Mordechai there.
