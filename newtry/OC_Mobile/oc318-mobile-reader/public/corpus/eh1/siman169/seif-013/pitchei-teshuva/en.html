Although the Tur copied this law in the name of the Rambam, the Rambam did not write it regarding chalitzah at all, but only regarding lashes and punishments. In the Mordechai, Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah, brought in the Beit Yosef, this matter is explained in the name of many Geonim, and all his words there can be answered, etc. What he wrote in that responsum, that even if we grant the Mordechai his view that he is not believed to testify that so-and-so is the deceased’s brother, see Beit Shmuel above, siman 127, s.k. 2, this is because most people are not the deceased’s brothers, and if so the witness comes to remove so-and-so from the majority, etc., end quote. I do not understand this, because in any case most people have a presumption of validity and would not say about themselves that they are the deceased’s brothers in order to perform chalitzah for her and cause her to stumble. Regarding this particular person, who also says of himself that he is the deceased’s brother, there is no majority of the world, and the witness is not coming to remove him from the majority at all. What he wondered there regarding the Mordechai, who wrote that permitting a yevamah to the public is a matter of ervah, see there in siman 54, where he discusses this more at length. See on this in the responsum of Rabbi Akiva Eiger brought in the previous s.k., and also in Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, end of siman 79, which I brought above in siman 157, s.k. 9. To resolve the view of the above Geonim, it appears to me that they truly hold like the view of the Rosh, above, siman 158, seif 3 in the gloss, that one witness is not believed regarding a yevamah to permit her to the public because she hates him, etc. All this follows the Mordechai’s explanation that "revealing a matter," which they said in HaCholetz regarding the deceased’s brother, means that he is otherwise established. I already wrote that the three pillars of halachic ruling did not explain it that way. I am also astonished at the Shulchan Aruch, for in siman 158, seif 3, he ruled that one witness is believed regarding a yevamah to permit her to the public. How then did he rule here that to testify that he is thirteen years old, valid witnesses are required? He discusses this somewhat and concludes: from all these reasons, in any case it appears to me that one should not be so stringent regarding testimony that a minor is thirteen years old. Although I do not have the power to disagree with the great early authorities, nevertheless, wherever it is possible to be lenient, if there is another side to be lenient, see what I wrote below, s.k. 15, one should be lenient. See there. It is somewhat surprising that the Gaon Noda BiYehudah did not bring the words of Shiltei Gibborim at the end of Kiddushin, which is a supporting Tanna for him. He wrote there in the name of Riaz: In Sefer HaTerumah, in Hilchot Chalitzah, it says that the father is not believed about his son to establish him as an adult regarding chalitzah, but the approaches of the Talmud are not like his words. The father is believed about the birth of his son to say that he was born at such-and-such time, or that he was born in such-and-such manner, whether to testify about him regarding monetary matters, such as to establish him as a firstborn and give him a double portion, or to disqualify him and say he was born with a disqualification, and he is believed about him to permit him to a mamzeret or to permit him to disqualified kohanim if he is a kohen. So too, if he comes to testify about him regarding other prohibitions, to establish him as an adult for all matters except lashes and punishments, etc. End quote. See there. See Magen Avraham, siman 571, s.k. 3, and what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 81, s.k. 7. In Responsa Chatam Sofer, part 2, siman 86, he wrote to the questioner: First of all, regarding the number of years he has, one should investigate through many people of the community of A.S. At first glance, if we invalidate the father’s testimony and require specifically two valid witnesses to testify about the years, then we need two witnesses who know the day of his birth, because all these people of that city who now testify that he is already thirteen years old all testify based on the father, and there is no substance in their testimony. But this is not so. Although it is true that all their testimony is based on the father establishing the son as thirteen years old, nevertheless, since he established this not at the time of the yevamah testimony, and it became established that way, he is believed even to cause stoning through him, like that woman who carried a child on her shoulder in Perek Asarah Yuchasin, and as the Rambam wrote in chapter 16 of Hilchot Issurei Biah. Greater than this, regarding an ordinary single witness, he is believed in this way regarding punishments. If we would not say so, no person would ever receive lashes or bring an offering for niddah, for who would say that she was a niddah, etc.? Thus, testimony about the years through the people of the community of A.S. is complete testimony and biblically valid, etc. End quote. It is astonishing that he did not mention at all the words of the Shulchan Aruch, that even according to the "some say" who are lenient that a presumption of neighbors helps, nevertheless we specifically require that it not be based on the father.
