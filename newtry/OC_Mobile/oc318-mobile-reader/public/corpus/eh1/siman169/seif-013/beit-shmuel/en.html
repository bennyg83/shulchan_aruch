According to the Rosh, it is valid, and he wrote the reason: the derivation from "tachash," that it must be of leather, is only an asmachta. One should not ask, according to this, why cloth socks are invalid. One must say that he holds like Tosafot, who wrote there that cloth socks are not considered a shoe because people do not usually make them from cloth. Everything depends on whether it is the way to make such a shoe. The derivation of "tachash" is only an asmachta. A wooden shoe is also invalid because it is not usual to make a shoe of wood, as written in Tosafot there. Rashi explained that according to the one who says socks are valid, the verse "tachash" is not precise. Learn from this that he too holds like these "some say." Tosafot there also implies that they hold the derivation of "tachash" is not an asmachta, but then one must explain why, in the dispute regarding the amputee’s wooden leg and socks, they wrote that the Rabbis invalidated because it is not the way to make such a shoe. Let them say that they hold the derivation of "tachash" is a full derivation. One must say as written nearby, that where it is made of wool that comes from leather, it is not excluded by "tachash." This explains what they wrote regarding socks. However, regarding what they wrote about an amputee’s wooden leg, which is wood, they gave the reason that it is the way to make a shoe and did not write that Rabbi Meir does not derive from "tachash." Perhaps since they were forced to explain socks as invalid because it is not the way to make a shoe, because it is not excluded by "tachash," they no longer needed to explain that according to Rabbi Meir, he does not derive from "tachash" regarding the amputee’s wooden leg. Even if he does not derive from "tachash," still, since it is not the way to make a shoe from them, why would it be valid? Rather, one must say the above reason. For halacha, the main ruling follows these "some say," for this is the view of Rashi, Tosafot, Rambam, Ramah, and Rashba, as the Maggid wrote in his name, and so ruled the Shiltei Gibborim. According to this, what the Tur wrote, that if he performed chalitzah with a muk it is valid, is according to his reasoning that anything from which shoes are normally made is valid, and "tachash" is not a full derivation. But according to the above decisors, it is a full derivation, and then even with a muk it is invalid. However, one can say that although "tachash" is a full derivation, nevertheless a muk made of wool that comes from leather is not excluded by "tachash." Although one made of hair is invalid, nevertheless a muk is valid, because perhaps it is called a shoe, whereas one made of hair is not called a shoe but a korka. According to Rashi and Rashba, who explained that cloth socks are excluded by "tachash," even though they are made of wool that comes from leather like hair, if so there is a difficulty in the sugya where he asked: If it is of leather and its straps are of hair, what is the law? He said to him: do we not read "tachash" regarding it? He said to him: if so, all of it too. That one is a korka. If woolen cloth is excluded by "tachash," then hair too should be. Why do we need the reason that it is called a korka? Why not exclude it from "tachash"? One can answer.
