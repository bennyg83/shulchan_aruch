And she should hold his foot in her left hand. The Bach, letter 19, wrote: This means that at the time she grasps the heel of the shoe with her right hand to remove it, she should grasp with her left hand the thickness of the flesh of the lower leg in the air, so that it should not sway or move, and she should slip the shoe from the heel with her right hand, etc. He concludes there that this is the custom, that everything is with the right, except that she holds the flesh of the lower leg with the left. This is what the Yerushalmi means by "she holds it with the left." See there. However, in Sefer Get Mekushar, in his glosses to Seder Chalitzah of Maharam, letter 83, he wrote: See in Kuntres Tov Chalitzah, where I wrote that she must grasp the place of the shoe and hold there, holding the shoe together with the foot so that it should not fall, even if the shoe was not tight. End quote. I found in Sefer Kerem Shlomo, in the responsa at the end of the book, siman 87, that one Rav wrote to him: It seems to me that the world practices that she holds the yavam’s heel with her left and removes the shoe with her right. In my humble opinion, this is not correct, because through this one can easily come to a stumbling block, that she will assist with her left hand, since she is holding the heel. Rather, the choicest way is what I saw in the Bach, etc. He also copied this in Sefer HaKovetz, that the left should not assist, but only hold the flesh of the lower leg, and this is proper practice and a mitzvah to publicize. He answered him and agreed that certainly she should not grasp the heel. See there. In my humble opinion, the words of the above Get Mekushar are more correct, for so is precise from the language of the Yerushalmi, from which the decisors derived this matter, brought by Tosafot, the Rosh, the Mordechai, the Maggid Mishneh, and the explanation of Seder Chalitzah, seif 71: How does she do it? She unties it with the right, holds it with the left, removes the heel with the right, and drags it with the right. In this there are variant readings, as explained in the Beit Yosef. But regarding "holds it," everyone agrees that the version is "with the left," as written in the Derishah. The language "unties it" and "drags it" refers to the shoe and not to the foot. Presumably, "holds it" also refers to the shoe, that she should hold the place of the shoe, as the above Get Mekushar wrote. What Sefer Kerem Shlomo wrote, that one can easily come to a stumbling block, etc., is not so, because the beit din sees and warns about this. Furthermore, there is more concern if most of the heel falls by itself, because even bedieved it is invalid. This is unlike here, where even if she removed it entirely with her left, it is valid, and according to Rabbenu Tam it is permitted even lechatchilah when the left assists, because assisting has no substance. The Bach himself concludes that this is the main view, except that the custom already followed the Semak, etc. See there. Therefore it appears that in a place where there is no custom like the Bach, or the custom is not clear, one should do like the above Get Mekushar. See what I wrote further on this in Seder Chalitzah in brief, seif 52, s.k. 14.
