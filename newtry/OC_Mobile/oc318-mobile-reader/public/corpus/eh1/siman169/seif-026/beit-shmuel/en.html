On the bare leg. This is the language of the Levush: he should wrap the straps around the leg also below the knee. This means that in the place where the shoe is open behind the leg, there are two holes, and between the holes it is whole. He should wrap the long straps between the loops until he reaches behind the leg at the upper hole, and he should also place them there aligned next to one another. Afterward he should wrap the straps a third time around the leg until they reach above the loops, on the top of the foot, called shinbein in German, for on the shinbein is also called "from upon his foot." See the Perishah. The reason is that in the place where the two long straps meet, in front and behind, they are folded over each other, and that is considered like a knot. Therefore that fold must be on the flesh of his leg. But from there onward, where the strap rests on the leather of the shoe, there is no concern, for it is not at all like a knot.
