(א) Even if he doubled the condition, from what they said that a mistaken get is invalid. It must therefore be where he doubled the condition, since we hold like Rabbi Meir, and in Ketubot 74a, “Now, every condition,” etc. This means that although Ravina and Rabbi Elazar said in Yevamot 57a that they disagree regarding a condition in chalitzah, and Ravina said that it requires a doubled condition, Tosafot already explained, s.v. Ravina, that they said this regarding kiddushin.
(ב) And even, etc. As above, because there is no condition in chalitzah. Even if they said, “Perform chalitzah for her, and through this you will marry her,” it would be valid were it not for the fact that he does not intend to perform chalitzah. This is not so in this kind of case.
(ג) And although chalitzah, etc. As above, because a mistaken chalitzah is valid.
(ד) Nevertheless, etc. As they said there: “Go and give it to her.” Even Rav Pappa said this only because he is not fitting for her, and the mitzvah rests upon him to perform chalitzah, so it is like a case of ones. This is as the Yerushalmi says there: Rabbi Mana said, if he said “on condition,” it must be given.
(ה) Like other, etc. Rashi there. The Ramban, Rashba, and Rosh wrote that against his will, from the law of condition, we do not require him to give, because whenever one says “on condition,” we do not obligate him to fulfill it. Rather, if he fulfills his condition, the act stands, etc. A proof is from what is stated in Gittin 74 and 75 regarding Hillel’s enactment, etc. If it were otherwise, since he became obligated in the money, it would be obvious that payment against his will is considered payment, as stated in Bava Metzia 73b, “he paid him the money,” etc. See Tosafot in Gittin there, s.v. miklal, etc. Rather, here, since the chalitzah cannot become void, he is obligated by the law of wages.
