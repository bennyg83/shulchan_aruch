It is a mitzvah that they add two. The reason is publicizing the matter. Rashi and the Nimukei Yosef explain, according to the first explanation, that this publicizes that she is a chalutzah and forbidden to a kohen. According to this, if she is already divorced, five are not needed. But according to the second explanation, five are needed so that people will come and seek to marry her. With these two, there is no concern if they take much payment. Darkei Moshe. So too if they are his relatives with a closeness that is not disqualifying by strict law, but only by stringency, as written in siman 68. The Nimukei Yosef wrote that if they explicitly designated three for the beit din and called two others only to fill out the number, then even converts and even disqualified people are valid for these two. That which the Talmud disqualifies regarding Rav and Shmuel, who were sons of converts for filling out the two, is because there all five formed one group, and it was not respectful to Rav Shmuel to exclude him from the three. So too the Or Zarua wrote that there is no concern with the two additional ones if they are not valid. This too is in such a case: according to the custom, where they do not make one group, and these two sit drawn away from the three, there is no concern at all. With this, what Terumat HaDeshen, siman 226, asked against Or Zarua from the incident of Rav and Shmuel is resolved, and there is no dispute at all among the decisors. What is inferred from the Rambam and Tur, that they hold there is concern that these two not be relatives, is because they hold that all five sit in one assembly, as implied there, since they did not write that the two sit away from the three. Also, what Terumat HaDeshen inferred from Hagahot Maimoniyot is not compelling. A convert whose mother is from Israel is disqualified lechatchilah to be among the two when they form one assembly, as written in the Talmud in the incident of Rav Shmuel bar Yehudah. Bedieved he is valid, since these two are only to publicize the matter.
