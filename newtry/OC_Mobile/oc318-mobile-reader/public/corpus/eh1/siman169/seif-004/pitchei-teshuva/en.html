Or disqualified. See Ba'er Heitev, what he wrote, "even if they accepted them for the yavam," etc. This is from the words of the Beit Shmuel in the name of Terumat HaDeshen. See Yeshuot Yaakov, s.k. 4, who wrote that although the Beit Shmuel wrote this plainly, nevertheless it is subject to dispute. From the words of the Rosh in Perek Mitzvat Chalitzah it is clear that specifically regarding something the Torah explicitly disqualified for a beit din of chalitzah, acceptance is not effective. But regarding something that we do not find explicitly, and it is only because it is like monetary law, it is no worse than monetary cases, where acceptance is effective. However, from the view of Tosafot there it is clear that they hold like the Terumat HaDeshen. See there. His father and mother are from Israel. See Ba'er Heitev, what he wrote regarding a shetuki, etc., but if most men are disqualified with respect to her, etc. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, part 2, siman 94, who wrote that even if his mother was in a Jewish neighborhood, it is difficult to rely on this in a city whose majority is non-Jewish and where the doors of the neighborhood are not locked, etc. See there regarding elevating the shetuki to the rabbinic seat, that it is certainly not proper for the reason the Rama wrote in that responsum, that it is a disgrace to the Torah and appears light in the eyes of people, etc. See there in the responsum of the Rama, that nevertheless if he ascended with permission, he does not descend, and it is like bedieved, that if she married she need not leave. But without appointing him as head, only to ordain him as fit to rule and judge, what the Rama wrote there from the Rambam, that if one is not fit for everything, we do not ordain partially, his words are astonishing. The Rambam speaks of one who does not know how to rule in the entire Torah, like what they said at the end of Horayot, but not of one who is unfit for one judgment because of a disqualification. However, for another reason one can say not to ordain him, because there is concern that out of shame he will conceal his status in a place where people do not know him and will become involved in chalitzah. See there.
