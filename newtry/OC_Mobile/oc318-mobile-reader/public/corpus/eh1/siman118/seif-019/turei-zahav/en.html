(ג) That they return to the givers of the dowry, etc. This is a dispute. In Responsa Mamonot, Hilchot Ishut siman 26, it is written that Rabbenu Tam's enactment applies where the daughter died within her first year, so that her heirs return everything she brought in. The communal custom is that if she died within two years, they return it to her heirs; if the teacher gave the dowry, they had to return to him what he gave and not to her father. Some return half of what she brought in if she died in the second year. In siman 35 there he writes that Maharam said to return the money to the one who gave it, and communal leaders testified that the simple custom within two years was to return half of what the deceased spouse brought in to the father or relatives. Responsa Rosh also speaks of returning the dowry to the giver or the heirs, and Mordechai mentions "to him or to her heirs." Taz explains the underlying logic: according to the view that it returns to the giver, Rabbenu Tam's enactment uprooted the husband's ordinary inheritance because of the verse "your strength will be spent in vain"; the giver lost both daughter and money. This applies even if the giver was not her father. According to the view that it returns to heirs, the reason is that usually the wife's relatives who are fit to inherit are the ones who gave the dowry, and the Sages did not distinguish within the enactment. Where a total outsider gave the dowry, everyone may agree that it returns to her heirs because the enactment was standardized. Rama's language that "such is the custom of the communities" does not prove that this is our custom in every community; it reports the custom as cited by that opinion. Therefore the halachah remains uncertain, though it is more reasonable in many cases to return to the giver.
