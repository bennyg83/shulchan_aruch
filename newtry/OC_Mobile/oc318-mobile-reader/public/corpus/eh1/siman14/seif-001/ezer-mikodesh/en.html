It seems the leniency applies even b'dieved, as with nursing a neighbor's child or the case of separation there, where even b'dieved they separate them; but here we do not find likewise, nor even for one who married in violation to penalize him b'dieved , for this is lighter than separation and nursing in several matters as stated. One may also say the reason they did not penalize b'dieved is that he can say "I do not want the servants in my house to serve me, and I have no satisfaction from them" , and who can answer him regarding matters of the heart? Therefore it seems this is not among rabbinic matters for which we excommunicate. One should also not quarrel b'dieved with anyone in this, since even if you saw him steal in this and he excuses himself saying as above , and all that is known that he knows the words of the Sages , one need not press him further; and even from afar one should judge him favorably, and there is no desecration of God's name in this. What I wrote elsewhere about what the Dagul MeRevavah wrote , that Shemini Atzeret counts toward the number of three festivals to marry after one's wife , someone told me now there is something like proof for this from what the poskim wrote, that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are not like festivals in this, implying that Shemini Atzeret is a festival; for Shemini Atzeret is a complete festival relative to Pesach, Shavuot, and Sukkot more than Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and regarding mourning too Shemini Atzeret is a festival in its own right. And I have now seen that this is not so decisive; for according to the reason the poskim wrote , that through the joy of the festival the heart forgets , one may say that just as the seventh day of Pesach its joy is joined with what precedes it, so Shemini Atzeret its joy is joined with what precedes it; and regarding joy the Sages did not count Shemini Atzeret separately, as with the joy of the seventh of Pesach and the first day of Shavuot which are equivalent because days without interruption between them are considered as one , perhaps so here too. Only regarding Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, which we hold are like festivals regarding mourning and are times in their own right, since days interrupt between them that are not also chol hamoed, there would be room to say they are like festivals for this. Therefore the poskim wrote that since they do not have so great an obligation of joy, they are not like festivals for this. And there is precision in the poskim's language: they wrote "whether close like Pesach and Shavuot, whether distant from each other" , and if Shemini Atzeret were a festival in its own right for this, they should have stated preferably "whether close like Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret." And that the poskim used this language , they used it regarding two festivals, for they hold like R' Yehudah, and one may say that for two festivals some distance is required, which is not so for three festivals according to us. Nevertheless on all this it seems one who is stringent on himself in this does so on the better side. And the reasoning to distinguish between one who holds two festivals and us who hold three festivals has no such basis; for just as for one who holds two festivals Shemini Atzeret is not a festival in its own right for this , and that is from the reason stated, since there is no interruption in time without joy between them , so too for one who holds three festivals, even though there is distance between two, nevertheless who will divide here not to require distance among all of them? And what they cited Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur is for amplification; for regarding mourning Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur cancel the entire thirty, while Shemini Atzeret cancels only seven days because it has make-up days. (Elsewhere I wrote about Tachanun after Sukkot. And now I see it is correct, as the Chesed l'Avraham wrote, not to say because of make-up days after Shemini Atzeret for sacrifices of Shemini Atzeret.) And also what he wrote there , the reason is because of the festival it is forbidden in what is forbidden through thirty , see Taz. Nevertheless "from where shall it come" , since in the halacha the aspect of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur is preferable to Shemini Atzeret for this, they cited amplification more. And from the aspect of blessing and song of Shemini Atzeret as a festival in its own right , to say this is joy in the aspect of "the blessing of Hashem makes rich and adds no sorrow," and in the aspect of song it is joy from this side too , there is also no such decisive proof; for in every Shabbat and Yom Tov there is some blessing, song, and joy, yet this is not like the joy of a festival that removes love from the heart; and what one dwells with his wife dwells in joy, and regarding a festival it is written "and you shall rejoice, you and your household." Yet despite all this, anyone who relies on what the Dagul MeRevavah wrote as above , one should not remove him from the category of a scrupulous person in Shulchan Aruch HaKadosh; for there are further reasons to be lenient: the Rosh and Tur ruled two festivals; and in Be'er Heitev he brought in the name of Agudah that Rosh Hashanah is like a festival; and in Darkei Moshe YD 392 he wrote regarding another matter there to rule like the Rosh and Tur , see there. Also it seems that besides what the Dagul MeRevavah wrote , a reason to be lenient because in these countries kiddushin is only under the chuppah (and this is a great enactment against the confusions that occurred in kiddushin matters through deceit as is known) , besides this it seems there is not even one in a hundred who will not have distress from delaying marriage because of the prohibition of yichud, needless to say with a maidservant in his house or other women who come to his house; but even if his daughter is in his house , though he is permitted to be alone with her , nevertheless she is not like a guardian to permit yichud with other women, and there is no greater affliction than this; for even with many women there is no permission of yichud, which is not so with his wife with him, or when she goes in and out. And here he was permitted because he has no one to serve him , as Beit Yosef wrote in this regarding a minister who said he would free someone from prison on condition he marry a woman immediately , see there. And one can resolve all Beit Yosef's difficulties there on Maharam's responsum , one may say he wrote this is not like small children to permit even within thirty; for the permission of having no one to serve him does not help here. And certainly he would distinguish well the style of that minister, who would not rely on his words and would return him to prison; and he knew that imprisonment carried no real liability, and it is not like captivity nor like small children, where sometimes there is a complete matter of saving life, which is not so in that imprisonment , and especially as is now known, since in a man's house or his friend's house on bail, and especially when a minister promises to release from prison on condition he marry so-and-so , this is only that there was some concern of stumbling because it is known they are brought only for their pleasure; therefore Maharam was stringent. And Beit Yosef's words are correct in this: in an event where captivity is like danger and tends that the minister will not change his promise, and there is no stumbling concern as above , one may permit after thirty; and all is correct.
