To gloss 2 at time of persecution, etc. — one may be lenient; see Choshen Mishpat and Beit Shmuel; Maharik siman 165: I found a copy in the name of Semak from Tzorfat in the name of R' Yosef Mekurbal who wrote: a kohen and his wife who apostatized under coercion and immediately when the decree passed they returned and repented — she does not have the law of those imprisoned through danger to life who are forbidden; there it deals when he has no intention to leave in any matter; if so when there is certainly upon her, etc. — in my view; but when they want to leave in peace, when she wants to apostatize — if it happened she apostatized and afterward returned — certainly she is not forbidden; so explained R' Yosef Mekurbal on the gloss of Tzorfat; and behold for you alongside that R' Yosef Mekurbal who was later wrote: where she is not delivered to them for death she is not forbidden even to a kohen's wife, etc. — Maharik; if so it is possible even though in Shulchan Arukh it is written in the name of the Rosh "textual error" and hints to R' Yosef Mekurbil; nevertheless it appears to me R' Yosef Mekurbal also rules like Ravina that through money does not apply and through danger to life specifically; and he can well say there is no difference through danger to life except when she has no intention to leave in any matter; and he also explains like Chizkiyah — when his judgment was concluded; but for Shulchan Arukh that through money specifically she is permitted and through danger to life does not apply — his reasoning does not go well, for it is no worse than through money and strong hand that she is forbidden though certainly their intention is to leave in any matter when they give them the money; also Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 32 and Taz siman 241 left in doubt; Taz: and per this it would appear permitted even for kehunah, for there is no concern perhaps they raped her, for a modest woman surrenders her soul lest they rape her, as proven in that sugya that for rape it is permitted; and in Semak above it implies not permitted except to her Israelite husband, etc.; and it is strained to say Semak disagrees with Tosafot, etc.; in my humble opinion I do not understand: even though it is proven a modest woman surrenders her soul even for rape and chooses death whereby thereby she may come to danger — nevertheless who is there to concern that he overpowers her and lies with her against her will in choosing death, rather in his choice to leave her alive and lie with her — what does her modesty help for this. Also the incident in Tosafot in that language is no proof to this view that there is concern even though she is modest she knew the law that rape is permitted — it is the foundation of the world and from the Torah she should surrender to death; and what they answer "there is also a kohenet" — does not mean even after the derashah she surrenders herself, but because of the kohenet they did not protest in their hands — see there; and see also Tosafot on "and to expound for them."
