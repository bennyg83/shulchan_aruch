Through danger to life — in the Tur he wrote: for example, when her husband is wanted, etc., and so in the Gemara; and it appears all the more if she herself is liable to death.
Perhaps she acquiesced — see in the responsum I brought siman 17 — that is responsum 12 relevant to this; and the law of a captive who was lain with by a gentile under coercion.
One may be lenient and permit them even to their kohen husbands — I wonder greatly: why are we not concerned perhaps she was raped? And in the source of this law, which is the responsum of the Rosh klal 32 siman 8, he did not bring this permission except for an Israelite wife that we are not concerned perhaps she acquiesced to prostitution — see there, as he wrote in this language regarding women who apostatized at a time of coercion and when they found a place to flee they fled and returned to the true faith — permitted to their husbands, for before she apostatized she was not saved by acquiescing to be lain with; and after she apostatized why should she acquiesce — she knows she has more peace from the gentile body and her heart is to Heaven; only from fear of death they switched to coerce them — they are not allowed; therefore there is no concern perhaps she acquiesced. And if he said: after she ate and drank with them perhaps she abandoned herself to prostitution, etc. — for it is known that one who finds a place to flee does not delay even one moment, etc. And in our rabbi's words he copied these words and did not write there "those under coercion are not allowed"; also he did not mention there even for a kohen — and there is an apparent practical difference: if so that they are not allowed to coerce even for a kohen she is permitted from concern of others who apostatized — only wonder what this helps for permitting a kohen, for in any case there is concern perhaps she was raped before she apostatized, for the permission the Rosh mentioned that willingness to be lain with does not help her — only does not help to save from this death — only helps regarding Israelite that we are not concerned for acquiescence; and if you want to learn permission for a kohen — that is when there are witnesses she was not lain with before she apostatized; then we say after apostatizing she is permitted to a kohen even without witnesses that afterward they are not allowed to rape her. But it does not appear to be lenient for this to a kohen, lest once a gentile found her in a hidden place and raped her; and the Rosh concluded only that there is no concern she acquiesced. (The words of the Maggid — the Taz inserted what Beit Shmuel wrote, that it deals with one who apostatized immediately at the time of the decree, etc., and concluded there is a difference from what he brought in Choshen Mishpat, etc.; but in truth one should not deviate from the above ruling, for certainly forbidden to a kohen for the reason of the Taz lest once a gentile found her, etc. — so is the language.) And this is regarding Israelite prohibition specifically; and in any case one cannot settle this wording that Rama wrote "even to kohanim." It appears there is a textual error here "even" — it should read "but not to their kohen husbands"; and even though the Levush copied as before us "even to their kohen husbands" — there is no proof from this, for he did not pay attention to scrutinize this; and what he mentioned in the responsum regarding coercion "they are not allowed" — he did not intend permission for a kohen, but rather: afterward there is no concern to say perhaps there was morale that one would take her by rape and she would acquiesce to one who is powerful and rules to save her from one gentile or the like that she would seem abandoned to all — she would choose this one; this is not so, for no fear is seen from this and she did not acquiesce; but in any case for a kohen she is forbidden; and why did our rabbi not copy there "they are not allowed to coerce" — for it is not needed for Israelite permission and for kohen permission it does not help, as it appears to me.
