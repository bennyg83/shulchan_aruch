Herself. To leave the faith. See Baer Heitev in the name of Beit Shmuel. See responsum Beit Ephraim Tinyana siman 24, who wrote: you asked about a married woman who had been married to this man for several years, and she left our faith, abandoned the religion, and went to the religion of the Ishmaelites; she was there approximately seven weeks with one Ishmaelite, and afterward returned to her father's house and to her husband's house. The woman and the husband wish to live together as before: is she permitted to him? You were not precise in your question, for you wrote that she left our faith and abandoned the religion. If that were so, she would certainly be forbidden even to her Jewish husband, because the lenient authorities brought by the Rama in siman 7 seif 11, at the end of the second gloss, have been rejected by all the later authorities. But as it appears, she still did not abandon the religion. If so, this is the case of one who vowed to leave the faith brought by the Rama, where she is permitted to her Jewish husband. The Beit Shmuel there distinguishes between a short time and a long time. I already wrote in the margin of the Shulchan Aruch that the Beit Shmuel learned this from the words of Maharik, but he did not examine carefully, for there it speaks of the wife of a kohen, where after a long time he was concerned for coercion, etc. Therefore, if this woman did not abandon the religion and it has not been clarified that she committed an act, and there is here only the promise to leave the faith and seclusion for seven weeks, then if after very thorough investigation she says that she did not commit zenut, in my opinion she is permitted to her Jewish husband. However, because of the severity of the matter, Heaven forbid relying on me alone until two great authorities of the generation agree. End quote. See responsum Chemdat Shlomo siman 5 regarding such a question. See further responsum Mayim Rabbim, Even HaEzer siman 1-2-3, on a similar matter; and see also responsum Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer simanim 21 and 22.
