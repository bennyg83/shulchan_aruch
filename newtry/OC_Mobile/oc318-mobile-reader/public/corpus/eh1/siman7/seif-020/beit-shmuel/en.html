Or a doubtful chalutzah. So wrote the Tur. The statement that the child is a doubtful chalal means that we impose upon him the stringencies of an Israelite and he does not eat terumah; but if he becomes impure, he receives thirty-nine lashes, for by Torah law he is a kohen. The Mechaber omitted this because he understood Rambam to disagree, for Rambam wrote in chapter 17 that if he married a doubtful chalutzah we do not remove her from him, and she and her child are valid; this implies there is no doubtful chalal at all. Bach distinguishes: if he married her, the Sages enacted that she and the children are valid so that he not have to divorce her, unlike where he had relations with her outside marriage. This must be the meaning according to this Hagah. Therefore in siman 4 he wrote generally that she need not leave him if he married a doubtful chalutzah. See Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 19, law 4, who wrote that wherever we do not remove her from him, all the more the child is not a chalal. See siman 6 regarding a rumor of chalitzah.
