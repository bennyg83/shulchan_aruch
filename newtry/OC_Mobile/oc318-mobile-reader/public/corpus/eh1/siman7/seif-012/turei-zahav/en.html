Even if she is found a virgin — Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rav HaMaggid that the Rambam learned this from a mishnah that a widow is forbidden outright from marriage — meaning: regarding ketubah he said every widow from marriage is presumed to have had intercourse; all the more regarding a Torah prohibition, etc. Since so, even if there are witnesses she was not hidden with him — for regarding ketubah we say in the first chapter of Ketubot the law is like one who had intercourse; but it appears to me there is a distinction regarding corpse impurity, as written later in this siman: certainly a chalal may become impure and is like a zar in all matters; if so, when there are no witnesses she was not secluded — the presumption of marriage that she is like one who had intercourse helps even if found a virgin, lest he had initial contact with her. But if there are witnesses she was not secluded — forbidden to say, for there is no clear presumption here and it is a safek like safek chalal — we go stringently. An incident occurred in this country: a certain virgin was captured among the mountains of Moab and was some time among them, then redeemed and married an Israelite and was found a virgin; I said nevertheless she is forbidden to a kohen if widowed, for we are concerned lest the captor had initial contact with her like a captive, as stated — that being found a virgin does not help. (The words of the Maggid — Beit Shmuel wrote s.k. 36: even if found a virgin — so the Rambam implies: even if witnesses testify she was not lain with, nevertheless she is presumed to have had intercourse, as he learned from Ketubot 12: Rav Ashi said even if witnesses testify, etc.; and Tosafot Yevamot 57 also hold she is desanctified by chuppah without intercourse, etc.; and the Taz wrote, etc.; but per Tosafot's view it is not so, for she is certainly desanctified by chuppah and her sons are chalalim; and so it appears to me primarily that even per the Rambam's view his explanation is not compelled. Also from it and in it it is compelled: why does he conclude "every married woman is presumed to have had intercourse" — the main point is like the Taz and Choshen Mishpat.)
