He did not give her his money — need to give a reason: behold even without redeeming her we can say that if he did not know she was pure he would not testify for her and commit a sin in marriage that he marry her; if you suspect him that he commits a sin — his inclination overcame him; if so, when he gives money too he is suspected for this. It appears the distinction is: when testifying for her he does not rely that he will marry her, for she will later confess she was defiled and will not want to commit a sin; not so when giving money — he loses his money if she later confesses. (I do not understand this — if so he could marry her too; consider.) (The words of the Maggid — I do not know why he did not understand this that he emended, for they are simple matters; and the intention of the Taz is thus: granted when testifying for her, because his inclination is strong over him, he thinks what will he lose even if she later confesses and testifies falsely — perhaps she will not confess and he will marry her; not so when giving his money — he loses; if so he certainly knows she was certainly pure — so is the language.)
