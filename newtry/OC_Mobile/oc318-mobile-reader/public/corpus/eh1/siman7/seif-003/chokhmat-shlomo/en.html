Question of Nizhnov — he acquired her place in Hilchot Pesach siman 248; see there with God's help; consider. When I was on this I was reminded of the language of the Gemara chapter 4 of Ketubot (51b): Rav Yehuda said — these women of thieves, etc., until "and if they left them and went on their own they are forbidden," etc. Behold the Rambam, Rif, and Rosh copied this, but the Tur omitted it — wonder why; certainly there is a practical difference since the Gemara brings it and the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. What appears to me: although when they left and went on their own we may also permit her since initially it was under coercion — nevertheless one may say that afterward it became willingly; still one may say the inclination clothed her and it is like beginning under coercion and ending willingly — she is permitted. But one may say R' Yehuda was a student of Shmuel, as known, and Shmuel from the anonymous view holds like his father who holds: beginning under coercion and ending willingly — forbidden; but we hold like Rava that beginning under coercion and ending willingly — permitted; also here permitted. Therefore the Tur did not copy it; consider. Also one may say per what I brought in my book siman 57 part 2 in haskamot from the Rambam's Mishnah commentary — it is proven he does not hold like Maharik; rather he holds when she prostituted thinking it was permitted she is permitted to her husband. If so, one may say since the Sages said in chapter 8 of Yoma: one who transgressed and repeated it becomes permitted to him — so too here, since initially she was accustomed with thieves it became permitted to her and is like an unintentional act; only R' Yehuda would not agree with Rav Huna who holds one who transgressed and repeated becomes permitted — rather he holds his law is like intentional; therefore he forbade her. But for us who hold like Rav Huna, as supported from the mishnah in chapter 8 of Yoma — also here she is permitted, for she is like unintentional. The Rif, Rambam, and Rosh hold like Maharik that she is forbidden also here — at least when she intended to transgress with a woman. But the Tur holds not like Maharik; therefore he holds she is permitted and therefore omitted it; consider. Perhaps God will grant us another time to explain more on this; consider.
