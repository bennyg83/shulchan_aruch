Or she married. So responsum Ridbaz what she married is as if said I am pure and bet din not protesting is as if permitted her — when known to bet din she was captive; must be no witnesses she was captive otherwise how bet din was silent — only at marriage no witnesses but she said was captive; concludes there even if married secretly does not leave; what mishnah taught bet din permitted — when not yet married; but if married without permission like permitted and not married even though sitting under husband and captive witnesses came — leaves because was involved in matter; but here in her power to marry another not kohen therefore trusted. Darkhei Moshe brought his words and here Shulchan Aruch omitted his words — implies does not rely on this view; possible if did not come to bet din only married and said I was taken captive — also did not rule like Ridbaz except specifically if came before bet din and said I am pure and bet din not yet permitted and married before them — ruled like Ridbaz because then marriage like permission; so hagahah language implies; see Darkhei Moshe what more wrote in name of that responsum if not known to bet din she was captive but one knew and she says pure — may marry her even Israelite that pure because miggo could take another; wrote on this Darkhei Moshe uncertain why omitted here Shulchan Aruch; all the more kohen's wife captive and he knows from this Israelite if pure forbidden though Israelite to any man and says pure; if he knows pure wrote above; regarding if permitted and afterward captive witnesses — law forbidden relative equals unmarried woman.
Two witnesses. But one witness she was defiled does not help at all — even if not married does not leave permission — so Choshen Mishpat; meaning there are captive witnesses therefore wrote does not leave permission — but do not permit her; but if no captive witnesses and one witness testifies defiled — permit as written nearby; there are captive witnesses and one witness defiled — nevertheless not like two defilement witnesses and does not leave — so Rashi there s.v. rather plain.
