And in what Beit Shmuel lengthens to challenge Chelkat Mechokek — in my view perhaps Maharik intended only unmarried and even when hidden for prostitution per his second answer; and what he derives from precision in Maharik's words in the incident there and also in what he concludes on Maharam's words — perhaps because he answered the husband who was himself the questioner: without hiding for prostitution he can rely on all the great ones who permit even lekhatchilah for unmarried — excluding when hidden for prostitution, since for unmarried he elevated that ab initio she is forbidden to kohen; it was fitting to tell the husband that if he wants to be stringent he does a mitzvah, as we find regarding forbidden relatives in an ugly matter; but to forbid from fear of will more than Israelite — not compelled. And perhaps even Rama z"l's intention who wrote one should be stringent is not absolutely to remove her against her will from her husband, but the husband should be stringent upon himself; and similarly for unmarried it is fitting that bet din be stringent lekhatchilah; and it did not appear to him to be stringent per first answer except per second; see in Maharik himself it implies one should not distinguish regarding forbidden relatives in concern of will between Israelite and another man's wife; and I brought proof not like Beit Shmuel.
