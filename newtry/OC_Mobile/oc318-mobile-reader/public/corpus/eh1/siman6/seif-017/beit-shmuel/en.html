An unmarried woman, etc. There are three approaches to this law. According to Rambam, two majorities are required initially for her to marry a kohen, even if she says she had relations with a fit man. According to other authorities, if she makes a clear claim that she was with a fit man, she is believed at least after the fact, and sometimes initially depending on the circumstances. A further approach distinguishes between ordinary marriage to an Israelite and the elevated standard made for kehunah. The practical conclusion is that for priestly lineage the Sages required additional safeguards, but the details depend on whether she states a definite claim and whether the surrounding population contains a majority of valid men.
And if she married she need not leave. Even with a majority of unfit she need not leave; rather the meaning is: with one majority of fit men too, only if she married she need not leave. And some say bedi'eved one majority suffices—not only with a majority of unfit is the law of bedi'eved needed.
