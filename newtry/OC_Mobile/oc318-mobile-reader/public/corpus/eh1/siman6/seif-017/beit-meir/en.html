An unmarried woman. Beit Shmuel wrote that the requirement of two majorities applies where there is at least a minority of disqualified men; but where all are fit, there is no concern. He explains the sugyot according to the levels of doubt and the special stringency of lineage. The matter depends on whether the doubt is only rabbinic or touches a Torah-level concern, and on whether we can rely on the ordinary presumption of fitness. Where there is no actual disqualified minority, the need for two majorities falls away, and the ordinary rules of majority and presumption suffice. This is the proper reading of the poskim and resolves the apparent tension between the discussions of a woman who claims she had relations with a fit man and the sugyot requiring two majorities for kehunah.
