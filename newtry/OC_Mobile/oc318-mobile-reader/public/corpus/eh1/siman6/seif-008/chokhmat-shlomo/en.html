Seif 13 The wife of a kohen who said to her husband, etc., is forbidden to every kohen in the world, etc. NB: See Beit Shmuel s.k. 25, who wrote that if she married another kohen she need not leave. I was asked by the learned R. Hirsch Treister of Husiatyn why she should not leave, since she made herself like a prohibited object by her own statement. I answered that he sensed the difficulty well; Beit Meir already preceded him and left it difficult. In my humble opinion Beit Shmuel may be understood as speaking where there are no witnesses that she said this under her first husband, but only she now says that she said so under her first husband. One might have thought that since by law she was always believed, and the Sages only enacted non-belief because perhaps she cast her eyes on another, that concern applies specifically when she says, "I was coerced under you," for if we believed that, all women might say so. But to say that she was coerced under her first husband and that she told her first husband so is uncommon, and perhaps the Sages did not enact their rule there. One might also say she has a miggo that she could have said "I was coerced under you"; Beit Shmuel in siman 68 treats some claims as believed because of the quality of the alternative claim. Therefore one might have thought she should be believed, and he teaches that nevertheless she is not believed, perhaps because she is acting with guile, as in the sugyot of a woman who impairs her ketubah and the first chapter of Bava Metzia. After reflection, however, Beit Shmuel's words can be resolved more simply: Beit Meir himself wrote that if she gives an explanation, an amatla, she is certainly believed, and Beit Shmuel speaks without an amatla. In Hilchot Niddah, siman 185, it is explained that if she comes and lies with him, that itself is like an amatla. Here too, once she married another kohen, the marriage functions as an amatla. Initially she may not marry until she explains the amatla, and there are views that an amatla does not help; but if she already married, she need not leave, because the marriage is itself an amatla. If she now repeats under the second husband that she was coerced under the first and has no amatla, then either her first conduct in marrying him serves as an amatla, or her present statement may be because she has set her eyes on another. Therefore she does not leave unless her husband believes her, in which case she leaves because she now strengthens her words that there is no amatla. With the help of Hashem, Beit Shmuel's words are settled. Examine carefully.
