Who is a zona—anyone who is not a bas Yisrael. To the Rambam: a gentile who did not commit harlotry is a zona and one who lies with her is lashed by Torah law; therefore here it deals if she is a Kutit and does not deal with a convert—and not like Choshen Mishpat who asked why he doubled his words, for he afterward wrote "and likewise a convert." To Tosafot and other poskim: only if she committed harlotry is the kohen who lies with her lashed; regarding a convert even when she did not commit harlotry—see siman 16.
That she is forbidden to marry him. So is the Rambam's view chapter 19; and so it seems from Rashi's explanation chapter HaIsha who was lain with by harlotry—meaning a bas Yisrael to a Netin or mamzer; hence among those liable for [negative] commandments she becomes a zona. But Tosafot chapter HaChalutz (Yevamot 44), the Rosh, Semag, and Raavad ruled she becomes a zona only from those liable for death, karet, or from a gentile and slave with whom kiddushin do not take effect. However, from scripture "when she becomes to a strange man" we derive that lying with a Netin, mamzer, Ammonite and Moabite convert disqualifies and she is forbidden to a kohen and her children too are chalalim when she marries, as the Semag wrote explicitly there. The Bach wrote: in truth there is no practical difference from this dispute except regarding lashes—if a kohen lies with her whether he is liable for lashes; but all agree she becomes a zona—only according to Tosafot, Raavad, and these other poskim they hold he is not lashed. To resolve the sugya where it is clear there that one is lashed for her—see Bach what he wrote; his words are very forced. It seems he wrote properly: all agree she becomes a zona, only he is not lashed. To resolve the sugya one may say: there in that sugya he learned the prohibition of kehunah from terumah by kal va-chomer, and they challenged "do we warn from logical inference?" and answered it is mere verbal revelation and one is lashed; and in this matter Abaye and Rava dispute in chapter Four Deaths and chapter HaNisrafim—to Abaye one is lashed for verbal revelation and to Rava one is not lashed; and the halakhah follows Rava against Abaye everywhere except yaaleh veyavo, kadesh, gamar; therefore these poskim hold he is not lashed. In Makkot 17 it states: even according to the one who says punishments from logical inference—nevertheless we do not warn from logical inference; hence for Rava even for verbal revelation we do not warn. The sugya Yevamot there must be according to Abaye who holds for verbal revelation one is punished because it is as though written explicitly; hence one need not distinguish between punishment and warning. At first glance it is astonishing regarding the Rambam who holds he is lashed.
One lain with by an animal. And likewise one lain with by a spirit or demon—even though "oxen and many" she is not forbidden to her husband—see responsa Maharam of Lublin §115.
She is not forbidden to marry. Even though during her niddah she is forbidden; and to the Rambam even chuppah forbids, as he wrote chapter 6 Laws of Marriage—nevertheless this woman is not forbidden and there is remedy for her prohibition.
Who declared herself ownerless. It implies there is no prohibition at all for a kohen to marry her; but the Semag wrote: since the Gemara rules the halakhah is not like R. Eliezer who said an unmarried man who lies with an unmarried woman—she is a zona—hence like the other tannaim there it is fit to be stringent. And according to Maharshal also the Tur holds thus; see siman 15 and siman 26.
And likewise a convert. Behold Rashi explained there in chapter HaIsha that it deals with one lain with by a gentile; and Tosafot, Rambam chapter 18, and Rosh hold: even if she was not lain with she is forbidden to a kohen—even a convert less than three years old we rule she is forbidden to a kohen; see end of siman 16. Their reason: because she came from gentiles steeped in lewdness. According to Raavad and Rashba they derive from scripture of Yechezkel "virgin of the seed of the house of Israel," as written end of Kiddushin; and so is the view of Tosafot there 61a. According to the Rambam we derive from scripture "zona" stated in the Torah; and from scripture of Yechezkel we derive that the verse means thus. See Maggid beginning of chapter 15: convert is not explicit in the Torah—and practical difference from this—see end of siman 165 there.
Except one who lies with the yevamah. Because kiddushin do not take effect, as written "she shall not be"—meaning the language of being—even though we rule she requires a get, that is stringency in general; but kiddushin do not take effect in her, so Maharshal and Bach. And what he wrote "except yevamah"—that is regarding those fit for kiddushin; but gentile and slave—all agree make her a zona.
