I was raped. See siman 115; see in Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri; and below siman 178. It seems: if it is known she secluded with one and says "I am defiled" she is believed—for there are circumstantial grounds; and so in the Gemara there they dispute only when she says "I was raped" whether she is believed; but if she says "defiled" she is believed. See Tosafot Ketubot 63 and in the Rosh chapter 2 of Yevamot and siman 115.
Forbidden to every kohen. That is lekhatchilah; but if she married a kohen she need not leave unless he believes her words; and so the Bach. And if one witness says she committed harlotry—if she denies, the witness is not believed; if she admits, that is the law that she said she committed harlotry. If she is silent, it depends on the dispute in Yoreh De'ah there whether silence is like admission or not.
For behold she admitted. The Raavad wrote: if she gives an excuse for her words that she said thus in order that he divorce her, she is believed; and the Maggid disagrees—since her husband is not obligated to divorce her by her word, she is not believed that she said thus to divorce him. According to this, even for an Israelite's wife who said "I am defiled" and gave this excuse she is not believed; however for other excuses even the Maggid agrees, as written in siman 115—even though the Maggid infers his law that she is not believed from the categories end of Nedarim where it teaches regarding one who says "I am defiled" she is forbidden to eat terumah and did not distinguish whether she gave an excuse for her words or not—it seems at first glance even other excuses do not help; otherwise the above difficulty still stands. One may say: only the excuse the Raavad mentioned applies to all women—why did he not distinguish; but other excuses—why did he not distinguish. Perhaps one may infer from the Raavad who wrote this law that an excuse helps for a kohen's wife and not for an Israelite's wife—for he holds it does not help since she forfeited her kesubah by her first words, as written in siman 115.
That she forbade herself. It seems: if she married a kohen her children are fit—for the Torah gave credibility only to the father to disqualify his sons, not to the mother; and if the father says he believes her words he can make his sons chalalim as one is believed regarding "she is a divorcee's son." However, when he did not find her virginity and she says "from betrothal I was raped"—one may say: if she married a kohen her children are safek chalalim since there is impropriety before us.
