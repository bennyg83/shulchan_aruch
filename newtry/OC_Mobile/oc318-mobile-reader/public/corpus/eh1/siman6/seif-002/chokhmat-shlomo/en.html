In that same seif Therefore, if he transgressed and married a doubtful chalutzah, etc. NB: There are differing views among the poskim about this. Some permit a doubtful chalutzah even initially to a kohen; see Beit Shmuel below in this siman, s.k. 12, and Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 8 in the name of Maggid Mishneh and his supporters. In my humble opinion the matter should be decided as follows. Where the doubtful chalutzah is a real doubt, such as where he betrothed one woman from among five women, or where there is doubt whether the child was a nine-month child from the first husband, so that by Torah law she requires chalitzah out of doubt, then she is rabbinically forbidden to a kohen like a definite chalutzah, at least initially. But if she bore a child from her husband and the child certainly was born, only it did not live thirty days, then by Torah law she requires no chalitzah at all, because most children are viable; the doubt of miscarriage is only rabbinic. In that case, the halachah certainly follows those who permit her even initially to a kohen. Proof for this distinction appears below in siman 164 seif 7: any yevamah whose doubt is rabbinic, whether she has a yavam bond or not, such as a yevamah who bore a child and it is unknown whether its months were complete, if she was betrothed to a kohen who is forbidden to a chalutzah, he does not perform chalitzah for her, for we do not forbid this man's wife to him because of a rabbinic doubt. This implies that with other doubtful chalutzot, betrothal to a kohen would not help and chalitzah would be required; but in this case of a child, which is only a rabbinic doubt, they are lenient if she was already betrothed. Just as that distinction applies there, so too it applies here when permitting a doubtful chalutzah to a kohen. Since in any event some authorities permit every doubtful chalutzah to a kohen, it is preferable to minimize the dispute. One should not object that if she performs chalitzah even out of doubt she becomes forbidden to the kohen, for Tosafot already explained that where chalitzah is done after marriage people may say the Sages investigated and found she definitely needed chalitzah, and they may conclude that a chalutzah is permitted to a kohen. But where the doubtful nature is known from the beginning, the concern is different. The Mechaber's statement that even the scent of a get disqualifies should be studied together with what I wrote elsewhere in my responsa. Beit Shmuel's question from the first chapter of Ketubot, that a lighter prohibition may require greater distancing, is resolved through the sugya in Gittin 53b and related sugyot; the poskim follow the view that with a lighter rabbinic matter we do not always require greater distancing. Examine carefully.
