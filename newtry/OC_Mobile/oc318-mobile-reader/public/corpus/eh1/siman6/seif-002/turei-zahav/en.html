And if after he gave her a get, etc. — when miyun cancels the get: he explains that he revealed upon her that she is a minor and the get is void; and the Gemara says specifically if the first one who divorced her took her back and she exercised miyun — this miyun helps to cancel the get; but if she married another after the get and he died — she is permitted to the first and there is no [issue of] returning his divorcee. But if after the get she married another and exercised miyun against him — this miyun does not help to cancel the get and she remains a divorcee and forbidden to return to the first, as the law of returning his divorcee. The reason: specifically his miyun helps, but not another's miyun; for if you say another's miyun helps — we are concerned that the first sold her with hints and winks, lest he hint to her husband while she is still under the second that she should exercise miyun and return to him, since he loves her though he divorced her against his will; not so with his miyun, for he sees she hates him and will not come to hint that she return to him after marrying another. And the Rambam wrote that there is also this distinction regarding kehunah: if she exercised miyun against the first — the get is void and she is permitted to a kohen; and another's miyun does not help and she remains a divorcee and forbidden to a kohen. The Tur challenged him: granted regarding returning to the first there is reason for the distinction as we mentioned — lest the first come to entice her to leave the second, therefore another's miyun does not help; but regarding permission to a kohen there is no distinction between another's miyun and his miyun. And Beit Yosef wrote the Tur's words are reasonable; nevertheless one can say they did not give their words to measures to say for this matter another's miyun cancels the get and for this matter it does not. Here in Shulchan Aruch he brought the Tur's view in the name of "some say" and did not bring the Rambam's view at all, even though the Rambam's words are dear to him everywhere; and though I am not fit to decide between two great mountains — in my humble opinion the essential view is like the Rambam, for from the Talmud she is forbidden to a kohen on account of a decree lest they see this woman is a divorcee from our forbidding her to the first husband on account of returning his divorcee; and now she married a kohen they will say a divorcee is permitted to a kohen. And clear proof for this from what we say in the beginning of chapter HaIsha: a doubt whether she needs chalitzah — she shall neither perform chalitzah nor marry another; and we say there the reason is lest it afterward become clear through witnesses that the chalitzah was in vain and she would be permitted to a kohen, and the public will err to say a chalutzah is permitted to a kohen; and even if they made a proclamation that the chalitzah was in vain — nevertheless lest some people not hear the proclamation and err to return the chalutzah to a kohen. Behold the matter is a fortiori: there, where according to truth it became clear through witnesses that she chalitzah in vain, they are concerned to permit her to a kohen — all the more so here where in truth the divorce is effective, for we forbid her to the first and they think it is a complete divorce even though she is a minor — divorce helps for her, as they were truly concerned when she was divorced in her minority from the first that they are disqualified from kehunah even though all see she is a minor; nevertheless they were concerned for kehunah. And behold now after miyun from the other they will also say thus — that divorce was not uprooted from her. Proof: from our forbidding her to the other husband; and if she marries a kohen they will say a divorcee is permitted to a kohen, for they do not know to distinguish the reason between them; therefore permission to a kohen is only with the first one's miyun after the get — then she may return to the first and they will know; but where divorce was uprooted from her there — in my humble opinion.
