Or a minor, and afterward, etc. — stam: even while she is still a minor; his method: chapter 2 of Laws of Sotah, chapter 3 of forbidden relations — even a minor who prostituted is forbidden. Maggid Mishneh: proof; Tosafot in Ketubot here challenged, s.v. "and another says," and you will still, etc.; Maggid Mishneh challenged him from Yerushalmi Sotah chapters 1–2: a minor who prostituted has no will to forbid herself to her husband; but the Rambam was wary and wrote: a minor of refusal age cannot be acquired — she has no will to forbid herself to her husband; and even if he were a kohen she would not be forbidden to him; likewise in forbidden relations there; he explains the Yerushalmi regarding a minor of refusal age; Niddah 52a and Rashi there, s.v. "and she," etc.: such as a minor, etc.; but the explanation does not fit the Yerushalmi. There in chapter 1 they asked ben Zoma: why is safek ritual immersion impure? He said: Sotah — what is ritually impure? He said: we find she is forbidden to her house; on them the rabbis dispute: R' Zeira, R' Yissa, R' Yochanan — a minor who prostituted has no will to forbid herself to her husband. We taught: whoever lacks understanding to be asked — his safek is pure; if he has understanding to be asked — his safek is impure. However, here — even though he has understanding to ask — his safek is pure. If per this explanation regarding a minor — the reason is that there is no kiddushin at all; if her father betrothed her — likewise she is forbidden. Likewise chapter 2 there: may her husband separate from her without her knowledge, etc.; four lacking atonement — one separates for them without knowledge: zav, zavah, leper, woman who gave birth; so a man separates for his minor child in a cradle — fine for zav, zavah, leper, childbirth; a minor can give birth, etc.; rather, a man separates for his deaf wife. Here, a minor Sotah — you cannot; R' Zeira and R' Yirmiyah in the name of R' Yochanan: a minor who prostituted has no will to forbid herself to her husband; you cannot regarding a deaf woman — as written: "and the woman said, Amen, Amen," etc.; if it exists — betrothal by her father. Further question: chapter 4 of brothers 33b — what is enticing, etc.; there it discusses definite kiddushin — mishnah: liable, etc.; Israel — permitted to rejoice, etc.; his words apply also to a kohen; chapter Baal Keri 61:2; see siman 178 seif 3 and siman 68 seif 7 in hagahah; some say further, etc.; Tosafot's challenge; Maggid Mishneh himself in the commentators' name.
Husband's stamp — language of the Yerushalmi: we taught there — she says "I am mukat etz," and he says "husband's stamp." What is the wonder in Ketubot — two hundred for confirmation? He cannot, because of safek Sotah — from R' Ila in the name of R' Elazar: found an open door — forbidden to confirm, because of safek Sotah. Maggid Mishneh wrote: Rambam's "husband's stamp" includes both claims regarding virginity, as siman 178 seif 3 and 7; Ketubot 9:2: Abaye said — we too, etc., do not split, etc.; also Rambam: even if she reconciles herself and says "I was raped" or "before betrothal" or "mukat etz" — she is not believed; difficult; see siman 68 seif 7 in hagahah; there answered for a novice; therefore: an adult or minor — in claiming marital relations there is a distinction: a bogeret has no claim of marital relations, as seif 3; he said "husband's stamp" — as above.
