Or after the kiddushin. Beit Yosef wrote that if he betrothed and immediately had relations, it appears that even the wife of a kohen is permitted, for since she certainly was not violated while under him, she is permitted. It appears the same applies if he came to her later, and witnesses testify that she was not separated from them during that entire time in a manner leaving no doubt that she had relations with another then; even the wife of a kohen would be permitted. It appears that this latter case speaks where she was suspect in his eyes and he intentionally stationed guards to testify about her. But otherwise, if witnesses simply testify that she did not have relations, their testimony certainly does not help, for a matter not placed upon people to notice is not fixed in their mind; perhaps they are mistaken and only think so. With a captive woman, even one witness that she was not defiled helps, because since she is under a presumptive prohibition, the witness intends to observe and testify with reliable knowledge. But with this woman, who knows that testimony will later be needed? Therefore there is no reliable testimony unless they were appointed for this purpose. The proof is from the responsum of Re'em, rule 59 siman 1, brought by Rama in Choshen Mishpat siman 29: if one witness says another person was with him, and that person says he was not with him, this is not contradiction, because a witness is not precise about who was present at the time of testimony. All the more so here, people do not carefully observe whether a woman committed adultery, unless as I explained. A marginal note objects that even designated witnesses may not help, from the Gemara that priestly youths were assigned to accompany her, and still the Gemara says perhaps she needed to relieve herself, for are the priestly youths hanging from her? The glossator answers that there they were not designated to guard her in that way, and when she needed to relieve herself they did not place it upon themselves to guard her. Thus the distinction remains.
