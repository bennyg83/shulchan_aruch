A kohen is forbidden — see responsum Chut HaShani siman 17: a man presumed a kohen fifteen years for all matters of sanctity — lifting hands, opening first, reading first, defiling for the dead — and now betrothed a divorcee and denied his kehunah, saying he was never a kohen and only claimed kehunah for honor. It is plain a kohen cannot deny a long-established presumption — even thirty days is a presumption even for capital cases (below siman 19 seif 2), all the more fifteen years. One cannot say he gave an excuse that he acted for honor — Ketubot chapter 2 (below siman 47 seif 4) — this fails for several reasons: the excuse is not clear; every act nullifies excuse — here he acted by ascending the platform, etc.; even for those who hold excuse helps (YD 185) — only briefly and not openly; one who acted so brazenly; many acts — lifting hands, signing as kohen, observing all laws of kehunah many times — excuse certainly does not help; wherever one makes a prohibition not as himself we establish he did not sin until madness entered him; presumption of lifting hands is stronger; excuse only removes damage, not benefit; sages' excuse is as if forced from outside, not self-sought benefit (Brit Avraham siman 94 letter 6 questioned this — see there); therefore sages did not mention excuse for all who make themselves like one witness (see below siman 115 seif 6 s.k. 29); long time — excuse implausible (Beit Shmuel below siman 19; Pitchei Teshuva there); though no fixed measure, excuse depends on sages' view — from all reasons I say he cannot deny kehunah and we compel divorce like any kohen. No distinction between today and earlier generations with lineage records — since all kohanim today are kohanim by presumption, this kohen is like others — see there; see below siman 47 seif 4; Minchat Ani siman 48: kohen who transgressed and betrothed unfit woman then divorced — need repentance? We rule like Rava Kiddushin 78 — betrothal no lashes — no prohibition? Perhaps no lashes but prohibition exists — needs atonement; Tosafot Bava Metzia 10b suggests prohibition; rejected — no proof; clear proof Sotah 44 — betrothal alone even rabbinic prohibition none — no atonement; see Tosafot Yevamot 10a R' Yehuda perhaps forbidden rabbinically lest he have relations — see there; found Bava Metzia below siman 15 seif 1 holds rabbinic prohibition exists, cites Tosafot Yevamot — Sotah 44 proves no rabbinic prohibition in betrothal; R' Yehuda HaGlili's baraita not for law but to explain verse before sages forbade betrothal — see there.
