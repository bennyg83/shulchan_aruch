So too the Rambam explains: in a place where she has no miggo — such as when she certainly sits on the testimony of two witnesses and there is doubt whether she is pure — and I say: in this safek why do we not establish her on her presumption that when she was born she was not a zona? One can say: since she was taken captive, presumably she was defiled, as above.
As a mere stringency she is disqualified for kehunah, etc. Beit Yosef in siman 150, which should read siman 13 below, brings this in the name of Responsa Rashba, siman 550, and writes as follows: all the more so this woman, about whom a rumor emerged and whom you required to receive a get, for people will say the Sages upheld the kiddushin and that it was proper kiddushin, etc. Even though he gave her a get only as a precaution, you would require him to make a proclamation, and even if we proclaim it, there may be one who heard about the kiddushin but did not hear the proclamation, like that case at the beginning of chapter HaCholetz. End quote. It follows from this that even after the fact, if a kohen married her he must divorce her. For at the beginning of chapter HaIshah SheHalach we also say this concern, that there may be one who heard of the chalitzah and did not hear the proclamation, etc., and there we say that because of this she may never marry; there is no greater after-the-fact case than this, for she has no remedy. So wrote Ran, brought by Beit Yosef, siman 6; regarding another matter it should read siman 156. So too the language of Rama implies, for he wrote that she is disqualified for kehunah, which implies even after the fact. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that where she was divorced only because of a mere rumor and the get was only because of stringency, if he transgressed and married her, we do not remove her from him. There is no proof for this leniency, and one should not be lenient in this.
