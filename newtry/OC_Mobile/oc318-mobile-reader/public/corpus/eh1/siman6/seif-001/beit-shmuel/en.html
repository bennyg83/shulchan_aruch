Only on account of a rumor of betrothal. In responsum Maharshal §25 he wrote: only when she is betrothed need we be concerned for such a rumor; but in general we need not be concerned. See Hagahot Maimoniot chapter HaOmer and responsum Ramban §132 and responsum of the Rosh in Tur siman 46 and Beit Yosef (Rav Saadiah Gaon); there it is explained that only if the court required a get—even though it was on account of a mere rumor—we are concerned; but if he gave a get on his own and it was not required by law, it is not a valid get. He brought Darchei Moshe there, and so Beit HaLevi. However, in responsum Rashba §350 it seems at first glance to contradict this, for there too it was betrothal and he divorced on his own without the court's knowledge, yet he rules there that it is a valid get. Perhaps in responsum §350 it is different: although by law he did not need to divorce, yet since he divorced on his own we say perhaps the matter is true that he betrothed her—unlike in the responsa the Beit Yosef cited, where there were only two witnesses and one died; then there is no concern at all and no get is needed at all.
Disqualified for kehunah. The Bach wrote: this is lekhatchilah; but she need not leave on that account. Therefore the Rashba's responsum must deal with betrothal to a kohen where he did not betroth or marry her; therefore he rules there she is forbidden to him.
