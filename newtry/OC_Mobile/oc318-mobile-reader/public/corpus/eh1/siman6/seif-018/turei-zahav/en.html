And likewise stam, etc. — this is the Tur's view who reads like our version "they went to them," etc. — meaning certainly they went to the city so it was fixed and there is only one majority of caravan people; stam implies we are not stringent to say perhaps she went there — rather there is also a majority of the city. The reason: since in any case there is one clearly and the second majority is doubt — we do not decree here on account of the city alone since in the city too there is doubt; and they did not decree on account of city majority but on account of caravan majority that is certainly fixed there (emendation: where he emended "city" should read "caravan" — poorly emended; rather thus is the explanation: what they said one majority does not help — this is if the city majority or fixed person is certain; but with doubtful fixed it is like two majorities — understand — end of words). And Beit Yosef wrote the reason of the Tur: because there is no stringency here except on account of lineage, and for this even lineage stringency is permitted as written. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran: in a place where if she married a kohen she need not leave bedi'eved — and likewise she is believed to validate the fetus since they were not stringent on it at all; for if they were stringent on it to enter the congregation there would be no remedy; therefore the child's law is like the woman's law when she married, etc. — end quote. It is clear: just as regarding the woman herself who is believed for a kohen bedi'eved — so too the law of her daughter to marry an Israelite ab initio even ab initio with majority unfit to enter the congregation, since there is no alternative; but to marry her daughter to a kohen — certainly forbidden ab initio like her mother. A kohen who had relations with a single woman and she bore a son and he admits the son is fit for kehunah and does not disqualify because his seed is not attributed after him — Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of the Rosh.
