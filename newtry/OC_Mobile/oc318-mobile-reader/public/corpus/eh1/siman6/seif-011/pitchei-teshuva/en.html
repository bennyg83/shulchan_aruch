Who was raped — Baer Heitev: all whose beginning of intercourse was by force, etc.; see responsum Noda BiYehudah tinyana siman 150 — brought Maharshal chapter Baal Keri siman 3: even woman if acts at end — we do not say yetzer clothed her; he wrote his ruling seems wondrous — since yetzer clothed and forced her, the act too is by force — see there. Noda BiYehudah tinyana 21: rule — where witnesses see from afar she is lain with and cannot determine consent or force, yet see she does not fight — in field we say rape; not fighting because she sensed weakness cannot fight, fears murder; screaming irrelevant in field; in city certainly consented since did not resist with all strength; no merit she knew weakness — should have screamed for help; where she says she fought until insertion with all strength — even in city without scream permitted to Israelite husband; beginning force end willingness counts as rape. Found likewise Ramban on Torah portion Ki Teitzei; brief in his responsa (see Nitziv); Maharam Alashkar siman 94 differed — perhaps Noda BiYehudah did not have his book (siman 15); if he had, might accept — he cites Rishonim: lain with in city — do not rely on her claim of rape without proof such as sword drawn or mouth stopped — see Maharam Alashkar; see Chavot Yair 145–146; Or Zaru'a Panim Me'irot part 3 siman 22; Chemdat Shlomo siman 60. Baer Heitev from Shulchan Arukh: woman saved husband — see Beit Meir below siman 178 seif 3 copied: even to save herself from fear he would kill her — forbidden (where rapist did not demand intercourse only threatened death and she appeased to escape). Baer Heitev: if force was for intercourse — Beit Meir wrote: not only if he overpowered and lay with her — plainly; even like Esther initially forced — not willing until Ahasuerus demanded with royal fear — forced for intercourse itself — permitted to husband Mordechai — see more; Pitchei Teshuva YD siman 257 s.k. 2 — examine. Noda BiYehudah tinyana 161: after Mahari"k in Beit Shmuel siman 148 s.k. 4 — permitting intentional prostitution to save lives is not Torah and not upright; cited responsa citing Beit Yaakov — Esther different with Mordechai and Sanhedrin and perhaps ruach hakodesh — see there.
