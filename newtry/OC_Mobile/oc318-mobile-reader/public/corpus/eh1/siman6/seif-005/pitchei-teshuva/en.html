He must divorce. See Ba'er Hetev, and what he wrote in the name of R. Yehudah HaLevi, that if one married a doubtful captive woman he need not divorce her. See Shaar HaMelekh, chapter 19 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, law 23, who wrote that at first glance the law is with him, since this is a double doubt and should be like an almanat issa, where Rambam and Shulchan Aruch above, siman 2 seif 5, write that if she married she need not leave because there are two doubts. In truth this is not so, for Issur VeHeter, brought by Peri Chadash in the rules of double doubt, wrote that Jewish cheeses left in a gentile's house, where there is doubt whether they were switched, are not called a double doubt. Since the Sages forbade gentile cheeses because of that doubt, the matter is treated as a definite prohibition, and two further doubts are needed besides the original doubt for which the Sages prohibited it. The rule that a rabbinic prohibition is permitted with one doubt applies only where the core prohibition is rabbinic because of a decree. So too here: since the Sages forbade a captive woman because of the doubt that she was defiled, it is treated as a definite prohibition and is not permitted either by double doubt or by the rule of doubtful rabbinic law.
