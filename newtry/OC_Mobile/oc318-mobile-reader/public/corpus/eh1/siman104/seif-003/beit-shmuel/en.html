(ב) And they did not write an audit document. Meaning: in any case where they announced the sale but were not exact in the appraisal, then if they overvalued by a sixth or undervalued by a sixth, the sale is void. At first glance it is difficult: why did the Shas not establish the first clause as a case where they announced but did not make an audit document? Then the Mishnah would be precise. Ran raised a similar difficulty. Perhaps the first and last clauses are established where they made an audit document, and nevertheless it does not help in a place where they do not announce. What was written nearby, that at a time when one need not announce and they erred by a sixth the sale is void even though they announced, means even if they announced and made an audit document.
(ג) And they overvalued by a sixth or undervalued. Although the buyer was wronged, and generally land has no ona'ah when one buys personally, here it is different because he buys from the judges: just as the orphans rely on the beit din, so the buyer relies on them. So wrote the Rosh in the name of Ri. However, Rambam and Ramban hold that even when one buys from beit din there is no ona'ah unless they brought the creditor down to an appraisal. See Choshen Mishpat siman 109.
