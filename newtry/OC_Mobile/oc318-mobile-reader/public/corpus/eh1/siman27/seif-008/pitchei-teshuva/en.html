You are my married woman — some say it is nothing. See in Sefer HaMakneh part 1 who wrote it appears where kiddushin and nissuin come as one such as he brought her into his house and betrothed her as explained below siman 33 in hagahah; and likewise per Maggid Mishneh who wrote above (will be brought below siman 61 s.k. 1) if he brought her to chuppah and afterward betrothed her she becomes married at time of kiddushin; and in such case language "my married woman" applies more than "my betrothed"; and relevant are documents of nissuin (meaning this is Gemara's intent Kiddushin folio 9b documents of nissuin — if he wrote in document "You are my married woman" as I wrote siman 33 end quote).
