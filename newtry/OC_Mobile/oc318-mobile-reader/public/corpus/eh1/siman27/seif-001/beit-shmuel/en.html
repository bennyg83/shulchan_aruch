Perutah. Below siman 31 s.k. 5 I wrote they do not betroth lekhatchilah with coin; and Ran wrote beginning Kiddushin and Tosafot and Rashba — if he betrothed with something attached to land it is kiddushin even though if he betrothed with document attached it is not kiddushin because we compare acquisition to release — therefore document attached is invalid like get attached; but money is not relevant to get — therefore if he betrothed with land she is betrothed. Also one can say get attached is invalid because it lacks cutting but kiddushin we hold even if it did not come to her hand she is betrothed like "give to so-and-so" — see Hagahot Maharit.
Worth perutah. Tosafot wrote beginning Kiddushin we derive from Hebrew slave that worth of money is like money; and in novellae of Rav Yosef Trani — if he conditioned on condition I will give such-and-such and gave worth of money against her will — it is not giving since he gave worth of money against her will. Difficult — behold Rashba wrote in teshuvah and Beit Yosef brought it siman 143 — even per one who says giving against will is giving — meaning the giver fulfilled giving; but for the recipient it is not giving for one cannot obligate her against will such as if she said give me a maneh and I will betroth myself to you and he gave against will — it is not kiddushin; per this even if he gave money and not worth of money it is not kiddushin since she did not consent. And possible there discusses when she conditioned the condition "give me a maneh" — then we say giving against will is not giving; but when he conditioned on condition I will give you — then even against will it is giving for he conditioned the condition; nevertheless afterward wrote in Maharit even in such case it is not giving. And see siman 143 there explained giving against will — Rosh holds it is doubt get; and Rambam holds it is rabbinically invalid; and presumably likewise in kiddushin this dispute applies. And apparently from Rosh's words chapter 4 what he wrote — he holds even giving against will and worth of money is doubt — for he wrote in the sugya there if he conditioned she will give a coat — coat specifically he says and not money for the coat; and reason is we say the husband intends to pain her; and Rosh wrote if she consented to take money it is giving — we learn the sugya discusses giving against will; nevertheless if he did not intend to pain — it is giving — we learn even giving against will and worth of money helps; however per what is written siman 143 there is no proof from Rosh — see teshuvah Mahara ben Sasson siman 65 and Choshen Mishpat siman 241 seif 7; and there is more to bring proof giving against will and worth of money is giving from Hebrew slave — except there in the novellae he rejects this proof and wrote Hebrew slave is different for Torah doubled the law; and apparently his words are wondrous for if so from where did Talmud beginning Kiddushin learn from Hebrew slave worth of money is like money.
And says to her. And if he said "Take your kiddushin from atop the land" — behold this is doubt kiddushin — teshuvah Shach.
You are betrothed to me. Tosafot wrote beginning Kiddushin — "to me" meaning to be mine; therefore "You are betrothed to me" is language of sanctification upon all people for my sake as if he said "You are betrothed and forbidden to all the world for my sake."
That she understand. Such is Tur's language — implies even if he said "You are betrothed to me" he must know she understands it is language of kiddushin — for not all women understand holy tongue; and likewise implied in Ran and Maggid and likewise Bach and not like Beit Yosef; nevertheless it appears all is according to the matter: if it is known this woman understands — it is kiddushin; and in other languages if she says she did not understand — it is doubt kiddushin lest she lie — and likewise R' Yehudah of Orleans and Bach. And what Rosh wrote — these languages are clear to us they are language of kiddushin — it is not in his power to say "I did not know" — one can explain his words: she is not believed to uproot kiddushin entirely but it is doubt; and this is what Tur wrote "provided she understand" — for where she would certainly be betrothed to him.
Spoke with her. Rambam wrote chapter 3 law 1 — he spoke with the woman about matters of kiddushin and she consented and he stood and betrothed etc.; and Kesef Mishneh wrote specifically that she said yes but silence does not help; nevertheless from other poskim it does not appear so and likewise from Hagahot Maimoniyot end Gittin as written nearby in hagahah — when they speak before her it suffices — we learn even if she was silent it helps; and one can say likewise for Rambam if she was silent it is consent. Also in Hagahot Maharit he challenged Kesef Mishneh; and per all specifically when they speak about matters of her kiddushin — see teshuvah Mahari of Lublin siman 2.
