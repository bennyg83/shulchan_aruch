You are my married woman etc. Rashba wrote in teshuvah — if he said "You are my married woman" it is not like "You are my wife"; nevertheless one can say it is still doubt kiddushin; and what he wrote it is not like "You are my wife" — meaning it is not definite kiddushin. And one can compare to saying for love and affection as written nearby; and likewise Taz. And all this discusses only when he did not speak with her about matters of kiddushin; but if he spoke with her about matters of kiddushin — she is certainly betrothed; and likewise in Hagahot Maharit and Bedek HaBayit — until here Gemara does not discuss except languages that can be interpreted for work or other matter; but "my married woman" does not imply any other meaning — if so when he spoke with her about matters of kiddushin she is certainly betrothed; and likewise Choshen Mishpat and not as implication of this hagahah.
And this when he spoke with her etc. And if he said in these languages he did not intend for kiddushin — one can say he is believed; and even per view of Beit Yosef who holds the man is not believed to say he did not understand it is language of kiddushin as written nearby — one can say these languages are different for Gemara doubts whether they are language of kiddushin — he is believed to say he did not intend for kiddushin; and likewise Choshen Mishpat.
For love and affection etc. Meaning language he said for love and affection is as if he said on condition there be love and affection between them and it is no less than saying "set apart for me"; and if both their intent was for kiddushin — get is needed — likewise Mordekhai. And it appears his intent — even if he spoke with her about matters of kiddushin even though it is not meaning for work — nevertheless since he said for love and did not say there be love between them — it is as if he said he gives to her for love that already exists between them — therefore it is only doubt kiddushin even if he spoke with her about matters of kiddushin. And one can disagree regarding saying "You are my married woman" as I wrote nearby — if he spoke with her about matters of kiddushin she is certainly betrothed.
