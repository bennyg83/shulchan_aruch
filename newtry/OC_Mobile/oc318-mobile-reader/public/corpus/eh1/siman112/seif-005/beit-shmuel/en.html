(ב) And likewise the daughter of a sheniyah, who has no ketubah or added ketubah. The Talmud asks: what is the law of her daughter? One may say that the Sages penalized her because she committed a prohibition, but not her daughter. Likewise, the daughter of a woman raped by her husband, who has no ketubah because he cannot divorce her, is a question whether she has the added ketubah. Likewise, the daughter of an arusah, for whom the Sages did not enact a ketubah until nisuin. In all these cases the question remains unresolved, and she has no sustenance. Ri"v wrote that if she seized, we do not remove it from her. Shiltei Giborim wrote that with limited assets we do not remove it from her, but with abundant assets they have no right except sustenance day by day; therefore we remove it from her. After removing it, perhaps they have no right at all. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cited Ri"v's words without qualification. However, in an unresolved question there is a dispute whether seizure helps, as I wrote in Choshen Mishpat in several places. According to Rambam, the daughter of a girl who performed mi'un has sustenance. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2 law 1, explained that this means she performed mi'un while pregnant; before she gave birth she could perform mi'un, and afterward she gave birth. See what I wrote on this in siman 155. Maharam of Tiktin and Bach explained that it speaks of mistaken kiddushin, but according to what I wrote nearby this is not so. The Ran and the Maggid Mishneh left in difficulty from where Rambam derived this law: since a girl who performed mi'un has no ketubah, from where do we know that her daughter has sustenance? It appears that Rambam holds the whole sugya speaks only where she had no ketubah at the time of marriage, and there the question is whether she has the added ketubah; but if she had a ketubah by virtue of the marriage and only afterward caused herself the loss, her daughter did not lose her own right. Would one say that if she committed adultery and lost the ketubah, her daughter loses her right? From this it is proven unlike Maharam above and Bach, for if the case were mistaken kiddushin, then retroactively there was no marriage at all and she had no ketubah from the very time of marriage.

But Rashi and Ran explained that he did not marry her, but only wrote her a ketubah while she was betrothed, and died.
