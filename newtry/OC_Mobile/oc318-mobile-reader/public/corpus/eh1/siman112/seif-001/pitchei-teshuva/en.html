The daughters are sustained. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, siman 95, regarding a case where a man died leaving a creditor, and his estate was insufficient to pay the debts. The assets were placed with an apotropos until the creditors would come, each to take his due. The deceased left two sons and a minor daughter. Since they assumed it would be necessary to pay all the creditors, everything remained set aside and nothing was given from it for the orphans' sustenance. The daughter's mother kept her with her, sustained and supported her, and married her off. After many years one creditor did not come to claim his debt and his location was unknown. The heirs then wished to divide the assets, and the daughter also came to claim the sustenance due to her from childhood until majority. The rabbi who asked ruled that all the assets belong to the sons and that the daughter has no claim for sustenance, since she had sustenance from elsewhere, her mother having sustained her; the enactment of sustenance for daughters was made only so that they not need to beg, and this one had already been sustained. The Noda BiYehudah answered that this reasoning is mentioned in Knesset HaGedolah, but he does not agree with it. In Hagahot HaTur, letter 6, it states: if someone suspected with her undertook to sustain her, she has no sustenance from the brothers, citing R. Y. Halevi, siman 2; but in the name of another sage he wrote that even so she has sustenance, and this latter view is preferable. In truth, however, the matter depends on the dispute between Rif and Rambam on one side, and Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh on the other, regarding the question in Ketubot 53 whether a betrothed girl has sustenance. The Mechaber deals with this dispute in seif 3 like Rif and Rambam, and Beit Shmuel, s.k. 6, wrote that the betrothed man can say kim li like Rashi. Therefore, the final ruling is as follows: if the assets remaining at the time of the deceased's death were abundant, see seif 11 for the definition of abundant, then even though he owed much to others they are still called abundant, as explained in seif 15. In that case all the assets are in the sons' possession, except that they must provide sustenance for the daughter; since this is a doubtful matter of law, they can say kim li like Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh. But if the assets were limited, meaning they sufficed only for the daughter's sustenance until she reached majority, then everything would go to the daughter. It is therefore doubtful whether everything belongs to the daughter or everything to the sons, and since the assets are deposited with an apotropos as a deposit, this is money in doubt and the sons and daughter divide it. However, this would apply if movables remained, for regarding movables, even with limited assets, the sons are at least equal to the daughters, as explained in seif 12; if so, the sons certainly have a right in these assets and the daughters are doubtful, and a doubt cannot remove a certainty. Although Rama wrote in the gloss there that where they write in the ketubah movables together with land, one could say movables are like land, Rama was not certain enough to decide that reasoning, and the daughters have only a double doubt. Thus, if the assets were abundant at death, the sons can certainly say kim li and everything belongs to them. If they were limited, as above, there would be room to examine the matter; however, since the rabbi who asked already ruled that everything belongs to the sons, we do not reverse the ruling.
