And some say specifically from erusin. In Perishah he wrote: if she became betrothed as a na'arah after her father's death and was divorced while still a na'arah, she has sustenance, because from the time her father died she had a lien and debt upon the brothers. Even though it lapsed when she became betrothed, nevertheless once she was divorced her right returned, comparable to a minor who married and then performed mi'un, who returns to her sustenance. Taz disagrees with this, for it is not comparable to mi'un, which uproots the first marriage, unlike erusin of a na'arah. According to the some say view, it implies that even if she became widowed after her father's death she has sustenance, but I do not know their source, for the Tur and Ran wrote specifically where she became widowed during her father's lifetime, and we have found no posek who holds that erusin gives her sustenance even after her father's death. I did not see any indication of this in the Tur. Perhaps he intended Rashi's view, who explained the sugya as from erusin and did not explain that she became widowed during her father's lifetime. But at first glance, it appears from Rashi that since we hold that an arusah has no sustenance, even if she became widowed she has no sustenance; that baraita holds that an arusah does have sustenance, but we do not rule so, rather like Rav who says that an arusah has no sustenance. There are four approaches in this: according to Rashi, even if she was widowed from erusin she has no sustenance, and this implies even during her father's lifetime. According to the Rosh and Ran, from erusin she does have sustenance when she was widowed during her father's lifetime. According to Rabbenu Tam, even from nisuin she has sustenance when she was widowed during her father's lifetime. According to Rambam, even if she married and became widowed after her father's death, she has sustenance. One should not ask: if so, why would one think that a girl who performed mi'un has no sustenance from nisuin? One may answer: a girl who performs mi'un is different, since she caused it, and one might have thought she has no sustenance. Beit Yosef; Kesef Mishneh.
