And behold per view of Tosafot even a thing that cannot last to bring it to another country where it is worth a perutah — nevertheless there is concern for kiddushin, for we are concerned lest there is someone from that place here where he betroths her; and if she leaves without a get he will say it is impossible she should leave without a get. But Rambam does not hold this concern — rather the concern is specifically with a lasting thing to bring to another place where it is worth a perutah — then we are concerned lest worth a perutah elsewhere and it can last to bring there; and if it is a thing that cannot last to bring elsewhere while wet and it is possible to dry it and last — it is a lasting thing — so too in responsum Maharik. Rosh wrote in name of R' Yehuda — even if it is known worth a perutah elsewhere they are only rabbinic kiddushin, for consecration applies only to its place and time; and since it is not worth a perutah in its place there is no kiddushin from Torah. Rosh holds likewise as R' Yehuda; Maggid in name of Ramban and Rashba; Rambam also holds so. However Rosh wrote Rambam holds when it is known worth a perutah elsewhere she is betrothed from Torah; and likewise Ran holds she is betrothed from Torah. And it seems it discusses when witnesses know worth a perutah elsewhere — then one can say she is betrothed from Torah; but if witnesses do not know — it is as though he betrothed without witnesses as written nearby — there is only concern for kiddushin because witnesses are uncertain lest worth a perutah elsewhere. And it seems even if witnesses know — must say the reason is as though she said "for me it is worth a perutah" since it is worth elsewhere; even though generally Ran is uncertain whether what she says "for me it is worth a perutah" helps — that is where witnesses do not know worth a perutah elsewhere; but if known worth a perutah it helps.
