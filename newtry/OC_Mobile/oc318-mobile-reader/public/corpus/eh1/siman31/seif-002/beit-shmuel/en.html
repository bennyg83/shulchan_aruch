And sometimes they err greatly in them. Meaning per explanation of Rosh — she thinks it is not worth so much; therefore there is no stringency unless he said it is worth such and such; and per Ran the reason is she thinks it is worth much more; therefore there is no distinction whether he betrothed unspecified or said in any case; and Rambam also holds the reason like Rosh and distinguishes between jewelry that a woman desires — then she relies — and in other matters she did not rely.
And if he betrothed her with a ring that has a stone — this is doubtful kiddushin. Because for several poskim she did not rely; and when there is forgery — or per some who say he brought nearby — she is not betrothed at all, for with precious stones when he betroths unspecified they say she thinks it is worth a large sum and she does not want to be betrothed except for a large sum; if so all the more in such a case where she thought it was gold and she does not want to be betrothed unless it is gold. However per Rashbatz we are concerned for kiddushin since he betroths her unspecified.
Nevertheless one should be stringent. Even though if he deceived her she is not betrothed — here is different for he did not deceive her but the witnesses.
They also customarily cover, etc. Even though they ask the witnesses if it is worth a perutah — nevertheless one can say she did not rely because she saw it is worth more; and similar to that which is in Choshen Mishpat siman 227 regarding ona'ah — if he said to him it is not worth but one zuz he can claim ona'ah because when he saw it is worth more he thought it is worth all he gave; so too she thought it is worth more — therefore they cover her face; and if it is not worth a perutah and she says "for me it is worth a perutah" — Ran is uncertain whether what she says "for me it is worth more" helps; and Mordechai wrote we do not betroth with a coin — and that is rabbinically; see Tosafot and Rosh first chapter of Kiddushin.
