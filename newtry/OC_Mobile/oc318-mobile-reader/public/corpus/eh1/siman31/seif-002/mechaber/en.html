Some say that if he betrothed her with something people are not expert in appraising and sometimes err greatly — such as precious stones and pearls and the like — and he said to her "Be betrothed to me with this stone that is worth fifty zuz" — it requires appraisal, for she cannot rely on it. And there are those who say that even if he betrothed her with a stone without saying it is worth fifty zuz it requires appraisal. Therefore the custom is to betroth with a ring that has no stone. {Rama: And if he betrothed her with a ring that has a stone, or with an ordinary ring and it turns out to be copper — even though it is not customary to betroth thus we are concerned for betrothal (Beit Yosef citing responsum of Rosh bar Tzemach and Mordechai first chapter of Kiddushin). And even if witnesses under the wedding canopy said it was gold and it turns out copper — even though for law it seems she is not betrothed — nevertheless for practical application one should rule stringently (responsum of Maimonides at end of Laws of Women). And nevertheless under the canopy they customarily ask witnesses whether the ring is worth a perutah so the bride knows she is betrothed only with something worth a perutah (Orach Chaim). They also customarily cover the faces of modest brides and they are not particular about what they are betrothed with (explained in responsum of Rashba).}
