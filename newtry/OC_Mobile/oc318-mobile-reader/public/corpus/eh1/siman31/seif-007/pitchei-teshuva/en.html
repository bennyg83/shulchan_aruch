Ba'er Heitev what is written; however Rosh wrote there Rambam holds when known worth, etc.; see Kav HaYashar who wrote on this: and it seems the reason that we require specifically perutah in kiddushin of a woman is from two reasons: one we require money — we derive kichah kichah from field of Ephron and it is written silver there and less than perutah is not called silver; two a woman does not transfer herself with less than perutah. And per this if worth elsewhere she is properly betrothed — since worth in Media it is money from Torah (meaning per Rambam it is so but per Rosh and several poskim it is not called money unless worth here); and that a woman with less than perutah does not transfer herself — since she extended her hand and accepted the kiddushin this reason does not apply as explained Kiddushin 11a; and afterward I found in Sefer HaMakneh he wrote so. And Ran wrote the reason brought in Beit Shmuel — since worth elsewhere and she agreed to accept kiddushin it is as though she said "for me it is worth," etc.; and per these reasons it seems specifically when he betrothed her with a date that has fixed price and all know it is not worth perutah and nevertheless she accepted; and also she betrothed herself by day and not through agent and saw it is not worth perutah — behold either she agreed even with less than perutah or for her it is worth; but with a thing unknown to all that it is not worth perutah or she betrothed herself at night or through agent — in this certainly no concern for kiddushin (it seems to me this refers to what adjoins kiddushin at night or through agent; not to what began but to thing unknown to all — here will explain its law below; and law is somewhat wavering for behold her reasoning is worth perutah — if so not applicable to say behold she agreed and we say woman with less than perutah does not transfer herself and is not betrothed. Per this with a thing requiring appraisal and not all know it is not worth perutah and can rely she did not want to transfer herself for less than perutah value; and that she accepted because she thought it is worth perutah — here it seems if it is a thing known worth elsewhere there is doubtful kiddushin — say she agreed say she erred and thought it is worth perutah; however with a thing unknown if worth elsewhere — here she is not betrothed at all for it is double doubt perhaps not worth anywhere — if so there is no Torah money; and even if you say worth elsewhere she erred and thought worth here; and with less than perutah here she did not want to transfer her soul, etc.; and with this early authorities' questions are resolved in that which he betrothed with a mat, etc. — end quote see there. And per this he wrote there in the asked case — one who betrothed a girl before witnesses with one riffel from a small barrel in her father's house and said to her "behold you are betrothed to me with this ring" as they thought and you are witnesses; and now he and she say for jest and mockery they intended from appraisal of experts the riffel is worth a tenth of perutah; and expanded there there is no kiddushin concern at all — for even if this riffel were his there would be no Torah kiddushin concern in such case only rabbinic concern lest there be someone from Media as said above; and now after this riffel is not his since he took it in her father's house and owners did not know to waive — even though one can say since it is wood and mere stick that they throw to garbage heaps — once he lifted it he acquired per all, etc.; nevertheless it seems specifically with a thing worth nothing such as sticks and crumbs his throwing to garbage in house helps; but if there were perutah worth in it this would not help, etc.; and if so in our case there is concern only for reason Tosafot gave lest someone from Media people be found here; and need to distinguish — if so one who betroths with robbery why not concerned for kiddushin lest there is one here who does not know it is robbery? Must say they were not concerned for this for presumably he knows the matter as it is; but with a date even knowing whole matter he will say he released without get because he betrothed her with a date and will think the date causes and not the worth; and if so here betrothing with stolen stick — in any case no concern: if this man from Media comes and knows matter as it is that he lifted stick from her father's house and betrothed her with it — if he thinks it was worth perutah then it is robbery; if he knew it is a thing with no perutah worth in our city and a thing she is not particular about and he acquired through owners' despair by throwing to loss — then she is not betrothed from reason of less than perutah and permitted in any case.
