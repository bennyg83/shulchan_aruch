He said to her be betrothed, etc. But Rambam wrote in the first two she is betrothed; and per his method to rule like "if you say" and "go" to your two halves with a perutah — here one can say he counts and proceeds unlike your two halves with a perutah where his intent is she be betrothed in halves and a woman is not betrothed in halves — Ran see there; and it seems to me such is view of Geonim — for otherwise all things he wrote that every "if you say" is plain is hidden; and here he says afterward and questions with your two halves with a perutah — collection.
She is doubtfully betrothed. Not like Rambam who explained "if you say" — for the last question your two halves with a perutah is strongest of all and nevertheless they resolved with teiku — Ra'avad, Rosh, Rashba, Ran; and nevertheless they reconciled his words.
