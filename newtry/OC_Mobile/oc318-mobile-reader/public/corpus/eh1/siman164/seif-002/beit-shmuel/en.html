(ב) And it did not live thirty days. At first glance, it appears that if the child died within thirty days, then on a Torah level it is a full-fledged child, as written in siman 156; even according to the Rabbis who disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, it is a full-fledged child. One may say that here we rely on the Rabbis, and if she married a kohen she need not leave him, for we say it was a full-fledged child and the chalitzah that he performed is no chalitzah at all, just as we rely on the Rabbis if she became betrothed to a kohen without chalitzah, as written in seif 7. So it would be proper to rely on the Rabbis when he performed chalitzah for her. But I saw in Nimukei Yosef that he does not hold so, for in the Mishnah he explains that a nonviable child means one who died within thirty days, and nevertheless he wrote that if she married a kohen she must leave him. This shows that in this case we do not rely on the Rabbis. Perhaps this is because there are two unfavorable factors: he already performed chalitzah, and we would be exempting her without chalitzah. According to this, if the yavam died, so there is no prohibition of being bound to the yavam and there is only one unfavorable factor, one may say that we rely on the Rabbis, as we rely in seif 7 where there is one unfavorable factor. Or perhaps this unfavorable factor is not comparable to that case, for here there is an appearance problem: he performed chalitzah for her, and now she is married to a kohen, which is worse.
