(ה) He exempted his wife at the time of his death. So wrote Mordechai, and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rav Natronai: Abba Shaul said "what can I do," etc., only where he wrote her exemptions at the time of kiddushin; then the condition helps only for himself. But if he exempted her at the time of his death, it helps, for then it is as though he gave her as a gift all the bundles he gave her or what she took herself. Therefore, even if he did not say "from me and from my heirs," the heirs cannot administer an oath to her. Mordechai there concludes and writes that Sha'arei HaRif disagrees with this and holds that if he did not write exemptions on the ketubah, he accomplished nothing to extract the ketubah. Bach understood that Rif holds that exemptions given at the time of death are worse, but this does not appear to me. Rather, Rif holds that he must specify that she is exempt from the oath of the ketubah.
