So forbidden to paramour. See book Bnei Yehuda letter 5 who brought in name of Tzitz Eliezer siman 46 who was in doubt: one who came in prostitution upon woman who was presumed married woman whose husband went overseas and both intended willfully for sin; afterward clarified when she prostituted her husband was already dead — is she permitted to paramour or not since they intended for sin; and he inclined to permit paramour by law; and he also brought one proof to permit — see there; and see Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 6 who also mentioned this doubt in name of later rabbis; and he wrote no doubt here she is certainly permitted — what of it that her intent was for prohibition since truly she did not prostitute; and also proof from alternate reading Ketubot 9: all who go out to war of beit din write bill of divorce to wife even though at time of act there was doubt, etc.; and see Tosafot there; and see responsum Brit Avraham part 4 siman 68; and bring a little below siman 115 seif 6 s.k. 24 on matter she prostituted and does not know with whom whether forbidden to entire world — see there.
