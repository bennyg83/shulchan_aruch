They warned him. See Maharam of Trani part 1 siman 187; and Kenesset HaGadol siman 10 on Beit Yosef — if there is a distinction whether they warned him before kiddushin or after kiddushin; one sage warned him and afterward another beit din permitted him — we do not remove unless the sage who warned is renowned in wisdom and they were not equal to him — Ramat part 1 there; and Mahari Karo in his responsum siman 3. I was in doubt whether specifically when they warned him from the type of prohibition he was doing — such as he was defamer and they warned him not to marry her because of the defamer's ta'am; but if they warned him because of another matter she does not leave since they did not warn him because of defamer. My opinion concludes: since he transgressed sages' words, even if they did not warn from the type of prohibition she leaves — Kenesset HaGadol — see there.
And we remove. Bach wrote it does not refer to defamer on maidservant but only defamer on a married woman; and part 4 disputes him that we do not lenient; and so Taz. Beit Shmuel wrote he ruled well, for Rivash who is later ruled leniently — see there. What part 4 wrote on Bach: the Mishnah teaches defamer on a married woman and removed him from under his hand — even if he married he divorces, and it does not teach even if he preceded and married — see there. He was not precise, for Bach means: the first clause teaches "and if he married we do not remove" — it should have taught "and if he preceded and married" like regarding two sisters, etc., which also teaches "preceded and married" to teach before they consulted beit din he married — therefore we do not remove; but they consulted beit din and forbade and nevertheless he married — we remove; for now what it teaches "and if he married" implies even if they consulted beit din and forbade and nevertheless he married we do not remove — impossible, for truth of law here is indeed even if they consulted we do not remove. Not so in the end regarding defamer on a married woman, etc. — no distinction; it teaches preceded and married or married stam — always we remove automatically; why should the tanna lengthen the Mishnah in vain. One may say: now it comes to err that if they preceded and married before consulting we do not remove regarding defamer on a married woman — this is not so, for biblically forbidden to paramour as Rashi on the Mishnah Yevamot 24b s.v. yotzi — see there; and see Tosafot 25b s.v. amar lehu matnita, etc.; and understand.
