Wherever they warned him, etc. — implies also the defamer from the maidservant mentioned in seif 5 has this law — that if he transgressed after warning he divorces. One may challenge: Rosh wrote in ch. Keitzad on two who betrothed two women (sisters — this one does not know which he betrothed, etc.) they do not perform yibbum for the second lest he encounter his brother's zekukah or chalutzah as stated there in the Mishnah: if they preceded and married without consulting beit din — but if they consulted and were told one should perform chalitzah and one yibbum and both went and performed yibbum, we remove from them, etc., since they transgressed beit din's words we remove from them; and so Rashi explained — end quote. From his precision on "preceded and married" and not "married" alone we learn: where we taught "married" alone it is permitted even if beit din warned him; if so here regarding defamer of family where we taught "and if he married he does not divorce" we should say even if they warned, it is permitted after-the-fact; and from this challenge Maharshal ruled after-the-fact permission if he married a maidservant; and likewise a sage who forbade the woman not to marry initially — if he married even after warning he does not divorce; the reason we do not remove in these is that even if true he had relations with her there is no biblical prohibition here, etc. He was not precise in this ruling, for also there in the Mishnah that teaches "preceded and married" there is no biblical prohibition — brother's chalutzah is forbidden only rabbinically; nevertheless Rosh was precise that after warning it is not called after-the-fact; likewise regarding rabbinic prohibition they decreed he may not marry the maidservant. That it did not teach here regarding maidservant "preceded and married" — to know: specifically there regarding yibbum where there is doubt and one side to permit saying "his hand married" and he relied on himself for permission and did not come to beit din — therefore it said "preceded and married" meaning he preceded himself to permission without beit din's authority; not so regarding maidservant where there is no side of permission except he transgressed sages' words — "preceded" language does not apply; nevertheless they did not penalize him to divorce since it is after-the-fact automatically; if they warned him it is like initially as with yevamah, and even after-the-fact we remove; and likewise a sage who forbade — as Shulchan Aruch implies; and so Beit Yosef in seif 12 wrote Tur this language in the name of Rosh (if the paramour raped her she is permitted) from the incident of Bathsheba as we say; and in ch. 1 of Ketubot 9 we say there she was by force — therefore they did not forbid her to David; and "just as forbidden to husband so forbidden to paramour" does not apply, for truly she was not forbidden since she was by force — therefore permitted also to paramour; and so Tosafot in ch. Bameh Madlikin.
