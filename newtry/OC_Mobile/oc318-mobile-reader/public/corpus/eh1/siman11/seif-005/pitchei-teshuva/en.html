Forbidden to him. Avnei Tzedek wrote meaning if husband kohen, etc. through Chelkat Mechokek was in doubt; and see book Bnei Yehuda letter 4 who brought Maggid Mishneh ch. 2 Sotah law 12 expanded this doubt for us and afterward resolved from Tosafot ch. Four Brothers: kohen's wife who was raped forbidden to paramour; and he weighed his words — no great proof from Tosafot's words; and he sided to bring proofs she is permitted to paramour — end quote; and see Yeshuot Yaakov above siman 6 s.k. 4 wrote he agreed in responsum forbidden to paramour — see there. Behold from all this clarified: Israelite wife who was raped plainly permitted to paramour; truly Beit Shmuel himself initially wrote plainly permitted as Tosafot ch. Bameh Madlikin: Torah-observant wife permitted to David since he came by force; however afterward Beit Shmuel was in doubt on law and wrote this depends on two answers in Talmud ch. 1 Ketubot; and per latter answer "bill of divorce he writes to wife" one may say force forbidden to paramour since he came willingly; and so Yerushalmi Sotah beginning as Tosafot brought there — see there (and see Kerem Shlomo who brought in Korban Eidah on Yerushalmi there wrote also Rama's view is thus; and what Rama wrote "and likewise if she became forbidden," etc. — printing error; must read "and likewise if she did not become forbidden," etc., and comes to teach us this law — see there; and in my humble opinion if we come to emend thus it seems more correct to say Rama comes to teach law explained in Avnei Tzedek previous s.k. that also if second paramour she is forbidden; and refers to beginning of Mechaber's words "since forbidden to husband on his account," etc. — on this Rama emended "and likewise if not forbidden on his account," etc.); and below siman 159 s.k. 9 Beit Shmuel wrote in any case per Rama's words there yevamah who prostituted forbidden to paramour because of penalty — automatically we hear Israelite wife raped forbidden to him because of penalty — see there; and see book Beit Meir — his view concludes Israelite wife raped permitted to paramour if not because of penalty; and thereby possible if he married he does not divorce — see there (also in responsum Chatam Sofer siman 56 wrote what Beit Shmuel and other Acharonim bring proof from Tosafot ch. Bameh Madlikin from there proves nothing, etc.; but seems this depends on dispute beginning ch. Keshem Hayah — per R' Akiva and his colleagues there who hold prohibition from paramour emerges from "and she was defiled" — then prohibition to paramour did not increase if no defilement to husband — first answer ch. 1 Ketubot force was, etc.; but Rabbi who holds two verses written "and she was defiled" — one for husband and one for paramour — found not dependent on each other; even if not defiled to husband nevertheless defiled to paramour, etc.; per this plain law follows R' Akiva and R' Yehoshua and R' Zecharyah ben Hakatzav against Rabbi; if so wherever not forbidden to husband not forbidden to paramour by law if not because of penalty as Rama wrote siman 159 — and this only initially and if she married she does not leave — see further there siman 159 s.k. 9); and what Avnei Tzedek wrote "if she was secluded with one permitted to him," etc. — see responsum Shevut Yaakov simanim 6 and 7 on this matter.
