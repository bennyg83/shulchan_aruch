{Rama: And likewise if she became forbidden, etc.} Beit Shmuel was in doubt to establish two versions ch. 1 of Ketubot: one may say Israelite wife who was raped is forbidden to paramour; and Rosh ch. HaBa al Yedei Hevel wrote: however "and she was defiled" — paramour's prohibition even though it is not written "or she was not seized" — there is a distinction between force and willingness, for it is not sensible to forbid her to paramour when permitted to husband — behold he closes permission even though one may strain that per explanation of version one his plain language implies for halakhah; also his words are detached from Tosafot ch. Four Brothers 95 end s.v. af al pi. In my humble opinion there is also proof from Mishnah we taught: defamer on a married woman and beit din removed him from under his hand — even if he married we remove; and Rav inferred from "removed him" specifically in manner beit din removed him — with witnesses — therefore forbidden to paramour; and if so still difficult: why teach beit din removed — that is ugly matter witnesses; behold even if beit din did not remove — with force — also Tosafot and Rosh. From this there is also to resolve Chelkat Mechokek's doubt: even kohen's wife who was raped is forbidden — since it teaches "whoever beit din removed from husband forbidden to paramour" — as implied from Rosh's words above; and what Beit Shmuel wrote the law depends on two answers even though it seems so from Yerushalmi — in my humble opinion in our Talmud there is no necessity that version one disputes in law; rather wants to resolve perhaps there was not complete force even though king, etc. — nevertheless there is room to hesitate; and see Tosafot version one in sugya captive of king and Nissim Yisraeli; and thereby the difficulty of Tosafot Shabbat Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel bring who expounded "purchased ones of willingness" and permission was with her — not so if she was by force she was on him in eternal prohibition — why Beit Shmuel brings siman 159 seif 3 because of penalty she is forbidden — perhaps Yerushalmi also but because of penalty and no contradiction from Mishnah above, for here if he married he does not divorce; and see siman 15 seif 28 in hagahah and Bach s.k. 4 brings proof for this hagahah from Tosafot ch. Ha'ish and includes also this law in hagahah's meaning here.
