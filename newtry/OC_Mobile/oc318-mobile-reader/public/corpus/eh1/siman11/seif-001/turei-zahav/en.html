And they came and found an ugly matter — the sugya of the Gemara Beit Yosef brings at length. Behold what the Gemara states and halakhah is like Rabbi on a non-decisive rumor and halakhah is like Rav on a decisive rumor — must say this is not to explain Rav's and Rabbi's words and say Rabbi holds to be stringent on a non-decisive rumor and Rav is lenient on a decisive rumor specifically; for if so it should not have said "halakhah," for "halakhah" implies they dispute; rather plainly Rabbi himself holds even a decisive rumor forbids and Rav holds even a non-decisive rumor there is no prohibition except here we rule halakhah to distinguish between them regarding the rumor whether it stopped or not. Behold there are three views: per Rif we distinguish between suspect and husband — from husband we do not remove in any case except with witnesses of defilement, but witnesses of ugly matter and a non-decisive rumor when she has no children; but with only one of these we do not remove even from the suspect. Per Rabbenu Tam per She'iltot of Rav Ahai there is no distinction between husband and suspect at all; in both, if there is a non-decisive rumor without witnesses of ugly matter but the husband himself saw the ugly matter, we remove from both — so Rosh wrote for this view. But for a non-decisive rumor the husband's sight alone suffices; and he resolves thus: are we not established that a rumor after marriage we are not concerned for — must resolve that here is different for there is also the husband's sight; and if it is a decisive rumor but there is an aggravating factor because witnesses saw the ugly matter, we also remove even from the husband — so Rosh wrote for this view: but if witnesses saw an ugly matter, presumption is as if they saw she was penetrated and we forbid her to her husband like an open door or like one witness after warning and seclusion, etc.; and comes what the baraita teaches at the end "and if witnesses of defilement came, even if she has many children she leaves" — meaning even if they did not see penetration but saw ugly matter, witnesses of defilement he calls it — end quote. Clear from this that for ugly matter even with a decisive rumor we remove from the husband even if she has children (and see responsum Bach siman 214 who challenged on this that it discusses only when he has no children — see there for law). If so, likewise in the second distinction — non-decisive rumor and husband's sight of ugly matter — children also do not help; therefore Rosh did not mention children in this view, neither in decisive rumor with husband's sight nor in witnesses of ugly matter without rumor; and likewise afterward in his challenge: behold for witnesses of ugly matter we remove, etc. — implies they are completely equal in this view for husband and suspect and pay no attention to children at all; and so in Rosh's brief rulings he did not mention at all distinguishing because of children in these two. One may challenge: behold we say halakhah is like Rabbi on a non-decisive rumor without witnesses, and Rabbi said on that baraita "when is this said — when she has no children," etc. — let it teach: if you wish say automatically Rabbi himself is not stringent when she has children and we are more stringent than Rabbi and remove even when she has children with non-decisive rumor alone. It seems to resolve: this baraita "when is this said — when she has no children" refers to any rumor whatsoever as Rashi explained for Rif: Rabbi said it removes a woman with any rumor whatsoever; if so, only here he distinguishes children or not; but for an actual rumor meaning not stopped, plainly it forbids like ugly matter even with children — and we rule thus as we ruled halakhah is like Rabbi on non-decisive rumor except where he is stringent even on any rumor without children — there we do not rule like him but like Rav who is lenient even without children. Third view here is Rosh per She'iltot — likewise we do not distinguish husband and suspect; in both we remove only with witnesses of defilement or with two aggravating factors — non-decisive rumor and also witnesses of ugly matter, which is like an open door or one witness after warning and seclusion; but with only one of these we do not remove her from husband or suspect. Behold also here Rosh's words did not mention children whether there is a distinction; and so in Rosh's brief rulings; except Tur mentioned Rabbenu Tam's view that if there are no witnesses but a rumor spread that she committed adultery with him because they saw ugly matter in her, etc., and he transgressed and married — if he has children she does not leave — against Rosh's words explicitly who compares to open door as I cited; and so I saw in the name of Maharshal: he was not precise, for per Rabbenu Tam's words even if she has children she leaves as Rosh wrote in his challenge in ch. 2 of Yevamot how Maharam resolved him — see there; and we do not distinguish children except without witnesses of ugly matter at all — end quote; and I do not understand his end words, for even with non-decisive rumor without witnesses of ugly matter certainly its law is like witnesses of ugly matter as we proved; and in Darkei Moshe he wanted to resolve it was a comparison to open door — when he claims open door he has no children from her; but for one witness after warning and seclusion there is no full comparison — end quote; and these words were not given to be written, for Rosh explicitly compares them to one witness after warning and seclusion, yet he says there is no full comparison; and further ch. 1 of Ketubot we say explicitly one witness after warning and seclusion is literally like open door — how distinguish between them; and further Rosh explicitly explains per Rabbenu Tam that "and if witnesses of defilement came, even if she has many children she leaves" discusses witnesses of ugly matter he calls witnesses of defilement; and Maharshal wants to explain Tur's words "because they saw ugly matter in her" does not mean there are witnesses to this but means the rumor spread because close ones or women saw thus; but with witnesses of ugly matter and an actual rumor, even if they have children she leaves — and this explanation does not need contradiction, for Tur's words are clear: first he wrote we require specifically witnesses who saw defilement and then she leaves even if she has children; afterward he wrote if there are not actual witnesses of defilement but witnesses of ugly matter and because of this the rumor spread — she does not leave if there are children; and truly this is not so; and further per Rosh's view he wrote explicitly: when there is an actual rumor and witnesses of ugly matter we remove, etc., if she has no children — behold with children it does not help even two aggravating factors; and from Rosh's words it is proven this is not so but these are actual witnesses of defilement and children do not help. Further many wonder at Tur in the name of Rif not to distinguish suspect and husband — yet Rif wrote explicitly the opposite as Beit Yosef wrote; and Darkei Moshe's answer there is far from satisfactory — see it. In my humble opinion, some came to fix this and emend in place of Rav Alfas to Rav Ahai — meaning the author of She'iltot we cited above per Rosh. Behold Shulchan Aruch's words in this seif to distinguish husband and suspect are from Rambam who holds like Rif's view we cited; except what Rama wrote here "and some say," etc. — I do not know who is master of this law; for per Rif we do not remove from husband except actual witnesses of defilement and an actual rumor with witnesses of ugly matter does not help; and per Rabbenu Tam per She'iltot we cited we remove even from husband with one of them — either actual rumor and husband's sight or witnesses of ugly matter alone; and if per Rosh himself — behold he holds no distinction between husband and suspect with one of them, yet here he distinguishes between them — therefore I do not know who said them and it is not like anyone. For practical halakhah we certainly should be stringent even regarding her husband based on witnesses of ugly matter and non-decisive rumor — this is both per Rabbenu Tam and per Rosh, even though per Rif and Rambam we do not remove from husband except actual witnesses of defilement; nevertheless we do not lenient against Rabbenu Tam and Rosh per She'iltot. If there are only witnesses of ugly matter alone, there is no power to compel him to divorce, even though per Rabbenu Tam we also compel in this; nevertheless since Rosh disputes him there is no power to compel; but certainly if the husband himself does not wish to dwell with her we do not compel him to dwell with her — in this even Rambam holds she leaves without ketubah if he wishes to divorce; and even in those regions that practice cherem not to divorce against one's will as stated siman 115 — and there the law of the writing was explained. Regarding the suspect, certainly we compel him to divorce for one of them as Rama wrote here — and this is correct for halakhah.
Spit above the canopy — after the suspect left, the husband found spit above what is on her bed — who threw spit upward if not the one lying with face upward; therefore she committed adultery and threw it at the time of relations. And it is simple: perhaps this came because she already had relations with her husband — strained to say this discusses as known that she did not have relations close to this with her husband; one may say: since in any case she acted improperly in what was hidden, therefore we attribute corruption to her and do not attribute to her husband who damaged her presumption as above.
Place of shoes — certainly they were a non-Jew's shoes and reversed so the viewer would not recognize; and if the shoes were here they could see whose they are.
One witness to an ugly matter, etc. — meaning even regarding the defamer it is nothing; for regarding her husband even one witness to actual adultery is nothing to remove her from her husband. It amazes me, for even regarding the defamer she does not leave even with one witness to adultery as Shulchan Aruch stated in this seif — why was it necessary to write here regarding ugly matter.
