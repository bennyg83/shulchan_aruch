A married woman. See responsum of Rama from siman 12 through siman 18.
And seclusion. So too Rambam ch. 2 of Hilchot Sotah and Tur siman 178; it is explained there she is forbidden to her husband after warning and seclusion, and it implies we compel the husband to remove her; except in Temple times there was an enactment to give her bitter waters to clarify if she is pure or impure, and nowadays without that enactment she is forbidden to her husband — see Beit Shmuel.
That she was secluded. It is explained in Rambam: even if the delay is from him — the husband did not wish to give her to drink — nevertheless she is forbidden to the paramour. See Beit Shmuel.
To the paramour. There is no distinction whether he is the first paramour who forbade her to the husband or a second paramour — Maharam of Tzfas siman 196 and Rabbi Akiva Eiger Ashkenazi siman 196, Shut Yaakov part 1 siman 94, and Kenesset HaGadol siman 171. The proof is Sanhedrin 41 — there it challenges: but they can say we came to forbid her to the second paramour and she was already forbidden to her husband — nevertheless she is forbidden to her paramour; see and understand; and see responsum Sha'ar Ephraim in the last pamphlet.
Forbidden to him. Meaning: if her husband is a kohen and a Yisrael had relations with her by force — rape of a Yisrael is permitted; and if her husband was a Yisrael she was permitted to husband and paramour as stated in Ketubot 9a. Nevertheless if her husband is a kohen and she is forbidden to her husband, and we hold where forbidden to husband forbidden to paramour — therefore if forbidden to her kohen husband because of this forced relations, she is also forbidden to the paramour even though the paramour is a Yisrael — Beit Shmuel. And Chelkat Mechokek s.k. 10 was in doubt about this — see there. And if she was secluded with one man she is permitted to him and her husband, for we hold we do not forbid for mere seclusion; nevertheless we warn him — if he truly had relations with her she is forbidden to him even though there are no witnesses — so Tosafot Ketubot 9a s.v. mipnei mah lo asruhu leDavid — end quote.
An ugly matter. See Beit Shmuel.
Kissing. One should be concerned if it discusses also that she was secluded and waited enough time for defilement and witnesses came and found them doing this ugly matter — for we should fear perhaps before witnesses came she was defiled; but if there was no seclusion at all here, or immediately after seclusion witnesses came and found them kissing each other and witnesses know clearly there was no defilement now — if we should fear that since this ugly matter presumptively we fear perhaps another time she was defiled, or perhaps we are not concerned except where there is seclusion where we fear she was defiled now — see responsum Ra'avan siman 25 part 4; and Beit Shmuel wrote from Rambam ch. 24 of Hilchot Ishut law 15 implies we are not concerned — see there.
Ugly. The husband may remove her with witnesses of ugly matter; but we do not compel him; and we do not advise him to keep her even nowadays when there is cherem he can remove — see Beit Shmuel and see Kenesset HaGadol.
To the defamer. Even because of mere slander he may not marry — Beit Shmuel.
The city gossiped. The rumor must be from another source and not because of witnesses of ugly matter — Bach, Beit Shmuel; see Darkei Moshe.
The rumor. Even if there are no enemies spreading the rumor, as long as he has enemies in the city we fear perhaps because of enemies the rumor emerged — Rivash part 1 siman 12, Mishpetei Tzedek part 2 siman 60. And it is not precise that the enemy is the defamer or defamed woman, but even enemies of the defamer's or defamed woman's father — see there Kenesset HaGagolah on Tur.
And some say if, etc. But all agree that for seclusion alone she is not forbidden even if she secluded for prostitution; if they were lying together clothed, as long as they did not see physical closeness it is only ugly matter — Maharshal, Beit Shmuel. Knocking and the paramour tapping on the woman's room door is not ugly matter — Maharam of Lublin siman 80. He spoke with her with a wink of the eye is not called ugly matter — Tomer Tzedek siman 33.
From her husband. Bach challenged: behold above siman 4 seif 15 he wrote a married woman about whom a rumor spread that she was prostituting under her husband and everyone gossips after her — we are not concerned for children, etc.; and if very promiscuous, etc. — implies; but we do not remove from husband — end quote Bach. He was not precise, for there there are no witnesses of ugly matter — so too Beit Shmuel s.k. 15 — see there. See Tosafot Sotah 27a s.v. domeh, etc.; and see part 4.
From the husband. Implies with non-decisive rumor even if the husband did not see ugly matter we remove from the suspect; and this rumor implies even if not established in beit din — therefore witnesses must be valid and not relatives. Rumor because of enemies even initially she is permitted to the suspect — Beit Shmuel.
Join together. Likewise two witnesses of defilement join even though defilement this one saw the other did not see — see Beit Shmuel.
Nothing. Even if he is believed to him like two, she is permitted even to the suspect — Beit Shmuel; and see part 4.
