Derishah and chakirah. If her claim is that she was asleep when he came upon her she is not believed — see Kenesset HaGadol on Tur seif 13.
Her husband. And likewise if she married the paramour and they come to forbid her to him — he too is called her husband; but if she has not yet married the paramour, and even though through accepting this testimony she is also forbidden to her paramour, nevertheless it seems testimony need not be accepted in his presence for acceptance of words — for she is forbidden to him automatically as forbidden to all kohanim even though they were not present at acceptance of testimony — part 4. One woman who entered with one man into a room and barred the doors with a lock and one witness saw through a small hole in the door and saw they were lying as adulterers do; and the maidservant in the house also saw and a rumor spread in the city how so-and-so committed adultery; and some people rose and collected court fees not in her husband's presence — if there is [ground] to forbid her to her husband, ruled in responsum Panim Me'irot she'elah 104 she is permitted to her husband — see there; and see responsum Bach siman 98 and 99 seif 6.
