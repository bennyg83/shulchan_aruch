(א) And some say that even with food and drink. This is the approach of most poskim, who disagree with Rambam and hold that Ravin does not dispute the Mishnah. The general rule is: if he did not eat, everything is returned, even if he is the one who retracts; she must pay for items normally consumed through use, and likewise for food and drink that he sent and she consumed, less one third. At first glance, if the item is still present and became worn, then if it remains fit for its original use she need not pay for the wear, as written at the beginning of siman 96 regarding dowry property. If they were not items normally consumed through use and she sold them, it appears her law is like that of a robber, and she pays according to their value at the time of the taking, without deducting one third; see Nimukei Yosef. If he ate or drank to the value of a dinar, then if he retracts we say he waived items normally consumed through use that were consumed. If they are items normally consumed through use and she used them but they were not consumed, this is an unresolved question in the Gemara and we do not extract from her; if he seized, seizure helps according to those poskim who hold that seizure helps in an unresolved question, as Chelkat Mechokek wrote in several places. Regarding beikha and savanta, he certainly waives. Rambam explained these as types of scarves and women's ornaments, while Ri"v explained them as shoes and scarves; even if they are still present she need not return them, because he certainly waives them, and I do not know why the Tur wrote nothing about this. Ri"v ruled that even if she is the one who retracts, she need not return them, and they are treated more leniently than food and drink. Items not normally consumed through use must be returned even if she sold them; I wrote nearby that she must pay their value at the time of sale, and even if she profited with the money she need not give him anything, as Nimukei Yosef wrote. If she retracted, she must pay for items normally consumed through use that were consumed, less one third of their value. Money is treated like items normally consumed through use, and clothing depends on the circumstances. Likewise, when one of them died, we also say that if he ate, he waived. The Gemara's question regarding what she must pay is where he ate one third of a dinar, whether we say he waived one third of what he sent; it also asks what the law is regarding appreciation; see Nimukei Yosef. The same applies if he sent it to him to eat, or if his agent ate it, and in all these cases we do not extract from her. In the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur, he wrote that if each sent to the other, but one party's item became worn, the other deducts the entire depreciation. Bach in Kuntrus Acharon explained that this speaks of shiddukhin before kiddushin, and therefore all the depreciation is deducted. This does not appear to me. Nimukei Yosef distinguishes between shiddukhin and erusin only regarding a case where he ate there, for one may say he waived for an arusah but not for a meshuddekhet. But regarding payment less one third, we hold in several places that one deducts a third; likewise, if a father leaves his orphans a borrowed cow and they ate it, they pay less one third. Therefore even with a meshuddekhet she need not pay more. The main explanation is like the Perishah's second answer: when the party who retracts is in possession, he pays less one third; but when each sent to the other, each is in possession, so all depreciation is deducted from the original value. According to this, even if he sent food and drink, they would deduct. However, one can say that in the case there, the retraction was from the bride's side, and therefore he ruled that the groom deducts from what he holds the entire depreciation from the original value, because there were two favorable factors: he was in possession and he was not the one who retracted. But if he is in possession and he is the one who retracts, one can say he does not deduct the entire depreciation; although he is in possession, since he retracts, he does not deduct all the loss.
