(ג) After matchmaking, everything returns. Beit Yosef wrote this in the name of Nimukei Yosef: without kiddushin he certainly waives nothing, even if he ate there and even if there was matchmaking, since they can retract; therefore the text spoke of his father-in-law, etc. Beit Yosef wrote that this does not appear from the responsum of Rashba that he will cite in this siman. Rashba's responsum, cited afterward, says that if the bride's father sent garments to the groom after the matchmaking and afterward the arrangement was voided, whether the groom retracted, etc., as Shulchan Aruch copied in the next seif. Darkei Moshe also brought Beit Yosef's words that there is a dispute between Nimukei Yosef and Rashba, since Rashba wrote that after matchmaking the law is like after erusin; therefore Rama wrote here, "and some disagree." I am very astonished: what dispute is there here? Certainly, both with matchmaking and with kiddushin, there is retraction and one must return, for there is an assessment of intent that he sent not as a gift but so that they marry one another; in this they are equal. But regarding kiddushin, specifically the groom must eat the value of a dinar; and in this respect matchmaking is stronger, for eating does not help, since even then he does not waive. From this gloss, "and some disagree," it sounds as if the dissenting view holds that after matchmaking not everything returns, but such a thing never was. Rama wrote here without sufficient examination.
