One who sends savlonot to his father-in-law's house — whether many or few, whether he ate an erusin feast there or not, whether she died or he died, or the man retracted — all savlonot return except food and drink. And likewise money and small vessels he sent her to use in her father's house — if she used them and they wore out or were lost they are not repaid; but if they still exist everything returns and he collects them in beit din, for it is known he sent them only for ornament. {Rama: Some say even food, drink, and small items that do not return — specifically when the groom ate there; then even what still exists does not return; but if he did not eat there everything returns (Tur there).} If she retracted — everything returns, and even food and drink she pays their value cheaply — meaning if food and drink were worth six she pays four. And if local custom was that every man makes a feast and feeds his friends, or distributes money to beadles and cantors and the like, and he did as everyone does, and she retracted — she pays everything, for she caused him monetary loss — provided he has witnesses how much he spent (Rama: Some say all this applies to what he sent after erusin; but before erusin, after matchmaking, everything returns (Nekudot Yosef chapter Mi She-meit, and so Ritva). And some dispute (Beit Yosef in name of Rashba).}
