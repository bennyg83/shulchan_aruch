Seif 6 in the gloss (to collect penalties). In the customary engagement conditions, as is known, one party claimed that since the set time for payment had passed, and he warned me and I did not pay, the bond was void, because he no longer wanted it to remain in force. The other party claimed that he had asked him for an extension of a month or two, and he was silent; when they came to judgment, he was ready to pay immediately. It appears that although the penalty can take effect through an extension of time even without new conditions, nevertheless silence as admission does not apply here. This is like one who says, I do not know whether you became obligated to me. Although the conditions were certainly presumed to create an obligation, once the time passed they are presumed void, and a doubtful extension of time is like a new event. Silence as admission does not apply so as to impose a new obligation on him. Also, although we administer an oath when one claims that his son or daughter refuses the match, that there was no collusion or deceit in the matter, nevertheless here there is no oath. There, the son and daughter are presumed to have agreed, because people do not throw money away for nothing and would not bring themselves into a loss for no reason; surely the man consulted the members of his household and they agreed. Although a person may change his mind, this at least creates grounds to suspect collusion, and we also see that his own mind has now changed. We need not add and say that his son and daughter also changed their minds, and according to his own words the change of mind was only with his son. Still, although this is a presumption that can change, it is proper to administer an oath to one who bound himself with a penalty in place of his son, because from the outset it was understood and accepted that the penalty was fixed at least as a boundary of an oath. Otherwise the engagement bond would have no real substance or permanence at all, and they completed the matter and obligated one another so as to impose an oath even in what is not intrinsically subject to an oath. Acceptance of a cherem, however, is much lighter, and through that alone it is not considered a bond except through the aspect of an oath. But where before us the time has passed and there is only a doubt whether they willingly extended the times, it appears that no oath is administered, though this requires further investigation. One who retracts from the conditions and afterward says that he is not retracting, thereby making himself disgraceful and repulsive to the other party, who must stand watching to see how his changeable spirit will end: it appears that the boundary in this is that immediately when one hears the other's retraction, he should warn him before two witnesses that what he now says will stand; if the warning can be made by a beit din tzedek, that is more proper. Afterward, even if in order not to pay the penalty he retracts and says that he wants the conditions, and even if he wants to deposit or give the other party a large security for this, nevertheless the other party can say: since when I wanted, you did not want, now that you want, I do not want; in any event you caused me shame. Reason supports saying this, since this is the ordinary pattern of the world's judgment, as Chazal said. If at first the one who gives food to the couple at his table lived in a pleasant dwelling, and afterward he lives in an unpleasant dwelling, whether they can cancel the match without penalty requires investigation. At first glance it is all the more so from the refusal by which a woman may refuse to change location even after she is already bound to him. There is also room to say that he must pay him for the dwelling as well, since in any event the obligation of food includes paying for them when they cannot live together with him, and so here he should pay according to the savings of a shared household. A match made against the custom to be concerned about identical names of the fathers-in-law, their wives, and the like, where one party later retracted because of the concern, may perhaps be like one who says, I thought I could accept it because I was not concerned; for fear breaks even a strong body, Heaven forbid. This is especially so if the one retracting suffered some incident, similar to the language of Chazal that although there is no divination, there is a sign. If they did not know of the concern according to the common way people understand it, it is a mistaken transaction. The rule that they did not know the loss of the worker does not apply, because in a mistaken sale that rule does not apply, and we extract even from one in possession. All the more so here, where condition does not apply in that way, whereas with a worker he should have stipulated clearly. Although we rule that they can retract from a kinyan sudar as long as they are still engaged in the same matter, in engagements it appears that even when they retract immediately while engaged in the same matter, shame applies. Before the kinyan sudar of writing the conditions there is not so much shame, and each person can say: I wanted to test whether the other would agree to match with me; when I see that he agrees without any leaning away, I will then consider the matter. Even if word spread that the other refused to match with him, he can say: after he was willing, I was the one who did not want it. But after the writing of conditions, it is already known that the one who retracts shames the other party. Even if there were a simple and well-known custom that people retract immediately after writing conditions while still engaged in the same matter, nevertheless people do not generally write conditions intending to retract immediately, and there is shame. Still, this requires careful investigation. Also, although the later authorities z"l wrote that brokerage fees for a match are not due until after they write conditions with penalties as customary, nevertheless sometimes even if they already wrote conditions, if it was voided immediately that same day because it became known that one of them had been mistaken, one cannot extract the matchmaker's fee. Since it became revealed retroactively that the conditions were void, had this person come to claim the penalty from the other party he could not have extracted it, because the other could claim there was a mistake concerning the essence of the conditions. But if the retraction was in a manner such that, had he come to claim the penalty, there would have been no claim against him that the mistake concerned the essence of the conditions retroactively, but only that he later regretted it because of his wife's will, or some relative, or some other cause that does not make the body of the conditions mistaken, then even if the retraction was immediately that same day, and he did not wish to claim the penalty from him, the matchmaker's fee is due.
