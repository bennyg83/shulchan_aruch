(א) there is no kiddushin return perhaps holds there kiddushin even get perhaps get further kiddushin.
(ב) perhaps etc. Beit Yosef in name of Rashba likewise it is.
(ג) But from marriage, etc. The reason given is that there is no decree here. I did not understand this: how does "the kiddushin return" apply after marriage? With erusin one can find such a case, where he or she dies and she retracts; but with marriage this does not apply. If he dies, she takes her ketubah and no more; if she dies, everything belongs to the husband, and no return is relevant. I later saw in Responsa Maharil, siman 23, that this refers to Rabbenu Tam's enactments where she died within two years. The glossator's words appear to mean that Taz's difficulty is only with Rama's wording in the name of Maharil, that "the kiddushin return"; such language applies only to an arusah, not to a married woman. With a married woman, the language of return is not appropriate, for everything is his; only if he dies or divorces her does he give her the ketubah and no more, and if she dies everything is his. The expression "return" applies only where she dies within a year or two. This answers Beit Shmuel's difficulty on Chelkat Mechokek, who said that Maharil holds kiddushin were not given to be sunk permanently, only that they are not returned lest people say, etc.; this does not apply after marriage. If so, Maharil speaks about ordinary marriages, whether he or she died or she was divorced, and not specifically where she died within a year or two. According to Taz's precision that the language of return does not apply where either spouse dies after a long time, it must be that Maharil speaks within a year or two, when the language of return applies. But as a matter of law there is no practical difference: Taz too holds that kiddushin were not given to be sunk permanently, and for marriage her ketubah follows Maharam of Lublin, as seen from Taz's statement that she takes her ketubah and no more.
