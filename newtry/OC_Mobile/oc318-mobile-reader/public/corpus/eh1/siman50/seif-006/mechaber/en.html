When they wish to make assurances for matchmaking, they do so in a manner that is not asmakhta — meaning they make a document from this party that he owes his fellow a maneh, and after obligating himself a maneh (as a gift with the bride's father) that if he fulfills the match and enters the chuppah with this engaged woman — the debt is forgiven; and afterward they make a document that his fellow owes this one a maneh with a gift that if he brings his daughter to the chuppah with this engaged man the debt is forgiven; and they place both documents with a third party. {Rama: Some say any penalties made in matchmaking are not asmakhta (Tosafot chapter Eizehu Neshekh and Rosh ch. 4 Nedarim); and so is widespread custom to collect penalties made in matchmaking, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 207 — specifically if penalty documents were already written; but if they accepted kinyan on condition to write documents and before writing one retracted and protested before witnesses not to write — he may protest the document (responsum Rosh kelal 34) (as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 39 and 243). Some say one who commits regarding his daughter (there kelal 43) or daughter's daughter (Rashba siman 771) or his relative that she marry so-and-so, and afterward the daughter does not wish — the committer is exempt from the penalty, for this is called coercion even though he did not stipulate — provided there is no trickery (and see below end of siman 57). And likewise other cases of coercion — such as the committer died — heirs need not fulfill and are exempt from the penalty; specifically a general commitment they are exempt, but if he obligated himself by document heirs must pay and coercion does not help (and see below siman 51 there). Two who matched between them and lived in one city and made penalties between them, and afterward one left the city and the second wants him to follow to betroth — if he does not wish he is exempt from the penalty (Rivash siman 177 and responsum Ramban 278). And see Choshen Mishpat siman 12 seif 9 whether the transgressor who wishes to pay the penalty is obligated to fulfill through kinyan acceptance.}
