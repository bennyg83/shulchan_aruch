(א) When they wish to make guarantees, etc. This implies that there is no distinction between matchmaking and other matters: even with matchmaking one must be careful that it not be an asmakhta. This is Rambam's view. The "some say" cited by Rama is the view of Tosafot and the Rosh, that because of the embarrassment that the one retracting from matchmaking appeases, there is no asmakhta. So too the Tur wrote here in the name of Ri. Immediately afterward, however, he wrote that one who gives collateral for the penalty has done nothing: "If each gives collateral for the penalty he obligated himself to pay," etc., because in such a case one does not acquire through collateral so long as he has not yet become obligated at all. But for a matter in which he is not yet obligated, no lien takes effect through collateral; rather, he must transfer it through a formal kinyan, not as an asmakhta, in an important beit din, so that he becomes legally obligated if he retracts, and then he gives the object as collateral and the lien takes effect on it, etc. If so, his words contradict one another, and they are adjacent. So Beit Yosef asked. He further asked on what the Rosh wrote in responsum, that there are other grounds to void the whole matter because of asmakhta, although the Rosh himself ruled that there is no asmakhta in matchmaking. In truth, the latter question is not difficult: when the Rosh wrote "other grounds," his intent was that according to other poskim there is asmakhta even in matchmaking, and therefore he called them other grounds, bringing them only for additional support. Nearby I will write another proper answer to this, with Hashem's help. But the first question is certainly difficult. One cannot say that the end of the Tur's words refers to other laws and not matchmaking, as Perishah wrote at length; if so, he should not have written it here but in Choshen Mishpat, and indeed it is written there in Choshen Mishpat siman 207. Also, he wrote "the collateral for the penalty" with the definite article, meaning the penalty he had just mentioned; otherwise he should have written "a penalty" without the definite article.
