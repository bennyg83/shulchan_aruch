In the Ran it is clear that even according to those decisors, finding blood helps only regarding money, but regarding prohibition it does not help, because perhaps she brought blood or perhaps it was blood from the sides. The Rambam and Rashba cited by the Maggid Mishneh can also be understood this way, that these "some say" speak only regarding money. The Chelkat Mechokek and Taz ask: since by law until the wound heals we attribute blood to virginity, what proof is blood that she had not previously had relations? I already wrote a proper resolution above. According to these decisors, one finds a case of an open-entrance claim where there is no blood, for otherwise it would be a blood claim; or in a Dorakti family, as the Perishah writes. Rashi’s explanation of the sugya where she says she is from a Dorakti family follows this view. See Ketubot 36 regarding a deaf-mute or imbecile woman: according to Tosafot, they are presumed injured by wood and have no claim against them; according to Rashi, the point is that if she were competent she could claim she was raped after betrothal. Regarding prohibition, where there is no double doubt, the matter depends on the dispute nearby.
