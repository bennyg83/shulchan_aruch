"I was raped" — she is believed. Even HaEzer ch. 1 Kesubos siman 18; Maggid Mishneh ch. 18 Laws of Forbidden Relations; Beit Yosef here; Beit Shmuel s.k. 15; Perishah on Kuntres Acharon siman 23. See Maggid Mishneh there halakhah 10 who brought Yerushalmi explicitly like the forbidders even saying plainly, not like Rabbeinu Yonah and supporters. Yerushalmi also brought responsum Maharam Alashkar siman 94 supporting forbidders. See there at length also end of siman 77. Nevertheless Noda B'Yehuda second edition siman 14 on a case where her husband went far away about a year and a half, she gave birth and claims raped by a wicked disqualified man while her husband abandoned her in a distant house with only a small daughter — is she permitted to her Israelite husband. He elaborated on Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh and brought Yerushalmi and wrote no proof from Yerushalmi, for Yerushalmi per its view that rape has voice overriding even certainty and even plain claim does not help; but our Talmud disputes Yerushalmi, for explicitly an Israelite's wife is permitted — certainty maybe not under him maybe rape — if certainty permits without plain claim, permitted even on one doubt (so too responsum Brit Avraham siman 78 letter 3). He further argued Rambam also holds thus, not like Maggid Mishneh, and elaborated concluding if her husband believes her and his heart does not rebuke him as she is presumed modest before him, he may rely on Rabbeinu Yonah and she is permitted. At the end he wrote Knesset Yechezkel brought in Maharam Alashkar's name that "I was raped" — she is believed means saying plainly "I was raped" without naming the adulterer, or even naming him if not present; but if present and contradicts her even against the husband as plain certainty, since the adulterer contradicts with plainness, considered plain against the husband too. He wrote to doubt this, deducing from Gemara R' Chiya: one saying open opening is believed to forbid her — why conclude specifically against himself as plain and plain, but not believed to forbid priests if he died before divorcing her, for regarding other kohanim it is plain and maybe. Thus not like Maharam Alashkar; in any case we need not send after the adulterer since not present we accept the woman's words. He concludes nevertheless for stringency do not rely on my view alone unless two great authorities of the generation agree. See there siman 15: what place Maharam Alashkar has is when that person contradicts saying not by rape but willingly (as the questioning Gaon thought — he answered far to say so, for then Knesset Yechezkel would not omit this). See Maharam Alashkar siman 94 — the case where that person completely contradicted saying never came upon her by rape or consent; per what is explained there Maharam Alashkar did not write this view definitively only "we can say," and he forbade the woman there for other reasons. But one completely contradicting he never had intercourse with her — she is permitted since the adulterer does not contradict her permission whose main permission is rape — we split her statement, etc. Further if the person contradicted before she was permitted, perhaps Maharam Alashkar's words apply; but if already permitted, the adulterer's contradiction does not help even if he says willing intercourse, like captives once permitted, etc. Maharam Alashkar's words are not so clear. See there siman 17 further on this; siman 16: regarding this woman claiming rape, I already informed you, what more shall I add; if the husband's heart does not rebuke and he trusts her righteousness saying she surely does not lie — this is as saying plain to me; good also to investigate neighbors if she is always presumed modest and no licentiousness heard — he may permit her. See siman 16: I am very doubtful regarding a woman saying "I was raped" — Rabbeinu Yonah permits and Rambam forbids as explained Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 18; I am doubtful even per Rambam she is not believed, but perhaps one witness testifying she was raped is believed to permit her to her husband because one witness is believed regarding prohibitions — specifically regarding the woman's credibility Rambam was stringent; even in Sotah where husband warned and she secluded and one witness testifies she was not defiled — in this contradiction where he peered through a hole and saw she did not commit adultery — certainly not believed to permit; proof from Ulla in Sotah 31 — captive differs since there are grounds for matter he warned and secluded. See there siman 16, 18, 19. The main Noda B'Yehuda words that plainly her heart is between her teeth and husband overseas and she says raped equals open opening law — per Rabbeinu Yonah and supporters permitted when presumption of fitness not lost — see responsum Knesset Yechezkel siman 78: his view is not that any woman whose husband was overseas and she bore illegitimate child saying raped is believed per Rosh and supporters; I saw no posek stated this explicitly; perhaps they relied on open opening where Israelite's wife father accepted betrothal under three years. Truly one may say "I was raped" for two reasons: rape has voice; and from embarrassment she says raped only regarding open opening where father accepted betrothal under three years with miggo she could say seduced as minor without voice or embarrassment. Further distinguish open opening claim — prohibition is not established except per her husband and she immediately says raped — therefore believed, unlike illegitimate child where prohibition was established before she said raped. I remember as a youth in Brisk a doctor came from Italy several years; his wife came pregnant with illegitimate child claiming raped on the road — they did not listen and forbade her to him, and I think he was Israelite, etc. Perishah on Kuntres Acharon Kesubos siman 31 disputes him, writing from these words there is room to permit a woman whose husband went overseas, tarried more than twelve months, she conceived and claims raped on the road — she is believed and permitted to her Israelite husband since there is plain and presumption of righteousness better than majority of consent as Tosafot taught majority consent is not a full majority; since I saw Knesset Yechezkel's case in Brisk where sages of the generation forbade her, I had to write it is not a general rule; and even though we do not answer lions, one may say in that case even per her claim of rape she transgressed by secluding with a warrior in an inn forbidden even with many kosher men until a guard sits — thus where she transgressed seclusion improperly presumption is greatly impaired and no rape claim; not so where she claims rape in a matter that can be established afterward — seems we should not forbid her to her Israelite husband; I say this only to learn, and more Rama (brought above seif 4 s.k. 14) would permit if she claims privately her husband came by name, even for forbidders. Also responsum A.A.Z. part 8 ch. 3 brings also illegitimate pregnant saying raped equals open opening questioned there about a woman whose husband is in another country, she conceived and bore illegitimate child; beit din investigated and she answered raped in sleep on the road from road weariness sleep seized her and she did not sense a man had intercourse; afterward her husband came asking to permit dwelling with her as he believes her rape claim and testifies he saw nothing unseemly since the wedding — he answered at length clarifying two doubts: whether sleep-rape is rape (see above siman 11 Ba'er Hetev s.k. 18); and whether a woman is believed saying raped where she has no miggo. Regarding first doubt — seems if intercourse in sleep she is not forbidden to husband, for we learn ch. 2 Keritot all forbidden relations whether awake or asleep — asleep is exempt; Tur siman 20 and Rama there in Darkhei Moshe and glosses Shulchan Aruch: therefore Tur wrote, etc., relevance nowadays in which intercourse a woman becomes forbidden — thus if she is asleep she is not forbidden. Though one may say Tur is not universal as Beit Shmuel there regarding intercourse with dead limb, and perhaps here too specifically Torah rape but sleep is considered inadvertent not rape; main proof from Darkhei Moshe from Nekudot Yosef is not necessary — Tosafot in Yerushalmi name and Beit Shmuel siman 11 s.k. 3: he is intentional and she inadvertent — she is permitted to husband; so too Talmud Sotah 25, plainly in sleep one may say considered rape, etc. Regarding second doubt if believed saying raped in sleep since no miggo — all know pregnant and husband distant — like witnesses she had intercourse — seems also believed per Rosh ch. 1 Kesubos Tur siman 68, etc.; even Mordechai ch. 2 Kesubos and Shulchan Aruch siman 178 gloss from Mordechai — those Geonim Mordechai brought hold like forbidders rejecting Rabbeinu Yonah; but ultimately Mordechai wrote to uphold Rabbeinu Yonah and permitted regarding open opening even on one doubt even without miggo — therefore wonder at Rama (see previous s.k.) and he elaborated concluding in the questioned case not only beit din need not remove her but even on the husband no obligation to remove since she says plainly raped in sleep by another and husband believes her — husband cleared of sin and may maintain her if sages agree. At end responsum Rabbi Yonatan of Prague wrote not to explicitly permit husband to dwell with her nor compel beit din to remove her; but expert rabbinical court of Krakow agreed permitting husband to dwell with wife. See there (see Chasam Sofer siman 131 on this). See also Knesset Yechezkel there about a woman who became insane while dwelling with her Israelite husband and was sent to a small city far away; per Jewish testimony she was sometimes dreaming and madness grew; husband overseas, rumor she is pregnant, beit din investigated — she cried describing a wagon driver from Lithuania, etc., intercourse in a field, threw stones, returned in madness, later in sanity sensed pregnancy, etc. — her words; husband came horrified asking if permitted; he answered at length (siman 76) possibly to permit even without her words per Rashi and Tosafot on open opening sugya and Ra'avad Sotah and Rif ch. 18 Laws of Marriage that seduction of minor is rape since no consent — so too deranged seduction is rape; all the more minor; if so also without words possible to permit even one doubt though no majority of consent against presumption of fitness — questioned Tosafot there, answered all establish afterward she was minor without voice; here in seclusion during madness no voice; Knesset Yechezkel challenged from this that we establish afterward she was minor and voice of rape applies because not ashamed to publicize — applies also here. But truly Beit Yaakov siman 6 refuted his proof and brought responsum Maharam Lublin rejecting Knesset Yechezkel for another reason; Knesset Yechezkel is lone against all these authorities Maharal, Beit Yaakov, Maharam Lublin, Perishah who all questioned plainly we establish afterward minor without voice. He further vast pilpul to prove not like Knesset Yechezkel — also without her words broad permission not like Rambam because here no voice. See siman 82 end letter 8: cannot retroactively determine when pregnancy recognized two years after act. Nevertheless Knesset Yechezkel's challenge stands if open opening has voice against majority consent — also here without words permission per forbidders of open opening through presumption of fitness; Knesset Yechezkel challenged further; Beit Yaakov siman 6 refuted; Maharam Lublin; he concluded not like Knesset Yechezkel, also without words permission because no voice per open opening forbidders; Rambam ch. 1 holds seduction is not rape; also deranged worse than minor and deaf as explained responsa and Ran Gittin 22b, Chullin 12b, Yevamot 104b, Rashba siman 26 and 493 — open chapter proof does not apply to deranged seduction (see below siman 178 seif 3 s.k. 7 — also inclined view Rabbi Akiva Eiger deranged seduction is rape for all). Especially per Maharik root 168: intent to sin with husband required — if like animal act certainly considered rape; Rambam minors everywhere lack intent — adult teaches minor and seducer is adult-on-minor; deranged certainly does nothing per adulterer since no mind, but like animal act not to sin with husband — permitted also per Rambam (siman 82 letter 7 wanted to innovate per this even per Rambam minor if adulterer younger than nine not adult-on-minor — rejected see there; siman 82 letter 6 even minor if so lacking mind to sin certainly permitted even per Rambam). Further possible even real rape in them no voice; also per Rambam possible establish afterward during madness by real rape (siman 81 letter 1 explained sleep-rape). Perhaps believed per her words per Rabbeinu Yonah as Panim Me'irrot part 3 siman 22 ruled without miggo; Knesset Yechezkel siman above raised without miggo to say raped; Kuntres Acharon on Sefas Yemini permits with plain and presumption of righteousness — all the more here we explained permission without words through presumption of fitness; plainly rely on forbidders with plain and maybe through presumption of righteousness. Further innovation even forbidding Rabbeinu Yonah "I was raped" against majority consent without miggo — here since pregnant, one may say from this itself room to permit: what all poskim dispute applies only saying "I was raped"; but here she on her own claims raped during madness (responsum Pnei Aharon siman there sleep intercourse — see siman 81 letter 1) — possibly more lenient when pregnant, for we rule siman 13 seif 6 like R' Yosi a harlot turns over not to conceive — need not wait three months; also for raped woman this applies ch. 4 Brothers — certainly sleep-rape or during madness no turning-over presumption supporting truth of her words — if not she would turn over; he elaborated (siman 81, 93 on this).
