She loses only the basic ketubah. It is explained in the Gemara, Rashi, Ran, and Mordechai that if the ketubah were biblical, he would not be believed to make her lose it. Since the ketubah is an enactment of the Sages, "they said and they said": they believed him to make her lose the ketubah because of the presumption that a man does not trouble himself with a feast and ruin it. If he hated her he would not make a feast. If he did not trouble himself with a feast, he is not believed, as written by the Ran and Maggid Mishneh. This applies specifically where he claims open entrance, where he cannot clarify and is believed only because of that presumption. But if he claims blood, he need not rely on this presumption because the matter can be clarified, and he is believed even without a feast. From the Rambam and Tur, however, it does not appear so at first glance; perhaps according to our custom, where they do not inspect and there is concern that blood was lost, even a blood claim requires the presumption of a feast.
But she has the additional ketubah. Since the reason is "they said and they said," he is believed only regarding what is from the enactment of Chazal. But the additional ketubah is a gift, and he is not believed to make her lose it. The Ran and Maggid Mishneh write that some say this distinction applies specifically to open-entrance claims, but with a blood claim he is believed even regarding the additional ketubah. They write that according to the Rambam there is no distinction between open-entrance and blood claims, and as written nearby, even in a blood claim the Rambam requires the reason of "they said and they said" and "a person does not trouble himself with a feast." It appears from the Rambam that specifically where she contradicts him there is a distinction between ketubah and additional ketubah; but if she was silent it may be like admission. However, the Rosh, Ran, and Tur write that the sugya speaks where she contradicts him or is silent, and the Gemara still gives the feast presumption, which shows that silence is not admission. The Rashbam and Shiltei Gibborim rule that she loses even the additional ketubah, but the main law appears like the many decisors, and he cannot say kim li like Rashbam and Shiltei Gibborim against them. In a blood claim, however, there may be room to say kim li like those who make her lose the additional ketubah.
That she had already had relations. Even if he does not bring proof that she had relations before erusin, but only clarifies that she had relations at the time of the act, this is enough when she contradicts him and says she was a virgin. But if she admits, then her admission must be that she had relations before erusin. Maharshal.
