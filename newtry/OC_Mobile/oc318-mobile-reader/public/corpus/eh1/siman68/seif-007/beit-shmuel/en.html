If he is a kohen. The rule is that where there is only one doubt, we are stringent in a biblical prohibition. With the wife of a Yisrael there is a double doubt: perhaps it was by force, and perhaps it was before betrothal. But with a kohen’s wife, or where her father accepted kiddushin before she was three, there is only one doubt and she is forbidden. One should not say there is still a double doubt, perhaps she was injured by wood or perhaps one of the other claims is true, because injury by wood is uncommon, and if it happened she would not be ashamed to claim it. Nor should one say perhaps it was by force and perhaps by consent when she was a minor, since seduction of a minor is like ones and permitted; this is all one doubt. Although a woman is not forbidden to her husband except through two witnesses or warning and seclusion, the Gemara at the beginning of Ketubot answers that an open-entrance claim is like two witnesses. The Mordechai’s wording should be read as "we hold," not as the Bach read it. The Chelkat Mechokek writes that nowadays, when kiddushin are under the chuppah and no one is secluded with the bride, if she is found not to be a virgin she is permitted to her husband, because certainly she had relations before the chuppah.
She is permitted to him. But she has no ketubah, because regarding ketubah there is only one doubt: perhaps she was raped while under him; but if she had relations before that, he married her under the presumption of virginity and she has nothing. The Chelkat Mechokek also writes this. It follows that if there were a double doubt she would not lose the ketubah, and a double doubt helps even to extract money, as explained in Tosafot and as I wrote above in the name of Responsa Ben Lev and Rashach. The Taz in siman 67 disagrees.
And some say that what she is forbidden, etc. This is the view of Rabbi Yonah, the Rosh, Ramban, and Rashba, and so ruled Shiltei Gibborim. It is explained in the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 18 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, and in Shiltei Gibborim, that even with the wife of a kohen, if she says, "I was raped before erusin," she is permitted to him.
