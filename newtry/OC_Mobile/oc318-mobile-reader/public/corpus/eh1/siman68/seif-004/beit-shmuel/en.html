He did not have relations with her. So wrote the Rambam, who speaks where she contradicts him. If he is a kohen, or if her father accepted kiddushin when she was under three years old, there is no double doubt and she is forbidden to him. Therefore the Chelkat Mechokek wrote that if he is a kohen he may not have relations with her. What is written here, "or he is secluded with her," speaks where he is a Yisrael and at kiddushin she was over three years old, so she is permitted to him by double doubt. Their claim here is monetary. Although when she contradicts him he is believed monetarily when he cannot clarify, here there is clarification through relations. Even though her presumption assists her, that she would not brazenly lie about a matter he knows, nevertheless where there is no double doubt he has made her forbidden to himself. In my view the Talmud does not speak where she contradicts him, but where she says he had relations by tilting, so it is her definite claim against his uncertainty; afterward, if he says explicitly that he did not tilt, and if he does not say so explicitly, she is believed. Since it can be clarified through wine barrels, they clarify it, but in truth she is permitted to him. The Tur can also be explained this way. Thus the Bach’s words on the Tur are correct, while the Chelkat Mechokek’s comments apply to the Mechaber, who used the Rambam’s language where she contradicts him.
They examine her. See Tosafot at the beginning of Ketubot, s.v. "most are expert," that this examination applies when one has relations through tilting. See Bach and Chelkat Mechokek.
Or he is secluded with her before witnesses and has relations. This means the seclusion is before witnesses, but the relations may not be before witnesses. It appears that he must inspect her and guard that no blood comes, since according to his words he already had relations and found no blood.
