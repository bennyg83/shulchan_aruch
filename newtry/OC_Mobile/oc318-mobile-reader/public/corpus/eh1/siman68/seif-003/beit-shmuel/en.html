Whether an adult woman. See the Rosh at the beginning of Ketubot, where he agrees with the Rambam’s version that every bogeret has blood. At first glance this contradicts what he wrote in Niddah at the beginning of Perek Tinoket, that even a bogeret, although she has no virginity claim, nevertheless regarding one who has initial mitzvah-relations and separates, the law applies even to a bogeret. Since some bogeret women have blood, Chazal did not distinguish. The Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 193, also writes even a bogeret, implying that some bogeret women have no blood. Yet here the Tur wrote that every virgin has blood, whether minor, na'arah, or adult. Tosafot in Ketubot 9 also write in the name of Rabbenu Chananel that with a bogeret he has no claim if he did not find blood, because some have blood and some do not. So too in Mishmeret HaBayit. If so, why is a bogeret given the first night and all blood is attributed to virginity? The answer is that we do not attribute stringently to menstrual blood, which is very uncommon outside the expected time. This also resolves the view that when blood is found he has no open-entrance claim. Even though a virgin is given four nights and a minor until the wound heals, this is because once he had relations and found blood we attribute it to virgin blood, for menstrual blood is uncommon; but when we do not know whether she was previously had relations, we say he is lying and this was the first act. According to the Rashba’s version in the Yerushalmi, some bogeret women have open entrance, some closed, and some have blood; according to that, the sugya cannot be resolved like Rashi or the Bach.
An open-entrance claim does not apply to a bogeret. The same applies to a woman injured by wood, as written by Tosafot, Mordechai, and Ran, and so it implies in Rambam, chapter 11. But according to Rabbenu Chananel, a woman injured by wood has a closed entrance.
