I am injured by wood. In Ketubot 12 it is explained that according to Rabban Gamliel she is believed because she claims with certainty and he claims uncertainty. Although generally we do not extract money on a definite claim against uncertainty, here she extracts the ketubah because she has a migo. Rashi explains that if she says she was raped after betrothal, she disqualifies herself to a kohen; therefore she has a migo that she could have said she was injured by wood. If she says she was injured by wood before marriage and therefore has only one hundred, she has a migo that she could have said she was injured under him and has two hundred. Although we do not usually say migo to extract, here, where she has a definite claim, he has an uncertain claim, and she has a migo, money is extracted. So wrote the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot. According to this, if she says "I was injured by wood under you," she is not believed, since there is no migo. The Gemara also gives another reason: we place her on her presumption of virginity, and presumably she was injured or had relations while under him. According to that reason, even if she says she was injured by wood under him, she is believed. The Rif brings only this reason, and so does the Ran, but the Rambam, Tur, and Shiltei Gibborim cite the first reason, so one should be concerned for it. If she contradicts him, she is not believed with a migo, because where he claims definite, her migo does not extract money; also, she may not want to say she was raped after betrothal because that disqualifies her to a kohen, and we do not say a migo of injured by wood because it is uncommon.
And if she says, "I was raped after betrothal." If it is certain that the event was not in the husband’s domain but in her father’s domain, then her father’s domain has been weakened and we say, "here it was found, here it was," meaning she had relations before kiddushin, unless she brings proof that she was raped after erusin. Tosafot, Perek HaMadir. If she says she was raped after betrothal, she has the ketubah but not the additional ketubah, since she was not a virgin before chuppah and he wrote the additional ketubah only on condition that she be a virgin. Shiltei Gibborim. According to this, the basic ketubah and additional ketubah are opposites: when she contradicts him she has the additional ketubah but not the basic ketubah; when she explains and says she was raped after betrothal, she has the basic ketubah but not the additional.
If he wants to keep her afterward. So wrote the Rambam and Tur. Tosafot, however, on Ketubot 11 write that the husband is believed because of the presumption of not ruining the feast only where she is forbidden to him or where the marriage is a mistaken transaction; but where she still has a ketubah of one hundred, he loses no feast, but rather gains one hundred, and is not believed. It follows also that where it is a mistaken transaction he must betroth and marry her again, as written in siman 60. Shiltei Gibborim writes that he must divorce her, and if he does not divorce her he is not believed. The Chelkat Mechokek explains the Rambam’s statement that he writes her a ketubah of one hundred as speaking where she agrees. Darkei Moshe did not accept this distinction and wrote that Shiltei Gibborim disagrees with the Rambam. The Rambam is not concerned that he creates a rumor in order to gain one hundred, and so wrote the Rosh: a person does not trouble himself with a feast and ruin it, even though he gains one hundred.
