(ג) And on the second one writes "who is called." This is difficult, for nearby it is written that for a foreign-language nickname one writes "hamkhuneh"; and it is also difficult from what Rama wrote in seif 14, that for a gentile name one writes "who is called." My teacher and father-in-law, z"l, entered into a distinction here and wrote that in responsum he elaborated on a practical case. I was in the holy community of Krakow when this case occurred, where the scribe wrote by my teacher and father-in-law's instruction "who is called" for a foreign-language name, based on Rama's words, and several rabbis objected, among them R. Neta, z"l, author of Megaleh Amukot. He told me that a stumbling block had emerged from my teacher and father-in-law's hand in this, and certainly there is a scribal error in this gloss; it should read "hamkhuneh." I said hastily that perhaps the gloss means that the phrase "who is called" can apply even to a foreign-language name when it is an actual alternate name, while "hamkhuneh" applies to a mere nickname. Nevertheless, for practice one must be careful, because the older authorities use "hamkhuneh" for such names.
