(ב) We write "who is called." At first glance this contradicts what was written nearby, that when one of the names is a foreign-language name, one writes "who is known as." Bach distinguished and wrote that where his father gave him two names when entering him into the covenant, Rama distinguishes between foreign-language names and sacred names; but if a new name was added afterward, one always writes "who is called," even for a foreign-language name. This distinction is not correct, as is clear from Responsa Maharam Mintz and Responsa Rama. It is more reasonable that the Rav Rama's intent here is not to teach whether one writes "who is known as" or "who is called"; rather, his intent here is that the Hebrew name is primary. So too wrote Taz and Nachalat Shivah. As a matter of law, it appears primary that where there is doubt one writes "who is called," as written in Responsa Rama, siman 84; see there. The term "who is called" applies even to a nickname, for that is what people call him. But with a Hebrew name the term "nickname" does not apply. So too wrote Sefer Shemot, folio 31.
