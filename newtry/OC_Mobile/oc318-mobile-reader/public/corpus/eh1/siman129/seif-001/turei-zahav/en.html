(א) And even after the fact. Only if he wrote the principal name alone is it valid; but if he wrote only the secondary name, it is not valid unless he includes with it "and every name," etc. This is what the Gemara means by "Miriam and every name": ab initio; and "Sarah and every name": also ab initio. But after the fact, "Sarah and every name" also helps. Sarah alone, however, obviously does not help even after the fact, for we found permission only with a widely known nickname, and there the nickname is like the principal name alone, which is valid even ab initio without "and every name." It follows that ab initio we require two advantages: mention of the principal name and also "and every name." After the fact, one advantage suffices, either the principal name alone or the secondary name with "and every name." But a secondary name without "and every name" does not help even after the fact. Taz continues to reconcile the Tur, Rabbenu Tam, Halachot Gedolot, and the Shulchan Aruch, and distinguishes between a true alternate name, a nickname, and a name that has become forgotten.
