(א) The name of the place of writing, "who is called." Although nearby he wrote that one writes "and every name," the two places are different. Taz wrote that where it is known to everyone that he has another name in another place, everyone agrees that the names are specified; the poskim disagree only where it is not known to everyone that he has another name. So too the Tur wrote in the name of Ramah, that one specifies both names. His reason is that it is doubtful whether the place of writing is primary, as stated in the Talmud, or whether the place of giving is primary, as stated in the Tosefta; therefore one specifies both. The Mechaber abbreviated this law, so I will briefly state the approaches. According to the Rosh, Ran, and Tur: if he has one name in the place of writing and another in the place of giving, and they know in the place of writing that he has another name in the place of giving, she must write both. If she wrote only one, even if she wrote the name of the place of giving, the get is invalid. This is proven in the Talmud, chapter HaSholeach: if he divorced his wife in Judea with his Judean name, meaning the place of giving as Tosafot wrote, she is not divorced until he divorces his wife in Judea with his Judean name and the Galilean name with it. But if they did not know that he had another name, the get is valid, even if afterward it becomes known that he had another name. According to Semag and Maggid Mishneh, however, the sugya has another explanation: if he has two names, one in the place of writing and one in the place of giving, even if they do not know that he has another name elsewhere, the get is invalid, lest people in that place say that this is not the same man who divorced. What Rav Ashi said, "but where he was not established with two names," means that he does not have a special name in each place; rather, the name he has in the place of giving is also known in the place of writing, and in both places they know both names. Even though he has another name in a third place that is neither the place of writing nor the place of giving, it is valid because he wrote the name of the place of writing and giving. This is the approach of Ba'al HaTerumah.
