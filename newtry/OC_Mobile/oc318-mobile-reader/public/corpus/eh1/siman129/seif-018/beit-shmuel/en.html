(ה) Two times, etc. With this kind of name change one writes demitkarei, all without a vav, because the later name always uproots the first name, and therefore it is not appropriate to say umitkarei. But with a nickname one writes vehamekhuneh, as written above: hamekhuneh Zalman vehamekhuneh Zamil. If his name was changed because of some other matter, it appears that one writes udemitkarei, for then we do not say that the first name was uprooted, unless it was changed because of illness. However, perhaps one could say that the Sages did not distinguish. If his name was changed and the first name fell out of use, Beit Yosef wrote that two Gittin should be given; but in Mordekhai, Perek HaSholeach, and in the Ashkenazic responsum cited by Beit Yosef, they wrote that one should divorce only with the second name, and so he wrote in siman 128. This is also found in Rashi at the end of Perek Bameh Madlikin, and so Levush ruled, and so Maharam's Seder wrote; this is the main ruling. Levush wrote that if the obsolete name was also written, the Get is invalid, even though it was his first name. In Ger HaMegarresh he wrote that if a convert's gentile name fell out of use and he wrote the gentile name, it is valid after the fact. At first glance this is surprising: why is it different from an Israelite name that fell out of use? But in truth this is not difficult at all, for with an Israelite name there is concern that people will say this is not the divorcing man but another person with that name; but no Jew has a gentile name. I wrote similarly above in the name of Taz.
