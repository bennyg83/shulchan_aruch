(א) If they have a nickname, one writes it afterward. The reason is that if one writes "kohen" before the nickname, it sounds as though "kohen" too is a nickname. What he wrote afterward, that if one writes "who is called" for someone, one writes it afterward, is difficult: what difference is there between "who is known as" and "who is called"? In Seder Gittin he wrote this in the name of Maharik, and wrote about it: perhaps one should distinguish between "who is known as" and "who is called," but he did not tell me the reason; he only instructed the scribe to write so according to his judgment. It appears to me that this is the distinction: the expression "who is called" refers to an actual name that belongs to him, and certainly if he were not truly a kohen they would not call him "kohen." Therefore one will not err if "kohen" is written after "who is called." But the expression "who is known as" can properly mean that people nickname him "kohen" for some reason, as is common with nicknames, and one may come to err if "kohen" is written after the nickname.
