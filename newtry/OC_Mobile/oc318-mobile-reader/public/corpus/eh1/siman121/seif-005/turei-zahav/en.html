(ב) That we examine him, etc. In the Gemara: let us be concerned that perhaps he is seized by a nodding sickness of "no, no," meaning an insanity that constantly teaches him to lower his head as a gesture of no or yes, and he is not answering their questions. "Shicha" means bowing. Alternatively, perhaps he is seized by a nodding of yes. Rav Nachman said that we ask him intermittently. Rashi explained: to pause between the nodding and the question, and afterward they return and ask him the same question. The Gemara asks: let us be concerned that perhaps he is seized by intermittent nodding. They say to him: one no, two yeses, two noes, and one yes. The academy of R. Yishmael taught: they say to him things of summer in the rainy season and things of the rainy season in summer. What are these? If a cloak, meaning they ask him in summer whether he wants a cloak, which is a winter garment, let us be concerned that perhaps he is cold; although he answers yes, he is not insane. Rather, they ask about fruit. The Tur wrote that they ask something to which it is fitting to answer, and if they see that his mind is settled and he indicates the answer no by lowering his head in the way people do for no, and yes for yes, three times for each, they write and give. They do not examine him three yeses one after another, but one no, two yeses, two noes, and one yes. They should not examine with things found in that season, but in summer ask whether he wants winter fruits; if he answers yes he is certainly insane, and if he answers no he is competent. This implies that the Tur requires both: the alternating order and examination with fruit. Tosafot hold that either alone helps, but the Tur requires both. The Shulchan Aruch ruled otherwise, that one does not need both examinations. It appears to me one should be stringent like the Tur, requiring both.
