One yes and two no. It is explained in the Talmud that if they ask him three times yes and three times no consecutively, it is ineffective, because perhaps he has adopted the pattern of yes, yes. If he answers no to a question requiring yes, we may not immediately presume him definitely insane; perhaps he has adopted the pattern of no, no, and we examine him further. But if they ask him in alternating fashion and he answers no where yes is expected, we presume him definitely insane and do not examine further. Tosafot and the Tur prove that even when asking alternately, they ask one yes and two no, one yes and two no, so that there not be three consecutive no answers. This is unlike the Mechaber’s wording of one yes, two no, one no, etc. Rashi’s first explanation of alternating means that he pauses between question and question; so wrote Ri"v, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, and one should be concerned for their words.
(ג) And some say that we examine him by means of fruit. It implies that these six examinations are required, and intermittently as above, even if he is examined by means of fruit. See Beit Yosef and Bach.
