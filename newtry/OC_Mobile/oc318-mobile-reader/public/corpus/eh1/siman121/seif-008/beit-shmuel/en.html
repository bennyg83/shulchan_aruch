And he signaled and said. Meaning that they said to him, "Shall we write a get for your wife?" and he nodded his head. So explained Tosafot, and they hold that examination is unnecessary; this is also implied by Rambam and the Tur. According to Rambam this works well, because he holds that if one became deranged after commanding them to write, by Torah law they write and give the get. Here, at the time he signaled to write, we are not concerned that his mind became confused, for he was healthy and then was slaughtered, and certainly when he signaled his mind had not yet become confused, as Tosafot wrote. At the time of delivery, we are not concerned lest his mind become confused, because that is only a rabbinic concern. But according to the Tur's approach, that this is a Torah concern, it is difficult why we are not concerned that his mind became confused. After the fact, if they did not examine him, this requires study, for we do not find such an examination except that the Ran wrote he was inclined to require examination. One can say that after the fact, even if they did not examine him, they give the get. Bach wrote that we are not expert in these examinations. Chelkat Mechokek wrote against this that it does not seem so from Rama's words, and the matter requires consideration. Nevertheless, it appears that Bach admits that an ill person who can speak does not require an actual examination; likewise one whose throat was cut, for according to most poskim no examination is needed at all.
