A sick person whose cure is in our hands. According to the Tur, if he became deranged at the time of writing and delivery, even if he was mentally sound when he commanded them to write, the get is void by biblical law, and there is no distinction between whether the cure is in our hands or not. So ruled Shiltei Gibborim, and he wrote that it does not even disqualify her for kehunah. But the Mechaber distinguishes, according to the Rambam, between a case where the cure is in our hands and where it is not, and this does not appear correct. It is more reasonable that the Rambam holds that if he was mentally sound when he commanded them to write, then although he became deranged at the time of writing and delivery, she is divorced by biblical law and the get is invalid only rabbinically. Therefore even according to Rabbi Yochanan it is only rabbinically invalid, even where the cure is not in our hands. So wrote the Bach and Chelkat Mechokek. This explains why no examination is needed where most of the signs were cut, as written at the end of the siman, and we are not concerned that he perhaps became deranged. Although at the time he hinted to write there is not such great concern that he became deranged, as Tosafot write, why are we not concerned that he became deranged at the time of writing and delivery? Rather, because this is only rabbinic, we are not concerned for it.
