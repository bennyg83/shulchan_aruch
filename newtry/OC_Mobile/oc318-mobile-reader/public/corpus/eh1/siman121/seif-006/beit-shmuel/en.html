He divorces by hint. This implies no examination is needed, as Beit Yosef wrote, because just as a mute or deaf-mute married by hint, so he divorces by hint. Beit Yosef wrote that this is implied by all the decisors, except that Ri"v rules he requires examination, and the Rashba also holds so, though he did not rule like them. However, the Rif and the Rosh at the beginning of Perek Mi SheAchazo write that a congenital deaf-mute is examined. The Maggid Mishneh also writes this. According to Beit Yosef, this examination is not an actual sanity examination but only checking whether he wants to divorce his wife. Hagahot Maimoniyot, however, writes in the name of the Rif that they examine him, so one should not be lenient in this. This hint means with his head or hands, but mouth movements do not help. Handwriting depends on the dispute: according to the Rambam’s approach, handwriting is like hinting and may help; but according to the other decisors I brought in siman 120, handwriting does not help, and bodily hinting is better. What the Bach wrote in the name of Tosafot, that handwriting helps, is not precise; Tosafot wrote that only at the beginning of the sugya, when the assumption was that writing is better than hinting, but according to the conclusion that hinting is better, handwriting does not help. If, however, he married through handwriting, one can say that just as he married through handwriting he can divorce through handwriting; likewise if he married through mouth movements, he divorces through mouth movements. A deaf-mute who divorces has a get written like the standard formula, and a document is given to the woman stating that he divorced her through beit din by hint. See Ri"v and Responsa Tzemach Tzedek for the formula. Even if he himself betrothed her, the scribe’s writing by his hint is effective. Although I brought above the dispute whether full agency is needed to write a get, one cannot prove from here that agency is unnecessary, because since his kiddushin are not full kiddushin, full agency is not required here and they were lenient with a deaf-mute. But generally full agency may be required. An insane person’s kiddushin are not kiddushin. If he was insane at kiddushin, recovered, and became insane again, perhaps when sound he had relations for the sake of kiddushin; this applies when he was secluded with her before witnesses. If he was not secluded before witnesses but lived with her as husband and wife, I have written several times that the decisors dispute this. The signs of insanity are explained at the beginning of Chagigah, and the Rosh rules that even one sign, if done in a deranged manner, makes him a shoteh. Beit Yosef derives similarly from the Rambam. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 35, and Yoreh Deah, siman 1, where the listed signs are examples, not exclusive.
