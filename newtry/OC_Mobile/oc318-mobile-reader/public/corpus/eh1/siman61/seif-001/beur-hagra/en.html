(א) "Some say etc." — Why Rav Ashi posed the question here. Some say etc. See what I wrote regarding ch. 6 of Yevamot — "there is chuppah for those forbidden in marriage" — and therefore Rav Ashi raised this question here rather than being troubled by the Mishnah in ch. 4 of Ketubot: "A woman never eats" etc. See also the Ran and the Rosh.
(ב) "And they must etc." — Source for the Rambam's requirement. And they must etc. The Rambam, following his own method, and as stated in the Gemara there: "Resh Lakish said... a chuppah fit for intimacy" etc.
(ג) "A woman etc. since etc." — Requires study. A woman etc. since etc. This requires further study; see the Chelkat Mechokek and the Beit Shmuel.
(ד) "She entered the chuppah etc." — Cross-references to simanim 55 and 57. She entered the chuppah etc. As stated above in siman 55, seif 3, and as I wrote above in siman 57: "How so" etc., "if" etc.
(ה) "And ab initio etc." — Source in ch. 1 of Ketubot. And ab initio etc. Gemara, ch. 1 [of Ketubot], and as stated there in the gloss at the end of siman 61.
(ו) "And the custom etc." — Source for fasting in Yerushalmi. And the custom etc. From the Yerushalmi on Bikkurim: "A scholar, a groom, and a prince have all their sins forgiven" — a groom, as it says "and he took Machalat" etc.
(ז) "If he betrothed etc." — Sources for not requiring new blessings. If he betrothed etc. The first blessings suffice, as stated in the chapter "HaMadir" [of Ketubot]: "If he betrothed her in error or for less than a perutah's worth" etc., "and if he was intimate with her, they have acquired" — indicating that neither re-entry into a new chuppah nor new blessings are required. Similarly in the chapter "Four Methods of Execution": "One who marries off his sons" etc. "close to their coming of age" etc. — and even though a minor's kiddushin amounts to nothing, intimacy is nonetheless permitted. Similarly in the chapter of the deaf-mute: "A deaf-mute, an imbecile, and a minor who married women" etc., "she has no claim against them" — "if they wished to keep them, she has a ketubah, and they have no kiddushin even rabbinicly," as I will write; see also the Haggahot Maimoniyot on Kiddushin, siman 884, which elaborated on this.
