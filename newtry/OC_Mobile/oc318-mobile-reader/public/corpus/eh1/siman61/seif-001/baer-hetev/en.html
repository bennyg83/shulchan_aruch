He brought her into marriage. If he brought her under the chuppah and she became a niddah during the chuppah, we say that the beginning of the chuppah effects acquisition. So ruled the Rosh.
A betrothed woman. This applies specifically when he died before she had an opportunity for relations. If she had an opportunity for relations before he died, she is no longer regarded as one who became a niddah. If the woman says that relations occurred, she is believed. See Raanach, part 1, siman 77. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that where it is known to everyone that he had no opportunity for relations, she is not believed. If she entered the chuppah and became a niddah, and the husband became ill, and after her days of niddah passed she immersed, and at the time of her immersion the husband recovered and remained well for a day or two before dying, then if he was healthy during that day or two and she says that relations occurred, she is believed. If he remained ill, she is not believed. Raanach, ibid.
The chuppah. If the kesubah was written and was found invalid, the chuppah is not considered to have been entered into in error, and another kesubah must be written for her. Responsa of the Geonim, siman 286.
Sickly. This is because she is entirely unfit for relations. At the end of siman 56, regarding a sickly woman whom he inherits, the case concerns one who is fit for relations. Bach. See the later authorities.
They fast. Two reasons are given. The first is that it is their day of forgiveness and their sins are forgiven. According to this reason, they should complete the fast. The second reason is lest they become intoxicated and their minds not be settled. According to this reason, they need not complete the fast. If the father accepts the kiddushin on behalf of his minor daughter, he must fast according to this reason. On days when fasting is not practiced, as explained in Orach Chayyim, they must take care not to pursue excess food and drink. Knesses HaGedolah cites a sage who ruled that on days when they do not fast, the chuppah should be held in the morning and they should not eat until after the chuppah. See there, folio 90b.
Seven blessings. See the later authorities, Responsa Radbaz, part 1, siman 185, and Mahariv, simanim 7 and 138.
The heavens. The groom should stand to the bride's left, so that the bride is at his right, as it is written, "The queen stands at your right." Some position the groom and bride toward the west with their faces toward the east, so that they face the Aron Kodesh, while the one reciting the blessing turns himself toward the east. Others position the groom and bride toward the south, and others toward the north. See Maharam Mintz, siman 109. In the Derashos Maharil it is written that their backs are toward the north and their faces toward the south. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that nowadays we are not particular about this. Two narrow mouthed flasks are taken as a sign that she is a virgin, while for a widow two open vessels are taken. Maharam Mintz, siman 109. The one reciting the seven blessings stands in the west facing east, and when he says "Grant abundant joy," he turns toward the groom and bride. Derashos Maharil.
