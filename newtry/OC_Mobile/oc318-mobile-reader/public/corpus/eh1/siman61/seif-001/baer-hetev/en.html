(א) "He brought her in." He brought her in. If he brought her to chuppah and she became a niddah during the chuppah, we say the beginning of the chuppah acquires — Rosh.
(ב) "Betrothed." Betrothed. This applies specifically when he died before she was fit for intimacy; but if she was fit for intimacy before he died, it is no longer called "she became a niddah." If the woman claims they were intimate, she is believed — Raanach, Part 1, siman 77. The Knesset HaGedolah wrote: this appears to apply unless it is publicly known that he had no opportunity to be intimate, in which case she is not believed. If she entered chuppah and became a niddah, and the husband fell ill — and after her days of niddah elapsed she immersed, and at the time of her immersion the husband recovered and was healthy for a day or two and then died — if during that day or two he was healthy and she claims they were intimate, she is believed; but if he was ill she is not believed — Raanach there.
(ג) "The chuppah." The chuppah. If the ketubah was written and found to be invalid, the chuppah is not considered performed in error, and a new ketubah must be written for her — Responsum of the Geonim, siman 286.
(ד) "Mortally ill." Mortally ill. Since she is not fit for intimacy at all. The ruling at the end of siman 56 that he does inherit a sick woman refers to a case where she was fit for intimacy — Bach; see the later authorities.
(ה) "They fast." They fast. There are two reasons: (1) because it is a day of forgiveness for them and their sins are pardoned — accordingly they should complete the fast; (2) lest they become intoxicated and their minds not be settled — accordingly they need not complete the fast. If the father accepts the kiddushin on behalf of his minor daughter, he must fast according to this second reason. On days when one does not fast, as stated in Orach Chayyim, one must be careful not to pursue excess food and drink. The Knesset HaGedolah cited in the name of a certain scholar that on days when one does not fast, the chuppah should be performed in the morning and one should not eat until after the chuppah — see there, folio 90b.
(ו) "Seven blessings." Seven blessings. See the later authorities and the Radbaz, Part 1, siman 185; and Mahariv, siman 7 and siman 138.
(ז) "The heavens." — Positioning at the chuppah; vessels; direction faced. The heavens. The groom should stand to the left of the bride so that the bride is on the groom's right, as it is written: "The queen stands at your right" (Psalms 45:10). Some position the groom and bride facing west with their faces toward the east, so their faces will be toward the Holy Ark; the one reciting the blessings turns himself so his face is toward the east. Some position the groom and bride facing south. Some position them facing north — Maharam Mintz, siman 109. The Maharil's homilies write that their backs should face north and their faces south. The Knesset HaGedolah writes that in our times we do not insist on this. Two narrow-mouthed flasks are taken as a sign that she is a virgin; for a widow, two open-mouthed vessels — Maharam Mintz, siman 109. The one who recites the seven blessings stands in the west facing east, and when he says "Rejoice and be glad" he turns his face toward the groom and bride — Maharil's homilies.
