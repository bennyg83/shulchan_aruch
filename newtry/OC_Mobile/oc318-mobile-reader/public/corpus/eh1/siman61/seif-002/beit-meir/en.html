"Behold she is as his wife." — Resolving the Beit Shmuel's challenge; chuppah = seclusion derived from Talmud folio 56; the im timtza lomar structure. See the Beit Shmuel's challenge against the Rambam: if chuppah means seclusion, what is the Talmudic question about a niddah becoming a niddah? One should say it is a forbidden chuppah that does not acquire — the same as the Ran's challenge. See siman 55 where, in my humble view, the Ran's proof from the statement about a forbidden act is not conclusive. Even according to this approach one may resolve: he derived that ordinary chuppah means seclusion from the Talmud's resolution (folio 56): "A chuppah too presents no difficulty, since ordinary chuppah exists for the sake of intimacy — hence it teaches us that seclusion by night is required." From this one certainly gathers that ordinary chuppah means seclusion — for otherwise, how would it be "ordinary" to be for intimacy? Furthermore, why should the ordinary practice be by night? This certainly indicates ordinary chuppah means seclusion. This resolves the Rosh's challenge: why was this question placed here? One must have thought that an ordinary chuppah involves seclusion without intimacy, and there would be nothing to question about fitness for intimacy in this regard. But once we learn from Rav Yosef's teaching that ordinary chuppah means seclusion, the question about fitness for intimacy is quite appropriate. One can also resolve the Chelkat Mechokek's challenge: he argued that since the first question necessarily does not concern inheritance under Torah law, and the second question concerns all matters — the Beit Shmuel wrote: "According to what I wrote in siman 56, there is no difficulty, and all the questions" etc. In my humble view this is certainly incorrect, for then: how is it possible that chuppah does not acquire until intimacy? If so, what purpose does this chuppah serve? Through intimacy alone she is as his wife for all purposes, as in siman 55. Even if one might compare them — saying the function of chuppah is to permit intimacy without specific marital intent, and the entire question is only regarding acquisitions — nevertheless one may object: if so, one should resolve it from Rav Huna, who in tractate Kiddushin derives through a kal va-chomer (a fortiori) that chuppah acquires: "A betrothal, which does not complete the marriage, acquires; a chuppah, which completes — does it not acquire?" If chuppah only acquires together with intimacy, how is this a kal va-chomer — intimacy alone also obviously acquires? Rather, the answer must be that the first question was only regarding the additional ketubah, and the second also regarding inheritance — see the Beit Shmuel there. According to what I wrote, one may say: the first question was always only regarding the additional ketubah, and nevertheless it was stated in the "if you say" form — because if the affinity of intimacy alone acquires, then nothing is learned from Rav Yosef's teaching, since he wrote only with reference to the affinity of the first night, which refers to chuppah — and we would not learn that ordinary chuppah means seclusion; obviously there would be nothing to question about whether a chuppah fit for intimacy is required. But if one says the affinity of chuppah acquires, then we learn from Rav Yosef that ordinary chuppah means seclusion — appropriately one may then question whether a chuppah fit for intimacy is required. This is clear.
