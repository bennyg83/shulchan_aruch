"That she is a niddah." — Custom regarding marrying a niddah; ring placement; when the Rambam's stringency applies. That she is a niddah. The Knesset HaGedolah wrote that the custom now is to marry women who are niddot even without informing the groom that she is a niddah. However, the Maharil's homilies wrote that if she is not pure, the groom does not touch her, but allows the ring to fall on her finger by itself. Our practice is not so — rather, the groom himself places the ring on her finger. For before he betrothed her with the ring she was not yet his wife, and immediately upon betrothing her with the ring the bride distances herself from the groom if she is a niddah. The Knesset HaGedolah wrote that the practice of betrothing and entering chuppah even if she is a niddah applies specifically when one wishes to marry now and it is impossible for her to purify — such as where her days of niddah and counting have not yet elapsed. But if her days of niddah and counting have elapsed and she is able to immerse, yet does not wish to immerse for some other reason, one should take into account the view of the Rambam that she should not marry until she purifies. I acted accordingly — see there, folio 30a, section 14.
