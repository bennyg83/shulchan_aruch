That she is a niddah. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that nowadays the custom is to marry women who are niddos even without informing the groom that she is a niddah. However, the Derashos Maharil states that if she is not tahor, he does not touch her, but allows the ring to fall onto her finger by itself. We do not practice this; rather, the groom himself places the ring on her finger, because before he betroths her with the ring she is not yet his wife. Immediately after he betroths her with the ring, the bride distances herself from the groom if she is a niddah. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that the rule permitting him to betroth her and bring her into the chuppah even when she is a niddah applies specifically when he wishes to marry now and she cannot immerse, such as when her days of niddah and clean days have not yet passed. If her days of niddah and clean days have passed and she is able to immerse, but she does not wish to immerse for another reason, it is proper to be concerned for the Rambam's view that she should not marry until she becomes tahor. I ruled accordingly in practice. See there, folio 30a, se'if 14.
