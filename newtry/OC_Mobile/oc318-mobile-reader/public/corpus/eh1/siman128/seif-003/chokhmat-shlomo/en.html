Seif 4 in the gloss. NB: See Beit Shmuel s.k. 53, where he answers in the name of Levush. This also answers what was difficult regarding Beit Shmuel s.k. 12 in the name of Maharik: if the majority draw water from one river and a minority from another, then if he wrote the second river some invalidate, even though Rama wrote that a change in the river is valid. One must say that since the majority do not draw from the second river, it is as though the city sits only on one river. When he writes both rivers, he changes the city's description, since in truth the city sits only on one. This resembles the case where the river is outside the techum, as Beit Shmuel wrote in the name of Levush. According to this, Maharik invalidates specifically where he wrote both rivers, for then it is a change of the city. But if he wrote only the second river by itself, it is valid, even though only the minority draw from it, because he wrote only one river and the city truly sits on one river; it is merely a change in the river, and Maharik agrees that such a case is valid. This appears clear to me in practice.
See also Beit Shmuel s.k. 14, who wrote that if only some draw from a spring or wells, one should not write them, since not all their uses are from there. Above in s.k. 12 he wrote that even if the majority draw from one river and a minority from another, one should write all of them. One must distinguish between a minority of people who draw from a source, where for that minority all their use is nevertheless from it, and therefore it is like rivers and all must be written, and the later case, which concerns only some use from the spring, while no person's entire use is from it. Therefore one does not write the spring or the wells. This appears to me a clear distinction; examine carefully.
