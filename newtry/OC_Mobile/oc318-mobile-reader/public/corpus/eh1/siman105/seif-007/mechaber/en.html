If, in the life of his father, a man sold the ketubah of his mother, so that in the circumstance that his father died first, and then his mother died, and he would consequentially inherit his mother's ketubah, and the purchaser would stand in his place to collect the ketubah from the estate, and he made a condition that if his mother complained about the sale, that [the purchaser] would not remove her from her complaint; and she subsequently died without having complained about the sale; the son may not say, "I stand in the place of my mother, and just like she could have complained about the transaction, I can also complain about the transaction and hereby abrogate it," for he has definitely relinquished his own right to complain.
