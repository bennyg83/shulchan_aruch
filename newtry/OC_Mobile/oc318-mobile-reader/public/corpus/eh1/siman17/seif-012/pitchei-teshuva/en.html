Some say that if there was a quarrel. Beit Meir writes that this cannot refer to the quarrel mentioned in the Gemara in the chapter HaIshah Shalom, and below in seif 48, where she had already been disproven after saying, 'You divorced me,' and was found to have lied. If so, the law should have been stated without qualification, since her presumption against brazenness had already been impaired, and it could not properly be written in the name of 'some say,' for everyone would agree. See there. Beit Meir also asks that in this very sugya the Gemara asks, 'What constitutes a quarrel? If she says, You divorced me, all women say this.' Rashi explains: at the time of anger. Rather, it means that she says, 'You divorced me,' which itself is generally said amid quarrel and anger, yet the Gemara nevertheless asks that she should be believed because of Rav Hamnuna's rule. See Korban Nesanel on this sugya, letter 16, who explains that there is no difficulty. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 10, likewise concludes that an ordinary quarrel is intended; see there.
