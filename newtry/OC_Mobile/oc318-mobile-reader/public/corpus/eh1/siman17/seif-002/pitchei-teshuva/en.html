And she accepted kiddushin. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 204, who wrote: One can also say as a matter of law that if a married woman extended her hand and accepted kiddushin from a kohen, this is not equivalent to her saying, 'I was divorced,' since in any event these kiddushin were accepted through a prohibited act. I seem to recall seeing this reasoning in the words of one of the great later authorities, though I do not remember whom. End quote. I note that it is R. Aharon Sasson, siman 18, cited in Baer Heitev, s.k. 3.
