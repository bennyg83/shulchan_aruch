But if he heard from an imbecile or a minor. If he said, so-and-so died, but did not say other matters concerning the circumstances of his death, such as that such-and-such eulogizers were there, it does not help. There is no distinction whether they themselves testify or whether the witness heard from them; so ruled the Rambam, and so Beit Yosef and Bach explain the Tur's words. What is written nearby, that we require innocent speech, may be explained as speaking here where it was not innocent speech. In Perishah he explains the Tur's words: if the children said, so-and-so died, it helps even if they are not speaking innocently; but if they said, we are coming from his burial, we do not rely on them until they say, such-and-such eulogizers were there, etc. The Taz wrote that this is specifically with a minor who has reached the age of pe'utot, whose buying and selling are valid; but younger than this, it does not help even if he speaks innocently. If a minor who has reached the age of pe'utot relates that he was at the killing, how it happened and what caused the killing, this is comparable to saying, we are coming from his burial and such-and-such eulogizers were there, etc., and she may marry on his word. End quote of the Taz.

And specifically immediately. Meaning, he testifies immediately after seeing him. It is not necessary that he saw the dead person immediately after death; rather, immediately refers to his seeing. He says, I have now come from the burial; so it is in the rulings of Mahari. What is written, even if they grew up, means as follows: not only in his childhood is he not believed, but even if he testifies when he is an adult he is also not believed. The Taz wrote as follows: it appears obvious that this immediately does not refer to testifying before bet din, that he would have to come before bet din right away; rather, right after he comes to people, if he says this incident before them, that is enough, and the listener may afterward go to bet din even after time has passed. End quote. In this matter they dispute, whether immediately is required: Raavyah and Raavan, as written in the rulings of Mahari there. And the Rambam, who wrote, we have now come, implies that he too holds specifically immediately. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that stringently we are concerned for the stringent view in this dispute; however, this depends on the law whether we are stringent where there is a dispute.
