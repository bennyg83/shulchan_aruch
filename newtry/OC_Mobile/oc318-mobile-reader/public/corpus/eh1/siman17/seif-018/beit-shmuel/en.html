Provided that there were not established. According to the Rif, the Rosh, and the Tur, even if there was an established same-name case, she is permitted if caravans are not common. The Perishah explains that this means two people named Yosef ben Shimon were not established in this city; rather, it was merely established that somewhere in the world there was another Yosef ben Shimon. The Bayit Chadash explains that two people with that name were established in the same place, but one had gone on his way many days earlier and one had gone recently. The case is that witnesses say the one who went recently died, and we do not say that the one who went many days earlier returned and then went recently. One may also say that one of them went to the city where the court told the messenger to say that so-and-so had died; so writes the Taz. At first glance this is difficult: why do we not suspect that the second man, who went on another road, returned and then went to this city, as in siman 132, where we suspect that perhaps he came on a "flying camel"? Perhaps here we are lenient even about this, just as we are lenient and do not require two favorable factors, both that the name was not established and that caravans are uncommon, as the Rosh writes. But it is difficult: what source allows this leniency? Granted, the fact that we do not require both favorable factors can rely on the Talmudic wording, since there are two versions in the Talmud: according to one, both favorable factors are required; according to the other, one is enough, and here we are lenient. But whence do we know to be lenient and not suspect that the other man came, as we suspect by a get? One may answer that there, two favorable factors are required: caravans are not common and the name was not established. Therefore we suspect that that other man may have come, for the name is still established. But here, one favorable factor permits her. Therefore, if caravans are not common she is permitted. Only if the name was established in that place does "caravans are not common" fail to help, since there are two Yosef ben Shimon there. But if one went on his way, we permit with one favorable factor, namely that caravans are not common. Although there is a concern that the other man may have returned, since two Yosef ben Shimon are not present here, she is permitted. The language of the Talmud in Yevamot 116b, "Rava said," still requires explanation. The reason we are stringent by a get is that it is possible to write another get. According to the Ramban and Rashba, the reason by a get is that it was found away from the place where it was written; had it been found at the place of writing, we would say "here it was found, here it was," and would not say it fell from someone else. Here the case is that Yosef ben Shimon went from here to that city where the court told the messenger that so-and-so had died, and therefore we say "here it was found, here it was."
And some say that all this is specifically, etc. This implies that even if they told the messenger, when you go to that city, say that so-and-so died, it does not help unless he mentioned the city. According to this, the three opinions brought here fit well: the first opinion holds that even in such a case he must mention the city name; the second opinion is lenient in such a case, but generally requires mention of the city name, and it does not help if he omits the city even if he says his name and his father's name; and the third opinion holds that his name and his father's name help without the city name. Yet we have not found anyone who holds this, for when she says to him, when you go to such-and-such place, say that so-and-so died, it is as though they said explicitly that so-and-so from that city died. Chelkat Mechokek also discusses at length these three approaches brought by the Rama, and we have not found these opinions stated explicitly.
That the witness mentioned the name of his city. The same applies if he said, the Jew Reuven died; this is as though he said, Reuven from my city died, since he said the Jew. Responsum Maharshdam siman 51. If he said, Reuven who works at a trade in the city died, certainly his intent is the nearby city; see there siman 53. Responsum She’erit Yosef siman 31 distinguishes between the first witness and the second witness who testifies that he heard, where there is more room for leniency even if he did not mention the city. But the name of the province does not help, while his name and family name do help. Maharshdam siman 35. In seif 11 one must say that he mentioned the city name. Darkei Moshe wrote that according to those poskim who hold that one must mention the city name, if he did not mention it she is forbidden even if caravans are not common and no other person with that name is established.
