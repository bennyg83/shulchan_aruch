Gloss: And even if they know, etc.; if one can say that he wrote, etc. Beit Shmuel and Taz infer this. This is the language of the Taz: because of the rumor that emerged; even if a writing is found in which one person wrote to another that so-and-so died, and the drowning is not mentioned, nevertheless we say that he wrote so because of the rumor, and in truth there is no substance to that rumor, since the water has no end. So it is explicit in Terumat HaDeshen there. It follows from this that if a rumor emerged about someone that he died, and afterward a Jewish writing was found saying that he died, even though that rumor did not contain clarification, we do not disqualify the writing by saying that he wrote so because of the rumor; for if the rumor is true there is no defect here, unlike the case we mentioned, where he drowned in water. End quote. None of this language that he copied is found in the Terumat HaDeshen before me. However, regarding a gentile speaking innocently about a person who was killed after a rumor had emerged that he was killed, where there is concern that he spoke by conjecture, this is found in the poskim in siman 161.
