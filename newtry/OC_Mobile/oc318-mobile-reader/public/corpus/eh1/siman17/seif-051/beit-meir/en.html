Some say that she need not leave. Beit Shmuel wrote: at first glance this is difficult, etc. In my humble opinion it may be resolved according to the words of Ramban and Chiddushei HaRashba, who wrote, to reject the Rambam's proofs, that since he holds that one witness and another witness contradict one another, if so she need not leave. The Mishnah did not also teach that if she married she need not leave, because throughout the Mishnah it does not teach a case where she married in prohibition, but only in permission; and this woman, had she married with permission, would not leave because of the first permission. End quote. If so, it also cannot be established where she married without permission of bet din, for that too is in prohibition. One can further say that since it was obvious to the Ran that an actual marriage without permission of bet din is stronger than permission of bet din without an act, the Gemara properly asks, is the reason because she actually married? For the Tanna should have taught us Ulla's law, which is the greater novelty. It answers that married is not literal, but means that they permitted her; it says married only to teach that permission to marry is required, and this is clear. However, in the previous comment, in my humble opinion, I brought a proper proof from the Yerushalmi like Maharshdam.
