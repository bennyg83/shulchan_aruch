His face. They are the cheeks. And we do not pay attention neither to eyes nor mouth — Beit Yosef in name of Ramah.
In them. And here for all a single identifying mark without signs suffices; see Beit Shmuel.
For borrowing. See Kenesset HaGedolah 43a seif 156. Tzitzit on him is preferable to a ring, purse, and wallet — we are not concerned for borrowing — Mahari Metrani part 2 siman 35. Amulet — we are not concerned for borrowing — Mahari Weil part 1 siman 139. If the local custom is not to wear that garment except he alone wears it — we are not concerned for borrowing — Ra'anach part 1 siman 75. He had an earring in his ear — Ra'anach part 1 siman 51 brought responsum of Maharar R' A who holds we are not concerned for borrowing; but he disputes and holds we are concerned for borrowing. One who was carrying a deposit is a prominent sign — for a deposit we are not concerned he lent to another for a person does not want his deposit in another's hand — responsum Beit Yosef siman 7 on laws of gentile incidentally. That we are concerned for borrowing applies specifically to one object or two he does not need and can manage without those vessels or has other vessels at home; but one who left his house in the garments on him would not lend them to others and remain naked — Mahari Weil ch. 2 siman 35; and so Ram Padua siman 36 — if they recognized his garments he was wearing before drowning when they came on the ship we are not concerned for borrowing. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote fourteen years ago such a case came before us. A sage wished to permit saying that even per the view we are concerned for borrowing it did not happen that he lent his vessels to others and remained naked — per Ram Padua and Mahari Weil; I disputed him for the language of concern for borrowing implies no distinction whether he has two garments and lends one and remains with two, or even if he has only one garment we are concerned for borrowing and he acquired others or exchanged them — Yoreh De'ah 43. Ring and wallet — in responsum Beit Yosef siman 6 on laws of gentile incidentally ruled we are concerned for borrowing. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote on it I debated much in the responsum and concluded ring, purse, and wallet — all agree we are not concerned for borrowing. And so Maharachash siman 52 and Penei Moshe siman 9. Maharachash siman 52 wrote even per Beit Yosef's responsum — namely a seal of the world; but a seal on which he wrote his name and nickname that people do not make seals in others' names — we are not concerned perhaps his name and nickname are so since he was not presumed so — Yoreh De'ah. On the Mechaber's ruling in Shulchan Aruch who ruled on his vessels it does not help even a prominent sign — we are concerned for borrowing. On the body itself need a prominent sign. Need resolve the Gemara Yevamot 120a and Tosafot there; examine and see Beit Shmuel.
A mole. Hold this rule: tall and stout and similar are inferior signs — they do not help even combined with many signs; and if she married on these signs she must leave. Intermediate signs are called prominent signs. It depends whether Torah signs help such signs or rabbinic signs do not help such signs. The conclusion holds signs are rabbinic. If she married on intermediate signs she does not leave — Ri'az in name of Ramah. A mole is an intermediate sign — Mahari Weil siman 63 and Beit Chadash in klal 101, Beit Shmuel.
They testify about him. Radach ruled if he specified and narrowed the place and said on limb Ploni in place Ploni it is a prominent sign. Rashdam siman 52 in responsum Mahari Weil siman 63 disputes him. Beit Yosef wrote in name of Radach if he has a mark on his finger from some illness and his finger remained crooked — the woman is permitted for crookedness in a finger and a mark on its side or on it is a prominent sign. He wrote on this need not say which finger — even if he said stam on a finger it is a prominent sign. Tzlaket wrote in responsum Mahari Weil it is not a prominent sign. Taz wrote if he said stam he has a scar it is not a sign; but if he knows its place precisely — namely on that limb near the head of the limb or at its end or in the middle — it is a sign like a hole on the side of letter Ploni. If the witness testifies about a scar and mark with precise place and does not know under which eye it does not help even though Mahari Weil siman 63 wrote one who had a pierced hole in one tooth and did not know which tooth — prominent sign for the hole itself is a very novel matter and need not state the place. Mahari Weil compares this case to that of the tooth — incorrect — end quote Taz.
Prominent. Prominent signs are such that only one in a thousand would have it — a strange and exceptional thing. A pierced hole in a tooth is a prominent sign. So too a crooked neck — Mahari Weil and Maharam Lublin siman 128. That he said such-and-such souls were there is a prominent sign. Specifically that he said they went this way — responsum Maharival and Maharam siman 77; and above s.k. 59 I wrote Rambam holds number is not a prominent sign. Large lip hair is not a prominent sign — R' Yehudah Aderabi siman 271. One eye dripping is not a prominent sign — Ra'ash in responsum siman 48. A fracture is not a prominent sign — Mekor Baruch siman 12. Tall stature and handsome appearance and curly locks are not a prominent sign — Mahari Weil siman 72. See Maharam Galanti siman 65. Lame on his thigh is not a prominent sign — Maharik shoresh 176. Maharam Lublin siman 56. His legs crooked and turned is not a prominent sign — Maharam Lublin siman 57. He had an eye like a lentil is a prominent sign — Ram Padua siman 20. His fingernails were damaged is a prominent sign — Ram Padua siman 22. A mark from a mole where the mole was uprooted from its place and a mark was made in its place is not a prominent sign — Mahari Weil siman 63 and there siman 65. The middle of his upper lip raised until his upper teeth were visible even with mouth closed is a prominent sign — Maharam of Toledo siman 76; see Maharival ch. 1 siman 8 and Mahari Weil siman 63. His mouth crooked from illness is a prominent sign — Mekor Baruch siman 23.
One hundred signs. In responsum Mahari Weil siman 63 wrote specifically inferior signs do not combine; but intermediate signs combine — through combination it becomes a very prominent sign — and so Taz. But in responsum Ra'ash he ruled even intermediate signs do not combine; see Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Yosef's gloss seif 71, 43a. In Pesakim of Maharai siman 161 he brings one view that signs of his vessels combine with other estimations; but he disputes and holds they do not combine. Mishpetei Shmuel siman 81 wrote it is fitting for a rabbi who says they combine to rely on him in time of distress — and so Choshen Mishpat s.k. 43. If there are signs on his body and on his vessels together they combine — Mahari Weil siman 150 in name of Maharan from a letter found written in name of Sefer Mattat Yoreh De'ah. Maharam Metrani ch. 1 end siman 113 and Ra'anach ch. 1 siman 75. But Ba'al Terumat HaDeshen in his letters siman 161 inclined to be stringent combining body signs with vessel signs; also Maharival ch. 1 klal 1 siman 8 and Rashbam siman 52 and Ram Aderabi siman 272 and Mishpat Tzedek ch. 2 siman 66 agreed body-vessel signs do not combine. Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Yosef's gloss siman 175, 43a brought many poskim who hold they do not combine and concludes in responsum I sided that intermediate signs on body and vessels — all agree they combine; and so Penei Moshe siman 15. See responsum Chakham Tzvi question 134.
On his body. Levush wrote a mark is a prominent sign. Mahari Weil wrote a mark is not a prominent sign. Taz wrote both explanations are true — namely likewise a mark is not a prominent sign; but if he narrows the place it is a prominent sign. And if we say likewise a mark is not a prominent sign — the other explanation if he does not narrow the place it is not a prominent sign. A mark is preferable to a mole for mark and scar are not made to change and are not common in one of his age — fit to rely on this — end quote; see responsum Chut HaShani siman 74.
Large. In Pesakim of Maharai likewise on teeth in the middle row of the upper jaw — implies on other teeth larger than common among most of the world is a prominent sign — Beit Shmuel and Choshen Mishpat.
Incidentally. In Terumat HaDeshen siman 239 he explained the words of R' Azriel who holds incidentally is believed only when he says died or was killed because he need not identifying mark; but I found Ploni killed with signs where there is concern perhaps it is another and needs identifying mark — and every identifying mark needs examination and teviat ayin — this incidentally whose intent is not to testify but speaks spontaneously we do not rely on him; for certainly an Israelite who intends to testify one can say he gave identifying mark intentionally; but incidentally his intent is not to permit — he does not pay attention even through a sign perhaps it does not help if he knew him in life for perhaps he did not examine well and when he saw similar signs he thought certainly it is the man he knew by signs. In responsum Ram Padua he ruled even if he testifies through identifying mark it helps; and so Maharam siman 39 and wrote thus was the practical ruling in Israel. And so responsum Rashak ch. 1 siman 183. And so Maharival; and so Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Yosef's gloss seif 82, 43a and b. If witnesses said we heard witnesses who said they had signs on this killed one — concern perhaps they were inferior signs for people's language calls even inferior signs "signs" — Beit Shmuel. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote in responsum I wrote even per those who are stringent that on witness from witness we do not rely that he recognized by signs — namely it was mentioned in the last witness's words how the first recognized — therefore we do not rely they recognized by signs; but when he explicitly mentions they recognized by signs only he does not know by which signs — whether prominent or not prominent — plainly on witness from witness we rely they recognized by prominent signs — Yoreh De'ah 43a; and so 28a; and he brings there many poskim on witness from witness we rely to be lenient — Yoreh De'ah.
