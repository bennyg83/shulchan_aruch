Provided that they asked him not in the presence of the Jews. Chelkat Mechokek wrote: and therefore the Ran wrote that if the agent asked, etc., and for nothing, etc. Beit Shmuel wrote that it is indeed proven that the Ran disagrees, for certainly there is no concern that the agent asked in the name of a Jew. In my humble opinion, if this is the reason, it is not so. Since the Ran took as obvious that presumably the gentile woman did not tell the gentile, my husband died, without a question, it is likewise presumably clear that it is not the way of a gentile agent coming from afar to ask a gentile woman whom he never saw, is your husband alive or dead, without giving some explanation that leads him to this question. If so, he already had to say that the woman wants a get from him, and Chelkat Mechokek wrote properly. Nevertheless, despite all this, in my humble opinion it appears proven as Beit Yosef understood, that the Ran disagrees, because he generalized that anyone who responds through a question is not considered speaking innocently, and he did not make it conditional on whether he senses that a Jew has a practical interest. This shows that he holds that even if the practical interest belongs to a gentile, whatever it may be, it is considered speech from their mouths, etc., and they believed only actual innocent speech without any question. So too, in my humble opinion, the implication of the Gemara inclines, that it is all called testimony even without any concern of permission for a Jew. Heaven forbid that I should disagree with Terumat HaDeshen z"l, but it seems to me that the Ran disagrees with him. What Beit Shmuel asked against the Rama, who rules leniently, may be answered: for this reason he brings it in the name of some who say, to indicate that there are those who disagree. Nevertheless, where there is great need he joined his view. See Kuntres Agunot of Maharchash, folio 15, which also appears to incline stringently.
