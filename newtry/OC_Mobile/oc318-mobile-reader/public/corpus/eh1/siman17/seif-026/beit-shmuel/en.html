But after three days they do not testify. Because the facial form has changed, and he thinks it is the missing person, while in truth it is possible that the missing person went on his way and died, and this is another man. If he died in the rainy season, in cold days, they may testify about him even after several days, for he certainly froze and congealed and the cold does not allow him to swell; this is even better than the coldness of water. So wrote the Ramban at the end of Yevamot, and so ruled responsum Mishnat Binyamin siman 104.

And similarly if he had a wound. The Tur wrote: we do not testify unless he saw him within three days. In what case? On dry land. But in water, if he has no wound, they testify even if he remained in the water several days, provided they saw him immediately when they brought him up from the water. On dry land, if his face is not wounded, they testify after several days through visual recognition of his body and form. This is difficult: what he wrote, that we do not testify unless it was within three days, must necessarily refer to where there is a wound on his face. If so, how do they testify in water? Responsum Maharshdam siman 54 answered that water does not aggravate a wound unless the wound is on his body and not on his face. According to this, the Tur speaks where he has a wound on his face, and so wrote the Mordechai. However, this does not imply so from the other poskim, and responsum Rashakh and responsum Mishnat Binyamin disagree with this; see Kuntres Agunot siman 174. In Derishah he wrote the opposite: specifically when the wound is on the facial features do we say that water aggravates the wound, but when the wound is on the body and not the face, we do not say it aggravates the wound. The Taz agreed with his words; see Bach. A wound made after death is not said to be aggravated by water; see Kuntres Agunot siman 208. So too wrote Chelkat Mechokek. Even if it was made during his life, if it healed we do not say the wound is aggravated by water, Chelkat Mechokek; responsum Mishnat Binyamin siman 63 did not write so.
Doubt whether he remained there. Even according to those poskim who hold that if there was doubt whether he died within three days we rule leniently, they agree here that we rule stringently, because after the first hour he deteriorates. But with testimony from the mouth of another witness who saw him dead after they raised him from the water, we assume leniently that the first witness saw him within the first hour. So wrote responsum Mahari ben Lev, responsum Maharshdam siman 21, and responsum Mishnat Binyamin siman 45.
But by a distinctive sign, even if he remained there. See Tosafot folio 115 regarding the incident of the two Torah scholars, where they permitted their wives by signs; in one answer they wrote that it was a distinctive sign. In the Gemara it says, and they saw them immediately and stated the signs. This implies that even if one states a distinctive sign, we require that they saw them immediately. One must say that the vav of and they stated the signs is a dividing vav, in place of or. So wrote the Mordechai. Some texts explicitly read: alternatively, they stated signs; but if they stated a distinctive sign, they need not have seen them immediately. See responsum Mizrachi siman 37, which implies that this is specifically where there is no concern that he changed after death. What the Tur marks on this law is not in the Tur; rather, the Beit Yosef wrote this law without proof. In responsum Mizrachi it is explicit like his words.
