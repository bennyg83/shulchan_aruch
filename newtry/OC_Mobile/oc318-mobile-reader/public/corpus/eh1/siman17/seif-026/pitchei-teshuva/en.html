Her mother-in-law. See Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 17, that they said specifically her mother-in-law, not the wife of her father-in-law; see there. What Baer Heitev wrote in the name of Shiltei Gibborim, that males similar to them, such as her father-in-law and her father-in-law's son, etc., is discussed in Beit Meir and Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 21, who write that this is a scribal error and it should read: her mother-in-law's son; see above s.k. 28. So too is it in the Yerushalmi. What Baer Heitev wrote further, "however, the yavam," etc., and the Chelkat Mechokek wrote even if he had sons, etc., see Beit Meir and Machatzit HaShekel, who write that the law is certainly like the Beit Shmuel, and there is no concern in this. Machatzit HaShekel adds that it appears the same applies where he has no sons, but she does not fall to yibbum because she is an ervah to the yavam: he is also believed to permit her to the public, just as where she has sons, for what difference is there? Nor is it relevant to say that the Sages did not distinguish, since they explicitly said he is not believed in a case of yibbum; but where there is no yibbum he is believed, and this is simple. Regarding the Chelkat Mechokek's doubt whether he is believed for chalitzah, he cites Rashdam siman 75, who decided that he is believed, but Machatzit HaShekel questions this. He also writes that the Beit Shmuel's inclination to be lenient nowadays, when yibbum is not practiced, requires study; see all there.
