Gloss: "Or even if he merely drowned, but they did not testify that he remained in the water long enough for his soul to depart." We learned this point from the teaching of Moshe Rabbenu, namely Ramban of blessed memory. In truth, however, it requires study what benefit this waiting accomplishes, for one must still suspect that he emerged within that period at a place beyond the observer's sight; and if so, even if she married she should have to leave, as in the case of waters that have an end where he did not wait. Rather, one must say that emerging from waters that have no end is not common, and therefore she need not leave. Thus one may say that even if he did not wait, if she married she need not leave; waters that have an end are different, for if he did not wait she must leave, since emerging from them is common. Maharchash likewise wondered about this, and this is also implied by Kesef Mishneh at the beginning of his comments; see there. Ramban's proof from the ship that sank in se'if 36 may be pressed aside: perhaps there the case is specifically a ship, where he did not see clearly that this person fell into the water and he may have escaped, and therefore we impose upon him the stringency of assuming life. But where he saw this person fall into the sea, perhaps no waiting is needed, for the reason just stated. One must then press the matter greatly: certainly it is unlikely that he swam underwater to a distance beyond the observer's sight and emerged there, and therefore if she married she need not leave; but at the place of the fall it is not so unlikely that he might surface immediately, and therefore one must wait the above measure. The words of Chelkat Mechokek in subparagraph 61 do not suffice. See Responsa Bach, no. 79.
