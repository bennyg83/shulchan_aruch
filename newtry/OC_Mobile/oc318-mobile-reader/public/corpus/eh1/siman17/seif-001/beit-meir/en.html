Hazakah: a woman does not act brazenly. Beit Yosef brings in the name of Eliyahu Rabba: if it is known through witnesses that she is his wife — this hazakah does not apply; rather it applies specifically when he came from elsewhere and there is no clear testimony that she is his wife but only through hazakah — the later hazakah comes and nullifies the earlier. Beit Yosef rejected this. In my view Tosafot Yevamos also follow this line on 116b s.v. ba-oto she'ah — they wrote: if so why don't we believe her from a migo that she could have said "he divorced me" per Rav Hamnuna even not in his presence — answer, etc. Question: it seems from that version that some explain that view — even not in his presence she is believed when she said "he divorced me"; how can one think thus? We learned: if there are witnesses she was a married woman and she says "I am divorced" — Eliyahu Rabba went this way; specifically if there are witnesses; but against hazakah she is not believed even not in his presence. And likewise when she married and afterward witnesses came — because she married permissibly she is again believed even against witnesses due to agunah. They challenged from R' Yehudah's mishnah of the woman who went — applies even to a husband via hazakah alone without witnesses — due to agunah she is believed; they challenged: let us believe from migo. Nevertheless for law the essence is as Beit Yosef wrote and Tosafot there, as Ran also explained in Gittin — if so there is no proof for Eliyahu Rabba's view; Shulchan Aruch — they do not distinguish.
