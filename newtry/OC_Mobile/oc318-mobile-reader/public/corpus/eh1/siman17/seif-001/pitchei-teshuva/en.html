Kiddushin take effect in her. See Baer Heitev, who wrote in the name of Knesset HaGedolah: if one woman died while under her husband, etc. See Sefer Birkei Yosef, who investigated a similar case regarding a husband who certainly died today and tomorrow came back to life, like the case mentioned in aggadah, see the first chapter of Megillah, where Rabbah rose and slaughtered Rabbi Zeira, and the next day he prayed for mercy and revived him. If, when Rabbi Zeira died, his wife became permitted to the marketplace, and when he came back to life the next day he had to betroth her again, or perhaps the husband's death permits only when he remains dead; but when he was not buried and came back to life through a prophet or saint, it is revealed that this death was not like the death of ordinary people, the first kiddushin did not lapse, kiddushin of any other man would not take effect in her, and when her husband lives he is immediately permitted to her. He wrote that at first glance there is proof from Yerushalmi Gittin, chapter Mi SheAchatzo, halachah 3, regarding one who gives a get from now if I do not come, and he died within the time: what is the law, is she permitted? Rabbi Yose says she is forbidden to marry; I say miracles may have been done for him and he lives. See there. Although the poskim are not concerned at all for this Yerushalmi, see below siman 144 seif 2, that is because we are not concerned in practice for miracles. Nevertheless, we learn that if after he died miracles were done for him and he lived, this wife is still bound to him, and the fact that he certainly died does not help her, since in the end he lived. One must consider the words of this Yerushalmi and the early authorities. These are his words. See Nechalat Tzvi, what he wrote in explanation of his words on this. What Baer Heitev wrote regarding the wife of a Sadducee, etc., see above at the end of siman 4 in the gloss and in the responsa I indicated there.
