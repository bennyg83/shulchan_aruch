Gloss: "Specifically where she was silent." See Chelkat Mechokek, who properly wondered why all the decisors omitted that ruling. He also infers from Rambam that Rambam does not accept it, since in law 20 he wrote the general principle that whenever the single witness on whose testimony she married is like the two who later came and contradicted him, then even if he is unfit for ordinary testimony, for example if he was a rabbinically disqualified robber who is nevertheless valid for testimony permitting a woman, and she married him, and afterward two similar witnesses came and said that the husband did not die, she must leave. But why should this be so? She is contradicting them, and the case is two against two. This requires study. Regarding the husband, see Chelkat Mechokek. What Beit Shmuel writes, that the husband who married the woman is not believed after the marriage, appears intended to resolve Chelkat Mechokek's difficulty, where he wrote "but this is difficult." If so, I do not understand his conclusion, "however, Rambam, etc., does not hold so"; on what basis does he decide this? In truth, the language of Nimukei Yosef can only be explained as Chelkat Mechokek explained it, and the matter requires study.
