But two, even if they did not say, we buried him. Because with two witnesses we do not say that they speak by conjecture. It appears that this refers specifically to two valid witnesses, and so it is explicitly implied by the Rosh, who brought proof from the sugya that one hundred women are like one witness; this implies that if they were like two witnesses, we would not say they speak by conjecture. This is also reasonable, for after the testimony of two witnesses we do not scrutinize further. So wrote responsum Roshakh, part 2 siman 6, and so wrote responsum Tzemach Tzedek there. Likewise, if one witness is before us and speaks in the name of two witnesses, it is as though the two witnesses were before us, and we are not concerned for conjecture. So wrote responsum Maharam and responsum Tzemach Tzedek there.
And I saw him afterward. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that this is the explanation of the words of the Rama, who wrote this law: that witness did not see the killing, but found a killed person and recognized that he was so-and-so; then he need not say, I buried him, since he did not see the killing and therefore is not speaking by conjecture. This is not like the implication of this gloss, which suggests that he did see the killing. However, one can say that it indeed speaks where he saw the killing, and what it says, I saw afterward that he died, means that he had certainly died. This is similar to what is written in seif 17. So wrote Beit Hillel and responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 88.
And some are lenient in this. See Darkei Moshe, who brings several poskim who disagree with this and hold that a gentile is no better than one witness. So too Chelkat Mechokek wrote that in practice one should not rely on these lenient authorities. Above I brought the view of Rabbeinu Simchah and Rabbi Natan, who are stringent regarding one who speaks innocently; these are the Rosh and Ran who are lenient here. The reason is that there the matter concerns recognizing him by visual identification, whether he is so-and-so; in that case one may say that one who speaks innocently does not pay attention to identify him well. Terumat HaDeshen brought their words and was uncertain in their explanation: whether even visual identification together with a distinctive sign is not believed, because the gentile is not precise about whether they are distinctive, or whether they spoke specifically of visual identification together with signs that are not distinctive. In responsum Maharam Padua, s.k. 20 and siman 36, he decided leniently. In any event, with bare visual identification it is obvious that he is not believed. But here he saw that he was killed; the only concern is that he speaks by conjecture, thinking he was killed, and with one who speaks innocently there is no concern that he speaks by conjecture.
