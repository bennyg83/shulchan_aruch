The Beit Shmuel further wrote that the same applies to one who knew, etc. With this reasoning, that knowledge is like sight, one can answer the difficulty of Ba’al HaMilchamot against Ba’al HaMa’or, and it is also the difficulty of Tzemach Tzedek siman 45 against the Rosh: why was the sugya forced to say that they stated identifying signs? Let it say that they were not present at the time of drowning, and therefore visual recognition alone suffices. According to the above, one can say that Rava’s initial assumption in the last chapter of HaIshah was that for a Torah scholar, if he had emerged, word of it would have spread. Although we conclude not like him leniently, stringently we say that for a Torah scholar there is publicity to his drowning, and everyone ordinarily knows.
