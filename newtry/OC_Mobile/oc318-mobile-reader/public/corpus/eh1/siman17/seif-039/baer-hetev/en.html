She leaves. This is Ran's view — namely if she married before the forbidding witness came; for if she married afterward it is explained above she leaves. See responsum Rashdam siman 59 who disputes Ran. Maharachash klal 174 agreed with Ran and so Maharival ch. 3 siman 8's reasoning. What Beit Shmuel challenged on Ran's method — what does the Gemara Yevamot 117b challenge on the Mishnah that teaches one witness says died and she married, etc. — the reason is she married; if she did not marry — no; why not establish the Mishnah she actually married and they did not permit her — see there. No question for we can further infer per the challenger's view who challenged what Ulla said wherever they believed him, etc. and Rashi there d.h. she does not leave from the first permission, etc. — specifically when they permitted her before the second witness came; but if the second witness came before they permitted her it is not yet considered two — see there; if so the challenger decided in his view the Mishnah also deals where they permitted her before the second witness came — difficult whence this to the challenger for Ran's law is not true. Thus the challenger was convinced to himself the Mishnah deals where they permitted her for otherwise why teach if she married she does not leave — it should teach she leaves — unlike Ran's view. Difficult whence this to the challenger that Ran's law is not true. Thus he should have challenged in what are we dealing — if we say they did not permit her — why teach and if she married she does not leave; if they permitted her — why teach she married. Rather certainly the challenger challenged plainly why teach specifically if she married — implies without marrying you find no remedy; but Ulla said, etc.; if so even with permission she may marry; examine. See Kenesset HaGedolah 31b who wrote preferable married without court permission than permitted to marry — see there.

Choshen Mishpat expanded and concluded court is not required — it is like other rulings of prohibition and permission and an individual suffices; also no court needed to accept testimony for witness from witness is valid — only for accepting testimony lest they say from knowledge — namely three needed to accept testimony; but for permitting agunah an individual suffices — see there.
