The Beit Shmuel further wrote: the reason we are stringent regarding a get, etc., is because another can be written. This reason was written by the Baal HaMaor and the Rosh to reconcile the Rif, and at first glance it is also proven from the sugya. For it is clear from the Tosefta that at least testimony to his name, his father's name, and his city suffices, and we are not concerned for two places named Sheviri or for two men named Yosef ben Shimon. So too appears from the plain incident at the end of the tractate concerning Yochanan Aryeh of Kefar Sichon. Yet according to the plain implication of the sugya in Gittin, according to R. Zeira in one version we are concerned even for two places named Sheviri where caravans are common; see siman 132, Beit Shmuel seif 4. If so, the question of Milchamot is certainly difficult: the sugya does not imply, at least, a distinction between a get and death, for if so, why did Abaye bring proof from a get? Rava could have said: even according to me, that I am not concerned regarding death, I am concerned regarding a get. But see siman 132, where I wrote to resolve the Beit Shmuel's wonder in the above seif 4 properly: by Talmudic law there is in truth no distinction, only the Rif z"l took this resolution for himself as practical law. According to the explanation of Milchamot, which is the method of the Rashba and Nimukei Yosef brought by the Beit Shmuel, certainly even his name, his father's name, and the name of his city cannot permit her if he was found in a place where caravans are common, for we are concerned for two Yosef ben Shimons and two places named Sheviri. See the above Kuntres Agunot, how he was forced to answer with difficulty according to them to settle the Tosefta and the above incident of Aryeh. According to them, the incident in Yevamot of the Exilarch must necessarily be that the witness and his name were such that it was known to them that he had left from that specific known Cordova, in a way that there was no room to doubt another Cordova. But on folio 31 he wrote: nevertheless, I have already written that I did not see any posek close to our time mention this, that according to the Ramban, Rashba, and Nimukei Yosef, where he did not specify the city, one must be stringent in every case. End quote. However, where we know of two cities with the same name, this certainly requires analysis before permitting simply through his name, his father's name, and his city. The Beit Shmuel abbreviated here in his citation. He wrote that the reason by a get is that it speaks where it was found not in the place of writing; if it had been found in the place of writing, we would say, here it was found and here it was, and we would not say perhaps it fell from another. It implies that if two Yosef ben Shimons were not established in that city, we are not concerned at all for two Yosef ben Shimons to require checking. This is certainly not so. For if so, what did Rabbah resolve in Gittin from, all acts of bet din should be returned? Is it explained that it speaks of a bet din not from that city? Rather, he abbreviated. Their intent is only to distinguish this: by gittin we are concerned even for two places named Sheviri where caravans are common, even where they were not established, because the get was found not in the place of writing, and the rule, here it was found and here it was, does not apply. Here, by contrast, it was known that Yitzchak the Exilarch left specifically from that Cordova, and the only doubt was perhaps the one missing in Cordova went elsewhere, and this other man who left and died had come to Cordova and left from there. For this we are not concerned because of, here it was found and here it was. But certainly even if a get is found in the place of writing, where caravans are common, we are concerned according to R. Zeira lest it fell from another, even if two Yosef ben Shimons were not known there, until we check that city. If so, Rabbah's proofs from acts of bet din work well: just as there, where no second Yosef ben Shimon was established, we are not concerned and do not require checking, so too with Sheviri, where the only concern was for two places named Sheviri, we do not presume such a place and are not concerned. So too in siman 132 he abbreviated when citing this method; see there. In truth, however, it appears from the plain words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 132 that they did not accept this explanation as primary at all.
