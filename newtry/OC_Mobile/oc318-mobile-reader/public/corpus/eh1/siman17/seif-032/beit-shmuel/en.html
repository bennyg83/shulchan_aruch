Perhaps he emerged elsewhere. For if he is in the water, he cannot live. Only in water that has no end are we concerned that perhaps he emerged elsewhere; but in water that has an end, where they can see all around it that he did not emerge, he is certainly dead. The Taz wrote: an incident occurred with one who walked on the water over the ice, and there was one open place there; he fell into that open place and drowned, and the water had no end. The sages of the generation permitted his wife, and one of them was Rabbi Maharsha. Their reason was that since the water was frozen, its law is like water that has an end.

Since they saw him drown in these garments. This law appears in Tosafot, Yevamot 120 and Gittin 26: if it is known that the get was lost in this vessel, we are not concerned for borrowing. What is written in siman 132, that if the get was found in the vessel in which the get had been, etc., and he knows that he did not lend it, does not speak where it is known that it was lost in this vessel, for in such a case we are not concerned for borrowing. Rather, there it speaks where he was accustomed to place the get in this vessel but does not know that it was lost in this vessel; then he must know that he did not lend it. However, here, if they saw one person drown and he was wearing these garments, and afterward a person was found in the water wearing these garments, one must be concerned that perhaps the first person emerged and lent his garments to another, and that other person drowned. Rather, here it speaks where one person drowned wearing these garments, and immediately afterward another was found wearing these garments; then they testify about him. Levush distinguishes between an outer garment and an inner garment, but his words are not necessary, and the Taz also challenged him.
