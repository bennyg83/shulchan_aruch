And even one hundred signs, etc. The Beit Shmuel wrote: but intermediate signs combine. The Be'er HaGolah brings in the name of the Gaon R. Ozer, who proved with proofs that they combine; it is printed at the end of the Shulchan Aruch. In my humble opinion, his proofs can be answered. His first proof is from a bundle of documents, and he answers the Rosh's question that there, since there are two signs, they are Torah-level signs. According to his words, regarding documents, since the reason that it is satisfactory to the loser of the object does not apply, they could be returned only with two signs, so that it would be a Torah-level sign. But from the words of most poskim in Choshen Mishpat siman 65 the opposite is proven. On the contrary, the stringent view was stringent only where both documents concern one borrower and lender, for the reason he concludes there; see the Shach there. Further, he explains the sugya in a remote way, namely that only according to the initial assumption, that we are not concerned for borrowing, was it difficult to him, because he proved from the Mishnah, which teaches, even though there are signs on his body and his garments, that combining signs also does not help to make them Torah-level. But according to us, we distinguish; and if so we are forced to explain the Mishnah's signs on his body and his garments as one sign on his body and one on his garments, and the garments do not help because we are concerned for borrowing. If the precision is that the word signs specifically implies two signs and not a mere sign, it certainly also implies that even many signs on his body do not help, just as many signs on garments do not help. The main point in my humble opinion is as the Rosh first explained: we see that Rava himself did not rely on the question, and although the reason was hidden from him, nevertheless one may say that signs are rabbinic. He then brings proof from prohibited foods, and finally a third proof from kilayim; in my humble opinion, from there the opposite is proven. For according to his words it is difficult: what did he prove that he holds signs are Torah-level? What implication is there in Rav Chisda's language when he said, examine those that resemble one another, since he did not say this about all features, ears, tails, and voice? On the contrary, since he did not say to check by signs, but rather those that resemble, it implies all the differences between them. Rather, it implies that one should not distinguish. From this it appears to me that this is why the Rambam in chapter 13 of Hilkhot Gezelah ruled that the signs by which we return a lost object are Torah law in every place, to include kilayim and prohibited foods, as the Gemara concludes in Perek HaZeroa, except in gittin and signs of a man to permit his wife, because we see that in Yevamot and Gittin they debated and left it in doubt in order to be stringent. According to this, the language in chapter 7 of Hilkhot Nachalot is precise, unlike the above Gaon's forced explanation, but rather as the Maggid Mishneh explained, which is the truth. So too the Kuntres Agunot of Maharachash agreed. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion, after the view of most poskim has already been decided that very distinctive signs are certainly Torah-level, and the Mishnat Binyamin cited by Beit Shmuel s.k. 72 established the rule that it not be found in one out of a thousand, the matter is given to the sages of the generation. They must judge whether in their view the two signs together also cannot be found in one out of a thousand, even though one of them by itself might be found in an intermediate way; then they may make from this a very distinctive sign. In my humble opinion, this is the peg on which everything depends.
