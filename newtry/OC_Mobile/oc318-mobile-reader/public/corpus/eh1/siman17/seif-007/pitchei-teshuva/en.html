She acts brazenly before him. See Baer Heitev, s.k. 2, who disputes the Beis Shmuel regarding a case where she first said, 'You divorced me,' and afterward he supported her statement. Beit Meir likewise concludes as a matter of law that once she began by saying to him in his presence, 'You divorced me,' she was already permitted, and his later admission does not harm her credibility; see there. However, Baer Heitev's challenge to Beis Shmuel from Gittin 89 is itself difficult. Beis Shmuel certainly does not say that she is not divorced at all, but only that she is not certainly divorced and remains doubtfully divorced. Even where the husband began by saying, 'I divorced you,' she is doubtfully divorced, as stated explicitly below at the beginning of siman 152. All the more so, where she first said in his presence, 'You divorced me,' and he later admitted it, she is at least doubtfully divorced, and if another man betrothed her she requires a get from the second. Accordingly, the Gemara in Gittin could not have established the case in this manner. See there and examine carefully.
