Seif 4 — All are believed to testify for her, etc. Behold in my composition edition 4 siman 21 I brought a safek in the name of another sage — perhaps Chazal did not believe one witness for a husband's death except if he testifies on actual death and not on other details of the matter; and I proved there there is no distinction in the matter; however it appears this is specifically if there is no testimony of witnesses or women and disqualified persons against the presumption of Torah prohibition; or one may say on the contrary it is preferable if they do not testify on actual presumption of Torah prohibition; and in any case it is not worse; therefore since a woman dayika uminsaba examines for presumption of Torah prohibition — Chazal believed them. But if they testify against the actual presumption — such as one came before us and established his name Yosef son of Shimon and married a woman, and afterward wandered and left his wife an agunah, and his wife heard from guests and youths that he changed his name and his name was Reuven Ba'al Milchamah [as in the incident that came before me responsum to Premishla] — and afterward came one witness that Reuven Ba'al Milchamah died — in this certainly she is forbidden, since there is no clear testimony before us that he changed his name, only she says so and she too did not hear from witnesses in court; if so since he was established before us by another name — this testimony that he changed his name is against the presumption; therefore it is like two presumptions of Torah prohibition and presumption that his name was established as Yosef son of Shimon; if so for one presumption the reasoning of dayika has power to harm the presumption; but for two presumptions one presumption that a woman dayika does not have power to diminish both; therefore we say the presumption of dayika is removed for presumption of married woman — still remains presumption that his name was established thus and so; and they are believed only with fit witnesses. Do not add to this; and particularly since this is the beginning of testimony — behold we say in Bekhorot that at the beginning of testimony Chazal were more stringent regarding married woman than generally — all the more we do not lighten here more and require clear testimony on this — so is correct in my humble opinion and there is no place to permit the woman in this; examine.
