A gentile and a Jew went out, etc. According to the Bach, no contextual connection is needed here; rather, we say that he is relating what happened to the one who went out with him. But generally, if he says so-and-so died without contextual connection, we are concerned that his intent is to permit her, for otherwise why did he say this? This distinction can properly be made according to the Tur. But the Rambam necessarily does not accept this distinction, for he wrote that even if he said so-and-so went out with him, there must be contextual connection, as written in seif 14. So too Chelkat Mechokek wrote that according to the Ran in seif 14, here too contextual connection is required.
That he left from here, etc. This implies that if he did not say that he left from here, but only said: the man who left with me died, we say perhaps that Jew who left with him from here went on his way, and another person joined him and died. But the Rosh ruled in a responsum that even in such a case it helps, and we say presumably the man who went with him from here is the one who died. Responsum Mahari ben Lev, responsum Roshakh, and responsum Mishnat Binyamin ruled like the Rosh, and this requires study. The Taz discusses this at length and rules not like the Rosh.
And specifically in such a case, where he does not mention the dead man's name. There are four approaches in this matter. According to the Ritva, one distinguishes: if the gentile takes it upon himself to announce that so-and-so died, then he certainly died and he need not say, I buried him. According to the Ran, the Maggid Mishneh, and the Rivash, if he recognizes him we do not require that he say, I buried him. In responsum Rama siman 101 he wrote that there is a distinction between the Maggid Mishneh and the Ran. According to Rabbi Yosef Migash, only when the gentile was present at the time of his death or killing are we concerned that he speaks by conjecture, and he must say, I buried him. According to responsum Maharam Alashkar, one must distinguish between where he went out with him and where he merely thought he died because he separated from him. But seif 14 requires explanation: why need he not say, I buried him? See responsum Maharam Lublin siman 6. Responsum Rashakh siman 7 wrote that we do not rule like the Rambam, and he need not say, I buried him.
And they are bound in a collar. So wrote the Rambam. This implies that if they are not bound in a collar, we are concerned that perhaps those people went elsewhere, because he did not say that they went out with him, but that they went by themselves; therefore we are concerned that perhaps they went elsewhere. From this we learn that a number is not a sign. In any event, according to the Taz, number and place are not a sign, unlike Mishnat Binyamin. If he identifies the exact place, it is a sign, similar to a hole near such-and-such a letter, Taz. Chelkat Mechokek infers from Rashi that he holds that a number is a sign, since he explained kolar as a group. See more on this below, and see Kuntres Agunot siman 273. Responsum Mishnat Binyamin siman 44 wrote that if a gentile recognizes one of them and testifies that the whole group was killed, we say presumably it was that group.
