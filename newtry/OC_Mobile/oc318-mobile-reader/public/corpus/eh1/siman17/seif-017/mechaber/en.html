A Samaritan and an Israelite went from us to another place and the Samaritan came and said "a man who went from here with me died" — they permit his wife even though the Samaritan does not know that man. And some say he must say "I buried him." {Rama: And specifically in this manner where he does not mention the name of the deceased and does not recognize him; but if he recognizes him we do not require "I buried him" in mesalfei lefi tumo (Magid Mishneh according to Rambam and so too Maharai in his rulings siman 223 and Beit Yosef in the name of the responsum of the Ran and Rivash). And likewise the custom spread — even in a place where some say we require "I buried him" — it does not mean literally "I buried him" but anything he says whose implication is that he certainly died and he did not say in a manner of doubt suffices (Beit Yosef in the name of the responsum of the Ran and Rivash).} And likewise if ten men went together from place to place and they were bound in a collar or carrying camels and the like, and the Samaritan spoke offhandedly and said that ten men who went from such-and-such place to such-and-such place and were carrying such-and-such all died — they permit their wives. And some say he must say "we buried them."
