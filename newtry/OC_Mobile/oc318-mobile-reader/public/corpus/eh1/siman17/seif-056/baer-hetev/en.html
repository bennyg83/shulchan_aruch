Was betrothed. A betrothed man secluded with his betrothed before witnesses — if they saw she was betrothed if she married another without a get she leaves from this and from that and all the ways thereof. If they did not see she was betrothed and her relatives slipped away from completing the matter and he returns to betroth her so his heart is secure — even though we are concerned perhaps he betrothed for betrothal if she married she does not leave — Radbaz ch. 1 siman 216. See Rashak ch. 1 siman 92.
From both. Some ruled in a time when they wish to be explicit they leave them and do not compel with a get; moreover if they recognize he is obligated to divorce it is a coerced get. Radbaz ch. 1 siman 89 disputes and wrote always she leaves with a get. So appears from Tur's words who wrote she leaves from this and from that and needs a get from both. His intent in saying and needs a get from both even though he said she leaves from this and from that — separation alone is not enough but she needs a get from both. Investigate per Radbaz who requires get whether they compel with rods or words; if must compel with rods see why Tur did not count in siman 154 among those compelled to divorce — see Kenesset HaGedolah 32b and Radbaz ch. 1 siman 59 and siman 271.
The first. Namely if the first took her back after the second's divorce she has no ketubah — and implies likewise if the second took her after the first's divorce she also has no ketubah because prohibition of both is equal — as forbidden to husband so forbidden to paramour — Choshen Mishpat, Beit Shmuel.
The worn garments. As a rule her law is as a woman who committed adultery as written siman 115 — see there. Whatever applies to her even if he took her before witnesses they do not remove from her; whatever she lost seizure does not help even not before witnesses — see Nimukei Yosef. Per Rashi she has not even existing worn garments — and so Rashba. Mechaber ruled like majority poskim as Nimukei Yosef wrote. Rashak ch. 2 siman 223 ruled no posek agrees with Rashi's view and even if husband is in possession they remove from his hand and he cannot say kims li like Rashi. But Rashdam siman 115 wavers he can say kims li — see there; Kenesset HaGedolah 9b.
She took. Even if she took not before witnesses — Nimukei Yosef; nevertheless.
The first. Likewise if she became pregnant.
Rabbinically. See Rambam ch. 10 Laws of Forbidden Relations and ch. 15 Laws of Forbidden Unions who did not bring this law only brought if the first returned to her before the second's divorce he is mamzer rabbinically and wrote nothing if the second came upon her after divorce or death of the first. Perforce he holds if the second came upon her after divorce or first's death he is not mamzer even rabbinically; therefore he also did not bring the law if after she married they told her husband was alive and a son born before death is mamzer and born after death is rabbinic mamzer — because he holds after the first died he is not mamzer at all even rabbinically per plain Mishnah unlike Tosafot and Ra'ash. If so Mechaber ruled rabbinic mamzer like Tosafot and Ra'ash — difficult for in the next seif he brought dispute and wrote first view he is not mamzer — behold per Tosafot and Ra'ash he is rabbinic mamzer. Beit Shmuel wrote you omitted Ra'ash's words that there is distinction between husband came or died — see there. Not necessary — one Ra'ash rejected this himself and we find none who hold so; also proved Tosafot do not hold this view for otherwise they prove nothing from the end — see 92a d.h. but the Sages say, etc. Further Shulchan Aruch's way is to bring first the more common view — see siman 1 what Choshen Mishpat wrote s.k. 11 in name of responsum Ma'aseh. If so Beit Shmuel's words are concealed who wrote first view is Ra'ash; second view Tosafot and Ra'ash — not Shulchan Aruch's way; uncertain. See Mi Ba'er on Mishnayot; see Relanach siman 42. Specifically a woman whose husband went abroad and she married by mistake — that is a fine they fined her because she was not precise. But a woman who intentionally married another or committed adultery with him and the first divorced her and she bore from the second — that child is valid and not even rabbinic mamzer — Relanach siman 42, R' Eliyahu Sasson siman 256.
