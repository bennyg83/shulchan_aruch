This one need not leave her permitted status. Beit Shmuel wrote: and that which we require, etc.; they answered, etc. On this basis, in siman 178 he wrote regarding the Shach, who wrote that this is explained in Tosafot; see there. For on the contrary, Tosafot in Sotah also explains according to Rabbeinu Chananel that it is not called one after another unless they ruled in between. In Yevamot and Ketubot they wrote this reasoning only according to Ulla's own answer, for regarding Sotah he holds that even when they come at once we say wherever, etc.; but how will the Mishnah in Yevamot be answered, where it is necessary that they permitted her? They distinguish that since her credibility is only rabbinic, he agrees that we do not say wherever, etc., except where they came one after another. Still, one may say according to our ruling, that in Sotah we rule like Rabbeinu Chananel and also require one after another, that this too means they ruled in between. Yet it is possible to develop the distinction of the answer even according to Rabbeinu Chananel: in a Torah matter, no ruling is needed, while in a rabbinic matter, as long as his testimony was not accepted by a ruling it is considered simultaneous. This rescues Rashi from Tosafot's challenge in Sotah and also settles the implication of the Tur in siman 178. These are the words of the Shach. Know that according to the answer in Yevamot and Ketubot one cannot ask: if there is a distinction between Torah law and rabbinic law in Ulla's rule, what does Yevamot ask, the reason is that she married, etc.; did Ulla not say, perhaps his whole law does not apply to rabbinic matters? This is not so, for if so, why when she married should she not leave? After all, one who has relations with her brings an asham. This is simple, and with this the glosses of Maharsha are also rejected. See below.
