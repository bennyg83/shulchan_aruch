That he is in the city. See Baer Heitev's objection to Beis Shmuel. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 32, likewise disagrees with Beis Shmuel on this point. Nevertheless, he concludes that the Rama's ruling is correct and firm according to Beis Shmuel's explanation: the second witness explains how he knows that the man died, and therefore there is no need to question the first witness even if he is in the city. But if the second merely says that he died and does not explain how the death occurred, then if the first witness is in the city he should certainly be summoned initially, as stated above in seif 5. If he is not in the city, however, there is no need to send to another city for him; see there.
