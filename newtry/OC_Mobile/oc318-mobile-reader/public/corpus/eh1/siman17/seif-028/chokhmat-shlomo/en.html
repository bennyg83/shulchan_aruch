Seif 58 — A woman who was betrothed and erred, etc. Behold from Rama's language that wrote "she must leave from this one and that one" — it implies she requires a get from the second; and in my opinion this law is very uncertain. Behold the source of this law is from chapter HaIshah Rabba — they decreed lest they say the first divorced and the second married; and in truth it is explicit there that it discusses her husband went to a distant land and they came and told her your husband died, etc.; per this one may say specifically in such a case — since she married based on one witness or two witnesses and from presumption they do not come to court to testify and she married based on complete permission of court — here there is concern that even if he comes and they say the first divorced and the second married — they will say the court certainly dayika and would not permit her except because they know the first divorced; but in her error thinking she was not betrothed — since it was not done by court nor by witnesses nor was the matter publicized that she married with permission from the outset — they will err to say the first divorced and the second married; and whence to be stringent here in a rabbinic matter — since the get from the second is only rabbinic; and proof for this directly from what is stated above in this siman seif 1 — "a married woman is among the forbidden relations and kiddushin do not take effect in her," etc.; and behold since afterward he mentioned rabbinic get in the end and in the beginning did not mention thus — it is proven kiddushin do not take effect even rabbinically; perforce it discusses she was betrothed not in her husband's presence — for if in his presence behold he wrote afterward she is betrothed to the second for a woman does not act brazenly, etc.; perforce it discusses not in her husband's presence; and so there is proof from siman 46 seif 5 — "the kiddushin of the second are nothing" proves she does not need a get at all even rabbinically; and why are we not concerned lest the first divorced, etc. — perforce in such a case we are not concerned for this. However these proofs — behold it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch above seif 57 that specifically when she married she needs a get from the second and not when only betrothed; nevertheless although we have no proof — since the Gemara and poskim specify specifically in such a case when her husband went to a distant land and they told her your husband died — and it does not teach when her husband is here and she went and married another — that she needs a get from the second; perforce in such a case she does not need a get from the second; and whence to be stringent in such a case. And even Rama who is stringent — it appears this is specifically in her error since in any case she married with permission and the matter was publicized — there is concern they will say the first divorced since she married with permission; but if she married another wantonly — it appears certainly she does not need a get from the second; and even though she excuses herself now that they told her at first her husband died — she is not believed to compel him to give her a get to be forbidden to her relatives; for what Chazal believed her to say her husband died is because of presumption dayika uminsaba; but since she already married and it is known her husband is alive — it appears she did not dayika; if so on the contrary the presumption of dayika supports the opposite that they did not tell her he died since she married wantonly; therefore she has no power to compel him to give a get — only she is forbidden for she made herself a piece of prohibition; per his words she needs a get from the second rabbinically and he is not obligated to give her a get — only we request from him as the explicit law in siman 48. And behold proof for my words from Yevamot 72 regarding a woman who did not wait after her husband three hours — and we establish it in erroneous kiddushin; and see Rashi there she does not need a get; nevertheless since it is the way of marriage the rabbis did not decree; difficulty: why not establish she married the first husband literally and they told her your husband died and she married another within three hours, or erred and thought she was not betrothed and married another — in this she needs a get from this one and that one; therefore they did not decree since it is rabbinic marriage and requires a get; nevertheless he is not profaned from the second — one may say she became pregnant from first intercourse and is not yet a zona; and he is not profaned, for he is profaned only from those disqualified from kehunah; as stated explicitly in Shulchan Aruch siman 7 seif 14 that one who has relations with forbidden relations in first intercourse is not profaned except from mamzer; therefore he does not defile them. And we say that wherever she does not need a get at all — they properly decreed only where she needs rabbinic get — then they did not decree; for either way from Torah even in promiscuity he is kohen and the rabbis decreed; and rabbinically behold it is marriage — why not answer thus; perforce she does not need a get from the second only when he went to a distant land and not when the husband is here; and when he went to a distant land it is not common that the time would be so close from his departure until the second marriage that there would not be three hours from the day of his departure until the second marriage; and there would be witnesses in the middle who told her he died; therefore it does not establish such a case; and still uncertain in resolving the above difficulty; however for the law it appears as I wrote; and even in this I do not decide — perhaps God will merit me another time to explain more on this; examine well.
