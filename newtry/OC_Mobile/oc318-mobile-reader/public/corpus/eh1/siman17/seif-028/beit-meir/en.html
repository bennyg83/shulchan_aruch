However, in my humble opinion, the main difficulty with inserting this explanation into the Tur is very great because of the plainness of the language. Also, the some-say opinion he brings requires study: to whom does it refer? The above Kuntres Agunot already sensed this, folio 26b. Were I not afraid, I would say a new interpretation: the Tur does not explain the Raavad's gloss according to the Maggid Mishneh's approach, that his concern is only that there not be another established person with this name whose life or death is unknown, for that is obvious, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote, that the Rambam agrees to it. Rather, his view is that even where another person with this same name is established as certainly alive, nevertheless we are concerned: just as one person with this name is established, perhaps there are more with this name whom we do not know, and perhaps that one is the dead man. This reasoning is mentioned in Chiddushei HaRashba to Gittin; I brought his language in siman 132, see there, where he explains Rabbah's reason for requiring two defects: the name is established and caravans are common. Since the Raavad saw that he was concerned for one defect, namely that the name is established, he holds that the entire ruling is not like Rava there but like Rabbi Zeira; and the same applies where caravans are common though the name is not established at all. These are the some-say authorities. Regarding this he wrote that since the Rif states plainly like Rava, that we are not concerned, meaning where caravans are common and the name is not established, so too we are not concerned where the name is established but caravans are not common. But where the established person is doubtful whether he is alive or dead, this is not what he discusses, for why should this woman be permitted more than that one? Yet from Chiddushei HaRashba itself it implies that Rabbi Zeira does not hold this reasoning at all, and the words of the Tur z"l still require study.
