And some distinguish. This is the view of the Rosh, according to how they establish there that they permitted her only through signs and not through visual recognition. He wrote that this is specifically one who saw the drowning, for then he speaks by conjecture that this is the one who drowned. The same applies if he knew that this person drowned: he says that this is the drowned person, as written in Derishah in the name of Maharshal, in responsum Mishnat Binyamin, in Kuntres Agunot siman 246, and in responsum Tzemach Tzedek. Then visual recognition does not help, only visual recognition together with signs and seeing him immediately; this means signs that are not distinctive, for then he must have seen him immediately. But if there is a distinctive sign, he need not see him immediately. One who neither saw nor knew that so-and-so drowned may rely on visual recognition without signs. As to whether we require that he saw him immediately, this is not explicit in the Rosh, and the poskim dispute it, as written nearby. According to this, if two witnesses say that they recognize him by visual recognition, even if they saw the drowning it helps, for with two witnesses we do not say they spoke by conjecture, as written above. So too wrote responsum Maharshdam, unlike the Bach. According to this, the sugya at the end of Yevamot in the incident of that man who drowned in the Tigris, etc., speaks where the witness saw the drowning; therefore they established there that he saw him at once, meaning that he had visual recognition and signs, for otherwise it would not help since he saw the drowning. One must say that there were no distinctive signs there, for otherwise even if he did not see him immediately it would also help, as written above. Below, seif 48, I discussed this at length.

And it is whole. The Rosh did not write this; rather, the Tur wrote it, combining Rabbenu Chananel's answer with the Rosh's answer and ruling stringently in accordance with both. According to Rabbenu Chananel's answer, the sugya concerns a body that is not whole, and therefore we require that it was seen immediately. According to the Rosh's answer, the witness saw the drowning, and that is why the Tur rules stringently: when he saw the drowning, visual recognition is ineffective even if the body is whole; and if he did not see the drowning but the body is not whole, visual recognition is also ineffective. If he did not see the drowning and the body is whole, however, he need not see it immediately. Accordingly, if the body is not whole, one needs non-exceptional identifying marks, visual recognition, and immediate viewing, for according to Rabbenu Chananel that is the case discussed in the sugya's phrase "and they saw them immediately." Rema did not write that one who did not see the drowning may testify even if he did not see the body immediately. This implies that he is stringent in this matter like the Baal HaMaor, who establishes the sugya of the man who drowned in the Tigris as a case in which the witness did not see the drowning, and nevertheless he must see the body immediately. See Mahari ben Lev and Radakh, responsum 21; they dispute the interpretation of the Rosh. According to Mahari ben Lev, the Rosh holds that even one who did not see the drowning must see the body immediately. He writes that perhaps the reason is that the dead body changes and can no longer be recognized; therefore even where the witness did not see the drowning and there is no concern that he speaks by assumption, he must still see it immediately, because later he may be unable to recognize it. On this explanation, even two witnesses must see it immediately. Or perhaps even one who did not see the drowning speaks somewhat by assumption, and therefore he must see it immediately, while with two witnesses there is no such concern. See R. Eliyahu Mizrachi, responsum 39, the responsa She'erit Yosef, Masat Binyamin 45 and 47, and the Chelkat Mechokek, who also wrote a responsum on this matter.
