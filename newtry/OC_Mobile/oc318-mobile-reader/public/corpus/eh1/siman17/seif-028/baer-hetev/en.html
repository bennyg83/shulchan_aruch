The later authorities. Nevertheless his words were not entirely rejected and we combine his words with other views for leniency — Choshen Mishpat, Beit Shmuel.
In water. This gloss refers to all that was said above — if they saw him immediately when raised from water they testify; on this wrote some distinguish one who saw the drowning does not testify unless he saw him immediately and has identifying mark with signs that are not prominent; but with a prominent sign need not see immediately. The reason since he saw the drowning he says in his imagination this is the one who drowned. Likewise if he knew this one drowned he says this is the one who drowned. But one who did not see the drowning or did not know he drowned — if he is whole identifying mark helps without signs. If not whole need signs. Regarding if he did not see the drowning whether he must see him immediately — poskim dispute; see Beit Shmuel. Choshen Mishpat in responsum. Kenesset HaGedolah 28b. Maharival ch. 1 klal 1 siman 1. Rashdam siman 51. Ra'anach siman 51. But all agree one who did not see the drowning and his body whole and there is doubt whether they saw him immediately or not — they do not permit his wife from double doubt if they saw him immediately and if you say they did not see him perhaps the law is as those who say whoever did not see the drowning and his body whole we do not require immediately — and it is reversible doubt — Kenesset HaGedolah 44a in name of Maharachash — see there; and so Sefer Mahari Weil siman 46; and above s.k. 88 I wrote in name of Kenesset HaGedolah in agunah of a woman one may not permit from double doubt — see there. Possibly in reversible double doubt it is different — and so responsum Mahari Levi siman 12 brought in Kuntres Agunot siman 189. And so responsa Devarim Ribbot siman 14 brought in klal 137. If two witnesses say they recognize through identifying mark alone and saw the drowning — whether it helps. Maharar Betzalel siman 21 wrote identifying mark alone helps with two witnesses even if they saw the drowning for with two witnesses "in his imagination" does not apply — and so Rashdam siman 59 and wrote there it is specifically two witnesses testifying before court; but witness testifying witness from witness — no; and specifically when one of the witnesses is a Jew but with two gentiles — no — R' Eliyahu Sasson siman 1; see Kenesset HaGedolah 28b and 47a. That which we say one who saw the drowning we are concerned he says in his imagination — namely where we know certainly he saw the drowning; but where it was not clarified whether he saw the drowning or he does not mention drowning in his words — no concern he saw the drowning and said in his imagination — Tomer Yesharim siman 247, Rashdam siman 51, Maharam Lublin siman 134, Mishpat Tzedek ch. 1 siman 8. If the witness who saw the drowning testified he saw he drowned and emerged outside and died through identifying mark without signs suffices — Ram Metrani ch. 1 siman 303. R' Eliyahu Sasson siman 7. Rivash siman 378 wrote not only saw the drowning but even heard the drowning is considered saw — and so Rashdam siman 51 and Mishpat Tzedek ch. 1 siman 8 and Maharar Betzalel siman 21; but R' Eliyahu Sasson siman 1 wrote all later poskim permit without seeing the drowning even if he only heard the drowning — see Kenesset HaGedolah 44a. Even per those who say heard drowning is considered saw — specifically when he knew truly; but if he knew only from hearing and sound — no Ra'ash siman 56. Witness from witness — no concern the first saw the drowning — Tomer Yesharim siman 247, 248. Mishpat Tzedek ch. 1 siman 8. Maharam Lublin siman 134. If witness from witness testifies they saw him immediately only it was not mentioned in his words — if they recognized through signs there is room to permit from double doubt doubt they recognized through signs and if you say they did not recognize perhaps the law is as those who say immediately need not signs — Kenesset HaGedolah in name of Maharachash 44a — see there and above s.k. 88 and s.k. 93 Beit Shmuel.
