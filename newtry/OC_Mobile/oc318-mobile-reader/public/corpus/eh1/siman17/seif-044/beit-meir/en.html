From the knee and above, we allow his wife to remarry. The first clause is stated in order to infer that at the knee itself we do not allow his wife to remarry. Although the Mishnah also says that from the knee and below we do not allow her to remarry, from which one could infer the opposite, since the inferences contradict one another we must rule stringently. This is not considered two contradictory stringencies, as is evident from what we rule in Yoreh De'ah 55, that if an animal was cut at the knee it is a treifah. So the Rosh z"l answered his son in responsa, klal 20 siman 16, in these words: there is no known decisive ruling here, but because of doubt we act stringently in every place. However, although in Yoreh De'ah we also have a doubt based on the dispute between Rashi and the Rif whether the lower or upper knee is meant, that is only with an animal, for in the Gemara we find that it has two knees; but with a human being there is no doubt. The knee is above the shin, the place between the shin and the thigh bone, as explained in the first chapter of Ohalot, and it is called in the vernacular kenia; a person has only one. What certainly requires explanation is what is called at the knee and what is above the knee. According to what the Shulchan Aruch and Rama adopted, based on Rivach, that within the knee means only between the ankle-joint and the shin, everything below that is from the knee and below; if so, here too above the knee means only within the thigh bone itself. But according to the view of the Shach and Pri Chadash, who accepted as primary the Rosh's explanation in responsum that the ankle-bones are called the knee, and Pri Chadash writes explicitly that according to this the main view is the Rosh's first explanation, so if it was cut between the lower joint and the ankle-bone it is kosher. It is clear that in his view the Mishnah's phrase from the knee and above is as though it said from the ankle-bone and above is treifah, and from the ankle-bone and below is kosher. If so, between the lower joint and the ankle-bone is certainly included in from the knee and below, and consequently cutting in the upper joint between the ankle-bone and the shin is certainly treifah; the doubt is only if it was cut within the ankle-bone. It would then emerge here leniently that if the foot is found with the ankle-bones attached to it, since in any event it was cut above the ankle-bones, we allow his wife to remarry. In my humble opinion this is not so, for two reasons. First, Pri Chadash rejected the words of Mahariva, but in my view they should at least not be rejected. The Shach wrote: in truth it is proven from the Rosh in his rulings that if it was cut within the ankle-bone it is treifah, but he does not explain how this is proven. Pri Chadash explains the Rosh's words: in the first path, where he derived the law from the inferences, he certainly discusses the ankle-bones and calls them the knee, as he does in the responsum; in the second path, where he derives it from the interpretation that the lower bone is called the knee, he is even more stringent, so that even if it was cut within the joint between the lower bone and the ankle-bones it is also treifah. I say that this cannot be attributed to the Rosh's intent. If so, according to the second path the difficulty of Tosafot there certainly applies: what does the Gemara ask from the case where the tzomet hagidin was removed? According to this interpretation, it is explicit in the Mishnah that immediately above the lower bone it is treifah, and there no one holds that there is tzomet hagidin. According to his explanation in the responsum, that he discusses the body of the ankle-bones, one cannot raise this difficulty as Yam Shel Shlomo does. Although it is not reasonable that Ulla holds like the opinion that the ankle-bone itself is the place, but rather like what we rule, the place where the butcher exposes it, meaning from the beginning at the end of the ankle-bone, and although according to the inference from the latter clause we rule that wherever it was cut within the ankle-bone it is treifah, which is below the tzomet hagidin, nevertheless they properly rejected his question, because that is not heard at all from the phrase from the knee and above, which indicates only above the ankle-bone, where the tzomet hagidin is present. Thus the Gemara properly asks: since it taught that if the tzomet hagidin was removed it is treifah, why do I need from the knee and above, as Rashi explained; that is the main question. But according to the second path the difficulty surely applies, and in my view there is no one who can untie it. Necessarily, according to that path the entire ankle-bone area is considered the head of the lower bone, as the Taz wrote in the name of Hagahot Issur VeHeter, and the discussion is only about the connective joint between the ankle-bones and the shin. According to this, it is proven that within the ankle-bone itself he holds it is kosher, for according to that explanation they are called the lower knee like the lower bone itself. If so, if in the first path he followed his way in the responsum and called the ankle-bone the knee, why did he write further to support the main law? On the contrary, according to the first path within the ankle-bone is treifah, while according to the second path it is kosher. Rather, the ruling and his responsum are not identical. In the rulings, in the first path, he called the connective joint between the ankle-bone and the shin the knee, and about it he ruled treifah from the inferences; in the second path too he ruled that same place treifah because he called the lower bone together with the ankle-bones the knee. In any case, according to his rulings, within the ankle-bone itself is kosher. On this Rivach relied, unlike the responsum, because the rulings are primary. Consequently here as well we do not allow the wife to remarry. Second, even according to Pri Chadash's interpretation and the responsum, I am not satisfied. Although the responsum once says that Rav Pappa and Ulla call the knee the small bones between the middle bone and the third, which sounds like Pri Chadash's understanding that the Mishnah's knee is the ankle-bone, this is impossible. First, the proof itself that he brings for this interpretation from Ohalot, which lists five bones in the knee, indicates only that knee is the general name of the place where the five bones lie, not that those bones themselves are called the knee. Further, immediately after that language he writes that anything that bends and kneels is called berekh and knee; thus he calls the entire bending place the knee, not only the bones. He further wrote that according to all views, the joints connecting the bones are called knee. In my humble opinion he means that the whole place between the lower bone and the shin is included in the name knee, with its bones, because that whole place bends and kneels. He came only to exclude what his son had thought, which is the second path in the rulings, where he began by writing: know, my son, that the lower bone is not called the knee. On this he proceeds throughout; but every place above the lower bone until the head of the shin, together with the bones, is called the knee. In all these places there is doubt: the joint below the ankle-bone, the ankle-bone itself, and the joint above the ankle-bone, and because of doubt it is treifah. This is also proven from what he answered his son above, to be stringent here too because of doubt. According to Pri Chadash, this is a remote circumstance, that the leg would be found cut in the middle of the ankle-bone; if the ankle-bone is attached to the foot, she may remarry, and if not, she certainly may not. It is forced to say that he discusses finding a foot with some ankle-bones attached and some missing, and likewise in this very responsum, where he writes regarding the question if the middle bone was separated from the lower bone: this is the doubt whether its legs were cut between the joints, and not merely separated, meaning with skin and flesh covering. For it is no worse than the whole middle bone, where if skin and flesh cover it, it is kosher; but if it was separated and not covered, it is treifah, and that too is because of doubt. According to Pri Chadash one would again have to press that he means it separated in the middle of the ankle-bones, with some attached to the lower bone and some to the upper. This certainly is not the implication of the language. The main interpretation in my humble opinion is as I explained. In my view, even Hagahot Issur VeHeter cited by the Taz, in the language that the connective joint is called the knee, means that the entire place between the lower bone and the shin is called the connective joint, which in Hebrew is perek, not as the Taz understood, specifically the joint between the ankle-bone and the upper bone; this still requires checking in the text of the book, which I cannot do. This is also the intent of the Tur for one who reads his language carefully, unlike the Pri Chadash's pressed explanation; see and examine carefully. If so, with an animal one certainly rules treifah even within the joint between the lower bone and the ankle-bone, based on his words in the responsum. Here, however, we allow the wife to remarry only when the cut is actually within the shin. Do not say that if the foot was found with the ankle-bones attached, then in any event the tzomet hagidin was severed, since we rule at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah 56 like the opinion that the place of the tzomet hagidin is above the ankle-bone, and Rashi explains that this measure is the lowest of them all, and if it was cut between the middle bone and that ankle-bone it is treifah; if so, here we should allow the wife to remarry. This is not so. First, there too we are stringent only because of doubt, as explained in Rashi there, and consequently here we must be stringent in the opposite direction, like the opinion that it is at the thickening and upward. Further, in my poverty I do not know whether in a human being the tzomet hagidin is in that place of the knee, for in an animal it is at the end of the shin bone, which is the lower knee, whereas here we are discussing what in an animal is called the upper knee, namely the end of the thigh bone, as explained above. Know that the language of Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh De'ah there, end of seif 1, where it says: and likewise if it was cut within the joint between the ankle-bone and the shin it is treifah according to this opinion, requires study according to Rashi's explanation, for if it was cut in that place without this reason it is treifah according to all because of severing the tzomet hagidin. What then is meant by according to this opinion? Necessarily, cut is imprecise; it means dislocated, with the tzomet hagidin attached to the ankle-bone and no skin and flesh covering, and he also follows the view of the responsum of the Rosh cited in the gloss there, seif 3. Examine carefully.
