Chelkat Mechokek explains that this means even if he came after she had been permitted to marry; if he came before permission, even a single other witness would prevent her from marrying. Kehillat Yaakov of Lissa disagrees and writes that after the court permitted her, the first witness is certainly not believed to retract. Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Or Zarua says the same, and the reason is that once she has been permitted she has a presumption of permissibility, and one witness is not believed against a presumption. Chelkat Mechokek's question from the case before permission is answered by the consequence that the children would be definite mamzerim rather than doubtful ones. Kehillat Yaakov finds it impossible that a witness could later undo the permission, especially after she remarried, for that would create ruin: the first witness could return after she had children and say he never testified, perhaps out of hatred. Kehillat Yaakov therefore rules that after permission the witness is not believed, but before permission the main law follows Ramban, Ran, Rosh, and other authorities that the first witness is believed; even if the person whose statement she reported is disqualified, his denial is like saying, "I fabricated it," and for that there is no difference between valid and disqualified witnesses. Pitchei Teshuvah finds the first point difficult. Rambam, cited below in seif 38, holds that even one valid witness is believed against a woman after permission, and Ramban and Rashba agree regarding the woman herself, as Maggid Mishneh explains. Maharchash even says that if the witness came after she had actually remarried, she must leave, though he hesitates somewhat. If so, the case here should be no worse than another witness. Pitchei Teshuvah suggests that Chelkat Mechokek read the Shulchan Arukh there according to the simple wording, which equates the woman herself with another woman, but Kehillat Yaakov's decisive ruling is against the later authorities who do not rely leniently on the woman herself. R. Akiva Eiger siman 113 also accepts Chelkat Mechokek's interpretation, questions it from Rabbenu Yerucham, and discusses Ramban and Shiltei Gibborim at length.
