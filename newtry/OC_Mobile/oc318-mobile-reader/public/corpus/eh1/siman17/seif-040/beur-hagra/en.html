One witness says, etc. This is the Mechaber's language, learned from Responsa HaRosh, klal 51, siman 7. Two witnesses testified that her husband died; one said that his son attended to his burial and buried him, while the other said that the son was not at his father's grave but merely wrote to him that his father had died. The Rosh answered that since both testify to his death, we are not concerned for contradiction in peripheral details, as stated in Yevamos 117, where one woman says he died and another, etc., and we rule like Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon. In truth, this is a grave error, for the Mishnah proves the opposite. Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon's reason is that a rival wife is not believed concerning the other, as written below in se'if 46. The Gemara asks, 'Let Rabbi Meir disagree,' etc., and Rabbi Elazar answers that it depends on the dispute, etc. It is clear that their disagreement concerns whether a rival wife may testify for the other. Some mistaken student wrote otherwise, and one who heard it misunderstood. The Rosh permitted there for a different reason, because the witnesses contradicted one another only in examination details, as written above in se'if katan 1. But where they contradict one another in essential inquiry, their testimony is void, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 30, se'if 2. The Shiltei Giborim explicitly ruled that in such a case they are not believed. The Beis Shmuel wrote that this accords with Rabbi Yoel in Tosafos there, s.v. 'for all,' etc., who wrote, 'but this one says,' etc., 'since both,' etc. His words are astonishing. Tosafos wrote that only according to Rabbi Meir, for according to Rabbi Elazar, Rabbi Meir holds that in every case the rival wife contradicts them, whereas Rabbi Yochanan holds that only in such a case. According to Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon, however, her contradiction has no effect in any case. The Yerushalmi states this explicitly: Rabbi Elazar said that Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon agree regarding witnesses. What difference is there between witnesses and a rival wife? They treated the words of a rival wife concerning the other as nothing. Rabbi Yochanan said: Who said this? Rabbi Elazar, because he heard and stated it. See the passage there. Rabbi Yochanan says that Rabbi Meir agrees in the first case. Another teaching was found: it too is subject to dispute. What difference is there between the second and first cases? They treated this one's words concerning the other as nothing. Rabbi Elazar said that Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon agree regarding witnesses, as above. This means that according to Rabbi Meir, only in this case are her words disregarded, whereas according to Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon they are always disregarded. Rabbi Abba and Rabbi Yehuda both hold that Rabbi Meir agrees in the first case, and both hold that Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon agree in the case of witnesses, unlike the Beis Shmuel.
