He further wrote: according to this, the sugya at the end of Yevamot speaks, etc., and that is where he had visual recognition and signs; otherwise it would not help, since they saw the drowning. This is also already explained in Kuntres Agunot of Maharachash. Nevertheless this is very forced, for if so one must say that the Gemara understood that the latter clause, which teaches that they testify only within three days, returns to the opening clause. What the opening clause teaches, that they testify only by the facial form together with the nose, even if there are signs, this testimony too is not accepted, and even with signs only within three days; for otherwise nothing is implied, since perhaps recognition by signs is stronger and helps even after three days. If so, the middle clause, which teaches although there are signs, should properly have appeared after the clause that they testify only within three days.
