Empty and reckless people. See Taz, Choshen Mishpat, siman 34, seif 25, who questions why the poskim omitted here, regarding an agunah, the law stated in Sanhedrin 26 that one suspected of sexual immorality is disqualified from testimony concerning a woman. Chelkas Mechokek at the end of siman 42 infers from the Rama's wording there, 'he is disqualified from testimony concerning a woman, whether for kiddushin or divorce,' that he is disqualified specifically from testimony about kiddushin and divorce, but remains valid to testify that a woman's husband died, since he is no worse than a slave or maidservant. Rashi, however, explains there that the disqualification excludes testimony that her husband died. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 29, writes that the Rama's wording there is not conclusive because that siman does not address agunah testimony. Nevertheless, the omission of this law by the poskim in the present siman supports the view that he is valid in an agunah case. He explains this at length and shows that Rashi's words are not a proof to the contrary; see there.
