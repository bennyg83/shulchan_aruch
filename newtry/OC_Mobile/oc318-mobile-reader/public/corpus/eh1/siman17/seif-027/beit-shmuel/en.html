However, if a report had circulated, etc. The Bayit Chadash and Chelkat Mechokek explain that this refers to the first opinion, which permits her. One should not say that this case is worse than one in which the report came out before three days; the ruling teaches that the report itself does not worsen the matter. Either way: if the report is true, he is dead; and if the report is false, it is as though no report had emerged. Still, the statement that she is permitted according to all opinions is not well settled. The Taz writes that the author decided this on his own reasoning: since a report also circulated, she is therefore permitted according to all. One should not object from Maharam Mintz, who writes that if one does not know when his relative died, we presume that he died recently, because we place him in the status of being alive, as stated in Yoreh De'ah 397. Why then do we not say here that he was killed within three days? The Taz raises this difficulty there. It may be answered that here Reuven went away from this place and we do not know where he is. We therefore leave Reuven in his presumption of life, and say that this dead man, although he appeared to them to be Reuven, had died more than three days earlier and changed, and is some other unknown person. The presumption of life does not attach to that unknown body, while Reuven remains alive. If the facial form is whole and uninjured, there is room to permit, and in such a case one combines Rabbenu Tam's view when there is doubt whether the identification was within three days. It is possible that even the stringent authorities agree in this case; see Maharshdam, responsum 56. Likewise, if the body is whole and there is doubt whether it remained after being brought up from the water, one may permit on the basis of a double doubt: perhaps it was within the first hour, and even if it was after the first hour, perhaps the law follows Rabbenu Tam, the Rosh, and the Tur, who hold that if the witness did not see the drowning he may testify even after several days. See Knesset Yechezkel, Rashakh, part 1, responsum 182, and Masat Binyamin, responsum 45.
