See Ba'er Heitev and Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 18-20, who defends Chelkat Mechokek both regarding the first rule, where she is not fit for levirate marriage, and the second rule, in our countries where levirate marriage is not practiced. He concludes this as practical law, against Beit Shmuel, Avodat HaGershuni siman 67, and Tzemach Tzedek siman 88. Responsa R. Akiva Eiger siman 86 also upholds Chelkat Mechokek, and Berit Avraham and Chemdat Shlomo discuss the same matter. Machatzit HaShekel cites Tzemach Tzedek, also brought by Beit Shmuel below in siman 158, that even according to Rif, Rambam, and Shulchan Arukh, who hold that a yevamah is believed to testify only where she is not fit for levirate marriage, the reason is that there is no hatred. Where hatred exists, she is not believed even if she cannot actually fall to levirate marriage. He then considers cases of a mother-in-law, a daughter of the mother-in-law, and other relatives after the concrete basis for hatred has ceased. If the woman married the son during the father's lifetime, then even after the father died without leaving assets, the mother-in-law may already have fixed hostility in her heart because she once feared that the daughter-in-law would consume her son's wealth. If, however, the son married her only after the father's death and the father died poor, there is no apparent cause for hatred, and similarly with the daughter of the mother-in-law where her brother married only after both parents died and inherited nothing. Still, perhaps the Sages made no distinctions within these categories. Such reasoning may support stringency, but not leniency without care.
