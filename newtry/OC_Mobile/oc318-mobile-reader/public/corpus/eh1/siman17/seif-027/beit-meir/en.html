One came and said to us, etc. One should not say that perhaps another Yitzchak ben Mikhael died. The Rambam, as cited in the Tur, writes that some say this is specifically where no other person with that name was established in that place, and caravans were not commonly passing there; but if either defect exists, we do not permit the wife to remarry. Rav Alfasi, however, writes that even if two people with that name are common there, or caravans are common, we permit her until both defects are present. This is also the conclusion of my father and master. See the Beit Shmuel. The Taz's explanation brought there has support in the sugya, as is explained at length in Knesset HaGedolah of Maharach Shash. The Beit Shmuel asks why we are not concerned that the second person, who went by another road, returned and went to this city, as is written in siman 132, where we are concerned that perhaps he came on a flying camel. He means the ruling there in section 4, "even if witnesses testify," etc.; and in the sugya one reason is perhaps he came by camel. He presses the matter greatly and finally writes that Rava's language on 116 requires settlement, meaning that Rava himself agrees with Rabbah even regarding a get that two defects are required. Why, then, did he fear that perhaps the other came by camel? In my humble opinion, according to the main reason in the sugya, that perhaps words are missing, as Tosafot say at the outset, there is no difficulty at all and no comparison. There the concern exists even without knowing that he came at all, and therefore there is no difficulty for the Shulchan Aruch. The difficulty is only from the sugya according to the reason that perhaps he came by camel. Even this, in my humble opinion, may not be difficult, as is explained here in the Milchamot: Rava says that even according to me, who say "here found, here it was," and that I ought to say that this person did not leave Nehardea, nevertheless, since the other man was from Nehardea and came and wrote a get in Sura, we are concerned also for the one who was in Nehardea that day. Thus there the reason we fear his arrival is that in any event the writer must have come from Nehardea to Sura, so perhaps the second also came. Here, however, one person certainly went to Aspamia, the place where the dead man was found, while the second is only doubtful. One may say that this resembles Tosafot in Gittin, s.v. "kan," who write that the case is where we know that the get fell from this Yosef ben Shimon; we do not fear that another also lost one here when caravans are not common. Although Rava himself agrees with Rabbah and fears perhaps he came by camel, there the case is different, as explained. Nevertheless it is not fully compelled that our case is like Tosafot's answer, for there it is known that this person's get fell in that place, and if we are to suspect the other established person we must assume both that he came and that a get also fell from him. Here, by contrast, the only concern is that he too came there. Perhaps, however, one may still say that the disappearance of this man from the world is comparable to the fall of the get. Since it is known that one person was in Aspamia and his trace was lost from Aspamia onward, we do not suspect that the second also came to Aspamia and that his trace was likewise lost from there.
