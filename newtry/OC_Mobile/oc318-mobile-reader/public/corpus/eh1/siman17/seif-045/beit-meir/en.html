One witness said, etc.; if he said, I buried him. From this the Maggid Mishneh inferred that the Rambam does not agree with the Rif to require, I buried him, even with two witnesses. But it is difficult for me according to how the Kesef Mishneh and the Chelkat Mechokek explain that what he wrote, or he drowned in the great sea and died, means that he remained there long enough for his soul to depart. First, why did he change the language, writing here, and died, whereas in seif 34 he wrote, and did not emerge? Further, regarding seif 34 one can say that he mentioned one witness as a novelty, that even so she may not marry ab initio; but here, how can he include it together with war, or he drowned in the great sea and died? Regarding war, he precisely says one witness to teach that with two witnesses, I buried him is not required. But regarding drowning in the great sea, if two witnesses said he drowned and died, which according to their explanation means he remained there, would she be permitted? Therefore it appears to me that the word died means: I saw him after he came up, lying dead. With two witnesses she is permitted; but with one witness there is concern that he is testifying about something whose majority ends in death, yet perhaps he was alive and the water confused him. This is like war, where he must say, I buried him; that is, according to the Rivash's explanation, they brought him up before us, which is like I buried him, or actual burial. If he says, I buried him, he is believed. For the poskim who are not concerned for approximation through mistaken substitution, but only for approximation whether he died or did not die, which is the method of the Mordekhai in the name of Ri and of Yam Shel Shlomo, here if they saw him immediately after he was brought up, so that there is no longer concern for swelling, then saying I buried him suffices to remove the concern that perhaps he did not die. According to the Rosh's method, however, here where he also testifies about the drowning and knows of it, the recognition must be joined with an intermediate sign. So too according to his method, even in war with one witness, even within three days, if he says, I buried him, based on recognition, saying that he recognized him after the war, signs must be joined because of the concern that perhaps he speaks by approximation: since he relies on himself that all those others were killed and he escaped, he says by approximation, this one who was in the war is the one found dead. So it is explicit in Shiltei Gibborim at the beginning of the chapter, and it is in Darkei Moshe at the end of s.k. 38 regarding a woman who testifies about her husband in war; he learned war from waters that have no end. The same applies according to the Rosh's method regarding one witness. As for what Shiltei Gibborim there on the second side rules in this language, and likewise if the witness testified that her husband drowned in water and they brought him up from there dead, and the witness recognized him, he is believed, even though he did not recognize him by signs but by visual recognition, for the witness does not speak in error, that is because he rules like the Rif, Ramban, and Yam Shel Shlomo, that with one witness the question was resolved that if he said, I buried him, he is believed. But according to the method of the Baal HaMaor and the Rosh, where if he knew of the drowning, signs are also required with one witness, the same applies in war. The words of the Chelkat Mechokek are brief, and the words of the Beit Shmuel require study. He wrote: if he drowned in water, etc., he must have a sign in him, which implies that he wishes to exclude war, where I buried him alone suffices. This is not so, for war and water have one law, as explained in Shiltei Gibborim and as appears to one who examines the sugya. Also, what he wrote about waters that have no end is not precise, for the same applies to waters that have an end where he did not wait long enough for his soul to depart; according to the Rosh's method, he too must reach solid recognition with an intermediate sign, because of concern for approximation through mistaken substitution, and to I buried him because of concern for approximation that perhaps he did not die.
