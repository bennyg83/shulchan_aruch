They found, etc. This is a dispute in the Yerushalmi cited by the Rif and Rosh. They rule like the one who permits, for the Babylonian Gemara in the chapter Mi SheAchazo agrees with him, as written in se'if 12 by the Ramban, Rashba, and other poskim. See the Beis Shmuel, who wrote that the Yerushalmi gives no basis to rule like the lenient view from that Gemara, because the Yerushalmi's dispute concerns a written memorandum, whereas Gittin concerns actual testimony. In truth, the one who permits allows even a written memorandum, deriving proof from 'from their mouths and not from their writing.' There it necessarily concerns a memorandum or similar document, as Tosafos wrote in Yevamos 31b, s.v. 'that they saw': it is difficult, etc.; and further, there, etc. The same applies to testimony, for beis din collects upon a document without further testimony. The one who prohibits, however, prohibits even a fully valid document and is more stringent here than in monetary law. The Yerushalmi says: The power of witnesses is greater than the power of a document, and the power of a document is greater than the power of witnesses. Witnesses who say, 'So-and-so died' or 'So-and-so was killed' permit his wife to marry; if they found written in a document that he died or was killed, they do not permit her. The power of a document is greater because one who lends with witnesses collects only from unencumbered property, but with a document he collects even from encumbered property. This necessarily refers to a complete document. It discusses permitting marriage first and does not say regarding monetary law that one may not collect from their writing. We, however, say in that chapter that testimony concerning a woman's husband's death is treated more leniently and is accepted in writing, necessarily unlike that opinion. The Beis Shmuel objected that the Rambam follows his own view that in Gittin the writing of one who became mute is effective, while the other poskim disagree, as written above in siman 120, se'if 5. This is imprecise, for the Gemara explicitly states that writing is effective for testimony of death. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'it teaches,' who write that even according to one who rejects Rav's rule, writing may work for other testimony. Although we do not rule like their broader view, no one disputes this case.
