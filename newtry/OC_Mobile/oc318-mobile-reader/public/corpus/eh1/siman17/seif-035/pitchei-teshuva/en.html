And I saw that he died, they question him. Birkei Yosef, letter 10, cites R. Moshe ibn Chaviv in Ezras Nashim, s.k. 54: It appears that if one merely says, 'So-and-so died,' and does not add, 'I saw that he died,' he is not questioned. We assume that his statement that so-and-so died means that he heard it from another, and it is as though he said, 'I heard that so-and-so died,' in which case no questioning is required. Therefore, the Rambam required questioning only of one who says, 'So-and-so died, and I saw that he died.' This appears correct as a matter of study, but not for practical ruling. End quote. Birkei Yosef questions this. The Rambam and Mechaber mention, 'I saw that he died,' as an even greater novelty, teaching that questioning is required even where he speaks decisively and clearly. He concludes that even when one merely says, 'So-and-so died,' he must be questioned; see there.
