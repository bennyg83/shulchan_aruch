There is one who says, etc. So wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot of Ketubot. In responsum Rashba it appears that if she married she collects her ketubah, and it is clear there that he speaks where she married on the basis of one witness. Therefore he wrote there that the orphan cannot enter into his property, because one enters an inheritance only on the basis of two witnesses. This implies specifically if she already married, but if she has not yet married, no. What is implied in Hagahot Maimoniyot, that even if she has not yet married she collects the ketubah, may be explained where two witnesses testified that he drowned in water that has no end. In that case, by law she is permitted, and only a rabbinic stringency forbids her to marry; therefore she collects the ketubah. But if there is only one witness, then by law she is forbidden, except that regarding an agunah the Sages were lenient and gave credibility to one witness; in water that has no end they were not lenient, and she cannot extract the ketubah. So too wrote Chelkat Mechokek. According to this, the one who says view brought here is not from the words of Hagahot Maimoniyot and responsum Rashba as found in the small Shulchan Aruch editions, and Baer Heitev understood it so, but this is not correct. The Rambam and Raavad disagree with this law and hold that she does not collect the ketubah, since she is forbidden to marry. See Maggid Mishneh, chapter 6 of Hilkhot Nachalah, Bach in siman 284, and below siman 160 and in this siman seif 43.
