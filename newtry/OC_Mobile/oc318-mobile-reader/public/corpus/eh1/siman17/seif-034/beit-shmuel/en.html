She need not leave. This is not specifically when he drowned in water that has no end; the same applies in every place where, according to the majority, he is dead: she need not leave if she married on the basis of a sage’s ruling or by mistake. But in those cases taught in the mishnah, they do not testify, and if she married she must leave. Likewise, in the three cases of R. Elazar ben Parta she must leave, and likewise if he fell into a pit of lions she must leave. Rivash siman 379. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ri"ו, who wrote that in all those cases taught as they do not testify, one who rules permissively is excommunicated even where she need not leave; this does not sound like the Rivash, for according to the Rivash in all those cases she must leave.

Unless he testified explicitly. The Taz wrote that here testimony from one witness by way of another does not help unless he said explicitly how he drowned. Although in several matters we are lenient with testimony heard from another witness, that is specifically in matters where the wording of the witness indicates that death is more likely than life. But if he merely says, he drowned, there is more reason to say that he drowned in water that has no end, since most who drown are in water that has no end. But if he says explicitly that it was water that has an end, only that he speaks generally and does not say that he truly drowned, and perhaps he did not remain until his soul departed, then with testimony from one witness by way of another we follow the lenient view. If he testified that he saw so-and-so drown and remained there long enough for his soul to depart, but there is doubt whether it was water that has an end or water that has no end, it appears that we follow the lenient view, for even with water that has no end, if she married she need not leave; therefore this is only a rabbinic doubt. End quote.
In water that has no end, we excommunicate him. At first glance this is difficult: what did this gloss add? Ri"ו wrote in the name of the Ramah that in every place we excommunicate him, even where she need not leave; and the Rivash holds that in every place she must leave, and he also holds that we excommunicate him. Why then did he write specifically regarding water that has no end, implying that in other places we do not excommunicate him? We have not found anyone who holds so. We do not excommunicate him if he erred in his ruling and says that he erred and retracts, as written in the Gemara in the incident of Rav Sheila, and so wrote Chelkat Mechokek.
