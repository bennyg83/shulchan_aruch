Gloss: until they know that he died. The Beit Shmuel wrote: if they say that her husband had been a goses for three days, we say that he certainly died; so wrote the Rosh, the Mordekhai, and the Agudah. See Yoreh De'ah siman 339. He repeated the matter at the end of s.k. 94 in a somewhat different style, because he saw there contradictions to this view of his, and wrote there: if he is a goses for three days, witnesses may testify about him, as written in Yoreh De'ah, and so wrote the Beit Hillel. End quote. This is the language of the Rosh in Perek Ve'elu Megalchin: it is taught in Evel Rabbati, a goses is like a living person for all matters. There was an incident involving a woman who was four days' travel away from her husband, and they came and told her, we left your husband as a goses. Rabbi Meir ruled that she should observe mourning, and brought proof from what we say in Perek Kol HaGet: but not a goses, because most people who are goses die. And that which was said in Shevuot, what does the Torah teach us by saying that most who are goses die? It teaches us that although most, etc., nevertheless when the goses is before us he is like a living person; but when he is not before us and they left him as a goses, we assume that he has already died. If so, in this case most people who are goses do not live three or four days. End quote. It is explicit that he ruled only on the basis of majority. If so, to permit a woman to remarry, it is obvious that this cannot be done by majority, as explained in seif 5. Accordingly, in the actual case of Maharam z"l, it is clear to me that he did not permit her to remarry. The Bach had difficulty from what is explained in seif 5 in the gloss, that wherever she is forbidden to remarry she is also forbidden to observe mourning, and he did not answer it. In my humble opinion, this must be because the distance was no more than four days' travel, and without doubt caravans were common; it was clear to Maharam that within the time relevant to permitting a woman to remarry it would be clarified whether he died or recovered, and he was not concerned at all that the doubt would remain and cause a stumbling block. But if it were in a place where caravans were not common, and it were possible that the doubt would remain forever, he certainly would have forbidden her to mourn as well. Certainly also, if he were only one or two days away and they reported that they had left him as a goses for one hour, so that only two days had passed from the beginning of the goses state, he would not have ruled that she should immediately mourn on the basis that most who are goses die, since perhaps he is still a goses until after three days of being a goses. However, the sugya in Perek Kohen Gadol does not imply like me in this point. At that stage he judged that most who are goses do not live three days, and most die; but the minority may remain goses for four or five days, some die afterward, and some even recover from this illness. In any case, we clearly hear from this that one cannot derive from his ruling any permission for testimony regarding a woman. All the more so regarding what the Beit Shmuel wrote in s.k. 94, that if he is a goses for three days they may testify about him, whose implication is that the witness testifies that he saw him as a goses for three days and left him as a goses. If so, he has left the majority according to Maharam, who wrote that most who are goses do not live three days; one may still say that he is from the minority and recovered from this illness. Tosafot in Yevamot 120b, s.v. lemeimra, and 121a, s.v. velo hi, decided that we do not testify concerning a goses. They even pressed themselves in their question and did not establish that which was taught, and even a goses, whose implication is that he conveys impurity but is not alive, as referring to a goses of three days. This proves that there is no distinction at all. Although the Rama z"l in Yoreh De'ah added the language that he certainly died, this is necessarily imprecise. For if not, we would infer that in the case mentioned there, where they left him as a goses for one hour and three days have now passed, his wife may remarry; Heaven forbid to say so, as is explained in the above Tosafot on the basis of an explicit Tosefta. Rather, because there was a difficulty: since we rule that in mourning we follow the lenient view, and we do not mourn for a child who dies within thirty days because we are concerned for the minority of nonviable infants, here too let us be concerned for the minority and not let her mourn. Therefore he wrote that he certainly died, meaning that this is an uncommon minority and we do not concern ourselves for it even according to Rabbi Akiva. But in testimony to permit a woman we are stringent because of the severity of forbidden relations, as Tosafot wrote in Yevamot 36b. Nonviable infants, by contrast, may be considered a common minority. One may also say, as I wrote, that in Maharam's case there was no concern for an ervah stumbling block. Tosafot in Yevamot 109, s.v. mechuverta, wrote that what we rule regarding nonviable infants, not to mourn, is because of concern for the stumbling block of chalitzah; if so, in our case, where there was no concern for a stumbling block, he ruled to mourn. However it may be, the Beit Shmuel's words in this matter are not halakhah in my humble opinion, and one cannot learn anything at all from Yoreh De'ah. The Beit Hillel, whom he brings as support, is actually a help against him, and this is astonishing. I later saw that Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek Kohen Gadol, siman 8, also learned from Maharam's ruling to permit a woman based on testimony that they left him as a goses. It seems to me that all the above words of Tosafot were then hidden from his honor's eyes. In Kuntres Iggun of Maharachash he raised many difficulties against the very ruling concerning mourning; see there, folio 56. What emerges from his words is that with a goses, even if she married, she must leave. It never entered my mind to learn from there as the Beit Shmuel distinguished between a goses for three days and a goses for one hour. However, from the above Tosafot, and likewise from Bekhorot 20b, s.v. chalav, it appears in my humble opinion not like Maharachash z"l in this point; rather, its law is like water that has no end regarding if she married. But to marry initially, it is clear that she is forbidden in every case.
