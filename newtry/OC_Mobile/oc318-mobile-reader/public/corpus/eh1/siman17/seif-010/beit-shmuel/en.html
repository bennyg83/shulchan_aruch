For we are concerned that perhaps it is a demon. It does not help if they saw the shadow of his shadow nowadays, as the Gaon brought by the Tur wrote, for nowadays we are not expert in the shadow of his shadow. At first glance it is difficult: why did he write in siman 141 seif 19 that one who was thrown into a pit and said, whoever hears my voice should write a get for my wife, they write it even though they do not recognize him; and some say that this is only where they saw a human form and the shadow of his shadow? Here he ruled that the shadow of his shadow does not help. Perhaps there, the body is lying before us and we see that it is a person; the only concern is perhaps the one who cried out to write the get was a demon and left, while this body belongs to someone else. Therefore we are not concerned if they saw the shadow of his shadow. But here, where there is only a voice saying, so-and-so died, we do not rely on the shadow of his shadow according to anyone. This settles the Rambam well: he did not bring this sugya, if they heard a voice saying so-and-so died. The law of one thrown in a pit who said, whoever hears my voice should write a get, he wrote in chapter 2 halachah 3, and the law of one standing at a distance he wrote at the end of halachah 3. Beit Yosef wrote that the reason he did not bring the sugya of if they heard a voice, etc., is because he rules like the Gaon. His words are surprising, for according to the Gaon the shadow of his shadow does not help; how then does he permit where he was thrown into a pit?
