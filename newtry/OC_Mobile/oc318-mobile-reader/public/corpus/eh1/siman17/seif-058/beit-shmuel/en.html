Rather, she was only betrothed. The rule is that where she married and thereby committed a prohibition, they penalized her so that she is forbidden to the first husband. According to the Rif this is only a penalty, while according to the Rosh it is not only a penalty; rather, because of the penalty we are concerned that people will say that the first husband divorced her and she married the second. But with kiddushin, where she committed no prohibition, they did not penalize her and we are not concerned that people will say this; rather, they will say it was mistaken kiddushin. A practical difference from this dispute is the law written in siman 159, as written in siman 15. If she secluded herself after the kiddushin, it is like marriage, all the more so if she was betrothed as a concubine, where only delivery to chuppah is lacking. See the responsum of Rashba brought by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 67. If the betrother gave her a get, she is forbidden to the first husband, for now people will say that the get was valid and the first husband is taking back his divorcee after betrothal; responsum Rashakh, part 1, siman 132. Responsum Maharshdam siman 62 disagrees with this.

Or the court ruled for her in error. Responsum Rashba learned this law from Michal, wife of David, who was permitted to David after her marriage to Palti because Saul’s court erred and ruled that where there is a loan and a perutah, his intent is for the loan. One should not challenge this from the sugya in Perek HaIshah Rabbah, where Rav Nachman thought that where she married on the basis of one witness, it is a ruling in error, and nevertheless she must leave both men. One can say that there she caused it by not investigating properly. However, it is difficult from what we hold, that if she married on the basis of two witnesses she is forbidden to her husband; there is no greater compulsion than this, and nevertheless she is forbidden to her husband. The Taz discusses this at length. See what I wrote in siman 31 in the name of Maharik.
