See Baer Heitev and Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 43, who discusses Maharai's words at length and concludes that Maharai too does not require fully incidental speech with all its conditions, meaning that the child must begin speaking spontaneously without being asked, as required below in seif 15. Rather, whenever the child gives an example and a reason for the matter in the manner of recounting an event, it is presumed to be incidental speech. He cites Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 14, who likewise holds that whenever a child relates the matter as a narrative, even if it is not fully incidental because it was elicited by a question, it is nevertheless presumed to be incidental speech unless it is evident that the child intends to testify. This resolves the Rama's omission of an explicit requirement of incidental speech, because in the Shulchan Aruch's case the children say, 'We have just come,' etc., which everyone considers incidental speech. In the addendum there he cites Maharach Shach that this law is disputed, and proves from Shiltei Gibborim on Kesubos 28 that a minor is believed in an agunah case even when he intends to testify; see there.
