A woman does not act brazenly. See the glosses of Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchos Ishus, halacha 13. He cites Ezras Nashim, who was uncertain about a betrothed woman whose father accepted kiddushin for her while she was a minor, and who, after reaching adulthood, extended her hand and accepted kiddushin from another man in the presence of her first betrothed. Perhaps this presumption applies only when she accepted the first kiddushin herself, for she is embarrassed before him because she received his kiddushin money and benefited from his kiddushin or his gifts, whereas if she received no benefit from him she may act brazenly. The Mishneh LaMelech rejects this distinction. Even where her father accepted her kiddushin, she does not act brazenly. This is also evident from Responsa Ra'anach, part 1, at the end of siman 8; see there.
