A Samaritan who spoke offhandedly first — even though they afterward asked him and examined him until he explained the entire incident — he is believed and they permit based on him. {Rama: But if they asked him first "where is our companion" and he told them he died — this is not called mesalfei lefi tumo; and specifically when they asked the Samaritan himself; but if they asked another Samaritan (Piskei Maharai siman 161) (or they searched in another city for a Jew and a rumor spread thus and a Samaritan came and spoke offhandedly that he died) (responsum of Maharam in laws of women) — even though he was with the Samaritan they asked, it is called mesalfei lefi tumo. If they were speaking together about the Jew and a Samaritan came to them and said "what are you speaking about" and they told him about the Jew whether he is alive or dead and he told them he is dead — this is not called mesalfei lefi tumo (in the responsum of the Rivash). If there was doubt whether mesalfei lefi tumo or not — they do not permit his wife, for safek deoraita lechumra. And likewise in every place where there is a dispute in these laws we go stringently (likewise there). Some say that which we say if they asked the Samaritan first it is not called mesalfei lefi tumo — this is specifically if an Israelite asks him; but if Samaritans ask him and he tells — it is called mesalfei lefi tumo, provided they asked him not in the presence of an Israelite (Terumat HaDeshen siman 239). And if an Israelite asked and the Samaritan said he died and indicated estimations that prove — even though it is not called mesalfei lefi tumo and she is forbidden to marry — nevertheless if she transgressed and married based on a sage who permitted her — she need not leave if there are proofs and estimations that he died (Piskei Maharai siman 139).}
