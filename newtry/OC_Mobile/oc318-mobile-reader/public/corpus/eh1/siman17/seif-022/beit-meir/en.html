Second gloss: But other gentiles. Beit Shmuel wrote: it appears that these three rulings, etc. If so, it is impossible to rule that a gentile speaking innocently from the mouth of another who spoke innocently is believed, unless it is also clearly ruled that a gentile who asked another gentile outside the presence of a Jew is considered speaking innocently, because otherwise one must presumably be concerned that perhaps the answer came through a gentile's question to a gentile. In my humble opinion, the opposite is explicit in Terumat HaDeshen. For at first he wrote: if so, in our case perhaps it was through a question; nevertheless, the distinction between a Jew asking and a gentile asking appears. Afterward he wrote: furthermore, it appears that we do not assume stringently and say that he told it through a question. Thus he wrote this furthermore as an even-if: even if a gentile who asked is not considered speaking innocently, nevertheless we are not concerned that perhaps it occurred through a question. But according to his reading of Terumat HaDeshen, that what it wrote, we are not concerned that perhaps it was through a question, means in the presence of a Jew, in a way that it would not be innocent speech, whereas through a gentile's question we are indeed concerned, only that it does not harm the status of innocent speech, then the language furthermore does not fit; see there.
