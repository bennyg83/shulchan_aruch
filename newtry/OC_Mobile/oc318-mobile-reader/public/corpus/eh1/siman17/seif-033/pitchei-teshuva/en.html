That the witness said. In Responsa Maharival, part 1 siman 1 and part 2 simanim 12 and 14, he wished to derive a leniency regarding water and war, where the witness must say "I buried him." If the witness did not say "I buried him" and is not before us, or if it is testimony from the mouth of another witness and the first witness is not before us, we may be lenient and assume that if the first witness were before us he would clarify how he knew with certainty that the man died, for example that he moved him from place to place, which is considered like "I buried him" as Rama wrote in seif 17, or the like; only at first he did not think it necessary to mention it. The same would apply where there is doubt whether enough time elapsed, and where there is doubt whether it was within three days: one may be more lenient with testimony heard from another witness than with the first witness himself. He supported this from Terumat HaDeshen's rulings, simanim 220 and 223. Some later authorities followed him in leniency. However, Responsa Raanach siman 65, cited in Responsa Maharit part 2 siman 31, and Maharit there and in part 1 siman 48, strongly rejected this reasoning. Kuntres HaRosh also objected to Maharival and cited Responsa Maharam Alashkar siman 25. Later he concludes in the name of Rashdam siman 51 that where the concern is rabbinic, such as endless waters and the like, one may rely on Maharival, unlike Responsa R. Betzalel Ashkenazi siman 21 and the Shach in siman 183, who followed Maharival even for a Torah concern. One who studies Maharival part 2 simanim 12 and 14 will see that he himself did not rely firmly on this reasoning and was unsure about it. See what I wrote above, seif 27 s.k. 123, in the name of Responsa Noda BiYehudah siman 48.
(33) But he said simply that he heard, etc. See Mahaneh Efraim, s.k. 26, who wrote that this law comes from Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev, cited in Darkei Moshe, and it is clear that it concerns a case where he testified explicitly that he heard from another, but did not clarify from whom. In such a case we do not suspect that the first witness was invalid. But where he says only "I heard" and does not say "I heard from another," there is room to doubt that perhaps he heard only a rumor. See Knesset HaGedolah, siman 139, where one rabbi was unsure about this, while Beit Yosef in his responsum, cited there in siman 148, tends to be lenient even in this case, apparently only where the witness is not before us. See Responsa Chatam Sofer siman 70 concerning an agunah whose husband had wandered far away for eighteen years. One testified that while in Odessa with his son he spoke with a man from Bohuslav, who said that in the synagogue in Bohuslav he heard that the man in question, who had a son in Horbate, died in Bohuslav. Chatam Sofer answered that the Mishnah at the end of Yevamot explicitly permits remarriage on the basis of a heavenly voice, and all the more where one says he heard that so-and-so died, even if we do not know from whom he heard; we do not suspect the minority possibility that it came from an invalid source. However, this applies where he at least heard from a known person. A general rumor, like women's talk, is worse for two reasons: sometimes a sick man is near death and people spread a report that he died, though later he lives; and sometimes a person disappeared and people assume by estimation that he died. Yet in the case before him, all agree the woman is permitted, because the report was that people in the synagogue said R. Hirsch Kirshner died there in Bohuslav. If it were only a momentary rumor because he had fainted, why would they say he died in Bohuslav, "here in our community"? It clearly meant that he had died there earlier and one person was telling another. Nor is there concern that it was mere estimation because he had disappeared, for they said he died specifically in Bohuslav. Therefore the report is clear enough, and one who permits this agunah loses nothing. A court of three should sit and permit her. See also Responsa Mahari M. of Brisk siman 23 on a similar matter.
