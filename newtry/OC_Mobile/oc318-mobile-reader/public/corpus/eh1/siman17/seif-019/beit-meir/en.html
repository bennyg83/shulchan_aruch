But if he heard from children, etc. The Beit Shmuel wrote: according to what I wrote nearby, that we require speaking incidentally, one may say that here it speaks where they were not speaking incidentally. His intent must be that according to what he wrote nearby in s.k. 4, that the law in the final clause, he heard from the children, speaks specifically where they were speaking incidentally. One must therefore say that this ordinary statement is considered speaking incidentally, for the Mishnah and the Rambam did not mention speaking incidentally. One may say that ordinary statements of he died do not help because they are not ordinarily regarded as speaking incidentally; but if we know that he said he died while speaking incidentally, he too is believed. By contrast, if the final clause speaks where they intended to testify, then there is no case at all in the Mishnah in which a minor is believed while speaking incidentally, and in every case where he merely says, he died, without further context, it does not help. This applies only according to the first opinion in the gloss to seif 14. He further wrote: and in the Perishah, etc. It is true that the language of the Tur indicates this, since he interrupted and wrote, whether they intended, before writing, and if one hears from them, etc. But the language of the Mishnah indicates the opposite, for the phrase, whether he intended, is stated with respect to the reports that come before it. So too the language of the Remezim, from which the Taz brought support, states: if he heard from the women that they said he died, or from the children, or that they said, we are coming from burying him, whether they intended for testimony or did not intend, he may testify that he died, provided that they said, such-and-such eulogizers, etc. Thus even the clause, we are coming, includes whether they intended, etc. This is as the Beit Shmuel wrote in s.k. 34, that the Tur implies, etc. Necessarily, in truth, even in the statement, we are coming, according to the Perishah one does not require speaking incidentally; rather, one requires the details of such-and-such eulogizers to exclude concern for the story of Kamtza. See seif 7. The Yam Shel Shlomo z"l, although he also explains the Tur according to the Perishah's approach, nevertheless wrote: I say that one who permits a woman based on testimony of minors who intended to testify deserves excommunication; even if she has several children, she must leave. He wrote further: according to this, it appears at first glance that even where the minor speaks incidentally, he is not believed except in an actual narrative, like the Mishnah, where the matter depends on an act of the child himself. However, the Or Zarua wrote that a minor who speaks incidentally immediately is valid. It appears that a minor is not considered to be speaking incidentally unless some statement precedes it, and not where he says, without introduction or connection, so-and-so died. Although below I will explain that my view inclines to leniency regarding a gentile speaking incidentally, that even in such a case he is believed, nevertheless a minor, who lacks legal understanding, requires some contextual words according to all. This is specifically where he does not say it immediately; but if he comes and reports the event immediately without intending to testify, then even ordinary speaking incidentally is believed. This may decide between Raavan and Ravyah: indeed we require immediacy in speaking incidentally where he says plainly, so-and-so died, but where the matter itself is expressed as incidental speech with introductory words, he is believed even after time has passed. In this, however, it is already explained in the Rama's glosses not like him, for even when they recount the matter, it must be immediate. Still, what he wrote, that with a minor all agree it is not considered speaking incidentally except with contextual words, certainly seems to be the opinion of the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. For according to them, a minor who intends to testify is not believed in any manner. Necessarily, the clause of hearing from the children speaks where they are speaking incidentally; and it implies that merely saying, he died, even incidentally, is not believed. This is unlike the Beit Shmuel's uncertainty mentioned above, where he wrote, according to what I wrote nearby, etc. Rather, with incidental speech of a minor, contextual words are required. In this I am astonished at the Yam Shel Shlomo in Bava Kamma there, who wrote about the answer, such as where the owners were chasing after them, etc.: I am astonished, what did Ravina ask against Rabbi Elazar? Perhaps a swarm of bees is different because there are two factors, the owners chasing and incidental speech. From this question he creates a new law, that where it is known that the owners did not despair, there is no need for the owners to chase. In my humble opinion, the sugya is compelled against him; the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilkhot Gezelah also makes no distinction at all. According to his words here, there is no difficulty: the chasing is needed because of the minor, in place of contextual words, and together with the chasing, which is grounds for the matter, it is called speaking incidentally. By this it is understandable why Rashi z"l was compelled to explain Rabbi Elazar's statement, that speaking incidentally is valid only for testimony concerning a woman, as referring to hearing from the children and not to testimony of a gentile, as the Kuntres Iggun of Maharachash strained to explain according to the view of the Tur and Shiltei Gibborim that he cites. Rather, it is because with a gentile speaking incidentally, according to most poskim contextual words are not required; if so, the question against Rabbi Elazar from a swarm of bees would not arise, since there are two factors, chasing and incidental speech. But if Rabbi Elazar speaks about incidental speech of minors, which requires contextual words, the statement, we are coming, is properly similar to chasing, and the question is sound. I later found that Pri Chadash, Yoreh De'ah siman 69 s.k. 40, initially explained as I did. What he wrote, alternatively, to distinguish between monetary matters and prohibitions, has no understandable basis; for if so, what is the question from a swarm of bees, where there are two factors, against Rabbi Elazar's statement? And what he wrote, and know, etc., according to what I have written this is not so, for there too there are contextual words, which are like owners chasing in the case of a swarm. Know that Rashi z"l here in Yevamot, and likewise Nimukei Yosef, explained, whether he intended for testimony, and the Tosafot Yom Tov wrote that they come to exclude intending to permit her; he left it requiring study. If this precision were exact, it would imply that he too explains like the Tur, that it refers to the testimony of minors. For if it were an independent matter, why should adults not be believed when intending to permit? But in my humble opinion this is not so, as is proven from Rashi's explanation in Bava Kamma, where he explains, we are coming from burying him, as speaking incidentally. Rather, this precision is not exact, or he used the term testimony because of the precision that regarding a gentile, when he intends it is not testimony, even if he intends to testify, as is the conclusion of the sugya.
