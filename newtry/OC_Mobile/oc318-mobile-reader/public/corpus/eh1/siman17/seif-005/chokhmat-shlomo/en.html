Seif 3 — A woman, etc., and they testified he died, etc. Behold I saw in Sefer Neta Sha'ashuim (siman 62) that he wants to prove testimony of death is rabbinic — for if from Torah, behold witness from witness is certainly rabbinic; if so how does the Gemara say in chapter 4 of Shevuot that all agree regarding testimony of death he is exempt when he spoke to her and not to court; behold the Gemara says gambling with dice is liable for an oath from the power of from Torah they see fit; if so here too from Torah his testimony is needed — he left it in uncertainty. And behold after examination he erred and compared two separate matters — oath of testimony applies only if his testimony is needed; but if his testimony is not needed, oath of testimony is inapplicable. Therefore in gambling with dice his testimony is needed, for without his testimony in truth the claimant is not paid; therefore since his testimony is needed, only we say even if he testifies it will not help here — we follow Torah law and from Torah he is fit for testimony and oath of testimony applies since his testimony is needed. But where now his testimony is not needed — for ultimately now the rabbis established her and if so without his testimony she may marry; and since in truth his testimony is not needed here — certainly we follow rabbinic law lest from another side he does not need testimony since the other pays automatically or as if other witnesses came for this or as if she does not seek witnesses at all; and wherever his testimony is not needed we follow rabbinic law. But where his testimony is needed only there is no benefit to his testimony here — we follow Torah law — examine well. See in my responsum on laws of yibum siman 169 what I proved they did not lighten for a woman because of takkanat ha'ishut except where the witness testifies on actual death and not on another matter; but for the law — uncertain; for in my responsum already to Zlatopol I proved the opposite and so is the implication of the poskim — uncertain in this now; perhaps God will merit me in time to speak at length on this; examine.
