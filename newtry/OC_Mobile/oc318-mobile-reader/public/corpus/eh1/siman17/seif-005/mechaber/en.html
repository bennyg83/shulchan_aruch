We already said that a witness who said "I heard that so-and-so died" — even if he heard from a woman who heard from a slave — he is fit for a woman's testimony and they permit on his word. {Rama: And even if he did not say from whom he heard but said simply "I heard" — he is fit; and we are not concerned lest the first was disqualified.} But if the witness or the woman or the slave said "so-and-so died" and "I saw him die" — they question him: how did you see and how did you know? If he testified with a clear matter he is believed; but if he testified with matters that mostly indicate death — they do not permit his wife; for one does not testify that a person died unless they saw him die with certainty and there is no doubt. {Rama: If they heard a voice of lamentation that mentioned him among the dead and eulogized him — they permit his wife (so too the Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Tosefta). Therefore one should protest to women not to eulogize a person based on estimations that prove he died, and they should not eulogize him until they know he died; and likewise his wife is forbidden to eulogize him or wear black as long as there is no testimony fit to permit her (Seder HaDorot and so too the Rivash siman 508). And likewise a court should not give a testimony document to a woman for what witnesses testified before them if that testimony is not fit to permit her — unless they wrote explicitly the reason they did not permit her on this testimony, lest damage come from a court that is not expert (there in the responsum of the Rivash).}
