But with disqualified witnesses, etc. In Mahari Weil, no. 164, he raised a difficulty against Terumat HaDeshen, who held that Maharam treated the "detestable" person only as rabbinically disqualified and therefore valid for testimony permitting a woman. If so, from where did Maharam know that we follow the majority of opinions, namely three gentiles speaking incidentally against the Jews? Perhaps we follow the majority to permit only where those who permit are disqualified in the same way as those who prohibit, such as two gentiles saying he died and one saying he did not die, or two women and one woman. But in Maharam's case, if this person is fit to testify by Torah law, from where do we know that we follow three gentiles leniently, when they are Torah-disqualified, as the language of the Gemara implies: "R. Nechemiah says: wherever the Torah believed one witness, follow the majority of opinions, and they treated two women like one woman," etc. It mentions only cases where their disqualification is equal. In my humble opinion, the unqualified language of all the decisors implies that they did not descend to such a distinction. The language of Shulchan Aruch, se'if 37, which is Rambam's language, also seems unlike him, for it says: "When does this apply? When two women or rabbinically disqualified witnesses came and said he did not die, etc.; but if a valid witness says he died and many women or disqualified witnesses say he did not die, this is like half against half." This law derives from the answer: "Wherever a valid witness came first, even one hundred women are considered like one witness." According to Mahari Weil this too is difficult: let us say that only women, who are Torah-disqualified, are one hundred like one, but rabbinically disqualified witnesses, since by Torah law they are valid and for testimony concerning a woman they are valid even rabbinically, should count as one against two and not as half against half. Rather, it seems that the text did not insist specifically on "women," but used the Gemara's expression "disqualified witnesses," meaning that all disqualified witnesses who are valid for testimony concerning a woman are equal; and the same applies to minority and majority: since both are disqualified witnesses, even though one is Torah-disqualified and the other rabbinically disqualified, they are equal and we follow the majority. This requires study.
