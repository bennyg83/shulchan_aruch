And even testimony from the mouth of another witness. Responsa Maharival, part 3, siman 8, concludes that a witness who testifies that he heard from the husband himself, in incidental conversation, that his name was so-and-so son of so-and-so, or that he was the husband of a particular woman, is considered only a witness testifying from another witness. Since he did not recognize him independently but learned these facts from the husband, he has the status of a disqualified witness. This makes a practical difference for the laws explained below in seifim 37 and 38. Responsa Maharashdam, siman 59, cites this and disagrees, holding that he has the status of a valid witness like one who actually saw the matter. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 14, brings a clear proof supporting Maharashdam; see there. See also Baer Heitev below, s.k. 121, and Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 45.
