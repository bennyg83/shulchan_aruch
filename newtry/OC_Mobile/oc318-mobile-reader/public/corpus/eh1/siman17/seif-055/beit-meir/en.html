The woman herself, etc. Two great authorities of the generation, namely R. Natan and R. Shmuel, brought in Terumat HaDeshen siman 239 and in the Beit Yosef, were uncertain: since we do not find explicitly that a gentile speaking innocently is believed regarding identification by visual recognition, one may say that he is believed only when he says, he died before me. Although we do not rule this way, that is because the Mishnah distinguishes between a Jew and a gentile only where he intends to testify; this implies that where he does not intend to testify, he is equal to a Jew. But regarding a woman, where we find no more than this language, and she came and said, my husband died, she is believed, whose plain meaning is that he died before my eyes, there is room to doubt whether she is believed through identification by visual recognition. Perhaps, because she is anxious to free herself from being an agunah, we are concerned that she will testify by approximation. The Mishnah therefore teaches that in war she is not believed even when he died before her. And if you say that since we see that regarding testimony from the mouth of another witness she is like any other witness, the same should apply to all matters, this is not so. Regarding testimony from another witness, it is clear that she is superior; the proof is that her testimony is explicit in the Mishnah and was established in the days of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel, whereas the testimony of an ordinary single witness is disputed by the Tannaim. Since both were established, one may properly learn by kal vachomer that she is believed regarding testimony from another witness. But regarding the concern of approximation, we see that she is inferior to an ordinary woman; the proof is the question about one witness in war, where they initially sought to resolve it from the incident of the Torah scholar whom Rabbi permitted on the testimony of women, and the same would apply in war, although she herself in war certainly is not believed. However, this entire doubt exists only according to the Baal HaMaor's method in the above sugya, where the Talmud mentions the concern of approximation through mistaken substitution. But according to the Mordekhai and the Yam Shel Shlomo in the view of the Rif and Rambam, where no hint is mentioned at all of a concern for approximation through mistaken substitution in relation to identification by visual recognition, except where he remained three days on dry land or an hour after being brought up from the water, where everyone agrees they do not testify, there is no room for this doubt in my humble opinion. Be that as it may, it is explained in the above Terumat HaDeshen that it was obvious to him that even the woman herself is believed. For he answered that what Tosafot stated, that witnesses are a kal vachomer from one who speaks innocently, refers to the woman herself, who is stronger than a gentile speaking innocently. His view is explicit: the woman herself too is not believed by visual recognition without a sign only in war and water; but on dry land she is believed. Although my answer is not compelled, for my question can be settled simply, as explained in the Kuntres Agunot of Maharachash, that regarding concern for lying, women are certainly preferable, while regarding approximation, one who speaks innocently is worse; nevertheless his law is certainly true and we do not examine further, etc. So it appears from the plain words of most poskim, and so it appears from Shiltei Gibborim that I will bring in seif 48. One can also infer this somewhat from the language of the Gemara on 116: Rav Shimi said, because she says by approximation. The plain implication is that in peacetime we are not concerned at all for her regarding any concerns of approximation, and this is the simple understanding.
