Even one witness. The reason one witness is believed here, while generally we hold that one witness is not believed against a presumption, as written in Yoreh De’ah siman 127, and here she has the presumption of a married woman, is that here the sages were lenient because of the enactment for an agunah. At the beginning of Perek HaIshah Rabbah the reason is given that the sages relied on the fact that she will investigate carefully because of the stringency they imposed upon her at the end. At the beginning of Perek HaIshah Shalom the reason is given that it is a matter likely to become revealed. See Maharik, root 32. What is written, even if the witness is a slave: although an ordinary slave is disqualified for testimony by Torah law because of transgression, one must say that here it speaks where it is known that he is fit, as written in Yoreh De’ah siman 1. So wrote the Bach. See Nimukei Yosef.
