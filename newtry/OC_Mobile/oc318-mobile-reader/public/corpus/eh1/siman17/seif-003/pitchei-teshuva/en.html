That she said it to her husband in his presence. See Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 91. The questioner was uncertain whether she must say this specifically before a beis din and in her husband's presence, such that a statement made outside beis din is ineffective. He writes: I am astonished, for everywhere it merely says that she said it to her husband, and nowhere is a beis din mentioned. When the Rivash writes that it is not called 'in his presence' unless both are before a beis din, he is discussing a claim that the husband is incapable, for many different causes may underlie such a claim and therefore a beis din must investigate it. But regarding 'you divorced me,' or that he had relations with her while she was a niddah, where there is no alternative cause upon which to attribute the claim, a beis din is certainly unnecessary. He brings another proof as well; see there.
