Seif 2 — The woman who said to her husband in his presence "you divorced me," etc. I was asked: why when she says "you divorced me" is she believed based on the presumption that a woman does not act brazenly, etc. — behold there are against it two presumptions: presumption of Torah prohibition and presumption of money for those who hold she collects her ketubah thereby; if so it is one against two. And even though the presumption that a woman does not act brazenly is a presumption that comes from reasoning and is preferable to other presumptions — nevertheless there is no reasoning to say it is more preferable than two presumptions. The questioner asked and I answered him: the difficulty here is only for one opinion; for regarding death she certainly collects her ketubah, and Tosafot challenged regarding one who had relations with her on Yom Kippur — behold he stands for a sin-offering, for two against two is rabbinic safek and from Torah we establish on presumption; and they answered: she examines and marries, etc. Difficulty: behold here too are two presumptions — also presumption of money — and still he stands for a sin-offering; strained to say she examines for two presumptions. Further difficulty: regarding falling in endless waters all wrote she leaves Torah prohibition for majority indicate death, and there is an opinion she is completely permitted — why does she take her ketubah thereby, and we do not follow money after majority? But in truth there is no beginning to this difficulty — for prohibitions and monetary matters are separate matters and do not combine; similar to what we hold in Choshen Mishpat siman 87 — where the essence of the claim is because of prohibition, even though there is practical difference for money, nevertheless he is exempt from oath; behold we follow the essence of the claim and do not regard what follows automatically. So too here one may say: behold when claiming ketubah she is not believed; only when not claiming she is believed to say "you divorced me," and the essence of the claim is because of prohibition — one should not judge what will follow afterward regarding money (the Maharach said a word of truth in his mouth, and so wrote the author of Pela'ah in his novellae end of chapter HaIshah ShenitArmelah and the Rosh). And see Yevamot 93b — R' Shimon asked regarding one witness with a yevamah; and in Tosafot there s.v. one witness with a yevamah until Rava said one witness is believed with a yevamah from a kal vechomer; and Rashi explained there and Tosafot wondered on him; and in my humble opinion one may say the Gemara certainly knew the reason one witness is believed is because of dayika uminsaba — only it needed to ask whether prohibitions and monetary matters are one matter and combine, or are separate matters and do not combine; therefore if here it is one matter — behold there is presumption of Torah prohibition also presumption of money — therefore one witness alone without dayika cannot reject two presumptions; perforce the reason is because of almoni and one presumption is rejected by avdei legluyei and one by dayika uminsaba; automatically with a yevamah where there is no presumption of money — obligation of ketubah applies either way when her husband died — therefore the reason of almoni alone suffices; or perhaps prohibition and money do not combine and there is only one presumption alone and dayika suffices; but almoni does not help to reject the presumption — therefore with a yevamah where dayika is inapplicable she is forbidden; and thus Tosafot's difficulty s.v. one witness with a yevamah is resolved; therefore Rava holds simply that prohibitions and money combine and there are two presumptions; and although we also need dayika — nevertheless we need almoni and it is proven the reason of almoni helps to overturn one presumption; therefore with a yevamah one reason of almoni alone suffices; therefore he says kal vechomer for prohibition of karet he permitted; and Rashi is settled — examine and understand, for I shortened. And behold what I wrote that with a yevamah there is no presumption of money — this is even though if she performs yibum she has no ketubah immediately; nevertheless as long as she did not perform yibum the yavam did not acquire his dead brother's property — if so he and the yavam are equal in them and he has no greater presumption than she to establish money on her presumption; on the contrary she has a clear debt and he has only safek if he wishes to perform yibum — therefore the property is not in his presumption more than hers. Examine and examine. And behold what I wrote above that prohibition and money do not combine — do not reject this from what we say at the end of Yevamot that R' Akiva holds witnesses for women do not require examination because there is ketubah to collect — it is similar to what the questioner above wanted to reject; but in truth it is not similar — for there from Torah even for money we require examination, only the rabbis enacted we do not require examination in such a case; therefore if it can be said ab initio the rabbis did not divide in their enactment — it depends on this: if there is also practical difference for money — since there is practical difference also for money it is in the category of such a case and one can say the rabbis did not divide in their enactment; but if there is no practical difference for money at all — it is inapplicable to say they did not divide, for it is not in the category of the enactment. But regarding Torah certainly prohibition and money do not combine — so is correct and plain; examine.
