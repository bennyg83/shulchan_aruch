In a time of distress. Ba'er Heitev cites the view that section 7 is lenient in this matter only with respect to a complete evildoer. Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 28 discusses the issue at length and cites Responsa Maharival III:75, which concludes that for testimony concerning a woman it is not necessary that the witness's beginning and end both be in fitness, even where he had been a complete evildoer. Machatzit HaShekel ultimately agrees: this is no worse than an apostate Jew speaking incidentally, who was disqualified as a witness at the time of seeing and still has no formal status as a witness, yet is effective for an agunah because there is no concern of falsehood. Certainly one who testifies after repenting should be acceptable. The addenda cite Maharchash to the same effect, and Pitchei Teshuvah refers to his discussion in Choshen Mishpat regarding one who returns to fitness and one who removes himself from an interest. Birkei Yosef likewise writes that the later authorities agree leniently that beginning and end in fitness are not required, as Maharam ibn Chaviv wrote in Ezrat Nashim, and as Kenesset HaGedolah and the rabbis of his generation ruled in practice. Yad Aharon and other later rabbis agree, against Maharikash in his glosses, who appears to incline to prohibition. See also Responsa Darkei Noam simanim 22 and 24.
