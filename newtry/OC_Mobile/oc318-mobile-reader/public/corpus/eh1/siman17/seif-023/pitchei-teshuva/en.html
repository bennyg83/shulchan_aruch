If he speaks incidentally, he is believed. In Nachalas Tzvi I cited a great contemporary scholar who wished to propose that even a non-Jew who does not speak incidentally may be relied upon regarding testimony of death, where he does not claim to have personally seen the death but says in the name of a particular Jew that the Jew told him directly that so-and-so died. The reason would be that he fears being caught in a lie, similar to the rule in Yoreh De'ah, siman 86, concerning a non-Jew selling eggs, etc. In my humble opinion it is difficult to accept this innovation, and I discussed it at length there; see there.
