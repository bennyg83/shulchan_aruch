Beit Shmuel wrote: according to the Maggid Mishneh's words, one must distinguish, etc. That distinction is certainly not reasonable, as explained in Kuntres Agunot of Maharchash, folio 14b; see there, for he brings proof from the Rivash for Chelkat Mechokek's reasoning that where the witness was present when the question was asked of his fellow and he answered, it is not considered speaking innocently. In practice this certainly requires study. Likewise, wherever there is a dispute, etc., see Beit Shmuel. See Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter HaIshah, who wrote regarding the dispute about found written, so-and-so died, that it appears the halachah follows the lenient view in testimony concerning women. At first glance it is also possible that this is the reason of the Rif and the Rosh, and they did not decide this dispute. To say that their view is stringent, for this the proofs of the Ran and Nimukei Yosef from chapter Mi SheAchatzo suffice; and to say that they relied on there too is somewhat forced. According to the above, it is understandable. Regarding an unresolved question, in Kuntres Agunot of Maharchash, folio 32, he wrote: the Rif and Rosh omitted the question of a future mother-in-law, while the Tur rules stringently, and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that the correct reason was not clear to him. It appears that the reason is because of what we say in Bekhorot: they were lenient at the end, but not at the beginning. Specifically at the beginning of the testimony, meaning the substance of the testimony, where they did not testify clearly that he died, one must be stringent. But at the end of the testimony, meaning that it was carefully investigated and they testified clearly that he died, only that there is doubt about the witnesses whether they are valid or invalid, we are lenient because of agunah, etc. Perhaps this was the intent of Beit Yosef. End quote. In my humble opinion, this matter of doubt about witnesses depends on the dispute whether testimony concerning a woman through one witness is Torah law, like the view of Nimukei Yosef in the name of Ritva and Maharik root 30 in the name of the Ramah z"l, or because even by Torah law one witness is believed when the matter is likely to be revealed, as implied from the words of Rabbenu Moshe; see Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilkhot Edut halachah 2, where it is explained that it is rabbinic, and see Penei Yehoshua, beginning of Gittin 2b, s.v. ve-ein davar sheb'ervah. If so, when the Sages disqualified witnesses like the five women, one may say that this is only rabbinic; and if we have a question whether this one too was disqualified, it is a rabbinic doubt and lenient. But according to the approach of most poskim, that all testimony of one witness and a woman is valid only rabbinically, then when we ask whether they also validated this witness or not, if they did not validate her she remains under her law and is invalid by Torah law; the doubt is stringent. See Beit Shmuel s.k. 160, and this is clear.
