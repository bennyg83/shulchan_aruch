We do not examine witnesses concerning women. The reason is that the matter is one that is likely to become revealed; alternatively, because she investigates carefully and then marries. The Rambam follows the first reason; see the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 13. If the witness is before us, we ask how he saw and how he knows, as stated above. See siman 11, where we rule that to forbid a woman to her husband requires formal interrogation and examination. Here, however, interrogation and examination are not required. Since she collects the ketubah, the case resembles monetary law, even though permitting her to another man is like capital law. In any event, capital cases do not apply in our time. But regarding forbidding her to her husband there is no monetary issue at all: if she is forbidden, she has no ketubah; and if she is permitted, she nevertheless does not collect the ketubah. Therefore it is treated as capital law. As for joining witnesses of impropriety to forbid her to her husband, witnesses are not joined in capital matters, as I wrote in siman 11. See the Beit Yosef there and the responsum of the Rosh.
