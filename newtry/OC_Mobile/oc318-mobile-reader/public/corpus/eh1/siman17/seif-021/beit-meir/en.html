And similarly if he said, so-and-so died. Beit Shmuel wrote: and in responsum Shevut Yaakov, etc., end quote, and this is not difficult, etc. I am surprised what difficulty he saw and looked at, for even the answer is already explained there. He should also have copied what is said there in the name of Maharlbach, that there is no proof from there, for perhaps the Tanna chose one of them; and in the answer of the one who resolves the sugya, the concern that perhaps he is a gentile is also answered from the teaching of the school of Rabbi Yishmael, that in a time of danger they write even though they do not recognize him. The Mishneh LaMelekh, halachah 11, wrote: I am surprised at this rejection, for his proof is from the questioner: before he asks, perhaps it was a co-wife, let him ask, perhaps it was a gentile. In my humble opinion, this is not difficult, for it was stronger for him to ask from a co-wife, since the concern that she intends to ruin her also applies there, unlike a gentile, as stated in the Taz. Rather, it appears that the Rivash follows the view of the Gaon in seif 10, that for us we are concerned lest it be a demon. Therefore he must distinguish between a co-wife, which is uncommon and we are not concerned for it in a case of danger, and a demon in a field, which is common. If so, he properly asks even at the conclusion that we should be concerned for a gentile, which is very common. The Bach rejects this by saying perhaps it speaks in the holy tongue and in a place where it is uncommon for gentiles to know it. Maharchash likewise rejects it, that since a gentile is not suspected of ruining her, his ascent and announcement that so-and-so died is itself considered contextual speech. Perhaps this too is the intent of the Taz, but it is forced.
