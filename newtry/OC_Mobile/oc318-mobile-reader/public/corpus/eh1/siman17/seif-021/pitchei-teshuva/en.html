And if they speak incidentally. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 14, cites the Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 69, s.k. 23, who writes that with a non-Jew familiar with Jewish law, incidental speech is found only when he relates another matter and, in the course of that account, mentions that he rinsed the meat, etc. Based on this, Machatzis HaShekel writes that although the gloss below, seif 14, states that even without linkage to another matter, a bare statement that so-and-so died is considered incidental speech, that applies specifically to a non-Jew unfamiliar with Jewish law. But a disqualified Jew, who knows Jewish law and knows that permission for the woman depends upon his testimony, must according to all opinions speak incidentally as part of another connected matter. He then brings proofs that there is no distinction in this regard between a non-Jew and a disqualified Jew, and concludes that everything depends upon the halachic authority's assessment of the circumstances; see there.
