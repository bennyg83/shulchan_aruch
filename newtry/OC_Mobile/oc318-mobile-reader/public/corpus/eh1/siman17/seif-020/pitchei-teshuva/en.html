Disqualified persons. Noda BiYehudah siman 45 was asked about three witnesses who testified together in an agunah case, identifying the husband by bodily marks, and one was then found to be a thief, biblically disqualified because of wickedness even for testimony concerning a woman. The question was whether the two valid witnesses are invalidated by the rule that when a group testifies and one is found related or disqualified the whole testimony is void, and if the first testimony is void whether they may at least testify again. Noda BiYehudah answers that this rule does not apply here. Those disqualified for testimony concerning a woman are disqualified only because they are suspected of lying; if we knew that the particular statement was true, the witness would be accepted, as with incidental speech. Therefore the valid witnesses, who are not suspected of lying, are not invalidated by joining him, and they need not testify again. R. Akiva Eiger siman 87 is unsure of this rule and cites Ran in the first chapter of Gittin as evidence that even in testimony concerning a woman the entire testimony may be void when one witness is invalid. In simanim 124-125 he raises the issue against the Gaon of Lissa, who had written simply like Noda BiYehudah, and then records the Gaon of Lissa's answer as correct and reasonable. Berit Avraham discusses it as well. Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 6 was asked whether the rule of "a complete matter, and not half a matter" invalidates testimony concerning a woman. Some contemporary scholars thought it did; he responds that this is no worse than relatives, who are biblically invalid for ordinary testimony but accepted in testimony concerning a woman. Only a disqualification rooted in suspicion of lying invalidates such testimony. Pitchei Teshuvah notes, however, that Responsa Mabit, as cited by Chatam Sofer, seems to support the stricter view, though he personally finds Yeshuot Yaakov more persuasive.
