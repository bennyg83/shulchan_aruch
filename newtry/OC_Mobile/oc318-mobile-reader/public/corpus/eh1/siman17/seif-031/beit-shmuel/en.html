Or they shot arrows at him. So writes the Rambam. According to the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, the Rambam did not mention the law of one cut to pieces. The Bayit Chadash likewise writes that the Rambam's statement, "if they stabbed him or shot arrows at him," is learned from the sugya at the beginning of HaIshah Shalom, where it says that sometimes they strike a man with an arrow or a spear and she assumes he certainly died, while there are people for whom a healing salve is made and they live. But if it appears that they cut both signs, witnesses may testify; and likewise if one saw that they wounded his brain, witnesses may testify about him. If, however, arrows were shot at him and we did not see what happened to him, we say that perhaps nothing happened to him, because a salve may have been prepared for him and the weapon did not prevail over him. The Beit Yosef, however, explains that when the Rambam writes "they stabbed him," he means the Mishnah's case of one cut to pieces, about whom one may not testify, because the Rambam rules stringently like Abaye's explanation there, which says that we are concerned that the knife may have been heated. Still, it appears that even according to the Beit Yosef, if the wound was in a place from which the soul departs, witnesses may testify. After all, where both signs were cut, the Rambam and Tur rule that one may testify. It must be that if the wound is in a fatal place, witnesses may testify; but if it is in a place from which the soul does not depart, even if he became a tereifah, witnesses may not testify even after twelve months, because we fear that the knife was heated and then he may live even longer than twelve months. This is unlike the Bayit Chadash, who wrote that after twelve months one may testify; that is incorrect, and the Chelkat Mechokek also objects to him. But where the soul departs, a heated knife does not help. Nimukei Yosef distinguishes differently: a heated knife helps where he was not rendered a tereifah but was merely cut to pieces; where he was rendered a tereifah, witnesses may testify, and a heated knife does not help. Shmuel's statement, "they taught this only where his soul does not depart," applies also to one cut to pieces. It follows that a heated knife does not help where he was rendered a tereifah. Similarly, in the Rambam's words one may distinguish: where he was rendered a tereifah, it helps, but where his soul departs, it does not. All this concerns one who is not moribund. If he is moribund, Tosafot write that if he is both moribund and cut to pieces, witnesses may testify about him; but if he is moribund and not cut to pieces, witnesses may not testify. If he has been moribund for three days, witnesses may testify about him, as stated in Yoreh De'ah; so too writes the book Beit Hillel.
