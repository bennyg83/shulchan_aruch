We already said that a Samaritan who speaks offhandedly — they permit based on him. How was he speaking offhandedly? He was conversing offhandedly and said "woe to so-and-so who died, how handsome he was and how much good he did with me"; or he was conversing and saying "when I was coming on the road and so-and-so who was walking with us fell and died, and we wondered at this thing how he died suddenly" — and the like of these matters that show his intention was not to testify — he is believed. And an Israelite who heard from a Samaritan speaking offhandedly testifies that he heard from him and his wife may marry on his word. {Rama: And likewise if he said "so-and-so died" speaking offhandedly — even though he did not say other words it is called properly mesalfei lefi tumo (Rivash siman 377) (and some are stringent) (Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran).} In what case? When there was no ulterior motive; but if there was an ulterior motive in the Samaritan's conversation — lest he intended otherwise, such as if he said to one "do such-and-such for me lest I kill you as I killed so-and-so" — this is not speaking offhandedly, for his intention is to instill fear for idolatry. {Rama: And likewise in a place where there is concern for falsehood that they taught him to say thus — one does not rely on him (R' Yonah Netiv 23).} And likewise if he heard from courts of Samaritans that said "we killed so-and-so" — they are not believed; for they hold themselves up with falsehood. And likewise all similar matters. {Rama: And some say all courts are not believed even if they say he was killed in judgment not by their hand (Tur in the name of the Rosh); and all this is specifically regarding courts; but other Samaritans speak offhandedly and say he was judged in courts of Samaritans — they are believed (Piskei Maharai siman 34).}
