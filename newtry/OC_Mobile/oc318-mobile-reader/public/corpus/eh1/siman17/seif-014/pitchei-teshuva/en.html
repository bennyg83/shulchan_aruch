One witness. See Baer Heitev. Regarding what he cites in the name of Maharashdam, that if at first, etc., and there is room to attribute, etc., the meaning is that this applies even if the witness first answered before a beis din. His further statement, that if his initial claim of ignorance was not made before a beis din, etc., is cited in Kovetz Edus, siman 352, where it says that even if the rabbi was present, since no other dayan was with him it was not considered testimony before a beis din. This appears to be the principal novelty, for otherwise the Shulchan Aruch below, seif 20, already states that even one who had objected, etc., based on a responsum of the Ran. See what I wrote there in Pitchei Teshuvah.
