Her ketubah. Namely two hundred zuz; but not additional amount and nedunya which is nitzba — see Kenesset HaGedolah 31b who brought many poskim who hold so. He also brings many poskim who hold she collects two hundred and additional amount and nedunya whether from inheritors whether from mortgaged property whether permitted on her own word whether through gentile incidentally whether through valid witness — see there. See Maharival ch. 2 siman 75 and Ram Metrani ch. 3 siman 134 and Rashdam ch. 1 siman 75 who wrote where she married through gentile incidentally she collects ketubah from inheritors — two hundred, additional amount, and nedunya. But siman 223 ch. 1 and Choshen Mishpat siman 248 wrote whether she married on her word whether through gentile incidentally she collects only two hundred not additional amount and nedunya — and so R' Eliyahu Levi siman 16; see Kenesset HaGedolah 31b. Mishpat Tzedek ch. 1 siman 9 wrote if the woman seized she can say kims li. If his memory was lost and she was permitted to marry she collects ketubah from inheritors — R' Eliyahu Levi siman 17.
Imbecile. What Choshen Mishpat wrote — Gemara is arranged for your words "sane woman may marry," etc. Yevamot 116b. Not necessary — one can say thus he means for your words sane woman may marry; namely sane who knows to tear garments and weep. Imbecile means she did not do so; but in truth she is not imbecile. And whether this or that she may marry refers whether she did so or not she may marry; but true imbecile — one can say no; examine; and so Tosafot Yom Tov on Mishnah 1 ch. HaIshah Shalom — see there; see Beit Yosef.
Her. Namely through the oath she swore on the ketubah they make her swear he died and the fixed time of the ketubah arrived — Choshen Mishpat, Beit Shmuel.
Her ketubah. From this language if she already married without court permission they give her ketubah for suspicion goes against her. Maharachash klal 174 wrote for law: if she says they permitted me to marry and I take ketubah — she may not marry to heed Ra'ash and Tur's words. However if she married she does not leave for it is fitting to rely on Rif and Rambam not to remove a woman from her husband. But if she said give me ketubah and permit me to marry even if she married she leaves since Rambam is alone in this — see there. Kenesset HaGedolah 49b disputes and expanded and ultimately raised whether she said give me ketubah and permit me to marry or said permit me to marry and give my ketubah — she may not marry lekhatchilah and if she married she does not leave. But if she said my husband died and give me my ketubah — yes and no and weak in her hands whether she leaves or not — see there 9a. See Choshen Mishpat, Beit Shmuel, Beit Yosef.
Died. From Ritva's language it appears he was stringent only when she brought the witness to court — then concern she hired him for the ketubah; but whenever the witness came alone to court why not believe him; if so difficult on Rama why he wrote whoever has a witness and did not write whoever brought the witness to court as Ritva's language — Choshen Mishpat. If one witness says I buried him — believed; see Beit Shmuel.
