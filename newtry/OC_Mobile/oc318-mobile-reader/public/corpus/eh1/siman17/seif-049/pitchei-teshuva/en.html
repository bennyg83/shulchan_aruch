For we are concerned that perhaps it is a demon. See Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel, and see Machatzit HaShekel, s.k. 34-36, who wrote at length with sharp analysis to answer the difficulties of the later authorities from below, paragraph 23, and from siman 141, paragraph 19. In the end he leaned toward leniency nowadays, when the ban of Rabbeinu Gershom not to marry two wives applies; from now on there is no concern for a rival wife, and there remains only the concern of a demon. With a single concern, they were lenient in a place of danger, and if so it is not necessary that they see the shadow of a shadow. In any event, where the shadow of a shadow is visible, we properly rely on it according to everyone and act upon it. However, from the words of Rashba and Ran in Gittin it appears that the concern for a demon is a closer concern than the concern for a rival wife, and one should be concerned for it independently. But from the words of Rif and the Rosh this is not compelled, as he wrote above. Therefore it appears that even nowadays one should not be lenient unless they saw his shadow of a shadow. But where they did see it properly, one may be lenient nowadays and need not be concerned for the words of the Gaon who says that we are not expert, for the reason we have written, especially since Bach is entirely lenient even without seeing a shadow, following Rambam and Ritva. If so, we too may rely on leniency at least where they saw his shadow of a shadow. He further wrote that the Gaon's statement that we are not expert in the matter of the shadow of a shadow is, at first glance, difficult, for this does not seem to require expertise: who does not know whether the shadow is longer than the height of the person? Perhaps he was speaking of the rest of the day, when considerable discernment is needed to distinguish according to the thickness or faintness of the shadow. If so, it appears that in the morning and evening, when the shadow is very long, one may properly rely even nowadays on seeing the shadow.
