A war was not established. It is explained in the Gemara that if it is not known whether there is war in the world, we need not ask her. This is what it says there: the Tanna taught peace in the world because he wanted to teach war in the world, as Nimukei Yosef wrote. This law is an unresolved question: is she believed through a migo, since if she wished she could have said there is peace in the world, or perhaps, since war has been established, she speaks by conjecture and a migo does not help because she is not lying but speaking by conjecture. Therefore, if she established that there was war and says, I buried him, even according to the Rambam she is believed, for with I buried him the concern is that perhaps she is lying; if so, the above migo applies. Even according to the second reason I wrote above, I buried him helps if she herself established the war. But Levush wrote another reason: when she says, I buried him in war, we say that because she is panicked to flee she speaks by conjecture and buried another man, thinking he was her husband. According to this, a migo would not help even in such a case, but I do not know from where he derived this reason.
