And she says with certainty that he need not leave. See Beit Shmuel s.k. 113, who compelled the conclusion that Rabbeinu Chananel holds in the law of a witness who testifies that if she married she must leave. In my humble opinion this is not compelled. One may say that he holds there that she need not leave, and this is because we compare this to one suspected regarding a maidservant, not to one suspected regarding a married woman, as stated in Nimukei Yosef there: regarding one suspected with a married woman there are witnesses to unseemly conduct and grounds for the matter, unlike a witness who testifies. His words are copied in Beit Shmuel siman 12. One may say that specifically where he testifies alone and there is no one contradicting him, there is room to distinguish between him and one suspected with a married woman; but where there is also one who contradicts him, he may be compared to one suspected with a married woman, for this too is called grounds for the matter. Therefore, if she married she must leave. What he inferred from responsum Rosh I did not understand at all, for there is a practical difference where the witness had a wife, as explained in siman 12 seif 4. The Ran cited one of two reasons and did not come to teach his own view or Rabbeinu Chananel's view, with a practical difference where he did not have a wife. According to this, it is possible that there is no dispute at all; even the Rambam, who rules in siman 12 that she need not leave, may agree here, where there is contradiction, that she must leave like Rabbeinu Chananel. In any event, one need not establish the case with three witnesses; rather, it can be established where the witness had a wife, and this is clear.
