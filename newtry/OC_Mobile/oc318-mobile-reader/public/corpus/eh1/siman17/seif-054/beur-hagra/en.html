There are those who are stringent. This follows from the statement there, 'Woe for the swift horseman,' etc. It is also somewhat implied by the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who write, 'Woe, woe for so-and-so,' etc. They similarly explain the statement, 'A pity for,' etc., meaning that he did me a kindness, etc. Addendum: this is also implied in Gittin 28b, 'The established rule is,' etc. Yevamos 25b apparently supports the latter view, but the Nimukei Yosef and Shitah Mekubetzes explain that since he said, 'Tell the wife,' etc., he intended to testify. Likewise, the Mishnah's statement, 'He died; I killed him,' etc., indicates intent to testify, paralleling the first clause concerning one who brings a get. Therefore the Gemara asks, 'He himself,' etc. Kesubos 26a also somewhat implies this from the example of incidental speech, 'like this,' etc., and Rabbi Chiya, etc., for all of that is otherwise superfluous, especially Rabbi Chiya's incident. The primary ruling follows the first opinion, as the Gemara asks, 'Where is this found? In incidental speech such as,' etc.; this shows that in the first incident it stated the core rule without qualification. The same applies to the incident of Chasa and to Gittin, chapter 3, 'The established rule is,' etc.
