Because a rumor had gone out that he died. See Beit Shmuel in the name of Responsa Beit Yosef; his wording is somewhat obscure, and Responsa Maharam Lublin siman 128 clarifies that it accords with Taz s.k. 8. Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 41 writes that Maharai's responsum might seem to imply concern even for an unspecified death rumor, but distinguishes that case as involving a gentile speaking incidentally, where all agree there is reason for concern, unlike a Jew who comes to testify. Machatzit HaShekel further explains that this rule applies only to someone who did not come before court to testify, but merely wrote or said to another in ordinary conversation or complaint, which resembles incidental speech and was not intended as testimony. If, however, someone came before court to testify or to permit the woman and stated simply that the husband died, then all agree we do not suspect that he testified merely from rumor. If the rumor was already known where he testified, he would have added nothing; and even if the rumor circulated only in the place from which he came, everyone knows that a court does not rely on rumor and that testimony must report the facts as they occurred. The same applies where testimony is sent to the court in writing. Responsa Masat Binyamin siman 105 is very strict, requiring even a gentile speaking incidentally to say at least that he knows clearly that the man died, lest he merely heard a vague report. Taz rejects this stringency, and Machatzit HaShekel writes that Masat Binyamin himself appears to have retreated from it in siman 109. In the addenda to Machatzit HaShekel, Seder Eliyahu distinguishes three cases: first, when a rumor says the man drowned, or the witness himself says he drowned and then died, all require explicit evidence of death, such as the sea casting him up or his leaving the water alive and later dying. Second, where it was already known that he entered a place of danger and a rumor of death then spread, a plain statement that he died may be based on general assumption; but if only the danger was known and no death rumor had spread, we do not suspect that the witness inferred death from the danger. Third, where a rumor says that a traveler from one place to another died, later authorities dispute whether a plain statement of death is suspected to rest on rumor. Seder Eliyahu adds that the dispute concerns a witness whose main purpose is to report the death. If the speaker's main purpose is another matter and the death is mentioned only incidentally, all agree there is concern that he called the man dead merely because of the rumor, especially where the other matter only makes sense if the man is assumed dead. These are matters of sound reasoning, and he also discusses gentile incidental speech in this context.
