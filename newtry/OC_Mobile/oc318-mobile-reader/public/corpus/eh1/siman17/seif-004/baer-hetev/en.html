Even incidentally it does not help — Kenesset HaGedolah in name of many poskim; see responsum Sha'ar Efraim siman 105 and responsum Shevut Ya'akov ch. 1 siman 99 and ch. 2 siman 115; see Beit Shmuel. And if this husband died and she married another — these women testify and even if they were enemies before — as brought in ch. 12 of Maharik and Rambam ch. 7 of Laws of Testimony it is clear a co-wife does not bring her the get even though the co-wife is now married to another; and above siman 141 seif 94. And Maggid Mishneh wrote there we learn laws there from laws here — accordingly it implies a co-wife is not believed even if she is now married to another.
Her mother-in-law. She'erit Yosef wrote males similar to these women mentioned such as her father-in-law and her husband's son are believed. However the yavam is not believed to testify to permit her to himself; but when his brother had children he is believed to permit her to others. And Choshen Mishpat wrote even if he had children he would not testify to permit — see there. Also uncertain in our time since we do not perform yibbum whether the yavam is believed to testify his brother died for chalitzah to the yevamah — Beit Shmuel disputes all his words; see responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 88 and responsum Mahari Mintz siman 10.
Her mother-in-law. The reason they said in Yevamot 114a: it is on her mind perhaps her husband will die and she will fall before her son and become his daughter-in-law — and according to this reason if she is not fit for yibbum to her son for reason of forbidden relation or another reason she testifies for her; and this law is a question in the Gemara — Beit Yosef ruled to be lenient. And Choshen Mishpat wrote possibly in these lands where yibbum is not practiced this reason does not apply — possibly rely on Beit Yosef's words and she testifies; Beit Shmuel disputes; see in Ra'ash 174.
