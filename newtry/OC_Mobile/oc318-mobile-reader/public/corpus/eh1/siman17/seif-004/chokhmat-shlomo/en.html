Seif 3 — A woman whose husband went to a distant land and they testified he died, etc. Behold in Yevamot (93b) it is asked regarding testimony of death whether it is because of almoni or because of dayika; and I wondered: why is it not resolved from there — behold the view of Rav Acha in chapter 1 of Sanhedrin that from Torah even one is fit to judge; how is one witness believed to extract money? I already challenged thus in my composition Turei Zahav on Yoreh De'ah 100 laws of bekhor in responsum 130 and wrote the reason is because of almoni; if so it is proven that for money one witness is believed by almoni; behold a matter of forbidden relations is derived matter from matter from money; if so here too he is believed by almoni. This is a question; one may say the Gemara's questions refer to that opinion in Sanhedrin that from Torah requires three; with this it is a question. Rambam ruled that the question was resolved; and it is true per what I wrote in several responsa already that not every matter of almoni is equal — there is almoni that helps from Torah as in ashtamud'inanhu, and there is not almoni immediately such as woman's testimony — here it does not help from Torah; yet it is not settled that in chapter 4 of Yevamot R' Eliezer and Ravina dispute regarding ashtamud'inanhu; and if two are required why does it not say it is proven R' Eliezer holds one suffices, for he holds one judges from Torah by almoni; and it is proven in any case where almoni immediately helps with one — how can one think here two are required; strained to say rabbinically two are required, for regarding woman's testimony the rabbis were lenient and from Torah we say they were more stringent here; behold how many leniencies they were lenient regarding woman's testimony. Further difficulty for Rivash who holds one does not help in ashtamud'inanhu except if they testify before the act and not at the time of the act — and from Torah one suffices; strained to say Rivash follows the opinion that requires three from Torah — uncertain in all this. And behold Tosafot there s.v. one witness with a yevamah — even according to the opinion that because of almoni one also needs the reason of dayika; only it is asked whether a small dayika suffices or not — see there; and apparently it lacks reason: if because of almoni he is believed from Torah — why need the reason of dayika? But the intent is per what we learn in Bekhorot (36b) there — since he has a claimant he trembles; it is proven explicitly there that one must distinguish regarding almoni between there is a claimant or not; therefore here too if dayika is applicable almoni helps, but if dayika is inapplicable almoni does not help. And behold one may say Rambam's words that he wrote the reason one witness is believed is because of almoni — all wondered on him, for in the Gemara it is an unresolved question; per the above it appears Rambam derived this from the sugya of Bekhorot where there are two versions for one version almoni helps even without a claimant, and for one version almoni does not help except where there is a claimant; and so too should be the Gemara's question in Yevamot whether the reason one witness is believed is because of almoni even without dayika like one version in Bekhorot that even without a claimant almoni helps, or only because of dayika — because there is a claimant almoni helps but without a claimant almoni does not help; and the Gemara's question is whether all like the first version in Bekhorot or the second; and if so since the Gemara concludes in Bekhorot the halakhah is like the first version that almoni helps without a claimant — we hold also one witness for a woman that almoni helps even without dayika; and from this Rambam derived thus. And in truth in Tosafot Bekhorot there they sensed from woman's testimony that almoni does not help except because of dayika; and in my humble opinion from this Rambam derived his ruling — we hold like avdei legluyei — examine well. And behold on what is asked in the Gemara there, etc., and we rule stringently — see in my edition 9 in my responsum to the country of Russia in my composition siman 219 where I wrote something new to distinguish, with God's help, where the witness is from a nearby place where it appears to court he would be shamed if the opposite were revealed — then from Torah he would be believed because of almoni, only rabbinically he is not believed because we decree lest it be a distant place; therefore because of dayika they established it on Torah and he is believed even rabbinically; but if the witness is in a distant place where it appears to court he would not be shamed if his falsehood were revealed — then from Torah he is not believed at all, only rabbinically he is believed because of dayika; and there is great practical difference between Torah prohibition and rabbinic — as I wrote there with God's help; see and examine. Behold with one witness for a husband's death there are opinions whether it is from Torah or rabbinically; and I saw in Sefer Neta Sha'ashuim part Even HaEzer siman 62 that he challenged those who hold it is from Torah — and witness from witness is certainly rabbinic; if so why do we say in Shevuot that all agree regarding testimony of death he is exempt — for he spoke to her and not to court; behold in gambling with dice since from Torah he is fit he is liable for an oath; if so here too from Torah her statement is ineffective — to obligate an oath see there; and in my humble opinion leave it in uncertainty; and in truth after examination there is no beginning to his difficulty — for there in gambling with dice since now they do not collect this debt since there are no witnesses — ultimately the court loses through him by his not testifying; therefore although rabbinically disqualified, since from Torah he is fit he is liable for an oath; but with a woman — since in any case rabbinically she is believed to say — she can go to court to testify and collect her ketubah; therefore she will not ultimately lose money through him; therefore even from Torah there is no oath liability here — it is as if it is known the borrower will pay him without witnesses — the oath is only denial of the matter in general; so too here — examine well. And what I wrote above in seif 2 "but where there is one to support her," etc. — see Beit Shmuel who brought a dispute on this; and see in my composition on Choshen Mishpat edition 3 in responsum 130 to Kolinits in Choshen Mishpat siman 87 what I wrote as proof for those who hold even with one witness she does not act brazenly — see there where I extended on this and proved from the Gemara and Tosafot with God's help; examine.
