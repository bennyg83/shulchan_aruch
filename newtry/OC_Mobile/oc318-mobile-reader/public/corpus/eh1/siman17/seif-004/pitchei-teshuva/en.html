You divorced me; she is believed. See responsum Minchat Ani siman 64, where he was asked regarding what we rule, that a woman who says to her husband in his presence, you divorced me, is believed because of the presumption that she would not be so brazen: is there a distinction here between retroactive effect and future effect, as we distinguish regarding a husband who says, I divorced my wife, where he is believed only for the future? This refers to the Gemara in Bava Batra 134b; see below, siman 152 seif 1 and the Beit Shmuel there. Or perhaps there it is different, because the credibility is based on his power to divorce her, and that reason applies only to the future; but here the reason is that she would not be brazen, so one could say that she would also not be brazen to advance the date. He answered that at first glance there is some proof that this presumption, that she would not be brazen, helps even retroactively. Otherwise, according to the view in Bava Batra that when a husband says retroactively, I divorced her, he is not believed even for the future because we do not divide his statement, and just as he is not believed retroactively so too he is not believed for the future, then when a woman says to her husband in his presence, you divorced me retroactively, we should say the same: we do not divide her statement, and she should not be believed at all, even for the future. Something like this should have been explained by the Talmud and the poskim. However, after analysis it appears that she is not believed retroactively. The reason is simple and compelled by Rav Hamnuna's language, for he said: there is a presumption that a woman does not brazenly face her husband. This shows that it is specifically before her husband, but before one who is not her husband she can be brazen. According to this, regarding the essential divorce, if we say she is lying and he did not divorce her, then he is her husband, and she cannot be brazen before her husband; therefore she is believed. But regarding advancing the date, one may well say she is lying and he did not divorce her at the time she says, but rather only now, a day or two ago; see Sefer Me'orei Tzedek s.k. 6. Even if you say that advancing the date is also considered brazenness, nevertheless it is not brazenness before her husband, for in any case now he is not her husband and is like another man. With this it is also resolved that even according to the view in Bava Batra that we do not divide a statement, here she is properly believed for the future. We do not say that just as she is not believed retroactively regarding advancing the date, so too she should not be believed for the future. That is not so, for the main reason she is not believed retroactively is only because we say she is now divorced and he is not her husband, and therefore she can be brazen. If so, how can you say she is not believed for the future? For then he is her husband, and how could she be brazen? This is not comparable at all to the case in Bava Batra where the husband says, I divorced her, for there the lack of credibility retroactively does not depend on credibility for the future; therefore we properly say that we do not divide his statement and he is not believed in either respect. But here, the main reason she is not believed retroactively is only because of the credibility for the future, etc.; see there.
