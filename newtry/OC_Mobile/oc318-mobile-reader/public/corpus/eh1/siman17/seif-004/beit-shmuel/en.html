Except for the five women. If this husband died and she married another, these women may testify, even though they hated her before; so wrote Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12. But in the Rambam, chapter 10, it is explained that her co-wife may not bring this one's get, even though her co-wife is now married to another man. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that he learned the laws there from the laws here. According to this, it apparently implies that she is not believed even if she is now married to another man. Below, siman 141, the Mechaber ruled that she is not believed even if she is married to another man. Perhaps she may not testify regarding that husband because she wants her co-wife to be forbidden to him as well. Responsum Shaar Ephraim siman 105 wrote that a co-wife may not testify even if she is speaking innocently. This is also proven from responsum Rivash, for he brings proof that if a gentile says, so-and-so died, it is considered innocent speech even without connected contextual words, from what appears in the Gemara at the end of Yevamot: if a voice was heard saying, so-and-so died, they allow her to marry. The Gemara asks: perhaps it was a demon; perhaps it was a co-wife. It did not ask: perhaps it was a gentile. This shows that even if it was a gentile she is permitted, because contextual words are not required and it is properly innocent speech. If you say that a co-wife speaking innocently is believed, then what is the Gemara asking, perhaps it was a co-wife? Rather, this proves that a co-wife is not believed even if she is speaking innocently. See more on this below.
And these are: her mother-in-law. The reason is explained in the Gemara: she says, this woman will consume all my labor and toil; the daughter of her mother-in-law says, this woman will inherit my father's and mother's labor; her yevamah says, this woman will become my co-wife; and her husband's daughter says, this woman comes in place of my mother. One might ask: if her yevamah is afraid that she may become her co-wife, what does her testimony accomplish, since we hold that when a woman underwent yibbum by mistake she is permitted to her husband, as written in siman 156? Tosafot in Yevamot 117 answers that nevertheless she has embarrassment, and also perhaps her husband will perform chalitzah and she will marry another, becoming forbidden to both. Shiltei Gibborim wrote that males corresponding to these women mentioned, such as her father-in-law, the son of her mother-in-law, and her husband's son, are believed. However, the yavam is not believed to testify in order to permit her to himself; but if his brother had children, he is believed to permit her to others. Darkei Moshe brought his words, and it implies that her yevamah is not believed even if this woman has children, for the yavam testifies where his brother has children, which implies that her yevamah does not testify. Perhaps the reason is that there is still concern that her children may die and she will become her co-wife. As for the unresolved question of a mother-in-law afterward, where her thought is that her husband has already died, Rashi already answered that the pain of a yevamah is bodily pain, while the pain of a mother-in-law is only because of money. One may also say that when the Sages said she shall not testify, we say they did not distinguish, as written nearby in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. But regarding men, the Sages said nothing; therefore we say that where there is suspicion he should not testify, but where there is no concern he may testify. Chelkat Mechokek is uncertain nowadays, when we do not perform yibbum, whether the yavam is believed to testify that his brother died in order to perform chalitzah for the yevamah. Although the yevamah cannot testify even nowadays, as implied by the Tur, who wrote in this siman that his yevamah does not testify, implying even nowadays, and the Maggid Mishneh in siman 287 also wrote that her yevamah who is her sister may not testify, which shows they did not distinguish between one yevamah and another, nevertheless one should not compare the yavam to the yevamah, as I wrote. However, there in Chelkat Mechokek it implies that even if he had children he would not rule permissively. At first glance it appears that even one who fears ruling should have no concern in this, as Shiltei Gibborim wrote explicitly. See responsum Mahari Mintz siman 14 and responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 88.
Even if she is not now her mother-in-law. There it is an unresolved question. The Beit Yosef wrote that the Rif and Rambam rule leniently, while the Tur, Ri"ו, and Maggid Mishneh rule stringently. The reason is that she anticipates that the husband may die and she will fall before the yavam, who is her son. If she is forbidden to her son as an ervah, Chelkat Mechokek ruled that she may testify for her. But according to what I wrote nearby in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, it is not a simple law that she may testify, for one can say that even here they made no distinction.
