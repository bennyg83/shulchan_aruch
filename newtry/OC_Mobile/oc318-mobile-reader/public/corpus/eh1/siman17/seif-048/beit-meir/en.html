Gloss: however, because of gossip. The Beit Shmuel wrote: according to this it appears, etc. According to this, one must say that what Tosafot wrote in Yevamot 78, that when the witnesses came one after the other she does not leave, whose implication is even where they had not permitted her, follows the answer of Ri. On this implication, that when Tosafot wrote one after the other she does not leave, it means even where they had not permitted her, the Beit Shmuel wrote in s.k. 111 that Tosafot wrote that she does not leave only specifically when both came at one time, like the view of Shiltei Gibborim. He means that at one time is specifically within the time of speaking of the other. This is very difficult to me. If so, what Tosafot wrote in responsum, that if one and one came at one time she does not leave, and if they came one after the other she is permitted to marry by Torah law were it not for gossip - according to his implication, the first one after the other must mean where they had not permitted her, and the second means where they had permitted her; but the main point is missing. Furthermore, according to his words, these words follow the view of Ri, that gossip is not a prohibition, as Maharsha z"l proves in Ri's answer. Any reader will see that Maharsha's proofs are because it appears that according to Ri's explanation, regarding a sotah, even literally at the same time, the one who is believed like two is considered like two. The same should apply to testimony about a woman: even at the same time, the witness who permits is considered like two by Torah law, and the distinction is only rabbinic. Therefore it seemed reasonable to him that what we learned, a witness and a witness, we do not consider, is only a rabbinic prohibition. Consequently, once they permitted her, the prohibition of gossip is necessarily light, not even a rabbinic prohibition, but only good counsel. In my humble opinion, even the glosses of Maharsha and his explanation are not correct. What he wrote, that it is compelled that even where a witness is believed only rabbinically he is considered like two, since the Mishnah did not teach, one witness says he died and two say he did not die, that the child is a mamzer, apparently in his view in truth the child is at least not a mamzer by Torah law because the one who says he died is considered like two, making it two against two. In my humble opinion this is certainly not so. It is obvious that he is a complete mamzer by Torah law, for Ulla's law does not apply at all in the presence of two witnesses, as is proven from the question, it is obvious, for the words of one do not stand in the place of two. See Rashi's explanation there. According to Maharsha's view, it should have said, it is obvious, for they are two against two. This is also difficult for the explanation of the Mishneh LaMelekh z"l; see and examine carefully. Without doubt to me there is a scribal error in Tosafot, and the main intent of Ri is according to the simple implication: to answer the first difficulty, for it seemed to him that if she married she should not leave by law, not merely because of gossip, like the Rosh's intent, that gossip is only as explained there. He holds that the plain meaning of our Talmud is so, and the question is proper. What Abaye answered in Ketubot according to Rabbi Yochanan, who said she should not marry, is rejected by Rava's answer there, like the Rambam's reasoning. Nevertheless the Rosh wrote the gossip concern due to another reason, based on the words of Rav Asi, which are certainly halakhah; the remaining words are in error. If so, what is this that Tosafot wrote, one and one at one time is permitted? Therefore, in my humble opinion their words here are obscure, but their intent is clearer from Ketubot and Sotah: it is not called one after the other unless they had permitted her. Any witness who came before they permitted her is called at one time, and even if she married she must leave. In this they disagree with the Rosh, for they make the gossip into a prohibition, whereas the Rosh, in order to avoid Tosafot's difficulty, makes the gossip only good counsel. This approach, that wherever there was no permission in between the law is like at one time, and even if she married she must leave, because Ulla's law does not apply in testimony about a woman, which is rabbinic, except where they came one after another through permission, is compelled in Ketubot. Otherwise, if one follows Shiltei Gibborim, that every one-after-another case, even without permission, means she may not marry but if she married she need not leave, it is difficult: from where did Ketubot force Rabbi Yochanan to say that she may not marry because of gossip, and why does it ask, if so, even initially as well? In truth Shiltei Gibborim does not mention gossip and permits where they permitted her even initially. One may say in his view that what he wrote, where they had not permitted her she may not marry, is because of gossip, and he distinguishes in gossip between where they permitted her and where they did not; but this is a rejected reasoning. According to Tosafot in Yevamot, where it is clear that even after they permitted her there is gossip, and this is Rabbi Yochanan's proof, how is it possible that where they did not permit her she does not leave by law, and if she married she does not leave? If so, what does the Gemara ask, if so, even initially as well? From where does it know that Rabbi Yochanan speaks where they came one after the other with permission? Perhaps he speaks where they had not permitted her. Necessarily, wherever they had not permitted her, it is literally like at one time, and if she married she must leave. Therefore the case must speak with permission, and the question is proper, forcing the explanation of gossip. In this way one must also press the view of Tosafot in Yevamot, s.v. one witness, so that they not contradict their view explained in the above places; perhaps this is their intent, see there. What the Beit Shmuel wrote in s.k. 111, that so it appears in the Tur, for in the first clause he did not write that if she married she must leave, this inference is nothing. Since he rules like the Rambam, that even at one time, if she married the witness she does not leave, and they inferred this from the Mishnah, that it did not teach, one witness says he died and one witness says he did not die, she may not marry and if she married she must leave, because that is not always so, the Tur therefore wrote in the first clause only: but if the witness came before they permitted her, she may not marry. This is the language of the Mishnah. It is the same law he wrote afterward: one witness says he died and one witness says he did not die, etc. According to the Beit Shmuel's inference, he should have been obligated to write explicitly, at least, at one time. Rather, this proves that in the Tur's view there is no distinction between literally at one time and one after another without permission. What he further wrote, that so wrote the Rambam, that if two came as one she leaves, is difficult: just as he inferred leniently from the final clause, he should infer stringently from the first clause, for in the first clause he wrote, and they permitted her. According to the Beit Shmuel's view that there is a middle law, namely that she may not marry, but if she married she does not leave, in the case of one after another without permission, why did the Rambam not explain it? Rather, nothing can be inferred from this. Wherever she had not been permitted, he calls it came as one. Ketubot and Sotah are not like Rashi's explanation there, according to which it is called came as one only within the time of speaking. If the Rambam's intent were like the Beit Shmuel, he should have explained within the time of speaking. Also, the difficulty I raised above from Ketubot would apply to him. So I found in Responsa Ramban siman 119, and likewise in Responsa Ran siman 47, that wherever she had not been permitted it is like at one time, and if she married she must leave. So too it is proven from the Yerushalmi cited by the Maggid Mishneh. It states there: one witness says he died, and she married, and another witness came and said he did not die, she does not leave. Because it said after she married, it implies that if he said it before she married, she must leave. Until here the sages disagreed only because she married the witness who testified, but if she married another, they agree that she leaves. This is difficult: certainly said before she married is similar to married and afterward said, namely that it was only one after the other. If so, if one after another without permission also means she does not leave if she married, why must this woman leave even if she married another, and all the more so according to Rabbi Meir if she married the witness? Rather, this proves that every case without permission is exactly like at one time. In my humble opinion, Shiltei Gibborim is a lone opinion and is not halakhah. In my humble opinion, from this Yerushalmi one may also infer according to the Rambam's explanation that if she married on her own after the witness's statement without permission of bet din, and afterward another witness came and said he did not die, she must leave, and the marriage itself is not considered like permission of bet din. For Rabbi Yochanan said this is the view of Rabbi Meir, and permitted married and afterward said only because she married the witness; but if she married another, she leaves. If marriage on her own counted like permission of bet din, then even if she married another she should not leave. The sages who disagree with him also disagree only where she married the witness, for even literally at one time she does not leave; but where she married another, it implies that all agree that even if she married and afterward he said it, she must leave. See Beit Shmuel s.k. 123. The some say opinion in the Shulchan Aruch there must disagree with the Rambam in interpreting the Yerushalmi. I later saw that in Chiddushei Ramban he wrote about the Yerushalmi: perhaps what it says, before she married, means where they had permitted her to marry, and he does not hold Ulla's law. If so, since he does not hold Ulla's law, perhaps for us who do hold Ulla's law, marriage too counts like permission. But the Maggid Mishneh already testified that the Ramban's explanation is not primary, and in my poverty it is not understandable to me at all. See Responsa Rivash siman 379.
