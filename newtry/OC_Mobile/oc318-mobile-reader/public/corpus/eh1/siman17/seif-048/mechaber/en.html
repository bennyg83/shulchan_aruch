In what case is a woman believed to say "my husband died" when there is peace between him and her and peace in the world; but if there is a quarrel between them — such as if she said "you divorced me before so-and-so and so-and-so" and those witnesses came and contradicted her (or her husband apostatized and left her an agunah) (Hagahot Alfasi) — and afterward she and her husband went to another country and she came and said "my husband died" — she is not believed (and even if she said "I buried him") (Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 13 of laws of divorce) — even though there is peace in the world. And even if one witness came and testified for her that her husband died — she may not marry, lest she hired him. And if she married she need not leave, for behold she has a witness. And likewise if there was war in the world and she came and said "my husband died in war" — she is not believed even though there is peace between them; she should not rely on matters that mostly indicate death and say "died" — such as the first and last were killed and her husband was among them, for she says "since these and these were killed, he was killed among them" — therefore she is not believed, even if she says "I buried him." And some say if she said "I buried him" she is believed; and if she said "died on his bed" she is believed according to all. {Rama: And likewise all who say he died or was killed far from the war are believed — for it is not relevant to say "in a manner of doubt" except when she says he died or was killed in actual war; but not in war — even if he went there near the battle line to take spoils — she is believed (in Mordechai chapter HaIshah Batra in the name of Rabeinu Yehudah).}
