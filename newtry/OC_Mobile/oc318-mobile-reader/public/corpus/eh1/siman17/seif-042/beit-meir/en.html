Perhaps he emerged from another place. The Rosh wrote in Perek HaCholetz, halachah 5, that we do not follow the majority of infants who are viable, because the minority of nonviable births is common, and we are concerned for a common minority, as with water that has no end, where his wife is forbidden. This appears to contradict what he wrote in the first chapter of Chullin, halachah 16, where he concludes that therefore if she married she need not leave in water that has no end, and we do not say to join the minority to the presumption of a married woman, because the minority who escape is uncommon. See Mishmeret HaBayit, folio 8. That is the main point: it is uncommon. The reason she is forbidden ab initio is as explained in Tosafot Chullin there and in several places in Yevamot, Bekhorot 20, and Bava Metzia 20b, that because of the severity of forbidden relations we are stringent ab initio.
