And if she married, she must leave. Meaning, even though she married permissibly on the basis of the first two witnesses, she must leave that permission because of the later witnesses. We say that she need not leave only with invalid witnesses, but when the later witnesses are valid they remove her, for it is a case of two witnesses against two witnesses, which is a doubt, and she remains in her presumption as a married woman. So too wrote Chelkat Mechokek. See Tosafot in the sugya in chapter 2 of Ketubot. In responsum Rivash siman 379 he wrote that even if they permitted her on the basis of two witnesses and afterward two witnesses came and said he did not die, she is forbidden to marry. This is unlike permission based on one witness followed by another witness who says he is alive; there, through the permission, the first witness becomes like two, and it is as though there is no doubt in the matter. Likewise, wherever it says she need not leave, if they merely permitted her, she should not marry. However, with a captive woman the permission helps: even though witnesses to captivity came after the permission, she remains permitted, because their testimony does not contradict the permission, for it is possible that although she was captive she did not commit zenut. Therefore the permission helps there, unless witnesses to defilement came.
