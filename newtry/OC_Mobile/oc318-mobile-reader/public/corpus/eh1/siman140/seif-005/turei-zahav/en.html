(ד) And some are stringent even in this. This is Ran in a responsum brought by Beit Yosef. Ran cites the view of R. Peretz HaKohen: although the Gemara asks whether one who acquires a get for a woman in a place of quarrel has effected acquisition for her, and concludes that even so it is a liability because of the principle that a woman prefers any marriage to being alone, nevertheless the early authorities wrote that where it is known to all that it is a benefit, such as with an apostate husband, it is a benefit for her, because that principle does not apply; everyone knows she does not want to live with him, and death is preferable to him. So too in this case, where we know that she requested the get many times and see that his intent is to benefit her, one may acquire through an agent for her. Ran disagrees and writes that since the Gemara concluded that even in a place of quarrel it is a liability, we say this generally, even where she demands divorce. He further cites Rashba that even if she demands divorce, one cannot acquire a get for her without her knowledge, because perhaps she changed her mind or wants to delay for ketubah or other reasons. Taz explains the dispute: according to the lenient view, where benefit is absolutely clear, the agent can acquire for her; according to Ran, the Sages treated divorce as inherently a liability unless she appoints the agent herself, since there may be hidden reasons not to receive it now. The note applies this to cases of apostasy, repeated requests for divorce, and whether beit din can presume benefit. Taz leans to caution with Ran where the woman did not appoint the agent, because a get requires her legal receipt and the default rule remains that divorce is a liability, not a benefit, unless the facts remove every possible concern.
