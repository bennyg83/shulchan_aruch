(א) The husband must bring proof. We say "here the defects were found, here they were"; therefore the husband must bring proof that his acquisition was mistaken. If she is a bogeret, even if she is in her father's house she is considered as in the husband's domain, and the husband must bring proof. In all these cases it makes no difference where she is physically standing: before marriage she is in her father's domain even if she is in the husband's house; after marriage, or if she is a bogeret, even if she is in her father's house she is not in her father's domain, and the husband must bring proof. See the Rosh. If the husband brings proof that these defects existed in her father's house, we do not say "here they were found and here they were before erusin," because she has two chazakot: bodily chazakah that she was born without defects, and the chazakah that a person does not drink from a cup unless he examined it. Against this stands the chazakah that a person is not appeased by defects. Where there are competing chazakot, the burden remains on the husband. The note continues by comparing the sugya's answers of Rava and Rav Ashi, the question whether the ketubah belonged to the father or to her when the doubt arose, and Rama's rule in Yoreh Deah that a doubt is judged from the time it arose. The conclusion is that for the principal ketubah the husband must prove the defects preceded erusin; for the additional ketubah, bodily chazakah may not help in the same way, because the husband's consent to add was made without knowledge of the defects.
