It is stated in the Tur, Choshen Mishpat, siman 245, se'if 4, and the Rama writes there: If there was a debt or a woman’s ketubah against the first recipient, he does not collect anything from these assets, because they belong to the second recipient. Maharam of Rothenburg did not write this way, for he wrote in a responsum regarding one who gave land to his son and stipulated that if the son died without children, the gift should return to his daughters. The son died without children, and they wished to remove the ketubah lien from that land. Maharam wrote that the widow collects from it. The implication is that according to the Rama, Maharam’s ruling cannot stand. I am very astonished, for on the contrary, from Maharam himself, as cited by the Mordechai in chapter Yesh Nochalin and by Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 10 of Hilchot Zechiyah, it is proven that he too holds like the Rama. Rather, in that case his reason was that since it was a gift to his son, who was fit to inherit him, the matter is only because of inheritance, and inheritance has no interruption. The later recipient does not acquire, and it is as if it fully belonged to him. This is unlike a case where the recipient is not fit to inherit. See there at the beginning of the siman. Both of these are the words of the living God. It is even more astonishing that in Darkei Moshe there, se'if 1, he writes: “The Mordechai wrote,” and so on, “and Maharam ruled,” and then says to see below, se'if 7, where the Tur brings the responsum of Maharam and writes that there are those who disagree. It must be that this refers to the Rama. But once the Darkei Moshe brings the reason that inheritance has no interruption, I do not know who disagrees. Similarly, one must wonder about the Rosh, of blessed memory, who brings these words of Maharam as proof for his matter. See there, kelal 84, and the Tur brings it. But according to the explanation of Maharam’s reason, there is no proof from it at all. This requires study.
