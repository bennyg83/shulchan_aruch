“Some say that if he died,” and so on, “since they were encumbered.” See Darkei Moshe, and see Chelkat Mechokek’s explanation of the Rama, of blessed memory, according to the answer of Maharil. What the Rama wrote, “but in a place,” and so on, “she collects from there,” means only what is actually held beyond the amount of the ketubah. In my humble opinion, if this is his intent, the main point is missing. Furthermore, although the language of Maharam somewhat implies a distinction, that if there is actually held property beyond the ketubah, the ketubah lien does not harm the remainder by making it “fit to come,” since he wrote “even if there is actually held property beyond her ketubah,” see siman 102 for the explanation, nevertheless the Gemara’s difficulty in chapter Chezkat HaBatim, page 55, “If so, you have nullified the inheritance of a firstborn son,” does not imply this. Rather, wherever the lien has the power to make property “fit to come,” as long as he did not remove it during his lifetime, all his property, even that which exceeds the debt, is considered fit to come. See there, and see the responsum of Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 149. See below, siman 163. It appears from the Darkei Moshe that he did not consider the answer of Maharil to be primary, and was satisfied with his own answer alone, since he writes, “Perhaps here it speaks in a place that follows the people of Judea.” According to Maharil’s answer, this would not be necessary. Here, he is speaking of a place where they cannot remove her by paying her ketubah, and specifically because such a maintenance lien has no fixed amount it is called fit to come. The law in siman 90, however, speaks in a place where they can remove her, and the maintenance lien is no stronger than a ketubah and other debts, and the property is therefore called actually held. For this reason, in siman 90 the Rama writes “even if they were encumbered to the ketubah,” but omits “and to maintenance,” unlike the Mordechai, because regarding maintenance the matter is not fixed and depends on local custom. However, this requires study, for the Maharam in chapter 25 brings proofs. One proof is that otherwise the inheritance of the firstborn son would be nullified. This is the language of the Gemara and Tosafot, as above. According to the Rama’s answer, this is difficult: If so, in a place that follows the people of Galilee, the inheritance of the firstborn son would be nullified. This proof can perhaps be rejected. One may say that specifically regarding the father, who was already in possession of the property, liens that come upon the property do not remove it from his possession and make it considered fit to come with respect to him. It is properly called actually held regarding the firstborn. But regarding the heir, where there are liens on the property from the deceased, it is not called actually held in his hand because of the earlier liens from the deceased that prevent the heir’s possession from taking effect. This kind of reasoning is found in Bava Metzia 70b, in Tosafot, s.v. ve'i, regarding firstborn inheritance in relation to a non-Jew’s lien. But he brings another proof from chapter Mi Shemet, from that which we learned: If the daughter married and died, his widow is maintained from his property. It is proven that the property remains with the husband. Why do we not say that after the daughter’s death, it should return to her heirs, since a husband does not inherit property that is only fit to come? Rather, it must be that nevertheless the property is considered actually held by the daughter. According to the answer of the Darkei Moshe, this is difficult. How can we say that the baraita is established according to the people of Judea, against the halacha? However, there is truly reason to wonder: Since we rule in siman 93, se'if 15, that the orphans must bring proof, according to the Gemara’s simple assumption that the property is in her possession, how can it be actually held for two parties, the widow and the heirs? It is obvious and logical that for the heirs it is only fit to come. If so, see siman 93, se'if 19, and siman 95, se'if 1. According to the second answer of Tosafot, which I have proven is the main answer, one can say that what the Gemara assumes simply, that the property is in her possession, is only according to the people of Galilee, whose view is halacha. If so, we abandon a forced reading of the verse and establish it according to its straightforward meaning. Therefore, the baraita in Mi Shemet follows the people of Judea, and the property is not in the widow’s possession but in the daughter’s possession. If it followed the people of Galilee, there would be the difficulty above. Therefore Maharam ruled plainly that they are truly fit to come for the heir, because they are in the possession of the widow. The answer of the Rama fits well. In truth, Maharil’s answer is very difficult to say about Maharam. In chapter 25 he expanded the explanation with proofs, yet in chapter Na'arah he would write a law based only on his own reasoning, with no proof, and that reasoning is weak and forced. It is difficult to know from where he derived it. However, in siman 111, se'if 8, I brought a proof for Maharil’s answer from the Rosh. The answer of the Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah cited in the Beit Shmuel, that here it speaks where she took an oath, also cannot settle the matter, for Maharam tied his reason to the maintenance lien, and regarding maintenance the conclusion of most decisors in siman 93 is that she does not need an oath.
