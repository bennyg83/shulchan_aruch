“Unless they first made an acquisition from her.” The language of the Rama in chapter 17 of Hilchot Ishut is: But if they first made an acquisition from the woman that she has no lien on this place, and afterwards the husband sold it, she does not seize it. The Beit Yosef, in siman 90 in Bedek HaBayit, derives from this that the Rama disagrees with the responsum of the Rosh, who wrote that if she removed her claim with respect to the husband, it does not help, because it is similar to a case where she sold to her husband, and she can say, “I only did it to give him satisfaction.” According to the Rama, this too is called a case where one bought from the woman and then returned and bought from the man. Nevertheless, the Beit Yosef concludes that even according to the Rosh, it is only where she removed her claim with respect to the husband that it does not help. But if the husband wishes to borrow, and the lender is concerned that perhaps the husband will die and his widow will collect her ketubah and the lender will lose his debt, and therefore the woman removes the ketubah lien from her husband’s property or from land designated for her, then in this case, since she is removing her lien until this debt is collected, and they made an acquisition from her, it is like buying from the woman first. When the man then borrows afterwards and encumbers his property, it is then like buying from the man afterwards, and the acquisition stands. What the Rosh wrote, that removal does not help, is because the removal was made to her husband, and it is comparable to buying from the man and afterwards from the woman. Removing the claim in favor of the husband is the same as removing it in favor of the man who already bought from the husband. But where she removes the lien in favor of a person who wishes to lend to the husband, and they made an acquisition from her, the Rosh agrees that it helps. See there, and examine carefully, for this requires study.
