The phrase omitted above, “and they are not collected from enhancement,” is explained by the Chelkat Mechokek as meaning even enhancement that came automatically, such as where the property rose in value. This is astonishing, for she is certainly no worse than a firstborn son, and he collects from automatic enhancement where the object did not change, such as land that produced alluvial improvement, or a palm tree that thickened, and certainly where the property rose in value. This is proven in Bechorot 52, where the Gemara says that “all of them” comes to include automatic enhancement, such as stalks that became ears and blossoms that became dates, which is automatic enhancement involving a change. A firstborn also does not take those. But where there is no change, it is obvious from the fact that she is grouped together with a firstborn that her law is not worse in this respect. Thank God, I found this explicitly in Choshen Mishpat, siman 166, in the Shach, end of se'if katan 29, who writes: Even a firstborn and a woman’s ketubah collect from enhancement caused by appreciation in value, according to all opinions. This is clear from the proofs of the Rosh from chapter HaIsha Shenaflu, concerning one who was owed money by his brother.
