Only from land, meaning even from him personally. This is also proven from the sugya in HaOmer, page 65b, and so it is stated there in the Ritva, and likewise in Nedarim 65b. See the Ran there. So too, in many places in Ketubot, in Tosafot, including in chapter HaIsha Shenaflu, page 81, they write that the dispute between Rabbi Meir and the Sages concerns collection even from the husband himself. However, in the responsa of Mahari ben Lev, book 2, kelal 55, he writes that this is not correct, for even if we say that a ketubah also collects from movable property during the husband’s lifetime according to Talmudic law, in accordance with the view of most decisors, and I do not know who these decisors are. See the Rambam, chapter 16 of Hilchot Ishut. According to the text of the Rashba, even the Rambam holds this way. I also do not know how they would reconcile the above-mentioned sugyot. However, from Rashi, of blessed memory, Ketubot 67a, s.v. purna, “to collect from orphans,” it is clearly implied that during the husband’s lifetime this is not considered nichsei melog, and this requires study. It is true that according to the Pnei Yehoshua, of blessed memory, page 81, s.v. dilma Rabbi Meir hi, who writes that it appears obvious to him that this depends on the dispute whether a ketubah is biblical or rabbinic, and Rabbi Meir follows his own view that ketubah is biblical, one could say that the decisors hinted to by the above Mahari hold like Rabbenu Tam, that we rely on Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that ketubah is biblical. The sugyot in HaOmer and Nedarim would then be surprising, since from there also it seems proven this way. But in my humble opinion, the words of Tosafot in Ketubot 10, s.v. ho'il vetiknu chachamim, explicitly imply unlike the Pnei Yehoshua. For they write that no Tanna anywhere states that a woman’s ketubah is collected from movable property except Rabbi Meir. It is clear that they do not hold that this depends on whether ketubah is biblical or rabbinic. This is also genuinely proven from Nedarim, for according to the Pnei Yehoshua there would be no reason to question Rabbi Akiva, since perhaps he too holds like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rabbi Meir. The Pnei Yehoshua further writes there: One should not say the opposite, that since ketubah is rabbinic so that it should not be easy for him to divorce her, then if he has no land it will be easy for him to divorce her. This is not correct, because nevertheless it will not be easy, for if he later acquires land it will become subject to payment. This answer is forced, for who will compel him to buy land? Furthermore, as long as he has no land, the woman does not rely on this, and this is close to a licentious union. One could also say that although movable property is not subject to collection, this only means that the court does not go down to movable property to collect from it. Nevertheless, he is obligated to pay, and they place him under ban until he pays. Some explain at the end of Bava Batra that lien is not biblical, even regarding free assets. See the Rambam and Ritva, Kiddushin 13. If so, the Gemara in Nedarim raises no difficulty. It appears to me that in the Beit Yosef, siman 119, there is a responsum of the Rashba in the name of the Rif: One who wishes to divorce and does not have the ketubah payment may not divorce, and we place him under ban if he does not obey the law. He brings proof from the Mishnah, “If he took her,” and if he said, “Here is your get and here is your ketubah,” he is permitted. This implies that without the ketubah he is not permitted. According to his words, there is no difficulty, for when he has nothing, if he divorces without the ketubah, they place him under ban. In the time of the Talmud, when he had movable property, he was at least not permitted to divorce, and therefore it is not easy for him. Although from the Rama there it appears that he disagrees based on the Rivash, nevertheless one may say that those who disagree hold like the Yerushalmi cited by the Tur in siman 93, that as long as he has not paid the ketubah, he remains obligated in her maintenance, and therefore it is also not easy for him to divorce her. According to the Yerushalmi, there is no proof from the above Mishnah to the law of the Rif, because if he does not say, “Here is your ketubah,” he will be obligated in her maintenance and medical care, and the divorce accomplishes nothing in that regard. The Rif is the one who disagrees with the Yerushalmi. This also appears from the responsum of the Rashba, who arranged the two rulings next to one another. In any case, one law is necessarily proven from the Mishnah. The position of the Rivash cited in Beit Yosef, siman 93, namely that he holds unlike the Yerushalmi and also does not hold like the Rif, requires study as to how he explains the Mishnah. See siman 79, where I explained the language of the Rambam, according to which the Mishnah is also resolved. However, according to the Rif, there is still a difficulty: What does the Gemara ask in Nedarim? In any case, he may not divorce until he pays the ketubah. Perhaps Rabbi Tarfon’s obligation means that they prohibit the divorce until he pays. It must be that this is not so, since he is not the one demanding the divorce; rather, she is demanding it because she made a vow. Therefore, although he caused the situation, nevertheless regarding the vow he is no worse than one who transgressed and divorced. If he transgressed and divorced, the Rif must agree that he no longer has the status of one who has nothing. The same applies in the time of the Talmud where he has no land, and therefore the Gemara properly asks. If so, the original difficulty returns, that it should be easy for him to divorce her privately, as we say in Bava Batra 174: Does everyone who divorces do so in court? The Ramban proves from this in Sefer HaZechut on Gittin 86, against the view of the Rif that according to Rabbi Elazar, witnesses of signature also effect the divorce. However, this requires study according to the beginning of Gittin, how we rely on an ordinary scribe, that they are knowledgeable in the law. The Rashba, when quoting the Ramban, indeed omits this proof. According to the Yerushalmi, however, it works well, because since he is obligated in her maintenance as long as he has not paid her, it is again not easy to divorce her. In Nedarim it also works well according to the decisors who hold that he cannot prohibit her through a vow unless he connects it to marital relations. After the divorce, however, he can do so, for he is truly feeding her. According to the Rambam, in any case he cannot feed her, and this should be examined. Perhaps nevertheless, even according to the Rif, one can answer with difficulty that since the prohibition against divorce remains upon him as long as he has not paid the ketubah, it is no longer easy for him to divorce her.
