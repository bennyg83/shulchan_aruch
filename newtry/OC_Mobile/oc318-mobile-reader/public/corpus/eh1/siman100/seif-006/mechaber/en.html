A widow — if no ketubah document emerges from her hand — she does not collect even principal ketubah. And likewise a divorcee — if the husband claims "I paid" or "she forgave" — even principal ketubah she has none until she produces a ketubah document. When does this apply? Where their custom is to write a ketubah. But where their custom is not to write a ketubah but rely on court conditions — behold she collects principal ketubah even though she has no ketubah document, whether divorced or widowed. {Rama: And if there are witnesses the ketubah was lost or burned — even where they write — it is like a place where they do not write (Hagahot Alfasi ch. HaKotev). And some say even where they write she collects her ketubah without ketubah document and they are not believed to say it was paid (Tur in name of R' Yitzchak). And needless to say principal ketubah — but even the tosefet that is custom in all lands to add (Mordechai end of HaNosei and responsum Maimoniot end of Ishut). And so seems the reasoning of the latter authorities (Maharik roots 18 and 114 and Maharai siman 229). But they did not practice in these lands to collect without ketubah. And for everyone — if the custom is not established, even though her ketubah is in her hand and is not validated — she does not collect with it (there in Maharai and Mahariu siman 113 and Maharik root 18). However, if the widow seized and has a migu that she could deny the property — she is believed on what she says he added for her (Mahariu siman 232). And see above siman 66. And some say if she already remarried afterward — she is not believed to collect further without ketubah (Mordechai ch. Almanah). And wherever she is believed she must swear she was not paid. And where she is not believed — if the husband is alive he must swear he paid; and if he is not alive — the orphans are exempt without oath (words of the Rav and so implied in Tur's language). If her ketubah was in her hand and was cut with a knife — she collects nothing, even though it was not cut checkerboard (Rivash siman 383).}
