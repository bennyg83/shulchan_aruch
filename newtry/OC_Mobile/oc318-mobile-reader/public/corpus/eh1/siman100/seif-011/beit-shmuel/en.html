(ב) And he gives her the principal ketubah. This is according to Rambam's approach, that he has no migo to exempt himself from the principal. But according to the approach of the other poskim, he is exempt even from the principal by the migo that he could say, "I did not divorce you." If there are get witnesses here, then he has no migo; he cannot say, "You already produced the get and I paid you." The Tur wrote that in a time of danger, when one is not permitted to keep the get, she collects with her ketubah even if there are no witnesses that he divorced her, although he has a migo that he could say, "I did not divorce." But in a place where they keep the get and it is not in her hand, since there is a weakness, if he has a migo that he could say "I did not divorce you," he is believed to say "I paid." I do not know from where the Tur derived this law. It is forced to say that he holds this migo, "I did not divorce," is weak and we do not say it before the get.
