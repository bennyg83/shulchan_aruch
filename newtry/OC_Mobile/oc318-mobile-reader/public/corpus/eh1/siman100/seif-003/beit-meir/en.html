If so, it sounds that he holds that the law of an ordinary ketubah is exactly like the law of a creditor. Then, even regarding this, if they have available cash, they must give cash. This still requires study, for from where does he know this? The reason that “he gave money and takes money” does not apply here. Why should she be elevated above a damaged party, whose law is to collect from the best-quality land? Nevertheless, even from the debtor himself, although movable property is certainly subject to payment, all agree that he may give bran, even if he has money, as is stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 419. Certainly this should apply to ketubah, whose law is to collect from inferior land. The words of the Beit Shmuel here also require study, for he states decisively that if collection from movable property is explicit, the heirs cannot push her off to collect from land. But this was not said in the Shiltei Gibborim except regarding a creditor, not regarding a ketubah, as is clear from his words in chapter Hakotev, page 106b in the Amsterdam editions. If so, not only according to the Sema, siman 419, that even regarding damages the choice is to collect land, because the above reason does not apply, then why should she be better than a damaged party? It is understandable regarding a creditor, where movable property of orphans was explicitly placed under lien, for then they have the same law as the borrower himself, and he is obligated to pay with something close to money, and so too the orphans. But regarding ketubah, even he himself, even if he explicitly pledged movable property, from where would help come to make her better than a damaged party? Even according to the Shach, that the damaged party has the choice, nevertheless in my humble opinion one cannot learn a woman from a damaged party. The damaged party’s law is at least to collect from the best property, and perhaps movable property compared to land is included in that. This is not so regarding a woman. A supporting proof to my view may be brought from the words of the Maggid Mishneh. In chapter 11 of Hilchot Malveh he writes that in this respect the power of the written stipulation is greater than the power of the enactment, with the practical result that the choice is in the hands of the lender. But in chapter 16 of Hilchot Ishut he is silent about this. Apparently this is because of the reason I have given. At first glance, the words of the Shach can stand only regarding dowry, and where he explicitly pledged the movable property of the orphans, as our custom is in writing a shetar tena'im according to the Nachalat Shivah. However, see siman 102, se'if 4, where the Beit Shmuel writes that the Ran wrote: If the land is worth ten and she wants to accept it for twenty, while the heirs wish to give ten, the law follows her. See Chelkat Mechokek there, siman 115, and Shach, se'if katan 19. In the responsa Panim Meirot, part 2, siman 46, he concludes at the end of his discussion that regarding an ordinary creditor, the heirs are considered in possession. But in his case, where it was a widow with heirs, it still requires study that he decided that the orphans are in possession because property remains in the possession of the orphans. This is understandable against the ketubah lien, but the widow also has, according to us, since we rule like the people of Galilee, a lien for maintenance. Regarding maintenance it is explained in siman 93, se'if 15, that the property is in her possession. Although we, according to custom, practice like the people of Judea, as explained there in the gloss, se'if 3, without doubt this is only where they can pay her the entire ketubah and remove her. It does not apply in this kind of case, where she remains with her maintenance lien. If so, she is the one considered in possession, and the law follows her, as the Ran ruled.
