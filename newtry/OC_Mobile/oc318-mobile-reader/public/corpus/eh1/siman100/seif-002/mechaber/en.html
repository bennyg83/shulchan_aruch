Her principal ketubah and tosefet are collected only from zibburit. And they are not collected from the enhancement that the property was improved after his death — whether heirs improved or purchasers improved and she comes to collect from them. And they are collected only from what is possessed, not from what is fitting. {Rama: A father who commanded to give a gift to his son after two or three years, or commanded not to give him his inheritance share until after two or three years — is called ra'uy and the son's wife does not collect her ketubah from it (Mahariu siman 40). Some say: if Yaakov died in Reuven his son's lifetime and left a widow supported from his property — Reuven's widow does not collect her ketubah from those properties, since they were liened to his father's widow when Reuven died (Mordechai end of Na'arah). And specifically where the heirs have no power to remove the widow from her sustenance; but where they can remove the widow — Reuven's widow collects from there (likewise there). Wages for work never in his hand is called ra'uy (Mordechai ch. Yesh Nochalin). His theft was in the thief's hand when he died and afterward the stolen item was returned — is called muchzak (so answered Maharam).} And a loan owed to the husband is muchzak and she collects from it even if it is on a gentile. (And all this refers only to principal ketubah and tosefet; but nedunya and other tzon barzel — their law is like any other debt (so writes Maggid Mishneh ch. 17 and Beit Yosef in name of Nissim and Rif and Mahariu). And some say even so they are collected only from zibburit (Rif beginning of Nezikin).)
