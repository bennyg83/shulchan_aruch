Regarding what he wrote, if the land is worth ten and she wishes to accept it for twenty, etc., the law is with her; this is from the Ran in Perek Na'arah. In Beit Shmuel he concludes: see Chelkat Mechokek, alluding to siman 115 and to Shach seif katan 19. See Sefer Beit Meir, who cited that Responsa Peri Moshe II, siman 46, raised this as a doubtful point of law. Therefore, regarding a creditor, the heirs are certainly in possession; and even with a widow against heirs, he also decided that the orphans are in possession, because assets are presumed to stand in the possession of the orphans. Beit Meir himself wrote that this still requires study: that is satisfactory against the lien of the ketubah, but for us, who rule like the people of Galilee, the widow also has a lien for sustenance, and regarding that it is explained in siman 93 seif 15 that the assets are in her possession. Although we, by custom, conduct ourselves like the people of Judea, as written there in seif 3, see what I wrote there in seif katan 5, without doubt that is only where they can remove the whole ketubah, not in this situation, where she remains with a lien for her sustenance. If so, she is the one in possession and the law is with her, as the Ran ruled. End quote. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Choshen Mishpat, siman 9, partially cited in Pitchei Teshuva to Choshen Mishpat siman 109 seif 1, s.v. shome'in layoreish; this requires study. See Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov, who wrote that specifically in such a case, where the land is worth ten and she wishes to accept it for twenty, which does not amount to ona'ah or to a mistake that a person would make, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 227 seif 29 in the gloss, that with land there is no ona'ah up to half, but more than half is ona'ah, she may accept it. But for more than twenty, beyond what a person would mistake, even with land she cannot say it is worth that to me; see there further. Regarding what Baer Heitev wrote, if there is money on hand and she claims money, etc.: in Choshen Mishpat siman 101 seif 1 regarding a creditor it says that when one comes to pay, if he has money he must give it to him and cannot push him off to land or movable property, etc. Shach wrote that the same law applies to collection of a ketubah, that one must give ready money if the orphans have money on hand. In Baer Heitev there he wrote that these words of Shach are mistaken, for in Even HaEzer siman 100 it is explicit that the heirs have permission to remove her with land; see there. See Responsa Radbaz IV, siman 115, to which Baer Heitev alludes here; it is explained there that there are three distinctions in this law. First, with an ordinary woman's ketubah, since she did not lay out money, even if he has money he may give her whatever he wishes; specifically with a creditor, since he laid out money that he lent him, therefore if he has money he gives him money, though if he has no money he gives movable property and we do not compel him to sell and bring money. But for a ketubah, even if he has money he gives her whatever he wishes. Second, this applies specifically where it is not written in the ketubah that she brought him money; but if it is written in the ketubah that she brought him such-and-such money, the law of this ketubah returns to the law of a creditor: if he has money he gives her money, and if he has no money we do not compel him to sell and bring money. Third, what we do not compel him to sell and bring money applies only where it is written generically that she brought him such-and-such money, and it does not say money on hand. But if it says money on hand, even if it does not say that if she comes to collect she collects money on hand, since he wrote that she brought him money on hand, he took the trouble to write so in order that she collect money on hand, and we compel him to sell and bring money. These are his words; see there. According to this, one may uphold Shach's words above, meaning where it was written in the ketubah that she brought him such-and-such money, for then her law is like that of a creditor. However, according to this, it applies specifically to what she brought in, not to the additional third; although in several places it is explained that it resembles the debt of dowry, nevertheless regarding this matter it is not comparable, because she did not lay out money, as Beit Shmuel wrote in siman 102 seif katan 5; see there. Although Radbaz spoke only about collecting from the husband himself, while with a widow from the heirs, even regarding an actual debt it is explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 107 seif 1 that if they inherited land and movable property and the document does not specify, the entire law is with the heirs, one may say that Shach speaks where it is explicit both in his lifetime and after his death, as is written nowadays.
