Gloss: “However, the heirs,” and so on. It is stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 101, se'if 1, that if one comes to pay, and he has money, he must give money and cannot push the creditor off to collect from land or movable property. The Shach writes that the same law applies to collecting a ketubah, that he must give ready cash if the orphans have actual money available, according to what is written in the Tur, Even HaEzer, siman 100, and so it is in the responsa of Rashdam. This book is not available to me. The Ba'er Heitev here also writes that if there is actual cash available, and she demands money, while the heirs wish to give her movable property, see Knesset HaGedolah and Radbaz. These books too are not available to me. The words of the Shach require study. If he is referring to the basic ketubah, it is clear here that even with land they can discharge their obligation to her, and certainly with movable property. Even if he is discussing dowry, which according to most decisors is like an ordinary debt, nevertheless regarding an ordinary debt itself it is explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 107, se'if 1: if they inherited land and movable property, the law follows the heirs. What he wrote, “according to what is written in the Tur here,” I do not know what he is hinting to. If he means the ruling in Shulchan Aruch, se'if 3, where the Shulchan Aruch writes, “If free property is found, she collects,” while the Tur writes, “If they find free land for me, they sell it and give her from it,” in my humble opinion this proves nothing. For he means that for his benefit, if he wishes to sell it by public announcement as is done with a borrower, perhaps someone will be found who will pay more than the appraisal. But if no buyer is found, she is obligated to collect according to the appraisal, for why should she be superior to an ordinary creditor? This is why the Beit Yosef writes that this is obvious. Rather, his intent must be to the ketubah documents of our time, in which we write that property that has responsibility and property that does not have responsibility, and even from the cloak on his shoulder, are subject to collection, during life and after death. The explanation, in my humble opinion, is that he explicitly places movable property under lien even after death, according to what is explained in the Rama, that the custom is to specify in every ketubah both land and movable property. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 21 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, in the course of his responsum, where he cites Mahari Weil. Although he too wrote that same responsum, saying: “Furthermore, I think that this is why we write ‘during my lifetime and after my death,’ so as to collect from what is only fit to come later,” in my humble opinion the simple explanation is as above. According to this, for us, the movable property of orphans is legally subject to collection, and therefore it is proper that they cannot push her off to collect from land, as in the above Choshen Mishpat, siman 107, which writes: “If they inherited land and movable property,” and it is not specified. This implies that if it is specified, the law is different. The Beit Shmuel, se'if katan 4, also brings this in the name of the Shiltei Gibborim.
