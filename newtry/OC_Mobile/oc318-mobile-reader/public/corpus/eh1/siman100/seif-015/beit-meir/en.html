“And similarly a divorcee, if the husband claims, ‘I paid.’” See Beit Shmuel, who writes: According to the words of the Shiltei Gibborim, one can say that the Rif, and so on. From the law of Rabbi Yochanan there would be a practical difference if the ketubah was burned, for then there is no concern that she may find the ketubah document, and one who claims “I paid” is not believed when claiming against an act of court. His words are astonishing. If it was burned in the presence of witnesses, why do we need the concept of an act of court? Any document that was burned accidentally in the presence of witnesses, the debtor is not believed, as is stated in Bava Kamma 98: if there are witnesses who know what was written in the document, let them write him a proper document. This is how we rule in Choshen Mishpat, siman 41. And if he means that she burned it not accidentally, I am astonished. With such a major weakness in her claim, should he not be believed even against acts of court? Even with all this, it does not help, for this is the language of the Rif: Shmuel established our Mishnah in a place where they do not write ketubot, and the halacha follows him. He explains that he rules like Shmuel, and not like Rav, who said that even in a place where they write ketubot, the get enables her to collect the basic ketubah. If the reason were that we do not write a receipt, Rav also holds that we do not write a receipt, and nevertheless he holds that the get enables her to collect the basic ketubah. Tosafot already wrote this in chapter HaKotev, s.v. get, “however,” and see there in Maharshal, Maharsha, and Pnei Yehoshua, for this requires study. Whatever the case may be, it is impossible to explain the Rif as ruling like Shmuel against Rav because otherwise a receipt would have to be written for a ketubah, since Rav holds everywhere that we do not write a receipt. The Rif is resolved without this. What the Beit Shmuel writes in se'if katan 29 regarding the question of the Chelkat Mechokek against the Rama is well directed, and so it is explained in the Tur, siman 106, that if the ketubah was lost, she collects even in a place where they write ketubot.
