But Rambam and Tur wrote that the law of yibbum applies. Beit Yosef explains that although there is room to say otherwise, their words imply that even in this case there is still a zikat yibbum. In my view, the matter depends on the reason for the exemption and whether the impediment existed at the time of falling to yibbum. Where the prohibition is not absolute in the same way, we cannot simply remove the yavam's bond. Therefore their ruling is understandable, and what seemed difficult from the earlier sources is not a proof against them.
The continuation of the matter is that one should not compare every case of doubtful yibbum to a case where the bond is certainly uprooted. If the doubt concerns whether the woman ever became obligated, or whether the yavam is fit to perform the mitzvah, we must treat it as a doubt in the mitzvah and in the prohibition. Therefore the language of Rambam and Tur is precise, and one should not be quick to say that there is no yibbum at all. This resolves the difficulty raised by the later authorities, and the practical law follows the distinction.
