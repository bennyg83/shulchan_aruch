(א) That he did not have relations after she was twelve years and one day old. Then, even if she is an adult, there is no Torah-level kiddushin, and therefore we are not concerned that the hairs may have fallen out. Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that in such a case women are believed that she has no hairs and she may perform mi'un, since there is no Torah concern, as written above. Likewise, where he did not have relations and women testify about her years that she is still not twelve years and one day old, they are believed. This may also be inferred from Rashi in Perek Ba Siman, Niddah 48b, who wrote that if they said the hairs are absent she may not perform mi'un because we are concerned that they fell out. This implies specifically where we say they may have fallen out; but where he did not have relations, she may perform mi'un. There is no proof that Tosafot disagree from what they wrote there, s.v. "Granted," that one cannot explain that the hairs are a wart and she may perform mi'un, for the latter clause teaches that she is not believed to perform mi'un. It must be that the case is where he did not have relations; otherwise, how could one have thought she may perform mi'un, since even if she is believed that these are hairs, we still suspect that other hairs fell out. Even if there were no hairs at all, she could not perform mi'un. Rather, it must be where he did not have relations, and nevertheless Tosafot wrote that she is not believed to perform mi'un. One can answer that their meaning is this: since the latter clause teaches that she is not believed to perform mi'un, it contradicts the first clause whether the whole baraita speaks where he had relations or where he did not. Rather, the first clause speaks regarding chalitzah, and in truth it speaks where he had relations. One can also say that if these hairs remained and did not fall out, we no longer suspect that she produced other hairs and they fell out, for since we saw these hairs and they did not fall out, why should we say she produced others and they fell out? According to this, Tosafot speak where he had relations, and nevertheless one might say she is believed to say she did not produce other hairs and may perform mi'un, and we do not suspect that new hairs fell out; therefore they needed to bring proof from the latter clause. The practical difference is where she was examined by men before the age and they saw hairs, and those hairs remained after twelve years. We no longer suspect that she produced other new hairs and they fell out, since the old ones did not fall out. Then even if he had relations after she was twelve, she may perform mi'un. At first glance it appears that all doubts here have a remedy: where a minor was married off by her mother and brothers and then fell before a yavam, she should perform mi'un before two and then perform chalitzah. She is then permitted to another either way: if she is an adult, she leaves through chalitzah; if she is a minor, she leaves through mi'un. Just as we find in several laws that she requires both mi'un and chalitzah, here too with mi'un and chalitzah no examination is needed and there is no doubt at all.
