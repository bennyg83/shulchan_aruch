(א) What is, etc. According to the Rif’s version in Gittin: the young children, etc., and corresponding to this she can become betrothed for mi'un. There, 59a: and until how much, etc. According to the Rif’s version, which reads, “a pebble and she throws it,” and corresponding to this she can be divorced, one must say that “a pebble and she throws it, a nut and she takes it” is one measure. If she distinguishes between her get and something else, meaning that she guards it, it is not like the nut, etc. Just as she knows how to distinguish between a nut and a pebble, so she distinguishes between a get and a nut or the like. But according to Rashi’s version, distinguishing her get is a higher level, and it is the level of the young children. The Rambam explains that for a get it is enough that she knows how to guard it alone, while for mi'un she needs the age of the young children, and knowing how to guard is like in divorce. He could likewise have written “a pebble and she throws it,” but he used the language of the baraita there, for this is the meaning of guarding her get and her kiddushin. Rava said “a pebble and she throws it” as a test, for then she knows how to guard kiddushin as well. So too the Rambam writes regarding divorce that she distinguishes between her get and another object. There is no distinction between here and get except that here she also needs the age of the young children, while there knowing alone is enough. But according to Rashi’s version, the knowing how to guard here does not require such a distinction between kiddushin and another object as by get, but only between a pebble and a nut. Also, here the age of the young children is not required. See above, siman 141, seif 6, where he brought the two opinions.
(ב) If she knows, like Rabbi Chanina ben Antigonus. The Rambam explains that both are required. Although in Gittin too it says “which mind can guard,” and explains: whoever distinguishes, etc., Rava divided them there into two matters of knowing. A pebble and she throws it, to separate between a pebble and a nut, is enough for gittin. But regarding mi'un, a greater distinction is needed, at the level of the young children. This is what he wrote: “and that they are kiddushin,” not, etc. Regarding gittin the Rambam wrote simply, “whoever distinguishes,” etc., meaning between a pebble and a nut, as the Beit Yosef wrote there. Rashi and Tosafot have the opposite version. See above, siman 141, seif 6.
(ג) Rather, she goes, etc. According to the explanation of Rabbenu Chananel, for if she would marry now, her marriage would be subject to mi'un, as written in seif 3. The Rashba has another approach. See there.
(ד) And some say that all this, etc. As written: her mother and brothers married her off, etc.
(ה) And some say, etc. This is the view of the Ra'avad in his glosses, and of the Ritva, that Rabbi Chanina ben Antigonus speaks where she was betrothed through her mother and brothers, while in Gittin it speaks where she was betrothed by herself.
