(א) From six years old, etc. This follows the version of Rif and Rambam in Perek HaMitkabel: the transactions of young children are valid, and corresponding to them a minor girl may be betrothed for purposes of mi'un. In Perek Beit Shammai, R. Chanina says that any girl who can guard her kiddushin requires mi'un. Rambam explains that R. Chanina refers to young children, namely from six to ten, each according to her sharpness, and that sharpness means that she knows how to guard kiddushin. But below age six R. Chanina did not speak, and even if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she does not require mi'un. Regarding a get, however, even below age six she can receive the get, namely if she can keep a pebble when it is thrown to her, and she is divorced from her father's kiddushin according to Rambam's version. The reason is explained by the Ran in Perek HaMitkabel: kiddushin are money and require guarding, so for kiddushin she must at least have the measure of young children. What Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 of Ishut, that according to Rambam even under six, if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she requires mi'un, is astonishing to me, for in chapter 11 of Geirushin he wrote explicitly that even if she knows how to guard kiddushin, she does not require mi'un, as the Mechaber wrote here. However, according to Ramakh, cited by Kesef Mishneh in chapter 4 of Ishut, even under six, whenever she knows how to guard her kiddushin, she requires mi'un. He holds these are two separate measures: young children generally require mi'un, and if she knows how to guard her kiddushin, even under six, she requires mi'un. So too according to the version of Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh there, the measure of keeping a pebble when thrown to her applies to mi'un; this is what R. Chanina meant by knowing how to guard her kiddushin, even under six, as Ran wrote in Perek HaMekabel, for mi'un is only rabbinic kiddushin and does not require the measure of young children. Above, in siman 141, the Mechaber brought their view according to their version, and accordingly for mi'un even a girl under six requires mi'un. Here he omitted their view; perhaps he holds that since it is only rabbinic kiddushin, we are lenient. Also according to Ritva's approach cited nearby in the gloss, which is the approach of Raavad, Baal HaMaor, and others, there are two separate measures, and R. Chanina speaks even of one under six, who requires mi'un when she was betrothed by her brothers. Therefore it appears one should be stringent that she requires mi'un even under six.
