(א) Two hairs, etc. This is the language of the Rambam. In the Rambam it is written that they must be in one place, like the Sages according to Rabbi Chiyya there. So ruled the Rif. In the Shulchan Aruch he omitted this, following his view that he wondered about the Rambam: from where did he get this? According to the Maggid Mishneh’s explanation, the Rambam wrote specifically in the place of the ervah, and wrote, according to his explanation, “in one place.” The Maggid Mishneh wrote: but other commentators did not explain this way, only that they should be in one place. I say that the Rambam also explains this way. What he wrote, “in the place of the ervah,” he learned from what is written in Yevamot 80b: the Rabbis taught, what is, etc., and as Tosafot wrote there, s.v. dehevi'ah: and if you will say, behold, etc. So too the Rambam wrote in his commentary there at the end of chapter 10: from here it will be explained to you, etc., and not like the first Tanna in Niddah there.
(ב) And there is one who, etc. Like the first Tanna there. Also, they do not need to be in one place, as written there: the halacha follows all of them to be stringent. Tosafot explains that this also refers to this. See there, s.v. halacha, etc. But the Rambam explains that it refers only to the Mishnah.
