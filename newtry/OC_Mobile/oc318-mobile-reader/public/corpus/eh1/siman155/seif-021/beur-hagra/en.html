(א) She brought, etc. This is the language of the Tur, and as the Rif wrote in Yevamot, that the question there, 109b, was not resolved. Rav Sheshet challenged Rav, and although he answered, the answer is forced. Furthermore, we say in Niddah 46a: Rav Dimi of Nehardea said, etc. This shows that if she brought signs, she cannot perform mi'un even if he did not have relations. But the Ra'avad wrote that there is no uncertainty at all, for the answer is proper. Rav Sheshet accepted his words, as it says, “he is greater,” according to the commentators who explain that this refers to Ravin. The sugya of the Gemara there follows him. As for Niddah, even Rav agrees that rabbinically she requires a get. So wrote the Rambam, and so wrote the Ramban and other decisors. This also appears in Niddah 52a in the Mishnah, “a young girl,” etc., and in the Gemara there: and Rabbi Yehudah agrees, etc. So too at the end of the first chapter of Chullin: wherever there is mi'un, etc., and Rav said: this, etc. This shows that Rav agrees, as above.
(ב) Rabbinically, as written in Niddah there: the halacha, etc. And these words, etc., as above.
(ג) And if they wish, etc. As written in Gittin 89b.
(ד) And if he came, etc. Like a woman whose husband went overseas. Rif. The Ra'avad challenged him, saying it is not comparable to there, since here it is only a rabbinic prohibition. The Ramban strengthened the words of the Rif from that passage in Gittin 79b. See Tosafot there, s.v. utzerichah, etc. The Rosh rejected his words. See there, because of “the coin that the Sages minted,” etc. It appears to me that there is a clear proof from the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter Cheresh, which I brought above, siman 10, seif 2, regarding the wife of a deaf-mute who married a competent man, that she is like a minor girl.
(ה) And the child is not, etc. This is not comparable to a woman whose husband went, etc., where the child from the first husband is a mamzer rabbinically. A proof is from what is written in Gittin 80b: Rabbi said, this is the view of Rabbi Meir, etc.; the child is valid, even though the get is invalid rabbinically.
