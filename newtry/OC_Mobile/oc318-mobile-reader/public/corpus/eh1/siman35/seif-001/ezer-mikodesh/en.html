Seif 1: Regarding forbidden to betroth woman until he sees her — perhaps if he heard from his mother or relative it suffices as if he saw himself. Also no decisive proof from Chazal ch. 3 speaking with her on road — there discusses concern of substitution through visual impression. Nevertheless perhaps some implication one may rely through another's seeing. In any case it tends that in all events he should see himself for woman's charm etc., and only regarding she was not substituted he relies on another who looks more and has visual impression. Therefore Beit Yosef wrote custom to make meal at night before chuppah so he sees her then, though presumably in all matchmaking there is prior seeing through relative or one he relies on. Some bring betrothed to their home on a holiday for this reason. Regarding Beit Yosef as above — appears one should not rely on leniency Derishah and Perishah wrote also here applies like Tosafot regarding forbidden to betroth minor daughter that due to exile travail they were lenient; for in above meal when no great impediment such leniency from that reason does not apply as much; and what they omit that meal is because they see themselves close to matchmaking or on holiday as above.
