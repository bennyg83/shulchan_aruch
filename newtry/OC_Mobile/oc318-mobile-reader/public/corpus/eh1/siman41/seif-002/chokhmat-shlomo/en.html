Seif 3 And some say that even the foreign women are not betrothed, etc. NB: See my responsum to the holy community of Ackerman, in my fifth edition, siman 252, where I wrote that this is specifically when they were not engaged in that matter. But if they were engaged in that matter, even the Rambam agrees that the foreign women are betrothed. This is only according to the Maggid Mishneh’s reason in the Rambam’s view, not according to the Ran’s reason. See there, with the help of Heaven. The Maggid Mishneh wrote to resolve the Rambam’s words from the rule that when one says, "Acquire, you and a donkey," the person acquires half, because one statement is different. But this is difficult from the first chapter of Gittin, 8b, where the Gemara discusses "all my property is acquired by you," etc. It is proven there according to Rava, and we rule like him, that even in one statement we split the statement. The Maggid Mishneh’s reasoning appears to follow Abaye, but Rava holds that even in one statement we split the statement. One cannot answer that there it is only for rabbinic matters such as "it was written before me," because if so, why does the Gemara say "according to whom, according to Rabbi Shimon"? It could have answered that it follows Rabbi Meir for biblical matters, while Rava speaks only in rabbinic matters. Necessarily, there is no such distinction, and this is not like the Maggid Mishneh. Regarding Tosafot there, s.v. "Abaye retracted," who write that he did not retract because of Rava’s question, in my humble opinion he certainly retracted because of Rava’s question. Since one statement is not split, there are two possibilities: either since it takes effect for this, it takes effect for that; or the reverse, since it does not take effect for this, it does not take effect for that. At first Abaye thought that migo can remove money from its possession, and therefore since he acquired himself, he acquired the property. Rava holds that migo is not effective to extract money, and therefore the property is not acquired. Once Rava introduced that migo cannot extract from possession, the reverse follows: since it cannot take effect for the property, it does not take effect for the other either, because the statement is one. Thus Abaye properly retracted because of Rava’s question. I later saw in Beit Meir, siman 41, that he hints to this sugya and to the view of the Maggid Mishneh, and I have explained the matter more fully, with the help of Heaven.
