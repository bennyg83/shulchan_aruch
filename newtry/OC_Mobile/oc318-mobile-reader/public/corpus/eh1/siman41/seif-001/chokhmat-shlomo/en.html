Seif 2 One who betroths, etc. NB: See Beit Shmuel, s.k. 2, where the reason is explained: we hold that anything that cannot take effect one after the other cannot take effect even simultaneously. I have a difficulty from the Talmud in Zevachim, Mishnah 3: if two held a knife and slaughtered they are exempt, but if they held a limb and brought it up they are liable. This is difficult according to Rabbi Yosi, for it is explained there in the latter clause that he holds that if one brought it up and then brought it up again, he is exempt and liable only once, even though he had knowledge in between. Necessarily, the reason is that we require the item to be whole and not lacking. If so, why should two who brought it up be liable? Since this cannot be done one after the other, for if he brought it up and then brought it up again he is exempt, whether it was he or another, because the reason is that it is not whole. If so, even simultaneously they should be exempt, for anything that cannot take effect one after another cannot take effect even simultaneously. There is no difficulty from the Mishnah itself, for one may say the first clause is also Rabbi Shimon’s view, who holds one liable when one brings it up and then brings it up again, and therefore it can take effect one after another. According to Rabbi Yosi, it may be that two who brought it up are truly exempt. But the Rambam is difficult, for he rules like Rabbi Yosi and also rules that two who brought it up are liable, while we hold that anything that cannot take effect one after another cannot take effect even simultaneously. Even if the Rambam rules like Rabbi Yochanan that with several limbs one is liable for each one, nevertheless with one limb he rules like Rabbi Yosi that if one brought it up and brought it up again he is exempt. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 19 of Hilchot Ma'aseh HaKorbanot, halacha 11. Why then did he rule that if two held one limb and brought it up, they are liable? With one limb it cannot take effect one after another, and therefore even simultaneously it should not take effect. This requires study.
