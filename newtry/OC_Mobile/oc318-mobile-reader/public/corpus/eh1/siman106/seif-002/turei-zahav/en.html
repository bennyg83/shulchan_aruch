(ב) And she was silent; some say she must uphold it. This is the view of Raavan in Mordechai at the beginning of chapter Almanah Nizonit. A case was asked of Raavan where Reuven's son had a sum in Shimon's hand. The son became ill, summoned witnesses, and gave that sum to his father Reuven while his wife did not object. He recovered somewhat, later became worse, summoned Shimon again and repeated the command before witnesses; this time his wife objected, and then he died. Shimon fulfilled the deceased's words, and afterward she sued Shimon. Raavan answered that Shimon acted lawfully and the widow has no claim against him, for even without the son's command the money should have gone to Reuven as heir. He cited the Mishnah in chapter HaKotev, where R. Akiva says that where a deceased left a woman's ketubah, a creditor, and an heir, the deposit is given to the heir, since the others need an oath and the heir does not. Here, since she needs a ketubah oath, it is given to the father. All the more where the deceased commanded it, for there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead. Taz questions Raavan's reasoning: the Mishnah there concerns movable property not in the heirs' domain before the Geonic enactment, but after that enactment movable property is collected like land while unencumbered. He also questions applying "mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead," since in Choshen Mishpat it applies only where the item was deposited now with a third party. Her initial silence is also difficult, since waiver by silence applies only in the case where he divided all his property among his sons and gave her something, because he honored her. The conclusion is that Raavan's proof is not simple, and after the Geonic enactment a ketubah may collect from such property; silence alone does not necessarily waive unless the classic conditions of division and honor apply.
