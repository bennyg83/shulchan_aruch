(ד) And she was silent when it became known to her. It is explained that although he did not divide in her presence, nevertheless when she was silent at the time it became known to her, she lost. This is the view of Rambam, Rosh, and Ran. According to Rashi, this is only when he divided in her presence, and so it is stated in Mordechai in the name of Maharam. According to Baal HaMaor, HaItur, and Rabbenu Chananel, it is specifically where she was there and accepted it. In this sugya it is clear that according to the first tanna three elements are needed: first, that she was there; second, that he wrote her even a minimal amount of land; third, that she accepted it, meaning that she waived explicitly. But if she was there and was silent and did not accept it, we say she did not waive. Likewise, if she accepted it but was not there at the time of division, one may say she merely wished to please her husband, as Rashbam and Tosafot explained. What we hold regarding ketubah that we do not say "I acted to please my husband" appears to me to be because we do not hold like that first tanna in full.
