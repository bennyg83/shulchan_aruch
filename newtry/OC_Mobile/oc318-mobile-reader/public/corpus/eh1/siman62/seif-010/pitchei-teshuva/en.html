See Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel: where a widower marries a widow by day and they do not eat until night, it remains doubtful whether to bless, and with doubtful berachot we are lenient. Chelkat Mechokek alluded to Responsa R. Menachem Azariah siman 40. I saw there that his view also agrees not to bless at night, but he wrote: what shall we do in places where the custom is to recite the seven berachot at the first meal eaten at night even for a widow married to a widower? They rely on a great authority, Sefer Agudah, who wrote that if a widower married a widow on Friday, it appears that the berachot are recited only at the night meal. Were it not that the custom spread not to distinguish between marriage on Thursday and marriage on Friday, and always to recite the seven berachot at night, one could say Agudah does not disagree with the great authorities above, and that Friday is different because one may not establish a feast then. But the custom fixed by elders proves there is no difference. I found that my teacher R. Y. Ch. wrote that it seems proper to distinguish: if they could have eaten the first meal while it was still day and chose not to eat until night, then the seven berachot are not recited at night; but if the chuppah was delayed until close to minchah ketanah, as is customary in some places, and there was no time to eat while it was still day, then the seven berachot are recited at night, because it is the first meal after the chuppah, and the time of obligation for the wedding meal did not arrive until now. He brought support for this. See also Responsa Radbaz, part 3.
See Beit Meir, who brought the above words of Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel and wrote that therefore it is proper not to make the marriage of a widower and widow until close to nightfall, or else to make the meal immediately with most of it by day. Even according to the gaon of Lissa, there is no ab initio remedy of having the chuppah by day and the yichud at night, because the yichud must be immediately after the chuppah, without interruption between the berachot under the chuppah and the later yichud; see above s.k. 1 in the name of Sefer HaMakneh. So concludes Responsa Bach siman 119. There it is explained that in Poland the custom is to perform even the chuppot of virgins between minchah and maariv near night; I have seen this custom. The first meal of bride and groom with the attendants alone, immediately after the chuppah, continues until the invited guests gather at night, and they do not interrupt with Birkat HaMazon until the main meal is finished; then they recite Birkat HaMazon with birkat chatanim without hesitation. But in this country they make the chuppah in the middle of the day, the bride and groom and attendants stop and recite Birkat HaMazon after their meal without any birkat chatanim, and only at night after the guests' meal do they recite birkat chatanim. This is not proper: even if at night one may bless because of panim chadashot, by day why should they not at least recite shehasimchah bime'ono and Asher Bara; and if ten are present, all the birkat chatanim should be recited. According to what I wrote above in s.k. 1, perhaps this custom has a basis, but it still requires care.
