(Seif 16:) And there is no remedy, etc. See Beit Shmuel and what he wrote, that perhaps there the condition was meant to distress her, and therefore he cannot waive it. What he wrote immediately before this, that one may say the Tur holds that with a condition that she give, it helps if he says "let it be in your hand without condition," even though there too it was meant to distress her, and for that reason "it is waived to you" does not help, must be explained as follows: specifically the language of waiver does not help if his intent was to distress her; but the language "let it be in your hand" helps even if his intent was to distress her. If a different condition was stipulated, it is not as though he said "let it be in your hand without condition," but rather as though he waived it to her; therefore it does not help, since his intent was to distress her. This is how his words must be explained; examine carefully.
