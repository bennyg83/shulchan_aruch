(ג) And there is no remedy to permit her. Since he gave her a valid Get, it is not in his power to permit her unless he betroths her; so wrote the Tur. From this it follows that he cannot nullify the condition at all, even if both of them consent. But this is difficult from what the Tur wrote at the end of siman 38 and below in this siman, in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, that he can nullify the condition. It appears that this is not comparable to what is written at the end of siman 38, for there the condition is if he comes within twelve months, which is not in his power to fulfill, and the kiddushin have not yet taken effect. What is written at the end of seif 7 concerns a condition that if the divorcing husband does not come within twelve months, even though the condition is passive and the divorcing husband has the power to fulfill it, nevertheless she is forbidden to marry because it is not in her power but in the power of the divorcing husband; in any such case, they have the power to nullify it. But here it is a complete Get, and she is permitted to marry immediately, because the condition is in her power to fulfill passively; therefore he cannot nullify the condition. However, one may say that the Tur follows his own reasoning, for he holds that with a condition on condition that you give me two hundred zuz, waiver does not help even if he says let it be a Get without condition; therefore even with this condition there is no remedy by saying let it be a Get without condition. But according to the poskim he cited in siman 5, that it helps if he says let it be a Get without condition, one may say it would help here too. However, according to what I wrote, the condition that she give is not in her power to fulfill, because perhaps she will lose everything she owns; one may say that there the condition is not in her power, and therefore he can nullify it. The reason it does not help when he says I waive it to you is because he intended to distress her. But here the condition is in her power and the Get takes effect immediately; it is not in their power to nullify it, even if he says let it be in your hand without condition. According to this, one may say that the Tur holds that with a condition that she give, it helps if he says let it be in your hand without condition. Yet it is difficult: why did the Tur write regarding a condition on condition that you serve my father for two years, and he then went back and stipulated on condition that you give two hundred zuz, that he did not deliver the Get on the first condition, for otherwise he could not stipulate another condition? Why can he not stipulate another condition? The first condition is one that prevents her from marrying, since it is active and also not in her power. Perhaps there, the condition is intended to distress her, so he cannot waive the condition and stipulate another. The rule is: a condition intended to distress cannot be waived; a condition not intended to distress, if it is in her power and passive so that she is permitted to marry another, cannot be nullified. Bach answers that everything depends on whether the condition is intended to distress or not. At the end of siman 38 and at the end of seif 7, the condition is not intended to distress and he can nullify it; but the condition that she not marry this man is intended to distress, and likewise the condition that you serve my father is intended to distress. According to this, the Tur's statement here that there is no remedy proves that he holds it does not help, if he stipulated that she give two hundred zuz, for him to say let it be in your hand without condition; otherwise there is a remedy, namely to say let it be in her hand, etc. But the language of the Tur here does not imply as Bach says.
