(א) One who divorces, etc. For Rav Huna and Rabbi Yochanan disagree there, 74a, only regarding "on condition," as they said there. But with "if," Rav Huna agrees with Rabbi Yochanan.
(ב) Therefore, etc. From what they said there, 76b: and let us be concerned that perhaps he appeased her. He explains: perhaps he nullified the get. Although it is also taught there on the Mishnah, the Rambam explains that this is what they said there: and according to the one who teaches it on the baraita, etc., "he did not come," meaning that even if he appeased her, it is nothing, since the condition was fulfilled retroactively. The Ramban and Ran have another approach there. See there, that even with "from now" or "on condition" there is a way to nullify it. Nevertheless, they learned this not from the Mishnah but according to the version in the baraita: on condition that I pass, etc. But I saw in the Rambam, chapter 9, halacha 10, that anything that is for her benefit she can waive. This is what is meant by "perhaps he appeased her." But in the Mishnah we are not concerned, because it is not the way, etc., and as written above, siman 144, seif 7. His proof is from what they said, 29b, "perhaps he appeased her," and the same applies here, since it has not yet taken effect, etc., as above. See Tosafot 18b, s.v. "perhaps," etc. Nevertheless, his view there is like the Rambam. [Collection:] Therefore he cannot nullify, etc. And what they said there, 76b, on the Mishnah, "from now," etc.: perhaps he appeased her, and even according to the one who teaches it on the baraita, etc., because people do not usually come secretly. From what he wrote in chapter 9, halacha 9, the Rambam already explained there that any condition that is for her benefit she can waive, as written above, siman 144, end of seif 7. What is written in the first clause, regarding "if," that he can nullify, etc., is from what they said, 29b, "perhaps he appeased her," according to the explanation of the Rif and Tosafot above, that he nullified it. The reason is that since it does not take effect until after thirty days, he can nullify it. And there, 34a: Abaye said, from where do I say this? For that case, etc.; Rava said: is that so, etc. It implies that if he explicitly nullified, according to everyone it is nullified. See Mordechai, Perek Mi She'achazo, siman 609, and the Rif further wrote, etc. So too here, since it does not take effect until fulfillment of the condition, as above, he can nullify it. Only Perek Mi She'achazo, which speaks of "on condition," says specifically that it depends on her will. This is also the view of Tosafot 18b, s.v. "perhaps," etc. But the Ra'avad, chapter 9, halacha 9, in his glosses, holds that even with "from now" he can nullify it, like the view of Ba'al HaItur. This is also the view of the Rosh, klal 46, seif 2. See there, where he discusses this at length and brings proof from Perek HaMadir: Do not say that Rav’s reason is waiver, etc. He wrote that in divorce, even a matter that is for the woman’s benefit can be nullified, since he divorces her against her will. See above, siman 38. The Tur also brought this in this siman. This is what is written in the gloss: and some say, etc. See Beit Shmuel at the end of siman 144, and other approaches, and what I wrote in siman 16.
(ג) Or to add, etc. Or, etc. There, 76a: The Rabbis taught: he said to her, etc.; but, etc. Rashi explained that he did not deliver it to her. See there, s.v. "he said to her before," etc. The Ran wrote that even before the latter two, he also did not deliver it, for otherwise his latter words would certainly nullify the first words. He wrote: but others explain that he delivered it to her the first time, and this Tanna holds that "on condition" is not like saying "from now."
(ד) And if he died, etc. The Mishnah, 76b: This is your get if, etc.
(ה) Or it was lost, etc. There, 74a, the dispute of Rav Huna and Rabbi Yochanan, and in this kind of case Rav Huna agrees, etc.
(ו) And if she married, she must leave. The commentators wrote that this is unlike the Rambam. See the Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh there.
(ז) And if he said to her, etc. The Mishnah, 76, and the Gemara in several places.
(ח) Or from today, etc. The Gemara there, and the Mishnah, 72a.
(ט) Or if he said to her, etc. See Tosafot 74a, s.v. "Rav Huna," etc. So too is the sugya there, 77a.
(י) Therefore, etc. As above, like the one who teaches it on the baraita. So wrote the Rif in a responsum and the She'iltot: since if the get was torn or lost it is valid, all the more so he cannot nullify it. The Ran brought them there. This is also the view of Tosafot, as above. The Ramban has a different approach in this. See there in the Ran.
(כ) And if it was lost, etc. Like Rav Huna there, and as above.
(ל) And even if he died, etc. The Mishnah, 76b, and there, 74a. It is also taught: This is your get, etc.
(מ) Lechatchilah, etc. There. They challenged this from what is stated there, 83a: even with all conditions, etc. Several approaches were written on this. See the Ran here and there. The Ran wrote that the clearest of all is the Ramban’s approach, to distinguish between "arise and do" and "sit and do not act." This is what he wrote: however, etc. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 241, seif 10.
(נ) And some say, etc. As they said there, 76b: a Tanna, and in chapter 9 of Bava Batra he rules explicitly like Rabbi Yochanan. But the first opinion holds that regarding gittin the halacha does not follow Rabbi Yochanan, as they said there, 84b: to exclude, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. "from this day," etc.; Tosafot 72a, s.v. "so," etc. This is the view of the latter opinion. See above, end of siman 137.
(ס) And some say that even, etc. From what is written, 76b: and there are those who teach it on the Mishnah. And there, 71a: The Rabbis taught: he said to her before, etc. So too the Ran wrote there in the name of others. So too the Ra'avad, chapter 9, halacha 9, and so too the Rosh, klal 46, seif 2, and the Tur brought it in this siman, and the gloss in the name of Ba'al HaItur. He wrote: but the Ramban and Rashba, of blessed memory, have already decided like our teacher with clear proofs.
