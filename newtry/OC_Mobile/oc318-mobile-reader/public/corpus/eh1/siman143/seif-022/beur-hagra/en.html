(א) He said to her, "on condition," etc. From what they said there: that you not go to the house, etc., although if he dies it is permitted, as written in Nedarim 46a. Rather, since she is forbidden to him forever, and the same applies here. See Be'er HaGolah, s.k. 10.
(ב) And some say, etc. As they said there, 83a, that with "on condition" he agrees, nevertheless even without qualification it is forever. But the Rambam establishes it as for a time. They also brought proof from the Tosefta: on condition that you not climb this tree, and on condition that you not climb this wall; if the tree was cut down or the wall collapsed, this is a get, as written in seif 2 and 3. However, this requires study: according to these "some say," why do I need the tree to be cut down, etc.? There is no concern that perhaps the condition will be fulfilled. Perhaps the Tosefta follows Rabbi Akiva, who says this is not severance whenever we are concerned for nullification of the condition, as the Ran wrote in the name of others, as above in seif 16. The case of "that you not go to your father’s house" was already answered in Kiddushin 5a, s.v. "on condition," etc., and in Gittin, page 82, and learn from this. The Rashba and Ran answer that "your father’s house" is not precise, but rather a particular house.
