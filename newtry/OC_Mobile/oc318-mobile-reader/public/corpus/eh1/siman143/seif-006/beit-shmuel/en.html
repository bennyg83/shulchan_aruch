And there are those who wrote. So wrote the Rosh: if he is willing to accept the money, it is a Get. Necessarily, one must say that the Rabbis and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagree where he is not willing. If so, it is difficult, for it is proven that giving against one's will is not considered giving according to the Rabbis, and we hold like them. So Derishah and Bach asked in the name of Maharshal, and they did not answer. One may say that giving against one's will means where the condition was to give money and she gave it against his will; but where the money is given in place of the article, and it is given against his will, we do not say that giving against one's will is considered giving. Therefore I wrote in Hilkhot Kiddushin at the beginning of siman 27 in the name of Maharshal. Do not object from the Rabbis' statement that he specifically meant the cloak; one could say that even if we did not say that he specifically meant it, giving against his will would still not count. Rather, certainly with money it is considered giving, which is difficult according to the Rosh, who holds that giving against one's will is a doubt. It is strained to say that this is what they mean: he specifically meant the cloak, and therefore it is not giving at all; but if he had conditioned on money, it would be a doubt. Perhaps the Rosh holds that giving into the hand of the beit din in his absence is considered giving when we do not know that he is unwilling, and she is permitted to marry, unlike the responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 73. Therefore the Rabbis say that he specifically meant the cloak, and therefore it is not giving even into the hand of the beit din in his absence. In Responsa Rashakh II, siman 99, he also ruled that giving into the hand of the beit din is considered giving. In truth, Rashba's proof is at first glance surprising, for he brought proof from Hillel's enactment to put the money in the chamber, implying that generally giving to the beit din is not considered giving. But there, he had revealed that he did not want it, which is different; if we do not know his view, one may say it is considered giving. However, it is difficult according to Rabbah there, who said that this enactment was not needed and explained that it follows the Rabbis. Even though according to the Rabbis he specifically meant the cloak because he intended only to distress her, and according to the Rosh the case concerns giving against his will, it would follow that even if she did not give the very thing he stipulated and gives against his will, it is nevertheless considered giving because he did not intend only to distress her. This is also difficult according to the words of Maharit that I cited at the beginning of siman 27.
