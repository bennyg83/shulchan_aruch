Just as one does not need to validate the get, etc. So wrote the Rosh at the beginning of Gittin and the other decisors. At first glance, several inferences can be made from this. First, specifically an agent for delivery is believed, because he is believed to say "befanai nichtav," and Chazal also gave him credibility to say that he is the agent. But an agent for receiving is not believed. It also implies that specifically outside Eretz Yisrael, where he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," he is believed, but not in Eretz Yisrael, unlike the Ramah. In seif 10 in the gloss it does not imply this. It also implies that even in one city he is believed, and even if the husband contradicts him. Nearby and also in siman 141 it does not imply this. The Taz wrote: a woman who was divorced needs a writing from beit din that she was divorced, and the Rav is not believed to testify about this alone. It is not comparable to validation of documents, for here he removes her from her presumed status. Therefore she needs an act of beit din, and then she can show in any place she comes that she is divorced.
