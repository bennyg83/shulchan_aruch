(א) If its first part. The reason the agent says "befanai nichtav" is because of validation, as written above. He does not need to say "it was written before me" except so that people not confuse it with ordinary validation of documents. Therefore he says "it was written before me" as a distinguishing mark. It is enough if the first part was written before him. According to Rashi and Tosafot, the line containing the names of the man and woman must be written before him. According to the Ran, also "you are permitted" must be written before him. From the words of the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur it implies that even if the names of the man, etc., were not written before him, he can say "befanai nichtav." So the author ruled plainly like the Rambam. However, one should be stringent like the view of Rashi and Tosafot. It implies that specifically when he says he heard the preparation of the parchment, he must say that the preparation was lishmah. But if it was written before him, we say that presumably they wrote it for his sake and her sake.
(ב) The sound of the quill. There are several explanations of this. See Rashi, Tosafot, and Hagahot Ashri. From the language of the Rambam, which is the language of the author, it implies that he heard the sound as they were writing on the parchment, like Rashi’s second explanation. Even the sound of part of the writing means the first part. According to Rashi’s first explanation, it means that he saw the cutting of the quill and the parchment in order to write the get lishmah. So wrote the Tur. But if he did not see the cutting and was only told in his presence to write the get, it is not effective. Hagahot Ashri wrote that if he heard the husband say, "write a get for my wife," and the scribe took the parchment and quill, it is effective.
(ג) And the witnesses signed before him. It implies that regarding the witnesses’ signatures, the sound of the quill is not effective. So too it implies from the Tur, even if the sound of the entire signature was heard. At first glance this is astonishing, for Tosafot and the Rosh wrote that one cannot validate a blind person through the sound of the quill because it looks false if he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," as written above. So too wrote the Ran. If they hold that regarding signatures the sound of the quill is not effective even for one who is not blind, what was their question regarding a blind person? This requires study. I later saw that Chiddushei Rashach also noticed this.
