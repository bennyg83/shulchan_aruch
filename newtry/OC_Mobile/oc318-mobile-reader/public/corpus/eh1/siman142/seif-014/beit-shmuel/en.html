(א) And some disagree. See Responsa Rivash, siman 385. The disputing view is the Ra'avad, chapter 12. He holds that the woman is similar to an agent: at the time he delivers the get, he is believed to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," but after he has delivered it, his statement is no longer effective. So too here, when the woman brings the get as an agent and she has not yet been divorced, her statement is effective, as written in the Talmud and in the previous law. But if she came and brought the get in her hand and says she was already divorced, her statement is no longer effective, just like an agent after he has delivered the get, and she needs witnesses. See there how he resolves the sugya in chapter 2. Therefore, in the first law, when she brings the get as an agent, she is believed. So too, if an agent brought the get in Eretz Yisrael and did not say "befanai nichtav," he is believed, since coming in the capacity of agency is different.
(ב) A husband who brings the get himself, etc. This law was written by Orchot Chaim and brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 141. At first glance this law is astonishing, for in the baraita it says that a husband who brought a get does not need to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," as written at the beginning of Gittin, page 5. In Orchot Chaim he wrote this language: so that the husband can say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," since the husband himself brings it, as we say at the beginning of Gittin, the husband himself, etc., because because of our sins we are not bnei Torah like the earlier ones, etc. It appears to me that Orchot Chaim holds like the Ra'avad, who holds the halacha follows Rabbah, who said that the reason the agent must say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" is because they are not expert in lishmah, and because witnesses are not available to validate it. He holds that the entire sugya there speaks after they learned, when one no longer needs to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam." So too, that which is taught, that two who bring a get, and a deaf-mute who became competent, do not need "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," speaks after they learned. Then one does not need "befanai nichtav." Likewise, the husband specifically after they learned does not need to say it. If so, now, because of our sins, we are not bnei Torah like the earlier ones, and therefore even the husband must say "befanai nichtav," for it is like before they learned. But according to us, who hold like Rava, who said the reason is validation, the husband does not need to say "befanai nichtav," since he himself brought the get and his challenge is not effective. He is also not suspected of deliberately ruining her, as written in Tosafot. The Rama added on his own that he must say "befanai nichtav" so as not to confuse it with another agent, since we are not bnei Torah. He holds that the reason that we are not bnei Torah means that because of this it is proper to decree lest it be confused with another agent. This does not appear to me, for if so, regarding two who bring a get we should also be concerned lest it be confused with another agent. The Talmud says that after they learned, nevertheless one person and a woman must say "befanai nichtav" lest the matter return to its ruin, and so as not to distinguish between agencies. Nevertheless, when the husband brings the get, or two bring it, we do not say "do not distinguish between agencies," and they do not need to say "befanai nichtav." We do not decree because of another agent.
