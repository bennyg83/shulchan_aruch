(א) From place to place. In the Talmud it is explained according to Rava, whom we rule like, that the reason one must say "it was written before me and signed before me" is because witnesses are not available to validate it. When he says "it was written before me and signed before me," the get is validated. In one country, witnesses are available to validate it, and one does not need to say "it was written before me and signed before me." The Rambam rules that from place to place one must say "it was written before me and signed before me" even in one country, while in one place one does not need to say "befanai nichtav." He holds that according to the conclusion, where the Gemara answers that the people of Mechoza are different because they travel, we learn that place-to-place is similar to the people of Mechoza, but within one place one does not need to say "befanai nichtav." So wrote the Maggid, chapter 7. Tosafot learned from this that even in one place its law is like the people of Mechoza.
(ב) He says: it was written before me and signed before me. The reason is as written nearby, and then the get is validated through him. Although generally we hold that validation requires two witnesses, here, because of the enactment for agunot, Chazal were lenient, since validation of documents is rabbinic, for we hold that witnesses signed on a document are considered as though their testimony was investigated in beit din, as written in the Talmud. Therefore, when he says "it was written before me and signed before me," even if the husband comes and challenges it, we do not pay attention to him. According to those decisors who hold that when he claims it is forged, validation is required biblically, and in the previous siman I wrote that this is the view of the author, according to this it is seemingly difficult: when the husband comes and challenges that the get is forged, why does validation by one witness help, since validation is required biblically? Perhaps if the husband comes immediately and challenges, the testimony of one witness is not effective. It is effective only when he does not challenge immediately at the time the agent says "it was written before me and signed before me," and then it is as though it was validated by two testimonies. Afterward, even if he challenges, we do not pay attention to him, since it was already validated. According to the Rambam, who holds that all testimony in a shtar is not biblical testimony, this sugya is difficult to explain. See Responsa Maharshdam, siman 1 and 2. When the get is validated through the agent, she collects the ketubah from purchasers, for this is learned from the ketubah text: when you marry another, you will take, etc. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 141, seif 53, that she does not collect from purchasers, because there the permission is not complete, for there is concern that he may yet come and challenge. Therefore she does not collect from purchasers. But here she is completely permitted, and the husband’s challenge is not effective. Therefore she collects from purchasers. However, regarding the additional ketubah, the Ran and Maggid, chapter 7, wrote in the name of the Ramban that she does not collect, for the derivation from the ketubah applies only to the main ketubah and not to the addition. Since she does not have it by the ketubah derivation, she does not have it by law, for we hold that validation of documents requires two witnesses regarding monetary matters. At first glance, it appears that even from the husband she does not collect the additional ketubah, since regarding money, validation by one witness is not effective, and we do not apply the ketubah derivation to the additional ketubah. All the more so when one brings a get in Eretz Yisrael, where she does not collect the additional ketubah from her husband by virtue of the ketubah text: she collects from liened property, but the ketubah derivation is not effective for the addition. However, one can say that for this matter authorization is effective, for it is as if the get was validated by two witnesses, and she may collect the additional ketubah.
(ג) And likewise an agent who brought a get from Eretz Yisrael, etc. But women who hate one another, some invalidate them from bringing a get in Eretz Yisrael, even if they say "it was written before me and signed before me," as written in siman 141. Regarding women we are more concerned, because they are not learned in the laws, and they think that the husband can challenge even when she says "it was written before me and signed before me." Since generally in Eretz Yisrael he can challenge, perhaps she errs in this and testifies because of hatred, thinking that the husband can challenge. But outside Eretz Yisrael, where he cannot challenge, we do not say that she thinks the husband can challenge.
(ד) This is invalid. So wrote the Rambam. According to the rule he wrote in chapter 10, if she married, she need not leave. Although according to the Rambam all testimony in a shtar is only rabbinic, nevertheless whenever there is no challenge against it, it is invalid only rabbinically. See Kesef Mishneh. But if the husband challenges and it cannot be validated, he holds that it was not done according to the enactment of Chazal, who enacted that if the husband challenges, validation is required. Therefore the get is void. See Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7. But according to Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur, if he did not say "it was written before me and signed before me," she must leave. So ruled Shiltei Gibborim, namely where he cannot take it from her, give it again, and say "befanai nichtav," as written in seif 7.
(ה) She is not divorced. So wrote the Rambam. According to this, the get is void, and if she married, she must leave, and the child is a definite mamzer. The Rosh wrote in the name of the Rambam that he is a safek mamzer, and so wrote the Tur. At first glance this is astonishing, for at the beginning of chapter 7 it is clear that he is a definite mamzer. The Bach wrote that what the Rosh wrote, "safek mamzer," is where the husband challenged after the get was lost. But if he challenges while the get still exists and it cannot be validated, he is a definite mamzer. This does not appear to me, for how could the Rosh write in the name of the Rambam something that he does not write? Certainly there is a scribal error in the Rosh. What the Tur wrote, that he is a safek mamzer, is because he holds like those decisors brought by the Maggid there, who hold that he is a safek mamzer, for perhaps it will yet be validated. What is written that when the get was lost she is doubtfully divorced does not mean regarding mamzer, for he wrote that when the husband challenges, the child is a safek mamzer. Learn from this that when the get was lost, it is not a safek mamzer, but rather she is doubtfully divorced regarding kiddushin from another. So wrote the Derishah. Here too it is proven that the Rosh and Tur hold that if he claims forgery, validation is required biblically, for otherwise he is not a mamzer and not a safek mamzer, since the signed witnesses are as though their testimony was investigated in beit din, and it is only rabbinically invalid. Rather, certainly they hold that validation is required biblically. I already proved this in the previous siman from the decisors brought by the Maggid. See Responsa Maharik, shoresh 77, and the Shach in Choshen Mishpat, siman 46. I do not know how they did not mention all these decisors, and the Shach did not mention what is explained in this siman and the previous siman. See Responsa Maharshdam, siman 1 and 2.
(ו) That it should not be given while the husband is in the city. Although here it speaks where he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," learn from this that he holds that the husband can always contradict him and say that he is not his agent. See what I wrote on this in the previous siman.
