(א) You are an agent for delivery, etc. If he said: you are an agent for delivery until there, and there you will be an agent for receiving, it is not a get at all, because the agency did not return to the husband. It is worse than what is written at the beginning of siman 141, for there the agent was fit to return his agency to the husband at the beginning of his agency, but here he was not immediately fit to return his agency. If he said to her: there, you shall make an agent for receiving, then it is similar to his courtyard that comes afterward, and it depends on the dispute at the beginning of siman 141.
(ב) Until such-and-such beit din. See Beit Yosef, what he wrote in the name of Tashbetz: if he sends through one person to give the get to his agent, then the first person is not an agent for divorce at all, for he performs only the act of a monkey, and he is not believed by his statement "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," unless he says that he shall appoint whichever agent he wants to deliver the get to. According to this, it appears to me that also where he told her to deliver it to a beit din, it is specifically if he told her to deliver it to whichever beit din she wants that what she says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" is effective. But if he designated the beit din and said, "deliver it to such-and-such beit din," then she was not made an agent at all, and what she says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" is not effective. One can distinguish, since the husband did not make the beit din his agent, but rather commands through her.
(ג) And she must say it, etc. Although if she said plainly, "I was divorced," she is believed when the get is in her hand, as written nearby, nevertheless when she says that she is an agent, she must say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" like any other agent, and she is not believed through migo. So wrote the Ran. If she delivered it to an agent for delivery and did not say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," it is not effective, because she has already performed her agency. So wrote Terumat HaDeshen. However, in Responsa Rivash, siman 318, he wrote that where the first agent made another agent, there is a remedy: the get should be returned to the first agent’s hand, and he should appoint him before beit din. So it also appears from seif 7, and as written in the Talmud: one who brings a get from overseas and did not say "befanai nichtav" should go back and take it from her. What difference is there whether he gave it into her hand or into the agent’s hand? One should not say that there it speaks where the husband gave him permission, for the husband is not there, since the Mishnah teaches "one who brings a get from overseas." Also, the husband’s permission is not effective for this matter. The Talmud gives another explanation in this sugya: where he said to her, "be an agent for delivery until there, and when you arrive there make an agent for delivery." Tosafot wrote that then she does not need to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam" when she gives the get to the agent, but only when she receives the get from the agent. The Rashba and Tur disagree with Tosafot and hold that she must say "befanai nichtav" at the time she gives the get to the agent.
