(א) Since there are witnesses, one does not need to say "befanai nichtav." The Bach wrote that even if the witnesses do not recognize the signatures, nevertheless, since they testify that the husband delivered this get to the blind person, he does not need to say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," because he would not deliberately ruin her. If the husband comes and challenges, it must be validated. See nearby.
(ב) And he gives it to her before those witnesses. So that it should be a whole matter and not half a matter. However, regarding this law the Rif and Tosafot disagree, as written in Tosafot, Perek Merubah, page 80. According to the Rif, whenever the observation of one group is not effective without the second group, it is half a matter. Therefore, if one group says that they saw one hair and a second group testifies about another hair, it is half a matter. But when three groups of witnesses testify to a chazakah of three years, each group testifying that he ate for one year, it is a whole matter, because each group is effective to remove from her the produce of that year. According to Tosafot, the matter depends on whether they could have seen more. With hairs, they could have seen two hairs and did not, so it is half a matter. But regarding chazakah, in that year they could not have seen more. According to this, the law here depends on this dispute. The Ramah, who holds here that it is half a matter, holds like the Rif, for according to Tosafot, those who saw the delivery could not have seen more. But according to the Rif, since delivery alone is not effective, it is half a matter. With this, what the Darkei Moshe asked is resolved. See there. I saw all this also in the Taz. As for why it is effective when she appoints an agent for receiving, and one group testifies that she appointed him as an agent for receiving while another group testifies that he received the get: according to Tosafot, this is fine, because they could not have seen more. According to the Rif, one must say that witnesses of the statement do not need witnesses of receiving, since the get is in his hand. Tosafot rejected this answer there, and so wrote the Rivash, siman 318, according to the Ramah. From this it is also proven that the Ramah holds like the Rif.
(ג) It is valid in every case. Meaning, even outside Eretz Yisrael, even if he was blind from the beginning, and even if there are no witnesses here that he delivered the get to him. Nevertheless, he must recognize the man and woman by voice recognition.
