(א) The husband appointed, etc. According to the Rambam, the husband cannot challenge. So wrote the Rosh at the beginning of Gittin: because he knows that if he comes to challenge, his challenge is void, he signs valid witnesses. So it also implies in Responsa Rivash, siman 318. See there in the Derishah. The Bach wrote there that the Rosh says the opposite, and this is astonishing to me. What the Tur wrote, that the husband can challenge, is the "some say" brought in the gloss. It is possible that he wrote this to be concerned for the view of Rashi, who explained there at the beginning of Gittin that two come, and if the husband comes to challenge they can validate it. So too he wrote in chapter 2: two who brought it do not need to say "befanai nichtav" because they are witnesses of validation. Learn from this that it is effective specifically when they can testify about his signature. The Tur is concerned for Rashi’s view.
(ב) For his agents are his witnesses. And it should be no worse than if they said "she was divorced before us," where they are believed. Therefore they must be valid to testify, and then this migo applies.
(ג) And he challenged. Meaning that he challenges by saying that he gave her a forged get. Although one does not deliberately ruin her, this means that we do not suspect this, but if he challenges and claims this way, his claim is a claim. This is unlike where he claims by contradicting them, saying that he did not send them, for there he is not believed according to anyone.
(ד) And some say even if he did not appoint, etc. So wrote Tosafot, the Rosh, the Ran, and the Rashba. They wrote that perhaps even the Rambam agrees to this, since there are two witnesses that he delivered the get from his hand to theirs, and no one is found who disagrees with this. What the Beit Yosef wrote, that the Ramah disagrees with this, is astonishing, for the Tur wrote about this: and so wrote the Ramah. Rather, the Ramah holds that the get must be delivered before those two together, as written nearby, but regarding the essential law we have not found anyone who disagrees. Perhaps he understood this from the Rambam’s language, where he wrote "he appointed two agents," implying specifically where the two are agents. In Responsa Rivash too, he wrote this in the name of "some say."
(ה) And there is one who requires. This is the reasoning of the Ramah: whether both testify that he delivered it to him, or whether one testifies, these witnesses must be present at the time of delivery of the get to her. All the more so when both are agents, they must be present at the time of delivery, for they must fulfill their agency. See Bedek HaBayit. The reason the witnesses must be present at the delivery is so that it not be half a matter. Since the agent will not say "befanai nichtav," and the essence of the get is through these witnesses who say that he delivered the get to him, they must be there. In seif 12 he ruled plainly like the Ramah, while here he wrote "there is one who requires." I saw in Bedek HaBayit that he asked here against the Ramah: why is this half a matter? The witnesses of agency are separate and the witnesses of delivery are separate. Therefore he wrote here "there is one who requires." The fact that he did not ask this above regarding a blind person may be because a blind person is not fit for agency at all without witnesses, so the Beit Yosef agrees that it is half a matter. But with a valid agent, where two testify that it was delivered to him in their presence so that he does not need to say "befanai nichtav," he holds that it is a whole matter. What it means that one testifies that the get was delivered to the agent in his presence is that he says the husband appointed him as an agent before two and delivered the get before him, as written at the beginning of siman 141, that we do not join the agent.
