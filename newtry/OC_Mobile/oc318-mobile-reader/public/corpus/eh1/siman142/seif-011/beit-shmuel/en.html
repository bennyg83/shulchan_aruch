(א) For he cannot say "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam." The sound of the quill is not effective for a blind person, because if he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," it looks false, and if he says "I heard the sound of the quill," he has not spoken according to the enactment of Chazal. So wrote the Rosh. See Tosafot and the Ran.
(ב) This one says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam." This means specifically before three, as the Rambam wrote, for he is disqualified as a judge because he is blind. But regarding the testimony that he says "befanai nichtav uvefanai nechtam," he is valid, for a woman and a relative are valid for this testimony.
(ג) By recognition of voice. See siman 132, where I wrote in the name of the Maggid that nowadays no person is believed by visual recognition, even a Torah scholar. One should not distinguish between visual recognition and voice recognition, for according to the sugya in Perek Gid HaNasheh it is proven that they are equal. At first glance it is difficult why here he rules that voice recognition is effective. One should not answer that we can test him whether he recognizes her, for if so, there too one could test him and place before him other gittin written like this one. Perhaps since we know that this is the wife of the sender of the get, we rely on the assumption that he too recognizes her, since he gives it to this woman and not to another woman. According to this, if we do not know whether this is the wife of the sender, we do not rely on the blind person, even though he says that he recognizes her by voice. Just as he must recognize the woman, so too he must recognize the man by voice recognition, as written in the Talmud, that he does not know from whom he takes it. This seif discusses where he originally could see, and then it was delivered to his hand, and he knew from whom he received the get. In the following seif, where it writes that it was delivered to a blind person, he must recognize both the man and the woman by voice.
(ד) Two valid witnesses who are not relatives. See siman 141, seif 36. There he did not write that it must be given before valid witnesses. Learn from this that what he wrote here is only lechatchilah. So wrote the Taz. The proof of Terumat HaDeshen there is not decisive. Therefore, bedieved, if she married, it is effective even with a relative or a woman, as with identifying the chalutz.
