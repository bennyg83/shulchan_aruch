And Rabbenu Tam explained, etc. See Tumim, siman 80, s.k. 9, who wrote that according to Rabbenu Tam we say this specifically where one pledged for his daughter and his grandson, but not where he pledged for another orphan girl. Otherwise, in the case where they disagreed about one who vowed money to an orphan girl, etc., the question would be difficult: it should be a dowry of bridegrooms according to Rabbenu Tam. Rather, it is clear that this was said only where one pledged for his daughter, for his entire intention was that his daughter benefit. But one who vows to marry off an orphan girl intends a mitzvah, and his mitzvah has been fulfilled. This is clear. By this is resolved what Beit Shmuel asked at the end of s.k. 4 against Maharshal and Bach, that where one pledged for his daughter and she died, her heirs do not acquire; why is this different from one who vows for an orphan girl? It is not difficult at all, for with an orphan girl there is no presumption of intent, whereas with his daughter there is a presumption of intent.
