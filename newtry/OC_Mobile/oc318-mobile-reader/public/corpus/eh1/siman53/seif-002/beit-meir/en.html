Therefore, the poskim, etc.; and she fell before his brother, etc. The Yerushalmi asks: are these not matters acquired by speech? It answers: one pledges on condition that he marry her. Tosafot in Perek Na'arah SheNitpatet, folio 47, inferred that if he married her and died, his brother collects from the father. They wrote that even according to Rabbeinu Tam's approach in the gloss below, this is understandable, since the daughter is alive, meaning that if she undergoes yibbum, she will benefit from the money through the yavam. One may ask according to the Rivash's approach in Shulchan Aruch siman 163, that the yavam does not inherit his brother's loan because it is only prospective property; if so, even if it was acquired by speech, what of it? In the end the yavam will not inherit, since it is a loan. Rather, who will inherit? The father of the deceased, and with regard to him Rabbeinu Tam's reason applies, for he wrote only on condition that his daughter benefit, and he is exempt from every angle, unless the yavam argues: nevertheless give me what you pledged to my brother, for I married your daughter like my brother. The Tanna says that he can say, etc. If so, what was the Yerushalmi's difficulty? According to this, it could even be speaking after she married. At first glance, according to Rabbeinu Tam one would be forced to accept Mahari Weil's view that a yavam inherits a loan. But one may say that the Yerushalmi understood that it speaks even of one who pledged a sack of actual coins, as below, where pledge by way of kiddushin also applies, and therefore it was forced to establish it at betrothal. But with a loan, according to Rabbeinu Tam, even if he died after marriage, the law is as I wrote according to the Rivash. Alternatively, it implies to the Yerushalmi even where the yavam is the sole heir and no one else is with him, in which case he certainly inherits even prospective property. Now the Rama ruled like the Rivash, and therefore a practical difference follows: if one pledged money that is not present, and the groom married and died before collecting, while her father, the yavam, is alive, this depends on the dispute of Rabbeinu Tam; according to him he is exempt. But below regarding a husband, whether this is called prospective property requires study. One may say that the law of the yavam is like his.
