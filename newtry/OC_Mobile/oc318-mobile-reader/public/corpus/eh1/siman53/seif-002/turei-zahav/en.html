One who pledges to his daughter, etc. I first explain the words of the Rosh and the Tur in this matter, on the Mishnah concerning one who pledges in the case of a yavam in Perek Metziat Ha-ishah. The Rosh cites Rav Hai Gaon: specifically where one pledged to his son-in-law, but if he pledged to his daughter, saying at the time of kiddushin, "I give my daughter such-and-such," the daughter acquires it. Even if the groom dies, the father cannot say, "I pledged to my daughter only on condition that she enter marriage with this groom." Nevertheless, if she did not marry and died, and she had a son, that son does not inherit that money. But if she underwent yibbum, the father cannot say, "I intended it for your brother," because he pledged to his daughter and the kiddushin were not voided. Maharshal explains that since her son does not inherit the money, she herself must not have acquired it; otherwise, by what right would the son not inherit? Accordingly, the father may retract if she marries someone else, because she has not yet acquired. But this is not the sense of the Rosh's language. If the Rosh meant to prove from the son that she had not acquired for other purposes after the groom's death, he would be proving one unestablished point from another. Who says the son truly would have inherited? Rather, the Rosh is disagreeing with the earlier writer on this very point. The earlier writer saw in Rav Hai only the distinction between pledging to the groom and pledging to the daughter, and understood that if the pledge was to the daughter she acquired in all cases, so that if she died her son would inherit. The Rosh argues that it is reasonable that the son does not inherit. This is like the case explained nearby, where one gives his daughter a gift not at the time of kiddushin and says it is on condition that she marry a particular man; if that man dies, she did not acquire at all. Here, although he gives to his daughter at the time of kiddushin and says nothing explicit, it is as though he said expressly, "for this particular man," because they are presently involved in that man's kiddushin. Therefore she acquires no independent right in the money except in relation to those kiddushin. The difference between pledging to the groom and pledging to the daughter concerns only yibbum. If he pledged to the groom and the groom died and she was taken in yibbum, the father can say, "I do not give to you." But if he said to his daughter at the time of kiddushin, "I give you," and the groom died and she was taken in yibbum, he must give that sum to the yavam. Since the daughter acquired at the time of kiddushin in relation to that groom, and the yavam comes through those same kiddushin, the yavam stands in the brother's place. Apart from that, after the groom's death she has no independent right; the money remains the father's.
