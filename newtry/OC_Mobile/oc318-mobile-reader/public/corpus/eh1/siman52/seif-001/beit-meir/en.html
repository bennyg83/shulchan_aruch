But with a minor, etc. The plain meaning implies that even if she reached the age of pe'utot, and even if she pledged not in her father's presence, her pledge has no substance. And although her sale is a sale, one must say that since this is only rabbinic, for her livelihood, in a case like this they left the matter according to Torah law. However, see Bach, who explains the Rosh: the reason he omitted the in-what-case statement is that he explained like Rashi, that it refers to the first Tanna of the baraita, that with a minor who pledges he agrees with Admon; therefore, since he rules like Admon, there was no need to bring it, for even with an adult woman we compel. It is still difficult: according to what the Tur explained, specifically where she pledged in her father's presence can she say, I thought my father would give; but where she pledged on her own, Admon agrees that she must remain until her hair whitens. He should have brought this as another practical difference, for where she pledged not in the father's presence, nevertheless with a minor we compel, since her acts have no substance. Perhaps one may say that he holds that with a minor who reached the age of pe'utot, the reason the first Tanna agrees with Admon is that everything speaks where she pledged in her father's presence. Although according to the first Tanna we do not accept the reasoning that an adult woman can say, I thought my father would give, nevertheless with a minor who pledges in her father's presence, even if she reached the age of pe'utot, we compel; the reason is that the first Tanna agrees about her, since in any case she is a minor, and the primary reliance of the betrothed man is ordinarily only on the father, so she can say, I thought my father would give. But where she pledged not in the father's presence, a minor who reached the age of pe'utot is equal to an adult woman and must remain. In this way it is proper that the Tur did not bring the in-what-case statement except according to the Rambam's approach, who rules like the Tanna of the Mishnah. But according to the Rosh's approach, which is the some-say view, there is no distinction except between pledging in her father's presence, where the law follows Admon and even with an adult woman we compel, and pledging not in his presence, where even a minor, once she reached the age of pe'utot, is like one who stipulated on condition that she remain. Regarding a minor who did not reach the age of pe'utot, it was unnecessary to teach anything, and even the Tanna did not discuss her, for it is obvious that her acts have no substance. If so, in practice, a minor who reached the age of pe'utot and pledged not in the father's presence requires study.
