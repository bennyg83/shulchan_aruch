(א) Since they did not read it immediately. With this the difficulty of Beit Yosef is answered. He challenged what R. Yerucham wrote: if they did not read it at all, she may not marry, but if she married she need not leave; and if they did not read it at all and it was burned or lost, she needs another get, etc. End quote. For also in the first clause one must say that it was lost, because if it is still in her hand, they can read it now. If so, his words contradict one another. Rama answers that the first clause speaks where after the giving they did not read it immediately, but only after time; therefore she may not marry ab initio. This is astonishing: what difference is there between immediately and afterward, and what is called immediately? It appears to me that certainly they saw the get immediately in her hand, and Ri holds as Mordechai wrote in the name of Maharam, brought by Beit Yosef nearby, that we require that at the time of giving the witnesses know that it was a get.
