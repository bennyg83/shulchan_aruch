Seif 2, "that they gave it to her with witnesses of delivery," etc. NB: Beit Yosef wrote two approaches to this. The first is that according to Rambam and those who support him in siman 133, that witnesses of signature alone suffice, here too it is enough that the witnesses of signature come before us and testify that no forgery was done. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that from the language of the Mechaber and Rambam it appears that it is enough for the witnesses of signature to come before us, provided that the get is given to her before witnesses of delivery. Although with an ordinary get witnesses of signature suffice, here it must be given before witnesses of delivery, only those witnesses need not be brought before us. With respect, his interpretation of the Mechaber is astonishing, for below in seif 4 the Mechaber writes that one may write on the shard of a flowerpot and give it to her only if the witnesses of delivery are before us, because a shard is something that can be forged. This explicitly implies that the witnesses of delivery themselves must be before us, and that witnesses of signature before us do not suffice. This requires study.
The second approach Beit Yosef wrote is that even according to Rambam, the witnesses of delivery must come before us because we decree: if witnesses of signature before us were enough, people might permit even where we have only witnesses who recognize the signatures. At first glance his words are hard to understand, for if so, we should also decree regarding witnesses of delivery lest the witnesses of delivery not be before us. It appears one should distinguish: with witnesses of signature, one who sees a get written on something forgeable being validated by witnesses of signature may not realize that they are required in order to testify that no forgery occurred; he may think they came only because no one else recognized their signatures, and he may therefore err and validate even through other recognizers of the signatures. But with witnesses of delivery, if he knows they are needed in order to testify that it was not forged, he will not err at all; and if he thinks they must be before us merely so we know that there were delivery witnesses at the time of giving, all the more he will not err. Nor will he learn from an ordinary get written on something not forgeable, since that case is plainly different. This appears to be the explanation of Beit Yosef's words.
Beit Yosef further gave the reason that it is like a document forged from within. Taz objected from what Beit Yosef himself later wrote, that if the text is on an erasure and the witnesses are on paper, it is valid with witnesses of delivery; if it were truly forged from within, witnesses of delivery would not help at all. Taz did not see the words of Chelkat Mechokek, who wrote that it is only like forged, not literally forged, and witnesses of delivery before us therefore help. Thus Taz's difficulty there is not difficult; see there.
