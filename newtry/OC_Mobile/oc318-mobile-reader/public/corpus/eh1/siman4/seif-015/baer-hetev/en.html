Prostitute. Same if witnesses came that she committed zenut — her sons are fit, for we follow the reason of majority of cohabitations attributed to the husband; therefore even if witnesses that she committed zenut — only for the end of the mishnah we mention that even with gossip and voice a kohen is concerned for her from Torah law. See Kenesset HaGedolah who expanded. And he wrote siman 6 seif 17 the Mechaber ruled: if a voice went out that she is a prostitute in the city — we are not concerned and she is permitted to a kohen. Later authorities answered: Dreyon is worse than voice of prostitute in the city. Bach wrote: here discusses married woman which is worse; below discusses single woman — see there. What Beit Shmuel wrote that in sugya Gittin it says "like tannaim" — proves discusses married woman and even so not concerned — see there. I did not understand — examiner in Gemara there 89a in Rashi and Tosafot will see the law is with Bach — see there. So too appears from Gemara Ketubot 36b — he challenged there "Rava said a voice went out for her" — implies discusses single woman — see there.
To the husband. Because the husband is available to her, unlike a fiancé — even if he certainly cohabited with her, because he is not regularly available to her — Beit Shmuel.
Torah. Meaning lekhatchilah; but bedi'eved if she married we do not remove. And what is written nearby "if her husband is kohen we are concerned for the sons" — meaning lekhatchilah — Beit Shmuel. Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 141 wrote: specifically with voice that ceased; but with voice that did not cease — certainly we remove. From this reason he ruled there regarding one woman who sat under her husband and a voice went out that did not cease that she committed zenut under him, and she was widowed and stood and married a kohen — we compel him to divorce, for it is voice from before the marriage — see there. See Mahari Weil siman 24.
Chalalim. For if she committed zenut under him once, even if she was raped she becomes a zona and is forbidden to kehunah; if her husband cohabited with her afterward the sons become chalalim. But if her husband is Israel — if she committed zenut intentionally, sons she bears afterward are fit even for kehunah — Beit Shmuel. If known her husband did not cohabit with her after the voice that she committed zenut — e.g. her husband was abroad and immediately when her husband left her a voice went out that she committed zenut — then doubt whether she conceived from husband before his departure and before the voice; then certainly just as the child is not mamzer from attributing to husband before departure, likewise not a chalal — one depends on the other — Choshen Mishpat.
For the sons. Meaning concern of mamzer, since it is not applicable to her to say majority of cohabitations from the husband. But most poskim do not say any mamzer concern even if she is very promiscuous — see Beit Shmuel and Kenesset HaGedolah.
