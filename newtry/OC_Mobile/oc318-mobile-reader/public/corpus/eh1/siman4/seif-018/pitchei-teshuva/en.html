And even if she miscarried in Tishrei, etc. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 19, who wondered greatly about this ruling; see there at length. See Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 78 ot 8, where he too wondered at Rama on this: from where should we permit her in such a case against the majority of viable infants and the majority that are born after nine months, etc.? He found it acceptable to resolve this because against those majorities there is the presumption that a woman who commits adultery turns over so as not to become pregnant; see above siman 13 seif 6. Presumably, then, the child is from her husband, since she did not turn over, and this supports the presumption of fitness and the presumption of righteousness. There in siman 81 ot 9 he wrote that he later found in Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 19, that he had raised this difficulty against Rama, as cited above, and analyzed the possibility of combining a doubt of coercion. Brit Avraham wrote against him that this is not correct, for even according to the poskim below, siman 308 seif 7 in the Hagah, who believe her when she says "I was coerced" against the rabbinic majority of willingness, nevertheless we do not believe her through a miggo of coercion; for Rama did not distinguish in this matter to forbid with the wife of a kohen or in a case like this, where we know she was always in a city in a place where coercion was impossible. See what I wrote above, siman 6 seif 11 s.k. 11. Rather, the main resolution is as I wrote. These are his words; see there.
