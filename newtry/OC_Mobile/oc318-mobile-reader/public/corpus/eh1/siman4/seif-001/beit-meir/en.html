And they are given. See Beit Shmuel, who wrote that Rabbenu Tam also holds according to this view. This is not merely similar; it is even stronger, for by Torah law they are forbidden forever, and David's decrees applied only to a slave. Nevertheless they have a fine, and a convert or freed slave is permitted to them, for they are not actual slaves, since their bodies were not subjugated to a specific person. See Chiddushei Rashba. He further wrote, "one may also say," etc. This requires study, for if so, how does he conclude, "and if one of these had relations with a Jewish woman, the child is valid"? Rather, in my humble opinion, that refers to them while still gentiles, and seif 6 begins the case where they converted.
