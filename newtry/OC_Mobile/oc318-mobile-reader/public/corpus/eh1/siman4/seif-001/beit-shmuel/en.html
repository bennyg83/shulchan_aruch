And the Nesinim. In chapter HaArel, Rava first said that the verse, "You shall not intermarry with them," refers to the seven nations while they are still non-Jews; after conversion they are permitted, except for the Nesinim, upon whom David decreed. Rava then said that the verse refers to them after conversion, whereas while they are non-Jews the concept of marriage does not apply. The Rambam, in chapter 12 of Hilchos Issurei Bi'ah, rules according to the first version: all seven nations are permitted after conversion, except for the Nesinim, who are from those nations and upon whom David decreed that they may never enter the congregation. According to this view there is no alternative Torah prohibition. This is also the view of Rashi at the beginning of chapter Eilu Na'aros and of the Baal HaMaor in chapter HaArel. The Ramban and Rashba, however, rule according to Rava's second version, so all seven nations and the Nesinim are forbidden by Torah law. Children born after conversion are permitted, except descendants of the Nesinim, who remain forbidden because of David's decree. Rabbeinu Tam also follows this approach that they are forbidden by Torah law, but he holds that David's decree concerned their servitude. All those listed here are therefore forbidden even after conversion. Se'if 5, which states that when a Jew has relations with one of these women the child follows her, refers to them before conversion. It may also refer to them after conversion: when a Jew has relations with one of them, the child follows the more disqualified parent and is disqualified.
