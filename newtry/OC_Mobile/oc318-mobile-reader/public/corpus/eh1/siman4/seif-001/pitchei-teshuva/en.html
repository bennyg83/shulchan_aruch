The child is valid. See Yaarot Devash, part 1, derush 17, where he wrote: It is known what is stated in the Gemara according to several views, that when a gentile has relations with a Jewish woman the child is a Jew, except that he is called a mamzer; and according to the view that the child is valid, the child is a gentile. But according to the view that the child is a mamzer, he is a Jew, only a mamzer; if so, Daryavesh, who was Esther's son, was a Jew, only a mamzer, etc. End quote. At first glance I was very surprised: what compelled him to say that according to the view that the child is valid, the child is a gentile, so that he therefore had to base his derush only according to the view that the child is a mamzer, which is not the halachah? We rule that the child is valid, and in truth from the plain wording of all the poskim it appears that the child is valid and is a complete Jew. It is true that Piskei Tosafot, chapter Asarah Yuchasin, siman 142, wrote so, but there is no necessity for this in the words of Tosafot there, Kiddushin 75b, s.v. "ve-Rabbi Yishmael," as Maharsha wrote there; he also wrote that Rashi's words contradict each other on this; see there. See further Maharsha, Yevamot 16b, s.v. "Amoraei," and examine carefully. Although one does not answer derush, this matter was not needed at all for his derush; on the contrary, his derush would have been more correct according to the halachah, that the child is valid and is also a Jew. I later saw in Shaar HaMelekh, chapter 15 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, law 3, that he discusses this law at length. He writes there to resolve the contradiction in Rashi's words that Maharsha noted: Rashi's view is always that the child is a Jew, and what he wrote in chapter HaOmer, page 68, that the child is valid through conversion, he wrote only according to the initial assumption there that wherever we cast the child after the Israelite, the child is a mamzer, etc. But Tosafot's words do contradict each other. He continues at length, and at the end brings a powerful proof that the child is a complete Jew from the sugya in Bekhorot 47, and in Yoreh Deah siman 305 seif 18, where it is explained that he is obligated in pidyon haben. If he required conversion, why would he be obligated in pidyon haben, since Scripture says "your sons"? Rather, certainly he is entirely valid and is like a complete Jew; see there.
I later found in Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, simanim 2 and 3, a great analysis of this law. The view of the author of the Lissa responsum, of blessed memory, was that he requires conversion, following the plain language of Rashi in Kiddushin 68b, s.v. "leima kasavar Ravina." As for Maharsha's difficulty that Rashi's words contradict what he wrote on page 76, s.v. "u-farchinan," he resolved it well, distinguishing between one born from a gentile and one born from a slave, who has no lineage. Nevertheless he is forbidden to a mamzeret, because his conversion differs from that of a full gentile and he cannot be compared to ordinary converts. A proof to this is from Bekhorot 47, mentioned by Shaar HaMelekh above. The reason is according to what is explained in Yevamot 62, that gentiles have lineage only while in their gentile status, but after conversion they have no lineage. Therefore he needs conversion because as long as he has not converted he is still attributed to his father; but when he converts, they no longer have lineage, and automatically after conversion we cast him after his mother, so he is like a complete Jew. His law is that of a Jewish firstborn, he is obligated in pidyon haben, and he is also forbidden to a mamzeret for this reason. The author of Chemdat Shlomo disagreed at length and wrote that there is no proof from that Rashi according to the conclusion; from Tosafot in chapter HaCholetz, Yevamot 47, the opposite is proven, that he does not require conversion. This is also proven from the approach of Rif and Rambam, who rule that when a gentile or slave has relations with a Jewish woman, the daughter is not disqualified for a kohen; if she required conversion, she would certainly be disqualified for kehunah. So too it is explicit from the words of Re'em on parashat Emor and from Ramban and the French sages he cites there. It is also explicit from the great later authorities: Beit Shmuel at the beginning of siman 4, Taz and Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 268 s.k. 6 and 10, that the halachah is that he does not require conversion; see there. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 150, also plainly assumes that he does not require conversion and is counted among the congregation of Israel for every matter of sanctity; see there. This also appears to be the view of the Gaon author of Tiferet LeMoshe in Yoreh Deah siman 267, whom I cited in Pitchei Teshuva there s.k. 11, since he permits circumcising him on Shabbat; see there. So too it is explained in Responsa Brit Avraham, siman 2 ot 2 and siman 6 ot 6; see what he wrote on this.
