A married woman who says about the fetus that it is not from her husband — she is not trusted to disqualify it. {Rama: And some say specifically a married woman, for the son has a presumption of fitness; but a betrothed woman who says about her son that he is a mamzer — even though the fiancé says he is his and that his hand never left her hand — some say the son is a doubtful mamzer; and some say he is trusted. (The rabbi's reasoning in Beit Yosef regarding betrothed discusses Nissim in chapter Almannah regarding this case that brought these two views.)} But a father who says about the fetus that it is not from him, or about one of his sons that he is not his son — is trusted to disqualify him, and he is a definite mamzer. If the son has sons, he is not trusted even about the son. And if she says she conceived from a gentile or slave — the child is fit, for the husband cannot deny this. {Rama: And that a father is trusted about his son — specifically where there was no presumption of fitness based on the father; but if he has a presumption of fitness based on the father, the father is no longer trusted about him except with witnesses (R' Yitzchak in name of Rama). A father who said about his son that he is a mamzer and afterward retracted and gave an explanation for why he spoke thus at first — he is trusted (Maharam of Rozanberg). That a father is trusted about his son — regarding one who is in the presumption of being his son, such as from his married wife; but a single woman who says this is so-and-so's son and he says she conceived from a mamzer — he is not trusted to disqualify him, and she is trusted about him to validate him (Tashbetz siman 267).}
