A foundling collected from the marketplace is a doubtful mamzer — for example, when there is no proof that he was not cast there to die. But if there is proof that he was not cast to die — whether proof on his body, such as that he is circumcised, or his limbs are set and aligned as they do for infants, or he is anointed with oil, or kohl was put in his eyes, or an amulet was hung on him; or proof from the place, such as that he was found in a place where many people are present, or hanging on a tree in a place wild animals do not reach, and it was near a city, or found in a nearby synagogue, or at the sides of a public domain — there is no concern of foundling, since they are concerned to guard him and he was not cast there except because of famine.
