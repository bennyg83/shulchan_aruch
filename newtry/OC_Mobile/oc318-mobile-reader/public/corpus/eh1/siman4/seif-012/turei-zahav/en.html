And there is one who says even if his master did not marry him off, etc. — this is Tur's view; Beit Yosef challenged: Gemara implies only when his master married him a woman; but marrying a woman in his presence he did not go free — so Rif and Rosh; Taz siman 267; possible our rabbi holds though he did not go free nevertheless for prohibited-woman concern we are concerned; if not that they freed him he would not leave him to do prohibition — therefore kiddushin take effect from safek — see there; and Maharshal wrote here we are concerned for kiddushin — see there. Wonder: Tur wrote here kiddushin take effect — implies if another came and betrothed her afterward we are not concerned for him and no get needed; per Beit Yosef it would be safek — need concern for other's kiddushin to resolve difficulty — therefore copied Gemara: R' Zeira said slave who married free woman in master's presence goes free; R' Yochanan said to him: you have it; and I teach: one who writes betrothal document for his maidservant — Rama: betrothed; Rashi: betrothal document means "you are betrothed to me" and he betrothed her; other version: not betrothed as Rava bar Rav Shila when master left him tefillin — likewise when master married him a woman; is there anything servants are not made to do prohibition and he does transgression marrying his maidservant; R' Nachman bar Yitzchak: the incident — he said to her "go out in it and be betrothed"; Rashi: if he said "be betrothed in it" alone certainly freed from outset; now he said "go out in it and be betrothed" — this is your manumission and betrothal document — reveals he did not free except through this; R' Meir holds language "betrothed to me" includes manumission language and she is fit to be betrothed in it and be betrothed; Rabbanan hold this language has no manumission language; Tur holds perforce we do not extract here — per Rabbanan betrothal document language is not manumission except in betrothal specifically — possible he does not free now completely but says for future when I free you you will be fit betrothed; not now; unlike marrying now — certainly transgression without manumission — certainly now he frees in this marriage as saying explicitly "you are manumitted" — inapplicable to marrying free woman — no manumission now; nevertheless since master lets him do what slave may not — we say already freed implicitly — proof if master wants to deny manumission; therefore equal when master married or married in his presence — in both prohibition and both proof already freed; Gemara distinguishes only tefillin — if he puts on himself before master no proof of prior manumission since no prohibition as Tosafot there when master left him — no prohibition for blessing in vain — women bless on sukkah and lulav and many mitzvot; slaves not accustomed to tefillin — if not freed would not put on — see there; only here distinction between when master married versus marrying himself.
