His master. See Beit Shmuel who challenged why Gemara establishes when his master married him a woman and not when he married in his master's presence — this challenge Beit Yosef and Bach also raised. In my view no difficulty — can say when we re-establish the baraita of one who writes a betrothal document for his maidservant saying "go out and be betrothed in it" — we need not establish when his master married him off, but establish it according to the plain meaning of the sugya that he married in his master's presence; see and examine. So too Kenesset HaGedolah in hagahah on Beit Yosef seif 3.
