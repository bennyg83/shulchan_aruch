After his master frees him, he is like an Israelite for all matters. If his master relinquished ownership of him, or his master left him tefillin, or his master served as prayer leader and they called him to ascend to read from the Torah, or his master married him to an Israelite woman — he is not permitted to an Israelite daughter until his master writes him a bill of manumission; nevertheless, we are concerned for his betrothal. Some say that even if his master did not marry him off but he married in his master's presence, we are concerned for his betrothal (and all the more so if he married his maidservant, we are concerned for his betrothal) (Tur).
