The second. After the second divorced her, if she conceived from the first — not mamzer, for even one who returns his divorcee is not mamzer. But if the second cohabited with her after the first divorced her — nevertheless mamzer rabbinically, as Shulchan Aruch siman 14 seif 56 — Rambam, Beit Yosef, Bach. Perishah and Choshen Mishpat divided and wrote the implication of Tosafot beginning chapter HaIsha Rabba (Yevamot 88b) "the child is mamzer from this and from that" — implies from the first always mamzer even after the second divorced — see there. Bach agreed with Beit Yosef because of fine — so too with the second even after first's divorce. But if first and second divorced and she committed zenut with the second — no mamzer from the second — see there. Rashi's words prove: if the first returned her after the second's divorce — not mamzer rabbinically, for he wrote child mamzer from this and from that if she bore from both — complete mamzer; if again the first returned and she bore to him — mamzer rabbinically — perforce. Explained from his words: he did not write from the first mamzer rabbinically except where from the second mamzer deoraita and that is without divorce. Tosafot's words also explained when the second did not divorce — then from the first mamzer, as they wrote 92a s.v. veHachamim — if the first part mamzer rabbinically the last why not mamzer? Just as they fined regarding husband they also fine regarding paramour — even after husband died or divorced forbidden to him and child mamzer from him — perforce. If discusses when second divorced — where do we find they fined regarding husband? Certainly when second did not divorce — see what Mechaber Beit Yehuda on this Tosafot. Indeed Rashi 89b s.v. le'osro beVat Yisrael appears opposite — wrote there it is a general fine and to make a fence for Torah — perforce even after divorced from second mamzer from first rabbinically and forbidden to Israelite; else before second's divorce cannot say general fine and fence — logic suggests forbidden to Israelite lest they say mamzer married Israelite and world will think from forbidden relation; therefore needs get from second lest they say, etc.; likewise so. But certainly even after divorced from second mamzer rabbinically — then this concern does not apply yet they forbade Israelite daughter — perforce because of general fine — see Rosh beginning chapter HaIsha sheHalakh Ba'alah laMedinah; see siman 17 s.k. 170 in Beit Shmuel and siman 16 s.k. 23 in Beit Shmuel what is written; see Kenesset HaGedolah siman 17 and hagahah on Beit Yosef siman 494; and siman 150 in hagahah on Tur seif 1; see Erlanach siman 42; responsum Maharaz Sasson siman 296; Rambam Galanti siman 120. Also I saw what Beit Shmuel wants to reject Choshen Mishpat's proof from Tosafot 89b — see there — I did not understand; if so why Tosafot and Rosh rejected it is not similar there they fined, etc. — see there — why not reject plainly there too regarding woman whose husband went abroad — if she divorced and afterward husband cohabited with her — not mamzer, similar to sotah, only if not divorced mamzer rabbinically because appears like forbidden relation. And indeed unresolved on Rama who copied Tosafot and Rosh: if she committed zenut and afterward husband cohabited — child not mamzer — unnecessary after Mechaber ruled specifically cohabited before divorced from second child mamzer; but after divorce if husband cohabited — not mamzer automatically like committed zenut and afterward husband cohabited — that is the law itself. For Tosafot and Rosh who brought view regarding woman whose husband went abroad — even after divorced from second if husband cohabited — mamzer because of fine; and 'A.D.' law likewise in sotah — needed to distinguish there no fine applies; but per Shulchan Aruch ruling — unresolved. Woman who was married and afterward was in presumption of divorcee and had no witnesses but voice and afterward bore and matter came out permissibly — Darkei Moshe, Beit Shmuel. One who expelled her husband suspecting her with so-and-so and court did not obligate him because there were no witnesses of seclusion or ugly matter — she went and married the suspect and bore him a child — even mamzer rabbinically is not — R' Yehuda of Arbi siman 254.
