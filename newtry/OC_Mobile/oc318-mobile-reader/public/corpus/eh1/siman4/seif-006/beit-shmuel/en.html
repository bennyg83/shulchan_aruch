The child follows the more disqualified parent. We rule that wherever kiddushin takes effect but the union is prohibited, the child follows the more disqualified parent, as taught in the Mishnah at the end of chapter HaOmer.
Even for kehunah. So R' Yochanan holds; though their cohabitation was in transgression nevertheless the daughter is valid; in chapter Almanah LeKohen Gadol an Ammonite convert who cohabited with an Jewish woman disqualified her; so the Rif, the Rambam chapter 8, and the Rosh. Therefore difficult on the Rosh; how he learned non Jew/slave with Jewish woman blemishes the child for kehunah from widow Kohen Gadol a fortiori when Ammonite convert proves he blemishes in cohabitation yet daughter valid for kehunah; the Rambam's reason: non Jew/slave the child valid for kehunah because holds a fortiori refuted; Tosafot challenged on sugya in HaAreil; Bach wrote on Ammonite convert who married Jewish woman; daughter valid for kehunah; but non Jew/slave because kiddushin do not take effect; also could derive from widow a fortiori because unequal prohibition and did not give reason kiddushin take effect. Appears he omitted Tosafot's words there.
Second the child. Maggid wrote on sugya "who are born to them"; Scripture tied them to birth, meaning tied even to mother; regarding father plainly tied. For poskim who follow mother; do not challenge "converts follow more disqualified party"; Rashi and Tosafot Kiddushin 64: only with two nations, not one nation.
