Like other foundlings. So wrote the Rambam, and he holds that the case speaks where there is concern that perhaps he is a mamzer. It appears from his language, where he writes "he is a doubtful non Jew regarding lineage," that he explains Rav's statement "but not regarding lineage" as meaning lineage such that we would not attribute him to a valid Jew: we rule stringently and do not follow the majority. That is, as long as he has not immersed, we say perhaps he is a non Jew and his kiddushin is not kiddushin; after he immersed, we are concerned that perhaps he is a mamzer. Even though the majority are non Jews, we do not follow the majority. Although regarding saving life we follow the majority, nevertheless regarding concern for mamzerut we are concerned that perhaps he is a mamzer. However, if there are two favorable majorities, it appears that even according to the Rambam's approach we follow the majority; if there are two non Jew majorities, we say after he converts that he is valid. For with her, if she does not say with certainty that she had relations with a valid man, two favorable majorities help at least after the fact, as written at the end of siman 6, even though the child has no presumption of valid status. Rashi explains that the case is where there is no concern here of a foundling or mamzer, because there are signs that he is not a foundling; the doubt is only perhaps he is a non Jew, and we are concerned specifically regarding lineage if the child is female, so that she is forbidden to a kohen even if there is a Jewish majority. But if there is a Jewish majority and he betrothed, the kiddushin is complete kiddushin; only regarding lineage did they make an elevated standard. Likewise, with a non Jew majority, after he immersed he is permitted to a mamzeret and we are not concerned for the Jewish minority. One should not ask why we follow the majority and do not say that anything fixed is considered half and half, for a found the child is like one who separated, and we hold that anything that separated is judged by the majority. This is also the view of Raavad in his glosses. Similarly, with her, when she does not say with certainty that she had relations with a valid man, two favorable majorities are required specifically for priestly lineage. And that which we hold regarding a shetuki, that even with a majority of valid men we are concerned perhaps he is a mamzer, as written nearby and in chapter Asarah Yuchasin, must mean that concern for mamzerut for an Israelite is also a lineage concern for a kohen. Maggid Mishneh wrote that after he converted he is permitted to a Jewish woman because of a double doubt: perhaps he was a non Jew, and if you say he was from Israel, nevertheless a doubtful mamzer is permitted by Torah law. Although the Sages decreed that a foundling is forbidden to enter the congregation, in such a case, where there are two doubts, they did not decree. But he is forbidden to a mamzeret, for there is concern that perhaps he is a Jew, and a foundling is forbidden to a mamzeret by Torah law. His words are astonishing, for the Talmud explains that a foundling is valid to marry an Israelite by Torah law, since "a certain mamzer shall not enter," but a doubtful mamzer may enter; and I already raised this difficulty above, and it requires study. From all this we hear that according to the Rambam's approach, even with a non Jew majority, after he converted he is forbidden to a mamzeret and forbidden to a Jewish woman. According to Raavad, with a non Jew majority he is permitted to a mamzeret and permitted to a Jewish woman. According to what I wrote, even where it is half and half he is permitted to a mamzeret. In Beit Yosef, since there is no definite prohibition when he marries a mamzeret or a Jewish woman, the first doubt, perhaps he is a non Jew, combines. If there are two non Jew majorities, we follow the two favorable majorities. See there in Pnei Yehoshua: it appears from the conclusion that with a shetuki and a foundling, because of the elevated standard they made for lineage, we are concerned lest he be a mamzer. But if there is a sign on the foundling, as written in the previous seif, that he is not a mamzer, he may marry a woman and we are not concerned that he may marry his sister. So wrote the Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchot Issurei Biah, law 29; see there.
