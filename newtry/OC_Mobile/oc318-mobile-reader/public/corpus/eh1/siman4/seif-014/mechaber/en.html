A woman whose husband was abroad and stayed there more than twelve months and gave birth after twelve months — the child is a mamzer, for a child does not remain in its mother's womb more than twelve months. Some say he is not presumed to be a mamzer; and since it is a dispute, he is a doubtful mamzer. {Rama: But within twelve months we are not concerned, for we say she remained thus long in her mother's womb (as in the incident in chapter HaAreil, and so too Maharik); and specifically if they did not see any ugly matter in her; but if they saw an ugly matter in her, we do not say she remained thus long, and we are concerned for him (in responsum Maimuni). A woman who conceived from her husband at the end of Sivan and gave birth at the beginning of Kislev — even though only five months passed between them, we are not concerned for her son to say she was pregnant before then, for months cause variation in birth (see below siman 156 seif 4), and he is a seven-month child (in responsum R' Yisrael Mintz siman 6). And even if she miscarried in Tishrei and heard the child crying, we are not concerned she was pregnant before then — it is possible for him to cry for five months, but he fell and was not fit to live (in Beit Yosef citing a responsum where the Radbaz so answered) (brought at end of Tur siman 178).}
