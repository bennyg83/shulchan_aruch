And if her husband is a kohen, etc. Beit Shmuel wrote: but if her husband is an Israelite, etc., because we do not hold like Rav Chisda in this, as written nearby, meaning seif 16 in the Hagah. So too it appears in Rabbenu Yerucham, siman 104, that regarding an Israelite it is obvious to him that even if she committed adultery and afterwards bore children from her husband, they are valid for kehunah. I found no one who arose to be stringent in this except Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter HaCholetz, siman 38, who wrote that a woman who commits adultery under her husband, whether he divorced and remarried her or never divorced her, has a child from her husband who is defective for kehunah according to those who hold here that with a gentile or slave the child is defective. He argues that all the more so here, where this kal vachomer exists and there is no reason of "she made her seed ownerless" as with a gentile or slave; therefore if a kohen married such a daughter, the children from her are chalalim. He wrote that his heart was not full enough to be lenient, for the kal vachomer is only an asmakhta and the mother alone is defective where there is no other reason. He wrote similarly in siman 55, while above in siman 38 he wrote that her daughter is defective and that if she married a kohen the children are rabbinically chalalim. What he wrote "rabbinically" contradicts his statement that his heart was not full enough to be lenient. In my humble opinion his main ruling is astonishing. First, the very refutation the Gemara asks simply, "what is unique about a widow, that she herself becomes profaned," applies here too, since immediately when she committed adultery under her husband she became a zonah and was disqualified to a kohen and from terumah. From this later intercourse with the husband she is not becoming profaned, for she already stands profaned, as Rashi writes in Yevamot. Second, Scripture says "she is an abomination"; the kal vachomer he uses can apply only according to the Rabbis who disagree with R. Yose ben Keifar in Yevamot 11b, for according to them there is a negative commandment with a sotah, unlike R. Yose ben Keifar, for whom it is only a positive commandment. According to the Rabbis, the rule that the children of one who remarries his divorcee are not defective is derived from "she is an abomination," and surely from the principle that Scripture does not leave its plain meaning; all the more this applies to the negative commandment of sotah, "after she was defiled." This is also proven in Yevamot 85b, and from the discussion whether she entices him. If her children would be chalalim, she would not entice him, as with a widow to a high priest. Therefore the essential law appears to me like Mahari Weil. With this one can also answer what Tosafot in Yevamot 44b left somewhat difficult: R. Akiva can expound "she is an abomination" regarding the children of a sotah, while agreeing that they are not mamzerim, as Abaye says that all agree in that case. He can also expound from the same phrase that her children are not defective. Even according to Ramban's glosses to the count of negative commandments, where he cites the Yerushalmi that R. Akiva says one who has relations with his sotah has a mamzer child, that may be after he divorced her, for then he holds kiddushin no longer takes effect with her; before he divorced her, while kiddushin still rests upon her, he surely agrees. See Tosafot there and Tosafot Kiddushin 68.
