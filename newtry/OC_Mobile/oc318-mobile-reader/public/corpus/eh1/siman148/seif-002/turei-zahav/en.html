And some say that this applies specifically when the get is in the husband’s hand. The reason is in the Ashri, Perek HaZorek, in the name of Ramah: when the husband gives it to her, the husband certainly did not have relations with her, for if he had relations between writing and delivery he would have been concerned for blemishing her children and would have written her another get. But through an agent she may not marry lechatchilah, for perhaps the agent gave it to her after seclusion and the husband did not know to write another get. The Taz discusses the difficulty that a get not delivered on the day of writing should be invalid because of the concerns of covering for her and produce. He answers that this whole siman speaks where the get was deposited with a third party to be given later. Such a deposit has publicity like a get sent from overseas, so there is no concern of covering for her or produce; the remaining concern is only that people may later say her get preceded her son. He further explains the cases of a conditional get in the same framework: the get was held by a third party until the condition was fulfilled. Therefore, when the husband himself later gives it after seclusion, there is a distinction from delivery by an agent or third party. This also resolves the Beit Yosef’s questions on the Tur and shows that the Tur need not disagree with the Rambam.
