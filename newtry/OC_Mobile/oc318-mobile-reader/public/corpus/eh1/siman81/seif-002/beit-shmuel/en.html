(א) A woman who said, let my hands be consecrated to their Maker, or who vowed that he not benefit, etc. Here the ruling is stated plainly according to Rambam, who holds that if she said konam, it takes effect even upon something not yet in the world. This is also the view of the Gemara. Konam also removes the matter from the lien. The reason her handiwork is not forbidden immediately is that the Sages strengthened the husband's lien; after the Get, the prohibition takes effect. Likewise, if she said let my hands be consecrated, even though her hands are subject to his lien, and the Talmud asks, are they not subject to him, so hekdesh cannot take effect, that was according to the initial assumption that hekdesh does not remove from a lien. But according to the conclusion that hekdesh removes from a lien, the hekdesh takes effect even though her hands are subject to him; only before the Get it does not take effect because the Sages strengthened the husband's lien so it does not take effect immediately, but after the Get it takes effect, even though at the time she consecrated them her hands were subject to him. So wrote Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12 of Hilkhot Nezirut. One may also say that the Gemara's question, are they not subject to him, follows Reish Lakish, who holds that she cannot say I will not be sustained and I will not work, as Tosafot wrote. But according to what we hold, that she can say I will not be sustained, the hekdesh takes effect, since she can say at any time I will not be sustained and I will not work. Although Rambam holds that he can consecrate her handiwork when he gives her sustenance, and we do not say it does not take effect because she can say at any time I will not be sustained, nevertheless when she consecrates it, the hekdesh takes effect because she can say I will not be sustained, etc. However, according to the Rosh and the Ran, one must say this applies specifically in the language of konam and in consecrating the hands; so it is ruled in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 234 in the gloss, and I do not know why he did not gloss here. If she is not sustained by him and she said let my hands be consecrated, then her handiwork is immediately forbidden. Hagahot Ashri. According to the one who says that excess produced with difficulty is hers and he eats the produce, if she said let my hands be consecrated, according to all opinions he must annul, for otherwise the hekdesh takes effect immediately on the excess, since he has only the produce and the Sages did not strengthen his lien. Tosafot.
