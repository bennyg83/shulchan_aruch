(א) And if she had relations, etc. As written above, siman 4.
(ב) And each one requires. 55. This is the version of the Maggid Mishneh, but it is not correct. Rather it should read: "and one requires," etc. So too is the version of the Tur. As written above in seif 5, the Rambam establishes that baraita on 20b, regarding a prohibition of mitzvah, etc., where both are forbidden. With this the wonder of the Maggid Mishneh against him there, chapter 7, halacha 10, is removed. What the Maggid Mishneh infers there, chapter 5, halacha 11, that the Rambam holds that even with equal ones she does not exempt from what is below, is not correct. He explains that he ruled like Rav, but this is not correct, as written below.
(ג) If one performed chalitzah, etc. He rules like Shmuel, as the Rif wrote, and like the latter answer. He explains that what is written, "when Shmuel says," etc., applies to the need to return to all the brothers specifically where the purpose is to exempt her rival. Then she must return. But if he performs chalitzah to each one separately, he does not need to return. So too it is explicit in his commentary at the end of chapter 5. See there. So too the Ramban and Rashba wrote that the approach follows Shmuel and speaks of equal ones, as written below: until each one performs chalitzah, etc. If it were not so, their chalitzah alone would suffice. He holds that with equal ones that fell before one brother, she exempts her rival, as above. The same applies before many brothers, where one performed chalitzah from all of them. But a divorcee, even if she performed chalitzah from all the brothers, it is not effective, for it is no better than if they fell before one brother, as written there in chapter 7, halacha 9, and as above, seif 18. His words are closed, as is his way. It is also possible to maintain the version of the above Maggid Mishneh: "each one," where they fell before several brothers, as written below. His approach is like that of the Rashba, who rules like Shmuel and like the latter answer, and wrote that we hold like the anonymous Mishnah of chapter 5, and not like what they forced in the Gemara to explain it according to the one who says there is no zikah. So too the baraita there, and so too the Mishnah that says he made maamar with this one and maamar, etc., and chalitzah. This also follows the Rambam’s approach. He disagrees with the Rashba only regarding two yevamin and two yevamot, and this is not mentioned in the Mishnah there or in the baraita there. The general rule is that his view is like the Rif and Rashba and like the latter answer of Shmuel, except regarding two yevamot and two yevamin.
(ד) And they disagreed with him, etc. See what is written there in seif 5. The general rule is that an excellent chalitzah exempts according to all opinions. Even according to the first answer, "he began and did not begin," the Rosh wrote in the name of Rivash that this is specifically there, because there their defects are equal, since it is karet, like the sister of his wife herself. Although Tosafot does not hold this way, nevertheless in law, certainly according to all opinions, it exempts, from the Gemara’s question regarding the one who received a get, etc. In the responsa of the Ramban, who rules like Rav, it does not exempt. According to the other decisors, who rule like Shmuel, it exempts. With an inferior one, according to the first language of Shmuel, it does not exempt, and so the Rosh ruled. According to the Rif and Ra'avad, it exempts, for they rule like the latter language. This is with brothers concerning one woman. With one yavam and two women, the inferior one certainly does not exempt, and the excellent one exempts. With equal ones, Rashi and those with him disagree with Ribam and Ramban, as written in the Rosh and as written there.
