(א) When they come to remove them, according to the one who says, etc. 51b: the latter clause teaches, and likewise, etc. And we hold that there is zikah, as written above, siman 159, seif 5.
(ב) And there is one who, etc. This is the view of the Rambam and other decisors, and so is the view of Tosafot, as written below. Seif 8 is the view of Rashi, as written in the Tur, and as Rashi wrote there, s.v. "they require," etc.; and the latter clause teaches, etc. Below he explains the source of their dispute. At first glance there is no distinction in the matter, for two yevamot are worse than two yevamin, as they said there: and if you wish, say, since, etc. But the Tur wrote: From the words of Rashi it appears that even according to the one who says that an inferior chalitzah requires, etc., with two yevamot and one brother, her rival is exempted through her chalitzah. Now Rashi wrote there: Rather, learn from this that they are equal, and her rival is exempt, unlike Rivan in Tosafot and the Rosh there. In this, Rashi’s words are very correct, for certainly according to Shmuel, when they are equal, she exempts her rival, unlike that language of "he began and he did not begin." This is as Rashi wrote in Perek Rabban Gamliel, 51a, s.v. "shall we say," etc., and rabbinically, etc. This is a great proof. There is another proof from what they said there, 52a: "How shall he say," etc. In all of them, it supports Shmuel, and in the middle clause should it say that it supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah, and is also not difficult for Shmuel? According to Shmuel it is simpler from the first clause. This is the view of all the decisors except Rivan. But what the Tur wrote, that even Rabbah bar bar Chanah agrees in this, the sugya of the Gemara implies this, for it says there, 51b: the latter clause teaches, etc., and it does not challenge from the first clause. And there, 53a, "shall we say this supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah," it does not say: the latter clause teaches. Rather, according to the above words of the Tur, the support is all the more so from the first clause, unlike an objection. So too it appears from Rashi in the Mishnah of Perek Rabban Gamliel, where he explains the whole Mishnah as chalitzah to one of them, and he does not mention any dispute. See the second discussion, s.v. "and chalitzah," etc., s.v. "maamar to this one and he had relations," etc., and s.v. "they require," etc. From this the Tur inferred and wrote: From Rashi’s words, etc. So too it appears from the language of the Mishnah, which says throughout the Mishnah get and chalitzah. But Tosafot there, s.v. "they require," etc., wrote like the Rambam, that according to Rabbah bar bar Chanah everyone agrees in two yevamot, and he says chalitzah incidentally to get. What is written on 52b, "the latter clause teaches," is comfortable for the Gemara to challenge from a comparable case, as the Ramban and Rashba wrote. And regarding what it says there, 53a, "shall we say this supports Rabbah bar bar Chanah," Tosafot on 51b, s.v. "Ben Azzai," wrote that it means from the latter clause: and likewise, etc. So too Rashi explains there, 53a, s.v. "they require," etc. Although according to Rashi it is also proven from the first answer, nevertheless Rashi did not want to derive by kal vachomer something explicit in the Mishnah: and likewise, etc. Furthermore, according to the questioner’s view, Rabbah bar bar Chanah also does not agree with two yevamot, for the first clause teaches "they require." But according to the conclusion, even according to Rabbah bar bar Chanah, one must explain "they require" in general in the first clause according to Rashi. The simple reading of the sugya implies like Rashi, as above. However, it is forced to say that Shmuel and Rav are opposites. Therefore the words of the Rosh appear correct, for he ruled like Shmuel, and then there is no distinction at all between yevamin and yevamot: where they are equal, in both cases they exempt, and where they are inferior, in both cases they do not exempt, like the first Tanna, as above. It emerges that there are three approaches in ruling: like Rav, or like Shmuel according to the first language, or like the latter language. In the approaches regarding exempting the rival, there are also three approaches: Rivan, Ramban, and Rashi, and two further approaches, those of the Rambam and Ra'avad, as written there.
(ג) And according to the one who says, etc. As written there and there, and like Shmuel there, 56 and 27. The Rif wrote there: the authorities ruled that the halacha follows Shmuel. The Ra'avad explained this as the first statement of Shmuel, for in the latter statement there is no one who disputes. So too the Rashba wrote, that according to Rav one must establish the above chapter of Rabban Gamliel like the one who says there is no zikah, against the halacha. So too it appears from the Rif, who brought the baraita there and ruled like the Rabbis, whereas according to Rav neither Rabban Gamliel nor the Rabbis apply. He ruled like the latter answer there: and if you wish, say, when Shmuel says, etc., although there is a superior one. This is the view of the Rif, for he brought only this answer. This is also the view of the Rambam, as written in seif 19, except that he explained it in another way. See there. So wrote the Ra'avad. But the Rosh ruled like the first answer: What is "one," etc. So too the Ra'avad wrote that one should do this lechatchilah, but bedieved the latter answer is primary. But the Ramban and Ramah rule like Rav. The Ramban wrote that even though we hold like Shmuel in the baraita, about this the Rif wrote that the authorities ruled like Shmuel, meaning the earlier authorities, because both are halacha. Even though we hold like the answer of Rav Ashi to Ulla, "as you said," etc., and he wrote that Rav Ashi applies only with an excellent chalitzah to exempt the divorcee. But where they are equal, we hold like Rav, who said that with the middle one, one performs chalitzah to both, and similarly where they fell at once before all three, as they said there. All the more so with rivals, where both are equal, one performs chalitzah to both, as written: "he began and he did not begin," etc. Even Rav Ashi disagrees only because they are not strong, etc., as written there: and if you wish, say, when Shmuel says, etc. This shows that it is stricter regarding exempting a rival. Since we hold like Rav, all the more so regarding exempting a rival. Regarding what it says there: They asked, the one who received a get, etc.; rather, is it not, etc., the Ramban explains like the Rosh in the name of Ribam, that both require chalitzah. So too the Rosh wrote in his name. The Ramban, of blessed memory, wrote that according to the words of Rabbi Eliezer, of blessed memory, etc., it teaches us that when we say, etc., and all the more so with the rivals, etc. But Rashi explained there, s.v. "they are equal," etc., that where both are equal, one chalitzah exempts, because specifically the inferior one does not exempt. This is as written in the first answer: since we have heard Shmuel, etc.; what is "one," etc. This shows that equal ones exempt. Even according to the latter answer, "and if you wish, say," etc., this reasoning remains regarding exempting a rival. What it says, "he began and did not begin," etc., was what he initially thought, and it is rejected in the conclusion. Even according to Rav, who says an inferior chalitzah requires, etc., even with equal ones, nevertheless regarding exempting a rival, equal ones do exempt, the opposite of Shmuel’s view according to the latter answer, for according to that answer a rival is more stringent. The Ramban also wrote this approach, but disagreed with it. A proof for Rashi’s view is from what they said there, 51a: Let this be a refutation of Shmuel, etc.; the latter clause teaches, etc. It does not challenge from the first clause against Shmuel and Rav. Rather, learn from this that with equal ones, regarding exempting a rival, everyone agrees that she is exempted, whether according to Rav or Shmuel. So too Rashi wrote there, s.v. "shall we say," etc.: from the Rabbis there is no objection, etc. This is what Rashi wrote on 52a, that the challenge to Rav is from the latter clause, as written here: and the latter clause teaches, etc. See the Ramban, who pressed himself on this and wrote that since it was not stated explicitly in their words, the Gemara did not challenge from it. The view of Rabbenu Chananel and the Rashba is like Rashi’s view. So too is the approach of the Rosh, who wrote: and this is primary, like Rashi’s explanation. He wrote that the first answer according to Shmuel is primary, and as written, "he began and did not begin" is necessarily like the latter answer. Therefore he ruled like the first answer, since we do not hold like the answer of "he began," etc., but like Rav Ashi. See Tosafot there, s.v. "what is one," etc., and s.v. "rather, since," etc. However, his words require study, for it is not proven that Rav Ashi holds specifically like the first answer, and the Lechem Mishneh wondered about him. Therefore the Rosh explains like Shmuel, since we hold like the first answer. It necessarily follows that Rav’s law is not so, except that he holds this specifically with equal ones. But the Rashba explains like the latter answer, that even an inferior one exempts according to the latter answer, as above. See Tosafot there, s.v. "he challenged him," etc. But Rivan explained, etc. The Rosh wrote: From here is proof that the halacha follows Shmuel, for Rabbi Yochanan holds like him. So too the Ramban and Rashba wrote, unlike Rashi, who wrote that the same question is also asked against Rav. One cannot say this, for what they said there: we learned, she was, etc., although Rabbi Yochanan said, "I do not know about sisters," etc., nevertheless all these should remain mistaken. And Rav Pappa answered: such as, etc. But according to Rav it is difficult. However, he wrote the second answer in Tosafot there: however, it appears to Ri as I explained, etc., that Rav necessarily is concerned for nullification, for he holds there is no zikah, as written at the beginning of the chapter: learn from this that there is zikah, etc., and there: but did Rav not say, etc. Nevertheless, one also needs the first answer of Tosafot, that Ri holds like Shmuel, for otherwise what is the challenge? But the first answer alone is insufficient, for Rav would still be difficult from the latter clause: we learned, etc. See the Ramban, who pressed himself on this and wrote that since it was not stated explicitly that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees with Rav, we do not reject Rav’s view. He gives another answer as well. See there. The Rambam has a different approach, explained further in seif 19. The general rule of the matter is that the latter statement of Shmuel is halacha according to all. But the Ramban rules in the first statement like Rav, and explains "and if you wish, say, when Shmuel says," etc., but Rav does not disagree even with equal ones. Rashi, however, holds that with equal ones it is not necessary even according to Rav regarding rivals. The Rosh rules like Shmuel according to the first answer, and the Rashba rules like the latter answer. Both agree in law with Rashi, that with equal ones she exempts the rival, as above. The fifth approach is that of the Rambam, as written below.
(ד) And that chalitzah, etc. This means specifically the sister of his zekukah, the sister of his chalutzah, one who received a get, and one who received maamar, where the zikah is weakened. The sister of his zekukah resembles the sister of his wife, where there is no zikah; the sister of his chalutzah resembles the sister of his divorcee; one who received a get resembles a chalutzah; and maamar resembles entry, where there is no zikah. So too a rabbinic sotah, regarding which we say at the beginning of chapter 6: Rabbi Shimon says her intercourse or her chalitzah, etc., and regarding this we say in the Yerushalmi: This means that Rabbi Shimon holds that an inferior chalitzah exempts her rival, because it resembles a biblical sotah, where there is no zikah, as written on 11a: impurity is written, etc. But otherwise, not. A proof is from what they said there: He said to him, you have taught it: she was, etc. Rather, is it not valid, etc.? And it says that he performs chalitzah to the disqualified one. And there: either she or her rival, etc. And there, 20b: Rava challenged: a prohibition of mitzvah and a prohibition, etc. See Tosafot 26b, s.v. "and chalitzah," etc. But the Rambam’s view is that Rashi holds that whenever he cannot perform yibbum, it is an inferior chalitzah, as written in chapter 15, halacha 9: the chalitzah of the sisters does not exempt the rivals, because the chalitzah of the sisters is not an excellent chalitzah, etc. It appears to me that the same law applies where he performed chalitzah to a sheniyah or to one forbidden by a lav, that she does not exempt her rival, as written above in seif 18 from that passage above, 11. It initially thought this way, but it does not stand in the conclusion. The case of prohibition of mitzvah, etc., he explains as where both are forbidden, so that equal ones exempt, as above. However, in the Tosefta, chapter 10, it is taught: One who was married to two women, the intercourse or chalitzah of one of them exempts her rival. If one of them was forbidden to one of the brothers by an ervah prohibition and he performed chalitzah to her, he has done nothing and has not exempted her rival. Rather, either she or her rival may enter yibbum with the other brothers. If she was a prohibition of mitzvah or a prohibition of kedushah, if he performed chalitzah to her or had relations with her, her rival is exempt. Rashba. See Maggid Mishneh there, and see below, seif 19. Here he writes plainly according to the view of Behag there.
