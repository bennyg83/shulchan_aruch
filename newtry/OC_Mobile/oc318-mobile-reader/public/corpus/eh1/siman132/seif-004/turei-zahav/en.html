(א) In the city where the get was written he was established, etc. This is the Tur's language, and Rashi also wrote similarly in Bava Metzia 18. My teacher and father-in-law wrote that Rashi implies that it refers to the place where the get was found, and from this he challenged the Tur for mentioning the place where it was written. Taz rejects this challenge. Since the formula of the get includes "I, so-and-so from such-and-such city," the place written in the get itself can identify whose get it is. Maggid Mishneh raised a similar difficulty on Rambam and answered that perhaps the city name was not written, and the get can still be valid. The Tur may likewise speak where the written place does not resolve the doubt. Taz discusses the relationship between two people with the same name, frequent caravans, the city of writing, and the city of finding. The governing issue is whether there is a real concern that the get belongs to another man of the same name. Where names and places identify clearly, the get may be returned; where the circumstances leave doubt, even without two established equal names in the writing city, frequent caravans can create concern. The note analyzes Rashi, Rambam, Maggid Mishneh, and the Tur to show that the place of writing is not an accidental phrase but part of the evidentiary structure of the get.

(י) And some ruled. This is Rambam's view according to the explanation written by Maggid Mishneh, which Beit Yosef brings briefly. He holds that according to R. Zeira, established names without frequent caravans neither add nor detract; rather, when caravans are frequent, he distinguishes what is called "immediate." There is a dispute whether "immediate" means that enough time did not pass for a person to pass there, which is a stringency, or that its measure is enough time for a person to stay there, which is a leniency. With one weakness, namely frequent caravans alone, we follow the lenient measure of "immediate"; and if there is another weakness, namely established names, we follow the stringent measure of "immediate."
