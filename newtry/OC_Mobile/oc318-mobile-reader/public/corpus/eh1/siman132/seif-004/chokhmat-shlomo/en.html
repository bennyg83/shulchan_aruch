In that same seif, see Beit Shmuel s.k. 10 and what he asked against the Mechaber from the Tur. Taz explains our text: even where the presence of another man with the same name has not been established and caravans are not common, we are nevertheless concerned if a person passed there. He explains that the Tur holds that where caravans are common, even if we do not know that anyone passed, we assume generally that someone passed and are concerned; all the more where we know someone passed. It seems possible to add reason to Taz's words. It is certainly unreasonable to say that the mere presence of caravans makes it more likely that a particular Yosef ben Shimon was there. Rather, the distinction is that in Torah doubts we are stringent, but with a double doubt we are lenient. Where caravans are not common, there are two doubts: perhaps no one passed there at all, and even if people passed, perhaps no one named Yosef ben Shimon passed. This is a double doubt. But where caravans are common, if someone passed there is no real doubt about whether people passed, for ordinarily people pass; there remains only the doubt whether his name was Yosef ben Shimon, and we rule stringently. One cannot add a second doubt by saying perhaps the get did not fall from him, because if it fell from him, his name necessarily was Yosef ben Shimon; this is a non-reversible double doubt once the defect before us is that a person passed there. It is unlike the case of a nail lodged in his back, where we do not know whether another person was involved at all. Thus where we know clearly that one person passed, all the more there is only one doubt and we are stringent.
See Beit Shmuel s.k. 10, who wrote that there is a scribal error in the Tur. Thank God, the book Baal HaItur came before me, and I found that under the third letter shin he wrote: our text is "one who brings a get and it was lost from him," and Rav Alfasi ruled stringently that if a person passed there and two men named Yosef ben Shimon were established, we are concerned like Rav Ashi. We wrote that even if there was no established duplicate and no common caravan, we are concerned. At first glance his words are difficult, since throughout the Talmud the dispute is only whether both conditions are required, or one alone, but where there are neither caravans nor an established duplicate, all agree there is no concern. Necessarily his meaning is that the Talmud speaks where we do not know whether a person passed there, and therefore at least one factor is needed: either common caravans or an established duplicate. But from Rif's language it sounds that even where we know someone passed, he still requires an established duplicate. Baal HaItur therefore wrote that even where caravans are not common and no duplicate has been established, we are concerned if a person actually passed there. This is the Tur's view, and the Tur holds that Rif agrees. What Baal HaItur then writes, "and so it is reasonable," must contain a scribal error that I could not correct. The Tur also writes that even if a gentile passed we are concerned, and Beit Yosef says this is implied by Rashba's novellae from the Yerushalmi. But Baal HaItur cites the Yerushalmi in a way that implies he holds that if a gentile passed we are not concerned, for he cites only the Yerushalmi's conclusion and no more. It appears he had another interpretation of the Yerushalmi. In any event we have gained that Baal HaItur disagrees with the Tur on this point, and it requires study.
