Under her husband's authority he inherits her. Ba'er Heitev. And see in Sefer Sha'arei HaMelekh chapter 10 of Laws of Marriage in the section Chuppah of Bridegrooms seif 4 what he wrote on this (and see in responsum Chasam Sofer siman 121 regarding a widow who entered the chuppah while a niddah and came under his authority and died before becoming pure). He elaborated on Beit Shmuel s.k. 7 and concludes there that he does not mourn for her, since entering his authority helps only for inheritance due to waiver, but not so that her husband a kohen become impure for her — automatically it is forbidden for him to mourn for her lest this cause stumbling regarding impurity. And as Tosafot similarly in Chullin 12a — they were lenient in mourning so as not to come to leniency in chalitzah, etc. And he concludes: all this when he did not know she was a niddah; but if he knew and entered intending to be with her afterward — see what the Rama wrote end of seif 61; and there are several investigations, etc. (see there s.k. 12).
