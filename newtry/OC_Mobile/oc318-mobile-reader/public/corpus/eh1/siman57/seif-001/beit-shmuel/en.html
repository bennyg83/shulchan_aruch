Since she came under her husband's authority he inherits her. But for other matters it does not help — he does not become impure for her, does not acquire what she finds, and does not annul her vows — as Rashi and Ran ruled; and so appears from the Rambam's language. See Tosafot in this sugya who hold he becomes impure for her and annuls her vows — and at first glance it is difficult: throughout the sugya folio 49 it appears that handing to the husband or his agents is no better than chuppah, and for several matters it is worse. Therefore per this, if she was not pure he does not inherit her per the Rambam's views — and so the questioner in responsum of the Rosh general siman 54, and so in Choshen Mishpat siman 60. Therefore per Yerushalmi that I brought in siman 54 — handing over does not help for a widow, for chuppah does not help for a widow; if so, say: what is different in end of seif regarding a widow whose agents did not go with her — the husband does not inherit her? Answer: even if her agents went with her he does not inherit. And from the Rambam who wrote this language — there is no proof, for the Rambam does not hold like Yerushalmi, as written in siman 63. And the Mechaber must hold that the law of handing over is like the law of chuppah — for chuppah discusses when her father is with her, or chuppah in her father's house; therefore we require chuppah fit for intimacy — then he inherits her. But when she is handed to the husband's agents to bring her to the husband, or a widow who went from her father's house to her husband's house — from that hour her inheritance is waived to her husband. And so Rashi there: since she was handed over, waiver takes effect. Therefore it appears even if she is a niddah he inherits her, for this law is not from the law of intimacy. And what is written in responsum of the Rosh that he does not inherit when she is a niddah — in truth the Rosh does not hold thus; rather the questioner wrote thus. And from this we learn: if she came to her husband on Shabbat — it is forbidden to be with her on Shabbat, for he had no share in her; and her inheritance he acquires due to waiver. And what is stated in the Talmud: this handing over is her entering the chuppah — follows Rav Ashi who said she eats terumah; but per Shmuel whom we rule like, that it helps only for inheritance — it is not due to chuppah but due to waiver. And a widow who went from her father's house to her husband's house, and the husband and his agents are not with her — nevertheless from when she came under her husband's authority he acquired her inheritance due to waiver. See siman 60. And Taz wrote: and it appears from this that even a widow for whom chuppah does not acquire — nevertheless here where the husband walks with her from the chuppah to the marriage house, he acquired her for everything due to handing over to his authority. And they did not say chuppah does not acquire a widow except when he does not walk with her — for in early generations they did not make chuppah on their columns; rather there was a special house for marriage, and thither the groom and bride go — and handing over his authority is not relevant there, for he did not walk with her but each comes independently. With this it is resolved why the Rama did not mention in siman 54 that for a widow chuppah does not acquire — for according to us, where the groom walks with the bride from our chuppah to the marriage house where he will be with her, he acquires her; therefore.
And some say that in his courtyard, etc. The Maggid wrote: also the Rambam should be said not to dispute this. And behold in his courtyard, if he is secluded with her it is chuppah and he acquires in all matters. Per this the baraita that teaches: if handed to the husband's agents, or if he has a courtyard and she entered with him there for marriage — he inherits her; but terumah she does not eat until she enters the chuppah — does not come to exclude except handing to his agents; for his courtyard is chuppah. And so Rashi in the second version: handing to his agents is marriage. See Rambam chapter 6 Laws of Terumot: by Torah law she should eat terumah from when she was betrothed, but the Sages forbade lest she feed terumah to her father, etc. And behold in the Talmud Ketubot folio 27 regarding the first Mishnah: a betrothed woman does not eat terumah — lest she feed her father, or lest a blemish be found in her and the betrothal be mistaken and she is a stranger retroactively. What is the difference between these two reasons? If he accepted the blemishes upon himself — the reason due to blemish is not relevant; and the reason lest she feed her father is relevant. And for the latter Mishnah there: she does not eat terumah until she enters the chuppah — and it says the latter Mishnah holds the reason is due to blemish; and holds before chuppah, even though he examines outside if there is a blemish — outside examination is not examination; therefore she does not eat terumah until she enters the chuppah, and then he examines inside. Uncertain on the Rambam why he wrote the reason lest she feed her father, and did not write the reason due to blemish — so challenged in Be'er HaGolah.
