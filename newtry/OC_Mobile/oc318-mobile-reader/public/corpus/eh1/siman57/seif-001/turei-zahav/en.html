And he entered with her on the way into his courtyard — so it should read; and so in the Gemara and Tur.
And was secluded with her there — it appears this seclusion does not mean seclusion fit for intimacy — that there is no person there but groom and bride; for then it is obvious he acquires even in her domain, as proven in siman 63 seif 5 that seclusion fit for intimacy acquires even for a widow. And further: behold it concludes at the end: he entered with the husband into a courtyard to lodge as travelers lodge in an inn — meaning there are also many people there; therefore also at the beginning it must discuss this Rabbi's view. Rather simply he did not intend this except to teach that this entering with him means she stayed with him there together that night — and not that she entered alone, as it says.
By herself from her father's house. Meaning: without the husband or his agents, as written afterward in the Gemara. But regarding the father or his agents — it neither raises nor lowers for a widow; specifically for a virgin na'arah who is in her father's authority — she did not leave his domain as long as he is with her. Not so for a widow.
And the husband is not with her — implies if the husband is with her, her husband inherits her even if she died on the way. And it appears from this that even for a widow — regarding whom we say in siman 63 that chuppah does not acquire her — nevertheless on the way mentioned here, where the husband walks with her from the chuppah to the marriage house, he acquired her for everything due to handing over to his authority. And even though the wedding is in the bride's father's house — it is not like her house, since the father already gave the room to the groom; even by way of loan it is like a sale. All the more if he brings her to the house where he will be with her. And they did not say chuppah does not acquire a widow except when he does not walk with her — and so wrote the Rav who edited. And his language: and it was already explained that she does not enter the husband's authority for her inheritance by going to him except if he or his agents are with her. Not so regarding chuppah in siman 63 — there he does not walk with her to the marriage house; for in early generations they did not make chuppah on columns, but there was a special house for marriage, and thither groom and bride go — and handing over authority is not relevant there, for he did not walk with her but each comes independently. With this it is resolved for me why the Rama did not mention in siman 54 that for a widow chuppah does not acquire — for according to us, where the groom walks with the bride from our chuppah to the marriage house, he properly acquires from handing over to his authority, as explained.
Or of both of them — per the one who says in siman 30 seif 3: when he gives betrothal into the authority of both, they are doubtful betrothal — there the reason is due to acquisition: if he acquires her that she be his wife, here there is doubt in the authority of both. But here he already acquired her at the time of betrothal — only he still needs marriage; and seclusion in general suffices. And with this the authority of both is properly said to mean they entered there for marriage.
