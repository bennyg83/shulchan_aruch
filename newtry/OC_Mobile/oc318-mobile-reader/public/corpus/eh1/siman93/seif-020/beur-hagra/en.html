(א) Or they gave. Tosafot 49b, s.v. "he," etc. See there. This is the view of all the decisors, unlike Ri bar Baruch in Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri.
(ב) If there is no, etc. From what is written in Gittin 52: come and hear, deathly ill, etc.
(ג) He consecrated, etc. For it is like a sale, as written in chapter 7 of Bava Kamma: what difference is there if he sold them to an ordinary person, etc.? Rav Nachman there. So too in Shevuot 42b: what difference is there with liened property, etc.? But not because hekdesh removes from a lien, for that applies specifically to bodily sanctity. See above, siman 90, seif 9, in the gloss.
(ד) Even after death. According to the explanation of Rivan there in Tosafot, s.v. "widow." See there, for Rabbenu Chananel and Behag disagree.
(ה) And now, etc. So too Tosafot wrote there, s.v. "Rabbi said," etc., and there, 51a, end of s.v. "from land."
(ו) However, etc. Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri in the name of Rabbi Shmuel. He wrote that what is written in Bava Batra there, "now, inheritance is biblical," etc., is a rejection, and we do not hold this way. A proof is from what is written in Ketubot 49b, that we do not hold like Reish Lakish, and they are not maintained from them, even though a gift is weak. Furthermore, we say in Bava Metzia 139a: rather, if you say, etc., even though he is a purchaser only rabbinically. All this is not enough to reject the words of Rava. This is the view of all the decisors, as written in Tosafot in Ketubot above. So too Rashbam wrote in Bava Batra 133a, s.v. "gift," etc. It appears in Mordechai that even in a deathbed gift to his sons, she is not maintained, because he rejects Rava’s statement. However, in the rulings of Mahari, siman 86, he wrote that one may say this is difficult only with a deathbed gift to others. His words are not compelling. It further appears that the same applies to land, for they rejected the words of Rava and proved it from the Talmud, and in their days it was specifically land. But the Rivash wrote in the name of Maharit, who wrote in the name of Ri, that movables given as a deathbed gift are not liened to the creditor, because the Geonim enacted this only regarding heirs. However, the Rivash wrote there that he did not find any of the authors who disagreed in this.
