(א) Lest she waived her ketubah, etc. This is Rambam's view in chapter 12 of Hilchot Ishut. He holds that specifically where her husband went overseas, we allot her sustenance even if her ketubah document is not in her hand, since she has sustenance by Torah law. But if her husband died, she has no sustenance until she brings the ketubah, because she eats by rabbinic enactment and also is sustained from heirs' property, and we plead on behalf of an heir. The language "until she brings the ketubah" implies that there is no remedy by an oath that she did not waive it so long as she does not bring the ketubah. Further, if there were a remedy by oath without a ketubah, why would we need the ketubah? Even when she brings the ketubah she must swear, as Rambam wrote in chapter 18 of Hilchot Ishut: my teachers ruled that she has no sustenance until she swears, for she comes to collect from orphans' property, and anyone who comes to collect from orphans' property collects only with an oath; and his view inclines to this. End quote. That is where the ketubah is in her hand, comparable to one who comes to collect from orphans, namely where the lender has the document in hand. If so, what is meant by "until she brings the ketubah," since in any case she needs an oath? One must say that truly, as long as the ketubah is not in her hand, no oath helps her at all. This raises a difficulty from Rambam's words in chapter 18: a widow who does not have her ketubah document in hand has no sustenance, lest she waived it or mortgaged it; even if the heir did not claim this, we claim it for him and say to her, "bring your ketubah or swear and take your sustenance," unless their practice is not to write a ketubah. This seems to show that an oath helps even without the ketubah. Kesef Mishneh wrote that he saw in an emended text: "bring your ketubah and swear," and this is primary. If so, Rambam's view is that wherever the ketubah is not in her hand, we do not allot sustenance at all, even by oath, for he compares this to collecting from orphans' property, where one does not collect even with an oath if the lender does not have the document.
