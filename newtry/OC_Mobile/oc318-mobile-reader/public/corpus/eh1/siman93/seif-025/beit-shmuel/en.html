Only through three trustworthy men. See Tosafot, folio 97: when she is doubtfully divorced, he is obligated in her sustenance, and she may sell without an expert beit din, like a widow. See siman 100. It is not necessary that one of them be learned and discerning, for otherwise this would be obvious; rather, here they are called experts relative to those generally called laymen, and laymen relative to completely ordinary people, but they must be expert in appraisal. These are the words of the Ran. It is explicit in all these laws that wherever an expert beit din is required, one of them must be learned and discerning. See Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, who wrote in the name of Rashi in Perek Elu Metziot that two suffice. This does not appear correct, for Rashi wrote that there only according to the initial assumption, that no beit din is needed at all and two suffice; but according to the conclusion, a beit din of three is required. The Tur wrote: if a widow sold and erred even by the smallest amount, her sale is void. The Mechaber omitted this in Shulchan Aruch because he objected in Beit Yosef that this was said in the Talmud only regarding a ketubah, whereas for sustenance, the dinar by which she erred also belongs to her, so she erred in her own property. But in Bedek HaBayit, Maharshal, and Darkei Moshe they wrote like the Tur. Perhaps it is considered an error in the orphans' property, since she is not given more than thirty days' sustenance at a time.
