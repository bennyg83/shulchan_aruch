And some say specifically, etc. From what is written Kesubos 73a "lest for two reasons," etc.; and Rashi there s.v. ella, etc.; but regarding, etc.; but otherwise she has a ketubah — difficult: a betrothed woman has no ketubah; and there the mishnah teaches "he brought her in," etc.; and Ran explained it discusses such a case where he brought her into his house — see siman 55 seif 6.
Or he betrothed, etc. See Tosafot Kiddushin 10a, s.v. "kol," and Rav Natan Gaon, etc.; and so the Rif wrote, as in section 55, paragraph 1. See the Ran there. This is specifically for the sake of marriage, but not for the sake of licentiousness. Thereby what is written in Yevamot 58a is resolved: "such as where he had intercourse with her, etc." This is also proven from the sugya itself, since it asks whether intercourse creates betrothal, etc.; therefore intercourse after betrothal plainly creates marriage. Although we say that one who has intercourse with his betrothed in his father-in-law's house is flogged, and since it is prohibited intercourse it should not acquire, as stated in the third chapter of Yevamot, one may say that he is flogged and does not acquire when it is for licentiousness, but if it is for the sake of marriage, he acquires and is not flogged, as the Ran explains at length. Nevertheless, this is not clear, for they stated generally that one who has intercourse with his betrothed, etc., and we say that there is a presumption that a person does not make his intercourse licentious. The main point is that two things are required: that he bring her into his house, and intercourse after betrothal. Since she is already betrothed, she becomes married only by his bringing her into his house. Intercourse for betrothal is a question in several places. It is also true that it must be for the sake of marriage. The Rambam and Shulchan Aruch therefore wrote in section 55, paragraph 1, "for the sake of marriage." In truth, we do not find intercourse creating marriage except through chuppah, meaning bringing her into his house and secluding himself with her. Chuppah alone also acquires, because it is the beginning of intercourse; the question was about intercourse alone. This is why it says that he had intercourse with her in her father's house: without bringing her to his house it makes only betrothal.
