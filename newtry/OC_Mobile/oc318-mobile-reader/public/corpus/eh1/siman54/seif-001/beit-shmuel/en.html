(א) To buy land with them. Chelkat Mechokek wrote in the name of the Ran that if the father left land for the daughter and she says, "Whatever my husband wishes, let him do," the husband has no authority to sell, for she herself has no authority to sell land she inherited from her father until she is twenty years old. At first glance this does not appear to be implied by his words. The Ran wrote all this regarding the language of R. Yose in the Mishnah, who said, "Is she nothing but a field, and she wishes to sell it?" On this he wrote that land left by her father she cannot sell; but if he left money and she bought land, she can sell it. If she says, "Whatever my husband wishes, let him do," this is only a gift, and she can give a gift even before age twenty, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 235. Therefore her gift stands, and then he can sell that land even if she is not twenty. All this follows the poskim who hold that she cannot sell before twenty. But Rabbenu Chananel and Rashba hold that she can sell even land left by her father.
And some say that we require that it was deposited with a third party initially for this purpose. See Choshen HaMishpat siman 252 for these laws. The Perishah wrote here that since this is for his daughter’s benefit, so that he not give it to her husband, we say that even if it was not deposited initially for this purpose, there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased. But this does not appear from the words of the Rosh, for he wrote according to Rabbeinu Tam’s answer that we say there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased only where it was already in the third party’s hand, like that mishnah, etc. This shows that here too, it must specifically have been deposited for that purpose.
