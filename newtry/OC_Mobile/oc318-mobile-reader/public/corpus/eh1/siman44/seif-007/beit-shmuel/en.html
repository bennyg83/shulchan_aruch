She is fully betrothed. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 15. All the more so, one who betroths a woman while he has another wife: although he violates Cherem deRabbeinu Gershom, she is nevertheless betrothed. This is from Darkei Moshe. A yevamah's rival wife is included among prohibitions involving negative and positive commandments; see siman 162. The rival wife of an ervah, however, is a brother's wife not in the context of a mitzvah and is among those liable for karet, and kiddushin does not take effect with them. A yevamah's rival wife is different. What is written, "except a yevamah," etc., is because Scripture says, "the wife of the deceased shall not be outside." We are uncertain whether this comes for a negative commandment, so that kiddushin takes effect with her, or whether "shall not be" means that there is no havayah with her and kiddushin does not take effect. This is from the Talmud.
