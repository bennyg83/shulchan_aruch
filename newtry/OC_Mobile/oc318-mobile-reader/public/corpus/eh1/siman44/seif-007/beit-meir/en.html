One who is half slave, etc. Chiddushei HaRashba wrote: one must examine this, for we say in Yevamot 45 that if one who is half slave had relations with a married woman, the child has no remedy. Necessarily, we must say his kiddushin is kiddushin, for if not, even he would not produce a mamzer according to the view that where a gentile or slave had relations with a Jewish woman, the child is fit. The Gemara explains the reason: we learn mamzer from the case of one's father's wife. Just as with one's father's wife, although he has no kiddushin with her, in general he does have kiddushin, excluding a gentile and slave, who have no kiddushin even in general. Therefore, if you say that for one who is half slave, etc., kiddushin does not take effect, then even when he has relations with an ordinary married woman the child is not a mamzer. There is another version there in the name of Rav Hai Gaon z"l: like one's father's wife, just as with a married woman whose offspring is attributed after him. Even according to this language, if you say his kiddushin is not kiddushin, his offspring is not attributed after him, as we say here, from where do we know he inherits? Rather, Rava here was merely rejecting, and the truth of the matter is that his kiddushin is kiddushin and his heirs inherit in the full sense. So Tosafot explained. End quote. In our Tosafot I did not find this. In Yevamot they wrote: and both derived it only from a married woman, etc. Some of my teachers wrote that according to that version it is not precise, for it is all the more so. I say that it can be reconciled, for the one who validates the child says as follows: one can only make the matter depend on whether kiddushin takes effect regarding one who has kiddushin generally; since he has kiddushin with others, and here he has none, it is evident that this is because of the severity of the forbidden relation, and it is for him like his father's wife. But regarding a gentile and slave, the fact that they have no kiddushin with a Jewish woman may not be because of the severity of the forbidden relation, but because they are not subject to kiddushin at all, even with one of their own kind. End quote. His words require explanation, for this is difficult: it is satisfactory regarding an unmarried woman, where one can say this; but where he has relations with a married woman, we see the severity of the prohibition in that even a Jew's kiddushin would not take effect. When a gentile or slave has relations with her, where did the severity of the forbidden relation go? At first glance one must say this is an external reasoning: the prohibition of a married woman was not stated with respect to them, so the severity of the forbidden relation is not found for them except in what we find, that kiddushin does not take effect at all, and this is true even with an unmarried woman and not because of the severity of the forbidden relation. If so, why was it difficult to him from one who is half slave? Granted that kiddushin does not take effect from his slave side, nevertheless from his free side at least the severity of the married-woman prohibition applies to him, and mamzerut properly applies. But here it implies that he first asked according to our version, and necessarily he retracted. In truth, what I wished to force, that the prohibition of a married woman does not apply to them, is not correct. It might be satisfactory for a gentile, though even this does not accord with the conclusion of the poskim who disagree with Rabbeinu Tam at the end of siman 178; but regarding a slave, why should it not apply by Torah law? He is obligated in all mitzvot in which a woman is obligated, and it is written, they shall be cut off, equating woman and man. So too it is explained in Niddah 47 in Tosafot, s.v. masar lehu. Therefore he holds that according to our version it is a scriptural decree: anyone who has no kiddushin in any manner, his children are not mamzerim, and he properly asks the question. See Sefer Atzei Arazim, who with difficulty resolves the question.
