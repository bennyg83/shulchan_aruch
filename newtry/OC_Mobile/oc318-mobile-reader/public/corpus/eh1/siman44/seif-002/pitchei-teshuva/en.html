And she is permitted. See Beis Shmuel, s.k. 2, who wrote that if a mentally competent man came and married her, or even a deaf-mute, she is forbidden to her husband, for just as she is forbidden to the paramour, so is she forbidden to the husband. In Be'er Heitev he wrote that since there is no Torah prohibition of adultery here, we do not penalize her. End quote. See Beis Meir, who wrote that perhaps the language here is abbreviated, for Be'er Heitev did not state this regarding the case in which a mentally competent man married her, which implies that she married knowingly, and if so, what does this have to do with a penalty? Rather, his words concern the wife of a deaf-mute whose husband traveled overseas, where they told her that her husband had died, she married in error, and afterward he returned. However, in my humble opinion, both of them overlooked the words of the Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Gerushin, halacha 5, who wrote: The same law applies to a woman concerning whom witnesses came and testified that her husband had died, and she married, and afterward he returned. Whether the second husband was mentally competent or a deaf-mute whose kiddushin is not complete, she must leave both of them, requires a get from each, and is permanently forbidden to both. This is derived from the Yerushalmi. End of his words. See there further regarding a deaf-mute's wife who committed adultery. He cites the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 11 of Hilchos Ishus, in the name of Rabbi Yechiel Bassan, who brought proofs that the wife of a deaf-mute who committed adultery remains permitted to her husband. His view apparently equates her with a yevamah who had relations with an outsider, as discussed below in siman 159, se'ifim 2 and 3, who remains permitted if she merely committed adultery, but is forbidden if she married another, whether unintentionally or intentionally. Beis Meir questioned this; see there.
