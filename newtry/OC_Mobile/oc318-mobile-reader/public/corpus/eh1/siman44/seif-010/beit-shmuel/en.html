A gentile. This means a Kuti who betrothed. Although we say in the first chapter of Chullin that they made them like full gentiles, they made them like full gentiles only stringently and not leniently, as written in Beit Yosef at the end of seif 4. Although at the end of the first chapter of Yevamot Shmuel says that a gentile or Kuti who betroths nowadays, we are not concerned that perhaps he is from the ten tribes, even in places where they are common, because they made them like complete gentiles even leniently, one must say that the statement in the first chapter of Chullin was said regarding other prohibitions, where perhaps we do not say this leniently, and if he betrothed we are concerned for kiddushin. But in Yevamot, Shmuel stated his law regarding kiddushin; this teaches that they made them like complete gentiles even leniently.
