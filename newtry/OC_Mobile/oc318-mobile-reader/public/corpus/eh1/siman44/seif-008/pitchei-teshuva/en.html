The questioner wanted to distinguish between kiddushin by money or document, which takes effect with women forbidden by a lav because the act of kiddushin alone involves no transgression, and kiddushin by intercourse, which should not take effect because the rule follows Rava that wherever the Torah says "do not do," if one did it the act is ineffective. Noda BiYehudah rejects this as mistaken. If so, the Talmud in Temurah could have found a simple practical difference between Abaye and Rava in one who betroths a woman forbidden by a lav through intercourse. Likewise, one who betroths by intercourse on Yom Kippur, or according to the view that intercourse with a virgin on Shabbat is forbidden because of wounding, would not effect kiddushin. This is untenable; there is no distinction between kiddushin by money, document, or intercourse. The rule that a prohibited act is ineffective does not apply here. Shechitah performed on Shabbat or Yom Kippur is valid, because that rule applies only to an act that can never be done permissibly. The substance of kiddushin with a woman forbidden by a lav could have been done permissibly by money or document; he merely performed it through a forbidden method, so the kiddushin stands. Further arguments support this: Noda BiYehudah elsewhere writes that the Torah's verses concerning kiddushin show that kiddushin can take effect even when done contrary to a prohibition, as with a high priest betrothing a widow. R. Akiva Eiger also writes that the rule of ineffectiveness applies only where voiding the act would undo the transgression, whereas here the transgression of forbidden intercourse remains even if the kiddushin is void; this point may depend on the dispute between Rambam and Ra'avad about lashes for intercourse with women forbidden by lav without kiddushin. The editor-son adds a proof from Ketubbot 74: although kiddushin by intercourse cannot be done through an agent, conditions nevertheless apply to it because the modes of kiddushin are compared to one another. Therefore, since kiddushin with women forbidden by a lav takes effect through money and document, it also takes effect through intercourse. Sha'ar HaMelekh writes likewise and answers Ran's statement that forbidden intercourse should not acquire: Ran said that only regarding rights such as inheritance and found property, lest the sinner profit, not regarding the validity of kiddushin itself. See also Tzla'ot HaBayit siman 4.
