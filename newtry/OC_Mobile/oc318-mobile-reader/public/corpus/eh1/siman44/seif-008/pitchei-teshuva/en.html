The questioner wished to distinguish and say that only kiddushin through money or a document takes effect with women forbidden by a negative commandment, since the act of kiddushin alone contains no transgression. If, however, he betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse, the kiddushin should not take effect, according to our ruling like Rava that wherever the Torah says, 'Do not do this,' if one did it, his act is ineffective. Noda BiYehudah replied that this is mistaken. If it were so, why did the Gemara at the end of the passage in Temurah labor to find a practical difference between Abaye and Rava? It could have identified the case of one who betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse. Moreover, according to your position, one who betroths a woman through intercourse on Yom Kippur would not effect kiddushin. Likewise, according to the opinion that intercourse with a virgin on Shabbos is forbidden because it causes a wound, one who betroths a virgin through intercourse on Shabbos would not effect kiddushin. All of this is astonishing. There is no distinction whatsoever between kiddushin through money, a document, or intercourse. The principle that if one transgressed, his act is ineffective does not apply here, for one who slaughters on Shabbos or Yom Kippur has performed a valid shechitah, and we do not say that his act is ineffective. The reason is as the Shach wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 208, that this principle applies only to an act that cannot be performed permissibly in any manner. Accordingly, even if he betrothed a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse, the essential kiddushin could have been performed permissibly through money or a document. He merely performed it in a prohibited manner, and therefore the kiddushin remains valid. Apart from this, one may further say, based on what Noda BiYehudah himself wrote in siman 129 concerning this principle, that with respect to kiddushin, Scripture revealed that the act is effective even when performed contrary to the Torah's warning, as where a Kohen Gadol betrothed a widow. The same applies to all prohibitions. See there. This will be cited below, siman 134, s.k. 6. One may further say based on Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 174, cited in Pitchei Teshuvah to Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, s.k. 1, that we say an act is ineffective only where nullifying the act would nullify the transgression. See there. Here, even were we to say that the act is ineffective and the kiddushin is void, the transgression of intercourse with a woman forbidden by a negative commandment would remain. This matter depends upon the dispute between the Rambam and the Ra'avad in chapter 15 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, whether lashes are incurred for intercourse with women forbidden by negative commandments without kiddushin. See there carefully. Responsa Maharit should also be examined, for it appears that he too raised the possibility that one who betroths a woman forbidden by a negative commandment through intercourse does not effect kiddushin, but rejected it because the prohibition would not thereby be remedied. I do not presently have the work before me. In the gloss there, the son of the author brought another proof from Kesubos, folio 74. Although kiddushin through intercourse cannot be performed through an agent, it may nevertheless be made conditional because the different modes by which marriage comes into being are compared to one another. Accordingly, regarding kiddushin with women forbidden by negative commandments, since kiddushin takes effect through money or a document, it likewise takes effect through intercourse, because the forms of kiddushin are compared to one another and may not be distinguished in any respect. See there. See also Shaar HaMelech, chapter 6 of Hilchos Ishus, in Kuntres Chuppas Chasanim, se'if 9, who likewise wrote as obvious that our ruling that kiddushin takes effect with women forbidden by negative commandments applies even where he betrothed her through intercourse, notwithstanding that it was prohibited intercourse. He brought several proofs for this. He wrote there that this is not contradicted by the Ran in the first chapter of Kiddushin, cited in Beis Shmuel, siman 55, s.k. 3, and siman 61, s.k. 2, who wrote that prohibited intercourse should not acquire. The Ran stated this only regarding the husband's rights to inherit from her, to her found objects, and to the work of her hands, so that a sinner should not profit. See there. See further Tzelos HaBayis, siman 4, regarding this matter.
