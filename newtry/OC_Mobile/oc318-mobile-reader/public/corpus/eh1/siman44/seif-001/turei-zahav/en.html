But the Sages instituted marriage for them. Meaning even marriage of a deaf-mute man to a deaf-mute woman, as Rashi explains.
She is permitted to her deaf-mute husband, and we are not concerned that people will say: the deaf-mute divorced her, this man married her, and afterward the deaf-mute took back his divorcee. Since a deaf-mute has no kiddushin or divorce by Torah law, there is no decree lest people err; for even if the truth were so, there would be no Torah prohibition, and we do not add decrees. So wrote Chelkat Mechokek. It appears that all the more so, if the deaf-mute divorces her, she is permitted to the competent man who betrothed her. The Tur wrote that nevertheless the deaf-mute woman cannot perform mi'un, for if she could perform mi'un no one would marry her, since there would be no time limit for mi'un, unlike a minor girl, where there is a limit: once she matures he will appease her until she grows up.
