They instituted marriage for them. See Beis Shmuel below, siman 121, s.k. 9, who refers to Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 77, regarding how to proceed when a deaf-mute effects kiddushin. I examined it and saw that he wrote as follows: I saw my teachers follow the practice that one who is accustomed to the deaf-mute goes with him to the chuppah, such as one of his brothers or another relative who grew up with him in the house and understands his signs. The person accustomed to him indicates the matter of kiddushin to him through signs, and the deaf-mute extends the ring to the woman and betroths her. End quote. See there. Regarding the kesubah and how it should be written, see below, siman 67, se'if 10, and what I wrote there in s.k. 2. Regarding the blessings beneath the chuppah, see what I wrote above, siman 34, s.k. 1.
