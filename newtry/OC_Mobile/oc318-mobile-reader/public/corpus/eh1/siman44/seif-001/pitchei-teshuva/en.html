They instituted marriage for them. See Beit Shmuel below, siman 121 s.k. 9, who refers to Responsa Tzemach Tzedek siman 77 on how to proceed when a deaf-mute man effects betrothal. Pitchei Teshuvah reports that Tzemach Tzedek writes that his teachers would have someone accustomed to the deaf-mute accompany him to the chuppah, such as a brother or other relative raised with him and familiar with his signs. That person would indicate the matter of betrothal to him by signs, and the deaf-mute would extend the ring to the woman and betroth her. For the ketubbah and how to write it, see below siman 67:10 and Pitchei Teshuvah there s.k. 2. For the blessings under the chuppah, see above siman 34 s.k. 1.
