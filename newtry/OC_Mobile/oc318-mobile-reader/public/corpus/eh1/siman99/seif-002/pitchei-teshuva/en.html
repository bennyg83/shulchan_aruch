Whether weekday garments. Nevertheless, as good counsel for ethical conduct, the garments of her widowhood that the heirs made for her in honor of the deceased should not be appraised against her. Beit Meir in the name of Rivash, siman 301. See further in Beit Meir above, siman 94, who wondered about the practice he saw in several batei din: when they settle with the widow, they permit giving her, beyond what is owed to her, various movables such as copper, polished copper, and even silver utensils. But we hold that even weekday garments made for her are appraised. At the end of the book, in the omissions, he wrote that he sought the reason for this practice from distinguished rabbis, and they answered only that this is what they had seen practiced by earlier authorities. He wrote that the reason seems to be that it is explained in siman 93 that we rule like the people of Galil, that the heirs cannot settle with her against her will; yet now the custom has spread to settle with her against her will, as written there in s.k. 5. Certainly, from the outset the practice was instituted to settle with her against her will, and in exchange, to appease her somewhat, they give her from various household utensils according to her need, even silver utensils. This is proper practice, and perhaps according to the wealth involved one may sometimes even add silver candlesticks. However, all this applies when she is being settled against her will, but not when she herself demands her ketubah and does not wish to be sustained from the estate, for in my opinion the custom developed only from that situation; see his words there.
