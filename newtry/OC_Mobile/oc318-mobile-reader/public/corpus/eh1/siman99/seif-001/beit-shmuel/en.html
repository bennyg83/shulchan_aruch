All her garments are appraised. This means those that the husband made for her. The reason is that he transferred them to her only so that she should stand before him in them, but he did not transfer them to her so that she could take them and leave.
(ב) Whom her husband divorced without a claim. But if she became corrupt toward him, he did not transfer ownership to her with that in mind. So wrote the Tur in the name of Baal HaItur. It appears that there is no distinction in this law even nowadays, when the ban of Rabbenu Gershom applies and we do not divorce a woman against her will. Nevertheless, even though he was willing, if he would not have wished to divorce her, her wish to divorce him would not be relevant; the reason that he did not transfer to her so that she could take and leave no longer applies. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchot Ishut, wrote: if she leaves against his will, such as those whom we compel to leave, we certainly appraise. Chelkat Mechokek wrote that this disagrees with Baal HaItur. But perhaps Baal HaItur speaks even where he divorces her, and nevertheless, since she caused it, we appraise; while Maggid Mishneh speaks even where he caused it, such as where he is among those whom we compel to divorce, and she requests the get, we appraise.
(ג) Of festivals and Shabbat, we appraise. Rosh and Tur wrote that we do not appraise. Rivash, however, wrote in the name of Rambam that we appraise, and so ruled Ritva; so too wrote Baal HaMaor. Rashba brought a dispute whether we appraise. The Mechaber rules that we appraise, because one does not extract from heirs where there is a dispute. If she is in possession, she can say: I hold like those poskim who say that we do not appraise. So too in Chelkat Mechokek. In truth, it is not explicit in Rambam that we appraise; rather, Rivash compares this law to collection by a creditor, who collects Shabbat garments. However, the Tur implies that he holds one should distinguish between them, for in this siman he ruled that we do not appraise, while regarding a creditor he brought the dispute in Choshen Mishpat siman 97. In Maharik, shoresh 10, it is explicit that he holds like Rivash, and he wrote the law of a divorcee, a creditor, and hekdesh, whether one collects from garments.
