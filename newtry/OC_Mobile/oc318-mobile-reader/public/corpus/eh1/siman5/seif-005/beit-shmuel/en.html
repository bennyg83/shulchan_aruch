Within the atarah. This is Rambam's text and per his reason he holds must surround entire atarah; however all commentators read pierced below atarah corresponding below [above] atarah — pierced below upper part of gid where atarah extends, so on other side in lower part of gid it is above atarah; Tosafot wrote specifically pierced through-and-through; if pierced below atarah until hollow of gid — kosher, not like Mordekhai and Agudah; and Tur and Tosafot imply even above atarah pierced until hollow kosher — their proof from stylus and gutter discusses above atarah; if holds discusses beginning upper flesh before atarah and cut diagonally until cut in lower part above atarah — then like through-and-through; still question on pierced through-and-through vs gutter and stylus; Gemara teaches even one side below atarah disqualified when pierced through; however if pierced in lower part until hollow — one can say disqualified because then properly said it drips; so in Derishah; Beit Chadash disqualifies pierced in upper part until hollow; Tosafot also hold thus per his explanation; nevertheless do not be lenient in practice; general rule below atarah if pierced until hollow in upper part of gid — we don't hold like Mordekhai who disqualifies in such case; sugya of gutter and stylus not difficult for them as Tosafot questioned; one can say pierced is different as Beit Chadash; nevertheless sugya pierced and closed Shmuel requires explanation for their words; if pierced in lower part above atarah until hollow — disqualified because then drips; Gemara pierced below corresponding above excludes pierced in upper until hollow without piercing lower part; in atarah if pierced until hollow disqualified below/above atarah per Tosafot seems kosher if pierced until hollow; Beit Chadash disqualifies; all this with hole semen can exit; if closed kosher in all holes and above atarah per Rashi if closed need not test torn because semen doesn't collect; per Raavam pierced kosher to enter congregation but doesn't produce — Semag, Mordekhai, Rosh, Agudah brought his words; likewise if semen duct closed kosher per them; if married — uncertain whether compel removal.
