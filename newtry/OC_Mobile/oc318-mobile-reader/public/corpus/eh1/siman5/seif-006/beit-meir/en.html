And this is primary, etc. One who examines the early authorities will see that the main dispute among them concerns the words of Rabbi Yishmael son of Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah, who said, I heard, etc. Rabbeinu Tam and those who follow his approach explained this as referring even to injury by human action, while the Rambam and those with him explain it specifically as by the hand of Heaven. Even Rabbeinu Tam did not mean that the observable fact of a man lacking one testicle and begetting children is by itself the proof; go and examine his children, and the disputants agreed to this. See Yam Shel Shlomo. The language of our Talmud is: the Sages taught, what is a petzu'a daka? Anyone whose testicles were crushed, even one of them, even if they were pierced, even if they were deficient. Rabbi Yishmael said: I heard from the mouth of the sages in Yavneh that anyone who has only one testicle is like a seris chammah and is valid. He said: I heard that a petzu'a daka by the hand of Heaven is valid. The Yerushalmi says: Rabbi Yishmael taught, I heard, etc., anyone who has only one testicle is not capable of begetting and is a seris chammah. It appears from Tosafot and Nimukei Yosef that they did not have the reading is not capable of begetting. Rabbi Yose said: I heard that the halakhah follows Rabbi Yishmael, but I do not know from what I heard it. Rabbi Immi said: Rabbi Yose would not have transmitted it from an insignificant person. Shmuel said: if a man with one testicle comes, I will validate him. Rabbi Chanah said that he holds like Rabbi Yishmael. Rabbi Yudan bar Chanan said: provided it is the left one, or the right one. A case came before Rabbi Immi and he said to her: I do not know whether I am permitting him to you by cutting, but know that a crushed man does not beget. There is a teaching that by human action he is invalid and by the hand of Heaven he is valid, and there is a teaching that whether by human action or by the hand of Heaven he is invalid. The one who says valid derives it from mamzer: just as mamzer is by human action, so petzu'a daka is by human action. The one who says both are included derives it from mamzer the other way: just as mamzer is by the hand of Heaven, since his formation is by Heaven, so too petzu'a daka by the hand of Heaven. They wished to say that the one who says by the hand of Heaven is valid is Rabbi Yishmael, and the one who says invalid is the Rabbis. Rabbi Acha said: all agree it follows the Rabbis; if scabs arose on him, did he scratch them or assist them? It is by human action just as it is by Heaven. In the Yerushalmi it is explicit that Rabbi Yishmael was said in dispute, since it teaches Rabbi Yishmael says, and since Rabbi Yose says the halakhah follows him, implying that others disagree. Therefore they wished to set the dispute about by the hand of Heaven as the dispute between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yishmael; even according to the conclusion, it is still taught as a dispute. But our Talmud says Rabbi Yishmael said, not Rabbi Yishmael says, which sounds more as though he comes to explain rather than dispute. It also appears to me that Rabbi Yudan bar Chanan, who said provided, etc., must be speaking of one testicle, and one cannot reject this as those who dispute Rabbeinu Tam rejected his proof by saying that if Rabbi Yishmael's reason is because it is by the hand of Heaven, why mention one testicle? They answered that this was merely common. Here that cannot apply, for this opinion placed a condition, provided, etc. If his reason were only that it is by the hand of Heaven, what difference would there be between left and right, or between wounded, crushed, or cut off? By the hand of Heaven, all forms of petzu'a would be valid. Necessarily he speaks of injury by human action, and his reason is that in the form he described the man can beget. Therefore I say that although Tosafot conclude that it sounds there, according to the conclusion, that the case is by human action, they did not decide this absolutely and did not base Rabbeinu Tam's proof from the Yerushalmi on this alone, for these words require study, as below. The main proof is as Nimukei Yosef wrote in the name of Ritva: the proof brought from the Yerushalmi that it validates one who lacks the left testicle even by human action comes from this opinion, which certainly speaks of human action. It further appears to me that Rabbi Immi, before whom the case came and who said, I do not know, etc., but know that he does not beget, from whom Tosafot apparently derived that according to the Yerushalmi he does not beget, must disagree with Rabbi Yudan and establish the dispute between Rabbi Yishmael and the Rabbis as concerning the hand of Heaven, and that case too was by the hand of Heaven. For if it were by human action and he does not beget, aside from the Rosh's difficulty of why this should be worse, one must also wonder: what is Rabbi Yishmael's reason for disagreeing with the first tanna, when the plain implication of the verse supports the first tanna, that crushed or cut in one testicle is enough? If Rabbi Yishmael had a reason to derive from a Scriptural decree that it applies only to two, or to distinguish between cut off and wounded in one, would the Gemara have been silent and not explained it, especially since the halakhah follows him? Rather, it is as stated. Rabbi Yudan, who said provided, must hold that such a man does beget, and Rabbi Yishmael's reason is that we see the Torah's concern is with one who does not beget, since it did not count generations for him; one who does beget is permitted. Therefore the response of the Rosh, Ritva, and Nimukei Yosef to the proofs for Rabbeinu Tam from the Yerushalmi, that the Yerushalmi speaks of by the hand of Heaven, must mean this: since according to the Yerushalmi's conclusion the reason seems to be only that it is by the hand of Heaven, our Talmud too, where it is not stated in dispute form, may be explained as by the hand of Heaven, as the expression he has no testicle indicates, as Rashba wrote, and not like Rabbi Yudan of the Yerushalmi. It is slightly difficult on this explanation why Rabbi Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel, a petzu'a daka is valid, as though it were his own view, if these are Rabbi Yishmael's words. Rabbeinu Tam and Ri, however, more plausibly explain Rabbi Yishmael's words even in our Talmud as referring to human action, like Rabbi Yudan, because of the difficulty that if it is by the hand of Heaven, why mention one testicle, and also because they observed that such people beget. But because our Talmud sounds as though Rabbi Yishmael comes to explain, and because the observable fact of begetting seemed to them primary only where one testicle was cut off, and because the reading in Rabbi Yudan is provided it is whole, proving that he distinguishes between wounded and removed, Rabbeinu Tam was uncertain whether Rabbi Yishmael came to dispute and validate all disqualifications with one testicle, according to the reading provided it is the right one, holding that all such men can beget, or whether he came only to explain and distinguishes in the case of cutting off alone between wounded and removed. Therefore in practice Rabbeinu Tam z"l relied to permit only where one was cut off, but not where one was wounded or crushed. Even if, had he come to dispute, the halakhah would follow him in those cases too, nevertheless, since our Talmud implies that he did not come to dispute, Rabbeinu Tam relied for practical ruling only where one was cut off, as the Tur and Rama write in his name. Even there he was concerned for the stringency of both readings, provided it is whole or provided it is the right one. More than this, I saw in Tosefta Yevamot chapter 10, after the words of Rabbi Yishmael son of Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah: Rabbi Yose said, there was an incident with one whose testicle was wounded, etc.; Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri said, his wife whom he had relations with became disqualified from kehunah. I therefore wonder at what I saw in the responsum of the Gaon Noda BiYehudah, may his lamp shine, in the cited siman, where at the end he concludes that we may combine the view of Rabbeinu Tam who validates everything when one testicle remains. I have not found such a view of Rabbeinu Tam. If he relied on what he developed in his analysis, that our Talmud validates all disqualifications in one testicle, there is certainly much to answer. One proof he wrote is from the Gemara's question about the man whose testicles were punctured by a thorn and who begot; he inferred that since it says by a thread and not by threads, this proves that only one was pierced. He asks: what is the question, since the baraita said only they were pierced, which could mean both? Rather, as he wrote, the first tanna makes no distinction between one and two. From this it follows, he says, that Rabbi Yishmael too validates all forms of disqualification in one, for otherwise how did the Gemara know to ask? Perhaps the first tanna said even one only by wounded, not by pierced. Necessarily the Gemara holds that there is no reason to distinguish in all disqualifications between one and two; according to the first tanna all invalidate even in one, and Rabbi Yishmael validates in one. I already wrote in this seif that there is no proof from this, and these words are also the reverse of Rabbeinu Tam's view. Still more, what he derived from this to answer why the Gemara asks, does piercing not prevent begetting, rather than does piercing not disqualify, is in my view not reasonable at all. The Gemara uses that wording because its question is from the observed fact that he begot. It is not plausible that Rabbi Yishmael would validate injury by human action even though he cannot beget merely because it is only in one testicle, for we have no reason for that, as stated. For the same reason I retract what first occurred to me, that Tosafot certainly disagree with the Rosh and explicitly hold that even if one was wounded and then removed he is valid, based on the reading before us in Tosafot in their citation of the Yerushalmi incident: he said to her, if you cut it off I will permit him to you, but know that he does not beget. I had thought they explain that he said this to the physician, speaking of a husband suffering in one testicle, that if you cut off that testicle you will permit him, meaning validate him for the congregation, but know that he nevertheless will not beget. In light of the above this certainly is not so; it is a textual error, and it should read as in the Yerushalmi. It also runs against the main proofs of Rabbeinu Tam from the fact that we see such a man begets. What he further wrote, that the matter is explicit in Rabbi Yishmael's words, since he said he is like a seris chammah, and after all a seris chammah does not beget, the Rosh already answered this; see there. What he wrote as another proof from the Gemara's question, and that the Gemara answered that Shmuel sent before Rav, etc., asking why he waited until the man begot and why he did not ask the law of the man himself, and why they left him with his wife, I do not know why it is objectionable to establish the case as one where he was married to a convert. It is even more likely that the incident occurred while he was unmarried, and it never occurred to them except that he was permitted to a convert, from whom he begot. What he further wrote as proof, raising a difficulty against the implication of Tosafot's explanation according to the reading whole, asking to what this condition applies, for if it refers to the one that remains, that is obvious, is not difficult. Aside from the fact that the Yerushalmi plainly shows that it comes to exclude one wounded in one testicle, as Rabbeinu Tam says, even if we say that Tosafot understood it to teach also about the remaining one, that it must be whole, there is no difficulty. One may say it comes to exclude a case where the remaining one was pierced, even not through to its cavity. This is reasonable: although a puncture that does not reach the cavity does not disqualify, when only one remains it must be whole and healthy from every side; otherwise he does not beget and is invalid. Against the Rosh's explanation he objected from our Talmud according to this reading, since it did not exclude this in the phrase this is the invalid one that returns to validity, and from this he proved that the reading right is primary. Penei Yehoshua already answered properly that one can say the ordinary form excludes it. In sum, I have not found in any way a proof from the Talmud or from Rabbeinu Tam to validate more than what is explained in the Tur and Rama in his name.
