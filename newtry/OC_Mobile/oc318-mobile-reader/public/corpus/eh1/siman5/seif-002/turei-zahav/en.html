Where the corona was cut off. To settle the words of the Gemara and the Tur, we must cite the source in Yevamot 75. The Gemara says that if even a hairbreadth remains of the corona, he is fit. Rashi explains that the corona is the raised ring surrounding the organ, and similarly explains the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter: the organ is counted only from the corona inward toward the body. The corona is the row of flesh encircling the place of circumcision and sloping down to its end. The Gemara then says that a hairbreadth must remain over most of its face and toward the head. Rashi explains: if a hairbreadth remains over most of the circumference, he is fit; but if it was cut toward the body and only a threadlike remnant of the corona remains around the whole organ on the underside, he is unfit. The Gemara further discusses a perforation below the corona whose opposite opening is above the corona. Rabbi Chiyya thought to permit it, but Rav Asi said that even any amount of the corona is indispensable. Rashi explains that the perforation begins below the corona at the end of the organ and exits diagonally above the corona toward the body; Rav Asi invalidates because even a minute puncture through the corona impairs its natural form. The Gemara also says that if it was cut like a quill, meaning on a slant, or like a gutter, meaning hollowed with sides on both edges and a channel in the middle, he is fit. This teaches that even though part of the thickness is missing and it may not warm in the ordinary way, or even though air reaches the hollow and cools it, he remains fit. The Gemara explains that "like a quill" and "like a gutter" apply even above the corona, for below the corona even if the organ itself is cut off he is fit. Rashi explains that if even a hairbreadth of the corona remains, he is fit although everything from the corona downward was cut. The Derishah cites scholars who understood the hairbreadth remnant as meaning that the raised flesh above the circumcision was cut, and the ring surrounding the organ was also cut diagonally from above to below, leaving only a thread over the majority. He objects that this would be identical with the case of a quill, and that the Gemara says any amount of the corona is indispensable. He therefore labors to explain "above" and "below" as referring only to the portion immediately near the head of the organ. In my view this presses the language unnecessarily. The correct explanation is that there are two aspects of the corona: the upper side visible to a person, and the lower side facing the ground, as the Yerushalmi cited by the Derishah speaks of an upper and lower corona. The upper corona extends with a clear endpoint: where the raised edge ends is called the corona, and beyond that is below the corona. On the lower side, however, the raised form narrows until near the head, where there is a channel between the two edges; each edge is part of the lower corona and continues until the very end at the opening. Thus on the underside there is no meaningful "below the corona," because the raised structure continues to the opening. It appears from Rashi that the term corona refers not to all the flesh but to the raised row itself. The Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah records a dispute among Spanish sages whether "corona" means the row itself or all the flesh; Rashi's wording indicates clearly that it means the raised row. The Mishnah and Gemara, when they say that if the corona was diminished and only a hairbreadth remained he is fit, speak only of this raised row visible to the eye, not of the inner flesh. Thus, if that raised row was cut and only a threadlike height remains toward the head over most of the circumference, it is enough, even though toward the ground it was cut entirely. If, however, the upper side toward the body was entirely removed and no hairbreadth remained there, while the lower side remained, he is unfit. The Mishnah speaks only of severing the raised corona itself. The later Gemara speaks of cutting the flesh within the corona like a quill or gutter, where the raised row itself still has a hairbreadth over most of it toward the body, and the possible invalidation would come only from the shape of the inner flesh; the Gemara therefore teaches that it is fit. This is called "above the corona" because the cut begins above, in the height of the flesh, and continues downward within the corona. If it were below the upper corona, where the raised ring ends, it would obviously be fit, for it is merely below the corona; even if there is a lower corona there, that does not matter, since the requirement of a hairbreadth applies to the upper corona. This also fits Rashi's language. The quill or gutter cut means that the flesh inside the corona was peeled away, but not all the way into the hollow of the organ; otherwise it would obviously be invalid, for even a perforation invalidates. The Tur's statement that at the head of the organ there is flesh, and where it joins the organ it is raised all around and is called the corona, refers to the raised surrounding row, as in Rashi, and this explains section 4.
