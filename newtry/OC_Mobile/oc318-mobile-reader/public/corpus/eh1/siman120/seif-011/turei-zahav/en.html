(א) The Sages enacted, etc. At the end of chapter HaMekabel the Gemara states: R. Yehudah enacted in a get: "write, either all of you or each one of you; sign, either all of you or any two of you." Rava said: sometimes he cuts off his words and says "all of you" but does not say "each one of you," and this can lead to invalidity, meaning perhaps one will not be present at the writing or not all will sign. Rather, Rava said: say "each one of you write," meaning that he should not say "all of you." It is difficult why they needed such an enactment. In the Tur, Rashi explained that the rabbinic enactment in a get is that when a person commands that a get be written in the presence of many, they should write explicitly that he said, "each one of you write," so that one not err and say that he commanded all of them. This implies that they write that he said so even if he spoke plainly. Other commentators explained that they enacted that his words be stated explicitly. From the language of this seif, which is Rambam's language, it implies like the other commentators. Nevertheless Rashi's explanation requires clarification: according to him, the writers explain, while the divorcing husband himself said only plain language. This is not plausible. If the husband's language indicates this explanation, why do the writing witnesses need an enactment? If there is another possible explanation, what does the enactment help, since perhaps the husband did not intend that explanation? It appears to me that the explanation is this: the husband did not say the language "all of you" at all; rather, he said before ten people, "write a get for my wife." Automatically only one needs to write for all and two sign for all. But the Sages saw that those two who write the matters the husband said to them should not write plainly that the husband commanded them, for in truth he did not command them individually but said to all of them generally "write." Although the law is that only two sign, he did not say so. Therefore they must write matters as they occurred, that he said before ten, "write," and on that basis we two of them write. This can lead others to error, for they will hear this and not know the distinction between saying "all of you" and not, and will think that just as here only two sign, so too where he says "all of you." Therefore, according to the Gemara's conclusion, the Sages enacted that those who write this testimony write that the divorcing husband said, "each one of you write and any two of you sign." This is not false, for in truth that is the law according to the husband's statement, since he did not say "all of you"; they write it explicitly only to prevent error.
