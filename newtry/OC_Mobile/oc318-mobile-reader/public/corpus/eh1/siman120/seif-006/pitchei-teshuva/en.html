What he wrote, that if he gives it to him on credit and has not yet paid him it is not theft: see Torat Gittin, who wrote that it appears that even if he took the parchment on credit but fixed a time for payment, she should not marry until that time passes, for perhaps he will not pay, and the seller will repeatedly demand his money, and the sale will be void retroactively, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 190:17, making the children mamzerim. With parchment, it does not help even if the woman wants to give the money herself, for the Sages did not transfer her parchment to him. See Get Mekushar, Seder Get Rishon, letter 17, who also brought this matter in the name of Beit Meir. From his language there it sounds somewhat that the same applies if the parchment belonged to the husband from the beginning, or through the scribe transferring it to him before the writing, but the scribe repeatedly demands payment for the writing of the get. In truth, in such a case the matter depends on whether an artisan acquires through the enhancement of the vessel, which will be explained nearby, and the view of Torat Gittin is that an artisan's acquisition does not apply here, as will be brought in the next seif katan. He disagreed with this ruling, saying it is not correct, for one who sells his field because of its poor quality acquires even where the seller repeatedly demands payment, as stated in Choshen Mishpat there, seif 11. All the more in this case, where the get has no value to the scribe and his main transfer to the husband is for the wage. It is not comparable to an ordinary object; see there, where he discusses several details at length. What Baer Heitev further wrote, that there is room to doubt if he stole parchment, etc., see Ginat Veradim, s.k. 6, and Sha'ar HaMelekh, Laws of Geirushin 1, who wrote about this.
