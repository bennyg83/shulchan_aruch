(ג) Even if he told them: tell the scribe. There are many explanations of this matter in the Tur and Ran, as Derishah and Perishah lengthened in the name of many commentators. Taz seeks to clarify the main point. The Tur here implies that the beit din themselves must write and sign, yet in siman 141 he writes explicitly that we do not say "words cannot be transmitted to an agent" where the sender explicitly tells the agent to tell another to act; for example, if he appoints someone to sign a get, that agent cannot appoint another, but if he explicitly tells him to tell another to sign, it can work. Here, however, the Tur writes that even for a get it does not help. One cannot distinguish that there the scribe and witnesses were named explicitly, because in siman 154 the Tur also writes that even if he told three people to tell named people to write and sign, they may not do so until they hear his voice. Taz therefore explains Ran carefully. Ramban thought that if the husband says "tell so-and-so to write and so-and-so to sign," this itself indicates agency. Ran rejects this because of the concern that the scribe or witnesses may sign out of embarrassment or misunderstanding. The defect is not merely rabbinic in every case; where agency was not properly created, it may be Torah-level. Beit Yosef's language is abbreviated and led to confusion. The correct distinction is between appointing the eventual scribe and witnesses directly as agents, even if through an instruction, and merely giving "words" to intermediaries without creating agency in the actual writers and signers. In gittin, because writing and signing must be done by the husband's command, the safer rule is that the scribe and witnesses hear from him directly unless a valid direct agency formula is clear.
