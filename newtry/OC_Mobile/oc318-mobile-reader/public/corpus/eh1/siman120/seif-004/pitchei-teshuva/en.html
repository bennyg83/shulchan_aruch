That the woman shall give. Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 4 writes that there is room to consider a case where the scribe transferred the parchment and ink to the husband in the usual way, and afterward the woman refuses to pay the scribe his fee. Can the scribe retract that transfer, or since he made no condition and transferred the parchment and ink without qualification, are they the husband's even though she paid nothing? In his view it is clear that the scribe may retract. The case resembles one who gives a gift because he heard that his son died overseas, where no explicit condition is needed because the gift is plainly given on that assumption. So too here, the transfer was plainly on the assumption of payment. This resolves the passage in the second chapter of Gittin in which a scribe said he could invalidate all bills of divorce. From this it is evident that Yeshuot Yaakov follows the view, cited nearby, that with respect to writing a get all agree that a craftsman does not acquire ownership through the improvement he adds to the object.
