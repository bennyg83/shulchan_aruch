(א) All of you sign. Beit Yosef infers from the Tur that if he said "write," his intent is that they write, and his intent was not for the signing. What the Mishnah teaches, "all of you write; one writes and all sign," is truncated language, and this is forced. I also wonder, for it is stated there: they sent from the house of Rav to Shmuel, etc., and Shmuel was uncertain whether one who says "write" means writing the get or signing with their hands. This implies that if he says "all of you write," one may say that he refers to the signing, and this requires study.

(ד) And the two who sign first, etc. According to the approaches of the Rosh, Rashba in his novellae, and the Ran, if the first signatories on a document or Get are invalid, it is valid unless it is known that they signed first; then in a document it is invalid, and in a Get, even though they signed with the husband's consent and he presumably intended the valid signatories to serve as witnesses, it is nevertheless invalid, because we decree Gittin because of documents: one who sees it will think the invalid signatories signed for testimony. The words of Beit Yosef on this matter are astonishing. All this speaks where the invalid signatories signed on the upper lines; but if they signed below, then the document and the Get are invalid by law. The Ran holds that if they signed in the middle it is also invalid. According to Tosafot, if they signed on the upper lines, even if we do not know whether they signed first, the Get is invalid, and we do not say that they signed merely to fill the space, since we hold that witnesses sign a Get only in one another's presence. If they signed in the middle or at the end it is also invalid. So Tosafot wrote in Gittin chapter 2 after Rabbenu Tam retracted and invalidated. If they signed at the end, the Get and the document are invalid; we do not say that a Get differs from a document because in a Get the primary witnesses are those who sign on the upper lines, since they do not sign separately from one another, whereas in documents the primary signatories are below, and therefore documents are invalid when invalid witnesses sign below but a Get is valid. So I wrote in Shach, and by this the sugya there would be answered regarding what it means when it says the difference between them is where one of them was found invalid. But Tosafot clearly does not say this, and Beit Yosef also wrote that there is no distinction between documents and Gittin. In all this there is no distinction between whether he said all of you and whether he did not say all of you. According to Rambam, in documents it is valid unless it is known that they all sat to sign, and the Mechaber ruled so in Choshen Mishpat siman 45. Here regarding a Get he wrote that the two who sign first are the witnesses, and if one of the first two is found invalid or related, it is invalid, because we decree on account of documents; but if not for that decree it would be valid because the witnesses to delivery are primary. So wrote Rambam; see Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh. Rama ruled in Choshen Mishpat that if the invalid signatories signed below it is invalid, but here he added no gloss, because he relied on what he wrote nearby, that one should be stringent in accordance with the poskim who hold that all of them sign as witnesses.
(ה) Provided that each one signs in the presence of all. Although the others sign only because of the condition, nevertheless they must sign in the presence of all, because we are concerned that perhaps they will not sign; "all of you" does not sound to people as requiring that all come to the signing. Therefore ab initio they must sign on one day to fulfill the condition. After the fact it is valid even if they signed not in one another's presence. See Ran, chapter 1 of Gittin, in the sugya "the halachah is that get witnesses sign only in one another's presence." From Tosafot it implies that if they did not sign in one another's presence, it is invalid, for they wrote on folio 18: "and the others from now until ten days, and in the presence of all"; and there it speaks after the fact. According to Raavad and Ramban, those who sign because of the condition need not sign before those who come as witnesses.
