And he wrote in Divrei Shaul, siman 244: a slaughterer who slaughtered a bird in a coop, and after checking the simanim properly while the bird still convulsed, he removed the crop with some trachea through its mouth — certainly the siman is shemuta or uprooted; if the slaughterer is an expert established in kashrut, he testifies with clear reasoning that after completing shechitah he checked the simanim — both for the law of slaughtering the majority and for the law of ikkur — by pressing the thumb on the hat brim as practiced here; also Maharik admits we attribute ikkur to what happened after convulsion; and especially when we see it convulses much — its strength is strong to expel its food from its mouth and protrude the siman from outside bottom to top, not the way most slaughtered fall by themselves below at the place of the shechitah cut — see there; and see there siman 246 on Taz's words here; and see in Lechem HaKodashim he wrote this briefly.
