(Siman 25, seif 1, in the gloss) However, one need not check after shemuta. In Peri Megadim he wondered: according to Rama's ruling (siman 24) that shemuta is not treifah, only shechitah does not permit it — if so, this statement of Shmuel "any butcher who does not know laws of shechitah," etc. — that ikkur means shemuta. For if according to Rashi, that is crop severance, it is difficult why shemuta is not also counted, and it would be proven shemuta is treifah from the outset and need not be considered; as Tosafot wrote there. And if we hold shemuta is not treifah — necessarily that ikkur of Shmuel is shemuta; and if so, if we say one need not check — why is it forbidden to eat from his shechitah? For even if we know the slaughterer did not check after ikkur — kosher. And to resolve: one may well say he holds like Rashi, that Shmuel's ikkur is crop severance; and why shemuta is not counted — because from the aspect of shemuta itself it is permitted to eat from it, for we are not concerned at all it was shemuta and examination is not needed at all. And that we hold shemuta is not treifah — not because he holds Shmuel's ikkur is shemuta, but it is derived from the sugya that Rami bar Yechezkel taught: there is no ikkur for a bird from the Torah; and if it were treifah, there would be no distinction between bird and animal — as I wrote. And regarding ikkur — the law that Beit HaGadol holds: if he did not check after ikkur — treifah. It amazes me: if so, when finding slaughtered chickens that are kosher — majority are available, not all experts — nevertheless perhaps in truth the slaughterer checked after ikkur and found they were uprooted; and for us it is like lost without examination of ikkur. And perhaps in truth for Beit HaGadol it is kosher only where he examines and finds the simanim in their place and were not uprooted; and it requires study.
