If, etc. According to the discussion cited above, even a woman without a fixed veset requires no examination at all. The requirement is derived instead from the Gemara on 12b: 'Rabbi Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: The halachah is,' etc., and 'the one who teaches,' etc. According to Rashi's explanation, this does not contradict the preceding discussion and no examination is required. Rabbenu Chananel, however, explains it like the alternative explanation cited by Rashi and rules in accordance with the statement that the halachah follows Rabbi Chanina ben Antigonus. The Rif rules likewise, but explains that she uses examination cloths only for the first three occasions and that the two cloths are both used after relations, one by him and one by her. The Rosh explains that the requirement is permanent, and that both cloths are hers, one before relations and one afterward. The Rambam explains similarly, except that he also requires the husband to examine himself afterward, for her status is no less stringent than that of a woman with a fixed veset. The baraita's novelty here is only that she must examine before relations. See the Rosh, who objects to the Rif's explanation; see also the Ran. The Maggid Mishneh resolves the objection, while the Raavad gives another resolution. See Beit Yosef. The Ramban and Rashba follow the Rif's basic approach, except that during those first three occasions they also require her to examine before relations, as stated by the Maggid Mishneh. This is the first opinion presented here.
