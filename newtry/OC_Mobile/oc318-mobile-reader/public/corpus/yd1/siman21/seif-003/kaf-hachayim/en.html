C) There. And if he slaughtered a majority of one of them in a bird, etc. In male geese, in most of them there are like two kanin—that is, sometimes his kaneh in the middle extends like another kaneh, except it is not made in separate rings like the way of the kaneh, and returns and connects to the kaneh; and we wrote below siman 33 om 40 that they are valid—that is, both of them; see there. Nevertheless regarding shechitah: if he slaughtered the bar oza above in the kaneh before it splits and slaughtered the kaneh alone—it is simple it is valid; but if it happened he slaughtered below where it splits and slaughtered only one kaneh—the Pri Megadim end om 2 was uncertain in this and left it tzarikh iyun; but Rav Zevach Shmuel in the she'eilot section siman 10 ruled treifah. And so ruled Mekadesh Me'at siman 33 om 21. And it is implied from his words we require a majority of both; and if he slaughtered the entire first kaneh and half the second kaneh—it does not help; see there. And so in Komatz Kelal 18 om 2; and so Zivchei Tzedek om 3.
D) There. And once they find what was slaughtered is more than half—even by a hair's-breadth—it suffices. So Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba and Ran; and so the Bach. Peri To'ar om 2; Machberet om 1; and in his book Berkei Yosef in she'eilot om 1; Turei Zahav siman 89 om 34. However, other latter rabbanim were stringent and require a majority recognizable to the eyes and not by any measurement. Peri To'ar om 3; Lechem HaPanim om 1; and he wrote: and so all slaughterers practice declaring treifah even b'dieved nowadays—not like Taz and Shulchan Aruch who wrote even a hair's-breadth is a majority. And so the Levush om 20; Be'er Heitev om 1; She'erit Menachem om 1; Erech HaShulchan om 5; She'eirit Shlomo om 4; Komatz om 3; Minchat Yehudah om 3. However, where there is no custom, She'erit Menachem wrote there that b'dieved one may validate by measurement of any amount; and so Komatz there; Minchat Yehudah there. And it appears even for those who hold any majority suffices: if he did not check the simanim immediately without interruption after shechitah, but waited a moment or moments and found only enough for a hair's-breadth—certainly treifah; for any such thing can come easily through convulsion, which is common—and we depend on what is common. Kereti om 4; Chakham Adam kelal 6 om 6; Minchat Yehudah there.
E) And Komatz wrote there regarding veshet: certainly one may not rely on measurement, because it stretches—and at will it widens and at will it shortens—only in the kaneh; see there. However, in our surroundings the Sages of Israel are expert to measure on two kanin that they insert along the length of the veshet as is known, and there is no concern the two cuts stretch equally; on the contrary, one should establish more than the kaneh. And measurement of the kaneh—to press both its sides, etc., one can recognize well; but there must be a majority of its hollow and its circumference. And beware in measuring the veshet lest anything remain, even a little of the membranes of the veshet that sometimes remain from going around—not as is circulated in the mouths of the Sages of Israel that this is from the membranes connecting kaneh to veshet, which do not exist; rather all is included in the category of simanim. Minchat Yehudah in Leket Yosher end om 10; Zivchei Tzedek om 5.
