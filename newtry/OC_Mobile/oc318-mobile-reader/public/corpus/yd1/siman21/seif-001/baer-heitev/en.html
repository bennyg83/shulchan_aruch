The slaughterer. And Derishah raised a difficulty: if so, according to this why do they permit l'chatchila to slaughter a bird with a knife that is not sharp, as above siman 18. And he answered: there it deals with slaughtering two simanim as one. And Shach wrote to permit even in animal if he slaughters two simanim as one; and Taz disagrees on this and permits only in bird and not in animal, because in a bird even if he precedes in one more, nevertheless b'dieved when a majority of one is cut there is no prohibition in the matter.
In a bird. And Shach explained: the kaneh is the membrane inside the gargeret that binds the rings together; but the rings themselves are called gargeret; and a majority of one like it means specifically a majority of the kaneh from the beginning of the membrane downward, and not a majority of the gargeret—that is, the thickness of the rings. And see below siman 24 and siman 34.
In animal. And Maharshal wrote: if the entire kaneh was slaughtered and the veshet—the entire outer skin was slaughtered and the inner skin—a majority of it was not slaughtered—it appears to me to forbid. And so wrote the Bach.
It suffices. And Taz wrote: since many poskim hold measurement does not help but only majority visible to the eyes, one should be stringent where there is no great loss; and see Peri Chadash and Be'er Heitev who were stringent even b'dieved.
