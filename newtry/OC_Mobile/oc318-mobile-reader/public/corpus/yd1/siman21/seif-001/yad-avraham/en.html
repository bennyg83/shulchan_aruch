(Siman 21 seif 1) The proper shechitah that cuts both, whether in animal and beast or in bird, and to this the slaughterer should intend. And in Kerem Shlomo he wrote in the name of Rashi that it is only d'rabbanan that they required both in a bird too because of distancing, etc.; and from this one learns that from the Torah it is only one in a bird—and behold explicitly so in Chiddushei HaRashba chapter HaShochet: from the Torah one siman suffices in a bird, for we say the verse cast [the law] upon the bird between fish and animal; and in animal too, majority of two, as we say there regarding kodshim we do not flay except because there is a mitzvah to flay.
(There) And if he slaughtered a majority of one of them in a bird, etc.—his shechitah is valid, provided when they measure, etc., even a hair's-breadth suffices. So explained Rashba and Ran—that which we say in the Gemara chapter HaShochet (daf 29) we require majority visible to the eyes, meaning by measurement; and so the Tur. However from Rashi's words there it appears majority visible to the eyes means majority that is recognizable—meaning without measurement—and not as Beit Yosef wrote that from Rashi's words one can infer like Rashba and Ran; and this is what Taz wrote. And Rashba has this from Rashi's explanation in the first chapter of Chullin (daf 21a) s.v. shedomin leshnayim, explained that majority visible to the eyes is a majority similar to two, and plain majority is precise. And this I found in Peri Chadash; and see Tuv Ta'am; and in Rosh Yosef he settles Peri Chadash's words; and I found another complete proof from what Rashi wrote in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (daf 37b) explicitly that majority that is recognizable is majority visible to the eyes, and majority by measurement is any majority; and this is clear proof for Peri Chadash's explanation in Rashi's view. And so from Berachot (daf 48) and Orach Chaim siman 197; and see in mishnah Bekhorot section 4. And so Taz and the acharonim require a complete majority recognizable to the eyes without measurement; and see what is written siman 9; and this is not as Radbaz part 1 siman 12 wrote that majority visible to the eyes means by measurement.
