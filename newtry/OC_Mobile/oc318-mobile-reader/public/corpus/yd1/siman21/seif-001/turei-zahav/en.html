And in a bird. In Derishah he raised a difficulty: since in a bird too l'chatchila two simanim are required, why is l'chatchila permitted to slaughter a bird with a knife that is not sharp, as is written in siman 24 per R' Oshaya, and Tur wrote in siman 18 "one slaughters with it"—which implies even l'chatchila. And he answered: in siman 18 it deals with slaughtering two simanim as one, and it deals even with animal—not as Beit Yosef wrote that it deals with bird—end. I say: that which follows from his answer—even in animal one could slaughter with a knife that is not sharp when slaughtering both simanim as one—is not clear at all to be lenient in this, that one rely on slaughtering exactly equally in each and not precede in one slightly more than its fellow—for perhaps he will precede in one more and come to complete prohibition. And his difficulty appears to be answered: since in a bird it is kosher in any case b'dieved when a majority of one is cut—in that [case] one may well rely that he will slaughter both, since even if it will not be so there is no prohibition in the matter, as stated.
Even by a hair's-breadth. Such is the view of Rashba and Ran; and that which is stated in the Gemara that we require a majority visible to the eyes—not "majority that is recognizable" does it say, as they said in Berachot "majority that is recognizable"—here they said nothing about recognition, but because they said half-and-half is like majority, even though it is not like majority except we view it in appearance of the heart as if it were majority—therefore they said "majority visible to the eyes" specifically we require, and not majority visible to the heart; if so Rashi's explanation does not imply thus, for he explained in the Gemara (daf 28) "visible to the eyes" is complete majority that is recognizable—end. And if so, since recognition is mentioned here, it is like that [case] in Berachot "majority that is recognizable" that Rashba and Ran cited—for they hang the matter on what was not mentioned here recognition, and Rashi wrote explicitly recognition; if so it implies a great majority. And so is stated in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim siman 10 seif 8 "majority visible to the eyes," and there it says majority that is recognizable; and so in Mordechai in the first chapter of Chullin it appears thus, who wrote: some wish to check by means of a feather that they insert in the simanim by way of the mouth to see if a majority was slaughtered—and it is an error, for perhaps there is not a majority here, and we require majority visible to the eyes—end; it implies we require a great majority, for if only to exclude from half-and-half, what relevance does this have to checking with a feather—for even according to one who says half-and-half is like majority, a feather check does not suffice. And in Agudah it is explained more, who wrote we require majority visible to the eyes, and since it is precise one may not be lenient—end; it implies we require a great majority. And so in our shechitot of Mahariv it is written: and not majority by measurement. And it appears to bring proof for the view of Ran and Rashba—for we say in the Gemara (daf 28) this expression "majority visible to the eyes" we require for treifot, for there they challenge on one who says half-and-half is like majority from this: they found half a kaneh defective and he added to it any amount—it is valid; and if you say half is like majority, behold it should be treifah! Rava said: it is different regarding treifah, for we require majority visible to the eyes. And if so, if you say they mean majority that is recognizable, as the Mordechai and Agudah hold—perforce perforation of the kaneh that disqualifies in its majority is specifically in a great majority; and if so, why did they say half a kaneh defective is valid—they should have said even a defective majority is valid as long as it is not a great majority visible to the eyes; rather perforce any amount of majority, even a hair's-breadth, is called majority visible to the eyes; automatically the same applies regarding shechitah leniently; if so certainly Shulchan Aruch ruled well like Rashba and Ran and Tur; nevertheless since from Rashi's language in Mordechai and Agudah it appears to be stringent, one should be stringent where there is no great loss—especially since Mahariv, who is later, wrote we require that the majority be recognizable to all and not by measurement, as stated.
