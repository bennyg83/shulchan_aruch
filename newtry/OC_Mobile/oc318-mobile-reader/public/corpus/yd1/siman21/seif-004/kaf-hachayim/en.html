D) There. And once they find what was slaughtered is more than half—even by a hair's-breadth—it suffices. So Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba and Ran; and so the Bach. Peri To'ar om 2; Machberet om 1; and in his book Berkei Yosef in she'eilot om 1; Turei Zahav siman 89 om 34. However, other latter rabbanim were stringent and require a majority recognizable to the eyes and not by any measurement. Peri To'ar om 3; Lechem HaPanim om 1; and he wrote: and so all slaughterers practice declaring treifah even b'dieved nowadays—not like Taz and Shulchan Aruch who wrote even a hair's-breadth is a majority. And so the Levush om 20; Be'er Heitev om 1; She'erit Menachem om 1; Erech HaShulchan om 5; She'eirit Shlomo om 4; Komatz om 3; Minchat Yehudah om 3. However, where there is no custom, She'erit Menachem wrote there that b'dieved one may validate by measurement of any amount; and so Komatz there; Minchat Yehudah there. And it appears even for those who hold any majority suffices: if he did not check the simanim immediately without interruption after shechitah, but waited a moment or moments and found only enough for a hair's-breadth—certainly treifah; for any such thing can come easily through convulsion, which is common—and we depend on what is common. Kereti om 4; Chakham Adam kelal 6 om 6; Minchat Yehudah there.
