It shall be stored away. Even if he wrote it to sell to an Israelite — Tosafot, Shach. And Taz wrote: what the Tur added — “and they do not read from it” — it teaches us that even in a private home they do not read from it privately, even if there is doubt who wrote it. And it seems to me there is a further distinction between this and other disqualifications — namely, any disqualification that is visible to all, certainly they read from it privately and there is no concern it will be read publicly; but a disqualification that is not evident, such as not being tanned for its sake, one must forbid reading from it even privately, lest they come to read from it publicly, as we find regarding doubtful treifah in siman 57, that one should not delay it due to the stumbling block — end quote. (And in Nekudot HaKesef he challenged him on this, writing it is not analogous to doubtful treifah — see there.) And see Orach Chaim siman 39.
He shall belittle it. And it implies in the Talmud that one should not say to a non-Jew to give it to him much cheaper than its value, lest he become angry and cast it into a lost place; and Taz wrote: when the non-Jew demands much more than its value, he should not immediately dismiss him and pull his hand away from him, but rather must engage him with words of appeasement, perhaps he will sell it for its value or a little more; and if afterward he still holds his position, then they leave it in his hand and let him go — but not before that — end quote.
That they plundered. And Shach wrote: it seems to me likewise in places where the non-Jews do not know how to write, we say that they presumably plundered them.
