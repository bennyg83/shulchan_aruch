(There, Taz seif katan 5) I have room to question this, and so forth. He did not examine Mordechai in Perek HaNizakin. This is exactly the student's question to Raavyah, and Raavyah answered him in this manner. The matter is simple. Payment for writing a Sefer Torah is far greater than payment for writing a Chumash. If the parchment was not processed for the sake of a Sefer Torah, it can no longer be repaired so as to become a valid Sefer Torah. The scribe therefore receives only the wage for a Chumash and loses a great amount. When the Gemara says he loses his entire wage, it means the entire wage of a Sefer Torah, which is why he is believed. Regarding Divine Names, however, Raavyah holds that he loses only the wage for writing those Names, while the Sefer Torah remains a proper Sefer Torah once the Names are corrected. He is therefore not believed.
