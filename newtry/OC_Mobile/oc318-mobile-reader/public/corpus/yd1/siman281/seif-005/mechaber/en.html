A Sefer Torah regarding which the scribe said after it left his hand, "I did not write the divine Names for their sake"—he is not believed to invalidate it; but he is believed to forfeit all his wages. And why is he not believed to invalidate it? Perhaps he intended only to cause loss to the buyer or to the one who hired him; and it seems he does not forfeit by this statement except the wages of the divine Names. Therefore, if he said, "This Sefer Torah—its hides are not processed for their sake," since he is believed to forfeit his wages, he is believed to invalidate it, for everyone knows that if the hides are not processed for their sake he has no wages at all. {Rama: Nevertheless, as the wages of a chumash in general he is obligated to pay him, for in any case he did not fall short of a chumash (Mordechai, chapter HaNizakin).}
