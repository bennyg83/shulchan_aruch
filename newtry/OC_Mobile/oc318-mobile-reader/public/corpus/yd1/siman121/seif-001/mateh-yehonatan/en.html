(Siman 121, Shulchan Aruch, seif 6) Metal utensils, even though, etc. See the Taz, seif katan 14, at length. One can say that according to the approach of the Rashba and those who agree with him, kashering that particular portion is entirely ineffective, and this is likewise because it does not expel anything. Now, above at the beginning of siman 94 the Mechaber explained that we calculate only against the portion inserted into the pot. This implies that there is no distinction whether one used the entire utensil or only part of it, nevertheless it does not expel from the entire utensil. This is stated without any proof from the Gemara, and requires further study, for from where did he derive this? Granted, there is no difficulty according to the Rashba, for one can say that he holds that just as it absorbs, so too it expels. The reason hag'alah of only part is ineffective is that hag'alah applied to part of the utensil lacks the power to expel everything absorbed above that portion. Indeed, it is possible that if part of a dairy spoon is inserted into a pot, one must calculate against the entire spoon. This is unlike the Mechaber, whose position is difficult, since the Rama wrote there that the first opinion is primary. One must say that the Mechaber was compelled to maintain that the portion above does not expel at all. For the Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayis challenges the Rashba from that which is stated in Zevachim, daf 96: "It was necessary only according to the one who said, etc., if he cooked in part of the utensil," etc. Therefore, the Ra'ah holds that certainly, if one wishes to use only the particular portion that underwent hag'alah, one need not perform hag'alah on the part above it. Accordingly, the Gemara can properly say that this is a stringency of a chatas, for with a chatas, even if he wishes to use only the portion that underwent hag'alah, he must nevertheless perform scouring and rinsing on the entire utensil. This is unlike other prohibitions. However, according to the majority of the poskim, who hold that the reason the entire utensil requires kashering is that the portion above does not expel everything at once, but rather continues to expel each time the utensil is used, the passage in Zevachim cannot be explained in this manner. For even regarding other prohibitions, b'dieved as well, if he uses that portion that underwent hag'alah it is forbidden. One must therefore say that the Rashba explains the passage in Zevachim as follows. He holds in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam that iruy removes the amount of a surface layer. A Baraisa teaches regarding a chatas that if one poured boiling liquid into it, it requires actual scouring and rinsing, unlike other prohibitions, where we say that as it absorbed so it expels, and kashering in a kli rishon is unnecessary; iruy suffices. According to this, the Gemara answers properly, "It was necessary only," etc. It is not discussing a case where one cooked in part of the utensil without filling it to the brim. Rather, it discusses a utensil filled all the way to the brim, but the cooking causes absorption only in part of the utensil, namely through iruy, which cooks only the amount of a surface layer. The distinction between a chatas and other prohibitions is that regarding a chatas, even though the absorption was only to the depth of a surface layer, full hag'alah is nevertheless required, whereas for other prohibitions kashering by iruy alone suffices. By contrast, when only part of the utensil was used and we are concerned that the absorbed substance traveled upward as well, there is no distinction at all between a chatas and other prohibitions, because hag'alah of only part of the utensil is ineffective for either. You must explain the Gemara this way, for otherwise it is difficult why the Gemara did not answer that this is the distinction between a chatas and other prohibitions with respect to iruy. Rather, it must be that this is indeed the Gemara's meaning. Examine carefully.
