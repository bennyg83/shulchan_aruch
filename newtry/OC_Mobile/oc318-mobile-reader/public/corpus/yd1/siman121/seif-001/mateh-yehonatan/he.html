Aruch seif 6) Metal vessels, even though, etc. See Taz s.k. 14 at length. And behold one may say according to Rashba and his supporters that kashering in that part does not help at all, and that is also that it does not emit anything; and behold the Mechaber explained above siman 94 that we measure only what was inserted in the pot, which implies not that the whole was used, not that part was used — nevertheless it does not emit from all of it without any proof from the Talmud; and it requires investigation how we know to say this. And granted for Rashba it is difficult, for one may say he holds that where it absorbed, likewise it emits; and that hagalah in part of the vessel does not help is because hagalah in part of the vessel lacks power to emit everything above it; and in truth it is possible: if one inserts part of a spoon of milk into a pot, one must measure against the entire spoon, not so according to the Mechaber it is difficult, for behold Rama wrote there that the first opinion is essential, and it must be that the Mechaber was compelled that necessarily what is above does not emit at all; and behold Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit challenges Rashba from that which is stated in Zevachim 96: it is not needed except according to the one who said, etc., if he cooked in part of the vessel, etc.; and therefore Ra'ah holds that certainly if he wishes to use only that part that was kashered, he need not kasher above it; and now it is well that he says this is a stringency regarding chatat: chatat, even if he does not wish to use only that part that was kashered, nevertheless requires rinsing and washing in the entire vessel; not so according to most poskim, that requiring kashering in the entire vessel is because it does not emit everything from what is above it, but emits constantly at the time of use; if so, one should not explain this Zevachim passage at all as he wrote, for behold even in other prohibitions b'dieved, if he uses that part he kashered, it is also forbidden; and it must be that Rashba explains this Zevachim thus: he holds like Rabbenu Tam's view that pouring nullifies to the depth of a peel; and it is taught regarding chatat: if he poured boiling into it, it requires actual rinsing and washing; not so in other prohibitions we say as it absorbs so it emits, and kashering in a first vessel is not needed, pouring suffices; accordingly the Gemara answers well: it is not needed, etc., and it does not deal with cooking in part of the vessel and not filling over all its sides, but it deals where he filled the entire vessel over all its sides, only that the cooking affects the absorption only in part of the vessel — namely through pouring, which does not cook except to the depth of a peel; the difference between chatat and other prohibitions is that in chatat, even though it was not absorbed to the depth of a peel, nevertheless full hagalah is required; not so in other prohibitions, kashering is not needed except through pouring; not so when used in part of it, for we are concerned the absorption traveled also above it — there is no distinction at all between chatat and other prohibitions, for hagalah in part of a vessel does not help them; and necessarily you must explain thus, for otherwise it is difficult why the Gemara did not answer thus, that there is a distinction between chatat and other prohibitions regarding pouring; necessarily the Gemara's explanation is truly thus — investigate.
