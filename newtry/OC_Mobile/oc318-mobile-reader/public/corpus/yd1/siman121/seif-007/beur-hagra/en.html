Old knife, etc. Language of the Gemara in Chullin (8b): an old one that he whitened in fire; and so they said in Tosefta and Sifrei: knives, etc. — they whiten; only in chapter 21 (40b) they said in boiling water; and Rabbenu Tam's explanation distinguishes between heter balei'a as above; and another explanation distinguishes between large and small, for a large one's use is through fire, not so a small one; and this even for the first explanation, as written in the baraita (Avodah Zarah 75b): things, etc.; however Rabbenu Meir wrote: even in a small one libun is required, because sometimes he repairs the milk wick; and Ran rejected his reasoning, since its primary use is not thus, he need not, for we follow primary use; and proof from lids, even though sometimes one may use hot; and Rabbenu Tam brought proof to distinguish large and small from Yerushalmi; and Tosafot rejected his proof, for Yerushalmi distinguishes large and small because in a large one there are grooves habitually; and Beit Yosef wrote: accordingly one may also answer and distinguish between whether it has grooves or not. And behold the Mechaber does not hold Rabbenu Tam's first explanation, that he holds chametz is called issura, as written in Orach Chaim there; and also he does not hold Rabbenu Meir's view that he explained there seif 6 like the Rosh and Ran; rather one may say like Rabbenu Tam's second explanation or Beit Yosef's explanation; and he ruled here stringently according to both resolutions; and this is what he says: whether large, etc., and if there are, etc., or he wishes, etc.; it implies even if there are no grooves, except in a small one without grooves it should be kashered through hagalah according to this opinion, only he did not wish to enter distinctions and was concerned for Rabbenu Meir's view; and even according to Rabbenu Meir's explanation, only the tip of the knife is needed, as the Rosh wrote there, even though he holds like Rashba in the preceding seif — nevertheless wrote: however, since the Jew does not use the knife through fire, he need not kasher the entire vessel through libun; except from Rabbenu Meir's words it does not appear so, and he holds that even in such a case we say hot in part, etc., to forbid and not to kasher; and he was concerned to be stringent in every case; but regarding chametz he wrote hagalah, because it is not a Jew's way to use through fire, as the Rosh wrote as above; and this is what he says: bought from gentiles, and the like of Chullin, Tosefta and Sifrei.

He whitens. As above regarding grooves from Yerushalmi cited; to cut hot — from Tosefta and Sifrei; to slaughter — from that in Chullin.
Or he sharpens, etc. And some say, etc. Mishnah there: he planes, etc.; and in the Gemara: and he inserts it, etc.; and Rashi explained it requires both; and the Rosh challenged: if so, why did it not say in the Mishnah except kasher part of it; and furthermore King Shapur did only insertion; rather it is one or the other; and it implies both are specifically to cut cold; and so Rashba; and as written in the hagahah; but Rambam explained that planing is even to cut hot, and insertion, etc., does not refer to the Mishnah but is a separate matter; for it is similar to the others that teach: whose way, etc., the spit, etc., that deals with using them hot; and this is what he says: or he sharpens, etc. (Likut) Or he sharpens: the entire Mishnah deals regarding hot; and so in Yerushalmi for Rabbenu Tam's explanation in Tosafot there d.h. said, etc.; and so in Yerushalmi that said, etc.; and therefore the Mishnah deals regarding hot; and even though Tosafot rejected Rabbenu Tam's words as written, and one may reject that proof, etc. — namely according to their view that they explain planing in cold, and therefore they forced this; and so Rabbi Baruch there and Or Zarua (end of section).
On the whetstone, etc. Rambam and Rashba; and like Rashi's explanation in the Mishnah.
And if not, etc. As written in chapter 21 (40b): he buries them in clay; and Rashba's girsa there: and afterward he removes them and boils them in boiling water; and even though Rabbi Eliezer holds libun is required, this suffices; and the same applies here.
However, etc. This refers to what is written: and if he cannot, etc. — meaning immersion does not forbid b'dieved; and as written seif 6 in the hagahah; and specifically, etc.; all the more so he holds we do not say at all hot in part, etc.
Or if, etc. For by law the essential matter is that hagalah helps and libun is not needed, except those whose use is through fire, such as very large ones on which they roast; and specifically regularly, as above.
