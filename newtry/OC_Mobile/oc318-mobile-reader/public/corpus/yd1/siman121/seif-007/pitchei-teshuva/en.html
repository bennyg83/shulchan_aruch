Regarding chametz on Pesach. Baer Heitev in the name of Shach: and accordingly also in a pan of meat or milk libun is required, etc.; and see in responsum Maor HaGolah Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who explained the reason in the passage "and in that [case]," and wondered at Shach, for a meat pan certainly hagalah helps, as we conclude in the sugya of Avodah Zarah regarding notar, since heter was absorbed regarding nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am; and the reason Menachem Azariah gave another reason for chametz is because he holds like Rashba that chametz is called issura balei'a, for its name is upon it; but for meat and milk it is obvious that hagalah helps; and he left it requiring investigation — see there. [See in responsum Chasam Sofer siman 111, who wrote that one should examine well the language of Menachem Azariah's responsum that Shach brought and raised to halachah with clear proofs that the matter is so: every absorption that is through cooking, even if there is no heter intermediary — such as if he cooked yayin nesekh and forbidden milk, and even Torah prohibitions such as wine for a nazir and the like — nevertheless as it absorbs through cooking, so it emits through cooking with hot water hagalah; for all beverages are called cooking except milk and fat; and as it absorbs so it emits; but what was absorbed through frying fat and milk — then it depends whether it was through heter fat intermediary, such as chametz fried in a pan whose fat is heter — even though absorbed through frying, nevertheless it emits through water; for although there is no as it absorbs through cooking so it emits through cooking, nevertheless we may say as it absorbs through heter intermediary so it emits through heter intermediary; to exclude a gentile's pan that absorbs without any heter intermediary at all, since there are two adverse factors, libun is required specifically; and with this the aforementioned responsum is understood, even though Shach did not understand thus — end of his words. And see in the previous responsum siman 110, who raised that a milk pan that was not ben yomo and meat was fried in it, when he comes to kasher it afterward (as above siman 93), one should permit through hagalah without libun; and permit further: that although it is clear in Magen Avraham for Yom Tov laws (cited in Baer Heitev here) that the world practiced prohibition to kasher a meat vessel for milk, nevertheless if they kashered otherwise for Pesach need, I practice to permit exchanging vessels as each person wishes from meat to milk or the reverse, since the kashering was not for the intent of meat and milk but only for chametz intent — there is no concern — see there.]
