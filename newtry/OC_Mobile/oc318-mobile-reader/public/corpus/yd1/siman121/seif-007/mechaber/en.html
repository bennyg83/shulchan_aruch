An old knife, whether large or small, that was purchased from a non-Jew: if he comes to use it with cold, if it has no nicks, he stabs it 10 times into hard ground; and it is required that each stabbing be into hard ground. Therefore, he should not stab in the place where he stabbed another stabbing (Tur in the name of the Ramah). And even to cut with it a sharp item such as a radish, this suffices. (And to use it regularly, it is no better than other utensils for which the practice is to perform hag'alah even for cold — the reasoning of the Rav in the words of the Arukh — as was explained.) And if it has nicks, or he wants to cut with it hot, or to slaughter with it, he makes it white-hot, or he sharpens it well over its entire surface with a blacksmith’s grindstone. {Rama: And there are those who say that sharpening helps only to cut with it cold, but not regarding hot (Beit Yosef in the name of poskim), and so is the practice l'chatchila. And if he cannot make the knife white-hot well because of the handle, he should make it white-hot and then perform hag'alah on it afterwards (Arukh). However, if he made it white-hot and did not perform hag'alah, even if it has nicks; or if he performed hag'alah and did not make it white-hot, and it has no nicks, and he cut with it hot food — the food does not become forbidden, even if the knife is ben yomo. And if he sharpened it well with a grindstone in every place and afterwards performed hag'alah, it helps even l'chatchila like libbun (Mordechai, chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, and Arukh), if he is able to clean the nicks in it.}
