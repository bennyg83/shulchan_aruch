And specifically if he used all of it, etc. One should examine Rama's view on this, for this is from the words of the Tur who disagrees with Rashba, who wrote the language of Shulchan Aruch in this seif; and the Tur wrote regarding him: it seems to me specifically when he used all of it, etc.; and if so, why did Rama write in this language as if it is agreed upon the language of Shulchan Aruch that precedes it? And one should not say that Rama explains that the Tur comes to explain Rashba's words in what he wrote; but regarding its kashering, the kashering did not succeed until he kashered all of it — namely if he must kasher because he used all of it in prohibition; but if he used part of it, kashering part suffices. This is not explicit in Teshuvat HaRosh the long version 128:2, that even if he used only part of it in prohibition he must kasher all of it; and so explicitly in Mishmeret HaBayit there, which is Rashba's words, as we will cite shortly; except that the Tur disagrees with Rashba on this, and the essential dispute depends on the sugya that is stated in chapter Dam Chatat (Zevachim 96), that we say regarding terumah we do not require rinsing and washing if he cooked terumah in a pot, but specifically if he cooked chatat in it; and we challenge there regarding terumah as well that hagalah is required for what was absorbed; and we answer: it is not needed except according to the one who said that if he cooked chatat in part of the vessel, the entire vessel requires rinsing and washing; and from this terumah was reduced, that rinsing and washing are required only where he cooked. And on this there is dispute between Rashba in Teshuvat HaRosh the long version and in Mishmeret HaBayit, and Beit HaEphraim — the Beit HaEphraim holds that just as terumah was reduced, so too non-sacred forbidden foods were reduced; and accordingly he wrote that in a matter requiring hagalah, it suffices when he kashered the place where prohibition was absorbed alone, for as it absorbs so it emits; but where libun is required, whitening the place of absorption alone does not suffice, but the entire vessel, for hot in part is hot in whole; and the reason for the distinction is that the kashering of libun does not cause emission of what is absorbed but destroys the prohibition within it; and if he whitened only the place of absorption, it destroys only in the place of whitening alone, and what was forbidden in the rest of the vessel remains; not so with hagalah kashering, which causes emission of what was absorbed — therefore when he kashered part through moisture, it emits everything. And Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit wrote: from that [sugya] in Zevachim there is no proof regarding a vessel that absorbed full prohibition like nevelah, that it need not kasher all of it; for we reduce there only regarding absorption of heter such as meat in milk, and the reason: anything whose taste of meat was weakened through hagalah of that part of the vessel in which he cooked is no longer fit for milk prohibition afterward; and in terumah as well he gave taste in this, as he wrote there; but in other full prohibitions like nevelah and the like, I say: if he cooked in part of the vessel, the entire vessel requires hagalah, etc., for through cooking it spreads and is absorbed throughout; and it is possible that therefore they said regarding knives for Pesach: kasher them and boil them in boiling water, because knives are used with wet things that have gravy to absorb — chametz on Pesach is forbidden like other prohibitions; however even if one used the knife with chametz hot, Rav Ashi said: whiten them in fire and boil them in boiling water — even if you find to say that part of the knife within the sheath absorbs, for hot in part is hot in whole; nevertheless it is possible that that absorption within the sheath is emitted through the whitening of the knife, and hagalah that he kashered it afterward suffices — end of his intent. It is clear: even though it absorbed through fire and requires kashering also through fire, nevertheless iron within the sheath in boiling water alone suffices, since the iron was whitened, the part within the sheath was also scorched; and on this basis I also resolved what Beit Yosef brought from the words of the Rosh at the end of Avodah Zarah: in the name of Riba, that knives of gentiles require libun, since the gentile's way is to repair wicks of milk in the mouth of the lamp while it burns; and Rabbenu Tam said: if you say it happens thus, whatever the Jew does not use through fire, hagalah suffices for him; and we do not see this, for since its use is through fire, hagalah alone does not suffice without libun, and it constantly emits little by little with all hagalot, etc.; and it appears that it suffices if he whitens the tip of the knife, according to what gentiles are accustomed to use it through fire, even though hot in part is hot in whole and the prohibition spread throughout; nevertheless, since the Jew does not use the knife through fire, this suffices — end of his words. And it is difficult: since the Rosh already refuted Rabbenu Tam's words that permitted on this reason that the Jew does not use it through fire, and he rejected them — how did he himself write thus at the end? And I saw many forced explanations on this; and it seems to me that he follows what we recalled, that the Rosh deals there in any case with hagalah, but because of concern for use with hot milk one must require libun; and certainly hagalah alone does not suffice for the Rosh where it absorbed through fire, except for this matter it helps that we do not say that since part of the knife — namely its head with milk wicks by the gentile — is hot, the whole is hot and we must whiten all of it; for this alone he said it is not so, but since part was whitened, the absorption absorbed in the rest of the knife is scorched for the matter that hagalah afterward helps him — so it appears clearly to my humble opinion. And behold the Tur holds not like Beit HaEphraim, who distinguishes between libun kashering and hagalah kashering, and not like Rashba who is stringent in every manner to kasher the entire vessel; but he holds in all kasherings, whether through libun or hagalah, if the part was kashered, the whole was kashered; and it appears the Tur is not lenient here except b'dieved, but not l'chatchila, for otherwise it is difficult — he himself wrote in Laws of Pesach that one must kasher the handles of vessels as well; but certainly there it deals l'chatchila. And if so, one should wonder at Rama why he wrote the Tur's words here as if they are a way of explaining Shulchan Aruch's words, when in truth he disagrees with them, as we explained; also regarding halachah one should wonder why he disagreed with Beit Yosef, who wrote that one should rule like the Rosh and Rashba who are stringent that kashering the part does not suffice; and one should heed them especially in a Torah prohibition. Nevertheless it appears to my humble opinion: regarding a spit on which unsalted meat was roasted and one wishes to whiten it to roast kosher meat — whitening the part suffices, namely where the prohibition reached; since even if he roasted permitted meat on it without any whitening at all it is kosher b'dieved, as written in siman 76 from the reason that fire draws what it draws; except that if he roasted nevelah meat on it, it appears to my humble opinion forbidden b'dieved, as I wrote in siman 76 that nevelah juice drips and drips, and we do not say regarding it as it absorbs so it emits, as is found in many places; and certainly the spit is forbidden in that place; and if so, those travelers do not act well who take a gentile's spit and whiten only the upper part where they roast and no more; but b'dieved certainly one should not forbid if he whitened only the part, similar to the law of vessel handles that are kosher b'dieved if he did not kasher them, as Rama wrote in Laws of Pesach; and even l'chatchila it appears that if he whitens a large part of the spit even where the prohibition did not reach, even if he did not whiten the entire spit, it suffices, since the place that was not kashered is far from the place of roasting — so it seems to me.
