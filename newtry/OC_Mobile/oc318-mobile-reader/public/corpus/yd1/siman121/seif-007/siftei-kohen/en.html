And so is the practice l'chatchila. But b'dieved, if he used it boiling after scouring, even if it is ben yomo it is permitted as after hagalah, for scouring is preferable to hagalah according to the Mechaber; and so is proven in Beit Yosef explicitly; and even though Rashba and his supporters hold hagalah is preferable and it is permitted to use boiling after hagalah and not after scouring — nevertheless we who rely b'dieved on the words of those who permit, the same applies regarding scouring, for we rely on Rambam and Ran, who hold scouring is effective to use boiling; and so is Ra'ah's view in Sefer Bedek HaBayit p. 124 — see there, who elaborated in his reason. Pri Toar wrote: if he kashered two ben yomo vessels or more in water that did not have sixty against both but there was sixty against one — if he kashered them at once, certainly their hagalah did not succeed; and if he kashered them one after another, some say their hagalah succeeded, for the taste of prohibition was nullified in the water before he kashered the second; and some forbid even in this because prohibition returns and is reawakened and forbids the second — end of his words; and this is from Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought: but to kasher many ben yomo vessels one after another — the great ones of the generations disagreed; some from their words appear to permit, and that is Raavad's view; but my teachers did not agree with him — end of his words. And in Issur VeHeter general rule 58 law 23 he wrote: even if he kashered in it so many ben yomo vessels until finally there is not sixty in the water against all of them, only that they kasher one after another and each time there is sixty against that vessel in it — even though in emission of taste of prohibition we say in a wet substance found its kind and it is reawakened, that is specifically to eat the prohibition itself; but to kasher another vessel in it, Semag wrote that nullification helps; and necessarily in such a case it deals, for if not it is obvious; and in nosen ta'am lifgam there is no room to establish that it is not prohibition, for the Torah forbade only a ben yomo pot; and furthermore, most times when they kasher vessels for Pesach need, even in large cauldrons one does not find sixty against all of them, and nevertheless they permit for that purpose; and furthermore, since the water must boil at the time of hagalah and the vessel is busy emitting in a short time when they insert it in the cauldron, it does not return and absorb as is stated below — end of his words. And I do not know: since the water is forbidden, why should the vessels not be forbidden again? And the proof he brought from Semag I do not recognize — Semag teaches us that it is permitted l'chatchila to kasher and there is no concern of nullifying prohibition l'chatchila; and what they kasher on Pesach many vessels is also no proof, for they are not ben yomo, as Issur VeHeter himself wrote there several times; and what he wrote that it does not return and absorb is also not so — consider if prohibition fell less than sixty and afterward a spoon was inserted there — would it not be forbidden? And furthermore, behold that vessel itself is forbidden when there is not sixty in the water against it — evidently we do not say it does not return and absorb; and so is simple in many places; and it appears Issur VeHeter combines many sides together to permit; and in my humble opinion there is no permission here, for we establish (above siman 98) taste is like substance deoraita; and we establish above seif 99 that it returns and is reawakened; if so, since the water is forbidden by law, why should the vessels not be forbidden? I again examined Rashba's responsum and found in siman 262 that Raavad and his dispute did not write their words regarding kashering vessels but in general regarding the prohibition itself they disagree whether it returns and is reawakened; and from this Rashba learned that just as they disagree regarding the prohibition itself, likewise they disagree regarding kashering vessels — see there, clear thus; and according to this, according to what was explained above siman 99 s.k. 30, we do not establish like Raavad but always it returns and is reawakened — if so, likewise here the vessels are forbidden; and so Rashba in that responsum, not like Issur VeHeter; also Pri Toar did not aim well in what he brought here two opinions, for we establish above end of siman 99 not like Raavad; also it is clear from Rashba's responsum that vessels return and absorb, not like Issur VeHeter's view.
