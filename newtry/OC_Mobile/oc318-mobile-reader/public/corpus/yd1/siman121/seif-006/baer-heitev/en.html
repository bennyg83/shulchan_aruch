Shach explained: it suffices also through kashering part of it, and that is b'dieved; but l'chatchila one must kasher all of it, as the Rav wrote in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 10; and Taz disagrees with this hagahah and ruled that kashering part of it does not suffice. Nevertheless he wrote that it appears regarding a spit on which un-salted meat was roasted and one wishes to whiten it to roast kosher meat — whitening part suffices, namely where the prohibition reached, since even if he roasted permitted meat on it without any whitening at all it is kosher b'dieved, as written in siman 76 from the reason that fire draws what it draws; except that if he roasted nevelah meat on it, it appears forbidden b'dieved (for nevelah juice drips and drips). However for travelers it appears that if he whitens a large part of the spit even where the prohibition did not reach, even if he did not whiten the entire spit, it suffices even l'chatchila, since the place that was not kashered is far from the place of roasting — end of his words. And Shach wrote: in metal vessels where prohibition was used in part of it and afterward heter was used in the other part that was not used the first time — it is permitted b'dieved; however this is specifically when it was forbidden through heat by cooking; but through heat by actual fire, all agree that the prohibition spread throughout it; therefore a forbidden spit on which meat was roasted over fire requires sixty against the entire spit, since when it emits in the meat through the moisture of the meat it carries its emission throughout — end of his words.
