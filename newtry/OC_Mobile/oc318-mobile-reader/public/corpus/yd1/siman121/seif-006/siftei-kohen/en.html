But regarding its kashering, etc. It implies that even if prohibition was used only in part of it, its kashering did not succeed until he kashered all of it — according to Rashba, and so too in Beit Yosef; and accordingly the Rav should have written in language "and some say" specifically, etc., so it is understood that he disagrees; except that there is no necessity in the Mechaber's words in Shulchan Aruch itself; and it is possible it deals when prohibition was used in all of it he wrote anonymously, and specifically, etc.; and so is his way in many places. And know that Rashba himself concedes that where heter was absorbed, such as if milk was used in part of it and he wishes to kasher it for meat — kashering part suffices for him, as written in Mishmeret HaBayit p. 128 end of 71a explicitly — see there.
And specifically, etc. So wrote the Tur; and in Beit Yosef he wrote that from the words of the Rosh at the end of Avodah Zarah it appears he holds like Rashba, who wrote that it suffices if he whitens the tip of the knife, because gentiles are accustomed to use it through fire, even though hot in part is hot in whole and the prohibition spread throughout; nevertheless, since the Jew does not use the knife through fire, this suffices — end of his words; and it implies that to use it through fire it is forbidden because we say hot in part is hot in whole to forbid; but not regarding kashering — end of his words. And in Derishah and Beit Chadash they elaborated greatly to reconcile that the Rosh is not against the Tur's view, and they forced the Rosh's words greatly — see there; and in my humble opinion it appears simple that the Rosh's words are as their plain meaning and he does not disagree with what the Tur wrote; and the Tur's view is like Ra'ah, who wrote in Bedek HaBayit p. 125 71a: know that returning to lime kilns is not kashering like hagalah in metal vessels, for there it is to emit what was absorbed; but libun in any place does not emit any prohibition in it but destroys it, consuming the prohibition within it; and therefore we derive from this: if a metal spit, that we establish hot in part is hot in whole, and prohibition was used in part of it — behold it is all forbidden as if used in all of it; and when he comes to kasher it through libun, it does not suffice to whiten only to the extent of that part used in prohibition, because libun does not emit its absorption but destroys it; and when it destroys only to the extent of that part, it does not suffice, for when he comes to use it in heter it will emit prohibition through that part that was not kashered; but in a metal pot where prohibition was used in part of it, kashering through hagalah to the extent of that part suffices, for hagalah is kashering of emission, and as it absorbs so it emits; and even though the absorption wafts through moisture more than the measure of use, so now through hagalah via moisture it emits everything — end of his words; and this is the Tur's view; and the Rosh deals with libun, and therefore the whole was not kashered, but he holds that nevertheless to use it not through fire this suffices; and it is possible Ra'ah also concedes to this; and that the Tur did not write that in libun one must kasher all of it is because he relied on what he wrote the reason because of as it absorbs so it emits — and this applies only to hagalah; and accordingly the Rav too, who wrote the reason because of as it absorbs so it emits, perhaps concedes in libun that even though prohibition was not used through fire except in part, he must whiten all of it — not like Pri Toar, who inserted in the Rav's words that when he kashered or whitened the part it is kosher because of as it absorbs so it emits; however all this according to their view that even though prohibition was used only in part, the absorption wafts through moisture more than the measure of use; and accordingly even according to Ra'ah and his supporters, that it is kashered through hagalah in its part — if he used it in heter before hagalah in that part where prohibition was not used, behold the heter is forbidden; but Maharam Mintz wrote in a responsum: prohibition that fell on the pot in one place does not require hagalah in all of it, for so said the Rosh; and that which we say hot in part is hot in whole — this is specifically for this matter: if the vessel became hot at one end and prohibition fell at the other end — for this matter hot in part is hot in whole helps, that its heat causes the prohibition to be absorbed at the other end; but it does not help that if prohibition fell on one side it should spread to the other side — end of his words. And Beit Chadash brings him and wrote on it: we do not establish thus, but we hold as Rashba wrote; and so is the view of most poskim — end of his words. And I do not know from where he has that the view of most poskim is like Maharam Mintz; and so we establish above siman 94, that a metal spoon inserted in a pot need not be measured except against what was inserted, not against the entire spoon; and Shach wrote siman 85, and Semag negative commandments 141 p. 52, and Sefer HaTerumah, and Gematriyot chapter 9 of Laws of Forbidden Foods, and Mordechai chapter 25, and Issur VeHeter general rule 37 law 2, and other poskim — the reason: even though hot in part is hot in whole, nevertheless we say its absorption travels throughout; and this is Maharam Mintz's words; and so too Maharai in Torat HaBayit seif 132 and in his rulings siman 151 — see there, who elaborated; and it is clear from his words that even though hot in part is hot in whole, nevertheless prohibition absorbed in a hot vessel does not spread more than the place where prohibition was used; and so we certainly establish; and it is possible the Tur and Rav in the hagahah who wrote because of as it absorbs so it emits do not mean that it carries absorption, but rather that it absorbed only in part — so at the time of emission there is prohibition only in part — investigate. And from this emerges: metal vessels where prohibition was used in part and afterward heter was used in the other part that was not used the first time — permitted b'dieved; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 58 law 41 — permitted b'dieved; however one may say this is specifically in heat through cooking, we do not say it carries absorption throughout; but in heat through actual fire, it is possible all concede to the Rosh and Ra'ah above that prohibition spreads throughout; and so it appears from Issur VeHeter general rule 37 law 2 explicitly, who wrote: one need not nullify the entire metal spoon, for even though hot in part is hot in whole, nevertheless since its heat is only through boiling and not through the fire itself, it does not carry and emit its absorption throughout — end of his words; it implies that through the fire itself it carries its absorption throughout; and it also appears from these words of Issur VeHeter that through fire it also carries its emission throughout; and it appears this is not against Ra'ah and his supporters above, who hold that libun does not help in part; for Issur VeHeter also concedes that in libun emission does not apply, for it does not emit but destroys; rather he comes to say where emission applies, it emits throughout, such as a forbidden spit on which meat was roasted over fire that requires sixty against the entire spit, since when it emits in meat through the moisture of the meat it carries its emission throughout — so it seems to me.
As it absorbs so it emits. And kashering part suffices, and that is b'dieved; but l'chatchila one must also kasher all of it, as the Rav wrote in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 10; and so too Maharshal there, and Issur VeHeter general rule 58 law 40; and with this there is no difficulty from what Beit Chadash challenged in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 11, the Tur's words contradict each other — see there.
