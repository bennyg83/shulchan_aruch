As long as it is ben yomo. This is the language of the Tur: one does not kasher the vessel until it is not ben yomo, or there are sixty parts in the water in which he kashered against it; but if it is ben yomo and there is not sixty against it, its hagalah did not succeed; and not only that, but even the vessel in which he kashered is also forbidden if it was of heter; and likewise if he kashered a meat vessel to eat milk in it or the reverse, it must not be ben yomo; and if he kashered in a forbidden vessel, it must not be ben yomo — end of his words. And I wondered that Rama did not also cite this, that when there are sixty parts in the water against the vessel he kashered, one may kasher even ben yomo, as he wrote. And regarding what the Tur wrote — and likewise if he kashered a meat vessel, etc., it must not be ben yomo — this is specifically l'chatchila; but b'dieved his hagalah succeeded, for it gives nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am of heter; and even in a meat bowl in a milk cauldron when both are ben yomo, the Tur ruled in the name of the Rosh in siman 95 that it is permitted; and in Hagahat Asheri end of 71a he brings in truth likewise regarding chametz on erev Pesach before the fourth hour that it is permitted even ben yomo, for it is nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am of heter; except that here it deals l'chatchila, for it is forbidden to cause nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am l'chatchila, as I wrote siman 92 seif 2; and for us there is prohibition even b'dieved if both are ben yomo, according to Sefer HaTerumot that the Tur brings there siman 95. And one may also say that it must not be ben yomo according to what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba, who was asked how we kasher a vessel that did not absorb prohibition except to the place of its absorption, and when they insert it in the cauldron it emits from the place of prohibition and absorbs heter; and he answered: since we kasher only when it is not ben yomo, we have no concern — end of his words.
And afterward he kashered them, etc. For immersion is not a prerequisite for hagalah, but rather that it emerge from impurity to purity through immersion. And some hold that it appears like one who immerses while a sheretz is in his hand if he immerses first.
