(Siman 121, Shach s.k. 8) Except through other liquids. In Mishbetzot Zahav (Orach Chaim siman 451 s.k. 16) he understood that the distinction is that oil burns and dries and is of fire, not so with water — see there. In Machatzit HaShekel on Menachem Azariah's responsum it appears not so, but rather with gentiles their way is to cook in a pan with forbidden milk liquids and the prohibition itself is absorbed in the vessel without intermediary; and it is called use through fire. But in a chametz pan all fryings are heter, only they boil and the chametz is absorbed in the vessel, and chametz absorbed through boiling is emitted through boiling — see there. And in Mishbetzot Zahav there he is uncertain regarding forbidden liquids whether it is called through fire, since they are not absorbed through intermediary; and he concludes not called through fire; and all resulted from not examining Menachem Azariah himself, for according to what we cited from Menachem Azariah it is clear that he holds forbidden liquids are called through fire. And behold what Shach concluded: and accordingly also in a pan of milk, etc. — in my humble opinion it is not proven; specifically for chametz he needed to find a reason that the liquids are heter; and if the liquids were chametz and called through fire, they require libun, for chametz is called issur balei'a; but milk is called heter balei'a, and through fire hagalah also helps, as we say in Avodah Zarah regarding notar. Although Pri Chadash (siman 93 s.k. 4) wrote: since Ran distinguishes between notar and chametz — in chametz it is called issura balei'a — so too in a pot of meat or milk it is called issura balei'a; in Machatzit HaShekel he was not precise, for it is clear in Rashba in Torat HaBayit (Bayit 4 p. 124) that chametz nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am is not called heter balei'a, for chametz — its name is upon it; not so from meat to milk. Behold explicitly that from meat to milk it is called heter balei'a more than chametz regarding nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am; if so, likewise it is called heter balei'a regarding that hagalah suffices; and so it is explicitly clear for halachah in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim (siman 499 seif 5).
