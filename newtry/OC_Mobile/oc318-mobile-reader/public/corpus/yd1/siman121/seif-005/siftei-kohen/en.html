But permitted to use, etc. Namely in all vessels except a knife that requires insertion, as below seif 7.
Cold, etc. Darkei Moshe wrote that even vinegar is permitted to place in it through rinsing and washing, as Rashba wrote (and the Mechaber chapter 7) seif 7 regarding radish with a knife or other sharp things — end of his words; and it appears from his words that the same applies to all other sharp things — permitted to place in it, all that was rinsed well; and above siman 91 end of s.k. 2, what I wrote in the name of Orach Chaim — it appears from his words that it is forbidden to place in it a sharp wet thing — see there; and from Rashba's words there is no proof that insertion is preferable; and it requires investigation; and it is possible there is a textual error in Darkei Moshe, and it deals with a stam vessel whose use is cold — namely that he wrote here in the hagahah at its end regarding vessels used in them cold that it is permitted to place in them vinegar and other sharp things, and he did not write thus prominently at the beginning regarding those used with hot things; and so it appears in Pri Toar chapter 3, that specifically when used with cold it is permitted to place sharp things in it — see there.
Or b'dieved. Meaning: that he acquired it and has no other vessel — permitted to use it; but if he used it b'dieved, the food is not forbidden — this need not be said.
And even vessels whose use is cold, if there is concern, etc. So it should read; and this is to use it regularly; but incidentally, even vessels certainly used hot are permitted to use through rinsing and scouring, as was explained; and see in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 6.
That they heated them, etc. Or that wine stood in it and the like within twenty-four hours, for it is considered pickled.
And even beds and tables, etc. See in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 20, explained that they practice to kasher tables through pouring.
And earthenware vessels, etc. This is the language of Issur VeHeter the long version, law 42; however in a vessel not subject to hagalah, such as earthenware cups in which a gentile drank — a Jew is permitted to buy and drink through rinsing, etc.; and all the more so glass vessels in which a gentile drank many days — permitted for a Jew to buy from him, rinse and immerse, etc.; and specifically drinking vessels; but other earthenware bought from a gentile — if there is only doubt that it was used hot, they require breaking; but new vessels bought from the market, even in earthenware, need not be concerned, for it is not their way to use new vessels. And according to these matters are the Rav's words in the hagahah, except that he abbreviated; however it is difficult, for behold in earthenware in which a gentile drank, we establish below siman 135 seif 4 that they require filling and pouring; and it is possible to hold like the Rosh and Tur there, that specifically those earthenware known to be from a type of earth that absorbs much; and according to this we do not establish thus, for it appears from the Mechaber's words there that he rules like Rashba and his supporters that every stam earthenware vessel also requires filling and pouring; however one may say that Issur VeHeter the long version deals here where they were already used before they came into the gentile's possession, for in such a case we say there that for everyone scouring suffices since they already absorbed their fill — see there.
