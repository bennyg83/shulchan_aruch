If he performed hag'alah on a utensil that requires libbun, it is forbidden to use it with hot, even not by means of fire. {Rama: But it is permitted to use it with cold, even l'chatchila, by way of rinsing and scrubbing well; and all the more so with a utensil that requires hag'alah. And this is specifically in an occasional manner, such as when it is in the house of a non-Jew, or b'dieved. But if he wants to use it regularly, some are stringent and say that even to use it with cold it requires hag'alah or libbun, a decree lest he come to use it with hot (Mordechai and Rokeach), and so is the practice. And even utensils whose use is with cold — if there is reason to be concerned that they warmed wine in them, such as silver utensils — the practice is to perform hag'alah, and one should not deviate (Arukh in the name of Raavyah). And even chests and tables that are purchased from non-Jews, the practice is to perform hag'alah lest hot water was spilled on them; and this is specifically l'chatchila, but b'dieved we are not concerned for all this (ibid.). And earthenware utensils that were used with cold — since hag'alah is impossible — this is considered b'dieved, and scrubbing and rinsing well suffices (ibid.), and it is permitted afterwards to put into it even sharp items such as vinegar and the like, and all the more so hard items such as spices and the like; and see below siman 91.}
