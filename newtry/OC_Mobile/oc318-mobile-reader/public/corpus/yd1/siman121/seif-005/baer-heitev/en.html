Cold. Darkei Moshe wrote that even vinegar is permitted to place in it through rinsing and washing; and it appears from his words that the same applies to all other sharp things — permitted to place in it, all that was rinsed well; and above siman 91 seif 1, what he wrote in the name of Orach Chaim — it appears from his words that it is forbidden to place in it a sharp wet thing; and it is possible there is a textual error in Darkei Moshe, and it deals with a stam vessel whose use is cold, but not if its use is hot — end of Shach's words.
B'dieved. Meaning: that he acquired it and has no other vessel — permitted to use it; but if he used it b'dieved, the food is not forbidden — this need not be said. Shach.
That they heated them. Or that wine stood in it and the like within twenty-four hours, for it is considered pickled — Shach. (And this is specifically to use it regularly; but incidentally, even vessels certainly used hot are permitted to use through rinsing and scouring, as was explained.)
For all. This applies only to what the Rav said in the hagahah; but what the Mechaber wrote — if he kashered a vessel that requires libun, etc. — even b'dieved it is forbidden, and this is simple — Taz. And see in Orach Chaim siman 451 seif 20, explained that they practice to kasher tables through pouring.
And permitted. Issur VeHeter wrote: that which they permit in earthenware is specifically drinking vessels; but other earthenware that one bought from a gentile — if there is only doubt that it was used hot, they require breaking; but new vessels bought from the market, even in earthenware, need not be concerned, for it is not their way to use new vessels — end of his words. However it is difficult, for behold in earthenware in which a gentile drank, we establish below siman 135 seif 4 that they require filling and pouring; and one may say that Issur VeHeter deals here where they were already used before they came into the gentile's possession, for in such a case we say there that for everyone scouring suffices since they already absorbed their fill — see there — end of Shach's words.
