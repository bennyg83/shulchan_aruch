This applies specifically, etc.; but if he said it twice, etc. Rama inferred in Darkei Moshe from the Gemara in the second chapter of Nedarim that, just as one who says twice, “I hereby become a nazir today,” must observe two periods of nezirus, the same should apply to vows and oaths. Mordechai and Tosafos, however, indicate that one nezirus takes effect upon another only when both are accepted on the same day, for the second declaration then clearly intends an additional period. They imply that if he first accepts thirty days and then forty days, the first thirty may count toward both because the second declaration merely adds days. Darkei Moshe applies this reasoning to vows as well. In my humble opinion, this proof is not compelling. The special rule is stated specifically regarding nezirus, which Scripture teaches can take effect upon an existing nezirus. The Mishnah itself illustrates “a vow within a vow” through repeated nezirus rather than through an ordinary vow, because a nazir’s undertaking is also called a vow. Ran explains that the second nezirus takes effect immediately within the period of the first, although it cannot be counted until the first concludes. Ordinary vows have no comparable rule. Tosafos likewise asks why the Mishnah abandons ordinary vows and answers that such a case cannot be found in ordinary vows; nezirus is different because it has a fixed thirty-day term. This appears to be the Beis Yosef’s intent in disputing the Tur and saying that twenty-two days suffice in the case before him, since the rule of repeated nezirus derives from the minimum thirty-day term and does not establish that every ordinary vow stacks upon itself. The Levush therefore omitted Rama’s statement that repeating a twenty-day abstention from meat creates forty days, and instead explains that only where the second vow adds days may the total be extended. Even should one interpret repeated declarations of a twenty-day abstention as intending a second twenty-day period, that result would flow from the person’s intent, not from a legal principle that one ordinary vow takes effect within another. If he intended the second vow merely to rest upon the first, he would be forbidden only twenty days. Rashba likewise distinguishes ordinary vows from nezirus. Rama’s ruling is therefore not conclusive.
Only if he made both vows on the same day, etc. Bach objects and compares the case to one who says twice, “I hereby become a nazir today,” concerning which Shmuel requires two periods of nezirus. He therefore says that one should be concerned for forty-two days even where both vows were made on one day. This is surprising, for Mordechai and Tosafos support Rama’s distinction, as stated in Darkei Moshe. Bach also appears to assume that ordinary vows and nezirus follow the same rule, but I have already explained that they do not.
