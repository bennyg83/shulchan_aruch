And also regarding other oaths. This implies that even an oath taken under duress involves a prohibition and requires release. Thus, if one swore to a coercer that certain produce would be forbidden to him unless it belonged to the king, he should obtain release rather than transgress. This is even more strongly implied by the Levush. In my humble opinion, however, Rambam does not appear to mean this. On the contrary, since regarding oaths under duress he writes, “It is permitted,” whereas regarding vain and mistaken oaths he writes that one is exempt, it follows that an oath under duress involves no prohibition at all. This is also implied by the Mishnah in the fourth chapter of Nedarim, where Beis Hillel says, “Even by an oath,” and by the Yerushalmi cited above in siman 232, seif katan 29, which indicates that coerced oaths are permitted just as coerced vows are. This is likewise clear from all the poskim, and from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch above, siman 232, seif 14. Such is the accepted ruling.
Nevertheless, there is a prohibition. Meaning that if he already swore, the oath should be released. This does not mean merely that it is forbidden initially to swear, for that is obvious even regarding vows, as stated above in siman 232, seif 13.
Even if he was compelled, etc. Meaning that he is compelled to swear by their courts, since under their law refusal would subject him to death. Nevertheless, if he possesses funds and can reach a settlement, he should settle. The Hagahos Ashri likewise writes that we compel him personally, since he has the means, so that Hashem’s Name not be desecrated. If he lacks the means to settle, he is fully coerced; even where desecration of Hashem’s Name is involved, he may swear while nullifying the oath in his heart. The Bach writes that he is not obligated to be killed rather than transgress in such a case, as stated above in siman 157. Nevertheless, it appears that others should assist him as much as possible, even from their own funds, to prevent desecration of Hashem’s Name.
If he was compelled and there is no desecration of Hashem’s Name, etc. Meaning that he is compelled by their courts, but no desecration of Hashem’s Name is involved. Even if he has the means to settle, he may nullify the oath in his heart, etc.
