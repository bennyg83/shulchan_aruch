The law, etc. From the statement on 25a, “The Sages taught: the stringency,” etc., it follows that in all other respects vows and oaths are equal. Likewise, Nedarim 18a asks, “What is the stringency,” etc., since a vow also, etc., and answers, “Rather,” etc. There, on 9a, the Gemara applies incomplete formulations to oaths, although Scripture states them only regarding vows and naziriteship, as explained there on 3a. See also the Ran, chapter 3 of Shevuot. In many places oaths are called vows, and proofs are brought from oaths to vows, both for leniency and for stringency, as in Nedarim 64b and 65a. Thus it is evident.
Whether, etc., and whether regarding what he intended, etc. The original teaching was stated concerning oaths.
Some say, etc. Mistaken vows are discussed in Nedarim 25b, and exaggerated vows there on 24b, according to the Ran’s version. The Rosh likewise writes there that this is the reading of the Tosefta and the Yerushalmi, as stated below. Vows of inducement are found in the Yerushalmi, cited by the Ran on 25b, s.v. “Just as.” Vows under duress follow from Shevuot 26a, “excluding one who was compelled,” and from Nedarim 25a, where Rav Huna said, “The tanna taught: Beit Shammai did not,” etc.
And this, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh, chapter 3 of Nedarim, section 6.
And also regarding the other, etc. Meaning, although we say that these four kinds of vows require no release by a sage, nevertheless, with oaths one should initially obtain release. But to swear initially is forbidden even in these cases, as stated in siman 232, seif 13. This applies only to three of the kinds of oaths. Regarding an oath made under duress, no one disputes the rule stated above, and one may even swear initially to a coercer, as stated there. The Yerushalmi also says so explicitly.
