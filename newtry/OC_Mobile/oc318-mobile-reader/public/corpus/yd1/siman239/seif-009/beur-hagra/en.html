One who attaches, etc., like Rava; and nevertheless attaching in a vow is like a vow, as written end of siman 204; and likewise if he transferred, etc. — Rif. But Rashi holds also attaching in a vow is nothing per Rava; and see there, s.v. V'Hu sheNadar, etc., and s.v. Taritz, etc.; but attaching, etc. But Rif explains he holds he specifies issur that he attaches; and since biblical issur attaches, therefore also in oath — for its essence is written in oath, as written "or he forbade issur upon herself with an oath," and from there we derive to vow; and the tanna taught in vow to include. And this is what he said "granted for Abaye," etc.; "but for Rava it is difficult," for he said issur does not attach, and here he said it attaches; and furthermore in oath too as above; and he answers Taritz, etc.: we do not deal with issur, rather he said "what matter that is forbidden like a vow — behold upon me," etc., meaning attaching in a vow is like a vow; but attaching in oath — no, for we do not learn attaching from issur, only from "when a man vows" until he vows on a vowed matter, as written chapter 2 of Nedarim 14a: we exclude forbidden matter; and the same one can say we exclude in a sworn matter. So Ran in the name of Ramban; and so Rosh; and Tosafos there, s.v. Ela, etc.; and Ran explains Taritz thus, etc., like Rashi: primarily vow applies, not until he tied it to a vowed matter; therefore attaching is also like vow since vow's essence is in attaching — not so in oath. And nevertheless attaching in vow is like vow — not like Rashi; and as Tosafos and Rif proved from chapter 4 of Nazir "one who said," etc.; and even though we say there specifically twenty-four, and attaching even after twenty-four, as written "on the day Gedaliah died," etc. — different there, for he did not say "and I like him" but "and I alone," and does not imply after twenty-four. And if you say perhaps because he said "and I" twenty-four, it is as if he said "and I am a nazir" — not due to attaching; he answered the end teaches the first was permitted, etc.; and we also taught "behold I am like Shimshon, like the husband of the night," etc., and many like it. Ran.
