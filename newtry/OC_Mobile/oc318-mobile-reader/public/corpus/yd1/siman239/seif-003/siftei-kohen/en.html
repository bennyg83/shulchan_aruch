The oath takes effect, etc. Although vows concerning intangible matters take effect only rabbinically, as stated above in siman 213, an oath takes effect by Torah law because it rests upon the person himself. For this same reason, an oath does not take effect against a mitzvah, although a vow does, as stated above in siman 215. A person cannot validly swear that he will not perform the mitzvah, such as swearing not to sit in a sukkah, since he is already obligated to sit there.
A ban of excommunication, etc. The Tashbetz explains that shamta, cherem, and nidui are all one category. It appears that one should not be lenient, for at the beginning of Nedarim the Gemara states that after teaching vows, which prohibit an object to the person, the Mishnah teaches consecrations, which likewise prohibit an object, in contrast to an oath, which prohibits the person with respect to the object. This shows that a cherem has the law of a vow. The primary ruling is therefore to impose upon it both the stringencies of oaths and those of vows.
