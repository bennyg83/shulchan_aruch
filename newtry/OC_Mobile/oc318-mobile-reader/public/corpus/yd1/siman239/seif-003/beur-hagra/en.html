Excommunication, etc. As stated in Pirkei deRabbi Eliezer, chapter 41: “A ban is an oath, and an oath is a ban,” etc. This is cited in Yalkut Shimoni to Joshua, section 18. There it says: “Know the power of a ban,” etc., as it is written, “For the great oath had been,” and asks: did all Israel actually take an oath? Rather, this teaches that a ban is an oath. It is also cited in Yalkut Shimoni at the end of Judges, section 76.
Some say, etc. A vow is more stringent than an oath because it takes effect even concerning a mitzvah, since it renders the object forbidden to him, as the Ran and the other authorities explain, and as stated in Nedarim 16b. The same applies to bans, as stated there on 2b.
