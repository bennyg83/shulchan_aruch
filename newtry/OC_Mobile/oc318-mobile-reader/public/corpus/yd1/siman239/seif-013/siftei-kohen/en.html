An oath does not take effect, etc. Maharam of Prague, responsum 181, discusses one who vowed that each time he performed a certain act he would pay one peshutah. What remedy is available, since the monetary obligation cannot be released before it takes effect? Must he therefore approach a sage for release after every occurrence, or do we say that once the first is released all are released, because none can take effect before the first? The Riva leaves the matter unresolved. Maharam Mintz explains the doubt as follows. If the declaration is comparable to repeated oaths not to eat the same loaf, the later oaths do not presently take effect; if the first is released, the second then takes effect, and release would be required each time. Alternatively, since he expressed everything in one oath, it may all constitute a single oath, so that release of the first releases all. The wording of the original question, which speaks of a person who made a vow, implies a genuine vow: he declared the act forbidden to himself and added that if he performed it he would pay a peshutah. Payment does not repair the violation of the vow; rather, the monetary obligation begins only after he transgresses. This resembles one who swore not to speak with a certain person and then swore that if he sought release from the first oath, meat would become forbidden to him. He may seek release from the first oath, but not yet from the second, because it has not taken effect. The questioner therefore remained uncertain whether release would be needed after every violation or whether release of the first would release them all.
