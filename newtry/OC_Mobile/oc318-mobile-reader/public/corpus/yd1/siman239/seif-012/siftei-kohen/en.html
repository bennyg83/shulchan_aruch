He said "behold I am," etc. Meaning: even if he says "behold I am a nazir today" he must observe two nezirut periods — meaning two sets of thirty days, for we rule even if he said "behold I am a nazir" only one day he must observe thirty days of nezirut; therefore since we rule nezirut takes effect on nezirut, he must observe two sets of thirty days one after another. And know nezirut applies before the Temple and not, in Land and abroad, as proven in chapter 3 of Nazir; only Rambam wrote chapter 2 of Nazir: one who vows nowadays abroad — they compel ascent to Eretz Yisrael to observe nezirut there; and all time abroad forbidden wine, impurity to dead, shaving, and all nezirut details, even though these days do not count — end quote; and so Semag negative commandments 243, page 74 beginning 73; and Raavad challenged Rambam there that they do not compel ascent, for tumah and taharah are not among us — behold we are all impure from the dead and no difference between Eretz Yisrael and abroad — end quote. And see Kesef Mishneh there and Tosafot Yom Tov chapter 3 of Nazir who extended on this; nevertheless plain nezirut applies everywhere and always; and so from Mechaber's laws of nezirut here and above end of siman 203, 206, siman 215 seif 5, end of siman 229, siman 234 s.k. 24. And that he did not write other nezirut laws — because Tur and other poskim also did not write them; and see Rambam and Semag distinctions; and also learn several laws for vows from them; and where they said safek nezirut leniently we rule safek nedarim stringently. And nevertheless it seems Rambam and Semag did not say they compel ascent except for standard nezirut due to impurity of lands of nations, as implied in Kesef Mishneh there; but vow of Samson nezirut, since impurity permitted and only wine and shaving forbidden — nezirut applies abroad; therefore Rambam and Semag did not write this law there, only before the law of Samson nezirut. However asking helps in nezirut like vows, as Mechaber wrote above end of siman 229; and so Rambam chapter 2 of Hilchot Nazir: one who vowed nezirut and regretted — he is asked of a sage and they release his nezirut like other vows — end quote. And in several places Kesef Mishneh did not write from where he derived this — and it is Gemara chapter 2 of Nedarim 17-18, and end of Nedarim 90, chapter 5 of Nazir 30, and in many places; perhaps due to obviousness Kesef Mishneh did not need to write it. However one who accepts Samson nezirut — asking does not help, as Rambam wrote chapter 3 of Hilchot Nazir law 14; and it is Gemara chapter 2 of Nazir 14, Makkot 22, and it is simple. And father and husband also annul nezirut of daughter and wife like vows, as Mechaber wrote above siman 234 s.k. 24; and so Rambam end of Hilchot Nedarim and chapter 9 of Hilchot Nazir — from Gemara as explained in Kesef Mishneh there. However in Samson nezirut it is written in responsum of Beit Yitzchak part 2 siman 45 page 22d, responsum of R' Yehuda Bei Rav that the husband cannot annul; and see in later responsa several responsa on laws of nezirut and Samson nezirut.
