He said, “I hereby become, etc.” Meaning that even if he says, “I hereby become a nazir today,” he must observe two periods of nezirus, each of thirty days. We rule that even one who accepts nezirus for a single day must observe thirty days; since nezirus can take effect upon an existing nezirus, he must therefore observe two consecutive thirty-day periods. Know that nezirus applies both when the Beis HaMikdash stands and when it does not, in Eretz Yisrael and outside it, as is evident at the end of the third chapter of Nazir. Rambam, however, writes at the end of the second chapter of Hilchos Nazir that one who accepts nezirus nowadays outside Eretz Yisrael is compelled to ascend to Eretz Yisrael and observe it there. While still outside Eretz Yisrael he may not drink wine, become impure through the dead, or cut his hair, and all details of nezirus apply to him, although those days do not count. Semag writes likewise. Raavad disputes Rambam and says that he is not compelled to ascend, since the laws of purity are not practiced among us and we are all impure through the dead, leaving no relevant distinction between Eretz Yisrael and elsewhere. See Kesef Mishneh and Tosafos Yom Tov there. In any event, it is clear that nezirus takes effect in every place and era. This is also implied by the Mechaber’s treatment of nezirus here and in the other cited simanim. He omitted the remaining laws only because the Tur and other poskim likewise omitted them. Rambam and Semag should be consulted for their distinctions, from which many laws of vows may also be learned. Although a doubtful nezirus is treated leniently, a doubtful vow is treated stringently. It nevertheless appears that Rambam and Semag compel ascent only in an ordinary nezirus, because of the impurity of foreign lands, as Kesef Mishneh implies. A person who accepted the nezirus of Shimshon, however, may become impure and is forbidden only wine and haircutting; he therefore observes that nezirus outside Eretz Yisrael. This explains why Rambam and Semag placed the requirement to ascend only before discussing the nezirus of Shimshon. Release by a sage is effective for ordinary nezirus just as for vows, as the Mechaber writes above at the end of siman 229 and Rambam rules at the beginning of the second chapter of Hilchos Nazir. Its source is found in several passages of Nedarim and Nazir. A person who accepted the nezirus of Shimshon, however, cannot obtain release, as Rambam rules, based on Nazir and Makkos. A father or husband may annul the nezirus of his daughter or wife just as he may annul vows, as the Mechaber and Rambam rule. Regarding the nezirus of Shimshon, however, the Mabit, citing Rabbi Yaakov Beirav, writes that a husband cannot annul it. See the later responsa for many rulings concerning ordinary nezirus and the nezirus of Shimshon.
