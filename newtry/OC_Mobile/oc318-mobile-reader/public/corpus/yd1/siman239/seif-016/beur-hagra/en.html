And even if they are. There is no saying that Hashem will be called the Lord, and Hashem’s people will be saved
And it is possible etc. but etc. Nedarim 14b; see 15b; Ran s.v. uleinyan; as 213.
And if he ate, etc. — see in the sugya there; and Rashi's view: everything depends on the first eating — whether he ate the condition first or the prohibition first; if he ate intentionally the oath takes effect, if unintentionally it does not; and even initially permitted to eat the second, as Ran wrote and as in the Gemara "we too taught konam," etc., permitted initially with his wife; and taught on it, etc.; and as written above "since my vows," etc., permitted in bread — all the more if he ate the prohibition first, permitted in the condition since the oath did not take effect. And he explains what is written there "first" — the condition is called first, and he ate it first; and "first intentionally and second unintentionally exempt" — the same if he ate prohibition first unintentionally and then condition intentionally — exempt. And incidentally in the end he taught thus; and in both exempt, exempt, and permitted as above — so Ran. And from what is written "first intentionally and second unintentionally liable" — specifically he ate condition first; but if he ate prohibition first intentionally and then condition unintentionally — not liable at all, neither sacrifice, for we require inadvertence in prohibition; nor lashes, since afterward he ate condition unintentionally. And Tosafos there, s.v. Amar, etc.; and here specifically says first intentionally, etc.; and therefore also said at the beginning first unintentionally, etc., as above; and both unintentionally exempt — whether he ate condition first, and it is obvious he is exempt even if second intentional. Both intentional — lashes, whether he ate condition first or prohibition first, for we rule like Rabbi Yochanan. And R' Yitzchak's view in Tosafos there: everything depends on eating the condition — if he ate intentionally the oath takes effect, whether he ate it first or last; if unintentionally it does not, whether first or last; and "first" means the condition, as Rashi — only specifically says first, etc., exempt, first, etc., liable — everything depends on eating first, only one can say if he ate first which is the condition before second which is prohibition, or ate second before — found he ate condition unintentionally and prohibition intentionally — exempt whether condition first or prohibition first. And this is the beginning: first unintentional and second intentional exempt; ate condition intentionally and prohibition unintentionally liable, whether condition first or prohibition first — and this is the end: first intentional and second unintentional liable, etc.; both unintentional, both intentional as above. The difference between Rashi and R' Yitzchak: if he ate prohibition first unintentionally and then condition intentionally — R' Yitzchak liable, Rashi exempt. And so Tur — no difference between them except in this; but wrote no practical difference except for sacrifice, etc.; but regarding prohibition no difference, and can eat, etc.; but of prohibition, etc.; and if he ate, etc., as written here in Shulchan Aruch — and his words are difficult, for there is also practical difference in prohibition in this case, that if he ate prohibition first unintentionally — per Rashi he can eat condition afterward even initially, and per R' Yitzchak even sacrifice liable as above. Found what is written here "and if he ate of condition first," etc., is per one opinion; but if he ate prohibition first it is in dispute; but in seif 17 he ruled like Rashi and it will be explained there. And Raavad's view is also like R' Yitzchak; and so R' Chaim — except he calls prohibition first and condition second, and has reversed version in Bava: first liable and second exempt. And another view for Rambam: also has R' Chaim's version and explains like Tosafos that condition is called first; explains liable meaning lashes; explains everything depends at time of eating prohibition, not caring if condition eaten unintentionally or intentionally — see Kesef Mishneh there; and his words are difficult, for he brings support konam if I ate and if I drank, etc. And Rabbenu Tam like Rashi, except explains first means whichever he eats first, whether condition or prohibition, beginning or end; and disputes Rashi: if he ate prohibition intentionally first and then condition unintentionally — also liable sacrifice, for we do not require inadvertence in eating prohibition, only at time of transgressing the oath. Now the sugya is plain: in both beginning and end no difference whether ate condition first or prohibition; and per Rashi need to distinguish — and see below seif 17.
