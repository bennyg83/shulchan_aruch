Since vows in error are permitted—if he swore that he will not eat this loaf if so-and-so said this thing, and it was found that he said it, he is permitted the loaf. And similarly if he attached the loaf to another, having said, "An oath that I will not eat this loaf if I eat that one," and he forgot and ate that one—the oath does not take effect upon him, and he may eat the conditional loaf first; and we are not concerned lest he forget and eat the forbidden one afterward. But the forbidden loaf—it is forbidden to eat, lest he forget and eat the conditional one. And if he ate the conditional loaf first in error, he is permitted the second; and if he ate it intentionally, he is liable for the second.
