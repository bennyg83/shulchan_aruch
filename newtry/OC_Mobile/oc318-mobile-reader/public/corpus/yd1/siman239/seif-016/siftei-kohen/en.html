If he swore "I will not eat," etc. Derishah siman 13 wrote: if so how do we find shevuat bitui? For Tur (and Mechaber) wrote above siman 236 that whether he swore on past or future he is liable sacrifice. One can say: certainly if he swore on something that is so and he is not certain — liable sacrifice, for he swore falsely, such as "oath that I ate today" and he did not eat — even though he forgot, behold he is inadvertent and liable sacrifice. But here he did not swear on something that is so, rather on a condition: "if so-and-so said this" and it turns out he said it — behold this is inadvertence and permitted to eat, for the oath does not take effect at all since he swore on a condition. And likewise a virgin [bread] with his fellow — end quote. And it is a wonder it escaped him Gemara Aruch that is explicitly against his words, for we learn in chapter Shevuot Shetayim (Shevuot 26 beginning 2b): Rava asked Rav Nachman: what is inadvertence of shevuat bitui for the past? If he knew — intentional; if he did not know — compelled? He said: one who says "I know this oath is forbidden, but I do not know if one is liable sacrifice for it or not." And so Rambam chapter 3 of Hilchot Shevuot: how are inadvertent oaths, such as he swore not to eat and remembered he ate, etc.; if so what inadvertent bitui oaths are liable sacrifice for the past — such as he swore not to eat and he knows he ate and this false oath he swore is forbidden, but he did not know one is liable sacrifice — this is inadvertence liable sacrifice for past bitui oaths — end quote. And nevertheless it seems regarding prohibition he tied it to a condition and it is not shevuot shegagot, only at time of oath it was clear to him that as his words so it is — such as he swore not to eat this bread if so-and-so said this, it was clear in his mind he did not say it; and so implied in Gemara regarding "your heart compelled you." But if it was not clear to him, only he doubted, or he does not know yes or no, and swore "if so-and-so said this I will not eat this bread" — this is not shevuot shegagot and forbidden in the bread — understand.
