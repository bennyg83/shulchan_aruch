It is forbidden to eat either one, etc. Each one is itself called the prohibited item; once he eats that item, he may no longer be trusted to remain careful regarding the condition.
He did not remember, etc. The Levush questions this, since even if he did not remember the oath, the second item should still be forbidden, and he discusses the matter at length. Bach likewise questions it and writes that according to Rabbi Yochanan the second item is forbidden. In Perishah he explains that when the person made each item dependent upon the other, he intended the first one eaten to serve as the condition. His oath was structured as follows: “If I eat this one, I will not eat the other.” The second item was to be the prohibited item; he did not intend each to function simultaneously as both condition and prohibition. Before he ate either, each bore both possible designations because he could choose which to eat first. Once he ate one, his intention identified it as the condition, leaving only the other as the prohibited item. Consequently, even if he later eats the second deliberately, there is no further liability for an oath, even according to Rabbi Yochanan, because the oath takes effect only when the condition is eaten deliberately.
