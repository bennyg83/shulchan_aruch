He heard his fellow, etc. The Rif writes that even in such a case it is not regarded as though he uttered an oath with his mouth, and according to his view there is not even a prohibition, for he rules the same regarding a loaf. The Ran, however, writes that in his view this case is valid linkage. He asks: since linkage is learned for vows, why should it not also be learned for oaths, just as incomplete formulations apply to oaths although written only regarding vows? Even Abaye learns linkage for vows from oaths. He further asks why the Mishnah does not list this too among the stringencies of vows over oaths. He answers that linkage to a loaf is ineffective for an oath because a vow attaches prohibition to the object, so another object can be linked to it; an oath, however, forbids the person, and therefore cannot be learned from vows. In the case of this seif, however, linkage is effective even for an oath. Hence the Mishnah did not list it as a stringency of vows. This also explains the wording, “It is not regarded as though he uttered an oath with his mouth,” rather than “it is entirely ineffective,” because there are cases in which it is effective. The phrase means only that it is not equivalent to direct utterance in every respect. See there.
