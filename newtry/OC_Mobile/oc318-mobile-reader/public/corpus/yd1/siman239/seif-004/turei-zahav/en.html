"Sitting in a sukkah is upon me." One should not raise the difficulty that sitting in a sukkah is an intangible thing. Tosafos answered that it is only considered intangible when he does not mention the object — since he is not speaking of something concrete. But when he mentions the prohibited object, it is a tangible thing. Accordingly, above in siman 234 seif 1, when she says "may my hands be consecrated to their Maker" — it is not that she literally said this, but rather she is considered as if she said it, since she mentioned her hands. So the Ran wrote.
Upon me through an oath. The Maharshal wrote: From here it is implied that the terminology of an oath is effective when expressed in the terminology of a vow, and it would have been effective had an oath taken effect regarding a mitzvah, end quote. His intent is that although at the end of siman 206 there is a dispute regarding this, and the Tur wrote there in the name of the Rosh that a vow expressed in the terminology of an oath, such as, “I vow that I will not eat,” is ineffective, nevertheless here an oath expressed in the terminology of a vow is effective. So too is ruled below in seif 11.
