A handshake, etc. Rabbeinu Tam ruled that it has the force of an oath, from the verse, (Ezekiel 17:18) “He despised the oath by violating the covenant, although he had given his hand,” etc. It is also said, (at the end of Chronicles) “He also rebelled against King Nebuchadnezzar, who had adjured him,” etc.; and likewise, (Proverbs 6:1) “You have struck your palm for a stranger,” etc. Nevertheless, since we hold that oaths may be released, a handshake is no stronger than an oath. This is the meaning of “initially,” etc. See siman 230.
This applies only, etc. This follows from Bava Metzia, chapter 5, (74a): “This commercial mark effects acquisition. For what legal consequence,” etc. Rabbeinu Chananel explains that it concerns such a case. Were it deemed an oath, the obligation would follow simply because he must fulfill his oath, as stated in Choshen Mishpat 207:19. Although that proof can be rejected in light of the gloss there, siman 209, seif 4, the statement there that one is subject to “He Who exacted punishment” implies that he need not actually fulfill it.
He raised, etc. For this constitutes an oath upon his faith, as explained above.
