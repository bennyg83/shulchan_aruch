Clapping the hands—its law is like the law of other oaths, and it has annulment. (And ab initio one should annul it only in a place where they annul an oath.) (Tur, and Tosafot in the name of Rabbeinu Tam are stricter regarding tefillin than regarding oaths.) (And see above simanim 203 and 234.) And this applies when it is in the manner of an oath; but when it is in the manner of merchants who clap hands one to another to confirm a transaction—it has no law of oath at all. {Rama: If he raised his hand and said, "I clap my hand to heaven" or "to my Rock"—he requires annulment. (Mordechai chapter 3 of Shevuot, responsum of R' Yitzchak, and responsum of Maimon.) One who gives a hand-clap to his fellow and attached it to his thought can forgive him; and if he did not attach it to his thought but made it in the manner of an oath, he cannot forgive him and requires annulment. (Also there.)}
