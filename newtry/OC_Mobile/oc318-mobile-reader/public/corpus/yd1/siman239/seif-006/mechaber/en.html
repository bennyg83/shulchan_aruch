One who swore about a rabbinic mitzvah, such as not to light the Chanukah lamp or not to read the Megillah—the oath takes effect. And similarly if he swore to fulfill a rabbinic mitzvah, he is liable for an oath if he nullifies it. The same law applies to a matter that is from rabbinic exposition and is not explicit in the Torah. When is this stated? When he swore not to do a rabbinic mitzvah, or to do one. But if he swore to transgress a negative commandment, even if it is rabbinic—an oath does not take effect upon him.
