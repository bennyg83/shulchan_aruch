One who swore etc. Be'er Hagoleh (see Rashba 294 — Rish Nazir not proven from "Torah comes to include rabbinic"; Rosh from gemara "and one under oath" — applies even after rabbinic enactment) — gemara arranged Yoma 74a etc. (Shevuot 24a per R' Elazar).
And likewise, etc. — for otherwise there is no negative commandment and "yes" (meaning even per Rosh who brought proof from Nazir, as Beit HaGadol wrote); and Tosafos wrote 2b, s.v. D'Chi, etc.; and the same here.
And “to speak like.” This is the case of Hashem’s promise to Abraham, “He who is the Lord’s servant, who is the Lord’s servant, and who is the same as the Lord’s wife, who is the Lord’s wife, and who is the Lord’s wife.”
In another matter, etc., but, etc. — we do not tell him "stand and transgress" in order to fulfill the oath, as written in Yevamos 90b: "he said to him, I want to seat you uncircumcised," etc.; and not that the oath does not take effect — for Ran wrote even in midrash of sages it takes effect, such as one who swore to harm himself and eat nevelot and terefot half a measure; and explicitly we say in Yoma 74a that in rabbinic matters a negative commandment takes effect, as above; and see above siman 238 seif 4; and so in many places — only he must release the oath and not fulfill it; and so Shach; and it is simple.
