One who swears, etc. See Be’er HaGolah. See Responsa Rashba, siman 614, who writes that the passage at the beginning of Nazir proves nothing, for its meaning is: would the Torah come to include something prohibited only rabbinically? It appears that the Rosh’s proof is from the Gemara’s wording, “Is he not already under oath,” etc., which implies that even after the rabbinic enactment the oath takes effect. This is also an explicit Gemara in Yoma 74a: “And should you say,” etc.; “there it is different,” etc. See also Shevuot 24a: “This is well according to Reish Lakish,” etc.
And likewise, etc. Otherwise it would not be capable of both affirmation and negation. This is so even according to the Rosh, who brought proof from the passage in Nazir, as stated in Be’er HaGolah. See Tosafot 20b, s.v. “For when,” etc.; the same applies here.
The same applies to something, etc. With this distinction the Ran answers Tosafot’s question on 23b, s.v. “Which establishes”: “Let it say,” etc. The difficulty is resolved although the rule is derived from a verse in Yoma 74a, and according to Rabbi Yochanan it is not merely an asmachta, for an oath nevertheless takes effect upon it. The Ran writes in his novellae that the Rif and Rambam therefore rule that an oath to injure oneself takes effect, although they rule like the anonymous Mishnah in the chapter HaChovel (90b) that one may not wound oneself, a rule derived there (91b) from “and He shall make atonement,” etc. See also the Ran to Nedarim 8a, s.v. “This teaches us,” etc.
When does this apply, etc.; but, etc. We do not tell him to arise and transgress in order to fulfill the oath, as in Yevamot 90b: “I could have answered you from an uncircumcised priest,” etc. This does not mean that the oath fails to take effect. The Ran writes that it takes effect even upon a rabbinic teaching, such as one who swears to injure himself or to eat a half-measure of nonkosher food. Yoma 74a explicitly states that an oath takes effect upon a rabbinic prohibition with respect to the negative commandment, as explained above. See also siman 238, seif 4. The same is stated in many places. Rather, he must obtain release from the oath and may not fulfill it. The Shach writes likewise, and the matter is clear.
