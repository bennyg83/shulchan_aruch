But if he swore to transgress a rabbinic mitzvah, etc. I wonder — above siman 238 seif 4 he ruled like Rambam that one who swears to eat less than an olive's worth of nevelah is liable for oath even though half a measure is forbidden from the Torah since not lashed from Torah; and here ruled even rabbinic prohibition — oath does not take effect. And it is possible he means the oath does not take effect that he transgress it, but he should release it; and so in siman 238 he means liable for the oath regarding needing release. Further one can say as in responsum of Maharal ibn Chaviv siman 103: per Ran's view a rabbinic matter is more stringent than half a measure forbidden from Torah — for when forbidden from Torah it is from an inclusion of "every," and there is no specific negative commandment for its prohibition; and anything whose prohibition is not explicit — oath takes effect (like a Torah matter that is only midrash of sages and not explicit in Torah) — not so when forbidden rabbinically, for there is "do not turn"; and further a rabbinic matter — one can say "establish their words as Torah" — end quote. And for practical law in ibn Chaviv's responsum there he extended; and so in responsum Beit Yitzchak part 2 siman 51, and Maharshal and Bach ruled: one who swore to transgress a rabbinic sin — even though some say the oath takes effect for sacrifice and lashes, nevertheless they compel him to ask about his oath and not transgress; and so Atzei Levanon; and implies even if he swore inclusively on permitted with forbidden he should not transgress but release his oath; and so explained in responsum of Ran regarding inclusive of principal and interest; and see Maharshal on Tur's words in the name of Rosh — end quote: "and our other rabbis do not agree to this, but compel him to ask about his vow" — and such is essential — end quote; and Bach brings it in his conclusion. It seems explained even one who swore to nullify a rabbinic mitzvah — they compel him even though I do not know which rabbis; also from plain meaning of all poskim and so above siman 226 it does not appear thus — nevertheless be stringent; and one can say this only speaks of the essential law, but certainly proper to compel him to ask about his vow.
