For his rabbi from whom he learned most of his wisdom—whether Scripture, Mishnah, or Gemara—he tears all his garments until he exposes his heart; and some say he tears only a handbreadth and never mends; and even from distant report he tears forever; and if he did not learn most of his wisdom from him he tears only like other dead he mourns; and even if he learned only one thing from him, whether minor or major, he tears. {Rama: Some say a tear for a non-prominent rabbi is also never mended (Tur in the name of Rosh, Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi, Tosafot, and Ran); Torah students who sit together, question, answer, and learn together—some say their law is like a non-prominent rabbi and some like a prominent rabbi (these are the two views in Tur); and some say they need tear only for the rabbi from whom they learned most of their wisdom, but fellow learners or one who enlightened them in one matter is only a stringency where practiced—and they do not instruct so (Mordechai, Sheiltot, Hagah Maimoniyot in the name of Ram)—therefore they practice leniently in these lands.}
