And some say that he tears, etc. See Tosafot in Bava Metzia 33a, s.v. u-mekar'in, etc., and above siman 242 seif 30, from the case of Torah scholars in Babylonia, as above.
A Torah scholar, etc. See Rashi there. It implies that his law is like that of a teacher who is not one's primary teacher; moreover, one can have only one primary teacher.
And some say that he need not, etc. They hold that everything stated there about Shmuel and about the Torah scholars of Babylonia is only an added stringency.
