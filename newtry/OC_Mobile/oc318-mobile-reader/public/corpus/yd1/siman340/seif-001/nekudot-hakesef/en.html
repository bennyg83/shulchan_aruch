(Siman 340, in Taz seif katan 5) He wondered at Masat Binyamin, but there is no difficulty. Masat Binyamin writes that he saw his teachers follow this practice. His reason is clear: the synagogue mantle is worn only for modesty, and even when a person goes to the market for business or to a friend's home he does not wear it. He wears it only when going to the synagogue, for modesty and honor of Hashem, so it is not considered ordinary clothing. Agudah and Semak speak of such a garment. A regular coat, however, is worn in the street, and one who goes without it is disgraced and regarded as one of the lowly, so it is clothing. Taz's question that people do not wear the synagogue mantle to the cemetery and therefore how Rama could allude to it is no difficulty. Does a garment requiring tearing cease to require it afterward merely because it was not worn when he went to the cemetery? Moreover, Masat Binyamin writes that some elders of Krakow wore it in the synagogue during prayer and likewise when a relative died, at the cemetery, etc.
