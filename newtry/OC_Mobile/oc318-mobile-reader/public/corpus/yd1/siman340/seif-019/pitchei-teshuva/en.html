See Mishnat Chachamim there, who wrote that it is obvious that in a time when capital cases are not judged, and instead the blasphemer is only placed under nidui, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 425, one should not examine witnesses of a blasphemer as they did when capital cases were judged. Only in order to fulfill the mitzvah of stoning did they pronounce the Name explicitly and also the wording of the blasphemy. But to distance him from the community and obligate him in nidui, the witnesses should not utter the Divine Name, and certainly not the language of the blasphemy. If they say that so-and-so blasphemed, he is distanced and placed under nidui. Therefore he left requiring study the words of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote "and even one who hears from the witnesses," for why should the witnesses utter the blasphemy in that time? According to the Shach's view that the witness does not utter the blasphemy, etc., this works well; but he already proved at the beginning of his words from the fact that it says "from the witnesses" and not "from the one who heard" that the Shulchan Aruch holds like the Rambam and Semag mentioned above.
