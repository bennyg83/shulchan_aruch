For a proper person who is not suspected, etc. The Bach wrote that according to Rabbeinu Yonah, such a person is not called a proper person. He is not called a proper person for whom one tears, even not at the moment of the soul’s departure, unless he conducts himself in the ways of proper people, pursuing mitzvot and acts of kindness. This also appears to be the Ramban’s view, and so we rule.
Provided that he is standing there, etc. From this language it somewhat implies that he is obligated to tear only in his presence. This does not appear to me, for according to the Rosh and Tur, even if he is standing in his house he is obligated to tear, even if he is not standing over him, when he knows between death and burial. So wrote the Bach; see there, where he elaborated. It also appears to me that this is the Mechaber’s view, for in Orach Chaim siman 547 seif 6 he wrote: “And for a proper person, he tears only if he knew between death and burial”; see there. However, nowadays the custom is to be lenient in every case.
And Torah scholars are exempt, etc. The view of the Raavad and the Rosh regarding the case when Rav Safra’s soul rested, where the Sages thought not to tear for him, etc., appears explicitly that Torah scholars are obligated; see there.
