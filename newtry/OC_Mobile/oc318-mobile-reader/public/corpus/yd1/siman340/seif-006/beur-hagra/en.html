And he is one who stands, etc. From the fact that they did not rend for Rav Safra and said "we have not learned from him, etc." And in tractate Semachot (ch. 9): a sage who died — all who were standing at the moment of his death rend for him, and those who were not standing at the moment of his death do not rend for him. And it is a scribal error where it says "and a sage" — it should read "a worthy person." The Rosh and Torat HaAdam.
And a Torah scholar, etc. Just as there is no obligation to stand before minors, as stated at the end of chapter 2 of Bava Metzia — for specifically a Torah scholar of Babylonia, etc. Torat HaAdam.
And some say, etc. The Ran in the name of "some say" (and according to our text in Mo'ed Katan 25a: "and if he is a worthy person, he is certainly obligated to rend" — this is clear as the first opinion. And it must be said that the last opinion does not have the word "to rend" and follows the text of Shabbat, and likewise in the Rosh on Mo'ed Katan there it is absent). And so it seems at first glance from the tractate Semachot cited above. But the first opinion explains that this refers to until the burial.
