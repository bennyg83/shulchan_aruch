And in Choshen Mishpat, etc. In my humble opinion it appears that the point of the Nimukei Yosef is: he wrote there that one who borrowed a garment from his fellow to go to the house of mourning so that he could go and return — end quote — meaning the borrower himself requested it from the lender, so the lender only intended to benefit the borrower by fulfilling his request, and he can say to him: I only lent it to you so that you would go and return. But here the lender wrote to his fellow, etc. — meaning the lender came on his own initiative to give the borrower the merit of fulfilling the commandment of comforting mourners — and if so, every day the commandment is incumbent upon him, as above. And the Bach and the Sema wrote other strained resolutions, and it is also not resolved according to their words for what purpose the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote this law here, where it has no relevance except in Choshen Mishpat, and also why the Beit Yosef cited the words of the Rambam from Choshen Mishpat here. But according to my view it is well — for Choshen Mishpat deals with monetary matters, and here it deals with the matter of prohibition. Examine this carefully.
