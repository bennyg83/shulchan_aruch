All the garments, etc.; and it is not, etc. For the halachah does not follow one against two. So rule the Rif and the other poskim. In the Yerushalmi, brought by the Rosh there at the end of seif 5: if he changed garments throughout all seven days, he must tear. Rabbi Chiyya Rabbah and Rabbi Chama father of Rabbi Hoshaya both said: all of them are forbidden to be resewn. Bar Kappara said: only the first is forbidden. Rabbi Chanina said: another dispute lies between them. According to the one who says both are forbidden to be resewn, he does not make the other days an addition, and even if he has several garments upon him, he must tear them all. According to the one who says that only the first is forbidden to be resewn, he treats the other days as an addition. Since we rule that they may not be resewn, the same applies to all his garments. This is from Torat HaAdam, and this is why it says "as the first time," etc.
(Likkut) For all the dead, perhaps, etc. It does not sound that way in Evel Rabbati at the end of chapter 10, where it teaches: on Friday night at twilight he turns, etc.; it implies this in all cases, just as he uncovers his head.
