Only, etc. This is the Tur, following the Rif's text there: both a man and a woman, the words of Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, for Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar says, etc. The one who disagrees with Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar holds that a woman tears only the upper garment. So their dispute appears in the Yerushalmi at the end of Moed Katan and in Evel Rabbati chapter 9. Thus "both a woman" means that a woman too tears all her garments, according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, excluding the first Tanna in Evel Rabbati and the Yerushalmi, who says that she tears only the upper garment because of modesty. Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar says that there is a remedy for this: the woman tears, etc. Nevertheless, since the Gemara brings only Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar's opinion, it follows that the halachah is like him. This is unlike the Bach, who wrote that according to the Rif's text no one disagrees with Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar. Also, what he wrote, that even according to our text one should rule like Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar because in rabbinic matters the halachah follows the lenient view, is not correct; that applies only when the views are equal, not to an individual against the many. The Beit Yosef wrote that perhaps the Tur holds that Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar is explaining the first Tanna and not disagreeing. See Tosafot in Yevamot 83a, s.v. beriah, etc., that Rabbi Yose does not disagree, etc. This is the Tur's way in several places. Therefore the Tur rules below at the end of siman 364 and the beginning of siman 374 like Rabbi Yose regarding the spine and skull. But what the Beit Yosef further wrote, that even if Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar disagrees, the Tur ruled like him because his reasoning is persuasive, does not seem correct; we should not rule by our own reasoning like an individual view unless the Talmud ruled explicitly that way. The Mordechai, however, ruled like the first Tanna. See below s.k. 42.
