(Seif 15, gloss) Or. The simple meaning suggests that the days do not count at all and that a full thirty days must be counted. However, in my humble opinion, one who studies Responsa Maimoniyot, siman 17, will see that the days before the festival count toward the seven, and the festival itself, even Shavuot and Rosh Hashanah, counts as seven, after which he completes the balance to thirty. Thus one day before Rosh Hashanah together with Rosh Hashanah itself equal fourteen days; seven days before Yom Kippur together with Yom Kippur equal another fourteen, totaling twenty-eight. He may therefore mend the tear permanently two days after Yom Kippur. For a father or mother, he may loosely stitch it. The principal novelty is that a festival occurring after the seven days does not cancel the entire thirty-day period with respect to permanent mending. Much later I found that Tiferet LeMoshe writes similarly.
