After thirty. It is also permitted to tack the tear on the eve of a festival, specifically close to evening after they have prayed Minchah. This is Mahari Weil siman 7.
And there is no distinction, etc. Meaning: although a festival cancels the decree of seven and thirty, as below in siman 399, that is specifically regarding mourning. But regarding tearing and all matters of tearing, we require actual seven and thirty. So the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Raavyah, and it is in Teshuvot Maimoniyot. However, nowadays the custom is to fully sew the tear whenever the mourning has ended. It appears this is because the reason of Raavyah is that the Talmud says mourning is separate and tearing is separate, whereas we rule like the Ramban below in seif 30, that just as one does not mourn for a minor, so one does not tear; and what the Talmud says, "mourning is separate and tearing is separate," means only that the rule that the halachah follows the lenient view in mourning does not apply to tearing. But for all other matters there is no distinction, as the Ramban wrote there; the Beit Yosef brings him and writes that this is how we rule. Likewise below in siman 399, where the poskim write anonymously that the festival cancels the decree of seven and thirty, it implies that the entire decree belonging to seven and thirty is canceled in every respect. So it appears to me.
