One who hears the Divine Name being blasphemed. See Tiferet LeMoshe, who writes that it requires study whether one who saw a Sefer Torah burned on Shabbat, or heard blasphemy on Shabbat, must rend on Motzaei Shabbat. Mishnat Chachamim, Hilchot Avodah Zarah, prohibition 13, Tzafnat Paneach, letter 10, also leaves this unresolved.
The descriptive Name. Tiferet LeMoshe writes that this means even erasable Divine appellations, such as "Gracious" and "Merciful," and one must rend; see there. Mishnat Chachamim states likewise, Tzafnat Paneach, letter 18.
From a Jew. Mishnat Chachamim writes that although one who hears blasphemy directly from a non-Jew need not rend, if a Jew then comes and reports that he heard the non-Jew blaspheme, the listener must rend, since he is now hearing the blasphemous formulation from a Jew. The same applies to the original listener who repeats the blasphemy. Since he did not rend when he heard it from the non-Jew, he is now obligated to rend when he himself repeats it; see there.
One who hears it from the witnesses. See Shach, seif katan 55, who writes that although the witness does not actually pronounce the blasphemy verbatim, nevertheless the listener who merely hears someone say that so-and-so blasphemed the Name, without himself hearing the actual blasphemous words, is treated differently. Mishnat Chachamim brings the view of Rambam, Semag, and Tosafot, contrary to the Yerushalmi, that whenever one merely says that a person blasphemed but does not repeat the blasphemy in its actual wording, there is no obligation to rend. He wonders why the Shach did not mention their view at all. He concludes that they should be followed leniently in this rabbinic matter, since the mitzvah of keriah in this case is rabbinic. He further writes that the Yerushalmi's second law, distinguishing one who hears from a second person and one who hears from a third, follows its own premise that even if the reporter does not repeat the actual blasphemy, but merely states that so-and-so blasphemed, one must rend. According to the Rambam and Semag, whom we follow leniently, the entire distinction falls away. If the listener hears an actual repetition of the blasphemous wording, he must rend even if he hears it from a third person; see there.
How did so-and-so bless, meaning curse, the Name? Mishnat Chachamim writes that nowadays, when capital cases are not judged and the offender is only placed under a ban, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 425, witnesses to blasphemy should not be examined in the manner used when capital punishment was administered. Only in order to carry out the mitzvah of stoning did the witnesses explicitly pronounce the Divine Name and the blasphemous formulation. For purposes of excluding the offender from the community and placing him under a ban, the witnesses should not utter the Name of Hashem, much less the blasphemous words. If they testify that so-and-so blasphemed, he is distanced and placed under a ban. He therefore leaves the Shulchan Aruch's phrase "even one who hears from the witnesses" in difficulty, for why should witnesses pronounce the blasphemy in our times? According to the Shach, who says the witness does not actually repeat the blasphemy, the wording is resolved. But Mishnat Chachamim had already inferred from the phrase "from the witnesses," rather than "from one who heard," that the Shulchan Aruch follows Rambam and Semag; see there.
And the witnesses need not rend. Mishnat Chachamim writes that if one person came and said that so-and-so blasphemed, requiring the listener to rend, and later another person came and reported the same thing, the listener need not rend again, because he has already heard and rent his garment. This parallels the rule that the witnesses do not rend again because they already rent when they first heard it; there is no distinction in this regard.
A Sefer Torah that was burned. See Tiferet LeMoshe, who leaves unresolved whether one rends for volumes of Talmud, Midrash, and the like, since they were permitted to be written under the principle of "It is a time to act for Hashem," and for that same reason they may be rescued from a fire. See also above, siman 270, seif 2.
