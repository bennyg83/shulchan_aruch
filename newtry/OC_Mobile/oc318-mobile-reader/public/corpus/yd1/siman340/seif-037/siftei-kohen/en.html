And he heard it from Israel. According to the view of the Ramban and the Tur, that for the explicit Name one is obligated to rend even in the case of a non-Jew — and "the explicit Name" means whether in its written form or in its spoken form; see the Bach.
And some say, etc. And the Bach disagreed with this, based on his own explanation of the words of the Rambam and the Sefer Mitzvot Gadol — that even for a Jewish apostate one is obligated to rend. But his words are not conclusive; see there.
And even one who hears from the witnesses, etc. That is, such as one who heard from the witnesses that so-and-so blasphemed the Name — even though the witness does not actually utter the blasphemy from his own mouth, the one who hears it from the witness is obligated to rend, and he need not hear it from two witnesses — rather even from one of the witnesses. And the same applies when the person is not a witness who testified in court, but simply heard the actual blasphemy: anyone who hears from the one who actually heard the blasphemy — even though this person did not utter the actual blasphemy from his own mouth but only said "so-and-so blasphemed the Name" — the one who hears is obligated to rend. But one who hears from someone who says "so-and-so blasphemed the Name" and that person himself did not actually hear the blasphemy but heard it from one of the witnesses — that last one is not obligated to rend. All this is written in Sefer Tashbetz, where it is found in the Yerushalmi. End quote of the Bach.
