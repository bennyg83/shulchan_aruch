And he must hear it from Israel. For if we were to rend for non-Jews, all garments would be filled with tears.
He rends two rendings. One for the parchment and one for the text.
And specifically when they burn it by force. This implies specifically burning, but other forms of destruction do not require rending — and so ruled the Beit Yosef, for so it is implied in the Yerushalmi. But this is not implied in our Talmud, for we say there that the daughter of the Neomitin wanted to swallow the tefillin of Rabbi Abbahu, and he said: I am obligated in two rendings; they said to him: thus said Shmuel — they only said this regarding by force, and as the incident that occurred with Yehoyakim. And they did not say "they only said this regarding burning specifically." And in the Yerushalmi it is stated thus: they only said this regarding a Torah scroll that the king of Israel burned by force. And clearly "the king of Israel" is not meant literally, as the Beit Yosef himself wrote, and this is the implication of all the decisors; consequently, also the mention of burning is not meant literally, since it does not appear in our Talmud — and certainly burning is not meant specifically. And so ruled my father-in-law z"l.
