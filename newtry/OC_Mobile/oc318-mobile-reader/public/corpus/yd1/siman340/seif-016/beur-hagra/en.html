(Supplement) And if the son, etc. Ramban writes concerning the incident on 22a: what honor is there in diminishing his father's honor? R. Yehudah was a great person for whom it was not fitting to bare the shoulder. Others wished to honor him by doing so with him, lest he do it alone. He was concerned for their honor though not for his own, because the honor of Torah is greater. Kiddushin 33b leaves unresolved the case of a son who is also his father's teacher; all the more so where disgrace is involved. Eliyahu Rabbah chapter 9 likewise teaches that even for father or mother, if he is not fit to bare the shoulder while others are, he does not. When R. Akiva's father died, others bared the shoulder before him and he did not. End of supplement.
