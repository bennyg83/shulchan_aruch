A nasi is like a father for removing shoulder, tearing outside, and rending all garments; those tearing for a sage who died—when they turn from behind the bier they basten and mend the next day; a sage whose report came—they basten that day and mend the next; for nasi and prominent rabbi they basten the next day and never mend; for a sage one removes the right shoulder, for av beit din the left, for nasi both sides; for father, mother, and prominent rabbi he may choose either side and may remove both. (Some say nowadays they do not practice removing shoulder at all (Kol Bo in the name of R. Yehuda)—and so they practice now).
