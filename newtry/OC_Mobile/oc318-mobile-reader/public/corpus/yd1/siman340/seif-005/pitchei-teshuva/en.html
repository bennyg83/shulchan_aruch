Or a woman. See Baer Heitev. What it cites in the name of Radbaz concerning one who was present at the departure of a soul on Shabbat is in part II, siman 838. Tiferet LeMoshe writes that he must rend on Motzaei Shabbat. His proof is from Shabbat 105b; see there. That passage also indicates that one rends even for a minor girl, contrary to Radbaz; see there carefully. He adds, however, that this applies only so long as the deceased has not yet been buried. If he did not rend until after burial, he is exempt. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 323, who disagrees and writes that it is an explicit Talmudic principle that once one did not rend at the proper time, he no longer rends unless there is a new arousal of grief. This is evident from Moed Katan 25a concerning the death of Rav Safra: the Sages did not rend, thinking that what had passed had passed, and Abaye told them that regarding a sage, one may rend so long as they are engaged in his eulogy. It follows that for another person who was not a sage, once the moment has passed it has passed; since he did not rend at the departure of the soul, he should not rend later. See there. Radbaz further writes that one who was with the deceased must rend even if he turned his face away at the moment the soul departed, since he was capable of seeing. But one who could not see, even though he was in the same room as the deceased, is not obligated to rend; see there.
