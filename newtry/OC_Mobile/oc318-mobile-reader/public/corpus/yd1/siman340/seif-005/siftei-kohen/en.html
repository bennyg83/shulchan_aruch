But one who is accustomed to transgress. Even for appetite, one does not tear or mourn for him.
And some say that an apostate, etc., until: and so is primary. This implies that even for an apostate killed by non-Jews, the primary ruling is that one does not mourn for him. So wrote the Atteret Zahav: some say one does not mourn for them, and so is primary. This requires study, for in the Darkhei Moshe he brought only Hagahot Ashri and the Or Zarua, who wrote that one does mourn for him, and brought no dissenter. In my humble opinion, one cannot disagree with this. It seems to me that they derived it from the Talmud, chapter Nigmar HaDin, Sanhedrin 47a-b: an apostate killed by non-Jews, since he was not executed lawfully, his death is an atonement, and one mourns for him. See there. So too it appears from the words of the Maharam brought by the Rosh in chapter Elu Megalchin; see there.
One mourns for him. The Atteret Zahav wrote that this is because there is a presumption that he had thoughts of repentance. He was not precise, for if so, all the more so one executed by court. Rather, the reason is in Hagahot Ashri and Or Zarua there: since he was killed, it is an atonement, meaning as stated in the Talmud, that since he was killed unlawfully, it is an atonement.
