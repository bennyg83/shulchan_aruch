He completes his reading in the valid scroll, etc. The Bach relied on the Raavyah cited by the Mordechai in chapter 2 of Megillah, and argued at length that another scroll should not be taken out; rather, the erroneous word should be read orally. See there. His proofs, however, are not conclusive. He brought a proof from Yoma, where a new blessing is required before reading from a second Torah scroll. Although the Raavyah himself gives this proof, it is not decisive, for the other poskim may maintain that Rav Huna disagrees with Reish Lakish. Alternatively, that case is different because the reader had not intended to read that passage or from that Torah scroll. This also answers the Bach's other proofs. See there. His claim that the delay constitutes an interruption is likewise incorrect, because anything connected to the reading is not an interruption, as explained above in siman 19 and siman 265, seif 5. It is clear that the Mechaber and the Rama were aware of the Raavyah's words in the Mordechai, since they are cited by the Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe. Nevertheless, they did not accept his ruling, because the custom had already spread in accordance with Rabbi Yosef Bei Rav. The Atteres Zahav also agrees with the Mechaber. See there.
