Starting from a wrong place. Dadaslav was read by what he read in the book that he was wrong with a few opinions in the Bible:
And he recites a blessing after it. Mordechai in chapter 2 of Megillah wrote: when a Torah scroll is missing a letter or a word or a verse, one should read the missing section by heart and not bring another Torah scroll, because of the concern for an unnecessary blessing — for if a second Torah scroll is brought out, it requires a blessing before and after it. And he wrote that it appears so to him, for we find in the chapter of the Kohen Gadol (Yoma) regarding a public reading of Torah: [the Kohen Gadol] rolls the scroll at Yom Kippur and reads [the section in Vayikra] and rolls it back and reads the second section by heart [from Bamidbar] — and the reason is to honor the congregation, namely not to make them wait while rolling; and one might ask: why not bring another scroll — the Talmud answers: it would be a disgrace to the first; and a second answer: because of the blessing — for when one rolls from one section to another section in the same scroll, no additional blessing is needed; but if one brings another scroll, a blessing before and after is needed; therefore in our case too one should not bring another scroll. And Beit Yosef challenged on Mordechai's proof: [the Kohen Gadol] is different because the second section [in Bamidbar] is also in that same scroll, just distant from it, so rolling to it is a possibility; and from there we draw that when rolling is not possible, one does not bring another scroll — but this is difficult, for [the Talmud] gives two reasons, one being disgrace and the other an extra blessing; and neither of these reasons should apply where the portion is missing from the scroll entirely. And behold Beit Yosef wrote that the blessing is the main reason for not bringing another scroll — meaning, the concern for an extra blessing is so severe that even where rolling is not possible one does not bring another scroll; and so Shulchan Arukh ruled. And it seems to me [the Taz] that Beit Yosef correctly understood Mordechai: for the reason of disgrace to the first is plainly relevant only where rolling is possible, while the reason of an extra blessing applies also where the section is simply missing and cannot be obtained by rolling; and therefore Mordechai concluded on the basis of the blessing-concern that one does not bring another scroll. And Maharshal wrote that all this is said regarding a deficiency in the middle of the Torah reading portion; but if a letter is missing from the last verse of the reading — one brings another scroll and reads from it, for there is no concern for disgrace and no extra blessing is needed since the blessing said after the first Torah scroll covers the last verse of the reading.
Rather, if there is a difficulty here, etc. — and it is a difficulty: for in the Talmud at the beginning of chapter Kohen Gadol [in Yoma], it is asked why not roll to the other section [in the same scroll] — implying that rolling to another section within the same scroll would be permitted, even requiring a blessing after the part read until the roll and a blessing before the resumed reading; and yet the answer given is that one reads by heart so as not to burden the congregation. And it is further asked: why not bring another scroll — and two answers are given: disgrace and extra blessing. Now if the extra-blessing concern is what drives us not to bring another scroll even when rolling is impossible, then these two questions in the Talmud — "why not roll" and "why not bring another" — are not parallel: rolling would require an extra blessing and yet [seemingly] remains preferable to reading from memory; only bringing another scroll is rejected on account of the extra blessing. To resolve: the Talmud first asks why not roll — and the answer is not given as "extra blessing" but rather as "burden to the congregation"; meaning, rolling within the same scroll is permitted even with the gap in blessings, since the blessing over a scroll covers all that is read from it in one session. The second question, why not bring another scroll, brings in an entirely different consideration: a new scroll is a new reading unit requiring its own blessings — hence the extra blessing is the decisive objection. Therefore when rolling is altogether impossible because the portion is missing, "why not roll" does not apply, and the remaining objection is "why not bring another scroll" — and since that objection is the extra blessing, that same blessing-concern prevents bringing another scroll even for a missing portion. This is precisely Mordechai's reasoning and the Shulchan Arukh's ruling, and Taz's conclusion: for the earlier part of the reading the concern is especially acute — the earlier readers have already recited blessings over this scroll, and bringing a replacement would require additional blessings entirely outside the original framework; but for the final reader, whose reading terminates the Torah-reading session, the matter requires further investigation, as Maharshal analyzed.
