And he recites the concluding blessing, etc. The Mordechai writes that if a Torah scroll is missing letters, words, or a verse, the reader should not interrupt to bring another scroll. Rather, he should read the missing material orally. He derives this from the High Priest on Yom Kippur, who read one passage from the Torah scroll and another orally, rather than bringing a second scroll, because of concern for the honor of the first scroll and an unnecessary blessing. He limits this ruling to a case where the reader has not yet read three verses, or where the aliyah cannot properly be ended before the defect. If he has already read three verses and may end the aliyah, he should recite the concluding blessing and another scroll should be brought. The Beit Yosef rejects this practice and follows Mahari Bei Rav, whose ruling is adopted here. The Mordechai's concern for an unnecessary blessing is not compelling. The High Priest knew from the outset that he had two separate required readings from two scrolls, and therefore each would require its own blessing. When a defect is unexpectedly discovered, however, the second scroll merely completes the same mitzvah. The original blessing can therefore extend to it, just as a blessing may cover additional food or additional acts of slaughter that were contemplated from the outset. The second scroll does not create a new mitzvah but repairs the interrupted first reading. The Mordechai's proof from a partially damaged Megillah is likewise inconclusive, because the laws of a Megillah differ from those of a Torah scroll. Nevertheless, there is a separate concern for the congregation's honor. If the defect is found during the final aliyah, when no later reader will require another scroll, bringing and rolling another scroll causes unnecessary delay. In that case, the reader should follow the Mordechai, read the defective word or passage orally, and complete the required reading in the same scroll. The same applies if the defect is discovered during the maftir reading. During earlier aliyos, however, another scroll will need to be brought in any event, and the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch should be followed: another valid scroll is brought and the reader continues from the place of the defect without repeating the opening blessing. Rolling and exchanging the scroll is not considered an interruption, just as the Gemara did not treat rolling the High Priest's scroll as an interruption.
