Another. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit in the name of Meil Tzedakah, who disputes Tzemach Tzedek, siman 119, cited by Magen Avraham, siman 143, seif katan 6. If a Sefer Torah has “their open lands” written with a different suffix, Tzemach Tzedek rules that another scroll need not be taken out, while Meil Tzedakah rules that another must be taken out because the error is audible. In siman 94 he discusses a Sefer Torah in which “Go, and I shall take you to another place” was written with a final kaf and without the letter hei, and rules that another scroll need not be taken out. In siman 55 he discusses the verse “You shall not test Hashem your God,” where the singular “your God” was written, and explains how it may be corrected. See also above, siman 275, seif katan 6, regarding “All the days of Noach were,” where the plural form was written; another scroll need not be taken out and the reader should read the proper singular form. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 178, rules that if “Esav my brother is a hairy man” was written with an extra yod, another scroll must be taken out. He further rules that if in Parashat Shemot “for from the water I drew him” was written with the letters transposed, another need not be taken out. Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah, part 1, siman 51, discusses a Sefer Torah in which “and you shall become impure through them” was written with an additional alef. He considers both sides and leans toward not taking out another scroll. See also Responsa Even Shoham, siman 41. Responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah, siman 5, writes that the matter does not depend merely on an added vav or yod, but on whether the added letter changes the meaning. Thus, where alternate spellings of “lamb” do not change the meaning, or “ascend” is written in a form that can also denote ascent, another scroll is not taken out. Responsa Meir Netivim, siman 13, disputes this and rules that a masculine form written as feminine, or the reverse, is a genuine change of language requiring another scroll. Sheelat Yaavetz, part 2, siman 108, likewise challenges the lenient view. See further the cited responsum, simanim 4, 5, and 6. Magen Avraham, siman 143, seif katan 7, cites the Shelah that another scroll is taken out even for the alternate spellings of “lamb.” Responsa Besamim Rosh, siman 288, with the gloss there, challenges this and writes that even if one follows the stringent view, it applies only when the less primary spelling replaces the primary one, not in the reverse case. Responsa Adnei Paz, siman 13, states that when such an error is found in the seventh aliyah and another Sefer Torah need not be taken out, one should not recite “This is the Torah” during hagbahah.
