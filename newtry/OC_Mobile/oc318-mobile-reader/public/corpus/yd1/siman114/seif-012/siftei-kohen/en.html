The Rashba was careful about saffron, etc. And the world now practices leniency plainly with saffron, and those of high station who are careful are few. And it appears to say that the Rashba was careful only because he knew certainly that in all that land they sprinkled wine on it. But we who do not know certainly — if so we say it is impossible that there not be one of them who does not sprinkle wine on it, and he saves all of them. And in this manner the Mordechai and the Agudah, Pesachim, wrote that even though gentiles fix bread with wine lees, the bread is permitted, for it is impossible that one gentile not fix with beer lees, and we say in Kesubos that if there is there one hidden one she saves all the priestesses — end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it in siman 123, and so too Issur VeHeter end of klal 22. And even though in Darkei Moshe he did not rule so to be lenient and to say it is impossible that one not fix with proper [kosher substance], as is written in the hagahah in seif 10 — that is because the way of the gentile is to fix with prohibited [substance], therefore we do not rely to be lenient, and so it appears in Darkei Moshe explicitly. But where it is not known that their practice is thus, but we stand and suspect to be stringent lest their practice be to fix with prohibited [substance], as in the time of the Rashba — if so it is obvious that one can say it is impossible that one not fix with proper [kosher substance]. And one can say that the Rashba knew that they mixed much wine into it, as is written in the responsum of the Rashba and in the words of the Mechaber, and there were not sixty in it. But in our saffron it appears that presumptively there are sixty in it, for one does not taste any taste of wine in saffron. And also the concern that they mix dry meat threads into it is not in the responsum of the Rashba in the edition before us, and even in the responsum of the Rashba in manuscript that was in the hands of the Mechaber it appears the Rashba was not concerned except because he knew they mixed many threads into it such that they were not nullified in majority, and they were so expert in their craft that they mixed in a manner that was not recognizable — therefore forbidden. But in all the saffron in our lands, the matter is known and clear that there is a majority that is recognizable as saffron — if so, even if dry meat threads are found in a minority of a minority, it is nullified in majority; and since so, it is permitted even ab initio to put it in a dish, for certainly there are sixty in the dish, as above siman 109 regarding honey in dry [substance] that was nullified in majority — permitted to nullify it even ab initio according to all in sixty. And there are poskim who permit even without sixty — see there. And furthermore, nullifying a prohibition ab initio does not apply here, since it is a doubt whether there is any prohibition here, as above siman 84 seif 38 and siman 115 seif 28. And furthermore one can say the Rashba was careful only with saffron that is moist — if so, also the dry meat threads were not completely dry, and if so they impart taste. But in these lands where the saffron is dry as wood, if so even if a dry meat thread is found we pay no attention to it, as was explained above siman 87 end of seif 10 in the hagahah regarding the tripe skin that dried as wood in a matter that there is no moisture in it — permitted. And even though there is also sometimes moist and wet saffron — this is because it was placed in cold and moist places, and perhaps originally it was dry as wood. And furthermore one can say specifically the Rashba was careful because he knew certainly that they mixed dry meat threads into it in a manner that had concern of prohibition; but nowadays who can tell us that they mix into it in a manner that is forbidden — perhaps they do not mix at all, and we do not establish a prohibition. And furthermore, perhaps the Rashba knew that the sellers of saffron were not craftsmen; but nowadays it is known that the saffron that comes from the lands where it grows — great craftsmen make it, and a craftsman does not harm himself, as above seif 5. And furthermore, it is known that in this time there is much saffron that is examined and clear that there are no dry meat threads in it.
