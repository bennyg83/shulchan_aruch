A measure of chalita. Chalita is a type of fruit called in a foreign tongue, and they cut its crate with a knife, and it is forbidden because of the greasiness of the knife. Rashi.
Outside the value of yayin nesech etc. Meaning: they estimate how much is their practice to mix in such things, and they sell outside the value of that wine.
And if it is known that one gentile is in the city etc. In Issur VeHeter general rule 43 law 12, likewise in the name of Maharash: and if there is one gentile in the city etc., it is permitted to buy stam from all the gentiles in the city, for it is like one hiding place that saves all the priestesses — end of his words. And investigate, for that case of the hiding place is in chapter 2 of Ketuvot (daf 27), and there it is preferable to here, for there each of the priestesses has a double doubt — doubt whether she was in that hiding place, if you will say she was not, perhaps she was not raped — and this does not apply here, for what help is it that this gentile does not mix for the others whose practice is to mix? Behold the others are as though this gentile were not among them. And one may say that here too it deals with doubt where it is not known which gentile does not mix, for no hint is mentioned here that it deals with where it is fully known which gentile he is. And further it is difficult: how does Maharash learn from that case of the hiding place, for there it helps only b'dieved to permit the priestesses to their husbands, whereas here he permitted l'chatchila to buy from every gentile. And it appears to me that here it does not deal with doubt which gentile, rather he is known and the doubt is regarding the others; and the teaching of Maharash is in this manner: there, regarding the hiding place where you come to permit all the priestesses at once, it is as though you come to ask about one daughter at once, as Tosafot wrote there — automatically you have no stringency from the side that we can say about each one that she was in the hiding place, for you permit all of them at once; if so you permit certainly even one who was not in the hiding place. But one must say that nevertheless, since there is a side of stringency for one of them, it also helps the others — similar to what we find in siman 110: if one of them is lost, it is permitted to eat the others two by two, specifically for the reason that certainly among them there is one who is pure — therefore it also helps the others. So too here with those priestesses, since there is one among them who has a good presumption, it helps all of them; so too here with those gentiles — even though here there is no doubt regarding this gentile who certainly does not mix, nevertheless the matter is balanced like there: there there is the weakness of one daughter, and this corresponds to the stringency that it is not known who was in the hiding place — therefore here we say since there is one gentile with a presumption of kashrut, we say the others too have a presumption of kashrut. However there it was a Torah doubt, namely the prohibition of rape — therefore permitted only b'dieved; but here it is a rabbinic doubt as Rama wrote — permitted even l'chatchila. But there is a difficulty in what Rama wrote: "all those whose mixing is not known" — plural language — behold even if it is known there is one who certainly mixes, all are forbidden as he concludes afterward. Therefore it appears to my view the essence: if there is one house that certainly mixes wine into it, it does not help him even if there is one house that certainly does not mix — it is literally similar to two paths, for all rabbinic enactments are enacted like Torah enactments; rather we follow the majority as he wrote afterward. And furthermore, that case of two paths also deals with rabbinic impurity as explained in Taz HaArukh daf 112.
That each who separates etc. — until here it deals here when there are three types — one certainly mixes, two certainly do not mix, third they are stam — and it deals with one who buys from the stam; shall we follow the majority? And one may challenge: let us decree lest he take from the fixed source, as is stated siman 110 seif 6. And one should not say this case is different here, for even if he takes from the fixed source there is only a rabbinic prohibition, namely stam yeinam — for he said above, if he takes from the fixed source it is a rabbinic prohibition, for from the Torah one in two is nullified, but the rabbis decreed on something important that it should not be nullified. And it is possible that in stam yeinam, where the Sages were lenient, Rashba relied above that they did not decree lest he take from the fixed source.
