It is forbidden to buy from them a basket of chiltit, mashed tiryat, and chilak — meaning small kinds of fish mixed together — because non-kosher fish are mixed with them and he cannot separate them. And pickles into which they sometimes put wine — this statement is to forbid in eating, but for benefit it is permitted. And if it is known that they all put wine, they are forbidden even for benefit. {Rama: And he sells all of it except the value of yayin nesach in it (Beit Yosef and Orchot Chayim in the name of Rashba). And if it is known that one gentile in the city does not mix wine into it even though the practice of other gentiles is to mix wine into it, it is permitted to buy from all of them as long as it is not known with certainty that he mixed wine into it, for we rely leniently lest they did not mix wine into it, and likewise anything similar in rabbinic prohibitions (Mordechai and Tur klal 44). And if it is known that some gentiles certainly put wine in it and some certainly do not put wine in it, we follow the majority, for everything separated from the majority is [considered] separated; but it is forbidden to buy from them in their houses, for anything fixed is like half and half in estimation (private opinion).}
