And if the wine was dear etc. In Tur it is written "and if they brought it from a place where the wine..." and Beit Yosef challenged from the Gemara that case of the ship etc., that it implies if there is any place on the road where wine would be cheap, we certainly are concerned perhaps that route goes if there is not a hindrance like those twists and turns in the Gemara. And in Derisha he explained that Tur deals with doubt whether there is a place on the road that is cheap — for this we are not concerned; and in the Gemara it deals where there is certainly a cheap place on the road — therefore they needed to give a hindrance of twists and turns. And so I saw in Darkei Moshe. And you may ask: behold the Gemara challenges "perhaps he came by way of Tyre," which implies it is not certain that the way is specifically there — and nevertheless he challenges to be stringent from doubt. And furthermore how can Tur write a new law and not mention the Gemara's law. And it appears to my view that here it deals where one goes up from the water to dry land to sell morayis; and if so there is good reasoning that even in Tyre which is on the seashore, and it is possible that there too he goes to sell morayis and there is much wine — therefore there is concern if there are no twists and turns. Whereas here in the Tur's words it deals where there is no cheap wine from that place from which they bring it until here — therefore there is no concern perhaps he went aside to a place where there is cheap wine; why should we be concerned for this, since from the outset his intention was to bring it here — so it appears to me correct.
