But if he brought the beer to his house and drinks it there it is permitted etc. It appears specifically until he brings it home and drinks it there, but he should not drink at the shop entrance — and this is the language of Rambam chapter 17 from Ma'achalot Assurot, as Beit Yosef wrote; and in the Gemara (Avodah Zarah 31b) it states: Rav Papa — they take it out to him at the shop entrance and he drinks; Rav Achai — they bring it to his house and he drinks. And Beit Yosef wrote on the words of the aforementioned Rambam that he seized the words of Rav Achai and not Rav Papa, even though the latter was later and master of the Talmud more than Rav Achai — in order to distance from matters of gentiles; see there. It is found that the view of Rambam and Shulchan Aruch is that he ruled like him to be stringent like Rav Achai, and thus we hold, unlike Peri Chadash gloss 3 who ruled like Rav Papa and wrote "and unlike Shulchan Aruch." And so too Rabbi Mazmor LeDavid challenged the words of the aforementioned Peri Chadash and agreed to the ruling of Shulchan Aruch. And it appears that Rama too holds thus from his not disagreeing. And so too Lechem HaPanim seif 1, Chokhmat Adam general rule 66 gloss 14. Arukh HaShulchan gloss Yud-Dalet. Zivchei Tzedek gloss 3.
