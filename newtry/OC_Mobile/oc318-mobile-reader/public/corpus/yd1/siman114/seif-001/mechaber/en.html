The laws of beer and other beverages of a non-Jew; it contains 12 se'ifim. All beer of a non-Jew — whether beer of dates, figs, barley, grain, or honey — is forbidden because of [the decree against] wedding feasts; and it is forbidden only at the place of its sale. But if he brought the beer to his house and drinks it there, it is permitted, for the essence of the decree is lest he dine with him; and they forbade it only when he fixes himself to drink in the manner that a person fixes for drinking. But if he enters the house of a non-Jew and drinks in a casual, incidental manner, it is permitted. And similarly, one who lodges in the house of a non-Jew is considered like his house, and it is permitted to send [someone] in the city to buy beer from gentiles.
{Rama: And there are those who permit beer of honey and grain beer (Mordechai in the name of Ravyah and Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot and many of the later authorities and Agudah Pesachim and Issur VeHeter klal 43 seif 6 and Semak daf 113); and thus they practice to be lenient in these lands.}
