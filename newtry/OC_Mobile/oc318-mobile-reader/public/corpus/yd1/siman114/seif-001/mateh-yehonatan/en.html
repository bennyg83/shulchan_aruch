(siman 114 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) Or that honey is forbidden because of wedding feasts. And one should challenge: behold, this law originates in the Rosh as Beit Yosef wrote, and the Rosh holds that what the Gemara says — gentile beer — likewise other beverages are included in the decree; if so according to this view even pomegranate wine and apple wine would be included in the decree, and how did the Mechaber write below seif 3 that pomegranate wine and apple wine are permitted to drink anywhere? And it appears we forbid only what we find in the Gemara — beer — and it appears according to what is there on daf 31, nevertheless they forbade gentile beer: Rami bar Chama said because of wedding feasts, Ran said because of uncovered wine; and he asks: uncovered of what? And they answer: it is not necessary except in a place where water is common. And if so it is plain that in other liquids where there is no concern of uncovered wine it is permitted. If so, one could say that even though the Rosh holds that by "beer" he means likewise other beverages, nevertheless pomegranate wine and apple wine were not forbidden at all since there is no prohibition of uncovered wine in them at all; and even though we establish only for the reason of wedding feasts, nevertheless since the Gemara does not say there is a difference between them, therefore regarding the law they do not disagree except in the reason of the matter; and if so, since according to the one who says because of uncovered wine pomegranate wine and apple wine were not forbidden, likewise according to the one who says because of wedding feasts they were also not forbidden; and the reason is because it is something not common and they did not decree at all — investigate.
