And they are not forbidden because of bishul akum, for they are eaten as they are, raw; and not because of absorption from gentile vessels, for meat and fat spoil in oil and honey, as above siman 103 seif 4. Alternatively, stam vessels are not ben yomo, as below siman 122. And see in the responsa of the Rav siman 54 regarding the law of permitting olive oil.
And the same applies to their hot water. For there is no concern because of bishulei goyim, for they are eaten raw, as it is stated in Shabbos, chapter Bameh Tomin (folio 56a), and Rashi explained in Avodah Zarah (folio 38b) because they do not change from their created state by fire.
To their hot water. And regarding vessels of a gentile one need not be concerned because of absorption from gentile vessels, for fat spoils [the water] for drinking — end of his words, Ayin Tzofim. And according to this, even vessels of a gentile that are ben yomo are permitted, for behold fat spoils in water. And one might say: certainly fat improves in water, and this is something the senses testify to. And so it is in several good places among the poskim, and among them Tosafos and the Rosh end of Pesachim chapter Gimmel, who wrote there explicitly that according to Rabbeinu Efraim it works out with what the Torah commanded to purge vessels from the law of ben yomo — yet there are not sixty in water against them, and the water imparts flavor, etc. — end of his words; hence even if water was heated in a vessel of a gentile that is ben yomo it is forbidden, and so it is proven explicitly in the words of the Ran, first words of the passage "to forbid it in the field" — see there — and it is simple. Rather the reason here that their hot water is permitted is because stam vessels of gentiles are not ben yomo, as below siman 122 seif 6 — see there — and so too the Ran there and other poskim, and it is simple.
