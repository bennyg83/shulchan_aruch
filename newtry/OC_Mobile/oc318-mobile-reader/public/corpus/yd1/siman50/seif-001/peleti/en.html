And to clarify all this briefly: they said in the Gemara Yevamot chapter 3: and all of them where there was doubt of kiddushin — close to him, close to her; and likewise safek divorce — chalitzah and not yibbum; and similarly — why did it not teach regarding divorce "close to him," etc.; and Rabbah answers: because the end teaches "this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility for the marketplace" — and you come to forbid her from doubt of divorce; we establish her presumption and she is permitted, and we do not say to be stringent to require chalitzah: if you say chalitzah, yibbum; unlike kiddushin — "this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility for yibbum," and we come to forbid her from doubt of kiddushin — we are stringent to require chalitzah. And what do you care if you say chalitzah, yibbum — she is permitted to yibbum by law in any case. Tosafot challenged on "this woman," etc., regarding safek drusah — why are we concerned; behold the animal has a presumption of permissibility; and if a defect was born from life — here too a defect from life; and nevertheless we go within the presumption. And they answer: drusah is common; and likewise "a thorn sat in the esophagus" — see there; and so Tosafot Chullin 43b s.v. k'savar. And behold the Darkei Moshe wrote in siman 41: an incident in Krakow in the brain where there was water and it was not known if the brain surrounded the water or not, and the sages of Krakow forbade — since a defect from life, we do not say "she was slaughtered, she is permitted," as Tosafot wrote in Yevamot. And Maharam of Lublin wondered — he brings a fault upon himself, for in Tosafot it is mentioned in the explanation of that which "close to him, close to her" in yibbum — safek close to him, close to her proves we rely on presumption even though a defect was born from life. However Shach wrote: it may be said they do not argue on the essence of the reasoning, only they thought to be stringent to require chalitzah and we do not say "if you say chalitzah, yibbum" — but they do not argue on the essence of the law that there is no presumption. And Rav Peri Chadash proved that otherwise what does Tosafot challenge from safek drusah — perhaps this baraita was taught after the conclusion that there is no presumption, since a defect from life. And it appears for practical law Rama's words are compelled — for in the Gemara they said there: Abaye said "his neighbor will testify about him," the tanna taught in Kiddushin, and the same for divorce — it is difficult how the mishnah was left undifferentiated and taught in Kiddushin and leaned to learn for divorce — behold the subjects are not equal in kiddushin; one may well say they were stringent for chalitzah; and if there is concern she will do yibbum thereby the law stands that she is permitted to yibbum, unlike divorce — how can we be stringent for chalitzah lest she do yibbum and stumble in a Torah prohibition; and even though in truth we are not concerned for this — if she does chalitzah, yibbum — nevertheless this reasoning exists; and how did the mishnah rely on this to say this comes in kiddushin to learn from it for divorce — behold their matters differ; and with this if she does yibbum it is not a prohibition, and with this she stumbles in ervah — therefore it must be said for the conclusion there is no presumption and it deteriorated from life; and if so, divorce and kiddushin are equal and one may well learn this from that. And this is correct and straight. And what Peri Chadash argued — if so what does Tosafot challenge from safek drusah; for practical law it is clear there in Chullin: Rabbah said a blind animal has no inspection from outside but from inside — there is a difference for safek drusah; and Rashba wrote in his novellae in the name of Rashi: that which they mentioned safek drusah is incidentally to teach that we are concerned for safek drusah, and we learn that Rabbah holds we are concerned for safek drusah; and nevertheless for Rabbah Tosafot proved with complete proof that even with a defect from life we follow presumption — and Tosafot challenged well from Rabbah to Rava. And on this matter I resolved what Tosafot answer: drusah is common; and if so Tosafot hold presumption is presumption of the body that it is not treifah — to say it was not gored; if not common it does not help, since it is not common. If so, in Ketubot she says "I am certain" — I hold like Rabbenu Gershom that she is believed that she has presumption of the body and we remove money even though "I am certain" is not common, as explained in Rosh and Yerushalmi; and see laws of migo in Rosh brought in Shach Choshen Mishpat siman 82 — "I am certain" is not common, and nevertheless since she has presumption of the body she is believed, and there is no need for "certainly" and "perhaps" — on daf 35 it is proven from the Gemara and Tosafot that even in "perhaps" and "perhaps" we claim "I am certain" even though not common. And likewise Ketubot 77a the Gemara challenges: R' Meir concedes regarding blemishes fit to return to her father's house that the father must bring proof; and the Gemara challenges: and why — the money is in your hand; and it is difficult what is the challenge — perhaps the explanation is blemishes fit to be born in a short time while she is in her husband's house; and if so this is presumption of the body since it is not common to be afterward in her husband's house — and so it must be said: even though not common, since presumption assists we follow it. And at first glance it requires further study; but in truth my answer above suffices for practical law — Tosafot did not say definitively this explanation of not common and common; for in truth, since there is a defect from life, presumption goes away — only Tosafot wrote this for Rabbah, for according to Rabbah one must say we are concerned for safek drusah because it is common; and for us there is no difficulty from "I am certain" — it may be said they hold like the other version there that the reason is not because of their presumption but from the power of migo; and for practical law: these are Rama's words directed to the above; and Rashba also agreed as Shach wrote. And regarding a defect by human hands, etc., Be'er Heitev wrote well that every defect from life cannot be established on its presumption — it must be according to the conclusion that this in Yevamot does not stand, and one cannot challenge: if so Tosafot hold that with a defect from life we do not follow presumption — if so why did Tosafot toil in Chullin 11a s.v. atya to forbid cheeses where a treifah animal was found and we do not say establish on presumption of kashrus; and now that it became treifah it has no clear presumption — see there; and they did not write plainly in Be'er HaGolah "blow" and such as sircha that certainly we declare treifah in them that there was a defect from life — then there is no presumption; and all the more it challenges on Rama, Semak, and Or Zarua who hold that with a defect from life we do not say we follow presumption — why below siman 81 did they rule regarding cheeses even in a blow in the hoof that before three days the cheeses are permitted, for we follow presumption; and behold there is a defect from life. And one may say this is not difficult in safek yibbum or safek drusah — "you say establish on her presumption she was not divorced and not gored" — the defect that happened to her from life opposes and weakens the power of presumption; unlike presumption of the body to say establish on her presumption — presumption of the body that now deteriorated does not establish the defect in life and does not oppose them; for on this we judge whether the defect was born today or yesterday, and we say it was born today and yesterday there was no defect at all — and both are correct. And what can still be reconciled in Rama's words: for without this, in winter days I examined Tosafot's words that appear s.v. atya — they wrote: one may learn from this practical halacha that if he made cheeses from many animals and afterward one was slaughtered and found treifah — all are forbidden, and one may not say establish the cow on its presumption, etc., end of his words. And one must understand what Tosafot wanted from this — that he made from many animals and one was found treifah; in brief it should have said: if he made cheeses from an animal's milk and it was found treifah — and similarly Tosafot wrote at the beginning of Niddah, see there; and therefore it appears that certainly even according to Maharam of Lublin and his supporters who hold like Rabbah — where a defect was born from life, nevertheless we follow presumption rabbinically and certainly forbidden, and we do not establish presumption to be lenient — for Abaye and Rava hold she requires chalitzah both in kiddushin and divorce; and this is because they hold to be stringent as Beit Yosef wrote; and therefore those who are stringent — not specifically because of yibbum and chalitzah alone that is mentioned here, for the entire nature of chalitzah is only a lav — and what is this lav more than other lavin in the Torah; if so, just as they hold to be stringent on the lav of chalitzah and not rely on presumption, so too regarding all prohibitions of lavin one may be stringent thus without relying on presumption — and this is simple and correct, and as they said in two and two — a safek d'rabbanan; the rabbis were stringent without relying on presumption, and the same here; and if so, rabbinically we certainly do not rely on presumption. And do not challenge: if so what did Tosafot challenge from safek drusah — establish her presumption even though a defect was born from life — perhaps rabbinically they are forbidden; one may say on 53b we learn that Shmuel forbids safek drusah from this safek drusah that came before Shmuel and he threw it in the river; and the Gemara challenges: but this is two safekot — safek treifah safek kosher; and you will say treifah — lest it not come to a stumbling block; and they answer: the first safek is from the Torah forbidden; and if so it is proven from the Torah it is forbidden, and they challenged well; and they answer: they are common; and if so it appears clear to all that with a defect from life we do not follow presumption rabbinically and it is forbidden — and they are correct for practical law in Rama's words; and this is correct.
