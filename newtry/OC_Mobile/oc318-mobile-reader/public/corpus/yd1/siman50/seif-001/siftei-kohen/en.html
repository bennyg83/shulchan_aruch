Every place, etc. — Thus wrote Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 41: an incident came before us in Krakow in the head of an animal where there was water, and before they knew the defect they cut the skull in two and part of the brain from the skull, and it was not known whether the brain surrounded the water or not, and we forbade it; and some wanted to permit from that which is taught: 'she was slaughtered, she is permitted' until you know what made her treifah — and this is not comparable, since here there is a clear defect from life, one may not permit for this reason; and so is implied in Tosafot chapter Four Brothers (Yevamot daf 31), end of his words. And according to these words are his words in the hagahah here, and Maharam of Lublin in his responsum siman 66 challenged the Rav's words and said: on the contrary, Tosafot there rejected this distinction and Tosafot's conclusion is that even where the defect was done from life, nevertheless one may establish it in a presumption of permissibility, as we say: 'this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility for the marketplace — do not forbid her from doubt,' and that which we are concerned for safek drusah and similarly above siman 33, a thorn sat in the esophagus — we are concerned lest it healed — this is because it is common. And that which Rashba wrote in his responsum siman 283 and 300, that when a needle was found stuck and there was no time to check whether it perforated from both sides — treifah — this too is because it is common; and likewise that which we practice to forbid water in the brain is also because it is common — see there where he elaborated in explaining these matters and in resolving Rashba's words, and wrote: 'I was forced to explain Rashba's words thus so that Tosafot's difficulty from above in chapter Four Brothers should not challenge his words,' end of his words in brief. And in truth one who examines Tosafot there will see as Maharam said; but it appears to me that the Rav's view is that Tosafot wrote thus for Rabbah who said in the Gemara there 'this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility,' etc.; but Abaye challenged Rabbah there from that which a house fell on him, etc. — why not say 'this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility,' etc.; and Abaye and Rava conclude that we do not establish 'this woman' in her presumption — if so, Tosafot who compare this law to 'she was slaughtered, she is permitted' prove that where we do not establish 'this woman' in a presumption of permissibility, likewise we do not say 'she was slaughtered, she is permitted' where a defect was born from life; and even though it is possible to say that for the conclusion too we say 'this woman stands in a presumption of permissibility,' only that for stringency we say she requires chalitzah, as Beit Yosef wrote there — and also in Tosafot's words it is somewhat strained to say they did not write thus for the conclusion; nevertheless, since it is not compelled and there is room here and there, and also the simple meaning of the Gemara there implies that Abaye argues on the essence of the law — if so, from where do we learn permissibility; perhaps the conclusion stands that we do not establish 'this woman' in her presumption — so it appears to me according to the Rav. And this that he wrote 'and so is implied in Tosafot chapter Four Brothers' means that they wrote there initially this distinction, and even though they rejected it there afterward — that was for Rabbah; but for Abaye and Rava, whom we rule like, that distinction may be true and stand. And for practical law it requires further study; for that which Maharam strained in Rashba's words is certainly not so — and it escaped him (and also the Rav) Rashba's words in his novellae on the first chapter of Chullin, who wrote: 'and where the bird's wing slipped off,' etc. '(and safek before slaughter we go stringently)' until we hang on a wolf — so wrote our Rabbi, may his memory be blessed, end of Rashba; and so wrote Ran there — all these matters; and he wrote that so explained Rav R' Yonah, may his memory be blessed; and that which Rashba said 'this entire safek in slaughter is invalid' — the same applies to all other treifot — namely, certainly every Torah safek is stringently; only that in the matter of 'every' it expanded safek in slaughter that we establish on a presumption of prohibition even where it is not common — and requires further study. And so is proven from Semak end of siman 200, daf 93b, who wrote: 'and every safek treifah that comes before a sage and is not clarified — such as the bone broke and emerged outside, or safek sircha, or any other safek where he does not know if they are before slaughter or after slaughter, and likewise buka d'atma where he scraped from its place and does not know if its nivin were eaten or not — in all of them Hagahot Gedolot ruled to prohibit and we do not say "she was slaughtered, she is permitted" as we say regarding a wolf that came and took the intestinal children,' etc. 'there there are some legs to the matter that it was done after slaughter; but where there are no legs to the matter at all we do not hang,' end of his words; and so Or Zarua general rule 50 din 5: 'if there is doubt whether its nivin were eaten or it was found after slaughter — safek in the meeting of sinews and all such cases — Hagahot Gedolot and Semak wrote that one must be stringent in its doubt and we do not say "she was slaughtered, she is permitted" except where there is something to hang on visible permissibility, such as a wolf that took the intestinal children before us and returned them perforated,' end of Or Zarua. Thus, wherever there is nothing to hang on neither for permissibility nor for prohibition — as in safek whether its nivin were eaten, and meeting of sinews, and such — we hang stringently and one may not conclude from Maharam of Lublin's responsum that from Semak and Or Zarua it implies we do not hang stringently except where the prohibition is common, per Tosafot's view above — and this is certainly not so. But from Semak and Semag siman 63 daf 143b and other poskim who wrote regarding a broken bone and he does not know if from life or after death, or dislocated thigh and he does not know if its nivin were eaten — that one must forbid — there is no comparison to the wolf, for there there are legs to the matter, unlike here where the doubts depend on lack of expertise. And so Maharai in Hagahot Sha'ar HaDin end of chapter 7 explained: wherever there is something to hang on for permissibility and prohibition, we hang leniently because of 'she was slaughtered stands in a presumption of permissibility until it is known,' etc., per Tosafot chapter Four Brothers; and all those poskim are fit to rely upon in great loss; and similarly. But without great loss one should rule like R' Yonah, Rashba, and Ran above — that where prohibition and permissibility are equal we hang stringently even where the defect was born only after slaughter; and even though it seems that what the poskim meant by 'safek of lack of expertise' is because there is nothing to hang on permissibility more than prohibition — and so is explained in responsa Masat Binyamin siman 50 daf 87b; and so in Or Zarua — nevertheless the simple meaning of the poskim does not imply thus, and likewise in Maharai's words there it implies that the main distinction is because it is lack of expertise — see there and requires further study. And likewise even where a defect was born from life, there is room to hang leniently in great loss where the prohibition is not more common than permissibility, per Maharam's responsum — for so appears the simple meaning of Tosafot Pesachim (daf 43a) s.v. k'savar, etc., and so is written in Pesakim Tosafot there: 'a safek was born from life and she is still in a presumption of prohibition — nevertheless "she was slaughtered, she is permitted,"' end of his words.
