His terumot. The Gemara explains the reason: since terumah is fit only for kohanim, and he comes to forbid it upon them, it is worth like ordinary dust and others acquire from hefker; and the Ran wrote that likewise one who forbade his property upon himself — others can take them by force from this reason; and Maharshal concludes that this does not apply to terumah here, for others cannot take them from him by force, rather he can give them to charity or other mitzvot; and furthermore even if they took them, we remove from their hands; nevertheless if they already ate they are exempt from paying, but if he was asked and something remained of that thing that was forbidden — for otherwise hatarah does not apply — they are obligated to pay what they ate — end quote.
Poor. Such as leket, shikhechah, and peah; likewise regarding charity that the court sends and takes collateral by force and gives to the poor, since we hold they compel for charity — so wrote the Beit Yosef.
