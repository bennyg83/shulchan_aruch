It is forbidden to buy from them or sell to them. This applies at all times and in every circumstance, even at equal value.
If he forbade his benefit to them, he may buy from them, etc. But a transaction at equal value is forbidden in every circumstance.
A readily marketable item. This is merchandise that merchants are eager to buy.
He may buy it from them for more than its value. Specifically for more, but not at equal value, and certainly not for less. Similarly, if he forbade them to himself, he may sell to them for more than its value, but not at equal value or for less. Although we rule that a person cannot forbid to another something that has not yet come into existence, this applies when he expressly forbids a future object, such as the fruit of a palm tree or property he will later acquire, or when he says, 'my property is forbidden to you,' which includes only property he owned at that time. When he forbids his benefit to another person, however, the prohibition depends upon him, and he already exists. If he later sells newly acquired property to those barred by the vow, he thereby benefits them, and it is forbidden. This is from the Ran, cited briefly by the Bach. See also the end of siman 204, seif katan 9.
If he forbade them to himself, etc. If he forbade them to himself and also forbade himself to them, all dealings with them are forbidden under every circumstance, and he must transact with members of other nations. This is stated in the Mishnah in Perek Ein Bein HaMudar, in the Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Nedarim, and in the poskim. The Rambam adds that he may neither conduct business with them nor allow them to conduct business with him; he may neither borrow an object from them nor lend one to them; and he may neither borrow money from them nor lend money to them. This is straightforward.
An item that lies before him. This is merchandise that merchants are not eager to buy, as though it lies upon the seller's face as a burden. All this applies where he forbade benefit. If he forbade their property to himself, however, even if he buys an item worth one hundred for two hundred, he may not benefit from the purchase, because his vow has already taken effect upon their property. This is from the Ran, cited by the Bach. Likewise, if he forbade his property to them, even if he sells them an item worth two hundred for one hundred, it is forbidden. Nevertheless, one who forbids his property can bind others only regarding property he already owned, as stated in seif katan 4 in the name of the Ran.
