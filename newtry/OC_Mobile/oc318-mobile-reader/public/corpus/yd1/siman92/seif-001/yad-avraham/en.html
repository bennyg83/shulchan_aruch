(Siman 92 seif 2 in the hagahah) A drop of milk that fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy, etc. If he did not stir at all, only that piece is forbidden — so Beit Yosef in name of Raavad, and so is proven explicitly in Tosafot and Rosh in ch. Gid HaNasheh (daf 100) regarding that which he said: a piece of nevelah and of unclean fish do not forbid until they impart taste in the gravy, etc. — and they wrote it deals when the permitted piece is placed entirely outside the gravy and on it is placed the nevelah piece, and only that piece became forbidden; and afterward when he stirred the pot, the whole became forbidden — end of his words. It is proven explicitly that without stirring only that piece was forbidden. And in Darkei Moshe he is uncertain whether this is specifically for R' Yehuda, which deals when the piece is entirely outside the gravy; but for Rashi, which deals when the piece on which the drop fell is partly in the gravy — other pieces are forbidden even without stirring at all. And he inferred from Tur's words in this siman a weak inference that the same applies to Rashi — without stirring at all other pieces in the pot are not forbidden. And Taz wrote explicitly that for Rashi, which deals when part is in the gravy, other pieces are forbidden even without stirring. And it was hidden from them Rashba's words in Torat HaKahanim and Taz siman 105 seif 4, and it is explained from their words explicitly that the same applies to Rashi who wrote, and these are his words: if issur fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy and he did not stir the pot, it only forbids kedei netilah; but if it is in the gravy — for Rashi entirely, and for R' Yehuda even part of it — the gravy steams the taste and it enters throughout — end of his words. Behold it is explained that for Rashi, if part of it — the gravy does not steam without stirring; and this is even if it is in the gravy, meaning even from that netilah that was forbidden — only its upper face is exposed, as Beit Yosef wrote — investigate well. And see Rashba brought in Beit Yosef siman 66. And so it appears to me proven in Rashi's explanation in ch. Kol HaBasar (daf 108 end of side 2) who explained on that which is said in the Gemara there: if he did not stir at all and did not cover at all — absorbs absorbs, discharges does not discharge. Rashi: the milk is absorbed in the piece but is not discharged from it to forbid other pieces without stirring — for the milk is on the roof of the piece — end of his words. It implies it deals when the piece is inside the gravy, only that its roof, meaning its upper face is exposed, where the drop fell and where it stands, and it does not spread from it outward without stirring. And so Hagahot Maimoniyot, brought by Beit Yosef, and these are his words: from Rashi's explanation it appears, etc., and as Beit Yosef explained his words — see there. And it appears to me that from these words of Rashi he derived thus: the same applies regarding combination of other pieces to nullify the drop — they do not combine even if it is in the gravy if its upper face is exposed; for all the more so if it does not forbid other pieces, it does not combine to nullify. And see Shach s.k. 6 and s.k. 13.
