And it is hard how he ruled and said forbidden — it must be noten ta'am, for if a gentile tastes and there is no taste it is permitted; therefore that which in the boiling cauldron what is absorbed is not discharged means completely that taste was discharged, but certainly some taste discharges until weak taste remains that kafeilah taste does not sense; and also what discharged is not full taste only weak taste, for for Rav his boiling cannot discharge full taste — therefore the milk is entirely permitted for it is not discharged; and weak taste does not forbid its mixture; and we require full taste to forbid mixture, not weak taste; but the meat since at first it absorbed full taste, although it returned and discharged and the taste became weak — behold it was already forbidden; or one may say, and more correct, there is doubt perhaps it discharged weak taste, and since chatichah na'asah nevelah even weak taste forbids as it is forbidden and one cannot rely on tasting — therefore that piece is forbidden, perhaps weak taste remained and cannot be tasted; but the cauldron is permitted since he preceded and removed; and if it discharged weak taste — what of it, it is not called min b'mino since its taste is weak from milk taste in the cauldron — then it is exceptional the second time, min in not its species, and is nullified; but Rambam for us, when he preceded and removed and does not require sixty — one cannot say it discharged weak taste, for all is rabbinic min b'mino and we are lenient in doubt — requires study.
