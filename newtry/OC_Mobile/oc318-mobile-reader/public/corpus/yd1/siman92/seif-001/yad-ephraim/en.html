If it did not break, it is like iruy. Responsum Teshuvat Chen siman 11: in iruy where the stream did not break, forbidden by law; one needs sixty against the kelipah. If the stream broke, one may rely to permit. And so in Chemdat Moshe in the name of Orach Meishor; and Beit Yosef wrote that without cooking it is rabbinic, and in other issurim one should be stringent when there is no great loss; and a pot that absorbed through iruy where the stream broke may be permitted after it was kli rishon: and so in responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 37: a milchig knife stuck into a meat pot by the fire into a meat piece; they lifted the piece to another pot; there was sixty in the first pot against the knife, but not against the meat piece in the second — they permitted the first pot even though it seems to depend on Rashi and R' Yehuda, etc. — see there.
