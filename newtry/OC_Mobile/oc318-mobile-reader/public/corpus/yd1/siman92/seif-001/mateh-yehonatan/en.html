(Siman 92 seif 1) A kezayit of meat, etc. From that piece is forbidden — that which the Mechaber ruled without specification that that piece is forbidden implies even if kafeilah tastes it and says there is no milk taste, nevertheless it is forbidden — and why? And one may answer: we are concerned lest at first there was milk taste in it and that piece was already forbidden, and afterward it discharged from it so that now there is no taste at all — nevertheless, since that piece was already forbidden, it does not return to permission, for this deals when he preceded and removed before it discharges. If so, necessarily there was no taste in it, for if so where did the taste in it go — it does not discharge anything before it rests from its boiling. And one cannot say that it does not discharge full taste but nevertheless discharges weak taste. If so, one may answer: perhaps at first there was full taste and now full taste discharged from it and weak taste remained in it, and therefore its taste is not sensed. If so, it is difficult per our words regarding that which in ch. 25 daf 108 side 1 Rav — a kezayit of meat fell into a cauldron of milk, etc., and challenges: it is nevelah milk, and answers when he preceded and removed — absorbs but does not discharge. And per our words it is still difficult: since there was milk inside the meat, if so even if it only discharges weak taste, why does it not forbid the milk in the cauldron? And one must say: since there is only weak taste, again there is no importance of its species here and it is only like not its species and nullified in sixty. And one may answer: nevertheless it is still difficult regarding that which he wrote that if he removed before it rests from boiling we rely on kafeilah's tasting, and it is impossible that weak taste discharges even before it rests from boiling — why is sixty not required; let us be concerned lest weak taste discharged from it, and on weak taste kafeilah does not sense through tasting. And further, both are milk and one cannot recognize the taste. And one must say: certainly only a slight weak taste discharges from it. And that piece is forbidden is because we are concerned lest that piece received milk taste at first — namely congealed such that when a slight taste is removed from it again its taste is not sensed. If so, we are concerned here lest this slight weak thing discharged from it, and for this reason its taste is not sensed — and if so that piece is properly forbidden. Although Rashi explains on the aforementioned daf side 1 that Rav's statement "since noten ta'am in the piece chatichah na'asah nevelah" refers to the mishnah, and the mishnah deals when part of the piece was in the gravy and part outside the gravy — even though he did not stir and cover at the end, nevertheless we forbid the whole piece, and this is because even without stirring and covering, since part is in the gravy, afterward without stirring and covering it discharges from it. And that they do not combine immediately with the gravy is from this piece, since it is not entirely in the gravy but absorbed in that whole piece before it came to the gravy — but afterward it discharged from it to the gravy and forbids the other pieces. And it is clear from the plain meaning that a drop of milk that fell — if there is noten ta'am — forbids even without stirring at all — in the latter part it teaches stirring and covering, implying in the first part it forbids without stirring and covering; and so it appears Taz explains there — and it appears that if there is not in it, permitted even that piece alone, and why be concerned lest there was taste in that piece and now discharged outside as above. Granted if we explain as Shach wrote that if part in gravy and part outside it does not discharge at all to the gravy except through stirring and covering — if so necessarily the mishnah of the milk drop deals that he stirred and covered at the end — if so one may permit the piece's taste before stirring and there was no taste in it. Not so per Taz's explanation of Rashi that the mishnah deals without stirring and covering, and there is no place to forbid the piece's taste — if so it is difficult, etc. And one must say Rashi follows his view that we do not rely on kafeilah except where there is sixty. If so that which implies in the mishnah "if there is noten ta'am" permitted — it follows there is not sixty in that piece either. If so we say: certainly if kafeilah tasted and said there is no taste, since we already hold there is sixty in it, stam there was never any taste at all and it is permitted even that piece, and we do not say in it that there was taste and afterward discharged. And if so for us who hold by law we rely on kafeilah even when there is no sixty — we do not need Rashi's explanation, for otherwise it would be difficult: if there is no taste in it, why should that piece be permitted? If so one should wonder somewhat at Maharshal and his supporters who ruled like Rashi, since they hold kafeilah's tasting without sixty — it was not for them to rule like him, for otherwise the difficulty as stated applies.
(There) But if he did not manage [in time], etc. — forbidden unless there is sixty in it. One may say accordingly on that which is taught in Chullin 94: a pot in which he cooked meat — he may not cook milk in it; and if he cooked and there is noten ta'am, and we say: who tastes — kafeilah tastes for him. And per what the Mechaber wrote, why do we rely on kafeilah? Let us say milk was absorbed in the pot and noten ta'am and returned and discharged it and forbids the rest of the milk, and it is min b'mino with the rest of the milk and would require sixty as here. And one may say: behold Ran explains that which challenges on that which Rav said: a kezayit of meat fell into a cauldron of milk, etc. — it is nevelah milk; and Ran asks: why noten ta'am? For since this milk that was absorbed is still connected to this milk that is outside it, which is sixty against the taste of meat — the taste of meat that this absorbed milk received was nullified. And one may say: since it was absorbed inside the meat it is immediately separated from the rest of the milk and stands alone, and properly received meat taste and noten ta'am. And behold per what we hold: if issur was absorbed in food, the issur cannot be squeezed out, for the issur cannot discharge from it; but if issur was absorbed in a vessel we say hag'alah helps and the issur can properly separate from it — and this is the reason: in absorption in food the issur clings to it and adheres more and is absorbed literally in the substance until it cannot give space between the adhesions forever; not so in absorption in a vessel, which does not cling and adhere so much with the vessel, and therefore in hag'alah it can properly separate from it. Accordingly one may say that which we say: if something was absorbed into something else it is considered as separated from what is outside it — this is if absorbed into the food as above, and it is all one body, and we properly say it is as separating and noten ta'am; not so what clings in a vessel, which does not cling so much with the vessel — we say it is still connected to the food inside the vessel, and we do not say that since it was absorbed it is noten ta'am. Accordingly it works out [and so too Peri Megadim at the beginning of siman 94 in Sha'ar HaTevunah this distinction in name of Mordechai]: for here, which deals with absorption inside the vessel, we say noten ta'am and properly requires sixty; not so there regarding a pot in which he cooked, etc. — for since it was absorbed in the vessel we do not say it should be as separated from meat and be noten ta'am and need no sixty at all to nullify it. But still difficult is that which the Mechaber wrote above at the end of seif 4: if he placed [milk] in the hide of the stomach — if there is noten ta'am in it, it is forbidden; it implies if there is no noten ta'am it is permitted even when there is no sixty. Let us say the milk absorbed in the stomach hide is noten ta'am and returned and discharged and would need sixty to nullify — there too it was absorbed in food. And one must say: since it is only forbidden through pickling, and from the Torah it is not forbidden at all in basar b'chalav except through cooking — the Mechaber holds we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, because he holds like Rabbenu Efraim that we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah except in basar b'chalav — and this is because since this alone is permitted, etc., only by their joining together it became forbidden — it is properly as one body of nevelah; and all this if forbidden through cooking, which is as a body of issur; not so through pickling and salting — even though they forbade eating, nevertheless we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah that the whole is considered one body, only it is considered like other issurim. [Hagahah: and so too in the Gra's chiddushim at the end of seif 7, that for Taz in siman 90 s.k. 4 that the Mechaber holds in basar b'chalav that is d'rabbanan we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — it works out.]
