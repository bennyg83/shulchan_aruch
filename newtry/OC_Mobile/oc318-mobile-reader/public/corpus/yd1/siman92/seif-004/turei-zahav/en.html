Except in basar b'chalav. The reason: each is permitted by itself, and when together they are forbidden — therefore the meat becomes issur, and one is lashed if he eats half a kezayit of meat and half a kezayit of milk; therefore sixty is required in the gravy to nullify the whole piece on which a drop of milk fell — so Tosafot, and explanation of their words: since in basar b'chalav there is no recognizable issur except after mixture, therefore the whole mixture is the substance of the issur that is the prohibition stated in Torah "mixing meat in milk"; not so in another thing that is issur by itself and that is the Torah prohibition, only here it mixed incidentally — you have only a Torah doubt, and chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply — so Maggid Mishneh.
And then even the piece itself, etc. It appears thus per what Tur wrote that we do not say "possible to squeeze out" forbidden in other issurim — automatically "possible to squeeze out" permitted and the piece itself is permitted; and see below siman 107 seif 1 he wrote the opposite, and that is the truth.
And some say we say in all issurim, etc. And this is rabbinic — therefore one may lean to permission in a place I will mention below in name of Maharshal; and it is explained in Orach Chaim general rule 24 that even in rabbinic issur we say chatichah na'asah nevelah — and this is like Rashba that I mentioned in siman 90 seif 1.
If the issur is attached, etc. In Orach Chaim he explains the reason in name of Semak: since it is attached to this issur, and likewise a worm in its body, and likewise a kosher fish found in its innards after cooking with a sheretz or non-kosher fish and there was not sixty in the fish itself against it — in all such cases we fear perhaps once it remained alone in the gravy at the end of pouring, or perhaps once they took it out boiling outside the pot and it became nevelah and after returning it was not permitted and forbids the rest until sixty against it. From this it appears in a fish where a sheretz was found or a piece of sheretz in its head and cooked thus with other fish one needs sixty against the whole head of the fish, for one law and one reason with what is found in its innards; and further proof from Orach Chaim general rule 36 regarding an apple with a worm — attached issur requires sixty in cooking against the whole apple if the apple itself does not have sixty against the worm; and so I saw in Maharil, etc. — peel the place for its taste is nullified in sixty even in cooking if there is sixty in the piece where it was found; and so he ruled on a piece of carp found in the head of a cooked fish to nullify its taste if there is sixty in the head of the fish against it. However per the reasoning it does not seem attached, for it rests where gravy can come from all sides; and since chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim is only rabbinic as I wrote, one may lean to be lenient here too and it is not chatichah na'asah nevelah — see siman 72 seif 3 what Taz and Shach wrote there.
Outside the gravy and issur fell on it. Meaning while still boiling the issur fell; but if first cold milk fell and afterward fell into a meat dish — it is obvious the meat itself with the rest of the dish nullifies the milk, since it was not forbidden until it came to the dish, and then the prohibition begins and nullification also begins in everything in the pot — so Orach Chaim general rule 28; and he wrote further that even if he raises that piece alone outside while boiling it is not forbidden and is not like attached issur, etc.
But if part of the piece is in the gravy, etc. This is R' Yehuda's view that we mentioned in seif 1 regarding a drop of milk on a meat piece — only Rama ruled here like his words in other issurim; and I already wrote this contradicts what Rama himself wrote in seif 2; and we hold like Rashi that even if part is in the gravy it is as wholly outside the gravy, stringently; nevertheless here in other issurim it is reasonable to rely on Rama that it does not become nevelah, per R' Yehuda's view.
If wet issur mixed with wet heter, etc. The reason in Beit Yosef in name of Mordechai: specifically in the piece itself from importance, for the issur pieces are recognizable and distinct — chatichah na'asah nevelah; but in wet with wet such as wine in wine or forbidden water in water, the issur is not recognizable and distinct and there is no issur importance — the second heter mixes with the first — end of his words; and Maharshal wrote ch. 3 siman 60: nevertheless it appears to me to distinguish that this is specifically when mixed not in the manner of cooking; but if it is the manner of cooking through imparting taste — issur mixed there and afterward fell from the gravy into permitted food — one needs sixty against all the gravy that fell, not to consider how much of the issur was absorbed in proportion in this gravy; but wet with wet such as wine in wine or water in forbidden water we can say we do not say all the permitted became nevelah, especially since it is rabbinic, and if more permitted was added afterward it is permitted — so it appears from his words that even not in case of loss we do not say in wet that became nevelah contrary to Rama here.
And if dry mixed with dry. Meaning not in the manner of cooking together — then its law is the issur is nullified in majority; therefore if first mixed not in majority one need not afterward majority against the whole first mixture; but if they cooked together certainly we say chatichah na'asah nevelah and need sixty in permitted against the whole piece. Tur wrote for R' Tam's view that even in other issurim "possible to squeeze it out" forbids and chatichah na'asah nevelah specifically in noten ta'am when there was not sixty in permitted to nullify the issur absorbed in it, but if forbidden in mashahu, even if forbidden due to its species forbidden in mashahu for R' Yehuda, the whole does not become nevelah — end of his words; and the source of this law is from what is stated in ch. Gid HaNasheh (daf 100) that Rav expounded: once taste entered a piece, chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids all the pieces because they are its species; and Abaye establishes it there that a nevelah piece was first cooked with one permitted piece and received taste from the nevelah, and afterward the nevelah was removed and many permitted pieces remained with the piece that was first forbidden — we say the piece that was forbidden became chatichah na'asah nevelah and forbids all the permitted pieces even if there is sixty against it, for we hold like R' Yehuda that min b'mino in mashahu; and Tosafot challenged: why do I need that the forbidden piece was forbidden from the nevelah giving taste in it — behold even in mashahu it can forbid it because it is its species, as it forbids another afterward in mashahu; and R' Tam answered: granted it itself becomes forbidden in any amount, nevertheless we do not say it becomes nevelah to forbid all the others, since it itself was only forbidden in any amount — end of his words; explained in this that although for R' Yehuda there is min b'mino in mashahu, nevertheless this is specifically when it was first forbidden in mashahu; but if afterward it cooked in its species it has no power to forbid if there is sixty against the piece that was forbidden, and we do not forbid from min b'mino in mashahu since the substance of issur is not here, only that it absorbed from issur in mashahu — therefore the other permitted pieces were permitted; but if the piece was first forbidden from much issur that gave taste in it, then it forbids in mashahu its species and sixty does not help against it. And since we say this reasoning in mashahu in min b'mino for R' Yehuda from Torah, as stated at the beginning of ch. Kisuy HaDam from the verse of blood of the bull — all the more we say thus in rabbinic mashahu such as chametz on Pesach and yayin nesech; certainly whatever was first forbidden due to its species in mashahu does not forbid afterward if it fell to others and there is sixty against it, for then mashahu does not forbid; and therefore Tur wrote even if forbidden due to its species forbidden in mashahu for R' Yehuda — meaning from Torah as I explained; and all the more chametz on Pesach for us whether in its species or not at sixty, for if forbidden in mashahu it does not become nevelah if it would forbid afterward in mashahu — we do not say two mashahu. And so I saw in Perishah that brought in name of Maharshal to explain the language "even" that Tur wrote per the intent I wrote; only he challenged on this that since its prohibition is in mashahu, what difference whether in the first mixture or the second — granted in mashahu of R' Yehuda it only forbids in its species, and when it afterward fell in heter that is not its species we estimate as if the issur itself fell there and nullify it at sixty, because the issur itself does not forbid there except at sixty, for specifically in its species it forbids in mashahu; not so in chametz on Pesach that even if we estimate as if the issur alone fell to the second mixtures, even in not its species nevertheless it forbids in mashahu — and if so what does the second mixture help more than the first — end of his words; honor to him in its place, but he did not analyze Tosafot properly, for there it deals that the forbidden piece fell in its species as we brought above, and nevertheless it does not forbid in mashahu — we do not say two mashahu. And all poskim already brought R' Tam's view above, and so Orach Chaim general rule 28 in name of Sefer HaTerumah, and Maharshal ch. Gid HaNasheh siman 58, and Rama in Levush on chametz on Pesach and yayin nesech forbidden in mashahu did not become nevelah, and if it returned and fell into another pot it is nullified in majority if not recognized, and if recognized throw it away and the rest are permitted — end of his words. And per this we must explain what Shulchan Aruch ruled in Orach Chaim siman 447 seif 1 that the law of chametz mixture on Pesach in mashahu is like other issurim at sixty — in the first mixture; but in the second mixture it does not forbid in mashahu others, since the first mixture was only through mashahu. And Maharach explained the "even" that Tur wrote above also for chametz on Pesach — that we do not say it became nevelah, only that Tur wrote this regarding chametz mixture after Pesach; on this Tur speaks — not that we need not say it became nevelah implies that during Pesach it is forbidden; and this is not the intent, for from Tur's ruling that even for R' Yehuda in its species in mashahu it did not become nevelah, all the more in chametz even during Pesach since its mashahu is only rabbinic; and so appears primary; only we must explain that which Tosafot wrote above in name of R' Tam that we do not say two mashahu — there it deals that the issur piece was recognizable and they did not permit there except its discharge that does not forbid in mashahu; and therefore in chametz on Pesach too one may not be lenient except when the piece was forbidden in mashahu and returned and fell to another and they removed it — its discharge does not forbid in mashahu as long as there is sixty against it; but in wet with wet mixed in this way one may not be lenient, since the first wet was already forbidden in its mixture — the second mixture does not help afterward, for that issur is never nullified; and likewise a piece in dry forbidden in mashahu and not recognized remains in issur, and if we say it is nullified in the majority of others this depends on the dispute Shulchan Aruch brought in siman 447 seif 9, and one should be stringent; and so Levush ruled there to be stringent in dry with dry; and therefore what he ruled here that if one does not recognize the forbidden piece on Pesach it is nullified in majority is not correct at all and also contradicts his own words — rather the issur remains forever in its issur on Pesach and we have no permission except if we recognize it and remove it, for its discharge does not forbid in mashahu since it was only forbidden first in mashahu — so it appears to me clear and correct.
