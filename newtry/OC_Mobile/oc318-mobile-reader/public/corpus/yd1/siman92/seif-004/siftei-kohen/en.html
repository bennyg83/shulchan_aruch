They did not say chatichah na'asah nevelah except in basar b'chalav, etc. The reason poskim wrote: meat and milk are each permitted by itself, and when together they are forbidden — therefore the meat itself becomes issur; not so in other issurim.
And then even the piece itself returns to being permitted, etc. One might say below siman 106 he wrote the piece itself remains in its issur because the issur in it is not fully discharged — end of his words; and the Rav in the gloss there understood these contradict each other; to avoid contradiction he explains explicitly: what he wrote here means per what he wrote earlier in basar b'chalav that even though the gravy is permitted with sixty, nevertheless the piece remains in issur; if it is fit to be honored and he does not recognize it, it is not nullified in a thousand — and he teaches that it is not so in other issurim but nullified in majority when not recognized since there is no issur from the substance itself, as below siman 101; and then even the piece itself returns to being permitted and permitted to eat all of it and need not throw one away as below siman 109.
And some say we say in all issurim chatichah na'asah nevelah, etc. Agur in name of Maharai wrote: nevertheless for all it is only rabbinic; Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe bring it; and so Ran that in other issurim it is rabbinic — I brought this in siman 22 s.k. 10 — see there.
But if part of the piece is in the gravy, etc., until but in basar b'chalav, etc., we say chatichah na'asah nevelah. The Rav ruled thus — all follows his reasoning like a puzzle — who like the wise knows its interpretation; but in truth it requires study in this matter regarding part outside the gravy, for what was written in Shulchan Aruch from Orach Chaim general rule 28 that even in other issurim and part in the gravy one should forbid that piece itself — is not so; on the contrary Orach Chaim wrote there explicitly several times that even in basar b'chalav if part is in the gravy the whole pot joins to nullify the issur and even the piece itself is permitted — see there; and this is ruling like R' Yehuda as I brought in s.k. 4, and Darkei Moshe brings it explicitly there in the conclusion to rule the law; and what the Rav wrote that in basar b'chalav even if part is in the gravy chatichah na'asah nevelah — also requires study, for it is against all poskim, for in the Gemara ch. 25 (daf 108–109) we say a drop of milk on a meat piece — the piece is forbidden and does not forbid all other pieces (until sixty against it) except through stirring and covering; and R' Yehuda and Rabbenu Tam explained: wholly outside the gravy; but if part is in the gravy the whole pot joins to nullify and then even the piece is permitted — and so ruled Semag (negative commandment 141 daf 51a), Semak siman 213 daf 101a, Sha'arim siman 95, Mordechai, Agudah ch. Kol HaBasar, Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit daf 99, Maharil, and other authors like R' Yehuda; and even for Rashi who explained when part is in the gravy it appears no chatichah na'asah nevelah to forbid the others except through stirring and covering; and it appears what the Rav wrote that in basar b'chalav even if part is in the gravy chatichah na'asah nevelah — this is when he stirred afterward until all entered the gravy, and not that we say from the start the whole pot joins to nullify because part is in the gravy as in other issurim; for at the outset, either the whole piece is outside the gravy and issur fell on it, etc. — it must deal with stirring; for if not, chatichah na'asah nevelah would not forbid the rest of the pot — therefore the conclusion also deals with this; and regarding what he wrote in other issurim nevertheless to be stringent to forbid that piece — it appears this is the Rav's own view, for he is in doubt whether we hold like Rashi or R' Yehuda, as I wrote in s.k. 7; but regarding the rest of the pot he did not want to be stringent since otherwise some say we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah except in basar b'chalav — requires study; and nevertheless it is already explained in s.k. 4 that the view of all authors and Acharonim is like R' Yehuda — see there.
Some say we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah if wet issur was mixed with wet heter and afterward everything was mixed with other heter, etc. Meaning: specifically when wet issur mixed with dry heter we say chatichah na'asah nevelah from importance, for the pieces of issur are recognizable and distinct — chatichah na'asah nevelah; but not when wet issur mixed with wet heter such as yayin nesech with kosher wine, or milk with fat, and afterward everything mixed with other heter — whether the other heter is wet or dry — requires study; and see Yeshu'ot Yaakov ch. 3 siman 60.
But not in basar b'chalav, etc. The language of Ikkarim (4:53a): however in basar b'chalav one should be stringent even in wet — end of his words; and per my humble opinion this is not stringency but that all agree here, as proven explicitly in the Gemara ch. 25 (daf 108): there they say: why is milk permitted — it is nevelah milk; for in basar b'chalav even in wet chatichah na'asah nevelah; and so Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 98a — see there.
We do not say in any issur chatichah na'asah nevelah. Meaning in both basar b'chalav and other issurim — rather whenever more heter is added afterward it is as if mixed from the start, and its law is as below siman 109; and see below siman 99 s.k. 5 and whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in salting — Peri Chadash ruled: even though they practice to estimate all salting at sixty, nevertheless since many poskim hold all salting only forbids kedei kezayit, one may permit for great loss or for a poor person in an important matter and say it is not chatichah na'asah nevelah — end of his words; and it appears there that even in a fatty matter that forbids until sixty as below siman 105 we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — and so appears primary, as I wrote in my book, and not like Be'er Sheva daf 82b; and see siman 22 s.k. 10 whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in kelipah — see there; and see below siman 134 s.k. 15 more on chatichah na'asah nevelah.
