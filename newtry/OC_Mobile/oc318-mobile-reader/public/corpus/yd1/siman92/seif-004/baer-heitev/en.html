Itself. Shach wrote: one might say below siman 106 he wrote the piece itself remains in its issur because the issur in it is not fully discharged, and the Rav in the gloss there understood these are contradictory and to avoid contradiction he explains thus in explanation: what he wrote here means per what he wrote earlier regarding basar b'chalav that even the gravy is permitted with sixty, nevertheless the piece remains in issur; if it is chatichah hefker and he does not recognize it it is not nullified in a thousand — and he teaches that it is not so in other issurim but nullified in majority when not recognized since there is no issur from the substance itself, as below siman 101; and then even the piece itself returns to being permitted and permitted to eat all of it and need not throw one away as below siman 109. The reason the Mechaber distinguishes basar b'chalav from other issurim is because in basar b'chalav there is no recognizable issur except after mixture — therefore the whole mixture is the substance of the issur forbidden by Torah "mixing meat in milk"; not so other things that are issur in themselves and that is the Torah issur, only here it mixed incidentally and you have only a Torah doubt and chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply — so Maggid Mishneh.
The issurim. Shach wrote in name of poskim that nevertheless for all it is only rabbinic in other issurim (and I already wrote above siman 90 that even in rabbinic issur we say chatichah na'asah nevelah).
Attached. Taz wrote in name of Semak: since it is attached to this issur, and likewise a worm in its body, and likewise a kosher fish found in its innards after cooking with a sheretz or non-kosher fish and there was not sixty in the fish itself against it — in all such cases we fear perhaps once it remained alone in the gravy at the end of pouring, or perhaps once they took it out boiling outside the pot and it became nevelah and after returning it was not permitted and forbids the rest until sixty against it. From this it appears in a fish where a sheretz was found or a piece of sheretz in its head and cooked thus with other fish one needs sixty against the whole head of the fish, for one law and one reason with what is found in its innards; and further proof from Orach Chaim general rule 36 regarding an apple with a worm — attached issur requires sixty in cooking against the whole apple if the apple itself does not have sixty against the worm; and so I saw in Maharil, etc. — peel the place for its taste is nullified in sixty even in cooking if there is sixty in the piece where it was found; and so he ruled on a piece of carp found in the head of a cooked fish to nullify its taste if there is sixty in the head of the fish against it. However per the reasoning it does not seem attached, for it rests where gravy can come from all sides; and since chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim is only rabbinic as I wrote, one may lean to be lenient here too and it is not chatichah na'asah nevelah — see siman 72 seif 3 what Taz and Shach wrote there, that the reason for attached issur is because it absorbs faster than other meat.
The gravy. Shach wrote: it appears what the Rav wrote in basar b'chalav that even if part is in the gravy chatichah na'asah nevelah — this is when he stirred afterward until all entered the gravy, and not that we say from the start everything joins to nullify because part is in the gravy as in other issurim; he teaches in basar b'chalav it is not so. Regarding what he wrote in other issurim nevertheless to be stringent to forbid that piece — it appears this is the Rav's own reasoning, for he is in doubt whether we hold like Rashi or R' Yehuda; but regarding the rest of the pot he did not want to be stringent since otherwise some say we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah except in basar b'chalav; nevertheless it is already explained that the view of all authors and Acharonim is like R' Yehuda.
Fell. Taz wrote in name of Maharshal: this is specifically when mixed not in the manner of cooking; but if it is the manner of cooking through imparting taste — issur mixed there and afterward fell from the gravy into permitted food — one needs sixty against all the gravy that fell, not to consider how much of the issur was absorbed in proportion in this gravy; but wet with wet such as wine in wine or water in forbidden water we can say we do not say all the permitted became nevelah, especially since it is rabbinic, and if more permitted was added afterward it is permitted — so it appears from his words that even not in case of loss we do not say in wet that became nevelah contrary to Rama here.
In any. Meaning even in basar b'chalav we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah in dry — its law as below siman 109. Regarding whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in salting, Terumat HaDeshen wrote: even though they practice to estimate all salting at sixty, nevertheless since many poskim hold all salting only forbids kedei kezayit one may permit for great loss or for a poor person in an important matter and say it is not chatichah na'asah nevelah; and it appears there that even in a fatty matter that forbids until sixty as below siman 105 we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — and so appears primary; see siman 22 whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in kelipah — see siman 134 regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah. Taz wrote what Rama wrote if dry mixed with dry means not in the manner of cooking together — then its law is the issur is nullified in majority; therefore if first mixed not in majority one need not afterward majority against the whole first mixture; but if they cooked together certainly we say chatichah na'asah nevelah and need sixty in permitted against the whole piece. He wrote further Tur for R' Tam's view that even in other issurim "possible to squeeze it out" forbids and chatichah na'asah nevelah specifically in noten ta'am when there was not sixty in permitted to nullify the issur absorbed in it, but if forbidden in mashahu even if forbidden due to its species forbidden in mashahu for R' Yehuda the whole does not become nevelah. The reason is from what is stated in ch. 25 — R' Tam's answer, etc. — and since we say this reasoning in mashahu in min b'mino for R' Yehuda which is from Torah, all the more we say thus in rabbinic mashahu such as chametz on Pesach and yayin nesech; certainly what was first forbidden due to its species in mashahu does not forbid afterward if it fell to others and there is sixty against it, for then mashahu does not forbid; and we do not say two mashahu. Tur wrote even if forbidden due to its species forbidden in mashahu for R' Yehuda — meaning from Torah as I explained; and all the more chametz on Pesach for us whether in its species or not at sixty, for if forbidden in mashahu it does not become nevelah if it would forbid afterward in mashahu — we do not say two mashahu. A dry piece forbidden in mashahu and not recognized remains in issur and has no permission except if recognized and removed, for its discharge does not forbid in mashahu since it was only forbidden first in mashahu — so Bach. (Nekudat HaKesef disagrees with Taz and wrote he asked all elder rabbis and all said with one mouth they never practiced to forbid all mixtures — see there many laws related to Pesach.)
