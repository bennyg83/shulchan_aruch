They did not say the piece itself becomes nevelah except in basar b'chalav; but not in other issurim — such as an olive-size of milk absorbed in a piece and there is not sixty in it to nullify it and it was forbidden, and afterward it was cooked with others — one needs only sixty for the olive-size of milk; and then even the piece itself returns to being permitted. {Rama: And some say we say in all issurim the piece becomes nevelah (Tosafot, Semag, Semak, Mordechai, and Acharonim) and so is the accepted custom and one should not change. And specifically if the issur is attached to the permitted piece, or the whole piece is outside the gravy and issur fell on it; but if part of the piece is in the gravy and the issur is not attached to it, we do not say the piece becomes nevelah, and the whole pot joins to nullify the issur — nevertheless one should be stringent to forbid that piece (Arukh general rule 28). And all this is in other issurim; but in basar b'chalav, even though the issur is not attached and part of the piece is in the gravy, we say the piece becomes nevelah. Some say we do not say the piece becomes nevelah if wet issur was mixed with wet heter and afterward everything was mixed with other heter, and they need only sixty against the issur that fell (Mordechai end of ch. 25, Rashba, Ran in the name of Raavad) — and one may rely on this in other issurim for great loss, but not in basar b'chalav. And if dry was mixed with dry, we do not say in any issur the piece becomes nevelah. And see below siman 99 s.k. 15 regarding chatichah na'asah nevelah. A vessel forbidden through absorption of issur — we do not say regarding it chatichah na'asah nevelah, and we do not require sixty except against the issur it absorbed (Mordechai end of ch. 89 and see below siman 98).}
