And still difficult: why did they give the parable of taste entering a piece — a piece of sixty and put in it taste of a zayit — even if they put only half a zayit, since in min b'mino it is not nullified, that half zayit of gravy forbids until there is sixty against it; and if it is not its species — what of it, behold the piece itself is also not its species relative to the gravy; and still difficult: why \"since it gave taste,\" etc. — rather learn that it is entirely lost; and without taste entering it is as if it does not exist — and at first glance this is compelling. However one may reject: the other pieces without gravy would nullify the absorption that is species in not its species — but this is strained, for behold it returns and is stirred with the nevelah kezayit placed there that was a zayit and a half — why should it be not its species — requires study. But go and learn what Rashba wrote in Mishmeret HaBayit daf 97: if issur did not give taste in the piece — although for the view that min b'mino is not nullified the piece is forbidden — nevertheless if the piece entered the gravy, sixty is not required even to nullify that absorption, for since it is mashahu it is as if it does not exist — behold he wrote explicitly that sixty is not required against the absorption; why — behold it absorbed issur, and what of it that it is not its species — and because it is not its species it should not require nullification; and Rashba did not mention at all that other pieces entered with it into the gravy until we say they nullify it — only the piece alone entered and from here it should be permitted; and perforce one must say as he wrote himself that mashahu is as if it does not exist — and so Rashba wrote in his novellae; and so it appears at length in Torat HaBayit; and so Ritva wrote in his novellae to Chullin: it appears more correct that Rav holds nothing forbids in mashahu except when it is visible; but when mixed between issur and heter it is not forbidden; and so Orach Chaim HaAroch general rule 24 din 3 wrote: a thing forbidden in mashahu from issur forbidden in mashahu — chatichah na'asah nevelah does not apply — behold he ruled without distinguishing between chametz and other issurim. And if so Taz spoke well, and so Chokhmat Adam — and it is clear he did not intend the remote path Shach spoke in Nekudat HaKesef on erev Pesach after chatzot and Rambam's views. And therefore I wonder at Magen Avraham who wrote on Taz that he exceeded measure to permit what also the Rav Chokhmat Adam, who was among the elder rabbis and student of Maharal of Prague, wrote to permit, and so Orach Chaim explicitly general rule 24 din 3 — end of his words; and the world practices on issur — it appears from the statement \"chametz\" they were concerned for Rosh's view as I wrote; one may question Ran's view and Ramban's and Ra'ah in Beit HaBayit that noten ta'am is not precise — even mashahu forbids; and for this branch there is issur, for it emerges from the mouths of sages of the generation; but to lean to permit is clear it is permitted. And there is some proof for Ran and Shach's words from that we learn: taste and not substance, etc. — for if it is a novelty, even without noten ta'am also; and Rava said the manner of cooking, etc. — learn that if it is a novelty one cannot say in it noten ta'am also, even mashahu also; and if so it is hard: we say for one tanna hag'alah of gentiles is a novelty as explained in the Gemara in Pesachim — if so even without noten ta'am also; and if so gentile vessels cooked in them should be forbidden even without noten ta'am also, for in any case it is a novelty that even a detrimental thing is forbidden — so too even noten ta'am also; and one must say: if so how do you find hag'alah of gentiles — behold taste is absorbed in water and returns and absorbs; and if they hag'alah in a large vessel that has sixty in the water against the gentile absorption, it forbids even without noten ta'am also — how can one hag'alah; only the difficulty is: behold the absorbed thing more than noten ta'am does not forbid its discharge again — and therefore one may say they hag'alah twice; and what of it that first it was absorbed in it — nevertheless afterward hag'alah helps since also the water does not forbid its discharge; and it helps that the first water was only forbidden in mashahu — and therefore one must say like Shach's view that whatever is forbidden in mashahu, whether its species or not its species, even a thing forbidden in mashahu forbids its discharge; if so per the view that hag'alah of gentiles is in mashahu — how do you find it, for forever the water forbids without end, for there is no difference between its species and not its species — requires study, for one may say these are different aspects — requires study.
