Into a pot of meat. This deals when the drop fell on one piece outside the gravy — for if inside the gravy certainly everything in the pot joins to nullify the drop; and Tur wrote the dispute of Rashi and R' Yehuda: R' Yehuda holds only when the whole piece is outside the gravy, such as one piece has part outside the gravy and this rests on it — therefore that piece alone is forbidden; but if part is in the gravy, boiling mixes the whole pot and everything joins to nullify the milk; and Rashi holds even if part is in the gravy not everything joins to nullify the milk; and there is a leniency for Rashi on one side — namely if part is in the gravy and this piece is forbidden it does not forbid the rest, as Darkei Moshe wrote; and it does not appear to my humble opinion that although the gravy below does not help nullify the drop of milk that fell since boiling does not reach there, nevertheless that piece itself is forbidden — the lower part from the upper part — and returns and forbids all the gravy and what is in it until sixty against the whole piece, for the whole piece becomes nevelah since part of the piece rests in the gravy the gravy conveys to the rest; and from these words of Shulchan Aruch which are Rambam's words it appears to me he holds like Rashi since he wrote if the piece was forbidden it forbids the rest — therefore it appears to me this is how we hold, although Maharshal in his Orach Chaim ruled like R' Yehuda, nevertheless in his book ch. 25 siman 49 he was stringent like Rashi.
That piece was forbidden. Terumat HaChacham general rule 22 wrote that Tur's words here contradict what he wrote in siman 105 seif 4 that if issur fell on the piece in the pot it only forbids kedei netilah; and Maharshal resolved that here it deals with a clear issur and there with a thick issur that does not spread as much. And per what I wrote in siman 87 seif 10 that according to Rosh and Tur milk is a fat issur and steams here too — there is no difficulty, for it is explained in siman 105 the distinction between weak and fat issur; and so I found afterward in Darkei Moshe siman 105 that he resolved thus, and wrote this is not like Rashba that Beit Yosef brings there that milk does not steam — but in siman 105 seif 7 it is proven there that milk does not steam — requires study.
But if he stirred from beginning to end. Not precise — rather if he stirred immediately it suffices, as Tur wrote explicitly; and Mahari ibn Chabib challenged that for R' Yehuda one need not stir but only that part be in the gravy; and it appears to me that here too it suffices, only since the pot is full and one cannot well insert the piece that rests outside on another piece that has part in the gravy, as I wrote above for R' Yehuda — therefore one must stir the pot so the upper become lower; and it is already explained that we hold like Rashi.
And likewise if he did not stir at all. These matters require study — behold they are Raavad's view in Beit Yosef, and these are his words: if he did not stir at all the other pieces are not forbidden, although if he did not stir gravy goes out and becomes nevelah from that piece to another piece — nevertheless they are not forbidden, for the milk must go out to the other pieces — end of his words; and if so it is hard why Rama wrote that sixty is required in the pot against the milk, for the milk does not go out to the other pieces at all; and if it does go out, behold it forbids and one would need sixty in the pot against the whole forbidden piece — requires study. And in Orach Chaim general rule 28 he also wrote to permit when there is sixty in the whole pot against the drop that fell on a piece that has part outside the gravy, because he holds like R' Yehuda — only he wrote that then the piece also returns to permission, unlike Rama here; and one must say Rama is stringent from his own reasoning to forbid that piece even per R' Yehuda, and so it appears from his words in seif 4 that he rules like R' Yehuda where part is outside the gravy, for leniency; and one might say Rama should have written this here in language of "some say," for Rambam who is the first view holds like Rashi that sixty is required against the whole piece, as I wrote below; and Rama's language implies he did not come to dispute but to add; and further it is hard that in seif 4 Rama concludes in basar b'chalav one should be stringent and here he ruled leniently, and it deals with basar b'chalav.
Although he did not stir and covered at the end, etc. Tur wrote this disputing Rambam and holds thus; and Beit Yosef wrote Rambam's reason from what we say in the Gemara "from this we learn R' Yehuda holds," etc. — it appears to Rambam that only in such a case is it permitted, but if he stirred at the beginning and not at the end, no — lest he did not stir well — requires study on this; for why do we say in truth in the Gemara "from this we learn R' Yehuda holds even if he stirred from beginning to end," etc. — for we have no proof on this except from what Rabbi said "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring but only at the end" — from this we learn R' Yehuda is more stringent; whence do you know he is so stringent even with stirring from beginning to end — perhaps he is stringent only with stirring immediately alone, which is one degree more than not stirring except at the end; but whence do you say he is stringent with stirring from beginning to end which is two degrees? Granted for Tur, stirring from beginning to end is one matter with stirring at the beginning alone — only he challenged what Tur saw to prove from Rambam's words more than from the Gemara's words, for there too they said the language "the words of the Sages appear correct with stirring from beginning to end" — therefore it is stirring immediately alone per Tur's view; and if so what is Tur's difficulty in Rambam's words more than the Gemara's? And it appears Tur holds one must infer in the Gemara: from "the words of R' Yehuda appear correct without stirring but only at the end" it implies with stirring at the beginning alone it is permitted; and afterward they said "the words of the Sages appear correct with stirring from beginning to end" — it implies stirring at the beginning alone does not help; and therefore one of them is not precise; and we find similar in Rosh that Beit Yosef brings in siman 95 that we go stringently; nevertheless here too one cannot infer to say an excess, for the reverse could also be asked; rather no proof applies here; therefore Rambam ruled stringently, namely that he mentioned permission only with stirring from beginning to end, although in prohibition he mentioned only stirring at the end alone — nevertheless he relied that we will explain stringently; and on this Tur disputes and holds the inference of the first part is primary, since for Rav we are not concerned lest he did not stir well — therefore with stirring at the beginning alone suffices, as Tur intended; but in truth one may resolve the Gemara's and Rambam's words so there is no dispute between Rambam and Tur in law, only in explaining the language that Tur explains "from beginning to end" means he did not cease stirring until he removed it from the fire — therefore he disputes Rambam per his reasoning; but Rambam explains otherwise: as long as there is substance of the drop of milk on the meat, what he stirred in the pot does not help, lest that part returned above afterward and again forbids that piece by itself since it still exists visibly on it — therefore we say he should stir until the substance of the drop ceases, and this is "from beginning to end" in the Gemara said regarding the end of the drop's existence; and so is Rambam's intent; but if the substance of the drop finished, Rambam concedes to Tur that it suffices if he afterward ceases to stir — as I wrote, correct. And in what Tur wrote afterward "and I do not understand his words," etc. — per Tur's understanding of Rambam's words that it deals after he waited a bit he stirred the pot — therefore he challenged what stirring helps, etc.; but Beit Yosef and Mahari ibn Chabib explained Rambam's words that it deals when he did not wait at all — and whence does Tur have to explain Rambam's words that it deals when he waited a bit and to raise his difficulty? One may say he proved thus from that he wrote "he stirs the pot so it will mix" — future tense — and did not say "if he stirred" as in the beginning, but certainly that here it deals that they instruct him thus if he comes to ask — so is Tur's intent; but per my humble opinion Rambam's words are not explained at all in this way; and at first I also inferred in his words "one may not nullify issur l'chatchila," and Mahari ibn Chabib thought to correct this and did not correct, for even if he did not wait and stirred immediately, nevertheless Rambam's language implies l'chatchila he should do so — and how can he insert the piece that has milk on it into the gravy and nullify the milk l'chatchila; rather the matter is clear that here Rambam deals regarding that in truth everything joins, namely it fell into the gravy; and likewise if it fell on a piece that rests outside the gravy only it is not known which it is — therefore we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah, as the Rav HaMaggid wrote: since he does not know which piece it is, one cannot say chatichah na'asah nevelah on it and cannot forbid everything since there is no noten ta'am in milk — end of his words. And Rambam comes here to say in what matter the joining of other pieces helps: if there is a gentile kafeilah who can taste whether there is milk taste, and if he does not taste milk taste it is permitted even though there is no sixty there, as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Rambam in siman 98 — therefore he said here if there is a gentile to taste, then he stirs everything so it mixes and afterward gives him to taste; for if he gives him to taste immediately, he will certainly taste milk taste from what floated above; and in truth if he mixes and gives him to taste he will not taste milk taste because the milk was nullified in the mixture; and we only declare forbidden if the gentile tastes milk taste after it is mixed well — and it turns out there will be loss in vain if he does not mix; therefore he said stir so everything mixes if there is milk taste in the whole pot — meaning not in what floated above alone, for then specifically it is forbidden; and likewise in a piece that is not known which it is; and the law is that other pieces also join with it, and if he gives one piece to taste it is possible he will taste that piece on which it fell and taste milk taste and from this forbid everything; and in truth if he mixes that milk taste will be nullified — therefore he stirs everything. And if so he properly said afterward: immediately if a gentile is not found to taste and we rely on him, they estimate at sixty, etc. — meaning in permission through gentile tasting he must stir first for the reason we said lest there be loss; but when there is no gentile and permission is through sixty, then in truth he need not stir at all since everything joins to sixty; and now there is no nullifying issur l'chatchila, for in any case it is mixed — only the stirring is for the owners' benefit; and all is well with heaven's help that He illuminates our eyes from heaven; and although our teacher Tur did not explain this in Rambam's words, we explain in Rambam's words.
