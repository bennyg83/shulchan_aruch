The piece. Shach wrote the explanation is thus: when it fell on one piece, the rest in the pot do not join to nullify the milk but need sixty in the piece itself to nullify the taste; when there is not sixty in the piece the piece becomes nevelah; when he stirs afterward and the piece enters among the rest of the pot it forbids the rest in the pot until sixty against the piece — but if he stirred immediately at the time of falling the piece immediately enters the whole pot and everything joins and it is not forbidden unless there is not sixty in the pot and piece against the milk, and same with covering. That which Tur wrote siman 105 seif 4 — if wholly outside the gravy it is only kedei netilah for R' Yehuda, and for Rashi even partly in the gravy — perhaps milk differs as a fat issur, or it deals with a thick issur that does not spread except through gravy; on the first answer it is hard from Rashba and Ran that milk is not called a fat issur — see Taz siman 87 seif 10 who proved milk is a fat issur and left the matter b'tz'ar.
Sixty. Shach wrote: Tur brought the dispute of Rashi and R' Yehuda — R' Yehuda holds only when the whole piece is outside the gravy, e.g. the piece beneath has part outside the gravy and this one rests on it; but if part is in the gravy, boiling mixes the taste in the whole pot and everything joins to nullify the milk. Rashi explains even if part is in the gravy not everything joins to nullify the milk. The view of all authors except Rashba and R' Yerucham is like R' Yehuda, and so all Acharonim; Taz wrote there is a leniency for Rashi on one side — if part is in the gravy and this piece is forbidden it does not forbid the rest; Taz wrote it does not appear so to him, for even though the gravy below does not help nullify the drop of milk that fell since boiling does not reach there, nevertheless that piece itself is forbidden — the lower part from the upper part — and returns and forbids all the gravy and what is in it until sixty against the whole piece, for the piece becomes nevelah since part of the piece rests in the gravy it conveys to the rest. (These words are astonishing in my eyes, for if so what difference is there in Rashi's view between stirring or not, and so it appears from Shulchan Aruch since he rules and likewise if he did not stir at all, if there is sixty in the pot against the drop of milk that fell it forbids only the piece alone — therefore it deals when it is in the gravy as explained later; and per his words in Darkei Moshe it implies one needs sixty against the whole piece — b'tz'ar.) And Taz wrote further from Shulchan Aruch's words which are Rambam's words it appears he holds like Rashi; and even though Maharshal in his Orach Chaim ruled like R' Yehuda, in his book ch. 25 siman 49 he was stringent like Rashi. (And it is hard what stringency there is in Rashi's view more than R' Yehuda's, for even when there is sixty in the pot there is stringency for Rashi that for R' Yehuda if it is in the gravy everything joins to nullify, not so for Rashi; nevertheless when there is not sixty in the pot it is the reverse — for Rashi only that piece is forbidden and for R' Yehuda everything is forbidden — and one must say he holds in truth for both stringently: if there is sixty we go stringently like Rashi, and if there is not sixty also stringently like R' Yehuda — requires study.)
Permitted. Nevertheless all other pieces that touched this forbidden piece need netilah as siman 105 seif 7. Shach wrote: one might say when there is not sixty in the pot it should also be permitted since he did not stir at all and it only imparted taste in that piece; it appears what requires sixty in the pot is when part is in the gravy, for the Rav is in doubt whether we hold like R' Yehuda who mixes taste in all or like Rashi — therefore the pot is permitted either way and the piece is forbidden lest he does not mix taste in all and the pot does not join. (Beit Yosef emends it should read thus, and likewise if he did not stir at all neither at start nor end nor cover at all — if there is not sixty in the piece against the drop of milk that fell it forbids only the piece alone and the rest of the pot is permitted.)
And likewise. Shach wrote: even though it appears one can explain the Mechaber's words that he requires stirring specifically from beginning to end — and therefore Rama should have written in language of "some say" — nevertheless since the Mechaber's words can also be explained thus, as Mahari ibn Chabib explained Rambam's words, therefore he did not write in language of "some say"; but in truth the Mechaber's view is not so, etc. — see there.
Stir. Shach wrote: and one might say how can he stir the pot that everything mix — perhaps there is taste in one piece and how can one nullify issur l'chatchila; one may say since it is not known on which it fell the whole pot enters doubt and there is no issue of nullifying issur; Rama who is Tur's view holds since it is possible to establish that a gentile tastes each piece and piece, if so not everything enters doubt and stirring afterward does not help — see siman 105.
