It is forbidden — see Peleti: that piece is forbidden because there is some fatness and pot steam also joins to spread in that piece, not the whole pot.
Only at the end — therefore at first that piece becomes nevelah and when stirring at the end it forbids the whole pot and needs sixty in the pot against the piece.
Until end of conclusion — for Talmudic language: if he stirred from the start he need not stir at the end; same with covering — Perishat Rashi.
And if he did not stir — if the piece is partly in the gravy and milk fell not opposite the gravy: for R' Yehuda as if in the gravy; for Rashi as if outside the gravy. Taz's view: that piece is forbidden and the rest of the pot does not join with it to nullify the milk in sixty; since it became nevelah it forbids the pot until sixty against the whole piece. Shach's view: it does not forbid the whole pot, only the piece, and all the more it does not join with the piece to nullify; but nevertheless need sixty in the pot against the milk that fell, for we say at falling it immediately spreads through the whole pot — see Peleti.
If there is sixty, etc. Shach explains the piece is half in the gravy; need sixty in the pot per R' Yehuda who spreads through the whole pot, only does not join to nullify per Rashi — see Peleti at length.
And not known, etc. See Peleti: Shach's view that since unknown we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah; I wrote it is not clear in Torah doubt in basar b'chalav; also that unknown means doubt whether it fell in the pot and became nevelah or did not and did not — proof from Talmud we do not establish prohibition; also nullified immediately at moment of falling before absorption — returns issur to permitted.
