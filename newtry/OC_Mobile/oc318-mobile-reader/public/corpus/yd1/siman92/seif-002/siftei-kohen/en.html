Milk fell, etc. Through the end of the seif is Rambam's language ch. 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot. The explanation: when it fell on one piece, the rest in the pot do not join to nullify the milk but there must be sixty in the piece itself to nullify the taste; when there is not sixty in the piece the piece becomes nevelah; when he stirs afterward and the piece enters among the rest of the pot it forbids again what is in the pot until sixty against the piece — but if he stirred immediately at the time the drop fell the piece immediately enters the whole pot and the whole pot joins and afterward one is not forbidden unless there is not sixty in the pot and piece against the milk, and same with covering. That which Tur wrote below siman 105 seif 4 — if wholly outside the gravy it is only kedei netilah for R' Yehuda, and for Rashi even if part is in the gravy — perhaps milk differs as a fat issur since there is greasiness of butter) and it absorbs in the whole piece as in the Gemara ch. Kol HaBasar, and agreed by all poskim below siman 105 seif 5 that a fat issur steams through the whole piece — so answered in Darkei Moshe siman 105; and that which Taz wrote siman 87 seif 10 that blood dripping on roast by the fire forbids only kedei netilah — end — because blood is not a fat issur as proven in several places. Or one may distinguish as Ran ch. 25 wrote, etc.: this piece since it stands in the pot, from the steam and moisture of the milk it spreads through all of it even outside the gravy — end of his words — see there; and below siman 105 although it also deals with a pot, one may say it deals with a thick issur that is not clear and certainly does not spread through the whole piece except through gravy — so divided Maharshal, and Perishah brings him; and the first distinction is hard from Rashba in Torat HaBayit (daf 99b) and Ran that milk is not considered a fat issur — see there; and so from Taz's words below siman 105 seif 7 they ruled that a weak issur does not steam from piece to piece without gravy and a fat issur steams as Rashba — and Rashba learns this from milk here that does not steam from piece to piece without gravy — but requires study; one may say it deals that the meat piece is fatty as Taz wrote siman 105 seif 5; and I expanded on this in my book and challenged Bach on the Rav there in siman 105 seif 5 — see there; here I shortened since we always estimate at sixty as below siman 105 — see there.
That piece was forbidden and they estimate, etc. The poskim and Tur brought the dispute of Rashi and R' Yehuda: R' Yehuda holds only when the whole piece is outside the gravy, such as the piece beneath has part outside the gravy and this one rests on it; but if part is in the gravy, boiling mixes the taste in the whole pot and everything joins to nullify the milk. Rashi explains even if part is in the gravy not everything joins to nullify the milk. The view of all authors (except Rashba and R' Yerucham, brought in Bedek HaBayit) is like R' Yehuda, and so all Acharonim; and so Maharshal in his glosses to Tur, and Perishah brings him — except in his book ch. Kol HaBasar siman 61 he did not rule thus.
But if he stirred from beginning to end, etc. This is Rambam's language, and Tur wrote on it: it appears one need only stir or cover immediately at the time of falling, and even if he ceased stirring afterward — end of his words; and R' Yehuda ibn Chabib wrote that Rambam's view is also thus, and that which he mentioned stirring from beginning to end was because at the outset he had to mention stirring at the end — for otherwise why would everything in the pot be forbidden — and therefore he also mentioned stirring at the end in the conclusion — end of his words; and per this the Mechaber should not have been silent and should have explained that stirring at the beginning alone suffices; but he followed his view written in Beit Yosef that Rambam requires stirring at beginning and end, and the Rav in the gloss that stirring at the beginning alone suffices; and this is what he wrote: and likewise if he stirred at the beginning, etc.; and he did not write this law in language of "some say" because the Mechaber's words can also be explained thus, as Mahari ibn Chabib explained Rambam's words; but the Mechaber's own view is not so, as I wrote; nevertheless the view of Mahari ibn Chabib and the Rav is primary, and so from Rashi (daf 109a) — see there; and so Maharshal ch. 25 siman 45 and Acharonim; and it appears Tur did not intend to challenge but to explain Rambam's words.
And likewise if he did not stir, etc. — if there is sixty in the pot against the drop of milk, etc. One might say that when there is not sixty in the pot it should also be permitted since he did not stir at all and it only imparted taste in that piece itself, as in the Gemara and poskim, and explicitly in Raavad's challenges ch. 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot — see there; and Darkei Moshe brings it briefly; and it appears what requires sixty in the pot is when part is in the gravy, for the Rav is in doubt whether we hold like R' Yehuda who mixes taste in all or like Rashi — therefore the pot is permitted either way and the piece is forbidden lest he does not mix taste in all and the pot does not join (and nevertheless what was written in Shulchan Aruch and Hagahot Maimoniyot in name of Rambam is a mistake and should read: and nevertheless in name of Rambam).
And the rest of the pot is permitted. See below siman 105 seif 7 s.k. 16 that nevertheless all other pieces that touched this piece need netilah.
And likewise if it fell, etc. This is Rambam's language, and Tur wrote: I do not understand his words — if the pieces are all in the gravy why need to stir, and if not in the gravy what does stirring help if he stirs afterward — end of his words; and the Rav in the gloss: and some dispute, etc.; and although Beit Yosef brought in name of Mahari ibn Chabib that Rambam also holds thus, and what he wrote "he stirs the whole pot" is not that he stirs afterward but comes to teach that when it is known on which it fell he stirs only until that piece is mixed among the pieces; and when not known, immediately when he saw the drop fall into the pot and did not pay attention to which piece it fell, he should hurry to stir or cover immediately so the piece will mix — nevertheless the Rav did not refrain from writing in language of "and some dispute" since the plain meaning does not imply this, although Tur also did not understand thus and likewise understood Rambam's words like Tur and the Rav; and this is primary in Rambam's words, as explained, and Bach explained Rambam's words like Mahari ibn Chabib's explanation (except he unnecessarily attributed to Rambam that he holds like R' Yehuda); and he wrote on the Rav: may his Master forgive him — and per my humble opinion the Rav's view is correct, as I wrote; but what Beit Yosef wrote that Rambam deals when the pieces are in the gravy and nevertheless stirring is required to mix well — is hard: if so what is "and likewise," and further this law comes to teach even when known on which piece it fell; and further gravy certainly mixes all pieces — why stir; and one should not challenge Mahari ibn Chabib on falling into gravy — one may say it deals when there is not sixty in the gravy itself but in combining the pieces he stirs them in the gravy; requires study. Or the question is: and likewise if it fell into the pot or into the pieces or into the gravy and it is not known on which piece it fell or whether it fell into the gravy — and he mentioned gravy generally because it matters for two pots — requires study. However the law is tangled per Mahari ibn Chabib's explanation; therefore the words are as their plain meaning, and Rambam holds that since it is not known on which piece it fell we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah but the whole pot enters doubt and therefore he stirs the whole pot and the whole pot joins to nullify — similar to what Rashba and Tur wrote siman 111: there were two pots and issur fell into one and he does not know which — and neither alone can nullify it but both join to nullify since whatever will mix in the future is viewed as already mixed — end of his words; and this is similar here; and so Orach Chaim general rule 29 din 4: where the issur is not clarified we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah. I later found Orach Chaim wrote similarly on Rambam's words: and what he wrote "and likewise if it fell," etc. — because since it is not known which piece it is, one cannot say chatichah na'asah nevelah on it and cannot forbid everything since there is no noten ta'am in milk — end of his words; clearly as I wrote; and this is clear Rambam's view per my humble opinion; and one should not challenge how can he stir the whole pot lest there be taste in one piece and how nullify issur l'chatchila — one may say since it is not known on which it fell the whole pot enters doubt and there is no issue of nullifying issur; and it is like what Maharai wrote in Torat HaEsh siman 171: this is not nullifying issur l'chatchila since it is doubtful whether any issur will mix at all — end of his words; I brought this in siman 84 s.k. 38 and below siman 196 s.k. 28; and here too it is doubtful whether there is taste in this piece; and it appears Tur also understood Rambam thus — but holds since it can be established through gentile tasting each piece, if so not everything enters doubt because tasting each piece will clarify — and since one piece would certainly be clarified as forbidden, what does stirring afterward help; and per Rashba's view one cannot clarify which pot it fell into — Rashba certainly deals only in min b'mino where one cannot establish through taste, as he brought this law in Torat HaBayit from terumah where the measure is a kav in min b'mino — see there.
