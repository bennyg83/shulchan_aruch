Milk fell into a meat pot — they taste the piece on which the milk fell; if there is no milk taste, everything is permitted; if the piece imparts milk taste, that piece is forbidden. (For us who do not rely on gentiles, we require sixty in the piece, and if not the whole thing is forbidden.) And they estimate in all of it: if everything in the pot — from the piece, vegetables, gravy, and seasoning — is enough that this piece is one-sixtieth of all, the piece is forbidden and the rest permitted. When is this said? When he did not stir the pot at the start when the milk fell but only at the end, and did not cover. But if he stirred from beginning to end, or covered from the time of falling until the end, everything joins to nullify the milk taste. {Rama: Also if he did not stir at all — neither at start nor end — and did not cover at all: if there is sixty in the pot against the drop of milk that fell, only the piece alone is forbidden and the rest of the pot is permitted (Beit Yosef in the name of Raavad and Maggid Mishneh in the name of Rambam). Also if he stirred at the beginning or covered immediately, even though he did not stir or cover at the end, the whole pot joins — provided he stirred and covered immediately when the issur fell there (Tur and Beit Yosef in the name of R' Yehuda ibn Chabib).} And likewise if it fell into the gravy or into pieces and it is not known on which piece it fell — he stirs the whole pot until everything is mixed again; if the whole pot has milk taste it is forbidden, and if not, permitted. If no gentile is found to taste and we rely on him, they estimate by sixty (end of Rambam's words). {Rama: And some dispute and hold that stirring the pot does not help unless he stirred immediately when the issur fell (Tur) — and so is the custom.}
