And if he stirred from beginning to end. The Mechaber ruled like Rambam that one must stir from beginning to end, and Rama wrote in the gloss: and some say stirring at the beginning alone suffices, and so is primary; and it is hard, for Tur wrote on Rambam's words that it appears one need only stir or cover immediately at the time of falling, and even if he ceased afterward — and Mahari ibn Chabib wrote that Rambam's view is also thus; and what he wrote stirring from beginning to end was because at the outset he had to mention stirring at the end — for otherwise why would everything in the pot be forbidden — and therefore he also mentioned stirring at the end in the conclusion; and per this the Mechaber should not have been silent and should have explained that stirring at the beginning alone suffices — but he followed his view in Beit Yosef that Rambam requires stirring at beginning and end, and the Rav in the gloss that stirring at the beginning alone suffices; and this is what he wrote: and likewise if he stirred at the beginning, etc.; and he did not write this law in language of "some say" because the Mechaber's words can also be explained thus, as Mahari ibn Chabib explained Rambam's words; but the Mechaber's own view is not so; nevertheless the view of Mahari ibn Chabib and the Rav is primary, and so from Rashi (daf 109a) — see there; and so Maharshal ch. 25 siman 45 and Acharonim; and it appears Tur did not intend to challenge but to explain Rambam's words.
And if he did not stir at all. The Mechaber wrote: if there is sixty in the pot against the drop of milk that fell, it forbids only the piece alone. One might say when there is not sixty in the pot it should also be permitted since he did not stir at all and it only imparted taste in that piece itself — as in the Gemara and poskim, and explicitly in Raavad's challenges ch. 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot — see there; and Darkei Moshe brings it briefly; and it appears what requires sixty in the pot is when part is in the gravy, for the Rav is in doubt whether we hold like R' Yehuda who mixes taste in all or like Rashi — therefore the pot is permitted either way and the piece is forbidden lest he does not mix taste in all and the pot does not join.
And likewise if it fell into the pot. This is Rambam's language, and Tur wrote: I do not understand his words — if the pieces are all in the gravy why need to stir, and if not in the gravy what does stirring help if he stirs afterward — end of his words; and the Rav in the gloss: and some dispute, etc.; and although Beit Yosef brought in name of Mahari ibn Chabib that Rambam also holds thus — nevertheless the Rav wrote in language of "some dispute" since the plain meaning does not imply this; and this is primary in Rambam's words as explained: since it is not known on which piece it fell, the whole pot enters doubt and joins to nullify — similar to two pots in siman 111.
And if he recognizes it he throws it away. When there is sixty against the piece; and if he does not recognize it the gravy is permitted when there is sixty in the pieces and gravy, and all the pieces are forbidden if the issur piece is fit to be honored — for we hold below siman 101 that in basar b'chalav it is issur from the substance itself; and if fit to be honored it is not nullified in a thousand; and if not fit to be honored it is nullified in majority as below siman 109 — see there.
