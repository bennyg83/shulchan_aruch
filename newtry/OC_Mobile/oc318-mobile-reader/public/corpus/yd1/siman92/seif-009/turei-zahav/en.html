But milk of yours, etc. Meaning from its mouth in the place where the wick burns — that side is considered through the fire itself; so Hagahot Sh"D in name of Maharai — automatically if it dripped from another side of the vessel where the wick is inside, it is not called boiling to forbid what drips on it; and greater than this he wrote there in the hagahah in name of Maharam: if a hot drop of milk fell on a knife or other cold vessel, one needs only scraping where the drop fell, since the drop ceased at the time of falling and did not come in a splash; and specifically when it falls on wood, but if it fell on iron stab ten times in the ground and enough — end of his words; and he distinguishes there between this law of Maharam, which is hot from boiling by fire, and the law of "milk of yours" above, which is from the fire itself — so is the explanation of this matter. Written in Maharil Laws of Pesach: one who purifies by iruy who pours boiling water on the vessel to purify it should beware to pour the boiling water specifically from the vessel in which it was cooked, which is called kli rishon, and not draw from the vessel containing the boiling water with another vessel and pour from it to purify, because the drawing vessel with it is kli sheini and the boiling water was nullified in cooking before he purifies with it; however if he leaves the drawing vessel within the vessel in which they cook the boiling water, and while it is there raises boiling water — it is properly called also kli rishon — end of his words. From these words it implies that if one draws from kli rishon and removes boiling water, it is afterward called kli sheini and has the law of iruy; and I wondered greatly at this, for behold while still in kli rishon the vessel one inserts in it is also kli rishon, for we purify vessels in kli rishon and discharge from them what is absorbed; and so we hold regarding inserting a forbidden spoon into a pot that forbids the spoon even if he removes the spoon from it — and how can one say here that when he removes the drawing vessel from kli rishon it becomes kli sheini because kli rishon name came upon it; who gives it another name afterward — behold kli rishon is its name even if removed from the fire, so too what was removed from boiling water inside kli rishon. And further, if he waited there a bit, Maharil concedes the name kli rishon remains — and if so who measures how long the waiting should be, as Tur wrote in Orach Chaim siman 452: Rif wrote one leaves vessels in the yorah until they discharge — and Rabbenu Avraham Azriel wrote who can measure, etc. — so too we may say here who can measure the waiting in this. And further proof from ch. Kirah: R' Shmuel bar Avdimi said: I entered after Rabbi to the bathhouse on Shabbat, etc., and he told him take with a kli sheini so the heat lessens; and do not err and say he means take with kli sheini from the bath which is kli rishon and we learn the drawing vessel is kli sheini — this is not so, for then he should have said simply take a vessel and give; and further it is clarified that what he said kli sheini refers to take — meaning at the time of taking it should be kli sheini; and certainly one cannot say thus, for at the time of taking from boiling water it is kli rishon since it is inside it; and also he told him it should be kli sheini after it leaves the water, for he said take kli sheini — learn he spoke at the time of taking and not about removing afterward; and further when he removed it, who put kli sheini name on it — can every kli rishon after it cools become kli sheini? And further Tosafot wrote there the main distinction between kli rishon and kli sheini is that kli sheini cools because its walls are not hot — therefore it goes on cooling; and this certainly has walls boiling, for they were heated in kli rishon's boiling water — only a simple matter: Rabbi said take from the water with some object and put it in a kli sheini — meaning pour it into a kli sheini like every kli sheini in the Talmud; and know that if there were novelty here that kli rishon becomes kli sheini, he would have stated this novelty where we learn from it that kli sheini does not cook well; also Tur in Orach Chaim siman 318 when he brought this law of take kli sheini, etc., wrote only if it was hot in kli sheini one may put into it, etc., and did not write that one draws from kli rishon and nevertheless puts into it — rather plainly this is not so; and explicit proof further from what Agudah brought in Orach Chaim general rule 60: a fly found in a bowl is permitted, for it is a double doubt: perhaps it fell in kli sheini, and even if you say kli rishon, perhaps when they removed the fly — namely in the spoon — there was sixty in the spoon — end of his words; and likewise regarding removing a forbidden egg in a spoon, and so Shach and Tur regarding removing a fly in a spoon siman 107 — behold they required sixty in the gravy in the spoon against the fly, meaning the spoon is considered kli rishon even after removing from the pot, for all the time it was in the pot there is no issur from the fly because there was sixty there, only after removing it in the spoon it forbids; and on this they said perhaps there was sixty in the spoon — learn the spoon still has kli rishon name even after removing; and so proven from Rama's ruling in siman 107: but if a little of the food was with the issur in the spoon and returned to the pot, sixty is needed also against the little food, for it became nevelah in the spoon — learn the spoon is kli rishon in what it draws from the pot and forbids the gravy in it until sixty. And I again saw in Terumat HaDeshen siman 185 regarding absorption inside the spoon becoming nevelah: one may say after removing the spoon from the boiling gravy, all absorption inside it is considered like kli sheini and neither discharged nor absorbed — all the more per the view that absorbed issur does not forbid absorbed heter; it appears to reconcile this with what I wrote: there he removes an empty spoon from the pot and there is only what is absorbed in it — in this we certainly say there is not so much heat in an empty vessel to discharge and absorb in what is absorbed in the absorption; and even in actual kli rishon removed from fire and emptied it appears to say thus — not so when there is still boiling gravy or food inside, certainly the spoon has kli rishon name like the pot; otherwise certainly there is no reason Terumat HaDeshen would dispute Shach, Tur, and Agudah I cited — rather certainly as I wrote. And I again saw in Tosafot ch. Am MiMazikin daf 33 s.v. kinisa: R' Yehuda would say it is a mitzvah min hamuvchar to purify each board alone and throw on it from that vessel itself that is boiling on the fire and that suffices, although boiling ceased when moving it from the fire; and some place a bowl inside a pan on the fire until boiling rises in the bowl and from that bowl they purify — and in this R' Yehuda is uncertain whether this helps as kli rishon or not, and left the matter to be stringent and forbade purifying with them, considering them kli sheini; and for melichah of chicken too it is forbidden, considering it kli rishon — end of his words. Behold Tosafot were uncertain here and raised to stringency; except Rosh there wrote in name of R' Yehuda that it is like kli rishon since he removed it from the yorah boiling; and so Tur in Yoreh De'ah siman 131 in name of R' Yehuda; and one must say they had a different text in Tosafot; and from Rama's words per Hagahot Maimoniyot in Orach Chaim siman 451 it implies he ruled stringently that it is not kli rishon, for he wrote there: and pour on them boiling water from kli rishon, etc. — therefore it appears to be stringent like Tosafot's words.
