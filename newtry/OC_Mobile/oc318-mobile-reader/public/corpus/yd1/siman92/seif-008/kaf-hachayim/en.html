And if it fell into the gravy or pieces and it is not known which piece — stir the whole pot, etc. — Rambam's language; Tur wrote he does not understand: if all pieces are in gravy, why stir? If not in gravy, what does stirring help afterward? — and this is what the gloss says: some dispute, etc. Rambam's reason in Beit Yosef: even if it fell in gravy, one must stir so it mixes well; all the more if it fell on pieces in gravy. Therefore it appears the case is all in gravy; still Rambam requires stirring to mix the milk. Other poskim offered other explanations. For practice, Zivchei Tzedek section 7: if pieces are in gravy, since we follow Shulchan Aruch we require stirring; b'dieved if he did not stir, still permitted since they are in gravy. If pieces are outside gravy or partly outside, per Rama's view we do not permit unless sixty against the drop in each piece; if any piece outside has no sixty, it is forbidden and all else permitted — see there. And if he stirred afterward, the forbidden piece may forbid the rest until sixty against it — see Zivchei Tzedek there; but if part was in gravy, one may permit the rest even without sixty against the piece — double doubt whether milk fell on it, whether we rule like R' Yehuda, etc. — see there and siman 110 on double doubt.
