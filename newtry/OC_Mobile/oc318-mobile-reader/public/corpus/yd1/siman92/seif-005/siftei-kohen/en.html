A drop of milk, etc. So wrote Semak: a drop that fell on the vessel wall — we are in doubt in ch. Dam Chatat whether it steams through the whole vessel or not; therefore when it fell opposite the gravy and there is sixty in the gravy from the drop it is permitted either way — if it steams through the whole vessel it is nullified, and if it does not steam then it should not forbid (meaning if it does not steam through the whole vessel it should not forbid because presumably it steams inward and is as if it fell in the food — so Beit Yosef explains); but when it fell in the empty place, perhaps it steams on the pot wall until near the gravy so there is not sixty from the drop, and that part of the vessel becomes nevelah, and when he pours the food, etc. — end of his words; and per these words they are the Mechaber's words, only abbreviated — and see siman 121 s.k. 17.
Only sixty against the drop. Meaning to permit the food, but the pot is nevertheless forbidden and he should pour the food immediately on the other side — as the Rav wrote in seif 6, and so Terumat HaChacham beginning general rule 56.
Then the food becomes forbidden. It deals when there is not sixty in the food against that empty place — namely sixty times sixty-one slightly less against the drop, for the drop is forbidden until sixty; therefore with the drop there is sixty-one slightly less. It appears poskim who wrote sixty of sixty drops like it suffices is not precise — requires study; and it appears one may not pour the food through that place of the pot, for one may not nullify issur l'chatchila; and even in a new pot it appears forbidden to pour lest perhaps it only steams in part of the vessel and was not nullified at first and now when pouring it will be nullified. What the Rav wrote in the gloss that in a new pot in every case one needs only sixty against the drop — meaning if he poured b'dieved suffices with sixty against the drop alone; but l'chatchila he may not pour — and this is not as he wrote "but if it is a new pot in every case permitted to pour" — for he refers to the Mechaber's words; but certainly as I wrote.
And he should not touch it until it cools. But piercing the vessel from below does not help — we fear perhaps the gravy will touch the wall forbidden through shaking — so Semak and poskim; and it appears also that pouring immediately on the other side does not help lest the pot shake — so Semak and poskim there, and Orach Chaim, and Maharshal in his book siman 37.
But if it is new, etc. Per the Rav's gloss siman 98 seif 5 one might be stringent to say even a new earthenware vessel chatichah na'asah nevelah — therefore here specifically for a drop of milk we say thus because all is still permitted; not for a drop of issur; but it is already explained siman 98 s.k. 20 that we cannot say the earthenware body becomes nevelah; if so here even for a drop of issur the law is thus; but in an old pot what is absorbed is chatichah na'asah nevelah — specifically an old pot beit yomo, for otherwise what is in the pot walls does not become nevelah since noten ta'am li'fegam, as below siman 98 — requires study.
As below siman 94 regarding the ladle. And even though it appears no distinction for the ladle — since it is new and he stirred afterward in the pot he should need only against what it absorbed — but here the pot is considered like old since they now cook meat in it and milk fell during cooking — nevertheless since you come to forbid the pot wall due to the drop, one must say perhaps cooking does not steam through the whole vessel and then the food above did not steam and if it steamed above the drop also spreads below; or say it deals with a new pot where the drop fell before the food was hot, or after the drop fell on a pot of water they put meat in it — requires study.
