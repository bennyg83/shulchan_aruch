The drop. Shach wrote: this is to permit the food but the pot is nevertheless forbidden and he should pour the food immediately on another side — so Semak: a drop that fell on the vessel wall — we are in doubt in ch. Dam Chatat whether it steams through the whole vessel or not; therefore when it fell opposite the gravy and there is sixty in the gravy from the drop it is permitted either way — if it steams through the whole vessel it is nullified, and if it does not steam then it should not forbid, meaning if it does not steam through the whole vessel it should not forbid because presumably it steams inward and is as if it fell in the food. (But I have difficulty: per this reasoning why is the pot forbidden — we should also say either way; and likewise why is it obvious that if it steams through the whole pot certainly the drop is nullified — we do not know how much discharges from it; therefore it appears as I wrote.) Taz wrote: if there is sixty in the food and it fell opposite the food it is permitted either way — if it steams in all of it, since it has steaming it enters inward into the food and the food nullifies it; if it does not steam, meaning it does not steam at all but stands in its place, there is no issur at all since it does not spread from its place and does not enter inward — Semak did not use language "if it steams through the whole pot" only because it follows Talmudic language; but in truth Semak's primary intent is as I wrote — and per this the pot is also permitted either way; if it steams inward it is nullified in sixty, and if it does not steam it should not forbid the second food cooked in it from the first. (But if it fell opposite the empty place we fear it steams a large part of the pot until near the gravy and perhaps a very large part spreads so there is not sixty in the food against that part — therefore forbidden to pour through that place. In this part of the pot the issur remains forever and nullification does not help — therefore do not touch until it cools.) Therefore Maharshal ch. 25 wrote in this even the pot is permitted from law to cook meat again, only l'chatchila forbidden to use it even for meat that appears as if forbidden in milk.
Place. Meaning that part of the vessel became nevelah.
Its remedy. Shach wrote: but piercing the vessel from below or pouring from another side does not help — we fear perhaps the gravy will touch the wall forbidden through shaking; and it deals when there is not sixty in the food against that empty place — sixty times sixty-one slightly less against the drop, for the drop is forbidden until sixty; therefore with the drop there is sixty-one slightly less. It appears nevertheless one may not pour the food through that place of the pot, for one may not nullify issur l'chatchila; and even in a new pot it appears forbidden to pour lest perhaps it only steams in part of the vessel and was not nullified at first and now when pouring it will be nullified. What the Rav wrote in the gloss that in a new pot in every case one needs only sixty against the drop — meaning if he poured b'dieved. (Peri Chadash wrote: however if this vessel where the drop fell is not normally used for a small amount but only in abundance, it is permitted to use it, for impossible to reach noten ta'am as below siman 99 and 122.)
Ladle. Shach wrote: and even though it appears there is no distinction for the ladle — since it is new and he stirred afterward in the pot he should need only against what it absorbed — but here the pot is considered like old since they now cook meat in it and milk fell on it during cooking — nevertheless since you come to forbid the pot wall due to the drop, one must say perhaps cooking does not steam through the whole vessel and then the food above did not steam and if it steamed above the drop also spreads below; or say it deals with a new pot where the drop fell before the food was hot, or after the drop fell on a pot of water they put meat in it — requires study. (Peri Chadash wrote: know all this deals with an uncovered pot; but if covered at the time of falling we do not distinguish between opposite food and empty place — in every case sixty against the drop suffices either way — if the drop does not steam inward it is not forbidden, and if it steams inward since the pot is covered everything rises from the bottom to the mouth and mixes the drop in the whole pot and nullifies in sixty — see there.) Per the Rav's gloss siman 98 seif 5 one might be stringent to say even a new earthenware vessel chatichah na'asah nevelah — therefore here specifically for a drop of milk we say thus because all is still permitted; not for a drop of issur — but it is already explained siman 98 s.k. 20 that we cannot say the earthenware body becomes nevelah, and therefore here even for a drop of issur the law is thus; but in an old pot what is absorbed in it is chatichah na'asah nevelah — specifically an old pot beit yomo, otherwise what is in the pot walls does not become nevelah since noten ta'am li'fegam, as below siman 98.
