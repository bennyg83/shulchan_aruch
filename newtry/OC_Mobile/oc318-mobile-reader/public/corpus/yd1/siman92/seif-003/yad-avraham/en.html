(There in Shach s.k. 16) And regarding whether we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in salting, etc. — there is room to permit for great loss, etc., and so is primary. And he explained the reason well in his long book that I brought siman 105. And Mahari Weil general rule 38 s.k. 5 did not see that book, and therefore attacked him for nothing. And this — that he did not see his long book — also caused him to leave Darkei Moshe's words in siman 105 seif 8 in requires study, for it implies he is lenient also in roasting. And per what he wrote there, one may be lenient also in roasting as I wrote there. Tur wrote per Rabbenu Tam's view who holds we say chatichah na'asah nevelah in other issurim and one must estimate sixty against the whole piece — specifically what was forbidden through noten ta'am; but what was forbidden in any amount did not become all nevelah — end of his words. It appears to me proven explicitly in Tosafot and Rosh in ch. Gid HaNasheh (daf 100) and Ran in ch. Kol HaBasar and in Rosh ch. 2 of Avodat Kochavim and Tur and Shulchan Aruch siman 134 seif 4: a thing forbidden due to any amount does not become nevelah. And if it afterward fell in other heter, one need not estimate except according to the measure of the first issur. And even wet in wet that mixed well — behold there is here literally an amount of the first issur. Even so one need not estimate against the whole mixture. And it is not comparable to what Taz wrote regarding chametz on Pesach, that we say two amounts in wet in wet — for there it is different, for also in this mixture the first issur forbids in any amount, and behold there is here an amount from it; but if in this mixture the first issur does not forbid in any amount but in noten ta'am, one needs that it impart taste in it. And so is proven explicitly in Asheri ch. 2 of Avodat Kochavim regarding wine offered to idolatry that fell into a pit of permitted wine — it was forbidden in any amount, and afterward a pitcher of water fell there — we say it removed its species as it is not, and min in not its species is great upon it and nullifies it. And Rosh wrote, and these are his words: and that which we do not say here chatichah na'asah nevelah and water would need to nullify permitted wine and forbidden wine — because it did not fall from the forbidden wine in the measure of imparting taste in the heter, only in any amount, and every thing forbidden in any amount — we do not say chatichah na'asah nevelah — end of his words. Beit Yosef brings it in this siman and siman 134. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 4 — see there. And there is practical difference regarding a thing that has permitters, that forbids in its species in any amount and not in its species in noten ta'am, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 102. And if it mixed in its species and was forbidden in any amount, and this mixture mixed in not its species whose measure is noten ta'am — the first issur must impart taste in it, and one need not estimate against the whole mixture, only according to the calculation of the first issur in it. However, if the mixture remains visibly in its forbidden state forever — for this is so even per R' Eliezer, as Tosafot and Rosh in ch. Gid HaNasheh and Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 106 wrote — this is clear. And again I saw in book Alei Zahav in Orach Chaim siman 447 that he challenged this reasoning of Rosh and left it in requires study; in my humble opinion it works out: nevertheless it does not forbid in any amount to the water, for it is not stricter than if now the forbidden wine fell into the water.
