Sixty. Meaning sixty in the food against the drop alone. Maharshal wrote nevertheless the pot is forbidden. Shach wrote: leave it to cool if possible; but if needed immediately for guests etc. need not wait until cool but pour on another side; and same for any great need, great loss, or poor person per the teacher's judgment. Responsum R' Moshe Minz siman 15 wrote the custom is to tell them the reason why he is lenient so they are not astonished why sometimes he forbids and sometimes permits. Taz wrote in name of Orach Chaim: if a drop of milk fell on the pot with water by the fire and within me'eit l'eit they cooked meat in it — even if there was not sixty in the water against the drop the meat is permitted, for if it did not pass first into the pot also the second time it will not pass, and the meat is permitted b'dieved; and even if it passes inward first, there is only milk in the pot according to the estimated amount in the water, for in permitted we do not consider the whole piece as absorption itself and against that small matter presumably there is sixty in the meat. (I found in Torat HaEsh: if a drop fell on the vessel's ear the food is not forbidden.)
Hot. Excluding cold, for then even the pot is permitted.
Prevails. Taz wrote: this deals specifically if spilled on the stove place that is not hot from the fire and yad soledes bo does not apply there; but if spilled on the stove by the fire and it is hot yad soledes bo there, the milk in it is like kli rishon and needs sixty from what we estimate of milk in appearance under the pot. Shach wrote: it appears from Maharshal's words that if the pot boils from the fire's heat even the spill is forbidden even the food if there is not sixty against what is beneath it. (Specifically when there is no ash where the milk spilled, for if there is ash the spilled milk on the ash becomes for detriment and does not forbid as below siman 95 seif 21 — Mahari Italia.)
Like iruy. Meaning we do not say it cooled because it flowed on the stove; however this is specifically when yad soledes bo (Peri Chadash wrote per siman 98 we do not distinguish whether the stream broke — any heat that is yad soledes bo forbids — see there).
Kelipah. Shach wrote: therefore even if there is food in the pot where kelipah remains one need not peel, for the pot is like kelipah.
For the lower. Shach wrote: and no distinction to what is written siman 105 seif 3 in the gloss that if permitted and issur rest together and one is cold, kelipah suffices — here since the stream comes from above it is like the upper; or since the pot stands in place it is considered lower; or here it splashes on the pot as Bach end siman 91. (Peri Chadash wrote: specifically when it splashes on the pot; but if milk dripped under the meat pot the milk has the law of lower and does not forbid the food in the pot — but per Rashal and Shach I wrote below that if the pot boils from the fire even cold spill forbids the food until sixty — see there; therefore even if milk dripped under the pot it also forbids the food; and per Taz's words if the stove place is hot yad soledes bo the milk is like kli rishon and forbids the food too — requires study.) Or it deals here that it splashes on the pot; and Taz wrote to forbid where there is no great loss even if it does not splash on the pot, for we hold even in salting we forbid where there is no great loss when they touch one another — all the more here with heat through fire.
To boil. Terumat HaDeshen wrote: it appears if it did not begin to boil and fell on the cover, what is in the pot is not forbidden at all even without sixty, for it is still like cold and does not steam into what is in the pot. Taz wrote: it appears regarding the cover itself it is considered boiling since it rests on the pot by the fire and is forbidden. Maharshal disagrees with Rama and wrote: although boiling rises to the cover, the gravy does not join with it — if there is not sixty on the cover against the issur that fell on it the cover becomes nevelah. It appears to me the pot opposite the gravy differs because the gravy itself is always there, not so the cover — perhaps at the time the drop fell boiling did not reach the cover itself, and a Torah doubt is stringent; only in rabbinic issur one may be lenient.
