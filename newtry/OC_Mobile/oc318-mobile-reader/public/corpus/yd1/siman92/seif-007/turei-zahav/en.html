Such as erev Shabbat, etc. Terumat HaChacham general rule 55 wrote the same for any great need such as for guests, great loss, or a poor person per the teacher's judgment — end of his words; and it is written in responsum Mahari Minz siman 15 that the custom is to tell them the reason why he is lenient so they are not astonished why sometimes he forbids and sometimes permits.
Even not opposite the fire. Maharshal wrote ch. Gid HaNasheh siman 37: nevertheless the pot is forbidden, lest it did not steam inward and the place of the drop is forbidden — therefore pour it from another side and one need not wait until cooling since it is erev Shabbat.
Through sixty. Meaning sixty in the food against the drop alone; Orach Chaim general rule 31 wrote: if a drop of milk fell on the pot with water by the fire and within me'eit l'eit they cooked meat in it, even if there was not sixty in the water against the drop the meat is permitted, for if it did not pass first into the pot, also the second time it will not pass, and the meat is permitted b'dieved; and even if it passes inward first, there is only milk in the pot according to the estimated amount in the water, for in permitted we do not consider the whole piece as absorption itself and against that small matter presumably there is sixty in the meat.
And if milk spilled, etc. So Hagahot Sh"D in name of Maharam; and Orach Chaim general rule 31 wrote in this in name of R' Yitzchak of Corbeil that sixty is required from what we estimate of milk in appearance under the pot — end of his words; and Maharshal ch. 25 siman 38 wrote this ruling did not appear correct to him, for it is only kli sheini; and he wrote one may distinguish where the pot stands by the fire, that the fire's heat in the pot boils the lower from cooling and absorbs well; and so appears primary — end of his words; and per my humble opinion without boiling of the pot there is no difficulty, for since it was heated by the fire and is hot where yad soledes bo applies, the milk in it is like kli rishon; and Maharam who permits deals when it spilled in a place of heating that is not hot from the fire and yad soledes bo does not apply there; and so appears primary for law.
It is also like kli sheini. Per my humble opinion this too is specifically when that place is not boiling and yad soledes bo does not apply, for otherwise it is like kli rishon, as I wrote below.
And if the stream did not break, etc. Terumat HaDeshen brought in Beit Yosef who wrote: if yad soledes bo in the milk where it touched the meat, it is like iruy from kli rishon and the meat is forbidden; and Beit Yosef wrote on this: and it appears the meat was cold, for otherwise why need he say the milk is considered iruy — behold even completely cold milk forbids the meat, for it is cold into hot; and since so, that which he said the meat is forbidden is only kedei kelipah, for hot into cold only forbids kedei kelipah — end of his words; Beit Yosef holds what touched the milk at the pot is the pot below and the milk above — therefore he is stringent with hot meat and cold milk to forbid all of it, and lenient with cold meat and hot milk that it only forbids kedei kelipah; and after this explanation Rama followed here; and per my humble opinion it is astonishing, for whatever touches the pot there is neither upper nor lower, but they have the law of touching each other, and its law is explained in siman 105 seif 3 that if one is cold and the other hot it only forbids kedei kelipah; and so I found in Maharshal ch. Gid HaNasheh siman 38 who compares this to touching — only he is stringent in touching as will be explained in siman 105; and therefore he challenged Terumat HaDeshen that I mentioned: why need the milk be boiling — behold since the meat is boiling it is forbidden as the law of cold and hot touching per his view; and he resolved that the milk flowed under the meat pot — behold the milk is lower and does not forbid if it were cold; per our view there is leniency if we say it is like touching each other, except one may not be lenient in what Beit Yosef and Rama were stringent; nevertheless per my humble opinion, since Rama wrote in siman 105 seif 10 that even in salting one should forbid where there is no loss in touching each other, all the more here with heat by fire one must be stringent here too, even with cold meat and hot milk where there is no great loss — as it appears to me.
And that the pot began to boil, etc. Terumat HaChacham general rule 55 concluded on this: and it appears to me if it did not begin to boil and fell on the cover, what is in the pot is not forbidden at all even without sixty, for it is still like cold and does not steam into what is in the pot — end of his words; and it appears regarding the cover itself it is considered boiling since it rests on the pot by the fire — and so one may explain Terumat HaChacham's language. And regarding the main law Rama wrote here, its law is like falling on the pot wall — Maharshal ch. Kol HaBasar siman 46 disputes and wrote: although boiling rises to the cover, nevertheless the gravy does not join with it — if there is not sixty on the cover against the issur that fell on it the cover becomes nevelah — end of his words; and so it appears to my humble opinion, for the pot opposite the gravy differs because the gravy itself is always there, not so the cover — perhaps at the time the drop fell boiling did not reach the cover itself; and a Torah doubt is stringent — only in rabbinic issur one may be lenient — as it appears to me.
