Of the permitted. Taz wrote: in Mordechai the reason is because grease in honey is noten ta'am lifgam, and he concludes there: but if it were for benefit it would forbid; and Beit Yosef holds the reason here is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am, and there is a difference in Mordechai's reason: even if there were actual meat in the pan in which they cooked the honey, then it would be forbidden and not noten ta'am bar noten ta'am; nevertheless here it is permitted because it is noten ta'am lifgam; if so Shulchan Aruch should not have omitted Mordechai's reason — end; and Shach wrote it is clear the case is they poured it by pouring and the stream did not stop; otherwise obviously permitted as above siman 98 seif 6; if so one must say only the honey is permitted but the bowl is forbidden per Rama's gloss in siman 95 seif 3; and one cannot say it is nevertheless permitted per Mordechai's noten ta'am lifgam, for Rama wrote in siman 103 seif 4 that where there is no great loss one should be stringent that grease in honey is not noten ta'am lifgam; possibly the case is a beverage made from honey, where all agree noten ta'am lifgam; nevertheless from Mechaber's language it implies even the bowl is permitted; perhaps since here there is a double advantage — even if cooked much, many poskim permit eating them in a dip, and some hold grease in honey is noten ta'am lifgam — permitted per Rama even for the bowl; and see siman 103 seif 2 in the gloss.
