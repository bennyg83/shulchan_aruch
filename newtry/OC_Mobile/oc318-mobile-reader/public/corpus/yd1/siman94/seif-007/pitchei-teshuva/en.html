It is kli sheni. Beit HaTosafot; and see in Solut LehMincha general rule 23 who wrote in Torat Eish he was inclined to uphold Maharshal's view; but see responsum Chasam Sofer siman 95 who expanded on this and wrote that in principal law there is not so much relevance, for we are accustomed to be stringent in all vessels even kli shlishi as long as yad soledes bo is in it, as Peri Chadash wrote in end siman 92 if not in great loss; and there are further views to permit; nevertheless if possible to kasher vessels and leave earthenware vessels to stand, we require kashering and standing, and sometimes also to boil kli sheni three times in boiling water after standing, as Tur siman 121 in the name of Ittur: whatever can be done in heter we do — then it means in great loss; and certainly when we do all the leniencies mentioned in great loss, even in solid matter Rama's view may be relied on, even in basar b'chalav where taste is like the essence it is stricter; nevertheless here it is certainly not full cooking as Minchat Yaakov proved end general rule 61, etc.; and all the more in other issurim that are not so strict everywhere — there is room to rely on the above. In principal law it is difficult from Tosafot Zevachim 96, etc., and so I received from my teachers the Gaon z"l to be lenient in solid matter in time of need — end; see there. And what he wrote that we are accustomed to be stringent in all vessels even kli shlishi, etc. — our teachers do not practice so; see Atzei Zeitim and Shach siman 105 and Peri Megadim there. And what he wrote at the end "and all the more in other issurim," etc. — at first glance it is not all the more; on the contrary in other issurim one should be stricter, for it does not depend on cooking method — there may be Torah concern — investigate.]
