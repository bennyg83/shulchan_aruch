Against the place of the knife, etc. Tur wrote in the name of R' Peretz: against the whole knife; and Shulchan Aruch did not bring it, for R' Peretz follows the view at the beginning of this siman regarding metal: part hot is all hot, and we do not rule so; therefore only against the place of cutting is required; but Rashal perek Gid HaNasheh siman 42 wrote one must estimate against the whole knife — the blade outside the handle, for everyone who cuts with a knife does not precisely measure how far he cut; and even if he says "clearly I only cut here" he is not believed, for every matter not dependent on him a person is not mindful — therefore one must estimate against the whole knife except the handle.
It does not forbid except the amount of a peel. Tur likewise in the name of R' Peretz; and from Tur's arrangement here it seems there is a textual error, and it should read: if it is ben yomo — if not, for presumably it is not ben yomo, since default vessels are not ben yomo — permitted, but a peel is required; and so in Hagahot Semak, and he wrote the reason is grease on the knife — default knives have grease on them. And it is difficult at first glance: in the first part, if there is sixty against the knife's place, why is a peel needed here if there is sixty against that grease on the knife — and what does a peel help if it carries taste throughout like the first part? Me'orei Or siman 105 wrote: if there is no sixty against the drop, all is forbidden even though the issur is slight; except it is clear that certainly there is sixty against the issur, or se'ah terumah in a hundred; but the difference is: in the first part, where the issur is from the knife's emission, one had to say it forbids up to sixty; but in the second part, permitted from the knife, only from grease on it, and it is a slight matter and there is sixty in the measure of a peel against it — therefore he did not need to mention sixty here since in any case a peel is required; and even in the first part where there is sixty against the whole knife, nevertheless a peel is required at the place the issur fell — slight in roasting where the issur is more attached than elsewhere; therefore that place must be peeled, and from this Rashba learned for every roasting where slight issur fell — sixty and also a peel; and so in Mordechai end perek Gid HaNasheh on Pesach that a peel is required even more than sixty; and so Ran perek Kol HaBasar; and so ruled Shulchan Aruch siman 105 seif 5. For this intent Tur wrote in the name of R' Peretz that a peel is required — meaning also in the first part where there is sixty against the knife's place; and he did not divide into two sections, but taught us: when it is not known to be ben yomo we go to leniency and sixty is not needed against the knife's place, but against the grease in actual form it is clear there is sixty and he did not need to mention it, and he concluded "but a peel is required" — both of them; and Shulchan Aruch that brought in the second part "it forbids only a peel" intended this too. I further saw in Hagahot Ashiri perek Gid HaNasheh on roasting requiring removal: he wrote there likewise what we said in perek Keitzad that grease on the knife is a slight matter, as in "salting is different, a little," etc. — end. There too he speaks after there is sixty; only concern from the place of falling in roasting here a peel suffices and full removal of the place is not required — so appears to me plainly; if there is no sixty against grease on the knife, all is forbidden, and one who is lenient is only among the astonished.
And all this with hot meat, etc. Taz wrote likewise, and I wonder that Rama is accustomed to bring Orach Chaim in all places and here did not bring his words, as in general rule 36, to forbid if one cut hot meat with a dairy knife that is ben yomo from hot use — through knife pressure it absorbs throughout and sixty is required against the whole blade, etc.; and one may say for kli sheni that in dry matter there are no cooling sides and it cooks; nevertheless all this while yad soledes bo is in it; and if there is no sixty the meat is forbidden even in benefit; and in kli sheni there are also sides of the vessel that cool it constantly even though there are no sides of kli rishon that hold heat long — nevertheless it absorbs; however Orach Chaim is not in the printed Darkei Moshe. Behold before us that even if hot meat is placed on a strainer, he is stringent to forbid the meat too — as long as it is very hot after coming from kli rishon; and so Rashal perek 3 siman 44: kli sheni applies only to water and gravy and anything liquid that mixes immediately and cools quickly; not to a piece of meat or fish and anything solid, for as long as yad soledes bo is in it it stays hot and exudes and absorbs well; therefore it seems rice and porridge too after pouring from pot to bowl — as long as yad soledes bo is in them they are like kli rishon for basar b'chalav, e.g. if the spoon is ben yomo from meat and the porridge absorbed from milk, or the reverse — the food is forbidden from basar b'chalav and the spoon too, and forbids another pot if inserted, or if the forbidden spoon the dish is forbidden from absorption even without gravy; but if that dish was already forbidden from absorption, the spoon is not likewise forbidden without gravy, since the food does not forbid from itself; and all the more if the food is milk or meat the spoon is not likewise forbidden to forbid another pot afterward — end; and Darkei Moshe siman 105 brings Rashal's words and disagrees: it seems to the eye that hot gravy holds heat more than meat and a thick thing; and it seems to me there is no proof from this, for here it is lesser since there is knife pressure and it is like radish at the beginning of siman 96 that we forbid from taste. And so it appears to me to prove from R' Peretz's words in Tur on this law: some want to permit hot meat cut with a dairy knife through a peel, as we say regarding the place of shechitah, etc.; and if he deals with kli rishon, who would want to be lenient in kli rishon with a peel — if in kli rishon a peel would suffice, how could one find in the world to forbid all of it from taste? Rather plainly he deals with kli sheni; and nevertheless he is stringent to require sixty since yad soledes bo is in it; and if not, the whole piece is forbidden; and Me'orei Or wrote in siman 105: if one cuts hot meat with a dairy knife in kli sheni and yad soledes bo, the knife is forbidden because meat exudes in it, but the meat is not forbidden, for kli sheni has no power to exude the absorbed in the knife and absorb into the meat. And it seems to me to be stringent where there is knife pressure; but without knife pressure one may be lenient as Rama.
