Against the place of the knife, etc. In the Tur he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz: against the whole knife. The Shulchan Aruch did not copy this because Rabbeinu Peretz follows his own view at the beginning of this siman, that with metal, if part of it is hot, all of it is hot, and we do not rule so. Therefore one needs only against the place where he cut. But Maharshal, Perek Gid HaNasheh, siman 42, wrote that one must measure against the whole knife, meaning the blade excluding the handle, because anyone cutting with a knife is not precise about how much of the knife he cut with. Even if he says "I am certain I cut only with this place," he is not believed, for a matter not placed upon a person to notice is not in his mind; end quote.
It forbids only the amount of a peel. So wrote the Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz: but peeling is required. According to the arrangement of the Tur's language here, it appears there is a scribal error, and it should read: whether it is ben yomo or not; presumably it is not ben yomo, since ordinary utensils are not ben yomo, and it is permitted, but peeling is required. So it is in Hagahot Semak. He wrote there that the reason is because of fat on the knife, for an ordinary knife has fat upon it. This is difficult: it seems at first that in the first case, if there is sixty against the place of the knife, peeling is not required; why then is peeling required here if there is sixty against that fat on the knife? And if there is not sixty against it, what does peeling help, since it carries the taste throughout, as in the first case? My father-in-law z"l wrote that it speaks where there is not sixty, and even so it forbids only the amount of a peel because the prohibition is small. He brought proof from Perek Keitzad Tzolin, Pesachim 76, regarding one who smeared the Pesach offering with oil of terumah, where peeling suffices. The Gemara asks there that since the lower one overpowers, all of it should be forbidden, and answers: smearing is different, for it is merely something slight. His words are astonishing, for since the prohibition spreads through all of it, as it is in a kli rishon, if there is not sixty it is certainly not a slight prohibition, but large in proportion to the meat that is forbidden by it, which lacks sixty. There is also proof from Torat HaBayit HaArukh, bayit 4, end of sha'ar 4: know that anything that spreads, if it fell on a known place of the piece, even though there is sixty to nullify the prohibition, one must remove its place, as written in Perek Keitzad Tzolin: if some of its sauce dripped on earthenware and returned onto it, one removes its place; end quote. He himself wrote in Mishmeret HaBayit there that the reason it does not forbid all of it is because the drip is a small matter and does not have enough to forbid all, etc. We learn that if there is not sixty against the drip, all is forbidden although the prohibition is small. Rather, it is simple that certainly there is sixty against the prohibition, or in terumah one hundred and one. The distinction is that in the first case, where the prohibition is because of emission from the knife, he needed to say that it forbids up to sixty. But in the latter case, where it is not ben yomo, it is permitted from the knife's side, only because of the fat upon it, which is a small matter, and there is sixty in the measure of a peel against it. Therefore he did not need to mention sixty here, since in any event peeling is required in every case, even in the first case where there is sixty against the whole knife. Nevertheless, one needs peeling at the place where the small prohibition fell in roasted food, for there the prohibition is more attached and stuck than elsewhere, and therefore that place must be peeled. From this the Rashba learned for any roasted item on which a small prohibition fell, that it requires sixty and also peeling. So it is explicit in the Mordechai at the end of Perek Gid HaNasheh regarding the Pesach case, that peeling is required even with more than sixty. So wrote the Ran in Perek Kol HaBasar, and so it is ruled in Shulchan Aruch siman 105 seif 5. For this purpose the Tur wrote here in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz that peeling is required, meaning also in the first case where there is sixty against the place of the knife. He divided it into two clauses only to teach that where it is not known to be ben yomo we rule leniently and do not require sixty against the place of the knife; but against visible fat it is obvious there is sixty and he did not need to mention it. He concluded "but peeling is required" regarding both. The Shulchan Aruch, which copied in the latter clause "it forbids only the amount of a peel," also intended this.
Later I saw in the Ashri, Perek Gid HaNasheh, regarding roasted food requiring removal of a place, that he wrote: and likewise that which is said in the first chapter, according to the one who says the slaughter area is hot, one peels; there it is different because the fat on the knife is a small matter, as we say in Perek Keitzad Tzolin, smearing is different because it is something slight, etc.; end quote. There too it speaks after there is sixty, except that there is concern because of the place of falling; in that, peeling suffices and removal of a place is not required. This appears simple to my humble mind: if there is not sixty against the fat on the knife, all is forbidden, and one who is lenient in this is only among those who astonish.
All this is with hot meat, etc. Torat Chatat also wrote this. I am surprised that the Rav Rama usually brings Issur VeHeter in all places, yet here he did not bring his words in klal 36, where he wrote to forbid if one cut hot meat, yad soledet bo, with a dairy knife that was ben yomo from hot use: because of the pressure of the knife it absorbs throughout and requires sixty against the whole blade, etc. This is unlike a kli sheni, for with a dry item there are no cooling walls, and it cooks; however, this applies only while it is yad soledet bo. If there is not sixty, the meat is forbidden even in benefit. In a kli sheni there are walls of the vessel that constantly cool it, and although here there are also no walls of the kli rishon maintaining the heat for a long time, nevertheless it does absorb, etc.; end quote. Thus before us it is clear that even if the hot meat lies on a plate, he is stringent to forbid the meat as well, namely as long as it is very hot after coming from the kli rishon. So too are the words of Maharshal, Perek Gid HaNasheh, siman 44: kli sheni applies only to water, sauce, and any liquid thing that mixes immediately and cools quickly, but not to a piece of meat or fish or any solid item, for as long as it is yad soledet bo it remains in its heat and properly emits and absorbs. Therefore it appears that millet and rice too, after being poured from a pot into a bowl, as long as they are yad soledet bo, are like a kli rishon. The practical difference is for meat and milk: if the spoon is ben yomo from meat and the millet absorbed milk, or the reverse, the food is forbidden because of meat and milk, and the spoon also becomes forbidden and later forbids another pot if inserted into it. Alternatively, if the spoon is prohibited, the millet is forbidden from absorption of prohibition, for a utensil can forbid another item even without sauce. But if that food was already forbidden by absorption, the spoon is not forbidden without sauce, since the food does not forbid by virtue of itself. All the more so if the food is dairy or meat, the spoon does not become like it and become ben yomo to later forbid another pot, for it is no worse than something forbidden by absorption, which does not forbid without sauce, all the more so a permitted utensil; end quote. In Darkei Moshe siman 105 he brings these words of Maharshal and disagrees, writing that it appears to the eyes that boiling sauce retains heat more than meat or a thick item. To me this is no proof, for here it is worse because there is pressure of the knife, and it resembles radish at the beginning of siman 96, which we forbid by taste. So it appears to my humble mind to prove from Rabbeinu Peretz's language in the Tur in this law. He wrote: some want to permit hot meat cut with a dairy knife by peeling, as we say regarding the slaughter area, etc. If he is speaking of a kli rishon, who is the one who would be lenient with a kli rishon by peeling? If a kli rishon suffices with peeling, how would one ever find a case where all is forbidden by imparted taste? Rather, it is obvious that he speaks of a kli sheni, and even so he is stringent to require sixty because it is yad soledet bo; otherwise the whole piece is forbidden. My father-in-law z"l wrote in siman 105 that where one cut hot meat with a dairy knife in a kli sheni and it is yad soledet bo, the knife is forbidden because the meat emits into it, but the meat is not forbidden, because the heat of a kli sheni lacks the power to emit what is absorbed in the knife and absorb it into the meat. According to what appears to my humble mind, one should be stringent where there is pressure of the knife; but where there is no pressure of the knife, such as the case of inserting a spoon mentioned by Maharshal, one may be lenient like the Rama.
