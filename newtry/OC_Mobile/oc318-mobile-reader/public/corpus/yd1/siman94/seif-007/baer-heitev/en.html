With a knife. Even if the knife was below and the meat above, we do not say "the lower prevails," for through knife pressure it absorbs much — so is clear in Orach Chaim; and Shach wrote: one may say why we say the place of shechitah is hot and a peel suffices even with a gentile's knife forbidden from grease, and nevertheless only a peel is needed — for there not all the animal is hot, only the place of shechitah alone; but a piece is entirely hot.
That he cut. Shach wrote this follows the first view in seif 1 which is primary; and even though part of it is hot, all of it is hot, nevertheless he did not carry his absorption throughout; therefore what Rama wrote in the gloss "against the knife" means the place that touched the meat; nevertheless certainly they spoke only when he knows clearly how far he cut; but from the default certainly the way is to cut with the whole knife and sixty is needed against the whole knife except the handle; and even though in siman 105 seif 7 they wrote Tur that if issur fell on a piece in the pot outside the gravy, removal suffices — one may say this case is different, for through knife pressure it spreads throughout; or that a default knife also absorbs from butter grease, and the like; or the case is fatty meat; and even though in siman 96 if one cut a radish with a forbidden knife it is forbidden only the amount of removal — one may say there is only radish sharpness; and even though some poskim hold radish sharpness is stronger than hot, so that even if the knife is not ben yomo they make it ben yomo for benefit — nevertheless for spreading throughout in cooking, hot is stronger.
Peel. Shach wrote: and even though in siman 10 and siman 96 and Orach Chaim siman 447 we rule a default knife is not clean — nevertheless the grease on it does not forbid more than a peel; and even though in siman 96 regarding radish it implies if not ben yomo and not wiped it forbids the amount of removal — one may say through radish sharpness and knife pressure they make what is in the knife's peel and on the knife's face ben yomo and forbid up to removal; and Taz wrote even in the first part, if there is sixty against the knife's place, nevertheless a peel is required; and see siman 105 seif 5 that whatever requires sixty also requires a peel; and likewise even if not ben yomo, if there is no sixty against grease on the knife, all is forbidden; and one who is lenient in this is only among the astonished — end.
Hag'alah. Shach wrote: that which is written regarding a ben yomo knife that hag'alah is required, and not so if not ben yomo — that is because it absorbed from basar b'chalav and is forbidden to use for meat or milk; meaning because it is not ben yomo it is permitted to use for other things such as fish and vegetables, as in siman 93 in the gloss; unlike ben yomo, for the knife became nevelah, therefore hag'alah is required.
Second. Shach wrote this is R' Avraham's own view; but Orach Chaim and Maharshal hold in solid matter such as meat and the like that is not liquid there is no difference between kli rishon and kli sheni — as long as yad soledes bo it is forbidden; and Taz wrote it seems to me to be stringent in solid matter even in kli sheni where there is further reason to be stringent, as here one may say through knife pressure; but where there is no knife pressure one may be lenient as Rama (Bach wrote in s.k. 5: if one cut hot meat with a dairy knife in kli sheni and yad soledes bo the knife is forbidden because the meat exudes in it, but the meat is not forbidden, for kli sheni has no power to exude the absorbed and absorb into the meat — end).
