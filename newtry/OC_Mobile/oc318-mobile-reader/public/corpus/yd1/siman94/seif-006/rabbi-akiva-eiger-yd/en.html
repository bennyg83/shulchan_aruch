At first glance it would seem the precise wording is for that reason and he did not write "they cooked in milk" — the case is a pot of milk, but there is no milk in the pot only something else; thus sixty against the meat helps and the onions themselves are permitted, for there is no concern milk taste entered the onions and forbade them and would forbid the dish afterward if there is no sixty against all the onions, for milk taste in the dish before entering the onions is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of the permitted; and if there is no sixty against the meat that is forbidden; even per Mechaber (siman 95 seif 3) here it is forbidden, for from onions to water it is not called noten ta'am bar noten ta'am since it was not absorbed in a vessel and did not become attached; but if they cooked in milk, one may say sixty is needed against all the onions, for milk entered them and became noten ta'am and went out and forbids, and the onions remain in their issur; but from Maharam of Lublin's words it does not appear so, and so from Shulchan Aruch gloss (siman 95 seif 2) — investigate:

But if there is sixty in the water we count ben yomo from use of the issur; and that which some say when they removed the spoon while it was boiling the water absorbed in the spoon became nevelah from the issur absorbed in it — nevertheless, since the issur that remained absorbed (and did not go out into the water, for what went out was nullified in sixty) is spoiled, we say the forbidden element is not stricter than the forbidding element, as Rama wrote below (siman 134 seif 2) in the gloss; but if there is no sixty in the water we are concerned something went out from the issur outside, and since the issur was outside the pot for a moment it is not spoiled; also according to what Rama wrote seif 2 that one time of sixty suffices, and it is explained in Terumat HaDeshen the reason there are two lenient views: one, lest when removing the spoon it is not a kli rishon to absorb milk taste from meat in it; second, Ramban's view that in absorbed matter there is no noten ta'am le'heter — if so, here too there is no issur from removing the spoon from the water:
