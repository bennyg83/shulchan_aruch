From meat. Meaning from the meat itself, or he cut them with a meat knife finely; but if he cut in one place of them — see above siman 96 what the law is.
That absorbed. Maharam of Lublin's responsum wrote: if there is sixty against the meat absorbed in the onion, then even the onion is permitted even though it was not crushed and softened, and one need not separate it; but if he cut the onion with a forbidden knife, even with sixty one must separate the onions from the dish by straining, for we hold "possible to squeeze it" is forbidden — end; and see above siman 96.
The pot. Shach wrote Beit Yosef wrote at end of siman 103: however, where there was absorption from basar b'chalav cooked in it, it appears to all that when hot water was heated in it within twenty-four hours it is considered as if the issur was cooked again in it, and we count twenty-four hours from heating the water, for in basar b'chalav all agree noten ta'am — end; and it is not clear, for the poskim' reason for noten ta'am in basar b'chalav is that each alone is permitted by itself and when together they are forbidden, therefore the meat itself becomes issur — unlike other issurim; if so here, where the absorbed matter in the pot was already forbidden, noten ta'am does not apply; nevertheless for us who rule noten ta'am in all issurim there is no relevance here in any case — end. But it is somewhat difficult: why is sixty needed here against milk the pot absorbed if they cooked water in it within twenty-four hours — do we not say noten ta'am bar noten ta'am from milk to pot and from pot to hot water heated in the interim and from hot water back to the pot, as in Sefer HaTerumah? One must say it is certainly not known clearly that all taste in the pot went to hot water and it is not noten ta'am bar noten ta'am but a doubt; nevertheless if the pot is not ben yomo it is permitted in any case — if at first everything went to the water it is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am, and if not it is noten ta'am lifgam; and if so, in other issurim why do we rule if one cooked water in a pot within twenty-four hours we consider it ben yomo from when the water was heated and we do not also say in any case whether the pot did not emit into the water or emitted — perhaps it emitted in less than sixty and that taste was nullified in sixty, for noten ta'am in other issurim is only rabbinic and a rabbinic doubt is lenient? One must say the rabbis enacted this since there is doubt how much it emits — we say in every place stringently and are concerned lest everything emitted and everything became nevelah; even though in siman 98 regarding pouring min b'mino it is permitted from rabbinic doubt — specifically regarding pouring we say so, for if not poured one could estimate; but here it is impossible to estimate in any way, therefore they enacted to estimate for the whole pot — investigate; and if water was cooked in an issur vessel within twenty-four hours, whether we say noten ta'am and need twenty-four hours from heating the water — see end siman 103.
