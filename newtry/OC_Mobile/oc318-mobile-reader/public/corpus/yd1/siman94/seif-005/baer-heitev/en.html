Forbidden. Behold Shach left Mechaber and Rama in doubt, for since here there are three noten ta'am: one entering the spoon, from the spoon to the water, and from the water to the pot — one may wonder why Mechaber forbids cooking meat in it, especially since he permits below siman 95 noten ta'am bar noten ta'am in cooking; and further: how did he write "since it was new," etc. — it implies if he first cooked milk in it in actual form it is forbidden to cook other things, and this is not as he wrote below siman 95 seif 3 that meat bowls rinsed in a dairy cauldron with boiling water, even if both are ben yomo, everything is permitted as noten ta'am bar noten ta'am; and on Rama too one may wonder, for he rules plainly like Mechaber, and it is not so even for us, for in three noten ta'am all agree; nevertheless it is not difficult — why should it not be permitted to cook even milk in it, since anyway one must establish one use for this pot, therefore one should establish meat use, for nevertheless more of the last taste remains primary; and it seems all the more if they used actual meat in this pot into which a ben yomo dairy spoon was first inserted — permitted to cook meat afterward; but after the first spoon insertion we would not permit the pot to cook meat l'chatchila, since anyway one must establish a use for this pot — one should establish from milk absorbed in it — end (and certainly a great refutation is on Beit Yosef and Rama, and it seems to resolve their words with difficulty: the case is that the spoons were ben yomo and presumably when ben yomo presumably a little grease sticks to it as in siman 95 seif 3 one must say clearly there was no grease stuck on it, etc.; if so it is actual imparting of taste, and nevertheless Rama holds b'dieved it is permitted as noten ta'am bar noten ta'am, for this taste gives to the pot and from the pot to the water — two, as stated):
Sixty. Meaning even if both are ben yomo. And Taz challenged per the first reason in siman 95 seif 3 on those who forbid: we are concerned lest the bowls touched the pan and likewise lest the spoon touched the pot body — what does sixty help, for the spoon still absorbs and there is a second taste; one must say he holds like the second reason there — concern lest the water was forbidden, etc.; and here there is no such concern since there is sixty.
And to forbid. Meaning even to cook other things in it. And Shach wrote one must read "in the type of the vessel" with bet, not "like the type of the vessel" with kaf; and the explanation: although the food is not forbidden, nevertheless one may not eat it neither with meat nor with milk, since one can eat it without that — it is like l'chatchila, and one should pour it into a vessel that is like the one that is ben yomo specifically (and Rashal wrote they eat from the pot with a new spoon).
Stringency. Taz wrote: consequently also what Shulchan Aruch wrote "if two spoons in a new pot," etc., that the pot is forbidden — this too is only a general stringency — end; and all this in food where the Torah spared Israelite money; but if they cooked water in a meat vessel and inserted a ben yomo dairy spoon, the water is forbidden as Rama wrote in siman 95 seif 3.
