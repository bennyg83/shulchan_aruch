If it was inserted, etc. — see what I wrote in Peleti: one need not be concerned with one insertion of a spoon absorbing an olive of meat and emitting, and the spoon absorbs an olive of meat and within the spoon there is noten ta'am from meat, and it emits again — behold here are two olives and one would need 120 olives, for an olive of meat and an olive of milk do not combine and do not nullify each other. Only two insertions is there concern: in the first insertion it emits an olive of absorbed forbidden meat and becomes noten ta'am, and in the second insertion likewise an olive of absorbed forbidden milk, for it absorbs and emits more than one — we are not concerned, and they are two olives of milk and require 120 olives. And see Peleti: many poskim hold absorbed milk does not become noten ta'am, for it joins what is in the pot and nullifies meat taste, and noten ta'am only when removing the spoon — then what is absorbed in the spoon becomes noten ta'am and what is in the pot does not combine since he removes from the pot; if so, on first removal the spoon absorbs and becomes noten ta'am and there is no emission here; but on second insertion it emits the absorbed amount — only one olive, and on removal it absorbs again but does not emit until a third insertion — behold two olives and 120 needed to nullify; this is with three insertions, not two — a decree lest there be three insertions, therefore they decreed each insertion needs sixty; and see Peleti that hag'alah is not difficult.
And it was not known — then it returns and is stirred; an olive of first insertion's emission with an olive of emission — unlike when known, then the first is nullified first and does not return and is stirred; and see siman 99.

And Shach's view is Rashi's, that we do not say noten ta'am in other issurim; if so there is no concern on second insertion, for it was already forbidden from basar b'chalav and is like other issurim; and Peri Chadash challenged and it is correct with him, for since the spoon absorbs from meat it again forbids milk and becomes noten ta'am — what of it that it was already forbidden.
That one time of sixty suffices — many authors hold in absorbed matter in a vessel we do not say noten ta'am, only in absorbed food, as we say "milk is nevelah," etc.; and it also seems when there are two olives of milk the Torah nullifies by majority, only rabbinically sixty, and this is only a doubt whether it absorbed and returned and emitted, and a rabbinic doubt is lenient.
