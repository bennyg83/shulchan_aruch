But if there is sixty in the water we count ben yomo from use of the issur; and that which some say when they removed the spoon while it was boiling the water absorbed in the spoon became nevelah from the issur absorbed in it — nevertheless, since the issur that remained absorbed (and did not go out into the water, for what went out was nullified in sixty) is spoiled, we say the forbidden element is not stricter than the forbidding element, as Rama wrote below (siman 134 seif 2) in the gloss; but if there is no sixty in the water we are concerned something went out from the issur outside, and since the issur was outside the pot for a moment it is not spoiled; also according to what Rama wrote seif 2 that one time of sixty suffices, and it is explained in Terumat HaDeshen the reason there are two lenient views: one, lest when removing the spoon it is not a kli rishon to absorb milk taste from meat in it; second, Ramban's view that in absorbed matter there is no noten ta'am le'heter — if so, here too there is no issur from removing the spoon from the water:
