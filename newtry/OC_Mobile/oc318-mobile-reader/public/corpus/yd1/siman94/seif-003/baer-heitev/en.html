The spoon. Taz wrote we do not say that what was inserted is already like hag'alah, for hag'alah requires boiling that raises a boil; and for issur it forbids only if yad soledes bo. And Shach wrote that per R' Avraham in siman 95 seif 3, even if there was only water or other things in the pot, if the pot was ben yomo and absorption was initially through the body of the meat the spoon is forbidden, and so we rule: the spoon is forbidden to use whether with meat, milk, or other things, for the spoon is noten ta'am; and see above siman 98 seif 5.
Its day. Meaning from the time of insertion, even if it is not ben yomo from when milk was cooked in it, since it was ben yomo at first insertion it became noten ta'am and forbids afterward even b'dieved if it is ben yomo from insertion; but if it is not ben yomo at second insertion everything is permitted, for although noten ta'am, now it is noten ta'am lifgam and permitted; but certainly l'chatchila it is forbidden to insert it in anything even if not ben yomo.
In benefit. (Meaning the bowl needs breaking and the food is thrown in the latrine, but not before a dog even if the dog is not his.) Hagahot Ashiri at the end of Avodah Zarah wrote if he afterward cooked when it is not ben yomo he throws the pot's value into the river and the food is permitted; and Taz disagrees.
Fruits. Beit Yosef wrote it implies even a moist thing, as long as it is cold, is permitted — end; and Bach challenged, for in siman 91 it is forbidden to place a moist thing in an issur vessel, and he answered that here it is like b'dieved where it is impossible unless the vessel is broken (but it is difficult for me, for in siman 121 Orach Chaim wrote that an earthenware vessel used for issur cold, since hag'alah is impossible, is called b'dieved and rinsing well suffices; Orach Chaim wrote this and Shach brought it — specifically a vessel not subject to hag'alah such as cups gentiles drank from: a Jew may buy and drink by rinsing; but other earthenware bought from a gentile, if there is only doubt it was used hot, requires breaking — it explicitly implies even for dry use it is forbidden, for it says breaking is required; and here Rama does not disagree with Beit Yosef — it implies even a moist thing is permitted to use, and investigate). And Taz in the name of Taz wrote even hot water to rinse the head or to do other things not needed for eating and drinking is permitted, and so is the practice; and per this practice one may be lenient also if a pot was forbidden from basar b'chalav and after twenty-four hours they cooked food in it — one need not throw the pot's value into the river; and Rashal disagrees and ruled one must throw, etc.; and all this in a pot that was forbidden; but food that was forbidden must be thrown specifically in the latrine, not before a dog even if the dog is not his.
