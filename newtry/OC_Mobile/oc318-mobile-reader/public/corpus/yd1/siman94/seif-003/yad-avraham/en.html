(seif 3) And if there is no sixty, everything is forbidden in benefit. What Beit HaTosafot wrote: even for a dog that is not his — so Orach Chaim general rule 21 and so Maharshal. In Sefer Machaneh Chaim siman 348 it is not so, and it seems he did not see Maharshal's words in Yesh Seder and Orach Chaim. And so in Hagahot Maimoniyot Laws of Chametz that burial is required specifically; and see Rashi Menachot (101b) that benefit is only for his dog whose sustenance is on him. And so Shabbat (175b) and Kiddushin (8b); and so inferred in Sefer Pe'er Yeshua from Rashi Pesachim (5) for the view that burning is not required specifically, only nullification in any matter — one may give to a dog that is not his. And in Sefer Orach Chaim he wrote Rashi cannot be upheld, for he holds chametz benefit is Torah law; but R' Eliezer who holds "do not eat leaven" is also benefit — he reads "leaven" for food for dogs. And his words are difficult, for Rashi there is for R' Akiva, as is clear there (23), and he holds like R' Abahu as Rashi explained there that he disagrees with R' Pedat; but even for R' Eliezer one may say he reads "leaven" to obligate the feeder, as in Yerushalmi; and this derashah comes for the view that the feeder is not obligated and also not to forbid benefit and also not for half-measure, as Rambam wrote.
(there) But permitted to place fruits or cold, etc. It seems "cold" is like fruits — dry matter entirely; but not moist. And as Tosafot perek Ein Ma'amidin (40) in the name of Rashi and Piskei Tosafot there siman 56 and Mordechai there and Beit Yosef and Be'er HaGolah — see there. With this Beit HaChayim's challenge on Taz why he does not require rinsing first is resolved, for I already wrote siman 91 that for entirely dry matter rinsing is not required even in earthenware — see there; but moist is forbidden to place in an issur vessel, and even rinsing the vessel does not help, only rinsing what was used in it — so wrote most poskim Rif, Ramban, Rashba, Ran, Maggid Mishneh, Kol Bo, Ra'avad — see siman 69.
