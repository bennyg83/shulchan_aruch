Without forcing itself—in the Gemara daf 45 we say: anything that stretches its neck and grazes only if it does not force itself—Rashi explained: by a person; and the Gemara asks: if it forced itself—what? Rashi explained: that it stretched its neck excessively at the time of shechitah; teiku; and R. Yehuda and R. Yehuda bar Ilai derived the matter between them: forced in the simanim and slaughtered—invalid—end; and Peri Chadash wrote from this it implies every doubt is in forcing itself; but if a person forced it—certainly invalid; and the Rambam in chapter 1 of laws of shechitah included in one: whether it forced itself or a person forced the simanim and he slaughtered—behold it is doubtful nevelah—end; and Peri Chadash challenged that forcing by a person is not in the category of doubt. And it appears the Rambam holds: certainly if he forced so much in the simanim to pull them out as it would extend if the animal extended—if it forced itself, certainly kosher; for what he wrote "and what he wrote animal" is only if it extended excessively in this—certainly invalid; and this is our doubt, for it is unknown whether the animal was extending by its forcing or not; therefore the Rambam included them in one.
