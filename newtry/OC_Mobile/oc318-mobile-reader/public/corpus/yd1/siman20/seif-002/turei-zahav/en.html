When one cuts it, it contracts. That is not the explanation that one part widens from another, this above and that below as some slaughterers understand—a thing that applies everywhere one cuts in living flesh; rather the explanation is that the skin wrinkles and makes folds in the place of shechitah and the cavity is covered, unlike in the crop where the cavity remains exposed and does not wrinkle.

Until the zefek. Meaning the roof of the zefek; and Beit Yosef wrote siman 20 to distinguish: if he slaughtered on the roof of the zefek, if white skin is seen it is known he touched it, because the red is more common in the esophagus from outside than the white; and the white is longer than the red; and another sign: on the roof of the zefek there are not two skins as in the esophagus.
