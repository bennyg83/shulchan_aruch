And know that he cut them etc.—the words of Shulchan Aruch are difficult to reconcile; and Shach already stood on this in s.k. 8 at length and confused the reader until it is hard to stand on it. And I will bring briefly: let us set aside the words of Tosafot and grasp only Ra'osh's words, who wrote on the mishnah "one who slaughters from the sides—it is kosher," that it deals with one who knows he cut the simanim before he cut the spinal column. And Rav Beit Yosef explained his words that nevertheless we require return of simanim too—and this is not so, for then there is still difficulty: what difference is there between from the sides and slaughtering opposite the nape—for in both, if he returned it is kosher? Accordingly, with knowing, even if he did not return it is kosher—for it could be the simanim were there and he slaughtered in them first, unlike opposite the nape: even if he knew, since he did not return it is invalid—for it says explicitly he knows, which is impossible to know at all—and understand. And if he returned, one can say Ra'osh holds in the default case and does not distinguish at all with return that knowledge too is required; and one should not challenge: if so, why did the Gemara infer in melikah that return is not needed, for otherwise melikah of the nape would be kosher—accordingly with return of simanim, if so slaughter too would be kosher? What difficulty—for perhaps it deals with one who did not know, and return does not help to validate the slaughter—this is not difficult, for it is similar to the beginning "one who slaughters from the sides"; accordingly it deals with knowing, as Ra'osh said; if so, at the end too it deals in this manner, and nevertheless invalid—and this is clear. If so, Shulchan Aruch who wrote "and know that he cut them" etc. did not need to write that he returned—for according to Ra'osh's words return is not needed. And if we explain as Shach wrote: "and know" is a vav of division, as if he said: or he knows etc.—this too is not settled, for Shulchan Aruch concluded "and the same applies to one who slaughters from the nape," and for the nape knowing without return does not help, as written; and behold Tosafot wrote: from the sides—we find to say specifically he did; but l'chatchila, even if he returned simanim, decree lest he not return; and with this Maharsha explained he holds the mishnah deals in all cases of return and no return, and in all of them specifically he did; and this is not decree upon decree, for one can say they are one decree. And in the default case they decreed not to slaughter from the sides l'chatchila; and it is possible even with knowing that he slaughters simanim too is included in the decree; and Rav Beit Yosef answered Tosafot who invalidate b'dieved if he did not return simanim; and Shach explained this means he did not know he cut in the simanim first—for if he knew, return is not needed, unlike the nape where return is needed in every case; and this is the essence of Shach's words. If so, one must still say: or he knew, as the Mechaber—and still difficult why with knowing alone on the nape it is kosher, for we require return too—and tzarich iyun. Therefore one must say the Mechaber certainly holds like Tosafot that we require return—for without return it is invalid; only with return Tosafot do not require, in their view, that he know he slaughtered simanim first, for from the default case he slaughtered them first, as Hagahot Ra'osh wrote; but nevertheless on the nape return does not help unless he knows clearly that he slaughtered simanim first; and if challenged: if so, why does the Gemara challenge according to the one who holds even in melikah he requires etc.—what difficulty, for perhaps it deals with one who did not know? One must say: just as in melikah certainly he must know clearly that he did not perform melikah on the simanim first but on the spinal column—for that is the essence of the mitzvah of melikah—if so, learn from it in shechitah: since the mitzvah of shechitah is in the simanim, he must know he slaughtered simanim first; so too one must say for this view. And the Mechaber holds Ra'osh and Tosafot disagree: Tosafot hold knowledge is not needed, only return suffices; and Ra'osh holds there is no nafka mina in return, only knowledge suffices; and the Mechaber is stringent like both—that return and knowledge are required—to satisfy both views; and this is also for the nape kosher, since there is return and knowledge—and understand.
