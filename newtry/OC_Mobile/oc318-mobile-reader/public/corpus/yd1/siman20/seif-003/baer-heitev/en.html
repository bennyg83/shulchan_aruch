The knife. And Shach wrote: from the Mechaber's words it appears two are required—returning simanim and also knowing he cut them before the spinal column; and in truth this is against the Gemara—for certainly with return alone it also helps; and one must say this "and know" is a vav of division, meaning: or he knew; and certainly from the default case, if he did not return, there is to forbid. And Taz wrote: for us there is no nafka mina at all in this law, for the main concern here is lest he cut the spinal column before the simanim, and we hold it is forbidden even if he cut the spinal column after the simanim, as written in siman 24 seif 7. And what Bach wrote that if he cut flesh of the neck before the simanim there is to forbid from concern lest when he begins to slaughter in the flesh he touches the esophagus slightly and returns and cuts in the flesh—Taz disagrees and brought proof for his words; also one need not be concerned for any aspect of prohibition of hesech as long as he did not raise the knife—end. A slaughterer who is left-handed and trains his hands to slaughter does not slaughter exactly in the middle of the neck but diagonally; and nevertheless he cuts the simanim and does not touch the spinal column—it is permitted even l'chatchila, and it is obvious one should not remove him for this. Teshuvat She'eilot siman 48.
