And know that he cut—see Peleti, for the view of Shach is a vav of division, and the intent is: or he knew; and in one of them it suffices. And see what I wrote on this: the main thing is the Mechaber switched and holds two are required—return and also must know he cut in the simanim.
And the same applies to one who slaughters from the nape—meaning also with return and knowing he cut simanim first; but without return, or if it is not clear to him that he slaughtered simanim first, he does not validate—and see Peleti.
From the nape—and if he did not cut any bone of the spinal column but only slaughtered the simanim, it is obvious it is kosher—for what concern is there? And to say a decree that he not cut the spinal column lest he cut the spinal column—from where do we innovate a decree that we do not find in the Talmud and early authorities? And Rav Berachot Taz was very lenient for us, for we establish one may not slaughter simanim and majority of spinal column—below siman 24; if so, there is no longer concern he will slaughter the nape first and afterward the simanim; he is lenient—and tzarich iyun. But without any cut at all, according to all it is kosher.
