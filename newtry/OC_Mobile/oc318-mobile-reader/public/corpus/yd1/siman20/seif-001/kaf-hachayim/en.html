(a) [Seif 1] The place of shechitah in the neck in the trachea toward the head from hat slope downward, etc.; and that is, leave in the wheat grains, etc. Our master the Ba'al HaTurim did not mention here nor in siman 24 "leave in the wheat grains"; and so Rashba in teshuvah ha'arokhah and Semak; and he wrote that from what is said daf 45a—it is possible because they were in doubt about the explanation of wheat grains, what it is and how far it extends and what is called "leave in the wheat grains." And in Sefer Yair Netiv it is written: the matter of these wheat grains—we are not expert; and I too searched and investigated and asked the mouths of the great inspectors expert in this craft properly, and they answered me they did not reach a conclusion, etc.; therefore one who fears the word of Hashem will not validate any hagramah that is above the large ring, since we are not expert and do not know what these wheat grains are or where they are; and so R' Yechiel: a slaughterer should distance himself from the large ring and slaughter below, even though it is simple to us that the entire neck is fit for shechitah, in order to remove doubt—end. And so commanded Mahari Segal not to validate at all from the large ring; and so Agur in Ashkenazi shechitot writes they practice to declare treifah for hagramah if he did not leave the full thread in the large ring toward the head across its entire face, as the rabbis of R. Yehuda bar Yehuda; and so I saw practiced Mahari Landau; and so the custom spread in all the provinces—end. Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tur part 2; and so Peri To'ar part 1: for practical halakhah, since nowadays we are not expert in wheat grains and it is also impossible to narrow the beginning of hat slope, it appears one must slaughter within the large ring and leave at least a hair's breadth from the large ring toward the head across the majority of the ring—and aside from the law that it is not called shechitah; and so Rambam, Ra'osh, Rashba, and so is the halakhah—end. And Rav Zivchei Tzedek wrote part 1: one should rule as Agur's words; and so they practice in the city of Baghdad—see there: if nothing remained of the large ring toward the head across its entire face they declare the animal treifah, only in a place of great loss; and how much more so for a great need, if the majority of the ring remained they validate it—see there.
(c) There. And its measure below until the head of the wing of the lung, etc. That it is on the left side; and when the animal grazes it protrudes more from the body than the right side, which is thicker—Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot; Shach s.k. 2; Peri Chadash part 3; Zivchei Tzedek part 2; and this measure is for stringency—Sha'arei Dura part 2; Zivchei Tzedek part 3; but Kometz part 2 wrote: if the cut in the trachea is found above that point—even so it is kosher; and if equal—this is hagramah; and he wrote that this is the implication of all the commentators—see there.
(d) And it appears that one estimates in this place without pulling the trachea forcibly upward, for on the side where the trachea by nature wrinkles somewhat—if one pulls the trachea forcibly, the great trachea that reaches there to the head of the wing of the lung will be pulled upward and come to leniency—Peri To'ar part 2; Zivchei Tzedek part 3; Minchat Yitzchak part 3; Zivchei Tzedek part 4.
