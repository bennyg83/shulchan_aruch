And "that is at the end of the trachea" etc.—and the view of Mahari"l and Mahari"v and the rest of the latter geonim is to be stringent not to slaughter above the large ring; and Beit Chadash wondered: behold the Gemara is arranged until hat slope is kosher; and Shach wrote they relied on what is said in Bava Kamma daf 99 that even though it would be kosher according to Rav, nevertheless they were stringent because of avodah zarah; and this too is so. And it is strained—for there it was within the large ring and discusses the dispute of the rabbis and R. Yehuda ben Beteira; but here above, since the view of R. Yehuda ben Antigonos is that it is kosher—from where do we derive to be stringent? And it appears there is a difficulty if the matters are as they sound simply: did the sages disagree with R. Yehuda ben Antigonos likewise, or that from the ring upward is a place fit for shechitah—and why did they call it hagramah, meaning deror? And Rashi wrote: anything not in the place of shechitah is called hagramah—behold according to R. Yehuda ben Antigonos alone it is a place of shechitah, so why call it hagramah? Therefore he holds that all agree that d'rabbanan it is forbidden to slaughter above the place of the large ring; only if he was derore half outside the ring and half inside—according to R. Yehuda ben Beteira it is treifah because majority in the ring is required; according to R. Yehuda ben Antigonos it is kosher, as with half trachea defective and the place of shechitah is d'oraisa kosher; therefore they called it hagramah for it is outside the place of shechitah—and understand well. It appears at first glance according to what Rashi said in the sugya "one who slaughters within the ring" etc., but in the rest of the rings his slaughter is not kosher in their majority at all except between ring and ring—and Rashi held this on column 2 s.v. lashon shlishi etc., for they wrote; and the first wording appears to me, and there is no obstacle to switch the halakhah, for there is no halakhah in all of them except like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos; and one must understand: if so, why did Rashi trouble to clarify which wording is correct and sharp in the chapter of the Gemara—behold all are mere pilpul; and for practical halakhah it does not stand as Rashi himself said. And especially Tosafot challenged Rashi's explanation that he holds one may not slaughter in the rest of the rings, and explained another explanation; and so in Piskei Tosafot—not like Rashi—that it is permitted to slaughter even within another ring; and all this is unnecessary, for since we establish halakhah like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, everything falls clearly as Rashi himself said. Therefore it appears: in any case one must understand how R. Yehuda ben Antigonos's testimony that until hat slope is called a place of shechitah—that the rest of the rings that do not surround the entire cavity, as Rashi explained, are fit for shechitah; and if this depends on the large ring being a place of shechitah, nevertheless to slaughter in the ring below it is forbidden because of what Rashi said that they do not surround the entire trachea; if so, one can say that even though R. Yehuda ben Antigonos's testimony is true that the place of shechitah is from hat slope, it is also possible that the rest of the rings are not a place of shechitah. But in truth this is not a difficulty, for from the large ring until hat slope the wheat grains and the like—all do not surround the entire cavity of the trachea, only the majority, and the remainder adhere in the skin and the skin surrounds; and accordingly, since R. Yehuda ben Antigonos said from the large ring until hat slope is fit for shechitah, we learn we do not require that it surround the entire cavity of the spinal column; and how much more so the rest of the rings that are below the large ring, which are called a place of shechitah—and they depend on each other. And accordingly we can understand what we say in the Gemara: R. Zeira ate hagramah of Rav and Shmuel, and the Gemara challenged: he has no stringency of "the place he left" etc.; and the Gemara answered: from Babylonia to Eretz Yisrael it does not apply; or R. Zeira does not intend to return. And one must understand: what is the difficulty—R. Zeira holds as explained there, like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, that from hat slope is a place of shechitah even though it does not surround its entire cavity; and how much more so hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of the rings, as said; and one must say: in Babylonia the prohibition of hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of the rings spread, how much more so above the ring, as said; and therefore it challenged "he has no stringency of the place he left" from there, for even though halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, in Babylonia they practiced prohibition; and it answered he does not intend to return. And accordingly we derive: in Babylonia we do not practice like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, and even hagramah of Rav and Shmuel in the rest of their rings they practiced to invalidate; and see below chapter Eizehu Mekoman regarding chelev on the maw that they challenged etc., that Rashi wrote: even in a place where the people of Eretz Yisrael and Babylonia disagree, we follow the residents of Babylonia—see there; and accordingly for us there is no R. Yehuda ben Beteira, and even hagramah of Rav and Shmuel is forbidden; and therefore Rashi troubled to clarify what is hagramah of Rav and Shmuel, and clarified the first wording: mainly in the rest of the rings it is forbidden; and he wrote there is no obstacle since halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, only we practice like the people of Babylonia to forbid in any case; from Torah law it is permitted, only by force of custom—and there is no obstacle at all in a mere custom—and understand. Therefore Tosafot challenged Rashi and raised that even for us they forbid like the people of Babylonia—this is the reconciliation; and so Piskei Tosafot—not like Rashi—for us there is a nafka mina that we practice like the people of Babylonia. And with this the words of Rashi in chapter 1 daf 99 in this hagramah that Rav invalidated are explained: Rashi explained he cut within one ring and was derore between ring and ring. And at first glance tzarich iyun: why did Rashi not explain simply as hagramah in Chullin—that he slaughtered, cut the large ring, and was derore above the ring? And also this hagramah that Rabbenu Tam wrote is one explanation that the house of Rashi on the mishnah in the rest of the rings one may not be derore and slaughter between ring and ring; and Rashi rejected that explanation by proof from the tosefta and in Tosafot. And it appears Rashi had a difficulty: we say R. Zeira ate hagramah of Rav and Shmuel and they told him "you are not from the place of Rav and Shmuel," he said "who said it? R. Chiya bar Abba"—and Rashi explained perhaps he heard from another etc.; and this hagramah that came before Rav and he declared treifah is explained that hagramah is forbidden—and it was in a place where R. Chiya bar Abba disagreed, for therefore he exempted the butcher from payment, and Rav and R. Elazar said he did a favor by forbidding it—hence Rav forbids. Therefore Rashi holds this hagramah is not from that, only this other hagramah—meaning all below the large ring: he cut in one ring and was derore between ring and ring. And that he rejected that explanation in the mishnah—certainly Rashi himself held that logically it is called hagramah only he rejected it by proof from the tosefta; and in truth from the tosefta there is no proof, for it is impossible to teach "leave a hair below," for according to the view and explanation of Rashi like Rav and Shmuel the rabbis hold one may not slaughter at all in the rest of the rings, for they are not called trachea at all since they do not surround the entire cavity; if so, how can we say in the tosefta to leave a hair below, for it is not a place of shechitah at all, and it is impossible to slaughter at all within the rest of the rings—and how can hagramah apply since it is impossible to slaughter within it at all? And in any case it is proven that also in the mishnah it is impossible to explain thus; and Rashi who proved from the tosefta and did not prove simply—as said—for it is impossible to slaughter in it and how does majority apply? Because there are three explanations as Rashi said, and for another explanation and wording it is kosher to slaughter within the rest of the rings; therefore Rashi brings proof from the tosefta: whichever explanation it will be, at least from the tosefta it is proven that explanation does not exist. But in truth if we say Rav and Shmuel permit in the rest of the rings like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, there would be a place of shechitah as R. Zeira holds; if so it is fine to say he slaughtered in other rings and was derore between ring and ring—it is invalid; and this was the incident in the aforementioned hagramah and Rav invalidated it even though one could say it is a place of shechitah—and understand. In any case we see in Babylonia they practiced prohibition not like the testimony of R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, and we follow two customs. And it appears also in Ra'osh one can infer he explains Rav and Shmuel who said even R. Yehuda did not say etc.—meaning majority of the ring does not suffice, rather we require majority of the cavity. And so Tur siman 24 and so in hints; and it is difficult: R. Zeira ate hagramah of Rav and Shmuel—and according to Ra'osh this is majority of the ring in general, and it was unnecessary as I said because he brought stringency from the place he left etc.; we follow the people of Babylonia who are stringent—yet this answer is not sufficient, for if so the norm of the law is that majority of the ring suffices for shechitah, even for treifah if majority of the ring is severed it is treifah; and explicitly we say below in chapter Eizehu Mekoman that Rav Chiya bar Abba checked majority of the cavity of the trachea and it was not so much severed and he validated it, and even Rav ate from it. And see Ramban in Milchamot Hashem who wrote this majority of thickness and majority of cavity applies only in the rest of the rings and not in the large ring—see there. And it is a great difficulty; and one must say that here Ra'osh agrees with R'azah: R. Zeira who ate hagramah—the explanation is not that only majority of the ring was slaughtered—for what relevance is this to the name hagramah; rather it deals with one who slaughtered majority of the ring and afterward was derore outside the large ring—therefore it is called hagramah (and see what Maharsha wrote on this); and R. Zeira who ate did not hold that majority of the ring suffices, only R. Zeira holds like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos that from the large ring upward is also a place of shechitah; if so, what he cut above the ring joins to the majority of the ring and is kosher; if so, on this the Gemara's difficulty applies, and it was unnecessary "stringency of the place he left" etc.; and it answers Abaye and R. Elazar's explanation etc., which teaches explicitly that the people of Babylonia practiced prohibition—and if so, we too follow the people of Babylonia and it is forbidden. And the reason the poskim were not concerned for this—one can say as Rif and Ramban wrote regarding the maw: since the prohibition they practiced in Babylonia is not from the essence of the law but from a corrective enactment, we do not follow it; and even so it appears thus, for there is nothing after the testimony of R. Yehuda ben Antigonos. And it appears more that he holds in what they say "and halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos"—Ran agrees with me—see there. One may wonder why Ran mentioned "agrees with me"—behold R. Yehuda, R. Yehuda bar Ilai, and R. Chiya ruled thus; why did Ran mention only Ran? Rather it is simple: practical halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos, for there is nothing after his testimony—only this is in Eretz Yisrael; but in Babylonia the prohibition already spread from the instruction of Rav and Shmuel, so that if R. Zeira intended to return he too would have been stringent to forbid; if so, there is no proof from R. Yehuda and R. Yehuda bar Ilai, for all were in Eretz Yisrael; therefore the Gemara brought proof from Ran, for he too was in Babylonia, and nevertheless said halakhah is like R. Yehuda ben Antigonos and did not mention there is prohibition in Babylonia—we learn the prohibition did not spread from Rav and Shmuel—and understand. In any case we derive they ruled well and have a place to rely—Mahariu and Mahari"l—not to slaughter l'chatchila from the large ring; and a slaughterer who slaughters above should be removed, for he is contemptuous and breaches the fence of our rabbinic decisors. And in any case one should be stringent in our times, for in our times we are not expert at all in touching the wheat grains and the like. And last year a student asked the renowned decisor R' Meir Prostitz, who now resides in Schweinfurt, regarding a slaughterer they accused of being derore above the large ring; and because there were many branches I agreed without removing him—and were it not for this, my view was to remove him; and do not forsake your mother's teaching.
