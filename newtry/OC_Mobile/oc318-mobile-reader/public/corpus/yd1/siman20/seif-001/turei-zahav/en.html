That is at the end of the trachea, etc. Thus wrote Kol Bo in the name of Rav Bar Tzeloni: the matter of hat slope is that opposite the large ring there is flesh there, and the mouth of the flesh is made like wheat grains, and upon those grains there is a covering similar to a hadas leaf, and upon it one bone from the large ring surrounds this flesh like a hat; and in the middle of this bone there is one slope in the body of the bone and it is called hat slope; and the sages said: from hat slope downward is kosher, for it is about a finger above the large ring—end of his words.
And l'chatchila he shall slaughter, etc. It implies that b'dieved it is kosher even above until hat slope; and so wrote Maharshal—that this measure of the large ring is for distancing because of concern for mishap, and in a place of great loss one must distinguish accordingly.
Without forcing itself. In the Gemara it is stated: Rava said, provided that it does not force itself—Rashi explained: to pull the simanim away; we require human hands; Rabbi Chanina asked: if it forced itself—what? Rashi explained: that it stretched its neck excessively at the time of shechitah; teiku—and for stringency. And Tosafot explained this ask: it forced itself—that it stretched its head more than necessary to take a vegetable from a pit—end of their words. It appears their intent is that they are not pleased to explain Rashi's explanation about the time of shechitah, for if it is more than what is normal to stretch when it grazes, it is certainly invalid since we already established the measure is when it grazes; therefore they explained that the doubt is in the explanation of "whenever it grazes"—for it is an animal's manner to stretch its head more to take a vegetable from a pit and afterward eat after it returns somewhat from the great stretch—shall we say this too is included in the measure they said "when it grazes," or shall we say only when it grazes in its simple sense and stretching to take the vegetable is not included in this—as explained, it is correct; and with this it is resolved why Tur did not bring that there is no distinction between forcing by a person and forcing by itself in shechitah.
