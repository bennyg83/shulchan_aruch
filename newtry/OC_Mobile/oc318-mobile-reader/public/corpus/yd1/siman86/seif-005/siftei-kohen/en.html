And if it is peeled, etc. Rambam's and Ramban's view (and so R'ah in Beit HaBachur daf 108 side 2) is that there is no distinction; and poskim wrote that this is how our Gemara implies, except that one should be concerned for the Yerushalmi; and in my humble opinion Rambam and his supporters — in the Yerushalmi it appears they do not distinguish in this way but distinguish that when not peeled one does not need sixty, but forty or fifty-one suffice because shells raise the prohibition; but when peeled a greater measure is required — and this speaks of an egg with a chick in it — see there and investigate.
If it is peeled it forbids. It appears that even though the others are also in their shells it is forbidden until sixty-one; and so explained Perishah seif 11, and Bach in his last comment wrote a great astonishment that he would write in a book a fabricated explanation that has no root or branch in the Gemara or in rulings of the Geonim; rather the matter is clear that when the others are not peeled, even if the forbidden one is peeled, it does not forbid — end of his words. And I am greatly astonished at him, for in the Yerushalmi from which poskim brought this law it is stated explicitly thus, and so too Rashba in Pesachim, Semag negative commandment 132 (daf 45 side 1), Semak siman 227, and Rashba in responsum siman 46, whose language is: And as we read in the Yerushalmi, etc. — peeled with not-peeled require a different measure — it teaches that whenever the forbidden egg is peeled it forbids the permitted ones even if the permitted ones are not peeled; the shell does not prevent absorption — end of his words. And in truth what they brought for this is what he wrote in his composition, whose language is: It appears one must explain that all the eggs are peeled; for if the others are not peeled, sixty-one would not be required because there are large and small eggs — for certainly the shell on sixty eggs fills what is missing from the small ones — end of his words. And this means certainly shells combine to nullify the prohibition, as the Rav wrote in his hagahah nearby; but we say thus: when you count shells to nullify, you need so many eggs that among them all there are sixty-one; and by way of example: if there are fifty permitted eggs and the shells hold ten eggs, that is insufficient (and it appears to my humble opinion from Chullin 98 side 1, where it says there one who came before R' Gamliel said: Father, I estimated in forty-one and I in forty-eight, and Rashi explained there the sugya — it appears he speaks of eggs discussed there; and he did not go to the end of his view that the wording of the baraita does not imply this generally, but it appears he speaks of eggs; and R' Chiya challenged there: Is there any forty in a seah? and the Gemara answers hyperbole — meaning certainly it is impossible that forty large eggs would amount to sixty-one; but according to Rashi's explanation there is room to challenge: why did he challenge? And why answer hyperbole? For if we estimate in half a seah it is lenient, and even in forty it suffices, as Mar bar Ashi said there who estimates in forty half-olives — and so is stated explicitly in Yerushalmi Terumot chapter Bitzel as explained — and this is clear) and not like Perishah seif 12 who resolved this that sixty in the combination of shells does not suffice because the Sages were concerned lest a forbidden egg be so large that its interior would be as large as other permitted eggs with their shells — end of his words; for this is a matter that has no dawn, and sense and perception contradict it — how is it possible that in shells of sixty eggs there would not be as much as one egg? Rather certainly the main point is as I wrote, and it is clear and true.
And one needs sixty-one permitted eggs, etc. The reason: many poskim wrote that because there are large and small eggs, and one who comes to nullify an egg does not pay attention to see if this one and those are exactly equal — therefore they added one — end of his words. And for this reason when it was cooked in a permitted dish one needs only sixty of the dish to nullify it — this is Mechaber's view; but Rambam in chapter 4 of Forbidden Foods law 19 wrote the reason is because it is a complete creature in itself they made a distinction and added to its measure — end of his words (and all this speaks only of nullifying its emission, not the body of the egg, as Rashba and Beit Yosef state, and it is plain). And I proved in my book that Rambam means the egg itself is a complete creature in itself, as Tosafot, Rosh, and Rashba in Pesachim in name of Raavad, and Beit Yosef brings this below siman 110 and other poskim — that an egg is a complete creature; and Rashba in his novellae daf 106 side 1, and Beit Yosef understood that Rambam calls a chick a complete creature (and therefore for us, who rule that peeled or pierced forbids even without a chick in it, one needs sixty-one even in a dish, to be stringent). And according to Rambam's reason, even in a dish one needs sixty-one of the dish to nullify it; however according to Rambam's reason, if it became tereifah in a bowl one needs only sixty; and according to poskim and Mechaber one needs sixty-one; and the Rav also wrote in his hagahah; and I wrote there that Rambam's reason appears primary in the Gemara, and so it appears in Tosafot Pesachim (daf 98 side 1) s.v. "The question is," etc. However, it is difficult: why did they write there and nevertheless they resolved with difficulty and did not resolve that certainly all Torah prohibitions are nullified in sixty, and also in eggs sixty in a dish suffices, except that it was cooked with eggs — this case is different because one who comes to measure does not pay attention — they added one to the measure so there will certainly be sixty; but certainly they hold the reason is not because there are large and small eggs, etc., but from the law one needs sixty-one — see there and investigate. Therefore one should be stringent for both reasons; and such is Maharshal in Pesachim siman 43 and Ran — to be stringent for both reasons; and see in siman 106 more laws of cooking eggs.
And an egg that has a chick or blood, etc. Because the meat imparts taste — and there is no difference between impure and pure since there is a chick in it; and the same applies to blood found in it — a drop of blood that spreads through all of it, as above siman 66, and it is considered as if there is a chick in it — so too poskim; and specifically when it is known the blood is in the place that forbids it; but in a doubt of mixtures it is permitted as above siman 66 and s.k. 4 in the hagahah, and as I wrote there, and above siman 98 s.k. 7.
