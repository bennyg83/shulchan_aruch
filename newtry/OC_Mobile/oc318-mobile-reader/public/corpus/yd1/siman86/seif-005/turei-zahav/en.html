In its shell it does not forbid. It will not impart taste when in its shell, since there is no substance there — only moisture; unlike an egg with a chick where there is actual prohibition that is a real substance — whatever emerges from it, even via the shell, forbids the others.
If it is peeled. It appears it was peeled alone and cooked with others that are not peeled; Perishah explained thus; and if the forbidden is peeled and the permitted are not peeled, everything is permitted — end of Perishah; and I am greatly astonished, for in the Yerushalmi from which poskim brought this law it is explicit, and so Rashba in Pesachim, Semag, Semak, and Rashba in a responsum — peeled with not-peeled require a different measure; it teaches that whenever the forbidden egg is peeled it forbids the permitted even if they are not peeled. And in truth what they brought is from his composition: it appears one must explain that all eggs are peeled; if the others are not peeled, sixty-one would not be needed because of large and small eggs — for certainly the shell of sixty eggs fills what is missing from the small. And this means shells certainly combine to nullify the prohibition, as the Rav wrote in his hagahah nearby; but we say: when counting shells to nullify, one needs enough eggs that in total there are sixty-one; by way of example: fifty permitted eggs whose shells hold ten eggs is insufficient (and it appears from Chullin 98 side 1 that this is implied). Not like Perishah seif 12. This has no dawn; sense and perception contradict that shells of sixty eggs would not equal one egg — rather the main point is as I wrote, and it is clear and true.
If pierced. See seif 95 seif 2 what I challenged on this.
And an egg that has a chick. If cooked alone with permitted eggs, sixty-one permitted eggs are required even if the egg with the chick is not peeled; all the more so in a dish it forbids up to sixty of the dish, and shells of permission combine as the Rav wrote in his hagahah nearby; but we say: when counting shells to nullify, one needs so many eggs that in total there are sixty-one; by example: fifty permitted eggs whose shells hold ten eggs is insufficient.
Or blood. Because the meat imparts taste — and there is no difference between impure and pure since there is a chick in it; and the same applies to a drop of blood that spreads through all of it, as above siman 66; it is considered as if there is a chick in it; so too poskim; specifically when the blood is known to be in the forbidding place; in a doubt of mixtures it is permitted as above siman 66 and s.k. 4 in the hagahah, and as I wrote there, and above siman 98 s.k. 7.
