Nevelah. Shach wrote: an egg of nevelah is forbidden only d'rabbanan, for from the Torah it is permitted, for it is impossible it should be completed after death; but an egg of tereifah is forbidden from the Torah because afterward it was completed in prohibition, and even if it became tereifah only near shechitah it is forbidden from the Torah (they do not distinguish) — see above end of seif 99 and end of siman 87 seif 5.
In a thousand. This applies also to an egg of nevelah, for since it is an egg of nevelah the prohibition is certainly established and it is not nullified; unlike when it is a doubt of an egg of nevelah which is a rabbinic doubt and lenient. But in a doubt of an egg of tereifah, which is d'oraisa, they were stringent. However, it requires study according to what Mechaber and Rama ruled in siman 110 that it should nullify in a majority — dry in dry, one in two is nullified — and specifically an egg that has a chick in it is a complete creature, but an ordinary egg is not. And it requires study.
Permitted. For it is a rabbinic doubt; and even if we know certainly from which chicken it is, only that there is doubt whether the chicken is nevelah or slaughtered — such as doubt in shechitah where the halachah is it is invalid — the egg is permitted.
