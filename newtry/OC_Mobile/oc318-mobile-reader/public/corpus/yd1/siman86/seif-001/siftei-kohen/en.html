And even if the seller says, etc. Explanation: Even if the seller says — even an Israelite established in kashrut that they are from such-and-such fowl and we recognize that that fowl is pure — we do not rely on him, for he is certainly impure; and even if he shows us the fowl and says this is the fowl from which the eggs come, he is not believed, for he certainly lies. And see above siman 83 s.k. 26.
Therefore one asks the Israelite hunter, etc. Mechaber's view is like Rambam chapter 3 of Forbidden Foods — specifically with an Israelite who is not established in kashrut we rely on him, but with a non-Jew we never rely on him; therefore he wrote that one does not buy eggs from a non-Jew unless he recognized them and has recognizable familiarity with them; and as Rambam wrote there, meaning specifically in such a case we buy from him, but when he says from such-and-such fowl and it is pure we do not rely on him. But Ramban, Ran, and Tur disagree and hold that with a non-Jew too we rely on him as with an Israelite who is not established in kashrut; and so too in Sifrei — this is the view of many poskim from what they wrote regarding fish innards in seif 2 of Avodah Zarah; and so I proved there from the Gemara in Pesachim that this is primary. I also proved there from the Gemara in chapter Kol HaBasar (Chullin daf 116) — see there; and this is Rama's view. And what he did not emend at all from what Mechaber wrote "therefore one does not buy," etc., is because he relied on what he wrote before — or non-Jew — meaning that with a non-Jew too we rely; and automatically it appears that Mechaber did not write this except according to his view; and Taz mixed up the matter by first writing "or non-Jew" and afterward writing "therefore one does not buy," etc. And see in siman 83 s.k. 24 and 25.
That it is from such-and-such fowl, etc. The reason: when he says it is from such-and-such fowl it is a presumption he does not lie because he fears lest they bring eggs of that fowl and they see they do not resemble these and he is shamed; unlike when he only says it is from a pure fowl — when they bring eggs of a pure fowl and they do not resemble them, he can say: I did not tell you these are from this fowl but from another fowl you do not recognize, and it is pure.
Unless he recognized them, etc. And according to Rama in his hagahah — even without recognizable familiarity we rely on him when he says it is from such-and-such fowl and we recognize that it is pure — as I wrote in s.k. 2.
And we are not concerned for them, etc. For most eggs are kosher.
