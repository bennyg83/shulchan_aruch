That the fowl began to form in it, etc. So too Tur in name of Rambam; and Perishah challenged: for Tur wrote above siman 66 that if it is known the chick is formed one is liable on it from the Torah; and he resolved: there, blood is found inside the egg and from it the chick is born — therefore forbidden from the Torah because of blood; unlike here where the blood already turned and became clotted and is dust and has no d'oraisa prohibition — end of his words. And Bach in a later pamphlet wrote on this that these are fabricated words without support or proof, and resolved in his composition that Tur holds like R' Yehudah who brought in the conclusion in siman 66 that he permits even blood if found outside the knot; if so for us no egg blood is forbidden d'oraisa, and Mechaber's words cannot be reconciled at all — for in siman 66 he wrote one is liable on it d'oraisa, and here he wrote a blow of rebelliousness — end of his words. But in truth, according to what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 66, Rambam (here) disagrees with what Tur wrote — if it is known the chick is formed one is liable d'oraisa — if so Mechaber's words in Shulchan Aruch cannot be reconciled; but one may say that Tur himself brought Rambam's words in general, and on Bach's challenge: for Tur wrote in siman 66 in general one is liable from the Torah, and Rosh in a responsum (Beit Yosef brings this in siman 66) holds that formed blood is forbidden d'oraisa; and what Tur wrote afterward that R' Yehudah permits blood even outside the knot does not contradict what he wrote first in general that one is liable d'oraisa — for certainly when found outside the knot it is entirely permitted, and when found on the knot, if it is formed blood, it is forbidden from the Torah; and moreover from what they wrote, Rosh and Tur afterward in siman 66 "and good to be stringent," etc. — see there, it appears explicitly that he holds formed blood is d'oraisa; only Perishah's words appear clear in their reason, and I found support and proof for his words from what Maharach in Pesachim wrote — that Ra'avyah received from Ra'avan: if blood is in the yolk in a place where the start of the chick is not recognizable, then only it is forbidden because of the law of blood; but if full of blood and the place where the chick begins is recognizable, it is permitted, for near the formation it is like mere dust — end of his words. And even though Beit Yosef wrote in siman 66 on this, and it appears these words are rejected and it is not the view of poskim — end of his words — one may say they disagree only on permitting entirely, but from the Torah it is nevertheless permitted; and Mechaber's words may also be reconciled thus, and he retracted from what he wrote in siman 66 that Rambam disagrees with Tur; and so it appears somewhat in his book Bedek HaBayit in this siman.
