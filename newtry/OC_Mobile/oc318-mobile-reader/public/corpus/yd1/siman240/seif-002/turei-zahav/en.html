Not in his presence. So the Rambam writes explicitly. This is self-evident — for in his presence it is forbidden to call someone by his father's name even if it is not a distinctive name, lest his father think he is calling him, and similarly anyone standing there might think so. There is no distinction whether the name is distinctive or not. Although the Tur's transcription of the Rambam does not mention "not in his presence," this is of no concern, for he relied on the obvious. The Derishah elaborated here to dispute the Rama's gloss of "not in his presence," but he was not precise at all, as will be explained in siman 242 with God's help.
