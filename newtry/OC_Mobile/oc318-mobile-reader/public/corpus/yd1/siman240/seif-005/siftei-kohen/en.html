He is not obligated to go begging, etc. Likewise, he need not honor his father from his own funds if doing so would force him himself to beg. So writes the Rosh, and Bach rules likewise.
Some say, etc. Beis Yosef writes that this is precisely implied by the wording of Rambam and Tur, which the Mechaber follows. According to that understanding, Rama should not have introduced the view as “some say.” Rama, however, follows his position in Darkei Moshe, where he writes that the phrase, “The son is not obligated to go begging,” implies that if he need not beg, he is obligated to support his father, even where the ordinary laws of charity would not obligate him to surrender so much. Bach cites Semag and Semak as likewise stringent. Nevertheless, in explaining Rambam and the Tur, Bach agrees with Beis Yosef, and Perishah writes similarly. This also resolves the statement that the court compels him to provide support. A court does not ordinarily compel fulfillment of honoring parents, a positive commandment whose reward is written alongside it; rather, it compels him under the laws of charity, which also include a negative commandment, as explained below at the beginning of siman 248.
