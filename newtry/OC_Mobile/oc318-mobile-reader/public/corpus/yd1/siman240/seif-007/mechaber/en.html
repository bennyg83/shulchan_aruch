He is obligated to stand before his father; and if the father is his son's disciple, each of them rises before the other. (Tur in the name of Rosh.) {Rama: And if the son wishes to waive his honor to serve his father — the matter is in his hands, for a rabbi who waives his honor — his honor is waived; and specifically in private, or even in public if he is established in his city such that everyone knows he is his father; but if the son is great in Torah and the father is not established in his city, there is concern for disgrace of the Torah if the son is disgraced before the father, and they should distance themselves from each other so that neither lightens his honor before the other. (All is the reasoning of the rabbi according to Beit Yosef.) And so did Maharam act with his father.}
