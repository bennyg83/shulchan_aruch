(Likut) A student etc. — for we say in the first chapter of Megillah: greatness of Torah etc.; and we say in chapter 4 of Eruvin (47) — R' Elazar: even etc., that it is not from everything; and even for any mitzvah that cannot be fulfilled through others, honor of father and mother is set aside, as written in seif 12 — end.
And similarly if, etc. As stated in seif 15; and as stated there, 1a, alternative version: "in this," etc.; see seif 5: "this," etc. — and certainly bodily pain; furthermore, they only stated "feeding him," etc. — which pertains to his father. See Tosafos 32a s.v. "R' Yehuda": "And it appears to R' Yehuda," etc.; and certainly for us, as above seif 5.
(Gleanings) And similarly, etc. See Tosafos Yevamos 6a s.v. "she'ken," etc.: "And it appears to R' Yehuda," etc. — thus the agency is not the mitzvah itself; only what pertains to feeding him, etc.; and certainly in such a case; see what is stated there.
(Gleanings) And similarly, etc. See Tosafos Yevamos 6a s.v. "she'ken": "And it appears," etc. This is the language of the Ramban and Rashba there — after they raised many challenges against Rashi's explanation there, they wrote: "The words of R' Chananel appear correct — that it refers to the negative commandment of driving an animal on Shabbos," etc. Even though the father told him to drive another animal — since the essence of honor is only in what benefits him, as stated in Kiddushin (31b): "What is honor?" etc. But if he told him to do something that has no benefit in it, etc. What is stated: "I might think that if his father told him 'become impure'" — not for nothing, but to bring fruits from the cemetery and the like. Similarly, "do not return" — so as not to neglect feeding him. Similarly, driving an animal for building the Beis HaMikdash, etc. See the Rashba — it is implied from his words that specifically to override a negative commandment; see there.
