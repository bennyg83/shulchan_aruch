For a blessing. Even within twelve months one may say "may his resting place be for a blessing" — only that after twelve months he should not say it; the Kesef Mishneh — because judgment of the wicked in Gehinnom is only twelve months, and so is the custom. Shach. And the Taz wrote: it is hard for me from what the Rema wrote in siman 376 — the custom is not to say Kaddish only eleven months so as not to hold him as wicked; and why should the Kesef Mishneh conclude "may his resting place be for a blessing" all twelve months; and it must be that regarding the Kesef Mishneh it is different — since after twelve months one must say zikhrono livrakhah and in the twelfth month one cannot say zikhrono livrakhah lest he has not yet merited the World to Come and there is falsehood here; and if he does not say anything at all there is disrespect since everyone knows he died — automatically it is impossible to be in sit and do not act; therefore it is better to mention him with the Kesef Mishneh formula which is not disrespect and also not falsehood, for he says "if there is judgment upon him I accept it" — for even on a completely righteous person they say so, and it is possible he did not see the face of Gehinnom; unlike regarding Kaddish it is more correct not to say anything and to be in sit and do not act; and even if he does not yet merit nevertheless no falsehood comes from his mouth; and he wrote further in the Gemara that one should say "his memory for a blessing for life of the World to Come"; and those who conclude zikhrono livrakhah are missing a letter and need to conclude zikhrono livrakhah — end quote.
