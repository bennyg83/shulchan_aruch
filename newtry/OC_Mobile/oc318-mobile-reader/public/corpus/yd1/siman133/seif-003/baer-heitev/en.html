All of it. Siftei Kohen writes that casting the value of the benefit into the Dead Sea does not help, as stated below in siman 134. It is possible that had he informed the employer at the outset that he was unwilling to transport that barrel, the employer would not have hired him at all.
A perutah. Turei Zahav writes that this requires examination. The Tanna in the Mishnah in the chapter HaZahav lists five laws involving a perutah and should also have listed that wine used for libation does not prohibit a mixture when its value is less than a perutah. Nekudot HaKesef answers that the Tanna listed some cases but omitted others. See there.
A barrel. Turei Zahav writes that in this case one need not cast anything into the Dead Sea. He may leave that barrel with the non-Jew and accept the remainder. In the first case, by contrast, the entire mixture had already acquired the status of a prohibited substance.
And ab initio. This applies even if their combined value is less than a perutah. Thus writes Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.
