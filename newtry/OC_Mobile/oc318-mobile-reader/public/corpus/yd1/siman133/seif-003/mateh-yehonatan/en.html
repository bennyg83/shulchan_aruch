(Siman 133 in Shulchan Aruch seif 3) A gentile who hired the worker, etc., that if it were less than a perutah. See Shach seif kaf-zayin, who wrote that he learned this from Rashba, etc.; and according to what Tosafot wrote there it is not, etc.; however in my humble opinion this is not necessary; and one may say that Tosafot also concede here — for the entire reason that all the hire is forbidden in benefit is because they are connected to each other: since he hired him for a hundred barrels, if he would not also pass the barrel of libated wine he would not pay him anything; if so, all the hire comes on account of the hire of libated wine; and all this if it is a perutah; if so there is insistence and he will not pay the rest either; but if there is no hire of libated wine of a perutah he certainly would not insist on this account not to pay him his wages — therefore the wages are permitted for everyone, since he does not benefit from libated wine [and this is the intent of Tosafot there, that he does not insist because it is less than a perutah; and if so, this is the same as that case there].
(There seif 6) Even if the gentile said to him: guard for free, etc. See Taz seif kaf-zayin, who wrote: this — that he did not state the reason that he holds a favor for him, etc. — see there; and in truth there is no difficulty — for that which he forbade above in seif 4 for free on account of holding a favor applies where he performed an action with libated wine, such as transferring it from place to place and the like; but here he performs no action, only sits and guards — why should it be forbidden? And the reason must be that he wants its existence; and the reason of holding a favor as above does not apply — and so it seems to me.
