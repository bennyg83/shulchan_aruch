All his wages are forbidden. The Tur writes in the name of the Ra'avad that he nevertheless has a remedy by casting into the Dead Sea the amount of benefit corresponding to the rental of that barrel. The Perishah writes in the name of the Maharshal that this applies if he already received the wages, implying that so long as he has not yet received them this remedy does not apply and he may not accept them. My father-in-law of blessed memory writes that even if he has not yet received them, this remedy applies. He should cast into the Dead Sea the amount of benefit represented by that barrel, and he may receive the remainder. He brings proof from seif 4, which states that a pledge may be taken corresponding to his wages. This implies that even before the non-Jew provides him with the benefit corresponding to receipt of his wages, taking a pledge against the wages is effective, end quote. In my humble opinion, neither view is correct. The primary reason is as stated in Toras HaBayis HaAruch, that we rule like R' Eliezer, who says in the chapter Kol HaTzelamim that one should cast the forbidden benefit into the Dead Sea. This applies even to one forbidden barrel that became mixed among other barrels. Therefore, once the mixture has already come into existence, why should casting the corresponding benefit into the Dead Sea not be effective merely because the money still remains in the non-Jew's hand? Nevertheless, before receiving the wages it is also inappropriate to say that he should cast some of his own money into the Dead Sea. If he afterward accepts the entire wage, he will receive it in a forbidden manner. What did he accomplish by previously casting away his own money? Rather, he should receive the entire wage from the non-Jew and then cast one part of it into the Dead Sea, or cast away the first portion he received from the non-Jew. Even if he does not cast away that very portion but instead casts away an equivalent amount from money he already possesses, that is sufficient, because one forbidden portion is now in his possession and he does not benefit from the amount equal to the prohibition. This is not so before he receives it.
Less than the value of a perutah is permitted b'dieved. This requires further study. Why did the Tanna not include this among the five cases involving a perutah listed in the Mishnah in the chapter HaZahav? He should also have counted this law, that wages earned for handling yayin nesech do not become forbidden when they are worth less than a perutah.
The wages for the barrel of wine are forbidden. Here it is unnecessary to cast anything into the Dead Sea. Rather, he should leave the payment for that barrel with the non-Jew and receive the remainder. This differs from the first clause, where the designation of prohibition has already fallen upon the entire amount through mixture.
