All his wages are forbidden. In the Tur it is written in the name of the Raavad: nevertheless, he has a remedy by taking the benefit to the Dead Sea, meaning the wage of that barrel. The Perishah wrote in the name of Maharshal that this means if he already received them, implying that as long as he has not yet received them this remedy does not apply and he is forbidden to receive them. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that even if he has not yet received them, this remedy applies: he should take the benefit of that barrel to the Dead Sea, and the rest is permitted for him to receive. He brings proof from what is written in seif 4, that they take a pledge corresponding to his wage, which shows that even before the gentile gives him benefit corresponding to receipt of his wage, taking a pledge corresponding to his wage is effective. In my humble opinion, it appears unlike both this view and that view. The essential reason is as written in Torat HaBayit HaAroch, that we rule like Rabbi Eliezer, who says in Perek Kol HaTzelamim that one takes the benefit to the Dead Sea, and this is so even with a barrel that became mixed among barrels. If so, whenever the mixture has already occurred, why should taking its benefit to the Dead Sea not help? What difference does it make that the money is still in the gentile’s hand? Rather, before receiving it, it also does not make sense to say that he should take from his own to the Dead Sea, for if he later receives all the wages, he will receive them in prohibition; what does it help that he already took from his own? Rather, he should receive all of it from the gentile and afterward take one of them to the Dead Sea, or the one he first received from the gentile. Even if he does not actually take one of them, but takes from what he already has of his own, that is sufficient, since one prohibited item is already in his hand and he will not benefit from the amount equal to the prohibition, unlike before that point.
Less than the value of a perutah is permitted after the fact. This requires study: why did the Tanna not count this in the Mishnah in Perek HaZahav, where he counts the five perutot? He should also have counted this, that the wage of yayin nesech does not prohibit with less than the value of a perutah.
The wage of a barrel of wine is forbidden. In this case one need not take anything to the Dead Sea; rather, he should leave it with the gentile and receive the rest. This is unlike the first clause, where the name of prohibition has already fallen upon everything through mixture.
