(Siman 133, seif 3) A non-Jew who hired the worker, etc., where the amount was less than a perutah. See Shach, seif katan 7, who wrote that this was learned from the Rashba, etc., but according to what Tosafos wrote there it is not so, etc. However, in my humble opinion this is not compelled, and one can say that Tosafos also agree here. The entire reason that all the wages are forbidden for benefit is that the components of the hire are connected to one another. Since he hired him to transport one hundred barrels, had he not transported the barrel of yayin nesech as well, he would not have paid him anything. Thus, all the wages result from the hire involving yayin nesech. This is so only where the wage for that barrel is worth a perutah, because then there is a matter over which the employer is particular, and he would not pay the remainder either. But if the wage attributable to the yayin nesech is not worth a perutah, he certainly would not be particular about that and refuse to pay the worker's wages. Therefore the wages are permitted according to all opinions, since the worker does not derive benefit from yayin nesech. This is the intent of Tosafos there when they wrote that he is not particular because it is worth less than a perutah, and accordingly it is the same as the case there.
