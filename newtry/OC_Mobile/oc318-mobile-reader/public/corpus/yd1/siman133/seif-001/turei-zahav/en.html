In a cemetery. For there it is not made for sowing — so wrote Beit Yosef; he sought a reason why they required a cemetery; and it is astonishing to me why he did not write as Tosafot wrote there, the passage beginning "and to bury them," etc., thus: and it seems one must bury them a little deep lest one come to dig after them; and after it becomes dust one need not fear lest he take it for fertilizer, for it is not customary to dig after graves for fertilizer — end his words. It seems to me the explanation of their words: initially they said lest one come to dig after them, for the ash itself is needed for many things; and therefore they feared lest one come to take the ash for some purpose; and afterward it was difficult for them — for one should fear lest he take from the dust even when it is deep, and that is after it becomes dust; for as long as it is ash it does not sprout, as written siman 28; therefore Tosafot wrote: after it becomes dust — that is, when it rests a long time everything returns to dust and then it sprouts — he takes it for fertilizer; for this they answered: since it rests in a cemetery there is no custom to dig in graves — meaning in a place of graves after fertilizer; unlike if it rested in another place, one may well fear lest he take from it for fertilizer — thus the explanation of their words. And it is difficult for me regarding Beit Yosef: why did he not also go this way, and why did he need to seek a reason from his own mind; and he had to say afterward that if ash is found in a pit they should not take it — for it is obvious that prohibition does not apply here, that people stole and dug — and all this unnecessarily, for it was already explained in Tosafot that we mentioned the reason for this.
