Or he casts it into the sea. Meaning, he grinds it and casts it into the sea. The unqualified term sea means any sea; the Dead Sea is not required, as it is in the case of fruit. Fruit remains intact, and ships travel on ordinary seas, so perhaps someone will find the fruit and derive benefit from it.
In a cemetery. The Beit Yosef explains that this is because a cemetery is not used for planting. He sought a reason why burial specifically in a cemetery was required. I am astonished that he did not write as Tosafos wrote there, s.v. VeLikberinhu, etc.: It appears that one must bury them somewhat deeply, lest someone come and dig them up. After they become earth, there is no concern that someone will take them for fertilizer, because people do not ordinarily dig among graves for fertilizer, end quote. It appears to me that their words mean as follows. Initially they say, lest someone come and dig them up, because ash itself is useful for several purposes, and they were therefore concerned that someone might take the ash for some use. They then ask that even when it is buried deeply, one should be concerned that someone may take the resulting earth. This refers to after it has become earth, because as long as it remains ash it does not promote growth, as written in siman 28. Tosafos therefore explain that after it has remained there for a long time and has entirely become earth, it can promote growth, and one might take it for fertilizer. They answer that because it is placed in a cemetery, people do not ordinarily dig in the place of graves for fertilizer. Had it been buried elsewhere, however, there would indeed be concern that someone might take it for fertilizer. This appears to me to be the meaning of their words. I find it difficult why the Beit Yosef did not follow this explanation and instead sought a reason of his own, which then forced him to say that if ash is found in a pit people will not take it because it is evident that it came from something forbidden, and that the principle that people steal and dig does not apply. All this is unnecessary, for the reason has already been explained by Tosafos as stated.
Therefore one should not be stringent regarding these laws, etc. In responsum 76, Maharam Padua cites the Hagahos Mordechai, which rules that it is forbidden to rent one's cellar to a non-Jew for storing yayin nesech. There is no distinction between the present era and the era of the Sages of the Gemara except regarding accepting yayin nesech in payment of a debt. It is also forbidden to pour yayin nesech for a non-Jew, but it is permitted to pour our wine for them. The first ruling is also cited by the Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim, in the name of Maharam, and by Rabbeinu Yerucham.
