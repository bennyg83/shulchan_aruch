Nevertheless it distresses him if it breaks, etc. And that he did not state the reason that he would hold a favor for him on this — as written seif 4 — it seems to me that the gentile tells him explicitly from the outset that he will not hold a favor for him on account of this; and therefore Tur wrote this, and even if the gentile said to him: guard for me for free, etc. — for ostensibly this is superfluous language; rather he should have written: and even for free he should not guard for him — except to teach what I mentioned, that the gentile told him explicitly so; nevertheless it is forbidden on account of wanting its existence.
