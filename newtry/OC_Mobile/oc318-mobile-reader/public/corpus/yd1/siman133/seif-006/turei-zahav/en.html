Nevertheless, he is distressed if it breaks, etc. The reason given is not that the non-Jew will feel indebted to him for this favor, as stated in seif 4. It appears to me that the non-Jew expressly tells him at the outset that he will not feel indebted to him on this account. For this reason the Tur writes here, Even if the non-Jew said to him: Guard it for me without payment, etc. At first glance, this wording is superfluous, and the Tur should simply have written that he may not guard it even without payment. Rather, it teaches the point stated above, that the non-Jew expressly told him that he would not owe him any favor. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because the Jew desires the continued existence of the yayin nesech.
