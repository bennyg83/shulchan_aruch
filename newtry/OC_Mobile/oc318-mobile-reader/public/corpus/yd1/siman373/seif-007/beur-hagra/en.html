It is forbidden, etc. Nazir 42b, Yerushalmi Nazir chapter 3, and Masechet Semachot chapter 4. See Tosafot Niddah 57a, s.v. perhaps, etc.
(Supplement) It is forbidden, etc. As stated in Nazir 42b. Although Eliyahu Rabbah chapter 4 records a dispute between R. Tarfon and R. Akiva, the law follows R. Tarfon here, for Rav Huna supports him. Moreover, an anonymous Mishnah followed by a dispute in a baraita is ruled according to the anonymous Mishnah, which says, 'They told him: do not become impure,' etc. The sugya in Shevuot 17a concerning a nazir in a grave also follows R. Tarfon. In the Yerushalmi the positions are reversed: if he left and reentered, R. Tarfon exempts and R. Akiva obligates. R. Tarfon argued, 'What additional desecration has he added?' R. Akiva replied that while there he had seven-day impurity; once he separated he had only evening impurity, and by reentering he again incurred seven-day impurity. R. Tarfon conceded. All this concerns liability, but all agree it is forbidden. This is unlike Rabbenu Tam. See Rosh, Laws of Impurity 6. End of supplement.
(Supplement) It is forbidden for a kohen, etc.; specifically, etc. Eliyahu Rabbah chapter 4, halachot 13, 15, and 16: an ordinary kohen, etc.; if he was standing, etc.; if he became impure, etc. End of supplement.
