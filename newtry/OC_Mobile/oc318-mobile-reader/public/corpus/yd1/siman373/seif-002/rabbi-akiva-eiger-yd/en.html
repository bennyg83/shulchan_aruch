(Shulchan Aruch, seif 8) And not for a doubtful relative. He may not become impure for them, and they may not become impure for him, as stated in the Mishnah and explicitly in Even HaEzer 3:9, contrary to Beit Yaakov 130. If a man is possibly the nine-month son of a first husband who was a kohen, or the seven-month son of a second husband who was an Israelite, and his principal teacher dies, one may argue that he may become impure for him. The Rosh cites an unresolved Yerushalmi whether a student may become impure for his teacher and rules stringently. Here there is a compound doubt: perhaps he is an Israelite, and even if he is a kohen, perhaps a kohen-student may become impure for his teacher.
