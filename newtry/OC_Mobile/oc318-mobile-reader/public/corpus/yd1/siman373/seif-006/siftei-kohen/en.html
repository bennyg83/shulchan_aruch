Until the golel is closed, etc. See above at the beginning of siman 375 in the Tur regarding what golel means: Rashi explained that golel is the cover of the coffin, while Rabbeinu Tam explained that golel is the stone placed upon the grave, etc.; see there. The Bach there wrote that we follow Rashi. The Ramban in Torat HaAdam, brought by the Tur there, wrote that according to Rashi, when they place him in the house in a coffin and seal the cover with nails with intent to place the coffin in a niche or grave, this is considered sealing, etc. The Ramban in Torat HaAdam wonders at him: how is it possible to say that if they sealed his coffin in the house, his son may no longer become tamei for him, etc.? The Beit Yosef brings this there. In my humble opinion, when Rashi explains everywhere that golel is the coffin cover, he does not mean even when it was sealed in the house; rather, he means from when the coffin cover is sealed in the grave. Know this from chapter Sho'el, Shabbat 152b, where it says that everything said in the presence of the dead is known until the golel is closed. Rashi there too explained that golel is the cover placed on his coffin, for it is written, "and the dust returns to the earth as it was." Rashi explains: his body is called dust, and when it returns to the earth as it was, immediately "the spirit returns," for he has no more spirit. According to this, if the coffin was sealed in the house with nails, even according to Rashi his son may become tamei for him until it is sealed in the grave.
