A betrothed woman. See Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Avel chapter 2, who questions whether, if a woman was betrothed by a rabbinic form of kiddushin, that rabbinic kiddushin can override the positive commandment that a kohen become tamei for his wife. He brings proof that rabbinic kiddushin do not have the power to override that positive command. His proof, however, is not decisive, because the Yevamot passage he cites shows only that rabbinic kiddushin cannot create a situation in which one transgresses the negative prohibition against becoming tamei. Here, by contrast, it may be that rabbinic kiddushin do have power to suspend the positive command to become tamei, through passive nonperformance. He further explains why he did not raise the same doubt regarding a rabbinically married woman, such as a minor whose marriage is rabbinic. In that case it is obvious that the kohen does not become tamei for her because she is presumed to have had relations, and the law is that he does not become tamei for a raped or seduced woman. Even if she entered chuppah and had not in fact had relations, a married woman is presumed to have had relations; see there.
