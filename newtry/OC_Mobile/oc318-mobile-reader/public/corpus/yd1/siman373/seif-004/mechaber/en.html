These are the relatives for whom he becomes impure: his wife — married and fit; but unfit, or divorced, or betrothed — he neither becomes an onen nor becomes impure for her, and likewise she neither becomes an onen nor becomes impure for him; and he becomes impure for his mother even if she became profaned; and for his son and daughter when it is clear to him that their months were complete, or they are thirty days and onward, even if unfit — except his son and daughter who are mamzer or from a maidservant or gentile woman; and for his brother and sister from his father, even if unfit, unless they are children of a maidservant or gentile woman; but for his brother and sister from his mother he does not become impure; and likewise not for his betrothed sister, even if betrothed to a kohen; and not for one raped or seduced; but he becomes impure for his sister who was betrothed and divorced and is mature, or a woman whose hymen was ruptured by wood; and he becomes impure for a yevamah, even if he wrote in her ketubah: if I die childless your ketubah returns to my father's house (meaning: her father's house).
