Rashi in chapter Arba'ah Achin, Yevamot 29, wrote: “and she does not become tamei” means she need not occupy herself with him. It is not literal, for the daughters of Aharon were not warned against tumah; rather, since it taught “she is not an onenet,” which was necessary, it also taught “she does not become tamei,” because it wished to teach two laws about her and about him. So I heard. To me it appears that “she does not become tamei” means on a festival, when Israelites are warned about tumah by “you shall not touch their carcasses.” End quote. According to Rashi's second explanation, it is forbidden for her to become tamei for him on a festival, because she too is warned to purify herself for the festival. It is astonishing that early and later poskim omitted this in the basic law, that one is obligated to purify oneself on the festival. Even according to the first explanation, regarding festival purity all agree that everyone is obligated to purify themselves. End quote from Beit Hillel. What he wrote, that all poskim did not bring the law that one is obligated to purify oneself, is not precise, for the Rambam brought it in chapter 16 of Hilkhot Tum'at Ochalin, and Semag in positive commandment 246. Poskim who discuss laws not practiced nowadays also bring this law. But the Rosh, Tur, and other poskim who bring only practiced laws do not bring it, because the principal law of purifying for the festival applies to the Temple period, when they needed to be pure to eat sacred foods, as Rashi and the Rambam wrote. Nowadays there is no practical difference between the festival and the rest of the year, for we are tamei with corpse tumah, we have no ashes of the red heifer, and tumah was also decreed on the lands of the nations; what purity applies? Although some poskim wrote that even nowadays one must purify himself on the festival, they did not mean corpse or creeping-animal tumah, but tumah that emerges from a person's body and the like. The purity also alludes to purity of the soul, for through bodily purity one awakens and refines the soul's purity, as stated in several places in Reishit Chokhmah and Shelah. This is clear. Therefore the poskim adopted Rashi's first explanation, that “she does not become tamei” is not literal, but means she is not obligated to occupy herself with burial matters. But after marriage, whether a kohen and certainly a Yisrael, whether man or woman, all are equal regarding becoming tamei for and occupying themselves with their seven dead relatives, both on the festival and on other days of the year; and if they do not wish to, we compel them. See Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 68.
