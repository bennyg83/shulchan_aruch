And likewise, etc. but, etc. In Torat HaAdam he cited a baraita: "the [burial of the] dead and circumcision — circumcision takes precedence, for this is a mitzvah for the living and that for the dead; the dead and the bride — the bride takes precedence; the mourner's house and the wedding house — the wedding house takes precedence; but if he has a livelihood in the mourner's house, the mourner's house takes precedence. But a groom who is a mourner — [the groom's duties take] precedence, because the groom eats and feeds others, while the mourner does not eat until others feed him." See Beit Yosef.
(Supplement) And likewise if there is no, etc. Eliyah Rabbah, chapter 11. "But another mourner, etc." Ibid., chapter 12. (End.)
