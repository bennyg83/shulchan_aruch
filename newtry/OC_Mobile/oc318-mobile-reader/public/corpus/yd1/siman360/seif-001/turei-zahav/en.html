A met mitzvah takes precedence. In the Tur he concludes: because it overrides Torah study and the reading of the Megillah, etc. This is in the Gemara, brought by the Rosh in chapter 2 of Ketubot, and he wrote there that although even a dead person who is not a met mitzvah also overrides Torah study, as written in siman 361, nevertheless it mentioned met mitzvah because of the other matters stated there in the Gemara, and the Tur followed the language of the Gemara. In Perek HaIshah Rabbah we say that a met mitzvah means anyone who calls and others do not answer. Tosafot in Nazir 43 explain this to mean that if the dead person, were he able to call, would have relatives who answer him, he is not a met mitzvah. See below siman 374 seif 3, where it is written differently.
