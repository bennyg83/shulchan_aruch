With an intestinal illness. See the Taz, end of seif katan 5, and Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Even HaEzer, siman 69.
To bless the sick. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit, who writes in the name of Mahari Weil that one should not recite a blessing for a sick person when the sick person is elsewhere. See Responsa Nachalat Shivah, siman 59, who disagrees and in practice blessed a sick person on Shabbat in the synagogue even though the patient was not in that city, but in a settlement more than an hour's journey away. He brought proof from Gittin, that even where one left an elderly or ill person, one acts on the presumption that he is still alive. A certain great scholar wrote to uphold Mahari Weil's position, and the author responded to him; see there from siman 16 through siman 81.
