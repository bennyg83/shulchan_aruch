With intestinal illness, etc. We are concerned that since she is sick and he is healthy, perhaps he will come upon her by force. But a woman may attend to a man, because since the man is sick there is no concern that he will come upon a healthy woman by force; since she is healthy and he is sick, she can save herself from him. This is the language of the Atteret Zahav, from the words of the Beit Yosef. But the Darkhei Moshe, Bach, and Derishah objected to the Beit Yosef on this and explained that since this law was stated regarding intestinal illness, this shows it applies only to intestinal illness, where when one relieves oneself there is exposure in front and behind, creating concern that his impulse may overpower him, etc.; see there. See above siman 195 s.k. 19 and below siman 352 seif 3.
And so the custom is to bless the sick, etc. See Orach Chayim siman 54 seif 2, where the Rav wrote that some have the custom to bless the sick between Yishtabach and Yotzer. In the Dinim VeHalakhot of Mahari Weil siman 8 it is written that one should not bless the sick on Shabbat and Yom Tov, and so he wrote in responsum siman 115. At the end of Orach Chayim siman 288, the Rav wrote that it is permitted on Shabbat to bless a dangerously ill person on that day.
Comforting mourners precedes, etc. For comforting mourners is kindness with the living and with the dead. The Bach wrote that when one cannot fulfill both, he leaves the sick person and engages in comforting mourners. But when it is possible to fulfill both, visiting the sick comes first, in order to request mercy for him that he live, or to sweep and clean before him, which is considered as though one gives him life. See above siman 240 seif 12. The law of one who forbade his fellow benefit from himself, whether he may visit him, is explained in siman 221 seif 4.
