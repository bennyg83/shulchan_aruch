It is permitted to sell it in such a case. That is, this follows the Tur's view based on the Rosh, who wrote: What is meant by "benefit from its price" that is forbidden? For example, if he sells it to someone who does not know that it is a peter ḥamor and takes from him the full value. But to take from him only what it is worth in excess of the lamb — since the buyer must pay the cost of the lamb [to the priest] — that is perfectly fine. The Tur derived from this that the core of the permission is that he does not take all the money but leaves with him the value of the lamb. If so, one may ask: why does he predicate the prohibition in the opening clause upon not informing the buyer? — for even if he informs him it is forbidden if he takes the full price! One may answer: indeed so, but it speaks of the usual way of things, for when he informs him, the buyer will certainly not pay the full price since he knows he must give a lamb to the priest. But if it happens that someone pays the full price even knowing he will need to give a lamb, the seller has done something forbidden — so the Tur understands it. But the Beit Yosef interpreted the words of the Rosh that it all depends on informing: if he informs him, it is permitted to receive the full money even without deducting the value of the lamb, since we say the buyer regards the peter ḥamor as precious and is willing to forgo the purchase price, or we say he gave him the value of the lamb as a gift. It appears from Rema's words that he rules like the Tur. In my opinion, it seems that since at the time he sells the peter ḥamor and informs him, both parties certainly intend only what is permitted and not what is forbidden — therefore whatever amount he stipulates at the time of the sale, he intends only that the buyer pay that sum for the permitted part, namely what it is worth after giving a lamb to the priest. Even though in the market it is not worth that much, this person intended only this exact amount — just as we find regarding overreaching [ona'ah] with household utensils, where there is no ona'ah since one sells his items at a premium and there is no prohibition in this. The prohibition applies only when he explicitly states that this sum is for all of it and he will not give a lamb to the priest. So it seems to me.
