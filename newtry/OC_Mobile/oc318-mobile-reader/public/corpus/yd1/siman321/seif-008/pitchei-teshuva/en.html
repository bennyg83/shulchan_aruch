Its money is forbidden. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Yoreh Deah, siman 84, who resolves the Raavad's objection that prohibitions of benefit generally do not transfer their prohibition to the money received for them, except idolatry and Sabbatical produce. He explains that this principle applies to prohibitions of benefit having no corrective remedy. A firstborn donkey, however, can be redeemed, after which it becomes permitted in benefit. The reason the donkey itself is forbidden in benefit is also to protect the kohen from loss. Therefore, if one sells it before redemption, the proceeds are forbidden for the same reason, because the owner still could have redeemed it. According to this explanation, its proceeds are forbidden only before the donkey is beheaded; after beheading, the proceeds are permitted. See Shaar HaMelech, chapter 5 of Hilchot Ishut, halachah 2, who gives another well-reasoned resolution of the same difficulty. See also Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 160.
