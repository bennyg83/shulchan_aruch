See Noda BiYehudah, Yoreh De'ah siman 84, who wrote to resolve the Raavad's challenge to this, namely that we rule that prohibitions of benefit do not transfer their prohibition to their proceeds except idolatry and shemittah. He wrote that this rule was said only regarding prohibitions of benefit that have no remedy. But a petter chamor has a remedy, for after redemption it becomes permitted in benefit; and even the petter chamor itself is forbidden in benefit only so that the kohen not lose out. Therefore, if he sold it, the money is forbidden for that same reason, since it is in his power to redeem it. He wrote that according to this we say the proceeds are forbidden only before breaking its neck; after the neck-breaking, its proceeds are permitted. See there. See Sha'ar HaMelekh, Hilkhot Ishut chapter 5 law 2, s.v. ve-da'at Rabbeinu, who also resolves the above question with good reasoning; see there.
