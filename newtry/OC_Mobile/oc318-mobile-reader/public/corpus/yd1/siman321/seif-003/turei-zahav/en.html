And if he redeemed it, it is redeemed. No further redemption is needed due to doubt, since it does not have bodily sanctity like a doubtful firstborn:
It is a doubt. The meaning is: if afterward the peter ḥamor [firstborn donkey] died before this redemption was given to the priest, he is not obligated to give it to the priest — since the Israelite can say: perhaps the redemption never took effect upon it. But if after the redemption was given to the priest the peter ḥamor died, the redemption remains with the priest, since he holds it in possession — for the owner himself gave it to him. This is not analogous to the case where the priest seized it [takfo kohen], from which we remove it, as above §315. One should raise a difficulty from what is written in Resp. Maharil §174 regarding a doubtful firstborn that the Israelite gave to the priest, and afterward discovered that according to strict law he had no obligation to give it — had he known this from the start he would not have given it — and therefore it is like [the case of] the priest who seized it, and we remove it from him. He compares it to one who sent money to his fellow to give to a non-Jew's hand and the non-Jew forgot — the money returns to its owner, because he did not send it with this understanding; this is in Ḥoshen Mishpat §183. This apparently contradicts the case here, for here too it was a doubtful case and he could say: had I known this was not a valid redemption I would not have given it to you. One may answer that here it is different — because at the time of the redemption, the priest in any case had an obligation upon him to receive a valid redemption; it is just that he erred and thought this was a valid redemption, and therefore the priest may retain it. This is not the case regarding a doubtful firstborn, where there is doubt whether he owes him anything at all.
