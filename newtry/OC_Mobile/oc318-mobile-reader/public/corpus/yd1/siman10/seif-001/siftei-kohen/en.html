New. — Rather, he cut with it a section of wood for avodah zarah—Gemara: because attendants of avodah zarah are not forbidden until they use them, as below siman 139.
Or old in which there is no concern of defilement from avodah zarah. — That is to say, that they kashered it in a manner in which there is no concern of defilement from avodah zarah, as below siman 121.
Permitted to slaughter with it a healthy animal. It implies even l'chatchila; and below siman 142 seif 2 he wrote: a knife of avodah zarah with which he slaughtered—is permitted, etc.—which implies it is forbidden l'chatchila when it speaks of one not kashered from defilement of avodah zarah; and so is explained from Hagahot HaRav there. And if a gentile's knife needs immersion before slaughter—see above siman 120 seif 5.
Because it spoils. — And this is not the benefit of keeping it alive—for its monetary value would have been greater after slaughter, when it stood for three things: to grow, to bear young, and for plowing, and for eating (Rashi); and see above siman 142.
And it is forbidden to slaughter with it a dangerously ill animal. — Thus wrote the Tur; and if he slaughtered with it, the Rashba wrote: he should take the benefit to the Dead Sea and it is permitted—and how much is that? Enough for the wage of a knife to slaughter with it. And I cannot (agree)—the Rosh wrote: one need not, for with such slight benefit it is permitted b'dieved, end of his words. The Mechaber speaks here only regarding the prohibition of warning not to slaughter with it l'chatchila; but regarding the prohibition of the animal itself he relies on what he wrote in siman 142, where these laws are primary—and thus: a knife of avodah zarah with which he slaughtered, etc., a dangerously ill animal is forbidden, therefore; and this is the language of the Rambam, meaning that the entire animal is forbidden, as he wrote there in s.k. 6. And it is astonishing on the Bach, who wrote: it appears from what the Shulchan Aruch wrote in general, and he did not write that one should take the benefit to the Dead Sea—that he ruled like the Rosh, etc. And also on the Taz it is difficult, for here he wrote: and this is specifically l'chatchila, but if he slaughtered with it one does not forbid it because of such slight benefit, therefore; and in siman 142 he wrote: and if he slaughtered with it, he takes the benefit to the Dead Sea. And it is possible that here he means not to forbid the entire animal—and this is strained; but it appears to me that all the Rosh's analysis and proofs are only to forbid the entire animal, similar to one who baked bread in an oven—for all the bread is forbidden in his view where it was not mixed, and taking benefit to the Dead Sea does not help, as below siman 142; but the Rosh concedes to the Rashba that here one takes the benefit of the knife-wage to slaughter with it to the Dead Sea, and so is proved explicitly in R' Yerucham there. But the Tur, Beit Yosef, Maharshal there siman 13, and other acharonim understood that the Rosh does not require even taking benefit to the Dead Sea—and this requires study. Nevertheless the Ran, Maharshal, and Bach ruled like the Rashba—and this is not like the Mechaber below siman 142; see there.
A knife of a gentile, etc. — From the Mechaber's general wording it appears he speaks of a plain knife; and he holds like the Rashba that although we hold below siman 122 that a plain vessel of a gentile is not ben yomo, a knife is different—because since its use is frequent, presumably its grease is smeared on its surface and forbids even if it is not ben yomo; and so too in siman 94 s.k. 28, that this is the Mechaber's and HaRav's view there, and so is the consensus of the poskim; and so appears explicitly from Rashi in the first chapter of Chullin daf 8b, s.v. tzonen, and from the words of all the poskim there regarding radish cut with a meat knife; and Maharshal there siman 12 inclines to the Rosh and his colleagues. However, if the knife is ben yomo—even if it appears to the eye that it is clean of all fat and he slaughtered with it—peeling is required, as appears from Tosafot, the Rosh, Semag end of negative commandment 137, Semak, Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 17 from Hilchos Ma'achalot Assurot; and it is possible that the Rashba also concedes to this, and the reason is: since it is ben yomo it absorbs in beit hashchitah what is in the knife's peel; and although there is no power in the heat of beit hashchitah to expel the knife's absorption, as the Rashba wrote (and the Tur cites it)—and his support that therefore sticking ten times in hard ground helps even if it is ben yomo, as explained in Tur Orach Chaim daf 15, 16—nevertheless, if he did not stick it, there is power in the heat of beit hashchitah to expel what is in the knife's peel; but if he stuck it and that thin peel went away, there is no power in the heat of beit hashchitah to expel the absorption inside that peel—and so he proved in Perishah 66; see there. And the Bach in his last pamphlet challenged him and ruled that for the Rashba, if it is clean—even if ben yomo—it is permitted; and I did not stand on the end of his view—and think.
That it has no nicks. — Meaning notches—for if it has notches, sticking does not help, as below siman 121; and so the Rambam there calls notches pegimot, and the Beit Yosef cites it there; and see there the laws of sharpening and sticking.
And afterward slaughtered with it—it is permitted. — It implies specifically b'dieved, but l'chatchila he should not slaughter; and this is specifically with sticking, as appears explicitly from the Rambam's words there; but sharpening appears from his words there that it helps even l'chatchila, and so the Mechaber in siman 121; and it is possible that even according to the Rav there that sharpening does not help for hot l'chatchila, nevertheless here for shechitah one may be lenient, since in any case there are poskim who permit even with sticking l'chatchila, and there are poskim who hold beit hashchitah is cold.
And therefore one should peel it. — Meaning by law; and so wrote in Darkei Moshe, and so Maharshal there and the Bach; and they seized on the Mechaber that he ruled like the Rambam where most poskim and nearly all agreed that peeling is required—for correct reasons. However, what the Bach wrote—that even b'dieved the dish is forbidden, like any thing cooked without peeling—see there siman 69 s.k. 64 [should be 60] and siman 91 s.k. 7 [should be 8].
Permitted to slaughter with it in such a manner, etc. — Meaning by sticking; and so is explained in Darkei Moshe and the Bach and Taz; and in Sefer Lechem at the end of daf 136 he wrote that this gloss must be deleted—see there; and I did not reach the end of his view (and he thought it means it should be turned over in the conclusion—but he should not slaughter, etc.—must be before the opening, and in a place of pressure, etc., end of his words).
