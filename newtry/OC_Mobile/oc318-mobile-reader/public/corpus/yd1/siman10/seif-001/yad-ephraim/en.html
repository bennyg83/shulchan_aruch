And he wrote in She'elot Ya'avetz siman 188: regarding what is written in Lechem HaPanim, that in the Temple they would hang a knife—for judgment of capital cases was done with it—and it was given to fix the shechitah knife, which becomes purified through immersion; and he wrote bitter things about avodah zarah: for what do we care about tumah and taharah, and meat that became tamei from a corpse is also permitted even for our kohanim to eat from it—only regarding the sword itself Rabbenu Tam and R' Chaim Kohen disagreed in chapter 7 of Nazir, etc. But one should be concerned: because it is prohibitions of benefit—for so the Sages taught regarding court executioners: a sword with which one was executed is buried with him; and especially if an Israelite judged with it—even though they killed not according to law, one should be concerned to judge it like an avodah zarah knife, forbidden to slaughter a dangerously ill animal with it—see there; and they already wondered at this in Beit Yehudah and Knesset HaGedolah and the Mechaber brought Ya'avetz's words and brought four (sources) from the Rambam in chapter 15 of Sanhedrin, who wrote: if they were not executed with it, only gentiles, and it does not require burial, and one may make it a knife; and it is close to say that even if an Israelite was executed with it, it is permitted—for gentiles were not commanded in this.
