(Siman 10, Shach s.k. 4) Because in its lifetime it stands for three things—for eating, to grow young, and for plowing, end of his words. And in Kerem Shlomo he explained: for a bird, which does not have these three things—for it does not stand for labor—if so it does not spoil; see there. And it escaped me and other acharonim: that which is explicit in the Gemara Sanhedrin chapter four deaths (daf 59b), that a bird is fit for labor—namely for plowing. And it is certainly that plowing that Rashi and the Shach mention is not specifically—for in every animal it speaks, namely whose legs were cut from the knee down, that it is fit only for plowing and is kosher. And as Rashi wrote in the first chapter of Chullin (daf 7b)—see there, and think. And see in Hagahot Aharon chapter HaZahav, that a lamb is not fit for labor—and think.
(Shach s.k. 5) Sharpening helps even l'chatchila. Meaning: even not in time of pressure—for in time of pressure even sticking is permitted l'chatchila, as the gloss says. But the Taz appears not to hold thus—for he wrote on what the gloss says: and in time of pressure permitted to slaughter with it in such a manner l'chatchila—explanation: if he sharpened on a whetstone or stuck in hard ground; therefore without pressure even sharpening does not help l'chatchila. And one may wonder on the Baal HaBayit Lechem Yehudah, who copied first the Shach's words and afterward the Taz's words as if in one blow—and it is not so, and requires investigation.
(In the gloss seif 1) And therefore one should peel it. The reason: beit hashchitah is boiling. And furthermore, through knife pressure it absorbs—as proved explicitly in the Gemara and Tosafot and Ashrei and other poskim in the first chapter of Chullin (daf 8), that we do not say beit hashchitah is boiling and absorbs except at the end of shechitah when the animal dies.
