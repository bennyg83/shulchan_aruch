New. Meaning: they did not use it with hot food; but nevertheless they already used it for any matter of avodah zarah—such as he cut with it a section of wood; for attendants of avodah zarah are not forbidden until they use them, as below beginning of siman 139.
Avodah zarah. Meaning: that they kashered it, as below siman 121.
Spoils. And the reason: because in its lifetime it stands for three things—to grow, bear young, and for plowing, and for eating; however one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Tov we do not say he spoils; only regarding avodah zarah we say thus; and the Taz wrote in siman 121 seif 12: the same applies to one under a vow of benefit from his fellow—that it is forbidden to slaughter an animal for him, for it is a daily occurrence that we hire a slaughterer, and we do not say he spoils.
Repairs. And he wrote in the Shach: and if he transgressed and slaughtered with it—see below siman 142, where it implies the entire animal is forbidden; but Maharshal and all the acharonim disagreed with the Mechaber and ruled that taking benefit to the Dead Sea suffices—meaning enough for knife-wage to slaughter with it; and the Rosh permits b'dieved, for with such slight benefit it is permitted b'dieved; and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that one should rule like the Rashba.
His (wheel). And he wrote in the Shach: from his writing in general it implies even with a plain knife that is not ben yomo he forbids, because its use is frequent—therefore he forbids if he did not sharpen on a wheel, for presumably grease is smeared on its face; however if the knife is ben yomo, even if it appears clean and he slaughtered with it—if he did not sharpen on a wheel and did not stick it, peeling is required, because as long as it was not stuck in hard ground, heat in beit hashchitah has power to expel what is in the knife's peel.
Times. And he wrote in the Shach: it implies from here that even if ben yomo, sticking helps; and he explained: specifically ten times in hard ground—not as people are accustomed to stick and leave it in the ground; rather the essence is to stick and withdraw and stick until ten times one after another in one day. However from the Mordechai's words it appears sticking does not help with a ben yomo knife, and one should be stringent with ben yomo that peeling is required; and from the Rambam's words it appears even for not ben yomo sticking does not help; and so if it has pegimot—meaning notches—sticking also does not help, as below siman 121.
Permitted. And so in the Shach: it implies specifically b'dieved it is permitted, and specifically with sticking; but sharpening is permitted l'chatchila, as below siman 121; and even according to the Rama who forbids there using it hot—nevertheless for beit hashchitah he concedes it helps.
To peel it. So in the Shach—and this is by law; however what the Bach wrote—that even b'dieved the dish is forbidden, like anything cooked without peeling—see siman 91; and according to the Beit Yosef's view that rinsing suffices, great rubbing with the hands well is required.
L'chatchila. The Taz explained: if he sharpened on a whetstone or stuck in the ground—and all this with a knife that is not ben yomo; but with ben yomo he should not do so l'chatchila.
Afterward. And so in the Shach in the name of the Bach: even with a knife that is not ben yomo and is clean, it is forbidden l'chatchila—and not like Maharshal.
