New is certainly not forbidden for what was designated for avodah zarah—only "new" means it did not absorb from grease and cooking an avodah zarah offering; only cutting wood for avodah zarah. However, if he cut wood to make an asherah for Canaanites—it is permitted (Peri Chadash in siman 101).
That there is no defilement of avodah zarah—that they kashered it in a manner that expulsion does not forbid.
Animal—and the same for a bird, for this too spoils; even though it does not stand for labor; and the proof is that Tosafot challenged from this that it spoils, since we say in Pesachim: slaughter on Shabbat for avodah zarah is called repairing—even though there it speaks of a bird, as above siman 4. And in such a case he brought the view of Damascus Eliezer, who doubts regarding a bird; and there is some room to doubt my proof that I wrote: in general, through shechitah it remains for eating—one could say regarding a bird considered important, he repaired it—what spoilage is here, for it stands only for eating? Unlike an animal, which stands for other things—unlike there with a bird sin-offering forbidden in eating; if so its law equals an animal's; and if for an animal repair from eiver min hachai does not help, so too for a bird one may not slaughter. Nevertheless Tosafot and the poskim who permitted this proof for the Mechaber should be wondered at—and think.
Spoils—for with avodah zarah we require complete benefit, as written: "it shall not cleave to your hand," etc.; and this is not complete benefit, for his wage comes out in his loss; but regarding spoilage in connection, where we hold on Shabbat one is exempt, we require complete spoilage, not lack of benefit.
That he sharpened—for the Rambam, sharpening helps like hag'alah; see below siman 122; and even for the Rama who argues below, here regarding shechitah, since in any case many permit, one can say the Rama concedes sharpening helps like hag'alah (Shach).
Fine—and this means it is known to be ben yomo, for a plain knife is not ben yomo; or grease was smeared on the knife's face—even then not ben yomo, for what is smeared on the knife is not spoiled.
Nicks—meaning notches; if so, visible grease there, and sticking does not help at all (Shach). And from here one learns it is permitted to slaughter with a knife that has above its edge markings and notches—not like Beit Hillel to declare treifah.
Rinsing—meaning complete rubbing, and ordinary rinsing does not suffice for blood required in every piece of meat; and if not ben yomo and no fat smeared—see Peleti, who appears even rinsing is not required, only slight rubbing.
Therefore one should peel it—for most poskim ruled halachah thus, like Rav who said peel; see Peleti, where I gave reason why the Rif ruled rinse, not like Rav.
Permitted l'chatchila—meaning at least by sticking; see Peleti, what he wrote: it is possible to say according to many authors' view that even not in time of pressure, by sticking it is permitted—and all the more in time of pressure.
He should not slaughter, etc.—and with not ben yomo, Maharshal permits and the Shach questioned to be stringent l'chatchila like the Bach; see Peleti s.k. 2, where I lowered the Rosh's view like Maharshal—and so it is correct.
