(Siman 10 seif 1) And forbidden to slaughter with it a dangerously ill animal. And regarding b'dieved see Shach; and it is difficult for me to say: since benefit is forbidden, it is included in "you shall not do"—and if he did, it does not help, and it is as if not slaughtered. Granted for another's animal for avodah zarah, where most poskim hold for the view "there is no benefit from a companion" (ein hana'ah l'chavero)—permitted even in eating—here there is no difficulty to say b'dieved it does not help, for the transgression does not depend specifically on shechitah—for even if he stabs for avodah zarah there is a transgression; if so, why say b'dieved it does not help—for nevertheless stabbing is before you and the transgression was done and cannot be fixed by "it does not help." And why do I say it is not difficult what Mahari"t challenged in his responsum on the mishnah: one who slaughters on Shabbat—his shechitah is valid—shall we say b'dieved it does not help? For there too the transgression is not because of shechitah but because of taking a soul, and it is repair to remove from eiver min hachai status—in this "it does not help" is not relevant, for it died before you and the eiver min hachai prohibition ceased. But here, slaughtering a dangerously ill animal with an avodah zarah knife—the transgression is that he benefits from avodah zarah so the animal not die; and if we judge the shechitah does not help and it is as if it died by itself and like ordinary stabbing—he benefits nothing from the knife; and especially according to what the Tur in Rosh Hashanah (daf 25) wrote regarding blowing a shofar of an olah: even for the view "after the fact it helps," that is in general—for even if the law were it does not help, nevertheless he committed a transgression violating the Merciful One's statement; but with a shofar of an olah, the benefit is fulfillment of the mitzvah, and if he does not fulfill, he neither benefits nor violates the Merciful One's statement—in this all agree it does not help—see there. And if so here slaughtering a dangerously ill animal, we should also say it does not help and he violated nothing—and think. What Peleti wrote to judge regarding shechitah: since shechitah is a mitzvah matter—not to eat meat it dies—is included in "mitzvot were not given for benefit," as explained in Eruvin 31a: an eruv in benefit prohibition depends—if they make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, it is included in mitzvot were not given for benefit; but if they make an eruv for a permitted matter, only that going without an eruv is forbidden—and if so, to save him from that prohibition and nevertheless it is called benefit—and similarly shechitah, since they slaughter for a permitted matter.
(Shach s.k. 6) There is power in the heat of beit hashchitah to expel what is in the knife's peel. This is astonishing—for if so, when slaughtering with a treifah knife, why does rinsing help? For the Rashba—the knife absorbed, only it does not expel with heat in beit hashchitah—yet nevertheless it absorbed what is in the knife's peel; and I would have said per the Rashba in Ma'adanei Yom Tov daf 73: their dispute regarding a knife with which he slaughtered kosher—some say rinsing required, some say not, only wiping—meaning certainly good wiping removes what is visible from the knife; and therefore there is an opinion it helps, for in cold pressure does not expel at all; and the one who requires rinsing holds that because of pressure it expels even in cold, and rinsing the knife is to expel absorption from the knife so it not expel afterward when cutting cold—see there. And that with a knife mentioned, even for cold sticking is required—since the absorption in hot joined, rinsing does not expel it and it expels afterward when cutting cold through pressure; if so, apparently the Shach's words are correct: in cold beit hashchitah regarding not expelling from the knife, nevertheless it is no worse than ordinary cold, that through knife pressure it expels slightly from the knife; and since it expels, the simanim absorb that slight amount up to a peel's worth—for regarding absorption we say through slight boiling in beit hashchitah it absorbs from the visible up to a peel's worth; if so, what difference whether it absorbs the visible itself or what was expelled from the knife? Therefore with a knife mentioned, where rinsing does not expel at all from the knife until afterward cutting cold expels greatly through pressure—therefore if he slaughtered without sticking, peeling is required; but with a treifah knife, absorbed in the knife not through hot, and rinsing helps to expel slightly from the knife until afterward the knife does not expel further when cutting cold—likewise it does not expel in shechitah, like cold. With this the Shach's words in s.k. 17 are also resolved: he concludes specifically here wiping helps for slaughtering with it, but not in general—and seemingly contradicts his own words (siman 91 s.k. 3) that wiping is like rinsing. According to the above it is fine: in general, where there is also pressure as in shechitah, wiping does not help; only in shechitah because simanim are busy. In siman 91, which speaks without pressure, wiping helps. However it still does not suffice—for ultimately why does wiping help with a treifah knife? Wiping only removes the visible, and when he slaughters afterward, through pressure it expels from the knife's peel—and one must say: in beit hashchitah, since busy, it does not absorb that slight amount; if so, with a knife mentioned, why without sticking is peeling required—the simanim did not absorb the slight amount in the knife's peel? And if with a knife mentioned rinsing does not help, with a treifah knife wiping should not help either—and we must say and distinguish: busy helps only with a treifah knife regarding absorption in the knife's peel that was not absorbed through hot—in this busy helps; but with a knife mentioned there is much absorption in the knife's peel, busy does not help—and still think.
(Be'er Heitev) However in the Mordechai first chapter of Chullin it appears even sticking does not help. And that with a treifah knife rinsing helps—must say either as Rashi wrote, the knife is hard to absorb, and therefore one can say it expels through heat in beit hashchitah; or that it absorbed much—namely with an avodah zarah knife it expels through heat in beit hashchitah, but with a knife that slaughtered treifah, absorbed through beit hashchitah is slight absorption and does not expel with heat in beit hashchitah. And what Tosafot Beit Shmuel wrote—that this is Tosafot's view from s.v. kegon shelivnah, etc., and that they did not answer when stuck because they wanted to answer even for the view beit hashchitah is boiling—for in my humble view there is no need to say that for the view beit hashchitah is boiling there is no distinction between expelling and absorbing, only for the view of pressure there is distinction—it absorbs but does not expel; or the knife does not absorb and does not expel through pressure, only simanim are accustomed to absorb from the visible. And as Rashi wrote: for the view of pressure there is distinction—the knife is hard to absorb, but for the view beit hashchitah is boiling, the knife also absorbs—so too one can say for the Rashba's distinction: it absorbs and does not expels—meaning for the view of pressure; but for the view beit hashchitah is boiling there is no distinction. And so appears from the Rashba's language; therefore no difficulty that the reason beit hashchitah is boiling—therefore we ruled halachah in cold, meaning because simanim are busy, etc.; and automatically also with a gentile's knife halachah is like Rav Huna bar bar Chana who rinses—nevertheless we hold stringently to peel, lest like the other language of pressure—in this we can uphold halachah like Rav and distinguish knife mentioned from treifah knife because it absorbs and does not expel; if so for practical law one can say also for Tosafot that sticking helps, since it does not expel; and it is precise in this Tosafot language in what he wrote: even for the view beit hashchitah is boiling—and why did they not teach plainly for the view peel? Rather certainly as above, that for the other language of Rav's reason because of pressure, sticking also helps for Rav; rather he came to answer even for the opposing view because beit hashchitah is boiling, which expels and sticking does not help; if so, on the contrary one should be supported from Tosafot's view that by law sticking helps; and so is explained from Tosafot s.v. vehalachta, etc.—therefore he holds beit hashchitah is boiling, and nevertheless to cut cold with it sticking is not required, etc.; and whence did they learn this—that Shmuel holds beit hashchitah is boiling? Perhaps he holds absorption is because of pressure—and if so, one should ask: with absorption in boiling, sticking is required, and with absorption through pressure, sticking is not required; and if they do not want to distinguish this and hold whatever absorbs, no distinction between absorption in hot and in cold—why did they need this length? Rather he holds beit hashchitah is boiling—he should have said briefly: rather it absorbs. Rather certainly as above, he holds like Rashi: the view that says cold holds beit hashchitah is cold and the knife does not absorb; and Shmuel, who forbade cutting boiling with it, therefore holds beit hashchitah is boiling, as Rashi wrote there explicitly; if so, one can say: since because of pressure it does not absorb, likewise it does not expel; therefore they needed to say the change of building a brick in light, because they want to answer even for the view beit hashchitah is boiling—for if for the view beit hashchitah is cold, just as it does not absorb, likewise it does not expel, and sticking helps—and so as above.
