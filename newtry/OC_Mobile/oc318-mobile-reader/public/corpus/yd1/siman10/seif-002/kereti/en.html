To slaughter, etc.—even when soiled with blood; regarding blood we say: certainly, since the simanim are busy expelling, they do not absorb—so write the acharonim, and it is simple.
With it, etc.—and rinsing is not required; see Taz, who brought in the name of the Bach and Hagahat Agudah that rinsing is required because of absorption of eiver min hachai; and the Taz and Peri Chadash are lenient; see Peleti, where I reconciled the Bach's wonder and proof.
Forbidden to cut boiling with it; and if he koshered by pouring, permitted l'chatchila—so Orach Chaim; and it is correct that Beit Shammai's rejection and knife pressure are no more than pouring.
Boiling—and if he cut, the Shach's view is b'dieved permitted; and we rely on the view that beit hashchitah is cold—see there, where he elaborated; and therefore permitted l'chatchila to peel. See Peleti, where I wrote the main thing is like Peri Chadash, that peeling is required in any case; and with this, peeling suffices, as appears from the Rashba's words; see Peleti, where I labored greatly to reconcile the Rosh and Tosafot—go read there.
And permitted to cut cold with it—even sharp things such as radish and the like (Peri Chadash in the name of the Rashba).
Cold with it—even if he slaughtered many times, since it is because of blood, and blood slides and is slick.
By rinsing—and from the Rosh's words, who wrote rinsing is not required except for a thick slice, we learn: for the view that rinsing is required, a thick slice does not help; and Maharshal disagrees and sent his hand to emend the Rosh—and the Shach's view is as written. Therefore the Tur did not mention here that a thick slice suffices, as in the later seif; and their reason: they are stringent here and lenient with a treifah knife—the Shach gave the reason that there is also the reason that simanim do not absorb, unlike when cutting cold; and this explanation is strained—for regarding wiping visible on simanim it is not relevant "since busy." Only the reason can be said: kosher slaughter-blood is like water and rinses treifah grease from blood, and it has no power to adhere in beit hashchitah—therefore wiping suffices; see Peleti, where there is another reason to distinguish.
That one rinse it first—the Taz wrote this unnecessarily, for even if he slaughtered treifah and slaughtered kosher, rinsing helps; and according to what I wrote, there is no difficulty—for kosher blood's rinsing is as explained.
