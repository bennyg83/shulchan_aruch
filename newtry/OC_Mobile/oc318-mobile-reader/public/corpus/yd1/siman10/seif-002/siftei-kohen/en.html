Even though it is soiled with blood—it is permitted, etc. It implies explicitly that it is permitted to slaughter with it when soiled even without any rinsing at all; and furthermore, regarding a knife with which one slaughtered treifah, the Taz wrote in s.k. 3 specifically that rinsing is required—and the poskim wrote the reason: because in any case there is much blood in beit hashchitah and it does not absorb, because the simanim are busy expelling blood; and see above s.k. 18.
But forbidden, etc. It implies that even if he slaughtered only once, it is forbidden afterward to cut boiling with it by rinsing; and so is the view of the poskim; and so ruled Darkei Moshe and Maharshal there.
Forbidden to cut with it boiling. And b'dieved, if he cut boiling without hag'alah—the Taz did not explain its law; and it appears that although he holds—and so Semag, R' Yerucham, and other poskim—that hag'alah is required, that is l'chatchila; but if he cut boiling b'dieved after rinsing well, it is permitted—for he brought Rashi's wording in general regarding cold beit hashchitah, and the knife does not absorb because it is hard to absorb; and so it appears to me to resolve the Tur's view and Rabeinu Yerucham with what the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Chullin and chapter 25—see there—that he wrote: according to the one who says beit hashchitah is cold, rinsing suffices (and even though regarding slaughtering treifah rinsing suffices, this is like b'dieved to kosher the knife between each treifah animal). And so the Rambam chapter 6: forbidden to eat boiling except with hag'alah, and if he cut boiling it is permitted, end of his words; and the Rav HaMagid wrote that he holds beit hashchitah is cold—see there. And even for the Rashba, who wrote that it is forbidden to cut boiling because it absorbs and returns and expels—nevertheless it appears that peeling suffices, for although it absorbed, it did not absorb more than a peel's worth—for even with a gentile's knife whose grease is smeared on its face and he slaughtered with it, peeling suffices even for the Rashba; therefore heat in beit hashchitah does not absorb more than a peel's worth; if so, the knife also absorbed a slight thing from beit hashchitah and does not forbid more than a peel's worth; and so Maharshal divides explicitly in chapter 25 siman 59 and rules thus; and so is explained in Orach Chaim general rule 58 law 14, who wrote: a knife with which he slaughtered and wants to cut something else hot needs hag'alah l'chatchila, for in beit hashchitah it boils, etc.; however if he cut b'dieved it is permitted with peeling even if hot, end of his words; and it appears that for this reason Orach Chaim and the Rav HaMagid permit hag'alah by pouring even though we hold below simanim 68 and 105 that pouring only expels a peel's worth—rather as explained: the knife did not absorb except a slight thing; and so Maharil in blood laws: peeling suffices if he cut boiling or something sharp.
And permitted to cut, etc. It implies that even if he slaughtered with it many times, it is permitted to cut cold by rinsing; and so the Taz; and so appears from the poskim; and so in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 6 in the name of Rabeinu Yehudah explicitly; and so appears in Darkei Moshe; and it is not comparable to one accustomed to slaughter treifot with it—for in seif 3 here it is different, because its prohibition is from blood, and blood slides and is not absorbed so much in the knife, as the poskim wrote.
By rinsing. It appears from the Taz not writing here too "or wipe with something hard" as adjacent in seif 3—therefore he holds that specifically there wiping helps because there is also the reason that the simanim are busy expelling blood, which is not so here; and so is proved explicitly in the Rosh in the first chapter of Chullin, who wrote: and even to cut cold with it we say in chapter 25: some say rinsing is required and some say rinsing is not required except for a thick slice; and the knife is the subject, end of his words—therefore according to what we hold, as those who say rinsing is required, wiping a thick slice does not help. And Maharshal in chapter 25 siman 59 wrote that it is a printing error in Ashrei and in the place it should say "and not in the Gemara," for the Gemara implies explicitly that for a thick slice its law is like rinsing, end of his words. And it is hard to corrupt all the books; and one can ask: if so, why did the Rosh need to say that a thick slice does not require it? And furthermore, we hold like the one who says rinsing is required—why did the Rosh need to explain the words of the one who says rinsing is not required? And furthermore, what relevance is this matter here? He should have written it below in chapter 25, where this matter's primary place is. Rather, certainly because the Rosh says here that the knife is the subject—not as Rashi explained, that it refers to cold meat cut with it—the Rosh comes to say: how is it possible that the knife is the subject? If so, why does the one who says rinsing is not required not require rinsing, when slaughter-blood is visible on its face? Therefore he says: rinsing is not required except for a thick slice. And that he did not state in general that rinsing is not required but only wiping—is because he holds that any wiping that is not like a thick slice is not wiping at all; and so the Rashba in Ma'adanei Yom Tov daf 73b on the words of the Ra'ah in Beit HaBechirah, who wrote: where did he find this simple comparison that they made good wiping like rinsing? And if because we say regarding a knife with which one slaughtered treifah: rinse it when cold—and if there is a thick slice, more is not needed—on the contrary, infer the opposite: if wiping and rinsing are equal, why say thus? It is obvious—and what does it teach? Rather, to say rinsing is not required; even wiping suffices. And how did he not pay attention to what they said: regarding a knife with which one slaughtered and wants to eat cold with it—rinsing is required, and they did not say wiping is required? We learn that rinsing is required and wiping does not suffice. And the one who says rinsing is not required—not to say that nothing is required to kasher it without anything, for slaughter-blood is on it, end of his words; and so it appears to my humble view from what we say regarding slaughter—and if there is a thick slice, more is not needed—and this was not omitted throughout the entire Gemara to say so in another place where rinsing is mentioned; therefore we say specifically regarding slaughter that wiping suffices here for the reason I explained; but not in general—and this is also the Rosh's view.
