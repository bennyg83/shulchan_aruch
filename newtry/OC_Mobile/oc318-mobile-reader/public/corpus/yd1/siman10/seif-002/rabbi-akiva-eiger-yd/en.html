(Shach s.k. 6) There is power in the heat of beit hashchitah to expel what is in the knife's peel. This is astonishing—for if so, when slaughtering with a treifah knife, why does rinsing help? For the Rashba—the knife absorbed, only it does not expel with heat in beit hashchitah—yet nevertheless it absorbed what is in the knife's peel; and I would have said per the Rashba in Ma'adanei Yom Tov daf 73: their dispute regarding a knife with which he slaughtered kosher—some say rinsing required, some say not, only wiping—meaning certainly good wiping removes what is visible from the knife; and therefore there is an opinion it helps, for in cold pressure does not expel at all; and the one who requires rinsing holds that because of pressure it expels even in cold, and rinsing the knife is to expel absorption from the knife so it not expel afterward when cutting cold—see there. And that with a knife mentioned, even for cold sticking is required—since the absorption in hot joined, rinsing does not expel it and it expels afterward when cutting cold through pressure; if so, apparently the Shach's words are correct: in cold beit hashchitah regarding not expelling from the knife, nevertheless it is no worse than ordinary cold, that through knife pressure it expels slightly from the knife; and since it expels, the simanim absorb that slight amount up to a peel's worth—for regarding absorption we say through slight boiling in beit hashchitah it absorbs from the visible up to a peel's worth; if so, what difference whether it absorbs the visible itself or what was expelled from the knife? Therefore with a knife mentioned, where rinsing does not expel at all from the knife until afterward cutting cold expels greatly through pressure—therefore if he slaughtered without sticking, peeling is required; but with a treifah knife, absorbed in the knife not through hot, and rinsing helps to expel slightly from the knife until afterward the knife does not expel further when cutting cold—likewise it does not expel in shechitah, like cold. With this the Shach's words in s.k. 17 are also resolved: he concludes specifically here wiping helps for slaughtering with it, but not in general—and seemingly contradicts his own words (siman 91 s.k. 3) that wiping is like rinsing. According to the above it is fine: in general, where there is also pressure as in shechitah, wiping does not help; only in shechitah because simanim are busy. In siman 91, which speaks without pressure, wiping helps. However it still does not suffice—for ultimately why does wiping help with a treifah knife? Wiping only removes the visible, and when he slaughters afterward, through pressure it expels from the knife's peel—and one must say: in beit hashchitah, since busy, it does not absorb that slight amount; if so, with a knife mentioned, why without sticking is peeling required—the simanim did not absorb the slight amount in the knife's peel? And if with a knife mentioned rinsing does not help, with a treifah knife wiping should not help either—and we must say and distinguish: busy helps only with a treifah knife regarding absorption in the knife's peel that was not absorbed through hot—in this busy helps; but with a knife mentioned there is much absorption in the knife's peel, busy does not help—and still think.
