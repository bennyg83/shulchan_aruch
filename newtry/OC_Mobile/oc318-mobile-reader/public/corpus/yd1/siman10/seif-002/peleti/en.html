Permitted to slaughter, etc.—the Bach brought in Hagahat Agudah to require rinsing because of eiver min hachai—for the Gemara challenged for the one who says in hot: why is treifah different, that it absorbs prohibition—for kosher also absorbed eiver min hachai, and the knife should require hag'alah. It did not challenge for the one who says cold: kosher also should require rinsing because it absorbed eiver min hachai. Rather we learn the true law requires rinsing; and Peri Chadash wrote the Rashba already stood on this difficulty. And to me it appears simply: certainly the Gemara knew—even according to the one who says once the majority is cut there is no longer eiver min hachai—it holds that beforehand it is also hot and absorbs; therefore for the one who says cold, rinsing helps to remove adhering grease; therefore a thick slice helps; if so, when he begins slaughter and grease of eiver min hachai adheres when reaching slaughter of the majority, when there is no longer eiver min hachai—then through shechitah in heat and knife pressure, the adhering eiver min hachai grease is fixed through slaughter of simanim that have no eiver min hachai, and spurting blood washes it. And there is no difficulty; but for the one who says hot, with full absorption, it fine asks: only hag'alah in boiling helps, and shechitah of the majority and absorption of eiver min hachai remain in the knife and do not leave through shechitah afterward—and think.
To cut boiling with it; and if he cut, the Tur did not explain whether forbidden entirely, or peeling, or kosher by rinsing the boiling thing cut with such a knife. To clarify, we copy the Rosh in chapter Kol HaBasar daf 102b: Rav Nachman said Shmuel said: a knife with which he slaughtered—forbidden to cut boiling with it; cold—some say rinsing required, some say not, except for a thick slice; and Rashi: forbidden to cut because beit hashchitah boiling absorbs the knife from blood, and forbidden to cut boiling because it returns and expels in it, end of his words. And the Rosh in chapter 25 wrote: forbidden to cut boiling; and the one above who says beit hashchitah is boiling requires hag'alah; and for the one who says cold, rinsing suffices. And in the first chapter of Chullin, after writing halachah like Ravina in cold and distinguishing gentile knife from treifah knife, he wrote: and Ravina's dispute is for slaughtering with them; but to cut boiling, even if he slaughtered kosher, we say in chapter 25: a knife with which he slaughtered—forbidden to cut boiling, even if he rinsed it—for even to cut cold, some say rinsing required, some say not, and the knife is the subject, end of his words. Here there are three contradictions: here, although he ruled like Ravina in cold, he ruled cutting boiling requires hag'alah—and Tosafot proved this, since in cold rinsing is required and the knife is the subject, we learn boiling requires hag'alah; and in chapter 25 he wrote for the one who says beit hashchitah cold—and this is Ravina—even cutting boiling, rinsing suffices by law like Ravina. If so, the matters contradict; and also where did the Rosh's proof go—if by rinsing, what difference between cutting boiling and cutting cold? And more wonder on the hints, who ruled cutting boiling requires hag'alah—according to the Rosh's premise for Ravina, who holds like me, rinsing suffices. The Shach sought to say: for the one who says beit hashchitah cold, rinsing is required if he already slaughtered and cut—b'dieved; but l'chatchila to cut with it, Ravina concedes hag'alah is required; and that cold suffices for slaughtering treifah is b'dieved to kosher the knife between treifah and kosher slaughter—this is the root of his words. And the Peri Chadash already rejected this—we do not find this reasoning in authors, and the Rosh's language cannot bear it at all. In my humble view, one could say briefly: Ravina concedes to Rav who said peel—not to slaughter with a gentile knife; unlike one slaughtering treifah—what shall he do, kosher between each slaughter? Therefore they were lenient. And so what Tosafot inferred: why does rinsing suffice and sticking is not required—answer: here it is like b'dieved, and he always sticks the knife ten times in hard ground. And what I wrote here can be understood per Maharsha there, s.v. sakin treifah, etc., who wrote Tosafot: to slaughter after slaughtering kosher—all agree permitted even for R' Elazar's colleague; but to cut boiling forbidden; and Maharsha wrote: it appears even the one who says here cold holds cutting boiling requires hag'alah—meaning he holds beit hashchitah is hot only because simanim are busy expelling and do not absorb—and this does not apply to boiling. Maharsha himself stood: Tosafot's answer that therefore sticking is not required is because beit hashchitah is cold—yet below boiling is required—see there. And it appears without this we can infer in Tosafot: why do they seek this—for Rav Elazar's colleague, slaughtering kosher is permitted in any case, but cutting boiling is forbidden—what difference to Tosafot for Rav Elazar's colleague, halachah is as he holds, and enough that Tosafot wrote so for Ravina, etc.—nevertheless cutting boiling is forbidden for the reason Maharsha wrote, as Tosafot prove through hag'alah—for otherwise what difference between cold and hot? Therefore cutting boiling requires hag'alah; but also in the above Rosh in chapter 25, who wrote for the one who says beit hashchitah hot—in hot, what did he seek from this? Halachah should stand like Ravina! And how could he think for the one who says cold, rinsing—proved from cold rinse that it does not exist? Rather the Rosh holds what the Gemara says: cold—some say rinsing required, some say not—who are they disputing, and what is their nature? To say Shmuel's words—Shmuel spoke only of boiling; but the Rosh holds Shmuel said plainly forbidden to cut boiling, and if he cut, Shmuel did not explain the law, did not enter the dispute—relied on the dispute of R' Elazar and Ravina, as the Rosh wrote: for this in hot, for this in cold; and therefore the Gemara says in cold "they said to him"—R' Elazar, who holds beit hashchitah boiling—if so, cutting boiling requires hag'alah; if so, cutting cold requires rinsing, one degree down; "they said to him"—Ravina, who holds beit hashchitah cold—if so, even cutting boiling requires only rinsing; if so, cutting cold requires no rinsing at all—and think. And if so, the Rosh wrote well: for the one who says cold, rinsing suffices—and no difficulty: cold requires rinsing, meaning for Ravina rinsing is not required. These are the disputants in the Gemara. This is clear and aims at truth's center. Tosafot's intent also revolves around this: they did not seek to infer that for Ravina, after kosher slaughter, he may slaughter again, yet cutting boiling is forbidden—this is not because Ravina truly requires hag'alah even for cutting boiling; rather they inferred on R' Elazar's colleague, who concedes permitted to slaughter if he slaughtered kosher, yet forbidden to cut boiling—as proved from "they said" regarding cold requiring rinsing, we learn boiling requires hag'alah—they inferred well and distinguished "since simanim are busy," etc. Also Tosafot's difficulty in the next statement, inferring cold requires rinsing, therefore boiling requires hag'alah—therefore even if beit hashchitah is boiling, cold suffices by rinsing and sticking is not needed—as Maharshal questioned. And it appears without this we can infer in Tosafot: why seek this—for Rav Elazar's colleague permitted in any case, yet forbidden to cut boiling—what difference? Halachah as he holds is enough Tosafot wrote for Ravina; nevertheless cutting boiling forbidden, etc. Also the next Tosafot difficulty: inferring cold requires rinsing, therefore boiling requires hag'alah—therefore even if beit hashchitah boiling, cold by rinsing, not sticking—as Maharshal questioned. It appears without this we can infer: why seek—for Ravina permitted in any case, yet cutting boiling forbidden—not because Ravina requires hag'alah for cutting boiling; rather inferred on R' Elazar's colleague from "they said" cold requires rinsing, we learn boiling requires hag'alah. They inferred well, distinguished simanim busy, etc. Also Tosafot's next difficulty on inferring cold requires rinsing therefore boiling hag'alah—even if beit hashchitah boiling, cold by rinsing without sticking—as Maharshal questioned. It appears Tosafot did not want in this discourse to descend into commentators' disputes; they took a short way: for R' Elazar's colleague certainly—and so Maharsha explains Tosafot hold beit hashchitah is hot. Therefore truth guides a spacious path for all without doubt: law as I wrote—not like the Shach, that if he cut, in any case peeling is required.
