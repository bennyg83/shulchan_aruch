Until one rinses it when cold. The reason: treifah grease adheres to the knife's sides and is absorbed in beit hashchitah; for although the simanim are busy expelling blood, nevertheless it absorbs grease—so write the poskim; and we divide below siman 70; and specifically here rinsing suffices and not above regarding the knife mentioned—for there the knife is already forbidden and the meat is soft to absorb; but here the knife is hard to absorb except by boiling, and not by heat in beit hashchitah; and here there is only grease on the knife, therefore rinsing suffices—Rashi and poskim. And for the Rashba, who holds the knife absorbs somewhat by heat in beit hashchitah and through the knife's pressure he holds that nevertheless it is not expelled by heat in beit hashchitah and the knife's pressure except in actual boiling, as he wrote in his novellae and in Tur Orach Chaim.
Between each slaughter and slaughter. Because we are concerned lest he find a treifah; and the Bach wrote: specifically for an animal; but for birds, where treifot are not common, the custom is not to wipe; however, because of the prohibition of eiver min hachai one should warn slaughterers to wipe even for birds; and wiping with birds' feathers does not help—rather something hard is needed, such as a piece of wet cloth, or rinsing when cold between each slaughter and slaughter, end of his words; and he brought proof from what the Gemara asks for the one who permits in hot: treifah also absorbs forbidden matter, etc. And one can reject his proof; and so Hagahot Ashrei from Mahariach and Maharshal first chapter of Chullin siman 12: rinsing is not required at all even for cold with a knife with which he slaughtered kosher; and so the Razah and Rashba in his novellae daf 10, 13, and Tur Orach Chaim, and the Mordechai, and the Agudah; and so appears explicitly in Ashrei and R' Yerucham; and so is the Taz's view, as he wrote in s.k. 12; and so Semag end of negative commandment 137, Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 6 from Hilchos Ma'achalot Assurot, and Orach Chaim general rule 58 law 15.
And if he slaughtered with it without rinsing, etc. So too the Tur; and they seized on him—Maharshal there and the Bach—that although l'chatchila rinsing suffices for the knife because it is hard to absorb except by boiling or because it does not expel—nevertheless where he did not rinse, it absorbed in beit hashchitah from grease that is on it visibly and peeling is required, for it is no worse than the knife mentioned—and as appears from Rashi; and so is explained in Sefer HaTerumah siman 70; and so the Rashba; and so Ra'avan siman 211; and so appears explicitly in the Ran in chapter 25: slaughtered treifah with it is like the knife mentioned in this regard.
He rinses beit hashchitah. One should not ask: in any case rinsing is needed before salting because of blood, as below beginning of siman 69; and even for roasting we hold rinsing is required, as the Rav wrote siman 77—the Rashba and Ran in the first chapter of Chullin in the name of Rabeinu Yona already answered: if because of blood, ordinary rubbing suffices until the water runs without appearance of blood; but now because of forbidden grease we require great rubbing with the hands well, in order to remove the knife's fat that adhered by estimation, end of his words (so too there for the one who says in the Gemara regarding the knife mentioned: rinse—but we hold peel). And so all of them (and with this one can answer what the Taz wrote: forbidden to slaughter with it until one rinses it when cold—and we should not say he may slaughter without rinsing and rinse afterward; presumably since his way is rinsing, as below siman 139, it must be—rather certainly here we require rinsing well, for ordinary rinsing does not suffice; and so one can say below siman 64 seif 7, where there too the Rashba and Rav HaMagid wrote that great rubbing with the hands well is required, etc., see there). And the Derishah strained himself here in vain, and these poskim's words escaped him.
And if he is accustomed to slaughter. Rashi explained: such as he slaughtered many animals and found several animals among them treifot on inspection—he should no longer slaughter except stick, etc.; and the Bach wrote: the kosher animals found among them were kashered by the wiping he did in the interim—and see there.
He needs sticking, etc. Meaning: and afterward slaughter l'chatchila; and so Darkei Moshe and Derishah; and even though with the knife mentioned above sticking does not help l'chatchila—this is because it absorbed much through actual boiling, which is not so here; and this distinction appears in Rashi and is mentioned in Tosafot and other poskim.
