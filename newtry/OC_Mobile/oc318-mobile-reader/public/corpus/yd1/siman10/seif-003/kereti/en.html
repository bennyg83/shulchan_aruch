Until one rinses it, etc.—and even with a gentile's knife we hold like Rav who said peel; Rashi answers: there is a difference to absorb in meat, which is soft—forgetting well—unlike expelling from a knife, which is hard, without such power; see Peleti, where I elaborated on these matters at length with several reasons.
Between each slaughter, etc.—lest a treifah occur; and the Bach wrote: this applies to an animal, where treifot are somewhat common; but for birds, treifot are not common and there is no concern; and what the Bach wrote that there is still concern because of eiver min hachai—the acharonim already disagreed; see Peleti, what he wrote reconciling the Gemara's words—see there.
He rinses beit hashchitah—so the Tur and acharonim; all challenged: for it was on it visibly from the knife's grease, absorbed, and requires peeling like a gentile's knife; see Peleti, where I argued for the Tur.
Treifot frequently—and he did not wipe well between slaughter and slaughter (Bach).
