This is fine. And the reason is in the Shach: because treifah grease adheres to the knife's sides and is absorbed in beit hashchitah; for although they are busy expelling blood, nevertheless it absorbs grease; and that rinsing suffices here and not above with a gentile's knife is because there the knife is already forbidden and the meat is soft to absorb; but here the knife is hard to absorb—it absorbs only through actual boiling; and here there is only heat in beit hashchitah, and here only grease on the knife—therefore rinsing suffices. And the Taz wrote another answer: a gentile's knife is different, since he used it frequently and much adhered—therefore it will not be removed by ordinary rinsing. And he wrote further in the Shach in the name of the Bach: because of eiver min hachai one should warn slaughterers to wipe even for birds between slaughter and slaughter (although treifot are not common in them); and wiping with birds' feathers does not help except with something hard such as cloth, or rinsing when cold—see there end of his words, which reconciles the custom of not wiping with birds' feathers. And the Shach disagrees on this and holds rinsing is not required between slaughter and slaughter—see there.
The slaughterhouse. And the Shach and Taz disagree on this and hold peeling is required; and one should not ask on the Mechaber's view that he ruled rinsing suffices—for in any case rinsing is needed before salting because of blood; and one must say: for blood, ordinary rubbing suffices, but now because of forbidden grease, great rubbing with the hands well is required in order to remove the knife's fat that adhered.
Hard (ground). The Shach explained: such as he slaughtered many animals and found several among them treifot on inspection—he should no longer slaughter except stick in hard ground and slaughter afterward l'chatchila—Darkei Moshe; and see Peri Chadash, who reconciles the custom of slaughterers who are lenient without sticking their knives. And the Bach wrote: the kosher animals found among them were kashered by the wiping done in the interim; and what helps here—sticking to slaughter afterward l'chatchila—even though with the knife mentioned above sticking does not help l'chatchila, because it absorbed through actual boiling, which is not so here. And if he transgressed and slaughtered without sticking, the Taz wrote he must peel beit hashchitah; and it appears that with three treifah slaughterings combined he is called accustomed to slaughter treifot with it—for with three times there is chazakah, as he wrote in siman 100 seif 4; but l'chatchila wiping well with hair suffices—except Maharshal wrote that now they do not wipe well as appropriate, only casually, and the teacher must warn them about this.
