With which he slaughtered treifah. Here it is not relevant to say: since the simanim are busy expelling blood they do not absorb, as in seif 2—for here it absorbed grease and the busyness of expelling blood does not nullify it; and that rinsing the knife helps is because beit hashchitah is not so boiling regarding expelling what the knife absorbed; rather the primary prohibition is from forbidden grease adhering on the knife's face, and it is absorbed in the simanim; therefore rinsing what is on the knife's face helps—so is the Rashba's view in Tur Orach Chaim: heat in beit hashchitah helps that the knife absorbs, but regarding expelling the absorption into the meat it does not help; therefore the Rashba called this "somewhat boiling," not fully boiling.
Until one rinses it when cold. Learn for a Kutim knife in seif 1: cold rinsing of the knife does not suffice—for a knife mentioned is different, since he used it frequently and much adhered to it; therefore it will not be removed by ordinary rinsing.
He rinses beit hashchitah. The Beit Yosef wrote on this: it is simple that just as rinsing helps for the knife itself, so too rinsing beit hashchitah suffices, end of his words; and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote on the Tur's words here: I wonder where he got this; and it appears by reasoning that peeling is required; and so in Sefer HaTerumah; and all the more—for even a knife mentioned that is well wiped, only absorbed prohibition in it, we say it absorbed through shechitah; all the more grease that adhered and is not wiped requires peeling, end of his words; and his reasoning appears good: it is simple that rinsing does not help for what was absorbed, only for what is on its face; and since with the knife mentioned beit hashchitah needs peeling, apparently heat in beit hashchitah causes the knife to absorb—so too it absorbs in it; and what does rinsing the meat help, since there is visible prohibition on the knife from treifah grease—otherwise the knife would not need rinsing; rather certainly it absorbed and re-absorbs afterward through heat in beit hashchitah; and so explicitly in Tur Orach Chaim, who wrote: and since so, even though we hold regarding a treifah knife like the one who says cold, we hold like Rav with a knife mentioned—that one peels, because prohibition adhered visibly on the knife's face and was absorbed in the meat with heat in beit hashchitah; and the same for a knife with which he slaughtered treifah: if he slaughtered before rinsing, he peels like Rav, etc. And it appears to resolve the Tur's view: since there is already dispute regarding a Kutim knife whether the meat needs peeling or rinsing—and although one should rule stringently there to peel—nevertheless here the prohibition is lighter than a knife mentioned; one may rely on the view that rinsing suffices even for a knife mentioned; and nevertheless for practical law one should rule stringently that here too peeling is required, as Rabbenu Yerucham wrote and as Orach Chaim concludes; and there is great wonder on the Beit Yosef, who wrote it is simple like the Tur's view, when there is a great and mighty dispute against him.
Accustomed to slaughter treifot with it, etc. For this is like a knife mentioned; if so, if he transgressed and slaughtered without sticking, it is obvious that here he must peel beit hashchitah—even according to the Tur I mentioned before; and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote: I cannot understand his view—who is accustomed to slaughter treifot with a knife? How would he know beforehand it would become treifah? And it is not found that a person designates a knife for treifah slaughter; rather it speaks that he slaughtered many animals and found several among them treifah on inspection—he should no longer slaughter with it except stick ten times in hard ground. And even if you say the Tur did not intend this, nevertheless one should instruct thus; and so one should do each time at harvest season from house to house, end of his words. And it appears to my humble view: certainly l'chatchila one need not be concerned when slaughtering many animals that many will be treifot one after another without interruption of kosher slaughter in the interim—and whenever there is kosher slaughter in the interim, it is not called "accustomed" to prohibition, and it is not like a knife mentioned; rather if it is clear he slaughtered animals one after another and they became treifah from what he slaughtered afterward without sticking in hard ground, he must peel that animal's beit hashchitah. And it appears that with three treifah slaughterings combined he is called accustomed to slaughter treifot with it—for with three times there is a chazakah, as I brought below siman 100 seif 4 regarding three worms; but l'chatchila wiping well with hair as written here suffices—except that Maharshal wrote that now they do not wipe well as appropriate, only casually, and the teacher must warn them about this.
