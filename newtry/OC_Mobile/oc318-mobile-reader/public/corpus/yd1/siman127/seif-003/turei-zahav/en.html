This refers to a case where the meat was already cut into small pieces — but if it is visibly apparent that it was de-veined, he is certainly trusted, for we can see that it is de-veined and there is no concern that one who is not an expert performed the de-veining, since all who engage in de-veining are experts — as Rema wrote in siman 65; and even though he wrote it is good to be stringent, that is only ab initio. Nor is there concern that he did not complete the de-veining, since that concern in siman 65 applies specifically when the de-veiner went away and may have intended to return and complete the work — but here there is no such indicator of doubt. In any case there is also a remedy: he can give it or sell it to the one who testified about it, since one is trusted about what is one's own — as stated nearby — and afterward the original owner can take it back; and we do not thereafter raise any concern. All this I wrote according to Rema's annotation about uncleared meat — but in truth, this case does not appear in Mordechai chapter HaIsha Rabbah or in Asheri chapter HaNizkain; only in Ran chapter HaIsha Rabbah does it appear. And I am astonished at this holy mouth who said so — for specifically with tevel and similar produce, where the entire batch was certainly forbidden and there was no permitted avenue at all before the tithes were separated, we say that since the prohibition became established with certainty, one witness is not trusted to remove it from its presumed prohibited status. But meat that is not de-veined was never presumed forbidden as such — it is only forbidden because of the veins or forbidden fat within it — and this certainly resembles what is stated afterward: if there were two pieces, one permitted and one forbidden, one witness is trusted to say this is the permitted one and this is the forbidden one — and this is straightforward: even if the permitted and forbidden were joined together, the same law would apply — which is exactly the law of un-de-veined meat, forbidden because one may encounter the forbidden element within it; and what is permitted after de-veining was also permitted before de-veining, just unclarified — and "the prohibition was established with certainty in this piece" does not apply here. Further proof from the words of Rosh in chapter Gid HaNashe, who wrote: "but an entire thigh is presumed permitted except for the gid," etc. — end of quote. And so too it is further clear from Ran in chapter HaNizkain: if there were kosher and non-kosher meat at the butcher, and one witness said "this piece is kosher," he is trusted — even though a prohibition was established at the butcher shop — since it was not established in this specific piece, he is trusted — end of quote. So too here, the meat was not presumed forbidden in itself — only because of the admixture of prohibition attached to it — and for this it is certainly straightforward that one witness is trusted to say this is the permitted portion and there is no prohibition in it. And this is evidenced from Tosafot chapter 3 of Kiddushin (65b) s.v. "they became impure," who wrote: one witness is trusted — that is, to permit — such as "this meat is de-veined," or "this piece is pure fat," etc. — and this must apply even when it is not in the witness's power to rectify it, as in the case of fat — and therefore one must say that these are categorized as situations where there was no presumption either for permit or prohibition from the outset — therefore one is trusted in all such cases to permit. And this is the actual daily practice — people believe anyone who says the meat is de-veined and eat on his testimony, even when the one who holds the meat cannot de-vein it himself; and according to this annotation of Rema, we should have forbidden such testimony since it is not in his power. Rather, certainly as I explained. So-and-so the scholar approved it for me — In Darkei Moshe it is written: a case came before us — Reuven prepared butter for Shimon, and Shimon sold much butter as kosher, saying Reuven had prepared it and given it all to him as kosher; and Reuven said he did not give it all to him as kosher — only some — and about the rest explicitly told him it was forbidden; and Shimon contradicts him. We were lenient since butter from gentiles has many who permit it and some places are accustomed to leniency. But for other prohibitions it seems forbidden — for it resembles "so-and-so the scholar approved it for me" in seif 1 — where if he said "I did not approve it" it is forbidden; and one cannot distinguish this by saying specifically when the prohibition was established with certainty — for if so he should have listed 15 laws instead of 14; one should not make distinctions here — end of Darkei Moshe. And in Derisha he copied Darkei Moshe's language, which lists 14 laws from Mordechai and Asheri, and cited this case as if from Mordechai and Asheri — but in truth it is Darkei Moshe's own words. And in my view one should not be lenient — for we said in seif 1 that once a ruling was in his possession he cannot thereafter contradict it if he said otherwise the first time — and this is the very first time; all the more so here where Shimon obviously lied, placing the approval on Reuven while Reuven contradicts him; and as the Rosh wrote in chapter HaNizkain, here there is no room to say the Torah believed one witness as two, since he himself admits that so-and-so prepared it and that one contradicts him — if so, there is nothing here but a mixture of non-Jewish butter in Jewish butter; and even though in siman 115 seif 1 Rema wrote that a mixture of butter is permitted, that is only with a majority of permitted butter as we proved there; and here majority of permitted is not clearly established — for if so, even if Shimon agreed with Reuven it should have been permitted here; and in any case it seems Shimon acted wrongly in mixing forbidden into permitted while lying — therefore one should be strict and forbid all his butter, for this is worse than nullifying a prohibition ab initio mentioned in siman 99 seif 5. So it seems to me. Afterward the responsa of Rema were published, and in siman 66 he wrote about butter and noted this difficulty from the Rosh in chapter HaNizkain, and resolved it by saying the case there is different — the mound had a presumed prohibition of tevel, whereas when the prohibition was not established, one is trusted. This is very perplexing — since there is no trustworthiness here for Shimon since Reuven contradicts him — what help is it that the prohibition was not established here? Ultimately Shimon's trustworthiness disappears and it becomes as butter of one who cannot be trusted. Furthermore, the Rosh cited other laws there and distinguished between where the prohibition was established or not — but here said nothing; and furthermore Rema cited proof from chapter HaCholetz — "I converted privately," etc. — "according to your own words you are a gentile and you are not trusted to disqualify your children" — implying even though the children have no presumed kashrus except through their father, since by his own words he is a gentile he is not trusted to disqualify his children — so too with this butter, since by his own words he is not a custodian of this butter, he is not trusted since the owners contradict him — end of quote. And I am astonished at this from a holy mouth — for Rashi explicitly writes in chapter HaCholetz (Yevamot 46a): "and a gentile has no testimony — since your children are presumed kasher, you are not trusted to disqualify them" — implying that even without the father they had a presumption of kashrus. Therefore it seems here with butter, it is as if found in the hands of one who cannot be trusted. My brother the Gaon, Maharr Yitzchak HaLevi of blessed memory, responded regarding butter sent via a gentile without a seal — that it is permitted after the fact, and we do not suspect he exchanged it for his own.
