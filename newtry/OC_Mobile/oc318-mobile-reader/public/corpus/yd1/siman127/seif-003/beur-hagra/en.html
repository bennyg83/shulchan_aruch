(Likkut) And a woman is believed regarding a matter, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud in the first chapter of Pesachim, on the teaching that "everyone is believed," etc., states: "Women are not included here," because they are lax regarding even minor searching. Terumas HaDeshen explains that "women are not included here" means that ordinarily they are believed even in a Torah-level matter, but the Gemara answers that because they are lax, in such a case they are not believed on a Torah level and are believed only for a rabbinic matter. See, however, the Rosh there, who explains "women are not included here" to mean that in this case they are not believed even for a rabbinic matter. End.

(Likkut) A minor, etc.; and if he testifies, etc. Tosafos in Pesachim cited above writes that the reason he was believed there is because it was within his power to correct the matter. Therefore, at the end of chapter 2 of Kesubos concerning techumin, which are rabbinic, they believed a person only when he testified as an adult about what he had seen as a minor, but not while he was still a minor, because it was not within his power. Tosafos in Eruvin 59a, s.v. "and techumin," writes likewise. This proves that a woman is believed regarding a rabbinic matter even when it is not within her power. The general rule is that where it is within their power to correct the matter, even a woman or minor is believed even regarding a Torah-level prohibition, as with slaughter by a woman and a minor who knows how to guard himself, etc., provided the matter does not involve substantial effort, as Tosafos writes there, or uncertainty with a basis for leniency, as shown by Pesachim, where the Gemara says specifically that the case is rabbinic. For a rabbinic matter, a woman is believed even where it is not within her power, as with techumin, and the same applies to a slave, but not to a minor, as above. Where it is within their power and the matter is rabbinic, even where there is effort or uncertainty, both a woman and a minor are believed, as in the case of chametz above. In short: where the matter is within their power and there is no uncertainty, both a woman and a minor are believed even on a Torah level. See above, siman 120, seif katan 36. If either of these two conditions is lacking, neither is believed on a Torah level. Regarding a rabbinic matter involving uncertainty, both are believed; where it is not within their power, a woman is believed but a minor is not. A slave has the same status as a woman.
