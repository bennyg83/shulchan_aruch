Avodat HaTaharah of the Rav Mahariyat, and what he wrote in the name of Beit Yaakov siman 150 — regarding one who said to two "the wine of one of you was libated and I do not know which" and they are silent — there is room to be lenient, etc.; see in Beit Yaakov there, that initially he wanted to say so from reasoning that their silence is not like admission, and afterward he brought proof from Tosafot chapter Two Nazirim that even in such a case their silence is like admission (except there he challenged Tosafot — requires investigation whence this, perhaps the mishnah deals when they believe his words — see there; nevertheless he does not disagree because of this with Tosafot; and see in responsum Shevut Yaakov what he challenged on this difficulty of his; and see in Sefer Matzmiach Yeshuah on Mordechai part 2 called Yeshuot Yaakov letter 7 what was forced to settle his grandfather Rav Beit Yaakov's view on this); and likewise there in the simanim. And he further wrote that if he said to one "as I thought I saw your wine libated and I forgot" and he is silent — his silence is also like admission, even though the implication of the poskim is not so; nevertheless it is proven in the Gemara chapter Two Nazirim; and it is also proven that even if he says he was not at the libation but stood from afar, the law is so — see there:
