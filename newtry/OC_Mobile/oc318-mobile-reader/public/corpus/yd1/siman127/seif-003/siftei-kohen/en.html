One witness is believed in prohibitions, etc. Meaning: you will find that one witness is believed in prohibitions to permit, even without the silence of the owner of the wine that would constitute admission; but to be stringent you will not find that the witness is believed without the silence of the owner of the wine — for whichever way one looks at it: where neither issur nor heter was established, it is forbidden from doubt without the witness; and where heter was established, the witness is not believed to forbid — as above seif 1 (and see there s.k. 15).
However, some say, etc. The Rama should not have written this as merely 'some say,' for it is an explicit Gemara in Kiddushin 66b concerning a blemished animal and a mikveh. Tosafos there learned from that sugya the law regarding yayin nesech and similar cases. The Gemara also proves that in a matter capable of verification the witness is believed even if the owner contradicts him, and Tosafos add that this applies even if another witness contradicts him. See there.
One witness is believed regarding it, etc. Meaning: even without the silence of the owner of the wine, such as when he is not before us or when he says he does not know.
Even to prohibit it. In the Rosh and Mordechai the text reads, 'even to permit it.' There is certainly greater novelty in permitting, because without the witness's testimony the matter would remain prohibited because of doubt. The Rama's wording can nevertheless be explained as follows: although ordinarily one does not find a case where a single witness is believed to prohibit against an established status, here he is believed even to prohibit. Although without his testimony there would already be a doubt, his testimony makes it a definite prohibition, which has practical consequences for a double doubt and similar laws.
This is permit and this is prohibition. For even though forbidden status was established in this place, nevertheless regarding this particular piece, forbidden status was not established there.
Even where forbidden status was established. Since it is in his hand to rectify it — the Rosh in the chapter Nezikin, distinction 4; and so it is proven in the Gemara at the beginning of the chapter HaIsha Rabba.
And a woman, etc., in a matter of prohibition. Even a Torah-level prohibition, to say "I fixed it"; and that is where it is in her hand to fix it, or it is hers — for otherwise she is not superior to one witness, who is not believed since forbidden status was established, as has been explained; and so it is in the Ran there, and in Tosafot in the first chapter of Pesachim (daf 4 side 2); and see in the responsum of Mahariyv at the end of siman 25.
Or prohibition. And likewise a matter that involves effort — women are not believed, for women are lazy, as Tosafot wrote there; and I have received that women are not believed regarding the removal of forbidden sinews from the hindquarters, for there is no matter more tedious than it, and due to their laziness they will not examine so thoroughly; and what the authorities wrote, that women are believed regarding removal of forbidden fat — they speak of standard removal, namely the front parts; so wrote Maharshal in the first chapter of Chullin siman 2; and see above siman 84 s.k. 34.
A minor does not have the status of a witness, etc. Rivash, responsum 245, implies that this concerns a case where the owner is not silently admitting but says that he does not know. If the owner remains silent in a manner constituting admission, the minor appears to be believed. Rivash follows the approach of Rashba and his school that an adult witness is believed even when the owner says, 'I do not know.' According to the Rama, who holds that an adult witness is not believed when the owner says that he does not know, the novelty is that a minor does not have the legal status of one witness at all. This matters where the owner admits that there are grounds for the matter, where another witness contradicts him and the owner is absent, where the matter can be verified, where no prior permissive or prohibitive status has been established, or where there are two pieces, one prohibited and one permitted. In all such cases an adult witness may be believed independently of the owner's silence, but a minor is not. Nevertheless, where the matter is in the minor's own control, the minor is believed even to remove something from its prior status, whether to permit or prohibit. Rivash writes that the rule in Sukkah that a minor who knows how to guard his hands may have pure foods eaten on their basis refers to pure foods actually in his possession; regarding something not in his possession, a minor is not believed to remove it from an established status, whether toward leniency or stringency. See Orach Chayyim, end of siman 457.
Nevertheless, regarding a sharp-minded minor, etc. See above at the end of siman 48.
