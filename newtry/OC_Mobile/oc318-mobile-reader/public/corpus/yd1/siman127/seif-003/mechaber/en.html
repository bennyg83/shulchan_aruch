One witness is trusted regarding issurim to permit, but not to be stringent. {Rama: However, there are those who say that regarding a matter that can be clarified — such as if one says to him, “Come and I will show you a non-Jew pouring a libation to your wine” — one must be concerned for his words (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot). And any matter that is not established as heter and not established as issur, one witness is trusted regarding it, even to forbid it (Ashiri, perek HaNizakin, and Mordechai, perek HaIshah Rabbah). But any place that a matter is established as issur, such as tevel or a piece of meat that is not menukkar, the witness is not trusted to permit it unless it is in his power to fix it. And if there are here 2 pieces, one of issur and one of heter, the witness is trusted to say “this is heter and this is issur” (Ran). And a person is trusted regarding what is his, even where issur is established. And see above siman 119, the law of one who is suspected regarding a matter, if he testifies about it. And see below siman 185, one who says, “So-and-so, the chacham, permitted this for me,” and the chacham denies it. And see in Even HaEzer siman 152 whether we say regarding testimony “the mouth that forbade is the mouth that permitted.” And a woman is trusted regarding a matter of issur to say, “I fixed it,” and this is specifically regarding definite issur, such as nikur of meat and the like; but regarding a doubt, perhaps there is no issur here — such as when she must clarify tamei fish from tahor — a woman is not trusted; and likewise regarding an issur that has sides to be lenient, a woman is not trusted, for a woman’s mind is light to be lenient. A minor does not have the law of a witness to be trusted regarding issurim; nevertheless, regarding a sharp and knowledgeable minor, where there are indications to his words, one should be stringent if he testifies about an issur. And if he testifies about a d’rabbanan issur to be lenient, and issur was not established — such as bedikat chametz — he is trusted, for the Sages trusted him for d’rabbanan; but if issur was established, he is not trusted at all.}
