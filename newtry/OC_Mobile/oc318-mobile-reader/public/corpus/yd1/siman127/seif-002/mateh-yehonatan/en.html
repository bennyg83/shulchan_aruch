(There in the gloss) And if the owners do not contradict him, even though another contradicts him, it does not help, etc. See Shach s.k. 13, what he challenged from the mishnah in Keritot: one witness says "he ate this forbidden fat" and one witness says "he did not eat" — he brings a provisional guilt-offering; evidently the one witness helps to exempt him from a definite offering; certainly see there; and one may say according to what Shulchan Aruch ruled above in s.s. 1 like Mahariyk, that wherever the Torah believed one witness, it is even when it is not a matter of sexual impropriety — it is also like two; and if so, Rama deals when they came one after another — meaning one witness came and said "your wine was libated" and he was silent; if so this is like two, and afterward one cannot contradict him, because the words of one are not in place of two; unlike if they came at once — even if he was silent, one may say he is not forbidden, for what forbids through his silence is not because his silence made it a piece of prohibition, but we say since he was silent he accepted his statement like two; and all this is when he was not contradicted — unlike when he was contradicted, his silence is nothing since his statement is nothing and there is no testimony at all. And accordingly what Rama wrote "and some say that one witness in contradiction," etc. — meaning even when they came one after another; and this is the view that the Torah did not believe him like two but like one; and since he was silent and accepted his testimony upon himself, he is believed; however specifically where there is no other contradicting him; but if there is here one witness who contradicts him, there is no testimony at all and it is permitted:
