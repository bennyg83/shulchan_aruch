The first time. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 71, concerning a worker hired to make wine and ensure its kashrut. He received his wages, and the wine's owner sold it as kosher and earned a large profit. The worker now says that through his negligence the wine was used for an idolatrous libation. He confesses his sin and wishes to return his wages, which amount to the considerable sum of twenty Reichsthalers. It appears that although he would have been believed if the wine were still in the first owner's possession, as explained in siman 127, here it has already been sold and the owner earned a large profit. If you believe the worker, you would extract that money from the owner based on the testimony of one witness. Proof comes from Gittin, where the Gemara asks, “In whose possession was the Sefer Torah?” etc. Since idolatrous wine nowadays is prohibited only rabbinically, one should not be so stringent.
