Rabbeinu Yerucham concluded at the end of netiv 15: and even if they contradict him; and he brings it Beit Yosef; and this is not necessary, for in the Gemara regarding a Torah scroll we say: since he is believed to cause him to lose his wages, he is believed to say he did not process the hides for their sake — that is because the other does not contradict him; but when they contradict him, one may say the owners are believed regarding their own (and even though in the first chapter of HaIsha Rabba it appears we do not say the owners are believed regarding their own except where he can fix it — meaning where forbidden matter was established, such as tevel and similarly; not so here — you will know, for behold regarding forbidden fat and "your ox gored," and regarding kiddushin it is in the chapter HaOmer that if the owners contradict him they are believed; and likewise regarding purity; and stam yeinam itself, even though it is not in his hand — if so one must say that that passage in HaIsha Rabba is because forbidden matter was established; and examine); and one witness contradicting the owner of the wine is nothing; and such appears somewhat in the hasagah of the Raavad at the end of Hilchot Metamei Mishkav uMoshav — see there, and examine; and perhaps for this reason the Mechaber omitted this novelty as well; however Raavan in siman 92 wrote like the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham; I later found in Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 2, aleph bet, regarding a Torah scroll, that he wrote: and there is no difference whether the owner of the scroll is silent or contradicts him, for he cannot know the truth of the matter — end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it below siman 481; behold that if they contradict him clearly he is not believed; therefore it appears to me that what is written at the end of netiv 15 regarding stam yeinam deals also to exclude silence specifically; and "and contradicts him" that he mentioned means that he told him "I do not believe you," and similarly.
