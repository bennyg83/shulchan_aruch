If it is in his hand, he is believed. Avodat HaTaharah and responsum part 2 siman 79 — to settle Shach's words in siman 127, who wrote that Beit Yosef holds that a worker's credibility is because of migo; and there is a practical difference regarding one who deposits cooked wine with his fellow and says it was libated before it came into his hand — for if the reason is migo, he is not believed, for with cooked wine he never had migo — see there; and see in Beit Efrayim on treifot in Kuntras HaRe'ayot, where I elaborated on the words of Peri Megadim and part 2 on this:
The first time. See Baer Heitev and Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, responsum 71. A worker was hired to produce kosher wine and received his wages. The owner sold the wine as kosher and earned a considerable profit. The worker later declared that through his negligence the wine had become yayin nesech. He confessed his sin and wished to return his wages, amounting to twenty Reichsthaler. Although he would have been believed had the wine remained in the original owner’s possession, as explained in siman 127, here it had already been sold and the owner had earned substantial profit. Believing the worker would therefore extract money from the owner on the testimony of a single witness. Proof is brought from Gittin concerning the question in whose possession a Torah scroll rested. Since the prohibition of ordinary non-Jewish wine nowadays is rabbinic, one should not be excessively stringent.
