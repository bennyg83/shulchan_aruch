If it is in his possession, he is believed. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 79, which reconciles Shach's words in siman 127. Shach writes that Beit Yosef holds that a worker's credibility is based on a migo. A practical difference arises when someone deposits cooked wine with another person and that person says it had been used for an idolatrous libation before it came into his possession. If the reason is migo, he is not believed, because with cooked wine he never had a migo. See there. See also Beit Efrayim on tereifot, Kuntres HaRayot, where I discuss at length the words of Panim Meirot and Chacham Tzvi concerning this.
