(Siman 127 seif 1) And he must tell him "your wine was libated in your presence," or that he told him "you knew," etc. And see in Shach who explained that otherwise it is not silence like admission, for he is silent because he does not know the matter clearly. And likewise he explains the words of Rambam in chapter 9 of Laws of Nazirites, who ruled: if he was silent or was in doubt, he brings an offering even on the testimony of one witness — meaning he was in doubt and is silent on that account; and two types of silence are taught: either due to admission or due to doubt that the matter is not clear, and nevertheless the witness is believed. And likewise Lechem Mishneh in chapter 8 of Shegagot explained Rambam's view thus: regarding "he does not know," he brings an offering — meaning he is silent because it is not clear to him and he did not say explicitly "I do not know"; but in responsum Shav Yitzchak siman 43 he explained Rambam's intent that it deals when the witness told him "you knew that a doubt was born to you" and he is silent, and therefore it is like admission; but otherwise it is not like admission; and apparently Rambam's intent is not like Shach and Lechem Mishneh, who ruled in chapter 2 of Laws of Maaser that one who sells produce to his fellow and after it left from tithe status told the buyer "they are untithed" — the law is he is not believed; and it implies he is not obligated to believe him when he does not know; and it must be that we teach him to say explicitly that he does not believe him, as written in the gloss there — for otherwise if he is merely silent because he does not know, the witness is believed to forbid; and likewise must be Rambam's intent in chapter 4 of Laws of Terumot:
