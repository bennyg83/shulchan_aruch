Rather every person is always believed even if it was never in his hand, when silent ; even if he had no way to know, or says "I do not know"; and in such a case that in Kiddushin and that in Gittin deals with a worker upon whom it is incumbent to inform, and therefore he is not believed except specifically when in his hand or the first time; but when they contradict him, he is never believed according to all; and likewise Ran in Gittin there briefly; and Rashba wrote: and you may ask whence for Abaye who says and teaches, etc. ; perhaps because silence is like admission as written in Yevamot there; one may say this is what he means: the reason, etc., specifically not "I do not know" (believed), for otherwise it should teach "I do not know"; and what is written in Yevamot there "and whence that he is believed," etc., refers to what is written above "whence for us from Scripture," etc., and still "whence for us from Scripture"; and what is written "know that it teaches," etc. ; the discussion there follows that reasoning they said in Keritot 12a: the reason of the rabbis is because a person is believed, etc.; but Abaye holds the reason is because of migo ; we resolve his words; and for that language at the beginning, because he is believed ; when the rabbis reject because they are not believed when contradicted, it is because of migo.
