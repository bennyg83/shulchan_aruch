If it is in his hand. The language of the Tur: such as when it was given into his hand to guard it; and he means that “in his hand” does not mean in his hand to libate it, but rather that it was given into his hand to guard it, and it is as if he were its owner over it; and not because of a migu; and so ruled the Rosh in the chapter Nezikin in distinction 11, and Mordechai at the beginning of the chapter HaIsha Rabba; but Beit Yosef did not bring the words of the Rosh and Mordechai, and on the contrary brought simply the words of Rashi, Rabbenu Tam, and the Rashba, who explained: anyone within whose hand it is to defile it; and perhaps for this reason he omitted the language of the Tur here; and likewise in Pri Chadash he explained the reason because it is within his power to diminish it; and the Rashba proved it in his chiddushim in the chapter Nezikin at the beginning of daf 79 with proofs; and it is implied in Tosafot in Kiddushin in the chapter HaOmer (daf 65 side 2) that he is believed because of a migu; and according to this one may consider: one who says to his fellow regarding his boiled wine that it was libated before the boiling is not believed) even if it is in his hand, since he has no migu.
Believed. Even if he did not tell him the first time he found it.
But if it is not in his hand. Meaning: even if it was in his hand initially, nevertheless since it is not now in his hand and he did not tell him the first time, he is not believed; but when it was never in his hand at all, even if he told him the first time, he is not believed when the owner of the wine contradicts him — except when he is silent; so it is explained in Tosafot, the Rosh, Mordechai, and their supporters; and the Mechaber abbreviated the matter.
If the owner contradicts him. And then it is permitted for everyone; and so too the rabbi wrote at the end of the seif; and even if he said “I do not know,” it is permitted for everyone — as written in Or Zarua general law 13 din 13 in the name of R. Chaim; and so too ruled Darkei Moshe.
Or he says, etc. It is written in Mordechai in the chapter HaOmer that if he says to him "perhaps you were not careful," it is considered as if he contradicts him; and Darkei Moshe brings it.
Does not know. See above s.k. 15.
And they teach him, etc. Meaning: they teach him before the witness tells him, that when the witness comes and says to him, he should say to him immediately within the time of speech "I do not believe you"; and they do not teach him to contradict him, for how would they teach him to lie.
And afterward he contradicted him. It appears to me specifically if he contradicts him afterward; but if he told him afterward "I do not know, and that I was silent at first was not because I admitted," etc. — the witness is believed according to all; and this is proven explicitly from the Gemara, from the incident of King Yannai in Kiddushin (daf 66 side 1) — see there and examine.
Some say, etc., he is believed. And above at the end of siman 1, seif 13, the Mechaber wrote simply that he is believed, and the rabbi ruled likewise; and here the rabbi wrote it in the language of “some say” because he wrote concerning it in Darkei Moshe: one must examine in siman 185 regarding a niddah who wears niddah garments, that an excuse does not help — and perhaps one may distinguish; and requires investigation — end of his words; and see above siman 185 s.k. 5, that according to Bach and the rabbi in responsum, this is resolved nicely — see there.
