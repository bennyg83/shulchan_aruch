And all those invalid for testimony, etc. This law is written in Darkei Moshe in the name of the Ran, and likewise it was written in Shulchan Aruch; and requires investigation, for the Ran in Kiddushin in the chapter HaOmer wrote explicitly that those invalid for testimony are not believed in prohibitions; and in responsum siman 25 he ruled that even one who is suspect regarding that matter is fit to testify regarding others — such as to say this meat was slaughtered properly, and similarly; and it appears one should distinguish: to permit, certainly he is believed to testify, for we hold one who is suspect regarding a matter testifies for him, as above siman 119 s.k. 18; but in the chapter HaOmer he deals explicitly with coming to forbid another's — such as to say "your wine was libated" — then he is not believed except through the silence of the owner of the wine, who relies on the words of the fit witness, as the Ran wrote there; and therefore, whoever is invalid does not rely on him, and it is not enough in his regard to forbid another's; nevertheless for the rabbi it is difficult, for behold he speaks now regarding "your wine was libated," and according to the Ran those invalid for testimony are not believed; and even if you say he deals with testifying to permit — it is difficult, for in such a case the Ran holds that even one who is suspect is believed, and such we hold above siman 119 seif 7 and siman 124 seif 9; and requires investigation; I returned and examined Darkei Moshe, and such is its language: Rashba wrote in responsum siman 264 that two brothers are only like one witness for prohibitions, whether to uphold a matter or regarding contradiction; further the Ran wrote siman 350 that even one unfit for monetary testimony is believed to testify in prohibitions if he is not suspect regarding that matter — end of his words; and it appears this "further the Ran wrote" is a textual error, for if so it is not relevant to say siman 350; and further, if so it should have said "the Ran wrote," what is "further wrote"? Rather one must say: further Rashba wrote, for Rashba wrote so in responsum siman 350: all those invalid for testimony are fit in prohibitions, and even an apostate for one matter is fit for another matter that is not that matter — until here is his language — see there; and not as written in Shulchan Aruch "Ran" (and in Rashba too it appears there he deals with testifying to permit; but when testifying to forbid it appears he agrees with the Ran that those invalid for testimony are not believed, as appears in the Gemara in the chapter HaOmer (daf 66 side 1), and as written in s.k. 9 in the name of Tosafot and Mordechai, and likewise in the responsum of Rashba at the end of siman 376); and nevertheless requires investigation, for it appears Rashba in responsum siman 350 deals specifically with an apostate — meaning one who is a habitual offender; but regarding one who is suspect, behold he wrote in responsum siman 64 that even one suspect regarding that matter is believed to testify regarding others; and likewise above siman 2 s.k. 20 in the name of Turat Chaim and Kessef Mishneh, to distinguish between apostate and suspect — see there; and one may resolve that the rabbi too, when he says "unless he is suspect regarding that matter," speaks of an apostate regarding that matter; and in siman 119 seif 7 he deals with suspect and not apostate; and even though there he says "one who is well known for a transgression" — meaning he is well known to have done the transgression once, which makes him suspect and not a habitual offender to be an apostate; however in siman 124 seif 9 regarding coerced persons, they are certainly habitual offenders; and it is possible that since they do so out of coercion and pressure, we judge them as suspect; and examine. And regarding the testimony of gentiles — poskim wrote in several places that gentiles are not believed, neither for issur nor for heter; and see above siman 118 s.k. 37, and siman 122 s.k. 3, and at the end of siman 129, and siman 130 at the end of seif 8 in the hagahah; and it appears that even if he has no benefit in his testimony, he is not believed to testify regarding others; and even though Semag negative commandment 137 on daf 41 at the end of side 2 wrote in the name of Rav Hai Gaon that we do not rely on the words of a gentile, neither for issur nor for heter — for issur, from what we say in the last chapter of Yevamot: a gentile who was selling produce in the market and said "these fruits are orlah," "these are tithed" — he said nothing; he did not intend this except to improve his sale, etc. — end of his words; it is not to say that specifically where he has benefit in his testimony, such as there where he intends to improve his sale, but generally he is believed to forbid; for the principle is always applied that we never rely on gentiles; rather the reason that he did not intend except to improve his sale is not precise, but the same applies everywhere one can find some excuse; and such appears in the responsum of Maharil siman 38; and likewise it appears in the Gemara in the last chapter of Yevamot; or also specifically there this reason is needed, since the produce is his; and likewise it appears in Turat Chaim general law 32 din 9; I later found likewise in the responsum of Rashba siman 118 explicitly — see there; and now one may ask on the proof from that passage in Yevamot, for they are the words of Rashbag and Rabbi — behold the Tosafeta disagrees, that a gentile is believed to be stringent and not to be lenient, and we hold the halacha is like Rabbi over his colleague (however in Mordechai in the chapter Elu Megalchin, and in Hagahat Maimoni chapter 6 of Hilchot Avodah Zarah, they brought the Tosafeta — the words of Rabbi: Rashbag says a gentile is believed to be stringent, etc.; however it is explained there in their words that we hold like Rashbag, that a gentile is believed, etc. — see there; and see above siman 16 s.k. 23); rather certainly, as the Rosh wrote in the chapter Elu Megalchin in the name of Maharam: Rabbi only disagrees because the produce is his; but generally he agrees with Rashbag; and such is proven there in the Rosh, that for Rashbag he holds that generally he is not believed, even without the reason that he did not intend except to improve his sale — not like the view of Maharshal that I brought above siman 122 in s.k. 4; and take this principle in your hand, that we always dismiss the testimony of a gentile as though he does not exist, and his words do not raise or lower regarding others; and such we say below siman 139 s.k. 63; however where he speaks innocently, his law was already explained in siman 98 s.k. 2 and siman 313 s.k. 4.
