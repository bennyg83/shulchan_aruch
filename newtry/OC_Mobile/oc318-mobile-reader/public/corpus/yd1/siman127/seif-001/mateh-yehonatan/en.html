(Siman 127 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) If it is in his hand. See Shach, who brought the dispute of Rosh and Rashi in explaining "if it is in his hand" — Rashi explained because of migo, and explained "in his hand" as in his hand to libate it; and Rosh explained that he gave it into his hand to guard it, and because it became in the owner's possession even if it is not in his hand to libate it; and it appears to me there is no dispute here, for Rashba in his novellae challenges the view of those who hold Abaye holds he is believed without migo only if it is in his hand — how does Rav Assi bring proof from the Yom Kippur service, and what does the Gemara mean: for if not that he is believed even though we heard him, perhaps he says afterward? For according to all, in such a case he is believed, for whoever is under his hand — even though he finished the service — is believed — end of Rashba's words there; and therefore one may say that certainly in explaining Abaye's words the Rosh explains like Rashi, that he means it is in his hand to libate it, and not like the Raavad, because of Rashba's challenge; and for Rashi the Gemara properly proves from such a case on Yom Kippur; however for us, who establish like Rava, it may well be said: if it is in his hand, even the second or third time he is believed, for the entire reason he is not believed the second time is because if it were true as he said, he should have said so immediately the first time; and all this applies only if they already returned it to him — if so, at the time they returned it, the wine mentioned was libated; but when it is still in his hand, he can say "I will inform him when I return it"; therefore there is no dispute at all — Rashi, who explains the reason because of migo, explains thus according to Abaye; and Rosh wrote this for us, who establish like Rava; and likewise one may say in the Mechaber's words — investigate. (There in the gloss) And if the owners do not contradict him, even though another contradicts him, it does not help, etc. See Shach s.k. 13, what he challenged from the mishnah in Keritot: one witness says "he ate this forbidden fat" and one witness says "he did not eat" — he brings a provisional guilt-offering; evidently the one witness helps to exempt him from a definite offering; certainly see there; and one may say according to what Shulchan Aruch ruled above in s.s. 1 like Mahariyk, that wherever the Torah believed one witness, it is even when it is not a matter of sexual impropriety — it is also like two; and if so, Rama deals when they came one after another — meaning one witness came and said "your wine was libated" and he was silent; if so this is like two, and afterward one cannot contradict him, because the words of one are not in place of two; unlike if they came at once — even if he was silent, one may say he is not forbidden, for what forbids through his silence is not because his silence made it a piece of prohibition, but we say since he was silent he accepted his statement like two; and all this is when he was not contradicted — unlike when he was contradicted, his silence is nothing since his statement is nothing and there is no testimony at all. And accordingly what Rama wrote "and some say that one witness in contradiction," etc. — meaning even when they came one after another; and this is the view that the Torah did not believe him like two but like one; and since he was silent and accepted his testimony upon himself, he is believed; however specifically where there is no other contradicting him; but if there is here one witness who contradicts him, there is no testimony at all and it is permitted:
(There in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) And if he was silent and afterward they contradicted him, etc. And Shach wrote in s.k. 17: it appears to me specifically if they contradict him, etc.; and this is proven in Kiddushin from the case of King Yannai — see there; and it appears his intent is from what we say there "how is it stronger with two," etc. — rather with one witness — see there; and if so it is proven that if he was silent and afterward said "I do not know," the witness is believed — for otherwise there is difficulty either way: if he did not first remain silent, without that one witness is not believed; and if he first remained silent and admitted it was true that his mother was captured, then even if witnesses came afterward, what of it — he already believed the first like two, and it is no worse than two against two; and one should not say it deals when he said "I do not know" from the start — it is difficult how the one witness was believed from the start since he said "I do not know"; or it deals when he was silent at first and afterward said "I do not know" — if so it is well to say that afterward two came and contradicted the first witness; and if so we heard that if witnesses had not come afterward the one witness would have been believed — such is certainly Shach's intent without doubt; however in truth this proof is not at all necessary, for when he is silent he is believed because silence is like admission, and this applies generally; but regarding King Yannai we see they did not believe him and did not accept him like two; therefore when afterward witnesses came and contradict the one witness, they are certainly believed; and silence like admission does not apply in such a case — investigate:
(There seif 3 in the gloss) However some say that in a matter that can be clarified, etc. See there Shach s.k. 24, that Rama should not have written in the language "some say," for it is an explicit Gemara in Kiddushin regarding a blemished person; and this is what it says there: and Abaye said forever that he contradicts him; and what you say — why is he believed? He told him "go and show," etc.; and from this Tosafot learned regarding gentile wine; and these are their words: but in a matter that can be clarified, etc. — come and I will show you — it is forbidden in this; certainly there is concern for his words and to investigate, as we say later in Bava Metzia "he can say go and show," and it can be clarified; therefore he is believed; and at first glance the comparison between libated wine and a blemished person is not understood at all; for there he told him "you are a blemished person," and since he can show and clarify immediately at the time he told him "you are a blemished person," and if he does not clarify it appears somewhat that he believes his words — therefore he must clarify; unlike regarding gentile wine, where he told him "come and I will show you" — he cannot clarify at the time of the statement; and if he does not clarify there is no concern of belief at all, for he can say he does not want to trouble himself to clarify the matter; nevertheless Tosafot compare them to each other; and accordingly Rama wrote in the language "some say," for it is not an explicit Gemara — one can well distinguish between gentile wine and a blemished person as above — investigate:
