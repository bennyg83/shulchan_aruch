(Siman 127, Shach, seif katan 11) And they instruct him, etc. It appears to me that 'they instruct him' is an application of the principle, 'Open your mouth for the mute.' This means that they tell him: if you do not know, say, 'I do not know.' The reason is that perhaps the litigant does not know the law, namely, that saying 'I do not know' is effective, and for that reason he remains silent. Had he known that saying 'I do not know' is effective, he would have said so. Accordingly, when the text says that they instruct him to say, 'I do not believe you,' it means that he does not know. This is clear.
