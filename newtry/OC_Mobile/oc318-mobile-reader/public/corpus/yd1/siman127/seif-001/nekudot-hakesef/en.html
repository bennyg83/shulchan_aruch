(Siman 127, Shach, seif katan 11) And we instruct him, etc. It appears to me that “we instruct him” is comparable to “open your mouth for the mute.” We tell him: If you do not know, say, “I do not know.” The reason is that perhaps the litigant does not know that answering “I do not know” is effective, and therefore remains silent. Had he known the law, he would have said so. Thus, “we instruct him to say, I do not believe you” means that we instruct him to say, “I do not know.” This is readily understood.
