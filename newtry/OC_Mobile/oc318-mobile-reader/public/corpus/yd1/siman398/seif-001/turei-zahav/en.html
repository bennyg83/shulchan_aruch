And some say that even mourning, etc. In the Tur he concludes: “Rather, only aninut on the first day is Torah law, and aninut alone and mourning alone are separate; what is forbidden in this is permitted in that. An onen is not forbidden on the first day by Torah law except in eating kodashim and maaser sheni. The Sages further imposed stringencies upon him in the matters explained in Perek Mi Shemeto”; end quote. This means that one is an onen on the day of death even after burial with respect to eating kodashim and maaser sheni, as stated in Perek Tevul Yom, Zevachim 100. The Sages were stringent before burial regarding eating meat and wine, marital relations, and exemption from all mitzvot, because his dead lies before him to bury. At that time he is permitted in wearing shoes, washing, shaving, and everything forbidden because of mourning above, until the golel is sealed. Then he is permitted in meat and wine and obligated in all mitzvot, except tefillin on the first day. What the Tur wrote, “what is forbidden in this is permitted in that,” is not an absolute rule, for marital relations are forbidden in both; rather, he said this regarding the other distinctions that we mentioned.
