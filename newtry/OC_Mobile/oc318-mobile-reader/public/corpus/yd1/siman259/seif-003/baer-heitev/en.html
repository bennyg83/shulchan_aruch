The community. Specifically the community, but not the charity administrator. Rama's statement in siman 256 concerning an appointed administrator applies only to a mitzvah purpose, not to an optional use. Taz.
An optional purpose. Shach writes that even if the item has not yet been used in the synagogue, so long as the donor's name remains attached to it, it may not be diverted to an optional purpose. If the owners consent and it has not yet been used, diversion is permitted; after use, even their consent does not permit it.
To change it. Authorities dispute whether it may be redirected to a lesser mitzvah. Bach distinguishes synagogue lamps and menorahs, which serve worshippers and are not themselves sacred objects, from articles of sanctity, to which the rule of increasing holiness applies. Rashba limits any redirection to a case where the synagogue already has enough lamps for its needs.
The owner's name has been forgotten. Shach implies that once the donor's name is no longer associated with the item, it may even be diverted to an optional purpose. A non-Jewish donor is treated more stringently because he may publicize that the Jews sold what he donated, causing desecration of Hashem's Name. A Jewish donor is presumed to accept rabbinic authority.
