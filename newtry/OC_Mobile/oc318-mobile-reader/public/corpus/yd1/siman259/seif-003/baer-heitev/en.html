The community. Specifically the community, but not the charity collector; so wrote the Rosh. Although in siman 256 seif 4 Rama wrote that the same applies to a collector appointed by the townspeople, that is specifically for a mitzvah matter, but not for a voluntary matter; end quote, Taz.
Voluntary matter. The Shach wrote: it implies even if they have not yet lit them in the synagogue. Although we hold that designation is not significant, nevertheless if the owner's name has not been forgotten, it is forbidden in every case to change it for a voluntary matter, except when the owners agree to the matter; then it is permitted to change it if they have not used them. But if they used them, even if the owners agree, nevertheless it is forbidden to change.
To change it. The Shach wrote: whether they may change it to a mitzvah lighter than this is a dispute among the poskim. From the Bach's words it appears that he distinguishes: regarding a lamp or menorah donated to the synagogue, even if they used it, one may change it for a mitzvah matter, even for a mitzvah lesser than it, because they gave it to the synagogue only in order to give light to those praying from siddurim and machzorim, and this is only an implement of mitzvah for the person himself. Only with implements of sanctity do we say that we ascend in holiness and do not descend. The Rashba wrote that all this is specifically where there are enough menorahs and lamps for the needs of the synagogue; but if there is not enough, the community is not permitted to change.
Was forgotten. The Shach wrote: it implies that if the owner's name was forgotten, it is permitted to change even for a voluntary matter. The reason is that with a gentile it is forbidden to change because he will shout and say, "I consecrated something for the synagogue of the Jews and they sold it," and there is desecration of the Name in the matter. This does not apply to a Jew, who obeys the words of the sages; and even if he does not obey, nevertheless there is not so much desecration of the Name. So wrote the Rosh in a responsum.
