The community may change it. Specifically the community may change it, even for a mundane purpose, but not the gabbai. So wrote the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Batra. Although above in siman 256 seif 4 the Rama wrote that the same applies to a gabbai appointed by the townspeople, that is specifically for a mitzvah purpose, where he is the gabbai of the townspeople.
They may not change it for a mundane purpose. One must say that also regarding the charities donated for the cemetery, mentioned in seif 2, the owners’ names have not faded from them; otherwise, what is the difference from here, where if the name has faded they may change it for a mundane purpose? Do not object from what the Tur wrote in siman 256 seif 4, which I copied there, distinguishing between a fixed matter and an incidental matter, etc.; why is it permitted to change it here if the name has faded, since this is not a fixed matter? One may answer that he distinguished this only regarding money belonging to the poor, where when it is incidental we say that those poor people acquired it as an addition to what they already have, and we act as the hands of the poor; therefore we may not cause them loss. This is not so with one who consecrates a vessel, where there is no one to acquire that item, and therefore everything depends on whether the name faded or did not fade, even incidentally. So it appears to me. I saw that Maharik, root 5, noticed this question, but his answer there does not settle it for me at all. What I have written is clear in my humble opinion, and it accords with the words of Mahari Weil that I brought in siman 256 seif 4.
A gentile who donates is forbidden, etc. For the ordinary gentile is noisy and constantly cries out that they acted improperly, and there is a chillul Hashem in the matter. But with a Jew, he obeys the words of the Sages, and even if he does not obey, there is not so much chillul Hashem. So wrote the Rosh there in a responsum.
