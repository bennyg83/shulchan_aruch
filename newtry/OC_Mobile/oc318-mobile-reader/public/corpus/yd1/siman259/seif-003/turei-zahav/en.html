The community may redirect it. Only the community may redirect it, even to a discretionary purpose, but not the charity collector. So wrote the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Batra. Although the Rama wrote above, in siman 256, seif 4, that the same applies to a collector appointed by the residents of the city, that means specifically that for a mitzvah purpose he has the authority of the townspeople.
They may not redirect it to a discretionary purpose. One must say that regarding donations made for a cemetery, mentioned in seif 2, the donors' names had likewise not ceased to be associated with the funds. Otherwise, why would that case differ from this one, where if the donors' names have been forgotten the funds may be redirected to a discretionary purpose? One should not challenge this from the Tur's distinction in siman 256, seif 4, which I copied there, between a fixed arrangement and an occasional donation. Why is redirection permitted here once the donor's name has been forgotten, when this is not a fixed arrangement? The answer is that the distinction applies specifically to money belonging to the poor. When it is an occasional donation, we say that the poor people then present acquired it as an addition to what they otherwise receive, and we act as the representatives of the poor. We therefore may not deprive them of it. This differs from one who consecrates a utensil, where no particular person has acquired it. Everything therefore depends upon whether the donor's name remains associated with it, even when the donation was occasional. So it appears to me. I saw that Maharik, shoresh 5, noticed this difficulty, but his resolution does not satisfy me at all. What I have written appears clear in my humble opinion and accords with Mahariv's words that I cited in siman 256, seif 4.
A donation from a non-Jew is forbidden, etc. An ordinary non-Jew continually protests and cries out that the funds were not used properly, creating a chillul Hashem. A Jew, however, obeys the words of the Sages. Even if he does not obey, there is not the same chillul Hashem. So wrote the Rosh there in a responsum.
