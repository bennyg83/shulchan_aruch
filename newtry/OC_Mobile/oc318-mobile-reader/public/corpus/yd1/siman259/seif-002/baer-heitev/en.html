Silver. She'erit Yosef distinguishes a Torah scroll from silver utensils. A Torah scroll is commonly deposited in a synagogue for public reading, so its presence there does not prove that it was donated to the congregation. Silver utensils, however, are not ordinarily left there unless dedicated; otherwise they would be brought only when needed and then returned home. Bach concludes that, according to local custom, a creditor may collect even from a Torah scroll in the ark because its owner could sell it in pressing circumstances. Shach agrees. See Choshen Mishpat 97.
