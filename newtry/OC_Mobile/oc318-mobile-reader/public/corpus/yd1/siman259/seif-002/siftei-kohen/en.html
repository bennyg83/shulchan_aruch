And all this where there is no known custom, etc. And then even if the one who consecrated claims a certain claim that he did not consecrate it for its kind to remove it from the presumption of consecration — except if he conditioned in public or before witnesses at the time he consecrated — so Maharshal in responsa siman 15, and Bach brings it and they wrote there likewise regarding a Torah scroll; and they learned thus from the words of Mahari"k shoresh 161 regarding silver vessels of consecration — see there. But in responsum of Mahari Cohen of Krakow siman 21 he wrote they disagree, for a Torah scroll — the way and custom of the world is to leave it in the synagogue and it is known that the father's name is called on the Torah scroll — who can remove it from the father's presumption? For what the Torah scroll was left for is no proof it belongs to the community, for in these times the main writing of a Torah scroll is to leave it to read from the Torah scroll in public; also its sanctity is great and it is not proper in every place to leave a Torah scroll — therefore there is no proof; for even though he left it for the synagogue there is no proof he consecrated it. But silver vessels — the custom is not to leave them in the synagogue; and if he did not consecrate them they would not leave them in the synagogue but would use them in the synagogue at time of need and afterward take them home. Mahari"k wrote in shoresh 161 that those silver vessels that he left there are in the presumption of the community; and also regarding a Torah scroll he wrote in shoresh 7 that it is in the presumption of the owner whose name is called on it — meaning on one who says "this is the Torah scroll of so-and-so" for the reason stated; and he extended there regarding who is fit to testify, for the townspeople have an interest in testimony if they do not withdraw; and also that there should likewise be another Torah scroll there. And similarly the Tur siman 36 that all have interest in testimony — his reasoning is sound and settles on the heart; also in what he distinguished between silver and Torah scroll the reasoning is sound, for it is difficult to remove a matter from its owners' presumption without clear proof. I extended on the law of a Torah scroll because I heard that one rabbi ruled also regarding a Torah scroll that it belongs to consecration — therefore I needed to bring proof and extend with proofs and reasons unlike his view — end quote.
