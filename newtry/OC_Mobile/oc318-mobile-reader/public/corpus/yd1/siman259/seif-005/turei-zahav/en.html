With wording whose meaning is uncertain, etc. In Choshen Mishpat, siman 250, I wrote that the Rama's position requires study. In siman 258, seif 1, he rules stringently that the second item is also charity, although that matter depends upon uncertain wording. Here, he rules leniently. In Choshen Mishpat there, seif 3, he likewise rules stringently, and this requires study. Mahari, in his writings, siman 73, ruled that regarding hekdesh and vows, whenever wording may be understood in either of two ways, we do not rule stringently, but leave the money in the possession of its current owner. The same applies to a document, where the holder of the document has the weaker position, end quote. After further consideration, I found a resolution of the Rama's position, and his words are correct. Whenever the doubt can never be clarified, such as the case in siman 258, where the issue is whether partial expressions apply to charity, if they do apply it is as though he explicitly said, “This too shall be charity.” Even if he claims that he intended the second item for his own expenses, he is not believed. The same applies to the case mentioned here by the Shulchan Aruch, where the money itself is uncertain whether it belongs to charity. In such cases we certainly rule stringently. This differs from a case where a person on his deathbed used wording whose intended meaning is uncertain. Had he remained alive and explained his intention, we would have believed him, as stated in siman 208: Unspecified vows are interpreted stringently, but their explanations are interpreted leniently. The heir may therefore say, “Had my father been alive, he would have explained that he intended the money to remain his.” We therefore do not remove it from the heirs' possession. I saw that the Levush distinguished between the opening and concluding cases by saying that in the opening case the person had no prior presumption of ownership over the money. This distinction has no basis. In the opening case too, the money was in his possession, for the Beit Yosef derives it from doubtful gleanings lying within his field. What I have written appears correct, with God's help.
