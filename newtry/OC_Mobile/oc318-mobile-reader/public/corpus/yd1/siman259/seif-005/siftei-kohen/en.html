But who is called, etc. It needs study, for the Rav compares Rashba's responsum to Gemara chapter 1 of Bava Batra in the name of avodah zarah — brief, and distinguishes between when heirs were called and are in possession and doubt of prohibition does not apply strictly regarding heirs; but this does not appear from Rashba's responsum siman 256 where he wrote: if we are in doubt whether his intent was to retract or not, it is reasonable to be lenient toward the heir since money is in the possession of its owner and heirs are like owners — he only consecrated conditionally and the poor who said the condition was fulfilled must bring proof; certainly like one who taught in a baraita in chapter Mi SheMet — he takes from them without proof and they do not take from him except with proof; and even though this is doubt of hekdesh we do not say safek de'isura lechumra — one, that hekdesh of the poor is ordinary money and we tell them bring proof and take; and like what they said in chapter Oto VeEt Beno (Chullin 79) regarding gifts of kilayim etc.; and therefore whether hekdesh of the poor or synagogue hekdesh, it is not hekdesh whenever there is doubt at its outset whether it took effect or not, and there is monetary presumption against it — we establish money in the presumption of its owner, as we say in chapter Mi SheMet; from which we learn that one who stands to retract his gift — a question was raised: if he consecrated all his property, what if he declared all his property ownerless, what if he gave part to the poor — unresolved, and unresolved there is for leniency; and likewise Rambam; and even per one who rules there strictly, it is reasonable here to be lenient, for there hekdesh was certainly consecrated but we doubt when he stands whether he retracts, but here we doubt whether hekdesh took effect at all — from doubt we do not remove from the owner's possession — end quote; and if so what proof does he bring from priestly gifts and one who stands to retract — there it is not heirs but there is no distinction, and always it is safek mamon lekula; and also from Mahar"i in his rulings siman 73 it appears explicitly there is no distinction except in every matter it is safek mamon lekula — he wrote there regarding hekdesh by asmakhta etc.; but one should not obligate from safek to be strict in a dispute of many authorities in charity, for in chapter Mi SheMet (Bava Batra 148b) they raised: if he consecrated his property, declared ownerless, gave part to the poor — what; and avodah zarah there ruled establish money in owner's presumption and the poor did not acquire and hekdesh since it stands unresolved; and fortunate is one who ruled there the poor acquired — from another reason as proven there — end quote; and also in responsum Rivash siman 170 it is clear there is no such distinction — see there. Also Gemara's words need study that he brought from baraita of Tosefta and Yerushalmi regarding leket shekechah and peah that safek is strict for charity — this baraita is brought in our Gemara chapter HaZerua VeHaLechayayim (Chullin 134a) and Rava explains there the reason is because grain stands under obligation of leket — implying otherwise it would be safek mamon lekula; and likewise Abaye challenges there regarding dough made before conversion exempt from challah, after conversion liable — safek chayav, he said safek isura lechumra safek mamon lekula — evidently safek of the poor is safek mamon and not safek isur; and Ran beginning of Nedarim (7a) regarding whether there is "hand" for charity — Rashba wrote regarding peah and charity we are strict since it stands unresolved; and likewise Ramban in his halachot that we hold every unresolved matter of prohibition strictly etc.; and I wonder, for the sugya is explicit in chapter HaZerua that safek mamon of the poor is safek mamon and we go leniently for the defendant etc.; and we say in chapter 1 of Yoma (9a) etc. until: but certainly safek mamon of the poor is not called safek de'isura at all but safek demamona lekula — therefore I am not clear from their words — examine; for Rashba's view in the responsum is also safek demamona lekula; also examine what Mahar"i and "fortunate" who ruled the poor acquired — from another reason implies he concedes safek of the poor is safek demamona lekula, but this is not so as Rosh wrote beginning of Nedarim on that question "he said this and that" — unresolved of Torah strictly — end quote; and why Rosh in chapter Mi SheMet needed another reason — it appears because of hefker that is clear to all it is safek mamon lekula as Ran wrote there, and regarding hefker there is no doubt it is safek lekula — end quote; again found in responsum R' Betzalel siman 15 bringing responsum Ran siman 1 and responsum Rivash siman 160 that they ruled safek mamon lekula, and it appears there plainly this is our established law, and likewise Mechaber ruled plainly and the Rav in Choshen Mishpat siman 200 seif 3 regarding the question in chapter Mi SheMet "if he consecrated his property" etc. — if he stands he may retract (and even if the poor seized, we remove from their hands — per Rivash that unresolved in Gemara does not help seizure; see Yam Shel Shlomo chapter 1 of Bava Kama siman 5); again found in responsum Mahari"v siman 173 — he wrote: if your soul wishes to say that since the woman said to give to charity, therefore it is like prohibition and safek isura lechumra as in chapter Mi SheMet we learn one who consecrated all his property — what; and it stands unresolved, and Rosh wrote from doubt we do not nullify hekdesh there where he consecrated all property without specification we can doubt his intent was whether he lives or dies and hekdesh began in life not after death; but here in our case she specified after death explicitly — one may say charity never began, property is in heirs' presumption; and likewise if one consecrated money he cannot extract in court and it is not holy — likewise when she died they are in heirs' possession — end quote; and the matters need study.
