(Siman 109, Shach s.k. 3) That flour in flour is not like wet in wet, and so is explained explicitly in Rambam (chapter 13 of Hilchot Terumot) — and see Beit Yosef MeLamed (chapter 1 of Hilchot Metamei Mishkav UMoshav):
(Seif 1 in the hagahah) Even dry in dry requires sixty — issur of an olive's bulk became mixed in two pieces, each of thirty olive-bulks, and it is not known which piece is the issur — one may say: since perhaps one of the larger ones is the issur, nevertheless there is a majority; if so, regarding concern lest he cook them, which is only d'rabbanan, it is a safek d'rabbanan — perhaps the small one is the issur — only forbidden to cook them (Pri Megadim s.k. 2 in ShaDa s.k. 14):
