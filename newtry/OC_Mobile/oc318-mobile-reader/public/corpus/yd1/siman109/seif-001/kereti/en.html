Same kind in its kind; but when not its kind, sixty is required, and even dry in dry — lest he cook them and it give taste:
And the issur, etc. The piece by itself is called dry in dry; only the taste that it gave to the piece beside it is called wet in wet — by way of parable: issur pieces in same kind in its kind according to R' Yehudah — in its kind it is not nullified, meaning wet in wet; but not dry in dry — the piece with its fellow is called dry in dry, but the taste it received from its fellow is called wet in wet and is not nullified:
And one does not recognize it; and flour — if it were wet in wet or dry in dry — there is dispute among poskim; Rambam's view is that it is not called wet in wet; in Tosafot there are divided opinions; Acharonim's view is that it is wet in wet; and see what Peleti wrote; and since it is not proven explicitly that flour is called wet in wet, therefore in same kind in its kind — even if it were wet in wet, from the Torah only majority is required — one should not be stringent with flour in flour, and in the majority it is nullified:
One in two; and if two in three — in dry, and likewise in wet, two measures in three became mixed — the last ones rose in the majority and are nullified; and see Peleti that from the Torah certainly nullified, only d'rabbanan according to Rambam and Rashal to be stringent that against three we require six; and one may be lenient since it is only d'rabbanan:
But he may not eat, etc. — for eating certainly issur that became mixed; but when eating by itself, one may say regarding each eating it is heter — therefore in mixtures of two in three, forbidden all four together, for he eats certain issur; and likewise one in three — permitted to eat by itself, and it is plain:
Even this, etc. See Peleti that this is a d'rabbanan stringency, and from the Torah permitted even one person:
And one does not recognize it — meaning it was broken into small pieces that are not recognizable:
Dry in dry — Shach's and Pri Chadash's view that it is d'rabbanan; and Terumat HaDeshen's view that from the Torah sixty is required; and see Peleti; and the essence: if an olive-bulk became mixed in each piece that is the measure of one in nine olive-bulks — forbidden from the Torah; but in more than that it is only d'rabbanan, lest he cook them and it give taste:
Between issur — see Shach who brought Or Zarua's view that in d'rabbanan issur, one in one — permitted for two people to eat them, but not one person; and Rama wrote that Rosh disagrees; and see Peleti:
D'rabbanan — same kind when not its kind, dry in dry, in d'rabbanan issur — Shach's view: one in two is nullified, for even if he cooks, he will not come to Torah issur; and the Rav, author of Minchat Kohen, challenged him; and according to what I wrote above, one should forbid dry in dry without the concern that if he cooks, etc. — plainly the essence is like Shach, and so poskim rule Rama's words:
