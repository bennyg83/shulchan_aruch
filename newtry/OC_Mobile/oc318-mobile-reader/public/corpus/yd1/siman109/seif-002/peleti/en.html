Rama wrote in Turei Chayim general rule 39 siman 2 in name of Hagahot Or Zarua: that which we require in dry matter one in two — this is in Torah issur; but in d'rabbanan, even if only one in one became mixed — permitted for two people, or one Israelite and one gentile to eat them — end; and the Rav wrote in Turei Chayim and Rosh chapter 1 of Beitzah disagrees on this — end. And all commentators wondered, for we do not find in Rosh that chapter any contradiction, and he mentioned a general rule. And it appears we say in chapter 1 of Beitzah: wood that fell on Yom Tov into the oven — add wood and nullify them, etc. — and it challenges: behold he nullifies issur l'chatchila! And we answer: this applies in d'oraisa, but in d'rabbanan we nullify; and for Rava Ashi who said every davar sheyesh lo matirin even in d'rabbanan is not nullified — no, this applies where it comes to issur in substance; but here it becomes light to issur — end. And Rashba's view: that version in d'rabbanan we nullify issur refers from what he did not say except in that version "becomes light" and comes only to answer that difficulty of sheylo yimatzu that is like Torah law; but the version itself refers in d'rabbanan we nullify. But Rosh wrote: specifically with wood that becomes light to issur we nullify d'rabbanan issur; but generally even d'rabbanan issur we do not nullify. And one must understand from where Rosh got this reasoning, for it does not appear so in the Gemara — must say the difficulty of Tosafot there s.v. Vechi challenged from a litra of dried figs — add and nullify them; and Tosafot answered to distinguish whether its essence is from Torah or not — this strained Rosh, therefore he explains we nullify only where it becomes light, etc.; and so Maharsha — Rosh resolves Tosafot's difficulty. And behold according to Hagahot Or Zarua there is no difficulty at all: granted d'rabbanan issur that became mixed one in one — permitted for two people — if so there is no issur at all — what of it that forbidden for one person, behold for two permitted, and there is no complete issur until we say one may not nullify issur; but regarding terumah his law of Or Zarua does not apply, for terumah is forbidden until it ascends one in a hundred — if so there is issur upon him and one may not nullify issur, and there is no difficulty, and Rosh need not say it does not refer to all d'rabbanan issurim; and perforce one must say Rosh did not accept Or Zarua's reasoning, and his words are very compelled — and requires study:
