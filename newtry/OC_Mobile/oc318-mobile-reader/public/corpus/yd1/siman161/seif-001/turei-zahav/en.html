Every matter is forbidden, etc. The Tur writes: except for land, such as where he lent him five vines, etc. This is derived from the generalization and specification in the verse, for we require something whose substance is money and which is movable. Land is excluded because it is not movable, and less than the value of a perutah is excluded because its substance is not money. The Ramah holds that less than the value of a perutah is considered money, except that it is not recoverable through the courts. The Rosh writes that servants and documents cannot be excluded, for ribbis applies only to a loan, which is given to be spent, whereas borrowing does not involve ribbis, but only rental payment. The Beit Yosef objects: Are servants and documents not subject to a loan, as where one gives him one servant on condition that after a time he give him two others, and similarly with a document, etc.? His meaning is that he will not return that same servant in kind, so that it bears the status of a loan. My revered father in law, of blessed memory, writes that in such a case there is indeed full ribbis. His words are astonishing, for the Beit Yosef said only that ribbis can also apply to servants in such a case. Nevertheless, the Torah excluded servants as well, for whenever land is excluded through a generalization and specification, as in the chapter HaZahav, we say that servants were also excluded because they are compared to land. The same applies here. Yet I find it difficult that if one says in this language, “Lend me one servant and I will return two to you,” the term borrowing does not apply. One who borrows a servant returns that servant, having borrowed him only for labor. If he says that he will not return him in kind, this is a sale. What difference does it make that he uses the language of borrowing? This resembles one who purchases an article from another and says, “Lend me this article and I will return such and such a sum in exchange.” Would the law of borrowing apply? Certainly not. Only with something intended to be spent, such as money, can the language of a loan apply. What is difficult to me is that the Rosh writes that this is considered rental payment, meaning where he returns the servant in kind and, in connection with the rental, gives him ribbis. This is truly forbidden even with a servant, for the Mishnah at the end of the chapter Eizehu Neshech forbids one to say, “Weed with me and I will hoe with you.” Rashi explains that this is because he delays payment for the other’s labor. So too writes the Tur in siman 160. This is comparable to a servant, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 227, Shulchan Aruch, seif 33, which writes concerning one who hires his fellow, etc., that it is as though he acquires him for a period, and there is no law of onaah for servants. If so, we should say the same regarding ribbis, since ribbis too was excluded by the same generalization and specification from which onaah was excluded. One can answer that servants were indeed excluded by Torah law in all the cases we mentioned, but they are forbidden by rabbinic law. This is comparable to an oath, concerning which land and servants are excluded by Torah law, yet a heses oath nevertheless applies. So too regarding ribbis, there is a rabbinic prohibition with every type of property. One should not object that, if so, we should likewise say that onaah applies by rabbinic law. The answer is that the Sages did not institute the extraction of money contrary to Torah law. Here, however, they did not require the extraction of money, for rabbinic ribbis is never returned; they merely forbade acting in this manner. According to this, the Beit Yosef is difficult, for he writes: “Although the Rosh writes that it is permitted, this apparently means by Torah law, but it is nevertheless possible that it is forbidden by rabbinic law.” End quote. Why does he use the language “possible”? It is certainly forbidden by rabbinic law, as we proved from the Mishnah mentioned above and as is also evident from what he writes at the end of siman 176 concerning one who hires a worker to work with him during the winter, etc. What the Beit Yosef writes regarding a document presents no difficulty at all. When one lends him the document, he is lending him the money represented by it, and the essence of the loan is the money. It is obvious that this involves full ribbis. A document is not excluded at all, since a loan does not apply to the document itself. The Beit Yosef further cites the view of the Rashba, who holds that we do not expound a generalization and specification here at all. According to him, nothing is excluded from the prohibition of ribbis. This also appears to be the implication of the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch here.
Compensation for waiting. Meaning, he gives him payment for waiting for the money.
