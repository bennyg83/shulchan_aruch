(Siman 161, seif 1) It is forbidden to borrow anything with an addition. The Tur writes, citing the explanation of Ri, that land is an exception. For example, one may lend another five loaded vines, and so forth. See Shitah Mekubetzet, who adds that this can occur where the lender says that the borrower may return this parcel together with an additional quarter-kav of land, or may instead give him an entirely different parcel of equivalent size. It is therefore not an exchange, because he may return the original land, and it is not a rental, because he may give different land. This requires comparison with Tosafot, page 69b, s.v. Mara Hadra. Tosafot conclude that since the implement suffers depreciation if returned in kind, even though the borrower was authorized to return a different implement, the transaction remains a rental because he may return the original item in its depreciated state. This remains so even if he ultimately returns a different hoe. The same reasoning would appear to apply here. Examine the matter carefully.
