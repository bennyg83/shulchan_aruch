Recognizable. Taz explains that otherwise the witnesses would be disqualified as wicked for signing an interest-bearing document. Shach writes that the case is where principal and interest were written separately. The witnesses are not disqualified even for Torah interest, because ordinary people understand the prohibition, 'Do not place interest upon him,' as directed mainly to lender and borrower. This appears to be the Mechaber's view in Choshen Mishpat 34 and 52.
It should be torn. Even where Torah interest is explicitly stated, we fear an erroneous court may collect it. This applies specifically where principal and interest are separately stated. If only the principal appears in the document, it plainly should not be torn. Shach.
The principal. We decree lest the lender collect interest under the guise of principal. Where the borrower admits the principal, some authorities permit collection, as Rama writes in Choshen Mishpat 52. The borrower need not surrender any profit earned with the lender's money, no matter how large, though he may appropriately be fined to give some profit to the city's poor. Taz writes that Rama cites a view exempting even the principal despite admission, but no genuine authority holds this and it is not halachah.
