It is recoverable through the courts. It appears that this applies specifically if he makes a claim. The proof is from Rashi in the chapter HaRibbis, 61a, who writes: “Beit din would compel him to return it if the borrower claimed it during his lifetime.” End quote. Based on this, I wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 9, regarding one who claimed that he had no means to pay and no money was found in his possession other than fixed ribbis that another person had already taken from him, and the creditor sought to collect that ribbis on behalf of the borrower, that the law is not with him. Since there is no claim by the borrower, beit din has no basis to compel the recipient of the ribbis to return it. There is further proof, for we say here that beit din does not descend to his property, but merely compels him to fulfill the positive commandment, “Your brother shall live with you,” as the Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Nimukei Yosef. This does not apply here, for extracting the ribbis will not provide for the life of the borrower.
