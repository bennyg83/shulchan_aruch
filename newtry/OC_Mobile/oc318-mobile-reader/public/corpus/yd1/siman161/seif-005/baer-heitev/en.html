They would compel. Taz writes that this applies only if the borrower demands repayment. Accordingly, where a debtor claims poverty and the only asset found is fixed interest previously paid to another, the creditor cannot demand that interest on the borrower's behalf, because absent the borrower's own claim beit din does not compel its return.
His property. Shach writes that if the lender returned the interest by order of beit din or voluntarily, and later seized property from the borrower to recover it, beit din may descend to his property, since this is theft. The same applies if he originally took fixed interest forcibly.
