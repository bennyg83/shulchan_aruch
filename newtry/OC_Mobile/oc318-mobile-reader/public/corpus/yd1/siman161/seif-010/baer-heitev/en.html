The dinar. Taz wrote in the Talmud it is stated he cannot say I rented at high price thinking I would profit from the ribbit and now that I must return the ribbit it is not worth it — he can say I thought and accepted; and it seems plain the same if he bought from him an object or land for the sum he took as ribbit — not enough to return the purchase now, for he already became obligated to him for that sum of money; and that they mentioned this regarding rent and not purchase teaches that even in rent where acquisition is not as strong as in purchase he can say I thought and accepted — all the more in purchase — and not like Perishah — end quote; and Beit Yosef wrote Rashba in a responsum was uncertain if one says I will give you twelve dinars ribbit on condition my courtyard that rents only for ten you rent for twelve — when we extract from him we extract only ten, for in the essence of the condition he could not accept this except as rent of the house that rents only for ten — end quote.
