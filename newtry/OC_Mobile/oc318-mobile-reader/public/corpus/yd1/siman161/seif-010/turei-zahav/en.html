The Gemara states that he cannot say: “The reason I rented it at an excessive price was that I thought I was receiving a profit through the ribbis, but now that I must return the ribbis, I rented it only at the rate paid by everyone else.” The other may answer him: “You considered the matter and accepted it.” It appears obvious that the same applies if he purchased from him an article or land for the amount he had taken as ribbis. It is not sufficient for him now to return the purchased item. Since he considered and accepted the transaction, it is as though he gave him the entire dinar in cash, for the item was worth a dinar to him. He cannot now compel him to take back the article, because he already became obligated to pay him a dinar for it, and the seller does not wish to accept in payment of his debt an article worth only half a dinar. The Gemara and the Tur discuss rental rather than a purchase in order to teach a greater novelty. Even with rental, where the acquisition is not as strong as in a purchase, he may nevertheless say, “You considered the matter and accepted it.” All the more so in a purchase, where the acquisition is stronger, he considered it and accepted it. The language of the Rambam likewise indicates this, for he writes: “Since he rented it from him for a dinar and accepted this upon himself.” This plainly applies to a purchase as well, since he purchased it for a dinar and accepted this upon himself. I wrote this because the Perishah infers from the Tur’s use of the word “rented,” rather than “purchased,” that if he purchased an article from him for a dinar although it was worth only half a dinar, he returns the article as he purchased it. This differs from rental, where he has already used it and is liable for the money, namely the rental payment for the use that has already passed. End quote. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct. Why should he pay for rental use more than the amount properly due? How does this differ from returning the article he purchased? If he returns the money due for the rental according to its actual value, this is comparable to returning the article as he purchased it. We see clearly that the essential reason is that he considered and accepted it, and this applies both to a purchase and to rental. The general law is likewise that if one knows that an article is worth less than the amount he gives for it and nevertheless pays that amount, he has no claim of onaah and the sale remains valid. Why should the rule be different here in a purchase? It is therefore clear that there is no distinction, and even in a purchase he must return a full dinar.
