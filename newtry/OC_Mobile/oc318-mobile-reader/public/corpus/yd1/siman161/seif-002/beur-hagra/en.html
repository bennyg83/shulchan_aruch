And if he comes, etc. According to R' Elazar, avak ribbis has the same status in this respect as fixed ribbis has according to R' Yochanan, and there one certainly must return it in order to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven. This follows from the statement that the prohibition was given for the purpose of fear, meaning to fulfill one's obligation toward Heaven, as explained by Rivan. Tosafos, however, on daf 62a, s.v. "a stipulation," etc., disagree with Rivan, and likewise in Bava Kamma 94a. They further bring proof from the Tosefta: "One who lent his fellow with ribbis and repented is obligated to return it." Since it says "and repented," this indicates that the teaching follows R' Yochanan.
(Likkut) And if he comes, etc. Tosafos in Bava Kamma 94b, s.v. "if," etc., and s.v. "in order to fulfill," etc., disagree with this. Ramban and the other authorities, however, explain Rivan's interpretation in Tosafos there, namely that the prohibition was given for the purpose of fear, etc. They also face Tosafos's difficulty: why does the Gemara ask, "What does he have with him?" if he need not return it even to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven? The passage in Bava Kamma can be resolved by Tosafos's first answer, that if he returned it, etc., or by their second answer. They did not accept Tosafos's third answer, because the Gemara's difficulty was from the verse, "Do not take interest from your brother," which applies when he has repented; according to that third answer, the issue would not depend on whether he is among "your people." Tosafos, however, follows that third answer. End.
(Likkut) And if he comes, etc. Ramban in Milchamos and the Rosh in his name, siman 17, hold this way. Ramban writes, etc., and all the more so one who lent him, etc.; even in order to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven, etc. See below, siman 166, seif 1. End.
(Likkut) Rashba disagrees. He writes in Bava Kamma 75a, based on the statement there, "Your words are of no effect," that the Jerusalem Talmud infers a general rule regarding penalties: whenever there are no witnesses, he is not obligated even in order to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven. Otherwise, Rabban Gamliel should have freed the slave in order to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven. Rashba writes that the same applies to avak ribbis. This also appears from the fifth chapter of Bava Metzia, daf 64-65, in the incident involving Rav Yosef bar Chama, where he said, "I retract," meaning only from that point onward; we do not find that he returned the rent for the slaves. According to Ramban and the Rosh, siman 17, however, there is no proof from that case. End.
(Likkut) And if he comes, etc. The proof brought by Nimukei Yosef from the Tosefta is not conclusive. The Tosefta at the end of chapter 5 teaches: "One who lends his fellow with ribbis and repents is obligated to return it. If he died and left it before his sons, his sons need not return it. Of this it is said, 'The wicked prepares and the righteous wears.' But if their father left them a cow, a field, a garment, or any object that entails responsibility, they are obligated to return it." It says "and repents" because of the latter clause, as explained at the beginning of chapter 9 of Bava Kamma and in Bava Metzia there. End.
(Likkut) Except for ribbis, etc. For he gave it as a gift, as stated there in the Mishnah, daf 75b, "a gift," etc. End.
