To appoint an agent. The verse says, “Her husband shall confirm it and her husband shall annul it,” not his agent. Although ordinarily a person’s agent is like himself, Scripture revealed otherwise here. The Tur concludes, “even concerning vows she already made.” This appears obvious. Could the agent be stronger than the husband, who cannot annul before she vows? The answer is that the Baraisa states: If one says, “Any vows my wife will make before I return from a certain place, annul them for her,” and the agent annuls them, perhaps they should be annulled. Scripture therefore says, “Her husband shall confirm it and her husband shall annul it,” according to R’ Yoshiyah. R’ Yonasan answers that throughout the Torah a person’s agent is like himself. This might imply that R’ Yoshiyah forbids only when the agent performs both acts, hearing and annulment, but not when he performs only one. The Tur therefore teaches that even if the husband himself heard and the agent performs only the annulment, it is ineffective. The Baraisa mentions both acts to teach the strength of R’ Yonasan’s permissive view.
