He did not hear the husband. O Lord, who has such a name, is the same as that of a divorce, and in section 2, provides a divorce to him or not, and if he does not have any other violation:
And afterward he betrothed her. All the more so if he actually took her in marriage — he cannot revoke what she vowed before she left his domain [as a wife of another], for a husband does not revoke vows that predate [his marital bond]; whereas regarding a betrothed woman we hold above that he can revoke prior vows. Here he cannot revoke even in partnership with the father, since at the time she vowed she was not eligible for revocation — having been married to another — and she will never again come under his authority to join with the father. And the same applies to what she vows now while betrothed: the father's partnership avails nothing — for this shares one reason with her having vowed while first married; one depends on the other: since he already has no rights in her vows, he likewise has no rights afterward. This was already written in accordance with my gloss in the adjacent passage. And Beit Yosef wanted to infer from the Tur's focusing on the prior vow that cannot be revoked — that therefore what she vows now he can revoke together with the father; and he entered into difficulties on account of this, saying the Tur refers only to delivery to the groom's agents and not actual marriage — in order to settle the Tur's words. But he himself does not hold so, as is evident from his words. And it seems to me that the Tur also holds as I have written. My father-in-law of blessed memory also wrote here that it refers only to entering the domain [without full marriage] — and therefore the groom can revoke what she vows while betrothed in partnership with the father. But in my view she has entirely left her father's domain even without a chuppah, through delivery to the groom's agents or entering his domain — and her father has no rights in the revocation of her vows forever; except that as a stringency we say that what she vows after delivery to the groom's agents, the groom may not revoke until after the chuppah, as written above.
