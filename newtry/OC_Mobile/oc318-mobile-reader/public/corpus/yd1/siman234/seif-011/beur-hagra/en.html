According to Rambam, etc. Rambam explains Rav Nachman’s statement on 69a, “The same rule applies,” etc., as referring to the latter clauses: whether the husband annulled or the father annulled, the father can no longer annul further, because the first annulment weakened the vow. The primary question concerns annulment by the husband, though the same applies to annulment by the father, as Rosh and Ran write there. Likewise, on 71a Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute whether annulment cuts off the vow or merely weakens it. That discussion concerns annulment by the husband, though the case there involves a layman who retracted and she then became betrothed. In any event, their dispute includes annulment by the husband. This is why 48b states: “Her husband heard and annulled for her,” etc., “this is what we learned,” etc., rather than including it with the opening clause, “When did they say,” etc. Rosh and Ran, however, maintain that the schools do not disagree in that clause. See Ran there, s.v. shama baalah, and Rosh 69a, s.v. hen hen, etc.
Afterward he heard, etc. If he had heard beforehand, Rambam does not disagree, for that is the opening clause: “then he heard and annulled,” etc. Beit Hillel do not disagree there, since the baraita groups together the cases where the husband did not hear and where he heard and remained silent. It therefore separates the latter clause, “Her husband heard,” etc., because that is where they dispute, as explained.
