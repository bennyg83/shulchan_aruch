The father annuls alone. We rule that when the betrothed man dies, his authority is transferred to the father. This differs from the father’s death, or when she leaves his authority through becoming a bogeres, for then the father’s authority is not transferred to the betrothed man, as stated in seif 9.
According to the Rambam, if the betrothed man heard, etc. These words are astonishing in my opinion. It is an explicit Talmudic rule that the authority returns to the father. The Rambam himself writes that if both the betrothed man and father heard, and the betrothed man then died, the father may annul because the husband’s authority has returned to him. One cannot say this applies only if the father had already heard, but if he first heard after the husband’s death he cannot annul. Nedarim 70a derives the father’s renewed authority from the verse, “And if she shall be,” comparing the period before a second betrothal to the period before the first. Just as before the first betrothal the father annuls alone, so after the betrothed man’s death, before a second betrothal, the father annuls alone. This includes even a father who first heard after the death. The Tur states explicitly that not only may the father annul a vow made after the betrothed man’s death, but even one made during his lifetime, implying even if the father first heard afterward. The Beit Yosef’s attribution to the Rambam arose from the text he copied. Had he copied the Rambam’s exact words, he would not have written this. The Rambam says: If the betrothed man heard and annulled, then died, and afterward the father heard, or if the father heard and annulled and the husband died before hearing, the father cannot annul those vows alone once they were seen by the first betrothed man. Rather, if she became betrothed again that same day, he annuls together with the new betrothed man, as explained. The Beit Yosef understood that without a later betrothed man the father has no remedy. In my opinion, the Rambam means only that where there is a later betrothed man on that same day, he stands in place of the first, and the father cannot annul without joining him, just as he could not annul without the first. If there is no second betrothed man, the father certainly annuls alone, for the first husband’s authority returned to him, as the Rambam already ruled. There is therefore no dispute between the Rambam and Tur. So it appears correct to me.
