Or he died after the day he heard it, the father cannot annul, etc. This implies that while the betrothed husband remains alive, the father can annul even after the day the husband heard it. This requires study, because it is true only according to the opinion above in seif 5, not according to Rambam there. Rambam's point here is that if the betrothed husband died, the father cannot annul even on the husband's day of hearing.
And according to Rambam, etc., and afterward he heard, etc. This implies specifically that the father heard only afterward. If he heard during the betrothed husband's lifetime, even though he did not annul then, he may annul afterward. Beit Yosef initially writes this, but later concludes the opposite. In Bedek HaBayit he retracts that conclusion. See there.
