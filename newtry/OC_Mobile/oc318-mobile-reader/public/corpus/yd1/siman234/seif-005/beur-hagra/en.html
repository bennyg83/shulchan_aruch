She married, etc. The Sifrei expounds: “But the vow of a widow or divorced woman,” etc. One might think this applies even if she was widowed from this man and married another; therefore Scripture says, “And if she vowed in her husband’s house,” in every case. We find similarly a widow married to a High Priest, and a divorcee or chalutzah married to an ordinary priest. The printed words “because we find” are a scribal error; it should read “and we find,” or “for example.” So too in Sifrei Zuta: What is taught by the entire passage concerning a woman? It includes a widow married to a High Priest, a divorcee or chalutzah married to an ordinary priest, a mamzer, a Samaritan, and a chalal. These unions are treated more leniently in certain monetary laws, such as the ketubah, produce, and worn property, as stated in Yevamot, chapter 9, mishnayot 4 and 5. See Tosafot, Yevamot 89b, end of s.v. keivan.

They must hear, etc. Semag writes that this follows from Nedarim 72a, where the betrothed man had not heard but the father heard, and the baraita says, “on that day,” etc. His words require study, and Lechem Mishneh already rejected them; see there. At first glance, however, Nedarim 68a implies this from, “or he heard and annulled and then died,” etc. This suggests that on the following day the father cannot annul, since had the betrothed man remained alive he could no longer annul, and the same should apply after his death. Ran rejects this proof there, s.v. ta shema, etc. Rosh likewise reads in his rulings: “or he heard, remained silent, and died on that day,” etc., and writes that the father annuls on the day he hears and need not, etc. Rambam also rules this way in chapter 11, law 16: “or the betrothed man also heard and annulled, or remained silent and died on that day.” Yet this does not prove the contrary, since there the betrothed man died and his authority reverted to the father, as Lechem Mishneh writes. Rather, Rambam derived the rule from the verses, as he states: “If her father restrained her on the day he heard,” and, “If she shall indeed be married to a man,” etc., “on the day he heard.” From this you learn that after the father annulled and the betrothed man heard, the latter annuls on the day the father heard; the same applies if the betrothed man heard and annulled, etc. Rambam understood the plain meaning of these verses to require that both hear on the same day. He did not explain “on the day he heard” in the ordinary sense because of the Sifrei, which teaches from “If her father restrained her on the day he heard” that, just as a husband’s hearing followed by silence on the day of the vow confirms it, so too a father’s; it derives this from “These are the statutes,” which compares father and husband. Likewise, the Sifrei derives the husband’s rule from “If on the day her husband hears he restrains her,” rather than from the earlier verse, “and her husband hears it on the day he hears.” This is why Rambam cited these verses here.
Some say, etc. So writes the Tur in the name of Ramban, adding that this is the more reasonable view. Beit Yosef writes that Rosh also holds this way, but that does not appear correct, as explained above. Lechem Mishneh likewise rejects that claim. This is why the Tur did not cite the view in his father’s name.
