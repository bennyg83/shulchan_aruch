Both on the same day. So the Tur wrote in the name of Rambam; and his reasoning: since it is written regarding a betrothed woman "and her husband heard on the day he heard it" — and this "on the day he heard it" has no independent meaning, since it is obvious that he would hear it on the day he hears; rather it certainly refers to what precedes it, "and her father heard, etc." — meaning the husband too must hear on the same day her father hears, specifically. The minority opinion is Ramban, who holds that "on the day he heard it" refers to what follows: "on the day he heard it and remained silent for her, etc." — meaning that for the silence to count it must be on that same day; but for ratification [hekamah], immediately suffices — for if he ratifies it, the father's revocation can no longer take effect at all, as written in seif 6. So it seems to me his reasoning is. And regarding the halacha, the Tur wrote of Ramban's view: "this seems more reasonable" — but from the Shulchan Arukh it is implied to be stringent like Rambam, since it is a biblical-level doubt.
