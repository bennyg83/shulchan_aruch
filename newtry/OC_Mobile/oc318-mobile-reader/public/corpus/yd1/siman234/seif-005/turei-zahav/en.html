He is obligated to support her. The Tur gives the example of a bogeres who waited twelve months and was then betrothed, or a widow who was betrothed and thirty days passed after the kiddushin. Although the bogeres already waited twelve months to prepare her ornaments, she still requires thirty days after kiddushin, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 53. There it says thirty days from the date she demanded that the marriage take place, and that is also the intent here. Therefore, the Tur did not write “from the day of kiddushin,” but “after the kiddushin,” relying upon the fuller law in Even HaEzer. The statement that the father cannot annul alone cannot refer to a bogeres, for her father has no authority over her. It refers to a minor or na’arah who became betrothed. The bogeres was mentioned only to teach that the husband cannot annul her vows.
Before she became betrothed. This is implied by the verse, “And if she shall be to a man, and her vows are upon her.” A betrothed man may annul earlier vows together with the father. Once she is married, however, the husband cannot annul vows that preceded the marriage, for a husband does not annul earlier vows, as it is written, “If she vowed in her husband’s house.” So wrote the Rosh at the beginning of the chapter “A betrothed maiden.”
Both on the same day. The Tur writes this in the Rambam’s name. The reason is that concerning a betrothed woman the verse says, “Her husband heard on the day he heard.” The phrase “on the day he heard” cannot merely mean the day on which he happened to hear, for that is obvious. Rather, it refers back to the preceding words, “Her father heard,” and teaches that the husband must hear on the same day as the father. The alternative opinion is the Ramban’s. He holds that “on the day he heard” refers forward to, “and he was silent to her,” meaning that silence depends upon the entire day. Confirmation, however, takes effect immediately, and once one party confirms the vow the other’s annulment can no longer help, as stated in seif 6. This appears to be his reasoning. The Tur writes that the Ramban’s view is more reasonable, but the Shulchan Aruch appears to rule stringently like the Rambam, because this is a Torah doubt.
