He went, etc. Gemara, Nedarim 69a: “This is the general rule,” etc.
The father delivered her, etc. Ibid., “to include where he delivered her,” etc.; see Ran there. Likewise Ketubot 49a, concerning the teaching of the school of Rabbi Yishmael, etc.: “Since she has left,” etc.
Some say, etc. This is the Tur’s view, and Rashi also writes so in Ketubot 48b, s.v. Shmuel; see there, s.v. tiyuvta. Tosafot there, s.v. uShmuel, disagree. Ketubot 49a also implies that the rule applies specifically if she was widowed, etc. The same follows in Nedarim according to Ran, s.v. zeh haklal, who explains the delivery, etc.; however, etc. Rambam, chapter 11, writes that when the father delivered her, the husband does not annul earlier vows. This implies that he may annul vows she made after delivery, as Ran holds. Kesef Mishneh and Bedek HaBayit also write this.
