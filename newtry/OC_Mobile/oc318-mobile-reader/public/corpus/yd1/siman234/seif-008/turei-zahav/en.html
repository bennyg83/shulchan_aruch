The Beit Yosef wondered at it and wrote that the conclusion of the poskim is otherwise, namely, that the father’s delivery of her to the husband’s agents is equivalent to chuppah for annulment of vows, as in the first opinion here. It appears to me that the Tur follows the Rosh in the chapter “A maiden who was seduced,” who rules like Shmuel that delivery to the husband’s agents is effective for inheritance but not for eating terumah. The reason is that the father thereby waives his inheritance rights, as Rashi explains on Kesubos 48a. This implies that annulment of vows, which is a matter of prohibition, resembles terumah, and she does not enter the husband’s authority until chuppah. Although Tosafos wrote that inheritance is not exclusive and that delivery is effective for all other matters, as the Beit Yosef cites, the Rosh distinguishes only inheritance from terumah, implying that he meant inheritance specifically, like Rashi. According to this view, even vows she made between delivery and chuppah could not be annulled then, because she had left the father’s authority but had not yet entered the husband’s. If he annulled after chuppah, it would seemingly be an annulment of earlier vows, which a husband cannot do. This is difficult from Nedarim 89a: “This is the rule: whenever she left his authority for even one moment, he cannot annul.” The Gemara says this includes a father who delivered her to the husband’s agents, so that the husband cannot annul earlier vows. The Rosh explains that if she vowed, was divorced, and was taken back, the husband cannot annul earlier vows. This implies that the intervening divorce is the reason. Without divorce, the husband may annul vows made after the father delivered her to his agents. Therefore, according to the Tur, his inability to annul between delivery and chuppah is merely a stringency. She is nevertheless considered his wife from the moment of delivery. After chuppah, on the day he hears, he may annul even vows made between delivery and chuppah. This is not called annulment of earlier vows, because even during that earlier period she was regarded as his wife, though he should not initially annul then. This appears the proper resolution, especially since all the poskim hold that delivery to the husband’s agents is effective for annulment, as the Beit Yosef writes. The Rosh’s rulings, which the Tur himself compiled, likewise state that delivery is effective for all matters except terumah.
