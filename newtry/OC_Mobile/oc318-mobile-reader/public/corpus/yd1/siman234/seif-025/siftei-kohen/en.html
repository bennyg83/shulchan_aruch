But if he cannot hear, etc. He must at least be capable of hearing. This excludes a deaf person who cannot hear at all.
Nevertheless, if, etc. This is demonstrated in the Gemara on page 72. The Rosh concludes in his commentary that he must say, 'They shall be annulled when I hear them.' Annulment is ineffective in any other formulation. In the case of a father, the daughter must still be under his authority when he later hears. Ran also appears to hold this way.
