Without hearing. This is the Rambam’s opinion. Since the vow is at least capable of being heard, similar to the rule that anything fit for mixing is regarded as mixed, he may annul without hearing by saying, “All vows you made are annulled,” without hearing them. The Gemara posed this as an unresolved question but later discussed it by way of “if you say,” and the Rambam generally rules according to such a conditional discussion. The Gemara challenges this from seif 10, “the practice of a Torah scholar,” since there he has not heard. It answers that he annuls only to test whether a vow exists, and when she tells him he then hears and annuls. The dissenting opinion is the Rosh, who does not treat an “if you say” discussion as a ruling. The Perishah attributed the Rambam’s reasoning to the verse saying “her father heard” but not saying “her husband heard.” Some mistaken student wrote this, for the verse explicitly says, “her husband heard.” The Gemara’s question is whether that wording is exact. The Rambam explicitly equates father and husband in this law.
