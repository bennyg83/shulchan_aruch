The father died, etc. Mishnah, Nedarim 70a.
Even if, etc. This is Rambam’s wording. His intent is that the baraita on 68b states, “Her father heard,” etc., “this is what we learned,” and, “Her husband heard,” etc., “the husband cannot annul,” etc. In both clauses it says “he did not manage,” but that expression is not exact. It was used incidentally because in the two clauses where the father returns and annuls the husband’s share, Beit Hillel disagree according to Rambam, as explained above in seif 11; there “he did not manage” is indeed specific. The same wording appears in the latter clause where the father annulled, discussed below in seif 16. Proof comes from the Mishnah on 70a, “the husband died,” etc., which the Gemara establishes even where the betrothed man had heard. Likewise 68a, “When does this apply?” necessarily concerns where both heard, as explained above in seif 11; therefore the opening clause, “the father died,” is similar. Rosh and Ran, however, maintain that even in the case where the father returns and annuls, “he did not manage” is not exact, as explained in seif 16. They therefore explain every occurrence of “he heard but did not manage” in the baraita as stating a greater novelty. The Tur follows this approach. In practical law, however, there is no difference here.
Likewise, if, etc. This is Shulchan Aruch’s wording, which adopts the baraita’s phrase “he did not manage,” though it is not exact. It needed only to say: if the father annulled, or the husband alone annulled, etc.
