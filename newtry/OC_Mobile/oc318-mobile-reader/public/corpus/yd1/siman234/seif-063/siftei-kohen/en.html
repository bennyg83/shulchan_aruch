She is distressed by one, etc. For example, one loaf is fine bread and the other coarse bread. Although above in seif 61 he may annul even if she vowed from an inferior type she never tasted, there she vowed only from that type. Here she vowed simultaneously from something distressing and something not distressing. Alternatively, there she prohibited the entire type, whereas here she prohibited only these specific loaves. This latter explanation appears more correct.
And he annulled both. According to Rambam and the Mechaber above in seif 36, he need not annul both together; whatever he annulled is annulled. According to the dissenting opinion there, Tur writes that he must annul both without specification. Then the loaf that causes distress is annulled, but if he expressly mentions only the distressing loaf, the annulment fails because he annulled only part of the vow. Tur appears to derive this from Rosh, who distinguishes a case where he expressly annulled only figs from one where he annulled without specification. In truth, Rosh makes that distinction only according to Shmuel, who allows annulment even of the loaf that causes no distress. Rabbi Yochanan disagrees and holds that only the distressing loaf can be annulled. According to Rabbi Yochanan, whose view we follow, even an express annulment of only the distressing loaf should be effective. This differs from figs and grapes, because there he could annul both, making partial annulment deficient. Here he has no authority over the non-distressing loaf, so he has annulled everything within his power. Ran explicitly explains that Shmuel reads 'he shall annul it' to require annulment of the entire vow, and therefore the authority to annul the distressing part necessarily extends to the other part. Rabbi Yochanan understands that the verse requires annulment of the entire portion involving personal affliction, and that is sufficient. Rabbeinu Yerucham also simply rules that he annuls for the one distressed and not for the other. This appears primary. Moreover, Rambam, Semag, and Mechaber above in seif 36 hold that even where he annulled only figs, that portion is annulled. Rabbeinu Yerucham says this is the view of most authorities.
