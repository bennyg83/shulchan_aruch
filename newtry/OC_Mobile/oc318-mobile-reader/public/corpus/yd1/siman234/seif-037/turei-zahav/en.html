And any expression indicating that he confirmed it, etc. The Beit Yosef cites the Rosh’s reason. Since silence throughout the day of hearing itself confirms the vow, any expression at all indicating confirmation is sufficient to prevent any later annulment, even on that same day.
Uprooting the vow from its beginning. One may ask from seif 51, which states that a husband does not uproot a vow from its beginning, and this is an explicit Talmudic rule that a husband merely cuts it off. Here, the meaning is that the husband uses wording that from now onward it should be as though she had never vowed, meaning that she has permission to do as she wishes. He has power to create that prospective effect, but not to make the vow retroactively nonexistent as a sage does.
