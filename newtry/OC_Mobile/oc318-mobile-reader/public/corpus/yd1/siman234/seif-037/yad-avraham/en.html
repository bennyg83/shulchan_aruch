Regarding the matter of cherem and nidui in ancient times — whether it is from the Torah. See in responsa Noda B'Yehuda chelek 2 that he concludes that Ran also reversed himself and wrote it is not from the Torah; and the same applies to nidui, for Ramban wrote it is only d'rabbanan; therefore we follow its safek to be lenient, for d'rabbanan is no stronger than halacha le-Moshe mi-Sinai whose safek we treat leniently. (Except that he distinguishes between cherem that a court would decree and what one accepted upon himself in cherem — that is d'oraisa; and Ranach already preceded this in responsa Mayim Amukim siman 55.) And so too in responsa Tur Even HaEzer siman 35. And so is implied in Shabbos chapter 3 and Rambam chapter 6 of Avodah Zarah that we go leniently in the matter of tumah of idolatry because it is rabbinic — and it implies d'rabbanan is like divrei soferim, for tumah of idolatry is derived in the chapter of R' Akiva from d'rabbanan. But this matter is not according to Sha'agat Aryeh in his book Tori Even chapter 1 of Megillah, that d'rabbanan are like d'oraisa and we go stringently in their safek — and he brought proof from Pesachim chapter 4 that doubt regarding terumah is forbidden; and so too from Chokhmat Adam in Shabbos (14). And so it appears correct from that which is stated in Megillah "I was young" — we do not bring proof from a minor regarding reading megillah; and if it were so that d'rabbanan are like divrei soferim, why do we not bring proof from testimony of a minor? For in rabbinic matters we hold he is believed when grown regarding what he saw as a child, as stated in the second chapter of Ketubot — rather we learn that d'rabbanan are like d'oraisa and therefore we are stringent in their safek. And so too proof from that which in many places in the Talmud scripture of d'rabbanan is called d'oraisa, as in Ketubot (52), Gittin (36), Chullin (17), and so too Bava Batra (160b), Nedarim (37b), Avodah Zarah (52b), Rosh Hashana (19), and the Rambam is stringent in their safek. However this is only what is explicit in d'rabbanan explicitly, not what we learn from midrash. And that of Shabbos is only an asmakhta and not explicit in words of Kabbalah. And that which Kesef Mishneh challenged on this in Laws of Megillah from Rambam's words there chapter 3 that reading megillah is divrei soferim — behold this is explicit words of Kabbalah — I already explained in my commentary to Rambam there that reading megillah too is not explicit but from midrash of the prophets themselves who enacted it and derived it by kal vachomer: just as from slavery to freedom they sang, from death to life all the more so, as stated in Megillah (14); and it is comparable to everything derived from Torah by kal vachomer and called divrei soferim. And so everything learned through the thirteen middot is called divrei soferim, as explained in Rambam beginning of Laws of Marriage — and reading megillah derived by kal vachomer is no better than other thirteen middot. And further, there is an opinion it was not given to be written; but what was given to be written, all may say it is like d'oraisa. And further, even the song itself they said at the sea — Moshe thought of it himself, as Rashi explained in Chumash, but we do not find he was commanded; therefore song is no better than other enactments of Moshe not called d'oraisa but only enactments of Moshe, as stated at end of Megillah and chapter Merubah. Rambam similarly wrote at beginning of Laws of Mourning. If so, this reasoning suffices to be as argued, and reading megillah is no better than song of Moshe. And that Rambam called divrei soferim the enjoyment and honor of Shabbos in Laws of Shabbos — although explicit in Nevi'im — one may say nevertheless what the honor and enjoyment are was not explained except from divrei soferim:
Regarding vows or oaths whether apply through agent — e.g. told agent vow or swear on my behalf whether applies to sender. Noda BiYehudah Makkot wrote not found explicit — implies we say agent like person — else wonder why condition in oath — condition requires act fulfillable through agent — if impossible through agent condition void; or can forbid through oath through agent. Beit Meir on Even HaEzer siman 120 challenged Noda BiYehudah — missed Ramban Bava Batra plainly nezirut not through agent yet condition applies; some distinguish condition Bava Batra and Bava Metzia only between person and fellow not between person and God — always condition; therefore vows and oaths not through agent; reason if because agent for opinion — Tosafot first chapter Bava Metzia wrote regarding agent for opinion agency void; or reason vow and oath not through agent because written your lips you shall keep and uttering lips — like commandment on body like laying tefillin not through agent; Hagahot Mordechai Ketubot — vow impossible through agent; answered there vow has condition from another reason — not need resemble Bava Batra and Bava Metzia only where act — unlike vow impossible speech alone voided by condition though impossible through agent — summary Beit Meir. Noda BiYehudah second edition Choshen Mishpat siman 247 noted himself Ramban and proved Tosafot first Nazir not through agent — seems main not Noda BiYehudah's son who brought proof against Tosafot and Ramban — proved exist through agent from Shevuot page 34 side 2 Rabbi Shimon finished oath deposit from trespass finished testimony — answer reasonable from trespass should learn deposit; if say told agent go swear on my behalf oath not apply to obligate sender in sacrifice — then one dispute oath deposit to trespass — trespass through agent obligates sender sacrifice — oath deposit not through agent obligate sender — still difficult Rabbi Shimon should learn deposit from testimony; or also oath prohibition through agent — end quote; in my humble opinion.
