A person annuls or upholds his wife's and daughter's vows in any language, even if it is not explicit. (And any language he says that indicates he upheld for her, even if he did so in order to distress her, is upholding) (Beit Yosef in the name of responsa of Rashba and Maharik shoresh 50; except that he brings proof there from Nedarim 87a regarding those sentenced to death, that matters of the heart are not [binding] matters; and in my opinion it is simple thus from above seif 21, and it is the Gemara at the end of the chapter Na'arah HaMe'orasah, and see Eben HaEzer siman 81). And how does he annul? He says three times "annulled," or "void," or "this vow is nothing," and similar matters whose content is uprooting the vow from its root, whether in her presence or not in her presence; but if he said to her "I do not want you to vow" or "there is no vow here" — behold he did not annul. And similarly one who says to his wife or daughter "forgiven for you," or "permitted for you," or "released for you," and anything similar in this matter — he said nothing. And some dispute and say that one who says "this vow is nothing" is not an annulment. (Rosh and Tur)
