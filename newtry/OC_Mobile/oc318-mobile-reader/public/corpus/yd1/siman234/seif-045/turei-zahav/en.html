“It is confirmed for you today.” The Gemara asks whether this is equivalent to saying, “It shall be annulled for you tomorrow,” or whether he did not say that. If one concludes that he did not, it is confirmation. The Gemara then asks about, “It shall be annulled for you tomorrow.” Perhaps he cannot annul tomorrow because he confirmed it today, or perhaps, since he did not explicitly say “confirmed today and annulled tomorrow,” his words mean that the annulment should begin today. If one concludes that confirmation today makes the vow existent tomorrow and tomorrow is no longer a time for annulment, the Gemara asks about, “It is confirmed for you for one hour.” Perhaps this is like saying, “It shall be annulled after one hour,” or perhaps he did not say that. If one concludes that he did not, it is confirmation. It then asks where he explicitly says, “It shall be annulled for you after one hour.” Perhaps confirmation for one hour is confirmation forever, or perhaps, since the entire day is a time both for confirmation and annulment, the later annulment is effective. The question remains unresolved, and we rule stringently that all these statements constitute confirmation. The Tur omits the third question, “confirmed for one hour,” because the fourth case explicitly says, “and annulled after one hour,” and still does not help, all the more so if he did not say the second phrase. My revered father-in-law wrote that the third case should be inserted in the Tur, but that is not precise. The Rambam counts the third question because he distinguishes the first three from the fourth. In the first three he rules that it is confirmation, not merely because of doubt, but because the Gemara’s successive “if you say” discussions treat them as resolved. The final question remains truly unresolved, so he writes it as a doubt. He therefore needed to mention the third case to teach that it is not doubtful like the fourth. This is straightforward.
