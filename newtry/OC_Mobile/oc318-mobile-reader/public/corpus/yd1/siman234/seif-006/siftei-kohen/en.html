If one of them upheld it, etc. Beit Yosef cites Rashba that this applies specifically if the father upheld it, but not if the husband did. Darchei Moshe cites him. This does not appear from the general language of the authorities, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch. Rashba was forced to this distinction by the law of one who betroths a woman on condition that she has no vows, where if vows are found and a sage releases them, the kiddushin is valid, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 39, seif 2. Ran already resolves this in Nedarim 67a. See there.
Even if he later sought release, etc. It appears to me that even if he sought release from his confirmation on the very day both heard the vow, he can no longer annul it.
