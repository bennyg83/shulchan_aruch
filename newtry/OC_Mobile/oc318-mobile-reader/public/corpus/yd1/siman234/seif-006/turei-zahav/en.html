And he sought release from the confirmation. Although seif 23 explains that release from confirmation is effective, that concerns a married woman, whose vow depends solely upon her husband. Here, with a betrothed woman, annulment requires the joint action of father and betrothed man, and no confirmation may interrupt between their annulments. Once there was even one moment when the second annulment could not be effective, namely after the first party confirmed the vow, it can never again become effective, even if that confirmation is later released. The Beit Yosef cites the Rashba’s difficulty from a man who betroths a woman on condition that she has no vows. If vows are found and she goes to a sage who releases them, she is betrothed because the vows are uprooted retroactively. Here too, the confirmation should be uprooted as though it never existed. The Beit Yosef was forced to explain that the statement, “one of them confirmed,” refers specifically to the father, but he himself writes that this is insufficient, and the poskim made no such distinction. The Ran on 67a resolves the question. When the first party annulled, the remaining half became weakened. Once that remaining half was ineffective even for one moment, it can no longer join with the earlier annulment.
