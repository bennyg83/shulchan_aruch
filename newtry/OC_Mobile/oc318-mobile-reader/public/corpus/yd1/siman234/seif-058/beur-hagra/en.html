Some say, etc. Ran and Rosh at the beginning of chapter 11, s.v. ve’elu, etc., write that there is, etc. See Ran 68a, s.v. lomar, etc. Rosh rules likewise at the beginning of chapter 11.
There are those who say, etc. They infer this from the statements on 79b, which speak throughout of a husband, and from 68a, “to teach that the husband,” etc. This is not a valid distinction, however, because the primary verse concerning self-affliction is written regarding the husband, and therefore the teaching speaks of the husband. The Sifrei likewise first derives the rule for the husband and afterward derives all the father’s laws from “These are the statutes.” Indeed, the school of Rabbi Yishmael derives it from “between a man and his wife, between,” etc. One cannot reject the explicit statements of the Sifrei and Yerushalmi.
There is an authority who says, etc. This was proposed to reconcile Rambam’s words, but it is untenable, for neither the Sifrei nor the Yerushalmi implies this. Nedarim 70b also says, “It compares,” etc. Perhaps this authority inferred from the Sifrei’s statement: “I know only a husband, who annuls only vows between him and her and vows involving self-affliction; from where do we know the father?” etc. “The husband is different, for the authority does not revert to him,” etc.; “can you say the same of the father, to whom authority reverts?” Yet that passage actually implies the opposite.
