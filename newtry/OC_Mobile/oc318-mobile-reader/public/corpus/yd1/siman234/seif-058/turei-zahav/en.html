That the father also, etc. The Sifrei derives this from, “between a man and his wife, between a father and his daughter,” comparing the father to the husband. The permissive opinion is the Rambam, who writes that we do not rule according to this Sifrei because neither the Bavli nor Yerushalmi mentions it, and the plain verse implies that only a husband is restricted to vows of self-affliction, whereas a father may annul other vows too. The dissenting opinion is Rabbeinu Yechiel in the Tur. He reconciles the Talmud with the Sifrei by explaining that the Sifrei concerns after she became betrothed. At that time the father may annul together with the husband only vows of self-affliction. Therefore, even if she later becomes divorced and returns to his authority, he cannot annul something he previously lacked power to annul. It appears obvious that matters between husband and wife have the same law as self-affliction regarding the father as well. The Sifrei compares father to husband, and a comparison is not partial. This differs from the Perishah, who says only self-affliction is included and labors to explain why. Rabbeinu Yechiel mentions self-affliction because he comments on the opening Mishnah of the final chapter of Nedarim, which likewise mentions only self-affliction regarding the husband, though the same law applies to matters between them. The Rosh writes this explicitly there.
