She vowed while betrothed and was divorced. She returns to her father's authority, and the father alone may annul even vows made during betrothal, as when the betrothed husband dies, above at the beginning of seif 11. Therefore, if she is betrothed again, the new betrothed husband may annul even vows made before the new betrothal, together with the father, as above in seif 5. Tur writes that if she was delivered for marriage, vowed, the husband did not hear, divorced her, and then betrothed her again, he cannot annul the original vow because she once entered his authority. Beit Yosef argues that he cannot annul even a new vow, because delivery is like nisuin. Her father therefore has no further authority, and a betrothed husband can never annul alone. This resembles divorce after nisuin below in seif 14. According to this, if she is delivered again for marriage, the husband could annul a vow made after the second delivery, because delivery is like nisuin. One should not rely on this leniency, however, since Tur and Bach disagree, and the Mechaber's own language above in seif 8 indicates stringency. Darchei Moshe and Perishah cite Beit Yosef without objection. All this applies where the husband did not hear before the divorce. If he heard before divorce, the divorce is equivalent to confirmation, as below in seif 20.
