He must intend the particular woman. This is derived from the word “her,” specifically her.
Regarding what is written, “for so they told him,” the Gemara on 60a asks whether “her” must be exact. Regarding tearing one’s garment, Scripture says, “for Shaul and for Yonasan his son,” yet a Baraisa teaches that if they told him his father died, he tore, and it was later discovered that his son died, he fulfilled the obligation. The Gemara answers that one case was unspecified and one explicit. According to Rashi and Tosafos, unspecified means that he did not intend either his son or father, and likewise with annulment he intended no particular woman, but whoever had vowed. Explicit means that he annulled specifically for his daughter, thinking it was she. The Ran and Rosh explain that even in the unspecified case he thought it was his daughter, but did not state orally that he acted for his daughter. The Tur’s language here follows Rashi and Tosafos. In the following case he writes merely that he was told, “A woman in your house vowed,” and he annulled, implying he did not think it was his daughter. Likewise, in siman 340 regarding tearing, he equates one who was told his father died and tore with one who was told merely that someone died, thought it was his father, and discovered it was his son. If the discovery came after the time of immediate speech, he did not fulfill the obligation. At first glance, this is difficult, for the Tur departs from the Rosh. It appears that after the Gemara first distinguishes between unspecified and explicit, Rav Ashi gives another answer, distinguishing within and after the time of immediate speech. The Ran says Rav Ashi does not dispute the first answer, while others say he does, as the Beit Yosef cites from the Rif and Rosh in siman 340. The Tur therefore ruled that according to those who say Rav Ashi disputes, his dispute applies only if “unspecified” is interpreted like the Rosh, where he still thought it was his daughter. Rav Ashi would reject that. If “unspecified” is interpreted like Rashi and Tosafos, meaning he intended whoever had vowed, that distinction remains reasonable and Rav Ashi certainly does not dispute it. Thus, the Tur does not actually disagree with the Rosh; the Rosh follows his view that Rav Ashi rejects the first distinction. The Rama here, however, appears to follow the Rosh, making the law depend specifically upon explicit oral identification, rather than the Tur. He may have ruled this way because siman 340, seif 24, likewise makes it depend upon speech. As practical halacha, one should distinguish. Annulment is Torah law, so we should be stringent that it is ineffective even without oral identification if he was mistaken and thought it was his daughter. It is effective only where he annulled without specifying any person, for whoever had vowed. Tearing is rabbinic, so one may rely on the lenient ruling stated there.
