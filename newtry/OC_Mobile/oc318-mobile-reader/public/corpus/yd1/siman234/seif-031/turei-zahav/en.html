And what is written: "for so they said to him" — in the Talmud (Nedarim 60) it is stated: does this imply he revokes only for her specifically? But regarding tearing [kriyah] — as it is written "for Saul and for Jonathan his son," and we taught: if they told him "your father died" and he tore, and it was then found it was his son — he has fulfilled his obligation for tearing. There is no contradiction: one case is stam [unspecified] and the other is mefurash [explicitly stated]. "Stam" means he tore for whoever it was; and "mefurash" in the case of revocation means he explicitly revoked for his daughter but thought she was his daughter — so explain Rashi and Tosafot there. But Ran and Rosh explain that even in the stam case he assumed it was his daughter, only he did not explicitly state in words that he was revoking for his daughter. And from the Tur's words here it is clear like Rashi and Tosafot — and therefore he did not write afterward regarding stam that he thought it was his daughter; rather he wrote: "if they told him a woman in your house vowed stam and he revoked" — it is a valid revocation; implying he did not think it was his daughter. Likewise below in siman 340 regarding tearing he compared them: "they told him 'your father died' and he tore," or "they told him in general and he thought it was his father but it turned out to be his son" — he has not fulfilled his obligation if it was after the time of speech; and this corresponds to what is written here. At first glance it is difficult to understand why he wrote against his father's [the Rosh's] view. It seems to me his intent is: since after the Talmud's answer "one is stam, one is mefurash," the Talmud further presents Rav Ashi's answer "here it is within the time of speech, there it is after the time of speech" — and Ran explains that Rav Ashi is not coming to dispute the first answer; while others explain that Rav Ashi disputes the stam/mefurash distinction, as Beit Yosef cited Rif and Rosh in siman 340. Therefore the Tur ruled: according to those who say Rav Ashi disputes — that would mean, per the Rosh's explanation that in stam too he assumed it was his daughter but did not say so — Rav Ashi would not agree with that leniency; but if we interpret stam according to Rashi and Tosafot as intending "whoever it may be" — this is reasonable and Rav Ashi certainly does not dispute it. Hence the Tur does not disagree with the Rosh — for the Rosh follows his reasoning that Rav Ashi disputes the stam/mefurash distinction; the Tur goes according to Rashi and Tosafot. However from Rema's words here it appears he follows the Rosh's explanation that the matter depends on explicit statement — not like the Tur. It seems he ruled thus because below in siman 340 seif 24 the Shulchan Arukh ruled that the matter depends on the statement. And it appears one should distinguish for halacha: regarding revocation which is biblical, one should be stringent — there is no valid revocation without explicit statement unless he was mistaken that she was his daughter; that is comparable to mefurash. But regarding tearing which is rabbinic, one can rely on the leniency as the Shulchan Arukh ruled below. So it seems to me.
