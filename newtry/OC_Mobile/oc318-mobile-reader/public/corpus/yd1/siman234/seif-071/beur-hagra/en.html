Specifically if she said, etc. Rosh writes this in his rulings there and in Ketubot, based on the Gemara’s statement: “Rather, Rav Huna said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, where she says,” etc. Rambam writes that specifically regarding consecration she must say, “Let them become consecrated,” etc., but with a konam this is unnecessary. Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer 81:2, rules likewise. Rambam’s reason is that once the Gemara concludes, “Konamot are different,” etc., the first answer, requiring that she say, etc., is no longer necessary. Although one cannot consecrate to the Temple treasury something not yet in existence, as Rambam writes in the laws of acquisition, one may consecrate an object with inherent sanctity, as shown at the beginning of chapter 5 of Temurah, 24b, concerning an animal about to give birth, etc., and 25a, where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that offspring, etc. Even so, he agrees in the first clause, though an embryo is not yet fully in existence, as stated in Kiddushin 62b and Bava Batra 142b. Nevertheless, this applies specifically when the animal is already pregnant, as Kiddushin asks: “According to whom does this accord?” etc. This is the meaning of, “Konamot are different,” etc. Rosh and Tur, however, maintain that this answer resolves only the difficulty that her hands are pledged to her husband; because the work is not yet in existence, she must still say, “Let them become consecrated,” etc. See Ran there, s.v. amar Rav Ashi. This also appears from Yevamot 93a and Kiddushin 63a: “It was stated concerning it,” etc., implying that this remains the conclusion. Tosafot in Kiddushin there, s.v. veyadayim, however, write, “the same applies,” etc., in accordance with Rambam. Rambam’s ruling nevertheless requires study, for if she merely says, “Let them become consecrated,” without using a konam, the Gemara’s difficulty remains: her hands are pledged to her husband.
Let them become consecrated, etc. She need not add, “when I am divorced,” because once the Gemara answers, “Konamot are different,” it resolves the difficulty that her hands are pledged, etc. See Beit Yosef and Tosafot in Nedarim there.
