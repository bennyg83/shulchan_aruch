'Konam, what I produce for you,' etc. Tosafot in Ketubot 59a write that even if the text reads 'what I do not produce,' it is not an intangible matter, citing the Mishnah, 'Konam, I will not place straw before your cattle,' which a husband cannot annul. This requires study. Nedarim teaches that 'Konam, my mouth speaking with you, my hands working with you, my feet walking with you' is intangible unless interpreted as, 'My mouth is forbidden for its speech, my hands for their work, and my feet for their walking.' Rosh and Ran explain that the words are treated as such an expression, and Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule accordingly in siman 213. That interpretation works where she mentions her mouth or hands, because the vow can grammatically refer to the body part and unspecified vows are interpreted stringently. But when she says only, 'Konam, what I produce for you,' she did not mention her hands. Indeed, the Gemara in Ketubot later resolves the case by saying that she declared her hands consecrated to their work, implying that before this resolution we did not treat her words as though she had said her hands were forbidden. Even if the wording is 'Konam, what I produce for you,' without 'I will not,' the same difficulty remains. Tur and Shulchan Aruch also write that if one says, 'My speech, my work, or my walking is forbidden to you,' the vow is ineffective because these are intangible. The case of placing straw presents no difficulty, because an intangible vow still takes effect rabbinically, as explained in the second chapter of Nedarim and above in siman 213. It appears that the present case likewise concerns rabbinic effect. According to the Gemara's conclusion that she said her hands are consecrated to their work, as Rama states, it takes effect by Torah law. This appears correct, though Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Nedarim requires study.
Specifically when she said, 'My hands shall be consecrated to their work.' Her hands already exist. Otherwise she cannot prohibit to him something that has not yet come into existence. See the end of siman 204.
'My hands shall be consecrated,' etc. This does not mean that she fully consecrates the products of her hands, for then the Mishnah should say, 'What I produce shall be consecrated,' rather than, 'Konam, what I produce for you.' It means that she said, 'My hands shall be consecrated regarding what they produce for you,' meaning that her hands shall be forbidden to him like consecrated property. Beit Yosef.
