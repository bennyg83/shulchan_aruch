That it is harmful to her. It appears to mean that it harms her health, although she desires to eat it. A similar expression appears regarding an animal in Bava Kamma 20a: If it ate wheat or something harmful to it, the owner is exempt. There too it refers to health. If the food merely appears undesirable to her because she has no desire for it, why should abstaining be called self-affliction? Therefore, seif 63 says, “and she suffers no distress from the second.” The Beit Yosef strained to reconcile that passage with this one in two ways. According to what I have written, there is no difficulty.
And she had not tasted, etc. The Rambam’s text reads, “or she had not tasted,” and this is also implied by Tosafos cited by the Beit Yosef. The wording here, “and she had not tasted,” may likewise be interpreted as “or she had not tasted.”
