A husband, etc. This follows from Ketubot 71a, where the Gemara establishes the case as, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. shenadrah: “he should not seek,” etc. This is certainly true where the husband merely remained silent, as the Gemara states there: “Was it not taught: a woman,” etc., “and he did not annul,” etc.; “if he said,” etc. See also 71b: “But here, where she vowed,” etc. Rashi in Yevamot 25b, s.v. beneder, writes similarly. Raem likewise explains the statement at the beginning of chapter 10 of Nedarim, “There is release from confirmation,” as referring to such a case. The Yerushalmi also accords with Raem’s explanation. See Hagahot Mordekhai at the end of chapter 3 of Shabbat.
Some say, etc. They infer this from Shabbat 46b, where Rabbi Chiya bar Ashi challenges from the law that vows may be annulled, etc., and the Gemara does not answer that three ordinary persons suffice, as it answers nearby. See Hagahot Mordekhai there. The Sifrei expounds: “Every vow and every oath,” etc.; Scripture says, “to afflict the soul.” Just as a vow of self-affliction is not released by others but is annulled by the husband, so too matters between husband and wife. Mordekhai, Gittin chapter 4, cites this in the name of Ri. Ketubot there says, “He puts a finger between her teeth,” etc.; if she could obtain release, what did he cause? The same argument shows that she cannot obtain release. Maharam of Rothenburg, responsum 18, discusses this at great length. Maharik writes: “I saw my teachers permit release after the husband’s silence, in accordance with the lenient authorities. Perhaps one can distinguish between silence and explicit confirmation. Therefore the proper practice is that the husband first obtain release from his confirmation,” etc. This is the basis of the wording here: “Some say,” etc.; “but,” etc.; “and it is proper,” etc.
