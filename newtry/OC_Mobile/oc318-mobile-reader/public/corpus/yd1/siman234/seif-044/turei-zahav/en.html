“It is confirmed and annulled for you.” This is the correct reading in the Tur as well, not the versions that read, “It is confirmed for you and annulled for you.” The Rosh writes that since he said “for you” only once, the two statements were simultaneous. The Ran writes that according to the Gemara’s version with “for you” twice, one must explain that he expressly said, “It is confirmed for you and annulled for you simultaneously,” using inclusive language to combine them. The Tur says only that he said the words simultaneously. Unless “for you” appears once, there is no language combining them. The Gemara leaves the question unresolved. The Rosh explains that perhaps we follow the first phrase, or perhaps both take effect together and we cannot determine whether it was confirmed or annulled. The Gemara resolves from Rabbah’s principle: anything that cannot take effect successively cannot take effect simultaneously. One who betroths two sisters at once betroths neither, since he could not betroth them one after another. So too, since annulment cannot follow confirmation, neither takes effect simultaneously. This implies that there is neither annulment nor confirmation, and he may still choose either. We do not rule stringently that it is confirmed, for the comparison to Rabbah’s case nullifies the words entirely. The Perishah likewise writes that he may do as he wishes. This appears the Tur’s view. The Rambam, however, rules that it is confirmed. He may hold this case stronger than simultaneous kiddushin of sisters, because he first said “confirmed” and then “annulled,” while “for you” applies to both. The confirmation therefore certainly does not disappear. The question is only whether the later “annulled” can cancel it, and the Gemara’s principle proves that it cannot. In the sisters’ case, “betrothed” is plural and applies equally to both. Here “for you” is not plural so as to place both statements fully together. The comparison is only for the principle that whatever cannot take effect successively cannot take effect simultaneously. This resolves the Beit Yosef’s difficulty with the Rambam.
