Or he said to her, 'It is confirmed for you and annulled for you simultaneously.' This is his wording, and he intends both to take effect at once.
There is neither confirmation, etc. He may afterward do as he wishes, either confirm or annul. Ateret Zekenim writes that although above in seif 41 one who confirmed in his heart may retract and annul within the brief time of a greeting, here he verbalized the confirmation. This explanation is unconvincing, because the rule that a statement made within that brief interval is treated as part of the original statement applies even to spoken words, except in kiddushin, divorce, blasphemy, and accepting an idol as a deity. Rather, here he does not intend to retract his first words, 'It is confirmed for you.' He says instead, 'It is confirmed for you and also annulled for you.'
