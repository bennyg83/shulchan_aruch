He married her. Meaning, he completed nisuin with her. Here, according to all opinions, she does not return to her father's authority, even regarding vows she makes after the husband's death, and she is under her own authority.
Or he delivered her to him, etc. Tur writes that she does not return to her father's authority except regarding vows made after the husband's death. Beit Yosef challenges this, arguing that even according to the dissenting opinion above in seif 8, delivery merely prevents the husband from annulling, but once she has left her father's authority there is no reason for her ever to return to it, even for later vows. Bach challenges Beit Yosef, but the plain meaning of Ketubot 49a supports Beit Yosef, as do Tosafot and Rashi there. Delivery resembles nisuin. The verse stating that the vow of a widow or divorcee shall stand refers to a vow made while she is widowed, and Rabbi Yishmael's baraita addresses that case. This is also evident from Rava's argument there concerning stoning and terumah. See there carefully.
