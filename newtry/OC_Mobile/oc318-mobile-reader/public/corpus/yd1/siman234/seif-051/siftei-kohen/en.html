And another person attached himself to her vow. This means that another person said, 'And I am like her.' See above, siman 229, seif 3. Bach objects that above in seif 37 the Mechaber follows Rambam's language that a husband's annulment uproots the vow from its inception, while here he follows Tur's language that a father and husband do not uproot it from its inception as a sage does. There is no contradiction. The Gemara asks whether a husband uproots the vow or merely cuts it off, specifically regarding another who attached himself to it, and concludes that he cuts it off. Regarding a sage, the Mishnah and Gemara plainly establish that he uproots it. Rambam himself rules both laws. Kesef Mishneh explains that when Rambam says a husband uproots the vow, he means that the vow took effect and the husband now removes and nullifies it, unlike merely declaring that he does not want her vow or that there is no vow. The Gemara's question is whether the annulment makes it as though the vow never existed, or whether it removes it only from now on after it took effect. The latter is called cutting it off. Thus, the statement here that a husband does not uproot like a sage means that although he removes it, he does not erase it retroactively as a sage does.
