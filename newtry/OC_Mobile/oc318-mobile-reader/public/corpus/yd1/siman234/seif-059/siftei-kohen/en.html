And some say, etc. Bach claims this is a scribal error and should read, 'Some say that cosmetics and adornment, etc.,' because no one holds that abstaining from bathing is not personal affliction. It appears, however, that it is not an error, as Ateret Zekenim also writes. It is Ramban's view as cited by Beit Yosef and mentioned by Rosh. Yet Beit Yosef himself appears to have intended Rosh's statement at the beginning of the final chapter of Nedarim that Ramban holds adornment and relations are matters between husband and wife and perhaps rules like Rabbi Yose. There 'Ramban' must be a scribal error for 'Rambam,' because immediately beforehand Rosh states that Ramban rules like the Sages and proves it at length. Ramban therefore clearly holds that bathing is personal affliction, as is also explicit in Beit Yosef and Tosafot Yom Tov. Rosh likewise implies this. The matter requires study.
