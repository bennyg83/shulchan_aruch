Which vows, etc. Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 11.
Applying eye paint and adornment. Ibid., 81b, following the Sages.
For example, etc. The Gemara’s conclusion there, 80b.
Even if she did not, etc. Ibid., 81a. Although this point is unnecessary according to the conclusion, as Ran writes on 81b, s.v. ela, the Gemara did not retract it; rather, a second difficulty was raised, etc.
Some say, etc. This is because the entire chapter follows Rabbi Yosi. The first view, however, is that of Ramban and Rosh, who rule like the Sages. See Ran 81a, s.v. ela lav, and 82b, s.v. vekeivan. In Rosh’s rulings the text says, “Rambam, of blessed memory, writes, and the halachah follows the Sages,” etc.; it should read “Ramban” with a final nun. Later, where it says, “and it is therefore reasonable that the halachah follows the Sages, but Ramban,” etc., it should read “Rambam” with a final mem.
For bathing, etc. This is the correct reading. Although Rambam’s text says “eye paint and adornment,” that is a scribal error, contrary to Beit Yosef, for Nedarim 81a explicitly says that even bathing is not self-affliction according to Rabbi Yosi.
All this, etc. Ran 79a, s.v. im etkashet, etc., writes, “and so it is stated,” etc. His meaning is that Ketubot 71b establishes the case as involving matters, etc., implying that according to all opinions these are matters between husband and wife. Tosafot there, s.v. hachi garsinan, however, reject that reading; see there. This requires study, because Nedarim 81a raises the question, etc., and Rav Huna says that he cannot annul unless, etc., showing that they disagree in such a case. See Rosh there, s.v. peirot, etc. Although Ran explains the dispute as concerning bathing, the matter still requires study.
