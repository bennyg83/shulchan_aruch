The benefit of your marital relations is forbidden to me. One may not feed a person something forbidden to him. This applies specifically if she says, “Your body is forbidden to me for the benefit of relations.” Otherwise, the vow does not take effect because it concerns something without tangible substance. This is the Rashba’s opinion. Tosafos, however, writes that even if she did not say so explicitly, we interpret the wording stringently as though she said, “Your body,” etc. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the Rivash’s name. The Shulchan Aruch’s language implies the stringent view of Tosafos.
Not to come near his wife. This means that he used wording in which the vow would ordinarily not take effect because he is obligated to her, as stated in the preceding seif. Here, however, she waived the obligation, and the vow therefore remains effective.
