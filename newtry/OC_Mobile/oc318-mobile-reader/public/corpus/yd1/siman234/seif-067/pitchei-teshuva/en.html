To annul. Perishah and Shach allow annulment because the vow may later take effect after divorce. Taz distinguishes marital relations, where the vow never presently takes effect, from her earnings, where it can take effect after divorce. See Bnei Ahuvah.
With his wife's consent, the vow takes effect. Bnei Ahuvah explains that her consent may validate a konam because rabbinic reinforcement of marital obligations applies only when she objects. He questions whether the same can apply to an oath, especially one of permanent abstention, because an oath does not create sanctity in the object.

Ra'anach infers that if her protest truly concerned deprivation of relations, the oath would not take effect because she could retract her permission. He also suggests that permission is not final until the husband accepts it; if she retracts before his oath, it is as though she never consented.
