The father does not annul his daughter's vows and the husband does not annul his wife's vows except on the day he hears them; and specifically the entire day and not hour-for-hour — that if he heard at the beginning of the night he annuls the entire night and the following day; and if he heard near nightfall he annuls only until it becomes dark. And if the day passed and he was silent, even if he did not intend in his silence except for her distress, he cannot annul. And if he was silent because he does not know that the husband has [the power] to annul, or even if he knows but she vowed a vow that he thinks he cannot annul, such as if he thinks it is not inuy nefesh — he can annul even after many days, and his silence is not upholding; and the hour of his knowledge is as if it were the hour of the vow or the day of hearing, and he annuls for her that entire day.
