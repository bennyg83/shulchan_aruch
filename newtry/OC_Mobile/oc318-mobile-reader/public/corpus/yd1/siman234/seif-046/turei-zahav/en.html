It is not annulled. This follows the conditional conclusion mentioned above. The Rama’s gloss, “and there are those who say it is confirmed,” is extremely puzzling. The first opinion likewise holds that it is confirmed. “It is not annulled” cannot mean that he may still annul it again later that day, for the Gemara explicitly says that if he confirmed it today it is considered existent, and no later annulment can help. The Ran and Rosh state this explicitly. In what, then, does the second opinion, the Tur, disagree with the first, the Rambam? The Beit Yosef explicitly writes that the Rambam considers all these cases confirmation. The Rama was not precise.
