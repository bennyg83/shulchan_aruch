Whether the mother. In the Gemara they say: oto v'et beno—I have only it and its son; whence do I include its son and it? It says "you shall not slaughter"—behold two. Rashi: he warned both, whether he slaughtered the mother or the daughter; and you do not find two performing one transgression except with three animals. How so? Daughter, mother, and daughter—for if mother and two sons, why do I need a verse—it is obvious; whether one or two, as this one is liable so that one is liable; and if cow and its daughter and daughter's daughter—it is obvious both are oto v'et beno; rather necessarily one slaughters the cow and the two slaughter mother and the third daughter—and it teaches us that for its son and it one is also liable—that is the second.
