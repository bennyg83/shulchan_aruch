(Siman 16 seif 4) And if he slaughtered the first at bein hashemashos, etc., until night of sixth. But at bein hashemashos of night of sixth he may not slaughter—for perhaps when he slaughtered the first at bein hashemashos it was then night and now still day; and truly it was hard for me why Tosafot conclude in Shabbat (daf 34b) all bein hashemashos are equal, only the doubt is perhaps all bein hashemashos is day or all night or part of first from day and part of second from night—if so if he slaughtered at bein hashemashos of night of fifth at beginning of bein hashemashos, he should be permitted to slaughter at bein hashemashos of tomorrow's day at its end—for if yesterday was night, all the more now at end of bein hashemashos which is night; and if now day, all the more yesterday at beginning of bein hashemashos was day; and I found in Ezrat Kohanim who was challenged on this by Turei Zahav and strained: truly only because of general stringency they forbade; and in sugya Shabbat where they say we cast it to stringency of two days—for what halachah—to tumah, why not state practical difference plainly for oto v'et beno: if doubt were only whether all from day or all from night, if slaughtered at bein hashemashos permitted to slaughter its offspring at bein hashemashos of morrow in any case, or both day or both night; but because we cast to stringency of two days lest part day part night—forbidden to slaughter offspring, lest when he slaughtered that one it was night and when slaughtering offspring it is day; and likewise practical difference in her vow at bein hashemashos husband cannot annul, lest at time of vow it was day and at annulment night and not "on the day he heard"; and even though immediately after vow he can annul since he could not annul before, it is like day he heard—nevertheless if he waited a bit he cannot annul afterward; and truly in responsum (siman 45) I wrote one may say he can annul from double doubt: perhaps when she vowed it was night; and if you say day, perhaps at annulment time it is day; and even though vows are matter that will be clarified and double doubt forbidden—nevertheless in husband's annulment which is against her will (he has no power that she release through sage and it is not called matter that will be clarified); however accordingly it is difficult here for oto v'et beno why not slaughter until night of sixth per Mechaber in Orach Chaim (siman 496) double doubt permitted even in matter that will be clarified; and see Shach (siman 110 s.k. 55)—if so he should be permitted to slaughter at bein hashemashos of night of sixth from double doubt: perhaps when he slaughtered mother it was day; and if you say night, perhaps also now when slaughtering offspring it is night—and requires study.
