That day. And Shach wrote: need not ask afterward—for much doubt: perhaps no mother; and even if it has a mother, perhaps not bought for shechitah; and even if for shechitah, perhaps from here and after several days.
That day. Shach's explanation: that he knows with certainty.
Of the festival. And Shach wrote: but not erev the first Yom Tov of the festival—for then people are busy with sukkah and lulav and have no leisure for so much shechitah.
To inform him. Meaning: neither for prohibition nor mistaken transaction—for possible he will slaughter them before these four seasons.
One day. And Shach wrote: it is also mistaken transaction if he did not inform; and in our time main shechitah for Yom Tov is before Yom Tov; and groom and bride slaughter a day or two before—need not inform in sale over two days; so Bach ruled.
Right in his hand. And Taz wrote: we do not learn from this if one swore not to slaughter his animal but keep for plowing and sold—even buyer cannot slaughter; this is not so—for we hold in Choshen Mishpat siman 208 if he swore not to sell and sold, it is a sale, not as Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 230; rather here it deals with annulling another's right the first already acquired; but on the object nothing applies even though his he may forbid upon another when it leaves his possession—see below siman 217 seif 8: prohibition applies to object, unlike oath on person who swore.
And successful. And Taz wrote: if they bought from one and second preceded and slaughtered—not called alert and successful; but l'chatchila forbidden to do so; and even a sinner is called so—for he stole first buyer's right; and this is Gemara Chullin daf 84—and requires study; and see Knesset HaGedolah and Shach.
