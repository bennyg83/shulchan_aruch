And if it is one of the seasons, etc. According to this reading: it is one of these seasons that the seller must warn even without specification if the first buyer did not inform explicitly he wants to slaughter today. In Tur it is written "if it is one of," without vav—automatically also in one of these seasons he need not warn unless the first buyer said explicitly he wants to slaughter today; and this is astonishing—for then why "one of the seasons"; and I saw they enter with strain for this; and to me it appears what the Tur wrote "or sold her to another to slaughter today" means: today he sold her for slaughter—that is, to exclude if he sold for plowing; it turns out he sold to him without specification for slaughter; nevertheless since the sale was made today he will certainly slaughter her today if it is one of the seasons—it turns out for the law everything goes in the buyer's possession; therefore it appears correct.
And erev the last Yom Tov. Because it counts as a festival by itself and was dear to them and they increase joy; and on the first Yom Tov they are busy with sukkah and lulav mitzvot; and erev Yom Kippur they were accustomed to fowl and fish.
Only the right in his hand. Ostensibly one could learn from this: if one swore not to slaughter his animal but only keep it for plowing and sold it—even the buyer cannot slaughter, for the seller had no right to slaughter and can sell only the right in his hand; and this is not so at all—for then it would be difficult from here on one who swore not to sell something and transgressed and sold, for we say in Choshen Mishpat siman 208 it is a sale and not as Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 230; and I wrote from this also below siman 228 seif 35. Rather it appears they did not say here he cannot sell only his right, but not to annul another's right in what he already acquired; but if there is no annulling another's right, the buyer has no obligation to fulfill the seller's oath—for as long as it was in his possession the oath applied, not when it came to another; and even though a person can forbid his own thing upon another—even when it leaves his possession, as Tur Yoreh Deah siman 217—there it is different, for the prohibition applies to the object and remains on it forever, unlike an oath that applies to the person who swore, as explained in Laws of Vows—therefore there is no obligation except while in his possession; but on the object nothing applies, as explained.
Alert and successful. Rashi explains: alert—for he did not do a prohibition; successful—that he will eat meat; and it appears to me since the Sages praised and called him alert, it implies there is virtue in one who preceded and slaughtered—that is, he cannot come to prohibition, unlike the second who might forget and do a prohibition and slaughter; therefore the court should not involve itself and say they should cast lots who slaughters first—for they have no right to annul each one's right to be called alert—that is, whoever each may slaughter; unlike when they bought from one and the second preceded and slaughtered before the first buyer—for he is not called alert but even a sinner is called so, for he stole the first buyer's right—as explained clearly.
