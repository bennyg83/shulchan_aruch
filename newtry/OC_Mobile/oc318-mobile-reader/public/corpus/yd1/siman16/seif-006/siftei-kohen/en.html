But the seller must warn the buyer. Even on other days that are not the four seasons if he slaughtered, etc.
Or if he sold her to another to slaughter her that day. Meaning: that he knows with certainty another will slaughter her that day—then he must inform so he not stumble through him; but it is not a mistaken transaction if he did not inform, for he can tell him slaughter tomorrow; but if he does not know with certainty another will slaughter her that day, he need not inform—even regarding prohibition, for perhaps also one who bought her will not slaughter her that day since it is not one of the four seasons.
And if it is one of the four seasons, etc., he must inform that he sold today, etc. Even though he does not know another will slaughter—for presumptively in these four seasons everyone buys for shechitah; and see Choshen Mishpat siman 199 seif 13.
And erev the last Yom Tov of the festival. For it is a festival by itself; and erev the first—the people are busy with sukkah and lulav and have no leisure for so much shechitah. Tosafot.
He need not inform him. Meaning: neither for prohibition nor mistaken transaction—for it is possible he will slaughter them before these four seasons—and requires study.
He must inform him. And it is also a mistaken transaction if he did not inform; and in our time, since the main shechitah for Yom Tov need is before Yom Tov, and likewise groom and bride slaughter a day or two before—he need not inform in a sale over two days; and so appears in Terumat HaDeshen; so too Bach.
And whoever slaughters first, etc. In the Talmud it is proved that even when they bought from one person, if the second preceded and slaughtered—he is alert and successful; therefore Bach was troubled and wrote: what the Tur wrote "and whoever slaughters, etc." is a matter on its own and refers above to when they bought from one person; and he strained unnecessarily, for in the Talmud it only says that if the second preceded he is alert and successful, but l'chatchila it is not proper to do so; and the Tur teaches us that when they bought from two people, whoever slaughters is alert and successful.
He is not concerned lest its mother or offspring was slaughtered that day. And he need not ask afterward—for there is much doubt: perhaps this one has no mother; and even if you say it has a mother, perhaps he did not buy it for shechitah; and even if for shechitah, perhaps from here and after several days.
