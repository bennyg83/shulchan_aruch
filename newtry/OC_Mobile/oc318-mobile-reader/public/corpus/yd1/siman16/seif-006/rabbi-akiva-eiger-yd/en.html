(Seif 6) And if he sold one to groom—the seller need not ask the buyer if buyer buys for groom; and if buyer buys for groom must inform the seller. See Turei Zahav in his chiddushim to Keritut (daf 16a) s.v. mishnah at end of first chapter.
(Shach s.k. 15) Even partial sheep. For we say regarding its son—all.
(There s.k. 17) Practical difference even for us. In my humble opinion here the law does not apply—that there (siman 34) if the prohibition itself is not so severe that there are no lashes, he is not disqualified for testimony from Torah; but here the oto v'et beno prohibition is severe for there are lashes; and here it is doubt whether concerned for father's seed—he is disqualified from doubt; and like eating doubt of milk doubt of fat—it appears disqualified from doubt; for even though we do not lash from doubt, nevertheless it is like without forewarning he is disqualified, for the prohibition itself is severe; and so he will not be certainly disqualified—perhaps he did no prohibition at all.
