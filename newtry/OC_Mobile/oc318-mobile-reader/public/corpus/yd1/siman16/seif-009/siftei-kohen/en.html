Only through shechitah. And included: if he slaughtered and it was found treifah—they receive lashes if he slaughters the second—for it did not become neveilah through shechitah; and so in the Talmud and poskim.
Or it became neveilah, etc. Bach wrote: however if it became neveilah in his hand through one of the matters we are stringent about, such as shehiyah in minority of the latter simanim, and the like in rabbinic disputes—one may not slaughter the son or mother after it; it appears to me perforce; and so Semag siman 167: if there was shehiyah or chaladah in minority of simanim, or combination of shehiyah, or a defective knife was found after slaughter—they forbade them from doubt; and likewise all such cases it appears forbidden to slaughter the second, and if he slaughtered he does not receive lashes perforce.
Therefore deaf-mute, etc. Bach wrote: it appears the same when the mother was slaughtered—it is permitted to give the son to deaf-mute to slaughter among themselves; and a fortiori permitted to sell to non-Jews even though the non-Jew will pierce it that day—end; and requires study—for regarding deaf-mute, though there is no oto v'et beno, nevertheless it is forbidden to give them to slaughter among themselves, even to throw to dogs, as above siman 1 seif 5—see there.
Among themselves, etc. But when others oversee them—the Mechaber's view is their shechitah is valid even if not expert and does not know how to train his hands, as I wrote in siman 1 s.k. 25; and even though I wrote there to forbid, nevertheless here one should be stringent in a d'oraisa prohibition—and examine; and see above siman 28 s.k. 23.
