Therefore deaf-mute, etc. It appears the same with an adult who does not know laws of shechitah; and that the mishnah mentions deaf-mute—even knowing ones, as siman 1—for it is plain even an adult who does not know, most of his acts are defective; and they said in first chapter of Chullin (daf 9) regarding adult who does not know: sometimes he was delayed and pressed and did not know—sometimes implies there is doubt and it is not plain; this "sometimes" refers to "and did not know," but delay or pressing are plain; and so proved from that which they said afterward "and if others see, etc., forbidden"—this is not so great a novelty, for here b'dieved shechitah is permitted; he should have stated the novelty: even adult who does not know, his shechitah is forbidden b'dieved if he slaughtered alone—for nevertheless forbidden to slaughter after him; rather certainly permitted; but since poskim did not mention this explicitly, I have no authority to permit oto v'et beno after shechitah of adult who does not know; nevertheless it appears clear if he transgressed and slaughtered after him there is no eating prohibition, even for those who forbid in seif 3; and further it appears plain: when the first animal slaughtered became forbidden from some doubt of treifot in shechitah—one should not be lenient because of that to slaughter its son that day; and regarding other treifot it is plain forbidden to slaughter after it—for it is disputed in the Gemara (daf 81) and halachah follows Rabbis who forbid, for shechitah that is not valid—its name is shechitah.
