After he sold them, etc. Implies if he said so at time of sale he is believed to forbid; and so from what Rosh wrote in chapter Oto V'et Beno: I saw written in name of Rav Achai: if a non-Jew merchant sells animals one must be concerned lest he sold the mother today; and it is not reasonable it is common, and we do not establish prohibition so long as the seller did not inform, etc.; if there is concern from doubt, etc.—end; implies if he informed at time of sale one must forbid slaughtering that day the animal bought from him; and it is plain if there is reason he does so to enhance his sale, his words are nothing, like that in last chapter of Yevamot (daf 122): non-Jew who sells produce and said they are orlah—his words are nothing; and see siman 122 seif 6 more on law of idolatry in this.
