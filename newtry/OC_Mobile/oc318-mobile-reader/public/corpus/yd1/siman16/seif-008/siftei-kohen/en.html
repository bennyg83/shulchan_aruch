But the goat that came upon the doe, etc. And Maharshal there siman 5 ruled like Rashba and Tur that it is permitted to slaughter the doe and her son even l'chatchila, as we say in the Talmud "sheep and its son the Merciful One said," and not "deer and its son"; and I do not know what Beit Yosef wrote in name of R' Yosef ibn Chaviv that Rambam's view forbids l'chatchila because of marit ayin—for what marit ayin applies when everyone knows it came from the goat; and if we say the viewer will be concerned for this—even regarding a regular son of a doe; and further, we do not find anywhere such marit ayin.
If that daughter of the doe, etc. Atzat HaZahav and Bach challenged: here Rambam and Mechaber hold we are certainly concerned for the father's seed—otherwise he would not receive lashes from doubt; and above in seif 2 they wrote "if known this is certainly its father they are not slaughtered in one day, and if he slaughtered he does not receive lashes—for the matter is doubtful whether it applies with males"; and they answered: there specifically they doubt whether oto v'et beno applies also with father because we expound "its son attached after it" to exclude the father whose son is not attached after it; but here it is plain we are concerned where the goat came upon the doe and she bore a female—for since this female has partial sheep it is considered as if entirely sheep; and if this female bore daughter or son and he slaughtered the female and her offspring—he receives lashes perforce; and it is astonishing one cannot distinguish thus as plain in the Talmud beginning chapter Oto V'et Beno (beginning daf 79) regarding that which Shmuel followed his reasoning there (and further, whence to doubt this exposition—and examine); and further it is astonishing—in Beit Yosef siman 13 he wrote Rambam's view is we doubt whether we are concerned for father's seed or not, and so Kessef Mishneh chapter 12 of Laws of Shechitah law 11; and further in chapter 13 law 4 Rambam wrote one born from behemah and chayah must cover blood and does not bless, and Mechaber below siman 28 seif 2—and if we hold certainly concerned for father's seed, why not bless; and in Perishah and Bach siman 28 they intended one matter to answer this because specifically regarding sheep we say "sheep" even partial sheep, but regarding chayah we doubt perhaps it requires complete chayah and does not count at all per sugya chapter Oto V'et Beno (daf 79b) that proves there explicitly that where we say sheep even partial sheep, likewise deer even partial deer—see there; and further it is astonishing—for Rambam chapter 9 of Laws of Bekhorim wrote: deer that came on goat and bore—offspring liable for half gifts, as "if sheep even partial sheep"; goat that came on doe—offspring exempt from gifts—end; and so Mechaber below siman 61 seif 8—apparently we doubt; and therefore one can say bring proof we are concerned and I will give you half; as we say in the Talmud; and further in chapter 9 of Laws of Kilayim law 6 Rambam wrote those born from donkey are forbidden with those born from horse, and so Mechaber below siman 297 seif 9—and this is because we doubt; if certainly concerned they are permitted as stated explicitly in Talmud beginning Oto V'et Beno—and requires study; and it is astonishing on what Maharshal wrote chapter Oto V'et Beno siman 2 that the halachah follows Chanania that oto v'et beno applies with father and son, and immediately after in siman 3 ruled regarding oto v'et beno we are not concerned for father's seed and permitted to slaughter father with son in one day—and requires study. And know there is practical difference even for us in what they slaughter—for then he is disqualified for testimony from the Torah, as we hold in Choshen Mishpat siman 34; and likewise regarding kiddushin of a woman with a non-Jew siman 42—see there.
And he slaughtered the goat and its son—he receives lashes. For it is plain we are concerned for the mother's seed; and here there is partial sheep, and we expound in the Talmud "sheep" even partial sheep.
