The goat and its son—he receives lashes. For "sheep and its son the Merciful One said" implies its son even any that is not sheep; and Tur wrote: deer that came on he-goat and bore daughter, and that daughter bore daughter, and he slaughtered her daughter and daughter's daughter—liable; and the reason: we hold like the Rabbis who dispute R' Eliezer and hold the Torah said "sheep" even partial sheep—nevertheless lashes because the daughter is partial sheep through its mother, for we follow the mother.
Forbidden to slaughter her and her son. Rashba and Tur disagreed and permitted even l'chatchila; and it appears to me Shulchan Aruch's reason per Rambam: Rav Chisda said all agree regarding that doe and her son he-goat is exempt—implies forbidden; and so in all exemptions of Shabbat except three and similar, Beit Yosef brought in siman 87 regarding milk; and it is possible there is a rabbinic prohibition since it teaches exempt and does not teach permitted—end; and the reason here: lest it be confused with doe and her son.
That daughter of the doe, etc. And her son—he receives lashes. Many wondered on this—for the conclusion in the Gemara is we doubt whether we are concerned for father's seed, as I mentioned in seif 2—why lashes here, lest we are not concerned for father's seed and the female has no father's seed at all and is not even partial sheep, and one cannot emend "he does not receive lashes"—Rambam concludes afterward likewise kilayim from goat and sheep—lashes; and on this matter there is room for further study in Rambam that Beit Yosef brought siman 297 in Laws of Kilayim that Rosh wondered on Rambam who ruled also lashes because concerned for father's seed, and Beit Yosef labored to find a way to emend his words.
