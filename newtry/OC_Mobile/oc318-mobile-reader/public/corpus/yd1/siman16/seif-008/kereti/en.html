Deer on goat—even though Gemara in Bekhorot implies behemah does not give birth—it is possible majority and all Chazal spoke is majority but minority conceives; and from this they spoke here. (And with this many difficulties are resolved.)
And its son—lashes—Peri Chadash wrote: certainly concerned for seed if sheep even partial sheep; and this is unnecessary—for mother is goat, what difference in its son—son is called even any; and need not be sheep even partial sheep.
And if he slaughtered—he does not receive lashes—this is Rambam's view: even though sheep the Merciful One said and not deer—nevertheless there is no Torah prohibition, but rabbinically forbidden; and Rambam supports from Rav Chisda: exempt and did not say permitted—learn exempt but forbidden; and Rashba and other poskim disagree and hold permitted l'chatchila; and it appears they depend on what afterward Rambam holds: therefore they forbade doe and her son—decree lest they slaughter her son and son's son and there be lashes as I wrote to him; but they hold even if slaughter son and son's son—no lashes; how decree when mistake has no clear prohibition—and requires study.
Lashes—many authors wondered; and see Peleti where I labored greatly to settle Rambam's words: even though he holds we doubt whether concerned for father's seed, as explained this siman seif 2—if so he-goat on doe, if not concerned for father's seed, this is entirely deer; nevertheless he holds from tradition "sheep even partial sheep"—that even if concerned for father's seed, partial seed and father's power is mixed in it—for otherwise how find kilayim forbidden in kodashim; and also if mother treifah and male kosher—offspring kosher, for zeh v'zeh gorem—learn there is father's power and it is partial sheep; therefore tradition "sheep even partial sheep" and liable for lashes; and see there what I wrote not to challenge from Gemara; and other poskim do not hold this reasoning per plain Gemara—no lashes at all, only doubt of prohibition perhaps concerned for father's seed and "sheep even partial sheep"; and much practical difference: one—if doubt in slaughter whether mother was slaughtered or only pierced, one could say offspring permitted from double doubt first that piercing suffices; and if you say shechitah—lest second is not behemah but chayah; and so many differences; and if deer on goat and bore daughter—that daughter with her son—all agree slaughterer liable for lashes, concerned for seed if certain and sheep even partial sheep—and plain.
