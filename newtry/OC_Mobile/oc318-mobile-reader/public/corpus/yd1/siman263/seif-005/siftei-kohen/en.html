Before he reaches, etc. Meaning, before he was circumcised. The same applies if he reached eight days of age but was not circumcised during his lifetime for some reason.
And we do not perform, etc. The Mechaber already wrote this in Orach Chaim, siman 526, seif 10.
It is forbidden to circumcise a non-Jew, etc. At first glance, the Rama's words imply that the circumcision of a non-Jew is itself prohibited. The same appears from the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 266, and the Levush explicitly gives the reason that since the non-Jew does not intend to convert, why should we remove his disgrace and mark him with the seal of the holy covenant? This cannot be correct. In Avodah Zarah 26b, in the sugya concerning those whom we neither raise from danger nor lower into danger, the baraita teaches: ‘A Jew may circumcise a non-Jew for the sake of conversion, but not for the sake of removing a morana.’ Rashi explains that morana is a worm in the foreskin, and the prohibition is because one may not heal him without payment, as stated there that we neither raise them nor lower them. Tosafot likewise explains that the reason is the prohibition against healing them gratuitously, but for payment it is permitted. The Rambam similarly writes at the end of Hilchot Milah: ‘A non-Jew who must have his foreskin cut because of a wound or boil that developed there may not have it cut by a Jew, because we neither rescue a non-Jew from death nor cause his death. This remains so even though a mitzvah results from this treatment, since he did not intend the mitzvah.’ Thus, the prohibition exists only because it is medical treatment. Apart from that concern, there is no independent prohibition of circumcising him, and it is permitted even when not performed for conversion, such as when done for payment or in a similar circumstance. The Mechaber, Rama, and the Levush himself likewise rule below, siman 268, seif 9. This also appears from Rabbeinu Yerucham, who did not record this law in Netiv 1 among the laws of circumcision, but in Netiv 17 among the laws concerning non-Jews whom we neither raise nor lower and the prohibition against healing them. The Rama's unqualified statement here, that it is forbidden to circumcise a non-Jew not for conversion, must therefore rely upon the qualification stated below in siman 268. The Levush's explanation, however, cannot be reconciled in this manner, for he expressly states that the reason is not to mark the non-Jew with the seal of the holy covenant, and that is incorrect, as explained.
