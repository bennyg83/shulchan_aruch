Beforehand. The same applies if the child reached eight days old but was not circumcised during his lifetime for some reason. Shach. If an infant was buried within eight days without circumcision with a flint, it is permitted to exhume him and circumcise him with a flint. Responsa Knesses Yechezkel, question 44.
For conversion. The Taz writes: It appears to me that the reason is that we say that Avraham Avinu stands and identifies one who is circumcised and does not allow him to enter Gehinnom. One who circumcises a non-Jew thereby weakens the identifying sign of circumcision that distinguishes Israel. End of his words. The Shach, however, explains that the case concerns circumcision for medical purposes, such as where the non-Jew needs his foreskin removed because of an injury or the like. It is forbidden to heal him without payment, but for payment it is permitted. See there.
