It may be explained that most of our greetings consist merely of saying “good morning,” which is not a formal inquiry after another's welfare and is permitted, as the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch state in Orach Chaim, siman 85. The Beit Yosef also cites the view that bowing is a formal greeting, while a verbal greeting such as “good morning, my master” is permitted. Darkei Moshe notes that the Zohar, Pekudei 103a, implies that the prohibition applies only when the Divine Name is used. Thus our ordinary greetings are not included in the prohibition. See siman 85.
