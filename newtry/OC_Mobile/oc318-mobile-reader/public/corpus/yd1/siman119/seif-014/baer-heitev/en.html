Scribes. And that which is stated in siman 39 seif 2 that if the lung was not checked it is kosher — that is only if it happened once through coercion or inadvertence; but that one who does not know how to check stands and checks intentionally and they ate based on his checking — certainly forbidden by law; and even though this checking is only of their words, for it is like the case of not nullifying prohibition l'chatchila — if they nullified intentionally it is forbidden; and so the Mechaber in siman 99 seif 6, end of his words Shach. And Taz wrote: and even though regarding a butcher we say in Choshen Mishpat siman 306 he is exempt to pay in this law that it is forbidden because of doubt — it is different there because you come to obligate him for his act; therefore one only obligates him for certainty and not doubt; unlike here between seller and buyer, even if the matter is doubt it is mistaken transaction and he would not have given money accordingly — therefore he returns his money. And in Beit Yosef he wrote in the name of Achai: an incident occurred with a needle found in the lung inside the meat after cutting and they forbade the animal, and what he sold and received money he does not return, because the prohibition is not certain but only because of doubt they forbade the animal since it cannot be checked by blowing, and regarding money the burden of proof is on the claimant, end of his words. Behold they compared this law to the butcher case I mentioned, and this is certainly not according to what I wrote above that even in doubt he must return money; nevertheless they judged well, for perhaps it deals with when they ate the meat before it became known it was tereifah, and in this there is no penalty to return money; or even if not yet eaten, perhaps the needle was swallowed before the buyer bought it (meaning regarding the seller who sold the whole animal to the butcher he returns money — investigate) lest it swallowed afterward, as Choshen Mishpat siman 232 seif 11: if within three days he bought, the claimant must bring proof; but if clear it was done with the seller's permission, such as after slaughter he sold to him — clearly mistaken transaction and he returns his money as long as he did not eat the meat; and if he ate, the selling butcher is exempt even though doubt d'oraisa prohibition — nevertheless no penalty applies since he himself did not know; unlike mistaken transaction, as long as meat remains it does not help him that he did not know, for whatever is because of a defect he must return even though he too benefited — plain in Choshen Mishpat siman 232 seif 18 in the gloss. But in truth if the seller knew this doubt and sold to him — certainly even if the buyer ate he returns his money, unlike Levush, see there, end of his words.
From an animal. Taz wrote: even though Shulchan Aruch wrote that if tereifah came because the lung was not checked properly we say if she ate the seller deducts and does not return — nevertheless that is only regarding something that d'oraisa does not require checking when there is no defect; but if there is a defect it automatically requires checking even d'oraisa, and the event occurred that they cannot check — even if the buyer ate the seller must return, for behold he sold doubt d'oraisa prohibition; and this is specifically if the seller knew the doubt, for then penalty applies; if he did not know, he does not return except when not eaten, from mistaken transaction. But Shach disagrees and wrote: since it is doubt, why should the seller return money if already eaten; if the buyer wants to say you have no greater defect than this, the seller says return my sale and I will return your money; now that he ate the seller says who can tell you that you ate tereifah — and so in Semak Choshen Mishpat siman 234 s.k. 1 regarding safek tereifah if eaten he need not return money; rather this is the explanation of Rama's words here: from an animal not checked properly which is certainly forbidden by their words — plainly he must return money while meat exists; but even if he sold something from an animal in which a defect arose requiring forbidding out of doubt, and the seller might say perhaps it is not tereifah and he will bring proof it is tereifah and return money — nevertheless it is as if certainly tereifah by their words and he must return money while meat still exists; and any safek tereifot voids the transaction if meat still exists; but if he already ate the meat the seller need not return anything, end of his words.
