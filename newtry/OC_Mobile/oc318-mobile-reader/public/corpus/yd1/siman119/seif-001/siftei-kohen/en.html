One suspected of eating, etc. It appears that not relying on him is only when known he is suspected; but on a plain Israelite we rely even on d'oraisa prohibition, as Tur wrote. In what case? A suspected person; but a plain person has presumption of kosher to eat with him — likewise Raavad end chapter 1 of Maaser; likewise Rashi wrote Syrian merchants are suspected; likewise R' Levi ibn Chaviv responsum 121; I also found proof from Yerushalmi chapter 8 of Shevi'it law 2: R' Yosi said they only spoke when one does not know if he is suspected; if clearly suspected — forbidden, etc. Not like Rambam (and he is the \"some say\" the Rav brought) who requires recognizing presumption of kosher status; likewise Darkei Moshe — custom unlike Rambam, presume everyone kosher. Difficult: in new simanim siman 1 Mechaber ruled like Rambam requiring trustworthy slaughterer; Beit Yosef there unlike Mordechai that plain Israelite is presumed kosher; and siman 65 Mechaber/Tur/Rambam — do not buy meat from butcher who slaughters for himself and sells without presumption; difficult also on Tur citing Rambam in siman 65; Taz distinguishes slaughtering butcher — common, many slaughter laws, easy disqualifications — if no presumption forbidden.
One may not rely on him regarding them — meaning to buy from him; but they rely when he says regarding others it is permitted, as Mechaber seif 7; see there and s.k. 5.
Forbidden to buy wine or other matters of prohibition concern — some say other literal Torah prohibitions; but even wine — stam yeinam is only d'rabbanan decreed lest definite libation wine; some say not only wine but anything with prohibition concern even entirely d'rabbanan like cheese and gentile bread where custom forbids; from Rav's conclusion likewise milk — not bought unless presumption of kosher; unlike Rambam there who permits milk from anyone — Maggid Mishneh learned from version lacking milk among Syrian restrictions; in our texts Chullin Avodah Zarah 39b includes milk with fish piece — Tosafot and Beit Yosef; Rav's view: milk like other matters.
However if one dined with him he may eat with him — not worse than suspected for selling forbidden (Taz next seif); Mechaber \"if one dined\" does not mean l'chatchila forbidden to lodge to eat — even suspected for selling may dine (Taz); Mechaber uses \"if one dined\" to teach even b'dieved after lodging forbidden to eat from suspected matters.
