Maharshal wrote: if the sages see he cannot exile from his place because dependents hang on him and his daughter's troubles or the like — they give him repentance in his place with enough restriction to stand in this balance. He also wrote that for one transgression they consider him a stumbling block and should warn him not to do so again. And nevertheless it appears one should forbid vessels retroactively whenever there is doubt even the first time. Shach wrote that even the first time he sold forbidden things they remove him, etc.; and so from the Mechaber seif 17 and what the Rav wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 306 seif 9: and some say a butcher and teacher even though they do not need warning, nevertheless we require presumption until they are established or permitted — we do not remove them. Ribash already answered: there it deals with only monetary loss and also perhaps they are misled or inadvertent; but one suspected of feeding prohibition — immediately they remove him. Rashba already wrote that a butcher from whom tereifah issued has no excuse to say he was inadvertent, for otherwise he would always say so; and regarding tereifah of vessels see above siman 1. Beit Yosef wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 34: according to Rambam, if he inspects for himself, since he has monetary benefit, even if he repented he is not restored until he goes where he is not recognized; one who inspects for others, since he has no monetary benefit therein, is restored through repentance.
