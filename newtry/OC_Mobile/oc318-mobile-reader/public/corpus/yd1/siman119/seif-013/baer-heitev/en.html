The money. Because of penalty — Rashi; and accordingly it appears specifically when it is known he knew they were forbidden things and sold them intentionally we penalize him; if not, no, for perhaps he was inadvertent (and one does not penalize for what he already ate — only that he need not pay except the value of tereifah cheaply; but what remains intact is mistaken transaction and he returns it and the buyer returns the money). And even though explained in seif 17 that a butcher has no excuse to say he was inadvertent — that is regarding prohibition but not to extract money, end of his words Shach. And Taz wrote it appears that even if the prohibition is safek tereifah — what he ate he ate and he returns the money and they did not consider his eating as benefit at all (and Nachalat Binyamin disagrees, for in safek tereifah even if the seller knew it was safek tereifah he does not return money when eaten; and so in Semak Choshen Mishpat siman 234; and what Taz brings from Bechorot is not proof, for there regarding prohibitions of benefit specifically we say so, or regarding a firstborn because he transgressed a rabbinic enactment, see there).
He shall pay. Since he did not cause him prohibition. Rashi.
Returns. The reason: Semak wrote in Choshen Mishpat that siman regarding d'rabbanan prohibition his eating is considered benefit; and Shach wrote the reason: regarding money they established it on Torah law and did not decree prohibition in order to extract money, end of his words.
