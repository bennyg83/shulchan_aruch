In Beit Yosef it is cited in the name of Rambam that a suspect — and all the more so an am ha'aretz — is trusted regarding another's [produce] to testify that it has been tithed, for a person does not sin for himself alone. And Beit Yosef raised a difficulty from the mishnah at the beginning of chapter 4 of Demai: one who buys produce from one who is not trusted and forgot to tithe it and asks him on Shabbat may eat, etc. — another said to him, etc. — why is he not trusted regarding another's [produce]? It seems to me that the case there involves concern of collusion — that they made an arrangement together — for Beit Yosef already wrote in his name that wherever there is such concern he is not trusted; and this appears precisely from the language of the mishnah: "another said to him who is not trusted" — meaning in a case where he is not trusted even regarding another's, namely where he says "mine has not been tithed but my fellow's has been tithed" — teaching us that even not in the fellow's presence he is not trusted. And my father-in-law of blessed memory resolved [this] by saying the case there is different since he forgot to tithe it, and since the obligation arose originally upon this person, the am ha'aretz can no longer be trusted to exempt him — but this is not precise, since the reason the mishnah specifies "forgot to tithe it" is that ab initio one should not rely on asking him on Shabbat, for this leniency was only permitted after the fact out of deference for Shabbat; and the proof is that after Shabbat one must separate tithes even for what was eaten on Shabbat, as Rambam wrote in chapter 12 of the laws of Tithes. And we learn from this approach that regarding one suspected in another matter, he may testify about his fellow's [produce] in any case where there is no apparent collusion or personal benefit in the matter. However I find a difficulty from the mishnah in chapter 4 of Demai: one who enters a city and knows no one there — he says: who here is trustworthy? — another answered: I am not trustworthy, so-and-so is trustworthy — he is trusted. And the Yerushalmi challenges: is one witness trusted? — and R' Shimshon wrote: even though elsewhere one witness is trusted, here since he is a suspect it is not appropriate to believe him; R' Yochanan said: the Sages were lenient for an inn-keeper who is unknown because of livelihood; and R' Shimshon wrote: therefore it specifies "one who does not know anyone" — but one who knows someone there, they did not permit — as is taught in the Tosefta — end of quote; and Rambam likewise wrote in his commentary and in his code, laws of Tithes chapter 12. If so, the difficulty stands — why did he permit a suspect to be trusted in all cases regarding another's [produce]? I subsequently saw that Raavad in his objections raises this very difficulty against Rambam at the end of chapter 12; and Kesef Mishneh resolved it by saying that Rambam holds that the statement of R' Yochanan in the Yerushalmi comes to establish the mishnah even according to R' Meir, who holds that a suspect regarding a matter does not adjudicate it or testify about it — but we hold like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, according to whom he does testify; therefore one need not say that the leniency applies only in the inn-keeper case. And I am astonished at this holy mouth — for Rambam himself wrote about that ruling that it applies specifically to an inn-keeper who does not know anyone there, as I just quoted; and moreover the Tosefta states this explicitly, as R' Shimshon mentioned above. If so, the difficulty returns to its place — why did he rule here that a suspect is trusted in all cases? And Raavad holds that we ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel only regarding the firstborn, since that restriction is only a penalty; but in all other places we hold that a suspect regarding a matter does not adjudicate it or testify about it — like R' Meir; and so it appears to me to rule in practice.
