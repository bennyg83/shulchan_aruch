Substitute. Shach wrote: only for selling he instructs permissibility, but to steal and substitute by hand he is not suspected. And that which appears in siman 118 seif 1 that one does not deposit with a suspected Israelite a d'oraisa forbidden item except with two seals and a d'rabbanan forbidden item with one seal — there it deals with one suspected to substitute, and they abbreviated there because I did not descend to explain there who is called suspected.
Ashamed. Shach wrote: from Rashba it appears where it is known she is not ashamed of the guest as is the way of an innkeeper today who cares nothing about the guest's food — it is permitted. But Tosafot explained the opposite — that she takes the good and gives the guest the bad because she toiled for him; accordingly where he pays her wages for trouble it is possible it is permitted, as Tosafot wrote regarding an agent: if one gives him wages he cannot impose an oath on him, for then he no longer instructs permissibility; and even for Rambam, from whose words it appears that even when one gives wages he can impose an oath because nevertheless he instructs permissibility lest he did not pay his wages properly — it is possible he concedes here regarding an innkeeper's wages.
