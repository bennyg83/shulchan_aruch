It is permitted to give from one's own food to one suspected of eating forbidden things so that he may prepare or cook for him, and we are not concerned lest he substitute, since he is not suspected of theft; but if one gives to someone who is concerned about his preparation and he is suspected of eating forbidden things — it is forbidden, lest what he gives become spoiled and he substitute his own. How so? If one gave to his mother-in-law who is suspected regarding the prohibition — it is forbidden, for she is ashamed before her son-in-law and wants her daughter's benefit and therefore substitutes bad for good; and likewise one who gives to an innkeeper who is suspected — sometimes she is ashamed before the guest and substitutes bad for good. {Rama: (Nevertheless it is permitted to deposit with him and that he return the items as deposited):}
