And what remains in the utensil is forbidden, etc. It is difficult, for above siman 124 seif 14 he ruled what remains inside is permitted, and that is because they did not stringently forbid his force in stream; and likewise below seif 8 he copied Tur's language that what comes out is forbidden for drinking, which implies what is inside is permitted; and one may challenge, for behold stream applies only to wine forbidden in benefit, as below siman 126 seif 5; and here since what comes out is forbidden only for drinking, why is what remains in the utensil forbidden because of stream; and likewise it is difficult on the rabbi — requires study. And for us everything is permitted in a place of loss, for whatever is forbidden by law for drinking — for us, since gentiles in our time are not idolaters — is permitted even for drinking, as above siman 124 seif 24.
Unless there is great loss. It is difficult, for behold without that it is permitted even in minor loss, as written above siman 124 seif 24, because gentiles in our time are not idolaters and all their touch is without intention; and it is strained to say his force is different, for it was already explained there that whatever is forbidden by law for drinking — for us entirely permitted; and further behold Rabbenu Tam and his camp hold touch of a non-Jew through something else is considered his force, which forbids by law only for drinking; and we establish not like Rabbenu Tam in this, as explained in Beit Yosef siman 124 and in Shulchan Aruch there seif 11 that his touch through something else is worse than his force and forbidden even in benefit; and nevertheless we establish above siman 124 seif 24 that touch of a non-Jew through something else in our time is permitted even for drinking — if so all the more his force; and one must say the rabbi did not write thus except by law, and the practical difference is in a place where it is known the gentiles are idolaters, also not to publicize the matter as written there in the hagahah that one does not publicize before the am ha'aretz; and likewise in several places one must say thus according to the rabbi's view; but for us permitted here even without great loss; and likewise in Sefer Api Ravrevi beginning of the page: it appears to be lenient in our time, for they are not accustomed to pour libations even without great loss.
Or he did not lift the utensil, etc. Requires study in this law, for what Beit Yosef brought in siman 124 from Rambam and the Rosh (regarding Morik Oriko) and Ran and afterward the rabbi followed — nothing appears from their words — see there; and likewise it appears in Terumat HaDeshen daf 131 side 1 that he wrote in the name of Ramban that if the non-Jew removed the stopper from the barrel, even though the wine is not agitated at all, the wine is forbidden for drinking, for it is like Morik Oriko and it is the force of a non-Jew with intention forbidden for drinking — until here is his language; and likewise above siman 124 seif 14 they forbade when the non-Jew removed the spigot because of his force; and even though Bach wrote there it is different for we consider removing the spigot as though he touched the wine through something else and agitated — end of his words — it is difficult, for if so why is what remains inside permitted; and further behold even if the spigot does not touch the barrel at all but is inserted in another spigot, the law there is thus — see there; rather we consider it his force as Taz wrote there explicitly; and further that if so we would consider it as though he touched the wine itself and agitated; and Darkhei Moshe resolved that a spigot is different, for it is customary thus to draw wine from the barrel — end of his words; and this too is strained, for likewise it is customary to pour without lifting; and further what difference here and there — here too it is his force; and likewise Bach challenged in his last pamphlet on this distinction — who told us whether it is his custom or not; and also what Beit Yosef wrote there that Rambam explained non-Jew Adna — that the non-Jew lifted the barrel — is difficult, for non-Jew Adna implies he tilted the barrel; and likewise in Tosafot perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60 side 1) s.v. non-Jew Adna and in Mordechai there in what is written: if there is a little wine in the barrel that must be lifted from one side it is forbidden — end of his words — it implies even if he did not lift the barrel but only tilted it on its side it is forbidden because of his force — see there; and so it appears explicitly from Rashba's words regarding Jew Adna and non-Jew Akuba — wine is forbidden in what they explained there in the name of Raavad, because when he tilts the vat he increases spilling wine from the barrel; and all the more according to what Ran explained there likewise — see there; and further proof from below seif 4 — examine carefully, for I was brief here; and for us who establish gentiles in our time are not idolaters — everything permitted in a place of loss.
