What remains. Shach challenged, for below siman 124 seif 14 he ruled that what remains is permitted; and he further challenged, for a stream applies only to wine forbidden in benefit, as below siman 126 seif 5; and here, since what comes out is forbidden only for drinking, why is what remains in the vessel forbidden because of the stream? And investigate. (And to me it appears: investigate, for the author holds that lifting is different, for it is customary to pour a libation thus; even though he did not rub, we say regarding it that a stream is a connection; and they made lifting a prohibition of drinking like rubbing for prohibition of benefit; and if so the author's words are correct — only on Rama one should challenge why we forbid below siman 124 what came out for drinking. And investigate.) And Shach wrote: for us everything is permitted in a place of loss, for everything forbidden by law for drinking — for us, since non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters — is permitted even for drinking, as siman 124 seif 24.
Great loss. Shach wrote: and it appears to me that perforce Rama did not write thus except by law; but for us it is permitted here even without a place of great loss.
He lifted. Behold Turei Zahav and Shach challenged Rama in what he wrote, that if he did not lift the vessel everything is permitted; and above siman 124 seif 14 we say that when the non-Jew removed the spigot it is forbidden because of his force; however Shach wrote that for us everything is permitted in a place of loss.
