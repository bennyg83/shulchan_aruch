And he lifted it and the wine came out, etc. Beit Yosef in siman 124 brought these words of Rambam and wrote they are from what they said in perek R' Yishmael: non-Jew Adna, Jew Akuba — forbidden, for when wine comes it comes through the force of a non-Jew; and it appears to Rambam specifically when the non-Jew lifts the barrel and pours the wine; but if he does not lift but only tilts it on its side and pours the wine into another utensil — permitted; and likewise it appears from Ran's words that he wrote he takes the barrel in his hand, etc.; and afterward he brought the Rosh's words that also wrote — behold the essence of libation is in lifting and pouring, etc. And I am astonished, for behold above siman 123 Tur and Rama wrote if a non-Jew treads wine, even though he did not touch with his feet — wine that comes through his force is forbidden; and this is the arranged Gemara in perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60) regarding his force — all disagree it is forbidden; and behold there he did not lift at all; and further it is difficult from what is written in siman 124 seif 14 that if the spigot does not pass the full thickness of the rims, the wine that comes out is forbidden because of the non-Jew's force; and behold there is no lifting; and Darkhei Moshe wrote on this difficulty and one may say perhaps there it is different, for it is customary to draw wine thus from the barrel — end; and this is nothing, for if the way of libation is only through lifting specifically — what concern do we have for the way of drawing wine thus; and behold in any case there is no way of libation in this; and even if this answer is true, the first difficulty is not resolved, for there wine is not relevant at all until after it comes through his force. And Maharach wrote that in removing the spigot the reason is not because of his force but we consider it as though he touched the wine through something else — he was not precise, for explicitly Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote it is his force if it does not pass all the rims; and in truth every touch through something else is called force, as Beit Yosef wrote explicitly siman 124; and further it is difficult — and behold we find explicitly in Tosafot perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60): therefore one should be stringent with people when they tilt the vat that has a little wine to one side, that a non-Jew should not tilt it, etc., because of the force of a non-Jew; and likewise in Hagahot Asheri; however if there is a little wine in the barrel that must be lifted from one side — forbidden, etc.; and likewise in Mordechai; and so is the implication of Tur's words here, who wrote the law of force of a non-Jew who pours from utensil to utensil and did not mention lifting is required; and further proof from what Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbenu Peretz that Rama brought nearby: if the non-Jew shakes the Jew's hand — forbidden where it would not have flowed without that, even though he did not lift anything; and what Beit Yosef brought proof from that Rambam mentioned lifting here — it appears he mentioned it because of his conclusion there: he lifted and did not agitate and did not touch — permitted; and perforce he also mentioned at the beginning lifting, for even though he lifted, stirring is required; and perforce to say thus, that if lifting specifically forbids — why (not) state at the end that if he did not lift, even though stream is forbidden; and this is a greater novelty than what he wrote: he lifted and did not agitate and did not touch — permitted, meaning without pouring; and this is proof for my explanation of Rambam's words that there is no answer; and one should not challenge from what Rambam wrote chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods, and these are his words: a non-Jew who touched wine and did not intend for this wine — permitted in benefit; how so? For example he fell on a wineskin of wine, or he extended his hand to a barrel intending it was oil and it was wine, and wine came through his force without intention — since he did not touch the wine, permitted for drinking; how so? For example he lifted a utensil of wine and poured into another utensil and he thought it was beer — behold this is permitted — end; from this we learn lifting is required for the practical difference; for as a greater novelty he said thus: even if he lifted and poured it is permitted in this; and what Beit Yosef brought from Ran's words — it is plain there is no proof from there, for Ran intended only to reveal he did not intend; behold Rif that the non-Jew drew from the utensil, as Beit Yosef himself wrote; and that which he stated the non-Jew took the barrel in his hand and pours — it is not precise but the manner of speech, that the manner is to take in his hands when he pours; and what he brought proof from the Rosh who mentioned lifting and pouring in passing — he did not examine well, for the Rosh challenged Rabbenu Tam who says pouring of a non-Jew forbids only because of his force, meaning for drinking specifically; and why should it not be complete libation since there is lifting and pouring like a complete libation; and on this he answered that not in his presence it is not the way of libation — end; there is no prohibition except because of his force; and in truth in his force we do not require even lifting, for prohibition of his force is because of "go, go, we say to the nazir," etc., as Rashi explained in perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 60); and if so what did Beit Yosef write that from the Rosh's words it appears there is no way of libation without lifting; and do we forbid because of libation — behold we forbid it only for drinking, and it is his force and we do not care about libation — end; it appears Beit Yosef did not write these words except as give-and-take of pilpul and not to establish thus as halachah; and perforce he did not establish thus in Shulchan Aruch; and Rama who emended here at the end of the seif "or he did not lift the utensil" — did not trouble to examine fully but relied on what Beit Yosef wrote; and one should not be lenient at all here in the force of a non-Jew even without lifting, for nevertheless there is force of a non-Jew here — so it appears to me clearly for practical halachah.
Forbidden for drinking. See what I wrote siman 124 seif kaf 30 — I have great astonishment at this regarding Tur and likewise regarding Shulchan Aruch.
And there are those who say we do not forbid, etc. Beit Yosef likewise wrote in the name of Rashi and challenged from what Tosafot and the Rosh wrote, the incident of Rashi with a non-Jew who poured into the cup, etc. — from this we learn the jug is forbidden because of stream to the force of a non-Jew; and it appears to me one who examines Asheri — it is obvious there is no difficulty at all, for he did not write this as an incident but wrote, and these are his words: Rashbam wrote in the name of Rashi that one may learn from here that even according to the one who says stream is connection, stream of a stream is not connection, etc.; and if so if a non-Jew emptied wine from a jug into a cup and the wine in the cup is forbidden because of stream, etc.; and if afterward he drew the barrel into the jug, what is in the barrel is permitted, for it is stream of a stream — end. Behold all these words follow the words of the one who says stream is connection, namely Raavad's view and his camp who hold stream is connection everywhere even in the force of a non-Jew; and that case of Rav Sheshet who permits stream to the force of a non-Jew is a mere rejection, as Beit Yosef wrote in his name; automatically there is stream to the force of a non-Jew; nevertheless what is in the barrel is permitted for everyone, for it is stream of a stream; and this is the main novelty to permit stream of a stream even for one who holds there is stream to the force of a non-Jew; and also in Tosafot the intent is thus, for they first wrote also even for one who says stream is connection, and adjacent to this they wrote: and an incident came before Rashi, etc., that then the wine in the cup was forbidden — meaning only for the words of the one who says it is forbidden; except nevertheless what is in the barrel is permitted as I mentioned; but Rashi himself holds even stream is permitted in this.
Nevertheless one should be stringent unless there is great loss. This is the language of "he is not precise" — for in great loss even in complete stream, namely wine forbidden through touch of a non-Jew, we are lenient, as written siman 126.
And if it was without intention, etc. In the Gemara it is stated that even smell decides the matter — meaning if it is old wine he certainly knew it was wine through the smell.
