(Siman 125 seif 1) Forbidden for drinking. At first glance there appears proof for this view from the incident of the child who permitted treading in the winepress with him. And behold nevertheless it is his force through treading, as we say in Ma'atzarta, and everything is forbidden because of stream through his force. Perforce his force is forbidden only for drinking; and for Rashba who holds the force of a non-Jew and also holds we say stream through his force — it works out, for he holds that every stream has no prohibition of benefit, being only as mixed and permitted except the value of yayin nesech in it. But for Raavad who holds stream of a non-Jew is difficult at first glance, and one must say that since the Jew treads with him it is the force of a Jew and the force of a non-Jew mixed together; however I do not know in such a case where they tread on the body of the grapes, as we say — and behold he touched with his foot — if it is called the force of a Jew and the force of a non-Jew mixed, for nevertheless in that place where the non-Jew treads, from those grapes wine drips from his force alone; and at first glance it is not called mixed except that both poured together or they loaded the beam on the grapes together — requires study for practical law. And behold according to Rambam that his force is forbidden in benefit, as Hagahot Maimoni wrote — I am greatly astonished at Rambam's words, and this is the Mechaber's language above (siman 123): a non-Jew who treads, etc., forbidden for drinking — requires great study.
