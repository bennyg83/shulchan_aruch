And what remains. Be'er Heitev and responsum Noda B'Yehuda: a non-Jew took wine from one barrel and filled others; his taking was through a siphon called a Heber — he drew wine from the barrel with it, lifted it, and placed it on another barrel so wine would flow there until it filled; and so he did for the whole set of barrels — he filled from the first barrel; and while he still let wine flow to a barrel, he did not completely remove his hand below from the hole in the siphon, lest the flow be thicker than the hole at the mouth of the barrel; therefore he held his finger there loosely so a thin flow would come out. And he wrote to permit, for touching the flow is not like touching all the wine — for one who holds that a stream is a connection; and even for one who does not hold a stream is a connection, he holds specifically when the flow descends to a barrel and afterward he touched the flow; but not so here, for when he removed his finger from the first barrel he placed his finger on the siphon below, and then it was not yet opposite the second barrel; and at the time of flow to the second barrel there is no new touch, only because of looseness the flow descends on his finger — for everyone it is not considered touch of the whole, only a stream; and there is reason to permit through sixty against the wine in the siphon, especially in a small flow of prohibition entering permission. And see there that also regarding the wine in the siphon itself there is reason to side — whether it is forbidden in benefit, for one may say it is being occupied, as he wrote siman 124; and regarding drawing wine through the siphon, lifting is not called his force, only because he draws air from inside the vessels the wine must come up to fill the voids from the air, and wine is not drawn from the force of the person walking, only the wind, etc.; therefore since wine in the siphon is nullified in sixty, there is reason to permit even for drinking even without loss, since Rashba and the author explicitly permit — see there.
