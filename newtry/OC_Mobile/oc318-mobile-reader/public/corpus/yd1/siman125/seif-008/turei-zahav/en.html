And if the non-Jew agitated the utensil. All this is Rambam's words chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods; and he follows his approach, for Tur wrote siman 124 in his name that it appears to him: a non-Jew holds an open utensil and agitates it — forbids even though he did not lift, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 124 seif 17; and this is the explanation of what they said in the Gemara: and if he tilts back and forth — forbidden; and here Tur also brought this law in this language; and if gentiles bring the utensil close to the side of the flow — for Rashi forbidden for drinking and for Rabbenu Yonah forbidden in benefit; and likewise the conclusion of Rabbenu Nissim — end is his language; language of closeness implies also without lifting, for it is possible to bring close without lifting; and perforce one should say what Tur wrote in siman 124 on Rambam's words that his words do not appear correct to Rabbenu Nissim — and Bach already challenged this; and above I copied in the name of Darkhei Moshe that here it deals with lifting; but it does not appear thus to me for practical halachah as I wrote; and likewise it appears from the Rosh's words who copied Rabbenu Yonah's words on this law that he lifts one side of the utensil, etc.; and behold certainly this is not lifting; and it appears to me that above nothing is proven that the non-Jew intends in his holding the utensil that it is for the sake of wine inside, unlike here where he brings it close to the side of wine flow — it is evident he acts so the wine will be received through force. And there are three disputes in one matter: Rambam's view who holds in every manner that the non-Jew agitates, even if he does not lift — forbids; and the Rosh holds there is prohibition in agitating through the utensil; and in Derishah he wrote that for the Rosh there is no prohibition in agitation at all except through touch — and he did not examine Asheri I mentioned — meaning when he lifts as the Rosh wrote perek R' Yishmael one opinion; but without lifting there is no way of libation — namely wherever the matter is not evident he acts for the sake of the wine as I wrote; but here in bringing close to wine flow he forbids even without lifting; but Rashba and Raavad hold there is no prohibition in agitation except when he agitates inside the wine utensil in his hand or through something else; and Rashba explained another explanation on what we say in the Gemara: and if he tilts back and forth — forbidden — meaning we are concerned when he tilts he touched the walls of the utensil for the flow of descending wine, and it is his touch through something else and perhaps he intends thus; and for all explanations there is no prohibition for us, for we permit touch of a non-Jew through something else as written above siman 124.
