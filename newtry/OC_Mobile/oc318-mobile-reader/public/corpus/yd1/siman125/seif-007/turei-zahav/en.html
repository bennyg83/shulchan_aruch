Such as he threw water into a large barrel. Beit Yosef explained that even if he intends to dilute in this it is permitted, since it is an uncommon matter the rabbis did not decree on it — end; and it is explained in Ran and Rashba; and specifically in a cup where the practice is to dilute, they forbade in it even a little water, because of decree lest he dilute wine in water or lest he touch the wine itself since he is accustomed to it — so Rashba and Ran wrote; and even though we now see he did not touch, nevertheless we are concerned lest another time he will not be careful at all, since he accustomed himself to this; and perforce it is not comparable here to what Tur wrote end of siman 128 that wherever we are concerned lest he touched it is forbidden even in benefit — for there we are concerned lest he touched now; and in vain Maharach troubled to resolve this difficulty. And what is written: but if he intends to dilute forbidden for drinking because of the decree I mentioned; and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba and Ran; and even though in the previous seif they were lenient b'dieved — here in cup dilution it is fit to decree more because he has closeness to it; but in bringing grapes to the winepress it is not so much closeness, etc.; and from this I learned nearby that if in those grapes there is complete wine already drawn — also there forbidden even b'dieved even though without intention; and even for us who are lenient now because gentiles are not idolaters — in this one should be stringent — so it appears to me for practical halachah.
