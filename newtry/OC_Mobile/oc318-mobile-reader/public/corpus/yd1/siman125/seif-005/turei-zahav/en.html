Forbidden even in benefit. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rosh: that which is forbidden not in his anger — we are concerned lest the non-Jew touched the wine and poured a libation; but in his anger we are not concerned lest he touched; and even though he touches, it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew through something else and permitted for drinking. Or alternatively: not in his anger is intentional touch of a non-Jew through something else and forbidden in benefit, for the rabbis did not permit measuring, etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote and our rabbi wrote like the latter language — end; explanation of his words: according to the first reason the main prohibition is lest he touched with his hand; automatically if there is clarification he did not touch with his hand — permitted even not in his anger; and according to the second explanation, also in this forbidden not in his anger, for it is intentional touch through something else; and Tur also wrote and he pushes until it fell into the wine — forbidden, etc.; it implies from the aspect of pushing it is forbidden and not because lest he touched with his hand; it turns out there is greater stringency according to the second explanation; and Maharach understood Beit Yosef intended to say there is a distinction between the Rosh's two explanations: for the first reason there is no prohibition of benefit; and on this he wrote Beit Yosef was not precise, for also according to the first reason forbidden in benefit; and in truth he did not descend to the truth of the Rosh's words but there is a practical difference between them as I wrote; and likewise it appears from Beit Yosef's words that he brought Rashba's language who holds like the latter version, as I wrote that he wrote "and he agitated the wine in the utensil," etc.; and there is further practical difference between these explanations for us who establish intentional touch of a non-Jew through something else is permitted, as Rama wrote siman 124 — if so according to the first explanation forbidden even for us, lest he touched with his hand; but according to the second explanation the main concern is touch through something else — permitted for us; it turns out there is greater stringency in the first explanation; and therefore it appears one should be concerned for the first explanation and forbid even for us in throwing not in anger when the object rolls and he pushes it until the wine, as long as it is not clear he did not touch with his hand.
And if he did so in his anger, etc. This contradicts what Shulchan Aruch itself wrote siman 124 seif 19; and that ruling stands against him from its greater source, as I wrote there that it is against the mishnah. And Maharach wanted to resolve that Rambam explains what they said in the Gemara "it goes from him" — specifically in a barrel it is more stringent, since it is a heavy thing and touch applies more than a stone or object; and therefore in his anger forbidden for drinking and not in his anger forbidden even in benefit — he was not precise, for behold explicitly in the mishnah we say there was an incident and they permitted it — it implies even for drinking; and further in the Gemara it challenges (on this) Rav Papa from that case of the barrel in siman 124 — and why did he not answer) that a barrel is different in siman 124 because it is heavy and touch applies more than a stone or object.
