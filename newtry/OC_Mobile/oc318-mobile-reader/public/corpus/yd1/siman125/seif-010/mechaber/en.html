If he was carrying an open utensil with wine and a Jew walks behind him: if it is lacking and he did not agitate it, it is permitted for drinking. (And there are those who say that even if he agitated it in the utensil, it is permitted here — Rashba, Raavad, Rosh, and Ran — and so is primary.) But if it is full, it is forbidden in benefit, lest he touched it. If the Jew walks at his side and watches him, even if it is full it is permitted, for it is impossible for him to touch without the Jew seeing him. And if he was carrying the utensil on a pole with two people, even if it is full and the Jew walks behind him, it is permitted; and the same law applies to one who carries a bucket by its ring (Tosafot, Sefer HaTerumah, and Semak). {Rama: Provided that the Jew assisted him when he inserted the pole into the ring of the utensil (Mordechai in the name of Raavyah), or that he saw when the non-Jew inserted it that he did not touch.}
