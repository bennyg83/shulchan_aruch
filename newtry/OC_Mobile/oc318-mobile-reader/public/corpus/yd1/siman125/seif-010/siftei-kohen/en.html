Permitted here. Meaning: here, since the utensil is wide — therefore we are concerned when full lest he touch it; therefore when lacking it is permitted; and as Ran wrote, since it is wide he does not want agitation lest it spill; therefore even if he lifted it is permitted here; and above siman 124 seif 8, which permits only if he agitated without lifting, deals with a narrow utensil (and what is written here in Shulchan Aruch Rashba, Raavad, and Rosh is an error — examine carefully).
On a pole with two people, etc. — permitted. For it is far from him and he cannot touch it. Tur.
Provided, etc. And if by tilting wine splashed on the non-Jew and returned and splashed into the wine — permitted, for there is no way of libation in this. Mordechai there and Darkhei Moshe brings it; and it means it splashed on the body of the non-Jew; for if on his garment, without that it is obvious it is permitted, for it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew through something else, as above siman 124 seif 24.
