Permitted b'dieved. But l'chatchila not; and they said in the Gemara the reason because of "go, go, we say to the nazir," etc.; and Rashba wrote in the long Terumat HaDeshen, and these are his words: Raavad explained the reason that wine is squeezed in dardoriyot, and when the non-Jew pours the grapes in the winepress, wine is also spilled through his hand; and because of this l'chatchila forbidden, for we decree lest complete wine pouring; but b'dieved permitted; and even though there is much wine spurting on the grapes, they did not decree b'dieved because he does not intend in that pouring for pouring wine but for pouring grapes that are mixed in them, that have not yet been trodden and wine has not been drawn from them; and there is no way idolaters pour thus — end; explanation of his words: since when he pours the grapes, wine among them is automatically also spilled — l'chatchila they decreed here because of "go, go," etc., meaning lest he not pour the grapes since he also pours clear wine among them; and even though there is no prohibition here, for there is no wine on it — nevertheless they decreed lest another time he pour complete clear wine; and nevertheless b'dieved permitted since he does not intend at all, even for that wine; for if he intended for that wine it would be fit to decree because of other complete wine; and likewise it would be fit to forbid even if he did not intend for wine pouring, such as where there was complete drawn wine in a special place — in this it would be fit to decree because of wine pouring alone; perforce here none of these apply; and likewise the intent of Tur in copying Rashba's words that he wrote: because the grapes have not yet been trodden and their wine not drawn and he does not intend for wine pouring but grape pouring — meaning he does not intend for pouring that wine itself that is mixed; and there is no complete wine at all — end; permitted b'dieved. I wrote this because Levush and Maharach explained Tur's words in what he wrote "and he does not intend for wine pouring" — for its own reason, meaning it is permitted since his intent is on grape pouring and not on the wine — it is his force without intention through something else that is permitted; and accordingly even if there was complete wine there, namely already drawn — permitted; and behold this is not so, for the language of Terumat HaDeshen I mentioned does not imply thus at all; and also from the law the matter is clear, that if there was complete clear wine there — even b'dieved it would be forbidden even if he intends grape pouring; for this is worse than what we find nearby in the next seif in water dilution that is forbidden even b'dieved, for the reason is because he has closeness to the wine they decreed more; all the more here where there is greater closeness, he carries the wine itself and is occupied in his work — they were more stringent; more than other things forbidden because of decree — so it appears to me clearly. And Rashba wrote there on this that Ramban explained the reason not like Raavad but lest the non-Jew touch wine in the winepress; and the practical difference between the explanations is our winepress where there is no wine floating on the grapes — for Raavad forbidden because of clear wine and for Ramban permitted — end; therefore Tur first wrote therefore a non-Jew who brought, etc.; and nevertheless l'chatchila forbidden to do it lest he touch wine — meaning Ramban's reason which is primary as Rashba also wrote in Terumat HaDeshen; and accordingly in our winepress permitted; and afterward he brought in the name of Rashba: there is one who ruled, etc., namely Raavad; and accordingly also in our winepress forbidden; and in Perishah he wrote that what Tur first wrote the law of baskets deals with perforated ones where no wine collects at all — in this everyone agrees permitted b'dieved; unlike dardoriyot where wine remains inside from what the grapes exude — therefore there are those who forbid as the rabbi wrote nearby — end. And his honor in his place; and we say when he sleeps and lies he said this word that there is no prohibition at all b'dieved for all views even in dardoriyot; and it is the arranged Gemara that only l'chatchila forbidden; and what Tur wrote in the name of Rashba: there is one who ruled it is forbidden, etc. — meaning l'chatchila; and what he explained that baskets means perforated — this is a proper explanation, for not in vain did Tur first state baskets and afterward dardoriyot; rather Tur informs us of a further practical difference between the explanations: for according to Ramban's explanation that the reason is lest he touch wine in the winepress — l'chatchila forbidden even in perforated baskets; unlike according to Raavad's explanation that afterward there is prohibition only in dardoriyot, namely they are not perforated; and it turns out for each explanation there is stringency and leniency: for Ramban there is stringency l'chatchila even in perforated ones and leniency in our winepress where there is no wine floating on grapes; and for Raavad the reverse; and it appears to me there is further practical difference from it, namely when the non-Jew brings grapes in unperforated baskets to another utensil that is not a winepress — for Raavad's reason forbidden and for Ramban permitted; and likewise it appears from Beit Yosef's words; and in Sefer HaTerumah he wrote, etc., that he concluded what Tosafot wrote that the prohibition is specifically to a covered winepress — according to Rashba's explanation, namely in the name of Ramban; it implies for Raavad forbidden even if not covered; and it is fit for us to take the stringency of all explanations; and likewise Tur's view appears. And according to this give-and-take, if there are two leniencies — the baskets are perforated and in our winepress permitted even l'chatchila — except one who wants to be stringent may say because of "do not divide" of the rabbis; but it appears to me clearly what I wrote; and that Tur did not write l'chatchila forbidden also in throwing a stone into the winepress — there is no relevance to l'chatchila lest we are dealing with fools who will let a non-Jew throw a stone into their wine — and in vain Perishah wrote it also applies to throwing a stone; but the matter is clear that whatever the Jew needs to do an action for the winepress he should distance that action from being done through a non-Jew because of "go, go," etc.; and what is written permitted even for drinking — the reason is he does not touch with his hand the wine except through the water; and in a moist thing touch is not significant; and also stream connection does not apply here, for they said connection only in wine to wine and not in water to wine; and it appears plain this law deals even in throwing water by hand itself; and even though Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ran: it is no worse than his force without intention, etc. — he did not call this force (lest) but because the non-Jew's touch is not in the wine it is no worse than other places where we are lenient in the force of a non-Jew; and therefore they needed to say here that water to wine is not considered stream; and in Derishah he understood it deals here specifically in throwing through a utensil the water; and therefore he troubled to resolve why need the reason that there is no stream here — behold it is permitted without that, for it is touch through something else without intention; and in truth he troubled in vain in this.
