And one does not tear, etc. Meaning, not only do we not inform him lest his mind become confused, but even if he found out, how can we command him to tear? Perhaps his worry will increase. So wrote the Bach in the name of the Ran.
And one does not weep, etc. The Bach wrote: it appears that this is the explanation. Not only that, but one does not weep or eulogize before him even for a dead person who is not a relative of the sick person, so that his heart not break. Meaning, since this sick person sees that so-and-so died and they weep and eulogize him in his presence, his heart breaks from fear lest he too die. For this reason they silence comforters before him, because through the comforters he remembers the death of that person and becomes afraid lest he too die. It is also close to explain that “one does not weep or eulogize” speaks of where a relative died for him and he found out, and likewise they silence those comforting him for his own dead.
