It is written in Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 64: if a debtor failed to pay on time and the creditor warned him that the money could have been profitably invested, and that if payment was delayed the debtor would have to reimburse the lost profit, and the debtor accepted this condition, the debtor may not later refuse payment on the ground of ribbit. Rather, the correct law is that such an arrangement itself contains prohibited ribbit, contrary to Sema and Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 81. Kiddushin 6 explicitly implies this from the case where the creditor extended the payment period, and Tosafot and Rosh were compelled to explain that passage differently rather than establish it as simple compensation for lost profit. Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 34, also implies this. Beit Hillel, siman 170, notes that under certain communal commercial regulations courts sometimes award market-loss compensation through the power of hefker beit din, but under strict halachah it is prohibited. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 190, and Responsa Rosh as codified in Shulchan Aruch siman 169, seif 42.
