He wrote in Responsa Shevut Yaakov I siman 64: One who did not repay his debt at its time, and the creditor warned him that he should repay it because he would be able to profit with the money, and if not, he would have to pay him the profit; and the debtor accepted his warning and agreed to give him the profit, but now he does not want to give it because of the prohibition of ribit. The law is clear that this involves the prohibition of ribit, not like the Sema and Shach in siman 81, as is explicitly proven in Kiddushin 6, where the Gemara establishes the case as one where he extended the time for her, and Tosafot and the Rosh were forced there to explain it, rather than establishing it in such a case. So too it appears from Responsa Mishnat Binyamin siman 34. See Beit Hillel siman 170, that the custom in civil courts is to award market loss in such a case because court expropriation is effective, but by strict law it is certainly forbidden. See Responsa Chavot Yair siman 190, and so it is proven from Responsa HaRosh, which is ruled in Shulchan Aruch siman 169 seif 42 as practical law; see there.
