(And if one salted the chalchulot from the inside he returns and salts from outside and it is permitted.) And we are not concerned the inner side will absorb from the outer since it is not so retained in blood — Or Zarua, Shach. One should not explain that the inner side of fat adhering to intestines will absorb from the outer side of fat — for it is as if not salted at all and there is so much blood on the inner side of fat as on its outer side; for this reason even b'dieved forbidden and worse than a piece salted on one side that is permitted for need as hagahah siman 69 wrote. And again I saw this reasoning in Nekudot HaKesef there. But in Magen Yisrael there s.k. 23 not so; and it appears he means the inner side of the intestines that already exuded its gravy and is not busy to exude from the outer — meaning from fat that was not salted we are not concerned for this since it is not so retained in blood. And this reason is compelled for one who says intestines salted with meat are forbidden b'dieved as seif 2 wrote. And it is difficult — if so how do we salt intestines with their fat and it would be forbidden b'dieved; and likewise what the hagahah wrote the custom to forbid l'chatchila — how do we salt intestines with their fat l'chatchila? Rather one must say fat is not so retained in blood; and so I found explicitly in Tur HaChadash general rule 15 law 12.
