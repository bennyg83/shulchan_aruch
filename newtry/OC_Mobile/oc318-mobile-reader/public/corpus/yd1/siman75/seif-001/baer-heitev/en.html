Pickled — meaning specifically that presumably we do not retain blood in them, but if pickled they require salting like other meat; and Taz and Shach wrote crop is not included in intestines for this matter and blood is retained in it; and also thin membranes specifically surround the kanta, but the kanta itself has blood.
They are permitted — from the presumption if they were not pickled.
The fat — Shach wrote: and even though Mechaber wrote siman 69 that l'chatchila one must salt meat from both sides and Rambam there forbids even b'dieved — if so here when one salts only on the outer side the fat was not salted where it adheres — one may say since there is not so much blood in fat and also there are thin threads, this suffices even l'chatchila.
Treifah — Shach wrote in the name of Tur HaChadash: however if there is sixty against the fat and sinews in it everything is permitted; and even though regarding meat cooked without salting we practice to forbid that piece even when there is sixty, as above siman 69 seif 11 — nevertheless here the fat melts in the pot; and further since no blood is visible to the eye in fat one may be lenient; and therefore we also do not say absorbed prohibition, only certainly because the blood is very slight it is fit to be lenient here, and in any case absorbed prohibition is only a stringency.
Fat — Shach explained: if it is known there was fat there to forbid, but one should not forbid b'dieved because it is impossible to scrape without fat remaining.
And he salts it — Shach wrote: even after twelve hours, or even after overnight for the first opinion in a doubt whether exuding gravy is overnight — it is also permitted to return and salt it; and we are not concerned the inner side will absorb from the outer since it is not so retained in blood; and in Tur HaChadash he wrote: he returns and rinses it and salts it.
B'dieved — Shach wrote Maharshal's view is to forbid even b'dieved whether the yolk alone remained or also the white, whether the shell is hard or soft; and Taz wrote in his name that it appears it absorbs well even if completely hardened, for even earthenware salted with meat even if perforated is forbidden to eat hot food in it — all the more here; and nevertheless it appears to him it does not forbid the hen salted with it like fish and fowl, end of his words; and in his Or Zarua he wrote further that if this forbidden egg was cooked it forbids what was cooked with it until sixty; and Taz concluded his words appear primary for practical halachah. (And one should not say why we do not say like kav kav — this is not so, for we only say thus where it exudes its own gravy, unlike an egg that exudes nothing — our eyes see it exudes neither blood nor gravy; and we say regarding a spit siman 76 seif 4 that kav kav does not apply there.)
