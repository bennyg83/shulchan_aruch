The law of salting intestines. In it are 3 seifim:
Blood is not retained in the intestines — such as in the kereis, in the stomach, in the thin membranes, and in the chalchulot (meaning the karkasha, which is the intestine attached to the rectum) — without the fat on them; therefore if they were cooked in a pot without salting it is permitted unless they were pickled: {Rama: And all the more so if they were salted in a non-perforated vessel they are permitted (Mordechai chapter 25). However l'chatchila they require salting in a perforated vessel and rinsing first like other meat (Mordechai and Arukh). And some disagree regarding the kereis and say blood is retained in it, and even b'dieved the kereis and beit ha-kosot are forbidden like other meat (Mordechai, Ran, and Or Zarua).} But fat on them — its law is like other meat; therefore when salting the chalchulot and other intestines one does not salt them on the inner side beyond the food but on the outer side where the fat adheres: {Rama: And if one salted the chalchulot from the inside and not from the outside, it is as if it was not salted at all; and if it was cooked thus it is treifah if there is fat on it from outside; and if it has not yet been cooked one returns and salts it from outside and it is permitted (Arukh in the name of Rashba). Eggs found in fowl after slaughter — if their white has not finished [forming] and only the yolk remains — they require salting like other meat, and it is permitted to salt them with meat (Beit Yosef siman 87). And if the white too has finished, even the hard shell on it as it is sold in the market — the custom is to salt it, but one should be careful not to salt it with meat (Maharai in Hagahot ShaDa). However b'dieved one need not be concerned; and all fat, even of fowl, is like other meat for salting and rinsing (Mordechai).}
