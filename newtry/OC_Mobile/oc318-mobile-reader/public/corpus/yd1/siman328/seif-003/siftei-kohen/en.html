And likewise a maidservant, etc. The Bach challenged the master on this, for in the words of Maharai it is explicit that he did not permit ab initio through a maidservant, even where she is accustomed to receive permission, unless there is concern of spoilage. Where there is definite spoilage, even a woman who is not a maidservant becomes an agent without the owner’s knowledge; see there. It implies that whenever she is not a maidservant and there is no concern of spoilage, it is forbidden for her to separate without the owner’s knowledge, although she kneads the dough and works with this dough, like the Beit Yosef’s view, who challenged the Semak; so the Bach ruled. However, he wrote that nevertheless, after the fact, if the kneader separated without permission and it is pleasing to the householder that he separated, it is challah and he need not separate again. But with another person who is not the kneader, even after the fact it is not challah, and he must separate again; see there.
But another must, etc. Meaning that if another person separates for the partners, he must take permission from all owners of the dough.
Although it has not yet become obligated. Because in any event it is in his power to bring dough and say, “This dough shall be challah upon flour when it is kneaded.” Tosafot.
