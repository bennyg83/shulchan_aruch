(There, seif katan 2) He proved that the text of Terumat HaDeshen should be emended, but not correctly. It is clear that Terumat HaDeshen agrees with Bach. His statement, 'even where there is only concern that the dough may spoil, we say it is a benefit for her,' means that where there is not even such concern, he was not certain enough to permit reliance on his proofs, because there are grounds to prohibit, as stated in the gloss to that responsum. Why separate unnecessarily? This rejects all of Taz's arguments. His further claim that Terumat HaDeshen later concludes, 'and furthermore one may say that there is concern the dough will spoil,' implying that initially he spoke without such concern, is incorrect. Anyone who examines the responsum will see that this is a gloss by Terumat HaDeshen's author, and on the contrary it explains that he was certain to permit only where there was concern of spoilage.
