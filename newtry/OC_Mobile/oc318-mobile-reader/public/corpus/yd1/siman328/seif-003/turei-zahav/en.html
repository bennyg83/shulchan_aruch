And likewise a maidservant, etc. In Terumat HaDeshen siman 188 he wrote this responsum regarding a maidservant who kneaded dough for the woman, and the woman went away and was delayed until it had fully leavened; if it waits longer the dough will spoil. Because of this the maidservant wants to separate challah without the householder’s authorization, and he ruled that it is proper to do so, since the dough would be spoiled and it is a benefit for the householder. One may confer benefit upon a person in his absence, and likewise the maidservant becomes her agent even without her knowledge for this reason. Even if there is only a concern that the dough may spoil, we say that it is a benefit for her, since it is normal that sometimes the householder gives permission to separate challah even in her presence. Likewise, she has the power to appoint an agent, as stated, etc. Although in the first chapter of Chullin we say that if one said to his agent, “Go separate terumah,” and another man heard and separated, his terumah is not terumah, one may say that only there it is ineffective because there is a matter of particular preference: he wants specifically this person, who knows and recognizes with what measure the householder usually separates, generously or sparingly. But with separating challah nowadays, where all are equal in taking a kezayit, one cannot say at all that there is particular preference about who separates and gives. Furthermore, one may say that because there is concern for the spoiling of the dough, there is no particular preference at all. So too regarding a get, if we know that it is a benefit for her and that she wants the get, there is no particular preference at all as to who receives the get in her place; end quote. Now, what he wrote, “even if there is only a concern that the dough may spoil, etc.,” has no explanation, for the first clause also speaks of this. One cannot say that the first clause speaks of definite spoiling and the latter clause of a mere concern of spoiling, for what difference is there between them? Certainly even with a doubt of spoiling there is a benefit for her to remove herself from doubt. Furthermore, why does he then bring that since it is normal that sometimes she gives permission, etc.? How does this relate to a doubtful spoiling? Even without a doubtful spoiling there is permission through this, and it does not depend on doubtful spoiling. Furthermore, he wrote afterward that specifically regarding terumah there is particular preference as to who should be his agent, since not everyone knows his measure; this implies that if not for this reason it would be permitted even through another person. Furthermore, afterward he concludes: “And furthermore, one may say that there is concern for the spoiling of the dough, etc.,” implying that at first he spoke without concern for the dough spoiling. Furthermore, if we say that in the latter clause he comes to permit and innovate that even in doubtful spoiling it is permitted, why does he bring proof from a get where we know that it is a benefit for her? That implies specifically where it is known to be a benefit, while here the main novelty is that it is not certain. Rather, it is simple in my humble opinion that there is a scribal error here, and it should read: “even if there is no concern of spoiling, etc.,” meaning that there is permission in any case because it is normal that she gives permission. So it is implied that the Rama read the words of Terumat HaDeshen, for he wrote, “and likewise a maidservant, etc.,” implying even without any concern for the dough spoiling. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, disagrees with the Rama and explains the words of Terumat HaDeshen in the way I mentioned, that the latter clause speaks of a concern of spoiling and not definite spoiling, and that only in this case may the maidservant separate challah. In my humble opinion I have written that it is impossible to explain so. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the author of Terumat HaDeshen himself did not rely on this to permit without concern that the dough might spoil, for at the end he mentioned the permission specifically because of spoiling, and in the question that he arranged there in Terumat HaDeshen he mentioned only the spoiling of the dough. It is known that this is the method of Terumat HaDeshen, that he himself arranged the questions. It appears to me to bring proof from the words of the Tur at the end of this siman, based on the Semak, where he wrote that if the kneader separated without authorization, it is challah. In the Semak itself the wording is: “It appears that if the householder says that he is pleased for the kneader to separate, he may separate; and if he separated without authorization, his challah is challah, like the one who says that workers who went lengthwise and crosswise in the winepress may separate terumah without the householder’s knowledge”; end quote. That view is in the Yerushalmi, brought by the Rash in chapter 3 of Terumot on the Mishnah that says there: workers have no permission to separate terumah except for the pressers, because they make the winepress tamei. The Rash explains: am haaretz owners hired chaver workers to tread grapes in order to separate terumah in purity; they may separate before the owners touch it and make the winepress tamei. In the Yerushalmi, Rabbi Yochanan said that once they walked lengthwise and crosswise, they immediately make the pit tamei, meaning that until that time the owners are careful not to touch them, but from then on they are no longer careful, because they think the terumah has already been separated. Therefore, if they walked lengthwise and crosswise in the winepress and left it, they should be concerned that the owners touched it; end quote. The Beit Yosef challenged this proof, saying it has no relevance to challah, for there it is different, since he hired them for this purpose, to separate terumah for him in purity; end quote. Because of this he rejected it from halachah and did not bring it in the Shulchan Aruch. This is difficult for me according to the Beit Yosef: what does the Mishnah teach us that the pressers may separate without authorization? It is obvious, since he hired them for this. Furthermore, why call them unauthorized? Every agent is certainly authorized. It appears that the Semak was also troubled by this question; therefore he explained that even if at times he does not explicitly tell him to separate terumah but hires him generally to do his work, he certainly wants this. This is also the implication of the Semak’s language, where he first wrote, “if the householder says that he is pleased for the kneader to separate, etc.,” and afterward wrote, “and if he separated without authorization, etc.” One belongs with the other and refers back to the first clause: whenever initially he disclosed his mind that he is pleased, we afterward say that even if on one occasion he separated without his knowledge, it is valid. The Tur’s language too, “if the kneader separated, etc.,” can be explained this way: he regularly appoints the kneader to separate, and therefore it is effective even if on one occasion he separated without his knowledge. It appears to me as halachah that on erev Shabbat, when women are accustomed to be particular that each should take challah, and it is considered by them an obligation to make breads in honor of Shabbat for the sake of challah, no maidservant should separate without the command of the householder unless there is concern that the dough will spoil. But on the other weekdays, the maidservant or one of the household members may do so, since she regularly gives them to separate and is not particular about this. So it appears in my humble opinion.
