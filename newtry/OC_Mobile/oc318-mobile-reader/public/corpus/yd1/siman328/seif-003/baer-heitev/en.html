Maidservant. The Bach challenged the Rav on this, for Maharai did not permit lechatchilah for a maidservant, even one who is regularly given permission, except where there is concern of spoilage; then certainly even a woman who is not a maidservant becomes her agent without her knowledge. It implies that if she is not a maidservant and there is no spoilage, it is forbidden to separate without her knowledge, even though she kneads the dough. However, he wrote that after the fact, if the kneader separated without permission and the owner is pleased that he separated, it is challah and one need not separate again; but another person who is not the kneader, even after the fact, has not made challah and one must separate again. End quote from Shach. The Taz discusses this law at length and concludes that in his view the halakhah is as follows: on Friday, when women are particular that each take challah herself, no maidservant should separate without the housewife's command unless there is concern that the dough will spoil. Birkei Yosef wrote that even if the dough will spoil on Friday, the maidservant has no permission to separate challah; but on other weekdays the maidservant or one of the household may separate challah, since she is accustomed to give them permission to separate and is not particular about it. End quote.
