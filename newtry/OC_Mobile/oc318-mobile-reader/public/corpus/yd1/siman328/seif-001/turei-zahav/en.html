Or to separate challah. Maharshal writes that the custom is to say both “terumah” and “challah.” This is puzzling, because if “challah” is the principal term, inserting “terumah” may interrupt between the blessing and the mitzvah. Semak explicitly says either term may be used. Women who use a different formula on Pesach than throughout the year are certainly mistaken. It is also sufficient to say “to separate challah”; there is no need to add “from the dough.” Although challah must indeed be separated from dough rather than flour, a blessing need not recite every legal detail.
