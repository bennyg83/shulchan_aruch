He writes that by strict law it is exempt from challah because it is not fit for Jewish consumption. Nevertheless, it should be rabbinically obligated because of marit ayin, like dog food at the end of siman 330, where even if shepherds do not eat it, if it has the appearance of bread it requires challah because of appearance. Still, no blessing should be recited. One who wishes to be stringent and separate with a blessing can follow a practice he received from his teachers in such doubtful blessing cases: recite the verses of "David blessed Hashem" through words containing Hashem's Name and kingship, and then conclude, "Blessed is He Who commanded us to separate challah." This avoids a concern of a blessing in vain. He further writes that Pnei Yehoshua on Berachot 12 appears to permit saying, "Berich Rachmana Mara Who commanded us to separate challah," because the Divine Name and kingship are not stated in Hebrew. However, this is not conclusive, since even in another language one may violate the prohibition against pronouncing Hashem's Name in vain.

Magen Avraham, siman 8, seif katan 2, writes that separating challah is not such a direct positive act because it is done to make one's food permissible, and therefore the blessing may be recited seated. According to this, even if she is clothed she could bless while seated. Yeshuot Yaakov, by the Gaon of Lviv, rejects this and distinguishes: where one can stand, one should not sit. Tzitzit is blessed standing for this reason. A naked woman cannot stand modestly, so she may bless seated because the normal reason to avoid sitting is that it appears disrespectful and lacking proper awe; where necessity compels the posture, there is no disrespect.
