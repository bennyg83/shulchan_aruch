He recites a blessing. See Peri Tevuah responsa siman 66 regarding bread made from flour that became wormy with mites. He wrote that by strict law it is exempt from challah, since it is not fit for a Jew to eat, but one should obligate it rabbinically because of marit ayin, as below at the end of siman 330 regarding dog-dough: even where the shepherds do not eat from it, if it has the form of bread it is obligated in challah because of marit ayin. However, one should not recite a blessing; see what I wrote above in siman 13 seif 2 regarding a ben pekuah, whether one recites a blessing on its shechitah. One who wants to be stringent and separate with a blessing should do as I received from my teachers in all such cases: say, in the form of prayer, the verses of "And David blessed: Blessed are You, Hashem, God of Israel," etc., through "Yours, Hashem, is the kingdom," which contain Name and kingship, and then conclude, "Blessed is He who commanded us to separate challah." In this way there is no concern of a blessing in vain. He further wrote that from the words of Penei Yehoshua in Berakhot 12 it appears that one may say "Berikh Rachamana Mara who commanded us to separate challah," for there too there is no vain mention of Name and kingship, since it is in Aramaic, and nevertheless he fulfills the blessing. But his words are not compelling, for one may say that even in a secular language the prohibition of mentioning the Name in vain applies; see there.
