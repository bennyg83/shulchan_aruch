Terumah. As it is written, “you shall lift terumah.” In biblical language, challah means the dough, as in “loaves mixed,” and many similar expressions; but in the language of the Mishnah it is everywhere called challah. This is what he means by “or,” etc. Nevertheless, it is proper to bless in biblical language, as stated at the beginning of Berachot chapter 6: Rabbi Yehudah says “borei minei desha'im,” and he does not say “vegetables” in the language of the Mishnah.
And this is where they are, etc. The Rambam and Tur. It implies this even according to what we rule, that buttocks are not ervah, as stated in Berachot there, “this one turns his face,” etc.; so too the Yerushalmi says here, “this is what you said: buttocks,” etc., brought by the Rash and the Rosh. It appears that the Rambam did not have the text “shall we say this supports,” but rather “this supports,” etc. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak's explanation stands on its own: even if her face is pressed down, the buttocks are still visible.
And in a place, etc. For he fulfilled with the first challah, as written at the beginning of siman 322, “and this reishit,” etc.
