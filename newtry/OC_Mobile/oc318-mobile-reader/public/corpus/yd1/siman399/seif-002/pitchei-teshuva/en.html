Private matters. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 52, that a mourner is forbidden to perform Birkat Kohanim. Magen Avraham, siman 129, seif katan 66, rules likewise.
And after the festival. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit in the name of Magen Avraham, that he puts on tefillin on the first day. Responsa Shevut Yaakov II, siman 25, disagrees and maintains that he does not put on tefillin on the first day, and on the second day he waits until after sunrise, as during an ordinary mourning period. Shaarei Teshuvah to Orach Chaim there writes that on the second day, before sunrise, there appears to be room for leniency.
He begins counting seven days. See Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 59, that one who buried his deceased during a festival and observes seven days after the festival is joined by his relatives in mourning with him. They must also change their garments on the first Shabbat after the festival, contrary to one authority who wished to exempt them in such a case.
From the final second day of Yom Tov. See Baer Heitev and the new Responsa Bach, siman 9, which argues at length for this view: the second day of Rosh Hashanah counts toward the seven days, and likewise a second day of Yom Tov in the Diaspora that falls on Shabbat counts, even though in Eretz Yisrael the mourner could not have observed mourning on that day either. He therefore completes only six additional days. Responsa Radbaz I, siman 61, likewise rules that the second day of Rosh Hashanah counts, as does the end of Sefer Masat Binyamin. Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon I, siman 98, disagrees. It distinguishes where the second day itself is the day of death and burial, in which case it counts, from where death occurred on the first day, so that the second day carries no Torah-level mourning and does not count. He interprets Radbaz's ruling that it counts as referring specifically to the second day being the day of death and burial. Rabbi Michael HaKohen agreed with him; see there.
