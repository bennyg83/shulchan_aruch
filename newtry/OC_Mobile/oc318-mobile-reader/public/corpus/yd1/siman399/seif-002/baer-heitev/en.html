The Festival. The same applies to Yom Tov Sheni or to Yom Tov Rishon when he wishes to bury him through non-Jews — the laws of aninuth apply to him as explained above in siman 341 se’if 1, and after he is buried all these laws apply to him — end of the Siftei Kohen:
Shavuot. The Siftei Kohen wrote: and Shabbat counts only as one day when he was buried during the Festival; and specifically when he was buried before Sukkot does Shabbat also count toward seven days (and see below se’if 11) — that is not the case here. And not like Maharsha”l in responsa who wrote that it cancels — he does not cancel mourning, but for counting the seven toward the thirty, Shabbat counts even if he was buried during the Festival. However, in Responsa Masa’at Binyamin he ruled like Maharsha”l, and so appears to be the view of the Drisha — end of his words.
