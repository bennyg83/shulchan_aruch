(Siman 399 in Taz s.k. 4) He challenged Maharshal, but there is no difficulty at all. From the words of the Rambam and from the question of the Talmud nothing is proven, for they speak of another festival, such as Shavuot or the seven days of the festival themselves. But if he was buried during the days of the festival, meaning buried before Shemini Atzeret, that is not what they discuss, and one may say that since Shemini Atzeret has an additional distinction, that it counts as seven, the same applies in such a case. Nor is there proof from the Baal HaMaor; on the contrary, one may say the opposite, as Maharshal wrote, that he is stringent only because the person did not know and did not hear, but one who knew, it is obvious that it counts toward the seven. What he wondered, that Maharshal did not examine the words of the Baal HaMaor, is no wonder with respect to the essential law. For although Maharshal was not precise about the identities of the authorities and confused the view of the lenient authorities with that of the Baal HaMaor, nevertheless from those who are lenient regarding news heard after the festival, Maharshal understood that the same applies in such a case, and that the stringent authorities agree. Also what he wrote, “and more than this I wondered about Responsa Maharar Binyamin, etc., that he did not examine the body of the law, and this should not be done, etc.” I say that the Mishnat Binyamin did examine the body of the law and wrote well. This is the language of Mishnat Binyamin there: “My teacher Maharshal already answered that it properly counts as seven, like the words of the Baal HaMaor, and not like some of the great authorities who hold that this is only when he buried his dead before the festival, but if he buried him during the festival days, Shemini Atzeret counts only as one day”; end quote. He does not mean that the Baal HaMaor is lenient regarding news after the festival, but rather that even the Baal HaMaor agrees that in such a case, where he heard during the festival, Shemini Atzeret counts toward the seven, as Maharshal wrote. “Not like some of the great authorities” refers to Ribam and Tosafot of Maharam whom Maharshal brought there. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion, nothing is proven from the Baal HaMaor, neither in this direction nor in that direction. Examine carefully.
