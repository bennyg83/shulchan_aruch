On chol ha-moed. The same applies on the second day of Yom Tov, or on the first day of Yom Tov where he wants to bury him through non-Jews: aninut takes effect upon him, as above in siman 341 seif 1. After he is buried, all these laws apply to him.
We do not say that Shemini Atzeret cancels. Shemini Atzeret counts only as one day when he buried during the festival. So wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot, brought by the Beit Yosef. Specifically when he buried before Sukkot does Shemini Atzeret also count as seven, below in seif 11, unlike here. So too the Bach wrote in the name of Tosafot and Mordechai and ruled this way, unlike Maharshal in responsum siman 5, who wrote that it does cancel; that is, even if it does not cancel, nevertheless for the count of seven with respect to the thirty, Shemini Atzeret counts even if he buried during the festival. However, Masat Binyamin responsa siman 71 ruled like Maharshal; see there. So too appears to be the view of the Derishah.
And in places where they observe two days, etc. The Bach wrote at the end of this siman that the same applies to the two days of Rosh Hashanah, such as where his dead died on the first day of Rosh Hashanah: he counts the seven from the second day of Yom Tov, for it too is rabbinic; see there.
