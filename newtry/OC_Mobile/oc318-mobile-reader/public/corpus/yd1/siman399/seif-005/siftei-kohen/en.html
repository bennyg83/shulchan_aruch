It is permitted to launder. The decree of seven days has already been annulled, but haircutting remains forbidden because the thirty-day decree has not been annulled when fewer than seven days preceded the festival.
It is forbidden until night. Because the act can be performed after dark.
And this is the custom. Terumat HaDeshen 287 writes that even if the death occurred on Friday and that Shabbat is the eve of a festival, bathing on Friday is permitted according to our custom. Although Rama elsewhere records customs prohibiting bathing and laundering for thirty days, those are stringencies, not the strict law. In honor of the festival the custom is not to be stringent. Maharam likewise permitted hot bathing near evening even after only one hour of mourning, despite his general view that bathing is forbidden for thirty days. Rama's stricter ruling in Orach Chaim 548 describes what would follow if the thirty-day restriction were strict law. Here he records the actual custom. The practical distinction is that after the festival, bathing and laundering remain forbidden until thirty days, because the festival annulled only the seven-day decree. [Citation 400.]
