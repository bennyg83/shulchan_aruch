The custom. Terumat HaDeshen wrote that even if his relative died on Friday and that Shabbat is the eve of the festival, according to our custom he may bathe on Friday. Do not ask how the Rav wrote “and so is the custom,” when above at the beginning of siman 381 he wrote that our custom is to prohibit bathing all thirty, and likewise at the beginning of siman 389 he wrote that we have the custom to prohibit laundering all thirty. One can answer that this is only a stringency, while by strict law it is permitted; here, because of the honor of the festival, they did not practice stringency. So wrote the Levush. Although in Orach Chayim 548:8 and 548:10 the Rav wrote that for us, who practice a prohibition of bathing all thirty, it is forbidden to bathe, for the festival nullified only the decree of seven, and the same applies to laundering in places where laundering is prohibited all thirty, that is because such is the proper ruling if the prohibition of bathing and laundering all thirty were by law. But here he writes that nevertheless the custom is not so. So wrote the Bach. It still must be explained why the Rav wrote that there if in the end the custom is not so. It appears to me that he holds that the custom is not so because of the honor of the festival; therefore, where there is no honor of the festival, it is forbidden. The practical difference is that after the festival, bathing and laundering are forbidden until thirty, for the festival nullified only the decree of seven. Examine carefully. End quote from Shach.
