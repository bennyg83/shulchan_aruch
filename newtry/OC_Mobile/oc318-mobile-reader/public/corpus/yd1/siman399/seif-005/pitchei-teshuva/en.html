From the final second day of Yom Tov. See Be'er Heitev. See the new Bach responsa siman 9, who discusses at length to support this view, that the second day of Rosh Hashanah counts for him, and likewise the second day of the diaspora festival that falls on Shabbat, even though even in Eretz Yisrael he could not have observed mourning on that day; nevertheless it counts for him and he completes six days after it; see there. So too Radbaz responsa I siman 61 ruled that the second day of Rosh Hashanah counts; see there. So too at the end of Masat Binyamin. However, in Perach Mateh Aharon I siman 98 he did not rule this way, and distinguished: where the second day is the day of death and burial, it counts; but if he died on the first day, so that on the second day there is no Torah-level mourning, then it does not count. He explains the above Radbaz this way too, that when he wrote that it counts, he meant where it was the day of death and burial. Rabbi Michael HaKohen z"l agreed with him; see there.
