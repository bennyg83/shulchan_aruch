An hour. Meaning any amount, and we do not require a full halachic hour, and even removing the shoe alone, having observed it briefly, suffices. And so wrote in Responsa Maharam Brisk. Taz and Siftei Kohen:
Wearing. He wrote in Responsa Maharam Brisk: a case occurred involving a groom who married on the eve of a Festival, and during the Festival one of his relatives died; and he ruled that even though a groom in general who experienced a bereavement during the seven days of celebration does not count thirty days until after the days of celebration — for the reason that he is more privileged than the Festival, since he is permitted in laundering and haircutting — that is not the case with this groom, whose days of celebration fall during the Festival, and consequently he is in any case forbidden in laundering etc. on account of the Festival; therefore the Festival counts toward his thirty days. And the Taz wrote that this is very surprising to him, for the sages only said it counts when there is a prohibition on account of his mourning, but that same prohibition also applies on account of the Festival — in that case it counts properly, since in any event he is observing the prohibition, and he can say: I am observing the prohibition on account of the mourning; but that is not the case with this groom, who has no obligation of mourning at all, and what he observes as a prohibition is on account of the Festival alone — how can it enter the mind to say that this should be counted as days of thirty. Therefore it appears that this ruling is incorrect, and rather the Festival days do not count for him at all, as with any other groom etc., see there. And regarding what the author stated that the Festival days count toward the thirty, the Siftei Kohen wrote that this means even when the death became known to him during the Festival, even though it was not known to him before the Festival, and not as written in the Beit Yosef that only when it became known before the Festival does it count — but perhaps the Beit Yosef also did not mean that precisely, or it is a scribal error and it should read “during the Festival” — end of his words:
His nails. This is according to the view of the author in Orach Chaim siman 532; but we rule strictly as the Rama wrote there, and he left the matter unstated here since this is not the place for the laws of the Festival. Siftei Kohen:
