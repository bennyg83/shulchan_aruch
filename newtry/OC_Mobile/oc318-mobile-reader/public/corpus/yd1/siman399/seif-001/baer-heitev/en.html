To wear. Responsa Masat Binyamin discusses a groom who married on Erev Yom Tov and experienced a bereavement during the festival. Ordinarily, if a groom becomes a mourner during the seven wedding days, he does not begin counting the thirty-day mourning period until after the seven wedding days, because wedding days are stronger than a festival: a groom is permitted pressed garments and haircutting, while a festival itself restricts these activities. Masat Binyamin argues that where the wedding days themselves fall during the festival, the groom is in any event forbidden pressed garments and haircutting because of the festival, and therefore those days should count toward the thirty days. Taz strongly rejects this. The festival counts only where the person actually bears a mourning prohibition that overlaps with the festival prohibition, so he can say the restriction he observes also reflects mourning. Here the groom has no mourning obligation at all during his wedding days; his restrictions arise only from the festival. Those days therefore cannot count toward the thirty-day period. Taz concludes that the festival days do not count for him, just as with any other groom. Shach writes that Mechaber's rule that festival days count toward the thirty applies even when one first hears of the death during the festival, not only when he knew before the festival, unlike the wording in Beit Yosef. Perhaps Beit Yosef did not mean to limit the law, or the text should read "during the festival.
