Even if he did not observe. Tur. The festival nevertheless counts toward the seven days, as explained below in siman 402, seif 7, and in Orach Chayyim.
Rather, he observes during the festival. It is like burial occurring during the festival, as explained in Orach Chayyim and in the Gemara concerning one who buried his dead two days before the festival according to the first Tanna. (Likkut) Rather, he observes during the festival. Ketubot 4a supports this, unlike Rambam, who writes that no mourning is observed during the festival at all. It appears Rambam had a variant reading "Shabbos" instead of "festival," comparable to Moed Katan 24a. Yet the context itself indicates the festival, because the days of chuppah are compared to a festival. Tosafot there and the Tosefta likewise say that a mourner does not observe public mourning during the festival but does observe private restrictions in his home. Evel Rabbati and the Yerushalmi say the same. The Yerushalmi explains that festivals interrupt mourning because marital relations are permitted during them, unlike Shabbos, then challenges that private mourning on a festival includes prohibition of marital relations. It ultimately explains that seven days cannot pass without Shabbos but can pass without a festival. See Torat HaAdam and Ritva.
And not by virtue of. See Beer HaGolah. This is unlike Rashi's explanation there. If Rashi were correct, the festival should count toward the seven days as well because private mourning and communal involvement are present. Rather, the phrase means that all thirty-day restrictions are practiced, while the public seven-day restrictions are not. Mahari Weil writes similarly. See the beginning of siman 400 in the gloss and Shach there. Torat HaAdam explains that these thirty-day laws are passive prohibitions and some of them are already festival restrictions, so observing all of them does not publicly mark him as a mourner. (Likkut) And not by virtue of. Proof is that even some seven-day restrictions are observed, such as not laundering one's clothing and receiving communal attention, yet because mourning is not observed in its proper form the days do not count. Torat HaAdam and Milchamot.
