One who buries, etc. See Orach Chaim, siman 548, and my comments there.
Even if he did not observe, etc. The Tur, because the day counts toward the seven days, as stated below, siman 402, seif 7, and in Orach Chaim there.
Rather, he observes during the festival, etc. It is as though he buried the deceased during the festival, as stated in Orach Chaim there and in the Gemara concerning one who buried his dead two days before the festival according to the first Tanna.
(Supplement) Rather, he observes during the festival, etc. As stated in Kesubos 4a, unlike the Rambam in chapter 10, who writes that no mourning is observed at all during a festival. It appears that he read 'Shabbos' instead of 'festival,' as in Moed Katan 24a. But the matter is proved from the passage itself, because marriage is compared to a festival. See Tosafos there, s.v. Mesaye'a. The Tosefta, chapter 2, likewise states that a mourner may not mourn openly during the festival, but observes mourning privately in his home. Evel Rabbasi, chapter 7, similarly states that during a festival he is as though not a mourner, yet observes privately. The Yerushalmi, on the Mishnah that Shabbos counts but festivals interrupt, gives the reason in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that marital relations are permitted, meaning on a festival, unlike Shabbos. It asks: did they not say there is no mourning on a festival, except that he observes privately? What is private observance? He is forbidden marital relations. It answers in the name of Rabbi Elazar: seven days can occur without a festival, but cannot occur without Shabbos. See Toras HaAdam, Milchamos at the end of Moed Katan, and Ritva to Moed Katan 23b. (End of supplement.)
Not by virtue of, etc. See Be'er HaGolah, unlike Rashi there, s.v. Deha Nahaga. According to Rashi, the mitzvos of the seven days should also count, since private practices are observed and the public attends to him. One must therefore explain that the phrase refers to all the restrictions of thirty days, because only practices within seven days are public prohibitions. Mahari Weil writes likewise. See below at the beginning of siman 400, the gloss and Shach, seif katan 2. Toras HaAdam and Milchamos explain that the reason is that these are prohibitions requiring passivity, and the festival itself also imposes some of them. Therefore, when he observes all of them as mourning, his mourning is not conspicuous. This implies that only matters fulfilled through refraining from action count, unlike Mahari Weil.
(Supplement) Not by virtue of, etc. Proof is that he observes even part of the seven-day restrictions, for he does not launder his clothing and the public attends to him. Yet because he does not observe mourning according to its law, it does not count. See Toras HaAdam and Milchamos at the end of Moed Katan. (End.) See siman 80. See siman 96. See siman 150.
