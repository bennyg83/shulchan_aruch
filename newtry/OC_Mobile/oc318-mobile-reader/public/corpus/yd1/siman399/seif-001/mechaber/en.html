The laws of mourning during a festival, Rosh HaShanah, and second days of diaspora. And it contains 14 seifim. One who buries his dead before the festival in a manner that mourning applies to him and he observed it — even one hour before the festival — the festival interrupts mourning and nullifies for him the decree of seven; and festival days count toward thirty — behold seven before festival, festival days, and he completes thirty after them; specifically if he observed mourning at that hour, even if he observed only private matters; such as if he heard close tidings on Shabbat that would be erev festival — even though he observes only private matters, festival interrupts; but if he erred (or) intentionally did not observe mourning, or it was close to dark and he could not observe — festival does not nullify mourning; and all the more if he did not know of the death before festival — festival does not nullify; rather he observes private matters during festival and counts seven after festival; in those seven his work is done through others and his slaves and maidservants work in private in his house, for since he was already nullified during festival days from work — even if not due to mourning but festival — ultimately he observed mourning law regarding work; therefore one need not be strict like other mourning; his work through others; all festival days many occupy themselves comforting him — therefore they do not comfort him after festival; festival counts toward thirty, for thirty-day laws — pressing, haircut — apply during festival; and not from festival law alone are they forbidden, but also from mourning law, for from festival law permitted to wear new white pressed garments and take nails with scissors and rejoice in friendship joy; and one coming from overseas, captivity, and the rest the Sages listed — permitted to cut hair and launder; but from mourning law forbidden in all like weekday.
