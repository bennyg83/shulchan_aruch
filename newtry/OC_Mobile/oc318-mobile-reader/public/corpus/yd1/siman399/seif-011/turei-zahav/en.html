Behold, twenty-two. It is difficult: let Shemini Atzeret be counted as a festival in its own right also regarding cancelling the thirty days, since the seven had been cancelled before the festival, and the festival is counted as seven, making fourteen, as we say regarding Yom Kippur in seif 10. The Rosh answered that here it is different, because he did not observe the law of thirty at all. Although he observed the prohibition of pressing clothes and haircutting, nevertheless, even without mourning these are forbidden because of the festival. It requires study to answer this according to the Ramban, who holds that there is an additional mourning prohibition beyond the festival prohibition, as mentioned above in this siman, seif 1. If so, it is difficult: why should Shemini Atzeret not cancel the thirty, since he already observed the law of thirty through the additional prohibition? It appears that nevertheless there is a difference from the ordinary thirty days on weekdays. There, the recognizable sign is in the essential law of pressing clothes and haircutting; here, the recognizable sign is only in the addition, and that is not such strong proof. It is enough that we say the festival counts toward the thirty, but not that it should be considered as having actually observed part of the thirty so that Shemini Atzeret should come and entirely cancel the remainder of the thirty. So it appears to me.
