And some say if there is a wound in the wall, etc. This is the view of Rabbenu Ephraim that the Rosh and Tur bring; and Maharshal wrote on it: what shall I do — he wants to decide against all the great rishonim and acharonim who explained red as kosher in all shades; and so Beit Yosef — this is kosher for all the other great ones; and therefore in a place of great loss one may rely on them; and it appears that even according to Rabbenu Ephraim who is stringent when there is a wound in the wall — nevertheless he agrees that if after inflation it returns to lung appearance — what is different from disqualifying appearances? It is established for them that they are on account of a flaw and are like punctured; nevertheless when they return to lung appearance it is kosher; and the same here — we are concerned for puncture on account of redness and the wound in the wall dominated; but if it did not redden — there is no prohibition on account of a wound in the wall; and whatever redness departs after inflation — it is as if it was not red at all, and we attribute the redness to something else; and so from the words of Rosh and Tur who distinguish for Rabbenu Ephraim between redness from itself that is kosher and redness on account of a wound in the wall that is treifah; and just as "from itself" deals in every regard even after inflation — so too "on account of wound" — the redness is in every regard, and then specifically treifah. And in hagahot of the R"tz in examinations he wrote: for treifot of redness for Rabbenu Ephraim — whitening after inflation does not help; and he wrote that Maharit and Maharak wrote thus; and so in Yoreh De'ah siman 39 — end. And behold — the response of those close before us, namely the words of the Tur — you learn that also those distant from us whose words are not well known to us — it is not true; where did the Tur write this? And on the contrary, at first glance it is proven the opposite in this siman, and as written nearby; and certainly Maharak and Maharit whom he brought certainly erred in their words, for they did not write thus. And whence shall we come to be stringent for concern of this flaw more than all flaws that Rashi wrote explicitly in Piskei Avot that no change disqualifies except after inflation? And all the more so since it is already explained that this treifah is complete permission for all poskim except Rabbenu Ephraim — certainly the Rosh — one should not lean to be stringent at all. So is found one hagahah — a received tradition on black appearance that is not a flaw until it enters within both membranes and the flesh; and one says: I am Israel of Bruna — one may not rely on a matter that is neither in the Gemara nor in poskim — end; and Maharshal brought this in his book and in Darkei Moshe; and it appears simple that the Rav Maharar Yisrael of Bruna disagrees with all that he mentioned first — namely also that which he wrote that it enters the membranes (for it does not enter the membranes at all) but rather the appearance from above on the lung is a disqualifying appearance and treifah; for if he disagreed only with what he wrote "and entered the flesh" — he would not have needed to mention the membranes at all; and so it appears from the words of the Talmud and poskim who mention only lung appearance and did not mention that one should peel the upper skin and see in the lower if the appearance entered. And further proof from that which is stated in Piskei Avot (daf 46): R' Yirmiyah did not want to permit lung in a market of vegetable sellers; and likewise he did not want to declare treifah, since he did not hear from his teacher — as Rashi explained there — it implies he holds for himself to forbid even though he did not see the plants except from outside, and perhaps they did not enter the membranes at all; therefore for us it would be the same for disqualifying appearances according to our halachah. And so we find in Darusha siman 57 seif 13: when the signs reddened — and it implies even if only one skin reddened, because redness weakens quickly and eventually punctures — so too here with flaw of black appearance; and so siman 52 — they were stringent regarding intestines even from outside alone; and so the R"tz in hagahah in examinations; but there are those who disagree to permit if both skins did not redden — not a correct matter. He further wrote in that hagahah: an incident with a lung that on its back was one thread [black], thick as two threads and long as a finger-measure; I examined by rubbing with the hand and at the head of the thread was one bubble full of pus; I felt that there was blood in it like in the tendons of the neck; I cut the thread below until blood came out to see if it would change to white; the appearance remained as at first and I declared it treifah immediately — end. See what is written in siman (32) [39] at its end. Simple matter: even in fowl we disqualify appearances like in behemah; and so I saw in the name of Maharshal.
