(Siman 38 seif 1) A lung in which is found any amount black as ink — treifah. The Taz brought one hagahah that wrote: received tradition on black appearance that is not a flaw until it enters within both membranes and the flesh. And the Taz ruled: even though the appearance did not enter within the membrane but only stands from above — also treifah. And Peri Chadash concludes: in any case we require that the appearance enter within both membranes. And behold — at first glance one may challenge Peri Chadash from that which is stated in the Talmud Piskei Avot (daf 46): Rava said — lung that reddened — part of it kosher, all of it treifah; and we conclude ela not so; and Hagahah seif 4 ruled like Rosh and R' Ephraim — not so and treifah; and so Tosafot in the name of R' Chananel and Shulchan Shel Avraham. And according to Peri Chadash it is difficult — how does reasoning come to declare treifah for red appearance? Behold the flaw is not recognizable except in the outer skin, while the inner skin without blaw is red; as is stated in the Talmud and poskim, and as written in siman 36; and as we say in Worms siman 33 seif 5: if both are red — there is no flaw here except in one skin; and as Rosh wrote at the beginning of Piskei Avot. And therefore there are poskim there to be lenient. But after examining well the Talmud and Rashi there — it is proven explicitly: according to the view that declares treifah for redness — it is because this came on account of congealed blood of the lung through a blow; and it is considered complete lung skin — impossible to redden the skin from outside without puncturing lung skin from the inside, and eventually the whole will puncture and emerge. If so, the flaw begins from within and dominates both membranes — and it comes out well. And the reason for this hagahah that the Taz brings — in my humble opinion: because one must understand that which we say in the Talmud: black red is only that it was flawed. And it is difficult — for the method of most poskim and Shulchan Aruch and Hagahah from them implies: redness without a wound in the wall is kosher — it is not on account of a wound that it is red; if so, how is it possible that such red becomes black, since red returns healthy and when it becomes black it was flawed? One must say: thus is established for them — black comes on account of red that was flawed before, namely blood congealed within lung flesh and punctured its skin from within, and this red did not return healthy and was flawed and turned black. And so is in Niddah (daf 19): blood of a wound when uprooted becomes black. If so, if it were so it would need to be black in both membranes and flesh where blood congealed and turned black; and since black is found only above — it is known it does not come from red that was flawed and congealed blood and skin punctured from within; rather it comes on account of congealed blood or the like in a healthy lung from outside — kosher. This is what was said to advocate for this hagahah.
