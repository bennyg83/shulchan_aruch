Limb or flesh, etc. — in the Gemara we say that there is in it only the mitzvah of separation; and according to the Rambam, this means there are no lashes, but there is d'oraisa prohibition; and Mishneh Lamelech gave the reason like half a measure that was included from kol, and nevertheless it was included only for prohibition and not for lashes; and Peri Chadash rejected: since there is no measure, eating is not relevant, therefore there are no lashes; and Mishneh Lamelech's words are correct: let it be resolved half a measure — behold according to the one who says it is a creature by itself, it was also included in Yoma from kol, and there is in it only prohibition and not lashes — what will Peri Chadash and Mishneh Lamelech say on this; one of them took it; only we learn that it was included for prohibition and not for lashes; and the same applies to dangling limb. And the view of Tosafot and Rashi and most commentators: their view is mitzvah of separation — forbidden rabbinically to separate from d'oraisa prohibition; therefore; but d'oraisa prohibition does not exist in them; and Peri Chadash brought for the Rambam's words proof from Tosafot Avodah Zarah that teaches dangling limb and flesh are forbidden for Israel and for a child of Noah — and if it is a rabbinical commandment, what is its nature to forbid a child of Noah; but one must understand this mitzvah of separation — we derive in the Gemara from the extension of flesh in the field treifah you shall not eat; and what relevance does a child of Noah have to this verse flesh in the field treifah; and see what I wrote that we stood in the name of Kesef Mishneh and Rashba on flesh that separated from a living animal that is born from flesh in the field treifah — what relevance does a child of Noah have to it; and all that I wrote in its reconciliation does not apply here; therefore it appears according to Rashi's explanation that the rabbis forbade limb and dangling flesh, and they relied on a verse — one can say that its prohibition they equated to full eivar min hachai, that also for a child of Noah it should be forbidden so there would not be a difference between it and full eivar min hachai. And this reasoning several authors wrote; see above siman 55; and for the Rambam it must deal with that he cut into dangling limbs in the animal beforehand, and he cut them after slaughter, and the verse was extended to forbid them; and for a child of Noah they have no slaughter — forbidden also, for who will permit them; and even for the one who permits still convulsing because of who is there anything — but in this even for Israel it is forbidden; and examine, for it is correct; I elaborated a bit here since the Shulchan Aruch abbreviated, and the laws themselves of Torah — I said it is good to elaborate a bit and to awaken the ear of learners.
