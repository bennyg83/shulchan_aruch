A limb from a living animal. In Tur it is written that it applies only in pure animals — and it is difficult: what practical difference is there, for an impure animal is forbidden in any case; and the Tur is not teaching the law of lashes, but teaches us a practical difference regarding giving to children of Noah — for eivar min hachai is forbidden to him; therefore it is forbidden for Israel to give it to him because of lifnei iver etc.; and he teaches us that this is only in pure animals, but an impure animal may be given to him, for he is not warned regarding this. It is further difficult: why did the Tur need to write that there is a prohibition of eating even less than an olive-bulk, for in all the Torah we hold that half a measure is forbidden from the Torah for eating; and what did he write here that he needed to write it; and one must also explain the expression therefore that the Tur wrote — it has no explanation; apparently also that which he wrote afterward, flesh that separated from a living animal is forbidden — if so, why did he need at first to write regarding a limb that has only flesh that it is forbidden; and it appears to me that the Tur came to warn lest we err to say that here there is no prohibition at all in less than an olive-bulk, from that which Rav said in the Gemara: eivar min hachai requires that there be in it an olive-bulk — what is the reason? Eating is written regarding it; and it appears at first glance that regarding prohibition too he said thus, that in less than an olive-bulk there is no prohibition at all; therefore he teaches us that it refers specifically to lashes; and the main intent of the Tur is to say lest we err to explain that which is written and flesh in the field — the intent is also on the prohibition of eivar min hachai; that is, the verse you shall not eat the soul applies to a limb that has flesh, sinews, and bones; and the verse and flesh in the field applies to a limb that has only flesh; but anything that is not a limb and is flesh has no prohibition at all — it teaches us that we do not explain thus, but from the verse of the soul it is well implied even if there is in it only flesh, for all is included in the first verse; for from that verse we learn prohibition for domesticated animal, undomesticated animal, and fowl, from the reason that the verse deals with every soul; and that it does not apply in impure animals is that the verse excluded them in that verse: whatever its flesh is permitted, its limbs are forbidden, as is in the Gemara; and since we learn from this verse that for all matters he speaks, automatically we know to forbid even in a limb that is flesh alone; and if so, necessarily the verse and flesh in the field applies even if it is not a limb, as explained.
