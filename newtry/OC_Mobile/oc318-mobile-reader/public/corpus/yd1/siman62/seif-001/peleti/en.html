Eivar min hachai, etc. — the Rambam's view: one who plucks from a limb that has no bone, even part of it, is liable; and Peri Chadash wrote that his proof is from that which we say chelev is like eivar min hachai, and the novelty is that the Torah permitted it, etc. And this is not proof, for according to all it is forbidden — it is a case of half a measure; only Raavad holds there are no lashes, and chelev that one thought was for prohibition not for lashes — only for prohibition in general, like half a measure; and also according to the one who says taste is like essence d'oraisa, one receives lashes for it — if so, if a whole limb from a living animal gave taste in a pot, is there no lashes for it? And we do not say to him: you should have cut from the limb, he does not receive lashes — why for its taste would he receive lashes? And necessarily, since according to all it is d'oraisa, it is as if he ate the whole limb whole, as in the baraita; and if so, chelev too one thought is forbidden because of impure animals to forbid their broth and sauce; and requires further study. And see what I wrote above siman 43, see there.
And fowl — and the Rambam at the end of Laws of Kings wrote that fowl appears that a child of Noah is not executed for it; it teaches that he is not warned from the Torah, for its warning is its death regarding a child of Noah; and see responsa Emunat Shmuel that he also wrote on this; and behold Raavad challenged him; and there is a practical difference for practical law whether it is permitted to give a limb from a living animal from fowl to a child of Noah — whether there is lifnei iver do not place a stumbling block, or not. And behold there is some proof for the Rambam from that which Tosafot challenged Chullin daf 33 s.v. echad nochri etc. — they challenged R' Meir who holds eivar min hachai does not apply in undomesticated animal and in fowl for Israel, but for an idolater it applies — who is there anything forbidden for an idolater and permitted for Israel; and Tosafot answered: nevertheless it is forbidden for Israel because it requires slaughter etc., end of his words; and it is difficult for the Rambam according to the one who says slaughter is not from the Torah — if so, prohibition of limb without slaughter also does not apply; behold he can pluck a limb and eat it, for the commandment and you shall slaughter does not apply to it; and Tosafot's difficulty is in its place; and necessarily it is proven that R' Meir who holds there is slaughter of fowl from the Torah; and it is difficult: if so, that which we learned Chullin daf 28 — flaying with a knife renders garments impure — let the knife help to purify from nevelah status, according to the one who says there is no slaughter for fowl from the Torah; and it is difficult for the one who challenges; necessarily for R' Meir, as Tosafot wrote explicitly there s.v. tehani; and it is difficult: if so, for R' Meir in any case it is compelled from the above that he holds there is slaughter for fowl from the Torah — and what is the difficulty; therefore the Rambam holds that a child of Noah is not commanded regarding eivar min hachai in fowl; and requires further study: who is there anything that is also permitted for children of Noah; and requires further study. And behold one can challenge the Rambam from that which we say Pesachim daf 73: according to the one who disqualifies in a bundle, exempt — that which we learned: one who slaughters a chatat on Shabbat for an idolater and outside — he is liable for three chatat — what did he repair? And Rashi explained: it lacks purification from nevelah status, for idolater's offering defiles in a tent; and they answer: he removes it from eivar min hachai status; and Rashi explained for a child of Noah that if he ate it he is not executed; and in Chullin daf 40 the Gemara proved from that which it did not teach slaughter, that in bird chatat it deals, see there (and see what I wrote siman 4, see there); and it is difficult for the Rambam's views — for behold in fowl there is no warning for a child of Noah at all; and if so, it is still difficult what did he repair; and requires further study. And what appears in its reconciliation is that one should infer from Rashi's explanation why he explained repaired from eivar min hachai for a child of Noah — perhaps plainly for Israel; and if it still did not help, for in any case it is forbidden because of idolater's offering and the like — one can say that in all of them an olive-bulk of flesh is required; but if he ate an olive-bulk of flesh with sinews and bones combined, he does not receive lashes because of other prohibitions, unlike one who eats a limb from a living animal who receives lashes in such a case, as the Rambam wrote and Tosafot explicitly; but when it was slaughtered, he removed it from eivar min hachai status; and if so, one who eats an olive-bulk combined of flesh and its bone and sinews — since there is no olive-bulk of flesh, he is exempt from other prohibitions, and it repaired well; and one need not invoke a child of Noah — one can say Rashi holds in the Gemara's explanation according to the one who disqualifies in a bundle exempt, as Tosafot wrote s.v. according to R' Yehudah — meaning R' Yehudah challenges according to R' Shimon; and according to his view it is difficult what did he repair; and if so, he challenges well what did he repair for eivar min hachai; and one need not invoke as above that we require an olive-bulk, for R' Shimon holds any amount for lashes, and they said olive-bulk only for offering; and if so, even in a minute amount of flesh there are lashes for all prohibitions and it is forbidden in eating and benefit; and if so, it is still difficult what did he repair; therefore Rashi needed to explain repaired regarding a child of Noah; but the Rambam does not hold that the difficulty is for R' Shimon, only the difficulty is for R' Yehudah who even according to his view disqualifies in a bundle, and one need not invoke blood exempt; and if so, one can well say that he repaired from eivar min hachai, for there is no liability on a minute amount of flesh with sinews and bones, and an olive-bulk of flesh is required — and all this is for Israel, and it does not deal with a child of Noah at all; and requires further study. But if it is difficult — this difficulty is from that which we say in the Gemara Chullin daf 101b: eivar min hachai applies in domesticated animal, undomesticated animal, and fowl, both pure and impure, etc.; and R' Yehudah said: both of them from one verse they expounded, as it is written you shall not eat the soul with the flesh; R' Yehudah holds: whatever you are commanded regarding its blood, you are commanded regarding its limbs; and these impure ones — regarding whose blood you are commanded, you are commanded regarding their limbs; and the Gemara challenges R' Yehudah: why do we need a verse — let eivar min hachai prohibition come and apply to impurity prohibition, since its prohibition applies to a child of Noah; and it is difficult: what is the difficulty — for even in fowl eivar min hachai applies, and this is the verse regarding whose blood you are commanded; unlike if not for the verse — only because its prohibition applies to a child of Noah this is not relevant in impure fowl, for a child of Noah is not warned regarding eivar min hachai; and requires further study at first glance. And what appears to me to reconcile in two ways: one way — Tosafot challenged s.v. bo etc., they challenged from the Torah you shall not eat — let eivar min hachai prohibition apply to impurity prohibition, according to the one who holds the prohibition stands for limbs — behold it is prohibition in a single body; and according to all, prohibition applies prohibition; and Tosafot answered that impurity prohibition preceded while it was not yet bound in sinews, see there end of his words; and this is in domesticated animal; but in fowl — behold the Rambam in Forbidden Foods chapter 3 halachah 8 ruled: impure fowl egg in which the chick began to develop inside and she ate it — she receives lashes because of fowl sheretz; behold as long as the chick is inside the egg, there is no prohibition of you shall not eat impure, only prohibition of sheretz; only when it goes out to the air of the world does the prohibition of you shall not eat impure apply; but inside the egg there is prohibition of eating sheretz; and see Kesef Mishneh what he wrote on this; and accordingly, when does impure chick prohibition of impurity apply — when it goes out from the egg to the air, for then the sheretz prohibition flies off and impurity prohibition applies, and it is also called eivar min hachai prohibition, for while alive it stands for limbs; and if so, it is prohibition in a single body — impure and eivar min hachai — and one cannot say prohibition does not apply to prohibition, as Tosafot's difficulty; and Tosafot answered that impurity prohibition did not precede — this is not relevant here, for impurity prohibition applies only from the time of exiting the egg, and then eivar min hachai applies; and if so, a verse is not needed — it is plain that it applies, for it is prohibition in a single body; and necessarily the verse comes for domesticated animal, where single-body is not relevant, as Tosafot wrote — he challenges well the Gemara: behold its prohibition applies to a child of Noah; and why need the prohibition does not stand for limbs; and if so, nevertheless impurity prohibition preceded — if so, it should be difficult for the one who holds the prohibition stands for limbs; and requires further study. And behold Maharsha on daf 103 was inclined that he challenged Resh Lakish who holds eivar min hachai does not apply to treifah because the prohibition does not stand for limbs — if so, why need the verse that eivar min hachai is not in impure, as written soul with flesh, whatever its flesh is permitted etc. — let it be derived that impurity prohibition preceded, and even if the prohibition stands for limbs, impurity prohibition is forbidden first, as Tosafot wrote, see there; and one must understand why he challenged thus to Resh Lakish and not to R' Yehudah who holds the prohibition stands for limbs — for in any case he agrees that impurity prohibition precedes while it was not yet bound in sinews; and if so, it is difficult why need a verse — how can eivar min hachai prohibition apply to preceding impurity prohibition, and they do not have it; and according to this, one can say that the verse was needed for fowl, for impurity prohibition applies only when it exits the egg, and then eivar min hachai prohibition applies, and he teaches us that it does not apply; therefore he challenged Resh Lakish who holds the prohibition does not stand for limbs — if so, in fowl too impurity prohibition precedes; and there is no answer. However, in any case one can reconcile: one can say that that which we say the prohibition does not stand for limbs — we derive from that which the Torah revealed whatever its flesh is permitted etc., that the prohibition does not stand for limbs, and impurity prohibition preceded; and see Tosafot — only the main difficulty of Maharsha, for behold impurity precedes even if the prohibition stands for limbs while not yet bound in sinews — from which is implied that his reasoning is external and one should not derive at all; but according to what is written well, one can derive from fowl that it applies only when it goes out to the light of the world; and requires further study. And one can say that at first glance it is difficult why need a verse — it is plain that eivar min hachai prohibition applies before impurity prohibition, for it is clear that the beginning of the animal's existence is in extreme smallness — it is difficult to depict that in a limb there would be an olive-bulk of flesh alone without sinews and bones; but it is certain that before there is flesh in the limb, there is from the beginning a bit of flesh and a bit of sinews and bones, until in combination they are an olive-bulk; and this testifies to the nature of offspring that before the limb grows and has only an olive-bulk of flesh, there is only a bit of flesh, but in combination with sinews and bone it is an olive-bulk; and if so, eivar min hachai prohibition precedes, for in its smallness it is eivar min hachai, for in combination there is here an olive-bulk of flesh, bone, and sinew; but impurity prohibition does not exist, for there is no olive-bulk of flesh here; and when it grows afterward and has an olive-bulk of flesh, then impurity prohibition applies, and eivar min hachai already applied; and even though nevertheless it is forbidden because of half a measure, since there are no lashes, and because of eivar min hachai prohibition there are lashes — if so, eivar min hachai prohibition preceded, and impurity prohibition will not apply again; and see Ran Tosafot Shevuot 22. And if so, it is difficult why need a verse — for behold eivar min hachai preceded; and it must be said that in truth in such a case it was not needed, only certainly there are large animals that immediately in their existence and formation their limbs were bound in sinews, large limbs, and they have an olive-bulk of flesh, like large rams and elephants; and one might have thought impurity preceded, for it applies immediately; therefore a verse was needed: whatever you are commanded regarding its blood, you are commanded regarding its limbs, etc.; and eivar min hachai applies also in this. And this is in domesticated animal; but in fowl born from the egg, it is impossible that in one of the many limbs there would be an olive-bulk of flesh without bone and sinew — go and consider how much strength this chick has until the egg contains it and wraps it (and possibly the bar yuchni egg that Chazal spoke of in riddles); and most limbs are only in combination of flesh with bones and sinews, before there is an olive-bulk of flesh; and if so, eivar min hachai prohibition preceded; and accordingly on the end, a verse is not needed — certainly eivar min hachai preceded; the verse is only for large animals; and he challenges well: behold its prohibition applies to a child of Noah; and requires further study. In this too the words of Maharsha mentioned above that he challenged why need the verse that its flesh is forbidden — let it be derived prohibition does not apply to prohibition; one can say it was needed in such a case where the limb does not hold an olive-bulk of flesh, only flesh, sinews, and bones; and as written, prohibition does not apply to prohibition is not relevant — if so, still how do we derive from one whose flesh is forbidden, for behold also in this its flesh is entirely permitted; and what practical difference whether it became forbidden from its species, only this is for Resh Lakish, but for R' Yehudah requires further study, for half a measure is forbidden from the Torah, therefore he did not challenge from Resh Lakish who holds half a measure is permitted. And in this too is reconciled that which Rashi wrote daf 103 that eivar min hachai came and applied to treifah because of miggo that it applies to a child of Noah — and he did not write because of miggo that it applies to limbs that do not have an olive-bulk of flesh, only in combination of sinews and bones it applies to all limbs, even very large ones, for Rashi holds that anonymously it teaches even in very large rams where there is no limb in which there would not be an olive-bulk of flesh. And in this is reconciled that which Tosafot wrote for Rava who said: he plucked a limb and rendered it treifah — from this we learn he holds the prohibition does not stand for limbs; if so, that which we say he plucked chelev from treifah, he is liable for three — how do you find it, for how can eivar min hachai apply to treifah; and why need inclusive prohibition, for Rava does not hold otherwise, for if so, why does he require specifically that it became treifah in it; and according to what is written it is well: this inclusive prohibition that applies to a child of Noah — Rava does not hold; but this inclusive prohibition he holds from miggo that it applies to a limb that does not have an olive-bulk of flesh, it applies also to all limbs; and Rava does not establish it that it deals in one animal, only in two, and there is no inclusive prohibition; but that which we say he plucked from treifah etc. — it deals in one animal, and he has inclusive prohibition, as written; and requires further study.
