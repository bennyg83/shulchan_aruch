A limb that separated from a living animal. Whether it has flesh, sinews, and bones, or whether it has only flesh alone — such as the tongue, spleen, kidneys, and eggs — it is forbidden to eat it, end of his words — Tur; and so too the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 5 of Forbidden Foods; and Rav HaMagid wrote there that so is explained in the sugya of the chapter of the displaced sinew, and it is agreed by all the commentators there; and it is implied from their words there that lashes apply also in this; and so is implied in the Talmud there. Further, the Tur wrote that eivar min hachai applies only in pure domesticated animal, undomesticated animal, and fowl, and not in impure ones — and this is explicit in the Talmud and all the poskim; and the Mechaber omitted all this because he follows his reasoning, for he challenged in his book Beit Yosef on the Tur that he inserted himself to write these laws, since for us there is no practical difference in this, for in the end they are forbidden in any case; but Bach wrote that there is a practical difference where it is forbidden because of eivar min hachai — it is a complete entity and is not nullified, as below siman 100; and his words are correct in this. But that which Bach wrote at the end of his words — and these are his words: and see that in pure animals, where there is eivar min hachai, it is forbidden to give it to children of Noah because of lifnei iver, do not place a stumbling block; for a child of Noah is commanded regarding eivar min hachai; and in impure animals it is permitted to give — end of his words; and so too in Hagahot Perishah — and these are his words: and our teacher says it does not apply in impure animals, that one may give it to him l'chatchila, for because of impure animal one does not transgress, since a child of Noah is not commanded regarding it; and eivar min hachai does not apply to him — one cannot say that a child of Noah transgresses eivar min hachai also in an impure animal, for is there anything that is permitted for Israel and forbidden for an idolater — end of his words — I did not grasp the end of his words, for behold in chapter 3 (daf 102) it is in the entire sugya and in the baraita of the dispute regarding Israel; but regarding a child of Noah all agree he is warned regarding impure animals; and so it is in the chapter of the hide and the sauce (Chullin daf 121b) in the entire sugya, see there; and so wrote the Rambam chapter 9 of Laws of Judges; and the Raavan in Sefer Yiraim siman 136; and Rashba in Teshuvot HaRosh HaArukh daf 30b; and the Raah in Sefer HaChinukh mitzvah 464; and Maharshal in Perek HaGah siman 63; and that which Hagahot Perishah wrote — who is there anything etc. — I omitted Rashi and Tosafot there; and Tosafot in the chapter of the slaughterer (Chullin daf 33b) s.v. echad ovdei kochavim, who wrote that here it is not relevant at all to challenge who is there anything etc., since in the end it is forbidden for Israel because of am ha'aretz etc., see there — and this is clear. However, in a dangling limb that is forbidden rabbinically, there are poskim who permit giving it to children of Noah, since there is in it only the mitzvah of separation, as written in siman 55 s.k. 11:
And flesh that separated, etc. It is implied from the words of the poskim that even if flesh separated from a whole limb, it is only because of flesh in the field treifah:
