One separates oil olives for pickled olives, but not from pickled olives for oil olives; from wine that is not cooked for what is cooked, but not from what is cooked for what is not cooked; from clear for what is not clear, but not from what is not clear for clear; from figs for dried figs by count, from dried figs for figs by measure—but not figs for dried figs by measure, nor dried figs for figs by count, so that one always separate with a favorable eye. And one separates wheat for bread, but not from bread for wheat, according to the calculation. And in all these, if he separated, his terumah is terumah. And it appears to me that nowadays, when terumah goes to destruction, one need not be strict about this; and likewise in other similar matters.
