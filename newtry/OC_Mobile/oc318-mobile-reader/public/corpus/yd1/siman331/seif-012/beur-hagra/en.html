They are liable if their obligation was established, etc. The Yerushalmi asks: what is Rabbi Eliezer's reason? "The bread of the land," wherever it may be. What is Rabbi Akiva's reason? "To the land to which I bring you," meaning there you are liable, but outside the Land you are not. Earlier it states: this is Rabbi Meir's view. Rabbi Yehudah says that produce of the Diaspora brought into the Land is exempt according to Rabbi Eliezer and liable according to Rabbi Akiva. Rabbi Eliezer derives "the bread of the land," not bread of the Diaspora; Rabbi Akiva derives "to the land to which I bring you," there you are liable whether the produce is of the Land or the Diaspora. Rabbi Akiva applies Rabbi Eliezer's verse to dough on a ship entering the Land: if its surface crusted inside the boundary, it is liable; outside, it is exempt. According to Rabbi Akiva everything follows the point at which the crust forms in the oven. The same applies to tithes. Thus the text says "if their obligation was established," for kneading according to the first Tanna and crusting according to Rabbi Akiva correspond to the threshing floor for tithes, after which casual eating is forbidden, as stated at the beginning of chapter 3 of Challah. The season of tithing, however, parallels adding water to the dough. The Rambam writes that they are liable in challah, and if their obligation for tithes was established in a Jew's possession, they are rabbinically liable in tithes. The Shulchan Aruch omits "rabbinically" because there is no practical difference nowadays. It appears that the Rambam learned that the tithe obligation is only rabbinic from Gittin 22a, concerning a tree whose roots are partly in the Land. If Rabbi Akiva's liability were biblical, the location of the roots would make no difference, because everything would depend on where the produce was when its obligation was established. Yet the Rambam's distinction between biblical challah and rabbinic tithes requires study, since both are derived from the same verse. The Kesef Mishneh also questions it. The cited Baraisa may concern produce that requires no threshing floor for tithes.
