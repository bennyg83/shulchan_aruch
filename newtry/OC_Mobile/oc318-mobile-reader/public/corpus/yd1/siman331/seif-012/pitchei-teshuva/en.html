The laws of charity. See Be'er Heitev. Regarding whether maaser kesafim is a full obligation, see Sha'ar Ephraim responsa siman 84, Chavot Yair responsa siman 224, She'eilat Yaavetz I siman 6, and Be'er Yaakov on this matter. It is astonishing that those great authorities overlooked the responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg, Prague edition siman 74, where it is explicit like the Bach's view, that it is neither Torah law nor rabbinic law, but only a custom; see there. I found in Teshuvah MeAhavah I siman 87 that he too wondered at them on this point, at length. So too Mishnat Chachamim, Hilkhot Yesodei HaTorah p. 17, concludes that maaser kesafim has no root in Torah law or rabbinic law; but if one practiced it, it is like a mitzvah custom that may not be canceled at all except for great need. See Makom Shmuel responsa siman 10, who was asked about a father of a bride who obligated himself in the customary conditions to pay the maaser from his own pocket, and now says that he wants to give the entire maaser to poor people from the bride's side. Can he do so? Is it like tithing from one's own property on behalf of another's, where the benefit of choosing the recipient is his, or are they not comparable? He answered that the bride's father has no such benefit of choice in this; rather, they should divide the maaser among the poor relatives of both sides, the groom and the bride. See there.
