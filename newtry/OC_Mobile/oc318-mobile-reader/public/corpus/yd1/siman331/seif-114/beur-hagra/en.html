If he was, etc.; and if, etc. This follows our text in the latter clause, where Rabbi Yehudah says he may finish, as the Rambam wrote in his commentary there. The words of the Kesef Mishneh are astonishing, and those of the Tosafot Yom Tov even more so. This is also the text at the end of chapter 4 of Yom Tov, not "when Rabbi Yehudah said he may finish," etc. But the matter requires study: how does the Gemara's difficulty there work, when it asks a contradiction from "if he was," etc.? So too it appears from the Yerushalmi in Terumot there and in Maaserot chapter 3: what is Rabbi Eliezer's reason? Because he began in permitted fashion. Rabbi Natan taught: it is not that Rabbi Eliezer said this because he began in permitted fashion, but rather Rabbi Eliezer says he should wait until after Shabbat or until he leaves the courtyard, and then finish. The core of this law requires study, for in any event Shabbat fixes, as written in seif 111. The Rash there already raised this question, and Tosafot there, s.v. Rabbi Eliezer, answered in their second answer that it refers to a cluster whose work was not completed. So too it implies from the Gemara, which asks a contradiction, etc. According to this, one must say that both Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehudah hold that a courtyard and Shabbat fix even something whose work has not been completed; if so, for us this law does not apply. The Rash there answered that since he began in permitted fashion, etc.; but in the Yerushalmi cited above Rabbi Natan disagrees with this. One must force the explanation that this is what it means: not because he began in permitted fashion may he finish on Shabbat and in the courtyard, but rather he should wait, etc. The sugya at the end of chapter 4 of Yom Tov must be explained as follows: it initially assumed that he may finish on Shabbat and in the courtyard, and therefore it must be speaking of something whose work had not been completed, in accordance with Rabbi Natan above, for beginning in permitted fashion cannot allow him to finish on Shabbat. The phrase "he may finish" is not precise, and is used only because Rabbi Yehudah says that even so he may not finish. The answer there, "there, as it teaches," etc., means even where the work was completed, and because he began in permitted fashion. This must be said according to the Rambam. But the language of the Yerushalmi does not imply this. Rather, Rabbi Natan's words follow Reish Lakish, who holds that Shabbat does not fix for after Shabbat unless one designated the produce for Shabbat; so too is the sugya in Yom Tov there. See Rashi there, 34b, s.v. Shabbat kove'at, etc., and Tosafot there, s.v. tinoket, etc., and s.v. Rabbi Eliezer above, in their first answer. On Shabbat itself it is forbidden to eat even if he did not designate them, as Rashi wrote there; this is why the Gemara there says, since it is written, "and you shall call," etc. Nevertheless, one may say that we do not rule this way, as stated there in the words of Rabbi Yochanan: Shabbat excludes, etc.; and Rabbi Yehudah follows his own view and does not accept Rabbi Natan's rule. (Likkut) See what I wrote there, that our Gemara follows Reish Lakish, and this is the view of Rashi and Tosafot. What it says there, that Shabbat sanctified the day upon him and Hillel forbids him for himself, also refers to that Shabbat itself, as stated in the Mishnah: if he was eating from a cluster, etc.
