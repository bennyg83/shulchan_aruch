Terumah gedolah—at this time, when it stands to be burned because of tumah—its measure is whatever amount it is: {Rama: And one gives it at this time to any kohen he wishes, whether a chaver or an am ha'aretz, and even if he is not of distinguished lineage, only that he is presumed to be a kohen and he burns it. And he may leave it and burn it [until] he can benefit from it at the time of burning; but a zar is forbidden to benefit from it at the time of its burning unless a kohen benefits with him. But other benefits that do not consume it are permitted even to zarim (Tur). A kohen who eats at an Israelite's—he gives him with terumah oil for a lamp, and even though the kohen stands and goes, one need not extinguish it until it goes out by itself (Mordechai chapter HaAlmanah for 23). And all this speaks of terumah that was made fit, for then it became tamei and is permitted to be burned; but if it was not made fit and did not become tamei, it is forbidden to burn it, and it is good to make it fit before treading so that it receives tumah and will be permitted to be burned (Tur).} And afterward he separates from the remainder one in ten, and it is called ma'aser rishon, and gives it to the Levi (and some say even to the kohen) (Tur). And on this it is said in the Torah, "For the tithe of the children of Israel," etc., "and to the children of Levi, behold I have given all the tithe in Israel," etc. And afterward he separates from the remainder one in ten, and it is called ma'aser sheni, and it belongs to its owner; and in this siman its laws will be explained. And on it it is said, "You shall surely tithe." And in this order one separates in the first year of the shemittah cycle and in the second and fourth and fifth. But in the third and sixth of the shemittah cycle, after separating ma'aser rishon he separates from the remainder one in ten and gives it to the poor, and it is called ma'aser ani. And in these two years there is no ma'aser sheni but ma'aser ani. And on it it is said, "At the end of three years you shall bring forth all the tithe of your produce," etc., "and the Levite shall come," etc. The shemittah year—all of it is hefker and has in it neither terumah nor ma'asrot at all. And outside the land, where there is no shemittah of land, one separates in the shemittah year in the land of Egypt and in Ammon and Moav ma'aser rishon and ma'aser ani. And in the land of Shinar one separates ma'aser sheni as in most years. And the true shemittah year was the year 5333. (And some say if a non-Jew sold fruits in the shevi'it to an Israelite and he completed their work in the hand of the Israelite, they are obligated in terumah and ma'aser and he separates ma'aser ani as the law of Ammon and Moav) (Beit Yosef, and so wrote Kaptor VaPerach):
