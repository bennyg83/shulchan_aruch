And it is permitted to feed, etc. That is, even if its work has been completed. In the Tosefta, Maaserot chapter 2, Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar says that an animal may not eat casually in a courtyard. He holds that the Sages disagree with him; see the Raavad's objections there.
(Likkut) And it is permitted to feed, etc. This is Mishnah 4 in chapter 3: "and the carobs," etc. He explains that their work had already been completed, for otherwise even a person would be permitted to eat them, since it would be casual eating, as stated: because it is, etc. Nevertheless, it is permitted for an animal. In the Yerushalmi there, Rabbi Yose ben Shaul said in the name of Rabbi: one may eat from the drying place only in its place, for outside its place it is as though its work has been completed. It says there that Rabbi Yose ben Shaul challenged Rabbi: did we not learn, "the carobs, before he brings them up to the roof"? He answered: do not challenge me from carobs; carobs are animal food. This does not mean that they are not human food, for if so they would be exempt from maaser, as above in seif 13. Rather, it means that he feeds them to an animal and casual feeding is permitted. See the Rash there on Mishnah 1, s.v. benei beito, and on Mishnah 4, s.v. ad shelo and s.v. morid, where he explains that it is specifically where their work was not completed; see there.
