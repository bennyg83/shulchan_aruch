Produce, etc. The Yerushalmi in chapter 2 of Maaser asks whether first tithe renders produce untithed. If the case is a pile already smoothed, all agree it is forbidden; if it is first tithe taken early while still in the ears, all agree it is permitted. The question concerns dates intended for pressing, or dried figs intended for trampling, from which terumah gedolah was separated, after which he decided to leave them as they are, and then separated first tithe. If the original intent applies retroactively, terumah renders them untithed; if only prospectively, first tithe renders them untithed. The ruling is lenient because the question is understood as resolved by the later statement: one who buys dates intending to press them or dried figs intending to trample them may not eat casually, and he separates tithes as from doubtful produce according to Rabbi Meir, whereas the Sages permit casual eating and require separation as from certain produce. This later passage, however, does not truly address the question. See there.
