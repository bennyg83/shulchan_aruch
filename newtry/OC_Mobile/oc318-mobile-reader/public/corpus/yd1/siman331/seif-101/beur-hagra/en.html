Provided, etc.; and although, etc.; because, etc. Rambam derived this from the first Mishnah there: one who transports, etc. Produce intended for cutting has not had its work completed, as above. See his commentary there: 'and if he has,' etc., because it is not fit, etc., as I wrote in seif katan 19. It cannot be because of purchase, since purchase does not fix produce whose work is unfinished. R. Shimshon was pressed by this point. Rambam holds that the opening case, when the employer stipulated food, likewise concerns unfinished produce, since laborers need not enter the house and the produce is like that taken to market. He infers this from the word 'to cut.' The inference is not compelling, for that wording may have been chosen because of the latter clause and because of his children and household. The later Mishnah therefore teaches one who brings his laborers to the field even where the work is completed. This requires study. [Citation references: 81.]
