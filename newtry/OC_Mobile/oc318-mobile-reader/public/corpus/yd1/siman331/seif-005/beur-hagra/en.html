A non-Jew sold, etc. Jerusalem Talmud, last chapter of Ma'asrot: it is taught — a field whose produce had reached one-third growth while in a non-Jew's possession, and an Israelite purchased it from him: R. Akiva says the additional growth is exempt, and the Sages say it is obligated. This applies specifically when it reached one-third growth, which is the stage of tithing obligation as written in ch. 1 of Ma'asrot and elsewhere. But before that point, all of it is obligated. As the Mishnah there distinguishes regarding Syria — except that the Mishnah there applies also to terumah, whereas here it applies only to tithes. This is when an Israelite completed the harvest, and as written in Bekhorot there, and as written in the preceding section. He ruled like the Sages, and even though in the aforementioned Mishnah regarding Syria he ruled not like R. Akiva, as written in §7, the Rambam holds that the two rulings are not dependent on each other — for it is possible that even the Sages agree with R. Akiva in Syria. [The Rambam explains the Baraita as referring to the Land of Israel, but in Ḥullin 136a it is taught explicitly regarding Syria.] (And there it states that R. Akiva obligates and the Sages exempt. In the Tosefta ch. 3 of Ma'asrot it cannot be relied upon; see there.) But the R"Sh on the Mishnah there wrote that the anonymous Mishnah cited above is in accordance with R. Akiva:
And he gives the tithe to the Levite in proportion, etc. And the same applies to terumat ma'aser, as written in the Rambam there.
