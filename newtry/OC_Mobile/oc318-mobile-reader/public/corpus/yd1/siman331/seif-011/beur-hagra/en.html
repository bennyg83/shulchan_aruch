A non-Jew's partnership, etc. Ḥullin 135b: "alternatively, a non-Jew's partnership, etc.":
How so, etc., but, etc. As written at the end of §19, and see what I wrote in Oraḥ Ḥayyim §413 on the laws of bereirah:
Even his portion, etc. Jerusalem Talmud, ch. 6 of Demai: it is taught — an Israelite and a non-Jew who purchased a field in Syria — it is like untithed produce and tithes mixed together. R. Shimon ben Gamliel says: the Israelite's portion is obligated; the non-Jew's portion is exempt. R. Yasa in the name of R. Ḥanina: what do they dispute? Where they divided the field while the grain was still standing; but if they divided a stacked pile, even Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel agrees with R. Akiva [that each stalk belongs to the partnership], and we rule in the Land of Israel like R. Akiva that there is no bereirah. This is what he writes: "even, etc., and needless to say, etc." And what the Jerusalem Talmud says regarding Syria is because we rule that a non-Jew has no acquisitional right in the Land of Israel; in Syria it is different, and as written in Gittin ibid. The Rambam wrote even in the Land of Israel when the non-Jew winnowed — that the non-Jew's portion is entirely exempt and the Israelite's portion is rabbinically obligated, as stated above. This is what he writes: "even though, etc., and they are obligated by their words."
Even if they divided a pile, etc. Even though in the Jerusalem Talmud ibid. it states that in such a case Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel agrees with R. Akiva — we do not rule thus, since in rabbinic matters there is bereirah entirely:
And it appears to me, etc. It appears that the same applies regarding a non-Jew's acquisitional right in our time, which is rabbinic — that there is an acquisitional right in the Land of Israel just as in Syria. See Rashi in Gittin ibid. s.v. be-Surya, etc. [and above end of s.k. 6]. This is what he writes in §2 in the gloss: "and some disagree, etc., but the custom is not so."
