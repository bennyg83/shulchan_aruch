(There in Shach s.k. 112) The courtyard. And a house establishes by Torah law, etc., until: a guarded courtyard. So wrote the Atzei Zahav, and this follows Rabbi Yochanan in Bava Metzia, Perek HaPoalim, 88; see there. But this is astonishing to me, for Rabbi Yannai disagrees there and holds that even a guarded courtyard does not establish produce for maaser by Torah law, and tevel is not obligated in maaser until it sees the face of the house. And we hold that wherever Rabbi Yannai and Rabbi Yochanan disagree, the halachah follows Rabbi Yannai, for he was Rabbi Yochanan’s teacher. The Rambam explicitly rules like Rabbi Yannai at the beginning of chapter 4 of Hilkhot Maaser; see there. Also in Yerushalmi Maaserot chapter 3, Rabbi Yochanan himself says that a courtyard is not by Torah law; see there. Also examine the Raavad’s glosses there, where he writes: “This is with something that has no threshing floor”; end quote. He writes likewise at the beginning of chapter 3 of Hilkhot Maaser. The Kesef Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 3 wonders about him and leaves it requiring study. In my humble opinion, an explicit Talmud in Perek HaPoalim 88b escaped him, where it says: “And if you wish, say that when Rabbi Yannai spoke, it was regarding olives and grapes, which are not items of a threshing floor; but regarding wheat and barley, the threshing floor is written explicitly.” This is exactly like the words of the Raavad. The question of the Kesef Mishneh was answered by Tosafot there, s.v. ki ka’amar Rabbi Yannai; see there. It appears to me that the Rambam holds like Rashi’s explanation there, that Rabbi Oshaya, who says that a person may act with artifice regarding his produce and bring it in with its chaff, does not accept that answer. And the Rambam explicitly brings Rabbi Oshaya’s law in chapter 3; see there. So it appears to me, not like the Bach; see there.
