One who purchases produce, etc. By Torah law, only one who completes the processing of his produce for his own consumption must tithe. One who completes it for sale is exempt by Torah law, as it says, 'You shall surely tithe the produce of your seed,' followed by, 'and you shall eat,' indicating liability only when he completes it for eating. He is nevertheless liable rabbinically. A purchaser is likewise exempt by Torah law, as it says, 'the produce of your seed,' but liable rabbinically. This applies where he purchased it after the seller completed its processing. If processing was completed in the purchaser's possession, he must tithe by Torah law. It has already been explained that a purchase fixes produce for tithing once processing is complete. Therefore, one who purchases detached produce for eating after the seller completed its processing becomes rabbinically fixed for tithing immediately.
