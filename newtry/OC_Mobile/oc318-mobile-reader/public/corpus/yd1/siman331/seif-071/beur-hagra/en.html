One may tithe, etc. So it appears from the Yerushalmi in Terumot chapter 2: one who separates from a cistern or from a barrel and says, "This is terumah on condition that it emerge intact from breakage," etc. Rabbi Yose bar Rabbi says that this applies to terumah gedolah, which requires min hamukaf, but with terumat maaser all other things are considered intact. If he was separating terumah and terumat maaser at once, it is not called intact. Yehudah bar Rabbi said: he did not intend to violate the Torah by making terumat maaser precede terumah gedolah. The Yerushalmi challenges that reasoning, for if so one should not be able to separate terumat maaser until he has named the maaser, as in Demai chapter 5. See the Rash at the end of Tevul Yom.
(Likkut) One may tithe, etc., and he need not, etc. This follows Yevamot 93a regarding Rabbi Yannai, etc. He did not need to separate terumah first, because "a threshing floor is not uprooted until terumah has been taken," as stated in the Yerushalmi in Maaserot chapter 3; therefore he took ten, etc. Here too the term "one may tithe" is general, even with respect to a Torah scholar, although terumat maaser is forbidden. Tosafot distance themselves from this, following their own view that distinguishes between weekday and Shabbat regarding terumat maaser. The Rambam, however, does not distinguish this way; see the gloss at seif 24.
(Likkut) One may tithe, etc. This is the Mishnah at the end of Gittin 31a: "one who leaves," etc. Tosafot there follow their view from 30b, where the Gemara asks, "Are they suspected," etc., and they answer that the matter is rabbinic, etc. The Rambam follows the opinion of those who explain otherwise and does not distinguish between Shabbat or Yom Tov and an ordinary weekday. The "terumot" mentioned in the Mishnah means terumat maaser.
But, etc. This is from Bekhorot 54a and from the Yerushalmi in chapter 2, where it is stated many times that one does not separate terumah or tithe from this upon that.
And not from, etc. This is from Menachot 31a and many other places, lest one come to tithe from what is obligated upon what is exempt, or from what is exempt upon what is obligated. That is why, if he did tithe, etc.; otherwise there would have been no reason to decree.
