The terumot, etc. Mishnah, chapter 1 of Kiddushin: every commandment that is, etc. And the Gemara in many places.
(Likkut) The terumot, etc. See chapter 11 of Hullin 136a and Bekhorot 27a. (End of quote.)
(Likkut) In the land of Shinar, etc., Syria, etc. Even today, as stated in Bekhorot (27a): "Rabbah abolishes it, etc." — and this disagrees with what Tosafot wrote in Hullin (7a) and Avodah Zarah (59a) — and from there it is implied according to one resolution that terumah is not practiced outside the Land today. (End of quote.)
Syria — one who buys, etc. Specifically one who buys [land] — but not [produce bought] from a non-Jew, as stated in the Mishnah, chapter 6 of Demai, Mishnah 11. See the Ra'am there. "And those lands" means Syria, etc. See the Rashbam there and in the Yerushalmi which states, etc. And the last chapter of Ma'asrot: "one who buys, etc." And the last chapter of Challah. See above from seif 7 through seif 11.
By rabbinic ordinance. Gittin 47a: "The case is of Syria, and he holds, etc." See Rashi there s.v. "be-Surya," etc. We rule like the opinion that says conquest [by an individual] is not considered conquest, as stated in Avodah Zarah 21a: "R. Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel: the law follows R. Yehuda" — and as stated in siman 151, seif 8, and as stated above in seif 9. And in chapter 1 of Gittin (8a) it says "obligated in tithes, etc." — implying even from Torah law (like the Land of Israel) — therefore it was necessary to say "he holds, etc." See Tosefta chapter 2 of Terumot. The reason that [individual] conquest is not considered conquest — see Tosafot in Gittin 8a s.v. "kibbush." But in the Sifri, etc. — and so wrote the Rambam, Ramban, and other decisors.
And other lands, etc. See Tosafot in Kiddushin 36b s.v. "kol," etc. — and one may say that therefore it requires, etc. — and so wrote Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 59a in the name of R. Yitzchak at the beginning of s.v. "batzar." But R. Yitzchak said: since they were not obligated, etc. — and as stated in the Gemara there: "while your cloak is still upon you, etc." And in chapter 1 of Hullin (6b) that Rabbi permitted Beit She'an. And in the Yerushalmi chapter 3 of Demai — that Rabbi permitted Caesarea. And what is written in Berakhot 36a that tithes apply outside the Land, and in Yom Tov 12b: "Rav Tuvi, etc.," and in Bekhorot 27a: "terumah outside the Land, etc." And a different approach of Rabbeinu Tam: that specifically for demai one is exempt outside the Land, but for certain produce one is obligated everywhere. According to him there is no distinction between places close and far — demai is exempt everywhere except in places conquered by the Babylonian returnees, as stated at the end of chapter 1 of Demai: "from Keziv, etc." [And further explained: specifically for vegetables is it exempt, as stated in seif 14 — see there.] See Tosafot in Kiddushin there and in Avodah Zarah there and in Hullin there, where they wrote that in any case today one is exempt outside the Land — as stated in chapter 4 of Challah in the Yerushalmi: "our teachers outside the Land used to separate terumot and tithes until the [Romans] came and abolished them, etc." See there.
