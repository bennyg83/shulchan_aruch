Syria, one who purchases. This applies specifically to produce a Jew purchases there, but not to produce remaining entirely in gentile ownership, as stated in Demai chapter 6, Mishnah 11. Rambam explains the Mishnah there accordingly. "These lands" refers to Syria. See Rash there and the Yerushalmi, as well as the final chapters of Ma'asrot and Challah. See below from seif 7 through seif 11.
By rabbinic law. Gittin 47a establishes the case in Syria and explains that conquest by an individual is not considered full conquest. See Rashi there. We rule that such conquest does not confer full sanctity, as in Avodah Zarah 21a, where R. Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel that the halachah follows R. Yose, and as stated in siman 151, seif 8. This also accords with what was explained above. Gittin 8a says the land is obligated in tithes, which sounds even biblical like Eretz Yisrael, and therefore the Gemara must explain the opinion concerning individual conquest. See Tosefta Terumot chapter 2. Tosafot in Gittin explain the reason that individual conquest is not considered full conquest, and Rambam, Ramban, and the other poskim rule accordingly.
And the other lands. See Tosafot, Kiddushin 36b, who explain why the Gemara needs a particular derivation. Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 59a write in the name of Ri that produce outside Eretz Yisrael was never biblically obligated. This accords with the Gemara there and with Rabbi's permitting Beit She'an in Chullin 6b and Caesarea in the Yerushalmi. Statements that tithes apply outside Eretz Yisrael, including Berachot 36a, Yom Tov 12b, and Bechorot 27a concerning terumah outside the Land, refer to rabbinic practice. Rabbenu Tam has another approach: only demai was exempt outside Eretz Yisrael, while definite untithed produce remained obligated everywhere. On his approach there is no difference between lands near or distant from Eretz Yisrael, because demai is exempt everywhere except places sanctified by those who returned from Babylonia. He also explains elsewhere that vegetables are especially exempt. See Tosafot in Kiddushin, Avodah Zarah, and Chullin. They conclude that nowadays produce outside Eretz Yisrael is exempt, based on the Yerushalmi in chapter 4 of Challah: the sages of the Diaspora separated terumot and ma'asrot until later authorities abolished the practice.
