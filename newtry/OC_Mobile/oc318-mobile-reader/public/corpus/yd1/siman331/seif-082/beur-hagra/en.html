And once its work is completed, etc. Although Bava Metzia 88a states that tevel is not obligated, there it speaks on the Torah level. On the Torah level, fixing and obligation are one matter, as stated there in the name of R. Yannai: tevel is not obligated, etc.; and R. Yochanan says even a courtyard fixes it. Therefore, before it sees the face of the house or courtyard, even a fixed meal is permitted in every manner. Rabbinically, however, a person may not eat a fixed meal, and produce being taken to market may not even be eaten casually, because it has been fixed rabbinically. By Torah law there is no distinction. See Rashba, responsum 361, for his explanation of the sugya, and Tosafot there, s.v. until it sees, etc. It appears that the statement that tevel is not obligated applies even to all six factors listed in seif 83, apart from the house. Rambam writes likewise at the beginning of chapter 4. This is disputed in the Yerushalmi, chapters 2 and 3 and the beginning of chapter 4: R. Yochanan says purchase, Shabbat, and courtyard are not from the Torah; R. Ami said in the name of Reish Lakish that the clearest of all is a guarded courtyard-house. Rambam rules like Reish Lakish because the Bavli sugya above indicates that even R. Yochanan agrees, while the Yerushalmi's formulation is reversed from our Gemara. This explains the Gemara's question, 'But a purchaser is obligated.' Rashba explains its question from 'once they are smoothed,' etc.: since he is entirely exempt by Torah law, how could the Sages prohibit even casual eating? Although the Mishnah states, 'When does this apply? When he takes it to market,' the Gemara was uncertain whether that clause governs the whole Mishnah or only the final case of vegetables. Its conclusion applies it to the entire Mishnah, and all the discussion there concerns Torah law. Rambam therefore omitted it here. Raavad, however, understands the passage even rabbinically and objects here, but his words are difficult, for he combines grain, wine, and oil. See Tosafot in the sugya, R. Shimshon on the Mishnah, and Rashba there.
Until they are fixed, etc. The Mishnah says until he reaches his house, mentioning one of the fixing factors. See the end of chapter 3 and the end of chapter 4 of Yom Tov, where it is stated that all fixing factors operate only after completion of the work.
