(In the gloss) And some say that in such a case its presumption is that it was tithed. The Magen Avraham wrote in Orach Chaim siman 409 s.k. 17 that one must examine the words of the Mishneh LaMelekh, Hilkhot Bekhorot chapter 4. In my humble opinion, the Mishneh LaMelekh’s words there are difficult, for the sugya explicitly says that if one found it separated, it is not presumed to have been separated. And the Rash and the Rosh wrote the reason: even though we rule like Rav Sheshet that in a Torah matter there is a presumption that an agent performs his agency, that is when the sender will rely upon him, and therefore the agent is careful. But with terumah, the agent says to himself that the owner will see that no terumah is beside the pile, and he will know that it was not separated; the agent does not imagine that the owner will think that he separated it after he heard from him. The Mishneh LaMelekh writes that where the sender does not know the measure of the pile, and gives the agent the benefit of choosing to whom to give it, so that the sender will assume that the agent separated, then according to the reason of the Rash and the Rosh we rely on the agent having performed his agency. Nevertheless, on that same day it is forbidden to eat until evening.
