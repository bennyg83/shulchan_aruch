A non-Jew, etc.; unlike, etc. His words are very astonishing. Even if an Israelite bought the land or the produce from a non-Jew, and the produce had grown while in the non-Jew's possession, if it had not yet reached the season for tithing it is obligated, even in Syria, as below in seif 7; all the more so here. He misunderstood the Rambam. The Rambam's meaning is that a non-Jew who bought land in Eretz Yisrael does not remove its sanctity; if an Israelite buys it back, it is not considered an individual conquest, and he separates terumot and maasrot and brings bikkurim by Torah law as though it had never been sold. But in Syria a non-Jew does have acquisition sufficient to remove it from maaser and shemittah. That is, in Eretz Yisrael according to the view that a non-Jew has no acquisition to uproot sanctity, it remains fully obligated; and even according to the view that he does have acquisition, once the Israelite buys it back it is only like an individual conquest and is rabbinic, like Syria. This is learned from Gittin: one who sells his field to a non-Jew and buys it back brings bikkurim. The statement that there is acquisition for a non-Jew in Syria begins a later set of laws and does not apply to this case. Indeed, in the present case there is no distinction: even according to the view that there is acquisition, and even in Syria, if the produce had not reached the season for tithing it is obligated. Nowadays this has no practical difference, because the whole obligation is rabbinic, as stated in seif 2. The Rambam rules that there is no acquisition for a non-Jew in Eretz Yisrael, following Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi in Bekhorot. The many passages that appear to imply that there is acquisition can be explained as referring to Syria, or according to Rabbi Meir, or to a case after miruach. What the Be'er HaGolah wrote in s.k. 8 in the name of the Kesef Mishneh is a great error: if the Israelite did not buy from him, it is certainly exempt according to everyone; and if he bought the produce from the non-Jew, the Rambam rules that there is no acquisition, as above. In Syria all agree that a non-Jew has acquisition. This is practically relevant for seifim 7-10, but here it makes no difference, since the present obligation in Eretz Yisrael is rabbinic.
