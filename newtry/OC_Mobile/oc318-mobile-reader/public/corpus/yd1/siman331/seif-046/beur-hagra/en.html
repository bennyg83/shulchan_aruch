He separated, etc. Kiddushin there, and Shevuot 26b; and see Tosafot in Gittin 31a, s.v. "thus, etc." (and it appears that the Sages do not disagree, etc.) and s.v. "in thought, etc." (and so certainly without speech, etc.) — and so wrote [it] in Bekhorot 59 and Menachot 54.
(Supplement) He separated, etc. Yom Tov 13b, and from there it is learned: "just as terumah gedolah may be separated by estimation and in thought, etc." — and the Sages do not disagree with Abba Elazar ben Gamliel except regarding tithes; and in chapter 1 of Terumot: "five [types of people] may not give terumah, etc. — a mute, etc." — and the Jerusalem Talmud says: "because of blessing, specifically." And in the beginning of chapter 2 of Kiddushin: "what is [it that distinguishes] these, that, etc." And in chapter 3 of Shevuot (26b): "because terumah and sacred things are two verses that teach [the same thing], etc." And Tosafot wrote in Gittin 31, and from there it is learned: that even for tithes the Sages do not disagree [with Abba Elazar ben Gamliel] except regarding estimation, but regarding thought they do not disagree — for the verse explicitly says therein: "and it shall be counted, etc." And see Rashi in Gittin there, s.v. "and in thought — one who sets his eyes to one side, etc." — "and both come forth, etc." — "and this one derives, etc." And so we say in the first chapter of Chullin (7a): "perhaps R. Meir set his eyes, etc." And even though one might say dema'i is different (and see Tosafot in Chullin there, 6b, s.v. "and permitted, etc. — and R. Tam says, etc. — and so it is implied, etc.") and so it is implied in Tosafot in Gittin there (s.v. "in thought") — nevertheless there is no distinction; and the proof (that in Chullin there we speak of certain produce) is from the statement there: "they were not suspected, etc." — and for dema'i it is permitted, as written in chapter 4 of Challah (and see there in the commentary on mishnah 6, which wrote: "even though they were not lenient regarding dema'i itself, as is proven, etc. — and this requires investigation"). And Tosafot there and from there [conclude] that nevertheless it is forbidden to separate tithes on Shabbat and Yom Tov — as written in chapter 5 of Yom Tov (36b, in the mishnah); and even dema'i — as written in chapter 2 of Shabbat (34a, in the mishnah) and chapter 7 of Dema'i — for ultimately R. Yehudah bar Pedat only permitted [it] in the beginning of chapter 23 of Shabbat on account of "sets his eyes on one side, etc." — and specifically in the case of mixed produce; but R. Shimon ben Elazar disagrees with him in a baraita; and certainly the first opinion disagrees as well. And it appears that the first opinion also disagrees with Rashba and forbids even setting one's eyes — even in mixed produce — from the fact that the Gemara says "R. Yehudah holds like Rashba" — implying the first opinion disagrees. But by calling out [the name of the produce] for ma'aser sheni one may separate even definite untithed produce [on Shabbat], as written in chapter 7 of Dema'i; and all this applies likewise to challah. (End.)
