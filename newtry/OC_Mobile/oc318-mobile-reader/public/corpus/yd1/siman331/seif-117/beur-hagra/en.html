And to a chaver. Yerushalmi there, cited by R. Shimshon.
And it is forbidden, etc. This is unlike R. Yose in chapter 3, as Rambam writes in his commentary. The Yerushalmi relates that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said: R. Yose son of Rabbi sent me an etrog and said, 'This came into his hand from Caesarea.' I learned three things: it was certainly untithed, it was impure, and no other etrog came into his hand. Had another come into his hand, he would have tithed one for the other. But let him tithe from it for the other? He was concerned for Bar Kappara's rule that people do not ordinarily send deficient gifts to their friends. Is this not the Mishnah in which R. Yose permits it provided he informs him? The passage teaches that although he disputes the Sages, he did not act according to his own ruling. This is why Rambam writes even from one chaver to another. Similarly, Demai chapter 1 relates that R. Yirmiyah sent R. Zeira a basket of untithed figs. Each assumed the other would not give or eat something untithed, and between them the tevel was eaten. This is the concern expressed by 'lest,' etc. [Citation references: 85, 214, 650.]
