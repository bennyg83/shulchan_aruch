One who separates terumah, etc. This requires study, for this Mishnah follows Rabbi, and so it is in the Tosefta there: one who separates terumah from a barrel, etc., these are Rabbi's words, for Rabbi says wine, etc. Nevertheless, the halakhah follows the sages. Although the anonymous Mishnah here follows him, the anonymous Mishnah at the end of chapter 2 follows Rabbi Yose bar Yehudah, as stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 2: this Mishnah is Rabbi Yose bar Yehudah's, for Rabbi Yishmael bar Rabbi Yose said in the name of his father, one separates terumah from wine for vinegar, but not from vinegar for wine; if he transgressed and separated, his terumah is terumah. Rabbi says wine and vinegar are two species, and one may not separate terumah or tithe from one for the other, etc. Although it is taught, “one who checks,” etc., and Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi dispute in Bava Batra 96a, that case can follow even the sages because he intended to separate only wine, as stated in the Tosefta there: the sages say, one species was in his mind, to separate wine for wine; what is in his hand is vinegar, so it is not terumah, etc. “He was checking,” etc. So too wrote Tosafot in Bava Batra 84b s.v. “wine,” etc. One can say that the Mishnah also speaks in this way, and there is no need to say the anonymous Mishnayot contradict each other; the Tosefta case speaks where he intended to separate even from vinegar. The Rambam wrote the language of the Mishnah. See the Rash there, and Tosafot Yevamot 89a s.v. “cucumber,” etc.
