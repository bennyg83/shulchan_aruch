And similarly, etc. Tosefta ch. 2 of Terumot. I will now copy the Tosefta relevant to this section. Ch. 1 of Demai: One may not sow untithed produce [tevel], one may not cover with tevel, and one may not work with a non-Jew using tevel. Ch. 7: A priest and a Levite who sold field produce to an Israelite while still attached to the ground — tithes are due from detached produce before it is winnowed; once it is winnowed, the tithes are his [the buyer's] — he separates terumah and terumat ma'aser and gives them to any priest he wishes, and the rest is his. If he [the priest or Levite] sold him produce before he winnowed it, the tithes are his [the seller's]; once he winnowed it, the tithes are theirs [the buyers'] — he separates terumah and terumat ma'aser and gives them to any priest he wishes. One who gave his field in tenancy to a non-Jew, a Samaritan, or to one who is not trustworthy regarding tithes — even if the produce has not yet reached the stage of tithing obligation, he must tithe through him. One who gave his field in tenancy to an am ha-aretz — before the produce reaches the stage of tithing obligation, he need not tithe through him; once it has reached the stage of tithing obligation, he must tithe through him. How does he do it? He stands at the threshing floor and takes and is not concerned about what they ate, since there is no liability for swindlers. [This is the reading of the Rambam; in our version it is written reversed: before, etc. — he must, etc., and once they reached, etc. — he need not, etc.] Ch. 8: Similarly — one who had ten barrels of untithed impure tithe [ma'aser tevel tamei] and saw that one of them spilled or was uncovered — he says: this one is hereby made terumat ma'aser for these nine; with oil it is forbidden, because of priestly property. Ch. 1 of Terumot: The thief, robber, and extortionist — their terumah is valid terumah and their tithe is valid tithe, and their sanctification is valid sanctification; if the owners are pursuing them, their terumah is not valid, their tithe is not valid, and their sanctification is not valid. A sikarikon — his terumah is valid, his tithe is valid, and his sanctification is valid. The son, the hired worker, the slave, and the wife may separate terumah from what they eat, but they may not separate terumah from everything — since no person may separate terumah from what is not his. A son from his father's produce, and a wife from her dough — these may separate, since they separate with permission. A tenant farmer who separated terumah, and then the owner came and protested — if he protested before the separation, the separation is not valid; if he protested after the separation, the separation is valid, but he may only take tithe from his own share. A worker who separated from the threshing floor — his separation is not valid; if the owner told him "gather my threshing floor," his separation is valid, for a threshing floor is not gathered before terumah has been taken. Trustees may separate terumah and tithes from orphans' property; they may sell houses, fields, vineyards, animals, male and female slaves to feed the orphans and to do, etc. Ch. 2: One may not separate terumah from produce of the Land of Israel on produce from Syria, nor from produce of Syria on produce of the Land of Israel. An Israelite who purchased a field in Syria — it is like one who purchases in the hallway of Jerusalem; he separates terumah and tithes from it. An Israelite and a non-Jew who purchased a field in Syria — it is like untithed produce and tithes mixed together. R. Shimon ben Gamliel says: the Israelite's portion is obligated; the non-Jew's portion is exempt. An Israelite who purchased a field in Syria — even if he went back and sold it to a non-Jew — it remains obligated in tithes and the sabbatical year, since it was already obligated. But tenant farming, leasing, ancestral estates, and a non-Jew who mortgaged his field to an Israelite — even if the Israelite enforced legal claim on it — it is exempt from tithes and from the sabbatical year, etc. But orlah and mixed seeds of a vineyard [kil'ei ha-kerem] are equal for a non-Jew in the Land of Israel, Syria, and outside the Land — except that R. Yehudah says a non-Jew has no kerem reva'i in Syria, while the Sages say he does. One may not separate terumah from grain that has not reached one-third growth. How does one know whether it has reached one-third or not? If one plants it and it sprouts, it is known that it reached one-third; if one plants it and it does not sprout, it is known that it did not reach one-third. Ch. 3: Why did they say a mute may not separate terumah? Because he cannot recite the blessing; nevertheless they said it. Why did they say a blind person may not separate terumah? Because he cannot distinguish between the good and the bad; nevertheless they said it. Why did they say a drunk may not separate terumah? Because he lacks proper discernment; even though they said a drunk's transactions are valid, nevertheless they said it. Why did they say a person with a seminal emission may not separate terumah? Because he cannot recite the blessing; nevertheless they said it. Why did they say a naked person may not separate terumah? Because he cannot recite the blessing — but he may cover himself with straw or stubble or anything and recite the blessing. If one was on his way to separate terumah, first tithe, and ma'aser ani — from when does he recite the blessing over them? From when he separates them. If he separated them and intends to still designate them by name, they do not have sanctity until he designates them by name; if he does not intend to designate them by name, once he separates them they are sanctified. One who separated terumah from a basket and found produce at the sides of the basket — these are terumah, since his intention was to separate on behalf of everything. One who separates terumah from a threshing floor must direct his mind toward what is in the grain pile, what is in the straw, what is on the sides, what is in the threshing floor. One who separates terumah from a winepress must direct his mind toward what is in the grape skins and what is in the grape seeds. One who separates terumah from a cistern must direct his mind toward what is in the grape pulp. If he did not direct his mind — the rabbinic enactment is that he separates for everything. One who brought his produce into his house — even if it is scattered — he separates from one [portion] for all. Two storerooms in a single upper story — he separates from each one separately. Sacks of grain, rounds of dried figs, and baskets of dried figs — they are all in one cluster and one separates and tithes from one for another. Ch. 4: One may separate wheat as terumah for bread, but not bread for wheat — except in proportion. One may separate figs as terumah for dried figs by measure, and dried figs for figs by count; but not figs for dried figs by count, nor dried figs for figs by measure. [All of this is a scribal error and should read the reverse, as written above in §63.] One may not separate from wet for dry, nor from dry for wet; but if he did separate, the separation is valid, etc. One may separate clear wine as terumah for wine that is not clear, but not wine that is not clear for clear wine. R. Yehudah says: even wine that is not clear for clear wine, provided he separates from the better. R. Yehudah b. R. Yose says in his father's name: one may separate wine as terumah for vinegar, but one may not separate vinegar for wine — only in proportion. [See above s.k. 108.] One who separated terumah from a barrel of wine and it was found to be vinegar — if it was known to have been vinegar before the separation, the separation is not valid; if it turned to vinegar after the separation, it is terumah; [if it is] doubtful — it is terumah and he must separate again: these are the words of Rabbi. For Rabbi says wine and vinegar are two different kinds, while the Sages say they are one kind. If one intended to separate wine for wine but what was in his hand was vinegar — if what was in his hand was vinegar, the separation is not valid; if what was in his hand was wine, he separates and then goes back and separates for the vinegar. If he was examining the barrel to separate from it on an ongoing basis and it turned out to be vinegar — within three days it is certain; from then onward it is doubtful. Ch. 5: Impure terumah is removed at one part in sixty; the following are removed at one in sixty, etc., and terumah that became impure through compulsion or in error. Ch. 10: How much should there be in terumat ma'aser of doubtfully tithed produce [demai] that one brings to a priest? One-eighth of an eighth. In what case? In demai; but in certainly untithed produce, even the smallest amount is forbidden. In what case? In impure [terumah]; but in pure [terumah], even the smallest amount. So it should read — and similarly you say regarding terumat of all other fruits: if it contains this measure, he gives it to the priest, and if not, he throws it into the fire and burns it. A priest's ox that was standing near an Israelite's ox, and similarly a priest's garment that was being woven alongside an Israelite's garment — one may light with them oil of terumah designated for burning; he goes up to the upper story and comes down to press, to do the priest's needs, but not to do the Israelite's needs; if he had a partnership with him in the store, it is permitted; and similarly a priest who was reclining in an Israelite's house — one may light for him the lamp with oil of burning terumah, even if the priest got up and left, one does not compel him to extinguish it until it goes out by itself. Ch. 2 of Ma'asrot: If one had a pile of onions, dried figs, or carobs on his rooftop — he sorts and eats, sorts and leaves on the table, sorts and throws before his animal, etc. — he may always feed bundles of sheaves until he makes them into bound sheaves. Ch. 3 of Ma'aser Sheni: One who sells produce to his fellow — if the seller says: I sold it to you as untithed, and the buyer says: you sold it to me as tithed — they compel the seller to set his own right. If the king's agents took his threshing floor, if, etc. One may not redeem second tithe in Jerusalem in our time; one may not separate second tithe in Jerusalem in our time; one may not desacralize second tithe in Jerusalem in our time; one may not sell second tithe in Jerusalem in our time; one may not take second tithe out of Jerusalem in our time; and if one took it out, it shall rot. One who says: the second tithe in this object is desacralized onto this issar without specifying a location — R. Shimon says: he designated the name; the Sages say: not until he says "to its north" or "to its south," etc. Ch. 6 of Berakhot: If one was on his way to separate terumot and tithes, he says: Blessed are You... who has sanctified us to separate terumah and tithes. From when does he recite the blessing over them? From when he separates them. Ch. 3 of Demai: A ḥaver [one scrupulous about tithes] who was sitting at the feasts of an am ha-aretz and at his meals — even if they see him take and eat immediately, take and drink immediately, he has no established presumption regarding tithes — perhaps he tithed in his heart; similarly a son of a ḥaver, etc.
