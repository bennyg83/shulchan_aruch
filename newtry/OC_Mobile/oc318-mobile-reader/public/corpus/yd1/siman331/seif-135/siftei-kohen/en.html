Just as one does not, etc. Since pure maaser sheni in Eretz Yisrael had to be brought to Jerusalem and eaten there, as explained above in seif 132, once it was brought there and entered the wall it had to be eaten within the wall and not taken outside the wall of Jerusalem, as it says, "You may not eat within your gates," etc., and "You shall eat before Hashem your God," etc. Once it entered the place of its eating, you may not take it out to eat it outside; if one ate it outside, he is lashed. But if he did not want to bring it there, he could redeem it and bring the money to Jerusalem, as it says, "and you shall give it for money," etc.; the maaser sheni then becomes non-sacred and he eats it in his home. But after he brought it to Jerusalem and brought it within the wall, he had to eat it specifically there and not redeem it, as it says, "if the place is too far from you," etc., "and you shall give it for money," etc.; it is redeemed when the place is distant, not when the place is near. All this applies to pure maaser sheni. But tamei maaser sheni is forbidden to eat, as it says, "I did not consume it in tumah," meaning whether it was tamei and I was pure, or I was tamei and it was pure. Where are we warned about both? Regarding bodily tumah, it is written, "a person who touches it and is tamei until evening shall not eat," etc., and maaser sheni is called sacred. Regarding its own tumah as well, it is written about a blemished bekhor, "within your gates you shall eat it, the tamei and the tahor," meaning even a tamei and a tahor person eat from it in one bowl and we are not concerned. Here, regarding maaser sheni, it says, "You may not eat within your gates," and we expound that the Merciful One says: that which I permitted you regarding a blemished bekhor, where it says "within your gates" and permits eating it in tumah, whether its own tumah or bodily tumah, since one with bodily tumah eats it and makes it tamei while a tahor eats with him, that is its own tumah; all this you may not eat within your gates regarding maaser. Thus tamei maaser sheni is not eaten. But it may be redeemed even in Jerusalem, as it says, "you are not able to carry it," and Chazal said that carrying here means eating, as it says, "and he sent portions from before him." The verse means: if the distance is great, namely pure maaser outside Jerusalem, or even if it is in Jerusalem but you cannot eat it because it became tamei, then "you shall give it for money," etc. This was its law in Eretz Yisrael. But nowadays, just as one does not eat pure maaser sheni in Jerusalem, since it is not redeemed in a near place while pure even though we do not eat it, and one does not redeem or sell it, and if it entered Jerusalem even nowadays one does not remove it from there but leaves it there until it rots, therefore one does not separate maaser sheni in Jerusalem nowadays. Rather, one takes the produce while still tevel outside Eretz Yisrael, separates it there, and redeems it there. If one separated it in the city nowadays, it must rot. When is this said? Regarding maaser sheni that did not become tamei. But if it became tamei, one redeems it in Jerusalem. One may prepare it to receive tumah so that it immediately becomes tamei when touched, since we are tamei, and it will then have a permitted redemption there in Jerusalem.
