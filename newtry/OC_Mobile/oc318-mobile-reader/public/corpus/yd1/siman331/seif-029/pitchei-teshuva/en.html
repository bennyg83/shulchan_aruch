A person may appoint an agent. See Mishnat Chachamim, beginning of Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah, Tzafnat Paneach, letter 28. He writes that if one appoints an agent to separate his terumah and the agent then separates it, the principal apparently cannot afterward seek annulment. Based on what is explained above in siman 323, we say that the agent has already completed his agency, and the principal no longer has the legal status of owner for purposes of annulment. See the general introduction there for a lengthy responsum in the name of the author's father. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 320, disagrees. He writes that where one appoints an agent to separate terumah or challah, it is obvious that both the agent and the owner can seek annulment. If the agent seeks annulment of his declaration and regrets from the outset the intent by which he designated this portion, the designation is uprooted retroactively and the heap or dough returns to tevel. The agent then separates another terumah or challah because his original agency was never completed. If the owner seeks annulment, however, he must regret the underlying decision to rely on that particular agent. The original agency is thereby undone, and a new appointment of agency is required. Although ordinarily once an agency has been completed it can no longer be canceled, one who appoints an agent to make a vow or designation on his behalf is different. As a best practice, both agent and owner should have their declaration annulled. Regarding the blessing already recited, there is no concern that it was a blessing in vain. Certainly one should not seek annulment without cause and thereby create the need for another blessing, but if for a valid reason one did annul and must separate again with a second blessing, the first blessing was not in vain. The mitzvah of separating terumah and challah includes the legal possibility of later seeking annulment, and the blessing was recited on a separation made in precisely that legally valid manner.
