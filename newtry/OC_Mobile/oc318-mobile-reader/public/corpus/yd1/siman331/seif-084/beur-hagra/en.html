It is permitted to employ a device, etc. This appears in chapter 5 of Berachot, chapter 1 of Pesachim, chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah, chapter 6 of Menachot, and chapter 2 of Niddah. See Rashi and Tosafot there. Only one's animal may eat a fixed meal, but a person may eat only casually, as stated in Yom Tov 13a: one who brought stalks inside, etc. This is unlike R. Ephraim, who permits even a fixed meal for a person in such a case based on the Menachot sugya. The other sugyot also seem to support him, but Rashi and Tosafot reconcile them all. Rashba, responsum 361, agrees with R. Ephraim and explains that 'his animal' refers to the device: he acts as though it is for his animal, so as not to appear to rob the priestly tribe. In Yom Tov, casual eating is permitted only while the grain remains in stalks, since it may yet become obligated if he removes, smooths, and brings it in; but if he winnowed it inside the house, even fixed eating is permitted. Nevertheless, Tosafot's view is primary, and Rambam's wording likewise indicates this, for he says that his animal, etc., and that he winnows only a little.
For, etc. Meaning, Tosafot's question in Bava Metzia there, s.v. not from when they are smoothed: this is difficult, etc. Tosafot distinguishes wheat and barley from cucumbers and gourds. Rambam instead distinguishes the preceding seif, where one begins to finish the entire crop, from here.
