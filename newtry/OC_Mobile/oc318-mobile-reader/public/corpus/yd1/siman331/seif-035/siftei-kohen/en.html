Produce belonging to partners. Levush explains that partners' produce is obligated because Scripture says “your terumot” and “your tithes” in the plural. Ordinarily either partner may separate without explicit permission, since each acts as the other's agent. If one separates and the other, unaware of it, separates again, the second separation is ineffective when their established practice is to rely on one another, even if the first chose a different generous or stingy measure. If their practice is unknown, the first is deemed the other's agent only when he separated the same measure the second intended. If he separated less, each separation is effective for that partner's own share. Levush questions how this applies to tithes, which always have a fixed measure. In the present era, he concludes, the second separation is ineffective both for tithes, because the first already removed the exact tithe, and for terumah, because any amount suffices and the produce was already repaired.
