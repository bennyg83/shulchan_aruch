And he need not, etc. Although the Yerushalmi in chapter 1, brought by the Rash there on mishnah 1, concludes there as halakhah, etc., that it is specifically after the fact, our Gemara does not accept that Yerushalmi. At the beginning of Kiddushin chapter 2 it implies that even after the fact it does not work, from the statement, “since,” etc.: “you,” and not partners, etc., and not one who separates terumah from what is not his. Since it says “and not one who separates,” it implies even after the fact. Likewise in Gittin 52a, regarding apotropos, it answers: here to feed, etc., unlike what the Yerushalmi says there. All such cases work similarly, such as partners where one did not separate the proper measure, and all the more so according to Rabbi Akiva; see the Rash there. Likewise regarding a sharecropper, as written in seif 37: if before, etc.
If they protested, etc. Yerushalmi, brought by the Rash there: in what case do we stand, etc.; what is “according to measure,” etc. He ruled like Rabbi Yose, who explains the sages' words, as he wrote in his commentary there. However, he should have said specifically according to his fellow's measure. One can say this is because at the end of the first chapter of Temurah the words of Rabbi Yose are taught in the language of the sages. Nevertheless, it is not compelled that our Gemara disagrees with the Yerushalmi, for one can say “according to his fellow's measure,” and he also did not explain plainly what it means. He explains “protesting against each other” unlike the Rash, and explains that each relies on his fellow; see Baer HaGolah.
And if he did not separate, etc. What is stated there, “the second one's terumah is terumah,” means also the second; Rabbi Akiva and the sages do not dispute at all regarding the first, and Rabbi Yose comes to explain when they dispute or to decide between them.
And it appears to me that nowadays, etc. His words require study, for regarding maaser what is “his fellow's measure,” when its measure is explicit? See Shach.
But for terumah, etc. For all are equal regarding its measure, and now there is no practical difference between a generous eye or, etc.
