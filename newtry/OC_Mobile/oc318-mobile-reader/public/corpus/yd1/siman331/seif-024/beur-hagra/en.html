Terumat maaser, etc. First chapter of Yom Tov 13b: he calculates, etc., and we rule like the sages. Tosafot, however, wrote that even according to the sages it is permitted by estimation, and according to Abba Elazar ben Gimmel it is a mitzvah to do by estimation. See Tosafot, Gittin 31a, Menachot 54b, and Bekhorot 58b. But the Rash agreed with the Rambam from the Gemara in Yom Tov above; see there in chapter 1 mishnah 7.

A matter, etc. Mishnah in chapter 4, and as stated in the Yerushalmi chapter 1 on the Mishnah “one may not separate terumah by measure,” etc.: there we learned that one who counts is praiseworthy, one who measures is more praiseworthy, and one who weighs is most praiseworthy of the three; and here you say so? Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said: here for terumah gedolah, there for terumat maaser. And a baraita taught Elazar ben Gimmel, etc.; see there. Tosafot in all the places cited above and the Rash there brought it.
If it was, etc.; from both. Yerushalmi chapter 4 there: “one who counts is praiseworthy.” Rav Huna said: praiseworthy more than one who separates by estimation. Rav Chiyya son of Rabbi Hillel said: the Mishnah says so, “and one who weighs is most praiseworthy of the three.” Rabbi Chinena said: explain it as referring to all three, and you learn nothing from it. Tosafot there and there and the Rash there brought it. According to the Rambam, Rav Huna establishes it like Abba Elazar ben Gimmel and holds that nevertheless measuring is more praiseworthy; Rav Chiyya says it follows the sages and estimation is forbidden. See the Rash there.
