Produce, etc.; if, etc. Like Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon in Menachot there, for the halakhah follows Rabbi Yehudah against his colleagues. Also, we rule like the view in Gittin there that a non-Jew has no acquisition in Eretz Yisrael; according to that view, “your grain” and not the processing of a non-Jew, as stated there and in Bekhorot 11b: who smoothed them, etc. Although the Mishnah in Demai chapter 5 follows Rabbi Meir, who obligates, it is not halakhah, for the sugya in Gittin above, according to the view that a non-Jew has no acquisition, holds that the non-Jew's smoothing exempts, and in any case it is exempt. See Tosafot there and there and there, who wrote at length about this. But the Rambam took the plain sense of the Gemara, and likewise the Mishnayot in Challah, that the rolling of a non-Jew exempts, as stated there in Menachot: who, etc. Although in Menachot 67 it says it is nevertheless rabbinically obligated, the Rambam explains that this refers to produce that grew in a Jew's land and was smoothed by a non-Jew.
