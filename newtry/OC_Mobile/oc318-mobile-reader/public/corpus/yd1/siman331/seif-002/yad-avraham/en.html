(Shulchan Aruch seif 31) One who separates terumah without authorization, etc.; if the owner came and said to him, “Go to the better produce,” his terumah is terumah. See the Taz, who elaborated to challenge this from the sugya in Bava Metzia, which establishes the case there as one where he had appointed him an agent. He concludes that since terumah is a mitzvah, we say that since now it is acceptable to him, presumably from the beginning it was also acceptable to him, and a disclosure of intent suffices. In Ketzot HaChoshen siman 262 he asks that the difficulty still remains: why should this help regarding one who went down into another’s field, etc.? There that reason does not apply, since the mitzvah is not incumbent upon another person to separate from his fellow’s produce. Therefore it appears that the reason he ruled that even if he did not appoint him an agent initially, a later disclosure of consent suffices, is because at the beginning of the second chapter of Kiddushin the Gemara asks why the verse says “also you,” and it does not answer that it is needed for this comparison; this shows that this comparison is not accepted. Also from the words of the Ran and the Rosh in Nedarim 36b, and the Chiddushei HaRitva in Gittin 66, it appears that disclosure of intent helps regarding terumah without agency. One may add flour and water to this: the Raavad in chapter 6 of Hilkhot Gerushin writes that the reason a minor cannot appoint an agent is because we expound “also you” to include your agents: just as you are of legal understanding, so too your agents must be of legal understanding. Ketzot HaChoshen at the end of siman 188 wrote regarding this that this is so only according to the view that a mufla samukh le-ish is not Torah-level; according to that view, specifically an adult and one of legal understanding is required even regarding terumah. But according to the view that a mufla samukh le-ish is Torah-level, and according to that view his terumah is terumah by Torah law, then one cannot exclude a minor from agency through “also you”; end quote. According to this, the Bava Metzia requirement that he must have appointed him an agent initially, and that a later disclosure of consent does not suffice, because it is written “also you,” just as you act with your knowledge, meaning initially, so too your agents must act with your knowledge initially, applies only according to the view that a mufla samukh le-ish is not Torah-level. But according to the view that it is Torah-level, we do not expound “just as you act with your knowledge,” nor do we exclude from “you” except one who separates what is not his and whose owner did not disclose at all that it is acceptable to him. But whenever he disclosed that it is acceptable to him, even at the end, we call it “you.” If so, the Rambam, who holds in Hilkhot Nedarim, Nezirut, and Terumot that mufla samukh le-ish is Torah-level, therefore does not expound at all “just as you act with your knowledge, so too your agents.” Therefore he follows his own view, that his terumah is terumah even when he did not appoint him an agent initially, provided that he disclosed afterward that it was acceptable to him.
