In our times, etc. For R. Yochanan and Reish Lakish do not dispute in Yevamot 81a except according to R. Yossi ; but according to the Rabbanan, R. Yochanan agrees, as stated there on 82b. See Tosafot there 81a s.v. "a"l R. Yochanan," etc., and there 82b s.v. "yerushah," etc. ; and R. Yochanan replied: regarding tithes, etc. ; and R. Yochanan replied: one may say, etc. And in the Yerushalmi, beginning of chapter 6 of Shevi'it, they dispute this ; as it is stated there: "From now on, since they were exiled, they should be exempt ; it is written: 'And all the congregation did, etc., for they had not done [so] since the days of Joshua bin Nun, etc.' ; this equates their coming in the days of Ezra to their coming in the days of Joshua: just as their coming in the days of Joshua they were exempt and then became obligated, so too their coming in the days of Ezra they were exempt and then became obligated. Through what did they become obligated? R. Yitzchak bar Chiyya said: from Torah law ; as it is written: 'And Hashem your God will bring you into the land which your fathers possessed, and you shall possess it' ; equating your possession to that of your fathers: just as your fathers' possession was by Torah law, etc. R. Elazar said: they voluntarily accepted the tithes upon themselves, etc." And so held the Amoraim in the last chapter of Terumot there ; like R. Elazar, that it is rabbinic ; as stated there: "R. Chalfata ben Shaul taught: a pot in which terumah was cooked, etc. ; like the one who says they voluntarily accepted the tithes upon themselves." Cited by the Rashbam there in Mishnah 8. And so too in many places in the Yerushalmi. And in the Gemara, chapter 3 of Pesachim 44a: "Leave terumah in our times ; it is rabbinic." And there 9a-10a: "Perhaps, etc., regarding terumah, etc." On this Yerushalmi the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch relied ; that even in the days of Ezra the terumah and tithes are rabbinic. And what is written in Yevamot 82b: "and I say according to R. Yochanan" ; implying the Rabbanan dispute R. Yochanan and hold that since the second sanctification was also annulled, [the obligation] was only rabbinic, but in the days of Ezra all agree one is obligated ; and so wrote Rashi there. But this is not correct, for we rule in chapter 1 of Hullin (7a) and elsewhere that the second sanctification was not annulled. Rather the meaning is: R. Yochanan holds that in the days of Ezra they became obligated from Torah law ; from the verse "first possession and second possession," as stated in the Yerushalmi cited above. But the Rabbanan hold that the second possession entailed no obligation at all ; as stated above ; only rabbinic, and it was also not annulled, even though it is rabbinic. And the Rambam wrote at the end of chapter 6 of Hilkhot Beit HaBehirah that the Temple and Jerusalem are sanctified with the first sanctification ; as stated at the end of chapter 1 of Megillah (10a), and at the end of Zevahim, and in Shevuot 16a ; and like Rava, whose Mishnah follows him and the Baraita of Abba Shaul accords with him ; as stated there. And there in the dispute of R. Elazar and R. Yochanan ; all agree, etc. See Tosafot there s.v. "tani," etc. But the sanctity of the Land was only sanctified with the second sanctification ; as stated in chapter 1 of Hullin and elsewhere. See Tosafot in Shevuot there s.v. "de-khulei alma," etc., and Tosafot in Yevamot 82b s.v. "yerushah," etc. And with this the difficulty of Tosafot in Megillah there s.v. "de-khulei alma" is resolved. Even though in the conclusion there they make them depend on each other ; as stated there and in Shevuot there: "but only like those Tannaim, etc." ; this is only because they were compelled to find a Tannaitic dispute; but in truth we do not rule this way, and we follow Rav Mari there. And even R. Yitzchak retracted, and there is no one who holds it was not sanctified for the future. And not like Rav Nachman. Therefore, at first he wished to cite a Tannaitic dispute from the Temple and rejected it ; "all agree, etc."
(Likkut) In our times, etc. Sefer HaTerumah also brings proof from the Midrash Rabbah on parashat Vayechi, and from Midrash Ruth, and from the Yerushalmi ; that they voluntarily accepted the tithes upon themselves. So too in the Gemara at the end of Makkot 23b ; unlike Rashi there ; but he wrote there that since they accepted it voluntarily, they are obligated from Torah law, as stated there, and as Tosafot in Yevamot wrote. But nonetheless, terumah in our times is rabbinic ; since even the sanctification of Ezra was annulled, unlike R. Yossi who says "first and second sanctification, etc." ; as stated in chapter 3 of Pesachim and chapter 6 of Nazir: "Leave terumah in our times ; it is rabbinic." And it cannot be said "he holds terumah, etc." ; implying this is the law. And so too R. Yochanan according to the Rabbanan. And certainly the sanctification of Joshua was annulled ; for Rabbi permitted Beit She'an, as stated there. He wrote: nonetheless, the sanctity of the Temple and Jerusalem ; the first sanctification ; was not annulled, as stated at the end of Zevahim and the end of chapter 1 of Megillah. And so too R. Yochanan held in Zevahim 107, and likewise in the beginning of chapter 2 of Shevuot where R. Yirmiyah ben Bavel poses a question, etc. And so in chapter 3 of Makkot (19a), and Zevahim, and Temurah, and elsewhere ; R. Yossi in the name of R. Yehudah: "Is it possible that a person could bring up second-tithe, etc." ; and concludes that it is sanctified for the future. Even though in chapter 4 of Bava Metzia (53b) and the end of Sanhedrin it answers that the partitions fell and he holds even the partitions did not sanctify for the future ; the law does not follow this. And the distinction between the Temple and Jerusalem on the one hand, and the Land on the other, is compelling ; for otherwise R. Yochanan would contradict R. Yehudah, and R. Yossi would contradict Reish Lakish who holds the Rabbanan dispute R. Yossi, and R. Yehoshua would contradict R. Yochanan ; as stated there in Tosafot, and see there where he elaborated. Nonetheless it requires further study to reject the sugya in chapter 1 of Hullin and chapter 1 of Chagigah and elsewhere where we say "it escaped his notice, etc." ; that the second sanctification is sanctified for the future. From all the above it is proven this is rabbinic, and thus the Rambam's words are compelling. He further wrote that today a non-Jew has possessory rights in the Land of Israel since [the obligation] is rabbinic ; as I wrote there (below in §28). And likewise Shemittah in our times. He wrote: and with this the difficulty of Tosafot in Sanhedrin 26a s.v. "agiston," etc., is resolved ; and what is stated there: "go out, etc., on account of [Roman] taxation" ; meaning even regarding Israelite land. (End of quote.)
