They are exempt. Even by rabbinic law. Although we rule that a non-Jew has no acquisitional right to exempt from tithes — that applies only when the produce came back into Israelite hands, as explained in §3. But as long as the produce is in the non-Jew's hands at the time of the winnowing, having been acquired before reaching the stage of tithing obligation [onat ha-ma'aser], they are entirely exempt:
Even though the non-Jew completed it. And winnowed it — for by Torah law it is exempt, since "your grain" [deganekha] excludes the grain of a non-Jew. It is obligated in all [tithes] by rabbinic law, since once the produce reached the Israelite's hand at the stage of tithing obligation and became rabbinically obligated, it does not revert to being exempt. And the Rambam wrote: similarly, a non-Jew who watches an Israelite's produce — since its winnowing was performed by the non-Jew, it is obligated in terumot and tithes only by rabbinic law. See the Kessef Mishneh there at length.
