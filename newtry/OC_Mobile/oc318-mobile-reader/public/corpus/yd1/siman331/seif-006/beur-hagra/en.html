An Israelite who sold, etc. Gittin 47a: come and hear, an Israelite who bought, etc. See Rashi there s.v. “for already,” etc., and s.v. “not,” etc. As stated according to the view that a non-Jew has acquisition, the same applies according to the view that he has no acquisition, where the non-Jew smoothed it. The Gemara did not answer in such a way because the phrase “and bought it back, etc., it is obligated” implies that he bought it while attached, as written in seif 9; see there. The Rambam wrote that even after they reached the season of tithes, they are obligated only rabbinically. Likewise, a non-Jew who completed the processing of an Israelite's produce: since its finishing was in the hand of a non-Jew, it is obligated in terumah and maaser only by rabbinic law. He explains that what is stated in Menachot 67a, “rabbinically, a decree because,” etc., refers to an Israelite's produce where he hires a non-Jew to smooth it, but a non-Jew's produce is exempt even rabbinically. This answers Tosafot's difficulty there s.v. “a decree,” etc.; and what they further asked there, “and if you say, that which we say,” etc. What is stated in Bekhorot, that if he certainly exchanged them they are obligated, refers to where an Israelite smoothed them, as they established above there. In Shulchan Aruch they omitted all this, for there is no practical difference nowadays, as written in seif 2.
