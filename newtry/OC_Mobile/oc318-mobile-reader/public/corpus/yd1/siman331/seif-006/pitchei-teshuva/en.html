A person may appoint an agent. See the book Mishnat Ḥakhamim, beginning of Hilkhot Yesodei ha-Torah, in Tzefnat Pa'ane'aḥ there, letter 28, who wrote that if one appoints an agent to separate his terumah, and the agent separated it, the owner is thereafter compelled to revoke it, as appears from what was explained above in §323 — since we say he already fulfilled his agency and no longer has the status of an owner regarding revocation. See the comprehensive introduction there, a lengthy responsum on this in the name of his father the Gaon z"l. [See Resp. Ḥatam Sofer §320, who disagrees with this view. He wrote there that one who appoints an agent to separate terumah or to separate ḥallah — it is clear that both can revoke: the agent and the master of the house. But if the agent revokes, he revokes from the root his intention to separate from this side, and the vow is uprooted from the start, and the heap or the dough reverts to its untithed state — so he must go back and separate another terumah or ḥallah, since he has not yet fulfilled his agency at all. But when the master of the house revokes, he must revoke from the root what he had in mind when he relied on the intention of this agent, and thus the original agency is nullified and he must go back and appoint an agent anew. Even though in general, once the agency is performed one can no longer nullify it — nevertheless, when one appoints an agent to make a vow on his behalf, etc. For the sake of a good outcome, both should have their vow released: the agent and the master of the house. And he said regarding the blessing — that since he already recited the blessing, there is no concern whatsoever of a blessing recited in vain. Even though it is improper to ask for no reason and cause an unnecessary blessing to be recited a second time, nevertheless if it happened that he repented for some reason and will go back and separate and recite the blessing a second time, there is no concern at all about the first blessing — for it was not in vain, since thus we are commanded and thus He commanded us to separate terumah and ḥallah with the understanding that one retains the ability to revoke them, and thus He commanded us to separate them on that understanding; and so he did. See there.]
