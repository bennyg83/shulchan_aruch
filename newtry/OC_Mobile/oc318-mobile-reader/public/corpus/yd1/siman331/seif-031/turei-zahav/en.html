One who separates terumah without authorization. There is much to wonder about this language of the Rambam. At the beginning of Perek Elu Metziot the Gemara brings a baraita that teaches the law of one who gathered from his fellow’s field without authorization and separated terumah: if the owner is concerned for theft, his terumah is not terumah; if he is not concerned for theft, his terumah is terumah. How does one know whether he is concerned, etc.? If he said to him, “Go to the better produce, etc.” The Gemara challenges Abbaye from this, for Abbaye says there that despair without knowledge is not despair, whereas here we say that since now it is pleasing to the householder that this person separated, certainly it was pleasing to him initially as well. Rava explained it according to Abbaye: here we are dealing with a case where he made him an agent. So too it is reasonable, for if he did not make him an agent, why should it be terumah? We require “with your knowledge,” etc. Since we rule like Abbaye in this, automatically it is not terumah unless he made him an agent. Why then did the Rambam rule here that even if he originally gathered in order to take it for himself, in which case he was not an agent at all, it is terumah? Granted, regarding the Tur, who also wrote this law but did not copy the second division, “or he went down into the field, etc.,” but only the first division, namely one who separates terumah without authorization, we can resolve it by saying that this is what he means: the separation was without authorization regarding which species he should take, but nevertheless he initially appointed him as an agent. Maharshal explained the Tur’s words this way. But regarding the Rambam, it is difficult, and the Beit Yosef already asked this. He answered that the Rambam holds that we do not rule like that resolution that it speaks where he appointed him as an agent. His reason is from what is stated there later in the Gemara: Mari bar Isak said to his sharecropper, “Why did you not bring the sages from these finer ones?” Yet Mar Zutra did not eat. Rav Ashi and Ameimar said to him: why did you not eat now? Rava said: they said “go to the better ones” only with terumah, because it is a mitzvah. And in the second chapter of Kiddushin 52, a certain brewer betrothed with beer-sediment; the householder came and said, “Why did you not bring from this sharper one?” Rava said: they said “go to the better ones” only regarding terumah. This implies that even in such a case, where they took his produce without authorization, if they separated terumah from it and the householder came and said, “Go to the better ones,” it is terumah. Consequently the sugya of Elu Metziot, which establishes it as where he made him an agent, falls away. I am astonished that a holy mouth would say this. From the case of Mari bar Isak there is obviously no proof, for they needed to establish it as where he appointed him as an agent only in a case where we must say that he was pleased retroactively, such as terumah; from this there was a difficulty for Abbaye, and therefore it was necessary to answer that he had made him an agent. But in the case of Mari bar Isak there is no need to say that he made him an agent, for the whole question there was against Mar Zutra, who did not want to eat after hearing Mari bar Isak say, “Why did you not bring from these finer ones?” This is clear. From the second chapter of Kiddushin there is also no proof at all. Although there too it speaks retroactively, namely whether the betrothal is betrothal retroactively, nevertheless there is no proof from there, for there Rava stated that law, and Rava follows his own view in Perek Elu Metziot that despair without knowledge is despair, and he need not establish the terumah baraita as where he appointed him an agent at all, as the Gemara says there explicitly: Rava explained it according to Abbaye as where he made him an agent, implying that he himself does not hold so. Although the Gemara says there, “so too it is reasonable,” because of the question that we require “with your knowledge,” and consequently even Rava should have to say it speaks where he made him an agent, and if so we must say that the Kiddushin sugya disagrees with the Elu Metziot sugya, since there it speaks without appointing him an agent, still the difficulty remains: granted, according to that reasoning the Talmud retracted from the question that we require “with your knowledge,” but who says it retracted from the question it asked there against Abbaye because of despair without knowledge? We do not see any answer to that question except as Rava explained according to Abbaye, that it speaks where he made him an agent. But Rava himself does not hold so, as in that statement of his in Kiddushin. This is certainly an irrefutable point. How can we find our hands and feet in the study hall to disagree with an explicit establishment in Perek Elu Metziot according to the halachah, with no support from anywhere? Tosafot in Kiddushin indeed explained that there too it speaks with an agent. Yet this is no question against the Rambam, for he explains according to the implication of the words, without an agent. What is even more exceedingly difficult to me regarding the Rambam’s view is that he wrote even where one went down into his fellow’s field without authorization in order to take produce for himself and afterward separated terumah, that it is terumah if better produce was found there. But the baraita says that whenever there is concern for theft, his terumah is not terumah, and here there is certainly theft, even if he says “go to the better ones,” according to Rava’s conclusion that “go to the better ones” helps only regarding terumah because it is a mitzvah, but elsewhere we say he said it out of embarrassment. For this reason Mar Zutra did not eat the fruit. One who examines Rashi there in Elu Metziot will see that he guarded himself from this question, for he explained the baraita’s phrase “if he is concerned for theft” as meaning whether what he separates without authorization is considered theft. He meant that the matter of terumah does not depend on whether there is actual theft of his fellow’s money; otherwise the question I mentioned would be difficult. Therefore he explained that it depends on whether separating without authorization is considered theft. But according to the Rambam, who wrote explicitly even with actual theft, namely that he gathered the produce in order to take it for himself, and he says that since there is no theft because the householder said “go to the better ones,” it is terumah, this is certainly a great wonder. In truth, regarding monetary theft, saying “go to the better ones” does not help; it helps specifically regarding terumah. This is undoubtedly a powerful question. What appears to me to resolve is that the Rambam holds: since we see that Rava and Mar Zutra say that “go to the better ones” helps specifically with terumah, because it is a mitzvah and it is pleasing to him, but not elsewhere, you must say that since it is a mitzvah we say that because now he is pleased, certainly initially too this was his intent, that whoever wishes to separate from his better produce may come and separate. This is not included in separating without authorization, for there it does not help because we have no proof that this person’s intent was so. You must say this, for otherwise it is difficult: we require “with your knowledge” according to all, and according to Rava it does not speak where he appointed him as an agent, as implied in Kiddushin. Rather, necessarily here we say that it is considered “with your knowledge,” as though he explicitly said at the beginning that he made him an agent for this. If so, we say the same for Abbaye: it need not speak where he made him an agent, and the Gemara’s question is not difficult, that since despair without knowledge is not despair, why here is it terumah? For this too we say the same logic, that it is as though he explicitly said so initially, since it is a mitzvah. The reason Rava explained according to Abbaye that it speaks where he made him an agent, and did not say that a mitzvah is different, is that Rava was not certain that according to Abbaye he holds this distinction that a mitzvah is different; therefore he answered that it speaks where he made him an agent. But he himself holds that a mitzvah is different. If so, we say in the conclusion that Abbaye too holds this distinction and there is no need to establish it with an agent. This is a necessary reasoning, because initially there were two questions: one specifically against Abbaye from despair without knowledge, and the second even against Rava from “with your knowledge.” Both can be answered by saying that he made him an agent. Since we see in Kiddushin that Rava does not establish it as where he made him an agent, we must say that he answers the second question by saying that a mitzvah is different. It is therefore pleasing to say that Abbaye too answers the question against him in this way. So it appears to me clear and correct according to the Rambam, who is not concerned with whether he made him an agent at all. As for what we asked above, that with actual theft Rava holds that “go to the better ones” does not help, so why should it be terumah whenever he gathered in order to take for himself? It appears to answer that the case is where everything he gathered for himself he separated entirely as terumah and left nothing for himself. Therefore there is no theft from the outset, because its end, that it was for a mitzvah, proves its beginning, as though he gathered it initially for terumah. Now that we have arrived at this, that according to the Rambam the case is without appointing him as an agent, the Tur’s view also works well, and his words mean as they sound, without appointing him as an agent. The words of the Semag, positive commandment 134, are also explicit without appointing him as an agent.
