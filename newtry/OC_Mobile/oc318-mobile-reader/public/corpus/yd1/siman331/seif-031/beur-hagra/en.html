One who separates, etc., or, etc. Kiddushin 52b, Bava Metzia 22a. The meaning of what is written there: how so — this is not comparable, i.e., all the more so applies to one who separates without permission — for even in such a case it takes effect if the produce is found to be superior:
In order to take them. The meaning is: even in such a case — and so it is evident there in the case of the tenant of Mari b. Isia, and in the case of the Sarsia agent. Even though in Bava Metzia ibid. we say according to Abaye — and we rule like him in the case of one appointed as agent — and we say "so too it is reasonable, etc." — the Rambam holds that according to the conclusion this does not apply, from what is stated there: Ameimar said to him, etc., and it was taught, etc., he said to him: thus R., etc. And similarly in Kiddushin, in the case of the Sarsia agent, etc. — and not as Tosafot labored to explain there s.v. im be-deshavviyei shali'aḥ — for the plain meaning of the Talmud does not imply so. The reason is that a religious duty is presumed to be agreeable, as written in Bava Metzia ibid.; see the Beit Yosef:
(Likkut) One who separates, etc. The Rambam's reading there: and does it make sense — and see Tosafot there s.v. ha-garsa, etc. — and the meaning is: and does it make sense? But indeed "all of you," etc. — meaning: and why do we need to resort to the superior produce? Since his agent is like himself! Rather, the case is, etc. But in truth there is no need for all of this — it is only needed for Rava's answer; but one need not resort to Rava's answer since "a religious duty is different" — and so it is evident from the case in Kiddushin 52b, where he did not appoint him as agent at all. And this is contrary to Tosafot there s.v. im, etc., who labored on this and wrote "and thus, etc.," going according to their reading that the text reads "so too it is reasonable, etc." (End.)
