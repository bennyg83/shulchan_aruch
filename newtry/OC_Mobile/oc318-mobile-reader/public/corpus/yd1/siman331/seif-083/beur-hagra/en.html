One of six, etc. The Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 4 says: light fixes for maaser, purchase fixes, salt fixes, terumah fixes, Shabbat fixes, and a protected courtyard fixes. All of these are explained in the Mishnah there. The Raavad objected: why did he not count seven, namely pickling? This is no question, for pickling and salting are counted as one, as stated in Chullin 97b and elsewhere: pickled is like cooked, salted is like boiling. Just as he does not count boiling by roasting and boiling by cooking as two, so too in the Yerushalmi cited above.
And all of them, etc. This is the end of chapter 4 of Yom Tov, and he holds that the same applies to light and salting. At first glance, at the end of the first chapter, where it says, "but he may not put into a pot," etc., it implies even before completion of the work. The Rash, however, answers there, s.v. aval, that since, etc.; or alternatively, etc.
How so? Produce that he intends, etc. As above in the preceding seif.
They entered the house. The same applies to a courtyard, even by Torah law, according to Rabbi Yochanan, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 4: just as a house fixes for maaser, so a courtyard fixes for maaser, etc., as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. He mentioned a house here only because a courtyard must be protected, as stated in the Yerushalmi above, in the Gemara in Bava Metzia there, and in the Mishnah in Maaserot chapter 3. He did not enter here into that distinction, and therefore used the case of a house; in chapter 4 he explains it. The words of the Kesef Mishneh are astonishing, for he infers from here that the Rambam ruled like Rabbi Yannai. But here he is not counting only Torah-level factors, since he lists all six things, and they are not all Torah law; only the house is, as above.
His. This is the Yerushalmi in chapter 2, brought by the Rash there on Mishnah 2: this teaches, etc.
Through the gate. This is in Bava Metzia there and at the beginning of chapter 6 of Berakhot.
And likewise if, etc. That is, after their work has been completed, and even casual eating is forbidden. This is why it says, "he may not eat," etc., for this entire seif deals with casual eating.
He brought them into the house, etc. This refers back to the earlier rule that a house fixes for maaser, and it is not speaking about selling them, etc. It is the rule stated above, that a house does not fix before completion of the work.
He began to complete, etc. This is in Bava Metzia there. See Tosafot there, s.v. lo mi-she-yefaksu, etc. It says "he began," as written later, "from when he begins to rub one," etc., and as stated, from when he began, etc.
And likewise, etc.; except for a basket, etc. This is Mishnah 4 in chapter 2, according to the Sages.
