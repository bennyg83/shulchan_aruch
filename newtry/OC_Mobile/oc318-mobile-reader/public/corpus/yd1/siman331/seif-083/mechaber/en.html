Four things fix fruits for ma'asrot: the house, sale, fire, and salt; terumah; and Shabbat. And all of them fix only a thing whose work was completed. How so? Fruits that he intends to bring to the house—even though their work was completed, he eats from them casual eating until they enter his house. They entered his house—they are fixed for ma'asrot and it is forbidden to eat from them until he tithes. And just as the house fixes for ma'aser, so the courtyard fixes for ma'aser—if the fruits entered the courtyard through the gate, they are fixed even though they did not enter inside the house. And likewise if he sold them, or cooked them in fire, or pickled them in salt, or separated terumah from them, or Shabbat entered upon them—he may not eat until he tithes, even though they did not reach the house. He brought them into the house before their work was completed—he eats from them casually. He began [to complete] their work after they entered the house—he is obligated to tithe everything. How so? He brought in gourds and melons before he rubbed them—once he begins to rub them, they are all fixed for ma'aser; and likewise everything similar. And likewise one who separates terumah from fruits whose work was not completed—it is permitted to eat from them casually, except for a basket of figs—for if he separated terumah before its work was completed, it is fixed for ma'aser:
