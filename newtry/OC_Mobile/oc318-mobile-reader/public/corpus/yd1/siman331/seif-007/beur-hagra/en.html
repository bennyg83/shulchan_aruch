A non-Jew sold, etc. Bekhorot 11b — except that an Israelite winnowed them in the non-Jew's domain. The Rambam explains: in the non-Jew's domain means that they reached the stage of tithing obligation while in the non-Jew's possession — as opposed to coming into the Israelite's possession, in which case he is obligated in everything, as written in §5. And what was written in §4 — "and if, etc., after they were detached" — the same applies here, that they reached the stage of tithing obligation while in the non-Jew's possession, even if they were still attached. But because the additional growth is still obligated, it is written in §5. And it states there that when an Israelite winnowed them — that implies if the non-Jew winnowed them, they are entirely exempt:
Since they came, etc. Because without this they are rabbinically obligated, as written in §6:
One who purchases, etc. Mishnah, last chapter of Ma'asrot. He ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as we rule by him where his reasoning is sound — and furthermore the Jerusalem Talmud states that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's view follows the approach of Rabban Gamliel, etc., as cited by the R"Sh there, and we rule like Rabban Gamliel as written in §8. He ruled not like R. Akiva but like the Sages [Tanna Kamma], and he omitted what is stated there: "and he picks in the normal way and continues" — implying he ruled like R. Yehudah. The Leḥem Mishneh on the Mishnah there writes that he understands Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel to hold the view of R. Yehudah and rule even more leniently; and since we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, all the more so like R. Yehudah. All this applies to attached produce, and all the more so to detached produce, as written in Gittin 47a that a non-Jew has acquisitional rights in Syria; see Rashi there s.v. she-ein kinyan, etc.
