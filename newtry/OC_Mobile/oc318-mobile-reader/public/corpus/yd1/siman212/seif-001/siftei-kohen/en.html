For a prohibition, etc., is written in it. The Ra'avad in his glosses to chapter 12 of Hilchos Shevuos and the Ran in chapter 2 of Nedarim explain that the reason is that the Torah contains passages of offerings and Divine Names, which are comparable to a davar hanadur, because through their writing and preparation for sanctity they become sacred. According to this, all the more so if one explicitly says, 'I make a vow by the Divine Names contained in it,' the vow takes effect even though he does not intend the prohibitions and vows written in the Torah. Likewise, if one vows by only some of the parchment sheets of a Sefer Torah, even though those particular sheets contain no prohibition or vow, the vow takes effect if they contain passages of offerings or Divine Names.
