"That is written." Ra'avad and Ran explain that the reason is that the Torah contains korbanos and Divine Names, which are like matters whose sanctity comes through designation, since by writing and preparation for holiness they become sacred. According to this, all the more so if he says explicitly, "I vow by the Divine Names contained in it," the vow takes effect even though he does not intend the prohibitions and vows written in the Torah. Likewise, if he vows by some of the Torah's parchment sheets, even if those sheets contain no prohibition or vow, since they contain Divine Names or passages concerning korbanos it is a vow. Taz writes: it appears to me that when one is obligated to take an oath, they should not administer it with the formula, "I hereby swear by the Sefer Torah," because he might later rationalize that his intention was only to the parchment. Rather, he should place his hand upon it; then the law is as though he holds it in his hand.
