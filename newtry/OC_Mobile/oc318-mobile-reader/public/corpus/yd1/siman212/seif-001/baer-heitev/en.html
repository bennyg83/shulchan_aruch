An am haaretz. It has already been explained above in siman 205 that nowadays everyone is judged like an am haaretz. Thus the Shach. That is written. The Raavad and the Ran wrote the reason: because korbanot and Divine Names are written in it, and these are like a davar hanadur, for through writing and preparation for holiness they become holy. According to this, all the more so if he explicitly said, "I vow by the Divine Names in it," it is a vow, even though his intent was not to the prohibition or vow written in it. Likewise, if he vowed by some sheets of the Torah, even if no prohibition or vow is written on those sheets, since Divine Names or korbanot are written in them it is a vow. The Taz writes that it appears to him that one who is obligated to swear should not be given an oath in this language, "I swear by the Torah scroll," for perhaps it will lead him to rule permissively for himself and say that his intent was to the parchment, unless he places his hand upon it; then his law is like one who holds it in his hand.
