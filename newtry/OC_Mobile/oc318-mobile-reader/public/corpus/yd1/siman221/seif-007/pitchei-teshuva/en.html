See responsum Zikhron Yitzchak siman 98 who wrote that if he slaughtered b'dieved, at first glance there is reason to forbid because we hold like Rava in Temurah that whatever the Merciful One said "do not do," if one did it is not effective — if so it is like dying on its own. However according to what Tosafot wrote there regarding one who swore not to divorce and transgressed and divorced — even for Rava it is effective since he uprooted the prohibition — so too here if he did it is effective — so ends his words. And see Nekudot HaKesef above siman 10 where I expanded on the matter of "if he did it is not effective" and wrote there that if because of this it does not apply, for the poskim do not hold the Tosafot's distinction between whether he uprooted or not, but for another reason it does not apply here alma — and it is kosher b'dieved — see there.
