The one who rents out, etc. Likewise Tosafot in Nedarim there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and they proved from what was stated in the preceding seif and rejected his reasoning specifically because they did not forbid explicitly — it was not his intention regarding what he rented; unlike when he forbade explicitly, as written in Arakhin 21a "how can he dwell" etc. And this is what is written here and in the preceding seif "and if he specified" etc. And they challenged from that in perek Af Al Pi (59b) where we say specifically when he redeems the field it becomes dedicated — and they resolved: specifically more than the rental price he can dedicate. And likewise that case in Arakhin — one cannot dwell because of the hekdesh portion; and that case in Af Al Pi is against the debt. And likewise they resolved in Arakhin there and in Ketubot there, and likewise Rosh in Nedarim there and likewise Ra"n. They further resolved in Nedarim there that regarding complete hekdesh specifically he can forbid the surplus over the debt, unlike konam; and likewise they resolved in Ketubot there that in such a case that case in Ketubot applies. And with this it is well what is written "and is there anything that these" etc. And further, since it is not upon the body of the field it is like konam — and this Ra"n also wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: specifically regarding hekdesh or general konam that is comparable to hekdesh, but not regarding specific konam — for only hekdesh uproots from lien, unlike konamot where the rabbis strengthened his lien as written in Ketubot. And Tosafot there s.v. konamot "if you ask" etc. "if you ask" etc., and likewise Rivash siman 255. And he wrote that Rabbeinu Tam follows his method that he holds even hekdesh of value uproots from lien as Tosafot wrote in Bava Kamma 33b s.v. mishum, and we learn from this — but all the later authorities rejected his words and hold like Rashi that specifically sanctity of the body uproots, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 117 — see there. They further resolved in Nedarim there to distinguish between real property whose end returns to the owner, unlike a landlord whose end returns to him — and likewise in Bava Kamma 91b, even though in Arakhin they refuted this reasoning, and likewise Rosh in Nedarim there, except that Rosh combined these two resolutions: that case in Ketubot there is because it is real property, and that case in Nedarim is not because they did not forbid explicitly but because it is konam. But in Bava Kamma there he wrote to distinguish between that case in Arakhin and that case in Ketubot as this writing between real property and landlord, and that case in Nedarim because they did not forbid explicitly — and he rejected in this the reasoning of Ritzba who holds like Rabbeinu Tam as above. And afterward he wrote the first resolution of Tosafot to distinguish between what is against the rental and the surplus, and challenged in Arakhin "how can he dwell" because of the hekdesh portion; and he wrote: from this it seems to me that regarding hekdesh that takes effect on the body of the land he can forbid the portion he has in it, unlike a benefit prohibition that does not take effect on the body of the land but only forbids his benefit upon the resident — as long as he rents from others the benefit is not his, for daily rental is a sale and behold he sold his benefit. So it appears to me practically — so ends his words. And this is the reason of those who say in konam he cannot forbid except in actual hekdesh, since benefit prohibition does not take effect on the body of the land; and likewise Taz wrote this reason. And from these words of Rosh it is explained that he holds like Ramban and the author that there is no distinction between mortgage and rental, unlike what is written in the conclusion in HaShutafin; and likewise appears the opinion of Tosafot in Ketubot perek Af Al Pi, and not as some distinguish in Tosafot in Arakhin (21a) and in Nedarim there; and likewise appears the opinion of Ran, and likewise appears the opinion of Rabbenu Yerucham netiv 1 part 1, and likewise appears the opinion of Rabbenu Yonah and Rashba who cited there, and likewise appears the opinion of Semag negative commandments 212 page 72a, and likewise is the main point. Also it is explained to you from these words of Rosh that even regarding general konam — meaning he forbids everyone — he cannot forbid since benefit prohibition does not take effect on the body of the land, unlike what Ran and Rivash wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam who distinguish thus and hold general konam, since it is comparable to hekdesh, uproots from lien; and likewise appears from the words of Taz that one should not distinguish here. And regarding the law, the words of Ran and Rivash that I brought in note 38 appear correct; and even though from the words of Tosafot in Nedarim and other places it appears one should not distinguish here, nevertheless for some of Tosafot's variations konam is permitted in every matter; and also for Rosh and Tur it is permitted with konam in every matter — if so, even if you say to be stringent, nevertheless when forbidding upon another, the majority of poskim and Ran and Rivash are fit to rely upon, since he does not forbid upon the tenant — if so, the one who forbids from the tenant benefits. So it seems to me; but otherwise one should be stringent with konam even if he forbade property liened to a lender, like the opinion of Ran and the poskim that konam is like sanctity of the body and uproots from lien. However Ran wrote in Ketubot end of perek Af Al Pi: nevertheless the matter appears that they excommunicate him until he releases his vow; and Beit Yosef brings this at the end of this siman. And see Choshen Mishpat siman 117.
