There are those who say that the prohibition takes effect upon him as well. Although the house is encumbered to the renter, the Gemara states that hekdesh removes a lien, and a konam is comparable to hekdesh. The second opinion holds that a konam is not included in the law of hekdesh and does not remove the renter’s lien, even if the landlord explicitly forbade it. It appears that nowadays, even if he declared it hekdesh, it would not remove the lien, for the Rosh wrote in a responsum, klal 13, that all of our present-day hekdesh is ordinary property, since it is only charity, etc.
He cannot forbid it upon him. This is Rabbeinu Tam’s opinion, as cited by the Beit Yosef, and it implies that this is so even when he forbids it explicitly. This is the second opinion mentioned nearby. The Tur cited the first opinion in Rabbeinu Tam’s name, but that refers only to the opening clause, that when one rents a house to another, etc., the prohibition does not take effect upon the house. The Tur’s subsequent conclusion, “But if he specified,” etc., is not Rabbeinu Tam’s opinion, for otherwise Rabbeinu Tam’s words would contradict one another. As practical halacha, one should be stringent in accordance with the first opinion, that an explicit prohibition can take effect. Consequently, here too, regarding a borrowed item, he may forbid it explicitly.
