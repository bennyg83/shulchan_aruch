He may, etc. See the Rosh, s.v. “we learned,” etc., and “the same applies,” etc.; Ran, s.v. “and regarding,” etc.; and Tosafot, Ketubot 108a, s.v. “this,” etc., unlike Rabbeinu Tam.
And even, etc. See both of the aforementioned sources, and Choshen Mishpat, beginning of siman 128.
It is forbidden to teach him, etc. This follows Rabbi Yochanan there, even when he is an adult. See Ran, s.v. “and Rabbi Yochanan,” etc.
And this applies, etc. As stated there on 35b: “If they are our agents,” etc.; and there on 36b: “Rather, with his consent,” etc., “where he says,” etc. It is evident that although he is performing a mitzvah, it is nevertheless forbidden in such a case.
Nowadays, etc. See the Rosh there, and Tosafot, Bekhorot 29a, s.v. “just as,” etc.
And it is permitted, etc. This follows Ran’s explanation, s.v. “It is permitted to give drink,” etc.
Some disagree, etc. This refers back to the preceding clause, “and this applies,” etc. The Rosh writes there that the restriction applies specifically to a matter requiring the owner’s consent, such as an offering or terumah, as stated there on 36b: “If you say that consent is required,” etc. This is also the meaning of the statement there on 35b: “If they are our agents,” etc., because consent is required; whereas “they are agents of Heaven,” etc., because consent is not required. See his commentary there, s.v. “rather, one who lacks,” etc.
