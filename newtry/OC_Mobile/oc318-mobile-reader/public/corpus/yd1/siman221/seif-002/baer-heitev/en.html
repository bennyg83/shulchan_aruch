His debt. This applies specifically when it is without his [the debtor's] knowledge — so wrote Ran. (Radbaz wrote: similarly, if [the vower] has a debt owed to him, he may forgive it — see there, vol. 2 siman 262, and see Knesset HaGedolah.):
To return it. And even if the lender was pressing him to pay, we say the borrower would have appeased him and he would forgive him — so wrote the Mechaber in Choshen Mishpat beginning of siman 128. And Bah ruled like the poskim that it is forbidden to pay his debt when it is by promissory note or collateral. However, when the debt is not such that he can be compelled to pay, but rather the borrower pays of his own will when he wishes — even if there was a promissory note or collateral, [the person under the vow] may pay on [the debtor's] behalf and keep the collateral for himself without giving it to the borrower; for although it was stipulated that he would not compel him to pay, nonetheless he is not obligated to return the collateral for free unless it was stipulated — then while under the vow, the one who pays must return it to him for free. See above siman 223 seif 4 — so ends Sh"akh:

His animal. Even extra food to fatten it is forbidden, since it raises its value — and this constitutes full benefit. Sh"akh. And Ran wrote: even if one feeds it more than needed for fattening — which actually harms it, since it is not meant for eating but for work, and over-feeding impairs its performance — and thus [the owner] derives no benefit, it is nonetheless forbidden:
