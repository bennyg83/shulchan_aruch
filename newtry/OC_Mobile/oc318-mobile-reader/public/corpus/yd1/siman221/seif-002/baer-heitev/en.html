His debt. This permission applies specifically when payment is made without the debtor's knowledge. Ran. Radbaz writes that if the prohibited person owes him money, he may likewise forgive the debt.
To return it. And even if the lender presses him to pay, we say the borrower would appease him and forgive him; so wrote the author in Choshen Mishpat siman 128, and Bach ruled like the poskim that it is forbidden to pay his debt when it is by way of a promissory note or collateral. However, when the debt is not such that they compel him to pay, but rather when the borrower wishes he pays of his own will; even if he had a promissory note or collateral he may pay while under a vow and take possession of the collateral for himself and not give it to the borrower; for although they stipulated that they will not compel him to pay, nevertheless he is not obligated to return his collateral for free unless they stipulated so; then while under a vow the one who pays must return it to him for free. And see above siman 223 seif 4; so ends Shach.
