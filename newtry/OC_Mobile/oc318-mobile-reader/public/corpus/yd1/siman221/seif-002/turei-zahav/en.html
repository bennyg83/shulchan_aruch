And he may support his wife, etc. The Tur writes in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that this applies specifically when he feeds them in his presence and says nothing, for then it is in the manner of a gift. Some explain that the case concerns providing them with additional food beyond their needs, etc. The Tur challenged the Rosh for citing this, since it applies only according to the Sages, etc. It appears clear that Rabbeinu Tam himself did not write this as practical halacha, but only to explain the words of the Talmud. The Talmud did not mean that this ruling follows only Chanan, but rather that it can follow even the Sages, as the Rosh wrote and the Beit Yosef cited. As practical halacha, however, Rabbeinu Tam certainly rules like Chanan, since the Gemara explicitly rules in accordance with him. The question is only why the Rosh cited these words of Rabbeinu Tam when they are not the practical halacha, as the Tur asked. The Beit Yosef answered that regarding vows we do not rule like Chanan. This is exceedingly astonishing, since the final chapter of Kesubos rules without qualification that the halacha follows Chanan. How can one say that this does not apply in every context? It appears to me that the Rosh cited Rabbeinu Tam’s words because of the subsequent opinion that explains the case as additional food. Although according to our ruling there is no practical difference, since it is permitted in every circumstance, there is nevertheless a practical difference regarding the latter clause, which teaches, “But he may not feed his animal.” That clause concerns even additional food, just like the first clause. This is also evident from the Rosh’s words, for after citing the alternative explanation he wrote that the ruling, “He may not feed his animal,” likewise concerns additional food. The Ran also wrote this, as cited by the Beit Yosef. This appears to be the Rosh’s intention. The Prishah raised a contradiction in Rabbeinu Tam’s words, for here he wrote that it is permitted specifically with additional food, whereas above, at the beginning of the siman, he permitted it entirely. He similarly challenged the Rosh’s citation of Rabbeinu Tam and entered into resolutions. There is no difficulty at all. Above, the Tur cited Rabbeinu Tam’s practical ruling, namely, like Chanan. Here, he cited Rabbeinu Tam’s explanation according to the Sages, solely to explain the Mishnah and not as practical halacha. This is clear from the Tur’s own question, and we have already resolved everything as stated.
It is forbidden to feed his animal. I have already written that this applies even to additional food. The Ran wrote that this is so even if he feeds it beyond the amount needed to fatten it, thereby harming it, for the animal is not intended for eating but for work, and when it is overfed it does not work properly. Thus, the owner does not benefit, yet it is nevertheless forbidden.
