Reuven who forbade his benefit upon Shimon, and Shimon became ill — Reuven may enter to visit him. And in a place where they give wages to one who sits with the sick to attend him — he may not sit with him, but visits him while standing. And if there is no custom to give wages to a doctor, he may heal him even with his hands, even if there is another doctor who would heal him. But if it is customary to give wages to a doctor, he may not heal him free; and he may not heal his animal. And even if there is no custom to give wages to a doctor, he may tell him, "Such-and-such medicine is good for her," and he will prepare it. And if he does not know how to prepare it and she will die if he does not prepare it — the forbidding party is permitted to prepare it. And if she dies, he may bring a coffin and shrouds. And if Shimon's son became ill, Reuven may enter to him, sit with him, and visit him (while standing). And if Shimon's property is forbidden to Reuven, since Shimon forbade him from his property, and Shimon became ill — Reuven enters to visit him (Ran), but does not sit with him. If his son became ill, he does not enter at all; rather he asks about him in the marketplace. And Reuven may testify for the forbidden Shimon, whether testimony of money or testimony involving life-and-death.
