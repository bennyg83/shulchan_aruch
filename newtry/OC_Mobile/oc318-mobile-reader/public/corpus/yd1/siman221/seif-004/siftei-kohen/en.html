To join it for him. Meaning, to attach it for him.
He may not sit beside him. If the visitor sat beside him without payment in a place where visitors are normally paid, he would be waiving his fee and thereby benefiting the patient if he did not take payment. Rashi. This implies that only sitting without payment is forbidden, but sitting for payment is permitted. The Rosh and Ran write likewise, and it is obvious, contrary to the Ateret Zekenim, who writes that it is forbidden even if he is paid.
Even if there is, etc. Because not every person merits being healed by every physician.
He may not heal him without payment. For payment, however, he may heal him even if another physician is available, for the reason stated above. The Ateret Zekenim also writes this, and it is obvious.
Even if there is no, etc. He nevertheless provides him benefit, although with respect to the physician himself he is performing a mitzvah.
But he may tell him which medicine, etc. This applies only when the medicine belongs to the owner of the animal. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran.
Reuven may enter, sit with him, and visit him; and if, etc. This is the correct reading. Meaning, even in a place where payment is normally given for sitting with a patient, Reuven may sit there without payment, because Shimon prohibited benefit only to himself, not to his son.
Because Shimon prohibited him from benefiting from his property. Therefore, although a person prohibited from benefit may not walk through the other's property, here we assess the sick person's intent and say that he did not include matters necessary to preserve his life. But if the visitor prohibited himself from the sick person's property, he may not enter the house at all, because walking through the property is forbidden and this assessment of intent does not apply. Ran.
Reuven enters to visit him. While standing, but not while seated. This is the correct reading.
But not while seated. Because the visit can be performed while standing.
His son became ill. Meaning Shimon's son. Reuven may not enter Shimon's house at all, because Shimon's house is forbidden to Reuven and Reuven benefits by walking through it. Although we presume that Shimon did not prohibit matters necessary for Reuven's own life, he may have prohibited matters necessary for Reuven's son's life.
Reuven may testify for Shimon. This applies whether Shimon prohibited Reuven from benefiting from him, or Shimon prohibited himself from benefiting from Reuven. The Ateret Zekenim writes this.
To testify, etc. The Ateret Zekenim writes that it is a mitzvah not to withhold testimony, as Scripture states, “If he does not testify,” and one cannot vow to nullify a negative commandment, as stated below in siman 239. This is difficult. If so, why mention testimony specifically, for the same should apply to every negative commandment. Furthermore, the wording here implies that he may testify and that no prohibition is involved. According to that explanation, it should have said that he is obligated. Rather, this case is different. Since he vowed against deriving benefit from the other, and vows take effect even regarding mitzvah matters, as stated above in siman 215, the prohibition applies. Even where a vow would not take effect, or where this was an oath, the prohibition against deriving benefit is a comprehensive prohibition and therefore takes effect, as stated below in siman 239, seif 4. Alternatively, the case concerns one who now wishes to observe the event in order to testify later. One might have thought that he may not do so because he thereby benefits the other; the ruling teaches that this is not considered benefit.
