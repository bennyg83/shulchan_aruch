(Ibid.) Whether the property of the owner of the lost object. This is the language of the Gemara. I find difficulty in the continuation there, 'But the property of the finder,' etc. See Ran, who holds that the owner benefits more in this case than when another pays his debt. Yet why should that matter? He benefits only through this specific object, which is permitted to him, since he prohibited only the finder's property, and this object is not included in that prohibition. This requires study.
