Who forbade his benefit. In the Gemara the wording is, “who forbade his property.” We therefore learn that even if one says, “Benefit from my money is forbidden to you,” the other person may not benefit from his body either, as in this case. This is because when payment is ordinarily given for that benefit, it is considered benefit from his property, as though he received the payment from him and then returned it to him.
Reuven may enter, etc. For the sick person’s property is not forbidden to the visitor. Rather, the visitor’s property is forbidden to the sick person.
To keep him company. Meaning, to associate with him, as ordinary companionship.
Rather, he visits him while standing. Because no payment is given for this.
Even if there is another doctor, etc. For a person does not merit healing through every doctor.
Even if doctors do not ordinarily take payment. For he nevertheless provides benefit to him, while the doctor himself performs a mitzvah. Gemara.
And the other person shall prepare it. Provided that the medicine belongs to the owner of the animal. Beit Yosef.
And it will die. For this is comparable to returning his lost object.
And if he died, etc. For the dead derive no benefit.
Reuven may enter and sit with him and visit him. The visitor forbade his benefit only to Shimon, not to Shimon’s son. We do not say that visiting the son also benefits the father. Some editions of the Shulchan Aruch read here, “while standing,” but this is a scribal error.
And if Shimon’s property is forbidden. Meaning that the sick person’s property is forbidden to the visitor. Reuven may enter to visit him, even though he benefits by walking through the sick person’s house. When the sick person forbade his property to the visitor, he did not intend to exclude him from something upon which his life depends, for our Sages said that anyone who visits the sick causes him to live. Nevertheless, the visitor may not sit, since standing is possible. The Ran wrote that if the visitor forbade the sick person’s property upon himself, he may not enter at all, for in that case the reasoning that the sick person did not exclude him from something upon which his life depends does not apply.
If his son became ill, he may not enter at all. For it is possible that he did intend to exclude him from visiting his son, since the son’s life is not the sick person’s own life.
