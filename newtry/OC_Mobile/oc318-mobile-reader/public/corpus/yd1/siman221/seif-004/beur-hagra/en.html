Reuven, etc., and in a place, etc. The Tur rules like Shmuel. Ran, however, rules according to the second answer, or alternatively according to the interpretation of the Rashba, etc.
And if there is no, etc. See the Rosh in his commentary and rulings. This parallels the law of returning a lost object in a place where payment is customarily taken, etc., and the law above concerning visiting the sick.
Even if, etc. The Rosh cites this in the name of the Yerushalmi. See Ran there, s.v. “rather, healing,” etc.
But if, etc., and even if, etc. As stated above there in the Rosh.
And if he is not, etc. See there, and Ran in the aforementioned passage. See there.
And if his son became ill, etc. The text “to carry” should read “to sit,” etc. See Ran there, s.v. “rather, according to Shmuel,” etc. What the Rosh writes in his commentary, s.v. “What is different,” etc., is an additional question; even sitting is permitted.
And if the property, etc. This follows Ulla’s answer. See Ran, s.v. “Ulla,” and the Rosh, s.v. “when the property,” etc.
If his son became ill, etc. There the Gemara challenges: “They raised an objection,” etc.
