If they were walking on the road and he has nothing to eat, he gives to another as a gift and the other is permitted in it. And if there is no other with them, he places it on a rock and says, "Behold it is ownerless for anyone who wishes," and the other takes and eats. In what matters is this said? When he gives to another as a gift without specification. But if he said to his fellow, "The feast is given to you as a gift, and let so-and-so who is forbidden my benefit come and eat with you at the feast" — behold, this is forbidden. And not only that, but even if he gives to him without specification and returns and says to him, "Do you wish that so-and-so come and eat with you?" — if his end proves his beginning, that he did not give to him except on condition that so-and-so come and eat, it is forbidden. For example, if it was a great feast and he wants his father or his teacher to come, who are forbidden his benefit, to eat from his feast — for behold his feast proves about him that he did not resolve to give it as a gift. And likewise everything similar.
