The person prohibited from benefit may not study from it. So it is written in the responsa of Maimonides cited by the Beit Yosef. The reason is that a person is not obligated to lend his sefer to another without his knowledge, just as he is not obligated to give it to him, for the sefer becomes damaged through use. He may therefore rent it out, and by lending it he provides the other person with the rental value that he would otherwise have needed to pay. The responsa of Maharam raised a difficulty from the ruling in seif 13 that one who is prohibited from benefiting from a shofar may use it to sound a mitzvah blast. I am astonished, for there is no difficulty. The responsa of Maimonides explicitly states that sefarim are not lent because they become damaged, since damage is common with them. This is not so with a shofar, which is not damaged when the person sounds it. The same applies to one prohibited from benefiting from a spring, etc. This is obvious in my opinion. According to what I will write shortly, there is no difficulty for an additional reason as well.
