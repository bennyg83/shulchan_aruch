He may marry his daughter. Meaning that Shimon may marry Reuven’s daughter. The Tur concludes: “Even though he thereby releases him from the obligation to support her, for he is obligated to provide for her.” This is difficult. Why did the Tur need to state this law? The Gemara itself asks that it is obvious, since an even greater ruling was taught, that the one who imposed the prohibition may support the children of the person prohibited by the vow, even though they are under that person’s authority, as stated above. It appears that the Tur was careful to prevent an error. Since there is a mitzvah to provide one’s daughter with a dowry, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 71, in the gloss, although coercion is not applied as it is for support, one might have thought that Shimon was permitted to marry the daughter only in a place where the custom is not to give a dowry. In such a place he provides no benefit. However, where the custom is to provide a dowry and he takes her without one, there would appear to be no greater benefit, and it should be forbidden. The Tur therefore teaches that this is not so. It is learned by a kal vachomer from what the Gemara states regarding support. Support is an actual obligation upon the father, and nevertheless releasing him from it is not considered benefit. All the more so is this true of a dowry, which is only a mitzvah. This is why the Tur elaborated and wrote that he releases him from the obligation of supporting her, which he is required to provide. If releasing him from an obligation does not make it forbidden, all the more so does releasing him from a mere mitzvah not make it forbidden. This appears clear and correct to me.
