Because he benefits him. During the rainy season, one body derives warmth from the other.
Even on a small bed. Mere perspiration does not add significant warmth.
And he may recline with him on a bed. On a small bed, even during the rainy season. We do not fear that he may sleep beside him. He may also eat with him at the same table, and we do not fear that he will eat from the other's food, because once one has prohibited the other from benefit, they presumably dislike one another more than two travelers who eat at one table, one eating meat and the other cheese. Rosh. See above, siman 88, seif katan 2.
If the practice is, etc., it is permitted. Even if one eats more than the other, he derives no benefit, because in any event they are provided with all they need.
Because he loosens the soil before him. This makes it easier for the other to plow afterward.
And he may not sell, etc. This is prohibited as a decree lest he purchase from him. Likewise, he may not lend an object or money to him, as a decree lest he borrow an object or money from him. Gemara. The Perishah writes that “he may neither sell to him nor buy from him” means only an average sale, as explained at the beginning of siman 227. This is incorrect. There, where he prohibited himself from all Jews, the restriction is not based on a decree and therefore applies only to an average sale. Here, however, the prohibition is a decree and applies in every type of sale. The Bach writes likewise. This is also explicit in Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchot Nedarim, cited by the Tur below in siman 227, and in the Ran at the beginning of page 43; see there. The Ateret Zekenim also did not explain correctly when he wrote that he may not sell to him lest he discount the price and benefit him, and may not buy from him lest he pay too much and benefit him. Even where the sale itself provides no benefit, it remains forbidden because of the decree.
