Permitted. We hold that a craftsman does not acquire ownership through the improvement made to an object. Even according to the contrary view, acquisition applies only where the improvement has substance, such as making a utensil from wood. Here he merely painted with pigments that have no independent substance. Rosh. See Choshen Mishpat 306.
I will do. The same permission applies where the other person already has abundant food. Tur and Rosh described the usual case. Mabit writes that a rabbi who prohibited benefit from a certain person, or vice versa, may still receive communal charity from a mixed public fund to which that person contributed. Shach.
Whoever feeds him. Bach writes that even when addressing one individual, the general formulation 'whoever feeds him' is permitted because he did not directly say, 'You shall feed him.' Perishah writes that in this case he must reimburse the full amount provided. See siman 235.
