One who gives, etc. An artisan does not acquire ownership through the improvement of an article, as stated in the ninth chapter of Bava Kamma (99a) and in Choshen Mishpat 307:2. Even according to those who disagree, dye has no independent substance and therefore cannot create a prohibition, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Nedarim. We infer from there that in such a case there is not even a rabbinic prohibition.
But he may not, etc. See Be’er HaGolah, and as stated there on 33a: “Who is the tanna,” etc.
But it is forbidden, etc. There the Gemara asks: “But did we not learn: Whoever,” etc., and answers: “These cases are not comparable,” etc.
It is likewise forbidden, etc. This follows from the statement there: “It is unnecessary to state,” etc. Were the law otherwise, one could still have answered that the case concerned an individual. See Tosafot there, s.v. “where he says,” etc.
