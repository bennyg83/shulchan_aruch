One who gives a board, etc., it is permitted. Since we hold that a craftsman does not acquire ownership through the improvement he makes to an article, he has no ownership in the article itself and no power to prohibit it. Even if a craftsman did acquire ownership through improvement, that would apply only where the improvement has substance, such as when one gives him wood and he makes a vessel. Here, the craftsman merely painted designs, which have no independent substance. Rosh. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 306, where the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule that a craftsman does not acquire through improvement, and see the Sema there, seif katan 6.
But he is not obligated to repay him. If he were obligated, the provider would be acting as his agent and the other person's gift would be forbidden to him. The same ruling applies when the other person has ample food; the text merely states the usual case. Tur and Rosh. The Mabit writes in responsa, volume 1, siman 116, that if a rabbi vowed against benefiting from a particular person's property, or vice versa, that person may give his charitable contributions and donations to the communal treasurers, even though the treasurers will support the rabbi from communal funds into which that person's donation was mixed. See there.
Likewise, he may say, “Whoever provides,” etc. The Perishah writes that in this case he must repay the full amount provided, because wording addressed to the public does not constitute agency. See below, siman 235.
Whoever supports, etc. The Bach writes that even if he says to one individual, “Whoever supports him will not lose,” it is permitted, since he did not use the singular formulation, “If you support him.” See below, siman 235, end of seif 2.
