A retained interest. Meaning that he receives a fixed assessment. Gemara. Rashi and the Rosh explain that the tenant accepted responsibility to pay the owner a tax each year. The Ran explains that the owner does not take a percentage of the bathhouse's profits, but receives a fixed amount each year. Rambam and the Semag explain that the landlord retains only the rental payment and no ownership interest in the bathhouse itself, as stated in the Beit Yosef.
It is permitted. Because his intent does not extend to property that he has rented out and from which he has withdrawn.
But if he explicitly prohibited it, etc. The Perishah writes that even the first opinion and the Rosh in the following seif concede here, because the owner does not prohibit the bathhouse to the tenant, but only to an unrelated Shimon. This is incorrect. The first opinion and the Rosh below explain the Gemara's ruling here, that if the owner retains no interest it is permitted, as referring to a konam. Tosafot at the beginning of chapter HaShutafin and the Rosh also imply that there is no distinction. The Ran, on the contrary, holds the reverse. The owner can prohibit the bathhouse to the tenant, and likewise can prohibit it to everyone, including the tenant, because a konam removes a lien and thereby wholly terminates the tenant's right. But he cannot prohibit it to another person, because the tenant's lien remains. When that other person benefits, he benefits from the tenant, not from the one who imposed the prohibition. The Rivash concludes likewise in responsum 355 and writes that this view appears correct.
