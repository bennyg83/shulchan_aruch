It is forbidden to pass through his field. Rashi explains at the beginning of the chapter “There is no difference between one prohibited by a vow” that this concerns a case where he shortens his route by doing so. However, from what is written below in seif 4, concerning the law, “And if Shimon’s property is forbidden,” etc., it appears that walking through it is forbidden in every circumstance.
With anything sold by measure, etc. Since he waives the rental fee for him in exchange for that money, he can use it to purchase food. Thus, the one who made the vow caused him benefit that leads to food. Rosh.
To display himself with it. So that he will appear important and they will give him a favorable portion.
Likewise, it is forbidden to purchase provisions for his meal. The Tur writes that this is an unresolved question in the Yerushalmi. One must explain why it is uncertain, and why this should be more permissible than all the cases listed until now. It appears that there is reason to distinguish. In all those cases, he benefits from something that he has no way to obtain without the other person, such as utensils or a horse. Here, however, he can purchase the food himself.
What is written, “Likewise, it is forbidden to warm his hands,” etc., is astonishing. Why should this be forbidden when he said, “benefit that leads to food”? The Yerushalmi cited by the Beit Yosef likewise proves that this question was asked concerning one who vowed from a loaf, and only there is it uncertain. The Levush placed, “Likewise, it is forbidden to warm,” etc., above, beside, “The benefit of your food is forbidden upon me,” and that is correct.
