The laws of one who vows benefit from his fellow; it contains fourteen se'ifim. Reuven who forbade his property upon Shimon, or who forbade himself from Shimon's property — the forbidden party is forbidden to pass through the other's field, even in a valley in summer days when people are not particular about foot-traffic there. And he is forbidden to borrow from him any utensils in the world. And if he did not vow from him except food, he is forbidden only to eat from his food. If he said, "Benefit of your food is upon me" — he is forbidden to eat from his food; and forbidden to chew wheat grains to place on his wound; but to borrow from him a sieve and winnowing fork is permitted. If he said, "Benefit that brings me to food is upon me" — he is forbidden to eat from his food; and permitted to chew wheat and place on his wound. And forbidden to benefit from everything that people rent out similarly; and what people do not rent out similarly is permitted if it has no connection to food. But if it has connection to food — such as a sieve and winnowing fork, and even a sack to bring fruit in it, and a donkey to bring fruit on it, and likewise a horse to ride to a banquet, and a ring to show at a banquet, and to pass through his field to a banquet — even though people do not rent such things out, it is forbidden. And likewise forbidden to buy feast needs for him. And likewise forbidden to warm his hands on his loaf. {Rama: An axe with which they split wood to cook food in it is called benefit that brings to food; but an axe with which they hoe is permitted to borrow (Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham).}
