It is permissible for his grandmother. It seems that the judgment was also the case for the work of stars in paragraph 1 and what we wrote there:
Permitted to slaughter for him a healthy animal. The reason it is considered spoil is as stated regarding slaughtering with a gentile's knife in siman 10 that it is not considered benefit; and although I am not fit to disagree with the Rav Baal Ohel Moed who originated this ruling as explained in Beit Yosef — nevertheless I said one should not rely on this ruling, for it is obvious that great benefit is that he slaughter for him and render it permitted for eating; and every day we hire a slaughterer to slaughter; and only regarding avodah zarah do we say there is no prohibition of avodah zarah in a gentile's knife; and explicit proof from Tosafot chapter HaOreg (Shabbat 106) s.v. chutz mechoveil etc. — "although every slaughterer spoils, as we say in chapter 1 of Chullin, a gentile's knife is permitted for slaughter; R' Yehudah agrees regarding Shabbat that he is liable and is not considered a spoiler — only regarding avodah zarah is he considered a spoiler, as written 'and nothing of the ban shall cleave to your hand' and he is not considered benefiting since his spoil exceeds his repair" — behold explicit before you that we do not derive for Shabbat from avodah zarah in this, and likewise nedarim since we say only regarding avodah zarah did the Merciful One reveal and not elsewhere; and although in chapter 1 of Chullin Tosafot wrote that only regarding Shabbat there is benefit since melacha machshevet the Torah forbade and he intended this benefit — from this there is no difficulty; on the contrary from there is proof that also in nedarim we follow the noder's intent and he intends benefit here; and further it is a fortiori in nedarim, for we find at the beginning of chapter Ein Bein HaMedir that even release is forbidden in maddir — meaning if one sells to his fellow intending to add one or two, and regarding maddir benefit we are strict even on slight benefit as Rosh and Ran wrote there; and for this reason we forbid at the beginning of this siman even treading of the foot where people are not particular, as Ran wrote there — a fortiori we should forbid such great benefit that enables and provides him food he could not eat otherwise; and further proof from what Raavad wrote in chapter 6 of Shevuot — if the circumciser or slaughterer swore not to benefit from city members, the public may circumcise and slaughter for free — what mitzvah is there in releasing his vow; but if he needs his labor and if he engages in his labor he cannot circumcise and slaughter, they release him; and if he swore they should not benefit from him, he may circumcise and teach their sons for free — behold he began twice with circumcision and slaughter and in the end did not mention slaughter that he would do for them free, but this is forbidden since he is not commanded regarding it; therefore it appears clear that if the Rav Rama saw what we mentioned he would not have ruled here leniently — and it is a full prohibition as appears to me.
