A healthy animal. Pnei Aryeh rules like Rama rather than Taz, but permits slaughtering only where no fee is normally charged or the slaughterer receives payment. It must also be unsolicited or framed generally, unless there is a mitzvah need such as Shabbat. Removing forbidden fats and blood vessels is permitted because the benefit is only fulfillment of a mitzvah, but salting is forbidden because it also improves the meat's taste.
But not one in danger. Zikhron Yitzchak discusses slaughter performed despite the prohibition. Although one might invoke the rule that an act forbidden by the Torah is ineffective, that principle does not invalidate this slaughter after the fact, and the animal remains kosher.
