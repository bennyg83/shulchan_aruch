(Seif 3) We do not rely on a gentile regarding small kids purchased from him who says they are eight days old. And even mesisach l'fi tumo he is not believed in Torah prohibition—Shach; and Pri Megadim wrote it appears from his words that all who say it is not known whether months completed is a mistaken presumption that if rabbinic, gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is believed as written in siman 98 and 137. But it is impossible to say so as proved in Shulchan Aruch siman 256 and 366, and so in book Beit Avraham. And they did not see Knesset HaGedolah already sensed this—for it is proved there majority of offspring are not stillborn, only rabbinically we concern for minority; and where there is majority we rely on gentile mesisach l'fi tumo as stated in Orach Chaim siman 513 regarding gentile selling eggs on Yom Tov 1. And so in responsa Tiferet Tzvi; and Pri Megadim wrote possibly this presumption is called that until now it was pregnant—and so in book Tiferet Tzvi. But this is certainly not so—for then in Orach Chaim there regarding eggs sold on Yom Tov we would also say thus; and explicitly Ran wrote an egg does not become presumption of prohibition—from which presumption of pregnant does not help; and now she gave birth—and this is because we say establish the offspring in presumption of permission it had in its mother's womb. And similarly Tosafot beginning of Bechoros wrote. But one may say according to what Noda B'Yehuda wrote part Even HaEzer siman 19 regarding stillborn of animal there is presumption of prohibition because it was in presumption it is not slaughtered, and we combine minority of stillborns to presumption it is not slaughtered—for if stillborn shechitah does not help; in present minority all say combine minority to presumption. And therefore one may say we do not rely here on gentile mesisach l'fi tumo—for there is presumption of prohibition. And this is not as Tiferet Moshe wrote simply that here there is no presumption of prohibition—for also on day one it is permitted if known months completed, and while alive until day eight the matter is revealed retroactively it was never stillborn and was before eight like a piece of doubt milk—end of his words. And according to Noda B'Yehuda's view as above.
And according to this one may also explain what the Binate Adam challenged regarding the words of the poskim who wrote that a doubtful nefel is a Torah-level doubt. Since there is a chazakah that supports the minority of animal nefalim, and it is a common minority, one may say that by Torah law we are concerned for it. So too it is proven from the words of the Peri Megadim here, that if there were here a chazakah toward prohibition it would be forbidden by Torah law. With this the words of the Turei Even in Rosh Hashanah 7 are rejected, as I explained in my work on the Rambam, Hilchot Bekhorot.
And with this what was difficult to me is also settled: even without mesiach lefi tumo, according to the majority view that even with a davar sheyesh lo matirin and an important item we may say kol deparish, as written at the end of siman 16. So too in the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 7 of Meilah, the rule that majority does not help with a davar sheyesh lo matirin is only in a mixture; but if one can say it came from the ordinary majority of permitted items, even with a davar sheyesh lo matirin we follow the majority where prohibition was not established. The Ran likewise wrote in the first chapter of Beitzah regarding the statement there, "when he did not check, say it was from yesterday," that although it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin we follow the majority, since prohibition was not established. This is unlike what the Tzelach wrote there in the Rambam's view, that because the Rambam omitted that sugya he holds we do not say kol deparish at all with a davar sheyesh lo matirin. However, Tosafot in Niddah 44 wrote that the minority of nefalim is a common minority; and according to what the Mishneh LaMelech wrote in chapter 3 of Hilchot Avot HaTumah, it is because of a scriptural decree that every doubtful nefel is forbidden. According to the Noda BiYehudah, this is settled well, because there is an established prohibition that it is not slaughtered. Accordingly it is also understandable why a sefek sefeika does not help here according to the Rashba, since there is a chazakah toward prohibition. But this is not settled according to the one who permits a sefek sefeika even where there is a chazakah of prohibition.
And see Shaar HaMelech, Hilchot Yom Tov, who challenged the Taz's answer here, that the Rashba holds a sefek sefeika is forbidden with a davar sheyesh lo matirin, for the Rashba, who is the source of this law, holds that a sefek sefeika is permitted with a davar sheyesh lo matirin. One may answer according to what is written in the gloss there, distinguishing between a davar sheyesh lo matirin in a Torah matter and in a rabbinic matter, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 513 and the Maggid Mishneh in Hilchot Yom Tov. It is implied there, however, that the Rambam permits even in a Torah matter. But the Shaar HaMelech wrote in Hilchot Maachalot Asurot that this is specifically where one doubt is about the item itself and one doubt is about the mixture, where prohibition was not established; but with a sefek sefeika within a mixture where prohibition was established, it is worse, as the Rosh wrote, and the Rambam forbids.
Further he wrote there that the reason majority helps at the end of siman 16 is because it is a majority that is before us; and in Sefer HaChayyim he wrote this on his own. The Tosafot on Demai brought by the Mishneh LaMelech in Hilchot Meilah is also a sefek sefeika: perhaps it is from produce of outside the Land, and perhaps from produce of the second year, and similarly money found and a box used, etc. There is no proof from there that we follow majority, since the Rambam wrote that even half and half is chullin, and necessarily the reason is that it is uncommon for a person to be careful with maaser sheni money. What he challenged there from what the Tur Yoreh Deah wrote, that we follow majority regarding chadash, may be answered that it is rabbinic; and similarly regarding maaser sheni one may say it speaks nowadays, when it is rabbinic. There is no proof that we follow a majority that is not before us in a Torah matter when it is not before us.
And I saw the author of Mishkenot Yaakov, Even HaEzer siman 40, who wrote that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's rule, that anything that did not live thirty days in a human or eight days in an animal is a nefel, is a Torah-level doubt, unlike Tosafot and the Rosh, who wrote that it is impossible except rabbinically, while by Torah law we follow the majority that offspring are viable. He brought proof from the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah regarding a blemished firstborn, that we count its year from birth, and the Gemara asks, "is it fit?" This implies that by Torah law we are not concerned regarding eating, for otherwise what is the question? Would we permit him, because of a rabbinic prohibition, to keep it beyond its year, which is a Torah prohibition? This is proof only according to the Rambam, who holds that the year of a blemished firstborn is Torah law, and with this the Turei Even's question is settled. But according to those who disagree and hold that it is only rabbinic, and there is proof for this from Yerushalmi Rosh Hashanah chapter 1, where R. Ela says that the year of a blemished firstborn is not clear, there is no proof at all from that passage in Rosh Hashanah. Moreover, even without this one may say that keeping it is passive inaction, and so it is proven in the Tosefta at the end of Makkot and in the Rambam, Hilchot Maaseh HaKorbanot; if so, the Sages have power to uphold their words and uproot a Torah matter passively. This also settles the Turei Even's question there. So too it answers what Mishkenot Yaakov challenged according to Tosafot and the Rosh: why does Shabbat ask, "how may we circumcise him?" If it is only a rabbinic concern lest he be a nefel, how does it ask simply that the mitzvah of circumcision in its time should be deferred because of this concern? According to what I wrote, this is not difficult, for since it is only passive inaction, the Sages have power to defer a Torah matter because of their decree and a shevut prohibition they forbade for some reason or because of doubt, as stated in Yevamot, Perek HaIshah Rabbah. It is therefore proper that because the Gemara thought there is a prohibition of making a wound because of the rabbinic concern lest he be a nefel, it resembles passive inaction. What he challenged, that since Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's verse is only an asmachta, how can we learn from it a leniency that once he lived thirty days in a human or eight days in an animal he leaves the status of nefel, is not difficult according to my view: since it is only rabbinic, and by Torah law in any event we follow the majority, the Sages said to be stringent and they relied on this passage to permit.
