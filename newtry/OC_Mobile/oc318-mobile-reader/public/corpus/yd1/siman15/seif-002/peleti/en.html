And if it is not known, etc.—the Rosh in first chapter of Beitzah wrote: "and that which he permitted to eat it on that day—that is when it is known its months are completed; but if it is not established to him that its months are completed, it is forbidden until the eighth night, as stated in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah; and also it must spread on the ground to remove doubt of crushing limbs, as stated in first chapter"—end of his words. And therefore the Tur in Orach Chaim and all challenged him—for this is against halachah that we rule the womb has no concern of crushing limbs; and the Gemara sought to support Rav Nachman from that calf born and we see it spread on the ground. But according to what we rule like Rav Nachman, there is nothing of R' Eliezer even if it did not spread at all—and as I wrote in name of the Rosh himself: even if it did not stand, and many commentators reconciled him. And I grasped first the view of Maharshal in Yeshuot Yaakov Chullin who explained the Rosh's intent: certainly when established its months completed, spreading on ground is unnecessary—as the Rosh wrote in first chapter; only because it is not established internally, eight-day waiting is required and also spreading on ground, and support from rabbis of Speyer brought in Rokeach. And Bach and Taz wondered on this—for he wrote as explained in first chapter and there is no mention at all of "its months completed" requiring spreading on ground; and also what relation have completed months to spreading on ground. And it appears to reconcile without rejecting the pleasant ones with both hands: and let us repeat why we require spreading—it must be said since months not completed it is very weak and there is more concern for crushing limbs—this is complete error: if truly not nine full months, behold full stillborn and waiting eight days and more does not help; only that eight days' waiting is testimony it is complete offspring and months finished. And since so, the question stands: what distinguishes this offspring from offspring we know is nine months, that it depends on knowledge. And if we say eight days alone do not testify months completed except combined with spreading hoof on ground—then all the reason in our sugya that spreading removes stillborn and shows months completed; however the Rosh did not say this, only to clarify there is no crushing—and if we say certainly if it does not walk on day born there is no deficiency of birth weakness, but this one waited eight days and nevertheless does not walk we are concerned for crushing. For if so in all offspring even when we know months completed and they do not walk several days after birth we would concern for crushing. Therefore it appears to say he holds that eight days' waiting for completed months is not in all offspring—there are offspring whose months did not fill and nevertheless waited many days as naturalists testified; only majority of offspring are not so; and in all Torah we follow majority—and majority waiting eight days are excellent offspring. And that the womb is not concerned for crushing is because majority of offspring even if cannot stand nevertheless have no crushing; but combine two minorities: minority not completed months and minority cannot stand—the majority is impaired, behold doubt and forbidden; and therefore we require spreading on ground and there is no concern at all—and this is correct in my humble opinion, and on this rabbis of Speyer in Rokeach intended. However still the Rosh's answer is needed—he wrote and it implies first chapter and there is no mention and no proof at all; and it appears what I already wrote in previous siman what is difficult in the sugya there that challenges from beraisa calf born on Yom Tov and answers yotzei dofen and again challenges from "and equal" if born with its blemish with it that this is prepared and answers it spread on ground—and does not answer immediately on the first beraisa thus; and also why call it "two" regarding spreading or standing. And it appears in the beraisa that Rav taught: calf born on Yom Tov permitted, chick forbidden—for this is prepared through its mother and this is not prepared. And see Maharsha who raised for Rav's ruling that it permits itself through shechitah if calf born eight days before Yom Tov and it is not established internally that months completed—forbidden on Yom Tov as muktzeh and prepared through mother does not apply—see there. And I expanded on this root in my work on Rambam's laws of Yom Tov. And therefore one may challenge: why teach calf and chick—teach all calf; and in such case calf whose months completed born on Yom Tov permitted as prepared through mother, but whose months not completed born before Yom Tov and on Yom Tov completed eight days—forbidden to slaughter as not prepared through mother; and chick would be learned a fortiori forbidden as prepared through mother does not apply at all. And one may say the beraisa incidentally taught us that even without spreading on ground born on Yom Tov permitted in shechitah and we are not concerned for crushing—and this is learned since it teaches stam born on Yom Tov and does not teach "it stood," and stam offspring does not stand on day born; and similar to end chick born on Yom Tov where standing does not apply at all. And this if it teaches chick; but if it teaches born and not established completed, etc., as above—certainly it deals with spreading, for without this forbidden as Maharshal above wrote; and perhaps the first part deals where it spread on ground. And we did not know that without standing also we are not concerned for crushing limbs—and this is the Gemara's difficulty challenging from calf born on Yom Tov intending that beraisa of Rav taught there, and proved as I wrote it does not deal with spreading as above, and brings support well for Rav. And the Gemara answers yotzei dofen—and if so no longer difficult—let it teach in the end where we do not know months completed and on Yom Tov completed eight days as above—one may say that which we rely on eight days' waiting to say certainly not stillborn is because otherwise majority supports—majority animals excellent offspring and she does not miscarry twins—and this eight days' waiting causes permission; and see Tosafot Shabbat and Bechoros in several places. And this in birth where majority animals do not miscarry stillborns; but if the womb was cut and the fetus extracted, the majority is that truly the animal did not give birth and for what was alone we do not rely. And if so, "not completed months" is unnecessary and all reconciled—and requires study. And Maharshal's words are required. But with all this Moses is true and his Torah true—Rama in Darkei Moshe, for from his place it is decided the Rosh wrote in Beitzah and not Chullin; only the Rosh admits all year permitted, but on Yom Tov not, lest treifah be found in inner organs and slaughter on Yom Tov without need; and he sensed himself the Taz's and later authorities' difficulty that we rule crushed bird permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov, and we answer from "one may not whiten roof tiles." And Rama's words there: it has presumption—and this born needs no presumption—this is essence of his words; see similarly. And Magen Avraham already challenged: also there there is no clear presumption—it must be said clear from force of majority, and even there majority excellent offspring. And the essence difficulty: what proof from first chapter where there is no proof at all—and would that it were not to contradict. And it appears Tosafot raised there in Chullin that even Rav Nachman deals only where there is deficiency that cannot stand and walk; but where there is no deficiency, all agree we are not concerned for crushing, for we follow majority of offspring. And they cry in the house of study: if so what did the Gemara resolve from those two beraisot of born on Yom Tov—perhaps there it deals where there is no deficiency, and why need the Gemara to answer it spread—in short it should reject there is no deficiency. And it appears certainly according to Rama's words that even on a weekday there is no concern, for we are not concerned for crushing; on Yom Tov there is concern to slaughter it on Yom Tov. And if so when we judge an animal because of crushing organs we do not judge whether permitted or forbidden—only whether permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov or forbidden. And if so it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, for he can wait until weekdays and slaughter without doubt; and I already wrote above siman 1 in name of Ran: in a davar sheyesh lo matirin we do not follow majority, for until you rely on majority wait until permission comes—and one need not certainly majority kosher animals without crushing—only until you slaughter on Yom Tov and rely on majority, wait until tomorrow and one need not majority for shechitah. And these are the Gemara's words supporting Rav Nachman that the womb has no concern of crushing at all; and therefore even if impaired and cannot walk and stand, nevertheless there is no deficiency—for there is concern only regarding standing we are not concerned, for we follow majority. If so on Yom Tov slaughter we do not follow majority as above, for it is davar sheyesh lo matirin—how slaughter on Yom Tov even if fit to stand. Or say there is no concern at all for crushing. And the Gemara's rejection: forever without ability to walk we are concerned; and regarding fit to walk, permitted from majority of offspring as Tosafot wrote—and this is truly slaughter on Yom Tov forbidden, for we do not follow majority—only it deals where certainly it spread before us, and not regarding fit to spread, and certainly it spread—then there is no concern and majority unnecessary and slaughter on Yom Tov permitted—and if you find to say. And if so even though we rule like Rav Nachman, nevertheless we learn from his words that if there is deficiency that cannot stand, we validate from majority; and if so on Yom Tov slaughter forbidden as above; but if proof to stand, according to Rav Nachman majority unnecessary and permitted on Yom Tov—and therefore it must be said what Rosh and Tur wrote that spreading does not mean clearly spread, only appearance of spreading to exclude cannot stand. However this does not seem in category of davar sheyesh lo matirin—for chametz on Pesach poskim hold it is not davar sheyesh lo matirin returning to prohibition next year, and one cannot say eat it in permission now. And if so also on first day of Pesach when seventh of Pesach arrives it will also be forbidden to slaughter for the aforementioned reason—and it returns to prohibition. But this is not so—for since it stood, there is no further concern; so too waiting from time to time—one rule applies to standing and waiting from time to time. And if so since born on first Yom Tov before second Yom Tov arrived, already passed several from time to time and no longer prohibition concern and davar sheyesh lo matirin applies—unlike fallen where standing does not help and waiting from time to time does not help until it walks or waits twelve hours, and still can return to prohibition if it did not walk and second Yom Tov arrived. And if so it is not called davar sheyesh lo matirin since possibility to return to prohibition; and since no davar sheyesh lo matirin we rely on majority and permitted to slaughter—and requires study. And with what I wrote, the Rosh's intent that spreading means there is no deficiency is reconciled—Peri Chadash in Orach Chaim challenged the Rosh: what support for Rav Nachman from calf born on Yom Tov—nevertheless spreading required to slaughter on Yom Tov; and if it deals without spreading there is no support; according to what I wrote it is fine that he knows there is no deficiency—only without Rav Nachman's words it would not help because of davar sheyesh lo matirin as above. However in any case I answered Peri Chadash's difficulty: one may infer in the Gemara that it spread and Rashi explained it extended its leg to stand, as they said "extended its hand to stand," etc.; and one must understand what Rashi sought from this until he brought proof on it and did not explain simply it truly spread. And it appears certainly it did not escape that it deals with standing—only this is not in reality in one moment when offspring is born it will stand on ground; and if so according to one who holds muktzeh for half Shabbat, this moment before standing would be muktzeh lest perhaps it will not stand and slaughter forbidden from crushing concern. And similarly they said regarding firstborn born with its blemish—at moment before they saw blemish it was set aside; and they answer court sat there on Yom Tov—and if so here standing is unnecessary—what does it help, and all the more there is no crushing concern at all (and this supports you—Ran holds on Yom Tov muktzeh at beginning of Beitzah; according to one who rules like R' Shimon none of this applies). And the Gemara answers it spread—Rashi: not actual spreading, which also is not in reality immediately at birth—only extended legs, and this can be true immediately at birth and there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat—and requires study. And if so even Peri Chadash's difficulty is reconciled—for truly even according to Rav Nachman spreading is only this, only l'chatchila to permit slaughter as above, and there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat concern—as we rule regarding tevel it is not called muktzeh since if one transgressed and fixed it, it is fixed—and requires study. And indeed if Tosafot's words on Rav Nachman's matter were without halachah and did not write like Rosh regarding without standing—one may say Tosafot truly hold if it stood checking is required, and so Gaon Maharig in his responsa brought in Chavot Yair. How do we say it spread, etc.—it is like standing, and we rule standing requires checking—and this should have been in Beitzah regarding crushed bird. And requires study—for we already challenged why not answer on that beraisa calf born on Yom Tov, etc. And according to the above it is fine that certainly it is not common it will walk immediately, only can stand; and if it stood, the Gaon's difficulty arises—resolve, etc.; but in second beraisa where in any case we establish there in Beitzah b'dieved and l'chatchila forbidden to slaughter, they answer well it spread and it is like standing—and if so how permitted l'chatchila to slaughter—for truly forbidden only if slaughtered there is no crushing concern. And with this Peri Chadash's difficulty is also reconciled—for without Rav Nachman's difficulty it is not hard according to Rosh's view that Rosh admits if it stood no checking needed since we are not concerned for crushing; and if so the Gemara knew it deals with standing and no difficulty—resolve that no checking needed at all—and requires study.
