We do not rely, etc.—Shach copied Rashba's responsa words that a gentile is not believed except in woman's testimony—from which even mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed here, for even regarding woman he is not believed except mesisach l'fi tumo. And he inferred well; however it is difficult on its body: behold this is only rabbinic prohibition—for from Torah even without waiting at all it is permitted, for we follow majority and majority of offspring are born complete months and excellent offspring; and therefore we rule from Torah no need for waiting; and see Tosafot Bechoros daf 20a "milk exempt" who gave reason why they were stringent rabbinically to require eight days and we do not follow majority. And apparently from Tosafot it is proved he does not hold like Ran that in davar sheyesh lo matirin we do not follow majority—for otherwise what difficulty why not follow majority excellent offspring—for he can wait eight days and it is permitted and it is davar sheyesh lo matirin. But in truth this is error—for Ran said where Chazal already fixed prohibition, like egg born on Yom Tov that is forbidden—and we come to judge this egg whether we follow majority it was not born on Yom Tov—on this we say it is davar sheyesh lo matirin; but here we judge that Chazal had no reason to fix prohibition at all except to rely on majority—in this Ran's law does not apply, and womb has no crushing concern; and Tosafot gave reason we follow majority. And even we may say wait until from time to time—only because of majority Chazal did not decree initially; and also the aforementioned regarding gentile—and requires study, for correct. And in my chiddushim I answered Tosafot's question—for we say in the Gemara that without following majority one could not eat meat lest perhaps at place of perforation he slaughtered—only we follow majority. And if so without waiting, behold one minority perhaps at perforation he slaughtered, and another minority perhaps it is stillborn—and combine two minorities and impaired; and similar reasoning Ran wrote regarding buying eggs lest impure bird—combine two minorities; and this too is similar—and requires study. In any case according to all it is only rabbinic prohibition; however from many things in Rashba it is evident his view that even in rabbinic prohibition a gentile is not believed mesisach l'fi tumo, and therefore he copied here; but according to what we rule in several places in Shulchan Aruch and Rama that in rabbinic matters mesisach l'fi tumo is believed—even here he is believed—and requires study.
