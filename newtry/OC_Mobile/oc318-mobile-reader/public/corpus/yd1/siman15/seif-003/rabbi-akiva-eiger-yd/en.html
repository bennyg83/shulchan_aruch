(Taz s.k. 4) It is difficult—for behold it is a double doubt. In Nekudat HaKesef he answered: how can you say "if you find to say not completed perhaps it has eight"—nevertheless not completed and it is stillborn. And in Sha'ar HaMelech chapter 1 laws of Yom Tov he contradicted this—for Peri Chadash raised in Rashba's view that double doubt turning over is not required; and if so still there is double doubt: doubt whether it waited eight days, and if you say it did not wait, perhaps months completed—see there. And in my humble opinion it is not so—for according to Shach's view that certainly not completed, eight days do not help; and if so there is no double doubt here—all is one, for we say perhaps it waited eight days and it is proved months completed, and if you say it did not wait eight days, perhaps without proof months completed; and if so all doubt is only whether months completed—and simple. And see in Teshuvot HaTashbetz (part 4 ch. 1 siman 2) who wrote for truth of the matter to permit stam offspring through double doubt; and that of Rashba that we do not rely on gentile mesisach l'fi tumo deals with smaller ones that resemble more that they did not wait eight days—see there. Afterward a rav came to my hand the book Ba'er Heitev Yoreh De'ah from the Gaavad of Berlin z"l and I saw he brought these Teshuvot HaTashbetz words and concluded: nevertheless from it at least one may rely well on signs Knesset HaGedolah brought; and so what Peri Chadash brought in name of Maharikash—end of his words. I was asked by one sage, thus: two witnesses contradicting each other in their testimony on a calf slaughtered whether it was five days old or not—for it is impossible one witness is believed to testify it is eight days old to remove it from presumption of stillborn; and there is room to side that he is believed—for all in his hand is believed even in presumption of prohibition; and so here it was in his hand to wait eight days. And one may also say there is no presumption of prohibition since permission comes automatically—and similar to what Tosafot Gittin (daf 2b) s.v. "one witness" wrote—end of his words. Answer: Behold all his words here are without supervision—for if we suppose there is presumption of stillborn, it is not called "in his hand"—for if it is stillborn, eight days will not be fulfilled. Also it is not similar to Tosafot Gittin—for there the essence of Tosafot's reasoning is what she says now that she counted seven clean days is not against presumption—for what we knew before that she saw blood before seven days, that evidence does not damage and does not cause prohibition now after seven days passed; for because of that evidence alone she is permitted now since seven days passed—only she comes to forbid lest she also saw afterward; and on this she was not presumed to see again—therefore regarding the other evidence it is not called presumption of prohibition; unlike here where presumption of stillborn causes it to remain in prohibition forever—there is full presumption of prohibition. And truly in essence of law the refutation that there is no presumption of stillborn at all—for from moment formed there is doubt whether viable or not; and witness testifying it is eight days old is like witness testifying it was clarified it was never stillborn and was always permitted; if so witness did not come to remove from presumption, only to clarify there was never presumption of prohibition—and simple. However what requires study—one may say there is presumption of pregnant and now she gave birth and it is not eight days old; and see Bava Metzia (daf 100) in Tosafot s.v. "this is," Sumchus, etc.; and I expanded elsewhere in explanation of their words with God's help—that it implies there is presumption, only it is not so strong against presumption of majority; also one may say since if stillborn its shechitah does not purify it and it is as if dead of itself—if so after shechitah this offspring has presumption of prohibition that it is not slaughtered, like any doubt in shechitah; and even if permissive witness came before shechitah while offspring alive—even according to his words it is still not slaughtered and does not remove from presumption, and his testimony only that there is remedy in shechitah—and this is not against presumption that all animals have remedy in shechitah. Nevertheless in my humble opinion in what he testifies it will be permitted after shechitah—when we come to permit eating through this witness's testimony, we remove from presumption of prohibition "not slaughtered"—here too one witness is not believed. And proof from sugya beginning of Gittin that they challenged and required two, etc.—it is presumption of prohibition even though there the agent testifies before divorce that this get is fit to divorce with, written for her name—and with this now he does not remove her from presumption, for even according to his testimony now she is a married woman—only he testifies it is possible to divorce her with this get, and this is not against presumption, for it is simple he can divorce with fit get; and nevertheless it is called presumption of prohibition in what is judged after giving through this witness's testimony. And if so a fortiori there—at hour he testifies certainly in husband's hand to divorce her with fit get and remove from presumption, all the more in our case where it is not in his hand to remove presumption lest stillborn and eight days will not be fulfilled. And somewhat supported our case is called presumption of prohibition from what we rule gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed to testify they are eight days—and the reason explained in Rashba's responsa (siman 243) that gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is believed only in woman's testimony alone; and it is hard apparently—for from Torah we follow majority, majority of offspring are viable, and rabbis required eight days as Tosafot Yevamos (daf 119) wrote; and from Torah we ruled in Even HaEzer (siman 256) regarding child dying within thirty, if kohen's wife she does not perform chalitzah—and therefore from Torah no need for stillborn, for we follow majority; and in all rabbinic matters gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is believed as terumah rabbinic as stated in Bava Kamma (daf 119); and one should not distinguish that in terumah the prohibition itself is rabbinic but here the root of stillborn prohibition is Torah—even though from Torah we follow majority, nevertheless since Chazal were stringent and concerned for stillborn it is like Torah and mesisach l'fi tumo does not help—so one may study in Magen Avraham (siman 513) who wrote in name of Maharshal in Beitzah eggs brought to sell on Yom Tov after Shabbat that gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is believed they were born before Yom Tov, since from Torah we follow majority—only rabbinically forbidden as davar sheyesh lo matirin, behold even though preparation there is Torah prohibition, nevertheless since from Torah we follow majority and concern is rabbinic, mesisach l'fi tumo is believed—if so here too. However in my humble opinion to be lenient: presumption of pregnant is not excellent presumption, since we cannot say "now she gave birth" and not limit time when she gave birth—it is not presumption according to Rashba in Tosefta Yevamos (siman 81) regarding cheeses; and even though we do not rule like Rashba, nevertheless according to Taz below (siman 497 s.k. 2) who wrote regarding dead before us the presumption of life was impaired even though in all presumptions we say "now it was impaired"—life presumption is different as it stands for that and will certainly die—see there—so too presumption of pregnant will certainly be removed, behold she gave birth before us. And I have proof for Taz from Tosafot Yevamos (daf 67a) s.v. "beginning disqualifications of kehunah"—see there; and their words are closed in their answer there: for what helps that now before us it is nine months—we say in all presumptions even if impaired before us we say "now it was impaired"; and for Taz it is fine presumption of minor stands to be removed in this—one should not establish presumption where it was removed before us. Also in my humble opinion that which Tosafot in Bava Metzia specifically mentioned presumption of pregnant—there birth is the essence of the action and we judge on this whether at time of pulling the cow she was still pregnant or already gave birth; on this we say since moment before pulling the cow she was certainly pregnant, we establish presumption she was also pregnant at pulling; and what we see she gave birth now—now she gave birth; unlike here where doubt whether this offspring is eight days—presumption of pregnant does not decide from this it is not eight days—and this is a fine distinction and correct with God's help; and proof from that Yevamos regarding doubt minor or adult—they did not mention presumption of pregnant to judge it is minor; and also not relevant in our case to say the offspring was in presumption it is not eight days old—we say also now it is not eight days old, for it is simple at every moment it becomes greater in years than before; and only from minor to adult is presumption relevant, for in one moment one exits minority to majority and changed—it is relevant to say we establish on presumption of minor; unlike here where day eight does not change the animal's essence or name. And eight days are to reveal it was never stillborn—but not that there be matter of change and renewal upon reaching eight days—simple there is no presumption. And also in presumption of minority Maharit expanded in responsa (part 1 siman 41) it is not presumption since day by day it grows; and he expanded on Tosafot Yevamos intent above—in any case here regarding eight days, which is not matter of minority and majority, certainly irrelevant to say presumption it is not eight days old. And the essence: what to judge in our case that there is presumption "not slaughtered"—nevertheless in my humble opinion since from Torah we follow majority of offspring—even though rabbis forbade because of doubt they made—nevertheless presumption was already removed through majority and it is mere doubt; and like combining minority to presumption. And that gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed—it must be said truly since root of Torah prohibition, mesisach l'fi tumo does not help—not like Maharshal regarding eggs above, and so proved from hagahah Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah (siman 316) who wrote specifically regarding cows that are not accustomed to be milked without offspring, but regarding goats accustomed to be milked without offspring we do not rely on this—and its root from Maharai (siman 130) explained there: specifically regarding cows because it is not their way at all to be milked without offspring—in this they combine leniency of mesisach l'fi tumo where there is no concern he says so to enhance his merchandise; but regarding goats accustomed to be milked without offspring we do not rely on mesisach l'fi tumo, for he is not believed except in woman's testimony alone—see there. And behold truly there is study: this principle that mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed is specifically in Torah law or rabbinic in presumption of prohibition; but rabbinic without presumption he is believed—see Shach (siman 98 s.k. 2). And if here regarding milking goats—from Torah exempt from bechorah, majority animals are not milked unless they give birth, and rabbinically they were stringent to say here combine minority to presumption and it becomes half and half; and since only rabbinically half and half and there is no presumption of prohibition—why is mesisach l'fi tumo not believed. And it must be said since bechorah prohibition is Torah—even though concern is only rabbinic, nevertheless mesisach l'fi tumo does not help; and Maharai goes according to his view who ruled in Terumat HaDeshen (siman 79) one who buys eggs from gentile on Yom Tov after Shabbat—gentile mesisach l'fi tumo does not help, since preparation is Torah law mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed—see there—even though from Torah there is majority they were already born, only rabbinically forbidden as davar sheyesh lo matirin—nevertheless mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed since its root is Torah; and since Rama ruled like Terumat HaDeshen regarding milking goats, it is proved we rule that in its root Torah and rabbinic concern, mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed—not like Maharshal; and the wonder on Magen Avraham who follows Maharshal's approach is fulfilled—and it implies from Magen Avraham's words what Rama ruled there that gentile mesisach l'fi tumo is not believed on eggs—he holds like Mordechai that there is no majority to permit, for eggs from moment born they eat them; and from this Magen Avraham derived in our countries where many eggs are common, certainly majority brought from those born—heavy mesisach l'fi tumo is believed (and Magen Avraham there hints see Yoreh De'ah siman 15—it appears his intent is our difficulty: if we say in root Torah and rabbinic concern mesisach l'fi tumo is believed, why not believed mesisach l'fi tumo they are eight days). And this is astonishing—for if so, why regarding milking goats is mesisach l'fi tumo not believed—we return to what is not called presumption of prohibition here. And one witness is believed even if called presumption of prohibition—this matter itself that one witness is not believed in place of presumption is an unresolved question not resolved in Yevamos—and here it is rabbinic doubt; and even though Taz (siman 15) called it doubt of one-day-olds that it is davar sheyesh lo matirin—Shach wondered correctly that the permitter is not certainly coming; and apart from this, here from necessity if permission comes he will wait eight days—automatically also now permitted; and even according to Taz that it is davar sheyesh lo matirin—nevertheless specifically in doubt in action we go stringently, but not in legal doubt as Peri Chadash wrote (siman 498 s.k. 3) and responsa She'eilat Ya'avetz (siman 90); and I also brought proof from Ran Nedarim (daf 46b) who wrote regarding one who vows these fruits on his fellow and similarly vows on himself fruits without "these," and he exchanged them—he is permitted, for it is rabbinic doubt; and apparently though regarding vow on fellow it is not davar sheyesh lo matirin, for the forbidden one cannot permit him—but regarding vow on himself behold it is davar sheyesh lo matirin—nevertheless in legal doubt we go leniently; and if so here that is legal doubt, if one witness is believed in presumption of prohibition we go leniently—from all these reasons it appears one witness is believed they are eight days old. And see responsa Rosh (kelal 52 siman 5) who wrote regarding two and two contradicting on woman's offspring whether it lived thirty days—for we follow majority of offspring, behold he was not concerned for presumption of prohibition assisting other witnesses; also according to Rambam (chapter 3 laws of Yibbum) one witness is believed to testify months completed; and so Mechaber ruled (siman 256 seif 5)—and it implies the same regarding testifying it lived thirty days, and all one matter. If so it appears this is exactly our case; and even though Rosh in responsa holds one witness, and as hagahah Shulchan Aruch there wrote—one may say it is different there because matters of forbidden relations are not less than two; and even among poskim who permit there is not such proof for our case—one may say there presumption assists and leans to support. Nevertheless one may say since sages were stringent, one may say regarding animal only because of decree "lest person," as Tosafot Yevamos wrote—and since regarding person itself one witness is believed, so too regarding animal. And what depends on contradictory witnesses—if they came two by two it is contradictory testimony as if they do not exist; and if one permissive witness came first, he is like two and one's words are not in place of two—as stated in Shulchan Aruch below (siman 39) regarding lung adhesions—even though one may say there majority kosher animals assists him, so too majority viable offspring assists; and even according to Ritva brought in Shach (siman 127) who holds in prohibitions one witness is believed and need not live like two—one should not establish like two; nevertheless in Shulchan Aruch (siman 39 there) ruled simply that also in prohibitions it is like two. And in any case from rabbinic doubt nothing emerges whether it is like two; and automatically in rabbinic matters we go leniently; and from davar sheyesh lo matirin side there is no concern as written—as explained in my humble opinion with God's help.
