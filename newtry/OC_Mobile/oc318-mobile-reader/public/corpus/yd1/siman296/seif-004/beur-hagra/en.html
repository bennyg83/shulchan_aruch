(Supplement) The ruling is like Shmuel — Yevamot at the end of chapter HaArel; and even though R' Huna said in the name of R' [X] disagrees — R' Ada in the name of Rav holds like him; and the ruling does not follow one against two — unlike the Rosh in Yevamot there who ruled like R' Huna; and see the Rosh in Mo'ed Katan (chapter 1, siman 13) on the mishnah "one may not stir up," etc.; and see Bach siman 414 seif 1 in the gloss where the Rosh and his associates are explained as ruling like R' Huna; and Rambam [ruled] like R' Elazar bar Ahavah (and see there) (end). Therefore, etc. Yerushalmi there: R' Yochanan said: all concede regarding grapes that they are forbidden; R' Elazar said: the one who forbids is not forbidden; and the one who does not forbid is forbidden — what do they dispute? One who drapes his own grapevine over another's grain; but one who drapes another's grapevine over another's grain — all agree that the one who forbids is forbidden; one who drapes another's grapevine over another's grain — let us hear from this: R' Yehudah said, a case occurred where one sowed a vineyard on Shemitah, and the case came before R' Akiva and he said: a person cannot forbid that which is not his — behold, neither the grapevine is his nor the grain is his; and it was challenged; and R' Shimshon cited it and ruled like R' Yochanan.
