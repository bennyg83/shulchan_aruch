(Siman 296 seif 1) And likewise if he sowed two kinds of vegetables and a kernel, or one vegetable seed and one grain seed etc. — he receives lashes. This is Rambam Hilchot Kilayim chapter 5 halachah 2. Kesef Mishnah thought Tosafot Menachot and Ran Rishit haGez argue with Rambam on vegetables in a vineyard — that they are not kilayim from the Torah, for they use language "grain of five species are d'oraisa kilayim in a vineyard," but vegetables imply not d'oraisa; to me this is not compelled — perhaps they came only to exclude other garden seeds that are not eaten, similar to hemp and turnip discussed there. However from Tosafot Eruvin (daf 92) it appears as Kesef Mishnah wrote on dira gedolah bekatanah and ein dira ketanah begedolah etc. — vines in the small one, permitted to sow the large one; Rashi: even though dira gedolah bekatanah, seeds in the large do not forbid vines in the small, for Menachot says hemp and turnip the Torah forbade, other seeds rabbinically forbidden — this case did not make a prohibition so the rabbis did not penalize; so when he sowed the large in permission, no penalty applies. Tosafot challenged: how permit leaving the seeds when they grow — since dira gedolah bekatanah, even what grew spontaneously we learned in Kilayim: one passing through a vineyard saw a vegetable in his vineyard and said when I return I will harvest — if he added two hundred it is forbidden once he despaired of harvesting; that Menachot case is when the vineyard owner did not know seeds were in his vineyard — end; appears Tosafot' reason seeds are rabbinic is because Kesef Mishnah holds per Tosafot Menachot vegetables in vineyard are not d'oraisa kilayim — therefore Tosafot troubled Rashi from our mishnah vegetables in vineyard forbidden even what grew by itself — if only rabbinic why penalize; vegetables in vineyard are also only rabbinic yet forbidden even spontaneous growth; Rashi's view can be proved — he holds like Rambam vegetables in vineyard are d'oraisa kilayim; Mezarchi wrote plainly on Rashi in Chumash Teitzei and did not mention dissenters at all. Wonder of Tosafot on Rashi from vegetable case forbidden even spontaneous — if d'oraisa unlike seeds.
However from Tosafot Yevamot (daf 81) one can prove the other side — Tosafot hold like Rambam vegetables in vineyard are d'oraisa kilayim — they wrote clover in vineyard is kilayim d'oraisa; Sukkah (daf 35) Tosafot hold clover is a vegetable species; so Tosafot Zevachim (daf 73) and Rosh Orlah chapter 3. Thus Tzlach's complaint on Maharam in Rosh Beitzah is removed — he wrote bundles of clover in vineyard are kilayim d'oraisa, but Rashi there clover is a legume species and not d'oraisa kilayim; even per Rambam vegetables in vineyard are d'oraisa but legumes all agree are not kilayim in vineyard, only rabbinically; per Tosafot Sukkah I brought, Tosafot hold clover is a vegetable species and like Rambam vegetables in vineyard are d'oraisa. I can bring proof clover is not a legume species per Tosafot from what is taught first chapter Eruvin (daf 28) — beans, barley, and clover sown for vegetable — his intent is annulled before everyone; therefore sowing them is liable and their vegetable is exempt — why teach these — are they not in the category of grain and legumes taught in Tosefta: grain and legumes sown for vegetable — intent annulled, vegetable exempt, seed liable for terumot and maasrot; Kesef Mishnah already challenged this in Terumot chapter 2 halachah 7 and answered: perhaps because of clover he teaches it; this is possible only if we say clover is not a legume species — then it is not in grain and legumes of Tosefta and therefore taught in the baraita in Eruvin; but if clover is a legume species, it is impossible to say because of clover he teaches this baraita — the difficulty stands: why teach it — he already taught legumes in Tosefta; therefore conclude clover is not a legume species.
I have proof for Rambam and Shulchan Aruch that vegetables in vineyard are d'oraisa kilayim from Sotah (daf 43b) regarding grafting a tree into a vegetable — if forbidden it is d'oraisa; if only rabbinic for them it should return even when RHG repented as proven there; see Tzlach who proved Rambam's view from the sugya in Kiddushin (daf 38).
