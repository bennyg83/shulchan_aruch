Provided, etc. — Or Zaru'a in mishnah 3 chapter 2 of Peah; and explained "barley," etc.; and Or Zaru'a there who explained they mix one with another; and Yerushalmi there: like pounding greens in a mortar or pounding on a fence — from what is said "barley pounds" and the fence does not pound — this says pounding on a fence; and Rosh brought here in Kilayim; and there is a error — there it says "pounding," etc., and must read "this says," etc. Per Rosh's explanation in Peah there: meaning they asked whether like greens in a mortar — that they should mix inside the fence per Rambam's explanation — or pounding on the fence meaning they mix one within another above the fence; and it is plain from what is asked "fence pounds" — learn that above the fence they mix one within another; and so Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchot Ma'aser. But if the fence separates below and the branches and foliage mix above touching the fence, etc. — rather one could say he means they mix above — not that on this side within that side, rather they mix by themselves, etc.; and it seems Rambam's version is "this says there is no pounding on a fence" — meaning fence pounds implies the fence is within the branches like greens in a mortar; but barley pounds implies the barley is like greens in a mortar on the fence.
In another case, etc. — Yerushalmi there: R' Chiya in the name of R' Yochanan — there must be a hollow space of four; R' Shimon bar Rav Yitzchak: this you say in a circle, but in a square one need not have a hollow of four; R' Chelbo ben Shaul said: there must be three handbreadths of earth from above; R' Yosi: this you say in a circle, but in a square one need not have three handbreadths of earth from above. From three to four — that is the mishnah; less than three is as sealed; from three to four completes four — sow immediately; R' Avin in the name of Shmuel; and in houses of kilayim they hide — a house in which they hide kilayim. Rosh explains what he wrote "from three," etc. — it refers to a ditch; but Rambam explains it as a house, as written in Semag; and so he wrote afterward R' Avin, etc.; and I do not know why he did not write that it completes.
