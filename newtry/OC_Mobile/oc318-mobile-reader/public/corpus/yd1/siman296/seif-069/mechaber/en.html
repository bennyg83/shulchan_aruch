All these measures that one distances between vines and grain or vegetable apply only in Eretz Yisrael or Syria; but in the Diaspora it is permitted to sow initially beside vines within the vineyard. And they forbade in the Diaspora only to sow two species of vegetable or two species of grain with vine seed casually. And if he told a non-Jewish child to sow for him in the Diaspora, it is permitted; but he may not tell an adult non-Jew lest it be confused with an Israelite. And even though it is permitted to sow vegetable beside the vineyard in the Diaspora, behold that vegetable sown there is forbidden in eating even in the Diaspora—when one sees him harvest and sell; but its doubt is permitted. {Rama: And some say that since it is permitted to sow vegetable beside vines in the Diaspora, it is permitted to eat that vegetable as well (Hagahot Maimoniyos chapter 10 of Ma'achalot Assurot law 9 and Terumat HaDeshen siman 194). And one should be concerned only in a place where there is reason to fear they were sown as two species of vegetable or two species of grain with vine seed casually (Mordechai on the first chapter of Kiddushin). Therefore they are accustomed to be lenient in these lands with vegetables found sown in vineyards, for it is not common that they were sown in prohibition.}
