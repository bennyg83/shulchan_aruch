A trained vine, etc. Raavad questioned [Rambam] on this — for this constitutes two trained vines with sacred space within eight cubits; and he explains it like the Rash [Shimon of Sens] there. And Rambam holds that something is not called a "trained vine" unless it has actually been trained [with supports], as written in seif 56; and that is the meaning of "in seif 59: since he trained them, etc." — and here the middle row adjacent to the fence has become ruined, and [the vine] is trained upon it.
(Supplement) A trained vine, etc. The explanation requires investigation as to why this should be less [in stringency] than any ordinary single trained vine. And the Rash forced himself [to interpret it] — "I saw [it] as according to Rivan, or, etc." — and his words are quite forced. But it seems [correct] per the interpretation of the Rash, who wrote: "for example, if one planted eleven grapevines alongside the fence on one side, and the middle vine became ruined — and [one wishes] to sow there — and not for the reason of Rivan, etc. — because the prohibition of a trained vine applies only opposite the vines in their width, where one can see all the vines; but in such a case one need only keep a distance of six handbreadths — like a single vine. And here, for a partial trained vine, the law is, etc." — and that is the meaning of what is stated there in the mishnah: "a trained vine that extends, etc." — meaning: in the form of this configuration — where the vine is planted at part of the wall in a corner — to sow at the other end of the wall; and R. Yosi said: if there are not four cubits in the vacant space — it should not come, etc. Unlike Rambam's interpretation in seif 63. (End.) And one out of sixty by the cubit. See Baer Heitev; and so it is in the Jerusalem Talmud there: "does this not conflict with R. Ze'era — for R. Ze'era said: eight, not counting the place of the crosses — he explained it as eight cubits and more; and why was it not taught? — and should we not hold like that which R. Yochanan said: all the measures the Sages stated [require] an additional handbreadth, and the hin they increased a little — therefore it was not taught."
