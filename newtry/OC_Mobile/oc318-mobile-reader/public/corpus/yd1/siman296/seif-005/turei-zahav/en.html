One who sees kilayim, etc. The Yerushalmi states that the law was taught regarding a worker, and that an owner occupied with his work is treated like a worker. In my opinion, a worker has authorization to enter the vineyard and could uproot the kilayim. If he leaves it, he is penalized personally, though others are not. An owner who is occupied and preoccupied with his work is similarly treated like a worker. If he says, “When I return I will uproot it,” the produce is prohibited to him but not to others. When he is not occupied, his failure is more serious and may prohibit it to others as well. Anyone who has permission to enter another person's vineyard and is not objected to is comparable to a worker. A mere passerby who sees kilayim in another person's vineyard is not penalized. This also explains why the Rambam did not separately cite the Yerushalmi: his expression, “one who sees and maintains it,” refers to someone who has power to uproot or preserve it.
