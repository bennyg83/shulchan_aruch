One who sees kilayim etc. Raavad — in Yerushalmi they said in action they taught: baal habayit who is busy with his craft — they made him like a worker — end. Kesef Mishnah explained: a worker engaged in labor; so baal habayit engaged in his craft is equal to prohibition when he says "when I return I will harvest," and if he added 100x100 as in seif 16; if he says "when I reach it I will harvest" — no prohibition; afterward he said baal habayit's law is like a worker if engaged in his craft — if he says "when I reach it" it is permitted; what is written here "worker" means one occupied, whether baal habayit or another; they did not say the language "worker" except to teach us that even one who is not his own — if he despaired, it is forbidden to him — end; and it is clear on his explanation — if so, that which cites Yerushalmi "they made baal habayit like a worker" would come to include one who says "when I reach it" permitted — and this is explicit mishnah chapter 5; this "worker" is even if not engaged in his craft, nevertheless he has relevance to go in the vineyard; therefore he can go there to uproot the kilayim — since he did not, it is forbidden to him as a penalty, not to others; afterward he said baal habayit when engaged in his craft and busy — his law is like a worker who does not forbid others when saying "when I return I will harvest"; but when not engaged and not busy — forbidden also to others; nevertheless there is a distinction between "when I reach" and "when I return" as seif 16 and Yerushalmi there concludes the distinction between worker and baal habayit regarding forbidding others; the end appears to explain the beginning — why they made him like a worker, as above; in any case it implies one who sees in his friend's vineyard has no penalty per Yerushalmi; it seems the same for anyone who has permission to go in his friend's vineyard and he is not particular — he is like a worker; this is why Rambam did not cite Yerushalmi here — he included it in what he wrote "one who sees" and upholds etc., implying one who can uproot or uphold as explained; Kesef Mishnah forced to align Rambam with his explanation — per what I wrote it fits.
