Whatever one sows under the tendrils and leaves emerging from the vine—behold he has caused hikdish, even though the seed is far from the root of the vine many amot. One who suspends the vine on part of a trellis (meaning: woven wood or reeds on which vine tendrils are suspended when very long)—he may not sow under the permitted part of the trellis, even though there are no leaves or tendrils on them; and if he sowed, since the seed is not under the vine's covering, behold it is permitted. And likewise if he suspended the vine on part of the beams of a tree that does not produce fruit, such as cedar and cypress. But if he suspended on part of a food tree, behold it is permitted to sow under the beams of the tree on which vine tendrils did not spread, for a person does not cancel a food tree and make it a trellis for a vine; and if tendrils spread after he sowed and covered the seed, behold he returns them elsewhere.
