One who rounds, etc. This follows Rav Huna in Nazir 57b, for the discussion in Bava Metzia cited above accords with his view. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'and Rav Adda,' etc., and Tosafot in Shevuot under the citation above.
But it is permitted, etc.; and there are, etc. Nimukei Yosef at the end of Makkot writes: 'Some are uncertain whether a Jew is prohibited from rounding the hair of a non-Jew according to Rav Huna, just as he is prohibited regarding a minor. The Rosh wrote that although Rav Huna forbids a man to round the hair of a minor, that case is different because the minor will eventually become subject to the commandment, whereas it is permitted to round the hair of a non-Jew. According to this, it is likewise permitted to round the hair of a woman.' Tosafot in Nazir, at the end of the cited passage, rejected the Rosh's reason and gave another reason to permit doing so for a non-Jew, while remaining uncertain regarding a woman. Tosafot in Shevuot, at the end of the cited passage, implies that it is forbidden to do so for a woman, for they wrote that Shmuel agrees with Rav Adda, etc.
And a minor, etc. This is obvious, since a minor is not subject to liability. Even a woman may round his hair according to Rav Huna. Rav Adda bar Ahavah disagrees only because he holds that a woman is subject to liability for rounding a man's hair.
