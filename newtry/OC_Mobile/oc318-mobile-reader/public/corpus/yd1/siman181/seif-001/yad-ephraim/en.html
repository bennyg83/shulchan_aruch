One who makes a bald patch, etc. The responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 80, writes that one may not permit shaving the beard first with scissors and then passing a razor over the skin where the hair had been, on the ground that no hair remains long enough to be grasped by the scissors. That argument is incorrect, for even in such a case it is properly considered shaving; see there.
Further, there in siman 81, he writes that there is no distinction between a razor and a sharp stone that cuts the hair, such as pigment or pumice stone. Even when using a depilatory spread like a clay plaster, which remains on the face until it dries and is then scraped away, one must take care not to scrape off the plaster with a knife or any other sharp implement, since this can easily result in destruction of the hair at the root; see there. Therefore, one should use a wooden splinter or a smooth bone implement for scraping, since with these there is no concern at all that the hairs will be cut; see there.
