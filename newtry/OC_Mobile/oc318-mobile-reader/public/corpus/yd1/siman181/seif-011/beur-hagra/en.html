And there are many opinions concerning them, etc. Rashi explains in Makkot that two corners are beneath the ear, at the protruding edge of the jaw; two are at the end of the jaw; and one is at the tuft of the beard. See his commentary to the Mishnah there, s.v. 'two,' and to the Gemara 21a, s.v. 'the joints of the beard.' In Shevuot, however, Rashi explains that the jaw is broad toward the temples, and every broad surface has two extremities. These make four corners, with the fifth at the tuft of the beard. Rabbenu Chananel explains that the two points where the jaw joins the temples are two corners, the two edges of the lip are two more, and the fifth is at the tuft of the beard. According to all these explanations, the fifth corner is at the tuft of the beard. The Rosh, however, after citing all the explanations, wrote that Mishnah Negaim, chapter 10, teaches: 'What is considered the beard? From the joint of the jaw until the prominence of the throat.' His words imply that, according to this, the fifth corner is at the upper part of the throat. The Remazim states this as well, and the Tur attributes it to Rabbenu Chananel. His words are astonishing, as Beit Yosef also objects, especially since Torat Kohanim explicitly teaches: 'The corner of their beard means the end of the beard, the tuft of the beard.' All the authorities agree that there is no corner beneath the throat. Semak writes: 'It is proper that a razor not pass over the beard. Rabbenu Tam forbids even beneath the chin with a razor, because at times one draws toward it the skin of the corner,' meaning the skin of the beard's tuft. Rabbenu Yonah gave another reason, namely that removing body hair with a razor is forbidden throughout the body, as stated at the beginning of siman 182. This is the meaning of the gloss here, 'and even,' etc. Nevertheless, Rabbenu Yonah's reasoning does not appear correct, for why should the portions of the beard outside the five corners differ from one another? Those areas are permitted even with a razor. The prohibition concerning the body applies only where hair does not grow so heavily and a person removes it merely for beautification, which is forbidden because of 'A man's garment shall not be upon a woman,' etc.
(Likkut) And there are many opinions concerning them, etc. The principal view is Rashi's explanation, and this is also stated in Eliyah Rabbah. This concludes the passage.
Therefore, etc. The Rosh and the other authorities.
