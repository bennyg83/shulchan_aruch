In the Tur it is written that according to R' Chananel, one of the pe'ot is at the end of the beard, at the throat; and Beit Yosef challenged this, for it is not mentioned in the Talmud — only in the Rosh's code in the view of R' Chananel, who mentioned "the lock of the beard below" — and the reason is that it draws toward itself the skin of the pe'ah at the end of the bone they call the chin. And in the Tosefta which the Rosh cites it is written: "the pe'ot of the beard [extend] from the joint of the jaw to the protrusion of the throat" — hence at the very least the Tur should have written "until the throat," implying that there is the principal fifth pe'ah; and therefore he left the matter requiring further clarification. And my father-in-law of blessed memory elaborated on this to say that the principal fifth pe'ah is specifically at the location of the throat itself, and the remaining hair beneath the neck is not included in the prohibition at all — but the Tosefta's language does not imply this, for it says "until the protrusion of the throat," not "at the throat" at all. The conclusion is: below the neck there is no biblical prohibition — only a rabbinic one — for the reason mentioned in Beit Yosef in the name of R' Yonah: that the Sages prohibited removing hair with a razor anywhere on the body, even on one's arms, since it resembles women's grooming; and afterward he wrote in the name of Sefer Mitzvot Katan another reason: that it is forbidden according to R' Chananel to use a razor below the chin since it sometimes draws the skin of the pe'ah toward it, etc. — and therefore scissors resembling a razor are permitted there.
