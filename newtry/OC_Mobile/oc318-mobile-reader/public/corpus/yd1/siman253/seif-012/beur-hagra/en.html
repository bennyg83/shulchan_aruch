One who needs, etc. See the Be'er HaGolah. The Hagahot Mordechai, however, disputes this and writes that the cited baraita refers to a householder. This is the primary view. The Tosefta at the end of Peah states: From gifts to the poor, one may not repay a loan, compensate another for prior favors, redeem captives, provide a wedding gift, or give anything to charity. One may, however, send from it an act of kindness, provided he informs the recipient, and one may give it to a fellow as a favor. We also learned explicitly in the Mishnah at the end of Bikkurim: 'Why did they say, etc.?' A creditor may collect his debt from it, and this certainly applies to gifts to the poor. The claim that we assess the donors' intent and presume they did not give it for such a purpose is also incorrect, for the poor person has already acquired it and may redirect it to any purpose, as stated in the chapter HaUmanin, Bava Metzia 78b. Certainly, therefore, his creditor acquires a right in it. See Mordechai there, where many authorities dispute this ruling.
