A poor person, etc., is not compelled, etc. The Bach writes that because everyone is obligated to give charity, even a poor person supported by charity, as stated at the beginning of siman 248, it was necessary to explain that if he does not give, he is not compelled. The reason, according to the Bach, is that the poor person can fulfill his obligation by giving to another poor person, who then gives back to him, as the Beit Yosef writes at the end of siman 251 in the name of the Hagahos Mordechai, and as the Mechaber rules in siman 251, seif 12. This is difficult, because according to that explanation, why must the text say that charity is accepted from him? It is obvious, since he is obligated to give. Rather, the present case concerns a poor person who lacks his own sustenance. Such a person is certainly not obligated to give charity until his own needs are met, as stated above in siman 251, seif 3 in the Rama. The beginning of siman 248 concerns one who has enough for his own support. Therefore, in the present case, he is not compelled to give.
