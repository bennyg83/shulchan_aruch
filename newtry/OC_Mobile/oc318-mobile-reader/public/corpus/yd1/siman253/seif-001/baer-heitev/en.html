From the tamchui. It is a large bowl and collectors gather in it food from householders and distribute to the poor two meals every day; and the charity fund is divided from erev Shabbat to erev Shabbat; and Taz wrote in Talmud we ask behold on Shabbat three meals are required per the rabbis — that is fifteen meals; and per Rabbi Chidka sixteen; and we answer this is Rabbi Akiva's view who said make your Shabbat profane and do not depend on creatures; and Rashi explained regarding Shabbat for one who can afford he should make Shabbat meals either per the rabbis or per Rabbi Chidka; but this one who must depend on creatures — he should make his Shabbat profane and not impose on others the honor of his Sabbaths; and Perishah brought this and wrote this is specifically per Rabbi Chidka but per the rabbis there is no difficulty; and he did not examine the sugya I brought for even per the rabbis we say thus — end quote (and in Nekudat HaKesef he wrote Perishah's words are correct for per the rabbis in any case it is difficult for what he needs to eat after Shabbat he eats on Shabbat; and so Rambam in commentary on mishnah end of Peah explicitly); and Semak wrote laws of fund and tamchui do not apply in our days; and Shach explained meaning all laws of fund per what will be explained in siman 256; but certainly every community of Israel has a charity fund; and Taz further wrote and from what is written end of siman Ran Shabbat they give him food for three meals — Tosafot already resolved for Shabbat meals he should not begin to take; but if he already took he takes also for Shabbat meals — end quote.

Silver. Tur wrote likewise a silver menorah and table must be sold; and Beit Yosef challenged for that Tanna who holds thus is challenged in Talmud; and Taz resolved there is a distinction of times — now that one table is for all reclining certainly obligated to sell if of silver; but in Talmud's time each had a small table before him — it is like eating utensils; and Rosh and other poskim wrote if after he took these utensils happened to him such as they fell to him in inheritance or found — they do not obligate him to sell; and Rashi further wrote if known he was wealthy at the time he took — court comes and collects from him what he took; and even though money he does not have they do not claim — nevertheless because of penalty they collect from him — end quote.
