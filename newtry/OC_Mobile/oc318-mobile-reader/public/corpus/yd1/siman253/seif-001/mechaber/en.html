Who is fit to take charity. And it contains 12 seifim. One who has food for two meals may not take from the soup kitchen; food for fourteen meals — he may not take from the charity fund; and if he has two hundred zuz and does not carry on business with them, or has fifty zuz and carries on business with them — he may not take charity; and if he has two hundred zuz lacking a dinar and does not carry on business with them — even if they give him a thousand zuz at once, behold he may take: {Rama: And one who goes from his home and travels from city to city to collect — all the way that he intended to travel when he left his home is called one time; and even if they gave him two hundred zuz in one city, he may accept more; and from here onward it is forbidden (Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra, in the name of Or Zaru'a).} And if he has much property but is in debt, or his wife's ketubah is liened against him — behold he may take; and if he has a house and much household utensils but does not have two hundred zuz — behold he may take, and he need not sell his household utensils, even if they are of silver and gold. When do these matters apply? With utensils for eating and drinking, clothing, bedding, and the like; but a comb or spoon of silver — he sells them and does not take from charity. And that which they do not obligate him to sell his silver and gold utensils — specifically as long as he need not take from the charity fund but takes secretly from individuals; but if he comes to take from the charity fund, they do not give him until he sells his utensils: {Rama: And likewise in a place where there is an enactment not to give charity to one who has a fixed amount — they do not count for him a dwelling house and his utensils (Mordechai, beginning of Bava Kama); and whoever is wealthy is forbidden to give to his children even though they are adults if they rely on their father's table (Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Metzia; Beit Yosef siman 252); and all this is the way of charity, but the way of a gift and honor — a person may receive, as they say: one who wishes to benefit may benefit like Elisha (Mordechai from Hagahot Bava Kama).}
