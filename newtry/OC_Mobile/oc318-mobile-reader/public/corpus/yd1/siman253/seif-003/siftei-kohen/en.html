One who owned land, etc. The Mechaber copied the language of the Rambam. His view is clearly based on the Rif's reading and concerns a wealthy person whose land, even if sold at the reduced winter price, would still leave him with two hundred zuz. If the land is not worth two hundred zuz even at the reduced price, everyone agrees that he may receive even one thousand zuz at once, since he is poor. The Bach writes likewise. Nevertheless, because he does not presently have two hundred zuz in cash and would have to sell cheaply during the rainy season, he is not compelled to sell. Instead, he is supported from charity up to one half of the land's normal value. We do not assess the exact current discount. Whether the reduction is small or large, he is supported up to one half, because it is normal for land to fall in price by as much as one half, but not more. If others can sell at a high price while he alone cannot find a buyer except at a low price, he may be supported even beyond one half of the land's value. This is the Rambam's view. Tosafot, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the Semag, following the Rif's reading, hold that the issue is not how much charity is given, but whether he can sell the land in winter for half its value. If he can, he is not supported; if he cannot, he is supported, even by more than half the land's value, because a reduction of up to one half is considered normal, but not more. The Tur writes that this is the primary view, and this is also the Rama's ruling in the name of some authorities. All this is clear, contrary to the Bach's objections to the Tur and the Rama. The words of the Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Kamma are also confused on this point, and Rabbi Mordechai of Tiktin already challenged them in his glosses to the Rif. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that when we say he is supported up to one half of the summer value, he is not given a large amount in one day. Rather, he receives his daily needs, day by day, until the total reaches one half of the land's value. The Mishnah's rule that one who lacks even one dinar from two hundred zuz may take even one thousand zuz at once applies to a person who is genuinely poor. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this.
Land, etc. This excludes his dwelling house, as the Tur writes. He is not required to sell the home in which he lives, as stated above in seif 1. The Beit Yosef writes likewise in the name of the Ramah and the Rosh, and so write Tosafot and the Mordechai.
At a high price, etc. If all land has fallen in value, including the property of others, then even if the land is worth less than half its usual price, he must sell it and may not take charity, provided that at the reduced price he will still have two hundred zuz. So rule the Tur and the poskim.
Rather, he continues eating from the poor man's tithe, etc. The Nimukei Yosef writes that he receives only the amount by which his land is discounted more than everyone else's, and the Beit Yosef cites him. This is not implied by the Ramban, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, or the other poskim. Rather, he is supported until he can sell at the proper price. This is the primary ruling.
