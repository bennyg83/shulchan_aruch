It is permitted to write the Divine Name, etc. This somewhat implies that writing over an erased or scraped area is preferable to suspending the Name above the line, as the Beit Yosef explains according to the Rosh, and as some authorities also maintain. The Tur similarly writes below in siman 279, seif 4, that scraping is not as disgraceful as suspension. Therefore, whenever the defect can be corrected either by suspension or by writing over an erased or scraped area, the scribe should not suspend it. I believe this is the accepted practice.
An erased or scraped area, etc. 'Erased' means that the ink was removed while still wet. 'Scraped' means that it was scraped away after it had dried.
There is no distinction, etc. This gloss is arranged here in all printed editions, and so it appears in the Levush. In truth, however, it does not belong here. Why would one think to distinguish here between a Torah scroll and the other sacred books? Since it is permitted in a Torah scroll to write over an erased or scraped area, it is certainly permitted in the other books. Rather, this gloss belongs above in seif 6, where the ruling that part of a Name suspended above the line is invalid applies even to the other sacred books. One may not write part of a Name suspended above the line in them either. This is also stated in Sefer HaTerumah. See there.
