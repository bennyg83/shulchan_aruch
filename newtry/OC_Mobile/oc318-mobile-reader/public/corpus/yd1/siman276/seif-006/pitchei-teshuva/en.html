Gold on them. See Noda Biyehudah second series Choshen Mishpat siman 181 — asked regarding gold plated on the holy ark whether permitted or forbidden because of erasing the Name; he wrote though Nekudot HaKesef in Hilchot Sefer Torah wrote if one threw gold on Names written in ink it is invalid — upper writing nullifies lower because it erases it, etc. — nevertheless in our case one may distinguish: there the Name was written in ink as required and in holiness, and gold disqualifies the Name from holiness, for halacha is to write in ink specifically — that is erasing; but when initially in another color, even if gold covers it, that is not erasing, for what difference between red and green color — therefore in our case no concern — see there. From this we learn permitted to pass quill over letters of the Name where writing aged, and no concern this erases underlying writing (and though in Talmud regarding dispute of R' Yehuda and Rabbanan on writing Yehuda it is explicit — one can say there different since not written in holiness and permitted to erase per Baer Heitev note 9); and I saw in writings of the great Rav R' Daniel z"l he ruled thus. [See above siman 271 note 18.] However it appears to me only when some ink color remains, or all ink jumped from all letters of the Name or from last three letters; but if last two letters' form is visible and first two letters all ink jumped leaving only reddish impression — no remedy by passing quill, per what I wrote below note 30 that the Name must be written in order; and see Magen Avraham Orach Chaim siman 32 seif 27. See Shames Tzedakah Choshen Mishpat siman 59 — wax drop on letters of Name certainly no disqualification here, unlike throwing gold where gold nullifies ink and hides from eye, but here letters are visible and wax does not nullify; if thick wax drop one may remove little by little with knife without erasure concern — see there.
