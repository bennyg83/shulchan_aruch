The attachments. In responsum Noda BiYehudah Yoreh De'ah siman 76: asked regarding a scribe who erred in parashat Tetzaveh and wrote Eloheikhem instead of Eloheihem — if permitted to peel the two letters kaf-mem and write in their place heh-mem, since prohibition of erasing attachments is only rabbinic, as Taz wrote; and he answered: nevertheless forbidden to peel; and even one who permits peeling the Name itself forbids here, because the Name in kaf-mem — its holiness is upon it — not so attachments — see there [and see in responsum Chatam Sofer siman 264, asked on a similar case where the scribe erred and wrote Eloheinu instead of Eloheikhem — if permitted to peel nun-vav and write kaf-mem in their place; and he brought Noda BiYehudah's words and also the Gaon Bnei Yonah's view: what they said "attached to the Name" means it should be so, such as it should be written in that place Eloheinu, and a mishap occurred in nun-vav of Eloheinu and erasure is needed — forbidden to erase, for they already sanctified the Name; but if the attachment was written in error, even though they sanctified it in thought, it is like sanctifying invalid offerings relative to the altar, and did not sanctify the Name, and erased explicitly. And he debated his words: perhaps what sanctifies an attachment is because it is comparable to Temple vessels — all who touch them; and behold the golden altar sanctifies even what is not fit for it at all. And he ruled for practical law: certainly the attachment, not in error, may not be peeled, for the reason the Gaon Noda BiYehudah suggested — it is lowering from holiness; and if written in error and the attachment not in the proper place — do not erase with complete erasure, but peeling that attachment is entirely permitted, for there is no issue of doubt of erasure, since all prohibition of erasing attachments is only rabbinic; and for lowering from holiness there is none, for behold even when written with the Name there was no connection; and further it causes invalidation — lowering for the entire sheet and for many names written there, etc.; and he concludes thus should be ruled — see there; and also in siman 274 there].
