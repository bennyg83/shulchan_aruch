And yes, Avery. A name like our formula:
And there are [those who], etc. Meaning: in a different way, following R' Chananel's reading in Tosafot there s.v. 'with alef,' etc. But in the Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Megillah it is stated explicitly like the first opinion: 'he wrote alef-dalet from Adoneinu; alef-heh from Akik; shin-dalet from Shadai; tzadi-bet from Tzvaot — behold, these may be erased,' etc.; 'he wrote yod-kuf from four letters [i.e., alef-lamed] from Elohim — it may not be erased; the general principle: anything whose counterpart — i.e., the same [Name] — remains valid elsewhere may not be erased.' And this is what is written above: 'or alef-heh,' etc. And the name they write, etc. Since it is not the letters of the [divine] Name — and even though it alludes to the Name; and as Rosh wrote in Shevuot there: 'all of Shlomo,' etc. — he does not mean that it may not be erased, but rather to understand its interpretation, etc.; and likewise he wrote in responsa, section 3, section 15; and likewise Rambam and other poskim — for he counted only the seven names mentioned above, and although 'Elohim' and similar names have many [instances] that are profane, and yet he counted them and did not count the others; except that in his rulings there he was uncertain — from the abovementioned Tosafot who said in one version that they may not be erased since they are part of the Ineffable Name, and similarly the first letter is the beginning of the divine Name and is like the head of the word — and even though it is not comparable to the Name, for there [the Name] consists of two letters and nothing is written after them, unlike here — nevertheless, one should be stringent without great need; see there; and this is what is written: 'if not,' etc.
