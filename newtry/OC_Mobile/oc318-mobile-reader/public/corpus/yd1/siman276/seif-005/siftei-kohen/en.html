If he wrote the Divine Names, etc. The Levush writes that this applies specifically to the Divine Names. Regarding the rest of the Torah scroll, although it may be written only in black ink and not with any other substance, as stated above in siman 271, seif 6, if it was written in ink and gold was applied over it, it remains valid. This does not appear correct. The reason that applying gold invalidates the writing is that the upper writing is itself considered writing and nullifies the lower writing, as stated in the Beit Yosef. What difference, then, is there between the Divine Names and the rest of the Torah scroll? The Divine Names were singled out only because elsewhere in the Torah scroll the defect may be repaired by erasing or scraping away the gold. With a Divine Name, however, the writing is invalid and cannot be repaired, because the Name may not be erased. The baraita therefore states that the sheet must be placed in genizah. The Beit Yosef writes this in Orach Chaim, siman 32, as does the Shulchan Aruch there in seif 3. The Levush himself likewise writes there that other writing is also invalid, except that a Divine Name has no remedy. See there. The Derishah in seif 4 appears to agree with the Levush's distinction, but this is incorrect, as I have written.
