After them. Taz and Shach wrote: but before them, such as lamed of le-Hashem, or bet of be-Hashem, or aleph of Elohim and the like — permitted to erase (but what is attached after them has holiness, for they already sanctified the Name). And Taz wrote: if he forgot to write the final mem of Eloheikhem, he can hang it between the lines, and there is no issue of hanging part of the Name, for this is not from the Name itself (and even one who wants to be stringent initially, nevertheless post factum it is plain not to invalidate). And if he forgot the yod of Eloheinu and wrote deficient Eloanu — there is no repair for this, only must remove the sheet entirely (for the yod is root of the Name). And nevertheless, since scribes have practiced to scrape a Name written in error, therefore if it is not easy to remove the sheet and write another, in this too he should do so — see there, where he extended greatly on what is explained on this matter in responsum of Mateh Binyamin. And Shach wrote: a profane name that is written like sacred names, such as "and you shall be like gods" and the like — permitted to erase; and also a sacred name not written for its holiness — permitted to erase specifically for repair; but to cut and store — permitted even not for repair; but written for its holiness — even to cut and store for repair is forbidden; and if many names were written not for their sake in a Torah scroll — forbidden to erase them even for repair, because it appears spotted — end quote.
