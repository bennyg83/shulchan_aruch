Forbidden, etc. — Sifrei parashat Re'eh: R' Yishmael says: from where for one who erases a letter from the Name that he transgresses a negative commandment? As it says "and you shall destroy their name... you shall not do so to Hashem your God." And so in Mishnat Soferim chapter 5 halacha 5.
And not so.” A.D. (L.B.):
These are the seven names. The Rambam, as a second formula written by the first ISA, is certainly a mistake there:
And some have the version, etc. — it is the latter version that Kesef Mishneh wrote in Rambam, and it is the correct one, for in the Gemara they counted it and it is certainly a sacred name; and so they counted it in Yerushalim first chapter of Megillah; and even though in Gemara and Yerushalim many are counted among them, they are only seven; and so in Mishnat Soferim chapter 4 halacha 1 — only the name Elohim was omitted from there; and there halacha 2: one who writes aleph-lamed-mem from four letters, etc. (Likut) And some have the version, etc. — meaning but the version Elok is included in Elohim; rather this is singular language and that is plural language, like Adon and Adonim; and the difference between them is providence and conduct in general or in particular. And he did not count the name Y-K, which is also included in the essential name, even though he counted the name aleph-lamed-mem, because it is one root meaning strength and power, as we find used in one place "to El my hand" (Genesis 31:29), "rams of the land" (Ezekiel 17:13); but the name Y-K is also from the essential name, used on Havayah and existence; therefore we do not find it elsewhere; and so in Gemara, Yerushalim, and Mishnat Soferim they did not count it alone; and so all seven names are arranged in the Gemara — only consider each name Elok as one. But per its translation Y-K is the name of fear; and it seems this is the dispute of Rashbam and R' Elazar in Yerushalim (chapter 2 of Chagigah) and in Midrash (parashat Bereishit 12); and in our Gemara we say like R' Elazar, as written in chapter 3 of Menachot 29b: "what is written ki b-Y-K," etc. (end).
That I will be. The same for the third Y-K — rather he speaks emphatically, that even "that I will be" is sacred; and it is not like Elohai Elohim where the second is profane, as written halacha 4; and they are only three.
