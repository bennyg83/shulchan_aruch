Elokai Elohim. Tur also wrote Elohai, and so in some Rambam manuscripts — for Elohai is also the essential Name and the yud is part of the root; so Kesef Mishneh, since we find in Elohim the yud is also root, likewise in Elohai. In responsum Maharar Benjamin in Masat Binyamin regarding Eloheinu where the sofer forgot the yud and wrote Elohenu without yud — some say scrape nun-vav and write yud then nun-vav, and do not say the Name was already sanctified for nun-vav since a letter is missing from the Name, therefore not written properly — not applicable to say Name already sanctified; some say remove that sheet; he agreed to hang the yud above, explaining there is reason to say the yud is not root but like kadoshinu u-votzreinu, and every Elohai in a verse — the yud is for attachment to the following word like Elohei ha-shamayim; he concludes even per Kesef Mishneh that it is root, nevertheless only for sanctity not to erase, but for hanging which is rabbinic it is fine, for no stringency on Elohai yud since not root; though in Yirmiyah 23 "ha-Elohai mi-karov" — there is no attachment there, pause — yud not root but plural like Elohim with missing mem; per his words one who erases Elohai yud is not lashed, for Rambam only lashes for erasing the Name itself, not attached letters — rabbinic prohibition; per Maharar Benjamin Elohai yud is attached — does not appear so per his view yud is for attachment — this is not general, for kadoshinu also has yud not for attachment; Elohei ha-shamayim — without yud there is still attachment if one said El ha-shamayim; yud does not create attachment but indicates plurality like adonei Yosef; Elohai indicates many divine powers though He is One — as Rashi on "va-yihyu ke-Elohim"; Yerushalmi counts Elohai among attached names not essential names — I do not know the proof, for there Elohai is patach meaning "my god" not essential; from Elohim derive three attached forms; Elohai with tzere is truly plural like Elohim; clear to me Tanna Kama holds nun-vav of Eloheikhem and nun-vav of Eloheinu are erased, not yud which is essential — if not he would say greatness that Elohai yud is erased; Maharar Benjamin wrote yud plainly erased per Tanna Kama since not called Name — the Rav should not say so, for holiness does not depend on pronunciation but on writing like final hei of Ehyeh and yud of Eloheikhem — also rest on essential Name. First way: scrape nun-vav — not sanctified since not written properly; nevertheless Elokim written before was sanctified and draws nun-vav — no proof needed, hear from braita: others say kaf of Eloheikhem not erased, Tanna Kama says erased — all erasure presumed improper writing; if not, how erase even other Torah letters; certainly improper writing yet others hold not erased because Name sanctified even in error — same for skipped yud; a fortiori not erase what has no error from skipped yud — Name clearly sanctified. If wrote properly and errored with meaningless letter — no holiness, all agree erase. Third way: remove sheet — best, not scrape mentions from Torah per Beit Yosef in name of Rosh and R' Yehuda his son; better remove sheet than verse of Torah; scribes practice scraping mistaken Names with support from Massekhet Soferim chapter 5 two holy Names — maintain first, block second; both mean scraping; Beit Yosef in name of R' Yitzchak of Alexandria; if cannot easily remove sheet, scrape too. Also in that responsum on "va-yitosh Elokah asah" — Elokah missing vav — no fix needed, sometimes full sometimes deficient — wonder: since our books write full with vav we are particular and should fix/hang vav or scrape Name and rewrite.
The second approach he mentioned there: to scrape the nun-vav on the grounds that here one does not say the Name was already sanctified, since it was not written according to its law. This too cannot be right, in my humble opinion — for certainly even here the Name of Alef-Lamed-Heh written before the nun-vav was sanctified, since Alef-Lamed-Heh is obviously a Name in its own right and is holy — why should it not also sanctify the letter attached to it, like all attached letters? Even though a skipped yud created a deficiency, the holiness of the Name that was written did not depart and remains in its place, drawing the attached letter — namely nun-vav — with it. And you need no further proof for this except from within the baraita itself: "Others" said the kaf-mem of Eloheikhem is not erased, while the Tanna Kamma holds it is erased — and every erasure is presumably of something written improperly as a Name, for otherwise how could one possibly say to erase even other letters of the Torah? Rather it is certainly something written improperly, and yet "others" hold it is not erased because the Name was already sanctified — which teaches that even when written by mistake the Name was sanctified; and the same applies to the mistake of a skipped yud of the Name, since in any event there is before us a holy Name — and a fortiori, in my humble opinion, what was written without error [namely Alef-Lamed-Heh] is not erased merely because the yud was skipped, since certainly the Name was sanctified. The matter is clear to me: wherever a holy Name is found, even on handles of utensils where we rule it must be stored away — if attached letters were written on it such as Eloheinu, the nun-vav is also not erased, just like the Name itself; for since it requires storage, certainly everything attached to what is pure is also pure. And do not raise a difficulty from what is written in seif 12: if he intended to write "Yehudah" but wrote the [Divine] Name — he erases the final heh, and we do not say the Name of Yud-Heh already sanctified it and therefore the dalet and heh he wrote are also holy as attached to the first Name — for that is no difficulty at all, for the concept of holiness through attachment applies only where there is some connection, namely where the attached part has some meaning, such as kaf-mem of Eloheikhem, which explains "the God of you [plural]," and this expression appears many times in the Torah — therefore if an error occurs with it anywhere, we say the Name was sanctified; not so with that dalet and heh he added to form "Yehudah" — there is no interpretation or connection to say these are attached to the Name Yud-Heh, for they have no meaning whatsoever, and everyone can see it is an error; and it is not for nothing that the baraita needed to specify "such as kaf-mem of Eloheikhem" and did not simply say "anything attached to the Name, whatever it may be" — but as I wrote: specifically in this kind, not in an error that has no meaning as a word in the Torah but is simply a mistake. And therefore: if one wrote the Name correctly and then erroneously wrote some other letter after it that has no interpretation or meaning — there is no holiness in it and by all opinions it is erased. So it seems to me correct and acceptable.
The third approach he mentioned — namely to remove the sheet entirely — is certainly the preferred approach, for one should not excise mentions [of the Name] from the Torah as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rosh and his son R' Yehudah; better to remove the sheet, etc. Nevertheless, since the scribes have already practiced scraping defective Names, there is a basis from Masechet Soferim chapter 5: "one who writes two holy Names — preserve the first and block the second"; some have the reading "excise [mechatev]" — and both mean language of excision. And so Beit Yosef cited this in the name of R' Yitzchak of Alexandria in the name of Rambam in a responsum; and in the aforementioned responsum [of Maharar Benjamin] he cited this — therefore if it is not easily possible to remove the sheet and write another, one may also do this. So it seems to me. And he further wrote in that responsum regarding a Torah scroll found with the verse "va-yitosh Eloah asahu" where "Eloah" is written deficient without vav — that no correction is needed, since it is sometimes written full and sometimes deficient, and even where it is written full it is not necessarily required to be so, etc. — end of quote. And this is puzzling: what does it matter that it is sometimes written full, since our books write it full with vav, and there is a specific concern here; and there is no fix by hanging the vav since it is part of the Name itself in this location; therefore one should also [excise] that Name and rewrite it. So it seems to me.
