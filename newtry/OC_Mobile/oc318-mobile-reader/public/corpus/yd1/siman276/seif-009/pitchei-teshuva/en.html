Forbidden to erase. See in Sefer Mishnat Chachamim beginning of negative commandment 3, who doubted one who needed to write a small Name and wrote a large Name, such as needed to write Y-H or El and wrote Y-H-V-H or Elohim; and similarly in adding and writing attached letters where not needed — if he can erase and make from large Name small Name; he extended on this and brought supports to permit, for even per poskim who forbid, they only forbade erasing and afterward repairing; but where erasure itself repairs, it is not called erasure at all, only repair; and even though here the Name was not invalid from the start, only written not in its place — permitted to erase; however if he wrote a complete superfluous Name in a Torah scroll — forbidden to erase; and some poskim forbade even to scrape, even though erasure itself repairs. One can distinguish between repairing the Torah scroll and repairing that Name (in my humble view the distinction is weak, for also writing a large Name is not called repairing the Name, only repairing the Torah scroll). And see further there, who wrote: even though the view of most poskim is that unstated is invalid in a Torah scroll, not like Tosafos Menachot 42b — nevertheless forbidden to erase it (implies even for repair forbidden); and even though Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semak: specifically if written intentionally; but unintentionally not, for unintentional is worse than unstated — unstated means he knows at least he writes letters of the Name, only it was not in his thought for the holiness of the Name; and unintentional means he does not know if he writes letters of the Name at all, and it is like one who acts unawares who caused unstated, and it is as if he wrote explicitly not for its sake — then it is not sacred and permitted to erase for need — see there. And see in responsum Noda BiYehudah tinyana Orach Chayim siman 17, who wrote regarding a synagogue in a village where one room was designated and prayers and supplications from names that are not erased were written on the walls; and nevertheless to smoke inside is forbidden, for it blackens the walls and causes erasure of the Name — see there. And see in responsum Ma'al Tzedakah siman 23, who brought in the name of Or Zarua, author of Panim Me'irot, who wrote in a case where they wanted to paint in a synagogue and on the walls was old writing of names that are not erased — there is no prohibition in this; and he brought proof from Sotah, that Israel wrote the Torah in seventy languages and coated with plaster — how did they pass erasing the Name? And he answered there is no prohibition, since after scraping the plaster the writing remains visible — see there; and the Gaon author of Ma'al Tzedakah there siman 24 rejected his proof and suggested forbidden — see there; and see from this in Panim Me'irot itself part 1 siman 45 in the middle of a responsum at length. And see further in Noda BiYehudah there, who wondered why Tur and Shulchan Aruch omitted the braita's law in Shabbat 120 regarding one on whose flesh the Name was written; and he settled with difficulty, because below siman 282 seif 10 it is explained forbidden to cause erasure of the Name; therefore pottery vessel is needed; and it is from the sugya of Megillah, and contradicts the sugya of Shabbat — see there; and as Shach end s.k. 19.
