It is forbidden to erase, etc. In a responsum I discussed these laws at length and concluded as follows. A word that is mundane in meaning, although written with the same letters as a sacred Name, such as the word meaning 'gods' in the verse 'You shall be like gods,' may be erased. A sacred Name that was not written with intent for its sanctity may be erased only when necessary for a repair. Cutting it out and placing it in genizah, however, is permitted even when not required for a repair. If it was written with intent for its sanctity, even cutting it out and placing it in genizah for purposes of repair is forbidden. If many Divine Names in a Torah scroll were written without the proper intent, they may not be erased even for purposes of repair, because the scroll would appear mottled. See there.
It is necessary, etc. From here it appears to me that those scribes who are meticulous not to write a Divine Name at the end of a line, and therefore sometimes improperly shorten or lengthen letters, are acting incorrectly. Likewise, when a Divine Name would occur at the end of a sheet, they make marks or long dots at the end of the sheet so that the Name will not appear there. This too is mistaken. Here it is explicitly permitted to write a Divine Name at the end of a sheet, provided that none of its letters extends beyond the line. The Hagahot Maimoniyot, cited by the Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe, also state, 'He should not write the Name at the end of the sheet,' which implies that writing it at the end of an ordinary line is permitted. Accordingly, the practice of improperly lengthening or shortening letters is contrary to law. The marks they place at the end of the sheet are also forbidden, since one may not add even a single dot to the Torah. Their custom in this matter should therefore be abolished.
