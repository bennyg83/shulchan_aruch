(Siman 276, Taz note 1.) All his words are incorrect; I answer first to first and last to last on what he wrote explaining Rosh and Tur — Bach already preceded. What he brings from Mordechai — view of R' Yaakov of Orleans and Rav R' Elchanan and in name of Rashi that mouth formation is required specifically — is imprecise; he erred in Mordechai's words, thinking when Mordechai wrote "and R' Elchanan further answered," and Rashi at beginning of Mechilta explained similarly, he meant R' Elchanan and Rashi hold like R' Yaakov of Orleans above — that is not so, for those are Mordechai's continuation: R' Yaakov of Orleans says the tanna who taught a sefer Torah written by an idolater is invalid does not require writing for its sake, for otherwise why need a verse to exclude idolater — derive it requires writing for its sake since unspecified is not for its sake, from what we say in chapter HaNizakin "the mentions in it I did not write for their sake" and he did not say "I wrote not for their sake"; R' Elchanan answered a sefer Torah is presumed for its sake and need not say he writes for its sake, for presumably so; and that in HaNizakin is as if he said "I wrote not for their sake" — that is it; then Mordechai cites R' Elchanan: if not that sefer Torah is presumed for its sake, he would need explicit utterance that he writes for its sake like regarding a get; and Rashi at beginning of Mechilta on "not for its sake" explains similarly. If so, R' Elchanan explicitly holds no need to say he writes for its sake, for unspecified refers to this; and "and likewise Rashi" means: that which we say if not presumed for its sake he would need explicit statement like a get — so Rashi explains at beginning of Mechilta, meaning beginning of tractate Gittin; and because it deals with chapter HaNizakin he called beginning of Gittin "beginning of Mechilta," meaning what Rashi said there daf 2a s.v. lefi she'ein baki'in leshmah, etc. — clear in his words there that since a get is not presumed for its sake, he must say explicitly. Possibly also Rashi holds in a sefer Torah no need for explicit statement since presumed for its sake. (Possibly also R' Yaakov of Orleans holds thus, and he only spoke for the sugya in HaNizakin; but for the tanna of sefer Torah written by idolater — plainly no writing for its sake required; his wording does not imply so, for he said this tanna holds sefer Torah does not require writing for its sake — implies this tanna holds thus, and we do not hold thus; and Beit Yosef's view appears so — he wrote Mordechai in name of R' Yaakov of Orleans and did not write Rashi and R' Elchanan, for certainly as I said.) His proof from HaNizakin that he said "a sefer Torah that I wrote for so-and-so, the mentions in it" — from his wording it seems this proof is from Rabbenu Shimshon cited in Mordechai in his name; and from what he says afterward "and he further brings a proof there" — possibly the first proof is his own; in any case not a proof at all, for he did not see Mordechai explains there: Rabbenu Shimshon holds a sefer Torah exists for its sake, but mentions certainly require remembering — meaning for their sake; therefore HaNizakin cites "the mentions in it I did not write for their sake" and not the rest of the sefer. Per the other explanation (R' Elchanan and R' Yaakov of Orleans above) one could say he cited mentions because he does not make himself wicked and for mentions he can say he forgot to write them for their sake — end of his words; behold explicitly the opposite of what Taz said "he does not make himself wicked" — one could say sefer Torah and mentions are equal: for R' Yaakov of Orleans both require explicit statement; for R' Elchanan neither requires explicit statement; therefore for sefer Torah he cannot say he forgot, for how forget writing whole sefer; and "for mentions he can say he forgot" — not per R' Elchanan but per R' Yaakov who holds sefer Torah also needs explicit statement; and so for R' Elchanan one can answer for mentions he can say he forgot to think. Forced to say "and per the other explanation" refers even to R' Yaakov of Orleans, not only R' Elchanan whom he relied on; moreover R' Elchanan cited "the mentions in it I did not write for their sake" — how not notice why mentions specifically? Rather as explained. "I wonder on holy mouth," etc. — I say certainly holy mouth said so, for not a proof at all — always say thought alone suffices; and there invalid because even thought was not present, for he intended to write Yehuda; and Maharshal likewise Beit Yosef briefly. Rather he preferred to say he intended a word not in the Torah there; if so plainly even no thought; and "this is not," etc. — only teaches even intending Yehuda, a great error, passing quill helps; forced — dispute of R' Yehuda and Rabbanan teaches Rabbanan disagree and hold even passing quill does not help, and we hold so as all poskim imply; if so, rather should teach even in such case Rabbanan invalidate. However one can say here power of leniency preferred to teach; and "nevertheless for practical law he directed well," etc. — already explained Mordechai does not rule so in name of many geonim; Bach's words stand properly. He also omitted Rama Orach Chaim siman 32 seif 9 who validates b'dieved when he thought and did not say explicitly — and that is the essential view.
