A Jewish king. For a gentile king, however, he must interrupt, as explained regarding prayer in Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 104.
He should not answer him. If he did answer, the scroll is not thereby invalid. This is implied by the Beit Yosef. The statement of the Hagahot Maimoniyot and Masechet Soferim that one who interrupted in the middle of a 'name' renders it invalid refers to dividing a single word into what appears to be two words, such as writing 'Reu ben.' The Mordechai explains it this way, as does Sefer HaTerumah, siman 205. This invalidity was already stated above in siman 274, seif 4: if the letters are separated so widely that the word appears as two words, it is invalid. The term 'name' there does not mean a sacred Divine Name. Rather, every word is called a 'name,' as in Masechet Soferim and in many passages of chapter HaBoneh, where the term 'name' is used for a word. This is explicit in the Mordechai's explanation, cited below at the end of siman 279. This is unlike the Bach, who understood it as referring to a sacred Name.
