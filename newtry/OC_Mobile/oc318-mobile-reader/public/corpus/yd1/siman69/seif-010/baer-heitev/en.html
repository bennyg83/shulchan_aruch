And knows. Shach wrote: Bach wondered on this — how is mesilat applicable when he knows that forbidden and permitted depend on this, as below siman 98. And one may answer: he does not hold like siman 98, for there we give him to taste whether there is prohibition in it or not; therefore when he knows that forbidden and permitted depend on this, certainly there is no mesilat here, unlike here where one may say that even though he knows Israelite custom, nevertheless we find it, for example he spoke offhand before others who did not ask him if he rinsed, and mesilat that he rinsed — apply yourself, for we hold in Even HaEzer siman 17 that an apostate for idolatry is believed for testimony of a woman in mesilat, and we do not say since he knows that forbidden and permitted depend on this his presumption is lying, rather certainly we find it in such a case. And Taz wrote: since the reason the gentile is believed here in mesilat is because it is only a rabbinic prohibition in blood that was salted — therefore I ruled in a woman who cooked meat and forgot whether she salted it first or not, and we permitted based on this that it is only a rabbinic safek lekula; and the reason we do not say establish it on its presumption that initially it was not salted is because there is a majority against the presumption, for a majority of times they salt first and majority is preferable to presumption.
