Or in m'slaket. Beit Yosef challenged: behold m'slaket is not believed except for testimony of a woman; and he answered: in rabbinic matters he is believed everywhere, as Maharai wrote in Torat HaBayit siman 79; and this meat, since salting is like hot, is blood that was cooked and one does not transgress on it; and Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe on this he did not need 'salting like hot,' for in any case it is only a rabbinic prohibition, for behold it is cooked before us; and why if a gentile plucked a chicken and we do not know if in kli rishon or kli sheni he is believed — for even if he did it in kli rishon there is no d'oraisa prohibition, therefore he is believed, end of his words; and Rashal also wrote on Beit Yosef he did not need to give the reason of cooking, for from melicha too d'oraisa prohibition applies; and an incident came to us: a woman cooked meat and forgot whether she salted it first or not, and we permitted from this, for it is only a rabbinic doubt to be lenient; and one may answer we establish it on its presumption that initially it was not salted — if so, why here do we permit from m'slaket because it is rabbinic and we do not say establish on presumption it was not rinsed initially? And I further found in an old collection: in doubt whether he rinsed the final rinsing, I found written it is kosher, since melicha is d'rabbanan — rabbinic doubt to be lenient, as in Hagahot Shach Devarim chapter 3 s.v. incident where two fruits were salted, end of his words; and although Beit Yosef siman 87 wrote in the name of Rashi and Rambam that they hold even in cooking there is d'oraisa prohibition — nevertheless here all later authorities were not concerned, since it is Talmud; and further one may give reason to permit: certainly the woman acted as usual and salted first, like the case of Kriat Shema: he erred in 'uvchartem' and began 'lemaan yirbu,' etc. — need not return) he kept his habit and comes — so it appears to me; and this) that we do not follow presumption here: there is majority against presumption, for majority of times they salt first, and majority is preferable to presumption, as Rosh beginning of Ketubot explained on the plain sense of 'an open opening I found':
