Or in m'slaket that he rinsed well. In Beit Yosef he wrote: even when the gentile knows Israel's custom he is believed in m'slaket — and Perishah and Bach wondered at this, how can m'slaket apply when he knows prohibition and permission depend on this; and Bach added to bring proof from below siman 98 — and one may distinguish: certainly it is not comparable to siman 98, for there we give him to taste whether there is prohibition in it or not, and otherwise the matter cannot stand; therefore when he knows prohibition and permission depend on this, certainly there is no m'slaket — unlike here, where one may say that even though he knows Israel's custom, nevertheless we find such as when he spoke offhand to others who did not ask him if he rinsed, and m'slaket that he rinsed — apply yourself, for we hold a mumar to idolatry is believed for testimony of a woman in m'slaket, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 17, and we do not say since he knows prohibition and permission depend on this his presumption is lying — rather certainly we find such in this case — and this is clear:
An idolater serves, etc. It appears to me therefore the Mechaber omitted the law of m'salket mentioned in Tur and poskim, for he follows his reasoning that he wrote in Beit Yosef: for Semag and Rivash m'salket does not help; only trembling helps for Semag, see there; and although Rivash argues that trembling does not help, nevertheless since Semak also holds trembling helps, he ruled m'salket does not help and trembling does (and it is written in Sefer Api Ravrevi daf 46a: it appears trembling applies to a gentile servant and maidservant). Or: because we hold an idolater is not believed in m'salket except for testimony of a woman alone; and Beit Yosef answered: this is specifically for a d'oraisa prohibition, unlike here where blood that was cooked (and likewise blood that was salted) is only d'rabbanan — and below s.s. 137 regarding whether an idolater is believed in m'salket for kashering forbidden vessels, which is d'rabbanan, he brought the poskim's dispute and ruled in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch there he is not believed — therefore he omitted m'salket here, as I wrote. But the Rav, who wrote 'and in one of its aspects it suffices,' etc., and did not write in the language of 'some say,' appears the Mechaber also holds one may say even the Mechaber holds like Semak that in one aspect it suffices, only he mentioned trembling and the same for the other; and so appears his view in Torat Chatat end of general rule 1 in what he wrote there in the name of Beit Yosef — and for his words one must say he does not disagree with siman 137, for here since there is also the reason the idolater is particular, he is more lenient (and see siman 122 seif 11); and correction of the Rav's wording who wrote 'and in one of its aspects,' etc., appears because the Mechaber mentioned m'salket — it appears because of blood the Mechaber wrote 'we rely on his words' — that we do not rely on his words at all, for even if he says nothing we say he rinsed from trembling — and as explained in Beit Yosef, and it is simple; therefore the Rav explained he states two matters: we rely on his words — meaning where we need to rely on his words specifically, namely m'salket, or if there was a Jew entering and exiting, etc. (and see siman 68 seif 12 and what is written there and siman 118 s.k. 32); nevertheless what I wrote is primary in the Mechaber's view, and the wording 'we rely on his words' is not precise — and we find such in many places, as siman 2 s.k. 13, see there:
