(There seif 16) And if he salted in an unperforated vessel, etc. And in the hagahah: and if it is a moist thing we require sixty against the peel from the bowl. Shach concludes that even if the bowl is also hot, sixty against its peel suffices, for the unperforated vessel was not forbidden entirely, only a peel's depth. And as the hagahah wrote siman 98 seif 4 — see there. And in my humble opinion, for earthenware one must estimate against the whole if it is hot, ta'tei gavar — see siman 91 and siman 105, for earthenware absorbs much as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniot chapter 6 regarding unperforated vessel — permitted in other vessels and forbidden in earthenware, and so the Acharonim as Mei Yisrael wrote general rule 11 s.k. 5 (see there). And so it is clear explicitly in Darkei Moshe siman 98, who wrote in the name of Ohr HaChaim in an incident where an unperforated earthenware vessel fell into a pot and he ruled to estimate against the whole and not suffice with a peel alone — see there. And all the more in earthenware that is not unperforated. And in my humble opinion what Rashba wrote in the name of Raavad and Beit Yosef brings siman 98, that in an unperforated vessel one must estimate against the whole — deals with earthenware. For it is impossible to say it deals with other vessels, for Rashba himself wrote in the name of Raavad and Beit Yosef brings siman 98: that which we say in the Gemara Perek Gid HaNashe (daf 97) regarding chelev in its entirety we estimate — if by what exits from it in a vessel we know how much exited, this is specifically in chelev or earthenware where one cannot separate the issur from it; but not in metal vessels and the like where one can separate the issur from it, we estimate well how much exited from it. And from this those who disagree with him hold that even in earthenware it does not absorb more than a peel's depth. And so it is clear in Rambam chapter 6 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, who wrote: a bowl in which one salted meat, even if it was a thick limb, is forbidden to eat hot in it forever, for blood was already absorbed in the pottery — see there. Behold he wrote blood was absorbed in the pottery, implying in the whole; otherwise behold the limb itself is only a small amount like a peel's depth, and examine. And since the view of Rambam and Raavad and Ohr HaChaim and Rashal is to forbid the whole earthenware vessel, one may not be lenient. And not like Chiddushei HaRamban siman 70. However, in Mei Yisrael general rule 80 s.k. 11 he decided to be lenient. And in responsum Mekom Shemuel he agreed — see there. And so Ohr HaChaim wrote general rule 24 din 9. And see what I wrote in our words siman 98 s.k. 5, that therefore the hagahah there wrote that through heated salting it is not absorbed in the vessel only a peel's depth; and if it was cooked in it, one need not estimate, only against the peel — meaning specifically the first time; but since it was cooked in it once without sixty, presumably what was in a peel's depth went and was absorbed in the whole and the permitted absorbed in it became nevela, as stated there seif 5 and siman 92 regarding a drop that fell, etc. — see there. And siman 99 seif 2. And if so, here too if the bowl was heated, one must estimate afterward against the whole unless in a place of great loss. And requires further study on Shach's words, for it seems the bowl was heated empty or without sixty, for he wrote presumably there is no sixty; if so, one must estimate the whole. And it is possible it deals where they did not use it for anything beforehand, and so in Pri Megadim siman 98 he left in requires further study. And see in Mei Yisrael, who wrote that bli o bli does not make bli nevela.
