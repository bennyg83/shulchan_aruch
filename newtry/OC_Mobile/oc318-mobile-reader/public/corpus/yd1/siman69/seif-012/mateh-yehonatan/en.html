(there seif 12) He shall not cook it, etc. And if they cooked, permitted. Shach s.k. 51, and he disagrees with liver in siman 73 where melicha does not help because of abundance of blood and presumably all was expelled in roasting and what did not expel in roasting will not expel in cooking; but here melicha does not help because blood already dried inside; according to his words if liver remained three days without melicha, also forbidden to cook after roasting. But Tosafot explained the reason permitting cooking liver after roasting: liver is all blood and the Torah permitted it, forbidden only rabbinically; and since it became roasted, even if blood remains, permitted; if so according to this reason even if it remained three days without melicha, also permitted to cook after roasting.
(there seif 14) Meat that remained, etc. And the same law if cooked without melicha. Shach s.k. 59 requires further study, for below we hold, etc. — see his words at length. One may say why we say nullified in majority: since we can rely on tasting whether blood taste or not, wherever we can rely on tasting, not nullified in majority; therefore must be that they already tasted and said no taste, or that at cooking time there was sixty against the unsalted piece and Rama goes according to his view that piece forbidden even with sixty; afterward that piece mixed with another and he says nullified, for no taste at all since there is sixty; if so also Shach who does not hold if they cook will impart taste — we see no taste at all, all taste left; only that piece forbidden for blood spreading place to place, and for that there is no concern if she cooks will impart taste.
