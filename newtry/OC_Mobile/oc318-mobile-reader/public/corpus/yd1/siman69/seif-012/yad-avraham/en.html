(There in hagahah gimel) But it is permitted to return and salt meat in it. Or even without perforation if the meat already remained in its salting period and was rinsed — the reason is there is no melicha for vessels, for a vessel does not expel through salt. And what is written here at length is a scribal error, for for the length we do not say there is no melicha for vessels without perforation, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 105 — see there. And one must say Hagahot Sh"D [and requires further study in Shach s.k. 83; and one may say that in other issurim it is different as Taz wrote siman 10]. And Shach wrote that even in earthenware we say thus, that there is no melicha for vessels. And to me requires further study, for it is possible that specifically in a perforated vessel there is no difference between earthenware and other vessels; but without perforation it seems this is in other vessels but not in earthenware, for Tosafot and Rosh chapter Keitzad Tzolin (daf 113) and Mordechai chapter Kol HaShabbat and Hagahot Sh"D wrote there is no melicha for wood. In truth Mordechai in chapter Kol HaBasar wrote in the name of Rabbenu Ephraim that there is no melicha for wood, metal, earthenware, and stone. And so in Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brings siman 70 and siman 105 and siman 137. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 4 regarding absorbed vessels of yayin nesech that one may return and salt meat in them — see there, and requires further study. However, even if we say thus, in earthenware as well there is no melicha for vessels; nevertheless it is no worse than cold, for we say in chapter Kol HaShabbat (daf 30): all vessels in which chametz was used cold, one may use for matzah cold. And many commentators explained: specifically chametz was used cold, but if chametz was used hot, forbidden to use for matzah even cold. And this applies to earthenware vessels. And there are two reasons: because earthenware vessels absorb much and expel even when cold, and therefore every cold use requires rinsing. And as Rashba wrote in the name of Rif and Ramban and so the Rav the Maggid chapter 5 of Hilchot Chametz, and so Rosh chapter Keitzad Tzolin, and so in my humble opinion the view of all of them, and Raavad, and Darkei Moshe brings siman 121, who wrote: earthenware bowls in which issur was used hot are forbidden to use even cold for moist things, because it is impossible to rinse them. And see in our words siman 91. And therefore, since it expels its expulsion into the salted meat, and melicha is like rotev for it, the meat is forbidden and rinsing does not help, as stated siman 10 regarding a knife — there one needs a peel. And in my humble opinion what the hagahah wrote, that it is permitted to salt in it meat already salted and rinsed, because there is no melicha for vessels — deals with other vessels and not earthenware. For who will be lenient against Rif and Ramban and Rashba and Hagahot Maimoniot and Rosh and all of them and Raavad, that even b'dieved one should forbid a peel's depth. And there is another reason: since it has no repair through hagalah, we are concerned lest he use it hot. And even for passing use it is forbidden. And so Mei Yisrael wrote general rule 11 s.k. 6 — see there, and my intention is according to his view, with God's help. And see there s.k. 11. And what Shach and Pri Chadash wrote, that presumably there is no sixty in the bowl against the peel — so in my humble opinion it is clear in the Gemara chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 111), that a bowl in which one salted meat is forbidden to eat hot in it. And he concludes that it is impossible to taste — Rashi: because it is forbidden, therefore it has no heter. Rif to Rava: and let him taste with an Aramaean kafila — did we not learn, etc.? He said: when I speak of forbidden, where there is no kafila. And Rif challenged well: why did he decide to say there is no heter for this hot when there is no kafila — behold with kafila one could permit? And from this we learn that by default there is no sixty against what was forbidden from the bowl, namely a peel's depth. For by law of the Gemara certainly melicha does not forbid more, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 105 and Shach. And earthenware is worse. And the law of kafila tasting — see siman 98.
