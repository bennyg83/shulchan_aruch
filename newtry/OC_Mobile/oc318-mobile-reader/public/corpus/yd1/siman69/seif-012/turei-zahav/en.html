And after they roasted it, he should not cook it. For perhaps not all its blood exited through roasting, and afterward through cooking it will exit:
And if they cooked it — permitted. Rashal ruled even b'dieved forbidden; and Maharach wrote his words do not appear correct. Rashal wrote chapter 25 siman 98 on meat that remained three days without melicha and afterward they salted it in a perforated vessel for the measure of melicha and afterward rinsed and salted again and placed it in an unperforated vessel and did not remain the measure of pickling, and they found gravy there — at first glance one should be concerned perhaps the first melicha had no power to expel all blood inside, since it hardened, but always expels little by little, therefore forbidden; and he answered it is not so, rather in any case permitted: if it did not exit in the first melicha, it will not exit in the second; and if it exited in the first melicha, everything exited. And one may distinguish why they said regarding something requiring libun and hag'alah it is forbidden to cut hot in it according to Rosh, because through hag'alah it expels little by little — there there is more expulsion, the hotter the more — therefore one should be concerned perhaps through first hag'alah that weakened its power it does not exit completely and additional heat is added after cutting hot in it — unlike melicha, etc. — therefore Rashal's explanation appears: in this concern that blood dried in the meat and is not aroused by melicha's heat to exit, we say properly in any case: if it was aroused to exit in the first melicha, as Rivash's view Beit Yosef brings, automatically one need not melicha a second time at all, and permitted in second melicha in unperforated vessel; and if it did not exit in first melicha and the drying remains in place, second melicha will not forbid it and permitted for roasting — unlike hag'alah, where there is no drying reason, only hag'alah lacks power to expel completely since libun is needed — nevertheless hag'alah's power moves what was absorbed somewhat, and when second boiling comes it exits outside — so Rashal's reason, and it is correct; and it appears if they salted only in unperforated vessel and did not first salt in perforated vessel, forbidden, lest some hardened blood exit through melicha. And so appears if he salted that three-day meat with other meat salted to expel its blood — that three-day meat is forbidden, lest it absorb from blood exiting from other meat; for although they said melicha does not help it, that is because its blood hardened inside — nevertheless the meat is not so hardened to say it will not absorb again; and regarding blood inside, Rivash Beit Yosef brings: there is no clarification that it did not exit through melicha, and it was fit to permit, only one should be concerned for Geonim's words where they practiced — end of his words; and therefore certainly we do not rely on anything to be lenient on this, and to say it will not absorb other blood, rather stringently we say lest its blood not exit through melicha since it hardened — end of his words; and to say that as it expels its blood it will also expel what it absorbs from other meat — one teacher permitted from as it absorbs so it expels, and it appears he erred, for certainly this is like salting meat already salted whose gravy and blood exited with other meat salted to expel its blood — we say throughout this siman it certainly absorbs from the prohibition and remains inside, since it has nothing to expel — so we say stringently; and further it appears to me: if he salted the three-day meat with other meat already salted and rinsed in unperforated vessel, other meat is also forbidden — here we say the opposite stringently, lest hardened blood exit through melicha and forbid other meat, since placed in unperforated vessel — all this clear according to my view.
