And even if they soaked it. Be'er Heitev; and in responsum Maaseh Moshe siman 80, meat soaked in seawater in a ship in an unperforated vessel — since the water was salty and unfit to drink because of their saltiness, it is forbidden; and Machatzit HaShekel disagreed and wrote several reasons to permit, that water has no power even though salty to expel like salt itself; and even if it is double safek d'rabbanan lekula, for blood that was salted is d'rabbanan; also sea water unfit for drinking is not because of saltiness but because they are bitter. And see Radbaz's responsum regarding netilat yadayim, that sea water when heated on fire diminishes in bitterness and becomes fit to drink, etc. — see there, and it seems he deals where it was already given initial rinse in other water, only he is concerned lest it is like unsalted in unperforated vessel; and in his view to permit for reasons he wrote there. However, if not yet rinsed, there is concern because of visible blood on it. And in my responsa Beit Aleph part 2, which is in manuscript with me, I wrote regarding spring water from salt wells that they draw and heat and make salt, which are very salty and impossible to drink because of saltiness, and residents there sometimes use such water instead of salt and took such water and soaked meat before salting instead of initial rinsing, and wrote there that although one may say such water is sharp like brine, nevertheless one may say they have no power to spread from place to place like other salty brine; and even if it soaked kevush period, one may say there is no power in the blood mixture to become entangled through the water's medium and return to be absorbed through salting and roasting; nevertheless concern for visible blood on it. And I ruled to permit without kevush period, and if it remained kevush period forbid without great loss, and in great loss permit for roasting as below siman 70 in the name of Noda BiYehudah regarding meat that fell into brine unsalted — see there.
However, in a place of great loss. And in Har HaKarmel siman 15 regarding great loss: there is no fixed amount, everything according to the eyes of the posek and according to time and circumstance and the person. And see in Sulam LaMinchah general rule 38, who brought in the name of Turei Daat siman 204 to distinguish between a barrel and a flask, brought below siman 126; and he also brought the words of Nishmat Shabbos siman 55 copied in Lechem HaPanim, who wrote that loss in principal more than a sixth is great loss even for a rich man. And in Beit Hadassah he wrote that great loss is a gold and a half polish, close to this measure as I heard in the name of the Maggid Rabbi Shmuel, may his memory be blessed; and the essence is there is no measure to the matter, only everything according to the posek's mind and according to time and place — the rich according to his wealth, etc., as written in Yoreh Deah siman 248 regarding measure of charity taken from women; and also according to the matter of the ruling whether there are many sides to leniency or not — all one matter, one who increases and one who decreases, provided he directs his heart — see there. And see in my Beit Aleph on treifot siman 39 what I wrote on this matter.
One may rely b'dieved. Be'er Heitev; and what he wrote "and it was cooked" — because otherwise he salts again and in any case, nevertheless one may not salt it with other meat, as Shiyurei Berachah wrote and in Degel Machaneh Ephraim, that even if not cooked it is considered b'dieved since it was already rinsed, he must salt again and there is loss; therefore he wrote that even Shach is stringent only if it remained the mil period, and b'dieved if it remained more than half an hour one may be lenient if it was already rinsed that he need not return to salt it.
Even in kli sheni. Be'er Heitev; and see in Teshuvat Chen siman 11 regarding beef placed in kli rishon; and what Be'er Heitev wrote about Taz's incident — see in Mei Yisrael general rule 1 s.k. 16 and in Sulam LaMinchah and in Shiyurei Berachah part 2 siman 69, that his view is like Knesset HaGedolah; and so Bahag; and so appears the view of Sefer HaMelamed end of laws of mikvaot, who wrote this law is in dispute — Tosafot Pesachim daf 9 s.v. shetehei, etc. — see there; and so the view of Rabbi Yitzchak in his book Or Yekarot; and Machatzit HaShekel wrote that in great loss as Taz and Pri Chadash and Peleti one may rely; and so appears Pri Megadim's view; and in Pri Megadim part 1 siman 11 he wrote: a woman sent meat from her city to her husband in another village for Shabbat and did not write in the letter that the meat was salted, and they cooked the meat thus and again a safek arose whether it was salted or not, and some want to compare to what Taz wrote s.k. 24 regarding a woman who forgot, etc., and he rejected this and ruled one may not permit unless with compelling indications that she certainly salted, such as the messenger could only come close to Shabbat so there was no time to salt and cook on Shabbat; however in Shiyurei Berachah part 2 siman 69 he wrote regarding one sent meat on erev Shabbat and stored it in an oven and again remembered perhaps it was not salted, and wrote several reasons to forbid until he asks and clarifies it was salted, because the way is to send before salting so it looks nicer; similar to what we say at the beginning of Ketubot; but putting water on meat does not sell well, also no reason to think it is something that can stand through licking the meat; and although Rashal forbids licking, this is not the law everywhere; and also possible through kafila tasting or rinsing a little visible blood, etc.; and apart from law, how permit something clarifiable after Shabbat, etc.; and not like Pri Megadim, who sided to be lenient that the messenger, etc., is not compelling indication; it is not compelling, for if her intent was to salt, she would have time to salt and store near Shabbat or roast on a spit needing less waiting; and since it will become clear, do not be lenient even in double safek; and so it was in truth, after Shabbat it became clear it was not salted and the delay caused damage through him — and so it seems to me to rule — see there.

Be'er Heitev; and in Shabbetai Yaakov siman 27: meat that was soaked in water twelve hours and afterward soaked in other water another hour — it does not combine, for it is not called kevush except twenty-four consecutive hours — see there; and see in responsum Keter Kehunah siman 36, who extended on this and concludes also to permit, and also in concern of d'oraisa issur the lenient one in non-great-loss did not lose — see there.
