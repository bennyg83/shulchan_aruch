Permitted b'dieved, and likewise, etc. Meaning certainly for the reason that it should soften, slight rinsing does not help, for it does not soften thereby — and it is obvious also we do not apply sixty here; rather in b'dieved we rely that the reason is only because of blood on the surface, and therefore slight rinsing helps; and likewise if there is sixty against the blood — and so is explained in Torat Chatat general rule 5, see there; and it appears that since it was salted, even though it was not yet cooked, it is still considered b'dieved and he need not return to rinse and salt it — examine and see s.k. 5:
If there were sixty, etc. Torat Chatat's wording there din 3: and even though you have no piece that has sixty against the blood on it — that is in general; but if we know there is sixty in it, it certainly helps, end of his words:
Other pieces are permitted. For regarding the others it is considered expelled blood, and as it absorbs so it expels — there:
If he salted and did not rinse, etc. So Beit Yosef wrote: the Rosh chapter 25 — if he salted without rinsing, some say there is no remedy for that meat, etc., until for practical halacha if it is a place of great loss or it is erev Shabbat and impossible to prepare otherwise — fit are the Rosh and R' Meir to rely on them, end of his words; and it appears Beit Yosef's view is to rely on them in a place of great loss if he rinsed and salted again specifically; and so it appears from his words here in Shulchan Aruch. And if so, what the Rav wrote 'however in a place of great loss one may permit' also deals with such a case — and so is explained explicitly the view of Pri Chadash and Maharshal in Orchot Shabbat and chapter of the slaughterer siman 18: there is no remedy for that meat through rinsing and salting again even in a place of great loss — and this is not primary; and below siman 70 it is explained that wherever rinsing and salting again is permitted, all the more so for roasting — see there:
