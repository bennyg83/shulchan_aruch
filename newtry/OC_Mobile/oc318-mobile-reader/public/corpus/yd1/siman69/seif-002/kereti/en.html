In great loss — meaning if he returned and salted it, for then we say it expels what it absorbed; but if he cooked it thus without salting again, one may be stringent even in a place of loss. Peri Chadash.
Permitted b'dieved, for we rely on those sages who hold the reason for rinsing is to remove visible blood — and any rinsing also removes blood; unlike the reason that it must soften — what would any rinsing help. And likewise if he rinsed first and afterward cut into many pieces, any rinsing helps, for necessarily: if to soften — he already rinsed first as needed and softened; and because he cut afterward there is visible blood — with slight rinsing it suffices.
If there were sixty and the piece was thirty against the blood on it — if we do not know for certain there is sixty. Orchot Chaim.
In the piece, etc.: it appears that even if there is sixty, nevertheless the peeling of the piece is forbidden, for every concern of melicha prohibition was from salt absorbing visible blood on it; and the law is that salt does not absorb more than k'dei klipah — only we are stringent to require sixty, and that is to be stringent, not to be lenient by combining to nullify the forbidden peeling — and see siman 105.
Other pieces are permitted, for regarding others it is considered visible blood on a piece — expelled blood, and as it absorbs so it expels; or since, etc.; and he said: behold this piece that is forbidden becomes nevelah and forbids others — for the forbidden does not forbid, since visible blood absorption does not travel with other absorptions.
