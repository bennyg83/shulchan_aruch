And some forbid — Beit Yosef brought in the name of Mordechai: an incident before Rabbenu Yehuda who salted a sheep's head from outside and not inside, and he ruled not to salt it a second time and not to cook it, etc.; it appears simple this deals when he already cut the head in two and should have salted also from inside like every piece; but if the head was not split, it is permitted if it was salted outside alone like any thick piece that need not be split at the time of melicha — and as Beit Yosef wrote siman 71, one must pierce the bone because of the brain and its membrane, that there be place for blood in them to exit, as Rama wrote there:
If not for need. But in great loss it is permitted from one side; and it appears simple all the more if he salted from two sides; only the salt did not spread everywhere — permitted, for Orchot Chaim general rule 1 wrote in this to permit b'dieved, and in general rule 7 ruled to forbid without salting from two sides; and so in Rashal's words chapter 25 siman 83 it appears this is more lenient — therefore it appears without salting everywhere it is permitted b'dieved even without great loss:
Within twelve hours. For then the meat expels gravy and thereby does not absorb from the other side; but afterward one should be concerned it will re-absorb what the un-salted side expelled from the side not yet expelled; and although in siman 70 Rama wrote in great loss one may say it expels gravy up to a full day — nevertheless here great loss does not apply, since there is permission in roasting, as his view above; and Maharach wrote: this is specifically when he did not rinse the meat after salting one side; but if he rinsed, salting the other side no longer helps, lest it absorb what the already-salted side absorbed from the other side, and as it absorbs so it expels does not apply, since it has no gravy, for the expulsion holes closed, as I wrote siman 70 — end of his words; and I wrote in siman 70 that the law that rinsing closes the holes of gravy expulsion is not agreed upon, and here all agree one should not forbid because of this, since there is permission in any case in a place of loss. And I wondered at Rama in siman 75 seif 1 regarding chalchulah that has fat, forbidden if cooked without melicha even b'dieved if he salted only the inner side, and nevertheless whatever was not cooked has permission to return and salt the outer side even after twelve hours — the reason: it is not so presumed in blood; and here there is more reason that the unsalted side is not presumed in blood, since b'dieved it is permitted in a place of loss — and why did he rule here specifically within twelve hours?
