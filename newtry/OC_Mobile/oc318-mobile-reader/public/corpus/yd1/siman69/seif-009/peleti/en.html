Even if it was not placed, etc. — before we stood on the root of this law, we must precede the law that Taz and Pri Chadash disagreed, for we say in chapter Kirah: melicha needs cooking like ox meat, for even in kli rishon it is not cooked except on fire; and Rosh brought it and wrote: however, if it was salted, even salted anew, although the meat is not cooked, nevertheless the moisture and salt and blood inside are cooked and absorbed in the meat and forbidden — end of his words. And the authors disagreed on Rosh's intent, for Rama in Torat Chatat holds even if it was not salted at all, nevertheless forbidden, for moisture cooks and "salted" Rosh cited is not precise; and proof: Tur, Orach Chaim siman 318 wrote forbidden to place live beef in kli rishon on Shabbat, for moisture cooks, and did not write "salted" — learn "salted" is not precise; and so ruled Orach Chaim siman 318, "salted" is not precise. However, Maharshal and Taz in Orach Chaim siman 318 s.k. 23 hold he did not write "salted" for nothing, only Rosh deals with blood matter — if so what of it that blood cooks, the meat does not absorb from blood, for it must cook on fire and until then it does not absorb; therefore Rosh requires "salted," for then salt absorbs blood. But for Shabbat law it is obvious moisture and blood cook without salt, and Tur in Orach Chaim forbade for Shabbat, did not mention salt — end of his words. And Pri Chadash thought strained that Rosh's words revolve there on salting for blood — what does this matter here, only deals with Shabbat issur, only although l'chatchila forbidden because he cooks moisture, nevertheless one may question why forbid b'dieved the piece that was not cooked, and if moisture cooked, wipe the moisture — therefore he said salt hastens to absorb and impossible to wipe — end of his words. And his words are strained, for what did Rosh want here saying salt and blood cooked — he should have said simply moisture; and how does salt cook, it is not cooked in kli rishon; and far-fetched in reality to wipe all moisture from every fiber and piece of meat — behold much moisture; and if he comes to wipe he must crumble meat to fine pieces indivisible; therefore according to plain language of Rosh it seems like Taz and Ri'az agrees, who wrote on a piece placed in kli rishon: permitted except if there is salt on it, and so Rosh — namely as Taz wrote. And truthfully one may question on Rosh, since he holds even if moisture cooks, nevertheless meat does not absorb in kli rishon — if so what does "salted" help to absorb, and is not heat of kli rishon more absorbing than salt? And if we say with salt and kli rishon heat they cause absorption — therefore Rosh needed to give reason moisture cooks, only briefly, even kli rishon does not cook meat but with salt combination it cooks — and one may resolve with strain. And after we explained these many views, it seems certain one who relies on Taz and Maharshal — they do not rebuke him with contempt, as I ruled some years ago when they sought to remove a posek's beard because he ruled beef placed in kli rishon hot water is not forbidden, and I wrote he has whom to rely on from great ones of the world. However, l'chatchila for law, requires further study to be lenient against Rama's ruling, for many reasons; and even if we say Rosh's intent as Taz, that although blood cooks, nevertheless not absorbed in meat, for kli rishon does not cook beef — Rosh followed regarding pouring after R' Tam's view in chapter Kirah regarding tray and pot, etc., who holds since he poured, absorbs a peel's depth, even cooking a little is one law — he has this view, and see Ran and Rashba in their novellae and Taharat HaAdam and Tosafot Zevachim; and if so Rosh inferred well: if kli rishon does not cook beef meat, also does not absorb from them; and therefore Rosh requires specifically the piece was salted. But according to Rashbam and many poskim, fire absorbs more than cooking, and pouring proves — if so what of it that beef is not cooked in kli rishon, blood is absorbed in kli rishon, fire absorbs — obvious; and so plain teachings of matters, absorption is not more than cooking, for certainly heat of salting is not more than place of kli rishon, as appears everywhere; if so if kli rishon does not help on beef, also heat of salting does not help on beef — therefore Rama's case that he salted slaughtered meat with nevelah meat, all should be in other meat; and so these atmata that he salted in gid hanasheh, etc. — this matter is hard to say; and therefore it is clear there is a difference between cooking and absorption, and heat of salting absorbs. Also I did not know whence Rosh said salt assists kli rishon — he found no support from Gemara and mentioned it; and even though moisture cooks, my heart was troubled whence Rosh innovated a law not found in Shas — these matters contradict nature — who can enter their secret, until I found proof from Gemara Zevachim: we say one who cooked in it or poured boiling into it — it breaks; and see Tosafot and Yerushalmi who learned from it pouring cooks, therefore breaking required as written "in which it shall be cooked," etc.; and Tosafot challenged: fine if he poured into the vessel — if the sin-offering was a calf and ox and he poured into the vessel, why break, behold it is better than full kli rishon, it does not cook; and so too pouring — perforce one must say moisture cooks, and so in pouring moisture cooks, and Scripture says "shall be cooked in it" and requires breaking; and why there mention salted, who mentioned salting — besides impossible to say salted there, besides whence for Rabbenu Tam and supporters to learn from there pouring cooks, perhaps there salting assists, and even in kli sheni some say it assists, all the more in pouring. But to innovate that salting assists kli rishon heat — I did not know whence; therefore more plausible like Rama's view, moisture cooks and is absorbed in meat, especially if not absorbed without salting — if so beef in milk and kli rishon, meat is not cooked and also its moisture is not absorbed — behold d'oraisa the meat is permitted, only d'rabbanan; and so here. If so, why do we challenge on Rava who said an olive's bulk of meat fell into a boiling pot, the meat is forbidden and the milk permitted — why is the milk nevelah, and we establish it with change — why not answer beef that is not forbidden only d'rabbanan and forbidden from absorbing milk, but milk is not forbidden from meat, for meat is permitted, not cooked at all, and in Bava Kama way it is forbidden; and answer: nevertheless d'rabbanan forbidden, behold in d'rabbanan we do not say issur chal al issur; and so one must say in what we answer "the Torah forbade the kid and not the milk" — we find nowhere milk from Bava Kama is permitted; and therefore the matter is forced, moisture cooks and is absorbed inside meat like Rama; and truthfully the Mechaber in siman 318 holds even moisture is not cooked without salt, and it does not appear so from Rambam who copied "melicha needs cooking" and omitted that ox meat needs cooking on fire and not kli rishon helps — why he omitted requires reason; but for Rama's view it is fine, for in any case moisture cooked and forbidden, and no difference for law, therefore they did not mention — and this is proof for Rama. And behold Ran in chapter HaRoeh wrote on that which we do not salt kids and lambs, challenged: if they are ox meat and need cooking, kli rishon heat does not cook them — obviously permitted to salt, unlike kids and lambs; and if we say milk nevertheless absorbs — Ran should not have answered that it expelled its blood, for for this kli rishon does not help; and one may say there is a difference between calf and ox, for the Gemara says ox meat — it cannot be we give a boundary how many days until ox status; who decides and judges this, and they said an ox one-day-old is called ox; therefore clear he holds moisture even without salt cooks, and Ran challenged well — examine. And in truth, although Rosh was lenient, we have to be stringent, for Rosh as appears from his words first chapter Chullin in the matter of slaughtering on Shabbat holds blood that spread place to place is permitted (and what he wrote in chapter Keitzad Tzolin regarding unperforated vessel that blood that spread place to place is forbidden is only Rif's language) — if so one may say even though kli rishon heat does not help on ox meat, nevertheless its heat is so great that at least it causes movement in blood to absorb to exit; and if so, although heat is not so strong as on fire to expel absorption outside, nevertheless blood spread to exit — and Rosh did not concern himself, for he holds permitted; but according to what we hold regarding unperforated vessel that the whole piece is forbidden because blood spread place to place, this too is similar for all — examine.
All the more in kli sheni it cooks — and Maharshal disagrees, for kli sheni does not cook; and Shach in s.k. 37 seems to agree with Maharshal and to rely on Rosh and poskim who wrote kli sheni cooks with salt combination — it is not so precise, and one may reject in several aspects, especially that which they wrote kli sheni cooks means other than beef, but for beef they agree even kli rishon does not cook, and Rosh deals with kli sheni and needs salt assistance; but if this is difficult, this is difficult — we teach: pot, etc., one may not pour boiling on them, but one may pour into the bowl and ladle; R' Yehudah says: for everything he puts except something with vinegar and brine; and they should have asked: does R' Yehudah argue on the first part leniently that even kli sheni requires vinegar and brine, or on the latter stringently that even kli sheni cooks with vinegar and brine leniently; and the sugya is established from R' Yehudah's baraita on the first part, that he holds kli sheni does not cook except with vinegar and brine — implying the first tanna and R' Yehudah who argue hold even with vinegar and brine and salting, kli sheni does not cook, only kli rishon; and we do not find dispute of first tanna and R' Yehudah on the latter part; and it is difficult for Ohr HaChaim and Rama: and one may say and distinguish between brine and brine — see Yerushalmi Shabbat chapter Kirah, that according to R' Yehudah salt is like wine brine like vinegar, and distinguish cooking from absorption — difficult, for Ohr HaChaim and Rama seized everything with language "cooked"; and what one may say, perhaps Ohr HaChaim's proof from Pesachim daf 40b: we do not smear the pot on Pesach, and one who wishes to smear puts the flour then vinegar, and one says even put vinegar then flour — who is the one who says? R' Chisda, R' Yehudah, who says for everything he puts except vinegar and brine — Rashi: for everything he puts spices in kli sheni except something with vinegar and brine that vinegar cooks them although prior — requires slight further study, for on Shabbat we establish R' Yehudah on the first part on kli rishon, not bowl and ladle which are kli sheni, as we establish from the baraita; and one must say smearing law is kli sheni law — if not, what does R' Yehudah ask, behold first tanna did not disagree with R' Yehudah on kli rishon, only holds it cooks even without vinegar and brine; and forced to say smearing, etc., is like kli sheni — and the question remains we do not find for R' Yehudah dispute on kli sheni as above; and one must say thus the baraita explanation: the one who says for all pots — the word "all" teaches both kli rishon and kli sheni, except something with vinegar and brine forbidden in all pots, both kli rishon and kli sheni, they do not disagree; if so, smearing deals with R' Yehudah who for vinegar and brine one law for kli rishon and kli sheni — this is what resolves Rashi's words, and still the question on Ohr HaChaim who ruled not like first tanna who disagreed with R' Yehudah. It seems he holds Tosafot Shabbat explained R' Yehudah argues on the first part not that kli rishon does not cook, only spices are hard to cook; if so, do not make a distant dispute — therefore first tanna argues with R' Yehudah that vinegar and brine do not cook in kli sheni, namely in hard spices; but in other soft things they agree it cooks, for even in hard spices he holds it cooks; and smearing is spices, therefore for first tanna it does not cook; and Ohr HaChaim deals with other things besides spices — strained. And more plausible: understand first tanna that even in brine and vinegar, kli sheni does not cook — why permit putting flour first then vinegar, for vinegar will cook them and prevent leavening? It must not act at all; and one must say they argue when there is vinegar and brine in the bowl in the dish and afterward one wants to put spices in it: first tanna permits, for vinegar and brine power was already nullified in the dish; R' Yehudah forbids nevertheless; but to put in kli sheni spices and pour vinegar with them immediately — vinegar combines with heat and cooks and is forbidden. And so in smearing, if he puts flour first then vinegar, vinegar cooks the flour so it not leaven — if so, from vinegar. And what appears in Ohr HaChaim's intent regarding salted meat placed in kli sheni — behold immediately it will nullify before combining with kli sheni heat and forbids. And in my novellae I said Rashi has proof for this, for the one who says for all pots means both kli rishon and kli sheni; Rashi holds like Rashbam that pouring does not cook — and examine Tosafot's question why teach "but one pours into the bowl" — teach a greater novelty, "but one pours boiling on them"; according to Rashi, to reconcile: because of R' Yehudah he cited even in the bowl with vinegar and brine it is forbidden, and if he taught pouring, one would say according to R' Yehudah he forbids in pouring with vinegar and brine, not in kli sheni — and he teaches even in this it is forbidden — examine.
All the more the salt is forbidden to eat — Shach in s.k. 41 wrote in the name of Efodi Rabrebbi that b'dieved when well dried it is permitted, and Shach left it in requires further study; and to me there is no doubt to forbid, for although Ran brought in the name of Ramban and other sages who hold the salt is permitted, for the blood was consumed and gone — none of them are proof, for they follow their line that we do not say issur vel heter in other issurim, and likewise it seems also squeezing is permitted according to Tosafot, for one depends on the other; if so, since absorbed blood was consumed and lost, obviously salt is permitted, for it was squeezed and lost. But according to what we hold in all issurim nevela, and likewise possible to squeeze it — forbidden; even for one who does not say issur vel heter — if so, this is because of blood absorbed in salt, not because of issur vel heter — behold the salt, even though absorbed blood was consumed, nevertheless: (1) to squeeze it is forbidden, and behold salting became forbidden from blood — how can you return it to permission; therefore there is no doubt it is forbidden.
