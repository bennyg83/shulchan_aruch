The salt. Taz wrote the reason that all the salt becomes nevelah; and Shach challenged this, for the Mechaber holds we do not say chatichah na'asis nevelah except in meat and milk — rather the reason is we do not know how much blood it absorbed; and if so, behold we hold a matter that works by taste is not nullified even in a thousand, and salt works by taste — this is not so, for specifically if the substance of the prohibition gives taste, which is not so here, for salt is forbidden only from the blood in it and blood does not work by taste; however, what Tur and the Rav wrote against the salt is not precise, rather we need sixty against the moisture of blood and salt on it from outside:
Combines. Shach wrote the reason: from the moment the piece entered the water all the salt and blood on it melted and fell into the pot, and it is not called issur davuk here, and even the piece itself is permitted — and thus all decisors practice, not like Maharshal — end of his words (and in Shnei Luchot HaBrit he ruled to be stringent for himself and forbid that piece even when there is sixty there, like Maharshal's view):
Second. Taz and Maharshal permit b'dieved, and one who is lenient like their words did not lose; and also Shach wrote that in great loss one may be lenient; and if it is permitted to leave unsalted beef in a kli rishon where yad soledes bo — Taz's conclusion is to permit, and he wrote such is Maharshal's view; and see in Orach Chaim siman 318 seif 9. And Shach wrote that vinegar and other sharp things like it also cook even in kli sheni (and see Peri Chadash). Taz wrote: an incident with a woman who cooked meat and forgot whether she salted it first, and we permitted based on safek d'rabbanan lekula, for blood in cooking is rabbinic; and he also mentioned a stringency and that she is accustomed all her days to salt before cooking — see there; and Shach in Nekudot HaKesef disagrees with him, for here an issur of blood was established — see there; and Peri Chadash agreed with Taz — see there:
