Sixty against the blood does not suffice, for the salt becomes entirely nevelah, as Beit Yosef wrote here in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot; but one may answer the piece itself becomes entirely nevelah and will require sixty against all of it, for this is not adherent prohibition, for as soon as it enters the pot the salt melts from the meat; and Rashal in Orchot Shabbat chapter 8 ruled one needs sixty against the piece; and Maharach wrote on him his words do not appear correct in this, nor did all decisors practice like him — end of his words; and one may answer: we hold a substance that imparts taste is not nullified even in a thousand; and salt imparts taste — Rosh Shach Devarim answered: this is specifically if the body of the prohibition imparts taste — unlike here, where salt forbids only because of blood in it, and blood does not impart taste; and Rashal chapter 35 siman 30 ruled the piece itself was forbidden because it was cooked in its blood, therefore it does not help nullify blood and salt in sixty, rather sixty is required against the whole piece — unless it was rinsed once, then the piece also helps nullify. And it appears to me one should not practice thus (for we hold siman 105 where there is no fat, it does not forbid in melicha more than k'dei klipah) — aside from rejecting his proof there from Maharai to the inquirer; therefore the main ruling is as Rama ruled, and so Maharach wrote in the name of Maharar Hirsch Sofer, who decided like Rama:
And the whole pot combines, etc. Meaning everything in the pot; but the pot itself — obviously it does not combine, as siman 99:
For the piece itself is certainly thirty against the salt, etc. So should read — and so in some books; and some texts read 'certainly it is sixty' — that is a scribal error; and Orchot Chaim wrote there are views that the piece itself is sixty against blood and salt on it — but one should not rule thus for practical halacha:
Only in kli sheni. Meaning the pot in which they placed this meat is kli sheni:
For since there is salt and gravy there, etc. He wrote this so it should not be difficult — behold we hold kli sheni does not cook — and this is Orchot Chaim's view; but Rashal chapter 25 siman 23 wrote: but b'dieved it is not forbidden, according to what he wrote above that kli sheni does not cook and also does not expel and absorb the blood in the salt in the meat, for the salt after its power ceased is no longer considered hot — and do not heed what Torat Chatat wrote to forbid even in kli sheni in the name of Orchot Chaim; also Maharai's view that kli sheni does not cook at all and does not expel and absorb together, end of his words; and it appears one who is lenient like Rashal did not lose, for his reason is reasonable; and the main matter is d'rabbanan regarding blood that was salted, one may go to leniency; and although Mordechai is uncertain in this, one may go to leniency; and it appears here that in kli rishon even without melicha it is forbidden — and so Torat Chatat general rule 2 seif 4, unlike Rabbenu Yerucham who permits raw meat not salted placed in a hot pot removed from the fire; and proof from Rosh's words, who wrote that even if the meat does not cook, nevertheless the moisture on it cooks — end of his words. Rosh in chapter Kirah on what we conclude in the Gemara that on Shabbat one may put salt in a pot removed from the fire, for salting requires cooking like ox flesh — appears here ox flesh was not cooked in kli rishon, and permitted to put raw meat in kli rishon removed from the fire; however if it is old salted meat, perhaps forbidden, for it hastens to cook; and even if salted anew, although the meat does not cook, the moisture and blood and salt inside cook and are absorbed in the meat and forbidden, end of his words. Tur in Orach Chaim siman 318: kli rishon cooks even after removed from the fire — therefore forbidden to put meat in it, even ox meat that needs much cooking and does not cook — nevertheless the moisture in it cooks, end of his words. And Torat Chatat wrote there: although from Rosh's words it appears specifically when salted the moisture cooks, because melicha helps boil the moisture — nevertheless one should not distinguish thus, since Orach Chaim wrote the moisture cooks even without melicha at all — end of his words) — and their words are astounding, for Rosh certainly deals there with Shabbat prohibition, not meat prohibition; he concludes the moisture of blood and salt returns and absorbs in the meat — if so, certainly he deals only when salted; for if not, and even without salt the meat is not forbidden, for ox meat itself does not cook, only the moisture cooks — therefore there is no prohibition to the meat except when salted, for salt absorbs blood in the meat; but if there is no salt, although blood moisture cooks, nevertheless it has no power to absorb, since the meat is not cooked; but Orach Chaim does not deal with meat prohibition but Shabbat prohibition — no difference whether salted or not, nevertheless there is Shabbat cooking prohibition because of the moisture — therefore according to this we gain: in ox meat not salted placed in kli rishon after removed from fire, Rabbenu Yerucham well wrote in the name of Rosh the meat is permitted; and Orach Chaim's words are for Shabbat specifically; but other meat perhaps hastens to cook as moisture cooks — therefore the meat too is forbidden, as I wrote; and I again saw in Orchot Chaim Rashal chapter 1 wrote: meat that was salted and did not remain the measure of melicha and was rinsed and placed in a pot — if it is other meat, it is forbidden even if placed only in kli rishon in which yad soledes bo, and he remembered before it began to boil; but ox meat is not forbidden in this, since it was already rinsed; and likewise raw meat not salted at all, end of his words — behold it matches what I wrote: (hagahah: and in one of its aspects it suffices — Semak: if an idolater serves in a Jewish house and they placed meat in a pot and we do not know if they rinsed it — they are believed in m'salket; and all the more if they know Jewish practice; and if there is a lad who knows rinsing, or entering and exiting — permitted because of trembling, etc. — many wondered at this, and all the more it appears when they know there is more permission in m'salket; and some emended in Derishah and Maharach the 'vav' of 'and if there is' to 'if there is,' meaning all the more if they know Jewish practice and there is a lad, etc.; but in the beginning it deals only when they do not know, since the main permission is from m'salket — and it appears to me this hagahah is not correct, for then permitted — he said at the end it is superfluous; at minimum he should have said 'permitted.' And certainly the text is authentic as in all books, and so in Rashal's copy of Semak's words; and this m'salket is when it appears he did not come to inform about this, such as when he tells another matter and from those words the story emerges that he rinsed the meat — we believe him, and his knowing Jewish practice does not harm, since it is clear he did not intend to inform about this; and if so, on the contrary there is an advantage in his knowing Jewish practice — there are two aspects of propriety; certainly he acted as Jews do, since he is his servant and intends to do his will; and there is also m'salket in the manner we mentioned — therefore all the more it helps here; and if there is a lad, etc., then m'salket is not needed — so it appears to me; and therefore Rama ruled well: in one of its aspects it suffices; and certainly he intended the m'salket we mentioned; but in any case one may say he intended to endear himself to the Jew, and certainly m'slaket does not help — so it appears to me for practical halacha:
