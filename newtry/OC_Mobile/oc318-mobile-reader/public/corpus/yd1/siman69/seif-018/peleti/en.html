And it remained, etc. — implies even if it did not remain salting period for cooking, nevertheless called boiling; and below siman 91 it seems before this time not called boiling — per what I wrote to distinguish between blood and other absorptions; however one may say even though not boiling, nevertheless at time of soaking it forbids, and vinegar proves it is not like rotev to absorb, nevertheless makes it kevush; and the same here, not called boiling, nevertheless makes it kevush. Only if not, question above on soaking that requires peel — why, behold not called boiling, and one must say deals where before putting in vessel he salted in unperforated vessel and remained time, afterward put in unperforated vessel and remained long enough to put on fire, etc., it is boiling brine, for in truth it remained salting period in combination of two vessels; and truthfully the law does not imply somewhat so, nevertheless for law it is correct.
Only a peel's depth — and if not known which side was placed inside the brine, the whole piece is forbidden — Pri Chadash; and it seems there is safek perhaps majority of piece was inside brine, but if clear to us there was not a majority, then majority nullifies, for one in two strictly is not required, only majority — see below laws of mixtures.
And there are those who forbid the whole piece, for blood was aroused to exit, etc., and it is blood that spread place to place — and one may challenge that which we say fish and birds salted one with another are forbidden, and Gemara challenged: if in unperforated vessel, why fish and birds, birds too; what is the question, Ravin who holds melicha is not like rotev concedes salt expels blood, for nature of salt is to expel blood — whence does Ravin need melicha for cooking, if so he concedes according to Rif's view in unsalted unperforated vessel it is forbidden, for blood was aroused to exit, etc.; if so the Gemara wanted to teach not like Ravin, melicha is truly like rotev to absorb and expel — but if it said birds and birds, one would say forbidden because blood spread place to place, but melicha is not rotev to absorb to another place, not like roasting — if so it teaches much; and answer: even birds and birds is explained thus, not simply even birds too, but in two ways: birds that expelled blood and birds that did not expel blood — one forbids its fellow because of absorption — learn melicha is like rotev. However this may be rejected: we teach even fish that did not expel at all, we do not say since they are busy it is forbidden — one may say.
And so is the practice — Pri Chadash wondered above siman 23 s.k. 2 on Rosh's words in chapter Keitzad Tzolin who drags after Rabbenu Peretz's words that blood spread place to place is forbidden, and in first chapter Chullin in matter of cooking on Shabbat he wrote like Tosafot that it is permitted — answer: in chapter Keitzad Tzolin also according to Rif, and in his words he does not hold only like Hagahot, and Rabbenu Peretz's practical case in unperforated vessel is R' Tam who forbade a chicken entirely that was salted in unperforated vessel, and R' Tam as Tosafot wrote in his name in matter of cooking on Shabbat wrote explicitly permitted regarding spread place to place — a) and contradicts — requires further study. And to resolve, it seems to distinguish in roasting, since fit to eat in its roasting, the blood that spread is blood in roasting which is d'rabbanan, only forbidden, and they were not stringent on blood that spread; and on this R' Tam's words refer to roasting that was not fully roasted, but in Rif's practical case of chicken immediately when placed in unperforated vessel blood immediately began to spread and it had no immediate outlet, being in unperforated vessel — examine; then still it was not salted blood and its issur is before salting, forbidden because of blood before salting, unlike in roasting it was roasted first — examine.
