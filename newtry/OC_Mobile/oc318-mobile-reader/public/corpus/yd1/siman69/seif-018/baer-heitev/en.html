Roasted. And the reason is in Shach: because it is pickled like cooked; and what he ruled in seif 15 that we need from time to time to consider it pickled like cooked — that is because there is no hot meliach there, and for that reason he ruled above that roasting is permitted, and here he forbids even for roasting; but less than this measure, even what is within the gravy is not forbidden except to the depth of a peel and the measure of melicha — see below siman 91 seif 5:
Peel. Meaning: where it touched the piece that was in gravy; and even if there is fat in it we do not say the blood travels with it, for everywhere we hold the forbidden cannot forbid except where the prohibition itself can go there by its nature, which is not so here — end of his words Shach:
Outside. Shach wrote: and he does not hold regarding a piece that fell into gravy that it is forbidden except what is within the gravy, as written in siman 70 that there it is salted according to its law and expelled all its blood — therefore it is forbidden only what is within the gravy, for blood does not seep upward; but when salted in a non-perforated vessel everything is forbidden, for blood spread from place to place and does not exit completely since it is in a non-perforated vessel, and it no longer exits neither through melicha nor through roasting; and even though in breaking the neck-bone of an animal in siman 67 it is forbidden to eat from it in the middle because blood spread from place to place, and nevertheless permitted through melicha and roasting — it must be said here it is worse, since it spread through melicha; or since it was salted in a non-perforated vessel it is pickled like cooked and melicha and roasting afterward do not help it:
They practice. And Shach wrote in the name of Maharshal: even though they practice thus, nevertheless if one mixed into two, even in cooking, sixty is not needed but it is nullified in a majority, since from the Torah one in two is nullified, only rabbinically sixty is needed — as those poskim who permit what is outside the gravy may rely on them in such a case — requires further study for practical halacha; and perhaps one may rely on it since in any case blood that was salted or cooked is only rabbinic; however it implies there that even if it was salted the time of melicha for a pot and also remained the time of melicha, there is permission in such a case:
Permitted. Taz wrote: even a peel is not needed, for one piece is not so pressed against its fellow that the lower one would be prevented from expelling; and an incident came to him where he placed a salted and rinsed goose on an unsalted goose and salted the upper goose and gravy flowed from it and a little of the lower one soaked in that gravy in a non-perforated vessel — it appears to me in this one may rely on Rosh and Rashba not to forbid except what rests in the gravy, and the lower goose is not like one salted in a non-perforated vessel, for there is no melicha at all here, only it has the law of pickled in gravy in siman 105, and there Rama brought 'some are lenient' regarding what is outside the gravy — end of his words. And Shach wrote: if a piece fell into gravy in a non-perforated vessel and there is doubt whether it fell there before it remained the time of melicha — what is outside the gravy is permitted, and all the more so if it is certain it fell there after it remained the time of melicha; but if it is certain it fell there before it remained the time of melicha — forbidden even what is outside the gravy, for since it had not yet expelled all its blood it is literally like salting in a non-perforated vessel — likewise in responsum Masat Binyamin siman 39; and see above end of seif 70:
