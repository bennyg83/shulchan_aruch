(There seif 20 in the hagahah) And even before the salting period the issur is not because of the knife but because visible blood exited; and if he wishes he may rinse there and return to salt there. From this it is proven that if he cut a piece in two before salting after rinsing, he need not return to soak it, only rinse the place of the cut, as I wrote s.k. 1; and so explicitly in Bedek HaBayit and Lechem HaPanim. And do not challenge: behold it is called visible blood and therefore since blood came on the meat the meat should be forbidden, for we hold end of siman 70 that if visible blood fell on meat during its salting period it is immediately forbidden and there is no measure to the matter. And one may say it is not called visible blood on the absorbed blood in the piece, only for the matter that he must rinse it, for if he salts on it the salt will become full from it and will not expel blood, as Mordechai wrote in the reason for initial rinsing; but for this matter it is not called visible. Rather absorbed wet blood that exits afterward through salting, as I wrote, and examine. And see what I wrote in our words siman 10 s.k. 4.
