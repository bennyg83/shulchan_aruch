And that piece became mixed with others. Meaning it became mixed with other pieces that had not yet remained three days and were not yet salted — permitted to cook all of them when he salts all of them; or it deals when all were already salted and one was salted after it remained three days and the others were salted within three days — permitted to cook all of them without melicha:
It is nullified in a majority. And it teaches us we do not say it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin for roasting, for for roasting it was never forbidden — and see siman 82 seif 4 in hagahah and what is written there:
In a majority. Requires further study, for we hold below siman 109 that min b'she'eino mino requires sixty because if he cooks them it imparts taste — if so here why does a majority suffice, behold the blood will impart taste when he cooks them? However for Maharai and the Mechaber one may distinguish: perhaps they hold that since meat that remained three days without melicha is only a stringency of the Geonim, the Geonim did not decree so far; and in such a case it is written in responsum of R' Aharon Sasson siman 179 regarding meat that remained three days without melicha and was cooked: since it is only a stringency of the Geonim, it appears one may rely on Ra'avad, who holds even in meat cooked without melicha one need not require sixty against the whole piece but estimates what exits from it — only one should not be lenient in practice, end of his words). But on the Rav it is difficult, for he wrote 'and likewise,' etc., appears it is nullified also in a majority, as Torat Chatat general rule 2 din 3 wrote explicitly; and meat cooked without melicha is forbidden by law; however for Orchot Chaim one may distinguish: he follows his view that dry in dry that is not min b'mino requires sixty — the reason is not because it imparts taste when cooked, but because it is called a davar sheyesh lo matirin partially to recognize the prohibition and remove it from there, etc., as he wrote in his name siman 98 s.k. 6 — if so here it cannot be recognized, for it is absorbed (and perhaps Maharai also holds this reason — examine); but the Rav, who holds the reason above and it is primary as written there, is difficult; and it is strained to distinguish that here it is different because the prohibition is absorbed; and it is possible here it comes only to teach us it is not forbidden because of a piece fit to honor — meaning 'majority' is not precise, rather sixty is required — requires further study:
And permitted to cook all of them. Specifically each one by itself, or even all of them in one pot when he increases permitted over it up to sixty — otherwise not, as below siman 109 seif 2, see there; and from the wording 'permitted to cook all of them' it appears one need not throw one away or give to an idolater — although in that siman the Rav wrote some are stringent to throw one away or give to an idolater; nevertheless here he stated simply like the Mechaber's words, for he already wrote there it is only a mere stringency; and here, since meat that remained three days without melicha is only a stringency of the Geonim, one should not be stringent:
