(Siman 69 seif 1) The Mechaber's wording "and if after he rinsed he cut each piece into two," etc. This law is from the Mordechai, and his proof from that stated in chapter 1 of Chullin that through the pressure of the knife it absorbs — even according to the one who says the place of slaughter is cold, likewise here through the pressure of the knife it expels. He challenges: did he not himself rule above that even according to the one who says the place of slaughter is cold — i.e. the place of slaughter is not completely hot but nevertheless is somewhat hot and through pressure it absorbs — not so here that it is completely cold; because of the pressure of the knife he says it does not expel. One may say that here, since it need only expel from meat that is soft, it is different. One may say that the pressure of the knife alone also expels, and his proof is thus: as in hot somewhat the pressure of the knife expels completely what was absorbed, likewise here that it is not absorbed so much in it, the pressure of the knife suffices to expel even when completely cold.
(Shach s.k. 3) However the Ran's opinion in chapter 1 of Chullin, etc., that if he cut it afterward into two he need not return and rinse it, etc. However one may say the Ran goes according to his reason, etc. — end from Shach's words. And at first glance it is difficult, for then the Ran's challenge returns to its place against the Mordechai, and one must say the Mordechai holds therefore three knives are needed lest he cut forbidden fats and afterward meat and rely on the butcher's rinsing and salt the meat without rinsing; if so, for visible blood on it only as much as a peel is forbidden, unlike forbidden fat that is forbidden throughout, for it spreads — requires further study.
(seif 13) Meat that was salted and a doubt whether salted within three days — permitted. One may be lenient according to what Taz wrote s.k. 4, incident of a woman who cooked meat and forgot if she salted, and she was permitted because blood in cooking is rabbinic and sixty rabbinic leniently; if so, even a doubt perhaps not salted at all is also permitted, for blood that was salted is only rabbinic and doubt leniently; if so why say specifically within three days permitted because it is stringency of Geonim — even without this reason permitted because rabbinic doubt leniently. One may say: since we hold melicha forbids only as much as a peel, salt has no more power than a peel, and we do not say blood that was salted is definitely rabbinic; therefore without the Geonic stringency I would say sixty stringently; and what Taz wrote sixty leniently is specifically after cooking. Therefore one may answer what Maharshal challenged on Rabbenu Yerucham that he need not give reason that blood in cooking is rabbinic, for from melicha too it would be d'oraisa forbidden — according to my words one may say Rabbenu Yerucham wants a reason that the idolater is believed even on salting meat, i.e. if the idolater says he salted, the law is thus; if so compelled to reason it is like blood in cooking, for if because of melicha more than a peel, not blood that was salted; requires further study. [Editor: his words are astonishing — if so how say because melicha is like boiling it is blood in cooking; nevertheless not boiling only peel measure; also what he answers here in doubt that remained three days deals before cooking — nevertheless will be rabbinic doubt after cooking; one must say because nullifying prohibition that was initially d'oraisa and wants to cook and make rabbinic doubt — end editor note on resolving Maharshal.]
(there seif 20) Meat that was salted and remained, etc. Taz s.k. 4 holds that after measure of melicha it is no longer called boiling and even tzir is not boiling; in my opinion it appears from Rosh that at least tzir is boiling even after measure of melicha — in Rashi's incident the reason not to forbid tzir is only mohal, etc., and although salt on meat forbidden because blood mixed in it, therefore must rinse meat well, etc. — either blood in salt dried inside and has no power to exit, or power ceased because it expelled and salt no longer considered boiling; hastily one may answer the latter: why say above that after measure melicha not blood only mohal, permitted because no boiling at all; nevertheless after measure tzir is boiling, therefore say tzir is only mohal and nullifies boiling power of tzir on meat that exited during measure of melicha; therefore proved not like Rama and Taz as written.
