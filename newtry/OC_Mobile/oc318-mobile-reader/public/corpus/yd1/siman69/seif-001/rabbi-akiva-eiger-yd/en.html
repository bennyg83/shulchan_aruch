(Siman 69, Shach s.k. 1) The reason is so it softens. Explained in Beit Yosef that when the cold is great they put water in the sun until its chill departs before rinsing in them, for cold water hardens and does not soften — see above (siman 36 seif 4). And b'dieved if he rinsed in very cold water, one should discuss whether it helps to rinse again in lukewarm water to soften, or since the meat hardened through cold water it will no longer soften; and one may learn to permit from that of meat full of ice at the end of this seif. And if he salted after rinsing in cold water, it has remedy through rinsing and melicha a second time — from both sides: for the reason of visible blood, rinsing in cold water helps; for the reason of softening, rinsing and melicha a second time help. And if he did not rinse and salted again and cooked thus — requires further study for practical law, for according to Taz s.k. 10 and Shach s.k. 14, slight rinsing is permitted b'dieved for we rely on the reason of visible blood (or combined with the reason lest the salt dissolve) — likewise permitted. But if we explain that slight rinsing permitted refers to above that permitted with rinsing and melicha a second time (and this appears from what is written in hagahah "and the same if there was sixty against visible blood," and in this that for all reasons forbidden except for visible blood — if so, permitted without rinsing and melicha a second time and without great loss, it would be compelled that we hold the primary reason is visible blood and we are not concerned at all for other reasons; and behold in "he salted and did not rinse" that rinsing and melicha a second time in great loss helps — behold we rely to say the reason is not visible blood; and Rama's hagahah words refer to remedy of rinsing and melicha a second time) — if so, likewise when he rinsed in cold water one may say forbidden without rinsing and melicha a second time, and even if cooked and there is no great loss (Av Beit Din — see what my grandfather the third wrote on Shach below s.k. 14).
(Seif 1 in hagahah) It is as if not rinsed at all. It is difficult for me: behold without rinsing at all, if he salted and cooked, even b'dieved and with great loss forbidden; and in this that he cut a piece, if he did not return and rinse them, permitted with great loss, for in great loss we rely on the reason of softening as explained in seif 2; and for that reason of softening he need not return and rinse as Shach wrote s.k. 3.
(there in hagahah) Only rinsed well. And if he rinsed well on one side and on the other side slight rinsing — one may say that suffices: for softening one may say that through one side also it softened; for visible blood slight rinsing helps — requires further study for practical law.
(Shulchan Aruch) Law of meat full of ice. If after it was salted and rinsed ice is found in its middle — see in Levit Chen on the Torah, parashat Acharei Mot.
(Taz s.k. 5) And specifically a wet matter in cold. It implies that dry cold is permitted, for the eater rinses; and even in something not customary to rinse we are not concerned lest he be negligent and eat without rinsing, since b'dieved we judge that the vessel was rinsed well (for from Beit Yosef when he used it boiling, permitted b'dieved) — therefore we are not concerned for negligence — see Taz (siman 91 s.k. 1); and here preferable, for b'dieved we rely that he acted properly, for we judge the vessel rinsed properly — not required to rinse the food.
(Seif 2) And some forbid. A chicken from outside — there is no visible blood — Peri Megadim below siman 72 in Shach s.k. 4.
(Shach s.k. 14) Still considered b'dieved. The conclusion of the later authorities is not so; rather Rama's intent is that with rinsing or with sixty, rinsing and melicha a second time help — for the reason of softening the second rinsing helps, and for the other reason the first rinsing helps. And if cooked through slight rinsing and melicha — requires further study; one may say permitted since there are two sides to permit, namely the reason lest salt dissolve, or because of visible blood slight rinsing helps; and without rinsing at all, only with sixty — one should forbid, since permitted only to taste because of visible blood, and we do not judge taste for substance, since in great loss they permit with rinsing and melicha a second time — we rely the reason is softening or lest salt dissolve; however in great loss requires further study. And see what I wrote in the margin above at the beginning of the seif that the implication is one law — slight rinsing with the law of sixty against visible blood; and since with sixty certainly impossible to be lenient without great loss and to rely on visible blood — likewise with slight rinsing, since Rama weighed one scale.
(Shach s.k. 3) And the same in fruit juice. Not understood: if from reasoning fruit juice is like rainwater, why need Ran's words? For otherwise we know rainwater helps and need not examine specifically!
(Beit Yosef at end) And requires further study. In my opinion one may say Turei Even's intent is certainly Tosafot's view that both first and final rinsing are permitted in fruit juice; and since Tosafot hold so, it is reasonable without compulsion, for we find no one who disagrees; and this is for final rinsing; but for first rinsing there is no proof from Tosafot, for perhaps they hold like poskim that first rinsing's reason is visible blood, therefore permitted in fruit juice; but for softening one may say fruit juice does not help — requires further study.
