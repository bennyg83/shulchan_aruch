One must rinse the meat before melicha. — The reason is so the flesh softens and its blood exits through melicha — Ran; and some say in Mordechai; and Mordechai wrote the reason the first rinsing is to remove the filth on the piece, for otherwise the salt fills up from it and the salt no longer expels the blood in the piece; and for these two reasons it helps if he returns and rinses and salts again even after time. But Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Semak the reason is because salt absorbs and does not expel blood that is visible; and so wrote in Semag (negative commandments 134 daf 48b) and so in Sha'arei Dura and in Hagahot Shach Devarim that blood on its surface is like visible blood that fell on roasting flesh and is forbidden, for we do not say as it absorbs so it expels, and likewise one must say for their view that regarding visible blood we do not say since it is busy expelling it does not absorb; and so wrote the Rav end of siman 70:

And if the butcher rinsed it, one need not rinse it at home. — And Orchot Chaim wrote beginning of general rule 4 and they bring it in Torat Chatat general rule 15 din 4 that it does not help even b'dieved what butchers rinse — whole ribs in the slaughterhouse as they are accustomed to pour on them in mere passing to beautify them, and even if they did not split them afterward, for it is not called the butcher rinsed until he soaked them a bit in a vessel, end of his words; and 'until he soaked in a vessel' is not precise, for if so when he did not rinse by mere passing but rinsed well — and as the Rav wrote nearby — therefore when he copied in Torat Chatat there the words of Orchot Chaim he copied 'it is not called the butcher rinsed except when he soaked it a bit like meat one rinses when one wishes to salt it,' end of his words; it appears to me. However, if we know the butcher rinsed, we say presumably he rinsed well — and so it appears in Bach:
He cuts every portion in two, etc. — He was precise to write 'he cuts,' meaning specifically with a knife he must return and rinse, as Mordechai wrote that through pressure of the knife it expels. However, Ran's view in the first chapter of Chullin regarding the butcher is that he needs three knives, for if he cut it afterward in two he need not return and rinse it — and likewise is compelled the view of R' Yosef ibn Chaviv whom Beit Yosef brought siman 6, see there; but one may say Ran follows his reasons, for he holds the reason for rinsing is not because of blood but so the flesh softens, as he wrote in his name in s.k. 1, and since they already pierced them in the slaughterhouse they already softened and we do not care that they cut afterward in two — and this is also R' Yosef ibn Chaviv's view. But for us, who hold the reason for rinsing is because of blood, as below seif 2, rinsing is needed even b'dieved:
Or if he removed the hooves of the legs after rinsing. — But if he did not remove them, everything would be considered one piece of meat and rinsing them as they are would suffice — meaning as the Rav wrote siman 68 seif 8 regarding feet salted in their shell: we are accustomed to forbid what is inside the shell because it is like salting in an unperforated vessel, and we do not forbid them because they were salted without rinsing (and then even what is outside the shell is forbidden, as written nearby) — rather certainly if he did not remove them, rinsing them as they are suffices, since there is no blood visible to the eye; and so is proven in Orchot Chaim beginning of general rule 17:
Only that he rinsed it well suffices for him. — Meaning he need not return and trouble himself to soak it even if it was not yet salted; but with slight rinsing it does not suffice and he must return and rinse it well; but if it was salted, even with slight rinsing it suffices b'dieved, as written in hagahah seif 2 — and so it appears explicitly in Torat Chatat beginning of general rule 15 as he explained; and in Sefer Lamed Chet daf 248b he wrote the Rav changed in his hagahot from what he wrote in Torat Chatat, see there — and for now as I wrote:
He should wait a little. — To exclude that he should not wait until it dries completely, for then the salt will not melt at all and will not expel blood — Torat Chatat there din 2:
They are accustomed not to use it, etc. — Meaning certainly by law it is permitted to use it for other things, for the vessel is not forbidden since one does not soak in it from above, for it is cold and does not absorb — only they are accustomed not to use it l'chatchila even cold, for we are concerned lest blood still adhere to it; however b'dieved it is permitted even if he used it hot, if he rinsed it beforehand:
