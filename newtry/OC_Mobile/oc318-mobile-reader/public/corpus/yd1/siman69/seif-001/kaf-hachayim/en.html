(1) [Seif 1] One must rinse the meat before melicha. Regarding blood that was salted or cooked — whether its prohibition is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — there is dispute among the great ones: Rashi's view in Chullin daf 109a at the end, s.v. halev, that blood that was cooked one is liable for karet; and likewise daf 123a s.v. hakafah — see there; and Beit Yosef wrote in siman 87 that this appears to be Rambam's view. And likewise Peleti end of siman 1 that Rif and Rambam are like Rashi. And likewise the Mechaber siman 1 that Rambam chapter 6 of Forbidden Foods law 6 is like Rashi — see there. And likewise Radbaz part 1 siman 199. But Tosafot there Chullin 109a s.v. halev and 123a s.v. hakafah wrote that blood that was cooked one does not transgress. And likewise Peri Chadash siman 1 that Tosafot, Rashba, Ran, and Ra'ah plainly hold blood that was salted is forbidden only rabbinically; and Beit Yosef the Mechaber there, and wrote that so is Ritva's view in his commentary to Chullin daf 111 — see there. And so appears Beit Yosef's view who wrote in this siman on Semak's words regarding an idolater serving in an Israelite's house, etc., that the idolater is not believed except with a woman's testimony — that is for a d'oraisa prohibition, but for a rabbinic prohibition believed in masiach lefi tumo; and this too is rabbinic prohibition, for since melicha is like boiling, it is blood that was cooked and one does not transgress — end of his words. And likewise at the end of this siman on what Maharshal wondered on Rashba, Beit Yosef wrote "it appears to me there is no place for wonder, for since blood that was salted one does not transgress, they were not concerned to decree on it" — end of his words; and Peri Chadash agreed at end of siman 1. However Hagahot Reuven siman 1 wrote: it appears primary to be stringent like Rashi and Rambam that blood that was salted or cooked is d'oraisa; and he further wrote that even according to those who say rabbinic, Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 75 wrote that since the essence of blood is d'oraisa, they were stringent in its doubt as Torah law — see there. It follows this matter is a second dispute; therefore it appears that if there are further sides to be lenient, one may rely on those who say blood that was salted or cooked is rabbinic and follow its doubt leniently. And see further below siman 87 seif 50.
