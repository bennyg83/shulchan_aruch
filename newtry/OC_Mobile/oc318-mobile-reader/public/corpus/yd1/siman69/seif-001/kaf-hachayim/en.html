(1) [Seif 1] One must rinse the meat before melicha. Regarding blood that was salted or cooked — whether its prohibition is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — there is dispute among the great ones: Rashi's view in Chullin daf 109a at the end, s.v. halev, that blood that was cooked one is liable for karet; and likewise daf 123a s.v. hakafah — see there; and Beit Yosef wrote in siman 87 that this appears to be Rambam's view. And likewise Peleti end of siman 1 that Rif and Rambam are like Rashi. And likewise the Mechaber siman 1 that Rambam chapter 6 of Forbidden Foods law 6 is like Rashi — see there. And likewise Radbaz part 1 siman 199. But Tosafot there Chullin 109a s.v. halev and 123a s.v. hakafah wrote that blood that was cooked one does not transgress. And likewise Peri Chadash siman 1 that Tosafot, Rashba, Ran, and Ra'ah plainly hold blood that was salted is forbidden only rabbinically; and Beit Yosef the Mechaber there, and wrote that so is Ritva's view in his commentary to Chullin daf 111 — see there. And so appears Beit Yosef's view who wrote in this siman on Semak's words regarding an idolater serving in an Israelite's house, etc., that the idolater is not believed except with a woman's testimony — that is for a d'oraisa prohibition, but for a rabbinic prohibition believed in masiach lefi tumo; and this too is rabbinic prohibition, for since melicha is like boiling, it is blood that was cooked and one does not transgress — end of his words. And likewise at the end of this siman on what Maharshal wondered on Rashba, Beit Yosef wrote "it appears to me there is no place for wonder, for since blood that was salted one does not transgress, they were not concerned to decree on it" — end of his words; and Peri Chadash agreed at end of siman 1. However Hagahot Reuven siman 1 wrote: it appears primary to be stringent like Rashi and Rambam that blood that was salted or cooked is d'oraisa; and he further wrote that even according to those who say rabbinic, Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 75 wrote that since the essence of blood is d'oraisa, they were stringent in its doubt as Torah law — see there. It follows this matter is a second dispute; therefore it appears that if there are further sides to be lenient, one may rely on those who say blood that was salted or cooked is rabbinic and follow its doubt leniently. And see further below siman 87 seif 50.
(2) There. One must rinse the meat before melicha, as stated in chapter Kol HaBasar (Chullin 113a): Shmuel said meat does not leave its blood except if one salts it very well and rinses it very well. Rav Huna said: salt and rinse. In the baraita it taught: rinse, salt, and rinse — they do not disagree: this where they rinsed in the slaughterhouse, this where they did not rinse in the slaughterhouse — end of matter. And Ran wrote in the name of Ra'ah: the first rinsing must remove blood on the surface of the meat, for salt in its heat expels only blood inside the meat that is moist, but not what dried on its surface; therefore if he does not rinse it first, one should be concerned that after he leaves the meat to expel blood and tzir, the blood will melt and be absorbed — end of matter. And he wrote another reason: why they rinse at first — so the meat softens and its blood exits through melicha; for if it hardens on its surface, salt will not have power to expel its blood. And the reason for rinsing after melicha is to rinse the moisture of blood on the surface of the meat that comes from its expulsion — end of matter. And Mordechai wrote: first rinsing to remove filth of blood on the piece, for otherwise salt fills from it and again salt will not expel blood in the piece. Beit Yosef. And Beit Yosef, Taz s.k. 1, wrote: it appears they disagree on this — some forbid and some permit in seif 2 regarding salting without rinsing first — for some permit who hold the second reason of Ra'ah, and some forbid who hold Ra'ah's first reason — see there. And likewise Shach s.k. 1 that according to Ran and Mordechai it helps if he returns and salts again even after time — see there. And for practical law, see below in seif 2 — see there.
(3) There. One must rinse the meat, etc. In cold countries and in a time of great cold they put water in the sun until its chill departs and afterward rinse the meat in them. Beit Yosef. Pitchei Teshuvah siman 1 letter alef. And see below seif 35.
(4) There in hagahah. And if the butcher rinsed it, one need not rinse it in the house. And what butchers practice to wash whole ribs in mere passing to beautify them — not called rinsing. Orchot Hayim general rule 4 seif 1; Turei Chaim general rule 15 law 4; Shach s.k. 2; Peri Chadash seif 3; Beit Lechem Yehudah seif 2; Kereti seif 3; Zivchei Tzedek seif 2.
(6) Visible blood that fell on meat and dried and hardened — rinsing and washing alone do not suffice; rather he must rub well because of blood that dried on its surface; and one should be stringent to rub well at the time of rinsing to remove what hardened on its surface, and clean by hand, since sometimes it does not slide off the meat entirely through mere rinsing and moisture of visible blood remains on its surface even after rinsing; and so a person should teach in his house to be careful in this. Ya'avetz. And in our country in summer days, pieces of meat remain with butchers, especially neck pieces on which blood dried and does not exit with mere rinsing; rather he must remove visible blood by hand and move his hand back and forth on the meat to rub well, and be very careful in this. Zivchei Tzedek seif 4.
(7) There. And if after he rinsed he cut, etc., he must return and rinse them. And this is when he cut with a knife, for he must return and rinse because through pressure of the knife it expels; but if he tore by hand, not required. Shach s.k. 3. Minchat Yitzchak on Turei Chaim general rule 15 seif 7; Kereti seif 4; Chochmat Adam in commentary seif 3; Pitchei Teshuvah seif 3; and the same for anything similar to a knife that expels blood through it — requires rinsing. Minchat Yitzchak there. And Peri Chadash end of seif 4 wrote that even if he did not cut with a knife but by hand, one should be concerned and must return and rinse. Beit Lechem Yehudah seif 3; Zivchei Tzedek seif 5 — and wrote: good to be careful.
(8) If a cat ate a little of the piece, it also appears he need not rinse the place of eating — requires further study. Chochmat Adam there; Pitchei Teshuvah there. And that which he was uncertain is according to Shach's words we wrote in the previous seif that he wrote specifically with a knife; but according to Peri Chadash's words there that even cutting by hand one should be concerned — if so, all the more so with a cat's eating.
(9) There in hagahah. Or he removed hooves of the legs, etc. — meaning shoes of an animal. Taz s.k. 3. And see above siman 68 seif 24.
(10) There in hagahah. Or he removed hooves of the legs after rinsing. But if he did not remove them, all is considered one piece of meat and rinsing them as they are suffices, since there is no visible blood. Shach s.k. 4; Shaarei De'ah seif 4; Mekor Chaim seif 7. And regarding if they were salted thus without removing hooves whether what is inside the hooves is forbidden — see above siman 68 seif 8 and our words there.
(11) There. He must return and rinse them. And not required that it soften, for already the meat softened. Mishnat Zekenim seif 3; Chochmat Adam seif 4; Beit Yitzchak part 1 in Amud HaZahav seif 10; margin of Maharsha; Zivchei Tzedek seif 7 — and when removing hooves after melicha, need only rinse the place of the hooves.
(14) There in hagahah. Rinsing meat l'chatchila he should be careful to soak up to half an hour, etc. — according to Ran's reason that rinsing is so the meat softens and expels its blood. Biur HaGra seif 4; and in Orchot Hayim shaar 1 seif 1 wrote more than half an hour, and likewise in Darkei Moshe seif 1, except because it is only custom he did not write here in hagahah to be precise to write more than half an hour.
(15) There in hagahah. Rinsing meat l'chatchila he should soak up to an hour, etc.; and if the face of the piece on one side was not covered in soak-water, l'chatchila suffices also to rinse that side well, for through soaking of the body of the piece all softens well. Peri Megadim in the Introduction; Mekor Chaim seif 16; Pitchei Teshuvah seif 5; Zivchei Tzedek seif 12.
(16) There in hagahah. And to rinse it well in the soak-water — meaning rinse until the waters become white without appearance of blood, as Shach wrote in siman 10 s.k. 21 in the name of Rashba and Ran. And likewise Chochmat Adam in this siman seif 1; and Zivchei Tzedek seif 9 wrote that in three times the waters whiten, except he wrote the main thing is rubbing well, for if he does not rub, perhaps even with rinsing three times the waters will not whiten. And likewise Ben Ish Chai in parashat Tazria seif 16 that presumably in three times the waters will be clean.
(17) And Zivchei Tzedek wrote there that in Baghdad they do not practice soaking half an hour, rather rinse and rub well as the Shulchan Aruch states at the head of the seif that one must rinse meat before melicha, and did not write this of half an hour. And likewise Ben Ish Chai there that there are places that do not practice soaking meat as the Shulchan Aruch that did not mention soaking, only rinsing; and so they practice in Baghdad — see there. But in our holy city Jerusalem, Sephardim too practice like our teacher's words to soak meat half an hour, and possible that in all places they practice so and one should not change.
(18) Meat that was soaked in a vessel in sea water — one sage arose to forbid, and it has no further remedy to salt it, since sea water is very salty, it is as meat salted in a non-perforated vessel; and Rav Moshe Mat part 1 siman 74 rejected his words and permitted for several reasons — see there. Shevet Sofer seif 1; Sdei Chemed seif 8.
(20) There in hagahah. But if he did not soak, only rinsed well, it suffices. And not required to trouble again to soak even if not yet salted; but with slight rinsing it does not suffice and he must return to rinse well. Shach s.k. 5; Peri Chadash seif 6; Lechem HaPanim seif 5; Lechem Panim in seif 1; Shaarei De'ah seif 5; Chochmat Adam seif 5; Mekor Chaim there; Beit Yitzchak in Amud HaZahav seif 6; Zivchei Tzedek seif 16.
