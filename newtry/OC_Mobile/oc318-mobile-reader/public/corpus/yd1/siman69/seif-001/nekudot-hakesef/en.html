(There) And I wondered on Rama, etc. There is no difficulty: there the reason was since blood is not held in the caul only in the fat in the caul — if he salted the inner side of the caul, behold he did not salt the fat at all even on one side, for the caul separates between the salt and the fat:
(There s.k. 24) And an incident came to us, etc. It appears to me he did not rule well, for certainly we do not say in such a case safek d'rabbanan lekula, for otherwise why did all the poskim here need speaking offhand or one entering and leaving — without this it would be permitted from safek d'rabbanan; and likewise above siman 68 seif 12 regarding meliga we only permit with one entering and leaving from fear of the gentile — without this it is forbidden even though it is safek d'rabbanan; and the old collection he found is certainly against all the poskim; rather certainly this case is different, since here an issur of blood was established, and wherever an issur was established we do not say safek d'rabbanan lekula, as written in Shulchan Gavoah siman 110 s.k. 63 din 20; or since it came to him from one side that it is rabbinic, as written there din 19; also one should not invent safek d'rabbanan to be lenient, as written there din 36, for we find many rabbinic doubts to be stringent — see there. And what he wrote 'and further there is reason to permit,' that which we say regarding Kriat Shema, etc. — it is not comparable; on the contrary here it is reasonable she did not salt, for melicha is a great matter — how is it possible she forgot such a matter; also what he wrote 'here there is a majority against the presumption,' etc. — it is not applicable, for majority is not applicable here — and requires clarification:
(There s.k. 25) In Derishah he wrote, etc. He did not copy well, for in Derishah he wrote thus: as Tur said, and likewise any minor knows the nature of rinsing — the minor need not know, rather since he is fit to know, the gentile fears lest the minor knows; but this 'since he knows' that the Rav Rama wrote refers to the gentile:
(There s.k. 29) In Turei Zahav, etc. It is like that hunter who finds a large animal and takes small ones and takes — and I in my book challenged Turei Zahav on this, and thus I wrote: Mordechai wrote in the name of responsum of R' Shimon son of Abraham that even for one who permits in meat, in a whole chicken that was cooked one should be stringent even though there is sixty in the water, because of the liver and intestines, as one who places them in a basket of water and blood expelled from them is absorbed in the chicken and no longer exits, etc. — end of his words; and this responsum is in responsum of Maimonides; and Turei Zahav wrote that likewise if there are no intestines, it is forbidden in chicken according to all, for since it is hollowed blood spread from place to place and forbidden in such a case even in time of pressure and great loss — end of his words. And likewise in Etz Chaim; and it is not clear, for he mentioned specifically liver and intestines — if not so it is no worse than a piece of meat; and even though it spreads from place to place, nevertheless from the moment it spread into the hollow it was nullified in water; and even in liver and intestines it is not forbidden except when they adhere to the bird — if not so, not as Rokeach siman 339 in the name of many great ones; and even though we do not hold like Rokeach there even in adhesion, that is because he follows his view that he has no issur davuk — but we do not hold thus; but when not adhering it is obvious we hold thus, as the Rav wrote in hagahah itself at end of siman 73, only we say since it is not adhering, therefore from the moment it was expelled it was nullified in water — and all the more so regarding the chicken itself one may say thus. It appears clear to me, and likewise in Sefer Be'er Sheva daf 86 he elaborated — see there — end of his words:
