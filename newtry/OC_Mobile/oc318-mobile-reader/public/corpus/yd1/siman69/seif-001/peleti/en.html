One must rinse, etc. — behold, in the body of the law, whether blood that was salted or roasted or cooked is forbidden d'oraisa or only d'rabbanan, most later poskim adopted the view of Tosafot and their supporters that it is only d'rabbanan. However, Rashi and Rambam hold d'oraisa, for in chapter Keitzad Tzolin Rashi explained: "the heart — we tear it, we do not tear it, he does not violate for bird heart, but for animal heart where there is an olive's bulk he violates even though it was already cooked"; and so in chapter Or VeRotav we say "he skimmed the blood and ate it" — Rashi explained "raw and cooked." And they wondered: behold we say in the Gemara Menachot daf 21a that blood in cooking and blood in salting one does not violate — therefore they ruled the opposite, that in truth one does not violate. And according to their words it is not understood to challenge Rashi, for Rashi follows his line that Rava sat and stated that law that blood in cooking one does not violate, and Abaye challenged from a baraita that teaches "he skimmed the blood and ate it — he is liable," and answers: here in heat that returns, here in raw that does not return; and the Gemara challenged: in raw, even though it returns, let us say since it was rejected, R' Yochanan said coagulated blood — since it was rejected, let it be rejected; and Rashi explained: he thought even in non-sacred animals (one should understand why he bothered to challenge from this baraita "he skimmed the blood and he is liable" on Rava and did not challenge on exempting coagulated blood even in non-sacred animals according to Rashi; and in the baraita he is liable for coagulated blood. And one may say exempt, for R' Yochanan cited karet, for we require blood fit for sprinkling of holy offerings, as Rashi wrote s.v. blood in cooking; and what we teach "he skimped and ate" is lashes, for there is no case where it would be less than blood of limbs that are not in the category of sprinkling and not for atonement and sprinkling, nevertheless they are under a negative commandment; and the same for coagulated blood; but to Ze'iri he challenged well, for he said one does not violate, implying there are no lashes either; and why he afterward challenged from R' Yochanan is not on the body of the law, only for proof that one should not distinguish between skimming in heat and skimming in cooking, for R' Yochanan exempts even coagulated though it returns, because once rejected it is rejected). And the Gemara answers: here in the inner sin-offering, here in the outer sin-offering — Rashi explained: what we teach "he skimmed the blood — he is liable" is in the outer sin-offering, and the same in non-sacred animals, for they are fit for atonement; and what Ze'iri said one does not violate is in the inner sin-offering; and on this the Gemara gives the reason for R' Chiya's view, who distinguishes between inner and outer, for in this it is written "and he shall take and place," and in this "and he shall dip and sprinkle and skim," etc., they are not fit for sprinkling; and Rava on his own says even in the inner sin-offering if one ate it he is liable, since corresponding to them it is fit in the outer sin-offering — perforce if so, the conclusion is Ze'iri who said one does not violate is according to R' Chiya who distinguishes inner from outer — he spoke of the matter in the inner, but not in the outer, and all the more in non-sacred animals; and for Rava who does not distinguish inner from outer, one is always liable, and all the more in non-sacred animals as we teach "he skimmed," etc., he is liable, and Ze'iri is not correct at all, and one is always liable. And see Tosefta printed in the Gemara, clear explicitly for Rashi's explanation, that Rava argues on Ze'iri who said one does not violate, and he is the later authority. Therefore, what is all the noise from the Acharonim and Pri Chadash among them on Rashi from this Gemara — for according to Rashi the matter is clear that in non-sacred animals all agree one violates, and Rava disagrees on the entire matter of Ze'iri, and there is no wonder on Rashi; and Tosafot who disagree with Rashi on this follow their line, for they challenged there in Tosefta on Rashi: how does Rava contradict things Abaye said in his name; and also what is the Gemara's difficulty, why need a verse, derive for me blood that was salted one does not violate; and what is the difficulty — for even if salted it does not immediately coagulate and remains clear, and therefore still fit for sprinkling and we read "and he shall sprinkle"; therefore they explained the distinction between inner and outer sin-offering is with clear and coagulated blood, but for blood that was salted or cooked, all agree it is disqualified. And in truth, for an understanding person the Tosafot's challenge on Rashi is easily resolved: what they challenged on the Gemara, why need a verse that one may not salt holy blood, derive blood that was salted — they challenged that to answer say it deals with clear blood, and this is not a difficulty, for Rashi himself explained blood that was salted in both non-sacred and holy — they are no longer fit; and although Rashi himself distinguishes sin-offerings that are eaten from those not eaten, implying Rashi holds this is all b'dieved, that he does not descend to distinguish between "and he shall take" and "and he shall sprinkle," and all are exempt, and if so the whole preceding sugya is according to him that he does not distinguish sin-offering from sin-offering and he did not hold the difference between "and he shall take" and "and he shall sprinkle," and if so it is obvious one should not distinguish coagulated from clear, and the Gemara challenged well why need a verse to exclude salting from blood; but for the conclusion that we have newly taught in the Gemara a difference between eaten and not eaten sin-offerings, for in this it is written "and he shall sprinkle," etc., it is obvious there is no place for the Gemara's challenge. And in truth, for the conclusion all the above is rejected, and it is obvious that for Rashi the Gemara's challenge why need a verse to exclude salting from blood does not apply to the conclusion, for it was needed for eaten sin-offerings; and perforce one must say it applies according to the view before the distinction was established; if so, Tosafot's challenge is resolved. And what they challenged, Rava on Rava, or that which Rava said is his own and his teacher's — one is his and one his teacher's, and it is common in Shas and Tosafot that we have the text Rava in place of Rabbah; if so, there is no challenge on Rashi. And there are clear indications for Rashi from that which we establish for him in tractate Keritut on bird heart that there is not an olive's bulk in its blood — it is strained to say therefore he spoke of raw heart not cooked; also proof from that which we derive in Pesachim and Nazir taste like essence from gentile cooking, and Tosafot challenged: whence for Nazir, perhaps for Nazir there is heter for its issur; and answer: even for Nazir the Torah commanded to kasher, and still ask: whence blood, perhaps there is heter for its issur if cooked; and needless to say Nazir proves, for also for Nazir there is heter for its issur, nevertheless forbidden as Tosafot wrote: what is Nazir to us that there is heter joining to issur from "his attendant" — unlike blood; and from this it is proven that for the conclusion blood in cooking is forbidden. Also for Rashi proof from that which we say Chullin daf 113b: to Shmuel, if so two kids remain, for Shmuel holds forbidden chelev and milk and nevelah come from one verse, blood too is not a kid, etc., and Tosafot challenged: what of it that blood is not a kid, on the contrary for this a kid is needed — see there. And behold, according to Rashi's explanation that issur chal al issur we derive in Bava Kama from this kid that Scripture includes and we learn from it chelev and nevelah, that from both of them issur applies — which do you exclude, for they are from the category of kid; and one may say thus the Gemara explains: blood too is not a kid, and if so issur chal al issur does not apply to them; and if you ask, to reconcile — but Tosafot follow their line that they hold blood in cooking is permitted d'oraisa, and if so there is no issur chal al issur in Torah, and needless as they challenged, and challenged well; therefore Rashi answered Tosafot's question, for he holds blood in cooking one violates; and if you ask, also Rambam's view is like Rashi, and so Rif, who copied this statement on "tear the heart," dealing with bird heart; and they wondered what difference for law — he copied it only to teach that animal heart one violates, for blood in cooking is forbidden d'oraisa, and therefore he did not copy anywhere that blood in cooking is d'rabbanan; and so Rosh did not copy anywhere that blood in cooking is d'rabbanan; and so Rosh, from his words in several places he is uncertain regarding blood in cooking, and Mahari Abohav copied Rambam's language in Bava Kama: one does not violate because of Bava Kama, implying one violates because of blood; therefore the Acharonim adopted this as primary, that blood in cooking is only d'rabbanan issur — for law requires further study.
