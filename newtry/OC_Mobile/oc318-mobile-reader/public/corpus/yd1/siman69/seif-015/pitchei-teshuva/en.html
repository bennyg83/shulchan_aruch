Be'er Heitev [and see Peri Megadim above siman 68 in Shaarei Tzedek s.k. 26 who wrote a question: three pots boiling, kli rishon from one — he poured on a quarter of the bird before final rinsing, and there is another quarter that was rinsed from its melicha, etc.; answer: what they flayed through irui is inferior, etc. — see there. And see in Beit Yehudah there who disagrees and what he wrote] in the name of Taz — incident of a woman, etc. And see in Sha'ar HaMelekh chapter 10 of laws of mikvaot law 6 in general rules of rabbinic doubts, rule 7, what he challenged on Taz, and what I wrote in siman 110 in laws of double doubt, letter chaf, and so on. And in responsum Shaviv, the Gaon R' Shabsai Katz, manuscript, wrote that he permitted in an incident that came before him — a woman who forgot if she salted the other side, for even Shach who forbids if salted on one side after rinsed, and likewise Nekudat HaKesef is stringent if she forgot if she salted — one may say in such a case permitted, for there the reason is a major act how she forgot; but in such a case, on the contrary, since she salted one side, the matter is habitual that she does — end of his words. And see in responsum Toldot Yitzchak end of siman 80. And see in responsum Orach L'Chaim, Panim Me'irot part 1 siman 11, asked regarding a woman who sent meat to her husband for Yom Kippur and did not mention in the letter if she salted the meat, and the husband cooked the meat without melicha — if permitted to eat the meat; he showed face to here and to there and concluded his heart wavered on this matter to permit the meat unless some indication joined proving she certainly salted, such as the messenger could not come except close to Shabbat so there was no time to salt and cook for Shabbat — then we say she certainly salted before she sent — see there. And see in responsum Shaviv part 2 siman 69 who disagrees with Peri Megadim in what he wrote such as the messenger could not come, etc., for this is not an indication that proves, perhaps the sender's intent was to salt some time before honoring Shabbat and to insulate it close to Shabbat or to roast on a spit through a little melicha that needs not so much time; and otherwise, since it is something that can be clarified, there is no side at all to permit through doubt, and even in double doubt one should be stringent — and see there what he further wondered on Peri Megadim in that responsum.
