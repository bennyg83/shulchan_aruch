And after they roasted it, do not cook it. Be'er Heitev; and in Shemesh Tzedakah siman 37 regarding meat that remained three days and immediately about half an hour after the three days ended they tore it into many tears and rinsed it, and some soaked it like other meat soaking and salted it, and after salting and second rinsing the redness goes and whiteness comes — Heaven forbid to permit cooking it, even if they salted it many times and the blood is clean and white — do not pay attention to this — see there. And see there one should not rule heter to cook after roasting, and likewise not permit salting after roasting and soaking it many days in an unperforated vessel, for kevush is like cooking, and therefore do not leave it many days such as (through smoking), for we are concerned lest he forget and cook it — see there. And see there the reason forbidden to cut meat that remained three days fine fine and mix fat and spices in it to fill pastries from it to eat them roasted on coals or buried in hot coals wrapped in paper or without paper, for intestines are hard and do not hold and push it out and the meat inside becomes as if cooked in a vessel and forbidden; and also there is reason to decree lest he come to cook from them, for we forbid to leave it, etc. — see there; and see Machatzit HaShekel that in his letter to David his view is to permit in such a case, and wrote on it that Even HaEzer disagreed with him and forbade; and regarding the essential law he already ruled in Shemesh Tzedakah, and other great ones that one should not leave them — see there. And see in Teshuvat Chen siman 20, who extended on the words of Ohr HaChaim and the Mechaber and concluded one should not budge from Ohr HaChaim's ruling that without piercing three days, slight rinsing does not help; and if he did not soak at all, forbidden to cook, only roast; and if he cooked, be lenient b'dieved; and if he pierced within three days, even if he gave slight rinsing within three days, permitted to cook; and all this after it already remained; but l'chatchila there is a difference if it is pierced — proper that he soak it well; and if not pierced, forbidden l'chatchila even if he soaks many hours in water, since the sages of Austria forbid even b'dieved, we have no permission to be lenient l'chatchila — see there, who brought from Torat Yekutiel that his view is that even b'dieved one should not be lenient even if pierced, only through slight soaking; and when not pierced, even through slight soaking one should not be lenient; and he brought proofs and it is not compelled; one who is stringent should be stringent for himself and not for others — see there. And see there siman 21, who wrote that in such a case where it remained and he passed water over it in mere pouring, we are stringent; nevertheless in slight loss it is permitted to cook it after roasting — see there. And what Be'er Heitev wrote that Taz wrote in the name of Rashal on the meat, etc. — see in Noda BiYehudah, who challenged this; and what he wrote regarding salted with other meat, etc. — behold Taz wrote a posek permitted and it seems he erred, etc.; and behold I found his brother the Gaon Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi, may his memory be blessed, in his responsa, who permits because of kebol'o kach polto, etc.; and what Mei Yisrael wrote general rule 14, that according to what Rama ruled to be lenient b'dieved if he cooked after roasting, etc. — see there, explained so in the words of Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi — see there. And see in Chiddushei HaRamban that his view is to be lenient in these laws, that Taz wrote according to this being only stringency of the Geonim — see there. And see in my responsa Beit Aleph siman 27: meat that remained three days that was salted with poultry meat together, and after salting and rinsing they cooked them together in a barrel and salted them as usual for long preservation and it was kevush thus many days — forbid even what is outside the brine because of taste, etc. — see there regarding taste. And see Radbaz part 2 siman 138; and see Chiddushei HaRamban that one may permit fat that remained three days without salting, that after salting as its law one cooks the fat in water that has sixty, since in great loss we hold that piece is permitted; and pour the water and this is not nullifying issur; and further that the fat returning to absorb from the water is not nullification of issur, for absorption comes after nullification; and further that our intent is only to liquefy the fat somewhat like siman 84; but to mix it with other fat it seems forbidden — see there. And at first glance according to the latter reason, even to mix with other fat it would be permitted if it is not his own fat, for permitted; on the contrary, somewhat better, for water is not the way to liquefy fat thus, it appears intended to nullify issur, unlike other fat that is not his own, whose way is to liquefy all his fat together and it does not appear as nullifying issur. However, it seems without the second reason alone it suffices to permit, for there he does not add to it only the quantity it has because of the fat, and likewise in baking bread from wormy flour already nullified — only that it did not come to a practical ruling in this; we say his intent is not to nullify. And similarly here if kosher fat absorbed blood and it is known there is sixty in the fat against the blood, although taste still forbids, permitted to liquefy it, for the intent is only to liquefy and not to nullify; and so in other issurim that fell into sixty, permitted to cook afterward; but nevertheless, when less than sixty fell, that it would be permitted to add from outside and say his intent is not to nullify — we never heard this; and although blood that was salted is only d'rabbanan and also blood in cooking, nevertheless Rama already wrote siman 99 that even to add in d'rabbanan is forbidden. And this is clear: if we say that in such a case it is permitted l'chatchila to nullify, then according to Tur and Mechaber siman 92 the piece itself returns to being permitted — there would be heter l'chatchila to cook a piece that absorbed issur in water that has sixty against the issur, and we say since anyway the way to cook meat is not intent to nullify but to cook the meat; or one may say that although he does it as he was, he makes nullification through the addition, for through this addition he innovates the act of nullification — there is concern of ein mevatlin; and see Chullin daf 100. However, when he liquefies in water and pours the water, it seems this is not nullifying issur, for this is like hagalah that permits because the intent is only to expel the issur and not to nullify it; and see Taz, Orach Chaim siman 452, and Pri Chadash siman 182, that what was nullified through expulsion in hagalah we do not care, since he does not benefit from it; nevertheless requires further study, for granted that piece is permitted because of what Taz wrote regarding meat cooked without salting, that what spread exited outside; nevertheless it is obvious that also in that piece remains part of the taste of blood that spread proportionally, only it is permitted because it was nullified in sixty, and this is b'dieved, but l'chatchila it does not leave the category of ein mevatlin; and in this, absorption after nullification — only at the time of nullifying the fat it takes its share of blood and is nullified in it — see there in Chiddushei HaRamban in explanations, who wrote do not say that according to Taz's reason, because it did not spread, he gives reason that it is taste of blood that did not spread — see there. And this is regarding taste of blood that did not spread; but blood that spread, which is the piece of issur, the meat receives taste at the time of separation from it; except that it was squeezed to the pot and only less than sixty remains — permitted; and in any case l'chatchila it should have been forbidden (and see Chiddushei HaRamban, who wrote according to the Mechaber even if it fell into another pot in less than sixty it does not forbid, for once squeezed its taste was weakened and weak taste of permitted does not forbid, for there is no name of taste on the essence; but here in blood of limbs that we say even if it did not spread from its place in the first pot it will spread in the second pot, this reason does not apply, for it is substance and substance is not squeezed through sixty; and since it now spread and there is not sixty in it, forbidden; and see Mishbetzot, who forbids too). However, Taz's answer on the Mechaber is astounding, for if ordinary blood were absorbed in the piece and fell into sixty, the piece would remain in its issur, for we say what did not exit — see Taz siman 106; and what is better about this blood, and who says it did not spread from it — perhaps most blood in it spread or all of it and part exited and part did not; and to say the nature of cooking is that what is aroused to exit, all exits, therefore in ordinary blood or issur we say perhaps remains inside what was not aroused to exit at all through cooking — whence do we have this? All the more for Rashba, who holds even what expelled outside, weak kafila taste and there is no issur taste — nevertheless forbidden, for there remains in it slight taste from this blood that separated, taste of issur; and so in blood from elsewhere; and certainly so. If so, what is better about blood of the piece; therefore the Mechaber's view seems like Rosh, who holds spreading place to place within the piece is permitted for us; if so, here too one may permit the fat through cooking in water that has sixty and pour the water, not because of Chiddushei HaRamban's reason, for according to Taz one should not permit except in great loss — we rely on Rosh; and if so, there is no issur here, for he does not intend to nullify, only to expel blood from inside the piece into the water and pour the water like hagalah water; and if remains inside, even if it spread inside place to place, permitted according to Rosh; and one may rely in great loss, especially here where the fat was salted as its law and there is only stringency of Geonim and also blood that was salted or cooked is d'rabbanan; and the essence issur ein mevatlin l'chatchila — the poskim agreed it is d'rabbanan; if so, do not be stringent here; but one should not rule thus for everyone, only for one who knows to be careful still that there be sixty in the water against the fat and not use the water for eating needs, only pour them — so in my humble opinion; and it is a simple matter that the posek who permits heter here must consider he does not lose his reward through firewood expense, for when he wants to do work in much water to cook the fat and there will be sixty times its quantity, he will certainly need to waste much firewood, for the water will go to loss; and if after all the trouble and firewood expense only a slight amount remains of the fat's equivalence, obviously the posek should not permit doing so, for the entire basis of this heter is built on great loss, that in great loss one may rely on the Mechaber that in sixty the piece itself is permitted, unlike when here is only saving a little — obviously one should not do so, and we do not find this heter except in a place where wood and trouble are cheap and fat expensive.
