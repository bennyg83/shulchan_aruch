Thus Semak: if an idolater serves in an Israelite's house and placed meat in a pot and we do not know if they rinsed it — not believed in masiach lefi tumo according to Peri To'ar; and all the more so if they know Jewish customs; and if there is a lad or maiden who knows the matter of rinsing, or an Israelite going in and out — permitted because he is afraid, etc. — end of his words. And all the great later authorities strained his wording as Taz wrote. And see in responsum Noda Biyehudah part Even HaEzer end of siman 31 who wrote to explain Semak's words with good reason: it is clear that the idolater is believed on a rabbinic prohibition where there are presumptive grounds, even if not masiach lefi tumo; and proof from what is written below siman 187 seif 8 that if it is recognizable the remedies helped, one may rely even on an idolater even if not masiach lefi tumo, and there it is rabbinic prohibition; and since so, here blood that was salted is rabbinic. And behold, if he knows their Jewish customs, called presumptive grounds to act like the custom since he serves in an Israelite's house; if so, three distinctions in the matter: if masiach lefi tumo — even if he does not know their customs and there are no presumptive grounds, believed because of masiach lefi tumo; if he knows their customs — presumptive grounds and believed even if not masiach lefi tumo; and possible that presumptive grounds with testimony of an idolater even if not masiach lefi tumo is preferable to masiach lefi tumo where there are no presumptive grounds; therefore Semak wrote the wording "and all the more so." And where there is a minor of understanding going in and out and the idolater knows their customs — permitted and not required even that the idolater say at all that he rinsed; and without his statement we say presumably from fear he certainly rinsed; unlike if no Israelite going in and out — even if he knows their customs, nevertheless not permitted presumptively, only if he says explicitly that he rinsed, even if not masiach lefi tumo. And with this Semak's wording is explained well — end of his words, see there.
