(Seif 10) A gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. Shach wrote in the name of Pe'er HaAdam that regarding his slave and maidservant fear applies; it seems that if he is not his slave, fear does not apply since he is not his slave. And so too Pe'er HaAdam wrote explicitly. And it is not clear, for it is evident plainly in the Gemara in the first chapter of Chullin regarding inspection of the knife for Kutim, and in Avodah Zarah regarding "the guard need not sit and watch" but even though he goes out and comes in it is permitted — that even for all gentiles who are not his slaves, fear applies when going out and coming in. And so it seems from the language of Hagahot Maimoniot in Hilchot Maachalot Assurot chapter 6 halacha 10. And in the laws of Tefillin it is brought in Orach Chaim siman 32 seif 10. And so in Mordechai, chapter Kol HaBasar siman 1266. And so I found explicitly in Shach siman 118, and he rejected the words of Ohr HaChaim who explained that it speaks only of his slave. And may God set me on the path of truth, for before me came sixty copies of Lechem HaPanim and I saw that he copied Shach's words and added at the end the word "forever"; and he meant that for his slave and maidservant that he acquired, fear applies forever even without going out and coming in; but Shach never intended at all to say that for one who is not his slave fear does not apply when going out and coming in. And this is clear. And what Turei Zahav wrote — a gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. — it seems at first glance that if he is not serving, behold it is forbidden in any case because of bishul akum. But for his slave and maidservant he is not forbidden according to one view that the Mechaber brought siman 113 seif 4. However, it is strained to explain thus the words of Tur, for he did not distinguish between his slave and maidservant and other gentiles. Also the words of the Mechaber are somewhat strained, for he brought two views in this siman 113. And one must say that therefore he wrote "a gentile serving in a Jewish home": if he is not serving, presumably he does not know the custom of Israel and fear does not apply, as the hagahah wrote. And what there is to infer — behold in any case it is forbidden because of bishul akum — it seems to me it deals where it was not yet cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, and then stirring by a Jew helps for all; unlike if it was cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, where stirring by a Jew does not help again according to Rashba that Tur brings there. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 9 — see there. And this is that they inferred Turei Zahav and Shach and wrote "and there the meat is in the pot," meaning it was not yet cooked at all, even like ben drusai, only that he placed the meat in the pot and it is forbidden because it was cooked without final rinsing. For even in kli sheni the hagahah in seif 9 forbids. And even boiling did not cook it in this like ben drusai, as is clear in Ashiri, chapter Kirah siman 11 — see there. And requires further study in siman 17. And it seems to me that in this the words of Beit Yosef are correct, who wrote that here it is only a d'rabbanan matter of blood that was salted, and Darkei Moshe wondered why he needed to say it is blood that was salted, for behold it is cooked before us — see there. And according to what I wrote, one must say it deals here where it was not yet cooked like ben drusai, for otherwise it would be forbidden because of bishul akum. And since it was not cooked like ben drusai, this is not blood in cooking, for blood in cooking is not such unless it was cooked like ben drusai. And proof for the matter from chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 112) regarding Bei Dugi, who challenged there "perhaps the lower reaches the upper" — what is he challenging, behold it is a d'rabbanan safek? Rather it is forced that if it did not reach, meaning it was not cooked like ben drusai, it is forbidden d'oraisa and it is a d'oraisa safek lechumra. And one may reject per what Chavot Da'at wrote, that even when its cooking rises we repair and extract from any safek, even d'rabbanan, as Mahari Abohav wrote in siman 10 and other Acharonim. However, for one who does not know the repair, it is not for him to forbid from safek. And so it is clear in Tosafot chapter Keitzad Tzolin (daf 111) s.v. dam, that it is d'oraisa. And so in responsum Beit Yaakov siman 172. And this is not like Ohr HaChaim general rule 6 siman 8, who wrote that before one-third roasting there is only d'rabbanan. At least according to Turei Zahav it must deal here where it was not cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai. However, the hagahah there seif 9 ruled that for his slave and maidservant he is not forbidden because of bishul akum, and that stirring by a Jew helps even after a gentile cooked it like ben drusai. And it is possible that although the meat was not yet cooked like ben drusai, the fatliquor of blood and salt was cooked. And as Rosh wrote, chapter Kirah siman 17.
