(5) However if we know the butcher rinsed, we say presumably he rinsed well. Shach there. Kenat Hagedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef seif 59; Peri Chadash there; Beit Lechem Yehudah there; Zivchei Tzedek seif 3 — and wrote that in Baghdad they do not have custom for the butcher to rinse at all; the main point is that in their days the butcher was the one who flays the meat, but among us the butcher does not know flaying, and therefore butchers do not rinse before flaying — what use is rinsing — see there. And in our holy city Jerusalem, although they sell flayed meat, butchers do not practice to wash the meat; rather the buyer, after he brings it home, washes and salts it.
