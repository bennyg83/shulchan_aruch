The water. (And it is not considered as hot to forbid the meat.) Shach wrote: not specifically water — the same applies to gravy; and he mentioned water to exclude where there is neither water nor gravy but only wiping of forbidden blood alone, for then salt returns and is absorbed in the meat and is forbidden in any case to the depth of a peel — see seif 20:
Slight. Shach wrote in the name of Orchot Chaim: even if a little gravy is found within the bowl it is permitted in a short time; and if actual gravy is found in the vessel it is forbidden immediately:
Treifah. Shach wrote: and it implies that even if the vessel is a day old it is permitted when it is not soiled (but if soiled there is no permission unless there was a little water in the vessel, as he concludes afterward):
Soiled. Taz wrote that in any case a little water is needed in the vessel; and Rashal ruled that even if the vessel is a milk pot soiled with remnants of dairy food, even if moist, it does not forbid unless actual dairy filth remained in it:
