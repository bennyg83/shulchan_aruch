Meat that was salted and remained for the measure of melicha and afterward was placed in a vessel without rinsing and became full of tzir — permitted; and according to this, meat that fell into tzir emerging from the meat after it remained for the measure of melicha is permitted; and there is one who forbids in this and in that, and one should be concerned l'chatchila. {Rama: And some forbid (Tosafot and Rosh and several later authorities) even b'dieved as much as a peel (hagahot of Shach Devarim and in Shaarei Dura and Mahariv and Maharash and Beit Yosef and HaAroch). And so is the custom; and specifically what is placed inside the tzir; but what is outside the tzir is permitted; and the vessel into which that tzir fell is forbidden; and likewise if the vessel was a non-Jewish vessel and has residue, the meat is forbidden, for even after the measure of melicha it is considered boiling (hagahot of Shach Devarim and Teshuvot HaRosh siman 159). But if it is great loss and there is also need of a festive meal, there is to be lenient and say that after it remained the measure of melicha its tzir is not considered boiling (another opinion, and see below siman 91). }And according to his words, even though the meat remained in salt for the measure, forbidden to eat from it until he rinses it very well; and forbidden to cut from it with a knife before rinsing; and if he cut, he must kasher it. {Rama: And some permit cutting with a knife after it remained the measure of melicha, for melicha does not apply to vessels, and so is the essential law that it is permitted (Teshuvot HaRosh siman 152 and Orchot Hayim general rule 37). But the knife requires rinsing afterward or sticking in the ground if the tzir dried on it (HaAroch). And even before the measure of melicha the prohibition is not because of the knife but because visible blood came on the meat; and if he wishes to rinse there well and return and salt there, permitted. }And when they wish to make preserved meat to keep after it remained in its salt in a perforated vessel for the proper measure, they rinse it very well and afterward return and salt it so it will keep; and even in a non-perforated vessel; and even according to this view, if he wishes to salt and eat roasted without rinsing he may do so and need not be concerned for blood on the salt, for fire draws it and prevents the salt from absorbing blood — and these matters are when he salts and raises it to roast; but if it remained in its salt the salt absorbs blood and is forbidden; therefore he rinses it very well and roasts and eats (and see below siman 76 regarding meat that was salted and not rinsed and was roasted thus).}
