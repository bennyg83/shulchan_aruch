(Seif 10) A gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. Shach wrote in the name of Pe'er HaAdam that regarding his slave and maidservant fear applies; it seems that if he is not his slave, fear does not apply since he is not his slave. And so too Pe'er HaAdam wrote explicitly. And it is not clear, for it is evident plainly in the Gemara in the first chapter of Chullin regarding inspection of the knife for Kutim, and in Avodah Zarah regarding "the guard need not sit and watch" but even though he goes out and comes in it is permitted — that even for all gentiles who are not his slaves, fear applies when going out and coming in. And so it seems from the language of Hagahot Maimoniot in Hilchot Maachalot Assurot chapter 6 halacha 10. And in the laws of Tefillin it is brought in Orach Chaim siman 32 seif 10. And so in Mordechai, chapter Kol HaBasar siman 1266. And so I found explicitly in Shach siman 118, and he rejected the words of Ohr HaChaim who explained that it speaks only of his slave. And may God set me on the path of truth, for before me came sixty copies of Lechem HaPanim and I saw that he copied Shach's words and added at the end the word "forever"; and he meant that for his slave and maidservant that he acquired, fear applies forever even without going out and coming in; but Shach never intended at all to say that for one who is not his slave fear does not apply when going out and coming in. And this is clear. And what Turei Zahav wrote — a gentile serving in a Jewish home, etc. — it seems at first glance that if he is not serving, behold it is forbidden in any case because of bishul akum. But for his slave and maidservant he is not forbidden according to one view that the Mechaber brought siman 113 seif 4. However, it is strained to explain thus the words of Tur, for he did not distinguish between his slave and maidservant and other gentiles. Also the words of the Mechaber are somewhat strained, for he brought two views in this siman 113. And one must say that therefore he wrote "a gentile serving in a Jewish home": if he is not serving, presumably he does not know the custom of Israel and fear does not apply, as the hagahah wrote. And what there is to infer — behold in any case it is forbidden because of bishul akum — it seems to me it deals where it was not yet cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, and then stirring by a Jew helps for all; unlike if it was cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai, where stirring by a Jew does not help again according to Rashba that Tur brings there. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 9 — see there. And this is that they inferred Turei Zahav and Shach and wrote "and there the meat is in the pot," meaning it was not yet cooked at all, even like ben drusai, only that he placed the meat in the pot and it is forbidden because it was cooked without final rinsing. For even in kli sheni the hagahah in seif 9 forbids. And even boiling did not cook it in this like ben drusai, as is clear in Ashiri, chapter Kirah siman 11 — see there. And requires further study in siman 17. And it seems to me that in this the words of Beit Yosef are correct, who wrote that here it is only a d'rabbanan matter of blood that was salted, and Darkei Moshe wondered why he needed to say it is blood that was salted, for behold it is cooked before us — see there. And according to what I wrote, one must say it deals here where it was not yet cooked like ben drusai, for otherwise it would be forbidden because of bishul akum. And since it was not cooked like ben drusai, this is not blood in cooking, for blood in cooking is not such unless it was cooked like ben drusai. And proof for the matter from chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 112) regarding Bei Dugi, who challenged there "perhaps the lower reaches the upper" — what is he challenging, behold it is a d'rabbanan safek? Rather it is forced that if it did not reach, meaning it was not cooked like ben drusai, it is forbidden d'oraisa and it is a d'oraisa safek lechumra. And one may reject per what Chavot Da'at wrote, that even when its cooking rises we repair and extract from any safek, even d'rabbanan, as Mahari Abohav wrote in siman 10 and other Acharonim. However, for one who does not know the repair, it is not for him to forbid from safek. And so it is clear in Tosafot chapter Keitzad Tzolin (daf 111) s.v. dam, that it is d'oraisa. And so in responsum Beit Yaakov siman 172. And this is not like Ohr HaChaim general rule 6 siman 8, who wrote that before one-third roasting there is only d'rabbanan. At least according to Turei Zahav it must deal here where it was not cooked in a gentile's hand like ben drusai. However, the hagahah there seif 9 ruled that for his slave and maidservant he is not forbidden because of bishul akum, and that stirring by a Jew helps even after a gentile cooked it like ben drusai. And it is possible that although the meat was not yet cooked like ben drusai, the fatliquor of blood and salt was cooked. And as Rosh wrote, chapter Kirah siman 17.
(There in hagahah gimel) But it is permitted to return and salt meat in it. Or even without perforation if the meat already remained in its salting period and was rinsed — the reason is there is no melicha for vessels, for a vessel does not expel through salt. And what is written here at length is a scribal error, for for the length we do not say there is no melicha for vessels without perforation, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 105 — see there. And one must say Hagahot Sh"D [and requires further study in Shach s.k. 83; and one may say that in other issurim it is different as Taz wrote siman 10]. And Shach wrote that even in earthenware we say thus, that there is no melicha for vessels. And to me requires further study, for it is possible that specifically in a perforated vessel there is no difference between earthenware and other vessels; but without perforation it seems this is in other vessels but not in earthenware, for Tosafot and Rosh chapter Keitzad Tzolin (daf 113) and Mordechai chapter Kol HaShabbat and Hagahot Sh"D wrote there is no melicha for wood. In truth Mordechai in chapter Kol HaBasar wrote in the name of Rabbenu Ephraim that there is no melicha for wood, metal, earthenware, and stone. And so in Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brings siman 70 and siman 105 and siman 137. And so in Shulchan Aruch there seif 4 regarding absorbed vessels of yayin nesech that one may return and salt meat in them — see there, and requires further study. However, even if we say thus, in earthenware as well there is no melicha for vessels; nevertheless it is no worse than cold, for we say in chapter Kol HaShabbat (daf 30): all vessels in which chametz was used cold, one may use for matzah cold. And many commentators explained: specifically chametz was used cold, but if chametz was used hot, forbidden to use for matzah even cold. And this applies to earthenware vessels. And there are two reasons: because earthenware vessels absorb much and expel even when cold, and therefore every cold use requires rinsing. And as Rashba wrote in the name of Rif and Ramban and so the Rav the Maggid chapter 5 of Hilchot Chametz, and so Rosh chapter Keitzad Tzolin, and so in my humble opinion the view of all of them, and Raavad, and Darkei Moshe brings siman 121, who wrote: earthenware bowls in which issur was used hot are forbidden to use even cold for moist things, because it is impossible to rinse them. And see in our words siman 91. And therefore, since it expels its expulsion into the salted meat, and melicha is like rotev for it, the meat is forbidden and rinsing does not help, as stated siman 10 regarding a knife — there one needs a peel. And in my humble opinion what the hagahah wrote, that it is permitted to salt in it meat already salted and rinsed, because there is no melicha for vessels — deals with other vessels and not earthenware. For who will be lenient against Rif and Ramban and Rashba and Hagahot Maimoniot and Rosh and all of them and Raavad, that even b'dieved one should forbid a peel's depth. And there is another reason: since it has no repair through hagalah, we are concerned lest he use it hot. And even for passing use it is forbidden. And so Mei Yisrael wrote general rule 11 s.k. 6 — see there, and my intention is according to his view, with God's help. And see there s.k. 11. And what Shach and Pri Chadash wrote, that presumably there is no sixty in the bowl against the peel — so in my humble opinion it is clear in the Gemara chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 111), that a bowl in which one salted meat is forbidden to eat hot in it. And he concludes that it is impossible to taste — Rashi: because it is forbidden, therefore it has no heter. Rif to Rava: and let him taste with an Aramaean kafila — did we not learn, etc.? He said: when I speak of forbidden, where there is no kafila. And Rif challenged well: why did he decide to say there is no heter for this hot when there is no kafila — behold with kafila one could permit? And from this we learn that by default there is no sixty against what was forbidden from the bowl, namely a peel's depth. For by law of the Gemara certainly melicha does not forbid more, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 105 and Shach. And earthenware is worse. And the law of kafila tasting — see siman 98.
(There seif 20 in the hagahah) And even before the salting period the issur is not because of the knife but because visible blood exited; and if he wishes he may rinse there and return to salt there. From this it is proven that if he cut a piece in two before salting after rinsing, he need not return to soak it, only rinse the place of the cut, as I wrote s.k. 1; and so explicitly in Bedek HaBayit and Lechem HaPanim. And do not challenge: behold it is called visible blood and therefore since blood came on the meat the meat should be forbidden, for we hold end of siman 70 that if visible blood fell on meat during its salting period it is immediately forbidden and there is no measure to the matter. And one may say it is not called visible blood on the absorbed blood in the piece, only for the matter that he must rinse it, for if he salts on it the salt will become full from it and will not expel blood, as Mordechai wrote in the reason for initial rinsing; but for this matter it is not called visible. Rather absorbed wet blood that exits afterward through salting, as I wrote, and examine. And see what I wrote in our words siman 10 s.k. 4.
