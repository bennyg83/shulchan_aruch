Permitted. Shach wrote the reason: gravy exiting from meat after it remained the time of melicha is only mere gravy; and even though the salt on the meat is forbidden because blood is mixed in it — nevertheless it is nullified in the gravy; and Taz challenged the Mechaber from what he wrote in seif 8, if he did not shake off the salt, etc., until 'for water nullifies the power of salt' — it implies that even after it remained the time of melicha specifically water is needed to nullify, otherwise it is considered hot; and one cannot answer that also in seif 8 if it filled with gravy it is permitted, rather it deals where there is only wiping of blood and forbidden salt alone, and therefore water is needed to nullify this — this is not so, for also there it deals after it remained the time of melicha, for all the blood already went and only mere gravy remained, like here; rather it is clear that for Rashi also there there is no prohibition at all, for the power of salt already ceased at the time of expulsion, and the Mechaber did not write there that specifically water is needed to nullify, only for the second view that forbids here it admits there if there is water to nullify that according to all it is permitted; and one may answer for Rashi's view: why do we forbid above if it was cooked without last rinsing — one may answer that in any case the salt is forbidden from blood within it, only it has no power to forbid the meat, and it is like cold blood that adhered, requiring rinsing; and also for Rashi, if he cut with a knife in the meat after the time of melicha, he needs only rinsing since there is only cold here, unlike for those who disagree, hag'alah is needed — end of his words:
Peel. Shach wrote: even though here there is no reason of spread from place to place, since it already remained the time of melicha — nevertheless we do not say iydei d'falit gravy lo bala since it is in a non-perforated vessel; and Maharshal disagrees with this hagahah and forbids everything in the gravy entirely and a little above it to the depth of a peel; and nevertheless he wrote that if that piece mixed with others, even one in two is nullified — in this one may rely on Rashi's view who is lenient in every case; and according to the Rav, sixty does not help to nullify, for wherever peeling is needed we do not nullify in sixty, as written in siman 105; and according to Maharshal, if there is sixty in what is within the gravy against the gravy it is permitted, but what is outside the gravy does not combine to nullify, for blood does not seep upward:
And the vessel. And it deals with a case where it is impossible to peel, for example much gravy fell in it or it is an earthenware vessel, as written in siman 91 seif 5:
Like hot. Shach wrote: it implies from Rama's words that when the vessel is not soiled, even if it is a day old it is permitted; and Taz ruled that if it is a day old, even if not soiled it is forbidden — and likewise Etz Chaim; and Shach challenged this, for Rama wrote in siman 105 seif 12 that if permitted meat was salted in a forbidden vessel, even in a non-perforated vessel it is permitted, for melicha is not so much like hot to expel what was absorbed from the vessel — and Etz Chaim himself copied Rama's words in his book; therefore it appears clear to permit if it is not soiled even if a day old. And what the Rav wrote that the meat is forbidden — he means the whole thing is forbidden if there is not sixty against the filth:
That he rinse it. Shach wrote: even though in this all agree rinsing is needed in the last time, as the Mechaber himself wrote in seif 7 — why did he come to innovate here; and it must be said he informs us that he must rinse before all its gravy finishes expelling, for if he waits until the end we are concerned lest afterward it return and absorb in the meat, as written in siman 70 seif 5:
For vessels. Shach explained: meaning blood m'sharek sharek and is not absorbed in the knife; but certainly there is melicha for vessels regarding that it absorbs well through melicha where there is no reason of m'sharek sharek — and for that reason a non-perforated vessel in which melicha was done is forbidden:
Rinsing. Even though the gravy on it has not yet dried; and Shach wrote: it implies even l'chatchila it is permitted to cut — and it is difficult, for in siman 91 we forbid placing cold permitted in a cold forbidden bowl, lest he eat without rinsing — and here he permits l'chatchila; and it is possible to say that specifically regarding a vessel we are concerned lest he forget to rinse, unlike forbidden on a knife where the prohibition visible on it he will not forget to rinse — therefore permitted even if there are indentations in the knife; and Maharshal ruled l'chatchila one should do hag'alah, and b'dieved permitted if he wiped with something hard; and one who is stringent to forbid without hag'alah — let him examine himself; and specifically for himself and not for others, only one should permit b'dieved even if he did not wipe and cut hot with it; and likewise if he cut in roasting that was not roasted completely he also ruled thus; however with a forbidden knife it implies forbidden l'chatchila to cut meat in it according to all, and b'dieved permitted; and see above siman 105 seif 12 in hagahah:
They rinse. This follows the last view above according to what the Mechaber ruled, that l'chatchila one should be concerned for it:
