And therefore meat that fell into gravy, etc. For it is no longer considered hot; and one may answer from what Tur wrote above, and there is no difference between fine salt and coarse salt, for water in the vessel nullifies the power of salt, etc. — appears without water it is hot even after the measure of melicha passed; one may say there he wishes to permit even against Rashi's practice who forbids, as the Mechaber wrote below — nevertheless he agrees above since water nullifies salt, and Tur wrote for clarity that it is according to all; and here he wrote 'and they permitted' Rashi — meaning this is not according to all; and Maharach explained Rashi's incident: because molten blood is permitted, and even though moisture of blood and salt on the meat mixed in it, molten blood nullifies the power of salt and it is not considered hot — what difference water, what difference molten blood; and Tur above comes only to exclude where there is neither water nor molten blood, only pressing pure forbidden blood and salt — forbidden, etc.; and this is not correct, for there too it deals after the measure of melicha, and all blood already left and what exits afterward is also molten blood, like here. And what he wrote in Rashi's reason that molten blood nullifies salt — certainly not so, for Rashi is not permitted through nullification, for then how did he compare it to water; rather it is clear for Rashi there is no prohibition at all, for the power of salt already ceased at the time of expulsion, as we said in the name of Rosh and Semag, or because it dried, or because salt's power ceased — no need to nullify at all; and do not challenge Rashi: why need final rinsing after melicha, and why they forbade above if cooked without final rinsing — no question at all, for certainly salt is forbidden because of blood inside, only it has no power to forbid the meat since it dried and is like cold blood adhering, needing rinsing, and there is nothing hot to absorb it in the meat, only molten blood, and it has no power to absorb in meat as water has no power — and on this they said in Rashi's name: what difference molten blood, what difference water; and therefore for Rashi if he cuts with a knife after the measure of melicha, he need only rinse, since there is only cold — unlike those who disagree below, who require hag'alah; and Beit Yosef wrote well: Rashi's incident disagrees with Iggeret R' Yonah whom Tur brings, not Maharach's view who wrote even for Rashi the prohibition is in the knife piece like R' Yonah, since there is prohibition of blood and salt and no molten blood to nullify — this is not so: Rashi's intent is not that molten blood makes permitted through its nullification of the prohibition, rather his intent is molten blood causes no prohibition and cold remains on the meat:
After it remained the measure of melicha — permitted. This is Rashi's incident; and the reason: because of the gravy we do not forbid it, for it is not blood, only molten blood of the meat, for after it remained the measure they rinse it to cook in a pot and are not concerned for gravy expelled from it; and although salt on the meat is forbidden because blood is mixed in it and therefore one must rinse the meat well — nevertheless that blood (that was salted) [in the salt] adheres in the salt and dries inside and has no power to absorb in the meat — otherwise every meat placed in water to rinse would be forbidden, for salt melts and blood in it is absorbed in the meat — what difference water, what difference molten blood, both are permitted; rather certainly blood of the salt dried inside and has no power to exit — so Tosafot and Rosh; Rosh further wrote alternatively: salt's power ceased because it expelled the blood, and because of that salt it is no longer considered hot; and one may distinguish R' Yonah who fell into a pitfall: if the pit was considered hot when very salty until inedible because of salt, even though the cook was salted long before — there salt's power did not cease; and so Semag — the second answer of Rosh we mentioned; and he wrote on it it is a fine reason; and through this answer Torat HaBayit siman 159 ruled, and Rama brought it nearby seif 7: one salted a goose and it was found treifah, and after the measure of melicha they placed it with kosher meat, and ruled there: since it is need of a mitzvah meal and also great loss, one may say since it already remained the measure of melicha it is no longer considered hot, for salt's power already ceased; but without great loss he wrote to be stringent, since Semag himself wrote meat and cheese touched when moist, if both salted and not dry, both forbidden — appears even after the measure of melicha; and also Sefer HaTerumah and Shach Devarim and R' Yitzchak of Corbeil hold even afterward it is considered hot — therefore one should not be lenient except in pressing need as above. And behold we can reconcile Semag's words at least so they do not contradict each other: it appears Semag holds we do not say this reasoning that salt's power ceased except when it expelled blood from the meat — unlike meat that already expelled all its blood and was rinsed and afterward they salted it while it exists — certainly considered hot, for that salt did not do the action we say its power ceased; and so the pitfall Rosh and Semag mentioned — therefore Semag wrote well: such salted meat if it touches cheese, it is still always hot as long as it is moist; and therefore we may say all who disagree in Torat HaBayit there also deal with this matter, and there is no dispute at all, and it is fit to rule even without great loss to permit; however from another side there is dispute: Semak brings on Rashi's incident in the name of Rivash: after the measure of melicha it no longer has the name hot regarding blood, but 'hot meat' applies — if that gravy fell on cheese it forbids; and therefore ruled Hagahot Shach Devarim, whom Rama brings seif 7: if he rinsed meat in a soiled dairy bowl — forbidden; and if so also in that treif goose of Torat HaBayit above one should forbid, for nevertheless it was hot meat and forbids the meat; but Rosh and Semag perforce have no such distinction, for otherwise they would not challenge R' Yonah at all; and certainly gravy too is not hot after the measure of melicha, and if any posek holds to distinguish, nevertheless Tosafot and Semag and Rosh in one explanation are fit to rely on them in great loss; and they ruled well — Maharai and Rama; and Rashal ruled above seif 9 this reasoning that salt's power ceased because it expelled blood — and above I brought his words, see there; only in chapter 25 siman 68 he disagrees with Maharai and wrote one should distinguish between meat and salt — and that which they did not answer thus in the pitfall case, because specifically in meat one may distinguish and say salt ceased power after expelling and its boiling power was absorbed in the meat, but in the pitfall one cannot distinguish thus: if salt is not hot, the pitfall is not hot either — perforce everything is hot, end of his words; and fit are Maharai and Rama to rely on them in mitzvah need and great loss, as written there:
K'dei klipah — and so is the custom.
