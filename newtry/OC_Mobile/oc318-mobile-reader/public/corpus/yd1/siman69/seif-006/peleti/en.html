An hour — the Rav, author of Taharat HaAdam, brought this law from that which we say in chapter Makom SheNahagu: on erev Pesach they advance the daily offering an hour so one hour remains free for roasting the Pesach — learn that roasting measure is an hour, and also for salting we estimate like roasting one hour, namely an average hour, for necessarily in Jerusalem day and night were equal and this hour is an average hour. However, the body and root of the matter derived from Pesach is not understood: why need an hour for roasting — it could be roasted on Shabbat itself at night, only time before Shabbat entry stuck in the oven, and Shabbetai has nothing in it, and so explained in the mishnah: they lower Pesach into the oven with darkening. If so, why need an hour time to advance the tamid order — perforce what is cited for roasting is not precise, only for preparing the roast, to gather wood (and they said at the beginning of Pesachim: one hour to gather wood and heat the oven, etc.), and if so perhaps Taharat HaAdam relied entirely; and perhaps Taharat HaAdam holds the hour time for Pesach roasting stated in the Gemara is precisely only what the Sages initially fixed when they fixed time for tamid and Pesach, they did not want to rely on this that Pesach would be roasted on Shabbat and lowered near darkening, only they fixed order and bound one hour time on erev Shabbat for roasting; and this is their statement only if it happened it was not roasted on erev Shabbat while day was still great, permitted to lower even on erev Shabbat with darkening, and not needed; but l'chatchila it was roasted on erev Shabbat and its time was one hour. However, even from this proof it follows Rambam's measure and the others who have changing measures for different mil walking, which is one-third hour, and for Rambam half hour approximately, for roasting that all its blood exits is if roasted like food of the Temple, and so below siman 73 if roasted like food of the Temple again blood exits — and this is clear that Pesach was roasted completely as fit for eating entirely, and as if holy food for thieves — see end of Pesachim that Pesach is written regarding it for anointing and greatness as kings eat, and therefore eaten on satiety — see there Rashba. Therefore if its entire roasting was an hour, then food of the Temple according to authorities who hold food of the Temple is one-third of its roasting — see below siman 112 — and if in one-third hour it was roasted so all blood exits, also in salting so, and one-third hour suffices, and all views flowed from Taharat HaAdam's proof — examine.
