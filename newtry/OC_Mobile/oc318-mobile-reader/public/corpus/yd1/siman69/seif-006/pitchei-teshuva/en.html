Be'er Heitev — what he wrote in the name of Maharshal, meat that hardened, etc. And see in Levit Chen on the Torah, parashat Acharei Mot, who wrote regarding meat that was salted and remained the measure of melicha and was rinsed and afterward ice was found in its middle — it does not help for this meat to return and salt it again after thawing, since the meat around it thawed at the time of melicha and expelled all its blood, melicha will no longer expel the blood inside like in the brain in siman 75, and there is no remedy for this meat except to roast it — end of his words, see there. And see in responsum Har HaKarmel, Yoreh De'ah part, siman 12, who rejected this reason but forbids for another reason — that part of the meat that already remained in its salt properly and its blood exited and was rinsed and the holes of expulsion closed — when they salt again it returns and absorbs from this inner meat — and again he raised that also for this reason one need not forbid — see there. And see in responsum Givat Shaul siman 16 that his view in this law is that if not yet rinsed there is remedy to permit by rinsing beforehand only to remove the moisture of tzir, and then certainly melicha a second time helps even not in a place of great loss; but if already rinsed well, no remedy except roasting; and if already salted again after rinsed as proper complete rinsing, one must forbid even for roasting — see there well. And see in Dagul Meryavah what he wrote on this. And what Be'er Heitev further wrote in the name of Mateh Yehonatan, meat that soaked in water and hardened, etc. — see on this in responsum Devar Shmuel siman 573 that his view is to forbid even if not soaked in ice, only from above; and see in responsum Kiryat Chana siman 29 who raised like Mateh Yehonatan that ice is not considered like water, therefore if not remained in ice, only from above, certainly permitted; and even if remained so three days, also not clear to forbid — see there.
