The Rav's view in Torat Chatat: l'chatchila one should not soak it within three days in order to leave it another three days; but b'dieved, if it already remained three days, it is permitted to cook it even if they only soaked a little within the three days, if it was pierced from the paths of the blood; and likewise in pressing circumstances or a place of great loss one may soak it even l'chatchila and leave it another three days, see there and in Torat Chatat's simanim — so the Rav's view is compelled, examine; and here he omitted all this because he wrote there that in many places they are accustomed to permit even l'chatchila, and in Krakow they practiced thus and he left them their custom; therefore he stated the matter simply here to be clear and plain; but in truth one should not be stringent in these laws, since we find no stringency of meat that remained three days without melicha in the Talmud; and Rivash siman 87 leaned on this, see there; nevertheless l'chatchila one should soak it well when one wishes to leave it another three days, as in Agur and Torat Chatat there:
