Less than half an hour. Be'er Heitev; and see Turei Meir s.k. 1 regarding soaking, and in Orach Chaim siman 321 I wrote in these laws — see there; and see in Dvar Shmuel siman 300, who was uncertain if permitted to return and extend the three days by another soaking, and wrote that since it is not mentioned in Shas one may be lenient in such safekot — see there, and brought briefly in Lechem Kodesesh — see there; and see in Sulam LaMinchah general rule 4, who wrote: and so I heard ruling of elder rabbis to soak meat specifically within three days like half an hour, but mere rinsing — not so, end of his words; and already practiced even l'chatchila through mere rinsing; and it seems one should not be lenient l'chatchila where possible through soaking half an hour, since he wishes to leave it another three days. And once it happened that a butcher took frozen meat from kitchens to his home and it thawed there and three days passed without soaking; however what thawed was within twelve hours, and the posek ruled the meat permitted, for after thawing from freezing and ice it is moist in the water of thawing and is like he rinsed through passing, which is permitted b'dieved; and I agreed with him if the butcher would say he sprinkled on its surface a little, because there was also great loss; nevertheless one should warn the butcher to soak it for half an hour before selling so it softens well, since sometimes the buyer has only an hour's urgency to cook and soaking in passing, and since this already passed three days, although we may rely on thaw water, nevertheless l'chatchila good to soften it now through soaking well — I shortened.
