Hot. Even after the vessel was rinsed. Shach. And what we say 'there is no melicha for vessels' — Beit Yosef explained: what was absorbed does not exit through melicha, but it absorbs well through melicha; and what already absorbed does not exit through melicha except through cooking — therefore it is forbidden to use that vessel with something hot even though melicha is like hot, meaning hot for roasting but not like cooked:
The bowl. Shach wrote: this hagahah requires further study; and it appears at first glance to explain that here it deals when the bowl is hot — therefore if it is a dry thing, a peel suffices; and if he placed in it dry hot that has a little moisture, removal is needed; and if it is a moist hot thing like gravy, sixty is needed against the peel from the bowl even where the gravy did not touch, and sixty is not needed against the whole bowl because initially it was not forbidden through melicha except to the depth of a peel — except it is forced to explain that it deals when the bowl is hot, and also forced to explain that sixty is needed against peel from the bowl even where gravy did not touch, and further forced to say it deals with something completely dry; rather certainly here it deals when it is cold and nevertheless a peel is needed even if he used it with something completely dry; and what he wrote 'and if it is a moist thing, sixty is needed against the peel from the bowl' — that is where he used the bowl. And if the bowl is hot and the meat cold, the lower overcomes, or both hot — sixty is needed against peel from the bowl; and stam stew does not have sixty against the inner peel of the bowl. But it is difficult according to this, for below siman 91 seif 4 Rama wrote: where the meat needs peeling, if they did not peel and cooked thus — permitted b'dieved, and that is because it is not fit to peel — if so, why is sixty needed here against peel of the bowl; and it must be said Rama holds here one should be stringent to require sixty against peel of the bowl, since in any case many poskim hold that after rinsing it is hot from the salt in it, and even though generally we do not hold thus, nevertheless here one should be stringent; and even though we hold that stam vessel has sixty against its peel — it is possible that is specifically in other vessels, but not in a bowl that is wide and not high, presumably there is no sixty — end of his words:
Rinsing. Turei Zahav wrote: in an earthenware vessel it is forbidden to use it without rinsing l'chatchila, and in other vessels it is permitted; however certainly even in other vessels one must wipe them well before use — and likewise Maharshal:
Perforation. Shach wrote: in Derishah he challenged why forbid from the reason of pickled if it remained the time that they place it on fire and it begins to boil, for through gravy the meat becomes pickled — and he left it requires further study; and one may answer: regarding 'there is no melicha for vessels' there is no distinction, for never is there power of salt and gravy to expel a vessel — and it appears even if meat remained in it overnight, at the time of expulsion it is nosen taam lifgam and is no longer a day old; however l'chatchila it is forbidden to leave it, for nosen taam lifgam is forbidden l'chatchila. And Taz explained: below it deals with meat soaked within gravy, unlike here where the vessel does not expel in such a short time this pickling except the measure of ordinary pickling that is overnight; however it is difficult since we hold there is no melicha for vessels to expel what is inside through melicha — all the more so it should be permitted to use it cold, and why did Rama write this law after the view that the Mechaber wrote that even cold it is forbidden; and it appears raw meat and the like are different, for its way is in rinsing it is permitted to leave in a forbidden vessel and we do not say lest he forget and eat without rinsing; but other things, even cold, it is forbidden to use this bowl in which meat was salted, lest he forget to rinse — and the Rav only came to inform us it is not meliach like hot, and it refers to above — and with this it is well explained why there is no contradiction from siman 105 seif 12 that it is forbidden to salt cheeses, etc. — see there:
