Sixty against peel from bowl — see Shach and Pri Chadash, and from their words we learn the bowl is not forbidden, only a peel's depth, for melicha does not forbid more than a peel's depth — needs teaching: behold brine that remained inside long enough to put on fire and boil — if so, it is kevush and forbids all of it as Shulchan Aruch wrote seif 18; and if there is no melicha for vessels, this means to expel, but not to absorb — and one must say kevush does not apply here, for kevush is called when brine surrounds on all sides, unlike here the vessel is surrounded outside by air, only brine inside the vessel and vessel surrounds it — not judged as kevush, only as mere salting — requires further study, for Magen Avraham siman 447 s.k. 97 extended and concluded in salting one should be stringent that if it remained long enough to boil in brine it expels from the vessel, meaning it is kevush even though outside is not kevush; and so if water remained in the vessel beforehand Ohr HaChaim holds it is kevush — see below siman 105; even though vessel walls outside are not kevush, compelled from many places from that which bowl in which one salted meat — why should it be forbidden, behold there is no melicha for vessels, as Rosh wrote regarding cheese molds — perforce must be from kevush reason, and cheese molds deals with holes, no soaking here — requires further study.
Peel — and Shach challenged why require peel from vessel and not peel from hot thing placed in it, ta'tei gavar, as Rama wrote in Torat Chatat — one may say since many authors hold the bowl is judged as hot, for brine was absorbed in it; if so, even for those who hold it is called cold, meaning brine in its place has no power to heat the bowl, nevertheless brine in its place stands, it is boiling, and when pouring boiling into it, obviously heat awakens heat, for this heat is certainly stronger than kli sheni heat, for this is at least kli rishon cooking, and all the more does not cook at all; nevertheless Rama wrote above in kli sheni with brine combination it cooks — if so, all the more in irui it cooks; therefore require peel against the whole vessel, not suffice with food peel — with this Rashi's question is resolved in chapter Keitzad Tzolin daf 111b, bowl in which one salted meat, perhaps taste through kafila, and Rashba challenged: behold it is not forbidden in pouring boiling into it, only a peel's depth from the food — if so, what taste in the matter that requires only a peel; and according to the above it is fine, for the whole food is forbidden and he challenged well on taste, etc. — examine.
Even without perforation — Derishah challenged: if it remained long enough to put on fire, behold it is kevush like cooking — answer: regarding vessel we do not equate brine kevush, for there is no melicha for vessels — this contradicts Magen Avraham's words in siman 447, clear also in vessel we say brine kevush long enough to put on fire — see there; if so, for Magen Avraham Derishah's question stands in its place. And one should understand that which cheese molds of gentiles — reason of those who permit: there are holes; and assumption: those who forbid, sometimes holes are plugged — unperforated vessel — learn in vessel there is soaking, etc., therefore distinguish between plugged holes and unblocked holes, then no soaking — learn kevush applies. Therefore it seems one should distinguish: if piece in salt and brine did not yet drip from it, no issur in vessels, for there is no melicha for vessels; but if brine dripped from it, brine equates it to boiling and it is also kevush in the vessel; and in Shulchan Aruch here it deals where brine did not drip; and one may be stringent; and in any case Magen Avraham's words are correct, for in cheeses where Maharai permitted molds and gave reason all issur absorbed is only d'rabbanan, cheese, etc., impure milk is not common — see Yesh Seder LaHalachah in Keitzad Tzolin; but in Maharai's case, chametz d'oraisa issur, Maharai concedes to forbid — see there. And do not challenge Magen Avraham's lines that in vessel there is soaking — that which we say Chullin 111b, there is no source for Ravin's statement melicha is not like rotev, for this pan in which they salt meat in it broke R' Ami, etc., and challenged: whence for us from Torah of salting it broke, perhaps only from Torah of kevush that they salted in it and remained long enough to put on fire and it is kevush and therefore it broke; and if Ravin also holds kevush is not like cooking, perhaps he holds kevush is not like cooking, only like rotev roasting, and melicha is not like rotev — must say the Gemara knew this pan incident, it did not remain time long enough to put on fire, nevertheless R' Ami broke, and it is clear. And one may say: whence does the Gemara say for Ravin kevush is not like cooking from this pan — it is not proven, only melicha is like rotev; whence for us to reject Ravin's words that it is not cooked; and one may say certainly he holds kevush is not cooking, and how will he disagree several mishnayot that kevush cooks in seeds — must say soaking beforehand he concedes it cooks, but brine kevush long enough to put on fire, etc., he holds it does not cook; and one must say the reason: there is no boiling thing that boils it until it is complete soaking — fine if melicha is like rotev, behold a thing that boils through remaining long enough to put on fire is kevush; but if melicha is not like rotev and it is cold and only because it remained a while it becomes soaking cooked — if so, one depends on another, if melicha is not like rotev, also brine kevush is not like cooking — examine.
