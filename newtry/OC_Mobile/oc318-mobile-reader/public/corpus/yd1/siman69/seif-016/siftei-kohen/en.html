And if he used it, etc. This hagahah requires further study; and after examining well Torat Chatat general rule 11 din 8, it appears to explain: it deals here when the bowl is hot — therefore if it is a dry thing, peeling suffices even if the bowl is hot, since it is dry (and this is when he put dry hot in it that has a little moisture, peeling is needed, as is proven from Rashba's novellae and in Torat HaBayit Ha'aroch, and see siman 94 s.k. 32 and siman 105 s.k. 22); therefore if it is hot moist like gravy, sixty are required against peeling from the bowl (and sixty against the whole bowl are not required, because at first it was not forbidden in melicha except k'dei klipah, as we hold below siman 98 seif 4 in hagahah) even where the gravy did not touch. But it is strained to explain it deals when the bowl is hot, and also strained to explain what he wrote 'sixty against peeling from the bowl' even where gravy did not touch, and further strained to say it deals in something completely dry; and in Torat Chatat there it perforce deals in dry with a little moisture, from what he wrote there: if the bowl is cold, peeling suffices — and he did not explain the second distinction when the bowl is hot, but certainly what he wrote at the end 'and if the bowl is hot' also applies to dry of the first part that has a little moisture, that sixty is needed for us, as siman 105 s.k. 22; but certainly in hagahah too it deals when it is cold — and plainly so — and nevertheless peeling is needed even if he used it for something completely dry, as appears in Torat Chatat general rule 23 din 2, see there — and as Tosafot hold the bowl is cold and nevertheless if he put hot dry in it, peeling is needed, as Rashba explains there; and what he wrote 'and if it is a moist thing we require sixty against peeling from the bowl' means where he used the bowl. And it is difficult on this: below siman 91 seif 4 the Rav wrote where meat needs peeling, if they did not peel and cooked thus, it is permitted b'dieved — meaning because it is not fit to peel — and so Torat Chatat general rule 23 din 5: and he further ruled at length that wherever peeling is required and one transgressed and cooked without peeling — permitted; and if so why is sixty required here against peeling from the bowl? According to my first explanation one may distinguish: we do not say thus except where the meat itself needs peeling alone in what touched — unlike here, where peeling alone in what touched does not suffice, for it was absorbed inside all that is in the bowl's peeling even where gravy did not touch; and further, since the bowl is hot, behold the dish in it is as if cooked in it, as the Rav wrote siman 98 seif 4: a vessel forbidden in melicha k'dei klipah afterward requires sixty against the peeling — and this is not comparable to siman 91 in hagahah, since the bowl's peeling itself forbids because it is hot and stands in existence and peeling applies, as siman 91 s.k. 7; but if we say it deals when the bowl is cold, then peeling is needed because the dish absorbed somewhat before it cooled — then sixty is required against the dish's peeling, and it does not stand in existence and peeling does not apply — examine; and one may say the Rav holds here one should be stringent to require sixty against the bowl's peeling, since in any case some poskim here hold that after rinsing it is hot from the salt in it — Ra'avad and his supporters, whom Rashba and Beit Yosef bring; if so, although in general we hold like Rabbenu Tam, nevertheless here one should be stringent like Rivash, who requires sixty against peeling in general; and so one must reconcile Rashba's words in Torat HaBayit HaKatzar and the Rav the Maggid and Beit Yosef bring, that his words in Torat HaBayit Ha'aroch daf 73 and in his novellae to Chullin (and Beit Yosef brings them) should not contradict — so it appears to me — examine well:
And we require sixty against peeling, etc. Below siman 137 it is proven plainly that a plain vessel has in what is inside sixty against the vessel's peeling; and it is possible this is specifically in other vessels, but in a bowl that is wide and not deep ordinarily there is not sixty — and so appears in Orchot Chaim general rule 36 din 2 that there is not sixty in what is in the bowl against its peeling; and so in Sefer Api Ravrevi beginning daf 60: plain dish — there is not sixty against the inner peeling of the bowl:
And some say even cold it is forbidden to use, etc. And in Torat Chatat general rule 18 din 8 he ruled: in an earthenware vessel it is forbidden l'chatchila to use without rinsing; in other vessels it is permitted — and so Maharshal ruled chapter 25 siman 60; but certainly in other vessels too one must wipe them well before use — as appears in Rashba there, and so Maharshal there:
But permitted to return and salt in it, etc. The reason: there is no melicha for vessels to expel what is inside through melicha — so Orchot Chaim there din 9 wrote the reason in the name of Orchot Chaim; and so in Hagahot Shach Devarim siman 17, whom Beit Yosef brings (and in general rule 17 he ruled thus even in earthenware). However it is difficult: since there is no melicha for vessels to expel through melicha, all the more one may use it cold; and if so why did the Rav write this law after the second view the Mechaber wrote, that even cold it is forbidden? It appears raw meat is different, as Taz wrote siman 91, regarding raw meat and the like whose way is rinsing (meaning before placing in a pot — see siman 70 s.k. 13): permitted to leave in a forbidden vessel and we do not say lest he forget and eat without rinsing, since its way is rinsing; but other things, even cold, are forbidden to use in this bowl in which meat was salted, for since rinsing is needed afterward, if he uses them we are concerned lest he forget to rinse — as Taz wrote there (and see siman 91 s.k. 2); and the Rav did not come to teach that one may return and salt in it, only that melicha is not like hot, and this refers above; and with this it is well resolved why it does not contradict what the Rav wrote siman 105 seif 12, that it is forbidden l'chatchila to salt cheeses in gentile molds, see there:
Or even without perforation, etc. Derishah siman 18 challenged: it should be forbidden from the reason it is pickling if it remained long enough that they place it on fire and it begins to boil, for through the gravy the meat becomes pickled — and he left it requiring further study; and one may distinguish: specifically regarding melicha initially in an unperforated vessel, that the meat is forbidden from the gravy itself, we say thus; but regarding 'there is no melicha for vessels' there is no difference, for salt and gravy never have power to expel a vessel; and it appears even if kosher meat soaked in it from above it is permitted, for although from above it is pickling literally like cooking that expels the vessel, as explained siman 105, nevertheless at the time of expulsion it is nosen ta'am lifgam, since it only expels after from above when it is not a ben yomo, as siman 105 s.s. 2; however l'chatchila forbidden to leave it from above, for nosen ta'am lifgam is forbidden l'chatchila — and as below siman 122.
