and Machatzit HaShekel wrote that in great loss as Taz and Pri Chadash and Peleti one may rely; and so appears Pri Megadim's view; and in Pri Megadim part 1 siman 11 he wrote: a woman sent meat from her city to her husband in another village for Shabbat and did not write in the letter that the meat was salted, and they cooked the meat thus and again a safek arose whether it was salted or not, and some want to compare to what Taz wrote s.k. 24 regarding a woman who forgot, etc., and he rejected this and ruled one may not permit unless with compelling indications that she certainly salted, such as the messenger could only come close to Shabbat so there was no time to salt and cook on Shabbat; however in Shiyurei Berachah part 2 siman 69 he wrote regarding one sent meat on erev Shabbat and stored it in an oven and again remembered perhaps it was not salted, and wrote several reasons to forbid until he asks and clarifies it was salted, because the way is to send before salting so it looks nicer; similar to what we say at the beginning of Ketubot; but putting water on meat does not sell well, also no reason to think it is something that can stand through licking the meat; and although Rashal forbids licking, this is not the law everywhere; and also possible through kafila tasting or rinsing a little visible blood, etc.; and apart from law, how permit something clarifiable after Shabbat, etc.; and not like Pri Megadim, who sided to be lenient that the messenger, etc., is not compelling indication; it is not compelling, for if her intent was to salt, she would have time to salt and store near Shabbat or roast on a spit needing less waiting; and since it will become clear, do not be lenient even in double safek; and so it was in truth, after Shabbat it became clear it was not salted and the delay caused damage through him — and so it seems to me to rule — see there.
