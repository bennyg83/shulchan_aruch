That was cooked — see in Peleti that the same applies in kli rishon even though it is beef — nevertheless the blood was cooked and absorbed.
Without melicha, etc.: if it was cooked and the woman forgot whether she salted and rinsed and extracted blood — Taz's view is to permit; and Shach and Peri Chadash disagreed; and see Peleti that I agreed with Taz, especially since it is d'rabbanan; and see Peleti where I discussed whether not to be stringent and take a piece to taste the piece in doubt, end of his words. Teshuvot Tzitz Eliezer: a woman salted meat and there was a piece of milk and she did not know if the milk was salted with the meat; and he ruled since milk with meat is min b'she'eino mino, and if there is not sixty it is a d'oraisa doubt to be stringent — and Peri Chadash s.k. 44 agreed with him; and see Peleti where I wrote that if he peeled all the flesh it is permitted and need not forbid further; and see Peleti why taste is not needed since according to his view it is min b'she'eino mino; but if fat mixed with milk it is min b'mino and a rabbinic doubt to be lenient — and Peri Chadash challenged this; and see Peleti where I agreed with Tzitz Eliezer.
That meat — and the meat is included in the sixty, for the permitted meat is only what absorbed blood — and from the Torah it should not combine to the forbidden owner. Peri Chadash.
Even where there is sixty — because we say it did not fully exit until it is nullified only in that piece: it separated from place to place and is forbidden — and see Peleti where I discussed that this is a rabbinic doubt, end of his words.
Meat — Taz wrote in the name of Maharshal: meat that remained three days and was salted in a perforated vessel and afterward in an unperforated vessel is permitted, for necessarily: if it expels, behold it expelled in the perforated vessel; and if it does not expel, even in an unperforated vessel it will not expel — and forbids. And if his words are reasonable, nevertheless one forbids only what is inside the gravy, per the author below siman 18; but to be stringent as in hagahah to forbid everything, since anyway the disputants are many and permit, and it is only a stringency of Geonim — we have no need to be stringent.
That remained — and if he salted it with other meat: Taz's view is to be stringent; and they challenged Shach, and the law is with him, for even though it does not expel blood, nevertheless it expels gravy, as the senses testify — if you salt such meat the vessel fills with gravy; and since he is busy expelling gravy he does not absorb.
