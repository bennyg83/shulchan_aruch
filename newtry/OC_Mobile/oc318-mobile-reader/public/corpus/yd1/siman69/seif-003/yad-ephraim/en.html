Be'er Heitev; and in responsum Maaseh Moshe siman 80, meat soaked in seawater in a ship in an unperforated vessel — since the water was salty and unfit to drink because of their saltiness, it is forbidden; and Machatzit HaShekel disagreed and wrote several reasons to permit, that water has no power even though salty to expel like salt itself; and even if it is double safek d'rabbanan lekula, for blood that was salted is d'rabbanan; also sea water unfit for drinking is not because of saltiness but because they are bitter. And see Radbaz's responsum regarding netilat yadayim, that sea water when heated on fire diminishes in bitterness and becomes fit to drink, etc. — see there, and it seems he deals where it was already given initial rinse in other water, only he is concerned lest it is like unsalted in unperforated vessel; and in his view to permit for reasons he wrote there. However, if not yet rinsed, there is concern because of visible blood on it. And in my responsa Beit Aleph part 2, which is in manuscript with me, I wrote regarding spring water from salt wells that they draw and heat and make salt, which are very salty and impossible to drink because of saltiness, and residents there sometimes use such water instead of salt and took such water and soaked meat before salting instead of initial rinsing, and wrote there that although one may say such water is sharp like brine, nevertheless one may say they have no power to spread from place to place like other salty brine; and even if it soaked kevush period, one may say there is no power in the blood mixture to become entangled through the water's medium and return to be absorbed through salting and roasting; nevertheless concern for visible blood on it. And I ruled to permit without kevush period, and if it remained kevush period forbid without great loss, and in great loss permit for roasting as below siman 70 in the name of Noda BiYehudah regarding meat that fell into brine unsalted — see there.
