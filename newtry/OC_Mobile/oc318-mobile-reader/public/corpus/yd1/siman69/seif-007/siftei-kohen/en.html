Permitted to rinse, etc. The Rav wrote in responsum 132 din 11: a question on what Ran wrote, whom I brought in my Torat Chatat, that one may rinse meat for a pot in fruit juice; you were precise — the first rinsing is not? You asked the reason; and one may say other liquids are repulsive to it, etc., end of his words; and this reasoning is weak; also requires further study, for it is not found thus in Ran's words in chapter of the slaughterer that one may rinse meat in fruit juice — perforce the Rav intended what is written in chapter of the slaughterer in the mishnah of the slaughterer: if blood did not exit, it is fit to be eaten with unclean hands, for it was not made fit through blood; they challenged here: after all, before eating it was made fit through water; if for a pot, say it was made fit through the water in it; if for roasting, or you find when it was rinsed by rain, etc., end of his words. It was reasonable to the Rav (or it was written in his text) that the same applies in fruit juice; and so Tosafot wrote in chapter of the slaughterer (Chullin daf 33a) explicitly: for roasting it deals, that rinsing is not required; or for a pot and it was rinsed in fruit juice, end of their words (and it is more plausible that 'Ran' is a scribal error for 'Tosafot'); if so, it appears explicitly that it deals with the first rinsing (and further) that otherwise the challenge returns to its place, for it was made fit in the first rinsing; and what he wrote 'for a pot' is because for roasting rinsing is not required at all — requires further study:
