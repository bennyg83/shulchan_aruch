(There Taz s.k. 24) And an incident came before us with a woman who cooked kosher meat and forgot whether she salted it first or not, and we permitted it because it is only a d'rabbanan safek lekula. And he said we establish it on its presumption that initially it was not salted, etc., for there is a majority against the presumption that most times they salt first, etc. Shaarei HaMelech, laws of mikvaot general rule 7, challenged Taz according to what Tosafot wrote in Pesachim (daf 9) in the name of R' Yochanan regarding a chaver who died and left produce — since it is certainly treifah d'rabbanan, a safek whether he tithed removes certainty. And here too, since this piece was certainly not salted, a safek does not remove certainty. And to reconcile: treifah produce were all in a presumption of issur status, unlike here regarding meat that was not salted. And in such a case Taz wrote siman 127 that it is not called itchezk issur when the issur is not on the whole piece but the issur was not clarified from the heter. And Shaarei HaMelech himself proved there in general rules 1 and 2 regarding itchezk issura in d'rabbanan, such as one in one that mixed — if the issur was recognizable from the heter, it is not called itchezk issura to forbid as a d'rabbanan safek born afterward, such as one was lost and it is unknown which. And so here, since it is not called itchezk issura of blood on the meat piece, we are lenient in the safek. And why rov was needed here is because the safek is in an action. And so in Taz, Orach Chaim siman 49, who gave as reason that safek in action is weaker. And what Binet Adam challenged on Taz, that he considered rov of those who salt — behold that is rov dependent on action — say that in d'rabbanan it helps, and Maharalach has support for this from Pesachim (daf 4) regarding one who rents a house on the fourteenth, that we hold the bed is in a presumption of having been checked, and this presumption is from the majority of people who check at that time as Maharalach wrote; and it is also rov dependent on action, and also itchezk issura of chametz in the house. And apply this here. And if one says here is a flaw — how did she forget a great action of salting and the like — say on the other side, how did a safek arise whether she salted; what brought it to this safek? Say she did a great action; but go learn from safek whether he prayed and certainty that he did not pray — he does not return to pray because prayer is d'rabbanan, and although he is certainly obligated to pray, if there is safek whether he prayed, how does certainty remove safek? Also how did he forget such a great action whether he prayed — it requires favor of heart, and do they speak of the feeble-hearted outwardly? All the more one is liable to remember salting, which hands are busy with all day. And if you say prayer is different because if we are lenient he may bless in vain — there is stringency as leniency, for here there is no blessing until we say there is concern for blessing in vain, and one may challenge one explanation in the Gemara Pesachim, etc., that even in d'rabbanan we do not say safek removes certainty; if so, why exempt from prayer and Kriat Shema for one who holds Kriat Shema is d'rabbanan — safek in reading, certainty he did not read, he does not return to read because it is d'rabbanan safek? And here there is no blessing until we say so. And strain to say that when they enacted d'rabbanan like prayer and Kriat Shema they are different, they enacted for certainty and not for safek; in any case we derive in such a case safek removes certainty, and all the more according to Tosafot there, that this "his animal, etc." is all d'oraisa, but d'rabbanan nevertheless he is obligated in tithes and it is certainly so, and the Gemara concedes that in d'rabbanan safek removes certainty. And Taz's words are clear, and he did not remove something improper from his hand; but still in its place as Shach wrote, what is the alarm — shall we take a kafila to taste whether there is taste of blood, certainly she forgot, and if not, certainly she did not forget; and because we are stringent not to rely on kafila in this matter we are lenient in the deed? And one must say as I wrote, that every piece is full of blood but they did not forbid except blood smeared on top, and therefore even if the taster tastes taste of blood there is no proof it was not salted as I wrote. And according to what I wrote, that this is in the category of safek removes certainty, the question is not difficult, for he can clarify by asking her mouth; in any case one need not clarify and need not taste kafila. And so in Chullin daf 97, the Gemara challenged: if so, if it fell into another pot it should not be forbidden; and Tosafot explained: even though it is safek whether all exited in the first pot or only part, it is d'rabbanan safek lekula. And one should understand: in falling into another pot that the Gemara forbids, he tasted kafila and therefore what d'rabbanan safek is here — tasting kafila would decide whether all exited in the first or not. And one must say the Gemara's question is where there is no kafila, and if we rely on d'rabbanan safek; if so, even for us who do not rely on kafila it is as if there is no kafila and we rely on d'rabbanan safek, and requires further study to be lenient, for one may reject that the Gemara's question is if the pot increased now and there is no taste of milk in it, nevertheless it is forbidden since initially there was less than sixty and nevela, and forbidden; unlike if safek d'rabbanan lekula, then it was never forbidden. But regarding tasting, in truth we do not rely — if it were possible to taste. Therefore requires further study. Tzitz Eliezer brought in Kereti: if safek whether milk was salted with meat or not, it is d'oraisa safek for it is species in a different species and forbidden; and so agreed Pri Chadash. And I say: if he peels the meat it is permitted, for milk spreads in salting per most poskim it does not spread and peel suffices; and see Turei Chayim general rule 38, and below siman 105 in Shach s.k. 33, that many authorities hold it does not spread more than a peel, and even those who are stringent did not decide; and certainly to spread until it stops, even in sixty, requires removal; and see there siman 105. And if so, there is double safek: perhaps there was no contact of its milk at all, and if you say there was contact, perhaps it does not spread more than a peel; and if you do not know which place touched until the place of peel is unknown, one in two is nullified as known, and see siman 92 in Shach. And what I wrote in Peleti, do not doubt at all. However, as Shach wrote, milk in meat is species in a different species and can be clarified by a gentile's tasting — how do we say lekula that we do not rely on him? And it seems from poskim' words that salting taste is weak and impossible to taste; and see Rambam Hilchot Maachalot Assurot chapter 15 halacha 34 — salted nevelah meat mixed with slaughtered meat is forbidden because the salt became nevelah, etc., and impossible to stand on taste or issur there — requires further study. Tzitz Eliezer wrote: if safek milk safek fat, it is species in species and nullified d'oraisa and is only d'rabbanan safek lekula; and Pri Chadash questioned him that there are two safekot to be stringent, perhaps they were salted together and perhaps the milk was salted and not the fat, etc. I did not understand his words: in the end there is safek whether the milk was salted or not — if it was not salted, permitted, and it is d'rabbanan safek — what difference whether two safekot or not; why should we be stringent in d'rabbanan issur?
