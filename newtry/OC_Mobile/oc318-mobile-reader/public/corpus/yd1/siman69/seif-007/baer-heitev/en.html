He shakes off. The wording of Rashba: that if he places it with its salt in a non-perforated vessel with water, the water breaks the power of the meat's expulsion and the blood on the surface of the meat and with the salt returns and is absorbed within it:
L'chatchila. But b'dieved if he only rinsed once and cooked thus — permitted, as below end of seif 9:
He washes it. Shach wrote: this hagahah needs explanation, for the Mechaber also says l'chatchila he shakes it off or washes it and rinses twice; and it is possible he holds that for the Mechaber, when rinsing by hand while holding the meat in the air, shaking off or washing is not needed and rinsing twice suffices — and on this Rama wrote 'and some say' they must rinse, etc., meaning even by hand he washes or shakes it off and rinses twice; and Taz wrote that this 'or he shakes off' is a scribal error and it should read 'and he shakes off,' for Rama holds washing in the air and also shaking off is like rinsing twice — see there:
Fruit juice. Taz and Shach wrote: even the first rinsing before melicha — requires further study (and Rama wrote siman 132: specifically the last rinsing, but not the first, for it is rinsed for him.
