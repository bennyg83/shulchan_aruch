Therefore he should rinse it or shake it, etc. One may wonder, for in Shulchan Aruch he also wrote shaking or rinsing and afterward rinsing twice; and on this Rama wrote 'and some say they need to rinse,' etc., appears shaking or rinsing does not suffice in place of one rinsing — and how did Rama return and write this counts as rinsing twice, and likewise Rama afterward wrote 'or he should put water in the vessel,' etc. — this 'or' has no explanation; and it appears to me the first 'or' is a scribal error: first Rama said 'therefore he should rinse and shake the salt,' etc. — this counts as rinsing twice, meaning the rinsing that one rinses in the air and also the shaking afterward count in place of the first rinsing; and afterward he spoke on the main matter that rinsing twice is needed in this way, that he first puts water in the vessel and then the meat — so it appears to me to reconcile. In hagahah: permitted to rinse meat in fruit juice — Darkei Moshe wrote this is from Ran chapter of the slaughterer, and so Torat Chatat; but in truth it is not found there at all; and I found this law in chapter of the slaughterer (Chullin daf 33) in Tosafot, who wrote there on what is stated there regarding meat not made fit to receive impurity because it was not rinsed in water, only in fruit juice. I wrote this according to what I saw in Rama's responsum 132 din 11: you also asked on what Ran wrote, whom I brought in my Torat Chatat, that one may rinse meat in fruit juice, etc., and you were precise — the first rinsing is not? You asked the reason; and one may say other liquids are repulsive to it and do not soften the flesh like water; and so Maharik wrote regarding a woman washing before immersion, whom doctors commanded she should not rub except with wine, for precision is needed whether she may rub with wine lest wine mat the hair — and the same regarding repulsive liquids, and not all natures are equal, end of his words. With apology, he erred first in the place the law is found, and when he wrote the responsum erred further to distinguish between rinsing before melicha and after; but in truth it is clear from Tosafot I mentioned there is no distinction at all; and one proof for both: both suffice in fruit juice — for if we do not hold like that answer, whence do we derive rinsing in fruit juice? There is no source except from chapter of the slaughterer in Tosafot; and he should have written here this law even for the first rinsing — and so is primary; and what he brought as proof from a woman washing in wine is nothing, for even for that it is not clear, as explained in Beit Yosef siman 199 in the name of Maharik; and some came to answer that Tosafot hold the first rinsing was not required, that they hold the reason for first rinsing is blood on the surface of the meat, and here there is no blood, for it was not made fit — nonsense: certainly no blood makes fit except shechitah blood; but blood after slaughter called extraction blood is certainly not fit, as chapter of the slaughterer (Chullin daf 36); and all the more blood of limbs that certainly exists in every animal — when one cuts meat, blood of limbs exits and first rinsing is required; and see Mishmeret HaBayit for Rashba daf 20, explained there that whatever requires melicha requires first rinsing, and no distinction whether blood did not exit at shechitah or elsewhere (and he brings there Tosafot's answer that it deals with roasting, and wrote it deals when he eats it all at once roasted, and then rinsing and melicha are not required):
