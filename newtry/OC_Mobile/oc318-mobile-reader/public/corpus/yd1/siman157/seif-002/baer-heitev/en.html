The decree. Shach explains that the decree is that anyone called a Jew will be killed. If they decree that anyone who refuses to wear non-Jewish clothing will be killed, one must die rather than comply. Darkei Moshe cites Terumat HaDeshen that even during persecution the prohibition applies only when the non-Jews recognize him and seek to force him to transgress. He may disguise himself in non-Jewish clothing where they do not recognize him, because there is no desecration of Hashem's Name.
And that they should think. Taz writes that he must nevertheless repent, because his own intention was to commit a prohibition.
Similarly. The Gemara permits a Torah scholar to say, 'I am a servant of fire,' to escape an unlawful tax, using an ambiguous phrase that can refer to an idolatry called fire while he intends Hashem, who is called a consuming fire. Maharshal limits this to a Torah scholar, who is not likely to become lax, and because the tax collectors act unlawfully. Bach permits a Jew traveling in danger to change his clothing to appear non-Jewish, provided he does not wear kilayim. Merchants and travelers commonly relied on this. Where non-Jews forbid Jews from passing through an area, one may similarly change clothing to pass. See siman 178:1 in the gloss. Shach.
