The decree. The Shach wrote: this means that they decreed that anyone called by the name Jew be killed. But if they decreed apostasy, that anyone who does not wear non-Jewish clothing be killed, he must be killed rather than transgress. So wrote Nimukei Yosef. In Darkhei Moshe he wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen that even in a time of apostasy it is forbidden only when the non-Jews recognize him and want to force him to transgress. But to wear a garment made like the garments of non-Jews is permitted, for then there is no desecration of the Name, since the non-Jews do not recognize him.
And he will think. The Taz wrote: it is obvious that nevertheless he must repent for this, for he at least intended prohibition.
The like. It is stated in the Gemara that a Torah scholar may say, “I am a servant of the fire,” in order to exempt himself from the tax, because this phrase can be understood in two ways: “fire” is the idolatry called Nura, while the Torah scholar intends Heaven, which is called “a consuming fire.” Maharshal wrote that this was permitted specifically to a Torah scholar, for we are not concerned that he will become more lax, and also because he is exempt from the tax and the non-Jews are coming against him by robbery, but not for an am haaretz. The Bach wrote that likewise, when one is traveling and there is greater danger to a Jew than to a non-Jew, he may change his clothing, provided he does not wear kilayim. So is the custom of most merchants and travelers. Also in places where non-Jews decreed that a Jew may not pass through, it is permitted to change one's clothing in order to pass there; it seems to me that the custom is to be lenient. See above siman 178 seif 1 in the gloss. End quote from Shach.
