It is forbidden for a person to say that he is a non-Jew in order that they not kill him; but if it is in order that they not recognize that he is a Jew, he may change his clothing at a time of decree, since he is not saying that he is a non-Jew. {Rama: And he may even wear kilayim (Nimukei Yosef, perek HaGozel Basra). And even though it is forbidden to say that he is a non-Jew, nevertheless he can say to them an expression that can be understood in 2 ways (Nimukei Yosef, perek HaGozel), and the non-Jews will understand that he is saying that he is a non-Jew, while he intends for something else. And likewise, if he can mislead them so that they think that he is a non-Jew, it is permitted (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 197). And likewise, in this manner, one whose heart raised filth and he desires a married woman: if his wife can come to him such that he will think that he came upon the ervah, it is permitted (there, in Nimukei Yosef). And all this is permitted only in a place of danger; but not in a place where there is no danger — for example, if he wears the clothing of a non-Jew so that they will not recognize that he is a Jew and he will evade the tax, or similar to this — it is forbidden (Asheri and Nimukei Yosef, perek HaGozel Basra; and Terumas HaDeshen, siman 196; and other poskim).}
