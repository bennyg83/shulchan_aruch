Liable to death like Sheva ben Bichri. It seems that the reason Sheva ben Bichri was specified — although by Torah law he was not liable to death but only by royal law for rebelling against David — nevertheless they hand him over if they seize him; and from this, even in our time, one who sins and rebels against his kingdom they hand over; and the same for other transgressions such as engaging in forgeries or other matters that pose danger — plainly they hand him over, and it is proper to hand him over even if they did not seize him, since he is like a rodef to other Israelites through his evil deeds done negligently — so it seems to me. And further it seems to me that where they are not permitted to hand him over there is no distinction between handing him over for death or other suffering or even for money — they are clearly worse than death as we say in Aleh Ne'arot (folio 33): had they flogged Chananiah Mishael and Azariah they would have worshiped the image — learn from this that suffering is worse than death; and we find that lashes are more severe than death; and proof that in the Yerushalmi it does not say the king demanded him for killing but simply "demanded him" — perhaps only for suffering alone; and we should be stringent out of doubt; and even regarding money we find in the chapter HaGozel batra that the verse says "like a wild ox in a net" — once he fell into gentile hands they no longer have mercy on him — so it seems to me.
And likewise women, etc. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran wrote that also regarding women, if they seized one of them they hand her over; and it seems this too depends on the dispute whether liable to death like Sheva ben Bichri that Rama mentions — according to the second opinion seizure of a woman does not avail; but it seems to me that if they seized one of them and said if you do not give her to us to defile her we will kill all of you — plainly seizure avails and they hand her over, since there is no act on the woman's part and it is not in the category of gilui arayot, and therefore she herself is not subject to "be killed and do not transgress" — therefore they hand her over if they seize her — so it seems to me.
