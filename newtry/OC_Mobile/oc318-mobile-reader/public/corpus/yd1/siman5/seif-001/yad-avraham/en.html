**See** Pe'er Yitzchak who challenged: why is kohen believed to say it was pigul—for person does not make himself wicked; if we say rabbinic decree on Yom Kippur or verse “it shall be pigul to him”—then one who slaughters friend's animal and says he slaughtered for idol worship should not be believed; per Noda BiYehudah part Orach Chayyim siman 23 reason pigul believed is he intends for good—not to cause kohanim to stumble so they not eat from it—as such case Tosafot Bava Metzia (3). So too regarding friend's animal he is believed. But he himself refuted in Gizei Zahav siman 256 regarding disqualifying for others—not believed to say he committed transgression and now wants to save friend from issur; and Rosh Ketubot (18) anusim are because of money, etc.; and even in documents gittin—and one may say he intends now to save from issur; but from sugya end Nedarim regarding “I became impure willingly” implies we do not say person makes himself wicked—and because they intend to escape issur—even though causing stumbling to husband—tzarich iyun. Also he wrote there “person does not make himself wicked” applies only where Torah called wicked—death, lashes, false witness testimony disqualification—or false oath; but saying he caused others to stumble—not disqualified for testimony or oath or capital punishment—no place “person does not make himself wicked”; also on violating positive command called wicked as Yevamot (20) and Nedarim—and Shita Mekubetzet; and when friend commits issur through his thought it is like doing act when he does not separate him.
