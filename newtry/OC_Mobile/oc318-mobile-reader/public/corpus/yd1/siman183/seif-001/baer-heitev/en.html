From her source. From the verse, “and she has uncovered the source of her blood,” our Sages derived that she becomes impure only through blood that issues from the source.
Involuntarily. Meaning, through jumping, whether involuntarily or voluntarily, in accordance with the woman’s natural condition arising on its own. Taz.
That she sense it. This is required by Torah law. Rabbinically, however, she is impure even if she did not sense it, as stated below in siman 187 and in many other places.
Like a mustard seed. The same applies even to less than that amount.
Clean days. This requirement is rabbinic. By Torah law, only a major zavah must count seven clean days. The Sages, however, imposed this stringency so that error would not result.
A niddah. She does not, however, require immersion, because once the severe prohibition of niddah has been removed from her, she may come to engage in promiscuity, which is a lesser prohibition. Responsa Rivash, siman 422. One who had relations promiscuously with an unmarried woman who was an adult and had reached the age when she could menstruate, and who now wishes to repent, must repent as one who had relations with a niddah, unless he knows with certainty that she had immersed. Maharam Mintz, siman 96.
