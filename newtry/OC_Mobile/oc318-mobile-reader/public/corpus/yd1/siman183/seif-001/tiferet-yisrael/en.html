Seif 1. See what our teacher the gaon z"l wrote in s.k. 1 — his intent to prove Gemara explanation in Niddah daf 57 regarding "she felt" and "say sensation of limb or sun" — that truly she did not feel, only she thinks she had sensation — from Tosafot s.v. say, etc., who challenged: why not say sensation of urine as said first ch. — and his challenge: here she truly felt — how to attribute to distant concern and exempt from sacrifice — do we rule leniently from stringent above is to be stringent to impure retroactively — per above explanation see there; and explanation of his words: here she is certainly liable sacrifice for since she says she felt — we say to exempt from sacrifice it is not valid sensation; but otherwise liable sacrifice; and no need to bring chavot azei d'oraisa for here she is truly liable sacrifice properly; and from that which is clear in Gemara there daf 72 — they said to them House of Av: if we brought to impurity to be stringent we do not bring to sacrifice to be lenient; and Rashi explained to bring chavot azei d'oraisa — there never chavot azei unlike here; and plain.
And behold, at first it occurred to me to prove the above explanation from the aforementioned Gemara. There the Gemara first challenges from the mishnah: a woman who relieved herself, if standing she is impure, if sitting she is pure. Rashi explains "sitting" that she has a wound in the place of urine, for menstrual blood does not normally exit together with urine. If she felt a sensation, why is she pure when sitting? The Gemara answers: she felt, but perhaps it was the sensation of urine. At first glance this is difficult, for the sensation must mean that she said her makor opened, and this certainly does not come from urine, since urine does not normally bring menstrual blood, as in Rashi's explanation. If so, according to the Terumat HaDeshen in siman 190, if she feels that her makor opened, even if she found nothing she is impure. Even though urine does not normally bring menstrual blood, it is no worse than a case where she found nothing, for in any event she felt a sensation, and sensation alone renders her impure. Similarly, according to the Darkei Moshe in siman 188, who disagrees with the Terumat HaDeshen's law, he nevertheless agrees that if she found blood she is impure, and here she found blood. This requires study: what did the Gemara answer? But if the case is without sensation at all, then when sitting she is properly pure, for this is not menstrual blood, since it does not normally exit with urine; and when standing, because it returns to the makor, she is impure. The question and explanation are clear. If so, also in the second answer, "the sensation of the organ and the cloth," the explanation is so. This requires study.
And behold, I also found, at first glance, a proof for the words of my grandfather, the gaon author, z"l, that inserting a cloth is a Torah-level doubtful sensation, from what appears on Niddah 14: if she examined with a checked cloth, wiped it on her thigh, and the next day found blood, Shmuel said she is impure as a niddah. This is difficult. If she felt a sensation, then according to the Terumat HaDeshen above, even if she found nothing she is impure. One cannot say that here the Terumat HaDeshen agrees that if she did not see she is pure because that sensation may be attributed to the cloth, as in the Gemara above, for the cloth does not enter to the makor. And if the case is without sensation, Shmuel holds that she is not impure until she feels. Necessarily one must say that the case is where she did not feel, but since she examined and used the cloth, we say perhaps she felt and it was the sensation of the cloth or organ. This requires study. See Sidrei Taharah for what he wrote on this.
But in truth both proofs can be rejected. One may say that there are no eyes in the makor to know from what the sensation came; rather, she feels pain, as explained in the Gemara on page 3 regarding a sleeping woman, etc. If so, we say that what she thinks is the opening of her makor is a mistake, and it is only from urine, and in truth it did not come to the makor. Similarly, one may say regarding the cloth and the organ that although they do not reach the makor, as the Rambam wrote that the organ enters inside only until the vestibule, one must say that it is clear only that she feels her pain. See what Tosafot wrote above according to Beit Hillel, even though she did not urinate; see there. There is room for this, and it requires study.
