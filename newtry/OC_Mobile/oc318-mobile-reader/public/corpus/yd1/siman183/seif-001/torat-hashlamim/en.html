And there is no distinction between an unmarried woman and a married woman regarding the prohibition of niddah. According to what is stated in the Gemara at the end of chapter HaMapelet: it was taught, R. Meir would say, why did the Torah say that a niddah is for seven days? Because he is accustomed to her and becomes repelled by her; the Torah said she shall be impure seven days so that she will be beloved to her husband as at the time she entered the chuppah. If so, with an unmarried woman, to whom this reason does not apply, she should not be impure seven days. But the implication of the Rav here is that in no respect is there a distinction; and so it is clear from what the poskim wrote regarding a kallah, as explained below in siman 192. One must say that this is merely a derashah, but nevertheless from the verses it implies that it applies both to a married woman and to an unmarried woman, as Rivash wrote in siman 425; and it is written, "and she shall be impure seven days," implying in every case. One may also say that we are compelled to explain that R. Meir, who said, "why did the Torah say that a niddah is for seven days," etc., and did not ask this regarding a zavah, did so because regarding a zavah there is certainly no difficulty: there are many impure persons whose impurity is seven days, such as a zav and one impure from a corpse, and some have only impurity until evening. Rather, the main difficulty regarding niddah is from a dilemma: if her impurity is severe, why do we say that even if she flows blood all seven days she immerses in the evening, which is plain in the Gemara and as the Tur wrote in this siman, see Beit Yosef; and if it is not so severe, why, when she sees even the smallest amount, is she impure seven days, when impurity until evening should suffice? Therefore we need R. Meir's reason, so that she be beloved to her husband. But regarding a zavah, certainly even though this reason does not apply, she needs seven clean days. If so, now that the Sages made all women like a zavah gedolah, one cannot distinguish between an unmarried woman and a married woman, and she requires seven clean days. One may also say that R. Meir said this according to the view of R. Shimon, who expounds the reason of the verse, as stated in Bava Metzia 115 regarding taking a widow's pledge; and the halachah is not like R. Shimon, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 97. If so, in this matter too the halachah is not like R. Meir, and therefore no distinction should be made at all.
