A woman, etc., from her uterus. Niddah 41b: From where is this derived, etc.? It applies specifically when the blood comes from the uterus, as stated in the Mishnah at the end of the second chapter, 17b, and in Torat Kohanim at the end of parashat Metzora. Rif cites it in the second chapter of Shevuot, and Rosh at the end of Niddah: "If a woman has a discharge." One might think this applies to a discharge from any location. The verse therefore states, "She has exposed the source of her blood," teaching that all blood, etc., must come from the uterus.
Whether, etc. There, 36b, and infer from this.
And this is, etc. Niddah 57b. This applies according to the Torah, as stated there, and the law stated here is the Torah law.
And even, etc. The Mishnah there. This applies to the entire preceding clause. See the Talmud there, 42a.
She observes, etc. This requirement is rabbinic.
There is no distinction, etc. As stated in the third chapter of Keritot (14a): "There are present in this case," etc.; and at the beginning of the third chapter of Ketubot: "And regarding the niddah, although," etc.
