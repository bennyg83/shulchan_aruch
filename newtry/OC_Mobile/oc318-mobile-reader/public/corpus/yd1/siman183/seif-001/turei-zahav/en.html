In the Tur: "lest the woman see during her days of niddah" — some emend it to read "lest, etc." due to Beit Yosef's difficulties; but I am not satisfied with this, for if so he would not have needed to write afterward "between zivah and niddah" — he should simply have said "so that she not err during the days of niddah itself," and zivah is not mentioned at all. Therefore it seems the Tur's text should read: "because lest they come to err regarding the prohibition of karet, they were stringent to declare any reddish appearance tamei — and lest a woman see, etc." — this is my proposed emendation. According to this, his words are well organized: due to concern for error in the appearance of blood they were stringent to declare all reddish appearances tamei; but there is a further concern arising from this — lest a woman see, etc. — and she would need "seven more days," meaning including the day she sees impure blood — that is, six more days. And in fact, even though this deals with the days of niddah, she must still have those days clean — for if she sees during them there would be doubt whether until the last day she had yet seen impure blood; rather, she needs the following six days to be clean — and according to this she only needs six clean days, hence he does not mention "clean" here, having included it in what he wrote afterward: "and lest they come to err between the days of niddah and those of zivah" — for perhaps she is in her days of zivah and saw impure blood three days and needs specifically seven clean days; and this is why the Tur concludes "she shall count seven clean days" — meaning without the day of seeing, like a major zavah; unlike what he wrote above "and she needs seven more days" — meaning including the final day of seeing. The meaning of the passage is this: there are three concerns. The first: in the appearance of blood they might err and declare it pure — therefore they were stringent to declare all reddish appearances tamei. There is a further concern regarding the days of niddah, as mentioned — therefore she needs six clean days. And there is yet a further concern that she may err between the days of zivah and niddah — therefore she needs seven clean days. And if you ask: why do we need the middle concern? — one can say: from where would we have come to be concerned about erring between niddah and zivah? — therefore he wrote the middle concern, because of which she needs at least six clean days; and if so there is room for error between the clean days of niddah and those of zivah — and therefore she needs seven clean days everywhere.
