In Tur: lest the woman see on niddah days — some emend "lest," etc., due to Beit Yosef's difficulties; and it did not rest with me — for if so he need not write afterward distinction between zivah and niddah but should say so she not err on niddah days themselves — who mentions zivah; perforce Tur must read: lest they come to err in karet prohibition — they were stringent to impure any red appearance; and lest she see, etc., as above to emend; and per this his words are well ordered: on concern of error in blood appearance they were stringent to impure any red appearance; only there is further concern because of this lest woman see, etc., and needs another seven days — meaning besides day of sight impure — i.e., another six days; and truly even though here deals with niddah days — nevertheless requires clean days; if she sees in them there will also be doubt lest until last day she still did not see impure blood but requires those days afterward clean; and per this she needs only six clean days; why did he not mention clean here — included in what he wrote afterward; and so they not err between niddah days and zivah days lest on zivah days she saw impure blood three days and needs seven clean days specifically; and this is what Tur concludes "she sits seven clean days" — meaning without day of sight like great zavah; unlike above "and needs another seven days" — meaning including last day of sight before it; and our Rav's words explained: three concerns here — first in blood appearance lest err to purify — therefore stringent to impure any red appearance; second regarding niddah days as mentioned therefore needed six clean; third concern lest err in days between zivah and niddah — therefore needs seven clean; and one may ask why middle concern — one may say from where to fear error between niddah and zivah — therefore wrote middle concern due to which needs at least six clean; and if so room to err between niddah clean days and zivah clean days — therefore everywhere seven clean.
