Even though it did not emerge outside. See the beginning of siman 188 for these laws. Tur writes, "lest the woman see during her days of niddah." Some emend this to "and lest," because of the Beit Yosef's questions, but this does not satisfy me. If so, Tur would not need afterward to write "between zivah and niddah"; he should simply have said that this is so she not err during the days of niddah themselves. What connection would zivah have? Therefore it appears that Tur should read: "Because they might err in a prohibition punishable by karet, they were stringent to deem every red appearance impure; and lest the woman see," etc. With this emendation his words are well ordered. Because of the concern that one might err in distinguishing blood colors, they were stringent to render every red appearance impure. There is a further concern: perhaps a woman will see, etc., and she will require another seven days. Here "another seven days" means including the day on which she saw impure blood, that is, another six days. Although the present discussion concerns the days of niddah, those days must still be clean, because if she sees during them the same uncertainty continues, for perhaps until the last day she had not yet seen impure blood. Thus the six days after the sighting must be clean. Accordingly she needs only six clean days, and therefore Tur does not call them "clean" here, but includes this in what he writes afterward: to avoid confusion between the days of niddah and the days of zivah, for perhaps she is in the days of zivah and saw impure blood for three days, in which case she requires specifically seven clean days. This is why Tur concludes, "she shall sit seven clean days for it," meaning seven days apart from the day of the sighting, like a zavah gedolah. Earlier, however, he wrote "she requires another seven days," meaning including the final day of the preceding sighting. Thus there are three concerns: first, error in the appearance of the blood, for which they were stringent to deem every red appearance impure; second, concern regarding the days of niddah, as explained, requiring six clean days; and third, that she may confuse the days of zivah with the days of niddah, for which she must always count seven clean days. If you ask why the middle concern is necessary, the answer is that without it there would be no basis to fear confusion between niddah and zivah. Once the middle concern requires at least six clean days, there is room to confuse the clean days of niddah with the clean days of zivah, and therefore seven clean days are always required.
