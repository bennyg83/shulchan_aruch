(Siman 226 in Shach s.k. 13) And his words are puzzling, etc. Now I have seen his words are correct, for perforce here it deals with one who forbade his fellow — meaning he said "my courtyard shall be forbidden upon you"; for if he forbade himself from his fellow's share, certainly even when he said "this courtyard" they compel him so it will all belong to his fellow and he will not enter it at all; and so it appears in Ashri that it deals with one who forbade his fellow — see there; also the language "my courtyard" that the Rosh and Tur wrote implies so; and if so when it deals with one who forbade his fellow, it is forbidden to sell to the partner as Rivash wrote; and although Rivash refers to one who forbade himself from his fellow's property — the same applies when he forbade his fellow from his property, for they have one reason; and likewise the Ran explicitly compares them reciprocally and the Beit Yosef brings it in this siman.
