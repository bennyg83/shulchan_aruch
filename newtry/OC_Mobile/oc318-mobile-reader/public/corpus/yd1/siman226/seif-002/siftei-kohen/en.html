They are forbidden to set up. Even if it does not have the law of division. The Atzei Zahav likewise wrote that although we hold that partners ordinarily are not particular with one another, nevertheless if they are particular they can object, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 161 seif 10; and here, regarding one prohibited by vow from benefit, they certainly are particular and are forbidden; end quote. So too wrote the Hagahot Perishah. But they did not aim correctly, for Tosafot and the Ran hold that since, if they were particular, they could object, then even if they are not particular, he benefits from the fact that the other did not prevent him, and it is forbidden; see there. This is explicitly proven from their words. However, Tosafot there wrote another reason in the name of some authorities: when one sets up millstones, most people come there and fill his share and his fellow's share; and similarly with raising chickens, for they spread through the entire courtyard. According to this, it appears that any use in the world that fills the whole courtyard, such as placing and spreading fruit throughout the whole courtyard and the like, is forbidden; and so wrote Maharshal there.
He is forbidden to set up, etc. Even where it does not have the law of division.
And they would compel the one who vowed, etc. Even where it does not have the law of division, for although the one prohibited from benefit may enter, nevertheless we are concerned lest they use a forbidden use, such as setting up an oven, etc. So wrote the Rosh and the Ran, and the Beit Yosef brings them; and such also appears to be the view of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchot Nedarim, and the Semag, negative commandment 242. However, the view of Rashi and Tosafot is that where entry into the courtyard is permitted, we do not compel. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 14 part 5, wrote that even where it does not have the law of division, we compel; and some say we do not compel, but the first view appears primary; end quote. It appears that when they want to divide, or when they want to proceed by "gud or agud" where it does not have the law of division, we do not compel; rather, they should do as they wish. So wrote the Bach where it has the law of division, and from there it follows where it does not have the law of division, by gud or agud.
The one who vowed, etc. The Ran wrote that only here, where one of them vowed, do we compel, since he is forbidden and his fellow is permitted, and we are concerned that he may become jealous of his fellow. But where they vowed benefit from one another, so that both are forbidden, we are not concerned, for each one remembers his prohibition; end quote. So too wrote the Atzei Zahav. But the Rosh and the Tur in Choshen Mishpat siman 180 wrote that if they vowed benefit from one another and one of them is accustomed to use it, we compel the one who is accustomed to sell; and so wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham. The Yerushalmi explains that with two times one is called accustomed, and the Ran brings it. It appears that their reason is that we do not compel when they vowed benefit from one another because, since both vowed, what makes one stronger than the other? Therefore we do not compel.
The one who vowed, etc. From the Mechaber's unqualified wording it implies that he rules like the Rosh and the Tur, that whether he prohibited himself from his fellow's share or prohibited his fellow from his own share, we compel; and so wrote the Atzei Zahav.
To sell. The Bach wrote: it appears that this is specifically selling to someone else, but to the partner it is forbidden to sell, even though he said only "my courtyard." So too wrote the Rivash in Responsa siman 357, that it is forbidden to the one who vowed himself, for he benefits from his house, since it comes to him through its power; end quote. His words are astonishing, for why should he not sell to the partner who did not vow? The Rivash there wrote only that where he says, "konam from your house," it is forbidden to sell the house to the one who vowed. It appears to me that the Bach also meant this: if that partner who did not vow wants to sell his share, it is forbidden to sell it to the one who vowed.
