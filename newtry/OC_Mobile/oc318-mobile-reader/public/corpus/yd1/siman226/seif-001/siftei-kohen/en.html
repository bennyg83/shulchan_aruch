The vow did not take effect. For there is berirah; and the Rosh and Ran in name of the Rashba explained: since perforce the courtyard is acquired to his fellow to walk in all of it, he has no power in his hand to forbid it upon him, for it is like forbidding upon his fellow what belongs to his fellow; and this is "there is berirah" that we say: every time he enters the courtyard we see as if the place of his treading is acquired to him alone, for on this condition the courtyard was acquired to them from the beginning, since his fellow cannot compel him to divide; and this berirah is from the essence of his acquisition, for it is like one who acquired for each one to be acquired to himself alone at the hour he treads with his foot at the hour of his treading; and Maharshal agreed there; and with this they resolve that which we say generally in many places that d'oraisa there is no berirah — but certainly this berirah here is not like general berirah, from the reason we said; and it implies that likewise all other use that is not like use of mill and oven and raising chickens is permitted when there is no law of division from this reason; and so Maharshal wrote there.
