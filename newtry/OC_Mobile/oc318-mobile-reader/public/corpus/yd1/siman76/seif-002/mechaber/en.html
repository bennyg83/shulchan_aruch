If one wished to salt roast and eat it without rinsing — he may do so and need not be concerned for blood on the salt; and some say this applies only when he salts and roasts it immediately; but if it remained in its salt the salt absorbs and it becomes forbidden — therefore he rinses it well before roasting: {Rama: And some say roast requires rinsing first (Ramban); and some say it also requires a little salting first (Rashi); and the custom is to rinse it first and also salt it a little when it is placed on the spit and roast it immediately before the salt fills with blood (Hagahat ShaDa); however if he did not rinse or salt at all, or salted without rinsing first and roasted thus — it is permitted provided it did not remain thus in its salting without rinsing for the time of salting; but if it remained so long before roasting it is forbidden (so it appears in Arukh general rule 5); and there is no distinction in all this between geese and other hollow birds (Mordechai and ShaDa and Beit Yosef and Or Zarua chapter 25 law 6); provided they are not filled with eggs or other meat — but if they are filled their law is like cooking and they require salting first like for a pot (ShaDa); and practice is to be stringent when roasting meat without salting not to turn the spit constantly so blood will flow (Arukh general rule 9); and b'dieved one need not be concerned; and there is no distinction whether one wishes to eat it thus roasted or to cook it afterward — only that he roast it first so it is fit to eat, meaning half roasted (there). Some say every roast requires rinsing after roasting because of blood adhering to it (Agudah and Agur); and so is the custom l'chatchila; however if he did not rinse, and even cooked it thus — permitted (it appears to me siman 73); and even if he salted first before roasting and did not rinse after salting — even so permitted and we are not concerned for blood and salt on it, for fire draws and absorbs (Or Zarua).}
