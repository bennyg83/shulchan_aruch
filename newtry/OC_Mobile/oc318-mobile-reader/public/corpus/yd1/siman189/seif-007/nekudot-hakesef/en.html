(Taz s.k. 31) He extended to refute Maharshal's words — all his words do not appear correct, for Tur's plain language is clear like Maharshal; what he wrote that "she changed" deals with all three onot — if so also what Tur wrote before "and if she stopped" deals thus — not a matter, for language "and if she stopped" implies language of stopping i.e. she did not see after it passed day 20; language "if she stopped from seeing" implies standing still. In opening he teaches leniency: even though she stopped i.e. did not see even once on day 20 she is not concerned forever; in closing he teaches leniency: even if she stopped entirely three onot the first veset is not uprooted if she afterward returns and sees like first veset. What he wrote further: per Maharshal's words there would be distinction between day intervals and month — but Tur explicitly wrote afterward like uprooting interval veset so month veset — no difficulty, for certainly for uprooting they are equal i.e. whatever uprooted and stopped three onot and did not see on day 20 at all or changed in three equal sightings — as interval veset is uprooted so month veset; however where she stopped and did not see on day 20 — though she is not concerned for interval of 20th i.e. sight of 40th — nevertheless veset of 20th is not uprooted i.e. when she returns and sees she must immediately be concerned for day 20; but when she stopped three onot since veset uprooted — even if she returns and sees she need not be concerned for day 20 only when she returns and sees on day 20 then first veset is re-fixed. With this all his later words are rejected as I will explain. What he wrote further Tur wrote at opening of siman that interval veset and day veset are equal regarding not uprooted in fewer than three times — no difficulty, for certainly not uprooted in fewer than three times i.e. need not be concerned for month veset as Maharshal wrote; likewise regarding if she afterward returns and sees she must immediately be concerned for day 20 — unlike stopping to see three times. What he said further proof from Tosafot HaRosh — is not proof, for there: from which time does she count 30th? From sight of 30th and not from sight of 20th she is accustomed to see, for habit did not come in fewer than 20th; if this one counts 20th from time of 20th — etc.; his essence is because habit was not accustomed to come in fewer than 20th and likewise conversely where habit was not accustomed in more than 20th — if she did not see on day 20 she is not concerned for day 40th. What he wrote the distinction Bach made — is clear per my view: for month veset even if she did not see on Rosh Chodesh one can say chance she did not see this Rosh Chodesh but on another she will see since accustomed on Rosh Chodesh; but for intervals cannot say so — for when she sees at end of 40th she will not see on interval of 20th — all Maharshal's words stand properly. What he wrote rather what Tur wrote — and if so wonder on Shulchan Aruch — not precise, for here since she stopped and did not see three onot veset of 20th is automatically entirely uprooted and even if she sees afterward need not be concerned for day 20 since she stopped three onot; no relevance to below seif 20 where veset was not uprooted only during pregnancy and nursing she need not be concerned for them since blood is removed — when days of pregnancy and nursing passed and she returns and sees she must be concerned for intervals she was accustomed to — unlike here. If so what he wrote Shulchan Aruch on veset day at end of seif is imprecise — not precise, for it is precise sharp reasoning: since she stopped from seeing three onot first veset did not return to its place until she returns to see on first veset day i.e. one sighting and afterward second sighting on day 20 — then first veset returns for everything and need not be concerned for new month veset which has other laws of fixed veset — all clear.
