However, if she had a veses, etc. This means, for example, that she was accustomed to see at the end of 29 days, and afterward she saw, counting from that time, at the end of 30 days, then at the end of 31 days, and then at the end of 32 days. In that case, even from the first two later sightings we already recognize a pattern of progression.
From her established veses. This means that she had been accustomed to see on the 20th day, and then changed and saw on the 21st, 22nd, and 23rd. The sighting on the 21st is also included in the count, since it too followed the same equal progression as those that came after it. In truth, there is no novelty in this. The Gemara stated it as a novel answer to a difficulty according to Shmuel. Nevertheless, one may say that it teaches a significant point, lest one think that the first sighting is counted only when the sighting on the 19th is a new sighting to which she had not previously been accustomed. The Derishah wishes to say that the first progression need not be equal to the others, meaning that even if she was accustomed to see on the 15th and afterward saw on the 20th, 21st, and 22nd, it is sufficient. This is entirely incorrect, for we require that its interval correspond to the others, which implies equal intervals, as is evident from the Gemara and from the wording of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch.
And one should be concerned for their words, etc. This is difficult, for even according to the first opinion there is one stringency. Namely, if she is subject to concern for two vestos, neither is uprooted unless the second becomes established, as stated in seif 13 at the beginning of the gloss. According to the later opinion here, since the progressive veses is established through three occurrences, the concern for her other veses is automatically uprooted and she no longer need be concerned for it. According to the first opinion, the progressive veses is not yet established, and she must still be concerned for the other veses as well. Therefore, my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that regarding this matter we rule according to the first opinion, meaning that we follow both opinions stringently. This is correct and will be explained in seif 13. I still require clarification regarding the Tur, who wrote that the Rosh ruled according to the first opinion, meaning like Shmuel in the Gemara and unlike Rav, whose view is the later opinion here. Yet in the chapter Shor Shenagach Arba'ah VaChamishah, the Rosh wrote on folio 8 that we rule like Rav in this matter. This requires further study.
