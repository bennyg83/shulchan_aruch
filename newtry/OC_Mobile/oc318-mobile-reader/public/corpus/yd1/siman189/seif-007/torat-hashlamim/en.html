Until she sees on the eighteenth of Tammuz. There are then three progressive steps, and she must be concerned for the nineteenth of Av, the twentieth of Elul, and so on.
However, if she previously had a veset. Bach, Taz, and Shach explain that the prior veset followed the same progressive pattern, such as an established veset on the fourteenth. The ruling teaches that the first sighting may not be combined with earlier sightings, because there are still only three sightings in the progressive sequence. Nevertheless, there are three sightings exhibiting progression. By contrast, according to the authorities who follow Rav and require only three sightings, if she originally had an established veset on the fifteenth, two subsequent progressive sightings do not suffice. This is explicit in the Gemara, Niddah 64a.
One should take their opinion into account stringently. Bach, Taz, and Shach write that one must observe the stringencies of both views. Thus she must be concerned for both the seventeenth and eighteenth of Tammuz. If the veset became established after three sightings, it is uprooted only after three contrary occurrences. Raavad writes similarly at the end of folio 96 in Baalei HaNefesh, for he was uncertain whether the rule that the halachah follows Rav in ritual prohibitions applies here, or whether, because their dispute also affects monetary-style legal classification, the halachah follows Shmuel. It requires study, since Rosh and Rashba, also cited by Beit Yosef, write that Raavad ruled like Rav.
