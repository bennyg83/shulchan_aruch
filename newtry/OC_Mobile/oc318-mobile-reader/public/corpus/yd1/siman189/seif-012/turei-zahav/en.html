For a veses of alternating months, the first sighting, etc. This means that she skipped one Rosh Chodesh: she saw on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, on Rosh Chodesh Sivan, and on Rosh Chodesh Av. She thereby established a veses for alternate Roshei Chodashim. Accordingly, she need not be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Elul, but only for Rosh Chodesh Tishrei, and so on forever. Here, Rosh Chodesh Nisan is included in the count even according to the first opinion in seif 7 regarding a progressive interval veses, which requires four sightings. There, we count the days of the month between the first and second sightings, and thereby the interval veses becomes recognizable. Thus, specifically the first and second sightings are connected in order to establish the veses, and the first sighting is therefore not counted independently, since by itself it has no connection to an interval. Here, however, the establishment is tied to Rosh Chodesh, and therefore the first Rosh Chodesh is included in the count, since it independently has relevance to the veses by virtue of being Rosh Chodesh. What difference does it make whether between the first Rosh Chodesh sighting and the second there was one Rosh Chodesh or two? Ultimately, the veses is specifically for Rosh Chodesh.
It is comparable to a progression. This means as stated in seif 11, that for a progressive veses she need not be concerned until she has established it three times. Therefore, here she need not be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Av.
She must be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar. This means both days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar. The first is the 30th day from her sighting and is the ordinary onah for a woman who has no fixed veses, as explained above. See seif 13.
