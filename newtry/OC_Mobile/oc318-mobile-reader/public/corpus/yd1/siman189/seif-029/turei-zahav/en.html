When people call her “mother,” etc. The Beit Yosef cites here a dispute in the Gemara in the names of R' Yehuda and R' Shimon, and writes that it is known that the halacha follows R' Shimon, since we rule like him against R' Yehuda. This is an error, for Eruvin 47 explicitly states that the halacha follows R' Yehuda against R' Shimon. The Beit Yosef wrote the same in siman 119, s.v. “one who is suspected,” but there too he was imprecise in the flow of his writing. Nevertheless, there we indeed rule like R' Shimon because Rava holds like him. Independently, the Beit Yosef was also imprecise here, for the R' Yehuda and R' Shimon mentioned are not Tannaim. They appear in an Amoraic statement of the Gemara, not in a Baraisa. They are Rav Yehuda and R' Shimon ben Lakish. This is also the text in Toras HaBayis HaAruch, folio 172.
And she is not concerned. The Beit Yosef cites a dispute between R' Shmuel and R' Zeira regarding a woman who is embarrassed but does not object. The Rambam ruled like the opinion that she does not object. It appears to me from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which state “she is not concerned,” that specifically where she is not embarrassed is she not considered elderly. Rather, only when she no longer has any concern at all, even by way of embarrassment, is she considered elderly. This is the stringent interpretation.
