So elderly that it would be expected. This applies even if she is personally particular, and likewise if she is no longer physiologically expected to see even though she is not particular. The matter does not depend on her personal attitude, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Yerushalmi.
And she is not concerned. The Gemara records a dispute whether the criterion is that she is no longer embarrassed or that she is no longer particular. The practical difference is a woman who is embarrassed but not particular. Rashba, Rambam, and Rabbeinu Yerucham rule according to the view that she is no longer particular. This appears correct because the Yerushalmi states that criterion explicitly, and the matter is rabbinic, so we rule leniently. Kesef Mishneh writes similarly. This is also the apparent view of Tur and the Mechaber. Their phrase, she is not concerned, means that she is not particular. This is unlike Taz, who understands them as ruling stringently according to the view that she is no longer embarrassed.
