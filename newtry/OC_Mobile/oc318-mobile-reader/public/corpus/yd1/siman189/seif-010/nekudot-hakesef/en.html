(There, s.k. 39) His distinction in the words of Raavad is not correct. Since we say that its law is like a veset of days alone, why should she not also have to be concerned before the onah? Furthermore, the poskim and the Tur later write that she must separate for the entire onah, just as with a veset of days alone, which implies that it has literally the law of a veset of days alone. Moreover, Raavad there also states: “Before the veset she is permitted, and if she examined herself at the time of the veset and did not see, she is permitted after the veset, etc.” This appears to refer back to the preceding statement, meaning that she is permitted after the veset. Rather, the meaning is that there Raavad teaches that even if she has a fixed day for those bodily signs, but no fixed veset for seeing blood, such as when she yawned or sneezed repeatedly on successive Roshei Chodesh but did not see, she has nevertheless established a fixed day for those signs. If she then yawned on two successive Roshei Chodesh and saw blood afterward, she must be concerned for a veset of yawning alone. Here, however, the discussion concerns a veset that itself arrives at a known time and becomes fixed. In that case, although the yawning has also become fixed, its law is like that of a veset of days alone. This is what the Rosh writes here in the name of Raavad, as cited by the Beis Yosef: “A bodily veset that was established three times at a known time, when that day of yawning arrives she must separate from her husband for the entire onah, like the law of a veset of days alone.” This implies that it applies specifically when the veset was established, as I explained. The words of the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 8 likewise imply this, as I explained. The Beis Yosef cites him below, at the beginning of page 137, in the passage beginning, “The Maggid Mishneh wrote,” and comments that his language is imprecise. According to what I have written, however, it is precise. Nevertheless, Responsa Rashba, siman 838, as cited by the Shulchan Aruch above in siman 187, se'if 11, implies that even for an unfixed veset she must separate for the entire onah, in accordance with the view of the Beis Yosef and as I wrote in the Siftei Kohen, s.k. 78. This is likewise implied by the plain language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here, and this appears to be the primary ruling in practice.
