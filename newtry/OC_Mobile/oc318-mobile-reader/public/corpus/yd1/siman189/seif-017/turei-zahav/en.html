It is not a veses, for she saw because of the external cause. This does not follow the Tur's view, for the Tur ruled that even jumping alone can establish a veses. Likewise, the ruling at the end of the seif, that the day itself has already been established three times, etc., does not follow the Tur, who ruled in that case that a veses is established for one day together with jumping, and that the jumping of the previous day caused her to see on the following Sunday. In seif 20, the Shulchan Aruch ruled that the yawning of the previous day caused the sighting, etc., which follows the Tur. At first glance the Shulchan Aruch contradicts itself. There is no difficulty, however, because yawning is different. It is not considered an external cause. Even one who holds that jumping alone does not establish a veses agrees regarding yawning, as stated in seif 19. The reason is that yawning is part of a woman's natural bodily condition at the time of her sighting, caused by excess bodily moisture, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the Rashba's name. Therefore, we may properly say that the yawning of the previous day caused her to see on Sunday, for women sometimes experience this. Jumping, by contrast, is merely an external cause.
