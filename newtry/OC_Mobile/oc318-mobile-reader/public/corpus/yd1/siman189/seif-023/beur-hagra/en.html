She ate, etc. The Gemara there. Beit Yosef writes that the words of the Rosh and Mordechai imply that eating three different types of foods establishes a veset. His words are difficult, for the wording of the Rosh follows the Gemara, while the Mordechai implies the opposite. In siman 17 he writes: “She is forbidden to engage in relations the entire day,” etc. “This is the law of a woman whose veset is fixed by days. But a woman whose veset is fixed by other signs,” etc., “such as yawning or sneezing,” etc., “or by eating garlic or peppers,” etc. The same appears in Torat HaBayit, 168b: “Some women are accustomed to see through a combination of a day and an act. How so? For example, she jumped on Sunday and saw, then jumped on Sunday and saw again; or she ate peppers on Sunday and saw, then ate peppers on Sunday and saw again,” etc. The poskim therefore omitted this as a separate category, because it is included in the law of jumping. They follow the Rashba, who holds that such a veset is established only together with particular days, like a veset of jumping. The Gemara mentions the case only to distinguish it from the other vesetot, which according to one opinion are established after one or two occurrences, whereas this one requires three. According to our ruling, however, all vesetot require three occurrences, since the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the chapter HaBa al Yevimto, so this distinction is unnecessary.
