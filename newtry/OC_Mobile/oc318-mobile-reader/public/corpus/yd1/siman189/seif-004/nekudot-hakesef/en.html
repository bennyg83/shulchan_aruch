(Taz s.k. 18) He wishes to prove that even though she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, she must still be concerned for the ninth of Iyar because of a twenty-day interval. His words are not convincing. His statement that we do not find that a sighting at a changed time uproots the concern that previously rested upon her is not valid. Here, once she saw on Rosh Chodesh, she changed her interval to twelve days, and since she had not established a fixed veset of intervals, the previous interval is uprooted by a single change. Regarding his further statement, “And moreover, if she were to see, etc.,” I say that this is indeed so. In such a case she would not establish a veset, because there are not three equal intervals. Since she saw on Rosh Chodesh, she uprooted the twenty-day veset that had previously been pending. The Perishah also holds this view; see there, examine his words carefully, and you will find it so. His further statement that there is a clear proof from the Tur at the end of this siman, in the case where she saw twice on Rosh Chodesh, is also imprecise. There the discussion concerns a veset of the month, and in that case the Bach and Perishah also agree, as stated in se'ifim 30 and 31. His objection to the Bach was correctly directed, but the Bach's ruling is nevertheless true, while the Taz's attempted proof to the contrary is no proof at all. There she had a fixed date on the twentieth. Therefore, if she had seen on the twentieth, we would not say that she changed her interval merely because she saw ten days later. She would still have had to be concerned ten days after the sighting on the tenth, namely, on the twentieth. This differs from the present case, where no veset of intervals had been established. Since all his proofs are nullified, we return to the law that if she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, she need not be concerned for the ninth of Iyar. This is implied by the plain meaning of Ramban's words. Indeed, the Taz himself wrote that the Rama's phrase “if she did not see” cannot comfortably be explained as merely stating a greater novelty.
