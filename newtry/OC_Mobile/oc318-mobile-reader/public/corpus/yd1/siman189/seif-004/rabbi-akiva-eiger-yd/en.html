(Seif 23, in the gloss) She ate garlic and then saw blood. Plainly, this applies specifically where she saw after eating three different pungent foods. If she ate garlic three times, she establishes a veset only for garlic. This resembles the law of a goring ox: if it gored an ox, a donkey, and a camel, it becomes forewarned for all species, but if it gored an ox three times, it becomes forewarned only for oxen. Accordingly, if she ate garlic twice and saw, and then ate an onion and saw, no veset was established. This creates a difficulty. The ruling implies that bodily symptoms are not combined with one another, whereas all pungent foods are combined. Why, then, does it give the example of yawning once and sneezing twice? Even regarding pungent foods, that pattern would not establish a veset. It should instead have mentioned three distinct symptoms, such as yawning once, sneezing once, and then experiencing heaviness of the head and limbs. This requires study.
