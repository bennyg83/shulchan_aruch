Even if she developed, etc.; and likewise, etc. As stated there, 46a, signs that appear before twelve years of age are merely a mole. If she did not develop the signs, she remains a minor even afterward, and even at twenty years of age, as stated there, 47b. This is also implied by Shmuel. This is unlike Rashi there, 5a, s.v. We are not concerned, who holds that either one of the two conditions suffices. The wording “when they reached” compelled him to explain so, but this is not correct, for the Tosefta, chapter 1, expressly teaches, “when she developed two hairs.” Thus writes Torat HaBayit. Rashi, however, explains that the passage there concerns a girl before twelve years of age, and this is what he means by saying that one of them suffices.
If they examined her, etc. As stated there, 46a: “The halachah is that we are concerned,” etc.; “This applies,” etc. The same applies here, since vesetot are rabbinic. This is specifically when she was examined. Without an examination, the presumption remains, as stated there. See Even HaEzer, siman 155.
She establishes, etc. There, 9b, the baraita teaches regarding a young girl, etc., that on the third sighting she is treated, etc. There, 10a, it discusses the period between the second and third sightings, etc. After the third sighting, however, all agree that she is impure and is treated like all other women.
Except that there is a distinction, etc. There, 9b, the Gemara states, “If three intervals passed over her,” etc. This differs from other women, for we do not rule like Rabbi Eliezer in the Mishnah, who says, “Every woman,” etc. As stated there, 9b: “The Sages taught: An incident occurred, and Rabbi ruled in accordance with Rabbi Eliezer,” etc. A young girl is different, for even the Rabbis agree regarding her. The challenge there, 10a, is only from the latter clause concerning a girl who reached the age when she is expected to see: “Say the latter clause,” etc. See Rashi there, s.v. Say the latter clause, etc.
And even if she resumed seeing at those same intervals, etc. There, 10a, the Gemara asks, “Did she resume seeing at regular intervals?” etc. Tosafot there, s.v. She sees, and s.v. In the majority, write that the passage follows Rabbi, whereas according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel she does not revert until the fourth sighting, because the first sighting does not combine with the later ones. The Rashba disagrees and writes that the passage may follow either Rabbi or Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for it is impossible that the entire discussion, including the dispute of Chizkiyah, Rabbi Yochanan, and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, should proceed contrary to the halachah. See Tosafot there, s.v. Rabbi Yochanan, and 10b, s.v. To be, etc. Only the concluding clause concerning a girl who reached the age when she is expected to see follows Rabbi. This accords with the reading of most texts cited by Tosafot above: “When she reached,” etc.; “Who is the authority? Rabbi,” etc.
She saw three sightings, etc. This is stated there in the baraita concerning an elderly woman.
