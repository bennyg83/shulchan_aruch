Rather the matter is clear as I wrote above: after the second interruption her law is not the same as after the first interruption, in the sense that she would need anew another two sightings in addition; rather with one additional sighting the first one she saw after the first interruption is now consolidated into the count of three sightings — namely, if there are after it two more sightings, whenever they may occur, needless to say if they occur at precisely ninety-day intervals in which case she has an established veset; but even if they do not occur at exactly ninety-day intervals, whether the second one after the first interruption does not occur at a ninety-day interval — that is the "first" of Rav Gidal — or even if the second also occurs at ninety days but the third does not occur at ninety days but at the average onah — that is the "second" of Rav Gidal — even though she does not have an established veset, she is nevertheless considered established in bleeding from the third sighting onward, and she must therefore be concerned about a me'et le'et from the third onward. And thus Rav Gidal's second ruling teaches that the second sighting causes her to be concerned about a me'et le'et since it is the third counting from the one after the first interruption. And accordingly what Rashba wrote "and he calls it the first time, etc." is also well explained: Rav Gidal taught us that she does not return to presumption of bleeding until she sees three more sightings; Rashba was troubled by this, for Rav Gidal said "the first time, etc." — and at first glance this refers to the very first time, meaning before the interruption, and if so she would need two more sightings separately after it; to which he answered that "that first time" refers to the one after the interruption, and therefore she is properly established in bleeding with three sightings; and to this he concluded "and now three intervals emerge" — meaning, according to this interpretation that it refers to after the interruption, the baraita that cited three ninety-day intervals makes good sense; for if we interpreted "first time" as referring to the one before the interruption, there would be only three sightings and two ninety-day intervals — but now it works out that there are three equal intervals, since we are now also including the sighting before the interruption, though for purposes of "established in bleeding" we do not include it. And all is well with God's help.
"The Lord led me on the way of truth" — for after I wrote this I found in Sefer Bedek HaBayit on Torat HaBayit HaAroch, page 170, that he too wrote that "the first time" refers to the first sighting after the first interruption, and that after the second interruption the first sighting after it also helps such that if she sees a third sighting shortly afterward — and all the more so if she sees a third sighting after a longer interval — it helps so that she is concerned about me'et le'et, since even when the third sighting is distant it helps. And he wrote that Rashba objected to this interpretation that I wrote, and that Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit complained about him that he said Rashba himself wrote that interpretation, when in fact [the Rashba himself] wrote it clearly in his organized method, etc. — behold I reached the same view as Rashba, thank God, as he himself testified that he explained it so. And in truth it is the plain interpretation of what I mentioned above; and Beit Yosef and after him my father-in-law of blessed memory did not fulfill their obligation of sufficient examination in this matter.
