Through three sightings, etc. Nevertheless, so long as she has not yet seen three times, she need not be concerned at all, even for a non-established veses. Other women must be concerned for such a veses until it is uprooted at least once, but this minor is different, for we say that it was merely incidental.
If she did not see, she need not be concerned, etc. This means that if she did not see until after 90 days had passed and then saw, she still need not be concerned, for the sighting was incidental. The text then adds that even if she returned to see at those same onos, where there is greater reason to say that she returned to her original veses, she still does not yet become concerned.
And even if she returned to see at those, etc. The general rule is that once she ceased for three onos, the original veses is entirely gone, and she is as though she had never seen before. She must begin again with three sightings, as at the outset, in order to become established as a woman who sees blood. Even if she ceases a second time and then returns, failing to see for another three onos, we do not say that she now returns once more to her earlier status and again requires three new sightings after the second cessation. We do not go that far. Rather, the first sighting after the first cessation begins the process of establishing her. If she later sees two more times, whenever they occur, she becomes established as a woman who sees. Consequently, there are two possible patterns among the three sightings after the first cessation. If they occur in a manner capable of establishing a veses, namely on the same date three times, or through four sightings creating three equal intervals including the first sighting after the cessation, she establishes a veses like any other woman. She can also establish an interval veses through only two further intervals after the first post-cessation sighting. Thus, if after that first sighting she sees twice more at 90-day intervals, we combine the final sighting before the cessation as the starting point of the first interval, yielding four sightings and three equal intervals. In my view, even if she had no established veses before the cessation and saw only once before it, once she afterward sees three times at 90-day intervals she establishes a 90-day interval veses. This is obvious. If the sightings after the cessation cannot create an established veses because they are not equally aligned, the three sightings nevertheless establish her as a woman who sees blood from that time onward, and her law is that of a woman with no fixed veses, as explained in siman 187. This is unlike the Rashba's statement, cited by the Beit Yosef s.v. “and what he wrote, even if she returned,” that a minor and an elderly woman need not be concerned at all for a non-established veses, which the Rama brings in seif 28. That applies only before she has seen three times, when she is not yet established as a woman who sees blood. The woman described here has seen three times and is certainly established as a woman who sees blood, so her law is that of an adult woman without a fixed veses. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch write here that she need not be concerned until she returns and establishes it three times, etc. The word “establishes it” might imply that the sightings must specifically occur in a pattern that creates a fixed veses, and that if they do not, she need not be concerned at all. This is not so. The phrase refers back to the case where she originally had a fixed veses. It means that she need not be concerned for that original veses unless she reestablishes it through three aligned sightings. Otherwise she becomes merely like a woman with no fixed veses. Toras HaBayis HaAruch does not say “until she establishes it,” but rather “until she sees three times,” as I have explained. Toras HaBayis HaAruch, cited by the Beit Yosef under the heading “the language of the Rashba,” states: “He called the first sighting the first sighting she saw after three onos had passed, for now three equal intervals emerge among them,” etc. End quote. The Beit Yosef was troubled, for there appear to be only three sightings and two intervals. He therefore forced an explanation that the “first sighting” means the sighting she sees after the initial sighting following the three onos. He strengthened this interpretation from the later statement of Rav Gidal: the first sighting suffices at its time, and the second renders her tamei retroactively for 24 hours. According to the Rashba, who holds that Rav Gidal's ruling follows halacha, the “first” would appear to mean the first sighting at the end of the onah, yet he says that at the second sighting she becomes tamei retroactively. According to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, however, the veses is not established until a third sighting at the end of an onah. The Beit Yosef therefore concluded that “first” means the first sighting after the one about which the question was asked, namely after she again saw at the onos. The “second” would then actually be the third sighting after the sighting at the end of the onah. Together with the sighting before the three onos, there are four sightings and three intervals. End quote. I stood astonished for a long moment at our master the Beit Yosef. According to his approach, even after the second cessation she is treated like after the first cessation, and does not become established as a woman who sees blood until three new sightings after the second cessation. Thus his “second” is actually the third. If so, Rav Gidal's first and second statements would be identical, and why did he say in the first that both the first and second sightings suffice at their own time, but in the second that the second sighting renders her tamei retroactively? This is astonishing. Furthermore, the Rashba explicitly wrote, as cited by the Beit Yosef: “Any girl who has not reached the age of seeing is presumed to have no blood, and the first sighting is merely incidental and does not join until she becomes established through other sightings. Therefore, the second sighting is itself only one sighting and cannot establish the first. But when she sees a third time, the first is established together with the two later sightings, and those two reveal that the first was not incidental.” End quote. The “first” plainly refers to the first sighting after the first cessation. Were it to refer to a later sighting, why should that one become established through subsequent sightings any more than the sighting immediately after the cessation? Surely that sighting too should become established through those that follow it. If so, why did Rav Gidal say in his first statement that the second sighting suffices only at its own time? According to the Beit Yosef's explanation, that is already the third sighting after the post-cessation sighting and should render her tamei retroactively because she has seen three times after the cessation.
Rather, the matter is clear as I wrote above. After the second cessation she is not treated like after the first cessation in the sense of requiring two entirely new sightings to join with the first after the second cessation. One additional sighting suffices to establish the first sighting after the initial cessation, and together with another third sighting she has three sightings. These may occur at any time. If they are aligned at 90-day intervals, she establishes a fixed veses. Even if they are not aligned, whether the second post-cessation sighting did not occur after 90 days, which is Rav Gidal's first case, or the second did occur after 90 days but the third occurred at the ordinary onah rather than after 90 days, which is Rav Gidal's second case, she is nevertheless established as a woman who sees blood and becomes tamei retroactively from the third sighting onward. This is why Rav Gidal said in his second statement that the second sighting renders her tamei retroactively, since it is the third sighting counting from the first sighting after the initial cessation. The Rashba's words, “and he called it the first sighting,” are now also properly understood. Rav Gidal taught that she does not return to an established status regarding blood until she again sees three sightings. The Rashba was troubled that Rav Gidal called it the “first” sighting, which would appear to refer to the sighting before the cessation. In that case only two further sightings would seem necessary. He therefore explained that “first” refers to the sighting after the cessation, and then she indeed becomes established through three sightings. The Rashba concludes that now three equal intervals emerge. According to our explanation that “first” refers to the sighting after the cessation, the Baraisa's statement of three 90-day intervals is settled. Had “first” referred to the sighting before the cessation, there would be only three sightings and two 90-day intervals. According to our explanation there are three equal intervals because we now combine the sighting before the cessation for calculating the interval, although we do not combine it for establishing her as a woman who sees blood. Everything is resolved, with God's help.
I was led by Hashem on the path of truth, for after writing this I saw in Bedek HaBayis on Toras HaBayis HaAruch, folio 170, that he likewise wrote that the “first sighting” is the first sighting after the first cessation, and that after the second cessation the first sighting following it also helps, so that if she then sees a third sighting at a shorter interval, all the more so does it help to render her tamei retroactively, since even a more distant third sighting helps. He wrote that the Rashba challenged the approach I have set forth, and that the Rashba in Mishmeres HaBayis complained that Bedek HaBayis attributed that approach to him, when in truth the Rashba himself had written it in proper order. Thus, I have, thank God, arrived at the Rashba's own view, as he himself testified that he explained it in this manner. In truth, this is the straightforward explanation I stated above. The Beit Yosef, followed by my father-in-law of blessed memory, did not fulfill the obligation of careful analysis in this matter.
