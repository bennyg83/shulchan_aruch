A woman who saw. Tur quotes Ramban: A monthly veset must be observed after one occurrence, but an interval veset cannot be observed until a second sighting, since before then she does not know the interval. Thus, a woman who sees on Rosh Chodesh Nisan must be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar. If she sees then, she must be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Sivan. If she sees then, the Rosh Chodesh veset is established. If she does not see on Rosh Chodesh Sivan, the Rosh Chodesh veset is uprooted, and she must be concerned for the second of Sivan, since she may establish a thirty-one-day interval. Beit Yosef, Bach, and Perishah understand that she must always observe three concerns: the interval veset, the corresponding calendar date, and the thirtieth day as the average interval. Shach argues at length that she observes only the interval veset and the corresponding calendar date, which itself is the average interval whether the month is full or deficient. His proofs are strong.
Whether she saw on the first of Iyar or did not see then. Bach and Perishah agree with Beit Yosef that if she saw on the first of Iyar she need not be concerned for the ninth. Shach agrees, both here and in Nekudot HaKesef. Taz discusses the matter at length to uphold Rama's view. This also appears explicitly from Raavad in Baalei HaNefesh, folio 56a; see there.
She need not be concerned for a progressive veset. For example, she saw on the fifteenth of Nisan. This is explained in the Gemara at the end of HaIshah and is accepted by the early and later poskim, as Beit Yosef explains above in seif 11. Shach writes that this is Rama's interpretation of Ramban and that it is correct, although Beit Yosef and other later authorities disagree. I have not found any actual dispute regarding this point. All later authorities agree. Their disagreement concerns only the preceding case, namely until one of them is uprooted. Perhaps there is a printing error.

Only eleven. In a calendar-date progression, the first sighting is counted. In a progression of intervals, however, the first sighting can never be counted, because every interval pattern requires four sightings. According to the opinion that the first sighting is not counted even for a calendar-date progression, five sightings are required for progressive intervals, as stated above in seif katan 8. Taz and Shach write similarly, although Shach leaves Rama's words requiring study. According to what I have written, they are resolved.
We always follow the beginning of the flow. The blood seen afterward is treated as additional blood that was added to the initial flow. She therefore establishes a progressive veset and must be concerned for the eighteenth of Tammuz, nineteenth of Av, and so on. The Mechaber rules similarly above in siman 184 seif 6. Maadanei Melech asks that this appears to contradict siman 184, where a woman whose veset sometimes comes two or three days early or late must separate for those additional days. Shach offers an answer. In truth, the cases are entirely different. There the woman has no established veset, and her entire sighting sometimes begins two or three days early or late. Here she has an established progressive veset determined by the beginning of the flow. She therefore observes the progressive veset based on its commencement.
