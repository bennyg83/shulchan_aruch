The first day. Taz questions Bach's statement that she need not observe the first day of Rosh Chodesh. Shach concludes that an ordinary onah means the corresponding calendar date in the next month, whether the month is full or deficient. Chacham Tzvi disagrees and maintains that the ordinary onah is always the thirtieth day from the prior sighting.
She is concerned. Bach disputes Rama and holds if she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar she is no longer concerned for 9th of it; and so Shach at length, supporting his words in Nekudot HaKesef; see there. But Taz agreed to Rav's view that even if she saw on Rosh Chodesh she must be concerned for all concerns; and he concluded the matter is clear that Moses is true and his Torah is true and his words here are widespread halachah in Israel; see there.
She does not. Shach implies that she need not observe the fourteenth of Iyar even though it is thirty days from the fifteenth of Nisan. This supports identifying the monthly veset with the ordinary onah.
For 17th. For skipping veset she need not be concerned as above; and also interval veset does not exist, for Nisan is full and Iyar deficient; thus 16th of Iyar is interval of 32 from 15th of Nisan, and 17th of Sivan is interval of 31 from 16th of Iyar; therefore she is also not concerned for 18th of Tammuz, for it is not interval of 31 but 32 from 17th of Sivan; she must be concerned for the last interval which is 31.
To the 18th. That is according to the first view above seif 7, but according to the alternative view there, she has already established a skipping veses with three sightings and is concerned here for the 18th of Tammuz, as the Rama concluded — that with a new skipping pattern, the alternative view holds that the first sighting counts in the tally. Accordingly, we are dealing here with a case where she previously had a fixed veses on the 15th of the month, so according to all views we attribute the sighting of the 15th of Nissan to her veses and it is not counted in the tally — thus far the Shach.
By interval. Shach explains a progressively increasing interval veset. After a twenty-day interval she observes the next twenty-day date and also the monthly date. If the interval then becomes twenty-one and later twenty-two days, she observes each new interval until repeated equal increments establish a skipping veset. Establishing such an interval pattern requires enough sightings to identify each interval.

For a skipping pattern. Taz notes that Ramban follows Shmuel, who ordinarily requires four sightings. Beit Yosef explains that where she previously had a fixed veset, even Shmuel agrees that three changed sightings suffice. Taz concludes that practical halachah is stringent like Rav and treats three sightings as establishing the pattern.
Primary. Shach explains that the earlier ruling does not require separation for all three possible days. Rather, once her established veset shifts earlier or later by two or three days, she must also separate on the days by which it regularly shifts.
