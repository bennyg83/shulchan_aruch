She must be concerned by day and by night. The daytime veses is not uprooted until it has been uprooted three times, while the nighttime veses is uprooted after a single occasion on which she does not see at night.
Or if the first sighting was by day, etc. This is the correct text. Likewise, afterward the text should read: or the first sighting was at night.
She must be concerned for the 1st of Iyar because of Rosh Chodesh Nisan. It is obvious that here too she must also be concerned for the first day of Rosh Chodesh Iyar, which is the 30th day from the sighting of Rosh Chodesh Nisan, as the ordinary onah for a woman who has no fixed veses, as stated in seif 12. Since this is obvious, it did not need to be explained. My father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that because only the 1st of Iyar was mentioned, we may infer that she is not concerned for the first day of Rosh Chodesh. I do not know why he would contradict his own words above, where he wrote that if she saw on Rosh Chodesh Nisan she must be concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar, meaning both days of Rosh Chodesh, as he himself explained.
Whether she saw on the 1st of Iyar or did not see, etc. In the Tur, citing the Ramban, the text reads here: and if she did not see, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote that this implies that if she did see on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, she need not be concerned for the 9th of Iyar. The reason is that if she returned and saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, we would say that the first sighting on Rosh Chodesh Nisan was the primary one, while the second sighting on the 20th of Nisan was only an additional flow of blood. She would therefore not be concerned either for the 9th of Iyar or for the 20th. But now that she did not see on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, it is revealed that the sighting on the 20th of Nisan was primary, and she must be concerned for both the 9th and the 20th of Iyar. The Darkei Moshe disagreed and wrote that from the words shortly afterward, “she must be concerned for both until one becomes established,” we infer that before one veses has been established three times she must be concerned for both. Furthermore, the text shortly afterward states that if she saw on the 9th, etc., which implies that although she has already seen twice at 20-day intervals, she must nevertheless be concerned for the days of the month and for the 20th of Iyar. The same applies here. Although she saw on the 1st of Iyar, she must still be concerned for the 9th because of the sighting on the 20th of Nisan. As for the wording “if she did not see on the 1st of Iyar,” it appears to me that it was stated as a greater novelty: although the veses of Rosh Chodesh Nisan was uprooted, she must nevertheless be concerned for the second veses so long as it has not been uprooted even once. End quote. This appears to me to be the primary view. The Rama therefore correctly amended the text here to read, “whether she saw or did not see, etc.” We do not find anywhere that a sighting at a changed time uproots a preexisting concern so long as the changed pattern itself has not been established according to its law, whether by three or four occurrences. Moreover, suppose she also saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, on the 9th of Iyar, and on the 28th of Iyar. Would she not thereby establish a 20-day interval veses beginning with the sighting on the 20th of Nisan? Certainly she would, for there are three equal intervals, and the additional sighting on Rosh Chodesh Iyar is irrelevant. How, then, could we permit her, merely because she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, not to be concerned at all for the 9th when this could still develop into an established veses? The Perishah wrote that although it is possible to establish a 20-day interval veses in this manner, we are not initially concerned for it before seeing three equal intervals. This is difficult, for whenever such a pattern is possible we certainly are concerned lest it develop, as we find throughout this siman, except for a progressive veses, for which there is no concern until it becomes established, as stated in seif 11. A further clear proof appears at the end of this siman in the Tur. If she saw three times on Rosh Chodesh, and on the third occasion she also saw on the 25th of the month and on Rosh Chodesh, she must also be concerned for the earlier day, the 25th, lest she be establishing another veses. This is a kal vachomer. There, three Rosh Chodesh sightings are clearly before us, and Rosh Chodesh is also the ordinary onah for a woman, yet she must still be concerned for the 25th. All the more so here, where only two sightings are before us, one on the 20th of Nisan and one on Rosh Chodesh Iyar. One of them must be primary and the other incidental, but how do you know that Rosh Chodesh is primary and that she need not be concerned for the 9th? Perhaps the sighting on the 20th of Nisan is primary and she must be concerned for the 9th of Iyar. The blood of Rosh Chodesh Iyar is not redder than the blood of the 20th of Nisan. It therefore appears primary, in accordance with the Rama, that even if she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar she must be concerned for the 9th and the 20th of Iyar. However, the Rama's explanation that the Tur's wording “if she did not see” was stated only as a greater novelty does not settle well with me, for if so, afterward too, where it says whether she saw or did not see on the 9th, it should have stated that same novelty. My father-in-law of blessed memory went very far in disagreeing even with the Rama and said that it is an outright error to require concern for the 9th when she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, because the sighting on Rosh Chodesh Iyar interrupted the interval. If she were to be concerned for a 20-day interval, she should count it from the sighting on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, as the Tur states shortly afterward: if she was accustomed to see on the 20th and changed to the 30th, both are forbidden, and when the 20th day from the sighting on the 30th arrives she is forbidden. This shows that she is not concerned for a 20-day interval from the sighting preceding the 30th, but rather for a possible sighting 20 days after the 30th-day sighting, in accordance with Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua at the end of the chapter Benos Kusim. End quote. I say that honor is the inheritance of the wise, but these words are like an inadvertent error before a ruler. On the contrary, that passage proves the Rama's view. There, we count only from the sighting on the 30th because there was no actual sighting earlier on the 20th; she had only been expected to see then, but she delayed until the 30th. Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua therefore holds that at the end of Benos Kusim we do not count from the time she was expected to see but from when she actually saw, in contrast to Rav Pappa, who holds that we count from when she was expected to see. This is clear in the sugya, and it is also evident from the wording of Rashi and Toras HaBayis HaAruch. They wrote that if we were to count from when she was expected to see, we would be concerned that the veses might come earlier after a shorter interval than her present sighting, and this is unreasonable: she changed by delaying, yet we would be concerned that she might now advance. End quote. This implies that if she actually saw also on the day when she was accustomed to see, we see that she indeed advanced, and she must certainly be concerned for the 20th day from that sighting and also for the 20th day from the sighting on the 30th. Accordingly, here too she must be concerned for a 20-day interval both from the 20th of Nisan and from Rosh Chodesh Iyar. It therefore appears obvious that if she actually saw also on the 20th and added another sighting on the 30th, then according to all opinions we count from the sighting on the 20th and require concern for a 20-day interval from it as well. The intervening sighting on the 30th does not benefit her by creating a leniency. Thus the matter is clear: Moshe is true and his Torah is true, and the Rama's words here are accepted halacha among the Jewish people. Whether she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar or did not see, all the stated concerns remain.
Until she establishes one veses according to its law. This means that the other veses was not established. For example, if the monthly veses was established first, she saw on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, Rosh Chodesh Iyar, the 14th of Iyar, the 25th of Iyar, and Rosh Chodesh Sivan. The Rosh Chodesh veses is established, while the interval vestos occurred only twice. At times, however, the interval veses becomes established first. For example, she saw on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, the 14th of Nisan, the 28th of Nisan, Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and the 12th of Iyar. She thereby establishes a 14-day interval veses, while the Rosh Chodesh vestos are not yet established. It has already been explained in seif 7 that regarding progressive vestos we follow the stringent view in such a case. Thus, if she saw on the 15th of Nisan, the 16th of Iyar, and the 17th of Sivan, and also saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar and Rosh Chodesh Sivan, we do not say that the progressive veses has already been established and that she need no longer be concerned for the monthly vestos. We do not rely on that progression leniently; rather, four progressive sightings are specifically required.
Or until one of them is uprooted, then she is no longer, etc. This means that even a single occasion on which that anticipated veses arrives and she does not see uproots it, since it had not yet been established three times. The correct text in the Tur is likewise “or until it is uprooted,” as the Beit Yosef testified that he saw in the Ramban's Hilchos Niddah.
And the same method applies to intervals and progressions. This means that the progression is not based on dates of the month, such as 15, 16, and 17, but on increasing intervals. She saw a first sighting, and 20 days later a second. When 20 days after the second sighting arrive, she must be concerned. If the 20th day arrives and she does not see, she no longer need be concerned for that interval. If she sees a third sighting on the following day, namely 21 days after the second, then when 21 days after the third arrive she must be concerned. If she does not see that day, she no longer need be concerned for that interval. If on the following day, 22 days after the third sighting, she sees a fourth sighting, then when the 22nd day after the fourth arrives she must be concerned. If she does not see, she no longer need be concerned for that interval. If on the following day, 23 days after the fourth sighting, she sees a fifth sighting, she has established a progressive interval veses. From then onward, she need be concerned only for the progression and need not be concerned for the 23rd day after the fifth sighting. So wrote the Tur in the name of the Ramban, requiring five sightings. This follows Shmuel, mentioned above in seif 7 according to the first opinion, which requires three completed progressions. One should not ask why above four sightings suffice, while here five are required. Above, the progression is based on dates of the month, 15, 16, and 17, so the date of the first sighting is independently recognizable. Here, however, we begin from her sighting after 20 days, and one cannot know where those 20 days begin without an earlier sighting. Therefore, the initial sighting before the 20-day interval and the sighting on the 20th day are considered together as one unit, since neither is meaningful without the other. According to the second opinion above, which follows Rav and holds that 15, 16, and 17 suffice, only four sightings are required here for the reason just stated. This is what the Rama later wrote: “There are those who say that for a monthly progression the first sighting is included in the count,” meaning the later opinion in seif 7. According to that view, four sightings suffice here, as explained. See what I will write shortly concerning the Rama's statement, “There are those who say that she must be concerned for the progression,” and what appears to me to be the Ramban's view in this ruling.
And her flow continued for three days. In the Tur the text reads four days, and regarding the 16th of Iyar it states there two or three days. It appears that this was written to teach a greater novelty. Although the sighting on the 17th in the first and second occurrences was not at the end of the onah but in its middle, we nevertheless regard it as the principal onah. Regarding the third occurrence on the 17th, it does not state that she saw on both the 17th and 18th, as it did regarding the first two occurrences, for had it done so it would have been obvious that she established a veses for the 17th and 18th, since those two dates were equal in every occurrence.
And in Sivan she began, etc. Something is missing here. The text should read: and in Iyar she saw on the 16th, and in Sivan, etc.
There are those who say that she must be concerned for the progression, etc. This is difficult, for these are the Ramban's words in the Tur, and the Tur already wrote above in the Ramban's name that we rule like Shmuel and require four sightings for a progressive veses. The Beit Yosef answered that here the case is one in which she already had a veses before she began seeing progressively, and in that case even Shmuel agrees that three sightings suffice, as stated in seif 7. The Ramban should therefore have explained that the case is of a woman who had been accustomed to see every 14 days, and then changed and saw on the 15th and her flow continued, etc. The Derishah cites the Maharshal, and my father-in-law of blessed memory likewise ruled, that the case concerns the law if she later sees a fourth time on the 18th of the month. According to this, the Tur's concluding words are settled: she is concerned for the dates on which she saw three times as for an established veses, and for the other dates as for a progressive veses, implying that the progressive veses is not yet established. End quote. I am astonished by their words. How can he write that she is concerned for the other dates as for a progressive veses? Since the progression has not been established according to its law, there is no concern for it when the date on which she would be expected to see according to the progression arrives, until she has already progressed three times according to its law, meaning four sightings, as stated in seif 11. And if the Tur's case is that she has already seen a fourth time on the 18th, then the progressive veses is as fully established as the veses of the 17th. Why, then, does he call only the veses of the 17th established? It appears to me that although the Ramban was quoted as ruling like Shmuel, the Ramban himself nevertheless rules stringently in practice like Rav, that three sightings suffice, as I wrote at the end of seif 7. A proof is that in the chapter HaIshah SheOsah Tzeracheha, the Rosh wrote that the Rambam ruled like Shmuel and that the Ramban also found that view reasonable. He did not write that the Ramban ruled like Shmuel, as he did regarding the Rambam, but only that it appeared reasonable to him. In actual practice, however, the Ramban is stringent that three sightings already create a prohibition. This is also evident from the wording of Toras HaBayis HaAruch. It first records the view of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Raavad like Rav, and then writes that the Rambam ruled like Shmuel and that the Ramban wrote that this seems reasonable. It then continues: “Nevertheless, one should be concerned for the words of Rabbeinu Chananel,” etc. End quote. The word “nevertheless” implies that the Ramban himself wrote this, meaning that although Shmuel's view appeared reasonable to him, one should in practice be stringent like Rav. Were “nevertheless” the Rashba's own language, he should have written simply that one should be stringent like Rabbeinu Chananel. Rather, it is necessarily as I have explained, that the Ramban himself wrote that one should be stringent like Rav in practice. One should not ask from what the Tur wrote earlier in the Ramban's name, concerning a sighting on the 23rd, etc., which implies that four sightings are required like Shmuel, as I mentioned shortly above. There, the question is one of leniency, namely that from then onward she need be concerned only for the progression. If we were to say that the progressive veses becomes established through three sightings, a leniency would result, for she would no longer be concerned for the 23rd day, as explained there. He therefore required four sightings there, because only then may we follow the pattern leniently. Here, however, if we say that the progressive veses becomes established through three sightings, the result is a stringency, since she must thereafter be concerned for that progression. In this respect we should be stringent like Rav. I already discussed this in seif 7. As for the Tur's statement that regarding the 17th she is concerned as for an established veses, it means as for an ordinary veses of a woman that occurs on an equal calendar date and is not progressive, as the Beit Yosef explained. According to the Maharshal's approach, the Tur should not have said “as for an established veses,” using the comparative kaf. This appears correct to me.
And there are those who say that there is no equal veses there, etc. Rather, there is only the progressive veses.
