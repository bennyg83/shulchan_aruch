She is concerned by day and by night. For both are forbidden, as below seif 14.
And neither one was established three times. For if the last was established three times — that is what the Mechaber then wrote: “or she sees, etc., she is concerned for the last alone”; and if the first was established three times but not the last — that is what the Mechaber wrote before: “and if she saw three times, etc., and the fourth, etc., she is concerned by day and by night”; and it is easily understood.
The woman who saw, etc. Tur 30: Ramban — veset ha-chodesh she is concerned at its beginning once; but veset ha-hefsek cannot be concerned until she sees a second sighting, for she does not know on which day she intervals; one says the woman who sees on Rosh Chodesh Nisan is concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar — if she saw she is concerned for Rosh Chodesh Sivan; if she saw, her veset was fixed for Rosh Chodesh; if she did not see on Rosh Chodesh Sivan the Rosh Chodesh veset was uprooted and she is concerned for the 2nd of Sivan; perhaps she will see and establish a veset for interval from 31st to 31st, for the sightings of Rosh Chodesh Nisan and Iyar are equal in interval to seeing on the 2nd of Sivan — Nisan full and Iyar deficient — and forever she is concerned for these concerns for veset ha-chodesh and interval until she establishes one of them three times as required. How: she saw on the 1st of Nisan and 20th, etc. Beit Yosef wrote: what he wrote that we are concerned she will establish from 31st to 31st is not to say she need not be concerned for another day, for she has no fixed veset and whoever has no fixed veset is concerned for the 30th day which is onah beinonis; rather it means that even if Rosh Chodesh comes on the 30th day of the sighting it does not have the law of a fixed veset but has the law of an unfixed veset to be concerned — end quote; Darkei Moshe: if she saw on the 2nd of Sivan or another day she must be concerned for the 30th day from that sighting; and so Perishah s.k. 65; and Bach seif 16: if she saw on the 2nd of Sivan when Rosh Chodesh Tammuz arrives she is concerned for the 2nd of Rosh Chodesh Tammuz because of onah beinonis which is 30, and for the 2nd of Tammuz which is for interval — end quote; and Taz also wrote like Beit Yosef and added she must be concerned for onah beinonis even if she does not see that day, and the 30th day is not uprooted once in a woman without fixed veset as a fixed veset in a woman with veset — end quote (and this is not correct at all — investigate). Accordingly likewise at the beginning of her sighting she must be concerned for the 30th day and the day of the month; and so in Perishah s.k. 63 and Bach: what Ramban wrote — the woman who sees on Rosh Chodesh Nisan is concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar — means for both days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar: for the 1st of Rosh Chodesh because it is the 30th day of her sighting which is onah beinonis, and for the 2nd of Rosh Chodesh because of Rosh Chodesh — end quote. But investigate this: if so Ramban and the Rav who came to explain what concerns she must have — why did they not write and explain she must also be concerned for onah beinonis? Rather from their plain words "the woman is concerned for veset ha-chodesh and interval" and "she is concerned for both until she establishes one" — it appears she need only be concerned for these two concerns and no more. Further wonder: certainly when we say onah beinonis is 30 days we mean she must be concerned for the 31st day of her sighting — onah beinonis is 30 days from beginning of sighting to beginning of sighting; and Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit 167b; Ramban: "he found her within the days of her onah — how long is onah? 30 days from beginning of sighting to beginning of next sighting, for most women see thus from thirty to thirty," etc.; Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 4 Avot HaTumah 30 Ramban on this; Rashi Chullin 15a on "all women are in presumption of purity for their husbands when he comes and finds her within the days of her onah" — within 30 days of sighting, but after 30 she needs examination since women ordinarily see at the end of onah — end quote; plainly even on the 30th day itself she is permitted since onah beinonis is 30 days; if so she need only be concerned for the 30th day from the end of the first day she saw — the 31st from beginning of sighting; and Tur above siman 184: if 30 days passed it counts as her veset arriving — ordinarily onah is 30 days and forbidden at the end of 30 until she examines — plainly need only be concerned for the 31st; and so from poskim and Taz below seif 27 who wrote three onot beinonis is 90 days — if onah were only 28 it would be 84; rather onah is 30 days from beginning to beginning. If so wonder why Ramban needed to say concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar — seems because of Rosh Chodesh Iyar; but without that she would be concerned for onah beinonis anyway. Likewise why need to say concerned for the 2nd of Sivan — perhaps she will establish interval; let it suffice as onah beinonis. Many difficulties for Beit Yosef, Perishah, and Bach who hold every woman must be concerned for the day of the month and onah beinonis — how say she must be concerned for onah beinonis because veset is from thirty to thirty when she must be concerned for the month-day she saw? Rather it appears: when we say onah beinonis is 30 days we mean month to month, full or deficient — we find this often; 30 days is a month; regarding bechor we rule 305 that it is not redeemed until 30 days pass — plainly in Gemara in many places; meaning a month as Yere'im siman 253 wrote. But Raavad in Ba'alei Nefesh wrote a woman must first be concerned for veset ha-chodesh and veset ha-hefsek and did not mention onah beinonis at all; and at the end of Sha'ar HaVesetot he listed all vesetot fixed and unfixed and did not list onah beinonis; Rambam ch. 8 Avot HaTumah 6: every day she saw she is concerned for the next day and did not mention onah beinonis of thirty days; Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen Sha'ar HaVesetot wrote many times one without fixed veset must be concerned for onah beinonis of 30 days and did not mention veset ha-chodesh; below siman 196 (8) Ramban and Taz on a deaf woman without fixed veset: she is like all women concerned from thirty to thirty and did not write she must be concerned for the month-day — rather onah beinonis is month to month on that month-day she must be concerned for the coming month and no more. Clear proof: poskim and Taz below seif 27 wrote three onot is 90 days — from when we say onah beinonis is 30 days as in Beit Yosef; Rambam ch. 4 Metamei Mishkav uMoshav 20: three onot are three months, brought by Beit Yosef below end s.v. what they wrote she saw three sightings from three aligned onot — plainly what we call onah beinonis 30 days means a month; therefore Ramban and Rashi explained onah beinonis is 30 days from beginning to beginning — in an ordinary full month; and likewise if the month is deficient it is 29 days; they did not need this explanation since ordinarily month is full; and since she need only be concerned for that month-day forever, no need to distinguish full and deficient — see above s.k. 33 and 43 for more proofs.
She saw on the 1st of Iyar or did not see, etc. In Tur in name of Ramban: if she did not see she is concerned for the 9th — plainly from "if she did not see" and afterward "she saw on the 9th or did not see" — if she saw on Rosh Chodesh Iyar she is not concerned for the 9th, as Beit Yosef and the Rav understood per his reasoning; Darkei Moshe: likewise if she saw as below — rather a greater matter: even though Rosh Chodesh veset was uprooted she must be concerned for the 20th that was not uprooted — end quote; brought by Ma'adanei Melekh 296b; unlike Bach who wrote perhaps the Rav saw thus in old Tur manuscripts — end quote. But the essence: if she saw on the 1st of Iyar she need not be concerned for the 9th, for she ceased when she saw in between and there is no interval of twenty days but of twelve; she changed to twelve and need only be concerned for the twelve-day interval from this sighting on the 1st of Iyar, as below seif 14; and she is also concerned for the 20th of Iyar because of veset ha-chodesh and for the 1st of Sivan because of Rosh Chodesh Iyar; but for veset ha-chodesh she is concerned whether she saw before or not, as long as she did not change her month-veset; and so Perishah s.k. 68 and Bach seif 16; see above s.k. 40.
She saw on the 15th of Nisan etc. This is the view of the Rabbi in his explanation of Ramban’s words, and it is correct even though Beit Yosef and other Acharonim do not hold this view.
She is concerned for the 15th of Iyar. For the monthly veset; and it implies she need not be concerned for the 14th of Iyar even though it is thirty days from the sighting on the 15th of Nisan; and this is proof for what I wrote in s.k. 30 that the monthly veset is the onah beinonis.
She is concerned for the 16th of Sivan. For the monthly veset (and also because of the interval); and she is not concerned for the 17th of Sivan because of the skip veset; and there is also no interval veset, for Nisan is full and Iyar is deficient — hence the 16th of Iyar is an interval of 32 from the 15th of Nisan, and the 17th of Sivan is an interval of 31 from the 16th of Iyar; and therefore she is also not concerned for the 18th of Tammuz which is not an interval of 31 but of 32 from the 17th of Sivan; and she must be concerned for the last interval which is 31.
And she is not concerned for the 18th. This is for Ramban — the first view above seif 7; but the second view there already established veset ha-dilug with three sightings and she is concerned here for the 18th of Tammuz, as the Rav concludes — only the second view, for in monthly skip the first sighting counts, etc.; per this it deals where she had a fixed veset before on the 15th of the month — then we judge the Nisan 15th sighting for her veset and it does not count, as above s.k. 11.
And so on this path, with intervals and skips, etc. That is, she skipped in interval — such as she saw on the first of Nisan and at the end of twenty she is concerned for this interval (that is, the 9th of Iyar which is an interval of 20 from the 20th of Nisan; however she is also concerned for Rosh Chodesh Iyar for the monthly veset); she skipped to the 21st day (that is, the 10th of Iyar, and on Rosh Chodesh Iyar she did not see) — she is concerned for the 21st day (that is, Rosh Chodesh Sivan; however she is also concerned for the 20th of Iyar lest she establish a veset by days of the month, since she saw the 20th of Nisan as above); the 21st day arrived and she did not see — she is permitted to have relations (on the 22nd, and need not be concerned for the interval in skip); she skipped to the 22nd day — she is concerned for the 22nd day (that is, the 23rd of Sivan); the 22nd arrived and she did not see — she is permitted to have relations (on the 23rd of her sighting, that is the 24th of Sivan — and this is for Shmuel; or for Rav in the case where she had a veset before for every twenty; but when she had no veset, for Rav it suffices with four sightings for the interval to establish a veset for skip in interval, as I wrote in s.k. 8); a sighting occurred to her on the 23rd day — she established a veset for skipping; from here onward she is only concerned for her skip. So wrote Tur in the name of Ramban, and I added explanation.
Only the second view, for in monthly skip etc. Requires investigation — the second view that in monthly skip the first sighting counts also holds for interval skip that the first sighting counts, as above s.k. 8 — see there and investigate; only that an interval is not known except by two sightings, as above seif 2; and it is not possible with fewer than four sightings even in equal intervals.
And she continued her sighting three days, etc. And it deals with where afterward she saw on the 16th of Iyar and on the 17th thereof; and some books explicitly have this reading, and so it is in the Tur.
And likewise the essence. Ma'adanei Melekh 296a wondered: above siman 184 seif 2 wrote if a woman changes her veset to advance two or three days or delay when her veset arrives she must separate from her two or three days before or after — end quote; it appears to resolve: above it does not mean she must separate all three days — what relevance to changing veset? Rather she had a veset and afterward changes to advance two or three days — then she must also separate before the veset per how she is accustomed to advance; e.g. if accustomed to advance three days she must separate that day — as written here; and this is what was written above and in siman 189.
