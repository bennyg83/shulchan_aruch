A pregnant woman, etc.; and a nursing woman, etc. This follows from the statement there, 10b, “during all the days of pregnancy, their law is sufficient from the time of discovery,” etc., and from the statement there that even if they repeatedly experienced a flow, their law remains sufficient from the time of discovery. This implies that the rule applies even if she saw several times. Tosafot there likewise write this, s.v. Their law is sufficient, etc. It follows Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan. Although Rabbi Yehudah, Rabbi Yose, and Rabbi Shimon disagree with Rabbi Meir, since Rabbi Yochanan and Shmuel hold like Rabbi Meir, that is the halachah. As has been explained, we do not follow a dispute among Tannaim when the Amoraim themselves dispute the matter. We likewise do not rule like Rabbi Yose in the Mishnah, who says regarding a pregnant or nursing woman that intervals passed, etc. Although a Tanna taught before him there, 10b, and he said to him, “You have opened,” etc., that Tanna merely came to explain the earlier teaching. Rabbi Yochanan likewise holds this view there, 9a. This is the view of the Raavad. The Ramban disagrees and writes that she can establish a veset during the nursing period, based on the statement there, 11b: “According to the one who says she must examine, lest,” etc.; “This is satisfactory,” etc. The Rashba rejects this proof and says that the passage follows Rabbi Yehudah, Rabbi Yose, and Rabbi Shimon, who apply their view to all of them, whereas we rule like Rabbi Meir. See there, 171b, where he discusses this at length.

Nevertheless, she is concerned, etc. Torat HaBayit cites this in the name of the Raavad. He compares it to the eleventh day according to Rav Pappa there, 39a and 39b, who says that she must be concerned even though she cannot establish a veset. The Raavad follows his own ruling in accordance with Rav Pappa. His reason is the statement there, 68b, where Rav Huna bar Chiya said, “When we say,” etc.; if we were to say that she is not even concerned for it, it would be regarded as nonexistent. The Rashba writes, however, that the other poskim rule like Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, and that even for a fixed veset she is not concerned during this period, as stated there. All the more so is this true for a non-fixed veset, since vesetot are rabbinic. Furthermore, the Gemara there says, “When Ravin came,” etc. The passage cited by the Raavad is no proof, because when a woman is presumed removed from bleeding, we may attribute a sighting to any minor cause even though she is not required to be concerned for that date at all. Nevertheless, the Rashba concludes that although this appears to be the primary ruling, one should take the Raavad's words into account.
