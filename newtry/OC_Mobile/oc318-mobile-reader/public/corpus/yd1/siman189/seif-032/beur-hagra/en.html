And the fourth time on the twentieth of the month, etc. This is the correct reading. It is from the Raavad; see Shach. The Raavad follows his own view that even today the law remains as in the Gemara, namely, that a woman does not establish a veset during the days of zivah, as stated there, 39a and 39b. He therefore mentions the twentieth day. Rosh Chodesh falls within the eleven days preceding the twentieth, so he says that she first established the pattern on Rosh Chodesh. The Raavad writes that although she cannot initially establish a veset during those days, once established it is not uprooted until it is displaced three times. This is the meaning of “and the fourth time on the twentieth of the month, and Rosh Chodesh,” etc. The Ramban, Rashba in Torat HaBayit, and Tur, however, have already ruled that nowadays a woman always establishes a veset. The daughters of Israel accepted additional stringency upon themselves, etc., so that they would not need to calculate the openings of niddah and zivah. Were this not so, one would still have to teach them those calculations. Accordingly, nine sightings are unnecessary. Six suffice: three on Rosh Chodesh and three on the twentieth of the month, or on an earlier date.
