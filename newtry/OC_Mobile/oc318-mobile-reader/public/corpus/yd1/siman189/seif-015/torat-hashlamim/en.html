She changed her sightings. This also refers to the preceding case. She had an established twenty-day veset and changed to thirty, thirty-two, and thirty-four days. There are only four sightings and two progressive intervals, so according to the first opinion she has no established veset. If she then sees after thirty-six days, there are five sightings and three progressive intervals, and she establishes a progressive veset. She must afterward be concerned for thirty-eight days, and so on. According to the second opinion cited above, four sightings suffice for a progressive interval veset. See what I wrote in seif katan 8 and see Shach.
She has no veset at all. She must be concerned for the thirty-four-day interval and for the average interval, as explained above. According to what was stated in seif 7, she must also be concerned for the progressive pattern.
The same applies if she ceased seeing for three intervals. Beit Yosef explains Tur in this manner. Maharshal interprets Tur differently and with clear reasoning, and Bach agrees. Although Derishah and Taz challenge him, see Nekudot HaKesef, which resolves all his words soundly.
On the day of the original veset. Meaning, she must first see once and then again twenty days later. Since the veset was uprooted by three absent intervals, she must reencounter the twentieth day itself. By contrast, if she missed the twenty-day interval only once, she need not continue counting that interval, because without a new sighting there is no interval to count. This differs from a monthly veset, for if she missed one Rosh Chodesh she remains concerned for the next until three Rosh Chodesh dates pass. Nevertheless, as soon as she sees again, whenever that may be, she immediately resumes concern for her original twenty-day veset, since it was uprooted only once. If three intervals passed, however, she must specifically see again at the original twenty-day interval. Shach in Nekudot HaKesef explains the Shulchan Aruch this way, unlike Taz. This differs from a pregnant or nursing woman, whose veset is merely suspended by her condition and therefore returns immediately when the condition ends.
