Nevertheless, etc. See Tosafot at the end of s.v. She sneezes.
And all these, etc. Meaning, she establishes these bodily vesetot even without particular days. They are unlike jumping, which results from an external compulsion. The proof is from the statement there, “If she was accustomed,” etc., followed by Rabbi Yose's statement, “also days and hours,” etc. This implies that the first clause concerns a pattern without fixed days. For this reason Rabbi Yose and Rabbi Yehudah do not disagree there, as explained above in seif 3; see there. Tosafot there, s.v. She ate, etc., write an even stronger point: “One may say,” etc.
And if it came, etc. In this case it is certainly comparable to jumping.
