Thus she establishes on the days of the month. Ra'ah wrote in Beit HaElohim that this does not refer to kiddush of the month and our shofar blast, but to the molad of the moon at the hour when it appears fit to fix the month by sight. But Rashba in Milchamot Hashem wrote that certainly the shofar blast causes it, and everything follows full and deficient months and leap years per the court's adjustments — for whatever the lower court does the upper court agrees, as it is written "which you shall proclaim" — "which you proclaim" at their appointed times; and so too regarding bodily renewal. As they expounded: "Lo alecha" — a minor girl of three years and one day who was betrothed, her virginity does not return; they counted a leap year in court and the year passed — her virginity returns; "Lo alecha" applies — end quote; and this is in Yerushalmi chapter HaNoder Min HaMevushal halachah 13 in name of R. Avin. And so Rosh in chapter HaIshah wrote that the matter depends on the shofar blast; and so we say at the end of Bnot Kutim (Niddah 38a) that the shofar blast causes it; likewise in Agudah there; and so in responsum of R. Yisrael Mintz siman 9 where he elaborated; and so appears the plain sense of Taz and the poskim. See in Asarah Ma'amarot ma'amar Im Kol Chai part 2 siman 17.
How she saw three times on day one of the week, etc. Meaning from first sight to second for equal count to third — e.g. saw day one of the week returned saw three weeks later day one of the week returned saw three weeks later day one of the week — fixed veset every three weeks on day one of the week; and though not equal month days likewise saw three times day five of the week at equal times fixed veset twice. Bach — plain.
She established a veset for herself by her sightings. And not comparable to the interval veset above seif 2 which requires four sightings — for there it is not tied to the days of the month or week, and you cannot say about an interval such-and-such days she sees until she sees a second time, for the first interval is not known except by the time between the first and second sighting; but here where it depends on the month or week, in the first sighting there is already a fixed time — for you say she saw on such-and-such of the month — Beit Yosef, and it is obvious.
