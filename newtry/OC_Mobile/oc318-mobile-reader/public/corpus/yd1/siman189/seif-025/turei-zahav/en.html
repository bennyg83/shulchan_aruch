The entire onah of the veses, etc. At first glance, the ruling in seif 24 that she is concerned only for the moment itself, and the Rama's ruling in siman 184, seif 2, that for a veses dependent on a bodily change she is concerned only at the time of the veses, would appear to refer only to a bodily veses, such as yawning or sneezing, that is not tied to a particular day. But this is not so. At the end of siman 184, the Beit Yosef cited the Raavad as writing that whether or not she has a fixed day for those signs, she is forbidden only at the time itself. This implies that there is no distinction between a veses fixed to a specific day and one fixed only to bodily signs. Yet the ruling here, that she is forbidden for the entire day as with any other veses tied to a date, is also from the Raavad, as the Rosh wrote in the chapter HaIshah SheOsah Tzeracheha. The Raavad's words therefore appear contradictory, as my father-in-law of blessed memory already asked at the end of siman 184 and left unresolved. There is no difficulty. Above, the Raavad did not say there is no distinction between a bodily veses fixed to a day and one not fixed to a day except regarding permission before the time of the bodily veses. In that respect she is permitted, unlike a veses of days alone. It is logical that there is no concern before the bodily sign, for both factors are required: the day and the bodily veses together cause the sighting. Therefore, the day by itself creates no concern before the bodily sign. This is why the Raavad wrote there that before the veses she is permitted. Here, however, the discussion concerns the period after the bodily veses began. In this respect there is a valid distinction. When the bodily veses is fixed to a day, she is forbidden from the beginning of the bodily sign until the end of that entire onah, whether day or night. When the bodily veses is not fixed to a day, she becomes permitted immediately after the bodily sign has ended. This distinction is clear in my humble opinion.
