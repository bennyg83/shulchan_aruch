The entire onah of the veset, etc. At first glance it implies that what he wrote in seif 24, that she is concerned only for its moment, and so the Rama ruled in siman 184 seif 2, that with a veset dependent on a bodily change she is concerned only at the moment of her veset, refers to where that bodily veset was not tied to days but only to yawning or sneezing without a specific day. But this is not so, for at the end of siman 184 the Beit Yosef brought the words of the Raavad, who wrote that whether she has a fixed day for those signs, etc., she is also forbidden only at its moment. This implies that there is no distinction between being fixed to a specific day and being fixed by itself alone. Yet the ruling here, that she is forbidden the whole day like other day-vesetot, is also the Raavad’s words, as the Rosh wrote in Perek HaIshah SheOsah Tzerakheha. It follows that the Raavad’s words contradict each other. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, already asked this at the end of siman 184 and left it in wonder. But there is no difficulty at all. Above, the Raavad said that there is no distinction between fixed on a day and not only regarding permission before the time of the veset, that it is permitted, unlike a veset of days alone. In this it is logical that there is no concern before the bodily veset, for both are needed: the day and the bodily veset specifically cause the seeing. Automatically there is no concern before the bodily veset at all because of the day, and therefore the Raavad wrote there that before the veset she is permitted. But here he is speaking after the bodily veset. In this one can properly distinguish: where it is fixed to a day, she is forbidden from the beginning of the bodily veset until the end of that onah, whether day or night; but with a bodily veset without a fixed day, she is permitted immediately after the bodily veset has ended. In this matter one may distinguish between them, and this is clear in my humble opinion.
