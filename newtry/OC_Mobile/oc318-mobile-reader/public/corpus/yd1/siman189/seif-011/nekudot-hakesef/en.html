(Taz s.k. 43) His explanation of the words of Rashba and Rav Gidal is correct. However, his practical conclusion that a young girl who saw three times must be concerned for an unfixed veset appears entirely incorrect to me. If so, how could Rashba, the Tur, the Mechaber, and Rama all write without qualification that she is not concerned for an unfixed veset at all? Rather, it appears that Rashba's entire analysis was only intended to prove that, regarding impurity, the law of a young girl is equal to that of other women, so that the first sighting joins the count. The practical consequence concerns a fixed veset, for even with a young girl the first sighting joins toward establishing it. When she has no fixed veset, however, she need not be concerned at all that she may see blood. This is implied by the plain language of Rashba, the Tur, and the later authorities in many places. Although, when she sees a third time, she conveys impurity for a full twenty-four-hour period, this means only that if she actually sees blood, she conveys impurity retroactively. As long as she has not seen, she need not be concerned that she may see, since she has not established a veset. The general rule is that a young girl need not be concerned for an unfixed veset. Examine this carefully.
