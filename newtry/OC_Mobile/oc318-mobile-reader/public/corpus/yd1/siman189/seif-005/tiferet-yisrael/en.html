Seif 5. Regarding what my grandfather, the Gaon of blessed memory, wrote in seif katan 4 on Shach, seif katan 26: the Shach concludes that if a woman saw on Rosh Chodesh of Shevat, Adar, and Nisan, and afterward skipped a month and saw in Sivan and then Av, we assign the Nisan sighting to the established monthly veset, so there are only two instances of an alternating-month veset. My grandfather challenged this from Bava Kamma, where the Gemara asks concerning an ox that gored on Thursday, Friday, Shabbos, Shabbos, and Shabbos: do we assign the first Shabbos to the first sequence, so that it became forewarned for all days, or to the later Shabbos sequence, so that it became forewarned only for Shabbos? The question remains unresolved, and Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchos Nizkei Mamon likewise rules that it is doubtful. If so, the cases appear completely parallel. Since the question remains unresolved and a veset is a rabbinic matter, the doubt should be resolved leniently in every direction, assigning Nisan to the alternating veset rather than the monthly veset, so that she need be concerned only every second month. See there. Before addressing the substance, I wished to consider whether there is any practical difference between the Shach's stringent view and my grandfather's proof for leniency. In the case of a woman who saw in Shevat, Adar, Nisan, then Sivan and Av, the immediate uncertainty concerns Rosh Chodesh Elul. According to the Shach, since Nisan belongs to the fixed monthly veset, she is prohibited in Elul because that monthly veset has been skipped only twice. According to my grandfather, if we leniently assign Nisan to the alternating pattern, Rosh Chodesh Elul would be permitted. Beyond that, however, there initially seems to be no practical difference, because if she sees in Elul it becomes clear that she remained with the monthly veset. His entire basis for leniency was doubt; once she sees in Elul, the matter is clarified in favor of the earlier, more common monthly pattern. Thus the doubt would concern only Elul itself. Although I am young in years and wisdom and unfit to place my head between two great mountains, I cannot refrain from speaking. It appears that everyone should agree that she is prohibited in Elul. Even if we rule leniently that she established an alternating veset, as my grandfather says, I already cited in the preceding seif katan Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, who rules that even when she certainly establishes a progressively changing veset, she must nevertheless be concerned for the next month lest she establish a monthly veset. My grandfather himself earlier asked why the Mechaber omitted Rambam's view, which is also Tosafos's view. If, as explained there, Rambam deals even with a doubtful establishment of a changing veset and everyone agrees to that concern, then here too, where there are multiple doubts and the proposed leniency rests only on doubt, she certainly must be concerned. If Rambam deals with a fully established changing veset, all the more so here, where there are added doubts, everyone should agree with Rambam and Tosafos that she is prohibited on the coming Rosh Chodesh Elul. Thus it is clear that she is prohibited in Elul, and in Elul the doubt will be clarified. The difficulty against the Shach is then what practical difference it makes whether we assign Nisan to the established monthly veset. According to what I have said, Rosh Chodesh Elul is forbidden in any event, and when Elul arrives the doubt will be clarified. One cannot say that if Nisan is not assigned to the established monthly veset, the Shach holds it is also not assigned to the alternating veset, leaving her as a woman with no veset who requires examination before relations and concern for intervals and the like. That would be strained and excessively stringent: she certainly has either a monthly or alternating veset, and in such a doubt we certainly rule leniently. Moreover, according to the Shach in siman 184, even a woman without a fixed veset, within 30 days, is treated like a woman with a veset and does not require examination for her husband; by day 30 the doubt will have been clarified. Nor can one say that although Elul is forbidden because she might establish the monthly veset, she may have relations close to her expected veset and is prohibited only during the actual onah, whereas if the veset is already fixed she is prohibited even near it. Above in siman 184 it is explained that one must separate near the veset even after a single prior sighting. It would be very forced to distinguish that this applies only when the prior sighting cannot be attributed to another veset, whereas in a doubtful case we might assign it to the alternating veset and, although we remain concerned that it might belong to another pattern, not require separation near the veset because the entire matter is rabbinic and a doubt is lenient. One must therefore say, with difficulty, that according to the Shach, who definitely assigns Nisan to the fixed veset, once the time passes she may not have relations without expressly asking her, as explained above in siman 184, unlike the Rif and Rambam, who permit relations even if she is asleep. If the monthly veset is only a concern, however, everyone agrees with Rambam and Rif that after the time passes he may have relations with her even while she sleeps, because there is a double doubt: perhaps Nisan belongs to the alternating veset and it is not her time to see, and even if Nisan belongs to the monthly veset, perhaps she did not see. This may be my grandfather's intention. Still, with respect to the basic prohibition on Rosh Chodesh Elul there is no difference, for even my grandfather agrees she is forbidden then. It is impossible to ignore the possible alternating veset when Rambam and Tosafos are stringent even without such uncertainty. When Elul arrives, the doubt is clarified: if she sees, we see that she established the monthly veset; if she does not see, it is clear that she established the alternating veset. Thus the difference is whether her husband must ask her explicitly after the Elul onah, or may have relations without asking, according to Rif and Rambam. This would explain why Shach says simply that Nisan is assigned to the monthly veset and therefore she is prohibited, while my grandfather objects that the Gemara leaves the analogous question unresolved and a rabbinic doubt is lenient, so she need be concerned only every second month, meaning that after the Elul onah has passed her husband may have relations without explicitly asking. As to the prohibition on Rosh Chodesh Elul itself, my grandfather likewise agrees. There may, however, be a substantial practical difference concerning uprooting the veset. If she did not see on Rosh Chodesh Elul, she is certainly permitted afterward. Yet if she then sees on the twentieth of Tishrei and does not see on Rosh Chodesh Tishrei, according to the Shach she still has a fixed Rosh Chodesh veset, because she has not yet uprooted it three consecutive times. According to my grandfather, the monthly veset requires no uprooting at all, while the alternating veset that she established would require three occasions of uprooting. This too requires study, because it may be rejected.
Regarding the substance of the objection, however, it appears possible to defend the Shach. Indeed, my grandfather's challenge would otherwise be stronger against the Gemara in Bava Kamma itself, because the question there should be resolved from the sugya in Niddah, and the Shach's ruling appears explicit in the Gemara, as I will explain. Bava Kamma 34 asks: if an ox gored on Shabbos, Shabbos, Shabbos, then Sunday and Monday, do we assign the last Shabbos to the Shabbos pattern, making it forewarned for Shabbos but not weekdays, or to the two days following Shabbos, making it forewarned for all days? It also asks about an ox that gored on Thursday, Friday, Shabbos, Shabbos, etc., concerning the first Shabbos. At first glance this is difficult because the Gemara compares patterns of goring to the dispute of Rav and Shmuel regarding veset. Niddah 64 states: a woman was accustomed to seeing on the fifteenth and changed to the sixteenth, then the seventeenth, then the eighteenth; the prior dates are released and she is prohibited only from the eighteenth onward. The Gemara challenges Rav, and Rav answers, "one who was already accustomed is different." Rashi explains: since she was certainly accustomed to see on the fifteenth, we assign the fifteenth sightings to the preceding fifteenth sightings; Rav's rule applies only where she has just begun to see or to a woman with no veset. Accordingly, if a woman saw twice on the fifteenth and now sees a third time on the fifteenth, thereby completing a fixed veset, and then changes to the sixteenth and seventeenth, Rav agrees that she still needs another progression to the eighteenth, because the third fifteenth is assigned to the fixed veset. Rav's view that two progressions suffice applies only where she was beginning a new pattern, not where that very fifteenth sighting completed an established veset. If so, why does Bava Kamma remain uncertain concerning an ox that gored Thursday, Friday, Shabbos, then Shabbos and Shabbos? This seems exactly analogous: Rav agrees that the first Shabbos should be assigned to the Thursday-Friday-Shabbos sequence and the ox should become forewarned for all days. One might answer that even if the fifteenth sighting that she was accustomed to was the third sighting completing the fixed veset, we would not assign it there, and the Baraisa's case of an accustomed woman means that she had already seen three times previously; Rashi was not precise when he said only that Rav's rule applies at the beginning. But this is strained. We find in the Mishnah only that a veset is established after three sightings, and on this approach sometimes four would be required. Moreover, it appears that the Baraisa specifically means that with the current fifteenth sighting she completes the three sightings that establish the veset. Otherwise it would also be difficult according to Shmuel, who always requires three changes: the Baraisa says specifically that she was accustomed to the fifteenth and then changed; if she were not already accustomed, according to Shmuel she would still require three changes, so why emphasize that she was accustomed? This is a standard style of proof throughout the Talmud. It is especially difficult for the poskim who rule like Shmuel because the Baraisa's continuation, "she changed to the twenty-first," etc., supports him; see the Rosh. Yet that Baraisa could also fit Rav if it concerned someone already accustomed to the twentieth. One could say that because the anonymous Gemara strained to say "this is also precise," etc., this is still weak. See Maggid Mishneh in chapter 8, who writes that a Baraisa was taught in accordance with Shmuel, though it could fit Rav as well; and especially since it says specifically "accustomed," and the halacha usually follows Rav in prohibitory law, the matter requires study. There is another difficulty: what novelty does "accustomed" add according to Shmuel? Even without an established veset, three instances are required. According to Rav it is understandable, because the Baraisa teaches specifically that an established pattern is different. One cannot say that the novelty is that an established veset is uprooted only after three changes, for the Mishnah itself explicitly states that a veset is not uprooted until it is changed the requisite number of times. In truth, the Gemara's own answer that the case of an accustomed woman is needed because otherwise one might think it is uprooted after two occasions is difficult, since that too is explicit in the Mishnah. Nor can one say that the Baraisa merely explains the Mishnah, because it has already taught the sequence itself. Therefore it appears that the case is where she had previously seen twice on the fifteenth and with the present fifteenth sighting completes the fixed veset, and in the following month changes to the sixteenth. Rav and Shmuel both hold that this fifteenth belongs to the fixed veset. The Baraisa therefore teaches a novelty: one might have thought to assign that fifteenth to the new progressive pattern, as in the unresolved question in Bava Kamma, because the following sightings display a progression; it teaches instead that the fifteenth belongs to the fixed veset and another three changes are required according to both Rav and Shmuel. This is what Rav means by "one who was accustomed is different": since in that month the fifteenth sighting completed a fixed veset, she is already treated as accustomed and that sighting is not counted toward the new progression. The Gemara then asks why the original questioner did not recognize that this was an established pattern, and answers that one might have thought that because there is uncertainty whether to assign the fifteenth to the monthly veset or to the progressing veset, the established veset could be uprooted with two subsequent changes; the Baraisa teaches otherwise. Rashi's words are therefore exact: Rav's statement applies specifically when she begins to see, and Shach's ruling is correct because it is explicit in the Gemara. Still, the earlier difficulty returns against the unresolved question in Bava Kamma: why not resolve it from this? It appears to me that there is a distinction. Regarding a veset, we properly assign the fifteenth sighting to the preceding monthly veset rather than to the progressing veset, because there is no reason to displace a common monthly veset in favor of an uncommon progressing veset. Regarding an ox that gored on two weekdays, however, there remains reason to assign the Shabbos goring to the other Shabbos goreings, because there is an independent rationale that Shabbos is not part of the weekday pattern at all. The Mishnah speaks of an ox that is forewarned for Shabbos, and Rashi explains that on Shabbos the owner rests from work and the animal's disposition changes; the Yerushalmi explains that the animal sees clean white clothing. There is also a contrary consideration: it already gored twice on weekdays, but two occasions did not make it forewarned, and it subsequently passed through weekdays without goring, as Maharsha explains there. This reveals that it was not forewarned for weekdays but only for Shabbos, so the first Shabbos should not be assigned to the earlier weekdays. By contrast, in the veset case the fifteenth sighting is a full and ordinary sighting that can as naturally belong to the monthly veset as to the progressing veset. There is no reason to displace the common pattern. In addition, with a woman one must in any event wait some interval to observe the next sighting, so what difference is there whether she waits 30 or 31 days? Thus the goring case has an independent reason to separate Shabbos from the weekday sequence and is not truly parallel to veset in this respect. The difficulty therefore falls away, and the Shach's words are correct.
