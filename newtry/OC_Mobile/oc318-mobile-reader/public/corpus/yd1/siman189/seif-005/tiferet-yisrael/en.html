However, regarding the substance of the question, it appears one can defend the gaon Shach. At first glance my grandfather the gaon's question is very strong against the Gemara in Bava Kamma: why not resolve it from the sugya in Niddah? The Shach's words appear to be explicit from the Gemara, as I will explain. In Bava Kamma 34 they ask: if an ox gored on Shabbat, Shabbat, and Shabbat, then on Sunday and Monday, what is the law? Do we assign the later Shabbat to the earlier Shabbat and make it muad for Shabbatot but not weekdays, or do we assign it to the two days after Shabbat and make it muad for all days? Similarly, if it gored on Thursday, Friday, Shabbat, Shabbat, etc., what of the first Shabbat? At first glance this is difficult, for the Gemara compares gorings to the dispute of Rav and Shmuel regarding veset, and in Niddah 64 it says: if she was accustomed to see on the fifteenth and changed to the sixteenth, then to the seventeenth, then to the eighteenth, all the earlier days are permitted and she is prohibited only from the eighteenth onward. The Gemara challenges Rav, and Rav answers that an accustomed woman is different. Rashi explains that if she was certainly accustomed to see on the fifteenth, the fifteenth sightings are assigned to the previous fifteenth; what I said applies only where she began to see or to a woman who has no veset. According to this, if a woman saw twice on the fifteenth of the month and today saw on the fifteenth, thereby establishing a veset, and afterward shifted to the sixteenth and then the seventeenth, Rav agrees that she needs another shift to the eighteenth. His statement that two shifts suffice is only if she began to see, not where this fifteenth sighting completes a fixed veset. If so, why does the Gemara ask regarding goring on Thursday, Friday, Shabbat, Shabbat, and Shabbat? This resembles veset, and Rav agrees that the fifteenth sighting is assigned to the fixed veset; so too the first Shabbat should be assigned to the Thursday-Friday gorings, making it muad for all days. One might say that even if the fifteenth sighting that she is accustomed to see is the third sighting that establishes the fixed veset, we do not assign it to that veset, and the baraita of "accustomed" speaks where she had already seen three times. Rashi was not exact and wrote only that he spoke of the beginning of seeing; he meant either case. But this is forced, for we find in the Mishnah only that she establishes after three times, and according to this sometimes she would not establish until four. Moreover, the baraita of the accustomed fifteenth seems specifically to speak where the sighting she saw today on the fifteenth establishes the veset for the third time; otherwise it is difficult according to Shmuel, who always requires three times, because the baraita would also be difficult for him. Why say "accustomed"? If she was not accustomed, would two times suffice? This is especially difficult for the poskim who rule like Shmuel because the baraita supports him. If according to Rav too it speaks of one accustomed to see on the twentieth, the proof is not exclusive. One could answer that the anonymous Gemara was forced by its precise reading, but this is strained. It is also difficult: what does "accustomed" teach according to Shmuel? Without being accustomed he also requires three times. One cannot say it teaches that an accustomed veset is uprooted by two times, for that is explicit in the Mishnah. Therefore it appears that the case is where she had seen twice before on the fifteenth and now the fifteenth sighting completed the fixed veset; the next month she changed to the sixteenth. Rav and Shmuel hold that we assign that fifteenth to the fixed veset, and the baraita teaches a novelty: one might have thought that since she shifted afterward, as in the Gemara's question in Bava Kamma about gorings, we assign that fifteenth to the pattern of shifting. It therefore teaches that we assign the fifteenth to the fixed monthly veset, and she still needs three further times, whether according to Rav or Shmuel. That is what Rav meant by saying "accustomed is different": since in that month she saw on the fifteenth and thereby fixed a veset, she is called accustomed and that sighting is not counted for the shift. The Gemara asks why the questioner did not understand this, and answers that one might have thought it is uprooted in two times, meaning one might have thought that because there is uncertainty we do not assign the fifteenth sighting to the monthly veset but to the shift; therefore it teaches otherwise. According to this, Rashi's words are exact: "I spoke only where she began to see." The Shach's words are correct, for they are explicit in the Gemara. But the question remains on Bava Kamma: why not resolve from here? It appears to my humble eyes that one should answer as follows. Regarding veset, we properly assign the fifteenth sighting to the prior monthly veset and not to the shifting veset, for why should we displace a common monthly veset before an uncommon shifting veset? But regarding gorings on two weekdays, one might still assign the Shabbat to the other Shabbatot, because there is independent reason that Shabbat is not counted with weekdays at all. The Mishnah there says an ox may be muad for Shabbatot, and Rashi explains that on Shabbat it rests from work and becomes self-assured, while the Yerushalmi says it is because it sees clean white clothing. There is also a counter-reason, since it already gored twice on weekdays, though two days do not make it muad, and since weekdays passed and it did not gore, as Maharsha explains there that the case is where it saw on weekdays and did not gore, it may be revealed that it became muad only for Shabbatot. Thus the first Shabbat is not necessarily assigned to the previous weekdays. But regarding veset, we assign the fifteenth sighting to the previous fifteenth sightings: first, because with a woman one must in any case pause and wait some time to see again, and what difference is there whether she waits thirty days or thirty-one? Second, the fifteenth sighting is a full valid sighting and can be assigned to the monthly veset just as to the shifting veset; why should the common be displaced? By contrast, with goring there is reason that the Shabbat sighting is not part of the weekday count. Therefore it is not comparable to veset in this respect, the question is removed, and the Shach's words are justified.
