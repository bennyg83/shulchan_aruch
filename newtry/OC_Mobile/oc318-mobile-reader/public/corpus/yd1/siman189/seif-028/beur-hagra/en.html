And likewise an elderly woman, etc. This is stated there, and as explained above in seif 33. The same applies to a young girl and an elderly woman: so long as she has not established a veset, she is like a pregnant or nursing woman, as stated in the Mishnah. The only distinction among them concerns the second sighting, as explained in seif 31 and as stated there in the passage, “In what case did they say,” etc. This is also stated there, 10b: “They taught this only regarding a virgin and an elderly woman,” etc. Consequently, with respect to vesetot, the only practical distinction is for an elderly woman who ceased seeing for three ordinary intervals, as stated there, 9b. Regarding a virgin, there is no practical distinction unless she has not yet reached the age when she is expected to see, as explained above. See Tosafot there, 9b, s.v. An elderly woman, etc. A virgin who has reached the age when she is expected to see is different, as stated in the Gemara: “When she reached the age,” etc.; “intervals passed,” etc. The Gemara establishes that clause specifically in accordance with Rabbi Eliezer, as explained above.
