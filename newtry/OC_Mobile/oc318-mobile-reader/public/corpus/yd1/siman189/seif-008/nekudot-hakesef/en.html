(Taz s.k. 32) And at first glance, etc. The Shach already preceded him in raising this point in s.k. 59. Gloss. In the glosses it is written that Rashba, the Tur, and the other poskim follow the law of the Gemara that a woman does not establish a veset during her days of niddah or during her days of zivah. For example, if she saw on the first of the month and again on the fifth, which falls within the days of niddah stemming from the first sighting, then even if this occurred three times she does not establish a veset for the fifth of the month, but only for the first of the month. Likewise, if she saw on the first of the month and again on the fifteenth, and the same applies to the eighteenth, which is still within her days of zivah, as the Perishah also wrote, she does not establish a veset for the fifteenth, because each sighting on the fifteenth falls within the days of zivah generated by the sighting on the first. This applies when the later sighting came from an already open source, as I explained. If, however, it came from a closed source, such as where she saw twice on Rosh Chodesh, and on the third occasion she saw on the twenty-fifth of the month and again on Rosh Chodesh, then although the third Rosh Chodesh sighting occurred during the days of niddah stemming from the sighting on the twenty-fifth, she nevertheless establishes a veset for Rosh Chodesh. This is because the first two sightings came from a closed source, while on the twenty-fifth an additional abundance of blood caused the flow to arrive early. She must also be concerned for the earlier date, lest she be establishing a separate veset. Although above, in se'if 13, the gloss states that once she establishes a veset she need no longer be concerned for a prior unfixed veset, this case differs because the third Rosh Chodesh sighting is not regarded as a full sighting, since it came from an open source. This is the gloss of the Derishah, and the Bach wrote likewise. The Mechaber and Rama omitted all these laws. It appears that they did so because the Tur and the poskim wrote in the name of Ramban that, since Jewish women nowadays accepted upon themselves the stringency of making no distinction between the days of niddah and the days of zivah, as explained above at the end of siman 183 in the Tur and Beis Yosef, there is likewise no distinction between them regarding the establishment of a veset. Therefore, if she saw every fifteen days, she establishes a veset. In truth, however, it is clear that Ramban said this only stringently, so that she may establish a veset even during the days of niddah and zivah, but not leniently. He certainly did not intend to dispute the Gemara. It has already been explained in many places above that some laws of an unfixed veset are more stringent than those of a fixed veset. A woman who has no fixed veset must be concerned for the veset of the month and for the interval, whereas one who has a fixed veset need not be concerned for them, as explained above in se'if 13. Rashba in Toras HaBayis HaAroch and the Maggid Mishneh likewise imply that Ramban ruled only stringently and not leniently. The Perishah in se'if 93 also implies this. He writes: “Accordingly, even nowadays one must rule stringently that a veset may be established during the days of zivah and niddah. A practical difference concerns uprooting, for even if it was uprooted twice, she must still be concerned for the third occasion.” His extra phrase “even nowadays one must rule stringently,” and the fact that he identifies only a stringent practical consequence and not a lenient one, show that we hold there is no distinction only for purposes of stringency. Far be it from us to dispute the Gemara for purposes of leniency. The Mechaber and Rama should therefore have cited these laws. In any event, we learn that whenever this siman states that she has established a veset, if the consequence would be lenient, this applies only when the sightings did not occur during her days of niddah or zivah. This concludes the gloss.
