And the seventeenth of Sivan. According to the first opinion cited by the Mechaber in seif 7, this case must concern a woman who previously had an established veset, as explained above. Perishah also writes this. This appears to be the intent of Tur and the Mechaber, who write that she saw three times in three months with progression, implying that all the sightings must form progressive steps. Without a prior established veset, the first sighting would not be considered part of a progression. Accordingly, here too one should observe the stringencies of both opinions, as stated in seif katan 14. Shach disputes Perishah and writes that Tur and Rashba imply that three sightings suffice here according to both Rav and Shmuel. His inference is not compelling. Their words can be explained as I have written. We have no basis to be lenient against Rabbeinu Tam, cited by Tosafot to Niddah 64a, who states explicitly that according to Shmuel four sightings are required even in such a case, especially since this law derives from Rabbeinu Chananel, as Rashba writes in Torat HaBayit.
And she remains concerned indefinitely. Bach writes in seif 11 that the same applies where the sequence repeats over two months. For example, she saw on the fifteenth of Nisan, sixteenth of Iyar, fifteenth of Sivan, sixteenth of Tammuz, fifteenth of Av, and sixteenth of Elul. She must then be concerned for the fifteenth of Tishrei and the sixteenth of Cheshvan, and so on. Raavad writes this in Baalei HaNefesh. The poskim mention three months rather than two to teach a greater novelty. Even though the third progression has occurred three times, and one might think the original pattern was uprooted and replaced by a newly established progressive veset, the ruling teaches that the entire sequence is one repeating progression.
