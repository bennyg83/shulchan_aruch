Four sightings are required, etc. One may challenge: veset ha-chodesh and veset ha-shavua are one in law, and we learn veset ha-chodesh from veset ha-shavua as explicit in the Gemara; if so plainly veset ha-dilug applies to both as to veset ha-chodesh. See Tosafot Bava Kamma 34 regarding the goring ox, likewise explicit. If so, for the view that we rule like Rav that two skips suffice and these are veset ha-chodesh, three sightings, likewise for veset ha-shavua. Example: a woman saw first on Sunday the 1st, at end of day 20 saw again on Friday the 6th, at end of twenty saw on Wednesday the 4th, behold three sightings and two skips in two days that are equal skips, behold veset ha-shavua for skip like veset ha-chodesh for skip. I wonder why the Acharonim did not notice this. My resolution: how can one say she established veset for skipping and veset ha-shaveh? We say it is more reasonable she established the equal veset since a woman rarely establishes veset for skip; therefore we say she is not concerned for skip until she establishes it three times. If so, truly when she saw three times with a thirty-day interval of two days she established veset ha-shavua in skip, only when she adds a fourth sighting at end of twenty. If so there are two types of veset here, either interval veset which is the equal veset, or veset ha-shavua in skip; Shulchan Aruch's intent is it is not established for interval until four times and then it is interval veset and not skip veset for the week. If difficulty: what difference, in either case she must forever be concerned for day twenty? Answer: if she saw in her youth or one onah passed and she did not see, if you judge this veset ha-shavua for skip, nevertheless at its time she is concerned for it is equal in law to veset ha-chodesh and there is no difference whether she sees in youth or one onah passed; but if we judge it for interval veset, if one onah she does not see at its time she is not concerned in youth, then interval is void and no concern as below.
