And in this manner she has an established veses. For otherwise, since she changed and did not see on the 20th day, the concern for the 20th would be uprooted, because it had not already been established through three occurrences.
Twenty days after the sighting on the 30th, she is forbidden. However, we do not count 20 days from the time when she had been expected to see on the 20th, since she did not see then but delayed until the 30th. See what I wrote concerning this at the beginning of seif 13, regarding whether she saw on the 1st of Iyar or did not see, etc.
And if, after she changed, etc. These are the Ramban's words in the Tur. At first glance, they contradict the Gemara. At the end of the chapter HaIshah SheOsah Tzeracheha it states: if she was accustomed to see on the 20th and changed to the 30th, both dates are forbidden. If the 20th arrived and she did not see, she is permitted to have relations until the 30th. If the 30th arrived and she saw, then the next 20th arrived and she did not see, the next 30th arrived and she did not see, and the next 20th arrived and she saw, the 30th is permitted, for it was uprooted through a single occurrence since it had not been established three times, while the 20th is forbidden. Rashi explains that she had been accustomed to the 20th and uprooted it only twice, for the final omission of the 20th following the second 30th on which she did not see was not an uprooting, since she did not change to the 30th then and did not see until the 20th of the month, which was her veses. The Rosh likewise wrote that if a woman skips her veses even three times, but does not see in the interim at another fixed time and then returns to see at the time of her original veses, the veses is not uprooted until she establishes another veses. End quote. This makes the Ramban's words difficult. Why did he write that she changed twice to the 30th and then saw on the 20th, implying that she had not yet uprooted the 20th three times, while in the Gemara just cited the law applies even if she uprooted the 20th three times, as Rashi explicitly explained? It appears that the Gemara refers to calendar dates of the month. Her original sighting was on the 20th of the month, and the change to the 30th was likewise based on calendar dates. Therefore, although three times passed without her seeing on the 20th of the month, when on the fourth occasion she sees on the 20th it is evident that her flow returned at its proper time, since the original veses is identifiable and defined by the date of the month. The Ramban here, however, applies the law to an interval veses, meaning that she originally saw every 20 days and changed to every 30 days, as is explicit from the phrase “when the 30th day after the sighting on the 30th arrives,” etc. If he had said that three 30-day intervals passed, that on the third she did not see on the 30th, and that afterward she saw on the 20th, this would not demonstrate that she returned to her original 20-day interval. Before the present sighting on the 20th she had not seen since the middle 30th-day sighting, so 50 days separate the two sightings, and the original interval is not recognizable. Therefore, he would have needed to say that she now saw two consecutive sightings 20 days apart, for only then would the original interval be recognizable. Since the Ramban was not discussing two new sightings, he stated his law where she saw on the 20th day after the second 30th-day sighting, for there the 20-day interval is clearly recognizable. From this sighting on the 20th she must again be concerned when another 20 days arrive. The same would indeed apply if, after the third 30th-day interval, she saw twice with 20 days between them; the original veses would return to its place. There is an additional novelty here: the 30th day becomes permitted even though it has not yet been fully uprooted, because the first veses has now returned to its place. Toras HaBayis HaAruch, folio 174, explains the Gemara above also as discussing an interval veses. Its phrase “the 20th day arrived and she saw” means 20 days after the 30th-day sighting. See there. It appears to me that there is a scribal error in his wording, which reads: “and she is concerned for the 30th day; the 30th day arrived and she did not see; the 20th day arrived and she saw; the 30th day is permitted,” etc. It should be amended to read: “the 30th day arrived and she saw.” It appears that this was also the Ramban's text of the Gemara. According to this, if she failed to see on the 20th three times, the veses is indeed uprooted even if she then did not see on the following 30th and later saw on the 20th after those 30 days. In the Rosh, however, the text is as in our Gemara. This requires further study concerning the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch, who did not note this.
