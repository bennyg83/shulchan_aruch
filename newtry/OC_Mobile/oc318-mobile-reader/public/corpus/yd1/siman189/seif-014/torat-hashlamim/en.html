She was accustomed to see on the twentieth day. Tur and the other poskim write that under Talmudic law a woman does not establish a veset during her days of niddah or zivah. The Mechaber and Rama omit these rules because Ramban writes that nowadays, when women have accepted the stringency of not distinguishing between niddah and zivah days, there is likewise no distinction regarding establishment of a veset. Nekudot HaKesef argues that Ramban intended this only stringently, to allow a veset to be established even during those days, but not leniently, since he would not reject the Gemara. In my opinion, Beit Yosef understood Ramban correctly. Rashba explains that if we distinguish among these days, women would need to master the calculations of niddah and zivah. This implies that no distinction should be made at all. Since veset law is rabbinic, there is no reason to be excessively stringent nowadays.
She saw after twenty days. The case is a woman whose established interval veset was every twenty days. She then changed and saw after thirty days twice, but on the third occasion she did not see at thirty days and instead saw later. If she afterward sees again at a twenty-day interval, she returns to her original establishment. Although she failed to see on the twentieth day three times, that veset was not uprooted because no new veset became established. An established veset is not uprooted merely because she skipped it three times unless another pattern replaces it. This is the implication of the Gemara, Rashi, Rosh, and Rashba, and Shach writes similarly in Nekudot HaKesef. Maadanei Melech explains that even before the third thirty-day interval arrives, if after the second thirty-day sighting she sees twenty days later, she immediately returns to her original veset and need not remain concerned for thirty days. Shach leaves that explanation difficult.
