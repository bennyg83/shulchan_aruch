(Taz s.k. 38) This is difficult, etc. There is no difficulty. Here, it is certainly impossible to say that she must be concerned except until she yawns. Therefore, the ruling states that she is forbidden to have relations until she examines herself, meaning that immediately after the yawning she is forbidden to have relations. If, however, a significant amount of time passed after the yawning and she felt nothing, she is permitted, since this is not a fixed veset, as is implied below in se'if 26. Above, in se'if 4, however, because the veset depends on a known time, once that time has passed, even immediately afterward, she is permitted so long as she felt nothing, for the known time has already passed. His suggestion that there the ruling is after the fact whereas here it is only initially has no meaning, for what distinction between after the fact and initially applies here?
