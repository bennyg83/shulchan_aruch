To provoke him. And if it appears that he does so, the kohen need not receive it — Mahariv. And the Shach wrote that he did not know how to reconcile the Rav's words unless there is an error in his words, and it should say as follows: and specifically with a doubt that comes of itself, but with a doubt that comes from a dispute of the authorities, such as one who bought a milking cow from a worshipper of stars, or the Jew was negligent in that he could have sold the bechor etc. And nevertheless this requires further study for the law regarding what he distinguished between a doubt of itself and a doubt of a dispute of the authorities, for in the teshuvah of Maharil it does not imply so, for regarding one who bought a milking cow from a worshipper of stars he wrote that the kohen must receive it, and he learned from a doubtful bechor, even though he brought there the reasoning of the poskim whether milk exempts from bechorah or not. Rather it appears that one must say because there were several doubts in a dispute of the authorities: that he sold to a worshipper of stars for money, which is a sale according to some poskim, as below siman 320 seif 6, and similarly that it was milking, which is exempt regarding a bechor according to some poskim; therefore he was lenient in the matter. But with one doubt of a dispute of the authorities alone, one should not be lenient, unless by law the main view appears like the one who is lenient, except that they are stringent; that in such a case it is possible that the kohen need not receive it. Also Mahariv ruled that if a poor kohen has a bechor and gives it to a rich kohen, he need not receive it. End of his words.
