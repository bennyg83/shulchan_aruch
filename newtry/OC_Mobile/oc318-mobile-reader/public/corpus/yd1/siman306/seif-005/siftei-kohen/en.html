And he slaughters it according to an expert, etc. Nowadays there are no experts, and we require three, as below siman 309 seif 2.
And he eats it and feeds it, etc. When it is treifah, it is forbidden to derive benefit from it, as below siman 307.
Even to a non-Jew, etc. Maharshal in chapter HaMakshah siman 4 wrote that nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition because of disgrace to kodashim. It is permitted to feed it to a non-Jew only by inviting him to eat together with him, but not to sell it to him, and not even to give him a raw piece. Likewise with dogs, only incidentally from the leftover meat with the bones, but not to throw it directly to dogs. These are his words.
Or to dogs. The Ran wrote in chapter HaMakshah that specifically to a non-Jew it is permitted, but to dogs it is forbidden because of "you shall eat" and not your dogs. Maharshal there in siman 4 objected to him without a compelling argument. What Maharshal wrote, that this is also implied by the Rosh in chapter Pesulei HaMukdashin, is not compelling. He means that the Rosh's statement in the latter clause regarding a treifah, that it is forbidden to dogs and to non-Jews, implies that in the first clause regarding a blemished animal it is permitted. This is not a proof; rather, it means that it is forbidden to dogs, and the same applies to a non-Jew, which we derive from dogs. The words of Tosafot in Pesulei HaMukdashin 32b, who wrote that it is permitted to a non-Jew and is not forbidden by "you shall eat" and not your dogs, etc., at first glance imply that to dogs it is indeed forbidden, though to a non-Jew it is permitted. So wrote the Rashba in his novellae to chapter HaMakshah 73: our teachers, the Tosafists z"l, answered that wherever it is permitted to a Jew, such as where a blemish fell in it, it is permitted even to invite a non-Jew to eat it, for it is compared to deer and hart; but to dogs it is forbidden because of "you shall eat" and not your dogs. So too Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote, netiv 20 part 2, that one may feed it to a non-Jew whenever it is permitted to a Jew, but it is forbidden to feed it to dogs even though it is permitted to a Jew, as we say in Pesachim: "you shall eat" and not your dogs, and the verse speaks of a blemished bekhor. Therefore one must be careful in this matter.
