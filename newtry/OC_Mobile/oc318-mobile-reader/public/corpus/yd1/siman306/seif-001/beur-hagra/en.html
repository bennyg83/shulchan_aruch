Whether in Eretz Yisrael. This is the Mishnah at the end of chapter 3 of Temurah: all consecrated animals that became blemished may be brought from outside Eretz Yisrael except a firstborn and animal tithe, concerning which R. Shimon explains the reason. See also Bechorot 36b and many other places.
(Likkut) And it is a mitzvah to consecrate it. This is Rambam's view. Tur, in the name of Rosh, disagrees, and Bach writes that the matter requires study. Rambam's reason is the sugya in Arachin 29a, where he rules like the Sages of the Mishnah. Kesef Mishneh questions this because although Ulla follows the Sages, Rav disagrees and is Ulla's teacher, and Rav Yehudah also acted in practice according to Rav. But the sugya there concerning R. Yishmael and the sanctity taking effect on sacred items aligns differently. Rosh follows Kesef Mishneh's objection and rules like R. Yishmael. Rambam, however, rules like the Sages regarding a firstborn, and therefore also like the Sages in Arachin. Proof that the halachah follows the Sages regarding firstborn comes from the discussions in the first chapters of Nedarim 13a and Nazir, which follow their position. I later found the same in Lechem Mishneh.
And they give. Meaning that although Chullin 131b and 132a say that the priestly gifts may be given even to a daughter of a kohen, and Rav Kahana ate gifts on account of his wife, while Pesachim says that Rav Kahana was not himself a kohen, nevertheless Rosh proves that pidyon haben may not be given to a daughter of a kohen. Tosafot in Kiddushin 8a give one answer that Rav Kahana received gifts because of his wife, as with other priestly gifts. Tosafot in Pesachim similarly explain. In Chullin, however, they prefer the answer that there were two different sages named Rav Kahana. Rosh proves that a daughter of a kohen cannot receive pidyon haben from the Gemara's exchange between Rava and Ulla, which derives the law from the designation to Aharon and his sons. Regarding pidyon haben the verse says that the redemption money is given to Aharon and his sons. Rosh further writes that firstborn humans and firstborn animals are compared, though I do not know where this comparison is established, since the Gemara says the comparison is only between firstborn humans and firstborn non-kosher animals.
