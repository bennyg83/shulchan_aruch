Where he was negligent. Chut HaShani 26 discusses this at length. According to Mahari Weil, a kohen must accept a definite firstborn even if the owner was negligent by failing to sell it. Regarding a doubtful firstborn, if the negligence is evident, such as where he was warned and instructed to sell it to a non-Jew but did not, or intended to provoke the kohen, the kohen need not accept it; otherwise he must. Chut HaShani limits this to an offspring of a dairy cow, which is technically exempt but customarily treated stringently. In other doubtful cases the kohen must accept it even where negligence is evident. He also corrects the Rama's text to mean that the animal was bought from a non-Jew and could have been sold, which is evident negligence. See the preceding seif katan in the name of Chavot Yair. Chatam Sofer 302 rules that negligent owners incur a rabbinic penalty for risking sacred property, but this does not automatically exempt the kohen. Where the owners can be compelled, it is preferable to compel them as a deterrent. If they act merely to harass the kohen, he should refuse it so people will not make sport of kohanim, and the owner must care for even a doubtful firstborn because of possible disgrace to sacred property. Such an owner is also suspected of inflicting a blemish. See siman 314:10. [Citation 316.]
