And Purim. Because they are rabbinic matters and need reinforcement. And what Rama wrote in Orach Chaim that he did not rule thus — Shach answers: it appears to the author to decide that specifically here, where he vowed to fast one day or two days all his days, there is a matter of reinforcement and significance, for he will need to fast all his days on Chanukah — if so, a rabbinic matter is uprooted. But in Orach Chaim, which deals explicitly with one who accepted so-and-so many days — he will fast on Chanukah and Purim once and a rabbinic matter is not uprooted thereby. And in truth the Rambam holds that in every case his vow is void — only the author decides thus from his own reasoning. And know that this is so, for in siman 203 the author wrote: but if he expressed it in language of accepting a fast, it does not override, etc. — and Chanukah and Purim are not mentioned, implying that if he accepted in vow-language it overrides Chanukah and Purim. And this contradicts what he wrote here in se'if dalet. If so, the author's own words contradict each other. Rather, one must distinguish: in siman 203 it deals with a vow for a known time, as he wrote; and regarding an oath see what the author wrote in Orach Chaim at the end of siman 318 and 570. But the Levush wrote there that even if he swore explicitly to fast on Rosh Chodesh he should not fast, even though Rosh Chodesh is rabbinic, since he has a Torah basis, etc., see there — and his words are not clear at all, for the Ran wrote in the first chapter of Nedarim that even a matter that is from the Torah, since it is not explicit in the verse but comes from derashah, an oath takes effect upon it. And likewise Rabbenu Yerucham below siman 236 se'if bet, and the author in siman 239 se'if vav, and likewise Bach — therefore one should release the oath and he does not fast, as Rabbenu Yerucham wrote there in Orach Chaim. And the Acharonim wrote in the name of the responsum of Maharam of Nisan, and likewise the other days when tachanun is not said, their law is like rabbinically forbidden days. However, it appears that for one who vowed in vow-language, even according to the Rambam he needs release, for Chanukah and Purim are considered more significant and the Sages reinforced them — unlike these others. But for other matters, all the law of rabbinically forbidden days applies to them — end quote. And in Derishah he brought a gloss in the name of Maharshal: one who vows to fast a month — they tell him "in this month that you vowed there are Yom Tov and Shabbatot," and he tells them "I did not intend them" — he does not even need release, for these are matters that prove intent — end quote. And Turei Zahav rules that release is needed whenever he spoke in vow-language; but if he said that it explicitly entered his mind not to fast except on weekdays, he is believed on this. Another gloss in his name: a woman who vowed to fast after Pesach from the second day until Rosh Chodesh — after Pesach she asks when she should begin. It appears she should begin after the days of Nisan if the custom in all places is that one cannot fast all the days of Nisan. And Bach wrote: this is specifically in language of accepting a fast in general; but in vow-language release is needed, for she vowed explicitly to fast after Pesach — it is no better than one who vowed to fast on Yom Tov — end quote:
