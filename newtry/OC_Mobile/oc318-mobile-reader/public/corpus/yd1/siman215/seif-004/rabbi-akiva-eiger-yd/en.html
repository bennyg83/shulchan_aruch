(There, seif 6) Some say that if he vowed concerning forbidden things. The Ran distinguishes between something intrinsically forbidden, such as a carcass, where a vow does not take effect because one prohibition cannot be superimposed upon another and the original prohibition already rests on the object, and a person who first swore not to eat and then declared the eating forbidden to himself by konam. In the latter case, the oath originally rested on his person, not the object, so the vow takes effect. The same applies if he declares Yom Kippur eating forbidden to himself by konam. Rashba 615 appears to agree. The Beit Yosef, however, cites a Rashba responsum that no vow takes effect in any such case, like the first opinion here.
