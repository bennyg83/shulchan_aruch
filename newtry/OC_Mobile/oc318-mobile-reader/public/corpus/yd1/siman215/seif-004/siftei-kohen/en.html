And see Orach Chaim siman 570 that he did not rule thus — meaning there he wrote: an individual who accepted upon himself to fast such and such days and Shabbatot and Yom Tov or Rosh Chodesh Chanukah and Purim or the like occurred among them — if he accepted in language "hara alai" which is language of neder, requires release of a sage, etc.; for Rambam in all of them he must fast on them unless they release him, except Chanukah and Purim — behold he wrote the first view anonymously and here wrote only Rambam's view anonymously. It appears to me to resolve: in Orach Chaim siman 518 Beit Yosef challenged Rambam how sages have power to uproot a Torah matter in a positive command where we tell him rise and eat; he answered because of fencing or there is reason and aspect that they have power to uphold their words — thus Mechaber should rule specifically here in a vow to fast one or two days all his life there is fencing and reason, for he would need to fast all his days on Chanukah — thus a rabbinic matter is uprooted; but in Orach Chaim dealing explicitly with one who accepted such and such days — he would fast Chanukah and Purim once or twice and the rabbinic matter is not greatly uprooted — cannot challenge Mechaber how we know Rambam holds thus, perhaps only for a vow all his life; for necessarily Rambam holds the vow is void, for he derived this law from what we say in first chapter of Taanit: an individual who accepted fast on second and fifth of the whole year and the Yom Tov days written in Megillat Taanit occurred among them — his vow is void, as explained in Beit Yosef siman 518 — there it says he accepted second and fifth of the whole year, implying only one year not all his life; for this reason Mechaber ruled and brought dissenters because Ran challenged Rambam's view from Yerushalmi, brought in Beit Yosef there — one can say this view was not rejected except for a timed vow, but for a vow all his life the vow is void; know there is a distinction as I explained, for in seif 3 Mechaber wrote: but if they did not express it except in language of accepting fast, it does not push off Shabbat, Yom Tov, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, or Purim — implies if he accepted in language of neder it pushes off Chanukah and Purim, and this contradicts what he wrote here in seif 4; if so Mechaber contradicts himself — forced to say Chanukah and Purim were mentioned for a reason; rather certainly in seif 3 it deals with a vow for a known time, as I explained also in Orach Chaim siman 570 where the Rav wrote practice follows first view unlike Yoreh De'ah siman 215 — end of his words; it did not enter his mind to distinguish thus; but per my distinction you will not find any posek who disagrees with Rambam on this (and Semag negative commandments 242 p. 71 also wrote like Rambam; and R' Yerucham part 4 chapter 6 wrote this is essential; and likewise responsum Binyamin Zev siman 265) — so it appears to me. Regarding oath see what Mechaber wrote in Orach Chaim siman 518 and 570; but Levush wrote in siman 518 that even one who swore explicitly to fast on Rosh Chodesh should not fast though Rosh Chodesh is rabbinic, since he has support in Torah they strengthened their words like Torah — oath does not take effect, etc. — his words are unclear; I do not know his source nor why he needs this (and even per Mechaber there in name of Rambam — because Rosh Chodesh is Torah-level). Possibly he intended to resolve what Beit Yosef challenged in Tur Orach Chaim siman 518 on what Tur wrote in Orach Chaim siman 570 regarding Rosh Chodesh — but this did not help, for Chanukah and Purim are also difficult; rather the essence: Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, and the like being rabbinic — oath takes effect on them; likewise Bach in Orach Chaim siman 570; and further even per Rambam who appears from his words Rosh Chodesh and the like are Torah-level — one can say he agrees oath takes effect on them, for one can say he holds as Ran chapter Shevuot Shetayim and first chapter of Nedarim (daf 8 side a): even a Torah matter since not explicit in Scripture but comes from derashah — oath takes effect; likewise the Rav below siman 236 seif 2 and Mechaber siman 239 seif 6; likewise Bach siman 236 note 4 that Rambam's view who wrote one who swore to injure himself — oath takes effect since injury comes only from derashah; also below siman 239 Mechaber brought Beit Yosef responsum of Ran who wrote he found support for his words from Rambam's words — this is unlike Mechaber in Orach Chaim there who wrote per Rambam oath does not take effect, therefore release the oath and he does not fast as the Rav wrote there. Acharonim in name of responsum Maharam Dim Nisan: likewise other days when tachanun is not said — their law is like days forbidden by their words; however it appears in a vow in language of neder even per Rambam requires release on them, for unlike Chanukah and Purim written in Megillat Taanit and not yet nullified, and they are more important and sages strengthened — unlike these; but for other matters all law of days forbidden by their words applies to them — so it appears to me.
