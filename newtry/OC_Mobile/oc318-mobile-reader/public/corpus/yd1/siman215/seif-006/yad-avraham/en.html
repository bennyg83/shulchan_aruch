(There seif 6) That if he vowed from forbidden things, such as notar and tereifah, etc. See Sefer Be’er Yaakov, who writes that the practical difference in saying that the prohibition of a vow takes effect upon notar and tereifah is that it becomes a davar she-yesh lo matirin and is not nullified. To me this requires study, for in Nedarim 59 it states that vows are considered a davar she-yesh lo matirin because there is a mitzvah to ask release from them, based on Rabbi Natan, that one who vows is as though he built a private altar, and one who maintains it, etc. This is specifically with a discretionary matter. But when he vowed to fulfill a mitzvah, there is no mitzvah in asking release, for he acted properly by vowing, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of Perek Konam Yayin. If so, in such a case it should not be a davar she-yesh lo matirin. However, one may uphold the words of Be’er Yaakov according to what is written below.
