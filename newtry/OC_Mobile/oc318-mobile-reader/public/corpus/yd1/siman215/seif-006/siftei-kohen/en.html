And some say that if he vowed regarding forbidden matters, etc. Similarly, if he said, “I swear that I will not eat this loaf,” and then said, “This loaf is forbidden to me as a konam,” he is liable twice. Likewise, if he swore that he would eat this loaf and afterward prohibited it to himself by a konam, he may not eat it, because vows take effect even upon a mitzvah obligation and upon an existing oath. The Ran writes this there.
But if, etc. This applies even according to the latter view, as stated by the Tur and the poskim.
For this is attaching the vow to something inherently forbidden. Nevertheless, in practice, some say that release is required even when one attaches a vow to something inherently forbidden, as stated above in siman 205.
Rich matzah is not included. That is, he may eat it without release from the oath. Nevertheless, one who swore not to eat matzah on the night of Pesach receives lashes and then eats matzah on the night of Pesach, as explained in Orach Chaim, siman 485, and below, siman 236, seif 5.
