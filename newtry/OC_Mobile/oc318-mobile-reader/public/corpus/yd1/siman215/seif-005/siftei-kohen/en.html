How so, if he said, “The eating of non-kosher meat is forbidden to me,” the vow does not take effect upon him, etc. The Bach writes that one may wonder why the Mechaber first rules without qualification that the vow does not take effect, following the Rashba in his responsum, and afterward writes that some say the vow does take effect. The Bach concludes that the primary ruling appears to follow Rashi, Tosafos, and the Tur. Yet his wonder itself is surprising, for the Mechaber first presents the Rashba’s view and then writes, “Some say,” which is the usual form throughout. Perhaps the Bach’s copy read “some say” without the conjunction “and.” As to his conclusion that the primary ruling follows Rashi and Tosafos, I say that had he examined Responsa Rashba, siman 615, where the Rashba develops numerous proofs and reasons for his position, he would not have written this. If those proofs did not appear correct to him, he should have cited and rejected them. Furthermore, what the Bach wrote concerning Tosafos is not compelling. Even according to Tosafos’ qualification, the rule applies specifically according to Rabbi Yehudah ben Beseira and not according to us. Otherwise, Tosafos’ additional proof from vows would not stand. On the contrary, Tosafos’ conclusion follows the Rashba, as is explicit in Piskei Tosafos, chapter Shevuos Shetayim, siman 18. See there. The Ran, however, in the second chapter of Nedarim, folio 18a, agrees with the Tur.
