It takes effect upon one who swore that he would sleep, etc. It appears that the meaning is as follows: If he swore, “I swear that I will sleep,” and afterward vowed, “Sleep is a konam upon me,” the vow takes effect, just as when one says to his wife, “The benefit of your marital relations is forbidden upon me,” the vow takes effect, as explained below. Although if he says, “My marital relations are forbidden to you,” it does not take effect because he is obligated to her, he may nevertheless forbid the benefit that he receives from her, even though he is obligated to her. So too here, even though he is obligated to sleep or to drink, he may forbid the benefit of sleep upon himself through the language of a vow. This is not comparable to what was written first, that if he swore not to eat this loaf and afterward said, “This loaf is a konam upon me,” it is not a vow. There, the vow is directed in the same manner as the oath. Here, however, the vow is directed opposite to the oath, and it therefore takes effect, just as in the case of, “The benefit of your marital relations is forbidden upon me.”
