In what case is release required, etc. — this is Ramban's view in Tur; let us explain Tur's words briefly in what Sefer HaMitzvot and the rest of his continuation brought per Beit Yosef Orach Chaim siman 518 — four poskim — and from this it will be explained there is room to examine Shulchan Aruch in seif 4: Tur wrote in name of Sefer HaMitzvot to distinguish between one who accepted to fast on Yom Tov itself or it occurred thus within the time, etc. — the poskim were challenged by two Talmudic places contradicting: in first chapter of Taanit if an individual accepted fast on second and fifth of the whole year and Yom Tov days in Megillat Taanit occurred among them, we nullify our decree on his vow — difficult, for we say vows take effect on a mitzvah matter; and we say they release for honor of Shabbat and Yom Tov — implies the vow takes effect. Ramban answered: vows take effect on mitzvah when he said complete neder language "hara alai eating on such a day"; but in first chapter of Taanit it deals with mere acceptance of fast, such as saying "I will fast tomorrow"; and in Semak siman 81 — that is Sefer HaMitzvot Tur brought, not as Beit Yosef understood Semag — therefore his challenge; but in truth it was Semak who wrote this distinction: vows on mitzvah deals with one who vows explicitly to fast on Yom Tov; Taanit deals when it occurred within the time he vowed to fast — in that case we say one who vows to fast did not intend to nullify sages' decrees, all the more Torah law on a day not for fasting; but plainly Sefer HaMitzvot also requires release in all cases, unlike Perishah in seif 1 and Beit Yosef Orach Chaim siman 518 who challenged Sefer HaMitzvot: we learned they do not decree public fast on Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim — if they began they do not stop — implies even if Rosh Chodesh etc. occurred within days they accepted to fast, they still fast; yet they do not begin fasting on them; and it seems from Rambam and Raavad more reason to postpone fast for one who vowed on Yom Tov itself than if it came within the vowed time — opposite Sefer HaMitzvot in Tur here. I know not even a whiff of difficulty — certainly regarding prohibition to accept fast initially, stricter when vowing on Yom Tov itself than if it arrived within passed time which is included — no court decrees public fast on Yom Tov; but regarding if one already passed and vowed to fast on Yom Tov itself, the vow takes effect more than if it came within the time where perhaps he did not consider it — this is plain; on this Ramban answers the two conclusions above — Tur brought Sefer HaMitzvot who distinguishes another matter from Ramban's distinction; and Tur wrote "as written in Sefer HaMitzvot" in dispute language because Sefer HaMitzvot does not disagree with Ramban's distinction between acceptance of fast and neder language — correct distinction; and it appears forced that if he said acceptance-of-fast language he forbids himself from eating, resembling oath which does not take effect on mitzvah except specifically neder as in beginning of this siman — but Semak did not wish to answer the conclusions thus since both use neder language; therefore answered with another distinction. Rambam made a third distinction: first chapter of Taanit deals with rabbinic Yom Tov where they strengthened their words that vow does not take effect even when vowing explicitly to fast on them; Tur concludes Rosh's conclusion is like Ramban; if so, since Shulchan Aruch ruled here like Ramban, why rule in next seif vow does not take effect on Yom Tov — that is Rambam's words and they disagree; in Orach Chaim siman 518 seif 1 he made the dispute, and here wrote like both — Rama wrote well in Orach Chaim not exactly so; Shulchan Aruch in Orach Chaim siman 518 ruled rabbinic Yom Tov — vow takes effect but not Torah-level; and Rama in Orach Chaim siman 570 wrote custom is not like Rambam, for here for halacha we have no distinction except between neder language and acceptance-of-fast language: in neder language it takes effect in all cases and requires release; in fast language it does not take effect at all and needs no release — that is Ramban's view.
And even if he did not accept upon himself to fast except on Shabbat and festivals — meaning not only at the beginning where he did not intend at all for Shabbat and festivals, but even if he explicitly accepted to fast on Shabbat and festivals, nevertheless the vow does not take effect since he did not say it in language of "behold upon me"; and gratuitously Mahari Chai wrote on Shulchan Aruch's language here that it is not precise. Incident: a man vowed to give from the profit of his property a tithe and so practiced several days, then his hand slackened and he regretted — it appears he can retract because profit never came and one does not consecrate what never came, as explained in chapter "An ox that gored four and five"; however if he said "this money — all profit reaching from it I will give a tithe from it," consecration takes effect since consecration came into existence — this Derishah brought in the name of Rashal in the name of Me'il Tzedek; we do not hold so, rather as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 212 seif 9: one who says when I take it I will give it to consecration — a vow name is upon him and he must fulfill his vow — so it appears to me. He further brought a gloss in his name that R. Eliezer of Metz: one who vows to fast a month — they tell him in this month you vowed there are festivals and Shabbatot; he says I did not intend them — he needs no release, for hidden matters — these are evident matters — end; and it appears this is like Sefer HaMitzvot we mentioned; and according to what we hold like Ramban, here too release is required whenever he said in vow language as above, except if he said explicitly it was on his mind not to fast except weekdays — he is believed, for he does not contradict his vow language of fasting one month; thereby the above gloss and Shulchan Aruch seif 2 requiring release is not about this but when festivals and Shabbat did not occur to him at vow time. Another gloss in his name: a woman vowed to fast after second and fifth days of Passover until Rosh Chodesh; after Passover they asked when she begins — it appears after Nissan days if the custom everywhere is not to say supplications all Nissan days — end; and Mahari Chai wrote this is specifically in language of accepting a fast generally, but in vow language release is required since she explicitly vowed to fast after Passover — no better than one who vows to fast on a festival.
