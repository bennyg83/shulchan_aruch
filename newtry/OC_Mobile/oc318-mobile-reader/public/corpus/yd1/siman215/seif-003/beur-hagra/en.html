In the manner that one says, etc. Rosh there, in the name of Ramban, derives this from the first chapter of Taanis (12a), “If our decree,” etc. See Orach Chaim, siman 570, and what is stated there.
And even if he did not accept, etc. This is unlike the Tur in the name of Sefer HaMitzvos, which appears in Semak, siman 81. Semak explains that the statement that vows take effect upon a mitzvah, etc., refers to one who accepted it explicitly, unlike the case in Taanis. This is incorrect, as the glosses there object, for the Mishnah teaches that one may propose Shabbos and Yom Tov as grounds for release, etc. The Tur was careful about this and interpreted Semak's words as qualifying Ramban's ruling, namely that Ramban discusses one who expressed himself only in the language of acceptance. This is what the Tur means by “and Sefer HaMitzvos wrote,” etc. In Beit Yosef, Orach Chaim, siman 418, he rejects the Tur's words and says that, on the contrary, when one explicitly accepted the fast, it certainly does not take effect once Shabbos or Yom Tov arrives, for it is stated there (Taanis, beginning of chapter 2), “We do not decree,” etc., and “if they began,” etc. The word “even” here is intended to reject Semak's position.
