And even if he did not accept upon himself to fast except on Shabbat and Yom Tov alone — no release needed if they did not express it only in language of accepting fast; Bach finds two difficulties: one — what is "even," on the contrary all the more he has no substance in accepting to fast on Shabbat and Yom Tov alone; and further he contradicts himself in his long book (Beit Yosef Orach Chaim siman 518) where he wrote from Ramban and Raavad it appears all the more so, and here he wrote "even" and was not precise — for in truth all the more so. I say he spoke well saying "even" — according to the first view that he did not intend Shabbat and Yom Tov; but even in the conclusion where he intended Shabbat and Yom Tov explicitly when he encountered them — why would his acceptance in language of neder take effect on Shabbat and Yom Tov when intending to vow on them, and in an unspecified neder encountering them no question is needed per Semag; thus I would say the same if he accepted in language of accepting fast, and likewise Orach Chaim siman 518 implies: where we say if Yom Tov occurs within the days he accepted to fast it does not push off Yom Tov — all the more where he accepted explicitly to fast that day, etc.; and so appears from Rambam and Raavad; if so, encountering them is explicit, and accepting explicitly to fast — though in truth all the more — nevertheless he had to say thus from implication since not explicit, lest one think it is less — as I wrote; and one must explain "even" that Rambam and Taz wrote below siman 317 seif 2, as written there note 6 — see there.
