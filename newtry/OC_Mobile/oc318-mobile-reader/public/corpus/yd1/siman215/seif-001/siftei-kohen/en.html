And they release his vow, etc. Derishah wrote: it implies that he holds that even regarding a sukkah, or if he vowed explicitly to fast on Shabbat and Yom Tov, they release him, etc., by telling him "had you known that it is forbidden to afflict oneself on Shabbat and Yom Tov," etc., and the like. And so it appears in Orach Chaim siman 570, and so is the main point, and so it appears from the words of Bach:
And some say there is not, etc. However, it appears they would give him makat mardut, as Rosh wrote in Nedarim at the end of the chapter of Rabbi Eliezer — and this is according to the Yerushalmi. And with this I would explain the words of Ra'avyah that they do not contradict each other: he wrote in Mordechai at the beginning of Shevuot in the name of Ra'avyah that there are no lashes for a vow, and afterward wrote: but in other places I found in the name of Ra'avyah that he commanded to lash Rabbi Yisrael who vowed not to return the halachah to his brother tomorrow — they opened for him with regret and commanded to lash him after his vow was released — end quote.
