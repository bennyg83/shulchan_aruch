And some say that it does not, etc. See Rosh 14b, s.v. “Do we leave,” and Ran there, s.v. “Rather,” etc. It appears that this is the reasoning of the first view, from the statement “Do we leave,” etc., and as stated regarding an oath in the Mishnah, Shevuos 29a, “One who swore,” etc., “this is,” etc. See also Ran 24b, s.v. “So too with oaths,” etc., and 25a, s.v. “Rather, Rava said,” etc. See Rosh in his rulings, chapter 9, end of seif 5, in the name of the Yerushalmi, which explains “he is lashed,” etc. This is the position of the first view, that it is nevertheless prohibited by rabbinic law, as Ran writes on 21a in the Mishnah, “It was taught in the Tosefta of our tractate,” etc., and as stated above, siman 232, seif 13. Nevertheless, his words require study. Since he writes that they release him from the vow, the vow evidently took effect, so why should he be lashed? It is not a vain oath. Raavyah likewise challenged this there. Also, what he writes there, that the vow does not take effect, is not understood, yet he nevertheless writes that they must release him from it. Raavyah already challenged him on this. Regarding what he writes there that he found in the name of Raavyah, etc., see Shach. The second view is primary.
