And some say there is not, etc. Rosh page 14b in the matter of "one who accepted," and Ran there in the matter of "but rather," etc. And it appears this is the reason for the first view from what is written "one who accepted," etc., and as is written regarding an oath in the Mishnah Shevuot 29a "one who swore," etc. "this is," etc., and Ran 24b in the matter of "thus oaths," etc., and likewise 1a in the matter of "but he said," etc., and Rosh in his rulings chapter 9 siman 365 in the name of the Yerushalmi explains "he is lashed," etc. — and this is the view of the first view that nevertheless it is rabbinically forbidden, as Ran wrote 21a in the Mishnah "it is taught in the Tosefta of Masechet," etc., and as was explained above siman 232 se'if yud gimmel. Nevertheless his words require investigation, since he wrote that they release him to him — evidently the vow takes effect — so why would he be lashed? It is not a vain oath. And likewise Ra'avyah challenged there. Also what he wrote there that the vow does not take effect is not understood. Nevertheless he wrote that they need to release him — and Ra'avyah already challenged him. And what he wrote there that he found in the name of Ra'avyah, etc. — Shach.
