Sitting. Shach writes that this is not an incorporeal prohibition, because he names the object that becomes forbidden. If he said, 'The benefit of the sukkah is forbidden to me,' he may still sit in it, because mitzvot were not given for personal benefit. Taz explains that a vow can prohibit the object upon him even where this prevents a mitzvah, whereas an oath cannot exempt him from a mitzvah obligation.
And they release him. Derishah writes that even if he explicitly vowed to fast on Shabbat or Yom Tov, or prohibited sitting in a sukkah, the vow should be annulled by asking whether he would have vowed had he known the relevant mitzvah prohibition. Bach and Shach agree.
Lashes. Shach writes that although Torah lashes do not apply, rabbinic disciplinary lashes may be administered.
