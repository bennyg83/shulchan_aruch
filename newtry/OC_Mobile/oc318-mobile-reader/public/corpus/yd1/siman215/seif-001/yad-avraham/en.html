(Siman 215 se'if 1) Vows take effect on a matter of mitzvah. How so — he said konam sukkah she'ani yoshev, etc. — it is forbidden to sit. And Shach wrote: if he said "the benefit of a sukkah is upon me," it is permitted to sit in a sukkah of mitzvah, for mitzvot were not given for benefit. And it appears it is not specifically when he said explicitly "benefit of a sukkah," for the same applies when he said without specification konam sukkah is upon me — for it implies only benefit. And so Ran in Nedarim (end of page 16) explicitly, and so from the Yerushalmi he brought there on page 34 regarding one who vowed from a loaf — what is the law to warm his hands with it? He explains: if this benefit is significant, it implies; for if this benefit were not significant, he certainly did not forbid himself with an unspecified vow. And so one could say from Rashi and Tosafot in Eruvin (page 30) regarding one who said "this loaf is upon me" — according to the one who says one may only make an eruv for a matter of mitzvah, and mitzvot were not given for benefit — it is permitted to make an eruv with it. And so Rosh HaShanah (page 28), and likewise in Sefer Eliyah Zuta in Orach Chaim. And see Kuntres HaNetivot in Eruvin. And regarding one who vows not to eat at a voluntary feast, Chavat Yair responsum 70 wrote: a housewarming feast — there is room to question, even though Scripture compares it to one who takes a wife and plants a vineyard, which are mitzvot as is in Targum Yonatan.
