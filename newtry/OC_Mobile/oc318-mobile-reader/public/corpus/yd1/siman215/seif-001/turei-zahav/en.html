They take effect upon a matter involving a mitzvah. The reason is that with a vow, since he forbids the object upon himself, saying, “The sitting in a sukkah is a konam upon me,” he is not regarded as one who vows to nullify the mitzvah. He did not accept anything upon himself; rather, he forbade the object upon himself, and one may not feed a person something forbidden to him. An oath, however, forbids the person himself from performing the act, and he has no authority to release himself from the obligation of a mitzvah.
And they release him from his vow. The Prishah wrote that the language of the Tur implies that release is ineffective in this case, since the Tur wrote, “Nevertheless, he should not fast,” etc., but did not write this regarding the first case, namely, “The sukkah in which I sit.” This is no inference at all, for certainly release is effective for every vow. The Tur teaches this specifically regarding a fast so that we should not say that fasting is also a mitzvah and that he should therefore fulfill his vow. The Prishah further wrote that the Sefer HaMitzvos cited afterward by the Tur also holds that there is no release for one who vows to fast on Yom Tov. This too is incorrect, and the words of the Sefer HaMitzvos will be explained below, with God’s help.
