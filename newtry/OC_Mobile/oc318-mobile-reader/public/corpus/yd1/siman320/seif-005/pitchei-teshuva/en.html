See Responsa Maharam Ziskind, siman 32, concerning a place where the custom is that the king takes the tenth offspring born to each of a person's animals—that is, as soon as the animal gives birth it is recorded by the king's scribe, and when the count shows one by one that ten offspring have been born to him, the king then takes the tenth offspring. But before ten offspring have been born to him, he is free to sell his offspring and animals without opposition, and he is not obligated to give the king anything. Now a Jew has one animal that gave birth for the first time and produced a firstborn—is he obligated in firstborn sanctity or not? May it be compared to a flock from which the king takes a tithe? He replied that it is obvious that this is not called "the hand of a non-Jew in the middle," and it is obligated in firstborn sanctity, and it is not comparable to that ruling for several reasons; see there.
