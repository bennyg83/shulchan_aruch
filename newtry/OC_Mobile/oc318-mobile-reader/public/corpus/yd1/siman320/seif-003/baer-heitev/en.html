One thousand. In Beit Yosef he wrote in the name of Tosafot in Bekhorot that even acquiring its ear is sufficient. However, in Tosafot at the end of Avodah Zarah they wrote that one should be stringent to transfer to the non-Jew something that makes it a treifah, such as the lung or the head. Still, the non-Jew need not pay the value of the head or lung; he may acquire it with a perutah, even though it is worth many times more, and there is no concern of mistaken sale, for we are witnesses that the Jew fully transfers it to the non-Jew in order to exempt himself. The Perishah asked: how is this different from siman 61 regarding gifts of kehunah, where the non-Jew must have a share in the whole animal, and if he is a partner only in the head, he is exempt only from the cheeks, etc.? According to Tosafot, why should an important part like the head be required and yet suffice? It appears that this is not difficult. Specifically here, regarding bekhor, where it is written “petter rechem in Israel,” we derive that the whole must be among Israelites; if an important part is removed from it, the whole obligation is nullified. This is unlike the gifts, where there is no scriptural derivation of that sort. Rather, wherever the non-Jew has a share in that particular part, he is exempt from gifts for that part and no more, for one part does not depend on the other at all. End quote from Taz.
