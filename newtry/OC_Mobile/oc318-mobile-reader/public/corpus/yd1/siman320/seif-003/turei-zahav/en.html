One part in a thousand. In the Beit Yosef it is written in the name of Tosafot at the beginning of Bekhorot that even [selling] an ear suffices, but that Tosafot at the end of chapter Avodah Zarah wrote that one should be strict and convey to the non-Jew something that would make the animal a treifah, such as the lung or the head. However, the non-Jew need not give money equal to the value of the head or lung — it suffices that he acquire it for a perutah, even though it is worth double, since there is no concern of a void transaction (bitul mekach), for we are confident the Israelite intends and conveys it to the non-Jew in order to be exempt [from the firstborn]. The Drisha raised a difficulty there: in what way does this differ from siman 61 regarding the priestly gifts — where a non-Jew must have a share in the entire animal, even a minimal share, and if the non-Jew is only a partner in the head there is no exemption except from the cheeks and arm [and stomach]. If so, according to Tosafot, who require specifically something significant like the head — why should that be sufficient? He answered as he answered. To me it seems there is no difficulty at all, for specifically regarding a firstborn — where the Torah states "the firstborn of Israel" — we derive that everything must belong to the children of Israel. Similarly in chapter Meru’beh regarding one who sold [all] except for one hundredth — from the verse "and sold" we expound "and sold all of it" — everything depends on one another, and if something significant is removed it nullifies the whole. But regarding priestly gifts, there is no such biblical derivation; rather, wherever the non-Jew has a share in [a limb], that portion is exempt from the gifts and no more — one limb does not depend on another at all.
