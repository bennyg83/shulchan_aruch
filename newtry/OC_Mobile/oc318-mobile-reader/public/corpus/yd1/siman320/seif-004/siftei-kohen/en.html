He had a share in one of them, etc. That is, only in seif 3 where the non-Jew had a partnership in general — even in one part in a thousand, which could be the heart or brain, the place upon which all the animal's vitality depends. But if the non-Jew's partnership is not in them in general but only in a specific limb — such as having in one of the two a single limb like a hand or leg — we look: would all of them be tangled if he cut, etc. So explained the Etz Yosef. In the Drisha it is written that the entire limb must belong to the non-Jew. The Ateret Zekenim explained it differently. See there. However, Tosafot at the end of chapter Avodah Zarah write that one must convey to the non-Jew something upon which the animal's vitality depends. So too writes the Hagahot Mordechai in chapter Isha Rabbah. One should be strict initially, and so writes the Agudah at the beginning of Bekhorot. It is good to convey something that would make it a nevelah or a treifah.
