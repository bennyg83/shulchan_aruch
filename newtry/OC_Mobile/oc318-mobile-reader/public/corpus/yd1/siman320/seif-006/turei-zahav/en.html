And the acquisition shall be that he accept a perutah, etc. The Tur explains the reason: according to Rashi's interpretation, money effects acquisition for a non-Jew without pulling; and according to R. Tam, only pulling effects acquisition, and one must pull the animal to the public domain or to a simta. Therefore, to satisfy both opinions, one should do thus:
Or he should accept money, etc. The Tur writes that the first method — acquiring for him the place — is preferable. The reason appears to be that acquisition by agav [along with the land] is superior to pulling, as written in Ḥoshen Mishpat §157 in the words of Ramah:
Or he should accept money, etc. It is written in Resp. Maharri Segal §174 that if he sold using only one method — money or pulling — it is a doubtful firstborn even after the fact, since we do not know which opinion we rule by. In Resp. Rema §87 he wrote that this applies specifically ab initio, that one needs both money and pulling; but after the fact we certainly rule like R. Tam — since all decisors ruled that money effects acquisition for an Israelite, the same applies to a non-Jew: pulling effects acquisition, etc. — and therefore if the Israelite did not pull the cow, it is obvious the cow still stands in the non-Jew's domain; and there is no distinction between a non-Jew acquiring from an Israelite or an Israelite from a non-Jew. See further there, and in Piskei Maharai §130 at its end, which states that even though transferring the fetus to the non-Jew is ineffective because it is something not yet in existence, there is nevertheless reason to say it is effective if the cow is pregnant and it is expected that the fetus will be born — analogous to the case at the beginning of chapter Ha-Mafkid: it is as if he said "my cow is acquired by you near the time of her theft." And he wrote there that if there is another permissive consideration — such as the case where she was seen to have been lactating beforehand — it is effective in combination. It is also written in that Resp. Maharai that one who stipulates with the non-Jew that he feed the cow in exchange for the calf, this is considered as giving him money for it.
