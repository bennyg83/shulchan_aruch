In our times, etc. Tosafot, 3b, s.v. deka, etc.:
And if he acquires, etc., or he acquires, etc. Ibid.:
(Likkut) And if he acquires, etc. As written in ch. 9 of Bava Batra 141b and in ch. 3 of Kiddushin (62b), that a fetus is something not yet in existence [davar she-lo ba le-olam]. But according to Rashbam, who ruled in Bava Batra 143a s.v. ha-re'iyyah etc., like R. Naḥman who said "when she gives birth, he acquires" — and explained there 142a s.v. likh-she-teled that the reason is because he considers a fetus as something that has come into existence — if so, the same applies here, that he acquires. But according to Tosafot at the end of ch. 1 of Gittin (13b) s.v. le-davar, etc. [and Tosafot in Ketubot 7b s.v. ha-mezakkeh, etc., which state that] the reason of R. Naḥman is that he holds the opposite of R. Huna — that for something not yet in existence, he acquires, and for something that has come into existence, he does not acquire — if so, R. Naḥman would concede here that he does not acquire. See the Hagahot ha-Mordekhai in Perek Mah de-Bava Batra, s.v. bein be-matnat, etc. And all the more so according to Tur and Shulḥan Arukh Ḥoshen Mishpat §210 s.1, who ruled like Ramah who ruled against R. Naḥman. See also what I wrote in Even ha-Ezer (end of §40) regarding the Kiddushin cited above (end):
(Likkut) If, etc. Since a fetus is of no legal effect as something not yet in existence, as written in the Tosefta and Jerusalem Talmud, and the Rosh in ch. 4 of Bava Batra §7 cites: one who sells the fetus of his cow or maidservant to another has done nothing whatsoever [and in Ḥoshen Mishpat §209 s.4]. And I am puzzled by the Hagahot ha-Mordekhai of Perek Mi she-Met, who wrote there (in the above Likkut) — see there. (One may answer for Rashbam that the Tosefta and Jerusalem Talmud speak of a case where he did not specify "when she gives birth" — but see Rashbam and the Hagahot ha-Mordekhai there.) (End.)
And the acquisition, etc., or he accepts, etc. Tosafot in Tractate Avodah Zarah 71a, end of s.v. Rav Ashi — and one should be careful also about Rashi's view in Bekhorot 3b s.v. kinyan gamur, etc. But as to the law, we rule as Tosafot wrote in Avodah Zarah ibid. and in Bekhorot 14a s.v. Rav Ashi, that pulling [meshikhah] by a non-Jew effects acquisition, and so too is the conclusion of the Talmud in Avodah Zarah ibid. 72a. And so ruled the Rambam in several places, and so ruled the Shulḥan Arukh above, §132 s.1 and s.2, and §146 s.3. [See §132 s.2 that monetary payment alone also effects acquisition, and see the Siftei Kohen there s.k. 17, and see what I wrote in Ḥoshen Mishpat §194.]
