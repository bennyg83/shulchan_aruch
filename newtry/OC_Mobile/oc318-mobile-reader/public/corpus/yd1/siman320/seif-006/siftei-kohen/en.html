In order to transfer, etc. Because the acquisition takes effect, similar to a tree for its fruits or a cow and maidservant for their offspring — since the cow is in existence in the world, and as explained in Ḥoshen Mishpat ibid.:
That he accept a perutah, etc. The non-Jew need not give money equivalent to the head or lung; it suffices to acquire for a perutah, even though it is worth more than double. Nevertheless there is no concern of annulment of the sale, since we are witnesses that the Israelite fully intended to transfer ownership to the non-Jew in order to be exempt. Tosafot, last chapter of Avodah Zarah:
And let him acquire the place, etc. Because there is a dispute among the decisors: R. Tam and his school hold that pulling [meshikhah] effects acquisition for a non-Jew but not money; Rashi and his school hold that money effects acquisition but not pulling. To satisfy both opinions one must perform both pulling and monetary acquisition. It implies that even after the fact, if he performed only one acquisition — pulling or money — he is not exempted from the firstborn obligation. So too wrote Resp. Maharil, and the Drishah cites him; and so too ruled the Beit Yosef and other later authorities. The Bach further wrote that even if he performed both pulling and money, but the non-Jew did not give the money at the time of the acquisition but only promised to give such-and-such an amount, and he trusted him — even though the non-Jew afterward gave what he had promised, that money does not effect acquisition. Needless to say, if the Israelite owed the non-Jew money and the non-Jew said "your cow is acquired by me for such-and-such money that you owe me," and the Israelite transferred it to him — it is obvious that this is not a monetary acquisition. All this was explained in his responsum. It further implies that the same applies when he purchased a cow from a non-Jew: according to R. Tam, only pulling effects acquisition; according to Rashi, money does; and thus if the Israelite performed pulling or gave money and she gave birth, he is obligated regarding the firstborn due to doubt — as is explained in Resp. R. Meir of Rothenburg §5 and in Resp. of the Rabbi §87. He also wrote there that if one purchased a cow from a non-Jew with money and she was stolen, and the non-Jew agreed to pay the Israelite the cow, and afterward the thief was identified and the cow gave birth — he is exempt from the firstborn, since once the non-Jew agreed to pay, the cow is acquired wherever she is. See there at length, and see further in the responsa of the later authorities many matters pertaining to these laws.
