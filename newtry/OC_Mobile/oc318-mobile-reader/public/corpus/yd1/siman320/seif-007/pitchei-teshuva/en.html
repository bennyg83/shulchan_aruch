Since the non-Jew. See Be'er Heitev in the name of the Shach, who wrote: what can you say, that it is a thing not yet come into the world, etc. See Dagul MeRevavah above, seif 6, who wrote that Mahari himself relied on this only together with other leniencies; see there. So too he wrote in Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah siman 191, that one should not rely on these words of the Rama, since Mahari himself did not rely on this alone. For this reason, regarding a bekhor where the owner of the animal said to a non-Jew before it gave birth, "If this animal gives birth to a male, it shall belong to the non-Jew," and the non-Jew was raising the animal for the Jew's benefit without any benefit of shearing or offspring, and afterward it gave birth to a male and the non-Jew wanted to keep it for himself in accordance with the owner's words, he wrote that it has the sanctity of a bekhor. At that time it was something not yet come into the world, and there is another defect in the statement "if it gives birth to a male," which also raises concern of asmachta; see there. In siman 192 he wrote that if the arrangement has no concern of asmachta, such as where he did not condition it on being male and the fetus was recognizable, then one may join the Rambam's view that this is considered to have come into the world. Even so, in practice I would deliberate; but since one authority already ruled that it is not a bekhor, relying on the Rama and Shach, it is difficult to overturn. Still, it should not be slaughtered as an unblemished animal; rather, he should instruct the non-Jew to cut its ear, and afterward the Jew may slaughter it. If it was already sheared and the wool sold, the money is permitted. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 312 on this case as well, and see also Chemdat Shlomo responsa siman 33 regarding this; study there at length. See also Teshuvah MeAhavah I simanim 176 and 177 at length.
