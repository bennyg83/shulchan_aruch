Since the non-Jew. See Baer Heitev in the name of Shach, and Dagul MeRevavah above in seif 6, who notes that Terumat HaDeshen himself relied on this point only together with other leniencies. Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 191, likewise writes that the Rama's ruling should not be relied upon by itself, since Terumat HaDeshen himself did not. He therefore rules concerning an owner who told a non-Jew before the animal gave birth, "If this animal gives birth to a male, it will belong to you," where the non-Jew merely raised the animal for the Jew without any right to wool or offspring, that the male born has firstborn sanctity, because at the time of the statement it was something not yet in existence. There is also a further defect because the condition "if it gives birth to a male" may constitute asmachta. In siman 192 he writes that if the arrangement contains no asmachta, such as where it was not conditioned specifically on a male, and the pregnancy was already recognizable, one may combine the Rambam's opinion that this is considered something already in existence. Even so, he would have hesitated in practice. Since another authority had already ruled that the animal was not a firstborn based on the Rama and Shach, he did not overturn that ruling, but required that it not be slaughtered while unblemished. Instead, a non-Jew should cut its ear and then the Jew may slaughter it. If it had already been shorn and the wool sold, the money is permitted. See there at length, and also Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 312, Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, siman 33, and Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah, part 1, simanim 176 and 177.
