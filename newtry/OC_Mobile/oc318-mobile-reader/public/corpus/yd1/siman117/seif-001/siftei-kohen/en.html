If it is an item designated for food, etc. To exclude horses, donkeys, and camels, for their ordinary designation is for labor. And the Beit Yosef brought in the name of Tosafos at the end of the chapter Merubah, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that the same applies to other tamei items that are designated for eating: if one raises them to smear hides with their fat in order to profit from them, or to sell them to another Jew who will smear with them, or to light [a lamp], it is permitted; but regarding a pig everything is forbidden because of an incident that occurred, as the Taz wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 9. And so wrote the Agudah, and the Rosh, and the Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Rosh and the Ra'ah; and not as the Bach wrote that the Rosh disputes Rabbeinu Tam and the Agudah. And from the wording of the Mechaber, who wrote ‘if it is an item designated for food,’ it does not somewhat imply this; rather, it is like the Rashba whom the Beit Yosef brought: that if it is designated for food and it is the practice of people to raise them in order to eat them, even though he does not trade in these items to sell them for the purpose of eating, it is forbidden. And so ruled explicitly in the Atur Zahav. And one can say that this is what he means: if it is an item that is designated always for eating, to exclude a case where he designated it for a purpose other than eating; and so is implied in the Beit Yosef, s.v. ‘the Rambam wrote,’ etc.; see there. And so is implied in the Kesef Mishneh there. Analyze this well.
It is forbidden to engage in commerce with it. The reason is lest he come to eat from them; but if he is not selling for the purpose of eating, there is no reason to decree, for specifically when he sells for the purpose of eating there is reason to decree lest he come to eat from them, since he is selling for eating, or lest he come to taste, as is the way of sellers of food. But when he is not occupied with it for the purpose of eating, there is no reason to decree. Alternatively, regarding ‘lest he come to eat,’ we are not concerned— are we dealing with wicked people who are not careful to eat prohibition? Rather, the reason is that one who sees that he trades with a prohibited item for the purpose of eating will think that just as he trades with them for eating, so too he eats from them; and therefore it is permitted where he is not selling for eating, even though it is an item that people commonly raise in order to eat, as I wrote in seif katan 1. And regarding a partner who transgressed and engaged in commerce with a prohibited item, see Choshen Mishpat, near the beginning of siman 182, in the Tur.
And even to purchase it in order to feed it to his non-Jewish workers is forbidden. According to the view of the Rav, and I do not know upon what many of our people relied who purchase treifos intentionally in order to feed their non-Jewish workers, and the matter is simple for them to permit; but this requires further study from where the Rav ruled this, for on the contrary, from the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the Beit Yosef brought it implies that it is permitted to purchase in order to feed his non-Jewish workers, for he forbade only purchasing in order to give to non-Jews; and the Beit Yosef wrote regarding it the reason: because a gift is like a sale, for if he did not receive benefit from him he would not give him anything. End of his words. (And likewise we say in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, daf 16a: if not that he troubled and appeased him, he would not have given him a gift; see there.) And if so, when he purchases in order to give it to non-Jews, it is like one who purchases to sell it to a non-Jew, for it is like commerce; but one who purchases in order to feed his workers that he has in his house, it implies that it is permitted, for this is not similar to commerce. And so is implied in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah (daf 63b); see there in Rashi, s.v. ‘and not because of yayin nesech’ and s.v. ‘that he carried’; analyze this well. And so is implied explicitly from Orchos Chayyim that the Beit Yosef brought, who wrote: we say in the Jerusalem Talmud: one who purchased pigs for the needs of non-Jewish workers, when he sells them he sells them only for their value. End of his words. It implies that when there was leftover and he comes to sell, he sells them only for the money he paid. And so wrote explicitly in the Atur Zahav: there are those who say that it is permitted to buy pigs to feed them to non-Jewish workers, etc.; but afterwards he wrote, and there are those who forbid, as written above. End of his words, and that is the view of the Rav.
Except for cheilev. That is, the cheilev of a tahor animal; but that of a tamei animal— it appears that the view of the Beit Yosef is that it is forbidden to engage in commerce with it, for in the verse it mentions only cheilev of a neveilah and a treifah. And that which the Mechaber wrote generally, ‘except for cheilev,’ is because he relied upon what he wrote, ‘for it is stated regarding it,’ etc.; and also because ordinarily one speaks of cheilev of a tahor animal. Not like the Orchos Chayyim who wrote that the verse does not distinguish between tahor and tamei. And I am also astonished at the Orchos Chayyim, for it is explicitly against the baraisa in Zevachim (daf 70a), and it is brought in Toras Kohanim, parashas Tzav, that this verse of ‘cheilev of a neveilah and a treifah,’ etc., speaks specifically of a tahor animal that has within its kind a treifah, and not of a tamei [animal]; see there. In any case, specifically cheilev that stands to be sold for eating is forbidden, but to smear hides, it was explained in seif katan 1 that it is permitted to sell it.
And if tamei [species] came into his hands, etc. In Tosafos in the chapter Kol Sha'ah it is stated that if it fell to him as an inheritance it is considered as ‘came into his hands’; and so is implied in the responsa of Menachem Azaryah siman 29; and so is implied from the words of the Tur in Choshen Mishpat, at the end of siman 9.
To a hunter, etc. Specifically to a hunter whose livelihood is that; but any other person, even though it came into his hands through trapping, they did not permit him— so wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the authorities. And in the responsa of Menachem Azaryah there, he ruled that even a hunter who is not established, and any person to whom it came into his hands, it is permitted to sell them; but the manner of commerce, even when it came into his hands and was merely incidental, is forbidden to purchase from them; except that if he transgressed and purchased, the Sages did not penalize him that it should be forbidden for benefit; see there, for he lengthened.
Tamei animals, birds, and fish, etc. This is the wording of the Tur: if tamei animals, birds, and fish came into a hunter’s hands, or if he trapped tamei together with tahor, it is permitted to sell them, provided that he did not intend for that.
And likewise one in whose house a neveilah, etc., came into his hands. This is an explicit verse (Devarim 14:21): ‘You shall not eat any neveilah; to the convert who is in your gates you shall give it, and he shall eat it, or sell it to a non-Jew.’ And this is the language of the Bach: it implies specifically the one to whom it came into his hands may sell it to a non-Jew because of his monetary loss; but another Jew is forbidden to buy from his fellow the neveilah or treifah that came into his hands in order to profit from it by selling it for a higher price to a non-Jew, for this too is commerce. End of his words. And so is implied in Tosafos in Sukkah (daf 39a), s.v. ‘and let him give it’; and so is implied from the view of R' Shimshon in his commentary to the Mishnayos, Shevi'is chapter 7 mishnah 3; see there. "," And it is written in the responsa Masaas Binyamin siman 25, in the gloss, that it is forbidden to kill goats by severing the throat when one needs their hides, in order afterwards to sell the meat to a non-Jew, for this is called directing one’s work toward a prohibited matter; and so wrote the Bach in Orach Chayyim siman 551 seif 10; see there.
It is permitted to sell them. And where he trapped tamei and tahor [species], he must sell the tahor and the tamei together, as Orchos Chayyim wrote; but not the tamei separately and not the tahor separately. And this is not comparable to a case where only tamei came up in his hand, for there they permitted him to sell, because if we do not permit him he will lose all his toil, and it is impossible in any other way; which is not so here— and not like the Beit Yosef.
And he must sell it immediately, etc. This gloss must be placed after ‘provided that he did not intend for that.’
And likewise it is permitted to collect, etc. And he must sell them immediately, and it is forbidden to keep them in order to profit from them; but to keep them until he can obtain their value, it is permitted to keep them even pigs. So wrote the responsa of Menachem Azaryah siman 30, and so wrote the responsa Masaas Binyamin siman 36. And see Choshen Mishpat siman 9.
For this is like rescuing them from their hand. Meaning, in ordinary circumstances we say that it is like rescuing them from their hand, not like Orchos Chayyim who wrote: and to take them in payment of a debt is forbidden, and if it is like rescuing them from their hand it is permitted. End of his words. And in the responsa of the Rashba that the Beit Yosef brought, it is explained that even pigs it is permitted to take in payment of a debt for this reason, that it is like rescuing them from their hand. And so it is in the responsa of Menachem Azaryah siman 30. And so wrote Rabbeinu Yeruham, netiv 15, end of os 27, in the name of the authorities: that if he collected them in payment of a debt, its law is like a case where they fell to him as an inheritance, that it is permitted to sell them little by little; see there.
And it is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew, etc. So wrote also the Mechaber in Choshen Mishpat siman 298 seif 6, and they were precise to write ‘to sell,’ for as a gift it is permitted, for in a gift there is no deception, as was explained in siman 65 seif 11; see there.
And anything whose prohibition is only by rabbinic law, etc. And it is implied that included in this is also morais of a non-Jew, which is forbidden only by rabbinic law, as the Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 111. And so it is in the Jerusalem Talmud, chapter 7 of Shevi'is, halacha 4: R' Yehoshua carried and gave (meaning, he engaged in buying and selling) in this morais. And even though the reason for the prohibition of morais is because they mix wine into it, as the Tur wrote in siman 114 seif 11, and their standard wine is forbidden for benefit as the Mechaber wrote at the beginning of siman 123— one can say that the morais is not sold for more because of the wine, and moreover that one can sell it excluding the value of the yayin nesech in it, as the Tur wrote in siman 134 seif 2 and the Rama in siman 154 seif katan 23; see there. The Bach wrote: and now some people are found who lease from the ministers towns and villages for a term, and throughout the term of the lease they raise pigs and feed them to non-Jewish workers, and there is no permission for this at all, etc. End of his words. And so wrote in the responsa of Menachem Azaryah siman 30; and see there, for he lengthened.
