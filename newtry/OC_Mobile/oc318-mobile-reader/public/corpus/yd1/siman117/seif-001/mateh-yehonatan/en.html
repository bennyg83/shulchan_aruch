(In siman 117 in Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) If it is an item designated for food, it is forbidden to engage in commerce with it. And see in the Bach, who brings the dispute between Tosafos and the Rashba: Tosafos holds that by Torah law it is forbidden to engage in commerce with a prohibited item, and the Rashba holds that it is only d'rabbanan. And the Taz asks: if so, what is the question in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, “if so, even l'chatchila as well,” etc.— perhaps indeed by Torah law it is permitted and it was forbidden only d'rabbanan, etc.; see there. And one can say that it is not difficult, for this is the explanation: since you come to permit benefit, if so we find an explicit permission, and the Sages would not have had [power] to forbid that which we see that the Torah explicitly permitted; and so wrote the Taz below regarding the sale of neveilos and treifos. And not like the Taz who wrote that the Rashba holds as well that it is forbidden by Torah law, for behold, the Shach (seif katan 1) brought a dispute in the name of Tosafos and the Rashba: Tosafos holds that it is permitted to sell an item designated for food in order to do something else with it, such as for labor; and that which forced the authorities to this is that which Tosafos asks in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, s.v. “but you may eat,” etc.: and if you say, we say in [the chapter] Merubah: “cursed is the man who raises pigs,” infer that by Torah law it is forbidden. And one can say that that which is forbidden by Torah law is in order to profit and to sell to non-Jews; but to smear [with it] or to light [a lamp] with it, by Torah law it is permitted; and nevertheless with pigs, such a case is forbidden. But the Rashba holds that if it is designated for food, even to use it for labor it is also forbidden to sell; if so, Tosafos’s question in Merubah is difficult— and this is not so; rather, it is impossible that the Rashba holds it is forbidden by Torah law. In contrast, if he holds that it is only d'rabbanan, Tosafos’s question is not difficult, for one can say that initially, in the days of the Chashmonaim, they decreed not to engage in commerce in order to profit with pigs, and afterwards Chazal decreed not to engage [in commerce] with any prohibited item. And now it is not like the Taz; and thus the Rashba holds that it is only d'rabbanan, for otherwise Tosafos’s question in Merubah [remains]. Analyze this well.
