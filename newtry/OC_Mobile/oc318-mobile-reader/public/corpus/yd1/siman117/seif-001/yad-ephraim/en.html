An item designated for food. See Ba'er Hetev, and in the responsa Gevuros Anashim, Yoreh De'ah, klal 1, siman 4: his view is that mumya— which is the flesh of a dead person that they embalm with spices and heal with— is forbidden for eating and for commerce, unlike the Rambam and the Maharik, who permit, since it has become unfit for a dog’s eating and is mere dust. And he wrote to forbid because one benefits from the flesh of a corpse, and even though it is not in the normal manner of benefit, as stated in Sanhedrin regarding the grave of Rav. And even though they are corpses of non-Jews, the view of many authorities is that one does not distinguish; see there. And see the responsa Yad Eliyahu siman 44 and siman 66, regarding one who bought a belt and it was from the hide of a non-Jew who was killed, that it is permitted; and the responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 89, who distinguishes between a slave acquired to him, and brings a proof from Tosafos, Bava Kamma daf 10, and in Yad Eliyahu Rabbah; see there.
